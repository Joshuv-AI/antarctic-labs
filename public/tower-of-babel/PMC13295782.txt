Confocal laser endomicroscopy-assisted endobronchial ultrasound-guided needle aspiration in diagnosing mediastinal and/or hilar lymphadenopathy (COLLABORATION-III): protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Ziwen Zheng, Li Xu, Chao Wu, Jiangrong Liao, Mingming Deng, Run Tong, Gang Hou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionEndobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) is an established first-line technique for sampling mediastinal and/or hilar lymphadenopathy (MHL). However, its diagnostic accuracy is constrained by the procurement of small-volume specimens, which may not reliably capture the diagnostically relevant tissue. Needle-based confocal laser endomicroscopy (nCLE) provides real-time in vivo microscopic imaging and has the potential to facilitate targeted tissue acquisition. EBUS-guided transbronchial mediastinal cryobiopsy (EBUS-TBMC) can yield larger and better-preserved specimens; however, its availability is limited. This study aims to evaluate whether nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA is non-inferior to EBUS-TBMC for diagnostic yield for MHL.Methods and analysisThe confocal laser endomicroscopy-assisted endobronchial ultrasound-guided needle aspiration (COLLABORATION III) trial is a prospective, multicentre, stratified, non-inferiority randomised controlled trial conducted across five centres in China. The study aims to enroll 640 adult participants presenting with unexplained MHL with a short-axis measurement ≥10 mm necessitating histopathological confirmation. Participants will be stratified based on the preprocedural likelihood of malignancy and randomised at a 1:1 ratio to undergo nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA or EBUS-TBMC. The primary endpoint is the overall diagnostic yield, defined as the proportion of procedures that obtain specimens sufficient for a definitive histological or cytological diagnosis, with a non-inferiority margin set at −10%. Secondary endpoints include diagnostic sensitivity for both malignant and benign conditions, rate of granuloma detection, procedure duration and incidence of adverse events. The primary analysis will be conducted on an intention-to-treat basis, supplemented by a per-protocol sensitivity analysis. Non-inferiority will be established if the difference in the diagnostic yield exceeds −10%.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the China–Japan Friendship Hospital (2025-KY-388). Written informed consent will be required from all participants. WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

Endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) is an established minimally invasive sampling method for mediastinal and/or hilar lymphadenopathies (MHLs) but is limited by the small volume of specimens that may miss focal pathology. EBUS-guided transbronchial mediastinal cryobiopsy (EBUS-TBMC) obtains larger tissue samples with a high diagnostic yield but requires specialised equipment and expertise, limiting its widespread adoption. Needle-based confocal laser endomicroscopy (nCLE) can provide real-time microscopic imaging to guide targeted sampling; however, no randomised trial has directly compared nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA with EBUS-TBMC.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

The confocal laser endomicroscopy-assisted endobronchial ultrasound-guided needle aspiration (COLLABORATION-III) trial is the first randomised controlled trial to compare nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA with EBUS-TBMC, assessing whether nCLE guidance can achieve a diagnostic yield that is non-inferior to that of EBUS-TBMC in patients presenting with unexplained MHL. Additionally, the trial aims to compare the diagnostic performance of these techniques in detecting granulomas and subtyping malignancies, including lymphomas.

Introduction

Endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) is an established and minimally invasive sampling method for mediastinal and/or hilar lymphadenopathy (MHL) with real-time ultrasonic visualisation that allows macroscopic needle positioning within the lymph nodes.1 2 Several clinical practice guidelines have recommended EBUS-TBNA as the first-line sampling method for assessing mediastinal involvement in various diseases, including malignancy staging and sarcoidosis.3 4 However, a fundamental diagnostic limitation of EBUS-TBNA is its acquisition of small histological cores. Hence, the diagnostic performance of this approach depends mainly on whether the limited tissue obtained can be consistently drawn from the specific microscopic area of pathology within a lymph node, especially in diseases in which the diagnosis relies on obtaining a specific pathological finding, such as the subtyping of malignancies and granulomas.5,7 Non-diagnostic samples in these cases can result in false-negative outcomes.

Consequently, it is widely acknowledged that the diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBNA is predominantly limited by the volume of specimens collected and the preservation of tissue architecture. In routine clinical practice, the fanning techniques and multiple needle passes are employed to sample various angles within the lymph node, but the diagnostic yield remains unsatisfactory in many cases.8 In addition, the use of larger-gauge needles, for example, 19 G, is intended to increase the volume of tissue obtained, but the technique does not involve guiding the operator to the optimal biopsy site,9 10 and the translation from the tissue volume to the diagnostic yield of different needle sizes was not observed in a previous study.11 Similarly, rapid on-set evaluation serves as a post-procedural quality control measure by assessing the adequacy of acquired specimens, but this does not provide real-time image-based guidance for targeting specific microscopic focal areas.12 While these strategies have demonstrated an increase in specimen quantity, they have not consistently resulted in an improved diagnostic yield. Collectively, these approaches may improve the likelihood of sampling diagnostically informative regions, but they do not augment the relative volume of tissue obtained from these regions within the constraints of the inherently limited specimen.

Recently, an advanced mediastinal sampling method called EBUS-guided transbronchial mediastinal cryobiopsy (EBUS-TBMC) was developed.13 Despite published studies showing that EBUS-TBMC could obtain a higher diagnostic yield than EBUS-TBNA via larger and better-preserved samples, the current lack of evidence regarding safety, procedural complexity, and specific equipment requirements prevents the routine use of EBUS-TBMC in clinical practice.14 15 In situations where EBUS-TBMC is not readily accessible, there is an unmet clinical need for strategies to improve the sampling accuracy of the widely utilised EBUS-TBNA within a lymph node.

Needle-based confocal laser endomicroscopy (nCLE) is a potential solution for real-time in vivo microscopic imaging at the cellular level. Additionally, preliminary studies have suggested that nCLE can identify distinct micro-architectural features of malignancy and granulomatous inflammation within the lymph nodes.16 17 While nCLE as an imaging modality is unable to modify the fundamental attributes of the EBUS-TBNA, it has the potential to improve the diagnostic yield of the limited specimen by guiding each pass towards regions containing the specific pathological features. Hence, we hypothesised that nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA might achieve a diagnostic yield that is not inferior to that of EBUS-TBMC. However, the evidence for this is lacking.

Methods and analysis

Aims

The confocal laser endomicroscopy-assisted endobronchial ultrasound-guided needle aspiration (COLLABORATION-III) trial primarily aims to identify whether nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA can achieve a diagnostic yield that is non-inferior to that of EBUS-TBMC. The secondary aims of this study are to assess the non-inferiority of nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA compared with EBUS-TBMC in granuloma detection rates among patients with a final diagnosis of granulomatous disease and in subtyping capabilities for malignant lymphoproliferative disorders.

Study design and setting

COLLABORATION III is a China-based non-inferior, stratified, randomised controlled trial. Five sites with devices for nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA and EBUS-TBMC, such as nCLE probes, ultrasonic bronchoscopes, and cryoprobes, were included. The study flow had been presented in figure 1. This trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT07040670) on 26 June 2025, prior to the enrolment of the participants.

Patient and public involvement

The COLLABORATION-III was designed without the involvement of patients or the public.

Participants

Consecutive patients with unexplained MHL requiring diagnostic bronchoscopy will be prospectively screened for eligibility. The inclusion criteria will include: (1) age ≥18 years at the time of screening; (2) presence of MHL with a short-axis diameter >10 mm on chest CT or positron emission tomography–CT, requesting histopathological confirmation; (3) clinical indication to undergo EBUS-guided mediastinal sampling as part of a routine diagnostic pathway based on an integrated assessment of clinical presentation, imaging features, and the need for histopathological diagnosis to guide subsequent management and (4) ability to understand the nature and purpose of the trial and to provide written informed consent before enrolment.

The exclusion criteria include:

(1) Absolute or relative contradiction to flexible bronchoscopy or mediastinal biopsy, such as:

coagulation disorders not correctable by standard measures, indicated by an international normalised ratio >1.5 and a platelet count <60 × 109 /L;

coagulation disorders not correctable by standard measures, indicated by an international normalised ratio >1.5 and a platelet count <60 × 109 /L;

severe pulmonary arterial hypertension (systolic pulmonary artery pressure >70 mm Hg);

severe pulmonary arterial hypertension (systolic pulmonary artery pressure >70 mm Hg);

haemodynamic instability requiring vasopressor support or

haemodynamic instability requiring vasopressor support or

respiratory insufficiency with arterial oxygen saturation <90% despite supplemental oxygen;

respiratory insufficiency with arterial oxygen saturation <90% despite supplemental oxygen;

(2) documented or suspected hypersensitivity to fluorescein sodium, lidocaine, midazolam or any other agent scheduled for use duringthe procedure;

(3) imaging or Doppler findings indicating an elevated bleeding risk at the intended biopsy site or lesions with features suggestive of cyst or abscess formation, such as a fluid-filled cavity, rim enhancement, suspected purulent content or necrosis with liquefaction;

(4) pregnancy or breastfeeding at enrolment;

(5) concurrent participation in another interventional clinical trial that could potentially interfere with the outcomes of the study and

(6) any other medical, psychological or social condition that, in the judgement of the investigating physician, would preclude safe participation or adherence to follow-up requirements.

Allocation

Following confirmation of eligibility, participants will be stratified according to the preprocedural likelihood of malignant disease as determined by a local multidisciplinary team based on clinical presentation, imaging features and laboratory results. Within each group, a central randomisation system employing computer-generated permuted blocks will assign participants at a 1:1 ratio to either the nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA or EBUS-TBMC arm. Allocation concealment will be ensured using sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes that will be opened immediately before the procedure.

Intervention

All procedures will be conducted under moderate sedation or general anaesthesia by bronchoscopists with substantial expertise in interventional pulmonology. A convex probe ultrasound bronchoscope will be used for all examinations and interventions. After conducting a standard airway inspection, the target lymph node will be identified and systematically assessed using EBUS and power Doppler to evaluate its size, echogenicity, vascular pattern and optimal sampling angle.

nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA

For nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA, a 19 G aspiration needle is introduced under EBUS guidance to establish a channel between the airway wall and the target lymph node. On successful access, an nCLE probe is introduced through the 19 G needle into the target lymph node. Real-time microscopic imaging is also conducted to identify areas of interest, with particular emphasis on morphological patterns indicative of malignancy or granulomatous inflammation, as documented in the literature.17 In particular, normal or reactive lymph nodes are characterised by a dark capsule with thin autofluorescent fibres and a cortex populated by individual dark grey lymphocytes that form loose aggregates or variably sized round follicles.17 Malignant involvement is identified by clusters of dark, pleomorphic tumour cells, which may or may not be accompanied by intercellular fluorescein, or by dense, cohesive dark clumps exhibiting directional streaming.18 In contrast, granulomatous inflammation is defined as brighter-toned, homogeneous and well-demarcated lesions relative to the surrounding background.17 Any other atypical structure that deviates from the normal pattern, even if it does not meet these specific criteria, is also regarded as a region of interest. Once a region of interest is identified, its location is documented using the graduated markings of the probe and corresponding EBUS landmarks. Subsequently, the nCLE probe is withdrawn while maintaining the 19 G needle in position. Tissue aspiration is then performed at the target site using the same needle. A total of five needle passes will be executed in this study: four specimens will be submitted for histopathological evaluation and one will be sent for microbiological analysis. Each pass will involve 10–20 to-and-fro movements within the lymph node.

EBUS-TBMC

Following standard EBUS evaluation, a channel will be established between the airway wall and the target lymph node. The method employed for tunnel creation, such as using a puncture dilation catheter, a 19 G needle or alternative techniques, will be determined by the operating bronchoscopist, taking into account the anatomical considerations, lesion characteristics and institutional protocols.19 20 A cryoprobe will be advanced through the established channel to the target lymph node under EBUS guidance. The biopsy procedure aimed to obtain specimens with diameters exceeding 6 mm, to ensure sufficient tissue for histopathological evaluation. Specific parameters for freezing time and probe size will be determined by the operating bronchoscopist, informed by real-time ultrasound monitoring, tissue response and the objective of acquiring high-quality diagnostic materials.21 Three cryobiopsy specimens will be collected from the target lymph node. Following each biopsy, both the bronchoscope and cryoprobe will be withdrawn simultaneously, and the airway will be re-examined for bleeding prior to subsequent passes. After completing three passes of cryobiopsy, a single TBNA pass with a 19 G needle is routinely done on the same lymph node for microbiological tests when an infection is suspected. This TBNA pass does not contribute to the diagnostic yield of the EBUS-TBMC procedure. Additionally, if enough cryobiopsy material remains after diagnosis and if clinically necessary, these specimens may be cultured for exploratory analysis, with results reported descriptively for hypothesis generation. A final comprehensive inspection of the biopsy site and airway will be conducted.

All participants will undergo standardised preprocedural preparation, intraprocedural monitoring and postprocedural observation in alignment with institutional guidelines. For both cohorts, the following data will be meticulously documented: the total duration of the procedure (measured from insertion to removal of the scope), device settings and parameters, number of biopsy passes, any intraprocedural adverse events and any technical difficulties encountered.

Standard references

The definitive diagnosis for each participant will be ascertained by an independent expert panel consisting of pulmonologists, radiologists and pathologists who will remain blinded to the initial group assignments. However, complete blinding of pathologists to the specimen type may not be entirely feasible because of the distinct morphological characteristics of needle aspirates and cryobiopsy samples. To reduce potential bias, all specimens will be processed and evaluated in accordance with standardised histopathological criteria without considering the acquisition method. Diagnosis will be based exclusively on morphological, cytological and immunohistochemical features. This composite reference standard will incorporate histopathological findings from all biopsy specimens, relevant microbiological results, clinical presentation, longitudinal progression, 6-month imaging follow-up and outcomes of subsequent diagnostic procedures.

Malignant diseases will be further stratified into specific subtypes in accordance with the WHO criteria, which include histological subtyping of lung cancer and specific classification of lymphoma variants.22 Benign conditions will be categorised into specific diagnoses, such as sarcoidosis, tuberculosis or silicosis, or as non-diagnostic inflammation or reactive changes when histopathological findings reveal non-diagnostic inflammatory patterns, provided that imaging 6 months after biopsy confirms lesion stability or regression without targeted therapy. All diagnostic determinations will be confirmed by established consensus criteria, thereby providing a robust reference standard for assessing the diagnostic accuracy of both index tests across the full spectrum of mediastinal lymphadenopathies.

Outcomes

The primary outcome of the COLLABORATION-III study is the overall diagnostic yield, defined as the proportion of procedures yielding specimens adequate for establishing a specific histological or cytological diagnosis in accordance with established reporting standards.23 The secondary outcomes to be assessed in the COLLABORATION-III are: (1) diagnostic sensitivity for malignant diseases overall and for specific malignant subtypes in accordance with WHO criteria,22 with a comparison of success rates in subtyping lung cancer and precisely classifying lymphoma variants between the two techniques; (2) diagnostic sensitivity for specific benign conditions; (3) the rate of granuloma detection, defined as the proportion of procedures in which non-caseating or caseating granulomas are identified through histological examination, regardless of the final diagnosis14; (4) the total duration of the procedure and (5) the incidence and severity of procedure-related adverse events, including bleeding, classified according to standardised grading schemes.24 The exploratory outcomes to be assessed are: (1) the DNA yield (ng) extracted from residual formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue of the diagnostic specimens in patients with a final diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer and its concentration is quantified using a fluorometric technique25; and (2) microbiological yield is measured as the percentage of samples testing positive for any pathogen, including Mycobacterium tuberculosis, via GeneXpert and MGIT culture, for both routine TBNA aspirates in both groups and cryobiopsy specimens in the EBUS-TBMC group.

Sample size calculation

The estimation of the target sample size was predicated on a non-inferiority design that incorporates stratified randomisation according to the suspected disease type in patients presenting with unexplained MHL undergoing diagnostic biopsy. Within the target population, it is projected that 54.5% of patients will exhibit suspected non-metastatic lymphadenopathy, encompassing benign granulomatous conditions such as sarcoidosis, tuberculosis and lymphoma. Conversely, 45.5% of patients are expected to present with suspected metastatic malignant lymphadenopathy, including mediastinal lymph node metastases originating from lung cancer or other solid tumours.13

In a cohort with suspected non-metastatic MHL, the diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBMC is estimated to be 96.7%, as supported by a recent randomised controlled trial.15 The anticipated diagnostic yield of nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA within this group is 93.0%, with a non-inferiority margin of 10% considered acceptable due to the potential safety and procedural benefits associated with the nCLE-assisted technique. In a cohort with suspected metastatic malignant MHL, the subtyping success rate for EBUS-TBMC is estimated to be 91%,13 whereas that for nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA is anticipated to be 90%, maintaining the same non-inferiority margin of 10%.

The target sample size was calculated separately for each group using methods for non-inferiority trials comparing two proportions, with a one-sided type I error (α) of 0.025 and a statistical power of 90%. A larger sample size was chosen to ensure sufficient power in both subgroups. Accounting for an expected dropout rate of 10%, the total required sample size is 640 participants, with 320 individuals allocated to each group. Sample size calculations were conducted using PASS 2021 software (NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, UT, USA).

Statistical analysis

The primary analysis will be performed on an intention-to-treat basis, including all randomised participants. Participants for whom a final reference diagnosis cannot be established will be classified as having a non-diagnostic outcome. A modified intention-to-treat analysis, restricted to participants who received the allocated intervention and had a final reference diagnosis established, will be conducted as a supplementary analysis. This population reflects real-world clinical practice by including cases in which pre-procedural suspicion differs from the final diagnosis, such as suspected non-metastatic disease ultimately proven to be a metastatic malignancy or lymphoma, thereby providing a conservative estimate of diagnostic performance. A per-protocol analysis will be conducted as a sensitivity analysis, with the per-protocol population defined to address the potential bias arising from disease spectrum effects. This analysis will examine whether the observed diagnostic performance remains consistent when restricted to participants with confirmed disease categories representative of the intended spectrum and will separately evaluate subgroups defined by concordance between suspected and final diagnosis.

The primary outcome of the diagnostic yield will be compared between the groups using the difference in proportions with a two-sided 95% CI. Non-inferiority of nCLE-assisted EBUS-TBNA relative to EBUS-TBMC will be concluded if the lower limit of the two-sided 95% CI for the difference in diagnostic yield is greater than −10%.

The analysis of secondary outcomes will be conducted as follows. Disease-specific sensitivity for both malignant conditions and their subtypes, as well as for benign conditions such as sarcoidosis and tuberculosis, will be calculated separately for each group using 2×2 contingency tables with a 95% CI. Comparisons between groups will be performed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. The granuloma detection rate and procedure time will be evaluated using the χ2 test for categorical variables and either an independent samples t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables, contingent on data distributions. Furthermore, rates of adverse events, including bleeding classified according to standardised grading schemes, will be compared between groups using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test.

To analyse the impact of disease composition on diagnostic outcomes, comprehensive contingency tables will be developed to cross-tabulate each test result category against the final reference diagnoses. Participants who exhibit nonspecific inflammatory findings and receive a final diagnosis of nonspecific benign lymphadenopathy after a 6-month follow-up period will be included in the modified intention-to-treat analysis and excluded from the pre-protocol analysis. Moreover, they will be excluded from the disease-specific sensitivity analysis because they do not correspond to any particular disease category of interest. Participants lost to follow-up without a final diagnosis will be classified as having a non-diagnostic outcome in the intention-to-treat analysis.

All statistical analyses will employ two-sided tests, with statistical significance established at p<0.05. However, a one-sided alpha level of 0.025 will be used for non-inferiority assessment of the primary outcome. Statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS software (version 26.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Ethics and dissemination

This study was designed and will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the China–Japan Friendship Hospital (approval number: 2025-KY-388). Any protocol amendments will be submitted for approval before implementation.

Prior to enrolment, all participants will provide written informed consent. To ensure participant confidentiality, unique study codes will be assigned, and all data will be securely stored in password-protected databases accessible exclusively to the research team. An independent data- and safety-monitoring board will supervise the trials. All adverse events will be documented and reported to the ethics committee in accordance with regulatory requirements. The results will be disseminated in peer-reviewed journals and conference presentations. Anonymised individual participant data underlying the published results will be made available on reasonable request to the corresponding author, starting 6 months post-publication, contingent on approval by an independent review committee and the signing of a data-sharing agreement. The complete protocol and statistical analysis plan will be provided as supplementary materials with the primary publications.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged in the COLLABORATION-III study. First, even if non-inferiority is demonstrated, any observed benefit would likely be due to an increased proportion of diagnostically informative tissue obtained from the small sample, rather than an improvement in specimen quality. It is important to note that nCLE serves as a guidance tool and cannot modify the intrinsic specific pathological findings. However, the specimen volume and architectural preservation are inherently constrained by the nature of EBUS-TBNA itself. Second, the non-inferiority margin of 10% was selected based on clinical relevance and feasibility considerations, and the robustness of the primary findings will be further examined through sensitivity analysis. Third, due to the nature of the interventions, it is not possible to blind the operating bronchoscopist to the assigned approach, which may introduce bias in the sampling process. Nonetheless, the primary outcome is evaluated against a composite reference standard by an independent expert panel that is blinded to group assignment, which may help mitigate this concern.
