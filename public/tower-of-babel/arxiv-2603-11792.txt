Duality for Delsarte’s extremal problem on compact Gelfand pairs
Elena E. Berdysheva, Bálint Farkas, Marcell Gaál, Mita D. Ramabulana, Szilárd Gy. Révész

Abstract
. We study Delsarte-type problems for positive definite functions on compact Gelfand pairs as infinite-dimensional linear programming problems. This setup includes, as a particular case, the case of compact Abelian groups. Depending on the restriction on the signs of the functions, we obtain two important particular cases, the Turán and Delsarte problems. These problems have been studied in relation to number theory, sphere packing, and statistics. In this paper, we describe their duals and prove a strong duality statement.

Introduction

Extremal problems for positive definite functions play an important role in harmonic analysis, number theory, and geometry. A common theme in this topic is to study these problems as linear programming problems, describe their duals, and seek a strong duality statement. In Révész

3.
Duality in infinite dimensional linear programming: Duffin’s approach

The aim of this section is to put together—in the form we need—known results concerning problems of infinite dimensional linear programming, and their duality. Duffin
[
16
]
seems to be the first to consider duality relations not only between the values of the primary and the dual problems, but allowing a linear programming problem with weakened restrictions on one of the sides. His ideas were generalized to a much more general setup (convex programming and even more general problems, and replacing topological duality of spaces by duality of linear spaces induced by a bilinear form), in particular, by Ioffe and Tikhomirov in
[
25
]
, and by Gol’shtein in a number of papers and the book
[
21
]
(the book
[
21
]
is unfortunately available only in Russian). The three mentioned sources are the foundation for this section.

Here we will mainly follow the presentation in Duffin’s paper
[
16
]
, but use notation and terminology adapted to our goals.

on

. Note that the bilinear form

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\frac{\int_{G}\varphi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\varphi,\sigma\rangle_{2}}:\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\frac{\int_{G}\varphi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\varphi,\sigma\rangle_{2}}:\varphi\in\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\right\}.

Extremal problem (
2
) is a particular case of the above problem with
\sigma=\delta_{e}
, the Dirac measure at
e
with
s=\widehat{\sigma}=\mathbbm{1}_{G}
.

We have discussed in Section
1
that the condition
e\in\mbox{\rm int\,}{\Omega_{+}}
is equivalent to the fact that the class
\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
is non-empty. Moreover, the construction described in Section
2
gives a function in the class
\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
which is non-negative and not identically zero, and therefore has a strictly positive integral. This implies that
\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})>0
. It follows, in particular, that in the supremum in (
9
) we may only consider functions
\varphi
with
f(\mathbbm{1}_{G})=\int_{G}\varphi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}>0
.

If
\varphi\in C(G)^{K}
,
\varphi\gg 0
, with
f(\mathbbm{1}_{G})=\int_{G}\varphi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=1
, then by (
8
)

(10)

\langle\varphi,\sigma\rangle_{2}\geq f(\mathbbm{1}_{G})s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})=s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})>0,

and

0<\frac{\int_{G}\varphi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\varphi,\sigma\rangle_{2}}\leq\frac{1}{s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})}

for such functions.

The extremal problem (
9
) can be reformulated in terms of the Fourier transforms
f
of functions
\varphi
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=\sup

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{f(\mathbbm{1}_{G})}{f(\mathbbm{1}_{G})s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})+\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)s(\gamma)}:\ \widecheck{f}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{1}{s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})+\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)s(\gamma)}:\widecheck{f}\ \in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}),\ f(\mathbbm{1}_{G})=1\right\}.

The considerations above show that

0<\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\leq\frac{1}{s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})}.

Instead of this problem, we shall consider the equivalent problem to find

\displaystyle u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}):=\inf

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)s(\gamma):\right.f\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\Gamma),\ \varphi|_{\Omega_{+}^{c}}\leq 0,\ \varphi|_{\Omega_{-}^{c}}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\text{where }\left.\varphi=1+\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)\gamma\right\}.

The two problems are connected by the equation

(11)

\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=\frac{1}{s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})+u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})}.

We consider the operator
T:\ell^{1}(\Gamma)\to C(G)^{K}
,

Tf:=\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)\gamma,\quad f\in\ell^{1}(\Gamma).

It is a linear operator from
\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
to
C(G)^{K}
. The problem
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
can be rewritten as

u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=\inf\left\{\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)s(\gamma):f\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\Gamma),\ Tf+\mathbbm{1}_{G}\in Q\right\}.

The adjoint operator of
T
(in the sense explained in Section
1
) is the operator
T^{*}:M(G)^{K}\to\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma)
,

(T^{*}\mu)(\gamma):=\begin{cases}\int_{G}\gamma\,\mbox{d}\mu,&\gamma\neq\mathbbm{1}_{G},\\
0,&\gamma=\mathbbm{1}_{G}.\end{cases}

Indeed, for
f\in\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
and
\mu\in M(G)^{K}
we have

\displaystyle\langle Tf,\mu\rangle_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)\gamma(x)\,\mbox{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)\int_{G}\gamma(x)\,\mbox{d}\mu(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)(T^{*}\mu)(\gamma)=\langle T^{*}\mu,f\rangle_{1}.

With the notation
b\in C(G)^{K}
being the constant function
b:=-\mathbbm{1}_{G}
and
c\in\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma)
being

c(\gamma):=\begin{cases}s(\gamma),&\gamma\neq\mathbbm{1}_{G},\\
0,&\gamma=\mathbbm{1}_{G},\end{cases}

we arrive at the primary problem

u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=\inf\{\langle c,f\rangle_{1}:f\geq_{\ell^{1}_{+}(\Gamma)}0,\ Tf\geq_{Q}b\}

which is exactly of the form (
3
).
The dual problem (
5
) is

v_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=\sup\{\langle b,\mu\rangle_{2}:\mu\geq_{Q^{*}}0,\ T^{*}\mu\leq_{L^{\infty}_{+}(\Gamma)}c\}.

Here

\langle b,\mu\rangle_{2}=\langle-\mathbbm{1}_{G},\mu\rangle_{2}=-\int_{G}\mbox{d}\mu=-\mu(G).

The condition
T^{*}\mu\leq_{L^{\infty}_{+}(\Gamma)}c
means that for
\gamma\neq\mathbbm{1}_{G}
we have
\int_{G}\gamma\,\mbox{d}\mu\leq s(\gamma)
. Thus, the dual problem can be written as

\displaystyle v_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{-\mu(G):\mu\in Q^{*},\ \int_{G}\gamma\,\mbox{d}\mu\leq s(\gamma)\text{ for all }\gamma\neq\mathbbm{1}_{G}\right\}

(12)

\displaystyle=-\inf\left\{\mu(G):\mu\in Q^{*},\ \int_{G}\gamma\,\mbox{d}\mu\leq s(\gamma)\text{ for all }\gamma\neq\mathbbm{1}_{G}\right\}.

Our next aim is to establish the strong duality relation
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=v_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
using Theorem
7
and Corollary
8
. We will even show that the primary problem

u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
is consistent, has a finite value and is well-posed.

We start with the first two claims. We have discussed above that the class
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
is non-empty, and that it is sufficient to consider only functions with
f(\mathbbm{1}_{G})=\int_{G}\penalty\ \varphi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=\penalty\ 1
. Fourier transforms
f=\widehat{\varphi}
of such functions are feasible in the problem
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
. Thus, the problem
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
is consistent. Since it is a minimization problem of a non-negative quantity, we conclude that it has a finite value.

To show that the problem
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
is well-posed, we consider the extended problems

u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon):=\inf\left\{\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{0\}}f(\gamma)s(\gamma):f\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\Gamma),\ Tf+\mathbbm{1}_{G}\in Q+G(\varepsilon)\right\}

with
0<\varepsilon<1
and
G(\varepsilon):=\{g\in C(G)^{K}:\|g\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\}
.
We use here the fact that the sets
G(\varepsilon)
build a basis of neighbourhoods of zero in the topology of
C(G)^{K}
. If
(f_{n})
is an asymptotically feasible sequence, then
(f_{n})\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\Gamma)
and

Tf_{n}+\mathbbm{1}_{G}=q_{n}+z_{n},\quad\text{where}\quad q_{n}\in Q,\ \|z_{n}\|_{C(G)^{K}}\to 0,

i.e.
Tf+\mathbbm{1}_{G}\in Q+G(\varepsilon)
for large enough
n
.

We will show that

(13)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)=u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}).

It turns out that the question is easier to handle for the classical Delsarte constraint where we only have a restriction on the set of positivity.

5.1.
Classical Delsarte constraint

We consider the case when
\Omega_{-}=G
, i.e. a restriction is given only on sets of positivity of functions
\varphi
. In this case
B=\emptyset
, the positive cone in the space
F=C(G)^{K}
is
Q=M=\{\varphi\in C(G)^{K}\penalty\ :\penalty\ \varphi|_{A}\leq 0\}
, and by Lemma
10
the positive cone in the space
F^{*}=M(G)^{K}
is
Q^{*}=M^{*}=\{\mu\in M(G)^{K}:\mu|_{\overline{A}}\leq 0,\ \mu|_{\overline{A}^{c}}\equiv 0\}
.

The extremal problem
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},G)
takes the form

\displaystyle u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},G)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)s(\gamma):\right.f\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\Gamma),\ \varphi|_{A}\leq 0,

\displaystyle\text{where }\left.\varphi=1+\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)\gamma\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)s(\gamma):f\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\Gamma),\ Tf+\mathbbm{1}_{G}\in M\right\},

and the extended problem
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},G;\varepsilon)
is

\displaystyle u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},G;\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)s(\gamma):f\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\Gamma),\ Tf+\mathbbm{1}_{G}\in M+G(\varepsilon)\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)s(\gamma):f\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\Gamma),\ Tf+\mathbbm{1}_{G}\leq\varepsilon\mathbbm{1}_{G}\ \text{on}\ A\right\}.

Arguing as Arestov and Babenko in
[
1
]
, we show that

(14)

u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},G;\varepsilon)=(1-\varepsilon)u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},G),\quad 0<\varepsilon<1.

To prove (
14
) we notice that for each
g
which is feasible in the problem
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},G;\varepsilon)
(i.e.,
g\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\Gamma)
and
\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{g}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}g(\gamma)\gamma+(1-\varepsilon)\mathbbm{1}\leq 0
on
A
), the function
f=\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}g
is feasible in the problem
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},G)
(namely,
f\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\Gamma)
and
\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)\gamma+\mathbbm{1}_{G}\leq 0
on
A
). Conversely, for each
f
feasible in the problem
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},G)
, the function
g=(1-\varepsilon)f
is feasible in the problem
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},G;\varepsilon)
. Obviously,

\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{g}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}g(\gamma)s(\gamma)=(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)s(\gamma),

and (
14
) follows. This immediately implies (
13
), and we arrive at the following statement.

Theorem 14
.

Let
(G,K)
be a compact Gelfand pair . Let
\Omega_{+}
be a
K
-bi-invariant symmetric Borel subset of
G
with
e\in\mbox{\rm int\,}{\Omega_{+}}
. Let
\sigma\in M(G)^{K}
be a positive definite measure satisfying Wiener’s condition. Then

u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},G)=v_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},G).

5.2.
The more general Delsarte-type constraint

We now come back to the general case when restrictions are posed on both the set of positivity and the set of negativity of
\varphi
.

Recall that

\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=\sup\left\{\frac{\int_{G}\varphi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\varphi,\sigma\rangle_{2}}:\varphi\in\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\right\},

where

\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=\{\varphi\in C(G)^{K}:\varphi\gg 0,\ \varphi(e)=1,\ \varphi|_{\Omega_{+}^{c}}\leq 0,\ \varphi|_{\Omega_{-}^{c}}\geq 0\}.

To establish the duality result, we consider the problem on the extended class

\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon):=\{\varphi\in C(G)^{K}:\varphi\gg 0,\ \varphi(e)=1,\ \varphi|_{\Omega_{+}^{c}}\leq\varepsilon,\ \varphi|_{\Omega_{-}^{c}}\geq-\varepsilon\},

and the extremal value

\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon):=\sup\left\{\frac{\int_{G}\varphi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\varphi,\sigma\rangle_{2}}:\varphi\in\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)\right\},

where
\varepsilon>0
.

We will again employ Assumption O that was introduced in Section
1
.

Lemma 15
.

Let
(G,K)
be a compact Gelfand pair. Let
\Omega_{+}
,
\Omega_{-}
be
K
-bi-invariant symmetric Borel subsets of
G
with
e\in\mbox{\rm int\,}{\Omega_{+}}
, and
\Omega_{+}^{c}
,
\Omega_{-}^{c}
satisfying Assumption O.
Assume that
\sigma\in M(G)^{K}
is a positive definite measure satisfying Wiener’s condition, and such that

\sigma=c\delta_{e}+\tau
, where
\tau\in M(G)^{K}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Haar measure and
c\geq 0
.
Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)=\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}).

Proof.

The proof uses ideas from the papers
[
5
]
by Berdysheva, Ramabulana, Révész,
[
18
]
by Gaál, Nagy-Csiha, and
[
32
]
by Ramabulana.

It is clear that

(15)

\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\subset\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon_{1})\subset\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon_{2}),\quad 0<\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}.

Consequently,

\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\leq\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon_{1})\leq\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon_{2}),\quad 0<\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}.

Since the quantity
\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)
decreases as
\varepsilon
monotonically decreases to
0+
, and is bounded below by
\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
, the limit

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)

exists and

(16)

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\geq\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})>0.

We wish to show that

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}).

Denote
\mathcal{A}_{n}:=\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}\left(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\frac{1}{n}\right)
.
Clearly,
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

Take
\psi_{n}\in\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}\left(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\frac{1}{n}\right)
such that

(17)

\mathcal{A}_{n}-\frac{1}{n}\leq\frac{\int_{G}\psi_{n}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}}\leq\mathcal{A}_{n}.

By the definition of the classes
\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}\left(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\frac{1}{n}\right)
we have that
\|\psi_{n}\|_{\infty}=1
. Invoking the fact that
\lambda_{G}(G)=1
, we have

\int_{G}|\psi_{n}|^{2}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}\leq\|\psi_{n}\|_{\infty}^{2}\lambda_{G}(G)=1.

Therefore, the sequence
(\psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
belongs to the closed unit ball of the space
L^{2}(G)
which is weakly sequentially compact (e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 3.18]
). Thus, there is a subsequence of
(\psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges weakly in
L^{2}(G)
to some function
\psi\in L^{2}(G)
. For simplicity we assume that
(\psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
itself is such a sequence.

Note that each class
\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}\left(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\frac{1}{n}\right)
is convex.
By Mazur’s Lemma (e.g.
[
7
, Corollary 3.8, Exercise 3.4]
), there exists a sequence
(\Psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\Psi_{n}\in\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}{\left(\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}\{\psi_{k}\}\right)}
that converges to
\psi
strongly in
L^{2}(G)
. Take
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
(\mathcal{A}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is monotonically decreasing, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\leq\mathcal{A}_{k}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}+\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }k\geq N.

Assume that
n\geq N
. Taking into account (
17
), we obtain for each
k\geq n

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}-\frac{1}{n}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}-\frac{1}{k}\leq\mathcal{A}_{k}-\frac{1}{k}\leq\frac{\int_{G}\psi_{k}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\psi_{k},\sigma\rangle_{2}}\leq\mathcal{A}_{k}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}+\varepsilon.

Therefore,

\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}-\frac{1}{n}\right)\langle\psi_{k},\sigma\rangle_{2}\leq\int_{G}\psi_{k}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}\leq\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}+\varepsilon\right)\langle\psi_{k},\sigma\rangle_{2}\quad\text{for all }k\geq n\geq N.

Each
\Psi_{n}
has the form
\Psi_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{(n)}\psi_{k}
, where
\alpha_{k}^{(n)}\geq 0
,
\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{(n)}=1
, and
\alpha_{k}^{(n)}=0
for all but finitely many
k
. By linearity we have

\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}-\frac{1}{n}\right)\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}\leq\int_{G}\Psi_{n}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}\leq\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}+\varepsilon\right)\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2},

and consequently

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}-\frac{1}{n}\leq\frac{\int_{G}\Psi_{n}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}+\varepsilon.

This implies

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\int_{G}\Psi_{n}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\int_{G}\Psi_{n}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}+\varepsilon.

Since this is true for any
\varepsilon>0
, we conclude that

(18)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\int_{G}\Psi_{n}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}.

Recall that
\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}\geq s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
by (
10
).

By the inclusion (
15
) and the convexity of the classes
\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}\left(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\frac{1}{n}\right)
, we have
\Psi_{n}\in\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}\left(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\frac{1}{n}\right)
. The sequence
(\Psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\psi
strongly and weakly in
L^{2}(G)
. The strong convergence in
L^{2}(G)
implies that
(\Psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
contains a subsequence that converges to
\psi
pointwise almost everywhere with respect to the Haar measure
\lambda_{G}
. For simplicity we assume that
(\Psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
itself is such a sequence.

The almost everywhere pointwise convergence implies that
\|\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. Since
(\Psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\psi
weakly in
L^{2}(G)
, we have that

\int_{G}\psi(\xi\star\widetilde{\xi})\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}\Psi_{n}(\xi\star\widetilde{\xi})\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}\geq 0

for any complex-valued function
\xi\in C(G)
. This means that
\psi
is an integrally positive definite function. Since
G
is compact (and in particular
\sigma
-compact),
\psi
agrees almost everywhere with respect to
\lambda_{G}
with a continuous positive definite function (e.g.
[
40
, Theorem 1.7.3]
). For simplicity we denote this continuous function again by
\psi
. The sequence
(\Psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to the continuous positive definite function
\psi
strongly and weakly in
L^{2}(G)
and pointwise almost everywhere with respect to
\lambda_{G}
.

Now we are going to exploit the fact that the sets
A=\Omega_{+}^{c}
,
B=\Omega_{-}^{c}
satisfy Assumption O. Since
\Psi_{n}\in\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}\left(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\frac{1}{n}\right)
we have
\Psi_{n}|_{A}\leq\frac{1}{n}
. If
x\in A
is a point where
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi_{n}(x)=\psi(x)
, then
\psi(x)\leq 0
follows immediately. Thus,
\psi\leq 0
holds almost everywhere on
A
. We want to show that
\psi|_{A}\leq 0
. Suppose, by contradiction, that there is
x_{0}\in A
with
\psi(x_{0})>0
. Since
\psi
is continuous, there exists an open neighborhood
V
of
x_{0}
such that
\psi(x)>0
for all
x\in V
. By Assumption O, the set
A\cap V\subset A
has a positive measure
\lambda_{G}(A\cap V)>0
and
\psi(x)>0
for any
x\in A\cap V
, which is a contradiction. This proves that
\psi|_{A}\leq 0
. Similarly,
\psi|_{B}\geq 0
.

Since the constant function
\mathbbm{1}_{G}
is in
L^{2}(G)
on the account of
G
being compact, by the weak convergence we have that

(19)

\int_{G}\psi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=\int_{G}\psi\mathbbm{1}_{G}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}\Psi_{n}\mathbbm{1}_{G}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}\Psi_{n}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}.

Consider the
K
-periodisation
\psi^{K}
of
\psi
. By Lemma
2
, we have that
\psi^{K}\in C(G)^{K}
,
\psi^{K}|_{\Omega_{+}^{c}}\leq 0
,
\psi^{K}|_{\Omega_{-}^{c}}\geq 0
, and

\int_{G}\psi^{K}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=\int_{G}\psi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}.

Moreover, since
\sigma
is
K
-bi-invariant, we have

\langle\psi^{K},\sigma\rangle_{2}=\int_{G}\psi\,\mbox{d}\sigma.

Since
\|\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, we have

\|\psi^{K}\|_{\infty}=\psi^{K}(e)=\int_{K}\int_{K}\psi(kek^{\prime})\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{K}(k)\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{K}(k^{\prime})\leq\int_{K}\int_{K}\mathbbm{1}_{G}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{K}(k)\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{K}(k^{\prime})=1.

Note that the function
\psi^{K}
satisfies all requirements in the definition of the class
\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
except for the condition
\psi^{K}(e)=1
. We establish below that
\psi^{K}
is not identically
0
, so in particular
\psi^{K}(e)>0
. The function
\frac{1}{\psi^{K}(e)}\psi^{K}
is then in the class
\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
.

For a positive definite measure
\sigma\in M(G)^{K}
satisfying Wiener’s condition we have by (
10
) that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}\geq s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})>0.

This implies, by the definition of the extremal constant, and by (
16
), (
18
) and (
19
), that the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}
exists and the following estimate holds:

(20)

\frac{\int_{G}\psi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\int_{G}\psi\,\mbox{d}\sigma}=\frac{\int_{G}\psi^{K}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\psi^{K},\sigma\rangle_{2}}\leq\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\int_{G}\Psi_{n}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}}=\frac{\int_{G}\psi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}}.

Whence it follows

(21)

\langle\psi^{K},\sigma\rangle_{2}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}\geq s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})>0.

We conclude, as asserted above, that
\psi^{K}(e)>0
.

Now we invoke the assumption that the measure
\tau
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Haar measure
\lambda_{G}
. In particular, the conditions
|\Psi_{n}|\leq\mathbbm{1}_{G}
and
\Psi_{n}\to\psi
hold also almost everywhere with respect to
\tau
. Moreover,
\mathbbm{1}_{G}
is integrable with respect to
\tau
. Thus, by the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\Psi_{n},\tau\rangle_{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}\Psi_{n}\,\mbox{d}\tau=\int_{G}\psi\,\mbox{d}\tau=\langle\psi^{K},\tau\rangle_{2}.

Now we have

\langle\psi^{K},\sigma\rangle_{2}=c\psi^{K}(e)+\langle\psi^{K},\tau\rangle_{2}

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}=c\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi_{n}(e)+\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\Psi_{n},\tau\rangle_{2}=c\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi_{n}(e)+\langle\psi^{K},\tau\rangle_{2}.

It follows by (
21
) that

\psi^{K}(e)\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi_{n}(e)=1.

But we know that
\|\psi^{K}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, and the continuity of
\psi^{K}
implies
\psi^{K}(e)=1
. That is,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\Psi_{n},\delta_{e}\rangle_{2}=\langle\psi^{K},\delta_{e}\rangle_{2}
.
Altogether, this gives

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\Psi_{n},\sigma\rangle_{2}=\langle\psi^{K},\sigma\rangle_{2}.

Hence, (
20
) takes the form

\frac{\int_{G}\psi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\int_{G}\psi\,\mbox{d}\sigma}=\frac{\int_{G}\psi^{K}\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\psi^{K},\sigma\rangle_{2}}\leq\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}=\frac{\int_{G}\psi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\int_{G}\psi\,\mbox{d}\sigma}.

It follows that all quantities above are equal, i.e.

\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon),

which proves the lemma.
∎

Recall that the Delsarte-type problem (
2
) asks for the maximum of the quotients
\frac{\int_{G}\varphi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\varphi(e)}
. As mentioned above, this corresponds to the measure
\sigma=\delta_{e}
.

The above lemma gives following strong duality statement.

Theorem 16
.

Let
(G,K)
be a compact Gelfand pair. Let
\Omega_{+}
,
\Omega_{-}
be
K
-bi-invariant symmetric Borel subsets of
G
with
e\in\mbox{\rm int\,}{\Omega_{+}}
. Assume that the sets
\Omega_{+}^{c}
,
\Omega_{-}^{c}
satisfy Assumption O.
Assume that
\sigma\in M(G)^{K}
is a positive definite measure satisfying Wiener’s condition, and such that

\sigma=c\delta_{e}+\tau
, where
\tau\in M(G)^{K}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Haar measure and
c\geq 0
.
Then

u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=v_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}).

Proof.

To prove the theorem, we must show (
13
).

The problem
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)
is equivalent to the problem

\displaystyle D_{G}^{K,\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\frac{\int_{G}\varphi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\varphi,\sigma\rangle_{2}}:\varphi\in C(G)^{K},\ \varphi\gg 0,\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 39.83385pt\int_{G}\varphi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=1,\ \varphi|_{\Omega_{+}^{c}}\leq\varepsilon,\ \varphi|_{\Omega_{-}^{c}}\geq-\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})+\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)s(\gamma)}:\right.f\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\Gamma),\ \varphi|_{A}\leq\varepsilon,\ \varphi|_{B}\geq-\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\hskip 39.83385pt\text{where }\left.\varphi=1+\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)\gamma\right\},

namely,

D_{G}^{K,\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})+u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)}.

Note that admissible functions
\varphi
in the problem
D_{G}^{K,\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)
satisfy
f(\mathbbm{1}_{G})

=\int_{G}\varphi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=1
. Since
\int_{G}\varphi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}\leq\varphi(e)\lambda_{G}(G)=\varphi(e)
, we also have
\varphi(e)\geq 1
. Now take
\psi:=\frac{\varphi}{\varphi(e)}
. The function
\psi
has the form

\psi(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{g}}g(\gamma)\gamma(x),\quad x\in G,

where
g:=\frac{f}{\varphi(e)}\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\Gamma)
. We have
\psi(e)=1
,
\psi|_{A}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\varphi(e)}\leq\varepsilon
,
\psi|_{B}\geq-\frac{\varepsilon}{\varphi(e)}\geq-\varepsilon
. Moreover,

\frac{1}{s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})+\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\setminus\{\mathbbm{1}_{G}\}}f(\gamma)s(\gamma)}=\frac{g(\mathbbm{1}_{G})}{\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{supp}{g}}g(\gamma)s(\gamma)}=\frac{\int_{G}\psi\,\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\psi,\sigma\rangle_{2}}.

We see that each such function
\psi
is in the class
\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)
. Hence,

D_{G}^{K,\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)\leq\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon).

Since
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)
is an extension of the minimization problem
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
, we have that
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\geq u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})+u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};)}\leq\frac{1}{s(\mathbbm{1}_{G})+u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=D_{G}^{K,\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)\leq\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon).

By Lemma
15
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-};\varepsilon)=\mathcal{A}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
,
which in turn implies (
13
). We conclude the problem
u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
is well-posed. This implies that the strong duality relation

u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=v_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})

holds true.

∎

Now we are going to deduce from Theorem
16
a generalisation of Theorem
1
from Section
1
. We denote by
T_{0}(G)^{K}
the subset of
M(G)^{K}
consisting of all
\tau\in M(G)^{K}
such that
\tau
is positive definite and
\tau(G)=\widehat{\tau}(\mathbbm{1}_{G})=0
.

Lemma 17
.

For the extremal value
v_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
introduced in (
5
) we have

(22)

v_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})+\sigma(G)=\sup\{z\in\mathbb{R}:\exists\ \mu\in Q^{*}\text{ and }\tau\in T_{0}(G)^{K}\text{ with }\sigma-z\lambda_{G}=\mu+\tau\}.

Moreover, the value on the right-hand side of the above equation (
22
) is positive.

Proof.

Recall that (see (
5
))

\displaystyle v_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})

\displaystyle=-\inf\left\{\mu(G):\mu\in Q^{*},\ \int_{G}\gamma\,\mbox{d}\mu\leq s(\gamma)\text{ for all }\gamma\neq\mathbbm{1}_{G}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{-\mu(G):\mu\in Q^{*},\ \widehat{\mu}(\gamma)\leq\widehat{\sigma}(\gamma)\text{ for all }\gamma\neq\mathbbm{1}_{G}\right\}.

We denote the right-hand side of (
22
) by
Z
.

Suppose that
z\in\mathbb{R}
is such that

\sigma=z\lambda_{G}+\mu+\tau

with some
\mu\in Q^{*}
,
\tau\in T_{0}(G)^{K}
. Taking Fourier transforms at
\gamma\neq\mathbbm{1}_{G}
gives

\widehat{\sigma}(\gamma)=\widehat{\mu}(\gamma)+\widehat{\tau}(\gamma)\geq\widehat{\mu}(\gamma).

Thus,
\mu
is feasible in (
5
).
Evaluating at
\mathbbm{1}_{G}
yields

\widehat{\sigma}(\mathbbm{1}_{G})=\sigma(G)=z+\widehat{\mu}(\mathbbm{1}_{G})=z+\mu(G),

so

z=\sigma(G)-\mu(G)\leq\sigma(G)+v_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}).

It follows that

Z\leq\sigma(G)+v_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}).

Conversely, if
\mu\in Q^{*}
satisfies
\widehat{\mu}(\gamma)\leq\widehat{\sigma}(\gamma)
for all
\gamma\neq\mathbbm{1}_{G}
,
then we take a positive definite
\tau
with
\widehat{\tau}(\mathbbm{1}_{G})=0
and
\widehat{\tau}(\gamma)=\widehat{\sigma}(\gamma)-\widehat{\mu}(\gamma)
for
\gamma\neq\mathbbm{1}_{G}
. Defining
z=\sigma(G)-\mu(G)
gives

\sigma-z\lambda_{G}=\mu+\tau.

Thus, this
z
is feasible in the problem
Z
. This gives

\sigma(G)-\mu(G)=z\leq Z,

and consequently

\sigma(G)+v_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\leq Z.

This proves (
22
).

Finally, if
\sigma
is a positive definite measure satisfying Wiener’s condition, then we have a representation

\sigma=\sigma(G)\lambda_{G}+0+\tau,

where
\mu\equiv 0\in Q^{*}
satisfies
\widehat{\mu}(\gamma)=0\leq\widehat{\sigma}(\gamma)
, and
\tau
is a positive definite measure with
\widehat{\tau}(\mathbbm{1}_{G})=0
and
\widehat{\tau}(\gamma)=\widehat{\sigma}(\gamma)
for
\gamma\neq\mathbbm{1}_{G}
. Therefore,
z=\sigma(G)=\widehat{\sigma}(\mathbbm{1}_{G})>0
is feasible in
Z
, and it follows that
Z\geq\sigma(G)>0
.
∎

Corollary 18
.

Let
(G,K)
be a compact Gelfand pair, and let
\Omega_{+}
,
\Omega_{-}
be
K
-bi-invariant symmetric Borel subsets of
G
with
e\in\mbox{\rm int\,}{\Omega_{+}}
, and
\Omega_{+}^{c}
,
\Omega_{-}^{c}
satisfying Assumption O. Then the linear programming problem

{\mathcal{A}}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=\sup\left\{\frac{\int_{G}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_{G}}{\langle\varphi,\sigma\rangle_{2}}:\varphi\in\mathcal{F}^{K}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\right\}

has dual problem

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})^{*}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}:\exists\ \mu\in Q^{*}\text{ and }\tau\in T_{0}(G)^{K}\text{ with }\sigma-\alpha\lambda_{G}=\mu+\tau\},

and we have the strong duality relation

{\mathcal{A}}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})={\mathcal{A}}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})^{*}.

Proof.

Let
Z
again denote the right-hand side of (
22
). By the above lemma,

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\sup\{z>0:\exists\ \mu\in Q^{*}\text{ and }\tau\in T_{0}(G)^{K}\text{ with }\sigma-z\lambda_{G}=\mu+\tau\}.

We have
\sigma-z\lambda_{G}=\mu+\tau
as above if and only if
\frac{1}{z}\sigma-\lambda_{G}=\frac{1}{z}\mu+\frac{1}{z}\tau
. Noting that the properties of
\mu
and
\tau
do not change by multiplication with a positive constant and taking
\alpha=\frac{1}{z}
, we see that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})^{*}

\displaystyle=\inf\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}:\exists\ \mu\in Q^{*}\text{ and }\tau\in T_{0}(G)^{K}\text{ with }\sigma-\alpha\lambda_{G}=\mu+\tau\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z}.

Now by (
11
), Theorem
16
and the above,

{\mathcal{A}}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=\frac{1}{\sigma(G)+u_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})}=\frac{1}{\sigma(G)+v_{\Gamma}^{\sigma}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})}=\frac{1}{Z}={\mathcal{A}}^{K,\sigma}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})^{*}.

∎
