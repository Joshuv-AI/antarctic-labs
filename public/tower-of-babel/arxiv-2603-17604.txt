Decidability of singularities in the Ekedahl–Oort stratification
Jean-Stefan Koskivirta, Lorenzo La Porta

Abstract
. For an abelian type Shimura variety and an odd prime p p of good reduction, we characterize the regularity in codimension one of Zariski closures of Ekedahl–Oort strata in terms of the Frobenius action on the root datum. We give an algorithm that detects codimension one singularities for arbitrary Ekedahl–Oort strata. When the Shimura datum is of split type, we relate the singularities of Ekedahl–Oort strata to a stack of G G -zips over the complex numbers. We study the existence of generalized Hasse invariants on this stack.



1.
Introduction

The Ekedahl–Oort (EO) stratification on the special fiber of a Shimura variety was first introduced by Ekedahl and Oort
[
16
]
, in the case of Siegel modular varieties. This was subsequently generalized by the work of many
[
21
,
22
,
19
,
6
]
. It has proved a very effective tool to study the geometry of such moduli spaces. For example, the theory of generalized Hasse invariants on EO strata in
[
3
,
1
]
made it possible to extend the construction of Galois representations attached to more general automorphic representations. The singularities appearing in the EO strata encode interesting arithmetic information. For this reason, it is an important problem to detect and measure such singularities. In this paper, building on the results of
[
9
]
, we address these questions.

Let us explain in further detail. Fix
p
an odd prime. Write
S
for the
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-fiber at some
p
-hyperspecial level
K
of an abelian type Shimura variety. Denote the EO strata in
S
by
(S_{w})_{w\in{}^{I}W}
, where
{}^{I}W
is a certain subset of the Weyl group of the relevant reductive group
G
, §
2.1.8
. Here,
I
denotes the type of the Hodge parabolic
P\subseteq G
attached to the Shimura datum. The smooth and normal loci of the Zariski closure
\overline{S}_{w}
can be written as unions of EO strata. It is a difficult problem to determine which EO strata appear therein. In the recent preprint
[
9
]
, the authors together with S. Reppen gave a criterion for the normality of a locally closed subcheme
U\subseteq S
which is a union of EO strata. Such a subcheme is called an
elementary
w
-open
if
U=S_{w}\cup S_{w^{\prime}}
, where
S_{w^{\prime}}
has codimension one in
\overline{S}_{w}
. This condition is equivalent to
w^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{I}(w)
, the set of lower neighbors of
w
in
{}^{I}W
with respect to a certain partial order
\preccurlyeq_{I}
, see §
2.1.10
.

For elementary
w
-open subschemes
U
, the authors gave a list of explicit conditions that are equivalent to the smoothness of
U
(itself equivalent to normality, in this setting). We recall the result below. To explain it, we need to introduce two notions. First,
U
is
w
-bounded
if
P_{w^{\prime}}\subseteq P_{w}
, where
P_{w}
,
P_{w^{\prime}}
denote the
canonical parabolic subgroups
of
w,w^{\prime}
respectively, see
[
9
, Def. 2.11]
. Second, for any intermediate parabolic subgroup
B\subseteq P_{0}\subseteq P
(where
B
is a fixed Borel subgroup), we consider the flag space
S^{(P_{0})}
, which parametrizes points of
S
endowed with a
P_{0}
-subtorsor of the
P
-torsor given by the Hodge filtration. Write
\pi_{P_{0}}\colon S^{(P_{0})}\to S
for the natural projection. There is a stratification
(S^{(P_{0})}_{w})_{w\in{}^{I_{0}}W}
, where
I_{0}\subseteq I
is the type of
P_{0}
, §
2.2.5
. We say that
U

admits a separating canonical cover
if the preimage of
U
under the map

(1.0.1)

\pi_{P_{w}}\colon\overline{S}^{(P_{w})}_{w}\to\overline{S}_{w}

coincides with
U^{(P_{w})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}S^{(P_{w})}_{w}\cup S^{(P_{w})}_{w^{\prime}}
.

Theorem 1

(
[
9
, Corollary 4.17]
)
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

The scheme
U
is smooth.

(2)

The scheme
U
is normal.

(3)

The scheme
U
is
w
-bounded and admits a separating canonical cover.

In this paper, we build upon Theorem
Theorem 1
and uncover several new aspects of the EO stratification. Our main results are twofold:

(1)

We give an algorithm which determines whether an elementary
w
-open subset is smooth (equivalently, normal) in terms of the root datum of
G
.

(2)

When the group
G
splits over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we show that the presence of singularities in the EO stratification is controlled by an object in characteristic zero.

1.1.
Algorithm for smoothness

Write
{\mathcal{X}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathop{\text{$G$-{\tt Zip}}}\nolimits^{\mu}
for the stack of
G
-zips associated to the Shimura datum, §
2.1
. It is defined as a quotient stack
[E\backslash G_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}]
, where
E
is the attached zip group, §
2.1.3
. Recall that there is a smooth surjective map

(1.1.1)

\zeta\colon S\to{\mathcal{X}}

constructed by Viehmann–Wedhorn, Zhang and Imai–Kato–Youcis
[
21
,
22
,
6
]
. The EO stratification is the pullback of the
zip stratification

({\mathcal{X}}_{w})_{w\in{}^{I}W}
of
{\mathcal{X}}
, which is itself given by the
E
-orbits
(G_{w})_{w\in{}^{I}W}
inside
G_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
.

Similarly to
\pi_{P_{0}}\colon S^{(P_{0})}\to S
, we have a flag space
\pi_{P_{0}}\colon{\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{0})}\to{\mathcal{X}}
(for any intermediate parabolic
P_{0}
), carrying a stratification
({\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{0})}_{w})_{w\in{}^{I_{0}}W}
. For
w\in W
, consider the stratum
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w}
in the total flag space
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}
. Its image
\pi_{B}({\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w})
is a union of certain zip strata of
{\mathcal{X}}
. There exists a unique zip stratum
{\mathcal{X}}_{v}
which is open dense in
\pi_{B}({\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w})
. This defines a map
w\mapsto v
that we denote by

(1.1.2)

\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\colon W\to{}^{I}W.

The subscript
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
is used to distinguish this map from an analogous map
\pi_{\mathbb{C}}\colon W\to{}^{I}W
which we define later. The map
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
is a section of the natural inclusion
{}^{I}W\subseteq W
. The computation of the map
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
is in general highly involved. We do not know an algorithm to determine it.

Set
z\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sigma(w_{0,I})w_{0}
, where
w_{0,I}\in W_{I}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}W(L,T)
and
w_{0}\in W
are the longest elements in
W_{I}
and
W
, respectively, and
\sigma
is the action of the
p
-power Frobenius on
W
. Write
\Xi\colon G(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\to{}^{I}W
for the map which takes an element
g\in G(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
to the unique element
w\in{}^{I}W
such that
gz^{-1}\in G_{w}
. There is an explicit algorithm that computes the restriction of the map
\Xi
to the subset
W\subseteq G(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
(this inclusion is defined by choosing representatives). In the case
G=\GL_{n,\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, we also give an algorithm to determine
\Xi
entirely, §
4.2
. For general strata, one needs to know the image by
\Xi
of infinitely many points in order to compute
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
. This is an obstacle to determining
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
algorithmically.

We show, however, that the image by
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
of certain special elements of
W
can be computed by an algorithm. We say that
w\in W
is
small
if
\ell(\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(w))=\ell(w)
, where
\ell\colon W\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is the length function. Write
W^{\rm sm}\subseteq W
for the set of small elements. There is an explicit, combinatorial characterization of smallness in terms of roots, which is computable, §
3.6
. One has the following key result:

Proposition 1

(Proposition
3.5.4
)
.

Let
w\in W^{\rm sm}
.

(1)

The underlying topological space of
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w}
is a single point.

(2)

One has
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(w)=\Xi(w)
.

In particular, the restriction of
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
to
W^{\rm sm}
can be computed algorithmically.

We now explain how to obtain an algorithm to determine the smoothness of elementary
w
-open subsets.
Let
w\in{}^{I}W
and consider
w^{\prime}
a lower neighbor of
w
in
{}^{I}W
. We have the corresponding elementary
w
-open subsets
U(w,w^{\prime})
and
{\mathcal{U}}(w,w^{\prime})
inside
S
and
{\mathcal{X}}
, respectively.
Write
\Gamma_{I_{w}}(w)
for the set of lower neighbors of
w
in the set
{}^{I_{w}}W
(with respect to
\preccurlyeq_{I_{w}}
), where
I_{w}\subseteq I
is the type of the canonical parabolic
P_{w}
. Set
\Gamma_{I_{w}}^{\rm sm}(w)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gamma_{I_{w}}(w)\cap W^{\rm sm}
.
We show that the three equivalent conditions of Theorem
Theorem 1
are also equivalent to the following:

(4)

One has
P_{w^{\prime}}\subseteq P_{w}
and
w^{\prime}\notin\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})
.

Since the map
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
can be algorithmically computed on small elements by Proposition 1, we obtain our first main result:

Theorem A

(Theorem
4.1.4
)
.

There exists an algorithm which determines whether
U(w,w^{\prime})
(equivalently,
{\mathcal{U}}(w,w^{\prime})
) is smooth (equivalently, normal).

In work in progress
[
10
]
of the authors together with S. Reppen, we are able to completely determine the singularities appearing in the Ekedahl–Oort stratification for unitary Shimura varieties of signature
(n-2,2)
at a split prime, using this algorithm.

1.2.
Independence from
p

A consequence of the proof of Proposition 1 and Theorem A above is that the smoothness of an elementary open
U
is entirely determined by the action of the Frobenius on the based root datum
\Phi
of
G
(with respect to an adequate choice of a Borel pair, see §
2.1.3
and §
6.6.4
). Consider now two odd primes
p,\ell
of good reduction. Write
\sigma_{p},\sigma_{\ell}
for the automorphisms of
\Phi
induced by the Frobenius at
p
and
\ell
, respectively. We deduce the following.

Theorem B

(Corollary
6.6.2
)
.

Assume that
\sigma_{p}=\sigma_{\ell}
. For
w\in{\vphantom{W}}^{I}{W}
and
w^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{I}(w)
, denote by
U_{p}
and
U_{\ell}
the corresponding
w
-open subsets in the mod
p
and mod
\ell
special fibers, respectively.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

U_{p}
is smooth (equivalently, normal).

(2)

U_{\ell}
is smooth (equivalently, normal).

1.3.
Length
2
strata

We illustrate our results with a concrete application to 2-dimensional strata in unitary Shimura varieties of signature
(r,s)
at a split prime of good reduction. Let
S
denote the special fiber of this Shimura variety. We assume that
r\geq s\geq 2
(in the case
s=1
, the Zariski closure of each EO stratum is smooth by
[
13
]
). In this case, there are exactly two elements of length
2
in
{}^{I}W
, that we denote by
w_{1}
,
w_{2}
(see §
4.5
for the exact parametrization), and a single length
1
element, denoted
w^{\prime}
. Set
U_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}S_{w_{i}}\cup S_{w^{\prime}}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. For
x
coprime to
n
, let
\inv_{n}(x)
denote the unique element
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
such that
kx\equiv 1\pmod{n}
.

Theorem C

(Theorem
4.5.6
)
.

(1)

If
\gcd(r,s)>3
, then
U_{i}
is not
w_{i}
-bounded (for
i=1,2
). In particular,
U_{i}
is not smooth.

(2)

If
\gcd(r,s)\in\{2,3\}
, then
U_{i}
is smooth for
i=1,2
.

(3)

If
\gcd(r,s)=1
and we set
m=\inv_{n}(s)
, one has

U_{1}\ \textrm{smooth}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ m>\frac{n}{2},\quad U_{2}\ \textrm{smooth}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ m<\frac{n}{2}.

1.4.
The case of a split prime

The second main part of this paper shows that for primes of good reduction at which
G
splits, the presence of singularities in the EO stratification is controlled by a characteristic zero object. Assume from now on that
G
admits a split Borel pair
(B,T)
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
B\subseteq P
. In particular,
P
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
Q
denote the opposite parabolic of
P
with respect to
T
and let
L=P\cap Q
be the common Levi subgroup. Recall that
{\mathcal{X}}
parametrizes tuples
({\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{I}}_{P},{\mathcal{I}}_{Q},\iota)
where
{\mathcal{I}}
is a
G
-torsor,
{\mathcal{I}}_{P}\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}
is a
P
-torsor,
{\mathcal{I}}_{Q}\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}
is a
Q
-torsor, and
\iota\colon({\mathcal{I}}_{P}/R_{\mathrm{u}}(P))^{(p)}\to{\mathcal{I}}_{Q}/R_{\mathrm{u}}(Q)
is an isomorphism of
L
-torsors. For any integer
m\geq 0
, let
{\mathcal{X}}_{m}
be the stack parametrizing such tuples, but with
\iota
now an isomorphism of
L
-torsors
({\mathcal{I}}_{P}/R_{\mathrm{u}}(P))^{(p^{m})}\to{\mathcal{I}}_{Q}/R_{\mathrm{u}}(Q)
(in particular,
{\mathcal{X}}={\mathcal{X}}_{1}
). Each
{\mathcal{X}}_{m}
admits a zip stratification
({\mathcal{X}}_{m,w})_{w\in{}^{I}W}
. There is an isomorphism
{\mathcal{X}}_{m}\simeq[E_{m}\backslash G_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}]
where
E_{m}\subseteq P\times Q
is the corresponding zip group, §
5.1.2
. Via this isomorphism, we can write
{\mathcal{X}}_{m,w}=[E_{m}\backslash G_{m,w}]
for a locally closed subscheme
G_{m,w}\subseteq G
. Using Galois cohomology, we show that
G_{m,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})=G_{0,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})
for all
m\geq 0
(Proposition
5.3.1
). In particular, the set
G_{m,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is independent of
m\geq 0
. This has the following consequence. Write
{\mathcal{U}}_{m}(w,w^{\prime})
for the elementary
w
-open subset
{\mathcal{X}}_{m,w}\cup{\mathcal{X}}_{m,w^{\prime}}
, where
w^{\prime}
is a lower neighbor in
{}^{I}W
of
w
. The case
m=0
is of particular interest.

Proposition 2

(Proposition
5.6.2
)
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer. The following are equivalent:

(1)

{\mathcal{U}}_{0}(w,w^{\prime})
is
w
-bounded and admits a separating canonical cover.

(2)

{\mathcal{U}}_{m}(w,w^{\prime})
is smooth (equivalently, normal).

Now, assume that
G
admits a reductive model
{\mathcal{G}}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
which is split over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(we say that
p
is a prime of
split good reduction
). Since Frobenius is not involved in the construction of
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
, this stack admits a natural model
{\mathscr{X}}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, defined similarly. Again, it carries a stratification
({\mathscr{X}}_{w})_{w\in{}^{I}W}
. Choosing an isomorphism
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\simeq\mathbb{C}
, we can also consider the stack
{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathbb{C}}
over
\mathbb{C}
. The notions of “
w
-boundedness” and “separating canonical cover”, as well as the formation of the flag space, make sense on
{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathbb{C}}
. We also have the analogue of
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
, that we denote by
\pi_{\mathbb{C}}\colon W\to{}^{I}W
.

Proposition 3

(Proposition
6.4.1
)
.

For
w\in W^{\rm sm}
, we have
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(w)=\pi_{\mathbb{C}}(w)
.

We conjecture the stronger statement
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}=\pi_{\mathbb{C}}
but we are unable to prove it at the moment. Write
{\mathscr{U}}_{\mathbb{C}}(w,w^{\prime})
for the corresponding elementary substack of
{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, i.e. the union of
{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathbb{C},w}
and
{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathbb{C},w^{\prime}}
. As a consequence, we obtain:

Theorem D

(Theorem
6.5.2
)
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

U(w,w^{\prime})
is smooth (equivalently, normal).

(2)

{\mathcal{U}}(w,w^{\prime})
is smooth (equivalently, normal).

(3)

{\mathscr{U}}_{\mathbb{C}}(w,w^{\prime})
is
w
-bounded and admits a separating canonical cover.

(4)

We have
P_{w^{\prime}}\subseteq P_{w}
and
w^{\prime}\notin\pi_{\mathbb{C}}(\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})

The equivalence of (
1
) and (

{\mathcal{U}}
and
{\mathscr{U}}_{\mathbb{C}}

1.5.
Hasse invariants in characteristic zero

It was proved in
[
3
]
that generalized Hasse invariants exist on all zip strata of
{\mathcal{X}}
, and hence on all EO strata by pullback via (
1.1.1
). We explore their existence on the stack
{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

By condition (
4
) of Theorem D, the singularities of elementary substacks are encoded by the map
\pi_{\mathbb{C}}\colon W\to{}^{I}W
. Despite our algorithm to determine
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
(equivalently,
\pi_{\mathbb{C}}
) on small elements, the computation can become quite involved, as shown in the various cases appearing in Theorem C. Moreover, we do not know how to determine
\pi_{\mathbb{C}}
on non-small elements. One motivation for studying Hasse invariants on
{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathbb{C}}
is that they make it possible to determine
\pi_{\mathbb{C}}
fully and explicitly, when they exist.

For
\lambda\in X^{*}(L)
, we have a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda)
on each of the stacks
{\mathcal{X}}
,
{\mathscr{X}}
,
{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathbb{C}}
. We say that a section
\mathsf{Ha}_{w}\in H^{0}(\overline{{\mathscr{X}}}_{\mathbb{C},w},{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda))
is a generalized Hasse invariant if its nonvanishing locus coincides with
{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathbb{C},w}
. Let
E_{w}
be the set of positive roots
\alpha
such that
ws_{\alpha}\leq w
and
\ell(ws_{\alpha})=\ell(w)-1
(where
s_{\alpha}
is the reflection with respect to
\alpha
).

Theorem E

(Theorem
7.5.3
)
.

Let
w\in{}^{I}W
and
\lambda\in X^{*}(L)
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a generalized Hasse invariant
\mathsf{Ha}_{w}\in H^{0}(\overline{{\mathscr{X}}}_{\mathbb{C},w},{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda))
.

(2)

There exists
\lambda_{0}\in X^{*}(T)
such that
\lambda=w\lambda_{0}-z\lambda_{0}
and
\langle\lambda_{0},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle<0
for all
\alpha\in E_{w}
.

In stark contrast with the characteristic
p
situation (and automorphic forms on Shimura varieties), the trivial line bundle (i.e.
\lambda=0
) sometimes admits such generalized Hasse invariants. For example, in the case
G=\GL_{n}
, with
L
of type
(n-1,1)
, generalized Hasse invariants of weight
\lambda=0
exist on each stratum (see §
7.6
). This case corresponds to a unitary Shimura variety of signature
(n-1,1)
at a prime
p
of good reduction which splits in the associated quadratic imaginary extension
\mathbf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
. For other signatures, Hasse invariants do not always exist on all strata, see §
7.6.10
.

2.1.
Generalities on stacks of

For our purposes, we need to work in a setting that allows fields of arbitrary characteristic. We review some basic facts about the stack of
G
-zips from
[
17

18
]
in the case of a general isogeny
\varphi
. We will later specialize to the cases when
\varphi
is a power of the Frobenius homomorphism, or the identity map. Let
K
denote an algebraically closed field.

2.1.1.
Zip data

Fix a connected, reductive group
G
over
K
and cocharacter
\mu\colon\mathbb{G}_{\textrm{m},K}\to G
. Fix also an isogeny
\varphi\colon G\to G
. To the triple
(G,\mu,\varphi)
, we attach a zip datum
{\mathcal{Z}}
as follows. First, write
P_{\pm}\subset G
for the pair of opposite parabolic subgroups afforded by
\mu
(see
[
3
, §1.2.1]
), with common Levi subgroup
L=\Cent(\mu)
. Put
P\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}P_{-}
,

, and
M\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\varphi(L)
. Set
{\mathcal{Z}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(G,P,Q,L,M,\varphi)
. We call

the zip datum attached to the triple
(G,\mu,\varphi)
.

2.1.2.
Frobenius-type zip data

An important example considered in this article is a zip datum attached to a Frobenius isogeny. In this context, we assume that
G_{0}
is a reductive group over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and that
K=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
is an algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. We let
\varphi_{0}\colon G_{0}\to G_{0}
be a positive power of the Frobenius homomorphism of

2.3.
Images of flag strata

If

is of Frobenius-type, the image of a stratum

is a union of zip strata of

, the image of a flag stratum is not necessarily a union of zip strata, as seen in the following example.

Example 2.3.1
.

Consider the group
G=\GL_{2,K}
, endowed with the trivial cocharacter
\mu
and the identity isogeny
\varphi=\iden
. Then,
{\mathcal{X}}_{\mathcal{Z}}
is the quotient of
G
by itself acting by conjugation. Let
B\subseteq G
denote the lower triangular Borel subgroup and
T\subseteq B
the diagonal torus. Identify
W
with the group
\left\{1,w\right\}
where
w=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
. Since

, we have
{}^{I}W=\{1\}
. Thus, there is a unique zip stratum, which is
{\mathcal{X}}
itself. The image of
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(B)}
by the projection

is the quotient stack
[E_{\mathcal{Z}}\backslash H]
where

is the union of conjugacy classes intersecting
Bw
. One checks easily that

H=G\setminus\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}\lambda&0\\
0&\lambda\end{matrix}\right)\ \mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\ \lambda\in K^{*}\right\}.

In particular,
H
is strictly contained in

.

However, for general zip data, when

is minimal or cominimal, one has

\pi_{P_{0}}({\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{0})}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w})={\mathcal{X}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}.

Note that this formula makes sense since

. For Frobenius-type zip data, we have a more precise result:

Proposition 2.3.2

(
[
3
, Prop. 2.4.3, Rmk. 2.4.4]
)

Assume that
{\mathcal{Z}}
is of Frobenius-type. Let

.

(1)

The underlying topological subspace of
{\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{0})}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}
is a point.

(2)

under the map

(3)

\pi_{\mathcal{Z}}

Proposition 2.5.1

We review some of the results of

Let

Let

-open substacks

We collect the statements of

.

Assume that

3.2.
Pointwise and quasi-pointwise strata

In this section, we assume that
{\mathcal{Z}}
is of Frobenius-type unless mentioned otherwise.

Definition 3.2.1
.

(1)

We say that a flag stratum
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(P_{0})}
is
pointwise
if its underlying topological space is reduced to a single
K
-point.

(2)

We say that a flag stratum
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(P_{0})}
is
quasi-pointwise
if its underlying topological space admits a dense
K
-point.

By Proposition
2.3.2
(
1
), minimal and cominimal strata are pointwise. Note that the dense point in a quasi-pointwise stratum is necessarily unique, by the irreducibility of
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(P_{0})}
. Equivalently,
{\mathcal{F}}_{w}^{(P_{0})}
is quasi-pointwise if the set
G_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{P_{0}},w}=E_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{P_{0}}}\cdot(wz^{-1})
admits an open dense
E^{\prime}_{P_{0}}
-orbit. We show that the property of being pointwise, resp. quasi-pointwise, is not affected if we replace
P_{0}
by a smaller parabolic
P_{1}
with
B\subseteq P_{1}\subseteq P_{0}
. First, we record a lemma about algebraic group actions.

Lemma 3.2.2
.

Let
H
be an algebraic group over
K
, and
X,Y
two irreducible
K
-varieties equipped with an action of
H
. Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a
H
-equivariant and generically quasi-finite morphism. Assume that
H
acts transitively on
Y
. Then,
H
acts transitively on
X
.

Proof.

If
C\subseteq X
is any
H
-orbit, the restriction of
f
to
f\colon C\to Y
is surjective, by
H
-equivariance and the fact that
H
acts transitively on
Y
. Hence,
\dim(C)\geq\dim(Y)
. Moreover, we have
\dim(X)=\dim(Y)
since
f
is generically quasi-finite. It follows that
\dim(C)=\dim(X)
for any
H
-orbit
C\subseteq X
. Since
X
is irreducible, the action of
H
on
X
is transitive.
∎

Lemma 3.2.3
.

Let
w\in{}^{I_{0}}W
be an element and
B\subseteq P_{0}\subseteq P
an intermediate parabolic subgroup.

(1)

{\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{0})}_{w}
is pointwise if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w}
is pointwise.

(2)

{\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{0})}_{w}
is quasi-pointwise if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w}
is quasi-pointwise.

Proof.

The map
\pi_{B,P_{0}}\colon{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}\to{\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{0})}
satisfies
\pi_{B,P_{0}}({\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w})={\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{0})}_{w}
, see (
2.3.2
). Thus, if
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w}
is pointwise (resp. quasi-pointwise), then so is
{\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{0})}_{w}
. Assume now that
{\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{0})}_{w}
is pointwise. The map
\pi_{B,P_{0}}\colon{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w}\to{\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{0})}_{w}
is finite étale (by the relative version of Proposition
2.3.2
(
3
), explained below the proposition).
Hence, the same is true for the map of schemes
\pi_{B,P_{0}}\colon H^{(B)}_{w}\to H^{(P_{0})}_{w}
. By Lemma
3.2.2
,
E_{\mathcal{Z}}
acts transitively on
H^{(B)}_{w}
, hence
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w}
is pointwise. Finally, assume that
{\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{0})}_{w}
is quasi-pointwise. Let
V\subseteq H^{(P_{0})}_{w}
be the unique dense
E_{\mathcal{Z}}
-orbit and let
V^{\prime}\subseteq H^{(B)}_{w}
denote the preimage of
V
by
\pi_{B,P_{0}}
. Applying Lemma
3.2.2
to
\pi_{B,P_{0}}\colon V^{\prime}\to V
, we conclude that
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w}
is quasi-pointwise.
∎

Lemma
3.2.3
lets us use the terminology "pointwise", "quasi-pointwise" unambiguously to refer to elements of
W
. Assume now that the torus
T
splits over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, some finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. This assumption implies that all zip strata
{\mathcal{X}}_{w}
and all flag strata
{\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{0})}_{w}
are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Lemma 3.2.4
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

The stratum
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(P_{0})}
is quasi-pointwise.

(2)

There exists a point
a\in G_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{P_{0}},w}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that the
E^{\prime}_{{\mathcal{Z}},P_{0}}
-orbit of
a
is dense in
G_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{P_{0}},w}
.

Proof.

The second condition clearly implies the first one. Conversely, assume that
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(P_{0})}
is quasi-pointwise. Denote by
x\in{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(P_{0})}
the unique dense point. Since the Galois group
\Gal(K/\mathbb{F}_{q})
acts on
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(P_{0})}
, we must have
\sigma(x)=x
by unicity of
x
, where
\sigma
is the
q
-power Frobenius element. This shows that
x
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Now,
x
corresponds to an
E^{\prime}_{{\mathcal{Z}},P_{0}}
-orbit
C\subseteq G_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{P_{0}},w}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Lemma
3.2.5
shows
C(\mathbb{F}_{q})\neq\emptyset
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2.5
.

Let
H
be a connected algebraic group over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
acting transitively on an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-variety
X
. Then,
X(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is nonempty.

Proof.

This follows from the triviality of
H^{1}(\Gal(K/\mathbb{F}_{q}),H(K))
. Let us explain in further detail. Fix a point
a\in X(K)
. By assumption, we can write
\sigma(a)=h_{\sigma}\cdot a
for some
h_{\sigma}\in H(K)
. Since
H^{1}(\Gal(K/\mathbb{F}_{q}),H(K))
is trivial, we can find
g\in H(K)
such that
\sigma(g)^{-1}g=h_{\sigma}
. Thus
\sigma(g)^{-1}g\cdot a=h_{\sigma}\cdot a=\sigma(a)
, hence
\sigma(g\cdot a)=g\cdot a
. This shows that
g\cdot a\in X(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
∎

3.3.
Hasse invariants

Assume that
{\mathcal{Z}}
is of Frobenius-type. Recall that any character
\lambda\in X^{*}(L)
gives rise to a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda)
on
{\mathcal{X}}
, see for instance
[
3
, §3.1]
. For
w\in{}^{I}W
, a
Hasse invariant
for the stratum
{\mathcal{X}}_{w}
is a section
\mathsf{Ha}_{w}\in H^{0}(\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}_{w},{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda))
whose non-vanishing locus coincides with
{\mathcal{X}}_{w}
. When
{\mathcal{Z}}
is of Frobenius-type, such Hasse invariants exist in most cases:

Theorem 3.3.1
.

Assume that
\varphi
is the
p^{m}
-power Frobenius isogeny of
G
, for some
m\geq 1
. Assume that
G
admits a representation with central kernel
\rho\colon G\to\GL_{N}
such that
\rho\circ\mu
has
d
different weights, and that
p^{m}\geq d
. Then Hasse invariants exist for all zip strata of
{\mathcal{X}}
.

Proof.

Although
[
5
, Corollary 6.2.1]
states a slightly weaker result, the statement follows immediately from the proof of
loc. cit.
∎

In particular, Hasse invariants exist for zip data coming from abelian type Shimura varieties. For an arbitrary reductive
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-group
G
, they exist when
p^{m}
is large enough. Under the assumption of the existence of Hasse invariants, we show that the image of a quasi-pointwise stratum is given by a single zip stratum.

Lemma 3.3.2
.

Assume that all zip strata in
{\mathcal{X}}
admit Hasse invariants. Let
w\in W
be a quasi-pointwise element and set
w_{1}=\pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(w)\in{}^{I}W
. Then,
\Pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(w)=\{w_{1}\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{Ha}_{w_{1}}\in H^{0}(\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}_{w_{1}},{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda))
be a Hasse invariant for
w_{1}
. Replacing
\lambda
with
N\lambda
and
\mathsf{Ha}_{w_{1}}
with
\mathsf{Ha}_{w_{1}}^{N}
, for some
N>0
if necessary, we can assume that
H^{0}({\mathcal{X}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{B},w},{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda))
is one-dimensional (
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
). Since
{\mathcal{F}}_{w}^{(B)}
admits a dense
K
-point,
H^{0}({\mathcal{F}}_{w}^{(B)},{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda))
is at most one-dimensional. Thus, since
\pi_{B}^{\ast}(\mathsf{Ha}_{w_{1}})\in H^{0}({\mathcal{F}}_{w}^{(B)},{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda))
is non-zero, we have
\dim_{K}(H^{0}({\mathcal{F}}_{w}^{(B)},{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda)))=1
. On the other hand, there exists a nonzero section
s\in H^{0}({\mathcal{X}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{B},w},{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda))
, hence by dimensionality
\psi_{B}^{*}(s)=\pi_{B}^{\ast}(\mathsf{Ha}_{w_{1}})
up to a nonzero scalar. If there were some
w_{2}\in\Pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(w)\setminus\{w_{1}\}
, then
\pi_{B}^{\ast}(\mathsf{Ha}_{w_{1}})
would vanish on some points of
{\mathcal{F}}_{w}^{(B)}
. On the other hand,
s\in H^{0}({\mathcal{X}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{B},w},{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda))
is nowhere-vanishing on
{\mathcal{X}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{B}}
, hence
\psi_{B}^{*}(s)
is nowhere-vanishing on
{\mathcal{F}}_{w}^{(B)}
. This is a contradiction.
Therefore, we must have
\Pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(w)=\{w_{1}\}
.
∎

3.4.
Small strata

In this section, we define the important notion of small strata. We assume that
{\mathcal{Z}}
is of Frobenius-type unless mentioned otherwise. We first show the following key inequality.

Lemma 3.4.1
.

For any
w\in W
, we have
\ell(\pi_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(w))\leq\ell(w)
.

Proof.

Set
w_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\pi_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(w)
. Then,
\pi_{B}
induces a map
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(B)}\to\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}}
. Since
{\mathcal{Z}}
is of Frobenius-type,
\pi_{B}({\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(B)})
is a union of zip strata. Hence
{\mathcal{X}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}}
is contained in the image of the map
\pi_{B}\colon{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(B)}\to\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}}
. We obtain similarly a map of schemes
\pi_{B}\colon H^{(B)}_{w}\to\overline{G}_{w_{1}}
whose image contains
G_{w_{1}}
. By comparing dimensions, we have
\dim(G_{w_{1}})\leq\dim(H^{(B)}_{w})
, hence
\ell(w_{1})\leq\ell(w)
by (
2.2.9
).
∎

Definition 3.4.2
.

For a general zip datum
{\mathcal{Z}}
, we say that
w\in W
is small if
\ell(\pi_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(w))=\ell(w)
.

We use the terminology
{\mathcal{Z}}
-small
if there is an ambiguity on the choice of the zip datum. We write
W^{\rm sm}\subseteq W
for the subset of small elements. Assume that
{\mathcal{Z}}
is of Frobenius-type and that
w
is small. Set
w_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\pi_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(w)
. For any intermediate parabolic
B\subseteq P_{0}\subseteq P
, we obtain a dominant morphism
\pi_{P_{0}}\colon{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(P_{0})}\to\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}}
between stacks of the same dimension. Hence, this map is generically quasi-finite.

Proposition 3.4.3
.

If
w\in W
is small, the stratum
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}
is quasi-pointwise.

Proof.

Recall that
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}
is identified with
[E_{\mathcal{Z}}\backslash H^{(B)}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}]
.
The first projection
\pr_{1}\colon G\times(P/B)\to G
yields a proper, surjective,
E_{\mathcal{Z}}
-equivariant map
\pr_{1}\colon\overline{H}^{(B)}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}\to\overline{G}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}}
.
Let
x\in H^{(B)}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}
be any point which maps to a point of
G_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}}
and let
S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}E_{\mathcal{Z}}\cdot x
be the orbit of
x
. By
E_{\mathcal{Z}}
-equivariance, the map
\pr_{1}\colon S\to G_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}}
is surjective, hence
\dim(S)\geq\dim(G_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}})=\dim(H^{(B)}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w})
, by our assumption that
w
is small. Hence
S
must be open dense in
H^{(B)}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}
. It corresponds to an open dense point in
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}
. ∎

Choose a finite extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
over which
T
splits. If
w
is small, Lemma
3.2.4
shows the existence of
a\in G_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{P_{0}},w}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that the
E^{\prime}_{{\mathcal{Z}},P_{0}}
-orbit of
a
is dense in
G_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{P_{0}},w}
. Moreover, the image of
a
by
\pi_{P_{0}}
must be contained in the stratum
{\mathcal{X}}_{w_{1}}
. In other words, we have

(3.4.1)

a\in(G_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{P_{0}},w}\cap G_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}})(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

Note that
G_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{B},w}=BwBz^{-1}
. The above discussion also shows the following corollary:

Corollary 3.4.4
.

For
w\in{}^{I_{0}}W
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

(2)

There exists
w_{1}\in{}^{I}W
such that

and
G_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{P_{0}},w}\cap G_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}}\neq\emptyset
.

(3)

There exists
w_{1}\in{}^{I}W
such that
\ell(w)=\ell(w_{1})
and
\left(G_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{P_{0}},w}\cap G_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}}\right)(\mathbb{F}_{q})\neq\emptyset
.

(4)

There exists
w_{1}\in{}^{I}W
such that
\ell(w)=\ell(w_{1})
and
\left(BwBz^{-1}\cap G_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}}\right)(\mathbb{F}_{q})\neq\emptyset
.

We end this section with a lemma on the set of small lower neighbors. Let

be an intermediate parabolic subgroup, of type

. For

, put
\Gamma_{I_{0}}^{\rm sm}(w)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gamma_{I_{0}}(w)\cap W^{\rm sm}

Lemma 3.4.5
.

For any

, one has

. In particular,
\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{0}}(w)\neq\emptyset

Proof.

Consider the proper map

to

, we deduce that for any lower neighbor

∎

3.5.
Exponent raising

When Hasse invariants exist on all zip strata, we can combine Proposition
3.4.3
with Lemma
3.3.2
to conclude that
\Pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(w)=\{w_{1}\}
where
w_{1}=\pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(w)
. In this section, we use what we call the “exponent raising method” to show that the assumption on the existence of Hasse invariants is superfluous.

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}
be a Frobenius-type zip datum. Fix an integer
r\geq 1
such that
T
splits over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
. We then have
\varphi^{r}(L)=L
. Hence, for any integer
m\equiv 1\pmod{r}
, we have
\varphi^{m}(L)=M
. Thus, we can consider the zip datum
{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(G,P,Q,L,M,\varphi^{m})
for each such
m\geq 1
.

Lemma 3.5.1
.

Let
x\in G
and
w\in{}^{I}W
. Assume that
x\in G_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}
. Then there exists
s\geq 1
such that
x\in G_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{m},w}
for all
m\equiv 1\pmod{rs}
.

Proof.

We can write
x=awz^{-1}b^{-1}
for some
(a,b)\in E_{{\mathcal{Z}}}
. Let
s\geq 1
such that
\overline{a}\in L
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}
. We thus have
\varphi^{m}(\overline{a})=\overline{b}
for all
m\equiv 1\pmod{rs}
, hence
(a,b)\in E_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}}
. The result follows.
∎

Proposition 3.5.2
.

Let
w\in W
and
w_{1}\in\Pi_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(w)
. There exists
r_{1}\geq 1
, multiple of
r
, such that
w_{1}\in\Pi_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}}(w)
for all
m\equiv 1\pmod{r_{1}}
.

Proof.

By assumption,
{\mathcal{X}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}}
intersects
\pi_{{\mathcal{Z}},B}({\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w})
. Hence there exists
x\in G_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}}\cap(BwBz^{-1})
. By Lemma
3.5.1
, there exists
r_{1}
, a multiple of
r
, such that
x\in G_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{m},w_{1}}\cap(BwBz^{-1})
for all
m\equiv 1\pmod{r_{1}}
. It follows that
{\mathcal{X}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{m},w_{1}}
intersects the image of
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{m},w}
. The result follows.
∎

Corollary 3.5.3
.

If
w\in W
is
{\mathcal{Z}}
-small, there exists
r_{1}\geq 1
, multiple of
r
, such that
w
is
{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}
-small for all
m\equiv 1\pmod{r_{1}}
.

We obtain the following result, available for all zip data of Frobenius-type
{\mathcal{Z}}
, without any assumption on the existence of Hasse invariants.

Proposition 3.5.4
.

If
w\in W
is
{\mathcal{Z}}
-small, then the following hold:

(1)

\Pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(w)=\{w_{1}\}
for
w_{1}=\pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(w)
.

(2)

{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(B)}
is pointwise.

Proof.

Let
w_{2}\in\Pi_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(w)
. By combining Proposition
3.5.2
and Corollary
3.5.3
, there exists
r_{2}\geq 1
multiple of
r
, such that for all
m\equiv 1\pmod{r_{2}}
, the element
w
is
{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}
-small and one has
w_{1},w_{2}\in\Pi_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}}(w)
. In particular,
w
is quasi-pointwise with respect to
{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}
and
w_{1}=\pi_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}}(w)
. Choosing
m
large enough, we can apply Lemma
3.3.2
to deduce
w_{2}=w_{1}
. For the second assertion, note that (
1
) gives a map
\pi_{{\mathcal{Z}},B}\colon H_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}^{(B)}\to G_{{\mathcal{Z}},w_{1}}
. Since
w
is small, this map is generically quasi-finite. By Lemma
3.2.2
, we deduce that
E_{\mathcal{Z}}
acts transitively on

, hence
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{{\mathcal{Z}},w}
is pointwise.
∎

3.6.
Characterizations of smallness in terms of roots

Assume here that
{\mathcal{Z}}
is of Frobenius-type. Recall that we write
\sigma
for the action of the corresponding Frobenius homomorphism on
W
and
\Phi
. We use the following terminology: elements of
\Phi_{L}
(resp.
\Phi\setminus\Phi_{L}
) are called
compact
(resp.
non-compact
) roots.
Fix
w\in W
. A sequence of roots
S=(\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{n})
is called a
w
-sequence
if

(1)

\beta_{i+1}=w\sigma(\beta_{i})
for all
0\leq i\leq n-1
,

(2)

\beta_{0}
is non-compact positive.

(3)

\beta_{n}
is non-compact.

(4)

\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n-1}
are compact.

For each positive non-compact root
\beta
, there is a unique
w
-sequence such that
\beta_{0}=\beta
, we denote it by
S_{w}(\beta)
. We say that
S_{w}(\beta)
is
positive
(resp.
negative
) if the last root in the sequence
\beta_{n}
is positive (resp. negative). Write
{\mathcal{S}}_{w}^{+}
(resp.
{\mathcal{S}}_{w}^{-}
) for the set of positive (resp. negative)
w
-sequences. Note that
|{\mathcal{S}}_{w}^{+}|+|{\mathcal{S}}_{w}^{-}|
is the number of positive non-compact roots, i.e.
\dim(R_{\textrm{u}}(Q))=\dim(R_{\textrm{u}}(P))
. For any
w_{1}\in W_{I}
and
w\in W
, one has
S_{w_{1}w\sigma(w_{1})^{-1}}(w_{1}\beta)=w_{1}S_{w}(\beta)
(note that
w_{1}\beta
is again positive non-compact since
w_{1}\in W_{I}
). It follows that

(3.6.1)

{\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{w_{1}w\varphi(w_{1})^{-1}}=w_{1}{\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{w}

and similarly for negative sequences. We will use repeatedly the fact that an element
w\in W
lies in
{}^{I}W
if and only if
w^{-1}(\Phi^{+}_{L})\subseteq\Phi^{+}
.

Lemma 3.6.1
.

If
w=w^{\prime}z^{-1}
with
w^{\prime}\in{}^{I}W
, then all compact roots appearing in a
w
-sequence are negative.

Proof.

Let
(\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{n})
be a
w
-sequence. We may assume
n\geq 2
. Note that
w^{\prime-1}\beta_{1}=z^{-1}\sigma(\beta_{0})=w_{0}\sigma(w_{0,I}\beta_{0})
. Since
\beta_{1}
is compact and
w^{\prime}\in{}^{I}W
, the roots
w^{\prime-1}(\beta_{1})
and
\beta_{1}
have the same sign. Since
\beta_{0}
is positive non-compact, so is
w_{0,I}\beta_{0}
. It follows that
\beta_{1}
is negative. Assume now that
\beta_{i}\in\Phi^{-}_{L}
with
i+1<n
. Again
w^{\prime-1}(\beta_{i+1})=w_{0}\sigma(w_{0,I}\beta_{i})
. Since
\beta_{i}
is compact negative,
w^{\prime-1}(\beta_{i+1})
is negative, and then so is
\beta_{i+1}
since
w^{\prime}\in{}^{I}W
.
∎

Lemma 3.6.2
.

For any
w\in W
, the dimension of
\Stab_{E}(w)
is equal to
|{\mathcal{S}}_{w}^{-}|
.

Proof.

Write
w=w^{\prime}z^{-1}
for
w^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}wz
. Assume first that
w^{\prime}\in{}^{I}W
(i.e. that
w^{\prime}
is minimal). In this case,
\pi_{B}({\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w^{\prime}})={\mathcal{X}}_{w^{\prime}}
, and the map
\pi_{B}\colon{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w^{\prime}}\to{\mathcal{X}}_{w^{\prime}}\simeq[1/\Stab_{E}(w)]
is finite (Proposition
2.3.2
). Moreover,
{\mathcal{F}}^{(B)}_{w^{\prime}}=[E^{\prime}\backslash Bw^{\prime}Bz^{-1}]
, hence
\dim(\Stab_{E}(w))=\dim(E^{\prime})-\dim(Bw^{\prime}Bz^{-1})=\dim(R_{\textrm{u}}(P))-\ell(w^{\prime})
. Thus, it suffices to show that
\ell(w^{\prime})=|{\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{w}|
. Since
w^{\prime}\in{}^{I}W
, one has
w^{\prime-1}(\Phi^{+}_{L})\subseteq\Phi^{+}
. Thus, if the roots
w^{\prime-1}(\beta)
and
\beta
have different signs, then
\beta
is non-compact. By Lemma
3.6.1
, all compact roots appearing in a
w
-sequence are negative. Let
(\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{n})
be a
w
-sequence (with
n\geq 1
). Then,
w^{\prime-1}(\beta_{n})=w_{0}\sigma(w_{0,I}\beta_{n-1})
is negative (even for
n=1
). Moreover, any positive non-compact root is either the tail of a positive
w
-sequence or the opposite of the tail of a negative
w
-sequence. This shows that the tails of positive
w
-sequences are exactly the positive roots whose sign is changed by
w^{\prime-1}
. Thus
\ell(w^{\prime})=\ell(w^{\prime-1})=|{\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{w}|
.

Now, for general
w
, let
v^{\prime}\in{}^{I}W
be the unique element such that
w\in G_{v^{\prime}}
, and set
v\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}v^{\prime}z^{-1}
. By Proposition
2.6.1
, we can write
w=w_{1}xv\sigma(w_{1})^{-1}
for some
x\in W_{I_{v^{\prime}}}
and
w_{1}\in W_{I}
. Since
\dim(\Stab_{E}(w))=\dim(\Stab_{E}(v))
, it suffices to show that
|{\mathcal{S}}^{-}_{w}|=|{\mathcal{S}}^{-}_{v}|
. The transformation
w\mapsto w_{1}w\sigma(w_{1})^{-1}
does not change the number of negative sequences, by (
3.6.1
). It remains to check that
|{\mathcal{S}}^{-}_{xv}|=|{\mathcal{S}}^{-}_{v}|
. Note that the tail of the
v
-sequence
S_{v}(\beta)
is the root
v\sigma(v)\cdots\sigma^{n-1}(v)\sigma^{n}(\beta)
for some
n\geq 1
. Since
v\sigma(I_{v^{\prime}})=I_{v^{\prime}}
, we have
v\sigma(x)=yv
for some
y\in W_{I_{v^{\prime}}}
. This implies (by induction on
n
) that we can write

(3.6.2)

(xv)\sigma(xv)\cdots\sigma^{n-1}(xv)=yv\sigma(v)\cdots\sigma^{n-1}(v)

for some
y\in W_{I_{v^{\prime}}}
. We deduce that the
v
-sequence
S_{v}(\beta)
is negative if and only if the
xv
-sequence
S_{xv}(\beta)
is negative. This terminates the proof.
∎

Remark 3.6.3
.

Let
X_{w}
denote the set of positive non-compact roots
\beta
such that the
w
-sequence

is negative. For

and

, let

(3.6.3)

\epsilon_{\beta}(u)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left((wuw^{-1})(w^{2}\varphi(u)w^{-2})\cdots(w^{m}\varphi^{m-1}(u)w^{-m}),u(w\varphi(u)w)\cdots(w^{m-1}\varphi^{m}(u)w^{-(m-1)})\right).

It is easy to see that

. Let

As a consequence of Lemma
3.6.2
, we find that for any

,

\dim(E\cdot(wz^{-1}))=\dim(E)-\dim(\Stab_{E}(wz^{-1}))=\dim(P)+|{\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{wz^{-1}}|.

Lemma 3.6.4
.

For any
w\in W
, we have
|{\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{wz^{-1}}|\leq\ell(w)
.

Proof.

For any positive
wz^{-1}
-sequence
(\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{n})
there is at least one

such that

and

∎

Proposition 3.6.5
.

Let
w\in W
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

The element

is small.

(2)

|{\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{wz^{-1}}|=\ell(w)
.

Proof.

By Proposition

(

By Lemma

, this amounts to

, which is equivalent to

.
∎

Remark 3.6.6
.

Using the characterization of Proposition

4.
Decidability of singularities

In this section we consider an arbitrary zip datum
{\mathcal{Z}}
of Frobenius-type. Let
w\in{\vphantom{W}}^{I}{W},\ w^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{Z}}(w),
and consider the elementary
w
-open substack
{\mathcal{U}}(w,w^{\prime})\subseteq{\mathcal{X}}_{\mathcal{Z}}
. By Theorem
3.1.3
, we know that
{\mathcal{U}}(w,w^{\prime})
is smooth if and only if it is both
w
-bounded and admits a separating canonical cover.

4.1.
General algorithm for smoothness

We start with a proposition which gives a more precise statement than Theorem
3.1.3
(
4
). We first explain the connection between small strata and separating canonical covers of zip strata. Assume that the elementary substack
{\mathcal{U}}(w,w^{\prime})
does not admit a separating canonical cover. By definition, this means that there exists an element
v\in\Gamma_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\}
such that
\pi_{P_{w}}({\mathcal{F}}^{(P_{w})}_{v})
intersects
{\mathcal{X}}_{w^{\prime}}
. We must necessarily have
\pi_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(v)=w^{\prime}
by Lemma
3.4.1
. In particular,

(4.1.1)

\ell(\pi_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(v))=\ell(w^{\prime})=\ell(w)-1=\ell(v).

Hence
v
is small. This implies:

Proposition 4.1.1
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

{\mathcal{U}}(w,w^{\prime})
is smooth (equivalently, normal).

(2)

P_{w^{\prime}}\subseteq P_{w}
and
w^{\prime}\notin\pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})
.

Proof.

It is clear that (
1
) implies (
2
). Conversely, if the substack
{\mathcal{U}}(w,w^{\prime})
does not admit a separating canonical cover, by the discussion above, then there exists
w_{1}\in\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\}
such that
w^{\prime}=\pi_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(w)
. This shows that (
2
) implies (
1
).
∎

We show that both conditions of Proposition
4.1.1
(
2
) are algorithmically decidable.

4.1.2.
w
-Boundedness

Since
P_{w}
,
P_{w^{\prime}},
are standard parabolic subgroups, the containment
P_{w^{\prime}}\subseteq P_{w}
is satisfied if and only if

. These finite sets can be computed explicitly by the formula

(4.1.3)

I_{w}=\bigcap_{m\geq 0}\varphi^{m}_{w}(I)

where
\varphi_{w}
is the operator
wz^{-1}\sigma
, see
[
9
, § 2.2.6]
,
[
17
, Prop. 5.6]
.

4.1.4.
Images of small elements

We explain the procedure to determine whether the second condition
w^{\prime}\notin\pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})
in Proposition
4.1.1
is satisfied. Recall the map
\Xi_{\mathcal{Z}}\colon G(K)\to{}^{I}W
defined in (
2.6.1
). Proposition
2.6.1
gives an algorithm to determine the restriction
\Xi_{\mathcal{Z}}|_{W}
. An immediate consequence of Proposition
3.5.4
is the following:

Proposition 4.1.2
.

For
w\in W^{\rm sm}
, one has
\pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(w)=\Xi_{\mathcal{Z}}(w)
.

Since
\Xi_{\mathcal{Z}}|_{W}
is algorithmically computable, so is
\pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(w)
for

. The algorithm to decide if an elementary substack admits a separating canonical cover is the following:

Algorithm 4.1.3
.

(1)

Enumerate
\Gamma_{I_{w}}(w)
.

(2)

Determine which elements of
\Gamma_{I_{w}}(w)
are small, using the characterization explained in §
3.6
. This gives the set
\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)
.

(3)

For each
w^{\prime\prime}\in\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\}
, compute
\pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(w^{\prime\prime})=\Xi_{\mathcal{Z}}(w^{\prime\prime})
(Proposition
4.1.2
). This gives the set
\pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})
.

(4)

Check if
w^{\prime}\in\pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})
.

Theorem 4.1.4
.

Algorithm
4.1.3
gives an effective procedure. Combined with §
4.1.2
, this gives an effective algorithm to determine whether
{\mathcal{U}}(w,w^{\prime})
is smooth (equivalently, normal).

4.2.
Dictionary for
\GL_{n}

We explain the connection between
\GL_{n,\mathbb{F}_{p}}
-zips and Dieudonné spaces, following computations from
[
12
]
. Let
\varphi
be the
p
-power isogeny of
\GL_{n,\mathbb{F}_{p}}
.

4.2.1.
Dieudonné spaces and
F
-zips

We consider the case
G=\GL_{n,\mathbb{F}_{p}}
with a cocharacter of type
(r,s)
, where
r+s=n
. Let
S
be the special fiber of a unitary Shimura variety of signature
(r,s)
at
p
and assume that splits in the associated imaginary quadratic field
\mathbf{E}
. If
A
is the underlying abelian variety of a point of
S
, then
A
is endowed with an action of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{E}}
and a compatible polarization. Write
\mathbb{D}(A)
for the Dieudonne module over
W(K)
of

, and
\overline{\mathbb{D}}(A)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{D}(A)\otimes_{W(K)}K
for the associated Dieudonne space.

The
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbf{E}}
-action yields a splitting
\mathbb{D}(A)=\mathbb{D}(A)_{\tau}\oplus\mathbb{D}(A)_{\overline{\tau}}
, where
\Gal(\mathbf{E}/\mathbb{Q})=\{\tau,\overline{\tau}\}

is split in
\mathbf{E}

(Frobenius and Verschiebung), and similarly for
\overline{\mathbb{D}}(A)=\overline{\mathbb{D}}(A)_{\tau}\oplus\overline{\mathbb{D}}(A)_{\overline{\tau}}

-vector space endowed with a
\sigma
-linear endomorphism

, a
\sigma^{-1}
-linear endomorphism

satisfying the conditions:

(1)

\Ker(F)={\rm Im}(V)
,

(2)

\Ker(V)={\rm Im}(F)
,

(3)

\rk(F)=r
,
\rk(V)=s
.

The triple

completely characterizes the Ekedahl–Oort stratum of a point. We call such a triple a

(r,s)
.

4.2.2.

-orbits

On the other hand, recall that the Ekedahl–Oort strata of

\displaystyle=(w_{0,I_{w_{1}}}s_{\alpha_{r}}w_{0,I_{w_{1}}}^{-1})(w_{0,I_{w_{1}}}w_{1}z^{-1}w_{0,I_{w_{1}}}^{-1})

\displaystyle=(w_{0,I_{w_{1}}}s_{\alpha_{r}}w_{0,I_{w_{1}}}^{-1})w_{1}z^{-1}=s_{w_{0,I_{w_{1}}}(\alpha_{r})}w_{1}z^{-1}.

Finally, since
\alpha_{r+1}\in I_{w_{1}}
and
\alpha_{r-1},\alpha_{r}\notin I_{w_{1}}
, we find
w_{0,I_{w_{1}}}(\alpha_{r})=s_{\alpha_{r+1}}(\alpha_{r})=s_{\alpha_{r}}(\alpha_{r+1})
by (
4.5.2
). We have showed:

(4.5.5)

w_{0,I_{w_{1}}}w^{\prime}_{1}z^{-1}w_{0,I_{w_{1}}}^{-1}=s_{\alpha_{r}}s_{\alpha_{r+1}}s_{\alpha_{r}}w_{1}z^{-1}=w^{\prime}z^{-1}.

This shows the claim that
w^{\prime}_{1}z^{-1}
and
w^{\prime}z^{-1}
lie in the same

-orbit and terminates the proof.
∎

Remark 4.5.4

In the case where

The split case

Notation

5.2.
Stabilizers

For any

, identify
\Stab_{E_{m}}(x)
with a closed subgroup of

. Note that

for all

Lemma 5.2.1
.

For any
x\in P_{w}(wz^{-1})Q_{w}
and any

, one has
\Stab_{E_{m}}(x)\subseteq P_{w}
.

Proof.

The case

is exactly the statement of Proposition
2.5.2
. We now consider the case

. Let
(x,y)\in\Stab_{E_{0}}(wz^{-1})
. By definition, one has
\overline{x}=\overline{y}
. Choose an integer

such that
\overline{x}\in L(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})

, hence

. By the case

.
∎

Viewed as a subgroup of

, since
E_{m}\cap(P_{w}\times G)\subseteq P_{w}\times Q_{w}
.

5.3.
The

-rational points

By our assumption, the locally closed subset

is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. In particular, we may speak of the

-points of this variety. Let
\Sigma_{m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(G_{m,w})_{w\in{}^{I}W}
denote the zip stratification of exponent

\Sigma_{m}
(for

) coincide on
G(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

Proposition 5.3.1
.

(1)

We have
G_{m,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})=G_{0,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})
for all
m\geq 0
.

(2)

The set
G_{m,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is independent of
m\geq 0
.

Proof.

First, we show
G_{m,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})\subseteq G_{0,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})
for any
m\geq 1
. Let
g\in G_{m,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})
and
(a,b)\in E_{m}
such that
g=awz^{-1}b^{-1}
. Since
g\in G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})
, we obtain
awz^{-1}b^{-1}=\varphi^{m}(a)wz^{-1}\varphi^{m}(b)^{-1}
, hence
(a^{-1}\varphi^{m}(a),b^{-1}\varphi^{m}(b))
lies in the stabilizer in

of

. By Lemma
5.2.1
, this stabilizer is contained is
E^{\prime}_{m,P_{w}}=E_{m}\cap(P_{w}\times Q_{w})
. Since

is connected, Lang’s Theorem shows that we can write

(5.3.1)

(a^{-1}\varphi^{m}(a),b^{-1}\varphi^{m}(b))=(x^{-1}\varphi^{m}(x),y^{-1}\varphi^{m}(y))

for

. We deduce that
(ax^{-1},by^{-1})\in E_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})
. Moreover, note that we have
E_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})=E_{0}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})
. We can write

(5.3.2)

Since
(ax^{-1},by^{-1})\in E_{0}
, this implies that

, by (
2.5.1
). This shows the inclusion
G_{m,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})\subseteq G_{0,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})
. Since

(5.3.3)

G(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})=\bigsqcup_{w\in{}^{I}W}G_{m,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})=\bigsqcup_{w\in{}^{I}W}G_{0,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}),

we must have
G_{m,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})=G_{0,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})
. This shows (
1
). For the second assertion, we have for

:

(5.3.4)

G_{m,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p})=G_{m,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})\cap G(\mathbb{F}_{p})=G_{0,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})\cap G(\mathbb{F}_{p})=G_{0,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p}).\qed

Example 5.3.2
.

Consider the case of

, endowed with the cocharacter
\mu\colon t\mapsto{\rm diag}(t,t,1)
. This corresponds to the cocharacter datum arising from to a Picard modular surface attached to a quadratic, totally imaginary extension

at a split prime
p
of

of good reduction. For any
m\geq 0
, the zip stratification is linear and consists of exactly
3
strata. The unique open stratum

is the same for all stratifications
\Sigma_{m}
(
m\geq 0
), and is given by the matrices of the form

(5.3.5)

x=\left(\begin{matrix}x_{11}&x_{12}&x_{13}\\
x_{21}&x_{22}&x_{23}\\
x_{31}&x_{32}&x_{33}\\
\end{matrix}\right)\quad\textrm{such that}\quad\delta(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left|\begin{matrix}x_{11}&x_{12}\\
x_{21}&x_{22}\end{matrix}\right|\neq 0.

The unique stratum of codimension one consists of matrices

such that

, where

(5.3.6)

\mathsf{Ha}^{(m)}(x)=x_{11}\left|\begin{matrix}x_{11}&x_{12}\\
x_{31}&x_{32}\end{matrix}\right|^{p^{m}-1}+x_{22}\left|\begin{matrix}x_{21}&x_{22}\\
x_{31}&x_{32}\end{matrix}\right|^{p^{m}-1}.

This formula still holds in the case

The third zip stratum, which is the unique closed stratum, is the complement of the above two strata. It is defined by the vanishing of both

. In particular, we see on this example that the various stratifications of

strongly depend on the choice of

be an intermediate parabolic. Let

, which is the zip stratum parametrized by

(1)

We have
G^{(P_{0})}_{m,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})=G^{(P_{0})}_{0,w}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}})

(2)

The set

is independent of
m\geq 0
.

We compare the images of strata for various values of

Proposition 5.4.1

Let

(1)

We first state the following lemma, whose proof is entirely similar to Lemma

(also for

Let

and

(depending on

Proof.

Recall that

is the set of

and

We have

Set

∎

(2)

The element
\pi_{m^{\prime}}(w)\in{}^{I}W
is independent of
m^{\prime}\geq 0
.

Proof.

In the case
m=0
, Proposition
5.4.1
implies that
{\mathcal{F}}_{m_{1},w}^{(P_{0})}
is
{\mathcal{Z}}_{m_{1}}
-small for some
m_{1}>0
, hence we may assume
m>0
. By Corollary
3.4.4
, there exists
w_{1}\in{}^{I}W
of length
\ell(w)
such that
\left(G^{(B)}_{m,w}\cap G_{m,w_{1}}\right)(\mathbb{F}_{p})\neq\emptyset
. Since this set is independent of
m\geq 0
, we deduce
G^{(B)}_{m^{\prime},w}\cap G_{m^{\prime},w_{1}}\neq\emptyset
for all
m^{\prime}\geq 0
, hence
w_{1}\in\Pi_{m^{\prime}}(w)
. By Lemma
3.4.1
(in the case
m^{\prime}\geq 1
) and Corollary
5.4.3
(in the case
m^{\prime}=0
), we must have
w_{1}=\pi_{m^{\prime}}(w)
. This terminates the proof.∎

By Lemma
5.5.2
, the set of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}
-small elements of
W
is independent of
m\geq 0
. Write simply
W^{\rm sm}
for this set. We have shown that the restriction of the map
\pi_{m}
to
W^{\rm sm}\subseteq W
is independent of
m
. This gives a partial answer to Conjecture
5.5.1
.

5.6.
Separating canonical covers

For
w\in{}^{I}W
and
w^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{I}(w)
, denote by

(5.6.1)

{\mathcal{U}}_{m}(w,w^{\prime})\subseteq{\mathcal{X}}_{m}

the corresponding elementary
w
-open substack inside
{\mathcal{X}}_{m}
. For a subset
A\subseteq W
, we simply write
\Pi_{m}(A)
for the subset
\bigcup_{w\in A}\Pi_{m}(w)
.

Proposition 5.6.1
.

For any
m\geq 0
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

{\mathcal{U}}_{m}(w,w^{\prime})
admits a separating canonical cover.

(2)

One has
w^{\prime}\notin\Pi_{m}(\Gamma_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})
.

(3)

One has
w^{\prime}\notin\pi_{m}(\Gamma_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})
.

(4)

One has
w^{\prime}\notin\pi_{m}(\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})
.

Proof.

For
m\geq 0
,
{\mathcal{U}}_{m}(w,w^{\prime})
admits a separating canonical cover if and only if

(a)

w^{\prime}\preccurlyeq_{I_{w}}w
(i.e. it admits a canonical cover), and

(b)

for all
v\preccurlyeq_{I_{w}}w
such that
v\notin\{w,w^{\prime}\}
, we have
\Pi_{m}(v)\cap\{w,w^{\prime}\}=\emptyset
.

Condition (b) is equivalent to
w^{\prime}\notin\Pi_{m}(\Gamma_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})
by Lemmas
3.4.1
(for
m\geq 1
) and
5.4.3
(for
m=0
). Moreover, since
\pi_{P_{w}}(\overline{{\mathcal{F}}}_{m,w})=\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}_{m,w}
, we must have
w^{\prime}\in\Pi_{m}(\Gamma_{I_{w}}(w))
. It follows that if
w^{\prime}\notin\Pi_{m}(\Gamma_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})
, then
w^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{I_{w}}(w)
. Hence, condition (b) implies condition (a). It follows that (
1
) and (
2
) are equivalent. Clearly, (
2
) implies (
3
), which implies (
4
). Conversely, suppose
w^{\prime}\in\Pi_{m}(v)
, for some
v\in\Gamma_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\}
. Since
\ell(w^{\prime})=\ell(v)
, we must have
w^{\prime}=\pi_{m}(v)
. Thus
v
is small, and
w^{\prime}\in\pi_{m}(\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})
. Hence (
4
) implies (
2
). This terminates the proof.
∎

We now explain the main result of this section. Note that condition (
4
) of Proposition
5.6.1
is independent of
m\geq 0
, by Lemma
5.5.2
. We deduce the following theorem.

Proposition 5.6.2
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer. The following are equivalent:

(1)

{\mathcal{U}}_{m}(w,w^{\prime})
admits a separating canonical cover.

(2)

{\mathcal{U}}_{0}(w,w^{\prime})
admits a separating canonical cover.

Finally, using Theorem
3.1.3
, we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 5.6.3
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer. The following are equivalent:

(1)

{\mathcal{U}}_{m}(w,w^{\prime})
is smooth (equivalently, normal).

(2)

{\mathcal{U}}_{0}(w,w^{\prime})
is
w
-bounded and admits a separating canonical cover.

(3)

P_{w^{\prime}}\subseteq P_{w}
and
w^{\prime}\notin\pi_{0}(\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})
.

In particular, the smoothness of
{\mathcal{U}}_{m}(w,w^{\prime})
is independent of
m\geq 1
. We do not know whether these conditions are also equivalent to the smoothness of the substack
{\mathcal{U}}_{0}(w,w^{\prime})
. We say that a scheme or stack
X
is
smooth in codimension one
if the complement of the smooth locus of
X
has codimension
\geq 2
.

Corollary 5.6.4
.

Let
w\in{}^{I}W
and
m\geq 1
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}_{m}(w)
is smooth in codimension one.

(2)

P_{w^{\prime}}\subseteq P_{w}
for all
w^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{I}(w)
and
\Gamma_{I_{w}}^{\rm sm}(w)=\Gamma_{I}(w)
.

(3)

P_{w^{\prime}}\subseteq P_{w}
for all
w^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{I}(w)
and
\Gamma_{I_{w}}^{\rm sm}(w)\subseteq\Gamma_{I}(w)
.

Proof.

We apply Corollary
5.6.3
to each lower neighbor of
w
in
{}^{I}W
. Assume (
1
). Let
w^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{I_{w}}^{\rm sm}(w)
and write
w_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\pi_{0}(w^{\prime})
. Since
{\mathcal{U}}_{m}(w,w_{1})
is smooth, we must have
w_{1}=w^{\prime}
hence
w^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{I}(w)
. Hence
\Gamma_{I_{w}}^{\rm sm}(w)\subseteq\Gamma_{I}(w)
. Moreover, for any
w^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{I}(w)
, the substack
{\mathcal{U}}_{m}(w,w^{\prime})
admits a canonical cover, hence
w^{\prime}\preccurlyeq_{I_{w}}w
. This shows
\Gamma_{I}(w)\subseteq\Gamma_{I_{w}}(w)
, and thus
\Gamma_{I}(w)\subseteq\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)
, since elements of
{}^{I}W
are small by Proposition
2.5.1
. Hence (
2
) holds. (
2
) implies (
3
) trivially. Finally, assume (
3
), and let
w^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{I}(w)
. Since
\pi_{0}
is the identity on
{}^{I}W
, we have
w^{\prime}\notin\pi_{0}(\Gamma^{\rm sm}_{I_{w}}(w)\setminus\{w^{\prime}\})
for each
w^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{I}(w)
. Therefore
\bigcup_{w^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{I}(w)}{\mathcal{U}}_{m}(w,w^{\prime})
is smooth by Corollary
5.6.3
.
∎

{\mathcal{G}}

6.1.1.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}

(3)

Define the zip group

{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(R)=\{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{P}}(R)\times{\mathcal{Q}}(R)\ |\ \overline{x}=\overline{y}\}\qquad\textrm{for any $\mathbb{Z}_{p}$-algebra $R$},

where
\overline{x},\overline{y}\in{\mathcal{L}}(R)

respectively. Define the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-stack

by

(6.1.4)

{\mathscr{X}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\backslash{\mathcal{G}}].

It is clear that

2

6.1.5.
Stratifications

Let
W\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}W({\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{T}})
be the Weyl group of

\Phi^{+}

for the type of
{\mathcal{P}}
. Set

\gamma\colon{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{P}}\to{\mathcal{G}}

\gamma(g,x)=x^{-1}g\overline{x}

(6.1.6)

\widetilde{\gamma}\colon{\mathcal{G}}\times\left({\mathcal{P}}/{\mathcal{B}}\right)\to{\mathcal{B}}\backslash{\mathcal{G}}/{}^{z}{\mathcal{B}}

{\mathcal{G}}_{w}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\pr_{1}({\mathcal{H}}_{w}),\quad{\mathcal{G}}^{*}_{w}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\pr_{1}(\overline{{\mathcal{H}}}_{w}).

For
w\in W

\overline{{\mathcal{H}}}_{w}=\bigcup_{w^{\prime}\leq w}{\mathcal{H}}_{w^{\prime}}

\widetilde{\gamma}
and a similar formula for the closure of
{\mathcal{C}}_{w}
. We deduce

(6.1.8)

{\mathcal{G}}^{*}_{w}=\bigcup_{w^{\prime}\leq w}\pr_{1}({\mathcal{H}}_{w^{\prime}}).

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
and
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
be the zip data attached to the generic and special fiber of
({\mathcal{G}},\mu)
, respectively. Since
{\mathcal{G}}
is split over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the twisted orders on
{}^{I}W
attached to
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
and
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
can be identified. We simply denote it by
\preccurlyeq

Proposition 6.1.1
.

(1)

{\mathcal{G}}
is the disjoint union of the sets
{\mathcal{G}}_{w}
for
w\in{}^{I}W
.

(2)

The set
{\mathcal{G}}_{w}
is locally closed in
{\mathcal{G}}
.

(3)

The set
{\mathcal{G}}^{*}_{w}
is closed in
{\mathcal{G}}
and coincides with the Zariski closure
\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}_{w}
of
{\mathcal{G}}_{w}
.

Proof.

For the first statement, it suffices to check it over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
where the result is already known. We claim that

(6.1.9)

{\mathcal{G}}^{*}_{w}=\bigcup_{w^{\prime}\preccurlyeq w}{\mathcal{G}}_{w^{\prime}}.

Indeed, this identity can be checked set-theoretically on the special and generic fibers, where we already know it holds. Since
\pr_{1}
is proper,
{\mathcal{G}}^{*}_{w}
is closed in
{\mathcal{G}}
for any

(6.1.10)

{\mathcal{G}}_{w}={\mathcal{G}}^{*}_{w}\setminus\bigcup_{w^{\prime}\prec w}{\mathcal{G}}_{w^{\prime}}={\mathcal{G}}^{*}_{w}\setminus\bigcup_{w^{\prime}\prec w}{\mathcal{G}}^{*}_{w^{\prime}}.

The second equality follows from (

is locally closed. Finally, since

∎

6.3.

For
w\in W
, write
\Pi_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(w)
for the set of

\Pi_{R}\colon W\to{\mathcal{P}}({}^{I}W),\quad\pi_{R}\colon W\to{}^{I}W.

Denote also by

the set of small elements over

, i.e. the set of

such that

. It is clear that

Lemma 6.3.1
.

For

, we have
\pi_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(w)\preccurlyeq\pi_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(w)
and
\pi_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(w)=\pi_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(w)

The second assertion immediately follows from the first one. Write

\pi_{\mathcal{B}}\colon{\mathscr{F}}^{({\mathcal{B}})}_{w}\to{\mathscr{X}}

(6.3.2)

\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(\overline{{\mathscr{F}}}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p},w}^{({\mathcal{B}})})=\overline{\pi_{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{F}}^{({\mathcal{B}})}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p},w})}\subseteq\overline{{\mathscr{X}}}_{w^{\prime}}=\bigcup_{w^{\prime\prime}\preccurlyeq w^{\prime}}{\mathscr{X}}_{w^{\prime\prime}}.

.
∎

6.4.
Reduction to characteristic
p

The goal of this section is to prove the following proposition, using a reduction mod
p
argument. Say that a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-scheme is
rational
if it is birational to an affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
. For example, the set
{\mathcal{B}}w{\mathcal{B}}
(called a Bruhat cell) is rational (because
{\mathcal{T}}
is split over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
). Note that the set
\mathbb{A}^{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
is Zariski dense in
\mathbb{A}^{n}
, hence the same is true for irreducible rational
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-schemes. Similarly, if a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-scheme
X
admits a surjective map
Y\to X
from an irreducible rational
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-scheme, then
X(\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
is Zariski dense in
X
.

Proposition 6.4.1
.

For any
w\in W
, one has
\pi_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(w)=\pi_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(w)
.

Proof.

The set
{\mathcal{H}}_{w}
considered in
6.1.5
can be written as follows:

(6.4.1)

{\mathcal{H}}_{w}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{(g,xB)\in{\mathcal{G}}\times({\mathcal{P}}/{\mathcal{B}})\ |\ x^{-1}g\overline{x}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{w}\}.

There is a natural surjective
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{0}
-invariant map
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\times{\mathcal{C}}_{w}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{w}
,
((a,b),x)\mapsto(axb^{-1},a{\mathcal{B}})
. Since the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-scheme
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\times{\mathcal{C}}_{w}
is rational, it follows that
{\mathcal{H}}_{w}(\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
is Zariski dense in
{\mathcal{H}}_{w}
. The map
\pi_{\mathcal{B}}\colon{\mathscr{F}}^{({\mathcal{B}})}_{w}\to{\mathscr{X}}
corresponds to the first projection
\pr_{1}\colon{\mathcal{H}}_{w}\to{\mathcal{G}}
, which is
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
-equivariant.

If we write
w^{\prime}=\pi_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(w)
, then
\pr_{1}({\mathcal{H}}_{w})\subseteq\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}_{w^{\prime}}
since
\pi_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(w)=\pi_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(w)
. Hence, the preimage of
{\mathcal{G}}_{w^{\prime}}
inside
{\mathcal{H}}_{w}
is open in
{\mathcal{H}}_{w}
and nonempty (because it contains a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-point), hence dense in
{\mathcal{H}}_{w}
by irreducibility. By the above, it follows that it contains a
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-point. Hence, there exists
x\in{\mathcal{H}}_{w}(\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
such that
\pr_{1}(x)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{w^{\prime}}
by
\pr_{1}
. In particular,
({\mathcal{H}}_{w}\cap{\mathcal{G}}_{w^{\prime}})(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\neq\emptyset
, hence

. This implies
\ell(\pi_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(w))\leq\ell(\pi_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(w))
. Combined with Lemma
6.3.1
, we obtain
\pi_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(w)=\pi_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(w)
.
∎

In particular, we obtain the inequality
\ell(\pi_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(w))\leq\ell(w)
for any
w\in W
, since we have established it for
\pi_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(x)
(Lemma
5.4.3
). The above proposition has also the following immediate consequence:

Proposition 6.4.2
.

We have
W_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{\rm sm}=W_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{\rm sm}
.

We expect that
\Pi_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}=\Pi_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
in general, but the above results will suffice for the purposes of this article.

6.5.

Set
G\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{G}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}

-power Frobenius isogeny of

. We retain the notations of §

. Let

Proposition 6.5.1

The following are equivalent:

7.5.
Hasse invariants

We say that a section

. Similarly,

on the stratum

is a Bruhat section. We recall two geometric lemmas:

Lemma 7.5.1
.

Let

be a surjective morphism of normal schemes. Let

be a line bundle on

Proof.

If
s
extends to

, then clearly
f^{*}s
extends to
X
. Conversely, assume that
f^{*}s
extends to

Since

is normal, it suffices to show that the divisor of

would have a pole along any codimension one subscheme

∎
