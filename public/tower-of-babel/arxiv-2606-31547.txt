A cohomological translation of the Kaplansky radical for profinite groups
S. Blumer, J. Feuerpfeil, L. C. Lopes, C. Quadrelli

Abstract
. The Kaplansky radical of a field consists of the nonzero elements represented by every norm quadratic form in two variables. D. Kijima and M. Nishi conjectured that, for quadratic extensions, the Kaplansky radicals are related by the norm map in a manner analogous to Hilbert’s Theorem 90. Although this H-conjecture was disproved by K.J. Becher and D.B. Leep, it is known to hold for several important classes of fields. We introduce a cohomological analogue of the Kaplansky radical for arbitrary profinite groups and primes p p , defined as the orthogonal of H 1 ​ ( G , F p ) {\rm H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}) with respect to the cup product with itself. For absolute Galois groups, this recovers the classical Kaplansky radical when p = 2 p=2 and the p − p- radical of Dario–Engler for arbitrary p. We also formulate a group-theoretic analogue of the H-conjecture, proving that, for fields, it is equivalent to the original conjectural property and depends only on the maximal pro- 2 2 quotient of the absolute Galois group. We establish this property for broad classes of fields, including local and global fields, rational function fields, and all fields whose maximal pro- p p Galois group is of elementary type. Beyond its arithmetic origins, we investigate the property for general pro- p p groups, proving its stability under several natural group-theoretic constructions and obtaining new examples, including generalized right-angled Artin pro- p p groups and fundamental pro- p p groups of suitable graphs of groups, many of which cannot occur as maximal pro- p p Galois groups. Keywords. Kaplansky radical, Galois cohomology, absolute Galois groups, Elementary Type conjecture, free pro- p p constructions. In the ’80s, in a series of papers, D. Kijima and M. Nishi investigated the norm maps between Kaplansky radicals in quadratic field extensions in analogy to the classical Hilbert 90 theorem (see

\displaystyle N_{\mathbb{K}(\sqrt{a})/\mathbb{K}}^{-1}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathbb{K}))={\mathcal{R}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathbb{K}(\sqrt{a}))\cdot\mathbb{K}^{\times}.

\mathbb{K}
be a field containing a primitive
{p^{\rm th}}
-root of unity, set

and let

be elements of
\mathbb{K}^{\times}
.
The following are equivalent.

(i)

One has

for some

(ii)

The cup product
\kappa(a)\smallsmile\kappa(b)\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})

Remark 1.8
.

It is well known that fixing an isomorphism of

-modules
\mu_{p}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}
— and thus a generator
\zeta
of
\mu_{p}
— yields an isomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G_{\mathbb{K}},\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong{}_{p}\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{K})
, where the former is the

, sending

to the class of the cyclic algebra

(see, e.g.,
[

Remark 1.9
.

By Lemma

we infer that, for

\displaystyle\ker(c_{\alpha})=\cores_{\ker(\alpha),G_{\mathbb{K}}}^{1}(\mathrm{H}^{1}(\ker(\alpha),\mathbb{F}_{p})),

where

denotes the linear map

.

1.3.
The Kaplansky radical and the
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-cup radical

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field containing a primitive
p^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity.
Recall that for
p=2
the Kaplansky radical
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathbb{K})
of
\mathbb{K}
is defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathbb{K})=\left\{{a\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}}\ \big|\ {D{K}\langle 1,-a\rangle=\mathbb{K}^{\times}}\right\}=\left\{{a\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}}\ \big|\ {\mathrm{im}(N_{\mathbb{K}(\sqrt{a})/\mathbb{K}})=\mathbb{K}^{\times}}\right\},

where
D{K}\langle 1,-a\rangle
denotes the subgroup of
\mathbb{K}^{\times}
whose elements are represented by the quadratic form
X^{2}-aY^{2}
(cf., e.g.,
[
21
,
17
]
).

For
p
arbitrary, the
p
-radical
\mathcal{R}_{p}(\mathbb{K})
of
\mathbb{K}
is defined as

\mathcal{R}_{p}(\mathbb{K})=\bigcap_{a\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}}N_{\mathbb{K}(\sqrt[p]{a})/\mathbb{K}}\left(\mathbb{K}(\sqrt[p]{a})^{\times}\right),

so that
\mathcal{R}_{2}(\mathbb{K})=\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathbb{K})
(cf.
[
6
, § 1]
).
Note that
{\mathbb{K}^{\times}}^{p}\subseteq{\mathcal{R}}_{p}(\mathbb{K})
. The following proposition is an immediate consequence of Lemma
1.7
.

Lemma 1.10
.

Let
\kappa:\mathbb{K}^{\times}\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(G{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
be the Kummer map. Then,
\kappa
induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{R}_{p}(\mathbb{K})/{\mathbb{K}^{\times}}^{p}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{\mathbb{K}})
— in particular,
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathbb{K})/{\mathbb{K}^{\times}}^{2}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathcal{R}_{2}(G_{\mathbb{K}})
for
p=2
.

We are now ready to translate the statement of the “H-conjecture”  into cohomological terms.

Proposition 1.11
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic not
2
.
Then the “H-conjecture” has positive answer for
\mathbb{K}
if and only if for any quadratic extension
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
the map

c_{\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}}:\mathcal{R}_{2}(\mathbb{L})\longrightarrow\mathcal{R}_{2}(G_{\mathbb{K}}),\qquad\alpha\longmapsto\cores_{G_{\mathbb{L}},G{K}}^{1}(\alpha)

is surjective.

Proof.

Assume that the “H-conjecture” has positive answer for
\mathbb{K}
, and let
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{K}(\sqrt{a})
be an arbitrary quadratic extension of
\mathbb{K}
.
Pick
\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{2}(G{K})
and
b\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}
such that
\kappa(b)=\beta
. Then
b\in{\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{K}}}(\mathbb{K})
by Lemma
1.10
.
By assumption, there exists
u\in\mathcal{R}_{\rm K}(\mathbb{K}(\sqrt{a}))
such that
N_{\mathbb{K}(\sqrt{a})/\mathbb{K}}(u)=b
. Using the commutative diagram (
2
) (with
p=2
), we have

\displaystyle\beta=\cores^{1}_{G_{\mathbb{K}(\sqrt{a})},G{K}}(\kappa(u))=c_{\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}}(\kappa(u)),

and
\kappa(u)\in\mathcal{R}_{2}(G_{\mathbb{K}(\sqrt{a})})
, again by Lemma
1.10
.
This shows that for
\mathbb{K}(\sqrt{a})/\mathbb{K}
the map
c_{\mathbb{K}(\sqrt{a})/\mathbb{K}}
is surjective as claimed.

Conversely, assume that the map
c_{\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}}
is surjective for every quadratic extension
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
. Given
a\in{\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{K}}}(\mathbb{K})
,
\kappa(a)\in\mathcal{R}_{2}(G_{\mathbb{K}})
by Lemma
1.10
, and hence there is
\gamma=\kappa(u)\in\mathcal{R}_{2}(G_{\mathbb{L}})
,
u\in\mathbb{L}^{\times}
, such that

\kappa(a)=\cores_{G{L},G{K}}^{1}(\gamma)=\cores_{G{L},G{K}}^{1}(\kappa(u)).

Moreover,
u\in\mathcal{R}_{\rm K}(\mathbb{L})
by Lemma
1.10
.
The commutativity of the diagram (
2
) implies that
\kappa(N_{\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}}(u))=\kappa(a)
.
Since
\ker(\kappa)=\mathbb{K}^{\times 2}
, one has

a=N_{\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}}(u)\cdot b^{2}=N_{\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}}(ub)\qquad\text{for some }b\in\mathbb{K}^{\times},

as
b^{2}=N_{\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}}(b)
. This completes the proof.
∎

1.4.

The following proposition shows that the

-cup radical — only depends on the maximal pro-

Proposition 1.12
.

Let

be a prime,

a profinite group and
G(p)
its maximal pro-

has the

Let

Remark 1.13
.

By the Merkurjev-Suslin Theorem — the “degree-2 step” of the celebrated Norm Residue Isomorphism Theorem —,
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})
is generated by cup-products of elements of degree 1 (cf., e.g.,
[
14
, Thm. 4.5.7–Thm. 4.6.6]
).
Since
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
is a free pro-
p
group if and only if
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})
is trivial (cf., e.g.,
[
27
, Prop. 3.5.17]
), one deduces that
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
is a free pro-
p
group if and only if
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p))=\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})
— as remarked by Dario–Engler using the
p
-radical, see
[
6
, §2]
.

Remark 1.14
.

The proof of Proposition
1.12
makes frequent use of the fact that we require the surjectivity of the corestriction only for normal subgroups
U
of index
p
. In the pro-
p
case, this poses no restriction, as all subgroups of index
p
are automatically normal.

One could also pose a stronger version of the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property, asking for the map induced by corestriction
\mathcal{R}_{p}(U)\to\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)
being surjective for all closed subgroups of index
p
. In this case the Proposition
1.12
does not remain valid, as the following concrete example shows:

Let
p
be an odd prime and set
G\coloneq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
with the canonical action. Then it is easy to see that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}
by the five-term sequence using that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
acts non-trivially on
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Now, since the cup product is graded-commutative we have
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Consider the subgroup
U\coloneq(p\mathbb{Z}_{p})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Then also
\mathcal{R}_{p}(U)\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}
and it is not difficult to verify by explicit computation that the corestriction map
\mathrm{H}^{1}(U,\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is trivial.

In contrast,
G(p)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, which has the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property — either by direct computation (cf.
1.15
) or using Example
1.17
.

1.5.
Examples of pro-
p
groups with the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property

Here we exhibit some concrete and basic examples of pro-
p
groups which satisfy — or do not satisfy — the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property.

Example 1.15

(pro-cyclic groups)
.

Let
G
be a non-trivial pro-cylic pro-
p
group, then one easily sees that
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)=\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}
except if
p=2
and
G\cong C_{2}
. In this case we find
\mathcal{R}_{p}(C_{2})=0
.

The unique subgroup
U
of
G
of index
p
is
G^{p}
. From the standard formula for corestriction one sees that if
U\neq 1
, then
\cores_{G^{p},G}^{1}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(G^{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is surjective. Thus
G
has the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property if and only if
G
is not isomorphic to
C_{p}
for
p
odd or
C_{4}
if
p=2
.

Example 1.16

(Powerful groups)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated
powerful
pro-
p
group, i.e., the closed subgroup
G^{\prime}\leq G
generated by the commutators is contained in the closed subgroup
G^{p}\leq G
generated by the
p
-powers, and also
G^{\prime}\leq G^{4}
if
p=2
(cf., e.g.,
[
8
, Def. 3.1]
). If
p=2
suppose further that
G
is torsion-free.

For
G
is not cyclic one has
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\geq 2
and the cup product induces an injective map
\Lambda^{2}(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}))\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
(cf.
[
36
, Thm. 5.1.6 and Cor 5.1.7]
). Therefore,
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)=0
, and
G
satisfies the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property by Corollary
1.6
. For example, this applies to finitely generated free abelian pro-
p
groups or elementary abelian groups.

Example 1.17

(Free pro-
p
groups)
.

Let
F
be a profinite group of
p
-cohomological dimension
1
, then
F
has the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property. In particular, free pro-
p
groups have the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property.

Indeed, since every open subgroup
U
of
F
has
p
-cohomological dimension
\leq 1
, we have
\mathcal{R}_{p}(U)=\mathrm{H}^{1}(U,\mathbb{F}_{p})
, and the statement is equivalent to showing that the corestriction

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(U,\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\mathbb{F}_{p})

is surjective. This is a consequence of
[
27
]
*Prop. 3.3.11.

Here we give an example of pro-
p
groups that is known not to occur as maximal pro-
p
Galois groups of fields containing a primitive
p^{\mathrm{th}}
-root of unity and that does not have the Kijima–Nishi property.

Example 1.18

(Heisenberg group)
.

For
p
odd, let
G
be the
Heisenberg pro-
p
group
, namely,

\begin{split}G&=\left\langle{x,y}\,\big|\,{[x,[x,y]]=[y,[x,y]]=1}\right\rangle\\
&=\left\langle{x,y,z}\,\big|\,{[x,y]=z,[x,z]=[y,z]=1}\right\rangle.\end{split}

Since the former is a minimal presentation, Proposition
1.3
implies
\alpha\smallsmile\beta=0
for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
, and thus
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)=\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Since
G
contains a two-generated abelian subgroup,
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\neq 0
, and hence, by the Norm Residue Isomorphism Theorem,
G
does not to occur as maximal pro-
p
Galois groups of any field containing a primitive
p^{\mathrm{th}}
-root of unity.

Now, consider the open subgroup
U\subseteq G
generated by
u\coloneq x^{p},y,z
. It is straightforward to verify that
U
is normal, has index
p
, and has minimal presentation

U=\left\langle{u,y,z}\,\big|\,{[u,y]=z^{p},[u,z]=[y,z]=1}\right\rangle

Therefore,
\dim\mathrm{H}^{1}(U,\mathbb{F}_{p})=3
. Since
U
is powerful, one has
\mathcal{R}_{p}(U)=0
(cf. Example
1.16
), and hence
G
does not have the Kijima–Nishi property.

1.6.
Testing the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property on open subgroups

Sometimes it can be hard to check the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property directly for a profinite group, as there might not be a direct description of all normal subgroups of index
p
(for example in the case of semidirect products). The next proposition says, that it is sufficient to test the property for an open subgroup
U
of
G
together with the surjectivity of
c_{U,P}
for this concrete subgroup. This is especially effective if
G
has virtual
p
-cohomological dimension
1
, as we will show in Proposition
1.20
.

Proposition 1.19
.

Let
G
be a profinite group containing an open subgroup
U
such that
c_{U,P}:\mathcal{R}_{p}(U)\to\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)
is surjective and
U
has the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property. Then also
G
has the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property.

Let
V
be an arbitrary open subgroup of index
p
in
G
. The index
[U:V\cap U]
is either
1
or
p
. If it is one, then
U
is contained in
V
and by
c_{U,G}=c_{V,G}\circ c_{U,V}
, we see that
c_{V,G}
is surjective.

Now assume that

has index
p
in
U
, and consider the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{R}_{p}(U\cap V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{R}_{p}(U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{R}_{p}(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)}

Here we have
N_{H}:\mathbb{F}_{p}[\omega]\to\mathbb{F}_{p}[\omega]
,
x\mapsto\sum_{h\in H}hx
. Notice that this map is zero when
H_{\omega}\neq 1
, and, if
H_{\omega}=1
, then it is surjective, as
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\omega]^{H}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}
, which is generated by
N_{H}(1)
. This shows that
N_{H}
is surjective, while the surjectivity of
c_{F,G}
follows from the fact that right, vertical arrow is an isomorphism.
∎

2.
Arithmetic considerations

In this section we sometimes write
\mathrm{H}^{n}(\mathbb{K},A)
instead of
\mathrm{H}^{n}(G{K},A)
for discrete
G{K}
modules
A
to simplify notation, when several fields are involved.

The goal of this section is to verify the Kijima–Nishi property for fields whose maximal pro-
p
Galois group is not necessarily finitely generated. Most of the results presented here are analogous to the case
p=2
, which was treated in
[
3
]
. Since their methods are specific to the theory of quadratic forms, we resort to more cohomological techniques, which nevertheless follow a similar philosophy.

2.1.
Local and global fields

By
[
33
, Ch. II, § 5, Thm. 4]
the maximal pro-
p

quotients of absolute Galois groups of local fields containing a primitive
{p^{\rm th}}
root of unity are either Demuškin pro-
p
groups or trivial — for a comprehensive survey on the structure of Demuškin groups we direct the reader to
[
27
, Ch. III, § 9]
.
By Corollary
1.6
, every Demuškin group satisfies the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property, as shown by the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let the pro-
p
group
G
be a Demuškin group.
Then
G
satisfies the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property.
In particular, if
\mathbb{K}
is a local field containing a primitive
p^{\mathrm{t}h}
root of unity, then
G_{\mathbb{K}}
satisfies the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property.

Proof.

By definition of Demuškin groups, the cup product induces a non-degenerate pairing

\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\times\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\simeq\mathbb{F}_{p}

(cf., e.g.,
[
27
, Def. 3.9.9]
).
Hence
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)=0
, so that
G
satisfies the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property by Corollary
1.6
.
∎

Before moving to global fields, we need the following lemma, which is a simple combination of Krasner’s lemma (
[
27
]
*Lem. 12.1.1) applied to the polynomials
X^{p}-a
together with Kummer duality. It is well known to experts.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(\mathbb{K},v)
be a discretely valued field containing a primitive
p^{\rm th}
root of unity and
\mathbb{K}_{v}
its completion. Then, the map
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K}_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})
induced by the choice of an inclusion
\mathbb{K}^{\rm sep}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{K}^{\rm sep}_{v}
is surjective.

The next theorem shows, that the
p
-radicals of global fields are trivial. By global fields we mean finite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
or
\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)
. Note that for infinite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
or
\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)
the statement is not true anymore, as one has
\Br(\mathbb{Q}^{\rm ab})=0
by
[
33
]
*§3.3 Prop. 9 or
\Br(\bar{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(t))=0
by Tsen’s theorem (cf.
[
14
]
*Thm. 6.2.8).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a global field containing a primitive
p^{\rm th}
-root of unity. Then
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{\mathbb{K}})=0
and, in particular,
\mathbb{K}
has the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property.

Proof.

Let
\alpha=\kappa(a)\in\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{\mathbb{K}})
. For any non-archimedean place
v
of
\mathbb{K}
we have, by Lemma
2.2
, that the associated local class
\alpha_{v}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K}_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})
satisfies

\displaystyle\alpha_{v}\smallsmile\beta=0\qquad\text{for all}\quad\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K}_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Therefore, for all
v
one has
\alpha_{v}\in\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{{\mathbb{K}}_{v}})
, which is trivial by Proposition
2.1
. Thus, for all
v
and for
a_{v}\in\mathbb{K}_{v}^{\times}
such that
\kappa(a_{v})=\alpha_{v}
, one has
a_{v}\in\mathbb{K}_{v}^{\times p}
. By the Grunwald–Wang Theorem (see for example
[
1
]
*Thm. IX.1) it follows that
a\in\mathbb{K}^{\times p}
, thus
\alpha=0
in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
.
∎

2.2.
Discretely valued fields and rational function fields

Next, we focus on rational function fields. It would be interesting to see if the approach is generalizable for curves of higher genus.
The classical Kaplansky radical was studied for function fields of arithmetic curves by G. Manzano-Flores in his PhD thesis
[
25
]
, where he related it to certain reduction properties of the curves.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(\mathbb{K},v)
be an
p
-henselian field with residue field
\mathbb{k}
. Assume that
\mathbb{k}
contains a primitive
p^{\mathrm{th}}
-root of unity (in particular, the characteristic of
\mathbb{k}
is different from
p
). Then
\mathbb{K}
has the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property. If
\mathbb{k}
is not
p
-closed, that is
G_{\mathbb{k}}(p)\neq 1
, then
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G{K})=0
.

Proof.

First we notice that if
G{k}(p)=1
, then
G{K}(p)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, since there are no unramified extensions and every
p
-extension is tamely ramified. Then the statement follows from Example
1.17
. In the other case, this is an immediate consequence of
[
40
]
*Thm. 3.6.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
p
be a prime number,
\mathbb{k}
a field containing a primitive
p^{\mathrm{th}}
-root of unity and set
\mathbb{K}\coloneq\mathbb{k}(t)
to be the rational function field in one variable over
\mathbb{k}
. Then
\mathbb{K}
has the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property, and, if
G_{\mathbb{k}}(p)
is infinite, then
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G{K})
is trivial.

Proof.

We recall that the
Faddeev sequence
(see for example
[
14
]
*Thm. 6.9.1 resp. Cor. 6.9.3) yields for all
i\geq 1

0\to\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathbb{k},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathbb{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})\overset{\sum_{v}\partial_{v}^{i}}{\longrightarrow}\bigoplus_{v}\mathrm{H}^{i-1}(\mathbb{k}_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{i-1}(\mathbb{k},\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Here,
v
ranges over all discrete valuations on
\mathbb{K}(t)
which are trivial on
\mathbb{K}
. Thus, they are given by non-zero prime ideals of
\mathbb{K}[t]
or by the infinite valuation corresponding to
t^{-1}
and
\mathbb{k}_{v}
is the finite extension of
\mathbb{K}
given by the unique monic generator of the ideal of
\mathbb{k}[t]
corresponding to
v
or
v=\infty
and its associated residue field is
\mathbb{k}
. The maps
\partial^{i}_{v}
are the so called
residue maps
, which are described in
[
14
]
*Const. 6.8.5 and factor through the completions
\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathbb{K}_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})
. The last map is the sum of the corestriction maps
\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathbb{k}_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathbb{k},\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Note that in contrast to the situation described in
[
14
]
*§6.9 we do not have to take into account Tate twists, as all fields under consideration contain a primitive
p^{\mathrm{th}}
-root of unity.

We now distinguish three cases:

•

G{k}(p)
is trivial:
Then
\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathbb{k},\mathbb{F}_{p})=0
for
i\geq 1
and the same holds for
\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathbb{k}_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Thus using the Faddeev sequence, we see that
G{K}
has
p
-cohomological dimension
1
and we get the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property by Example
1.17
.

•

G{k}(p)\neq 1
is finite:
In this case
p=2
and
G_{\mathbb{k}}(2)\cong C_{2}
by the pro-
p
version of Artin-Schreier theorem (cf.
[
4
]
) and furthermore, the characteristic of
\mathbb{k}
is
0
. Now by a theorem of L. van den Dries and P. Ribenboim
[
38
]
it follows that the sequence

\displaystyle 1\longrightarrow G_{\bar{\mathbb{k}}(t)}\longrightarrow G_{\mathbb{K}}\longrightarrow G_{\mathbb{k}}\longrightarrow 1

is split. We need some basic facts from étale cohomology, for which we refer to
[
26
]
. We identify
G_{\bar{\mathbb{k}}(t)}=\varprojlim\pi_{1}^{\rm\acute{e}t}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{\mathbb{k}}}\smallsetminus S,\bar{\eta})

where
S
ranges over the finite subsets closed points of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{\mathbb{k}}}
, which are stable under the
G_{\mathbb{k}}
action and
\bar{\eta}
is a fixed algebraic closure of the generic point. Then we get that

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(\bar{\mathbb{k}}(t),\mathbb{F}_{p})={\varinjlim}_{S}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\rm\acute{e}t}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{\mathbb{k}}}\smallsetminus S,\mathbb{F}_{p})={\varinjlim}_{S}\mathbb{F}_{p}[S],

from which one can deduce that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\bar{\mathbb{k}}(t),\mathbb{F}_{p})
is the permutation module generated by the discrete
G_{\mathbb{k}}
-set
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\bar{\mathbb{k}})
. The Faddeev sequence implies that
G_{\bar{\mathbb{k}}(t)}
has cohomological dimension
\leq 1
. Hence, Corollary
1.20
is applicable and we get the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property of
\mathbb{K}
.

•

G{k}(p)
is infinite:
In this case we have
G_{\mathbb{k}_{v}}(p)\neq 1
for all places
v
of
\mathbb{K}
and thus the corresponding completion
\mathbb{K}_{v}
has trivial
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cup radical by Proposition
2.4
.

Let now
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{\mathbb{K}})
. Then for any discrete valuation, we have
\alpha_{v}\!\smallsmile\!\beta=0
for every
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K}_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})
, by Lemma
2.2
, and thus
\alpha_{v}\in\mathcal{R}_{p}(\mathbb{K}_{v})
, which is trivial by Proposition
2.4
. Therefore,
\alpha
is contained in the kernel of the localization map

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to{\bigoplus}_{v}\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K}_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Assume that
\alpha\neq 0
and let
\mathbb{L}
be the field corresponding to
\ker(\alpha)
, then the completion
\mathbb{L}_{v}
coincides with
\mathbb{K}_{v}
for all
v
. In particular
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
is unramified at every
v
. Since every étale cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{k}}
comes from an étale cover of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{k})
we have
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{f}\mathbb{K}
for some
p
-extension
\mathbb{f}/\mathbb{k}
and hence for every
v
with residue field
\mathbb{k}_{v}=\mathbb{k}
we have
\mathbb{L}_{v}=\mathbb{f}\mathbb{K}_{v}\neq\mathbb{K}_{v}
. This yields
\alpha=0
and the desired statement.

∎

Now by applying the statement of Theorem
2.5
inductively, one deduces the following corollary:

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field containing a primitive
p^{\mathrm{th}}
-root of unity. If
\mathbb{K}/\mathbb{k}
is a purely transcendental extension (not necessarily of finite transcendence degree), then
\mathbb{K}
has the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property. We have
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{\mathbb{K}})=0
if the transcendence degree is at least
2
or
G_{\mathbb{k}}(p)
is infinite.

Remark 2.7
.

This yields a large class of fields for which the Elementary Type Conjecture is not applicable, as their maximal pro-
p
Galois groups are not finitely generated, but nevertheless have the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property.

2.3.
Fields not possessing the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property

Similar to the case
p=2
not every field has the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property. For a construction we follow the proof by K.J. Becher and D. B. Leep
[
3
]
*Lem. 4.7 and Lem. 4.8 replacing smooth conics by Severi–Brauer varieties of dimension
p-1
. For basic properties of Severi–Brauer varieties we refer to
[
14
]
*§5.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field containing a primitive
{p^{\rm th}}
-root of unity and
\mathbb{L}
a cyclic degree extension of degree
p
. Then there exists an extension
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}/\mathbb{K}
with the following properties:

(1)

\mathbb{K}^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{K}^{\rm sep}=\mathbb{K}
;

(2)

\mathbb{L}\mathbb{K}^{\prime}/\mathbb{L}
is purely transcendental;

(3)

The image of the restriction
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K}^{\prime},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is contained in the image of
\cores^{1}_{\mathbb{L}\mathbb{K}^{\prime},\mathbb{K}^{\prime}}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{L}\mathbb{K}^{\prime},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K}^{\prime},\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}{L}
be a set of representatives of the isomorphism classes of Severi–Brauer varieties of dimension
p-1
over
\mathbb{K}
having a rational point over
\mathbb{L}
. For a variety
Y/\mathbb{K}
, we denote by
\mathbb{K}(Y)
the function field of
Y
. Now we set

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}^{\prime}\coloneq\varinjlim_{S\subseteq\mathcal{B}{L}\,\text{ finite}}\mathbb{K}\big({\prod}_{X\in S}X\big)

Since each Severi–Brauer variety is geometrically integral, so are their finite products. By
[
22
]
*Chapter 3, Cor. 2.14 this implies (
1
).

For each
X\in\mathcal{B}{L}
we have
X{L}\cong\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{L}}^{p-1}
and thus
\mathbb{L}\mathbb{K}(X)/\mathbb{L}
is purely transcendental, which implies the same for finite products. This implies (
2
).

For (
3
), we write
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{K}(\sqrt[p]{\alpha})
for a suitable
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}
. Let
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
and
\beta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K}^{\prime},\mathbb{F}_{p})
be its restriction. Then, by Lemma
1.7
, we have
\beta^{\prime}\in\im(\cores^{1}_{\mathbb{L}\mathbb{K}^{\prime},\mathbb{K}^{\prime}})
if and only if
\beta^{\prime}\smallsmile\kappa(\alpha)=0
. Consider the cup product
\beta\smallsmile\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
, which vanishes under the restriction to
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{L},\mathbb{F}_{p})
, and thus corresponds to a Severi–Brauer variety
X\in\mathcal{B}{L}
. Since
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
contains
\mathbb{K}(X)
, we see that
X_{\mathbb{K}^{\prime}}\cong\mathbb{P}^{p-1}_{\mathbb{K}^{\prime}}
and therefore the restriction of
\beta\smallsmile\alpha
to
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{K}^{\prime},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is trivial. This finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field containing a primitive
{p^{\rm th}}
-root of unity. Then there exists an extension
\mathbb{K}_{\infty}/\mathbb{K}
such that
\mathbb{K}_{\infty}
does not have the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property.

Proof.

We can assume without loss of generality that
\mathbb{K}
has a cyclic degree
p
-extension
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
and that
\mathbb{K}
contains
\zeta_{p}
. Otherwise, we simply replace
\mathbb{K}
by
\mathbb{K}(\zeta_{p},t)
. Write
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{K}(\sqrt[p]{\alpha})
and
\mathbb{K}_{0}\coloneq\mathbb{K}
. Then we define inductively
\mathbb{K}_{i+1}\coloneq\mathbb{K}_{i}^{\prime}
with
\mathbb{K}_{i}^{\prime}
as in Lemma
2.8
for the cyclic extension
\mathbb{L}\mathbb{K}_{i}/\mathbb{K}_{i}
, which has degree
p
as
\mathbb{L}\cap\mathbb{K}_{i}=\mathbb{K}
by (
1
). Finally, we set
\mathbb{K}_{\infty}=\varinjlim\mathbb{K}_{i}
.

By Lemma
2.8
, we see that
\mathbb{L}\mathbb{K}_{\infty}/\mathbb{K}_{\infty}
is purely transcendental, and thus by Corollary
2.6
we conclude
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{\mathbb{L}\mathbb{K}_{\infty}})=0
. It remains to show that
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{{\mathbb{K}}_{\infty}})\neq 0
. In fact, we will show that the class of
\alpha
defines a non-trivial element in
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{\mathbb{L}\mathbb{K}_{\infty}})
. The non-triviality is clear.

By the second statement of Lemma
2.8
, we see that for each
\beta_{i}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K}_{i},\mathbb{F}_{p})
we have
\alpha\smallsmile\res_{\mathbb{K}_{i},\mathbb{K}_{i+1}}(\beta)=0
. Now, since
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K}_{\infty},\mathbb{F}_{p})=\varinjlim\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K}_{i},\mathbb{F}_{p})
, we conclude that
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{{\mathbb{K}}_{\infty}})
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

The construction by K.J. Becher and D.B. Leep — and, consequently, our proof — heavily relies on the construction of highly transcendental extensions.
It would be interesting to investigate whether there exists an algebraic extension
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}/\mathbb{K}
such that

has the

-Kijima–Nishi property, while

does not.

3.
Pro-
p
groups of elementary type

3.1.
Pro-
p
groups of elementary type and orientations

In order to define the family of pro-
p
groups of elementary type, it is useful to recall the notion of an orientation of a pro-
p
group.
Let
1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
denote the multiplicative group of principal units of the ring of
p
-adic integers — i.e.,
1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\left\{{1+p\lambda}\ \big|\ {\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.
An
orientation of a pro-
p
group
G
is a morphism of pro-
p
groups
\theta\colon G\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
; the pair
(G,\theta)
is called an
oriented pro-
p
group
(in
[
10
]
, an oriented pro-
p
group is called a
cyclotomic pro-
p
pair
).

Every Demuškin group
G
comes endowed with a canonical orientation
\theta_{G}\colon G\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, as described by J. Labute in
[
20
, § 3]
, which is precisely the cyclotomic character (whence the name) if
G=G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
for some field
\mathbb{K}
containing a primitive
{p^{\rm th}}
-root of unity — see also
[
29
, § 5.3]
.
For the purposes of this work we do not need a description of such an orientation.

Definition 3.1
.

The family of pro-
p
groups of elementary type is the smallest family of oriented pro-
p
groups
(G,\theta)
with
G
finitely generated, containing:

(a)

oriented pro-
p
groups
(F,\theta)
, where
F
is a finitely generated free pro-
p
group, and
\theta\colon F\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is any orientation,

(b)

oriented pro-
p
groups
(G,\theta_{G})
, where
G
is a Demuškin group, and
\theta_{G}\colon G\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is its canonical orientation;

and satisfying the following:

(c)

if
(G_{1},\theta_{1})
and
(G_{2},\theta_{2})
are of elementary type, then also
(G_{1}\ast_{p}G_{2},\theta)
— where
\theta\colon G_{1}\ast_{p}G_{2}\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the orientation induced by
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
via the universal property of free pro-
p
products — is of elementary type,

(d)

if
(G_{0},\theta_{0})
is of elementary type, then also
(N\rtimes G_{0},\theta)
— where
N\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

ghg^{-1}=h^{\theta_{0}(g)}\qquad\text{for all }g\in G_{0},\>h\in N,

and
\theta\colon(N\rtimes G_{0})\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the orientation satisfying
\theta|_{G_{0}}=\theta
and
\theta(h)=1
for all
h\in N
— is of elementary type.

The subsequent subsections aim to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(G,\theta)
be a group of elementary type. Then
G
has the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property.

We quickly summarize the strategy of the proof. By Proposition
2.1
the statement is true for Demuškin groups and by Example
1.17
for free pro-
p
groups. Thus, it remains to show that the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property is preserved under the operations described in
(c)
and
(d)
. This will be done in Proposition
3.7
and Corollary
3.10
.

Remark 3.3
.

As stated in the Introduction, in
[
7
]
R.P. Dario sketches a proof for a positive answer to the “H-conjecture” under the assumption that the maximal pro-2 Galois group of
\mathbb{K}
is a pro-2 group of elementary type, considering the behavior of the Kaplansky radical with respect to free pro-2 products and cyclotomic semidirect products applied to the maximal pro-2 Galois group of the fields taken into consideration.

Before proceeding to the proof, we state that groups of elementary type satisfy a much stronger property than just the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property. This is due to the fact, that each open subgroup of a group of elementary type is still of elementary type:

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group of elementary type, then for any open subgroup
U
of
G
, the map
c_{U,G}:\mathcal{R}_{p}(U)\to\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)
is surjective.

Proof.

Choose a sequence of open subgroups
G=U_{0}\supseteq U_{1}\supseteq...\supseteq U_{r}=U
, such that
[U_{i}:U_{i+1}]=p
for each
i
. Then each map
\mathcal{R}_{p}(U_{i+1})\to\mathcal{R}_{p}(U_{i})
is surjective, as each
U_{i}
— by being of elementary type — has the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property by Theorem
3.2
. Hence, their composition is also surjective, which coincides with the claimed one, by transitivity of the corestriction.
∎

We tried to find more examples of groups satisfying this stronger property, but every (finitely generated) example constructed later turned out to be (virtually) of elementary type. In the following remark, we explain this observation for certain pro-
p
subgroups of profinite completions of limit groups:

Remarks 3.5
.

If

is a limit group (e.g. the fundamental group of an orientable closed surface), i.e., a finitely generated fully residually free group (see the definition and example in
[

]
), we can show that any finitely generated pro-

subgroup of

satisfies the

-Kijima–Nishi property by showing that these groups are of elementary type (see
[

]
). It is shown in
[

(jointly with Corollary

) that if

G=A\amalg_{C}B

where

is a self-centralizing procyclic subgroup of a pro-
p
limit group

(see the definition in
[
35
]
) and

contains

as a direct summand, then for any subgroup

of

,

satisfies the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property and

is of elementary type (see
[

]
and
[

, Thm. 1.4]
).

3.2.
Free pro-
p
products

The following lemma shows that
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cup radicals of free pro-
p

groups can be completely described in terms of the radicals of their free factors. For the definition of free pro-
p
product of an infinite family of pro-
p
groups, we refer to
[
27

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G_{i}
for
i\in I
be a family of pro-
p
groups and let
G=\coprod_{i\in I}G_{i}
be its free pro-
p
product. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)\cong\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{i}).

Proof.

By
[
27
]
*Thm. 4.1.4 we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle\rho^{n}:\mathrm{H}^{n}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathrm{H}^{n}(G_{i},\mathbb{F}_{p}),\quad\alpha\longmapsto\sum_{i\in I}\res_{G,G_{i}}^{n}(\alpha).

Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{n}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
and
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{m}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
and write
\rho(\alpha)=\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}
,
\rho(\beta)=\sum_{i\in I}\beta_{i}
. As the restriction maps commute with the cup product, we have

\displaystyle\rho^{n+m}(\alpha\smallsmile\beta)=\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\smallsmile\beta_{i}.

In the light of that, it is not hard to see that
\rho^{1}
induces the desired isomorphism; indeed,
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
belongs to
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)
if and only if all of its components
\alpha_{i}
are contained in
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{i})
.
∎

As a consequence, one has the the following result for free pro-

Let

First, assume that each

Let

*Thm. D.3.1 or

\displaystyle U\cong F\ast_{p}\Big(\coprod_{i\in I,s_{i}\in S_{i}}(G_{i}^{s_{i}}\cap U)\Big).

[

contains

In practice, it is not straightforward to check that a pro-

The action of a pro-

-Kijima–Nishi property.

Finitely generated pro-

of the fundamental group of a compact

(see
[

). So, verifying if

3.3.
Cyclotomic semidirect products

The following proposition shows that so-called
p
-almost direct products
have trivial
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cup radical. A semidirect product
N\rtimes Q
, is called
p
-almost direct, if
Q
acts trivially on
\mathrm{H}^{1}(N,\mathbb{F}_{p})
. If
N
is already a pro-
p
group, this is equivalent to asking that the action of
Q
on
N/\Phi(N)
is trivial. Note that any direct product is
p
-almost direct.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
G=N\rtimes Q
be a
p
-almost direct product of profinite groups such that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(Q,\mathbb{F}_{p})
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}(N,\mathbb{F}_{p})
are non-trivial, then
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)=0
.

Proof.

Consider the following Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence

\displaystyle E_{2}^{s,t}=\mathrm{H}^{s}(Q,\mathrm{H}^{t}(N,\mathbb{F}_{p}))\Longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{s+t}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Since
G
is a semidirect product we get by
[
27
]
*Pro. 2.4.5 that all the differentials
d^{*,1}_{2}
are trivial. This implies that

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(Q,\mathbb{F}_{p})\oplus\mathrm{H}^{1}(N,\mathbb{F}_{p})^{Q}\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(Q,\mathbb{F}_{p})\oplus\mathrm{H}^{1}(N,\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Note that this splitting is canonical, since
G
is a semidirect product. Furthermore, on
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
there is a decreasing filtration
F^{*}\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
such that the following sequence is exact:

\displaystyle 0\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(Q,\mathbb{F}_{p})\to F^{1}\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\overset{\pi}{\to}\mathrm{H}^{1}(Q,\mathrm{H}^{1}(N))\to 0

The canonical map
\mathrm{H}^{1}(Q,\mathbb{F}_{p})\otimes\mathrm{H}^{1}(N,\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(Q,\mathrm{H}^{1}(N,\mathbb{F}_{p}))
is injective and thus the same holds for the multiplication map
E_{2}^{1,0}\otimes E_{2}^{0,1}\to E_{2}^{1,1}
.

Now, assume that
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)
and write
\alpha=\alpha_{Q}+\alpha_{N}
. For every
\beta=\beta_{Q}+\beta_{N}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
we have
\alpha\smallsmile\beta=0
and hence in particular
\alpha\smallsmile\beta\in F^{1}\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
. We have

\displaystyle\pi(\alpha\smallsmile\beta)=\alpha_{Q}\otimes\beta_{N}-\beta_{Q}\otimes\alpha_{N}.

Now if
\alpha_{Q}\neq 0
, then we choose
\beta=0+\beta_{N}
for some
\beta_{N}\neq 0
and if
\alpha_{N}\neq 0
, we choose
\beta=\beta_{Q}+0
for some
\beta_{Q}\neq 0
. In either of the cases
\pi(\alpha\smallsmile\beta)\neq 0
, contradicting
\alpha\smallsmile\beta=0
. Therefore
\alpha=0
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
G_{0}
be a non-trivial pro-
p
group (not necessarily finitely generated) and
\theta:G_{0}\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
an orientation, and set
G=N\rtimes_{\theta}G_{0}
for a free abelian pro-
p
group
N
. Then
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)=0
, and therefore
G
satisfies the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property.

Proof.

Since
\theta(G_{0})\subseteq 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
the action of
G_{0}
on
\mathrm{H}^{1}(N,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is trivial and thus the conditions of Proposition
3.9
are satisfied.
∎

The
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cup radical of free profinite constructions

In this section we will investigate the behavior of the

-cup radical in free profinite constructions and, in particular, address a natural question arising from Proposition

4.1.
Amalgamated products and

-extensions

For an overview on amalgamated free pro-

First, we recall the definition of amalgamated free pro-

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G_{1}=\left\langle{V_{1}}\,\big|\,{R_{1}}\right\rangle

If

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{p})\oplus\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{1}(H,\mathbb{F}_{p})}

We adopt Serre’s definition of a finite graph. While the definition by L. Ribes and P. Zalesskii (see

Definition 4.8
.

A finite (Serre) graph

\Gamma
consists of a finite set
V(\Gamma)
, a finite set

and two maps

E(\Gamma)\to V(\Gamma)\times V(\Gamma),\quad e\mapsto(d_{0}(e),d_{1}(e))\quad\text{and}\quad E(\Gamma)\to E(\Gamma),\quad e\mapsto\bar{e}

satisfying the following conditions: for each

we have
\bar{\bar{e}}=e
,
\bar{e}\neq e
and

. A finite graph is

if any two vertices can be connected by a path

, i.e., they are extremities of a sequence of adjacent edges; a connected graph is a

if it has no reduced closed path

for all

.

Definition 4.9

A

p

where

Let

be a maximal subtree of

. Then the

G_{\Gamma,f}
has the
p
-Kijima–Nishi property.

Proof.

Let
I
be the set of isolated vertices of
\Gamma
, and let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be the induced subgraph of
\Gamma
spanned by
V\smallsetminus I
. If
F
is the free pro-
p
group on
I
, then
G_{\Gamma,f}
splits as a free pro-
p
product
F\amalg G_{\Gamma^{\prime},f^{\prime}}

(here
f^{\prime}
denotes the restriction of
f
to
\Gamma^{\prime}
), concluding the proof in the light of Proposition
3.7
.
∎

5.2.
\Delta
-RAAGs

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a finite graph, and let
G_{\Gamma}
be its associated pro-
p
RAAG. Denote by
\Delta
the group of order two, and let
x_{0}
be its generator. Let
{\bf z}:V\to G_{\Gamma}
be a set-theoretic function such that the induced map
a_{\bf z}:G_{\Gamma}\to G_{\Gamma}
defined by
v\mapsto{\bf z}_{v}v^{-1}{\bf z}_{v}^{-1}
is an automorphism of
G_{\Gamma}
of order two.

These data define a
\Delta
-action
\delta_{\bf z}:\Delta\to\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\Gamma})
defined by
\delta_{\bf z}(x_{0})=a_{\bf z}
, and hence a split extension
G_{\Gamma,\bf z}\coloneq G_{\Gamma}\rtimes_{\delta_{\bf z}}\Delta
, called the
\Delta
-RAAG associated with
\Gamma
and
\bf z
(see
[
15
]
).

Proposition 5.3
.

If
\Gamma
is a non-empty finite graph, and
G_{\Gamma,\bf z}
is a
\Delta
-RAAG defined on
\Gamma
, then
\mathcal{R}_{2}(G_{\Gamma,\bf z})=0
, and hence
G_{\Gamma,\bf z}
satisfies the
2
-Kijima–Nishi property.

Proof.

Since
\Delta
acts by conjugation with the elements
\mathbf{z}_{v}
on the generators
v
of
G_{\Gamma}
we see that the induced action on
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{\Gamma},\mathbb{F}_{2})
is trivial and hence
G_{\Gamma,\mathbf{z}}
splits as a
2
-almost direct product and, by Proposition
3.9
, its
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-cup radical is trivial.
∎

As a consequence, we deduce case
(v)
from Theorem
B
: if
\mathbb{K}
is a formally real Pythagorean field of finite type — i.e., if
-1
is not a square, the sum of two squares is a square, and
\mathbb{K}^{\times}/\mathbb{K}^{\times 2}\simeq\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{K},\mathbb{F}_{2})
is a finite group —, then its maximal pro-
2
Galois group is a
\Delta
-RAAG (cf.
[
15
, Thm. 2.1]
), and hence
\mathbb{K}
satisfies the
2
-Kijima–Nishi property.

6.
A generalization in the absence of roots of unity

If a field
\mathbb{K}
does not contain a primitive root of unity, then the definition of the radical already poses problems. It is not hard to see that, for example,
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}})=\mathbb{F}_{p}
when
p\nmid\ell-1
. In this section we adapt our definition of the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cup radical to this situation and summarize generalizations of some the results given in Section
2
to fields that do not contain a primitive
{p^{\rm th}}
-root of unity.

We first generalize our definition for the radical for
p
-oriented profinite groups
. These are pairs
(G,\theta)
, where
G
is a profinite group and
\theta:G\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
a continuous homomorphism. For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define
\mathbb{F}_{p}(k)
to be the discrete
G
-module with
\sigma(x)\coloneq\theta(\sigma)^{k}\cdot x
. More generally, if
M
is a discrete
p
-torsion
G
-module, one defines the
k^{\rm th}
-Tate twist of
M
to be
M(k)\coloneq M\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}(k)
.

We now define

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{p}(G,\theta)\coloneq\left\{{\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})}\ \big|\ {\alpha\smallsmile\beta=0\text{ for all }\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}(1))}\right\}.

If
G=G_{\mathbb{K}}
for a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
\neq p
, then there is a natural
p
-orientation
\theta_{\mathbb{K}}
given by the action of
G
on
\mu_{p^{\infty}}(\mathbb{K}^{\rm sep})\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, as
\Aut(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Then the cup product between elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mu_{p})
can be identified with the map

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\otimes\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mu_{p})\to{}_{p}\Br(\mathbb{K}),\quad\chi\otimes\kappa(b)\mapsto[(\chi,b)],

where
(\chi,b)
is the cyclic algebra associated with
b\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}
and
\chi
(see
[
14
]
*Const. 2.5.1 and Prop. 4.7.1). Thus, this generalizes Remark
1.8
.

The next lemma is clear from the definitions and shows that
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G,\theta)
essentially only depends on the reduction of
\theta
modulo
p
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
be two
p
-orientations on a profinite group
G
. If
\theta(\sigma)\equiv\theta^{\prime}(\sigma)\pmod{p}
for every
\sigma\in G
, then
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G,\theta)=\mathcal{R}_{p}(G,\theta^{\prime})
.

In particular, if
\im\theta\subseteq 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, then
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G,\theta)=\mathcal{R}_{p}(G)
.

We generalize Corollary
3.10
to this more general context. We give a full proof of the statement, as it highlights the techniques quite well.

Proposition 6.2

(Cyclotomic semidirect products)
.

Let
(G_{0},\theta)
be a
p
-oriented profinite group. Define
G\coloneq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rtimes_{\theta}G_{0}
and let
\theta^{\prime}:G\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
be the composition of
\theta
with the projection onto the second factor.

Then
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G,\theta^{\prime})=0
. In particular, for any
p
-henselian field
\mathbb{K}
, whose residue field is of characteristic
\neq p
and not
p
-closed one has
\mathcal{R}_{p}(G_{\mathbb{K}},\theta_{\mathbb{K}})=0
.

Proof.

We can without loss of generality assume that
\theta\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, since otherwise the semidirect product is
p
-almost direct and hence the statement follows by Proposition
3.9
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then we have a spectral sequence

\displaystyle E^{s,t}_{2}(k)\coloneq\mathrm{H}^{s}(G_{0},\mathrm{H}^{t}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p}(k)))\Longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{s+t}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}(k))

Note that
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p}(k))\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}(k)
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p}(k))\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}(k-1)
as
G_{0}
-modules. By
[
27
]
*Prop. 2.4.5, the spectral sequence degenerates, and we have for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\to\mathrm{H}^{n}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p}(k))\to\mathrm{H}^{n}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}(k))\overset{\partial^{n}(k)}{\to}\mathrm{H}^{n-1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p}(k-1))\to 0.

From this, we immediately see that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Note that for
k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
there is a canonical product
E^{*,*}_{2}(k)\otimes E_{2}^{*,*}(k^{\prime})\to E^{*,*}_{2}(k+k^{\prime})
coming from the isomorphism
\mathbb{F}_{p}(k+k^{\prime})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}(k)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}(k^{\prime})
.

We note that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}(1))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}(1))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{0}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\times}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial^{1}(1)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\smallsmile}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial^{2}(1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\times}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\smallsmile}

By the identification
\mathrm{H}^{0}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}
the cup product in the lower row is just multiplication by scalars.

Given
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{p}(G,\theta^{\prime})
, we let
\alpha_{0}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p})
be the unique element such that
\smash{\inf^{1}}(\alpha_{0})=\alpha
. Now choose
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}(1))
such that
\lambda\coloneq\partial^{1}(1)(\beta)\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle 0=\partial^{2}(1)(\alpha\smallsmile\beta)=\alpha_{0}\smallsmile\partial^{1}(1)(\beta)=\lambda\alpha_{0}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p})

and thus
\alpha_{0}=0
. This shows the first statement. The second one follows from the first one by writing
G{K}
as semidirect product of the absolute Galois group of the residue field with a procyclic group (see
[
33
]
*Exercises II §4), where the action is given by a variant of
\theta{K}
. The same arguments as above apply in this case.
∎

We recover several of the statements of Section
2
in this more general setting, leading us to believe that this generalized definition of the radical is indeed the right notion in the case where
\mathbb{K}
does not contain a primitive
{p^{\rm th}}
-root of unity

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be either of the following fields and
p
a prime different from the characteristic of
\mathbb{K}
, then
\mathcal{R}_{p}(\mathbb{K},\theta_{\mathbb{K}})=0
:

(i)

local field;

(ii)

global field;

(iii)

a purely transcendental extension of a field
\mathbb{k}
, for which
G_{\mathbb{k}}(p)
is infinite.

Proof.

The case
(i)
follows immediately from local Tate duality and the proofs of
(ii)
and
(iii)
follow verbatim the ones of Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
2.5
.
∎
