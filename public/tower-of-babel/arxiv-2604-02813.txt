Multi-Band Patch Antenna Array for Out-of-Band Aided Millimeter Wave Communication
Faruk Pasic 1 , Jure Soklič 2 , Robert Langwieser 1 , Stefan Schwarz 1 and Christoph F. Mecklenbräuker 1

Abstract
Future wireless communication systems will integrate both sub-6 GHz and MMW ( MMW ) frequency bands within multi-antenna architectures to meet the increasing demand for high data rates. In such multi-band systems, reliable information obtained from the sub-6 GHz band can be exploited to support communication at MMW frequencies. To ensure that both systems experience similar multi-path propagation effects, the sub-6 GHz and MMW antenna arrays have to be co-located and precisely aligned. However, such a configuration may adversely alter the radiation characteristics of the arrays, potentially degrading their performance. In this paper, we investigate the impact of positioning a MMW antenna structure in front of a sub-6 GHz antenna structure. Through both simulations and measurements, we evaluate how the presence of the MMW structure affects the radiation pattern of the sub-6 GHz one. The results demonstrate that the influence of the MMW structure on the sub-6 GHz performance is minor, indicating that co-located configurations are feasible with negligible degradation.

I
Introduction

Due to the high utilization of conventional sub-6 GHz frequency bands, the available bandwidth is limited, which constrains data transmission rates.
In contrast,
MMW
frequency bands (24 GHz – 300 GHz)
[
1
]
offer significantly wider bandwidths, making them well-suited to support the growing demand for high data rates
[
7
]
.
Furthermore,
MMW
systems are increasingly being deployed in conjunction with sub-6 GHz systems to enable multi-band communication and improve reliability
[
10
,
6
]
.

Compared to
MMW
bands, which suffer from high propagation losses, sub-6 GHz bands exhibit more favorable propagation characteristics, resulting in more robust link reliability.
This makes them a valuable source of out-of-band information that can be leveraged to support communication at
MMW
frequencies.
So far, numerous methods that exploit sub-6 GHz out-of-band information to aid
MMW
communication have been proposed
[
2
,
9
,
3
,
8
]
.
These methods employ co-located and aligned sub-6 GHz and
MMW
antenna arrays (sharing the same central point) to ensure that both systems experience similar multi-path propagation effects.

However, the design of such co-located and aligned multi-band antenna arrays poses significant practical challenges.
A common implementation involves placing the
MMW
array in front of the sub-6 GHz array.
While this configuration allows for spatial alignment, it can adversely affect the radiation pattern of the sub-6 GHz array, potentially degrading its performance.
Therefore, it is crucial to evaluate the performance of such multi-band antenna structures under realistic hardware constraints.

II
Multi-Band Antenna Array Design

We consider a multi-band antenna array composed of co-located sub-6 GHz and
MMW
arrays, operating simultaneously in the radiative far-field regime.
The sub-6 GHz array comprises
M^{\left({\rm s}\right)}
elements, while the
MMW
array consists of
M^{\left({\rm m}\right)}
elements (see
Fig.

1
).
The arrays are precisely aligned, sharing the same geometric center, with an inter-array spacing of
h_{\rm a}
, as illustrated in
Fig.

1
.
The sub-6 GHz and
MMW
arrays are implemented as
ULA
of patch antenna elements.
In both arrays, adjacent antenna elements are mutually separated by
\Delta d^{\left({\rm b}\right)}=\,0.5\,\lambda^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
, where
\lambda^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
denotes the wavelength corresponding to the respective frequency band.
Each patch antenna element has dimensions
l_{\rm p}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\times w_{\rm p}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
and is fed by a microstrip line of width
w_{\rm m}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
with inset feed dimensions
l_{\rm cut}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
and
w_{\rm cut}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
.

Fig.

2
shows top and bottom layers of a single patch antenna.
The patch elements are designed to resonate at the carrier frequency
f_{\rm c}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
of their respective systems.
The patch elements are etched on a substrate of thickness
h_{\rm s}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
with copper thickness
h_{\rm c}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
, relative dielectric constant
\varepsilon_{\rm r}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
and dissipation factor
\tan\delta^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
.
The total array dimensions are given by
l_{\rm s}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\times w_{\rm s}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
, where
l_{\rm s}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}=\Delta d^{\left({\rm b}\right)}+\lambda^{\left({\rm b}\right)}/2
and
w_{\rm s}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}=M^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\Delta d^{\left({\rm b}\right)}+\lambda^{\left({\rm b}\right)}/2.

Moreover, we validate the accuracy of our antenna design in terms of return loss through simulations in ANSYS HFSS
[
5
]
.
For the sub-6 GHz array, the antenna resonates at 2.55 GHz with a return loss of 18.47 dB, whereas the
MMW
array resonates at 25.5 GHz with a return loss of 13.16 dB.
These results demonstrate satisfactory impedance matching at the target frequencies in both frequency bands.
Furthermore, we complement the simulations results with experimental measurements.
A single sub-6 GHz patch element is manufactured in-house according to the design parameters listed in
Table

I
.
We measure the return loss of the fabricated patch antenna using a
VNA
(
VNA
) (Rohde&Schwarz ZVA8) under controlled laboratory conditions.
The measured results show that the antenna resonates at 2.5625 GHz with a return loss of 18.22 dB, corresponding to only a 0.5% deviation from the target frequency of 2.55 GHz.
This small shift can be attributed to fabrication tolerances and substrate parameter variations, which are common in practical antenna prototyping.
At the exact target frequency of 2.55 GHz, the measured return loss is 12.96 dB, which still indicates satisfying impedance matching and acceptable performance for practical operation.

IV
Conclusion

Both simulation and measurement results confirm that the presence of the
MMW
array has only a minor impact on the radiation characteristics of both a single sub-6 GHz patch element and a sub-6 GHz
ULA
.
Similarly, the sub-6 GHz array has a negligible influence on the main-lobe performance of the
MMW
array, while beneficially reducing back-lobe radiation.
These findings demonstrate that co-located sub-6 GHz and
MMW

ULA
configurations are feasible and introduce only negligible performance degradation.
Nevertheless, future work should extend this analysis to uniform planar array configurations to assess radiation effects in two-dimensional antenna structures.
