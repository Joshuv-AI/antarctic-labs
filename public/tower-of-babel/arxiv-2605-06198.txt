Abstract
We address the joint estimation of the number of targets and their direction-of-arrivals (DoAs) using antenna arrays. Target-number estimation can be formulated as a model-order selection problem and solved with the information theoretic criteria (ITC) . The ITC minimize an objective function that balances a likelihood term and a complexity penalty. However, direct application of the ITC requires maximum-likelihood DoA estimates for each candidate model order, which is computationally prohibitive because it entails a multidimensional search over all angle combinations. To reduce complexity, many radar processing exploit greedy methods such as orthogonal least squares (OLS) . In this paper, we explore three distinct methods to integrate the ITC model-order selection into the OLS estimation procedure for joint target-number and DoA estimation. Specifically, we propose the disjoint rank-based, the joint selection-based, and the hybrid rank-and-selection-based ITC - OLS algorithms. Each algorithm is derived under both the Akaike information criterion (AIC) and the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) frameworks. Numerical simulations show that the proposed hybrid ITC - OLS algorithm consistently outperforms both the other proposed variants and a baseline method from the literature.



I
Introduction

In many radar applications, antenna arrays are used to estimate the
direction-of-arrivals (DoAs)
of multiple targets from noisy observations
[
16
,
9
,
18
,
15
]
.
Although the
maximum likelihood (ML)
estimator can asymptotically attain the
Cramér-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB)
, its computational complexity grows exponentially with the number of targets; consequently, it is often unsuitable for real-time operation
[
10
]
.
Low-complexity heuristics such as MUSIC are commonly used in
DoAs
estimation scenarios
[
19
]
.
However, these subspace-based methods degrade when target signals are highly correlated
[
15
]
.
Alternatively, greedy techniques like
orthogonal least squares (OLS)
, can be used as fast approximate solvers for the above
ML
problem
[
5
,
2
,
24
]
.
This method iteratively build a solution by selecting one
DoA
at a time based on a least squares criterion.
This significantly reduces the computational burden compared to exhaustive search methods such as the
ML
estimator.
In the litterature, the number of targets, which typically amounts to the number of steps in the greedy algorithm, is often assumed to be known
[
15
]
.
However, in practice, it is usually an unknown parameter that must be estimated from the observed data.

Building upon this observation, this work addresses the joint estimation of the number of targets and their
DoAs
within an
OLS
-based framework.
A common strategy is to stop the greedy search using a residual-energy criterion
[
3
,
12
,
17
,
6
]
.
Yet fixed-threshold stopping remains difficult to tune in practice because the optimal threshold depends on the noise and received signal levels.
In contrast, within the
ML
framework, target-number estimation is naturally formulated as a model-order selection problem.
It can be solved by the
information theoretic criteria (ITC)
that select the model minimizing an objective function that balances a likelihood term against a model complexity penalty
[
1
,
20
,
23
,
21
,
14
,
7
,
4
]
.
The
Akaike information criterion (AIC)
and the
Bayesian information criterion (BIC)
are two widely used
ITC
that differ by their model complexity penalty terms
[
21
]
.
Nevertheless, a direct use of
ITC
requires
ML
parameter estimation for every candidate model order, i.e., for each possible number of targets; this requirement again limits real-time applicability.

Given the identified gaps in the litterature, our key contributions
are the following:

•

We propose
three novel algorithms
that combine the
ITC
target-number estimation with the
OLS
greedy estimation of target
DoAs
:
disjoint, joint, and hybrid
ITC
-
OLS
. Each algorithm is derived under both the
AIC
and the
BIC
formulations.

•

We show that the need for
ML
estimates in
ITC
-
OLS
can be circumvented by introducing an

-correction parameter
in the
ITC
objective. This parameter
controls the tradeoff between hit rate and false-alarm rate at high
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)
while preserving performance in other regimes.

•

The proposed
ITC
-
OLS
algorithms are applied in a
practical scenario
considering a
passive radar (PR)
setup
based on unknown
orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM)
data frames. The
PR

estimates
DoAs
without decoding the transmitted symbols
.

•

Through numerical simulations, we demonstrate that among the proposed algorithms,
hybrid
ITC
-
OLS
provides the
best overall performance

the
AIC
-
OLS
consistently outperforms the

-
OLS
.

\mathrm{SNR}=\frac{\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}|\alpha_{k}|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}.

III
Joint Target Number and DoA Estimation

This section presents three algorithms that combine the
ITC
for target-number detection with the
OLS
greedy estimation of target
DoAs
.
In this context,
ITC
estimate
K
by minimizing an objective function balancing two terms: one likelihood term that quantifies the fit of the model to the observed data, and one model complexity penalty term that discourages overfitting by penalizing a higher number of targets.
In this work, the term
ITC
refers to both the
AIC
and the
BIC
criteria, which differ by their model complexity penalty terms.
The three approaches are detailed in the following subsections, and their performance is compared in Section
IV
.

III-A

Disjoint rank-based
ITC
-
OLS

In this disjoint approach, the number of targets is first estimated using rank-based
ITC
, agnostically of the
DoA
estimator.
Building upon this estimate,
OLS
is then used to estimate the target
DoAs
for the inferred target number.

III-A
1
Target Number Estimation with Rank-based
ITC

The rank-based
ITC
estimates the number of targets
K
by analyzing the rank of the sample covariance matrix
[
23
]
,

\mathbf{R}\textstyle=\frac{1}{DQ}\sum_{d=0}^{D-1}\sum_{q=0}^{Q-1}\mathbf{y}_{d,q}\ {\mathbf{y}}^{\dagger}_{d,q},\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times M}.

(7)

Letting
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{M}
be the ordered eigenvalues of
\mathbf{R}
, the rank is estimated as

\widehat{K}=\argmin_{\widetilde{k}}\text{ITC}^{\text{Rank}}(\widetilde{k})
.
The discrete function of the rank
\text{ITC}^{\text{Rank}}
is defined as

\text{ITC}^{\text{Rank}}(\widetilde{k})=-C(\widetilde{k})\log\left(\frac{\prod_{i=\widetilde{k}+1}^{M}\lambda_{i}^{1/(M-\widetilde{k})}}{\frac{1}{(M-\widetilde{k})}\sum_{i=\widetilde{k}+1}^{M}\lambda_{i}}\right)+\ \nu(\widetilde{k}),

(8)

with
C(\widetilde{k})=2DQ(M-\widetilde{k})
, and with the model complexity penalty term
\nu(\widetilde{k})
defined as
\nu_{\text{AIC}}(\widetilde{k})=2\widetilde{k}(2M-\widetilde{k})
for
AIC
and
\nu_{\text{BIC}}(\widetilde{k})=\widetilde{k}(2M-\widetilde{k})\log(DQ)
for
BIC
.
The likelihood term in (
8
) evaluates how well the observed sample covariance matrix
\mathbf{R}
fits with a rank
\widetilde{k}
matrix, while
\nu(\widetilde{k})
penalizes higher
\widetilde{k}
, corresponding to a higher number of targets.

III-B

Joint Selection-based
ITC
-
OLS

In contrast to the decoupled rank-based
ITC
approach, prior works have derived selection-based
ITC
that depend on the
ML
estimates of the target
DoAs

{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}}_{k}^{\text{ML}}
for each candidate model order
k

[
21
,
18
]
.
These
ITC
criteria have a likelihood term that evaluates the fit of the model defined in (
5
) for
k
targets to the received signal vectors
\mathbf{y}_{d,q}
, penalized by a model complexity term as follows:

\text{ITC}^{\text{\lx@glossaries@gls@link{main}{ml}{{{}}ML} }}(k)=\frac{-2}{\sigma^{2}}\Tr\left[\mathbf{P}^{\bot}\big({\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}}_{k}^{\text{ML}}\big)\ \mathbf{R}\right]+\ \eta(k).

(12)

The penalties are defined as
\eta_{\text{AIC}}(k)=2k(1+2DQ)
for
AIC
and
\eta_{\text{BIC}}(k)=k(1+2DQ)\log(DQ)
for
BIC
.
However, computing
ML
estimates for every candidate number of targets is computationally prohibitive.

To overcome this issue, we propose to use the greedy
OLS
algorithm to provide
DoA
estimate and introduce a modified
ITC
criterion within the
OLS
iterations.
This is achieved by conditioning the update step in Algorithm
1
on the value of the following proposed
ITC
-
OLS
objective at each iteration
k
,

\text{ITC}^{\text{\lx@glossaries@gls@link{main}{ols}{{{}}OLS} }}(k)=\frac{-2}{\max(\sigma^{2},\sigma_{c}^{2})}\Tr\left[\mathbf{P}^{\bot}\big({\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}}_{k}\big)\ \mathbf{R}\right]+\ \eta(k).

(13)

Because
OLS
estimates
{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}}_{k}
are available at each step rather than
ML
estimates
{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}}_{k}^{\text{ML}}
, the criteria in (
13
) include an
ML
correction parameter
\sigma_{c}^{2}
, which is interpreted as a minimum noise-variance level.
In the
OLS
workflow, instead of systematically adding the next estimated angle
\widehat{\theta}_{k+1}
to
{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}}_{k}
, we add
\widehat{\theta}_{k+1}
only if it improves the objective, i.e.,

\text{ITC}^{\text{\lx@glossaries@gls@link{main}{ols}{{{}}OLS} }}(k+1)<\text{ITC}^{\text{\lx@glossaries@gls@link{main}{ols}{{{}}OLS} }}(k).

(14)

This acts as a stopping criterion for the
ITC
-
OLS
iterations.
At low
SNRs
,
\sigma_{c}^{2}
in (
13
) has no impact because
\sigma^{2}
dominates the denominator.
At very high
SNRs
, i.e., as
\sigma^{2}\to 0
,
ML
estimates almost perfectly fit the ground truth, and the residual term
\Tr\left[\mathbf{P}^{\bot}\big({\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}}_{k}^{\text{ML}}\big)\mathbf{R}\right]
tends to zero for
k=K
.
For greedy
OLS
estimates, however, this residual term does not necessarily vanish as
\sigma^{2}\to 0
.
Consequently, as this term is strongly amplified by the denominator in (
13
), the stopping criterion in (
14
) can only be triggered for large penalty term (i.e. large
k
).
This leads to overfitting by introducing spurious targets close to already detected ones.
Therefore, at high
SNRs
,
\sigma_{c}^{2}
provides a trade-off between detection rate and false-alarm rate.
Suggested values of
\sigma_{c}^{2}
are discussed in Section
IV
.

III-C

Hybrid Rank and Selection-based
ITC
-
OLS

The selection-based
ITC
-
OLS
method can be further improved by combining it with rank-based
ITC
.
First, before running
OLS
,the rank of the sample covariance matrix
\mathbf{R}
is estimated to provide a preliminary estimate
\widehat{K}
using the criterion in (
8
).
Next, during the first
\widehat{K}
iterations of
OLS
, the estimated angles
\widehat{\theta}_{k+1}
are systematically added to
{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}}_{k}
.
As a result, the algorithm enforces a minimum number of selected targets determined by the estimated rank of
\mathbf{R}
.
For subsequent steps, i.e., when
k+1>\widehat{K}
, the newly estimated angle
\widehat{\theta}_{k+1}
is added only if it improves the selection-based
ITC
-
OLS
objective in (
13
); therefore enabling the detection of more than
\widehat{K}
targets.
This combination is motivated by complementary operating regimes.
At low
SNRs
, selection-based
ITC
-
OLS
tend to underestimate the number of targets due to the division by
\sigma^{2}
in (
13
).
Rank-based
ITC
are typically more robust to noise and provide a reliable, yet conservative, target-count estimate.
At medium
SNRs
, selection-based
ITC
-
OLS
can improve the rank-based conservative target-count estimate by leveraging accurate
OLS
angle estimates.
At high
SNRs
, the
ML
correction parameter
\sigma_{c}^{2}
in (
13
) can be tuned to control the tradeoff between hit and false-alarm rates.
Therefore preventing overestimation of the number of targets.

V
Conclusion

This paper studies the joint estimation of the number of targets and their
DoAs
by integrating the
ITC
model-order selection into the
OLS
greedy search.
Three methodologies are proposed: the disjoint rank-based, the joint selection-based, and the hybrid rank and selection-based
ITC
-
OLS
algorithms.
Each algorithm was derived under both the
AIC
and the
BIC
frameworks.
Numerical simulations demonstrate that the proposed hybrid
ITC
-
OLS
achieves the best overall performance by exploiting the complementarity of the rank-based and selection-based
ITC
components,across all considered parameters regimes.
Furthermore,
AIC
consistently outperforms
BIC
in the evaluated scenario.
A key feature of the joint and hybrid strategies is the
ML
correction parameter, set to
\sigma_{c}^{2}\approx-35
dB for the
AIC
and
\sigma_{c}^{2}\approx-45
dB for the
BIC
, which controls the hit/false-alarm tradeoff at high
SNR
without degrading the performance at lower
SNR
regimes.
