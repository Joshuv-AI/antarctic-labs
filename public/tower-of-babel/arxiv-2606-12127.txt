Ionization-Induced Electrostatic Hose Instability in Electron-Beam-Sustained Plasmas
Jia-Hong Chen, Yi Yu, Jian Chen, Zhi-Bin Wang

Abstract
We report the discovery of a previously unrecognized electrostatic hose instability in electron-beam-sustained plasmas, driven by the coupling between the electron beam centroid and the plasma generated via the beam-impact ionization. Unlike the conventional hose instability of relativistic beams propagating in underdense plasmas, this instability requires only ionization-capable electron beams readily produced by common emission processes and sheath acceleration, indicating broad relevance across various discharges. A linear theory is developed to predict the hosing frequency and growth rate, and particle-in-cell/Monte Carlo simulations confirm both the onset of instability and the theoretical predictions.

In this Letter, we identify an distinct electrostatic hose instability driven by beam-impact ionization in electron-beam-sustained discharges. Similar to the conventional electron hose instability, the newly identified instability also manifests itself as a growing transverse oscillation of the beam centroid. However, unlike conventional hose instability requiring ultra-intense beams capable of expelling most plasma electrons and forming an ion-focusing channel, the instability reported here naturally arises due to the coupling of the beam and the plasma it generates. It can be triggered by ionization-capable electron beams readily produced through common emission processes and sheath acceleration, making it broadly relevant to low-temperature plasma discharges. We develop a linear theory to predict the hosing frequency and instability growth rate, and validate the theoretical predictions using first-principles particle-in-cell/Monte Carlo collision (PIC/MCC) simulations. We also demonstrate that the instability can lead to beam breakup and generate intense oscillatory particle and energy fluxes to the sidewalls, potentially degrading the low temperature plasma device performance in practical applications.

Transverse profiles of electron-beam-sustained plasmas.—

Before deriving the hose instability, we need to first calculate the transverse profiles of electron-beam-sustained plasmas. We consider a nonrelativistic electron beam propagating through a background neutral gas without magnetic confinement [Fig.

&\qquad\left.-\exp\!\left(\frac{e\phi(x)}{kT_{e}}\right)-\frac{n_{b}(x)}{n_{e,c}}\right\},\end{split}

Ionization-induced hose instability.—

Having obtained the transverse profiles, we next examine their stability to transverse hose perturbations. We introduce small transverse displacements to the beam electron and plasma electron channel (denoted by
X_{b}
and
X_{e}
, respectively)
9
;
10
;
52
;
24
. For the beam electrons, a rigid transverse displacement produces a density perturbation at the beam edge

n_{b1}(x)=n_{b0}X_{b}\delta(x-b).

(2)

For the plasma electrons, a transverse displacement
X_{e}
yields the density perturbation

n_{e1}(x)=-\,\frac{en_{e}^{(0)}(x)}{kT_{e}}\,\frac{d\phi_{0}}{dx}\,X_{e}.

(3)

For generality, we define the edge of the plasma-electron channel as the position
x=x_{e}
where the equilibrium electron density decreases to
1/\mathrm{e}
of its central value (see blue dashed line in Fig.

Besides the electron response, the response of ionization-generated ions is also crucial to the development of the new instability, which is the primary difference from the conventional hose type. Since the ions are produced locally through the beam-impact ionization, their density perturbation is assumed to follow the beam displacement
X_{b}
. As detailed in Appendix C of SM
43
, the ion density perturbation can be written as
n_{i1}(x)=S_{i}(x,\omega)X_{b}
, where

S_{i}(x,\omega)=\frac{\bigl[1-\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({-2\pi\nu_{i}/|\omega_{r}|}}}\right)\bigr]\nu_{i}n_{b0}H(x-b)}{\sqrt{2e/m_{i}}\,\bigl[\phi_{0}(b)-\phi_{0}(x)\bigr]^{1/2}}.

(4)

The factor [
1-\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({-2\pi\nu_{i}/|\omega_{r}|}}}\right)
] represents the probability that ionization occurs within one hosing period. In the limit of
\nu_{i}\ll|\omega_{r}|
, this factor approaches zero, recovering the pure electron hose case.

Inserting the density perturbations (
2
)–(
3
) into the linearized Poisson equation

\frac{d^{2}\phi_{1}}{dx^{2}}=4\pi e\bigl(n_{e1}+n_{b1}-n_{i1}\bigr),

(5)

the potential perturbation can be written as
\phi_{1}(x,t)=X_{b}(t)\psi_{b}(x)+X_{e}(t)\psi_{e}(x)

;
24
,
where the shape functions
\psi_{b}
and
\psi_{e}
satisfy

\begin{cases}\psi^{\prime\prime}_{b}(x)=-4\pi e\bigl[S_{i}(x,\omega)-n_{b0}\delta(x-b)\bigr]\\[2.0pt]
\psi^{\prime\prime}_{e}(x)=-4\pi e\,\dfrac{en_{e}^{(0)}(x)}{kT_{e}}\,\phi^{\prime}_{0}(x)\end{cases}.

(6)

These equations can be solved numerically with the Dirichlet conditions.

For plasma electrons, the transverse displacement follows

m_{e}\frac{d^{2}X_{e}}{dt^{2}}=e\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial x}|_{x_{e}}.

(7)

While the displacement for electron beam channel follows

m_{e}\left(\frac{d^{2}X_{b}}{dt^{2}}+\nu_{b}\frac{dX_{b}}{dt}\right)=e\left.\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial x}\right|_{b}.

(8)

Therefore, the transverse dynamics can be reduced to two coupled oscillator equations

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}{X}_{b}+\nu_{b}\partial_{t}{X}_{b}+\Omega_{bb}^{2}X_{b}+\Omega_{be}^{2}X_{e}&=0\\
\partial_{t}^{2}{X}_{e}+\Omega_{ee}^{2}X_{e}+\Omega_{eb}^{2}X_{b}&=0\end{cases},

(9)

where the coupling constants
\Omega_{bb},\Omega_{be},\Omega_{eb},\Omega_{ee}
are defined as

\begin{cases}\Omega_{bb}^{2}=-\frac{e}{m_{e}}\psi_{b}^{\prime}(b),\quad&\Omega_{be}^{2}=-\frac{e}{m_{e}}\psi_{e}^{\prime}(b)\\[2.0pt]
\Omega_{eb}^{2}=-\frac{e}{m_{e}}\psi_{b}^{\prime}(x_{e}),\quad&\Omega_{ee}^{2}=-\frac{e}{m_{e}}\psi_{e}^{\prime}(x_{e})\end{cases}.

(10)

Assuming normal‑mode solutions
X_{b},X_{e}\propto e^{-i\omega t}
, we obtain the dispersion relation

\begin{split}\omega^{4}&+i\nu_{b}\omega^{3}-\left(\Omega_{bb}^{2}+\Omega_{ee}^{2}\right)\omega^{2}\\
&-i\nu_{b}\Omega_{ee}^{2}\omega+\Omega_{bb}^{2}\Omega_{ee}^{2}-\Omega_{be}^{2}\Omega_{eb}^{2}=0\end{split},

(11)

with
\omega=\omega_{r}+i\gamma
. The hose instability corresponds to a positive growth rate,
\gamma>0

11

Summary.—

We report a novel ionization-induced electrostatic hose instability in electron-beam-sustained discharges. Unlike the conventional electron hose instability that relies on an ultra-intense beam capable of expelling most plasma electrons, the instability identified here arises naturally from the coupling between the beam and the plasma generated through beam-impact ionization. Therefore, it can occur in many low temperature plasma discharges sustained by ionization-capable electron beams.

We develop a linear theory to predict the hosing frequency and growth rate of this instability and validate the theory using two-dimensional PIC/MCC simulations. Both the linear theory and simulations show that the hosing frequency is closely linked to the ionization frequency, confirming its ionization-driven nature. The simulations also demonstrate that the instability leads to beam breakup and induces intense, high-frequency particle and energy fluxes to the wall, highlighting its potential impact on discharge performance and the need for effective instability mitigation strategies.

Appendix C: Linear Theory of Hose Instability

For a small displacement
X
and a function
f(x)
,

f(x-X)=f(x)-X\frac{df}{dx}(x)+o(X)

In the zeroth-order state, the beam-electron density is taken as a step function,

n_{b}^{(0)}(x)=n_{b0}H(b-x),

where
b
is the beam radius. A positive beam displacement
X_{b}
is defined such that the beam boundary moves from
x=b
to
x=b+X_{b}
. Expanding
H(b-(x-X_{b}))
gives,

\begin{split}H(b-x+X_{b})&=H(b-x)+X_{b}\frac{dH(b-x)}{d(b-x)}+o(X_{b})\\
&=H(b-x)+X_{b}\delta(b-x)+o(X_{b})\end{split}.

Thus the first-order beam-electron density perturbation is

\boxed{n_{b1}(x)=n_{b0}X_{b}\delta(x-b)}.

The zeroth-order plasma-electron density is assumed to satisfy the Boltzmann relation

n_{e}^{(0)}(x)=n_{e,c}\exp\left(\frac{e\phi_{0}(x)}{kT_{e}}\right).

Expanding
\exp\left(\frac{e\phi_{0}(x)}{kT_{e}}\right)
gives,

\begin{split}\exp\left(\frac{e\phi_{0}(x-X_{e})}{kT_{e}}\right)=&\exp\left(\frac{e\phi_{0}(x)}{kT_{e}}\right)\\
&-X_{e}\frac{e\phi_{0}^{\prime}(x)}{kT_{e}}\exp\left(\frac{e\phi_{0}(x)}{kT_{e}}\right)+o(X_{e})\end{split}.

Thus the plasma-electron density perturbation in the displacement form becomes

\boxed{n_{e1}(x)=-\frac{en_{e}^{(0)}(x)}{kT_{e}}\frac{d\phi_{0}}{dx}X_{e}}.

The zeroth-order ion density is written as

n_{i}^{(0)}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\nu_{i}n_{b0}H(b-\xi)\,d\xi}{\left(2e/m_{i}\right)^{1/2}\left[\phi_{0}(\xi)-\phi_{0}(x)\right]^{1/2}}.

Thus the first-order ion density perturbation can be defined as,

\begin{split}n_{i1}^{(b)}(x)&=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\nu_{i}n_{b1}d\xi}{\left(2e/m_{i}\right)^{1/2}\left[\phi_{0}(\xi)-\phi_{0}(x)\right]^{1/2}}\\
&=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\nu_{i}n_{b0}X_{b}\delta(\xi-b)d\xi}{\left(2e/m_{i}\right)^{1/2}\left[\phi_{0}(\xi)-\phi_{0}(x)\right]^{1/2}}.\end{split}

Since

\int_{0}^{x}f(\xi)\delta(\xi-b)\,d\xi=f(b)H(x-b),

the ion density perturbation due to the displacement of the ionization source is

n_{i1}^{(b)}(x)=\frac{\nu_{i}n_{b0}X_{b}H(x-b)}{\left(2e/m_{i}\right)^{1/2}\left[\phi_{0}(b)-\phi_{0}(x)\right]^{1/2}}.

However, when the ionization frequency
\nu_{i}
is not sufficiently larger than the hosing frequency, the ionization cannot follow the beam displacement instantaneously. Over one oscillation period
T=2\pi/|\omega_{r}|
(with
\omega_{r}=\operatorname{Re}\omega
), the efficiency with which ionization builds up the density perturbation scales as
1-e^{-\nu_{i}T}
. Thus, in the frequency domain the ion density perturbation becomes

{n}_{i1}(x,\omega)=\frac{[1-\exp\left(-2\pi\nu_{i}/|\omega_{r}|\right)]\nu_{i}n_{b0}H(x-b)}{\left(2e/m_{i}\right)^{1/2}\left[\phi_{0}(b)-\phi_{0}(x)\right]^{1/2}}X_{b}.

In the limit
\nu_{i}\gg|\omega_{r}|
the bracket
[1-\exp\left(-2\pi\nu_{i}/|\omega_{r}|\right)]
tends to
1
, recovering the instantaneous‑ion result; for
\nu_{i}\ll|\omega_{r}|
the perturbation is strongly suppressed, which accounts for the mismatch between the ionization rate and the hosing frequency.

For later use, we define the ion source response function

S_{i}(x,\omega)=\frac{(1-\exp\left(-2\pi\nu_{i}/|\omega_{r}|\right))\nu_{i}n_{b0}H(x-b)}{\left(2e/m_{i}\right)^{1/2}\left[\phi_{0}(b)-\phi_{0}(x)\right]^{1/2}}.

Then the ion density perturbation can be written compactly as

\boxed{n_{i1}(x)=S_{i}(x,\omega)X_{b}}.

The first-order Poisson equation is

\frac{d^{2}\phi_{1}}{dx^{2}}=4\pi e\left(n_{e1}+n_{b1}-n_{i1}\right).

Substituting the displacement forms of
n_{i1}
,
n_{e1}
, and
n_{b1}
, one obtains

\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}\phi_{1}}{dx^{2}}=4\pi e[&X_{b}\left(n_{b0}\delta(x-b)-S_{i}(x,\omega)\right)\\
&-\frac{en_{e}^{(0)}(x)}{kT_{e}}\frac{d\phi_{0}}{dx}X_{e}]\end{split}.

Because the equation is linear in
X_{b}
and
X_{e}
, the first-order potential can be decomposed as

\phi_{1}(x,t)=X_{b}(t)\psi_{b}(x)+X_{e}(t)\psi_{e}(x),

where
\psi_{b}
is the potential response to a unit beam displacement and
\psi_{e}
is the potential response to a unit plasma-electron displacement.

The two response functions satisfy

\boxed{\frac{d^{2}\psi_{b}}{dx^{2}}=-4\pi e\left[S_{i}(x,\omega)-n_{b0}\delta(x-b)\right]}

and

\boxed{\frac{d^{2}\psi_{e}}{dx^{2}}=-4\pi e\frac{en_{e}^{(0)}(x)}{kT_{e}}\frac{d\phi_{0}}{dx}}.

For a half-domain description of a hose-like transverse displacement, the first-order potential is taken to be odd with respect to the beam center. Thus one may impose

\psi_{b}(0)=0,\qquad\psi_{e}(0)=0.

At the outer boundary, a simple grounded condition is used,

\psi_{b}(L)=0,\qquad\psi_{e}(L)=0.

The beam centroid is driven by the first-order electric field at the inner side of the beam boundary. Including a beam-electron collision frequency
\nu_{b}
, its equation of motion is written as

m_{e}\left(\frac{d^{2}X_{b}}{dt^{2}}+\nu_{b}\frac{dX_{b}}{dt}\right)=e\left.\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial x}\right|_{b^{-}}.

Substituting

\phi_{1}(x,t)=X_{b}(t)\psi_{b}(x)+X_{e}(t)\psi_{e}(x),

gives

m_{e}\left(\frac{d^{2}X_{b}}{dt^{2}}+\nu_{b}\frac{dX_{b}}{dt}\right)=e\left[X_{b}\psi_{b}^{\prime}(b^{-})+X_{e}\psi_{e}^{\prime}(b^{-})\right].

This can be written as

\boxed{\frac{d^{2}X_{b}}{dt^{2}}+\nu_{b}\frac{dX_{b}}{dt}+\Omega_{bb}^{2}X_{b}+\Omega_{be}^{2}X_{e}=0}

with

\boxed{\Omega_{bb}^{2}=-\frac{e}{m_{e}}\psi_{b}^{\prime}(b^{-})}

and

\boxed{\Omega_{be}^{2}=-\frac{e}{m_{e}}\psi_{e}^{\prime}(b^{-})}.

The plasma-electron displacement is assumed to occur around a characteristic position
x_{e}
, which is chosen from the zeroth-order density profile. The characteristic plasma-electron displacement position is defined by the point where the hump density decays to
1/\mathrm{e}
of its central value, i.e.,

\boxed{n_{e}^{(0)}(x_{e})=\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}}n_{e}^{(0)}(0)=\frac{n_{e,c}}{\mathrm{e}}}.

Neglecting collisional damping of the plasma electrons, the plasma-electron displacement satisfies

m_{e}\frac{d^{2}X_{e}}{dt^{2}}=e\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial x}|_{x_{e}}

Substituting the response decomposition for
\phi_{1}
, one obtains

m_{e}\frac{d^{2}X_{e}}{dt^{2}}=e\left[X_{b}\psi_{b}^{\prime}(x_{e})+X_{e}\psi_{e}^{\prime}(x_{e})\right].

Therefore,

\boxed{\frac{d^{2}X_{e}}{dt^{2}}+\Omega_{ee}^{2}X_{e}+\Omega_{eb}^{2}X_{b}=0}

where

\boxed{\Omega_{eb}^{2}=-\frac{e}{m_{e}}\psi_{b}^{\prime}(x_{e})}

and

\boxed{\Omega_{ee}^{2}=-\frac{e}{m_{e}}\psi_{e}^{\prime}(x_{e})}.

Thus, in the instantaneous-ion-response approximation, the coupled displacement equations are

\boxed{\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}X_{b}}{dt^{2}}+\nu_{b}\frac{dX_{b}}{dt}+\Omega_{bb}^{2}X_{b}+\Omega_{be}^{2}X_{e}&=0,\\
\frac{d^{2}X_{e}}{dt^{2}}+\Omega_{ee}^{2}X_{e}+\Omega_{eb}^{2}X_{b}&=0.\end{split}}

Assuming normal-mode solutions of the form

X_{b},\;X_{e}\propto\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({-i\omega t}}}\right),

the coupled equations become

\left(-\omega^{2}-i\nu_{b}\omega+\Omega_{bb}^{2}\right)X_{b}+\Omega_{be}^{2}X_{e}=0,

and

\Omega_{eb}^{2}X_{b}+\left(-\omega^{2}+\Omega_{ee}^{2}\right)X_{e}=0.

A nontrivial solution requires the determinant to vanish, giving the dispersion relation

\boxed{\left(-\omega^{2}-i\nu_{b}\omega+\Omega_{bb}^{2}\right)\left(-\omega^{2}+\Omega_{ee}^{2}\right)-\Omega_{be}^{2}\Omega_{eb}^{2}=0}.

Equivalently, this can be written as a fourth-order polynomial in
\omega
,

\boxed{\begin{split}\omega^{4}&+i\nu_{b}\omega^{3}-\left(\Omega_{bb}^{2}+\Omega_{ee}^{2}\right)\omega^{2}\\
&-i\nu_{b}\Omega_{ee}^{2}\omega+\Omega_{bb}^{2}\Omega_{ee}^{2}-\Omega_{be}^{2}\Omega_{eb}^{2}=0\end{split}}.

With the convention
\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({-i\omega t}}}\right)
, a root

\omega=\omega_{r}+i\gamma

corresponds to an instability when

\gamma>0.
