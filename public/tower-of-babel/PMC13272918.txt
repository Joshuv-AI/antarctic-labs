Study on the mechanical characteristics of the stress relaxation in the carbonate rock after high-temperature acidification
Authors: Junchao Yang, Songcai Han, Lili Li, Jianxin Peng, Junyan Liu, Jiangyu Liu, Zhongying Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To elucidate the mechanism by which acidification influences wellbore stability in deep reservoir formations, this study investigates the rheological and mechanical behaviors of the carbonate rock subjected to high-temperature acid etching. A novel experimental system was developed to characterize the stress relaxation behavior of the acid-etched carbonate rock, and the characteristics of the stress relaxation curves under various acid etching conditions and strain levels were systematically analyzed. Combined with Burgers model and the Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm, the evolution of rheological parameters of the carbonate rock under different acid etching regimes was quantitatively evaluated. The results indicate that the acid-etched carbonate rock exhibit significant rheological mechanical properties due to the presence of developed microcracks and complex pore structures. Under the identical acid etching duration and temperature, the initial stress, residual stress, and time required for stress relaxation stabilization all increase with increasing the strain level. Overall, the stress relaxation magnitude prior to the core fracture ranges from 15 to 25 MPa, and the stabilization time for the core stress relaxation falls between 5 and 7 h. The stress relaxation behavior of the acid-etched carbonate core is well described by the Burgers model. Introduction

At present, acidifiaction and acid fracturing technologies have been widely applied in the oil and gas development of deep carbonate rock reservoirs1–5. Under the action of acid etching, the rock surface micro-structures are eroded and damaged, and the rock mechanical properties appear significant changes, thereby altering the final reservoir modification effect. However, due to the influence of high temperature, the acid-rock reaction rate in deep reservoirs accelerates, which leads to a rapid decrease in stress around wells and significantly increase the wellbore stability risk. Especially, many engineering problems such as wellbore shrinkage, wellbore collapse and large-scale sand production often appear after the open-hole completion of deep carbonate reservoirs due to acidifiaction. Therefore, in order to provide a theoretical basis for the wellbore stability analysis after acidification and offer certain theoretical references for the selection of the acid fracturing completion methods, it is necessary to conduct experimental study on the mechanical properties of the carbonate rocks after high-temperature acid etching.

A large number of experiments on the rock microscopic structures under the room temperature have shown that carbonate rocks are mainly composed of inorganic minerals such as calcite (CaCO3), dolomite (CaMg(CO3)2), and quartz (SiO2). and calcite and dolomite are both prone to chemical reactions with acids. The dissolution of calcite and dolomite not only alters the rock physical properties, but also changes the rock micro-structures6–9. For example, Xu et al.6 conducted an acid dissolution experiment to understand the influence of rock micro-structures on the acid etching. Rock micro-structures offer control over the dolomite acid-rock reaction. The loose micro-structure of dolomite depicts a fast acid-rock reaction, a rough surface, while the compact micro-structure of dolomite depicts a slow acid-rock reaction and a uniform etching. Zhou et al.7 studied the micro-structure and pore structure of the acid-etched softened layer of the carbonate rock from aspects such as acid-rock reaction kinetics, acid-etched morphology, and micro-structure characterization. Due to the presence of the softened layer, acidic substances are more likely to break through the rock surface, forming a large number of uneven dissolved pores inside the rock, which leads to the change of the pore structure and distribution in the rock. Lai et al.8 observed SEM images of the carbonate rock after acid etching and found that the loose micro-structure formed by the acid-rock reaction mainly includes inter-granular dissolution pores, in-granular dissolution pores, and dissolution cracks at the micrometer scale.

Subsequently, through indoor acid-etched rock triaxial mechanical experiments, scholars have found that the deterioration of the micro-structure of carbonate rocks caused by acid etching will change their macroscopic mechanical properties, mainly reflected in the reduction of mechanical parameters such as the elastic modulus and compressive strength of the rocks10–19. For example, Chi et al.10 Found that the compressive strength and elastic modulus of the acid-etched rock are smaller than that of the unacid-etched rock under the room temperature. Zhang et al.11 investigated the deterioration mechanism of the mechanical properties of the carbonate rock after acid etching under different acid etching conditions. The compressive strength and elastic modulus of the carbonate rock significantly decrease due to acid etching, and the mechanical properties of the carbonate rock with relatively low cementation strength are weakened. Chen et al.14 pointed out that the higher the acid etching temperature and the longer the acid etching time, the smaller the elastic modulus, Poisson’s ratio and tensile strength of the carbonate rock. Then, a large number of scholars’ studies show that the mechanical parameters of carbonate rocks have a significant time effect during acid etching20–25. For example, Yang et al.20 fitted the compressive strength and elastic modulus of the carbonate rock after high-temperature acid etching with the acid etching time. With the increase of the acid etching time, The compressive strength and elastic modulus of the carbonate rock decrease from “rapid” to “slow” and eventually “stabilize” and generally show a“power function” change trend. Huang et al.21. provided a detailed explanation of the above phenomenon. The change trend of the carbonate rock mechanical properties is closely related to the dissolution process, and the dissolution process can be divided into two stages: acid-rock reaction and hydrolysis reaction. During the acid-rock reaction stage, the sample porosity grows rapidly, mainly manifested as the development from medium pores to large pores, and the complexity of the sample micro-structure increases rapidly. Specially, the rock mechanical properties decline rapidly in the acid-rock reaction stage. During the hydrolysis reaction stage, the sample porosity increases slowly, mainly manifested as the development of small pores, and there is no significant change in the micro-structure of the sample. The rock mechanical properties gradually decline in the hydrolysis reaction stage.

At present, a small number of scholars have began to focus the study on the rheological mechanical properties of carbonate rocks after acid etching, and initially have explored the change characteristics of the creep curves of acid-etched carbonate rocks26–36. For example, Xie et al.26 conducted the two-stage triaxial creep experiments of the carbonate sample after acid etching under the room temperature. There is a significant creep phenomenon for the carbonate sample after acid etching. Jiang et al.28 pointed out that the greater the acid concentration, the greater the creep deformation of the limestone sample under the room temperature. In addition, the creep effect of carbonate rocks after acid etching will also have an impact on the long-term conductivity of the acid etching fracture. For example, Abass29 pointed out that the creep of the rock after acid etching will change the acid etching fracture opening and the contact point of the fracture surface, thereby reducing the long-term conductivity of the fracture. Zhou et al.30 pointed out that the significant reduction in the conductivity of the acid-etched fracture in the short term of production is closely related to the elastic–plastic deformation at the contact point of the fracture surface and the creep of the limestone.

In conclusion, the deterioration of the internal structures of carbonate rocks caused by acid etching is the main reason for the alteration of their macroscopic mechanical properties. However, obvious limitations still exist in current research, which are mainly reflected in the following three aspects: First, most existing studies focus on the variation laws of basic mechanical properties of rocks after acid etching, such as elastic modulus and uniaxial compressive strength, whereas systematic research on the rheological mechanical properties of acid-etched rocks is relatively scarce.Second, the available studies on the rheological properties of acid-etched rocks only stay at the intuitive analysis of creep curve characteristics, and fail to conduct an in-depth analysis of the deformation mechanism from the perspective of viscoelastic constitutive models. Third, current rheological experiments on acid-etched rocks are mainly conducted under room-temperature creep conditions, and research on the stress relaxation rheological characteristics of acid-etched rocks at high temperatures is extremely insufficient, resulting in a notable lack of relevant experimental and theoretical investigations. Therefore, a systematic analysis is needed to study the stress relaxation rheological characteristics of carbonate rocks after high-temperature acid etching.

The main purpose of this paper is to provide theoretical support for the study of rheological mechanical response mechanism in deep carbonate reservoirs acidification. In Sect. "Experimental process", a novel acid etching experiment is designed to investigate the rheological characteristics of carbonate rocks under different acid etching conditions. In Sect. "Analysis of the influence of high-temperature acid etching on Stress Relaxation of the carbonate rock", the influence mechanism of acid etching on the carbonate rock rheological properties is studied, and the characteristics of the stress relaxation curve of the carbonate rock after acid etching are analyzed. In Sect. "Rheological constitutive model and the parameter identification of the sample after acid etching", Combining Matlab and the Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm, the four rheological parameters of the carbonate rock after acid etching are obtained by fitting Burgers viscoelastic model, and the variation laws of the four rheological parameters under different conditions are analyzed. Finally, the evolution equation of the overall rheological parameters and porosity of the carbonate rock are established.

Experimental process

Carbonate core preparation

The rock blocks used in this study were taken from the outcrop of the Sinian system Dengying group in Suining city, Sichuan province. The selected rock blocks undergo processing procedures such as coring, grinding and cutting to produce standard columnar samples with a diameter of 25 mm and a height of 50 mm. The length error of all carbonate rock samples was less than ± 0.2 mm, the end face flatness error of the rock samples was less than ± 0.02 mm, and the angle error between the end face and the axis of all rock samples was less than ± 0.15°, as shown in Fig. 1a. The high-temperature resistant and anti-corrosion paint was evenly applied to the upper and lower surfaces of the samples to ensure that the height of the samples after acid etching meets the requirements of the triaxial testing instrument, as shown in Fig. 1b. A total of 60 standard processed rock samples are obtained in this study. To select the homogeneous samples, 15 samples with significant deviations from the mean P-wave velocity were excluded based on the acoustic wave measurement. Then, the porosity, permeability and pore diameter tests of the remaining 45 samples were sequentially performed, and some of the experimental instruments are shown in Fig. 2. The acid etching experiments of the samples with 15% hydrochloric acid under different acid etching conditions were conducted through the SYF-3 type acid rock reaction system. The acid rock reaction system mainly consists of an acid rock reaction chamber, an acid liquid chamber, a nitrogen cylinder, an intermediate container capable of storing gas and generating gas pressure, two heating systems, two temperature controllers, gas valves for regulation the pressure during the acid rock reaction process, a pressure sensor, liquid discharge valves, and reading meters distributed throughout the acid rock reaction system nodes, as shown in Fig. 3. Multi-level stress relaxation experiments

The multi-level stress relaxation experiments of the rock samples after acid etching under different high temperatures were conducted through the TAW-1000 multi-functional rock triaxial experiment device developed independently, as shown in Fig. 5. The loading rate was set as 0.1 mm/min during the rock stress relaxation loading. In order to minimize the influence of the compact stage on the rock sample stress relaxation experiments as much as possible, the first stress relaxation level was conducted when the axial strain of the rock samples after acid etching reached 0.2% during loading, and then the stress relaxation level was performed every time the axial strain increased by 0.1% until the rock sample broke. Considering the influence of the experimental fluctuations and other factors, when the stress variation was less than 0.03 MPa/h during the stress relaxation of the rock samples after acid etching at a certain strain level, it is considered that the stress relaxation of the acid etched rock sample at this strain level has basically stabilized. The time required for the acid etched rock sample to reach stability under single-level stress relaxation is less than 8 h, so the time for each stress relaxation level was set as 30,000 s. In addition, in order to simulate the effective stress level of the Dengying formation reservoir, the effective confining pressure for the multi-level stress relaxation experiments of the acid etched rock samples was maintained at 50 MPa. Analysis of the influence of high-temperature acid etching on Stress Relaxation of the carbonate rock

Influence of acid etching on the carbonate rock rheological property

The rock rheological property is one of the important factors affecting the modification of deep carbonate reservoirs. In order to verify that acid etching has an important impact on the carbonate rock rheological property, combined with the scanning electron microscope (SEM) images and stress relaxation curves of the carbonate rock before and after high-temperature acid etching, the changes in the carbonate rock rheological property before and after high-temperature acid etching are studied. In order to avoid the influence of the loading process on the rock stress relaxation effect as much as possible and obtain the stable stress relaxation curve, the stress relaxation curve at the 0.2% strain level in the early stage of loading is selected as the research target, and the stress relaxation characteristics of the carbonate rock before and after acid etching are compared and analyzed. For the sample SH-0 at the 0.2% strain level, there is no obvious the rapid relaxation stage during the stress relaxation. There are the slow relaxation and stable relaxation stages during the sample H-0 stress relaxation at the 0.2% strain level, and the entire relaxation degree is extremely small, as shown in Fig. 6a. However, during the stress relaxation for the acid-etched sample SH-3 at the 0.2% strain level, the stress rapidly decreases by 2.21 MPa within the first 400 s, and the stress decreases slowly by 2.57 MPa within 4–5 h. Finally, the stress tends to stabilize and remains basically unchanged with the increase of time. Therefore, there are obvious and complete stress relaxation curve features for the sample SH-3 after high-temperature acid etching at the 0.2 strain level, as shown in Fig. 6b. The stress relaxation degree for the sample after acid etching increases at the 0.2% strain level with the increase of the acid etching temperature, and the reduction range of the rapid relaxation stage also increases, as shown in Fig. 6c and d.Fig. 6Acid-etched sample stress relaxation curves at 0.2% strain level.

Acid-etched sample stress relaxation curves at 0.2% strain level.

In order to clarify the relationship between the micro-structure changes of the carbonate rock after acid etching and its rheological property, the micro-structure characteristics of the rock under different acid etching temperature were analyzed. The degree of the sample micro-structure deterioration increases with the increase of the acid etching temperature. As shown in Fig. 7a, the surface of the unacid etched sample is dense and smooth, and there are only a few holes and micro-cracks on the sample surface. As the acid penetrates the sample, the sample surface begins to become rough, and micro-cracks and holes start to appear on the sample surface, as shown in Fig. 7b. Then, as the acid etching temperature increases, micro-cracks and holes are constantly appear on the sample surface, and the complex fracture network structure and pore throat structure are formed through the mutual development and expansion of these micro-cracks and holes, as shown in Fig. 7c. Finally, with the continuous increase of the acid etching temperature, the sample surface becomes uneven, and a large number of micro-cracks and holes are densely distributed on the sample surface, presenting a “honeycomb” structure, as shown in Fig. 7d. Combined with the characteristics of the stress relaxation curve of the sample after acid etching and the micro-structure changes of the sample after acid etching, it can be known that the uneven dissolution effect of the acid leads to an increase in the internal crack-hole structure of the sample, thereby reducing the sample mechanical properties and enhancing the sample rheological properties. The large number of the crack-hole structures is one of the most important reasons for the enhanced rheological properties of the sample after acid etching.Fig. 7Sample micro-structure change under different acid etching temperatures20.

Rheological constitutive model and the parameter identification of the sample after acid etching

Stress–strain isochronous curve of the sample after acid etching

The stress–strain isochronous curves of the sample after acid etching under different conditions are obtained based on the acid-etched sample multi-level stress relaxation experimental data. The deformation characteristics of the sample after acid etching are analyzed by taking the sample after acid etching at 160 °C as an example, and some of the curves are described as shown in Fig. 9.Fig. 9Stress–strain isochronous curves of samples after acid etching at 160 °C.

Stress–strain isochronous curves of samples after acid etching at 160 °C.

The shapes of the stress–strain isochrous curves are very similar under different stress relaxation time. The slope of the stress–strain isochronous curve slightly decreases with the increase of the stress relaxation time duration. Taking the sample SH-22 as an example, within the strain level range of 0.2–0.7%, the stress–strain isochronous curve is approximately a straight line, indicating that the sample SH-22 can be regarded as a linear viscoelastic body within this strain level range. Within the strain level range of 0.7–0.9%, the slope of the curve decreases somewhat, and the slope of the stress–strain isochronous curve changes little at different stress relaxation time duration, indicating that the plastic deformation proportion in the total deformation of the sample SH-22 is relatively small. Therefore, the sample SH-22 can also be approximately regarded as a linear viscoelastic body within the strain level range of 0.7–0.9%. In conclusion, the linear viscoelastic model can be used to character the rheological property of the carbonate rock after acid etching based on the stress–strain isochronous curves of the sample after acid etching under different conditions.

At present, the constitutive models mainly used to describe the rheological property of materials include the Burgers model, the Maxwell model, and the generalized Maxwell model, etc. Among them, the Burgers model has been widely applied in practical engineering due to few model parameters, simple solution, and excellent ability to describe the rheological characteristics of viscoelastic materials. Therefore, the Burgers model is used to describe the rheological property of the sample after acid etching in this section from the perspective of the stress relaxation, and the parameters of the selected rheological model are determined simultaneously.

Burgers stress relaxation model

The combined model formed by the series connection of the Kelvin model and the Maxwell model is called the Burgers model, abbreviated as the Bu model, as shown in Fig. 10. The symbol of the Burgers model can be described as: Bu = M − K = (H − N) − (H//N).Fig. 10Burgers model.

Burgers model.

The total strain \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varepsilon_{M}$$\end{document} of the Maxwell model consists of two parts: the strain of the elastic element \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varepsilon_{H}$$\end{document} and the strain of the viscoelastic element \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varepsilon_{N}$$\end{document}, as follows:3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varepsilon_{M} = \varepsilon_{H} + \varepsilon_{N}$$\end{document}

Among them, the stress–strain relationship of the elastic element in the Maxwell model is described as follows:4\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\frac{{\partial \varepsilon_{H} }}{\partial t} = \frac{1}{{E_{1} }}\frac{{\sigma_{M} }}{\partial t}$$\end{document}where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$E_{1}$$\end{document} is the elastic modulus of the Maxwell model, MPa; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{M}$$\end{document} is the total stress of the Maxwell model, MPa.

Meanwhile, the viscoelastic element in the Maxwell model satisfies viscosity Newton law:5\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\frac{{\partial \varepsilon_{N} }}{\partial t} = \frac{{\sigma_{M} }}{{\eta_{1} }}$$\end{document}where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta_{1}$$\end{document} is the viscosity parameter of the Maxwell model, MPa h.

For the Kelvin model, under the action of the total stress \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{K}$$\end{document}, the strain of the two elements is the same, but the total stress is shared by the two elements due to the parallel connection between the elastic element and the viscoelastic element. The stress of the elastic element is set as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{Z}$$\end{document}; the stress of the viscoelastic element is set as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{X}$$\end{document}; and the total strain of the Kelvin model is set as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varepsilon_{K}$$\end{document}, as follows:7\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{K} = \sigma_{Z} + \sigma_{X}$$\end{document}

The stress–strain relationships of the elastic element and the viscoelastic element are described respectively as follows:8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{Z} = E_{2} \varepsilon_{K}$$\end{document}9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{X} = \eta_{2} \frac{{\partial \varepsilon_{K} }}{\partial t}$$\end{document}where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$E_{2}$$\end{document} is the instantaneous elastic modulus of the Kelvin model, MPa; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta_{2}$$\end{document} is the viscosity parameter of the Kelvin model, MPa h.

In the three-dimensional stress state, the stress tensor \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{ij}$$\end{document} inside the rock is composed of the spherical stress tensor \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{ij}$$\end{document} and the eccentric stress tensor \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{m}$$\end{document}, follows as:16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{ij} = S_{ij} + \delta_{ij} \sigma_{m}$$\end{document}where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta_{ij}$$\end{document} is the Kronecker function.

Among them, the spherical stress tensor represents a stress state in which forces are uniformly applied in all directions, and it only alters the volume of the micro-element within the rock but not its shape. The eccentric stress tensor represents the set of stress components that remain only related to shape distortion after stripping away the part of the total stress that causes volume change.

The volume change of the micro-element within the rock is regarded as elastic, and the rheological properties are caused by the eccentric strain within the rock micro-element. Then, the sample relaxation equation of the Burgers model in a three-dimensional stress state can be solved by connecting (6) to (21), as follows:19\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{ij} (t) = \frac{{2e_{ij}^{0} }}{{\sqrt {p_{1}^{2} - 4p_{2} } }}\left[ {\left( { - q_{1} + q_{2} \alpha } \right)\exp \left( { - \alpha t} \right) + \left( {q_{1} - q_{2} \beta } \right)\exp \left( { - \beta t} \right)} \right]$$\end{document}20\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_{1} = \frac{{\eta_{1} }}{{G_{1} }} + \frac{{\eta_{1} + \eta_{2} }}{{G_{2} }},p_{2} = \frac{{\eta_{1} \eta_{2} }}{{G_{1} G_{2} }},q_{1} = \eta_{1} ,q_{2} = \frac{{\eta_{1} \eta_{2} }}{{G_{2} }}$$\end{document}21\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha = \frac{1}{{2p_{2} }}\left( {p_{1} + \sqrt {p_{1}^{2} - 4p_{2} } } \right),\beta = \frac{1}{{2p_{2} }}\left( {p_{1} - \sqrt {p_{1}^{2} - 4p_{2} } } \right)$$\end{document}where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G_{1}$$\end{document} is the instantaneous shear modulus of the Maxwell model, Pa; Burgers stress relaxation model fitting method

According to formula (19) to (21), the stress relaxation equation of Burgers model is very complex, and the model parameters are difficult to obtain directly through the experiments or solve through the simple operations. The least square method or regression analysis method is usually adopted to obtain the four parameters of the stress relaxation equation of Burgers model to solve the problem of difficult model solution. Especially, the least square method is widely used to solve the stress relaxation equation of Burgers model due to the high fitting accuracy. However, this method still has the computational problem of inaccurate selection of the initial parameter values, which leads to slow convergence speed of iteration or non-convergence of iteration. To avoid the above computational problems, the improved least square method (Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm) is adopted to solve the parameters of the stress relaxation equation of Burgers model. So it is necessary to compare the shear modulus \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G$$\end{document} and the instantaneous shear modulus \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G_{1}$$\end{document} for the sample under the same acid etching conditions to further verify the correctness of the stress relaxation fitting results of Burgers.

Variation of the fitting parameters with acid etching conditions

Based on the fitting results of the sample stress relaxation curves, under the same strain level and acid etching time, compared with the acid etching temperatures of 40 °C and 80 °C, the instantaneous shear modulus \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G_{1}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G_{2}$$\end{document} and the viscosity coefficients \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta_{1}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta_{2}$$\end{document} fitted by Burgers model of the carbonate sample with the acid etching temperatures of 120 °C and 160 °C are smaller. Especially the sample viscosity coefficient values under high-temperature and low-temperature conditions differ significantly, even by 1–2 magnitude orders, as shown in Fig. 17. This phenomenon is closely related to the influence of temperature on the evolution of the internal micro-fractures and holes of the sample. On the one hand, under the same acid etching time and strain level, the higher the acid etching temperature, the more micro-fractures and holes inside the sample, the greater the sample deterioration degree, the weaker the sample mechanical properties, and the smaller the sample instantaneous sample shear modulus \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G_{1}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G_{2}$$\end{document}. On the other hand, the increase of the micro-fractures and holes within the sample leads to a reduction in the interaction forces between the sample particles, thereby enhancing the fluidity between the sample particles and further reducing the sample viscosity coefficients \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta_{1}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta_{2}$$\end{document}. In addition, during the sample stress relaxation experiment, the higher the experiment temperature, the faster the physical and chemical properties of the mineral particles that make up the sample change, which leads to more micro-fractures and holes inside the sample, thereby reducing the sample viscosity coefficient \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta_{1}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta_{2}$$\end{document}. Conclusions analysis

Conclusions

Multi-stage stress relaxation tests were performed on acid-etched core samples derived from the Dengying Formation. Comparative analysis was conducted between the shear modulus \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G$$\end{document} measured via tests and the instantaneous shear modulus \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G_{1}$$\end{document} serving as the fitting parameter. Then, the evolution patterns of acid etching duration and temperature on the fitting parameters of the Burgers model were quantitatively analyzed. The specific experimental conclusions are summarized as follows:A pronounced sudden stress drop phenomenon occurs during the stress relaxation process of acid-etched samples at a specific strain level. Under identical acid etching duration and temperature conditions, the initial stress, residual stress and the stabilization time required for stress relaxation of the acid-etched samples all increase with the rising strain level.The stress relaxation characteristics of acid-etched carbonate cores can be accurately characterized by Burgers model. The shear modulus \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G$$\end{document} obtained from the triaxial compression test is basically consistent with the instantaneous shear modulus \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G_{1}$$\end{document} fitted by the Burgers model, which further verifies the reliability of the adopted fitting model and the improved Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm.Under the same strain level and temperature, the instantaneous shear modulus \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G_{1}$$\end{document} exhibits a linear decreasing trend with prolonged acid etching duration, whereas the instantaneous shear modulus \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G_{2}$$\end{document} and viscosity coefficients \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta_{1}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta_{2}$$\end{document} decline exponentially. For a fixed strain level and acid etching duration, the Burgers model fitting parameters obtained at high temperatures (120 °C and 160 °C) are lower than those at low temperatures (40 °C and 80 °C).At a constant sample porosity, the overall fitting parameters of the model for acid-etched samples generally decrease with elevated temperature. Additionally, taking acid etching duration as the intermediate variable, the evolutionary equations characterizing the correlation between the overall model fitting parameters and porosity of acid-etched samples are established.

A pronounced sudden stress drop phenomenon occurs during the stress relaxation process of acid-etched samples at a specific strain level. Under identical acid etching duration and temperature conditions, the initial stress, residual stress and the stabilization time required for stress relaxation of the acid-etched samples all increase with the rising strain level.

Engineering implications

This study investigating the rheological mechanical properties of the carbonate rock subjected to acid etching carries highly direct and profound application significance for the optimal design of acid fracturing treatments. On the one hand, prolonged acid etching duration induces a pronounced exponential reduction in the instantaneous shear modulus and viscosity coefficient of the carbonate rock. This rheological degradation mechanism necessitates precise engineering control over key acid injection parameters, including acid volume, acid concentration, and acid-rock reaction time, to prevent excessive acidization that triggers severe deterioration of rock mechanical strength. Excessively extended acid etching will soften the rock matrix excessively, triggering substantial rheological deformation of fracture surfaces under the reservoir closure stress and ultimately compromising fracture conductivity. On the other hand, elucidating the creep and stress relaxation behaviors of acid-etched carbonate rocks enables targeted selection of specialized acid fluid systems (e.g., gelled acid, emulsified acid, and other retarded acid systems) tailored to the in-situ temperature and stress conditions of target reservoirs. Such tailored acid systems can decelerate the acid-rock reaction kinetics, realize deep acid penetration, and simultaneously mitigate the extent of rock strength weakening in the near-wellbore zone, thereby preventing fracture wall collapse and maintaining the structural stability of acid-etched fractures.

Limitations

On the one hand, constrained by the operational requirements of triaxial mechanical testing, the design of the acid etching experiments is subject to certain inherent limitations. During the core acidification tests in this study, it is difficult to fully replicate the in-situ fluid flow dynamics and seepage conditions within the actual reservoir environment, leading to discrepancies between laboratory simulation and subsurface engineering scenarios. On the other hand, pseudo-triaxial loading was adopted to conduct stress relaxation tests in this experimental scheme, whereas deep reservoir rocks are typically subjected to a true triaxial stress state characterized by unequal three principal stresses. Coupled with the extremely complex stress distribution surrounding the wellbore, the simplified stress loading path adopted in this work may fail to fully reproduce the progressive damage and rheological evolution mechanisms of carbonate rocks under actual downhole working conditions.
