Dynamic sparse point voxel transformer for 3D point cloud instance segmentation of dormant apple trees
Authors: Ruiming Du, Sinuo Li, Kenong Xu, Lailiang Cheng, Yu Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Three dimensional (3D) instance segmentation is essential for precision characterization of tree architecture at the branch level, which supports both tree fruit crop breeding and the development of robotic systems for orchard management. Existing methods usually use sparse convolution-based operation, which requires a coordinate quantization preprocess to generate sparse tensors, risking the loss of geometric details for fine-grained downstream phenotyping tasks. To overcome this challenge, we developed the dynamic sparse point-voxel transformer (DSPVFormer) model for the efficient and accurate 3D instance segmentation of high-resolution point clouds for dormant apple trees. Our hybrid DSPVFormer architecture maximizes the use of raw point features by dynamically mapping and aggregating the raw point features into the sparse voxel embeddings, capturing strong geometric features that may be discarded during quantization. Evaluations demonstrate that DSPVFormer achieved statistically significant improvements over baseline models on most instance segmentation metrics, which are further translated into more accurate phenotyping evaluation including branch counting and pruning map generation. These advances directly benefit downstream applications in plant phenotyping and robotic pruning for tree crops such as apples. Meanwhile, experimental results on phenotyping tasks suggested that phenotyping-specific evaluation metrics should be prioritized over upstream computer vision performance metrics to realize the full potential of high-throughput phenotyping for real-world applications.

Introduction

The tree fruit industry faces significant economic and logistical pressures primarily due to a high dependency on a diminishing workforce of skilled manual labor [1]. Pruning, particularly during the dormant seasons, is a key horticultural practice essential for managing tree architecture, optimizing light interception, and balancing vegetative growth with fruit production, directly influencing fruit quality and yield [2]. Labor shortage, increasing labor cost, and the requisite expertise to perform these precise tasks motivate the development of automated solutions [3]. Robotic pruning has emerged as a promising technology to address this gap, offering great potential to combine the precise pruning decision-making through advanced computer vision techniques with the efficiency of mechanization and automation [4].

The successful implementation of robotics pruning relies on an accurate and robust perception pipeline that can characterize complex three-dimensional (3D) structures of fruit trees. This requires the perception ability to distinguish individual tree organs from a dense, unstructured 3D representation, typically a point cloud acquired by LiDAR or RGB-D sensors [5,6]. Therefore, the instance-level 3D segmentation of identifying each branch and trunk serves as the foundational perception capability, which enables subsequent phenotyping tasks such as branch skeletonization and pruning strategy determination [3].

3D segmentation-based plant phenotyping has largely evolved with the advances of 3D sensing and computer vision techniques [7,8]. This progress has been further strengthened by the recent efforts that focus on deep-learning (DL) based solutions with superior ability learning geometric features directly from data [9,10]. In general, these learning approaches for point cloud segmentation can be categorized into three types: point-based, voxel-based, and hybrid methods. Point-based methods, pioneered by architectures like PointNet [11], operate directly on the raw, unstructured point clouds. These models excel at preserving fine-grained geometric details. However, their reliance on point down-sampling limits their capability to capture sufficient receptive field and the scalability to efficiently process large, dense point clouds with satisfactory accuracy.

Conversely, voxel-based methods discretize the 3D space into a regular grid of voxels and apply 3D convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to this structured representation [12]. This approach benefits from hierarchical convolution through the encoder-decoder architecture and can efficiently capture a broader spatial context. However, the primary drawback of dense 3D convolution is the high computational cost and memory consumption, which limits its scalability to analyze high-resolution point clouds. This presents significant challenges for processing plant point clouds because most of the plant point clouds are collected through laser scanning with high spatial resolution aiming for precise downstream phenotyping applications such as characterizing thin branches [13] or tiny organs [14]. The needs for 3D segmentation-based plant phenotyping emphasize the ability to process high-resolution point clouds efficiently and accurately.

Since most of the point clouds obtained through LiDAR or RGB-D sensors only represent the surface of the objects, after voxelization, the resulting voxels are usually highly sparse. This motivates exploiting the sparsity within the data [15,16]. In this context, sparse convolution is a voxel-based cornerstone technique which uses expressive and generalizable sparse tensors (i.e., sparse voxels) to represent input data and convolves only on non-empty spaces [17], significantly reducing the computational cost and memory consumption. Following this, many sparse convolution-based algorithms [18] and engineering-efficient backends (e.g., SpConv [19], Minkowski Engine [20], TorchSparse [21]) have been proposed for 3D segmentation tasks, showing great compatibility and potential for processing high-resolution plant point clouds [22]. Nevertheless, even with these efficient tools, aggressively increasing the resolution of sparse representation (i.e., the number of occupied voxels) or purely expanding the feature dimensions of sparse convolutional layers may still lead to significant computation overhead (i.e., the curse of dimensionality) [20]. Besides, the sparse voxels may also cause detail loss through stride convolutions on deeper layers. This inspires the development of hybrid methods that leverage both point-based and sparse voxel-based methods to complement each other [23,24], which underline two key advantages: 1) efficient computation on sparse voxels and feature extraction from sparse convolutions in voxel-based branch, and 2) fine-grained information compensatory from low-weight point-based branch.

In practice, an essential step in sparse convolutional pipelines is the construction of sparse tensors via coordinate quantization [20,25]. This process aggregates points within a voxel grid and only keeps a single representative point per occupied voxel, substantially reducing computational cost and enabling efficient processing of large-scale and sparse 3D data. Building upon this approach, recent hybrid point-voxel methods further introduce point-based branches operating on quantized coordinates, aiming to compensate for the detail loss caused by voxel down-sampling in the sparse convolutional branch. Such designs have proven effective for 3D perception in autonomous driving and urban scenes, where point clouds are highly sparse, and appropriate quantization would significantly reduce the computation cost and memory consumption without compromising too much information from the raw inputs.

However, 3D plant point clouds pose a fundamentally different challenge in this context. High-resolution plant scans are characterized by dense, highly clustered points, fine-grained structures, and complex self-occlusions, especially around thin branches and junctions. Under voxel quantization, many geometrically distinct points may be projected into the same voxel and subsequently discarded, leading to loss of critical morphological details that are essential for downstream phenotyping tasks. This challenge leads to two computational questions: 1) Can raw point-level features that are otherwise lost during quantization be dynamically leveraged to enhance sparse voxel representation for improved segmentation? 2) How does such an improvement in segmentation quality affect downstream phenotyping applications?

To address these questions, we proposed the dynamic sparse point-voxel transformer (DSPVFormer) for instance segmentation of dormant apple tree point clouds. The key contribution lies in developing the dynamic point-voxel feature interaction mechanism, which integrates raw point features into sparse voxel embeddings based on the dynamic mapping of raw points and voxels, rather than solely relying on static quantized representations. This design enables the network to emphasize fine-grained plant structures that are vulnerable to quantization-based information loss, while retaining the computational advantages of sparse convolution. Furthermore, we systematically evaluate the impact of DSPVFormer on downstream phenotyping tasks, including branch counting and pruning decision making, to analyze the impact of instance segmentation improvement on practical phenotyping applications.

DSPVFormer: dynamic sparse point-voxel transformer

Model architecture overview

The DSPVFormer is a hybrid deep segmentation neural network that leverages the point features to enhance the voxel embeddings. It mainly contains three parts: 1) a dynamic quantization encoder, 2) a sparse point-voxel convolution semantic head (SPVConv), and 3) a transformer decoder instance head (Fig. 1). Before the input into the semantic head (i.e., SPVConv), the raw input point cloud P∈RNp×Cin (where Np is the number of points in the input point cloud) is fed into a dual-path encoder which consists of both sparse quantization and dynamic mapping to combine the point-wise features into the voxel embeddings V∈RNv×Cin (where Nv is the number of voxels (i.e., quantized points), and Nv ≪ Np). Notably, the sparse quantization and dynamic mapping share the same point-to-voxel mapping to recover raw point resolution from voxels in the inference stage. After this, SPVConv converts the augmented voxel embeddings into voxel sparse tensors V″∈RNv×C. This sparse voxel embeddings with initialized semantic and instance queries are fed into three sequential transformer decoder layers to predict the semantic masks Msem∈RKsem×Nv and instance masks Mins∈RKins×Nv (where Ksem and Kins are the number of semantic and instance queries defined by the user, respectively). Finally, the segmentation results are obtained through thresholding these masks with the mask scores.Fig. 1DSPVFormer architecture overview. Dual-path encoder

Sparse quantization serves as an essential preprocessing step that transforms raw point clouds into sparse tensor representations for efficient convolution computation, but it inevitably introduces geometric information loss (Equations (1) and (2)):(1)qi=pi/v,i=1,…,Np(2)V={q1,…,qNp},|V|=Nv

where pi∈R3 is the i-th raw point coordinate, v is the voxel size, and qi is the quantized voxel coordinate. Because multiple points can share the same quantized coordinate, the sparse quantized set V only keeps the Nv unique voxel coordinates, reducing the resolution from Np to Nv. This deduplication defines a surjective point-to-voxel mapping ϕ: {1, …, Np} → {1, …, Nv}, where only one representative point per voxel is retained. Since Nv ≪ Np in practice, the individual geometric detail of non-representative points is lost through the Np → Nv mapping.

To address this challenge, we propose a dual-path encoder that maximizes the utilization of raw point features while maintaining the capability of the subsequent sparse convolution. The dual-path encoder operates along two complementary pathways that share the same point-to-voxel mapping, ensuring spatial alignment between the raw points and their corresponding voxels (quantized coordinates). The sparse quantization path follows the standard coordinate quantization process, which retains one representative point per occupied voxel following a linear transformation. In contrast, the dynamic mapping path only provides the raw point-to-voxel mapping (Np → Nv) without removing any points, and it incorporates a scatter encoder (Fig. 1(b)) that encodes raw point-wise features into enriched voxel embeddings by aggregating information from all points falling within that voxel, rather than discarding the features of non-representative points. At the end of the dual-path encoder, the voxel embeddings from both pathways are concatenated (V′∈RNv×C, where C = 64 + 32 = 96 in our implementation) for the subsequent processing.

This dual aggregation strategy ensures that the middle voxel embedding V′ captures not only the geometric information from the representative points through quantization but also the collective characteristics of all points contained within that voxel through the scatter encoder, retaining a combination of the original geometric and refined information from the input point cloud.

Sparse point-voxel convolution

A powerful semantic head is crucial for extracting the semantic features from the voxel embeddings for downstream segmentation tasks. The sparse point-voxel convolution (SPVConv) has been proven very effective for point cloud segmentation [24]. SPVConv is first proposed in [24], refactored in mmdetection3D library [28], and then adopted in Plant Segmentation Studio (PSS) [22] for plant point cloud semantic and instance segmentation. In our implementation, we followed the settings in PSS to integrate the SPVConv into the DSPVFormer.

SPVConv is a sparse convolution-based hybrid network that operates on quantized point clouds. The quantized points are further voxelized for sparse convolution in the sparse voxel-based branch and it's combined with a lightweight multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to have a more fine-grained feature from the quantized points in the point-based branch. These two parallel branches significantly enhance the segmentation performance by leveraging the complementary strengths from both representations. The input to SPVConv is the catenated voxel embeddings V′∈RNv×C from the dual-path encoder. Detailed introduction of SPVConv and our implementation can be found in previous studies [22,24].

Transformer decoder

The transformer decoder plays a pivotal role in the DSPVFormer architecture by leveraging both self-attention and cross-attention, allowing the model to capture complex global relationships between feature representations and segmentation queries.

In our implementation, the transformer decoder consists of three sequential layers which takes the sparse voxel embeddings V″∈RNv×C from the SPVConv semantic head as input, as well as a set of learnable semantic and instance queries. At each layer, self-attention facilitates interaction among queries, while cross-attention enables each query to selectively aggregate information from all voxel locations. The queries are iteratively refined by the transformer decoder layers, progressively capturing richer information. Following this refinement, the decoder generates sparse voxel-wise semantic masks Msem∈RKsem×Nv and instance masks Mins∈RKins×Nv, which are then thresholded based on the mask scores and incorporated with the voxel-to-point mapping in dual-path encoder to generate the final point-wise segmentation outputs.

Integrating the transformer decoder into DSPVFormer presents significant benefits. The sparse voxel and query features communication from the attention mechanism enables the network to model rich contextual relationships that are crucial for distinguishing complex organ structures and instances within plant point clouds. Additionally, the query-based design streamlines the simultaneous prediction of multiple semantic classes and object instances, improving both efficiency and segmentation accuracy. The original architectural and our implementation details about the transformer decoder can be found in [18] and [22].

Model implementation, training, and evaluation

For sparse quantization and dynamic mapping, we used a voxel size of 0.005 × 0.005 × 0.005. We set the maximum number of input points to 180,000 for a more cost-efficient training and testing. This threshold can be adjusted according to specific tasks. In the dual-path encoder, the final concatenated voxel embeddings feature from sparse quantization branch is 64, and 32 from the dynamic mapping branch. We intentionally reduced the embedded features from scatter encoder since Np features are encoded into Nv, and usually Nv ≪ Np. Large feature dimension from scatter encoder may lead to unstable training and testing. For SPVConv, the encoder and decoder features were [96, 96, 128, 256] and [256, 128, 96, 96], respectively, and we used the torchsparse library [21,24] for sparse convolution. For transformer decoder, we used 2 semantic queries and 150 instance queries (i.e., Ksem = 2, Kins = 150).

During training, we used the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 0.0001 and weight decay of 0.05, the batch size was 4 and the maximum number of training epochs was 512. During testing, we used the epoch with the best performance on AP and set the batch size to 1. For other configurations, we followed the default settings in the plant segmentation studio (PSS) framework [22]. All experiments in this study were conducted in PSS on an Ubuntu (20.04 LTS) system with an NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU (48 GB memory).

DSPVFormer was evaluated on the COS dataset against representative baseline models under the SS and RS partitioning strategies. Based on recent advances in point cloud instance segmentation, SoftGroup and Mink-SG were selected as grouping-based baselines, while OneFormer3D and SPVFormer were selected as query-based baselines. Mean intersection over union (mIoU) and average precision (AP) were used as the evaluation metrics for semantic and instance segmentation tasks.

The pipeline focuses on two primary phenotypic traits derived from segmented branch instances. Branch count is defined as the number of detected branches per tree and derived from the branch instance segmentation results where the branch instance masks are thresholded at different IoU thresholds (0.25, 0.5, and 0.9) to generate the branch count. Each of the models from the 10 repeated trials under SS was integrated with the pipeline for phenotypic trait extraction and subsequent phenotyping performance evaluation.

Branch skeletonization

Branch skeletonization is a pivotal step in branch-level phenotyping to simplify the characterization of the complex 3D structure of branches into skeleton points that capture the main branch topology. We adopted the pc-skeletor library [29] to extract branch skeletons from a branch instance mask that was either annotated manually or inferenced by a model such as DSPVFormer (Fig. 2). Pc-skeletor uses a Laplacian-based contraction to extract the contracted points (i.e., skeleton points) by iteratively contracting the input point cloud [30]. In order to alleviate the artifacts from the skeletonization process, especially around the junction between branches and trunk or among adjacent branches, we excluded the trunk points and iteratively extracted the skeleton for each segmented branch. After this process, the segmented branch instances were used for branch counting and length calculation. Subsequently, we re-assembled all the branch skeletons to form the skeleton for that tree without the trunk points (Fig. 2(b)) for successive pruning decision making.Fig. 2Branch skeletonization pipeline. Ablation experiments

In addition to segmentation and phenotyping evaluation, ablation studies were carried out to further understand DSPVFormer architecture and its generalizability. Specifically, we analyzed the contributions of major modules in DSPVFormer, impacts on model performance using different query options in the transformer decoder, and model generalizability on additional datasets beyond COS. For ablation experiments evaluation, we reported the mean and standard deviation of mIoU and AP across 5 repeated trials.

Dual-path encoder contribution

A major module of DSPVFormer is the dual-path encoder that explicitly aggregates and uses features from both quantized voxels and raw points. To investigate the role of the dual-path encoder, we tested three additional encoder configurations including i) only sparse quantization encoder, ii) only dynamic mapping encoder, and iii) dual-path encoder with element-wise addition, and compared them with the default DSPVFormer with dual-path encoder and channel-wise concatenation. All models were trained on COS using the SS partitioning strategy with the same optimization schedule and hyperparameters as the default DSPVFormer. AP of branch instance segmentation was used as the evaluation metric because the dual-path encoder aimed at capturing granular details from raw points for plant organs rather than major components such as tree trunk.

Decoder query option impact

Although DSPVFormer is designed for instance segmentation, it predicts both semantic and instance segmentation masks through the transformer decoder with both types of queries, exploiting the complementary features provided by the two tasks. To examine this query design choice, we evaluated DSPVFormer models with three query configurations: instance queries only (semantic outputs disabled), semantic queries only (instance outputs disabled), and joint training with both query types enabled. All the models were trained on COS using the SS partitioning strategy. The mIoU and class-wise IoU were used as the evaluation metrics for semantic segmentation, while AP was used for instance segmentation.

Model generalizability

While DSPVFormer is designed for instance segmentation of dormant trees in high-density 3D point clouds at the organ level, it is important to assess its generalizability to other 3D point cloud datasets for plant phenotyping. To this end, we tested and compared DSPVFormer with the four baseline models on three additional datasets. One dataset consists of point clouds of apple trees acquired using a consumer grade RGB-D camera [31], representing the same crop species but with relatively lower point density and resolution. The other two datasets, Pheno4D-RS [32] and ZJURapeSeed [14] datasets, contain high density 3D point clouds of maize, tomato, and rapeseed plants, representing different crop species. The same training configuration described in Section 2.2.5 was used, and mIoU and AP were used as evaluation metrics for semantic and instance segmentation performance, respectively.

Statistical analysis

To statistically analyze model performance, the Kruskal-Wallis test (SciPy 1.15.3) was used to evaluate the statistical significance of performance metrics for different models in all the experiments. Post-hoc pairwise comparisons were conducted using Dunn's test with Holm adjustment (scikit-posthocs 0.12.0) to control the family-wise error rate.

Random split (RS) results

When the RS data partitioning was used, DSPVFormer consistently achieved the best AP for instance segmentation, whereas Mink-SG showed the highest mIoU (93.19%) for semantic segmentation (Table 2). SoftGroup still showed a substantially lower AP yet with a reasonable mIoU (92.68%), whereas Mink-SG boosted the instance segmentation to an AP of 57.92%. These observations confirmed that DSPVFormer can achieve steady improvements in instance segmentation while maintaining comparable semantic segmentation accuracy regardless of data partitioning. Additionally, the Minkowski sparse convolution is the driving factor to the instance segmentation performance boost as compared to the SoftGroup baseline, which aligns with previous studies [22]. Detailed statistical tests for the random split are reported in Tables S3 and S4 in the Supplementary Material.

It is noteworthy that DSPVFormer achieved the highest AP for branch instance segmentation under both SS and RS settings, yet did not attain the highest mIoU in semantic segmentation, particularly among transformer-decoder-based models. To better understand this discrepancy, we further analyze class-level semantic segmentation performance for trunks and branches, and examine potential causes related to the choice of query types in the transformer decoder (Section 3.3.2).

Branch pruning results

DSPVFormer achieved the best pruning accuracy with a CD of 27.65 ± 1.72 (mm), followed by SPVFormer (27.81 ± 1.77 (mm)) and OneFormer3D (28.97 ± 1.14 (mm)) (Table 3). It should be noted that such pruning accuracy improvement of CD could be only numerical (Fig. S1(c) in the Supplementary Material), indicating potential performance fluctuations in downstream tasks despite solid segmentation improvements. Detailed statistical tests for branch counting and pruning-map characterization are reported in Tables S5 and S6 in the Supplementary Material.

Because of the high pruning accuracy, qualitative pruning results from the proposed pipeline could inform branch pruning decision (Fig. 3). Three types of pruning errors were observed from the results. The first one was mis-pruning (Fig. 3(a)). This was caused by the incorrect segmentation in which multiple segments were predicted within a single branch instance. The second one was over-pruning led by the incompleteness of the input branch point cloud (Fig. 3(b)). Ablation studies

Dual-path encoder

The dual-path encoder with feature concatenation achieved the best AP for branch instance segmentation, followed by dynamic mapping alone for 56.69%, sparse quantization alone for 56.36%, and dual-path encoder with element-wise feature addition for 55.34% (Table 4). Dynamic mapping alone outperforming sparse quantization alone demonstrates that aggregating features from all raw points within a voxel via the scatter encoder produces improved expressiveness than retaining only one representative point from that voxel. Channel-wise concatenation of both pathways surpassed either single pathway, indicating that the two encoders capture complementary information that is best preserved as separate feature channels. Element-wise addition even underperformed than the single-pathway variants, showing that fusing the two feature streams into a shared channel before the semantic head collapses their structural distinctiveness. Overall, semantic-only training yielded the highest IoU-Trunk (94.42%), IoU-Branch (89.39%), and mIoU (91.90%) among the three settings, whereas joint training achieved the highest AP (57.43%) compared with 56.36% for instance-only training (Table 5). Semantic-only training attains the best mIoU because the full decoder capacity is dedicated to per-point label prediction without competing instance attention. The mIoU of joint training was somewhat negligible (0.09%) compared to that of semantic-only, confirming that the trade-off strongly favors joint optimization for phenotyping-focused applications. Cross-dataset evaluation

Among the three additional datasets, DSPVFormer achieved the highest mIoU (95.07%) on the apple tree dataset collected by Kinect v2, the best AP for stem and leaf on Pheno4D-RS, while Mink-SG achieved the highest branch AP (43.98%) on the apple tree dataset and the best AP and mIoU on ZJURapeSeed (Table 6). These results indicate that DSPVFormer generalizes most consistently to dense tree-like structures, whereas grouping-based Mink-SG remains advantageous for packed, repetitive small-organ segmentation such as siliques. It was noteworthy that Mink-SG substantially outperformed all three transformer-decoder models for silique AP but drastically underperformed on Pheno4D-RS, suggesting possible dataset-dependent model architecture preference. In particular, this performance pattern was related with the point density (i.e., number of points per semantic class or instance). For instance, Pheno4D-RS has the highest point density at the semantic level (Fig. 4(a)), whereas the silique instances in the ZJURapeSeed dataset have a considerably lower density than the instances in other datasets (Fig. 4(b)). Detailed discussion on this aspect is provided in section 4.3. Discussion

Dual-path encoder architecture

The ablation results (Section 3.3, Table 4) demonstrate the main motivation for DSPVFormer: that voxel quantization discards geometrically meaningful information in dense plant point clouds, and that a dedicated pathway aggregating all within-voxel raw points is necessary to recover it. The consistent advantage of channel-wise concatenation over both single-pathway configurations confirms that sparse quantization and dynamic mapping serve structurally complementary representational roles, where the former mostly preserving point positional precision and the latter largely capturing collective within-voxel distributions. The failure of element-wise addition further indicates that summation-based feature fusion is less effective in this hybrid architecture. From a model design perspective, these findings suggest that when constructing hybrid encoders for dense plant point cloud segmentation, feature fusion through concatenation is preferable. Furthermore, as supported by the cross-dataset results (Section 3.4), the benefit of dynamic mapping is expected to scale with more raw points per organ instance rather than with the overall sample-level density alone. When each instance contains few or noisy points, details from raw points within a voxel can be limited, resulting in effectiveness reduction of the dual-path encoder.

Query optimization during training

The query ablation (Table 5) shows that joint training of semantic and instance queries reduces mIoU by a small margin (0.09%) relative to semantic-only training, with the small drop distributed across both IoU-Trunk (0.04%) and IoU-Branch (0.15%) rather than concentrated in any single semantic category (Table S9 in the Supplementary Material). When both query types are active, the transformer decoder shifts representational emphasis toward instance segmentation precision, at a slight expense of per-point semantic performance, consistent with the implicit regularization commonly observed in multi-task attention architectures. For the branch-level phenotyping tasks targeted in this study (i.e., branch counting and length-based pruning), accurate instance delineation is the critical requirement, making this small mIoU trade-off acceptable.

Cross-dataset performance

The dataset-dependent ranking of DSPVFormer and Mink-SG can be more clearly interpreted by examining point cloud density: the total number of points per semantic class per sample (Fig. 4(a)) and the number of points per instance (Fig. 4(b)). DSPVFormer achieved the strongest instance AP on COS and on the stem and leaf classes of Pheno4D-RS. These are precisely the cases in which each organ instance is supported by a relatively large amount of raw points: roughly 103-104 points per branch in COS and 104-105 points per stem/leaf in Pheno4D-RS. Mink-SG, in contrast, led branch AP on Apple tree (Kinect V2) and dominated silique AP on ZJURapeSeed. Apple tree (Kinect V2) point clouds are not only sparse at the sample level (∼104 points) but are also acquired with a consumer-grade depth sensor, so each branch instance contains comparatively more noisy points. ZJURapeSeed siliques are even more extreme: although the per-sample point count is moderate (Fig. 4(a)), each silique instance receives one to two orders of magnitude fewer raw points than branch instances in COS (Fig. 4(b)). Thus, all three transformer-decoder models were greatly worse than Mink-SG on silique AP under this regime.

This pattern suggests that DSPVFormer, and query-based architectures more generally, deliver their best performance when each organ instance has sufficiently large raw points to expose local geometric details that the dual-path encoder can aggregate through dynamic mapping. When organ instances contain only a limited number of points, or when those points are themselves of low fidelity, the additional signal that dynamic mapping is designed to recover is simply not present, so the dual-path encoder offers diminishing returns. In such cases, grouping-based pipelines such as Mink-SG that propagate sparse-convolution features through local clustering tend to be more competitive, as observed for Kinect-derived apple trees and densely packed siliques. As a practical guideline, DSPVFormer is the preferred choice when each target instance is densely sampled, whereas grouping-based methods such as Mink-SG remain the more robust option for sparser or lower-quality acquisitions and for densely packed small-organ segmentation.

State-of-the-art metrics vs. practical phenotyping applications

Indeed the DSPVFormer achieved statistically significant AP differences against some baselines in SS, however, the segmentation and phenotyping improvements did not consistently achieve statistical significance, rather they are only numerically different on RS, and also on the accuracy of pruning map characterization as measured by the Chamfer distance. These findings suggest two things: 1) The statistical significance of the segmentation accuracy improvements may be less solid by only evaluating the model on a single split of the datasets. To get a more robust statistical evaluation, the model should be evaluated on multiple splits of the datasets. 2) The incremental advances in segmentation metrics do not always guarantee equivalent improvements in subsequent phenotyping outputs such as pruning guidance. While optimizing for state-of-the-art metrics (e.g., AP) often serves as the primary benchmark and research focus for 3D plant segmentation, researchers should recognize that practical phenotyping applications, in our case, the branch trait measurement and pruning map production, may be less sensitive to small advances in segmentation accuracy.

Limitations and future work

The input data quality and quantity are very important for both the segmentation task and phenotyping applications. The noise and incompleteness in input point clouds can directly result in branch segmentation and tree pruning decision errors when constructing the skeleton graph (Fig. 3). Moreover, the statistical power of the comparison between methods may be constrained by the sample size. Currently, the input point clouds are collected by a commercial 3D scanner with limited views and low throughput in the orchard, which may not be able to capture complete and precise tree structures especially for thin branches. Future work should focus on improving the data quality and quantity through either more advanced 3D sensing hardware with higher throughput and precision, or more advanced 3D reconstruction algorithms with better inference speed and reconstruction quality.

Conclusion

In this study, we proposed DSPVFormer for the instance segmentation of dormant apple tree point clouds. DSPVFormer achieves the best instance segmentation and satisfactory semantic segmentation on the high-density apple tree dataset. While such segmentation improvements increased the phenotyping accuracy, the accuracy increase varied considerably with no consistent statistical significance. This inspires us to rethink the research intention for plant phenotyping requiring 3D point cloud segmentation: effective advances in 3D plant phenotyping would not only require state-of-the-art segmentation performance but also other factors (e.g., data quality) that influence the practical realization of such segmentation performance improvements in real-world applications.

For the wider adoption and impact of advanced 3D plant segmentation models in real-world agriculture applications, it is important to evaluate model performance using metrics for both computer vision tasks and phenotyping-specific applications. Collaborative efforts with domain experts are also essential to define phenotyping metrics that are both technically robust and agronomically meaningful, and to ensure that advances in algorithm development are translated into tangible benefits for crop management and breeding programs.
