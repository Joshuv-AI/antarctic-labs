Effective electron coupling to phonon mechanical angular momentum in helical systems
Akihito Kato, Nobuhiko Yokoshi, Jun-ichiro Kishine

Abstract
In chiral crystals, two types of phonon angular momenta have been introduced. One is crystal angular momentum (CAM) arising from the rotational or screw-rotational symmetry and the other is mechanical angular momentum (MAM) associated with the circular motion of atomic displacements about equilibrium positions. Recently, the electron–phonon coupling that respects the screw-rotational symmetry is derived, whereby the CAM between electrons and phonons is interconverted. Here, we show that, in addition to CAM, MAM can also be converted to the electronic degrees of freedom by deriving a second-order perturbative Hamiltonian proportional to phonon MAM. This finding highlights that the electronic motion is directly affected by phonon MAM, and consequently, that phonon degrees of freedom can play a crucial role in phenomena related to electronic orbital and spin polarizations.

I
Introduction

Chirality is defined as a lack of improper rotational symmetry.
In chiral crystals, this symmetry breaking induces a phonon-band splitting between modes of opposite crystal angular momentum (CAM)
Zhang and Niu 2015
;
Zhang and Murakami 2022
;
Tsunetsugu and Kusunose 2023
;
Kato and Kishine 2023
, which was recently confirmed by Raman and resonant inelastic X-ray scatterings using the circularly polarized light
Ishito et al. 2022
;
Ishito et al. 2023
;
Oishi et al. 2024
;
Ueda et al. 2023
.
CAM, also referred to as pseudo angular momentum, is a conserved quantity arising from the discrete rotational or screw-rotational symmetry of the crystal, which was originally introduced for the classification of electronic bands
Božovic 1984
.
CAM is distinct from mechanical angular momentum (MAM)
Zhang and Niu 2014
, which is associated with circular motion of atomic displacements about equilibrium positions and thus with phonon spin angular momentum
Vonsovskii and Svirskii 1962
, in analogy to the photon spin angular momentum of circularly polarized light
(see Table
1
for the comparison of two angular momenta).
The chirality-induced phonon-band splitting is expected to provide a route for the interconversion of angular momentum between electrons and phonons via electron–phonon interactions.
This consideration may be relevant to clarifying the role of phonon degrees of freedom in chirality-induced spin selectivity
Bloom et al. 2024
;
Fransson 2020
;
Du et al. 2020
;
Fransson 2023
;
Funato et al. 2024
;
Inui et al. 2020
and other chirality-induced phenomena
Rikken et al. 2002
;
Furukawa et al. 2017
;
Ohe et al. 2024
;
Bousquet et al. 2025
.

In this study, we focus on the phonon MAM and its interaction with electronic degrees of freedom, while neglecting electronic spin, through the investigation of the electron–phonon interaction inherent in chiral crystals. Recently, characteristic features of the microscopic electron–phonon interaction in chiral systems have been derived by two of the present authors
Tateishi et al. 2025
: Screw-rotational symmetry is respected, whereby the appropriate rotational phase factors of equivalent atoms in the unit cell
Hu et al. 2024
are preserved. As a consequence, selection rules that describe the interconversion of CAM between electrons and phonons are derived. Additionally, in contrast to conventional theoretical frameworks
Rössler 2009
, transverse phonons, thereby mechanically circulating phonons, couples to electrons. This latter point motivates us to explore the possibility of interconversion between phonon MAM and electronic degrees of freedom.

The present work develops this previous study to elucidate the effect of the phonon MAM on an electronic system.
For this purpose, second-order perturbation theory is employed via the Schrieffer–Wolff transformation
Schrieffer and Wolff 1966
;
Winkler 2003
, whereby direct coupling of electrons to the phonon MAM is demonstrated.
This result establishes that not only the phonon CAM but also the phonon MAM can be converted to an electronic system and thereby advances our understanding of various electronic and phononic phenomena observed in chiral systems.

The remaining part of this paper is organized as follows:
In Sec.
II
, we introduce a model single helix and its Hamiltonian consisting of the electronic and phononic degrees of freedom.
In Sec.
III
, we perform the Schrieffer–Wolff transformation to obtain the the second-order perturbation terms of the electron–phonon Hamiltonian, and clarify that a second-order term is proportional to the phonon MAM.
We also discuss the limitation and generality of our result and briefly mention its effect on the electronic orbital angular momentum.
Section
IV
is devoted to the concluding remarks.

II
Model

For simplicity, we consider a single helix in chiral tellurium (
Te
) belonging to the space group
P3_{1}21
.
The helix is assumed to consist of
N
unit cells, and atoms along the helix are indexed by an integer
\ell
(
=1,2,\ldots,3N
).
The equilibrium position of the
\ell
th atom is given by
\bm{R}_{\ell}=(\rho\cos((\ell-1)\alpha),\rho\sin((\ell-1)\alpha),c(\ell-1)/3)^{\top}
,
where
\rho
is the helix radius,
c
is the lattice constant along the
z
axis, and
\alpha=2\pi/3
is the rotation angle.
The helix is taken to belong to the line group
L3_{1}=\{\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{p}\mid p=0,\ldots,3N-1\}

Božovic 1984
;
Bozovic et al. 1978
, generated by the threefold screw operator
\hat{\mathcal{R}}=(\hat{C}_{3}\mid c/3)
, which is expressed as a combination of the threefold rotation
\hat{C}_{3}
and the
c/3
translation along the
z
axis.
The irreducible representation (irrep.) of this group is specified by the crystal wavenumber
k=2\pi n/cN
with
n=0,1,\ldots,N-1
and by the CAM
m=0,\pm 1
.
Hereafter, these are collectively denoted by
\Gamma=(k,m)=(k_{\Gamma},m_{\Gamma})
.
The character of the irrep.
\Gamma
for
\hat{\mathcal{R}}^{p}
is given by
\chi_{\Gamma}(p)=e^{-ip(kc/3+m\alpha)}
.
Reflection about
xz
plane transforms the helix to its enantiomer belonging to the line group
L3_{2}
, which corresponds to inverting the rotation angle,
\alpha=2\pi/3\to-2\pi/3
.
Because this operation also inverts CAM,
m\to-m
, the
L3_{2}
counterpart of subsequent results of
L3_{1}
is easily obtained by changing the irreps.
\Gamma=(k,m)
to the irreps.
\bar{\Gamma}=(k,-m)
or
(-k,m)
, which is the chiral partner of
\Gamma
.
In accordance with the electronic case, the phononic system is subject to the crystal symmetry, and its states are thus expressed by the irrep. of the group.

The Hamiltonian of the electron–phonon coupled system is decomposed into three ones,

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{ph}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el-ph}}
.
First, the electronic Hamiltonian is written in diagonal form as

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}=\sum_{\Gamma,\mu}E_{\Gamma\mu}\outerproduct*{\Psi_{\Gamma}^\mu}{\Psi_{\Gamma}^\mu},

(1)

where
E_{\Gamma\mu}
and
\ket*{\Psi_{\Gamma}^\mu}
denote the band energy and the corresponding eigenstate of the
\mu
th branch in the irrep.
\Gamma
, respectively (see Appendix
A.1
).
The phononic system is described by the displacement vector about equilibrium position with the irrep.
\Gamma
,
\bm{u}_{\Gamma}=\hat{u}_{\Gamma}\bm{v}_{\Gamma}
, and its conjugate momentum operator,
\bm{p}_{\Gamma}=\hat{p}_{\Gamma}\bm{v}_{-\Gamma}
, where
\hat{u}_{\Gamma}
and
\hat{p}_{\Gamma}
satisfy the commutation relation
[\hat{u}_{\Gamma},\hat{p}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}]=i\hbar\delta_{\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}}
, and
\bm{v}_{\Gamma}
is the eigenvector of the dynamical matrix with the eigenvalue
\omega_{\Gamma}^{2}
being the square of the frequency (the phononic band).
The phononic Hamiltonian is then given by

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{ph}}=\sum_{\Gamma}(\frac{1}{2M}\hat{p}_{-\Gamma}\hat{p}_{\Gamma}+\frac{M\omega_{\Gamma}^{2}}{2}\hat{u}_{-\Gamma}\hat{u}_{\Gamma}),

(2)

where
M
is the atomic mass.
In this study, only the phononic bands within the single branch is considered, and therefore, the branch index is omitted.

Finally, the electron–phonon coupling Hamiltonian,
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el-ph}}
,
derived in Ref.
Tateishi et al. 2025
(for details, see Appendix
A.2
), is written as

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el-ph}}=\sum_{\Gamma}\hat{\bm{u}}_{\Gamma}\cdot\hat{\bm{W}}_{\Gamma}=\sum_{\Gamma}\hat{u}_{-\Gamma}\hat{W}_{\Gamma},

(3)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{W}}_{\Gamma}=\sum_{\gamma,\mu\mu^{\prime}}\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma+\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}\outerproduct*{\Psi_\gamma^\mu}{\Psi_{\gamma+\Gamma}^{\mu'}},

(4)

\displaystyle\hat{W}_{\Gamma}=\bm{v}_{\Gamma}\cdot\hat{\bm{W}}_{\Gamma}=\sum_{\gamma,\mu\mu^{\prime}}V_{\gamma\mu,\gamma+\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}\outerproduct*{\Psi_\gamma^\mu}{\Psi_{\gamma+\Gamma}^{\mu'}},

(5)

define the electronic contribution.
Here, the coupling vector
\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma+\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}
represents the susceptibility for the electronic transition
\ket*{\Psi_\gamma^\mu}\to\ket*{\Psi_{\gamma+\Gamma}^{\mu'}}
, and thus,
V_{\gamma\mu,\gamma+\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}=\bm{v}_{\Gamma}\cdot\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma+\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}
is the coupling constant projected onto the phonon eigenmode
\bm{v}_{\Gamma}
.
It should be noted that not only the longitudinal but also the transverse components of
\bm{v}_{\Gamma}
enter the coupling, indicating that the circulating phonons carrying angular momentum can influence electronic motion.
This feature arises because the Hamiltonian is derived while respecting the screw-rotational symmetry of the helix, whose action induces both longitudinal and transverse motions,
whereas the conventional framework relies on lattice translational symmetry and involves only the longitudinal motion.
Moreover, the coupling
\bm{v}_{\Gamma}\cdot\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma+\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}
directly reflects the selection rule:
electronic transition from the irrep.
\gamma+\Gamma
to
\gamma
are mediated only by the phonon modes of the irrep.
\Gamma
.
All Hamiltonians introduced above satisfy time-reversal symmetry, as expressed by the relations

E_{\Gamma\mu}=E_{-\Gamma\mu}
,
\omega_{\Gamma}=\omega_{-\Gamma}
, and
\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}=\bm{V}_{-\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime},-\gamma\mu}
.
Furthermore, chiral symmetry breaking is reflected in the inequalities

E_{\Gamma\mu}\neq E_{\bar{\Gamma}\mu}
,
\omega_{\Gamma}\neq\omega_{\bar{\Gamma}}
,
and
\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}\neq\bm{V}_{\bar{\gamma}\mu,\bar{\gamma^{\prime}}\mu^{\prime}}
.

III
Schrieffer–Wolff Transformation

To elucidate phonon effects on electronic dynamics in the electron–phonon coupled system,
the following unitary (Schrieffer–Wolff) transformation
Schrieffer and Wolff 1966
;
Winkler 2003
is performed:

\displaystyle\hat{H}\longrightarrow\tilde{H}

\displaystyle=e^{\hat{S}}\hat{H}e^{-\hat{S}}

\displaystyle=\hat{H}+[\hat{S},\hat{H}]+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{S},[\hat{S},\hat{H}]]+\cdots,

(6)

where the operator
\hat{S}
is assumed to take the form

\hat{S}=\frac{i}{\hbar}\sum_{\Gamma}(\hat{p}_{-\Gamma}\hat{F}_{\Gamma}+\hat{u}_{-\Gamma}\hat{G}_{\Gamma}).

(7)

To determine the electronic operators
\hat{F}_{\Gamma}
and
\hat{G}_{\Gamma}
,
the condition

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el-ph}}+[\hat{S},\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{ph}}]=0.

(8)

is imposed.
By neglecting higher-order contributions, the transformed Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}
can be approximated to the second order in the electron–phonon coupling as

\tilde{H}\approx\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{ph}}+\hat{H}_{2}.

(9)

where the second-order term is given by
\hat{H}_{2}=[\hat{S},\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el-ph}}]
.
Eq. (
8
) is satisfied when

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{F}_{\Gamma},\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}]-\frac{1}{M}\hat{G}_{-\Gamma}=0\\
&\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{G}_{\Gamma},\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}]+\hat{W}_{\Gamma}+M\omega_{\Gamma}^{2}\hat{F}_{-\Gamma}=0\end{aligned}\right.

(10)

holds.
By solving these equations,
the matrix elements of
\hat{F}_{\Gamma}
and
\hat{G}_{\Gamma}
are obtained as

\displaystyle(\hat{F}_{\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=g_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}(\Gamma)(\hat{W}_{-\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}},

(11)

\displaystyle(\hat{G}_{\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{iM}{\hbar}E_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}g_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}(\Gamma)(\hat{W}_{\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}},

(12)

where
(\hat{A})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}=\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\Psi_{\gamma}^{\mu}}{\hat{A}}{\Psi_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime}}}
denotes the matrix element of an electronic operator
\hat{A}
.
Here,
g_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}(\Gamma)
is defined as

g_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}(\Gamma)\equiv\frac{\hbar^{2}}{M}\frac{1}{(E_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}})^{2}-(\hbar\omega_{\Gamma})^{2}},

(13)

and
E_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}=E_{\gamma\mu}-E_{\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}
is the band energy difference.

As a result, the second-order term is given as

\hat{H}_{2}=\sum_{\Gamma}\hat{X}_{\Gamma}+\frac{i}{\hbar}\sum_{\Gamma\Gamma^{\prime}}(\hat{Y}_{\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}}\hat{u}_{-\Gamma^{\prime}}\hat{p}_{-\Gamma}+\hat{Z}_{\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}}\hat{u}_{-\Gamma^{\prime}}\hat{u}_{-\Gamma})

(14)

with
\hat{X}_{\Gamma}=\hat{F}_{\Gamma}\hat{W}_{\Gamma}
,

\hat{Y}_{\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}}=[\hat{F}_{\Gamma},\hat{W}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}]
,
and
\hat{Z}_{\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}}=[\hat{G}_{\Gamma},\hat{W}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}]
.

III.1
Emergence of phonon MAM in electron–phonon coupling

In the regime where the atomic mass is sufficiently larger than the electronic mass,
the electronic back-action on phonons is negligible.
Therefore, the phonon operators in Eq. (
14
) can be replaced with their thermal equilibrium expectation values:

\hat{u}_{-\Gamma^{\prime}}\hat{p}_{-\Gamma}\to\aab*{\hat{u}_{-\Gamma^{\prime}}\hat{p}_{-\Gamma}}

and
\hat{u}_{-\Gamma^{\prime}}\hat{u}_{-\Gamma}\to\aab*{\hat{u}_{-\Gamma^{\prime}}\hat{u}_{-\Gamma}}
.
Namely, the phonon effects are treated at the mean-field level.
To evaluate these quantities,
the phonon operators are expressed in terms of the annihilation (
\hat{a}_{\Gamma}^{\dagger}
) and the creation (
\hat{a}_{\Gamma}
) operators, which satisfy
[\hat{a}_{\Gamma},\hat{a}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}}

as
\hat{u}_{\Gamma}=\sqrt{\hbar/2M\omega_{\Gamma}}(\hat{a}_{\Gamma}+\hat{a}_{-\Gamma}^{\dagger})
and
\hat{p}_{\Gamma}=i\sqrt{\hbar\omega_{\Gamma}M/2}(\hat{a}_{\Gamma}^{\dagger}-\hat{a}_{-\Gamma})
.
Using the relation
\aab*{\hat{a}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\Gamma}}=\delta_{\Gamma\Gamma^{\prime}}f(\omega_{\Gamma})

for the Bose–Einstein distribution
f
,
Eq. (
14
) becomes

\displaystyle(\hat{H}_{2})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}\sum_{\Gamma}\left[(\hat{X}_{\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu^{\prime}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(\hat{Y}_{\Gamma,\Gamma}+\frac{i}{M\omega_{\Gamma}}\hat{Z}_{\Gamma,-\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu^{\prime}}(f(\omega_{\Gamma})+\frac{1}{2})\right].

(15)

Thus, only the diagonal elements with
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
contribute to the Hamiltonian,
which are given by

\displaystyle(\hat{X}_{\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu^{\prime}}=\sum_{\mu^{\prime\prime}}g_{\gamma\mu,\gamma-\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}}(\Gamma)V_{\gamma\mu,\gamma-\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}}V_{\gamma-\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime},\gamma\mu^{\prime}},

(16)

\displaystyle(\hat{Z}_{\Gamma,-\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{iM}{\hbar}\sum_{\mu^{\prime\prime}}\left[E_{\gamma\mu,\gamma+\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}}g_{\gamma\mu,\gamma+\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}}(\Gamma)V_{\gamma\mu,\gamma+\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}}V_{\gamma+\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime},\gamma\mu^{\prime}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad+\left.E_{\gamma\mu^{\prime},\gamma-\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}}g_{\gamma\mu^{\prime},\gamma-\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}}(\Gamma)V_{\gamma\mu,\gamma-\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}}V_{\gamma-\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime},\gamma\mu^{\prime}}\right],

(17)

and
(\hat{Y}_{\Gamma,\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu^{\prime}}=(\hat{X}_{\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu^{\prime}}-(\hat{X}_{-\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu^{\prime},\gamma\mu}^{\ast}
.
All these elements include the factor
V_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\pm\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}}V_{\gamma\pm\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime},\gamma\mu^{\prime}}=(\bm{v}_{\pm\Gamma}\cdot\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\pm\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}})(\bm{v}_{\mp\Gamma}\cdot\bm{V}_{\gamma\pm\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime},\gamma\mu^{\prime}})
, which can be rewritten as

\displaystyle V_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\pm\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}}V_{\gamma\pm\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime},\gamma\mu^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\pm\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}}^{\top}\mathcal{P}_{\pm\Gamma}\bm{V}_{\gamma\pm\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime},\gamma\mu^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\quad\mp\frac{i}{4\hbar}\bm{L}_{\Gamma}\cdot(\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\pm\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}}\times\bm{V}_{\gamma\pm\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime},\gamma\mu^{\prime}}),

(18)

where the first and second terms are contributed from the symmetric and antisymmetric part of the phonon polarization tensor, respectively.
In Eq. (
18
),
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}=\bm{v}_{-\Gamma}\bm{v}_{\Gamma}^{\top}+\bm{v}_{\Gamma}\bm{v}_{-\Gamma}^{\top}
is the symmetric phonon polarization tensor, and

\bm{L}_{\Gamma}=-2i\hbar\bm{v}_{-\Gamma}\times\bm{v}_{\Gamma},

(19)

is the phonon MAM in the irrep.
\Gamma
(for details, see Appendix
A.3
).
This result establishes that the electron–phonon coupling contains a term directly proportional to the phonon MAM.
Recently, the term “axial phonon” has been introduced for a phonon mode carrying CAM and/or MAM
Juraschek et al. 2025
. Thus, Eqs. (
15
)-(
19
) can be regarded as the direct coupling between electrons and axial phonons. Because phonon modes with nonzero wavenumber carrying angular momentum is clearly chiral, these also include a direct coupling to chiral axial phonons.
It should be noted that this coupling directly reflects the presence of the transverse components of phonon displacement in the electron–phonon interaction,
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el-ph}}
.
Otherwise, only the
z
-component of
\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\pm\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}
is involved in the quantity
\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\pm\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime}}\times\bm{V}_{\gamma\pm\Gamma\mu^{\prime\prime},\gamma\mu^{\prime}}
, which is identically equals to zero.
For the line group
L3_{2}
,
\bm{v}_{\Gamma}
and
\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}
in Eq. (
18
) are replaced by
\bm{v}_{\bar{\Gamma}}
and
\bm{V}_{\bar{\gamma}\mu,\bar{\gamma^{\prime}}\mu^{\prime}}
, respectively, while the MAM operator
\bm{L}_{\Gamma}
is replaced by
-\bm{L}_{\bar{\Gamma}}
.

A possible band dispersions of the effective electronic Hamiltonian
\hat{H}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{el}}=\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}+\hat{H}_{2}
is presented in Appendix
B
.

III.2
Resonant cases

For the resonant cases, where the condition
E_{\gamma\mu,\gamma-\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}\simeq\pm\hbar\omega_{\Gamma}
is satisfied,
the factor
g_{\gamma\mu,\gamma-\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}(\Gamma)
diverges, and hence, the second-order perturbative treatment of
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el-ph}}
becomes inadequate.
In this case, the mixed electron–phonon eigenstate forms:
For
E_{\gamma\mu,\gamma-\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}\simeq\hbar\omega_{\Gamma}
,
two electronic states
\ket*{\Psi_\gamma^\mu}
and
\ket*{\Psi_{\gamma-\Gamma}^{\mu'}}
are coupled by the phononic mode in irreps.
\Gamma
with its energy being given by

E_{\pm}\simeq\frac{1}{2}(E_{\gamma\mu}+E_{\gamma-\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}+\hbar\omega_{\Gamma})\pm\vab*{V_{\gamma\mu,\gamma-\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}}.

(20)

Because this eigenenergy depends on a square root of the phonon MAM,
both for the resonant and off-resonant cases, the band energy is affected directly by the phonon MAM in chiral crystals.
Additionally, phonons carrying MAM involve both longitudinal and transverse lattice displacements, the latter allowing optical excitation by circularly polarized fields. Under the resonance condition, the strong electron–phonon hybridization is expected to promote an efficient transfer of angular momentum from the phonon to the electronic degrees of freedom, leading to an enhanced electronic response such as the orbital angular momentum discussed in the next section.

More generally, in the higher-order perturbation theory,
due to the conservation of the phonon occupation number,
the higher-order Hamiltonian always includes the pair of
\bm{v}_{\Gamma}
and its time-reversal partner,
\bm{v}_{-\Gamma}
, which is rewritten as the factor
\bm{v}_{-\Gamma}\times\bm{v}_{\Gamma}\propto\bm{L}_{\Gamma}
.
Therefore, irrespective of the perturbative order,
the electronic system is coupled directly to the phonon MAM in chiral crystals.

III.3
Effect on electronic orbital angular momentum

Finally in this section, we briefly discuss a possible influence of the electron–phonon coupling on the electronic OAM
Xiao et al. 2010
.
The OAM operator is defined by

\hat{\bm{L}}_{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{m_{\mathrm{e}}}{2}(\hat{\bm{r}}\times\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{v}}\times\hat{\bm{r}}),

(21)

where
m_{\mathrm{e}}
is the mass,

\hat{\bm{r}}
is the position operator, and

\hat{\bm{v}}=(i/\hbar)[\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}},\hat{\bm{r}}]
is the velocity operator of an electron.
For our purpose, it is sufficient to discuss the
z
component of OAM although we can formulate the other components using the similar procedure
Xiao et al. 2010
;
Pezo et al. 2022
.
Let
\ket*{\phi_\ell^\lambda}
denote the atomic orbital
\lambda
centered at
\bm{R}_{\ell}
.
Assuming that
\ket*{\phi_\ell^\lambda}
is spatially localized, and hence, is the eigenstate of the position operator as
\hat{\bm{r}}\ket*{\phi_\ell^\lambda}=\bm{R}_{\ell}\ket*{\phi_\ell^\lambda}
, the matrix element of the
z
-component of OAM becomes (see Appendix
A.4
for the derivation)

\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}{\hat{L}_{\mathrm{el},z}}{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}=-m_{\mathrm{e}}\sum_{\ell,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{R}_{1+\ell}\times\bm{R}_{1})_{z}J_{\ell;\Gamma\mu}^{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda},

(22)

where
(\bm{R}_{1+\ell}\times\bm{R}_{1})_{z}=-\rho^{2}\sin(\ell\alpha)
is the area spanned by the vectors associated with the hopping from site
1
to
1+\ell
, and

J_{\ell;\Gamma\mu}^{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}(U_{\Gamma})_{\lambda^{\prime}\mu}^{\ast}(U_{\Gamma})_{\lambda\mu}e^{i\ell\Phi_{\Gamma}^{\lambda^{\prime}}}\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\phi_{1+\ell}^{\lambda^{\prime}}}{\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}}{\phi_{1}^{\lambda}},

(23)

is the corresponding hopping rate of
\ket*{\Psi_\Gamma^\mu}
from orbital
\lambda
to
\lambda^{\prime}
.
Therefore, OAM can be interpreted as the sum of the hopping rate weighed by the hopping area.
In general, OAM for Bloch electrons is divided into an intra-atomic and inter-atomic contributions
Pezo et al. 2022
;
Burgos Atencia et al. 2024
.
Whereas the former is interpreted as the average magnetic quantum number of the eigenstate described by the coupling matrix
U_{\Gamma}
, the latter is associated with the circular trajectory of the electronic motion, which is described by the transition matrix elements
\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\phi_{1+\ell}^{\lambda^{\prime}}}{\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}}{\phi_{1}^{\lambda}}
.
When we include the contribution from electron–phonon interaction,
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}
is replaced by
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}+\hat{H}_{2}
, which indicates that the electron–phonon interaction, and hence the phononic MAM, directly affect the OAM through modifying the electronic couplings.
Because electron transport via next-nearest-neighbor coupling has an interatomic contribution opposite to that via nearest-neighbor coupling, the electron–phonon interaction may either increase or decrease the OAM, as determined by a more detailed analysis.

Equations (
22
) and (
23
) also suggest an experimental route to detect the direct electronic coupling to phonon MAM in our formulation: this coupling should appear as a modulation of the electronic OAM in response to phonon excitation and the associated modulation of phonon MAM, for example by circularly polarized terahertz waves
Luo et al. 2023
;
Basini et al. 2024
;
Davies et al. 2024
.

IV
Concluding Remarks

In this study, we analyzed the electron–phonon coupling in chiral crystals.
As a result of the screw-rotational symmetry, both the longitudinal and transverse modes of phonons are involved in the coupling, and hence, the phononic MAM is coupled directly to the electronic systems.
This indicates that in chiral systems the angular momentum of the electronic and phononic degrees of freedom can be interconverted, which has an implication of the phononic enhancement of the orbital
Liu et al. 2021
;
Cho et al. 2025
;
Sato et al. 2025
;
Yao et al. 2025
;
Nabei et al. 2026
and spin-polarizations
Kim et al. 2023
.
This can be verified by detecting the electronic angular momentum induced by the selective excitation of phonons with circularly polarized terahertz waves.
Extension of our result to the general chiral crystals and molecules and exploration of the possibility of the electronic control of the phonon MAM will be left for future studies.

Appendix A
Microscopic Description of the Model

A.1
Electronic Hamiltonian

Let
\ket*{\phi_\ell^\lambda}
denote an electronic state with atomic orbital
\lambda
centered at
\bm{R}_{\ell}
.
This state can be decomposed into a symmetry-adapted basis
\ket*{\phi_\Gamma^\lambda}
,
which transforms as
\hat{\mathcal{R}}\ket*{\phi_\Gamma^\lambda}=e^{i(kc/3+m\alpha)}\ket*{\phi_\Gamma^\lambda}
,
according to

\ket*{\phi_\ell^\lambda}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3N}}\sum_{\Gamma}e^{i(\ell-1)\Phi_{\Gamma}^{\lambda}}\ket*{\phi_\Gamma^\lambda},

(24)

where
\Phi_{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\coloneq kc/3+(m+m_{\lambda})\alpha
is a phase factor
and
m_{\lambda}
is the magnetic quantum number of orbital
\lambda
.
The electronic Hamiltonian is then expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\ell^{\prime}\lambda\nu}t_{\ell^{\prime},\ell}^{\lambda\nu}\outerproduct*{\phi_{\ell'}^\lambda}{\phi_\ell^\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3N}\sum_{\ell\ell^{\prime}\lambda\nu}\sum_{\Gamma\Gamma^{\prime}}t_{1+\ell^{\prime}-\ell,1}^{\lambda\nu}e^{i(\ell^{\prime}-\ell)\Phi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\chi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\ast}(\ell-1)\chi_{\Gamma}(\ell-1)\outerproduct*{\phi_{\Gamma'}^\lambda}{\phi_{\Gamma}^\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\nu\Gamma}t_{\Gamma}^{\lambda\nu}\outerproduct*{\phi_{\Gamma}^\lambda}{\phi_{\Gamma}^\nu},

(25)

where
t_{\Gamma}^{\lambda\mu}=\sum_{\ell}t_{1+\ell,1}^{\lambda\nu}e^{i\ell\Phi_{\Gamma}^{\lambda}}

is the generalized Fourier transform of the electronic coupling
t_{\ell^{\prime},\ell}^{\lambda\nu}=\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\phi_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\lambda}}{\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}}{\phi_{\ell}^{\nu}}
.
To derive Eq. (
25
),
the screw symmetry relation
t_{\ell^{\prime},\ell}^{\lambda\nu}=e^{-i(\ell-1)(m_{\lambda}-m_{\nu})\alpha}t_{1+\ell^{\prime}-\ell,1}^{\lambda\nu}
and the great orthogonality theorem for characters are used.
By solving the eigenvalue equation in the orbital space,

t_{\Gamma}U_{\Gamma,\mu}=E_{\Gamma\mu}U_{\Gamma,\mu}
,
and defining the unitary matrix as
U_{\Gamma}=(U_{\Gamma,\mu=1},U_{\Gamma,\mu=2},\ldots)
,
the eigenstates are obtained as
\ket*{\Psi_\Gamma^\mu}=\sum_{\lambda}(U_{\Gamma})_{\lambda\mu}\ket*{\phi_\Gamma^\lambda}
, which yields

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}=\sum_{\Gamma\mu}E_{\Gamma\mu}\outerproduct*{\Psi_\Gamma^\mu}{\Psi_\Gamma^\mu}.

(26)

A.2
Electron–Phonon coupling Hamiltonian

The atomic position of the
\ell
th atom,
\bm{r}_{\ell}
,
is written as the sum of the equilibrium position and the displacement
\bm{u}_{\ell}
,
\bm{r}_{\ell}=\bm{R}_{\ell}+\bm{u}_{\ell}
.
This displacement modulates the electronic couplings as

t^{\lambda\mu}(\bm{r}_{\ell^{\prime}}-\bm{r}_{\ell})\approx t_{\ell^{\prime},\ell}^{\lambda\mu}+(\bm{u}_{\ell^{\prime}}-\bm{u}_{\ell})\cdot\left.{\frac{\partial t^{\lambda\nu}(\bm{r})}{\partial\bm{r}}}\right\rvert_{\bm{r}=\bm{R}_{\ell^{\prime}}-\bm{R}_{\ell}},

(27)

where the first term, defined by
t_{\ell^{\prime},\ell}^{\lambda\mu}\equiv t^{\lambda\mu}(\bm{R}_{\ell^{\prime}}-\bm{R}_{\ell})
,
and the second term contributes to the electronic and the electron–phonon coupling Hamiltonians, respectively.
Following the Ref.
Barišić et al. 1970
;
Tateishi et al. 2025
, the spatial derivative of
t^{\lambda\nu}
is treated as

{\partial t^{\lambda}(\bm{r})\mskip 0.0mu/\mskip 0.0mu\partial\bm{r}}|_{\bm{r}=\bm{R}_{\ell^{\prime}}-\bm{R}_{\ell}}=-\zeta t_{\ell^{\prime},\ell}^{\lambda\nu}\bm{Q}_{\ell^{\prime},\ell}
, where
\zeta
characterizes the spatial extent of the electronic coupling and
\bm{Q}_{\ell^{\prime},\ell}=(\bm{R}_{\ell^{\prime}}-\bm{R}_{\ell})/\vab*{\bm{R}_{\ell^{\prime}}-\bm{R}_{\ell}}
is the normalized bonding vector.

For chiral systems with screw-rotational symmetry, it is convenient to employ the circular basis defined by

\bm{e}^{(\pm)}=(\bm{e}_{x}\mp i\bm{e}_{y})/\sqrt{2}
and
\bm{e}^{(0)}=\bm{e}_{z}
,
Each basis vector is the eigenvector of threefold rotational operator as
\hat{C}_{3}\bm{e}^{(s)}=e^{im_{s}\alpha}\bm{e}^{(s)}
, where
m_{s}
(
m_{\pm}=\pm 1
, and
m_{0}=0
) is the helicity of the phonon spin angular momentum in analogy with the photon spin angular momentum of circularly polarized light.
Using the circular basis,
the bonding vector is expanded as
\bm{Q}_{\ell^{\prime},\ell}=\sum_{s}q_{\ell^{\prime}-\ell}^{(s)}e^{im_{s}\theta_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}}\bm{e}^{(s)}
, where the coefficients are given by
q_{\ell}^{(\pm)}=\pm i\cos\gamma_{\ell}/\sqrt{2}
and
q_{\ell}^{(0)}=\sin\gamma_{\ell}
.
Here,
\gamma_{\ell}=\arctan(c\ell/6\rho\sin(\ell\alpha/2))
is the helical angle, and
\theta_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}=(\ell^{\prime}+\ell-2)\alpha/2
.

Next, the phonon displacement vector is expanded in the circular basis as
\bm{u}_{\ell}=\sum_{s}u_{\ell}^{(s)}\bm{e}^{(s)}
, with coefficients

u_{\ell}^{(s)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3N}}\sum_{\Gamma}e^{-i(\ell-1)\Omega_{\Gamma}^{s}}\hat{u}_{\Gamma}v_{\Gamma}^{(s)},

(28)

where
\Omega_{\Gamma}^{s}=kc/3+(m-m_{s})\alpha
is the phase factor,

\hat{u}_{\Gamma}=\sqrt{\hbar/2M\omega_{\Gamma}}(\hat{a}_{\Gamma}+\hat{a}_{-\Gamma}^{\dagger})
is the quantized displacement operator, and
v_{\Gamma}^{(s)}
is the
s
th component of the eigenvector of the dynamical matrix.
The inner product
(\bm{u}_{\ell^{\prime}}-\bm{u}_{\ell})\cdot\bm{Q}_{\ell^{\prime},\ell}
is then evaluated as

\displaystyle(\bm{u}_{\ell^{\prime}}-\bm{u}_{\ell})\cdot\bm{Q}_{\ell^{\prime},\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3N}}\sum_{\Gamma,s}e^{-i(\ell^{\prime}-\ell)\alpha/2}(1-e^{-i(\ell^{\prime}-\ell)\Omega_{\Gamma}^{s}})

\displaystyle\quad\times(\bm{u}_{\Gamma}\cdot\bm{e}^{(s)})\chi_{\Gamma}^{\ast}(\ell^{\prime}-1)q_{\ell^{\prime}-\ell}^{(s)},

(29)

where
\bm{u}_{\Gamma}=\hat{u}_{\Gamma}\bm{v}_{\Gamma}
is the irreducible phonon displacement vector.
To take this out of the summation over
s
, the identity
(v_{\Gamma}^{(s)})^{\ast}=\bm{v}_{\Gamma}\cdot\bm{e}^{(s)}
is used.

Finally, in a similar manner to the derivation of
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}
,
the electron–phonon coupling Hamiltonian

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el-ph}}=-\sum_{\ell\ell^{\prime}\lambda\nu}\zeta t_{\ell^{\prime},\ell}^{\lambda\nu}(\bm{u}_{\ell^{\prime}}-\bm{u}_{\ell})\cdot\bm{Q}_{\ell^{\prime},\ell}\outerproduct*{\phi_{\ell'}^\lambda}{\phi_\ell^\nu},

(30)

can be written, after straightforward but lengthy algebra, as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el-ph}}=\sum_{\Gamma}\bm{u}_{\Gamma}\cdot\hat{\bm{W}}_{\Gamma},

(31)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{W}}_{\Gamma}=\sum_{\gamma,\mu\mu^{\prime}}\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma+\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}\outerproduct*{\Psi_\gamma^\mu}{\Psi_{\gamma+\Gamma}^{\mu'}},

(32)

and

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{\gamma\mu,\gamma+\Gamma\mu^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell\lambda\nu s}\frac{\zeta}{\sqrt{3N}}t_{1+\ell,1}^{\lambda\nu}e^{i\ell\Phi_{\gamma}^{\lambda}}(1-e^{-i\ell\Omega_{\Gamma}^{s}})e^{-i\ell m_{s}\alpha/2}

\displaystyle\quad\times q_{\ell}^{(s)}(U_{\gamma+\Gamma}^{\dagger})_{\mu\lambda}(U_{\gamma})_{\nu\mu^{\prime}}\bm{e}^{(s)}.

(33)

A.3
Mechanical Angular Momentum of Phonons

The phonon mechanical angular momentum (MAM) operator along the screw axis (the
z
axis) is defined as

\hat{L}_{z}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{3N}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{\ell}\times\hat{\bm{p}}_{\ell})_{z},

(34)

where
\hat{\bm{p}}_{\ell}
is the canonical momentum operator at the
\ell
th atomic site.
In the circular basis, this is rewritten as

\hat{L}_{z}=i\sum_{\ell=1}^{3N}(\hat{u}_{\ell}^{(+)}\hat{p}_{\ell}^{(-)}-\hat{u}_{\ell}^{(-)}\hat{p}_{\ell}^{(+)}).

(35)

To evaluate the MAM in the thermal equilibrium state,

\hat{u}_{\ell}^{(s)}
and
\hat{p}_{\ell}^{(s)}
are decomposed into the symmetry-adapted basis,
the former of which is already given by

\hat{u}_{\ell}^{(s)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3N}}\sum_{\Gamma}e^{-i(\ell-1)\Omega_{\Gamma}^{(s)}}(\frac{\hbar}{2M\omega_{\Gamma}})^{1/2}(\hat{a}_{\Gamma}+\hat{a}_{-\Gamma}^{\dagger})v_{\Gamma}^{(s)},

(36)

and the latter is,
by making use of
\hat{p}_{\ell}^{(s)}=M\dot{\hat{u}}_{\ell}^{(s)}
and
M\dot{\hat{u}}_{\Gamma}=\hat{p}_{-\Gamma}
, obtained as

\hat{p}_{\ell}^{(s)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3N}}\sum_{\Gamma}e^{-i(\ell-1)\Omega_{-\Gamma}^{(s)}}i(\frac{\hbar M\omega_{\Gamma}}{2})^{1/2}(\hat{a}_{\Gamma}^{\dagger}-\hat{a}_{-\Gamma})v_{-\Gamma}^{(s)}.

(37)

Substituting these expressions yields

\aab*{\hat{L}_{z}}=\sum_{\Gamma}\hbar(\vab*{v_{\Gamma}^{(-)}}^{2}-\vab*{v_{\Gamma}^{(+)}}^{2})(f(\omega_{\Gamma})+\frac{1}{2}).

(38)

where the time-reversal symmetry relation
v_{-\Gamma}^{(-s)}=(v_{\Gamma}^{(s)})^{\ast}
is used.
This is the thermal average of the phonon spin angular momentum of each irrep.
\Gamma
,
because the quantity
\vab*{v_{\Gamma}^{(-)}}^{2}-\vab*{v_{\Gamma}^{(+)}}^{2}=-\sum_{s}m_{s}\vab*{v_{\Gamma}^{(s)}}^{2}
is the mean value of the spin angular momentum of each eigenvector.

This procedure cannot be generalized to
x
and
y
components,
because the above derivation explicitly relies on the circular basis along
z
axis.
Nevertheless, it is always possible to express the mean spin angular momentum along the
a
axis (
a=x,y,z
) as
-2i\sum_{b,c=x,y,z}\epsilon_{abc}(v_{\Gamma}^{b})^{\ast}v_{\Gamma}^{c}
.
Therefore, we can extend the definition of the phonon MAM to

\displaystyle\aab*{\hat{\bm{L}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Gamma}L_{\Gamma}(f(\omega_{\Gamma})+\frac{1}{2}),

(39)

\displaystyle\bm{L}_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=-2i\hbar\bm{v}_{\Gamma}^{\ast}\times\bm{v}_{\Gamma}=-2i\hbar\bm{v}_{-\Gamma}\times\bm{v}_{\Gamma}.

(40)

A.4
Orbital Angular Momentum of Bloch Electrons

Orbital angular momentum (OAM) operator of electrons is defined by

\hat{\bm{L}}_{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{m_{\mathrm{e}}}{2}(\hat{\bm{r}}\times\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{v}}\times\hat{\bm{r}}),

(41)

where
m_{\mathrm{e}}
is the electronic mass,

\hat{\bm{r}}
is the position operator, and

\hat{\bm{v}}=(i/\hbar)[\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}},\hat{\bm{r}}]
is the velocity operator.
The matrix element of the
z
-component of OAM becomes

\displaystyle\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}{\hat{L}_{\mathrm{el},z}}{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{im_{\mathrm{e}}}{\hbar}\sum_{b,c}\epsilon_{zbc}\left[E_{\Gamma\mu}\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}{\hat{r}_{b}\hat{r}_{c}}{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.-\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}{\hat{r}_{b}\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}\hat{r}_{c}}{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}\right].

(42)

Rewriting in terms of
\ket*{\phi_\ell^\lambda}

and assuming that the atomic orbital state
\ket*{\phi_\ell^\lambda}
is spatially localized, and hence, is the eigenstate of the position operator as
\hat{\bm{r}}\ket*{\phi_\ell^\lambda}=\bm{R}_{\ell}\ket*{\phi_\ell^\lambda}
, the first term of the right-hand-side of above equation yields

\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}{\hat{r}_{b}\hat{r}_{c}}{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}=\frac{1}{3N}\sum_{\ell}R_{\ell,b}R_{\ell,c},

(43)

where the unitary condition of
U_{\Gamma}
is used.
This term does not contribute to OAM, because the equality
\sum_{b,c}\epsilon_{abc}R_{\ell,b}R_{\ell,c}=0
is satisfied.
In the similar manner, the second term of Eq. (
42
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}{\hat{r}_{b}\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}\hat{r}_{c}}{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\sum_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(U_{\Gamma})_{\lambda^{\prime}\mu}^{\ast}(U_{\Gamma})_{\lambda\mu}e^{i\ell\Phi_{\Gamma}^{\lambda^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad\times w_{bc}^{\ell}\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\phi_{1+\ell}^{\lambda^{\prime}}}{\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}}{\phi_{1}^{\lambda}},

(44)

with
w_{bc}^{\ell}=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}R_{\ell+\ell^{\prime},b}R_{\ell^{\prime},c}/3N
.
Using
\sum_{bc}\epsilon_{zbc}w_{bc}^{\ell}=-\rho^{2}\sin(\ell\alpha)
,
the OAM is finally expressed as

\displaystyle\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}{\hat{L}_{\mathrm{el},z}}{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{im_{\mathrm{e}}\rho^{2}}{\hbar}\sum_{\ell}\sum_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(U_{\Gamma})_{\lambda^{\prime}\mu}^{\ast}(U_{\Gamma})_{\lambda\mu}e^{i\ell\Phi_{\Gamma}^{\lambda^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sin(\ell\alpha)\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\phi_{1+\ell}^{\lambda^{\prime}}}{\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}}{\phi_{1}^{\lambda}}.

(45)

This is rewritten as

\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}{\hat{L}_{\mathrm{el},z}}{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{\mu}}=-m_{\mathrm{e}}\sum_{\ell,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{R}_{1+\ell}\times\bm{R}_{1})_{z}J_{\ell;\Gamma\mu}^{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda},

(46)

where
(\bm{R}_{1+\ell}\times\bm{R}_{1})_{z}=-\rho^{2}\sin(\ell\alpha)
is the area spanned by the vectors associated with the hopping from site
1
to
1+\ell
, and

J_{\ell;\Gamma\mu}^{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}(U_{\Gamma})_{\lambda^{\prime}\mu}^{\ast}(U_{\Gamma})_{\lambda\mu}e^{i\ell\Phi_{\Gamma}^{\lambda^{\prime}}}\innerproduct*{[}{[}3]{\phi_{1+\ell}^{\lambda^{\prime}}}{\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}}{\phi_{1}^{\lambda}},

(47)

is the corresponding hopping rate of
\ket*{\Psi_\Gamma^\mu}
from orbital
\lambda
to
\lambda^{\prime}
.
Therefore, OAM can be interpreted as the sum of the hopping rate weighed by the hopping area.

When we include the contribution from electron–phonon interaction,
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}
is replaced by
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}^{\prime}=\hat{H}_{\mathrm{el}}+\hat{H}_{2}
in Eq. (
45
), which indicates that the electron–phonon interaction directly affect the OAM.

Electronic Band Dispersions

Electronic energy bands (a) without and (b) with the electron–phonon interaction, respectively.
Other parameters are set to
\hbar^{2}\zeta^{2}/M=\quantity{3.0d-6}{}
and
T=\quantity{0}{}
.

For simplicity, the inter-branch electronic transitions are neglected.
In this case, the matrix elements in Eq. (
15
) are simplified to

(\hat{X}_{\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu}=g_{\gamma\mu,\gamma-\Gamma\mu}(\Gamma)\vab*{V_{\gamma\mu,\gamma-\Gamma\mu}}^{2}
,
(\hat{Y}_{\Gamma,\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu}=(\hat{X}_{\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu}-(\hat{X}_{-\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu}
, and

(\hat{Z}_{\Gamma,-\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu}=-(iM/\hbar)\sum_{p=\pm}E_{\gamma\mu,\gamma-p\Gamma\mu}(\hat{X}_{p\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu}
.
Thus, the second-order Hamiltonian becomes

(\hat{H}_{2})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu}=\sum_{\Gamma}[1+\frac{2E_{\gamma\mu,\gamma-\Gamma\mu}}{\hbar\omega_{\Gamma}}(f(\omega_{\Gamma})+\frac{1}{2})](\hat{X}_{\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu},

(48)

where two terms in
(\hat{Y}_{\Gamma,\Gamma})_{\gamma\mu,\gamma\mu}
are canceled out.

In numerical calculation, we set
\rho=\quantity{0.12}{}
and
c=\quantity{0.591}{}
,
and assume that only the
p
-atomic orbitals with the nearest-neighbor coupling are incorporated in the electronic system.
In this case, the Slater–Koster overlap integrals are estimated to be
V_{pp\sigma}=\quantity{3.02}{}
and
V_{pp\pi}=\quantity{-0.75}{}
.

(a) and (b) depict the electronic band dispersions without and with coupling to the lowest optical modes, respectively,

m=+1
and
-1
at
k=0
and for

and
0
for
k=\pi/c
correctly reflecting the symmetry of the line group
Božovic 1984
.

k=0

m=-1
