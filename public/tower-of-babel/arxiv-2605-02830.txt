Null Controllability for a Multi-Dimensional Degenerate Parabolic Equation with Degenerated Interior Point This work was carried out with the support of the National Natural Science Foundation of China under grant nos. 12131008 and U23B2033, and National Key R&D Program of China under grant no. 2024YFA1013101.
Dong-Hui Yang, Bao-Zhu Guo, Jie Zhong

Abstract
In this study, we study the null controllability of a multi-dimensional degenerate parabolic equation characterized by a degenerate interior point. The control domain, which is an arbitrary inner region, does not encompass the degenerate point. To tackle this problem, we adopt a new approximation methodology. Specifically, we approximate the degenerate partial differential equations (PDEs) with a series of uniformly elliptic PDEs, notwithstanding their limited regularity. We then derive the Carleman estimate for these approximate uniformly parabolic equations and establish the observability inequality, which ultimately paves the way for demonstrating the null controllability of the system. Keywords: Degenerate partial differential equations, approximation, Carleman estimate, null controllability. AMS subject classifications (2010): 35J70, 35K65, 49Q10, 93B05.

When

For

is called an

weight if there exists a constant

such that for all cubes
K
in

\left(\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w(x)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p-1}\leq c.

(2.1)

The infimum of the set of constants

for which (
2.1
) holds is referred to as the

constant of

.
It is evident that

is an
A_{1+\frac{2}{N}}
weight, given that

is an
A_{p}
weight if and only if
-N<\alpha<N(p-1)
. Below, we define

A function

if

\int_{\Omega}u^{2}w\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

The inner product on

is given by

(u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)}=\int_{\Omega}uvw\,\mathrm{d}x,

and the corresponding norm is

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2}w\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It is well-known that
(L^{2}(\Omega;w),(\cdot,\cdot){L^{2}(\Omega;w)})
is a Hilbert space and
(L^{2}(\Omega;w),\|\cdot\|{L^{2}(\Omega;w)})
is a Banach space.
We define

H^{1}(\Omega;w)=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega;w)\colon\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\in L^{2}(\Omega;w),\,i=1,\cdots,N\right\},

where

are the distributional derivatives with respect to the spatial variables
x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}
. The inner product on

is

(u,v)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}=\int_{\Omega}uvw\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}w\,\mathrm{d}x,

and the norm is

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2}w\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{2}w\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We define

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)=\text{the closure of }\mathcal{D}(\Omega)\text{ in }H^{1}(\Omega;w),

where
\mathcal{D}(\Omega)=C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is the space of test functions.
We denote by
H^{-1}(\Omega;w)
the dual space of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, which is a subspace of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
. It is also well-known that
(H^{1}(\Omega;w),(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)})
is a Hilbert space and
(H^{1}(\Omega;w),\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)})
is a Banach space.

(2.11)

, we cannot derive the following classical results:

\int_{U}(\partial_{k}^{h}u)^{2}w\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}w\mathrm{d}x\text{ or }\int_{U}(\partial_{k}^{h}u)^{2}w^{h}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}w\mathrm{d}x.

This issue arises from the non-transform invariance of the measure
w\mathrm{d}x
and the fact that
w=|x|^{\alpha}\notin C^{1}(\Omega)
(see (
2.13
) and the non-meaningfulness of
\int_{\Omega}|x+he_{k}|^{\alpha-2}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x
for
0\neq h\in{\mathbb{R}}
).

Lemma 2.6

Let
u(\cdot)
be a solution of equation (
1.1
). Then for any
U\Subset\Omega
,

|x|^{3+\frac{3\alpha}{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}\in L^{2}(U\times(0,T)),\quad\forall j,k\in J_{N}.

Proof.

This is essentially a problem of elliptic regularity. We only need to prove the case where
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;w)
is a weak solution of the following system

\begin{cases}-\Div(w\nabla u)=g\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
g=\chi_{\omega}f-\partial_{t}u\in L^{2}(\Omega),\end{cases}

(2.14)

and then

|x|^{3+\frac{3\alpha}{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}\in L^{2}(U),\quad\forall j,k\in J_{N}.

(2.15)

The partial differential operator
-\Div(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla\bullet)
has a discrete point spectrum

0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\cdots\rightarrow+\infty

from Lemma
2.3
. Then there exists an orthonormal basis
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq L^{2}(\Omega)

(
[
10
, Theorem 7, Appendix E, p.645]
), and moreover,
\{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, where
\Phi_{n}
is the eigenfunction of
-\Div(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla\bullet)
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}\ (n\in\mathbb{N})
. We denote
g=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}g_{n}\Phi_{n}
with
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
, then
u=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}g_{n}\lambda_{n}^{-1}\Phi_{n}
is the solution of (
2.14
). Hence, to show (
2.15
), it suffices to show

|x|^{3+\frac{3\alpha}{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{n}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}\in L^{2}(U),\quad\forall j,k\in J_{N}.

We split the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
Let
W
be an open set satisfying
U\Subset W\Subset\Omega
. Take
\zeta\in C^{\infty}(\Omega),0\leq\zeta\leq 1
and

\zeta=1\quad\text{on }U,\quad\zeta=0\text{ on }{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus W.

Let
0<|h|<\frac{1}{2}\min\{\dist(U,\partial W),\dist(W,\partial U)\}
. For any
k\in J_{N}
, choose

v=-\partial_{k}^{-h}\left[\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+\alpha})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}u\right],

where
\partial_{k}^{h}u
is the difference quotient.
It is evident that
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
. Multiplying
v
on both sides of the following equation

\begin{cases}-\Div(w\nabla\Phi_{n})=\lambda_{n}\Phi_{n},&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\Phi_{n}=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.16)

we obtain

\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha}\nabla\Phi_{n}\cdot\nabla v\mathrm{d}x=\lambda_{n}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{n}v\mathrm{d}x.

(2.17)

We have

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha}\nabla\Phi_{n}\cdot\nabla v\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{k}^{h}\left(|x|^{\alpha}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n}\right)\partial_{i}\left[\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+\alpha})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left[(|x|^{\alpha})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n}+(\partial_{k}^{h}|x|^{\alpha})\partial_{i}\Phi_{n}\right]\\
&\hskip 41.25648pt\times\bigg[2\zeta(\partial_{i}\zeta)|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+\alpha})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n}+(2+\alpha)\zeta^{2}|x|^{\alpha}x_{i}(|x|^{4+\alpha})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n}\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+(4+\alpha)\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x+h|^{2+\alpha}(x+h)_{i})\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n}+\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+\alpha})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n}\bigg]\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+2\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x+A_{1}+A_{2},\end{split}

where
(x+h)_{i}
is the
i
-th component of
x+h
, with

\begin{split}A_{1}&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}(|x|^{\alpha})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n}\bigg[2\zeta(\partial_{i}\zeta)|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+\alpha})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n}+(2+\alpha)\zeta^{2}|x|^{\alpha}x_{i}(|x|^{4+\alpha})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n}\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt+(4+\alpha)\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}\left(|x+h|^{2+\alpha}(x+h)_{i}\right)\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n}\bigg]\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}A_{2}&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{k}^{h}|x|^{\alpha})\partial_{i}\Phi_{n}\bigg[2\zeta(\partial_{i}\zeta)|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+\alpha})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n}+(2+\alpha)\zeta^{2}|x|^{\alpha}x_{i}(|x|^{4+\alpha})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n}\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+(4+\alpha)\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}\left(|x+h|^{2+\alpha}(x+h)_{i}\right)\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n}+\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+\alpha})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n}\bigg]\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Next, we estimate the terms
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. We have

\begin{split}A_{1}&\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+2\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\hskip 12.80373pt+16\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{i}\zeta)^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+2\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x+8(2+\alpha)\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{\alpha}(|x|^{4+2\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\hskip 12.80373pt+8(4+\alpha)\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{2+2\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}A_{2}&\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+2\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x+2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}|x|^{\alpha})^{2}(\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\hskip 12.80373pt+\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla\Phi_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{i}\zeta)^{2}|x|^{4+\alpha}(|x|^{8+2\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}|x|^{\alpha})^{2}(\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\hskip 12.80373pt+\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla\Phi_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2+\alpha}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{8+2\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}|x|^{\alpha})^{2}(\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\hskip 12.80373pt+\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla\Phi_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2+\alpha}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{4+\alpha}(|x|^{6+2\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}|x|^{\alpha})^{2}(\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Similar to (
2.12
), we obtain

|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\partial_{k}^{h}|x|^{\alpha}=\partial_{k}^{h}|x|^{\alpha+1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}-(|x|^{\alpha})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}

is a bounded function on
\Omega
.
Then, from Lemma
2.5
,
we have

\begin{split}|A_{1}|,|A_{2}|&\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+2\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x+C\|\Phi_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2},\end{split}

where the constants
C>0
depend only on
\alpha
and
m
. These show that

\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha}\nabla\Phi_{n}\cdot\nabla v\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+2\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x-C\|\Phi_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2},

(2.18)

where the constants
C>0
depend only on
\alpha
and
m
.

Step 2.
Note that

\begin{split}\lambda_{n}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{n}v\mathrm{d}x&=\lambda_{n}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\lambda_{n}\|\Phi_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}\end{split}

from Lemma
2.5
, where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
and
m
. This, together with (
2.18
), yields

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+2\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\lambda_{n}\|\Phi_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2},

(2.19)

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
and
m
.

Finally, since

(|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n}=\partial_{k}^{h}(|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})-(\partial_{k}^{h}|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}})(\partial_{i}\Phi_{n}),

and noting that
|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{U}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{2+\alpha})^{h}\left[\partial_{k}^{h}(|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})\right]^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{U}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{4+2\alpha})^{h}(\partial_{k}^{h}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{U}|x|^{2+\alpha}(|x|^{2+\alpha})^{h}\left(\partial_{k}^{h}|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{2}(\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x<+\infty\end{split}

by Lemma
2.5
. That is,
|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}})^{h}\partial_{k}^{h}(|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})\in L^{2}(U)
for all
0<|h|<\frac{1}{2}\dist(U,\partial\Omega)
. Hence, there exists a function
v_{k}\in L^{2}(U)
and a subsequence
h_{l}\rightarrow 0
such that

|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}})^{h_{l}}\partial_{k}^{h}(|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})\rightarrow v_{k}\text{ in }L^{2}(U).

For any
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(U)
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{U}v_{k}\psi\mathrm{d}x&=\lim_{h_{l}\rightarrow 0}\int_{U}\left[|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}})^{h_{l}}\partial_{k}^{h}(|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})\right]\psi\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\lim_{h_{l}\rightarrow 0}\int_{U}\left[\partial_{k}^{h}(|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})\right]\left[|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}})^{h_{l}}\psi\right]\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-\lim_{h_{l}\rightarrow 0}\int_{U}\left(|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n}\right)\partial_{k}^{-h}\left[|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}})^{h_{l}}\psi\right]\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-\int_{U}|x|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})\partial_{k}\left(|x|^{2+\alpha}\psi\right)\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-(2+\alpha)\int_{U}|x|^{1+\frac{3\alpha}{2}}x_{k}(\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})\psi\mathrm{d}x-\int_{U}|x|^{3+\frac{3\alpha}{2}}(\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})\partial_{k}\psi\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

which implies that

\partial_{k}\left(|x|^{3+\frac{3\alpha}{2}}(\partial_{i}\Phi_{n})\right)=v_{k}+(2+\alpha)|x|^{1+\frac{3\alpha}{2}}x_{k}\partial_{i}\Phi_{n}\in L^{2}(U).

This completes the proof of the lemma.

\square

Remark 2.3

From Lemma
2.6
, we have
|x|^{3+\frac{3\alpha}{2}}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
for
i,j\in J_{N}
. However, it is challenging to obtain
|x|^{\beta}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
for
\beta<3+\frac{3\alpha}{2}
. This makes the first step (
2.10
) of the Carleman estimate (or, the subsequent computation and estimation) impossible. This is the distinction between the higher-dimensional case and the one-dimensional case. This is why we employ the approximation method in the following.
There is limited literature on the properties of the second derivatives of degenerate partial differential equations. However, for unique continuation properties or observability inequalities, the existence of some form of second or higher derivatives of the degenerate partial differential equations is a fundamental necessity.

3
Approximations

In this section, we employ uniformly parabolic equations to approximate the equation (
1.1
). To this end, we introduce weight functions to approximate the
A_{1+\frac{2}{N}}
weight function
w(\cdot)
(Lemma
3.1
). Subsequently, we present one of the main results of this paper, Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 3.1

There exists a
C^{2,1}
function
\psi_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=|x|\quad\text{for }|x|\geq\varepsilon,\quad|x|\leq\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq 2\varepsilon\quad\text{on }[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon],

and

\psi_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R},\quad|\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|\leq C\text{ on }\mathbb{R},\quad|\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}|\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon},\text{ and }|\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}|\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\text{ on }\mathbb{R},

(3.1)

where the constants
C>0
are absolute.

Proof.

We consider the following polynomial function

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{4}a_{i}x^{4-i},

where
a_{0},\cdots,a_{4}
are constants to be determined. We impose the conditions

\begin{split}\psi_{\varepsilon}(-\varepsilon)=\varepsilon,\quad\psi_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon,\quad\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(-\varepsilon)=-1,\quad\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\varepsilon)=1,\quad\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(\pm\varepsilon)=0,\end{split}

which yield the solution

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{3\varepsilon}{8}+\frac{3}{4\varepsilon}x^{2}-\frac{1}{8\varepsilon^{3}}x^{4},\quad x\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon].

(3.2)

This function satisfies the required properties.

\square

Remark 3.1

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and consider the function
\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)
defined in Lemma
3.1
. We have

\begin{split}\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}&=\frac{3}{2\varepsilon}x-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{3}}|x|^{2}x,\quad\frac{\partial^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=\delta_{ij}\left[\frac{3}{2\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{3}}|x|^{2}\right]-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}x_{i}x_{j},\\
\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon}&=N\left[\frac{3}{2\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{3}}|x|^{2}\right]-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}|x|^{2},\\
(D^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}&=\left(\frac{3}{2\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{3}}|x|^{2}\right)^{2}x-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\left(\frac{3}{2\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{3}}|x|^{2}\right)|x|^{2}x\end{split}

(3.3)

on
B_{\varepsilon}
, and

\begin{split}\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}=\frac{x}{|x|},\quad\frac{\partial^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=\delta_{ij}|x|^{-1}-x_{i}x_{j}|x|^{-3},\quad\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon}=(N-1)|x|^{-1},\quad(D^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}=0\end{split}

(3.4)

on
\Omega\setminus B_{\varepsilon}
.

Denote

\begin{split}w_{\varepsilon}(x)=(\psi_{\varepsilon}(x))^{\alpha}=(\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|))^{\alpha},\quad x\in\Omega.\end{split}

Lemma 3.2

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. Then the function
w_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
is an
A_{1+\frac{2}{N}}
weight. Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
such that the
A_{p}
constant
c(w_{\varepsilon},1+\frac{2}{N})\leq C
.

Proof.

It is evident that
w_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
is an
A_{1}
weight since

\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w(x)\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{4m}{\varepsilon}\operatorname*{ess\ \!inf}_{K}w,

and
\operatorname*{ess\ \!inf}_{K}w_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
. By the property
A_{p_{1}}\subset A_{p_{2}}
for
1\leq p_{1}<p_{2}
, we conclude that
w_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
is an
A_{1+\frac{2}{N}}
weight.
Next, we show that
\{c(w_{\varepsilon},1+\frac{2}{N})\colon\varepsilon\in(0,1)\}
is uniformly bounded above, where
c(w_{\varepsilon},1+\frac{2}{N})
is the
A_{1+\frac{2}{N}}
constant of
w_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
.
Firstly, for
y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{N})\in\Omega
, consider the case
K=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(y_{i}-a,y_{i}+a)
for
0<a\ll\varepsilon
. We have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w_{\varepsilon}(x)\mathrm{d}x&\leq 2(2a)^{-N}\iint_{B_{\sqrt{N}a}}w_{\varepsilon}(y)\mathrm{d}r=2(2a)^{-N}w_{\varepsilon}(y)|B_{\sqrt{N}a}|=\frac{|B_{1}|N^{\frac{N}{2}}}{2^{N-1}}w_{\varepsilon}(y),\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\left(\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w_{\varepsilon}(x)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{N}}&\leq 2w_{\varepsilon}(y)^{-1}\left(\frac{|B_{1}|N^{\frac{N}{2}}}{2^{N}}\right)^{\frac{2}{N}}=w_{\varepsilon}(y)^{-1}\frac{|B_{1}|^{\frac{2}{N}}N}{2}.\end{split}

These imply that

\begin{split}\left(\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w_{\varepsilon}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w_{\varepsilon}(x)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{N}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{N}}|B_{1}|^{1+\frac{2}{N}}N^{1+\frac{N}{2}}.\end{split}

Secondly, for
y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{N})\in\Omega
, consider the case
K_{y}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(y_{i}-a,y_{i}+a)
for
a\approx\varepsilon
. We have

\begin{split}&\left(\frac{1}{|K_{y}|}\int_{K_{y}}w_{\varepsilon}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\frac{1}{|K_{y}|}\int_{K_{y}}w_{\varepsilon}(x)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{N}}\\
&\leq(3\varepsilon)^{\alpha}\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\int_{B_{\sqrt{N}\varepsilon}}|x|^{-\frac{N\alpha}{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{N}}=(3\varepsilon)^{\alpha}\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}|\partial B_{1}|^{\frac{2}{N}}\left(\int_{0}^{\sqrt{N}\varepsilon}r^{N-1-\frac{N\alpha}{2}}\mathrm{d}r\right)^{\frac{2}{N}}\\
&\leq\frac{3^{\alpha}}{2^{2+\frac{2}{N}}}|\partial B_{1}|^{\frac{2}{N}}(2-\alpha)^{-\frac{2}{N}}N^{2-\alpha-\frac{2}{N}}\end{split}

for
|y|\leq 2\varepsilon
. The other cases are analogous to the case of
|x|^{\alpha}
.
Thirdly, for
y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{N})\in\Omega
, consider the case
K_{y}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(y_{i}-a,y_{i}+a)
for
\varepsilon\ll a
. Then,

\begin{split}&\left(\frac{1}{|K_{y}|}\int_{K_{y}}w_{\varepsilon}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\frac{1}{|K_{y}|}\int_{K_{y}}w_{\varepsilon}(x)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{N}}\\
&\approx\left(\frac{1}{|K_{y}|}\int_{K_{y}}w(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\frac{1}{|K_{y}|}\int_{K_{y}}w(x)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{N}}\leq c\left(w,1+\frac{2}{N}\right).\end{split}

Overall, we obtain that
c(w_{\varepsilon},1+\frac{2}{N})\leq C
, where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,N
and
\Omega
. Moreover,
C>0
is independent of
\varepsilon>0
.

\square

Lemma 3.3

Let
z\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w_{\varepsilon})
. Then,

(N+\alpha-2)\|w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|z\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w_{\varepsilon})}.

(3.5)

Moreover,

\|z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w_{\varepsilon})}\leq\frac{2m}{N+\alpha-2}\||\nabla z|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w_{\varepsilon})},

(3.6)

and

\|z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{2m^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{N+\alpha-2}\||\nabla z|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w_{\varepsilon})}.

(3.7)

Proof.

Since
w_{\varepsilon}\in C^{4}(\overline{\Omega})
, we have

\begin{split}2\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}z(x\cdot\nabla z)\mathrm{d}x&=\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}x\cdot\nabla z^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\Div\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}z^{2}x\right)\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}z^{2}\Div\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}x\right)\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-(N+\alpha-2)\int_{\Omega}z^{2}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}x-(2-\alpha)\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}z^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha-3}[\psi_{\varepsilon}-x\cdot\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}]\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Then,

\begin{split}(N+\alpha-2)\int_{\Omega}z^{2}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}x&\leq-2\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}z(x\cdot\nabla z)\mathrm{d}x=2\int_{\Omega}\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}}z\right)\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}}x\cdot\nabla z\right)\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq 2\left(\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}z^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}|x|^{2}|\nabla z|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq 2\left(\int_{\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}z^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla z|^{2}w_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

by
\alpha\in(0,2)
, and
\psi_{\varepsilon}-x\cdot\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}=\frac{3}{8\varepsilon^{3}}(\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2})^{2}\geq 0
on
B_{\varepsilon}
, and Lemma
3.1
. This shows that

(3.13)
