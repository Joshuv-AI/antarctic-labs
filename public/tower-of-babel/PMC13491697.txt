Current situation and influencing factors of knowledge, attitude and practice of breast engorgement among postpartum women: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Ai Li, Suhua Tu, Qiong Guo, Jun Gao, Qingxiu Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo understand the current situation of knowledge, attitude and practice of breast engorgement in postpartum women and analyze its influencing factors.MethodsFrom August 2019 to December 2019, by using the method of convenient sampling, 530 postpartum women in the department of obstetrics in 3 tertiary hospitals were investigated with the breast engorgement knowledge, attitude and practice and general data questionnaire. ResultsThe scores of breast engorgement of knowledge, attitude, practice and total KAP were (8.90 ± 3.17), (33.83 ± 3.54), (31.67 ± 1.41) and (74.41 ± 12.26). The results of regression analysis showed that participation in school during pregnancy and knew breast engorgement were influencing factors of breast engorgement knowledge score. Graduate and above (maternity), participation in school during pregnancy and vaginal delivery were influencing factors of attitude score towards breast engorgement. Artificial feeding and mixed feeding were influencing factor of practice score of breast engorgement. Participation in school during pregnancy, knew breast engorgement, artificial feeding, mixed feeding and commercial and service personnel (spouse) were influencing factors of total score of KAP of breast engorgement (P<0.05). ConclusionThe level of knowledge about breast engorgement is low, the attitude towards breast engorgement is positive, and the practice to breast engorgement need to be improved. Health care workers should formulate and implement targeted nursing education and intervention to improve the level of knowledge, attitude and practice of breast engorgement in order to reduce the incidence of breast engorgement.Keywords: Postpartum women, Breast engorgement, Knowledge, Attitude and practice, Influencing factors

In recognition of the importance of breastfeeding for maternal and infant well-being, and for society at large, the World Health Organization recommends that all babies should be exclusively breastfed for the first six months of life and then continue to receive breast milk, along with appropriate complementary foods, up to two years of age or beyond [1]. According to studies, the exclusive breastfeeding rate of 6 months in China is 29.2%, which is far lower than the national target, One of the most common factors associated with premature cessation of breastfeeding is dificulty with lactation, including breast engorgement [2].

Breast engorgement is breast fullness caused by vasodilation and milk production at the early stage of lactation, which can occur in different periods during lactation [3, 4]. The most common is in the early stage of lactation, with an incidence of 20%-75% [5], characterised by hard, painful, tight breasts and dificulty breastfeeding, In severe cases, it can lead to infection, mastitis and even breast abscess. It is usually due to compromised milk removal, either from separation of mother and newborn, restrictive feeding practices and/or inefective sucking, or less commonly overproduction of milk [6]. Breast engorgement is closely related to maternal feeding behavior. Therefore, early education and self-management are the key to prevent breast engorgement [7–9]. According to the theory of knowledge, attitude and practice [10, 11], knowledge is the basis of practice change, and attitude is the driving force of practice change, and practices are conducive to health under the two effects. Clinical studies have found that through breast engorgement knowledge education, maternal breast engorgement knowledge scores are higher and the incidence of breast engorgement is lower [12, 13], but the relevant studies do not mention the correlation between knowledge, attitudes, and practices, and lack of knowledge, attitudes and practices based on breast engorgement knowledge education of the status quo. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to understand the current situation and influencing factors of maternal breast engorgement knowledge, attitudes and practice, in order to provide reference for the prevention and nursing intervention of breast engorgement.

Research objects and methods

Research objects

Source of cases

The convenience sampling method was used to select the obstetric inpatients of three tertiary hospitals August 2019 to December 2019 as the survey subjects, which women hospitalized 1 day or more after delivery.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) The subjects included primiparas and multiparas, delivery spontan and secsio sesarea. (2) informed consent and voluntary participation in the study. Exclusion criteria: (1) persons with cognitive impairment or mental illness; (2) language and communication disabilities.

Sample size calculation

The formula [14] for sample size calculation is , where Z is the Z-score corresponding to a 95% confidence level (1.96), δ which is the allowable deviation error, take 0.05; ρ represents the expected incidence, because the incidence of breast engorgement ranges from 20% to 75% [5], in order to ensure sufficient sample size, the incidence of 50% is calculated. That is Z1−α/2=1.96, α = 0.05, δ = 0.05, ρ = 0.50, which was brought into the formula to calculate the sample size of 385. Considering 10% sample missing, 424 samples need to be included. In this study, 550 questionnaires were actually distributed.

Methods

The convenience sampling method was used.

Survey tools

General information questionnaire: including maternal age, occupation, education level, whether she has attended maternity school during pregnancy, whether family members have medical workers, etc.

Breast engorgement knowledge, attitude and practice questionnaire: A total of 34 items, first-level indicators (knowledge, attitude, practice), 7 s-level indicators. ① Knowledge dimension: there were 16 items in 3 secondary indicators, including basic knowledge of breast engorgement, knowledge of breastfeeding, prevention and treatment of breast engorgement; ②Attitude dimension: there were 8 items in 2 secondary indexes, including psychological cognition and intentional behavior; ③ Practice dimension: there are 10 items in total, including 2 secondary indexes of positive practice and dangerous practice.

Score: The answer to the knowledge dimension of breast engorgement is 1 point, and 0 points are no or uncertain. The attitude and practice dimensions of breast engorgement were scored on a Likert 5-point scale, and the attitude dimensions were: strongly disagree- strongly agree (1–5 points); Practice dimension: never-always (1–5 points). Among them, the 1st, 11th, 15th, 16th, 28th, 29th, 30th, 31st, 33rd, and 34th questions are inverse assignment questions. The Maternal breast engorgement knowledge, attitude and practice questionnaire was formed on the basis of literature research, clinical practice, expert consultation, etc., and a formal questionnaire was formed through the pre-survey. Among them, the overall Cronbach,s α coefficient of the questionnaire was 0.82, and the total content validity index (S-CVI/Ave) was 0.954, indicating that the questionnaire had good reliability and validity.

Survey methods

Paper questionnaires were used, and after obtaining the consent of participating hospitals and departments, the trained team members used unified guidance terms to explain the investigation purpose and notes for filling in the questionnaire to the survey subjects, written informed consent was obtained from each participant. After the survey subjects filled in the questionnaire independently, the investigators checked on the spot and verified and supplemented the incomplete and wrong filling with the survey subjects.

Data processing and statistical analysis

All data were double-checked and entered using Excel 2010, and SPSS 25.0 was used for data processing and statistical analysis. P<0.05 indicated statistically significant differences. Mean standard deviation was used for quantitative data, and adoption rate or composition ratio was used for qualitative data. T-test and variance analysis were used to compare the scores of maternal breast engorgement with different demographic characteristics. Multiple linear regression was used to analyze all dimensions of breast engorgement and the single factor of total score.

Results

General demographic data of the respondents

In this study, a total of 550 questionnaires were distributed, 544 were recovered, with an effective recovery rate of 98.9%, 14 invalid questionnaires were eliminated, and 530 valid questionnaires were obtained. Among them, the average maternal age was ( 28.22 ± 4.91) years, the number of postpartum days was (3.06 ± 1.31) days, the gestational week was (273.44 ± 11.47) days, and the number of parities was (1.61±0.69) times, the age of the spouse (1.61 ± 0.69) years, maternal occupation: other 38.3%, spouse occupation: professional and technical personnel 34.3%, the education level of the mother and the spouse of the high school and below 46.8%, 48.5%, respectively, the per capita monthly household income of 2500–5000 yuan is 42.6%. The incidence of breast engorgement was 29.2%, the rate of exclusive breastfeeding was 35.3%, the first breastfeeding time was ≤ 30 min (36.5%), the normal delivery was 43.2%, 9.81% attended the pregnant woman’s school during pregnancy, 77.9% had no medical workers in the family members, and 48.3% knew about breast engorgement.

The total score of breast engorgement KAP was (74.41 ± 12.26) points, and the score percentage was 70.19%. The scores of each dimension and item of responses are shown in Tables 1, 2 and 3.

Comparison of maternal breast engorgement knowledge, attitude and practice scores with different characteristics

Maternal breast engorgement knowledge score, practice score, attitude score and total score of KAP were used as dependent variables, and the general conditions of mothers and their spouses were used as independent variables for univariate analysis. The analysis results showed that there were statistically significant differences in knowledge scores of whether family members had medical workers, whether they participated in pregnant women’s schools, whether they knew breast engorgement, and the age of their spouses (P<0.05). There were statistically significant differences in attitude scores of maternal occupation and education level, family members had medical workers, pregnancy participation in pregnant women’s school, delivery method, feeding method, breast engorgement knowledge, spouse occupation and education level (P<0.05).There were statistically significant differences in practice scores of whether there were medical workers in family members, whether they participated in pregnant women’s schools during pregnancy, feeding methods, whether they knew breast engorgement, and whether they knew about breast engorgement, and the age, occupation and education level of their spouses (P<0.05).

Discussion

Analysis of the current situation of maternal breast engorgement

Analysis of the current situation of maternal knowledge of breast engorgement

Approximately half of the women had a poor score of breast engorgement knowledge. This study is consistent with the findings of Thomas [15], who noted that about 57% of women lack knowledge to prevent or treat breast engorgement after childbirth. In addition, in a cross-sectional study of 100 women [16].The majority of postpartum mothers (52%) had an average knowledge of breast engorgement, with the average knowledge score being the highest in symptoms (64.16%) and the lowest score on factors causing breast engorgement (42.62%), while in this study, the maternal treatment of hot and cold compresses during breast engorgement scored the lowest, with an error rate of 86.9%-89.1%, which may be related to the traditional Chinese belief that cold water should not be touched during confinement and only hot water should be used. Therefore, medical workers should inform mothers of the time, cause, prevention and treatment of breast engorgement to reduce the occurrence of breast engorgement.

Analysis of the current situation of maternal attitude of breast engorgement

Almost all of the women had a positive attitude towards breast engorgement. Among them, 98.3% of the women felt the need to know how to deal with breast engorgement, which was consistent with the findings of Anderson [17], When the prevention and solution measures related to breast engorgement are mastered, the occurrence of breast engorgement can be reduced, and the breastfeeding rate can be improved, which to some extent also indicates that the mothers in this study lack a certain confidence in the knowledge to breast engorgement. Studies have shown that predisposing factors for breast engorgement include lack of early infant suckling, supplementation of infant formula, delayed breastfeeding, and restriction of breastfeeding time and frequency [6, 8]. These factors can be prevented through early and effective management, especially early and effective breastfeeding [6, 18].

Analysis of the current situation of maternal practice of breast engorgement

Close to two thirds of women had positive practices to prevention breast breast engorgement, but they still do things that cause breast engorgement. Under the influence of traditional concepts, more than half of the maternal choose stew foods to promote breast milk secretion. The Guidelines for Breastfeeding Promotion Strategies (2018 edition) [19] believe that it is an unwise choice for parturients to consume too much stew foods to increase breast milk secretion, because there is currently a lack of strong evidence that these traditional lower milk foods can increase breast milk secretion. However, contrary to the study by Xing Chunfeng et al. [20], the latter study found that the consumption of galacturient decoction on the third day after delivery would increase the lactation volume. At this time, the increase of lactation volume was out of balance with the feeding demand of the newborn, which could easily cause milk accumulation and lead to breast engorgement. This study also found that some women gave their newborns more or less milk bottle to supplement milk powder because they were worried about their lack of milk, indicating that most women had insufficient confidence in their lactation [21]. Using a bottle too early and adding formula milk to the baby’s diet can cause confusion of the nipples, making it difficult for the baby to suck effectively, resulting in damage to the mother’s nipples, nipple pain, and even edema of the nipple and areola. This, in turn, further affects the baby’s sucking and milk drainage, creating a vicious cycle, which leads to breast engorgement. Therefore, the “three noes” management of no milk powder, feeding bottle and pacifier should be done well in obstetrics, and optimized lactation guidance should be provided for maternal. At the same time, guide the maternal and their families to identify the signals of hunger and fullness of infants and children, and responding feeding [22], and achieve unrestricted feeding and effective sucking.

Analysis of influencing factors of maternal breast engorgement

Graduate and above(maternity) is an influential factor in the attitude score of maternal breast engorgement, which is contrary to the research result of Indian scholar Sharma [16], in the latter study, education level has been proved to have statistical significance on maternal breast engorgement knowledge score. This study pointed out that the average knowledge score of postgraduates and above was 25.20 points, while the average education level from primary to secondary school was 19.00 points. This difference may be due to the different questionnaires and regions used. In this study, the highest score of maternal breast engorgement knowledge was 16 points. In addition, mothers with lower education levels may face more problems such as work and family income, and have limited access to knowledge, thus limiting the establishment of positive attitudes.

Participation in maternity school during pregnancy was an influential factor for maternal breast engorgement knowledge score, attitude score and total score of KAP. In this study, only 9.81% of pregnant women participated in maternity school. Studies have shown [13] that multiple teaching activities can improve maternal knowledge of breast engorgement, prevent breast engorgement, and promote successful breastfeeding. Similar views [23] were also expressed in another scholar’s study. Through prenatal education, the maternal knowledge score of the experimental group increased from 10.20 to 20.76 points, and that of the control group increased from 9.83 to 10.03 points, with statistical significance (P<0.05). The incidence of breast engorgement was 13.3% in the experimental group and 63.3% in the control group, indicating that prenatal education was effective to reducing the incidence of breast engorgement. In addition, scholars such as Liu Jun et al. [24]. found that the evidence-based prevention and management plan for breast swelling can enhance the mothers’ awareness and self-efficacy of breastfeeding. The breastfeeding knowledge score of the control group was (11.22 ± 3.07) points, while that of the experimental group was (12.65 ± 2.88) points. The difference between the two groups was statistically significant (t= -2.637, P < 0.05). The breastfeeding self-efficacy score of the control group was (41.75 ± 10.04) points, and that of the experimental group was (46.08 ± 11.73) points. The difference between the two groups was also statistically significant (t=-2.174, P < 0.05).

Knowing breast engorgement is a factor affecting the knowledge score of breast engorgement and the total score of KPA. In this study, 48.3% of the people knew about breast engorgement. Developmental psychology believes that [25] with the improvement of knowledge and skills or the ability to acquire resources, when they encounter various problems, they will actively seek help, solve the problems through various channels and ways, and achieve the purpose of improving their behavior. In Anderson study [17], through the education of midwives and lactation consultants on breast engorgement, 65% of midwives and lactation consultants were able to correctly identify and manage the engorgement, and the recommendations on breast engorgement given to mothers tended to be consistent, which improved the satisfaction of mothers. Although the subjects of this study were midwives and lactation consultants, it also showed that mastering the knowledge of breast engorgement promoted the change of behavior and improved the overall level of KAP.

Feeding mode was the influencing factor of maternal practice score and total score of KAP, among which the practice score of artificial feeding and the total score of KAP were lower than those of exclusive breastfeeding, and those of exclusive breastfeeding were lower than those of mixed feeding. This discrepancy may be due to the fact that women who are artificially fed or separated from each other have no breastfeeding experience, making it difficult to make positive choices about the breastfeeding-related practice components.

Vaginal delivery is the influencing factor of maternal attitude score, and the attitude score of maternal is higher than that of cesarean section. Wu Min’s study [26] pointed out that the incidence of breast engorgement in vaginal delivery was lower than that in cesarean section, and the incidence of breast engorgement in vaginal delivery was about 10%, and the incidence of cesarean section was between 35.3% and 42.2%. This research result was also confirmed by other studies [27]. This may be because women who had a vaginal delivery recover more quickly than those who had a cesarean section, and no wound pain trouble, can be earlier, more convenient to breastfeed, reduce the possibility of breast engorgement.

The total KAP score of those whose spouse engaged in business or service industry was lower than that of spouse engaged in professional technology. The reason may be that the spouses engaged in professional and technical work have more time to acquire knowledge and pass it on to the pregnant women than the business service workers. On the other hand, the professional and technical workers are more sensitive to theoretical knowledge or technology, so that they can quickly acquire key information and have an impact on the pregnant women. It can be seen that family support has an important impact on maternity. Studies have found that family-centered social support can improve the level of breastfeeding self efficacy, breastfeeding rate, and reduce unnecessary breastfeeding problems and postpartum depression [28].

Summary

To sum up, in this study, maternal knowledge of breast engorgement was lacking, maternal attitude towards breast engorgement was more positive, and the practice affecting breast engorgement needed to be improved. Maternal education level, maternity school, whether knew breast engorgement, feeding mode, delivery mode and spouse's occupation were the influencing factors for maternal knowledge, attitude and practice of breast engorgement. Therefore, medical institutions should carry out personalized education, explore online and offline education models of maternity school and postpartum home services, attach importance to family-centered social support and empowerment education, help women master breast engorgement prevention and management skills, improve the level of maternal breast engorgement knowledge and practice, so as to reduce the occurrence of breast engorgement and increase the breastfeeding rate. However, due to the limitation of time and conditions, this study only took the inpatients in the obstetrics department of 3 tertiary hospitals as the investigation objects. In the future research, the scope of investigation can be expanded to understand the overall situation of maternal knowledge, attitude and practice of breast engorgement more comprehensively, so as to provide reference for breast engorgement prevention and nursing intervention.
