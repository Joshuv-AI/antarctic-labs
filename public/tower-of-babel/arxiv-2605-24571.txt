t t -tone edge coloring of graphs
Hadeel Al Bazzal

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce the notion of t t -tone edge coloring. A t t -tone edge k k -coloring of a graph G G assigns to each edge of G G a set of t t distinct colors from { 1 , … , k } \{1,\dots,k\} such that any two edges at distance d d share fewer than d d common colors. The t t -tone chromatic index of G G , denoted by τ t ′ ​ ( G ) \tau^{\prime}_{t}(G) , is the minimum integer k k for which G G admits a t t -tone edge k k -coloring. We focus on the case t = 2 t=2 and establish several upper bounds on τ 2 ′ \tau^{\prime}_{2} . In particular, for every graph G G with maximum degree Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 2 \Delta(G)\geq 2 , we prove that τ 2 ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 6 ​ Δ ​ ( G ) − 4 \tau^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq 6\Delta(G)-4 , improving the corresponding bound derived from the vertex analogue. We also show that every tree T T with Δ ⁡ ( T ) ≥ 3 \Delta(T)\geq 3 satisfies τ 2 ′ ​ ( T ) = 2 ​ Δ ​ ( T ) \tau^{\prime}_{2}(T)=2\Delta(T) . Furthermore, every planar graph G G satisfies τ 2 ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ max ⁡ { 41 , 3 ​ Δ ​ ( G ) + 5 } \tau^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq\max\{41,3\Delta(G)+5\} , while every outerplanar graph G G satisfies τ 2 ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ max ⁡ { 14 , 3 ​ Δ ​ ( G ) } \tau^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq\max\{14,3\Delta(G)\} . For subcubic graphs G G , the vertex analogue yields τ 2 ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 12 \tau^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq 12 . We improve this bound to 11 11 for claw-free subcubic graphs and to 10 10 for 2 2 -degenerate subcubic graphs. Finally, we propose two conjectures concerning optimal bounds for cubic and K 4 K_{4} -free cubic graphs, and establish them for series-parallel subcubic multigraphs and subcubic outerplanar graphs, respectively.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, all graphs are assumed to be simple and finite unless stated otherwise. We denote the vertex set and edge set of a graph
G
by
V(G)
and
E(G)
, respectively. In a proper coloring of a graph
G
, a color (integer) is assigned to each vertex of
G
such that adjacent vertices receive distinct colors. The chromatic number of
G
, denoted by
\chi(G)
, is the minimum integer
k
for which
G
admits a proper coloring. Over time, this classical notion has inspired numerous generalizations. For instance, in a distance-
t
coloring
[
10
]
, the condition is strengthened so that any two vertices at distance at most
t
receive distinct colors. Another extension is the notion of a
t
-tone
k
-coloring
[
3
,
8
]
. A
t
-tone
k
-coloring of a graph
G
assigns to each vertex of
G
a set of
t
distinct colors from
\{1,\dots,k\}
such that any two vertices at distance
d
share fewer than
d
common colors. The minimum integer
k
for which
G
admits a
t
-tone
k
-coloring is called the
t
-tone chromatic number of
G
, denoted by
\tau_{t}(G)
. Note that the distance between two distinct vertices
u
and
v
in
G
, denoted by
d_{G}(u,v)
, is the length of a shortest path between
u
and
v
in
G
.

Edge analogues of several vertex coloring notions have also been studied. In particular, a proper edge coloring of a graph
G
assigns colors to each edge of
G
such that adjacent edges receive distinct colors. The chromatic index of
G
, denoted by
\chi^{\prime}(G)
, is the minimum integer
k
for which
G
admits a proper edge coloring. Furthermore, a distance-
t
edge coloring
[
9
]
of a graph
G
assigns a color to each edge of
G
such that any two edges at distance at most
t
receive distinct colors. The distance-
t
chromatic index of
G
, denoted by
\chi^{\prime}_{t}(G)
, is the minimum integer
k
for which
G
admits a distance-
t
edge coloring. This parameter is related to graph powers, since
\chi^{\prime}_{t}(G)=\chi((L(G))^{t})
, where
L(G)
denotes the line graph of
G
, and
G^{t}
denotes the
t
-th power of a graph
G
, obtained by joining all pairs of vertices at distance at most
t
. In the context of distance-
t
edge coloring, the distance between two edges
e,e^{\prime}\in E(G)
is defined as the distance between the corresponding vertices in the line graph
L(G)
of
G
. We adopt the same definition throughout this paper and denote this distance by
d_{G}(e,e^{\prime})
.

While
t
-tone coloring has been investigated for vertices, to the best of our knowledge, no edge analogue of this notion has yet been studied. In this paper, we introduce and study the notion of
t
-tone edge coloring.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
k,t\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leq t\leq k
. Let
\binom{\{1,\dots,k\}}{t}
denote the set of all
t

\{1,\ldots,k\}

t
-tone edge

-coloring
of
G
is a mapping
f:E(G)\to\binom{\{1,\dots,k\}}{t}
such that for any two edges
e,e^{\prime}\in E(G)
, we have
|f(e)\cap f(e^{\prime})|<d_{G}(e,e^{\prime})
.

A graph admitting a
t
-tone edge
k
-coloring is said to be
t
-tone edge
k
-colorable. The
t
-tone chromatic index of
G
, denoted by
\tau^{\prime}_{t}(G)
, is the minimum integer
k
such that
G
admits a
t
-tone edge
k
-coloring.

Given a
t
-tone edge coloring
f
of
G
, we call
f(e)
the label of
e
and the elements of
\{1,\dots,k\}
colors. When no ambiguity arises, we omit set brackets in labels; that is, the label
\{a,b\}
is written simply as
ab
. Edges
e
and
e^{\prime}
(or vertices
x
and
y
) are considered
neighbors
when their distance is one, and
second neighbors
when their distance is two.

The degree of a vertex

in
G
, denoted by
d_{G}(v)
, is the number of edges incident with
v
. The maximum vertex degree of
G
is denoted by
\Delta(G)
. Similarly, the degree of an edge

of
G
, denoted by
d_{G}(e)
, is the number of edges adjacent to
e
. In particular, if
e=xy
, then
d_{G}(e)=d_{G}(x)+d_{G}(y)-2
. For an edge
xy\in E(G)
, we also define

\eta_{G}(xy)=\left|\bigl(N_{G}(x)\cup N_{G}(y)\bigr)\setminus\{x,y\}\right|=|N_{G}(x)\cup N_{G}(y)|-2.

Graph families are denoted by
C_{n}
,
P_{n}
, and

for the cycle, path, and complete graph on

for the star on

\tau^{\prime}_{t}(K_{1,n})=tn
.

The notion of
t
-tone edge coloring generalizes proper edge coloring. In particular, when
t=1
, we obtain
\tau^{\prime}_{1}(G)=\chi^{\prime}(G)
for every graph
G
. Note that, for each
t
, the parameter
\tau^{\prime}_{t}
is monotone under taking subgraphs: if
H
is a subgraph of
G
, then
\tau^{\prime}_{t}(H)\leq\tau^{\prime}_{t}(G)
. A natural lower bound for
\tau^{\prime}_{t}(G)
arises from the local obstruction
K_{1,\Delta(G)}
. Consequently, for every graph

\tau^{\prime}_{t}(G)\geq t\,\Delta(G)
.

Since a
t
-tone edge coloring of a graph

t
-tone coloring of its line graph

, several results on
t
-tone coloring can be transferred directly to the edge-setting. In particular, using
\tau^{\prime}_{t}(G)=\tau_{t}(L(G))
, we obtain the following bounds for
\tau^{\prime}_{t}(G)
. Bickle and Phillips
[
3
]
proved that
\tau_{t}(G)\geq\tau_{t-1}(G)+2
for every nonempty graph
G
and for
t\geq 2
. Applying this result to
L(G)
immediately yields the following lower bound.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
G
be a nonempty graph and
t\geq 2
. Then
\tau^{\prime}_{t}(G)\geq\tau^{\prime}_{t-1}(G)+2
.

Fonger et al.
[
8
]
established that
\tau_{t}(G)\leq\tau_{t-1}(G)+\chi(G^{t})
for
t\geq 2
. This allows us to obtain the following upper bound on
\tau^{\prime}_{t}(G)
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
G
be a graph and
t\geq 2
. Then
\tau^{\prime}_{t}(G)\leq\tau^{\prime}_{t-1}(G)+\chi^{\prime}_{t}(G)
.

Another general upper bound follows from a result established in
[
4
]
, which gives the best known upper bound for
\tau_{t}(G)
. It states that for every integer
t
and every nonempty graph
G
,
\tau_{t}(G)\leq(t^{2}+t)\Delta(G)
. Applying this inequality to the line graph
L(G)
and using the fact that
\Delta(L(G))\leq 2\Delta(G)-2
, we obtain the following upper bound.

Corollary 1.3
.

For every integer
t
and every nonempty graph
G
,
\tau^{\prime}_{t}(G)\leq(t^{2}+t)(2\Delta(G)-2)
.

The
t
-tone chromatic number is known exactly for all values of
t
in the case of paths. Bickle and Phillips
[
3
]
proved that
\tau_{t}(P_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\max\Bigl\{0,\,t-\binom{i}{2}\Bigr\}
for every integer
n\geq 1
. Since
L(P_{n})=P_{n-1}
, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
P_{n}
be a path of order
n\geq 2
. Then,

\tau^{\prime}_{t}(P_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\max\Bigl\{0,\,t-\binom{i}{2}\Bigr\}.

2
2-tone edge coloring of graphs

In this section, we study the case
t=2
. In this case, a
2
-tone edge coloring of a graph
G
assigns to each edge of
G
a set of two colors such that adjacent edges receive disjoint sets and edges at distance two receive distinct sets.

A natural starting point is the class of cycles. In
[
3
]
, it was shown that
\tau_{2}(C_{n})
equals six when
n\in\{3,4,7\}
, and equals five otherwise. Since
L(C_{n})=C_{n}
, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.1
.

For the cycle
C_{n}
,

\tau^{\prime}_{2}(C_{n})=\begin{cases}6,&\text{if }n\in\{3,4,7\}\\
5,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

For a graph
G
, we have
\tau^{\prime}_{2}(G)=0
if
G
is empty, and
\tau^{\prime}_{2}(G)=2
when
\Delta(G)=1
. If
\Delta(G)=2
, then
G
is a disjoint union of paths and cycles, and hence
\tau^{\prime}_{2}(G)\in\{5,6\}
by Corollaries
1.4
and
2.1
. Therefore, it suffices to consider graphs with
\Delta(G)\geq 3
.

The primary approach in this paper involves constructing a
t
-tone edge
k
-coloring of a graph iteratively.

Definition 2.1
.

A
partial
t
-tone edge
k
-coloring
of a graph
G
is a function

f:E^{\prime}\to\binom{\{1,\dots,k\}}{t}
, where
E^{\prime}\subseteq E(G)
, such that for every pair of edges
e,e^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
, we have
|f(e)\cap f(e^{\prime})|<d_{G}(e,e^{\prime})
. Edges in
E(G)\setminus E^{\prime}
are called
uncolored
.

Note that a
t
-tone edge
k
-coloring of a subgraph
H\subseteq G
is not necessarily a partial
t
-tone edge
k
-coloring of
G
, as the distance between a pair of edges in
G
may be strictly less than their distance in
H
. This issue, however, does not arise when the subgraph is formed by vertex deletion.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be obtained from a graph
G
by deleting a vertex. Since removing a vertex does not decrease the distance between any pair of edges in
G^{\prime}
, any
t
-tone edge
k
-coloring of
G^{\prime}
is a partial
t
-tone edge
k
-coloring of
G
.

We now introduce the following terminology.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
f
be a partial
2
-tone edge coloring of a graph
G
, and let
e\in E(G)
be an uncolored edge. A
valid label
for
e
is a label by which
f
can be extended to
e
. A color is
free at
e
if it does not appear on any edge adjacent to
e
. Let
f_{r}(e)
denote the number of free colors at
e
. A
candidate label
for
e
is a label consisting only of free colors.

The notions of partial coloring, valid label, free color, and candidate label were introduced by Cranston, Kim, and Kinnersley
[
4
]
for
t
-tone coloring; the definitions above are their edge analogues.

As mentioned in the introduction, every graph
G
satisfies
\tau^{\prime}_{t}(G)\geq t\Delta(G)
. In particular, for
t=2
, we obtain
\tau^{\prime}_{2}(G)\geq 2\Delta(G)
. We now show that this bound is sharp for trees with maximum degree at least three.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
T
be a tree with
\Delta(T)\geq 3
. Then
\tau^{\prime}_{2}(T)=2\Delta(T)
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta=\Delta(T)
. Since
T
contains
K_{1,\Delta}
as a subgraph and
\tau^{\prime}_{2}(K_{1,\Delta})=2\Delta
, the monotonicity of
\tau^{\prime}_{2}
under subgraphs implies that
\tau^{\prime}_{2}(T)\geq 2\Delta
. It remains to show that
T
admits a
2
-tone edge coloring using
2\Delta
colors.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that this is false, and let
T
be a counterexample with the minimum number of vertices. Then
T
is not a star, and hence it contains at least two internal vertices. Fix a root
r
with
d_{T}(r)=\Delta
, and let
v
be a non-leaf vertex at maximum distance from
r
. Then every neighbor of
v
other than its parent
p
(possibly
p=r
) is a leaf. Let
v_{1}
be one such leaf, and set
T^{\prime}=T-\{v_{1}\}
. Since
r\in T^{\prime}
,
\Delta(T^{\prime})=\Delta
. By the minimality of
T
, the tree
T^{\prime}
admits a
2
-tone edge
2\Delta
-coloring
f
. By Remark
2.1
,
f
is a partial
2
-tone edge coloring of
T
.

We claim that
d_{T}(v)\geq 3
. Otherwise, since
d_{T}(v)\geq 2
, we have
d_{T}(v)=2
, and thus
v_{1}
and
p
are the only neighbors of
v
in
T
. Hence,
f_{r}(vv_{1})\geq 2\Delta-2
. Thus, there are at least
\binom{2\Delta-2}{2}
candidate labels for
vv_{1}
. The second neighbors of
vv_{1}
are precisely the edges incident with
p
other than
vp
, and there are at most
\Delta-1
such edges. Since
\binom{2\Delta-2}{2}>\Delta-1
for
\Delta\geq 3
, at least one candidate label is valid for
vv_{1}
, a contradiction.

Thus,
d_{T}(v)\geq 3
, and hence
v
is adjacent to at least two leaves. Consequently,
f_{r}(vv_{1})\geq 2
. Let
a
and
b
be two colors free at
vv_{1}
. If the label
ab
does not appear on any second neighbor of
vv_{1}
, then
ab
is a valid label for
vv_{1}
, a contradiction. Therefore, the label
ab
must appear on a second neighbor of
vv_{1}
, which implies that there exists an edge incident with
p
, distinct from
vp
, assigned
ab
. Let
v_{2}
be a leaf neighbor of
v
distinct from
v_{1}
, and let
f(vv_{2})=cd
. Since the second neighbors of
vv_{2}
are precisely the edges incident with
p
other than
vp
, one of which is colored
ab
, we recolor
vv_{2}
with
ca
, and assign
db
to
vv_{1}
, a contradiction.∎

Before proceeding further, we introduce the following additional terminology.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
e=xy
be an edge of a graph
G
. A vertex
u\in V(G)
is an
intermediate vertex
of
e
if
u
is adjacent to
x
or
y
and is incident with a second neighbor of
e
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
f
be a partial
2
-tone edge coloring of a graph
G
, and let
e\in E(G)
be an uncolored edge. A second neighbor
e^{\prime}
of
e
is called a
forbidding edge
for
e
if
f(e^{\prime})
is a candidate label for
e
. In this case, we say that
e^{\prime}

forbids
a label for
e
.

For any subset of candidate labels
L
, we say that
e^{\prime}
is a
forbidding edge for
e
under
L
if
f(e^{\prime})\in L
. In this case, we say that
e^{\prime}

forbids a label from
L
for
e
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
f
be a partial
2
-tone edge coloring of a graph
G
, and let
e\in E(G)
be an uncolored edge. If
L
is a family of pairwise intersecting candidate labels for
e
, then each intermediate vertex of
e
can be incident to at most one forbidding edge for
e
under
L
. Otherwise, two such edges would be adjacent, requiring their labels to be disjoint, which contradicts the fact that
L
is pairwise intersecting.

With these definitions in place, our first step is to determine the maximum size of a family of pairwise intersecting candidate labels for a given edge.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f
be a partial
2
-tone edge coloring of a graph
G
, and let
e
be an uncolored edge of
G
with
f_{r}(e)=m>2
. Let
L
be a largest family of pairwise intersecting candidate labels for
e
. Then,

|L|=\begin{cases}m-1,&\text{if }m>3\\
3,&\text{if }m=3\end{cases}.

Proof.

If
m=3
, then the result follows since all
2
-element subsets of a
3
-element set are pairwise intersecting. Assume now that
m\geq 4
. By the Erdős–Ko–Rado Theorem
[
6
]
, which states that for
n\geq 2k
, the largest intersecting family of
k
-subsets of an
n
-element set has size
\binom{n-1}{k-1}
, setting
n=m
and
k=2
yields
|L|=\binom{m-1}{1}=m-1
.∎

A natural approach for extending a partial
2
-tone edge coloring of a graph
G
to an uncolored edge
e\in E(G)
is to ensure that the number of candidate labels for
e
exceeds the number of its second neighbors. When this condition fails, we instead exploit families of pairwise intersecting labels, as formalized in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f
be a partial
2
-tone edge coloring of a graph
G
, and let
e
be an uncolored edge of
G
. Let
L
be a set of pairwise intersecting candidate labels for
e
. If
|L|>m
, where
m
is the number of intermediate vertices of
e
, then
f
can be extended to
e
in at least
|L|-m
distinct ways.

Proof.

Let
m
be the number of intermediate vertices of
e
in
G
. By Remark
2.2
, each such vertex is incident to at most one forbidding edge for
e
under
L
; thus,
e
has at most
m
forbidding edges under
L
. Since each forbidding edge corresponds exactly to one label in
L
, at least
|L|-m
labels in
L
are valid for
e
. Each such label yields a distinct extension of
f
to
e
.∎

Since the number of intermediate vertices of
e
is at most
\eta_{G}(e)
, Lemma
2.2
immediately yields the following corollary.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
f
be a partial
2
-tone edge coloring of a graph
G
, and let
e
be an uncolored edge of
G
. Let
L
be a set of pairwise intersecting candidate labels for
e
. If
|L|>\eta_{G}(e)
, then
f
can be extended to
e
in at least
|L|-\eta_{G}(e)
distinct ways.

To establish a baseline for our main result, we recall the best known upper bound on the
2
-tone chromatic number. It was shown in
[
2
]
that every graph
G
with
\Delta(G)\geq 2
satisfies

\tau_{2}(G)\leq 2\Delta(G)-1+\left\lceil\frac{1+\sqrt{1+8\Delta(G)(\Delta(G)-1)}}{2}\right\rceil.

Applying this bound to the line graph
L(G)
yields the following corollary.

Corollary 2.3
.

For every graph
G
with maximum degree
\Delta\geq 2
,

\tau^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq 4\Delta-5+\left\lceil\frac{1+\sqrt{1+8(2\Delta-2)(2\Delta-3)}}{2}\right\rceil.

We improve upon this result by providing the following upper bound.

Theorem 2.2
.

For every graph
G
with
\Delta(G)\geq 2
,
\tau^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq 6\Delta(G)-4
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph with maximum degree
\Delta=\Delta(G)
. Let
k=6\Delta-4
and let
E(G)=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}\}
. Starting with all edges uncolored, we extend our partial edge coloring to
e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}
in order. Consider an edge
e_{i}
with
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Since
d_{G}(e_{i})\leq 2\Delta-2
, it follows that
f_{r}(e_{i})\geq 2\Delta
. Let
L
be a largest family of pairwise intersecting candidate labels for
e_{i}
. Since
\Delta\geq 2
, we have
f_{r}(e_{i})\geq 4
. Hence, Lemma
2.1
implies that
|L|\geq 2\Delta-1
. Since
\eta_{G}(e_{i})\leq 2\Delta-2
, we have
|L|>\eta_{G}(e_{i})
. Therefore, by Corollary
2.2
, at least one label in
L
is valid for
e_{i}
, and the coloring can be extended to
e_{i}
. Proceeding iteratively, we obtain a
2
-tone edge
k
-coloring of
G
.∎

Theorem
2.2
improves the bound of Corollary
2.3
whenever
\Delta(G)>4
. Asymptotically, our result reduces the leading constant from approximately
4+2\sqrt{2}\approx 6.83
to
6
, yielding an improvement of about
0.83\Delta
colors. When
\Delta(G)=4
, both results yield the same bound, namely
20
. For
\Delta(G)\leq 3
, however, Corollary
2.3
provides a sharper bound (
13
) than the one obtained here (
14
).

We now turn to planar and outerplanar graphs. Our proofs rely on the minimal counterexample method together with the structural properties of planar and outerplanar graphs provided by the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
1
]
)
.

For every planar graph
G
there exists
u\in V(G)
such that
d_{G}(u)\leq 5
and
u
has at most two neighbors with degree at least
11
.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
7
]
)
.

For every outerplanar graph
G
there exists
xy\in E(G)
with
d_{G}(x)=1
, or
d_{G}(x)=2
and
d_{G}(y)\leq 4
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For every planar graph
G
,
\tau_{2}^{\prime}(G)\leq\max\{41,\,3\Delta(G)+5\}
.

Proof.

Assume, for a contradiction, that the statement is false, and let
G
be a counterexample with minimum order. Set
\Delta=\Delta(G)
and
k=\max\{41,\,3\Delta+5\}
.

By Lemma
2.3
, there exists
u\in V(G)
with
d_{G}(u)\leq 5
such that at most two of its neighbors have degree at least
11
. Let
G^{\prime}=G-u
. By the minimality of
G
,
G^{\prime}
admits a
2
-tone edge
k
-coloring
f
. By Remark
2.1
,
f
is a partial
2
-tone edge
k
-coloring of

We now prove that every subcubic outerplanar graph satisfies Conjecture
