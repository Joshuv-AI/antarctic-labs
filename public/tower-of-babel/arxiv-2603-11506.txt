Introduction to Dieudonné modules and supersingular abelian varieties revisited
Chia-Fu Yu

Abstract
. In this Note we present an expository account for Dieudonné modules and revisit supersingular abelian varieties. We give a simple proof of the uniqueness of products of two or more supersingular elliptic curves (a theorem due to Deligne, Ogus and Shioda), and of Oort’s theorem for superspecial abelian varieties.



1.
Introduction

The purpose of this Note is two-fold. One is to introduce basic theory for Dieudonné modules. The other is to explain their arithmetic applications to supersingular abelian varieties in more details.
Though it contains essentially no new results (except Lemma
27
and its applications); some proofs given are different from the original one and elementary.
This Note may serve as a supplement to the article by Harashita
[
14
]
in this volume and Karemaker’s lecture notes for the 2024 Arizona Winter School
[
17
]
.

Let
p
be a prime number.
In this Note, all ground fields are of characteristic

p
, unless specified otherwise.

Recall that an elliptic curve
E
over a field
K
is called
supersingular
if
E[p](\overline{K})=0
. Let
\Lambda
be the set of isomorphism classes of supersingular elliptic curves over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
.
Let
E\in\Lambda
,
B:=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits^{0}(E)
be the endomorphism algebra of
E
, which is a quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
ramified precisely at
p
and
\infty
, and
O_{B}:=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E)
be the endomorphism ring of
E
.
The Deuring–Eichler correspondence states that there is a bijection

(1.1)

\Lambda\simeq\mathop{\rm Cl}\nolimits(O_{B}),

where
\mathop{\rm Cl}\nolimits(O_{B})
denotes the set of isomorphism classes of right
O_{B}
-ideal classes, and one has the adelic interpretation:

\mathop{\rm Cl}\nolimits(O_{B})\simeq B^{\times}\backslash(B\otimes\mathbb{A}_{f})^{\times}/\widehat{O}_{B}^{\times}.

Here
\mathbb{A}_{f}
is the finite adele ring of
\mathbb{Q}
. The well-known class number formula then shows

(1.2)

|\Lambda|=\frac{p-1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}\left(1-\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{3}\left(1-\left(\frac{-4}{p}\right)\right),

where
(\cdot/p)
denotes the Legendre symbol.

We shall explore a higher dimensional generalization of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), and Dieudonné theory comes into play.

2.
Dieudonné modules

2.1.

Throughout this paper,
k
denotes a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
. Let
W:=W(k)
denote the ring of Witt vectors over

k
, and
L=W[1/p]
the fraction field of
W
.
Let
\sigma
be the Frobenius map on
W
and

L
induced by the endomorphism
\sigma:k\to k
,
a\mapsto a^{p}
for
a\in k
. We shall assume
k
to be algebraically closed in Lemma
4
, Theorem
5
and Subsection
2.4
.

If
H
is a commutative group or a sheaf of commutative groups on a scheme
S
, and
m
is an integer, denote by
H[m]
the kernel of the multiplication map
m:H\to H
.

Definition 1
.

(1) A
Dieudonné module
over
k
is a finite
W
-module
M
together with a
\sigma
-linear map
F
(Frobenius) and a

\sigma^{-1}
-linear map
V
(Verschiebung) on
M
such that
FV=VF=p
.

(2) (cf.
[
10
, p. 45–46]
) A
p
-divisible group
over
k
is a direct limit
G=\varinjlim G_{n}
of commutative finite locally free group scheme
G_{n}
over
k
such that

(i)

G_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}G_{m}[p^{n}]
for all integers
m\geq n\geq 0
;

(ii)

For any
n\geq 0
, the multiplication map
p:G_{n+1}\to G_{n}
is faithfully flat (i.e. flat and surjective).

Let
A_{k}=W(k)[F,V]
denote the non-commutative ring modulo the relations:

FV=VF=p,\quad Fa=a^{\sigma}F,\quad aV=Va^{\sigma},\quad a\in W.

Then a Dieudonné module
M
is a finitely generated
A_{k}
-module that is a finite
W
-module. If
G=\varinjlim G_{n}
is a
p
-divisible group, then there exists an integer
h\geq 0
, called the
height
of
G
and denoted by
h(G)
, such that
\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits G_{n}=p^{nh}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Example 2
.

(1)

M_{1}=\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle
is a free
W
-module of rank two with relations

F(e_{1})=e_{1},\quad V(e_{1})=pe_{1},\quad F(e_{2})=pe_{2},\quad V(e_{2})=e_{2}.

(2)

M_{2}=\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle
is a free
W
-module of rank two with relations

F(e_{1})=V(e_{1})=e_{2},\quad F(e_{2})=V(e_{2})=pe_{1}.

(3)

If
X
is an abelian variety, then
X[p^{\infty}]:=\varinjlim X[p^{n}]
is a
p
-divisible group of height
2\dim X
.

Classical Dieudonné theory is stated as follows.

Theorem 3

(cf.
[
10
, p. 69–70]
)
.

There is an
anti-equivalence of categories between the category of
p
-divisible
groups over
k
and the category of
W
-free Dieudonné modules over
k
.

For simplicity, Dieudonné modules will refer as

W
-free Dieudonné modules. The Dieudonné module associated to a
p
-divisible group
G
is denoted by
M(G)
.
The contravariant Dieudonné functor
G\mapsto M(G)
is exact and commutes with any perfect field base change and the dual. The dual of
M
is defined by

M^{t}:=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{W}(M,W),\quad F(f)(x):=f(V(x))^{\sigma},\quad V(f)(x):=f(F(x))^{\sigma^{-1}}

for any
f\in M^{t}
and
x\in M
. Then there is a natural isomorphism between
M(G^{t})
and the dual
M^{t}
of
M=M(G)
, where
G^{t}
is the Serre dual of
G

[
21
, Sect. 1.4]
.
Moreover, we have
h(G)=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits_{W}M
and there is a natural isomorphism
\mathop{\rm Lie}\nolimits(G)\simeq\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{k}(M/FM,k)
.

The Dieudonné module
M(E):=M(E[p^{\infty}])
associated to an elliptic curve
E
over
k
is a Dieudonné module
M
of rank
2
satisfying
\dim_{k}M/FM=\dim_{k}M/VM=1
.

Lemma 4
.

Assume that
k
is algebraically closed. Then
M=M(E)
is either isomorphic to
M_{1}
or isomorphic to
M_{2}
in Example
2
. Moreover,
E
is ordinary (resp. supersingular) if
M\simeq M_{1}
(resp.
M\simeq M_{2}
).

Proof.

Put
\overline{M}:=M/pM
. Then we have either (a)
F\overline{M}\neq V\overline{M}
, or (b)
F\overline{M}=V\overline{M}
. In case (a), the maps
F:F\overline{M}\to F\overline{M}
and
V:V\overline{M}\to V\overline{M}
are bijective. Then there exist elements
e_{1},e_{2}\in M\smallsetminus pM
such that
Fe_{1}\equiv e_{1}\mod pM
and
Ve_{2}\equiv e_{2}\mod pM
.
We now show that
F^{\infty}M:=\cap_{m\geq 1}F^{m}M
is a free
W
-module of rank
1
. Consider the short exact sequence

0\to F^{m}M/p^{m}M\to M/p^{m}M\to M/F^{m}M\to 0,

and
F^{m}M/p^{m}M
and
M/F^{m}M
are
W
-modules of length
m
. Since
F^{m}e_{1}\not\in pM
for all
m\geq 1
, we see that both are free
W/p^{m}W
-modules of rank
1
. This shows that
F^{\infty}M
is a free
W
-module of rank
1
. Since the map
F:F^{\infty}M\to F^{\infty}M
is bijective, there exists a generator
e_{1}\in F^{\infty}M
such that
Fe_{1}=e_{1}
. Similarly,
V^{\infty}M:=\cap_{m\geq 1}V^{m}M
is a free
W
-module of rank one and there exists a generator
e_{2}\in V^{\infty}M
such that
Ve_{2}=e_{2}
and therefore
M\simeq M_{1}
.
Since
F
is isomorphic to
F^{\infty}M
, the Dieudonné submodule
F^{\infty}M
is étale, that is, it corresponds to the étale
p
-divisible group
E[p^{\infty}]^{e}
of height one over
k
. This shows that
E[p](k)=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
and
E
is ordinary. In case (b), we have
F^{2}M=pM
. The skeleton
M^{\diamond}:=\{m\in M:F^{2}m=pm\}
is a Dieudonné module over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
and we have the isomorphism
M^{\diamond}\otimes_{W(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})}W(k)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}M
; see
[
21
, Sect. 5.7]
. Taking an element
e_{1}\in M^{\diamond}\smallsetminus(F,V)M^{\diamond}
and letting
e_{2}:=Fe_{1}
, then we have
M\simeq M_{2}
.
Since there is no étale part of the
p
-divisible group
E[p^{\infty}]
,
E
is supersingular.

2.2.
Newton polygons and
a
-numbers

To any relatively prime nonnegative integers
a
and

b
with
(a,b)\neq(0,0)
, one associates a Dieudonné module over
{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}

M_{a,b}:=W({{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})[F,V]/(F^{a}-V^{b}).

The rational number
\lambda=b/(a+b)
is called the slope of
M_{a,b}
.

Theorem 5

(Manin-Dieudonné
[
22
, Chap. II, “Classification Theorem”,
p. 35]
)
.

Assume that
k
is algebraically closed. For every Dieudonné module
M
over
k
, there exist relatively prime pairs
(a_{i},b_{i})\neq(0,0)
of
nonnegative integers and integers
m_{i}\geq 1
for
i=1,\dots,r
such that
there is an isomorphism of so called rational Dieudonné modules

(2.1)

M\otimes_{W(k)}L\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}M_{a_{i},b_{i}}^{m_{i}}\otimes_{W({{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})}L.

We shall give a proof of the Manin-Dieudonné Theorem in Section 4.
The
slope sequence
of
M
is defined to be the tuple
(\lambda_{1}^{\oplus(a_{1}+b_{1})m_{1}},\dots,\lambda_{r}^{\oplus(a_{r}+b_{r})m_{r}})
ordered so that
\lambda_{i}<\lambda_{i+1}
for all
i
,
where
\lambda_{i}:=b_{i}/(a_{i}+b_{i})
, and the rational numbers
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}

are called the
slopes
of
M
. When
k
is an arbitrary perfect field, the slope sequence and slopes of a Dieudonné module
M
over
k
are defined to be those of
M\otimes_{W(k)}W(\bar{k})
, where
\bar{k}
is an algebraic closure of
k
.

The
a
-number
of
M
is defined by

a(M):=\dim_{k}M/(F,V)M.

One has
a(M\otimes W(k^{\prime}))=a(M)
for any perfect field extension
k^{\prime}/k
. One also has
a(M^{t})=a(M)
.
Clearly,
a(M_{1,1}^{\oplus g})=g
. Conversely, if
M
is
a Dieudonné module with
\dim M/VM=\dim M/FM=a(M)=g
and
k
is algebraically closed, then we have
M\simeq M_{1,1}^{\oplus g}\otimes W(k)
. The proof is the same as that in Lemma
4
.

For any abelian variety
X
over
k
, the contravariant Dieudonné module associated with
X
is defined by
M(X):=M(X[p^{\infty}])
.
The slope sequence and slopes of an abelian variety
X
over a field
K
are defined as those of
M(X\otimes_{K}\overline{K})
. The
a
-number of
X
is defined by

(2.2)

a(X):=\dim_{\overline{K}}\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(\alpha_{p}\otimes\overline{K},X\otimes\overline{K})

where
\alpha_{p}:={\rm Spec}\,{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}[t]/(t^{p})
is the kernel of the Frobenius morphism
{{{\mathbb{G}}}_{\rm a}}\to{{{\mathbb{G}}}_{\rm a}}
,
x\mapsto x^{p}
of the additive group
{{{\mathbb{G}}}_{\rm a}}
over
{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
. Then
a(X)=a(M(X\otimes\overline{K}))
.

Remark 6
.

By
[
21
, Proposition 2.5]
, for any commutative finite group scheme
G
over a field
K
there exists a smallest subgroup scheme
A(G)
of
G
containing all of the images
\alpha_{p}\to G
over
K
. We give an example showing that
A(G)\otimes_{K}K^{\prime}\subsetneq A(G\otimes_{K}K^{\prime})
may occur for a field extension
K^{\prime}/K
.

Consider the commutative finite group scheme
G
over a non-perfect field
K
such that its Cartier dual
G^{D}={\rm Spec}\,A
is a subgroup scheme of
({{{\mathbb{G}}}_{\rm a}})^{2}
, where
A=K[x,y]/(x^{p^{2}},y^{p}-\lambda y^{p})
with
\lambda\in K\smallsetminus K^{p}
. Let
K^{\prime}:=K[\mu]
, where
\mu^{p}=\lambda
. As any map
g:\alpha_{p,K}\to G
induces a map
g^{D}:G^{D}={\rm Spec}\,A\to\alpha_{p,K}^{D}=\alpha_{p,K}={\rm Spec}\,K[t]/(t^{p})
, the element
f=(g^{D})^{*}(t)
satisfies
m^{*}(f)=f\otimes 1+1\otimes f
and
f^{p}=0
, where
m^{*}:A\to A\otimes_{K}A
is the co-multiplication of
A
. Therefore any map
(g^{D})^{*}:K[t]/(t^{p})\to A
factors through the subalgebra
K[T]\subseteq A
, where

T:=\{f\in A:m^{*}(f)=f\otimes 1+1\otimes f,f^{p}=0\}.

Alternatively, the surjective map
g^{D}:G^{D}\to\alpha_{p,K}
factors through
G^{D}\twoheadrightarrow H
, where
H={\rm Spec}\,K[T]
. Then
A(G):=H^{D}\subseteq G
is the smallest subgroup scheme containing all of the images
\alpha_{p}\to G
over
K
.
To show
A(G)\otimes_{K}K^{\prime}\subsetneq A(G\otimes_{K}K^{\prime})
, it suffices to show
T\otimes_{K}K^{\prime}\subsetneq T^{\prime}
, where

T^{\prime}:=\{f\in A\otimes_{K}K^{\prime}:m_{K^{\prime}}^{*}(f)=f\otimes 1+1\otimes f,f^{p}=0\}.

One computes that

\begin{split}T&=\{f\in A:f\text{ is an additive function and }f^{p}=0\}\\
&=\{f\in\langle 1,x,x^{p},y,y^{p}=\lambda x^{p}\rangle_{K}:f^{p}=0\}=\langle x^{p}\rangle_{K}.\end{split}

Similarly, we have

T^{\prime}=\{f\in\langle 1,x,x^{p},y\rangle_{K^{\prime}}:f^{p}=0\}=\langle x^{p},y-\mu x\rangle_{K^{\prime}}.

Thus,
T\otimes_{K}K^{\prime}\subsetneq T^{\prime}
and
A(G)\otimes_{K}K^{\prime}\subsetneq A(G\otimes_{K}K^{\prime})
.

Observe that
\dim_{K}\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{K}(\alpha_{p,K},G)=\dim T
. We have then

\dim_{K}\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{K}(\alpha_{p,K},G)<\dim_{K^{\prime}}\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{K^{\prime}}(\alpha_{p,K^{\prime}},G\otimes_{K}K^{\prime}).

This explains why one defines
a(X)
by (
2.2
).

2.3.
Cartier-Dieudonné theory

We briefly introduce Cartier-Dieudonné theory. The theory classifies commutative smooth formal groups over a general base and provides a framework for deformation theory of
p
-divisible smooth formal groups in terms of covariant Dieudonné modules.
We follow the convenient setting of
[
25
, Section 0]
and

[
8
, Section 2, p. 1011]
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring of characteristic
p
.
Let
W(R)
denote the ring of
Witt vectors over
R
, equipped with the Verschiebung
\tau
and
Frobenius
\sigma
:

\begin{split}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots)^{\tau}&=(0,a_{0},a_{1},\dots)\\
(a_{0},a_{1},\dots)^{\sigma}&=(a_{0}^{p},a_{1}^{p},\dots).\end{split}

Let
{\mathrm{Cart}_{p}}(R)
denote the Cartier ring
W(R)[F][\![V]\!]
modulo the
relations

\bullet

FV=p
and
VaF=a^{\tau}
,

\bullet

Fa=a^{\sigma}F
and
Va^{\tau}=aV,\ \forall\,a\in W(R).

Definition 7
.

A left
{\mathrm{Cart}_{p}}(R)
-module is
uniform
if it is complete and
separated in the
V
-adic topology. A uniform
{\mathrm{Cart}_{p}}(R)
-module
M
is

reduced
if
V
is injective on
M
and
M/VM
is a finite free

R
-module. A (covariant)
Dieudonné module over
R
is a finitely generated
reduced uniform
{\mathrm{Cart}_{p}}(R)
-module.

Definition 8
.

(1) Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. An
n
-dimensional
commutative smooth formal group
over
R
is a smooth formal scheme
G
over
R
of dimension
n
together with a commutative formal group law (
m:G\widehat{\otimes}_{R}G\to G
and
\varepsilon:{\rm Spec}\,R\to G
). More precisely,
(G,m)\simeq(\mathop{\rm Spf}\nolimits R[\underline{X}],F(\underline{X},\underline{Y}))
, where
\underline{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
,
\underline{Y}=(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})
and

F(\underline{X},\underline{Y})=(F_{1}(\underline{X},\underline{Y}),\dots,F_{n}(\underline{X},\underline{Y}))\in R[\underline{X},\underline{Y}]^{n}

such that

(i)

F(\underline{X},\underline{Y})=\underline{X}+\underline{Y}+
terms of higher degree;

(ii)

F(\underline{X},F(\underline{Y},\underline{Z}))=F(F(\underline{X},\underline{Y}),\underline{Z})
;

(iii)

F(\underline{Y},\underline{X})=F(\underline{X},\underline{Y})
.

It follows from (i) and (ii) that
F(\underline{X},0)=F(0,\underline{X})=\underline{X}
and there exists a unique power series
\psi(\underline{X})\in R[\underline{X}]
such that
F(\psi(\underline{X}),X)=F(X,\psi(\underline{X}))=0
.

(2) A
p
-divisible smooth formal group
over
R
is a commutative smooth formal group over
R
such that

(i)

G=\bigcup_{n}G[p^{n}]
;

(ii)

the multiplication map
p:G\to G
is surjective

as fppf sheaves on the category of Artinian
R
-algebras.

The definition of
p
-divisible groups over a general base
R
of characteristic
p
is analogous to Definition
1
(2). A connected
p
-divisible group can be also regarded as a
p
-divisible smooth formal group.

Theorem 9

(
[
40
]
,
[
9
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

There is an equivalence of categories between the category of finite-dimensional commutative smooth formal groups over
R
and the category of
covariant Dieudonné modules over
R
.

We denote the covariant Dieudonné functor by
{\bf D}_{*}
. The tangent space of a commutative smooth formal group
G
is canonically isomorphic to

{\bf D}_{*}(G)/V{\bf D}_{*}(G)
.

If
G
is a connected
p
-divisible group over
k
, then the covariant Dieudonné module
{\bf D}_{*}(G)
is a torsion-free Dieudonné module
M
over
k

(Definition
1
) such that
V^{m}M\subseteq pM
for some
m\geq 1
. Moreover,
there is a canonical isomorphism
M(G)^{t}\simeq{\bf D}_{*}(G)
of Dieudonné modules.
Let
G^{(p)}
denote the pull-back of
G/k
by the absolute Frobenius map
F_{{\rm Spec}\,k}:{\rm Spec}\,k\to{\rm Spec}\,k
.
The relative Frobenius morphism
F_{G/k}:G\to G^{(p)}
induces the operator
F
on
M(G)
and the operator
V
on
{\bf D}_{*}(G)
, respectively. The relative Verschiebung morphism
V_{G/k}:G^{(p)}\to G
induces the operator
V
on
M(G)
and the operator
F
on
{\bf D}_{*}(G)
, respectively. Thus, the map
V_{G/k}:\mathop{\rm Lie}\nolimits(G^{(p)})\to\mathop{\rm Lie}\nolimits(G)
induces the
\sigma
-linear map
F
on
{\bf D}_{*}(G)/V{\bf D}_{*}(G)
.

The covariant functor
{\bf D}_{*}
can only apply to the smooth part of a
p
-divisible group. In order to carry the information of the étale part,
the covariant Dieudonné module of an abelian variety
X
over
k
is defined by

D_{*}(X):={\bf D}_{*}(X[p^{\infty}]^{0})\oplus M(X[p^{\infty}]^{e})^{t},

where
X[p^{\infty}]^{0}
and
X[p^{\infty}]^{e}
are respective connected and étale parts of the
p
-divisible group
X[p^{\infty}]
.

2.4.
The construction of deformations

Let
M_{0}
be the covariant Dieudonné module of a
p
-divisible smooth formal group
G_{0}
over an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
g:=\dim_{k}M_{0}/VM_{0}
and
h=\dim_{k}(VM_{0})/pM_{0}
.
Choose a
W
-basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{g+h}
of
M_{0}
such that

(2.3)

\begin{split}Fe_{i}&=\sum_{j=1}^{g+h}a_{ij}e_{j},\quad\quad(i=1,\dots,g),\\
e_{i}&=V(\sum_{j=1}^{g+h}a_{ij}e_{j}),\quad(i=g+1,\dots,g+h)\end{split}

for some invertible matrix
(a_{ij})
with entries in
W
.

Theorem 10

(
[
25
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a local Artinian
k
-algebra with residue field
k
.
There is a one-to-one correspondence between isomorphism classes of deformations of
M_{0}
over
R
and maps

\begin{split}d:VM_{0}/M_{0}&\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{k}(M_{0}/VM_{0}),\\
\overline{e}_{i}&\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{g}\bar{d}_{ij}\otimes\overline{e}_{j},\quad(g+1\leq i\leq g+h),\end{split}

where
\overline{e}_{i}
,
\overline{e}_{j}
are the images of
e_{i},e_{j}
in
VM_{0}/pM_{0}
and
M_{0}/VM_{0}
respectively. Set

d_{ij}=(\bar{d}_{ij},0,...)\in W(R)
for
g+1\leq i\leq g+h
and
1\leq j\leq g
, and
d_{ij}=0
otherwise. The Dieudonné module
M_{d}
corresponding to the map
d
is defined by the generators
e_{1},\dots,e_{g+h}
and relations

(2.4)

\begin{split}Fe_{i}&=\sum_{j=1}^{g+h}a_{ij}\left(e_{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{g}d_{jk}e_{k}\right),\quad\quad(i=1,\dots,g),\\
e_{i}&=V\left(\sum_{j=1}^{g+h}a_{ij}\left(e_{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{g}d_{jk}e_{k}\right)\right),\quad(i=g+1,\dots,g+h).\end{split}

Furthermore every Dieudonné module over
R
which reduces to
M_{0}
over
k
is isomorphic to exactly one Dieudonné module
M_{d}
.

3.
Proof of the Manin-Dieudonné theorem

We provide a proof of the Manin-Dieudonné Theorem for the reader’s convenience, as this is a very important result in Dieudonné theory. We follow the reference Demazure
[
10
]
.
In this section, we let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p
, and assume it to be algebraically closed in Subsection
3.3
.

3.1.
F
-spaces

Recall that
L=W[1/p]
denotes the fraction field of the ring
W=W(k)
of Witt vectors over
k
.

3.2.
Endomorphism algebras of
F
-spaces

Recall that a cyclic algebra over a field
K
of any characteristic of degree
r
is a triple

(K_{r}/K,\tau,a):=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1}K_{r}\eta^{i}=K_{r}\langle\eta\rangle,

where

K_{r}/K
is a cyclic extension of degree
r
with Galois group
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{r}/K)=\langle\tau\rangle
,
a\in K^{\times}
, and
\eta
is an element satisfying the relations

\eta^{r}=a\quad\text{and}\quad\eta x=\tau(x)\eta,\quad x\in K_{r}.

Every cyclic algebra
(K_{r}/K,\tau,a)
is a central simple algebra over
K
of degree
r
(i.e. of dimension
r^{2}
over
K
). One has
(K_{r}/K,\tau,1)\simeq\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{r}(K)
, and

(K_{r}/K,\tau,a_{1})\simeq(K_{r}/K,\tau,a_{2})\iff a_{1}a_{2}^{-1}\in\mathop{\rm Nr}\nolimits_{K_{r}/K}(K^{\times}_{r}).

If
K
is a nonarchimedean local field, then every central simple algebra
A/K
of degree
r
is isomorphic to
(K_{r}/K,\sigma,a)
, where
K_{r}/K
is the unramified extension of degree
r
,
\sigma
the arithmetic Frobenius in
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{r}/K)
and
a\in K^{\times}
. The invariant of
A
is defined by

\mathop{\rm inv}\nolimits(A):=\frac{v(a)}{r}\ \ {\rm mod}\ \mathbb{Z},

where
v
is the normalized valuation of
K
so that
v(\pi)=1
for any uniformizer
\pi
of
K
.

Let
\lambda=s/r\in\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}
be the unique unramified extension of
{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}
of degree
r
. There exist
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
ar-bs=1
. Put

\begin{split}K^{\lambda}&:=\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}\langle\xi\rangle:\ \xi^{r}=p,\ \xi\alpha=\sigma^{-b}(\alpha)\xi,\ \alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}\\
&=(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}/{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}},\sigma^{-b},p).\end{split}

Then
K^{\lambda}
is a central simple algebra over
{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}
of degree
r
. Putting
\xi^{\prime}:=\xi^{s}
, one has

(\xi^{\prime})^{r}=p^{s},\quad\xi^{\prime}\alpha=\xi^{s}\alpha=\sigma^{-bs}(\alpha)\xi^{s}=\sigma(\alpha)\xi^{\prime}.

and hence
K^{\lambda}=(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}/{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p},\sigma,p^{s})
. Thus,

\mathop{\rm inv}\nolimits(K^{\lambda})=\lambda\ \ {\rm mod}\ \mathbb{Z}.

Theorem 12
.

Let
\lambda=s/r
and assume
k\supseteq\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
. Then
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(N_{k}^{\lambda})\simeq(K^{\lambda})^{\mathrm{opp}}
and it is a central division algebra over
{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}
of degree
r
with invariant
-\lambda\mod\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Put
N:=L\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}}K^{\lambda}
, an
L
-vector space of dimension
r
with basis
1\otimes\xi^{i}
,
i=0,\dots,r-1
, and define
F(b\otimes\xi^{i}):=\sigma(b)\otimes\xi^{i+s}.
Then

(i)

As
F
-spaces, one has

(N,F)\simeq(L[\xi],F_{s})\simeq N^{\lambda}_{k}.

(ii)

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(N,F)
is given by right multiplication by
K^{\lambda}
.

Statement (i) is clear and we show (ii). Let
f\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(N,F)
. Set

N^{\diamond}:=\{x\in N:F^{r}(x)=p^{s}x\}=1\otimes K^{\lambda},

the subspace of constant sections of
p^{-s}F^{r}
. This is an
F
-space over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
. It is easy to check
f:N^{\diamond}\to N^{\diamond}
. As
N^{\diamond}=1\otimes K^{\lambda}
,
f
is uniquely determined by its restriction
f|_{K^{\lambda}}
. On
K^{\lambda}
,
F
acts as left multiplication by
\xi^{s}
because

F(b\xi^{i})=\sigma(b)\xi^{i+s}=\xi^{s}(b\xi^{i}),\quad b\in\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}.

Therefore,
f
commutes with left multiplication by
K^{\lambda}=\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}(\xi^{s})
and

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(N^{\diamond},F)=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{K^{\lambda}}(K^{\lambda})=\{\text{right multiplication by }K^{\lambda}\}.

This proves
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(N,F)\simeq(K^{\lambda})^{\mathrm{opp}}
.

Remark 13
.

(i)

N^{\lambda}_{k}
is effective if and only if
\lambda\geq 0
.

(ii)

N^{\lambda}_{k}
arises from a
p
-divisible group if and only if
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
.

(iii)

If
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
, then
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(N^{\lambda}_{k},N^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{k})=0
.

If
M\subset N^{\lambda}_{k}
is an
F
-lattice and let
\{p^{a_{1}},\dots,p^{a_{r}}\}
be the the elementary divisors of the
W
-sublattice

in
M
, then
a_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i}a_{i}=s
. This shows
\lambda=s/r\geq 0
. If
pM\subset FM\subset M
, then
0\leq a_{i}\leq 1
and
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. Conversely, if
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
, then
W[F,V]/(F^{r}-V^{s})
is a Dieudonné module in

. This shows (i) and (ii).

To see (iii), let
f\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(N^{\lambda}_{k},N^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{k})
. Since
N^{\lambda}_{k}=L\otimes N^{\lambda}
, it suffices to show

. Write
\lambda=s/r
and
\lambda^{\prime}=s^{\prime}/r^{\prime}
. For any

, put

. As
F^{rr^{\prime}}x=p^{sr^{\prime}}x
, one has
F^{rr^{\prime}}y=p^{sr^{\prime}}y
.
Write

with

. It follows from

and

that

p^{sr^{\prime}}a_{i}=\sigma^{rr^{\prime}}(a_{i})p^{rs^{\prime}}

for all

. As

, one has

for all

.

3.3.
The Manin-Dieudonné Theorem

Theorem 14
.

Assume that
k
is algebraically closed.

(1)
The category of
F
-spaces over
k
is semi-simple.

(2)
Every simple object is isomorphic to
N^{\lambda}_{k}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
.

(3)
Let
N
be an
F
-space over
k
. Then there are rational numbers
\lambda_{i}
and positive integers
m_{i}
for
i=1,\dots h
, and there is an isomorphism

N\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{h}(N^{\lambda_{i}}_{k})^{m_{i}}

of
F
-spaces. Moreover, the set
\{(\lambda_{i},m_{i})\}_{1\leq i\leq h}

is uniquely determined by
N
.

The proof consists of the following 5 lemmas.

Lemma 15
.

For any integers
\alpha,\beta
, the map

\varphi:L\to L,\ x\mapsto p^{\beta}x^{(\alpha)}-x

is surjective, where we write
x^{(n)}:=\sigma^{n}(x)
.

Proof.

We need to solve the equation (
*
)
p^{\beta}x^{(\alpha)}-x=b
for each
b\in L
.

Suppose
\beta>0
. Take

x=-(b+p^{\beta}b^{(\alpha)}+p^{2\beta}b^{(2\alpha)}+\cdots).

We find
x-p^{\beta}x^{(\alpha)}=-b
and thus equation
(*)
is solvable.

Suppose that
\beta<0
. Put
x^{\prime}:=p^{\beta}x^{(\alpha)}
. Then
x=p^{-\beta}x^{\prime(-\alpha)}
and

x^{\prime}-p^{-\beta}x^{\prime(-\alpha)}=p^{\beta}x^{(\alpha)}-x=b.

As
-\beta>0
, this equation is solvable for
x^{\prime}
and so as equation
(*)
.

Suppose that
\beta=0
. We solve the equation by successive approximation. We may assume
b\in W
. Suppose
x^{(\alpha)}-x-b=p^{m}a_{m}
for some
a_{m}\in W
. Put
x^{\prime}=x+p^{m}y
and we have

x^{\prime(\alpha)}-x^{\prime}-b=p^{m}(a_{m}+y^{(\alpha)}-y).

Since
k
is algebraically closed, there exists
y\in W
such that

a_{m}+y^{(\alpha)}-y\!\!\!\mod{pW}=\bar{a}_{m}+\bar{y}^{p^{\alpha}}-\bar{y}=0,

where
\bar{y}
denotes the image of
y
in
k
.
Replacing
x
by
x^{\prime}
, one has
x^{(\alpha)}-x-b=p^{m+1}a_{m+1}
for some
a_{m+1}\in W
. As
W
is complete, the equation
x^{(\alpha)}-x-b=0
is solvable.

Lemma 16
.

Any short exact sequence of
F
-spaces of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{k}^{\lambda}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
and
N^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{\lambda^{\prime}}(N_{k}^{\lambda^{\prime}})^{m_{\lambda^{\prime}}}
is a direct sum of
N_{k}^{\lambda^{\prime}}
, splits.

Proof.

Replacing the exact sequence by a suitable twisting, we may assume that
\lambda=s/r
and all
\lambda^{\prime}=s^{\prime}/r^{\prime}
are positive.

We first show that the map

(3.1)

F^{r}-p^{s}:N_{k}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\to N_{k}^{\lambda^{\prime}}

is surjective. Since
F
commutes with
p
, one has

F^{rr^{\prime}}-p^{sr^{\prime}}=(F^{r}-p^{s})(F^{r(r^{\prime}-1)}+F^{r(r^{\prime}-2)}p^{s}+\dots+p^{s(r^{\prime}-1)}).

If the map
F^{rr^{\prime}}-p^{sr^{\prime}}
is surjective, then so is the map
F^{r}-p^{s}
. Thus, it suffices to show that the map
F^{rr^{\prime}}-p^{sr^{\prime}}:N_{k}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\to N_{k}^{\lambda^{\prime}}
is surjective. Let
e_{1}^{\prime},\dots,e_{r^{\prime}}^{\prime}
be an
L
-basis for
N_{k}^{\lambda^{\prime}}
, and one has
F^{rr^{\prime}}e_{i}^{\prime}=p^{rs^{\prime}}e_{i}^{\prime}
. One computes

(F^{rr^{\prime}}-p^{sr^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}e^{\prime}_{i}\right)=\sum_{i}(a_{i}^{(rr^{\prime})}p^{rs^{\prime}}-p^{sr^{\prime}}a_{i})e_{i}^{\prime},

where
a_{i}\in L
. Set
x=p^{sr^{\prime}}a_{i}
, then we have

p^{rs^{\prime}-sr^{\prime}}x^{(rr^{\prime})}-x=a_{i}^{(rr^{\prime})}p^{rs^{\prime}}-p^{sr^{\prime}}a_{i}.

Since the equation
p^{rs^{\prime}-sr^{\prime}}x^{(rr^{\prime})}-x=b_{i}
is solvable for any
b_{i}\in L
by Lemma
15
, there exists
a_{i}
such that
a_{i}^{(rr^{\prime})}p^{rs^{\prime}}-p^{sr^{\prime}}a_{i}=b_{i}
. Thus, the map
F^{r}-p^{s}
is surjective.

To show the short exact sequence splits, we need to construct an element
x\in N
such that
(F^{r}-p^{s})(x)=0
. Then the map
N_{k}^{\lambda}\to N
,
e_{1}\mapsto x
gives a section of
\varphi
.

Choose an element
x\in N
such that
\varphi(x)=e_{1}
. Since
(F^{r}-p^{s})(e_{1})=0
, one has
\varphi((F^{r}-p^{s})(x))=0
and
(F^{r}-p^{s})(x)\in N^{\prime}
. As the map
F^{r}-p^{s}
in (
3.1
) is surjective, there exists an element
x^{\prime}\in N^{\prime}
such that
(F^{r}-p^{s})(x^{\prime})=(F^{r}-p^{s})(x)
. Replacing
x
by
x-x^{\prime}
, one has
(F^{r}-p^{s})(x)=0
. This completes the proof of the lemma.

Let
R:=L[F]
denote the non-commutative polynomial ring over
L
with relation
Fa=\sigma(a)F
for
a\in L
, and
W[F]
be its subring with coefficients in
W
. Let
W[p^{1/r}][F]
, for

, denote the non-commutative polynomial ring over

with relations
Fa=\sigma(a)F
for
a\in W
and
Fp^{1/r}=p^{1/r}F
.

Lemma 17
.

Let
F^{n}+a_{1}F^{n-1}+\dots+a_{n}\in W[F]
with
n\geq 1
. Then there exist co-prime integers
r\geq 1
and
s
, and elements
b_{0},\dots,b_{n-1},u\in W[p^{1/r}]
with

invertible such that

(3.2)

F^{n}+a_{1}F^{n-1}+\dots+a_{n}=(b_{0}F^{n-1}+\dots+b_{n-1})(F-p^{s/r})u

in

Proof.

Let
\lambda:=\min\{\frac{v(a_{1})}{1},\dots\frac{v(a_{n})}{n}\}
. Then

for all integers
1\leq i\leq n
, and
i\cdot\lambda=v(a_{i})
for some
i
. Write
\lambda=s/r
and
a_{i}=p^{is/r}\alpha_{i}
for each

, then
\alpha_{i}\in W[p^{1/r}]
and
\alpha_{i}
is a unit for some

b_{i}=p^{is/r}\beta_{i}
,

, and

. Multiplying

by the left hand side of (

(F^{n}+a_{1}F^{n-1}+\dots+a_{n})v=v^{(n)}F^{n}+v^{(n-1)}a_{1}F^{n-1}+\dots+va_{n}.

The corresponding right hand side is

b_{0}F^{n}+(b_{1}-p^{s/r}b_{0})F^{n-1}+(b_{2}-p^{s/r}b_{1})F^{n-2}+\dots+(b_{n-1}-p^{s/r}b_{n-2})F-b_{n-1}p^{s/r}.

Comparing the coefficients, one obtains

or equivalently,

(3.3)

\begin{split}v^{(n)}&=\beta_{0},\\
\alpha_{1}v^{(n-1)}&=\beta_{1}-\beta_{0},\\
&\vdots\\
\alpha_{n-1}v^{(1)}&=\beta_{n-1}-\beta_{n-2}\\
\alpha_{n}v&=-\beta_{n-1}.\end{split}

Thus, Equations (

) are solvable if and only there exists a unit

such that

(3.4)

P(v):=v^{(n)}+\alpha_{1}v^{(n-1)}+\dots+\alpha_{n-1}v^{(1)}+\alpha_{n}v=0.

We solve Equation (

) by successive approximation. Clearly, the equation

P(\bar{v})=\bar{v}^{p^{n}}+\bar{\alpha}_{1}\bar{v}^{p^{n-1}}+\dots+\bar{\alpha}_{n}\bar{v}=0

has a nonzero solution
\bar{v}
in

for some

Suppose we have

Put

or equivalently to solve the equation

in

Lemma 18
.

Let

be a nonzero

\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
and a nonzero map

.

Proof.

Replacing

is a simple

for a monic polynomial

. Replacing

by a suitable

for all

. By Lemma

, we have

for co-prime integers

,

, a polynomial

and an invertible element

Put

Observe that

where the isomorphism in the second row is the map

and the last map is the natural projection. Then the composition gives a nonzero map

as

M(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\varphi_{i})\simeq M_{0}/M_{i}
, we have
V(M_{0}/M_{i})=V(M(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\varphi_{i}))=V(M(\alpha_{p}))=0
. Since
\varphi_{i}
are minimal isogenies, any isomorphism
M_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}M_{2}
lifts to a unique automorphism of
M_{0}
.
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(M_{0})
acts the set of lines of
M_{0}/VM_{0}
through the quotient
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(M_{0})\twoheadrightarrow\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})
, and the action of
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(M_{0})
factors through the action of
\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(k)
by linear fractional transformations.
Since
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}(t_{1})=\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}(t_{2})
, we have
t_{2}=(at_{1}+b)/(ct_{1}+d)
, for some
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})
and hence
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{\rm DM}(M_{0})
acts transitively on
\mathbb{F}_{p^{4}}\smallsetminus\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. This shows
M_{1}\simeq M_{2}
and hence the proposition.

We see from Lemma
27
that the
p
-divisible group of a supersingular abelian variety
X
characterizes
X
up to finitely many possibilities. Moreover, such a finite set is completely determined by
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)\otimes{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
. Thus, the local endomorphism ring
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)\otimes{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
serves as a good and convenient invariant for studying supersingular abelian varieties as well as for exploring the geometry of the moduli space
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
of
g
-dimensional principally polarized abelian varieties.
In some sense, Theorems
22
and
23
are just instances of such a characterization in terms of geometry.
For
g=3
, the structure of
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(X)\otimes{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}
has been explored in
[
19
]
. The authors also describe how this invariant varies in the moduli space
{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
, or more precisely in a model irreducible component
{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}_{3,\eta}
of
3
-dimensional rigid polarized flag type quotients (PFTQ’s). Based on
[
19
]
, one can further compute the size of
\Lambda_{X}
for each
(X,\lambda)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
using the same method of Lemma
27
.

In
[
16
]
the authors determine precisely when a principally polarized abelian variety is determined by its
p
-divisible group. Namely, they prove:

Theorem 32

(
[
16
, Corollary B]
)
.

A principally polarized abelian variety
(X,\lambda)
over
k
is uniquely determined by its polarized
p
-divisible group if and only if
X
is supersingular and one of the following three cases holds:

(i)

g=1
and
p\in\{2,3,5,7,13\}
;

(ii)

g=2
and
p=2,3
;

(iii)

g=3
,
p=2
and
a(X)\geq 2
.

By Lemma
27
, one obtains the following characterization of an abelian variety by its
p
-divisible group in terms of arithmetic.

Corollary 33
.

An abelian variety
X
of dimension
>1
over
k
is determined by its
p
-divisible group if and only if
X
is supersingular and
\mathop{\rm Nr}\nolimits(O_{p}^{\times})={{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}^{\times}
.

It is desirable to reveal this arithmetic condition in term of geometry. For example, what is the locus in a parameter space
{\mathcal{X}}\to S
for abelian varieties defined by the condition
\mathop{\rm Nr}\nolimits(O_{p}^{\times})={{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}^{\times}
? For
g=2
, we have the following result.

Proposition 34
.

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}
be the moduli space of isogenies
\varphi:E_{0}^{2}\otimes k\to X
of degree
p
, and fix an identification
{\mathcal{Z}}={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.
Let
{\mathcal{Y}}\subseteq{\mathcal{Z}}
denote the locus consisting of
\varphi
satisfying
\mathop{\rm Nr}\nolimits(O_{p}^{\times})={{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}^{\times}
. Then

{\mathcal{Y}}=\begin{cases}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}&\text{if $p=2$;}\\
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{4}})&\text{if $p>2$.}\end{cases}

By Proposition
31
the locus
{\mathcal{Y}}
consists of
\varphi
where the abelian surface
X
is superspecial (
t\in{\mathbb{P}}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})
), or in Case (II), or in Case (I) and
p=2
. A priori,
{\mathcal{Y}}
is a subvariety defined over
k
but it is actually defined over
{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
as it is stable under the
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}/{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})
-action.

Oort’s conjecture
[
12
, Question 4]
states that when
g\geq 2
, every geometric generic member in
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
has automorphism group
\{\pm 1\}
.
For
g=2
and
p>2
, Oort’s conjecture has been proved by Ibukiyama
[
15
]
, and by Karemaker and Pries
[
18
]
independently, with a counterexample in
p=2
. Oort’s conjecture for
g=3
and
p>2
has been proved by Karemaker, Yobuko and the author
[
19
]
, with again a counterexample in
p=2
. In a recent preprint
[
20
]
Karemaker and the author prove the following result.

Theorem 35
.

Oort’s conjecture holds true for the case where either
g=4
, or
g
is even and
p\geq 5
.

In a recent preprint
[
11
]
, Dragutinović obtains a different proof of Oort’s conjecture when
g=4
and
p>2
, as well as a new proof when
g=3
and
p>2
, using the moduli space of curves in both cases.
