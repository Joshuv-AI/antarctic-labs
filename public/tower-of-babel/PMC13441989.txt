Ligand-enabled distal desaturative lactonization of aliphatic acids
Authors: Tanay Pal, Md Saimuddin Sk, Yazhinimuthu C M, Somnath Kar, Animesh Ghosh, Debabrata Maiti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Transition-metal catalysis is a fundamental strategy for transforming inert C−H bonds into valuable functional motifs1,2. However, achieving regioselective activation of remote C−H bonds remains challenging, particularly in unbiased hydrocarbon frameworks2,3. In this context, distal C(sp3)−H bonds are especially difficult to functionalize, as conformational flexibility favours proximal C−H activation3,4. In this study, we demonstrate a ligand-enabled strategy using designed O-allyl amido ester ligands for palladium-catalysed activation of γ-methylene and methine C−H sites in unbiased aliphatic carboxylic acids, derived from readily available fatty and cyclic acid feedstocks. This protocol enables direct transformation of aliphatic carboxylic acid substrates into distal desaturated γ-lactones and double-dehydrogenated γ-spirolactones. Mechanistic studies are consistent with a pathway involving Pd(II)-mediated γ-C(sp3)−H activation, followed by dehydrogenation and intramolecular cyclization. These lactones, with an unsaturated arm, serve as key intermediates for the formation of complex natural products and pharmaceuticals. For instance, muricatacin (from soursop/Laxman phal) and its analogue were rapidly assembled in three steps from margaric acid using this strategy and evaluated for anticancer activity, thereby demonstrating the potential of our approach for providing a rapid access to biologically relevant frameworks for traditional medicine. The introduced distal desaturation further opens up new avenues for remote functionalization, streamlining access to diverse bioactive molecules with improved step and atom economy.

Main

Fatty acids constitute an important class of aliphatic carboxylic acids, in particular, long-chain saturated fatty acids are prevalent in nature and widely used in pharmaceuticals, polymers, lubricants and surfactants5. Their structural simplicity, commercial availability and natural abundance make them attractive starting materials for sustainable synthesis. Direct and selective functionalization of these readily available, simple hydrocarbon frameworks could provide streamlined access to structurally complex molecules. Among these structurally valuable motifs, γ-lactones, including dehydrogenated butyrolactones and spirolactones, are widespread in bioactive natural products, fragrances and therapeutic agents4,6–11. The introduction of remote unsaturation into aliphatic frameworks represents a valuable transformation that can further promote late-stage diversification12,13. However, executing these transformations in linear-chain carboxylic acids remains challenging because these substrates possess electronically and sterically unbiased sp3 C−H bonds with similar reactivity profiles, limiting catalyst-controlled differentiation and site-selective activation. Strategies that can promote catalyst-controlled regioselective C–H activation11, therefore, remain highly desirable in these systems. Although elegant metal-mediated desaturation strategies at proximal positions have been explored, these transformations are largely directed by electronically or conformationally activated sites near polar functionalities14–21. On a complementary spectrum, distal unsaturation in long-chain aliphatic carboxylic acids remains less developed, particularly at δ or ε positions owing to the absence of any substrate bias. Existing examples often rely on pre-installed olefins or structural constraints such as the Thorpe–Ingold effect, generally requiring tedious multistep substrate preparation20–28. Based on our previous findings in cyclic acid systems25, we believed that γ-C−H activation of linear-chain carboxylic acids could generate a γ,δ-unsaturated acid intermediate that undergoes intramolecular oxypalladation and π-migration to furnish a δ,ε-unsaturated γ-lactone, thereby combining γ-lactonization and distal desaturation in a single sequence.

Implementation of this pathway requires overcoming of several intrinsic challenges associated with γ-C−H activation in unbiased systems. Most notably, β-C−H bonds are generally more accessible for functionalization because their activation involves the formation of kinetically favoured five-membered palladacycles, consequently leading to an elevated bar of difficulty in γ-methylene activation, which proceeds through six-membered metallacycle formation20,21 (Fig. 1a). Moreover, several additional factors further complicate γ-activation in unbiased acids. First, the absence of Thorpe–Ingold constraints disfavours γ-activation25–32. Second, flexible alkyl chains hinder the formation of defined transition states. Another factor is weak carboxylate coordination, which can lead to poor metal binding or side reactions. Compared with γ-methylene sites, γ-methine C−H activation poses additional challenges arising from increased steric hindrance, lower C−H acidity and less favourable metal–carbon bond formation. Intrigued by these limitations, we questioned whether a suitably designed ligand framework could enable catalyst-controlled remote site selectivity in flexible aliphatic systems. Guided by these considerations, we sought to develop ligand frameworks that can enable remote γ-C−H activation in saturated fatty acids to promote distal desaturation and lactonization. We prepared a series of N,O-protected amino acid (NOPA) ligands, among which an O-allyl amido ester (OAAE) variant bearing an amidate donor, a π-functional side chain and a sterically encumbered iso-butyl substituent emerged as particularly effective (Fig. 1b). This architecture was visualized to provide a flexible coordination environment that can promote site-selective γ-methylene and γ-methine activation in the absence of strong intrinsic substrate bias. Using this strategy, we have developed a ligand-enabled palladium-catalysed dehydrogenative lactonization of unbiased aliphatic carboxylic acids to access δ,ε-desaturated γ-lactones. This methodology provides efficient access to diverse distal desaturated lactone frameworks, including dehydrogenated butyrolactones and spirolactones, which can be further transformed into bioactive scaffolds such as muricatacin analogues and L-factor, highlighting the utility of this strategy for C−H activation and valorization of renewable feedstocks.

Optimization of the reaction conditions

We proposed that lactonization proceeds through an initial γ-C(sp3)−H activation followed by sequential dehydrogenation and cyclization steps, analogous to previously reported Pd-catalysed unsaturated lactonization25. Realizing this sequence at γ-methylene and γ-methine positions in unbiased aliphatic acids demands ligand frameworks capable of promoting remote C−H activation, stabilizing key Pd-intermediates, and suppressing competitive pathways (Fig. 2). Initial optimization with undecylic acid using Pd(OAc)2 (10 mol%), Na2HPO4 (2 equiv.), Ag2CO3 (2 equiv.) and a range of pyridone-based and mono-protected amino acid (MPAA) ligands in hexafluoroisopropanol (HFIP) at 110 °C afforded low yields (<4%) (Supplementary Information section 3 and Supplementary Table 1). Quinoline–pyridone ligands promoted α,β-dehydrogenation of the corresponding acids (Supplementary Information section 10b), indicating that these ligand classes are not suited for the desired transformation. We, therefore, sought to design ligand frameworks that can electronically influence multiple elementary steps of the Pd catalytic cycle, including C−H activation, β-hydride elimination and reductive elimination, while also stabilizing key organopalladium intermediates. Guided by previous studies demonstrating that π-coordinating motifs can modulate transition-metal reactivity through electronic perturbation of the metal centre and transient ligand–metal interactions1,33–35, we proposed that incorporation of π-coordinating side handles into ligand scaffolds could tune both the coordination environment and electronic properties of Pd. To test this hypothesis, we prepared a series of NOPA ligands by incorporating side chains with varying π-character into MPAA scaffolds, including alkyl, aryl, benzyl, alkynyl and allyl substituents (Supplementary Information section 3 and Supplementary Table 1), to systematically evaluate the influence of side-chain structure on catalytic performance. These ligands were selected to provide varying combinations of π-character, steric environment and conformational flexibility. Allyl substituents were expected to provide a conformationally flexible and accessible π-functional framework with moderate steric demand, whereas aryl groups provide comparatively rigid and sterically demanding π-rings. Benzyl substituents retain aromatic π-character while offering greater spatial adaptability through a flexible methylene linker, and alkynes contribute a linear π-system with reduced conformational flexibility. By contrast, alkyl substituents lack π-functionality altogether.

Among the ligands examined, allyl-containing systems exhibited the most pronounced enhancement in reactivity. Introduction of an allyl substituent into an N-acetyl-valine (L10) increased the yield sevenfold (29%), and subsequent optimization identified N-acetyl-leucine allyl ester (L14) as optimal ligand, affording the product in 42% yield (Fig. 2a). We designate this allyl-containing NOPA motif as an OAAE ligand. By contrast, benzyl-, aryl- and alkynyl-substituted analogues (L15–L17) delivered diminished yields, indicating that increased rigidity or altered π-distribution is not beneficial in this transformation. Ligands lacking π-functional side chains were largely ineffective, showing a strong dependence of reactivity on side-chain π-functionality. Further modification of the allyl side arm showed that perturbation of its spatial arrangement or electronic environment by substitution at either the terminal olefin or adjacent methylene position (L18–L20) or by relocation to a homoallylic framework (L21–L23) led to a marked decrease in reactivity. Altogether, these structure–activity relationships highlight the importance of both the identity and spatial disposition of the allyl-derived side handle for catalytic performance.

To probe the origin of the allyl effect, the interaction between L14 and Pd(OAc)2 was examined by 1H NMR spectroscopy in the presence of base at different temperatures. Base addition attenuated and perturbed the amide NH signal, consistent with amidate involvement during interaction with Pd species. Small but reproducible upfield shifts were observed for the allylic −OCH2− and terminal olefinic protons (Δδ ≈ 0.03–0.07 ppm), whereas the internal olefinic proton remained largely unaffected (Supplementary Information section 10a). Time-dependent spectral changes indicate interconverting species converging towards a dominant equilibrium state with minor coexisting components. Subtle differences between the terminal and internal olefinic resonances are consistent with non-uniform perturbation33 of the allyl π-system, and may reflect transient, spatially differentiated interactions between the allyl moiety and the Pd centre rather than a discrete coordination mode. In line with these observations, the ligand–Pd ensemble may adopt a flexible ‘puckered’ or tub-shaped conformation in solution (Fig. 1b), in which ligand flexibility brings the π-functional side chain into proximity with the Pd centre without implying a single well-defined static structure. Together with precedent in the literature for metal–alkene interactions and alkene-mediated modulation of Pd reactivity1,33–35, these observations suggest that the allyl-derived side chain may influence the electronic environment of Pd(II), potentially affecting its electrophilic character. Beyond these putative ligand–metal interactions, the amide unit likely facilitates base-assisted concerted metalation–deprotonation, whereas the sterically encumbered isobutyl side chain seems important for the observed reactivity. Although the present data support a role for the allyl-derived side chain in modulating catalyst behaviour, the precise nature of the underlying ligand–Pd interactions remains under investigation. Collectively, these observations suggest that the OAAE ligand creates a catalytic environment conducive to the proposed C−H activation and downstream reaction sequence.

Mechanistic studies were then conducted to validate the plausible dehydro-lactonization sequence. The transformation is proposed to proceed by initial γ-C(sp3)−H activation followed by β-hydride elimination to generate a γ,δ-unsaturated acid intermediate (Int-C), which can subsequently undergo intramolecular oxypalladation and a second β-hydride elimination to furnish the distal desaturated γ-lactone (Fig. 2b). To evaluate the competence of this intermediate, control experiments were performed (Supplementary Information section 10). The β,γ-unsaturated acid failed to furnish the desired product and instead produced α,β-unsaturated γ-lactone, thereby excluding β-activation pathways, whereas the γ,δ-unsaturated acid furnished the desired γ-lactone in near-quantitative yield, strongly supporting its role as a catalytically relevant intermediate (Supplementary Information section 10).

These mechanistic insights, together with the observed ligand structure–activity relationships, collectively defined the behaviour of L14, enabling distal selectivity and improved formation of the desaturated γ-lactone under the optimized conditions. The pronounced ligand dependence observed here highlights the central role of ligand architecture in governing catalytic outcome. Under optimized conditions of Pd(OAc)2 (10 mol%), L14 (30 mol%), Ag2CO3 (2.5 equiv.), Na2HPO4 (3 equiv.) and NaOCOCF3 (0.5 equiv.) in HFIP at 120 °C for 36 h, the desired lactone (1) was obtained in 60% isolated yield.

Substrate scope

Having established the optimized OAAE-enabled reaction conditions, we next examined a range of naturally occurring fatty acids spanning the C9–C22 chain-length range, which furnished δ,ε-unsaturated γ-lactones (1–7) by regioselective γ-C(sp3)−H activation and dehydro-lactonization in moderate to good yields (Fig. 3a). Although substrates across the entire chain-length range were compatible, longer-chain fatty acids generally afforded somewhat reduced yields, probably reflecting solubility limitations in HFIP. Additional fatty acids of varying chain lengths exhibited similar reactivity (Supplementary Information and Supplementary Fig. 1). We next examined substrates bearing functional groups, conformational bias and aryl substitution (Fig. 3b). Ester- and maleimide-containing acids were well tolerated, affording products (8 and 9) with good chemoselectivity. β-Substituted acids furnished γ-lactones 10 and 11 by preferential γ-methylene activation over competitive methyl sites (10), including selective formation of an internal olefin in 11, consistent with thermodynamic alkene stabilization. An aryl-containing substrate underwent selective transformation without perturbation of aryl C−H bonds affording lactone (12). Notably, this dehydrogenated lactone has been reported to exhibit cytotoxic activity against leukemic T-cell lines36. Cyclohexylbutanoic acid afforded desaturated γ-lactone (13), a potential intermediate for hexahydronaphthalene carboxylate synthesis37. Complex terpenoid-derived substrates prepared from α-ionone and farnesol also proved compatible, affording γ-lactones 14 and 15 bearing distal olefins with varying degrees of substitution, demonstrating compatibility of the protocol with structurally elaborate frameworks.

We next investigated substrates containing competing acyclic γ- and cyclic γ′-methylene sites within cyclohexylacetic acid frameworks (Fig. 3c). In contrast to previously reported MPAA-based systems25, the present OAAE ligand framework preferentially activated alternative acyclic long-chain γ-methylene positions over cyclic methylene sites, affording complementary distal desaturated spirolactone products. This reversal of the typical selectivity pattern25 highlights the role of ligand architecture in controlling regioselective C(sp3)−H activation when multiple methylene sites compete. Cyclohexane substrates bearing ring substituents were well tolerated (16–20), whereas the unsubstituted analogue produced a mixture of spirocyclic and bicyclic lactones (21), indicating diminished selectivity in the absence of ring substitution. Expansion to larger cyclic frameworks afforded [7,5]-, [15,5]-spirolactones (22 and 23), respectively. Additional substrates exhibited similar reactivity and selectivity trends (Supplementary Information and Supplementary Fig. 1). Consistent with this distinct selectivity profile relative to previous MPAA systems25, no H2 evolution was detected under the reaction conditions, supporting a Pd(II)/Pd(0) manifold in which Ag(I) mediates reoxidation of Pd(0) to Pd(II) during the catalytic cycle. Deuterium-labelling experiments using d2-HFIP showed no deuterium incorporation at the γ-position, indicating that γ-C−H activation is irreversible under this protocol and probably contributes to observed regioselectivity. These observations further distinguish the present catalytic system from the previously reported MPAA system25 (Supplementary Information section 10).

Finally, we introduced greater steric demand by replacing one methylene hydrogen with an alkyl substituent, thereby generating more challenging γ-methine C−H sites, which are sterically inaccessible, least acidic and kinetically disfavoured relative to methylene and methyl C−H bonds (Fig. 3d). An acyclic carboxylic acid bearing a tertiary γ-methine C−H bond proved to be a competent substrate under the optimized conditions, affording mixtures of mono- and di-dehydrogenated lactones (24). We next investigated cyclic substrates of greater steric complexity. Cyclohexyl propanoic acid derivatives furnished γ-methine activation followed by tandem double dehydrogenation and lactonization to form spirocyclic unsaturated lactones (25–28). Substrates bearing substituents at different ring positions, including electron-donating and electron-withdrawing groups, were generally well tolerated. Variations in substitution pattern influenced regioselectivity, probably reflecting differences in preferred β-hydride elimination pathways during the dehydro-lactonization sequence. Benzylic methine substrates required modified conditions to selectively furnish mono-dehydrogenated lactone (29). Moreover, a substrate bearing a fully substituted β-site, leaving only a γ-methine C−H bond available for activation, afforded the mono-dehydrogenated lactone (30), further supporting γ-methine C−H activation and indicating that secondary dehydrogenation requires an accessible β-hydrogen (Supplementary Information section 10). Across the substrate scope, incomplete conversion and recovery of starting material suggest partial catalyst deactivation (Supplementary Information section 9). Nevertheless, these results demonstrate that the Pd-OAAE system can engage challenging γ-methine C−H bonds and enable access to diverse spirocyclic and unsaturated γ-lactone frameworks.

Applications

To demonstrate the synthetic utility of this methodology, we applied it to the synthesis of pharmaceutically and industrially relevant γ-lactone scaffolds (Fig. 4), typically prepared through multistep routes4,6–8. Soursop (Laxman phal)-derived muricatacin (31), a δ-hydroxy-γ-lactone motif found in annonaceous acetogenins with reported antimalarial, cytotoxic and pesticidal activities, has inspired numerous elegant syntheses, including asymmetric approaches38,39. In this context, the present strategy enables a concise three-step synthesis from readily available margaric acid, albeit as racemate (Fig. 4a), providing a complementary entry to this framework. The generality of this approach was further demonstrated through the synthesis of diverse γ-lactone derivatives. L-factor (32), an autoregulator38, was accessed in three steps from capric acid. Cognac lactone (33), widely used in flavour and fragrance applications40, and dodecalactone (34), a bioactive compound in insect secretions, were each obtained from simple fatty acids in fewer steps compared with reported routes40,41. The method was also applied to the preparation of dl-3-n-butylphthalide (35), a clinically used drug for ischaemic stroke42, synthesized in two steps from ortho-alkylated benzoic acids using our protocol. This contrasts with the traditional four-step synthesis from phthalic anhydride43. γ-ethyl-γ-phenyl-butyrolactone (36), a scaffold of pharmacological interest44,45, was synthesized through step-economic γ-C−H activation. Moreover, γ-decalactone (37), an industrially important flavour compound46, was prepared under standard conditions. Further transformations highlight the versatility of products: base-mediated γ-lactone ring opening afforded γ-hydroxylated δ,ε-unsaturated carboxylates (38), whereas conversion to hydroxy-oxolane (39) provides access to intermediates relevant to macrolide synthesis47. Finally, late-stage functionalization of mycophenolic acid formed the corresponding distal desaturated lactone (40), illustrating compatibility with structurally complex molecules.

Beyond these applications, δ,ε-desaturated γ-lactones obtained through this protocol serve as versatile platforms for further diversification (Extended Data Fig. 1a). The distal olefin readily undergoes epoxidation (41 and 44) and oxidative dihydroxylation (42 and 45), while the resulting epoxides can be opened by nucleophiles to generate functionalized derivatives (43, 46 and 47). Reductive manipulations further expand the accessible chemical space, enabling the formation of 1,4-diols (48), hydroxylated tetrahydrofuran derivatives (49) or long-chain alcohols (50). Additional transformations, including Grignard addition (51) and electrophilic bromination (52), further underscore the synthetic flexibility of these desaturated γ-lactones (Supplementary Information section 8).

In silico studies

Docking studies identified several analogues with improved binding affinities relative to muricatacin (Supplementary Information section 11). For COX-2, compounds 46 and 42 exhibited stronger binding (−9.3 and −8.8 kcal mol−1) than muricatacin (−7.2 kcal mol−1), with compound 46 forming hydrogen bonds with Arg-106 and Tyr-341 (Extended Data Fig. 1b). Molecular dynamics simulations confirmed stable compound–protein complexes (Extended Data Fig. 1c). For caspase-3, compounds 42 and 46 also exhibited enhanced binding compared with muricatacin. Based on these results, selected analogues were advanced for further biological evaluation (Supplementary Information section 11).

MTT assay

Compounds 42, 43, 45 and 46, together with muricatacin (31), were evaluated against HT-29 and HCT-116 colorectal carcinoma cells using an MTT assay (Supplementary Information section 11). Compounds 42 and 43 exhibited dose-dependent growth inhibition, whereas compounds 45 and 46 showed limited activity (Extended Data Fig. 1d,e). Compound 43 exhibited potent cytotoxicity, with IC50 values of 55.11 μM (HT-29) and 60.83 μM (HCT-116), comparable to muricatacin (53.14 μM and 53.60 μM, respectively), whereas compound 42 was less active. Notably, compound 43 was synthesized in only three steps from margaric acid, highlighting the efficiency of the developed strategy for rapid analogue generation.

Conclusion

We have developed a general strategy for the synthesis of three distinct classes of distal desaturated γ-lactones from readily available aliphatic carboxylic acids, including butyrolactones and spiro-lactones. These results address the longstanding challenge of regioselective γ-C−H activation in unbiased aliphatic carboxylic acids. The OAAE ligand framework is proposed to modulate the Pd coordination environment, enabling selective activation of remote methylene and methine sites. The versatility of this ligand system is further demonstrated by complementary γ-methylene selectivity in cyclic acetic acids. Mechanistic studies, supported by control experiments and deuterium-labelling investigations, indicate irreversible γ-C(sp3)−H activation. The synthetic utility of the method is illustrated through concise access to bioactive molecules, including EFBL, cognac lactone, dodecalactone, peach lactone, dl-NBP, muricatacin and L-factor. Furthermore, a muricatacin analogue prepared in three steps exhibited anticancer activity comparable to that of muricatacin. Overall, this work expands the scope and wider application of aliphatic C(sp3)−H activation and highlights its potential for the valorization of renewable feedstocks in drug discovery and natural product synthesis.
