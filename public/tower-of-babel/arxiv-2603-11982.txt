Approximate Reduced Lindblad Dynamics via Algebraic and Adiabatic Methods
Tommaso Grigoletto 1, Alain Sarlette 2,3, Francesco Ticozzi 1,4,5, Lorenza Viola 4

Abstract
\justify We present an algebraic framework for approximate model reduction of Markovian open quantum dynamics that guarantees complete positivity and trace preservation by construction. First, we show that projecting a Lindblad generator on its center manifold – the space spanned by eigenoperators with purely imaginary eigenvalue – yields an asymptotically exact reduced quantum dynamical semigroup whose dynamics is unitary, with exponentially decaying transient error controlled by the generator’s spectral gap. Second, for analytic perturbations of a Lindblad generator with a tractable center manifold, we propose a perturbative reduction that keeps the reduced space fixed at the unperturbed center manifold. The resulting generator is shown to remain a valid Lindbladian for arbitrary perturbation strengths, and explicit finite-time error bounds, that quantify leakage from the unperturbed center sector, are provided. We further clarify the connection to adiabatic elimination methods, by both showing how the algebraic reduction can be directly related to a first-order adiabatic-elimination and by providing sufficient conditions under which the latter method can be applied while preserving complete positivity. We showcase the usefulness of our techniques in dissipative many-body quantum systems exhibiting non-stationary long-time dynamics.



keywords
Open quantum systems; Markovian dynamics; model reduction; adiabatic elimination

1
Introduction

Markovian models for open quantum systems provide a paradigmatic setting for describing irreversible continuous-time quantum dynamics
[
3
]
. As such, they are widely used to account for the influence of a memory-less environment in a variety of both natural and engineered physical platforms of relevance to quantum science and technology. Markovian (semigroup) master equations, governed by a generator in canonical Lindblad form (also referred to as a
Lindbladian
henceforth), have long been used to describe noise effects in fields ranging from quantum optics
[
17
]
to atomic and condensed-matter physics
[
60
,
51
]
, as well as to design open dynamics achieving non-unitary quantum-control tasks such as autonomous state stabilization
[
53
,
54
,
59
,
32
]
.

However, even while not explicitly accounting for the environmental degrees of freedom, the resulting dynamical model may still be too large to simulate, or otherwise too complex to interpret and use for the purpose at hand. Notably, the rise of new research areas including open quantum many-body systems and driven-dissipative quantum matter
[
22
,
50
]
, along with the possibility to push experimental investigations in increasingly more powerful quantum simulators
[
4
]
, heighten the need for smaller-dimensional, “reduced” descriptions that retain a valid quantum nature and reliably capture the dynamics of degrees of freedom of interest, without carrying the full complexity. A compelling scenario of interest arises, in particular, in regimes where dissipation does not merely relax the system to a steady state, but to a richer asymptotic structure known as the
center manifold
and tied to the occurrence of purely imaginary eigenvalues of the Lindblad generator
[
49
]
.
In such a case,
non-stationary long-time dynamics
and “oscillating coherences” may ensue despite the underlying dynamical generator being time-invariant
[
20
,
15
,
68
]
–
allowing for time-translation symmetry breaking and the emergence of so-called boundary time crystals
[
34
,
63
]
.

From a system-theoretic standpoint, the challenge of obtaining dynamical models of lower complexity falls under the broad framework of
model reduction
(MR) techniques
[
5
]
.
Exact
MR, in particular, aims to construct models that reproduce the evolution of selected quantities of interest without error, for arbitrary times. A systematic approach to achieve exact MR for quantum Markovian dynamics has been recently developed in a series of papers
[
30
,
28
,
29
,
27
]
,
by combining Krylov operator spaces and algebraic methods. Notably, for continuous-time dynamics, the exact reduced model (if one exists) can be guaranteed to
also
be in Lindblad form. In general, however, exact reproduction of the target evolution may be too strong a requirement, with the above operator-algebraic approach resulting in no reduction – especially for models that are already approximate as they are inferred from noisy tomographic data or imperfect system characterization
[
33
,
19
]
.
Approximate
MR is then often pursued by leveraging time-scale separation ideas, with
adiabatic elimination
(AE) being a prominent example for obtaining a linear evolution equation as a series expansion on a low-dimensional operator subspace
[
7
,
24
]
. Yet, a recurring problem therein is that approximate reductions may cease to represent a valid Markovian quantum dynamics
[
45
,
48
,
57
]
. This may happen in two conceptually distinct ways. First, the reduced evolution may fail to be described by a valid, completely-positive and trace-preserving (CPTP) super-operator. Second, even when a reduced Lindblad master equation is formally established, and the CPTP property is thus ensured, the construction may implicitly replace density operators by elements of an operator subspace that does not carry an algebraic structure – preventing their interpretation as valid quantum states. For both quantum-information and device-level interpretations, this distinction matters: A useful reduced model should also point to a physically admissible interpretation of
what the reduced system is
. In finite dimension, this is naturally expressed by requiring the reduced state space to arise from an associative
∗
-algebra (a direct sum of full matrix algebras), so that the reduced states are valid density operators and their dynamics acts on an identifiable effective quantum system
[
1
]
.

In this paper, we show that the algebraic perspective of exact MR can be used to obtain approximate MR procedures that remain physically consistent by construction, and have a direct connection with AE. More specifically, our main contribution is twofold:

(i) We develop an
asymptotically exact
reduction based on the center (peripheral) manifold of a Lindbladian which, as noted above, governs the long-time behavior of the dynamics. Since such a set has the structure of a subalgebra, one can obtain a reduced Markovian dynamics, with the additional property that the reduced long-time evolution is unitary. The associated reduced description is asymptotically exact, in the sense that any initial error outside the center manifold decays exponentially with a rate controlled by the spectral gap of the Lindblad generator, and is the minimal one with such property.

(ii) We consider the case where the evolution of interest may not have a center manifold suitable for the desired reduction, but can be seen as an (analytic) perturbation of a Lindblad generator that does. In this scenario, we propose to construct a

CPTP-preserving
perturbative reduction. The key choice is to keep the reduction maps fixed at the unperturbed value, so that the reduced state space remains an algebra even when the underlying generator is perturbed. The resulting reduced generator is again Lindbladian for all perturbation strengths. As a tradeoff, the reduced system only approximately matches the true system dynamics, and we provide explicit finite-time error bounds that quantify the leakage from the unperturbed center sector.

In establishing the above results, we also gain important insight into the relationships and differences between algebraic and AE approaches. On the one hand, one may observe that, under perturbations, purely imaginary spectral components typically acquire small negative real parts, producing a slow subspace that is invariant, but need no longer carry a
∗
-algebra structure. In that sense, our analysis makes it clear how the AE methods tracking of the “slow” space may have better long-time accuracy, at the cost of losing complete positivity or the direct interpretation of the reduced variables as a quantum subsystem. On the other hand, we also elucidate how, at first order, AE possesses a genuine gauge freedom: different choices lead to different effective generators on the reduced coordinates, with our perturbative CPTP-preserving reduction coinciding with first-order AE for a
specific
gauge choice. In doing so, we uncover an important (and hitherto unappreciated) structural role for such a gauge freedom, while additionally providing general conditions under which the first-order AE generator can be chosen to be of Lindblad form. Numerically, we demonstrate how arbitrary, randomized gauge choices perform poorly, whereas enforcing complete positivity improves stability and long-time performance. Overall, our results delineate a practical tradeoff between accuracy at long times, guaranteed physicality of the reduced dynamics, and algebraic interpretability of the reduced state space.

The content is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the relevant class of dynamical models and formally defines their center manifold, along with the associated CP projection. Section 3 shows how the existing framework for exact MR may be extended to yield an asymptotically exact MR onto the center manifold, and illustrates its usefulness on a dissipative XXZ spin chain exhibiting persistent oscillations. After reviewing the essential AE formalism, Sec. 4 shows how, within the AE framework, a reduced generator can be constructed at first order, that retains the CPTP property. Finite-time error bounds are derived, and the algebraic and AE-based MR approaches are quantitatively compared in illustrative perturbed spin-chain models. Two appendixes provide complete proofs of the general claims and supporting derivation for the dissipative spin-chain example.

2
Quantum dynamical semigroups and center manifolds

2.1
Quantum dynamical semigroups

In this paper, we focus on finite-dimensional quantum systems described on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The set of linear (bounded) operators acting on
\mathcal{H}
is denoted by
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, and the set of density operators by
\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{H})\equiv\{\rho\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})\,|\,\rho=\rho^{\dagger}\geq 0,\,{\rm tr}(\rho)=1\}
. We will use the notations
[\cdot,\cdot]
and
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
to denote the commutator and anti-commutator between two operators, respectively.

For the class of systems we consider, the evolution of the state
\rho(t)
in the Schrödinger picture is described by a linear, time-invariant equation of the form

\dot{\rho}(t)=\mathcal{L}[\rho(t)],

(1)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the generator of a valid, completely-positive and trace-preserving (CPTP)
quantum dynamical semigroup

[
3
]
. It is well-known
[
41
,
26
]
that a generator
\mathcal{L}
of a (time-invariant) quantum dynamical semigroup,
\{e^{\mathcal{L}t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, can be expressed in a canonical Lindblad (diagonal) or, if a fixed operator basis is chosen, a Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad (GKLS) form.
Explicitly, in units where
\hbar=1
, we may write

\mathcal{L}(\rho)=-i[H,\rho]+\sum_{k}\mathcal{D}_{L_{k}}(\rho),

where
H=H^{\dagger}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
describes the Hamiltonian (coherent) contribution to the dynamics,
L_{k}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
are so-called Lindblad (or noise) operators, and the dissipator
\mathcal{D}_{L}(\rho)\equiv L\rho L^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{L^{\dagger}L,\rho\}
. While the representation of the generator
\mathcal{L}
in terms of
H,\{L_{k}\}
is not unique
[
41
,
35
]
, specific forms are often suggested by physical considerations. As mentioned, a master equation of the above form can be taken to describe a wide class of open quantum systems undergoing Markovian dynamics.

2.2
Center manifold

At the heart of this work is the notion of the dynamical center manifold:

Definition 1
.

Given a Lindblad generator
\mathcal{L}
, the center manifold is defined as

\mathscr{C}\equiv{\rm span}\{X\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})\,|\,\exists\omega\in\mathbb{R}\text{ s.t. }\mathcal{L}(X)=i\omega X\,\}.

Equivalent (or related) notions of limit cycle sets, peripheral spectral eigen-operators, or rotating points have been discussed in the literature
[
61
,
62
]
, along with connections to the notion of decoherence-free subspaces and information-preserving structures
[
49
,
12
]
. Note that the center manifold
\mathscr{C}
always includes the set of
fixed (stationary, or steady)
operators,

\ker(\mathcal{L})\equiv\{X\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})|\,\mathcal{L}(X)=0\}\subseteq\mathscr{C},

which, in the case of finite-dimensional Lindblad generators as we consider, is always non-empty. Since any such Lindblad generator
\mathcal{L}
is stable
[
62
]
,
\mathscr{C}
is attractive and any trajectory converges exponentially to it. Let the
spectral gap

\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}
of
\mathcal{L}
be defined as the absolute value of the real part of the slowest, non-purely-imaginary eigenvalue
[
58
]
1
1

1

Sometimes the spectral gap is defined in the literature to quantify the convergence to the steady-state manifold, in which case the maximum is taken over all nonzero eigenvalues; an “oscillating gap” has also been introduced
[
31
]
, where the maximum is taken over all non-purely-real eigenvalues.
, that is,

\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\equiv\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in{\rm sp}(\mathcal{L})\\
\lambda\neq i\omega\end{subarray}}|\Re(\lambda)|.

While the spectral gap determines the asymptotic convergence rate of arbitrary trajectories to
\mathscr{C}
,
the following bound holds for arbitrary times:

Lemma 1

(Exponential convergence to
\mathscr{C}
)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lindblad generator with spectral gap
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}

and let
\mathcal{P}
be a projector onto
\mathscr{C}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}^{2}=\mathcal{P}
and
{\rm Im}\,\mathcal{P}=\mathscr{C}
. Then, for all
\rho_{0}\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{H})
, and for all
\delta>0
, there exists
\Gamma>0
such that

\left|\left|e^{\mathcal{L}t}\rho_{0}-\mathcal{P}(e^{\mathcal{L}t}\rho_{0})\right|\right|\leq\Gamma e^{-(\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}-\delta)t},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

(2)

where
\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|
is an arbitrary operator norm.

A proof is included in Appendix
A
. The result is a direct consequence of the fact that finite-dimensional quantum dynamical semigroups are stable, hence
\mathcal{L}
only admits trivial (one-dimensional) Jordan blocks associated to purely imaginary eigenvalues, see e.g.,
[
62
, Prop 6.2]
.

In the following, we use the term super-operator to mean any linear map on
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
, and call any idempotent
\mathcal{P}:\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
, such that
\mathcal{P}^{2}\equiv\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}
a
projector
. Furthermore, we say that
\mathcal{P}
is
orthogonal
if it is self-adjoint with respect to the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product i.e.,
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}
. Given another super-operator
\mathcal{B}
, we say that
\mathcal{P}
is a
\mathcal{B}
-spectral projector
if it commutes with said super-operator, i.e.,
[\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B}]=0
.

By definition,
\mathscr{C}
corresponds to the eigenspace of
\mathcal{L}
whose eigenvalues have
zero
real part: it thus consists of the variables which persist in the long-time dynamics, by either settling down to a stationary state or converging towards a state that supports “oscillating coherences”
[
2
,
15
]
. As noted, characterizing non-stationary long-time evolution is highly relevant in the context of non-equilibrium many-body dynamics, but also important in
scenarios where fast unitary dynamics is present, which one wants to avoid treating as time-dependencies in a rotating frame
[
45
]
. Mathematically, a key property of
\mathscr{C}
, which we will use in the rest of the paper, is the fact that the
\mathcal{L}
-spectral projection onto
\mathscr{C}
is CP. What follows builds on known results, namely, the structure of the fixed points of CPTP maps and Lindblad generators, see e.g.,
[
42
,
35
]
, and the structure of the eigenspaces associated to limit cycles and peripheral spectra of CPTP maps
[
61
]
. In particular, this structure is discussed in detail in

[
35
, Sec. 2.2]
for both the case where a full-rank steady state exists and the general case. Adapting
[
61
, Theorem 8]
to the Lindblad setting, we have the following result:

Proposition 1

(CPTP projection onto
\mathscr{C}
)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a
Lindblad generator, and let
\mathscr{C}
be its center manifold. Then there exists a CPTP, not necessarily orthogonal (w.r.t.
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{HS}
),
\mathcal{L}
-spectral projector
\mathcal{P}
onto
\mathscr{C}
, i.e.,
{\rm Im}\,\mathcal{P}=\mathscr{C}
,
\mathcal{P}^{2}=\mathcal{P}
,
[\mathcal{P},\mathcal{L}]=0
and
\mathcal{P}
is CPTP.

A proof is again included in Appendix
A
.

3
Asymptotically exact model reduction

3.1
Tools: Model reduction via operator algebras

In this subsection we recall relevant results from the literature about CPTP projectors and their images
[
42
,
62
,
55
,
11
,
12
,
61
]
, as well as model-reduced dynamics on distorted algebras
[
28
]
. Our first two results, contained in Proposition
2
and Theorem
1
, holds in general, without any assumption on the relationship between the projector
\mathcal{P}
and the generator
\mathcal{L}
. A relation between
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{L}
emerges once the properties the reduced dynamics should retain are specified. In Sec.
3.2
, we shall indeed see how to combine these general results with the projection onto the center manifold to obtain a reduced Lindblad generator.

Proposition 2

(Fixed-point structure)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a CPTP projector and let
\mathscr{A}
be its image, i.e.,
\mathscr{A}\equiv{\rm Im}\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{R}
be the Hilbert space where
\mathscr{A}
has no support, in such a way that
\mathcal{H}={\rm supp}(\mathscr{A})\oplus\mathcal{H}_{R}
.
Then there exist a decomposition of the Hilbert space,

\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{k}(\mathcal{H}_{F,k}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{G,k})\oplus\mathcal{H}_{R},

(3)

a unitary operator
U\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
, and a set of density operators
\tau_{k}\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{H}_{G,k})
such that

\mathscr{A}=U\bigg[\bigoplus_{k}\big(\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H}_{F,k})\otimes\tau_{k}\big)\oplus\mathbb{0}_{R}\bigg]U^{\dagger}.

(4)

Furthermore,
\mathscr{A}
is a
*
-algebra closed with respect to the product
X\cdot_{\sigma}Y\equiv X\sigma Y
, where

\sigma=U\bigg[\bigoplus_{k}(\mathbb{1}_{F,k}\otimes\tau_{k}^{-1})\oplus\tau_{R}\bigg]U^{\dagger},

with
\tau_{R}
being an arbitrary state on
\mathcal{H}_{R}
.
Let then
W_{k}
be a linear operator from
{\rm supp}(\mathscr{A})
onto
\mathcal{H}_{F,k}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{G,k}
, such that
W_{k}W_{k}^{\dagger}=\mathbb{1}_{F,k}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{G,k}
. Then,
\mathcal{P}
restricted to
\mathscr{A}
takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}|_{\mathscr{A}}(X)

\displaystyle=U\bigg\{\bigoplus_{k}\Big[{\rm tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{G,k}}\left(W_{k}XW_{k}^{\dagger}\right)\otimes\tau_{k}\Big]\bigg\}U^{\dagger}

(5)

and is orthogonal with respect to the inner product
\left<X,Y\right>_{\sigma,\lambda}={\rm tr}[X^{\dagger}\sigma^{\lambda}Y\sigma^{1-\lambda}]
for
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

A proof of this result can be found in the above-mentioned references. In the literature, a
*
-algebra closed with respect to a modified product is also referred to as a
distorted algebra

[
12
]
, while the block structure provided in Eq. (
4
) is known as the
Wedderburn decomposition
.

The above representation theorem provides us with a structure for both the algebra and the CPTP projection on the support of the fixed states. From this structure, one can observe that a distorted algebra
\mathscr{A}
is isomorphic to a
smaller
representation, which we denote
\check{\mathscr{A}}
, where the weighted copies are removed, i.e.,
\check{\mathscr{A}}\equiv\bigoplus_{k}\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H}_{F,k})\subseteq\mathfrak{B}(\check{\mathcal{H}})
, with
\check{\mathcal{H}}=\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{H}_{F_{k}}
. This allows us to find a factorization in terms of two maps
[
30
]
,

\mathcal{R}:\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathfrak{B}(\check{\mathcal{H}})
,
{\rm Im}\mathcal{R}=\check{\mathscr{A}}
and
\mathcal{J}:\check{\mathscr{A}}\to\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
{\rm Im}\mathcal{J}={\mathscr{A}}
, such that

\mathcal{R}\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{I}_{\check{\mathscr{A}}},\qquad\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{R}.

Furthermore, one can observe
[
30
]
that these two factors can be chosen to be CPTP, i.e.,

\mathcal{R}(X)=\bigoplus_{k}{\rm tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{G,k}}(W_{k}XW_{k}^{\dagger})=\bigoplus_{k}X_{F,k}=\check{X},\quad\forall X\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H}),

(6)

and for all
\check{X}\in\check{\mathscr{A}}
,
\check{X}=\bigoplus_{k}X_{F,k}
,

\mathcal{J}(\check{X})=U\bigg(\bigoplus_{k}X_{F,k}\otimes\tau_{k}\bigg)U^{\dagger}.

(7)

We name
\mathcal{R}
the
reduction super-operator
and
\mathcal{J}
the
injection super-operator
. These reduction and injection super-operators naturally allow us to compute the reduced dynamics
\check{\mathcal{L}}\equiv\mathcal{R}\mathcal{L}\mathcal{J}
restricted to the algebra
\check{\mathscr{A}}
. To provide the initial condition for the reduced model in
\mathscr{A},
we consider the application of
{\mathcal{P}}
to the initial state. From now on, we will assume that we first reduce the dynamics to
{\rm supp}(\mathscr{A}),
and then consider the compressed description we have just introduced. Importantly, we can prove that the reduced generator
\check{\mathcal{L}}
is a valid Lindblad generator by leveraging the structure of the reduction and injection maps. We here report
[
28
, Theorem 4]
for completeness:

Theorem 1

(Reduced Lindbladian on algebras)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a CPTP projection, with
\mathscr{A}
its image
\mathscr{A}={\rm Im}\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{B(H)}
, and let
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{J}
be the CPTP factorization of
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{R}
, as defined in Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
). Then, for any Lindblad generator
\mathcal{L}
, its restriction to
\check{\mathscr{A}}
,
\check{\mathcal{L}}\equiv\mathcal{R}\mathcal{L}\mathcal{J},
is also a Lindblad generator, that is,
\check{\mathcal{L}}:\check{\mathscr{A}}\to\check{\mathscr{A}}
and
\{e^{\check{\mathcal{L}}t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a quantum dynamical semigroup.

This theorem ensures that,
regardless
of the image of the projector
\mathcal{P}
, as long as
\mathcal{P}
is CPTP, the reduced model,
\dot{\check{\rho}}(t)=\check{\mathcal{L}}(\check{\rho}(t))
, is in Lindblad form. In order to compute the reduced Hamiltonian and noise operators that enter
\check{\mathcal{L}}
, given a representation
(H,\{L_{k}\})
of
\mathcal{L}
, one can use the procedure described in
[
27
, Appendix]
.

Remark 1

(Beyond exact MR)
.

With the above algebraic tools in hand, the key issue becomes how to choose a sub-algebra
\mathscr{A}
, such that the reduced model is actually a good approximation of the original one. In
[
28
,
30
]
, a framework for
exact
MR is developed, in which knowledge of the initial states or observables of interest is leveraged to construct minimal sub-algebras that ensure perfect reconstruction of the original dynamics.
Exact reductions may be too stringent a requirement for obtaining useful models in settings of practical relevance, however. In the next section, we show how to construct reduced CPTP models that are at least
asymptotically exact
, with exponentially decaying errors. It is also important to stress that Theorem
1
only provides a
sufficient
condition for quantum MR. This means that, while we can guarantee that
\check{\mathcal{L}}
is Lindblad whenever the projector
\mathcal{P}
is CPTP, it is possible to find reduced generators that are Lindblad even if this is not the case.

3.2
Reduction on the center manifold

Combining the result of Proposition
1
with those of Proposition
2
, it follows that the center manifold
\mathscr{C}
of a Lindblad generator
\mathcal{L}
is a distorted algebra. Accordingly,
\mathscr{C}
has a Wedderburn decomposition

\mathscr{C}=U\bigg[\bigoplus_{k}\big(\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H}_{F,k})\otimes\tau_{k}\big)\oplus\mathbb{0}_{R}\bigg]U^{\dagger},

for some unitary
U\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
and a set of density operators
\tau_{k}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H}_{G,k})
. We then have the following:

Theorem 2

(Asymptotically exact reduction)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a center manifold of a Lindbald generator
\mathcal{L}
. Let then
\mathcal{P}
be the CPTP projection onto
\mathscr{C}
, and
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{J}
be the two CPTP factors
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{R}
as defined in Eq. (
6
) and (
7
). Define
\check{\mathcal{L}}\equiv\mathcal{R}\mathcal{L}\mathcal{J}
. Then the following hold:

1.

For all
\rho\in\mathscr{C}
and all
t\geq 0
, we have

e^{\mathcal{L}t}(\rho)=\mathcal{J}\,e^{\check{\mathcal{L}}t}\,\mathcal{R}(\rho).

2.

For all
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{H})
, we have

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\big[e^{\mathcal{L}t}(\rho)-\mathcal{J}e^{\check{\mathcal{L}}t}\mathcal{R}(\rho)\big]=0.

3.

For all
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{H})
and for all
\delta>0
, there exist a constant
\Gamma>0
such that

\big|\big|{e^{\mathcal{L}t}(\rho)-\mathcal{J}e^{\check{\mathcal{L}}}\mathcal{R}(\rho)}\big|\big|\leq\Gamma e^{-(\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}-\delta)t},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

where
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\in\mathbb{R}
is the spectral gap of
\mathcal{L}
.

4.

The restricted dynamics on the center manifold is unitary, of the form
\check{\mathcal{L}}(\check{\rho})=-i[\check{H},\check{\rho}]
, where
\check{H}=\bigoplus_{k}H_{F,k}
and
H_{F,k}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H}_{F,k})
for all
k
.

The proof is provided in Appendix
A
. Notice that, differently from
[
61
, Theorem 8, point 2]
, in the continuous-time case there can be
no
permutations of blocks of the same dimension. Therefore, each block of the Wedderburn decomposition of
\mathscr{C}
is invariant and evolves independently of the others. The reduced generator
\check{\mathcal{L}}
is the
minimal
model (in the sense of the smallest dimension) that is capable of exactly reproducing the asymptotic behavior of the reference system: for a reduced model to correctly reproduce the asymptotic dynamics for all initial conditions, the associated subspace must necessarily contain the entire center manifold, and the minimal such projection on such subspace is precisely
\mathcal{P}
.

The results we obtained thus far may be aptly illustrated in the context of a paradigmatic many-body dynamical model, as we turn to next.

3.3
Case study: Non-stationary coherent long-time dynamics in a dissipative XXZ chain

Consider a system composed of
N
spin-
\frac{1}{2}
degrees of freedom (qubits) in one dimension, whereby
\mathcal{H}=\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}_{i},

\mathcal{H}_{i}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{2},
and
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{4})^{\otimes N}.
As usual, let
\sigma_{q}
, with

\sigma_{\pm}^{(k)}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{x}^{(k)}\pm i\sigma_{y}^{(k)})

\sigma_{q}^{(N+1)}=\sigma_{q}^{(1)}

We take the spins to have a common natural frequency
\omega

\displaystyle H=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\bigg[\frac{\omega}{2}\,\sigma_{z}^{(j)}+\frac{A_{xy}}{2}\big(\sigma_{x}^{(j)}\sigma_{x}^{(j+1)}+\sigma_{y}^{(j)}\sigma_{y}^{(j+1)}\big)+A_{z}\,\sigma_{z}^{(j)}\sigma_{z}^{(j+1)}\bigg],

21
]

Notably, in the purely dissipative limit (

L_{j}=\sqrt{\gamma}\,\sigma_{+}^{(j)}\sigma_{-}^{(j+1)},\quad\forall j=1,\dots,N,\quad\gamma>0.

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\frac{N}{2}}

\displaystyle={\rm span}\{\left|00\dots 0\right>\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\frac{N}{2}-1}

\displaystyle={\rm span}\{\left|10\dots 0\right>,\left|01\dots 0\right>,\dots,\left|00\dots 1\right>\},

\displaystyle\,\,\,\vdots

Clearly, the two states

\displaystyle={\rm span}\big\{\left|0_{L}\middle>\middle<1_{L}\right|,\left|1_{L}\middle>\middle<0_{L}\right|\big\}\oplus\ker\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}\oplus\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{M}}\mathbb{C}\mathbb{1}_{d_{m}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\rho)

\displaystyle=W_{0}\rho W_{0}^{\dagger}\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{M}}{\rm tr}[W_{m}\rho W_{m}^{\dagger}],

4
Parametric models and perturbation theory

4.1
Perturbative setting

In the previous section we showed how, given a Lindblad generator
\mathcal{L}
, one can compute its center manifold
\mathscr{C}
and determine the Hamiltonian
\check{H}
that governs the dynamics in the asymptotic time limit. In most practical cases, however, computing
\mathscr{C}
analytically for given
\mathcal{L}
may be unfeasible. A perturbative approach may then be used, as the one we describe next.

To develop a perturbative scheme, we assume that the evolution in Eq. (
1
) is governed by a generator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
which is Lindblad for all
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
and depends
analytically
upon
\varepsilon
. Thanks to the analyticity assumption, a Taylor expansion around
\varepsilon=0
is then possible, namely,

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\varepsilon^{k}\mathcal{L}_{(k)},

where the unperturbed generator
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}\equiv\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon=0}
is assumed to be sufficiently simple to study, and the terms
\mathcal{L}_{(k)}
represent the perturbing contributions. As a special but paradigmatic setting, consider the case where
\mathcal{L}
may be expressed in the form
\mathcal{L}\equiv\mathcal{L}_{0}+\Delta\mathcal{L},
where
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is Lindblad and has a known, or easy to compute, center manifold
\mathscr{C}_{0},
and
\|\Delta\mathcal{L}\|=\varepsilon_{0}\ll\|\mathcal{L}_{0}\|
for some super-operator norm. Then one can formally define
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}\equiv\mathcal{L}_{0},

\mathcal{L}_{(1)}\equiv\Delta\mathcal{L}/\varepsilon_{0}
and consider the linearly perturbed model
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{L}_{(0)}+\varepsilon\mathcal{L}_{(1)},
which matches the model of interest for
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}.

Note that, for
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
to be a Lindblad generator, a
sufficient
condition is to require that all orders of
\mathcal{L}_{(k)}
are Lindblad generators. While this is a common assumption in the adiabatic elimination literature
[
7
,
57
]
, it is
not
necessary: for instance,
\mathcal{D}_{L+\varepsilon K}(\rho)
is a valid Lindblad generator for all
\varepsilon,K,L
; however, its Taylor expansion takes the form

\mathcal{D}_{L+\varepsilon K}(\rho)=\mathcal{D}_{L}(\rho)+\varepsilon\Big(L\rho K^{\dagger}+K\rho L^{\dagger}-\tfrac{1}{2}\{K^{\dagger}L+L^{\dagger}K,\rho\}\Big)+\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{D}_{K}(\rho),

(13)

where the first-order term need not be in Lindblad form
3
3

3

Taking e.g.,
K=\sigma_{x}
and
L=\sigma_{z}
on a qubit, the first-order term yields the Bloch equations
\dot{x}=2z
,
\dot{y}=0
,
\dot{z}=2x
. These
cannot
correspond to a valid Lindbladian, since the generator features a positive eigenvalue.
. In what follows, we shall only assume that
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
is a valid Lindblad generator for all
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, with no requirement on the
\mathcal{L}_{(k)}
.

Within the above perturbation setting, we can highlight two different, yet related, center manifolds; specifically:

•

\mathscr{C}_{0}
: the center manifold of the unperturbed generator
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
(with
\check{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
its isomorphic smaller representation);

•

\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}
: the center manifold of the perturbed generator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
.

For sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, one can prove that
\dim(\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon})\leq\dim(\mathscr{C}_{0})
, see e.g.,
[
36
]
. In what follows, we assume to be able to compute
\mathscr{C}_{0}
.

4.2
Approximate model reduction: Reduced dynamics on the unperturbed center manifold

As we have seen in Proposition
2
,
\mathscr{C}_{0}
is a distorted algebra, and restricting a Lindblad generator onto a distorted algebra yields a reduced generator that retains the Lindblad property as per Theorem
1
. To obtain a first-order approximation of the long-time dynamics associated to
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
, we can thus project the perturbed dynamics onto the unperturbed center manifold
\mathscr{C}_{0}
. As a consequence of Theorem
1
, we have the following:

Corollary 1

(Approximate Lindblad reduction)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
be a Lindblad generator for all
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, with
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}=\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon=0}
. Let
\mathscr{C}_{0}
be the center manifold of
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
,
\mathcal{P}_{(0)}
be the CPTP
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
-spectral projector onto
\mathscr{C}_{0}
and
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}
and
\mathcal{J}_{(0)}
the CPTP factors of the projector
\mathcal{P}_{(0)}
, as defined in Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
).
Then the reduced generator
\check{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}\equiv\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{J}_{(0)}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\varepsilon^{k}\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{L}_{(k)}\mathcal{J}_{(0)}
is Lindblad and leaves
\check{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
invariant.

While being asymptotically exact as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, the reduced model is otherwise approximate for all times, since the unperturbed center manifold
\mathscr{C}_{0}
is
not
, in general,
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-invariant. The error that the reduced model incurs when the initial state belongs to
\mathscr{C}_{0}
can be bounded as follows:

Theorem 3

(Error bounds)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
be a Lindblad generator analytic in
\varepsilon
, with
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}=\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon=0}
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{(0)}
be the CPTP
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
-spectral projector onto the unperturbed center manifold
\mathscr{C}_{0}
, and let
\check{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}\equiv\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{J}_{(0)}
. Then, for all
t\geq 0
, we have:

(i)

\displaystyle\left|\left|e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{P}_{(0)}-\mathcal{J}_{(0)}e^{t\check{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\right|\right|_{\rm sop}\leq t|\varepsilon|\left|\left|\mathcal{L}_{(1)}\right|\right|_{\rm sop}+O(t^{2},\varepsilon^{2}),

(14)

(ii)

\displaystyle\left|\left|\mathcal{P}_{(0)}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{P}_{(0)}-\mathcal{J}_{(0)}e^{t\check{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\right|\right|_{\rm sop}\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\left|\left|\mathcal{L}_{(1)}\right|\right|_{\rm sop}^{2}+O(t^{3},\varepsilon^{3}),

(15)

where
\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|_{\rm sop}
denotes any super-operator norm.

A proof of this result is given in Appendix
A
. Note that the above bounds are only useful as long as their respective right-hand-side remains small. In this sense, as Eq. (
14
) is dominated by how quickly the true evolution may exit the unperturbed center manifold
\mathscr{C}_{0}
, it does no better than integrating the perturbing vector field over time. By following the trajectory projected onto
\mathscr{C}_{0}
, Eq. (
15
) provides a bound that scales more slowly than linearly in
\varepsilon t
.

Remark 2

(Emerging unitarity)
.

As a special case of our formalism, we can recover existing results on “emerging unitarity” in strongly dissipative dynamics and dissipation-assisted computation over noiseless subsystems, as investigated in
[
64
,
65
]
. Specifically, linearly perturbed models of the form
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{L}_{(0)}+\varepsilon\mathcal{L}_{(1)}
are considered therein, and the center manifold of the unperturbed generator
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
is assumed to consist
only
of steady states,
\mathscr{C}_{0}\equiv\ker\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
, with
\varepsilon\equiv 1/T
, and
T>0
much longer than the natural relaxation timescale. The “dissipation-projected dynamics” is then obtained via projection onto the fixed-points set. Assuming that the perturbative term is purely Hamiltonian, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}_{1}(\cdot)=-i[H_{1},\cdot]
, it is shown in the above papers that the projected dynamics
\mathcal{P}_{(0)}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{P}_{(0)}
onto
\ker\mathcal{L}_{0}
is also purely Hamiltonian. This can be seen as an application of Corollary
1
in conjunction with
[
28
, Corollary 1]
. Likewise, the emergence of unitary dynamics over the steady-state manifold is discussed in a scenario where the Lindblad operators are linearly perturbed
[
65
,
6
]
, namely,
L\mapsto L+\varepsilon K
. As noted in Eq. (
13
), such a perturbation generates, to order
\varepsilon^{1}
, a contribution of the form
L\rho K^{\dagger}+K\rho L^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{K^{\dagger}L+L^{\dagger}K,\rho\}
: when projected onto
\ker\mathcal{L}_{0}
, one may verify that this indeed yields a Hamiltonian generator, as claimed in
[
65
, Proposition 4]
. Using Corollary
1
and the procedure described in
[
27
, Appendix]
, our formalism also makes it possible to characterize the effects of the perturbation to
O(\varepsilon^{2})
, which are encoded in
\mathcal{D}_{K}(\rho)
and reintroduce dissipation in general.

4.3
Approximate model reduction via adiabatic elimination

An important set of methods that operates under assumptions similar to those of Sec.
4.1
is AE
[
7
]
, a specialization to the quantum domain of classical time-scale separation techniques for linear systems. We first briefly recall the essential ideas, building on results from perturbation theory for linear operators
[
36
]
, as seen through the lenses of our current framework. We then provide a new result on the “gauge degrees of freedom” that are intrinsic in the AE formalism
[
7
,
45
]
[Theorem
4
], highlighting the connection with the approximate Lindblad MR provided in Corollary
1
.

4.3.1
The slow operator subspace.

Let us denote by
\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
the eigenspace of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
corresponding to perturbing the center manifold
\mathscr{C}_{0}
of
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
. That is,
\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
is the operator subspace with, say,
\dim(\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon})=\dim(\mathscr{C}_{0})\equiv d_{0}
,
spanned by the eigenoperators associated to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
, which for
\varepsilon=0
satisfy
\Re(\lambda_{\varepsilon=0})=0
. For
\varepsilon
small enough, perturbation theory
[
36
]
implies that
\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
, with
\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
corresponding to the slowest eigenvalues of the perturbed generator. Because
\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
is a sum of eigenspaces, it is
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-invariant. This allows one to study the evolution of the system restricted to the
perturbed
center manifold
\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
– resulting in a reduced model, defined on
\check{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
, that describes the evolution of the slowest components of the dynamics.

Let us denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
the
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-spectral projector onto
\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
, i.e.,
{\rm Im}(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})=\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
and
[\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon},\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}]=0
. Since we are projecting onto the
perturbed
space
\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon},
a CPTP
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
might no longer exist, in general. From
[
36
]
, we know that
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
is analytic in
\varepsilon
. Its Taylor expansion reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\varepsilon^{k}\mathcal{P}_{(k)},

(16)

where
\mathcal{P}_{(0)}
is the zeroth-order term,
\mathcal{P}_{(0)}=\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon=0}
, which coincides with the
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
-spectral projector onto
\mathscr{C}_{0}
. Ref.
[
36
]
further proves that
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
can be obtained from
\mathcal{P}_{(0)}
trough an analytic change of basis
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
, namely:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{P}_{(0)}\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}.

(17)

By Proposition
1
, it follows that
\mathcal{P}_{(0)}
is CPTP and, as we have seen above, it can be factorized into
\mathcal{P}_{(0)}=\mathcal{J}_{(0)}\mathcal{R}_{(0)}
, with
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(0)}=\mathcal{I}
, and
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}
and
\mathcal{J}_{(0)}
as in Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
). If we now substitute the factorization of
\mathcal{P}_{(0)}
into Eq. (
17
), we obtain

\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}=\big(\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{J}_{(0)}\big)\,\big(\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\big)\equiv\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}\,\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon},

a factorization in terms of two functions,
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}:\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\check{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
and
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}:\check{\mathscr{C}}_{0}\to\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
, which are analytic in
\varepsilon
and obey
{\rm Im}\,\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}=\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{I}_{\check{\mathscr{C}}_{0}}.
Since the mapping between
\mathscr{C}_{0}
and
\check{\mathscr{C}_{0}}
can be defined up to any isomorphism, which reflects an internal “choice of coordinates”, the two super-operators
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
are not unique in general. In the AE context, this isomorphism may itself depend on
\varepsilon
and it is referred to as a “gauge degree of freedom”
[
7
,
45
]
.

4.3.2
The adiabatic elimination equations.

To obtain the reduced model on
\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}
, we can proceed as follows. Consider a state
\rho_{0}\in\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
. Since
\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
is
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-invariant,
\rho(t)=e^{\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}t}\rho_{0}\in\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
for all
t\geq 0
. Then, since
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
acts as the identity over
\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\rho(t)=\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}e^{\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}t}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}\rho_{0}=\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}e^{\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}t}\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{0}),

where the second equality follows from the fact that the projector
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
commutes with
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
. Defining
\hat{\rho}_{0}\equiv\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{0})\in\check{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
and
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}\equiv\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon},
one obtains the reduced model

\dot{\hat{\rho}}(t)=\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}(\hat{\rho}(t)),
where we have introduced the symbol
\,\hat{\cdot}\,
to distinguish the generator obtained through AE from the one obtained via the algebraic MR procedure described in Sec.
4.2
.

AE
[
7
]
aims to compute the dynamics
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
on
\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
, as well as the perturbed injection map
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
, by using a Taylor expansion in
\varepsilon
.
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
can be computed directly, without the need to obtain the reduction map
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
. This is done by leveraging the so-called
invariance condition
: since
\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
is invariant under
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
, we have that
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
. By substituting
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
with its factorization
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
and right-multiplying by
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
, one obtains the invariance condition as given in
[
7
]
:

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}.

(18)

To solve Eq. (
18
), both
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
and
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
are expanded into their respective Taylor series, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\varepsilon^{k}\mathcal{J}_{(k)},

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\varepsilon^{k}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(k)}.

Substituting these two expansions into Eq. (
18
) yields

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\varepsilon^{k}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{(j)}\mathcal{J}_{(k-j)}\bigg)=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\varepsilon^{k}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\mathcal{J}_{(j)}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(k-j)}\bigg).

Since the equality must hold for all
\varepsilon
, it must hold separately for every order
\varepsilon^{k}
. This results in a set of equations to solve:

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{0}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(0)}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}_{(0)}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)},

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{1}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{(1)}\mathcal{J}_{(0)}+\mathcal{L}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}_{(1)}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)}+\mathcal{J}_{(0)}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(1)},

\displaystyle\vdots

(19)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{k}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{(j)}\mathcal{J}_{(k-j)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\mathcal{J}_{(j)}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(k-j)}\;.

The main idea is to solve this set of equations iteratively, starting at order
\varepsilon^{1}
and solving for
\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
and
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(1)}
, then increasing each in order, until one stops at a given maximum order, say,
K
, to have a truncated approximation of accuracy
\varepsilon^{K}

[
7
]
. At order
\varepsilon^{k}
, assuming that all the equations have been solved up to order
\varepsilon^{k-1}
means that all the terms
\mathcal{J}_{(j)}
and
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(j)}
have been computed for all
j=0,\dots,k-1
, so the resulting equation contains
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(k)}
and
\mathcal{J}_{(k)}
as the only unknowns.

4.3.3
Solving the adiabatic elimination equations.

The set of equations (
19
) is under-determined, reflecting the free,
\varepsilon
-dependent isomorphism between
\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
and
\check{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
, thus the gauge degree of freedom
[
7
,
45
]
. The latter involves an interplay between the map
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
and the dynamical generator
\hat{\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}}
, on which our reformulation of the problem provides added insight.

Specifically, let us focus on the leading orders
\varepsilon^{0}
and
\varepsilon^{1}
. Consistent with
\mathscr{C}_{0}
being “simple to analyze”, we assume we have determined factorization maps
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}
and
\mathcal{J}_{(0)}
for the projector
\mathcal{P}_{(0)}
. To finalize the order
\varepsilon^{0}
, we can take the invariance condition and left-multiply by
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}
both sides, obtaining
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)}=\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{L}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(0)}
. From Theorem
1
, we know that
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)}
is Lindblad.
Similarly, by taking the invariance condition from Eq. (
19
) at order
\varepsilon^{1}
, left-multiplying by
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}
and recalling that
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{L}_{(0)}=\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)}\mathcal{R}_{(0)}
, we obtain:

\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(1)}=\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{L}_{(1)}\mathcal{J}_{(0)}+\big[\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)},\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}\big]\,.

(20)

While the above depends manifestly on both unknowns
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(1)}
and
\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
, there are at least two situations in the AE literature where
\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
drops out from Eq.(
20
):

•

\mathscr{C}_{0}=\ker\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
, as in
[
7
]
: this means there are no rotating points (only stationary operators) in the center manifold for
\varepsilon=0
, hence
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)}=0
.

•

\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}=0
, as in
[
45
]
: this corresponds to a particular gauge choice, shown to be valid in their context.

In the above cases, Eq. (
20
) directly expresses
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(1)}
from known elements, which can then be plugged into the un-projected
\varepsilon^{1}
-equation in (
19
) to determine
\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
(in the second case, together with the associated constraint
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}=0
). Notably, the following result proves that this two-step procedure, solving (
20
) then (
19
), is in fact
well-posed
also in the general case, at least for orders
\varepsilon^{0}
and
\varepsilon^{1}
:

Theorem 4

(Freedom at first-order AE)
.

With reference to the iterative setting explained above, we have
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(1)}=\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{L}_{(1)}\mathcal{J}_{(0)}+\big[\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)},\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}\big]
, where
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
is a free variable.

A proof is given in Appendix
A
. This implies that the reduced generator can be computed
independently
of the part of
\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
that determines
\mathcal{P}_{(1)}
, hence one can first compute
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(1)}
based on an arbitrary choice of
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
, and then substitute it into Eq. (
19
) to determine
\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
.

Remark 3

(Limitations of AE)
.

Unlike the projection performed in Sec.
4.2
, AE attains a MR whose accuracy can in principle be pushed, by solving Eqs. (
19
) to high-enough order, arbitrarily close to the true behavior of the perturbed system initialized in
\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}
. As a tradeoff, AE does
not
guarantee that
\hat{\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}}
is Lindbladian, however. Indeed, unlike
\mathcal{P}_{0}
, the projector
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
is not
a priori
guaranteed to be CPTP. Hence, proving whether (and when) one obtains reduced dynamics in Lindblad form has been the subject of significant effort in the AE literature, with mixed results
[
57
]
. In
[
8
,
66
]
, a Lindbladian
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon=0}
with static center manifold
\mathscr{C}_{0}
, perturbed by a Hamiltonian, is shown to
always
yield
\hat{\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}}
Lindblad at order
\varepsilon^{2}
, modulo a proper gauge choice. The authors of
[
23
,
24
]
have established further special conditions in this sense, at the next order and for a non-stationary center manifold
\mathscr{C}_{0}
. Conversely, situations have been identified where conventional gauge choices do not lead to Lindblad dynamics, at higher order
[
48
]
or for non-stationary
\mathscr{C}_{0}

[
45
]
. This feature is best highlighted in
[
57
]
, where MR to a qubit-like
\check{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
is considered, and parameter regimes are identified for which, provably,
no
gauge choice can make
\hat{\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}}
Lindblad at order
\varepsilon^{4}
.

4.3.4
Guaranteeing CPTP dynamics at first order.

Our approach allows one to directly establish the existence of Lindblad generators that solve the AE equations to the first order. In fact, the truncated generator at order
\varepsilon^{1}
reads

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{R}_{(0)}(\mathcal{L}_{(0)}+\varepsilon\mathcal{L}_{(1)})\mathcal{J}_{(0)}+\varepsilon[\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)},\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}],

along with the truncated reduction and injection maps
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\varepsilon}\equiv\mathcal{R}_{(0)}+\varepsilon\mathcal{R}_{(1)}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\varepsilon}\equiv\mathcal{J}_{(0)}+\varepsilon\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
, and the reduced approximate initial “state”
\tilde{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(0)=\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{0})
. Note that, if the original generator is
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{L}_{(0)}+\varepsilon\mathcal{L}_{(1)},
and we assume that
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
obeys
[\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)},\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}]=0
, then the generator obtained from the algebraic MR of Sec.
4.2

coincides
with the reduced generator obtained from AE truncated at order
\varepsilon^{1}
, that is,
\check{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
. This allows us to obtain the following sufficient condition:

Corollary 2

(First-order AE Lindblad dynamics)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{L}_{(0)}+\varepsilon\mathcal{L}_{(1)}
be a Lindblad generator for all
\varepsilon
, and let
\mathcal{P}_{(0)},\mathcal{R}_{(0)}
and
\mathcal{J}_{(0)}
be as in Theorem
2
. Then, if
[\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)},\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}]=0
, the generator
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
obtained from AE truncated to order
\varepsilon^{1}
is also a Lindblad generator for all
\varepsilon
.

The proof of this result follows trivially from Corollary
1
. Note that Corollary
2
extends previous results on the Lindblad property of AE at order
\varepsilon^{1}
. In particular, in Ref.
[
7
]
the authors prove that, under the assumption that
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)}=0
,
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
is Lindblad regardless of the choice of
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
. Here, we extend this result to scenarios where
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)}\neq 0
, characterizing a set of choices for
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
(which includes
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}=0
), such that
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
remains in Lindblad form. On the other hand, as stated, Corollary
2
only provides a
sufficient
condition: it is possible that, for certain
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
with
[\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)},\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}]\neq 0
,
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
remains nonetheless in Lindblad form.

Remark 4
.

While having
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}
in Lindblad form ensures that the corresponding reduced model can be interpreted as a standalone quantum system, its relation to the original model may require more: we may want the reduced “state”
\tilde{\rho}_{\varepsilon}=\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\varepsilon}(\rho)
to be a valid density operator for any initialization, and any possible final state in the reduced model to be mapped back to a density operator via
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\rho}_{\varepsilon})
. This property has not been proved in general in the AE context. In contrast, the algebraic MR approach guarantees these properties, since the corresponding reduction and injection maps
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}
and
\mathcal{J}_{(0)}
can be chosen to be CPTP.

An important question naturally arises at this point, if we consider the
error
induced by the approximation, i.e.,
\min_{\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}}\big|\big|e^{\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}t}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\varepsilon}e^{\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon}t}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\varepsilon}\big|\big|
: Given that
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
can be chosen arbitrarily, is there a choice that
minimizes
this error? While obtaining a complete answer is beyond our present scope, some intuition may be gained by numerically exploring how different choices of
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
affect the error incurred by the reduced dynamics. We address this issue by example in the next subsection for the same model we considered previously, which also helps us to showcase other aspects of the connections between algebraic and adiabatic approaches and the results we established.

4.4
Illustrative applications

Example 1: Model reduction in a perturbed dissipative XXZ spin chain.—
Let the dissipative XXZ spin-chain model introduced in Sec.
3.3
define our unperturbed model,
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
. Consider a purely Hamiltonian perturbation of the form
\mathcal{L}_{(1)}(\cdot)=-i[H_{1},\cdot]
whereby, physically, the parameters acquire site-dependent disorder:

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\bigg[\frac{\omega_{j}}{2}\sigma_{z}^{(j)}+A_{x,j}\sigma_{x}^{(j)}\sigma_{x}^{(j+1)}+A_{y,j}\sigma_{y}^{(j)}\sigma_{y}^{(j+1)}+A_{z,j}\sigma_{z}^{(j)}\sigma_{z}^{(j+1)}\bigg],

with values of
A_{x,j},A_{y,j},A_{z,j}
and
\omega_{j}
sampled from a uniform distribution over
[0,2)
.

Thanks to the simple form of the perturbation, the reduced perturbed generator can be readily computed, namely,
\check{\mathcal{L}}(\cdot)=\mathcal{R}\mathcal{L}\mathcal{J}(\cdot)=-i[\check{H}_{\varepsilon},\cdot],
with the new reduced Hamiltonian given by
\check{H}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{J}^{\dagger}(H_{0}+\varepsilon H_{1})=\check{H}_{\frac{N}{2},\varepsilon}\oplus 0_{(N-1)\times(N-1)},
where
\check{H}_{\frac{N}{2}}=\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{0}+\varepsilon\bar{\omega})\sigma_{z}

and
\bar{\omega}\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{N}\omega_{j}.

Example 2: Model reduction in a perturbed spin chain with dephasing.—
As a second example, inspired by the analysis in
[
67
, Chapter 3]
, we consider a purely dissipative spin-
1/2
chain, again under periodic boundary conditions, for which a dominant dephasing dynamics is perturbed by both unital and non-unital transverse dissipation. That is, the Lindblad generator is given by
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{L}_{(0)}+\varepsilon\mathcal{L}_{(1)},
with
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}\equiv-i[H_{0},\cdot]+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{D}_{\sigma_{z}^{(j)}}
,
H_{0}\equiv A_{z}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma_{z}^{(j)}\sigma_{z}^{(j+1)}
,
A_{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
\mathcal{L}_{(1)}\equiv\sum_{k}\mathcal{D}_{L_{k}}
, where the noise operators
L_{k}
are

\displaystyle L_{x,j}=\mu_{x,j}\sigma_{x}^{(j)},\quad L_{+,j}=\mu_{+,j}\sigma_{+}^{(j)},\quad L_{-,j}=\mu_{-,j}\sigma_{-}^{(j)},\quad\mu_{x,j},\mu_{+,j},\mu_{-,j}\geq 0,\>\forall j.

In this case, the unperturbed center manifold corresponds to the diagonal algebra
\mathscr{C}_{0}=\ker\mathcal{L}\equiv{\rm span}\{\left|s\middle>\middle<s\right|\}
, where
s
denotes all the bitstrings of length
N
. Let then
\mathcal{P}
be the CPTP projector onto the diagonal
{\bf p}=[{\bf p}_{1},\ldots{\bf p}_{n}]^{\top}={\rm diag}(\rho)
, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}(\rho)=\sum_{s}\left|s\middle>\middle<s\right|\rho\left|s\middle>\middle<s\right|,
and
\mathcal{R},\mathcal{J}
its two factors

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}:

\displaystyle\>\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\mapsto\mathbb{C}^{n},\quad\rho\mapsto\mathcal{R}(\rho)=\sum_{s}\left|s\middle>\middle<s\right|\rho\left|s\right>,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}:

\displaystyle\>\mathbb{C}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{C}^{n\times n},\quad{\bf p}\mapsto\mathcal{J}({\bf p})=\sum_{s}\left|s\middle>\middle<s\right|{\bf p}_{s},

where
n\equiv 2^{N}
. Notably, by projecting
\mathcal{L}_{(1)}
onto
\mathscr{C}_{0}
we obtain, similarly to
[
10
]
, a
classical
model of the form
\dot{\bf p}(t)=L{\bf p}(t)
, where
{\bf p}\equiv\mathcal{R}({\rho})
is a probability distribution (i.e.
[\bf p]_{j}\geq 0
and
{\bf 1}^{T}{\bf p}=1
) and
L
is a Metzler matrix (i.e.,
[L]_{j,k}>0
,
\forall j\neq k
), with
{\bf 1}^{T}L=0
. One can further note that, in this case,
\mathcal{L}_{(1)}
leaves the steady-state manifold
\mathscr{C}_{0}
invariant.

For concreteness, let us first study the action of noise operators acting only on the first qubit, i.e.,
L_{x,1},L_{+,1},L_{-,1}.
If we denote by
0*
any bitstring of length
N
that starts with
0
and, correspondingly, by
1*
the identical one with the first bit flipped, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{u=x,+,-}\mathcal{D}_{L_{u,1}}(\left|0*\middle>\middle<0*\right|)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}(\left|1*\middle>\middle<1*\right|-\left|0*\middle>\middle<0*\right|),\quad\alpha_{1}\equiv\mu_{x,1}^{2}+\mu_{-,1}^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{u=x,+,-}\mathcal{D}_{L_{u,1}}(\left|1*\middle>\middle<1*\right|)

\displaystyle=\beta_{1}(\left|0*\middle>\middle<0*\right|-\left|1*\middle>\middle<1*\right|),\quad\beta_{1}\equiv\mu_{x,1}^{2}+\mu_{+,1}^{2}.

The action of
L_{x,1},L_{+,1},L_{-,1}
onto
\mathscr{C}_{0}
can thus be described by

\begin{bmatrix}-\alpha_{1}&\beta_{1}\\
\alpha_{1}&-\beta_{1}\end{bmatrix}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{2^{N-1}}.

One can verify that such a matrix is indeed Metzler, as
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\geq 0
. Combining the effect of the dissipators on all spins, we obtain the reduced approximate generator in the Metzler-matrix form

L=\varepsilon\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbb{1}_{2^{j-1}}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}-\alpha_{j}&\beta_{j}\\
\alpha_{j}&-\beta_{j}\end{bmatrix}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{2^{N-j}}\bigg).

(21)

Since in this case
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{0}=0
, the AE generator truncated at order
\varepsilon^{1}
coincides with the generator described by the above equation.
Thus, the only difference between the two dynamics stems from the different reduction and injection maps.

Numerical simulation results are shown in Fig.
4
(left). One can observe that, because of the special structure of the perturbation
\mathcal{L}_{(1)}
, there is
no
error in the probability of measuring
\left|0\dots 0\middle>\middle<0\dots 0\right|
. In fact, there is no error also in measuring all the others states
\left|s\middle>\middle<s\right|
(we plotted only the first probability solely for ease of presentation). On the other hand, in Fig.
4
(right), one can notice a fast convergence of the error norm, which is due to the exponential decay of the coherences.

Remark 5
.

Note that, if we were to represent the reduced state
{\bf p}_{t}
as a density operator
\rho(t)
, it would seem as if there is no apparent MR, as one still needs a density operator of size
2^{N}\times 2^{N}
. On the other hand, the MR becomes obvious when representing the reduced state as a probability vector, since this makes it apparent that the coherences of the state are not relevant for the dynamics. The same “hidden” reduction happens for more general block-diagonal matrices, since the MR we achieve does not come only from the presence of repeated blocks (
\otimes\mathbb{1}_{G,k}
terms). In particular, when the center manifold
\mathscr{C}
has the block structure
\mathscr{C}=\bigoplus_{k}\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H}_{F,k})
, the only parts of the state that are relevant for the dynamics are the blocks along the diagonal, and the off-diagonal blocks can be discarded. Representing the state as a single density operator is then only a matter of representation convenience and smaller representations could be proposed.

5
Conclusions and outlook

In this paper, we have shown how the operator-algebraic framework developed for exact MR of Markovian quantum dynamics may be brought to bear in developing
approximate
MR procedures to characterize the long-time dynamics on a center manifold. Depending on whether the Lindblad dynamics defining the full model has a center manifold that is directly suitable for the intended reduction or can be seen as an analytic perturbation of a Lindblad dynamics that does, we have shown how to achieve MR in a way that is asymptotically exact or makes natural contact with AE techniques. Specifically, by realizing how the “gauge degree of freedom” appearing in AE equations can be reformulated as a factorization choice for the projector in the algebraic method, we have elucidated how the two MR methods can be made to match at first order, and thus share their respective advantages and limitations.
By design, the algebraic method returns a reduced generator that is in Lindblad form – hence retains the CPTP properties of a valid,
quantum
reduced model; but, in the perturbative setting we consider, it maintains a fixed order of error with respect to the exact model evolution. In contrast, AE can in principle be carried to higher orders of approximation and improved error performance, but with the risk of unavoidably losing the CPTP property.

We have illustrated the applicability of our methods in analyzing the non-stationary long-time dynamics arising in many-body spin-chain models of interest for non-equilibrium physics and quantum simulation. We expect they will prove useful in other applications of interest, notably, in the context of quantum reservoir engineering, where AE is routinely employed
[
46
]
.
Likewise, our techniques should also apply beyond the finite-dimensional setting we focused on, subject to appropriate truncation and mathematical modifications
[
47
]
. This may be especially relevant to bosonic models that exhibit single or multiple limit cycles
[
40
,
9
,
37
]
, with emphasis on quantum synchronization studies
[
39
,
43
]
, as well as to bosonic quantum error correction
[
52
]
.

A natural question for future work would be to extend the algebraic treatment of perturbed models to higher orders of approximation. This may also enable making contact with the idea of a “dissipation-projected hierarchy” put forward in
[
66
]
for the case where the unperturbed center manifold
\mathscr{C}_{0}
includes only steady states. In our context, some reduced model in a space isomorphic to
\mathscr{C}_{0}
does exist, and it should “mirror” the one obtained by higher-order AE, in an appropriate sense. However, it is currently unclear how to compute such model with an approach that would preserve the structural CPTP constraint, as the algebraic MR approach guarantees.

Let us denote by

\{q_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}

\mathcal{P}

\mathcal{P}\equiv\lim_{k\to+\infty}e^{q_{k}\mathcal{L}}.

\mathscr{F}\equiv{\rm span}\{X\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})|\mathcal{L}(X)=(-\mu+i\omega)X,\mu>0\}

\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})=\mathscr{C}\oplus\mathscr{F}.

Then, if
\mu>0

e^{\mathcal{L}q_{k}}(X)=e^{-\mu q_{k}}e^{i\omega q_{k}}X

\mathcal{P}(X)=X

\forall X\in\mathscr{F}

\mathcal{P}

The proof of the third point follows from the exponential convergence of the center manifold given in Lemma

imposes:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}[L_{0},X_{A}^{1}]=0,\\
L_{0}X_{B}^{1}-X_{B}^{1}L_{S}=L_{B}^{1},\\
L_{S}X_{C}^{1}-X_{C}^{1}L_{0}=-L_{C}^{1},\\
[L_{S},X_{D}^{1}]=0,\end{cases}

while the projector condition
\mathcal{P}_{(0)}\mathcal{P}_{(1)}+\mathcal{P}_{(1)}\mathcal{P}_{(0)}=\mathcal{P}_{(1)}
imposes
X_{A}^{1}=0
and
X_{D}^{1}=0
. The second and third Sylvester equations admit a unique solution, since
L_{0}
and
L_{S}
share no eigenvalues. Let us denote by
X_{B}^{1*}
and
X_{C}^{1*}
their solution. This results in the term at order
\varepsilon^{1}
of the projector being

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{(1)}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&X_{B}^{1*}\\
\hline\cr X_{C}^{1*}&0\end{array}\right].

Let
\mathcal{R}_{(1)}=\big[\begin{array}[]{c|c}R_{A}^{1}&R_{B}^{1}\end{array}\big]
and
\mathcal{J}_{(1)}=\big[\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{A}^{1}&J_{B}^{1}\end{array}\big]^{T}.

Then, the constraints are
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}+\mathcal{R}_{(1)}\mathcal{J}_{(0)}=0
and
\mathcal{J}_{(1)}\mathcal{R}_{(0)}+\mathcal{J}_{(0)}\mathcal{R}_{(1)}=\mathcal{P}_{(1)}
, which become:

R_{A}^{1}=-(J_{A}^{0})^{-1}J_{A}^{1}(J_{A}^{0})^{-1}

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}R_{B}^{1}=(J_{A}^{0})^{-1}X_{B}^{1*},\\
J_{B}^{1}=X_{C}^{1*}J_{A}^{0}.\end{cases}

This finally results in

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{(1)}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}-(J_{A}^{0})^{-1}J_{A}^{1}(J_{A}^{0})^{-1}&(J_{A}^{0})^{-1}X_{B}^{1*}\end{array}\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{(1)}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}J_{A}^{1}\\
\hline\cr X_{C}^{1*}J_{A}^{0},\end{array}\right],

where
J_{A}^{1}
is a free variable. The term of order
\varepsilon^{1}
in the reduced dynamics is then

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(1)}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{L}_{(1)}\mathcal{J}_{(0)}+[\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)},\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}]=(J_{A}^{0})^{-1}L_{A}^{1}J_{A}^{0}+[(J_{A}^{0})^{-1}L_{0}J_{A}^{0},J_{A}^{1}].

As stated in the theorem, the term
\mathcal{R}_{(0)}\mathcal{J}_{(1)}
appearing on the right side of the commutator is indeed a free variable. This concludes the proof.

One may note that for
J_{A}^{0}=\mathbb{1}
, we obtain

\mathcal{R}_{(1)}=\big[\begin{array}[]{c|c}-J_{A}^{1}&X_{B}^{1*}\end{array}\big]
,
\mathcal{J}_{(1)}=\big[\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{A}^{1}&X_{C}^{1*}\end{array}\big]^{T}
and
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{(1)}=L_{A}^{1}+[L_{0},J_{A}^{1}].

At first sight, the choice of
J_{A}^{1}
now appears to modify the dynamics. However, it is actually equivalent to a basis change in
\mathscr{C}_{0}
, up to terms of order
\varepsilon^{2}
. The latter would appear through the impact of
J_{A}^{1}
on the next order. Indeed, the spectrum of the dynamics on the exact reduced model (infinite Taylor expansion) should be a physical quantity, independent of gauge choices.
∎

We start by explicitly showing that the angular momentum operator

is a conserved quantity and the generator of a group of strong symmetries.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the Lindblad generator with the Hamiltonian and noise operators given in Eqs. (
8
) and (
9

\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(J_{z})=0
and

is a strong symmetry for the model, i.e.,
[U_{\varphi},H]=[U_{\varphi},L_{j}]=0
.

Proof.

We first prove that
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(J_{z})=0
. The fact that
[H,J_{z}]=0
is well-known in the literature, see e.g.,
[
25
]
. It remains to prove that
\sum_{j}\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}_{L_{j}}(J_{z})=0
, where
\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}_{L}(X)\equiv L^{\dagger}XL-\frac{1}{2}\{L^{\dagger}L,X\}
. One way to show this is to notice that
[J_{z},L_{j}]=[J_{z},L_{j}^{\dagger}]=0
, for all
j
, with
L_{j}=\sigma_{+}^{(j)}\sigma_{-}^{(j+1)}
. Let us rewrite

\displaystyle\sigma_{+}^{(j)}\sigma_{-}^{(j+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\big(\vec{\sigma}^{(j)}\cdot\vec{\sigma}^{(k)}-\sigma_{z}^{(j)}\sigma_{z}^{(j+1)}\big)+\frac{i}{4}\big(\sigma_{y}^{(j)}\sigma_{x}^{(j+1)}-\sigma_{x}^{(j)}\sigma_{y}^{(j+1)}\big),

with
\vec{\sigma}^{(j)}\cdot\vec{\sigma}^{(k)}\equiv\sigma_{x}^{(j)}\sigma_{x}^{(k)}+\sigma_{y}^{(j)}\sigma_{y}^{(k)}+\sigma_{z}^{(j)}\sigma_{z}^{(k)}
. Since the operators
\vec{\sigma}^{(j)}\cdot\vec{\sigma}^{(j+1)}
are invariant under collective rotations generated by
J_{z}

[
38
]
, it is immediate to see that the operator following the factor
\tfrac{1}{4}
above commutes with
J_{z}
. Direct calculation using the Pauli commutation rules shows that the commutator between
\sigma_{z}^{(j)}+\sigma_{z}^{(j+1)}
(hence
J_{z}
) and the operator following the
\tfrac{i}{4}
factor also vanishes. The fact that
[J_{z},(\sigma_{+}^{(j)}\sigma_{-}^{(j+1)})^{\dagger}]=0
may be established in an entirely similar manner.

Since the continuous group

is generated by

, and the latter operator commutes with

and all the

[U_{\varphi},H]=[U_{\varphi},L_{j}]=0

∎

w
and
w^{\prime}
. By iterating this argument, we get to a state with
|\rho_{w^{(k)},{w^{\prime}}^{(k)}}(t)|\neq 0
where
w^{(k)}=*101*
and
{w^{\prime}}^{(k)}=*100*
. The action of
\mathcal{D}_{L_{j}}
on the two leftmost identified bits, as expressed through (
24
), will contribute a positive term to
|\rho_{w^{(k+1)},{w^{\prime}}^{(k+1)}}(t)|
, where
w^{(k+1)}=*011*
and
{w^{\prime}}^{(k+1)}=*010*
. Now,
{w^{\prime}}^{(k+1)}
features a sequence
10
at a place where
w^{(k+1)}
features
00
. This means that, to be in the set where
\tfrac{d}{dt}V=0
, we must have
|\rho_{w^{(k+1)},{w^{\prime}}^{(k+1)}}(t)|=0
. The whole argument thus shows that, in the invariant set where
\tfrac{d}{dt}V=0
, we cannot have
|\rho_{w,w^{\prime}}(t)|\neq 0
, because this would ultimately imply
|\rho_{w^{(k+1)},{w^{\prime}}^{(k+1)}}(t)|\neq 0
, for which
\tfrac{d}{dt}V\neq 0.
By contradiction, and since
w\neq w^{\prime}
were chosen arbitrarily, we must thus conclude that the largest invariant set where
\tfrac{d}{dt}V=0
necessarily requires
|\rho_{w,w^{\prime}}(t)|=0
for all
w\neq w^{\prime}
and
w,w^{\prime}\neq 0_{L},1_{L}
. The LaSalle invariance principle thus ensures that any state will asymptotically converge to this situation.
∎

It follows from the above results that, when
A_{xy}=0
, the kernel and the center manifold are
fully
characterized by Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
) in the main text.

Corollary 5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the Lindblad generator with the Hamiltonian and noise operators given in Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
). If
A_{xy}=0
, then the only equilibria of
\mathcal{L}
are the states
\bar{\rho}_{m}
in Eq. (
10
), and the center manifold
\mathscr{C}
coincides with Eq. (
11
).

For the kernel of
\mathcal{L}
, we can conclude the proof for
arbitrary
values of
A_{xy}
by invoking a standard genericity argument from
[
54
, Lemma 5]
, which we report here for completeness:

Lemma 3
.

Let
A_{\varphi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be analytic in
\varphi\in\mathbb{R}
and let
r=\max_{\varphi\in\mathbb{R}}\rank(A_{\varphi})
. Then the set
\Omega=\{\varphi\in\mathbb{R}|\rank(A_{\varphi})<r\}
is such that
\mu(\Omega)=0
, where
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the Lindblad generator with the Hamiltonian and noise operators given in Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
).
For almost all choices of
A_{xy}
, we have that
\ker\mathcal{L}
coincides with the states
\bar{\rho}_{m}
defined in Eq. (
10
).

Proof.

From Proposition
3
we have that
\dim\ker(\mathcal{L})\geq N+1
, thus
\rank(\mathcal{L})\leq n-N-1
. Then from Corollary
5
, for
A_{xy}=0
we indeed have
\dim\ker(\mathcal{L})=N+1
, thus
\rank(\mathcal{L})=n-N-1
. By applying Lemma
3
, the set of
A_{xy}
such that
\dim\ker(\mathcal{L})>N+1
is a measure zero set.
∎

For the center manifold
\mathscr{C}
, we are not aware of an exact equivalent to Lemma
3
. A perturbative argument could be invoked to show that the conclusions of Corollary
5
remain true at least for
A_{xy}
sufficiently small. As remarked in the text, numerical evidence suggests they may hold in general.
