Strengthening workplace emergency preparedness through a university CPR and first aid responder programme: four-year experience
Authors: Henrietta Bánfai-Csonka, József Betlehem, János Musch, Bálint Bánfai
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

Universities represent complex workplace environments with large and heterogeneous populations. However, preparedness for medical emergencies, including cardiac arrest, remains variable across Europe. Early cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and rapid automated external defibrillator (AED) use substantially improve survival after cardiac arrest1; however, workplace CPR preparedness and AED accessibility are not uniformly addressed by current European Union regulations.2 The Alliance for Workplace Awareness and Response to Emergencies (AWARE) recently emphasised the need for stronger workplace emergency preparedness policies, including broader CPR training and AED availability across European workplaces.3

The University of Pécs comprises approximately 25,000 students and several thousand employees, including academic staff, researchers, laboratory personnel, administrative staff, librarians, and reception/security personnel. To strengthen workplace emergency preparedness across this diverse institutional environment, a university-wide first aid responder programme was launched in 2022. Since its implementation, 266 employees have completed the initial training course, while annual refresher sessions have generated 390 participations, reflecting the programme's sustained operation and ongoing engagement.4, 5

The programme primarily focuses on CPR and AED use while also addressing other common first aid emergencies relevant to workplace settings. Initial training consists of a 10-h practice-oriented course emphasising hands-on skills and team-based emergency response. Participants already integrated into the system attend annual 4-h refresher sessions built around scenario-based simulations and structured debriefing. Training is delivered by paramedics experienced in CPR and first aid education.

Beyond scheduled courses, continuous engagement is supported through an online community platform where participants can access updated recommendations, explanatory educational materials, case discussions, and peer support. Parallel to the educational programme, AED deployment across the university has expanded, with 15 devices currently available in different institutional units.

To explore participant experiences and perceived preparedness after four years of implementation, a feedback questionnaire was distributed among workplace first aid responders. Preliminary analyses focus on perceived confidence in emergency response, willingness to intervene, AED-related confidence, and the perceived value of refresher training and continuous educational support (Table 1). A total of 84 respondents reported high perceived preparedness following participation in the programme. Most participants felt confident in initiating CPR (mean 5.11 ± 1.00) and using an AED (5.22 ± 0.98). Respondents rated scenario-based refresher training (5.56 ± 0.84) and regular retraining (5.74 ± 0.68) as particularly important. Perceived usefulness of continuous online educational support was also high, including case discussions (5.64 ± 0.80) and updated materials (5.59 ± 0.82). During follow-up, 23.8% and 35.7% reported encountering workplace and non-workplace emergencies, respectively, and all who encountered such events reported providing assistance.

A four-year university-based first aid responder programme was associated with high self-reported levels of preparedness and willingness to act among participants, and positive evaluation of continuous support and refresher training. These findings support the integration of structured workplace CPR and AED responder systems into institutional emergency preparedness strategies in large organisations.
