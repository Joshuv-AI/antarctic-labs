Non-Isomorphic Optimal Cayley Graphs
Francesc Comellas

Abstract




Introduction

We describe optimal degree-diameter Cayley graphs that are non-isomorphic to those currently reported. The graphs were identified using SageMath 10.8 programs generated within seconds by
Perplexity AI
and made operational after only minor modifications. One program uses groups from the GAP SmallGroups Library
[
1
]
and constructs Cayley graphs of prescribed order and diameter by examining generating sets either exhaustively (for groups of small order) or through randomized searches. Because the program often explores a large number of generating sets, some computations are extremely time-consuming and may require several days to complete. The resulting Cayley graphs have the same order as their underlying groups.

A second program searches the Cayley graph databases available at
https://graphsym.net/
and identifies graphs with minimum diameter for a given order and degree.

These computations revealed numerous graphs that are non-isomorphic to those listed as optimal by Marston Conder in a forthcoming paper
[
6
]
, and previously described on the Combinatorics Wiki website
[
5
]
. Several of the newly identified graphs exhibit distinct structural properties, including differences in girth, algebraic connectivity, automorphism groups, cycle counts, and distance distributions. As alternative optimal graphs, they may provide useful examples for future investigations of extremal problems involving degree, diameter, and girth.

The collection of non-isomorphic graphs reported here is not exhaustive, since the search procedure relies partly on randomized methods, especially for graphs of larger order.

Notation.
Throughout the paper, the following abbreviations are used:

•

MC denotes optimal Cayley graphs listed by Marston Conder on the Combinatorics Wiki:

http://combinatoricswiki.org/wiki/Description_of_optimal_Cayley_graphs_found_by_Marston_Conder
.

•

HGxxxx denotes a graph from the House of Graphs database:

https://houseofgraphs.org/
.

•

SG denotes GAP’s
SmallGroup
notation. For example, SG24x10x1 represents graph 1 (of several) coming from
SmallGroup(24,10)
.

•

P denotes cubic and tetravalent Cayley graphs from the compilation of Potočnik, Spiga, and Verret
[
13
]
, available at
https://graphsym.net/
. For example, P84x142 denotes Cayley graph number 142 among the 426 tetravalent Cayley graphs of order 84.

The included SageMath links execute code online directly from the sparse6 representation of the graphs embedded in the script. No local SageMath or Python installation is required.

All 80 optimal Cayley graphs are isomorphic to Wegner/MC32.

MC32/Wegner/SG(32,8)->32/SG(32,11)->16/SG(32,50)->32

C. Delorme (1992), in the context of the general degree-diameter problem, noticed the existence of this graph with 32 vertices, degree 6 and diameter 2 known as the Wegner graph and provided its edge list.

Paul Hafner pointed out (July 1998) that the Wegner graph is isomorphic (with automorphism group of order 1920) to the graph described by Lutz Twele
[
16
]

(the graph is also listed in Sampels’ PhD thesis).
Hafner provided also a ’prettier’ representation for this group and graph, all generators being involutions,
<f>
is the centre:

<a,b,c,d,e,f|a^{2},b^{2},c^{2},d^{2},e^{2},f^{2},(af)^{2},(bf)^{2},(cf)^{2},(df)^{2},(ef)^{2},ababf,cbade,dbaec,ebacd,acacf,ecadb>

For some more information refer to: M.J.Dinneen, Group-Theoretic Methods for Designing Networks (http://www.cs.auckland.ac.nz/CDMTCS/researchreports/082nznews.pdf).

Guillermo Pineda informed (May 8, 2008) on the existence of a paper in LNCS v.4123 pp. 853-857 (2006) by Sergey G. Molodsov
[
12
]

reporting a computer search which generated all largest diameter 2 degree 6 graphs, resulting in 6 non isomorphic graphs with order 32 (one of them is the non Cayley
K_{4}\mathord{*}X_{8}
and another the Wegner graph), and thus all these graphs are optimal for the general degree.diameter problem, but onlythe Wegner graph is optimalin our context.
