Abstract
. We introduce notions of path indicators and path correspondences for finite quantum graphs, study their basic properties, and compute them explicitly for classical graphs, trivial graphs, and complete quantum graphs.

1.
Introduction

A finite quantum graph is a noncommutative generalisation of a finite graph in which
the vertex set is replaced by a finite-dimensional
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
B

and the edge set is encoded by one of the two equivalent formulations: a quantum adjacency operator
[
MRV18
]
, or an operator bimodule over

B^{\prime}

[
Wea12
,
D2024a
]
, each generalising the adjacency matrix of a
classical graph. Quantum graphs arise as confusability graphs of quantum channels
[
DSW2013a
]
and underlie the study of the graph isomorphism
[
BCEHPSW2020a
,
MRV2019a
]
. The subject has developed rapidly in recent years, with progress on classification results in low dimensions
[
Gromada2021a
,
Matsuda2022
]
, operator algebras associated with quantum graphs and their properties
[
BEVW2022a
,
BHINW2023a
,
HIN26
]
, connectivity
[
CGW25
]
, and quantum Cayley graphs
[
W2024a
]
.

Paths are among the most informative structures carried by a graph.
In particular, the concatenation of edges into paths also provides the fundamental data for
building the graph
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras. It is therefore natural, in passing to
quantum graphs, to ask how edges concatenate into quantum paths and what
structure the resulting path spaces carry, a question raised in the
introduction of
[
BHINW2023a
]
. The aim of this paper is to develop this
theory for paths of finite length, in the spirit of
[
BHINW2023a
]
, by
introducing and studying path correspondences for finite quantum graphs.

We begin by fixing notation and recalling the necessary definitions and
results in Section
2
. We shall work with the quantum adjacency
operator formulation of quantum graphs
[
MRV18
]
. The main results are presented in
Section
3
. For a given finite quantum graph
\mathcal{G}
, we
define the sequence
\{\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
of path indicators,
construct the sequence
\{\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
of path
correspondences over the vertex algebra
B
of
\mathcal{G}
, and study some of
their basic properties. In particular, we show that the concatenation maps

U_{r,s}\colon\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}^{r}\otimes_{Z(B)}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}^{s}\to\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}^{r+s}
are
Z(B)
-balanced and turn
\{\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
into a subproduct system
[
ShS09
]
over
Z(B)
. Finally, in Section
4
, we compute the edge indicators and edge correspondences for classical graphs, complete quantum graphs, and trivial quantum graphs.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hilbert modules and
\textup{C}^{*}
-correspondences

A
\textup{C}^{*}
-correspondence over a
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
B
is a right Hilbert
B
-module
\mathcal{H}
along with a
∗
-homomorphism
\pi_{\mathcal{H}}\colon B\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, where
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the right
B
-linear adjointable operators on
\mathcal{H}
. This gives a left
B
-action on
\mathcal{H}
, that commutes with the right
B
-action on
\mathcal{H}
, by multiplication:
b\cdot\xi:=\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(b)\xi
for all
b\in B
and
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
faithful
if
\pi_{\mathcal{H}}
is faithful,
nondegenerate
or
essential
, if
B\cdot\mathcal{H}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be
\textup{C}^{*}
-correspondences over
B
. Suppose
\pi_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}\colon B\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{i})
are
∗
-homomorphisms inducing the left
B
-action on
\mathcal{H}_{i}
, for
i=1,2
. The algebraic tensor product
\mathcal{H}_{1}\odot\mathcal{H}_{2}
carries a natural
B
-valued semi-inner product defined by
\langle x_{1}\otimes y_{1},x_{2}\otimes y_{2}\rangle_{B}:=\langle y_{1},\pi_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}(\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle_{B})y_{2}\rangle_{B}
, for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
. Then we obtain a right Hilbert
B
-module
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{H}_{2}
from
\mathcal{H}_{1}\odot\mathcal{H}_{2}
by separation and completion. The kernel of the quotient map
\mathcal{H}_{1}\odot\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{H}_{2}
is the linear span of
\{xb\otimes y-x\otimes\pi_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}(b)y\mid x\in\mathcal{H}_{1},y\in\mathcal{H}_{2},b\in B\}
. Moreover, there is a left
B
-action, commuting with the right
B
-action, given by
b\cdot(x\otimes y):=\pi_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}(b)x\otimes y
, for all
x_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
,
y\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
b\in B
. Thus, the interior tensor product
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{H}_{2}
canonically becomes a
\textup{C}^{*}
-correspondence over
B
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
B
be a finite dimensional
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
\psi\colon B\to\mathbb{C}
be a faithful state. Then
B
acts on
B\otimes B
from the right by
(x\otimes y)\cdot b=x\otimes yb
. We equip
B\otimes B
with a
B
-valued inner product
\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle_{B}:=(\psi\otimes\textup{id}_{B})(\xi_{1}^{*}\xi_{2})
, for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in B\otimes B
. Faithfulness of
\psi
and finite dimensionality of
B
make the separation and completion redundant; hence
B\otimes B
is a right Hilbert
B
-module. Furthermore,
B\otimes B
is a
\textup{C}^{*}
-correspondence over
B
with respect to the left action of
B
on the first tensor factor of
B\otimes B
, that is,
b\cdot(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}):=ax_{1}\otimes x_{2}
, for all
b,x_{1},x_{2}\in B
.

More generally, for nonnegative integer
n
, we may view
B^{\otimes(n+1)}
as
\textup{C}^{*}
-correpondence over
B
. We define a
B
-valued inner product on
B^{\otimes(n+1)}
by
\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle_{B}:=(\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes\textup{id}_{B})(\xi_{1}^{*}\xi_{2})
for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in B^{\otimes(n+1)}
. The left and right actions of
B
on
B^{\otimes(n+1)}
are given by
b_{1}\cdot(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n+1})\cdot b_{2}:=b_{1}x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n+1}b_{2}
for all
b_{1},b_{2},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n+1}\in B
. In particular,
B^{\otimes(n+1)}
is faithful and essential
B
-Hilbert module.

2.2.
Finite quantum spaces

Let
B
be a dimensional
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
B
and let
\psi\colon B\to\mathbb{C}
be a faithful state. Then we obtain a Hilbert space
L^{2}(B)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}L^{2}(B,\psi)
by equipping
B
with the inner product
\langle x,y\rangle=\psi(x^{*}y)
. Faithfulness of
\psi
ensures
L^{2}(B)=B
. The multiplication map
m\colon B\otimes B\to B
is viewed as a linear operator
L^{2}(B)\otimes L^{2}(B)\to L^{2}(B)
, and
m^{*}\colon L^{2}(B)\to L^{2}(B)\otimes L^{2}(B)
is the Hilbert space adjoint
m
. For a given
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, a state
\psi\colon B\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be a
\delta
-form
if
mm^{*}=\delta^{2}\textup{id}_{B}
.

This gives us the standard prototype for
finite quantum spaces
as pairs
(B,\psi)
, consisting of a finite dimensional
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
B
and a faithful
\delta
-form
\psi\colon B\to\mathbb{C}
.

Since
B
is finite dimensional, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
n_{1},\cdots,n_{N}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
B=\oplus_{a=1}^{N}M_{n_{a}}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
\{e_{kl}^{a}\mid k,l=1,\cdots,n_{a},a=1,\cdots,N\}
be a set of standard matrix units. Without of loss of generality, we assume that the density matrix with respect to the usual trace
Q=\oplus_{a=1}^{N}Q^{(a)}
is diagonal, with the
k
-th entry of
Q^{(a)}
being
\psi(e_{kk}^{a})
. Furthermore,
\psi
is a
\delta
-form if and only if
Q^{(a)}
is invertible and
\sum_{k=1}^{n_{a}}\psi(e_{kk}^{a})^{-1}=\delta^{2}
for all
a=1,\cdots,N
. The analytic extension of the modular automorphism group for
\psi
is defined by
\sigma_{z}^{\psi}(b)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}Q^{iz}bQ^{-iz}
for all
b\in B
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Recall the adapted matrix units
\{f_{kl}^{a}\mid k,l=1,\cdots,n_{a},a=1,\cdots,N\}
for the quantum space
(B,\psi)
from
[
BHINW2023a
]
. They are defined by

(2.2)

f_{kl}^{a}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\psi(e_{kk}^{a})\psi(e_{ll}^{a})}}e_{kl}^{a},\qquad\text{$1\leq k,l\leq n_{a},1\leq a\leq N$.}

Furthermore, they satisfy the following relations:

(2.3)

\displaystyle m^{*}(f_{kl}^{a}):=\sum_{r=1}^{n_{a}}f^{a}_{kr}\otimes f^{a}_{rl},

\displaystyle\qquad f^{a}_{kl}f^{b}_{rs}:=\delta_{a,b}\delta_{l,r}\frac{1}{\psi(e^{a}_{ll})}f_{ks}^{a}.

We also note that
1_{B}=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{a}}\psi(e_{kk}^{a})f_{kk}^{a}
and

\sigma_{-i}^{\psi}(f_{kl}^{a})=\frac{\psi(e_{kk}^{a})}{\psi(e_{ll}^{a})}f_{kl}^{a}
for all
k,l=1,\cdots,n_{a}
,
N=1,\cdots,N
.

3.
Quantum path indicators and path correspondences

Let
(B,\psi)
be a finite quantum space with a
\delta
-form
\psi
. A linear operator
A\colon L^{2}(B)\to L^{2}(B)
is said to be a
quantum adjacency matrix
if

(3.1)

m(A\otimes A)m^{*}=\delta^{2}A.

A
finite directed quantum graph

\mathcal{G}
is a triple
(B,\psi,A)
consisting of a finite quantum space
(B,\psi)
and a
quantum adjacency matrix

A\colon L^{2}(B)\to L^{2}(B)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{G}=(B,\psi,A)
is a finite directed quantum graph. Define
T\colon B\otimes B\to B
by

(3.2)

T\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}m\circ\chi\circ(\sigma^{\psi}_{-i}\otimes\textup{id}_{B}),\qquad\text{where $\chi$ is the tensor flip.}

Then
T^{*}=(\sigma_{-i}^{\psi}\otimes\textup{id}_{B})\circ\chi\circ m^{*}
is the Hilbert space adjoint of the linear operator
T\colon L^{2}(B)\otimes L^{2}(B)\to L^{2}(B)
.

Definition 3.3
.

Suppose
k
is a positive integer. The
k-length quantum path indicator

\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{k}\in B^{\otimes(k+1)}
is defined recursively as follows:

(3.4)

\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{0}=1_{B},\quad\text{and}\quad\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{k}=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes(k-1)}}\otimes(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)T^{*})\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{k-1}\in B^{\otimes(k+1)}\quad\text{for $k\geq 1$}.

The
k-length quantum path space
is defined by

(3.5)

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}^{k}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\text{span}\{(x\otimes 1_{B})\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{k}(1_{B}\otimes y)\mid x,y\in B^{\otimes k}\}\subseteq B^{\otimes(k+1)}.

We recall the edge indicator
\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{G}
is defined by
\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)m^{*}(1_{B})\in B\otimes B
and the edge correspondence
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}B\cdot\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\cdot B\subseteq B\otimes B
from
[
BHINW2023a
]
.
In particular, we get
\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{1}=\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)T^{*}(1_{B})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(\sigma_{-i}^{\psi}\otimes A)\chi\left(\sum_{a=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{a}}\psi(e_{kk}^{a})m^{*}(f_{kk}^{a})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)\left(\sum_{a=1}^{N}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{a}}\psi(e_{kk}^{a})\sigma_{-i}^{\psi}(f_{lk}^{a})\otimes f_{kl}^{a}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)\left(\sum_{a=1}^{N}\sum_{l,k=1}^{n_{a}}\psi(e_{ll}^{a})f_{lk}^{a}\otimes f_{kl}^{a}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)m^{*}(1_{B}).

Subsequently,
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}^{1}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}
is the edge correspondence, which is a
\textup{C}^{*}
-subcorrespondence of
B\otimes B
over
B
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}^{k}
is a
\textup{C}^{*}
-subcorrespondence of
B^{\otimes(k+1)}
over
B
for every positive integer
k
.

3.1.
Properties of path indicators and correspondences

In this section, we record basic properties of quantum path indicators and quantum path spaces.

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose
k
is a positive integer. Then
A^{k}(x)=\delta^{2^{k}}(\psi^{\otimes k}\otimes\textup{id}_{B})(x\cdot\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{k})
for all
x\in B
.

Proof.

The statement for
k=1
is proved in
[
BHINW2023a
]
*Proposition 2.3 (1). Suppose the statement holds true for
k=n
. For

x,y\in B
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\delta^{2^{n+1}}(\psi^{\otimes(n+1)}\otimes 1_{B})(x\cdot\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{n+1}),y\rangle_{\psi}

\displaystyle=\delta^{2^{n+1}}\langle\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{n+1},x^{*}\otimes 1_{B}^{\otimes n}\otimes y\rangle_{\psi^{\otimes(n+2)}}

\displaystyle=\delta^{2^{n}}\langle(\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes n}}\otimes(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)T^{*})\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{n},x^{*}\otimes 1_{B}^{\otimes n}\otimes y\rangle_{\psi^{\otimes(n+2)}}

\displaystyle=\delta^{2^{n}}\langle\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{n},x^{*}\otimes 1_{B}^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes T(1_{B}\otimes A^{*}(y))\rangle_{\psi^{\otimes(n+1)}}

\displaystyle=\delta^{2^{n}}\langle\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{n},x^{*}\otimes 1_{B}^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes A^{*}(y))\rangle_{\psi^{\otimes(n+1)}}

\displaystyle=\langle\delta^{2^{n}}(\psi^{\otimes n}\otimes 1_{B})(x\cdot\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{n}),A^{*}(y)\rangle_{\psi}=\langle A^{n}(x),A^{*}(y)\rangle_{\psi}=\langle A^{n+1}(x),y\rangle_{\psi}.\qed

For any pair of positive integers
(r,s)
, define

\iota_{r,s}\colon B^{\otimes(r+1)}\otimes B^{\otimes(s+1)}\to B^{\otimes(r+s+1)}
by

(3.7)

\iota_{r,s}(x\otimes y)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}(\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes r}}\otimes T\otimes\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes s}})(x\otimes y),\quad\text{for all $x\in B^{\otimes(r+1)}$, $y\in B^{\otimes(s+1)}$.}

In the following, we find an alternative description of quantum path indicators.

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose
k
is a non-negative integer. Then
\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{k+1}=\iota_{1,k}(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{k})
.

Proof.

The statement is trivial for
k=0
. For the remaining cases, we proceed by induction. Let the matrix coefficients
\{A^{rsb}_{kla}\mid k,l=1\cdots,n_{a},r,s=1,\cdots,n_{b},a,b=1,\cdots,N\}
of
A
with respect to the adapted matrix units
\{f_{kl}^{a}\mid kl,l=1,\cdots,n_{a},a=1,\cdots,N\}
be given by

A(f_{kl}^{a})\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\sum_{b=1}^{N}\sum_{r,s=1}^{n_{b}}A_{kla}^{rsb}f_{rs}^{b}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)m^{*}(1_{B})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\sum_{a=1}^{N}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{a}}\psi(e^{a}_{kk})f_{kl}^{a}\otimes A(f_{lk}^{a})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{a}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{n_{b}}\psi(e^{a}_{kk})A_{lka}^{rsb}f_{kl}^{a}\otimes f_{rs}^{b}.

Using it, we compute

\displaystyle\iota_{1,1}(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{4}}\iota_{1,1}\left(\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{a}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{n_{b}}\psi(e^{a}_{kk})A_{lka}^{rsb}f_{kl}^{a}\otimes f_{rs}^{b}\otimes\sum_{c=1}^{N}\sum_{x,y=1}^{n_{c}}\psi(e_{xx}^{c})f_{xy}^{c}\otimes A(f_{yx}^{c})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{4}}\sum_{a,b,c=1}^{N}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{a}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{n_{b}}\sum_{x,y=1}^{n_{c}}\psi(e^{a}_{kk})\psi(e_{xx}^{c})A_{lka}^{rsb}f_{kl}^{a}\otimes f_{xy}^{c}\sigma_{-i}^{\psi}(f_{rs}^{b})\otimes A(f_{yx}^{c})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{4}}\sum_{a,b,c=1}^{N}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{a}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{n_{b}}\sum_{x,y=1}^{n_{c}}\frac{\psi(e^{a}_{kk})\psi(e_{xx}^{c})\psi(e_{rr}^{b})}{\psi(e_{ss}^{b})}A_{lka}^{rsb}f_{kl}^{a}\otimes f_{xy}^{c}f_{rs}^{b}\otimes A(f_{yx}^{c})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{4}}\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{a}}\sum_{r,s,x=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{\psi(e^{a}_{kk})\psi(e_{xx}^{b})}{\psi(e_{ss}^{b})}A_{lka}^{rsb}f_{kl}^{a}\otimes f_{xs}^{b}\otimes A(f_{rx}^{b})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{4}}\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{a}}\sum_{r,s,x=1}^{n_{b}}\psi(e_{kk}^{a})A^{rsb}_{lka}f_{kl}^{a}\otimes\sigma_{-i}^{\psi}(f_{xs}^{b})\otimes A(f_{rx}^{b})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{4}}\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{a}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{n_{b}}\psi(e_{kk}^{a})A^{rsb}_{lka}f_{kl}^{a}\otimes(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)T^{*}(f_{rs}^{b})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{4}}\sum_{a=1}^{N}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{a}}\psi(e_{kk}^{a})f_{kl}^{a}\otimes(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)T^{*}(A(f_{lk}^{a}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{4}}\left(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)T^{*}\right)\left((\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)m^{*}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{a}}\psi(e_{kk}^{a})f_{kk}^{a}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)T^{*})\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}=\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}.

Hence, the statement holds true for
k=1
. Suppose the statement holds true for
k=n
. We compute

\displaystyle\iota_{1,n+1}(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{n+1})

\displaystyle=(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes T\otimes\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes(n+1)}})\left(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes n}}\otimes(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)T^{*})\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes(n+1)}}\otimes(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)T^{*})\left(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes T\otimes\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes n}})(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes(n+1)}}\otimes(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes A)T^{*})\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{n+1}=\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{n+2};

the first and the fourth equalities use (
3.4
), the second equality is trivial, and the third equality uses (
3.7
) and the the induction hypothesis.
∎

The above description of quantum path indicators becomes particularly useful because it provides a convenient framework for defining the composition of quantum path indicators and the associated quantum path correspondences. We conclude this section with a proof of the following result concerning the composition of quantum path indicators and quantum path correspondences.

Theorem 3.9
.

Suppose
r,s
are positive integers. Then

(1)

\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{r+s}=\iota_{r,s}(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{r}\otimes\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{s})

(2)

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}^{r+s}=\iota_{r,s}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}^{r}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}^{s})
.

Proof.

Firstly, we prove by induction, that for every positive integer
r

(3.10)

\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{r}=(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes T^{\otimes(r-1)}\otimes\textup{id}_{B})(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes r})\in B^{\otimes(r+1)}.

For
r=1
, the equality is trivial. Assume for
r=r^{\prime}
the equation (
3.10
) holds true.
Using (
3.8
) together with the induction hypothesis in the following computation, we obtain (
3.10
) for

.

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{r^{\prime}+1}=\iota_{1,r^{\prime}}(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{r^{\prime}+1})

\displaystyle=(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes T\otimes\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes r^{\prime}}})(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{r^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes T\otimes\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes r^{\prime}}})\left(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes T^{\otimes(r^{\prime}-1)}\otimes\textup{id}_{B})\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{r^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes T\otimes T^{\otimes(r^{\prime}-1)}\otimes\textup{id}_{B})(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes r^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes T^{\otimes r^{\prime}}\otimes\textup{id}_{B})(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes(r^{\prime}+1)}).

Then combining (
3.8
) and (
3.7
) below we prove the statement
(1)
.

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{r+s}=(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes T^{\otimes(r+s-1)}\otimes\textup{id}_{B})(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes(r+s)})

\displaystyle=(\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes r}}\otimes T\otimes\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes s}})\left((\textup{id}_{B}\otimes T^{\otimes(r-1)}\otimes\textup{id}_{B})(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes r})\otimes(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes T^{\otimes(s-1)}\otimes\textup{id}_{B})(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes s})\right)

\displaystyle=(\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes r}}\otimes T\otimes\textup{id}_{B^{\otimes s}})(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{r}\otimes\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{s})=\iota_{r,s}(\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{r}\otimes\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}^{s}).

Next we prove by induction that for every positive integer
r

(3.11)

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}^{r}=(\textup{id}_{B}\otimes T^{\otimes(r-1)}\otimes\textup{id}_{B})(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes r})\subseteq B^{\otimes(r+1)}.

For
r=1
, the equality is trivial. Assume for
r=r^{\prime}
we the equation (
3.11
) holds true. Using
(
3.5
) together with the induction hypothesis in the following computation, we obtain (
3.11
)
for
r=r^{\prime}+1
