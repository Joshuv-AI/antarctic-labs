Low Mach number limit and optimal time decay rates of the compressible Navier-Stokes-transport system in critical Besov spaces Thanks: ∗ ​ Corresponding author
Fucai Li, Jinkai Ni ∗, Yuzhu Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the Navier-Stokes-Transport (NST) system in the framework of Besov spaces. This system contains of a compressible Navier-Stokes system for the density and momentum of a fluid, and a transport equation for the potential temperature of the fluid. In stark contrast to the well-known Navier-Stokes-Fourier (NSF) system where the temperature satisfies a parabolic type equation providing dissipative effect for the temperature and the density, the temperature in our NST system enjoys a transport equation which precludes a dissipative mechanism for the density, leading to significant different effects to the whole system. We first establish the global well-posedness of strong solutions to the compressible NST system in critical Besov spaces over ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . Furthermore, by introducing the Mach number ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , we rigorously prove the low Mach number limit as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 , showing that the solutions converge to that of the incompressible inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes system. This singular limit holds globally in time, even for ill-prepared initial data. To address the challenge posed by the lack of dissipation on the density and temperature, we develop a refined energy analysis and establish optimal time decay rates for strong solutions in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 . Notably, the density remains uniformly bounded in time, displaying asymptotic behavior fundamentally distinct from that in the NSF system, where the density possesses a dissipative structure via the momentum and temperature equations and exhibits temporal decay.

Introduction and main results

1.1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate a fluid model
in the framework of Besov spaces. This model contains of a compressible Navier-Stokes system for the density and momentum of a fluid, and a transport equation for the potential temperature of the fluid. It describes the evolution of the fluid under the assumption that diabatic processes and molecular diffusion effects on potential temperature are neglected, and is widely used in meteorology and astrophysics
(see
[
27
,
2
,
37
]
and references therein). Mathematically, this model in
\mathbb{R}^{d}(d\geq 2)
can be formulated as follows (​​
[
45
]
):

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+{\rm div}\,(\rho u)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+{\rm div}\,(\rho u\otimes u)-\mu\Delta u-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla{\rm div}\,u+\nabla P(\rho,\theta)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho\theta)+{\rm div}\,(\rho\theta u)=0,\end{aligned}\right.

with the initial data

(1.2)

\displaystyle(\rho(t,x),u(t,x),\theta(t,x))|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0}(x),u_{0}(x),\theta_{0}(x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here, the unknown functions
\rho=\rho(t,x)>0
,
u=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\theta=\theta(t,x)>0
represent the density, velocity field, and potential temperature of the fluid, respectively, where
t>0
denotes time and
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes the spatial position. The scalar pressure
P=P(\rho,\theta)
is given by

\displaystyle P(\rho,\theta)=A(\rho\theta)^{\gamma},\quad A>0,\quad\gamma>1.

The parameters
\lambda
and
\mu>0
are the viscosity coefficients satisfying the physical condition
2\mu+\lambda>0
. In the earlier literature, the system (
1.1
) is usually referred as the
Navier-Stokes equations with entropy transport
, for example, see
[
52
,
48
]
. Due to the link between potential temperature and entropy, to emphasize the effect of potential temperature, the system (
1.1
) is
renamed as
Navier-Stokes equations with potential temperature transport
in
[
45
]
. In current paper, we call the system (
1.1
) as

Navier-Stokes-Transport
(

)
system
for presentation brevity.

Due to its physical importance, complexity, and mathematical challenges, there have been a lot of studies on the NST system (
1.1

\mathbb{R}^{3}

[

, which addressed the case
\gamma\geq 9/5
. Maltese et al.
[

investigated global weak solutions to the NST system under the conditions
\gamma>3/2
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or

\mathbb{R}^{2}

Later, the concept of dissipative measure-valued (DMV) solutions for the system (

There are also a few results on the singular limits to the compressible NST system (

Until now, the singular limit to the physically relevant case

Based on the aforementioned results on the system (
1.1
), the objectives of this paper are two folds: (1) We establish the global well-posedness and optimal decay rates of the strong solutions to the system (
1.1
) in the framework of

.
(2) We investigate the low Mach number limit of (
1.1

By criticality, we mean that the scaling transformation preserving the norms of the function space in which the solution lies leaves (
1.1
) invariant. This central idea, originating from the work of Fujita and Kato
[
29

, asserts that the “optimal” function spaces for the well-posedness of system (
1.1

\displaystyle\rho_{\nu}(t,x)=\rho(\nu^{2}t,\nu x),\quad u_{\nu}(t,x)=\nu u(\nu^{2}t,\nu x),\quad\theta_{\nu}(t,x)=\theta(\nu^{2}t,\nu x),

along with the corresponding scaling of the initial data:

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(x)\leadsto\rho_{0}(\nu x),\quad u_{0}(x)\leadsto\nu u_{0}(\nu x),\quad\theta_{0}(x)\leadsto\nu\theta_{0}(\nu x),

for any
\nu>0
. It is then evident that
\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}\times\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\times\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}
constitutes a critical space for the system (
1.1
).

In order to state our results, we fist give some analyses on the system (
1.1
). To address the difficulties from the strong nonlinearity of the pressure in the momentum equation(
1.1
)
2
, following
[
59
]
, we combine the equations for
\rho
and
\theta

1.1

\rho
,

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+{\rm div}\,(\rho u)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+{\rm div}\,(\rho u\otimes u)-\mu\Delta u-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla{\rm div}\,u+\nabla P=0,\\
&\partial_{t}P+u\cdot\nabla P+\gamma P{\rm div}\,u=0.\end{aligned}\right.

It is evident that the system (
1.3
) constitutes a hyperbolic-parabolic system, where (
1.3
)
1
and (
1.3
)
3
are transport equations, and dissipation arises only from
u
. We consider the initial value problem for (
1.3
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
, subject to the initial data

(1.4)

\displaystyle(\rho,u,P)(0,x)=(\rho_{0},u_{0},P_{0})(x)\to(\rho_{\infty},0,P_{\infty})\quad\text{as}\quad|x|\to\infty,

where
\rho_{\infty}>0
and
P_{\infty}>0
are positive constants.
For the sake of simplicity, we set
\rho_{\infty}=P_{\infty}=1
.
By setting
a=\rho-1
and
z=P-1
, we rewrite system (
1.3
) as

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}a+{\rm div}\,u=-{\rm div}\,(au),\\
&\partial_{t}u-\mu\Delta u-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla{\rm div}\,u+\nabla z\\
&\quad=-u\cdot\nabla u-f(a)\nabla z+f(a)(\mu\Delta u+(\mu+\lambda)\nabla{\rm div}\,u),\\
&\partial_{t}z+\gamma{\rm div}\,u=-u\cdot\nabla z-\gamma z{\rm div}\,u,\end{aligned}\right.

with the initial data

(1.6)

\displaystyle(a(t,x),u(t,x),z(t,x))|_{t=0}=(a_{0}(x),u_{0}(x),z_{0}(x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
f(a)
is defined by

\displaystyle f(a)=-\frac{a}{1+a}.

Let
\omega=\gamma a-z
. From the equations (
1.5
)
1
and (
1.5
)
3
, it follows that

(1.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega+\omega{\rm div}\,u-(\gamma-1)z{\rm div}\,u=0,

which yields a new transport equation for the derived quantity
\omega
. Although
\omega
does not possess a dissipative structure, it provides a crucial link between the unknowns
a
and
z
, thereby overcoming the difficulty introduced by the additional linear term
\mathrm{div}\,u
in (
1.5
)
1
and (
1.5
)
3
.

In this paper, we shall also investigate the asymptotic behavior of (
1.3
) in the low Mach number regime. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
be the Mach number, a dimensionless parameter.
Inspired by
[
19
, Section 5.1]
and
[
21
]
, we consider the following change of variables:

(1.8)

\displaystyle(a,u,z)(t,x):=\varepsilon(a^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon},z^{\varepsilon})(\varepsilon^{2}t,\varepsilon x),

along with the corresponding transformation of the initial data:

(1.9)

\displaystyle(a_{0},u_{0},z_{0})(x):=\varepsilon(a^{\varepsilon}_{0},u^{\varepsilon}_{0},z^{\varepsilon}_{0})(\varepsilon x).

In addition,
\mu=\varepsilon\bar{\mu}
,
\lambda=\varepsilon\bar{\lambda}
, where
\bar{\mu}
and
\bar{\lambda}
are constants independent of
\varepsilon
.
It is straightforward to verify that
(a^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon},z^{\varepsilon})
satisfies the following scaled compressible NST system:

(1.10)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}a^{\varepsilon}+\frac{{\rm div}\,u^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}+{\rm div}\,(a^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}\big((1+\varepsilon a^{\varepsilon})u^{\varepsilon}\big)+{\rm div}\,((1+\varepsilon a^{\varepsilon})u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon})+\frac{\nabla z^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\\
&\quad=\bar{\mu}\Delta u^{\varepsilon}+(\bar{\mu}+\bar{\lambda})\nabla{\rm div}\,u^{\varepsilon},\\
&\partial_{t}z^{\varepsilon}+\frac{\gamma{\rm div}\,u^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}+u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla z^{\varepsilon}+\gamma z^{\varepsilon}{\rm div}\,u^{\varepsilon}=0,\\
&(a^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon},z^{\varepsilon})|_{t=0}:=(a^{\varepsilon}_{0},u^{\varepsilon}_{0},z^{\varepsilon}_{0}).\end{aligned}\right.

Assume that
a_{0}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}
,
u_{0}^{\varepsilon}
and
z_{0}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{P_{0}^{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}
are uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
in some suitable sense. Passing to the limits
(a^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon},z^{\varepsilon})
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

and assuming that
(\varrho,v,0)
is the limit, then
it is readily to check that
(\varrho,v)
satisfies the following incompressible inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes system:

(1.11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}(\varrho v)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v-\bar{\mu}\Delta v+\nabla\Pi=0,\\
&{\rm div}\,v=0,\end{aligned}\right.

with the initial data

(1.12)

\displaystyle(\varrho(0,x),v(0,x))=(\varrho_{0}(x),v_{0}(x))\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad|x|\rightarrow\infty.

This process corresponds to the well-known low Mach number limit, which is both a mathematically challenging and physically significant problem. Formally, due to the appearance of the singular terms
\frac{\nabla z^{\varepsilon}}{{\varepsilon}}
and
\frac{{\rm div}u^{\varepsilon}}{{\varepsilon}}
in (
1.10
), it is difficult to derive uniform estimates with respect to the Mach number
\varepsilon
and to rigorously establish convergence to the incompressible
inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes equations (
1.11
).

Let’s mention that there have been extensive studies on the mathematical justification of the low Mach number limit in the framework of Sobolev spaces, initiated by Ebin
[
23
]
and Klainerman-Majda
[
36
,
35
]
for local strong solutions of the compressible Navier-Stokes and Euler equations in the whole space or a torus with well-prepared initial data.
These foundational results were subsequently extended by numerous authors in various settings, particularly for the isentropic case, over the past few decades; see, for example,
[
3
,
55
,
33
]
and the references cited therein.
However, fluid dynamics equations are inherently coupled with thermodynamic variables such as temperature or entropy. Therefore, it is both natural and significant to investigate the low Mach number limit for non-isentropic fluid systems. In this context, the limit process becomes substantially more complex due to the intricate coupling among the velocity, pressure, and thermal fields.
A major breakthrough on low Mach number limit for the non-isentropic Euler equations with ill-prepared initial data was achieved by Métivier and Schochet
[
51
]
, who achieved this goal by employing advanced tools such as pseudo-differential operators and wave packet transforms. Further developments in this direction can be found,
for example, in
[
1
,
25
,
26
,
34
]
and the cited references therein.

In the study of slightly compressible fluids, identifying an appropriate functional framework is a crucial aspect, as it naturally leads to the search for more suitable function spaces when considering the low Mach number limit. Regarding the low Mach number limit for the isentropic Navier-Stokes equations in critical Besov spaces, one may refer to
[
16
]
for the case of periodic boundary conditions and to
[
17
]
for the whole space. See also
[
21
]
on non-isentropic fluid equations. Very recently, Fujii
[

established the low Mach number limit for the isentropic Navier-Stokes equations in a scaling-critical Besov space, where it was emphasized that the method used to prove the convergence does not require distinct arguments depending on the spatial dimension.

Main results

Remark 1.3
.

In stark contrast to

1.3.
Strategies in our proofs

Different from the classical compressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier system studied
[
49
,
50
,
15
]
,
where the density

exhibits a dissipative structure and possesses a long-time decay rate, in our compressible NST system (

This absence poses a significant challenge in establishing the global well-posedness and time decay rates
of solutions to the system (
1.5
).

In the proof of Theorem
1.2
, in contrast to the approaches developed in
[
12
,
14
]
and
[
19
, Section 4]
, where the orthogonal projectors
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
are applied to decompose the velocity field into divergence-free and potential components, respectively,
we estimate the incompressible part
\mathbb{P}u
and the potential part
\mathbb{Q}u
separately in
both low and high frequency regimes
. In particular, for the high-frequency component, the effective velocity method introduced in
[
21
,
31
]
is employed.
In this paper, we treat the subsystem (
1.5
)
2
–(
1.5
)
3
as a
new
hyperbolic-parabolic system in the variables
(u,z)
. By applying hyperbolic-parabolic theory in
[
54
]
, we construct a frequency-localized Lyapunov inequality (see (

)–(
3.12
)), which captures the dissipative behavior of

across different frequency ranges: at low frequencies

,
(u,z)
behaves like a heat-diffusive system, while at high frequencies

, it exhibits a damping effect.
Furthermore, certain nonlinear terms such as
f(a)\nabla z
,
f(a)\Delta u
, and
f(a)\nabla{\rm div}\,u
, lead to a loss of derivatives due to the absence of a dissipative structure in the density

. This necessitates more refined estimates for both the high- and low-frequency components of the solution.

On the other hand, for the remaining subsystem (
1.5
)
1
, the main difficulty lies in establishing an estimate for
\|a\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}-1})}
, as the linear term
{\rm div}\,u
poses a significant obstacle — particularly because
\|u\|_{L_{t}^{1}(\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}})}
cannot be controlled. To overcome this issue, we analyze the equation (
1.7
) governing the new variable
\omega=\gamma a-z
, and establish an estimate for

in the space

(see Lemma

. Consequently, we obtain uniform a priori estimates with critical regularity. Combined with the local well-posedness result established in Theorem

To prove Theorem

17

converges to (

For the remaining subsystem (

1.4.
Structure of this paper

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we introduce the necessary preliminaries on notations, the Littlewood–Paley decomposition, and certain mixed Besov spaces.
In Section 3, we establish a priori estimates for the solution
(a,u,z)
to
prove the global well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (

)–(
1.6
).
In Section 4, we rigorously justify the low Mach number limits stated in Theorem

.
In Section 5, we derive the optimal decay rates of

in

-type Besov norms.
For the upper bound estimates of
(u,z)
, we first derive decay estimates at low regularity; furthermore, under a smallness assumption on the initial data
(u_{0},z_{0})
, we obtain decay estimates at higher regularity.
For the lower bound analysis, we first employ spectral analysis to establish pointwise estimates of the solution at low frequencies, and then combine linear and nonlinear analysis to derive the time decay rates of
(u,z)
.
Finally, Appendix A collects several fundamental properties of Besov spaces and essential technical lemmas.

cB\leq A\leq CB
. For any Banach space
X
and functions
f,g\in X
, we define
\|(f,g)\|_{X}:=\|f\|_{X}+\|g\|_{X}
. For any
T>0
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
L^{p}(0,T;X)
denotes the space of measurable functions
f:[0,T]\to X
such that the mapping
t\mapsto\|f(t)\|_{X}
belongs to
L^{p}(0,T)
, equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(0,T;X)}=:\|\cdot\|_{L_{T}^{p}(X)}
. Let
\mathcal{F}(f)=\widehat{f}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}(f)=\check{f}
denote the Fourier transform of
f
and its inverse, respectively.

2.2.
Littlewood-Paley decomposition

We now recall the Littlewood–Paley decomposition. For further details, interested reader can refer to
[
4
, Chapters 2–3]
. Let
\chi(\xi)
be a smooth, radial, non-increasing function supported in
B(0,\frac{4}{3})
such that
\chi(\xi)=1
for all
\xi\in B(0,\frac{3}{4})
. Define
\phi(\xi):=\chi\big(\frac{\xi}{2}\big)-\chi(\xi)
. Then the function
\phi
satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{-k}\xi)=1\quad\text{for all }\xi\neq 0,\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{supp}\,\phi\subset\Big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,\Big|\,\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\Big\}.

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the homogeneous dyadic block
\dot{\Delta}_{k}
is defined by

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{k}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\phi(2^{-k}\cdot)\mathcal{F}(f)\big)=2^{kd}h(2^{k}\cdot)\ast f,\quad\text{where}\quad h:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\phi.

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the class of all polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}/\mathcal{P}
represent the
tempered distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
modulo polynomials. Then for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}
, one gets

\displaystyle f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\dot{\Delta}_{k}f,\quad\text{where}\quad\dot{\Delta}_{k}\dot{\Delta}_{l}=0\quad{\rm if}\quad|k-l|\geq 2.

With the aid of those dyadic blocks, we next present the following definition of homogeneous Besov spaces and mixed space-time Besov spaces.

2.3.
Besov spaces and Chemin-Lerner spaces

Definition 2.1
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
, the homogeneous Besov spaces
\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}
are defined as

\displaystyle\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}:=\big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}\,\big|\,\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}:=\big\|\{2^{ks}\|\dot{\Delta}_{k}u\|_{L^{p}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|_{l^{r}}<\infty\big\}.

It is natural to recall the class of mixed space-time Besov spaces introduced by Chemin and Lerner
[
11
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

For
T>0
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq\varkappa,p,r\leq\infty
, the homogeneous Chemin-Lerner spaces
\widetilde{L}^{\kappa}(0,T;\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})
are defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}^{\varkappa}(0,T;\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})=\big\{f\in L^{\varkappa}(0,T;\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h})\,\big|\,\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\varkappa}(0,T;\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}:=\big\|\{2^{ks}\|\dot{\Delta}_{k}f\|_{L^{\varkappa}_{T}(L^{p})}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|_{l^{r}}<\infty\big\}.

By applying Minkowski’s inequality, one has

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\varkappa}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\varkappa}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}\quad{\rm if}\quad\varkappa\leq r;\quad\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\varkappa}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}\geq\|f\|_{L^{\varkappa}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}\quad{\rm if}\quad\varkappa\geq r.

where
\|\cdot\|_{L_{T}^{\varkappa}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}
is the usual Lebesgue-Besov norm. Moreover, we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}):=\big\{f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})\,\big|\,\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}<\infty\big\}.

Restricting Besov norms to the low- and high-frequency components of distributions is central to our approach. We consistently use the following notation for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\!\!\|f\|^{\ell}_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}:=\big\|\{2^{ks}\|\dot{\Delta}_{k}f\|_{L^{p}}\}_{k\leq 0}\big\|_{l^{r}},&\|f\|^{\ell}_{\widetilde{L}^{\varkappa}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}:=\big\|\{2^{ks}\|\dot{\Delta}_{k}f\|_{L^{\varkappa}_{T}(L^{p})}\}_{k\leq 0}\big\|_{l^{r}},\\
\!\!\|f\|^{h}_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}:=\big\|\{2^{ks}\|\dot{\Delta}_{k}f\|_{L^{p}}\}_{k\geq-1}\big\|_{l^{r}},&\|f\|^{h}_{\widetilde{L}^{\varkappa}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}:=\big\|\{2^{ks}\|\dot{\Delta}_{k}f\|_{L^{\varkappa}_{T}(L^{p})}\}_{k\geq-1}\big\|_{l^{r}}.\end{array}\right.

Define

\displaystyle f^{\ell}:=\sum_{k\leq-1}\dot{\Delta}_{k}f,\quad f^{h}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}\dot{\Delta}_{k}f.

For any
s^{\prime}>0
, it holds that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\!\!\|f^{\ell}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}^{\ell}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s-s^{\prime}}}^{\ell},&\|f^{h}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}^{h}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s+s^{\prime}}}^{h},\\
\!\!\|f^{\ell}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\varkappa}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\varkappa}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}^{\ell}\lesssim\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\varkappa}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s-s^{\prime}})}^{\ell},&\|f^{h}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\varkappa}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\varkappa}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}^{h}\lesssim\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\varkappa}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s+s^{\prime}})}^{h}.\end{array}\right.

In addition, we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}\cap\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s^{\prime}}}=\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}^{\ell}+\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s^{\prime}}}^{h},\quad s<s^{\prime},\\
&\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}+\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s^{\prime}}}=\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}^{\ell}+\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s^{\prime}}}^{h},\quad s>s^{\prime}.\end{aligned}\right.

Finally, to investigate the low Mach number limit of the scaled NST system (
1.10
) as it converges to the incompressible inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes equations (
1.11
) , we introduce several auxiliary notations. For any function
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define

\displaystyle f^{\ell,\varepsilon}:=\sum_{2^{k}\varepsilon\leq 2^{k_{0}}}\dot{\Delta}_{k}f,\quad f^{h,\varepsilon}:=\sum_{2^{k}\varepsilon>2^{k_{0}}}\dot{\Delta}_{k}f,

where
k_{0}
is a sufficiently large nonnegative integer depending only on the spatial dimension
d
. Additionally, we introduce the following norms:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{s}}^{\ell,\varepsilon}:=\sum_{2^{k}\varepsilon\leq 2^{k_{0}}}2^{ks}\|\dot{\Delta}_{k}f\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{s}}^{h,\varepsilon}:=\sum_{2^{k}\varepsilon\geq 2^{k_{0}}}2^{ks}\|\dot{\Delta}_{k}f\|_{L^{2}}.

,
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Define the ball
\mathcal{B}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}~|~|\xi|\leq R\}
and the annulus
\mathcal{C}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}~|~\lambda_{1}r\leq|\xi|\leq\lambda_{1}R\}
. For any
u\in L^{p}
and
\lambda_{1}>0
, it holds that

\left\{\begin{aligned} {\rm{supp}}\,\mathcal{F}(u)\subset&\lambda_{1}\mathcal{B}\Rightarrow\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{m+d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}{})}\|u\|_{L^{p}},\\
{\rm{supp}}\,\mathcal{F}(u)\subset&\lambda_{1}\mathcal{C}\Rightarrow\lambda_{1}^{m}\|u\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{m}\|u\|_{L^{p}}.\end{aligned}\right.

By virtue of Lemma
A.1
, the following properties of Besov spaces can be readily established.

Lemma A.2
.

(​​
[
4
, Chapter 2]
)

The following properties hold:

•

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty
and
1\leq r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq\infty
, it holds that

\displaystyle\dot{B}^{s}_{p_{1},r_{1}}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{s-d(\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{p_{2}})}_{p_{2},r_{2}}.

•

For
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
, we have the chain of continuous embedding as follows:

\displaystyle\dot{B}^{0}_{p,1}\hookrightarrow L^{p}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{0}_{p,\infty}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{q,\infty}^{\varsigma},\quad\varsigma=-d\Big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\Big).

•

If
p<\infty
, then
\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}
is continuously embedded in the set of continuous functions decaying to 0 at infinity.

•

The following real interpolation property is satisfied for
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
s_{1}<s_{2}
, and
\theta\in(0,1)
:

(A.1)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\theta s_{1}+(1-\theta)s_{2}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\theta(1-\theta)(s_{2}-s_{1})}\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{s_{1}}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{p,1}^{s_{2}}}^{1-\theta}.

•

For any
\lambda_{1}>0
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
, the following scaling relationship holds:

(A.2)

\displaystyle\|u(\lambda_{1}\cdot)\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}\backsim\lambda_{1}^{s-\frac{d}{p}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}.

•

For any
\varepsilon>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle H^{s+\varepsilon}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{s}_{2,1}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{s}.

•

Let
\Lambda^{\sigma}
be defined by
\Lambda^{\sigma}:=(-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}u:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(|\xi|^{\sigma}\mathcal{F}(u)\big)
for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in{\mathcal{S}}^{{}^{\prime}}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then
\Lambda^{\sigma}
is an isomorphism from
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}
to
\dot{B}^{s-\sigma}_{p,r}
.

•

Let
1\leq p_{1},p_{2},r_{1},r_{2}\leq\infty
,
s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

\displaystyle s_{2}<\frac{d}{p_{2}}\quad\text{\text{or}}\quad s_{2}=\frac{d}{p_{2}}~\text{and}~r_{2}=1.

Then the space
\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},r_{1}}\cap\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}
endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},r_{1}}}+\|\cdot\|_{\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}}
is a Banach space and has the weak compact and Fatou properties
:
If
u_{n}
is a uniformly bounded sequence of
\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},r_{1}}\cap\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}
, then an element
u
of
\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},r_{1}}\!\cap\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}
and a subsequence
u_{n_{k}}
exist such that
u_{n_{k}}\rightarrow u
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},r_{1}}\cap\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}}\lesssim\liminf_{n_{k}\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n_{k}}\|_{\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},r_{1}}\cap\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}}.\end{aligned}

To effectively control the nonlinear terms, we require the following Morse-type product estimates.

Lemma A.3
.

(​​

The following statements hold:

•

Let
s>0

1\leq p,r\leq\infty

\displaystyle\|uv\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}},

\displaystyle\|uv\|_{\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}}}\|v\|_{\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}}}.

Let the real numbers

,

and

\displaystyle\|uv\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s_{1}+s_{2}-\frac{d}{p}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}\|v\|_{\dot{B}_{p,1}^{s_{2}}}.

Let the real numbers

and

\displaystyle\|uv\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{s_{1}+s_{2}-\frac{d}{p}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}\|v\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{s_{2}}}.
