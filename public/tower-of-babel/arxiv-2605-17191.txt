Quantitative Stability for Minimizing Yamabe Metrics with minimal boundary
Runze Lin, Bao Yu

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the stability of minimizing Yamabe metrics on compact manifolds with boundary, in the sense introduced by Escobar. We show that if a function nearly minimizes the Yamabe energy, then the associated conformal metric is quantitatively close to a minimizing Yamabe metric within its conformal class. Moreover, this closeness is controlled by an appropriate power of the Yamabe energy deficit. Keywords: Yamabe metrics, quantitative stability, minimal boundary, Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction, Lojasiewicz distance inequality.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. The Yamabe problem is to find a metric
\tilde{g}
, conformal to
g
, such that the scalar curvature of
\tilde{g}
is a constant. Given a metric
\tilde{g}
conformal to
g
, i.e.
\tilde{g}=u^{4/(n-2)}g
for a smooth positive function
u
on
M
, the scalar curvature
R_{\tilde{g}}
of
\tilde{g}
is given in terms of
u
and the scalar curvature
R_{g}
of
g
by

(1)

R_{\tilde{g}}=u^{1-2^{*}}\left(-\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\Delta u+R_{g}u\right),

where
2^{*}=2n/(n-2)
. Hence, this problem is to find a positive solution to the following equation

(2)

-\Delta u+c_{n}R_{g}u=Cu^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}},

where
c_{n}=\frac{(n-2)}{4(n-1)}
. In particular, it can be viewed as a variational problem. Solutions to the Yamabe problem are critical points of the following Yamabe energy functional

(3)

Q(u)=\ \frac{\int_{M}|{\nabla}u|^{2}+c_{n}R_{g}u^{2}\,d{\rm{vol}}_{g}}{\left(\int_{M}u^{2^{*}}\,d{\rm{vol}}_{g}\right)^{2/2^{*}}}=\frac{c_{n}\int_{M}R_{\tilde{g}}\,d{\rm{vol}}_{\tilde{g}}}{{\rm{vol}}_{\tilde{g}}(M)^{2/2^{*}}}

and the Yamabe constant of
(M^{n},g)
is defined as

(4)

Y(M,[g])=\inf\{Q(u):u\in W^{1,2}(M),u\geq 0\}.

It is well known that
Y(M,[g])
is invariant under a conformal change of the metric
g
.

The Yamabe problem was solved by the combined efforts of the works of Yamabe
[
23
]
,
Trudinger
[
22
]
, Aubin
[
2
]
and Schoen
[
17
]
. Yamabe’s proof is based on the variational approach. In
[
23
]
, he attempted to prove that
Y(M,[g])
is always achieved. However, Trudinger
[
22
]
pointed out that Yamabe’s proof is correct only when the Yamabe constant
Y(M,[g])\leq 0
, and fixed it when
Y(M,[g])<\epsilon
for some constant
\epsilon
(depending on the Sobolev constant). In
[
2
]
, Aubin proved the existence under the condition

(5)

Y(M,[g])<Y(S^{n},[g_{0}]),

where
(S^{n},g_{0})
denotes the standard metric of the
n
-sphere. Aubin also verified (
5
) for the case
n\geq 6
and
(M,g)
is not locally conformally flat. Schoen
[
17
]
established (
5
) for the remaining cases by the positive mass problem (see
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
). A complete proof of the affirmative answer to the Yamabe problem was eventually obtained.

Once the existence of minimizers is solved, it is natural to ask the stability: if
Q(u)
is close to the infimum
Y(M,[g])
, to what extent will it affect the distance from
u
to the minimizers?

In
[
7
]
, Brezis and Lieb raised this question on the sphere, asking that given a function
u\in W^{1,2}(S^{n})
, whether the energy deficit
Q(u)-Y(S^{n},[g_{0}])
controls the distance from
u
to the family of minimizers. By means of explicit characterization of the minimizers on the round sphere by Aubin
[
3
]
and Talenti
[
21
]
(see also Obata
[
16
]
), Bianchi and Egnell give a positive answer: for any nonnegative
u\in W^{1,2}(S^{n})

(6)

Q_{(S^{n},g_{0})}(u)-Y(S^{n},[g_{0}])\geq c\left(\frac{\inf\left\{\|u-v\|_{W^{1,2}(S^{n})}\ |\ {v\in\mathcal{M}_{(S^{n},g_{0})}}\right\}}{\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(S^{n})}}\right)^{2},

where
\mathcal{M}
denotes all minimizers of the functional
Q
,
c
only depends on the dimension, and the exponent
2
is sharp under the

W^{1,2}
norm. However, for a general closed Riemannian manifold, the minimizers are not known in any explicit form. By elegantly integrating the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction with the Łojasiewicz distance inequality, Engelstein, Neumayer, and Spolaor
[
9
]
derived the following quantitative stability result.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
9
]

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a
C^{\infty}
closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
that is not conformally equivalent to the round sphere. There exist constants
c>0
and
{\gamma}\geq 0
, depending on
(M,g)
, such that

(7)

Q(u)-Y(M,[g])\geq c\,d(u,\mathcal{M})^{2+{\gamma}}\,\qquad\forall u\in W^{1,2}(M;{\mathbb{R}}_{+})\,.

Moreover, there exists an open dense subset
\mathcal{G}
in the
C^{2}
topology on the space of
C^{\infty}
-conformal classes of metrics on
M
such that if
[g]\in\mathcal{G}
, we may take
{\gamma}=0
.

Furthermore, their result is optimal, as the existence of manifolds with a strictly positive exponent
\gamma
is proven.

Motivated by the question mentioned above, our purpose in this paper is to establish a stability result for a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary.
From now on,
(M^{n},g)
denotes some smooth compact n-dimensional Riemannian manifold with boundary unless we specify otherwise. We use
L_{g}
to denote
-\Delta+c_{n}R_{g}
,
B_{g}

to denote
\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}+\frac{n-2}{2}h_{g}
,
\nu
to denote the outward unit normal vector on
\partial M
with respect to
g
, and
h_{g}
to denote the mean curvature of
\partial M
.
So, the Yamabe problem corresponding to
M
is to find a metric
\tilde{g}
, conformal to
g
, such that the scalar curvature of
\tilde{g}
and the mean curvature for
\partial M
of
\tilde{g}
are both constants.

Let
u>0
be some positive function on
M
, let
\tilde{g}=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g
, and calculate the mean curvature
h_{\tilde{g}}
as

(8)

h_{\tilde{g}}=\frac{2}{n-2}u^{-\frac{n}{n-2}}B_{g}u.

There are two types of Yamabe problem with boundary:

Type (i)
[
10
]
: find a metric
\tilde{g}
conformal to
g
such that the scalar curvature
R_{\tilde{g}}
is constant in

and the mean curvature

vanishes on
\partial M
.

Type (ii)
[
11
]
: find a metric
\tilde{g}
conformal to
g
such that the scalar curvature
R_{\tilde{g}}
vanishes in
M
and the mean curvature
h_{\tilde{g}}
is constant on
\partial M
.

Combined with (
1
), we can get the following two Yamabe equations corresponding to Type (i) and Type (ii)

(9)

\begin{cases}L_{g}u=Cu^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ \penalty\ M^{\circ},\\
B_{g}u=0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{on}\penalty\ \partial M,\\
\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ u\geq 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ M.\end{cases}

(10)

\begin{cases}L_{g}u=0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ \penalty\ M^{\circ},\\
B_{g}u=Cu^{\frac{n}{n-2}}\penalty\ \text{on}\penalty\ \partial M,\\
\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ u\geq 0\penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ M.\end{cases}

In this paper, we will discuss the Type (i) Yamabe problem. Compared to the case of the closed manifold, there is also an energy functional

(11)

Q(u)=\ \frac{\int_{M}|{\nabla}u|^{2}+c_{n}R_{g}u^{2}\,d{\rm{vol}}_{g}+\frac{n-2}{2}\int_{\partial M}h_{g}u^{2}d\sigma_{g}}{\left(\int_{M}u^{2^{*}}\,d{\rm{vol}}_{g}\right)^{2/2^{*}}}.

Moreover, the Yamabe constant is defined as

(12)

Y(M,\partial M,[g])=\inf\{Q(u):u\in W^{1,2}(M),u\geq 0\}.

Y(M,\partial M,[g])
is invariant under a conformal change of the metric
g
and
Y(M,\partial M,[g])\leq Y(S^{n}_{+},\partial S^{n}_{+},[g_{0}])
, where
Y(S^{n}_{+},\partial S^{n}_{+},[g_{0}])
denotes the Yamabe constant
of the hemisphere
S^{n}_{+}
equipped with the standard metric.

In
[
10
]
, Escobar showed the existence of the Type(ii) Yamabe problem provided
Y(M,\partial M,[g])<Y(S^{n}_{+},\partial S^{n}_{+},[g_{0}])
, and he also proved that
Y(M,\partial M,[g])<Y(S^{n}_{+},\partial S^{n}_{+},[g_{0}])
holds in the dimension
3\leq n\leq 5
if
M
is not conformally equivalent to the hemisphere
S^{n}_{+}
. In dimension
n\geq 6
, Escobar was able to verify this inequality under the assumption that
\partial M
is not umbilic. Brendle and Chen
[
6
]

considered the remaining case:
n\geq 6
and
\partial M
is umbilic. They verified the remaining case subject to the validity of the Positive Mass Theorem.

In this paper, we consider the stability of minimizers of the Yamabe metrics. For the type(ii) Yamabe problem, Borquez, Caju and Hanne
[
5
]
proved the quantitative stability for the Yamabe minimizers using the method of Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction and the Łojasiewicz distance inequality. So we will handle the type(i) problem.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subset W^{1,2}(M)
denote the set of all minimizers of
Q(u)
. Define

(13)

d(u,{\mathcal{M}})=\frac{\inf\left\{\|u-v\|_{W^{1,2}(M)}\ |\ v\in{\mathcal{M}}\right\}}{\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(M)}}.

Our result is a quantitative stability estimate for minimizers of the Yamabe functional.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a
C^{\infty}
compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with boundary such that
Y(M,\partial M,[g])<Y(S^{n}_{+},\partial S^{n}_{+},[g_{0}])
. There exist constants
c>0
and
{\gamma}\geq 0
, depending on
(M,g)
, such that

(14)

Q(u)-Y(M,\partial M,[g])\geq c\,d(u,\mathcal{M})^{2+{\gamma}}\,\qquad\text{for all }u\in W^{1,2}(M;{\mathbb{R}}_{+})\,.

Remark 1.3
.

Similarly to the closed case, we expect the quadratic stability in Theorem
1.1
to hold generically, i.e. there exists an open dense subset
\mathcal{G}
in the
C^{2}
topology on the space of
C^{\infty}
-conformal classes of metrics on
M
such that if
[g]\in\mathcal{G}
, we may take
{\gamma}=0
. This property comes from Schoen
[
18
]
that generically, there are finitely many critical points of
Q
and each one is non-degenerate. However, these tools are still lacking in the boundary setting.

Remark 1.4
.

In the closed case, there are some examples (See in
[
18
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
12
]
) with super quadratic growth, i.e.
\gamma>0
. In
[
12
]
, Frank showed that on the manifold
S^{1}(1/\sqrt{d-2})\times S^{d-1}(1)
with the standard product metric, the stability exponent is
4
. One can simply take
S^{1}(1/\sqrt{d-2})\times S^{d-1}_{+}(1)
and apply the above result.
So we can get the example with
\gamma>0
in the minimal boundary case.

We are also able to obtain a corollary from Theorem
1.2
, a conformally invariant stability estimate. We can define the following distance

(15)

||g_{u}-g_{v}||=\left(\int_{M}|u-v|^{2^{*}}d{\rm{vol}}_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}},

where
g_{u}=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g
, and
2^{*}=\frac{2n}{n-2}
. Similarly, in the case of

, we define

(16)

||g_{u}-g_{v}||_{*}=\left(\int_{M}\frac{1}{c_{n}}|{\nabla}u-{\nabla}v|^{2}+R_{g}(u-v)^{2}d{\rm{vol}}_{g}+2(n-1)\int_{\partial M}h_{g}(u-v)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

for any
g\in{\mathcal{M}}(M,g)
with
{\rm{vol}}_{g}(M)=1
. We may claim that although
||-||
and

are both defined with respect to a fixed conformal representative

, but it is independent of this choice.(see, e.g. in
[

]
)

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact
C^{\infty}
Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with boundary such that
Y(M,\partial M,[g])<Y(S^{n}_{+},\partial S^{n}_{+},[g_{0}])
. There exist constants
c>0
and
{\gamma}\geq 0
, depending on
M
and
[g]

(17)

Here

This corollary can be directly derived from Theorem

for more details on the conformal invariance of the involved quantities.

2.
Properties of the Yamabe Energy and Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction

Recall the Yamabe energy:

(19)

Q(u)=\ \frac{\int_{M}|{\nabla}u|^{2}+c_{n}R_{g}u^{2}\,d{\rm{vol}}_{g}+\frac{n-2}{2}\int_{\partial M}h_{g}u^{2}d\sigma_{g}}{\left(\int_{M}u^{2^{*}}\,d{\rm{vol}}_{g}\right)^{2/2^{*}}}.

Proof.

The map

Let

Let

Define

\langle\mathcal{A}\xi,\zeta\rangle=\frac{1}{2}{\nabla}_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}Q(v)[\xi,\zeta]
.

Let

. Define the quotient space

Indeed, to see injectivity directly, suppose

[{\mathcal{A}}w]=0
