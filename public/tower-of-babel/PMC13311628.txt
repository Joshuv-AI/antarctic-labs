Estimation of ED50 and ED95 of oliceridine required to suppress bronchoscopic responses during remimazolam sedation: study protocol for an up-and-down sequential allocation trial
Authors: Anhong Li, Yuancheng Zhou, Xueying Zhou, Ke Jiang, Bailong Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundFlexible bronchoscopy frequently provokes airway reflexes that impair patient comfort and procedural quality. Remimazolam provides effective sedation but often requires adjunctive opioids to suppress coughing and movement. Oliceridine is a G-protein-biased μ-opioid receptor agonist that may provide effective pain relief while minimising respiratory and haemodynamic side effects. However, the optimal dose of oliceridine combined with remimazolam for bronchoscopy remains unknown.Methods and analysisThis is a prospective, single-centre, sex-stratified, up-and-down sequential allocation trial. Adult patients (18–65 years, American Society of Anesthesiologists I–III) undergoing painless flexible bronchoscopy will receive intravenous oliceridine followed by remimazolam (0.3 mg/kg). The initial oliceridine dose is 40 µg/kg and is adjusted in 10 µg/kg steps according to the Dixon method based on bronchoscopic response. The primary outcome is the ED50 and ED95 of oliceridine for suppressing bronchoscopic responses during remimazolam sedation in male and female patients. ED50 and ED95 values with 95% CIs will be estimated using probit regression. Secondary outcomes include respiratory and haemodynamic events, rescue medication use, recovery characteristics and satisfaction scores.Ethics and disseminationApproval for the study has been granted by the Ethics Committee of Guiyang First People’s Hospital. Introduction

Flexible bronchoscopy (FOB) is a common and effective medical procedure widely used for the diagnosis and treatment of various respiratory diseases, such as chronic bronchitis, lung tumours and pneumonia.1 2 However, this procedure may provoke coughing, gag reflexes and haemodynamic fluctuations that impair procedural conditions and patient tolerance.3 Adequate sedation and analgesia are therefore essential to ensure safety, comfort and procedural efficiency.

Remimazolam is a novel benzodiazepine derivative with a rapid onset and short duration of action, which has been shown to be effective for sedation in various clinical procedures.4,6 Due to its quick onset and short half-life, remimazolam has demonstrated favourable safety during endoscopic procedures, including bronchoscopy.7,9 However, its sedative effect alone may not be sufficient to adequately suppress reflexive reactions during FOB, such as coughing and gagging, making adjunctive opioid analgesia necessary.

Opioids are frequently administered alongside remimazolam during FOB to help control the cough reflex and enhance patient comfort.7 10 Conventional opioids, such as remifentanil and sufentanil, exert their analgesic effects by binding to μ-opioid receptors, activating G-protein and β-arrestin-dependent signalling pathways. However, these medications are associated with several side effects, such as respiratory depression, itching, nausea and vomiting, all of which are linked to the β-arrestin-mediated signalling pathway.11 12 Oliceridine, a newer G-protein-biased opioid, primarily activates the G-protein signalling pathway while significantly reducing β-arrestin recruitment.12,15 This selective action allows oliceridine to maintain effective analgesia while reducing the common opioid-related side effects, positioning it as a promising alternative for sedation during FOB.

Therefore, combining the selective opioid receptor agonist oliceridine may provide a more effective approach. However, high-quality evidence regarding the optimal dosage of oliceridine combined with remimazolam for bronchoscopy, particularly for different gender patients, is currently lacking. This study aims to determine the median effective dose (ED50) and 95% effective dose (ED95) of fumarate oliceridine required to suppress bronchoscopy-related reflex reactions under a fixed dose of 0.3 mg/kg remimazolam sedation in male and female patients, using the classical Dixon up-and-down sequential method. This will provide direct evidence for precise drug dosing in clinical practice, optimising patient safety and comfort.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This protocol outlines a prospective, single-centre, dose-finding study that employs an up-and-down sequential allocation method to determine the ED50 and ED95 of oliceridine for suppressing bronchoscopy responses during sedation with remimazolam.

Sample size calculation

Previous studies using Dixon’s up-and-down method have generally used approximately six to eight crossover pairs as an empirical range for stable estimation of ED50.16 17 In the present study, participants were stratified by sex (male and female), with an anticipated sample size of 20–30 patients per group, resulting in a total of 40–60 patients. Recruitment was continued until eight crossover points had been obtained in each group to improve the robustness of the dose–response estimation.

ED50 and ED95 calculation

After acquiring eight turning points, the median dose of oliceridine needed to suppress reflex responses during FOB will be calculated by averaging the midpoint doses from each pair of patients. The ED50 and ED95 values will be estimated using the modified Dixon up-and-down method, along with probability unit analysis.18 19 An initial intravenous bolus of oliceridine will be administered at a dose of 40 µg/kg, and the subsequent dose for the next patient will be adjusted based on the response to FOB, with a step size of 10 µg/kg.20 Recruitment will be terminated after the completion of 8 turning points. Intervention

Patients are asked to fast and avoid drinking for 6–8 hours before the procedure. On entering the procedure room, an intravenous line is inserted into the upper limb, and non-invasive monitoring is initiated for blood pressure (NIBP), ECG, pulse oximetry (SpO2) and respiratory rate (RR). Oxygen is administered via mask for at least 5 min (6 L/min). All patients are then slowly administered the pre-determined dose of oliceridine intravenously, followed by a 2-minute wait. Afterward, 0.3 mg/kg of remimazolam is injected, and once the patient’s consciousness is lost (modified Observer’s Assessment of Alertness/Sedation Scale (MOAA/S) ≤ 2), a reinforced endotracheal tube is inserted approximately 6–8 cm into the patient’s oropharynx. The tube is connected to a ventilator for high-flow ventilation (100% oxygen concentration, 6–10 L/min oxygen flow). The tube’s position is adjusted according to the bronchoscope’s visual display during the procedure.

Bronchoscopy was performed by the same experienced pulmonologist using a transnasal approach. A syringe was used to inject 2% lidocaine through the side port for surface anaesthesia in the following target areas: trachea 100 mg, vocal cords, tracheal bifurcation and bilateral main bronchi 40 mg each.

Assessment of bronchoscopy response

Bronchoscopy response is evaluated using a bronchoscopy scoring system that considers vocal cord movement, coughing intensity and limb movement.19 20 Each variable was scored on a 4-point scale, which can be found in the online supplemental material 1. The total score for bronchoscopy ranges from 3 (indicating the best response) to 12 (indicating the worst). A physician, who is blinded to the group assignment and was not involved in the trial during the initial evaluation, assesses all scores.

A bronchoscopy response is considered when the final bronchoscopy score exceeds 6. If the score surpasses 6, extra intravenous boluses of oliceridine (10 µg/kg each) are given. Once the procedure is completed, flumazenil is promptly administered to counteract the sedative effects. As this is an exploratory study, a maximum cumulative dose has not been predefined. All dosing will be closely monitored for safety, and cumulative doses and any adverse events will be recorded for each participant.

The primary outcome, including the ED50 and ED95 of oliceridine for suppression of bronchoscopic reflexes during remimazolam sedation, together with corresponding 95% CIs, will be estimated using probit regression analysis based on sequential allocation data. Exploratory subgroup analyses according to sex may be conducted to assess potential differences in dose requirements.

Comparisons between groups will be performed using Student’s t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables and the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, as appropriate. Safety outcomes, including respiratory depression, hypotension and other adverse events, will be descriptively summarised and compared between groups when applicable. A two-sided p value <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

This trial will be conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol has been approved by the Institutional Review Board of Guiyang First People’s Hospital (approval date: 12 November 2025; Ethics Approval No.: Zhu Yi Yi Ethics 2025-S138). Informed consent

The informed consent forms will be obtained by the recruiting physicians at the participating centres. The informed consent forms are provided as online supplementary documents (onlinesupplemental materials 2 3).

Confidentiality

Participant data forms and electronic databases will be securely stored by the coordinating centre for a period of 10 years after completion of the study.

Data access

During the data collection period, the investigators at the participating centres will be the only personnel authorised to access the electronic trial data. After the final dataset has been generated from the raw data, access to the dataset will be restricted to the statisticians and the authors of the final manuscript. Prior to the completion of blinded data interpretation, the coding of the randomised controlled trial groups will be known only to the investigators.

Dissemination policy

The results of this study will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations. After publication of the primary study findings, a letter containing information about the study results will be sent to the patients who participated in the trial.

Discussion

By identifying the ED50 and ED95 values, this research will provide crucial information to guide the clinical application of oliceridine in routine bronchoscopy procedures. Moreover, understanding potential gender differences in dosing will allow for more individualised patient care, optimising both safety and efficacy.

Traditional opioids also activate β-arrestin proteins, which can lead to unwanted side effects such as respiratory depression, itching, nausea and vomiting.21,23 These side effects often limit the clinical use of opioids.24 25 Oliceridine, a newer opioid, has been approved for managing acute pain, as it selectively activates G-proteins.26 27 In contrast to conventional opioids, oliceridine has a much lower activation of β-arrestin, which results in fewer side effects.2 28 For example, Wu et al used oliceridine for gastroscopy and found that this group had a lower incidence of hypotension, PONV and higher patient satisfaction scores than the sufentanil group.20 To the best of our knowledge, our results represent the first investigation of the ED50 and ED95 of oliceridine required to suppress bronchoscopy responses under remimazolam sedation.

This study has several limitations. First, its open-label design may have introduced bias related to patient and operator expectations. Second, the primary outcome was based on a bronchoscopy response scoring system. Although this scoring system has been adopted in previous dose-finding studies during fibreoptic bronchoscopy under sedation,19 20 it has not been universally accepted in anaesthesia research. Furthermore, assessment of vocal cord movement, cough severity and limb movement inevitably involves a degree of subjective clinical judgement. Although standardised assessment criteria and trained evaluators were used to minimise variability, interobserver variability and observer-related bias cannot be completely excluded. Nevertheless, several objective secondary outcomes, including haemodynamic parameters and adverse events, were also evaluated and may provide complementary evidence regarding patient responses during bronchoscopy. Finally, although the sequential method is effective for estimating ED50 and ED95 values, it may not fully reflect the entire spectrum of patient responses. Therefore, further large-scale, multicentre studies are warranted to validate our findings and optimise dosing strategies.

In conclusion, this study provides important reference values for determining the optimal dose of oliceridine to suppress fibreoptic bronchoscopy responses under remimazolam sedation. The findings may contribute to improving patient comfort, safety and procedural conditions during bronchoscopy and provide a basis for future clinical investigations of oliceridine in procedural sedation.
