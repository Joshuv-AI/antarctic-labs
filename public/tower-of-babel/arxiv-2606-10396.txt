Nonlinearization of bilinear equations of the sine-Gordon type, nonlinear Schrödinger type and Benjamin-Ono type
Jin Liu, Da-jun Zhang, Xin Zhang, Xuehui Zhao

Abstract
This is a continuation of the paper [Commun. Theor. Phys., 77 (2025) 115006] on the nonlinearization of bilinear equations. The sine-Gordon type and nonlinear Schrödinger type bilinear equations are introduced by Jarmo Hietarinta during his search for integrable bilinear equations. In this paper, we provide a formulation to convert these two types of bilinear equations into nonlinear forms. In addition, the nonlinearization related to the equations involving the Hilbert transformations is also considered. Bell polynomials are employed in the nonlinearization and illustrative examples are provided. Keywords: Hirota bilinear equation, sine-Gordon type, nonlinear Schrödinger type, Hilbert transformation, Bell polynomial PACS Numbers: 02.30.Ik, 02.30.Ks, 05.45.Yv



1
Introduction

Hietarinta searched for integrable bilinear equations from
the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) type
[
11
]
,
the modified KdV (mKdV) type
[
12
]
,
the sine-Gordon (sG) type
[
13
]
and the nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) type
[
14
]
.
For the KdV-type bilinear equations, Hirota found they always have 2-soliton solutions
no matter whether they are integrable or not
[
18
]
.
Hirota conjectured having a 3-soliton solution indicates a kind of integrability,
which is later referred to as the integrability for bilinear equations in Hirota’s sense.
In Hietarinta’s search results, he listed out possible
bilinear equations of the KdV type, the mKdV type and the sG type
that have 3-soliton solutions
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
,
while for the NLS-type bilinear equations which involve complex conjugation,
he pointed out having a 2-soliton solution might indicate the integrability
[
14
]
.
In Hietarinta’s investigation, he found some new integrable bilinear equations,
but whose nonlinear forms are difficult to write out.
Recently, we developed an approach to convert bilinear equations into nonlinear forms
by utilizing Bell polynomials
[
43
]
,
by which we are able to nonlinearize the KdV-type and mKdV-type bilinear equations.
The sG type and NLS type are still outstanding.
The difficulties are how to recover trigonometric functions
in the sG type and how to deal with the involved complex conjugate functions in the NLS type.
In this paper, we will provide a formulation to convert these two types of bilinear equations into their nonlinear forms.
In addition, the nonlinearization related to the equations involving the Hilbert transformations
will also be considered.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall the basic notions and notations of the Bell polynomials and their
connections with Hirota’s bilinear derivatives.
Section
3
and Section
4
illustrate with examples
the procedure to convert the sG-type and NLS-type bilinear equations to their nonlinear forms, respectively.
In Section
5
, we introduce some properties of the Hilbert transformation,
and consider nonlinearizations related to the equations involving the Hilbert transformations.
Finally, concluding remarks are given in Section
6
.

Preliminary

Hirota’s bilinear derivatives.
For details one can refer to

,

For
F
and
G

∞

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{\ell})
,
their Hirota’s bilinear derivative is defined as
[

D^{n_{1}}_{x_{1}}\cdots D^{n_{\ell}}_{x_{\ell}}F(\mathbf{x})\cdot G(\mathbf{x})=(\partial_{x_{1}}-\partial_{x_{1}^{\prime}})^{n_{1}}\cdots(\partial_{x_{\ell}}-\partial_{x_{\ell}^{\prime}})^{n_{\ell}}F(\mathbf{x})G(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})|_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=\mathbf{x}},

(2.1)

where
D
is called Hirota’s bilinear operator.

Let
h=h(x)
be a
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}
function of
x
and
denote
h_{r}=\partial^{r}_{x}h
for
r=1,2,\cdots
.
Then, the so-called Bell polynomials are generated by
h(x)
are
[
1
]

Y_{[nx]}(h)\doteq Y_{n}(h_{1},h_{2},\cdots,h_{n})=e^{-h}\partial^{n}_{x}e^{h},~~~~~n=1,2,\cdots.

(2.2)

These polynomials can also be generated recursively by

Y_{0}=1,~~Y_{n+1}(h_{1},h_{2},\cdots,h_{n+1})=(\partial_{x}+h_{1})Y_{n}(h_{1},h_{2},\cdots,h_{n}),

(2.3)

which agrees with the (potential) Burgers hierarchy (see
[
9
,
24
]
).
Hereafter we call the polynomials defined in (
2.2
)

Y
-polynomials, the first three of which read

\displaystyle Y_{[x]}=h_{1},

(2.4a)

\displaystyle Y_{[2x]}=h_{2}+h_{1}^{2},

(2.4b)

\displaystyle Y_{[3x]}=h_{3}+3h_{1}h_{2}+h_{1}^{3}.

(2.4c)

Replacing
h(x)
by
h(-x)
in (
2.2
), one can find that

\displaystyle Y_{n}(-h_{1},h_{2},\cdots,(-1)^{n}h_{n})=(-1)^{n}Y_{[nx]}(h),

(2.5)

of which a special case yields

Y_{[(2n+1)x]}(h)|_{h_{1}=h_{3}=\cdots=h_{2n+1}=0}=0.

(2.6)

In addition, using the relation

(FG)^{-1}\partial^{n}_{x}(FG)=\sum_{p=0}^{n}\binom{n}{p}(F^{-1}\partial^{n-p}_{x}F)(G^{-1}\partial^{p}_{x}G),

(2.7)

and taking
F=e^{h}
,
G=e^{g}
,
one can obtain an addition formula for
Y
-polynomials:

Y_{[nx]}(h+g)=\sum_{p=0}^{n}\binom{n}{p}Y_{[(n-p)x]}(h)Y_{[px]}(g),

(2.8)

where
\binom{n}{p}=\frac{n!}{p!(n-p)!}
.

In 1-dimension case, the relation between Hirota’s bilinear derivatives and
Bell polynomials is described as the following
[
9
,
24
]

(FG)^{-1}D^{n}_{x}F\cdot{G}=Y_{n}(h_{1},\cdots,h_{n})|_{h_{m}=f_{m}+(-1)^{m}g_{m}},

(2.9)

where
f_{m}=\partial^{m}_{x}f,~g_{m}=\partial^{m}_{x}g
.

We also need to introduce binary deformation of the Bell polynomials:

\mathcal{Y}_{[nx]}(v,u)=Y_{n}(h_{1},\cdots,h_{n})|_{h_{2j-1}=v_{2j-1},\,h_{2j}=u_{2j}}.

(2.10)

and call them
\mathcal{Y}
-polynomials,
where
v_{m}=\partial^{m}_{x}v,~u_{m}=\partial^{m}_{x}u
.
The first three of them are

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{[x]}(v,u)=v_{1},

(2.11a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{[2x]}(v,u)=u_{2}+v^{2}_{1},

(2.11b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{[3x]}(v,u)=v_{3}+3u_{2}v_{1}+v^{3}_{1}.

(2.11c)

In terms of the binary Bell polynomials, the relation (
2.9
) is written as

(FG)^{-1}D^{n}_{x}F\cdot G=\mathcal{Y}_{[nx]}(v=\ln(F/G),u=\ln(FG)),

(2.12)

where
u=f+g
and
v=f-g
.
When
G=F
, we have
v=0
, and then in light of (

) and (
2.6
)
we have

\mathcal{Y}_{[(2j+1)x]}(v=0,u)=0,

(2.13)

which agrees with the property
D^{2j+1}_{x}F\cdot F=0
.
We introduce
P
-polynomials for the case of

P_{[2jx]}(u)\equiv\mathcal{Y}_{[2jx]}(0,u),

(2.14)

where the first three read

\displaystyle P_{[0]}(u)=1,

(2.15a)

\displaystyle P_{[2x]}(u)=u_{2},

(2.15b)

\displaystyle P_{[4x]}(u)=u_{4}+3u^{2}_{2}.

(2.15c)

In this case, the bilinear derivative
D^{2j}_{x}F\cdot F
can be formulated by the
P
-polynomials:

F^{-2}D^{2j}_{x}F\cdot F=P_{[2jx]}(u=2\ln F).

(2.16)

Next, we come to the multidimensional generalization.
Assume
h=h(\mathbf{x})
is an
\ell

of
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{\ell})

(2.17)

The

Y_{[n_{1}x_{1},\cdots,n_{\ell}x_{\ell}]}(h)\doteq Y_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{\ell}}(\{h_{r_{1},\cdots,r_{\ell}}\})=e^{-h}\partial^{n_{1}}_{x_{1}}\cdots\partial^{n_{\ell}}_{x_{\ell}}e^{h}.

(2.18)

Examples are the following. When

, we have

\displaystyle Y_{[x_{1},x_{2}]}=h_{1,0}h_{0,1}+h_{1,1},

\displaystyle Y_{[2x_{1},x_{2}]}=2h_{1,0}h_{1,1}+h_{1,0}^{2}h_{0,1}+h_{2,0}h_{0,1}+h_{2,1},

\displaystyle Y_{[3x_{1},x_{2}]}=3h_{1,0}^{2}h_{1,1}+3h_{1,1}h_{2,0}+3h_{1,0}h_{2,1}+h_{0,1}(h_{1,0}^{3}+3h_{1,0}h_{2,0}+h_{3,0})+h_{3,1},

In addition, from the definition, it holds that

Y_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{\ell}}(\{(-1)^{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{\ell}}h_{r_{1},\cdots,r_{\ell}}\})=(-1)^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{\ell}}Y_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{\ell}}(\{h_{r_{1},\cdots,r_{\ell}}\}).

(2.19)

The binary Bell polynomials of the multidimensional case can be introduced

\mathcal{Y}_{[n_{1}x_{1},\cdots,n_{\ell}x_{\ell}]}(v,u)\doteq Y_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{\ell}}(\{h_{r_{1},...,r_{\ell}}\})

(2.20a)

where

h_{r_{1},\cdots,r_{\ell}}=\biggl\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{r_{1},\cdots,r_{\ell}},&\mathrm{if}~r_{1}+\cdots+r_{\ell}~\mathrm{is~even},\\
v_{r_{1},\cdots,r_{\ell}},&\mathrm{if}~r_{1}+\cdots+r_{\ell}~\mathrm{is~odd}.\end{array}

Again, we provide some examples.
In 2-dimension case, i.e.,

, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{[x]}(v,u)=v_{x},

(2.21a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{[2x]}(v,u)=u_{2x}+v^{2}_{x},

(2.21b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{[x,y]}(v,u)=u_{x,y}+v_{x}v_{y},

(2.21c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{[3x]}(v,u)=v_{3x}+3v_{x}u_{2x}+v_{x}^{3},

(2.21d)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{[2x,y]}(v,u)=v_{2x,y}+2v_{x}u_{x,y}+v^{2}_{x}v_{y}+u_{2x}v_{y},

(2.21e)

where we use

to denote

.
The simplest example for 3 dimensional case (

(2.22)

For the multidimensional binary Bell polynomials, we also have

\mathcal{Y}_{[n_{1}x_{1},\cdots,n_{\ell}x_{\ell}]}(v=0,u)=0,~~~\mathrm{if}~n_{1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+n_{\ell}~\mathrm{is~odd}.

When

(2.23)

and some examples are

\displaystyle P_{[2x]}(u)=u_{2x},

(2.24a)

\displaystyle P_{[x,y]}(u)=u_{x,y},

(2.24b)

\displaystyle P_{[2x,2y]}(u)=2u^{2}_{x,y}+u_{2x}u_{2y}+u_{2x,2y},

(2.24c)

\displaystyle P_{[3x,y]}(u)=u_{3x,y}+3u_{2x}u_{x,y}.

(2.24d)

In the multidimensional case, the addition formula reads

Y_{[n_{1}x_{1},\cdots,n_{\ell}x_{\ell}]}(f+g)=\sum_{p_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}\cdots\sum_{p_{\ell}=0}^{n_{\ell}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{i}}{p_{i}}Y_{[(n_{1}-p_{1})x_{1},\cdots,(n_{\ell}-p_{\ell})x_{\ell}]}(f)Y_{[p_{1}x_{1},\cdots,p_{\ell}x_{\ell}]}(g).

(2.25)

Introducing
F=e^{f(\mathbf{x})}

3.1
The sG-type bilinear equations

In 1987, Hietarinta
[
13
]
considered the following type of coupled bilinear equations
(named as the sG-type):

\displaystyle A_{1}(D_{x},D_{t})F\cdot F+B_{1}(D_{x},D_{t})F\cdot G+C_{1}(D_{x},D_{t})G\cdot G=0,

(3.1a)

\displaystyle A_{2}(D_{x},D_{t})F\cdot F+B_{2}(D_{x},D_{t})F\cdot G+C_{2}(D_{x},D_{t})G\cdot G=0,

(3.1b)

where
A_{i}
and
C_{i}
(
i=1,2
) are some binary even polynomials. The above system has
a 1-soliton solution of the following form when the polynomials
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are proportional
(including the case that one of the
B_{i}
is 0, without loss of generality, we set
B_{1}\neq 0
):

\displaystyle F=1,

(3.2a)

\displaystyle G=e^{\eta},~~\eta=px+\Omega t+\eta^{(0)},

(3.2b)

where
p,\Omega,\eta^{(0)}\in\mathbb{C}
,
p
and
\Omega
are subject to the dispersion relation:

B_{1}(p,\Omega)=0.

(3.3)

When
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are proportional and
B_{1}\neq 0
, Hietarinta looked for the 2-soliton solution of
the following form

\displaystyle F=1+A_{12}e^{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}},

(3.4a)

\displaystyle G=e^{\eta_{1}}+e^{\eta_{2}},~~\eta_{i}=p_{i}x+\Omega_{i}t+\eta_{i}^{(0)},

(3.4b)

where
p_{i},\Omega_{i},\eta_{i}^{(0)}\in\mathbb{C}
,
i=1,2
,

A_{12}
is to be determined, and
\eta_{i}
satisfies the dispersion relation

B_{1}(p_{i},\Omega_{i})=0,~~i=1,2.

(3.5)

It turns out that, to make (
3.4
) to be a solution to (
3.1
),
apart from (
3.5
),
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
still need to satisfy the following conditions
[
13
]
:

\displaystyle A_{12}A_{i}(p_{1}+p_{2},\Omega_{1}+\Omega_{2})+C_{i}(p_{1}-p_{2},\Omega_{1}-\Omega_{2})=0,

(3.6a)

\displaystyle B_{i}(-p_{j},-\Omega_{j})=0.

(3.6b)

where
i,j=1,2
.

To sum up, if the sG-type bilinear system (
3.1
) satisfies the following conditions, then it has
the 1 and 2-soliton solutions of the form like (
3.2
) and (
3.6
)
[
13
]
:

•

B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are proportional,
B_{1}\neq 0
and

B_{1}(p_{j},\Omega_{j})=B_{1}(-p_{j},-\Omega_{j})=0,~~j=1,2;

(3.7)

•

A_{i}
and

satisfy (see equation (24) in
[
13
]

A_{1}(p_{1}+p_{2},\Omega_{1}+\Omega_{2})C_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2},\Omega_{1}-\Omega_{2})=A_{2}(p_{1}+p_{2},\Omega_{1}+\Omega_{2})C_{1}(p_{1}-p_{2},\Omega_{1}-\Omega_{2});

(3.8)

•

assume

A_{2}\neq 0
, then

A_{12}=-\frac{C_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2},\Omega_{1}-\Omega_{2})}{A_{2}(p_{1}+p_{2},\Omega_{1}+\Omega_{2})}.

(3.9)

Under the existence of a 2-soliton solution, Hietarinta studied the sG-type equations
which may have 3-soliton solutions of the following type

\displaystyle F=1+A_{12}e^{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}+A_{13}e^{\eta_{1}+\eta_{3}}+A_{23}e^{\eta_{2}+\eta_{3}},

(3.10a)

\displaystyle G=e^{\eta_{1}}+e^{\eta_{2}}+e^{\eta_{3}}+A_{12}A_{13}A_{23}e^{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}+\eta_{3}},

(3.10b)

where

(3.11)

The main results of the sG-type bilinear equations possessing 3-soliton solutions are as follows:

•

For

3.2
Nonlinearization

In this section, by some examples we show how nonlinear equations are recovered from the sG-type bilinear equations.

The first one is a toy example of the sG-type bilinear equations (see Table I (Accepted final result) of
[
13
]
)

\displaystyle D_{x}^{2}(F\cdot F-G\cdot G)=0,

(3.12a)

\displaystyle(D_{x}^{2}+1)F\cdot G=0.

(3.12b)

Using the formula (
2.28
)
and the transformations

v=\ln(F/G),~~u=\ln(FG),

(3.13)

for equation (
3.12a
) we have

\displaystyle(FG)^{-2}D_{x}^{2}(F\cdot F-G\cdot G)=

\displaystyle G^{-2}(F^{-2}D_{x}^{2}F\cdot F)-F^{-2}(G^{-2}D_{x}^{2}G\cdot G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{-2}P_{[2x]}(2\ln F)-F^{-2}P_{[2x]}(2\ln G).

From (
2.24a
) and noticing that
2\ln F=u+v,~2\ln G=u-v
, we arrive at

e^{-(u-v)}(u+v)_{xx}-e^{-(u+v)}(u-v)_{xx}=0,

(3.14)

i.e.

u_{xx}=-\frac{e^{v}+e^{-v}}{e^{v}-e^{-v}}v_{xx}=-v_{xx}\coth(v).

(3.15)

Similarly, for (
3.12b
), using (
2.27
) we find

(FG)^{-1}(D_{x}^{2}+1)F\cdot G=\mathcal{Y}_{[2x]}(v,u)+1=u_{xx}+v_{x}^{2}+1=0.

(3.16)

Substituting (
3.15
) into (
3.16
) we have

-v_{xx}\coth(v)+v_{x}^{2}+1=0.

(3.17)

Then we introduce
w
by applying a variable transformation

v=\ln\bigg(\tan\frac{w}{4}\bigg),

(3.18)

we obtain

w_{xx}=-\sin w,

(3.19)

where

w=4\arctan(e^{v})=4\arctan(F/G).

(3.20)

It is notable that (
3.19
) has a three-soliton solution
[
13
]

while it is an ordinary differential equation. Similar examples are found
in other one-dimensional cases (e.g. Table I, II, III in
[
11
]
),
which means that ordinary differential equations may possess multi-soliton solutions.

The second example is the standard bilinear sG equation:

\displaystyle D_{x}D_{t}(F\cdot F-G\cdot G)=0,

(3.21a)

\displaystyle(D_{x}D_{t}-1)F\cdot G=0.

(3.21b)

Using the formula (
2.27
), from (

) we have

\displaystyle(FG)^{-2}D_{x}D_{t}(F\cdot F-G\cdot G)=

\displaystyle G^{-2}P_{[x,t]}(2\ln F)-F^{-2}P_{[x,]t}(2\ln G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-u}[(e^{v}-e^{-v})u_{xt}+(e^{v}+e^{-v})v_{xt}]=0,

where the polynomial (
2.24b
) is used.
It then yields

u_{xt}=-\frac{e^{v}+e^{-v}}{e^{v}-e^{-v}}v_{xt}=-v_{xt}\coth(v).

(3.22)

Similarly, (
3.21b
) yields

(FG)^{-1}(D_{x}D_{t}-1)F\cdot G=\mathcal{Y}_{[x,t]}(v,u)-1=u_{xt}+v_{x}v_{t}-1=0.

(3.23)

Substituting (
3.22
) into (
3.23
) we have

-v_{xt}\coth(v)+v_{x}v_{t}-1=0.

(3.24)

Introducing variable
w
as (
3.18
),
the above equation becomes the sG equation

w_{xt}=\sin w,

(3.25)

and the transformation between

and
F
and
G
takes (
3.20
).
In the above procedure, the transformation (
3.18
) plays a key role in the formulation
of the sine function.

For the last example we consider a high dimensional case of the sG type equations
(see

13
]
, Table II, the column “Generalizations with

\displaystyle(D_{x}D_{y}+1)F\cdot G=0,

(3.26a)

\displaystyle D_{x}D_{t}(F\cdot F-G\cdot G)=0.

The second equation gives rise to (

\displaystyle(FG)^{-1}(D_{x}D_{y}+1)F\cdot G

The NLS-type bilinear equations and nonlinearization

In this section, we will show the nonlinearization of the NLS-type bilinear equations.
The NLS-type bilinear equations considered in
[
14
]
by Hietarinta are the following:

\displaystyle B(D_{x},D_{t})G\cdot F=0,

(4.1a)

\displaystyle A(D_{x},D_{t})F\cdot F=C(D_{x},D_{t})G\cdot G^{*},

(4.1b)

where

denotes complex conjugate, the binary polynomials

\displaystyle A(0,0)=0,

(4.2a)

\displaystyle[B(X,T)]^{*}=B(-X^{*},-T^{*}),

\displaystyle[C(X,T)]^{*}=C(-X^{*},-T^{*}),

Bilinear equations in this type automatically possess a 1-soliton solution in the following form

(4.5)

and

Note that in this type the bilinear equations do not always have 2-soliton solutions.
In
[
14

Hietarinta searched the above NLS-type bilinear quations that allow
having 2-soliton solutions, and he claimed that having 2-soliton solutions implies integrability for this type of
bilinear equations.

5.1
Hilbert transformation

The Hilbert transformation of a given function
f(x),~x\in\mathbb{R}
, is defined as

\mathcal{H}[f(x)]=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{f(s)}{s-x}\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{x-\epsilon}+\int_{x+\epsilon}^{+\infty}\right)\frac{f(s)}{s-x}\mathrm{d}s,

(5.1)

where P.V. denotes the Cauchy principal value of the integral.

The Hilbert transform has the following properties that play important roles in the variable transformations.

Proposition 5.1
.

[
21
]

If both
f(x)
and
f_{x}(x)
are
functions in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
space where
1<p<+\infty
,
then the transformation
\mathcal{H}
and differentiation
\partial_{x}
commute,
i.e.

\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}[f(x)]=\mathcal{H}[\partial_{x}f(x)].

(5.2)

Proposition 5.2
.

[
21
]

If function
f(z),~z\in\mathbb{C}
, is analytic in the upper half plane
and
f(z)\to 0
for
\mathrm{Im}\,z\geq 0
as
|z|\to\infty
,
then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, there is

\mathcal{H}[f(x)]=if(x).

(5.3)

Similarly, for the function
g(z)
analytic in the lower half plane, and
g(z)\to 0

for
\mathrm{Im}\,z\leq 0
as
|z|\to\infty
, we have

\mathcal{H}[g(x)]=-ig(x),~~x\in\mathbb{R}.

(5.4)

Proposition 5.3
.

Consider two functions
F(z),G(z),z\in\mathbb{C}
,
which are analytic in the upper half and lower half complex plane, respectively.
If their zeros are located in the lower half and upper half complex plane, respectively,
i.e.

\displaystyle F(s_{j})=0,~~\mathrm{Im}\,s_{j}<0,~~(j=1,2,\cdots,M),

(5.5a)

\displaystyle G(z_{j})=0,~~\mathrm{Im}\,z_{j}>0,~~(j=1,2,\cdots,N),

(5.5b)

(5.6)

where

Note that
\partial_{x}F(z)=\partial_{z}F(z)

Similarly,
\partial_{x}G(z)=\partial_{z}G(z)

Proof.

Consider two functions

(5.7)

(5.9)

Similarly, there is

\mathcal{H}[g(x)]=-ig(x),~~x\in\mathbb{R}.

(5.10)

Then we have

which is (
5.6

∎

Let us start from the following bilinear form:

(F^{*}F)_{t}=\frac{1}{2i}(F^{2}-{F^{*}}^{2}).

(5.34)

Directly employing the transformation (
5.18
), we obtain

u_{t}=\frac{e^{v}-e^{-v}}{2i}.

(5.35)

We further assume
v,v_{x},v_{t}
belong to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
space where
1<p<+\infty
.
This allows us to have
u=-i\mathcal{H}[v]
from (
5.13
)
and then
u_{t}=-i\mathcal{H}[v_{t}]
in light of Proposition
5.1
.
Thus from (
5.35
) we obtain

\mathcal{H}[q_{t}]=\sin q,~~~(q=-iv).

(5.36)

This is known as the sine-Hilbert equation
[
30
]
and is related to the bilinear form
(
5.34
) via
q=i\ln(F^{*}/F)
.

The next example is (cf.
[
40
]
)

D_{t}G^{*}\cdot F=\alpha(G^{*}F^{*}-GF),~~~\alpha\alpha^{*}=\frac{1}{4},

(5.37)

where

v^{\prime}=\ln\frac{G^{*}}{F},~~u^{\prime}=\ln FG^{*}

(5.38)

Equation (
5.37
) can be represented as

(FG^{*})^{-1}D_{t}G^{*}\cdot F=\mathcal{Y}_{[t]}(v^{\prime},u^{\prime})=v^{\prime}_{t}=\alpha\bigg(\frac{F^{*}}{F}-\frac{G}{G^{*}}\bigg),

(5.39)

which gives

v^{\prime}_{t}=\alpha e^{\frac{1}{2}(u^{\prime*}-u^{\prime})}\left(e^{-\frac{1}{2}(v^{\prime*}-v^{\prime})}-e^{\frac{1}{2}(v^{\prime*}-v^{\prime})}\right).

(5.40)

Multiplying it with its complex conjugate form yields

v^{\prime}_{t}v^{\prime*}_{t}=-\frac{1}{4}\left(e^{v^{\prime*}-v^{\prime}}+e^{-(v^{\prime*}-v^{\prime})}-2\right),

(5.41)

where we have replaced
\alpha\alpha^{*}
with
\frac{1}{4}
.
To go further, we write its left-hand side into

v^{\prime}_{t}v^{\prime*}_{t}=\frac{1}{4}[(v_{t}^{\prime*}+v^{\prime}_{t})^{2}-(v_{t}^{\prime*}-v^{\prime}_{t})^{2}].

(5.42)

For the right-hand side of (
5.41
), using the identity

\ln\frac{FG}{F^{*}G^{*}}=2i\arctan\frac{\mathrm{Im}(FG)}{\mathrm{Re}(FG)}=2i\arctan\frac{FG-F^{*}G^{*}}{i(FG+F^{*}G^{*})}

(5.43)

and (
5.38
), we have

\displaystyle e^{v^{\prime*}-v^{\prime}}+e^{-(v^{\prime*}-v^{\prime})}-2

\displaystyle=\frac{[e^{\frac{1}{2}(u^{\prime*}-v^{\prime*}+u^{\prime}+v^{\prime})}-e^{\frac{1}{2}(u^{\prime*}+v^{\prime*}+u^{\prime}-v^{\prime})}]^{2}}{e^{(u^{\prime*}+u^{\prime})}}

\displaystyle=2\frac{(FG-F^{*}G^{*})^{2}}{2FF^{*}GG^{*}}=2\frac{(FG)^{2}+(F^{*}G^{*})^{2}}{2FF^{*}GG^{*}}-2

\displaystyle=2\cos\bigg(i\ln\frac{FG}{F^{*}G^{*}}\bigg)-2.

(5.44)

Introducing

w=i(v^{\prime*}-v^{\prime})=i\ln\frac{FG}{F^{*}G^{*}}

(5.45)

and assuming
\partial_{t}\ln F\to 0
(for
\mathrm{Im}\,z\geq 0
)
and
\partial_{t}\ln G\to 0
(for
\mathrm{Im}\,z\leq 0
) as
z
goes to
\infty
,
it then follows from Proposition
5.3
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[w_{t}]=\mathcal{H}[i\partial_{t}(v^{\prime*}-v^{\prime})]=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\bigg[i\partial_{t}\ln\frac{FG}{F^{*}G^{*}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\bigg[i\partial_{t}\ln\biggl(\frac{F}{G^{*}}\bigg/\frac{F^{*}}{G}\biggr)\bigg]=-\partial_{t}\ln\frac{FF^{*}}{G^{*}G}=(v^{\prime*}+v^{\prime})_{t}.

(5.46)

Then, combining (
5.41
), (
5.42
), (
5.44
) and the above equation,
we get a nonlinear equation

w_{t}^{2}+(\mathcal{H}w_{t})^{2}+2\cos w-2=0.

(5.47)

This is known as the modified sine-Hlibert equation
[
40
]

and is related to the bilinear form (
5.37
) via the transformation (
5.45
).

The third example is (cf.
[
34
]
)

\displaystyle(iD_{t}-2{i}\lambda D_{x}-D_{x}^{2}-\mu)F\cdot\tilde{G}=0,

(5.48a)

\displaystyle(iD_{t}-2{i}\lambda D_{x}-D_{x}^{2}-\mu)\tilde{F}\cdot{G}=0,

(5.48b)

\displaystyle(D_{x}+i\gamma)F\cdot{G}-i\nu\tilde{F}\tilde{G}=0

(5.48c)

with
\lambda,\gamma,\mu,\nu
being constants,
and where, apart from
F
and
G
as described in Proposition
5.3
,

\tilde{G}
and
\tilde{F}
are functions
analytic in the upper half and lower half complex plane, respectively,
while their zeros are respectively located in the lower half and upper half complex plane.
In addition,
\partial_{x}\ln\tilde{G}\to 0
(for
\mathrm{Im}\,z\geq 0
)
and
\partial_{x}\ln\tilde{F}\to 0
(for
\mathrm{Im}\,z\leq 0
) as
z
goes to
\infty
.
Except for
v
and
u
defined as in (
5.12
), we also introduce

\tilde{v}=\ln\frac{F}{\tilde{G}},~~\tilde{u}=\ln F\tilde{G},~~\tilde{\tilde{v}}=\ln\frac{\tilde{F}}{G},~~\tilde{\tilde{u}}=\ln\tilde{F}G.

(5.49)

Using the formula (
2.27
), we can convert the bilinear system (
5.48
) into the following form

\displaystyle i\mathcal{Y}_{[t]}(\tilde{v},\tilde{u})-2i\lambda\mathcal{Y}_{[x]}(\tilde{v},\tilde{u})-\mathcal{Y}_{[2x]}(\tilde{v},\tilde{u})-\mu=0,

(5.50a)

\displaystyle i\mathcal{Y}_{[t]}(\tilde{\tilde{v}},\tilde{\tilde{u}})-2i\lambda\mathcal{Y}_{[x]}(\tilde{\tilde{v}},\tilde{\tilde{u}})-\mathcal{Y}_{[2x]}(\tilde{\tilde{v}},\tilde{\tilde{u}})-\mu=0,

(5.50b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{[x]}({v},{u})+i\gamma=i\nu\frac{\tilde{F}\tilde{G}}{GF}.

(5.50c)

These lead to

\displaystyle i\tilde{v}_{t}-2i\lambda\tilde{v}_{x}-(\tilde{u}_{xx}+\tilde{v}_{x}^{2})-\mu=0,

(5.51a)

\displaystyle i\tilde{\tilde{v}}_{t}-2i\lambda\tilde{\tilde{v}}_{x}-(\tilde{\tilde{u}}_{xx}+(\tilde{\tilde{v}}_{x})^{2})-\mu=0,

(5.51b)

\displaystyle(\tilde{u}+\tilde{v}-\tilde{\tilde{u}}+\tilde{\tilde{v}})_{x}+2i\gamma=2i\nu e^{\tilde{\tilde{v}}-\tilde{v}},

(5.51c)

where for the last equation we have used the relation
\tilde{u}+\tilde{v}-\tilde{\tilde{u}}+\tilde{\tilde{v}}=-2v
.
Introduce

w=\tilde{\tilde{v}}-\tilde{v}=\ln\frac{\tilde{F}\tilde{G}}{{G}F}.

(5.52)

Then, subtracting (
5.51b
) from (
5.51a
) and differentiating (
5.51c
) with respect to
x

yield

\displaystyle iw_{t}-2i\lambda w_{x}-(\tilde{\tilde{u}}-\tilde{u})_{xx}-w_{x}(\tilde{\tilde{v}}+\tilde{v})_{x}=0,

(5.53a)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\tilde{v}}+\tilde{v})_{xx}-(\tilde{\tilde{u}}-u)_{xx}=2i\nu w_{x}e^{w}.

(5.53b)

Eliminating
(\tilde{\tilde{u}}-u)_{xx}
yields

iw_{t}-2i\lambda w_{x}-(\tilde{\tilde{v}}+\tilde{v})_{xx}+2i\nu w_{x}e^{w}-w_{x}(\tilde{\tilde{v}}+\tilde{v})_{x}=0.

(5.54)

Note that
\mathcal{H}
is a linear operator, which means

\mathcal{H}[w_{x}]=\mathcal{H}[(\tilde{\tilde{v}}-\tilde{v})_{x}]=\mathcal{H}\bigg[\partial_{x}\ln\frac{\tilde{F}\tilde{G}}{{G}F}\bigg]=\mathcal{H}\bigg[\partial_{x}\ln\frac{\tilde{F}}{F}\bigg]+\mathcal{H}\bigg[\partial_{x}\ln\frac{\tilde{G}}{G}\bigg].

(5.55)

Making use of formula (
5.6
) in Proposition
5.3
we have

\mathcal{H}[w_{x}]=-i\partial_{x}\ln(F{\tilde{F})+i\partial_{x}\ln(G\tilde{G})}=i\partial_{x}\ln\frac{\tilde{G}G}{\tilde{F}F}=-i(\tilde{\tilde{v}}+\tilde{v})_{x}.

(5.56)

Further, we assume
\tilde{v}_{x},\tilde{v}_{xx},\tilde{\tilde{v}}_{x},\tilde{\tilde{v}}_{xx}

belong to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
space where
1<p<+\infty

so that from the above relation we have
\mathcal{H}[w_{xx}]=-i(\tilde{\tilde{v}}+\tilde{v})_{xx}
.
Thus (
5.54
) yields

w_{t}-2\lambda w_{x}+2\nu e^{w}w_{x}-\mathcal{H}[w_{xx}]-w_{x}\mathcal{H}[w_{x}]=0,

(5.57)

which is known as the modified BO equation
[
34
]

and is connected with the bilinear form (
5.48
) via the transformation (
5.52
).

6
Concluding remarks

This paper is a continuation of our previous research
[
43
]

on the nonlinearization of bilinear equations.
In this paper, we have illustrated how the sG-type and NLS-type bilinear equations
are converted to their nonlinear forms.
We also explored the BO-type equations that contain the Hilbert transformations.
We have introduced some properties of the Hilbert transformations,
which are useful in the nonlnearization/bilinearization of the BO-type equations.
In our procedure,
solutions (i.e.
F,G
, etc) of the bilinear equations of this type
are required to satisfy special properties for the analytic area and location of zeros.
Sometimes we also need the involved functions are “good” enough so that
their Hilbert transformation commutes with differential operations, e.g.

\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}[v]=\mathcal{H}[v_{x}]
.
Note that in practice these requirements can be satisfied in construction of soliton solutions
(which are called rational solutions in
[
14
]
),
e.g.
[
8
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
.
The understanding of these properties of the Hilbert transformations
is useful not only in the nonlnearization of the BO-type bilinear equations
but also in finding bilinear forms for the nonlinear equations with Hilbert transformations.
In addition, there are periodic solutions (also known as multiphase solutions), e.g.

[
34
,
5
,
8
,
37
]
.
These solutions do not satisfy the condition for
F
and
G
given in Proposition
5.3
.
We have provided an example and given some comments in Remark
5.1
.
For the nonlinearizarion related to the periodic Hilbert transformation,
we would like to investigate it elsewhere.

In this paper, we did not consider the NLS-type equations with Hilbert transformations,
e.g.

i\psi_{t}+\alpha\mathcal{H}[\psi_{x}]+\beta|\psi|^{2}\psi=0

(6.1)

which has interesting physical contexts
[
7
,
20
]
, where
\alpha
and
\beta
are constants.
Bilinear method has been applied to some NLS-type equations with Hilbert transformations
[
32
,
33
,
26
]
.
Note that no matter whether the bilinear equations are integrable or not,
the sG-type bilinear system (
3.1
) always has a 1-soliton solution and 2-soliton solution,
the NLS-type bilinear system (
4.1
) always has a 1-soliton solution
and the BO-type bilinear equation (
5.15
) always has a 1-soliton solution.
This means it is possible to use bilinear method to investigate non-integrable equations.
We believe our research in this paper as well as in our previous one
[
43
]

on the nonlinearization of bilinear equations can bring new understanding
in the study of various types of models from the bilinear approach
[
15
,
19
]
.
