 Executive Summary
This annual review provides the projected dose estimates of radionuclide inventories
disposed in the 200 West Area Low-Level Waste Burial Grounds (LLBGs) since
September 26, 1988. All the trenches in these burial grounds except two have remained
inactive since July 2004. The only active trenches that continue to receive low-level and
mixed low-level waste are Trenches 31 and 34 that are located in the southern portion of
the 218-W-5 Burial Ground. During this reporting period (fiscal year 2025, from
October 1, 2024, through September 30, 2025), waste was disposed in Trench 31 only.

The original performance assessment (PA) for the 200 West Area LLBGs was performed
in 1995 with an addendum completed in 1996 (WHC-EP-0645, Performance Assessment
for the Disposal of Low-Level Waste in the 200 West Area Burial Grounds;
HNF-SD-WM-TI-798, Addendum to the Performance Assessment Analysis for Low-Level
Waste Disposal in the 200 West Area Active Burial Grounds). In October 2023, a new PA
(DOE/RL-2021-26, Performance Assessment of the Active Trenches in 200 East and 200
West Low-Level Burial Grounds at the Hanford Site) was approved that focused on
calculating the dose from the two currently active trenches (Trench 31 and 34). The
Operating Disposal Authorization Statement (ODAS) for the 200 West Area Burial
Grounds was revised accordingly (Ellis, 2023, Revised Operating Disposal Authorization
Statements for the 200 East and 200 West Low-Level Waste Burial Grounds at Hanford).

The performance metrics are compared with the performance objectives defined in
U.S. Department of Energy requirements (DOE O 435.1, Radioactive Waste
Management, and its companion documents DOE M 435.1-1, Radioactive Waste
Management Manual, and DOE-STD-5002-2017, Disposal Authorization Statement and
Tank Closure Documentation, U.S. Department of Energy). The estimated dose metrics
pertaining to the two active trenches (Trench 31 and 34) are based on the new PA, while
the results for the inactive trenches are based on the original PA, in order to remain
consistent with the revised ODAS.

Overall, there are no substantive changes to primary PA assumptions and no changes to
the PA analysis conclusion. All performance objectives are currently satisfied, and waste
acceptance practices (HNF-EP-0063, Hanford Site Solid Waste Acceptance Criteria)
continue to be sufficient. Continued groundwater monitoring in and around
the 200 West Area LLBGs indicates no impacts from the LLBG waste. Compliance with
DOE O 435.1 and the Disposal Authorization Statement is maintained.

3.1 Projected Dose Estimates from the Disposed Waste to Evaluate Compliance with
 DOE O 435.1
On October 2, 2023, the revised Operating Disposal Authorization Statement (ODAS) for the 200 West Area

conservative assumptions used in the model accommodate this potentially nonconservative condition.

4.1 Low-Level Waste Management Area 3
Groundwater monitoring of the well network at LLWMA-3 (Figure 4) within the 200-ZP-1 Operable Unit in
the 200 West Area continued for this reporting period under Resource Conservation and Recovery Act of
1976 (RCRA) and Atomic Energy Act of 1954 (AEA) requirements (DOE/RL-2009-68, Interim Status
Groundwater Monitoring Plan for the LLBG WMA-3).

Due to the 2019 critical mean exceedances, DOE prepared an interim status groundwater assessment
plan (DOE/RL-2019-32, Interim Status Groundwater Quality Assessment Plan for the Low-Level Burial
Grounds Waste Management Area-3), and the plan was revised in June 2021 to update the monitoring
well network, sampling frequency for the new wells, and constituents for the proposed new wells
(DOE/RL-2019-32). Several new monitoring wells were installed in 2023 with sampling activities. Three of
these wells are downgradient from the active trenches 31 and 34 of 218-W-5. One new well is between
218-W-5 and 218-W-3A.

The water table in the 200 West Area is influenced by ongoing pump-and-treat (P&T) operations focused
on removal of carbon tetrachloride from the unconfined aquifer. Numerous injection and extraction wells
exist that cause localized groundwater mounds and a cone of depression in the 200 West Area
(Figure 5). The general direction of groundwater flow is eastward in the region encompassing
the 200 West Area (Figure 5). Three 200 West Area injection wells (W7-14, W10-35, and W10-36) are
located within the boundaries of LLWMA-3 (Figure 5), and another injection well is located east of
LLWMA. West of the injection wells, the groundwater flow direction across LLWMA-3 varies due to the
influence of P&T remediation wells. East of the P&T injection wells, the general direction of groundwater
flow in 2024 was eastward and the average gradient was 4.6×10-3 m/m, with an estimated velocity of
0.37 m/d (1.2 ft/d) (Table 1-2 in DOE/RL-2024-29). Beneath Trenches 31 and 34, the average gradient is
3.43×10-3 m/m, and the estimated velocity is 0.27 m/d (0.89 ft/d) (Table 1-2 in DOE/RL-2024-29).

During the reporting period, all wells shown in Figure 4 were sampled as scheduled, and the sampling
results are summarized in Table 4-2 of DOE/HFO-2024-41. Carbon tetrachloride was observed above the
primary drinking water standard (DWS) of 5 μg/L in downgradient wells 299-W10-29, 299-W10-30, and
299-W10-31. Nitrate (not a dangerous waste constituent) was observed above the DWS of 45 mg/L in
downgradient wells 299-W10-31 and 299-W10-204. The 2024 results are consistent with previous data
and indicate that the wells are influenced by regional carbon tetrachloride and nitrate plumes. Based on
assessment of results from RCRA monitoring conducted it was concluded that LLWMA-3 has not
impacted groundwater with dangerous waste constituents in CY 2024 (DOE/RL-2024-29).

As with other LLWMAs, DOE monitors for AEA radionuclides as described in DOE/RL-2000-72,
Performance Assessment Monitoring Plan for the Hanford Site Low-Level Burial Grounds, and
DOE/RL-2021-39, Performance Assessment Monitoring Plan for the Active Low-Level Burial Ground
Trenches 31, 34, and 94 at the Hanford Site. Iodine-129, technetium-99, uranium, and tritium were
monitored semiannually in the upgradient and three downgradient RCRA monitoring wells.
The concentrations for these analytes were below DWSs for all wells during this reported period. Uranium
concentrations were consistently less than 2 µg/L. Iodine-129 and technetium-99 in the LLWMA-3
monitoring wells were below the detection limits (Figures 4-165 and 4-163 in DOE/HFO-2024-41).
The 2024 groundwater data do not indicate a release associated with 218-W-5 Burial Ground
Trenches 31 and 34.

Figure 6 presents the monitored groundwater concentrations between 2016 and 2025 for technetium-99

4.3 Air Monitoring for Radionuclides for 200 West Area
Atmospheric releases of radioactive materials from Hanford Site facilities and operations to
the surrounding region are potential sources of exposure to humans. Radioactive constituents in air are
monitored at Hanford Site facilities and operations at locations away from site facilities, offsite around
the perimeter, and in nearby and distant communities. Information about these ambient air monitoring
efforts and detailed descriptions of air sampling and analysis techniques are provided in
the DOE/RL-91-50, Hanford Site Environmental Monitoring Plan.

A network of continuously operating samplers at 78 locations across the Hanford Site was used during
2024 to monitor radioactive airborne materials in air near Hanford Site facilities and operations (details
are reported in Table 5-4 in DOE/HFO-2025-12). The samplers were primarily located at or within
approximately 500 m (1,640 ft) of sites and facilities that have the potential for or a history of
environmental releases. The samplers were primarily located in the prevailing downwind direction.
Samples were collected according to a schedule established before the 2019 monitoring year.

Airborne particle samples were collected at each location by drawing air through a cellulose filter.
The filters were collected bi-weekly, field-surveyed for gross radioactivity, held for at least 5 days, and
then analyzed for gross alpha and beta activity. The 5-day holding period is necessary to allow for
the decay of naturally occurring, short-lived radionuclides that would otherwise obscure the detection of
longer-lived radionuclides associated with emissions from nuclear facilities. The gross radioactivity
measurements were used to indicate changes in trends in the onsite facility environment.

Air sampling was conducted at 23 locations in the 200 West Area. The results of this monitoring program,
as reported in DOE/HFO-2025-12, indicated that all radionuclides of concern were below analytical
detection limits.
