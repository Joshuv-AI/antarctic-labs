Machine learning versus deep learning for screening ischemic stroke among asymptomatic population
Authors: Shenghua Qin, Yingjie Wang, Shuyuan Chu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Ischemic stroke puts great health burden in public. However, the diagnosis is based on head CT or MRI scanning. We aim to develop classifier models to screen asymptomatic ischemic stroke based on clinical data without imaging. The subjects (age ≥ 20 years) were recruited into our study who attended health examination. We developed models to classify subjects with or without ischemic stroke using support vector machine (SVM), random forest (RF), extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), and convolutional neural network (CNN). The candidate variables included sex, age, height, weight, blood pressure, blood lipid levels, fasting plasma glucose, hyperlipidemia, smoke history, alcohol drinking, diabetes, hypertension and coronary heart disease. Models included 141 subjects with small artery occlusion (SAO), 70 with large-artery atherosclerosis (LAA) and 211 controls. Classifier models to diagnose ischemic stroke, and further SAO and LAA, were developed with machine learning and deep learning algorithms. The best performance of model was based on RF, followed by CNN. The blood pressure, blood lipid and fasting plasma glucose were important factors and contributed to classifier models with machine learning algorithms. In conclusion, classifier models for ischemic stroke could be developed with machine learning and deep learning algorithms based on laboratory testing data without imaging. The models with RF showed best performance, followed by CNN. Those models may provide a tool for primary care doctors to screen asymptomatic stroke in general population, which could benefit to stroke patients from early diagnosis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-57045-9.

Introduction

Ischemic stroke accounts for 69.9 million prevalent cases, 7.8 million incident cases, 3.6 million deaths, and 70.4 million disability adjusted life years (DALYs) in 20211. For diagnosis of ischemic stroke, head computerized tomography (CT) scanning or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scanning is essential2. Thus, we aim to develop classifier models for asymptomatic patients based on basic laboratory tests without CT or MRI. The models would be applicable in primary health care institution to screen ischemic stroke in asymptomatic population.

Small artery occlusion (SAO) is a distinct type of ischemic stroke according to the Trial of ORG10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment (TOAST) classification. SAO is responsible for approximately 20% of all ischemic strokes in Europe3,4, but up to 31.3% of cases in China5. About 9.5% incidence in minor acute ischemic stroke are due to SAO6. Similarly, large-artery atherosclerosis (LAA) is also a type of TOAST classification. It’s a consequence of systemic and progressive atherosclerosis, and accounts for approximately 15% of all ischemic strokes7. Therefore, in this study, we further developed classifier models to screen SAO and LAA among general population.

In this study, we developed classifier models with machine learning algorithms, including support vector machine (SVM), random forest (RF) and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost). Those algorithms are classifiers, which have been widely used in medical research and showed good performance8–10. We also try to develop model with deep learning algorithm for tabular clinical data, so typical deep learning algorithm, i.e. convolutional neural network (CNN), was selected. CNN algorithm with tabular clinical data was reported for stroke prediction11. We further compared the performance of models with machine learning algorithm and deep learning algorithm. Our study would be helpful to screen asymptomatic ischemic stroke among general population in primary health care institutions.

However, the percentage of smoking and alcohol drinking were increased in LAA group and SAO group, when compared with controls.

Model performance to classify stroke from controls

The models to classify stroke patients from controls developed based on machine learning algorithm and deep learning algorithm. The sample size of training data was 337. The performance of machine learning models showed that the area under receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of SVM, RF and XGBoost models was 0.873, 0.906 and 0.903, respectively. The accuracy of SVM, RF and XGBoost models was 0.812, 0.859 and 0.788, respectively. The positive predictive value (PPV) respectively was 0.818, 0.859 and 0.791. The negative predictive value (NPV) was 0.667, 0.829 and 0.730. The sensitivity was 0.680, 0.880 and 0.800. The specificity was 0.914, 0.829 and 0.771. The Brier score was 0.151, 0.113 and 0.132. The F1 score was 0.813, 0.859 and 0.789. (Fig. 1; Table 2, Supplement file 1)

Discussion

In this study, we developed calssifier models to screen asymptomatic ischemic stroke, and further SAO and LAA with machine learning and deep learning algorithms. The best performance of model was based on RF, followed by CNN. The blood pressure, blood lipid and fasting plasma glucose were important factors and contributed to classifier models with machine learning algorithms.

Most models for stroke diagnose with artificial intelligence are based on imaging data12–14. In our study, all models didn’t include imaging variables, which would be applicable to screen asymptomatic ischemic stroke in primary health care institution. The diagnostic models with machine learning and deep learning algorithms for ischemic stroke were showed AUC between 0.873 and 0.906. The accuracy, PPV and F1 score of models for ischemic stroke were around or higher 0.8, confirming good performance of those models. Recent report on diagnostic model for ischemic stroke based on plasma biomarkers showed AUC was around 0.815. Recently, representation learning approaches for laboratory values was reported16. A meta-analysis of machine learning models in ischemic stroke showed pooled AUC of models was 0.87, in which the AUC of SVM was 0.82, random forest was 0.85, and XGBoost was 0.8217. Moreover, a comprehensive systematic review of deep learning models for ischemic stroke showed AUC was between 0.729 and 0.87618. Thus, our models were showed good performance in classifying ischemic stroke.

Our models for LAA were showed AUC between 0.850 and 0.934. The models for SAO were showed better performance than stroke or LAA, of which AUC were between 0.906 and 0.974. Previous study reported LAA and SAO models which were based on machine learning algorithms with demographics and clinical information19. That previous study showed the best AUC for LAA and SAO respectively were 0.919 and 0.918, which were similar with ours. Later, a study with 7443 Chinese samples showed the AUC of machine learning models for LAA was 0.67, and the AUC for SAO was 0.7020. Our models included fewer complex variables than those previous study, and were also showed good performance. Thus, our models may be more applicable in to screen ischemic stroke in primary health care institution.

Among models for ischemic stroke, SAO and LAA, those based on RF algorithm were showed highest AUC than others. AUC of CNN was followed that. Those suggested that RF and CNN would be appropriate algorithms to classify ischemic stroke. Previous study to classify diabetes showed similar results as ours, in which the AUC of RF model was similar with CNN model21. RF is classical algorithm of machine learning, and integrates decision trees to promote the efficiency and accuracy22, which could show important variables in the model. In contrast, CNN is deep learning algorithm, which are difficult to interpret the model’s findings. They are frequently referred to as “black boxes” because of their complicated internal workings.

In our study, machine learning models showed an increase of blood pressure, TG, TC, LDL and fasting plasma glucose levels, and a decrease of HDL level were important factors, and contributed to classifier models for stroke, SAO and LAA. Our findings were consistent with previous reports7,19. Those findings suggested that it would be benefit for subjects at risk of stroke to manage blood pressure, TG, TC, LDL, HDL and fasting plasma glucose level.

We acknowledged a major limitation that our models were not external validated. Thus, the generalizability of our models couldn’t be fully evaluated. Future study should validate the models with external data.

In conclusion, the classifier models for stroke, SAO and LAA could be developed with machine learning and deep learning algorithms based on data of laboratory tests without CT or MRI imaging. The models with RF algorithm showed best performance, followed by CNN. Those models may provide a tool for primary care doctors to screen asymptomatic ischemic stroke in general population, which could benefit to stroke patients from early diagnosis.

Methods

Subjects

Subjects aged ≥ 20 years who attended routine health examinations were recruited in Guilin People’s Hospital from January 2024 to October 2025. All laboratory testing and quality control were carried out by laboratory department of the hospital. The study protocol was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Guilin People’s Hospital, and confirmed to the declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from each subject.

Ischemic stroke patients were included as following: (1) age ≥ 20 years; (2) females or males; (3) were taken head CT scanning; (4) were diagnosed as acute ischemic stroke of small artery occlusion (SAO) or large-artery atherosclerosis (LAA) by neurologists when positive imaging was detected from CT scanning. They were excluded when they had neurological symptoms, had history of subarachnoid hemorrhage, cerebral hemorrhage or other hemorrhagic diseases, or he’s a patient with hematological diseases, systemic vasculitis, and vascular malformations, chronic infections, autoimmune diseases, malignant tumors, head injuries, brain tumors, multiple sclerosis, intracranial infections, venous sinus thrombosis, aneurysms, or arteriovenous malformations.

The inclusion criterion for controls were as following: (1) age ≥ 20 years; (2) females or males; (3) were taken head CT scanning to exclude stroke. The exclusion criterion for controls were the same as those for stroke patients.

Diagnostic criteria

Ischemic stroke diagnosis was based on the appearance of brain lesion in imaging from CT scanning2, since subjects took CT scanning for their health examination in our study. The SAO and LAA were diagnosed according to TOAST classification system23. Hyperlipidemia was diagnosed according to 2023 Chinese guideline24.

Hypertension patient was diagnosed according to 2023 ESH Guidelines25, or he had medical history of hypertension and was taking medicine for treatment. Diabetes patient was diagnosed according to the guidelines from the American Diabetes Association in 202326, or he was taking medicine to control blood glucose. Patient with Coronary heart disease (CHD) was diagnosed according to 2023 AHA/ACC/ACCP/ASPC/NLA/PCNA Guideline for Chronic Coronary Disease27, or he had medical history of CHD and were taking medicine for treatment.

Statistical analysis

Difference between groups were analyzed using t-test or chi-square test. P values < 0.05 were considered to be statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using R 4.4.3. Group data are expressed as the mean±standard deviation (SD).

Machine learning models and deep learning models

80% of data were randomly selected into training dataset to develop the model. The other 20% were testing dataset to test the model. The training data were standardized using z-score transformation, and the testing data were also transformed using the same parameters as those from the training data. The candidate variables in models included sex, age, height, weight, SP, DP, TC level, TG level, HDL level, LDL level, hyperlipidemia, fasting plasma glucose, smoke history, alcohol drinking, diabetes, hypertension and coronary heart disease.

Machine learning models were developed with SVM, RF and XGBoost algorithms using Python 3.7.6 programming language (http://www.python.org), scikit-learn 22.2 library (https://scikit-learn.org/stable/). With training dataset, hyper parameters were estimated using grid-search and 10-fold cross-validation. The kernel of SVM was linear. The SVM feature importance was selected by recursive feature elimination (RFE) from feature_selection.RFE in training dataset across 10-fold cross-validation . The feature importance scores of ranking were computed on the training set.

Deep learning model was developed with CNN algorithm under Keras 2.3.1 environment using Tensor Flow 2.0.1 as backend and Python 3.7.6 (http://www.python.org). The candidate variables in deep learning model were the same as those in machine learning models. Combinations of hyper parameters were selected to tune them. The candidate hyper parameters of CNN models for selection were filters 32 or 64, kernel size 2 or 3, dense units 64 or 128, dropout rate 0.3, 0.4 or 0.5, learning rate 0.001 or 0.0001.

The best hyper parameters for models were selected when it contributed into the highest efficiency of the model. The performance of models was assessed based on AUC, accuracy, PPV, NPV, sensitivity, specificity, , Brier score and F1 score.
