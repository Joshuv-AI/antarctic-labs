Optimized doubly-curved ARH-based metastructure to augment specific vibrational energy conversion using a hybrid constraint multi-objective genetic algorithm
Authors: Reza Ansarian, Mehrdad Motavasselolhagh, Roohollah Talebitooti, Davood Younesian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study focuses on the optimal design of an Auxetic Re-entrant Honeycomb (ARH) metamaterial structure aimed at enhancing energy harvesting (ES) performance in doubly-curved sandwich shells (DCSS). To accomplish this, the geometric parameters of the ARH are optimized using a Multi-Objective Genetic Algorithm (MOGA) and considering geometric and physical constraints. Initially, the study provides an accurate calculation for the density of the ARH layer and compares it with previous models. Then, the governing electromechanical coupled equations are derived using Modified First-Order Shear Deformation Theory (MFSDT) and Hamilton’s principle, assuming simple support boundary conditions at all four edges. Utilizing Galerkin’s principle, the frequency response function (FRF) of the specific output power is analyzed. In addition to evaluating the free vibration results against authoritative references, the finite element simulation is also used to validate both the eigenfrequencies and FRFs data derived from the analytical solution. The optimization algorithm aims to maximize the specific power output while minimizing the resonant frequency of the structure. The results from optimizing the ARH core layer demonstrate that the new designs achieve ES capabilities that are 42.96 times greater than those with an isotropic core and 6.92 times greater than those of the conventional ARH. Additionally, the optimized structures show improved stiffness metrics compared to their pre-optimization counterparts. Thus, this design successfully enhances both ES and structural behavior, particularly in terms of stiffness.Keywords: Multi-objective genetic algorithm (MOGA), Energy harvesting, Doubly-curved sandwich shell (DCSS), ARH metastructure, Specific powerSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Vibration-based piezoelectric ES has received widespread attention in recent years as one of the most significant approaches in mechanical engineering to energize self-powered sensing networks, monitoring systems, and smart structures. Liang, et al.1 demonstrated that, despite ongoing advancements in the development of piezoelectric materials, the primary limitation in power harvesting stems from structural dynamics. As a result, effective energy conversion is generally limited to narrow regions around the resonant frequencies. Subsequent studies also clarified that this limitation is fundamentally mechanical and is not only related to the design of the electrical circuits, but also involves factors such as stiffness distribution, boundary conditions, and the modal properties of the structure2,3. Recognizing this challenge, Shin, et al.4 demonstrated that the simultaneous activation of multiple vibrational modes can significantly enhance the total energy harvested, particularly when the performance criterion focuses on the amount of energy generated within a specific frequency range. Additionally, nonlinear dynamic effects, whether intentionally introduced through geometric design or arising naturally at higher excitation amplitudes, have been identified as effective methods to increase the operational bandwidth and reduce sensitivity to frequency mismatches5–7. Furthermore, mechanical tuning strategies, such as adjusting the effective inertia and stiffness distribution, have been reported as practical ways to improve resonance-based ES without altering the active material system8–10.

Curvature is a common feature in many real mechanical components, making it important to explore piezoelectric ES from curved plates and shells. Early electromechanical models demonstrated that curvature leads to a coupling of membrane and bending behaviors, fundamentally changing the distribution of strains available for piezoelectric energy conversion11,12. Jha and Ray13 found that in doubly-curved shells, this coupling can enhance strain energy in specific resonant modes, resulting in a higher electrical output compared to flat structures. Numerical and experimental studies on doubly-curved shells have confirmed that the presence of curvature enhances the modal spectrum and leads to stronger resonant responses under harmonic excitation14,15. Furthermore, even moderate curvature can alter the nodal patterns of vibration modes and change the regions of strain concentration. This is particularly important when considering continuous piezoelectric layers on the face sheets16,17. Recent studies have expanded these concepts to include layered and sandwich shells, demonstrating that curvature interacts with layer sequencing and shear effects. This interaction provides a broader design space for vibration analysis, active control, and resonance-based ES18–20. Moreover, significant research has focused on investigating and analyzing the dynamic-vibrational behavior of composite sandwich cylindrical shells under various boundary and environmental conditions21,22. Dong, et al.23 developed a composite corrugated shell structure that offers lightweight advantages and superior mechanical properties for engineering applications such as aerospace. In another study, Dong, et al.24 presented a more accurate and comprehensive analytical model for corrugated cylindrical sandwich shells in real engineering applications, such as engine shells and aircraft fuselages. This model aids in detecting and preventing dynamic behavior issues in components during service. Additionally, the combination of a honeycomb structure with a corrugated structure as a core layer has been explored to determine the vibration behavior of cylindrical sandwich shells under different environmental and thermal conditions25,26.

Given the strong dependence of the amount of energy harvested by piezoelectric systems on factors such as geometric configuration, stiffness distribution, and electromechanical arrangement, optimization has become a key approach in designing these ES systems. Biswal, et al.27 demonstrated that classical parametric optimization, which adjusts geometric dimensions, thickness ratios, and boundary conditions, can significantly enhance the harvested power at resonant frequencies for planar harvesters. Following this, He, et al.28 introduced topology optimization techniques to address challenges like charge neutralization and inefficient strain utilization. They showed that optimal material redistribution could substantially improve electromechanical coupling and energy output. Similar findings have been reported in studies focused on multi-degree-of-freedom planar harvesters driven by topology optimization29. As optimization methods advanced, research efforts naturally shifted towards broadband and multi-amplitude objectives. Hu, et al.30 utilized evolutionary and combinatorial strategies to optimize multi-amplitude harvesters, achieving notable improvements in ES within a specific frequency range. Concurrently, new frameworks for broadband topology optimization have been developed, which enhance power generation efficiency across a broader frequency spectrum by selectively activating multiple vibrational modes31. In recent years, optimization frameworks have been enhanced to include shape and geometry-based designs. Peralta, et al.32 applied shape optimization to piezoelectric energy harvesters using isogeometric analysis (IGA), enabling smooth geometric modifications. By optimizing for peak voltage output at a target frequency, they demonstrated improved ES capabilities. Their approach was later refined by integrating IGA with particle swarm optimization, resulting in better convergence and stability for the design process33. Additionally, utilizing parametric analysis alongside shape optimization has demonstrated that unconventional geometries can effectively tune natural frequencies and ES output in a targeted manner34. On the other hand, optimization in layered and composite structures has introduced greater design flexibility. Shukla and Pradyumna35 demonstrated that optimizing composite laminates with variable stiffness can significantly improve ES efficiency and broadband performance, by simultaneously adjusting fiber orientation and structural configuration. In addition to deterministic formulations, multi-objective and robust optimization strategies are increasingly being utilized to address realistic mechanical constraints. These frameworks aim to maximize harvested energy while also limiting stress, mass, and displacement36,37. To reduce the computational cost of repeated finite element analyses, particularly for shell and curved plate models, researchers have explored surrogate model-based and data-driven optimization methods. Jegadeesan, et al.38 utilized an artificial neural network-genetic algorithm (ANN–GA) framework to expedite the optimization process of vibration harvesters. Similarly, Selim, et al.39 demonstrated the effectiveness of machine learning approaches in navigating high-dimensional design spaces. Mhiri, et al.40 proposed an optimization approach for improving piezoelectric ES performance using a neural network surrogate model and a genetic algorithm. In a related effort, topology-driven designs that prioritize manufacturability have been proposed to address challenges in manufacturing and the local polarization of piezoelectric materials41. The optimization of nonlinear harvesters with self-coupling structures has been proposed, focusing on enhancing both bandwidth and output power simultaneously42. Additionally, there have been targeted reports on the modeling and optimization of harvesters for specific frequency ranges. This approach is closely related to formulating goals aimed at maximizing the harvested energy within the frequency range near resonances43.

Mechanical metamaterials offer innovative ways to manipulate vibrational properties and strain transfer beyond traditional geometric and material designs. Among these materials, auxetic structures, which are characterized by a negative Poisson’s ratio, can enhance both transverse and shear strain components due to their unique deformation mechanisms. This makes them particularly effective for piezoelectric energy conversion44. Subsequent research has demonstrated that utilizing auxetic substrates and configurations can significantly improve electromechanical coupling and ES output by concentrating strain under resonant excitation45,46. In sandwich configurations, auxetic re-entrant honeycomb (ARH) cores significantly influence the dynamic stiffness and the shape of vibration modes. This means that the geometry of the unit cell can be adjusted to control resonance and strain distribution47,48. Recent reviews and comparative studies have highlighted that designs based on acoustics hold great promise, particularly for tuning resonance and enhancing the effective bandwidth in vibration ES systems49,50. However, most existing research has concentrated on beams or flat plates, typically using fixed auxetic geometries51. As a result, the combined effects of curvature, sandwich structures, and the systematic tuning of ARH cores on energy conversion performance, especially in doubly-curved shells, have not been thoroughly explored in the mechanical engineering literature.

This study aims to determine the optimal design of an ARH meta-structure to enhance the ES performance of doubly-curved structures. The key point of this research lies in the application of a MOGA that considers the geometric parameters of the ARH, in conjunction with geometric and physical constraints, to improve the ES capabilities of a DCSS. Initially, the study presents an exact calculation for the density of the ARH layer and compares this with previous models. The electromechanical governing coupled equations are then derived using the MFSDT and Hamilton’s principle, assuming simple support boundary conditions on all four edges. By employing the Galerkin principle and simplifying the resulting differential equations, the FRF of specific power output is investigated based on the geometric and physical parameters of the structure. Next, the study identifies parameters with a significant influence on the ES results, which are then selected as optimization variables. The objective functions focus on maximizing specific output power and minimizing the structure’s resonance frequency. Optimal results are generated using the genetic algorithm, leading to an evolutionary improvement in ES capability of the DCSS with an auxetic meta-structure core after optimization. The discrepancy between the analytical and finite element natural frequencies can be attributed to three main factors. First, the complex geometry of the ARH structure poses challenges for mesh generation, particularly at the angled corners of doubly-curved shells, leading to localized mesh quality degradation that can affect system stiffness through variations in the effective degrees of freedom. Second, the auxetic structure is not symmetric and exhibits direction-dependent properties along the and axes, reducing simulation accuracy in regions with higher strain where mesh quality is suboptimal. Third, the mechanical properties of the auxetic layer derived from its geometry vary dynamically during the simulation, whereas they are assumed to be constant in the analytical formulation. Therefore, these factors contribute to the observed discrepancy and make the largest error of 5.92% in the third mode justified, confirming both approaches’ correctness and accuracy.

Electric resistance

To explore how electric resistance affects the maximum specific power generated and the resonance frequency, three different ARH-core shells, as shown in Fig. 5, were examined along with an isotropic-core shell. As illustrated in Fig. 6, ARH-core shells consistently generate significantly more power across the range of electric resistance values. Notably, the shell with the AUX-II core produces approximately 8.8 times greater specific power at its optimal resistance (around 10 Ω) compared to the isotropic-core shell at its optimum (about 40 Ω). In addition, the ARH-core shells tend to have lower resonance frequencies. While the AUX-II core delivers the highest specific power, the AUX-III core achieves the lowest resonance frequency. The maximum specific power for ARH-core shells fluctuates with resistance and typically peaks near 10 Ω, whereas the isotropic-core shell follows a distinct pattern with its optimal value at 40 Ω. This difference is attributed to the unique mechanical properties, such as negative Poisson’s ratios, of ARH-core structures compared to conventional shells.

Auxetic geometric parameters

The four-dimensional graphs presented in Fig. 7 analyze how ARH geometric parameters affect the ES outputs, with the z-axis representing the maximum generated specific power and the color bar as the fourth dimension indicating the resonance frequency. The electric resistance of is considered, which is optimal for the ARH-core shells in the proposed configuration, as discussed previously. The analysis reveals that increasing M and N results in a decrease in specific power; specifically, the lowest values of M and N (both set at 5) yield about four times the specific power compared to configurations with M and N at 15. Additionally, the resonance frequency decreases with an increase in M or a decrease in N. In analyzing this observation, it can be stated that increasing the number of unit cells ( M and N) increases the elastic modulus of the layer and consequently increases the structural stiffness. This increase in stiffness reduces the strain transferred from the ARH core layer to the piezoelectric layers, thereby reducing the output power. Additionally, as the density of the core layer rises due to an increase in the number of ARH cells, there is a further decline in specific power. However, directly analyzing the changes in resonance frequency is complicated by the simultaneous variations in stiffness and mass, as well as the indirect relationships of these parameters with the respective values.

Conclusion

This study mainly aims to determine the optimal design of an ARH structure to enhance the ES capabilities in double-curved sandwich structures. The optimization process utilizes a combination of a MOGA, geometric and physical constraints, and the ARH parameters (parameters with significant impact on the results) as optimization variables. To facilitate this, the coupled electromechanical governing equations using MFSDT and Hamilton’s principle were extracted and simplified, assuming simple support boundary conditions at all four edges. Employing Galerkin’s principle, the FRF for the specific output power was then derived based on the geometric and physical parameters of the structure. The optimization focuses on two main objective functions: maximizing the specific output power and minimizing the resonant frequency of the structure. In the results section, we first extracted the first six natural frequencies and their corresponding shape modes from the free vibration solution of the structures, using both semi-analytical solutions in MATLAB software and the simulated model in COMSOL software. The comparison between the two approaches revealed a maximum error of 5.92%, which indicates an acceptable level of accuracy. Additionally, after analyzing how various parameters affect the ES performance of the structure and implementing the optimization algorithm on the initial design, the key results are presented as follows:

Increasing the values of M and N results in a decrease in specific power. In particular, configurations with the lowest values of M and N (both set to 5) produce approximately four times the specific power compared to configurations where M and N are both set to 15. Additionally, the resonant frequency of the structure decreases as M increases or as N decreases.

This study demonstrates that utilizing optimized ARH structures as the core layer in sandwich structures can improve both ES capabilities and structural stiffness by adjusting the geometric and structural parameters of the ARH. Therefore, future research will focus on enhancing ES conditions in sandwich shells by investigating composite structures that incorporate advanced ARH metamaterials. The present study assumes simply-supported boundary conditions and harmonic excitation; extending the framework to other boundary conditions and arbitrary loading profiles remains an important direction for future work.
