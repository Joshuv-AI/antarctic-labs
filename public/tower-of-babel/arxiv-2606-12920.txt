An Oskolkov–Zhizhiashvili Criterion for Rectangular Fourier Sums
Ushangi Goginava

Abstract
. Let S 𝐧 ​ f S_{\mathbf{n}}f denote the symmetric rectangular partial sums of the trigonometric Fourier series of a function on the d d -dimensional torus. We prove a summable endpoint criterion at the Zhizhiashvili critical scale for all d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 and 1 ≤ p ≤ 2 1\leq p\leq 2 . The criterion allows a general summable secondary weight at the iterated-logarithmic level and contains, as special cases, a double-logarithmic endpoint criterion and an L p L^{p} Oskolkov-type corollary. In particular, it answers the Zhizhiashvili–Marcinkiewicz problem for 1 < p < 2 1<p<2 and sharpens Zhizhiashvili’s classical sufficient conditions in the endpoint cases p = 1 p=1 and p = 2 p=2 .

1.
Introduction

The one-dimensional theory of pointwise convergence of Fourier series is
shaped by the Carleson–Hunt theorem: if
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
and
1<p<\infty
, then the Fourier series of
f
converges almost everywhere to
f

[
5
,
11
]
. In several variables the situation is
essentially different, because there are many inequivalent ways to let the
set of frequencies tend to infinity. In this paper we study symmetric
rectangular partial sums

S_{\mathbf{n}}f(x)=\sum_{|k_{1}|\leq n_{1}}\cdots\sum_{|k_{d}|\leq n_{d}}\widehat{f}(\mathbf{k})e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot x},\qquad\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}),

and convergence in the sense of Pringsheim, that is,

S_{\mathbf{n}}f(x)\to f(x)\qquad\text{as}\qquad\min_{1\leq j\leq d}n_{j}\to\infty.

A fundamental warning was given by Fefferman
[
9
]
. Unlike the one-dimensional
Carleson–Hunt theorem, continuity alone does not force Pringsheim
convergence of rectangular partial sums in dimension two. More precisely,
Fefferman constructed continuous functions of two variables whose double
Fourier series diverge in the rectangular sense; this result made clear that
multidimensional rectangular convergence requires additional quantitative
smoothness assumptions
[
9
,
8
]
. We
shall use the
L^{p}
modulus of continuity

\omega(\delta,f)_{p}=\sup_{\|h\|_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\leq\delta}\left\|f(\cdot+h)-f(\cdot)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

as the measure of that smoothness.

The systematic study of almost everywhere convergence of multiple Fourier
series under logarithmic moduli of continuity was initiated by
Zhizhiashvili
[
18
, Ch. 2]
. The following theorem is the starting point of the present
paper.

Theorem Zh

(Zhizhiashvili)
.

Let
d\geq 2
. The following assertions hold.

(i)

If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and, for some
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.1)

\omega(\delta,f)_{1}\leq\frac{A(f)}{(\log(1/\delta))^{d+\varepsilon}},\qquad 0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{2},

then the rectangular Fourier series of
f
converges almost everywhere in
the sense of Pringsheim.

(ii)

If
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and, for some
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.2)

\omega(\delta,f)_{2}\leq\frac{A(f)}{(\log(1/\delta))^{d/2+\varepsilon}},\qquad 0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{2},

then the rectangular Fourier series of
f
converges almost everywhere in
the sense of Pringsheim.

This is one of the central results in Zhizhiashvili’s theory of multiple
trigonometric Fourier series; see
[
17
,
18
]
. Zhizhiashvili explicitly asked how sharp the logarithmic powers in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are. In particular, the exact
endpoint, where the positive number
\varepsilon
is removed, is a natural
multidimensional analogue of a classical Zygmund-type problem in one
dimension.

The sharpness picture is delicate. Bakhbukh and Nikishin proved that there
are continuous functions with logarithmic modulus of continuity of order
1/\log(1/\delta)
for which the double Fourier series diverges everywhere
[
1
,
8
]
. In even
dimensions Bakhvalov later constructed continuous examples with logarithmic
moduli matching the expected critical powers and with rectangular divergence
in the
\lambda
-restricted sense
[
2
,
3
]
. These
results show that the endpoint cannot be treated as a routine consequence of
uniform continuity.

A second major benchmark is Oskolkov’s two-dimensional refinement (see
[
12
]
). In one of
its standard forms, Oskolkov’s theorem says that if
f\in C(\mathbb{T}^{2})

and the uniform modulus satisfies

\omega(\delta,f)_{\infty}\,=o\left(\frac{1}{\log(1/\delta)\,\log\log\log(e^{e}/\delta)}\right),\qquad\delta\rightarrow 0+,

then the double Fourier series converges almost everywhere in the
rectangular sense; see
[
12
]
and the discussion in
[
3
,
8
]
. This theorem is genuinely two-dimensional.
The corresponding sharp Oskolkov-type assertion for dimensions
d>2
appears
to remain open. This distinction is important below: our
L^{p}
consequence
is a proved
d
-dimensional sufficient criterion with a summable secondary
factor, not a solution of the sharp higher-dimensional Oskolkov problem.

There is also a Marcinkiewicz–Plessner line of ideas. In dimension one,
Plessner’s
L^{2}
theorem and Marcinkiewicz’s extension to
L^{p}
,
p>1
, give
almost everywhere estimates for Fourier partial sums in terms of Fourier
coefficients and moduli of continuity; see
[
4
,
19
]
.
Zhizhiashvili
[
18
, Ch. 2]
extended the Plessner part to several variables and used it in
the proof of Theorem Zh (ii). He then asked for a
multidimensional analogue of Marcinkiewicz’s theorem. In the present setting
this leads to the following Zhizhiashvili–Marcinkiewicz problem (see
[
18
, Ch. 2]
).

Problem 1.1

(Zhizhiashvili–Marcinkiewicz problem)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
1<p<2
. Does the logarithmic
condition

(1.3)

\omega(\delta,f)_{p}\leq\frac{A(f)}{(\log(1/\delta))^{d/p+\varepsilon}},\qquad 0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{2},

with arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, imply almost everywhere Pringsheim
convergence of the rectangular Fourier series of every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
?

The main theorem below answers Problem
1.1
. It is a
near-endpoint result at the dimensionally correct logarithmic power and also
includes the endpoint spaces
p=1
and
p=2
. Thus the improvement is new
even in the cases covered by Zhizhiashvili’s original
L^{1}
and
L^{2}

theorem.

For
0<\delta\leq 1/2
set

(1.4)

\Lambda_{1}(\delta)=\log(1/\delta),\qquad\Lambda_{2}(\delta)=\log\log(e^{e}/\delta),\qquad\Lambda_{3}(\delta)=\log\log\log(e^{e^{e}}/\delta).

The constants inside the logarithms are used only to keep the expressions
positive on the whole interval
0<\delta\leq 1/2
.

Theorem 1.2

(Main summable Zhizhiashvili endpoint theorem)
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
1\leq p\leq 2
, and let
\psi:[1,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be non-increasing and satisfy

(1.5)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\psi(k)<\infty.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Assume that

(1.6)

\omega(\delta,f)_{p}\leq A(f)\,\Lambda_{1}(\delta)^{-d/p}\,\psi(\Lambda_{2}(\delta)),\qquad 0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Then the rectangular partial sums
converge to
f
almost everywhere in the sense of Pringsheim; that is,

S_{\mathbf{n}}f(x)\to f(x)\qquad\text{for almost every }x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}

as
\min_{1\leq j\leq d}n_{j}\to\infty
.

Corollary 1.3

(Double-logarithmic endpoint criterion)
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
1\leq p\leq 2
,
\eta>0
, and let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. If

(1.7)

\omega(\delta,f)_{p}\leq\frac{A(f)}{(\log(1/\delta))^{d/p}(\log\log(e^{e}/\delta))^{1+\eta}},\qquad 0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{2},

then
S_{\mathbf{n}}f(x)\to f(x)
almost everywhere in the sense of
Pringsheim.

Corollary 1.4

(
d
-dimensional
L^{p}
Oskolkov-type consequence)
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\eta>0
,
1\leq p\leq 2
, and let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. If

(1.8)

\omega(\delta,f)_{p}\leq\frac{A(f)}{(\log(1/\delta))^{d/p}\log\log(e^{e}/\delta)(\log\log\log(e^{e^{e}}/\delta))^{1+\eta}},\qquad 0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{2},

then the
d
-dimensional rectangular Fourier series of
f
converges almost
everywhere in the sense of Pringsheim.

Remark 1.5

(The Oskolkov comparison)
.

Corollary
1.4
may be viewed as a
d
-dimensional
Oskolkov-type analogue at the
L^{p}
Zhizhiashvili scale. The comparison has
the following precise meaning. Oskolkov’s classical theorem is a
uniform-norm theorem for continuous functions on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, with a
different secondary logarithmic scale. Corollary
1.4
is
instead an
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
sufficient criterion valid in every dimension

d\geq 2
. Thus Corollary
1.4
should not be read as a
solution of the sharp higher-dimensional Oskolkov problem.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorem
1.2
is stronger than a solution of Problem
1.1
, because it replaces the arbitrary positive
logarithmic margin in (
1.3
) by the critical
power and an arbitrary summable iterated-logarithmic weight. Corollary
1.3
gives the simple double-logarithmic form, while Corollary
1.4
gives the
L^{p}
Oskolkov-type form emphasized above.
In particular, the theorem contains the endpoint cases
p=1
and
p=2
and
improves the sufficient conditions in Theorem Zh
from
(\log(1/\delta))^{-d/p-\varepsilon}
to the sharper critical scale
with summable secondary factors. It should be noted that the case
p=1
of Corollary
1.4
was established by Stokolos
[
16
]
. For the case
p=2
, we also refer to
[
10
]
, where almost everywhere convergence of multiple Fourier series with respect to a general orthonormal system was established.

2.
Notation and rectangular partial sums

Let

\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}),\qquad\mathbb{T}^{d}=(\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}))^{d},

equipped with normalized Haar measure
dm_{d}(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\,dx
. For
t\in\mathbb{T}
write

|t|_{\mathbb{T}}=\operatorname{dist}(t,2\pi\mathbb{Z})\in[0,\pi],

and for
h=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{d})\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
put

\|h\|_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}=\max_{1\leq j\leq d}|h_{j}|_{\mathbb{T}}.

For
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, set

\mathbf{k}\cdot x=k_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+k_{d}x_{d},\qquad e_{\mathbf{k}}(x)=e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot x}.

If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, its Fourier coefficients are

\widehat{f}(\mathbf{k})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(x)e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot x}\,dm_{d}(x),\qquad\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

For
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, the symmetric
rectangular partial sum is

(2.1)

S_{\mathbf{n}}f(x)=\sum_{|k_{1}|\leq n_{1}}\cdots\sum_{|k_{d}|\leq n_{d}}\widehat{f}(\mathbf{k})e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot x}.

Equivalently,

S_{\mathbf{n}}f=f*(D_{n_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes D_{n_{d}}),

where

D_{n}(t)=\sum_{|m|\leq n}e^{imt}

is the one-dimensional Dirichlet kernel.

For
1\leq p<\infty
,

\left\|f\right\|_{p}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}\,dm_{d}(x)\right)^{1/p},

and for
p=\infty
we use the essential supremum norm. The modulus of
continuity is

(2.2)

\omega(\delta,f)_{p}=\sup_{\|h\|_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\leq\delta}\left\|f(\cdot+h)-f(\cdot)\right\|_{p},\qquad\delta>0.

All logarithms are natural. Constants denoted by
C
may change from line to
line; dependence on parameters is indicated by subscripts.

3.
de la Vallée Poussin approximation

We recall the product de la Vallée Poussin means and the approximation
estimate needed in the proof. Let

F_{N}(t)=\sum_{|m|<N}\left(1-\frac{|m|}{N}\right)e^{imt},\qquad N\geq 1,

be the Fejér kernel. With normalized measure,
F_{N}\geq 0
,
\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{N}\,dm_{1}=1
, and
\left\|F_{N}\right\|_{1}=1
. Define

(3.1)

V_{N}(t)=2F_{2N}(t)-F_{N}(t),\qquad N\geq 1.

Its Fourier multiplier
v_{N}
is given by

(3.2)

v_{N}(m)=\begin{cases}1,&|m|\leq N,\\
2-|m|/N,&N<|m|<2N,\\
0,&|m|\geq 2N.\end{cases}

Thus
V_{N}
reproduces frequencies
|m|\leq N
and has spectrum in
(-2N,2N)
.
Also

(3.3)

\left\|V_{N}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\leq 2\left\|F_{2N}\right\|_{1}+\left\|F_{N}\right\|_{1}=3.

For
d\geq 2
, put

V_{N}^{[d]}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}V_{N}(x_{j}),

and define

(3.4)

\mathcal{V}_{N}f=f*V_{N}^{[d]}.

Equivalently,

(3.5)

\widehat{\mathcal{V}_{N}f}(\mathbf{k})=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}v_{N}(k_{j})\right)\widehat{f}(\mathbf{k}),\qquad\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

The following elementary properties will be used repeatedly.

Lemma 3.1

(de la Vallée Poussin properties)
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
N\geq 1
.

(V1)

\mathcal{V}_{N}f
has spectrum contained in
[-2N,2N]^{d}
.

(V2)

If
Q
is a trigonometric polynomial with spectrum contained in
[-N,N]^{d}
, then
\mathcal{V}_{N}Q=Q
.

(V3)

\left\|\mathcal{V}_{N}f\right\|_{p}\leq 3^{d}\left\|f\right\|_{p}
.

(V4)

If
1\leq p<\infty
, then

(3.6)

\left\|f-\mathcal{V}_{N}f\right\|_{p}\leq C_{p,d}\,\omega(1/N,f)_{p},\qquad N\geq 2.

Proof.

The first two assertions follow directly from the multiplier formula (
3.2
). The third follows from (
3.3
) and
Young’s inequality, because
\left\|V_{N}^{[d]}\right\|_{1}\leq 3^{d}
.

For the last assertion, let
E_{N}(f)_{p}
be the best
L^{p}
approximation of
f

by trigonometric polynomials with spectrum contained in
[-N,N]^{d}
. The
multidimensional Jackson inequality gives

E_{N}(f)_{p}\leq C_{p,d}\omega(1/N,f)_{p};

see, for example,
[
7
,
19
]
. Choose
Q_{N}
with
\left\|f-Q_{N}\right\|_{p}\leq 2E_{N}(f)_{p}
. Since
\mathcal{V}_{N}Q_{N}=Q_{N}
,

f-\mathcal{V}_{N}f=(f-Q_{N})-\mathcal{V}_{N}(f-Q_{N}),

and the
L^{p}
boundedness of
\mathcal{V}_{N}
yields (
3.6
).
∎

4.
Finite rectangular maximal estimates

For
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
define the fixed finite rectangular maximal operator

(4.1)

\mathcal{M}_{r}f(x)=\sup_{0\leq n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\leq 2^{r}}|S_{\mathbf{n}}f(x)|.

This definition is independent of the spectrum of any polynomial to which it
will later be applied.

We also write

(4.2)

\mathcal{M}P(x)=\sup_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}|S_{\mathbf{n}}P(x)|

for a trigonometric polynomial
P
.

Proposition 4.1

(Finite maximal estimate)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

(i)

For every
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

(4.3)

\left\|\mathcal{M}_{r}f\right\|_{1}\leq C_{d}(1+r)^{d}\left\|f\right\|_{1}.

(ii)

For every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

(4.4)

\left\|\mathcal{M}_{r}f\right\|_{2}\leq C_{d}(1+r)^{d/2}\left\|f\right\|_{2}.

(iii)

For
1<p<2
and every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

(4.5)

\left\|\mathcal{M}_{r}f\right\|_{p}\leq C_{p,d}(1+r)^{d/p}\left\|f\right\|_{p}.

Consequently, if
P
is a trigonometric polynomial with
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{P}\subset[-2^{r},2^{r}]^{d}
, then

(4.6)

\left\|\mathcal{M}P\right\|_{p}\leq C_{p,d}(1+r)^{d/p}\left\|P\right\|_{p},\qquad 1\leq p\leq 2.

Proof.

We first prove the
L^{1}
estimate. Put

D_{r}^{*}(t)=\sup_{0\leq n\leq 2^{r}}|D_{n}(t)|.

The standard Dirichlet-kernel bound

|D_{n}(t)|\leq C\min\{n+1,|t|_{\mathbb{T}}^{-1}\}

implies

(4.7)

\left\|D_{r}^{*}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C(1+r).

For
0\leq n_{j}\leq 2^{r}
,

|S_{\mathbf{n}}f|\leq|f|*(D_{r}^{*}\otimes\cdots\otimes D_{r}^{*}).

Taking the supremum over such
\mathbf{n}
and applying Young’s inequality
gives (
4.3
).

For
L^{2}
, let
Q_{r}f=S_{(2^{r},\ldots,2^{r})}f
. Then
S_{\mathbf{n}}f=S_{\mathbf{n}}Q_{r}f
whenever
0\leq n_{j}\leq 2^{r}
, and hence

\mathcal{M}_{r}f\leq\sup_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}|S_{\mathbf{n}}Q_{r}f|.

We use the following product-logarithmic maximal inequality for rectangular
partial sums. In the one-sided form it is the maximal inequality appearing
in the proof of the Main Theorem of Chen
[
6
, Main Theorem, p. 549,
inequality (*)]
. In dimension
2
, a sharper predecessor was
proved by Sjölin
[
13
, Theorem 7.2, pp. 85–86]
; see also the
partial-summation argument in
[
14
, p. 4]
. The symmetric version
follows from the one-sided estimate by decomposing the frequency space into
the
2^{d}
orthants. For every trigonometric polynomial
Q
on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
these results give

(4.8)

\left\|\sup_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}|S_{\mathbf{n}}Q|\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{d}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\widehat{Q}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\log(2+|k_{j}|).

Since
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{Q_{r}f}\subset[-2^{r},2^{r}]^{d}
, the product of
logarithms in (
4.8
) is bounded by
C_{d}(1+r)^{d}
on the support.
Parseval’s identity therefore gives

\left\|\mathcal{M}_{r}f\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{d}(1+r)^{d}\left\|Q_{r}f\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{d}(1+r)^{d}\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2},

which proves (
4.4
).

For
1<p<2
, apply the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem to the fixed
sublinear operator
\mathcal{M}_{r}
. If
\theta\in(0,1)
is determined by

\frac{1}{p}=(1-\theta)\cdot 1+\theta\cdot\frac{1}{2},

then the interpolated power of
(1+r)
is

d(1-\theta)+\frac{d}{2}\theta=\frac{d}{p}.

This proves (
4.5
). We cite
[
15
]
for this
standard interpolation theorem for sublinear operators.

Finally suppose
P
has spectrum in
[-2^{r},2^{r}]^{d}
. If some
n_{j}>2^{r}
,
replacing
n_{j}
by
2^{r}
does not change the action of the
j
-th
one-dimensional frequency projection on
P
. Hence
\mathcal{M}P=\mathcal{M}_{r}P
. Estimate (
4.6
) follows from (
4.3
) when
p=1
, from (
4.4
) when
p=2
, and from (
4.5
) when
1<p<2
.
∎

5.
Proof of the main theorem and its consequences

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let

r_{k}:=2^{k},\qquad N_{k}:=2^{r_{k}-1},\qquad k=1,2,\ldots,

Set

P_{k}=\mathcal{V}_{N_{k}}f,\qquad P_{0}=\mathcal{V}_{1}f.

By Lemma
3.1
,

(5.1)

\left\|f-P_{k}\right\|_{p}\leq C_{p,d}\omega(1/N_{k},f)_{p}.

Since

\log N_{k}\asymp r_{k},\qquad\Lambda_{2}(1/N_{k})=\log\log(e^{e}N_{k})\asymp\log(e+r_{k})\asymp k,

there exists a constant
c=c(d,p)>0
such that, for all sufficiently large
k
,

(5.2)

\left\|f-P_{k}\right\|_{p}\leq C_{p,d}A(f)\,r_{k}^{-d/p}\,\psi(ck).

In particular,
P_{k}\to f
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

Define the frequency blocks

g_{0}=P_{0},\qquad g_{k}=P_{k}-P_{k-1},\quad k\geq 1.

Then

(5.3)

f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}g_{k}\qquad\text{in }L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}).

Moreover, by Lemma
3.1
,
g_{k}
is a trigonometric
polynomial with spectrum contained in
[-2^{r_{k}},2^{r_{k}}]^{d}
.

Put

M_{k}(x)=\mathcal{M}g_{k}(x)=\sup_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}|S_{\mathbf{n}}g_{k}(x)|.

For every
1\leq p\leq 2
, Proposition
4.1
gives

(5.4)

\left\|M_{k}\right\|_{p}\leq C_{p,d}(1+r_{k})^{d/p}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{p}.

On the other hand, (
5.2
) yields, for
k\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f-P_{k}\right\|_{p}+\left\|f-P_{k-1}\right\|_{p}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,d}A(f)\,r_{k-1}^{-d/p}\,\psi(c(k-1)).

Combining (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), and using
r_{k}=2r_{k-1}
, we obtain

(5.6)

\left\|M_{k}\right\|_{p}\leq C_{p,d}A(f)\,\psi(c(k-1)),\qquad k\geq 2.

Because
\psi
is non-increasing and (
1.5
) holds,

(5.7)

\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\psi(c(k-1))<\infty.

For example, each interval
[m/c,(m+1)/c)
contains at most a bounded number
of integers. Hence

(5.8)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|M_{k}\right\|_{p}<\infty.

Since the Haar measure of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is normalized and
p\geq 1
, this
implies

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|M_{k}\right\|_{1}<\infty.

By monotone convergence,

(5.9)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}M_{k}(x)<\infty

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Let
E_{0}
be the set of points for
which (
5.9
) holds.

For
x\in E_{0}
, the numerical series
\sum_{k\geq 1}g_{k}(x)
converges
absolutely, because
|g_{k}(x)|\leq M_{k}(x)
. Define

F(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}g_{k}(x),\qquad x\in E_{0}.

Since
P_{K}=\sum_{k=0}^{K}g_{k}
and
P_{K}\to f
in
L^{p}
, we have
P_{K}\to f
in
measure. On the other hand,
P_{K}(x)\to F(x)
for every
x\in E_{0}
, and hence

P_{K}\to F
in measure on
E_{0}
. By uniqueness of limits in measure,
F=f

almost everywhere on
E_{0}
. Therefore there is a set
E\subset E_{0}
of full
measure such that

(5.10)

f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}g_{k}(x),\qquad x\in E.

Fix
x\in E
. For
K\geq 1
, put

R_{K}=f-P_{K}.

By (
5.10
),

(5.11)

|R_{K}(x)|\leq\sum_{k=K+1}^{\infty}M_{k}(x).

The polynomial
P_{K}
has spectrum contained in
[-2^{r_{K}},2^{r_{K}}]^{d}
, and
therefore

(5.12)

S_{\mathbf{n}}P_{K}=P_{K}\qquad\text{whenever }n_{j}\geq 2^{r_{K}}\text{ for all }j.

For such
\mathbf{n}
,

(5.13)

S_{\mathbf{n}}f(x)-f(x)=S_{\mathbf{n}}R_{K}(x)-R_{K}(x).

We estimate
S_{\mathbf{n}}R_{K}(x)
. The multiplier identity (
3.5
) shows that the Fourier coefficients of
g_{k}=P_{k}-P_{k-1}
vanish on the cube
[-N_{k-1},N_{k-1}]^{d}
. For fixed
\mathbf{n}
, this implies
S_{\mathbf{n}}g_{k}=0
for all sufficiently large
k
. Since the tail
\sum_{k>K}g_{k}
converges to
R_{K}
in
L^{p}
and the
operator
S_{\mathbf{n}}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for fixed
\mathbf{n}
, we get

S_{\mathbf{n}}R_{K}(x)=\sum_{k=K+1}^{\infty}S_{\mathbf{n}}g_{k}(x),

where only finitely many terms are nonzero. Hence

(5.14)

|S_{\mathbf{n}}R_{K}(x)|\leq\sum_{k=K+1}^{\infty}M_{k}(x).

Combining (
5.11
), (
5.13
), and (
5.14
), we obtain

|S_{\mathbf{n}}f(x)-f(x)|\leq 2\sum_{k=K+1}^{\infty}M_{k}(x)

whenever
n_{j}\geq 2^{r_{K}}
for every
j
. Taking the limit superior as
\min_{j}n_{j}\to\infty
gives

\limsup_{\min_{j}n_{j}\to\infty}|S_{\mathbf{n}}f(x)-f(x)|\leq 2\sum_{k=K+1}^{\infty}M_{k}(x).

Finally let
K\to\infty
. The right-hand side tends to zero by (
5.9
). Thus
S_{\mathbf{n}}f(x)\to f(x)
for every
x\in E
, and
E
has full measure.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Apply Theorem
1.2
with
\psi(t)=t^{-1-\eta}
. The series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-1-\eta}
converges, so the corollary follows.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Apply Theorem
1.2
with the given
p
and

\psi(t)=\frac{1}{t(\log(e+t))^{1+\eta}},\qquad t\geq 1.

This function is non-increasing and
\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}1/(k(\log k)^{1+\eta})<\infty
. Moreover,

\log(e+\Lambda_{2}(\delta))\asymp\Lambda_{3}(\delta),\qquad 0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Thus the hypothesis (
1.8
) implies (
1.6
) with this choice of
\psi
, after changing the
constant
A(f)
. The conclusion follows from Theorem
1.2
.
∎

6.
Open question

We close with the natural Oskolkov–Zhizhiashvili endpoint problem suggested
by Theorem
1.2
. The exponent
d/p
below is the same
dimensionally critical exponent as in (
1.6
).

Problem 6.1

(Oskolkov–Zhizhiashvili
L^{p}
endpoint problem)
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
1\leq p\leq 2
, and let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Does the
little-
o
condition

(6.1)

\omega(\delta,f)_{p}=o\left(\frac{1}{(\log(1/\delta))^{d/p}\log\log\log(e^{e^{e}}/\delta)}\right),\qquad\delta\to 0+,

imply almost everywhere Pringsheim convergence of the rectangular Fourier
sums of
f
?

Theorem
1.2
proves convergence under stronger summable secondary
assumptions. For instance, choosing

\psi(t)=1/(t(\log(e+t))^{1+\eta})
in (
1.6
) gives the
sufficient condition

\omega(\delta,f)_{p}=O\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda_{1}(\delta)^{d/p}\Lambda_{2}(\delta)\Lambda_{3}(\delta)^{1+\eta}}\right),\qquad\eta>0.

Problem
6.1
asks whether these summable secondary factors
can be replaced by the weaker Oskolkov-type triple logarithm

\Lambda_{3}(\delta)
, while preserving the dimensionally correct critical
power
\Lambda_{1}(\delta)^{-d/p}
. Thus it lies between the summable criteria
proved here and the bare critical endpoint question with no secondary
logarithmic factor. Such a result would be new in the endpoint cases
p=1

and
p=2
; for
d>2
it would give a higher-dimensional
L^{p}
analogue of
the two-dimensional Oskolkov phenomenon. The present
approximation–maximal method does not settle
(
6.1
): with the logarithmic blocks used in the
proof, (
6.1
) gives only block bounds of order

o(1/\log k)
, which need not be summable.
