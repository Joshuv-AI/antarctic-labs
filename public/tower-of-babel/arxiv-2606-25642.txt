A hybrid C 0 C^{0} -interior penalty method for the nematic Helmholtz–Korteweg equation
Tim van Beeck

Abstract
The nematic Helmholtz–Korteweg equation models the propagation of time-harmonic acoustic waves in nematic Korteweg fluids, such as nematic liquid crystals. The PDE augments the classical Helmholtz equation with two additional fourth-order terms, one of which is anisotropic in the direction of the nematic field. We refine the previous continuous analysis of Farrell et al. (2025) by using the Cordes condition and present a C 0 C^{0} -hybrid interior penalty discretization. The proposed discretization offers greater flexibility than C 1 C^{1} -conforming methods and is well-suited for applications in three dimensions and on curved domains. We prove stability of the method for any polynomial degree greater than or equal to two, independent of the spatial dimension, provided that the anisotropy is sufficiently small. Further, we show that the sequence of discrete solutions converges to the continuous solution under minimal regularity assumptions and derive convergence rates if the continuous solution has additional regularity. Finally, we illustrate the capabilities of the method through numerical examples.

is sufficiently small and discuss the injectivity of the problem.

3.1
Weak formulation

In preparation for the forthcoming analysis, we rewrite the highest-order term in (
1
) as

\displaystyle\Delta(\alpha\Delta u+\beta\mathbf{n}^{\top}\!(D^{2}u)\mathbf{n})=\Delta(\mathbb{A}\!:\!D^{2}u),\qquad\mathbb{A}\coloneqq\alpha\mathbb{I}+\beta\bm{n}\otimes\bm{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},

(9)

where
\mathbb{A}\!:\!\mathbb{B}=\operatorname{tr}(\mathbb{A}^{\top}\mathbb{B})
,
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, is the Frobenius inner product. Let
\gamma\coloneqq\operatorname{tr}\mathbb{A}/|\mathbb{A}|^{2}_{F}
, where
|\cdot|_{F}
is the Frobenius norm. Then, we define the Hilbert space and inner product

\displaystyle V\coloneqq\{v\in H^{2}(\Omega):v=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{\!\!D}\},\qquad(\cdot,\cdot)_{V}\coloneqq\gamma^{-1}(\Delta\cdot,\Delta\cdot)_{\Omega}+(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

(10)

and denote by
\|\cdot\|_{V}^{2}\coloneqq(\cdot,\cdot)_{V}
the induced norm. To obtain the weak formulation of (
1
), we multiply with a test function
v\in V
, integrate by parts, and use the boundary conditions (
2
). This gives

\displaystyle(\Delta(\mathbb{A}\!:\!D^{2}u),v)_{\Omega}-(\Delta u,v)_{\Omega}=(\mathbb{A}\!:\!D^{2}u,\Delta v)_{\Omega}+(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{\Omega}-i\theta(u,v)_{\Gamma_{\!\!R}}.

(11)

Altogether, we define the sesquilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot):V\times V\to\mathbb{C}
as

\displaystyle a(u,v)

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\mathbb{A}\!:\!D^{2}u,\Delta v)_{\Omega}+(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{\Omega}-k^{2}(u,v)_{\Omega}-i\theta(u,v)_{\Gamma_{\!\!R}}.

(12)

Then, the weak formulation of (
1
) is given by: find
u\in V
such that

a(u,v)=(f,v)_{\Omega}\quad\forall v\in V.

(WF)

3.2
Fredholmness of the continuous problem

The highest-order terms in (
12
) vanish when testing with a harmonic function, but the nematic term couples with the full Hessian. Thus, we need to ensure that all second order derivatives can be controlled by the Laplacian. In other words, we need the following elliptic regularity result as a critical ingredient of the forthcoming analysis. For this, we assume the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
to either have a smooth boundary or to be a convex polygon. For two-dimensional domains with sound-soft boundary conditions, it is sufficient if
\Omega
is polygonal.

Lemma 4

(Elliptic regularity)
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is sufficiently regular. Then, there exists a constant
C_{\!R}\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\|D^{2}u\|_{\Omega}\leq C_{\!R}\left(\|\Delta u\|_{\Omega}+\|u\|_{\Omega}\right),\qquad\forall u\in V.

(ER)

If
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{\!\!D}
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, is convex or
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is polygonal, the Miranda–Talenti inequality holds, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|D^{2}u\|_{\Omega}\leq\|\Delta u\|_{\Omega},\qquad\forall u\in V=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(MT)

Proof.

The first estimate follows from classical elliptic regularity results
(
Grisvard 2011
, Thm. 2.3.3.2)
. For the sufficiently regular domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have that
\|u\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\left(\|\Delta u\|_{\Omega}+\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right)
. We use the compactness of the embeddings
H^{2}\hookrightarrow H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{2}
and Ehrling’s lemma
(
Renardy and Rogers 2004
, Thm. 7.30)
to replace the
H^{1}
-norm with the
L^{2}
-norm. The special case (
MT
) is the
Miranda–Talenti
inequality, see, for instance,
Maugeri et al. 2000
or
(
Neilan et al. 2017
, Rem. 2.37)
for the proof that the convexity assumption is not necessary for two-dimensional polygonal domains.
∎

An immediate consequence of (
ER
) is that the norm
\|\cdot\|_{V}
is equivalent to the
H^{2}
-norm and that the sesquilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is bounded with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{V}
.

The higher-order term
(\mathbb{A}\!:\!D^{2}u,\Delta v)_{\Omega}
has the same structure that frequently appears in the analysis of second-order PDEs in non-divergence form, for instance, as in
Smears and Süli 2013
;
Neilan et al. 2017
;
Sprekeler 2026
.
A crucial component in the analysis of those problems is the
Cordes condition

Cordes 1956
, which limits the anisotropy induced by the matrix
\mathbb{A}
.
We work directly with its quantitative form: the Frobenius norm of
\gamma\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{I}
, with
\gamma\coloneqq\operatorname{tr}\mathbb{A}/|\mathbb{A}|^{2}_{F}>0
, is strictly smaller than one. This allows us to use the pointwise estimate

\displaystyle|\gamma\mathbb{A}\!:\!D^{2}u-\Delta u|\leq|\gamma\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{I}|_{F}|D^{2}u|_{F},\quad\forall u\in V,\text{ a.e. in }\Omega.

(13)

In the setting of the Miranda–Talenti inequality, as in
Smears and Süli 2013
;
Neilan et al. 2017
;
Sprekeler 2026
, we can estimate the right-hand side of (
13
) with (
MT
) and
|\gamma\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{I}|_{F}<1
is sufficient to obtain (
G
). In the general case, we have to take into account the elliptic regularity constant.

Lemma 5

(Gårding inequality)
.

Assume that
C_{\!R}|\gamma\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{I}|_{F}<1
. Then, there exist constants
C_{V},C_{L^{2}}>0
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)\geq C_{V}\|u\|^{2}_{V}-C_{L^{2}}\|u\|^{2}_{\Omega}\qquad\forall u\in V.

Proof.

Let
\delta_{C_{\!R}}\coloneqq C_{\!R}|\gamma\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{I}|_{F}
for which by assumption
\delta_{C_{\!R}}<1
. Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the pointwise estimate (
13
), and
Lemma

4
, we estimate

\displaystyle\left|(\gamma\mathbb{A}\!:\!D^{2}u-\Delta u,\Delta u)_{\Omega}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\gamma\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{I}|_{F}\|D^{2}u\|_{\Omega}\|\Delta u\|_{\Omega}

(14)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\!R}|\gamma\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{I}|_{F}\left(\|\Delta u\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\|u\|_{\Omega}\|\Delta u\|_{\Omega}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{C_{\!R}}(1+\epsilon)\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{\Omega}+\frac{\delta_{C_{\!R}}}{4\epsilon}\|u\|_{\Omega}^{2},

where we used a weighted Young’s inequality with
\epsilon>0
in the last step. Since
\delta_{C_{\!R}}<1
by assumption, we can choose
\epsilon>0
small enough such that
\delta_{C_{\!R}}(1+\epsilon)<1
and further estimate that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}(\mathbb{A}\!:\!D^{2}u,\Delta u)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma}\|\Delta u\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\operatorname{Re}(\gamma\mathbb{A}\!:\!D^{2}u-\Delta u,\Delta u)_{\Omega}

(15)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ContCoercivity:CordesConsequences}}}{{\geq}}\frac{1}{\gamma}\left(1-\delta_{C_{\!R}}(1+\epsilon)\right)\|\Delta u\|_{\Omega}^{2}-\frac{\delta_{C_{\!R}}}{4\gamma\epsilon}\|u\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

Thus, we obtain that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\{a(u,u)\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\delta_{C_{\!R}}(1+\epsilon)}{\gamma}\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{\Omega}+\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{\Omega}+\|u\|^{2}_{\Omega}-(1+k^{2}+\delta_{C_{\!R}}(4\gamma\epsilon)^{-1})\|u\|^{2}_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{1-\delta_{C_{\!R}}(1+\epsilon),1\}\|u\|^{2}_{V}-C_{L^{2}}\|u\|^{2}_{\Omega}.

∎

Let
V^{\prime}\coloneqq\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega):v=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{\!\!D}\}
and
W\coloneqq L^{2}(\Omega)
. Since
V\hookrightarrow V^{\prime}\hookrightarrow W
, where the embeddings are compact due to Rellich’s theorem,
Lemma

1
applies with
(V,V^{\prime},W)
. Consequently, the operator associated with (
12
) is Fredholm with index zero. Before discussing injectivity, we first investigate the smallness assumptions on
\mathbb{A}
.

For
\alpha>0
,
\beta>-\alpha
, the matrix
\mathbb{A}\coloneqq\alpha\mathbb{I}+\beta\bm{n}\otimes\bm{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is symmetric positive definite with

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d-1},\lambda_{d})=(\alpha,\dots,\alpha,\alpha+\beta),\qquad|\mathbb{A}|^{2}_{F}=d\alpha^{2}+2\alpha\beta+\beta^{2},\qquad\operatorname{tr}\mathbb{A}=d\alpha+\beta.

(16)

Therefore, we can obtain an explicit smallness assumption on
\beta
, depending on
\alpha
, the elliptic regularity constant
C_{\!R}
and the dimension
d
.

Lemma 6
.

With
\gamma\coloneqq\operatorname{tr}\mathbb{A}/|\mathbb{A}|^{2}_{F}>0
, we have that
|\gamma\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{I}|^{2}_{F}=(d-1)\beta^{2}|\mathbb{A}|_{F}^{-2}
.
Further,
C_{\!R}|\gamma\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{I}|_{F}<1
if and only if

\displaystyle\beta\in(\alpha\mu^{-1}(1-\sqrt{1+d\mu}),\alpha\mu^{-1}(1+\sqrt{1+d\mu})),\qquad\mu\coloneqq C_{\!R}^{2}(d-1)-1.

(17)

Proof.

We assume
\mu>0
and calculate with the definition of
\gamma
that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{A}|^{2}_{F}|\gamma\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{I}|_{F}^{2}=|\mathbb{A}|^{2}_{F}\left(\gamma^{2}|\mathbb{A}|^{2}_{F}-2\gamma\operatorname{tr}(\mathbb{A})+d\right)=d|\mathbb{A}|^{2}_{F}-(\operatorname{tr}(\mathbb{A}))^{2}.

After inserting (
16
), some algebraic manipulations yield the first claim. Using this expression, we have that
C_{\!R}^{2}|\gamma\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{I}|^{2}_{F}<1
if and only if

\displaystyle C_{\!R}^{2}(d-1)\beta^{2}<|\mathbb{A}|^{2}_{F}=d\alpha^{2}+2\alpha\beta+\beta^{2}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad q(\beta)\coloneqq\mu\beta^{2}-2\alpha\beta-d\alpha^{2}<0.

Since
\mu>0
for
d\geq 2
,
C_{\!R}\geq 1
, the inequality is satisfied for
\beta_{-}<\beta<\beta_{+}
, where
\beta_{-}
and
\beta_{+}
are the negative and positive roots of the quadratic polynomial
q(\cdot)
. Solving for the roots with the quadratic formula yields (
17
). The special case
\mu=0
only occurs when
d=2
and
C_{\!R}=1
, in which case we interpret
\mu=0
as a limit
\mu\to 0
.
∎

For large elliptic regularity constants
C_{\!R}
, (
17
) enforces that
|\beta|=\mathcal{O}(\alpha C_{\!R}^{-1})
. For convex domains with
\Gamma_{\!\!D}=\partial\Omega
, we may take
C_{\!R}=1
due to (
MT
). For
d=2
, we obtain
\mu=0
and consequently
\beta\in(-\alpha,\infty)
, where we interpret the division by zero as a limit from the right. In other words, when (
MT
) is applicable, the smallness requirement is always satisfied for
\beta>-\alpha
in the two-dimensional setting. In contrast, we obtain
\beta\in(-\alpha,3\alpha)
for
d=3
. In the general setting, we conclude from
Lemma

6
and
Lemma

5
that the operator associated with (
12
) is Fredholm with index zero assuming that
\beta
satisfies (
17
). To establish the well-posedness of the problem, we need to show that the operator is injective. We recall the well-posedness result from
Farrell et al. 2025
.

Lemma 7

(Injectivity)
.

The operator associated with (
12
) is injective for all non-resonant wave numbers if
|\beta|<C_{\beta=0}
, where
C_{\beta=0}
depends on the stability constant of the isotropic problem.

Proof.

The statement follows from
(
Farrell et al. 2025
, Thm. 3.2)
. Essentially, the argument is based on a classical perturbation argument
Kato 1966
, where we first show that the isotropic problem with
\beta=0
is well-posed and then infer well-posedness of (
WF
) for all
\beta
such that
|\beta|<C_{\beta=0}
.
∎

The constant
C_{\beta=0}
degenerates when
k^{2}
approaches an eigenvalue of the isotropic operator
\alpha\Delta^{2}-\Delta
, forcing
\beta\to 0
. However, we actually expect the anisotropic problem with
\beta>0
to have its own set of resonant frequencies
\mathcal{K}_{\text{res}}
related to the eigenvalues of the operator
\Delta(\mathbb{A}\!:\!D^{2})-\Delta
, cf. also
Remark

2
. We thus make the following assumption.

Assumption 8

(Injectivity under (
17
))
.

The operator associated with (
12
) is injective for all
\beta
satisfying (
17
) and non-resonant wave numbers
k^{2}\not\in\mathcal{K}_{\text{res}}
.

A proof of the injectivity assumed in
8
is beyond the scope of this article. The non-symmetric volume term introduces compounding difficulties: a spectral analysis of non-symmetric perturbations of self-adjoint operators requires additional technical machinery
Cuenin and Tretter 2016
, and classical uniqueness arguments for impedance problems, such as unique continuation
(
Ern and Guermond 2021
, Lem. 35.10)
or Morawetz–Rellich identities
Graham et al. 2019

\operatorname{Im}a(u,u)
.

4.2
Projection operators

To apply the theory from
Section

, we have to establish a suitable projection operator that satisfies (

Since
u\in V
implies that
\nabla u\in H(\operatorname{div},\Omega)
, the jump of the normal derivative across interfaces is well-defined and continuous. Formally, we identify
u\in V
with a tuple
\underline{u}\in V\times L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{h})
via the mapping

\displaystyle V\to V\times L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{h}),\quad u\mapsto\underline{u}\coloneqq(u,\operatorname{tr}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}(\partial_{\bm{\nu}}u)),

which implies that
[\![\underline{\partial_{\bm{\nu}}u}]\!]=0
. Setting
\Delta_{h}\underline{u}=\Delta u
, the discrete norm
\|\cdot\|_{V_{h}}
is well-defined for
\underline{u}\in V\times L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{h})
with
\|\underline{u}\|_{V_{h}}=\|u\|_{V}
, and in particular,
\mathcal{S}_{h}(\underline{u},\underline{v}_{h})=0
for all
\underline{v}_{h}\in V_{h}
.

In preparation, we discuss a suitable interpolation operator
\underline{\Pi}_{h}:V\cap H^{2+s}(\Omega)\to V_{h}
,
s\geq 0
. For each element
T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
, we define

\displaystyle(\underline{\Pi}_{h})|_{T}:V\cap H^{2+s}(T)\to\mathbb{P}^{p}(T)\times\mathbb{P}^{p-1}(\partial T),\quad u\mapsto(\Pi_{h}^{p}u,\Pi_{F}^{p-1}(\partial_{\bm{\nu}}u)),

(26)

where
\Pi_{h}^{p}
is the Lagrange interpolation operator and
\Pi_{F}^{p-1}
is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection on
\mathbb{P}^{p-1}(F)
,
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
. The Lagrange interpolation operator is well-defined on
V
, since
H^{2}\hookrightarrow C^{0}
for
d\in\{2,3\}
.

Lemma 9
.

For all
u\in V\cap H^{2+s}(\Omega)
,
s\geq 0
, we have that

\displaystyle\|\underline{u}-\underline{\Pi}_{h}u\|_{V_{h}}\leq Ch^{\min\{p-1,s\}}\|u\|_{H^{2+s}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Standard interpolation results
(
Ern and Guermond 2021
, Thm. 11.13)
for the Lagrange interpolation operator yield that

\displaystyle|u-\Pi_{h}^{p}u|_{H^{m}(T)}\leq Ch^{r-m}|u|_{H^{r}(T)},\qquad 2\leq r\leq p+1,\ m\leq r.

\displaystyle|\underline{\Pi}_{h}u|_{\mathcal{S}_{h}}=\|h^{-1/2}\Pi_{F}^{p-1}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}(u-\Pi_{h}^{p}u)\|_{\partial{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\leq C\left(h^{-1}|u-\Pi_{h}^{p}u|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}+|u-\Pi_{h}^{p}u|_{H^{2}({\mathcal{T}_{h}})}\right),

in the last step.
Applying (

As a candidate for the projection operator, we define

\displaystyle(\underline{\pi}_{h}u,\underline{v}_{h})_{V_{h}}=\gamma^{-1}(\Delta u,\Delta_{h}\underline{v}_{h})_{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}+(u,v_{h})_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\quad\forall\underline{v}_{h}\in V_{h}

Lemma 10
.

For all

Proof.

By definition of

\displaystyle\|\underline{u}-\underline{\pi}_{h}u\|_{V_{h}}^{2}=(\underline{u}-\underline{\pi}_{h}u,\underline{u}-\underline{\Pi}_{h}u)_{V_{h}}\leq\|\underline{u}-\underline{\pi}_{h}u\|_{V_{h}}\|\underline{u}-\underline{\Pi}_{h}u\|_{V_{h}}.

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\|\underline{u}-\underline{\pi}_{h}u\|_{V_{h}}=0\quad\forall u\in V\cap H^{2+s}(\Omega),s>0.

Theorem 14

We close the analysis of the discrete problem with an estimate on the rate of convergence in

(Convergence estimate)
.

Suppose that
\beta

Let

Appendix A
Details on

For completeness, we state the result from
(

The following theorem shows the stability of the discrete problem if the weakly T-coercive structure can be transferred to the discrete level in a suitable manner. We state the result for the associated (sequences of) bounded linear operators and note that the notion of asymptotic consistency from

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\|A_{h}p_{h}u-p_{h}Au\|_{V_{h}}=0\qquad\forall u\in V.

Definition 2

We call a sequence of operators
