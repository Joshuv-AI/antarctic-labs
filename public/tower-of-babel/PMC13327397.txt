A bibliometric assessment of the Indian Journal of Psychiatry: Trends, impact, and collaboration (2009–2024)
Authors: Sandeep Grover, Raju Vaishya, Brij M. Gupta, Namita Mahapatra, S. M Chaman, S Madhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:The Indian Journal of Psychiatry (IJP) serves as a premier repository for psychiatric research in India.Aim:This study conducts a comprehensive bibliometric analysis to evaluate the journal’s publication trends and citation impact from 2009 to 2024.Methods:Bibliographic data were retrieved from the Scopus database, covering the period from January 2009 to December 2024. A final dataset of 2,357 publications was analyzed for this paper.Results:The journal experienced robust growth, registering an average annual publication growth rate of 9.16% over the 16 years. The journal’s Impact Factor rose significantly from 0.384 in 2010 to a peak of 3.1 in 2022. Authors from India contributed 88.63% of the total output, anchored by institutions such as the National Institute of Mental Health and Neuro Sciences, Bangalore, and the Post Graduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, although international contributions from countries like Switzerland (Citation per paper [CPP]: 22.36), Germany (CPP: 17.68), Canada (CPP: 17.68), and Italy (CPP: 17.63) demonstrated a higher citation efficiency compared to those from India (11.6). The authorship pattern revealed a definitive shift from single-author (reduced from 28.65% to 9.9%) to collaborative, multiauthor research. Thematically, research concentrated on core areas such as depression and schizophrenia, while displaying responsiveness to emerging crises like the COVID-19 pandemic.Conclusion:The IJP has consistently demonstrated a trajectory of upward influence and academic reputation over the past 16 years. While the journal serves as a vibrant ecosystem for domestic research, future strategies should focus on leveraging international collaborations to enhance global visibility and citation impact further.

INTRODUCTION

Scientific publications serve as repositories of professional knowledge, stimulate research, and act as a permanent record of the expanding horizons of knowledge in any field.[1] The Indian Journal of Psychiatry (IJP), the official publication of the Indian Psychiatric Society, which was established in 1949 as the Indian Journal of Neurology and Psychiatry, has been central to the mission of expanding psychiatric knowledge. The journal was renamed as the Indian Journal of Psychiatry in 1958. Over the last 76 years, the journal has undergone significant change,[2] and among the few journals published in India, that publishes 12 issues annually. Currently, the IJP is an open-access, peer-reviewed journal indexed in major international databases, including PubMed Central, Scopus, and Web of Science (https://journals.lww.com/indianjpsychiatry/pages/default.aspx), and has experienced a general upward trajectory in its impact factor and global reach.

Over time, the journal’s focus has been guided by contemporary issues, with early issues featuring articles on psychoanalysis. The current focus of the journal is on publishing more methodologically sound and rigorous research.[3]

In the current publishing industry, evaluating the quality of journals is a challenging task. Several methods have been proposed to evaluate the quality of a journal.[45] Among these, one method is bibliometric analysis. Consequently, bibliometric analysis has become an indispensable tool for systematic evaluation. By quantifying publication patterns, citation dynamics, and authorship networks, bibliometrics provide deep insights into the dissemination of knowledge and the thematic evolution of a domain.[6] The existing literature demonstrates the diverse applications of these parameters for assessing different journals.[7] Although there are bibliometric evaluations for some journals published in India, there is no such analysis for the IJP. Accordingly, utilizing Scopus data and visualization techniques, this paper aims to analyze the IJP’s publication structure (i.e., number of publications, impact factor, keywords, etc.), citation dynamics, and prominent contributors to the journal over the last 16 years.

METHODOLOGY

The bibliographic data for this study were retrieved from the Scopus database, which was chosen for its comprehensive and broader coverage of peer-reviewed medical literature. The search strategy focused on the source title to isolate publications specifically published in IJP. The study period was restricted to 16 years, covering the period from January 1, 2009 (i.e. the time frame from which the IJP got listed in PubMed) to December 31, 2024. The specific search query employed was: SRCTITLE (“Indian Journal of Psychiatry”) AND PUBYEAR >2008 AND PUBYEAR < 2025 AND (LIMIT-TO (EXACTSRCTITLE, “Indian Journal of Psychiatry”)).

The initial search yielded a total of 3,131 documents. After applying the requisite filters and date limitations, a final dataset of 2,357 publications was retained for analysis. The bibliographic records were exported in Comma-Separated Values (CSV) format for further processing. The data analysis involved quantitative and visual methodologies using the following software:

The different parameters that were assessed in the study included Citations per paper (CPP), Relative Citation Index (RCI), and Total Link Strength (TLS). CPP reflects the number of times a paper in the database has been cited. The RCI is computed as the actual number of citations received by the paper divided by the expected citation rate of its peer field. TLC is a standardized quantitative metric that evaluates the overall weight and intensity of the connection between a specific item (such as an author, country, or keyword) and all other items in the dataset.

RESULTS

Journal metrics: The bibliometric indicators for the IJP between 2009 and 2024 show a robust upward trajectory in scholarly influence, despite minor fluctuations in recent years. The journal’s Impact Factor (IF) experienced an eightfold increase, rising from 0.384 in 2010 to a peak of 3.1 in 2022, before settling at 1.7 in 2023. Similarly, the CiteScore grew steadily from 0.7 in 2011 to a high of 4.4 in 2022. The SCImago Journal Rank (SJR) mirrored this pattern, climbing from 0.125 in 2010 to a maximum of 0.771 in 2022 [Supplementary Table 1].

Annual growth and citation trend: Between 2009 and 2024, the IJP published 2,357 papers, exhibiting a robust average annual growth rate of 9.16%, with output rising from 86 papers in 2009 to a peak of 228 in 2020 and 222 in 2024. While papers with Indian authors (i.e., papers with at least one Indian author) dominated the landscape, accounting for 88.63% of the total output (2,089 papers) and achieving a higher citation impact (11.60 CPP) compared to foreign contributions (11.49 CPP), the foreign papers (i.e. papers with authors from countries other than India) demonstrated a significantly higher annual growth rate of 29.25%. The articles published in the journal accumulated 26,726 total citations with an overall CPP of 11.33. Notably, the papers published in the earlier period (2009–2016) achieved a higher CPP of 17.22 compared to 7.93 for papers published in the later period (2017–2024) [Table 1].

Document types: An analysis of document types among the 2,357 publications (2009–2024) indicates that the IJP is focussed on primary research, with original articles constituting 50.8% (1,197 papers) of the total output. Letter to the Editor (20.7%) and review articles (17.35%) followed as the next most common formats. In terms of citation impact, conference papers (which typically indicate award/oration papers published in the journal) registered the highest CPP at 36.86, surpassing reviews (21.06 CPP) and original articles (12.10 CPP) [Supplementary Table 2].

Citation Distribution Pattern: The citation analysis [Supplementary Table 3] reveals a highly skewed, long-tail distribution, where a select core of elite papers drives the journal’s impact factor, while the majority of research receives modest attention. Out of 2,357 publications, 81.3% were cited at least once; however, the bulk of these (54.1%) clustered within the 1–10 citation range. As citation frequency increased, paper volume tapered sharply: Only 2.8% of publications were cited 51–100 times, and a mere 1.25% papers were cited more than 100 times. At the apex, only six “landmark” papers (0.25%) surpassed 240 citations.

Authorship pattern: The authorship pattern of the IJP demonstrates a clear and consistent shift from single-author to multiauthor collaboration papers [Supplementary Table 4]. During the years 2009–2012, single-author papers formed 28.65% of all the papers, and this reduced to 19.20% during the period of 2013–2016, 16.6% during the period of 2017–2020, and 9.9% for the period of 2021–2024.

Most Productive Countries: Notably, the majority of papers (2,089, 88.6% of the total) originated from India. However, in addition to India, the journal published papers with authors from 74 more countries. While papers from India surpassed the average productivity threshold (i.e., the papers by authors from India had a CPP higher than the mean for all the papers, irrespective of country of the author), papers from several other countries demonstrated superior citation efficiency. Publications from authors from seven nations, i.e., Switzerland (CPP 22.36), Canada, Germany, Italy, the United States of America (USA), and the United Kingdom (UK), registered citation impact and Relative Citation Indices (RCIs) significantly above the group average [Table 2].

NETWORK COLLABORATION MAP BETWEEN AUTHORS FROM DIFFERENT COUNTRIES

While the collaboration network among authors from top countries was evaluated, a network of five distinct clusters with a Total Link Strength (TLS) (which is a measure of overall intensity of the connection between the authors) of 290 emerged, involving authors from the top 13 countries. Authors from India occupied the central node, anchoring the network through strong ties with the authors from the USA and the UK Cluster. Other significant groupings included a diverse interregional block comprising Australia, China, and Germany (Red Cluster), as well as a North American-Asian axis connecting Canada, Iran, and the USA (Green Cluster). Smaller clusters involving authors from Nepal, Turkey, and Pakistan also highlight regional connections [Supplementary Figure 1].

Cluster 2 (Green) represents the clinical and pharmacological research front, focusing on severe psychiatric illnesses and interventions. Key terms include “Psychosis” and “Bipolar Disorder,” alongside frequent references to pharmacotherapies like “Olanzapine,” “Risperidone,” and “Electroconvulsive therapy,” highlighting a deep integration between psychopathology and medical treatment.

DISCUSSION

The bibliometric assessment of the IJP from 2009 to 2024 reveals that the journal is in a phase of significant robust growth, mirroring the broader positive trends observed in Indian medical publishing. Between 2009 and 2024, the journal’s Impact Factor increased eightfold, rising from 0.384 in 2010 to a peak of 3.1 in 2022, while the total publication output grew at an annual average rate of 9.16%. The rise in these metrics underscores a notable improvement in the journal’s academic reputation, particularly since 2020, thereby solidifying its standing in psychiatric research. This upward trajectory aligns with similar trends noted for other Indian journals.[8910]

Over the years, the journal has predominantly published papers from Indian authors. However, the number of papers from authors from other countries has almost doubled in recent years (2017–2024) compared to earlier years (2009–2016). A lower number of publications in the earlier years could be attributed to the lower IF of the journal, compared to the impact factor in the recent years, especially when the IF peaked in the year 2022. Additionally, the increase in papers from authors from other countries may also be related to improvements in CiteScore and SJR, as well as the expansion of the Indian diaspora in various countries.

However, the publication of papers with authors from 74 more countries reflects that the journal is more widely accepted internationally and has an international reputation.

Over the years, the articles published in IJP have been cited widely with an average CPP of 11.33, suggesting that every paper published in IJP is cited at least 11 times. Another important finding is that 81.3% of the papers published in the journal are cited at least once. These findings again reflect the quality of publications in the journal. Although the papers published in the earlier period (2009–2016) achieved a higher CPP of 17.22 compared to 7.93 for papers published in the later period (2017–2024), this decline could be attributed to the lag time required for recent publications to accrue citations despite increased productivity.

However, an important thing to note is that the journal’s IF, Cite Score, and SJR have been driven by a small number of publications (about 4.3%) that have been cited more than 50 times. This suggests that there is still scope for improvement in the selection of articles for publication. When one examines the HCPs, it is apparent that articles focusing on COVID-related research were more often cited. This pattern may reflect the general trend seen for higher citations of papers related to COVID-19 published during the initial part of the pandemic. Overall, the IJP relies on a small fraction of high-quality contributions to significantly elevate its overall standing in the global scholarly community.

In terms of publication types, IJP is a journal with a primary focus on publishing original research articles, followed by Letters to the Editor and review articles. In terms of citations, the original research and reviews remain the core drivers of the journal’s influence, collectively accounting for nearly 88% of all citations. These findings suggest that the original research published in the journal is widely read and cited. Another important finding that is evident is that conference papers, that is, award papers, are more often cited than other types of papers. This reflects the quality of the data-based award papers and orations, which are of a higher quality compared to other kinds of publications.

A core finding of this study is the definitive shift from single-author contributions to multiauthor, collaborative research. This also reflects the evolution of psychiatric research toward greater teamwork, institutional cooperation, and interdisciplinary engagement. Across the 16 years, the authorship pattern reveals a progressive transformation in the research culture of the IJP from individual-driven studies to collaborative, team-oriented research endeavours. This evolution reflects the broader global trend in scientific publishing, where interdisciplinary research teams and networked collaborations contribute to higher productivity, diversified expertise, and greater research impact.

In terms of field of research, the higher frequency of keywords like depression, anxiety and anxiety disorder, schizophrenia, psychosis, bipolar disorder, and alcoholism and alcohol consumption reflect the commonly researched areas in the country.

In terms of institutional distribution of publications, the current bibliometric analysis reveals that the premier institutes in the country contribute disproportionately to the journal’s publications, which are cited more frequently. These findings can be interpreted in different ways. First, this may be a reflection of the research culture in the premier institutes. Additionally, this finding reflects higher access to funding and the ability to carry out better-quality research. However, this finding also reflects a differential research culture and infrastructure in the majority of institutes in the country, suggesting a need to improve these aspects.

The findings of the present analysis must be interpreted in the light of its limitations. First, this analysis was based on the SCOPUS database, which provides a more conservative estimate of the citations compared to the Google Scholar database. The findings of disproportionate representation of research from certain institutes and researchers do not necessarily reflect the overall research output from the country. Many authors with similar or better-quality research publish their papers in various international journals with more favorable indexing parameters. The analysis of the data was limited to 16 years only. Accordingly, the findings do not accurately reflect the journal’s overall publication pattern since its inception. The reduction in the CPP for recent years reflects a shorter time frame for these citations to accrue, rather than the quality of the research. We could not find bibliometric analysis for a psychiatric journal. Hence, we could not compare the findings of the present study with the existing data for a psychiatric journal.

CONCLUSION

The bibliometric analysis of the IJP (2009–2024) highlights a transformative era characterized by an eightfold increase in Impact Factor and a consistent rise in scholarly output. The journal has evolved into a hub for collaborative research, where domestic apex institutions, such as NIMHANS and PGIMER, drive the publication volume, while international partnerships significantly enhance the citation impact. Thematically, the publication landscape remains anchored in core clinical areas like depression and schizophrenia yet displays remarkable agility in addressing emerging challenges such as the COVID-19 pandemic. Despite a skewed citation distribution where a small elite cohort of papers garners the majority of attention, the journal’s overall metrics reflect a strengthening academic reputation and global reach. Future growth will likely depend on leveraging these strengths to internationalize authorship further and broaden the global dissemination of Indian psychiatric research.
