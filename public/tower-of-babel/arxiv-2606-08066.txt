Stable Initial-State Recovery from Dynamical Samples using Nagy-Type and Pollard-Hilding-Type Frame Perturbations
Ruchi, Lalit Kumar Vashisht Department of Mathematics, University of DelhiEmails: rgarg@maths.du.ac.in & \& lalitkvashisht@gmail.com

Abstract
Stimulated by Aldroubi and his collaborator’s recent work on dynamical sampling, we consider a homogenous discrete dynamical system of the form f n = A ​ f n − 1 = A n ​ f , f 0 = f , \displaystyle f_{n}=Af_{n-1}=A^{n}f,\quad f_{0}=f, where A A is a bounded linear operator on a separable Hilbert space ℋ \mathcal{H} , which is known as the evolution operator, and f 0 ∈ ℋ f_{0}\in\mathcal{H} is the unknown initial state. The associated dynamical samples are given by the collection { ⟨ A n f , g ⟩ : g ∈ 𝒢 , 0 ≤ n < L ( g ) } \{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle:g\in\mathcal{G},0\leq n<L(g)\} , where 𝒢 ⊂ ℋ \mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{H} , is a finite or countable sampling set, and L : 𝒢 → ℕ ∪ { ∞ } L:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\} is a function. We analyze the stability of perturbed dynamical sampling systems in the sense of Nagy and Pollard–Hilding. More precisely, we establish sufficient conditions for the stable recovery of an initial state from perturbed dynamical samples obtained by changing the sampling vector, the evolution operator, or simultaneously both, within the framework of Nagy-type and Pollard–Hilding-type perturbation of frames. In our work, the sampling set 𝒢 \mathcal{G} consists of a single vector g g , that is, | 𝒢 | = 1 |\mathcal{G}|=1 , and L ⁡ ( g ) = ∞ L(g)=\infty . Introduction

Dynamical sampling is a growing area of research that is centered at the confluence of sampling theory, frame theory, operator theory, and signal processing.. The field of dynamical sampling has developed rapidly since the pioneering work of Lu et al.
[
38
]
and Aldroubi et al.
[
1
,
2
]
, and now includes several active directions such as initial-state recovery, system identification, and source recovery
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
40
,
41
]
. The central objective of dynamical sampling is to recover a function or signal from samples generated through the action of an evolution operator on a collection of sampling vectors. More precisely, let
A
be a bounded linear operator on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{W}
be a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
. A general discrete dynamical sampling system is of the form

\displaystyle f_{n}=Af_{n-1}+w,\,\,f_{0}=f,\,w\in\mathcal{W},

where
A
is called the evolution operator,
f_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
is the initial state, and
w\in\mathcal{W}
is the source term or forcing term. Given a finite or countable subset
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{H}
, known as the sampling set, and a function
L:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, the associated measurements are
\{\langle f_{n},g\rangle:g\in\mathcal{G},0\leq n<L(g)\}
.
These measurements are referred to as space-time samples or dynamical samples. The general dynamical sampling problem is to determine conditions on
A
,
\mathcal{G}
and
L:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, that allow the recovery of the unknown components of the system from the given measurements. Depending on the setting, the unknown quantity may be the initial state
f
, the evolution operator
A
, or the source term
w
. In applications, it is important that the sampling data enable stable recovery that remains robust in the presence of perturbations and noise. By stable recovery, we mean that there exists a well-defined bounded linear reconstruction operator
\mathscr{R}:\ell^{2}(S)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
satisfying
\mathscr{R}(\{\langle f_{n},g\rangle\}_{(g,n)\in S})=f,\,\text{for all}\,f\in\mathcal{H}
, where
S=\{(g,k):g\in\mathcal{G},0\leq k<L(g)\}
and under suitable conditions on
A
,
L
, and
\mathcal{G}
, the collection of measurements
\{\langle f_{n},g\rangle:(g,n)\in S\}
forms a square-summable sequence in
\ell^{2}(S)
. An important special case arises when the source term vanishes, i.e.,
w=0
. In this homogeneous setting, the system reduces to
f_{n}=Af_{n-1}=A^{n}f
, and the associated dynamical samples become
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle:g\in\mathcal{G},0\leq n<L(g)\}
.
The corresponding recovery problem is to determine conditions on
A
,
\mathcal{G}
and
L
that allow the reconstruction of the initial state
f\in\mathcal{H}
from these measurements. In this case, the problem is closely connected with frames (see below), since
\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle=\langle f,(A^{*})^{n}g\rangle
, where
A^{*}
denotes the Hilbert-adjoint of
A
. Consequently, recovery of the initial state is equivalent to determining whether the collection
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{g\in\mathcal{G},\;0\leq n<L(g)}
, is complete or forms a frame for
\mathcal{H}
. Such systems arise naturally in applications related to signal processing, wireless sensor networks, and time-varying signals
[
38
]
. Completeness guarantees uniqueness of recovery, while the frame property ensures stable reconstruction. This connection was established due to Aldroubi and Petrosyan in the following proposition:

Proposition 1.1
.

[
4
]

In a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the following statements hold:

1.

Any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can be recovered from
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{g\in\mathscr{G},\;0\leq n<L(g)}
if and
only if the system
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{g\in\mathscr{G},\;0\leq n<L(g)}
is complete in
\mathcal{H}
.

2.

Any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can be recovered from
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{g\in\mathscr{G},\;0\leq n<L(g)}
in a stable way if and
only if the system
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{g\in\mathscr{G},\;0\leq n<L(g)}
is a frame in
\mathcal{H}
.

Recall that a discrete
frame
for separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a countable collection
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
of members of
\mathcal{H}
such that the following inequality
(called the
frame inequality
) holds for some positive real numbers
A_{o}
and
B_{o}
:

\displaystyle A_{o}\|\varphi\|^{2}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{I}}|\langle\varphi,\varphi_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq B_{o}\|\varphi\|^{2}\ \text{for all}\ \varphi\in\mathcal{H}.

(1.1)

A_{o}
and
B_{o}
are called the
lower frame bound
and
upper frame bound
of the frame
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
, respectively. We say that
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
satisfies the

lower frame condition
if the left inequality in (
1.1
) holds. If
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
satisfies only upper inequality in (
1.1
), then we say that
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
is a
Bessel sequence
with the
Bessel bound

B_{o}
, or it satisfies the
upper frame condition
.
If
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
is a Bessel sequence, then the map
T:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{I})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
, given by

\displaystyle T\{c_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{I}}c_{k}\varphi_{k}

is called the
synthesis operator
or
pre-frame operator
of
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
. Its Hilbert-adjoint operator
T^{*}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{I})
is called the
analysis operator
of
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
that is given by
T^{*}\varphi=\{\langle\varphi,\varphi_{k}\rangle\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
. The
frame operator
of the Bessel sequence
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
is the composition:

\displaystyle S=TT^{*}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H},\quad S\varphi=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{I}}\langle\varphi,\varphi_{k}\rangle\varphi_{k}.

The frame operator of a Bessel sequence is bounded, linear, and self-adjoint; but may not be invertible on
\mathcal{H}
. If
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
, then the frame operator is invertible on
\mathcal{H}
. This gives the stable reconstruction formula as a series, not necessarily unique, for each element of the space
\mathcal{H}
:

\displaystyle\varphi=SS^{-1}\varphi=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{I}}\langle\varphi,S^{-1}\varphi_{k}\rangle\varphi_{k}\ \text{for every}\ \varphi\in\mathcal{H}.

Scalars
\langle\varphi,S^{-1}\varphi_{k}\rangle
,
k\in\mathbb{I}
, are called the
frame coefficients
. Frame coefficients associated with a frame contain all information of signals in the underlying signal space. For basic theory of frames and its applications, we refer to texts by Christensen
[
20
]
, Han
[
27
]
, Heil
[
28
]
, Krivoshein, Protasov Skopina
[
36
]
, Young
[
47
]
, Zhang and Jorgensen
[
48
]
.

One of the natural questions in dynamical sampling is the stability of reconstruction procedures under perturbations. In practical situations, the underlying evolution operator governing the dynamics, the sampling vector, or both simultaneously may be affected by modeling errors, noise, or numerical inaccuracies. Consequently, it becomes important to determine whether the stable recovery properties persist when the underlying dynamical sampling system is perturbed. This motivates the study of perturbation theory in the context of dynamical sampling systems in Hilbert spaces. In particular, we analyze the stable reconstruction of the initial state of a homogeneous discrete dynamical system from perturbed dynamical samples obtained through perturbations of the sampling vector, the evolution operator, or both simultaneously. The analysis is carried out using Nagy-type and Pollard–Hilding-type perturbation results for frames. Throughout this work, we restrict ourselves to the case where
\mathcal{G}=\{g\}
and
L(g)=\infty
.

1.1
Related work

The field of dynamical sampling is structured around three core pillars: initial state recovery or space-time trade-off
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, system identification
[
5
,
41
]
, and source recovery
[
10
,
13
,
14
,
40
]
. The conceptual roots of dynamical sampling can be traced back to the work of Lu et al. on spatiotemporal sampling of diffusion fields, where the trade-off between spatial and temporal measurements was investigated in the context of signal processing and sensor networks
[
38
]
. A systematic mathematical formulation of dynamical sampling was introduced by Aldroubi et al. in 2013, where they studied the recovery of signals evolving under an operator from coarse spatial samples taken over time
[
1
,
2
]
.

The fundamental problem of initial-state recovery was studied extensively in
[
2
]
, where the homogeneous setting
w=0
was considered. In this work, the sampling vectors were chosen from finite or countable orthonormal bases of the underlying Hilbert space, and the evolution operator was taken to be a discrete convolution operator. The authors established conditions under which a signal can be recovered from its space-time samples and highlighted the role of the trade-off between spatial and temporal sampling densities. In
[
6
]
, the authors studied the problem in both finite and infinite-dimensional settings. For the finite-dimensional case, they obtained explicit reconstruction conditions for diagonalizable as well as general matrices acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, and showed that vectors can be recovered from suitable space-time samples by appropriately choosing spatial sampling locations and temporal measurements. In the infinite-dimensional setting, the analysis was carried out for certain classes of self-adjoint operators on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
. Another important development in initial-state recovery involved operator orbits and iterative systems of the form
\{A^{n}g\}_{g\in\mathcal{G},\;n\geq 0}
, where researchers investigated and analyzed their frame and Bessel properties, thereby linking dynamical sampling with frame theory and operator orbits
[
4
,
18
]
. Since then, the connection between dynamical sampling and frames generated by iterative actions of operators has become an active area of research. The field subsequently expanded to system identification, where the evolution operator itself is unknown. Researchers also used generalized Krylov subspace methods to recover the unknown evolution operator and its spectrum from partial space-time observations
[
5
]
, and studied dynamical sampling systems generated by iterative actions of normal operators
[
7
]
. In 2017, Sui Tang studied system identification in dynamical sampling and introduced reconstruction methods connected with convolution operators and Prony-type techniques
[
41
]
. Applications of dynamical sampling to bandlimited functions can be found in
[
9
,
42
]
.

Another significant direction in the field is source recovery, where the objective is to identify unknown forcing terms driving the dynamics. More recent works have studied dynamical sampling in the presence of additive noise
[
8
]
, established recovery conditions for periodic and spatially constant sources in the discrete setting
[
12
]
, and developed predictive algorithms in the continuous setting for detecting burst-like forcing terms even in the presence of noise and measurement errors
[
11
]
. These approaches are robust under Lipschitz-continuous background noise and measurement perturbations. In
[
30
]
, the authors studied dynamical sampling on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}})
, where the sampling vectors are chosen from the standard basis and the evolution operator is a convolution operator generated by a kernel in
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}})
.
By 2025, the field reached another milestone with the appearance of a comprehensive survey on dynamical sampling
[
15
]
. Beyond these three principal directions, dynamical sampling is increasingly finding applications in graph signal processing and mobile sampling, where sensors move along prescribed trajectories
[
24
,
31
,
46
]
. For applications of dynamical sampling in tensor products, we refer to Zhang and Jorgensen
[

In some situations, such as when there is a loss of frame coefficients during signal processing, we need to process the signal using a different family of vectors that can preserve both frame conditions. Perturbation theory, which is concerned with a family of vectors in Banach spaces that are close to a given family of vectors with some fundamental properties, leaving the family with the same property, has been one of the most active and important topics in pure mathematics over the past seven decades. The earliest research work in papers
[
29
,
37
,
39
]
on Paley-Wiener-type, Nagy-type and Pollard–Hilding-type showed that completeness and basic sequences are preserved under these variants of perturbation. Invariance of fundamental properties under different types of perturbation is highly applicable in differential equations and operator theory
[
35
]
. In a normed space
\mathcal{X}
, the stability of completeness of sequences in the sense of Paley and Wiener says that “if a sequence
\{y_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{X}
is “near” a complete sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{X}
in some suitable sense, then
\{y_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is also complete in
\mathcal{X}
”. More precisely, we say that two sequences
\{x_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
\{y_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in a normed space
\mathcal{X}
satisfies the
Paley-Wiener property
, or closed in the sense of Paley-Wiener, if there exists a real number
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that
\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}(x_{j}-y_{j})\|\leq\lambda\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}x_{j}\|
holds for any scalars
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots a_{n}
.
For Paley-Wiener-type stability for completeness of sequences, we refer to
[
29
,
37
]
.
In
[
39
]
, Retherford generalized stability results in the sense of Paley-Wiener for basic sequences and completeness of sequences in complete linear metric spaces and normed spaces that are defined as follows:

Definition 1.2
.

[
39
]
Two sequences
\{x_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
\{y_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in a normed space
\mathcal{X}
are said to have the property

1.

for
Pollard-Hilding
if for each positive real number
k
, there exist real numbers
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
with
0\leq\lambda_{i}<\text{min}\{1,2^{1-\frac{1}{k}}\},i=1,2
, such that

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}(x_{j}-y_{j})\Big\|\leq\left(\lambda_{1}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}x_{j}\Big\|^{k}+\lambda_{2}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}y_{j}\Big\|^{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}

holds for any scalars
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots a_{n}
.

2.

for
Nagy
if there exist real numbers
\lambda\in[0,1)
,
\nu\in[0,1)
and
\mu
with
0\leq\mu,\,\mu^{2}\leq(1-\lambda)(1-\nu)
such that

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}(x_{j}-y_{j})\Big\|^{2}\leq\lambda\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}x_{j}\Big\|^{2}+\mu\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}x_{j}\Big\|\cdot\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}y_{j}\Big\|+\nu\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}y_{j}\Big\|^{2}

holds for any scalars
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots a_{n}
.

Stability in the sense of Nagy, with
\mu=0
, for completeness of sequences in separable Hilbert spaces was proved by Pollard in
[
37

. This is generalized Paley-Wiener-type stability for complete sequences in separable Hilbert spaces. Also see a published paper by Hilding

Recall that frames for a signal space enable the stable reconstruction of each signal within that space. Therefore, its analysis is lacking if the perturbation of frames is not studied. Further, in the perturbation of frames, it is important that their fundamental properties must be invariant. In

Our paper contributes perturbation problems for dynamical sampling systems in separable Hilbert spaces for the stable reconstruction of signals. More precisely, we study the stable recovery of an initial state of a homogenous discrete dynamical system from perturbed dynamical samples obtained by changing the sampling vector, the evolution operator, or simultaneously both using Nagy-type and Pollard–Hilding-type frame perturbations. Outline of the work

The paper is outlined as follows: In Section
2
, we recall some basic results related to frames and inequalities that will be used in the sequel. Section
3
is devoted to the stable recovery of the initial state of homogenous dynamical sampling system discussed above under Nagy-type frame perturbation involving perturbations of the sampling vector, evolution operator, and simultaneous perturbations of both. In particular, Theorem

gives the stable recovery under simultaneous perturbation of the sampling vector and the evolution operator. In Section

investigate perturbation of the dynamical samples induced by changes in the evolution operator within the framework of Pollard–Hilding-type frame perturbation, while Theorem

3
Dynamical Samples using Nagy-Type Perturbation of Frames

We begin the section with the stability of dynamical sampling systems under perturbation of the sampling vector. More precisely, in the following result we show that stable recovery of the initial state from the samples
{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle}_{n\geq 0}
is preserved when the sampling vector
g
is replaced by another vector
h
satisfying a Nagy-type frame perturbation condition.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
g,h\in\mathcal{H}
and let
A
be a bounded linear operator acting on
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that

(i)

any
f\in\mathcal{H}
is recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way and there exist real numbers
\lambda
,
\mu
,
\nu\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}

\displaystyle\quad+\nu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2},\;\;l\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},

(3.1)

for all finite scalar sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

(ii)

2(1-\nu)\epsilon^{2}-(\mu+2\nu)\epsilon-\mu>0
and
\max{\{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2},\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu\}}<1
, where
\epsilon>0
.

Then, any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can also be recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,h\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.

Proof.

In view of Proposition
1.1
, it is sufficient to prove that
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
is a frame of the space
\mathcal{H}
.
We prove this in the following two steps:

Step (I):
In this step, we show that
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
is a Bessel sequence.

By hypothesis
(i)
, every
f\in\mathcal{H}
is recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way. Therefore, by Proposition
1.1
,
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
. Let
T_{g}
be the pre-frame operator of
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
.
For
h\in\mathcal{H}
, consider the following:

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}.

Squaring on both side and using inequality (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+2\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}

\displaystyle\quad+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+2\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}

\displaystyle\quad+\nu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\lambda)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+2\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}+\nu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}.

(3.2)

By using Young’s Inequality given in Theorem
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2},

and

\displaystyle 2\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}^{2}.

Using above two inequalities in (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1-\nu)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\lambda)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\epsilon\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}^{2}+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}.

Again using inequality (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\left(1-\nu-\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\lambda+\epsilon+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}+\frac{\nu}{\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}.

This gives

\displaystyle\left(1-\nu-\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}-\frac{\nu}{\epsilon}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\lambda+\epsilon+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}\right).

\big(
using Young’s Inequality given in Theorem
2.5
\big)
.
This implies that

\displaystyle\left(1-\nu-\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}-\frac{\nu}{\epsilon}-\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon^{2}}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\lambda+\epsilon+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon}+\frac{\mu}{2}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}.

Since
2(1-\nu)\epsilon^{2}-(\mu+2\nu)\epsilon-\mu>0
, above inequality becomes

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{(\mu+2)\epsilon^{3}+[2(1+\lambda)+\mu]\epsilon^{2}+2\lambda\epsilon}{2(1-\nu)\epsilon^{2}-(\mu+2\nu)\epsilon-\mu}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

for all finite scalar sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. Thus, by Theorem
2.2
,
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
is a Bessel sequence, and its pre-frame operator
T_{h}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
given by

T_{h}\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h
,
is well-defined and bounded.

Step (II):

In this step, we prove the lower frame condition for
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
. Let
T^{\dagger}_{g}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
be the pseudo-inverse of
T_{g}
. Then, for any
f\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\displaystyle T^{\dagger}_{g}f:=T^{*}_{g}(T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}f=\{\langle f,(T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g\rangle\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\ \big(\text{by Theorem \ref{P2_Th2.6}}).

Note that if inequality (
3.1
) holds for any finite sequence of scalars, then it holds for any sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
. By invoking (
3.1
) for
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=T^{\dagger}_{g}f
, we get

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(A^{*})^{n}h}

\displaystyle\quad+\nu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}.

That is,

\displaystyle\norm{f-T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}f}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\norm{f}^{2}+\mu\norm{f}\,\norm{T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}f}+\nu\norm{T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}f}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\norm{f}^{2}+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}\norm{f}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}\norm{T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}f}^{2}+\nu\norm{T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}f}^{2}\ \big(\text{using Theorem \ref{Young}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}\right)\norm{f}^{2}+\left(\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu\right)\norm{T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}f}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sqrt{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}}\,\norm{f}+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu}\,\norm{T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}f}\right)^{2}\ \text{for all}\ f\in\mathcal{H}.

This implies that
\norm{f-T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}f}\leq\sqrt{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}}\,\norm{f}+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu}\,\norm{T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}f}\ \text{for all}\ f\in\mathcal{H}
.
As
\max{\{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2},\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu\}}<1
, by Lemma
2.4
,
T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}
is invertible, and

\norm{(T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g})^{-1}}\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu}}{1-\sqrt{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}}}.

Now, every
f\in\mathcal{H}
can be written as follows:

\displaystyle f=T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}(T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g})^{-1}f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle(T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g})^{-1}f,(T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g\rangle(A^{*})^{n}h.

Therefore, for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{f}^{4}={\langle f,f\rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \langle(T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g})^{-1}f, (T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle\langle(A^{*})^{n}h, f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g})^{-1}f, (T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(A^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{g}}\norm{(T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g})^{-1}f}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(A^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{g}}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu}}{1-\sqrt{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}}}\right)^{2}\norm{f}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(A^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(A^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}\geq\alpha_{g}\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}}}{1+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu}}\right)^{2}\norm{f}^{2}\,\,\text{for all}\,\,f\in\mathcal{H},

where
\alpha_{g}
is the lower frame bound of
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
.
This gives the lower frame condition for
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
. Hence, by Proposition
1.1
, every
f\in\mathcal{H}
can be recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,h\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.
∎

The following example illustrates Theorem
3.1
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
. Consider the left shift operator
A:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
defined by
A(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots)
. Then, its Hilbert-adjoint operator
A^{*}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
is
the right shift operator. That is,
A^{*}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)=(0,x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
, or
A^{*}e_{n}=e_{n+1}\;\;\text{for all}\;\;n\geq 0
. Let
g=e_{0}
,
h=2e_{0}
. Then,
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}=\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
which is an orthonormal basis of the space
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
. Hence, every
f\in\mathcal{H}
is stably recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
. Now,
g-h=-e_{0}
. Thus,
\{(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)\}_{n\geq 0}=\{-e_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}=\{2e_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. Also, for any finite scalar sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}=\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}e_{n}}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2};

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}^{2}=\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(-e_{n})}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2};

and

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}=\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(2e_{n})}^{2}=4\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}.

Choose
\lambda=0.4
,
\mu=0.1
,
\nu=0.1
and using above three equations in condition
(i)
of Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(0.4)\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}+(0.1)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\times 2\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+(0.1)\,4\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}.

It is easy to see that for any
\epsilon\in(\frac{1}{3},12)
, the quantity
2(1-\nu)\epsilon^{2}-(\mu+2\nu)\epsilon-\mu>0
and
\max{\{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2},\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu\}}<1
. Hence, by Theorem
3.1
, any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can be stably recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,h\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
.

Remark 3.3
.

If we replace first term in condition
(ii)
of Theorem
3.1
by
(1-2\nu)\epsilon-\mu>0
, that is, a linear condition on the parameter
\epsilon
. Then, squaring on both sides of the following inequality

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)},

and using inequality (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+2\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+2\lambda\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}+2\nu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}.

This gives

\displaystyle(1-2\nu)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+\lambda)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+2\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}.

(3.3)

Now, Young’s Inequality given in Theorem
2.5
yields

\displaystyle\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}.

(3.4)

Using (
3.4
) in (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1-2\nu)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+\lambda)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\mu\epsilon\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}.

After collecting terms, we get

\displaystyle\left(1-2\nu-\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2(1+\lambda)+\mu\epsilon\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2},

or,

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\mu\epsilon^{2}+2(1+\lambda)\epsilon}{(1-2\nu)\epsilon-\mu}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

for all finite scalar sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. Hence, the pre-frame operator
T_{h}
of
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is bounded and satisfies

\displaystyle\norm{T_{h}\{a_n\}_{n=0}^{\infty}}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\mu\epsilon^{2}+2(1+\lambda)\epsilon}{(1-2\nu)\epsilon-\mu}\right)\norm{T_{g}\{a_n\}_{n=0}^{\infty}}^{2}\leq\beta_{g}\left(\frac{\mu\epsilon^{2}+2(1+\lambda)\epsilon}{(1-2\nu)\epsilon-\mu}\right)\norm{\{a_n\}_{n=0}^{\infty}}^{2}

for all
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
.
This gives the upper frame condition for
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
. Note that the upper frame bound is different than that given in Theorem
3.1
. Thus, we have the following new variant of the Nagy-type frame perturbation of the sampling vector in the stable recovery of the initial state:

Theorem 3.4
.

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
3.1
, if we replace first term in condition
(ii)
by
(1-2\nu)\epsilon-\mu>0
, then, every
f\in\mathcal{H}
can also be recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,h\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.

.

.

The next theorem provides sufficient conditions for the stable recovery of the initial state under Nagy-type perturbation of the dynamical samples obtained by perturbing the evolution operator.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{H}
and let
A
and
B
be bounded linear operators acting on
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that

(i)

any
f\in\mathcal{H}
is recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way and there exist real numbers
\lambda
,
\mu
,
\nu\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}

\displaystyle\quad+\nu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{2},

(3.5)

for all
l\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and for all finite scalar sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

(ii)

2(1-\nu)\epsilon^{2}-(\mu+2\nu)\epsilon-\mu>0
and
\max{\{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2},\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu\}}<1
, where
\epsilon>0
.

Then, any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can also be recovered from the samples
\{\langle B^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.

Proof.

First, we show that
\{(B^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the upper frame condition. By hypothesis, every
f\in\mathcal{H}
is recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way. Then, by Proposition
1.1
,
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
. Then, by Theorem
2.1
, the map
T_{A}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
given by

T_{A}\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g

is a bounded linear operator with
\norm{T_{A}}\leq\sqrt{\beta_{A}}
.

Consider

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}.

Squaring on both side and using inequality (
3.5
), we get

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+2\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}

\displaystyle\quad+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+2\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}

\displaystyle\quad+\nu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\lambda)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+2\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}+\nu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{2}.

(3.6)

By using Young’s Inequality given in Theorem
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{2},

(3.7)

and

\displaystyle 2\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}^{2}.

(3.8)

Using (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) in (
3
), and after collecting terms, we get

\displaystyle\left(1-\nu-\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}\right)\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\lambda+\epsilon+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\lambda+\epsilon+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}+\frac{\nu}{\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\lambda+\epsilon+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{2}\right)\ \big(\text{by Theorem \ref{Young}}\big).

This gives

\displaystyle\left(1-\nu-\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}-\frac{\nu}{\epsilon}-\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon^{2}}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\lambda+\epsilon+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon}+\frac{\mu}{2}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2},

or

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{(\mu+2)\epsilon^{3}+[2(1+\lambda)+\mu]\epsilon^{2}+2\lambda\epsilon}{2(1-\nu)\epsilon^{2}-(\mu+2\nu)\epsilon-\mu}\right)\norm{T_{A}\{a_n\}_{n=0}^{\infty}}^{2},

for all finite scalar sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.
This gives the upper frame condition for
\{(B^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
.

Next, we show that
\{(B^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the lower frame condition. Let
T_{B}
be the pre-frame operator of
\{(B^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
. Since
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
is a frame, the frame operator
S_{A}=T_{A}T^{*}_{A}
is invertible, and
T^{\dagger}_{A}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
is given by

\displaystyle T^{\dagger}_{A}f=\Big\{\langle f,(T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g\rangle\Big\}_{n=0}^{\infty},\,f\in\mathcal{H}.

Applying inequality (
3.5
) to the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=T^{\dagger}_{A}f
, we get

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(B^{*})^{n}g}

\displaystyle\quad+\nu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(B^{*})^{n}g}^{2},

or,

\displaystyle\norm{f-T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}f}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\norm{f}^{2}+\mu\norm{f}\,\norm{T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}f}+\nu\norm{T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}f}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\norm{f}^{2}+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}\norm{f}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}\norm{T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}f}^{2}+\nu\norm{T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}f}^{2}\ \Big(\text{by Theorem \ref{Young}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}\right)\norm{f}^{2}+\left(\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu\right)\norm{T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}f}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sqrt{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}}\,\norm{f}+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu}\,\norm{T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}f}\right)^{2}\ \text{for all}\ f\in\mathcal{H}.

This gives

\displaystyle\norm{f-T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}f}\leq\sqrt{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}}\,\norm{f}+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu}\,\norm{T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}f}\ \text{for all}\ f\in\mathcal{H}.

Thus, using
\max{\{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2},\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu\}}<1
and Lemma
2.4
, the operator
T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}
is invertible on
\mathcal{H}
, and

\norm{(T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A})^{-1}}\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu}}{1-\sqrt{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}}}.

Now, every
f\in\mathcal{H}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle f=T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}(T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A})^{-1}f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle(T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A})^{-1}f,(T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g\rangle(B^{*})^{n}g.

Using this, we compute

\displaystyle\norm{f}^{4}={\langle f,f\rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle(T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A})^{-1}f,(T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g\rangle\langle(B^{*})^{n}g,f\rangle\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A})^{-1}f, (T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}g,f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{A}}\norm{(T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A})^{-1}f}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}g,f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{A}}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu}}{1-\sqrt{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}}}\right)^{2}\norm{f}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}g,f \rangle}^{2}\ \text{for all}\ f\in\mathcal{H},

which entails

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}g,f \rangle}^{2}\geq\alpha_{A}\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{\lambda+\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}}}{1+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}+\nu}}\right)^{2}\norm{f}^{2}\,\,\text{for all}\,\,f\in\mathcal{H}.

This gives the lower frame condition for
\{(B^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
.
Hence, by Proposition
1.1
, every
f\in\mathcal{H}
can also be recovered from the samples
\{\langle B^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.
∎

\displaystyle\{(B^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}=\{e_{n}+\alpha e_{n-1}+\alpha^{2}e_{n-2}+\ldots+\alpha^{n}e_{0}\}_{n\geq 0},

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}+\nu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}^{2},\;\;l\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},

\displaystyle\quad+2\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(h-g)}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}

\displaystyle\quad+2\norm{\sum_{n =0}^{l}a_{n}((B^{*})^{n}-(A^{*})^{n})h}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((B^{*})^{n}-(A^{*})^{n})h}^{2}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(h-g)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((B^{*})^{n}-(A^{*})^{n})h}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(h-g)}^{2}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(h-g)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((B^{*})^{n}-(A^{*})^{n})h}^{2}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle=3\left[\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((B^{*})^{n}-(A^{*})^{n})h}^{2}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(h-g)}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+3\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\lambda\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+3\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}

\displaystyle\quad+3\nu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}^{2}+3\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}\ \big(\text{by using \eqref{P2_S1_5}}\big).

(3.13)

By using Young’s inequality given in Theorem
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}\leq\frac{\mu\epsilon}{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2\epsilon}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}^{2}.

Using this, after collecting terms in inequality (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{3\mu}{2\epsilon}-3\nu\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}^{2}\leq\left(3(1+\lambda)+\frac{3\mu\epsilon}{2}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}.

That is,

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{3\mu\epsilon^{2}+6(1+\lambda)\epsilon}{2(1-3\nu)\epsilon-3\mu}\right)\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

(3.14)

for all finite scalar sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. By hypothesis,
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
is a Bessel sequence.
Thus, from inequality (
3.14
), we conclude that
\{(B^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
is a Bessel sequence.

Next, we show that
\{(B^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the lower frame condition in
\mathcal{H}
. Let
T_{B_{h}}
be the pre-frame operator of
\{(B^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\alpha_{A_{g}}
be a lower frame bound of
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
. If
T_{A_{g}}
is the pre-frame operator of
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
, then the frame operator
S_{A_{g}}=T_{A_{g}}T^{*}_{A_{g}}
is invertible on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
T^{\dagger}_{A_{g}}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
be the pseudo-inverse of
T_{A_{g}}
. Then,

T^{\dagger}_{A_{g}}f=\Big\{\big\langle f,(T_{A_{g}}T^{*}_{A_{g}})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g\big\rangle\Big\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. Squaring both sides in the following inequality

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}g-(B^{*})^{n}h)}

\displaystyle=\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}g-(A^{*})^{n}h+(A^{*})^{n}h-(B^{*})^{n}h)}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}+\norm{\sum_{n =0}^{\infty}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})h},

we get

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}g-(B^{*})^{n}h)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}+\norm{\sum_{n =0}^{\infty}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})h}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left[\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}^{2}+\norm{\sum_{n =0}^{\infty}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})h}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}+2\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}

\displaystyle\quad+2\nu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}^{2}\quad\big(\text{by using \eqref{P2_S1_5}}\big).

(3.15)

Applying inequality (
3
) to the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=T^{\dagger}_{A_{g}}f
, we obtain

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A_g}T^{*}_{A_g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle((A^{*})^{n}g-(B^{*})^{n}h)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A_g}T^{*}_{A_g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(A^{*})^{n}g}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\mu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A_g}T^{*}_{A_g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(A^{*})^{n}g}\,\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A_g}T^{*}_{A_g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(B^{*})^{n}h}

\displaystyle\quad+2\nu\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A_g}T^{*}_{A_g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(B^{*})^{n}h}^{2},

or,

\displaystyle\norm{f-T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g}f}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda\norm{f}^{2}+2\mu\norm{f}\,\norm{T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g}f}+2\nu\norm{T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g}f}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda\norm{f}^{2}+\frac{2\mu\norm{f}^{2}\,\epsilon}{2}+\frac{2\mu}{2\epsilon}\norm{T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g}f}^{2}+2\nu\norm{T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g}f}^{2}\ (\text{by Theorem \ref{Young}})

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\lambda+\mu\epsilon\right)\norm{f}^{2}+\left(\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}+2\nu\right)\norm{T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g}f}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sqrt{2\lambda+\mu\epsilon}\,\norm{f}+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}+2\nu}\,\norm{T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g}f}\right)^{2}.

Since,
\max{\{2\lambda+\mu\epsilon,\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}+2\nu\}}<1
. By using Lemma
2.4
,
T_{B_{h}}T^{\dagger}_{A_{g}}
is invertible, and

\displaystyle\norm{(T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g})^{-1}}\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}+2\nu}}{1-\sqrt{2\lambda+\mu\epsilon}}.

Now, every
f\in\mathcal{H}
can be written as

\displaystyle f=T_{B_{h}}T^{\dagger}_{A_{g}}(T_{B_{h}}T^{\dagger}_{A_{g}})^{-1}f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle(T_{B_{h}}T^{\dagger}_{A_{g}})^{-1}f,(T_{A_{g}}T^{*}_{A_{g}})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g\rangle(B^{*})^{n}h.

Therefore, for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{f}^{4}={\langle f,f\rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \langle(T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g})^{-1}f, (T_{A_g}T^{*}_{A_g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle\langle(B^{*})^{n}h, f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g})^{-1}f, (T_{A_g}T^{*}_{A_g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{A_{g}}}\norm{(T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g})^{-1}f}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{A_{g}}}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}+2\nu}}{1-\sqrt{2\lambda+\mu\epsilon}}\right)^{2}\norm{f}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}.

This gives
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}\geq\alpha_{A_{g}}\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{2\lambda+\mu\epsilon}}{1+\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\epsilon}+2\nu}}\right)^{2}\norm{f}^{2}
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}
. Thus,
\{(B^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the lower frame condition. Hence, by Proposition
1.1
, any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can also be recovered from the samples
\{\langle B^{n}f,h\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.
∎

4
Dynamical Samples using Pollard-Hilding-Type Frame Perturbation

In this section, we investigate the stability of dynamical sampling systems under Pollard–Hilding-type frame perturbation. We begin with the stable recovery of the initial state that is preserved under Pollard–Hilding-type frame perturbation of the sampling vector.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
g,h\in\mathcal{H}
and let
A
be a bounded linear operator acting on
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that

(i)

any
f\in\mathcal{H}
is recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.

(ii)

For each positive real number
k
, there exist constants
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}\geq 0
such that

\max{\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}}<\min{\{1,2^{k-1}\}}
and

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}\leq\left[\lambda_{1}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}},\;\;l\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},

(4.1)

for all finite scalar sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

Then, any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can also be recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,h\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.

Proof.

First, we show that
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the upper frame condition. By hypothesis
(i)
,
every
f\in\mathcal{H}
is recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way. Therefore, by Proposition
1.1
,
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
.
For
h\in\mathcal{H}
, consider the following:

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}+\left[\lambda_{1}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}}\,\big(\text{using \eqref{P2_S2_1}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\left[\left[\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}+\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}\right]^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}}.

Note that the last step in above follows from Theorem
2.6
.
This implies that

\displaystyle(1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}})\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}

\displaystyle\leq(1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}})\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g},\quad\text{where}\quad c=\begin{cases}1,&\;\text{if}\;k\geq 1,\\
2^{1-k},&\;\text{if}\;k\leq 1.\end{cases}

That is,

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}\leq\frac{(1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}})}{(1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}})}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\ \Big(\text{as}\max{\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}}<\min{\{1,2^{k-1}\}}\Big),

for all finite scalar sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. This gives the upper frame condition for
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
.

Now, we show that
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies lower frame condition. Let
T_{g}
and
T_{h}
be the pre-frame operators of
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
, respectively. Let
\alpha_{g}
be a lower frame bound of
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
. Recall that the pseudo-inverse of
T_{g}
is given by

T^{\dagger}_{g}f=\{\langle f,(T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g\rangle\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
,
f\in\mathcal{H}
. Note that if inequality (
4.1
) holds for any finite sequence of scalars, then it holds for any sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
. By invoking (
4.1
) for
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=T^{\dagger}_{g}f
, we get

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n})g \rangle((A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\lambda_{1}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(A^{*})^{n}h}^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}}.

Therefore, for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{f-T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}f}\leq\left[\lambda_{1}\norm{f}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}f}^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}}\leq c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}\norm{f}+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}\norm{T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}f}\quad\big(\text{by Theorem \ref{Theorem 2.8}}\big).

As,
\max{\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}}<\min{\{1,2^{k-1}\}}
, by using Lemma
2.4
,
T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}
is invertible, and

\displaystyle\norm{(T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g})^{-1}}\leq\frac{1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}}{1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}}.

Note that
f=T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g}(T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g})^{-1}f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle(T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g})^{-1}f,(T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g\rangle(A^{*})^{n}h
for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
.
Using this, we compute

\displaystyle\norm{f}^{4}={\langle f,f\rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \langle(T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g})^{-1}f, (T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle\langle(A^{*})^{n}h, f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g})^{-1}f, (T_{g}T^{*}_{g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(A^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{g}}\norm{(T_{h}T^{\dagger}_{g})^{-1}f}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(A^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{g}}\left(\frac{1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}}{1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}}\right)^{2}\norm{f}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(A^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}.

Therefore,
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(A^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}\geq\alpha_{g}\left(\frac{1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}}{1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}}\right)^{2}\norm{f}^{2}
for all

f\in\mathcal{H}
.
Thus,
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the lower frame condition for
\mathcal{H}
. Hence, by Proposition
1.1
, any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can also be recovered from
\{\langle A^{n}f,h\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.
∎

We now present an example to illustrate Theorem
4.1
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
and let
\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. Consider the left shift operator
A
given in Example
3.2
.
Let
g=e_{0}
,
h=2e_{0}
. Then,
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}=\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
which is an orthonormal basis of the space
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
. Hence, every
f\in\mathcal{H}
is stably recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
. Now,
g-h=-e_{0}
. Thus,
\{(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)\}_{n\geq 0}=\{-e_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{(A^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}=\{2e_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. Also, for any finite scalar sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}=\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}e_{n}}^{k}=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}};

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}^{k}=\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(-e_{n})}^{k}=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}};

and

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{k}=\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(2e_{n})}^{k}=2^{k}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}.

Choose
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}
and using above three equations in condition
(ii)
of Theorem
4.1
, we get

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}^{k}=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}+2^{k-1}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}

as if
k\geq 1
, then
2^{k-1}\geq 1
; and if
0<k<1
, then
\frac{1}{2}<2^{k-1}<1
. Therefore, for every
k>0
,

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}^{k}\leq\lambda_{1}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}h}^{k}.

Also,
\max\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}=\max\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\}=\frac{1}{2}
and
\min\{1,2^{k-1}\}=\begin{cases}1,&\;\text{if}\;k\geq 1,\\
2^{k-1},&\;\text{if}\;0<k<1.\end{cases}

This implies,
\max{\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}}<\min{\{1,2^{k-1}\}}
. Hence, by Theorem
4.1
, any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can be stably recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,h\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
.

The following theorem investigate perturbation of the dynamical samples induced by changes in the evolution operator within the framework of Pollard–Hilding-type frame perturbation.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{H}
and let
A
and
B
be bounded linear operators acting on
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that

(i)

any
f\in\mathcal{H}
is recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.

(ii)

For each positive real number
k
, there exist constants
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}\geq 0
such that
\max{\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}}<\min{\{1,2^{k-1}\}}
and

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}\leq\left[\lambda_{1}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}},\;\;l\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},

(4.2)

for all finite scalar sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

Then, any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can also be recovered from the samples
\{\langle B^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.

Proof.

Suppose each
f\in\mathcal{H}
is recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way. Then, by Proposition
1.1
,
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
.
By hypothesis (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}+\left[\lambda_{1}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\left[\left[\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}+\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}\right]^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}}.

The last step follows from Theorem
2.6
. This implies that

\displaystyle(1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}})\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}

\displaystyle\leq(1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}})\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g},\quad\text{where}\quad c=\begin{cases}1,&\;\text{if}\;k\geq 1,\\
2^{1-k},&\;\text{if}\;k\leq 1.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}\leq\frac{(1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}})}{(1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}})}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}\ \Big(\text{as}\ \max{\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}}<\min{\{1,2^{k-1}\}}\Big),

for all finite scalar sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.
Hence,
\{(B^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the upper frame condition. To show
\{(B^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the lower frame condition: Let
T_{A}
be the pre-frame operator of
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
, and let
\alpha_{A}
be its lower frame bound. Using inequality (
4.2
) for the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}:=T^{\dagger}_{A}f=\{\langle f,(T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g\rangle\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, we get

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n})g \rangle((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\lambda_{1}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(B^{*})^{n}g}^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}}.

Let
T_{B}
be the pre-frame operator of
\{(B^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
. Then, by using Theorem
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{f-T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}f}\leq\left[\lambda_{1}\norm{f}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}f}^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}}\leq c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}\norm{f}+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}\norm{T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}f}.

By using Lemma
2.4
,
T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A}
is invertible, and
\norm{(T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A})^{-1}}\leq\frac{1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}}{1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}}
, as

\max{\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}}<\min{\{1,2^{k-1}\}}
. We compute,

\displaystyle\norm{f}^{4}={\langle f,f\rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \langle(T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A})^{-1}f, (T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle\langle(B^{*})^{n}g, f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A})^{-1}f, (T_{A}T^{*}_{A})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}g,f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{A}}\norm{(T_{B}T^{\dagger}_{A})^{-1}f}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}g,f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{A}}\left(\frac{1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}}{1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}}\right)^{2}\norm{f}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}g,f \rangle}^{2}.

This gives
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}g,f \rangle}^{2}\geq\alpha_{A}\left(\frac{1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}}{1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}}\right)^{2}\norm{f}^{2}
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}
. Thus,
\{(B^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the lower frame condition in
\mathcal{H}
. Hence, by Proposition
1.1
, any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can also be recovered from the samples
\{\langle B^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.
∎

The following example demonstrates Theorem
4.3
.

Example 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
and let
\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. Consider the left shift operator
A
given in Example
3.2
.
Let
g=e_{0}
. Then,
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}=\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
which is an orthonormal basis of the space
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
. Hence, every
f\in\mathcal{H}
is stably recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
.
Now, for any
k>0
,
2^{\frac{k-1}{k}}>0
. By using Archimedian Property, for every
k>0
, there always exist an
\epsilon\in(0,1)
such that
\epsilon<2^{\frac{k-1}{k}}
. Let
B:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
be a bounded linear operator such that
B^{*}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)=(0,\beta_{0}x_{0},\beta_{1}x_{1},\beta_{2}x_{2},\ldots)
, where
\beta_{i}=1-\frac{\epsilon}{2^{i+1}}
. Then,
\{(B^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}=\{P_{n}e_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, where
P_{n}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\beta_{i}
and
\{((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g\}_{n\geq 0}=\{(1-P_{n})e_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. Also, for any finite scalar sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}=\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}e_{n}}^{k}=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}};

(4.3)

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}^{k}=\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(1- P_{n})e_{n}}^{k}=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n(1-P_{n})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}};

(4.4)

and

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{k}=\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(P_{n}e_{n})}^{k}=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n P_{n}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}.

(4.5)

Note that,
1-\epsilon<P_{n}\leq 1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}
. This implies,
\frac{\epsilon}{2}\leq 1-P_{n}<\epsilon
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n(1-P_{n})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}<\epsilon^{k}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}.

Now, using (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), for every
k>0
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})g}^{k}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n(1-P_{n})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{k}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}+\epsilon^{k}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{l}\absolutevalue{a_n P_{n}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}g}^{k},

where we choose
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\epsilon^{k}
.
Also,
\max\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}=\max\{\epsilon^{k},\epsilon^{k}\}=\epsilon^{k}
and
\min\{1,2^{k-1}\}=\begin{cases}1,&\;\text{if}\;k\geq 1,\\
2^{k-1},&\;\text{if}\;0<k<1.\end{cases}

This implies,
\max{\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}}<\min{\{1,2^{k-1}\}}
. Hence, by Theorem
4.3
, any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can be stably recovered from the samples
\{\langle B^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
.

The next theorem establishes the preservation of stable recovery of the initial state unde simultaneous perturbations of the sampling vector and the evolution operator.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
g,h\in\mathcal{H}
and let
A
and
B
be bounded linear operators acting on
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that

(i)

any
f\in\mathcal{H}
is recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.

(ii)

For each positive real number
k
, there exist constants
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}\geq 0
such that
\max{\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}}<\min{\{1,2^{k-1}\}}
and

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}+\norm{\sum_{n =0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})h}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\lambda_{1}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}},\;\;l\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},

(4.6)

for all finite scalar sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

Then, any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can also be recovered from the samples
\{\langle B^{n}f,h\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.

Proof.

First, we show that
\{(B^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies upper frame condition. By hypothesis
(i)
, every
f\in\mathcal{H}
is recovered from the samples
\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way. Therefore, by Proposition
1.1
,
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
. Now, consider the following and using (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}

\displaystyle=\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((B^{*})^{n}h-(A^{*})^{n}h+(A^{*})^{n}h-(A^{*})^{n}g+(A^{*})^{n}g)}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}+\norm{\sum_{n =0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})h}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\lambda_{1}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}

\displaystyle\leq c^{\frac{1}{k}}\left[\left[\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}+\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}\right]^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}}+\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}.

Observe that the final step follows from Theorem
2.6
. Consequently,

\displaystyle(1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}})\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}

\displaystyle\leq(1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}})\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g},\quad\text{where}\quad c=\begin{cases}1,&\;\text{if}\;k\geq 1,\\
2^{1-k},&\;\text{if}\;k\leq 1.\end{cases}

Therefore, by hypothesis
(ii)
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}\leq\frac{(1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}})}{(1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}})}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}

for all finite scalar sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. Hence, by Theorem
2.2
,
\{(B^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies upper frame condition.

Now, we prove that
\{(B^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies lower frame condition. Let
T_{A_{g}}
be the pre-frame operator of
\{(A^{*})^{n}g\}_{n\geq 0}
, and let
\alpha_{A_{g}}
be its lower frame bound.
Now, consider the following and using (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}g-(B^{*})^{n}h)}

\displaystyle=\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}g-(A^{*})^{n}h+(A^{*})^{n}h-(B^{*})^{n}h)}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}(g-h)}+\norm{\sum_{n =0}^{\infty}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})h}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\lambda_{1}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(B^{*})^{n}h}^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}}.

(4.7)

Let
T^{\dagger}_{A_{g}}
denotes the pseudo-inverse of
T_{A_{g}}
. Then, by applying (
4.7
) to
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=T^{\dagger}_{A_{g}}f
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A_g}T^{*}_{A_g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n})g \rangle((A^{*})^{n}g-(B^{*})^{n}h)}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\lambda_{1}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A_g}T^{*}_{A_g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(A^{*})^{n}g}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f, (T_{A_g}T^{*}_{A_g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle(B^{*})^{n}h}^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}}.

Let
T_{B_{h}}
be the pre-frame operator of
\{(B^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
. Then, by using Theorem
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{f-T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g}f}\leq\left[\lambda_{1}\norm{f}^{k}+\lambda_{2}\norm{T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g}f}^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{k}}\leq c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}\norm{f}+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}\norm{T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g}f}.

By using Lemma
2.4
,
T_{B_{h}}T^{\dagger}_{A_{g}}
is invertible, and
\norm{(T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g})^{-1}}\leq\frac{1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}}{1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}}
, as

\max{\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}}<\min{\{1,2^{k-1}\}}
. Note that every
f\in\mathcal{H}
can be expressed as:

\displaystyle f=T_{B_{h}}T^{\dagger}_{A_{g}}(T_{B_{h}}T^{\dagger}_{A_{g}})^{-1}f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle(T_{B_{h}}T^{\dagger}_{A_{g}})^{-1}f,(T_{A_{g}}T^{*}_{A_{g}})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g\rangle(B^{*})^{n}h.

Using above representation of
f\in\mathcal{H}
, we compute

\displaystyle\norm{f}^{4}={\langle f,f\rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \langle(T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g})^{-1}f, (T_{A_g}T^{*}_{A_g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle\langle(B^{*})^{n}h, f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g})^{-1}f, (T_{A_g}T^{*}_{A_g})^{-1}(A^{*})^{n}g \rangle}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{A_{g}}}\norm{(T_{B_h}T^{\dagger}_{A_g})^{-1}f}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{A_{g}}}\left(\frac{1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}}{1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}}\right)^{2}\norm{f}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2},

which yields

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\langle(B^{*})^{n}h,f \rangle}^{2}\geq\alpha_{A_{g}}\left(\frac{1-c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}}{1+c^{\frac{1}{k}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{k}}}\right)^{2}\norm{f}^{2}\,\,\text{for all}\,\,f\in\mathcal{H}.

This gives lower frame condition for
\{(B^{*})^{n}h\}_{n\geq 0}
. Hence, by Proposition
1.1
, any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can also be recovered from the samples
\{\langle B^{n}f,h\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
in a stable way.
∎

The following example serves to illustrate Theorem
4.5
.

Example 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
and
A:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be same as given in Example
3.2
. Take
B=A
,
g=e_{0}
, and
h=2e_{0}
. Then,
B^{*}=A^{*}
and
\{((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})h\}_{n\geq 0}=\{0\}_{n\geq 0}
. Also,
\norm{\sum_{n=0}^{l}a_{n}((A^{*})^{n}-(B^{*})^{n})h}

=0
. Then, by using the same step as in Example
4.2
, the hypothesis of Theorem
4.5
is satisfied. Hence, by Theorem
4.5
, any
f\in\mathcal{H}
can be stably recovered from the samples
\{\langle B^{n}f,h\rangle\}_{n\geq 0}
.

The stable recovery results obtained in this section naturally lead to the following concluding remark concerning the stability of general frames in separable Hilbert spaces under Pollard–Hilding-type perturbation.

Remark 4.7
.

Let
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a frame for
\mathcal{H}
and
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{H}
. If
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
are close in the sense of Pollard–Hilding, which is given in the conditions of Theorem
4.1
, then
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
constitutes a frame for
\mathcal{H}
.

5
Conclusion

A sequence
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
complete
if
\overline{\text{span}}\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}=\mathcal{H}
. Harry Pollard, in
[
37
]
, proved a Paley-Wiener-type stability result for complete sequences in Hilbert spaces. Further, he also gives a criterion for incomplete sequences in Hilbert spaces in terms of a Paley-Wiener-type condition. After four years, Hilding
[
29
]
presented a Paley-Wiener-type stability result for completeness of a sequence in terms of a linear transformation on Hilbert space.
Near the middle of the 20th century, Retherford
[
39
]
generalized stability results in the sense of Paley-Wiener for basic sequences and completeness of sequences in complete linear metric spaces and normed spaces and introduced Nagy-type and Pollard–Hilding-type stability conditions [Definition
1.2
].
Casazza and Kalton, in
[
17
]
, extended the Paley-Wiener-type stability results to linear operators on Banach spaces. Frames in separable Hilbert spaces are complete sequences which satisfies an operator inequality, also known as the frame inequality, that is a powerful tool in both pure and engineering science. In
[
16
]
, Casazza and Christensen showed applications of Paley-Wiener stability to frames in separable Hilbert spaces. Paley-Wiener type stability conditions for frames and Riesz bases were reviewed in
[
28
,
47
]
.

On the other hand, Aldroubi et al.
[
2
,
4
,
7
]
studied conditions
on
A
,
\mathcal{G}
and a function
L:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, that allow the stable recovery of the initial state of the homogenous discrete dynamical system of the form
f_{n}=Af_{n-1}=A^{n}f,f_{0}=f
, from the set of samples

\{\langle A^{n}f,g\rangle:g\in\mathcal{G},0\leq n<L(g)\}
, where
A
is the evolution operator,
f_{0}
is the initial state, and
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{H}
, is a finite or countably infinite set.

In this work, we give applications of Nagy-type and Pollard–Hilding-type stability conditions to homogenous dynamical sampling systems for stable recovery of the initial state in terms of frames. More precisely, we establish sufficient conditions for the stable recovery of an initial state from perturbed dynamical samples, which are derived by altering the sampling vector, the evolution operator, or both concurrently within the context of Nagy-type and Pollard–Hilding-type perturbations of frames. A further interesting direction would be applications of perturbation of weaving frames
[
21
,
22
]
in the stable recovery of source terms and initial states in dynamical sampling systems.
