On the Conicality of Causally Simple, Future Cohesive Spacetimes
Claudio F. Paganini ∗

Abstract
. The notion of conicality, recently introduced in [ 6 ] , captures the extent to which the joint future of a finite set in spacetime uniquely determines the generating subset via its light cone structure. In the same paper it was mentioned that conicality holds for Minkowski spacetime of dimension 1 + N 1+N with N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 and it has been conjectured that this property holds more generally. In this work, we show that neither homotopy with Minkowski space nor global hyperbolicity alone are sufficient for the spacetime to satisfy conicality. We then establish that causally simple, future cohesive spacetimes of dimension 1 + N 1+N with N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 satisfy the condition. This class of spacetimes in particular includes TIP spacetimes, which can be understood as the timelike past of an observer. This is in line with the origin of causal modeling since the past of an observer is the natural domain for the description of experiments.



1.
Introduction

The causal structure of a Lorentzian manifold describes the ordering of events in a spacetime. In recent work
[
6
]
, the notion of
conicality
was introduced as an order-theoretic property capturing the extent to which the joint future of a finite set of points determines the generating subset via its light cone structure. This concept arises naturally at the interface between Lorentzian geometry and causal modeling, where one seeks to understand how abstract causal relations can be faithfully embedded into spacetime.

More precisely, conicality expresses a rigidity property of the causal future operation: the intersection of future sets of finitely many events encodes sufficient information to reconstruct the subset of generators. In
[
6
]
, it was shown that Minkowski spacetime of dimension
1+N
, with
N\geq 2
, satisfies conicality, while the property fails in
1+1
dimensions. Based on these observations, it was conjectured that conicality persists for a significantly broader class of spacetimes satisfying suitable causal regularity assumptions.

The motivation for this conjecture is rooted in causal modeling. In frameworks such as those developed in
[
14
,
11
,
13
,
8
,
7
,
12
,
2
,
1
,
4
]
, causal relations are represented abstractly via directed structures encoding dependencies among variables. Embedding such structures into spacetimes
[
15
,
16
,
6
]
requires compatibility between the combinatorial notion of causality and the geometric one induced by light cones. Conicality can be interpreted as a condition ensuring that joint future regions behave in a manner consistent with causal inference, preventing degeneracies in which different generating sets produce indistinguishable observable futures.

In this paper, we will prove the following theorem, relaxing the conjectured causality condition from globally hyperbolic in
[
6
]
to causally simple.

Main Theorem
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a causally simple, future cohesive spacetime of dimension
1+N
with
N\geq 2
, then
(\mathcal{M},g)
is conical according to Definition
2.7
.

In an example, we will demonstrate that global hyperbolicity alone is not sufficient for conicality to hold, but that we need to require additionally that the spacetime is future cohesive. This property is satisfied in particular by TIP spacetimes. These can be understood as the timelike past of an observer.

The proof is entirely based on methods from Lorentzian causality theory, which is a rich field of on-going mathematical research, see, e.g.,
[
9
]
and references therein. Causal simplicity ensures that the causal relation is closed and that null geodesics in the boundary of the causal future of a point intersect the point itself. The key insight enabling this result is the fact that for spacetime dimensions larger than three an arbitrary small open subset of the causal boundary is sufficient to identify the point uniquely. Furthermore, the result uses the fact that the definition of conicality requires the property to hold only for finite sets. Counter examples for infinite sets will be provided.

An interesting observation on the side is that for the joint future, and therefore the spanning set (see, Definition
2.6
), to be non-trivial, all points in the set need to be elements of the timelike past of a timelike curve. These TIP spacetimes are also proven to be future cohesive. This observation guarantees that conicality holds for any experimental data an observer (i.e. an entity conducting experiments) will ever collect, as all real world data has to originate from somewhere in the observers past light cone. Hence the result covers the class of spacetimes that are most relevant in the context where the conjecture originated.

The present result therefore strengthens the conceptual link between Lorentzian geometry and causal modeling introduced in
[
6
]
by identifying precise mathematical conditions under which spacetimes can faithfully encode abstract causal structure.

Conical Spacetimes

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a Lorentzian manifold with dimension
\operatorname{dim}\mathcal{M}=1+N

(-,+,\dots,+)

The metric

Definition 2.7

(Conicality)

Let

Remark 2.8

This definition differs from

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a poset. If
{\rm span}(L_{i})={\rm span}(L_{j})
for two finite subsets
L_{i},L_{j}\subset\mathcal{T}

JF(L_{i})=JF(L_{j})
.

Proof.

Write
{\rm span}(L_{i})=L_{i}^{1}\cup\dotsb\cup L_{i}^{n}
, where each
L_{i}^{k}
is a minimal subset of
L_{i}
. Likewise, write
{\rm span}(L_{j})=L_{j}^{1}\cup\dotsb\cup L_{j}^{m}
, where each
L_{j}^{k}
is a minimal subset of
L_{j}
. Then

JF(L_{i})=\bigcap_{x\in L_{i}}J^{+}(x)=\bigcap_{k=1}^{n}\bigcap_{x\in L_{i}^{k}}J^{+}(x)=\bigcap_{x\in{\rm span}(L_{i})}J^{+}(x).

Similarly,

JF(L_{j})=\bigcap_{x\in L_{j}}J^{+}(x)=\bigcap_{k=1}^{m}\bigcap_{x\in L_{j}^{k}}J^{+}(x)=\bigcap_{x\in{\rm span}(L_{j})}J^{+}(x).

The right-hand side of both equalities are the same since
{\rm span}(L_{i})={\rm span}(L_{j})
.
∎
