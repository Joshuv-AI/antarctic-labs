Lifetime and spectral function of topological heavy fermions
Nemin Wei, Felix von Oppen, Leonid I. Glazman

Abstract
Twisted bilayer graphene provides a paradigmatic platform for exploring the interplay between electronic topology and strong correlations. Within the topological heavy fermion model [Song and Bernevig, Phys. Rev. Lett. 129, 047601 (2022)], topology and electron interactions are brought together by including a weak hybridization between the bands of itinerant c c - and localized f f -electrons. Hybridization infuses concentrated Berry curvature into the f f -band, while leaving it flat. These band features have motivated recent proposals of a Mott semimetal phase above the flavor-ordering temperature at charge neutrality. In this work, we develop an analytic theory of the quasiparticle dispersion and lifetime in the Mott semimetal. We reformulate the interacting flat-band Hamiltonian as an on-site Hubbard interaction defined on a set of non-orthogonal orbitals, and compute the electron Green’s function using the equation-of-motion method, in close analogy with the Hubbard-III approximation. Unlike the conventional Hubbard model, in our case this approximation is controlled by a well-defined small parameter in the theory. We evaluate the electron self-energy and demonstrate the emergence of well-defined low-energy quasiparticles with the dispersion and relaxation rate proportional to the interaction strength. The quasiparticle spectrum is well-resolved in energy and in momentum down to the very vicinity of the Fermi level. Our results illustrate unconventional spectral properties arising from strong correlations and nontrivial quantum geometry, and have direct relevance for spectroscopic probes such as quantum twisting microscope experiments.

The topological nature of the flat bands appears to be in tension with the phenomenology of localized electrons. One approach to resolving this tension is to construct exponentially localized orbitals by incorporating both flat and remote bands

;
Datta et al. 2023
;
Carr et al. 2019

The results of the two approaches are consistent with each other in predicting a remarkable reconstruction of the elementary excitation spectrum, even without an intervening symmetry-breaking transition.

A striking prediction from both numerical

;
Haule et al. 2019
;
Datta et al. 2023

The identification of elementary excitations of a strongly-interacting electron system inevitably raises the question of their lifetimes and their affinity to the free-electron excitations. At a qualitative level, this question was partially addressed in Ref.

However, the intriguing question of the “quality” of the low-energy elementary excitations close to the
\Gamma
point of the mBZ remained open. The goal of our work is to evaluate the lifetime of excitations in that part of the spectrum.

Truncating this chain after the second equation allows one to find the electron self-energy

To find our key results for the quasiparticle lifetime, we keep two more consecutive levels of the chain of equations. Factorizing the highest-order correlation function into lower-order ones allows us to obtain a closed system of equations which correspond to the Hubbard-III approximation. This approximation generates self-energy corrections of order

the dependence of the relaxation rate

\epsilon_{k}\tau_{k}\sim(Ns^{2})^{-1}\gg 1

\epsilon_{k}\sim M

Below, we first present our main results without entering technical details in Sec. II. The equation of motion methods and Hubbard-I approximation are introduced in Sec. III. Section IV provides details of the theory at the Hubbard III level for the projected THF Hamiltonian with

II
Main Results

The THF model consists of a periodic lattice of localized

-electrons hybridizing with itinerant

(2)

In the chiral-flat limit,

We now turn to the details of the derivation, starting with the Hubbard-I approximation in this section. To treat the strong local correlations resulting from the on-site repulsion

\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{z}}Pf_{i}P=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{s}z}}\sum_{\bm{k}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{i}}\frac{k}{\sqrt{k^{2}+k_{\star}^{2}}}d_{\bm{k}\sigma},

(22)

with normalization constant

z=\int_{\text{mBZ}}\frac{d^{2}k}{\Omega_{m}}\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+k_{\star}^{2}}\approx 1-s^{2}\ln\frac{1}{s^{2}}.

(23)

The operators
\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}
constitute a nonorthogonal basis distinct from the Wannier basis of the flat bands, and obey the anticommutation relations

\displaystyle\{\tilde{f}_{i\sigma},\tilde{f}_{i^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}\}=0,

(24)

\displaystyle\{\tilde{f}_{i\sigma},\tilde{f}_{i^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\lambda_{ii^{\prime}},

(25)

where
\lambda_{ij}
[see Eq. (
11
)] satisfies
\lambda_{ij}=\lambda_{ji}^{*}=\lambda_{ji}
.

In terms of the operators
\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}
,
we obtain a compact real-space expression for the projected interaction in Eq. (
II
),

\tilde{H}_{U}=\frac{U}{2}\sum_{i}\left(\sum_{\sigma=1}^{N}\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}-\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}.

(26)

Strictly speaking, the interaction strength is reduced to
Uz^{2}
, but we have neglected the rescaling factor
z^{2}\approx 1
.

Equation (
26
) corresponds to a Hubbard interaction on a non-orthogonal basis. Unlike the conventional Hubbard interaction,
\tilde{H}_{U}
can transfer electrons between different sites,

[\tilde{f}_{i\sigma},\tilde{H}_{U}]=U\sum_{i^{\prime}}\lambda_{ii^{\prime}}\delta n_{i^{\prime}\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{i^{\prime}\sigma},

(27)

with

\delta n_{i\bar{\sigma}}\equiv\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\neq\sigma}\left(\tilde{f}_{i\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{i\sigma^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\right).

(28)

Thus,
\tilde{H}_{U}
can give rise to a finite dispersion of electrons, even if the band is flat in the absence of interactions (
M=0
limit).

In this work, we study the single-particle Green’s function of the projected THF model at charge neutrality using the equation of motion method
Zubarev 1960
. We denote the retarded Green’s function of two operators
A
and
B
, which are odd under fermion parity, as

\displaystyle\langle\langle A;B\rangle\rangle\equiv-i\theta(t)\langle\left\{A(t),B(0)\right\}\rangle.

(29)

Here,
\theta(t)
is the Heaviside function, and the angular bracket stands for ensemble averaging.
The Green’s function satisfies the equation of motion

i\partial_{t}\langle\langle A;B\rangle\rangle=\delta(t)\langle\{A(0),B(0)\}\rangle+\langle\langle[A,\tilde{H}];B\rangle\rangle.

(30)

We focus first on the chiral-flat limit,
M=0
. Using the relation Eq. (
27
) and the shorthand notation
\psi_{i\sigma}\equiv\delta n_{i\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}=\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}\delta n_{i\bar{\sigma}}
for the ‘trion’ operator
Ledwith et al. 2025b
, we derive the equations of motion

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda_{ji}\delta(t)+U\sum_{l}\lambda_{jl}\langle\langle\psi_{l\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle,

(31)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\langle\langle\psi_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda_{ji}\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\rangle\delta(t)+U\langle\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+U\sum_{l\neq j}\lambda_{jl}\langle\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\delta n_{l\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\langle\langle[\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}},\tilde{H}]\tilde{f}_{l\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle.

(32)

We restrict our discussion to the paramagnetic Mott state in which the charge is frozen,
\sum_{\sigma}f_{j\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{j\sigma}=N/2
, but flavor is thermally disordered, i.e.,
\langle f_{j\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{j\sigma}\rangle
is independent of
\sigma

32

\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\rangle=0,\quad\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}^{2}\rangle\approx\frac{1}{4},

(33a)

and

\langle\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}^{2}f_{j\sigma};f_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle\approx\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}^{2}\rangle\langle\langle f_{j\sigma};f_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle.

(33b)

Relations (
33a
) and (

) become exact in the atomic limit (

s
in case of
N=2

The Green’s functions in the second and third lines of Eq. (
32
) both involve two different sites at the same time
t
, and hence are suppressed by a factor of
s^{2}
.

To the zeroth order in

, we retain only the first line of Eq. (
32

33a

33b
). This corresponds to the Hubbard-I approximation

Fourier transforming,

\langle\langle A;B\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\ \langle\langle A;B\rangle\rangle e^{i\omega t},

(34)

and solving Eqs. (

\langle\langle d_{\bm{k}\sigma};d_{\bm{k}\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\frac{1}{\omega-\Sigma(\bm{k},\omega)},

(35)

with the self-energy in Hubbard-I approximation,

\Sigma_{\rm I}(\bm{k},\omega)=\frac{U^{2}}{4\omega}\frac{{k^{2}}}{({k^{2}+k_{\star}^{2}})}.

(36)

The poles of the Green’s function in Eq. (

In our derivation, the dispersion of the Hubbard bands arises from the nonlocal orbital overlap,
\lambda_{ij}(i\neq j)

IV
Hubbard-III approximation

In this section, we evaluate the right-hand side of Eq. (
32
) to the next order in
s^{2}
by truncating the equations-of-motion hierarchy following Ref.
Hubbard 1964
, which we refer to as the Hubbard-III approximation. Unlike in the Hubbard model, this approximation becomes controlled in our case by the small parameter
s^{2}
.
For simplicity, we focus on the chiral-flat limit
M=0
in this section.

In line with the original Hubbard-III approximation
Hubbard 1964
, we separate the emerging correction into scattering and resonance broadening contributions. These arise from the two last terms on the right-hand side of Eq. (
32

, the contributions are additive in the self-energy and can therefore be considered separately.

IV.1
The scattering correction

The equation of motion for the Green’s function appearing in the second line of Eq. (

\begin{split}&i\partial_{t}\langle\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\delta n_{l\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle\\

&+U\sum_{r\neq l}\lambda_{lr}\langle\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\delta n_{l\bar{\sigma}}\delta n_{r\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle\\
&-\langle\langle\big[\tilde{H},\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\delta n_{l\bar{\sigma}}\big]\tilde{f}_{l\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle.\end{split}

(37)

Here,
j\neq l
and therefore
\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\delta n_{l\bar{\sigma}}\rangle\sim O(s^{4})

2
2

2

Because two fermionic operators at different sites have
O(s^{2})
overlap, we expect that the correlation between two density operators at different sites, each of which is composed of a pair of fermionic operators, should be
O(s^{4})
is negligible.
All remaining terms on the right-hand side of Eq. (
37
) are proportional to
U
and contain a larger number of the fermionic operators than the left-hand side. To truncate the chain of equations of motion, we first drop the third and fourth terms as their Green’s functions generically involve three different sites at the same time
t

3
3

3

The Green’s functions at
r=j

t
. However, this single case can be neglected due to its small weight in the summation
(\lambda_{lj}\sim O(s^{2}))
.

than the second term. Next, we may also factorize the second term in the same way as in Eq. (

Thus, Eq. (

(38)

The Green’s function on the right-hand side of the equation now has fewer fermionic operators. However, it remains distinct from those appearing in Eqs. (
31
) and (
32
). To close the equations, we write down the equation of motion for the Green’s function appearing in the right-hand side of Eq. (

At

Combining Eqs. (

\begin{split}\omega\langle\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}&-\sum_{r\neq j}t_{lr}^{\scriptscriptstyle U}(\omega)\langle\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\\
&\quad=\frac{\omega}{U}t_{lj}^{\scriptscriptstyle U}(\omega)\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\end{split}

(40)

with an interaction-induced hopping amplitude

t_{lj}^{\scriptscriptstyle U}(\omega)=\frac{U^{2}\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}^{2}\rangle}{\omega}\lambda_{lj}.

(41)

A method to solve this type of linear equation was presented in the original paper introducing the Hubbard-III approximation
Hubbard 1964
. For completeness, we provide the derivation in Appendix
B
. The solution of Eq. (

\displaystyle\langle\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\frac{\omega}{U}\sum_{r\neq j}W_{lr,j}t_{rj}^{\scriptscriptstyle U}\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.

(42)

Here, we introduce

\displaystyle W_{lr,j}(\omega)=g_{lr}(\omega)-\frac{g_{lj}(\omega)g_{jr}(\omega)}{g_{jj}(\omega)},

(43)

with the Green’s function
g_{jl}(\omega)=[\omega-\hat{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle U}(\omega)]_{jl}^{-1}
. Note that the Fourier transform of
g_{jl}
in momentum space corresponds to the Hubbard-I Green’s function

g(\bm{k},\omega)=\sum_{i}g_{ij}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}_{j}-\bm{R}_{i})}=\frac{1}{\omega-\frac{U^{2}}{4\omega}\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+k_{\star}^{2}}}.

(44)

Plugging Eq. (
42
) into the Fourier transform of Eq. (
38

\omega\langle\langle\psi_{j\bar{\sigma}};f_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\approx U\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}^{2}\rangle\langle\langle f_{j\bar{\sigma}};f_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega},

valid within the accuracy of Hubbard-III approximation we obtain

\langle\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\delta n_{l\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\frac{\omega}{U}\sum_{r\neq j}W_{lr,j}t_{rj}^{\scriptscriptstyle U}\langle\langle\psi_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.

(45)

This expression allows us to compute the scattering correction in Eq. (

\begin{split}&U\sum_{l\neq j}\lambda_{jl}\langle\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\delta n_{l\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=c(\omega)\omega\langle\langle\psi_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.\end{split}

(46)

The coefficient

\displaystyle c(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\bm{k}}g(\bm{k},\omega)\bar{\lambda}(\bm{k})}{\sum_{\bm{k}}g(\bm{k},\omega)}

\displaystyle\approx-s^{2}\ln{\Big(1-\frac{U^{2}}{4\omega^{2}}\Big)},

(47b)

where
\bar{\lambda}(\bm{k})=\sum_{i\neq j}\lambda_{ij}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}_{i}-\bm{R}_{j})}
denotes the Fourier transform of the intersite orbital overlap
\lambda_{ij}(i\neq j)
. The last equation is derived by expanding Eq. (
47a

s^{2}
. The complex logarithmic function has a branch cut along the negative real axis. Its imaginary part ultimately contributes to a finite relaxation rate.

IV.2
The resonance broadening correction

The resonance broadening correction arises from the last term on the right-hand side of Eq. (

\begin{split}&\langle\langle[\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}},\tilde{H}]\tilde{f}_{l\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle\\

Similar to the evaluation of the scattering correction, we need to write the equations of motion for the correlators appearing here, and then truncate the chain of equations by factorizing the higher-order correlation functions appearing in those equations.

In evaluating the Green’s functions on the right-hand side of Eq. (

VI
Conclusion and Discussion

In this work, we studied the electron spectra of the paramagnetic Mott state in charge neutral magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene (MATBG). Despite the topological obstruction to constructing exponentially localized symmetric Wannier orbitals from MATBG flat bands, we show that the dominant electron-electron interactions within the flat bands can be described by an on-site Hubbard interaction in a nonorthogonal basis. The intersite orbital overlap is set by the fraction
s^{2}
of the moiré Brillouin zone over which
f
- and
c
-electrons strongly hybridize. Treating
s^{2}
as a small parameter, we compute the electron self-energy to the first order in
s^{2}
using the equation of motion method, and obtain concrete predictions for the electron spectral function. These can be probed by measurements with a quantum twisting microscope, which allow for spectroscopy with a high resolution of energy and momentum
Inbar et al. 2023
;
Xiao et al. 2025
;
Wei et al. 2025a
;
Wei et al. 2025b
.

Our approach is adapted from the Hubbard-III approximation for the Hubbard model. We highlight the similarities and differences in the resulting self-energy. In both cases, the Hubbard-III self-energy is diagonal in the site basis,
\Sigma_{ij}(\omega)\propto\delta_{ij}
, but the nonorthogonality of the orbitals renders the self-energy momentum dependent in our case. Moreover, we find well-defined quasiparticles with relaxation rate much smaller than the bandwidth (
\sim U
) near the
\Gamma
point where Berry curvature concentrates. This behavior contrasts sharply with the conventional strong-coupling Hubbard model (hopping
t\ll U
), where the bandwidth
W\sim t
and the quasiparticle broadening
\sim t^{2}/W\sim W
are of the same order.

While the first-order expansion in
s^{2}
captures qualitative features of the electron spectra, higher-order corrections are expected to be important in several respects. First, the self-energy in Eq. (
71
) exhibits a logarithmic divergence at
\omega=0,\pm M,\pm U/2
, signaling the breakdown of the first-order expansion near the band edges.
Second, while the experimental value of
s^{2}
remains difficult to determine at present, some theoretical estimates such as
s\sim 0.25
in Ref.
Ledwith et al. 2025a
suggest
(N+1)s^{2}\sim 1
. This suggests that higher-order corrections can be quantitatively significant in real MATBG samples. Moreover, our theory is restricted to the paramagnetic Mott state, which exists within a finite temperature window
s^{2}U\ll k_{B}T\ll U

Ledwith et al. 2025a
;
Hu et al. 2025
. At lower temperatures, nonlocal exchange interactions can drive flavor polarization
Song and Bernevig 2022
;
Xie and MacDonald 2020
;
Bultinck et al. 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Lian et al. 2021
, accompanied by strong nonlocal collective fluctuations beyond the scope of Hubbard-III approximation. Extending the present framework to incorporate these effects remains an important open direction. The modified Hubbard model formulated here can serve as a useful starting point for more advanced approaches, such as dynamical mean-field theory and its cluster extensions
Georges et al. 1996
.

We have focused on the dominant on-site repulsion among
f
-electrons. Additional interactions, including those between
c
-and
f
-electrons and among
c
-electrons, can further reduce the quasiparticle lifetime. However, due to the low density of states in the Hubbard-I/III spectra near the
\Gamma
point, these residual interaction effects are expected to be weak in this region of the moiré Brillouin zone, as proposed in Ref.
Hu et al. 2026
.

Although our analysis focuses on half filling of the flat bands (
\nu=0
), the method can be extended to other integer fillings. It will also be interesting to compute thermodynamic and transport properties in future work
5
5

5

P. Nosov, E. Khalaf, and P.J. Ledwith (2026), to appear
.
More broadly, our approach may be applicable to other topological flat-band systems that admit a heavy-fermion description
Liu et al. 2026
;
Xie et al. 2025
, providing a route to understand correlation effects in flat bands with nontrivial quantum geometry.

Appendix A
Hubbard-I Green’s function

In this section, we evaluate the Hubbard-I approximation for the electron Green’s functions at charge neutrality, and then use them to compute equal-time correlation functions. We first write the equations of motion, Eqs. (
62
) and (

\displaystyle\omega\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\rho}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{ji}\delta_{\sigma,\rho}+\sum_{l}\lambda_{jl}\langle\langle\psi_{l\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\rho}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j}\sum_{\mu}t_{ji}^{\sigma\mu}\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{i\mu};\tilde{f}_{i\rho}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega},

(73)

\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\rho}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\int_{\bm{k}}g(\bm{k},\omega)_{\sigma,\rho}\hat{\lambda}(\bm{k})e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}_{j}-\bm{R}_{i})},

(75a)

(75b)

where

(76)

Here,
\omega_{+}=\omega+i0^{+}

\hat{\lambda}(\bm{k})=\sum_{i}\lambda_{ij}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}_{i}-\bm{R}_{j})}=\frac{1}{z}\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+k_{\star}^{2}}.

(77)

According to the equilibrium KMS condition, the equal-time correlation function of two operators
A

B

75a

76

(79)

Appendix B
W_{jl,i}

In this section, we discuss the solution of a class of linear equations that frequently arise in the Hubbard-III approximation. These equations take the following form,

\omega{\bm{G}}_{lj}(\omega)=\sum_{r\neq j}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{lr}(\omega){\bm{G}}_{rj}(\omega)+{\bm{S}}_{lj}(\omega),\quad l\neq j,

(82)

where
{\bm{G}}_{lj}
denotes a Green’s function. Note that
{\bm{G}}
can in general depend on multiple real-space coordinates, and we suppress those that are irrelevant for this discussion. The bold symbol indicates that the Green’s functions are allowed to be a vector with multiple components (flavors).
This equation effectively describes a particle hopping on a lattice that contains a single impurity at site
j
but is otherwise uniform.
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{lr}
represents a translationally invariant ‘hopping’ matrix. The impurity hybridizes with the particle and generates a source term
{\bm{S}}(\omega)
in the equation.

Because this equation does not involve
\hat{\bm{G}}_{jj}
and
{\bm{S}}_{jj}
, we can set
\hat{\bm{G}}_{jj}={\bm{S}}_{jj}=0
at the moment and rewrite the equation as

\begin{split}\omega{\bm{G}}_{lj}(\omega)=&\sum_{r}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{lr}(\omega){\bm{G}}_{rj}(\omega)+{\bm{S}}_{lj}(\omega)\\
&-\delta_{lj}\sum_{r}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{jr}(\omega){\bm{G}}_{rj}(\omega),\ \ \ \forall l.\end{split}

(83)

For
l\neq j
, this equation is equivalent to Eq. (
82
), while for
l=j
, both sides of the equation vanish.
Therefore,

{\bm{G}}_{lj}(\omega)=\sum_{r}\hat{g}_{lr}(\omega){\bm{S}}_{rj}(\omega)-\hat{g}_{lj}(\omega)\sum_{r}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{jr}(\omega){\bm{G}}_{rj}(\omega),

(84)

with

\hat{g}_{lj}(\omega)=\big[\omega\hat{\mathcal{I}}-\hat{\mathcal{H}}(\omega)\big]_{lj}^{-1}.

(85)

This leads to the self-consistent equation

\displaystyle\bm{C}(\omega)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{r}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{jr}(\omega){\bm{G}}_{rj}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{lr}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{jl}(\omega)\hat{g}_{lr}(\omega){\bm{S}}_{rj}(\omega)-\sum_{l}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{jl}(\omega)\hat{g}_{lj}(\omega)\bm{C}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\hat{g}_{jj}(\omega)^{-1}\sum_{l}\hat{g}_{jl}(\omega)S_{lj}(\omega).

(86)

To arrive at the last equation, we have used
\sum_{l}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{jl}(\omega)\hat{g}_{lr}(\omega)=\omega\hat{g}_{jr}(\omega)-\delta_{jr}
and
S_{jj}=0
. Plugging Eq. (
86
) into Eq. (
84
), we obtain the solution to Eq. (
82
), the main result of this section,

\displaystyle{\bm{G}}_{lj}(\omega)=\sum_{r\neq j}\hat{W}_{lr,j}(\omega){\bm{S}}_{rj}(\omega),

(87)

\displaystyle\hat{W}_{lr,j}(\omega)=\hat{g}_{jl}(\omega)-\hat{g}_{lj}(\omega)\hat{g}_{jj}(\omega)^{-1}\hat{g}_{jr}(\omega).

(88)

If
\hat{g}(\omega)
is proportional to an identity matrix in flavor space, Eqs. (
88
) and (
86
) reduce to Eqs. (
43
) and (
47a
) in the main text, respectively.

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-U\langle\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle+U\langle\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

(112)

On the right-hand side of the equation, we retain only Green’s functions that contain fermionic operators on at most two different sites. This means that (i) we drop the commutators
[\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}},\tilde{H}]
and

[\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}},\tilde{H}]
, we need to keep both the onsite scattering term and the term that transfers electrons at site

to site

. They correspond to the last two terms on the right-hand side. All the dropped terms give rise to corrections of higher order in
s^{2}
.

The right-hand side of Eq. (
D.1
) can be further simplified by combining the second and third terms using

\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma}+\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma}=2\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma}.

(113)

After some algebra, we arrive at

\begin{split}&i\partial_{t}\langle\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle\\
\approx&-\frac{U}{4}\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle\\

(114)

The last term arises from factorizing the Green’s function in the last line of Eq. (
D.1
),

\begin{split}&\langle\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}f_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}f_{l\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle\\
\approx&\ \langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\langle f_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}f_{l\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle.\end{split}

Because the flavor moment in the Mott state is thermally disorder, the following relation holds

\displaystyle\langle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{N-2}{N-1}\langle\langle\psi_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle.

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{U}{4}\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{r\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle+2U\langle\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{3}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle-U\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\langle\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma},\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle+U\lambda_{jl}\langle\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}f_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}f_{l\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle-U\sum_{r\neq l}\lambda_{rl}\langle\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\delta n_{r\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle+U\sum_{r\neq j,l}\lambda_{jr}\langle\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\delta n_{r\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle+U\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{jr}\langle\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\delta n_{r\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle-U\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{jr}\langle\langle\delta n_{r\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma},\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle.

(117)

We kept only the terms generated by the commutators
[\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger},\tilde{H}],[\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}},\tilde{H}]
, and
[\tilde{f}_{j\sigma},\tilde{H}]
. The summations in the last four lines exclude terms at
r=j
or
l
. Those excluded terms are combined into the first four lines on the right-hand side of the equation. The fifth and sixth lines on the right-hand side generically involve three different sites and are of higher order in
s^{2}
. Moreover, the last two lines cancel to the desired accuracy due to the factorization for
r\neq j,l
,

\begin{split}&\langle\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\delta n_{r\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle\\
\approx&\ \langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\langle\langle\delta n_{r\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle.\end{split}

(118)

Next, we simplify the first four lines on the right-hand side of Eq. (
117
) by truncating the correlation functions using Eq. (
102
) and the following factorizations:

\begin{split}&\langle\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}f_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}f_{l\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle\\
\approx&\ \langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\langle f_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}f_{l\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle,\end{split}

(119)

and

\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\approx\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle,

(120)

with

\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{N-2}{N-1}.

(121)

Eq. (
117
) reduces to

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\langle\langle\big(\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle^{c}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{U}{4}\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{r\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle+2U\langle\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle-U\langle\psi_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\langle\langle\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma},\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{U}{2}\lambda_{jl}\langle\delta n_{l\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle.

(122)

We then Fourier transform Eq. (
122
) to frequency space and, using the definitions in Eq. (
103d
), recast it as the equation of motion for
F^{1,2}
, Eq. (
104b
).

D.3
F^{0,1}

Here, we compute the equation of motion for
F^{0,1}
. First,

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle^{c}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\ 2U\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle-U\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\rangle\langle\langle\psi_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle-U\lambda_{jl}\langle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle-U\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{rl}\langle\langle\delta n_{r\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle+U\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{jr}\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\delta n_{r\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle.

(123)

We have separated the terms at
r=j
from the summations. The equation of motion generates higher-order Green’s functions on the right-hand side of Eq. (
123
), which we analyze in turn.

The first term can be expressed in terms of
F^{0,2}(\omega)
using the relation

\begin{split}&\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\\
=&\ F_{lj,i}^{0,2}(\omega)+\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega},\end{split}

(124)

where we applied Eq. (
121
).

The third term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
123
) can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle\langle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\delta n_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\langle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\delta n_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle-\frac{1}{4}\langle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle,

(125)

with
\delta n_{i\sigma}=f_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{i\sigma}-1/2
. Because

\displaystyle\delta n_{i\bar{\sigma}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\delta n_{i\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}+\delta n_{i\sigma})^{2}=\frac{1}{4}+\delta n_{i\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}+2\delta n_{i\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\delta n_{i\sigma},

(126)

and the substitution
\delta n_{i\bar{\sigma}}^{2}\rightarrow 1/4
in the Green’s functions is accurate up to
O(s^{2})
corrections,
we can make the following substitution in Eq. (
D.3
),

\langle\langle\delta n_{i\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\delta n_{i\sigma}...,\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle\approx-\frac{1}{2}\langle\langle\delta n_{i\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}...,\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle.

(127)

Together with the approximation Eq. (
102
), we find that

\displaystyle\langle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\ \frac{3}{4}\Big(\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle-\frac{N-2}{N-1}\langle\langle\psi_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle\Big).

(128)

Here we used Eq. (
116
) for flavor-disordered Mott states.

The fourth term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
123
) can be rewritten in terms of
F^{1,1}
via the relation

\displaystyle\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{rl}\langle\langle\delta n_{r\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{rl}F_{rj,i}^{1,1}(\omega)+\frac{N-2}{N-1}\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{rl}\langle\psi_{r\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\langle\psi_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.

(129)

In the last line of Eq. (
123
), we factorize the Green’s function and obtain

\begin{split}&\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{jr}\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\delta n_{r\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\\
\approx&\ \frac{N-2}{N-1}\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{jr}\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\psi_{r\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\langle\psi_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.\end{split}

(130)

Next, we plug Eqs. (
124
), (
127
), (
D.3
) and (
131
) into the frequency-space Fourier transform of Eq. (
123
).
In the resulting expression, the last term of Eq. (
D.3
) and the right-hand side of Eq. (
130
) can be combined via the following relation,

\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{jr}\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\psi_{r\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle-\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{rl}\langle\psi_{r\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\approx\frac{1}{4}(\xi_{jl}-\lambda_{jl}),

(131)

where we used
\langle\psi_{j\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle=\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\psi_{r\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle=-\xi_{rj}/4
[see Eq. (
80
)] and the approximation
\xi_{jj}=1+O(s^{2})
. Furthermore, we can make the substitution
\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\approx 4\omega\langle\langle\psi_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}/U
. After some algebra, we find that Eq. (
123
) becomes

\begin{split}&\omega\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}^{c}\\
=&-U\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{rl}F_{rj,i}^{1,1}(\omega)+2UF_{lj,i}^{0,2}(\omega)\\
&+\frac{N-2}{N-1}\big[\frac{\lambda_{lj}+\xi_{lj}}{2}U-\frac{\xi_{lj}}{4}\omega\big]\langle\langle\psi_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.\end{split}

(132)

Finally, using the definition of
F^{0,1}
Eq. (
103b
), we obtain the equation of motion for
F^{0,1}
, Eq. (
104c
), from Eq. (
132
).

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle^{c}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 2U\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{3}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle-U\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\delta n_{j\bar{\sigma}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle-U\lambda_{jl}\langle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle-U\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{rl}\langle\langle\delta n_{r\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle+U\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{jr}\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{r\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle.

(133)

We now analyze the higher-order Green’s function on the right-hand side.

The first term can be reduced to a lower-order Green’s function using the truncation Eq. (
102
). The result can be rewritten in terms of
F^{1,1}
as follows,

\begin{split}&\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{3}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\\
=&\ F_{lj,i}^{0,1}(\omega)+\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\psi_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\\
&+\frac{N-2}{N-1}\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\langle\psi_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.\end{split}

(134)

The third line of Eq. (
133
) can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle\langle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\ \frac{3}{4}\Big(\frac{N-2}{N-1}\langle\langle\psi_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle-\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle\Big).

(135)

The fourth term can be rewritten in terms of
F^{1,2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{rl}\langle\langle\delta n_{r\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{rl}F_{rj,i}^{1,2}+\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{jr}\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\psi_{r\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.

(136)

In the last line of Eq. (
133
), we factorize the Green’s function and obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{jr}\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{r\sigma^{\prime}}\delta n_{r\overline{\sigma^{\prime}}}\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r\neq j}\lambda_{jr}\langle\tilde{f}_{l\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\psi_{r\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\delta n_{j\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}^{2}\rangle\langle\langle\tilde{f}_{j\sigma};\tilde{f}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}

(137)

Plugging Eqs. (
134
)- (
D.4
) and using the definition Eq. (
103b
), we arrive at the equation of motion for
F^{0,2}
, Eq. (
104d
)
