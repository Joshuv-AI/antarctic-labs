Holographic Open/Closed Exchange in Double Deeply Virtual Compton Scattering: Fixed– j j Structural Matching to the ± \pm -Basis Wilson Coefficients
Kiminad A. Mamo

Abstract
We show that, in the collinear regime, the fixed– j j holographic double deeply virtual Compton scattering (DDVCS) amplitude contains the same hypergeometric hard kernel as the ± \pm -basis Wilson coefficients of perturbative QCD. Starting from the t t –channel Witten diagram, we derive the closed-string fixed– j j amplitude and obtain the even-spin open-string channel by a parallel replacement rule. After holographic collinear factorization, the upper photon vertex is universal and model independent: in the conformal limit it depends only on the pure-AdS bulk wave functions of the two virtual photons and yields an exact Gauss hypergeometric function of η 2 / ξ 2 \eta^{2}/\xi^{2} . The Mellin exponent δ X ​ ( j ) = j + Δ X ​ ( j ) − 2 = 2 ​ j + γ X ​ ( j ) \delta_{X}(j)=j+\Delta_{X}(j)-2=2j+\gamma_{X}(j) is fixed by Witten-diagram z z -power counting, while all infrared model dependence is isolated in lower hadronic conformal moments. Comparing with the singlet vector Compton form factor in the conformal operator product expansion, we find that at a single matching scale Q = μ = μ 0 = μ ∗ Q=\mu=\mu_{0}=\mu_{\ast} the open channel matches the ( + ) (+) eigenchannel and the closed channel matches the protected ( − ) (-) eigenchannel. The sharpest anchor is the first physical even moment j = 2 j=2 , together with the distinct j − 1 \sqrt{j-1} and j − 2 \sqrt{j-2} branch-point structure of the open and closed trajectories. Logarithmic running deforms only the scale dependence, not the channel dictionary. The result is a fixed– j j , fixed-scale structural matching statement for holographic DDVCS/DVCS, not a claim of all-scale equality or a global fit.

V
Physical amplitude, operator meaning, and phenomenological scope

Having established the fixed–
j
dictionary, we now return to the physical amplitude and to the question of what this structure means beyond the formal matching itself.

The fixed–
j

ddvcs
​ Compton form factor. The reggeized amplitude is obtained by the even-signature Sommerfeld–Watson transform,

\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(\bar{s},\bar{t},\chi;Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})=\sum_{X=o,c}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{X}}\frac{\mathrm{d}j}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\,\xi_{+}(j)\,\widehat{\mathcal{H}}^{(X)}_{\rm holo}(j),\qquad\xi_{+}(j)=\frac{1+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\pi j}}{\sin\pi j},

with only even spins contributing in the sector considered here.

After holographic collinear factorization the operator interpretation is immediate. The upper vertex is the universal three-point function of two electromagnetic currents and a normalized spin–
j

\text{upper vertex}\sim\langle J^{\mu}J^{\nu}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}^{(j)}_{X}\rangle,\qquad\text{lower vertex}\sim\langle P^{\prime}|\mathcal{O}_{X}^{(j)}|P\rangle,\qquad X=o,c.

(34)

The universal hypergeometric function is therefore the holographic analogue of the perturbative Wilson coefficient, whereas the bottom moments
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{N}^{(X)}
are the holographic analogue of the nonperturbative conformal moments.

This is the sense in which the paper is conceptual rather than numerical. It does not offer a new global fit ansatz for cross sections. What it buys phenomenologically is a controlled operator interpretation. Once the universal kernel is fixed, the nonperturbative information resides in conformal moments that may be modeled, extracted, or compared across frameworks, including lattice-QCD determinations of local moments and form factors. This is also the sense in which the present analysis bridges the high-energy Regge picture and the collinear-factorization picture: the former supplies the reggeized continuation, while the latter fixes the operator language in which the fixed–
j
amplitude is interpreted.

The role of the
j=2
anchor should be read in exactly this way. We do not claim that the full reggeized amplitude is exhausted by the first even moment. We claim something more precise and more robust: the first physical even moment fixes the protected versus unprotected channel assignment, and therefore fixes the operator dictionary that organizes the full fixed–
j
amplitude. Once that dictionary is established, the Sommerfeld–Watson reconstruction in Eq. (
33

The closed channel is the rigorous starting point. form a row vector, the two basis changes are

\begin{pmatrix}H_{j}^{+}(\eta,t;\mu)\\[2.15277pt]
H_{j}^{-}(\eta,t;\mu)\end{pmatrix}=U_{j}\begin{pmatrix}H_{j}^{\Sigma}(\eta,t;\mu)\\[2.15277pt]
H_{j}^{g}(\eta,t;\mu)\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bigl(C_{j}^{\Sigma},C_{j}^{g}\bigr)=\bigl(C_{j}^{+},C_{j}^{-}\bigr)\,U_{j}.

(110)

Thus

\displaystyle C_{j}^{\Sigma}\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi},\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}};\alpha_{s}(\mu)\right)

\displaystyle=C_{j}^{+}\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi},\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}};\alpha_{s}(\mu)\right)+b_{j}\,C_{j}^{-}\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi},\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}};\alpha_{s}(\mu)\right),

(111a)

\displaystyle C_{j}^{g}\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi},\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}};\alpha_{s}(\mu)\right)

\displaystyle=a_{j}\,C_{j}^{+}\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi},\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}};\alpha_{s}(\mu)\right)+C_{j}^{-}\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi},\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}};\alpha_{s}(\mu)\right).

(111b)

Therefore the fixed–
j
singlet amplitude can be written equivalently as

\widehat{\mathcal{H}}^{\rm sing}_{\rm pQCD}(j;\xi,\eta,t,Q^{2};\mu)=\xi^{-j}\,C_{j}^{\Sigma}\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi},\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}};\alpha_{s}(\mu)\right)H_{j}^{\Sigma}(\eta,t;\mu)+\xi^{-j}\,C_{j}^{g}\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi},\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}};\alpha_{s}(\mu)\right)H_{j}^{g}(\eta,t;\mu).

(112)

Hence the singlet vector Compton form factor in the quark–gluon basis is

\mathcal{H}_{\rm pQCD}^{\rm sing}(\xi,\eta,t,Q^{2};\mu)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}[1+(-1)^{j}]\,\xi^{-j}\left[C_{j}^{\Sigma}\,H_{j}^{\Sigma}+C_{j}^{g}\,H_{j}^{g}\right].

(113)

If one wishes to express the result directly in terms of the quark–gluon input moments at

\mu_{0}
, one may first evolve in the diagonal basis and only then rotate back. Defining

E_{j}^{(\pm)}(\mu,\mu_{0})=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+}(\mu,\mu_{0})&0\\[2.15277pt]
0&\mathcal{E}_{j}^{-}(\mu,\mu_{0})\end{pmatrix},

(114)

the effective quark–gluon-basis coefficient row vector is

\bigl(C_{j,\rm eff}^{\Sigma},C_{j,\rm eff}^{g}\bigr)=\bigl(C_{j}^{+}\,\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+},C_{j}^{-}\,\mathcal{E}_{j}^{-}\bigr)\,U_{j}.

(115)

Explicitly,

\displaystyle C_{j,\rm eff}^{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+}(\mu,\mu_{0})\,C_{j}^{+}\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi},\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}};\alpha_{s}(\mu)\right)+b_{j}\,\mathcal{E}_{j}^{-}(\mu,\mu_{0})\,C_{j}^{-}\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi},\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}};\alpha_{s}(\mu)\right),

(116a)

\displaystyle C_{j,\rm eff}^{g}

\displaystyle=a_{j}\,\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+}(\mu,\mu_{0})\,C_{j}^{+}\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi},\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}};\alpha_{s}(\mu)\right)+\mathcal{E}_{j}^{-}(\mu,\mu_{0})\,C_{j}^{-}\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi},\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}};\alpha_{s}(\mu)\right).

(116b)

Thus the full singlet vector CFF may also be written directly in terms of the input quark and
gluon conformal moments at
\mu_{0}
as

\mathcal{H}_{\rm pQCD}^{\rm sing}(\xi,\eta,t,Q^{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}[1+(-1)^{j}]\,\xi^{-j}\left[C_{j,\rm eff}^{\Sigma}\,H_{j}^{\Sigma}(\eta,t;\mu_{0})+C_{j,\rm eff}^{g}\,H_{j}^{g}(\eta,t;\mu_{0})\right].

(117)

This form makes the relation between the diagonal
\pm
representation and the physical
quark–gluon basis completely explicit.

B.5
Fixing the normalization constants
c_{j}^{\pm}
by matching to the forward amplitude

The conformal-symmetry prediction fixes the
functional
dependence of the nonforward
Wilson coefficients on
\eta/\xi
, but it does not by itself fix the overall normalization
constants
c_{j}^{\pm}
. These constants are fixed by requiring that the nonforward coefficient
functions reduce, in the forward limit
\eta\to 0
, to the standard forward DIS Wilson
coefficients.

Indeed, since

{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{j}{2}+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{\pm}}{4},\frac{j+1}{2}+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{\pm}}{4};j+\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{\pm}}{2};\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi^{2}}\right)\;\xrightarrow[\eta\to 0]{}\;1,

(118)

the nonforward coefficients reduce to

C_{j}^{\pm}\!\left(0,\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}};\alpha_{s}\right)=c_{j}^{\pm}(\alpha_{s})\times\Bigl[\text{the corresponding scale factor}\Bigr].

(119)

Hence the constants
c_{j}^{\pm}
are precisely the forward Wilson-coefficient normalizations
written in the
\pm
basis.

Let

\bm{c}_{j}^{(\Sigma g)}(\alpha_{s})\equiv\bigl(c_{j}^{\Sigma}(\alpha_{s}),\,c_{j}^{g}(\alpha_{s})\bigr)

(120)

denote the forward Wilson-coefficient row vector in the singlet quark–gluon basis. Since the
Wilson coefficients transform as row vectors, while the conformal moments transform as column
vectors, the basis change is

\bm{c}_{j}^{(\Sigma g)}(\alpha_{s})=\bm{c}_{j}^{(\pm)}(\alpha_{s})\,U_{j},\qquad\bm{c}_{j}^{(\pm)}(\alpha_{s})\equiv\bigl(c_{j}^{+}(\alpha_{s}),\,c_{j}^{-}(\alpha_{s})\bigr),

(121)

with
U_{j}
given in (
95
). Therefore

\bm{c}_{j}^{(\pm)}(\alpha_{s})=\bm{c}_{j}^{(\Sigma g)}(\alpha_{s})\,U_{j}^{-1}.

(122)

Using

U_{j}^{-1}=\frac{1}{D_{j}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-a_{j}\\[4.30554pt]
-b_{j}&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad D_{j}=1-a_{j}b_{j},

(123)

one obtains the general matching formulas

\displaystyle c_{j}^{+}(\alpha_{s})

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{j}^{\Sigma}(\alpha_{s})-b_{j}\,c_{j}^{g}(\alpha_{s})}{D_{j}},

(124a)

\displaystyle c_{j}^{-}(\alpha_{s})

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{j}^{g}(\alpha_{s})-a_{j}\,c_{j}^{\Sigma}(\alpha_{s})}{D_{j}}.

(124b)

These equations are completely general in the present normalization convention. In particular,
they show that once the forward DIS coefficients are known in the quark–gluon basis, the
constants
c_{j}^{\pm}
are fixed uniquely.

At LO, the virtual photon couples directly only to the singlet quark operator, so the forward
quark–gluon coefficient row vector is simply

\bm{c}_{j}^{(\Sigma g),(0)}=\bigl(c_{j}^{\Sigma,(0)},\,c_{j}^{g,(0)}\bigr)=(1,0).

(125)

Therefore the LO normalization constants in the
\pm
basis are

\displaystyle c_{j}^{+,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D_{j}},

(126a)

\displaystyle c_{j}^{-,(0)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{a_{j}}{D_{j}}.

(126b)

Using the definitions of
a_{j}
,
b_{j}
, and
D_{j}
, these may be written in the equivalent
exact forms

\displaystyle c_{j}^{+,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma\Sigma,(0)}-\gamma_{j}^{-,(0)}}{\gamma_{j}^{+,(0)}-\gamma_{j}^{-,(0)}}=\frac{\gamma_{j}^{+,(0)}-\gamma_{j}^{gg,(0)}}{\gamma_{j}^{+,(0)}-\gamma_{j}^{-,(0)}},

(127a)

\displaystyle c_{j}^{-,(0)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma g,(0)}}{\gamma_{j}^{+,(0)}-\gamma_{j}^{-,(0)}}.

(127b)

Thus, within the normalization convention of (
95
), the LO coefficients

c_{j}^{\pm}
are
not
equal to
1
and
0
; rather, they are the entries of the forward
quark coefficient
(1,0)
rewritten in the
\pm
basis.

Consequently, the strict tree-level nonforward Wilson coefficients are

C_{j}^{\pm,\mathrm{tree}}\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi}\right)=c_{j}^{\pm,(0)}{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{j}{2},\frac{j+1}{2};j+\frac{3}{2};\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi^{2}}\right).

(128)

If one wishes instead to keep the LO anomalous-dimension improvement at a conformal fixed point
or with LO running coupling, the same normalization constants
c_{j}^{\pm,(0)}
multiply the
corresponding scale-dependent factors appearing in
(
102
) and (
107
).

Finally, we stress that these explicit values of
c_{j}^{\pm}
depend on the normalization convention
chosen for the
\pm
eigenvectors. A different normalization of the eigenvectors rescales

c_{j}^{\pm}
and
H_{j}^{\pm}
inversely, leaving the physical products
C_{j}^{\pm}H_{j}^{\pm}
and hence
the Compton form factor unchanged.

B.6
Large–
N_{c}
limit of the normalization constants
c_{j}^{\pm}

It is useful to make the large–
N_{c}
behavior of the LO normalization constants

c_{j}^{\pm,(0)}
completely explicit. Starting from
Eq. (
127
), one may rewrite the exact LO coefficients as

\displaystyle c_{j}^{+,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\frac{\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma\Sigma,(0)}-\gamma_{j}^{gg,(0)}}{\sqrt{\left(\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma\Sigma,(0)}-\gamma_{j}^{gg,(0)}\right)^{2}+4\,\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma g,(0)}\gamma_{j}^{g\Sigma,(0)}}}\right],

(129a)

\displaystyle c_{j}^{-,(0)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma g,(0)}}{\sqrt{\left(\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma\Sigma,(0)}-\gamma_{j}^{gg,(0)}\right)^{2}+4\,\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma g,(0)}\gamma_{j}^{g\Sigma,(0)}}}.

(129b)

We now take the standard ’t Hooft large–
N_{c}
limit,

N_{c}\to\infty,\qquad\lambda\equiv g^{2}N_{c}\ \text{fixed},\qquad n_{f}\ \text{fixed}.

(130)

Then

C_{F}=\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{2N_{c}}=\frac{N_{c}}{2}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}}\right),\qquad C_{A}=N_{c},\qquad\beta_{0}=\frac{11}{3}N_{c}-\frac{2}{3}n_{f}.

(131)

Hence the LO anomalous dimensions scale as

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma\Sigma,(0)}

\displaystyle=N_{c}\,\widehat{\gamma}_{j}^{\,q}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}}\right),\qquad\widehat{\gamma}_{j}^{\,q}=2S_{1}(j)-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{j(j+1)},

(132a)

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}^{gg,(0)}

\displaystyle=N_{c}\,\widehat{\gamma}_{j}^{\,g}+\mathcal{O}(N_{c}^{0}),\qquad\widehat{\gamma}_{j}^{\,g}=4S_{1}(j)-\frac{11}{3}+\frac{4}{j(j+1)}-\frac{12}{(j-1)(j+2)},

(132b)

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma g,(0)}

\displaystyle=-2n_{f}\,r_{j}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}}\right),\qquad r_{j}\equiv\frac{j^{2}+j+2}{j(j+1)(j+2)},

(132c)

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}^{g\Sigma,(0)}

\displaystyle=-N_{c}\,s_{j}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}}\right),\qquad s_{j}\equiv\frac{j^{2}+j+2}{(j-1)j(j+1)}.

(132d)

It is convenient to introduce

\Omega_{j}\equiv\widehat{\gamma}_{j}^{\,q}-\widehat{\gamma}_{j}^{\,g}=\frac{13}{6}-2S_{1}(j)-\frac{5}{j(j+1)}+\frac{12}{(j-1)(j+2)},

(133)

and

R_{j}\equiv r_{j}s_{j}=\frac{(j^{2}+j+2)^{2}}{(j-1)j^{2}(j+1)^{2}(j+2)}.

(134)

Then the discriminant in (
129
) becomes

\displaystyle\left(\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma\Sigma,(0)}-\gamma_{j}^{gg,(0)}\right)^{2}+4\,\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma g,(0)}\gamma_{j}^{g\Sigma,(0)}

\displaystyle=N_{c}^{2}\,\Omega_{j}^{2}+8n_{f}N_{c}\,R_{j}+\mathcal{O}(N_{c}^{0}),

(135)

and therefore

\sqrt{\left(\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma\Sigma,(0)}-\gamma_{j}^{gg,(0)}\right)^{2}+4\,\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma g,(0)}\gamma_{j}^{g\Sigma,(0)}}=N_{c}|\Omega_{j}|\left[1+\frac{4n_{f}R_{j}}{N_{c}\,\Omega_{j}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\right)\right].

(136)

Substituting (
136
) into
(
129
) yields the large–
N_{c}
expansion

\displaystyle c_{j}^{+,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[1+\operatorname{sgn}(\Omega_{j})\Bigr]-\operatorname{sgn}(\Omega_{j})\,\frac{2n_{f}R_{j}}{N_{c}\,\Omega_{j}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\right),

(137a)

\displaystyle c_{j}^{-,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2n_{f}}{N_{c}}\,\frac{r_{j}}{|\Omega_{j}|}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\right).

(137b)

Equivalently, in piecewise form,

\displaystyle\Omega_{j}>0:\qquad c_{j}^{+,(0)}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{2n_{f}R_{j}}{N_{c}\,\Omega_{j}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle c_{j}^{-,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2n_{f}}{N_{c}}\frac{r_{j}}{\Omega_{j}}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\right),

(138a)

\displaystyle\Omega_{j}<0:\qquad c_{j}^{+,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2n_{f}R_{j}}{N_{c}\,\Omega_{j}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle c_{j}^{-,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2n_{f}}{N_{c}}\frac{r_{j}}{|\Omega_{j}|}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\right).

(138b)

For the even-
j
vector sector relevant here, one finds

\Omega_{2}=\frac{4}{3}>0,\qquad\Omega_{j}<0\quad\text{for all even }j\geq 4.

(139)

Thus the lowest nontrivial moment is special:

\displaystyle c_{2}^{+,(0)}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{n_{f}}{2N_{c}}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\right),

(140a)

\displaystyle c_{2}^{-,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{f}}{2N_{c}}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\right).

(140b)

By contrast, for even
j\geq 4
one has

\displaystyle c_{j}^{+,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2n_{f}R_{j}}{N_{c}\,\Omega_{j}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\right),

(141a)

\displaystyle c_{j}^{-,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2n_{f}}{N_{c}}\,\frac{r_{j}}{|\Omega_{j}|}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\right).

(141b)

Finally, one should keep in mind that in the normalization convention adopted in
(
95
), the labels
+
and
-
do not coincide uniformly with a purely
quark-like or gluon-like channel at large
N_{c}
. In particular, when
\Omega_{j}<0
the entry

b_{j}=\frac{\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma\Sigma,(0)}-\gamma_{j}^{+,(0)}}{\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma g,(0)}}\sim N_{c}

(142)

is large, so the eigenmoment
H_{j}^{-}
carries an
\mathcal{O}(N_{c})
quark admixture. Therefore the
physically relevant quantities are the products
c_{j}^{\pm}H_{j}^{\pm}
, which remain finite and are
independent of the normalization convention chosen for the
\pm
eigenvectors.

B.7
Expansion of
\gamma_{j}^{\pm,(0)}
near
j=2

It is useful to have the LO singlet eigen-anomalous dimensions expanded analytically around the
first nontrivial even spin
j=2
. We therefore set

j=2+u,\qquad|u|\ll 1,

(143)

and expand the harmonic sum as

S_{1}(2+u)=\frac{3}{2}+\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{5}{4}\right)u+\mathcal{O}(u^{2}).

(144)

Using the LO singlet anomalous-dimension entries introduced above, one finds

\displaystyle\gamma_{2+u}^{\Sigma\Sigma,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{3}C_{F}+\frac{C_{F}}{18}\left(12\pi^{2}-85\right)u+\mathcal{O}(u^{2}),

(145a)

\displaystyle\gamma_{2+u}^{\Sigma g,(0)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{3}T_{F}n_{f}+\frac{11}{18}T_{F}n_{f}\,u+\mathcal{O}(u^{2}),

(145b)

\displaystyle\gamma_{2+u}^{g\Sigma,(0)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{8}{3}C_{F}+\frac{29}{9}C_{F}\,u+\mathcal{O}(u^{2}),

(145c)

\displaystyle\gamma_{2+u}^{gg,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}T_{F}n_{f}+\frac{C_{A}}{36}\left(24\pi^{2}-65\right)u+\mathcal{O}(u^{2}).

(145d)

It is convenient to introduce the trace and determinant of the LO singlet matrix,

T_{j}^{(0)}\equiv\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma\Sigma,(0)}+\gamma_{j}^{gg,(0)},\qquad D_{j}^{(0)}\equiv\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma\Sigma,(0)}\gamma_{j}^{gg,(0)}-\gamma_{j}^{\Sigma g,(0)}\gamma_{j}^{g\Sigma,(0)}.

(146)

Near
j=2
, these become

\displaystyle T_{2+u}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=T_{0}+T_{1}\,u+\mathcal{O}(u^{2}),

(147a)

\displaystyle D_{2+u}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=D_{1}\,u+\mathcal{O}(u^{2}),

(147b)

with

\displaystyle T_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}\left(2C_{F}+T_{F}n_{f}\right),

(148a)

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{F}}{18}\left(12\pi^{2}-85\right)+\frac{C_{A}}{36}\left(24\pi^{2}-65\right),

(148b)

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2C_{F}}{27}\left[C_{A}\left(24\pi^{2}-65\right)+T_{F}n_{f}\left(12\pi^{2}-5\right)\right].

(148c)

Since the LO eigenvalues satisfy

\gamma_{j}^{+,(0)}+\gamma_{j}^{-,(0)}=T_{j}^{(0)},\qquad\gamma_{j}^{+,(0)}\gamma_{j}^{-,(0)}=D_{j}^{(0)},

(149)

and since
D_{2}^{(0)}=0
, one obtains immediately

\gamma_{2}^{-,(0)}=0,\qquad\gamma_{2}^{+,(0)}=T_{0}=\frac{4}{3}\left(2C_{F}+T_{F}n_{f}\right).

(150)

The vanishing of
\gamma_{2}^{-,(0)}
is the LO manifestation of the singlet momentum-sum rule.

To first order in
u=j-2
, the eigenvalues therefore expand as

\displaystyle\gamma_{2+u}^{-,(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{D_{1}}{T_{0}}\,u+\mathcal{O}(u^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{F}\Bigl[C_{A}\left(24\pi^{2}-65\right)+T_{F}n_{f}\left(12\pi^{2}-5\right)\Bigr]}{18\left(2C_{F}+T_{F}n_{f}\right)}\,(j-2)+\mathcal{O}\!\big((j-2)^{2}\big),

(151a)

\displaystyle\gamma_{2+u}^{+,(0)}

\displaystyle=T_{0}+\left(T_{1}-\frac{D_{1}}{T_{0}}\right)u+\mathcal{O}(u^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}\left(2C_{F}+T_{F}n_{f}\right)+\frac{4C_{F}^{2}\left(12\pi^{2}-85\right)+T_{F}n_{f}\Bigl[C_{A}\left(24\pi^{2}-65\right)-160C_{F}\Bigr]}{36\left(2C_{F}+T_{F}n_{f}\right)}\,(j-2)+\mathcal{O}\!\big((j-2)^{2}\big).

(151b)

Thus, near
j=2
the minus eigenvalue starts linearly from zero, while the plus eigenvalue
remains finite and receives a regular linear correction. These formulas are useful when analyzing
both the forward normalization constants
c_{j}^{\pm}
and the behavior of the singlet fixed–
j

Compton amplitude in the vicinity of the first even spin.
