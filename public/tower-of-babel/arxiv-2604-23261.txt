Mabuchi solitons and Mabuchi constants on Fano admissible manifolds Thanks: The second author was supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Numbers JP21K03234, 26K06790.
Shotaro Murayama, Yasufumi Nitta

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the existence of Mabuchi solitons on admissible manifolds as defined by Apostolov–Calderbank–Gauduchon–Tønnesen-Friedman. We prove that a Fano admissible manifold admits a Mabuchi soliton if and only if the Mabuchi constant is less than 1. We also provide an explicit formula for the Mabuchi constant on Fano admissible manifolds, which generalizes that of Mabuchi. Using this formula, we completely determine the existence and non-existence of Mabuchi solitons on Fano admissible manifolds over the complex projective space 𝐏 n \mathbf{P}^{n} .

Primary 53C25; Secondary 14J45, 32Q26.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

A
Mabuchi soliton
, introduced by Mabuchi in
[
7
]
, is a generalization of a Kähler–Einstein metric.
Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional Fano manifold, and let
G
be a maximal compact subgroup of
\Aut^{0}(X)
.
Let
\omega
be a
G
-invariant Kähler metric on
X
representing
2\pi c_{1}(X)
.
By the
dd^{c}
-lemma, there exists a unique function
h_{\omega}\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbf{R})^{G}
satisfying

\Ric(\omega)-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}h_{\omega},\quad\int_{X}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=0.

This function
h_{\omega}
is called the
Ricci potential
of
\omega
.

A Kähler metric
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)^{G}
is called a
Mabuchi soliton
if the gradient

\grad_{\omega}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})

with respect to
\omega
is a real holomorphic vector field on
X
.
It is immediate that any
G
-invariant Kähler–Einstein metric is a Mabuchi soliton.
Moreover, a Mabuchi soliton is a Kähler–Einstein metric if and only if the Futaki invariant of
X
vanishes (see
[
7
, Section 1]
).

An obstruction to the existence of Mabuchi solitons is given by the
Mabuchi constant
.
Let
P_{\omega}
be the space of Killing potentials with respect to
\omega
, and let
\Pi_{\omega}^{G}\colon C^{\infty}(X,\mathbf{R})\to P_{\omega}
be the
L^{2}
-projection.
Then Mabuchi showed that the constant

M_{X}\coloneq\max_{X}\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})

is independent of the choice of
\omega
(
[
7
, Section 2]
).
This
M_{X}
is called the
Mabuchi constant
of
X
.
The Mabuchi constant satisfies
M_{X}<1
if
X
admits a Mabuchi soliton.
In this paper, we study a special class of manifolds, called admissible manifolds (
[
1
, Section 1]
), and prove that the converse holds for Fano admissible manifolds.
(We also point out that a similar equivalence holds for toric Fano manifolds; see
[
12
]
for details)
Moreover, we give an explicit formula for the Mabuchi constant of Fano admissible manifolds, generalizing
[
7
, Theorem 6.5]
.
Using this, we completely determine the existence and non-existence of Mabuchi solitons on admissible manifolds over a complex projective space
\mathbf{P}^{n}
.

1.2.
Main results

An
admissible manifold
is a projective bundle
\mathbf{P}_{Y}(E_{0}\oplus E_{\infty})\to Y
satisfying the following conditions:

•

Y
is covered by a product
\tilde{Y}\coloneq\prod_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Y_{a}
(
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbf{Z}_{>0},\#\mathcal{A}<\infty
) of simply-connected compact Kähler manifolds
(Y_{a},g_{a},\omega_{a})
of real dimension
2d_{a}
, with
(g_{a},\omega_{a})
being pullbacks of tensors on
Y
.

•

E_{0}
and
E_{\infty}
are holomorphic projectively-flat Hermitian vector bundles over
Y
of ranks
d_{0}+1
and
d_{\infty}+1
, respectively, satisfying

\frac{c_{1}(E_{\infty})}{d_{\infty}+1}-\frac{c_{1}(E_{0})}{d_{0}+1}=\left[\frac{\omega_{Y}}{2\pi}\right],\quad\omega_{Y}\coloneq\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a},

where
\varepsilon_{a}\in\{-1,1\}
(
a\in\mathcal{A}
).

Assume further that each
\omega_{a}
is a Ricci positive Kähler–Einstein metric with
\Ric(\omega_{a})=\varepsilon_{a}s_{a}\omega_{a}
for
s_{a}\in\mathbf{R}
.
Then
X
is a Fano manifold if and only if

\displaystyle\begin{cases}s_{a}>d_{0}+1&\text{(if $\varepsilon_{a}=1$)},\\
s_{a}<-(d_{\infty}+1)&\text{(if $\varepsilon_{a}=-1$)}\end{cases}

for any
a\in\mathcal{A}
(
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
).
Such an
X
is called a
Fano admissible manifold
.

Our first result shows that, in Fano admissible manifolds, the existence of a Mabuchi soliton can be completely determined by the Mabuchi constant.

Theorem 1.1

(cf. Corollary
5.11
)
.

A Fano admissible manifold
X
admits a Mabuchi soliton if and only if
M_{X}<1
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is obtained as a consequence of a more general existence theory for
v
-solitons
, and will be given in Section
5.2
.
In general, the existence of a
v
-soliton is obstructed by the
v
-Futaki invariant (cf. Proposition
5.6
).
For Fano admissible manifolds, however, we show that the existence of an admissible
v
-soliton is in fact equivalent to the vanishing of the
v
-Futaki invariant (see Theorem
5.8
). On the other hand, the
v
-Futaki invariant corresponding to Mabuchi solitons always vanishes.
Therefore,
1.1
follows immediately from Theorem
5.8
.

By Theorem
1.1
, we can determine the existence of Mabuchi solitons on Fano admissible manifolds in terms of the Mabuchi constant.
We next compute the Mabuchi constant for Fano admissible manifolds.
Let

\Omega\coloneqq 2\pi c_{1}(X),\quad x_{a}\coloneq\frac{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}{2s_{a}+d_{\infty}-d_{0}}\quad(a\in\mathcal{A}),

and define the
characteristic polynomial

p_{\Omega}
of
\Omega
by

p_{\Omega}(x)\coloneq(1+x)^{d_{0}}(1-x)^{d_{\infty}}\prod_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{a}}{x_{a}}+\varepsilon_{a}x\right)^{d_{a}}

(cf. Section
3.3
).
For
i\in\{0,1,2\}
, set

b_{i}\coloneq\int_{-1}^{1}x^{i}p_{\Omega}(x)\,dx.

In the case
d_{0}=d_{\infty}=0
, Mabuchi obtained an explicit formula for
M_{X}
in terms of
b_{0}
,
b_{1}
,
b_{2}
(
[
7
, Theorem 6.5]
).
We extend this to general Fano admissible manifolds as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(cf. Theorem
6.1
)
.

Let
w\coloneqq\dfrac{d_{0}-d_{\infty}}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}
.
Then

\displaystyle M_{X}=\frac{b_{0}|b_{1}-wb_{0}|-b_{1}(b_{1}-wb_{0})}{b_{0}b_{2}-b_{1}^{2}}=1+\frac{b_{0}(|b_{1}-wb_{0}|-(b_{2}-wb_{1}))}{b_{0}b_{2}-b_{1}^{2}}.

Finally, we consider admissible manifolds over the complex projective space
\mathbf{P}^{n}
and discuss the existence of Mabuchi solitons on them.
Any Fano admissible manifold
X
over
\mathbf{P}^{n}
can be written as

X=\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{P}^{n}}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{0}+1)}\oplus\mathcal{O}(k)^{\oplus(d_{\infty}+1)})

for some
k\in\mathbf{Z}
satisfying
k\geq 1
and
n+1>k(d_{0}+1)
.
Moreover, the existence of a Mabuchi soliton on
X
is equivalent to the condition
M_{X}<1
(see Corollary
5.11
).
Using Theorem
1.2
, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3

(cf. Theorem
7.1
)
.

The Fano admissible manifold
X
admits a Mabuchi soliton if and only if

(k,d_{\infty})=(1,0)\quad\text{or}\quad(n,k,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(1,1,0,1).

In all other cases,
M_{X}>1
.

Theorem
1.3
can also be interpreted from the viewpoint of stability.
By the Yau–Tian–Donaldson type correspondence for Mabuchi solitons, established by Han and Li
[
4
]
, the existence of a Mabuchi soliton on a Fano manifold is equivalent to
uniform relative Ding stability
(see also
[
12
,
10
,
11
,
5
]
).
Moreover, by
[
11
, Theorem 1]
, the Mabuchi constant of any relatively Ding semistable Fano manifold is bounded above by 1.
Combining these results with Theorem
1.3
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 1.4
.

X
is uniformly relatively Ding polystable if and only if

(k,d_{\infty})=(1,0)\quad\text{or}\quad(n,k,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(1,1,0,1).

Otherwise,
X
is relatively Ding unstable.

Organization of the paper

We start by reviewing basic notions on Mabuchi solitons in Section

.
The Mabuchi constant is also introduced.
In Section

After introducing admissible manifolds, we discuss admissible Kähler classes and admissible Kähler metrics, which are the main tools of this paper, as well as the characteristic polynomial associated with an admissible Kähler class.

In Section

2.
Mabuchi solitons and the Mabuchi constant

We first recall the definitions of Mabuchi solitons and the Mabuchi constant.
Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional Fano manifold, and let
G
be a maximal compact subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}^{0}(X)
.
Let
\omega
be a
G
-invariant Kähler metric on
X
representing
2\pi c_{1}(X)
, and denote by
h_{\omega}
the Ricci potential of
\omega
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)^{G}
be a
G
-invariant Kähler metric on
X
.
We say that
\omega
is a
Mabuchi soliton
if the gradient

\grad_{\omega}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})

with respect to
\omega
is a real holomorphic vector field on
X
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)^{G}
be a
G
-invariant Kähler–Einstein metric on
X
:
\Ric(\omega)=\omega
.
Then
h_{\omega}=0
and hence

\mathrm{grad}_{\omega}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})=\mathrm{grad}_{\omega}0=0.

Therefore,
\omega
is a Mabuchi soliton.

Example 2.3

(
[
7
, Example 5.8]
)
.

Let
X\coloneqq\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{P}^{n}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n}}(1))
be the Fano manifold obtained by blowing up
\mathbf{P}^{n}
at a point.
Then
X
admits a Mabuchi soliton but does not admit a Kähler–Einstein metric.

Next, we explain the Mabuchi constant, which is known as an obstruction to the existence of Mabuchi solitons.
Let
P_{\omega}
denote the space of Killing potentials with respect to
\omega
, i.e., the space of real-valued smooth functions
f\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbf{R})
such that
\grad_{\omega}f
is a real holomorphic vector field on
X
.
Let
\Pi_{\omega}^{G}\colon C^{\infty}(X,\mathbf{R})^{G}\to P_{\omega}
be the
L^{2}
-projection with respect to
\omega
.
Then a
G
-invariant Kähler metric
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)^{G}
is a Mabuchi soliton if and only if
\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})=1-e^{h_{\omega}}
.
Moreover, Mabuchi showed in
[
7
]
that the constant

\max_{X}\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})

is independent of the choice of
\omega\in 2\pi c_{1}(X)^{G}
.

Definition 2.4
.

We define
M_{X}\coloneqq\max_{X}\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})
and call it the
Mabuchi constant
of the Fano manifold
X
.

The following examples show that the Mabuchi constant serves as an obstruction to the existence of Kähler–Einstein metrics and Mabuchi solitons.

Example 2.5
.

Assume that
2\pi c_{1}(X)^{G}
contains a Kähler–Einstein metric
\omega
.
Then
h_{\omega}=0
, and hence

\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})=\Pi_{\omega}^{G}0=0.

Therefore, we have
{\displaystyle M_{X}=\max_{X}\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})=0}
.

Example 2.6

(
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Assume that
2\pi c_{1}(X)^{G}
contains a Mabuchi soliton
\omega
.
Then

\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})=1-e^{h_{\omega}}<1

holds on
X
.
Hence,
{\displaystyle M_{X}=\max_{X}\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})<1}
.

3.
Admissible manifolds

3.1.
Definition of admissible manifolds

In this section, following the description in
[
1
]
, we explain the definition of admissible manifolds. For details, we refer the reader to
[
1
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

An
admissible manifold
is a projective bundle
\mathbf{P}_{Y}(E_{0}\oplus E_{\infty})\to Y
satisfying the following conditions:

•

Y
is covered by a product
\tilde{Y}\coloneq\prod_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Y_{a}
(
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbf{Z}_{>0},\#\mathcal{A}<\infty
) of simply-connected compact Kähler manifolds
(Y_{a},g_{a},\omega_{a})
of real dimension
2d_{a}
, with
(g_{a},\omega_{a})
being pullbacks of tensors on
Y
.

•

E_{0}
and
E_{\infty}
are holomorphic projectively-flat Hermitian vector bundles over
Y
of ranks
d_{0}+1
and
d_{\infty}+1
, respectively, satisfying

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}(E_{\infty})}{d_{\infty}+1}-\frac{c_{1}(E_{0})}{d_{0}+1}=\left[\frac{\omega_{Y}}{2\pi}\right],\quad\omega_{Y}\coloneq\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a},

where
\varepsilon_{a}\in\{-1,1\}
(
a\in\mathcal{A}
).

Let
X=\mathbf{P}_{Y}(E_{0}\oplus E_{\infty})
be an admissible manifold.
By the condition (
3.1
) above, there exist Hermitian metrics on
E_{0}
and
E_{\infty}
such that the curvatures of their Chern connections are given by
\Omega_{0}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{E_{0}}
,
\Omega_{\infty}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{E_{\infty}}
, and satisfy
\Omega_{\infty}-\Omega_{0}=\omega_{Y}
.
Let
(g_{0},\omega_{0})
and
(g_{\infty},\omega_{\infty})
denote the fiberwise Fubini–Study metrics of
\mathbf{P}(E_{0})
and
\mathbf{P}(E_{\infty})
.
We normalize them so that their scalar curvatures are
2d_{0}(d_{0}+1)
and
2d_{\infty}(d_{\infty}+1)
, respectively.
We also set
\varepsilon_{0}\coloneqq 1
and
\varepsilon_{\infty}\coloneqq-1
.

We summarize below the notation for admissible manifolds used in this paper:

(1)

We set
\hat{\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq\{a\in\mathcal{A}\cup\{0,\infty\}\mid d_{a}>0\}
.

(2)

Let
\tilde{E}_{0}
and
\tilde{E}_{\infty}
be the pullbacks of
E_{0}
and
E_{\infty}
to
\tilde{Y}
, respectively.
Since
\tilde{Y}
is simply-connected, they can be written as

\tilde{E}_{0}=\mathcal{E}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{0}+1)},\quad\tilde{E}_{\infty}=\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\otimes\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{\infty}+1)}

for some holomorphic line bundles
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
over
\tilde{Y}
.
Let
\mathcal{L}\coloneqq\mathcal{E}_{0}^{-1}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
.
Then
\mathcal{L}
is a holomorphic line bundle on
\tilde{Y}
, and can be written as

\mathcal{L}=\bigotimes_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{L}_{a},

where each
\mathcal{L}_{a}
is a holomorphic line bundle over
Y_{a}
satisfying

c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{a})=\left[\frac{\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}}{2\pi}\right].

(3)

Let
e_{0}\coloneqq\mathbf{P}_{Y}(E_{0}\oplus 0)
,
e_{\infty}\coloneqq\mathbf{P}_{Y}(0\oplus E_{\infty})
.
Their universal covers are, respectively,
Y_{0}\times\tilde{Y}\coloneqq\mathbf{P}^{d_{0}}\times\tilde{Y}
and
\tilde{Y}\times Y_{\infty}\coloneqq\tilde{Y}\times\mathbf{P}^{d_{\infty}}
.

(4)

The blow-up of
X
along
e_{0}\cup e_{\infty}
is given by
\hat{X}\coloneqq\mathbf{P}_{\hat{Y}}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\hat{\mathcal{L}})\to\hat{Y}
, where
\hat{Y}\coloneqq\mathbf{P}_{Y}(E_{0})\times_{Y}\mathbf{P}_{Y}(E_{\infty})\to Y
and
\hat{\mathcal{L}}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{E_{0}}(1)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{E_{\infty}}(-1)
.
In this case, for
\omega_{\hat{Y}}\coloneqq\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}
we have
c_{1}(\hat{\mathcal{L}})=[\omega_{\hat{Y}}/2\pi]
.
We say
a blow-down occurs
if
d_{0}>0
or
d_{\infty}>0
.

(5)

Let
\hat{e}_{0}
and
\hat{e}_{\infty}
denote the zero and infinity sections of
\hat{X}
, respectively.

3.2.
Admissible Kähler classes and metrics

In this section, we explain a special class of Kähler metrics on admissible manifolds, called
admissible Kähler metrics
.
As in the previous section, we refer the reader to
[
1
]
for further details.

We begin by describing admissible Kähler classes, which serve as the natural setting for admissible Kähler metrics.
Let
X=\mathbf{P}_{Y}(E_{0}\oplus E_{\infty})
be an admissible manifold.

Definition 3.2
.

A Kähler class
\Omega
on
X
is called an
admissible Kähler class
if its pullback to
\hat{X}
can be written as

(3.2)

\displaystyle c\left(\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\frac{[\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}]}{x_{a}}+\hat{\Xi}\right)

for some
c\in\mathbf{R}
,
x_{a}\in\mathbf{R}\setminus\{0\}
with
x_{0}=1
,
x_{\infty}=-1
, where
\hat{\Xi}
is the Poincaré dual of
2\pi[\hat{e}_{0}+\hat{e}_{\infty}]
.
If
c=1
, the admissible Kähler class is said to be
normalized
, or is called a
normalized admissible Kähler class
.

Let
\Xi
be a 2-form on
X
whose pullback to
\hat{X}
is
[\omega_{0}]-[\omega_{\infty}]+\hat{\Xi}
.
Then a Kähler class
\Omega
is admissible if and only if

c\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{[\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}]}{x_{a}}+\Xi\right)

for some
c\in\mathbf{R}
and
x_{a}\in\mathbf{R}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proposition 3.3

(
[
1
, p.557]
by the discussion therein)
.

Let
c\in\mathbf{R}
and
x_{a}\in\mathbf{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
Let a cohomology class
\Omega\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R})
be given by (
3.2
).
Then the following two conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\Omega
is an admissible Kähler class of
X
.

(2)

c\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}
and
0<\varepsilon_{a}x_{a}<1
for any
a\in\mathcal{A}
.

Let
\Omega
be an admissible Kähler class of
X
given by (
3.2
).
By Proposition
3.3
,
c\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}
and
0<\varepsilon_{a}x_{a}<1
for any
a\in\mathcal{A}
.
The scalar multiplication on the fiber of
E_{0}
induces a
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-action on
X
.
By identifying
X_{0}\coloneqq X\setminus(e_{0}\cup e_{\infty})\cong\hat{X}\setminus(\hat{e}_{0}\cup\hat{e}_{\infty})
with an open subset of
\hat{X}
,
X_{0}
is a principal
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-bundle over
\hat{Y}
associated to the holomorphic line bundle
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
.

We now consider the
S^{1}
-action on
X
obtained by restricting the
\mathbf{C}^{\ast}
-action.
The Hermitian metrics on
E_{0}
and
E_{\infty}
induce a fiberwise moment map
z\colon X\to[-1,1]
.
The critical submanifolds of
z
are given by

z^{-1}(\{1\})=e_{0},\quad z^{-1}(\{-1\})=e_{\infty}.

(A concrete construction of
z
can be found in
[
1
, Section 2.1]
)
Let
T
denote the infinitesimal generator of the
S^{1}
-action on
X
.
Let
\theta
be a connection form on
X_{0}
satisfying

(3.3)

\displaystyle d\theta=\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}.

Definition 3.4
.

A Kähler metric
\omega\in\Omega
on
X
is called an
admissible Kähler metric
if

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&g=c\left(\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\frac{1+x_{a}z}{x_{a}}\varepsilon_{a}g_{a}+\frac{dz^{2}}{\Theta(z)}+\Theta(z)\theta^{2}\right),\\
&\omega=c\left(\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\frac{1+x_{a}z}{x_{a}}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}+dz\wedge\theta\right)\end{split}

on
X_{0}
, where
g
is the Riemannian metric associated to
\omega
, and
\Theta
is a smooth function on
[-1,1]
satisfying

(1)

\Theta(x)>0
(
x\in(-1,1)
),

(2)

\Theta(\pm 1)=0
,
\Theta^{\prime}(\pm 1)=\mp 2
.

If
c=1
, the admissible Kähler metric
\omega
is said to be
normalized
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega
be an admissible Kähler metric in the class
\Omega
defined by (
3.4
).
It is immediate that
z
is a moment map for
\dfrac{1}{c}\omega
:

-dz=\iota_{T}\dfrac{1}{c}\omega.

Moreover, the complex structure on
X
corresponding to this Kähler structure is obtained by taking the pullback of the complex structures on each
Y_{a}
(
a\in\mathcal{A}
), together with the condition
d^{c}z=\Theta(z)\theta
.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
\omega
be a Kähler metric on
X
, and suppose that it can be written in the form (
3.4
) on
X_{0}
.
Then, using (
3.3
), one can show that
\omega
belongs to
\Omega
.
Furthermore, the 2-form
d(z\theta)
, regarded as a differential form on
X_{0}\subset\hat{X}
, extends to the whole of
\hat{X}
, and its de Rham cohomology class coincides with
\hat{\Xi}
.
For details, see
[
1

3.3.
Characteristic polynomials

In this section, we explain the characteristic polynomial associated with an admissible Kähler class.
As will be stated in Proposition
3.7
below, this polynomial coincides, up to a constant multiple, with the
Duistermaat–Heckman polynomial
associated with the
S^{1}
-action on
X
.

Let
X=\mathbf{P}_{Y}(E_{0}\oplus E_{\infty})
be a
d
-dimensional admissible manifold, and let
\Omega
be an admissible Kähler class on
X
given by (
3.2
).

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\lambda_{a}\coloneq\varepsilon_{a}/x_{a}
for each
a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}
(note that
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{\infty}=1
), and define

\displaystyle p_{\Omega}(x)\coloneqq\prod_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}(\lambda_{a}+\varepsilon_{a}z)^{d_{a}}=(1+x)^{d_{0}}(1-x)^{d_{\infty}}\prod_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\lambda_{a}+\varepsilon_{a}x)^{d_{a}}.

We call
p_{\Omega}
the
characteristic polynomial
of the admissible Kähler class
\Omega
.

A careful reading of the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 6]
yields the following proposition.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega
be an admissible Kähler metric on
X
given by (
3.4
).
Then

\displaystyle\int_{X}f(z)\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}=2\pi c^{d}\left(\prod_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\vol(Y_{a},\omega_{a})\right)\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)p_{\Omega}(x)\,dx

for any integrable function
f
on the closed interval
[-1,1]
.

4.
Fano admissible manifolds

4.1.
Definition of Fano admissible manifolds

In this section we explain a necessary and sufficient condition for an admissible manifold to be Fano, following
[
2
]
.
We here assume that
Y=\tilde{Y}=\prod_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Y_{a}
and each
\omega_{a}
is a Ricci positive Kähler–Einstein metric on
Y_{a}
:
\Ric(\omega_{a})=\varepsilon_{a}s_{a}\omega_{a}
(
a\in\mathcal{A}
).
Let
X
be an admissible manifold over
Y
.
Since
Y
is simply-connected,
X
can be written as

X=\mathbf{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{0}+1)}\oplus(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{\infty}+1)})),

where
\mathcal{L}
is a holomorphic line bundle over
Y
satisfying
\mathcal{L}=\otimes_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{L}_{a}
with each
\mathcal{L}_{a}
a holomorphic line bundles over
Y_{a}
satisfying
c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{a})=[\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}/2\pi]
.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

For
X
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

X
is a Fano manifold.

(2)

For each
a\in\mathcal{A}
,

\begin{cases}s_{a}>d_{0}+1&(\text{if }\varepsilon_{a}=1),\\
s_{a}<-(d_{\infty}+1)&(\text{if }\varepsilon_{a}=-1).\end{cases}

Moreover, if
X
satisfies these conditions, then
2\pi c_{1}(X)
is an admissible Kähler class, and satisfies

(4.1)

\displaystyle 2\pi c_{1}(X)=c\left[\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{[\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}]}{x_{a}}\right)+\Xi\right]

for

(4.2)

\displaystyle c=\frac{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}{2},\quad x_{a}=\frac{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}{2s_{a}+d_{\infty}-d_{0}}\quad(a\in\mathcal{A}).

Definition 4.2
.

If
X
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
4.1
,
X
is called a
Fano admissible manifold
.

Here we note that it is shown in
[
2
]
that any such
X
admits a Kähler–Ricci soliton.

4.2.
Ricci potentials

Let
X=\mathbf{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{0}+1)}\oplus(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{\infty}+1)}))
be a Fano admissible manifold.
Let
\Omega\coloneqq 2\pi c_{1}(X)
, and let
\omega\in\Omega
be an admissible Kähler metric given by (
3.4
).
The goal of this section is to compute explicitly the Ricci potential of
\omega
.
First, by
[
2
, (3.2)]
we have

\Ric(\omega)=\Ric\left(\frac{\omega}{c}\right)=\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\Ric(\omega_{a})-\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log(p_{\Omega}(z)\Theta(z))

on
X\setminus(e_{0}\cup e_{\infty})
.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=c\left(\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\frac{1+x_{a}z}{x_{a}}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}+dz\wedge\theta\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\frac{c}{x_{a}}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}+cz\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}+c(dz\wedge\theta),

which can be written as

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}s_{a}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}+\left(\frac{d_{\infty}-d_{0}}{2}+cz\right)\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}+c(dz\wedge\theta)

since for any
a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}

\frac{c}{x_{a}}=s_{a}+\frac{d_{\infty}-d_{0}}{2}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle\Ric(\omega)-\omega

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\Ric(\omega_{a})-\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log(p_{\Omega}(z)\Theta(z))

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}s_{a}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}-\left(\frac{d_{\infty}-d_{0}}{2}+cz\right)\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}-c(dz\wedge\theta).

Using
\Ric(\omega_{a})=\varepsilon_{a}s_{a}\omega_{a}
, this simplifies to

\displaystyle\Ric(\omega)-\omega=-\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log(p_{\Omega}(z)\Theta(z))-\left(\frac{d_{\infty}-d_{0}}{2}+cz\right)\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}-c(dz\wedge\theta).

Since
d^{c}z=\Theta(z)\theta
, we have

\displaystyle dz\wedge\theta=d(z\theta)-zd\theta=d\left(\frac{z}{\Theta(z)}d^{c}z\right)-zd\theta,

and using
{\displaystyle d\theta=\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Ric(\omega)-\omega

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log(p_{\Omega}(z)\Theta(z))-\left(\frac{d_{\infty}-d_{0}}{2}\right)d\theta-cd\left(\frac{z}{\Theta(z)}d^{c}z\right).

Define functions on
(-1,1)
by

a_{\Theta}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\Theta(x)},\quad b_{\Theta}(x)\coloneqq\frac{x}{\Theta(x)},

and let
A_{\Theta},B_{\Theta}
be their primitives.
Then

\displaystyle d\theta=d\left(a_{\Theta}(z)d^{c}z\right)=d\left(A_{\Theta}^{\prime}(z)d^{c}z\right)=dd^{c}A_{\Theta}(z),

\displaystyle cd\left(\frac{z}{\Theta(z)}d^{c}z\right)=cd\left(b_{\Theta}(z)d^{c}z\right)=cd\left(B_{\Theta}^{\prime}(z)d^{c}z\right)=dd^{c}(cB_{\Theta}(z)),

and hence

\displaystyle\Ric(\omega)-\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\left(-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(z)-2cB_{\Theta}(z)-\log(p_{\Omega}(z)\Theta(z))\right).

Lemma 4.3
.

Define a smooth function
h\colon(-1,1)\to\mathbf{R}
by

h(x)\coloneqq-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(z)-2cB_{\Theta}(z)-\log(p_{\Omega}(z)\Theta(z)).

Then
h
extends uniquely to a smooth function on the closed interval
[-1,1]
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the derivative of
h

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=(-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(x)-2cB_{\Theta}(x)-\log(p_{\Omega}(x)\Theta(x)))^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})-2cx-\Theta^{\prime}(x)}{\Theta(x)}-\frac{p_{\Omega}^{\prime}(x)}{p_{\Omega}(x)}

extends smoothly to the closed interval
[-1,1]
.
We first consider a neighborhood of
x=1
.
Since
\Theta(1)=0
and
\Theta^{\prime}(1)=-2
, we can write

\Theta(x)=-2(x-1)+\Theta_{1}(x)(x-1)^{2}

for some smooth function
\Theta_{1}
defined near
x=1
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\Theta^{\prime}(x)=-2+2\Theta_{1}(x)(x-1)+\Theta_{1}^{\prime}(x)(x-1)^{2},

and hence

\displaystyle-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})-2cx-\Theta^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-2d_{\infty}-(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)(x-1)

\displaystyle\quad-2\Theta_{1}(x)(x-1)-\Theta_{1}^{\prime}(x)(x-1)^{2}

and

\displaystyle\frac{-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})-2cx-\Theta^{\prime}(x)}{\Theta(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2d_{\infty}}{(-2+\Theta_{1}(x)(x-1))(x-1)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{-(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)-2\Theta_{1}(x)-\Theta_{1}^{\prime}(x)(x-1)}{-2+\Theta_{1}(x)(x-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{\infty}}{x-1}+\Theta_{2}(x),

where
\Theta_{2}
is a smooth function defined near
x=1
.
On the other hand, writing
p_{\Omega}(x)
near
x=1
as

\displaystyle p_{\Omega}(x)=(1-x)^{d_{\infty}}p_{1}(x),

where
p_{1}
is a polynomial function satisfying
p_{1}(1)\neq 0
, we have

p_{\Omega}^{\prime}(x)=-d_{\infty}(1-x)^{d_{\infty}-1}p_{1}(x)+(1-x)^{d_{\infty}}p_{1}^{\prime}(x).

Thus,

\displaystyle-\frac{p_{\Omega}^{\prime}(x)}{p_{\Omega}(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{\infty}(1-x)^{d_{\infty}-1}p_{1}(x)}{(1-x)^{d_{\infty}}p_{1}(x)}-\frac{(1-x)^{d_{\infty}}p_{1}^{\prime}(x)}{(1-x)^{d_{\infty}}p_{1}(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{\infty}}{(1-x)}+p_{2}(x),

where
p_{2}
is a smooth function defined near
x=1
.
Combining the above expressions, we obtain

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})-2cx-\Theta^{\prime}(x)}{\Theta(x)}-\frac{p_{\Omega}^{\prime}(x)}{p_{\Omega}(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{\infty}}{x-1}+\Theta_{2}(x)+\frac{d_{\infty}}{(1-x)}+p_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\Theta_{2}(x)+p_{2}(x)

which shows that
h^{\prime}
extends smoothly near
x=1
.
The argument near
x=-1
is similar.
Therefore,
h^{\prime}
extends smoothly to the closed interval
[-1,1]
, and so does
h
.
∎

We still denote this extension by
h
, and define
h_{\omega}\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbf{R})
by

h_{\omega}\coloneqq h(z).

Noting that
A_{\Theta}
and
B_{\Theta}
are defined only up to additive constants, we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

By choosing
A_{\Theta}
and
B_{\Theta}
so that

\int_{X}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}=0,

the function
h_{\omega}=h(z)
is the Ricci potential of
\omega
.

Remark 4.5
.

It is shown that the Ricci potential is a function of
z
in
[
6
, Corollary 3.3]
.
Proposition
4.4
provides an explicit expression of this function.

By Proposition
4.4
, we see that
1-e^{h_{\omega}}
is a function of
z
.
In fact the following stronger statement is also known.
This fact plays a very important role in Sections
5.2
and
6
.

Proposition 4.6

(
[
1
, Proposition 6]
,
[
8
, Theorem 9.1]
)
.

There exist constants
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{R}
such that

\displaystyle\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})=\alpha z+\beta.

Finally, we show the following proposition, which is crucial for proving Theorem
1.2
in the introduction.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
w\coloneqq\dfrac{d_{0}-d_{\infty}}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}
.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{X}(z-w)e^{h_{\omega}}\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}=0.

Proof.

We first apply Proposition
3.7
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{X}ze^{h_{\omega}}\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}

\displaystyle=2\pi c^{d}\left(\prod_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\vol(Y_{a},\omega_{a})\right)\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(x)-2cB_{\Theta}(x)}\frac{x}{\Theta(x)}\,dx.

We next compute the integral on the right-hand side.
Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(x)-2cB_{\Theta}(x)}\frac{x}{\Theta(x)}\,dx=\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(x)}\left(-\frac{1}{2c}e^{-2cB_{\Theta}}\right)^{\prime}(x)\,dx.

Integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(x)-2cB_{\Theta}(x)}\frac{x}{\Theta(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle=\left[-\frac{1}{2c}e^{-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(x)-2cB_{\Theta}(x)}\right]_{-1}^{1}+w\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(x)-2cB_{\Theta}(x)}\frac{1}{\Theta(x)}\,dx.

By Lemma
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 1}(-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(x)-2cB_{\Theta}(x))

\displaystyle=h(1)+\lim_{x\to 1}\log(p_{\Omega}(x)\Theta(x))=-\infty,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-1}(-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(x)-2cB_{\Theta}(x))

\displaystyle=h(-1)+\lim_{x\to-1}\log(p_{\Omega}(x)\Theta(x))=-\infty.

Hence the boundary term vanishes:

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{1}{2c}e^{-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(x)-2cB_{\Theta}(x)}\right]_{-1}^{1}=0.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(x)-2cB_{\Theta}(x)}\frac{x}{\Theta(x)}\,dx=w\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(x)-2cB_{\Theta}(x)}\frac{1}{\Theta(x)}\,dx.

Substituting this into the expression for the integral over
X
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}ze^{h_{\omega}}\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}

\displaystyle=2\pi c^{d}w\left(\prod_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\vol(Y_{a},\omega_{a})\right)\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-(d_{\infty}-d_{0})A_{\Theta}(x)-2cB_{\Theta}(x)}\frac{1}{\Theta(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle=w\int_{X}e^{h_{\omega}}\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}.

∎

5.
v
-solitons on Fano admissible manifolds

5.1.
v
-solitons

We now temporarily leave the setting of admissible manifolds and consider a general Fano manifold.
Let
X
be a
d
-dimensional Fano manifold, and let
T\subset\Aut^{0}(X)
be a compact torus.
Let
\Omega\coloneqq 2\pi c_{1}(X)
, and let
\omega\in\Omega
be a
T
-invariant Kähler metric.
Denote by
\mu_{\omega}\colon X\to\mathfrak{t}^{\ast}
the moment map associated with the
T
-action.
We normalize
\mu_{\omega}
so that for every
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}
,

\int_{X}\langle\mu_{\omega},\xi\rangle e^{h_{\omega}}\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}=0,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\mathfrak{t}^{\ast}\times\mathfrak{t}\to\mathbf{R}
denotes the natural pairing.
With this normalization, the image
P\coloneqq\mu_{\omega}(X)
is independent of the choice of
T
-invariant Kähler metric
\omega\in\Omega
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
v\in C^{\infty}(P,\mathbf{R}_{>0})
.
A
T
-invariant Kähler metric
\omega\in\Omega
is called a
v
-soliton
if it satisfies

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Ric(\omega)-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log(v(\mu_{\omega})).

Example 5.2
.

If
v(x)=1
for all
x\in P
, then the equation (
5.1
) becomes

\Ric(\omega)-\omega=0,

so a
v
-soliton reduces to a Kähler–Einstein metric.

Example 5.3
.

Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}
, and set
v(x)=e^{\langle x,\xi\rangle}
for each
x\in P
.
Then the equation (
5.1
) becomes

\Ric(\omega)-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\langle\mu_{\omega},\xi\rangle,

and such a metric is known to be a Kähler–Ricci soliton.

Example 5.4
.

Mabuchi showed in
[
8
]
that Mabuchi solitons are a special case of
v
-solitons as follows.
Let
X
be a Fano manifold and assume
M_{X}<1
.
Fix a maximal compact subgroup
G\subset\Aut^{0}(X)
.
For a Kähler metric
\omega\in\Omega
, the vector field
\grad_{\omega}\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})
is real holomorphic on
X
, and in fact induces an
S^{1}
-action on
X
(see
[
3
]
and
[
8
]
).
For a suitable constant
a\in\mathbf{R}
, define
\mu_{\omega}\coloneqq\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})-a\colon X\to\mathbf{R}
.
Then
\mu_{\omega}
is a normalized moment map for this
S^{1}
-action.
Define a function
v\colon P\to\mathbf{R}
by
v(x)\coloneqq 1-a-x
.
Since
M_{X}<1
, we have
v>0
on
P
.
A direct computation shows that
\omega
is a Mabuchi soliton if and only if

\displaystyle\Ric(\omega)-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log(v(\mu_{\omega})).

Thus, Mabuchi solitons are
v
-solitons for this choice of
v
.

Next, we introduce an obstruction to the existence of
v
-solitons, analogous to the Futaki invariant for Kähler–Einstein metrics.
Let
\omega\in\Omega
be a
T
-invariant Kähler metric, and let
\mu_{\omega}\colon X\to\mathfrak{t}^{\ast}
be the normalized moment map.
Let
\mu_{\mathrm{DH}}
denote the Borel measure on
P
obtained as the pushforward of the Liouville measure
\omega^{d}/d!
under
\mu_{\omega}
.
It is known that
\mu_{\mathrm{DH}}
is independent of the choice of
\omega\in\Omega
.
This measure is known as the
Duistermaat–Heckman measure
on
P
associated with the
T
-action on
X
.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
v\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbf{R}_{>0})
be a weight function and define a linear map
\Fut_{v}\colon\mathfrak{t}\to\mathbf{R}
by

\Fut_{v}(\xi)\coloneqq\int_{P}\langle x,\xi\rangle v(x)\,d\mu_{\mathrm{DH}}.

This is called the
v
-Futaki invariant
of
X
.

Proposition 5.6
.

If
\Omega
contains a
v
-soliton, then
\Fut_{v}=0
.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in\Omega
be a
v
-soliton on
X
.
Then by (
5.1
) there exists a constant
a\in\mathbf{R}
such that

\log(v(\mu_{\omega}))=a+h_{\omega},

hence
v(\mu_{\omega})=e^{a+h_{\omega}}
.
Therefore, for any
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}
we have

\displaystyle\Fut_{v}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\langle\mu_{\omega},\xi\rangle v(\mu_{\omega})\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}=\int_{X}\langle\mu_{\omega},\xi\rangle e^{a+h_{\omega}}\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}

\displaystyle=e^{a}\int_{X}\langle\mu_{\omega},\xi\rangle e^{h_{\omega}}\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}=0

by the normalization of
\mu_{\omega}
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

It is known that the
v
-Futaki invariant associated with Mabuchi solitons always vanishes.
In fact, using the notation of Example
5.4
, this follows from

\displaystyle\Fut_{v}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\langle\mu_{\omega},\xi\rangle v(\mu_{\omega})\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\langle\mu_{\omega},\xi\rangle\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}-\int_{X}\langle\mu_{\omega},\xi\rangle\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\langle\mu_{\omega},\xi\rangle\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}-\int_{X}\langle\mu_{\omega},\xi\rangle(1-e^{h_{\omega}})\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\langle\mu_{\omega},\xi\rangle\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}-\int_{X}\langle\mu_{\omega},\xi\rangle\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}

\displaystyle=0.

5.2.
Existence result for Fano admissible manifolds

In this section, we study the existence problem of
v
-solitons on Fano admissible manifolds.
Let
X=\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{0}+1)}\oplus(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{\infty}+1)}))
be a Fano admissible manifold, and let
\Omega\coloneqq 2\pi c_{1}(X)
be the admissible Kähler class given by (
3.2
).
Consider the
S^{1}
-action on
X
induced by scalar multiplication on
\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{0}+1)}
, and let
T
,
z
and
\theta
be as defined in Section
3.2
.
If
\omega\in\Omega
is an admissible Kähler metric, then by Proposition
4.7
, the normalized moment map is given by

\mu_{\omega}\coloneqq c(z-w),

where
w=(d_{0}-d_{\infty})/(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)
.
Let
P\coloneqq\mu_{\omega}(X)
, and let
v\in C^{\infty}(P,\mathbf{R}_{>0})
.
Define a function
u\in C^{\infty}([-1,1],\mathbf{R}_{>0})
by
u(x)\coloneqq v(c(z-w))
.
Then
\omega
is a
v
-soliton if and only if it satisfies

\Ric(\omega)-\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log(u(z)).

The goal of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 5.8
.

For a Fano admissible manifold
X
, the following three conditions are equivalent:

(1)

X
admits an admissible
v
-soliton.

(2)

The
v
-Futaki invariant of
X
vanishes.

(3)

{\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(x-w)u(x)p_{\Omega}(x)\,dx=0}
.

Remark 5.9
.

The equivalence in Theorem
5.8
is likely known to experts (see
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
for a related result).
However, since no explicit proof appears in the literature, we include one here for the reader’s convenience.

Before proving Theorem
5.8
, we present some corollaries.
From Example
5.2
, we immediately obtain the following, which was obtained in
[
2
]
.

Corollary 5.10

(
[
2
, Corollary 3.1]
)
.

For a Fano admissible manifold
X
, the following three conditions are equivalent:

(1)

X
admits a
G
-invariant Kähler–Einstein metric.

(2)

The Futaki invariant of
X
vanishes.

(3)

{\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(x-w)p_{\Omega}(x)\,dx=0}
.

Noting that the
v
-Futaki invariant corresponding to Mabuchi solitons always vanishes (cf. Proposition
4.6
and Remark
5.7
), Theorem
5.8
yields the following corollary.

Corollary 5.11
.

A Fano admissible manifold
X
admits a Mabuchi soliton if and only if
M_{X}<1
.

Now we shall prove Theorem
5.8
.
The following proof proceeds largely along the lines of
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
.
The implication (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) follows from Proposition
5.6
.
The equivalence of (2) and (3) is clear.
Thus, it remains to prove (3)
\Rightarrow
(1).
Let
\omega\in\Omega
be an admissible Kähler metric given by (
3.4
), and define
F\coloneqq p_{\Omega}\Theta\in C^{\infty}([-1,1],\mathbf{R})
.
Then
F
satisfies

(5.2)

\displaystyle F(x)>0\quad(x\in(-1,1)),

(5.3)

\displaystyle F(\pm 1)=0,\quad F^{\prime}(\pm 1)=\mp 2p_{\Omega}(\pm 1).

Proposition 5.12
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\omega
is a
v
-soliton.

(2)

F
satisfies

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{(uF)^{\prime}(z)}{(up_{\Omega})(z)}=-(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)(z-w).

Proof.

From the computation in Section
4.2
, on
X\setminus(e_{0}\cup e_{\infty})
,

\displaystyle\Ric(\omega)-\omega=\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\Ric(\omega_{a})-\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}c\left(\frac{1}{x_{a}}+z\right)\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}-\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log(F(z))-c(dz\wedge\theta).

Thus,
\omega
is a
v
-soliton if and only if

(5.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\Ric(\omega_{a})-\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}c\left(\frac{1}{x_{a}}+z\right)\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}-\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log(u(z)F(z))-c(dz\wedge\theta)=0.\end{split}

Computing
dd^{c}\log(u(z)F(z))
, we obtain

\displaystyle dd^{c}\log(u(z)F(z))

\displaystyle=d\left(\frac{(uF)^{\prime}(z)}{(uF)(z)}d^{c}z\right)=d\left(\frac{(uF)^{\prime}(z)}{(up_{\Omega})(z)}\theta\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(uF)^{\prime}}{up_{\Omega}}\right)^{\prime}(z)dz\wedge\theta+\frac{(uF)^{\prime}(z)}{(up_{\Omega})(z)}d\theta

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(uF)^{\prime}}{up_{\Omega}}\right)^{\prime}(z)dz\wedge\theta+\frac{(uF)^{\prime}(z)}{(up_{\Omega})(z)}\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}.

Thus, the equation (
5.5
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\left[\Ric(\omega_{a})-\left\{\frac{1}{2}\frac{(uF)^{\prime}(z)}{(up_{\Omega})(z)}+c\left(\frac{1}{x_{a}}+z\right)\right\}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left(\frac{(uF)^{\prime}}{up_{\Omega}}\right)^{\prime}(z)+2c\right\}dz\wedge\theta=0,

in other words,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\Ric(\omega_{a})-\left\{\frac{1}{2}\frac{(uF)^{\prime}(z)}{(up_{\Omega})(z)}+c\left(\frac{1}{x_{a}}+z\right)\right\}\varepsilon_{a}\omega_{a}=0\quad(a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}),

(5.7)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(uF)^{\prime}}{up_{\Omega}}\right)^{\prime}(z)+2c=0.

Furthermore, since each
\omega_{a}
is a Kähler–Einstein metric the equation (
5.6
) reduces to

(5.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{(uF)^{\prime}(z)}{(up_{\Omega})(z)}+c\left(\frac{1}{x_{a}}+z\right)=s_{a}\quad(a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}).

The equation (
5.7
) is obtained by the derivative of (
5.8
).
Thus,
\omega
is a
v
-soliton if and only if
F
satisfies (
5.8
).
As in (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle s_{a}-c\left(\frac{1}{x_{a}}+z\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[(d_{0}+1)(1-z)-(d_{\infty}+1)(1+z)]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)(z-w),

and hence (
5.8
) reduces to a single equation (
5.4
).
∎

We are now ready to prove the implication (3)
\Rightarrow
(1).
Assume
X
satisfies (3), and consider the boundary value problem

(5.9)

\displaystyle\frac{(uF)^{\prime}(x)}{(up_{\Omega})(x)}=-(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)(z-w),

(5.10)

\displaystyle F(\pm 1)=0.

It is clear that any solution of (
5.9
) is given, up to an additive constant, by

\displaystyle F(x)=-\frac{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}{u(x)}\int_{-1}^{x}(t-w)u(t)p_{\Omega}(t)\,dt.

This function
F
is smooth on the closed interval
[-1,1]
and satisfies
F(-1)=0
.
Moreover, by assumption (3), we also have
F(1)=0
.

Next, we compute the derivatives at the endpoints.
We obtain

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\pm 1)

\displaystyle=-(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)(\pm 1-w)p_{\Omega}(\pm 1)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2(d_{0}+1)p_{\Omega}(-1)&\text{if $x=-1$},\\
-2(d_{\infty}+1)p_{\Omega}(1)&\text{if $x=1$}.\end{cases}

In particular,
if
d_{0}>0
, then
p_{\Omega}(-1)=0
and hence
F^{\prime}(-1)=2p_{\Omega}(-1)=0
.
If
d_{0}=0
, then clearly
F^{\prime}(-1)=2p_{\Omega}(-1)
.
Thus, in all cases, we have
F^{\prime}(-1)=2p_{\Omega}(-1)
.
A similar argument shows that
F^{\prime}(1)=-2p_{\Omega}(1)
.
Therefore,
F
satisfies the condition (
5.3
).

We now verify that
F
satisfies the condition (
5.2
).
It suffices to show that
uF>0
on the open interval
(-1,1)
.
First note that
w\in(-1,1)
because

\displaystyle w=\frac{d_{0}-d_{\infty}}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}\geq-\frac{d_{\infty}}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}>-1,

and

\displaystyle w=\frac{d_{0}-d_{\infty}}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}\leq\frac{d_{0}}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}<1.

Let
x\in(-1,1)
arbitrarily.
If
x\leq w
, then
t-w<0
on
[-1,x)
, and hence

\displaystyle(uF)(x)=-(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)\int_{-1}^{x}(t-w)u(t)p_{\Omega}(t)\,dt>0.

If
x>w
, then
t-w>0
on
[x,1)
, and we obtain

\displaystyle(uF)(x)

\displaystyle=-(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)\left[\int_{-1}^{1}(t-w)u(t)p_{\Omega}(t)\,dt-\int_{x}^{1}(t-w)u(t)p_{\Omega}(t)\,dt\right]

\displaystyle=(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)\int_{x}^{1}(t-w)u(t)p_{\Omega}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle>0

Thus,
uF>0
holds for all
x\in(-1,1)
.

Finally, define
\Theta\colon(-1,1)\to\mathbf{R}
by
\Theta\coloneqq F/p_{\Omega}
.
It is clear that
\Theta
is a positive smooth function on the open interval
(-1,1)
, and from the expression

\displaystyle\Theta(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}{u(x)p_{\Omega}(x)}\int_{-1}^{x}(t-w)u(t)p_{\Omega}(t)\,dt,

we analyze its behavior near
x=-1
.
Since
u(x),x-w\neq 0
near
x=-1
, we can write

\displaystyle u(x)p_{\Omega}(x)=(1+x)^{d_{0}}g_{-}(x),\quad\int_{-1}^{x}(t-w)u(t)p_{\Omega}(t)\,dt=(1+x)^{d_{0}+1}h_{-}(x),

where
g_{-}
,
h_{-}
are smooth around
x=-1
and nonvanishing at
x=-1
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle(1+x)^{d_{0}}(x-w)g_{-}(x)

\displaystyle=(x-w)u(x)p_{\Omega}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{-1}^{x}(t-w)u(t)p_{\Omega}(t)\,dt\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(d_{0}+1)(1+x)^{d_{0}}h_{-}(x)+(1+x)^{d_{0}+1}h_{-}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=(1+x)^{d_{0}}\left[(d_{0}+1)h_{-}(x)+(1+x)h_{-}^{\prime}(x)\right]

and

\displaystyle(x-w)g_{-}(x)=(d_{0}+1)h_{-}(x)+(1+x)h_{-}^{\prime}(x).

This shows that

\displaystyle\Theta(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)(1+x)^{d_{0}+1}h_{-}(x)}{(1+x)^{d_{0}}g_{-}(x)}

\displaystyle=-(1+x)\frac{(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)h_{-}(x)}{g_{-}(x)},

and hence
\Theta
extends smoothly to
x=-1
with

\displaystyle\Theta(-1)=0,\quad\Theta^{\prime}(-1)=2.

A similar argument shows that
\Theta
extends smoothly to
x=1
with

\displaystyle\Theta(1)=0,\quad\Theta^{\prime}(1)=-2.

Therefore,
\Theta
satisfies all the required conditions, and hence defines an admissible Kähler metric
\omega\in\Omega
.
By construction, this metric is a
v
-soliton.
This completes the proof of Theorem
5.8
.

6.
The Mabuchi constant of Fano admissible manifolds

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
1.2
.
Let
X=\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{0}+1)}\oplus(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{\infty}+1)}))
be a Fano admissible manifold, and let
\Omega\coloneqq 2\pi c_{1}(X)
.
For
i\in\{0,1,2\}
, set

\displaystyle b_{i}\coloneqq\int_{-1}^{1}x^{i}p_{\Omega}(x)\,dx.

Since
p_{\Omega}>0
on
(-1,1)
, we have
b_{0},b_{2}>0
.
Moreover, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
b_{0}b_{2}-b_{1}^{2}>0
.

Using Propositions
4.6
and
4.7
in Section 4.2, we obtain the following.

Theorem 6.1

(
[
7
, Theorem 6.5]
for cases
d_{0}=d_{\infty}=0
)
.

Define
w\coloneqq\dfrac{d_{0}-d_{\infty}}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}
.
Then

\displaystyle M_{X}=\frac{b_{0}|b_{1}-wb_{0}|-b_{1}(b_{1}-wb_{0})}{b_{0}b_{2}-b_{1}^{2}}=1+\frac{b_{0}(|b_{1}-wb_{0}|-(b_{2}-wb_{1}))}{b_{0}b_{2}-b_{1}^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in\Omega
be an admissible Kähler metric given by (
3.4
).
Since constant functions and
z
are Killing potentials of
(X,\omega)
, Propositions
3.7
and
4.6
imply that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{X}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}=\int_{X}\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}(\alpha z+\beta)\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}=2\pi c^{d}\left(\prod_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\vol(Y_{a},\omega_{a})\right)(\alpha b_{1}+\beta b_{0}).

Hence we obtain

(6.1)

\displaystyle\alpha b_{1}+\beta b_{0}=0.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{X}z(1-e^{h_{\omega}})\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}z\Pi_{\omega}^{G}(1-e^{h_{\omega}})\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}=\int_{X}(\alpha z^{2}+\beta z)\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}

\displaystyle=2\pi c^{d}\left(\prod_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\vol(Y_{a},\omega_{a})\right)(\alpha b_{2}+\beta b_{1}).

On the other hand, by Proposition
4.7
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}z(1-e^{h_{\omega}})\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}z\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}-w\int_{X}e^{h_{\omega}}\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}=\int_{X}z\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}-w\int_{X}\frac{\omega^{d}}{d!}

\displaystyle=2\pi c^{d}\left(\prod_{a\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\vol(Y_{a},\omega_{a})\right)(b_{1}-wb_{0}).

These give us that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\alpha b_{1}+\beta b_{0}=b_{1}-wb_{0}.

By solving (
6.1
) and (
6.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{b_{0}b_{1}-wb_{0}^{2}}{b_{0}b_{2}-b_{1}^{2}},\quad\beta=-\frac{b_{1}^{2}-wb_{0}b_{1}}{b_{0}b_{2}-b_{1}^{2}},

and

\displaystyle M_{X}

\displaystyle=\max_{x\in[-1,1]}(\alpha z+\beta)=|\alpha|+\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{0}|b_{1}-wb_{0}|-b_{1}(b_{1}-wb_{0})}{b_{0}b_{2}-b_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{b_{0}(|b_{1}-wb_{0}|-(b_{2}-wb_{1}))}{b_{0}b_{2}-b_{1}^{2}},

as required.
∎

7.
Mabuchi solitons on Fano admissible manifolds over
\mathbf{P}^{n}

In the final section, we concern the existence and non-existence of Mabuchi solitons on Fano admissible manifolds over
\mathbf{P}^{n}
.
Let
X
be a Fano admissible manifold over
\mathbf{P}^{n}
.
Then
X
can be written as

(7.1)

\displaystyle X=\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{P}^{n}}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{0}+1)}\oplus\mathcal{O}(k)^{\oplus(d_{\infty}+1)})

for some
k\in\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and
d_{0},d_{\infty}\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
In that case,

\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{P}^{n}}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{0}+1)}\oplus\mathcal{O}(k)^{\oplus(d_{\infty}+1)})\cong\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{P}^{n}}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{\infty}+1)}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-k)^{\oplus(d_{0}+1)})

and so we may assume
k\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 1}
.
By Theorem
4.1
,
X
is Fano if and only if
k(d_{0}+1)<n+1
.
In what follows, we assume these conditions.
Moreover, by Corollary
5.11
, the existence of a Mabuchi soliton on
X
is equivalent to
M_{X}<1
.
The purpose of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 7.1
.

The Fano admissible manifold
X
admits a Mabuchi soliton if and only if

(k,d_{\infty})=(1,0)\quad\text{or}\quad(n,k,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(1,1,0,1).

In all other cases,
M_{X}>1
.

To prove Theorem
7.1
, we compute
M_{X}
by using Theorem
6.1
.
Let

\lambda\coloneq\frac{2(n+1)+k(d_{\infty}-d_{0})}{k(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)}.

Then, for each
i\in\{0,1,2\}
,

b_{i}=\int_{-1}^{1}(\lambda+x)^{n}(1+x)^{d_{0}}(1-x)^{d_{\infty}}x^{i}dx.

Proposition 7.2
.

b_{1}-wb_{0}>0
.
In particular, we have

M_{X}=1+\frac{b_{0}(b_{1}-wb_{0}+wb_{1}-b_{2})}{b_{0}b_{2}-b_{1}^{2}}.

Proof.

Define
a\in\mathbf{R}
by

a\coloneqq\dfrac{n+1-k(d_{0}+1)}{k(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)}.

By assumption,
a>0
and

\lambda-1=\frac{2[n+1-k(d_{0}+1)]}{k(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)}=2a.

Therefore,

\displaystyle b_{1}-wb_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}(\lambda+x)^{n}(1+x)^{d_{0}}(1-x)^{d_{\infty}}(x-w)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(\lambda+2u-1)^{n}(2u)^{d_{0}}(2-2u)^{d_{\infty}}(2u-1-w)\,du

\displaystyle=2^{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+n+2}\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n}u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}}\left(u-\frac{d_{0}+1}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}\right)\,du.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}}\left(u-\frac{d_{0}+1}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}\right)

\displaystyle=(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)u^{d_{0}+1}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}}-(d_{0}+1)u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}}[(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)u-(d_{0}+1)]

\displaystyle=u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}}[(d_{0}+1)(u-1)+(d_{\infty}+1)u]

\displaystyle=-(u^{d_{0}+1}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}+1})^{\prime}.

Hence, integration by parts yields

\displaystyle b_{1}-wb_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2^{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+n+2}}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n}(u^{d_{0}+1}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}+1})^{\prime}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{n2^{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+n+2}}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n-1}u^{d_{0}+1}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}+1}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{n2^{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+n+2}}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}a^{n-1-j}B(j+d_{0}+2,d_{\infty}+2),

where
B(x,y)
is the beta function.
Therefore,
b_{1}-wb_{0}>0
follows from
a>0
.
The second assertion follows immediately from Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Using Corollary
5.10
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 7.3
.

For any
k\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
d_{0},d_{\infty}\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
X=\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{P}^{n}}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus(d_{0}+1)}\oplus\mathcal{O}(k)^{\oplus(d_{\infty}+1)})
does not admit a Kähler–Einstein metric.

Now, set

I\coloneq b_{1}-wb_{0}+wb_{1}-b_{2}.

Then

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}(\lambda+x)^{n}(1+x)^{d_{0}}(1-x)^{d_{\infty}+1}(x-w)dx

holds.
Moreover, the condition
M_{X}\leq 1
(resp.
M_{X}=1
) is equivalent to
I\leq 0
(resp.
I=0
).

Lemma 7.4
.

If the following inequality holds, then
M_{X}>1
holds:

(7.2)

\frac{n(d_{\infty}+1)-(d_{0}+1)}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4}\geq\frac{n+1-k(d_{0}+1)}{k(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)}.

In particular, in that case
X
does not admit a Mabuchi soliton.

Proof.

Define
b\in\mathbf{R}
by

b\coloneqq\frac{d_{0}+1}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}.

Then
b>0
, and

w+1=\frac{2(d_{0}+1)}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}=2b.

Moreover,

(7.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I&=\int_{0}^{1}(\lambda+2u-1)^{n}(2u)^{d_{0}}(2-2u)^{d_{\infty}+1}(2u-(w+1))2\,du\\
&=2^{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+n+3}\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n}u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}+1}(u-b)\,du\end{split}

holds, where
a
is defined in the proof of Proposition
7.2
.
Since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n}u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}+1}(u-b)du

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}a^{n-j}\left[\int_{0}^{1}u^{j+d_{0}+1}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}+1}du-b\int_{0}^{1}u^{j+d_{0}}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}+1}du\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}a^{n-j}[B(j+d_{0}+2,d_{\infty}+2)-bB(j+d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+2)]

and

\displaystyle B(j+d_{0}+2,d_{\infty}+2)-bB(j+d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+2)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{j+d_{0}+1}{(j+d_{0}+1)+(d_{\infty}+2)}-\frac{d_{0}+1}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}\right]B(j+d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+2)

\displaystyle=\frac{(d_{\infty}+1)j-(d_{0}+1)}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}\frac{B(j+d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+2)}{j+d_{0}+d_{\infty}+3}

\displaystyle=\frac{(d_{\infty}+1)j-(d_{0}+1)}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2}\frac{B(j+d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+3)}{d_{\infty}+2},

we have

I=\frac{2^{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+n+3}}{(d_{0}+2)(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}a^{n-j}[(d_{\infty}+1)j-(d_{0}+1)]B(j+d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+3).

Therefore, if we set

S_{0}\coloneq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}a^{n-j}B(d_{0}+1+j,d_{\infty}+3),\quad S_{1}\coloneq\sum_{j=0}^{n}j\binom{n}{j}a^{n-j}B(d_{0}+1+j,d_{\infty}+3),

then, since

I=\frac{2^{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+n+3}}{(d_{0}+2)(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)}[(d_{\infty}+1)S_{1}-(d_{0}+1)S_{0}]

and
S_{0}>0
, the condition
I\geq 0
(resp.
I=0
) is equivalent to

(7.4)

\displaystyle\frac{S_{1}}{S_{0}}\geq\frac{d_{0}+1}{d_{\infty}+1}\quad\left(\text{resp. }\frac{S_{1}}{S_{0}}=\frac{d_{0}+1}{d_{\infty}+1}\right).

Since

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}a^{n-j}\int_{0}^{1}t^{j+d_{0}}(1-t)^{d_{\infty}+2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}t^{d_{0}}(1+t)^{d_{\infty}+2}(a+t)^{n}dt

\displaystyle=a\int_{0}^{1}t^{d_{0}}(1+t)^{d_{\infty}+2}(a+t)^{n-1}dt+\int_{0}^{1}t^{d_{0}+1}(1+t)^{d_{\infty}+2}(a+t)^{n-1}dt,

using

\displaystyle nx(x+y)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x+y)^{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}j\binom{n}{j}x^{j}y^{n-j},

we obtain

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}j\binom{n}{j}a^{n-j}\int_{0}^{1}t^{d_{0}+j}(1-t)^{d_{\infty}+2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}t^{d_{0}}(1-t)^{d_{\infty}+2}nt(a+t)^{n-1}dt

\displaystyle=n\int_{0}^{1}t^{d_{0}+1}(1-t)^{d_{\infty}+2}(a+t)^{n-1}dt.

Now let us set

K\coloneq\int_{0}^{1}t^{d_{0}+1}(1-t)^{d_{\infty}+2}(a+t)^{n-1}dt,\quad L\coloneq\int_{0}^{1}t^{d_{0}}(1-t)^{d_{\infty}+2}(a+t)^{n-1}dt.

Then
K,L>0
, and

\frac{S_{1}}{S_{0}}=\frac{nK}{aL+K}

Define
f\colon(0,+\infty)\to\mathbf{R}
by

f(t)\coloneq\frac{nt}{aL+t}.

Then, since
aL>0
,
f
is a strictly increasing function.
Next, if we set

g(t)\coloneq t^{d_{0}}(1-t)^{d_{\infty}+2},\quad h(t)\coloneq(a+t)^{n-1},

then

\displaystyle K=\int_{0}^{1}tg(t)h(t)\,dt,\quad L=\int_{0}^{1}g(t)h(t)\,dt,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}g(t)\,dt=B(d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+3),\quad\int_{0}^{1}tg(t)\,dt=B(d_{0}+2,d_{\infty}+3)

hold.
Here, we investigate the sign of

B(d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+3)K-B(d_{0}+2,d_{\infty}+3)L.

•

In the case
n=1
.

Since
h(t)=1
, we have
K=B(d_{0}+2,d_{\infty}+3),L=B(d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+3)
and hence
B(d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+3)K-B(d_{0}+2,d_{\infty}+3)L=0
.

•

In the case
n\geq 2
.

Since
h^{\prime}(t)=(n-1)(a+t)^{n-2}>0
,
h
is strictly increasing on
[0,1]
, and

\displaystyle B(d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+3)K-B(d_{0}+2,d_{\infty}+3)L

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}tg(t)h(t)\,dt\int_{0}^{1}g(t)\,dt-\int_{0}^{1}g(t)h(t)dt\int_{0}^{1}tg(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}tg(t)h(t)\,dt\int_{0}^{1}g(s)\,ds-\int_{0}^{1}g(t)h(t)\,dt\int_{0}^{1}sg(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(t-s)h(t)g(s)g(t)\,dsdt

holds.
By a change of variables, we have

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(t-s)h(t)g(s)g(t)\,dsdt=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(s-t)h(s)g(t)g(s)\,dsdt

and

\displaystyle B(d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+3)K-B(d_{0}+2,d_{\infty}+3)L

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}((t-s)h(t)+(s-t)h(s))g(s)g(t)\,dsdt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(h(s)-h(t))(s-t)g(s)g(t)\,dsdt.

Therefore, since
(h(s)-h(t))(s-t)\geq 0
, we have
B(d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+3)K-B(d_{0}+2,d_{\infty}+3)L\geq 0
.

From the above,

K\geq\frac{B(d_{0}+2,d_{\infty}+3)}{B(d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+3)}L=\frac{d_{0}+1}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4}L

holds, and so, by the monotonicity of
f
,

\frac{S_{1}}{S_{0}}=\frac{nK}{aL+K}\geq\frac{n(d_{0}+1)}{a(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4)+d_{0}+1}

follows.
Therefore, if (
7.2
) holds, then

\displaystyle\frac{n(d_{0}+1)}{a(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4)+(d_{0}+1)}\geq\frac{d_{0}+1}{d_{\infty}+1},

and so (
7.4
) follows.
In particular, since
I\geq 0
, we have
M_{X}\geq 1
.
Furthermore, the following holds.

(1)

In the case
n=1
.

Since
k(d_{0}+1)<n+1
, we have
n=k=1
and
d_{0}=0
.
Hence, it holds

a=\frac{n+1-k(d_{0}+1)}{k(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)}=\frac{1}{d_{\infty}+2}

and
B(d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+3)K-B(d_{0}+2,d_{\infty}+3)L=0
.
In particular, we have

K=\frac{d_{0}+1}{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4}L.

Here, if we assume
M_{X}=1
, then
I=0
, and so by (
7.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d_{0}+1}{d_{\infty}+1}=\frac{S_{1}}{S_{0}}=\frac{nK}{aL+K}=\frac{n(d_{0}+1)}{a(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4)+(d_{0}+1)}.

Rearrangement of this gives us

\displaystyle d_{\infty}^{2}+d_{\infty}-4=0,

which contradicts
d_{\infty}\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Therefore,
M_{X}>1
.

(2)

In the case
n\geq 2
.

Since
h
is a strictly increasing function,
s=t
is the only possibility for
s,t\in[0,1]
satisfying
(h(s)-h(t))(s-t)=0
.
Hence the set on which the integrand vanishes has measure zero in
[0,1]\times[0,1]
, and therefore

\displaystyle B(d_{0}+1,d_{\infty}+3)K-B(d_{0}+2,d_{\infty}+3)L

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(h(s)-h(t))(s-t)g(s)g(t)dsdt>0

and hence

\frac{S_{1}}{S_{0}}=\frac{nK}{aL+K}>\frac{n(d_{0}+1)}{a(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4)+(d_{0}+1)}\geq\frac{d_{0}+1}{d_{\infty}+1}.

This and the equivalence in (
7.4
) gives us that
M_{X}>1
.

From the above, we see that
M_{X}>1
holds in either case.
∎

7.1.
The case
k\geq 2
or
d_{\infty}\geq 1

We first show a non-existence result for Mabuchi solitons.

Proposition 7.5
.

If
k\geq 2
, or
k=1
and
d_{\infty}\geq 1
, then
M_{X}>1
holds, except in the cases
(n,k,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(1,1,0,1)
and
(n,k,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(2,1,0,1)
. In particular,
X
does not admit a Mabuchi soliton.

Proof.

First note that the inequality (
7.2
) in Lemma
7.4
is equivalent to

(7.5)

\displaystyle\left[(d_{\infty}+1)(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)-\frac{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4}{k}\right]n-\frac{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4}{k}+2(d_{0}+1)\geq 0.

Define
f\colon[1,+\infty)\to\mathbf{R}
by

f(t)\coloneq\left[(d_{\infty}+1)(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)-\frac{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4}{k}\right]t-\frac{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4}{k}+2(d_{0}+1).

(1)

The case
k\geq 2
.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle(d_{\infty}+1)(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)-\frac{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4}{k}

\displaystyle\geq(d_{\infty}+1)(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)-\frac{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4}{2}

\displaystyle=\left(d_{\infty}+\frac{1}{2}\right)(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)-1

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
(k,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(2,0,0)
.

(a)

The case
(k,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(2,0,0)
.

Since

f(t)=\left(1\cdot 2-\frac{4}{2}\right)t-\frac{4}{2}+2=0,

the inequality (
7.2
) holds for any
n\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 1}
.

(b)

Cases other than (a).

Since
k\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle f(1)

\displaystyle=(d_{\infty}+1)(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)-\frac{2(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4)}{k}+2(d_{0}+1)

\displaystyle\geq(d_{\infty}+1)(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)-(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4)+2(d_{0}+1)

\displaystyle=d_{\infty}(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)+2d_{0}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Therefore, since
n\geq 2
and
f
is monotonically increasing, the inequality (
7.2
) is maintained by
f(n)\geq f(1)\geq 0
.

(2)

The case
k=1
.

First note that

(d_{\infty}+1)(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)-(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4)=d_{\infty}(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)-2>0

and hence
f
is a monotonically increasing function.

(a)

The case
d_{0}\geq 1
.

Since

\displaystyle f(n)>f(1)

\displaystyle=(d_{\infty}+1)(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)-2(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+4)+2(d_{0}+1)

\displaystyle=(d_{\infty}-1)(d_{0}+d_{\infty}+2)+2(d_{0}-1)\geq 0,

the inequality (
7.2
) holds.

(b)

The case
d_{0}=0
.

If
d_{\infty}\geq 2
, then the inequality (
7.2
) holds from
f(1)\geq 3\cdot(0+2+2)-2>0
.
If
d_{\infty}=1
, then

f(3)=3\left((1+1)(0+1+2)-\frac{0+1+4}{1}\right)-\frac{0+1+4}{1}+2(0+1)=0,

and so, except in the cases
(n,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(1,0,1)
and
(n,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(2,0,1)
, the inequality (
7.2
) holds.

Therefore, if
k\geq 2
, or
k=1
and
d_{\infty}\geq 1
, then
M_{X}>1
holds, except in the cases
(n,k,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(1,1,0,1)
and
(n,k,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(2,1,0,1)
. In particular,
X
does not admit a Mabuchi soliton.
∎

Proposition 7.6
.

If
(n,k,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(1,1,0,1)
, then
X
admits a Mabuchi soliton.
On the other hand, when
(n,k,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(2,1,0,1)
,
M_{X}>1
and hence
X
does not admit a Mabuchi soliton.

Proof.

First, assume that
(n,k,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(1,1,0,1)
.
Then, since

\lambda=\frac{2(1+1)/1+1-0}{0+1+2}=\frac{5}{3},\quad w=\frac{0-1}{0+1+2}=-\frac{1}{3},

we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\frac{5}{3}+x\right)(1-x)^{2}\left(x+\frac{1}{3}\right)dx=\int_{-1}^{1}\left(x^{4}-\frac{22}{9}x^{2}+\frac{8}{9}x+\frac{5}{9}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{5}x^{5}-\frac{22}{27}x^{3}+\frac{5}{9}x\right]_{-1}^{1}=-\frac{16}{135}<0.

In particular,
M_{X}<1
and
X
admits a Mabuchi soliton by Corollary
5.11
.
Next, if
(n,k,d_{0},d_{\infty})=(2,1,0,1)
, then, since

\lambda=\frac{2(2+1)/1+1-0}{0+1+2}=\frac{7}{3},\quad w=\frac{0-1}{0+1+2}=-\frac{1}{3},

we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\frac{7}{3}+x\right)^{2}(1-x)^{2}\left(x+\frac{1}{3}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\left(x^{5}+3x^{4}-2x^{3}-\frac{194}{27}x^{2}+\frac{91}{27}x+\frac{49}{27}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{3}{5}x^{5}-\frac{194}{81}x^{3}+\frac{49}{27}x\right]_{-1}^{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{16}{405}

\displaystyle>0.

This shows
M_{X}>1
and that
X
does not admit a Mabuchi soliton.
∎

7.2.
The case
k=1
and
d_{\infty}=0

Next we show a result of existence for Mabuchi solitons.
The following proposition completes the proof of Theorem
7.1
.

Proposition 7.7
.

If
k=1
and
d_{\infty}=0
, then
X
admits a Mabuchi soliton.

Proof.

Let
a
and
b
be real numbers defined in the proofs of Proposition
7.2
and Lemma
7.4
, respectively.
Then, by (
7.3
) in Lemma
7.4
, we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=2^{d_{0}+d_{\infty}+n+3}\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n}u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{d_{\infty}+1}(u-b)du

\displaystyle=2^{d_{0}+n+3}\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n}u^{d_{0}}(1-u)(u-b)du,

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)-(d_{0}+1)}{d_{0}+2}=\frac{n-d_{0}}{d_{0}+2},\quad b=\frac{d_{0}+1}{d_{0}+2}.

Here, by setting

\alpha\coloneqq-\frac{1}{2(d_{0}+2)},\quad\beta\coloneqq-\frac{d_{0}+1}{2(d_{0}+2)},

we obtain

u^{d_{0}}(1-u)(u-b)=\alpha\left(\frac{d}{du}u^{d_{0}+1}(1-u)^{2}\right)+\beta u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{2}.

Therefore, by integration by parts,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=2^{d_{0}+n+3}\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n}\left[\alpha\Bigl(\frac{d}{du}u^{d_{0}+1}(1-u)^{2}\Bigr)+\beta u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{2}\right]du

\displaystyle=2^{d_{0}+n+3}\left\{\Bigl[\alpha(a+u)^{n}u^{d_{0}+1}(1-u)^{2}\Bigr]_{0}^{1}-\int_{0}^{1}\alpha n(a+u)^{n-1}u^{d_{0}+1}(1-u)^{2}du\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+2^{d_{0}+n+3}\int_{0}^{1}\beta(a+u)^{n}u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{2}du

\displaystyle=2^{d_{0}+n+3}\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n-1}u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{2}(-\alpha nu+\beta(a+u))du

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{d_{0}+n+2}}{d_{0}+2}\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n-1}u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{2}[(n-d_{0}-1)u-a(d_{0}+1)]du.

Thus, when
n-d_{0}-1=0
, we have

I=-\frac{2^{d_{0}+n+2}}{d_{0}+2}\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n-1}u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{2}a(d_{0}+1)du\,<0

and hence
M_{X}<1
, which shows that
X
admits a Mabuchi soliton.
From now on, assume
n-d_{0}-1>0
.
If we set

c\coloneq\frac{a(d_{0}+1)}{n-d_{0}-1}=\frac{(n-d_{0})(d_{0}+1)}{(n-d_{0}-1)(d_{0}+2)},

we have
c>0
and

I=\frac{2^{d_{0}+n+2}(n-d_{0}-1)}{d_{0}+2}\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n-1}u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{2}(u-c)\,du.

Hence, by setting

J\coloneq\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n-1}u^{d_{0}+1}(1-u)^{2}du,\quad K\coloneq\int_{0}^{1}(a+u)^{n-1}u^{d_{0}}(1-u)^{2}du,

we have
I=J-cK
, and
I\leq 0
(resp.
I=0
) if and only if

(7.6)

\displaystyle\frac{J}{K}\leq c\quad\left(\text{resp. }\frac{J}{K}=c\right).

In what follows, we show that
\dfrac{J}{K}<c
.
First,

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}a^{n-1-j}\int_{0}^{1}u^{j+d_{0}+1}(1-u)^{2}du

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}a^{n-1-j}B(j+d_{0}+2,3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}a^{n-1-j}\frac{2}{(j+d_{0}+2)(j+d_{0}+3)(j+d_{0}+4)},

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}a^{n-1-j}\int_{0}^{1}u^{j+d_{0}}(1-u)^{2}du

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}a^{n-1-j}B(j+d_{0}+1,3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}a^{n-1-j}\frac{2}{(j+d_{0}+1)(j+d_{0}+2)(j+d_{0}+3)}.

Hence, by setting

p_{j}\coloneq\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}a^{n-1-j},\quad q_{j}\coloneq\frac{2}{(j+d_{0}+1)(j+d_{0}+2)(j+d_{0}+3)}

we have

J=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}p_{j}q_{j}\frac{j+d_{0}+1}{j+d_{0}+4},\quad K=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}p_{i}q_{i}.

Next, if we set
r_{j}\coloneq\dfrac{1}{K}p_{j}q_{j}
, then
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}r_{j}=1
and

\frac{J}{K}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}r_{j}\frac{j+d_{0}+1}{j+d_{0}+4}.

Define
f\colon[0,+\infty)\to\mathbf{R}
by
f(t)\coloneq\dfrac{t+d_{0}+1}{t+d_{0}+4}
.
Then
f
is a strictly increasing concave function.
Hence we have

\frac{J}{K}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}r_{j}f(j)\leq f\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}r_{j}j\right).

Here, we point out that

\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}r_{j}j\leq\frac{\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}p_{j}j}{\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}p_{i}}

holds.
In fact, since
p_{j}\geq 0
and
q_{j}
is monotonically decreasing in
j
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}p_{j}q_{j}j\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}p_{i}-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}p_{j}j\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}p_{i}q_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}j(q_{j}-q_{i})p_{j}p_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}j(q_{j}-q_{i})p_{j}p_{i}+\sum_{j<i}j(q_{j}-q_{i})p_{j}p_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}j(q_{j}-q_{i})p_{j}p_{i}+\sum_{i<j}i(q_{i}-q_{j})p_{i}p_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}(j-i)(q_{j}-q_{i})p_{j}p_{i}\leq 0.

Hence,

\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}r_{j}j=\frac{\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}p_{j}q_{j}j}{\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}p_{i}q_{i}}\leq\frac{\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}p_{j}j}{\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}p_{i}}.

Furthermore, since

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}p_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}a^{n-1-i}=(1+a)^{n-1},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}p_{j}j=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}j\cdot a^{n-1-j}=(n-1)(1+a)^{n-2}

and
f
is monotonically increasing,

\frac{J}{K}\leq f\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}r_{j}j\right)\leq f\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}p_{j}j}{\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}p_{i}}\right)=f\left(\frac{n-1}{1+a}\right).

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{n-1}{1+a}\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(\frac{(d_{0}+2)(n-1)}{n+2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2d_{0}+3)n+d_{0}}{2(d_{0}+3)n+d_{0}+6}

and

(7.7)

\displaystyle\frac{J}{K}\leq\frac{(2d_{0}+3)n+d_{0}}{2(d_{0}+3)n+d_{0}+6}.

follows.
Finally, let us show

(7.8)

\displaystyle\frac{(2d_{0}+3)n+d_{0}}{2(d_{0}+3)n+(d_{0}+6)}<c.

First, by a direct computation, the condition (
7.8
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle d_{0}n^{2}+(d_{0}^{2}+12d_{0}+12)n-4d_{0}(d_{0}+1)>0.

Moreover, since
d_{0}+1<n
, we have in fact

\displaystyle d_{0}n^{2}+(d_{0}^{2}+12d_{0}+12)n-4d_{0}(d_{0}+1)

\displaystyle>d_{0}n^{2}+(d_{0}^{2}+12d_{0}+12)n-4d_{0}n

\displaystyle=n(d_{0}n+d_{0}^{2}+8d_{0}+12)

\displaystyle>0.

Therefore, by (
7.8
) and (
7.7
) we obtain

\frac{J}{K}\leq\frac{(2d_{0}+3)n+d_{0}}{2(d_{0}+3)n+(d_{0}+6)}<c.

Hence, by (
7.6
), we obtain
I<0
, namely
M_{X}<1
.
∎
