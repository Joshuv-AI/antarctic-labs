Tangle structure trees II: trees of tangles and tangle-tree duality
Hanno von Bergen and Reinhard Diestel

Abstract
. Tangle structure trees, introduced in [ 2 ] , offer a unified data structure that displays all the tangles of a graph or data set together with certificates for the non-existence of any other tangles, either locally or overall. In this paper we apply tangle structure trees to derive new versions of the two fundamental tangle theorems: the tree-of-tangles theorem, and the tangle-tree duality theorem. We extend the tree-of-tangles theorem to ℱ \mathcal{F} -tangles that need not be profiles. When ℱ \mathcal{F} consists of stars of separations, as it does in classical tangle-tree duality theorems, we show how to convert tangle structure trees that certify the non-existence of ℱ \mathcal{F} -tangles into tree-decompositions that certify this in the way known from graph tangles, as S S -trees over ℱ \mathcal{F} .

The unified way in which tangle structure trees serve both these purposes still allows us to extract, if desired, separate corollaries of the two types. In

\mathcal{F}\!
-tangle structure tree of
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
. It is unique if the order function on
S
is injective.

Note that the uniqueness part of
Theorem

3.3
does not require the existence part: an injectively ordered separation system clearly has at most one thoroughly ordered
\mathcal{F}
-tangle structure tree, regardless of what
\mathcal{F}
is.
In particular,
\mathcal{F}
need not be standard or rich. But we know from
[
2
, Theorem 4.8]
that
\mathcal{F}
, assuming it is standard, will be rich for
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
if the order function on
S
is injective and an
\mathcal{F}
-tangle structure tree exists.

Thoroughly ordered structure trees can be large. But there is a way to reduce them. Given an
\mathcal{F}
-tangle structure tree
(T,r,\beta)
, consider an edge
vw
of
T
such that
w
is a child of
v
. We call
vw

necessary
for a tangle leaf
\ell\geq w
of
T
if
\beta(vw)
is a
\leq
-minimal element of the tangle
\lfloor\beta_{\ell}\rfloor
, or equivalently, of
\beta_{\ell}
. We call
vw

necessary
for a forbidden leaf
\ell\geq w
if every subset of
\beta_{\ell}
in
\mathcal{F}
contains
\beta(vw)
. A node
v
of
T
is
necessary in
T
if for every child
w
of
v
there exists a leaf
\ell\geqslant w
such that
vw
is necessary for
\ell
. If every node of
T
is necessary, then
T
is
irreducible
.

It is easy to make a tangle structure tree irreducible by contracting edges at unnecessary nodes and deleting certain subtrees
[
2
, Section 5]
. This preserves efficiency
and being ordered, but not being thoroughly ordered. Our second existence theorem for
\mathcal{F}
-tangle structure trees gains efficiency and irreducibility over that from
Theorem

3.3
at the expense of losing the thoroughness of its ordering:

Theorem 3.4

(
[
2
, Theorem 6.1]
)
.

Let
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
be an ordered separation system, and let
\mathcal{F}
be any set that is rich and standard for
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
. Then
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
has an efficient and irreducible ordered
\mathcal{F}
-tangle structure tree.

See
[
2
, Theorem 6.2]
for a long list of properties of these
\mathcal{F}
-tangle structure trees that make them useful for tangle analysis, both in theory and in applications.

4.
Tangle-tree duality

If we apply
Theorem

3.4
to a separation system
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
that has no
\mathcal{F}
-tangles, it tells us that there is an
\mathcal{F}
-tangle structure tree all whose leaves are forbidden. Let us call such structure trees
\mathcal{F}
-trees
. Conversely, if there is an
\mathcal{F}
-tree of
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
then
S
has no
\mathcal{F}
-tangle, by
Theorem

3.1
.

Theorem

3.4
thus implies
the following dichotomy:

Theorem 4.1

(
[
2
, Theorem 6.4]
)
.

Let
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
be an ordered separation system, and let
\mathcal{F}
be standard and rich for
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
. Then exactly one of the following assertions holds:

(i)

there exists an
\mathcal{F}
-tangle of
S
;

(ii)

there exists an
\mathcal{F}
-tree of
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
.

In the case of
(ii)
, the
\mathcal{F}
-tree can be chosen to be ordered, irreducible and efficient.

Note that, unlike in all known theorems of this type, the set
\mathcal{F}
of forbidden subsets need not consist of stars of separations (see
Section

2
).

In the special case that
\mathcal{F}
does consist of stars of separations, we shall see in
Corollary

4.5
that – as long as the
\mathcal{F}
-tree in
(ii)
is irreducible, which the theorem says we may require – the separations in
\beta(E(T))\subseteq{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
will be nested. In contexts where separation systems describe genuine separations, of a set or of a graph, say, nested sets of separations are known to cut up the structure they separate in a tree-like way – for example, by a tree-decomposition of the graph. In this way,
\mathcal{F}
-trees impose a tree structure on such a set or graph, which in turn implies that its separations we are considering have no
\mathcal{F}
-tangle.

This phenomenon is one of the corner stones of classical tangle theory, known as
tangle-tree duality
. Our aim now is to derive such a dichotomy theorem also in our context, as an application of our more comprehensive tangle structure trees. Our version,
Corollary

4.6
below, will not imply the classical tangle-tree duality theorem for abstract separation systems
[
7
]
, because our richness assumptions made for
\mathcal{F}
are different. But it will offer the same dichotomy. And we shall see in
[
3
]
that it implies all known instances of the classical theorem for concrete structures, such as graphs or matroids.

To carry this out formally, let us introduce the terms in which classical tangle-tree duality is cast. Let
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T)
denote the set of orientations of the edges of a tree
T
. For an oriented edge
\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt=(t,t^{\prime})
of
T
we call
t=:i(\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt)
its
initial node
and
t^{\prime}=:t(\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt)
its
terminal node
, and we denote its inverse
(t^{\prime},t)
by
\kern-1.0pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 0.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt
. We think of
t(\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt)
as the node that
\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt

points towards
. There is a natural partial ordering
\leq
on
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T)
in which
\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt>\kern-2.0pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptf}\limits^{\kern 2.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt
if
f\neq e
and the unique path in
T
from
e
to
f
starts at
t(\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt)
and ends at
i(\kern-2.0pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptf}\limits^{\kern 2.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt)
.
With the involution
{}^{*}\colon\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt\mapsto\kern-1.0pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 0.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt
, this makes
({\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T),\leq,^{*})
into a separation system.

Since every two edges
e,f
of
T
have comparable orientations, the elements of
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T)
are all nested. And for every node
t
of
T
, the set

{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptF}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}\!_{t}:=\{\,\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt\in{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T):t(\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt)=t\,\}

is a star in this separation system.

Now let
({\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt},\leq,^{*})
be any other separation system – typically, but not necessarily, consisting of separations of a set or graph. An
S
-tree

(T,\alpha)
is a tree
T
together with a map
\alpha\colon{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T)\to{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
that satisfies
\alpha(\kern-1.0pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 0.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt)=\alpha(\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt)^{*}
for every
\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt\in{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T)
. It is an
S
-tree
over
a set
\mathcal{F}
if
\alpha({\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptF}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}\!_{t})\in\mathcal{F}
for every
t\in V(T)
.

If
\alpha
preserves the partial ordering
\leq
on
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T)
, i.e. if
\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt\leq\kern-2.0pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptf}\limits^{\kern 2.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt
implies
\alpha(\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt)\leq\alpha(\kern-2.0pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptf}\limits^{\kern 2.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt)
,
and the image of
\alpha
contains no degenerate separations, then the sets
\alpha({\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptF}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}\!_{t})\in\mathcal{F}
will be stars in
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
, because the
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptF}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}\!_{t}
are stars in
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T)
.
Conversely:

Lemma 4.2

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
(T,\alpha)
be an
S
-tree over a set stars.
If
\alpha
is injective on
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptF}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}\!_{t}
for every node
t
of
T
, then
\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt\leq\kern-2.0pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptf}\limits^{\kern 2.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt
implies
\alpha(\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt)\leq\alpha(\kern-2.0pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptf}\limits^{\kern 2.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt)
for all
\kern-1.5pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0pte}\limits^{\kern 1.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt,\kern-2.0pt{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptf}\limits^{\kern 2.5pt\raise 0.60275pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}\kern-1.0pt\in{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T)
. In particular,
\alpha({\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T))
is a nested subset of
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
.

Conversely, for every nested, regular, finite separation system
({\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt},\leq,^{*})
there is an
S
-tree
(T,\alpha)
over stars such that
\alpha({\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T))={\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
and
\alpha
is injective on the stars
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptF}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}\!_{t}
.

We remark that if
\alpha
is not injective on some
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptF}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}\!_{t}
, then
\alpha({\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T))
can fail to be nested.

Lemma

4.2
motivates the study of
S
-trees over sets
\mathcal{F}
of stars (of separations): they make precise, in a more general way than tree-decompositions do, the ‘tree-like way’ in which nested sets of separations cut up any underlying structure.

The existence of an
S
-tree
(T,\alpha)
over a set
\mathcal{F}
, not necessarily consisting of stars, precludes the existence of an
\mathcal{F}
-tangle of
S
.
Indeed, given any orientation
\tau
of
S
, the set
\alpha^{-1}(\tau)

is an orientation of the edges of
T
. Since trees contain no cycles, this orientation will contain
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptF}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}\!_{t}
for some
t
, so that
\alpha({\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptF}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}\!_{t})\subseteq\tau
. But as
(T,\alpha)
is an
S
-tree over
\mathcal{F}
we have
\alpha({\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptF}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}\!_{t})\in\mathcal{F}
, so
\tau
is not an
\mathcal{F}
-tangle.

The converse implication, that there is an
S
-tree over
\mathcal{F}
whenever
S
has no
\mathcal{F}
-tangle, holds only when
\mathcal{F}
consists of stars. But assuming this is not enough. In
[
7
]
, which offers the most general theorem of this type to date, there is the further assumption that
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
must be
\mathcal{F}
-separable
. This is a richness condition on
\mathcal{F}
, different from ours, which requires
\mathcal{F}
to also contain stars which its elements ‘induce on the sides’ of certain separations in
S
. The precise notion of
\mathcal{F}
-separability is quite technical, but immaterial here, so we refer the reader to
[
7
,
6
]
for details.

The following dichotomy between
\mathcal{F}
-tangles and
S
-trees over
\mathcal{F}
is the classical tangle-tree duality theorem for abstract separation systems:

Theorem 4.3
.

[
7
]

Let
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
be a separation system inside a universe
{\vec{U}}
of separations,
and let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.32289pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}}\!

be a set of stars that is standard for
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
.
If
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
is
\mathcal{F}
-separable, then exactly one of the following assertions holds:

(i)

there exists an
\mathcal{F}
-tangle of
S
;

(ii)

there exists an
S
-tree over
\mathcal{F}
.

When
\mathcal{F}
consists of stars, we can compare
Theorem

4.3
with what our more general
Theorem

4.1
says for such
\mathcal{F}
. Both theorems guarantee that if
S
admits no
\mathcal{F}
-tangle then it has an easily detectable certificate in the form of a certain tree-like structure: an
S
-tree over
\mathcal{F}
in the case of
Theorem

4.3
, and an
\mathcal{F}
-tree of
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
in the case of our new
Theorem

4.1
.

The following result shows that our
Theorem

4.1
is not only more general, because its
\mathcal{F}
need not consist of stars, but also stronger than
Theorem

4.3
, because we can obtain
S
-trees over
\mathcal{F}
from
\mathcal{F}
-trees when
\mathcal{F}
does consist of stars. In fact,
every
irreducible
\mathcal{F}
-tree of
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
then has the property that its edge labels in
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
are precisely the edge labels of some
S
-tree over
\mathcal{F}
:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
be a separation system without trivial elements,
{\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.32289pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}}}\!\!
a set of stars, and
(T,r,\beta)
an irreducible
\mathcal{F}
-tree of
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
. Then there exists an
S
-tree
(T^{\prime},\alpha)
over
\mathcal{F}
and a map
\gamma\colon V(T^{\prime})\cup{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptE}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}(T^{\prime})\to V(T)\cup E(T)
such that

(i)

\gamma
is a bijection from the nodes of
T^{\prime}
to the leaves of
T
;

(ii)

\gamma
is a bijection from the oriented edges of
T^{\prime}
to the edges of

Submodular order functions

Submodular order functions are a key ingredient to much of tangle theory. We shall need them in this paper too, both for our tree-of-tangles theorems in

[
7
]
in

7

In both these applications we shall need tangle structure trees that are not just ordered but thoroughly ordered. These exist reliably only when our order function is injective. If it is not -- which often happens in theoretical applications

8

8

Order functions used for real-world tangle applications, such as in clustering, tend to be injective. But the standard order function on graph separations, for example, is not.

– we have to tweak it slightly to make it so. We shall prove in this section that we can do this while preserving its submodularity.

We say that an order function

on a set
S

refines

o(r)<o(s)\ \Rightarrow\ o^{\prime}(r)<o^{\prime}(s)\quad(\forall r,s\in S).

Thus,

implies

. But we do not have the same implication for ‘

need not be constant on the subsets of

whose elements have a common value under

.

Let
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
be a separation system with an order function

, and let
\mathcal{F}
be any set. Recall that the
\mathcal{F}
-tangles of the subsets

of

are the
\mathcal{F}
-tangles
in

{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}
with respect to
o
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
o^{\prime}
refines

, then the tangles in
{\hskip-1.0pt{{{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptS}\limits^{\kern 3.0pt\raise 0.45206pt\vbox to0.0pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow$}\vss}}}}}\hskip-1.0pt}

If
