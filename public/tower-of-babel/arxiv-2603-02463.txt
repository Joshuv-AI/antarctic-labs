Error analysis of scalar auxiliary variable finite element methods for the Landau–Lifshitz–Bloch equation
Agus L. Soenjaya

Abstract
. The Landau–Lifshitz–Bloch (LLB) equation is a well-established micromagnetic model for describing magnetisation dynamics in ferromagnets at elevated temperatures. In this paper, we propose and analyse two fully discrete, conforming finite element schemes based on the scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) approach for solving the LLB equation in the high-temperature regime above the Curie point. The first scheme employs a semi-implicit Euler time discretisation, while the second is based on a linearly extrapolated BDF2 method. Both schemes are linear, unconditionally stable with respect to the energy norm, and satisfy a discrete energy law involving the SAV-based energy functional that approximates the true micromagnetic energy. Under suitable regularity assumptions, we establish unconditional energy stability and derive optimal-order error estimates in 𝕃 2 , ℍ 1 \mathbb{L}^{2},\mathbb{H}^{1} , and 𝕃 ∞ \mathbb{L}^{\infty} norms. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first rigorous error analysis of a fully discrete SAV-based method for a quasilinear vector-valued problem, as well as the first linear, energy-stable scheme for the LLB equation in the high-temperature regime that achieves second-order temporal accuracy.

1.
Introduction

The Landau–Lifshitz–Bloch (LLB) equation is a fundamental model for describing the dynamics of magnetisation in ferromagnetic materials at elevated temperatures
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
11
]
, particularly near or above the Curie temperature, where the standard Landau–Lifshitz model becomes inadequate. The LLB equation incorporates both transverse and longitudinal fluctuation mechanisms, making it well-suited for modelling thermally induced magnetisation dynamics in heat-assisted magnetic recording (HAMR), for instance. Its nonlinear structure, however, presents considerable analytical and numerical challenges. Therefore, the development and analysis of stable and accurate numerical methods for solving the LLB equation are crucial for both theoretical understanding and practical applications in computational micromagnetics.

We will introduce the model next. Let
\mathscr{D}
be a bounded domain. The dynamics of the magnetisation
\boldsymbol{u}:[0,T]\times\mathscr{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
under the influence of an effective field
\boldsymbol{H}:[0,T]\times\mathscr{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
above the Curie temperature is given by the LLB equation, which is a vector-valued second-order quasilinear PDE
[
15
]
. The problem reads:

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}=-\gamma\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{H}+\alpha\boldsymbol{H}\,\qquad

for
(t,\boldsymbol{x})\in(0,T)\times\mathscr{D}
,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}=\sigma\Delta\boldsymbol{u}-\kappa\mu\boldsymbol{u}-\kappa|\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}\boldsymbol{u}\qquad

for
(t,\boldsymbol{x})\in(0,T)\times\mathscr{D}
,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}(0,\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{u}_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for }\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathscr{D},

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{u}}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}=\boldsymbol{0}}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for }(t,\boldsymbol{x})\in(0,T)\times\partial\mathscr{D},

where
\gamma>0
is the electron gyromagnetic ratio,
\alpha>0
is the damping coefficient,
\sigma>0
is the exchange damping coefficient,
\kappa=(2\chi)^{-1}
is a positive constant related to the longitudinal susceptibility of the ferromagnet, and
\mu>0
is a constant related to the equilibrium magnetisation magnitude. For simplicity of presentation, we set
\sigma=\mu=1
throughout the analysis.

The effective field
\boldsymbol{H}
is the negative variational derivative of the micromagnetic energy functional
\mathcal{E}
defined by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[\boldsymbol{u}]:=\int_{\mathscr{D}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}}\right|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\left|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{4}(\left|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right|^{4}+1)\right)\mathrm{d}x.

In (
1.2
), we consider contributions from the exchange energy and the Ginzburg–Landau internal energy, leading to the effective field as stated in (
1.1b
). Other lower order terms, such as the anisotropy energy and the external magnetic energy, could be taken into account without difficulties, but are omitted for simplicity of presentation. It is known that a system described by (
1.1
) dissipates energy over time
[
16
,
28
]
.

We now review relevant mathematical results for the LLB equation in the regime above the Curie temperature. The existence of a global weak solution to (
1.1
) in smooth bounded domains is established in
[
15
]
, while the unique existence of a global strong solution to (
1.1
) in polytopal domains is proven in
[
16
]
. The existence of a global smooth solution in an unbounded domain, under an additional smallness condition on the initial data when
d=3
, is shown in
[
22
]
.

Several numerical schemes for solving (
1.1
) have been proposed in the literature. An energy-stable, but
nonlinear
, finite element method for a
regularised
version of (
1.1
) is proposed and analysed in
[
27
]
, where optimal order convergence is demonstrated. This regularised problem includes the Landau–Lifshitz–Baryakhtar equation
[
2
,
30
]
, another micromagnetic model at elevated temperatures. A different regularisation of the LLB equation is introduced in
[
16
]
, where a linear, though not necessarily energy-stable, finite element scheme is proposed. In
[
12
]
, a simpler biharmonic regularisation was employed in the numerical treatment of the stochastic LLB equation to improve the regularity of the solution; see also
[
29
]
for related results.

An optimally convergent finite element method (which is either linear or energy-stable, but
not
both) to approximate the solution of (
1.1
) directly is later proposed and analysed in
[
28
]
; see also
[
26
]
for the case below the Curie temperature. A conditionally stable, nonlinear scheme is also proposed in
[
3
]
, where convergence towards weak solutions (without rate) is shown conditionally, but subject to a severe time-step constraint.

The aim of this paper is twofold. First, we propose fully discrete, conforming finite element schemes for the LLB equation (
1.1
) that are both linear and stable in the energy norm by employing the scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) approach
[
24
,
25
]
. Second, we rigorously analyse the stability and convergence of these schemes, proving optimal-order error estimates under suitable regularity assumptions on the solution. Notably, the second scheme introduced in this paper employing BDF2 time discretisation attains second-order accuracy in time (assuming sufficiently regular exact solution), in contrast to existing schemes in the literature which are limited to first-order temporal accuracy.

Recently, the SAV methodology has gained wide popularity for designing energy-stable schemes for various gradient flows and semilinear PDEs
[
6
,
14
]
, including the Cahn–Hilliard equation
[
6
]
, the Navier–Stokes equation
[
19
,
33
]
, the phase field crystal equation
[
31
]
, the Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard system
[
18
,
32
]
, and the magnetohydrodynamic system
[
20
]
. This list is by no means exhaustive.

It is worth noting that a common feature of the systems mentioned above is that they are
semilinear
. In contrast, the LLB equation (
1.1
) is
quasilinear
and is
not
a gradient flow due to the term
-\gamma\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{H}
, which complicates the analysis. Furthermore, the unknowns
\boldsymbol{u}
and
\boldsymbol{H}
are vector-valued, precluding the use of mean value theorems commonly employed in the analysis of SAV-based schemes for scalar-valued gradient flows. We overcome these inherent difficulties and derive optimal order convergence by treating the cross product term semi-implicitly, employing the Ritz projection, and carefully applying discrete Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities and Sobolev embeddings in the derivation of key superconvergence estimates. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first rigorous error analysis of a fully-discrete SAV-based scheme applied to a quasilinear system.

An important feature of (
1.1
) is the dissipation of micromagnetic energy (
1.2
) over time, and it is essential to maintain this property at the discrete level.
As discussed in
[
28
]
, finite element schemes that are not provably energy-stable may produce non-physical solutions with non-decreasing energy. The SAV approach proposed in this paper enables the construction of linear schemes that are unconditionally stable with respect to the energy norm. Moreover, these schemes satisfy a discrete energy law involving a modified energy functional that approximates the true micromagnetic energy.

This paper is organised as follows. Notations and preparatory results employed in the paper are collected in Section
2
. Error analysis for the SAV FEM with linearised Euler and linearised BDF2 time discretisation are established in Section
3
and
4
, respectively. The main theorems of this paper are Theorem
3.8
and Theorem
4.8
, where optimal-order error estimates in the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
and
\mathbb{H}^{1}
norms, as well as in the
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}
norm (for
d\leq 2
) are proven.

2.
Preliminaries

Some notations, SAV formulations of the problem, and essential details of the finite element approximation employed in this paper are outlined in the following subsections.

2.1.
Notations

Some notations used throughout this paper are defined in this section. The function space
\mathbb{L}^{p}:=\mathbb{L}^{p}(\mathscr{D};\mathbb{R}^{3})
denotes the usual space of
p
-th integrable functions taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\mathbb{W}^{s,p}:=\mathbb{W}^{s,p}(\mathscr{D};\mathbb{R}^{3})
denotes the usual Sobolev space of
functions on
\mathscr{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We
write
\mathbb{H}^{s}:=\mathbb{W}^{s,2}
and set
\mathbb{W}^{0,p}=\mathbb{L}^{p}
. Here,
\mathscr{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d=1,2,3

is an open and bounded convex polytopal domain. The Neumann Laplacian operator acting on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
-valued functions is denoted by
\Delta
.

If
X
is a Banach space, the spaces
L^{p}(0,T;X)
and
W^{s,p}(0,T;X)
denote respectively the usual Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces of functions on
(0,T)
taking values in
X
. For simplicity, we write
W^{s,p}_{T}(\mathbb{W}^{m,r}):=W^{s,p}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{m,r})
and
L^{p}_{T}(\mathbb{L}^{q}):=L^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{q})
. Throughout this paper, we denote the scalar product in a Hilbert space
H
by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{H}
and its corresponding norm by
\|\cdot\|_{H}
. We will not distinguish between the scalar product of
\mathbb{L}^{2}
vector-valued functions taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the scalar product of
\mathbb{L}^{2}
matrix-valued functions taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
, and denote them by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

Finally, the constant
C
in the estimate denotes a generic constant which takes different values at different occurrences. If
the dependence of
C
on some variable, e.g.
T
, is highlighted, we will write

C(T)
.

2.2.
SAV formulations

Let
\mathcal{E}[\boldsymbol{u}]
be the energy functional given by (
1.2
).
Define

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}]:=\int_{\mathscr{D}}\frac{\kappa}{4}\left(\left|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right|^{4}+1\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Note that
\boldsymbol{H}=-\delta\mathcal{E}/\delta\boldsymbol{u}
and that
\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}]\geq\frac{1}{4}\kappa\left|{\mathscr{D}}\right|
. We also define the function
g
by

(2.2)

\displaystyle g(\boldsymbol{u}):=\kappa\left|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right|^{2}\boldsymbol{u}.

We introduce a scalar variable
r(t):=\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}]}
, so that
r(0)=\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}_{0}]}
. With these functions, we rewrite the LLB equation (
1.1
) by employing the SAV approach (setting
\sigma=\mu=1
) as follows:

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}=-\gamma\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{H}+\alpha\boldsymbol{H}\,\qquad

for
(t,\boldsymbol{x})\in(0,T)\times\mathscr{D}
,

(2.3b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}=\Delta\boldsymbol{u}-\kappa\boldsymbol{u}-\frac{r(t)}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}]}}g(\boldsymbol{u})\qquad

for
(t,\boldsymbol{x})\in(0,T)\times\mathscr{D}
,

(2.3c)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}r=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}]}}\left\langle g(\boldsymbol{u}),\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}\right\rangle\qquad

for
t\in(0,T)
,

(2.3d)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}(0,\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{u}_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for }\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathscr{D},

(2.3e)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{u}}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}=\boldsymbol{0}}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for }(t,\boldsymbol{x})\in(0,T)\times\partial\mathscr{D},

At the continuous level, this system is equivalent to (
1.1
) as
r(t)/\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}]}\equiv 1
by definition.
Taking the inner product of (
2.3a
) and (
2.3b
) with
\boldsymbol{H}
and
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}
, respectively, we obtain

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}[\boldsymbol{u}]\leq 0,

which is the energy dissipation law obeyed by (
1.1
).

2.3.
Finite element approximation

Let
\mathscr{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=1,2,3
, be a convex polytopal domain. Let
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h>0}
be a family of quasi-uniform triangulations of
\mathscr{D}

into intervals (in 1D), triangles (in 2D), or tetrahedra (in 3D)
with maximal mesh-size
h
.
To discretise the LLB equation, we
introduce the conforming finite element space
\mathbb{V}_{h}\subset\mathbb{H}^{1}
given by

(2.5)

\mathbb{V}_{h}:=\{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{\mathscr{D}};\mathbb{R}^{3}):\boldsymbol{\phi}|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\;\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

where
\mathcal{P}_{1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{3})
denotes the space of linear polynomials on
K
taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

As a consequence of the Bramble–Hilbert lemma, for
p\in[1,\infty]
, there exists a constant
C
independent of
h
such that for any
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{W}^{2,p}
, we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\inf_{\chi\in{\mathbb{V}}_{h}}\left\{\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{\chi}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}+h\left\|{\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{\chi})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\right\}\leq Ch^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{2,p}}.

In the analysis, we shall use several projection and interpolation operators. The existence of such operators and the properties that they possess will be described below (also see
[
5
,
9
,
10
]
).
Firstly, there exists an orthogonal projection operator
\Pi_{h}:\mathbb{L}^{2}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
such that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},

with the property that for any
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{W}^{2,p}
,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}+h\left\|{\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\leq Ch^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{2,p}}.

Next, we introduce the discrete Laplacian operator
\Delta_{h}:\mathbb{V}_{h}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
defined by

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\nabla\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},

as well as the Ritz projection
R_{h}:\mathbb{H}^{1}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
defined by

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\nabla\boldsymbol{v},\nabla\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

For any
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{W}^{2,p}
, the approximation property for the Ritz projection holds
[
5
,
17
,
21
,
23
]
, namely:

(2.11)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{s,p}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2-s}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{2,p}},\quad s\in\{0,1\},\;p\in(1,\infty).

Furthermore, we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\left|{\ln h}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{2,\infty}}.

In two-dimensional polygonal domains, the discrete Sobolev inequality holds
[
4
]
: there exists a constant
C
independent of
h
such that for all
v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\leq C\left|{\ln h}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}.

Finally, for any
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
, the following discrete Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality holds:

(2.14)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1-\frac{d}{4}}\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{d}{4}}+\left\|{\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{d}{4}}\right).

Inequality (
2.14
) is shown in
[
13
, Appendix A]
).

3.
SAV-FEM with first-order semi-implicit time discretisation

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
be the approximation in
\mathbb{V}_{h}
of
\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})
, where
t_{n}:=nk\in[0,T]
for
n=0,1,\ldots,N
and
N=\lfloor T/k\rfloor
. For any function
\boldsymbol{v}
, we write
\boldsymbol{v}^{n}:=\boldsymbol{v}(t_{n})
, and define for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{v}^{n}:=\frac{1}{k}(\boldsymbol{v}^{n}-\boldsymbol{v}^{n-1}).

A linear fully-discrete SAV FEM with first-order semi-implicit time discretisation can be described as follows.
We start with
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}=R_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
and
r_{h}^{0}=\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0})\in\mathbb{R}
. Given
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1},r_{h}^{n-1})\in\mathbb{V}_{h}\times\mathbb{R}
, we find
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},r_{h}^{n})\in\mathbb{V}_{h}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(3.1a)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\gamma\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n},\phi\right\rangle+\alpha\left\langle\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n},\phi\right\rangle,\quad

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},

(3.1b)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{t}r_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}]}}\left\langle g(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}),\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\right\rangle,

where

(3.2)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\kappa\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\Pi_{h}\left[\frac{r_{h}^{n}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}]}}g(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1})\right].

Here,
\Delta_{h}
and
\Pi_{h}
denote the discrete Laplacian and the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
projector, respectively, and the functions
\mathcal{F}
and
g
are defined in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
).
The above scheme can be effectively implemented in the following algorithm:

Algorithm 3.1

(Semi-implicit linear SAV-FEM)
.

Let
h>0
and
k>0
be given.

Input
: Given
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
and
r_{h}^{0}=\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}]\in\mathbb{R}
.

For

n=1
to
N
, where
N=\lfloor T/k\rfloor
,
do
:

(1)

Substitute equation (
3.2
) into (
3.1a
), and compute
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
;

(2)

Compute
r_{h}^{n}
using (
3.1b
).

Output
: a sequence of discrete functions
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\}_{1\leq n\leq N}
and real numbers
\{r_{h}^{n}\}_{1\leq n\leq N}
.

Let us define the modified energy
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
of the finite element solution
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
as

(3.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]:=\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left|{r_{h}^{n}}\right|^{2}.

Note that
\left|{r_{h}^{n}}\right|^{2}
is an approximation to
\mathcal{F}[u^{n}]
, and thus the modified energy
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
approximates the actual energy
\mathcal{E}
.
To derive optimal-order convergence, throughout this section we assume the following regularity for the exact solution
\boldsymbol{u}
:

(3.4)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}\in L^{\infty}_{T}(\mathbb{W}^{2,\infty})\cap W^{1,\infty}_{T}(\mathbb{H}^{1})\cap W^{2,\infty}_{T}(\mathbb{L}^{2}).

We first show that the numerical scheme is well-posed.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
and
r_{h}^{n-1}\in\mathbb{R}
be given. For sufficiently small
k>0
, there exists a unique
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
and
r_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}
solving the system (
3.1
)–(
3.2
).

Proof.

For each
h>0
, define a linear operator
S_{h}:\mathbb{V}_{h}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
by
S_{h}\boldsymbol{v}:=\Pi_{h}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{v}\right)
. Note that
S_{h}
is skew-symmetric with respect to the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-inner product, in the sense that
\left\langle S_{h}\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle=0
for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
. Consequently, for any
\alpha>0
, the linear operator
T_{h}:=\alpha I-\gamma S_{h}
is positive definite, and thus is invertible on
\mathbb{V}_{h}
. We denote its inverse by
T_{h}^{-1}:=(\alpha I-\gamma S_{h})^{-1}
, which is also positive definite. Moreover,
\left\|T_{h}^{-1}\right\|\leq 1/\alpha
.

Now, equation (
3.1a
) can be written as
\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}=\frac{1}{k}T_{h}^{-1}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\right)
, which by (
3.2
) implies

\displaystyle T_{h}^{-1}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle=k\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-k\kappa\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\frac{kr_{h}^{n}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}]}}\Pi_{h}\left[g(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1})\right].

Let
\mathcal{A}_{h}:=-k\Delta_{h}+k\kappa I+T_{h}^{-1}
.
Using (
3.1b
) and rearranging the above equation, we see that (
3.1
)–(
3.2
) is equivalent to finding
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
which satisfies

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}+\frac{k}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}]}}\left\langle g(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}),\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\right\rangle\Pi_{h}\left[g(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1})\right]=\boldsymbol{f}_{h}^{n-1},

where
\boldsymbol{f}_{h}^{n-1}
contains only data from the previous step, namely

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{f}_{h}^{n-1}:=T_{h}^{-1}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}-\frac{kr_{h}^{n-1}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}]}}\Pi_{h}\left[g(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1})\right]+\frac{k}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}]}}\left\langle g(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}),\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\right\rangle\Pi_{h}\left[g(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1})\right].

Since
\mathbb{V}_{h}
is finite-dimensional, it suffices to show that the linear system (
3.5
) has at most one solution; uniqueness then implies existence. Writing (
3.5
) as
\mathcal{B}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}=\boldsymbol{f}_{h}^{n-1}
, we note that the operator
\mathcal{B}_{h}
is positive definite on
\mathbb{V}_{h}
. The desired uniqueness then follows immediately, completing the proof of the proposition.
∎

Next, we show the unconditional stability property of scheme (
3.1
) by establishing a modified energy law satisfied by the discrete solution.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
be given by Algorithm
3.1
. For
n=1,2,\ldots,\lfloor T/k\rfloor
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}],

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]
was defined in (

Proof.

Setting
\boldsymbol{\phi}=\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}
in (
3.1
) and taking the inner product of (

) with
\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
, then combining the resulting equations, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]-\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}]\right)+\alpha\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{k}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left|{r_{h}^{n}-r_{h}^{n-1}}\right|^{2}\right)=0.

This implies (
3.6
), completing the proof of the proposition.
∎

With the energy stability property, we can show the following uniform boundedness of the finite element solutions.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
be given by Algorithm
3.1
. For
n=1,2,\ldots,\lfloor T/k\rfloor
, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+\left|{r_{h}^{n}}\right|^{2}+k\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{\mathrm{S}}.

Consequently, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle k\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\leq C_{\infty},

where
C_{\mathrm{S}}
and
C_{\infty}
are constants depending on
T
, but are independent of
n
,
h
, and
k
.

Proof.

By summing (
3
) over
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]+\alpha k\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}],

which implies (
3.8
).
By rearranging the terms, noting the stability of
\Pi_{h}
, we obtain

\displaystyle k\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ck\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+Ck\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+Ck\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|{r_{h}^{j}}\right|^{2}\left\|{g(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ck\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+CTC_{\mathrm{S}}+Ck\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|{r_{h}^{j}}\right|^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}}^{6}

\displaystyle\leq Ck\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+CTC_{\mathrm{S}}+CTC_{\mathrm{S}}^{4}\leq C,

where
C_{\mathrm{S}}
is the constant in (
3.8
). Inequality (
3.9
) then follows from (
2.14
), noting (
3.8
) again.
∎

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}^{n}

\displaystyle=(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-R_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{n})+(R_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}^{n})=:\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{H}^{n}

\displaystyle=(\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}-R_{h}\boldsymbol{H}^{n})+(R_{h}\boldsymbol{H}^{n}-\boldsymbol{H}^{n})=:\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n},

\displaystyle r_{h}^{n}-r^{n}

\displaystyle=e^{n},

is the Ritz projection defined by (

(3.13)

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n},\nabla\boldsymbol{\phi}\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n},\nabla\boldsymbol{\phi}\right\rangle=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{v}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}

In the proof of the error estimates, the following vector identity is often used:

(3.16)

\displaystyle 2\boldsymbol{a}\cdot(\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{b})=\left|{\boldsymbol{a}}\right|^{2}-\left|{\boldsymbol{b}}\right|^{2}+\left|{\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{b}}\right|^{2},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

\displaystyle\leq C\left|{r_{h}^{n}}\right|\left\|{\frac{g(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}]}}-\frac{g(\boldsymbol{u}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}^{n-1}]}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+C\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\right)h^{4}+Ck^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{20}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Similarly, for the next term, by (
3.18
) and (
3.22
),

\displaystyle\left|{I_{9}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\right)k^{2}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+Ch^{4}.

For the term
I_{10}
, by the same argument as in the estimate for
I_{8}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|{I_{10}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+C\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\right)h^{4}+Ck^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{20}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Next, for the term
I_{11}
, by Young’s inequality, the definition of
g
, and (
2.11
),

\displaystyle\left|{I_{10}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|{e^{n}}\right|^{2}+C\left\|{g(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left|{e^{n}}\right|^{2}+Ch^{4}.

For the next term, by (
3.17
), (
3.14
), (
2.11
), and Young’s inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\left|{I_{12}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\left|{e^{n}}\right|^{2}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-1}+\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}}^{2}\leq C\left|{e^{n}}\right|^{2}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+Ch^{4}.

Finally, for the last term, by Young’s inequality, (
3.15
), and the assumed regularity for
\boldsymbol{u}
in (
3.4
), we infer that

\displaystyle\left|{I_{13}}\right|\leq C\left|{e^{n}}\right|^{2}+Ck^{2}.

Altogether, we collect these estimates and continue from (
3
). Summing over
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and rearranging the terms, we obtain for sufficiently small
k>0
,

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+\left|{e^{n}}\right|^{2}+\alpha k\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ck(h^{4}+k^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\right)+Ck\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+Ck\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left|{e^{j}}\right|^{2}.

By the discrete Gronwall lemma, noting the stability estimate (
3.9
), we deduce

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+\left|{e^{n}}\right|^{2}+\alpha k\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(h^{4}+k^{2})\exp\left[Ck\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(h^{4}+k^{2}),

proving (
3.25
).

Finally, estimate (
3.26
) follows from (
3.25
) and the discrete Sobolev inequality in 2D (
2.13
). This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

The following theorem establishes an optimal rate of convergence for scheme (
3.1
) in various norms.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
be given by Algorithm
3.1
, and let
\boldsymbol{u}
be the solution of (
2.3
). For sufficiently small
h,k>0
, and
n=1,2,\ldots,\lfloor T/k\rfloor
,

(3.30)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}}\leq C(h^{2-s}+k),\quad s\in\{0,1\}.

If
\mathscr{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then

(3.31)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\leq C(h^{2}+k)\left|{\ln h}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Furthermore, the energy functional
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]
is a good approximation of
\mathcal{E}[\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})]
in the sense that

(3.32)

\displaystyle\left|{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]-\mathcal{E}[\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})]}\right|\leq C(h+k).

The constant
C
in (
3.30
), (
3.31
), and (
3.32
) is independent of
n
,
h
, and
k
.

Proof.

The estimate (
3.30
) follows from (
3.10
), (
3.25
), (
2.11
), and the triangle inequality. Correspondingly, the estimate (
3.31
) follows from (
3.10
), (
3.26
), (
2.12
), and the triangle inequality.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\left|{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]-\mathcal{E}[\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})]}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathscr{D}}\left|{\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right|^{2}-\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})}\right|^{2}}\right|\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{\mathscr{D}}\left|{\left|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right|^{2}-\left|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})}\right|^{2}}\right|\mathrm{d}x+\left|{\left|{r_{h}^{n}}\right|^{2}-\left|{r(t_{n})}\right|^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}+C\left|{r_{h}^{k}-r(t_{k})}\right|,

where in the last step we used the elementary inequality
\left|{\left|{\boldsymbol{a}}\right|^{2}-\left|{\boldsymbol{b}}\right|^{2}}\right|\leq\left(\left|{\boldsymbol{a}}\right|+\left|{\boldsymbol{b}}\right|\right)\left|{\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{b}}\right|
together with Hölder’s inequality. This implies (
3.32
), in view of (

), as required.
∎

4.
SAV-FEM with linearised BDF2 time discretisation

In this section, we construct a linear fully-discrete SAV FEM based on BDF2 time discretisation. Let us define some notations. Firstly, for any function
\boldsymbol{v}
, we write
\boldsymbol{v}^{n}:=\boldsymbol{v}(t_{n})
. Secondly, for a sequence of discrete functions
\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{n}\}
, we write

(4.1)

\displaystyle\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}^{n-1}:=2\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{n-1}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{n-2}.

For
n\geq 2
, we also define

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{v}^{n}:=\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{v}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{v}^{n}-\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{v}^{n-1}\right)=\frac{3\boldsymbol{v}^{n}-4\boldsymbol{v}^{n-1}+\boldsymbol{v}^{n-2}}{2k}.

The scheme based on the linearised BDF2 time-stepping is described as follows. We start with
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
and
r_{h}^{0}=\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0})\in\mathbb{R}
, where the functional
\mathcal{F}
is defined in (
2.1
). Given
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1},r_{h}^{n-1})
and
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-2},r_{h}^{n-2})
, we seek
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},r_{h}^{n})\in\mathbb{V}_{h}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n},\phi\right\rangle+\alpha\left\langle\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n},\phi\right\rangle,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{t}r_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}]}}\left\langle g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}),\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\right\rangle,

where

(4.4)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\kappa\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\Pi_{h}\left[\frac{r_{h}^{n}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}]}}\,g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1})\right].

Here,
\Delta_{h}
and
\Pi_{h}
are, respectively, the discrete Laplacian and the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-projection operators. The functions
\mathcal{F}
and
g
are defined in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), respectively.

Note that since (
4.3
) is a two-step scheme, with
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}
and
r_{h}^{0}
given, we also need to define
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{1}
and
r_{h}^{1}
, for instance by performing one step of the implicit BDF2 method or
1/k
steps of the linearised Euler method (
3.1
) with time-step size
k^{2}
. For simplicity of presentation, we assume that
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{1}
and
r_{h}^{1}
have been properly initialised such that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{1}-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{1})}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}}+\left|{r_{h}^{1}-r(t_{1})}\right|\leq C(h^{2-s}+k^{2}),\quad s\in\{0,1\},

where
C
is independent of
h
and
k
.

The above scheme can be effectively implemented in the following algorithm:

Algorithm 4.1

(Linearised BDF2 SAV-FEM)
.

Let
h>0
and
k>0
be given.

Input
: Given
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
and
r_{h}^{0}=\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}]\in\mathbb{R}
.

(1)

Compute
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{1},r_{h}^{1})
, say by employing one step of the implicit BDF2 method or
1/k
steps of the linearised Euler method (Algorithm
3.1
) with time-step size
k^{2}
.

For

n=2
to
N
, where
N=\lfloor T/k\rfloor
,
do
:

(1)

Substitute equation (
4.4
) into the first equation in (
4.3
), and compute
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
;

(2)

Compute
r_{h}^{n}
using the second equation in (
4.3
).

Output
: a sequence of discrete functions
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\}_{1\leq n\leq N}
and real numbers
\{r_{h}^{n}\}_{1\leq n\leq N}
.

Well-posedness of Algorithm
4.1
follows by the same argument as in Proposition
3.2
.
The stability and convergence properties for the scheme will be derived next.
To this end, we first define a modified energy
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]
of the finite element solution
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
given by

(4.6)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]

\displaystyle:=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]+\frac{1}{2}\left\|{2\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\left\|{2\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left|{2r_{h}^{n}-r_{h}^{n-1}}\right|^{2},

with
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]
defined in (
3.3
). We also assume the following regularity for the exact solution
\boldsymbol{u}
:

(4.7)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}\in L^{\infty}_{T}(\mathbb{W}^{2,\infty})\cap W^{1,\infty}_{T}(\mathbb{H}^{1})\cap W^{2,\infty}_{T}(\mathbb{H}^{1})\cap W^{3,\infty}_{T}(\mathbb{L}^{2}).

In the analysis, the following identity will be useful: for any
\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(4.8)

\displaystyle 2\boldsymbol{a}\cdot(3\boldsymbol{a}-4\boldsymbol{b}+\boldsymbol{c})=\left|{\boldsymbol{a}}\right|^{2}-\left|{\boldsymbol{b}}\right|^{2}+\left|{2\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{b}}\right|^{2}-\left|{2\boldsymbol{b}-\boldsymbol{c}}\right|^{2}+\left|{\boldsymbol{a}-2\boldsymbol{b}+\boldsymbol{c}}\right|^{2}.

The following unconditional energy stability result can now be established.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
be given by Algorithm
4.1
. For
n=1,2,\ldots,\lfloor T/k\rfloor
,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]\leq\widehat{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}].

Here,
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]
is a modified energy of the finite element solution
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
defined in (
4.6
).

Proof.

Setting
\boldsymbol{\phi}=\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}
in (
4.3
), taking the inner product of (
4.4
) with
\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
, and multiplying the second equation in (
4.3
) with
2r_{h}^{n}
, then combining the resulting equations, noting (
4.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]-\widehat{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}]+\alpha k\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-2\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}+\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-2}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\kappa}{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-2\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-2}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left|{r_{h}^{n}-2r_{h}^{n-1}+r_{h}^{n-2}}\right|^{2}=0,

which implies (
4.9
).
∎

Remark 4.3
.

We note that
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]
defined in (
4.6
) can be written as

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]:=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}]+2\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}-\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\kappa\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left(r_{h}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}-r_{h}^{n-1}\right),

where
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{n}+\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{n-1}\right)
for any
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
. Given sufficient regularity of the exact solution, this energy functional is an approximation to the original energy
\mathcal{E}
as
h,k\to 0^{+}
.

Next, we show the following stability estimate.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
be given by Algorithm
4.1
. For
n=1,2,\ldots,\lfloor T/k\rfloor
, we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+\left|{r_{h}^{n}}\right|^{2}+k\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{\mathrm{B}}.

Consequently, we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle k\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\leq C_{\infty},

where
C_{\mathrm{B}}
and
C_{\infty}
are constants depending on
T
, but are independent of
n
,
h
, and
k
.

Proof.

Summing (
4
) over
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, we obtain (
4.11
). Finally, the proof of (
4.12
) follows similarly to that of (
3.9
), but now makes use of (
4.4
) and (
4.11
) instead.
∎

To facilitate the proof of the error analysis, we split the approximation error as before, namely:

(4.13)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}^{n}

\displaystyle=(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-R_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{n})+(R_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}^{n})=:\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n},

(4.14)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{H}^{n}

\displaystyle=(\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}-R_{h}\boldsymbol{H}^{n})+(R_{h}\boldsymbol{H}^{n}-\boldsymbol{H}^{n})=:\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n},

(4.15)

\displaystyle r_{h}^{n}-r^{n}

\displaystyle=e^{n},

where
R_{h}
is the Ritz projection defined by (
2.10
).
We will also make use of the quantities
\overline{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{n},\overline{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{n},\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{n}
, and
\overline{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{n}
which are defined similarly, noting (
4.1
).

Several elementary estimates are collected next. Assume the regularity of
\boldsymbol{u}
stated in (
4.7
). For
p\in(1,\infty)
, there exists a positive constant
C_{p}
such that

(4.16)

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{v}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{v}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{v}^{n}-\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{v}^{n-1}\right)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\leq C_{p},

(4.17)

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{v}^{n}-\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq Ck\int_{t_{n-2}}^{t_{n}}\left\|{\partial_{t}^{3}\boldsymbol{v}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\mathrm{d}t\leq Ck^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{W^{3,\infty}_{T}(\mathbb{L}^{p})}.

Consequently, for
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n}
defined in (
4.13
) and (
4.14
),

(4.18)

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}-\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n-1}\right)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\leq C_{p}h^{2},\quad\text{and }\;\left\|{\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\leq C_{p}h^{2}.

Further preliminary estimates will be needed in the proof of the main theorem, which we now derive.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
be given by Algorithm
4.1
. Let
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}
and
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}
be given by (
3.10
). Furthermore, let the functions
\mathcal{F}
and
g
be given by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), respectively. Then

(4.19)

\displaystyle\left\|{\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}]}}-\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}]}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}}+C\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{n-2}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}}+C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}},

(4.20)

\displaystyle\left\|{\frac{g(\boldsymbol{u}^{n})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}^{n}]}}-\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}]}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{2},

where
C
is a constant independent of
n
,
h
, and
k
.

Proof.

We first prove (
4.19
). By the same argument as in (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}]}}-\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}]}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq C\left\|{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1})-g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+C\left|{\mathcal{F}[{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}}]-\mathcal{F}[{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}}]}\right|.

The first term on the right-hand side can be estimated as in (

\displaystyle\left\|{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1})-g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}}^{2}+\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}}^{2}\right)\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{n-1}+\overline{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-2}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}}+C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}}.

By the same argument as in (

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{F}[{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}}]-\mathcal{F}[{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}}]}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{2}+\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{2}\right)\left(\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}+\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}\right)\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{n-1}+\overline{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-2}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\overline{\rho}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}.

Altogether, we infer (
4.19
).

Next, we show (
4.20
). By the same token, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|{\frac{g(\boldsymbol{u}^{n})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}^{n}]}}-\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}]}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}}\leq Ck^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}_{T}(\mathbb{H}^{1})},

where in the last step we used Taylor’s theorem. Noting the regularity of
\boldsymbol{u}
, we deduce (
4.20
), thus completing the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
be defined by scheme (
4.3
). Let
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}
and
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}
be given by (
4.13
), and let
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n}
be given by (

(4.21)

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\right)\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+C\left(1+\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\right)h^{2}+Ck^{2},

where
C
is a constant independent of
n
,
h
, and
k
.

Proof.

Subtracting the weak formulation of (
2.3a
) from the corresponding equation in scheme (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}+\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}+\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}\times(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n}),\boldsymbol{\phi}\right\rangle-\gamma\left\langle(\overline{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{n-1}+\overline{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{n-1})\times\boldsymbol{H}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}\right\rangle

(4.22)

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma\left\langle(\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})\times\boldsymbol{H}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}\right\rangle+\alpha\left\langle\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}\right\rangle,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{h}\big[\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}\big]-\Pi_{h}\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}-\gamma\Pi_{h}\big[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}\times(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n})\big]

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma\Pi_{h}\big[(\overline{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{n-1}+\overline{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{n-1})\times\boldsymbol{H}^{n}\big]-\gamma\Pi_{h}\big[(\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})\times\boldsymbol{H}^{n}\big]+\alpha\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}+\alpha\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n},

where
\Pi_{h}
is the orthogonal projection defined by (

).
Noting the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
stability of
\Pi_{h}
, we have by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+C\left\|{\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{n-1}+\overline{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{2}+Ch^{2}+C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+Ch^{2}\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}+C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

where in the last step we used (
4.17
), (
2.11
), and the regularity of the solution assumed in (
4.7
). This implies (
4.21
), completing the proof of the lemma.
∎

The following proposition establishes an auxiliary superconvergence estimate.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}

4.14

(4.24)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\leq C(h^{4}+k^{4})\left|{\ln h}\right|.

n

h

k

Proof.

Firstly, recall that by subtracting the weak formulation of (

In a similar manner, noting (

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n},\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n},\nabla\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle-\kappa\left\langle\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n},\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{e^{n}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}]}}\left\langle g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}),\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle-r_{h}^{n}\left\langle\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}]}}-\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}]}},\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle

(4.25)

\displaystyle\quad+r^{n}\left\langle\frac{g(\boldsymbol{u}^{n})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}^{n}]}}-\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}]}},\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

Next, subtracting (

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{t}e^{n}+\mathcal{D}_{t}r^{n}-\partial_{t}r^{n}

(4.26)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}]}}-\frac{g(\boldsymbol{u}^{n})}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}^{n}]}},\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}\right\rangle+\left\langle\frac{g(\boldsymbol{u}^{n})}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}^{n}]}},\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}\right\rangle.

Now, set
\boldsymbol{\phi}=\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}

\boldsymbol{\chi}=\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}
in (
4
), and multiply (
4
) by
2e^{n}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2k}\left(\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{\kappa}{2k}\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{k}\left(\left|{e^{n}}\right|^{2}-\left|{e^{n-1}}\right|^{2}\right)+\alpha\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\kappa}{2k}\left(\left\|{2\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|{2\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-1}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-2}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{k}\left(\left|{2e^{n}-e^{n-1}}\right|^{2}-\left|{2e^{n-1}-e^{n-2}}\right|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2k}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}-2\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-1}+\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-2}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\kappa}{2k}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}-2\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-1}+\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n-2}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{k}\left|{e^{n}-2e^{n-1}+e^{n-2}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}+\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}\right\rangle-\left\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n},\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}\right\rangle+\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\left\langle(\overline{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{n-1}+\overline{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{n-1})\times\boldsymbol{H}^{n},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}\right\rangle+\gamma\left\langle(\boldsymbol{u}^{n}-\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})\times\boldsymbol{H}^{n},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}\right\rangle-\alpha\left\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}\right\rangle-\kappa\left\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n},\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-r_{h}^{n}\left\langle\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}]}}-\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}]}},\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}\right\rangle+r^{n}\left\langle\frac{g(\boldsymbol{u}^{n})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}^{n}]}}-\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}]}},\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+e^{n}\left\langle\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}]}}-\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}]}},\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}\right\rangle+\frac{e^{n}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}]}}\left\langle g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}),\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+e^{n}\left\langle\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}]}}-\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}]}},\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}\right\rangle+e^{n}\left\langle\frac{g(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}^{n-1}]}}-\frac{g(\boldsymbol{u}^{n})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[\boldsymbol{u}^{n}]}},\mathcal{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}+\ldots+J_{14}.

Similarly, for the third term, by (

\displaystyle\left|{J_{3}}\right|

\displaystyle\left|{J_{5}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{4}+\frac{\alpha}{20}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left|{J_{6}}\right|

Next, by Young’s inequality and (
4.21

\displaystyle\left|{J_{7}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+C\left(1+\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\right)h^{4}+Ck^{4}+\frac{\alpha}{20}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

), noting that we have established (

∎
