Connecting Flavor and Baryon Asymmetry via
Leptogenesis in Effective Froggatt-Nielsen Theory

Abstract
We investigate the hierarchical flavor structure of the Standard Model in a Froggatt-Nielsen (FN) framework, where the spontaneous breaking of a U ​ ( 1 ) FN U(1)_{\rm FN} symmetry by a complex flavon field generates fermion masses and mixing patterns through higher-dimensional operators. Extending the setup with three right-handed neutrinos (RHNs), light neutrino masses arise via the Type-I seesaw mechanism. Allowing complex FN coefficients enables a consistent description of the CKM and PMNS matrices while inducing CP-violating signatures in meson decays. Building on our previous work, where the lightest RHN acts as a viable dark matter (DM) candidate produced through freeze-in or freeze-out mechanisms, we investigate the origin of the baryon asymmetry of the Universe. The heavier RHNs generate a lepton asymmetry through out-of-equilibrium decays, including both Standard Model channels and additional flavon-induced processes in which the flavon appears as an intermediate or final-state particle. We compute the corresponding one-loop CP asymmetries and incorporate these effects in the Boltzmann equations. We show that although freeze-in and freeze-out DM production occur in two qualitatively distinct regions of the FN symmetry-breaking scale v ϕ v_{\phi} , successful thermal leptogenesis can be achieved in both regimes. In the large- v ϕ v_{\phi} (freeze-in-compatible) region the results approach the standard leptogenesis limit, while in the freeze-out-compatible region the lower value of v ϕ v_{\phi} implies lighter RHNs, requiring resonant enhancement. This tightly constrained framework, in which v ϕ v_{\phi} simultaneously controls RHN masses and the interaction strengths of the flavon and DM sectors, provides a predictive and unified description of flavor hierarchies, neutrino masses, CP violation, dark matter, and baryogenesis within a single effective theory.

Introduction

Within the Standard Model (SM), fermion Yukawa couplings span several orders of magnitude, with no underlying explanation for their observed structure. The Froggatt-Nielsen (FN) mechanism provides an elegant and economical framework to address this puzzle by invoking an additional abelian flavor symmetry under which SM fermions carry generation-dependent charges
[
15
]
. The spontaneous breaking of this symmetry, mediated by a scalar field (known as ‘flavon’), naturally generates hierarchical Yukawa couplings through higher-dimensional operators suppressed by a large flavor scale. Consequently, fermion masses and mixing angles emerge as powers of a small symmetry-breaking parameter, offering a unified and predictive explanation for the observed flavor hierarchies.

While no direct signal of physics beyond the SM has been observed so far, neutrino oscillation data provide unambiguous evidence for new physics
[
16
,
2
]
. The introduction of right-handed neutrinos (RHNs) within the FN framework not only enables a natural realization of the Type-I seesaw mechanism
but also allows the FN symmetry to control the structure of the neutrino mass matrix. Beyond the flavor puzzle and neutrino physics, cosmological observations demand explanations for two additional phenomena absent in the SM: the nature of Dark Matter (DM) and the origin of the baryon asymmetry of the Universe
[
1
]
.

In this work, we extend the conventional FN setup in two essential directions: first, by introducing three RHNs, and second, by allowing the effective FN coefficients to be complex.
The inclusion of RHNs facilitates the generation of light neutrino masses via the seesaw mechanism
[
14
]
, while the presence of irreducible complex FN coefficients plays a central role in reproducing the observed CP-violating phases in both the quark and lepton sectors. As a result, the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM)
[
8
,
20
]
and Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata (PMNS)
[
25
,
22
]
matrices are accurately described within the same framework. These complex couplings also give rise to nontrivial flavor signatures, particularly in the quark sector, leading to testable effects in flavor observables such as rare and CP-violating
B
-meson decays.

In our previous work
[
23
]
, we demonstrated that a minimal FN extension of the SM with three RHNs can successfully account for DM, with the flavon acting as a portal between the dark sector and the SM. In that framework, the lightest RHN emerges as a viable DM candidate, with the observed relic abundance achieved through both freeze-in and freeze-out mechanisms, while remaining consistent with all relevant phenomenological and experimental constraints.
The present work builds on this setup by addressing the origin of the baryon asymmetry. While the lightest RHN remains the DM candidate and is effectively decoupled from thermal leptogenesis dynamics, the two heavier RHNs can naturally generate the observed baryon asymmetry through standard thermal leptogenesis
[
17
]
. Their CP-violating decays receive additional flavon-mediated contributions induced by complex FN coefficients. These new CP-violating sources enhance the lepton asymmetry generated in out-of-equilibrium RHN decays, which is then converted into baryon asymmetry through electroweak sphaleron processes
[
21
]
. We show that successful leptogenesis can be achieved while remaining consistent with low-energy flavor observables, fermion mass hierarchies, neutrino oscillation data, and the DM phenomenology established in our earlier work. In particular, we analyze two parametrically distinct regimes of the FN symmetry-breaking scale
v_{\phi}
, corresponding to freeze-in and freeze-out DM production. In the freeze-in regime,
v_{\phi}\sim 10^{8}\,\text{GeV}
and flavon-mediated interactions are highly suppressed, whereas in the freeze-out regime,
v_{\phi}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)\,\text{TeV}
and these interactions are significantly stronger. Despite this hierarchy and the resulting differences in RHN dynamics, efficient thermal leptogenesis can be realized in both regions.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
, we introduce the FN framework, including the field content, charge assignments, and effective interactions relevant for fermion masses and mixings. Section
3
presents the experimental constraints, with emphasis on
B_{d,s}
-meson and kaon mixing observables. In Sec.
4
, we derive the CP asymmetries and Boltzmann equations relevant for thermal leptogenesis. Numerical results are presented in Sec.
5
, where we analyze parameter regions compatible with freeze-in and freeze-out DM scenarios (Secs.
5.1
and
5.2
). Finally, Sec.
6
summarizes our conclusions, while technical details are collected in the appendices.

2
Froggatt-Nielsen framework

At energies below the FN scale, flavor dynamics are encoded in effective operators involving SM fields, the flavon, and the RHNs. The corresponding interaction Lagrangian for the flavon field
\phi
, the SM fermions, and three SM-gauge-singlet RHNs
N_{R}^{i}
can be written as
[
14
,
23
]
:

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle=c^{ij}_{d}\left(\frac{\phi}{M}\right)^{n^{ij}_{d}}\bar{Q}^{i}Hd_{R}^{j}+c^{ij}_{u}\left(\frac{\phi}{M}\right)^{n^{ij}_{u}}\bar{Q}^{i}\;i\sigma_{2}H^{*}u_{R}^{j}+c^{ij}_{e}\left(\frac{\phi}{M}\right)^{n^{ij}_{e}}\bar{L}^{i}He_{R}^{j}

\displaystyle+c^{ij}_{\nu}\left(\frac{\phi}{M}\right)^{n^{ij}_{\nu}}\bar{L}^{i}\,i\sigma_{2}H^{*}N_{R}^{j}+\frac{1}{2}c^{ij}_{N}\left(\frac{\phi}{M}\right)^{n^{ij}_{N}}M\overline{N_{R}^{c\,i}}N_{R}^{j}\ +\ \text{h.c.}\,.

(1)

We introduce an additional abelian
U(1)_{\rm FN}
symmetry under which SM fermions carry generation-dependent charges. The corresponding charge differences are defined as

\displaystyle n^{ij}_{u}\equiv q_{Q_{i}}-q_{u_{j}},\penalty\ \penalty\ n^{ij}_{d}\equiv q_{Q_{i}}-q_{d_{j}},\penalty\ \penalty\ n^{ij}_{e}\equiv q_{L_{i}}-q_{e_{j}},

(2)

\displaystyle\penalty\penalty\displaystyle n^{ik}_{\nu}\equiv q_{L_{i}}-q_{N_{k}},\penalty\ \penalty\ n^{ij}_{N}\equiv-q_{N_{i}}-q_{N_{j}}\,.

All exponents satisfy
n_{f}^{ij}\geq 0
, implying
q_{N_{i}}\leq 0
, while the coefficients
c_{x}^{ij}
are taken to be
\mathcal{O}(1)
for
i,j=1,2,3
.

Spontaneous breaking of the FN symmetry occurs when
\phi
acquires a vacuum expectation value (vev),

\displaystyle\phi=v_{\phi}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(s+ia)\,,

(3)

whose effects are transmitted to SM fermions through higher-dimensional operators suppressed by powers of
\epsilon\equiv\langle\phi\rangle/M
. Here,
M
denotes the flavor-dynamics scale, identified with the mass scale of heavy vector-like FN fields that have been integrated out. After electroweak symmetry breaking, with
H^{T}=\left(0,v_{\rm EW}+h/\sqrt{2}\right)
, the quark and charged-lepton mass matrices are

m_{f}^{ij}=c_{f}^{ij}\epsilon^{n_{f}^{ij}}v_{\text{EW}}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{with}\penalty\ \penalty\ f=u,d,e\,.

(4)

Using the following unitary rotations from flavor to mass eigenstates,

\displaystyle f_{L}^{i}\to U_{L}^{ij}f_{L}^{j},\qquad f_{R}^{i}\to U_{R}^{ij}f_{R}^{j}\,,

(5)

the mass matrices are diagonalized. The leading dimension-four interaction terms for the scalar and pseudoscalar fields are then given by

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{\text{scalar}}=\sum_{f=u,d,e}\bigg[

\displaystyle m_{i}^{f}\left(1+\frac{h}{\sqrt{2}v_{\text{EW}}}\right)\bar{f}^{i}f^{i}

\displaystyle+ia\left((g_{+}^{f})_{ij}\bar{f}^{i}\gamma_{5}f^{j}+(g_{-}^{f})_{ij}\bar{f}^{i}f^{j}\right)

\displaystyle+s\left((g_{+}^{f})_{ij}\bar{f}^{i}f^{j}+(g_{-}^{f})_{ij}\bar{f}^{i}\gamma_{5}f^{j}\right)\bigg]\,,

(6)

where the couplings are
[
14
]

\displaystyle(g_{+}^{f})_{ij}\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(U_{L}^{f\dagger}\hat{q}_{Q}U_{L}^{f}-U_{R}^{f\dagger}\hat{q}_{f}U_{R}^{f}\right)_{ij}\frac{m_{j}^{f}+m_{i}^{f}}{v_{\phi}}\,,

(7)

\displaystyle(g_{-}^{f})_{ij}\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(U_{L}^{f\dagger}\hat{q}_{Q}U_{L}^{f}+U_{R}^{f\dagger}\hat{q}_{f}U_{R}^{f}\right)_{ij}\frac{m_{j}^{f}-m_{i}^{f}}{v_{\phi}}\,.

(8)

Here,
(\hat{q}_{X})_{ij}=q_{X_{i}}\delta_{ij}
are the diagonal matrices of the FN charges. Note that because of the presence of generation-dependent FN charges, the flavon couplings cannot be diagonalized simultaneously with the mass matrices. As a result, flavor-changing interactions are generated.
The fermion masses and mixing pattern depend sensitively on the
U(1)_{\rm FN}
charge assignments, in particular on charge differences. Adopting the standard choice for
\epsilon
as the Cabibbo angle,

\displaystyle\epsilon=\frac{v_{\phi}}{M}\approx 0.23\,,

(9)

the following charge differences reproduce the observed mass spectrum:

\displaystyle n^{ij}_{u}=\begin{pmatrix}8&4&3\\
7&3&2\\
5&1&0\end{pmatrix},\penalty\ \penalty\ n^{ij}_{d}=\begin{pmatrix}7&6&6\\
6&5&5\\
4&3&3\end{pmatrix},\penalty\ \penalty\ n^{ij}_{e}=\begin{pmatrix}9&6&4\\
8&5&3\\
8&5&3\end{pmatrix}\,.

(10)

Although the individual FN charges
q_{X}
are not unique, their implications for the quark and lepton sectors (including the RHNs) are tightly constrained. Before discussing those details, we highlight a few key features of the model.

First, the scalar potential for the complex flavon field
\phi
is discussed in Ref.
[
23
]
. As
\phi
is responsible for the spontaneous breaking of
U(1)_{\rm FN}
, the associated pseudoscalar
a
(see Eq. (
3
)) is a Goldstone mode and is massless at this stage. A soft
U(1)_{\rm FN}
-breaking term can generate a finite mass
m_{a}
. The scalar component
s
acquires a mass of order
m_{s}\simeq\sqrt{2}\,v_{\phi}
, leading to a hierarchical spectrum with
m_{a}\ll m_{s}
. As noted in Ref.
[
23
]
, the heavy scalar
s
decouples from low-energy phenomenology, so in the present analysis the pseudoscalar mass
m_{a}
remains the only relevant free parameter in the flavon sector.

Second, as extensively explored in Ref.
[
23
]
, the lightest of the three RHNs,
N_{1}
, serves as the DM candidate. Its stability is ensured by imposing an additional
Z_{2}
symmetry under which only
N_{1}
is odd. This symmetry forbids the couplings
c^{i1}_{\nu}=0
and
c^{1k}_{N}=c^{k1}_{N}=0
for
k=2,3
, thereby preventing
N_{1}
from mixing with the other RHNs and from decaying into SM states.

We now return to the discussion of the individual FN charge assignments for the fermions in our model. The quark sector is relatively straightforward: the observed quark masses are obtained by diagonalizing the mass matrices in Eq. (
4
), while the CKM matrix is determined from the corresponding rotation matrices, given by

V_{\rm CKM}=U_{L}^{u\dagger}U_{L}^{d}\,.

(11)

A possible assignment is

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}q_{Q_{1}}&q_{Q_{2}}&q_{Q_{3}}\\
q_{u}&q_{c}&q_{t}\\
q_{d}&q_{s}&q_{b}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}3&2&0\\
-5&-1&0\\
-4&-3&-3\end{array}\right).\penalty\ \penalty\ \end{gathered}

The coefficients
c_{u,d}^{ij}
appearing in Eq. (
2
) are determined by performing a
\chi^{2}
analysis using the experimentally measured quark masses and CKM matrix elements. In this work, we allow these coefficients to take complex values. The resulting best-fit parameters are presented in Appendix
A
.

The FN charge assignments of the RHNs are especially important in our framework: they control both the light-neutrino mass/mixing structure and leptogenesis. Expanding the last three terms of Eq. (
2
) around the FN and electroweak vevs, we obtain

-\mathcal{L}_{m_{\rm lepton}}=m_{e}^{ij}\overline{e^{i}_{L}}e_{R}^{j}+m_{D}^{ij}\overline{\nu^{i}_{L}}N_{R}^{j}+\frac{1}{2}m_{M}^{ij}\overline{N^{ci}_{R}}N^{j}_{R}+\text{h.c.}\,,

(18)

where the mass matrices are

\displaystyle m_{e}^{ij}=c_{e}^{ij}\epsilon^{{n}_{e}^{ij}}v_{EW},\penalty\ m_{D}^{ij}=c_{\nu}^{ij}\epsilon^{{n}_{\nu}^{ij}}v_{EW},\penalty\ m_{M}^{ij}=c_{N}^{ij}\epsilon^{n_{N}^{ij}}M.

(19)

The Type-I seesaw mechanism generates the mass matrix for the light neutrinos as

m_{\nu}=m_{D}m_{M}^{-1}m_{D}^{T}\,.

(20)

We adopt the top-down parametrization introduced in Ref.
[
9
]
to connect high-energy seesaw parameters with low-energy neutrino observables. Without loss of generality, we work in the basis where the RHN Majorana mass matrix is diagonal:

m_{M}=D_{m_{M}}=\text{diag}(m_{N_{1}},m_{N_{2}},m_{N_{3}}),\qquad m_{N_{1}}\leq m_{N_{2}}\leq m_{N_{3}}.

(21)

The Dirac mass matrix in the Lagrangian (
18
) can be diagonalized via a bi-unitary transformation

m_{D}=V_{L}^{*}D_{m_{D}}V_{R}^{\dagger}\,,

(22)

where
D_{m_{D}}
= diag
(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
,
y_{i}\geq 0
and
y_{1}\leq y_{2}\leq y_{3}
.
Here
V_{R}
and
V_{L}
are unitary matrices acting in the flavor spaces of the left-handed lepton doublets and RHNs, respectively.
Substituting Eq. (
22

m_{\nu}=m_{D}D_{m_{N}}^{-1}m_{D}^{T}=V_{L}^{*}D_{m_{D}}V_{R}^{\dagger}D_{m_{N}}^{-1}V_{R}^{*}D_{m_{D}}V_{L}^{\dagger}\,.

(23)

It is convenient to define

m_{\nu}^{\prime}=D_{m_{D}}V_{R}^{\dagger}D_{m_{N}}^{-1}V_{R}^{*}D_{m_{D}},

(24)

which is independent of the left-handed rotation matrix
V_{L}
. The light-neutrino mass matrix
m_{\nu}
(in Eq. (

with a unitary matrix

W_{L}^{T}\,m_{\nu}^{\prime}\,W_{L}=D_{m_{\nu}}.

(25)

The PMNS matrix is therefore given by

U_{\rm PMNS}=V_{L}\,W_{L}.

(26)

Thus, starting from the high-energy parameters

,
m_{N_{i}}
,
V_{R}
, and

, one can construct the light-neutrino mass matrix

in the basis where both the charged-lepton mass matrix

and the RHN mass matrix

are diagonal.

We emphasize that the DM candidate

does not participate in the seesaw mechanism. Consequently, one light neutrino remains massless in this framework. The interactions of

arise solely from the last term in Eq. (
2

to the scalar (
s
) and pseudoscalar (

) flavon components.

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{\text{DM}}\

\displaystyle\supset\ \frac{1}{2}\,c_{N}^{11}\epsilon^{n_{N}^{11}}\left(1+n_{N}^{11}\,\frac{s+ia}{\sqrt{2}v_{\phi}}\right)M\overline{N_{R}^{c\,1}}N_{R}^{1}\ +\ \text{h.c.}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,m_{\text{DM}}\overline{N^{1}}N^{1}+g_{\rm DM}\left(s\overline{N^{1}}N^{1}+ia\overline{N^{1}}\gamma_{5}N^{1}\right),

(27)

Constraints from meson mixing

In the FN framework, generation-dependent flavor charges induce non-universal scalar and pseudoscalar couplings to fermions. Consequently, tree-level flavor-changing neutral currents arise from boson exchange. These effects are most pronounced in channels with heavier fermions, since the relevant couplings in Eqs. (
7
) and (
8

[
23
]

The off-diagonal element

The matrix elements of the four-quark operators

3.2
Kaon mixing

6
Summary and discussion

In this work, we studied a minimal and unified extension of the Standard Model based on the Froggatt-Nielsen framework, aimed at addressing several open questions in particle physics and cosmology. An abelian
U(1)_{\rm FN}
flavor symmetry, spontaneously broken by a complex flavon field, generates observed hierarchies in fermion masses and mixings through higher-dimensional operators. Extending the setup with three right-handed neutrinos enables a simultaneous explanation of neutrino masses (via Type-I seesaw), dark matter, and baryogenesis.

Allowing complex effective FN coefficients is central to this construction. These couplings reproduce the observed CP-violating phases in both quark and lepton sectors, yielding a consistent description of CKM and PMNS matrices. They also induce nontrivial flavor signatures, especially in the quark sector, leading to testable effects in observables such as neutral-meson mixing and CP-violating
B
decays.

Building on our earlier work, where the lightest right-handed neutrino
N_{1}
provides the correct dark matter relic abundance through freeze-in or freeze-out, we focused here on the origin of baryon asymmetry. In this analysis,
N_{1}
remains effectively decoupled from leptogenesis, while
N_{2}
and
N_{3}
generate lepton asymmetry through out-of-equilibrium CP-violating decays. We included both new decay channels and additional flavon-induced contributions to standard decay modes, with the flavon appearing as both intermediate and final-state particle. These effects introduce additional CP-violating sources and modify decay and/or scattering rates of heavier right-handed neutrinos. The resulting lepton asymmetry is then converted to baryon asymmetry through electroweak sphalerons.

A distinctive feature of this framework is its restrictive structure: all relevant mass scales and interaction strengths in right-handed neutrino and flavon sectors are controlled by a single parameter, the FN symmetry-breaking scale
v_{\phi}
. This differs from many leptogenesis models where right-handed neutrino masses and Yukawa couplings are largely independent. Here, the same FN charge assignments that make DM viable also tightly constrain the right-handed neutrino spectrum, yielding a highly correlated setup.

We demonstrated that successful thermal leptogenesis is possible in parameter regions compatible with both freeze-in and freeze-out DM scenarios. In the freeze-in regime, where
v_{\phi}
is large (
\mathcal{O}(10^{7}-10^{9})
GeV), flavon-induced effects are suppressed and results smoothly approach standard thermal leptogenesis. In the freeze-out-compatible region, the much smaller scale
v_{\phi}\in\mathcal{O}(1-10)
TeV implies low-mass right-handed neutrinos, so resonant enhancement is required to reach the observed asymmetry, at the cost of significant fine-tuning.

In conclusion, this work establishes the Froggatt-Nielsen framework as a coherent and economical effective theory that simultaneously addresses flavor hierarchies, neutrino masses, CP violation, dark matter, and baryogenesis. A natural next step is to investigate an ultraviolet-complete realization, which could clarify the origin of FN symmetry and its dynamics while opening broader phenomenological opportunities across low- and high-energy searches.

To evaluate the absorptive part of the one-loop amplitude, we employ the Cutkosky cutting rules, which relate the imaginary part of a loop diagram to a phase-space integral over on-shell intermediate states. As an illustrative example, we consider the contribution to the CP asymmetry, defined in Eq. (
54
), arising from the interference between the tree-level amplitude and the one-loop vertex diagram for the
N_{\beta}\to\nu_{\alpha}H
channel. We first compute the corresponding imaginary part:

\displaystyle 2\text{Im}\left\{\mathcal{A}^{\text{tree}}_{H}\,\mathcal{A}^{\text{*vert}}_{H,H}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}^{\text{tree}}_{H}\int d\Pi_{i,H}\,\tilde{\delta^{\prime}}\,\frac{\Tr\left\{(\not{p}_{2}+M_{\nu_{\alpha}})P_{R}(\not{p}_{1}+M_{N_{\beta}})P_{R}\not{q}P_{L}M_{N_{j}}\right\}}{(q-p_{3})^{2}-M_{N_{j}}^{2}}\,,

(109)

where
d\Pi_{i,H}\tilde{\delta^{\prime}}
represents the measure for the on-shell intermediate state, defined as

d\Pi_{i,H}\,\tilde{\delta^{\prime}}\equiv\dfrac{d^{3}\vec{p}_{{\nu_{i}}}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{\nu_{i}}}\dfrac{d^{3}\vec{p}_{H}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{H}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p_{1}-p_{\nu_{i}}-p_{H})\,.

(110)

Working in the rest frame of the decaying RHN, where
p_{1}=(M_{N_{\beta}},\vec{0})
, and taking the massless limit for all particles except the RHNs, the on-shell conditions for the internal states imply

|\vec{p}_{\nu_{i}}|=|\vec{p}_{H}|
and
E_{\nu_{i}}=E_{H}=M_{N_{\beta}}/2\,.
Using these kinematic relations, we perform the Dirac traces and evaluate the phase-space integral over the cut diagram, which yields

\displaystyle 2\text{Im}{\{A^{\text{tree}}_{H}\,A^{\text{*vert}}_{H,H}\}}=\dfrac{M^{2}_{N_{\beta}}M_{N_{j}}}{16\pi}\int d\cos\theta\dfrac{E_{2}-|\vec{p}_{2}|\cos\theta}{p^{2}_{2}-M_{N_{\beta}}(E_{2}+|\vec{p}_{2}|\cos\theta)-M^{2}_{N_{j}}}\,,

(111)

where
\theta
denotes the angle between the momenta of the internal light neutrino and the outgoing lepton. The numerator of the CP asymmetry, defined in Eq. (
53
), is obtained by integrating the interference term over the final-state phase space. The corresponding measure for the external particles,
\tilde{\delta}d\Pi_{\nu,H}
, is defined as

\tilde{\delta}d\Pi_{\nu_{\alpha},H}\equiv\frac{d^{3}\vec{p}_{\nu_{\alpha}}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{\nu_{\alpha}}}\frac{d^{3}\vec{p}_{H}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{H}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p_{N_{\beta}}-p_{\nu_{\alpha}}-p_{H})\,.

(112)

Performing the integration for the vertex correction yields

2\int\tilde{\delta}d\Pi_{\nu,H}\text{Im}{\{A^{\text{tree}}_{H}\,A^{\text{*vert}}_{H,H}\}}=\dfrac{M^{2}_{N_{\beta}}}{64\pi^{2}}\sqrt{x}\left(1-(1+x)\ln{\left(\dfrac{1+x}{x}\right)}\right)\,,

(113)

where
x\equiv M^{2}_{N_{j}}/M^{2}_{N_{\beta}}
is the squared mass ratio of the heavy neutrinos.
We repeat analogous calculations for the remaining three diagrams, namely the self-energy diagram with an internal Higgs, as well as the vertex and self-energy diagrams with an internal flavon. In the latter case, the flavon mass is kept finite, which modifies the kinematics and the phase-space integrals. Evaluating these contributions, we obtain

\displaystyle 2\int\tilde{\delta}d\Pi_{\nu,H}\text{Im}{\{A^{\text{tree}}_{H}\,A^{\text{*self}}_{H,H}\}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{M^{2}_{N_{\beta}}}{64\pi^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{x}}{1-x}\right)\,,

(114)

\displaystyle 2\int\tilde{\delta}d\Pi_{\nu,H}\text{Im}{\{A^{\text{tree}}_{H}\,A^{\text{*vert}}_{H,\phi}\}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{M^{2}_{N_{\beta}}}{64\pi^{2}}(1-y_{a})\sqrt{x}\left\{1-\left[1+\dfrac{x}{(1-y_{a})}\right]\ln{\left(\dfrac{1+x-y_a}{x}\right)}\right\}\,,

(115)

\displaystyle 2\int\tilde{\delta}d\Pi_{\nu,H}\text{Im}{\{A^{\text{tree}}_{H}\,A^{\text{*self}}_{H,\phi}\}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{M^{2}_{N_{\beta}}}{64\pi^{2}}(1-y_{a})^{2}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{x}}{1-x}\right)\,.

(116)

where
y_{a}=m^{2}_{a}/M^{2}_{N_{\beta}}
.
We now turn to the denominator of Eq. (
54
), which is obtained by evaluating the tree-level two-body decay width, given by

\displaystyle\int\tilde{\delta}d\Pi_{\nu,H}|A^{\text{tree}}_{H}|^{2}=\frac{M^{2}_{N_{\beta}}}{8\pi}\,.

(117)

This completes the derivation of the contribution to the CP asymmetry arising from the decay process
N_{\beta}\to\nu_{\alpha}H
. We next consider the case in which the final-state particle
X
is the flavon
\phi
, taken to be massive (
m_{a}\neq 0
). In this case, the phase-space integration is modified accordingly. Evaluating the two-body decay
N_{\beta}\to\phi\nu_{\alpha}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int\tilde{\delta}d\Pi_{\nu,\phi}|A^{\text{tree}}_{\phi}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{M^{2}_{N_{\beta}}}{8\pi}(1-y_{a})^{2}\,.

(118)

This kinematic modification propagates through the loop calculation, leading to corresponding changes in the loop functions. As a result, the imaginary parts of the interference terms for the vertex and self-energy diagrams are modified, yielding the following expressions.

\displaystyle 2\int\tilde{\delta}d\Pi_{\nu,\phi}\text{Im}{\{A^{\text{tree}}_{\phi}\,A^{*\text{vert}}_{\phi,H}\}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{M^{2}_{N_{\beta}}\sqrt{x}}{64\pi^{2}}(1-y_{a})\left\{1-\left[1+\dfrac{x}{(1-y_{a})}\right]\ln{\left[\dfrac{1+x-y_a}{x}\right]}\right\}\,,

(119)

\displaystyle 2\int\tilde{\delta}d\Pi_{\nu,\phi}\text{Im}{\{A^{\text{tree}}_{\phi}\,A^{*\text{self}}_{\phi,H}\}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{M^{2}_{N_{\beta}}}{64\pi^{2}}(1-y_{a})^{2}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{x}}{(1-y_{a})-x}\right)\,,

(120)

\displaystyle 2\int\tilde{\delta}d\Pi_{\nu,\phi}\text{Im}{\{A^{\text{tree}}_{\phi}\,A^{*\text{vert}}_{\phi,\phi}\}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{M^{2}_{N_{\beta}}}{64\pi^{2}}(1-y_{a})^{2}\sqrt{x}\left\{1+\left(\dfrac{2y_{a}-(1+x)}{(1-y_{a})^{4}}\right)\ln{\left[\dfrac{2y_a-(1+x)}{y_a^2-x}\right]}\right\}\,,

(121)

\displaystyle 2\int\tilde{\delta}d\Pi_{\nu,\phi}\text{Im}{\{A^{\text{tree}}_{\phi}\,A^{*\text{self}}_{\phi,\phi}\}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{M^{2}_{N_{\beta}}}{64\pi^{2}}(1-y_{a})^{4}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{x}}{1-x}\right)\,.

(122)

These results provide the analytical expressions for the one-loop corrections in the presence of a massive pseudoscalar final state
\phi
. As a consistency check, we verify that in the massless limit,
y_{a}\to 0
, each expression reduces to the corresponding result obtained for a massless scalar.
