Abstract
. This paper studies a class of p p -Laplace equations with cubic polynomial nonlinearity Δ p ​ v + ( v − a 1 ) ​ ( v − a 2 ) ​ ( v − a 3 ) = 0 \Delta_{p}v+(v-a_{1})(v-a_{2})(v-a_{3})=0 on complete Riemannian manifolds M M with lower Ricci curvature bounds, where a 1 < a 2 < a 3 a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3} are real constants and Δ p v = div ( | ∇ v | p − 2 ∇ v ) \Delta_{p}v=\operatorname{div}(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v) denotes the p p -Laplace operator. Depending on whether the solution lies in the intervals ( a 1 , a 2 ) , ( a 2 , a 3 ) (a_{1},a_{2}),(a_{2},a_{3}) or ( a 1 , a 3 ) (a_{1},a_{3}) , we employ, respectively, a logarithmic transformation or a hyperbolic tangent transformation to convert the original equation to another one for further analysis. Through a detailed analysis of the lower-bound estimate for the linearized operator of the new equation, and by combining Saloff-Coste’s Sobolev inequality with a Moser iteration, we establish Cheng-Yau type gradient estimates under an additional assumption on p p . 1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with a class of
p
-Laplacian equations with cubic polynomial nonlinearity:

(PCN)

\Delta_{p}v+(v-a_{1})(v-a_{2})(v-a_{3})=0,\quad{\rm in}\,\>M,

where
(M,g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold equipped with a Riemannian metric
g
, and
a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}
are real numbers. Here, the
p
-Laplacian on
(M,g)
is defined by

\Delta_{p}v=\mathrm{div}(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v),\quad\forall\,v\in W^{1,p},

which is understood in the distributional sense. In particular, when
p=2
, it is exactly the standard Beltrami-Laplacian.

1.1.
Background and Motivations

The gradient estimate that combines curvature conditions with the maximum principle has dominated research on gradient estimates for geometric partial differential equations on manifolds over the past half-century. Yau
[
43
]
and Cheng and Yau
[
2
]
established the gradient estimate, i.e., the so-called “Cheng-Yau type gradient estimate”, for the positive solution of the harmonic function equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Delta v=0

on complete Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded from below.
Suppose
(M,g)
is an
n
-dimensional complete noncompact Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature
\operatorname{Ric}_{g}(M)\geq-(n-1)\kappa g
, the Cheng-Yau type gradient estimate of (
1.1
) is precisely formulated as

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{B_{R/2}(o)}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v^{2}}\leq C(n)\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}R}{R}\right)^{2},\quad\forall\,B_{R/2}(o)\subset M,

which provides profound geometric and regularity characterizations of solutions. For the above harmonic function equation, the gradient estimate exhibits an extremely concise form. The elegance of this estimate lies in the fact that its expression depends only on three fundamental quantities: the dimension
n
of the manifold, the curvature parameter
\kappa
, and the radius
R
of the domain. It is precisely this simplicity that makes it a highly general and widely applicable result.

It is worth emphasizing that such a gradient estimate carries powerful analytical implications.
From it, one can directly derive the classical Harnack inequality (an inequality that describes the preservation of nonnegativity and the control of upper and lower bounds of solutions in local regions, reflecting the regularity of solutions and the strong maximum principle). Furthermore, the gradient estimate provides a natural approach to Liouville-type theorems: under whole-space or boundedness conditions, the growth constraint on the gradient implies that the solution must be constant. Thus, the gradient estimate for the harmonic equation is not only concise in form and minimal in its dependence on parameters, but also analytically powerful, serving as a key bridge connecting geometry, analysis, and partial differential equations.

Over the past two decades, many authors have used similar techniques to prove gradient estimates, Liouville theorem and Harnack inequalities for
p
-Laplacian equation:

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}v=0.

Kotschwar and Ni
[
15
]
proved the gradient estimates of the
p
-Laplacian equation under the assumption that the sectional curvature of the complete Riemannian manifold is bounded from below by a constant. Later on, Wang and Zhang
[
36
]
adopted Nash-Moser iteration to establish gradient estimates for positive
p
-Laplacian functions under weaker curvature condition, i.e., the Ricci curvature is bounded from below.
Wang and Zhang’s work is vital, reflected not only in the replacement of sectional curvature by Ricci curvature but also in the introduction of the powerful tool, i.e., the Nash-Moser iteration.

On the other hand, many mathematicians paid attentions to the following semilinear elliptic equation named after Lane-Emden on a Riemannian manifold
M
or an Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Delta v+v^{q}=0.

For instances, Gidas and Spruck
[
5
]
proved the Liouville theorem in the case
1\leq q<\frac{n+2}{n-2}
for
n\geq 2
. Recently, Wang and Wei
[
37
]
adopted the Nash-Moser iteration scheme to prove a local Cheng-Yau type gradient estimate, which is of the same form as in
[

q\in\left(-\infty,\quad\frac{n+1}{n-1}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{n(n-1)}}\right).

This gradient estimate also reveals the non-existence of the positive weak solution to the above Lane-Emden equation provided the Ricci curvature of
M
is nonnegative and hence improves the Liouville theorem due to Gidas and Spruck. Later on, for the case

in (
1.2
) Lu
[

established the local Cheng-Yau type logarithm gradient estimate for positive solutions to Lane-Emden equation (
1.2
) on a complete Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature bounded from below.

One also considered superlinear elliptic problems on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}v+h(v)=0.

In the case

1.2.
Main results

Before stating the main results in this paper, we would like to give the definition of the weak solution to the
p
-Laplacian equation with cubic polynomial nonlinearity (
PCN

Definition 1.1
.

The function

is called the weak solution of the
p
-Laplacian equation with cubic polynomial nonlinearity (
PCN
) on
\Omega

v\in C^{1}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega)
and satisfies the following equality

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\langle\nabla v,\nabla\psi\rangle+\int_{\Omega}(v-a_{1})(v-a_{2})(v-a_{3})\psi=0,\quad\forall\,\psi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).

The first theorem is the classical Cheng-Yau type gradient estimate for
p
-Laplacian equation with cubic polynomial nonlinearity (
PCN

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

•

Suppose

Corollary 2.3
.

Suppose

is a solution (as in
1.1
) with the bounds

, and

then there exist two constants

and
\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}
defined in (

) such that the following inequality holds pointwise on

(2.20)

\displaystyle\dfrac{f^{2-\alpha_{0}-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha_{0}}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha_{0}})\geq\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}f^{2}-(n-1)\kappa f-\dfrac{\mathfrak{C}_{1}}{2}|\nabla f|f^{\frac{1}{2}},

where

, and

with

(2.21)

\displaystyle w=-(p-1)\ln\big(a_{3}-v\big).

Proof.

Define

\tilde{u}(x):=a_{3}-v(x),\qquad\tilde{c}:=a_{3}-a_{2}>0,\qquad\tilde{b}:=a_{2}-a_{1}>0.

Since
a_{2}<v<a_{3}

\tilde{u}=a_{3}-v\in(0,\tilde{c})

\ |\nabla\tilde{u}|=|\nabla v|

\displaystyle(v-a_{1})(v-a_{2})(v-a_{3})=(a_{3}-\tilde{u}-a_{1})(a_{3}-\tilde{u}-a_{2})(-\tilde{u})=-\tilde{u}\bigl(\tilde{u}-\tilde{c}\bigr)\bigl(\tilde{u}-(b+\tilde{c})\bigr),

where we have used
\tilde{b}+\tilde{c}=a_{3}-a_{1}
.
Thus the original equation (
PCN

\Delta_{p}\tilde{u}+\tilde{u}\bigl(\tilde{u}-\tilde{c}\bigr)\bigl(\tilde{u}-(\tilde{b}+\tilde{c})\bigr)=0

which is identical in form to the transformed equation in

Hence, by applying
2.1

applies directly to

(and consequently to

∎

2.2.
The low-bound under the hyperbolic tangent transformation

In Section
2.1
, the translation transformation together with the logarithmic transformation is effective because it converts algebraic-type nonlinearities involving
v
into exponential ones, which are convex functions of
w
.
Convexity ensures that linear combinations of different exponential terms, after completing squares in the estimate of
H(w)
, yield a dominant positive term
\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}f^{2}
under suitable conditions.

Unfortunately, as shown before, the standard logarithmic transformation fails when
v
occurs from
a_{1}
, goes through
a_{2}
and then approaches
a_{3}
.
To recover the method in Section
2.1
, for any solution
v
locating in
(a_{1},a_{3})
and satisfying (
PCN
) we set the conventions

(2.22)

\displaystyle m=\frac{a_{1}+a_{3}}{2},\quad L=\frac{a_{3}-a_{1}}{2}>0,\quad\delta=\frac{m-a_{2}}{L}=\frac{a_{1}+a_{3}-2a_{2}}{a_{3}-a_{1}},

and then introduce the hyperbolic tangent transformation:

(2.23)

\displaystyle v=m+L\tanh w,

which maps
\mathbb{R}
onto the finite interval
(a_{1},a_{3})
.
By the way, the hyperbolic tangent function is the combination of the exponential function, i.e.,

\tanh w=\dfrac{e^{w}-e^{-w}}{e^{w}+e^{-w}}.

This insight will enable us to obtain the Harnack inequality, see the proof of
Theorem 1.5
for more detail.

Lemma 2.4
.

Under the transformation (
2.23
), the
p
-Laplacian equation with cubic polynomial nonlinearity (
PCN
) transforms to

(2.24)

\Delta_{p}w-2(p-1)\tanh w|\nabla w|^{p}-L^{4-p}(1-\tanh^{2}w)^{2-p}(\delta+\tanh w)=0.

Proof.

The mapping
v=m+L\tanh w
is bijective from
w\in(-\infty,\infty)
to
a_{1}<v<a_{3}
, with parameters
m
and
L
as defined in (
2.22
).
Differentiating the transformation
v
, we obtain

\nabla v=L(1-\tanh^{2}w)\nabla w=L\operatorname{sech}^{2}w\nabla w\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\nabla v|^{2}=L^{2}\operatorname{sech}^{4}w|\nabla w|^{2}.

Recall that
\Delta_{p}v=\operatorname{div}(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v)
. We compute

\displaystyle|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v=L^{p-2}\operatorname{sech}^{2(p-2)}w\cdot|\nabla w|^{p-2}\cdot(L\operatorname{sech}^{2}w\nabla w)=L^{p-1}\operatorname{sech}^{2p-2}w|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w,

and thus, by taking the divergence,

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}v

\displaystyle=L^{p-1}\Big[\nabla(\operatorname{sech}^{2p-2}w)\cdot(|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w)+\operatorname{sech}^{2p-2}w\operatorname{div}(|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w)\Big]

\displaystyle=L^{p-1}\operatorname{sech}^{2p-2}w\Big[\Delta_{p}w-2(p-1)\tanh w|\nabla w|^{p}\Big],

where we have used

\nabla(\operatorname{sech}^{2p-2}w)=-2(p-1)\tanh w\operatorname{sech}^{2p-2}w\nabla w.

We turn to computing the cubic polynomial term as

\displaystyle v-a_{1}=L(1+\tanh w),\quad v-a_{3}=L(\tanh w-1),\quad v-a_{2}=L(\delta+\tanh w)

and hence

\displaystyle(v-a_{1})(v-a_{2})(v-a_{3})

\displaystyle=L^{3}(1+\tanh w)(\delta+\tanh w)(\tanh w-1)

\displaystyle=-L^{3}\operatorname{sech}^{2}w(\delta+\tanh w),

where we have used

(1+\tanh w)(\tanh w-1)=\tanh^{2}w-1=-\operatorname{sech}^{2}w.

Now, substituting the above results into (
PCN
) we get

L^{p-1}\operatorname{sech}^{2p-2}w\Big[\Delta_{p}w-2(p-1)\tanh w|\nabla w|^{p}\Big]-L^{3}\operatorname{sech}^{2}w(\delta+\tanh w)=0

and, by dividing by
L^{p-1}\operatorname{sech}^{2p-2}w
(which is strictly positive), we then obtain

\Delta_{p}w-2(p-1)\tanh w|\nabla w|^{p}-\frac{L^{4-p}}{\operatorname{sech}^{2p-4}w}(\delta+\tanh w)=0.

Since

\operatorname{sech}^{4-2p}w=(1-\tanh^{2}w)^{2-p},

the desired equation can be verified.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
n\geq 2
, and

\displaystyle p\in\left[\dfrac{2n}{n+1},\quad\frac{2n}{n+1}+\frac{(n-1)(1+\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}})}{(n+1)\delta^{2}}\right]\cap\>(1,\infty).

Then there exists
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(n,p)>\frac{3}{2}
such that for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
there holds the following inequality pointwise on
\{x:f(x)>0\}
:

(2.25)

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq\mathfrak{C_{n,p}}f^{2}-(n-1)\kappa f,

where
f=|\nabla w|^{2}
with
w
given in (
2.23
), and

(2.26)

\mathfrak{C_{n,p}}:=\begin{cases}2(p-1),&\text{if}\,\,p\geq n,\\[5.69054pt]
\dfrac{2(p-1)^{2}}{n-1},&\text{if}\,\,1<p<n.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall that the expression of the operator
\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})
has been given in (
2.3
) with
w
given in (
2.23
). Its estimate will be carried out in four steps.

Step 1. The rough lower-bound.

Let

\displaystyle B(w):=L^{4-p}(1-\tanh^{2}w)^{2-p}(\delta+\tanh w).

Differentiating both sides of the equation (
2.24
) and taking inner product with
\nabla w
, we arrive at the following

(2.27)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\nabla\Delta_{p}w,\nabla w\rangle&=2(p-1)\left[(\nabla\tanh w\cdot\nabla w)f^{p/2}+\tanh w\cdot\frac{p}{2}f^{p/2-1}\langle\nabla f,\nabla w\rangle\right]+B^{\prime}(w)f\\[5.69054pt]
&=2(p-1)\operatorname{sech}^{2}wf^{1+p/2}+p(p-1)\tanh wf^{p/2-1}\langle\nabla f,\nabla w\rangle+B^{\prime}(w)f,\end{aligned}

since

\nabla\tanh w=(1-\tanh^{2}w)\nabla w=\operatorname{sech}^{2}w\nabla w.

Choosing an orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}
such that
e_{1}=\frac{\nabla w}{|\nabla w|}

and substituting (
2.9
)-(
2.10
) and (
2.27
) into (
2.3
), we obtain a lower-bound estimate

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})

\displaystyle\geq(2\alpha-1)(p-1)w_{11}^{2}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{n}w_{ii}\right)^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla w,\nabla w)

\displaystyle\quad+f^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left[2(p-1)\operatorname{sech}^{2}wf^{1+p/2}+p(p-1)\tanh wf^{p/2-1}\langle\nabla f,\nabla w\rangle+B^{\prime}(w)f\right]

\displaystyle=(2\alpha-1)(p-1)w_{11}^{2}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{n}w_{ii}\right)^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla w,\nabla w)

\displaystyle\quad+2(p-1)\operatorname{sech}^{2}wf^{2}+2p(p-1)w_{11}\tanh wf+B^{\prime}(w)f^{2-\frac{p}{2}}.

The
p
-Laplacian can be expressed as

\Delta_{p}w=f^{p/2-1}\left[(p-1)w_{11}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}w_{ii}\right].

From equation (
2.24
), we obtain

(p-1)w_{11}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}w_{ii}=2(p-1)\tanh wf+B(w)f^{1-p/2},

and then,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=2}^{n}w_{ii}\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle\left[2(p-1)\tanh w\cdot f+B(w)f^{1-p/2}-(p-1)w_{11}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4(p-1)^{2}\tanh^{2}w\cdot f^{2}+B(w)^{2}f^{2-p}+(p-1)^{2}w_{11}^{2}

\displaystyle+4(p-1)\tanh w\cdot B(w)f^{2-p/2}-4(p-1)^{2}\tanh w\cdot fw_{11}

\displaystyle-2(p-1)B(w)f^{1-p/2}w_{11}.

Thus,

(2.28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})&\geq\underbrace{(p-1)\left[\left(2\alpha-1\right)w_{11}^{2}-\frac{2B(w)}{n-1}f^{1-p/2}w_{11}+2\tanh w\left(p-\frac{2(p-1)}{n-1}\right)fw_{11}\right]}_{:=\mathcal{T}_{1}}\\[5.69054pt]
&\quad+\underbrace{\left[\frac{4(p-1)^{2}\tanh^{2}w}{n-1}+2(p-1)\operatorname{sech}^{2}w\right]f^{2}+\frac{B(w)^{2}}{n-1}f^{2-p}}_{:=\mathcal{T}_{2}}\\[5.69054pt]
&\quad+\underbrace{\left[\frac{4(p-1)\tanh w\cdot B(w)}{n-1}+B^{\prime}(w)\right]f^{2-p/2}}_{:=\mathcal{T}_{3}}\ +\ \operatorname{Ric}(\nabla w,\nabla w).\end{split}

Step 2. Analysis of the nonnegativity of
\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2}
.

Regarding
\mathcal{T}_{1}
as a quadratic term of
w_{11}
and choosing
\alpha>\dfrac{3}{2}
, we observe that the minimum of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is given by

(2.29)

\mathcal{T}_{1}\geq-\dfrac{p-1}{4\mathcal{A}}\left(\mathcal{B}^{2}f^{2}+2\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}f^{2-p/2}+\mathcal{C}^{2}f^{2-p}\right)\geq-\dfrac{p-1}{4\mathcal{A}}\left(2\mathcal{B}^{2}f^{2}+2\mathcal{C}^{2}f^{2-p}\right),

where the Young’s inequality has been used and the notation is denoted as follows

\mathcal{A}=2\alpha-1>0,\quad\mathcal{B}=2\tanh w\left[p-\frac{2(p-1)}{n-1}\right],\quad\mathcal{C}=-\frac{2B(w)}{n-1}.

Combining (
2.29
) with the term
\mathcal{T}_{2}
, we choose
\alpha
large enough and thus obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left[\frac{4(p-1)^{2}\tanh^{2}w}{n-1}+2(p-1)\operatorname{sech}^{2}w-\dfrac{(p-1)\mathcal{B}^{2}}{2\mathcal{A}}\right]f^{2}+\left[\frac{B(w)^{2}}{n-1}-\dfrac{(p-1)\mathcal{C}^{2}}{2\mathcal{A}}\right]f^{2-p}

\displaystyle\geq\left[\frac{2(p-1)^{2}\tanh^{2}w}{n-1}+2(p-1)\operatorname{sech}^{2}w\right]f^{2}+\frac{B(w)^{2}}{2(n-1)}f^{2-p}

\displaystyle\geq\left[2(p-1)+2(p-1)\frac{p-n}{n-1}\tanh^{2}w\right]f^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}2(p-1)f^{2},\quad&{\rm if}\>p\geq n;\\[5.69054pt]
\dfrac{2(p-1)^{2}}{n-1}f^{2},\quad&{\rm if}\>p<n.\end{cases}

Step 3. Analysis of the nonnegativity of
\mathcal{T}_{3}
.

Recall

\delta=\dfrac{a_{1}+a_{3}-2a_{2}}{a_{3}-a_{1}}\in(-1,1),

and

B(w)=L^{4-p}(1-\tanh^{2}w)^{2-p}(\delta+\tanh w).

Thus

\displaystyle B^{\prime}(w):=\dfrac{{\rm d}B(w)}{{\rm d}w}

\displaystyle=L^{4-p}(1-\tanh^{2}w)^{2-p}\left[(1-\tanh^{2}w)-2(2-p)\tanh w(\delta+\tanh w)\right]

\displaystyle=L^{4-p}(1-\tanh^{2}w)^{2-p}[(2p-5)\tanh^{2}w-2(2-p)\delta\tanh w+1],

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{3}=L^{4-p}(1-\tanh^{2}w)^{2-p}\left[\frac{2p(n+1)-5n+1}{n-1}\tanh^{2}w+\frac{2p(n+1)-4n}{n-1}\delta\tanh w+1\right]f^{2-p/2},

where
L>0
and

1-\tanh^{2}w=\operatorname{sech}^{2}w\in(0,1].

Define

(2.30)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(z)=\frac{2p(n+1)-5n+1}{n-1}z^{2}+\frac{2p(n+1)-4n}{n-1}\delta z+1,\quad\forall\,z\in{\mathbb{R}}.

We will analyze the infimum of
\mathfrak{g}(\tanh w)
with
\tanh w\in(-1,1)
and then confirm
\mathfrak{g}(\tanh w)\geq 0
so that
\mathcal{T}_{3}\geq 0
.
This can be done in the following way.

Case 1:
Arguments on the case
2p(n+1)-5n+1=0
, i.e.,
p=\frac{5n-1}{2(n+1)}
.

Since
\delta\in(-1,1)
and
\tanh w\in(-1,1)
, there holds

\mathfrak{g}(\tanh w)=\delta\tanh w+1>0.

Case 2:
Arguments on the case
2p(n+1)-5n+1>0
, i.e.,
p>\frac{5n-1}{2(n+1)}
.

The function
\mathfrak{g}(z)
on
{\mathbb{R}}
is a convex quadratic function with a minimum at

z_{0}=-\frac{\delta[2p(n+1)-4n]}{2[2p(n+1)-5n+1]}.

The infimum value of
\mathfrak{g}(\tanh w)
with
\tanh w\in(-1,1)
is either at
z_{0}
(if
z_{0}\in(-1,1)
) or at one of the endpoints.

\clubsuit
If
\delta=0
, then
z_{0}=0
. Hence,
\mathfrak{g}(\tanh w)\geq 1
due to (
2.30
).

\clubsuit
We assume
\delta\neq 0
and, further,

p\leq\dfrac{2n}{n+1}+\dfrac{n-1}{(n+1)(2-|\delta|)}.

This implies
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\backslash(-1,1)
.
If
z_{0}\leq-1
, the infimum on
(-1,1)
occurs at
t=-1
:

\mathfrak{g}(-1)=\frac{2p(n+1)-5n+1}{n-1}-\frac{[2p(n+1)-4n]\delta}{n-1}+1=\frac{[2p(n+1)-4n]}{n-1}(1-\delta)>0.

If
z_{0}\geq 1
, the infimum on
(-1,1)
occurs at
t=1
:

\mathfrak{g}(1)=\frac{2p(n+1)-5n+1}{n-1}+\frac{[2p(n+1)-4n]\delta}{n-1}+1=\frac{[2p(n+1)-4n]}{n-1}(1+\delta)>0.

Summing up the permissible range of
p
above, we obtain

\displaystyle p\in\left(\dfrac{5n-1}{2(n+1)},\quad\dfrac{2n}{n+1}+\dfrac{n-1}{(n+1)(2-|\delta|)}\right]

where the assumption
\delta\neq 0
makes sure the upper-bound of the set belongs to
\left(\frac{5n-1}{2(n+1)},\>\frac{3n-1}{n+1}\right)
and thus the range is nonempty.

\clubsuit
We assume
\delta\neq 0
and

p>\dfrac{2n}{n+1}+\dfrac{n-1}{(n+1)(2-|\delta|)}

which implies
z_{0}\in(-1,1)
, we observe

\mathfrak{g}(z_{0})=1-\frac{\delta^{2}\left(\frac{2p(n+1)-4n}{n-1}\right)^{2}}{4\left(\frac{2p(n+1)-5n+1}{n-1}\right)}.

We require the additional condition to confirm
\mathfrak{g}(z_{0})\geq 0
, that is

\displaystyle\frac{2n}{n+1}+\frac{(n-1)(1-\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}})}{(n+1)\delta^{2}}\leq p\leq\frac{2n}{n+1}+\frac{(n-1)(1+\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}})}{(n+1)\delta^{2}}.

A direct calculation shows, for any
\delta\in(-1,1)\backslash\{0\}
there holds

\displaystyle\dfrac{5n-1}{2(n+1)}<

\displaystyle\frac{2n}{n+1}+\frac{(n-1)(1-\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}})}{(n+1)\delta^{2}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\dfrac{2n}{n+1}+\dfrac{n-1}{(n+1)(2-|\delta|)}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{2n}{n+1}+\frac{(n-1)(1+\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}})}{(n+1)\delta^{2}}.

We thus summarize the range of
p
referring to
\delta\in(-1,1)
as follows:

\displaystyle p\in\left(\dfrac{5n-1}{2(n+1)},\quad\dfrac{2n}{n+1}+\frac{(n-1)\big(1+\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}\,\big)}{(n+1)\delta^{2}}\right].

Case 3:
Arguments on the case
2p(n+1)-5n+1<0
, i.e.,
p<\frac{5n-1}{2(n+1)}
.

The infimum of
\mathfrak{g}(\tanh w)
with
\tanh w\in(-1,1)
must occur at one of the endpoints:

\mathfrak{g}(-1)=\frac{2p(n+1)-5n+1}{n-1}-\frac{[2p(n+1)-4n]\delta}{n-1}+1,

\mathfrak{g}(1)=\frac{2p(n+1)-5n+1}{n-1}+\frac{[2p(n+1)-4n]\delta}{n-1}+1.

Since
\delta\in(-1,1)
, it yields

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(-1)\geq 0\Leftrightarrow(2p(n+1)-4n)(1+\delta)\geq 0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad p\geq\frac{2n}{n+1},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(1)\geq 0\Leftrightarrow(2p(n+1)-4n)(1-\delta)\geq 0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad p\geq\dfrac{2n}{n+1}.

We have both
\mathfrak{g}(-1)\geq 0
and
\mathfrak{g}(1)\geq 0
for

\displaystyle p\in\left[\dfrac{2n}{n+1},\>\dfrac{5n-1}{2(n+1)}\right)\neq\varnothing

since
\dfrac{5n-1}{2(n+1)}-\dfrac{2n}{n+1}=\dfrac{n-1}{2(n+1)}>0
for
n\geq 2
.

Up to now, we have finished the analysis of all the situations.

Step 4. The permissible range of
p
.

Taking account to the range of the
p
-Laplacian, i.e.,
p\in(1,\infty)
,
we claim that the permissible range of
p
for the nonnegativity of
\mathcal{T}_{3}
is

\displaystyle p\in\left[\dfrac{2n}{n+1},\quad\frac{2n}{n+1}+\frac{(n-1)(1+\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}})}{(n+1)\delta^{2}}\right]\cap\>(1,\infty)\quad{\rm for}\quad\delta\in(-1,1).

Combining all these cases and replacing
\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla w,\nabla w)
by its lower bound
-(n-1)\kappa f
in (
2.28
), we claim that

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq-(n-1)\kappa f+\begin{cases}2(p-1)f^{2},&\text{if }p\geq n,\\[5.69054pt]
\dfrac{2(p-1)^{2}}{n-1}f^{2},&\text{if }1<p<n.\end{cases}

The proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 3.1

)

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that all assumptions in

are true, and
\Omega=B_{R}(o)\subset M

\displaystyle\quad\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}\ +\ \frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{t}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}R^{-2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}\eta^{2}\ +\ \frac{\mathfrak{C}_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2},

where
\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}
and
\alpha_{0}
have been chosen in (
2.19
) and the constants
\mathfrak{C}_{1},\mathfrak{C}_{2},\cdots,\mathfrak{C}_{11}
depend on

.

Proof.

We choose a test function
\psi=(f_{\varepsilon})^{t}\,\eta^{2}
, where
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
is a non-negative cut-off function,

f_{\varepsilon}=(f-\varepsilon)^{+}
with
\varepsilon>0
, the constant
t>1
is to be determined later.
Then, multiplying (
2.4
) by the test function and integrating over
\Omega
, we obtain

\displaystyle\quad 2\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}\alpha_{0}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}}f_{\varepsilon}^{t}\eta^{2}-2\alpha_{0}(n-1)\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}f_{\varepsilon}^{t}\eta^{2}-\mathfrak{C}_{1}\alpha_{0}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}|\nabla f|^{2}f_{\varepsilon}^{t}\eta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\left\langle f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\nabla f^{\alpha_{0}}+(p-2)f^{\frac{p}{2}-2}\left\langle\nabla f^{\alpha_{0}},\nabla u\right\rangle\nabla u,\ \nabla\psi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}t\alpha_{0}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-2}f_{\varepsilon}^{t-1}|\nabla f|^{2}\eta^{2}+(p-2)t\alpha_{0}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-3}f_{\varepsilon}^{t-1}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle^{2}\eta^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}2\eta\alpha_{0}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-2}f_{\varepsilon}^{t}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\eta\rangle+2\eta\alpha_{0}(p-2)f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-3}f_{\varepsilon}^{t}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\eta\rangle.

Using the inequalities that

\displaystyle f^{t-1}_{\varepsilon}|\nabla f|^{2}+(p-2)f_{\varepsilon}^{t-1}f^{-1}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle^{2}\geq\mathfrak{C}_{2}f_{\varepsilon}^{t-1}|\nabla f|^{2}

where
\mathfrak{C}_{2}=\mathrm{min}\{1,p-1\}>\frac{p-1}{p}
, and

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}^{t}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\eta\rangle+(p-2)f_{\varepsilon}^{t}f^{-1}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\eta\rangle\geq-(p+1)f_{\varepsilon}^{t}|\nabla f||\nabla\eta|,

and then letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\quad 2\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\mathfrak{C}_{2}t\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-3}|\nabla f|^{2}\eta^{2}\\[5.69054pt]
&\leq 2(n-1)\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}+\mathfrak{C}_{1}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p-3}{2}+t}|\nabla f|^{2}\eta^{2}+2(p+1)\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-2}|\nabla f||\nabla\eta|\eta.\end{split}

We have to claim that these integrals make sense since
u\in W_{loc}^{2,2}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,\beta}(\Omega)
, which deduced
f\in C^{\beta}(\Omega)
and
|\nabla f|\in L^{2}_{loc}
(cf.
[
3
,
30
,
31
]
).

Notice the following two Cauchy’s inequalities

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{1}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p-3}{2}+t}|\nabla f|\eta^{2}\leq\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{2}t}{4}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-3}|\nabla f|^{2}\eta^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{1}^{2}}{\mathfrak{C}_{2}t}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2},

and

\displaystyle 2(p+1)f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-2}|\nabla f||\nabla\eta|\eta\leq\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{2}t}{4}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-3}|\nabla f|^{2}\eta^{2}\ +\ \frac{4(p+1)^{2}}{\mathfrak{C}_{2}t}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2},

then it deduces that,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}\ +\ \frac{\mathfrak{C}_{2}t}{2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-3}|\nabla f|^{2}\eta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(n-1)\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}\ +\ \frac{4(p+1)^{2}}{\mathfrak{C}_{2}t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2},

where we have chosen
t>1
large enough such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{1}^{2}}{\mathfrak{C}_{2}t}\leq\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}.

Moreover, substituting the following estimate into the left hand side of (
3.1
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla\left(f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\nabla f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\right|^{2}\ +\ f^{\alpha_{0}+t-1+\frac{p}{2}}|\nabla\eta|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\alpha_{0}+2t+p-2)^{2}}{16}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-3}|\nabla f|^{2}\eta^{2}\ +\ f^{\alpha_{0}+t-1+\frac{p}{2}}|\nabla\eta|^{2},

it arrives at

\displaystyle\quad\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}\ +\ \frac{4\mathfrak{C}_{2}t}{(2\alpha_{0}+2t+p-2)^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left(f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(n-1)\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}\ +\ \left(\frac{4(p+1)^{2}}{\mathfrak{C}_{2}t}+\frac{8\mathfrak{C}_{2}t}{(2\alpha_{0}+2t+p-2)^{2}}\right)\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

We claim that there exist
\mathfrak{C}_{3}=\frac{4\mathfrak{C}_{2}}{(2\alpha_{0}+p)^{2}}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{4}=\frac{4(p+1)^{2}}{\mathfrak{C}_{2}}+2\mathfrak{C}_{2}
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{t}\leq\frac{4\mathfrak{C}_{2}t}{(2\alpha_{0}+2t+p-2)^{2}}\quad\quad{\rm and}\quad\quad\frac{4(p+1)^{2}}{\mathfrak{C}_{2}t}+\frac{8\mathfrak{C}_{2}t}{(2\alpha_{0}+2t+p-2)^{2}}\leq\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{4}}{t}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\quad\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{t}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left(f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(n-1)\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

After that, due to the Saloff-Coste’s Sobolev inequality in
3.1

\displaystyle e^{-C_{n}(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left(f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right)\right|^{2}+R^{-2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}\eta^{2},\quad{\rm for}\>n\geq 3,

and

\displaystyle e^{-C_{n^{\prime}}(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)}V^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left(f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right)\right|^{2}+R^{-2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}\eta^{2},\quad{\rm for}\>n=2,\>n^{\prime}=4,

we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{t}e^{-C_{n}(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2(n-1)\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{tR^{2}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}\eta^{2},\quad{\rm for}\>n\geq 3,

and

\displaystyle\quad\beta_{2,p,\alpha_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{t}e^{-C_{4}(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)}V^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{tR^{2}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}\eta^{2},\quad{\rm for}\>n=2.

Here and after, for convenience, we fix
n^{\prime}=4
for the case
n=2
. Setting

\displaystyle c_{1}(n,p,\alpha_{0})=\max\left\{C_{n}+1,\,2,\,\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{1}^{2}}{\mathfrak{C}_{2}\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}}\right\}

and
t_{0}=c_{1}(n,p,\alpha_{0})(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R),
then for any
t\geq t_{0}
, the inequality (
3.2
) is true. Therefore, by choosing
t\geq t_{0}
satisfying

\displaystyle 2(n-1)\kappa R^{2}\leq\frac{2(n-1)}{c_{1}^{2}(n,p,\alpha_{0})}t_{0}^{2}\quad\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\quad\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{t}\leq\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{c_{1}(n,p,\alpha_{0})},

there exists a non-negative constant
\mathfrak{C}_{5}=\mathfrak{C}_{5}(n,p)
such that

\displaystyle 2(n-1)\kappa R^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{t}\leq\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}.

Thus,

(3.3)

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\\[5.69054pt]
\geq&\left\{\begin{aligned} \beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}\ +\ \frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{t}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\Omega)},\quad{\rm for}\>n\geq 3,\\[5.69054pt]
\beta_{2,p,\alpha_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}\ +\ \frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{t}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)},\quad{\rm for}\>n=2.\end{aligned}\right.\end{split}

The proof is completed.
∎

3.2.
Local
L^{\gamma}
-upper bound of the gradient

In this section, we will prove the
L^{\gamma}
-upper bound of the gradient of the weak solutions to the
p
-Laplacian equation with cubic polynomial nonlinearity (
PCN
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq-(n-1)\kappa
. Suppose that
v
is a weak solutions to the
p
-Laplacian equation with cubic polynomial nonlinearity (
PCN
) on the geodesic ball
B_{R}(o)\subset M
, then there exists a constant
\mathfrak{C}_{8}=\mathfrak{C}_{8}(n,p)>0
such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\gamma}(B_{\frac{3R}{4}}(o))}\leq\mathfrak{C}_{8}V^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}},\quad\gamma:=\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{n}{n-2}\left(\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}-1\right),&\quad{\rm for}\>n\geq 3,\\[5.69054pt]
2\left(\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}-1\right),&\quad{\rm for}\>n=2,\end{aligned}\right.

where
V
is the volume of geodesic ball
B_{R}(o),
and
\alpha_{0}
is fixed as same as in
3.2
.

Proof.

Setting

\Omega_{1}=\left\{f>\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}R^{2}}\right\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Omega_{2}=\Omega\setminus\Omega_{1},

a direct calculation shows

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}\ +\ \frac{\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{2}}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}}{2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}\ +\ \frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}V}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}

where the volume of
B_{R}(o)
denoted by
V.
Recall (
3.3
), then it draws, by substituting above inequality and choosing
t=t_{0}
,

(3.5)

\begin{split}&\quad\frac{\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}}{2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{t_{0}}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\Omega)}\\[5.69054pt]
&\leq\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}V}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{4}}{t_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2},\quad{\rm for}\>n\geq 3,\end{split}

and

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\quad\beta_{2,p,\alpha_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{t_{0}}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\\[5.69054pt]
&\leq\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}V}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{\beta_{2,p,\alpha_{0}}R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{4}}{t_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2},\quad{\rm for}\>n=2.\end{split}

According to the method of cut-off function, we choose the function
\eta_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{R}(o))
satisfying

0\leq\eta_{1}\leq 1,\qquad|\nabla\eta_{1}|\leq\frac{C(n)}{R}\quad\mathrm{in}\ B_{R}(o),\qquad\eta_{1}\equiv 1\quad\mathrm{in}\ B_{\frac{3R}{4}}(o).

As a consequence, by considering the proposition of above cut-off function, choosing
\eta=\eta_{1}^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}}
, and using the Hölder’s inequality and the Cauchy’s inequality, we obtain

(3.7)

\begin{split}\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{4}}{t_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}&\leq\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{6}t_{0}}{R^{2}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{\frac{2\alpha_{0}+p+2t_{0}-2}{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}}}\\[5.69054pt]
&\leq\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{6}t_{0}}{R^{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}}\eta^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}-1}{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}}}V^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}}}\\[5.69054pt]
&\leq\frac{\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}}{2}\left[\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}}\eta^{2}+\left(\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{6}t_{0}}{\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}}V\right],\end{split}

where we have used

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{4}R^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\leq\mathfrak{C}_{4}|C(n)|^{2}\left(\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}\right)^{2}\eta^{\frac{2\alpha_{0}+p+2t_{0}-2}{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}}}\leq\mathfrak{C}_{6}t_{0}^{2}\eta^{\frac{2\alpha_{0}+p+2t_{0}-2}{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}}}.

Substituting (
3.7
) into (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), it arrives at for
n\geq 3

\displaystyle\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{t_{0}}{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}e^{t_{0}}V^{1-\frac{2}{n}}R^{2}\left[\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{6}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{6}t_{0}}{\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}\right]

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{C}_{7}^{{}^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}}e^{t_{0}}V^{1-\frac{2}{n}}t_{0}^{3}\left(\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}

and for
n=2

\displaystyle\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{t_{0}}{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}e^{t_{0}}V^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{2}\left[\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{\beta_{2,p,\alpha_{0}}R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{6}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{6}t_{0}}{\beta_{2,p,\alpha_{0}}R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}\right]

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{7}^{{}^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}}e^{t_{0}}V^{\frac{1}{2}}t_{0}^{3}\left(\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}

where the constant

\mathfrak{C}_{7}=\mathfrak{C}_{7}(n,p)=\left(2\dfrac{\mathfrak{C}_{5}+\mathfrak{C}_{6}}{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}+1\right)\left(\dfrac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}+2\mathfrak{C}_{6}}{\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}}+1\right)

satisfies

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{7}^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}\geq\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}}{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}\left(\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{5}}{\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}+\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{6}}{\mathfrak{C}_{3}t_{0}}\left(\frac{2\mathfrak{C}_{6}}{\beta_{n,p,\alpha_{0}}t_{0}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}.

Denoting

\mathfrak{C}_{8}:=\mathfrak{C}_{7}\sup\limits_{t_{0}\geq 1}e^{\frac{t_{0}}{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}}t_{0}^{\frac{3}{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}}

and

\gamma:=\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{n}{n-2}\left(\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}-1\right),&\quad{\rm for}\>n\geq 3,\\[5.69054pt]
2\left(\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t_{0}-1\right),&\quad{\rm for}\>n=2,\end{aligned}\right.

we obtain the following result from (
3.8
) and (
3.9
)

\displaystyle\left\|f\eta^{\frac{2}{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}}\right\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq\mathfrak{C}_{7}e^{\frac{t_{0}}{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}}t_{0}^{\frac{3}{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}}\frac{V^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\leq\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{8}V^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}.

Here we have used

t_{0}^{\frac{3}{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1}}\leq t_{0}^{\frac{3}{t_{0}}}\leq\max\limits_{x\geq 1}x^{\frac{3}{x}}=e^{\frac{3}{e}}.

Based on
\eta\equiv 1\>\mathrm{in}\>B_{\frac{3R}{4}}(o)
, it deduces

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\gamma}\left(B_{\frac{3R}{4}}(o)\right)}\leq\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{8}V^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}.

The proof of this lemma is completed.
∎

3.3.
The gradient estimate by Moser iteration: proof of Theorem
1.2

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq-(n-1)\kappa
and
n\geq 2
.
If
v
is a weak solutions to the
p
-Laplacian equation with cubic polynomial nonlinearity (
PCN
) on the geodesic ball
B_{R}(o)\subset M
, then there exists a constant
\mathfrak{C}_{11}=\mathfrak{C}_{11}(n,p)>0
, such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(R_{\frac{R}{2}}(o))}\leq\mathfrak{C}_{11}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}R}{R}\right)^{2},

where
V
is the volume of the geodesic ball
B_{R}(o)
.

Proof.

Recall (
3.3
) without the first term in the left hand side

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}}{R^{2}}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}\ +\ \frac{\mathfrak{C}_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\geq\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{t}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\Omega)},\quad{\rm for}\>n\geq 3,\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{3}}{t}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)},\quad{\rm for}\>n=2.\end{aligned}\right.

Setting
r_{k}=\frac{R}{2}+\frac{R}{4^{k}}
and
\Omega_{k}=B_{r_{k}}(o)
, and choosing the cut-off function sequence
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset C^{\infty}(\Omega_{k})
satisfying

(3.11)

0\leq\eta_{k}\leq 1,\qquad|\nabla\eta_{k}|\leq\frac{4^{k}C}{R}\ \mathrm{in}\>B_{r_{k}}(o)\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\eta_{k}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{in}\>B_{r_{k+1}}(o),

and then replacing
\eta
by
\eta_{k}
in (
3.10
), we obtain for
n\geq 3

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{3}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{2}{n}}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t_{0}-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta_{k}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\Omega_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}t\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}_{k}\ +\ \mathfrak{C}_{4}R^{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}t+C^{2}\mathfrak{C}_{4}4^{2k}\right)\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}

and for
n=2

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{3}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}t+C^{2}\mathfrak{C}_{4}4^{2k}\right)\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+},
we set
\beta_{1}:=\gamma,\
and

(3.12)

\beta_{k+1}:=\begin{cases}\dfrac{n}{n-2}\beta_{k}=\left(\dfrac{n}{n-2}\right)^{k+1}\gamma,&\quad{\rm for}\>n\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]
2\beta_{k}=2^{k+1}\gamma,&\quad{\rm for}\>n=2,\end{cases}

and
t=t_{k}
in such a way that

\displaystyle t_{k}+\frac{p}{2}+\alpha_{0}-1=\beta_{k}.

By

t_{k}<\beta_{k}=\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\dfrac{n}{n-2}\right)^{k}\left(t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+\alpha_{0}-1\right)\quad{\rm for}\>n\geq 3,\\[5.69054pt]
2^{k}\left(t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+\alpha_{0}-1\right)\quad{\rm for}\>n=2,\end{aligned}\right\}\leq 4^{2k}\left(t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+\alpha_{0}-1\right)

drew from the facts that
\alpha_{0}
is large enough and
\dfrac{n}{n-2}<4^{2}
,
together with
t_{0}\geq 1
and

\mathfrak{C}_{9}:=\mathfrak{C}_{3}^{-1}\left[\mathfrak{C}_{5}+\mathfrak{C}_{5}\left(\frac{p}{2}+\alpha_{0}\right)+C^{2}\right],

we then have for
n\geq 3

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{3}\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\beta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{t_{0}}V^{-\frac{2}{n}}\left[\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}\left(t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+\alpha_{0}-1\right)\left(\frac{n}{n-2}\right)^{k}+C^{2}4^{2k}\right]\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\beta_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{t_{0}}V^{-\frac{2}{n}}4^{2k}\left[\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}\left(t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+\alpha_{0}-1\right)+C^{2}\right]\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\beta_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathfrak{C}_{9}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}V^{-\frac{2}{n}}4^{2k}\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\beta_{k}},

and for
n=2

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{3}\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\beta_{k+1}}\eta_{k}^{4}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{t_{0}}V^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[\mathfrak{C}_{5}t_{0}^{2}\left(t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+\alpha_{0}-1\right)2^{k}+C^{2}4^{2k}\right]\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\beta_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathfrak{C}_{9}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}V^{-\frac{1}{2}}4^{2k}\int_{\Omega_{k}}f^{\beta_{k}}.

Taking
\beta^{-1}
-th power of the both sides of above inequality and then substituting (
3.11
), it holds

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\beta_{k+1}}(\Omega_{k+1})}\leq\begin{cases}\left(2\mathfrak{C}_{9}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}V^{-\frac{2}{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}}4^{\frac{2k}{\beta_{k}}}\|f\|_{L^{\beta_{k}}(\Omega_{k})}&\quad{\rm for}\>n\geq 3;\\[5.69054pt]
\left(2\mathfrak{C}_{9}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}V^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}}4^{\frac{2k}{\beta_{k}}}\|f\|_{L^{\beta_{k}}(\Omega_{k})}&\quad{\rm for}\>n=2.\end{cases}

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}=\frac{\beta_{1}^{-1}}{1-\frac{n-2}{n}}=\frac{n}{2\beta_{1}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{\beta_{k}}=\frac{n^{2}}{4\beta_{1}},

letting
k\rightarrow\infty
and choosing

\mathfrak{C}_{10}=\mathfrak{C}_{10}(n,p,\mathfrak{C}_{9})\geq\left(2\mathfrak{C}_{9}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2\beta_{1}}}4^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2k}{\beta_{k}}},

it derives to for all
n\geq 2

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\frac{R}{2}}(o)\right)}\leq\left(2\mathfrak{C}_{9}t_{0}^{3}e^{t_{0}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2\beta_{1}}}4^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2k}{\beta_{k}}}V^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\|f\|_{L^{\beta}\left(B_{\frac{3R}{4}}(o)\right)}\leq\mathfrak{C}_{10}V^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\|f\|_{L^{\beta}\left(B_{\frac{3R}{4}}(o)\right)}.

In fact, it is easy to observe that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{9}^{\frac{n-2}{2(\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1)}},\quad t_{0}^{{\frac{3(n-2)}{2(\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1)}}},\quad e^{{\frac{(n-2)t_{0}}{2(\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1)}}},\quad 16^{{\frac{n(n-2)}{2(\alpha_{0}+t_{0}+\frac{p}{2}-1)}}}

are all uniformly bounded, which enable an upper bound depending on
n
and
p
.

Utilizing the estimate (
3.4
) in
3.3
and
\mathfrak{C}_{11}=\mathfrak{C}_{10}\mathfrak{C}_{8}c_{1}
,
we finally gain the
L^{\infty}
upper-estimate to the gradient function
f=|\nabla w|^{2}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{R}{2}}(o))}\leq\mathfrak{C}_{11}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}R}{R}\right)^{2}.

We finish the proof of the lemma as well as Theorem
1.2
.
∎

We will use the gradient estimate in

to prove

and

.

Proof of
Theorem 1.4
:

The choice
\kappa=0
in
Theorem 1.2
concludes that in the case
a_{1}<v<a_{2}
, there holds

\dfrac{|\nabla v(x_{0})|}{v(x_{0})-a_{1}}\leq\sup\limits_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})}\dfrac{|\nabla v|}{v-a_{1}}\leq C(n,p)R^{-1},\>\,\forall\,x_{0}\in M;

in the case
a_{2}<v<a_{3}
,

\dfrac{|\nabla v(x_{0})|}{a_{3}-v(x_{0})}\leq\sup\limits_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})}\dfrac{|\nabla v|}{a_{3}-v}\leq C(n,p)R^{-1},\ \,\forall\,x_{0}\in M;

and in the case
a_{1}<v<a_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{(a_{3}-a_{1})^{2}|\nabla v(x_{0})|^{2}}{4[(v(x_{0})-a_{1})(a_{3}-v(x_{0}))]^{2}}\leq\sup\limits_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})}\dfrac{(a_{3}-a_{1})^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}}{4[(v-a_{1})(a_{3}-v)]^{2}}\leq C(n,p)R^{-1},\ \,\forall\,x_{0}\in M.

In fact, recall

\nabla v=L(1-\tanh^{2}w)\nabla w,\quad v-a_{1}=L(1+\tanh w),\quad\mbox{and}\quad v-a_{3}=L(\tanh w-1)

in Section
2.2
, we claim

\displaystyle\nabla w=\dfrac{(a_{3}-a_{1})\nabla v}{2(v-a_{1})(a_{3}-v)}.

And then letting
R\rightarrow\infty
, it gives

\displaystyle\nabla v(x_{0})=0,\quad\,\forall\,x_{0}\in M,

which concludes that
v\equiv\mathrm{const}
and
\Delta_{p}v=0
.
Recalling the cubic polynomial nonlinearity in (
PCN
), we observe the only three constant solutions are
v=a_{1}
,
v=a_{2}
and
v=a_{3}
. With this insight, we claim

•

If
p
satisfies (
1.6
) and
a_{1}<v<a_{2}
or
a_{2}<v<a_{3}
, the equation (
PCN
) admits no solution.

•

If
p
satisfies (
1.9
) and
a_{1}<v<a_{3}
, the equation (
PCN
) admits the unique solution
v=a_{2}.

Here end the proof.
∎

Proof of
Theorem 1.5
:

According to
Theorem 1.2
,

\displaystyle|\nabla w(x)|\leq C(n,p)\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}R}{R},\ \>\forall\,x\in M.

As a result, by letting
R\rightarrow\infty
,

(3.13)

\displaystyle|\nabla w(x)|\leq C(n,p)\sqrt{\kappa},\quad\forall\,x\in M.

For any fixed
x_{0}\in M
,
\forall\,x\in M
, we choose a minimizing geodesic
\varphi(t)
connecting
x
and
x_{0}
:

\displaystyle\varphi:[0,\ell]\rightarrow M,\quad\varphi(0)=x_{0},\quad\varphi(\ell)=x,

where
\ell=\mathrm{dist}(x,x_{0})
is the distance between
x
and
x_{0}
. It is easy to see

(3.14)

\displaystyle w(x)-w(x_{0})=\int_{0}^{\ell}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}w\circ\varphi(t)\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{\ell}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}w\circ\varphi(t)\right|\mathrm{d}t\leq|\nabla w||\varphi^{\prime}(t)|\ell=C(n,p)\sqrt{\kappa}\ell,

thus,

(3.15)

\displaystyle w(x_{0})-C(n,p)\sqrt{\kappa}\ell\leq w(x)\leq w(x_{0})+C(n,p)\sqrt{\kappa}\ell.

•

For the transformation
w=-(p-1)\ln(v-a_{1})
with
a_{1}<v<a_{2}
in Section
2.1
, we note that
\ell\leq\frac{R}{2}
and obtain

\displaystyle e^{-C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)/\penalty 2}\leq\dfrac{v(x)-a_{1}}{v(x_{0})-a_{1}}\leq e^{C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)/\penalty 2}

and thus

\displaystyle\dfrac{v(x)-a_{1}}{v(y)-a_{1}}\leq e^{C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)},\quad\forall\>x,\,y\in B_{R/\penalty 2}(x_{0}).

Similarly, for the transformation
w=-(p-1)\ln(a_{3}-v)
with
a_{2}<v<a_{3}
, there holds

\displaystyle\dfrac{a_{3}-v(x)}{a_{3}-v(y)}\leq e^{C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)},\quad\forall\>x,\,y\in B_{R/\penalty 2}(x_{0}).

•

For the transformation

w=\operatorname{arctanh}\left(\dfrac{v-m}{L}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\ln\dfrac{1+\frac{v-m}{L}}{1-\frac{v-m}{L}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\ln\dfrac{v-a_{1}}{a_{3}-v}

with
a_{1}<v<a_{3}
in Section
2.2
,
we note that
\ell\leq\frac{R}{2}
and obtain

\displaystyle-2C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)\leq\ln\dfrac{v(x)-a_{1}}{a_{3}-v(x)}-\ln\dfrac{v(x_{0})-a_{1}}{a_{3}-v(x_{0})}\leq 2C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R),

which deduces to

\displaystyle\left|\ln\frac{\big(v(x)-a_{1}\big)\big(a_{3}-v(y)\big)}{\big(v(y)-a_{1}\big)\big(a_{3}-v(x)\big)}\right|\leq 4C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R),

i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{\big(v(x)-a_{1}\big)\big(a_{3}-v(y)\big)}{\big(v(y)-a_{1}\big)\big(a_{3}-v(x)\big)}\leq e^{4C(n,p)(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)},\quad\forall\>x,y\in B_{R/\penalty 2}(x_{0}).

This completes the proof.
∎
