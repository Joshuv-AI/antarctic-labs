Rigidity of balls in the solid mean value property for polyharmonic functions Thanks: MSC2020 : 31B30, 35B05, 35J30 (primary); 35G05, 35E20 (secondary). Thanks: Keywords : bilaplacian, polylaplacian, higher-order operators, mean value theorem, Kelvin transform.
Nicola Abatangelo

Abstract
. We show that balls are the only open bounded domains for which the mean value formula for polyharmonic functions holds. We do so by adapting an argument of Ü. Kuran for harmonic functions. Also, we provide a quantitative version of the same result.



1.
Introduction

The mean value property is a tool of the utmost importance in the theory of harmonic functions,
as it yields maximum principles, the Liouville theorem, the Harnack inequality,
and it ties the theory of PDEs with the potential theory of stochastic processes.

Here we are interested in the analogue property enjoyed by polyharmonic functions,
i.e.
,
functions solving

\Delta^{m}u=0\qquad\text{in some\penalty\ $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}$ open and bounded, where\penalty\ $m\in\mathbb{N}$ with\penalty\ $m\geq 2$.}

Our main goal is to show that the corresponding
solid
mean value property can only hold on balls,
as it has been shown for harmonic functions in
[
5
,
4
,
9
]
.

Mean value formulas for polyharmonic functions can be traced back to
[
11
]
and
[
10
]
.
Later, similar ones were re-discovered in
[
1
]
.
Below, we present the formulations of
[
1
]
*Table B and
[
2
]
*Theorem 3.1.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq 2
. A function
u\in L^{1}(D)\cap C^{0}(D)

is
m
-polyharmonic in
D
if and only if for any
x_{0}\in D
,
r>0
such that
B_{r}(x_{0})\subseteq D
,
and
0<\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{m}\leq 1
it holds

(1)

\displaystyle u(x_{0})=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}\fint_{B_{\alpha_{k}r}(x_{0})}u=\fint_{B_{r}}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}u(x_{0}+\alpha_{k}y)\Bigg)dy

where
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}>0
are constants depending on
m
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}

satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}=1.

To our purposes, in the notations of the preceding Lemma,
we also need to know the explicit dependence
of the coefficients
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}
on
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}
.
For this reason, it is useful to recall it also to ensure their positivity
(this is indeed not explicitly stated in the references).
By
[
1
]
*Table B and
[
2
]
*equations (3.1) and (3.2),
defining

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}\ni\ V:=\big[\alpha_{i}^{2j}\big]_{1\leq i\leq m,\ 0\leq j\leq m-1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&\alpha_{1}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{1}^{2(m-1)}\\
1&\alpha_{2}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{2}^{2(m-1)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\alpha_{m}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{m}^{2(m-1)}\end{array}\right]

and

(6)

\displaystyle v_{k,1}\text{ as the minor obtained by removing from\penalty\ $V$ the\penalty\ $k$-th row and the first column},\ k=1,\ldots,m,

one has

(7)

\displaystyle c_{k}=\frac{v_{k,1}}{\det V}\qquad\text{for any }k=1,\ldots,m.

The matrix
V
is usually called a
Vandermonde matrix
.
The fact that
0<\alpha_{1}<\ldots<\alpha_{m}

makes it an example of totally positive matrix,
meaning a matrix whose minors are all positive
(see for example the monograph
[
6
]
*Paragraph 3.0):
for this reason,
v_{k,1}>0
and
c_{k}>0
for any
k=1,\ldots,m
.
The fact that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}=\frac{1}{\det V}\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{k+1}v_{k,1}=1

is a consequence of the Laplace’s expansion for the determinant of
V
.

In the particular case
m=2
,
i.e.
, in the particular case of the bilaplacian operator,
the following mean value formula can be found in
[
7
]
*equation (4’),
although it remains a consequence of the one obtained in
[
11
]
.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. A function
u\in L^{1}(D)\cap C^{0}(D)

is biharmonic in
D
if and only if for any
x_{0}\in D
,
r>0
such that
B_{r}(x_{0})\subseteq D
,
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
it holds

\displaystyle u(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\alpha^{2}}\fint_{B_{\alpha r}(x_{0})}u-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1-\alpha^{2}}\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\alpha^{2}}\fint_{B_{r}}u(x_{0}+\alpha z)\;dz-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1-\alpha^{2}}\fint_{B_{r}}u(x_{0}+z)\;dz.

In order to state our main result, we need the following notation.
For
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
open,
x_{0}\in\Omega
, and
\lambda>0
denote by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\lambda}(x_{0}):=\big\{x_{0}+\lambda(x-x_{0}):x\in\Omega\big\}.

In this way,
\Omega_{\lambda}(x_{0})
accounts for a rescaling of
\Omega

around its point
x_{0}
.
It is in general not true that
\Omega_{\lambda}(x_{0})\subseteq\Omega
whenever
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
as this would give that
\Omega
is star-shaped around
x_{0}
.
Nevertheless, for
\Omega
bounded and
\lambda
sufficiently small this will be true:
on the one hand—being
\Omega
open—one can find
r>0
such that
B_{r}(x_{0})\subseteq\Omega
,
and on the other hand—being
\Omega
bounded—
d_{\Omega}:=\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<+\infty
;
then, for
\lambda\in(0,r/d_{\Omega}]
, it will hold that
\Omega_{\lambda}(x_{0})\subseteq B_{r}(x_{0})\subseteq\Omega
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded,
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq 2
,
x_{0}\in\Omega
, and
r>0
such that
B_{r}(x_{0})\subseteq\Omega
.
If for any
m
-polyharmonic function
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\Omega)
,
0<\alpha_{1}<\ldots<\alpha_{m-1}\leq r/d_{\Omega}
,
it holds

(8)

\displaystyle u(x_{0})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m+1}c_{m}\fint_{\Omega}u+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}\fint_{\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}(x_{0})}u

\displaystyle=\fint_{\Omega}\Bigg((-1)^{m+1}c_{m}u(y)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}u\big(x_{0}+\alpha_{k}(y-x_{0})\big)\Bigg)dy

where
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}>0
have been defined in (
7
),
then
\Omega
is a ball centered at
x_{0}
.

Remark 1.4
.

Mind that (
8
) is the analogue of (
1
)
using
\Omega
instead of
B_{r}(x_{0})
as “averaging domain”
and fixing
\alpha_{m}=1
.

Let us now introduce the notation

{\mathcal{H}}_{m}(\Omega)=\Big\{u\in L^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega):u\text{ is\penalty\ $m$-polyharmonic in }\Omega\Big\},

and, inspired by
[
3
]
*equation (1.2), the higher-order Gauss mean value gap

\displaystyle G_{m}(\Omega,x_{0}):=\sup\Bigg\{\frac{1}{M_{\alpha}(u)}\Bigg|u(x_{0})-\fint_{\Omega}\Bigg((-1)^{m+1}c_{m}u(y)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}u\big(x_{0}+\alpha_{k}(y-x_{0})\big)\Bigg)dy\Bigg|:

(9)

\displaystyle u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{m}(\Omega),u\not\equiv 0,\text{ and }\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1}\in\bigg(0,\frac{r}{d_{\Omega}}\bigg]\Bigg\},

where

(10)

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}(u):=\fint_{\Omega}\Bigg|(-1)^{m+1}c_{m}u(y)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}\,u\big(x_{0}+\alpha_{k}(y-x_{0})\big)\Bigg|\;dy.

It is possible to give an alternative version of Theorem
1.3

in quantitative form, by estimating the difference between the domain
\Omega

and the ball
B_{r}(x_{0})
in terms of the above Gauss mean value gap.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded,
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq 2
,
and
x_{0}\in\Omega
with
r:=\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)>0
.
There exists
C>0
, depending only on
n
and
m
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})|}{|\Omega|}\leq C\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{m^{2}-m}}{r^{m^{2}-m}}\,G_{m}(\Omega,x_{0}).

We give the proof of Theorem
1.3
in Section
2

after a preliminary Lemma. Section
3
contains the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
Finally, we defer to the Appendix some facts about the coefficient matrix
V

defined in (
1
) that we need in our analysis.

2.
Rigidity of balls: proof of Theorem
1.3

We follow a strategy designed in
[
9
]
for the analogue problem
in the harmonic case. For this, we first need to carefully pick a specific

m
-polyharmonic function.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N},m\geq 2
.
For any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m-1}\in\mathbb{R}
, the function

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{z\}\ x\longmapsto\frac{|x|^{2}-|z|^{2}}{{|x-z|}^{n}}\>\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\!\Big(\lambda_{k}^{2}-|x|^{2}\Big)

is
m
-polyharmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{z\}
.

Proof.

Let us consider the function

\displaystyle h(x):=z\cdot x\>\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\!\Big(\big(\lambda_{k}^{2}-|z|^{2}\big)|x|^{2}-2\big(\lambda_{k}^{2}+|z|^{2}\big)z\cdot x+\big(\lambda_{k}^{2}-|z|^{2}\big)|z|^{2}\Big)\qquad\text{for any }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

As
h
is a polynomial of degree
2m-1
, it is
m
-polyharmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Also, we may as well write it as

\displaystyle h(x)=z\cdot x\>\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\!\Big(\big(\lambda_{k}^{2}-|z|^{2}\big)|x-z|^{2}-4|z|^{2}z\cdot x\Big)\qquad\text{for any }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Its Kelvin transform, with pole at
z
, is given by

\displaystyle Kh(x)

\displaystyle={|x-z|}^{2m-n}h\bigg(2|z|^{2}\frac{x-z}{{|x-z|}^{2}}+z\bigg)

\displaystyle={|x-z|}^{2m-n}\bigg(2|z|^{2}\frac{z\cdot x-|z|^{2}}{{|x-z|}^{2}}+|z|^{2}\bigg)\times

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\Bigg[\big(\lambda_{k}^{2}-|z|^{2}\big)\frac{4|z|^{4}}{{|x-z|}^{2}}-4|z|^{2}\bigg(2|z|^{2}\frac{z\cdot x-|z|^{2}}{{|x-z|}^{2}}+|z|^{2}\bigg)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{|x-z|}^{n}}\bigg(2|z|^{2}z\cdot x-2|z|^{4}+|z|^{2}|x-z|^{2}\bigg)\times

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\Bigg[\big(\lambda_{k}^{2}-|z|^{2}\big)4|z|^{4}-4|z|^{2}\bigg(2|z|^{2}z\cdot x-2|z|^{4}+|z|^{2}|x-z|^{2}\bigg)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{4^{m-1}|z|^{2+4(m-1)}}{{|x-z|}^{n}}\big(|x|^{2}-|z|^{2}\big)\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\Bigg[\lambda_{k}^{2}-|z|^{2}-2z\cdot x+2|z|^{2}-|x-z|^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{4^{m-1}|z|^{2+4(m-1)}}{{|x-z|}^{n}}\big(|x|^{2}-|z|^{2}\big)\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\Big(\lambda_{k}^{2}-|x|^{2}\Big)\qquad\text{for any }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{z\}.

As the Kelvin transform preserves the
m
-polyharmonicity
(see
[
8
]
*Lemma 6.14)
our claim is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
r:=\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)>0
so that
B_{r}(x_{0})\subseteq\Omega

and it is possible to choose
z\in\partial B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\partial\Omega
.
For simplicity, and without loss of generality, suppose that
x_{0}=0
.

Suppose we are given an
m
-polyharmonic function
u
in
\Omega
such that
u(0)=0
and

\displaystyle 0=(-1)^{m}c_{m}\fint_{\Omega}u+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}c_{k}\fint_{\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}}u.

Then

\displaystyle 0=(-1)^{m}c_{m}\int_{\Omega}u+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}\frac{c_{k}}{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}}u.

The analogous mean value formula holds on balls (
cf
. (
1
) with
\alpha_{m}=1
),
so subtracting the two we obtain

(11)

\displaystyle 0=(-1)^{m}c_{m}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}}u+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}\frac{c_{k}}{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}\setminus B_{\alpha_{k}r}}u.

We now choose

(12)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}:=\bigg(\frac{r}{2d_{\Omega}}\bigg)^{m-k}\qquad\text{for any }k=1,\ldots,m-1.

This choice entails in particular that (since
2r\leq d_{\Omega}
)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}=\frac{r}{2d_{\Omega}}\,\alpha_{k+1}<\alpha_{k+1}\qquad\text{for any }k=1,\ldots,m-2,

and therefore

(13)

\displaystyle B_{\alpha_{k}r}\subseteq\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}\subseteq B_{\alpha_{k+1}r}\qquad\text{for any }k=1,\ldots,m-1.

Define

(14)

\displaystyle u(x):=\frac{|x|^{2}\big(r^{2}-|x|^{2}\big)}{{|x-z|}^{n}}\>\prod_{k=2}^{m-1}\!\Big(\alpha_{k}^{2}r^{2}-|x|^{2}\Big)\qquad\text{for any }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{z\}.

By the preceding Lemma,
u
is
m
-polyharmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{z\}

and
a fortiori
in
\Omega
. Note that
u(0)=0
.
Also,
u
alternates sign on the balls
B_{\alpha_{k}r}
, meaning that

\displaystyle u\geq 0\text{ in }B_{\alpha_{2}r},\quad u\leq 0\text{ in }B_{\alpha_{3}r}\setminus B_{\alpha_{2}r},\quad u\geq 0\text{ in }B_{\alpha_{4}r}\setminus B_{\alpha_{3}r},\quad\textit{etc...}

This means that

\displaystyle(-1)^{m+1}u\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}}\geq 0,\qquad(-1)^{k+1}u\chi_{B_{\alpha_{k+1}r}\setminus B_{\alpha_{k}r}}\geq 0\quad\text{for any }k=1,\ldots,m-1.

By (
13
), it also holds

(15)

\displaystyle(-1)^{m+1}u\chi_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}}\geq 0,\qquad(-1)^{k+1}u\chi_{\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}\setminus B_{\alpha_{k}r}}\geq 0\qquad\text{for any }k=1,\ldots,m-1,

which is incompatible with (
11
), unless
|\Omega\setminus B_{r}|=0
.
∎

3.
Stability via the Kuran gap: proof of Theorem
1.5

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded,
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq 2
,
and
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Then
G_{m}(\Omega,x_{0})<+\infty
.

Proof.

Owing to Lemma
1.1
, for any
u\in{\mathcal{H}}_{m}(\Omega)
it holds

u(x_{0})=\fint_{B_{r}}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}u(x_{0}+\alpha_{k}y)\Bigg)dy\qquad\text{for any }r>0\text{ such that }B_{r}(x_{0})\subseteq\Omega.

Therefore, recalling (
9
) and (
10
),

\displaystyle\Bigg|u(x_{0})-\fint_{\Omega}\Bigg((-1)^{m+1}c_{m}u(y)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}u\big(x_{0}+\alpha_{k}(y-x_{0})\big)\Bigg)dy\Bigg|\leq

\displaystyle\leq|u(x_{0})|+M_{\alpha}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{B_{r}}\Bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}u(x_{0}+\alpha_{k}y)\Bigg|dy+M_{\alpha}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega|}{|B_{r}|}M_{\alpha}(u)+M_{\alpha}(u).

Thus

G_{m}(\Omega,x_{0})\leq\frac{|\Omega|}{|B_{r}|}+1,

which proves our claim.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Suppose without loss of generality that
x_{0}=0
and that
r:=\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\Omega)
.
Pick
z\in\partial B_{r}\cap\partial\Omega
.
Let us consider the function defined in (
14
)
(together with (
12
)),
which we recall being
m
-polyharmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{z\}
and, therefore, in
\Omega
.
Then

\displaystyle G_{m}(\Omega,0)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{M_{\alpha}(u)}\Bigg|u(0)-(-1)^{m+1}c_{m}\fint_{\Omega}u-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}\fint_{\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}}u\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\Bigg|(-1)^{m+1}c_{m}\int_{\Omega}u+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{c_{k}}{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}}u\Bigg|.

Note that in virtue of Lemma
1.1
we have

(-1)^{m+1}c_{m}\int_{B_{r}}u+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{c_{k}}{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\int_{B_{\alpha_{k}r}}u=|B_{r}|u(0)=0

and therefore

\displaystyle(-1)^{m+1}c_{m}\int_{\Omega}u+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{c_{k}}{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}}u=(-1)^{m+1}c_{m}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}}u+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{c_{k}}{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}\setminus B_{\alpha_{k}r}}u.

So,

(16)

\displaystyle G_{m}(\Omega,0)\geq\frac{1}{|\Omega|M_{\alpha}(u)}\Bigg|(-1)^{m+1}c_{m}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}}u+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{c_{k}}{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}\setminus B_{\alpha_{k}r}}u\Bigg|.

We have already noticed, see (
15
),
that
(-1)^{k+1}u\chi_{\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}\setminus B_{\alpha_{k}r}}\geq 0

for any
k=1,\ldots,m
.
This allows us to drop the absolute value in (
16
) and write

\displaystyle G_{m}(\Omega,0)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{|\Omega|M_{\alpha}(u)}\Bigg[(-1)^{m+1}c_{m}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}}u+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{c_{k}}{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{\alpha_{k}}\setminus B_{\alpha_{k}r}}u\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Omega|M_{\alpha}(u)}\Bigg[(-1)^{m+1}c_{m}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}}u+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}}u(\alpha_{k}x)\;dx\Bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{1}}{|\Omega|M_{\alpha}(u)}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}}u(\alpha_{1}x)\;dx.

In this setting, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}}u(\alpha_{1}x)\;dx\geq\frac{\alpha_{1}^{2}r^{2}\big(r^{2}-\alpha_{1}^{2}d_{\Omega}^{2}\big)}{(\alpha_{1}d_{\Omega}+r)^{n}}|\Omega\setminus B_{r}|\prod_{k=2}^{m-1}\Big(\alpha_{k}^{2}r^{2}-\alpha_{1}^{2}d_{\Omega}^{2}\Big)

and, using the choice done in (
12
),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}}u(\alpha_{1}x)\;dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{r^{2m}}{2^{2m-2}d_{\Omega}^{2m-2}}\bigg(\frac{r^{m-1}}{2^{m-1}d_{\Omega}^{m-2}}+r\bigg)^{-n}|\Omega\setminus B_{r}|\prod_{k=2}^{m}\bigg(\frac{r^{2m-2k+2}}{2^{2m-2k}d_{\Omega}^{2m-2k}}-\frac{r^{2m-2}}{2^{2m-2}d_{\Omega}^{2m-4}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2m-n}}{2^{2m-2}d_{\Omega}^{2m-2}}\bigg(1+\frac{r^{m-2}}{2^{m-1}d_{\Omega}^{m-2}}\bigg)^{-n}|\Omega\setminus B_{r}|\prod_{k=2}^{m}\frac{r^{2m-2k+2}}{2^{2m-2k}d_{\Omega}^{2m-2k}}\bigg(1-\frac{r^{2k-4}}{2^{2k-2}d_{\Omega}^{2k-4}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq C_{m,n}\frac{r^{2m-n}}{d_{\Omega}^{2m-2}}|\Omega\setminus B_{r}|\prod_{k=2}^{m}\frac{r^{2m-2k+2}}{d_{\Omega}^{2m-2k}}

\displaystyle=C_{m,n}\frac{r^{2m-n}}{d_{\Omega}^{2m-2}}\frac{r^{2m^{2}-2-2\sum_{k=2}^{m}k}}{d_{\Omega}^{2m^{2}-2m-2\sum_{k=2}^{m}k}}|\Omega\setminus B_{r}|

\displaystyle=C_{m,n}\frac{r^{m^{2}+m-n}}{d_{\Omega}^{m^{2}-m}}|\Omega\setminus B_{r}|.

We then deduce

\displaystyle C_{m,n}c_{1}|\Omega\setminus B_{r}|\leq\frac{d_{\Omega}^{m^{2}-m}}{r^{m^{2}+m-n}}M_{\alpha}(u)|\Omega|G_{m}(\Omega,0).

Finally, we estimate (recall (
10
))

\displaystyle|\Omega|\,M_{\alpha}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\Bigg|(-1)^{m+1}c_{m}u(y)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{k+1}c_{k}\,u\big(\alpha_{k}y)\big)\Bigg|\;dy\leq c_{m}\int_{\Omega}|u|+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}c_{k}\int_{\Omega}\big|u(\alpha_{k}y)\big|\;dy

where, for any
k=1,\ldots,m-1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big|u(\alpha_{k}y)\big|\;dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}|y|^{2}}{{|\alpha_{k}y-z|}^{n}}\>\prod_{j=2}^{m}\!\Big|\alpha_{j}^{2}r^{2}-\alpha_{k}^{2}|y|^{2}\Big|\;dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}|y|^{2}}{{|\alpha_{k}y-z|}^{n}}\>\bigg[\prod_{j=k+1}^{m}\!\Big(\alpha_{j}^{2}r^{2}-\alpha_{k}^{2}|y|^{2}\Big]\bigg)\bigg[\prod_{j=2}^{k}\!\Big|\alpha_{j}^{2}r^{2}-\alpha_{k}^{2}|y|^{2}\Big|\bigg]\;dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}|y|^{2}}{{|\alpha_{k}y-z|}^{n}}\>\bigg[\prod_{j=k+1}^{m}\!\Big(\alpha_{j}^{2}r^{2}\Big)\bigg]\bigg[\prod_{j=2}^{k}\!\Big(\alpha_{j}^{2}r^{2}+\alpha_{k}^{2}d_{\Omega}^{2}\Big)\bigg]\;dy

\displaystyle\leq|\Omega|\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}d_{\Omega}^{2}}{\big(r-\alpha_{k}d_{\Omega}\big)^{n}}\bigg[\prod_{j=k+1}^{m}\!\Big(\alpha_{j}^{2}r^{2}\Big)\bigg]\bigg[\prod_{j=2}^{k}\!\Big(\alpha_{j}^{2}r^{2}+\alpha_{k}^{2}d_{\Omega}^{2}\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{n}|\Omega|}{r^{n}}\bigg(\frac{r}{d_{\Omega}}\bigg)^{2m-2k}d_{\Omega}^{2}r^{2m-2k}\bigg(\frac{r}{d_{\Omega}}\bigg)^{2\sum_{j=k+1}^{m}(m-j)}d_{\Omega}^{2k-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{n}|\Omega|}{r^{n}}\bigg(\frac{r}{d_{\Omega}}\bigg)^{2m-2k}d_{\Omega}^{2k}r^{2m-2k}\bigg(\frac{r}{d_{\Omega}}\bigg)^{m^{2}-m-2mk+k^{2}+k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{n}|\Omega|}{r^{n}}\bigg(\frac{r}{d_{\Omega}}\bigg)^{2m-2k}d_{\Omega}^{2k}r^{2m-2k}\bigg(\frac{r}{d_{\Omega}}\bigg)^{m-k-1}

Appendix A
The coefficient matrix

We have introduced in (
1
) the matrix
V
of the coefficients needed in the
mean value formula (
1
). We collect here the properties that we need in our analysis.
Most of these are surely well known to the expert reader,
but since they might fall outside the cultural background of many others,
we provide some quick justifications.

First, let us compute its determinant.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
V
be the matrix in (
1
). Then

\det(V)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\Big(\alpha_{j}^{2}-\alpha_{i}^{2}\Big).

Proof.

Let us proceed by induction. Let
V_{k}
be the
(m-1)\times(m-1)
matrix obtained by
removing from
V
the
m
-th row and
k
-th column, for
k=1,\ldots,m
.
By using the Laplace’s expansion for the determinant, we write

\det(V)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{k+m}\alpha_{m}^{2(k-1)}\det(V_{k}).

In this setting,
\det V
is a polynomial of degree (at most)
m-1
in the variable
\alpha_{m}^{2}
.
This polynomial has roots in
\alpha_{1}^{2},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1}^{2}
and so

\det(V)=\det(V_{m})\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\Big(\alpha_{m}^{2}-\alpha_{i}^{2}\Big).

As the submatrix
V_{m}
is of Vandermonde type, we use the induction hypothesis to say

\det(V_{m})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m-1}\Big(\alpha_{j}^{2}-\alpha_{i}^{2}\Big)

and deduce the thesis.
∎

When
V
has entries like the ones defined in (
12
), we also need the minors
listed in (
6
). This calculation is performed in the next Lemma.

Lemma A.2
.

For
x\in(0,1)
, let

\overline{V}:=\Big[x^{2j(m-i)}\Big]_{1\leq i\leq m,\,0\leq j\leq m-1}.

If
\overline{v}_{k,1}
is the minor obtained by removing from
\overline{V}
the
k
-th row
and the first column, then

\overline{v}_{k,1}=\frac{x^{k^{2}-k}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\Big(1-x^{2(k-i)}\Big)\prod_{j=k+1}^{m}\Big(1-x^{2(j-k)}\Big)}\,\det(\overline{V}).

In particular,

0<\frac{\overline{v}_{k,1}}{\det(\overline{V})}<\frac{x^{k^{2}-k}}{\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{m-1}}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\overline{v}_{k,1}

\displaystyle=\det\Big[x^{2j(m-i)}\Big]_{1\leq i\leq m,\,i\neq k,\,1\leq j\leq m-1}

\displaystyle=\det\Big[x^{2(m-i)}x^{2(j-1)(m-i)}\Big]_{1\leq i\leq m,\,i\neq k,\,1\leq j\leq m-1}

\displaystyle=\left[\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{m}x^{2(m-i)}\right]\det\Big[x^{2(j-1)(m-i)}\Big]_{1\leq i\leq m,\,i\neq k,\,1\leq j\leq m-1}

\displaystyle=x^{m^{2}-3m+2k}\det\Big[x^{2(j-1)(m-i)}\Big]_{1\leq i\leq m,\,i\neq k,\,1\leq j\leq m-1}.

The determinant we are left to compute is the one of a Vandermonde matrix,
so by the previous Lemma we know that

\displaystyle\det\Big[x^{2(j-1)(m-i)}\Big]_{1\leq i\leq m,\,i\neq k,\,1\leq j\leq m-1}=

\displaystyle\quad=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq m\\
i,j\neq k\end{subarray}}\Big(x^{2(m-j)}-x^{2(m-i)}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad=\det(\overline{V})\left[\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\Big(x^{2(m-k)}-x^{2(m-i)}\Big)\prod_{j=k+1}^{m}\Big(x^{2(m-j)}-x^{2(m-k)}\Big)\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad=\det(\overline{V})\left[x^{2(m-k)(k-1)+(m-k)(m+k-3)}\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\Big(1-x^{2(k-i)}\Big)\prod_{j=k+1}^{m}\Big(1-x^{2(j-k)}\Big)\right]^{-1}.

We then deduce

\frac{\overline{v}_{k,1}}{\det(\overline{V})}=\frac{x^{k^{2}-k}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\Big(1-x^{2(k-i)}\Big)\prod_{j=k+1}^{m}\Big(1-x^{2(j-k)}\Big)}.

∎
