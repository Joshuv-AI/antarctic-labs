1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete gradient Ricci soliton with the potential function
f
satisfying

(1.1)

\displaystyle\text{Ric}+\nabla^{2}f=\lambda g

for some constant
\lambda
, where
Ric
is the Ricci tensor of
g
and
\nabla^{2}f
denotes the Hessian of the potential function
f
.
The Ricci soliton is said to be shrinking, steady, or expanding accordingly as
\lambda
is positive, zero, or negative, respectively.

A gradient Ricci soliton is a self-similar solution to the Ricci flow which flows by diffeomorphism and homothety. The study of solitons has become increasingly important in both the study of the Ricci flow introduced by Hamilton
[
11
]
and metric measure theory. Solitons play a direct role as singularity dilations in the Ricci flow proof of uniformization. In
[
17
]
, Perelman introduced the ancient
\kappa
-solutions, which
play an important role in the singularity analysis, and he also proved that suitable blow down limit of ancient
\kappa
-solutions must be a shrinking gradient Ricci soliton. In
[
18
]
, Perelman proved that any two dimensional non-flat ancient
\kappa
-soluition must be the standard
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and he also classified three-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton under the assumption of nonnegative curvature and
\kappa
-noncollapseness. Due to the work of Perelman
[
18
]
, Ni-Wallach
[
16
]
, Cao-Chen-Zhu
[
6
]
, the classification of three-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton is complete.

One particularly important case is shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature. About this direction, Professor Huai-Dong Cao conjectured that Ricci shrinker has constant scalar curvature if and only if it is isometric to a finite quotient of
\mathbb{R}^{n-k}\times\mathbb{N}^{k}
, where
\mathbb{N}^{k}
is an Einstein manifold with
Ric(g_{\mathbb{N}})=\frac{1}{2}g_{\mathbb{N}}
.

Very recently, Cheng and Zhou
[
9
]
confirmed Cao’s conjecture in dimension
n=4
, together with works of Petersen-Wylie
[
19
]

and Fernández-López and García-Río
[
10
]
.
For four-dimensional complete shrinking gradient Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature, by
[
10
]
the possible values of R are
\{0,2\lambda,3\lambda,4\lambda\}
. Moreover, if
R=0
or
R=4\lambda
, then the soliton is Einstein; If
R=3\lambda
, they are finite quotient of
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}

[
10
]
.
Cheng and Zhou
[
9
]
handled the most subtle case
R=(n-2)\lambda=2\lambda
. In this case, the Ricci curvature is non-negative by the work of Fernández-López and García-Río
[
10
]
.
In addition, the Riemannian curvature is bounded since Munteanu-Wang
[
14
]
proved four-dimensional complete shrinking gradient Ricci solitons with bounded scalar curvature has bounded Riemannian curvature.
However, for
n
-dimensional (
n\geq 5
), we cannot even guarantee the non-negativity of Ricci curvature and the boundedness of Riemannian curvature.
Moreover, we point out that even if the
n
-dimensional (
n\geq 5
) shrinking gradient Ricci soliton has non-negative Ricci curvature and bounded Riemannian curvature, Cao’s Conjecture is still open.

During the paper, we always assume that
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
.

In Cheng-Zhou’s work
[
9
]
, they applied the weighted Laplacian
\Delta_{f}
to the quantity
\textrm{tr}(\text{Ric}^{3})
, the trace of the tensor
\textrm{Ric}^{3}
, for four-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature
2\lambda
and then derived the following nice inequality

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\left[f(\textrm{tr}(\text{Ric}^{3})-\frac{1}{4})\right]\geq 9f\left[\textrm{tr}(\text{Ric}^{3})-\frac{1}{4}\right].

Using integration by parts, they concluded that
\textrm{tr}(\text{Ric}^{3})-\frac{1}{4}=0

over
M
, implying that the Ricci curvature has rank 2, and thus they obtained the rigidity result.

In this short note, we provide a new proof to Cheng-Zhou’s Theorem, and the main Theorem is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

Suppose
(M^{4},g,f)
is a four-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with
R=1
, then it is isometric to a finite quotient of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Remark
. Our new proof is inspired by studying the asymptotic geometry at infinity. Naber
[
15
]
proved that a shrinking gradient Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g,f)
converge along the integral curve of
f
to
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}^{n-1}
, where
\mathbb{N}^{n-1}
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton. In our case,
\mathbb{N}
is of dimension three, and the three-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton is completely known. Another important new thing is that some geometric quantities can be calculated clearly under constant scalar curvature condition, which was unknown before.

In Section 3, based on the point-picking argument, we prove the Riemannian curvature is bounded. With the curvature bound, we can prove that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\rightarrow 0
. In Section 4, we prove the key estimate of
|\nabla Ric|^{2}
,
and finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Notations and basic formulas on gradient shrinking Ricci solitons

In this section, we recall the notations and basic formulas on gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature. For details, we refer to
[
5
,
9
,
11
,
19
]
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete gradient shrinking Ricci soliton satisfying (
1.1
).
By scaling the metric
g
, one can normalize
\lambda
so that
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
. In this paper, we always assume
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
and the gradient shrinking Ricci soliton equation is as follows,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\text{Ric}+\nabla^{2}f=\frac{1}{2}g.

At first we recall some basic formulas which will be used throughout the paper:

(2.3)

\displaystyle dR=2Ric(\nabla f,\cdot),

(2.4)

\displaystyle R+\Delta f=\frac{n}{2},

(2.5)

\displaystyle R+|\nabla f|^{2}=f,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}R=R-2|Ric|^{2},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}R_{ij}=R_{ij}-2R_{ikjl}R_{kl}.

where
\Delta_{f}=\Delta-\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla\right\rangle
is the weighted Laplacian, and
\Delta_{f}
acting on the function
is self-adjoint on the space of square integrable functions with respect to the weighted measure
e^{-f}dv.
In general, the weighted Laplacian
\Delta_{f}
acting on tensors is given by
\Delta_{f}=\Delta-\nabla_{\nabla f}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M^{4},g,f)
be a four-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature
1
. Then

(2.8)

K_{1\alpha}=\frac{\nabla_{\nabla f}R_{\alpha\alpha}+\lambda_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{\alpha})}{f}

for
\alpha=2,3,4
.

Proof.

From the Ricci identity, we have

\displaystyle-R(\nabla f,e_{\alpha},\nabla f,e_{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\nabla_{\beta}f_{\alpha k}-\nabla_{k}f_{\alpha\beta}\right)f_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{\beta}R_{\alpha k}-\nabla_{k}R_{\alpha\beta}\right)f_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{\nabla f}R_{\alpha\beta}+\nabla_{\beta}(R_{\alpha k}f_{k})-R_{\alpha k}f_{k\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{\nabla f}R_{\alpha\beta}-R_{\alpha k}\left(\frac{1}{2}g_{k\beta}-R_{k\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{\nabla f}R_{\alpha\beta}-\left(\frac{1}{2}R_{\alpha\beta}-\sum_{k=1}^{4}R_{\alpha k}R_{k\beta}\right),

where (
2.3
) was used in the third equality. Therefore, we see

(2.9)

R(e_{1},e_{\alpha},e_{1},e_{\beta})=\frac{\nabla_{\nabla f}R_{\alpha\beta}+\left(\frac{1}{2}R_{\alpha\beta}-\sum_{k=1}^{4}R_{\alpha k}R_{k\beta}\right)}{f}

due to
|\nabla f|^{2}=f
. (
2.8
) holds by setting
\beta=\alpha
in (
2.9
).
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
(M^{4},g,f)
is a shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with
R=1
, then
Ric\geq 0
.

Proof.

This fact is known in the literature
[
10
]
, for completeness, we give a proof.
Let
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\lambda_{4}
be the eigenvalues of Ricci curvature. By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})^{2}\leq 2(\lambda_{3}^{2}+\lambda_{4}^{2}),

since
\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}=1-\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda_{3}^{3}+\lambda_{4}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{1}^{2}-\lambda_{2}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle(1-\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}\leq 2(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{1}^{2}-\lambda_{2}^{2}),

hence

\displaystyle 2(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2})+(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{2}\leq 2(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}),

this implies that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
is always nonnegative. Combining with
Ric(\nabla f)=\frac{1}{2}dR=0
gives the desired results.
∎

3.
Curvature bound and uniform decay of
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}

In this section, based on the point-picking argument, we will prove the Riemannian curvature is bounded.
By the similar argument, we can also prove that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\rightarrow 0
.

In order to prove the curvature bound, we recall the following result.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
8
]
)
.

Given a complete noncompact Riemannian manifold with unbounded curvature, we can find a sequence of point
p_{j}
divergent to infinity such that for each positive integer
j
, we have
|Rm(p_{j})|\geq j
and

\displaystyle|Rm(x)|\leq 4|Rm(p_{j})|

for
x\in B(p_{j},\frac{j}{\sqrt{|Rm(p_{j})|}})
.

The following backward pseudolocality Theorem will also be useful.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
13
]
)
.

For any
\alpha>0
, there is
\epsilon(n,\alpha)
such that the following holds.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in I}
be a Ricci flow induced by a shrinking gradient Ricci soliton. Given
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
and
r>0
, if

\displaystyle|B_{t_{0}}(x_{0},r)|\geq\alpha r^{n},\quad|Rm|\leq(\alpha r)^{-2}\quad on\quad B_{t_{0}}(x_{0},r),

then

\displaystyle|Rm|\leq(\epsilon r)^{-2}\quad on\quad P(x_{0},t_{0};(1-\alpha)r,-(\epsilon r)^{2},0).

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose
(M^{4},g,f)
is a four-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with
R=1
, then its curvature is bounded.

Remark
. Munteanu-Wang
[
14
]
proved that four-dimensional Ricci shrinker has bounded curvature if its scalar curvature is bounded. Here in this special case we can give a simple proof.

Proof.

Suppose not, by Lemma
3.1
, then there exists a sequence of points
\{p_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
divergent to infinity such that for each positive integer
j
, we have
|Rm(p_{j})|\geq j
and

\displaystyle|Rm(x)|\leq 4|Rm(p_{j})|

for
x\in B(p_{j},\frac{j}{\sqrt{|Rm(p_{j})|}})
.

By the
\kappa
noncollapsed theorem in
[
12
]
and the scalar curvature is bounded for
(M,g,f)
, we get that
\text{Vol}(B(p_{j},r))\geq\kappa r^{4}
for
0<r<1
and some
\kappa
depending only on the soliton.

For the shrinking gradient Ricci soliton
(M,g,f)
,
the associated Ricci flow
(M,g(t),p_{j})
is defined on
(-\infty,0)
, where

g(t):=(-t)\phi^{*}_{t}g,\quad\frac{d\phi_{t}}{dt}=\frac{\nabla f}{-t}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{-1}=\mathrm{Id}.

Now we can use Theorem
3.2
with
r=|Rm|(p_{j})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
to derive that

\displaystyle|Rm|\leq(\epsilon r)^{-2}\quad on\quad B(p_{j},g(-1),\frac{j}{2}r)\times[-1-(\epsilon r)^{2},-1].

Then we can apply Hamilton’s compactness theorem to obtain that the rescaled manifolds
\left(B(p_{j},g,\frac{j}{2\sqrt{|Rm(p_{j})|}}),|Rm(p_{j})|g,p_{j}\right)
converge to a smooth complete Riemannian manifold
(M_{\infty}^{4},g_{\infty},p_{\infty})
with
|Rm(p_{\infty})|=1
which is Ricci flat because
(M^{4},g)
has bounded Ricci curvature and
|Rm(p_{j})|\rightarrow\infty
, moreover
(M_{\infty}^{4},g_{\infty},p_{\infty})
has Euclidean volume growth.

Since the integral curves of
f
passing through
p_{j}
is a geodesic with respect to
(M,g)
, the geodesic segment of these curves contained in
B\left(p_{j},g,\frac{j}{2\sqrt{|Rm(p_{j})|}}\right)
will converge to a geodesic line in
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty})
, then Cheeger-Gromoll’s splitting theorem
[
7
]
implies that
M_{\infty}^{4}=\mathbb{R}\times N^{3}
, where
N^{3}
is Ricci flat, hence flat. This contradicts with
|Rm|(p_{\infty})=1
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose
(M^{4},g,f)
is a four-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with
R=1
, then
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\rightarrow 0
at infinity.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary, then there is a sequence of points

q_{i}\to\infty
but
(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})(q_{i})\geq\delta
for some
\delta>0
.
For four-dimensional shrinker
(M,g,f)
with
bounded curvature, the associated Ricci flow
(M,g(t),q_{i})
defined on
(-\infty,0)
, where

g(t):=(-t)\phi^{*}_{t}g,\quad\frac{d\phi_{t}}{dt}=\frac{\nabla f}{-t}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{-1}=\mathrm{Id},

is
\kappa
-noncollapsed, where
\kappa=\kappa(n,V_{f}(M))
. By Hamilton’s
Cheeger-Gromov compactness theorem, the associated Ricci flow
sub-converges to an ancient
\kappa
-noncollapsed solution

(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),q_{\infty})
. Consider a sequence of functions

f_{i}(x):=\frac{f(x)-f(q_{i})}{|\nabla f(q_{i})|},

which satisfies

|\nabla f_{i}(q_{i})|=1\quad\text{and}\quad|\mathrm{Hess}\,f_{i}|=\frac{|\tfrac{1}{2}g-Ric(g)|}{|\nabla f(q_{i})|}

on
(M^{n},g(t))
. Since
|\nabla f(x)|\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
,

|\nabla f(q_{i})|\to\infty
as
q_{i}\to\infty
. Combining this with the fact that the curvature is bounded, we deduce that
|\mathrm{Hess}\,f_{i}|\to 0
uniformly as

i\to\infty
. Hence, along the convergence of
(M^{n},g(t),q_{i})
, the sequence
of functions
f_{i}(x)
smoothly converges to a limit
f_{\infty}
satisfying

|\nabla f_{\infty}(q_{\infty})|=1
and
\mathrm{Hess}\,f_{\infty}\equiv 0

on
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t))
. This implies that

(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),q_{\infty})
isometrically splits
as
(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}^{3},g_{\mathbb{R}}+g_{\mathbb{N}^{3}(t)})
, where
(\mathbb{N}^{3},g_{\mathbb{N}^{3}(t)})

is a three-dimensional ancient
\kappa
-solution with
R=1
at
t=-1
.
By the recent works of
[
1
,
3
,
4
]

and
[
2
]
for the compact case, there are four possibilities for
(\mathbb{N}^{3},g_{\mathbb{N}^{3}(t)})
.

(1).
\mathbb{N}^{3}
is isometric to
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma
with
R=1
at time
t=-1
. Hence the corresponding Ricci flow solution is
g_{\mathbb{N}^{3}(t)}=(2-4t)g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
, which contradicts the fact that the time span of
g_{\mathbb{N}^{3}(t)}
should be
(-\infty,0)
from the convergence process.

(2).
\mathbb{N}^{3}
is isometric to a finite quotient of the Type II ancient solution
constructed by Perelman, this contradicts
R=1
.

(3).
\mathbb{N}^{3}
is isometric to the Bryant soliton, this also contradicts
R=1
.

(4).
\mathbb{N}^{3}
is isometric to
(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})/\Gamma
,
then
(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})(q_{\infty})=0
.

In conclusion,
(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\to 0
uniformly at infinity.

∎

4.
key estimate of
|\nabla Ric|^{2}

In this section, we establish a key estimate for
|\nabla Ric|^{2}
on a
4
-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with
R=1
. More precisely, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose
(M^{4},g,f)
is a shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with
R=1
, then
|\nabla Ric|^{2}\leq-0.9(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})+K_{12}
on
M\setminus D(a)
for some large
a>0
.

Proof.

For simplicity, throughout the proof, we denote
K_{ij}=R(e_{i},e_{j},e_{i},e_{j})
.
Following similar argument as Theorem
3.4
, one sees that
K_{ij}\rightarrow 0
except for
K_{34}
. By
(2.7)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{f}|Ric|^{2}=|Ric|^{2}+|\nabla Ric|^{2}-2K_{ij}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}.

This jointly with
(2.6)
yields

\displaystyle|\nabla Ric|^{2}

\displaystyle=2K_{ij}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}-|Ric|^{2}

\displaystyle=4K_{23}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}+4K_{24}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{4}+4K_{34}\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=o(1)(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})+4K_{34}\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}-\frac{1}{2}

Since

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=K_{12}+K_{13}+K_{14}

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=K_{21}+K_{23}+K_{24}

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}=K_{31}+K_{32}+K_{34}

\displaystyle\lambda_{4}=K_{41}+K_{42}+K_{43},

we get

\displaystyle 2K_{34}

\displaystyle=2K_{12}+\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}-\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=2K_{12}+1-2(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}).

Combined with
\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}=(\lambda_{3}-\frac{1}{2})(\lambda_{4}-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})-\frac{1}{4}
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla Ric|^{2}

\displaystyle=o(1)(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})+2\left(2K_{12}+1-2(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left((\lambda_{3}-\frac{1}{2})(\lambda_{4}-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})-\frac{1}{4}\right)-\frac{1}{2}.

Notice that

\displaystyle 2(\lambda_{3}-\frac{1}{2})(\lambda_{4}-\frac{1}{2})

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda_{3}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}+(\lambda_{4}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{3}^{2}+\lambda_{4}^{2}-(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{1}^{2}-\lambda_{2}^{2}-(1-\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}.

then we can obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla Ric|^{2}

\displaystyle=o(1)(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})+(2|K_{12}|+1-2(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}))(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle\quad(2K_{12}+1-2(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}))(1-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}))-\frac{1}{2}(2K_{12}+1-2(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}))-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=o(1)(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})+K_{12}.

So
|\nabla Ric|^{2}\leq-0.9(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})+K_{12}
on
M\setminus D(a)
for some large
a>0
.

∎

As a consequence of Theorem
4.1
, we get the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Suppose
(M^{4},g,f)
is a shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with
R=1
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma(s)}|\nabla Ric|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-0.8\int_{\Sigma(s)}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})+\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Sigma(s)}\nabla f\cdot\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})

for almost everywhere
s\geq a
, where
a>0
is large enough.

Remark
.
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
is only Lipschitz continuous and differentiable almost everywhere. Here
\langle\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}),\nabla f\rangle
can also be understood as follows: At any
p\in M
, choose
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
such that
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
are the eigenvectors corresponding to the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}
, where
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}
are the smallest two eigenvalues of Ricci curvature, take parallel transport along all the geodesics starting from
p
, then we obtain two smooth vector fields
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
in a neighborhood of
p
such that
e_{1}(p)=e_{1}
,
e_{2}(p)=e_{2}
. It is easy to check that
(\nabla_{\nabla f}Ric)(e_{1},e_{1})+(\nabla_{\nabla f}Ric)(e_{2},e_{2})=\nabla f\cdot\left(Ric(e_{1},e_{1})+Ric(e_{2},e_{2})\right)=\langle\nabla f,(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\rangle
if
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
is differentiable at
p
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, we know
K_{12}=\frac{\nabla f\cdot\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})}{f}+\frac{\lambda_{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{2})}{f}
.
hence

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma(s)}|\nabla Ric|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-0.9\int_{\Sigma(s)}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})+\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Sigma(s)}\nabla f\cdot\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})+\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Sigma(s)}\lambda_{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle\leq-0.8\int_{\Sigma(s)}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})+\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Sigma(s)}\nabla f\cdot\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})

for
s\geq a
, where
a>0
is large enough.

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(M^{4},g,f)
be a four-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with
R=1
. then

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma(s)}\langle\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}),\nabla f\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}\leq 0

for sufficiently large almost everywhere
s
.

Proof.

For the purpose, we consider the following one parameter of diffeomorphisms,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\frac{\partial F}{\partial s}=\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}},\\
&F(x,a)=x\in\Sigma(a).\end{cases}

Then
\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}=\langle\nabla f,\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\rangle=1
,
and the advantage of
F
is that it maps level set of
f
to another level set, in particular
f(F(x,s))=s
.

Suppose
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}
are local coordinate chart of
\Sigma(a)
, on
\Sigma(s)
, let
g(s)(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}):=g(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}})
,
d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}=\sqrt{det(g_{ij})}dx
, where
dx=dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}\wedge dx_{3}
.
Next we compute the derivatives of
d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}
.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\sqrt{det(g_{ij})}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2g^{ij}\langle\nabla_{\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial s},\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}}\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{ij}\langle\nabla_{\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}}\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}},\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}}\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\nabla f|^{2}}g^{ij}\nabla^{2}f(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}})d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\nabla f|^{2}}g^{ij}\left(\frac{1}{2}g(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}})-Ric(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}})\right)d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\nabla f|^{2}}(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{2}{2})d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}=\frac{1}{2s}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}.

Hence, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}\right)=\left(-\frac{1}{2}s^{-\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\frac{1}{2s}\right)d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}=0.

Define a function

\displaystyle I(s)=\int_{\Sigma(s)}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)},

since
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
is Lipschitz continuous,

I(s)
is also Lipschitz continuous, hence differentiable almost everywhere,
and then we can compute the derivative of
I(s)
as follows:

\displaystyle I^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{ds}\int_{\Sigma(s)}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma(s)}\langle\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}),\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\rangle\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma(s)}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma(s)}\langle\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}),\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\rangle\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\Sigma(s)}\langle\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}),\nabla f\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)},

where we have used
|\nabla f|^{2}=s
in the last equality.

Moreover, since
I(s)
tends to zero as
s\rightarrow\infty
by Theorem
3.4
, there exists a sufficiently large
b>a
such that
I^{\prime}(b)\leq 0
, i.e.

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma(b)}\langle\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}),\nabla f\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(b)}\leq 0.

Finally, we claim that

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma(s)}\langle\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}),\nabla f\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}\leq 0

for almost everywhere
s
with
s\geq b
.
In fact, if not, assume there is some
c>b
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma(c)}\langle\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}),\nabla f\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(c)}>0.

Similarly, because
I(s)
tends to zero as
s\rightarrow\infty
, there exists sufficiently large
d>c
such that
I^{\prime}(d)<0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma(d)}\langle\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}),\nabla f\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(d)}<0.

Then it follows from Corollary
4.2
that

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma(d)}|\nabla Ric|^{2}d\sigma_{\Sigma(d)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-0.8\int_{\Sigma(d)}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\,d\sigma_{\Sigma(d)}+\frac{1}{d}\int_{\Sigma(d)}\langle\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}),\nabla f\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(d)}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 0,

which is a contradiction. So, We finish the proof of Proposition
4.3
.
∎

Now we can finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

We apply Corollary
4.2
and Proposition
4.3
to infer

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma(s)}|\nabla Ric|^{2}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}=0\,\,\,\text{and}\int_{\Sigma(s)}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\,d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}=0

for sufficiently large almost everywhere
s
due to the nonnegativity of
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
. Thus
\nabla Ric=0
and
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=0
on
M\setminus D(s)
by the continuity of
\nabla Ric
and
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
.
Since Ricci curvature is nonnegative, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}\equiv 0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}\equiv\frac{1}{2}.

Due to the analyticity of gradient Ricci soliton,
\nabla Ric=0
on
M
.
Finally, De Rham’s splitting theorem implies that
(M^{4},g,f)
is isometric to a finite quotient of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{N}^{2}
, where
\mathbb{N}^{2}
is a two-dimensional Einstein manifold with Einstein constant
\frac{1}{2}
, has to be isometric to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
We have completed the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

∎
