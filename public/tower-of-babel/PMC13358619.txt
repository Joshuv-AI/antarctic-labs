TikTok, Xiaohongshu, and Bilibili as sources of medical information on lung cancer: a multi-platform content evaluation
Authors: Jian Ding, Yikun Cheng, Weizhen Guo, Qian Xue, Cheng Yang, Zegeng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLung cancer remains a major public health burden, and short-video platforms have become an important source of health information. This study aimed to evaluate the characteristics and information quality of lung cancer-related videos on TikTok, Xiaohongshu, and Bilibili.MethodsA cross-sectional observational study was conducted using lung cancer-related videos collected on December 25, 2025. “Lung cancer” was used as the search keyword on TikTok, Xiaohongshu, and Bilibili. After duplicates and irrelevant content were removed, 563 videos were analyzed. Video characteristics, uploader types, and content categories were recorded. Information quality and reliability were evaluated using the Global Quality Scale (GQS) and the Decision-making Information Support Criteria for Evaluating the Reliability of Non-randomized Studies (DISCERN) instrument by two independent reviewers. Multivariate ordinal logistic regression was used to examine the associations among uploader type, interaction metrics, video content, and video presentation format with video quality (GQS) and information reliability (DISCERN) separately.ResultsBilibili videos exhibited significantly higher user engagement metrics than TikTok and Xiaohongshu videos (all P<0.001). Regarding information quality, TikTok videos achieved significantly higher GQS scores than Xiaohongshu videos and higher DISCERN scores than both Xiaohongshu and Bilibili videos. Content produced by professional institutions and individuals scored significantly higher than that from non-professional sources. Videos focusing on lung cancer classification and disease mechanisms received the highest quality scores. A strong positive correlation was observed among popularity metrics. However, no significant correlation was observed between popularity indicators and quality scores across all three platforms. Ordinal logistic regression revealed that professional institution uploaders [odds ratio (OR) =5.33, P=0.03] and lung cancer classification content (OR =2.09, P=0.01) were independently associated with higher GQS scores. For DISCERN scores, professional individual uploaders (OR =8.81, P<0.001) and lecture-style presentations (OR =8.82, P=0.02) were significantly associated with higher scores.ConclusionsLung cancer-related short videos on Chinese platforms demonstrate high user engagement but considerable variability in information quality across platforms. Enhanced professional involvement, improved platform governance, and increased public critical literacy are urgently needed to optimize digital health communication for lung cancer prevention and control.

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancerrelated death worldwide, and its high incidence and mortality pose a major public health challenge in China (1,2). Short-video platforms have become a primary channel for disseminating health information (3,4). However, the quality of lung cancer-related content varies considerably, and public understanding of evidence-based medical advances remains limited (5-9). Therefore, dissemination of evidence-based knowledge on lung cancer prevention and treatment through digital media is urgently needed to enhance public health literacy and promote timely medical consultation (10,11).

Studies on lung cancer information on social media have largely focused on single-platform analyses (12). What remains less understood is how platform‑specific algorithmic logics and user cultures shape this disconnect within China’s distinct shortvideo ecosystem. Three major Chinese platforms differ fundamentally. TikTok prioritizes early engagement signals, favouring short, emotionally charged content (13). Bilibili emphasises dwell time and completion rates, promoting longer, lecturestyle videos (14). Xiaohongshu centres on lifestyle and personal experience sharing, where medical information often lacks professional oversight (15). These differences provide a natural context to examine how algorithmic incentives and content norms influence information quality and user engagement.

Three leading Chinese platforms differ in ways that directly shape lung cancer content. These differences provide a basis to examine how algorithmic incentives and content norms influence lung cancer information quality and user engagement through the lenses of digital health literacy and misinformation ecology. We therefore conducted a cross-sectional evaluation of lung cancer-related videos on TikTok, Bilibili, and Xiaohongshu. The objectives were to compare information quality and engagement across platforms, identify factors associated with higher quality, and offer platform-tailored recommendations for improving digital health communication.

Methods

Search strategy and data collection

This cross-sectional observational study analyzed short videos collected from TikTok, Bilibili, and Xiaohongshu. On December 25, 2025, the keyword “lung cancer” was used to retrieve videos from each platform. To minimize personalized bias, all searches were conducted using newly created accounts in incognito mode, preventing prior activity or algorithmic tailoring from influencing results (16,17). Results on each platform were retrieved using default ranking algorithms, without additional filters. This approach ensured that the collected videos were consistent with the platforms’ routine ranking criteria. The inclusion criterion was videos related to lung cancer. The exclusion criteria were duplicate content and videos containing irrelevant content. For each included video, data on title, likes, comments, favorites, shares, source, information type, and content were extracted and analyzed (Figure 1).

Flowchart depicting the screening and selection of lung cancer-related short videos.

Video content and uploader type

Videos were categorized into six groups based on content: causes and disease mechanisms, prevention, diagnosis, treatment, lung cancer classification, and personal experience sharing. Uploaders were classified into four types: (I) professional individual—individuals with formal medical or healthcare qualifications or relevant academic backgrounds; (II) non-professional individual—individuals without formal medical or healthcare training; (III) professional institution—organizations with a defined medical or healthcare function; and (IV) non-professional institution—organizations without a primary healthcare role.

Video quality assessment

Video quality and reliability were evaluated using established and widely cited tools for assessing health information quality (18). The Global Quality Scale (GQS) assesses the instructional quality, accessibility, and usefulness of online health information (19) using a 5-point scale, with higher scores indicating better-organized content and more useful information. The Decision-making Information Support Criteria for Evaluating the Reliability of Non-randomized Studies (DISCERN) instrument is a structured tool designed to evaluate the reliability and quality of health information for patients and healthcare providers (20). Although originally developed for written materials, DISCERN has been widely applied to shortvideo content in recent health communication research (21-23). DISCERN scores range from 15 to 75, with higher scores indicating greater reliability and quality. Full scoring details are available at http://www.discern.org.uk. Two independent reviewers evaluated all videos. Before formal scoring, both reviewers underwent standardized training on the GQS and DISCERN tools, including review of original manuals and illustrative cases. Discrepancies were defined as differences of more than one point on the GQS or more than five points on the total DISCERN score. Such cases were resolved through discussion to reach consensus; if unresolved, a senior expert served as the arbitrator to determine the final score.

Statistical analysis

The Shapiro-Wilk test indicated that all continuous variables were significantly non-normally distributed. Continuous variables are presented as medians with interquartile ranges, and categorical variables as frequencies with percentages. Comparisons between groups were performed using the Kruskal-Wallis H test, followed by post-hoc pairwise comparisons using the Dunn test with adjusted P values. Categorical variables were compared using the Chi-squared test. Cohen’s kappa coefficient was used to assess inter-rater agreement, with values ≥0.80 indicating substantial agreement. Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was used to evaluate associations between variables. Multivariate ordinal logistic regression was used to assess associations among the uploader type, interaction metrics, video content, and presentation format with video quality (GQS) and information reliability (DISCERN), analyzed separately. Before the regression model was constructed, the variance inflation factor (VIF) was tested for all independent variables, and values of <10 indicated that multicollinearity was within a manageable range. P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.4.0.

Results

Video characteristics

The number of lung cancer-related short videos showed a continuous increasing trend from 2017 to 2025, with the most pronounced growth observed in 2025 (Figure 2A). A total of 577 lung cancer-related videos were included in this study, of which 36.0%, 33.8%, and 30.2% were from TikTok, Xiaohongshu, and Bilibili, respectively (Figure 2B). Table 1 summarizes the baseline characteristics of these videos across the three platforms. Specifically, 192, 198, and 173 videos were obtained from TikTok, Xiaohongshu, and Bilibili, respectively. Analysis of general video characteristics revealed significant differences in user engagement metrics among the three platforms (P<0.001). Bilibili videos demonstrated significantly higher numbers of likes, comments, shares, and favorites compared with those on Xiaohongshu and TikTok. Regarding information quality, both GQS and DISCERN scores differed significantly across platforms (P<0.001). TikTok videos achieved significantly higher GQS scores than Xiaohongshu (P<0.001), but no significant difference was observed between Bilibili and TikTok (P=0.41). For DISCERN scores, TikTok outperformed both Xiaohongshu (P<0.001) and Bilibili (P=0.001), whereas no significant difference was observed between Xiaohongshu and Bilibili (P=0.09).

Video uploaders and video content

Regarding uploader composition, TikTok was predominantly represented by professional individuals and non-professional institutions, while non-professional individuals and professional institutions accounted for smaller proportions. Xiaohongshu was primarily composed of non-professional individuals, followed by professional institutions and non-professional individuals, whereas non-professional institutions constituted a smaller proportion. Bilibili, by contrast, was largely dominated by professional institutions, followed by non-professional individuals, with professional individuals and non-professional institutions accounting for a lower proportion (Figure 2C).

In terms of content themes, TikTok content primarily focused on treatment, causes, disease mechanisms, prevention, and lung cancer classification. Xiaohongshu content emphasized prevention and personal experience sharing. Bilibili, conversely, concentrated more on lung cancer diagnosis and prevention (Figure 2D).

Video quality assessment

The Cohen’s weighted kappa coefficient was used to calculate the inter-rater agreement. The values were κ=0.83 for GQS and κ=0.81 for DISCERN, indicating substantial agreement between evaluators. At the platform level, TikTok demonstrated significantly higher GQS scores than Xiaohongshu, but no significant difference compared with Bilibili. For DISCERN scores, TikTok scored significantly higher than both Xiaohongshu and Bilibili (Figure 3). By uploader type, videos uploaded by professional individuals and professional institutions had significantly higher GQS and DISCERN scores than those from non-professional individuals and non-professional institutions. No significant differences were observed between professional individuals and professional institutions (Figure S1). Regarding content themes, videos related to lung cancer classification and causes and disease mechanisms achieved significantly higher GQS and DISCERN scores than other categories (Figure S2).

Correlation analysis of video variables

Spearman correlation analysis showed that likes, comments, shares, and favorites were all significantly positively correlated across the three platforms. GQS and DISCERN scores were also significantly positively correlated, with the strongest association observed on Bilibili (ρ=0.81). However, no significant correlations were found between engagement metrics and video quality scores (Figure 4).

Ordinal logistic regression analysis

We employed ordinal logistic regression to identify factors associated with video quality. The video quality score (GQS) was used as the dependent variable, while the independent variables included the video interaction index (derived from the first principal component of a principal component analysis of likes, shares, favorites, and comments), video content category, uploader type, and presentation format (Figure 5). The video reliability score (Discern) was significantly associated with content type. Videos addressing lung cancer classification were more likely to achieve higher quality scores [odds ratio (OR) =2.092; 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.179–3.710; P=0.01], as were videos explaining disease causes and mechanisms (OR =1.896; 95% CI: 1.189–3.024; P=0.007). Uploader type was also a significant factor; videos uploaded by professional institutions were more likely to receive higher quality scores (OR =5.331; 95% CI: 1.171–24.272; P=0.03) compared with those from non-professional sources. Other variables, including the presence of advertisements, personal experience sharing, diagnostic information, prevention strategies, treatment content, and video presentation format, were not significantly associated with GQS (all P>0.05). The composite video interaction index was likewise not significantly associated with video quality (P>0.05). Multicollinearity diagnostics indicated acceptable model stability (all VIFs <10).

Ordinal logistic regression for (A) GQS score and (B) DISCERN score. CI, confidence interval; DISCERN, Decision-making Information Support Criteria for Evaluating the Reliability of Non-randomized Studies; GQS, Global Quality Scale; Ref, reference.

A second ordinal logistic regression analysis was conducted using the video reliability score (Discern) as the dependent variable, with the same set of independent variables (Figure 5B). The overall video quality score (GQS) was significantly associated with uploader type, particularly for professional individual uploaders (OR =8.813; 95% CI: 4.259–18.240; P<0.001). Content-related factors also showed significant associations with higher DISCERN scores, including causes and disease mechanisms (OR =3.456; 95% CI: 2.050–5.824; P<0.001), diagnosis (OR =2.871; 95% CI: 1.786–4.615; P<0.001), treatment (OR =2.308; 95% CI: 1.416–3.764; P=0.001), prevention (OR =2.099; 95% CI: 1.326–3.322; P=0.002), and lung cancer classification (OR =3.002; 95% CI: 1.613–5.589; P=0.001). Furthermore, the presentation format “lecture” was significantly associated with higher DISCERN scores (OR =8.815; 95% CI: 1.379–56.360; P=0.02). Other variables, including advertisements, personal experience sharing, the composite video interaction index, and other uploader types and presentation formats, were not significantly associated with DISCERN scores (all P>0.05). Multicollinearity diagnostics remained acceptable (all VIFs <10).

Qualitative characterization of misinformation themes

Overall, 54.8% (68/124) of videos uploaded by non‑professional individuals contained misinformation. Five major themes were identified: (I) oversimplified treatment benefits (n=18); (II) misleading claims promoting traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) as a substitute for standard care (n=14); (III) confusion between screening and diagnosis (n=10); (IV) exaggerated risks of surgery or chemotherapy (n=12); and (V) misinformation on targeted therapy and immunotherapy (n=14). Discussion

This study conducted a cross-sectional analysis of lung cancer-related content on three major short video platforms (TikTok, Bilibili, and Xiaohongshu), systematically revealing the characteristics, quality differences, and dissemination patterns of information in this field. It provides important empirical evidence for understanding the communication ecology of lung cancer health information in the short video era. The study findings show that from 2017 to 2025, the number of lung cancer-related short videos increased steadily, with a marked surge in 2025. This trend is highly consistent with the global rise in attention to lung cancer prevention and control, as well as the popularization of short video platforms (24-26). The distribution of the sample across the three platforms was relatively balanced, reflecting the broad public interest in lung cancer among different user groups. However, significant differences existed among platforms in terms of interaction characteristics, quality levels, uploader types, and content themes, highlighting the complexity and heterogeneity of lung cancer health information dissemination.

Significant differences in user engagement were observed across social media platforms in the dissemination of lung cancer health information, which are closely related to the differences in platform positioning, content ecosystems, and user behavior patterns. Interaction index analysis revealed that Bilibili had significantly higher numbers of likes, comments, shares, and favorites than Xiaohongshu and TikTok. This is closely linked to Bilibili’s positioning and user composition. The platform emphasizes knowledge-based and in-depth content, with users actively seeking professional information and engaging in discussion. Its community atmosphere encourages in-depth dissemination, facilitating higher visibility of high-quality health content. By contrast, Xiaohongshu primarily focuses on lifestyle sharing, with users exhibiting more fragmented browsing behavior and lower engagement with serious medical topics such as lung cancer. TikTok’s algorithm prioritizes content with high entertainment value and communication efficiency, and medical health content competes with a diverse information stream, resulting in comparatively lower engagement than on Bilibili. These findings indicate that platform-specific dissemination characteristics significantly influence the reach of lung cancer health information, and appropriate channels should be selected based on communication objectives (27,28). Although social media can rapidly disseminate to larger audiences, its effectiveness depends heavily on the alignment between content format and user motivation (29). Moreover, national-level health communication on lung cancer prevention and control remains limited, with particularly insufficient coverage among ethnic minority populations (30). Future strategies should combine platform algorithmic logic and user profiles to design scientifically accurate yet engaging content, incorporating behavior change techniques to shift users from passive viewing toward active health behavior intentions.

The quality assessment results reveal significant disparities in the dissemination of lung cancer-related information across different platforms. The TikTok platform outperformed Xiaohongshu in both DISCERN and GQS scores, indicating its superiority in information reliability and content organization (31). TikTok also achieved significantly higher DISCERN scores than Xiaohongshu and Bilibili, and its GQS scores were also higher than those of Xiaohongshu. This advantage may be attributed to the platform’s relatively stricter content review policies and a gradually developing ecosystem of professional medical creators, which together improve the accuracy and accessibility of health information (32). Although Bilibili demonstrated higher user engagement, its DISCERN scores were relatively lower. This might be due to some professional institutions’ content being overly technical and insufficiently adapted for lay audiences (33,34). Of note, videos produced by professional individuals and institutions achieved significantly higher quality scores than those produced by non-professional sources, confirming the importance of professional expertise in ensuring the accuracy of medical information. Furthermore, content related to lung cancer classification, causes, and disease mechanisms achieved higher quality scores, likely because these topics are more standardized and less controversial. Conversely, content related to treatment and personal experience sharing is more susceptible to individual variability and potential information bias (35). This phenomenon suggests that while patient experience sharing is valuable, platforms should strengthen the scientific oversight of treatment-related content and guide users to distinguish between evidence-based information and personal anecdotes to reduce the risk of health misinformation. Ordinal logistic regression analysis further demonstrated that the reliability score (DISCERN score) was a strong predictor of video quality (GQS score), while uploader type (particularly professional institutions) and content focusing on lung cancer classification or etiology/mechanisms significantly increased the probability of higher-quality ratings. These findings highlight the importance of prioritizing professional sources and standardized thematic content in improving the overall quality of lung cancer health information.

Spearman correlation analysis unveiled that likes, comments, shares, and favorites were significantly positively correlated across all three platforms, consistent with the general patterns of information dissemination (36). GQS and DISCERN scores were also significantly positively correlated, particularly strongly on Bilibili, supporting the consistency and effectiveness of both assessment tools (37-39). However, no significant correlation was found between dissemination metrics and content quality, suggesting that dissemination popularity is not driven by information quality. Indeed, some low-quality or even misleading content may gain high visibility due to its topicality (40-43). The ordinal logistic regression model also confirmed that the composite video interaction index was not significantly associated with video quality. This phenomenon of “disconnect between quality and dissemination” is particularly concerning in the medical field, as it may mislead public perception and negatively influence disease prevention and treatment decision-making. Existing evidence shows that only approximately 12% of health-related video studies have adopted strict methodological standards (such as defining clear search time windows, using incognito mode, and reporting inter-rater reliability) (37), highlighting the lack of effective regulation in the current content ecosystem. Over half (54.8%) of videos from non‑professional individuals contained misinformation, with oversimplified treatment claims and promotion of TCM as a substitute for standard care being the most common theme. Lung cancer treatment decisions are complex and guideline‑driven; hence, such misinformation may have direct clinical consequences. For oncologists, this means patients may present with unrealistic expectations or decline evidence‑based therapies. This pattern likely reflects several factors. Short‑video platforms rarely prereview medical content, and their recommendation algorithms often favor emotionally charged or absolute claims, inadvertently amplifying misleading narratives (44). In addition, many individual creators lack formal training and tend to overgeneralize from personal experience (45).

Beyond misinformation, systemic shortcomings persist across these platforms, including uneven quality, a disconnect between popularity and reliability, and a high proportion of non‑professional content. Optimizing the lung cancer health information ecosystem, therefore, requires multi‑dimensional interventions. Platforms should strengthen medical content review, algorithmically promote highquality professional videos, and introduce credibility labels such as “medically reviewed” (46). Professional oncologists should produce more engaging and accessible content that balances rigor with clarity, directly countering common false claims. Regulatory agencies need to establish quality standards for short‑video health information to guide its release and dissemination. Viewers can also benefit from simple heuristics, such as checking uploader credentials, treating absolute language (“cures all”, “no side effects”) as a red flag, and cross‑referencing with official guidelines.

This study provides empirical evidence to support targeted digital health communication strategies, with direct implications for improving public literacy and reducing disease burden. However, this study has several limitations. The cross-sectional design does not capture dynamic changes in content quality. Given the rapid evolution and continual updating of short video content, these findings may not fully reflect temporal variations in content quality (47). Future research should adopt longitudinal designs to monitor quality trends, conduct fine-grained content analysis to identify characteristics and dissemination pathways of misleading information, and assess the actual effect of online health information on public cognition and healthcare-seeking behavior through user-based surveys. Additionally, establishing a standardized quality certification system for medical information, combined with algorithmic optimization and professional endorsement, may help enhance the visibility and dissemination of high-quality content, thereby fostering a healthy and efficient ecosystem for lung cancer health information communication (48,49).

Conclusions

This study systematically evaluated lung cancer-related health information across major short-video platforms. The findings indicate that while the overall quality of content requires improvement, content released by professional medical individuals or institutions is significantly more reliable than that from non-professional sources. Notably, no significant association was observed between video dissemination popularity and information quality, suggesting that widely disseminated videos are not necessarily scientifically accurate and may even pose misinformation risks. To improve the online health information environment, platforms should optimize content moderation and recommendation mechanisms to prioritize high-quality information, content creators should maintain scientific rigor and professionalism, and the public should strengthen critical appraisal skills when engaging with highly popular health content.
