The Case of a 55‐Year‐Old Man With Leg Weakness
Authors: Ada Breitenbucher, Connor Welsh, Kelly Braun, Michael V. Robers
Published: 2026

Summary of Case

In this case, a 55‐year‐old man presented with worsening lower extremity weakness, numbness, and urinary retention for the past 3 weeks. Physical exam was notable for bilateral lower extremity weakness, worse proximally, with diminished sensation and hyperreflexia at the patella bilaterally. MRI of the spine showed an enhancing thoracic spinal cord lesion. Serum studies showed positive MOG antibody titer 1:40, supporting the diagnosis of MOGAD‐related longitudinally extensive transverse myelitis. The patient initially improved with immunotherapy treatment with plasma exchange and high‐dose steroids. However, after discharge, his symptoms and spinal cord edema on repeat imaging later worsened. There was concern that the patient was misdiagnosed, as he did not improve as expected despite appropriate treatment. Repeat diagnostic work up was done with similar serum and CSF results. Eventually, biopsy of the spinal cord lesion was done, which pathologically showed primary glioblastoma multiforme as the final diagnosis. The patient was initiated on concurrent chemotherapy and radiation; however, he progressively became more debilitated with worsening medical complications. He elected to transition to hospice care approximately 3 months after his initial presentation.

This case highlights the importance of reexamining the diagnosis if a patient's course does not progress as expected. Correctly diagnosing spinal cord lesions remains a diagnostic challenge; this case reviews the key imaging differences between the demyelinating spinal lesions of MOG‐AD and spinal cord tumors as well as key differences in the history, disease progression, and treatment of these conditions. Additionally, caution should also be taken when relying on antibody titers for diagnosis, as the specificity and sensitivity of the test vary at different titer levels. Having a low threshold to consider alternative diagnoses was crucial to arriving at the correct diagnosis in this case.
