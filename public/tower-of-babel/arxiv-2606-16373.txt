Higher-order spectral perturbation expansions II: Kernel matrices and manifold learning
Bernhard Stankewitz, Martin Wahl

Abstract
We study spectral concentration bounds for kernel matrices as approximation of the corresponding kernel integral operator. Results are established under weak assumptions on the data setting and the reproducing kernel relying only on a Mercer condition and a local Weyl law. This allows us to deal with key features of kernel matrices, such as large multiplicities, large effective dimension, and heavy-tailed distributions. Our results apply to infinite dimensional principal component analysis, manifold learning, and Bayesian nonparametric statistics. We illustrate this via two prototypical examples: The heat kernel on the sphere and a wavelet prior from Bayesian nonparametrics. Keywords : kernel matrix, principal component analysis, high-dimensionality, multiplicities, heat kernel, manifold learning, Bayesian nonparametrics. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : 62H25, 62R30, 47A55, 47B80

It replaces the idealized, prototypical setting of sub-Gaussian distributions and simple eigenvalues from

The first motivating example for this work is the kernel matrix problem in the context of manifold learning.
Assume that we observe
n

d

This kernel matrix approximates the heat semigroup at time

Under which conditions on the three involved parameters

The generator of the heat semigroup is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the sphere.
Similarly, we can take the difference quotient of the kernel matrix with small

When are the associated eigenvalues and eigenvectors approximations of the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the sphere?
These questions lie at the basis of non-linear dimensionality reduction methods, such as Laplacian Eigenmaps and Diffusion Maps.
They are also difficult to answer for several reasons.
First, there are large multiplicities for small eigenvalues.
Second, there is a large effective dimension, especially for small
t
.
Third, the Karhunen-Loève coefficients are usually neither independent nor uniformly bounded.

A second example stems from Bayesian nonparametric statistics.
In the context of Gaussian process (GP) regression, when the estimation target function is endowed with a GP prior, contraction rates of the posterior are characterized by the eigenvalue decay of the population kernel operator.
In modern setting with large sample sizes

So far, here, guarantees are only available under a singular eigenvalue regime of the kernel operator together with uniform (moment) bound on the eigenfunctions

Motivated by this, we consider a concentration guarantees for a truncated wavelet prior, as it is standard in the Bayesian literature

2
Spectral concentration for kernel matrices

2.1
Problem setup

We consider the following scenario. Let
K\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a normalized kernel matrix defined by

\displaystyle K_{ij}:=n^{-1}k(X_{i},X_{j}),

(2.1)

where
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are i.i.d. random variables and
k:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a function.
In the sequel, we will work under the following assumptions.

Assumption 1

(Data setting)
.

X_{1},\dots X_{n}
are i.i.d. copies of a random variable
X
taking values in a polish space
(\mathcal{X},d)
equipped with its Borel-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{B}_{\mathcal{X}}
. We denote by
\mathbb{P}^{X}
the distribution of
X
.

Assumption 2

(Kernel function)
.

k:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is symmetric and positive semidefinite, meaning that
k(x,y)=k(y,x)
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
and
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{j}k(x_{i},x_{j})\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, all
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
, and all
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{X}
.

Assumption 3

(Mercer’s condition)
.

k:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}}k(x,x)\,\mathbb{P}^{X}(dx)<\infty.

(2.2)

Under Assumption
2
, the kernel matrix
K
is symmetric and positive semi-definite.
Hence, by the spectral theorem, there are eigenvalues
\widehat{\lambda}_{1}\geq\dots\geq\widehat{\lambda}_{n}
and an associated orthonormal basis of eigenvectors
\widehat{v}_{1},\dots,\widehat{v}_{n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle K\widehat{v}_{j}=\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\widehat{v}_{j}\qquad\text{for all }1\leq j\leq n,

(2.3)

i.e.,
K
has the spectral representation

\displaystyle K=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\widehat{v}_{j}\widehat{v}_{j}^{\top}.

(2.4)

Under Assumption
3
, then, the following holds.
First, continuity of
k
implies that
k
is measurable with respect to the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{B}_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}
and the latter is equal to the product-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{B}_{X}\otimes\mathscr{B}_{\mathcal{X}}
, see
[
18
, Proposition 4.1.7]
.
Using also Equation (
2.2
), we arrive at
k\in L^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{X}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{X})
, as can be seen from the inequality
k(x,y)^{2}\leq k(x,x)\cdot k(y,y)
, which follows from the positive semidefiniteness of
k
, Fubini’s theorem, and (
2.2
).
Thus, we we can consider the kernel integral operator

\displaystyle L_{k}:L^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{X})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{X}),\qquad f\mapsto\int_{\mathcal{X}}k(\cdot,y)f(y)\,\mathbb{P}^{X}(dy).

(2.5)

Using also Assumption
2
,
L_{k}
is and compact linear operator, see
[
15
, Theorem 4.2.16]
.
It is self-adjoint and our assumptions are also sufficient for it to be positive, see Lemma
7.1
.
Hence, by the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators, there is a (possibly finite) sequence of positive eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots>0
and a (in this case also finite) orthonormal system of eigenfunctions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{X})
such that
(\phi_{j})
is an orthonormal basis of
(\operatorname{ker}L_{k})^{\perp}
satisfying

\displaystyle L_{k}\phi_{j}=\lambda_{j}\phi_{j},\quad\text{for all }j\geq 1,\qquad\operatorname{tr}(L_{k})=\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}=\int_{\mathcal{X}}k(x,x)\,\mathbb{P}^{X}(dx)<\infty

(2.6)

and

\displaystyle k(x,y)=\sum_{j\geq 1}\langle k(x,\cdot),\phi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}}\phi_{j}(y)=\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{j}(y)

(2.7)

for
\mathbb{P}^{X}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{X}
-almost all
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
, where the right-hand side convergences in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{X}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{X})
.
The latter latter series also converges absolutely and uniform on compact sets, which is the content of Mercer theorem.

Lemma 2.1

(Mercer’s theorem)
.

Under the above Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
, the eigenfunctions
\phi_{j}
are continuous and we have that

\displaystyle k(x,y)=\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{j}(y)

(2.8)

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
, where the right-hand side converges absolutely and uniformly on compacts.

For a proof in the special case of
\mathcal{X}
being a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
equipped with the Lebesgue measure see
[
6
, Theorem 4.23]
. Note that the same argument also works under our more general setup replacing the bounded domain assumption with the fact that
\mathbb{P}^{X}
is finite. For an alternative proof in the special case of self-adjoint semigroups under a trace class hypotheses see
[
15
, Theorem 7.2.5]
. The latter setting is considered in the heat kernel example in Section
3.1
.

In addition to Assumptions
1
–
3
, we assume that the eigenfunctions of the kernel operator satisfy a local Weyl law, i.e., that their squares are bounded on average in each block. Importantly, we account for potential multiplicities of the population eigenvalues
(\lambda_{j})_{j\geq 1}
.
Hence, let
(\mu_{r})_{r\geq 1}
denote the sequence of distinct eigenvalues of
\Sigma
,
I_{r}:=\{j\geq 1:\lambda_{j}=\mu_{r}\}
and
m_{r}=|I_{r}|
the multiplicity of
\mu_{r}
.

Assumption 4

(Local Weyl law)
.

There exists a constant
L\geq 1
such that the orthonormal basis
(\phi_{j})_{j\geq 1}
satisfies that almost surely

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in I_{r}}\phi_{j}^{2}(x)\leq Lm_{r}\qquad\text{ for all }r\geq 1\text{ and all }x\in\mathcal{X}.

(2.9)

Our theory is also able to cover weaker assumptions that are frequently satisfied by the
(\phi_{j})_{j\geq 1}
, such as a bound of the form

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\phi_{j}^{2}(x)\leq Lm^{1+\gamma}\qquad\text{ for all }J\geq 1\text{ and all }x\in\mathcal{X}

(2.10)

for some
\gamma\geq 0
.
This is true, for example, for the important class of Matèrn kernels, see
[
44
]
and the case
\tau=1
was already partially explored by
[
55
]
in the context of manifolds learning.
Such bounds also feature prominently in other nonparametric problems, see
[
38
,
3
]
.
The stronger Assumption
4
, however, leads to the cleanest probabilistic estimates, so that we do not treat weaker conditions in this paper.

A fundamental problem is to relate the spectral characteristics of
K
to those of the integral kernel operator
L_{k}
.
In many of the applications mentioned in the beginning of the introduction, key features of
L_{k}
are spectral decay, large multiplicities, large traces, and eigenfunctions that are not uniformly bounded.
This provides an interesting random operator setting from high-dimensional statistics and machine learning, which is not yet as well understood as the spiked covariance model or covariance matrices under the Marchenko–Pastur limit.

2.2
The kernel trick

The eigenvalue and eigenvector problem for the kernel matrix can be reformulated in terms of an empirical covariance operator. First, introduce the Hilbert space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\Big\{u=\sum_{j\geq 1}\beta_{j}\phi_{j}:\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\beta_{j}^{2}}{\lambda_{j}}<\infty\Big\}\quad\text{ with }\quad\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\langle u,\phi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}}\langle v,\phi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}}}{\lambda_{j}}.

(2.11)

Then
\mathcal{H}
is a separable Hilbert space with orthonormal basis
(u_{j})_{j\geq 1}
given by
u_{j}=\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\phi_{j}
for all
j\geq 1.

Moreover, using Lemma
2.1
, it is easy to check that
k(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{H}
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and that the reproducing property
h(x)=\langle h,k(x,\cdot)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
holds for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
and all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
Consequently,
\mathcal{H}
is the unique reproducing kernel Hilbert space induced by
k
, see also
[
50
,
57
]
.
Moreover, the continuity of
k
and the reproducing property imply that the map
x\mapsto k(x,\cdot)
from
(\mathcal{X},d)
to
(\mathcal{H},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}})
is continuous such that
k(X,\cdot)
is a random variable taking values in
\mathcal{H}
.
Since

\mathbb{E}\|k(X,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}^{2}=\int k(x,x)\mathbb{P}^{X}(dx)<\infty,

it is strongly square-integrable and we can introduce the covariance operator

\displaystyle\Sigma=\mathbb{E}(k(X,\cdot)\otimes k(X,\cdot)),

(2.12)

which is a positive, self-adjoint trace class operator (ee e.g.
[
28
, Theorem 7.2.5]
). The following close relationship of the covariance operator
\Sigma
and integral operator
L_{k}
is well-known (see e.g.
[
60
, Theorem 4.1]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

\Sigma
is the restriction of
L_{k}
to
\mathcal{H}
. In particular, the spectral decomposition of
\Sigma
is given by

\displaystyle\Sigma=\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}u_{j}\otimes u_{j},

(2.13)

where
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots>0
are the non-zero eigenvalues of
L
and
u_{j}=\lambda_{j}^{1/2}\phi_{j}
for all
j\geq 1
.

Proof.

For the first statement note that for
f\in\mathcal{H}
we have

\displaystyle\Sigma f=\mathbb{E}(k(X,\cdot)\langle f,k(X,\cdot)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}})=\mathbb{E}(k(X,\cdot)f(X))=Lf,

(2.14)

where we used the reproducing property in the second equality.
The second statement is a consequence of the first one using the definition of
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

We summarize the above results in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
hold. Then the
\mathcal{H}
-valued random variable
k(X,\cdot)
is strongly square-integrable with (unnormalized) covariance operator
\Sigma
. Moreover, defining the Karhunen-Loève coefficients

\displaystyle\eta_{j}=\frac{\langle k(X,\cdot),u_{j}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}}=\phi_{j}(X),\qquad j\geq 1,

(2.15)

we have the series representation

\displaystyle k(X,\cdot)=\sum_{j\geq 1}\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\eta_{j}u_{j}\qquad\mathbb{P}^{X}\text{-a.s.},

(2.16)

with convergence with respect to the norm
\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|\cdot\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}}
.

In the data setting, we can introduce the embedded random variables

\displaystyle k(X_{1},\cdot),\dots,k(X_{n},\cdot)

(2.17)

taking values in
\mathcal{H}
, and the empirical covariance operator

\displaystyle\widehat{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k(X_{i},\cdot)\otimes k(X_{i},\cdot),

(2.18)

which is a positive, self-adjoint linear operator on
\mathcal{H}
of rank at most
n
.
Similar as above the empirical covariance operator
\widehat{\Sigma}
has the same non-zero eigenvalues as
K
and the eigenvectors can be easily translated using the sampling operator
S_{n}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by
S_{n}h=(h(X_{1}),\dots,h(X_{n}))^{\top}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The spectral decomposition of
\widehat{\Sigma}
is given by

\widehat{\Sigma}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j}

with
\widehat{\lambda}_{1}\geq\dots\geq\widehat{\lambda}_{n}
being the eigenvalues of
K
and an orthonormal system
\widehat{u}_{1},\dots,\widehat{u}_{n}
in
\mathcal{H}
satisfying
(\widehat{u}_{j}(X_{1}),\dots,\widehat{u}_{j}(X_{n}))^{\top}=(n\widehat{\lambda}_{j})^{1/2}\widehat{v}_{j}
for all
j=1,\dots,n
such that
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}>0
.

It follows from Lemmas
2.2
and
2.4
that the eigenvalue and eigenvector problem for
K
can be translated to the eigenvalue and eigenvector problem of the (empirical) covariance operators
\widehat{\Sigma}
and
\Sigma
. This fact is often called the kernel trick, as one only has to compute the inner products
k(X_{i},X_{j})
, while the explicit coordinates of the embedded random variables
k(X_{1},\cdot),\dots,k(X_{n},\cdot)
in the typically infinite-dimensional reproducing kernel Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
are not required.

2.3
Main result

In this section, we formulate our main result in the kernel matrix setting. The behavior of our bounds, which will be formulated for the
r
-th eigenblock, is governed by the relative rank.

Definition 2.5

(Relative rank)
.

For
r\geq 1
the relative rank is defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma):=\sum_{s\neq r}\frac{m_{s}\mu_{s}}{|\mu_{s}-\mu_{r}|}+\frac{m_{r}\mu_{r}}{g_{r}},

(2.19)

where
g_{r}:=\min(\mu_{r-1}-\mu_{r},\mu_{r}-\mu_{r+1})
is the
r
-th spectral gap.

The following result analyzes the eigenvalues
(\widehat{\lambda}_{j})_{j\in I_{r}}
and eigenvectors
(\widehat{v}_{j})_{j\in I_{r}}
of the kernel matrix
K
within a block
I_{r}
. It compares these to the eigenvalue
\mu_{r}
and the eigenfunctions
(\phi_{j})_{j\in I_{r}}
of the integral kernel operator
L_{k}
, where the latter are evaluated at the observations and considered up to an orthogonal transformation.

Theorem 2.6

(Bounds for eigenvalues and eigenspaces)
.

Assume that Assumptions
1
–
4
hold and let
\gamma\geq 1
.
Then there are constants
c,C>0
depending only
\gamma
and
L
such that the following holds.
Let
r\geq 1
be such that

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{r}}{g_{r}}\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)\leq c\frac{n}{\log n}.

(2.20)

Then the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the kernel matrix satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{r}}}\Big\|\Big(\frac{\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r}}{\mu_{r}}\Big)_{j\in I_{r}}\Big\|_{2}\leq C\Big(\sqrt{\frac{m_{r}\log n}{n}}+\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)\frac{\log n}{n}\Big)

(2.21)

and

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \inf_{O\in O(m_{r})}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{r}}}\Bigg\|(\widehat{v}_{j})_{j\in I_{r}}O-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{j}(X_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\phi_{j}(X_{n})\end{pmatrix}_{j\in I_{r}}\Bigg\|_{2}

(2.22)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sqrt{\sum_{s\neq r}\frac{m_{s}\mu_{r}\mu_{s}}{(\mu_{r}-\mu_{s})^{2}}\frac{\log n}{n}}+\sqrt{\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)\frac{\log n}{n}}+\frac{\mu_{r}}{g_{r}}\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)\frac{\log n}{n}\Big)

with probability at least
1-n^{-\gamma}
.

A proof of Theorem
2.6
can be found in Section
7
.
If we combine the upper bounds into one (possibly less sharp) term, then (
2.21
) and (
2.22
) imply

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{r}}}\big\|(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})_{j\in I_{r}}\big\|_{2}\leq Cg_{r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{r}}{g_{r}}\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)\frac{\log n}{n}}

(2.23)

and

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \inf_{O\in O(m_{r})}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{r}}}\Bigg\|(\widehat{v}_{j})_{j\in I_{r}}O-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{j}(X_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\phi_{j}(X_{n})\end{pmatrix}_{j\in I_{r}}\Bigg\|_{2}\leq C\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{r}}{g_{r}}\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)\frac{\log n}{n}}

(2.24)

with probability at least
1-n^{-\gamma}
.

Eigenvalues of kernel matrices have been extensively studied in the literature. More recent work focuses on relative eigenvalue error bounds (see e.g.
[
7
,
42
,
5
]
). Such eigenvalue concentration bounds up to constants are used in prediction problems and lead to effective rank conditions (see
[
4
,
29
]
). However, such bounds do not include joint eigenvalue and eigenprojection concentration bounds, as it is often needed in dimensionality reduction methods (functional and kernel PCA, Diffusion Maps, Laplacian Eigenmaps, etc.).

Similarly as in the previous work
[
30
,
31
,
56
]
, the bounds are formulated in terms of the relative rank. The difference is that we explicitly work with kernel matrices under realistic assumptions. This leads to challenges in terms of large multiplicities
m_{r}
and eigenfunctions
\phi_{j}
that are neither uniformly bounded (as assumed in
[
30
,
31
]
), nor lead to independent and sub-Gaussian Karhunen-Loève coefficients (as assumed in
[
56
]
).

While a linear analog of (
2.22
) is well-known in the context of principal component analysis and the left-hand side of (
2.22
) is part of the different formulations of the Davis-Kahan bound, it has, to the best of our knowledge, not yet appeared in the literature on kernel matrices. It shows the non-linear dimensionality reduction property of kernel matrices. The eigenvectors of kernel matrices give a new coordinate system for the embedded data points, approximating the eigenfunctions of the kernel integral operator evaluated at the data points.

We conclude this section by presenting two main steps in the proof of the main result. To formulate the first step, let

\displaystyle P_{r}:=\sum_{j\in I_{r}}u_{j}\otimes u_{j}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widehat{P}_{r}:=\sum_{j\in I_{r}}\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j}

(2.25)

be the projections on the
r
-th eigenspace of
\Sigma
and
\widehat{\Sigma}
respectively,

\displaystyle R_{r}:

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\neq r}(\mu_{s}-\mu_{r})^{-1}P_{s}

(2.26)

be the
r
-th reduced resolvent, and
E:=\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma
.

Proposition 2.7

(Deterministic bounds)
.

Suppose that

\displaystyle\delta_{r}^{\prime}:=\max\big(\||R_{r}|^{1/2}E|R_{r}|^{1/2}\|_{\infty},g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{\infty},g_{r}^{-1}\|P_{r}EP_{r}\|_{\infty}\big)<1/4-\varepsilon,

(2.27)

for some
\epsilon\in(0,1/4)
. Then there is a constant
C>0
depending only on
\varepsilon
such that

\displaystyle\|(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})_{j\in I_{r}}\|_{2}\leq C(\|P_{r}EP_{r}\|_{2}+g_{r}^{1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\delta_{r}^{\prime})

(2.28)

and

\displaystyle\inf_{O\in O(m_{r})}\Bigg\|(\widehat{v}_{j})_{j\in I_{r}}O

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{j}(X_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\phi_{j}(X_{n})\end{pmatrix}_{j\in I_{r}}\Bigg\|_{2}\leq\Big\|\Big(\frac{\widehat{\lambda}_{j}}{\mu_{r}}-1\Big)_{j\in I_{r}}\Big\|_{2}

(2.29)

\displaystyle+C(\|R_{r}EP_{r}\|_{2}+\mu_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}+g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\delta_{r}^{\prime})

where the infimum is taken over all orthogonal matrices of dimension
|I_{r}|
.

Proposition
2.7
is a consequence of the higher-order spectral perturbation expansions that we present in Section
5
. The operator norms in
\delta_{r}^{\prime}
is bounded in Section
4
using a standard operator Bernstein inequality for sums of independent random self-adjoint operator. The following proposition gives a (surprisingly) simple probabilistic description of theinvolved perturbation terms. It relies on the local Weyl law in Assumption
4
. Recall that a random variable
Z
is called sub-exponential with parameter
K
, if

\displaystyle\|Z\|_{\psi_{1}}:=\sup_{p\geq 1}p^{-1}\|Z\|_{L^{p}}\leq K.

(2.30)

This is equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|Z|\geq Ct)\leq 2\exp(-t/K)\qquad\text{for all }t\geq 0,

(2.31)

where
C>0
is an absolute constant.

Proposition 2.8

(Probabilistic bounds)
.

If Assumptions
1
–
4
are satisfied, then the following holds.

(i)

The random variable
\|P_{r}EP_{r}\|_{2}^{2}

guarantee uniform control along the whole singular value decomposition of the truncated wavelet prior.

Corollary 3.10

(Uniform control for truncated wavelet priors)
.

In the situation above, for any
\alpha>1/2
,

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq r\leq R_{\alpha}}\max_{j\in I_{r}}\frac{|\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r}|}{\mu_{r}}\leq C\sqrt{n^{-\frac{2\alpha-1}{2\alpha+1}}\log n}

(3.40)

with probability at least
1-n^{-1}
.

Proof.

This follows simply by estimating the maximum difference over the eigenblock against the
\ell^{2}
-norm of the differences, using the worst case bound from Corollary
3.9
and accounting for the added multiplicity
m_{r}=2^{r}
on both sides.
Finally the uniformity in
r=0,\dots,R_{\alpha}
follows with a union bound.
∎

Proofs of the probabilistic estimates

In this section, we provide a detailed proof of Proposition

using moment inequalities for U-statistics.
Here, Assumption

on the growth behavior of the eigenfunctions are instrumental in deriving probabilistic bounds on the pertubation terms. The following arguments are also able to cover other assumptions that are frequently satisfied by the
(\phi_{j})_{j\geq 1}
as discussed in Section

Proof of Proposition
2.8
(Probabilistic bounds).

Inserting Lemma
2.3
, the term
\|P_{r}EP_{r}\|_{2}^{2}
can we written as

\displaystyle\|P_{r}EP_{r}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mu_{r}^{2}\sum_{j,k\in I_{r}}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{j}(X_{i})\phi_{k}(X_{i})-\delta_{jk}\Big)^{2}

(4.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{r}^{2}\sum_{j,k\in I_{r}}(\phi_{j}(X_{i})\phi_{k}(X_{i})-\delta_{jk})^{2}

(4.2)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq n}\mu_{r}^{2}\sum_{j,k\in I_{r}}(\phi_{j}(X_{i})\phi_{k}(X_{i})-\delta_{jk})(\phi_{j}(X_{i^{\prime}})\phi_{k}(X_{i^{\prime}})-\delta_{jk}).

(4.3)

We introduce the function

\displaystyle h(x,y)=\mu_{r}^{2}\sum_{j,k\in I_{r}}(\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{k}(x)-\delta_{jk})(\phi_{j}(y)\phi_{k}(y)-\delta_{jk}),\qquad x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Then,

(4.5)

as can be seen from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Assumption
4
. We now have

\displaystyle\|P_{r}EP_{r}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(X_{i},X_{i})+\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq n}h(X_{i},X_{i^{\prime}}).

(4.6)

By (

), we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(X_{i},X_{i})\leq 2L^{2}\frac{m_{r}^{2}\mu_{r}^{2}}{n}\qquad a.s.

(4.7)

Moreover, by symmetrization (see
[

[

\displaystyle\forall p\geq 1,\qquad

(4.10)

Inserting (

\displaystyle\forall p\geq 1,\qquad\mathbb{E}^{1/p}\Big|\frac{1}{\binom{n}{2}^{1/2}}\sum_{1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq n}h(X_{i},X_{i^{\prime}})\Big|^{p}\leq 8L^{2}m_{r}^{2}\mu_{r}^{2}p,

meaning that

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq n}h(X_{i},X_{i^{\prime}})\Big\|_{\psi_{1}}\leq 8\sqrt{2}L^{2}\frac{m_{r}^{2}\mu_{r}^{2}}{n}.

Combining (

\displaystyle\leq 16\sqrt{2}L^{2}\frac{m_{r}^{2}\mu_{r}^{2}}{n}.

(4.13)

This gives claim (i). Inserting again Lemma
2.3
, the second term

can we written as

\displaystyle\|P_{r}E|R_{r}|^{1/2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mu_{r}\sum_{s\neq r}\frac{\mu_{s}}{|\mu_{r}-\mu_{s}|}\sum_{j\in I_{r},k\in I_{s}}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{j}(X_{i})\phi_{k}(X_{i})\Big)^{2}

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{r}\sum_{s\neq r}\frac{\mu_{s}}{|\mu_{r}-\mu_{s}|}\sum_{j\in I_{r},k\in I_{s}}\phi_{j}(X_{i})^{2}\phi_{k}(X_{i})^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq n}\mu_{r}\sum_{s\neq r}\frac{\mu_{s}}{|\mu_{r}-\mu_{s}|}\sum_{j\in I_{r},k\in I_{s}}\phi_{j}(X_{i})\phi_{k}(X_{i})\phi_{j}(X_{i^{\prime}})\phi_{k}(X_{i^{\prime}}).

This time, we introduce the function

\displaystyle h(x,y)=\mu_{r}\sum_{s\neq r}\frac{\mu_{s}}{|\mu_{r}-\mu_{s}|}\sum_{j\in I_{r},k\in I_{s}}\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{k}(x)\phi_{j}(y)\phi_{k}(y),\qquad x,y\in\mathcal{X},

(4.15)

which is again a symmetric and degenerate kernel satisfying

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in\mathcal{X}}|h(x,y)|\leq L^{2}m_{r}\mu_{r}\sum_{s\neq r}\frac{m_{s}\mu_{s}}{|\mu_{r}-\mu_{s}|},

(4.16)

as can be seen from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Assumption
4
. Using the decomposition

\displaystyle\|P_{r}E|R_{r}|^{1/2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(X_{i},X_{i})+\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq n}h(X_{i},X_{i^{\prime}}),

(4.17)

the claim (ii) now follows from proceeding as in the case of the first term. Claim (iii) follows analogous to claim (ii).
∎

Beyond the bounds on the perturbation terms, we also need a probabilistic estimate for
\delta_{r}
to employ our perturbation bounds in the setting of Section
2
.
Here, we formulate the following non-asymptotic bound.

Proposition 4.1

(Non-asymptotic bound on
\delta_{r}
)
.

Assume that Assumptions
1
–
4
hold. Then, for any
\gamma>0
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{r}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{4\gamma L\frac{\mu_{r}}{g_{r}}\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)\frac{\log n}{n}}+\frac{4\gamma}{3}L\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)\frac{\log n}{n}

(4.18)

with probability at least
1-4\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)n^{-\gamma}
.
In particular,
\delta_{r}^{\prime}<1/4
with probability at least
1-4\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)n^{-\gamma}
if
L\gamma\mu_{r}/g_{r}\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)\leq cn/\log n
for some sufficiently small constant
c>0
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1

(Non-asymptotic bound on
\delta_{r}
).

The claim follows from (operator) Bernstein inequality. We set

\displaystyle X^{\prime}:

\displaystyle=(|R_{r}|^{1/2}+g_{r}^{-1/2}P_{r})k(X,\cdot)

(4.19)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\neq r}\sum_{j\in I_{s}}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{s}}{|\mu_{s}-\mu_{r}|}}\phi_{j}(X)u_{j}+\sum_{j\in I_{r}}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{r}}{g_{r}}}\phi_{j}(X)u_{j}.

By Assumption
4
, we
immediately obtain

\displaystyle\|X^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\neq r}\frac{\mu_{s}}{|\mu_{s}-\mu_{r}|}\sum_{j\in I_{s}}\phi_{j}^{2}(X)+\frac{\mu_{r}}{g_{r}}\sum_{j\in I_{r}}\phi_{j}^{2}(X)

(4.20)

\displaystyle\leq L\sum_{s\neq r}\frac{m_{s}\mu_{s}}{|\mu_{s}-\mu_{r}|}+L\frac{m_{r}\mu_{r}}{g_{r}}=L\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma).

For
\xi:=X^{\prime}\otimes X^{\prime}-\mathbb{E}(X^{\prime}\otimes X^{\prime})
, this yields

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|X^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|X^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}^{2}\leq 2L\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma),

(4.21)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}\xi^{2}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbb{E}(X^{\prime}\otimes X^{\prime})^{2}\|_{\infty}=\|\mathbb{E}\|X^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}^{2}(X^{\prime}\otimes X^{\prime})\|_{\infty}\leq L\Big(\frac{\mu_{r-1}}{\mu_{r-1}-\mu_{r}}\lor\frac{\mu_{r}}{g_{r}}\Big)\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle\leq 2L\frac{\mu_{r}}{g_{r}}\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma),

since
\mu_{r-1}/(\mu_{r-1}-\mu_{r})\leq 1+\mu_{r}/(\mu_{r-1}-\mu_{r})\leq 2\mu_{r}/g_{r}
.
Finally,

\displaystyle\tr\mathbb{E}\xi^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tr\mathbb{E}(X^{\prime}\otimes X^{\prime})^{2}=\mathbb{E}\|X^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}^{4}\leq L\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)\mathbb{E}\|X^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle=L\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)\Big(\sum_{s<r}\frac{m_{s}\mu_{s}}{\mu_{s}-\mu_{r}}+\frac{m_{r}\mu_{r}}{g_{r}}+\sum_{s>r}\frac{m_{s}\mu_{s}}{\mu_{r}-\mu_{s}}\Big)\leq L\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)^{2}.

Writing
\delta_{r}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}
with
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}

i.i.d. copies of
\xi
and applying Lemma 5 from
[
17
]
with, in the notation there,

\displaystyle R:=2L\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma),\qquad V:=2nL\frac{\mu_{r}}{g_{r}}\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)\qquad\text{and}\qquad D:=\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)

(4.22)

finishes the proof.
∎

5
Higher-order spectral perturbation bounds

In this section, we present the spectral perturbation bounds that are at the basis of our main results in the kernel setting. We follow the strategy in
[
56
]
, which in itself extends a line of research
[
37
,
35
,
43
,
30
,
31
]
.
Although this part of our proof follows the steps of
[
56
]
, we need several modifications to account for multiplicities and the associated fact that individual empirical eigenvalues no longer admit a perturbation series in this case.

5.1
Notation

The results presented here are more general than the kernel setting.
However, we keep a notation that is suggestive of Section
2
.
Consider therefore two general positive, self-adjoint, compact operators
\Sigma
and
\widehat{\Sigma}
on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
We consider
\widehat{\Sigma}
as an approximation of
\Sigma
and define the perturbation operator
E=\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma
so that
\widehat{\Sigma}=\Sigma+E
.
By the spectral theorem, there is a sequence
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots
of eigenvalues of
\Sigma
(converging to zero), together with an orthonormal system of eigenvectors
u_{1},u_{2},\dots
such that
\Sigma=\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}u_{j}\otimes u_{j}
.
Similarly, there exists a sequence
\widehat{\lambda}_{1}\geq\widehat{\lambda}_{2}\geq\dots
of eigenvalues of
\widehat{\Sigma}
, together with an orthonormal system of eigenvectors
\widehat{u}_{1},\widehat{u}_{2},\dots
such that
\widehat{\Sigma}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j}
.

For notational convenience, we assume that
(u_{j})_{j\geq 1}
and
(\widehat{u}_{j})_{j\geq 1}
are orthonormal bases of
\mathcal{H}
. This allows us to cover the kernel setting from Section
2
, as can be seen as follows. First, this is already true for
(u_{j})_{j\geq 1}
, as the reproducing Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
in (
2.11
) only includes the eigenvectors corresponding to the positive eigenvalues of
L_{k}
and
\Sigma
. Moreover, in Section
2
, the empirical covariance operator
\widehat{\Sigma}
is a finite rank operator, so that the finite orthonormal system of eigenvectors corresponding to the non-zero eigenvalues can be extended to an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
.

As before, we consider the non-increasing sequence
(\mu_{r})_{r\geq 1}
of distinct eigenvalues of
\Sigma
,
I_{r}=\{j\geq 1:\lambda_{j}=\mu_{r}\}
and
m_{r}=|I_{r}|
the multiplicity of
\mu_{r}
. For any
r\geq 1
, we abbreviate
P_{r}=\sum_{j\in I_{r}}u_{j}\otimes u_{j}
and
\widehat{P}_{r}=\sum_{j\in I_{r}}\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j}
.

For
r\geq 1
, we introduce the eigengap

\displaystyle g_{r}=(\mu_{r-1}-\mu_{r})\land(\mu_{r}-\mu_{r+1})

(5.1)

and

\displaystyle\delta_{r}

\displaystyle:=\|(|R_{r}|^{1/2}+g_{r}^{-1/2}P_{r})E(|R_{r}|^{1/2}+g_{r}^{-1/2}P_{r})\|_{\infty},

(5.2)

\displaystyle\delta_{r}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\||R_{r}|^{1/2}E|R_{r}|^{1/2}\|_{\infty}\lor g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{\infty}\lor g_{r}^{-1}\|P_{r}EP_{r}\|_{\infty},

where the perturbation
E
is weighted based on the reduced resolvent

\displaystyle R_{r}:=\sum_{s\neq r}(\mu_{s}-\mu_{r})^{-1}P_{s}.

(5.3)

By elementary norm inequalites we have
\delta_{r}\leq 2\delta_{r}^{\prime}
and
\delta_{r}^{\prime}\leq\delta_{r}
, as well as
\delta_{r}\leq\|E\|_{\infty}/g_{r}
.

5.2
Perturbation bounds for eigenvalues and spectral projectors

In
[
56
]
it has been shown that if
\lambda_{j}=\mu_{r}
is a simple eigenvalue with
m_{r}=1
, then the
j
-th perturbed eigenvalue
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}
and its corresponding eigenprojector
\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j}
admit Taylor expansions in the perturbation
E
provided that
\delta_{r}<1/2
(or
\delta_{r}^{\prime}<1/4
). This itself extends the classical perturbation bound stating that the
j
-th perturbed eigenvalue
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}
and its corresponding eigenprojector
\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j}
admit Taylor expansions in the perturbation
E
provided that
\|E\|_{\infty}<g_{r}/2
, see, e.g., Theorem 3.9 in
[
32
]
. The goal of this section is to show that extensions of
[
56
]
exist in the case of multiple eigenvalues. This requires a new statement for the expansions of eigenvalues, since the
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}
,
j\in I_{r}
now do not individually possess expansions anymore.

For
r\geq 1
, we introduce the Taylor coefficients

\displaystyle P_{r}^{(n)}:=(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{n+1}\geq 0\\
k_{1}+\dots+k_{n+1}=n\end{subarray}}R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\dots ER_{r}^{(k_{n+1})},

(5.4)

where
R_{r}^{(0)}=-P_{r}
and

R_{r}^{(k)}=R_{r}^{k}
for
k\geq 1
.
For instance, we have

\displaystyle P_{r}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=P_{r},\qquad P_{r}^{(1)}=-P_{r}ER_{r}-R_{r}EP_{r}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad

(5.5)

\displaystyle P_{r}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=P_{r}ER_{r}ER_{r}+R_{r}EP_{r}ER_{r}+R_{r}ER_{r}EP_{r}-R_{r}^{2}EP_{r}EP_{r}

\displaystyle-P_{r}ER_{r}^{2}EP_{r}-P_{r}EP_{r}ER_{r}^{2}.

The following first main result presents a projector perturbation series in the case of multiple eigenvalues.
The proof of Theorem
5.1
is presented in Section
6
.

Theorem 5.1

(Projector perturbation series)
.

Let
\delta_{r}^{\prime}<1/4
.
Then, the series of Hilbert-Schmidt operators

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{r}^{(n)}
converges absolutely and for any
integer
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\|\widehat{P}_{r}-\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}P_{r}^{(n)}\|_{2}\leq 4g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\frac{(4\delta_{r}^{\prime})^{p-1}}{(1-4\delta_{r}^{\prime})^{2}}.

(5.6)

In particular, it follows that
\widehat{P}_{r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{r}^{(n)}
.

In the case of single eigenvalues a similar bound holds for the perturbed eigenvalues.
More precisely, for
m_{r}=1
and
j\geq 1
such that
\lambda_{j}=\mu_{r}
, one can formulate a similar result for
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r}
with Taylor coefficients given by
\mu_{r}^{(n)}=\operatorname{tr}(P_{r}^{(n-1)}E)+\operatorname{tr}(P_{r}^{(n)}R_{r}^{-1})
,
n\geq 1
, where
R_{r}^{-1}
denotes the pseudoinverse of
R_{r}
, see
[
56
]
.
In the case of multiple eigenvalues, we formulate an expansion of
\widehat{P}_{r}(\widehat{\Sigma}-\mu_{r})
.
For
n\geq 1
, we define

\displaystyle Q_{r}^{(n)}:=\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}P_{r}^{(l)}EP_{r}^{(n-1-l)}+\sum_{l=0}^{n}P_{r}^{(l)}(\Sigma-\mu_{r})P_{r}^{(n-l)}.

(5.7)

For instance, we have

\displaystyle Q_{r}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=P_{r}EP_{r}

(5.8)

\displaystyle Q_{r}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=-P_{r}EP_{r}ER_{r}-P_{r}ER_{r}EP_{r}-R_{r}EP_{r}EP_{r}.

If
m_{r}=1
, then we have
\operatorname{tr}(Q_{r}^{(n)})=\mu_{r}^{(n)}
,
n\geq 1
, as can
be seen from the identity
P_{r}^{(n)}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}P_{r}^{(l)}P_{r}^{(n-l)}
,
n\geq 0
.
In the generalized setting, we have

Theorem 5.2

(Eigenvalue perturbation series)
.

Let
\delta_{r}^{\prime}<1/4
. Then, for any
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\|\widehat{P}_{r}(\widehat{\Sigma}-\mu_{r})-\sum_{n=1}^{p}Q_{r}^{(n)}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 8\big(\|P_{r}EP_{r}\|_{2}+g_{r}^{1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\delta_{r}^{\prime}\big)\frac{(p+1)(4\delta_{r}^{\prime})^{p-1}}{(1-4\delta_{r}^{\prime})^{2}}.

Given the Taylor expansions in Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
, one might ask what are the best perturbation bounds that can be derived from these Taylor appoximations.

Corollary 5.3

(Eigenvalue perturbation bound)
.

If
\delta_{r}^{\prime}<1/4-\varepsilon
for some
\epsilon\in(0,1/4)
, then

\displaystyle\|(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})_{j\in I_{r}}\|_{2}\leq C_{\varepsilon}(\|P_{r}EP_{r}\|_{2}+g_{r}^{1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\delta_{r}^{\prime})

(5.9)

with
C_{\epsilon}=17/(4\epsilon)^{2}
.

Proof.

Corollary
5.3
follows from Theorem
5.2
in combination with the Hoffman-Wielandt inequality. First, note that the finite family
(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})_{j\in I_{r}}
coincides with
(\lambda_{k}(\widehat{P}_{r}(\widehat{\Sigma}-\mu_{r}I)\widehat{P}_{r}))_{k=1}^{m_{r}}
.
Combining this with the Hoffman-Wielandt inequality, we get

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j\in I_{r}}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{m_{r}}(\lambda_{k}(\widehat{P}_{r}(\widehat{\Sigma}-\mu_{r}I)\widehat{P}_{r}))^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

(5.10)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{m_{r}}(\lambda_{k}(\widehat{P}_{r}(\widehat{\Sigma}-\mu_{r}I)\widehat{P}_{r})-\lambda_{k}(P_{r}EP_{r}))^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{m_{r}}(\lambda_{k}(P_{r}EP_{r}))^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widehat{P}_{r}(\widehat{\Sigma}-\mu_{r}I)\widehat{P}_{r})-P_{r}EP_{r}\|_{2}+\|P_{r}EP_{r}\|_{2}.

Note that we can apply the finite-dimensional version of Hofffman-Wielandt (see e.g.
[
51
]
), since both
\widehat{P}_{r}
and
P_{r}
have an
m_{r}
-dimensional range.
The claim now follows from Theorem
5.2
with
p=1
using that
Q_{r}^{(1)}=P_{r}EP_{r}
.
∎

Corollary
5.3
only includes (upper bounds) for the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of the two leading Taylor coefficients
Q_{r}^{(1)}
and
Q_{r}^{(2)}
, and is thus expected to be close to optimal (see
[
56
]
for a more detailed discussion).

Corollary 5.4

(Projector perturbation bound)
.

If
\delta_{r}^{\prime}<1/4-\varepsilon
for some
\epsilon\in(0,1/4)
, then

\displaystyle\|\widehat{P}_{r}-P_{r}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\|R_{r}EP_{r}\|_{2}+C_{\epsilon}g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\delta_{r}^{\prime}

(5.11)

with
C_{\epsilon}=16/(4\epsilon)^{2}
.

Corollary
5.4
follows from Theorem

5.1
in the case that
p=2
.

6
Proofs of the spectral perturbation bounds

6.1
Eigenvalue separation and Taylor remainder

When
\delta_{r}<1
, we obtain a guarantee that the perturbed eigenvalues
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}
stay close to their unperturbed counterparts.
In particular, for
\delta_{r}<1/2
, the groups corresponding to
\mu_{r-1},\mu_{r}
and
\mu_{r+1}
are contained in separate intervals that do not overlap.

Lemma 6.1

(Eigenvalue separation)
.

If
\delta_{r}<1
, then we have

\displaystyle|\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r}|

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{r}g_{r}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{for all }j\in I_{r},

(6.1)

\displaystyle\widehat{\lambda}_{k}-\mu_{r+1}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{r}(\mu_{r}-\mu_{r+1})\quad\qquad\ \ \text{for all }k\in I_{r+1},

\displaystyle\widehat{\lambda}_{k}-\mu_{r-1}

\displaystyle\geq-\delta_{r}(\mu_{r-1}-\mu_{r})\qquad\ \ \;\text{for all }k\in I_{r-1}.

Proof.

Set

\displaystyle T_{\geq r}:=\sum_{s\geq r}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{r}+\delta_{r}g_{r}-\mu_{s}}}P_{s},\qquad T_{\leq r}:=\sum_{s\leq r}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{s}+\delta_{r}g_{r}-\mu_{r}}}P_{s},

(6.2)

where the sum is taken over the indices of the distinct eigenvalues
(\mu_{r})_{r\geq 1}
only.
We have

\displaystyle\|T_{\geq r}ET_{\geq r}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|(|R_{r}|^{1/2}+(\delta_{r}g_{r})^{-1/2}P_{r})E(|R_{r}|^{1/2}+(\delta_{r}g_{r})^{-1/2}P_{r})\|_{\infty}

(6.3)

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{r}^{-1}\|(|R_{r}|^{1/2}+g_{r}^{-1/2}P_{r})E(|R_{r}|^{1/2}+g_{r}^{-1/2}P_{r})\|_{\infty}\leq 1.

For any
j\in I_{r}
, this implies

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \Big\|\sum_{k,l\geq j}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{j}+\delta_{r}g_{r}-\lambda_{k}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{j}+\delta_{r}g_{r}-\lambda_{l}}}(u_{k}\otimes u_{k})E(u_{l}\otimes u_{l})\Big\|_{\infty}

(6.4)

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{\geq r}ET_{\geq r}\|_{\infty}\leq 1.

Proposition 1 from
[
30
]

then yields
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\lambda_{j}\leq\delta_{r}g_{r}
.
Analogously, we obtain

\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\lambda_{j}\geq-\delta_{r}g_{r}
, which
completes the proof of the first claim.
By considering

\displaystyle T_{>r}:=\sum_{s>r}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{r}+\delta_{r}g_{r}-\mu_{s}}}P_{s},\qquad T_{<r}:=\sum_{s<r}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{s}+\delta_{r}g_{r}-\mu_{r}}}P_{s},

(6.5)

the second and third claim can be verified analogously.
∎

In the situation of Lemma
6.1
, we obtain
an explicit Taylor remainder.

Lemma 6.2

(Taylor remainder)
.

Suppose that
\delta_{r}<1
.
Then, for any integer
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{r}-\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}P_{r}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p-1}\sum_{k_{1},\dots k_{p},\geq 0}R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\dots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(p-k_{1}-\dots-k_{p})}

(6.6)

with

\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}=-\sum_{j\in I_{r}}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})^{-k}\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j}
,
for
k\leq 0
and
\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}=\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}
,
for
k>0
, where

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{r}:=\sum_{s\neq r}\sum_{j\in I_{s}}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})^{-1}\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j}.

(6.7)

is the empirical reduced resolvent.
It is well defined, since
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r}\neq 0
for

j\in I_{s},s\neq r
due to Lemma

6.1
.

Proof.

We prove the statement in Equation (
6.6
) via induction
over
p\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
p=1
, we write
\widehat{P}_{r}-P_{r}
as

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{r}-P_{r}

\displaystyle=(I-P_{r})\widehat{P}_{r}-P_{r}(I-\widehat{P}_{r}).

(6.8)

Using

\displaystyle(u_{j}\otimes u_{j})E(\widehat{u}_{k}\otimes\widehat{u}_{k})

\displaystyle=(\widehat{\lambda}_{k}-\lambda_{j})(u_{j}\otimes u_{j})(\widehat{u}_{k}\otimes\widehat{u}_{k})

(6.9)

and Lemma
6.1
, the second term on the right-hand is given by

\displaystyle P_{r}(I-\widehat{P}_{r})=\sum_{j\in I_{r}}\sum_{s\neq r}\sum_{k\in I_{s}}(\widehat{\lambda}_{k}-\mu_{r})^{-1}(u_{j}\otimes u_{j})E(\widehat{u}_{k}\otimes\widehat{u}_{k})=P_{r}E\widehat{R}_{r}.

(6.10)

Further, with analogous reasoning, the first term on the right-hand side is
given by

\displaystyle(I-P_{r})\widehat{P}_{r}=\sum_{k\in I_{r}}\sum_{s\neq r}(\widehat{\lambda}_{k}-\mu_{s})^{-1}P_{s}E(\widehat{u}_{k}\otimes\widehat{u}_{k})=-\sum_{k\in I_{r}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(\widehat{\lambda}_{k}-\mu_{r})^{l-1}R_{r}^{l}E(\widehat{u}_{k}\otimes\widehat{u}_{k}).

For the last equality, we have used a geometric series expansion of

(\widehat{\lambda}_{k}-\mu_{s})^{-1}=-(\mu_{s}-\mu_{r})^{-1}/(1-(\widehat{\lambda}_{k}-\mu_{r})/(\mu_{s}-\mu_{r}))
,
which is well defined due to Lemma
6.1
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{r}-P_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}R_{r}^{l}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(1-l)}+R_{r}^{(0)}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(1)}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}R_{r}^{l}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(1-l)},

which establishes the induction hypothesis.

For the induction step, we assume that the statement is true for a fixed

p
and consider the fact that the induction hypothesis can be written as

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{r}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=R_{r}^{(0)}-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}R_{r}^{(l)}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(1-l)}.

(6.11)

We will show that analogously

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\qquad\ -\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}R_{r}^{(l)}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k+1-l)},\qquad k<0;

(6.12)

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=R_{r}^{(k)}-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}R_{r}^{(l)}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k+1-l)},\qquad k\geq 0.

(6.13)

Setting
k:=p-k_{1}-\dots-k_{p}
, it then follows from Equations
(
6.12
), (
6.13
) and the induction hypothesis
that

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{r}-\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}P_{r}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p-1}\sum_{k<0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{p}\geq 0\\
k_{1}+\dots+k_{p}=p-k\end{subarray}}R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\dots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E\Big(-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}R_{r}^{(l)}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k+1-l)}\Big)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{p-1}\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{p}\geq 0\\
k_{1}+\dots+k_{p}=p-k\end{subarray}}R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\dots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E\Big(R_{r}^{(k)}-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}R_{r}^{(l)}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k+1-l)}\Big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{p+1}\geq 0}R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\dots ER_{r}^{(k_{p+1})}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(p+1-k_{1}-\dots-k_{p+1})}+P_{r}^{(p)},

which yields the statement in Lemma
6.2
by rearranging
the terms.

It remains to be shown that Equation (
6.12
) and
(
6.13
) are true for
k<0
and
k>0
respectively.
For
k<0
, we have that

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j\in I_{r}}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})^{-k}P_{r}(\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j})-\sum_{j\in I_{r}}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})^{-k}(I-P_{r})(\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j}),

where, using Equation (
6.9
), the first
term on the right-hand side is equal to

\displaystyle-\sum_{j\in I_{r}}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})^{-k-1}P_{r}E(\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j})=-R_{r}^{(0)}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k+1)}.

(6.14)

Reusing the geometric series expansion from before, the second term on the
right-hand side can be written as

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -\sum_{s\neq r}\sum_{j\in I_{r}}\sum_{j^{\prime}\in I_{s}}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})^{-k}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{s})^{-1}(u_{j^{\prime}}\otimes u_{j^{\prime}})E(\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j})

(6.15)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j\in I_{r}}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})^{-k}\sum_{s\neq r}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{s})^{-1}P_{s}E(\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in I_{r}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})^{-k-1+l}R_{r}^{(l)}E(\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j})=-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}R_{r}^{(l)}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k+1-l)}.

Together, this yields Equation (
6.12
).

For
k>0
, we write

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=(I-P_{r})\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}+P_{r}\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}.

(6.16)

Focusing on the first term on the right-hand side, we note that inserting (
6.9
) into
R_{r}E\widehat{R}_{r}
yields
(I-P_{r})\widehat{R}_{r}=R_{r}(I-\widehat{P}_{r})-R_{r}E\widehat{R}_{r}

and inductively

\displaystyle(I-P_{r})\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}

\displaystyle=R_{r}^{k}(I-\widehat{P}_{r})-\sum_{l=1}^{k}R_{r}^{l}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{k+1-l}.

(6.17)

Together, Equations (
6.16
) and
(
6.17
) then amount to

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}

\displaystyle=R_{r}^{k}(I-\widehat{P}_{r})-\sum_{l=1}^{k}R_{r}^{l}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{k+1-l}+P_{r}\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}

(6.18)

\displaystyle=R_{r}^{k}-\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}R_{r}^{l}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(l-1-k)}-\sum_{l=1}^{k}R_{r}^{l}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{k+1-l}+P_{r}\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}

\displaystyle=R_{r}^{k}-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}R_{r}^{l}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(l-1-k)}+P_{r}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{k+1}=R_{r}^{k}-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}R_{r}^{l}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(l-1-k)},

where again, we have used Equation (
6.9
)
and the geometric series expansion to obtain

\displaystyle-R_{r}^{k}\widehat{P}_{r}

\displaystyle=-R_{r}^{k}(I-P_{r})\widehat{P}_{r}=-R_{r}^{k}\sum_{s\neq r}\sum_{j\in I_{s}}\sum_{j^{\prime}\in I_{r}}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j^{\prime}}-\lambda_{j})^{-1}(u_{j}\otimes u_{j})E(\widehat{u}_{j^{\prime}}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j^{\prime}})

(6.19)

\displaystyle=R_{r}^{k}\sum_{j^{\prime}\in I_{r}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j^{\prime}}-\mu_{r})^{l-1}R_{r}^{l}E(\widehat{u}_{j^{\prime}}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j^{\prime}})=-\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}R_{r}^{l}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(l-1-k)}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

6.2
Norm estimates and contraction inequalities

In order to bound the Taylor remainder, it will be necessary to enumerate and estimate the summands in the terms
P_{r}^{(n)}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

The number of
(n+1)
-tuples

(k_{1},\dots,k_{n+1})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+1}
such that

k_{1}+\dots+k_{n+1}=m
is equal to

\binom{n+m}{n}\leq 2^{n+m}
. Moreover, the number
(n+1)
-tuples

(k_{1},\dots,k_{n+1})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+1}
such that
k_{1}\geq 1
and

k_{1}+\dots+k_{n+1}=m
is equal to

\binom{n+m-1}{n}\leq 2^{n+m-1}
.

Lemma 6.4

(Elementary norm estimates)
.

For
n\geq 1
and tuple
(k_{1},\dots,k_{n+1})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+1}
with
k_{1}+\dots+k_{n+1}=m
, it holds that

(i)

\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{n+1})}\|_{\infty}\leq g_{r}^{n-m}\delta_{r}^{\prime n}.

Moreover, if
k_{a}=0
for some
1\leq a\leq n+1
and
m\geq 1
, then

(ii)

\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\dots ER_{r}^{(k_{n+1})}\|_{2}\leq g_{r}^{n-m-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\delta_{r}^{\prime n-1}

Analogous results hold for
R_{r}^{(k_{1})}
or
R_{r}^{(k_{n+1})}
replaced by
|R_{r}|^{1/2}
and setting
k_{1},k_{n+1}=1/2
.

Proof.

For the statement in (i), we focus on the case where
k_{1},k_{n+1}\geq 1
.
The other cases follow analogously.
Set

\displaystyle S_{r}^{(k)}:=\begin{cases}P_{r},&k=0,\\
|R_{r}|^{1/2},&k>0.\end{cases}

(6.20)

Then,

\displaystyle\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\dots ER_{r}^{(k_{n+1})}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq g_{r}^{-\sum_{a=1}^{n+1}(k_{a}-1)^{+}-1}\prod_{a=1}^{n}\|S_{r}^{(k_{a})}ES_{r}^{(k_{a+1})}\|_{\infty},

(6.21)

where
\sum_{a=1}^{n+1}(k_{a}-1)^{+}
is the sum of the values of
the
(k_{a})_{a}
in excess of one.
The factors in the last product are of the form

\displaystyle\||R_{r}|^{1/2}E|R_{r}|^{1/2}\|_{\infty},\qquad\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{\infty}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\|P_{r}EP_{r}\|_{\infty}

(6.22)

and the number of values
k_{a}
equal to zero is given

\displaystyle|\{a:k_{a}=0\}|

\displaystyle=n+1-m+\sum_{a=1}^{n+1}(k_{a}-1)^{+},

(6.23)

where
n+1-m
is the minimal number of zeros possible and the sum is
the number of zeros for the particular instance of the
(k_{a})_{a}

exceeding the minimum.
Redistributing,
|\{a:k_{a}=0\}|
many factors
g_{r}^{-1}

onto the terms in Equation (
6.22
) yields

\displaystyle\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\dots ER_{r}^{(k_{n+1})}\|_{\infty}\leq g_{r}^{n-m}\delta_{r}^{\prime n}.

(6.24)

For the statement in (ii), we note that for
k_{a}=0
, there has to
exist a term
|R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}
in the product from Equation
(
6.21
).
By consecutively applying the inequality

\|AB\|_{2}\leq\|A\|_{\infty}\|B\|_{2}
, this yields (ii).
∎

Finally, we need to quantify the contractive properties of the
operators
\widehat{R}^{(k)}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 6.5

(Contraction)
.

If
\delta_{r}<1/2
, then

(i)

\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}\leq\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}/(1-2\delta_{r})
;

(ii)

\|P_{r}\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}\|_{2}\leq(1-\delta_{r})^{-k}g_{r}^{-k-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}/(1-2\delta_{r})
for any
k\geq 1
;

(iii)

\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq(1-\delta_{r})^{-k-1}g_{r}^{-k+1/2}
for any
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

For (i), Equation (
6.9
) yields that for

s\neq r
,

\displaystyle\|P_{s}\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in I_{s}}\sum_{j\in I_{r}}\|(u_{k}\otimes u_{k})(\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k\in I_{s}}\sum_{j\in I_{r}}\frac{\|(u_{k}\otimes u_{k})E(\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}}{(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\lambda_{k})^{2}}

(6.25)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-\delta_{r})^{2}}\sum_{k\in I_{s}}\sum_{j\in I_{r}}\frac{\|(u_{k}\otimes u_{k})E(\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}}{(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})^{2}}=\frac{1}{(1-\delta_{r})^{2}}\frac{\|P_{s}E\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}^{2}}{(\mu_{s}-\mu_{r})^{2}},

where Lemma
6.1
implies the first inequality and
also guarantees that all denominators are well defined.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{s\neq r}|\mu_{s}-\mu_{r}|^{1/2}P_{s}\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{1-\delta_{r}}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}E\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}.

(6.26)

Using
I=P_{r}+I-P_{r}
and

I-P_{r}=|R_{r}|^{1/2}|R_{r}|^{-1/2}
, we have

\displaystyle\||R_{r}|^{1/2}E\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}+\||R_{r}|^{1/2}E(I-P_{r})\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}

(6.27)

\displaystyle\leq\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}+\||R_{r}|^{1/2}E|R_{r}|^{1/2}\|_{\infty}\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}+\delta_{r}\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}

Inserting this into Equation (
6.26
), yields

\displaystyle\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}}{1-\delta_{r}}+\frac{\delta_{r}\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}}{1-\delta_{r}},

(6.28)

which gives the result after rearranging.

For (ii), by Lemma
6.1
,

\displaystyle\|P_{r}\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sum_{s\neq r}\sum_{j\in I_{s}}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})^{-2k}\|P_{r}\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j}\|_{2}^{2}}

(6.29)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\delta_{r})^{-k}g_{r}^{-k}\|(I-P_{r})\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}\leq(1-\delta_{r})^{-k}g_{r}^{-k-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}.

By (i), we then obtain

\displaystyle\|P_{r}\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq g_{r}^{-k-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\frac{(1-\delta_{r})^{-k}}{1-2\delta_{r}}.

(6.30)

Finally, for (iii), by Equation (
6.17
) with

k=1
,

\displaystyle\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{R}_{r}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}(R_{r}(I-\widehat{P}_{r})-R_{r}E\widehat{R}_{r})\|_{\infty}

(6.31)

\displaystyle\leq\||R_{r}|^{1/2}(I-\widehat{P}_{r})\|_{\infty}+\||R_{r}|^{1/2}E(P_{r}+I-P_{r})\widehat{R}_{r}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq g_{r}^{-1/2}+\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\widehat{R}_{r}\|_{\infty}+\||R_{r}|^{1/2}E|R_{r}|^{1/2}|R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{R}_{r}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq g_{r}^{-1/2}+\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{\infty}g_{r}^{-1}(1-\delta_{r})^{-1}+\delta_{r}^{\prime}\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{R}_{r}\|_{\infty},

\displaystyle\leq g_{r}^{-1/2}+g_{r}^{-1/2}\frac{\delta_{r}^{\prime}}{1-\delta_{r}}+\delta_{r}^{\prime}\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{R}_{r}\|_{\infty},

where we have used Lemma
6.1
for the third inequality.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{R}_{r}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{g_{r}^{-1/2}}{(1-\delta_{r})^{2}}

(6.32)

and for
k>1
, by Lemma
6.1
,

\displaystyle\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{R}_{r}\|_{\infty}\|\widehat{R}_{r}^{k-1}\|_{\infty}

(6.33)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{g_{r}^{-1/2}((1-\delta_{r})g_{r})^{-(k-1)}}{(1-\delta_{r})^{2}}=g_{r}^{-k+1/2}(1-\delta_{r})^{-(k+1)},

which finishes the proof.
∎

6.3
Derivation of the perturbation series

The set of Lemmas from the last section, now allows to prove the result for the perturbation series of the projectors.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
(Projector perturbation series).

The absolute convergence follows by applying Lemma
6.4
(ii) directly.
From Lemma
6.2
, it now follows that

\displaystyle\|\widehat{P}_{r}-\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}P_{r}^{(n)}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{p}\geq 0\\
k_{1}+\dots+k_{p}=p-k\end{subarray}}\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}\|_{2}.

(6.34)

For the summands on the right-hand side of Equation
(
6.34
), we distinguish the cases
k\leq 0

and
k\geq 1
.

First, consider the case
k\leq 0
. By Lemma
6.1
,
we have

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{j\in I_{r}}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r})^{-2k}\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E(\widehat{u}_{j}\otimes\widehat{u}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}

(6.35)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{j\in I_{r}}|\widehat{\lambda}_{j}-\mu_{r}|^{-2k}\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\delta_{r}^{-2k}g_{r}^{-2k}\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}^{2}.

(6.36)

Thus, by the identiy
I-P_{r}=|R_{r}|^{1/2}|R_{r}|^{-1/2}
and
simple properties of the Hilbert-Schmidt norm, we get

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}\|_{2}

(6.37)

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{r}^{-k}g_{r}^{-k}\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}EP_{r}\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}+\delta_{r}^{-k}g_{r}^{-k}\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E(I-P_{r})\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{r}^{-k}g_{r}^{-k}\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}EP_{r}\|_{2}+\delta_{r}^{-k}g_{r}^{-k}\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E|R_{r}|^{1/2}\|_{\infty}\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}.

Using the norm estimates from Lemma
6.4
, we can
now treat the last two terms separately.
For the first term, Lemma
6.4
(ii) yields

\displaystyle\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}EP_{r}\|_{2}\leq g_{r}^{k-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\delta_{r}^{\prime p-1}.

(6.38)

For the second term, we use Lemma
6.4
(i) together
with the contraction result from Lemma
6.5
(i) to obtain

\displaystyle\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E|R_{r}|^{1/2}\|_{\infty}\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{P}_{r}\|_{2}\leq g_{r}^{k-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\frac{\delta_{r}^{\prime p}}{1-2\delta_{r}}.

(6.39)

Together, Equations (
6.37
),
(
6.38
), (
6.39
) and the inequality
\delta_{r}^{\prime}\leq\delta_{r}
yield

\displaystyle\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\Big(\delta_{r}^{\prime p-1}\delta_{r}^{-k}+\frac{\delta_{r}^{\prime p}\delta_{r}^{-k}}{1-2\delta_{r}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\frac{\delta_{r}^{\prime p-1}\delta_{r}^{-k}}{1-2\delta_{r}}.

From there it follows that

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \sum_{k\leq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{p}\geq 0\\
k_{1}+\dots+k_{p}=p-k\end{subarray}}\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\dots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}\|_{2}

(6.40)

\displaystyle\leq g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\frac{\delta_{r}^{\prime p-1}}{1-2\delta_{r}}\sum_{k\leq 0}2^{2p-1-k}\delta_{r}^{-k}\leq 2g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\frac{(4\delta_{r}^{\prime})^{p-1}}{(1-2\delta_{r})^{2}}

by bounding the number of summands via Lemma
6.3
.

Next, consider the case
k\geq 1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}EP_{r}\|_{\infty}\|P_{r}\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}\|_{2}+\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E|R_{r}|^{1/2}\|_{2}\||R_{r}|^{-1/2}\widehat{R}_{r}^{k}\|_{\infty}.

Lemma
6.4
(i) immediately gives

\displaystyle\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}EP_{r}\|_{\infty}\leq g_{r}^{k}\delta_{r}^{\prime p}

(6.42)

implies that there exists some
k_{a}=0

1\leq a\leq p

6.4
(ii) gives

\displaystyle\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E|R_{r}|^{1/2}\|_{2}\leq g_{r}^{k-1}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\delta_{r}^{\prime p-1}.

(6.43)

The two remaining terms can directly be treated by Lemma

(ii) and (iii).
Together we obtain that for

\displaystyle\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}\|_{2}

(6.44)

\displaystyle\leq g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\frac{\delta_{r}^{\prime p}(1-\delta_{r})^{-k}}{1-2\delta_{r}}+g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\delta_{r}^{\prime p-1}(1-\delta_{r})^{-(k+1)}

\displaystyle\leq g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\frac{\delta_{r}^{\prime p-1}(1-\delta_{r})^{-k}}{1-2\delta_{r}}.

From there, it follows that

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{p}\geq 0\\
k_{1}+\dots+k_{p}=p-k\end{subarray}}\|R_{r}^{(k_{1})}E\cdots ER_{r}^{(k_{p})}E\widehat{R}_{r}^{(k)}\|_{2}

(6.45)

\displaystyle\leq g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\frac{\delta_{r}^{\prime p-1}}{1-2\delta_{r}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{2p-1-k}(1-\delta_{r})^{-k}\leq 2g_{r}^{-1/2}\||R_{r}|^{1/2}EP_{r}\|_{2}\frac{(4\delta_{r}^{\prime})^{p-1}}{1-2\delta_{r}}

by bounding the number of summands again via Lemma
6.3
.
Combining Equation (
6.40
) and (
6.45
) finishes the
proof.

Proof of Lemma

In the proof, we will use the following simple inequalities for the exponential function

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e^{x}-1}\leq\frac{1}{x},\qquad\frac{1}{1-e^{-x}}\leq 1+\frac{1}{x},\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{e^{x}-1}\leq\Big(1+\frac{1}{x}\Big)e^{-x}

For (i), we only consider the case

For (ii) and (iii), again, we focus on the case

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{r}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s<r}\frac{m_{s}e^{-t\nu_{s}}}{e^{-t\nu_{s}}-e^{-t\nu_{r}}}+\frac{m_{r}e^{-t\nu_{r}}}{\min(e^{-t\nu_{r}}-e^{-t\nu_{r+1}},e^{-t\nu_{r-1}}-e^{-t\nu_{r}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(1+\frac{R^{2}}{td}\Big)\Big(1+d+\sum_{s>2}\binom{d+s-1}{s-1}e^{-(s-2)\frac{dt}{R^{2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(1+\frac{R^{2}}{td}\Big)\Big(1+d+(d+1)\sum_{s>2}\binom{d+s-2}{s-2}e^{-(s-2)\frac{dt}{R^{2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle=C\Big(1+\frac{R^{2}}{td}\Big)\Big((d+1)\sum_{s=2}\binom{d+s-2}{s-2}e^{-(s-2)\frac{dt}{R^{2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle=C\Big(1+\frac{R^{2}}{td}\Big)\Big(\frac{d+1}{(1-e^{-\frac{td}{R^{2}}})^{d+1}}\Big).

Applying the assumption
td/R^{2}\geq\log 2
and the inequality (
7.28
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{2}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(1+\frac{R^{2}}{td}\Big)\Big(\frac{d+1}{(1-e^{-\frac{td}{R^{2}}})^{d+1}}\Big)\leq C(d+1)\exp\Big(2(d+1)e^{-\frac{dt}{R^{2}}}\Big),

(7.31)

which finishes the proof.
∎
