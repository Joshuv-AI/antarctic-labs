Coarse Balanced Separators in Fat-Minor-Free Graphs
Édouard Bonnet, Hung Le, Marcin Pilipczuk, Michał Pilipczuk

Abstract
Fat minors are a coarse analogue of graph minors where the subgraphs modeling vertices and edges of the embedded graph are required to be distant from each other, instead of just being disjoint. In this paper, we give a coarse analogue of the classic theorem that an n n -vertex graph excluding a fixed minor admits a balanced separator of size 𝒪 ⁡ ( n ) \mathcal{O}(\sqrt{n}) . Specifically, we prove that for every integer d d , real " > 0 \varepsilon>0 , and graph H H , there exist constants c c and r r such that every n n -vertex graph G G excluding H H as a d d -fat minor admits a set S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) that is a balanced separator of G G and can be covered by c ​ n 1 2 + " cn^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon} balls of radius r r in G G . Our proof also works in the weighted setting where the balance of the separator is measured with respect to any weight function on the vertices, and is effective: we obtain a randomized polynomial-time algorithm to compute either such a balanced separator, or a d d -fat model of H H in G G .

Introduction

Coarse graph theory
is a relatively young direction in structural graph theory, whose aim is to describe the structure in graphs understood as metric spaces. As suggested in one of the foundational works of Georgakopoulos and Papasoglu
[
10
]
, the hope is that many classic notions and results of structural graph theory, particularly the theory of graph minors, could be lifted to suitable coarse analogues. The usual way of obtaining such a coarse analogue is to replace any constraints of disjointness or intersection with the requirement that the relevant objects are far or close to each other, respectively. A good example of such a lift is the coarse analogue of the notion of a minor, called a
fat minor
. Formally, for graphs
G
and
H
,
we say that a graph
G
contains
H
as a
d
-fat minor
, for a parameter
d\in\mdmathbb{N}
, if there are connected subgraphs
\{B_{u}\colon u\in V(H)\}
and
\{B_{e}\colon e\in E(H)\}
of
G
such that

•

whenever
u
is an endpoint of
e
, the subgraphs
B_{u}
and
B_{e}
intersect; and

•

except for the above, all the subgraphs in
\{B_{f}\colon f\in V(H)\cup E(H)\}
are at distance at least
d
from each other in
G
.

The collection of subgraphs
\{B_{u}\colon u\in V(H)\}
and
\{B_{e}\colon e\in E(H)\}
as above is called a
d
-fat model
of
H
in
G
.
Thus, the notion of a fat minor requires the constituent parts of the model to be far from each other, rather than just disjoint as in the classic notion of a minor.

In the light of all the deep advances of the classic theory of graph minors, the following question becomes natural: Supposing
G
excludes a fixed graph
H
as a
d
-fat minor, what can be said about the metric structure of
G
? Georgakopoulos and Papasoglu
[
10
]
conjectured that such graphs are

to graphs excluding
H
as a classic minor. Roughly speaking, this would mean that the metric space of a graph excluding
H
as a
d
-fat minor can be mapped to a metric space of some
H
-minor-free graph

so that the mapping preserves the distances up to a constant multiplicative and additive factor (both depending on
d
and

). If this were true, then the wealth of known properties of

-minor-free graphs could be pulled through the quasi-isometry to their coarse analogues that would apply to graphs excluding
H
as a
d
-fat minor.

Unfortunately, as shown by Davies, Hickingbotham, Illingworth, and McCarty
[
7
]
, the conjecture of Georgakopoulos and Papasoglu turns out to be false; see also the newer works
[
2
,
3
]
for simpler and stronger counterexamples. However, many of its potential consequences — structural properties of graphs excluding a fat minor — can still be true, at least to some extent. In this work we identify one such property: the existence of balanced separators of sublinear size.

To measure the balance of a separator, it will be convenient to speak about vertex-weighted graphs. A

Our main result is a weaker form of

Our techniques.

Our proof of
Theorem

2
is inspired by the following result of Korhonen and Lokshtanov
[
11
]
about the existence of balanced separators in induced-minor-free graphs.

Theorem 5

(
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

For every graph
H
, every
m
-edge weighted graph
G
that is
H
-induced-minor-free has a balanced separator of size at most
\mathcal{O}_{H}(\sqrt{m}\log m)
.

We note that in
[
11
]
, this result is stated for unweighted graphs, but the proof can be easily lifted to the weighted setting. (Alternatively, the weighted result can be obtained from the unweighted one using a theorem of Dvořák and Norin
[
8
, Theorem 1]
.) Also, the formulation in
[
11
]
postulates a stronger bound of
\mathcal{O}_{H}(\sqrt{m})
, but the proof is partly based on a faulty lemma from an earlier work of Lee
[
13
]
. Without reliance on this lemma, the proof goes through with an extra
\log m
factor.

The idea of Korhonen and Lokshtanov in the proof of
Theorem

5
is to exploit the classic result of Leighton and Rao
[
14
]
on the gap between product multicommodity flows and balanced separators. Roughly speaking, from this result it follows that if a weighted graph
G
does not admit a balanced separator of small size, then in
G
there is a multicommodity flow
\lambda
that pushes, for every pair of vertices
u,v
, a significant amount of flow from
u
to
v
, and at the same time every vertex of
G
carries only a relatively small amount of flow. Such a flow
\lambda
can be understood as a well-distributed system of paths in
G
. Korhonen and Lokshtanov show that by sampling paths from this system, one can find, with positive probability, an induced minor model of the supposedly excluded graph
H
, leading to a contradiction.

In our proof of
Theorem

2
, we show that assuming the graph is suitably sparse, the same strategy can be used to expose a fat minor model of
H
, not only an induced one. The precise notion of sparseness has to be chosen carefully. We achieve it using the following clustering result of Filtser
[
9
]
: for every graph
G
and
\varepsilon>0
, one can partition the vertex set of
G
into subsets inducing subgraphs of diameter
\mathcal{O}(1/\varepsilon)
, called
clusters
, so that every ball of radius
2
in
G
intersects only
\mathcal{O}(n^{\varepsilon})
clusters. The Leighton–Rao argument is then applied to the graph
G^{\prime}
obtained from
G
by contracting every cluster to a single vertex. Thus, a balanced separator of
G^{\prime}
of size
\mathcal{O}_{H}(n^{1/2+\varepsilon})
can be naturally lifted to a balanced separator of
G
that can be covered by
\mathcal{O}_{H}(n^{1/2+\varepsilon})
balls of bounded radius.

The proof of
Theorem

3
is much less involved. We first apply a simple clustering scheme that vertex-partitions the graph into connected subgraphs, each dominated by a single vertex, so that contracting the subgraphs yields a graph with
\mathcal{O}_{H}(n^{4/3}\log^{2/3}n)
edges. Then we apply
Theorem

5
as a black-box in the contracted graph, and again lift the obtained balanced separator to the original graph.
Theorem

4
follows by the same approach, but we apply an off-the-shelf clustering result for
K_{t,t}
-induced-minor-free graphs due to Chudnovsky, Codsi, Ajaykrishnan, and Lokshtanov
[
6
]
.

For

Weighted graphs and balanced separators have already been defined in
Section

1
. Note that we use vertex weights only to measure the balance of separators. In particular, there are no weights on the edges and the lengths of paths are measured by the number of edges. When taking (induced) subgraphs in the context of weighted graphs, we naturally restrict the weight function to the remaining vertices.

A

Minors.

The notions of minors, fat minors, and induced minors were also already introduced in

[

on induced minors, it will be useful to work with the following relaxed notion of fat models.
A

-fat model
of a graph

in a graph

is a pair of mappings
(\phi,\pi)
such that

maps vertices of
H
to vertices of

,

maps edges of
H

The following simple lemma, directly lifted from an analogous statement in

-fat model of the

.

.
We set

For every edge

be the middle edge of the

We set

We now show that

Concurrent flows and balanced separators.

Our proof of
Theorem

2
crucially relies on the duality between multicommodity flows and balanced cuts, proved by Leighton and Rao
[
14
]
. Let us recall the relevant results.

The work of Leighton and Rao concerns the
Product Multicommodity Flow Problem
(PMFP) on directed edge-capacitated graphs.
An instance of (directed) PMFP is a triple
(D,c,\pi)
where

•

D
is a (directed) graph,

•

c\colon E(D)\to\real_{\geqslant 0}
is an edge-capacity function, and

•

\pi\colon V(D)\to\real_{\geqslant 0}
is a vertex demand function.

For every pair of distinct vertices
u,v\in V(D)
,
the
demand
from
u
to
v
is
\pi(u)\pi(v)
units of commodity
(u,v)
.
The goal is to find a (multicommodity) flow — union of flows of commodity
(u,v)
for every pair of distinct vertices
u,v\in V(D)
— that does not exceed the capacity of any edge and routes for all these pairs simultaneously
q\cdot\pi(u)\pi(v)
units of the commodity
(u,v)
from
u
to
v
, for the largest possible value
q
. Formally, a multicommodity flow of value
q\in\real_{\geqslant 0}
is a function
f\colon\mathcal{P}(D)\to\real_{\geqslant 0}
, assigning each path
P\in\mathcal{P}(D)
the amount of flow passing through
P
, so that

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}(D),e\in P}f(P)\leqslant c(e)\quad\textrm{for all }e\in E(D)~\textrm{ and}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{u,v}(D)}f(P)=q\cdot\pi(u)\pi(v)\quad\textrm{for all }u,v\in V(D),u\neq v.

On the other hand of the duality we have balanced cuts, where the balance is measured as follows: For a partition
(U,\overline{U})
of
V(D)
such that
\pi(U)\pi(\overline{U})>0
(with
\pi(X):=\sum_{v\in X}\pi(v)
), we define its
weighted ratio cost
as

\frac{\sum\limits_{e\in E_{D}(U,\overline{U})}c(e)}{\pi(U)\cdot\pi(\overline{U})},

where
E_{D}(U,\overline{U})
denotes the set of edges of
D
with tail in
U
and head in
\overline{U}
.
With these definitions in place, we can recall the main result of Leighton and Rao.

Theorem 6

(
[
14
, Theorem 17]
)
.

There is a randomized polynomial-time algorithm that given an instance
(D,c,\pi)
of the Directed Product Multicommodity Flow Problem and a real
q\in\real_{\geqslant 0}
, outputs either

(1)

a multicommodity flow of value
q
in
(D,c,\pi)
, or

(2)

a partition
(U,\overline{U})
of
V(D)
of weighted ratio cost
\mathcal{O}(q\log p)
, where
p\coloneqq|\{v\in V(D)\colon\pi(v)>0\}|
.

We remark that the formulation of
[
14
, Theorem 17]
is non-algorithmic, but the proof is effective and can be turned into a randomized polynomial-time algorithm; see the remarks after
[
14
, Theorem 7]
.

Similarly as in
[
11
]
, for our purposes we need to translate the duality provided by
Theorem

6
to the setting of vertex separators, vertex-capacitated flows, and (vertex-)weighted undirected graphs.
The translation uses a standard, simple reduction scheme: splitting every vertex

into two. However, we need to first introduce the relevant terminology.

The role of multicommodity flows will be played by
concurrent flows
.
For a weighted graph
G
, a
concurrent flow
in
G
is a function
\lambda\colon\mathcal{P}(G)\rightarrow\real_{\geqslant 0}
that assigns each path
P\in\mathcal{P}(G)
the amount of flow passing through
P
, so that for every pair of vertices
(u,v)\in V(G)\times V(G)
with
u\neq v
, we have

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{u,v}(G)}\lambda(P)=\mathbf{w}(u)\mathbf{w}(v).

By
{}_{G,\lambda}(v)
we denote the total amount of flow passing through
v
, that is,

{}_{G,\lambda}(v)\coloneqq\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}(G),v\in P}\lambda(P)

(1)

The
congestion

\gamma
of the concurrent flow
\lambda
is
\max_{v\in V(G)}{}_{G,\lambda}(v)
.

On the other hand, the role of balanced cuts will be played by balanced separations. Recall that a
separation
in a (weighted) graph
G
is a pair of vertex subsets
(A,B)
such that
A\cup B=V(G)
and there is no edge with one endpoint in
A\setminus B
and the other in
B\setminus A
. Then
A\cap B
is the
separator
of the separation
(A,B)
.
Assuming
G
is weighted, we define the
sparsity
of
(A,B)
as

\alpha_{G}(A,B)\coloneqq\frac{|A\cap B|}{\mathbf{w}(A)\cdot\mathbf{w}(B)}.

With all the definitions in place, we can prove the desired variant of the result of Leighton and Rao.

Lemma 3

(undirected vertex-weighted variant of
[
14
, Theorem 17]
)
.

There is a randomized polynomial-time algorithm that, given a weighted
n
-vertex graph
G
and a real
\gamma>0
, outputs either:

(1)

a concurrent flow in
G
with congestion at most
\gamma
, or

(2)

a separation in
G
of sparsity
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log n}{\gamma}\right)
.

Proof.

We perform the standard trick of splitting every vertex into two.
Let
D
be the directed graph with edge capacities
c\colon E(D)\to\real_{\geqslant 0}
obtained from
G
as follows:

•

for every
u\in V(G)
, add to
D
two vertices
u_{\text{in}}
and
u_{\text{out}}
and connect them by an edge
(u_{\text{in}},u_{\text{out}})
of capacity
1
and an edge
(u_{\text{out}},u_{\text{in}})
of capacity
\infty
; and

•

for every edge
uv\in E(G)
, add to
D
edges
(u_{\text{out}},v_{\text{in}})
and
(v_{\text{out}},u_{\text{in}})
of capacity
\infty
.

Edges of capacity
\infty
can be avoided by using a large constant, e.g.
|V(G)|^{2}\cdot\mathbf{w}(G)+1
, instead.
Finally, for every

, we set

\pi(u_{\text{in}})\coloneqq\mathbf{w}(u)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\pi(u_{\text{out}})\coloneqq 0.

Thus,
(D,c,\pi)
is an instance of the Directed Product Multicommodity Flow Problem. Let us verify that multicommodity flows in this instance are in correspondence to concurrent flows in

.

Claim 1
.

For any real
q>0
,

(D,c,\pi)
has a multicommodity flow of value

if and only if
G
has a concurrent flow of congestion
1/q
.

Proof.

First, suppose there is a multicommodity flow
f
in
(D,c,\pi)
that, for every pair of distinct vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, sends
q\cdot\pi(u_{\text{in}})\pi(v_{\text{in}})
units of flow from
u_{\text{in}}
to
v_{\text{in}}
. Note that there is a natural map from
\mathcal{P}_{u_{\text{in}},v_{\text{in}}}(D)
to
\mathcal{P}_{u,v}(G)
defined by contracting every vertex pair

back to
w
. Hence, we may map

to a flow
\lambda_{0}
in

that sends
q\cdot\mathbf{w}(u)\mathbf{w}(v)
units of flow from
u
to
v
, for every pair of distinct vertices
u,v
, and has congestion
1
(this corresponds to the edges of the form
(w_{\text{in}},w_{\text{out}})
having capacity
1
). By rescaling
\lambda_{0}
by a multiplicative factor of

, we obtain a concurrent flow
\lambda
in

with congestion
1/q
.

Conversely, suppose
G
has a concurrent flow
\lambda
of congestion
1/q
. Similarly as above, we may map
\lambda
to a multicommodity flow
f_{0}
in
(D,c,\pi)
that sends
\mathbf{w}(u)\mathbf{w}(v)=\pi(u_{\text{in}})\pi(v_{\text{in}})
units of flow from

u_{\text{in}}
to
v_{\text{in}}
, for every pair of distinct vertices
u,v
, and uses capacity
1/q
at every edge of the form
(w_{\text{in}},w_{\text{out}})
. By rescaling
f_{0}
by a multiplicative factor of
q
, we obtain a multicommodity flow

of value

that respects all the edge capacities.
∎

We apply the algorithm of

.
If this application yields a multicommodity flow of value

. So we may assume that we have computed a partition

with weighted ratio cost bounded by
\mathcal{O}(\log n/\gamma)

Observe that every edge in

must be of the form
(u_{\text{in}},u_{\text{out}})
for some

, because the other edges have infinite capacity, which would render the weighted ratio cost of

infinite (or too large).
Let

be the set comprised of all vertices

.
Then,

\sum_{e\in E_{D}(U,\overline{U})}c(e)=|E_{D}(U,\overline{U})|=|S|.

Let
A\subseteq V(G)
be the set of vertices

such that

, and

be the set of vertices

such that

; thus

. Note that due to the existence of an edge
(u_{\text{out}},u_{\text{in}})
of capacity

, for each vertex

we must have

or
u\in B
. Moreover, since every edge
uv
of

gives rise to edges
(u_{\text{out}},v_{\text{in}})
and
(v_{\text{out}},u_{\text{in}})
of capacity

, it cannot happen that

(implying
u_{\text{out}}\in U
) and

(implying

is a separation of

, with separator

.

Finally, observe that the sparsity of

is

\alpha_{G}(A,B)=\frac{|S|}{\mathbf{w}(A)\cdot\mathbf{w}(B)}=\frac{\sum\limits_{e\in E_{D}(U,\overline{U})}c(e)}{\mathbf{w}(A)\cdot\mathbf{w}(B)}.

Note that
\pi(U)=\mathbf{w}(A)

Thus we have

\alpha_{G}(A,B)\leqslant\frac{\sum\limits_{e\in E_{D}(U,\overline{U})}c(e)}{\pi(U)\cdot\pi(\overline{U})},

which in turn is bounded by

We now transform, by repeated applications, the second outcome of

Lemma 4
.

There is a randomized polynomial-time algorithm that, given a weighted
n
-vertex graph
G
with
W\coloneqq\mathbf{w}(G)
and a real
\gamma>0
, outputs either:

(1)

an induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
such that
\mathbf{w}(G^{\prime})\geqslant W/2
, a concurrent flow in
G^{\prime}
with congestion at most
\gamma

of size
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{W^{2}\log n}{\gamma}\right)
such that
S\cap V(G^{\prime})=\emptyset
and every connected component of
G-S
is either disjoint from
G^{\prime}
or contained in
G^{\prime}
; or

(2)

a balanced separator of
G
of size
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{W^{2}\log n}{\gamma}\right)
.

Proof.

We build a sequence
G_{1},G_{2},G_{3},\ldots
of induced subgraphs of
G
as follows. Note here that each
G_{i}
, as an induced subgraph of
G
, inherits the weight function
\mathbf{w}\coloneqq\mathbf{w}_{G}
from
G
.

Set

and

.
Perform the following loop: While
\mathbf{w}(G_{i})\geqslant W/2
, apply
Lemma

3
to
G_{i}
with congestion parameter
\gamma
.
If this application yields outcome
(1)
, then we break out of the while loop.
Otherwise, we obtain a separation
(A_{i},B_{i})
in
G_{i}
with sparsity at most
\frac{c\log n}{\gamma}
, for some universal constant
c
.
By swapping
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
if necessary, we assume that
\mathbf{w}(A_{i})\geqslant\mathbf{w}(B_{i})
.
We set
G_{i+1}\coloneqq G_{i}[A_{i}\setminus B_{i}]
and
i\leftarrow i+1
, and proceed to the next iteration.
This ends the body of the loop.

When we exit the while loop, we have built separations
(A_{1},B_{1}),\ldots,(A_{\ell},B_{\ell})
of induced subgraphs
G_{1}\supseteq\ldots\supseteq G_{\ell}
, respectively. For
i\in[\ell]
, denote
S_{i}\coloneqq A_{i}\cap B_{i}
.
We claim that if we have not broken out of the while loop (upon finding a concurrent flow), then
S\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\in[\ell]}S_{i}
is a balanced separator of
G
.
Note that by construction,
V(G)\setminus S
is partitioned into
B_{1}\setminus A_{1},B_{2}\setminus A_{2},\ldots,B_{\ell}\setminus A_{\ell}
, and
A_{\ell}\setminus B_{\ell}
, with no edge between two distinct parts.
For each
i\in[\ell]
, we have
\mathbf{w}(B_{i}\setminus A_{i})\leqslant W/2
, because
\mathbf{w}(B_{i})\leqslant\mathbf{w}(A_{i})
.
By the condition of exiting the loop, we have
\mathbf{w}(A_{\ell}\setminus B_{\ell})\leqslant W/2
. So
S
is indeed a balanced separator of
G
.

We are left with arguing that
|S|\leqslant\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{W^{2}\log n}{\gamma}\right)
.
For every
i\in[\ell]
we have

\alpha_{G_{i}}(A_{i},B_{i})=\frac{|S_{i}|}{\mathbf{w}(A_{i})\cdot\mathbf{w}(B_{i})}\leqslant\frac{c\log n}{\gamma},~~\text{thus}

|S|=\sum_{i\in[\ell]}|S_{i}|\leqslant\frac{c\log n}{\gamma}\cdot\sum\limits_{i\in[\ell]}\mathbf{w}(A_{i})\cdot\mathbf{w}(B_{i}).

Note that the sets
B_{1},\ldots,B_{\ell}
are pairwise disjoint, and we can upper bound every term
\mathbf{w}(A_{i})
by the total weight
W
. Therefore

|S|\leqslant\frac{cW\log n}{\gamma}\cdot\sum\limits_{i\in[\ell]}\mathbf{w}(B_{i})\leqslant\frac{cW^{2}\log n}{\gamma},

and

constitutes a valid outcome (
(2)
).

Now, suppose we have broken out of the while loop because a concurrent flow
\lambda
of congestion at most
\gamma
was found in
G^{\prime}\coloneqq G[A_{\ell}\setminus B_{\ell}]
. By the invariant of the loop,
G^{\prime}
is an induced subgraph of
G
such that
\mathbf{w}(G^{\prime})\geqslant W/2
. And by construction,
S
is disjoint with
V(G^{\prime})
and every connected component of
G-S
is either contained in
G^{\prime}
or disjoint with
G^{\prime}
. Also, by the same calculation as above, we have
|S|\leqslant\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{W^{2}\log n}{\gamma}\right)
.
We conclude that
G^{\prime}
,
\lambda
, and

satisfy the desired properties of outcome (
(1)
).
∎

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum\limits_{P\in\mathcal{P}(\widehat{G}^{\prime}),X\in P}\lambda(P)=\frac{{}_{\widehat{G}^{\prime},\lambda}(X)}{M^{2}}\leqslant\frac{\gamma}{M^{2}}.

Let us define the set of relevant pairs of edges of
\ddot{H}
:

\Pi\coloneqq\left\{\,(uv,u^{\prime}v^{\prime})\in E(\ddot{H})\times E(\ddot{H})\ \mid\ |\{u,v,u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\}|=4\,\right\}.

Note that for any

(\phi(u),\phi(v),\pi(uv))
and

are independent joint variables, and hence

Therefore, for all

(3)

Observe that assuming
\phi
is injective (that is, event
I
), that
(\phi,\pi)
is a crude
3
-fat model of
\ddot{H}
is equivalent to the following assertion: for all

and

, we do not have

and

simultaneously. Therefore, we have the following inclusion of events:

\overline{F}\cap I\quad\subseteq\quad\bigcup_{(uv,u^{\prime}v^{\prime})\in\Pi}\ \bigcup_{(X,X^{\prime})\in\Xi}\ A_{X,uv}\cap A_{X^{\prime},u^{\prime}v^{\prime}}.

Hence, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\,[\overline{F}\cap I]

\displaystyle\leqslant|\Pi|\cdot|\Xi|\cdot\frac{1}{4h^{2}c\cdot n^{1+\varepsilon}}

by union bound and (

),

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{4}

by

.

(4)

By combining (

\mathbb{P}\,[F]\geqslant\mathbb{P}\,[F\cap I]\geqslant\frac{1}{4}
; or in other words,
(\phi,\pi)

Proofs of
Theorems

and

In this section we prove our results for graphs excluding an induced minor:
Theorems

3
and
4
. We need some terminology.
A
star partition
of a graph
G
is a connected partition
\mathcal{X}
of
G
such that for every
X\in\mathcal{X}
, there is a vertex
v_{X}\in X
satisfying
X\subseteq N[v_{X}]
.
In other words, it is a connected partition of strong radius at most 1.

Lemma 5
.

Every
n
-vertex graph
G
admits a star partition
\mathcal{X}
such that
G/\mathcal{X}
has
\mathcal{O}(n^{4/3}\log^{2/3}n)
edges.

Proof.

We set
k\coloneqq\lceil n^{1/3}\log^{2/3}n\rceil
, and assume that
k\geqslant 2
without loss of generality.
Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{h}
be a maximal sequence of distinct vertices such that for every
i\in[h]
,
v_{i}
has at most
k
neighbors in
G-\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1}\}
.
Let
M\coloneqq\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{h}\}
, and let
E_{M}
be the set of edges of
G
with at least one endpoint in
M
.
By construction,
|E_{M}|\leqslant k|M|\leqslant kn
.

By the maximality of
M
, the graph
G^{\prime}\coloneqq G-M
has minimum degree greater than
k
.
Thus
G^{\prime}
admits a dominating set of size at most
\frac{2(n-h)\log k}{k}

[
5
]
.
In particular,
G^{\prime}
admits a star partition
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
with at most
\frac{2(n-h)\log k}{k}
parts.
We extend it to a star partition
\mathcal{X}
of
G
by adding every singleton
\{v_{1}\},\ldots,\{v_{h}\}
.

The number of edges of
G/\mathcal{X}
is at most

|E_{M}|+{\frac{2(n-h)\log k}{k}\choose 2}\leqslant kn+\frac{4n^{2}\log^{2}k}{k^{2}}=\mathcal{O}(n^{4/3}\log^{2/3}n).\qed

Then
Theorem

3
is an easy consequence of combining
Lemmas

5
and
5
.

Proof of
Theorem

3
.

Apply
Lemma

5
to
G
, yielding a star partition
\mathcal{X}
of
G
such that the quotient graph

has
\mathcal{O}(n^{4/3}\log^{2/3}n)

Noting that

also excludes

to

of

. Then

is a balanced separator in

that is
(\mathcal{O}_{H}(n^{2/3}\log^{4/3}n),1)
-coverable.
∎

5
Conjectures

Our work raises multiple questions about the existence of coverable balanced separators in fat-minor-free and induced-minor-free graphs. The most prominent one is whether
1
holds, or at least improving the additional
\mathcal{O}(n^{\varepsilon})
factor to a polylogarithmic function of
n
. The only source of the
\mathcal{O}(n^{\varepsilon})
factor is the clustering scheme of
Lemma

1
, hence we propose the following.

Conjecture 2
.

For every graph
H
there are constants
p,d\in\mathbb{N}
such that every graph
G
that excludes
H
as a
3
-fat minor admits a connected partition
\mathcal{X}
of weak diameter at most
d
such that for every
u\in V(G)
,
B_{G}(u,2)
intersects at most
p
clusters of
\mathcal{X}
.

We note that resolving
2
would not automatically resolve
1
because of the additional
\log n
factor stemming from the gap between multicommodity flows and balanced cuts (
Theorem

6
). However, proving a weaker form of
2
with
p
replaced by
\mathcal{O}(\log^{\alpha}n)
, for some
\alpha\in\real_{\geqslant 0}
, would directly translate to the weaker form of
1
with
c
replaced by
\mathcal{O}(\log^{\alpha+1}n)
. We note that in
2
, it is not at all clear that the condition “every ball of radius
2
intersects few parts of
\mathcal{X}
” is the only correct one; other forms of asserting the sparsity of
\mathcal{X}
may also be useful.

Noting that containing
H
as a
2
-fat minor entails containing
H
as an induced minor,
Theorem

2
implies that any
n
-vertex
H
-induced-minor-free graph has an
(\mathcal{O}_{H}(n^{1/2+\varepsilon}),\mathcal{O}(1/\varepsilon))
-coverable balanced separator, for any
\varepsilon>0
. As witnessed by
Theorems

3
and
4
, the induced-minor-free case may permit a broader range of tools, hence we state the following weaker form of
1
.

Conjecture 3
.

For every graph
H
there exist constants
c,r\in\mathbb{N}
such that every
n
-vertex weighted graph
G
that excludes
H
as an induced minor has a balanced separator that is
(c\sqrt{n},r)
-coverable.

It would be particularly elegant if
r=1
sufficed in
3
, and
Theorem

3
gives an indication that this could indeed be the case. Towards this goal, we propose the following strengthening of
Lemma

5
as a first step.

Conjecture 4
.

For every graph
H
there exist constants
c,q\in\mathbb{N}
such that every
n
-vertex graph
G
that excludes
H
as an induced minor admits a connected partition
\mathcal{X}
such that every cluster of
\mathcal{X}
is
(q,1)
-coverable and the quotient graph
G/\mathcal{X}
has at most
c\cdot n
edges.

Again, a weaker form of this statement, with
c
or
q
(or both) replaced by a subpolynomial function of
n
, would also be interesting.
