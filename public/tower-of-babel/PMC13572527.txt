Research note: Serum and glucocorticoid-induced kinase 1 is associated with inflammatory responses to Eimeria tenella and modulates cytokine expression, sporozoite-positive cell proportion, and apoptosis in chicken cells
Authors: Jianqiang Tang, Meihui Tang, Liyue Dong, Areej Arif, Jin Zhang, Tao Zhang, Genxi Zhang, Kaizhou Xie, Zhenhua Zhao, Guojun Dai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Avian coccidiosis, caused by several species of the genus Eimeria, remains a major constraint on poultry production, with Eimeria tenella (E. tenella) representing an important pathogenic species that primarily infects the chicken cecum. Host genes associated with the inflammatory response may provide useful candidates for resistance evaluation. In our previous transcriptome analysis, serum and glucocorticoid-induced kinase 1 (SGK1), fatty acid binding protein 1 (FABP1), and bone morphogenetic protein 3 (BMP3) were identified as candidate genes in yellow-feathered broilers. Here, these genes were re-evaluated by RT-qPCR in a commercial Jinghai Yellow chicken population after E. tenella infection, followed by correlation analysis with inflammatory cytokine expression and further in vitro functional analysis of SGK1. Cecal tissues from infected birds showed increased expression of IL-6, CXCLi2, TNF-α, SGK1, FABP1, and BMP3, whereas IL-10 was decreased. Among the 3 candidate genes, SGK1 showed the most consistent associations with inflammatory mediators in both control and infected birds, displaying positive correlations with IL-6, CXCLi2, and TNF-α and a negative correlation with IL-10. In DF-1 and HD11 cells, SGK1 overexpression reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression and secretion and increased IL-10, whereas SGK1 knockdown produced the opposite pattern under basal and sporozoite-stimulated conditions. Flow-cytometric analysis further showed that SGK1 manipulation affected the proportion of fluorescent sporozoite-positive cells and the apoptotic response induced by sporozoite exposure. These findings suggest that SGK1 is associated with the early cecal inflammatory response to E. tenella and may modulate early host-cell responses, including cytokine expression, sporozoite-positive cell proportion, and apoptosis, in chicken-derived cell models. Therefore, SGK1 represents a promising candidate for further investigation in host-response studies of E. tenella, although the downstream signaling pathways and intracellular parasite-development processes involved remain to be defined.

Introduction

Avian coccidiosis is caused by several species of the genus Eimeria remains an important enteric disease in poultry. Among these species, Eimeria tenella (E. tenella) primarily infects the chicken cecum and is particularly harmful because it causes severe mucosal injury, hemorrhagic inflammation, impaired intestinal health, and reduced flock productivity (Kim et al., 2022). Given the increasing constraints on prophylactic anticoccidial drug use and the relatively high cost of effective vaccines, identifying host-response genes associated with coccidiosis is important for understanding disease-related immune mechanisms and providing candidate targets for genetic improvement of disease resistance in poultry (Boulton, et al., 2018).

In a previous transcriptome study of resistant and susceptible Jinghai Yellow chickens, serum and glucocorticoid-induced kinase 1 (SGK1), fatty acid binding protein 1 (FABP1), and bone morphogenetic protein 3 (BMP3) were identified as candidate genes associated with the host response to coccidiosis (Yu, et al., 2024). However, the expression patterns of these genes had not been further validated in an independent commercial population after E. tenella infection, and their functional relevance to inflammatory regulation in chicken cells remained unclear. Among these candidates, SGK1 is of particular interest because it is a serine/threonine kinase implicated in the regulation of inflammatory signaling and cytokine production (Zhang, et al., 2023). Therefore, the objective of the present study was to re-evaluate the expression of these 3 candidate genes in cecal tissues from infected and uninfected birds and to further assess the functional role of SGK1 in DF-1 and HD11 cells under basal and E. tenella sporozoite-stimulated conditions.

Materials and methods

Animals and sample collection

To maintain consistency with the population used in our previous transcriptome study and minimize sex-related biological variation, a total of 80 healthy 1-day-old female yellow-feathered broilers were purchased from Jiangsu Jinghai Poultry Group Co., Ltd. (Haimen, China). All birds were reared in coccidia-free animal rooms with ad libitum access to antibiotic-free feed and drinking water. At 18 days of age, the birds were randomly divided into two groups: the infected group and the control group, with 40 birds per group. Each bird in the infected group was orally gavaged with 3.5 × 10⁴ sporulated E. tenella oocysts (kindly provided by the Parasitology Laboratory of Yangzhou University), while the control group received normal saline. No mortality was observed in either group from inoculation to sample collection. At 4.5 days post-infection (dpi), all birds were euthanized by rapid cervical dislocation, and cecal tissue was collected from each bird for subsequent analysis. The animal experimental protocols were approved by the Animal Welfare Committee of Yangzhou University (permit number: SYXK (Su) IACUC 2012-0029).

SGK1 overexpression vector and small interfering RNA

The SGK1 (XM_015284007.4) overexpression vector and siRNAs were designed and synthesized by Suzhou GenePharma Co., Ltd. (Suzhou, China). The siRNA oligo for SGK1 were the following sense strand: 5′-GCCAGUCCCCAACUCUAUUTT-3′; anti-sense strand 5′-AAUAGAGUUGGGGACUGGCTT-3′. The siRNA-NC was used, with the sequence: sense strand 5′-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3′; anti-sense strand 5′-ACGUGACACGUUCGGAGAATT-3′.

In vitro functional assay of SGK1 in DF-1 and HD11 cells following E. tenella stimulation

Sporozoites were isolated from E. tenella oocysts stored in 2.5% potassium dichromate at 4 °C, as previously described by (Bai et al., 2024). DF-1 and HD11 cells were seeded in 12-well plates at 3 × 10⁵ and 2 × 10⁵ cells/well, respectively, and cultured at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ until 70% confluence. Cells were transfected using jetPRIME® reagent (Polyplus-transfection, Illkirch, France) with either pcDNA3.1-SGK1 (800 ng/μL) or empty vector, or siRNA-SGK1 (20 μmol/L) or siRNA-NC, following the manufacturer's protocol. At 24 h post-transfection, purified sporozoites were resuspended in pre-warmed complete medium, pre-incubated at 37 °C for 2 h, and added to designated wells at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 3:1 (sporozoites: cells; approximately 9 × 10⁵ and 6 × 10⁵ sporozoites per well) (Yu et al., 2022). Control wells received an equivalent volume of sporozoite-free medium. After 6 h of co-culture, cells were harvested and stored at −80 °C for the analysis of cytokine expression levels. All experimental conditions were performed three biological replicates.

Flow-cytometric analysis of sporozoite-positive cells and apoptosis

To evaluate early host cell–sporozoite interaction, purified E. tenella sporozoites were fluorescently labeled with CFDA SE Cell Proliferation Assay and Tracking kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and added to SGK1-manipulated and control DF-1 and HD11 cells under the same sporozoite-stimulated conditions described above. After 6 h of co-culture, cells were thoroughly washed to remove unbound sporozoites, and the proportion of fluorescent sporozoite-positive cells was quantified by flow cytometry. Flow-cytometric data were acquired and analyzed using CytExpert software (v.2.3). This readout was interpreted as the proportion of sporozoite-positive cells rather than definitive intracellular invasion. In addition, to assess sporozoite-induced apoptosis, DF-1 and HD11 cells were collected after sporozoite stimulation and analyzed by flow cytometry using Annexin V-FITC/PI staining kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The proportions of apoptotic and viable cells were calculated and compared between SGK1-manipulated groups and their corresponding controls.

RNA isolation, and cDNA synthesis

Total RNA was isolated from cecal tissues and cell samples using TRIZOL reagent (Tiangen, Beijing, China) following the manufacturer's instructions. Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized from total RNA using the HiScript III RT SuperMix for qPCR (+gDNA wiper) kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) according to the protocol, and preserved at −20°C.

Primer information and RT-qPCR analysis

All primers were synthesized by Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The primer sequences were as follows: IL-6, forward 5′-AAATCCCTCCTCGCCAATCT-3′ and reverse 5′-CCTCACGGTCTTCTCCATAAA-3′; CXCLi2, forward 5′-AGTTCATCCACCCTAAATCC-3′ and reverse 5′-CACACCTCTCTTCCATCCTT-3′; TNF-α, forward 5′-GCCCTTCCTGTAACCAGATG-3′ and reverse 5′-ACACGACAGCCAAGTCAACG-3′; IL-10, forward 5′-CGGGAGCTGAGGGTGAA-3′ and reverse 5′-GTGAAGAAGCGGTGACAGC-3′; SGK1, forward 5′-GGGTCCCGCTTTGACTTACTC-3′ and reverse 5′-GAAAGTCCGATGGCTTGGCA-3′; FABP1, forward 5′-CAGGAGAGAAGGCCAAGTGT-3′ and reverse 5′-CACGGATTTCAGCCCCTTCA-3′; BMP3, forward 5′-TGTACCCTGAGGCAGATCACA-3′ and reverse 5′-ATTCTCTGAACCTTTCCTTCCCC-3′; and β-actin, forward 5′-CCTGAACCTCTCATTGCCA-3′ and reverse 5′-GAGAAATTGTGCGTGACATCA-3′. All primer pairs were used at an annealing temperature of 60°C. The expected amplicon lengths were 105 bp for IL-6, 106 bp for CXCLi2, 71 bp for TNF-α, 272 bp for IL-10, 272 bp for SGK1, 80 bp for FABP1, 124 bp for BMP3, and 152 bp for β-actin. Amplification efficiencies ranged from 97.5% to 106.6% across all primer pairs. In addition, all primer pairs generated specific amplification products as confirmed by melting-curve analysis, supporting the use of these assays for relative expression analysis. RT-qPCR was performed to detect mRNA expression levels using the ChamQ SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Low ROX Premixed) kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer's protocol.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay

The production of IL-6, CXCLi2, TNF-α, IL-10 of chicken DF-1 and HD-11 cells in cell supernatant was determined by the commercial kits (R&D Systems, USA). The detection protocols were conducted according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Relative mRNA expression levels were calculated using the 2⁻ΔΔCt method with β-actin as the internal reference gene (Livak and Schmittgen, 2001). In the in vivo experiment, each bird was considered an independent experimental unit (n = 40 per group). In the in vitro assays, each independent cell culture experiment was considered one biological replicate (n = 3), and technical qPCR replicates were averaged before analysis. Data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Normality and homogeneity of variance were evaluated before analysis. Differences between 2 groups were assessed using an independent-samples 2-tailed Student’s t-test, and correlations were evaluated using Pearson’s correlation coefficient (r). Correlation analyses were performed separately for the control and infected groups. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results and discussion

Following E. tenella infection, the cecal mRNA expression of the pro-inflammatory mediators IL-6, CXCLi2, and TNF-α was significantly increased, whereas IL-10 expression was significantly decreased (P < 0.05; Fig. 1A). This expression profile is consistent with an early cecal inflammatory response, in which IL-6 and CXCLi2 contribute to innate immune activation and inflammatory cell recruitment, while TNF-α promotes local inflammatory signaling (Tomal et al., 2023). The decrease in IL-10 at 4.5 dpi may reflect a stage-specific predominance of pro-inflammatory signaling. Previous temporal analyses of E. tenella infection showed that cecal IL-10 expression increased mainly at 6–8 dpi, suggesting that compensatory anti-inflammatory regulation becomes more prominent at later stages of infection (Yu et al., 2021). At the same time, the 3 transcriptome-derived candidate genes, SGK1, FABP1, and BMP3 were all significantly upregulated in infected birds (P < 0.05; Fig. 1A). To distinguish which of these infection-responsive genes was most closely associated with the inflammatory profile, we next performed correlation analysis between candidate-gene expression and cytokine expression in cecal tissues from control and infected birds. Among the 3 genes, SGK1 showed the most consistent pattern, being positively correlated with IL-6, CXCLi2, and TNF-α and negatively correlated with IL-10 in both groups (P < 0.05; Fig. 1B). In contrast, FABP1 showed no significant association with these mediators in either group, whereas BMP3 showed only limited associations in the control group and no clear relationship after infection (P > 0.05; Fig. 1B). Thus, although all 3 candidate genes were infection-responsive, SGK1 was the only candidate that showed a stable and biologically coherent association with both pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory mediators, supporting its prioritization for subsequent functional analysis.

We therefore examined whether direct manipulation of SGK1 altered cytokine expression in vitro. Overexpression plasmid and knockdown oligo of SGK1 were synthesized, and met the requirements for subsequent experimental studies (P < 0.05; Fig. 2A and 2B). In both DF-1 and HD11 cells, SGK1 manipulation altered cytokine expression under sporozoite-free basal conditions. SGK1 overexpression reduced the mRNA expression of IL-6, CXCLi2, and TNF-α and increased that of IL-10, whereas SGK1 knockdown produced the opposite pattern (P < 0.05; Fig. 2C and 2E). Consistent with these transcriptional changes, SGK1 overexpression decreased the secretion of IL-6, CXCLi2, and TNF-α and increased IL-10 secretion, whereas SGK1 knockdown produced the reverse effect (P < 0.05; Fig. 2C and E). Similar bidirectional effects were observed under E. tenella sporozoite-exposed conditions (P < 0.05; Fig. 2D and F), suggesting that SGK1-associated cytokine modulation is maintained during early host cell–sporozoite stimulation. However, cytokine responses after sporozoite exposure may also be influenced by the extent of host cell–sporozoite interaction. Therefore, additional flow-cytometric analyses were performed to evaluate sporozoite-positive cell proportions and apoptosis. SGK1 manipulation affected the proportion of sporozoite-positive DF-1 and HD11 cells (P < 0.05; Fig. 2G), suggesting that SGK1 may influence early host cell–sporozoite interaction under the present experimental conditions. Flow-cytometric apoptosis analysis further showed that SGK1 affected the apoptotic response induced by sporozoite exposure. SGK1 overexpression reduced the proportion of apoptotic cells, whereas SGK1 knockdown increased apoptosis after sporozoite stimulation (P < 0.05; Fig. 2H). These results suggest that SGK1 is associated not only with cytokine modulation but also with cellular injury-related responses during early sporozoite exposure.

Nevertheless, the present study did not define a downstream inflammatory signaling pathway, nor did it fully distinguish surface-associated from internalized sporozoites or assess intracellular parasite development. Therefore, the sporozoite-positive cell data should not be interpreted as definitive evidence of productive intracellular invasion. Further mechanistic studies are required to determine whether SGK1 regulates specific inflammatory pathways, alters parasite internalization or intracellular development, or acts through a combination of these mechanisms.

Notably, the use of both DF-1 and HD11 cells strengthened the functional interpretation of this study. DF-1 cells provide a nonprofessional structural cell model that reflects local tissue-associated responses to sporozoite stimulation, whereas HD11 cells represent professional innate immune effector cells with strong cytokine-producing capacity (Jaime, et al., 2020). Evaluating SGK1 in these 2 biologically distinct chicken cell types allowed us to test whether its effect was restricted to a single cellular background or was reproducible across different host-response compartments. The observation that SGK1 produced the same overall anti-inflammatory pattern in both models suggests that its effect is not cell-type restricted, but is instead conserved across 2 relevant chicken cellular contexts. This 2-cell-type validation therefore provides stronger support for the biological relevance of SGK1 than would be obtained from a single-cell-model experiment alone.

Taken together, the present data refine our previous transcriptome-based candidate list by showing that, among SGK1, FABP1, and BMP3, SGK1 had the strongest and most consistent relationship with inflammatory mediators in vivo and showed clear bidirectional regulatory effects on cytokine expression, sporozoite-positive cell proportion, and apoptosis in vitro. These findings identify SGK1 as a promising host-response candidate involved in the chicken response to E. tenella. It should be noted that the in vivo analyses were conducted only at 4.5 dpi, the present findings represent a stage-specific snapshot of the host response and do not define the full temporal wave of host-pathogen interactions. Future studies incorporating multiple sampling points will be required to determine whether the observed associations are maintained across different stages of infection.
