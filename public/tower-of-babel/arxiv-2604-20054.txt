Non-abelian p p -curvature and a non-abelian Katz’s formula
Michael Barz

Abstract
. Let k k be a field of characteristic p , p, and f : X → S f:X\to S a smooth proper morphism of smooth k k -schemes. Katz’s formula [ 11 ] gives a relationship between the Kodaira–Spencer map of f f , and an invariant called the p p -curvature of the Gauss–Manin connection associated to f . f. Recently, Lam–Litt [ 12 ] proved a variant of Katz’s formula in non-abelian Hodge theory, and suggested that it should be possible to give a more conceptual proof of their formula using the stacky approach to p p -adic Hodge theory. In this article, we realize their suggestion, explaining how this rather concrete phenomena observed by Katz [ 11 ] and Lam–Litt [ 12 ] can be explained in a conceptual way using sheared de Rham stacks , as developed by Bhatt–Kanaev–Vologodsky–Zhang [ 3 ] and Drinfeld [ 10 ] (though we prove a slightly different statement than Lam–Litt [ 12 ] ). We do not assume the reader has any background in the theory of de Rham stacks, and along the way we give some general constructions related to non-abelian connections which may be of use in other contexts.

2.
Review of sheared de Rham stacks

As in the introduction, fix
k
a field of positive characteristic, and
S
a smooth
k
-scheme. In this section, we introduce the
sheared
de Rham stack
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}.

2.1.
The characteristic 0 story

We start by recalling some work of Simpson
[
16
]
in characteristic 0. For a complex algebraic variety
Y/\mathbb{C},
Simpson
[
16
]
defined a functor

(Y/\mathbb{C})^{\widehat{\dR}}:\Alg_{\mathbb{C}}\to\Set

by the formula
(Y/\mathbb{C})^{\widehat{\dR}}(A):=Y(A_{\red}),
where
A_{\red}:=A/\nil(A)
for
\nil(A)
the nilradical of
A.

This functor is an fppf sheaf, has the remarkable property that it turns algebraic de Rham cohomology into coherent cohomology: the coherent cohomology of the fppf space
(Y/\mathbb{C})^{\widehat{\dR}}
is canonically identified with the algebraic de Rham cohomology of
Y,
and the category
\Qcoh((Y/\mathbb{C})^{\widehat{\dR}})
is canonically identified with the category of quasi-coherent
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-modules. As linear algebra is easier than differential algebra, in many situations it is convenient to switch from differential algebra on
Y
to linear algebra on
(Y/\mathbb{C})^{\widehat{\dR}}
; this is especially the case in non-abelian Hodge theory, as we shall see in this note.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that
(Y/\mathbb{C})^{\widehat{\dR}}(A)
is the set of all
A
-valued points of
Y,
modulo the equivalence relation that two points are equal if they are
infinitesimally close
(meaning that they become the same when restricting to
A_{\red}
).

Roughly, one can think the importance of nilpotence is because of formal power series: when
x^{\prime}-x\in\nil(A)
is nilpotent, any formal power series of the form
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}T^{n}
can be evaluated at
T=x^{\prime}-x,
whereas normally infinite sums in a ring do not make sense without first imposing a topology on the ring and discussing convergence.

Remark 2.2
.

The proof of
Theorem 2.9
also makes this analogy with power series precise; it is stated in positive characteristic, but the same proof strategy works (with even more ease) in characteristic 0.

2.2.
The sheared de Rham stack of
\mathbb{A}^{1}

We now return to

Warning 2.3

(Warning for experts)
.

Some readers may be familiar with an object

called the de Rham stack of

which geometrizes

Inspired on Simpson’s work

[

]
and Bhatt–Kanaev–Mathew–Vologodsky–Zhang
[

]
defined a positive characteristic object
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}},
called the
sheared de Rham stack
, with the following properties:

(1)

(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}
is an fpqc stack
\Alg_{k}\to\Gpd,

(2)

quasicoherent sheaves on
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}
are just
\mathcal{D}_{S/k}
-modules,

(3)

the algebraic de Rham cohomology of

is canonically identified with the coherent cohomology of

Remark 2.4
.

We refer a reader to the upcoming work Bhatt–Kanaev–Mathew–Vologodsky–Zhang
[
3
]
for proofs of these claims; we will not use them in our proof of Katz’s formula, but we state these claims for motivational purposes. Similarly, we will use that vector bundles on
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}
are simply vector bundles with flat connection on
S
when explaining a more concrete interpretation of our version of Katz’s formula, but the proof of our main theorem is purely formal and does not rely on understanding this.

The reader might observe now that Simpson’s
(Y/\mathbb{C})^{\widehat{\dR}}
was a
space
(that is, its functor of points is set valued), whereas the sheared de Rham stack is a
stack
– its functor of points is groupoid valued.

We now explain the origin of this discrepancy and give the definition of sheared de Rham stacks. In
Remark 2.1
, we explained that
(Y/\mathbb{C})^{\widehat{\dR}}
can be obtained by quotienting
Y
be the relation of ‘infinitesimal closeness,’ which is useful because one can evaluate arbitrary formal power series on nilpotent elements of a ring. However, the formal power series which arise in calculus often take the form
\sum_{n}a_{n}\frac{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{n}}{n!}.
In characteristic 0, these denominators can be ignored, but in characteristic
p
it is a big deal.

Thus, instead of saying that
x_{1},x_{2}
are close if
x_{2}-x_{1}
is nilpotent, we now say
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are close if
x_{2}-x_{1}
admits
nilpotent divided powers
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
A
be a ring. An element
a\in A
is said to admit
nilpotent divided powers
if there is a sequence
(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},...)
of elements of
A
such that

(1)

a_{0}=1,

(2)

a_{1}=a,

(3)

for all
n,m,
we have
\binom{n+m}{n}a_{n+m}=a_{n}a_{m},

(4)

for all
n\gg 0,
we have
a_{n}=0.

Remark 2.6
.

We think of
a_{n}
as being
a^{n}/n!
; the relationship
\binom{n+m}{n}a_{n+m}=a_{n}a_{m}
is then because

\frac{a^{n}}{n!}\cdot\frac{a^{m}}{m!}=\frac{a^{n+m}}{n!m!}=\frac{a^{n+m}}{(n+m)!}\cdot\binom{n+m}{n}.

While being nilpotent is a
property
of an element, having nilpotent divided powers is
structure
: there are possibly different nilpotent divided power structures on an element
a\in A.
Thus, instead of taking the set of all
A
-valued points of
S
and quotienting by an equivalence relation, we really should be using a groupoid, to remember that elements can be the same for multiple reasons.

Example 2.7

(The affine line)
.

Suppose that
S=\Spec k[x]
is the affine line. Then
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}:\Alg_{k}\to\Gpd
is
almost

-algebra

to the groupoid
\mathcal{G}(A),
where

(1)

of
\mathcal{G}(A)
are elements

(2)

an

in
\mathcal{G}(A)
is the data of a nilpotent divided power structure on the difference

We say

is actually a sheafification of this functor. However, for a

As semiperfect

Definition 2.8
.

We define
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}:\Alg_{k}\to\Ab
the functor

\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(A)=\{(a_{0},a_{1},...)\mid a_{\bullet}\text{ is a nilpotent PD sequence}\}.

The group structure is given by

a_{\bullet}+b_{\bullet}:=(1,a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+a_{1}b_{1}+b_{2},a_{3}+a_{2}b_{1}+a_{1}b_{2}+b_{3},...).

Note that there is a natural action of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
on
\mathbb{G}_{a},
given on points by

(a_{0},a_{1},...)\times b:=a_{1}b.

.

Let
M

-equivariant structure on

\nabla:M\to Mdx.

Proof.

Consider the ring
R^{\prime}=k[x_{1},x_{2},(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2}/2!,...],
where we adjoined a divided power sequence for

Set

This topological ring

Thus

so that a

This
\eta
takes the form

\eta(1\otimes s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{n}}{n!}\otimes\nabla_{n}(s),

for certain set-theoretic functions
\nabla_{n}:M\to M,
where the cocycle condition on
\eta
forces
\nabla_{n}\nabla_{m}=\nabla_{n+m},\nabla_{0}=\id.
In particular,
\eta
is uniquely determined by
\nabla_{d/dx}:=\nabla_{1}.
Observe

\displaystyle x_{2}\otimes s+x_{2}(x_{2}-x_{1})\otimes\nabla_{d/dx}(s)+\cdots

\displaystyle=x_{2}\eta(1\otimes s)

\displaystyle=\eta(x_{2}\otimes s)

\displaystyle=\eta(1\otimes xs)

\displaystyle=1\otimes xs+(x_{2}-x_{1})\otimes\nabla_{d/dx}(xs)+\cdots

\displaystyle=x_{1}\otimes s+(x_{2}-x_{1})\otimes\nabla_{d/dx}(xs)

\displaystyle=x_{2}\otimes s+(x_{2}-x_{1})\otimes(\nabla_{d/dx}(xs)-s).

Passing to the quotient where
(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2}/2!=0,
we deduce
\nabla_{d/dx}(xs)=s+\nabla_{d/dx}(s).
In other words,
\nabla
always obeys the Leibniz rule and hence defines a connection; it’s easy to see any connection gives rise to a valid
\eta,
so we conclude.
∎

2.3.
Sheared de Rham stacks in general

There are two approaches to the definition of the sheared de Rham stack of a general smooth
k
-scheme
S.
Perhaps the most obvious route is to quotient
S
by the nilpotent PD-hull of the diagonal of
S\times_{k}S.
However, there is an extremely technically convenient alternative route, via a procedure called ‘transmutation.’

Recall Simpson’s de Rham stack was defined by
(Y/\mathbb{C})^{\widehat{\dR}}(A)=Y(A/\nil(A)).
In particular, to define the de Rham stack, one only needs to understand the functor
A\mapsto A/\nil(A)).

In positive characteristic, as we have already seen, instead of quotienting by the ideal of nilpotent elements, we will need to quotient by some sort of groupoid of nilpotent PD structures. This is mostly simply done using Drinfeld’s notion of
quasi-ideals
.

Remark 2.10

(Review of Drinfeld’s quasi-ideals)
.

We review a few ideas of Drinfeld
[
8
]
. Let
R
be a ring. A
quasi-ideal
is an
R
-module
I
together with a map
d:I\to R
such that
d(x)\cdot y=d(y)\cdot x
for all
x,y\in I.

In this situation, Drinfeld constructs a
ring groupoid

\Cone(I\xrightarrow{d}R),
which is a homotopical enhancement of the ring
R/\im(d).

Definition 2.11
.

Consider the quasi-ideal
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}
of
Definition 2.8
. We define a ring stack
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR}}
as
\Cone(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}).

Remark 2.12
.

We refer a reader to section 2 of
[
2
]
for discussion of how the Drinfeld cone interacts with sheafification, as
\mathbb{G}_{a}
is not quite a ring but a sheaf of rings. For
A
semiperfect, though, it is literally true that

\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR}}(A)=\Cone(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(A)\to A).

We now define the sheared de Rham stack, using essentially the same definition as Simpson but replacing
A_{\red}
with
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR}}(A).

Definition 2.13
.

The
sheared de Rham stack
of a smooth
k
-scheme
S
is the fpqc sheaf

(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}:\Alg_{k}\to\Gpd

defined by

(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}(A)=S(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\dR}(A)).

Remark 2.14

(Relative sheared de Rham stacks)
.

For a general smooth morphism
f:X\to S,
we define the
relative sheared de Rham stack

(2.15)

(X/S)^{\widehat{\dR}}\simeq(X/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}\times_{(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}}S.

Remark 2.16
.

One can still define
(X/S)^{\widehat{\dR}}
or
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}
without assuming smoothness, but in the general case it seems these objects are not so well-behaved.

2.4.
On
p
-curvature

When constructing the sheared de Rham stack of
\mathbb{A}^{1},
we discussed how ‘admitting divided powers’ is a structure instead of a property, and so it is more natural to quotient by a groupoid instead of an equivalence relation. But what if we had just quotiented by the equivalence relation of ‘admitting nilpotent divided powers’? This ends up resulting in an invariant called
p
-curvature
. A slightly different approach to
p
-curvature via de Rham stacks can be found in section 3 of
[
2
]
.

To begin, we remark that the equivalence relation ‘admitting nilpotent divided powers’ is quite simple.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
A
be an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra. Then
a\in A
admits nilpotent divided powers if and only if
a^{p}=0.

Proof.

If
a
admits nilpotent divided powers
(a_{1},a_{2},...),
then there is some
a_{p}\in A
such that

a^{p}=p!\cdot a_{p}

(this follows by repeatedly applying the divided power relation
a_{n}a_{m}=\binom{n+m}{n}a_{n+m}
with
m=1
). But
p!=0
in
A,
so
a^{p}=0\cdot a_{p}=0.

Conversely, assume
a^{p}=0.
Then it is easy to check that

a_{n}=\begin{cases}a^{n}/n!&n<p,\\
0&n\geq p,\end{cases}

is a nilpotent PD sequence.
∎

By
Lemma 2.17
, the image of the quasi-ideal
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}
is the ideal
\alpha_{p}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a}.
Thus, if instead of quotienting by a groupoid we wanted to quotient by an equivalence relation, then we should transmute by
\Cone(\alpha_{p}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a})
instead of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR}}=\Cone(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}).

Remark 2.18

(The Frobenius twist of a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-module)
.

By a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-module
, we mean an fpqc sheaf of abelian groups on
\Alg_{k}
endowed with an action of the abelian sheaf
\mathbb{G}_{a}.
For a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-module
M,
we write
F_{*}M
to denote the
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-module with the same underlying abelian sheaf, but with the action twisted by Frobenius: on
A
-points, for
\lambda\in A,m\in M(A),
we define

\lambda\cdot m:=\lambda^{p}m,

where the left hand side is the
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action on
F_{*}M,
and the right hand side is evaluated using the
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action on
M.

Lemma 2.19
.

We have
\Cone(\alpha_{p}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a})\simeq F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}
as a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-module.

Proof.

The commutative square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{G}_{a}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x\mapsto x^{p}}

of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-modules induces an injective
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-linear map

(2.20)

\Cone(\alpha_{p}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a})\to F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}.

This is surjective as a map of sheaves, because locally in the flat topology we can find
p^{\text{th}}
roots.
∎

Remark 2.21
.

In particular, the transmutation of
S
with respect to
\mathbb{G}_{a}/\alpha_{p}
is just
S^{\prime},
the relative Frobenius twist of
S
over
k.
The natural map
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR}}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}/\alpha_{p}
of ring stacks induces via transmutation a morphism

(2.22)

(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}\to S^{\prime}.

We now study how far
(2.22)
is from being an equivalence. This boils down to understanding how far apart
\alpha_{p}
and
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
are.

Lemma 2.23
.

There is a short exact sequence of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-modules

(2.24)

0\to F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\alpha_{p}\to 0.

The map
F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
is given on
A
-points by the formula

(a_{0},a_{1},...)\mapsto(b_{0}=1,b_{1}=0,b_{2},b_{3},...),

where

b_{n}:=\begin{cases}0&p\nmid n,\\
a_{n/p}&p\mid n.\end{cases}

Remark 2.25
.

The map
F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
is reminiscient of the Verschiebung; this analogy is made a little more precise by the Witt vector model of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR},c}
; see section 2.7 of Bhatt
[
5
]
for more information.

Proof.

By Lucas’ theorem that
\binom{pn}{pm}\equiv\binom{n}{m}\pmod{p},
it’s easy to check that
b_{\bullet}
is a PD sequence whenever
a_{\bullet}
is.

This map
F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
is injective (because a PD sequence
a_{\bullet}
in characteristic
p
is determined by its values
a_{pn}
on the multiples of
p
), and
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-linear (the Frobenius twist on the source corrects the shift by
p
).

As
a_{\bullet}\in F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(A)
is always sent to a
b_{\bullet}
with
b_{1}=0,
we see that this map has image contained in the kernel of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\alpha_{p}.
It also surjects onto the kernel; for any PD structure
(1,0,\epsilon_{2},...)
on 0, the PD structure
(1,\epsilon_{p},\epsilon_{2p},...)
will be sent to
(1,0,\epsilon_{2},...).
The surjectivity of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\alpha_{p}
is
Lemma 2.17
, so we conclude.
∎

Definition 2.26
.

We set
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\psi_{p}}:=\mathbb{G}_{a}\oplus B(F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}),
and write
S^{\psi_{p}}
to denote the transmutation of
S
with respect to
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\psi_{p}}.

This
S^{\psi_{p}}
helps us explain the difference between
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}
and
S^{\prime}.
We can extract a useful invariant of connections, called
p
-curvature
, out of
S^{\psi_{p}}.

Remark 2.27
.

Suppose
S
is a smooth
k
-scheme. A quasicoherent sheaf on
S^{\psi_{p}}
is then, by deformation theory (note
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\psi_{p}}
is a
split
square-zero extension of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
), just a quasicoherent sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
S
together with a map
\psi_{p}:\mathcal{E}\to F_{\abs}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{E}
such that
\psi_{p}\wedge\psi_{p}=0.
We give a slightly more concrete way to see this for
S=\mathbb{A}^{1}
in
Remark 2.28
.

Remark 2.28
.

Let
M
be a
k[x]
-module. We now spell out what a
F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
-equivariant structure on
M
is. The description is very close to the description of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
-equivariant structures from
Theorem 2.9
.

To give such an equivariant structure it is equivalent to give
M
the structure of a continuous
H=k[x_{1},x_{2}]\left\langle x_{2}-x_{1}\right\rangle/(x_{2}-x_{1})
-comodule, where
H
denotes the Hopf algebra

k\left[\frac{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{p}}{p!},\frac{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2p}}{(2p)!},...\right].

This comodule structure takes the form of a map

\rho:M\to H\widehat{\otimes}_{k[x]}M,

and so we may write

\rho(s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{np}}{(np)!}\otimes\psi_{n}(s)

for various set-theoretic functions
\psi_{n}:M\to M.
The comodule axioms then become
\psi_{0}=\id,\psi_{n+m}=\psi_{n}\circ\psi_{m},
so that everything is determined by the endomorphism
\psi:M\to M.
It’s easy to see every possible
\psi
gives rise to such a comodule, so that
F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
-equivariant structures are the same as endomorphisms of
M.

Construction 2.1

(The
p
-curvature)
.

Suppose
S
is a smooth
k
-scheme. Let
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
be a vector bundle with flat connection on
S.
Viewing
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
as a vector bundle on
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}},
we define the
p
-curvature
of
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
to be its pullback along the map

S^{\psi_{p}}\to(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}

obtained by transmuting
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\psi_{p}}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR}}.
In light of
Remark 2.27
, we can view a vector bundle on
S^{\psi_{p}}
as a vector bundle on
S
together with a flat
F_{\abs}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{S}
-twisted endomorphism. As the quotient map
\mathbb{G}_{a}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR}}
factors through
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\psi_{p}}
(since
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\psi_{p}}
is defined by quotienting
\mathbb{G}_{a}
by a smaller quasi-ideal), the pullback of
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
to
S^{\psi_{p}}
has underlying vector bundle
\mathcal{E},
and hence the
p
-curvature can be viewed as a morphism

\psi_{p}:\mathcal{E}\to F_{\abs}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{E}.

Proposition 2.29
.

Let
S
be a smooth
k
-scheme. Recall that Cartier descent furnishes
F_{\abs}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{S}
with a natural flat connection. For any vector bundle with flat connection
(\mathcal{E},\nabla),
the
p
-curvature

\psi_{p}:\mathcal{E}\to F_{\abs}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{E}

is horizontal. In fact,
\psi_{p}
agrees with the classical construction of
p
-curvature (as given by Katz
[
11
]
).

Remark 2.30
.

The observation that
p
-curvature is horizontal seems to have first been made by Ogus
[
13
]
. A different proof of this fact, also using de Rham stack’s, can be found in Section 3 of
[
2
]
.

Proof.

Checking a map is horizontal (or, for the latter part, that two maps are equal) can be done etale locally, so we may assume
S=\mathbb{A}^{d}_{k}.
In fact, by looking at the maps
\psi_{p,d/dx_{i}}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E},\nabla_{d/dx_{i}}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E},
we can check this one coordinate at a time, and hence we can even assume
d=1
to make the notation simpler.

In the case where
S=\Spec k[x],
we then just check directly, using the explicit identification of connections and
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
-equivariant structures explained in the proof of
Theorem 2.9
. Specifically, let
(M,\nabla)
be a vector bundle with flat connection on
S.
The corresponding
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
-equivariant structure on
M
is the map

\rho:M\to k[x,x^{2}/2!,...]\widehat{\otimes}_{k[x]}M

defined by

\rho(s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\otimes\nabla_{d/dx}^{n}(s).

Restricting this to
F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
corresponds to quotienting
k[x,x^{2}/2!,...]
by the ideal
(x),
so that only the terms
x^{np}/(np)!
survive. As in
Remark 2.28
, we see that the endomorphism
\psi_{p,d/dx}:M\to M
encoding the
F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
-equivariant structure is just
\nabla_{d/dx}^{p},
exactly recovering the usual formula for
p
-curvature, and so we conclude.
∎

There is also a more precise claim about the relationship between
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}\to S^{\prime}
that one can make.

Remark 2.31

(The torsor property)
.

The map
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}\to S^{\prime}
constructed in
(2.22)
is in fact a torsor for
B\mathbb{V}(F_{S/k}^{*}T_{S^{\prime}/k}),
as shown in Proposition 2.7.1 of Bhatt’s
[
5
]
; though Bhatt’s argument was given in the non-sheared case, it goes through essentially verbatim in the sheared case, as explained in section 2.3 of
[
2
]
.

3.
Non-abelian Griffiths filtered connections

Fix
k
a field of characteristic
p,
and
S
a smooth
k
-scheme.

As remarked in the introduction, one of the most important structures on algebraic de Rham cohomology is its Hodge filtration. The Hodge filtration interacts with the Gauss–Manin connection via a condition called Griffiths transversality.

Remark 3.1

(Griffiths transversality)
.

We recall that, for a vector bundle with flat connection
(\mathcal{E},\nabla:\mathcal{E}\to\Omega^{1}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{E})
on
S,
a decreasing filtration
F^{\bullet}\mathcal{E}
is said to obey
Griffiths transversality
if
\nabla(F^{i})\subseteq\Omega^{1}_{S}\otimes F^{i-1}.

In this section (
Definition 3.13
), we give the non-abelian analogue of a ‘filtration obeying the Griffiths transversality condition.’ In
Section 5
, we will see that the non-abelian Gauss–Manin connection admits a non-abelian Hodge filtration obeying Griffiths transversality, which is why we need to develop this notion.

3.1.
Filtered stacks

To begin, we recall Simpson’s theory of non-abelian filtrations.

Notation 3.2
.

We write
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
for the quotient stack of
\Spec k[\lambda]
by
\Spec k[\lambda^{\pm 1}],
with the usual action. We recall that
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
has two physical points: an open immersion
\Spec(k)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
, corresponding to the open orbit of all nonzero points, and a closed immersion
B\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m},
coming from the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant closed immersion
\{0\}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}.

Inspired by the Rees construction, which identifies filtered vector spaces with quasicoherent sheaves on
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m},
Simpson made the following definition.

Definition 3.3
.

A
filtration
on a prestack
T
is a map of prestacks
\tilde{T}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
together with an isomorphism
T\simeq\tilde{T}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}\Spec(k).
The
associated graded
of a filtration
\tilde{T}
is the prestack
\gr(\tilde{T}):=\tilde{T}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}B\mathbb{G}_{m}.
In particular,
\gr(\tilde{T})
admits a natural map to
B\mathbb{G}_{m}.

3.2.
Review of Simpson’s work and the Hodge-filtered sheared de Rham stack

In this section, we will define a certain stack
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}
over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
, called the
Hodge-filtered sheared de Rham stack
and studied by Drinfeld
[
7
]
and Bhatt–Lurie
[
4
]
(see also Bhatt’s
[
5
]
), which geometrizes Hodge-filtered de Rham cohomology in the same way that
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}
geometrizes de Rham cohomology.

The idea behind
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}
goes back to Simpson
[
16
]
. Vector bundles on
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}
are the same as vector bundles on
S
with flat connection; however, there is a natural 1-parameter deformation of the notion of flat connection. A
\lambda
-connection
, for
\lambda\in k,
on a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
S
is a map

\nabla:\mathcal{E}\to\Omega^{1}_{S/k}\otimes\mathcal{E}

obeying the
\lambda
-Leibniz rule

\nabla(f\cdot s)=f\nabla(s)+\lambda df\otimes s.

The moduli stack of
\lambda
-connections then lives over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\lambda}.
However, if
\lambda^{\prime}=u\lambda
for a unit
u\in k^{\times},
then
\lambda
-connections and
\lambda^{\prime}
-connections are in bijection (rescale by
u
). Thus it is slightly more natural to view the moduli of
\lambda
-connections as living over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}.

We now define
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}
as some modification of the de Rham stack which lives over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
and geometrizes
\lambda
-connections.

Example 3.4

(The Hodge-filtered de Rham stack of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
)
.

In the case of
S=\mathbb{A}^{1},
recall that we defined
(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}
as the fpqc stack whose functor of points sent a semiperfect
k
-algebra
A
to the groupoid whose objects are elements
x\in A,
and whose isomorphisms
x\simeq y
are nilpotent PD structures on
y-x.

To get
\lambda
-connections instead of connections, one just slightly tweaks this groupoid: for any
\lambda\in k,
we define
(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}/k)^{\lambda-\widehat{\dR}}
as the fpqc stack sending a semiperfect
k
-algebra
A
to the groupoid whose objects are again elements
x\in A,
but where isomorphisms
x\simeq y
are now the datum of a nilpotent PD sequence
(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},...)
in
A
such that
\lambda\epsilon_{1}=y-x.
Thus

(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k)^{\lambda-\widehat{\dR}}\simeq\Cone(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\xrightarrow{\lambda}\mathbb{A}^{1}).

Definition 3.5
.

Inspired by
Example 3.4
, we define
(\mathbb{G}_{a}/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}
as the ring stack

(3.6)

(\mathbb{G}_{a}/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}:=\Cone(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(-1)\to\mathbb{A}^{1})

over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}.

Remark 3.7
.

Let us explain what we mean by this twist by
-1.
It is a consequence of working over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}.

First, we explain a simpler sort of twist. When defining a stack over
\mathbb{A}^{1},
your test objects are now
k[\lambda]
-algebras instead of
k
-algebras. A
k[\lambda]
-algebra can be viewed as a
k
-algebra
A
together with a map
\lambda:A\to A.
The twist by
-1
indicates that the map
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(A)\to A
which we quotient by to get our groupoid of points
\Spec(A)\to(\mathbb{A}^{1}/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}
should not just be the canonical forgetful map, but instead should be
\lambda
times the canonical forgetful map.

To get our meaning of twist, recall that a map
\Spec(A)\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
, for a
k
-algebra
A,
is just the data of some
L\in\Pic(A)
together with an
A
-linear map
t:L\to A.
By
(3.6)
, we just mean that the functor of points of
(\mathbb{G}_{a}/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+},
as an
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
-stack, is such that for a semiperfect
k
-algebra
A
with a map
\Spec(A)\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m},
the mapping space

\Map_{\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}((\Spec(A),L\in\Pic(A),t:L\to A),(\mathbb{G}_{a}/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+})

is the groupoid whose objects are elements
x\in A
and where an isomorphism
x\simeq y
is the data of some
\epsilon\in\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(A)\otimes_{A}L
such that
(d\otimes t)(\epsilon)=y-x,
for
d:\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(A)\to A
the forgetful map.

Definition 3.8

(Hodge-filtered sheared de Rham stacks in general)
.

Via transmutation, we then (following Bhatt
[
5
]
, definition 2.5.1) define
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
as the functor

(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}(\Spec(A)\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}):=S(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR},+}(\Spec(A)\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m})).

Remark 3.9
.

The
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
gives us a filtration on
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}
in the sense of
Definition 3.3
, because
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR},+}|_{\lambda=1}=\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR}}.

Remark 3.10

(Relative Hodge-filtered de Rham stacks)
.

For a smooth morphism
X\to S,
we generally define

(X/S)^{\widehat{\dR},+}=(X/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}\times_{S^{\widehat{\dR},+}}S.

3.3.
The Hodge stack

The Hodge-filtered sheared de Rham stack
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}
lives over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}.
The stack
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
has two physical points,
\Spec(k)
and
B\mathbb{G}_{m}.
We already saw (in
Remark 3.9
) that base changing along
\Spec(k)
turns the Hodge-filtered sheared de Rham stack into the ordinary sheared de Rham stack; in effect, it forgets the filtration.

It is then natural to ask what the base change over
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
is. Viewing a quasicoherent sheaf on
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
as a filtered vector space, the pullback to
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
corresponds to taking the associated graded (in fact, quasicoherent sheaves on
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
are just
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
k
-vector spaces). Thus we expect that the base change of
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}
to
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
will have something to do with the associated graded of the Hodge filtration. This inspires the following name.

Definition 3.11
.

We define the
Hodge stack
of
S
over
k
to be

(S/k)^{\Hodge}:=(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}B\mathbb{G}_{m}.

Remarkably,
(S/k)^{\Hodge}
has a very simple geometric structure.

Proposition 3.12
.

There is an equivalence

(S/k)^{\Hodge}\simeq B_{S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}}(T_{S/k}(-1)).

In other words,
(S/k)^{\Hodge}
is a split gerbe over
S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}.
Here, by
T_{S/k}(-1),
we denote the vector bundle
T_{S/k}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}(-1)
on
S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}
(where
\mathcal{O}(-1)
is the dual of the canonical line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
on
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
).

Proof.

We use the argument of Bhatt
[
5
]
, footnote 18. Over
B\mathbb{G}_{m},
the map
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(-1)\to\mathbb{G}_{a}
is just the zero map, and hence

\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR}}|_{B\mathbb{G}_{m}}=\mathbb{G}_{a}\oplus B\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(-1).

This is the split square-zero extension of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
by
B\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(-1),
and so we conclude by deformation theory.
∎

3.4.
Griffiths-filtered non-abelian connections

We can now introduce the notion of a
Griffiths-filtered
non-abelian connection alluded to in the introduction to this section.

Definition 3.13
.

A
Griffiths-filtered non-abelian connection
over
S
is a Cartesian diagram of prestacks
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{E}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}.}

Base changing this diagram of
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
-stacks along the open immersion
\Spec(k)\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m},
we get a Cartesian diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}},}

which we call the
underlying non-abelian connection
.

Remark 3.14
.

The data of this Cartesian diagram is a bit redundant; one can recover it from just the map
\tilde{E}^{\prime}\to(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}.

We end this section by explaining a certain useful invariant of Griffiths-filtered connections.

3.5.
Digression: A non-abelian variant of Cartier duality

Recall that
Cartier duality
identifies quasicoherent sheaves on
B\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}
with quasicoherent sheaves on
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})
(we don’t state this more precisely because we will not use this claim in the article). As the former objects are simpler than the latter, Cartier duality is incredibly computationally useful.

We now introduce a (weak) non-abelian variant of Cartier duality. One reason for its relevance has already been seen: above, we showed
(S/k)^{\Hodge}\simeq B_{S}\mathbb{V}(\Omega^{1}_{S})^{\#,\wedge}.
As we are considering non-abelian connections in this paper, instead of connections, we will have prestacks over
B_{S}\mathbb{V}(\Omega^{1}_{S})^{\#,\wedge}
instead of quasicoherent sheaves on it. We now explain a simple construction to extract an easily understood invariant from a prestack over
B_{S}\mathbb{V}(\Omega^{1}_{S})^{\#,\wedge}.
This construction will be used again in the sequel, which is why we give it in so much generality now.

We first introduce some notation.

Definition 3.15
.

Let
F:\Sch_{S}^{\op}\to\Gpd
be a prestack over
S.
Its
tangent prestack
is the functor

\mathcal{T}_{F/S}:\Sch_{S}^{\op}\to\Gpd

given by
\mathcal{T}_{F/S}(Z)=F(Z[\epsilon]),
for
Z[\epsilon]
the trivial square-zero extension of
Z
by
\mathcal{O}_{Z}.

Note that there is a natural map
\mathcal{T}_{F/S}\to F,
corresponding to the closed immersion
Z\hookrightarrow Z[\epsilon].

We now give two constructions.

Situation 3.1
.

Fix
\mathcal{E}
a vector bundle on
S,
and
F:\Sch_{S}^{\op}\to\Gpd
a prestack, with structure morphism
p:F\to S.

Set
S^{\prime}=S\oplus\mathcal{E}
the trivial square-zero extension of
S
by
\mathcal{E},
and
F\oplus p^{*}\mathcal{E}
the trivial square-zero extension of
F
by
p^{*}\mathcal{E}.
That is,
F\oplus p^{*}\mathcal{E}
is the prestack

Z\mapsto\Hom_{S}(Z,S^{\prime})\times F(S^{\prime}).

Construction 3.1

(Infinitesimal automorphism to derivation)
.

Suppose we are in
3.1
. Fix an automorphism of prestacks

\eta:F\oplus p^{*}(\mathcal{E})\simeq F\oplus p^{*}(\mathcal{E}).

Given this automorphism
\eta,
we will construct a map of
S
-prestacks

\theta:\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})\times_{S}F\to\mathcal{T}_{F/S}.

Indeed, fix a test scheme
s:Z\to S.
We need to produce a map

\theta(Z):(\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})\times_{S}F)(Z)\to F(Z[\epsilon]).

The groupoid
(\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})\times_{S}F)(Z)
has as its objects the pairs
(\alpha:s^{*}\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{O}_{Z},x\in F(Z))
and has isomorphisms
(\alpha,x)\simeq(\alpha,x^{\prime})
in bijection with isomorphisms
x\simeq x^{\prime}
in
F(Z).

On objects, we define
\theta(Z)(\alpha,x)
as follows. Writing
Z^{\prime}:=Z\oplus s^{*}(\mathcal{E})
the trivial square-zero extension, we can combine
\alpha,x
to get a composite map

x_{\alpha}:Z[\epsilon]\xrightarrow{\alpha}Z^{\prime}\xrightarrow{x^{\prime}}F\oplus p^{*}(\mathcal{E}).

Here, recalling

Z^{\prime}=Z\times_{S}S^{\prime},F\oplus p^{*}(\mathcal{E})=F\times_{S}S^{\prime},

we set
x^{\prime}=(x,\id_{S^{\prime}}).

We then define

\theta(Z)(\alpha,x):=\pi(\eta(x_{\alpha})),

for
\pi:F\oplus p^{*}(\mathcal{E})\to F
the projection. As
\theta(Z)
is defined by taking
x
and applying various compositions, isomorphisms
x\simeq x^{\prime}
can be mapped to isomorphisms in
F(Z[\epsilon]),
and similarly we can construct coherences proving
\theta(Z)
is functorial in
Z.
Thus we get our map
\theta,
as desired.

Remark 3.16
.

When
\eta=\id,
the map
\theta
is just the canonical map

\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})\times_{S}F\to F\hookrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{F/S},

where the second map is the zero-section arising from the natural map
Z[\epsilon]\xrightarrow{\epsilon=0}Z
of
S
-schemes. This is because we always have a commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F\oplus p^{*}(\mathcal{E})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}

Construction 3.2
.

Fix
S
an arbitrary prestack, and choose a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
S.
Let
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{S}\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

be a Cartesian diagram of
S
-prestacks. Here,
s:S\to B_{S}\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}
is the natural section of the structure map
B_{S}\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge})\to S
(recalling that maps to
B_{S}\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}
classify
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}
-torsors, this section
s
just classifies
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}
itself, viewed as a torsor over
S
).

In this situation, we can construct a map of prestacks

\theta:\mathbb{V}(\varphi^{*}\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{T}_{E/S}

as follows.

This diagram gives rise to a
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}
-action on
E
, with action map
a:\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}\times_{S}E\to E
defined as the composition

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}\times_{S}E

\displaystyle\simeq(S\times_{B\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}}S)\times_{S}(S\times_{B\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}}E^{\prime})

\displaystyle\simeq S\times_{B\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}}E^{\prime}\times_{B\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}}S

\displaystyle\to S\times_{B\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}}E^{\prime}

\displaystyle\simeq E.

We first construct, from this action, an automorphism
\eta
of
E\oplus\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})
(the base change of
E
along
S\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\to S
).

The groupoid of
Z
-points of
E\oplus\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})
is
\Hom_{S}(Z,S\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\vee})\times E(Z).
As
S\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
is just the zero section of
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E}),
we get a closed immersion
i:S\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}.
Base changing this closed immersion along
E
and composing with the action map
a
from above, we get a map

E\oplus\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})^{\#,\wedge}\times_{S}E\xrightarrow{a}E.

This map, together with the projection
E\oplus\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})\to S\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\vee},
defines a nontrivial map

\eta:E\oplus\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})\to E\oplus\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee}).

An inverse to
\eta
can be obtained via a similar construction, and so
\eta
is an automorphism. In particular, we can now apply
Construction 3.1
to construct our desired map
\theta
.

3.6.
Non-abelian Higgs fields

We now explain the non-abelian analogue of the notion of Higgs fields. For the reader’s benefit, we start by recalling Higgs fields in the abelian case.

Remark 3.17

(What is a Higgs fields in the abelian case?)
.

Suppose we have a vector bundle with flat connection
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
on
S,
together with a decreasing filtration
F^{\bullet}\mathcal{E}
obeying Griffiths transversality:

\nabla(F^{n}\mathcal{E})\subseteq\Omega^{1}_{S/k}\otimes F^{n-1}\mathcal{E}.

As the filtration is decreasing, Griffiths transversality is slightly weaker than saying
\nabla(F^{n}\mathcal{E})\subseteq\Omega^{1}_{S/k}\otimes F^{n}\mathcal{E}.
In other words,
\nabla
need not preserve the filtration
F^{\bullet},
but it isn’t that far from preserving it. The
Higgs field
of
(F^{\bullet},\mathcal{E},\nabla)
is some linear invariant measuring how far
\nabla
is from preserving
F^{\bullet}.
The Higgs field is defined to be the map

\theta:\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\gr^{n}_{F}\mathcal{E}\to\Omega^{1}_{S/k}\otimes\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\gr^{n-1}_{F}\mathcal{E}

obtained as the direct sum of the maps

\theta_{n}:\gr^{n}_{F}\to\Omega^{1}_{S/k}\otimes\gr^{n-1}_{F}

given by
\theta_{n}(s)=\nabla(s).
This is well-defined on the graded pieces thanks to Griffiths transversality. Moreover, unlike
\lambda,
these maps
\theta_{n}
are actually linear, because

\theta_{n}(fs)=df\otimes s+f\nabla(s)=0+f\theta_{n}(s),

because the term
df\otimes s\in\Omega^{1}_{S/k}\otimes F^{n}
will vanish in
\Omega^{1}_{S/k}\otimes\gr^{n-1}_{F}=\Omega^{1}_{S/k}\otimes F^{n-1}/F^{n}.

To construct a non-abelian analogue of Higgs fields, we simply apply
Construction 3.2
to the split gerbe
(S/k)^{\Hodge}.

Construction 3.3

(Non-abelian Higgs field)
.

Let
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},+}}

be a Cartesian diagram (that is, a Hodge-filtered non-abelian connection). This is a Cartesian diagram of stacks over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
; base change to
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
to get a Cartesian diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gr(E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\prime}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}B\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(S/k)^{\Hodge}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

But
(S/k)^{\Hodge}
is a split gerbe, as we just explained, so
Construction 3.2
can be applied to construct a map

\theta:\mathbb{V}(\varphi^{*}T_{S/k}(-1))\to\mathcal{T}_{\gr(E)/(S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})}.

4.
Non-abelian conjugate filtrations and
p
-curvature

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p
and
S
a smooth
k
-scheme. In the introduction, we alluded to the existence of a second filtration on algebraic de Rham cohomology; we now explore it via de Rham stacks.

4.1.
The conjugate filtration

Algebraic de Rham cohomology in positive characteristic carries an extra filtration besides the Hodge filtration, called the
conjugate filtration
; see the discussion of Katz
[
11
]
for a more classical perspectives.

Bhatt
[
5
]
(particularly his section 2.7) takes the following perspective on the conjugate filtration. Recall from
Remark 2.31
that
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}\to S^{\prime}
is a torsor for
B\mathbb{V}(\Omega_{S^{\prime}/k}^{1})^{\#,\wedge}.
It is then natural to put
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}
in a 1-parameter family of stacks over
S^{\prime}
which degenerates to the split torsor. As with the Hodge-filtered de Rham stack, it is convenient to view this degeneration as living over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
instead of
\mathbb{A}^{1}.

Definition 4.1
.

In this construction, we work over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}.
Following Bhatt
[
5
]
’s construction 2.7.8, we define a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-module
G_{u}
as the pushout
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{u},}

where
F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(+1)
is the canonical map induced by the twist, and
F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
is the Verschiebung-type map of
Lemma 2.23
.

The quasi-ideals
0:F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(+1)\to\mathbb{G}_{a}
and
\can:\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}
agree on
F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
(recall
Lemma 2.23
), and hence there is a natural map
G_{u}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}
making
G_{u}
into a quasi-ideal.

We define
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR},c}:=\Cone(G_{u}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}).

Remark 4.2

(Signs)
.

When studying the Hodge-filtered de Rham stack, we frequently encountered twists by
-1.
This negative one ultiamtely came from the fact that Griffiths transversality deals with
decreasing
filtrations. As the conjugate filtration is instead
increasing
, this
G_{u}
is defined using a twist by
+1.

Remark 4.3

(An alternative motivation)
.

Let
\lambda
denote the coordinate on
\mathbb{A}^{1}.
Then, for
t\in k,
vector bundles on
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR},c}|_{\lambda=t}
are, by the same argument as in
Theorem 2.9
, naturally identified with vector bundles with flat connection
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
together with a
horizontal
integrable map
\theta:\mathcal{E}\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}/k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
such that
t\theta=\psi_{p}.
The horizontalness comes from the constraint that the
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
and
F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}
-equivariant structures commute, just as it did in
Proposition 2.29
. Thus this
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR},c}
is just the de Rham stack way of accessing the stack Lam–Litt
[
12
]
dubbed
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}.

Definition 4.4
.

We define the
conjugate-filtered de Rham stack
of
S
as the stack
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},c}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
obtained via transmutation with respect to
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR},c},
so that

(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR},c}(\Spec(A)\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m})=S(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR},c}(\Spec(A)\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m})).

Remark 4.5
.

As with the de Rham stack, there are natural maps of
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
-ring stacks

(4.6)

\mathbb{G}_{a}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR},c}\to F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a},

the first one coming from the fact that
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR},c}
is a quotient of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
(by the quasi-ideal
G_{u}
) and the second one being the map induced by
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{u}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{G}_{a}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{G}_{a},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id}

the map
G_{u}\to\alpha_{p}
being given by the sum of the canonical map
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\to\alpha_{p}
with the zero map
0:F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(+1)\to\alpha_{p}.

Transmuting
(4.6)
, we get natural maps
S\times\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\to S^{\widehat{\dR},c}
and
S^{\widehat{\dR},c}\to S^{\prime}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m},

whose composition is just the Frobenius
F_{S/k}\times\id_{\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}:S\times\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\to S^{\prime}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}.

Remark 4.7

(Relative conjugated-filtered de Rham stacks)
.

For a smooth morphism
X\to S,
we generally define

(X/S)^{\widehat{\dR},c}=(X/k)^{\widehat{\dR},c}\times_{S^{\widehat{\dR},c}}S.

4.2.
The associated graded of
S^{\widehat{\dR},c}

As with the Hodge-filtered de Rham stack, we can take the associated graded of
S^{\widehat{\dR},c}.

Definition 4.8
.

The
conjugate Hodge stack
is defined as

S^{\Hodge,c}:=S^{\widehat{\dR},c}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}B\mathbb{G}_{m}.

Remark 4.9
.

This
S^{\Hodge,c}
can also be obtained by transmutation with respect to
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\widehat{\dR},c}|_{\lambda=0}.

Proposition 4.10
.

There is a canonical isomorphism

S^{\Hodge,c}\simeq B_{S^{\prime}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}}\mathbb{V}(T_{S^{\prime}/k}(+1)).

Proof.

Over
B\mathbb{G}_{m},
the pushout diagram defining
G_{u}
becomes simply
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{u}|_{\lambda=0},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

and so
G_{u}|_{\lambda=0}\simeq\alpha_{p}\oplus F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(+1),
recalling from
Lemma 2.23
that
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}/F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}\simeq\alpha_{p}.
From
Lemma 2.19
, we have
\mathbb{G}_{a}/\alpha_{p}\simeq F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a},
and so

\Cone(G_{u}|_{\lambda=0}\to\mathbb{G}_{a})\simeq\Cone(F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(+1)\xrightarrow{0}F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a})=F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}\oplus B(F_{*}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\#,\wedge}(+1)).

Exactly as with the Hodge stack, deformation theory now tells us that
S^{\Hodge,c}
is a canoncially split
B\mathbb{V}(T_{S^{\prime}/k}(+1))
-torsor over
S^{\prime},
as desired.
∎

4.3.
Conjugate filtered connections and their
p
-curvature

Analogously to Griffiths-filtered non-abelian connections, we can also define a notion of non-abelian connections equipped with something behaving like the conjugate filtration.

Definition 4.11
.

A
conjugate-filtered non-abelian connection
is a Cartesian diagram of
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
-prestacks
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\widehat{\dR},c}.}

Just as the description of
(S/k)^{\Hodge}
as a split gerbe allowed us to define non-abelian Higgs fields,
Proposition 4.10
allows us to extract a certain interesting invariant out of conjugate-filtered non-abelian connections.

Remark 4.12

(The abelian situation)
.

We first explain what the abelian analogue of the following construction is. Suppose
G_{\bullet}
is an increasing filtration on a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
endowed with a connection
\nabla
such that
\nabla
preserves
G_{i}
and such that
\gr^{G}(\mathcal{E})
has
p
-curvature zero. In this situation,
\psi_{p}
, which of course sends
G_{i}
to
G_{i},
actually does a little better: it even sends
G_{i}
to
G_{i+1}.
Thus we can view
p
-curvature as a graded map
\mathcal{E}\to F_{S/k}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{S^{\prime}/k}(-1)\otimes\mathcal{E},
with the twist by
-1
representing that the grading gets shifted.

Construction 4.1

(Conjugate-filtered
p
-curvature)
.

Fix a conjugate-filtered non-abelian connection. Base changing to
B\mathbb{G}_{m},
we get a Cartesian diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gr(E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gr(\tilde{E})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\Hodge,c}.}

We call from
Remark 4.5
that there is a natural map
\nu:S^{\Hodge,c}\to S^{\prime}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m},
and the composition
S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}\to S^{\Hodge,c}\to S^{\prime}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}
is just
(F_{S^{\prime}/k},\id_{B\mathbb{G}_{m}}).

Recall from
Proposition 4.10
that
S^{\Hodge,c}\to S^{\prime}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}
is actually a canonically split gerbe. Call the splitting
s:S^{\prime}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}\to S^{\Hodge,c}.
Composing this with the map
S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}\to S^{\Hodge,c},
we get a Cartesian diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gr(E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gr(\tilde{E})\times_{S^{\Hodge,c}}(S^{\prime}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gr(\tilde{E})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\prime}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\Hodge,c}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\prime}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{S/k},\id)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id}

Applying
Construction 3.2
to the right square of this diagram, setting
\gr(E)^{\prime}:=\gr(\tilde{E})\times_{S^{\Hodge,c}}(S^{\prime}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}),
we get a map

\alpha:\mathbb{V}(\varphi_{1}^{*}T_{S^{\prime}/k}(+1))\to\mathcal{T}_{\gr(E)^{\prime}/(S^{\prime}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})}.

We then define

\psi_{p}:\mathbb{V}(\varphi^{*}F_{S/k}^{*}T_{S^{\prime}/k}(+1))\to\mathcal{T}_{\gr(E)/(S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})}

as the base change of
\alpha
along
(F_{S/k},\id):S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}\to S^{\prime}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}.

5.
Katz’s formula

Finally, we can now prove Katz’s formula.

5.1.
The isomonodromy foliation

Fix
k
a field of characteristic
p,
and
S
a smooth
k
-scheme. Fix
f:X\to S
a smooth proper morphism.

Warning 5.1
.

Throughout this section, we will write
S^{\widehat{\dR}}
to denote
(S/k)^{\widehat{\dR}},
as everything will be done over
k.

Recall from
Definition 1.5
that a non-abelian connection on
S
is a stack over
S^{\widehat{\dR}}.
In the introduction, we defined

\mathcal{M}_{\dR}(X/S,n):\Sch_{S}\to\Gpd

as the functor sending an
S
-scheme
T
to the groupoid of triples
(\mathcal{E},\nabla,\xi)
where

(1)

\mathcal{E}
is a rank
n
vector bundle on
X\times_{S}T,

(2)

\nabla:\mathcal{E}\to\Omega^{1}_{(X\times_{S}T)/T}\otimes\mathcal{E}
is a flat connection,

(3)

\xi:\det(\mathcal{E})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{S}T}
is an equivalence.

This moduli stack
\mathcal{M}_{\dR}(X/S,n)
of vector bundles with flat connection (which also appears in Lam–Litt
[
12
]
) will be the underlying object of the non-abelian Gauss–Manin connection.

We asserted in the introduction that this moduli stack was also a certain mapping stack; we now check this assertion.

Lemma 5.2
.

The functor
\mathcal{M}_{\dR}(X/S,n)
coincides with the functor of points of the functor

\Map_{S}((X/S)^{\widehat{\dR}},B\SL_{n}):\Sch_{S}\to\Gpd

sending an
S
-scheme
T
to the groupoid of
\SL_{n}
-bundles on
(X/S)^{\widehat{\dR}}\times_{S}T.

Proof.

Fix
T
an
S
-scheme. By definition, a
T
-point of the mapping stack is just a map

(X/S)^{\widehat{\dR}}\times_{S}T\to B\SL_{n}.

But note (recalling the language of relative sheared de Rham stacks from
Remark 2.14
, and especially
(2.15)
)

\displaystyle(X\times_{S}T/T)^{\widehat{\dR}}

\displaystyle=(X\times_{S}T)^{\dR}\times_{T^{\dR}}T

\displaystyle=(X^{\dR}\times_{S}T^{\dR})\times_{T^{\dR}}T

\displaystyle=X^{\dR}\times_{S}T.

In particular, a map
(X/S)^{\widehat{\dR}}\times_{S}T\to B\SL_{n}
is the same as an
\SL_{n}
-bundle on
(X\times_{S}T/T)^{\widehat{\dR}}
; but
\SL_{n}
-bundles on this relative de Rham stack are just rank
n
bundles on
X\times_{S}T
with a flat connection relative to
T
and with an isomorphism
\det(\mathcal{E})\simeq\mathcal{O}
compatible with connections (because it is really an isomorphism in
\Qcoh((X/S)^{\widehat{\dR}})
), exactly coinciding with the functor of points of
\mathcal{M}_{\dR}(X/S,n),
as desired.
∎

Definition 5.3
.

The
non-abelian Gauss–Manin connection
is the non-abelian connection furnished by the Cartesian diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\dR}(X/S,n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{S^{\widehat{\dR}}}(X^{\widehat{\dR}},B\SL_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\widehat{\dR}}.}

Remark 5.4
.

Here, by
\Map_{S^{\widehat{\dR}}}(X^{\widehat{\dR}},B\SL_{n}),
we mean the prestack
\Alg_{k}\to\Gpd
where
\Spec(A)
is sent to the groupoid of pairs
(p,\mathcal{E})
where
p
is an object of the groupoid
S^{\widehat{\dR}}(A),
and
\mathcal{E}
is an
\SL_{n}
-bundle on the base change of
(X/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}
along
p:\Spec(A)\to S^{\widehat{\dR}}.

An isomorphism
(p,\mathcal{E})\simeq(p^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
in this groupoid is just the data of an isomorphism
\alpha:p\simeq p^{\prime}
in
S^{\widehat{\dR}}(A),
together with an isomorphism
\beta:\mathcal{E}\simeq\tilde{\alpha}^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}),
where
\tilde{\alpha}:p^{*}(X/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}\to(p^{\prime})^{*}(X/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}
is the isomorphism of stacks induced by
\alpha.

Remark 5.5
.

We will not use any fpqc descent properties of this mapping prestack, so we do not verify it satisfies any. It seems checking this would be a bit subtle, as
(X/k)^{\widehat{\dR}}
is in general not an algebraic stack, so existing results in the literature on descent properties of mapping prestacks do not seem to apply.

5.2.
The Hodge filtration

Definition 5.6
.

The
Hodge filtration
on the non-abelian Gauss–Manin connection is the Griffiths-transverse filtration (in the sense of
Definition 3.13
) furnished by the Cartesian diagram.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{S\times\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}((X/S)^{\widehat{\dR},+},B\SL_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{S^{\widehat{\dR},+}}(X^{\widehat{\dR},+},B\SL_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\widehat{\dR},+}}

Definition 5.7
.

We define the
Dolbeault stack
as the
S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}
-stack

\mathcal{M}_{\Dol}:=(S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})\times_{S\times(\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m})}\Map_{S\times\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}((X/S)^{\widehat{\dR},+},B\SL_{n})\simeq\Map_{S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}}((X/S)^{\Hodge},B\SL_{n}).

Remark 5.8
.

We can describe
\mathcal{M}_{\Dol}
a little more concretely. As
(X/S)^{\Hodge}
is a split gerbe over
X\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}
(by
Proposition 3.12
), a vector bundle on

is the same as a vector bundle on

– equivalently, a graded vector bundle

on

– together with a flat map

\theta:\mathcal{E}\to\Omega^{1}_{X/S}(+1)\otimes\mathcal{E},

where the
+1

\theta
sends
\mathcal{E}_{n}
to
\mathcal{E}_{n-1}.

Thus a point of
\mathcal{M}_{\Dol}
can be thought of as a triple
(\mathcal{E},\theta,\xi),
where
\mathcal{E},\theta
are as above, and
\xi

\det(\mathcal{E})\simeq\mathcal{O}
intertwining

with zero.

Applying
Construction 3.3
(our construction of non-abelian Higgs fields) to the Hodge filtration (

), we produce a morphism

\Theta_{X/S}:\mathbb{V}(\pi^{*}T_{S/k}(-1))\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{\Dol}/(S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})}.

of
\mathcal{M}_{\Dol}
-prestacks.

5.4.
Identifying the Hodge points

We are finally ready to state and prove the main theorem.

Theorem 5.12

(The non-abelian Katz’s formula)
.

There is a Cartesian diagram of stacks
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\Dol,c}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\Dol}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{c}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{\abs},[-1])}

and for

as defined in
(5.9)
and

\psi_{X/S}|_{\lambda=0}:\mathbb{V}(\pi_{c}^{*}F_{S/k}^{*}T_{S^{\prime}/k}(+1))\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{\Dol,c}/(S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})}

\varphi^{*}\Theta_{X/S}=\psi_{X/S}|_{\lambda=0}.

Proof.

Consider the Cartesian diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec(k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec(k)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Frob}

\displaystyle\simeq(\tau,[-1])^{*}B_{S\times\mathbb{G}_{m}}(T_{S/k}(-1))

\displaystyle\simeq(\tau,[-1])^{*}B_{X\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}}(T_{X/k}(-1))^{\#,\wedge}

we deduce from

and

that the base change, along

of the Cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{S^{\Hodge}}(X^{\Hodge},B\SL_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}}
