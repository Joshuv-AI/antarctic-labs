On 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -positive links
Benjamin Bode, Paula Truöl

Abstract
. 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -positive links form a subset of strongly quasipositive links that strictly contains the set of all non-split braid positive links. Analogous to Baader’s characterisation of positive links as precisely the strongly quasipositive and homogeneous links, we show that 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -positive links are precisely the strongly quasipositive links that are the closures of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -homogeneous braids. This complements previous characterizations of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -positive links by Rudolph and Banfield as links arising as boundaries of positive Hopf-plumbed baskets, or closures of staircase braids. We examine the behavior of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -positive links under cabling operations and connected sums, and demonstrate that all strongly quasipositive, fibered knots with at most 12 crossings are 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -positive. Additionally, we compare 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -positivity with other positivity notions for links and compile open questions.

1.1.
Staircase braids and cabling

\mathcal{T}
-positive links have previously been characterized by Rudolph
[
43
]
as those links that arise as the boundary of a positive Hopf-plumbed basket. That is, they have a Seifert surface that can be obtained from a disk
D
by iteratively plumbing positive Hopf bands, with each plumbing arc contained within the original disk
D

Section

2
, particularly
Theorem

2.5
in
Section

2.7.1
, for more details.
Banfield
[
6
]
provided yet another characterization of
\mathcal{T}
-positive links.
A strongly quasipositive braid in
B_{n}
is called a

if it contains a positive power of the dual Garside element

\delta=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\dots\sigma_{n-1}\in B_{n}.

Specifically, a staircase braid can be written as a product of positive BKL-generators containing the subword
\delta

\delta
in the dual Garside normal form of the braid (see
[
8
, Theorem 3.10]

Theorem 1.4

(
[
6
, Corollary 5.11]
)
.

A link is
\mathcal{T}
-positive if and only if it arises as the closure of a staircase braid.

In light of

\mathcal{T}
-positive knots. Given a knot

Let

Sufficient conditions like the one in

\mathcal{T}

\mathcal{T}

2.1.
BKL-generators of the braid group

The braid group
B_{n}
has a classical presentation in terms of

\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}
which is due to Artin
[
3
]
. For our purposes, a different presentation due to Birman–Ko–Lee
[
8
]
is more convenient. In this presentation, there are generators

for
1\leq i<j\leq n
, called

or

. The relations among them are

(2)

\displaystyle a_{i,j}a_{k,\ell}

\displaystyle=a_{k,\ell}a_{i,j}

\displaystyle\text{if }(i-k)(i-\ell)(j-k)(j-\ell)>0,

(3)

\displaystyle a_{i,j}a_{j,k}

\displaystyle=a_{i,k}a_{i,j}=a_{j,k}a_{i,k}

\displaystyle\text{for all }i,j,k\text{ with }1\leq i<j<k\leq n.

In terms of the Artin generators

, the BKL-generators can be expressed as

\displaystyle a_{i,j}=\left(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\cdots\sigma_{j-2}\right)\sigma_{j-1}\left(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\cdots\sigma_{j-2}\right)^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad a_{i,i+1}=\sigma_{i}.

Words in the BKL-generators represent geometric braids.
Figure

1
shows the geometric braid which corresponds to the BKL-generator

2.7.1.
Positive Hopf-plumbed baskets

A Seifert surface is called a
Hopf-plumbed basket
if it can be obtained from a disk
D
by iteratively plumbing Hopf bands such that each plumbing arc is contained in the initial disk
D
. It is a
positive Hopf-plumbed basket
if all involved Hopf bands are positive.
As a consequence of
Proposition

2.3
, Hopf-plumbed baskets, as special cases of iterative Hopf plumbings, are fiber surfaces and their boundaries are fibered links.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
43
]
)
.

A link is
\mathcal{T}
-positive if and only if arises as the boundary of a positive Hopf-plumbed basket. In particular,
\mathcal{T}
-positive links are fibered.

The various characterizations of
\mathcal{T}
-positive links can be summarized as follows:

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\{\text{strongly quasipositive links}\}\cap\{\mathcal{T}\text{-homogeneous links}\}\\

2.7.2.
\mathcal{T}
-positivity and positivity

Let us provide two examples of knots which are

-positive, but not positive. The mirrors of the knots

shows that

. Indeed, descriptions as positive Hopf-plumbed baskets for Seifert surfaces for

and

can be read off from

.

Positive knots are not necessarily

-positive either, as can easily be seen by considering positive knots that are not fibered. Examples of such knots are plenty

. In this context, we would like to highlight the following interesting open problem.

Question 2.6

(
1.8
)
.

Are there positive, fibered knots which are not
\mathcal{T}
-positive? Equivalently, are there positive, fibered knots which do not arise as the boundary of a positive Hopf-plumbed basket?

Positive, fibered knots arise as the boundaries of positive Hopf plumbings. This follows from work of Cromwell
[
11
, Theorem 5 and its corollaries]
; see also
[
25
, Figure 2]
. There are examples of knots that are the boundary of a positive Hopf plumbing but not of a positive Hopf-plumbed basket; that is, they are not
\mathcal{T}
-positive. Indeed, there are infinitely many such knots, as we shall explain now. Recall that the
genus
of a knot

,

, is the minimal genus of any Seifert surface for

. For every

, Misev
[
31
]
constructed infinitely many knots that arise as the boundary of a positive Hopf plumbing of genus

. In contrast, the number of Hopf-plumbed baskets of a given genus is finite, which implies the claim.
However,
2.6
asks for something stronger to be true. In
Corollary

5.3
in
Section

5
, we show that there are no positive, fibered knots with at most 12 crossings which provide counterexamples to

, since they are all
\mathcal{T}
-positive.

We have seen in

2.7.4.

\mathcal{T}
-positive knots are strongly quasipositive and fibered, by definition and e. g. by

Staircase braids and cabling

In this section, we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1

(
Proposition

1.5
)
.

Let

and let
K
be a knot which is the closure of a staircase braid
B=\delta P\in B_{n}
for some BKL-positive word

. If

, then the cable knot
K_{p,q}
is represented by a staircase braid.

Remark 4.2
.

It is well known that, given a strongly quasipositive, fibered knot
K
, the cable knot
K_{p,q}
is strongly quasipositive whenever
q\geq 1

[
22
]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, explicit strongly quasipositive braid representatives for

have not yet been documented in the literature. The proof below provides an algorithm for obtaining these representatives.

Proof of
Proposition

4.1
.

Throughout this proof, let
p\geq 2
. Suppose that
B=\delta P\in B_{n}
is a staircase braid representative for
K
where

is a BKL-positive word. Recall that

\delta_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\delta=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdots\sigma_{n-1}\in B_{n}

is the dual Garside element. For every

, we claim that there is a staircase braid
B_{p,q}
on
pn
strands with closure

, i. e.
B_{p,q}
is a BKL-positive word containing
\delta_{pn}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdots\sigma_{pn-1}
.

Given a diagram
D
for
K
, the
standard diagram

for

is obtained by taking
p
parallel (blackboard) copies of each strand of

and adding
pw-q
negative
(1/p)
-twists to the
p
parallel strands, where

is the writhe of

. See e. g.
[
26
, Figure 7]
for an example. When applied to a braid diagram
D
on
n
strands, this procedure gives rise to a braid diagram
D_{p,q}
on
pn
strands representing
K_{p,q}
.

Starting from the braid diagram
D
on
n
strands associated to the staircase braid word

, we first claim that the standard braid diagram

is BKL-positive. Indeed, after a braid isotopy, each BKL-generator in

translates to a product of
p
many BKL-generators in the
(p,0)
-cable
D_{p,0}
. More precisely, the BKL-generator

becomes

(7)

\displaystyle a_{pi,pj}a_{pi-1,pj-1}\cdots a_{p(i-1)+1,p(j-1)+1}=\prod_{k=0}^{p-1}a_{pi-k,pj-k}.

Figure

6.
Further observations on
\mathcal{T}
-positivity

In this section, we explore the relationship between
\mathcal{T}
-positivity and the unknotting number, the braid index, connected sums, visual primeness and positive trefoil plumbings.

6.1.
\mathcal{T}
-positivity and unknotting number

The
unknotting number

u(K)
of a knot
K
is the minimal number of crossing changes needed to transform a diagram of
K
into a diagram of the unknot. The equality
u(K)=g(K)
is known to hold true for braid positive knots
K
and more generally, for positive trefoil plumbings
K
; see
[
37
,
4
,
25
]
. Moreover, Stoimenow
[
47
, Conjecture 4.1]
conjectured that
u(K)=g(K)
is true for positive, fibered knots
K
(see also
[
32
, Conjecture 5]
). The authors of
[
25
]
presented infinitely many potential counterexamples
\mathcal{K}_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, to this conjecture. In light of the following example, it seems worth noting that all of them are
\mathcal{T}
-positive knots, as explained in
Section

2.7.3
.

Example 6.1
.

The
\mathcal{T}
-positive knot
K=m(12n_{642})
fulfills
u(K)\neq g(K)
, since it has
u(K)\geq 3
, but
g(K)\leq 2
; see also
[
27
]
. In fact, from the knot diagram of
K
drawn in
Figure

5
(right), it is easy to find a Seifert surface of genus
2
for
K
which is a positive Hopf-plumbed basket. So
K
is
\mathcal{T}
-positive by
Theorem

2.5
and
g(K)=2
(see
Section

2.5
). On the other hand, a Seifert matrix computation shows that the first homology of the double branched cover of
S^{3}
branched along
K
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{3}
has dimension
3
as an
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-vectorspace, so
3\leq u(K)

[
50
]
.

6.2.
\mathcal{T}
-positivity and braid index

1.8
asks whether there are positive, fibered knots which are not
\mathcal{T}
-positive. In
Section

5
, we have seen that there are no such knots with low crossing numbers. Another frequently used measure of complexity of knots is the
braid index
, that is, the minimal number of strands of a braid representing a given knot. We now briefly examine the relation between
\mathcal{T}
-positivity and the braid index.

Proposition 6.2

(
[
48
]
)
.

If
K
is a strongly quasipositive, fibered knot that arises as the closure of a braid on three strands, then
K
has a staircase braid representative on three strands, so it is
\mathcal{T}
-positive.

Proof.

By Stoimenow
[
48
, Theorem 1.1]
, there is a strongly quasipositive
3
-braid that represents
K
. Since
K
is fibered, from
[
48
, Theorem 3.3,
(2)\Rightarrow(5)
]
it follows that
K
has a
3
-braid representative which contains
\sigma_{1}^{k}\sigma_{2}^{\ell}\sigma_{1}^{m}
or
\sigma_{2}^{k}\sigma_{1}^{\ell}\sigma_{2}^{m}
as subword for
k,\ell,m>0
, or is
\sigma_{1}^{k}\sigma_{2}^{\ell}
. Thus
K
has a staircase representative containing
\delta=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}
on three strands and is therefore
\mathcal{T}
-positive by
Theorem

1.4
.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

No knots with braid index
2
or
3
provide counterexamples to
1.8
.

Proof.

If the closure
K
of a braid on three strands is positive and fibered, it is strongly quasipositive
[
42
,
33
]
and fibered. By
Proposition

6.2
,
K
is then also
\mathcal{T}
-positive.
∎

Proposition

6.2
implies that, if a knot
K
is
\mathcal{T}
-positive and of braid index
3
, then it is the closure of a staircase braid on three strands. However, a
\mathcal{T}
-positive knot with braid index
3
is not necessarily the closure of a
T
-positive braid on three strands for some espalier
T
, as the following example shows.

Example 6.4
.

The knot
K=10_{161}
has braid index

and
a_{1}^{2}a_{1,3}a_{2}a_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{2}
is a staircase braid representative on three strands. However, if

had a

-positive braid representative for some espalier

with three vertices, then up to conjugating this braid, it would be a positive

-braid representative for

. So

would be braid positive, which it is not
[

]
.

6.3.
\mathcal{T}
-positivity and connected sum

We record the following relations of
\mathcal{T}
-positivity and
\mathcal{T}
-homogeneity to the operation of connected sum of knots.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
be espaliers.
Let
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
be knots that are closures of a
T_{1}
-homogeneous braid and a
T_{2}
-homogeneous braid, respectively. Then the connected sum
K_{1}\#K_{2}
is the closure of a
T_{1}*T_{2}
-homogeneous braid, where
T_{1}\ast T_{2}
is the espalier given by the vertex-connected sum of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
along the right-most vertex of
T_{1}
and the left-most vertex of
T_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
K_{1}
is the closure of a
T_{1}
-homogeneous braid, it is the closure of a braid that is represented by a BKL-homogeneous word, say
A
, that only consists of
T_{1}
-generators. Likewise,
K_{2}
is the closure of a braid that is represented by a BKL-homogeneous word, say
B
, that only consist of
T_{2}
-generators.
Let
n_{1}
be the number of vertices of
T_{1}
and let
n_{2}
be the number of vertices of
T_{2}
. Equivalently,
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
are the number of strands of
A
and
B
, respectively. We define
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}:B_{n_{1}}\to B_{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}
to be the inclusion homomorphism that sends a BKL-generator
a_{i,j}
,
1\leq i<j\leq n_{1}
to
a_{i,j}
(interpreted as a generator of
B_{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}
). Similarly, we define
j_{n_{1},n_{2}}:B_{n_{2}}\to B_{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}
to be the injective homomorphism that sends
a_{i,j}
to
a_{i+n_{1}-1,j+n_{1}-1}
for all
1\leq i<j\leq n_{2}
.
Then the closure of
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}(A)j_{n_{1},n_{2}}(B)
is the connected sum
K_{1}\#K_{2}
because
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}(A)
only contains generators of the form
a_{i,j}^{\varepsilon}
with
1\leq i<j\leq n_{1}
,
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
, and
j_{n_{1},n_{2}}(B)
only contains generators of the form
a_{i,j}^{\varepsilon}
with
n_{1}\leq i<j\leq n_{1}+n_{2}-1
,
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
.

Observe that
T_{1}*T_{2}
is again an espalier. Let
\mathcal{G}_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=G(T_{1})
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=G(T_{2})
denote the set of
T_{1}
-generators and
T_{2}
-generators, respectively. Then the set of
T_{1}*T_{2}
-generators is precisely
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}(\mathcal{G}_{1})\cup j_{n_{1},n_{2}}(\mathcal{G}_{2})
.

The word
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}(A)j_{n_{1},n_{2}}(B)
only contains
T_{1}*T_{2}
-generators (or their inverses). The homomorphisms
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}
and
j_{n_{1},n_{2}}
map BKL-generators with positive signs to BKL-generators with positive signs and BKL-generators with negative signs to BKL-generators with negative signs. Since
A
and
B
are
T_{1}
-homogeneous and
T_{2}
-homogeneous, respectively, for every generator
a\in\mathcal{G}_{1}
there is a fixed sign
\varepsilon_{a}\in\{\pm 1\}
so that all instances of
a
in
A
carry the sign
\varepsilon_{a}
. Likewise, for every generator
b\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
there is a fixed sign
\varepsilon_{b}\in\{\pm 1\}
so that all instances of
b
in
B
carry the sign
\varepsilon_{b}
. It follows that all instances of
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}(a)
in
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}(A)j_{n_{1},n_{2}}(B)
carry the same sign
\varepsilon_{a}
. Likewise, every instance of
j_{n_{1},n_{2}}(b)
in
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}(A)j_{n_{1},n_{2}}(B)
carries the same sign
\varepsilon_{b}
. Since the set of
T_{1}*T_{2}
-generators is exactly the set of images of
T_{1}
-generators and
T_{2}
-generators (under
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}
and
j_{n_{1},n_{2}}
, respectively), for every
T_{1}*T_{2}
-generator all of its instances in
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}(A)j_{n_{1},n_{2}}(B)
carry the same sign. Since
K_{1}\#K_{2}
is not a split link, it follows that
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}(A)j_{n_{1},n_{2}}(B)
is a
T_{1}*T_{2}
-homogeneous braid.
∎

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
be espaliers.
Let
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
be knots that are closures of a
T_{1}
-positive braid and a
T_{2}
-positive braid, respectively. Then the connected sum
K_{1}\#K_{2}
is the closure of a
T_{1}\ast T_{2}
-positive braid.

Proof.

If in the proof of the previous proposition all signs in
A
and
B
are positive, then so are all the signs in
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}(A)j_{n_{1},n_{2}}(B)
. In particular, it is a
T_{1}*T_{2}
-positive braid word.
∎

The proof of Proposition
6.5
discusses the connected sum operation on knots as an operation on braid words. On the level of the corresponding braided surfaces this is a Murasugi sum, where the disk corresponding to the last strand of
B_{1}
is identified with the disk corresponding to the first strand of
B_{2}
via a certain homeomorphism. Using different homeomorphisms to glue these same two disks produces different Murasugi sums, which were called
braided Stallings plumbings
by Rudolph
[
41
]
. This operation was initially used by Stallings to prove that closures of homogeneous braids are fibered
[
46
]
. See also
[
10
]
for a discussion of braided Stallings plumbings in the context of braided open books.

If
A
and
B
are two BKL-words on
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
strands, respectively, then a braided Stallings plumbing of
A
and
B
contains the same letters as
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}(A)j_{n_{1},n_{2}}(B)
, only possibly in a different order. See the proof of Proposition
6.5
for the definition of
i_{n_{1},n_{2}}
and
j_{n_{1},n_{2}}
. In particular, the same argument as in the proof of Proposition
6.5
proves the result for any braided Stallings plumbing (not only the connected sum) of any two
\mathcal{T}
-positive/
\mathcal{T}
-homogeneous braids (or, to be more precise, for any braided Stallings plumbing of any two braided surfaces that are represented by
\mathcal{T}
-positive/
\mathcal{T}
-homogeneous braid words).

However, it is not true that every Murasugi sum preserves the property of being
\mathcal{T}
-positive, as can be seen from the fact that there exist infinitely many positive Hopf plumbings (Murasugi sums of the
\mathcal{T}
-positive Hopf link) that are not positive Hopf-plumbed baskets
[
31
]
and therefore not
\mathcal{T}
-positive (see also
Section

2.7.2
).

6.4.
\mathcal{T}
-positivity and visual primeness

Having proved that the connected sum of
\mathcal{T}
-positive links is again
\mathcal{T}
-positive, we now turn to the question whether it is trivial to detect from a given
\mathcal{T}
-positive braid diagram if its closure is a connected sum.

Let
L
be a link in
S^{3}
. We say that an embedded sphere
S\subset S^{3}
is a
decomposition sphere
for
L
if it intersects
L
in exactly two points and it divides
L
into two non-trivial parts in the following sense. The sphere
S
divides
S^{3}
into two balls
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
glued along their common boundary
S
. We write
\alpha=L\cap B_{1}
and
\beta=L\cap B_{2}
, both of which are simple arcs with exactly two distinct endpoints on
S
. Connecting these endpoints with any simple embedded arc
\gamma
on
S
thus produces two links
L_{1}=\alpha\cup\gamma
and
L_{2}=\beta\cup\gamma
. Since
S
is simply connected, the isotopy types of
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
do not depend on the chosen
\gamma
. In order for
S
to be a decomposition sphere we require neither
L_{1}
nor
L_{2}
to be an unknot.

If
L
admits a decomposition sphere, we say that it is
composite
. Otherwise, it is
prime
.

In general, it can be difficult to see from a given link diagram
D
if it represents a prime link or a composite link. However, if it is possible to draw in the diagram plane an embedded circle
C
that intersects
D
in exactly two points and none of the two resulting pieces of
D
(one on the inside of
C
and one on the outside of
C
) turns into a diagram of the unknot by joining its endpoints via an arc of
C
, then
D
represents a composite link. We can interpret
C
as the intersection of a decomposition sphere with the diagram plane. We call
C
a
decomposition circle
for
D
.

Diagrams with decomposition circles can thus be thought of as those link diagrams for which it is obvious that they represent composite links.

Definition 6.7
.

A link diagram
D
of a link
L
is said to be
visually prime
if it satisfies the following property. If
L
is not prime, then there exists a decomposition circle for
D
, which gives rise to a decomposition sphere for
L
.

The study of link diagrams from which (lack of) primeness is easily detectable in this sense goes back to seminal work by Menasco
[
29
]
and Cromwell
[
12
]
, who showed, respectively, that alternating diagrams and positive braid diagrams are visually prime. The latter result was generalized by Ozawa
[
34
]
to include all positive link diagrams. More recently, Feller, Lewark and Orbegozo Rodriguez proved that all homogeneous braid diagrams are visually prime
[
16
]
.

Since
\mathcal{T}
-positive braids are a generalization of positive braids (and, likewise,
\mathcal{T}
-homogeneous braids of homogeneous braids), it is natural to ask if
\mathcal{T}
-positive braid diagrams are also visually prime. We prove that this is not the case by providing a counterexample.

The following is a quick test to see if a diagram permits a decomposition circle. We associate to a diagram
D
a planar graph as follows. First, forget about the signs of the crossings, so that
D
becomes a planar graph with as many
4
-valent vertices as crossings of
D
and edges corresponding to the arcs of
D
between two crossings. Let
\Gamma
be the dual graph of this graph, i. e. the vertices of
\Gamma
correspond to the regions of the diagram
D
(i. e. connected components of
\mathbb{R}\backslash D
), and there is an edge between two vertices of
\Gamma
whenever the corresponding regions of
D
share an edge in the graph obtained from
D
. We can view
\Gamma
as a metric graph by assigning to every edge the length 1. If there exists a decomposition circle for
D
, then there exists a non-trivial loop of length 2 in
\Gamma
.

Proposition 6.8
.

The braid diagram of the
\mathcal{T}
-positive braid
a_{1,2}a_{2,3}a_{1,2}a_{2,3}a_{2,4}a_{2,4}a_{2,4}
is not visually prime.

Proof.

Although the closure of the braid is the connected sum of two positive trefoils, the closed braid diagram fails the above test. The corresponding graph
\Gamma
, shown in
Figure

11
, does not have any non-trivial loops of length two. In the figure, the vertices of the graph
\Gamma
on the right are labelled as the regions of the diagram
D
on the left. Since there are no non-trivial loops of length two in
\Gamma
, the diagram
D
does not have a decomposition circle and is therefore not visually prime.
∎

-positive knot which is not a positive trefoil plumbing.

Question 6.9
.

Are there infinitely many knots which are
\mathcal{T}
-positive, but not positive trefoil plumbings?

On the other hand, the following proposition follows almost immediately from
[
25
]
.

Proposition 6.10
.

There exist infinitely many knots that are positive trefoil plumbings, but not
\mathcal{T}
-positive.

Proof.

[
25
]
. This gives rise to infinitely many knots of genus two that are positive trefoil plumbings. Since there are only finitely many positive Hopf-plumbed baskets of a given genus, this shows the claim.
∎

2.7.2

-positive.
