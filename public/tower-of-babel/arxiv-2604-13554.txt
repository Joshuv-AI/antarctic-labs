1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

The oracle identification problem asks: given black-box access to a
unitary representation
\rho(g)
of a finite group
G
, how many
quantum queries suffice to determine the group element
g
?
Copeland and Pommersheim
[
3
]
introduced a
character-theoretic framework for this problem and determined the
query complexity for the symmetric group
S_{N}
, building on earlier
quantum oracle models
[
1
,
10
]
and the
adversary method
[
2
,
6
,
9
]
.
The representation theory of wreath products, of which the
hyperoctahedral group
B_{N}=\{\pm 1\}^{N}\rtimes S_{N}
is the simplest
nonabelian case, is classical
[
7
,
4
,
8
]
.
We extend the Copeland–Pommersheim framework to
B_{N}
.

1.2.
The hyperoctahedral group

The
hyperoctahedral group

B_{N}=\{\pm 1\}^{N}\rtimes S_{N}

is the group of signed permutations on
N
letters. It is the
Weyl group of type
B_{N}
(equivalently, type
C_{N}
) and acts as the
symmetry group of the
N
-dimensional hyperoctahedron
(cross-polytope).
Elements are pairs
(\sigma,\varepsilon)
with
\sigma\in S_{N}

and
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{N})\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}
,
and the group has order
\lvert B_{N}\rvert=2^{N}\cdot N!
.
Its natural representation

V=V_{((N-1),(1))}
acts faithfully on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
by

\rho(\sigma,\varepsilon)\lvert x\rangle=\varepsilon_{x}\lvert\sigma(x)\rangle
.
The oracle
U=P_{\sigma}D_{\varepsilon}
factors into a permutation
matrix
P_{\sigma}
and a diagonal sign matrix

D_{\varepsilon}=\diag(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{N})
, giving rise
to two distinct oracle models.

1.3.
Main contributions

Our three main results are as follows.

(I)

Factor-
2
simulation bound (Theorem
3.6
).

For every decision problem
\varphi\colon B_{N}/\{\pm I\}\to\{0,1\}
,

Q_{\mathrm{decomp}}(\varphi)\leq 2\,Q_{\mathrm{signed}}(\varphi).

The bound is tight: for the modified negative-fixed-point problem

\widetilde{\varphi}
on
B_{2}
we exhibit

Q_{\mathrm{signed}}(\widetilde{\varphi})=1
and

Q_{\mathrm{decomp}}(\widetilde{\varphi})=2
.

(II)

Oracle identification formula
(Theorem
5.9
).

For
N\geq 2
,

Q_{LV}(B_{N})=2(N-1),

with the sign-of-permutation representation

\sgn(\sigma)=V_{((1^{N}),\varnothing)}
as the unique bottleneck
for
N\geq 3
(and one of four co-bottlenecks for
N=2
).

(III)

\gamma_{2}
-character bridge conjecture
(Conjecture
5.14
).

The adversary bound equals the eccentricity of
I(V)
in the tensor
product graph:

\gamma_{\mathrm{adv}}=\ecc_{T(B_{N},V)}(I(V))=2N-3
.

1.4.
Context

The Copeland–Pommersheim framework
[
3
]

shows that nonadaptive algorithms are optimal for oracle
identification with group representations and expresses the optimal
success probability via the cumulative spectral reach.
Passing from
S_{N}
to
B_{N}
introduces new representation-theoretic
phenomena — notably the
\varepsilon
-parity obstruction and the
resulting double-step structure — which are the focus of this
paper.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The hyperoctahedral group
B_{N}

Definition 2.1
.

The
hyperoctahedral group

B_{N}=\{\pm 1\}^{N}\rtimes S_{N}

consists of pairs
(\sigma,\varepsilon)
with
\sigma\in S_{N}
and

\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{N})\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}
.
The group law is

(1)

(\sigma,\varepsilon)\cdot(\tau,\delta)=\bigl(\sigma\tau,\;(\varepsilon_{\tau(1)}\delta_{1},\;\varepsilon_{\tau(2)}\delta_{2},\;\dots,\;\varepsilon_{\tau(N)}\delta_{N})\bigr).

The group has order
\lvert B_{N}\rvert=2^{N}\cdot N!
.

2.2.
Irreducible representations

Definition 2.2
.

The irreducible representations of
B_{N}
are indexed by

bipartitions

(\alpha,\beta)\vdash N
, i.e., pairs of
partitions
\alpha,\beta
with
\lvert\alpha\rvert+\lvert\beta\rvert=N
.
We write
V_{(\alpha,\beta)}
for the corresponding irreducible
representation.

2.3.
The natural representation

Definition 2.3
.

The
natural representation

V=V_{((N-1),(1))}
of
B_{N}
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
by

(2)

\rho(\sigma,\varepsilon)\lvert x\rangle=\varepsilon_{x}\,\lvert\sigma(x)\rangle,\qquad x\in\{1,\dots,N\}.

In matrix form,
\rho(\sigma,\varepsilon)=P_{\sigma}D_{\varepsilon}
,
where
P_{\sigma}
is the
N\times N
permutation matrix of
\sigma

and
D_{\varepsilon}=\diag(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{N})
is the
diagonal sign matrix.

Proposition 2.4

(Character formula)
.

The character of the natural representation is

(3)

\chi_{V}(\sigma,\varepsilon)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\colon\sigma(x)=x\end{subarray}}\varepsilon_{x}.

Proof.

The diagonal entries of
P_{\sigma}D_{\varepsilon}
are

(P_{\sigma}D_{\varepsilon})_{xx}=\delta_{\sigma(x),x}\,\varepsilon_{x}
.
Summing over
x
gives (
3
).
∎

Proposition 2.5

(Faithfulness)
.

The natural representation
\rho
is faithful:

\ker(\rho)=\{e\}
.

Proof.

If
P_{\sigma}D_{\varepsilon}=I_{N}
, then for every
x
,

\varepsilon_{x}\lvert\sigma(x)\rangle=\lvert x\rangle
.
This forces
\sigma(x)=x
for all
x
and
\varepsilon_{x}=+1
for
all
x
, hence
(\sigma,\varepsilon)
is the identity element.
∎

2.4.
Oracle models

Definition 2.6

(Signed oracle model)
.

The algorithm has black-box access to the unitary

U=P_{\sigma}D_{\varepsilon}
acting on the query register
\mathbb{C}^{N}
.
Each call to
U
(or
U^{\dagger}
) counts as one query.
Between queries, the algorithm may apply arbitrary unitaries on the
query register tensored with an ancilla register of arbitrary
dimension. We write
Q_{\mathrm{signed}}(\varphi)
for the minimum number of
queries needed to compute a decision problem
\varphi
with certainty.

Definition 2.7

(Decomposed oracle model)
.

The algorithm has separate black-box access to
P_{\sigma}
and

D_{\varepsilon}
, each acting on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Each call to either oracle
counts as one query.
The adjoint oracles
P_{\sigma}^{\dagger}=P_{\sigma^{-1}}
and

D_{\varepsilon}^{\dagger}=D_{\varepsilon}
are also available at unit
cost.
The total query count is
t_{P}+t_{D}
, and the
algorithm may choose classically which oracle to call at each step.
We write
Q_{\mathrm{decomp}}(\varphi)
for the minimum total queries needed.

2.5.
The Copeland–Pommersheim framework

Definition 2.8

(Oracle identification)
.

Given a group
G
and a faithful unitary representation

\rho\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})
, the
oracle identification
problem
asks to determine
g\in G
from queries to the oracle

\rho(g)
.

Definition 2.9
.

For a representation
\rho
and integer
t\geq 1
, let

I(\rho^{\otimes t})
denote the set of irreducible representations
appearing as constituents of
\rho^{\otimes t}
. The

cumulative spectral reach
is

R_{\leq t}(\rho)=\bigcup_{s=1}^{t}I(\rho^{\otimes s})
.

Theorem 2.10

(Copeland–Pommersheim)
.

The optimal success probability after
t
queries to
\rho(g)
is

(4)

P_{\mathrm{opt}}^{(t)}=\frac{1}{\lvert G\rvert}\sum_{\pi\in R_{\leq t}(\rho)}(\dim\pi)^{2},

and nonadaptive algorithms are optimal.

Definition 2.11

(Tensor product graph)
.

Assume
\rho\cong\rho^{\ast}
(i.e.
\rho
is self-dual).
The
tensor product graph

T(G,\rho)
has vertex set

\widehat{G}
(the set of all irreducible representations of
G
)
and an edge
\pi\sim\tau
whenever

\tau\in I(\rho\otimes\pi)
.
(Self-duality ensures symmetry:
\tau\in I(\rho\otimes\pi)
iff

\pi\in I(\rho\otimes\tau)
, so the graph is undirected.)

Remark 2.12
.

The natural representation
V=V_{((N-1),(1))}
of
B_{N}
is real
(its character is real-valued), hence self-dual. All later
applications of
T(G,\rho)
use
\rho=V
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Under the self-duality hypothesis of
Definition
2.11
, the query complexity
satisfies

(5)

Q_{LV}(G,\rho)=\ecc_{T(G,\rho)}\bigl(I(\rho)\bigr)+1,

where

\ecc(S)=\max_{\pi\in\widehat{G}}\,d_{T}(\pi,S)
is the
eccentricity of the set
I(\rho)
in the tensor product graph.

Proof.

The cumulative reach
R_{\leq t}
captures all irreducible
representations at graph distance
\leq t-1
from
I(\rho)
: the
first tensor power gives
I(\rho)
(distance
0
), the second adds
neighbours (distance
1
), and so on. Full identification requires

R_{\leq t}=\widehat{G}
, which occurs at
t=\ecc(I(\rho))+1
.
∎

3.
Oracle Models and the Factor-
2

Separation

3.1.
The global phase obstruction

Proposition 3.1

(Global phase indistinguishability)
.

For any quantum algorithm making
t
queries to the oracle

U_{(\sigma,\varepsilon)}
, the output distribution is identical on
input
(\sigma,\varepsilon)
and
(\sigma,-\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

Let the algorithm produce the final state

\lvert\psi_{\mathrm{final}}\rangle=V_{t}(U\otimes I_{A})\,V_{t-1}\cdots V_{1}(U\otimes I_{A})\,V_{0}\lvert\psi_{0}\rangle,

where
V_{0},\dots,V_{t}
are query-independent unitaries and

U=P_{\sigma}D_{\varepsilon}
acts on the query register.
Since
\rho(\sigma,-\varepsilon)=-\rho(\sigma,\varepsilon)
,
replacing
U
by
-U
multiplies each oracle application by
-1
,
yielding
(-1)^{t}\lvert\psi_{\mathrm{final}}\rangle
.
The same holds for
U^{\dagger}
queries: since

(-U)^{\dagger}=-U^{\dagger}
, each adjoint query also acquires a
factor of
-1
.
This global phase does not affect measurement probabilities.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Any decision problem

\varphi\colon B_{N}\to\{0,1\}
computable from the signed oracle must
satisfy

\varphi(\sigma,\varepsilon)=\varphi(\sigma,-\varepsilon)

for all
(\sigma,\varepsilon)\in B_{N}
, i.e.,

\varphi
is well-defined on the quotient
B_{N}/\{\pm I\}
.

3.2.
The modified negative-fixed-point problem

Definition 3.3

(Modified NFP)
.

The
modified negative-fixed-point
function

\widetilde{\varphi}\colon B_{N}\to\{0,1\}
is

\widetilde{\varphi}(\sigma,\varepsilon)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\sigma=\mathrm{id}\text{ and }\varepsilon\notin\{(+1,\dots,+1),(-1,\dots,-1)\},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proposition 3.4

(Well-definedness)
.

The function
\widetilde{\varphi}
is constant on global phase
equivalence classes

\{(\sigma,\varepsilon),(\sigma,-\varepsilon)\}
for all
N\geq 2
.

Proof.

If
\sigma\neq\mathrm{id}
, then both elements map to
0
. If

\sigma=\mathrm{id}
and
\varepsilon=(+1,\dots,+1)
, then

-\varepsilon=(-1,\dots,-1)
; both are “all-equal” and map to
0
.
If
\sigma=\mathrm{id}
and
\varepsilon
has mixed signs, then

-\varepsilon
also has mixed signs (since
N\geq 2
), and both map
to
1
.
∎

Remark 3.5

(Why not parity)
.

An alternative using parity of the number of negative signs
fails for odd
N
: if
\varepsilon
has
k
negatives, then

-\varepsilon
has
N-k
negatives, and
k+(N-k)=N
is odd, so

k
and
N-k
have different parities.
The “mixed signs” definition works uniformly for all
N\geq 2
.

3.3.
The factor-
2
simulation theorem

Theorem 3.6

(Factor-
2
simulation)
.

For any decision problem

\varphi\colon B_{N}/\{\pm I\}\to\{0,1\}
,

(6)

Q_{\mathrm{decomp}}(\varphi)\leq 2\,Q_{\mathrm{signed}}(\varphi).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
t
-query signed oracle algorithm producing

\lvert\psi_{\mathrm{final}}\rangle=V_{t}\,(U\otimes I_{A})\,V_{t-1}\cdots V_{1}\,(U\otimes I_{A})\,V_{0}\,\lvert\psi_{0}\rangle.

Replace each occurrence of
U\otimes I_{A}
by the product

(P_{\sigma}\otimes I_{A})(D_{\varepsilon}\otimes I_{A})
.
Since
P_{\sigma}D_{\varepsilon}=U
exactly, the resulting state

\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{\mathrm{final}}\rangle=V_{t}\,(P_{\sigma}\otimes I_{A})(D_{\varepsilon}\otimes I_{A})\,V_{t-1}\cdots V_{1}\,(P_{\sigma}\otimes I_{A})(D_{\varepsilon}\otimes I_{A})\,V_{0}\,\lvert\psi_{0}\rangle

equals
\lvert\psi_{\mathrm{final}}\rangle
. The simulation is exact.
Each of the
t
signed-oracle calls is replaced by one call to

D_{\varepsilon}
and one call to
P_{\sigma}
, for a total of
2t

decomposed queries.
For
U^{\dagger}=D_{\varepsilon}P_{\sigma^{-1}}
queries, replace
each
U^{\dagger}\otimes I_{A}
by

(D_{\varepsilon}\otimes I_{A})(P_{\sigma^{-1}}\otimes I_{A})
, again
costing
2
decomposed queries.
∎

Corollary 3.7

(No super-constant separation)
.

No decision problem on
B_{N}
admits
Q_{\mathrm{signed}}=O(1)
and

Q_{\mathrm{decomp}}=\omega(1)
. In particular, no polynomial separation is
possible between the two models.

Proof.

If
Q_{\mathrm{signed}}(\varphi)\leq C
for a constant
C
, then

Q_{\mathrm{decomp}}(\varphi)\leq 2C=O(1)
by Theorem
3.6
.
∎

Remark 3.8

(Asymmetry)
.

The reverse simulation — using the signed oracle to simulate
individual decomposed queries — is not straightforward. Simulating
a query to
D_{\varepsilon}
alone requires computing

D_{\varepsilon}=UP_{\sigma}^{-1}
, which needs the inverse permutation
oracle. Whether a general bound

Q_{\mathrm{signed}}\leq C\cdot Q_{\mathrm{decomp}}
exists remains open.

3.5.
Tightness for
B_{2}
:
decomposed oracle lower bound

Theorem 3.11

(Decomposed oracle lower bound for
B_{2}
)
.

Q_{\mathrm{decomp}}(\widetilde{\varphi})\geq 2
.

Proof.

A
1
-query decomposed algorithm queries either
P_{\sigma}
or

D_{\varepsilon}
(not both).

Case 1: one query to
P_{\sigma}
.

The elements

g_{1}=(\mathrm{id},+,+)
and
g_{2}=(\mathrm{id},+,-)

share
P_{\mathrm{id}}=I
, making them indistinguishable.
Yet
\widetilde{\varphi}(g_{1})=0\neq 1=\widetilde{\varphi}(g_{2})
.

Case 2: one query to
D_{\varepsilon}
.

The elements
g_{2}=(\mathrm{id},+,-)
and

g_{6}=((12),+,-)
share

D_{(+,-)}=\diag(1,-1)
, making them indistinguishable.
Yet
\widetilde{\varphi}(g_{2})=1\neq 0=\widetilde{\varphi}(g_{6})
.

In both cases the algorithm faces a pair of inputs with identical
oracle operators but differing required outputs.
Therefore
Q_{\mathrm{decomp}}(\widetilde{\varphi})\geq 2
.
∎

3.6.
Tightness for
B_{2}
:
decomposed oracle upper bound

Theorem 3.12

(Decomposed oracle complexity of
\widetilde{\varphi}

on
B_{2}
)
.

Q_{\mathrm{decomp}}(\widetilde{\varphi})=2
.

Proof.

It suffices to exhibit a
2
-query decomposed algorithm, since the
lower bound
Q_{\mathrm{decomp}}\geq 2
is given by
Theorem
3.11
.

Algorithm.

(1)

Query
D_{\varepsilon}
:

Prepare
\lvert+\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\lvert 0\rangle+\lvert 1\rangle)
.
Apply
D_{\varepsilon}
. Apply the Hadamard gate
H
.
Measure in the computational basis; let
b_{1}
be the outcome.

(2)

Query
P_{\sigma}
:

Prepare
\lvert 1\rangle
(the first coordinate basis state).
Apply
P_{\sigma}
.
Measure in the coordinate basis
\{\lvert 1\rangle,\lvert 2\rangle\}
;
set
b_{2}=0
for outcome
\lvert 1\rangle
and
b_{2}=1
for outcome

\lvert 2\rangle
.

(3)

Output:

\widetilde{\varphi}=b_{1}\wedge\overline{b_{2}}
.

Analysis of Query 1.

D_{\varepsilon}\lvert+\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\varepsilon_{1}\lvert 0\rangle+\varepsilon_{2}\lvert 1\rangle)
.
After the Hadamard:

•

\varepsilon=(+,+)
:

D_{\varepsilon}\lvert+\rangle=\lvert+\rangle
,

H\lvert+\rangle=\lvert 0\rangle
, so
b_{1}=0
.

•

\varepsilon=(-,-)
:

D_{\varepsilon}\lvert+\rangle=-\lvert+\rangle
,

, so
b_{1}=0
.

•

\varepsilon=(+,-)
:

D_{\varepsilon}\lvert+\rangle=\lvert-\rangle
,

H\lvert-\rangle=\lvert 1\rangle
, so
b_{1}=1
.

•

\varepsilon=(-,+)
:

D_{\varepsilon}\lvert+\rangle=-\lvert-\rangle
,

H(-\lvert-\rangle)=-\lvert 1\rangle
, so

.

Thus

if and only if
\varepsilon
has mixed signs.

Analysis of Query 2.

We work in the coordinate basis

of
\mathbb{C}^{2}

(matching the group action
\sigma\colon\{1,2\}\to\{1,2\}
).
Prepare
\lvert 1\rangle
and apply

: the outcome is

.
For
\sigma=\mathrm{id}
: outcome
\lvert 1\rangle
, set

.
For
\sigma=(12)

\lvert 2\rangle
, set

.

b_{1}\wedge\overline{b_{2}}=1
if and only if
\varepsilon
has mixed

and

\sigma=\mathrm{id}
, which is precisely

\widetilde{\varphi}=1
.
∎

(Individual oracles are insufficient)
.

Neither
P_{\sigma}
alone nor

alone can compute

\widetilde{\varphi}
on

in any finite number of queries.

Proof.

alone:

For any number of queries
t
, elements

and

yield the same oracle

at every query, hence

,

and

\widetilde{\varphi}(g_{2})\neq\widetilde{\varphi}(g_{6})
,

t
suffices.
∎

4.
The Reduction Formula

4.1.
The
\varepsilon
-parity
selection rule

Proposition 4.1

(Parity of the natural character)
.

For all
(\sigma,\varepsilon)\in B_{N}
,

(8)

\chi_{V}(\sigma,-\varepsilon)=-\chi_{V}(\sigma,\varepsilon).

Proof.

\chi_{V}(\sigma,\varepsilon)=\sum_{x\colon\sigma(x)=x}\varepsilon_{x}
.
Replacing
\varepsilon
by
-\varepsilon
negates each summand.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

\chi_{V}^{t}(\sigma,-\varepsilon)=(-1)^{t}\chi_{V}^{t}(\sigma,\varepsilon)
.

Definition 4.3

(
\varepsilon
-parity of irreps)
.

An irreducible representation

of

is

\varepsilon
-even
if

\chi_{(\alpha,\beta)}(\sigma,-\varepsilon)=\chi_{(\alpha,\beta)}(\sigma,\varepsilon)

for all
(\sigma,\varepsilon)
, and
\varepsilon
-odd
if

\chi_{(\alpha,\beta)}(\sigma,-\varepsilon)=-\chi_{(\alpha,\beta)}(\sigma,\varepsilon)

for all
(\sigma,\varepsilon)
.
The
\varepsilon
-parity of

is
(-1)^{\lvert\beta\rvert}

(since the central element
(\mathrm{id},-1,\dots,-1)\in B_{N}
acts on

V_{(\alpha,\beta)}
by the scalar
(-1)^{\lvert\beta\rvert}
).

Proposition 4.4

(Parity selection rule)
.

The irreducible constituents of
V^{\otimes t}
satisfy:

(i)

if
t
is even, all constituents are
\varepsilon
-even;

(ii)

if
t
is odd, all constituents are
\varepsilon
-odd.

In particular,
\sgn(\sigma)=V_{((1^{N}),\varnothing)}
is

\varepsilon
-even and can only appear in even tensor powers.

Proof.

Suppose
W
appears in

, so

\langle\chi_{V}^{t},\,\chi_{W}\rangle_{B_{N}}\neq 0
.
For each
(\sigma,\varepsilon)\in B_{N}
, pair it with

(\sigma,-\varepsilon)
. By

,

\chi_{V}^{t}(\sigma,-\varepsilon)=(-1)^{t}\chi_{V}^{t}(\sigma,\varepsilon)
.
If

has parity

(the “wrong” parity), then
the contributions from
(\sigma,\varepsilon)
and

(\sigma,-\varepsilon)
cancel, giving

\langle\chi_{V}^{t},\,\chi_{W}\rangle=0
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.5

(Parity obstruction for odd tensor powers)
.

For any odd positive integer

,

(9)

\langle\chi_{V}^{t},\,\chi_{\sgn}\rangle_{B_{N}}=0.

Proof.

For odd

,

\chi_{V}^{t}(\sigma,-\varepsilon)=-\chi_{V}^{t}(\sigma,\varepsilon)
while

\chi_{\sgn}(\sigma,-\varepsilon)=\sgn(\sigma)=\chi_{\sgn}(\sigma,\varepsilon)
.
Grouping terms
(\sigma,\varepsilon)
and
(\sigma,-\varepsilon)

in the inner product sum:

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{V}^{t},\,\chi_{\sgn}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lvert B_{N}\rvert}\sum_{\sigma}\sgn(\sigma)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon\\
\varepsilon_{1}=+1\end{subarray}}\bigl[\chi_{V}^{t}(\sigma,\varepsilon)+\chi_{V}^{t}(\sigma,-\varepsilon)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lvert B_{N}\rvert}\sum_{\sigma}\sgn(\sigma)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon\\
\varepsilon_{1}=+1\end{subarray}}\bigl[\chi_{V}^{t}(\sigma,\varepsilon)-\chi_{V}^{t}(\sigma,\varepsilon)\bigr]=0.\qed

4.2.
The reduction formula

Theorem 4.6

(Reduction to
S_{N}
)
.

For any positive integer
m
,

(10)

\langle\chi_{V}^{2m},\,\sgn(\sigma)\rangle_{B_{N}}=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\sgn(\sigma)\cdot p_{m}\bigl(\lvert\Fix(\sigma)\rvert\bigr),

where
f(\sigma)=\lvert\Fix(\sigma)\rvert
and the
Rademacher moment
polynomial
is

(11)

p_{m}(f)=\mathbb{E}\biggl[\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{f}X_{i}\Bigr)^{\!2m}\biggr],\qquad X_{1},\dots,X_{f}\;\text{iid Rademacher}\;(\Pr[X_{i}=\pm 1]=\tfrac{1}{2}).

Proof.

By definition of the inner product over
B_{N}
,

\langle\chi_{V}^{2m},\,\sgn(\sigma)\rangle_{B_{N}}=\frac{1}{2^{N}N!}\sum_{(\sigma,\varepsilon)\in B_{N}}\chi_{V}(\sigma,\varepsilon)^{2m}\cdot\sgn(\sigma).

For fixed
\sigma
, write
f=\lvert\Fix(\sigma)\rvert
. Then

\chi_{V}(\sigma,\varepsilon)=\sum_{x\in\Fix(\sigma)}\varepsilon_{x}

depends only on the signs at fixed points.
The sum over
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}
factorises: the

N-f
coordinates at non-fixed points contribute freely (a factor of

2^{N-f}
), and the
f
fixed-point coordinates contribute

\sum_{(\varepsilon_{x})_{x\in\Fix(\sigma)}\in\{\pm 1\}^{f}}\Bigl(\sum_{x\in\Fix(\sigma)}\varepsilon_{x}\Bigr)^{\!2m}=2^{f}\cdot p_{m}(f).

Combining,

\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}}\chi_{V}(\sigma,\varepsilon)^{2m}=2^{N-f}\cdot 2^{f}\cdot p_{m}(f)=2^{N}\cdot p_{m}(f)
.
Substituting back:

\langle\chi_{V}^{2m},\,\sgn(\sigma)\rangle_{B_{N}}=\frac{1}{2^{N}N!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\sgn(\sigma)\cdot 2^{N}\cdot p_{m}(f(\sigma))=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\sgn(\sigma)\cdot p_{m}(f(\sigma)).\qed

Remark 4.7
.

The Reduction Formula transforms a computation over
B_{N}
(a group of
order
2^{N}N!
) into one over
S_{N}
(order
N!
), at the cost of
introducing the moment polynomial
p_{m}
. This is possible because
the sum over signs
\varepsilon
factorises from the sum over
permutations
\sigma
.

4.3.
The degree of
p_{m}

Theorem 4.8

(Degree of the moment polynomial)
.

The polynomial
p_{m}(f)
has degree exactly
m
in
f
.
More precisely,

(12)

p_{m}(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}S^{\mathrm{even}}(2m,j)\cdot\binom{f}{j},

where
S^{\mathrm{even}}(2m,j)
denotes the number of surjections
from
\{1,\dots,2m\}
to a
j
-element set in which every fibre has
even cardinality. Moreover,
S^{\mathrm{even}}(2m,j)>0
for all

1\leq j\leq m
and
S^{\mathrm{even}}(2m,j)=0
for
j>m
.

Proof.

Expand the
2m
-th power:

p_{m}(f)=\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{2m})\in[f]^{2m}}\mathbb{E}[X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{2m}}].

The expectation
\mathbb{E}[X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{2m}}]
equals
1
if and
only if every distinct index among
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{2m}\}
appears an
even number of times, and
0
otherwise (since

\mathbb{E}[X^{2k}]=1
and
\mathbb{E}[X^{2k+1}]=0
for iid Rademacher variables).

Classify tuples by the number
j
of distinct indices. Each index
must appear at least twice, so
2j\leq 2m
, giving
j\leq m
. For

j>m
no valid tuples exist.

For a given
j
-element subset
T\subseteq[f]
, the number of
tuples using exactly the indices in
T
(each an even number of
times) is
S^{\mathrm{even}}(2m,j)
. The number of
j
-element subsets
of
[f]
is
\binom{f}{j}
. Therefore

p_{m}(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}S^{\mathrm{even}}(2m,j)\binom{f}{j}
.

Positivity.

For
1\leq j\leq m
, we exhibit an explicit surjection. Map positions

\{1,\dots,2(m-j+1)\}
to label
1
(a fibre of size

2(m-j+1)\geq 2
), and for
r=2,\dots,j
map positions

\{2(m-j+1)+2(r-2)+1,\;2(m-j+1)+2(r-2)+2\}
to label
r

(fibres of size
2
). This is surjective with all fibres of even
size, so
S^{\mathrm{even}}(2m,j)\geq 1
.

Leading coefficient.

For
j=m
, each fibre has size exactly
2
(since
2j=2m
). The
count is

S^{\mathrm{even}}(2m,m)=\binom{2m}{2}\binom{2m-2}{2}\cdots\binom{2}{2}=\frac{(2m)!}{2^{m}}.

Since
\binom{f}{j}
is a polynomial of degree
j
in
f
and the
top term corresponds to
j=m
, we have
\deg(p_{m})=m
with leading
coefficient

S^{\mathrm{even}}(2m,m)/m!=\frac{(2m)!}{2^{m}\cdot m!}=(2m-1)!!>0
.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Writing
p_{m}(f)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{m,j}\,f^{j}
in the monomial basis,
the leading coefficient is

c_{m,m}=(2m-1)!!>0

and
c_{m,0}=p_{m}(0)=0
.
Explicitly:

\displaystyle p_{1}(f)

\displaystyle=f,

\displaystyle p_{2}(f)

\displaystyle=3f^{2}-2f,

\displaystyle p_{3}(f)

\displaystyle=15f^{3}-30f^{2}+16f.

Proof.

p_{1}
:

p_{1}(f)=\mathbb{E}[(\sum_{i=1}^{f}X_{i})^{2}]=\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}]+\sum_{i\neq j}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{j}]=f
.

p_{2}
:

p_{2}(f)=\mathbb{E}[(\sum_{i=1}^{f}X_{i})^{4}]
.
Nonzero contributions come from: (a) all four indices equal,
giving
f
terms of value
1
; (b) two pairs of equal indices
(
i\neq j
), giving
\binom{4}{2}\binom{f}{2}=6\cdot\tfrac{f(f-1)}{2}=3f(f-1)
terms.
Total:
f+3f(f-1)=3f^{2}-2f
.

p_{3}
:
An analogous computation gives

p_{3}(f)=15f^{3}-30f^{2}+16f
.
∎

5.

5.1.
The tensor product graph

T(B_{N},V)

The tensor product graph
T(B_{N},V)
has vertex set
\widehat{B_{N}}
and
edges determined by the following Pieri-type rule.

Theorem 5.1

(Pieri rule for
B_{N}
)
.

Let
V=V_{((N-1),(1))}
. The tensor product

V\otimes V_{(\lambda,\mu)}
decomposes as a multiplicity-free
direct sum

V\otimes V_{(\lambda,\mu)}=\bigoplus_{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}V_{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})},

where the sum runs over all bipartitions
(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
obtained
from
(\lambda,\mu)
by one of the two operations:

(a)

remove a removable box from
\lambda
and add an addable box
to
\mu
;

(b)

remove a removable box from
\mu
and add an addable box
to
\lambda
.

Each such
(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
appears with multiplicity
1
.
In particular, each tensor step changes
\lvert\mu\rvert
by
\pm 1
.

Proof.

Let

H_{N}:=\operatorname{Stab}_{B_{N}}(N)\cong B_{N-1}\times C_{2},

and let
\eta_{N}
denote the nontrivial character of the final
C_{2}

factor. We claim
V\cong\Ind_{H_{N}}^{B_{N}}(\triv\boxtimes\eta_{N})
.
To see this, choose coset representatives
t_{i}\in B_{N}
with

t_{i}(N)=i
for
i=1,\dots,N
, and identify the basis vector

e_{i}
of
V
with
t_{i}\otimes 1
in the induced module.
For
g=(\sigma,\varepsilon)\in B_{N}
, write
g\,t_{i}=t_{\sigma(i)}\,h

with
h\in H_{N}
; the final
C_{2}
component of
h
is

\varepsilon_{i}
, so

g\cdot(t_{i}\otimes 1)=t_{\sigma(i)}\otimes\eta_{N}(h)=\varepsilon_{i}\,(t_{\sigma(i)}\otimes 1)
,
which matches
\rho(\sigma,\varepsilon)e_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}\,e_{\sigma(i)}
.
By the tensor-induction identity (projection formula),

V\otimes V_{(\lambda,\mu)}\cong\Ind_{H_{N}}^{B_{N}}\!\left(\Res_{H_{N}}^{B_{N}}(V_{(\lambda,\mu)})\otimes(\triv\boxtimes\eta_{N})\right).

Now apply the standard branching rule for the wreath product

C_{2}\wr S_{N}
(see James–Kerber
[
7
, Ch. 4]
,
Ceccherini-Silberstein–Scarabotti–Tolli
[
4
, Ch. 4]
,
or Doeraene–Iommi Amunátegui
[
5
]
):

\Res_{H_{N}}^{B_{N}}V_{(\lambda,\mu)}\cong\bigoplus_{\square\in\mathrm{Rem}(\lambda)}V_{(\lambda\setminus\square,\mu)}\boxtimes\triv\;\oplus\;\bigoplus_{\square\in\mathrm{Rem}(\mu)}V_{(\lambda,\mu\setminus\square)}\boxtimes\eta_{N}.

Tensoring with
(\triv\boxtimes\eta_{N})
flips the
C_{2}
-label, so the
first family induces back by adding one box to
\mu
, while the
second family induces back by adding one box to
\lambda
. Thus one
obtains exactly the two box-transfer families in the statement and no
same-component moves. Multiplicity-freeness follows because the two
families are disjoint (they change
\lvert\mu\rvert
by
+1
or
-1
), and
within each family the pair (removed box, added box) is determined by
the resulting bipartition.
∎

The
\varepsilon
-parity alternation
(Proposition
4.4
) is consistent with

\lvert\mu\rvert
changing by
\pm 1
at each step.

Theorem 5.2

(Row bound)
.

Let
\std=V_{(N-1,1)}
be the standard representation of
S_{N}

(
N\geq 2
). If
V_{\lambda}
appears as a constituent of

\std^{\otimes k}
, then
\lambda
has at most
k+1
parts (rows).

Proof.

By induction on
k
.

Base case (
k=0
).

\std^{\otimes 0}=\triv=V_{(N)}
, which has
1=0+1
part.

Inductive step.

Assume every constituent
V_{\lambda}
of
\std^{\otimes k}
has

\ell(\lambda)\leq k+1
parts. We show every constituent
V_{\mu}
of

\std^{\otimes(k+1)}=\std^{\otimes k}\otimes\std
satisfies

\ell(\mu)\leq k+2
.

Since
V_{\mu}
appears in
\std^{\otimes(k+1)}
, there exists a
constituent
V_{\lambda}
of
\std^{\otimes k}
with

V_{\mu}\subseteq V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{(N-1,1)}
.
Using
V_{(N-1,1)}=\perm-\triv
:

V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{(N-1,1)}=V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\perm}-V_{\lambda}.

Every constituent of
V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{(N-1,1)}
is a
constituent of
V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\perm}
. By Frobenius
reciprocity,

V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\perm}=\Ind_{S_{N-1}}^{S_{N}}\bigl(\Res_{S_{N-1}}^{S_{N}}(V_{\lambda})\bigr).

By the branching rule for restriction
S_{N}\to S_{N-1}
:
the constituents
V_{\nu}
of
\Res(V_{\lambda})
are obtained from

\lambda
by removing one removable box, so

\ell(\nu)\in\{\ell(\lambda)-1,\ell(\lambda)\}
.
By the branching rule for induction
S_{N-1}\to S_{N}
:
the constituents
V_{\mu}
of
\Ind(V_{\nu})
are obtained from
\nu
by
adding one box, so

\ell(\mu)\in\{\ell(\nu),\ell(\nu)+1\}
.
Combining:

\ell(\mu)\leq\ell(\nu)+1\leq\ell(\lambda)+1\leq(k+1)+1=k+2
.
∎

.

5.3.
The exterior power identity

Theorem 5.4

(Exterior power)
.

For the standard representation
\std=V_{(N-1,1)}
of

with

,

(14)

\bigwedge^{N-1}(\std)=\sgn=V_{(1^{N})}.

In particular,
\sgn

.

Proof.

Since
\dim(\std)=N-1
, the top exterior power

is one-dimensional, hence equals a

. The group

has exactly two

and
\sgn
), so

\bigwedge^{N-1}(\std)\in\{\triv,\sgn\}
.

\bigwedge^{N-1}(\std)(\sigma)=\det(\std(\sigma))
.
The full permutation representation on

decomposes as

, so

.

5.4.
Vanishing of

\langle\sgn,\,\chi_{\perm}^{j}\rangle_{S_{N}}

for
j<N-1

Proposition 5.6
.

For
S_{N}
with
N\geq 2
:

(15)

\langle\sgn,\,\chi_{\perm}^{j}\rangle_{S_{N}}=0\quad\text{for }j<N-1,\qquad\langle\sgn,\,\chi_{\perm}^{N-1}\rangle_{S_{N}}=1.

Proof.

For each
j\geq 0
, let
X_{j}=[N]^{j}
with the coordinatewise action of

S_{N}
. A tuple
(a_{1},\dots,a_{j})\in X_{j}
is fixed by
\sigma
if and
only if each coordinate is fixed by
\sigma
, so the permutation
character of
X_{j}
is

\chi_{X_{j}}(\sigma)=\lvert\Fix_{X_{j}}(\sigma)\rvert=\lvert\Fix_{[N]}(\sigma)\rvert^{j}=\chi_{\perm}(\sigma)^{j}.

Thus
\langle\sgn,\,\chi_{\perm}^{j}\rangle_{S_{N}}
is the multiplicity of
\sgn

in the permutation module on
X_{j}
.

The
S_{N}
-orbits in
X_{j}
are indexed by the equality pattern of the
coordinates, equivalently by set partitions of
[j]
into
r

nonempty blocks, where
r
is the number of distinct values appearing
in the tuple. For such an orbit choose a representative whose set of
values is
\{1,\dots,r\}
. Its stabiliser is isomorphic to
S_{N-r}
:
the used values must be fixed pointwise, while the remaining
N-r

values may be permuted arbitrarily.

Now fix one orbit
O=S_{N}\cdot a
. A copy of
\sgn
inside the
permutation module on
O
is the same as a function

\varphi:O\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying

\varphi(\tau\cdot x)=\sgn(\tau)\varphi(x)\qquad(\tau\in S_{N},\;x\in O).

Such a function is determined by the single value
\varphi(a)
, and it
is well defined if and only if
\sgn(h)=1
for every
h
in the
stabiliser of
a
. Hence the orbit contributes one copy of
\sgn
if
its stabiliser is contained in
A_{N}
, and contributes no copy
otherwise. Since the stabiliser is isomorphic to
S_{N-r}
, this
happens exactly when
N-r\leq 1
.

If
j<N-1
, then every orbit has
r\leq j\leq N-2
, hence
N-r\geq 2
,
so no orbit contributes. Therefore

\langle\sgn,\,\chi_{\perm}^{j}\rangle_{S_{N}}=0\qquad(j<N-1).

If
j=N-1
, then a contributing orbit must have
r=N-1
. There is
exactly one such orbit, namely the orbit of tuples with pairwise
distinct entries, and its stabiliser is trivial. Hence this orbit
contributes exactly one copy of
\sgn
, while every other orbit
contributes none. Therefore

\langle\sgn,\,\chi_{\perm}^{N-1}\rangle_{S_{N}}=1.

∎

. By Corollary

Factor 1:
c_{N-1,N-1}>0
.

By Corollary
4.9
,

c_{N-1,N-1}=(2(N-1)-1)!!=(2N-3)!!>0
.

Factor 2:

\langle\sgn,\,\chi_{\perm}^{N-1}\rangle_{S_{N}}>0
.

Since
\perm=\triv\oplus\std
,

\langle\sgn,\,\perm^{\otimes(N-1)}\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\binom{N-1}{k}\langle\sgn,\,\std^{\otimes k}\rangle.

For
k<N-1
,
\langle\sgn,\,\std^{\otimes k}\rangle=0
by
Corollary
5.5
.
Only the
k=N-1
term survives:

\langle\sgn,\,\perm^{\otimes(N-1)}\rangle=\binom{N-1}{N-1}\langle\sgn,\,\std^{\otimes(N-1)}\rangle=\langle\sgn,\,\std^{\otimes(N-1)}\rangle\geq 1,

where the inequality holds because

\bigwedge^{N-1}(\std)=\sgn\subseteq\std^{\otimes(N-1)}
by
Theorem
5.4
.

Conclusion.

Both factors are positive, so

\langle\chi_{V}^{2(N-1)},\,\chi_{\sgn}\rangle_{B_{N}}=(2N-3)!!\cdot\langle\sgn,\,\perm^{\otimes(N-1)}\rangle_{S_{N}}>0
.
∎

5.7.
Synthesis

Theorem 5.9

(Oracle identification complexity of
B_{N}
)
.

For the hyperoctahedral group
B_{N}
with
N\geq 2
and natural
representation

,

(20)

Q_{LV}(B_{N})=2(N-1),

with the sign-of-permutation representation

\sgn(\sigma)=V_{((1^{N}),\varnothing)}
as the unique bottleneck
for

(and one of four co-bottlenecks for

).

Proof.

The proof combines three ingredients.

(a) Lower bound.

By the Parity Selection Rule
(Proposition
4.4
),
\sgn(\sigma)
can only

5.7

(whose proof rests on the vanishing

\sgn(\sigma)

for any

(b) Upper bound.

5.8
,
\sgn(\sigma)

(c) Parity.

No odd tensor power

(or any other odd

) contains

.

Therefore

(under the identification of

.

5.9.
The bipartition distance formula

Theorem 5.10

(Bipartition distance)
.

For all bipartitions
(\alpha,\beta)\vdash N
,

(22)

d_{T}\bigl(((N),\varnothing),\;(\alpha,\beta)\bigr)=2(N-\alpha_{1})-\lvert\beta\rvert,

where
\alpha_{1}
is the largest part of
\alpha

(
with
\alpha_{1}=0
when
\alpha=\varnothing
)
.

Proof.

Lower bound.

Let
d
be the graph distance, and let
p
(resp.
q
) count

\alpha\!\to\!\beta
(resp.
\beta\!\to\!\alpha
) box transfers
along a shortest path. Then
p-q=\lvert\beta\rvert
and
p+q=d
.
At each removal from row
1
of
\alpha
, the first-row length

\alpha_{1}^{(s)}
decreases by
1
; at each addition to row
1
, it
increases by
1
; otherwise it is unchanged.
If
r_{1}\leq p
removals hit row
1
and
a_{1}\leq q
additions hit
row
1
, then
r_{1}-a_{1}=N-\alpha_{1}
, so

N-\alpha_{1}\leq r_{1}\leq p=(d+\lvert\beta\rvert)/2
, giving

d\geq 2(N-\alpha_{1})-\lvert\beta\rvert
.

Upper bound.

We now construct the upper-bound path directly in two phases.

Phase A:

Build
\beta
(
\lvert\beta\rvert
steps).

Transfer boxes from row
1
of
\alpha
to
\beta
along a
saturated chain

\varnothing=\beta^{(0)}\subset\cdots\subset\beta^{(\lvert\beta\rvert)}=\beta_{\mathrm{target}}
.
After this phase,
\alpha=(N-\lvert\beta\rvert)
is a single row and

\beta=\beta_{\mathrm{target}}
.

Phase B:

Rearrange
\alpha
(
2(\lvert\alpha\rvert-\alpha_{1})
steps).

Let
a:=\lvert\alpha\rvert=N-\lvert\beta\rvert
. List the boxes of the Young
diagram of
\alpha
outside the first row as

c_{1},\dots,c_{m}
, where
m=a-\alpha_{1}
, in row-major order: top to
bottom and, within each row, left to right. For
0\leq r\leq m
, let

\gamma^{(r)}
be the partition formed by taking a first row of
length
a-r
together with the boxes
c_{1},\dots,c_{r}
below it.
Because
c_{1},\dots,c_{r}
is an initial segment of the diagram of

\alpha
and
a-r\geq\alpha_{1}
, each
\gamma^{(r)}
is a valid
partition. Moreover,

\gamma^{(0)}=(a),\qquad\gamma^{(m)}=\alpha,

and
\gamma^{(r+1)}
is obtained from
\gamma^{(r)}
by removing the
rightmost box of row
1
and adding the next box
c_{r+1}
.

Choose any addable box
Q
of
\beta
(if
\beta=\varnothing
, take

Q=(1,1)
). For each
r=0,\dots,m-1
, perform the two tensor-graph
steps

(\gamma^{(r)},\beta)\to(\gamma^{(r)}\setminus\{\text{last box of row }1\},\,\beta\cup\{Q\})\to(\gamma^{(r+1)},\beta).

The first move is legal by Theorem
5.1
. The second is
legal because
Q
is removable in
\beta\cup\{Q\}
and

c_{r+1}
is addable to

\gamma^{(r)}\setminus\{\text{last box of row }1\}
by the
construction of
\gamma^{(r+1)}
. Thus each pair of steps advances
the
\alpha
-component from
\gamma^{(r)}
to
\gamma^{(r+1)}

while restoring
\beta
.

Total:
\lvert\beta\rvert+2(\lvert\alpha\rvert-\alpha_{1})=\lvert\beta\rvert+2(N-\lvert\beta\rvert-\alpha_{1})=2(N-\alpha_{1})-\lvert\beta\rvert
.
∎

Corollary 5.11

(Bottleneck uniqueness)
.

The maximum of
d_{T}(((N),\varnothing),(\alpha,\beta))
over all
bipartitions is
2(N-1)
, achieved uniquely by

(\alpha,\beta)=((1^{N}),\varnothing)
when
N\geq 3
.
For
N=2
, three bipartitions —

((1^{2}),\varnothing)
,
(\varnothing,(2))
, and

(\varnothing,(1^{2}))
— all attain the maximum distance
2
.

Proof.

For
((1^{N}),\varnothing)
:
\alpha_{1}=1
,
\lvert\beta\rvert=0
, so

d_{T}=2(N-1)
.
For
\lvert\beta\rvert=0
,
\alpha\neq(1^{N})
:
\alpha_{1}\geq 2
, so

d_{T}\leq 2(N-2)<2(N-1)
.
For
\lvert\beta\rvert\geq 1
and
\lvert\alpha\rvert\geq 1
:

\alpha_{1}\geq 1
, so

d_{T}=2(N-\alpha_{1})-\lvert\beta\rvert\leq 2(N-1)-1
.
For
\alpha=\varnothing
:
\lvert\beta\rvert=N
and
d_{T}=2N-N=N
.
When
N\geq 3
,
N<2(N-1)
, so
((1^{N}),\varnothing)
is the unique
maximiser. When
N=2
,
N=2=2(N-1)
, giving two additional
maximisers
(\varnothing,(2))
and
(\varnothing,(1^{2}))
.
∎

5.10.
The signed generating function and exact multiplicity

Theorem 5.12

(Signed fixed-point generating function)
.

For
N\geq 1
,

(23)

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\sgn(\sigma)\,x^{f(\sigma)}=(x-1)^{N-1}(x+N-1),

where
f(\sigma)=\lvert\Fix(\sigma)\rvert
.

Proof.

Let
J
be the
N\times N
all-ones matrix and set
A=J+(x-1)I_{N}
.
Expanding the determinant by permutations:

\det(A)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\sgn(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{N}A_{i,\sigma(i)}.

An entry
A_{i,\sigma(i)}
equals
x
if
\sigma(i)=i
(the diagonal
entry
1+(x-1)=x
) and
1
otherwise. Hence

\prod_{i}A_{i,\sigma(i)}=x^{f(\sigma)}
, giving

\det(A)=\sum_{\sigma}\sgn(\sigma)\,x^{f(\sigma)}
.
On the other hand,
J
has eigenvalues
N
(multiplicity
1
) and
0

(multiplicity
N-1
), so
A
has eigenvalues
x+N-1
and
x-1

(with multiplicity
N-1
), whence

\det(A)=(x-1)^{N-1}(x+N-1)
.
∎

Corollary 5.13

(Exact multiplicity at first appearance)
.

(24)

\langle\chi_{V}^{2(N-1)},\,\sgn(\sigma)\rangle_{B_{N}}=(2N-3)!!\qquad\text{for all }N\geq 2.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.10
,

\langle\chi_{V}^{2(N-1)},\,\sgn\rangle_{B_{N}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}c_{N-1,j}\cdot\langle\sgn,\,\chi_{\perm}^{j}\rangle_{S_{N}}.

5.6
,

\langle\sgn,\,\chi_{\perm}^{j}\rangle_{S_{N}}=0
for

and

\langle\sgn,\,\chi_{\perm}^{N-1}\rangle_{S_{N}}=1
.
Hence

\langle\chi_{V}^{2(N-1)},\,\sgn\rangle_{B_{N}}=c_{N-1,N-1}\cdot 1=(2N-3)!!

by Corollary
4.9
.
∎

6.1.
The oracle difference matrix and its spectral decomposition

Definition 6.1
.

For the oracle identification problem on
B_{N}
with natural
representation
V
, define the
oracle difference matrix

\Delta_{V}\in\mathbb{C}^{|B_{N}|\cdot N\times|B_{N}|\cdot N}
with

N\times N
blocks

\Delta_{V}[g,h]\;=\;I_{N}-\rho(g)^{\dagger}\rho(h),\qquad g,h\in B_{N}.

Each block
\Delta_{V}[g,h]=I-U_{g}^{\dagger}U_{h}
need not be Hermitian,
since
U_{g}^{\dagger}U_{h}
is unitary but not necessarily self-adjoint.
Define the
normalised
oracle difference matrix

\widetilde{\Delta}_{V}:=\frac{1}{\lvert B_{N}\rvert}\,\Delta_{V}
.
Its Fourier transform is block-diagonal: in the

\pi
-isotypic sector (
\pi\in\widehat{B_{N}}
), the eigenvalues of

\widetilde{\Delta}_{V}
are

\begin{cases}+1&\text{in the trivial sector
(multiplicity $\dim V=N$),}\\
-1&\text{with multiplicity $d_{\pi}\cdot n_{\pi}(V)$
if $\pi\in I(V)$,}\\
\phantom{-}0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(The unnormalised matrix
\Delta_{V}
has eigenvalues
\pm\lvert B_{N}\rvert

and
0
with the same multiplicities.)

6.2.
The Schur product and Hermiticity

Definition 6.2
.

For a weight matrix
\Gamma=(\Gamma[g,h])_{g,h\in B_{N}}
with scalar
entries, the
Hadamard
/
Schur product

\Gamma\circ\Delta_{V}
is the block matrix with
(g,h)
-block

\Gamma[g,h]\cdot\Delta_{V}[g,h]
.

Proposition 6.3
.

If
\Gamma
is Hermitian
(
\Gamma[g,h]^{*}=\Gamma[h,g]
for
all
g,h
)
, then
\Gamma\circ\Delta_{V}
is Hermitian.

Proof.

The
(g,h)
-block of
(\Gamma\circ\Delta_{V})^{\dagger}
is

\bigl[\Gamma[h,g]\cdot(I-U_{h}^{\dagger}U_{g})\bigr]^{\dagger}=\Gamma[h,g]^{*}\cdot(I-U_{g}^{\dagger}U_{h})=\Gamma[g,h]\cdot\Delta_{V}[g,h]=(\Gamma\circ\Delta_{V})[g,h],

using
(U_{h}^{\dagger}U_{g})^{\dagger}=U_{g}^{\dagger}U_{h}
and the
Hermiticity of
\Gamma
.

Appendix B
Proof Details

B.1 Permutation character orthogonality

Proposition B.1
.

For
N\geq 3
:

\langle\operatorname{sgn},\chi_{\mathrm{perm}}\rangle_{S_{N}}=0
.

Proof.

The permutation character decomposes as

\chi_{\mathrm{perm}}=\chi_{\mathrm{triv}}+\chi_{\mathrm{std}}
.
For
N\geq 3
, the sign representation

\operatorname{sgn}=V_{(1^{N})}
is a distinct irreducible from both

V_{(N)}=\mathrm{triv}
and
V_{(N-1,1)}=\mathrm{std}
.
By Schur orthogonality,

\langle\operatorname{sgn},\mathrm{triv}\rangle=0
and

\langle\operatorname{sgn},\mathrm{std}\rangle=0
, hence

\langle\operatorname{sgn},\chi_{\mathrm{perm}}\rangle=0
.
∎

Remark B.2
.

For
N=2
,
\operatorname{sgn}=\mathrm{std}
, so the inner product
equals
1
.
This is consistent with

\langle\chi_{V}^{2},\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\rangle_{B_{2}}=1
.

B.2 The Exterior Power Identity

Proposition B.3
.

For the standard representation

\mathrm{std}=V_{(N-1,1)}
of
S_{N}

(\dim=N{-}1)
:

\textstyle\bigwedge^{N-1}(\mathrm{std})=\operatorname{sgn}_{S_{N}}=V_{(1^{N})}.

Consequently,
\operatorname{sgn}\in I(\mathrm{std}^{\otimes(N-1)})
,
and this is the minimum tensor power in which

\operatorname{sgn}
appears.

Proof.

Upper bound.

Since
\dim(\mathrm{std})=N{-}1
, the top exterior power

\bigwedge^{N-1}(\mathrm{std})
is one-dimensional.
Its character is

\chi_{\bigwedge^{N-1}(\mathrm{std})}(\sigma)=\det\bigl(\mathrm{std}(\sigma)\bigr).

The standard representation is obtained from the permutation
representation on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
by quotienting out the all-ones
vector.
In the basis
\{e_{1}{-}e_{N},\ldots,e_{N-1}{-}e_{N}\}
, the determinant
of the matrix representing
\sigma
is
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
.

To see this, observe that the permutation representation acts on

\mathbb{C}^{N}
by
\sigma\cdot e_{j}=e_{\sigma(j)}
.
The quotient space
\mathbb{C}^{N}/\langle\mathbf{1}\rangle
inherits
a well-defined action, and the matrix of
\sigma
in the basis

\{e_{j}-e_{N}\}_{j=1}^{N-1}
is obtained by applying elementary column
and row operations to the permutation matrix of
\sigma
.
Since each adjacent transposition changes the sign of the determinant
by
-1
, the result is
\det=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
.

Therefore

\bigwedge^{N-1}(\mathrm{std})=\operatorname{sgn}
, and since the
exterior power is a subrepresentation of
\mathrm{std}^{\otimes(N-1)}

(via the antisymmetriser), we conclude

\operatorname{sgn}\in I(\mathrm{std}^{\otimes(N-1)})
.

Lower bound.

By Theorem
5.2
, every irreducible constituent of

\mathrm{std}^{\otimes k}
has at most
k{+}1
rows.
The sign representation
V_{(1^{N})}
has
N
rows, so it cannot
appear unless
k{+}1\geq N
, i.e.
k\geq N{-}1
.
∎
