Highly Incremental: A Simple Programmatic Approach for Many Objectives (Extended Version)
Philipp Schröer, Joost-Pieter Katoen

Abstract
We present a one-fits-all programmatic approach to reason about a plethora of objectives on probabilistic programs. The first ingredient is to add a reward-statement to the language. We then define a program transformation applying a monotone function to the cumulative reward of the program. The key idea is that this transformation uses incremental differences in the reward. This simple, elegant approach enables to express e.g., higher moments, threshold probabilities of rewards, the expected excess over a budget, and moment-generating functions. All these objectives can now be analyzed using a single existing approach: probabilistic wp-reasoning. We automated verification using the Caesar deductive verifier and report on the application of the transformation to some examples.

Different objectives currently require separate

Our presentation is based on

\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{rew}({\mathcal{T}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})}))=\mathsf{E}(f(\mathsf{rew}({{\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}})))~.

In terms of weakest pre-expectation semantics, we can express

\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{rew}({\mathcal{T}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})}))=\textsf{{wp}}\llbracket{\mathcal{T}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})}\rrbracket(0)=\mathsf{E}(f(\mathsf{rew}({{\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}})))
.
In particular, the

statement reflects the telescoping idea from
Equation

.
For an expectation
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X}\in\mathbb{E}
, we have:

\textsf{{wp}}\llbracket{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{reward}}}~{a})}\rrbracket({\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X})~{}={}~\lambda{\sigma}.~{\underbrace{f(\sigma(\tau)+a(\sigma))}_{\text{(2) set}}-\underbrace{f(\sigma(\tau))}_{\text{(1) reset}}+\underbrace{{\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X}(\sigma[{\tau}\mapsto{\tau+a}])}_{\text{next}}}~.

The program transformation preserves healthiness conditions similar to
wp
.

Theorem 4.2
(Monotonicity)

Let
{\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}\in\textsf{{pGCL}}
and let

be monotone with

.
For all
{\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X}\in\mathbb{E}
, we have
\textsf{{wp}}\llbracket{\mathcal{T}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})}\rrbracket({\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X})\preceq\textsf{{wp}}\llbracket{\mathcal{T}_{g}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})}\rrbracket({\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X})
.

The transformation is linear with respect to scaling and shifting.

Theorem 4.3
(Linearity)

Let
{\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}\in\textsf{{pGCL}}
and let
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty}
be monotone.
Then, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we have
\textsf{{wp}}\llbracket{\mathcal{T}_{\alpha\cdot f+\beta}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})}\rrbracket(0)=\alpha\cdot\textsf{{wp}}\llbracket{\mathcal{T}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})}\rrbracket(0)+\beta
.

For linear transformation functions, we can avoid the ghost variable
\tau
from the transformed program
\mathcal{T}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})
entirely.

Theorem 4.4
(Ghost Buster)

Let

be of the form

for some

Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})~{}={}~{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{reward}}}~{\beta}{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{;}~}}\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{reward}}}~{a})~{}={}~{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{reward}}}~{\alpha\cdot a}~.

The transformation is well-behaved with respect to composition of transformation functions.
In essence:
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{g}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}))=\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{g\circ f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})

Theorem 4.5
(Composition of Transformations)

Let
{\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}\in\textsf{{pGCL}}
and let

\textsf{{wp}}\llbracket{{\tau^{\prime}}\,{\coloneqq}\,{f(\tau)}{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{;}~}}\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{g}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}))}\rrbracket=\textsf{{wp}}\llbracket{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{g\circ f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}){\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{;}~}}{\tau^{\prime}}\,{\coloneqq}\,{f(\tau)}}\rrbracket~.

4.2
Transforming Rewards of Markov Chains

To prove soundness of the program transformation (
Theorem

4.1
), we define a reward transformation for Markov chains directly.
The state space is augmented with a dimension
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty}
to track the accumulated reward of the original Markov chain, which is zero in the initial state
\sigma_{1}^{\prime}
, and the reward function is adjusted to collect the incremental differences.
Here, we restrict to monotone
f
with
f(0)=0
for simplicity.

Definition 2
(MC Transformation)

Let MC
\mathcal{M}=(\mathsf{States},\mathsf{Prob},\sigma_{1},\mathsf{rew})
and monotone
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty}
with
f(0)=0
.
7
7

7

The restriction
f(0)=0
in
\mathcal{T}_{f}(\mathcal{M})
can be easily lifted by using the function
g(x)=f(x)-f(0)
for
\mathcal{T}_{g}(\mathcal{M})
, and prepending a new initial state
\sigma_{0}^{\prime}
with reward
f(0)
. The full construction is presented in
Section

0.A.3
.

We define
\mathcal{T}_{f}(\mathcal{M})=(\mathsf{States}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty},~\mathsf{Prob^{\prime}},~\sigma_{1}^{\prime},~\mathsf{rew}^{\prime})
where

•

\sigma_{1}^{\prime}=(\sigma_{1},0)
,

•

\mathsf{Prob^{\prime}}((\sigma,\alpha),(\sigma^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}))=\begin{cases}\mathsf{Prob}(\sigma)(\sigma^{\prime})&\text{if }\alpha+\mathsf{rew}({\sigma})=\alpha^{\prime},\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

•

\mathsf{rew}^{\prime}((\sigma,\alpha))=f(\alpha+\mathsf{rew}({\sigma}))-f(\alpha)
.

Note that the transformation does not preserve the size of the reachable state space, because the added dimension
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty}
tracks the reward accumulation.

Example 1
(Reachable States Explosion)

Let MC
\mathcal{M}=(\mathsf{States},\mathsf{Prob},\sigma_{1},\mathsf{rew})
with
\mathsf{States}=\Set{\sigma_{1}}
,
\mathsf{Prob}(\sigma_{1})(\sigma_{1})=1
,
\mathsf{rew}(\sigma_{1})=1
.
Let
f(x)=x
.
The transformed MC
\mathcal{T}_{f}(\mathcal{M})
has an infinite path
(\sigma_{1},0),(\sigma_{1},1),(\sigma_{1},2),\ldots
with probability one.
The set of reachable states is
\Set{(\sigma_{1},\alpha)\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
, which is infinite.

The paths of the transformed Markov chain
\mathcal{T}_{f}(\mathcal{M})
correspond one-to-one to the paths of the original
\mathcal{M}
, from which the soundness theorem follows.

Lemma 3

Let MC
\mathcal{M}=(\mathsf{States},\mathsf{Prob},\sigma_{1},\mathsf{rew})
and
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty}
be monotone with
f(0)=0
.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For every
\sigma_{1}\ldots\sigma_{n}\in\mathsf{Paths}^{=n}_{\mathcal{M}}
, there is a one-to-one mapping to
(\sigma_{1},\alpha_{1})\ldots(\sigma_{n},\alpha_{n})\in\mathsf{Paths}^{=n}_{\mathcal{T}_{f}(\mathcal{M})}
in
\mathcal{T}_{f}(\mathcal{M})
such that:

1.

same path probabilities:
\mathsf{Prob}(\sigma_{1}\ldots\sigma_{n})=\mathsf{Prob^{\prime}}((\sigma_{1},\alpha_{1})\ldots(\sigma_{n},\alpha_{n}))
,

2.

\alpha_{i}
track previous untransformed rewards:
\forall 1\leq i\leq n.\,\sum_{j<i}\mathsf{rew}({\sigma_{j}})=\alpha_{i}
,

3.

transformed rewards:
f(\mathsf{rew}({\sigma_{1}\ldots\sigma_{n}}))=\mathsf{rew}^{\prime}({(\sigma_{1},\alpha_{1})\ldots(\sigma_{n},\alpha_{n})})
.

It follows that the expected
f
-transformed reward of the original Markov chain
\mathcal{M}
equals the expected reward of the transformed Markov chain
\mathcal{T}_{f}(\mathcal{M})
.

Theorem 4.6
(Soundness)

For each MC
\mathcal{M}
and monotone
f
:

\mathsf{E}(f(\mathsf{rew}({\mathcal{M}})))=\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{rew}^{\prime}({\mathcal{T}_{f}(\mathcal{M})}))~.

Note that one can lift the monotonicity requirement of function
f
.
For the soundness of the transformation, it suffices that
f(\tau+a)-f(\tau)\geq 0
holds for all actually occurring values
\tau
and
a
in the Markov chain.

\tau^{k}
on termination.
However, as explained in
Section

3.2
, this approach is deficient for diverging programs.

\Citeauthor
*DBLP:conf/tacas/KuraUH19
[
24
]
focus on tail probabilities of run-times and make use of higher moments to obtain bounds on them.
They synthesize upper bounds on the
k
-th moment via template-based invariants.
In
[
37
]
,
[
37
]
present an automated approach for the inference of higher moments of expected runtimes of probabilistic programs.
As for the previous approach, proof rules have to be proven specifically for their semantics.
Lower bounds are also considered and used to derive central moments.
Their approach is automated, and uses template-based invariant synthesis for interval bounds.
Algebraic methods for higher moments of step-indexed expected value have been used in
[
30
]
.
On programs with certain restrictions, e.g. acyclic non-linear variable dependencies and variables in conditions only assuming finitely many values, they can compute closed-form solutions for all moments.
Hardness results for higher moments of step-indexed expected values are presented in
[
31
]
.

8
Conclusion

We presented probabilistic programs with reward statements to obtain a one-fits-all approach for analyzing multiple objectives, e.g. expected runtimes, expected visiting times, higher moments of expected rewards, or the expected excess over a threshold.
All reduce to the “classical”
wp
calculus, eliminating the need for dedicated calculi/semantics for each objective.
Therefore, existing proof rules and tools for
wp
can be reused in our setting.
Future work includes the incorporation of programs with non-determinism; programmatic reward modeling works in this setting as well, but the reward transformation introduces additional subtleties due to additional information introduced by the counter variable
\tau
.

Markov chains therefore carry tuple-valued rewards:

\mathcal{M}=(\mathsf{States},\mathsf{Prob},\sigma_{1},\mathsf{rew}),\qquad\mathsf{rew}\colon\mathsf{States}\to(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty})^{n}.

A finite path
\pi=\sigma_{1}\ldots\sigma_{k}
has reward
\mathsf{rew}({\pi})\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{rew}(\sigma_{i})\in(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty})^{n}
.
For each
i
, let
{\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}_{i}
be obtained from
{\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}
by replacing each tuple reward by its
i
-th component.
Then, the tuple-valued weakest pre-expectation transformer is defined componentwise by

\displaystyle\textsf{{wp}}^{n}

\displaystyle\colon\textsf{{pGCL}}\times\mathbb{E}^{n}\to\mathbb{E}^{n},

\displaystyle\textsf{{wp}}^{n}\llbracket{\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}\rrbracket({\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X}_{1},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X}_{n})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(\textsf{{wp}}\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}_{1}}\rrbracket({\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X}_{1}),\ldots,\textsf{{wp}}\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}_{n}}\rrbracket({\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X}_{n})\right).

Generalizing the transformation.

Recall that the reward program transformation (
Definition

1
) extends as explained in
Section

5.5
: we introduce fresh ghost variables
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n}
and define

\mathcal{T}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})\coloneqq{\tau_{1}}\,{\coloneqq}\,{0}{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{;}~}}\cdots{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{;}~}}{\tau_{n}}\,{\coloneqq}\,{0}{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{;}~}}{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{reward}}}~{f(0,\ldots,0)}{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{;}~}}\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})~,

where for
reward
statements, the inner transformation is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{reward}}}~{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})})\coloneqq~

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{reward}}}~{f(\tau_{1}+a_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n}+a_{n})-f(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n})}{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{;}~}}

\displaystyle{\tau_{1}}\,{\coloneqq}\,{\tau_{1}+a_{1}}{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{;}~}}\cdots{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{;}~}}{\tau_{n}}\,{\coloneqq}\,{\tau_{n}+a_{n}}~.

The other cases are defined analogously to the single-reward case (c.f.
Figure

10
), by applying the transformation recursively.
Similarly, the MC transformation (
Definition

2
) extends as follows, assuming
f(0,\ldots,0)=0
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{f}(\mathcal{M})={}

\displaystyle(\mathsf{States}\times(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty})^{n},~\mathsf{Prob^{\prime}},~(\sigma_{1},(0,\ldots,0)),~\mathsf{rew}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{Prob^{\prime}}((\sigma,\overline{\tau}),(\sigma^{\prime},\overline{\tau}^{\prime}))={}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathsf{Prob}(\sigma)(\sigma^{\prime})&\text{if }\overline{\tau}^{\prime}=\overline{\tau}+\mathsf{rew}(\sigma),\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\mathsf{rew}^{\prime}((\sigma,\overline{\tau}))={}

\displaystyle f(\overline{\tau}+\mathsf{rew}(\sigma))-f(\overline{\tau})~,

Soundness of the Markov chain transformation (
Lemmas

3
and
4
) lifts directly to tuple-valued accumulated rewards, using the same path coupling and telescoping arguments as in the scalar case, and we obtain

\mathsf{E}(f(\mathsf{rew}({\mathcal{M}})))=\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{rew}^{\prime}({\mathcal{T}_{f}(\mathcal{M})})).

Theorem

4.1
, i.e.
\mathsf{E}(f(\mathsf{rew}({{\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}})))=\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{rew}({\mathcal{T}_{f}({\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S})}))
, follows by the same argument.

\textsf{{wp}}\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{while}}}~({\neg done})~\{~{\ldots}~\}}\rrbracket(0)~{}={}~\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Phi_{0}^{n}(0).

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}^{0}(0)

\displaystyle~{}={}~0,

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}^{1}(0)

\displaystyle~{}={}~\left[{\neg done}\right]\cdot(2\cdot\tau+1),

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}^{2}(0)

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}^{4}(0)

\displaystyle~{}={}~\left[{\neg done}\right]\cdot(\nicefrac{{1}}{{8}}\cdot(30\cdot\tau+37))~.

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}^{n}(0)

\displaystyle~{}={}~\left[{\neg done}\right]\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\cdot((2^{n+1}-2)\cdot\tau+3\cdot(2^{n}-1)-2\cdot n)\right)~.

\displaystyle~{}={}~\left[{\neg done}\right]\cdot(4\cdot\tau+6)~.

0.C.1
Relation to the Amortized Expected Runtime Calculus

An extension of the
ert
calculus called the
amortized expected runtime calculus
was presented in
[
5
]
.
Its
aert
transformer computes the expected change of a potential function
\pi\colon\mathsf{States}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\infty}
, i.e. an expectation.
By
[
5
, Theorem 5.4]
:
\textsf{{aert}}_{\tau}\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}}\rrbracket({\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X})=\textsf{{wp}}\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0.5016,0.2203,0}S}}\rrbracket({\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X}+\pi)-\pi
.
Thus,
aert
operates on the lattice
\mathbb{A}
of
amortized runtimes
, which can be negative expectations, bounded below by the negation of the potential function, i.e.
\mathbb{A}=\Set{{\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X}\colon\mathsf{States}\to(\mathbb{R}\cup\infty)\mid{\color[rgb]{0,0.2695,0.418}X}\geq-\pi}
.

However, the analysis of the potential function in the
aert
transformer reasons about the expected value of the potential function
on termination
only.
This is implied by their Theorem 5.4: the potential function
\pi
is only added to expectation on termination (c.f.
Section

3.2
).

For further illustration, consider the infinite loop
{\color[rgb]{0,0.4348,0.3145}{\texttt{while}}}~({\texttt{true}})~\{~{{\tau}\,{\coloneqq}\,{\tau+1}}~\}
that uses the assignment
{\tau}\,{\coloneqq}\,{\tau+1}
to increase the runtime variable
\tau
by one in each iteration.
We attempt to analyze the change in potential of a counter variable
\tau
, i.e. choose
\pi=\tau
.
We have
