Multiple Tor modules: rigidity and Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequences
Arindam Banerjee, Marc Chardin, Rafael Holanda

Abstract
. We extend some properties of a pair of ideals described in terms of Tor modules to any number of ideals, including the well-known rigidity property. Those extensions require the development of a homological theory for spectral sequences arising from multiple complexes. Out of this theory, two new complexes associated with quotients by sums and quotients by products of the given ideals emerge, and their homologies are related via the Tor-independence property. In the multigraded setting, we describe the support regions of Tor modules for quotients by sums and products of ideals generated by variables in terms of each other.

1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper, by default,
ring
means a commutative and unitary ring.

For a pair of ideals
I,J
in a ring
R
, the study of the modules
\textnormal{{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(R/I,R/J)
has been one of the very popular research topics. The module
\textnormal{{Tor}}_{1}^{R}(R/I,R/J)
is isomorphic to
(I\cap J)/IJ
, while
\textnormal{{Tor}}_{2}^{R}(R/I,R/J)
is isomorphic to the kernel of the natural map
I\otimes_{R}J\rightarrow IJ
.

The vanishing of
Tor
modules sheds light on various properties of the corresponding pair of ideals; for instance, if
R
is a regular local ring, the vanishing all these for
i>0
is equivalent to the vanishing of the first one (
i=1
), and if
R/(I+J)
is Cohen-Macaulay (e.g. primary for the maximal ideal of
R
) then this first one vanishes if and only if both
R/I
and
R/J
are Cohen-Macaulay and the codimension of
I+J
is the sum of the codimensions of
I
and
J
in
R
. Geometrically, it corresponds to a proper intersection of Cohen-Macaulay schemes
[
Ser65
]
.

Serre and Auslander
[
Ser65
,
Aus61
]
proved the rigidity property for Tor modules (
\textnormal{{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(R/I,R/J)=0\Rightarrow\textnormal{{Tor}}_{j}^{R}(R/I,R/J)=0,\forall j\geq i
) over unramified regular local rings; this was then completed by Lichtenbaum
[
Lic66
]
for arbitrary regular local rings.

We here extend several of the above-mentioned results to the case of more than two ideals, and also treat the case of modules that need not be cyclic. An incarnation of the module
\textnormal{{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(R/I,R/J)
is as the
i
-th homology module of the tensor product double complex built from flat
R
-resolutions of
R/I
and
R/J
. It naturally generalises to any number
n
of ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}
as the
i
-th homology of the multicomplex obtained by taking the tensor product of flat resolutions of the quotients
R/I_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
. The definition and several properties of these modules figure in
[
Gro61
]
, which also presents these for complexes of sheaves.

We first present here a generalization of the Auslander-Serre result for an arbitrary finite collection of ideals (or of finitely generated modules), and its declination in geometric terms, together with some additional features that are only of interest for three or more modules.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem
2.6
and Corollary
2.7
)
Assume that
R
is a regular local ring containing a field
and
M_{1},\ldots,M_{s}
are non zero finitely generated

R
-modules. Then,

(1)

(a)

\textnormal{{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s})=0
implies

\textnormal{{Tor}}_{j}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s})=0
for all
j\geq i
.

(b)

For any

and

,
\textnormal{{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s})=0
implies

\textnormal{{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{t})=0
.

(2)

The following

(a)

\textnormal{{Tor}}_{1}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s})=0
and

M_{1}\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}M_{s}
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(b)

The codimension of
M_{1}\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}M_{s}

the sum of the projective dimensions of the

In the case of two ideals over an arbitrary ring, there is a nice description of the first two Tor modules, as recalled above, and an important sequence intimately linked to this description:

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R/IJ\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\theta}
\textstyle{R/I\oplus R/J\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R/(I+J)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

\textnormal{coker}(\theta)=R/(I+J)=\textnormal{{Tor}}_{0}^{R}(R/I,R/J)
) and such that
\ker(\theta)=(I\cap J)/IJ=\textnormal{{Tor}}_{1}^{R}(R/I,R/J)
.

As an illustration, we will now describe the case of three ideals
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\subseteq R
.
Then, to get an extension of the sequence above, consider the maps

\textstyle{R/I_{2}I_{3}\oplus R/I_{1}I_{3}\oplus R/I_{1}I_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{R/I_{1}\oplus R/I_{2}\oplus R/I_{3}}

\textstyle{R/I_{1}\oplus R/I_{2}\oplus R/I_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{R/(I_{2}+I_{3})\oplus R/(I_{1}+I_{3})\oplus R/(I_{1}+I_{2})}

where both

{\footnotesize\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&-1\\

Also, assuming

The modules

.

What allows us to investigate the (co)homology of such complexes is the homological counterpart of the theory developed in

These homological constructions also connect Tor modules relative to quotients by sums of these ideals to ones relative to quotients by products. Under Tor-independence conditions, it takes the form of two spectral sequences:

Proposition 1.4
.

If any subset of
\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}\}
is Tor-independent, then for any
R
-module
M
, there exist two spectral sequences

(1)

(Corollary
4.7
)

E^{1}_{p,q}=\oplus_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}}\textnormal{{Tor}}^{R}_{q}(M,R/I_{i_{1}}+\cdots+I_{i_{p}})\Rightarrow\textnormal{{Tor}}^{R}_{q-p+1}(M,R/I_{1}\cdots I_{n}).

(2)

(Corollary
4.11
)

E^{1}_{p,q}=\oplus_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}}\textnormal{{Tor}}^{R}_{q}(M,R/I_{i_{1}}\cdots I_{i_{p}})\Rightarrow\textnormal{{Tor}}^{R}_{q+p-1}(M,R/I_{1}+\cdots+I_{n}).

Since ideals generated by sets of variables with empty two-by-two intersections in a polynomial ring (over any ring and with arbitrary grading) are Tor-independent, it allows us to apply the spectral sequences in Proposition
1.4
to prove that Tor modules with respect to sums have the same support region (i.e., the non-vanishing degrees of their graded components) as Tor modules with respect to products, see Proposition
4.17
. This fact has consequences in the study of extensions of the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity to a multigraded setting and is in part at the origin of these investigations.

2.
Multiple Tor modules

Let
M_{1},\ldots,M_{s}
be
R
-modules and
F_{\bullet}
be the tensor products
over
R
of the canonical free resolutions of
M_{1},\ldots,M_{s}
. Then

\textnormal{{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s}):=H_{i}(F_{\bullet}).

We note that the tensor product over the empty set (i.e.,

, while for
s=1

\textnormal{{Tor}}_{0}^{R}(M_{1})=M_{1}
and
\textnormal{{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(M_{1})=0

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module and
F_{\bullet}
be a
complex of flat

-modules. If

H_{i}(F_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}L_{\bullet})\simeq H_{i}(F_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}M).

Proof.

Consider the spectral sequence with

E^{1}_{p,q}=H_{q}(F_{p}\otimes_{R}L_{\bullet})\simeq F_{p}\otimes_{R}H_{q}(L_{\bullet})

and
E^{2}_{p,q}=H_{p}(E^{1}_{\bullet,q})
that
abuts to
H_{p}(F_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}L_{\bullet})
and note that by

H_{q}(L_{\bullet})=0
for

H_{0}(L_{\bullet})=M
.∎

Notice that if

is the tensor product of any choice of flat resolutions of

M_{1},\ldots,M_{s}
, then
H_{i}(F_{\bullet}^{\prime})\simeq\textnormal{{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s})
. Also,

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
M,M_{1},\ldots,M_{s}
be
R
-modules and
F_{\bullet}
be the tensor products
over

of free resolutions of
M_{1},\ldots,M_{s}

\textnormal{{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s},M)\simeq H_{i}(F_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}M).

In particular
\textnormal{{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s},R)\simeq\textnormal{{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s})
.

If

is local regular of dimension

\textnormal{{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s})=0
for

. Also notice in this case

\textnormal{{Tor}}_{n(s-1)}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s})\simeq R/\mathfrak{m}\not=0

Let

be

-modules. There exists a

E^{2}_{p,q}=\textnormal{{Tor}}_{p}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s},\textnormal{{Tor}}_{q}^{R}(N_{1},\ldots,N_{t}))\ \Rightarrow\ \textnormal{{Tor}}_{p+q}^{R}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s},N_{1},\ldots,N_{t}).

The double complex

If

Proposition 3.2
.

With notations as above, the following holds:

(1)

For any
{\underline{q}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
H_{p}(C_{\bullet,{\underline{q}}}^{\Bbbk})=0
for
p\not=0
and

H_{0}(C_{\bullet,{\underline{q}}}^{\Bbbk})=C_{{\underline{q}}}^{\Im}.

C_{\bullet,{\underline{\bullet}}}^{\Bbbk}

H_{i}(C_{\bullet}^{\Bbbk})\simeq H_{i}(C_{\bullet}^{\Im}),\forall i.

Proof.

Item (1) is equivalent to the exactness of
C_{\bullet,{\underline{q}}}^{\Bbbk}
for
{\underline{q}}\not\in\Im

{\underline{q}}\in\Im

C_{p,{\underline{q}}}^{\Bbbk}=0

p=0
and
C_{0,{\underline{q}}}^{\Bbbk}=C_{{\underline{q}}}
. Assume that
{\underline{q}}
has exactly
t\geq 1
coordinates equal to zero and

K_{p}({{\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{t\ {\rm times}}}};C_{{\underline{q}}})\subseteq K_{p}({{\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{n\ {\rm times}}}};C_{{\underline{q}}})

e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{p}}
for
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p}\}\subseteq\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{t}\}
.

C_{\bullet,\bullet}^{\Bbbk}

\mathbb{N}^{n}
the complex

For (3) recall that

\mathbb{S}^{p}:=\bigoplus_{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{p}}M_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{i_{p}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{P}_{p}:=\bigoplus_{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{p}}H_{0}({}^{+}(F_{\bullet}^{M_{i_{1}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes F_{\bullet}^{M_{i_{p}}})^{\Im}[p-1])

for
p>0
. The complexes
\mathbb{S}^{\bullet}
and
\mathbb{P}_{\bullet}
are well defined (up to isomorphism, according to Remark
4.1
) via the Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequences (
1
) and (
2
). All the results above could then be extended along the same lines of proof.

4.4.
Multigraded Tor modules

We close the paper by relating the non-vanishing region of Tor modules of
R
-modules
M
against products and sums. Let
S
be a commutative unitary ring,
G
an abelian group, and
R=S[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
with a
G
-grading. By a graded
R
-module, we mean an
R
-module with a
G
-grading.

Definition 4.16
.

The support of a graded
R
-module
M
is

\textnormal{Supp}_{G}(M):=\{\gamma\in G:M_{\gamma}\neq 0\}.

Given a graded
R
-module
M
and a homogeneous ideal
I
of
R
,

\mathbb{T}_{j}^{I}(M):=\textnormal{Supp}_{G}(\textnormal{{Tor}}_{j}^{R}(M,R/I))

and
{\mathbb{T}}^{I}(M):=\cup_{j}\mathbb{T}_{j}^{I}(M)
.

Let
J\subseteq[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. For each set of variables
\mathbf{X}_{J}:=\{X_{i},\ i\in J\}
, write
B_{J}
for the ideal generated by these variables.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
M
be a graded
R
-module. If
J_{1},\ldots,J_{s}
are non empty subsets of
[n]
such that
J_{i}\cap J_{j}=\emptyset
for any
i\neq j
, then

\cup_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}}{\mathbb{T}}^{B_{J_{i_{1}}}\cdots B_{J_{i_{p}}}}(M)=\cup_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}}{\mathbb{T}}^{B_{J_{i_{1}}}+\cdots+B_{J_{i_{p}}}}(M).

Proof.

For any
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq s
, The Koszul complex
K_{\bullet}(\mathbf{X}_{J_{i_{1}}};R)\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}K_{\bullet}(\mathbf{X}_{J_{i_{p}}};R)\simeq K_{\bullet}(\mathbf{X}_{J_{i_{1}}}\cup\cdots\cup\mathbf{X}_{J_{i_{p}}};R)
is the minimal free resolution of
R/B_{J_{i_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup J_{i_{p}}}
which precisely means that the ideals
B_{J_{1}},\ldots,B_{J_{s}}
are strongly Tor-independent. Given
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{u}
, from the spectral sequence in Corollary
4.7

E_{p,q+p-1}^{1}=\oplus_{i_{1}\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{p}\leq i_{u}}\textnormal{{Tor}}^{R}_{q+p-1}(M,R/B_{j_{1}}+\cdots+B_{j_{p}})\Rightarrow\textnormal{{Tor}}_{q}^{R}(M,R/B_{i_{1}}\cdots B_{i_{u}})

we get

{\mathbb{T}}^{B_{i_{1}}\cdots B_{i_{u}}}(M)\subseteq\cup_{i_{1}\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{p}\leq i_{u}}{\mathbb{T}}^{B_{i_{1}}+\cdots+B_{i_{p}}}(M).

On the other hand, for each
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{u}
the spectral sequence in Corollary
4.11

E^{1}_{p,q-p+1}=\oplus_{i_{1}\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{p}\leq i_{u}}\textnormal{{Tor}}_{q-p+1}^{R}(M,R/B_{j_{1}}\cdots B_{j_{p}})\Rightarrow\textnormal{{Tor}}_{q}^{R}(M,R/B_{i_{1}}+\cdots+B_{i_{u}})

assures that
{\mathbb{T}}^{B_{i_{1}}+\ldots+B_{i_{u}}}(M)\subseteq\cup_{i_{1}\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{p}\leq i_{u}}{\mathbb{T}}^{B_{j_{1}}\cdots B_{j_{p}}}(M)
, whence the result.
∎
