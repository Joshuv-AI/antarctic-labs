Statistical Estimation of Monge Transport Maps via Brenier Potentials
Elsa Cazelles, Edouard Pauwels, Léo Portales

Abstract
We introduce and analyze a statistical estimator for Monge transport maps: solutions to the quadratic optimal transport problem in the Euclidean space. For absolutely continuous source measures, this map is uniquely defined as the gradient of a convex function, a result known as Brenier’s theorem. Without absolute continuity, the problem is relaxed, maps are replaced by coupling measures, and optimal couplings are supported on the subdifferential of a convex function, a Brenier potential. This characterization is the basis for our Monge transport map statistical estimator, for measures known only through finite samples. The resulting Brenier potential has a simple closed form expression based on the dual solution of the discrete sampled problem. In particular, our methodology does not rely on smoothness or continuity of the Monge transport map and requires no computation beyond primal-dual solutions of the discrete finite dimensional problem. We exhibit convergence rates for this estimator based on a new error bound for the quadratic optimal transport problem. In the semi-discrete setting, where the target measure is finitely supported, our estimator enjoys sharper convergence rates. Finally, using similar proof techniques, we provide novel convergence rate for empirical couplings.

Introduction

Optimal transport, as initially formulated by Monge, seeks a map which “pushes-forward” a source probability measure
\mu
onto a target probability measure
\nu
while minimizing the quadratic cost. The classical Monge problem is written over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as follows, over the class of measurable mappings
T

\underset{T_{\sharp}\mu=\nu}{\inf_{T\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}\|x-T(x)\|^{2}d\mu(x),

(1.1)

where
\sharp
denotes the push forward operation. The constraint can be equivalently formulated as
\int f(y)d\nu(y)=\int f(T(x))d\mu(x)
for all measurable
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
A solution to this problem, when it exists, will be called a
Monge map
.

Optimal transport is one of the key mathematical problems of the late 20-th century, both as an analytical tool and as a mathematical modeling framework with many applications
[
Villani, 2008
,
Santambrogio, 2015
,
Peyré and Cuturi, 2019
]
. We limit ourselves to the quadratic case, as in (
1.1
). Our question of interest is the statistical estimation of
T
for source and target
\mu,\nu
known only through independent samples, or equivalently, based on discrete empirical measures
\hat{\mu}_{n},\hat{\nu}_{m}
. We introduce a general estimator for
T
, and provide finite sample guaranties under mild assumptions on problem data: compact support of the source and target measures, and absolute continuity of the source measure.

Monge problem admits a (semi)dual formulation over the set of convex functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as follows,

\displaystyle\inf_{\varphi\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)d\mu(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi^{*}(y)d\nu(y),

(1.2)

where
\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the set of convex lower semicontinuous proper functions over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\varphi^{*}\colon y\mapsto\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-\varphi(x)
is the Legendre-Fenchel conjugate. A celebrated result of Brenier
[
Brenier, 1991
]
ensures that for an absolutely continous source measure
\mu
, the solution to (
1.1
) exists, is unique and is the (sub)gradient of a convex function
\varphi\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This function
\varphi
turns out to be an optimal solution to problem (
1.2
)
[
Santambrogio, 2015
]
. Solutions to (
1.2
) will be called Brenier potentials.

These elements are well known
[
Villani, 2008
,
Villani, 2021
,
Santambrogio, 2015
]
, and we provide a complete description in
Section

2
. We also expand on the discrete optimal transport problem, between finitely supported measures, with the corresponding finite dimensional linear programming formulation
[
Peyré and Cuturi, 2019
]
.

1.1
Overview of the main contributions

An explicit Brenier potential.

For discrete measures
\mu_{n}
and
\nu_{m}
, supported on
n
and
m
points respectively, we show that the dual problem (
1.2
) admits an explicit solution which takes the form of the maximum of
m
affine functions. The coefficients of these affine functions are directly deduced from the support points of the target
\nu_{m}
and the dual solution of the discrete optimal transport linear program, see Section
3.1
and in particular Definition
3.1
. This Brenier potential has basically the same computational cost as computing the discrete optimal transport problem, as most solvers provide primal-dual solutions. Its structure is reminiscent of the Laguerre tesselation occurring in semi-discrete optimal transport
[
Aurenhammer et al., 1998
]
, but in a discrete-discrete setting. This construction serves as a basis for our statistical estimator.

Finite sample guaranties.

In a statistical setting, given population source (which is absolutely continuous) and target probability measures
\mu,\nu
, and empirical measures
\hat{\mu}_{n}
,
\hat{\nu}_{m}
supported on
n
and
m
points respectively, we have the following under mild assumptions.

•

As known, the population problem (
1.1
) for
(\mu,\nu)
admits a solution
T=\nabla\bar{\varphi}
, where
\bar{\varphi}
solves (
1.2
).

•

For the discrete random problem between
\hat{\mu}_{n}
and
\hat{\nu}_{m}
, we have an explicit Brenier map estimator
\hat{\phi}
, solution to (
1.2
) as described in the previous paragraph.

It is therefore natural to consider
\nabla\hat{\phi}
as a statistical estimator for
\nabla\bar{\varphi}
. This estimator enjoys computational simplicity (closed form expression), without any parameter to estimate or tune.

The main statistical question to be addressed is that of the convergence of
\nabla\hat{\phi}
as the sample sizes
n
and
m
increase. Noting that the convex subdifferential (see
[
Rockafellar, 1970
]
) satisfies
\partial\hat{\phi}(x)=\{\nabla\hat{\phi}(x)\}
for almost all
x
, it is possible to invoke the main results of
[
Segers, 2022
]
to obtain that, almost surely,
\partial\hat{\phi}
converges graphically to
\partial\bar{\varphi}
, restricted to the interior of the support of
\mu
. This is a qualitative result, illustrating the fact that
\nabla\hat{\phi}
is a relevant estimator. However, the contribution of
[
Segers, 2022
]
remains qualitative and the underlying notion of convergence is difficult to explicit in functional analysis terms. We considerably extend this result for our specific estimator and provide a finite sample upper bound on the quantity
\|\nabla\hat{\phi}-\nabla\bar{\varphi}\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}
. This upper bound holds under mild assumptions: compact support of
\mu,\nu
, and upper bounded density of
\mu
. In particular we make no assumption regarding the convexity of the support of
\mu
or any lower bound on its density, and the population Monge map
\nabla\bar{\varphi}
may be discontinuous. This is our main statistical result, it is given in
Section

3.2
. A visualization of the estimator and its convergence is given in
Figure

.

.

A dual error bound.

The key analytical element which allows to devise our finite sample estimate is an error bound for Problem (
1.2
). We show in
Section

4
that for compactly supported probability measures
\mu,\nu
, the source
\mu
with an upper bounded density and
\bar{\varphi}
solution to (
1.2
), there is
c>0
such that for any convex function
\phi
in an appropriate subclass of
\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds that

\displaystyle c\|\nabla\phi-\nabla\bar{\varphi}\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}^{4}\leq\int\phi-\bar{\varphi}d\mu+\int\phi^{*}-\bar{\varphi}^{*}d\nu.

(1.3)

Choosing
\phi=\hat{\phi}
, our Brenier map estimator from empirical measures
\hat{\mu}_{n},\hat{\nu}_{m}
, the right hand side can be further upper bounded by
W_{1}(\mu,\hat{\mu}_{n})+W_{1}(\nu,\hat{\nu}_{m})
for which finite sample upper bounds exist in expectation in
[
Fournier and Guillin, 2015
]
, see
[
Fournier, 2023
]
for explicit multiplicative constants. This results in statistical rates roughly of order
O(n^{-\frac{1}{4d}}+m^{-\frac{1}{4d}})
. We do not know if these rates could be improved for the proposed estimator, or for other estimators. The closest lower bound is
n^{-\frac{1}{d}}
, but under considerably more restrictive conditions on the measures, see Section
3.3
.

The error bound is of independent interest for quadratic optimal transport beyond statistical estimation. It relates for example to stability properties for Monge maps
[
Gigli, 2011
]
with applications in machine learning
[
Mérigot et al., 2020
]
. The proof combines Carlier’s remainder for Fenchel-Young inequality
[
Carlier, 2023
]
with the covering number estimate from
[
Carlier et al., 2025
]
, which we use as a measure of discontinuity for gradients of Lipschitz convex functions.

Extensions.

The proposed framework and techniques are extended to provide refined statistical bounds for the semi-discrete optimal transport problem in
Section

3.4
. In
Section

3.5
, quantitative convergence guaranties of empirical optimal couplings toward optimal couplings are given, based on quantitative stability bounds in
Section

5
. The consistency of empirical coupling is due to
[
Segers, 2022
]
.

1.2
Related work

Statistical optimal transport.

Research on the topic has considerably expanded in the last decade. This includes many variations: semi-discrete, entropic regularization, convergence of the optimal transport cost, of the optimal map, etc. We focus on the estimation of quadratic optimal transport Monge maps. We refer to
[
Balakrishnan et al., 2025
]
for a recent review and will comment here on the specificity of our approach and the closest references.

The idea of estimating Monge maps based on the dual optimal transport formulation was explored in
[
Hütter and Rigollet, 2021
]
and expanded in
[
Manole et al., 2024
,
Divol et al., 2025
]
. This literature deploys the tools of non parametric statistical estimation to tackle the problem. The main drawback of this approach is that it relies on smoothness of the population transport map, which is required to be an element of a well identified function space, such as that of bi-Lipschitz diffeomorphisms. Regularity of optimal transport is a topic of notable mathematical difficulty and sufficient conditions impose considerable restrictions. The main specificity of our work with respect to this literature is that we do not directly apply nonparametric estimation to
\nabla\varphi
, but rather leverage a new error bound in (
1.3
). This allows to shift the regularity requirements from the Monge map
\nabla\varphi
to the Brenier potential
\varphi
and leverage known convergence rates for empirical measures
[
Fournier and Guillin, 2015
]
. As a consequence our main result holds under explicit and versatile assumptions on the problem: compacity of the supports, absolute continuity of the source, and an upper bound on its density. A striking consequence is that the target Monge map is not even required to be continuous. The only available occurrence of statistical convergence toward possibly discontinuous Monge maps is
[
Pooladian et al., 2023
]
which is limited to the semi-discrete setting. A more detailed discussion on these aspects is given in
Section

3.3
.

Estimators for Monge maps.

A diversity of Monge map estimators have been proposed in the literature with various statistical analyses. These include M-estimators
[
Hütter and Rigollet, 2021
,
Manole et al., 2024
,
Divol et al., 2025
,
Chewi et al., 2025
]
, entropic regularization
[
Seguy et al., 2018
,
Pooladian and Niles-Weed, 2021
]
, kernel based estimators
[
Perrot et al., 2016
,
Gunsilius, 2022
]
, input convex neural networks
[
Makkuva et al., 2020
]
, one nearest neighbors and barycentric projection
[
Manole et al., 2024
]
, convex extrapolation and smoothing
[
Paty et al., 2020
,
Hallin et al., 2021
,
De Lara et al., 2021
]
.

Interestingly, our proposed Brenier potential has not been considered explicitly. It is a natural estimator as it is the solution of Problem (
1.2
) with discrete measures and it admits a closed form solution based on the optimal transport linear program dual solutions. As discussed in
Remark

3.4
, our estimator corresponds to the limit as entropic regularization vanishes of the estimator proposed in
[
Seguy et al., 2018
]
and analyzed in
[
Pooladian and Niles-Weed, 2021
,
Pooladian et al., 2023
]
. Finally,
[
Vacher and Vialard, 2022
]
explores the distance between gradients of convex potentials and the dual loss, under smoothness condition on the source density, in a statistical setting.

Stability of optimal transport maps.

The error bound in (
1.3
) translates into a Hölder stability bound for Monge maps. This topic is of interest more broadly for optimal transport

[
Gigli, 2011
,
Mérigot et al., 2020
,
Li and Nochetto, 2021
,
Delalande and Merigot, 2023
,
Letrouit and Mérigot, 2024
]
. Our error bound directly implies a similar stability bound, allowing for variations of both source and target probability measures.
A more precise discussion of the relation to existing art is given in
Section

4.4
.

Semi-discrete optimal transport.

Our estimator is strongly related to Laguerre tesselation, adapted to a discrete setting, a notion which has very strong ties with semi-discrete optimal transport
[
Aurenhammer, 1987
]
. Similar piecewise linear estimators are studied in convex geometry
[
Gu et al., 2013
]
, in relation to optimal transport and its applications
[
Lei et al., 2019
]
.

Finally, we specify our results to the semi-discrete setting, that is for
\nu
a discrete probability measure, showing that it is possible to remove the exponential dependency in the ambient dimension
d
. This is in line with existing statistical results on this topic
[
Pooladian et al., 2023
,
Sadhu et al., 2024
]
. Statistical aspects for the semi-discrete setting are further discussed in
Section

3.4
.

Preliminaries on optimal transport

We briefly recall basic concepts in optimal transport as well as important notions for the paper.

2.1
Primal and dual problems

The problem in (
1.1
) does not always have a solution. The Kantorovich relaxation replaces maps by probability couplings with marginals
\mu
and
\nu
(
i.e
elements of
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
), as follows

\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}d\gamma(x,y).

(2.1)

Duality is typically expressed in terms of c-concave functions
[
Santambrogio, 2015
, Section 1.6.1]
. Focusing on the quadratic cost allows us to adopt a classical alternative: expressing duality with usual convexity notions. Both approaches are equivalent up to a change of variables
[
Santambrogio, 2015
, Proposition 1.21]
. Problem (
2.1
) admits the following dual

\sup_{\varphi\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{2}-\varphi(x)\right)d\mu(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{\|y\|^{2}}{2}-\varphi^{*}(y)\right)d\nu(y).

(2.2)

This formulation is sometimes called ”semi-dual”, as it has a single decision variable while there are two marginal constraints in the primal. When
\mu
is absolutely continuous, Brenier’s Theorem tells us that the optimal Monge map
T
in (
1.1
) is almost-everywhere the gradient of
\varphi\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, an optimal solution to (
2.2
), called a
Brenier potential
. In the absence of continuity when the measures are compactly supported, optimal couplings
\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
exist for the Kantorovich problem (
2.1
). Moreover such solution
\gamma
is supported on the graph of the subdifferential of
\varphi
,
i.e.

\supp\gamma\subset\graph(\partial\varphi)
, a solution to Problem (
2.2
). These are classical facts
[
Villani, 2008
,
Villani, 2021
,
Santambrogio, 2015
]
which we summarize in the following theorem. We provide a proof for the latter for completeness in
Section

A.2

2.2
Optimal transport between finitely supported measures

Our basic framework for statistical estimation is for finitely supported source and target measures. Throughout this section, we consider the special case when
\hat{\mu}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
,
\hat{\nu}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}\delta_{y_{j}}
, where
a_{i}>0
for
i=1,\ldots,n
,
b_{j}>0
for
j=1,\ldots,m
, such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}=1
, and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
,
y_{1},\ldots,\>y_{m}
are atoms in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The optimal transport problem between such discrete measures falls within the scope of
Theorem

2.1
, but can also be finitely represented, resulting in a linear program. This has been extensively studied, see for example
[
Peyré and Cuturi, 2019
]
for a recent account of computational aspects.

The primal problem (
PP
) is written as a finite dimensional linear program over matrices
[
Peyré and Cuturi, 2019
, Section 2.3]
:

\min\Big\{\langle C,P\rangle\>\Big|\>P\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n\times m},\>\sum_{j=1}^{m}P_{i,j}=a_{i},\>\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i,j}=b_{j},\>\forall i=1,\ldots,n,\>j=1,\ldots,m\Big\},

(PP-discr)

where
C\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
is a cost matrix defined as
C_{i,j}=\frac{1}{2}\|x_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n,\>j=1,\ldots,m
. This is obtained by identifying the matrix
P
above with a coupling
\gamma
between
\hat{\mu}
and
\hat{\nu}
as in
Theorem

2.1
, with the identification
\int f(x,y)d\gamma(x,y)=\sum_{i,j}f(x_{i},y_{j})P_{i,j}
for all measurable
f
.

The corresponding dual linear program is given as follows
[
Peyré and Cuturi, 2019
, Section 2.5]

\max\left\{\langle f,a\rangle+\langle g,b\rangle\Big|\>f\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,g\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\>f_{i}+g_{j}\leq C_{i,j},\>\forall i=1,\ldots,n,\>j=1,\ldots,m\right\}.

(DP-discr)

An application of strong duality ensures that the values of (
PP-discr
) and (
DP-discr
) are the same and both are attained
[
Peyré and Cuturi, 2019
, Proposition 2.4]
. Contrary to the coupling matrix
P
in (
PP-discr
), the dual variables in (
DP-discr
) do not have a direct interpretation in terms of convex functions as in
Theorem

2.1
.

3
Main results on the estimation of Brenier potentials and Brenier maps

3.1
An estimator based on discrete dual solutions

We are now in position to describe our Brenier potential estimator, which is based on the following explicit Brenier potential.

Definition 3.1

(Explicit Brenier potential)
.

Let
\hat{\mu},\hat{\nu}
be two discrete measures over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that
\hat{\mu}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
,
\hat{\nu}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}\delta_{y_{j}}
, where
a_{i}>0
,
i=1\ldots n
,
b_{j}>0
,
j=1,\ldots,m
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}=1
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\>y_{1},\ldots,\>y_{m}
atoms in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
g\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
be an optimal solution for (
DP-discr
), the associated Brenier potential is given by

\varphi_{g}\colon x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\max_{k=1,\ldots,m}\langle x,y_{k}\rangle+g_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{k}\|^{2}.

(3.1)

Complementary slackness for linear programming allows to verify the optimality condition from
Theorem

2.1
.

Lemma 3.2

(Optimality of the explicit potential)
.

Let
\hat{\mu},\hat{\nu}
be as in
Equation

3.1
, let
P
be an optimal matrix in the primal (
PP-discr
) and
(f,g)
be an optimal pair of dual variables in (
DP-discr
). Then

\displaystyle P_{i,j}>0\implies y_{j}\in\partial\varphi_{g}(x_{i})

where
\varphi_{g}
is given in
Equation

3.1
. As a consequence
\varphi_{g}
is optimal for (
DP
) with
\mu=\hat{\mu}
,
\nu=\hat{\nu}
, and

\displaystyle\min_{\varphi\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\int\varphi d\hat{\mu}+\int\varphi^{*}d\hat{\nu}=\min_{g\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\,\int\varphi_{g}d\hat{\mu}+\int\varphi_{g}^{*}d\hat{\nu}.

Proof.

The constraint in (
DP-discr
) writes for all
k=1,\ldots,m
,
i=1,\ldots,n
,
f_{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|x_{i}-y_{k}\|-g_{k}
.
For any
i=1,\ldots,n,\>j=1,\ldots,m
such that
P_{i,j}>0
, complementary slackness
[
Peyré and Cuturi, 2019
, Proposition 3.2]
implies that
f_{i}+g_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\|x_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2}
, and
f_{i}=\min_{k=1,\ldots,m}\frac{1}{2}\|x_{i}-y_{k}\|^{2}-g_{k}
with minimizing index
j
. This results in the following implications.

\displaystyle\qquad P_{i,j}>0

\displaystyle\implies\min_{k=1,\ldots,m}\frac{1}{2}\|x_{i}-y_{k}\|^{2}-g_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\|x_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2}-g_{j}

\displaystyle\iff\max_{k=1,\ldots,m}\langle x_{i},y_{k}\rangle-\frac{\|y_{k}\|^{2}}{2}+g_{k}=\langle x_{i},y_{j}\rangle-\frac{\|y_{j}\|^{2}}{2}+g_{j}

\displaystyle\iff\varphi_{g}(x_{i})=\langle x_{i},y_{j}\rangle-\frac{\|y_{j}\|^{2}}{2}+g_{j}\quad\implies\quad y_{j}\in\partial\varphi_{g}(x_{i}),

where we used
\partial\varphi_{g}(x_{i})=\conv\{y_{j},\,\varphi_{g}(x_{i})=\langle x_{i},y_{j}\rangle-\frac{\|y_{j}\|^{2}}{2}+g_{j}\}
.
We identify
P
with an optimal coupling
\gamma\in\Pi(\hat{\mu},\hat{\nu})
so that
P_{i,j}>0\iff(x_{i},y_{j})\in\supp(\gamma)
. This allows us to use the third point of Theorem
2.1
showing that
\varphi_{g}
is an optimal Brenier potential for (
DP
).
∎

The potential
\varphi_{g}
in
Equation

3.1
is convex and globally Lipschitz. As such, its gradient is well defined Lebesgue almost everywhere, for example using Rademacher’s theorem.
Setting for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
I(x)=\{j=1,\ldots,m|\varphi_{g}(x)=\langle x,y_{j}\rangle+g_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{j}\|^{2}\}
, the (non-empty) set of active indices, we have from classical results (see
e.g.

[
Rockafellar, 1970
, Theorem 23.9]
),
\partial\varphi_{g}(x)=\conv\{y_{j},\,j\in I(x)\}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

\partial\varphi_{g}
is a singleton almost everywhere and any selection is a Monge map for discrete measures. In what follows we will consider the following simple selection.

Definition 3.3

(Monge map for discrete measures)
.

Let
\hat{\mu},\hat{\nu}
be as in
Equation

3.1
, and let
(f,g)
be optimal in (
DP-discr
).
Any selection
\hat{T}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that
\hat{T}(x)\subset\partial\varphi_{g}(x)
is a Monge map for discrete measures. For example,

\hat{T}(x)=\frac{\sum_{j\in I(x)}y_{j}}{\sum_{j\in I(x)}1},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In the coming sections, we will consider the Monge map defined in
Definition

3.3
for empirical probability measures
\hat{\mu}_{n},\hat{\nu}_{m}
, which defines our proposed estimator.

Remark 3.4
.

The estimator proposed in
[
Seguy et al., 2018
]
and analyzed in
[
Pooladian and Niles-Weed, 2021
,
Pooladian et al., 2023
]
is based on the entropic regularized version of (
PP-discr
), a widespread approach in numerical optimal transport

[
Cuturi, 2013
,
Peyré and Cuturi, 2019
]
. With entropic regularization, the dual in (
DP-discr
) remains very similar, the constraint being replaced by an exponential penalty term in the functional. Using the main result of
[
Cominetti and Martín, 1994
]
, see also
[
Genevay et al., 2016
,
Weed, 2018
]
, as the regularization penalty parameter
\epsilon
vanishes, the primal-dual solutions of the entropic regularized problem converge to primal-dual solutions of the unregularized problem. Combining this with asymptotic properties of exponential weights, the estimator in
Definition

3.3
is exactly the limit as
\epsilon\to 0
of the estimator of
[
Seguy et al., 2018
,
Pooladian and Niles-Weed, 2021
,
Pooladian et al., 2023
]
based on entropic regularization.

From a practical perspective, the main advantage of the estimator in
Equation

3.1
compared to entropic regularization is that there is no regularization parameter
\epsilon
to tune, and that it bypasses the hurdles of running a variant of the Sinkhorn algorithm for small
\epsilon
, typically required for transport map estimation, a notorious numerical issue
[
Peyré and Cuturi, 2019
,
Chhaibi et al., 2025
]
. On the other hand, having one less hyperparameter to tune may be a disadvantage as a well tuned
\epsilon
should perform at least as good as the vanishing limit.

3.2
Statistical setting and convergence of the Monge map estimator

In this section, we consider two compactly supported population measures, the source having a density. In a statistical setting, they are approximated by empirical measures, obtained from i.i.d samples, as summarized in the following.

Assumption 1
.

Let
R>0
and
M>0
be scalars such that
\mu
,
\nu
are compactly supported in
B_{R}
, and
\mu
has density upper-bounded by
M
. For any positive integers
n
and
m
, consider i.i.d random samples
X_{1}\ldots,X_{n}
and
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}
with respective distribution
\mu
and
\nu
. We then define the empirical probability measures
\hat{\mu}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{X_{i}}
and
\hat{\nu}_{m}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{Y_{i}}
. The Monge map estimator is
\hat{T}_{n,m}
as in
Definition

3.3
for empirical discrete measures
\hat{\mu}_{n}
and
\hat{\nu}_{m}
.

We now state our main result, its proof is an application of
Corollary

4.7
, a stability bound given in Section
4
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Under
1
for all
M>0,\>R>0
and
d\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
there exists
C>0
such that the following holds. Let
\bar{\varphi}
be an optimal Brenier potential, optimal in (
DP
) for measures
\mu
and
\nu
. For measures
\hat{\mu}_{n}
and
\hat{\nu}_{m}
,
n
,
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)-\hat{T}_{n,m}(x)\|d\mu(x)\right]\leq C\begin{cases}\left(n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2}\right)^{1/4}&\text{if}\>d=1,\\
\left(n^{-1/2}\log(1+n)+m^{-1/2}\log(1+m)\right)^{1/4}&\text{if}\>d=2,\\
\left(n^{-1/d}+m^{-1/d}\right)^{1/4}&\text{if }d>2.\end{cases}

Proof.

From Definition
3.3
there exists
g\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and a function
\varphi_{g}:x\mapsto\max_{i=1,\ldots,m}\langle x,y_{i}\rangle+g_{i}-\frac{\|y_{i}\|^{2}}{2}
for which
\hat{T}_{n,m}
is a selection of its subdifferential. This function is globally
R
-Lipschitz (in particular
2R
-Lipschitz on
B_{2R}
). We can thus apply
Corollary

4.7
to this Brenier potential along with Jensen’s inequality and obtain

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)-\hat{T}_{n,m}(x)\|d\mu(x)\right)\right]^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)-\hat{T}_{n,m}(x)\|d\mu(x)\right)^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C^{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(W_{1}(\mu,\hat{\mu}_{n})+W_{1}(\nu,\hat{\nu}_{m})\right)\right].

where
C
is the multiplicative constant of Corollary
4.7
.
We now apply
[
Fournier and Guillin, 2015
, Main Theorem]
. For any integer
q\geq 3
we have the following inequality

\mathbb{E}\left[W_{1}(\mu,\hat{\mu}_{n})\right]\leq K_{d,q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|x\|^{q}d\mu(x)\right)^{1/q}\times\begin{cases}n^{-1/2}+n^{-1+1/q}&\text{if}\>d=1,\\
n^{-1/2}\log(1+n)+n^{-1+1/q}&\text{if}\>d=2,\\
n^{-1/d}+n^{-1+1/q}&\text{if }d>2\end{cases}

where
K_{d,q}>0
is a constant depending only on
d
and
q
given in
[
Fournier and Guillin, 2015
]
. We choose
q=3
for example and upper bound the dominated term
n^{-2/3}
by
n^{-1/2}
. This yields the result.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

The previous theorem can easily be adapted to the case where only one measure is empirical. For example, in Section
3.4
, we consider the semi-discrete case in which
\mu
is approximated by samples and
\nu
is a fixed discrete probability measure.

Remark 3.7
.

Let us consider the case where
\mu
admits an
L^{1}
Poincaré inequality, in the sense of
[
Bakry et al., 2008
, Equation (1.2)]
. This holds true for example if
\mu
has a log-concave density
\exp(-V)
for a
C^{2}
, convex lower bounded
V

[
Bakry et al., 2008
, Corollary 1.9]
or a bounded density away from
0
on a convex domain
[
Acosta and Durán, 2004
]
. In these cases, the explicit empirical Brenier potential in
Equation

3.1
converges to a Lipschitz optimal Brenier potential
\varphi
from
Theorem

2.1
, up to an additive term, at the same rate as in
Theorem

3.5
. Indeed, any Lipschitz function can be approximated, together with its gradient, by a smooth function.
Theorem

3.5
allows to control the
L^{1}
distance of gradients, and Poincaré inequality gives, up to an additive constant, a control of the
L^{1}
distance of potentials. The resulting rates on convergence of potentials are probably suboptimal.

3.3
Comparison with existing art on Monge map estimation

For simplicity, we discuss the case
n=m
and
d>2
corresponding to case 3 of
Theorem

3.5
. Our convergence rate is related to that of the empirical Wasserstein distance. It has been established in
[
Fournier and Guillin, 2015
]
that
\mathbb{E}[W_{1}(\mu,\hat{\mu}_{n})]=O\left(n^{-\frac{1}{d}}\right)
, and this rate is attained for the uniform distribution on the hypercube. Our rate is similar, up to the
1/4
exponent, which arises from the stability result of
Corollary

4.7
(see also
Section

4.4
). The literature on convergence rates of transport map estimators is vast, we start with
[
Manole et al., 2024
]
which describes
n^{-1/d}
rates in
L^{2}
norm. They are obtained under the following assumption.

Assumption 2

(Monge diffeomorphism assumption)
.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be two compactly supported measures. There exists an optimal Brenier potential in (
DP
) whose gradient is a bi-Lipschitz diffeomorphism.

Remark 3.8
.

In
[
Manole et al., 2024
]
this assumption is written as a condition on the Hessian of the potential given by
\frac{1}{\lambda}I_{d}\preceq\nabla^{2}\varphi\preceq\lambda I_{d}
, for some
\lambda>0
.

The obtained rate in
[
Manole et al., 2024
]
is min-max optimal against the Monge diffeomorphisms class. Several estimators are proposed. One is based on one-nearest neighbor combined with barycentric projection, computationally efficient, but not the gradient of a convex function. The second is a least-squares estimator constrained to the class of gradients of convex functions, with a computational overhead compared to mere computation of the empirical Wasserstein distance, and in addition, for which
\lambda
needs to be known.

In this context, our estimator
\hat{T}_{n,m}
and its analysis in
Theorem

3.5
has substantial advantages described in the following.

1.

Since
\hat{T}_{n,m}
is the sub-gradient of a convex function, it serves as a natural estimator for Monge maps as it is the smallest class of functions containing all such maps.

2.

It does not require any computation beyond dual optimal
g\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
in
Equation

3.1
from the discrete dual problem. This is given as a byproduct for many solvers.

3.

Our assumptions on the population Brenier potentials are much more applicable compared to
2
. In particular we do not even require continuity of the Monge map. We are not aware of any quantitative analysis in this setting.

Yet, our analysis retains statistical rates similar to the Wasserstein rate up to the
1/4
exponent which is independent of the dimension. It would be interesting to know if this rate could be further refined to
n^{-1/d}
for our estimator under
2
, this question is left for future work.

Beyond
[
Manole et al., 2024
]
, let us mention other works. The first contribution to consider statistical estimation of Monge maps based on the dual transport problem is
[
Hütter and Rigollet, 2021
]
, which also works under
2
. The authors establish min-max results for transport map estimators whose convergence rate approaches
n^{-1/2}
in
L^{2}
norm for Monge maps of high differentiability order. These estimators become computationally expensive as the dimension grows. Another example is
[
Pooladian and Niles-Weed, 2021
]
which considers entropic transport maps, and
[
Divol et al., 2025
]
for estimation in abstract function spaces (e.g. shallow neural networks and RKHS), both under
2
. We are not aware of any quantitative analysis beyond this assumption, in particular allowing for possibly discontinuous Monge transport maps, except
[
Pooladian et al., 2023
]
which is limited to the semi-discrete case.

Let us now discuss explicit sufficient conditions on the source and target measures ensuring that
2
, given in abstract form, holds true. This is actually one of the important challenges in optimal transport beyond statistical estimation, see
[
Hütter and Rigollet, 2021
, Remark 3]
,
[
Manole et al., 2024
, Section 2]
and
[
Villani, 2008
, Section 12]
. The most widely known sufficient conditions on the measures is Caffarelli’s celebrated result on smoothness of Monge maps
[
Caffarelli, 1992a
,
Caffarelli, 1992b
,
Caffarelli, 1996
]
(summarized in
[
Villani, 2008
, Theorem 12.50]
). It states that, if both the source and target measures are supported on compact convex sets with Hölder continuous densities, bounded away from
+\infty
and
0
, then the Monge diffeomorphism assumption holds true. The scope of applicability is much narrower compared to ours: compactly supported measures, one of them with an upper bounded density.

3.4
The semi-discrete case

In this section, we consider an absolutely continuous source measure
\mu
and a fixed discrete target measure
\nu
, a setting known as semi-discrete optimal transport, as summarized in the following assumption.

Assumption 3
.

Let
R>0
and
M>0
,
\mu
,
\nu
be supported in
B_{R}
, such that
\mu
has density upper-bounded by
M
. Assume further that
\nu=\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}\delta_{y_{i}}
for some fixed integer
m>1
, support points
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and weight vector
b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}>0
such that
\sum_{i=j}^{m}b_{j}=1
. For any positive integers
n
, consider i.i.d random samples
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
distributed according to
\mu
. We then define the empirical measure
\hat{\mu}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{X_{i}}
. Our Monge map estimator is
\hat{T}_{n}
as in
Definition

3.3
for discrete measures
\hat{\mu}_{n}
and
\nu
.

Using similar ideas as in
Lemma

3.2
, the dual formulation in
Theorem

2.1
in the semi-discrete setting has a finite dimensional equivalent expression. This type of argument is typical of the semi-discrete setting
[
Lévy, 2024
]
and is most often presented with Kantorovitch potentials. We use here the dual framework of
Theorem

2.1
and present proof details for completeness.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\nu
be as in
3
, both with compact support in
B_{R}
. For any
g\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, let
\varphi_{g}\colon x\mapsto\max_{k=1,\ldots,m}\langle x,y_{k}\rangle+g_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{k}\|^{2}
as in
Equation

3.1
.
The following three minimization problems have the same value, each of them being attained:

\displaystyle\min_{\varphi\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\int\varphi d\mu+\int\varphi^{*}d\nu

(3.2)

\displaystyle\min_{g\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle\int\varphi_{g}d\mu+\int\varphi_{g}^{*}d\nu

(3.3)

\displaystyle\min_{g\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle\int\varphi_{g}d\mu+\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|y_{j}\|^{2}-g_{j}\right).

(3.4)

Furthermore, for
g\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
optimal in (
3.4
), we have
\varphi^{*}_{g}(y_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\|y_{j}\|^{2}-g_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,m
, and
g_{j}
can be chosen in
[-3R^{2},3R^{2}]
, for all
j=1,\ldots,m
, with
g_{m}=0
.

Proof.

For any
g\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\varphi_{g}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have for all
j=1,\ldots,m

\displaystyle\varphi_{g}^{*}(y_{j})

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle y_{j},x\right\rangle-\varphi_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle y_{j},x\right\rangle-\max_{k=1,\ldots,m}\left\{\left\langle x,y_{k}\right\rangle+g_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{k}\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|y_{j}\|^{2}-g_{j}+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle y_{j},x\right\rangle+g_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{j}\|^{2}-\max_{k=1,\ldots,m}\left\{\left\langle x,y_{k}\right\rangle+g_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{k}\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|y_{j}\|^{2}-g_{j}.

(3.5)

Furthermore, if there is
x
such that
\varphi_{g}(x)=\left\langle x,y_{j}\right\rangle+g_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{j}\|^{2}
, then (
3.4
) is actually an equality.

Let us show that (
3.2
)
=
(
3.3
).
Theorem

2.1
ensures that the infimum in (
3.2
) is attained. Furthermore, we obviously have (
3.3
)
\geq
(
3.2
).
Consider an optimal
\varphi\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for (
3.2
). It must hold that
\max_{j}\varphi^{*}(y_{j})<+\infty
. For all
j
, set
g_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\|y_{j}\|^{2}-\varphi^{*}(y_{j})
we have

\displaystyle\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{y}\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-\varphi^{*}(y)\geq\max_{k=1,\ldots,m}\left\langle x,y_{k}\right\rangle-\varphi^{*}(y_{k})=\varphi_{g}(x)

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(y_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|y_{j}\|^{2}-g_{j}\geq\varphi_{g}^{*}(y_{j})

\displaystyle\forall j=1,\ldots,m,

where we used (
3.4
).
We deduce that (
3.2
)
\geq
(
3.3
), so that both value are equal, and the infimum in (
3.3
) is also attained.

Let us now show that (
3.3
)
=
(
3.4
).
First, (
3.4
) ensures that
\eqref{eq:semiDiscreteSemiDual2}\leq\eqref{eq:semiDiscreteSemiDual3}
. The proof of equality follows by the following claim, for example applied to an optimal
\bar{g}
in (
3.3
).

Claim 3.10
.

For any
\bar{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, there exists
g\geq\bar{g}
coordinatewise such that
\varphi_{g}=\varphi_{\bar{g}}
and (
3.4
) is an equality for
\varphi_{g}
for all
j=1,\ldots,m
. Furthermore,
g_{j}>\bar{g}_{j}
for all
j
such that (
3.4
) is not an equality for
\varphi_{\bar{g}}
.

Let us now prove the claim. Set
\bar{J}=\{j=1,\ldots,m|\,\exists x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\varphi_{\bar{g}}(x)=\left\langle x,y_{j}\right\rangle+\bar{g}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{j}\|^{2}\}
. For all
j\in\bar{J}
, (
3.4
) is an equality and we may set
x_{j}
such that
\varphi_{\bar{g}}(x_{j})=\left\langle x_{j},y_{j}\right\rangle+\bar{g}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{j}\|^{2}
. Fix any
q\not\in\bar{J}
, it holds that (
3.4
) is strict, and actually

\displaystyle\min_{x}\,\varphi_{\bar{g}}(x)-\left\langle x,y_{q}\right\rangle-\bar{g}_{q}+\frac{1}{2}\|y_{q}\|^{2}>0,

since the infimum of a polyhedral convex function is attained (see
[
Rockafellar, 1970
, Chapter 19]
, the infimum is expressed as a bounded linear program whose value is always attained). We may denote the argmin by
\bar{x}
and set

\displaystyle g_{q}=\min_{x}\,\varphi_{\bar{g}}(x)-\left\langle x,y_{q}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|y_{q}\|^{2}=\varphi_{\bar{g}}(\bar{x})-\left\langle\bar{x},y_{q}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|y_{q}\|^{2}>\bar{g}_{q},

and
g_{k}\colon=\bar{g}_{k}
for
k\neq q
. We have
\varphi_{\bar{g}}\leq\varphi_{g}
by construction, since
g\geq\bar{g}
coordinatewise.

For any
x
such that
\varphi_{g}(x)>\langle x,y_{q}\rangle+g_{q}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{q}\|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=\max_{k\neq q}\langle x,y_{k}\rangle+g_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{k}\|^{2}=\max_{k\neq q}\langle x,y_{k}\rangle+\bar{g}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{k}\|^{2}\leq\max_{k}\langle x,y_{k}\rangle+\bar{g}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{k}\|^{2}=\varphi_{\bar{g}}(x).

For any
x
such that
\varphi_{g}(x)=\langle x,y_{q}\rangle+g_{q}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{q}\|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=\langle x,y_{q}\rangle+\min_{z}\,\varphi_{\bar{g}}(z)-\left\langle z,y_{q}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|y_{q}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{q}\|^{2}\leq\varphi_{\bar{g}}(x).

Thus
\varphi_{g}=\varphi_{\bar{g}}
.

In addition for all

We now prove the last statement of the Lemma. Let

Comparison with existing semi-discrete Monge map estimators.

A semi-discrete Monge map estimator based on the entropic optimal transport map between the empirical source and target measures is studied in
[
Pooladian et al., 2023
]
. Similarly to
Section

3.2
, their analysis assumes that the source population measure admits a density that is bounded both from above and below on its convex compact support. Under this assumption, they establish convergence rate in
L_{2}(\mu)
of order
O\left(\left(m/n\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\right)
which they show to be minimax optimal in
n
.

By contrast, our analysis results in a convergence rate of order
O\left(\left(m/n\right)^{\frac{1}{8}}\right)
in
L^{1}(\mu)
without requiring convexity of the support nor that the density of the source measure is lower bounded away from zero.
We do not know whether this rate is minimax optimal under these general assumptions. Moreover, it remains unclear whether our convergence rate analysis could be improved under the stronger regularity assumptions considered in
[
Pooladian et al., 2023
]
. Addressing both questions is left for future work. Finally, our estimator compares favorably to the baseline, one nearest neighbor estimator, which suffers from the curse of dimensionality in the semi-discrete setting, as described in
[
Pooladian et al., 2023
]
.

and
\nu
, optimal for (
DP
) as in
Theorem

2.1
. Then for any
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{2R,2R}
(as in
Lemma

2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\int\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)-\nabla\phi(x)\|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int\phi d\mu+\phi^{*}d\nu-\int\bar{\varphi}d\mu+\bar{\varphi}^{*}d\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{4}},

where
C=\max\left(268\sqrt{R},4(8R^{3}MC_{R,d})^{1/4}\right)
with
C_{R,d}
defined in
Theorem

4.3
.

Proof.

We want to establish and upper bound on
\delta:=\int\phi d\mu+\int\phi^{*}d\nu-\left(\int\bar{\varphi}d\mu+\int\bar{\varphi}^{*}d\nu\right)
using
Theorem

4.3
, see
Remark

4.4
.
Let
v\in\partial\phi(0)
, we have
\|v\|\leq 2R
since
\phi
is
2R
-Lipschitz on
B_{2R}
. Since
\phi^{*}
is also
2R
Lipschitz on
B_{2R}
, we have for all
y\in\supp\nu\subset B_{R}
:

\displaystyle\phi^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=\phi^{*}(y)-\phi^{*}(v)+\phi^{*}(v)\leq 2R\|y-v\|+\phi^{*}(v)\leq 6R^{2}+\phi^{*}(v)

We also have
0\in\partial\phi^{*}(v)
, so that
v\in\argmin\phi^{*}
, and by the Fenchel-Young identity we obtain
\phi^{*}(v)=-\phi(0)
and thus for all
x\in\supp\mu\subset B_{R}
and all
y\in\supp\nu
:

\displaystyle\phi(x)+\phi^{*}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\phi(x)+6R^{2}+\phi^{*}(v)=6R^{2}+\phi(x)-\phi(0)\leq 6R^{2}+2R\|x\|\leq 8R^{2}.

By
Lemma

2.2
, we may assume that
\bar{\varphi}\in\mathcal{C}_{2R,2R}
. We may repeat the same argument for
\bar{\varphi}
to obtain
\delta\leq 16R^{2}:=\bar{\delta}
. Upon applying Theorem
4.3
for
q=1
, we have in case 1, with
L=2R
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\nabla\phi(x)-\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)\|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{\frac{L}{R}}\delta^{1/2}+\frac{32}{R}\delta\leq\left(8\sqrt{2}\bar{\delta}^{1/4}+\frac{32}{R}\bar{\delta}^{3/4}\right)\delta^{1/4}\leq 268\sqrt{R}\delta^{1/4}

And in case 2, we have
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\nabla\phi(x)-\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)\|d\mu(x)\leq 4(8R^{3}MC_{R,d})^{1/4}\delta^{1/4}
, which concludes.
∎

Corollary

4.6
allows to obtain a stability bound for gradient of Brenier potentials.

Corollary 4.7
.

For any
d,R,M
there is
C
positive such that for any
\mu,\nu,\bar{\varphi}
as in
Corollary

4.6
, for any
\hat{\mu}
,
\hat{\nu}
supported on
B_{R}
with
\phi_{0}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
an associated Brenier potential which is
2R
-Lipschitz on
B_{2R}
, we have

\displaystyle\int\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)-\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(W_{1}(\mu,\hat{\mu})+W_{1}(\nu,\hat{\nu})\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Proof.

From
Lemma

A.1
, as in
Lemma

2.2
, there is a Brenier potential
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{2R,2R}
, such that
\phi=\phi_{0}
on
B_{2R}
and
\phi^{*}=\phi_{0}^{*}
on
\supp\mu
. We deduce that
\nabla\phi=\nabla\phi_{0}
on
\supp\mu
.
Let
C>0
be as in
Corollary

4.6
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C^{4}}\left(\int\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)-\nabla\phi_{0}(x)\|d\mu(x)\right)^{4}=\frac{1}{C^{4}}\left(\int\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)-\nabla\phi(x)\|d\mu(x)\right)^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\int\phi d\mu+\phi^{*}d\nu-\int\bar{\varphi}d\mu-\bar{\varphi}^{*}d\nu

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\int\phi d\mu+\phi^{*}d\nu-\int\bar{\varphi}d\mu-\bar{\varphi}^{*}d\nu+\int\bar{\varphi}d\hat{\mu}+\bar{\varphi}^{*}d\hat{\nu}-\int\phi d\hat{\mu}-\phi^{*}d\hat{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\phi-\bar{\varphi}d(\mu-\hat{\mu})+\int\phi^{*}-\bar{\varphi}^{*}d(\nu-\hat{\nu})\ \leq\ 3R(W_{1}(\mu,\hat{\mu})+W_{1}(\nu,\hat{\nu})),

where the first inequality is by
Corollary

4.6
, the second is because
\phi
is optimal for the dual problem associated to
\hat{\mu}
and
\hat{\nu}
, and the last one is because
\phi-\bar{\varphi}
and
\phi^{*}-\bar{\varphi}^{*}
are both
3R
Lipschitz so that
[
Villani, 2021
, Theorem 1.14]
can be applied.
∎

4.4
Connection with existing literature on stability of optimal transport map

Corollary

4.7
relates to the stability of gradient of Brenier potentials. This is most often stated in terms of
L^{2}(\mu)
providing an Hilbertian embedding of the Wasserstein space. We present the
L^{2}
version of
Corollary

4.7
based on
Theorem

4.3
.

Corollary 4.8
.

In the same setting as
Corollary

4.7
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}-\nabla\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(W_{1}(\mu,\hat{\mu})+W_{1}(\nu,\hat{\nu})\right)^{\frac{1}{5}}.

Sketch of proof.

The proof follows the same reasoning as
Corollary

4.6
and
Corollary

4.7
. The term
\delta:=\int\phi d\mu+\int\phi^{*}d\nu-\left(\int\bar{\varphi}d\mu+\int\bar{\varphi}^{*}d\nu\right)
can be bounded by
16R^{2}
similarly to
Corollary

4.7
.
Theorem

4.3
with
q=2
leads to
\delta
terms with exponents between
1/5
and
1
. Boundedness of
\delta
and the same arguments as in
Corollary

4.7
show that, up to constants the smallest exponent,
1/5
dominates.
∎

This result generalizes the ideas of Monge embedding, extensively studied for a fixed source measure
[
Gigli, 2011
,
Mérigot et al., 2020
,
Delalande and Merigot, 2023
,
Carlier et al., 2025
]
. Indeed
Theorem

4.3
implies that

\displaystyle\|T_{\nu_{1}}-T_{\nu_{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq CW_{1}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})^{1/5}

(4.6)

where
T_{\nu_{1}},T_{\nu_{2}}
are Monge maps between
\mu
and
\nu_{1}
, and
\mu
and
\nu_{2}
respectively. In this Monge embedding context,
Corollary

4.8
has several interesting properties: it allows both the source and target to move, the Hölder exponent is
1/5
and it does not require convexity of the support or lower boundedness of the density (it does not require a Poincarré-Wirtinger inequality).

These can be discussed in light of existing literature. First, we are not aware of such stability results allowing both the source and target to move. Second
[
Delalande and Merigot, 2023
, Theorem 4.2]
provides a
1/6
exponent for
\mu
supported on a convex compact set with density bounded away from zero and infinity. These conditions were relaxed in
[
Letrouit and Mérigot, 2024
]
. Let us emphasize that the cited results are deeper than the Monge embedding discussed in this section: they also provide stability for the Brenier potentials (not only their gradients), which require a form of connectedness of the domain with nonvanishing density (hence the Poincarré inequality requirement).

Additionally, the exponent in
Equation

4.6
cannot exceed
1/2
. This was shown in
[
Gigli, 2011
]
, a different example being provided in
[
Mérigot et al., 2020
, Proposition 2.1]
. The
1/2
exponent is achieved under
2
, see
[
Hütter and Rigollet, 2021
, Proposition 10]
, and the optimal exponent under broader assumptions is not known. Finally, let us note that an improvement of the exponent in
Corollary

4.7
or
Corollary

4.8
would directly result in an improvement in
Theorem

3.5
.

5
A stability bound for optimal couplings

This section presents a stability result for couplings. We start with two technical lemmas and then present the main result of the section.

5.1
Preliminary results on perturbation and stability of optimal couplings

The following lemma allows to perturb the support of optimal couplings with a control in Wasserstein distance.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\tilde{\mu}
be compactly supported in
B_{R}
and
\tilde{\phi},\bar{\varphi}
be
L
Lipschitz on
B_{R}
. Assume that
\tilde{\nu}
is another measure, such that
\tilde{\gamma}
is the optimal coupling between
\tilde{\mu}
and
\tilde{\nu}
supported on the graph of
\partial\tilde{\phi}
. Then there is a coupling
\gamma_{0}
, supported on the graph of
\partial\bar{\varphi}
with first marginal
\mu_{0}
supported in
B_{2R}
, such that

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\tilde{\gamma},\gamma_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R^{2}+4L^{2}}{2LR}\left(\int\bar{\varphi}-\tilde{\phi}d\tilde{\mu}+\int\bar{\varphi}^{*}-\tilde{\phi}^{*}d\tilde{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\mu_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{2L}\left(\int\bar{\varphi}-\tilde{\phi}d\tilde{\mu}+\int\bar{\varphi}^{*}-\tilde{\phi}^{*}d\tilde{\nu}\right)

Proof.

Fix any
x,y\in\supp\ \tilde{\gamma}
,
\lambda>0
, set
z_{\lambda}(x,y)=\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\bar{\varphi}}(x+\lambda y)
and
T(x,y)=(z_{\lambda}(x,y),y+(x-z_{\lambda}(x,y))/\lambda)
, characterized as

\displaystyle y+\frac{x-z_{\lambda}(x,y)}{\lambda}\in\partial\bar{\varphi}(z_{\lambda}(x,y)).

(5.1)

We have
\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\bar{\varphi}}(x+\lambda v)=x
for any
v\in\partial\bar{\varphi}(x)
, the
\mathrm{prox}
mapping being
1
-Lipschitz, we deduce

\displaystyle\|z_{\lambda}(x,y)-x\|\leq\lambda\|y-v\|\leq 2\lambda L,

where we have used Lipschicity of
\tilde{\phi}
and
\bar{\varphi}
in a neighborhood of
x\in\supp\tilde{\mu}
, so that
y\in\partial\tilde{\phi}(x)
and
v\in\partial\bar{\varphi}(x)
have norm at most
L
.
Now, choose
\lambda=R/(2L)
, we have
\|z_{\lambda}(x,y)-x\|\leq R
and
z_{\lambda}(x,y)\in B(0,2R)
.

Since
\tilde{\phi}
is a Brenier potential for the pair
\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\nu}
, we have for
\tilde{\gamma}
almost all
x,y
,
\tilde{\phi}(x)+\tilde{\phi}^{*}(y)=\left\langle x,y\right\rangle
. Using Carlier’s inequality in
Theorem

4.2
and the definition of
z_{\lambda}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int\bar{\varphi}-\tilde{\phi}d\tilde{\mu}+\int\bar{\varphi}^{*}-\tilde{\phi}^{*}d\tilde{\nu}

\displaystyle=\int\bar{\varphi}(x)+\bar{\varphi}^{*}(y)-\left\langle x,y\right\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}(x,y)\geq\int\frac{1}{\lambda}\|x-z_{\lambda}(x,y)\|^{2}d\tilde{\gamma}(x,y)

(5.2)

Set
\gamma_{0}=T_{\sharp}\tilde{\gamma}
and
\mu_{0}
its first marginal. We have that
\supp\,\gamma_{0}\subset\graph\ \partial\bar{\varphi}
by construction, see (
5.1
) and
\mathrm{Supp}\ \mu_{0}\subset B(0,2R)
by the argument above. We also have
\|T(x,y)-(x,y)\|^{2}=(1+1/\lambda^{2})\|x-z(x,y)\|^{2}
so that

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\tilde{\gamma},\gamma_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\int\|T(x,y)-(x,y)\|^{2}d\tilde{\gamma}(x,y)=\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\right)\int\|x-z_{\lambda}(x,y)\|^{2}d\tilde{\gamma}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\lambda+\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\left(\int\bar{\varphi}-\tilde{\phi}d\tilde{\mu}+\int\bar{\varphi}^{*}-\tilde{\phi}^{*}d\tilde{\nu}\right),

where the last inequality is from (
5.2
).
This is our first inequality.

Let us proceed to the second inequality. Denoting by
\pi_{x}
the projection on the
x
coordinate, we have that
(\pi_{x},z_{\lambda})_{\sharp}\tilde{\gamma}
has first marginal
\tilde{\mu}
and second marginal
\mu_{0}
. Indeed,
\mu_{0}
is the first marginal of
\gamma_{0}
, i.e.
\mu_{0}=\pi_{x\sharp}\gamma_{0}=\pi_{x\sharp}(T_{\sharp}\tilde{\gamma})=(\pi_{x}\circ T)_{\sharp}\tilde{\gamma}=z_{\lambda\sharp}\tilde{\gamma}
. Similarly,
\pi_{y\sharp}[(\pi_{x},z_{\lambda})_{\sharp}\tilde{\gamma}]=[\pi_{y}\circ(\pi_{x},z_{\lambda})]_{\sharp}\tilde{\gamma}=z_{\lambda\sharp}\tilde{\gamma}
is the second marginal of
(\pi_{x},z_{\lambda})_{\sharp}\tilde{\gamma}
. Thus,
(\pi_{x},z_{\lambda})_{\sharp}\tilde{\gamma}
defines a valid coupling for
\tilde{\mu}
and
\mu_{0}
and we deduce our second inequality

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\tilde{\mu},\mu_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\int\|x-z_{\lambda}(x,y)\|^{2}d\tilde{\gamma}(x,y)\leq\lambda\left(\int\bar{\varphi}-\tilde{\phi}d\tilde{\mu}+\int\bar{\varphi}^{*}-\tilde{\phi}^{*}d\tilde{\nu}\right)

∎

The next lemma allows to control the stability of optimal couplings supported on the graph of the same subdifferential, as a function of the variations of the source marginal.

Lemma 5.2
.

Fix
R>0
,
M>0
.
Let
\mu,\beta
be supported in
B_{R}
such that
\mu(A)\leq M\lambda(A)
for any measurable set
A
. Assume that
\bar{\varphi}
is convex lower semicontinuous,
L
-Lipschitz on
B_{R}
. Set
\gamma=(id,\nabla\bar{\varphi})_{\sharp}\mu
, and assume that
\theta
is a coupling with first marginal
\beta
and is supported on the graph of
\partial\bar{\varphi}
. Consider the constant
C_{R,d}
in
Theorem

4.3
.

Case 1:
if
\left(\frac{6W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)}{(2MC_{R,d})^{2/3}}\right)^{\frac{3}{8}}>\frac{R}{8}
, then

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\gamma,\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{256L^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)+L^{2}MC_{R,d}\left(\frac{6W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)}{(2C_{R,d})^{2/3}}\right)^{\frac{3}{8}}.

Otherwise:

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\gamma,\theta)

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)+5L^{2}(2LMC_{r,d})^{\frac{1}{2}}W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Proof.

Denote by
S
the optimal mapping from
\mu
to
\beta
, see
Theorem

2.1
.
Define
\gamma_{x}
the disintegration of
\gamma
, such that for
\mu
almost all
x
,
\supp\ \gamma_{x}\subset\partial\bar{\varphi}(x)=\{\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)\}
, and for any measurable function on
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\int h(x,y)d\gamma(x,y)=\int h(x,y)d\gamma_{x}(y)d\mu(x)
. Similarly,
\theta_{z}
is the disintegration of
\theta

such that
\supp\ \theta_{z}\in\partial\bar{\varphi}\left(z\right)
for all
z
and for any measurable function on
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\int h(x,y)d\theta(x,y)=\int h(x,y)d\theta_{x}(y)d\beta(x)
.

Consider a coupling
\Gamma
, between
\gamma
and
\theta
as follows, the marginal in
x
is
\mu
and the remaining variables are independent conditioned on
x
, with distribution

\displaystyle y,\tilde{x},\tilde{y}|x\sim\gamma_{x},S(x),\theta_{S(x)}.

We have
S_{\sharp}\mu=\beta
so that the second marginal of
\Gamma
is
\theta
. The first marginal is obviously
\gamma
. The associated quadratic cost provides an upper bound on the
W_{2}
distance between the couplings,

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\gamma,\theta)\leq\,

\displaystyle\int\|x-\tilde{x}\|^{2}+\|y-\tilde{y}\|^{2}d\Gamma(x,y,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=\int\|x-S(x)\|^{2}+\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)-\tilde{y}\|^{2}d\theta_{S(x)}(\tilde{y})d\mu(x)

Choose
\eta>0
and
\alpha\geq 0
. Set
\Omega_{\eta}=\left\{x|\|x-S(x)\|\geq\eta\right\}
, by Markov’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mu(\Omega_{\eta})\leq\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\int\|x-S(x)\|^{2}d\mu(x)=\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta).

We deduce that

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\gamma,\theta)\leq\left(1+\frac{4L^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\right)W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)+\int_{x\not\in\Omega_{\eta}}\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)-\tilde{y}\|^{2}d\theta_{S(x)}(\tilde{y})d\mu(x)

where we have used that
\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)\|\leq L
for
\mu
almost all
x
, and
\theta
is supported on its graph so that
\|\tilde{y}\|\leq L
for
\mu
almost all
x
and
\theta_{S(x)}
almost all
\tilde{y}
, by Lipschicity of
\bar{\varphi}.

Set
\Sigma_{\eta,\alpha}=\left\{x\not\in\Omega_{\eta}|\mathrm{diam}(\partial\bar{\varphi}(B(x,\eta)))\geq\alpha\right\}
. We have, using similar bounds on
\nabla\bar{\varphi}
and
\tilde{y}
,

\displaystyle\int_{x\not\in\Omega_{\eta}}\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}(x)-\tilde{y}\|^{2}d\theta_{S(x)}(\tilde{y})d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq 4L^{2}\mu(\Sigma_{\eta,\alpha})+\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4L^{2}M\ \mathrm{Vol}(B(0,8\eta))\ \mathcal{N}(\Sigma_{\eta,\alpha}\cap B(0,R),8\eta)+\alpha^{2}.

We get for any
\eta>0
,
\alpha\geq 0

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\gamma,\theta)\leq\left(1+\frac{4L^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\right)W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)+4L^{2}M\ \mathrm{Vol}(B(0,8\eta))\ \mathcal{N}(\Sigma_{\eta,\alpha}\cap B(0,R),8\eta)+\alpha^{2}

(5.3)

We are now going to treat two cases and choose corresponding values for
\eta
and
\alpha
, note that we do not optimize constants.
First consider the case
\left(\frac{6W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)}{(2MC_{R,d})^{2/3}}\right)^{\frac{3}{8}}>\frac{R}{8}
. We choose
\eta=\frac{R}{8}
,
\alpha=0
, and obtain using the volume estimates in
Lemma

A.2
and the definition of
C_{R,d}
from
Theorem

4.3
,

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\gamma,\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{256L^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)+4L^{2}M\mathrm{Vol}(B(0,8\eta))

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{256L^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)+\frac{1}{8}L^{2}MC_{R,d}R

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{256L^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)+L^{2}MC_{R,d}\left(\frac{6W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)}{(2C_{R,d})^{2/3}}\right)^{\frac{3}{8}}

Second consider the case
\left(\frac{6W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)}{(2MC_{R,d})^{2/3}}\right)^{\frac{3}{8}}\leq\frac{R}{8}
.
We get from (
5.3
) and
Theorem

4.1
, for any
\eta\leq R/4
and
\alpha\geq 0
,

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\gamma,\theta)\leq\left(1+\frac{4L^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\right)W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)+\frac{L^{3}MC_{R,d}\eta}{\alpha}+\alpha^{2}

Choosing
\alpha=(L^{3}MC_{r,d}\eta)^{\frac{1}{3}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\gamma,\theta)\leq\left(1+\frac{4L^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\right)W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)+2(L^{3}MC_{r,d}\eta)^{\frac{2}{3}}

Choosing
\eta=\left(6\frac{W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)}{(2MC_{r,d})^{2/3}}\right)^{\frac{3}{8}}\leq\frac{R}{8}
, we obtain

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\gamma,\theta)\leq\left(1+\frac{4L^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\right)W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)+2(L^{3}MC_{r,d}\eta)^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)+4L^{2}\left(\frac{1}{6}(2MC_{r,d})^{2/3}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)^{\frac{1}{4}}+2(L^{3}MC_{r,d})^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\frac{6}{(2MC_{r,d})^{2/3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)+W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)^{\frac{1}{4}}L^{2}(2MC_{r,d})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(4\cdot 6^{-3/4}+2\cdot 6^{1/4}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)+5L^{2}(2LMC_{r,d})^{\frac{1}{2}}W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\beta)^{\frac{1}{4}}

∎

5.2
Stability of optimal couplings under variations of source and target

The following is the main stability result of this section. A simpler corollary is given right after. The main idea of the proof is illustrated in
Figure

.

Theorem 5.3
.

For any
d,R_{\mu},R_{\nu},M
, there is
C>0
such that the following holds.
Let
\mu
and
\nu
be probability measures supported on
B_{R_{\mu}}
and
B_{R_{\nu}}
respectively, with
\mu(A)\leq M\lambda(A)
for any measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\bar{\varphi}\in\mathcal{C}_{2R_{\mu},2R_{\nu}}
be an optimal Brenier potential (as in
Lemma

2.2
) for these measures and
\gamma
be their optimal coupling. Let
\tilde{\mu}
,
\tilde{\nu}
be probability measures also supported on
B_{R_{\mu}}
and
B_{R_{\nu}}
respectively with associated Brenier potential
\tilde{\phi}\in\mathcal{C}_{2R_{\mu},2R_{\nu}}
and coupling
\tilde{\gamma}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C^{4}}W_{2}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})^{4}\leq W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})+\left(\int\bar{\varphi}-\tilde{\phi}d\tilde{\mu}+\int\bar{\varphi}^{*}-\tilde{\phi}^{*}d\tilde{\nu}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Proof.

We start with
Lemma

5.1
which applies under the hypotheses of
Theorem

5.3
with the same notations with
R=R_{\mu}
and
L=2R_{\nu}
.
We get
\gamma_{0}
supported on
\partial\bar{\varphi}
with first marginal
\mu_{0}
supported on
B_{2R_{\mu}}
. Since both
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma
are supported on
\partial\bar{\varphi}
with marginals in
B_{2R_{\mu}}
on which
\bar{\varphi}
is Lipschitz,
Lemma

5.2
applies with
\theta=\gamma_{0}
,
R=2R_{\mu}
and
L=2R_{\nu}
.

All Wasserstein distances are bounded by constants depending on
d,M,R_{\mu},R_{\nu}
. Similarly as in the proof of
Corollary

4.6
, it is possible to obtain
C>0
by adjusting the constants in
Lemma

5.2
so that only the smallest power dominates in all cases, resulting in
W_{2}(\gamma_{0},\gamma)\leq CW_{2}(\mu_{0},\mu)^{\frac{1}{4}}
. Note again that both
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma
are supported on
\partial\bar{\varphi}
and their first marginal,
\mu_{0}
and
\mu
are supported on
B_{2R_{\mu}}
on which
\bar{\varphi}
is assumed to be Lipschitz. We have

\displaystyle W_{2}(\tilde{\gamma},\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}(\tilde{\gamma},\gamma_{0})+W_{2}(\gamma_{0},\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}(\tilde{\gamma},\gamma_{0})+CW_{2}(\mu_{0},\mu)^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq W_{2}(\tilde{\gamma},\gamma_{0})+C(W_{2}(\mu_{0},\tilde{\mu})+W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu}))^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Set
\delta=\int\bar{\varphi}-\tilde{\phi}d\tilde{\mu}+\int\bar{\varphi}^{*}-\tilde{\phi}^{*}d\tilde{\nu}
.
Considering
Lemma

5.1
, we may adjust the constant
C
such that

\displaystyle W_{2}(\tilde{\gamma},\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\delta}+C\left(W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})+\sqrt{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

Similarly as in the proof of
Corollary

4.6
,
\delta
is bounded by a constant depending only on
d,M,R_{\mu},R_{\nu}
, may keep the smallest powers and adjust the constant
C
to get desired estimate.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

For any
d,R_{\mu},R_{\nu},M
, there is
C>0
such that
for any probability distribution
\mu
,
\nu
supported on
B_{R_{\mu}}
and
B_{R_{\nu}}
respectively, with
\mu(A)\leq M\lambda(A)
and optimal coupling
\gamma
, for any probability distribution
\tilde{\mu}
,
\tilde{\nu}
also supported on
B_{R_{\mu}}
and
B_{R_{\nu}}
respectively, with optimal coupling
\tilde{\gamma}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C^{8}}W_{2}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})^{8}

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})+W_{2}(\nu,\tilde{\nu}).

Proof.

By
Lemma

2.2
there is an optimal Brenier potential
\bar{\varphi}\in\mathcal{C}_{2R_{\mu},2R_{\nu}}
, for measures
\mu,\nu
. The same is true for measures
\tilde{\mu}
,
\tilde{\nu}
and Brenier potential
\tilde{\phi}\in\mathcal{C}_{2R_{\mu},2R_{\nu}}
. The hypotheses of
Theorem

5.3
are satisfied for
\bar{\varphi}
and
\tilde{\phi}
. Furthermore using
Theorem

2.1
for measures
\mu
and
\nu
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\bar{\varphi}-\tilde{\phi})d\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\bar{\varphi}-\tilde{\phi})d\nu\leq 0.

From
Theorem

5.3
there is
C>0
, such that for any
\mu,\nu,\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\nu}
as in the statement, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C^{4}}W_{2}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\,W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})+\left(\int\bar{\varphi}-\tilde{\phi}d\tilde{\mu}+\int\bar{\varphi}^{*}-\tilde{\phi}^{*}d\tilde{\nu}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\,W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})+\left(\int\bar{\varphi}-\tilde{\phi}d(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)+\int\bar{\varphi}^{*}-\tilde{\phi}^{*}d(\tilde{\nu}-\nu)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(5.4)

In view of applying
[

, Theorem 1.14]
, we remark that
\bar{\varphi}-\tilde{\phi}
and
\bar{\varphi}^{*}-\tilde{\phi}^{*}
are

Lipschitz respectively. Therefore

\displaystyle\int\bar{\varphi}-\tilde{\phi}d(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)+\int\bar{\varphi}^{*}-\tilde{\phi}^{*}d(\tilde{\nu}-\nu)

\displaystyle\leq 4R_{\nu}W_{1}(\tilde{\mu},\mu)+4R_{\mu}W_{1}(\tilde{\nu},\nu)

\displaystyle\leq 4\max(R_{\mu},R_{\nu})(W_{2}(\tilde{\mu},\mu)+W_{2}(\tilde{\nu},\nu)).

(5.5)

Combining (
5.4
) and (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K^{4}}W_{2}(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})^{4}

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})+2\sqrt{\max(R_{\mu},R_{\nu})}(W_{2}(\tilde{\mu},\mu)+W_{2}(\tilde{\nu},\nu))^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq W_{2}(\mu,\tilde{\mu})+2\sqrt{\max(R_{\mu},R_{\nu})}\left(W_{2}(\tilde{\mu},\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}+W_{2}(\tilde{\nu},\nu)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

With a similar reasoning as the one made in the proof of

and

we can adjust the constants in the previous equation so that only the smallest powers dominates. This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

The formula above implies unicity of the optimal coupling between
\mu
and
\nu
(take
\tilde{\mu}=\mu
,
\tilde{\nu}=\nu
). This coincides with the absolute continuity assumption, which is sufficient for unicity.

6
Conclusion

We have proposed an estimator for Monge maps, it is given by the gradient of a convex function and enjoys a closed form expression based on discrete dual solutions. Moreover, we derived finite sample guaranties leveraging a dual error bound for Brenier dual potentials, for both the general and semi-discrete cases. Up to powers, these bounds match existing results in the literature under considerably stronger assumptions. Our results hold in a very general and explicit setting: compact support and upper bounded source density. This allows to consider, in particular, possibly discontinuous Brenier potentials as well as source measures with disconnected support.

Two questions remain. First, regarding the optimality of our finite sample bounds: could they be improved under our general assumptions or are there matching lower bounds? Second, regarding the adaptability of our estimator: under the Monge diffeomorphism assumption described in
Section

3.3
, does it achieve existing minimax rates? These questions are left to future work.

Lemma A.1
.

For all
\varphi_{0}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which is
R_{\nu}
-Lipschitz on
B_{R_{\mu}}
there exits
\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{R_{\mu},R_{\nu}}
such that
\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{0}
over
B_{R_{\mu}}
, and
\varphi^{*}_{0}=\varphi^{*}_{1}
on
\partial\varphi_{0}(B_{R_{\mu}})
.

Proof.

Define
\varphi_{1}:=\varphi_{0}+\delta(.|\bar{B}_{R_{\mu}})
where
\delta(.|C)
denotes the indicator function which values
0
in
C
and
+\infty
elsewhere. By construction, we have that
\varphi_{1}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and is
R_{\nu}
-Lipschitz on
B_{R_{\mu}}
.
It also results classically that
\varphi_{1}^{*}
is globally
R_{\mu}
-Lipschitz.
Indeed,
\varphi_{1}^{*}
is finite everywhere as it is defined by maximization of an upper semicontinuous function on a compact set. Therefore,
\partial\varphi_{1}^{*}
has full domain and
x\in\partial\varphi_{1}^{*}(y)
implies
y\in\partial\varphi_{1}(x)
(
[
Rockafellar, 1970
, Theorem 23.5]
) implying that
\varphi_{1}(x)
is finite . By construction of
\varphi_{1}
this means that
\|x\|\leq R_{\mu}
and by
[
Rockafellar, 1970
, Theorem 24.7]
,
\varphi_{1}^{*}
is
R_{\mu}
-Lipschitz.

For the last part, since
\varphi_{0}=\varphi_{1}
on
B_{R_{\mu}}
, it holds that
\partial\varphi_{0}=\partial\varphi_{1}
on
B_{R_{\mu}}
. For any
y\in\partial\varphi_{0}(B_{R_{\mu}})
, there is
x\in B_{R_{\mu}}
such that
y\in\partial\varphi_{0}(x)
. Since we have
\varphi_{1}(x)=\varphi_{0}(x)
and also
y\in\partial\varphi_{1}(x)
, it holds that
\varphi_{1}^{*}(y)=\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-\varphi_{1}(x)=\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-\varphi_{0}(x)=\varphi_{0}^{*}(y)
using the equality case of Fenchel-Young’s inequality
[
Rockafellar, 1970
, Theorem 23.5]
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

For all
r>0
we have the following upper bounds on the volume of balls of radius
r
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(B(0,r))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}+1}}\left(\frac{\pi e}{\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}r^{d}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(B(0,8r))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}+1}}\left(\frac{\pi e64}{\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}r^{d}.

Proof.

The Gamma function admits the following lower bound, given for example in
[
Olver et al., 2010
, Section 5.6]
: for all
z>0
,
\Gamma(z)\geq\sqrt{2\pi}z^{z-1/2}e^{-z}
. Combining this bound with the expression of the volume of
B_{r}
:
\mathrm{Vol}(B_{r})=\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}r^{d}
we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(B_{r})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}e^{-\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}}r^{d}=\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}+1}}\left(\frac{\pi e}{\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}r^{d}.

We deduce the bound on
\mathrm{Vol}(B(0,8r))
.
∎

A.4
Measure theoretical lemmas

The following are classical, we provide proof arguments for completeness.

Definition A.3
.

The support of a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the smallest closed set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mu(X)=1
. That is:

\supp\mu=\bigcap\left\{X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\>|\>X\>\text{closed},\>\mu(X)=1\right\}.

Or equivalently:

\supp\mu=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\>|\>\mu(B(x,\epsilon))>0\>\forall\epsilon>0\}.

Definition A.4
.

For
i=1,2
we denote
\pi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
the projection mapping on the first and respectiely second component. That is for every
(x_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
:
\pi_{i}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{i}
.

Proposition A.5
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure and
f
be a non-negative lower semi-continuous function. Then
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fd\mu=0
if and only if
\supp\mu\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\>f(x)=0\}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\supp\mu
. For all
\epsilon>0
we always have
\int_{B(x,\epsilon)}fd\mu=0
. On the other hand we also have
\int_{B(x,\epsilon)}fd\mu\geq\inf_{t\in B(x,\epsilon)}f(t)\mu(B(x,\epsilon))
. By definition of the support
\mu(B(x,\epsilon))>0
for all
\epsilon>0
. It results that for any
x\in\supp\mu
and for any
\epsilon>0,

\inf_{t\in B(x,\epsilon)}f(t)=0
. Taking the limit as
\epsilon\longrightarrow 0
we thus obtain
\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\inf_{t\in B(x,\epsilon)}f(t)=\lim\inf_{t\rightarrow x}f(t)\geq f(x)
by lower semi-continuity. This shows that
f(x)=0
.
∎

Lemma A.6
.

Let
\mu,\>\nu
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
. Then
\pi_{1}(\supp\gamma)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\>\exists y\in\supp\nu\>\text{s.t}\>(x,y)\in\supp(\gamma)\}
.

Proof.

First of all we have the trivial inclusion
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\>\exists y\in\supp\nu\>\text{s.t}\>(x,y)\in\supp(\gamma)\}\subset\pi_{1}(\supp\gamma)

Suppose that there exists
(x,y)\in\supp\gamma
such that
y\notin\supp\nu
. Then there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\nu(B(y,\epsilon))=0
. However:

\begin{split}\nu(B(y,\epsilon))&=\gamma(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times B(y,\epsilon))\geq\gamma(B(x,\epsilon)\times B(y,\epsilon))\geq\gamma(B((x,y),\epsilon).\end{split}

The last term is necessarily positive because
(x,y)\in\supp\gamma
. This contradiction shows that
\pi_{1}(\supp\gamma)\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\>\exists y\in\supp\nu\>\text{s.t}\>(x,y)\in\supp(\gamma)\}
.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

Let
\mu,\>\nu
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
. Then
\supp\mu=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\>\exists y\in\supp\nu\>\text{s.t}\>(x,y)\in\supp(\gamma)\}
.

Proof.

We first work on the left to right inclusion. We first suppose that there exists
x\in\supp\mu
such that
\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have
(x,y)\notin\supp\gamma
. This means that there exists
\epsilon_{y}>0
such that
\gamma(B((x,y),\epsilon_{y}))=0
. Since
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is separable there exists a dense family
(y_{n})_{n\geq 1}
such that
\mathbb{R}^{d}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}B(y_{n},\epsilon_{n})
, where
\epsilon_{n}:=\frac{\epsilon_{y_{n}}}{\sqrt{2}}
. Then

\begin{split}\gamma\left(\bigcup_{n\geq 1}B(x,\epsilon_{n})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)&=\gamma\left(\bigcup_{n\geq 1}B(x,\epsilon_{n})\times B(y_{n},\epsilon_{n})\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}\gamma(B(x,\epsilon_{n})\times B(y_{n},\epsilon_{n}))\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}\gamma\big(B((x,y_{n}),\sqrt{2}\epsilon_{n})\big)=0.\end{split}

However
\gamma\left(\bigcup_{n\geq 1}B(x,\epsilon_{n})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)=\mu\left(\bigcup_{n\geq 1}B(x,\epsilon_{n})\right)>0
, hence a contradiction. This shows that
\supp\mu\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\>\exists y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\>\text{s.t}\>(x,y)\in\supp(\gamma)\}
. We use Lemma
A.6
to conclude the first inclusion.

To show the second one let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that
(x,y)\in\supp\gamma
for some
y\in\supp\nu
. Then we have for all
\epsilon>0

\gamma(B((x,y),\epsilon))>0
. Thus:

\begin{split}\mu(B(x,\epsilon))&=\gamma(B(x,\epsilon)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\geq\gamma(B(x,\epsilon)\times B(y,\epsilon))\geq\gamma(B((x,y),\epsilon))>0.\end{split}

This shows that
x
belongs indeed to the support of
\mu
.
∎
