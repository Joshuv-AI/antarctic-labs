Hoffman colorability of graphs with smallest eigenvalue at least − 2 -2
Bart De Bruyn, Thijs van Veluw

Abstract
In accordance with the Cameron-Goethals-Seidel-Shult Classification Theorem, we extend the characterization of Hoffman colorability of line graphs from (Abiad, Bosma, Van Veluw, 2025) to all connected graphs with smallest eigenvalue at least − 2 -2 ; we give a characterization of Hoffman colorability of generalized line graphs, and we completely classify the Hoffman colorable exceptional graphs. The 245 Hoffman colorable exceptional graphs from this classification admit a natural partial ordering, and we determine the 29 graphs that are maximal in this respect, in a way similar to the classification of maximal ( E 8 E_{8} -representable) exceptional graphs as described in (Cvetković, Rowlinson, Simić, 2004). Lastly, as a byproduct and also similarly as in (loc. cit.), we determine all 39 graphs that are maximal with respect to being representable in the E 7 E_{7} root system.



1
Introduction

For a simple graph
G
, write
\lambda_{\max}(G)
and
\lambda_{\min}(G)
for respectively the largest and smallest eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix of
G
. The
Hoffman bound
is a well-known spectral lower bound for the chromatic number of a non-empty graph
G
:
\chi(G)\geq h(G)
, where

h(G)\coloneqq 1-\frac{\lambda_{\max}(G)}{\lambda_{\min}(G)}.

(1)

A graph is called
Hoffman colorable
if the Hoffman bound is tight, and every optimal coloring is then called a
Hoffman coloring
. From the spectrum of regular complete multipartite graphs
K_{m,m,\dots,m}
, it can be easily read off that they are Hoffman colorable. Also non-empty bipartite graphs (graphs with chromatic number 2) are Hoffman colorable, since their spectra are symmetric around 0. In both cases, the multipartition is a Hoffman coloring. Bipartite and regular complete multipartite graphs are not the only examples; a Hoffman colorable graph that is neither bipartite nor regular complete multipartite is called
non-trivially Hoffman colorable
, see for example the graph from Figure
1
.

\lambda_{\max}=1+\sqrt{5},\quad\lambda_{\min}=\frac{-1-\sqrt{5}}{2}

Figure 1:
The smallest connected non-trivially Hoffman colorable graph.

Hoffman colorings have been studied in
[
3
,
12
]
for (strongly) regular graphs, and in
[
1
,
2
]
for general graphs. Tightness of a related spectral lower bound for the chromatic number in terms of the eigenvalues of the normalized Laplacian matrix has also been studied
[
4
,
8
]
. In
[
5
,
10
]
, the Hoffman bound is used to classify strongly regular and distance-regular graphs with a given small chromatic number. Studying Hoffman colorability of graphs is interesting because the Hoffman bound is not only a lower bound for the chromatic number, but also for numerous variations of the chromatic number. This includes the vector chromatic number
\chi_{v}

[
13
]
, which is related to the Lovász
\vartheta
-number
[
14
]
(see
[
13
, Theorem 8.2]
), and the quantum chromatic number
\chi_{q}
; we have

h(G)\leq\chi_{v}(G)\leq\chi_{q}(G)\leq\chi(G)

by
[
15
, Corollary 4.1]
and
[
14
, Theorem 6]
. For a Hoffman colorable graph, we get the values for these variations of the chromatic number for free by sandwiching. In particular, for Hoffman colorable graphs, the quantum chromatic number is computable, while for graphs in general it is not known to be computable
[
15
]
.

Of particular interest to this paper are the results of
[
2
]
, which we briefly discuss. The
Decomposition Theorem

[
2
, Theorem 3.3]
, among other things, entails that the induced subgraph of a connected Hoffman colorable graph on a choice of at least two color classes is again Hoffman colorable. We will refer to such induced subgraphs as
chromatic components
, so that Hoffman colorability is closed with respect to chromatic components. Using the Decomposition Theorem, one can characterize Hoffman colorability in line graphs
[
2
, Theorem 3.12]
; the
line graph

L(G)
of a non-empty graph
G
is defined as the graph having the edges of
G
as vertices, such that two
G
-edges are adjacent in
L(G)
whenever they share a vertex.

Line graphs have the spectral property that the smallest eigenvalue is bounded below by
-2
. The well-known Cameron-Goethals-Seidel-Shult Theorem spectrally generalizes line graphs by classifying all connected graphs having all eigenvalues at least
-2
. In short, there is an infinite family, the
generalized line graphs
, and finitely many
exceptional graphs
, which can be represented in the
E_{8}
root system. Induced subgraphs of generalized line graphs are also generalized line graphs. Therefore, if a connected graph
G
with smallest eigenvalue at least
-2
contains an exceptional graph
H
as an induced subgraph, then
G
is exceptional as well. This defines a partial order on the set of exceptional graphs, and there are 473 exceptional graphs that are maximal with respect to this partial order
[
9
, Section 6.1]
.

A very natural follow-up question of the characterization of Hoffman colorable line graphs from
[
2
]
is therefore whether it is possible to extend it to generalized line graphs, or even to exceptional graphs. As it turns out, this is possible, and this is exactly the main subject of this article. In particular, we show that Hoffman colorability of generalized line graphs is equivalent to an explicit condition in terms of the graph structure which we call
chromatically balanced
. Moreover, we determine that there are exactly 245 Hoffman colorable exceptional graphs and among these, we determine those that are maximal with respect to chromatic components; there are exactly 29 of them. The complete statement of the classification can be found in ?THM?
. As an important ingredient of our proof of ?THM?
, we find that, among the graphs that can be represented in the
E_{7}
root system, there are exactly 39 that are maximal with respect to induced subgraphs. Using ?THM?
, we completely classify all connected non-trivially Hoffman colorable graphs
G
with fewer than
3\chi(G)
vertices (?THM?
).

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we present our main contributions. In Section
3
we set out the preliminaries that are necessary for our proofs. In Sections
4
and
5
we prove ?THM?
, in the cases of generalized line graphs and exceptional graphs respectively. In Section
6
we prove ?THM?
. Lastly, in Section
7
we give (a reference to) an
E_{8}
-representation for each of the 245 Hoffman colorable exceptional graphs from ?THM?
.

Let

For

and all

\chi(L(G;a_{1},\dots,a_{n}))=\max(\chi(L(G)),\max_{1\leq i\leq n}(a_{i}+\deg_{G}(v_{i}))).

(2)

Now, for a graph

and an integer

, we define the

, denoted

where

for every

. This way, its chromatic number is equal to
c
, as the inputs of the inner maximum function of (

) are all equal to

, which is also at least
\chi(L(G))
.

3
Preliminaries

Before we can prove the main results, we provide additional background information, and fix some of the notation we use.

(Induced) subgraphs

A graph
H
is a
subgraph
of a graph
G
if
V(H)\subseteq V(G)
and
E(H)\subseteq E(G)
. The subgraph is
proper
if
H\neq G
. Let
X
be a set of vertices in a graph
G
. The
induced subgraph of
G
on
X
(denoted
G[X]
) is the graph with vertex set
X
, together with all the edges between vertices of
X
that are in
G
.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
6
, Proposition 3.2.1]
)
.

If
H
is a subgraph of
G
, then
\lambda_{\max}(H)\leq\lambda_{\max}(G)
. If
G
is connected and
H
is a proper subgraph, then
\lambda_{\max}(H)<\lambda_{\max}(G)
. If
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
, then
\lambda_{\min}(G)\leq\lambda_{\min}(H)
.

In particular, if
G
has smallest eigenvalue at least
-2
, and
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
, then
H
also has smallest eigenvalue at least
-2
.

Hoffman colorings

A set
C
of vertices of a graph is a
coclique
if no pair of vertices of
C
is adjacent. A
coloring
of a graph
G
is a partition of the vertex set of
G
into cocliques. The
chromatic number

\chi(G)
of
G
is the smallest number of cocliques needed for a coloring. A graph is
bipartite
if
\chi(G)\leq 2
. A coclique is
\nu
-regular
if every vertex outside
C
is adjacent to precisely
\nu
vertices of
C
.

As mentioned before, the Hoffman bound (
1
) gives a spectral lower bound on the chromatic number. For regular graphs, the Hoffman bound follows from the
ratio bound
:

Theorem 3.2

(ratio bound, Hoffman (unpublished, see
[
11
]
))
.

Let
G
be a non-empty
k
-regular graph on
n
vertices with smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}
and let
C
be a coclique in
G
. Then

|C|\leq\frac{n}{h(G)}=\frac{-n\lambda_{\min}}{k-\lambda_{\min}},

with equality if and only if
C
is
(-\lambda_{\min})
-regular.

A coclique that attains the ratio bound is called a
Hoffman coclique
. A Hoffman coloring of a regular graph now necessarily partitions the vertex set into Hoffman cocliques:

Proposition 3.3

(
[
5
, Proposition 2.3]
)
.

Let
G
be a regular graph. Then a coloring of
G
is a Hoffman coloring if and only if it is a partition of
G
into Hoffman cocliques. In this case, every vertex is adjacent to precisely
-\lambda_{\min}(G)
vertices of every color class other than its own.

For general graphs, we also know structure of Hoffman colorings through the Decomposition Theorem, which can be seen as an extension of ?THM?
.

Theorem 3.4

(Decomposition Theorem,
[
2
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

Let
G
be a Hoffman colorable graph with positive eigenvector
x
and let
H
be a chromatic component of
G
. Then
H
is Hoffman colorable,
\lambda_{\min}(G)=\lambda_{\min}(H)
, and
x|_{H}
is a positive eigenvector for
H
.

A connected graph has an (up to scaling) unique positive eigenvector by the Perron-Frobenius Theorem
[
6
, Theorem 2.2.1]
, and this belongs to the largest eigenvalue. A disconnected graph
G
has a positive eigenvector if and only if the largest eigenvalue of all the connected components of
G
are equal. In this case the dimension of the eigenspace of the largest eigenvalue of
G
is equal to the number of connected components of
G
.

So, if
H
is a dichromatic component of a Hoffman colorable graph
G
with a positive eigenvector, then since
H
is bipartite, its spectrum is symmetric around 0, and so
\lambda_{\max}(H)=-\lambda_{\min}(H)=-\lambda_{\min}(G)
. Furthermore, since
H
has a positive eigenvector by the Decomposition Theorem, every connected component
K
of
H
has the same largest eigenvalue as
H
. Hence

\lambda_{\max}(G)=(\chi-1)\lambda_{\max}(K)\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{\min}(G)=\lambda_{\min}(K)

(3)

for every connected component
K
of every dichromatic component of
G
.

As said before, using the Decomposition Theorem, Hoffman colorability of line graphs can be characterized
[
2
]
.

Theorem 3.5

(
[
2
, Theorem 3.12]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. If
L(G)
is non-trivially Hoffman colorable, then either
G
is 1-factorable, or
G
is the graph from Figure
2
(in which case
L(G)
is the graph from Figure
1
).

A graph is
1-factorable
if its edges can be partitioned into perfect matchings. A
perfect matching
or
1-factor
is a set
M
of edges such that every vertex of the graph is incident with exactly one edge of
M
. A graph
G
is 1-factorable if and only if
G
is regular, and the chromatic number of
L(G)
is equal to the valency of
G
.

Remark 3.6
.

Note that for a 1-factorable graph
G
with valency
k
the
k

is just

. Therefore, in the context of generalized line graphs, the class of line graphs of 1-factorable graphs from ?THM?
fits into the larger class of chromatically balanced generalized line graphs from ?THM?
.

The Decomposition Theorem also allows to obtain a characterization for Hoffman colorability of cone graphs
[
2
]
. The
cone graph

\widehat{G}
over a graph

is the graph obtained by adding a vertex (the so-called

) to

Theorem 3.7

(
[
2
, Theorem 3.8]
)
.

Let

be a non-empty graph. Then
\widehat{G}
is Hoffman colorable if and only if
G
is regular and Hoffman colorable with color classes of size
\lambda_{\min}(G)^{2}
. In this case
\lambda_{\min}(\widehat{G})=\lambda_{\min}(G)
, and this is an integer.

We also need the following result in our proofs.

Proposition 3.8

(
[
2
, Corollaries 3.5 and 3.6]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph with a positive eigenvector. Then for every Hoffman coloring of
G
, every vertex is adjacent to at least one vertex of every other color class. Consequently, if a Hoffman coloring of
G
has a color class of size 1, then

is a cone graph.

Cameron-Goethals-Seidel-Shult Theorem

The famous Cameron-Goethals-Seidel-Shult Theorem characterizes the connected graphs that have smallest eigenvalue at least
-2
.

Theorem 3.9

(Cameron-Goethals-Seidel-Shult Theorem (
[
6
, Theorem 8.4.1 (i)]
))
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. Then
G
satisfies
\lambda_{\min}(G)\geq-2
if and only if
G
is a generalized line graph or an
E_{8}
-representable graph.

We recall the definition of
E_{8}
-representable graphs. Consider the
E_{8}
root system, which can be explicitly constructed as the subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{8}

In this section we revisit the regular Hoffman colorable graphs of the second layer. This has two important reasons. For one, the results in this section offer a different approach to prove ?THM?

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

be a Hoffman colorable graph with a positive eigenvector. If

has an optimal coloring with one color class of size 2, and one color class of size 3, then

Proof.

Let

be the dichromatic component defined by the color class of

of size 2 and the one of size 3. By ?THM?s
and
, we know that

has a positive eigenvector and minimum degree at least 1. There are only four possibilities for

∎

Lemma 6.2

Let

be a connected Hoffman colorable graph with a positive eigenvector such that

Proof.

Consider an optimal coloring of

Furthermore, since
