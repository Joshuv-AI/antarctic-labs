On the Robustness of AoA as an Authentication Feature Under Spoofing: Fundamental Limits from Misspecified Cramér–Rao Theory
Sotiris Skaperas, Arsenia Chorti

Abstract
The robustness of angle-of-arrival (AoA) as a physical-layer authentication (PLA) feature under spoofing attacks is studied, assuming a digital uniform linear array verifier. The verifier estimates the AoA assuming a legitimate user’s single-source model, whereas the received signal is generated by a multi-antenna adversary at a different angle, leading to a model mismatch. Closed-form expressions are derived for the misspecified Cramér–Rao bound, the PLA decision threshold, the spoofing detection, false-alarm and misdetection probabilities. Simulation results validate the theoretical findings and highlight the impact of the signal-to-noise-ratio, array geometries, spoofing precoding and number of snapshots on authentication robustness.



I
Introduction

Angle of arrival (AoA)-based physical layer authentication (AoA-PLA) is an established approach for static node identification by exploiting the spatial signature of the received signal
[
6
,
3
]
. In AoA-PLA, the verifier estimates the AoA from the received pilots and compares it with the enrolled AoA of the legitimate node
1
1

1

We note in passing that although a single AoA does not suffice to uniquely locate a legitimate node, extensions are straightforward by employing both azimuth and elevation angles in conjunction with the time of flight
[
3
]
.
. Recent studies have proven that AoA is robust against spoofing (impersonation)
[
8
,
7
]
, by analyzing the mean square error between legitimate and adversarial observed signals. However, these works did not provide analytical results for the authentication accuracy, spoofing detection, false alarm and misdetection probabilities.

In this work, we close this gap; under spoofing, the verifier estimates the AoA under a nominal model, whereas the received observations are generated by a different adversarial model, corresponding to a precoded multi-antenna signal. As a result, AoA estimation under attack becomes a misspecified estimation problem. Leveraging the misspecified Cramér–Rao bound (MCRB) framework
[
4
,
9
]
allows us to characterize analytically and obtain closed form expressions for authentication-relevant AoA estimation limits under spoofing.

The MCRB framework has in the past been used in wireless localization under hardware or geometry mismatch
[
2
,
10
]
, while the authors in
[
1
]
studied ghost-target spoofing in cooperative integrated sensing and communication localization over a multi-gNB network. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work proposing to use the MCRB machinery in order to derive closed form expressions for the spoofing detection, false alarm and misdetection probabilities. The key contributions of this paper are as follows:

•

We formulate AoA-PLA under spoofing as a misspecified estimation problem, derive the MCRB and the pseudo-true AoA for a ULA verifier and a spoofer with an arbitrary number of antennas and precoding vector.

•

We derive closed form expressions for the spoofing detection, false alarm and misdetection probabilities and identify the asymptotic limits of AoA-PLA.

•

We validate the analysis via Monte Carlo simulations and assess the impact of SNR, array size, snapshots, and spoofing geometry on authentication performance.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section II the assumed and spoofed signal models are presented, according to
[
8
]
. In Section III the MCRB and the pseudo-true AoA are derived and the authentication hypothesis test probabilities are evaluated. In Section IV simulation results are presented, while Section V concludes the paper.

II
System Model

We consider a verifier with an
M
-element ULA, inter-element spacing
d
, wavelength
\lambda
, and wavenumber
\kappa\triangleq 2\pi d/\lambda
. We assume a narrowband far-field line-of-sight (LoS) channel and known, deterministic, unit-modulus pilots
\{p_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
(e.g.,
p_{k}\equiv 1
), where
K

g_{\mathrm{u}}\in\mathbb{C}
is assumed known; the unknown CSI case, requiring joint AoA and CSI estimation, is left for future work.

II-A

Assumed (Legitimate) Model

We assume a single-antenna legitimate node with AoA
\theta_{\mathrm{u}}\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
relative to the verifier, so that the received signal at snapshot
k
is given by

\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{u}}_{k}=g_{\mathrm{u}}\mathbf{a}(\theta_{\mathrm{u}})\,p_{k}+\mathbf{n}_{k},\qquad k=1,\ldots,K,

(1)

where
\mathbf{n}_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M})
is independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) spatially white circularly symmetric complex Gaussian noise with variance
\sigma^{2}
per antenna, and

\mathbf{a}(\theta_{\mathrm{u}})=\left[e^{-j\kappa m\sin\theta_{\mathrm{u}}}\right]_{m=0}^{M-1}
is the ULA steering vector. For known
g_{\mathrm{u}}
and
p_{k}\equiv 1
, we adopt the equivalent normalized model in
[
8
]
, so that (
1
) can be written as

\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{u}}_{k}=\mathbf{a}(\theta_{\mathrm{u}})+\mathbf{n}_{k},\qquad k=1,\ldots,K.

(2)

II-B

True (Spoofed) Model

Following the spoofing model in
[
8
]
, an
L
-antenna impersonator is assumed so that the received signal is given by

{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{A}}_{k}=\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{n}_{k},\qquad k=1,\ldots,K,

(3)

where
\mathbf{n}_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M})
has the same statistics as in the assumed model. The spoofed signal is expressed as
\mathbf{s}\triangleq\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}q_{\ell}\,\mathbf{a}(\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell}),
where
\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell}\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
is the AoA of the
\ell
-th antenna element and
q_{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}

is the
\ell
-th precoding weight with
q_{\ell}=\beta_{\ell}e^{j\phi_{\ell}}
,
\beta_{\ell}\geq 0
,
\phi_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
.

III
Misspecified Cramér Rao Bound

Let
\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
denote a generic candidate AoA in the assumed model. Accordingly, we define the mismatch vector as
\bm{\Delta}(\theta)\triangleq\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{a}(\theta)
. In the following, we use dot notation for derivatives with respect to
\theta
.
Let
\mathbf{X}=[\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{K}]
denote the observed snapshots at the verifier. Under the assumed model, the log-likelihood is given by
\ln p(\mathbf{X};\theta)=-MK\ln(\pi\sigma^{2})-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{a}(\theta)\|^{2}.

Thus, the score function
u(\mathbf{X};\theta)\triangleq\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\ln p(\mathbf{X};\theta)
is expressed as

u(\mathbf{X};\theta)=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Re\!\left\{\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\big(\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{a}(\theta)\big)\right\},

obtained using that
z+z^{*}=2\Re\{z\}
. Then, the negative second derivative is

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}\ln p(\mathbf{X};\theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}\ln p(\mathbf{x}_{k};\theta)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Re\!\left\{\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)-\ddot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\big(\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{a}(\theta)\big)\right\}.

(4)

III-A

Cramér Rao Bound

Evaluating the expectation of the negative second derivative of the log-likelihood in (
4
) with respect to the assumed model
p(\mathbf{X};\theta)
, so that
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_{k}\mid\theta]=\mathbf{a}(\theta)
,
\forall{k}
, the second term vanishes and the Fisher information reduces to

J(\theta)\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{assumed}}\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}\ln p(\mathbf{X};\theta)\right]=\frac{2K}{\sigma^{2}}\|\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)\|^{2}.

(5)

We denote by
\Gamma(\theta)\triangleq\|\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)\|^{2}
. For a ULA the explicit expression for
\Gamma(\theta)
is given by

\Gamma(\theta)=\kappa^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}m^{2}=\kappa^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\frac{(M-1)M(2M-1)}{6}.

(6)

Then, for
K
snapshots, the CRB is given by

\mathrm{CRB}_{K}(\theta)=\frac{1}{J(\theta)}=\frac{3\sigma^{2}}{K\,\kappa^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\,(M-1)M(2M-1)}.

(7)

III-B

MCRB Under Model Mismatch

The MCRB for the scalar parameter
\theta
is expressed as

\mathrm{MCRB}_{K}(\theta)=A(\theta)^{-1}B(\theta)A(\theta)^{-1},

(8)

where
A(\theta)
and
B(\theta)
are computed over
K
i.i.d. snapshots.
A(\theta)
is defined as the expected observed information of the assumed log-likelihood, under the true model; using
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{true}}[{\mathbf{x}}_{k}]=\mathbf{s}
, equivalently

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{true}}[{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-\mathbf{a}(\theta)]=\bm{\Delta}(\theta)
,
\forall{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle A(\theta)

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{true}}\!\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}\ln p({\mathbf{X}};\theta)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2K}{\sigma^{2}}\left(\Gamma(\theta)-\Re\!\left\{\ddot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\bm{\Delta}(\theta)\right\}\right)=\frac{2K}{\sigma^{2}}D({\theta}).

(9)

In the following we denote the scalar

D(\theta)\triangleq\Gamma(\theta)-\Re\!\left\{\ddot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\bm{\Delta}(\theta)\right\},

(10)

where
D(\theta)
is the mismatch dependent curvature term.
B(\theta)
is given by the second moment of the score function under model mismatch, expressed as

\displaystyle B(\theta)\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{true}}\!\left[u({\mathbf{X}};\theta)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Var}_{\mathrm{true}}\!\left[u({\mathbf{X}};\theta)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{true}}\!\left[u({\mathbf{X}};\theta)\right]\right)^{2}.

(11)

Since
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{true}}[{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-\mathbf{a}(\theta)]=\bm{\Delta}(\theta)
, the mean of the score under the true model is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{true}}[u({\mathbf{X}};\theta)]

\displaystyle=\frac{2K}{\sigma^{2}}\Re\!\left\{\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\bm{\Delta}(\theta)\right\}.

(12)

We next compute the score variance under the true model. Given the snapshots are i.i.d. and

{\mathbf{x}}_{k}=\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{n}_{k}
with
\mathbf{n}_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M})
across
k
,
the randomness in
u({\mathbf{X}};\theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}u({\mathbf{x}}_{k};\theta)
is due solely to
\{\mathbf{n}_{k}\}
, hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}_{\mathrm{true}}[u({\mathbf{X}};\theta)]=\frac{4}{\sigma^{4}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\Re\{\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\mathbf{n}\}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\sigma^{4}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left\{\left|\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\mathbf{n}\right|^{2}\right\}=\frac{2K}{\sigma^{2}}\Gamma(\theta),

(13)

since for a complex Gaussian random variable

z=\dot{\mathbf{a}}^{H}\mathbf{n}
,
\operatorname{Var}(\Re\{z\})=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[|z|^{2}]
, and
\mathbb{E}[|z|^{2}]=\sigma^{2}\|\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)\|^{2}
.
Combining (
12
) and (
13
) yields the second moment

\displaystyle B(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{2K}{\sigma^{2}}\Gamma(\theta)+\left(\frac{2K}{\sigma^{2}}\Re\{\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\bm{\Delta}(\theta)\}\right)^{2}.

(14)

We define the scalar

\eta(\theta)\triangleq\Re\{\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\bm{\Delta}(\theta)\},

which quantifies the mismatch induced by the spoofed mean signal. Then, (
14
) can be written as

B(\theta)=\frac{2K}{\sigma^{2}}\Gamma(\theta)+\left(\frac{2K}{\sigma^{2}}\eta(\theta)\right)^{2}.

(15)

Using (
9
) and (
15
), (
8
) can be written as

\displaystyle\mathrm{MCRB}_{K}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2K}\frac{\Gamma(\theta)}{D(\theta)^{2}}+\left(\frac{\eta(\theta)}{D(\theta)}\right)^{2}.

(16)

III-C

Pseudo-True Parameter
\theta_{0}

The estimator-relevant MCRB is obtained by evaluating (
16
) at the pseudo-true parameter
\theta_{0}
, defined as the parameter that minimizes the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence between the true distribution
p_{\mathrm{true}}({\mathbf{X}})
and the assumed likelihood
p({\mathbf{X}};\theta)

\theta_{0}\triangleq\arg\min_{\theta\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(p_{\mathrm{true}}({\mathbf{X}})\,\|\,p({\mathbf{X}};\theta)\right).

(17)

Since
p_{\mathrm{true}}({\mathbf{X}})
and
p({\mathbf{X}};\theta)
are complex Gaussian with identical covariance
\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{MK}
and respective means
\mathbf{s}
and
\mathbf{a}(\theta)
replicated across snapshots, (
17
) is equivalent to

\theta_{0}=\arg\min_{\theta}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{a}(\theta)\|^{2}=\arg\min_{\theta}\|\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{a}(\theta)\|^{2}.

(18)

At
\theta_{0}
, the stationarity condition and the score function imply

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{true}}\!\left[u({\mathbf{X}};\theta_{0})\right]=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\eta(\theta_{0})=0,

(19)

which follows by (
III
), (
III-B
),
\bm{\Delta}(\theta)\triangleq\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{a}(\theta)
and
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{true}}[{\mathbf{x}}_{k}]=\mathbf{s}
,
\forall k
. Evaluating (
16
) at
\theta_{0}
, the second term vanishes and

\displaystyle\mathrm{MCRB}_{K}(\theta_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2K}\ \frac{\Gamma(\theta_{0})}{D(\theta_{0})^{2}}=\left(\frac{\Gamma(\theta_{0})}{D(\theta_{0})}\right)^{2}\mathrm{CRB}_{K}(\theta_{0}).

(20)

The closed-form expression of (
20
) at
\theta_{0}
follows from (
6
), (
7
), and the expression of
D(\theta)
derived in the Appendix.

III-D

PLA Binary Hypothesis Test

Authentication at the verifier is formulated as a binary hypothesis test on the
K
observed snapshots
\mathbf{X}=[\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{K}]
, (obtained either from (
1
) or from (
3
)):

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}:\;

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}\!\big(\mathbf{a}(\theta_{\mathrm{u}}),\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M}\big),\quad k=1,\ldots,K,\ \text{(legitimate)}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}:\;

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}\!\bigg(\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}q_{\ell}\,\mathbf{a}(\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell}),\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M}\bigg),\text{ }k=1,\ldots,K.

(21)

We adopt a typical AoA verification test
T(\mathbf{X})\triangleq|\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\mathrm{u}}|
, where
\theta_{\mathrm{u}}
is the legitimate AoA and

\hat{\theta}\triangleq\ \arg\min_{\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\big\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\mathbf{a}(\theta)\big\|^{2}

(22)

is the maximum likelihood (ML) estimator under
\mathcal{H}_{0}
(and the quasi-ML AoA estimator under
\mathcal{H}_{1}
); this choice avoids explicit estimation of the spoofing parameters
\{\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell},q_{\ell},L\}
.
The verifier rejects
\mathcal{H}_{0}
if
T(\mathbf{X})>\tau
, where
\tau
is chosen to meet a target false alarm probability
P_{FA}=\alpha
and is expressed analytically under
\mathcal{H}_{0}
via a Wald approximation.

Under
\mathcal{H}_{0}
,
\{\mathbf{x}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
are i.i.d. given
\theta_{\mathrm{u}}
. Since
\hat{\theta}
in (
22
) is the ML estimator under
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, standard regularity conditions imply
\sqrt{K}\,(\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\mathrm{u}})\overset{a}{\sim}\mathcal{N}\!\big(0,\mathrm{CRB}_{1}(\theta_{\mathrm{u}})\big)
,
K\rightarrow\infty
; see
[
5
]
. Equivalently,
\hat{\theta}
is asymptotically Gaussian with variance
\mathrm{CRB}_{K}(\theta_{\mathrm{u}})
, i.e.,
\hat{\theta}\overset{a}{\sim}\mathcal{N}\!\big(\theta_{\mathrm{u}},\mathrm{CRB}_{K}(\theta_{\mathrm{u}})\big)
, as
K\rightarrow\infty
. Then, standardizing with
Z_{0}=(\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\mathrm{u}})/\sqrt{\mathrm{CRB}_{K}(\theta_{\mathrm{u}})}\overset{a}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, we obtain

P_{\mathrm{FA}}(\tau)\triangleq\Pr\!\left(|\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\mathrm{u}}|>\tau\mid\mathcal{H}_{0}\right)\approx 2Q\!\left(\frac{\tau}{\sqrt{\mathrm{CRB}_{K}(\theta_{\mathrm{u}})}}\right).

(23)

where
Q(x)=1-\Phi(x)
and
\Phi(\cdot)
is the standard normal cdf. Hence, the threshold for false-alarm level
\alpha
is given by

\tau(\alpha)=\sqrt{\mathrm{CRB}_{K}(\theta_{\mathrm{u}})}\;\Phi^{-1}\!\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right).

(24)

For finite
K
, the threshold can also be calibrated numerically under
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, e.g., via Monte Carlo estimation of the
(1-\alpha)
-quantile of
|\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\mathrm{u}}|
.

Under
\mathcal{H}_{1}
,
\hat{\theta}
is the quasi-ML estimator and, under standard regularity conditions for misspecified ML
[
4
]
, satisfies

\sqrt{K}\,(\hat{\theta}-\theta_{0})\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}\!\big(0,\mathrm{MCRB}_{1}(\theta_{0})\big)
,
equivalently
\hat{\theta}\overset{a}{\sim}\mathcal{N}\!\big(\theta_{0},\mathrm{MCRB}_{K}(\theta_{0})\big)
, as
K\to\infty
. Defining
\delta\triangleq\theta_{0}-\theta_{\mathrm{u}}
and standardizing via
Z_{1}\triangleq(\hat{\theta}-\theta_{0})/\sqrt{\mathrm{MCRB}_{K}(\theta_{0})}\overset{a}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, the misdetection probability is expressed as

\begin{split}P_{MD}(\tau)\triangleq\Pr\!\left(|\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\mathrm{u}}|\leq\tau\mid\mathcal{H}_{1}\right)\\
\approx\Phi\!\left(\frac{\tau-\delta}{{\sqrt{\mathrm{MCRB}_{K}(\theta_{0})}}}\right)-\Phi\!\left(\frac{-\tau-\delta}{\sqrt{\mathrm{MCRB}_{K}(\theta_{0})}}\right).\end{split}

(25)

Hence,
P_{SD}\triangleq\Pr\!\left(|\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\mathrm{u}}|>\tau\mid\mathcal{H}_{1}\right)=1-P_{MD}
denotes the spoofing detection probability. Under
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, the corresponding probability of legitimate node detection is given by
P_{D}=1-P_{FA}
. Key insights of this analysis are listed below:

III-D
1
Asymptotic behavior of AoA-based authentication

•

For any fixed
\tau>0
,
P_{FA}(\tau)\to 0
as
K\to\infty
.

This follows from (
23
),
\frac{\tau}{\sqrt{\mathrm{CRB}_{K}(\theta_{\mathrm{u}})}}=\tau\sqrt{\frac{K}{\mathrm{CRB}_{1}(\theta_{\mathrm{u}})}}\xrightarrow[K\to\infty]{}\infty,

and
Q(x)\xrightarrow[x\to\infty]{}0
.

•

If
|\delta|=|\theta_{0}-\theta_{\mathrm{u}}|>0
,
P_{MD}(\tau(\alpha))\rightarrow 0
as
K\to\infty
. Since
\tau(\alpha)\to 0
, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,|\delta|)
,
\tau(\alpha)\leq\varepsilon
for all sufficiently large
K
, so
P_{MD}(\tau(\alpha))\leq\Pr\!\big(|\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\mathrm{u}}|\leq\varepsilon\mid\mathcal{H}_{1}\big)
. By the triangle inequality,
\{|\hat{\theta}-\theta_{\mathrm{u}}|\leq\varepsilon\}\subseteq\{|\hat{\theta}-\theta_{0}|\geq|\delta|-\varepsilon\}
then

P_{MD}(\tau(\alpha))\leq\Pr\!\big(|\hat{\theta}-\theta_{0}|\geq|\delta|-\varepsilon\mid\mathcal{H}_{1}\big)\to 0,
since
|\delta|-\varepsilon>0
and
\hat{\theta}\xrightarrow{p}\theta_{0}
.

•

If
|\delta|=0
,
P_{MD}\not\to 0
as
K\to\infty
, since
P_{MD}(\tau(\alpha))\rightarrow{}2\Phi\!\left(\Phi^{-1}\!\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{CRB}_{1}(\theta_{\mathrm{u}})}{\mathrm{MCRB}_{1}(\theta_{0})}}\right)-1
.

III-D
2
Variance sensitivity of AoA-PLA under spoofing

•

If
|\delta|\leq\tau
,
P_{SD}
is strictly increasing in estimation variance
\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}^{2}\triangleq\mathrm{MCRB}_{K}(\theta_{0})
. Differentiating
P_{SD}
with respect to
\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}
and using
\frac{d}{dx}Q(x)=-\phi(x)
,
\phi(\cdot)
the standard normal pdf:

\frac{\partial P_{SD}}{\partial\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}^{2}}\left[(\tau-|\delta|)\phi\!\left(\frac{\tau-|\delta|}{\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}}\right)+(\tau+|\delta|)\phi\!\left(\frac{\tau+|\delta|}{\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}}\right)\right]>0
, since
\phi(.)>0
and
|\delta|\leq\tau
.

•

If
|\delta|>\tau
,
P_{SD}
is non-monotonic in
\sigma_{\theta}
, with a unique minimizer
\sigma_{\theta}^{\star\,2}={2|\delta|\tau}/{\ln\!\big(\frac{|\delta|+\tau}{|\delta|-\tau}\big)}.
The former yields from
\partial P_{SD}/\partial\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}=0
. We rewrite
h(\sigma_{\hat{\theta}})\triangleq\ln\!\left(\frac{|\delta|+\tau}{|\delta|-\tau}\right)-\frac{2|\delta|\tau}{\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}^{2}}=0
. Since
|\delta|>\tau>0
, the function
h(\sigma_{\hat{\theta}})
is continuous on
(0,\infty)
, and

h^{\prime}(\sigma_{\hat{\theta}})=\frac{4|\delta|\tau}{\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}^{3}}>0,\text{ }\forall\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}>0,

hence
h(\sigma_{\hat{\theta}})
is strictly increasing. Moreover,
\lim_{\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}\to 0^{+}}h(\sigma_{\hat{\theta}})=-\infty
and
\lim_{\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}\to\infty}h(\sigma_{\hat{\theta}})>0.
Therefore, by the intermediate value theorem, there exists a solution
\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}^{\star}>0
to
h(\sigma_{\hat{\theta}})=0
, while strict monotonicity implies that this solution is unique. Solving
h(\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}^{\star})=0
yields

\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}^{\star\,2}={2|\delta|\tau}/{\ln\!\left(\frac{|\delta|+\tau}{|\delta|-\tau}\right)}.
Also, for
\sigma_{{\hat{\theta}}}^{2}\xrightarrow{}0
,
\partial P_{SD}/\partial\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}<0
and for
\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}^{2}\xrightarrow{}\infty
,
\partial P_{SD}/\partial\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}>0
. Hence,
\sigma_{\hat{\theta}}^{\star\,2}
is the unique global minimum.

These key results confirm the findings in
[
8
]
with respect to the robustness of AoA against spoofing attacks, irrespective of the number of antennas at the adversarial node, as long as the spoofer is not in the same AoA as the legitimate node.

V
Conclusions and Future Directions

In this work a novel approach is introduced for analyzing the impact of spoofing on AoA-PLA systems with ULA verifiers. Leveraging the MCRB framework under
\mathcal{H}_{1}
, we propose closed form expressions for the threshold
\tau(\alpha)
, the
P_{SD}
, the
P_{FA}
, and
P_{MD}
and provide insights for the asymptotic limits of AoA-PLA. Our findings demonstrate that, in the given legitimate and attacker model and for typical SNR and array sizes, AoA is robust against spoofing and can be considered a trustworthy authentication feature. Simulated results validate the theoretical analysis and highlight the impact of the spoofing geometry and system parameters. Future work will extend the analysis to unknown CSI, multipath fading, multi-user interference and optimal spoofer precoding analysis.

Appendix A
Derivation of
\eta(\theta)
and
D(\theta)

From the definition of the ULA steering vector, let
\mathbf{\Lambda}\triangleq\operatorname{diag}(0,1,\dots,M-1)
. Then, the first- and second-order derivatives of
\mathbf{a}(\theta)
are

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=-j\kappa\cos\theta\,\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbf{a}(\theta),

(26)

\displaystyle\ddot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\big(j\kappa\sin\theta\,\mathbf{\Lambda}-\kappa^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\,\mathbf{\Lambda}^{2}\big)\mathbf{a}(\theta).

(27)

We also define
r_{\ell}\triangleq e^{j\kappa(\sin\theta-\sin\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell})},\text{for }\ell=1,\dots,L.

From (
III-B
) and

\bm{\Delta}(\theta)\triangleq\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}q_{\ell}\mathbf{a}(\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell})-\mathbf{a}(\theta)
,
it follows

\eta(\theta)=\Re\!\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}q_{\ell}\,\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\mathbf{a}(\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell})-\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\mathbf{a}(\theta)\right\}.

(28)

Since
\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\mathbf{a}(\theta)
is purely imaginary, (
28
) reduces to

\eta(\theta)=\Re\!\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}q_{\ell}\,\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\mathbf{a}(\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell})\right\}.

(29)

For each attacker angle
\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell}
,

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\mathbf{a}(\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell})

\displaystyle=j\kappa\cos\theta\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}m\,e^{j\kappa m(\sin\theta-\sin\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell})}

\displaystyle=j\kappa\cos\theta\,S_{1}(r_{\ell}),

(30)

where
S_{1}(r_{\ell})\triangleq\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}mr_{\ell}^{m}
is the first weighted geometric sum. For
r_{\ell}\neq 1
,

S_{1}(r_{\ell})=\frac{r_{\ell}\big(1-Mr_{\ell}^{M-1}+(M-1)r_{\ell}^{M}\big)}{(1-r_{\ell})^{2}},

(31)

whereas for
r_{\ell}=1
, i.e.,
\theta=\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell}
,

S_{1}(1)=\frac{M(M-1)}{2}
.

Substituting into (
29
) gives

\eta(\theta)=\Re\!\left\{j\kappa\cos\theta\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}q_{\ell}\,S_{1}(r_{\ell})\right\}.

(32)

Using
\Re\{jz\}=-\Im\{z\}
, we obtain

\eta(\theta)=-\kappa\cos\theta\;\Im\!\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}q_{\ell}\,S_{1}(r_{\ell})\right\}.

(33)

Next, since
\|\mathbf{a}(\theta)\|^{2}=\mathbf{a}(\theta)^{H}\mathbf{a}(\theta)=M
is constant, it follows that

\Re\{\ddot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\mathbf{a}(\theta)\}=-\|\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)\|^{2}=-\Gamma(\theta)
. Then, from (
10
),

D(\theta)=-\Re\!\left\{\ddot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\mathbf{s}\right\}
.

Define
\nu_{\ell}(\theta)\triangleq\kappa(\sin\theta-\sin\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell}).
Then,

\displaystyle\ddot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)^{H}\mathbf{a}(\theta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\Big(-j\kappa m\sin\theta-\kappa^{2}m^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\Big)e^{jm\nu_{\ell}(\theta)}

\displaystyle=-j\kappa\sin\theta\,S_{1}(r_{\ell})-\kappa^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\,S_{2}(r_{\ell}),

(34)

where
S_{2}(r_{\ell})\triangleq\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}m^{2}r_{\ell}^{m}
is the second weighted geometric sum. For
r_{\ell}\neq 1
,

\displaystyle S_{2}(r_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{\ell}}{(1-r_{\ell})^{3}}\Big(1+r_{\ell}-M^{2}r_{\ell}^{M-1}

\displaystyle+\big(2M^{2}-2M-1\big)r_{\ell}^{M}-(M-1)^{2}r_{\ell}^{M+1}\Big),

(35)

whereas for
r_{\ell}=1
,

S_{2}(1)=\frac{(M-1)M(2M-1)}{6}
.
Therefore,

D(\theta)=\Re\!\left\{j\kappa\sin\theta\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}q_{\ell}\,S_{1}(r_{\ell})+\kappa^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}q_{\ell}\,S_{2}(r_{\ell})\right\},

(36)

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle D(\theta)

\displaystyle=-\kappa\sin\theta\;\Im\!\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}q_{\ell}\,S_{1}(r_{\ell})\right\}+\kappa^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\;\Re\!\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}q_{\ell}\,S_{2}(r_{\ell})\right\}.
