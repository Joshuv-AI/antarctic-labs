Abstract
. A fundamental problem in quantum coding theory is to determine the maximum size of quantum codes of given block length and distance. A recent work introduced bounds based on semidefinite programming, strengthening the well-known quantum linear programming bounds. However, floating-point inaccuracies prevent the extraction of rigorous non-existence proofs from the numerical methods. Here, we address this by providing rational infeasibility certificates for a range of quantum codes. Using a clustered low-rank solver with heuristic rounding to algebraic expressions, we can improve upon 18 18 upper bounds on the maximum size of n n -qubit codes with 6 ≤ n ≤ 19 6\leq n\leq 19 . Our work highlights the practicality and scalability of semidefinite programming for quantum coding bounds.



1.
Introduction

Quantum error correcting codes are widely thought to play an integral part in future quantum computers,
making the computation reliable by protecting quantum information from environmental noise and decoherence.
A long-standing problem is to understand for what parameters a quantum code exists.
In particular, we ask the question:
given block length
n
and distance
\delta
,
what is the maximum code size
K
for which a

(\!(n,K,\delta)\!)_{2}
quantum code exists?
Similar to the classical situation,
both analytical
[
7
,
3
,
37
,
34
,
15
,
14
,
41
]
and linear programming (LP) methods
[
38
,
3
,
32
,
41
]
provide upper bounds on the parameters of quantum codes.
At the same time, explicit
and randomized constructions
serve as lower bounds
[
11
,
29
,
4
,
31
,
39
,
13
,
33
,
2
,
26
]
.
Tables of upper and lower bounds on achievable parameters can be found in Refs.
[
12
,
33
,
16
,
14
]
.

Inspired by semidefinite programming (SDP) bounds for classical codes
[
40
]
,
a recent work by the authors introduced a similar machinery for quantum codes
[
24
]
.
However, both linear and semidefinite programming are limited by the fact that they operate with floating-point arithmetic.
The resulting approximate infeasibility certificates then yield coding bounds that are, at best,
formally unproven but correct;
while, at worst, give entirely spurious bounds due to numerical errors.

Here, we rectify this situation by introducing rigorous certificates that act as formal proofs of coding bounds.
The key idea is to use numerical semidefinite programs from which one can extract rational certificates:
a method introduced by Ref.
[
27
]
and
refined since in a sequence of papers
[
17
,
6
,

,

]
and which is related to central questions in semialgebraic geometry
[

,

]
.
These methods have now also found their way to quantum information theory
[

]
.

In this work, we employ a low-rank solver with a heuristic rounding method by Leijenhorst et al.
[
20
,
5
]
,
available as a Julia package at
https://github.com/nanleij/ClusteredLowRankSolver.jl
. This solver finds an approximate numerical solution, which is then rounded to an exact solution in the field of algebraic numbers.
This results in rigorous proofs of the three coding bounds
found by Ref.
[

as well as improves upon

with block length

All infeasibility certificates can be found at
https://github.com/ganglesmunne/SDP_bounds_on_quantum_codes_rational_certificates
.

2.
Linear programming bounds on quantum codes

A qubit quantum code encodes a
K
-dimensional Hilbert space into a subspace of
n
-qubit Hilbert space.
A code is said to have a distance
\delta
if every
error that acts on at most
\delta-1
subsystems can be detected or
acts as identity on the code space.
Consequently, the code can corrects errors that affect at most

\lfloor(\delta-1)/2\rfloor
subsystems.
A qubit quantum code can then be characterized by its parameters
(\!(n,K,\delta)\!)_{2}
,
where
n
is the block-length,
K
the size of the code, and
2
stands for qubits.
Let
\Pi
be the projector onto a
K
-dimensional code subspace. Denote by
\mathcal{E}_{n}
a matrix basis composed of
n
-fold tensor products of the three Pauli matrices

(1)

X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\,,

and the
2\times 2
identity matrix
\mathds{1}
.
We define the weight
\wt(E)
of an element
E\in\mathcal{E}_{n}
as the number of coordinates

E
acts non-trivially on.
The Knill-Laflamme conditions then give a complete characterization of quantum error-correcting codes
[
18
]
:
Let
\Pi
be a projector onto a code subspace of rank
K
acting on
(\mathds{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
.
The code
\Pi
has distance
\delta
, if and only if
for all
E\in\mathcal{E}_{n}
with
0\leq\wt(E)<\delta
,

(2)

\displaystyle\Pi E\Pi=c_{E}\Pi\,,

holds where
c_{E}\in\mathds{C}
depends only on
E
.
A code with
c_{E}=\tr(E)/2^{n}
for all
E
with

0<\wt(E)<\delta
is called
pure
.

The Knill-Laflamme conditions can also be formulated in the following way
[
30
]
: A projector
\Pi
of rank
K
corresponds to a code with distance
\delta
, if and only if

(3)

\displaystyle KB_{j}(\Pi)=A_{j}(\Pi)\quad\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq j\leq\delta-1\,,

where

(4)

\displaystyle A_{j}(\Pi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\in\mathcal{E}_{n}\\
\wt(E)=j\end{subarray}}\tr(E\Pi)\tr(E^{\dagger}\Pi)\,,\quad\quad B_{j}(\Pi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\in\mathcal{E}_{n}\\
\wt(E)=j\end{subarray}}\tr(E\Pi E^{\dagger}\Pi)\,.

are the quantum weight enumerators.

The two enumerators are linearly related by the
quantum MacWilliams identity

[
38
,
30
]
,

(5)

\displaystyle B_{j}(\Pi)

\displaystyle=2^{-n}\sum^{n}_{i=0}K_{j}(i;n)A_{i}(\Pi)\,,

where
K_{j}(i;n)
are the quaternary Krawtchouk polynomials
[
22
]
,

(6)

\displaystyle K_{j}(i;n)=\sum^{n}_{\alpha=0}(-1)^{\alpha}3^{j-\alpha}\binom{i}{\alpha}\binom{n-i}{j-\alpha}\,.

In terms of the enumerator polynomials
A(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}x^{n-j}y^{j}A_{j}
and likewise for
B(x,y)
, the identity reads

(7)

B(x,y)=A\Big(\frac{x+3y}{2},\frac{x-y}{2}\Big)\,.

The enumerators satisfy

\displaystyle A_{0}(\Pi)

\displaystyle=K^{2}\,,

\displaystyle A_{j}(\Pi)

\displaystyle\geq 0\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\text{for}\quad 0\leq j\leq n\,,

(8)

\displaystyle KB_{j}(\Pi)

\displaystyle\geq A_{j}(\Pi)\quad\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq j\leq n\,,

with equality in the last expression for
0\leq j\leq\delta-1
.

The Shadow enumerator
is also non-negative
[
32
]
,

(9)

\displaystyle S_{j}(\Pi)

\displaystyle=2^{-n}\sum^{n}_{i=0}(-1)^{i}K_{j}(i;n)A_{i}(\Pi)\geq 0\,,

for
0\leq j\leq n
. It can also be expressed in terms of a polynomial transform,

(10)

S(x,y)=A\Big(\frac{x+3y}{2},\frac{y-x}{2}\Big)\,.

To determine whether a quantum code with parameters
(\!(n,K,\delta)\!)_{2}
does not exist, one can formulate the following linear program (LP).

find

\displaystyle\{A_{j}\}^{n}_{j=0}

subject to

\displaystyle A_{0}=K^{2}\,,

\displaystyle A_{j},S_{j}\geq 0\quad\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq j\leq n\,,

\displaystyle KB_{j}\geq A_{j}\quad\quad\text{for}\quad\delta\leq j\leq n\,,

(11)

\displaystyle KB_{j}=A_{j}\quad\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq j\leq\delta-1\,,

and where
B_{j}
and
S_{j}
are linearly related to
A_{j}
via Eqs. (
5
) and (
9
), respectively.
The linear program in Eq. (
2
) can be used to obtain upper bounds on the allowed parameters of quantum codes.
If the LP in Eq. (
2
) is infeasible,
no code with the given parameters exists.

Additional constraints restrict the LP in Eq. (
2
) in
certain cases further.

(a)

Pure codes
are those for which detectable errors result in states that are orthogonal to the unaffected state,

\mathinner{\langle i_{L}|}E\mathinner{|i_{L}\rangle}=0
for all
E
with
0<\wt(E)<\delta
and all logical code basis
\mathinner{|i_{L}\rangle}
.
They satisfy

(12)

\displaystyle A_{j}

\displaystyle=0\quad\quad\quad\text{for}\quad 0<j<\delta\,,

(b)

Self-dual codes
1
1

1

Not to be confused with self-dual CSS codes.
are defined to be pure codes with
K=1

2
2

2

Quantum codes with
K=1
fulfill the Knill-Laflamme conditions trivially.
For this reason, they are additionally assumed to be pure,
which implies that
\tr(\Pi E)=0
for
0<\wt(E)<\delta
with
E\in\mathcal{E}_{n}
.
.
Thus they satisfy Eq. (
12
) and additionally
[
32
]
,

\displaystyle S_{j}

\displaystyle=0\quad\quad\quad\text{if}\quad n-j\quad\text{is odd}\,,

(13)

\displaystyle B_{j}

\displaystyle=A_{j}\hskip-1.70709pt\quad\quad\,\,\,\text{for}\quad 0\leq j\leq n\,.

(c)

Additive (or stabilizer) codes
with parameters
[\![n,k,\delta]\!]_{2}
encodes
k
to
n
qubits, and thus
K=2^{k}
with
k\in\mathds{N}
.
For additive codes

A_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}

and
one of the following two cases holds
[
3
]
,

(14)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}A_{2j}=\begin{cases}2^{n-k-1}\quad\text{(Type I)}\\
2^{n-k}\quad\quad\!\text{(Type II)}\,.\end{cases}

We use parameters
[\![n,k,\delta]\!]_{2}
for additive codes and
(\!(n,K,\delta)\!)_{2}

for general (e.g. non-additive) quantum codes.

Example 1
.

The stabilizer group of the five-qubit additive code
[\![5,1,3]\!]_{2}

is generated by

(15)

S=\langle XZZXI,IXZZX,XIXZZ,ZXIXZ\rangle\,.

Its code projector is

(16)

\Pi=\frac{1}{2^{n-k}}\sum_{s\in S}s\,.

from which we can compute the weight distribution
A(\Pi)=(4,0,0,0,60,0)
.

3.
Semidefinite Programming bounds on quantum codes

We sketch the semidefinite programming (SDP) bounds as derived by Ref.

[
24
, Theorem 22]
.
The formulation is inspired by the fact that the Lovász theta number upper bounds classical codes
[
21
]
,
and corresponds to an intermediate level of a complete hierarchy presented in Ref.
[
24
, Theorem 7]
.

Below, we present three SDP formulations:
the main SDP, a Lovász type SDP, and the symmetry-reduced version of
the main SDP. For the numerical results in Section
4
,
we only use the symmetry-reduced SDP.

3.1.
Main SDP

The key idea is the following: Let
\varrho=\Pi/K
be a state proportional to the projector of a code subspace
\Pi
.
Let
\Gamma
be a matrix which is indexed by all elements of the
n
-qubit Pauli basis

\mathcal{E}_{n}
, with entries determined by the pair
E_{a},E_{b}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}
as

(17)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ab}(\varrho)=\tr(E^{\dagger}_{a}\varrho)\tr(E_{b}\varrho)\tr(E_{a}E^{\dagger}_{b}\varrho)\,.

By convention, we set
E_{0}=\mathds{1}
and thus
\Gamma_{00}(\varrho)=1
.
One can show that
\Gamma(\varrho)
is positive semidefinite,
because it can be constructed as a moment matrix from
\varrho\succeq 0
.
Importantly, the quantum weight enumerators
A_{j}
[Eq. (
4
)] can be obtained from
\Gamma
by summing over its diagonal elements,

(18)

\displaystyle A_{j}(\varrho)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{a}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}\\
\wt(E_{a})=j\end{subarray}}\Gamma_{aa}(\varrho)\,.

From
A_{j}(\varrho)
one can obtain
B_{j}(\varrho)
through the
quantum MacWilliams identity [Eq. (
5
)].
One can thus write the Knill-Laflamme conditions in Eq. (
3
) in terms of the entries of the moment matrix
\Gamma(\varrho)
as

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{K}{2^{n}}\sum^{n}_{i=0}K_{j}(i;n)\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{a}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}\\
\wt(E_{a})=i\end{subarray}}\Gamma_{aa}(\varrho)\quad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{b}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}\\
\wt(E_{b})=j\end{subarray}}\Gamma_{bb}(\varrho)\quad\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq j\leq\delta-1\,.

The matrix
\Gamma(\varrho)
satisfies several additional conditions,
which follow from the fact that
\Gamma

is constructed from a quantum state that is proportional a the projector of rank
K

[
24
, Section 5]
.
Considering only the real part of
\Gamma(\varrho)
,
a necessary condition for an

(\!(n,K,\delta)\!)_{2}
quantum code to exist
is the feasibility of the following semidefinite program,

find

\displaystyle\Gamma\succeq 0\,,

subject to

\displaystyle\Gamma_{00}=1\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ab}=0\hskip-1.13791pt\quad\quad\,\,\text{if}\quad E^{\dagger}_{a}E_{b}=-E_{b}E^{\dagger}_{a}\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ab}=\Gamma_{cd}\,\,\quad\text{if}\quad E_{a}E^{\dagger}_{b}=E^{\dagger}_{b}E_{a}\quad\text{and}\quad(E^{\dagger}_{a},E_{b},\omega_{ab}E_{a}E^{\dagger}_{b})\quad\,

\displaystyle\hskip 93.89418pt\text{is a permutation of}\,\,(E^{\dagger}_{c},E_{d},\omega_{cd}E_{c}E^{\dagger}_{d})\,,

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{b=0}\Gamma_{ab}=\frac{2^{n}}{K}\Gamma_{aa}\,,

(20)

\displaystyle\frac{K}{2^{n}}\sum^{n}_{i=0}K_{j}(i,n)\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{a}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}\\
\wt(E_{a})=i\end{subarray}}\Gamma_{aa}\,\,\,=\,\,\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{b}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}\\
\wt(E_{b})=j\end{subarray}}\Gamma_{bb}\quad\,\text{if}\quad 0\leq j\leq\delta-1\,,

where
N=4^{n}-1
and
\omega_{ab}\in\{\pm 1,\pm i\}
.
If the SDP in Eq. (
3.1
) is infeasible,
then a code with parameters
(\!(n,K,\delta)\!)_{2}
does not exist.

Additional constraints can be imposed to restrict the SDP (
3.1
) further.

(a)

Pure codes.

One can additionally impose

(21)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ab}=0\quad\quad\text{for}\quad 0<\wt(E^{\dagger}_{a}E_{b})<\delta\quad\text{or}\quad 0<\wt(E^{\dagger}_{a})<\delta\quad\text{or}\quad 0<\wt(E_{b})<\delta\,.

(b)

Self-dual codes.
The conditions in (a) apply, together with

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{i=0}(-1)^{i}K_{j}(i,n)\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{a}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}\\
\wt(E_{a})=i\end{subarray}}\Gamma_{aa}

\displaystyle=0\quad\quad\quad\text{if}\quad n-j\quad\text{is odd}\,,

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{K}{2^{n}}\sum^{n}_{i=0}K_{j}(i,n)\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{a}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}\\
\wt(E_{a})=i\end{subarray}}\Gamma_{aa}\,\,\,

\displaystyle=\,\,\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{b}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}\\
\wt(E_{b})=j\end{subarray}}\Gamma_{bb}\quad\,\text{for}\quad 0\leq j\leq n\,,

(c)

Additive codes.
one can further impose that

(23)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ab}\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\Gamma_{ab}\in\mathbb{Z}.

Also, the constraint for additive codes of type I/II reads,

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{a}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}\\
\wt(E_{a})=2j\end{subarray}}\Gamma_{aa}=\begin{cases}2^{n-k-1}\quad\text{(Type I)}\\
2^{n-k}\quad\quad\!\text{(Type II)}\,.\end{cases}

Remark 2
.

Note that of the original LP constraints (
2
),
Eq. (
3.1
)
only contains non-negativity of
A_{j}\geq 0
and the Knill-Laflamme conditions,
but not

KB_{i}-A_{i}\geq 0
and
S_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
.
While they can, in principle, be added via the diagonal elements of
\Gamma
, they did not make any difference for the codes in our table.

Remark 3
.

Ref.
[
24
]
lists

\tr(\Gamma)=2^{n}/K
as additional SDP constraint.
However, one can check that this constraint is redundant, as it can be obtained by combining

(25)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0b}=\Gamma_{bb}\,,\quad\sum^{N}_{b=0}\Gamma_{0b}=\frac{2^{n}}{K}\Gamma_{00}\,,\quad\Gamma_{00}=1\,.

3.2.
Lovász SDP for self-dual codes

A relaxation of the main SDP
can be done in terms of the Lovasz number
for self-dual codes (
K=1
).
This drops or modifies the last three conditions of Eq. (
3.1
):
a) the group structure imposed on
\Gamma
is relaxed to a unitary and anti-commutativity structure, resulting in
\Gamma_{aa}=\Gamma_{a0}
and
\Gamma_{ab}=0
if
E_{a}E_{b}=-E_{b}E_{a}
;
the constraint of the
b) Knill-Laflamme conditions (
19
)
for the case of pure codes [Eq. (
21
)]
are
applied;
c) the off-diagonal projector constraints are dropped.

In what follows, we consider simple graphs
G=(V,E)
only, i.e. unweighted, undirected graphs without loops.
The size of the largest subset of vertices in
G
in which no pair of vertices are adjacent is the independence number
\alpha(G)
.
In the context of coding theory, a classical code can be interpreted as a graph
G
whose independence number
\alpha(G)
equals the size of the code
[
21
]
.
Although computing this quantity is NP-complete
[
9
]
,
it is known that
\alpha(G)\leq\vartheta(G)
, where
\vartheta(G)
is the Lovász number of a graph
[
21
]
.
Thus, the Lovász number can be used to upper bound the size of a classical code.
It is known that
\vartheta
can be computed in polynomial time via an SDP,
and the symmetry-reduction of a slight variation of it
leads to the classical Delsarte bound based on linear programming
[
36
]
.

A quantum analogue was derived in Ref.
[
24
]
for self-dual quantum codes via the formulation in Eq. (
3.1
).
To see this, consider a self-dual quantum code
and
define a graph
G
whose vertices
V
are those Pauli strings whose weights are larger or equal to the code distance, i.e. all
E_{a}\in V
for which
\wt(E_{a})\geq\delta
.
Let two vertices
a
and
b
be connected, written
a\sim b
, if
E_{a},E_{b}\in V
satisfies

E_{a}E^{\dagger}_{b}=-E_{b}E^{\dagger}_{a}
or
0<\wt(E_{a}E^{\dagger}_{b})<\delta
.
The Lovász theta number can be defined in terms of an SDP as
[
21
,
8
]
,

\displaystyle\vartheta(G)=\max_{M}\quad

\displaystyle\tr(M)

subject to

\displaystyle M_{aa}=m_{a}\quad\,\,\text{for all}\quad a\in V\,,

\displaystyle M_{ab}=0\quad\,\,\quad\text{if}\quad a\sim b,

(26)

\displaystyle\Delta=\begin{pmatrix}1&m^{T}\\
m&M\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0\,.

Ref.
[
24
]
showed that if a self-dual quantum code with parameters
(\!(n,1,\delta)\!)_{2}
exists, then the Lovász number of the graph
G
satisfies
2^{n}\leq 1+\vartheta(G)
.
This is seen by noting that the structure of Eq. (
3.2
) is obtained by relaxing some conditions of Eq. (
3.1
) in the case of self-dual codes.
In particular, the matrix
\Delta
is equivalent to the relaxed version of
\Gamma
, where all rows and columns corresponding to the elements with weight
0<\wt(E_{a})<\delta
are removed.
That these rows and columns can be removed follows from
\Gamma\succeq 0
together with Eq. (
21
).
By Remark
3
, self-dual codes satisfy
\tr(\Gamma)=2^{n}
and thus,
\tr(\Delta)=\tr(M)+1
must be at least
2^{n}
.
Therefore,
while the Lovász number bounds the
size
of classical codes,
it also bounds the
existence
of self-dual quantum codes.

3.3.
Symmetry-reduced SDP

For numerical purposes, the SDP (
3.3
) scales in manner
far beyond current solver capabilities
(for details see Remark
4
).
We thus use a symmetry-reduced version based on the non-binary Terwilliger algebra
[
10
]
.
We explain how this reduction works.
Recall that the quantum weight enumerators are obtained by averaging over all Pauli strings of a given weight [see Eq. (
4
)].
In particular,
the non-negative function
\tr(E\Pi)\tr(E^{\dagger}\Pi)
is averaged over all Pauli strings of constant weight to obtain the

A
-enumerator.
In a similar spirit we average the matrix
\Gamma

while keeping its positive semidefiniteness.

Denote by
s(E)
the support of a Pauli string, that is, the subsystems it acts non-trivially on.
Denote by
\wt(E)=|s(E)|
its weight.
Now average the matrix
\Gamma
as follows,

(27)

\displaystyle\lambda^{t,p}_{i,j}(\varrho)=\!\!\!\!\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{a},E_{b}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}\\
|s(E^{\dagger}_{a})|\,=\,i\\
|s(E_{b})|\,=\,j\\
\lvert s(E^{\dagger}_{a})\cap s(E_{b})\rvert\,=\,t\\
\lvert s(E^{\dagger}_{a}E_{b})\rvert\,=\,i+j-t-p\\
\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\Gamma_{ab}(\varrho)\,.

We call
\lambda^{t,p}_{i,j}(\varrho)
the
matrix weights
, and it can be checked that

(28)

A_{i}(\varrho)=\lambda^{0,0}_{i,0}(\varrho)=\lambda^{i,i}_{i,i}(\varrho)

for all
i
.
Furthermore,
\lambda^{t,p}_{i,j}(\varrho)=\lambda^{t,p}_{j,i}(\varrho)
since
\Gamma_{ab}(\varrho)=\Gamma_{ba}(\varrho)
.
Note that the range of valid
(i,j,t,p)
for any
E_{a},E_{b}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}
is restricted to the set

(29)

\mathcal{I}(n)=\{(i,j,t,p)\,:\,0\leq p\leq t\leq i,j\quad\text{and}\quad i+j\leq t+n\,\}\,.

Following the symmetry-reduction based on the Terwilliger algebra
[
10
]
, one has

(30)

\Gamma(\varrho)\succeq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,k\in\mathds{N}_{0}\\
0\leq a\leq k\leq n+a-k\end{subarray}}\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,p\in\mathds{N}_{0}\\
0\leq p\leq t\leq i,j\\
i+j\leq t+n\end{subarray}}\alpha(i,j,t,p,a,k)x_{i,j}^{t,p}(\varrho)\right)_{i,j=k}^{n+a-k}\succeq 0\,,

where
x^{t,p}_{i,j}(\varrho)=\lambda^{t,p}_{i,j}(\varrho)/\gamma^{t,p}_{i,j}
with
\gamma^{t,p}_{i,j}
and
\alpha(i,j,t,p,a,k)
defined in Eq. (
41
) and (
A
) respectively.
From constraints on

\Gamma(\varrho)

one obtains constraints on
x^{t,p}_{i,j}(\varrho)

via
Eq. (
27
).
Then the SDP of Eq. (
3.1
)
can be relaxed to
[
24
]
,

find

\displaystyle x^{t,p}_{i,j}

To certify the infeasibility of a primal problem,
the dual of an SDP can be used:
Consider primal minimization and dual maximization problems

For testing primal feasibility problem one sets

In our case, a solution of Eq. (

To obtain reliable infeasibility certificates, we employ the low-rank SDP solver by Cohn, Leijenhorst, and de Laat

Originally developed to compute bounds on spherical codes, this high-precision primal-dual interior-point solver can exploit both low-rank structures and the clustering of constraints in semidefinite programs.
The solver comes with a heuristic rounding method for extracting solutions over rational or algebraic numbers.

To verify positive semidefiniteness of the obtained solutions,
we rely on matrix diagonalization

4

[
