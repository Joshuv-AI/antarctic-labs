Dressed Fock Spaces in Gauge Theory and Gravity
Sangmin Choi, Prahar Mitra

Abstract
Four-dimensional gauge and gravitational theories exhibit long-range interactions that require asymptotic particles to be dressed by clouds of soft photons and gravitons. Faddeev-Kulish dressings render scattering amplitudes infrared-finite, but the resulting multi-particle states do not factorise into tensor products of dressed one-particle states. We show that this loss of Fock-space factorisation is not fundamental, but reflects an inappropriate choice of infrared variables. The real soft divergence is reproduced by the Goldstone modes of asymptotic symmetries, while the Coulomb phase is reproduced by new zero modes of the radiative fields that we introduce here. In these variables, infrared-finite dressed multi-particle states admit the usual Fock-space factorisation into single-particle dressed states.



I
Introduction

In the scattering problem, one considers a quantum system whose Hamiltonian
H
can be decomposed into a free part
H_{0}
and an interaction
V
,
H=H_{0}+V
. The scattering amplitude is then defined as

\begin{split}S_{{\beta}{\alpha}}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\bra{\b}e^{iH_{0}T}e^{-iH(2T)}e^{iH_{0}T}\ket{\a}.\end{split}

(1)

In the standard treatment, the initial and final states are taken to be collections of particles that are widely separated at early and late times. These are well approximated by non-interacting multi-particle eigenstates of
H_{0}
, namely Fock states,

\begin{split}\bra{\b}&=\bra{0}{\mathcal{O}}^{+}_{1}(p_{1})\cdots{\mathcal{O}}^{+}_{m}(p_{m}),\\
\ket{\a}&={\mathcal{O}}^{-}_{m+1}(p_{m+1})\cdots{\mathcal{O}}^{-}_{n}(p_{n})\ket{0},\\
\end{split}

(2)

where
{\mathcal{O}}_{i}^{+}(p_{i})
(
{\mathcal{O}}_{i}^{-}(p_{i})
) is an annihilation (creation) operator for the
i
th particle. We suppress spin, helicity, internal quantum numbers, Bose/Fermi symmetrisation, and the use of normalisable wavepackets, none of which will play an essential role in this paper. We will refer to the scattering amplitude for such states as the Fock amplitude.

The description above is appropriate in theories with a mass gap. It fails, however, in four-dimensional gauge and gravitational theories, where photons and gravitons mediate long-range interactions that persist even when particles are far-separated. In such theories, the Fock amplitude vanishes due to infrared divergences
Mott 1931
;
Bloch and Nordsieck 1937
;
Yennie et al. 1961
;
Weinberg 1965
. This problem was investigated in QED by Faddeev and Kulish (FK)
Kulish and Faddeev 1970
(building on earlier work of Chung

, Kibble
Kibble 1968a
;
Kibble 1968b
;
Kibble 1968c
;
Kibble 1968d
, and Dollard
Dollard 1964
), and in gravity by Ware, Saotome and Akhoury
Ware et al. 2013
. These works showed that infrared-finite amplitudes can be obtained by replacing ordinary Fock states with dressed asymptotic states of the form

\begin{split}\,_{\text{FK}}\bra{\b}&=\bra{\b}Z^{+}_{\beta},\qquad\ket{\a}_{\text{FK}}=Z^{-}_{\alpha}\ket{\a}.\end{split}

(3)

Scattering amplitudes evaluated in the FK states are finite and non-vanishing
Chung 1965
;
Kulish and Faddeev 1970
;
Choi et al. 2018
. The dressing factor
Z
is a unitary operator that has the form
e^{R}e^{i\Phi}
.
R
is linear in the soft (low-energy) photon and graviton creation and annihilation operators and linear in the charge- or energy-density operator, so the operator
e^{R}
produces a coherent cloud of soft photons and gravitons. In particular, this implies that FK states have finite energy, but do not have a well-defined particle number. On the other hand, the operator
\Phi
is quadratic in the charge- or energy-density operator, so that the “Coulomb phase operator”
e^{i\Phi}
acts on pairs of one-particle states.
1
1

1

In the language of quantum optics,
e^{R}
is a
displacement operator
for soft photons and gravitons and a
phase-shift operator
for matter particles.
e^{i\Phi}
is a
multi-mode cross-Kerr operator
for matter particles.
This implies that a multi-particle FK state does not factorise into a tensor product of one-particle dressed states, e.g.

\begin{split}\,_{\text{FK}}\!\bra{\b}&\neq\bra{0}{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}^{+}_{1}(p_{1})\cdots{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}^{+}_{m}(p_{m}),\\
\ket{\a}_{\text{FK}}&\neq{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}^{-}_{m+1}(p_{m+1})\cdots{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}^{-}_{n}(p_{n})\ket{0}.\end{split}

(4)

This lack of factorisation of FK states obscures several basic properties of dressed amplitudes. For example, it is not known in general how to formulate an LSZ reduction formula directly for dressed amplitudes (see
Dybalski 2017
for related work); and the factorisation of dressed amplitudes on physical poles is subtle (see
Lippstreu 2025
for related work).

These observations motivate the central question of this paper: is the failure of Fock-space factorisation a fundamental feature of four-dimensional gauge and gravitational theories, or is it a consequence of the particular framework used to describe the infrared sector? In this paper, we reformulate the dressing so that the infrared-divergent factor of the amplitude is captured by single-particle operators acting on an enlarged asymptotic Hilbert space. This space contains both hard particle degrees of freedom and zero-energy soft modes, and the hard and soft sectors commute. In this description, ordinary asymptotic creation and annihilation operators admit a soft-hard factorisation

\begin{split}{\mathcal{O}}^{\pm}_{i}(p_{i})=U_{i}^{\pm}(p_{i}){\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}^{\pm}_{i}(p_{i}),\end{split}

(5)

where
U^{\pm}_{i}
acts only on the soft sector, while
{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}^{\pm}_{i}
creates or annihilates the corresponding hard particle in the dressed asymptotic Hilbert space. We show that correlators of
U^{\pm}_{i}
reproduce the infrared-divergent factors of the ordinary Fock amplitude. It follows that the scattering amplitude evaluated in the tensor product multi-particle states

\begin{split}\,_{\text{D}}\!\bra{\b}&=\bra{0}{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}^{+}_{1}(p_{1})\cdots{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}^{+}_{m}(p_{m}),\\
\ket{\a}_{\text{D}}&={\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}^{-}_{m+1}(p_{m+1})\cdots{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}^{-}_{n}(p_{n})\ket{0}\end{split}

(6)

is infrared finite. These states can be expressed in terms of the Fock states (
2
) as

\begin{split}\,_{\text{D}}\!\bra{\b}&=\bra{\b}{\tilde{Z}}^{+}_{\beta},\qquad\ket{\a}_{\text{D}}={\tilde{Z}}^{-}_{\alpha}\ket{\a},\end{split}

(7)

where

\begin{split}{\tilde{Z}}_{\beta}^{+}=(U_{1}^{+}\cdots U_{m}^{+})^{-1},\qquad{\tilde{Z}}_{\alpha}^{-}=(U_{m+1}^{-}\cdots U_{n}^{-})^{-1}.\end{split}

(8)

In essence, the many-body FK dressing (
3
) is replaced here by a product of single-particle soft operators whose contractions reproduce the same infrared phase.

The asymptotic Hilbert space comprising states of the form (
6
) therefore admits a Fock-like tensor-product representation, and the associated amplitude is infrared finite and non-vanishing. In particular, the construction preserves the advantages of FK dressing while restoring the tensor product structure needed for standard notions of asymptotic particles, factorisation, and scattering amplitudes. This suggests that infrared-finite scattering in four-dimensional gauge theories and gravity can be formulated in a way that is fully compatible with a Fock-space description, provided the soft sector is represented appropriately.

To construct the operators
U_{i}

;
Pasterski 2021
;
Raclariu 2021

;

;

;
He et al. 2024b
;
Choi and Mitra 2026

also
be represented in terms of soft degrees of freedom, but
not
in terms of the aforementioned Goldstone modes. Instead, we uncover two
new
zero-energy scalar modes

that live on the three-dimensional hyperboloid

that arises in the de Boer-Solodukhin description of the blow-up of
i^{\pm}

;
Campiglia and Laddha 2015

The paper is organised as follows. In section
II
, we review infrared divergences in four-dimensional Abelian gauge theories, and also review how the real infrared divergence is reproduced by a Goldstone dressing. In section

\begin{split}&\langle{\tilde{A}}^{\pm}_{a}(\nu,P){\tilde{A}}^{\pm}_{b}(\nu^{\prime},P^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{ie^{2}}{2}{\delta}(\nu+\nu^{\prime})\\
&\qquad\qquad\times[\partial_{a}\partial^{\prime}_{b}+\nu^{2}\partial_{a}{\sigma}\partial^{\prime}_{b}{\sigma}][e^{i\nu{\sigma}}(\coth{\sigma}-i\nu)].\end{split}

(36)

This is derived by solving the Schwinger-Dyson equation that follows from the action (
35
). This is a second-order differential equation. One of the integration constants is fixed by requiring that the coincident point singularity
{\sigma}\to 0
matches the one in flat spacetime. The other integration constant is fixed by Neumann boundary conditions on
\partial H_{3}
.
8
8

8

For every
\tau
,
\partial H_{3}
is the celestial sphere on
{\mathcal{I}}^{\pm}
(see Figure
1
). The Neumann boundary condition corresponds to allowing radiative flux through the celestial boundary.

Setting
\nu=\nu^{\prime}=0
, we find that the two-point function of the zero mode
N_{a}^{\pm}
is given by

\begin{split}\langle N^{\pm}_{a}(P)N^{\pm}_{b}(P^{\prime})\rangle&=\frac{ie^{2}}{2}{\delta}(0)\partial_{a}\partial^{\prime}_{b}\coth{\sigma}.\end{split}

(37)

From this, it follows that
N^{\pm}_{a}
is purely longitudinal and we can write

\begin{split}N^{\pm}_{a}\equiv\partial_{a}N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}}.\end{split}

(38)

Note that, just like the zero mode
C(x)
(
20

N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}}
is only defined up to additive shifts,

\begin{split}N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}}(P)\sim N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}}(P)+c.\end{split}

(39)

The two-point function of
N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}}
can be easily extracted from (
37
). Before we do that, let us discuss the divergent factor
{\delta}(0)
. This factor arises from the infinite volume of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\pm}
, as
{\delta}(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\text{d}}\tau
and is therefore related to the infrared divergences in the gauge theory. In the previous section, we regulated this infrared divergence by introducing the cutoffs
\mu
and
\Lambda
. These same cutoffs must therefore also regulate
{\delta}(0)
. Since the cutoffs are imposed in energy space, we need to relate the parameter
\nu
to
{\omega}
. To do this, we exploit the now standard relationship between the scaling dimension
\Delta=1+i\nu
and energy
{\omega}
given by the Mellin transform

\begin{split}2\pi{\delta}(\nu-\nu^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\text{d}}{\omega}\,{\omega}^{i(\nu-\nu^{\prime})-1}.\end{split}

(40)

Setting
\nu=\nu^{\prime}

\begin{split}{\delta}(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\text{d}}{\omega}}{{\omega}}.\end{split}

(41)

The divergent integral is precisely the logarithmic infrared divergence in gauge theories. We use the cutoffs to regulate this
\int_{0}^{\infty}\to\int_{\mu}^{\Lambda}

\begin{split}{\delta}_{\text{reg}}(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}.\end{split}

(42)

With all this, we find that the longitudinal mode is Gaussian, and its connected two-point function is

\begin{split}\langle N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}}(P)N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}}(P^{\prime})\rangle&=\frac{ie^{2}}{4\pi}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\coth{\sigma}.\end{split}

(43)

This is precisely the two-point function of a massless scalar field in
H_{3}
with Neumann boundary conditions (
\Delta=0
). In addition, we also have

\begin{split}\langle N^{+}_{\text{ph}}(P)N^{-}_{\text{ph}}(P^{\prime})\rangle&=0.\end{split}

(44)

This follows from the fact that the full Maxwell action has the form
S|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{+}}+S|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{-}}+S|_{\mathcal{D}}
and so the
N^{+}
and
N^{-}
modes decouple from each other. Altogether, we have

\begin{split}\langle N^{\eta}_{\text{ph}}(P)N^{\eta^{\prime}}_{\text{ph}}(P^{\prime})\rangle&=\frac{ie^{2}}{4\pi}{\delta}_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\coth{\sigma}.\end{split}

(45)

To end this section, we present an alternative (but related) derivation of (
38
) and (
45
). We start with (
35
) and separate the integral over
\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}
into a piece
\nu\in[-{\epsilon},{\epsilon}]
and a separate piece

\begin{split}S^{\pm}&\sim\frac{1}{e^{2}}\int_{-{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon}\frac{{\text{d}}\nu}{2\pi}\!\int_{H_{3}}\!\!{\text{d}}^{3}P\!\left(-\frac{1}{4\nu^{2}}|{\tilde{F}}^{\pm}_{ab}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|{\tilde{A}}^{\pm}_{a}|^{2}\right).\end{split}

(46)

As we take

\nu=0

\begin{split}S^{\pm}&\sim\frac{{\epsilon}}{2\pi e^{2}}\int_{H_{3}}{\text{d}}^{3}P(\partial_{a}N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}})^{2}.\end{split}

(47)

This is the action of a massless scalar on
H_{3}
and its connected two-point function (with Neumann boundary conditions) is

\begin{split}\langle N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}}(P)N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}}(P^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{ie^{2}}{2{\epsilon}}\coth{\sigma}.\end{split}

(48)

This is precisely (

Dressing the Coulomb Phase

In this section, we show that the zero mode introduced in the previous section also contributes to the Wilson line dressing (

To evaluate this, we therefore proceed as follows. We consider the path

that starts at the origin

on

The appearance of the reference point

\begin{split}i{\tilde{\Phi}}^{\eta_{i}}_{i}=i\eta_{i}Q_{i}\left[N^{\eta_{i}}_{\text{ph}}(P_{i})-\langle N^{\eta_{i}}_{\text{ph}}(P_{\star})\rangle\right].\end{split}

(51)

All we have done is replace the operator

with its one-point function
\langle N^{\eta_{i}}_{\text{ph}}(P_{\star})\rangle

Now that we have constructed the dressing, we can use the two-point function (

\begin{split}\langle\prod_{i}{:}e^{i{\tilde{\Phi}}^{\eta_{i}}_{i}}{:}\rangle=e^{-i\imaginary{\Gamma}_{\text{ph}}}\end{split}

V
Infrared-Finite Fock Amplitudes in Gravity

The construction of the zero mode in perturbative gravity exactly mirrors the construction in gauge theory. We very briefly review this here.

The infrared-divergent soft factor (
9
) for gravity is given by

\begin{split}{\Gamma}_{\text{gr}}&=\frac{i{\kappa}^{2}}{8}\sum_{ij}\eta_{i}\eta_{j}\int_{\mu}^{\Lambda}\frac{{\text{d}}^{4}\ell}{(2\pi)^{4}}\\
&\qquad\quad\times\frac{(p_{i}\cdot p_{j})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}m_{i}^{2}m_{j}^{2}}{(\ell^{2}-i{\epsilon})(p_{i}\cdot\ell-i\eta_{i}{\epsilon})(-p_{j}\cdot\ell-i\eta_{j}{\epsilon})},\end{split}

(53)

where
{\kappa}=\sqrt{32\pi G_{N}}
. This evaluates to

\begin{split}{\Gamma}_{\text{gr}}&=-\frac{{\kappa}^{2}}{64\pi^{2}}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\sum_{ij}\eta_{i}\eta_{j}m_{i}m_{j}\frac{{\sigma}_{ij}\cosh( 2 \s_{ij} )}{\sinh{\sigma}_{ij}}\\
&\qquad\quad-\frac{i{\kappa}^{2}}{64\pi}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\sum_{i\neq j}{\delta}_{\eta_{i},\eta_{j}}m_{i}m_{j}\frac{\cosh( 2 \s_{ij} )}{\sinh{\sigma}_{ij}}.\end{split}

(54)

\real{\Gamma}_{\text{gr}}
is reproduced by an operator
\tilde{R}_{{\text{gr}},i}^{\eta_{i}}
that is qualitatively similar to the one in QED (see
Ware et al. 2013
;
Choi et al. 2018
for details). Gravitational theories are invariant under diffeomorphisms which act as

\begin{split}h_{\mu\nu}(x)\to h_{\mu\nu}(x)+\partial_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}(x)+\partial_{\nu}\xi_{\mu}(x).\end{split}

(55)

As in gauge theories, it is convenient to decompose this into a “radiative part”
{\hat{h}}_{\mu\nu}(x)
and a pure gauge mode
C_{\mu}(x)

\begin{split}h_{\mu\nu}(x)={\hat{h}}_{\mu\nu}(x)+\partial_{\mu}C_{\nu}(x)+\partial_{\nu}C_{\mu}(x),\end{split}

(56)

where
{\hat{h}}_{\mu\nu}
is defined in terms of the linearised Riemann tensor as

\begin{split}{\hat{h}}_{\mu\nu}(x)=-2x^{\rho}x^{\sigma}\int_{0}^{1}{\text{d}}s\,s(1-s)R^{(1)}_{\mu\rho\nu{\sigma}}(sx).\end{split}

(57)

The pure gauge mode here is defined up to global Poincaré transformations,

\begin{split}C_{\mu}(x)\sim C_{\mu}(x)+a_{\mu}+{\omega}_{\mu\nu}x^{\nu}.\end{split}

(58)

The gauge transformation (
55
) then acts as

\begin{split}{\hat{h}}_{\mu\nu}(x)\to{\hat{h}}_{\mu\nu}(x),\qquad C_{\mu}(x)\to C_{\mu}(x)+\xi_{\mu}(x).\end{split}

(59)

Small diffeomorphisms (
\xi_{\mu}\to 0
at infinity) are redundancies in our description of the system, but large diffeomorphisms are physical symmetries known as BMS supertranslations and superrotations
Bondi et al. 1962
;
Sachs 1962
;
Barnich and Troessaert 2010
. The Ward identities associated with these symmetries are the leading and subleading soft-graviton theorems, respectively
Strominger 2014
;
He et al. 2015
;
Cachazo and Strominger 2014
. Supertranslations are spontaneously broken by the Fock vacuum down to the four global translations and the associated Goldstone mode is denoted by

Kalyanapuram 2021
;
Kapec and Mitra 2022
and its two-point function is

\begin{split}\langle C_{\text{gr}}({\hat{q}})C_{\text{gr}}({\hat{q}}^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{{\kappa}^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}(-{\hat{q}}\cdot{\hat{q}}^{\prime})\ln( - {\hat q} \cdot{\hat q}' ).\end{split}

(60)

This is the two-point function for a two-dimensional scalar field with
\Delta=-1

Kapec and Mitra 2022
;
Choi and Mitra 2026

VI
Dressing for Massless Particles

The dressings introduced in the previous sections are defined only when the particles are massive. In this section, we explain how we can modify the dressings when one or more particles involved in the scattering amplitude are massless.

Consider the case where
m_{i}\to 0
. In this limit
p_{i}^{\mu}\to q_{i}^{\mu}={\omega}_{i}{\hat{q}}_{i}^{\mu}
, where we parametrise the null momentum
q_{i}
in terms of the energy
{\omega}_{i}
and the direction
{\hat{q}}_{i}^{\mu}
. From its definition, we see that the rescaled vector (
11
) diverges as
P_{i}^{\mu}\to\frac{{\omega}_{i}}{m_{i}}{\hat{q}}_{i}^{\mu}
. In the embedding formalism, this is the standard limit which maps a bulk point
P_{i}
in AdS to a boundary point
{\hat{q}}_{i}
with the conformal factor being
z_{i}=m_{i}/{\omega}_{i}
. The massless dressing can then be obtained by taking the usual extrapolate limit of our bulk AdS operators.

Let us do this for gauge theories first. The dressing is given by

\begin{split}U_{i}&=\exp\left(i\eta_{i}Q_{i}\int{\text{d}}^{2}{\hat{q}}\,{\mathcal{K}}_{2}(P_{i},{\hat{q}})C_{\text{ph}}({\hat{q}})\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad{:}\exp\left(i\eta_{i}Q_{i}N_{\text{ph}}^{\eta_{i}}(P_{i})\right){:}.\end{split}

(73)

For the first term in the exponential, we note that the boundary limit of the bulk-boundary propagator (
26
) is given by

\begin{split}{\mathcal{K}}_{\Delta}\left(P_{i},{\hat{q}}\right)&~\to~z_{i}^{2-\Delta}[{\delta}^{(2)}({\hat{q}}_{i},{\hat{q}})+\cdots]\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{\Delta-1}{\pi}\left[\frac{z_{i}^{\Delta}}{(-2{\hat{q}}_{i}\cdot{\hat{q}})^{\Delta}}+\cdots\right],\end{split}

(74)

where the
\cdots
denote subleading terms in
z_{i}=m_{i}/{\omega}_{i}
. For the second term in the exponential, we recall that
N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}}(P)
is a bulk scalar operator with
\Delta=0
. The associated boundary operator is then given by the extrapolate dictionary as

\begin{split}N^{\eta}_{\text{ph}}({\hat{q}})=\lim_{z\to 0}N^{\eta}_{\text{ph}}(P).\end{split}

(75)

The boundary two-point function can also be obtained by applying the extrapolate dictionary,

\begin{split}\langle N^{\eta}_{\text{ph}}({\hat{q}})N^{\eta^{\prime}}_{\text{ph}}({\hat{q}}^{\prime})\rangle&=\frac{ie^{2}}{4\pi}{\delta}_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}.\end{split}

(76)

Using (
74
) and (
75
), we then find that in the massless limit the dressing reduces to

\begin{split}U_{i}&=e^{i\eta_{i}Q_{i}C_{\text{ph}}({\hat{q}}_{i})}\,{:}e^{i\eta_{i}Q_{i}N_{\text{ph}}^{\eta_{i}}({\hat{q}}_{i})}{:}\,.\end{split}

(77)

In particular, we see that in the massless limit, the operator
U_{i}
reduces to a simple boundary operator. Using (
22
) and (
76
), it is easy to show that this dressing correctly reproduces the infrared divergence in gauge theories.

It should be noted that in a gauge theory with charged massless particles, the structure of infrared divergences is more complicated than the one presented in (
12
). This is due to collinear divergences that arise in addition to soft divergences, both of which contribute to the infrared-factorisation of the amplitude. Part of this can be fixed by replacing the first exponential operator in (
77
) by a normal-ordered exponential that removes the singularities that occur when
{\hat{q}}_{i}\to{\hat{q}}_{j}
. This, however, does not by itself give a complete treatment of the collinear sector. We leave a more systematic discussion of this issue for future work.

In gravitational theories, the dressing is

\begin{split}U^{\text{gr}}_{i}&=\exp\left(-i\eta_{i}m_{i}\int{\text{d}}^{2}{\hat{q}}\,{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(P_{i},{\hat{q}})C_{\text{gr}}({\hat{q}})\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad{:}\exp\left(-i\eta_{i}m_{i}N^{\eta_{i}}_{\text{gr}}(P_{i})\right){:}.\end{split}

(78)

The massless limit of the first term in the exponential can be determined using (
74
). For the second term in the exponential, we recall that
N^{\pm}_{\text{gr}}(P)
is a bulk scalar operator with
\Delta=-1

VII
Summary and Conclusions

In this work, we have argued that the failure of FK dressed states to factorise into a product of one-particle states is not a fundamental feature of gauge and gravitational theories, and have demonstrated that when expressed in terms of the appropriate soft modes, the Hilbert space of dressed states again admits a tensor product structure like that of a Fock space. The central ingredient in this construction is a novel zero mode. By analysing the structure of the gauge and gravitational field within the light cone, we have identified a gauge-invariant zero-energy degree of freedom that is distinct from the Goldstone mode associated with large gauge transformations and supertranslations. The two-point function of this zero mode exactly reproduces the imaginary part of the infrared divergence, namely the Coulomb phase.

This fills an important gap in the literature. While it is known that the Goldstone modes reproduce the real part of the infrared divergence, the corresponding construction for the Coulomb phase has been less clear. For massless matter, the Coulomb phase can be accounted for through a shift prescription in the Goldstone two-point function
Gonzo et al. 2022
. To the best of our knowledge, however, no construction in terms of soft degrees of freedom has been available for the Coulomb phase in the general massive case. Our analysis shows that the missing ingredients are the gauge-invariant zero modes
N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}}
and
N^{\pm}_{\text{gr}}
. Moreover, the massless limit of our result correctly reproduces the shift prescription. This provides a derivation of the prescription from first principles.

We close by discussing a few directions for future work. First, the field
C_{\text{ph}}(P)
on
H_{3}
that appears in the massive dressing is not an independent degree of freedom, but is related to the Goldstone mode
C_{\text{ph}}(\hat{q})
on the celestial sphere by a bulk-to-boundary propagator, as in (
25
). It would be interesting to understand whether the new zero mode
N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}}(P)
admits a similar description in terms of the soft-photon mode
N^{\pm}(\hat{q})
on the celestial sphere, which would identify it as the symplectic partner of the Goldstone mode. If this is the case, then the massive construction developed here may provide a useful framework for studying the symmetry interpretation of simultaneous double soft limits. The sum of two generic null momenta is timelike. Thus, the total momentum associated with a generic simultaneous soft limit naturally defines a point on
H_{3}
. The zero mode
N^{\pm}_{\text{ph}}
may provide the appropriate ingredient for identifying the symmetries associated with such limits.

Second, the soft modes that appear in our construction correspond to fields on
H_{3}
at special values of the mass (
m^{2}=0
for gauge theory and
m^{2}=-1
for gravity). At these special points, we find that the action exhibits an enhanced symmetry. It would be interesting to understand this structure further, and in particular to explore whether it can be related to the infinite-dimensional symmetry algebras that appear in celestial holography, such as
w_{1+\infty}
.

It would also be useful to make the relation to the traditional FK dressings more explicit. The dressings constructed here reproduce the same infrared divergences as the FK dressings, so there should be a relation between the two descriptions. For the real part of the dressing, such a relation is already understood in terms of Goldstone modes. Our results suggest that the new zero modes are the corresponding variables for the Coulomb phase. It would be interesting to see whether an appropriate extension of the soft phase space makes the equivalence with the phase part of the FK dressing manifest.

The tensor-product factorisation of the dressed Hilbert space also allows one to revisit questions that were obscured by the non-factorisability of the traditional description. In particular, it would be very interesting to formulate an LSZ-like reduction formula directly for these dressed states. One would also like to understand how the dressed amplitudes factorise into lower-point amplitudes when an internal momentum goes on shell.

Finally, it would be instructive to extend this construction to higher dimensions. Although the logarithmic infrared divergences of four dimensions are absent in higher-dimensional gauge and gravitational theories, the underlying soft structure remains. It is therefore natural to ask whether the zero modes identified here have analogues in higher dimensions.
