Finding a Solution to the Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv Theorem in Linear Time
Sunghyeon Jo

Abstract
The Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv theorem states that every sequence of 2 ​ n − 1 2n-1 integers contains a subsequence of length n n whose sum is divisible by n n . Choi, Kang, and Lim gave a simple deterministic O ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) O(n\log n) algorithm for finding such a subsequence, and Leung recently improved this to O ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ log ⁡ log ​ n ) O(n\log\log\log n) . We give a deterministic linear-time algorithm. The core is a linear-time algorithm for the following prime target subset-sum problem: given p − 1 p-1 nonzero residues in ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} and a target residue, find a subset with the prescribed sum. Our algorithm maintains a compact arithmetic-progression representation of reachable sums. When two progressions intersect, a bounded Frobenius interval in their sum allows them to be merged into one longer progression, with enough growth to pay for the update. When the representation either contains a full progression or covers all nonzero residues, the target residue is recovered constructively. The standard multiplicative reduction then extends the prime algorithm to arbitrary moduli.



1
Introduction

Let
A=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{2n-1})
be a sequence of integers. The
constructive Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv problem asks for indices

I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,2n-1\}
such that

|I|=n,\qquad\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{n}.

The classical multiplicative proof reduces the composite case to
smaller moduli, so the algorithmic core is the prime case.

For prime
p
, after sorting the input by residues, either some residue
class occurs at least
p
times, or the problem reduces to the
following target subset-sum task.

Given nonzero residues
d_{1},\ldots,d_{p-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\ast}
and a target

\tau\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, find
J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,p-1\}
such that

\sum_{j\in J}d_{j}=\tau.

Cauchy–Davenport proves existence, since

\{0,d_{1}\}+\cdots+\{0,d_{p-1}\}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

The question is how to find one representation in linear time without
maintaining the full sumset after every insertion.

The algorithm of Choi, Kang, and Lim grows a represented sumset by one
new residue at a time and uses
O(\log p)
time per inserted
difference. Leung’s later algorithm first transforms the input into a
small number of arithmetic progressions and then packs those
progressions in linear time; the transformation phase is responsible
for the remaining
O(n\log\log\log n)
factor. Our algorithm removes
this preliminary transformation. It maintains arithmetic-progression
components online, and every detected intersection is immediately
converted into a merge that lengthens the representation.

2
Arithmetic progression components

All residues in this section are elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, where
p
is
prime. For
v\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\ast}
and
0\leq\ell\leq p-1
, write

\operatorname{AP}(v,\ell)=\{0,v,2v,\ldots,\ell v\}.

The
covered nonzero residues
of this progression are

v,2v,\ldots,\ell v
.

The algorithm processes
d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{p-1}
from left to right.
After some prefix has been processed it maintains active components

C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}
. Each component
C
has:

v(C)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\ast},\qquad 0\leq\ell(C)\leq p-1,\qquad\xi(C)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

It also has a reconstruction map: for every integer

0\leq t\leq\ell(C)
, a subset
R_{C}(t)
of the processed input indices
assigned to
C
is represented such that

\sum_{i\in R_{C}(t)}d_{i}=\xi(C)+t\,v(C)\pmod{p}.

The assigned input-index sets of distinct active components are
disjoint. Define the global shift

X=\sum_{C}\xi(C).

Definition 1
.

The active family is
disjoint
if the covered nonzero residues of
all active components are pairwise disjoint. Equivalently, no two
pairs
(C,t)
and
(D,u)
, with
1\leq t\leq\ell(C)
and

1\leq u\leq\ell(D)
, cover the same nonzero residue.

Lemma 2

(reachable set invariant)
.

If the active components are
C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}
, then every residue in

X+\operatorname{AP}(v(C_{1}),\ell(C_{1}))+\cdots+\operatorname{AP}(v(C_{r}),\ell(C_{r}))

is the sum of a subset of the processed input differences. Moreover,
such a subset can be recovered from the component reconstruction maps.

Proof.

Choose coefficients
0\leq t_{j}\leq\ell(C_{j})
. The union of the
disjoint subsets
R_{C_{j}}(t_{j})
has sum

\sum_{j}\bigl(\xi(C_{j})+t_{j}v(C_{j})\bigr)=X+\sum_{j}t_{j}v(C_{j}).

This is exactly the desired residue.
∎

3
The merge lemma

The essential operation replaces two colliding progressions by one
longer progression.

Lemma 3

(bounded Frobenius interval)
.

Let
x,y
be coprime positive integers with
x,y>1
. Let

\ell\geq x
and
m\geq y
, and put

F=(x-1)(y-1),\qquad S=\ell y+mx.

Then every integer
N
with

F\leq N\leq S-F

has a representation

N=\alpha y+\beta x,\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq\ell,\quad 0\leq\beta\leq m.

Proof.

Fix a residue class modulo
x
. Since
\gcd(x,y)=1
, there is a
unique
a_{0}\in\{0,\ldots,x-1\}
such that
a_{0}y
belongs to this
class. For this class, all representations with coefficient of
y

congruent to
a_{0}
modulo
x
are obtained from the intervals

[a_{0}y+kxy,\;a_{0}y+kxy+mx]\qquad(k\geq 0),

restricted to integers in the fixed residue class. Consecutive
intervals overlap or touch in that residue class: their starting points
differ by
xy
, while the available
\beta x
term covers

0,x,2x,\ldots,mx
, and
m\geq y
.

Let
a_{1}
be the largest integer at most
\ell
with

a_{1}\equiv a_{0}\pmod{x}
. Thus every integer in the fixed residue class
between
a_{0}y
and
a_{1}y+mx
is represented with

0\leq\alpha\leq\ell
and
0\leq\beta\leq m
.

It remains to check that the interval
[F,S-F]
has no element of this
residue class outside these endpoints. If an integer
N
in this
residue class were smaller than
a_{0}y
, then

N\leq a_{0}y-x\leq(x-1)y-x=F-1
, so
N<F
. Hence every

N\geq F
in the class is at least
a_{0}y
.

For the upper endpoint, suppose that
N\equiv a_{1}y+mx\pmod{x}
and

N>a_{1}y+mx
. Then
N\geq a_{1}y+mx+x
. But

S-F-(a_{1}y+mx)=(\ell-a_{1})y-(x-1)(y-1)\leq x-1,

because
0\leq\ell-a_{1}\leq x-1
. Therefore no integer in the same
residue class can be both larger than
a_{1}y+mx
and at most
S-F
.
This proves that every
N\in[F,S-F]
is represented.
∎

Lemma 4

(progression merge)
.

Let two active components have progressions

\operatorname{AP}(v,\ell)
and
\operatorname{AP}(w,m)
. Suppose there are positive integers

x\leq\ell
and
y\leq m
such that

xv=yw\quad\text{in }\mathbb{Z}_{p},

and suppose
\gcd(x,y)=1
. Put

g=v/y=w/x\quad\text{in }\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Then the two components can be replaced by one component with direction

g
and length
L
as follows.

If
x=1
, set

F=0,\qquad L=m+\ell y.

Equivalently, for
x=1
and
v=yg,w=g
,

\operatorname{AP}(v,\ell)+\operatorname{AP}(w,m)=\operatorname{AP}(g,m+\ell y).

If
y=1
, set symmetrically
F=0
and
L=\ell+mx
.

If
x,y>1
, set

F=(x-1)(y-1),\qquad L=mx+\ell y-2F.

Then

Fg+\operatorname{AP}(g,L)\subseteq\operatorname{AP}(v,\ell)+\operatorname{AP}(w,m).

If
L\geq p-1
, the algorithm may replace this by length
p-1
,
because
\operatorname{AP}(g,p-1)=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
In both cases the replacement is constructive: a coefficient

0\leq t\leq L
can be converted into coefficients

0\leq\alpha\leq\ell
and
0\leq\beta\leq m
satisfying

F+t=\alpha y+\beta x.

Proof.

First suppose
x=1
. Then
v=yg
and
w=g
, and the collision
condition gives
y\leq m
. For each
\alpha=0,\ldots,\ell
, the set

\alpha yg+\operatorname{AP}(g,m)
is the interval

\{\alpha yg,\ldots,(\alpha y+m)g\}
. Consecutive intervals overlap or
touch because
m\geq y
. Their union is

\operatorname{AP}(g,m+\ell y)
. The case
y=1
is symmetric.

Now assume
x,y>1
. Since
v=yg
and
w=xg
,

\operatorname{AP}(v,\ell)+\operatorname{AP}(w,m)=\{(\alpha y+\beta x)g:0\leq\alpha\leq\ell,\;0\leq\beta\leq m\}.

By Lemma
3
, every integer

N\in[F,\ell y+mx-F]
has a bounded representation

N=\alpha y+\beta x
. Taking
N=F+t
for

0\leq t\leq\ell y+mx-2F=L
gives the desired inclusion.
∎

Remark 5

(computing the bounded coefficients)
.

For the case
x=1
, a coefficient
0\leq t\leq m+\ell y
is represented
by

\alpha=\min\{\ell,\lfloor t/y\rfloor\},\qquad\beta=t-\alpha y.

Then
0\leq\beta\leq m
: if
\alpha<\ell
, then
\beta<y\leq m
, and if

\alpha=\ell
, this follows from
t\leq m+\ell y
. The case
y=1
is
symmetric, using

\beta=\min\{m,\lfloor t/x\rfloor\},\qquad\alpha=t-\beta x.

For
x,y>1
, the proof of Lemma
3
gives an explicit
formula once
y^{-1}\pmod{x}
is known. Let

a_{0}\equiv Ny^{-1}\pmod{x},\qquad 0\leq a_{0}<x,\qquad b_{0}=\frac{N-a_{0}y}{x}.

The proof above gives
b_{0}\geq 0
. Put

k=\max\left\{0,\left\lceil\frac{b_{0}-m}{y}\right\rceil\right\},\qquad\alpha=a_{0}+kx,\qquad\beta=b_{0}-ky.

Then
N=\alpha y+\beta x
, and the endpoint argument in
Lemma
3
gives
0\leq\alpha\leq\ell
and

0\leq\beta\leq m
. This is constant time on a word RAM once the
integer inverse of
y
modulo
x
is available.

4
Prime target subset sum

We now give the linear algorithm for the target subset-sum task.

Algorithm
PrimeTarget

Input:
prime
p
, nonzero residues

d_{1},\ldots,d_{p-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\ast}
, and target
\tau\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

1.

Start with no active components and global shift
X=0
.

2.

For
k=1,\ldots,p-1
:

(a)

Insert the new factor
\{0,d_{k}\}
. If there is an
active component
C
with direction
d_{k}
, first try to extend

C
in place from length
\ell(C)
to
\ell(C)+1
. This only
requires checking the new covered residue

(\ell(C)+1)d_{k}
. If it is not already covered, install this
one new residue. If the total length is
p-1
, stop;
otherwise continue. If the new residue creates a collision,
make the extended component the temporary component. If there
is no active component with direction
d_{k}
, create a temporary
component
\operatorname{AP}(d_{k},1)
.

(b)

Merge the temporary component with the active
disjoint family. Scan its unheld covered residues in increasing
coefficient order. When the first collision with an active
component is found, remove that active component and merge the
two components by Lemma
4
. The merge result
becomes the new temporary component. If its untruncated length
is at least
p-1
, truncate it to length
p-1
, record it as a
full component, and stop.

(c)

If the temporary component has no collision, add it to
the active family. If the total length is
p-1
,
stop.

3.

If a full component
C_{\ast}
was produced, let
X
denote the
sum of the offsets of
C_{\ast}
and of all other active components.
Choose coefficient
0
in every other active component and choose
the unique coefficient
t\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
satisfying

t\,v(C_{\ast})=\tau-X
.

4.

Otherwise the final family is disjoint. Find
\tau-X
. If it
is
0
, choose coefficient
0
in every active component.
Otherwise it is covered by exactly one active component; choose
the corresponding coefficient there and coefficient
0
in all
other components.

5.

Reconstruct and output the union of the component subsets.

Lemma 6
.

PrimeTarget
returns a subset
J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,p-1\}

such that
\sum_{j\in J}d_{j}=\tau
.

Proof.

The reachable-set invariant holds initially. Extending an existing
component of direction
d_{k}
is valid because

\operatorname{AP}(d_{k},\ell)+\{0,d_{k}\}=\operatorname{AP}(d_{k},\ell+1).

Creating a new component is the same identity with
\ell=0
. If the
new endpoint of an in-place extension is not already covered, the
family remains disjoint and the invariant is immediate. If it
collides, the extended component enters the same merge loop as any
other temporary component. Merging is valid by Lemma
4
.
If the first collision gives
xv=yw
, then
\gcd(x,y)=1
. Otherwise, with

h=\gcd(x,y)>1
, the smaller equality

(x/h)v=(y/h)w
would give an earlier unheld temporary coefficient
colliding with the same active component; it cannot be a held cell
because held cells are disjoint from active cells. The new component
has offset equal to the sum of the two old offsets plus
Fg
, and its
reconstruction map is obtained by converting each coefficient
t
to
bounded coefficients
\alpha,\beta
and then using the two old
reconstruction maps.

Thus Lemma
2
holds after every update.

There are two termination cases. First, a merge may produce a
component
C_{\ast}
whose untruncated length is at least
p-1
. After
truncation to length
p-1
, its progression

\operatorname{AP}(v(C_{\ast}),p-1)
is all of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Therefore, with coefficient

0
in every other component, the full component alone realizes the
residue
\tau-X
, where
X
includes the offset of
C_{\ast}
.

Second, no full component is produced. Then the algorithm stops in a
disjoint state with no temporary component: either after an in-place
extension or after adding a temporary component to the active family.
During the merge loop, count the temporary component as part of the
represented family. Inserting a new difference increases the total
length by one, and every non-full merge has new length at least the sum
of the two lengths it replaces, by the length formulas in
Lemma
4
. Hence after processing all
p-1
differences,
or earlier if the algorithm stops by the length test, the final total
length is at least
p-1
. Disjointness implies that the covered
nonzero residues are pairwise disjoint elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\ast}
, so the
total length is at most
p-1
. Therefore the covered nonzero residues
are exactly all of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\ast}
. If

\tau-X=0
, choose coefficient
0
in every component; otherwise choose
the unique covered occurrence of
\tau-X
.

In both cases Lemma
2
converts the chosen
coefficients into a subset of the input differences with sum
\tau
.
∎

5
Linear implementation

We use the word-RAM convention that arithmetic, division with
remainder, and modular inverses on
O(\log p)
-bit words are
constant-time operations. The inverse needed by a merge node is stored
with that node. The data structure is direct.

Maintain an owner array
O[1],\ldots,O[p-1]
. If residue
r
is
currently covered by an installed active component
C
as

t\,v(C)
, then
O[r]=(C,t)
; otherwise
O[r]
is empty. A component
also stores the list of owner-array cells it currently owns.

The merge loop is performed with one temporary component. Most of its
covered cells are not installed in the owner array. The only exception
is a prefix inherited from a removed active component in the special
cases below; such cells are marked as
held
by the temporary
component and are not scanned again. When a prefix is retained, the
corresponding component record is reused for the new temporary
component, so the owner-array entries do not have to be rewritten. The
held cells remain disjoint from all active cells.

We scan the unheld covered residues of the temporary component and
store the scanned empty cells in a temporary list; the owner array is
not changed during this scan. If no collision is found, the temporary
list is written to the owner array, the held cells become ordinary
owner cells of the same component, and the component becomes active.
If a collision with an active component
D
is found, the temporary
list is discarded,
D
is removed from the active family, and the two
components are algebraically merged into a new temporary component.
Owner cells that cannot be retained as a prefix of the new temporary
component are cleared at this moment. During one lifetime of a
temporary component, each unheld covered cell is scanned at most
once: after the first collision that lifetime ends. Thus between
merge-loop steps, the installed active family is disjoint; during a
merge loop, the only non-active owner cells are explicitly held by
the current temporary component.

For same-direction collisions, the temporary component and the collided
active component have
x=y=1
, and their merge is simply another
progression in that direction. We coalesce them by keeping the owner
entries already held by the active component as a held prefix of the
new temporary component, and installing only the new suffix when the
coalesced component becomes active.

In-place extension of an active component of direction
d_{k}
is handled
similarly: the old owner entries are kept, and only the new endpoint is
queried. If it is empty, one cell is written. If it collides, the
extended component is detached from the active family and treated as
the temporary component, with its old owner cells held, for the
following merge.

Lemma 7

(amortized update bound)
.

All owner-array updates and collision searches in
PrimeTarget

take
O(p)
total time.

Proof.

Let

\Lambda=\sum_{C}\ell(C)

be the total length of the active components and, during a merge-loop
step, the current temporary component. Held cells of a
temporary component are counted once, as part of that temporary
component. We evaluate
\Lambda
only before and after an insertion or
merge transition; removing an active component and creating the merged
temporary component are one transition. With this convention, inserting
a new difference increases
\Lambda
by one.

We use the following accounting convention. An unheld temporary cell
carries constant credit, enough to scan it once and, if no collision is
found first, install it once. A fresh singleton receives this credit
from the input insertion; in an in-place extension, the same unit of
insertion credit pays for the one endpoint query and write. When a
merge creates a new temporary component, its cost is charged to the
credit already carried by old temporary cells, plus the positive
increase in
\Lambda
when the merge has one. The case analysis below
shows that this pays for the work and for the credit carried by the
resulting temporary component. Held prefix cells need no new credit
because they are not rewritten or scanned again.

Consider a merge of a temporary component of length
\ell
and an
active component of length
m
. Until the final paragraph of this
proof, assume that the untruncated new length is less than
p-1
; a merge that
reaches length
p-1
is the final update and is handled separately.

If
x,y>1
, the new length is

L=mx+\ell y-2(x-1)(y-1).

The increase in length is

\Delta=L-\ell-m=(m-y)(x-1)+(\ell-x)(y-1)+x+y-2.

Since
\ell\geq x
and
m\geq y
, this is positive. Moreover

\Delta-(\ell+m-2)=(m-y)(x-2)+(\ell-x)(y-2)\geq 0.

Thus the scan that finds the collision, deletion of the old covered
cells of the active component, later installation of the new covered
cells, and the credit assigned to the new temporary component cost

O(\ell+m+L)=O(\Delta)
. Scanned empty cells of the old temporary
component are discarded when the collision is found; their work was
paid by their own credit.

If
x=1
and
y>1
, then the active component has direction
g
and
length
m
, while the temporary component has direction
yg
. The new
progression is
\operatorname{AP}(g,m+\ell y)
. We keep the owner entries for the
old direction-
g
component as a held prefix and only scan or install
the suffix
m+1,\ldots,m+\ell y
. Cells held by the old temporary
component are not prefix cells in the new direction and are cleared.
No term depending on
m
appears, because the old direction-
g
owner
cells are retained rather than scanned, deleted, or reinstalled.
The work is
O(\ell+\ell y)
, and this is
O(\Delta)
for

\Delta=\ell(y-1)
and
y>1
.

If
y=1
and
x>1
, then the temporary component already has direction

g
, while the active component has direction
xg
. The active
component is deleted, and the new progression is

\operatorname{AP}(g,\ell+mx)
. The old temporary prefix is either already held or
is paid for by the credit on its unheld cells; its scan or installation
work is not charged again. The remaining work is to delete
m
active
cells and to scan or install a suffix of length
mx
. Since

mx+m=O(m(x-1))=O(\Delta)
for
x>1
, this remaining work is

O(\Delta)
.

Finally consider the same-direction case
x=y=1
. This merge has no
increase in total length. The installed prefix already owned by the
active component is kept as a held prefix, and no old owner cell is
rewritten or scanned again. The only cells that may still be
scanned or installed are suffix cells supplied by the temporary
component, and each such cell is paid for by the credit it already
carries. Repeated same-direction coalescences therefore charge
distinct temporary cells and do not need an increase in
\Lambda
.

Under these abstract transitions,
\Lambda
is nondecreasing: insertions
increase it by one, same-direction coalescences preserve it, and all
other non-full merges increase it by the positive
\Delta
computed
above. Before a full component is produced, the active family is
disjoint and has total length at most
p-1
, while the current
temporary component, if any, has length at most
p-1
. Hence

\Lambda<2p
throughout all non-final merge-loop work, and the total
positive increase available for the charges above is
O(p)
. If a
merge creates untruncated length at least
p-1
, this is the final
update; its scan and deletions cost
O(p)
, and we spend
O(p)
once
to install the full progression. Thus all owner-array work is linear.
∎

Lemma 8
.

The final reconstruction in
PrimeTarget
takes
O(p)
time.

Proof.

Each component is represented by a merge/extension tree whose leaves
are input indices. An extension node either keeps the previous
coefficient or uses the new index once. A merge node converts one
coefficient
t
into two child coefficients
\alpha,\beta
by the
formula in the remark after Lemma
4
. Then it recurses
on the two children.

Every recursive branch that reaches a leaf outputs that leaf at most
once, because active components use disjoint input indices and each
merge combines disjoint subtrees. Nodes with coefficient
0
may still
encode a nonempty offset, but their expansion is part of the same
merge tree and outputs only leaves belonging to that component. The
number of leaves over all active components is at most the number of
processed differences, which is at most
p-1
; in the full-component
termination case, include that full component in the same count. Hence
reconstruction is linear.
∎

Theorem 9
.

PrimeTarget
solves the prime target subset-sum task in

O(p)
time and
O(p)
space.

Proof.

Correctness is Lemma
6
. The owner-array work
is
O(p)
by Lemma
7
; reconstruction is
O(p)
by
Lemma
8
. All other work is a linear scan of
the input and component metadata. The arrays and merge trees store

O(p)
cells and nodes.
∎

6
Prime EGZ

Algorithm
PrimeEGZ

Input:
prime
p
and integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{2p-1}
.

1.

Reduce the input modulo
p
and counting-sort the indices by
residue. Let the sorted sequence be

b_{1},\ldots,b_{2p-1}
.

2.

If for some
1\leq i\leq p
we have

b_{i}\equiv b_{i+p-1}\pmod{p}
, output

b_{i},\ldots,b_{i+p-1}
.

3.

Otherwise define, for
1\leq i\leq p-1
,

d_{i}=b_{i+p}-b_{i}\pmod{p},\qquad\tau=-\sum_{j=1}^{p}b_{j}\pmod{p}.

Use
PrimeTarget
to find

J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,p-1\}
with

\sum_{i\in J}d_{i}=\tau
.

4.

Output

\{b_{i}:1\leq i\leq p,\;i\notin J\}\cup\{b_{i+p}:i\in J\}.

Theorem 10
.

PrimeEGZ
returns
p
input elements whose sum is divisible by

p
in
O(p)
time.

Proof.

If Step 2 succeeds, sortedness implies that all
p
elements in the
block have the same residue; their sum is
0
modulo
p
.

Otherwise no residue occurs
p
times. Hence

b_{i}\not\equiv b_{i+p}\pmod{p}
for every
1\leq i\leq p-1
, so every

d_{i}
is nonzero. By Theorem
9
,

PrimeTarget
finds
J
with
\sum_{i\in J}d_{i}=\tau
. The
output is obtained from the base set
b_{1},\ldots,b_{p}
by replacing

b_{i}
by
b_{i+p}
for each
i\in J
. Its sum is

\sum_{j=1}^{p}b_{j}+\sum_{i\in J}(b_{i+p}-b_{i})\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{p}b_{j}+\tau\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

The output has exactly
p
elements. Counting sort and all later
steps are linear in
p
.
∎

7
Arbitrary moduli

The standard multiplicative reduction preserves linearity.

Algorithm
EGZ

Input:
integer
n\geq 1
and integers

a_{1},\ldots,a_{2n-1}
.

1.

If
n=1
, return the single element.

2.

If
n
is prime, call
PrimeEGZ
.

3.

Otherwise choose a prime divisor
p
of
n
and write

n=pq
.

4.

For
h=1,\ldots,2q-1
, take any
2p-1
remaining elements,
apply
PrimeEGZ
to find
p
of them whose sum is divisible
by
p
, remove this block
B_{h}
, and set

c_{h}=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{x\in B_{h}}x.

5.

Recursively solve
EGZ
for modulus
q
on

c_{1},\ldots,c_{2q-1}
. If it selects
q
of the values
c_{h}
,
output the union of the corresponding blocks
B_{h}
.

Theorem 11
.

The constructive Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv problem can be solved
deterministically in
O(n)
time and
O(n)
space.

Proof.

Correctness is the usual multiplicative proof. Each block
B_{h}
has

p
elements and sum
pc_{h}
. The recursive call selects
q
values

c_{h}
whose sum is divisible by
q
. Therefore the union of the
corresponding
q
blocks has
pq=n
elements and total sum

p\sum c_{h},

which is divisible by
pq
.

Let
T(n)
be the running time. We may precompute smallest prime
factors up to
n
in
O(n)
time and space, so choosing a prime divisor
at each recursive step stays within the final linear bound. The
block-generation loop can be implemented by a
single pool of unused elements: each call consumes
2p-1
elements from
the pool and returns the unselected
p-1
elements to it. Hence every
input element participates in
O(1)
pool operations at this recursion
level. If
n=pq
with
p
prime, the composite step makes
2q-1
calls
to
PrimeEGZ
on modulus
p
, one recursive call on
q
, and
performs linear additional bookkeeping. By Theorem
10
,

T(pq)\leq(2q-1)O(p)+T(q)+O(pq)=T(q)+O(pq).

Since
q\leq n/2
for composite
n
, the recurrence sums to

T(n)\leq O(n)+O(n/2)+O(n/4)+\cdots=O(n).

The arrays, blocks, and reconstruction data use linear space.
∎
