1.
Introduction

The characters of irreducible complex representations of finite groups exhibit interesting arithmetic behavior, especially when considered in infinite families. Note that the character values of finite groups are algebraic integers, in fact, they are sums of roots of unity. One may study the average arithmetic behavior of character tables for a family of finite groups and ask how often the entries satisfy a prescribed congruence or
divisibility condition. The most prominent example is the family of
symmetric groups
S_{N}
. For this family, the character values are integers. The irreducible characters and conjugacy classes
of
S_{N}
are both parametrized by partitions of
N
, and hence the
character table has
p(N)^{2}
entries, where
p(N)
denotes the number of partitions of
N
.

Miller
[
10
]
conjectured, based on extensive computations, that for
every fixed prime number
\ell
, almost every entry in the character table of

S_{N}
is divisible by
\ell
as
N\to\infty
. This conjecture was proved
by Peluse and Soundararajan
[
12
]
. They showed that
if a prime
\ell\leq(\log N)/(\log\log N)^{2}
, then the number of entries in the
character table of
S_{N}
which are not divisible by
\ell
is
O\left(p(N)^{2}N^{\frac{-1}{12\ell}}\right)
. In particular, for every fixed prime
\ell
, the proportion of entries in the
character table of
S_{N}
not divisible by
\ell
tends to
0
as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Prior to this, the conjecture was known for
\ell\leq 13
, cf.
[
8
,
14
]
. Subsequently, Peluse and Soundararajan
[
13
]
proved the stronger prime-power form of Miller’s conjecture. Namely, they showed that for every fixed prime power
\ell^{r}
, almost every entry in the character table of
S_{N}
is divisible by
\ell^{r}
as
N\to\infty
. Such results rely on both combinatorial methods as well as tools from analytic number theory; they underscore the growing scope of arithmetic statistics in representation theory.

The purpose of the present paper is to investigate analogous questions
for a different natural family of finite groups, namely
G_{q}=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
where
q
ranges over powers of odd primes. We shall see that the answer for these groups is
different from that of the family of symmetric groups
S_{N}
as
N\rightarrow\infty
. We fix a prime number
\ell
. Instead of almost every
entry being divisible by
\ell
, we show that the limiting proportion
of entries not divisible by
\ell
is exactly
1/2
. For each odd prime power
q
, let

N_{\ell}(q)
denote the number of entries in the character table of

G_{q}
which are not divisible by
\ell
, in the sense of divisibility in
the ring of algebraic integers. Let
M_{0}(q)
(resp.
M_{0}^{\prime}(q)
) be the number of entries in the character table that are equal (resp. not equal) to
0
. The total number of entries in the character table of
G_{q}
is

M_{0}(q)+M_{0}^{\prime}(q)=(q^{2}-1)^{2}.

We define

\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}(q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{N_{\ell}(q)}{(q^{2}-1)^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}(q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{N_{\ell}(q)}{M_{0}^{\prime}(q)}

and prove the following asymptotic formula.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\ell
be a fixed rational prime. As
q\to\infty
over odd prime
powers, one has

\lim_{q\to\infty}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}(q)=\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{q\to\infty}\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}(q)=1.

That is, asymptotically one half of the entries in the character table
of
G_{q}
are not divisible by
\ell
, and one half are divisible
by
\ell
. Further, most nonzero entries are not divisible by
\ell
.

More precisely, we prove the following quantitative form of Theorem

1.1
. For every fixed prime number
\ell
and every

\epsilon>0
, one has

N_{\ell}(q)=\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon})

as
q\to\infty
over odd prime powers. Moreover, the number
M_{0}(q)
of
zero entries in the character table satisfies

M_{0}(q)=\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon}).

For further details, we refer to Theorem
4.3
. Thus the main contribution to the entries divisible by
\ell
comes from
the zero entries, while almost all nonzero entries are not divisible by

\ell
.

Our result also settles the question for powers of

\ell
. Indeed, if a nonzero algebraic integer is not divisible by

\ell
, then it is not divisible by any power
\ell^{r}
. Since almost all
nonzero entries in the character table of
G_{q}
are not divisible by

\ell
, it follows a fortiori that, for every fixed
r\geq 1
, almost all
nonzero entries are not divisible by
\ell^{r}
. Thus the limiting proportion
of all entries not divisible by
\ell^{r}
is again
1/2
, and the limiting
proportion of nonzero entries not divisible by
\ell^{r}
is
1
.

Miller
[
9

also considered an archimedean analogue of the divisibility
question for the symmetric groups. Since the character values of

are integers, one may ask how often the nonzero entries in the character

are positive or negative. For

an angular-distribution question. We study how the arguments of the nonzero character values are distributed
in
[0,2\pi]
.

Let
\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q})
be the set of isomorphism classes of complex irreducible representations of
G_{q}
. For a nonzero complex number

for its argument. If

is an interval, let

P_{q}(I)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\#\{(\pi,C)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q}),\ C\text{ a conjugacy class of }G_{q},\ \chi_{\pi}(C)\neq 0,\ \operatorname{arg}(\chi_{\pi}(C))\in I\}}{\#\{(\pi,C)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q}),\ C\text{ a conjugacy class of }G_{q},\ \chi_{\pi}(C)\neq 0\}}.

Thus
P_{q}(I)
is the proportion of nonzero entries in the character table
whose argument lies in
I
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
I=[a,b]\subset[0,2\pi]
. As
q\to\infty
over odd prime powers,

\lim_{q\to\infty}P_{q}(I)=\frac{b-a}{2\pi}.

For related work on character values of finite general linear groups, we
mention the work of Gurevich and Howe
[
6
]
. The authors study asymptotics for the normalized trace
\frac{\operatorname{tr}\pi(g)}{\dim\pi}
via a harmonic-analytic approach.

We conclude by mentioning some natural further directions. The results of this paper suggest that the statistics of character tables can vary substantially with the family of finite groups under consideration. It would be interesting to study the analogous questions for other families of finite groups of Lie type. A first natural case is
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, either with
n
fixed and
q\to\infty
, or with
q
fixed and
n\to\infty
. The irreducible characters of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
were parametrized by Green
[
5
]
. More generally, Deligne–Lusztig theory
[

provides a uniform framework for studying irreducible characters of finite reductive groups. We pose the following question for a fixed algebraic group and varying finite field.

2.1.
Conjugacy classes in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

Throughout the paper
q
is a power of an odd prime number and
G_{q}=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. We write
B
,
T
,
N
, and
Z
for the standard Borel subgroup, diagonal torus, unipotent subgroup, and center, respectively. In particular,
B
consists of the upper triangular matrices in
G_{q}
,
T
of the diagonal matrices,
N
of the matrices
\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
, with
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
Z
of the scalar matrices.

The conjugacy classes of
G_{q}
are divided into four families. First, there are the scalar classes
aI
, where
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Second, there are the split regular semisimple classes, represented by
\operatorname{diag}(a,b)
, where
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
a\neq b
. These are parametrized by unordered pairs
\{a,b\}
. Third, there are the elliptic classes, namely the regular semisimple elements whose characteristic polynomial is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
is viewed as a two-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, multiplication gives an embedding

(2.1)

\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\xrightarrow{\sim}G_{q}.

The elliptic classes are represented by
\iota(\alpha)
, with
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Finally, there are the non-semisimple Jordan classes represented by
\begin{pmatrix}a&1\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}
, with
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.
The total number of conjugacy classes equals

(q-1)+\frac{(q-1)(q-2)}{2}+\frac{q^{2}-q}{2}+(q-1)=q^{2}-1.

2.2.
Irreducible representations of
G_{q}

Given a representation

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{q}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(V),

the character of
\rho
is defined as follows:

\chi(g)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{trace}\rho(g)

for
g\in G_{q}
. In this subsection, we recall the standard classification of the irreducible complex representations of
G_{q}
following
[
2
, Ch.2]
.

The first family consists of one-dimensional representations. Such representations are of the form
\chi\circ\det
, where
\chi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a character. There are
q-1
one-dimensional representations.

The second family consists of principal series representations. Let
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be characters. We regard
\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}
as a character of
B
by sending
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}
to
\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(d)
. If
\chi_{1}\neq\chi_{2}
, the induced representation
\operatorname{Ind}_{B}^{G_{q}}(\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2})
is irreducible. Moreover, interchanging
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
gives an isomorphic representation, and these are the only identifications. Therefore, the number of principal series representations is
(q-1)(q-2)/2
.

The third family consists of twists of the Steinberg representation. The representation
\operatorname{Ind}_{B}^{G_{q}}(1)
decomposes as a direct sum
1\oplus\operatorname{St}
, where
\operatorname{St}
is the Steinberg representation. We note that this is also the permutation representation of
G_{q}
on
G_{q}/B
. Twisting the
q
dimensional representation
\operatorname{St}
by
\chi\circ\det
, where
\chi
ranges over the characters of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, gives
q-1
irreducible representations.

The fourth family consists of cuspidal representations, i.e., the irreducible representations not containing the trivial character of
N
. We recall the
explicit construction following
[
2
]
. Let

E=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, viewed as a two-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Then
E^{\times}
embeds in
G_{q}
via the map
\iota
(see (
2.1
)). Fix a nontrivial character

\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}N\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. If

\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a character, then its restriction to

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\subset E^{\times}
gives a character of
Z
. Hence, we obtain
a character
\theta\otimes\psi
of
ZN
by

(\theta\otimes\psi)(zu)=\theta(z)\psi(u),\quad\text{for}\quad z\in Z\quad\text{and}\quad u\in N,

where
Z\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
via
aI\mapsto a
.

A character
\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is called regular if

\theta\neq\theta^{q}
, where
\theta^{q}(\alpha)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\theta(\alpha^{q})
.
Consider the virtual representation

\pi_{\theta}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G_{q}}(\theta|_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\otimes\psi)-\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G_{q}}\theta.

where
\theta|_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}
is interpreted as a character of
Z
. Then
\pi_{\theta}
is in fact a cuspidal representation of

G_{q}
of dimension
q-1
, cf.
[
2
, p. 47]
. Moreover,

\pi_{\theta}\simeq\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\theta^{\prime}=\theta\text{ or }\theta^{\prime}=\theta^{q}.

Every cuspidal representation of
G_{q}
is obtained in this way.

The non-regular characters are precisely those fixed by Frobenius. These
are exactly the characters which factor through the norm map

N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{\times}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, and hence there are
q-1
of them.
Since
E^{\times}
has
q^{2}-1
characters in total, there are

q^{2}-1-(q-1)=q(q-1)
regular characters. Dividing by the Frobenius
equivalence
\theta\sim\theta^{q}
, we obtain
q(q-1)/2
cuspidal
representations.

2.3.
Divisibility of character values

Let
K
be a number field containing the values of the characters under consideration, and let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be its ring of integers. If
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
, we say that
\ell
divides
\alpha
if
\alpha\in\ell\mathcal{O}_{K}
, equivalently if
\alpha/\ell\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
. This notion is independent of enlarging
K
. In particular, since
0/\ell=0
, the value
0
is divisible by every prime
\ell
.

We shall repeatedly use the following elementary observations about sums of roots of unity. Let
\Phi_{n}(x)
denote the
n
-th cyclotomic polynomial.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
n\geq 1
, one has

\Phi_{n}(1)=\begin{cases}0,&n=1,\\
p,&n=p^{r}\text{ for some prime }p\text{ and some }r\geq 1,\\
1,&n>1\text{ and }n\text{ is not a prime power}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For
n=1
, we have
\Phi_{1}(x)=x-1
, and hence

\Phi_{1}(1)=0.

For
n>1
, we have that

\frac{x^{n}-1}{x-1}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\Phi_{d}(x).

Evaluating at
x=1
, we get

n=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\Phi_{d}(1).

We prove the desired formula by induction on
n
. We have
\Phi_{2}(x)=x+1
so the case
n=2
is immediate. Assume that the formula has been proved for all integers
m
with

1<m<n
. We prove it for
n
. First suppose that
n=p^{r}
is a prime power. We find that

p^{r}=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Phi_{p^{j}}(1).

By the induction hypothesis, for
1\leq j<r
, we have

\Phi_{p^{j}}(1)=p.

Therefore,

p^{r}=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}\Phi_{p^{j}}(1)\right)\Phi_{p^{r}}(1)=p^{r-1}\Phi_{p^{r}}(1).

Hence,

\Phi_{p^{r}}(1)=p.

Now suppose that
n>1
is not a prime power. Write its prime factorization as

n=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{a_{i}},

where
s\geq 2
.
By the induction hypothesis, for every proper divisor
d<n
,

\Phi_{d}(1)=\begin{cases}p_{i},&d=p_{i}^{j},\\
1,&d>1\text{ and }d\text{ is not a prime power}.\end{cases}

Since
n
itself is not a prime power, every prime-power divisor of
n
is
proper. Therefore

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
1<d<n\end{subarray}}\Phi_{d}(1)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\prod_{j=1}^{a_{i}}p_{i}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{a_{i}}=n.

Thus,

n=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\Phi_{d}(1)=\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
1<d<n\end{subarray}}\Phi_{d}(1)\right)\Phi_{n}(1)=n\Phi_{n}(1).

Hence
\Phi_{n}(1)=1
when
n
is not a prime power, as required.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\zeta
be a root of unity and let
\ell
be a rational prime.
Then
(1+\zeta)/\ell
is an algebraic integer if and only if either

\zeta=-1
, or
\ell=2
and
\zeta=1
.

Proof.

If
\zeta=-1
, then
1+\zeta=0
, and hence

(1+\zeta)/\ell=0
is an algebraic integer. If
\zeta=1
and

\ell=2
, then
(1+\zeta)/\ell=1
, and this is again an algebraic
integer.

Conversely, suppose that
(1+\zeta)/\ell
is an algebraic integer. Let

m
be the order of
\zeta
. We work in the cyclotomic field

\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
, where
\zeta_{m}
is a primitive
m
-th root of unity,
and let
\Phi_{m}
be the
m
-th cyclotomic polynomial. Replacing

\zeta
by
\zeta_{m}
, the assumption implies that
1+\zeta_{m}
is
divisible by
\ell
as an algebraic integer. Therefore
\ell^{\varphi(m)}
divides
\left|N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})/\mathbb{Q}}(1+\zeta_{m})\right|
. We observe that

\left|N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})/\mathbb{Q}}(1+\zeta_{m})\right|=|\Phi_{m}(-1)|.

According to Lemma
2.1
, we have that

\Phi_{n}(1)=\begin{cases}0,&n=1,\\
p,&n=p^{r}\text{ for some prime }p\text{ and some }r\geq 1,\\
1,&n>1\text{ and }n\text{ is not a prime power}.\end{cases}

When
m=2n
with

\Phi_{2n}(X)=\Phi_{n}(-X)

|\Phi_{m}(-1)|=|\Phi_{n}(1)|.

From this one finds that

2,&m=2^{r}\text{ for some }r\geq 2,\\
p,&m=2p^{r}\text{ for some odd prime }p\text{ and some }r\geq 1,\\
1,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for
m>1
. Assuming that
m>2
, this
contradicts the divisibility

\ell^{\varphi(m)}\mid\left|N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})/\mathbb{Q}}(1+\zeta_{m})\right|.

Therefore the only possible cases are
m=1
, with
\ell=2
, and

m=2
. Equivalently, either
\zeta=1
and
\ell=2
, or

\zeta=-1
.
∎

3.
The Character values

The first step is to compute
\chi(C)
in all
16
cases, where
C
ranges over the four types of conjugacy classes in
G_{q}
from section
2.1
and
\chi
ranges over the four types of irreducible representations of
G_{q}
from section
2.2
. After computing them, we will be able to prove asymptotic formulas for
N_{\ell}(q)
.

If
H\leq G
is a subgroup and
\sigma
is a class function on
H
, then the character of the induced representation
\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}\sigma
is given by

(3.1)

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}\sigma}(g)=\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in G\\
x^{-1}gx\in H\end{subarray}}\sigma(x^{-1}gx);

see
[
15
, Theorem 12, p. 30]
.
We begin by considering the characters of the one-dimensional representations.

Lemma 3.1

(One-dimensional representations)
.

Let
\chi=\varepsilon\circ\det
be a one-dimensional representation of
G_{q}
.
Then we have that

\displaystyle\chi(aI)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(a^{2}),

\displaystyle\chi\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(ab),\quad a\neq b,

\displaystyle\chi\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&1\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(a^{2}),

\displaystyle\chi\left(\iota(\alpha)\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(\alpha^{q+1})\quad\text{for}\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q},

where
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\hookrightarrow G_{q}
is the natural inclusion from (
2.1
).

Proof.

The first three assertions are immediate. We note that for
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

\det\iota(\alpha)=\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha)=\alpha^{q+1}.

Thus the last assertion follows.
∎

Next we compute the character values of principal series representations.

Lemma 3.2

(Principal series representations)
.

Let
\pi=\operatorname{Ind}_{B}^{G}(\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2})
with
\chi_{1}\neq\chi_{2}
, and let
\chi
be its character. Then:

\chi(g)=\begin{cases}(q+1)\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(a),&g=aI,\\[6.0pt]
\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(b)+\chi_{1}(b)\chi_{2}(a),&g\sim\operatorname{diag}(a,b),\ a\neq b,\\[6.0pt]
\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(a),&g\sim\begin{pmatrix}a&1\\
0&a\end{pmatrix},\\[6.0pt]
0,&g\sim\iota(\alpha),\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We use the induced character formula (
3.1
) to find that

\chi(g)=\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in G_{q}\\
x^{-1}gx\in B\end{subarray}}(\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2})(x^{-1}gx).

If
g=aI
, then every conjugate lies in
B
, and the value is simply

\chi(g)=\frac{|G_{q}|}{|B|}\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(a)=(q+1)\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(a),

since
|G_{q}/B|=|\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=q+1
.

If
g
is split semisimple, say
g\sim\operatorname{diag}(a,b)
with
a\neq b
, then
g
is conjugate into
B
by
x\in B\sqcup Bw
where
w=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
. The contribution of all
x\in B
(resp.
x\in Bw
) is
\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(b)
(resp.
\chi_{1}(b)\chi_{2}(a)
). This gives the stated formula.

If
g
is a Jordan block, then it lies in
B
and has a unique eigenvalue
a
. Any eigenvector of
g
must lie in the line spanned by
e_{1}
. Suppose that
x^{-1}gx\in B
. Then since
x^{-1}gx
is not scalar and also has the same eigenvalues as
g
, we may write

x^{-1}gx=\begin{pmatrix}a&\ast\\
0&a\end{pmatrix},

where
\ast\neq 0
. Thus any eigenvector of
x^{-1}gx
must also belong to the line spanned by
e_{1}
. It follows that
x
stabilizes the line spanned by
e_{1}
, i.e.,
x\in B
. We deduce that

\chi(g)=\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(a).

Lastly, if
g\sim\iota(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then it is not conjugate into
B
, since
B
consists of matrices whose characteristic polynomial splits over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Hence
\chi(g)=0
.
∎

Lemma 3.3

(Steinberg representations)
.

Let
\pi=\operatorname{St}
, the Steinberg representation. The character
\chi
of
\pi
is given as follows.

\chi(g)=\begin{cases}q,&g=aI,\\[6.0pt]
1,&g\sim\operatorname{diag}(a,b),\ a\neq b,\\[6.0pt]
0,&g\sim\begin{pmatrix}a&1\\
0&a\end{pmatrix},\\[6.0pt]
-1,&g\sim\iota(\alpha),\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall from Section
2.2
that
\operatorname{Ind}_{B}^{G_{q}}(1)=\operatorname{St}\oplus 1.

Since trace is additive, we have the identity

\chi=\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{B}^{G_{q}}(1)}-1.

The computation of
\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{B}^{G_{q}}(1)}
is the same as in Lemma
3.2
(where the assumption
\chi_{1}\neq\chi_{2}
has not been used in the proof). The result follows from this.
∎

Next we consider characters of cuspidal representations.

Lemma 3.4

(Cuspidal representations)
.

Let

\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a regular character and let
\pi_{\theta}
be the corresponding cuspidal representation as defined in section
2.2
. Let
\chi
denote its character. Then

\chi(g)=\begin{cases}(q-1)\theta(a),&g=aI,\\[6.0pt]
0,&g\sim\operatorname{diag}(a,b),\ a\neq b,\\[6.0pt]
-\theta(a),&g\sim\begin{pmatrix}a&1\\
0&a\end{pmatrix},\\[12.0pt]
-\theta(\alpha)-\theta(\alpha^{q}),&g\sim\iota(\alpha),\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall from section
2.2
that we fix a nontrivial
character

\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}N\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

After identifying
N
with the additive group of
\mathbb{F}_{q}

by

x\longmapsto u_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},

we interpret
\psi
as an additive character
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{q}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. We identify
Z
with
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
by
a\mapsto aI
. Since

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\subset E^{\times}
, the restriction of
\theta
to

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
gives a character of
Z
. We define a character
\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}ZN\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
by

(3.2)

\eta(aI\,u_{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\theta(a)\psi(x),

for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
The standard construction gives the cuspidal representation attached to

\theta
as the virtual representation

\pi=\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G}\eta-\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G}\theta.

Thus we have

\chi(g)=\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G}\eta}(g)-\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G}\theta}(g).

We now compute this difference for each of the four types of conjugacy
classes in
G_{q}
. First suppose that
g=aI
is scalar, with
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Since

aI
is central, we have
x^{-1}gx=aI
for every
x\in G_{q}
. Hence

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G_{q}}\eta}(aI)=\frac{1}{|ZN|}\sum_{x\in G_{q}}\eta(aI)=\frac{|G_{q}|}{|ZN|}\theta(a).

Now

\frac{|G_{q}|}{|ZN|}=q^{2}-1,

and so

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G}\eta}(aI)=(q^{2}-1)\theta(a).

Similarly,

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G_{q}}\theta}(aI)=\frac{1}{|E^{\times}|}\sum_{x\in G_{q}}\theta(a)=\frac{|G_{q}|}{|E^{\times}|}\theta(a).

Since
|E^{\times}|=q^{2}-1
, we get

\frac{|G_{q}|}{|E^{\times}|}=q(q-1).

Therefore

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G}\theta}(aI)=q(q-1)\theta(a).

Subtracting, we obtain

\chi(aI)=(q^{2}-1)\theta(a)-q(q-1)\theta(a)=(q-1)\theta(a).

Next suppose that

g\sim\operatorname{diag}(a,b),\quad\text{where}\quad a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times},\quad\text{with}\quad a\neq b.

We show that both induced characters vanish. Every
element of
ZN
has the form

aI\,u_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}a&ax\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}.

Such an element has only one eigenvalue, namely
a
. In particular,
no element of
ZN
is conjugate to a split regular semisimple element
with two distinct eigenvalues
a
and
b
. Hence there is no

x\in G_{q}
such that
x^{-1}gx\in ZN
, and therefore

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G}\eta}(g)=0.

On the other hand, every element of
E^{\times}
is either scalar
or has irreducible characteristic polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Indeed, if

\alpha\in E^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, then multiplication by

\alpha
has characteristic polynomial equal to the minimal polynomial
of
\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is irreducible of degree
2
. Thus
no element of
E^{\times}
is conjugate to
\operatorname{diag}(a,b)
with

a\neq b
. Hence

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G}\theta}(g)=0

and consequently

\chi(g)=0.

Now suppose that
g
is a non-semisimple Jordan block, with eigenvalue

a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. We write

g=\begin{pmatrix}a&1\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}=aI\,u_{a^{-1}}.

It is more convenient to compute with
g=aI\,u_{1}
. This gives the same conjugacy class, since all nontrivial unipotent
Jordan blocks with eigenvalue
a
are conjugate.

First consider the contribution from
E^{\times}
. Every element of

E^{\times}
is semisimple as an element of
G_{q}
, because
E^{\times}

is contained in a torus. But
g=aIu_{1}
is not semisimple. Hence
g

is not conjugate to any element of
E^{\times}
, and therefore

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G}\theta}(g)=0.

It remains to compute
\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G}\eta}(g)
. By the induced character formula,

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G}\eta}(g)=\frac{1}{|ZN|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in G_{q}\\
x^{-1}gx\in ZN\end{subarray}}\eta(x^{-1}gx).

Suppose that
x\in G_{q}
satisfies
x^{-1}gx\in ZN
. Then from (
3.2
), we have that

\eta(x^{-1}gx)=\eta(aIx^{-1}u_{1}x)=\theta(a)\eta(x^{-1}u_{1}x).

Since
x^{-1}u_{1}x\in ZN
and both its eigenvalues are equal to
1
, we have that
x^{-1}u_{1}x\in N
. Therefore,

\theta(a)\eta(x^{-1}u_{1}x)=\theta(a)\psi(x^{-1}u_{1}x).

Thus we find that

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G_{q}}\eta}(g)=\frac{\theta(a)}{|ZN|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in G\\
x^{-1}u_{1}x\in N\end{subarray}}\psi(x^{-1}u_{1}x).

It is easy to see that
x^{-1}u_{1}x\in N
if and only if
x\in B
, the Borel subgroup of
G_{q}
. Writing

x=\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\
0&t\end{pmatrix}
, a direct computation gives

x^{-1}u_{1}x=u_{t/r}

and therefore,

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G}\eta}(g)=\frac{\theta(a)}{|ZN|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\\
s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\end{subarray}}\psi(t/r).

The summand does not depend on
s
, so the sum over
s
contributes a
factor of
q
. We obtain

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G}\eta}(g)=\frac{\theta(a)}{(q-1)}\sum_{r,t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\psi(t/r).

For fixed
r
, the quotient
t/r
runs once
through
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
as
t
runs through
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Therefore

\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\psi(t/r)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\psi(u).

Since
\psi
is a nontrivial additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi(u)=0,

and hence

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\psi(u)=-1.

It follows that

\sum_{r,t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\psi(t/r)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}(-1)=-(q-1).

Substituting this into the induced character formula gives

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G}\eta}(g)=\frac{\theta(a)}{(q-1)}\cdot(-(q-1))=-\theta(a).

Since the
E^{\times}
-induced character contributes
0
, we obtain

\chi(g)=-\theta(a)

on the non-semisimple Jordan classes.

Finally suppose that
g=\iota(\alpha)

for some
\alpha\in E^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}.
Note that
g
is not conjugate to any element of
ZN
, because every
element of
ZN
has a repeated eigenvalue in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, whereas
g
has
irreducible characteristic polynomial. Therefore

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G_{q}}\eta}(g)=0.

It suffices to compute
\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G_{q}}\theta}(g)
. By the induced character formula,

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G_{q}}\theta}(g)=\frac{1}{|E^{\times}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in G_{q}\\
x^{-1}gx\in E^{\times}\end{subarray}}\theta(x^{-1}gx).

We need to understand the elements
x\in G_{q}
such that

x^{-1}gx\in E^{\times}
. Moreover,
if
x^{-1}gx\in E^{\times}
, then it has the same characteristic
polynomial as
g
with roots
\alpha
and
\alpha^{q}
. Thus we have that
x^{-1}gx\in\{\iota(\alpha),\iota(\alpha^{q})\}
. First suppose that
x^{-1}gx=\iota(\alpha)=g
. The centralizer of
g
is
E^{\times}
since
g
is regular semisimple. Thus there are
|E^{\times}|
such elements, and their total contribution is
|E^{\times}|\theta(\alpha)
.

Next consider the elements
x\in G
such that

x^{-1}gx=\iota(\alpha^{q}).

Choose one
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear automorphism
y\longmapsto y^{q}
of
E
representing
the Frobenius map and identify it with an element
\sigma\in G_{q}
. Note that
\sigma^{-1}=\sigma
. We have that

\sigma^{-1}\iota(\alpha)\sigma=\iota(\alpha^{q}).

Now,
x^{-1}gx=\iota(\alpha^{q})
if and only if

x^{-1}gx=\sigma^{-1}\iota(\alpha)\sigma\iff(x\sigma^{-1})^{-1}g(x\sigma^{-1})=\iota(\alpha)=g.

It follows that all solutions to
x^{-1}gx=\iota(\alpha^{q})
form the coset
E^{\times}\sigma
. Once again, there are

|E^{\times}|
such elements, and their total contribution is

|E^{\times}|\theta(\alpha^{q}).

Therefore

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in G\\
x^{-1}gx\in E^{\times}\end{subarray}}\theta(x^{-1}gx)=|E^{\times}|\theta(\alpha)+|E^{\times}|\theta(\alpha^{q}).

Dividing by
|E^{\times}|
, we get

\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G}\theta}(g)=\theta(\alpha)+\theta(\alpha^{q}).

Since the
ZN
-induced character contributes
0
, we conclude that

\chi_{\theta}(g)=0-\bigl(\theta(\alpha)+\theta(\alpha^{q})\bigr)=-\theta(\alpha)-\theta(\alpha^{q}).

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Density results for average divisibility

In this section, we shall prove the main result of the article. The first large nonzero block comes from evaluating principal series
representations on split regular semisimple conjugacy classes. In this
block, the character values have the form

\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(b)+\chi_{1}(b)\chi_{2}(a),

which is a sum of two roots of unity.

Given a real valued function
f(q)
and a positive function
g(q)
, we write

f(q)=O(g(q))

to mean that
\frac{|f(q)|}{g(q)}
is bounded. Let
\epsilon
be a positive real number and
g_{\epsilon}(q)
be a positive function which depends on
\epsilon
. We write

f(q)=O_{\epsilon}(g_{\epsilon}(q))

to mean that there is a constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
which depends on
\epsilon
such that

\frac{|f(q)|}{g_{\epsilon}(q)}<C_{\epsilon}

for all values of
q
.

The following lemma shows that the
entries in this block which are divisible by
\ell
are rare enough to
contribute only to the error term.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Then the number of ordered quadruples

(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},a,b)\in\left(\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\right)^{2}\times(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{2}

with
\chi_{1}\neq\chi_{2}
and
a\neq b
, for which
\ell
divides

\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(b)+\chi_{1}(b)\chi_{2}(a),

is
O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon})
.

Proof.

Setting

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad t\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=b/a,

we find that

\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(b)+\chi_{1}(b)\chi_{2}(a)=\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(b)\left(1+\frac{\chi_{1}(b)\chi_{2}(a)}{\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(b)}\right)=\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(b)\bigl(1+\rho(t)\bigr).

Since
\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(b)
is a root of unity, it is a unit in the ring
of algebraic integers. Therefore divisibility of
\chi_{\pi}(g)
by

\ell
is equivalent to divisibility of
1+\rho(t)
by
\ell
. By Lemma
2.2
,
1+\rho(t)
is divisible by
\ell
if and only if either:

•

\rho(t)=-1
, or,

•

\ell=2
and
\rho(t)=1
.

We show that these cases occur for at most
O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon})
quadruples
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},a,b)
.

We set
X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}
. There are
(q-1)(q-2)
ordered character pairs
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
with
\chi_{1}\neq\chi_{2}
and
(q-1)(q-2)
ordered element pairs
(a,b)
with
a\neq b
. For each fixed nontrivial character

\rho\in X
, there are exactly
q-1
ordered pairs

(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
with
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}=\rho
. Likewise, for each fixed

t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\setminus\{1\}
, there are exactly
q-1
ordered pairs

(a,b)
with
b/a=t
. We call a pair

(\rho,t)\in\bigl(X\setminus\{1\}\bigr)\times\bigl(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\setminus\{1\}\bigr)

exceptional if
1+\rho(t)
is divisible by
\ell
. It remains to count the number of exceptional pairs.
Let
d
be the order of
\rho
. Then
d>1
and

d\mid(q-1)
. The image of
\rho
is the cyclic group of
d
-th roots
of unity, and every value in the image has exactly
\frac{q-1}{d}
preimages in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

First consider the condition
\rho(t)=-1
. This has solutions if and
only if
-1
belongs to the image of
\rho
, equivalently if
d
is
even. The number of solutions is exactly
\frac{q-1}{d}
. If
d
is odd, there are no solutions. In either case, the number of
solutions to
\rho(t)=-1
is at most
\frac{q-1}{d}
. Next consider the condition
\rho(t)=1
, which only matters when

\ell=2
. The kernel of
\rho
has size
\frac{q-1}{d}
. Therefore, for a fixed character
\rho
of order
d
, the number of
exceptional
t
’s is at most
2\left(\frac{q-1}{d}\right)
.

There are
\varphi(d)
characters
\rho
of order
d
in

X
. Hence the number of exceptional pairs

(\rho,t)
is at most

2(q-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid q-1\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}.

Since
\varphi(d)/d\leq 1
, this is bounded by

2(q-1)\tau(q-1),

where
\tau(n)
denotes the number of positive divisors of
n
. It is a standard fact (see for example
[
11
, p. 10]
) that
\tau(n)=O_{\epsilon}(n^{\epsilon})
. In particular,

\tau(q-1)=O_{\epsilon}(q^{\epsilon}).

Now we count the number of quadruples
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},a,b)
such that
\ell
divides
1+\rho(t)
, where
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}
and
t\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=b/a
. By earlier remarks, each
(\rho,t)

arises from
(q-1)^{2}
ordered quadruples

(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},a,b)
. Hence their count is at most

2(q-1)^{3}\tau(q-1)=O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon}).

∎

We next analyze the second large nonzero block, namely the cuspidal
representations evaluated on elliptic conjugacy classes. The character
values in this block are controlled by expressions of the form

-\theta(\alpha)-\theta(\alpha^{q}).

As in the principal-series case, divisibility by
\ell
reduces to a
root-of-unity condition. The relevant cyclic group is now

E^{\times}/\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, which has order
q+1
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Then the total number of pairs
(\theta,\alpha)
where
\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a regular character and
\alpha\in E\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, such that
\ell
divides

\theta(\alpha)+\theta(\alpha^{q})

is at most
O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon})
.

Proof.

We write

\theta(\alpha)+\theta(\alpha^{q})=\theta(\alpha)\left(1+\frac{\theta(\alpha^{q})}{\theta(\alpha)}\right).

Setting
\delta_{\theta}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\theta^{q-1}
, we have that

\theta(\alpha)+\theta(\alpha^{q})=\theta(\alpha)\bigl(1+\delta_{\theta}(\alpha)\bigr).

The element
\theta(\alpha)
is a unit in the ring of algebraic integers. Thus divisibility by
\ell
is equivalent to divisibility of

1+\delta_{\theta}(\alpha)
by
\ell
. The character
\delta_{\theta}
is trivial on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and therefore factors through the quotient
Q\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=E^{\times}/\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. This quotient is cyclic of order

\frac{|E^{\times}|}{|\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}|}=\frac{q^{2}-1}{q-1}=q+1.

Since
\theta
is regular,
\delta_{\theta}\neq 1
. Consider the map
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\widehat{E^{\times}}\rightarrow\widehat{Q}
which is defined by

F(\theta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\delta_{\theta}.

The kernel of
F
consists exactly
of the Frobenius-invariant characters, i.e., the characters
\theta
satisfying

\theta^{q}=\theta
: there are
q-1
such elements. Since
E^{\times}
has
q^{2}-1
characters,
the image of
F
has size
q+1
. This is precisely the number of characters of
the quotient
Q=E^{\times}/\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Thus every character of
Q

occurs as
\delta_{\theta}
, and each occurs for exactly
q-1
choices of

\theta
.

A cuspidal representation is determined by the
Frobenius orbit
\{\theta,\theta^{q}\}
of a regular character
\theta
, and an
elliptic conjugacy class is determined by the Frobenius orbit

\{\alpha,\alpha^{q}\}
, with

\alpha\in E^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Both orbits have size
2
.
Hence, if we count ordered pairs
(\theta,\alpha)
, then each entry
in the cuspidal/elliptic block is counted exactly four times.

We now count the pairs
(\theta,\alpha)
such that
1+\delta_{\theta}(\alpha)
is divisible by
\ell
. Since
\delta_{\theta}
is trivial on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, the value

\delta_{\theta}(\alpha)
depends only on the coset

u=\alpha\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\in Q
. The condition

\alpha\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
is equivalent to
u\neq 1
. Therefore the
ordered data relevant to the divisibility question reduce to pairs

(\delta,u)\in\bigl(\widehat{Q}\setminus\{1\}\bigr)\times\bigl(Q\setminus\{1\}\bigr).

We call such a pair exceptional if
1+\delta(u)
is divisible by

\ell
. By Lemma
2.2
, this can happen only when:

•

\delta(u)=-1
, or,

•

\ell=2
and

\delta(u)=1
.

Let
d
be the order of the nontrivial character
\delta
. Then

d\mid q+1
. Since
Q
is cyclic of order
q+1
, every value in the
image of
\delta
has exactly
(q+1)/d
preimages in
Q
. Thus

\delta(u)=-1
has no solutions unless
d
is even, and in all cases it
has at most
(q+1)/d
solutions. Similarly, the equation

\delta(u)=1
, after imposing
u\neq 1
, has at most
(q+1)/d

solutions. Hence, for a fixed
\delta
of order
d
, there are at most

2(q+1)/d
exceptional choices of
u
.

For every
d|q+1
, there are
\varphi(d)
characters of order
d
in
\widehat{Q}
.
The total number of exceptional pairs
(\delta,u)
is at most

2(q+1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid q+1\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\leq 2(q+1)\tau(q+1).

Each pair
(\delta,u)
lifts to exactly
(q-1)^{2}
ordered pairs

(\theta,\alpha)
: there are
q-1
choices of
\theta
with

\delta_{\theta}=\delta
, and
q-1
elements of
E^{\times}
lying above
the coset
u
. Hence the number of exceptional ordered pairs

(\theta,\alpha)
is at most

2(q-1)^{2}(q+1)\tau(q+1)=O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon}).

∎

We now assemble the estimates from the two large nonzero blocks together
with the vanishing of the two large off-diagonal blocks. The principal
series characters vanish on elliptic classes, while the cuspidal characters
vanish on split regular semisimple classes. These two zero blocks give the
main term for
M_{0}(q)
. On the other hand, Lemmas

4.1
and
4.2
show that almost all
entries in the two large nonzero blocks are not divisible by
\ell
, and
therefore give the main term for
N_{\ell}(q)
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\ell
be a prime number and
\epsilon>0
. Then,

N_{\ell}(q)=\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O\left(q^{3+\epsilon}\right)

and

M_{0}(q)=\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O\left(q^{3+\epsilon}\right).

Proof.

We first prove the estimate for
N_{\ell}(q)
. From Tables
1
and
3
, one can see that the one-dimensional and Steinberg families together contain
2(q-1)
representations. Since there are
q^{2}-1
conjugacy classes, the total number of entries in rows
1
and
3
of Table
1
is
O(q^{3})
. Similarly, the scalar and Jordan classes together give
2(q-1)
columns and thus all entries in columns
1
and
4
together have density zero. Thus only the principal series and cuspidal rows evaluated on split regular semisimple and elliptic classes can affect the main term, whereas all other entries in Table
1
contribute to the error term.

By Lemma
3.2
, principal series characters vanish on elliptic classes, and therefore, this block does not contribute to
N_{\ell}(q)
. By Lemma
3.4
, cuspidal characters vanish on split regular semisimple classes, hence this block also does not contribute to
N_{\ell}(q)
. It remains to analyze principal series characters on split regular semisimple classes and cuspidal characters on elliptic classes.

First, we analyze the character values in principal-series rows on the split regular semisimple
columns. Let
\pi=\operatorname{Ind}_{B}^{G_{q}}(\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2})
where
\chi_{1}\neq\chi_{2}

and let
g\sim\operatorname{diag}(a,b)
with
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
a\neq b
. By Lemma
3.2
, the relevant character value is

\chi_{\pi}(g)=\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(b)+\chi_{1}(b)\chi_{2}(a).

It follows from Lemma
4.1
that the total number of pairs
(\pi,g)
such that
\ell
divides
\chi_{\pi}(g)
is at most
O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon})
.

We now treat the cuspidal rows on the elliptic columns. Let
E=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and let

\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}

be a regular character. Let
\pi_{\theta}
be the corresponding cuspidal
representation, as explained in section
2.2
. Let
\chi_{\theta}
be the character of
\pi_{\theta}
. If
g\sim\iota(\alpha)
with
\alpha\in E^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
,
then Lemma
3.4
gives

\chi_{\theta}(g)=-\left(\theta(\alpha)+\theta(\alpha^{q})\right).

According to Lemma
4.2
, there are at most
O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon})
pairs
(\theta,\alpha)
for which
\ell
divides
\chi_{\theta}(g)
.

Combining the preceding estimates, we have that

N_{\ell}(q)=\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon}).

The main contribution to
M_{0}(q)
comes from the two large blocks which
vanish identically. Namely, principal-series characters vanish on elliptic
classes, and cuspidal characters vanish on split regular semisimple classes.
Thus these two blocks contribute

2\cdot\frac{(q-1)(q-2)}{2}\cdot\frac{q(q-1)}{2}=\frac{q(q-1)^{2}(q-2)}{2}=\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O(q^{3})

zero entries.

It remains to check that the number of entries outside of these blocks that vanish is
O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon})
. The small rows and columns contribute
O(q^{3})
entries in total.
In the principal-series/split and cuspidal/elliptic blocks, a zero entry is
in particular divisible by
\ell
, so Lemmas
4.1
and

4.2
show that the number of zero entries in these blocks is

O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon})
. Hence, we find that

M_{0}(q)=\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon}).

This completes the proof.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Fix
0<\epsilon<1
. By the preceding theorem, we have

N_{\ell}(q)=\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon})

as
q\to\infty
through odd prime powers. Since

(q^{2}-1)^{2}=q^{4}-2q^{2}+1=q^{4}+O(q^{2}),

it follows that

\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}(q)=\frac{N_{\ell}(q)}{(q^{2}-1)^{2}}=\frac{\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon})}{q^{4}+O(q^{2})}.

Dividing numerator and denominator by
q^{4}
, and using

q^{3+\epsilon}=o(q^{4})
, we obtain

\lim_{q\to\infty}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}(q)=\frac{1}{2}.

It remains to compute the limiting proportion among the nonzero entries.
By the same preceding theorem,

M_{0}(q)=\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon}).

Hence

M_{0}^{\prime}(q)=(q^{2}-1)^{2}-M_{0}(q)=\left(q^{4}+O(q^{2})\right)-\left(\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon})\right),

and therefore

M_{0}^{\prime}(q)=\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon}).

Consequently

\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}(q)=\frac{N_{\ell}(q)}{M_{0}^{\prime}(q)}=\frac{\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon})}{\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon})}.

Again dividing by
q^{4}
, we get

\lim_{q\to\infty}\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}(q)=1.

Finally, since the proportion of entries not divisible by
\ell
tends to

1/2
, the proportion of entries divisible by
\ell
is

1-\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}(q),

and hence also tends to
1/2
. This proves the theorem.
∎

5.
Angular distribution for nonzero character values

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. The proof will make use of the following variant of Weyl’s criterion (see
[
7
, Chapter 1, Theorem 2.1]
).

Proposition 5.1
.

For every odd prime power
q
, let
A_{q}
be a finite multiset of real numbers in
[0,2\pi]
. Suppose that for every nonzero integer
n
, one has

(5.1)

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{|A_{q}|}\sum_{\theta\in A_{q}}e^{in\theta}\right)=0

as
q\to\infty
. Then for any closed interval
I=[a,b]\subseteq[0,2\pi]
we have that

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{\theta\in A_{q}\mid\theta\in I\}}{|A_{q}|}=\int_{I}d\theta=\frac{b-a}{2\pi}.

Proof.

We note that the statement of the result does not follow directly from Weyl’s criterion, but the proof is similar. For
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
let
\delta_{\theta}
be the Dirac measure supported at
\theta
. Let
\nu_{q}
be the probability measure on
[0,2\pi]
defined by

\nu_{q}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{|A_{q}|}\sum_{\theta\in A_{q}}\delta_{\theta},

where multiplicities are included. Given a set
J\subseteq[0,2\pi]
, we have that

\nu_{q}(J)=\frac{|A_{q}\cap J|}{|A_{q}|}.

Let
m
denote normalized Lebesgue
measure on
[0,2\pi]
, so that

dm(\theta)=\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}.

By (
5.1
),

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{in\theta}\,d\nu_{q}(\theta)\right)=0

for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Since also

\int_{0}^{2\pi}1\,d\nu_{q}(\theta)=1=\int_{0}^{2\pi}1\,dm(\theta),

this is equivalent to saying that

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{in\theta}\,d\nu_{q}(\theta)\right)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{in\theta}\,dm(\theta)

for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

By linearity, the same convergence holds for every trigonometric
polynomial. We now pass from trigonometric polynomials to arbitrary
continuous functions on the circle by uniform approximation. Let
f
be a
continuous function on
[0,2\pi]
. By the Stone–Weierstrass theorem,
for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a trigonometric polynomial
P
such
that

\|f-P\|_{\infty}<\epsilon.

Since
\nu_{q}
and
m
are probability measures, we have

\left|\int_{0}^{2\pi}(f-P)(\theta)\,d\nu_{q}(\theta)\right|<\epsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\int_{0}^{2\pi}(f-P)(\theta)\,dm(\theta)\right|<\epsilon.

Therefore

\left|\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\theta)\,d\nu_{q}(\theta)-\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\theta)\,dm(\theta)\right|<2\epsilon+\left|\int_{0}^{2\pi}P(\theta)\,d\nu_{q}(\theta)-\int_{0}^{2\pi}P(\theta)\,dm(\theta)\right|.

The last term tends to
0
as
q\to\infty
, since
P
is a
trigonometric polynomial. Hence

\limsup_{q\to\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\theta)\,d\nu_{q}(\theta)-\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\theta)\,dm(\theta)\right|<2\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, it follows that

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\theta)\,d\nu_{q}(\theta)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\theta)\,dm(\theta)

for every continuous function
f
on
[0,2\pi]
. Thus
\nu_{q}

converges weakly to normalized Lebesgue measure on the circle.

Recall that a subset
J
is called an
m
-continuity set if its boundary has Lebesgue measure
0
. It follows from the Portmanteau theorem (see
[
1
, Theorem 2.1, (i) implies (v)]
) that

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{q}(J)=m(J).

Since
I
is an
m
-continuity set, we deduce that

\lim_{q\to\infty}\nu_{q}(I)=m(I)=\frac{b-a}{2\pi}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Let
q
be an odd prime and
\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q})
be the set of isomorphism classes of complex irreducible representations of
G_{q}
. The group of characters
X_{q}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}
acts on
\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q})
by twisting:

\eta(\pi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi\otimes(\eta\circ\det),

where
\eta\in X_{q}
and
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q})
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
E=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, let
\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a regular
character and
\pi_{\theta}
denote the associated cuspidal representation. Let
\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a character. Then

\pi_{\theta}\otimes(\eta\circ\det)\simeq\pi_{\theta\cdot(\eta\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}})}.

Proof.

We first recall a general elementary fact about induction. Let

H\leq G_{q}
, let
\sigma
be a representation of
H
, and let

\lambda
be a one-dimensional representation of
G_{q}
. Then

\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G_{q}}(\sigma)\otimes\lambda\simeq\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G_{q}}\bigl(\sigma\otimes\lambda|_{H}\bigr),

see
[
4
, Exercise 3.16, p. 34]
. We apply this with
\lambda=\eta\circ\det
. Recall that the cuspidal
representation attached to
\theta
is constructed as the virtual
representation

\pi_{\theta}=\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G_{q}}\varphi_{\theta}-\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G_{q}}\theta,

where

\varphi_{\theta}(aIu_{x})=\theta(a)\psi(x),\qquad a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times},\ x\in\mathbb{F}_{q},\quad\text{ and }\quad u_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

We claim that twisting both induced terms by
\eta\circ\det
replaces

\theta
by

\theta^{\prime}=\theta\cdot(\eta\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}}).

First consider the
E^{\times}
-term. For
\alpha\in E^{\times}
, viewed
as an element of
G_{q}
via the embedding
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{\times}\hookrightarrow G_{q}
, one has

\det(\iota(\alpha))=N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha).

Therefore

(\eta\circ\det)|_{E^{\times}}=\eta\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}}.

By the induction-tensor compatibility recalled above,

\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G_{q}}\theta\otimes(\eta\circ\det)\simeq\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G_{q}}\bigl(\theta\cdot(\eta\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}})\bigr)=\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G_{q}}\theta^{\prime}.

Next consider the
ZN
-term. For
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
u_{x}\in N
,
we have

\det(aIu_{x})=a^{2}.

On the other hand, regarding
a
as an element of

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\subset E^{\times}
, one has

N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(a)=a^{q+1}=a^{2},

since
a^{q}=a
. Thus

(\eta\circ\det)(aIu_{x})=\eta(a^{2})=(\eta\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}})(a).

Hence

\varphi_{\theta}(aIu_{x})\,(\eta\circ\det)(aIu_{x})=\theta(a)\psi(x)\eta(a^{2})=\theta^{\prime}(a)\psi(x).

This is precisely the character
\varphi_{\theta^{\prime}}
of
ZN
. Therefore

\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G_{q}}\varphi_{\theta}\otimes(\eta\circ\det)\simeq\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G_{q}}\varphi_{\theta^{\prime}}.

Combining the two terms in the virtual construction of
\pi_{\theta}
, we
obtain

\pi_{\theta}\otimes(\eta\circ\det)\simeq\operatorname{Ind}_{ZN}^{G_{q}}\varphi_{\theta^{\prime}}-\operatorname{Ind}_{E^{\times}}^{G_{q}}\theta^{\prime}=\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}.

Since
\theta^{\prime}=\theta\cdot(\eta\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}})
, this proves the lemma.
∎

Denote by
\operatorname{Stab}(\pi)
the stabilizer of
\pi
, consisting of
\eta\in X_{q}
such that
\eta(\pi)\simeq\pi
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{q}\subset\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q})
be the set of the one-dimensional representations,
the Steinberg twists, and those principal-series or cuspidal representations
\pi
for which
\operatorname{Stab}(\pi)
is nontrivial. Then we have that

|\mathcal{B}_{q}|=O(q).

Proof.

The one-dimensional representations and the Steinberg twists together contribute

2(q-1)
rows. We now count the remaining rows with nontrivial stabilizer.

Let
\pi=\operatorname{Ind}_{B}^{G_{q}}(\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2})
with
\chi_{1}\neq\chi_{2}
be a principal-series representation. Suppose that
\pi
has nontrivial
stabilizer under twisting. Then there exists a nontrivial character

\eta\in X_{q}
such that

\operatorname{Ind}_{B}^{G_{q}}(\chi_{1}\eta\otimes\chi_{2}\eta)\simeq\operatorname{Ind}_{B}^{G_{q}}(\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}).

Using the standard equivalence relation for principal series, this implies

\{\chi_{1}\eta,\chi_{2}\eta\}=\{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\}.

Since
\eta\neq 1
, the equality cannot hold with

\chi_{1}\eta=\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}\eta=\chi_{2}
. Therefore one must have
\chi_{1}\eta=\chi_{2}

and
\chi_{2}\eta=\chi_{1}
. It follows that
\eta^{2}=1
. Since
q
is odd, there is a unique
nontrivial quadratic character
\eta_{2}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Thus the principal-series
rows with nontrivial stabilizer are precisely of the form
\operatorname{Ind}_{B}^{G_{q}}(\chi\otimes\chi\eta_{2})

where
\eta_{2}
is the quadratic character. There are at most
q-1
such representations.

We next consider the cuspidal representation with nontrivial stabilizer. Let
E=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, and let

\pi_{\theta}
be the cuspidal representation attached to a regular
character
\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{\times}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Assume that there exists a nontrivial character
\eta\in X_{q}
such that
\eta(\pi_{\theta})\simeq\pi_{\theta}
. By Lemma
5.2
we have that:

\pi_{\theta}\otimes(\eta\circ\det)\simeq\pi_{\theta\cdot(\eta\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}})},

and therefore,

\pi_{\theta\cdot(\eta\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}})}\simeq\pi_{\theta}.

This means that

\theta\cdot(\eta\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}})=\theta\quad\text{or}\quad\theta\cdot(\eta\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}})=\theta^{q}.

The first equality gives
\eta\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}}=1
. Since the norm map

N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{\times}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
is surjective, this implies

\eta=1
, contrary to our assumption. Thus the second equality must hold:

\eta\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}}=\theta^{q}\theta^{-1}.

Restricting this equality to
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\subset E^{\times}
, we obtain

\eta(a^{2})=1\qquad\text{for all }a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times},

because
\theta^{q}(a)=\theta(a)
for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. It follows that
\eta^{2}=1
, and therefore
\eta
must be the unique quadratic character
\eta_{2}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

It remains to count the possible
\theta
satisfying
\theta^{q}\theta^{-1}=\eta_{2}\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. Consider the homomorphism

\widehat{E^{\times}}\longrightarrow\widehat{E^{\times}},\qquad\theta\longmapsto\theta^{q-1}.

Let
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
be characters of
E^{\times}
such that

\theta_{1}^{q-1}=\theta_{2}^{q-1}=\eta_{2}\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}}.

Then
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\theta_{1}\theta_{2}^{-1}
is in the kernel of the above homomorphism. This kernel consists exactly of the Frobenius-invariant characters and has size
q-1
.
Suppose that
\theta_{1}
is a solution to

\theta_{1}^{q-1}=\eta_{2}\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, then any other solution is of the form
\theta_{1}\alpha
. Since there are at most
q-1
choices for
\alpha
there are consequently at most
q-1
choices for
\theta

satisfying

\theta^{q}\theta^{-1}=\eta_{2}\circ N_{E/\mathbb{F}_{q}}.

Passing from regular characters
\theta
to cuspidal representations

\pi_{\theta}
can only decrease this number. Hence there are at most
O(q)
cuspidal representations with
nontrivial stabilizer. This proves that

|\mathcal{B}_{q}|=O(q).

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\mathscr{C}_{q}
denote the set of conjugacy classes of
G_{q}
. For a complex number
z
, write

u(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}e^{i\operatorname{arg}(z)}&\text{ if }z\neq 0;\\
0&\text{ if }z=0.\\
\end{cases}

Let
A_{q}
be the multiset of arguments

A_{q}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\arg(\chi_{\pi}(C))\mid\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q}),\ C\in\mathscr{C}_{q},\ \chi_{\pi}(C)\neq 0\right\},

where each pair
(\pi,C)
with
\chi_{\pi}(C)\neq 0
is counted once. Thus

|A_{q}|=M_{0}^{\prime}(q),

the number of nonzero entries in the character table of
G_{q}
. By Theorem
4.3
,

M_{0}^{\prime}(q)=\frac{q^{4}}{2}+O_{\epsilon}(q^{3+\epsilon}).

By Proposition
5.1
, it is enough to show that for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,

(5.2)

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{M_{0}^{\prime}(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q})\\
C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}\end{subarray}}u(\chi_{\pi}(C))^{n}\right)=0.

Fixing
n
, we prove the stronger estimate

(5.3)

S_{q}(n)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q})\\
C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}\end{subarray}}u(\chi_{\pi}(C))^{n}\right|\leq C_{0}|n|q^{3},

where
C_{0}>0
is an absolute constant.
Since
M_{0}^{\prime}(q)\sim q^{4}/2
and
n
is fixed, the limit (
5.2
) must vanish.

If
C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}
, then
\det(g)
is independent of the choice of

g\in C
; we denote this common value by
\det C
. For every

\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q})
, every
\eta\in X_{q}
, and every
C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}
,
one has

\chi_{\pi\otimes(\eta\circ\det)}(C)=\eta(\det C)\chi_{\pi}(C),

and therefore,

(5.4)

u\left(\chi_{\pi\otimes(\eta\circ\det)}(C)\right)^{n}=\eta(\det C)^{n}u(\chi_{\pi}(C))^{n}.

We recall from the statement of Lemma
5.3
that
\mathcal{B}_{q}
consists of one-dimensional representations,
the Steinberg twists, and those principal-series or cuspidal representations
\pi
for which
\operatorname{Stab}(\pi)
is nontrivial. Since each term

u(\chi_{\pi}(C))^{n}
has absolute value
\leq 1
, we have that

\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\mathcal{B}_{q}\\
C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}\end{subarray}}u(\chi_{\pi}(C))^{n}\right|\leq|\mathcal{B}_{q}||\mathscr{C}_{q}|.

Note that
|\mathscr{C}_{q}|=q^{2}-1
and by Lemma
5.3
,
|\mathcal{B}_{q}|=O(q)
. Therefore, we find that

|\mathcal{B}_{q}||\mathscr{C}_{q}|=O(q^{3}).

Therefore, (
5.3
) follows if we prove that

(5.5)

\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\mathcal{\operatorname{Irr}}(G_{q})\setminus\mathcal{B}_{q}\\
C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}\end{subarray}}u(\chi_{\pi}(C))^{n}\right|\leq C_{1}|n|q^{3}

for some absolute constant
C_{1}>0
.

Since representations outside
\mathcal{B}_{q}
have trivial stabilizer,
\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q})\setminus\mathcal{B}_{q}
decomposes into free
X_{q}
-orbits
\operatorname{Orb}_{q}=\operatorname{Orb}_{X_{q}}\left(\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q})\setminus\mathcal{B}_{q}\right)
. For each orbit
\mathcal{O}
, fix a representation
\pi_{\mathcal{O}}\in\mathcal{O}
. From (
5.4
), we deduce that

(5.6)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\mathcal{\operatorname{Irr}}(G_{q})\setminus\mathcal{B}_{q}\\
C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}\end{subarray}}u(\chi_{\pi}(C))^{n}=\sum_{\mathcal{O}\in\operatorname{Orb}_{q}}\sum_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}}\left(\sum_{\eta\in X_{q}}\eta(\det C)^{n}\right)u(\chi_{\pi_{\mathcal{O}}}(C))^{n}.

We note that
|X_{q}|=q-1
. By the orthogonality of characters of the finite abelian group

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
,

(5.7)

\sum_{\eta\in X_{q}}\eta(\det C)^{n}=\begin{cases}q-1,&(\det C)^{n}=1,\\
0,&(\det C)^{n}\neq 1.\end{cases}

Consequently, a free orbit can contribute in the column
C
only if
(\det C)^{n}=1
.
Let
\mu_{|n|}
be the
|n|
-th roots of unity in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Observe that

|\operatorname{Orb}_{q}|\leq\frac{|\operatorname{Irr}(G_{q})|}{|X_{q}|}=\frac{q^{2}-1}{q-1}=q+1.

From (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), we find that

\begin{split}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\mathcal{\operatorname{Irr}}(G_{q})\setminus\mathcal{B}_{q}\\
C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}\end{subarray}}u(\chi_{\pi}(C))^{n}\right|\leq&(q-1)\sum_{\xi\in\mu_{|n|}}\sum_{\mathcal{O}\in\operatorname{Orb}_{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}\\
\det C=\xi\end{subarray}}|u(\chi_{\pi_{\mathcal{O}}}(C))|^{n}\\
\leq&(q-1)\sum_{\xi\in\mu_{|n|}}\sum_{\mathcal{O}\in\operatorname{Orb}_{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}\\
\det C=\xi\end{subarray}}1\\
=&(q-1)|\operatorname{Orb}_{q}|\sum_{\xi\in\mu_{|n|}}\#\{C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}\mid\det C=\xi\}\\
\leq&(q^{2}-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in\mu_{|n|}\\
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\end{subarray}}\#\{C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}\mid\operatorname{trace}C=t,\quad\det C=\xi\}\\
\leq&|n|q(q^{2}-1)\operatorname{max}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in\mu_{|n|}\\
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\end{subarray}}\#\{C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}\mid\operatorname{trace}C=t,\quad\det C=\xi\}.\end{split}

For fixed
d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we claim that

\#\{C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}\mid\operatorname{trace}C=t,\quad\det C=d\}\leq 2.

The characteristic polynomial

\operatorname{ch}_{C}(X)=X^{2}-tX+d

has a repeated root whenever
t^{2}-4d=0
. First consider the case when
\operatorname{ch}_{C}(X)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a root. Then
\alpha
and
\alpha^{q}
are the roots of
\operatorname{ch}_{C}(X)
and
C
is conjugacy class of either
\iota(\alpha)
or
\iota(\alpha^{q})
. Thus there are two choices for
C
. Next, assume that
\operatorname{ch}_{C}(X)
splits in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
be its roots up to multiplicity. If
a\neq b
then
C
is the conjugacy class of
\operatorname{diag}(a,b)
. On the other hand, if
a=b
then
C
is the conjugacy class of
\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}
or
\begin{pmatrix}a&1\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}
. Thus in either case, there are at most
2
choices for
C
. This proves the claim.

Therefore, we have shown that

\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\mathcal{\operatorname{Irr}}(G_{q})\setminus\mathcal{B}_{q}\\
C\in\mathscr{C}_{q}\end{subarray}}u(\chi_{\pi}(C))^{n}\right|\leq 2|n|q^{3},

thus proving (
5.5
) and the estimate (
5.3
). This completes the proof of the Theorem.
∎
