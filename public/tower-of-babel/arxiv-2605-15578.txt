Abstract
. In this paper, we study λ \lambda -biharmonic Riemannian submersions, which generalize biharmonic Riemannian submersions. We prove non-existence results for λ \lambda -biharmonic Riemannian submersions from ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -dimensional Riemannian manifolds with constant sectional curvature c c to n n -dimensional Riemannian manifolds. Our results show that the critical value λ = 2 ​ ( n − 1 ) ​ c \lambda=2(n-1)c plays a decisive role. When λ ≠ 2 ​ ( n − 1 ) ​ c \lambda\neq 2(n-1)c , we prove a non-existence theorem, although a dimensional assumption is needed in the positive curvature case. On the other hand, when λ = 2 ​ ( n − 1 ) ​ c \lambda=2(n-1)c , we prove a non-existence theorem in the nonnegative curvature case, whereas in the negative curvature case, we construct explicit examples. The only remaining local case is the positively curved case with λ ≠ 2 ​ ( n − 1 ) ​ c \lambda\neq 2(n-1)c and n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 , while in the complete connected positive-curvature setting the theorem of Gromoll and Grove yields harmonicity in all dimensions.

Introduction

A harmonic map is defined as a critical point of the energy functional

E(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|d\phi|^{2}d\mu_{g},

where
\phi:(M,g)\to(N,h)
is a smooth map, and
d\mu_{g}
is the volume element on
M
.
Harmonic maps are powerful tools for studying the geometric structures of manifolds.
In particular,
\phi
is harmonic if and only if its tension field

\tau(\phi)=\operatorname{tr}_{g}(\nabla d\phi)

vanishes identically on
M

[
13
,
14
]
.

However, for maps from a torus to a sphere, a harmonic map may fail to exist depending on the geometric conditions
[
12
]
.
In such failure cases, the best map is the one that minimizes the degree of failure.
The quantity that measures this failure is the integral of the squared norm of the tension field,
known as the bienergy.

Eells and Lemaire
[
13
]
introduced the bienergy functional in 1983, which is defined by

E_{2}(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\tau(\phi)|^{2}d\mu_{g}.

This naturally leads to the problem of minimizing the bienergy.
There are two main approaches to this optimization problem.

(1)

Unconstrained approach:
Seeking critical points of the bienergy
E_{2}
alone,
neglecting any constraints on the energy functional (the classical study of biharmonic maps).

(2)

Constrained approach:
Imposing a constraint on the energy
E(\phi)
to prevent it from becoming excessively large (the focus of the present study).

Following the derivation of the first variation formula by Jiang
[
22
]
in 1986,
the study of biharmonic maps,
defined as the critical points of the bienergy functional,
has advanced significantly.
According to this first variation formula, a map is biharmonic if it satisfies the following fourth-order nonlinear partial differential equation

(1.1)

\tau_{2}(\phi)=\left(\nabla^{\phi}_{e_{i}}\nabla^{\phi}_{e_{i}}-\nabla^{\phi}_{\nabla^{M}_{e_{i}}e_{i}}\right)\tau(\phi)-R^{N}\bigl(d\phi(e_{i}),\tau(\phi)\bigr)d\phi(e_{i})=0.

Here,
\nabla^{\phi}
denotes the induced connection, and
R^{N}
denotes the Riemannian curvature tensor of
N
, defined by

R^{N}(X,Y)Z=\nabla^{N}_{X}\nabla^{N}_{Y}Z-\nabla^{N}_{Y}\nabla^{N}_{X}Z-\nabla^{N}_{[X,Y]}Z,

for vector fields
X,Y,Z
on
N
, and
\{e_{i}\}
is a local orthonormal frame field of
M
.

Since harmonic maps (
\tau(\phi)=0
) always satisfy the biharmonic equation,
biharmonic maps naturally include harmonic ones as trivial solutions.
Consequently, as represented by Chen’s conjecture
[
7
]
and the BMO conjecture
[
2
]
,
much of the research has focused on the existence of proper biharmonic maps
(i.e., biharmonic maps that are not harmonic)

[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
10
,
17

,

,
33
,
32
]
.
In our previous work
[
27
]
, we also classified biharmonic Riemannian submersions.

On the other hand, in 2008,
Loubeau and Montaldo
[
23
]
introduced the concept of biminimal immersions
by considering a constrained variational problem using a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
.
In this paper, we adapt this idea to the setting of Riemannian submersions.

A Riemannian submersion is a fundamental tool
for understanding the structure of a manifold
by decomposing it into a lower-dimensional base space and fibers
[
30
]
.
While immersions focus on the geometry of the image submanifold,
submersions emphasize the geometry of the fibers.

Indeed, while the study of isometric immersions has been a fruitful and primary focus in the literature on biharmonic maps,
the dual case of biharmonic Riemannian submersions has remained relatively underexplored.
Nevertheless, this area has been actively investigated in recent years by several researchers, including Wang and Ou, among others

[

In this paper, we define a
\lambda
-biharmonic Riemannian submersion as follows.

Definition 1.1

(
[
11
]
,
[
23
]
)
.

Let
\phi:(M^{m},g)\to(N^{n},h)
be a Riemannian submersion between Riemannian manifolds.
If there exists a real constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi
is a critical point of the
\lambda
-bienergy

E_{2,\lambda}(\phi):=E_{2}(\phi)+\lambda E(\phi),

then
\phi
is called a
\lambda
-biharmonic Riemannian submersion
.
In this case, the critical points of the
\lambda
-bienergy satisfy

\tau_{2,\lambda}(\phi):=\tau_{2}(\phi)-\lambda\,\tau(\phi)=0,

which follows from the Euler–Lagrange equations for harmonic and biharmonic maps.

Remark 1.2
.

When
\lambda=0
, the
\lambda
-bienergy functional reduces to the classical bienergy
E_{2}(\phi)
.
Accordingly, a
0
-biharmonic Riemannian submersion coincides with the usual biharmonic Riemannian submersion.
Thus, the notion of a
\lambda
-biharmonic Riemannian submersion naturally generalizes the classical theory,
encompassing the case
\lambda=0
and unifying the constrained and unconstrained approaches.

Definition 1.3
.

A
\lambda
-biharmonic map
\phi
is called
proper
if it is not harmonic,
that is,

\tau_{2,\lambda}(\phi)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\tau(\phi)\neq 0.

The results of this paper include a detailed classification of

\lambda
-biharmonic Riemannian submersions from manifolds with constant sectional curvature
c
,
based on the relationship between
\lambda
and
c
.
These results are presented as a non-existence theorem and an explicit construction of a proper
\lambda
-biharmonic submersion.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall basic definitions and notations used throughout the paper.

In 2011, Wang and Ou
[
34
]
introduced the integrability data

\{f_{1},f_{2},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\sigma\}

to analyze the structure of Riemannian submersions from 3-dimensional manifolds.
This approach provides a powerful framework for analyzing the biharmonic equation
originally derived by Jiang
[
22
]
(see (
1.1
)),
enabling us to reduce the equation to a system of scalar functions on the manifold.

Later, in 2019, Akyol and Ou
[
1
]
generalized this integrability data to the form
\{f_{ij}^{k},\kappa_{i},\sigma_{ij}\}
for arbitrary dimensions.
The specific settings and definitions are as follows.

Let
\phi:(M^{n+1},g)\to(N^{n},h)
be a Riemannian submersion.
A local orthonormal frame adapted to
\phi
consists of horizontal lifts of vector fields from the base manifold
N
, which locally span the horizontal distribution on
M
.
Since basic vector fields locally span the horizontal distribution, such a frame can always be constructed (cf. page 2 in
[
15
]
).

Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n},e_{n+1}\}
be an orthonormal frame adapted to the submersion
\phi
, where
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
are horizontal lifts of the orthonormal frame
\{\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}\}
on
N
, and
e_{n+1}
is vertical along the fiber.

The Lie bracket
[e_{i},e_{n+1}]
is vertical and does not contribute to the horizontal component
(Lemma 3,
[
30
]
).
Moreover, the bracket
[e_{i},e_{j}]
of horizontal lifts corresponds under
\phi
to the bracket
[\varepsilon_{i},\varepsilon_{j}]
on
N
, preserving the Lie bracket structure
(Lemma 1,
[
30
]
).

If we assume that

(2.1)

[\varepsilon_{i},\varepsilon_{j}]=F^{k}_{ij}\varepsilon_{k},

where, in the sequel,
F^{k}_{ij}\in C^{\infty}(N)
and the Einstein convention is used, then we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle[e_{i},e_{n+1}]=\kappa_{i}e_{n+1},

\displaystyle[e_{i},e_{j}]=f^{k}_{ij}e_{k}-2\sigma_{ij}e_{n+1},\quad i,j=1,2,\dots,n,

where
f^{k}_{ij}=F^{k}_{ij}\circ\phi
,
\kappa_{i}
, and
\sigma_{ij}\in C^{\infty}(M)
for all
i,j=1,2,\dots,n
. We will call
\{f^{k}_{ij},\kappa_{i},\sigma_{ij}\}
the integrability data of the adapted frame of the Riemannian submersion
\phi
. It follows from (
2.2
) that

(2.3)

f^{k}_{ij}=-f^{k}_{ji},\quad\sigma_{ij}=-\sigma_{ji},\quad(i,j,k=1,2,\dots,n).

Using the integrability data, the
\lambda
-biharmonic condition can be rewritten as follows.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\phi:(M^{n+1},g)\to(N^{n},h)
be a Riemannian submersion with the adapted frame
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n+1}\}
and the integrability data
\{f^{k}_{ij},\kappa_{i},\sigma_{ij}\}
.
Then, the Riemannian submersion
\phi
is
\lambda
-biharmonic if and only if

(2.4)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}e_{i}e_{i}(\kappa_{k})+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}P_{ii}^{j}e_{j}(\kappa_{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i}e_{i}(\kappa_{k})

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left[2e_{i}(\kappa_{j})P_{ij}^{k}+\kappa_{j}(e_{i}P_{ij}^{k})+\kappa_{j}P_{ij}^{l}P_{il}^{k}-\kappa_{i}\kappa_{j}P_{ij}^{k}-\kappa_{j}P_{ii}^{l}P_{lj}^{k}\right]

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Ricci}^{N}(d\phi(\mu),d\phi(e_{k}))+\kappa_{k}\lambda=0,\quad k=1,2,\dots,n,

where

P^{k}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-f^{j}_{ik}-f^{i}_{jk}+f^{k}_{ij}\right)\text{ for all }i,j,k=1,2,\dots,n,

and

\mu=(\nabla^{M}_{e_{n+1}}e_{n+1})^{\mathcal{H}}.

Proof.

By the definition of
\lambda
-biharmonic maps and the proof of Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 3.1 in

[
1
]
, we have

\displaystyle\tau_{2,\lambda}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\tau_{2}(\phi)-\lambda\tau(\phi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\Bigl\{\nabla^{\phi}_{e_{i}}\nabla^{\phi}_{e_{i}}\tau(\phi)-\nabla^{\phi}_{\nabla^{M}_{e_{i}}e_{i}}\tau(\phi)-R^{N}\bigl(d\phi(e_{i}),\tau(\phi)\bigr)d\phi(e_{i})\Bigr\}

\displaystyle-\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\Bigl\{\nabla^{\phi}_{e_{i}}d\phi(e_{i})-d\phi\bigl(\nabla^{M}_{e_{i}}e_{i}\bigr)\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}e_{i}e_{i}(\kappa_{k})+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}P_{ii}^{j}e_{j}(\kappa_{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i}e_{i}(\kappa_{k})

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left[2e_{i}(\kappa_{j})P_{ij}^{k}+\kappa_{j}(e_{i}P_{ij}^{k})+\kappa_{j}P_{ij}^{l}P_{il}^{k}-\kappa_{i}\kappa_{j}P_{ij}^{k}-\kappa_{j}P_{ii}^{l}P_{lj}^{k}\right]

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Ricci}^{N}(d\phi(\mu),d\phi(e_{k}))d\phi(e_{k}))+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\kappa_{k}\lambda d\phi(e_{k})=0.

Thus the proposition follows.
∎

This equation (
2.4
) is called the
\lambda
-biharmonic equation
.

In this paper, we use both
\sigma_{ij}
and
\sigma_{i,j}
to denote the same function defined on a manifold.
The comma is used in expressions like
\sigma_{i,j+1}
for clarity, especially when indices involve arithmetic operations.

Furthermore, the following proposition holds for general Riemannian submersions, which allows us to simplify the
\lambda
-biharmonic equation mentioned above.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

\phi:(M^{n+1}(c),g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)

be a
\lambda
-biharmonic Riemannian submersion with an adapted frame

\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n},e_{n+1}\}

and the integrability data

\{f_{ij}^{k},\kappa_{i},\sigma_{ij}\}_{i,j,k=1,\dots,n}.

Let
U
be a nonempty open set on which
\kappa:=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{n})^{\mathsf{T}}\neq 0
. Then there exists
a nonempty open subset
V\subset U
and an adapted orthonormal frame on

such that

\kappa_{2}=\cdots=\kappa_{n}=0

and

\sigma_{i,i+j}=0\qquad(i=1,\dots,n-2,\ j\geq 2).

Proof.

The proof is the same as that of Lemma 3.2 in our previous paper
[
27
]
.
∎

Proof of the Main Theorem

In this section, we prove the main theorem concerning
\lambda

By using the reduction provided by Proposition
2.2

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\phi:(M^{n+1}(c),g)\to(N^{n},h)
be a
\lambda
-biharmonic Riemannian submersion.
Assume that one of the following holds.

(1)

c\geq 0
,

(2)

c<0

\lambda\neq 2(n-1)c

Then, for any orthonormal frame

\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n},e_{n+1}\}

We first show that
e_{n+1}(f^{k}_{ij})=e_{n+1}(\sigma_{ij})=0

This part holds for an arbitrary Riemannian submersion and the argument is the same as in our previous work

Next, we show

From the definition of the integrability data and Koszul formula, we obtain the following relations:

\left\{\,\begin{aligned} &\nabla^{M}_{e_{i}}e_{j}=P^{k}_{ij}e_{k}-\sigma_{ij}e_{n+1}\quad\text{for any }i,j,k=1,2,\dots,n,\\

Using (
3.1

(3.2)

\left\{\,\begin{aligned} &-R^{M}_{a(n+1)cd}=e_{a}(\sigma_{cd})+P_{al}^{d}\sigma_{cl}-P_{ac}^{l}\sigma_{ld}-\kappa_{c}\sigma_{ad}+\kappa_{d}\sigma_{ac}-\kappa_{a}\sigma_{cd}=0,\\

\displaystyle-\sigma_{ad}e_{n+1}(\kappa_{c})+\sigma_{ac}e_{n+1}(\kappa_{d})-\sigma_{cd}e_{n+1}(\kappa_{a})=0\quad\text{for any }a,c,d=1,2,\dots,n.

Example 1
.

Let
H^{m}(c)
and
H^{n}(c)
be hyperbolic spaces with
m>n
, represented as

\displaystyle H^{m}(c)

\displaystyle=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},x_{n},\dots,x_{m-1},z)\mid z>0\},

\displaystyle H^{n}(c)

\displaystyle=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},z)\mid z>0\},

with the standard metrics

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{cz^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}dx_{k}^{2}+dz^{2}\right),

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{cz^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}dx_{i}^{2}+dz^{2}\right).

Then,
\phi:H^{m}(c)\to H^{n}(c)
,

\phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},x_{n},\dots,x_{m-1},z)=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},z)

is a proper
\lambda
-biharmonic Riemannian submersion with
\lambda=2(n-1)c
.

An orthonormal basis for
(H^{m}(c),g)
is given by

\displaystyle e_{i}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-c}z\partial_{x_{i}}\quad(1\leq i\leq n-1),

\displaystyle e_{n}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-c}z\partial_{z},

\displaystyle e_{n+j}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-c}z\partial_{x_{n-1+j}}\quad(1\leq j\leq m-n).

Consequently, the tension field
\tau(\phi)
and the bitension field
\tau_{2}(\phi)
are given by

\displaystyle\tau(\phi)

\displaystyle=-(m-n)\sqrt{-c}\,d\phi(e_{n})\neq 0,

\displaystyle\tau_{2}(\phi)

\displaystyle=2(-c)^{\frac{3}{2}}(n-1)(m-n)d\phi(e_{n}).

Now, consider the
\lambda
-bitension field defined by

\tau_{2,\lambda}(\phi)=\tau_{2}(\phi)-\lambda\tau(\phi)
.
By setting
\lambda=2(n-1)c
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tau_{2,\lambda}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\tau_{2}(\phi)-2(n-1)c\tau(\phi)

\displaystyle=2(-c)^{\frac{3}{2}}(n-1)(m-n)d\phi(e_{n})-2(n-1)c\{-(m-n)\sqrt{-c}d\phi(e_{n})\}

\displaystyle=0.

Thus,
\phi
is a proper
\lambda
-biharmonic Riemannian submersion with
\lambda=2(n-1)c
.

4.
\lambda
-biharmonic Riemannian submersions from complete Riemannian manifolds with constant sectional curvature

By Theorem
1.4
and the example above, the remaining problem is (b) in TABLE 1, that is,
c>0
and
\lambda\not=2(n-1)c
.
For the global case the problem is completely resolved by the classification theorem of Gromoll and Grove (cf.
[
19
]
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\phi:(M^{n+1}(c),g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)
be a Riemannian submersion from a complete connected Riemannian manifold of constant sectional curvature
c
. Assume that one of the following holds.

(1)

c\geq 0
,

(2)

c<0
and
\lambda\neq 2(n-1)c
.

Then
\phi
is
\lambda
-biharmonic if and only if it is harmonic.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.4
, we only need to consider
c>0
.
Since
M^{n+1}(c)
is complete, connected, and has positive constant
sectional curvature, its universal Riemannian covering is
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}(c)
.
Lifting
\phi
to this covering, we obtain a Riemannian submersion from

\mathbb{S}^{n+1}(c)
whose fibers define a one-dimensional metric foliation.
By Gromoll and Grove
[
19
]
, this foliation is the Hopf
foliation. Hence its leaves are totally geodesic circles. Since the
covering map is a local isometry, the fibers of
\phi
are minimal.
Therefore
\phi
is harmonic.
∎
