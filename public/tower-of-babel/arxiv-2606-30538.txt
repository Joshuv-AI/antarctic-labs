Abstract
. A complete classification of weak ∗ continuous, measure-valued valuations is established on star bodies in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . Consequences are an integral representation of rotation equivariant, measure-valued valuations and a characterization of dual area measures.

1.
Introduction

In his celebrated work, Hadwiger
[
18
]
classified continuous, translation and rotation invariant, scalar-valued valuations on convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and obtained a characterization of intrinsic volumes. Schneider
[
36
]
extended Hadwiger’s results to measure-valued valuations. He established a classification of weak
∗
continuous, locally determined, translation invariant, and rotation equivariant valuations on convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with values in the space of signed Radon measures on the unit sphere and a characterization of area measures
(see
[
37
]
for comprehensive information on intrinsic volumes, area
measures, and valuations on convex bodies).

Dual intrinsic volumes were introduced by Lutwak
[
31
]
within the framework of the dual Brunn–Minkowski theory. They are defined on the space
\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}
of star bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, equipped with the topology induced by the radial distance (see Section
2
for precise definitions).
A map
\operatorname{Z}
defined on
\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}
with values in an additive abelian semigroup is a
valuation

if

(1)

\operatorname{Z}(K\cup L)+\operatorname{Z}(K\cap L)=\operatorname{Z}(K)+\operatorname{Z}(L).

for every
K,L\in\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}
. In
[
38
,
39
,
42
]
, extending results by Klain
[
20
,
21
]
, a classification of continuous, scalar-valued valuations on
\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}
was established, and a characterization of dual intrinsic volumes was obtained (see
[
17
,
37
]
for more information on the dual Brunn–Minkowski theory and its applications).

The aim of this paper is to establish classification results for measure-valued valuations on
\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}
. Let
M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
denote the space of non-negative Radon measures on the unit sphere
{S^{n-1}}
and
\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
the non-negative real numbers. Our main classification result is given in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

A map
\operatorname{Z}\colon\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
is a weak
{}^{\,*}\!
continuous valuation satisfying
\operatorname{Z}(\{0\})=0
if and only if there exist
\mu\in M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
and a
\mu
-strong Carathéodory and uniformly controlled map

such that

for

-almost every

\langle\operatorname{Z}(L),g\rangle=\int_{{S^{n-1}}}\langle U(\rho_{L}(s),s),g\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

for every
L\in\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}
and
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
.

Here,

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

Let
{S^{n-1}}
be the unit sphere of the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
C({S^{n-1}})
denote the Banach space of continuous functions on
{S^{n-1}}
, endowed with the supremum norm

\|f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}=\sup\{|f(s)|:{s\in{S^{n-1}}}\}.

We will denote the cone of non-negative functions in
C({S^{n-1}})
by
C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. Let
\Sigma_{n}
be the Borel
\sigma
-algebra in
{S^{n-1}}
and
B({S^{n-1}})
the Banach space of bounded Borel measurable functions
g\colon{S^{n-1}}\to\mathbb{R}
, endowed with the supremum norm. We write
B({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
for the cone of non-negative functions in
B({S^{n-1}})
. Note that the formal inclusion of
C({S^{n-1}})
into
B({S^{n-1}})
is a linear isometric embedding.

2.1.
Star bodies

Given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
[0,x]=\{\lambda x:0\leq\lambda\leq 1\}
denote the line segment joining the origin with
x
. A set
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a
star set
if
[0,x]\subset L
for every
x\in L
.
For a star set
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
radial function

\rho_{L}\colon{S^{n-1}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
is given by

\rho_{L}(s)=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0:\lambda s\in L\}.

A star set
L
is called a
star body
whenever
\rho_{L}\in C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. Conversely, for
f\in C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, there exists a star body
L_{f}
such that
f=\rho_{L_{f}}
.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}
denote the set of star bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that star bodies are always bounded.

For
K,L\in\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}
, the
radial sum

K\mathbin{\tilde{+}}L
is defined as the star body with radial function satisfying

\rho_{K\mathbin{\tilde{+}}L}=\rho_{K}+\rho_{L}.

The
radial metric
is defined by

\delta(K,L)=\inf\{\varepsilon\geq 0:K\subset L\mathbin{\tilde{+}}\varepsilon B^{n},L\subset K\mathbin{\tilde{+}}\varepsilon B^{n}\},

where
B^{n}
denotes the Euclidean unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is easy to check that

\delta(K,L)=\sup\{|\rho_{K}(s)-\rho_{L}(s)|:s\in{S^{n-1}}\}=\|\rho_{K}-\rho_{L}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}.

We equip
\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}
with the topology induced by the radial metric.

2.2.
Valuations on star bodies

Given an additive abelian semigroup
X
, a function
\operatorname{Z}\colon\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}\to X
is a
valuation
if

(2)

\operatorname{Z}(K\cup L)+\operatorname{Z}(K\cap L)=\operatorname{Z}(K)+\operatorname{Z}(L)

for all
K,L\in\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}
.

Note that if
K,L\in\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}
, then both
K\cup L
and
K\cap L
are in
\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}
. It is easy to see that

\rho_{K\cup L}=\rho_{K}\vee\rho_{L},\hskip 28.45274pt\rho_{K\cap L}=\rho_{K}\wedge\rho_{L},

where for functions
f,g\colon{S^{n-1}}\to\mathbb{R}
and
s\in{S^{n-1}}
, we set

(f\vee g)(s)=\max\{f(s),g(s)\},

(f\wedge g)(s)=\min\{f(s),g(s)\}.

Therefore, we can identify the valuation
\operatorname{Z}\colon\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}\rightarrow X
with the valuation (in the function space sense)
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\rightarrow X
,
with the natural identification given by

(3)

\operatorname{V}(f)=\operatorname{Z}(L_{f}),

where
L_{f}
is the star body with radial function
\rho_{L_{f}}=f
. In the function space setting, the valuation property (
2
) becomes

(4)

\operatorname{V}(f\vee g)+\operatorname{V}(f\wedge g)=\operatorname{V}(f)+\operatorname{V}(g)

for every
f,g\in C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
.

2.3.
Measure-valued valuations

General valuations are often hard to describe, so continuity assumptions are used to single out the most relevant classes of valuations. In this paper, we focus on weak
∗
continuous valuations on star bodies taking values in the space of measures on the sphere.

Let
M({S^{n-1}})
be the space of signed Radon measures on
{S^{n-1}}
, equipped with the variation norm
\|\mu\|=|\mu|({S^{n-1}})
. The classical Riesz representation theorem (cf.
[
2
, Theorem 7.2.8]
) states that
M({S^{n-1}})
can be identified with the Banach space dual of
C({S^{n-1}})
. The duality pairing is given by

(5)

\mu(f)=\langle\mu,f\rangle=\int_{{S^{n-1}}}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

for
f\in C({S^{n-1}})
and
\mu\in M({S^{n-1}})
.

We recall that, given a Banach space
X
, its dual
X^{*}
consists of all bounded linear functionals
x^{*}\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
, and the weak
∗
topology in
X^{*}
is defined as the coarsest topology making continuous the evaluation on points of
X
, in other words, the topology induced by neighborhoods of the form

\{x^{*}\in X^{*}:\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|x^{*}(x_{i})-x_{0}^{*}(x_{i})|<\varepsilon\}

for every
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{m}\subset X
,
x_{0}^{*}\in X^{*}
and
\varepsilon>0
.

We say that a valuation
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})\rightarrow M({S^{n-1}})
is
weak
{}^{\,*}
continuous
if it is continuous when we consider the norm topology in
C({S^{n-1}})
and the weak
∗
topology in
M({S^{n-1}})
, or, equivalently, if the scalar-valued valuation

\operatorname{V}_{g}\colon C({S^{n-1}})\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

defined by

\operatorname{V}_{g}(f)=\langle\operatorname{V}(f),g\rangle=\int_{{S^{n-1}}}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}\operatorname{V}(f)(s),

is continuous for every
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
.

We denote the orthogonal group in dimension
n
by
\operatorname{O}(n)
.
Given a function
f\colon{S^{n-1}}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\phi\in\operatorname{O}(n)
, we will often need the rotation of
f
by
\phi
, which we will denote by
\phi f=f\circ\phi^{-1}
.

Similarly, given
A\in\Sigma_{n}
, the rotation of
A
by
\phi
corresponds to the Borel set
\phi A=\{\phi(t)\colon t\in A\}
, which in terms of the corresponding characteristic functions
\chi_{A}
can be written as
\phi\chi_{A}=\chi_{\phi A}
.

We say that a valuation
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})\to M({S^{n-1}})
is
rotation equivariant
if

\operatorname{V}(\phi f)(\phi B)=\operatorname{V}(f)(B)

for every
f\in C({S^{n-1}})
,
B\in\Sigma_{n}
, and
\phi\in\operatorname{O}(n)
;
equivalently, by the Riesz representation theorem,

\langle\operatorname{V}(\phi f),\phi g\rangle=\langle\operatorname{V}(f),g\rangle

for every
f,g\in C({S^{n-1}})
and
\phi\in\operatorname{O}(n)
.

It is convenient to work with valuations defined on the cone of non-negative, continuous functions,
C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. We remark that there is more than one way to extend a valuation
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})
to a map defined on the whole space
C({S^{n-1}})
: define
\operatorname{V}^{\pm}\colon C({S^{n-1}})\to M({S^{n-1}})
by

\operatorname{V}^{\pm}(f)=\operatorname{V}(f\vee 0)\pm\operatorname{V}((-f)\vee 0).

It is straightforward to check that
\operatorname{V}^{\pm}
also satisfies the valuation property (
4
). Conversely, given a valuation
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})\to M({S^{n-1}})
, we define the maps
\operatorname{V}_{\pm}\colon C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})
by

\operatorname{V}_{\pm}(f)=\operatorname{V}(\pm f).

It is clear that
\operatorname{V}_{\pm}
are valuations. Assuming that
\operatorname{V}(0)=0
, we can always write

(6)

\operatorname{V}(f)=\operatorname{V}_{+}(f_{\scriptscriptstyle+})+\operatorname{V}_{-}(f_{\scriptscriptstyle-}),

where
f_{\scriptscriptstyle+}=f\vee 0
and
f_{\scriptscriptstyle-}=(-f)\vee 0
.

We remark that the classification results that we will derive for valuations on
C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
can be immediately extended to classification results for valuations on
C({S^{n-1}})
with values in the space of signed Radon measures on
{S^{n-1}}
such that
\operatorname{V}(0)=0
and
\operatorname{V}(f)\in M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
for non-negative
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
using the above decomposition. Therefore, in this work, we will focus on valuations
\operatorname{V}:C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
which satisfy
\operatorname{V}(0)=0
.

2.4.
Strong Carathéodory property

The following notion appeared in
[
13
]
. Let
\mu\in M({S^{n-1}})
be given. A map

K\colon\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\times{S^{n-1}}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\mu
-strong Carathéodory
if the mapping
s\mapsto K(\lambda,s)
is Borel measurable on
{S^{n-1}}
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
and there exists a Borel set
S_{0}\subset{S^{n-1}}
with
\mu(S_{0})=\mu({S^{n-1}})
such that for every
s\in S_{0}
the mapping
\lambda\mapsto K(\lambda,s)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. If
K
is
\mu
-strong Carathéodory on
\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\times{S^{n-1}}
, then, for every
f\in C({S^{n-1}})
, the mapping

s\mapsto K(f(s),s)

is Borel measurable.

In the sequel we will need to extend this definition to cover the case of functions
U:\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\times{S^{n-1}}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{+}
where in
M({S^{n-1}})_{+}
we consider the weak
∗
topology. We will say that
U
thus defined is
\mu
-strong Carathéodory if, for every
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
, the map
U_{g}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\times{S^{n-1}}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
U_{g}(\lambda,s)=U(\lambda,s)(g)
is
\mu
-strong Carathéodory.

3.
Vector-Valued Valuations on Star Bodies

This paper aims to classify measure-valued valuations defined on star bodies. Still, some preliminary results are better understood when stated and proved in slightly greater generality.

In this section, we will focus on valuations defined on
C({S^{n-1}})
with values on an arbitrary dual Banach space
X^{*}
(with the dual norm
\|\cdot\|_{*}
), that is, valuations
V:C({S^{n-1}})\to X^{*}
. In the next sections we will restrict to the case
X^{*}=M({S^{n-1}})
.

We say that a valuation is
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})\to X^{*}
is
bounded on bounded sets
if, for every
\lambda>0
, there exists
\gamma\geq 0
such that

\|\operatorname{V}(f)\|_{*}\leq\gamma

for every
f\in C({S^{n-1}})
with
\|f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq\lambda
.

By
[
38
, Lemma 3.1]
, every continuous, scalar-valued valuation on
C({S^{n-1}})
is bounded on bounded sets. It follows from this fact and the Uniform Boundedness Principle, that weak
∗
continuous valuations are bounded on bounded sets:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X^{*}
be a dual Banach space. If
\,\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})\to X^{*}
is a weak
∗
continuous valuation, then
\operatorname{V}
is bounded on bounded sets.

Proof.

Given
\lambda>0
, define
\mathcal{M}\subset X^{*}
by
\mathcal{M}=\{\operatorname{V}(f)\colon\|f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq\lambda\}
. For every
x\in X
, the scalar-valued valuation
\operatorname{V}_{x}\colon C({S^{n-1}})\to\mathbb{R}
, given by
\operatorname{V}_{x}(f)=\langle\operatorname{V}(f),x\rangle
, is continuous. Therefore, it follows from
[
38
, Lemma 3.1]
that

\sup\{|\langle x^{*},x\rangle|:x^{*}\in\mathcal{M}\}<\infty.

Since this holds for every
x\in X
, it follows from the Uniform Boundedness Principle (cf.
[
14
, Theorem 3.15]
) that
\mathcal{M}
is bounded in norm.
∎

The main tools to describe and classify continuous, scalar-valued valuations on star bodies in
[
38
,
39
,
42
]
came from Measure Theory. Specifically, the Riesz representation theorem for linear functionals on
C({S^{n-1}})
was used several times.

A key idea in those papers was to use the fact that the values of a valuation on continuous functions which approximate pointwise a characteristic function of the form
\lambda\chi_{A}
, with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
and
A\in\Sigma_{n}
, can be used to define a family of measures
\omega_{\lambda}
on
\Sigma_{n}
which depend continuously on
\lambda
. This is essentially the same as extending a valuation
\operatorname{W}\colon C({S^{n-1}})\to\mathbb{R}
to a valuation
\overline{\operatorname{W}}\colon B({S^{n-1}})\to\mathbb{R}
in a canonical way.

In this paper, we will use the vector-valued version of this approach. Let us start by considering scalar-valued valuations obtained by composing with elements in
X
.

Given a weak
∗
continuous valuation
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})\to X^{*}
and
x\in X
, let
\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{x}\colon B({S^{n-1}})\to\mathbb{R}
be the canonical extension of
\operatorname{V}_{x}=\langle\operatorname{V},x\rangle
to the space of bounded Borel functions as constructed in
[
38
]
. For completeness, we outline the extension construction here without providing proofs of the statements. Details can be found in
[
38
]
.

First, by
[
38
, Theorem 1.3]
, every valuation
\operatorname{W}\colon C({S^{n-1}})\to\mathbb{R}
can be written as a difference of non-negative valuations, that is, there exist non-negative valuations
\operatorname{W}^{+}
and
\operatorname{W}^{-}
such that
\operatorname{W}=\operatorname{W}^{+}-\operatorname{W}^{-}
(however, this decomposition need not be unique). The following construction defines a Radon measure for every
\lambda>0
and each sign
\pm
: For
A\in\Sigma_{n}
, we set

(7)

\omega_{\lambda}^{\pm}(A)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\\
G\text{ open}\\
A\subset G\end{subarray}}\,\,\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\text{ closed}\\[1.0pt]
K\subset G\end{subarray}}\inf\Big\{\operatorname{W}^{\pm}(f)\colon f\in C({S^{n-1}}),\,\chi_{K}\leq\frac{f}{\lambda}\leq\chi_{G}\Big\},

and

(8)

\omega_{\lambda}=\omega^{+}_{\lambda}-\omega^{-}_{\lambda}.

For a simple function
g=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\chi_{A_{i}}
with pairwise disjoint
A_{i}\in\Sigma_{n}
and
\lambda_{i}>0
, we define

\overline{\operatorname{W}}(g)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\omega_{\lambda_{i}}(A_{i}).

This definition admits a unique continuous extension to
B({S^{n-1}})
, and it can be checked that

\overline{\operatorname{W}}(f)=\operatorname{W}(f)
for every
f\in C({S^{n-1}})
(see
[
38
, Theorem 6.1]
). Note that the extended valuation is continuous (with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}
). In particular, the following identity will be useful (see
[
38
, Lemma 5.6]
): for every closed set
C\subset{S^{n-1}}
and
\lambda\geq 0
, we have

(9)

\omega_{\lambda}(C)=\lim_{i\to\infty}\operatorname{W}(f_{i})

whenever
f_{i}\in C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
are such that
\|f_{i}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq\lambda
,
f_{i}(s)=\lambda
for
s\in C
, and
f_{i}(s)=0
when
\mbox{dist}(s,C)\geq 1/i
(where
\mbox{dist}(s,C)
is the infimum of the spherical distances of
s
and
t\in C
).

Next, we show that the extension procedure is linear.

Lemma 3.2
.

For valuations
\operatorname{W}\colon C({S^{n-1}})\to\mathbb{R}
, the extension procedure from
\operatorname{W}
to
\overline{\operatorname{W}}\colon B({S^{n-1}})\to\mathbb{R}
is linear in
\operatorname{W}
, that is,

\overline{\alpha\operatorname{W}_{1}+\beta\operatorname{W}_{2}}=\alpha\overline{\operatorname{W}_{1}}+\beta\overline{\operatorname{W}_{2}}

for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and scalar-valued valuations
\operatorname{W}_{1},\operatorname{W}_{2}
on
C({S^{n-1}})
.

Proof.

For every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, let
\omega^{1}_{\lambda}
and
\omega^{2}_{\lambda}
be the measures induced by
\operatorname{W}_{1}
and
\operatorname{W}_{2}
, respectively, in the procedure described above, and let
\omega_{\lambda}
be the measure induced by
\alpha\operatorname{W}_{1}+\beta\operatorname{W}_{2}
. For every closed set
C
and every sequence
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
such that
\|f_{i}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq\lambda
,
f_{i}(s)=\lambda
for
s\in C
and
f_{i}(s)=0
for
\mbox{dist}(s,C)\geq 1/i
, we obtain by (
9
) that

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}(C)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}(\alpha\operatorname{W}_{1}+\beta\operatorname{W}_{2})(f_{i})

\displaystyle=\alpha\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{W}_{1}(f_{i})+\beta\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{W}_{2}(f_{i})=\alpha\,\omega^{1}_{\lambda}(C)+\beta\,\omega^{2}_{\lambda}(C).

By the regularity of the measures involved, the same identity holds for arbitrary sets in
\Sigma_{n}
. Hence, the linearity of the extension procedure holds on simple functions. The result now follows from the continuity of the extended valuations and density of simple functions in
B({S^{n-1}})
.
∎

In the following, we introduce scalar measures associated to a weak
∗
continuous valuation
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})\to X^{*}
with
\operatorname{V}(0)=0
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
and
x\in X
, let

(10)

\nu_{\lambda,x}=\omega_{\lambda}

be the scalar-valued measure associated to
\operatorname{W}=\langle\operatorname{V},x\rangle
in (
8
).

Now, given
A\in\Sigma_{n}
, we consider the linear and continuous mapping
x\mapsto\nu_{\lambda,x}(A)
. The linearity of this mapping follows from Lemma
3.2
and the continuity from the definition of
\nu_{\lambda,x}(A)
and Proposition
3.1
. Note also, that for a fixed
x\in X
the mapping
A\mapsto\nu_{\lambda,x}(A)
is a measure on
\Sigma_{n}
.

We will use the following notion of control measure.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\operatorname{V}:C({S^{n-1}})\to X^{*}
be a weak
∗
continuous valuation. For every
x\in X
and
\lambda\geq 0
, let
\nu_{\lambda,x}:\Sigma_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the associated (and previously defined) measures. A scalar measure
\mu:\Sigma_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be a control measure of
\operatorname{V}
if there is
\zeta:\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, called control function, such that

(11)

|\nu_{\lambda,x}(A)|\leq\|x\|\zeta(\lambda)\mu(A)

for every
\lambda\geq 0
,
A\in\Sigma_{n}
and
x\in X
, where
\|x\|
denotes the norm of
x
in
X
.

4.
Construction of a control measure and further results from the scalar case

Throughout the remainder of the paper we restrict to the case where
X=C({S^{n-1}})
and denote by
\|\cdot\|
the dual norm on
M({S^{n-1}})
, dropping the subscript
*
. A key difference with respect to the scalar case is that while every scalar valuation can be written as the difference of two non-negative valuations, this property fails for vector-valued valuations. To see this, it suffices to consider a valuation defined by a continuous linear operator
T:C(S^{n-1})\to M(S^{n-1})
that is not absolutely summing (hence, it cannot be written as a difference of positive linear operators, see
[
12
, Chapter 6]
). Thus, from now on we focus on
valuations
\operatorname{V}:C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
that are weak
∗
continuous and satisfy
\operatorname{V}(0)=0
.

In previous works
[
38
,
39
]
, a control measure was constructed for scalar valuations. Let us first recall this construction. Given a non-negative valuation
\operatorname{W}:C({S^{n-1}})\to\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
and
\lambda_{0}\geq 0
, a Radon measure
\mu_{\lambda_{0}}:\Sigma_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
can be defined such that on open sets
G\subset{S^{n-1}}

(12)

\mu_{\lambda_{0}}(G)=\sup\{\operatorname{W}(f)\colon f\prec G,\,\|f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq\lambda_{0}\},

where
f\prec G
means that the support of
f
is contained in
G
.

The key property of
\mu_{\lambda_{0}}
, and the one needed for the arguments in the previous works
[
38
,
39
]
, is its “control” property: for every
A\in\Sigma_{n}
and
f\in C({S^{n-1}})
,

(13)

f\prec A\,\text{ and }\,\|f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq\lambda_{0}\,\text{ imply }\,|\operatorname{W}(f)|\leq\mu_{\lambda_{0}}(A).

Suppose that
f\in C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. Let us consider the constant one function
\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}
. It follows from the positivity of
\operatorname{V}
that

0\leq\langle\operatorname{V}(f),g\rangle\leq\langle\operatorname{V}(f),\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle

for
g\geq 0
.
Hence,

(14)

|\operatorname{V}_{g}(f)|=|\langle\operatorname{V}(f),g\rangle|=|\langle\operatorname{V}(f),g_{+}-g_{-}\rangle|\leq\langle\operatorname{V}(f),|g|\rangle\leq\operatorname{V}_{\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(f)\,\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}

for
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
.

Let
\mu_{\lambda_{0},\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}
be the measure defined in (
12
) associated to the scalar-valued valuation
\operatorname{V}_{\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}=\langle\operatorname{V},\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle
. It follows from (
14
) that for every
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
,
f\in C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
and
A\in\Sigma_{n}
with
\|f\|\leq\lambda_{0}
and
f\prec A
, we obtain

|V_{g}(f)|\leq V_{\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(f)\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq\mu_{\lambda_{0},\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(A)\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}.

In order to handle arbitrarily large functions
f\in C({S^{n-1}})
, we will use the following measure:

(15)

\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{i,\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}}{2^{i}\|\mu_{i,\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}\|},

where
\|\mu_{i,\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}\|
denotes the variation norm. Note that the above series is absolutely convergent and, therefore, defines a proper, non-negative, regular measure. Although the measure
\mu
may be defined on a larger
\sigma
-algebra, we restrict to Borel sets, since this is sufficient for our purposes.

Now let us see that the measure
\mu
is a control measure in the sense of (
11
). It follows easily that if
f\in C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
,
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
and
A\in\Sigma_{n}
with
f\prec A
one has

(16)

|V_{g}(f)|\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\mu_{m,\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(A)\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}2^{m}\|\mu_{m,\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}\|\mu(A)

for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq m
. Define now the function
\zeta
as follows

(17)

\zeta(t)=2^{\lceil t\rceil}\|\mu_{\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}},\lceil t\rceil}\|.

where
\lceil t\rceil=\min\{m\in\mathbb{Z}:t\leq m\}
denotes the ceiling function.

Finally, to check that
\mu
is a control measure we have to verify inequality (
11
). Let
C\subset{S^{n-1}}
be a closed set, let
\lambda>0
and let
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
. Consider a sequence
\{f_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
with
\|f_{m}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq\lambda
,
f_{m}(s)=\lambda
for
s\in C
and
f_{m}(s)=0
whenever
\mbox{dist}(s,C)\geq 1/m
. By (
9
),

|\nu_{\lambda,g}(C)|=|\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{g}(\lambda\chi_{C})|=\lim_{m\to\infty}|\operatorname{V}_{g}(f_{m})|\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\zeta(\lambda)\mu(C).

The regularity of the measures implies now that the same inequality holds for Borel sets. That is, for every
A\in\Sigma_{n}
,

|\nu_{\lambda,g}(A)|\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\zeta(\lambda)\mu(A).

Hence,
\mu
is a control measure with
\zeta
as the control function.

We state for further reference the following trivial fact about the control function:

Fact 4.1
.

\zeta
is non-decreasing and constant on each interval
[m-1,m)
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Our next objective is to adapt the integral representation result for scalar-valued valuations from
[
39
, Theorem 1.1]
. In that paper, the representation was obtained for a specific control measure which does not necessarily coincide with our
\mu
. Thus, our goal is to obtain an integral representation of
\operatorname{V}_{g}
with respect to the measure
\mu
defined in (
15
). This representation with
\mu
follows directly from the proof of
[
39
, Proposition 2.2]
where it becomes apparent that the only requirement on the measure
\mu
is the control property. In particular, one can prove the following result.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\operatorname{V}\colon C(S^{n-1})_{+}\rightarrow M(S^{n-1})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
be a weak
∗
continuous valuation, and consider
\mu
as above. For
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
, there is a
\mu
-strong Carathéodory mapping
K_{g}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\times{S^{n-1}}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\operatorname{V}_{g}(f)=\int_{{S^{n-1}}}K_{g}(f(s),s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

for every
f\in B({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. Moreover,
K_{g}
can be chosen to satisfy the following conditions.

•

For each
\lambda\geq 0
, the function
K_{g}(\lambda,\cdot)
coincides with the Radon–Nikodym derivative with respect to
\mu
of the measure
\nu_{\lambda,g}
associated to the scalar-valued valuation
\operatorname{V}_{g}
as in (
10
). In particular, we have
K_{g}(\lambda,\cdot)\in L_{1}({S^{n-1}},\mu)
.

•

The function
K_{g}(\cdot,s)
is continuous for every
s\in{S^{n-1}}
.

Proof.

For the sake of completeness we include the details in Appendix
A
.
∎

We will repeatedly rely on the fact that
K_{g}(\lambda,\cdot)
belongs not only to
L_{1}({S^{n-1}},\mu)
, but also to
L_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}({S^{n-1}},\mu)
. This will be established in the next lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
, then the function
K_{g}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\times{S^{n-1}}\to\mathbb{R}
in Lemma
4.2
satisfies

K_{g}(\lambda,s)\leq\zeta(\lambda)\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}

for
\mu
-almost every
s\in{S^{n-1}}
and for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
.

Proof.

We consider
K_{g}(\lambda,\cdot)
as an element of
L_{1}({S^{n-1}},\mu)
. On the one hand, it follows from the definition of Radon–Nikodym derivative and Lemma
4.2
that

\nu_{\lambda,g}(A)=\int_{A}K_{g}(\lambda,s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

for every Borel set
A\subset{S^{n-1}}
.
On the other hand, for every
\varepsilon>0
, it follows from the regularity of
\mu
that there is an open set
G\supset A
with

(18)

{\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\zeta(\lambda)}\,\mu(G\setminus A)\leq{\varepsilon}.

It also follows from the definition of
\nu_{\lambda,g}
in (
7
) that there is a closed set
C\subset G
such that

(19)

\nu_{\lambda,g}(A)\leq\inf\{\langle\operatorname{V}(f),g\rangle:f\in C({S^{n-1}}),\,\lambda\chi_{C}\leq f\leq\lambda\chi_{G}\}+\varepsilon.

Since we have
\mu_{\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}},\lambda}(G)=\sup\{\langle\operatorname{V}(f),\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle:f\in C({S^{n-1}}),\,0\leq f\leq\lambda\chi_{G}\}
and
\langle\operatorname{V}(f),g\rangle\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\langle\operatorname{V}(f),\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle
, it follows that

(20)

\nu_{\lambda,g}(A)\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\zeta(\lambda)\mu(G)+\varepsilon.

Combining (
18
) and (
20
), we have

\nu_{\lambda,g}(A)\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\zeta(\lambda)\mu(G)+\varepsilon\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\zeta(\lambda)\mu(A)+2\varepsilon.

Since this happens for every
\varepsilon>0
, we obtain that

\int_{A}K_{g}(\lambda,s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\nu_{\lambda,g}(A)\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\zeta(\lambda)\,\mu(A)

for every Borel set
A\subset{S^{n-1}}
, which proves the result.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For every
g,h\in C({S^{n-1}})
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
K_{g}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\times{S^{n-1}}\to\mathbb{R}
from Lemma
4.2
satisfies

K_{g}(\lambda,s)+K_{h}(\lambda,s)=K_{g+h}(\lambda,s)\quad\text{and}\quad K_{\alpha g}(\lambda,s)=\alpha K_{g}(\lambda,s)

for
\mu
-almost every
s\in{S^{n-1}}
and for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
.

Proof.

It follows from the Radon–Nikodym representation theorem that

\displaystyle\int_{A}K_{g+h}(\lambda,s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\nu_{\lambda,g+h}(A)

\displaystyle=\nu_{\lambda,g}(A)+\nu_{\lambda,h}(A)

\displaystyle=\int_{A}K_{g}(\lambda,s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)+\int_{A}K_{h}(\lambda,s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{A}(K_{g}(\lambda,s)+K_{h}(\lambda,s))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

for every Borel set
A\subset{S^{n-1}}
. Consequently, the first equality holds
\mu
-almost everywhere in
s\in{S^{n-1}}
. The second identity follows analogously.
∎

5.
Characterization of weak
∗
continuous valuations

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the set (of restrictions to
{S^{n-1}}
) of polynomials with rational coefficients defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It follows from the Stone–Weierstrass Theorem that
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a (countable) dense subset of
C({S^{n-1}})
. For our purposes, we could actually take any countable dense set in
C({S^{n-1}})
, closed under sums and multiplication by rational numbers, instead of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Let
\left(p_{j}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be an enumeration of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and let
(\lambda_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be an enumeration of
\mathbb{Q}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. We consider now the functions
K_{p_{j}}
as in Lemma
4.2
.

For every pair
(p_{i},p_{j})\subset\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let us consider

S^{m}_{i,j}=\{s\in{S^{n-1}}:K_{p_{i}}(\lambda_{m},s)+K_{p_{j}}(\lambda_{m},s)\neq K_{p_{i}+p_{j}}(\lambda_{m},s)\}.

By Lemma
4.4
, we obtain that
S^{m}_{i,j}
is Borel,
\mu(S^{m}_{i,j})=0
and, therefore, the set
S_{1}=\bigcup_{i,j,m\in\mathbb{N}}S^{m}_{i,j}
is also Borel with
\mu(S_{1})=0
.

Observe that for each
s\notin S_{1}
and all
i,j,m\in\mathbb{N}
, the relation
K_{p_{i}}(\lambda_{m},s)+K_{p_{j}}(\lambda_{m},s)=K_{p_{i}+p_{j}}(\lambda_{m},s)
holds.

Now, for
\lambda\not\in\mathbb{Q}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, consider
\{\lambda_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
converging to
\lambda
.
Since
K_{p}(\cdot,s)
is continuous for every
s\not\in S_{1}
and every
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, for every
s\not\in S_{1}
,

\displaystyle K_{p_{i}}(\lambda,s)+K_{p_{j}}(\lambda,s)

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}K_{p_{i}}(\lambda_{m},s)+K_{p_{j}}(\lambda_{m},s)

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}K_{p_{i}+p_{j}}(\lambda_{m},s)=K_{p_{i}+p_{j}}(\lambda,s).

Hence,
p_{i}\mapsto K_{p_{i}}(\lambda,s)
is additive for every
\lambda\geq 0
and every
s\not\in S_{1}
. It is well known that every additive function is
\mathbb{Q}
-linear. Therefore,

K_{\alpha p}(\lambda,s)=\alpha K_{p}(\lambda,s)

for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
, every
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, and every
s\not\in S_{1}
.

Next, we will make a similar procedure for the boundedness of the mapping. For every
p_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and every
\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, let

S^{m}_{i}=\{s\in{S^{n-1}}:K_{p_{i}}(\lambda_{m},s)>\zeta(\lambda_{m})\|p_{i}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\}.

By Lemma
4.3
,
S^{m}_{i}
is Borel and
\mu(S^{m}_{i})=0
. Let
S_{2}=\bigcup_{i,m\in\mathbb{N}}S^{m}_{i}
which is also Borel and satisfies
\mu(S_{2})=0
. Again, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, take a sequence
\{\lambda_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
with
\lambda_{m}\to\lambda
. Since
K_{p_{i}}(\cdot,s)
is continuous, for every
s\not\in S_{2}
,

K_{p_{i}}(\lambda,s)=\lim_{m\to\infty}K_{p_{i}}(\lambda_{m},s)\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\zeta(\lambda_{m})\|p_{i}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}.

By Fact
4.1
, we have
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\zeta(\lambda_{m})\leq\zeta(\lambda)
. Thus, for every
s\not\in S_{2}
and every
p_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, we have

K_{p_{i}}(\lambda,s)\leq\zeta(\lambda)\|p_{i}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}.

Finally we define

(21)

S={S^{n-1}}\setminus\left(S_{1}\cup S_{2}\right),

which is Borel and has
\mu
-full measure.

For every
\lambda\geq 0
and every
s\in S
, consider the mapping

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
p_{i}&\longmapsto&K_{p_{i}}(\lambda,s),\end{array}

which is a linear and bounded with respect to the uniform norm, by the previous discussion. Therefore, we can consider its linear and continuous extension

(22)

U(\lambda,s):C({S^{n-1}})\to\mathbb{R}

which satisfies the following fact.

Fact 5.1
.

For every
s\in S
, we have
\|U(\lambda,s)\|\leq\zeta(\lambda)
.

For every
s\in S
, we obtain a continuous linear functional such that

U(\lambda,s)(p_{i})=\langle U(\lambda,s),p_{i}\rangle=K_{p_{i}}(\lambda,s)

for every
p_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and every
\lambda\geq 0
. Hence, for every
A\in\Sigma_{n}

\nu_{\lambda,p_{i}}(A)=\int_{A}K_{p_{i}}(\lambda,s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\int_{A}U(\lambda,s)(p_{i})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s).

Next, we show that a similar result holds not only for
p_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
but also for every
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
.
Specifically, we show that
s\mapsto U(\lambda,s)(g)
, considered as an element in
L_{1}({S^{n-1}},\mu)
, coincides with the Radon–Nikodym derivative of
\nu_{\lambda,g}
, that is,
U(\lambda,s)(g)=K_{g}(\lambda,s)
as elements of
L_{1}({S^{n-1}},\mu)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
. For every
A\in\Sigma_{n}
, we have

\nu_{\lambda,g}(A)=\int_{A}K_{g}(\lambda,s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\int_{A}U(\lambda,s)(g)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s).

Proof.

Let
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, and let
S\subset{S^{n-1}}
be defined as in (
21
), and consider the function

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle\longmapsto U(\lambda,s)(g).

Note that this is well-defined as
U(\lambda,s)\in M({S^{n-1}})=C({S^{n-1}})^{*}
for
s\in S
.

Choose a sequence
(p_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
which converges to
g
in the supremum norm. Note that, for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
and for every
s\in S
, we have
U(\lambda,s)(p_{i})=K_{p_{i}}(\lambda,s)
, and, therefore,
s\mapsto U(\lambda,s)(p_{i})
is Borel.
Also, note that it follows from the continuity of
U(\lambda,s)
that
\lim_{i\to\infty}U(\lambda,s)(p_{i})=U(\lambda,s)(g)
for every
s\in S
.
In particular, the function
s\mapsto U(\lambda,s)(g)
is Borel on
{S^{n-1}}
(where we set
U(\lambda,s)(g)=0
for
s\notin S
).

Fact
5.1
yields that
\|U(\lambda,s)\|\leq\zeta(\lambda)
for every
s\in S
. Therefore, for every
s\in S
we have

|U(\lambda,s)(g)|\leq\zeta(\lambda)\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}.

In particular, the map
s\mapsto U(\lambda,s)(g)
defines an element in
L_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}({S^{n-1}},\mu)\subset L_{1}({S^{n-1}},\mu)
.

We can choose the sequence
(p_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
so that
\|p_{i}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq 1+\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, the constant function
\zeta(\lambda)(1+\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty})\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}
dominates
U(\lambda,\cdot)(g)
and
U(\lambda,\cdot)(p_{i})
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Finally, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, for every
A\in\Sigma_{n}

\displaystyle\nu_{\lambda,g}(A)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\nu_{\lambda,p_{i}}(A)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{A}K_{p_{i}}(\lambda,s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{A}U(\lambda,s)(p_{i})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\lim_{i\to\infty}U(\lambda,s)(p_{i})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\int_{A}U(\lambda,s)(g)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s).

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

For every
s\in S
and every
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
, the mapping

\lambda\mapsto U(\lambda,s)(g)

is continuous on

.

Proof.

Fix

,

\displaystyle|

\displaystyle U(\lambda_{i},s)(g)-U(\lambda,s)(g)|

\displaystyle\leq|U(\lambda_{i},s)(g-p_{j})|+|U(\lambda,s)(g-p_{j})|+|U(\lambda_{i},s)(p_{j})-U(\lambda,s)(p_{j})|

\displaystyle\leq\|g-p_{j}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}(\|U(\lambda_{i},s)\|+\|U(\lambda,s)\|)+|U(\lambda_{i},s)(p_{j})-U(\lambda,s)(p_{j})|

The previous result shows that for every

, the function of two variables

is
\mu
-strong Carathéodory, and in particular, it is measurable. Moreover,

for every

We now have all the tools to prove our main result Theorem

. First, we state it in the context of valuations on functions.

Theorem 5.4
.

A map
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}

∗
​ continuous valuation with

\mu\in M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}

\mu
-strong Carathéodory and uniformly controlled map

for
\mu
-almost every

\langle\operatorname{V}(f),g\rangle=\int_{{S^{n-1}}}\langle U(f(s),s),g\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

and every

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
be a weak
∗
continuous valuation and let
\mu
and
\zeta
be defined as in (

) and (

, the map

It remains to prove that (

and

Note that,

For the converse implication, note first that since

Note that the

Clearly, from this result, Theorem

.

If
\,\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}

∗

Moreover, this extension satisfies that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\langle\overline{\operatorname{V}}(h_{i}),g\rangle=\langle\overline{\operatorname{V}}(h),g\rangle

and for every

Proof.

First, recall that, for every

In particular, this means that measure-valued weak
∗
continuous valuations on star bodies can be extended to bounded Borel star sets preserving continuity. The following is an elementary observation that we include for the sake of clarity.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
\mu\in M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. Let

•

For every
\lambda\geq 0
,

for
\mu
-almost every

•

The valuations defined through

and

coincide. That is, for every

and

Proof.

Assume that for every
\lambda\geq 0
, there exists
S_{\lambda}\subset{S^{n-1}}
of
\mu
-full measure such that if

. For every

\left\langle\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\chi_{A_{i}}\right),g\right\rangle=\int_{{S^{n-1}}}\left\langle U_{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\chi_{A_{i}},s\right),g\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\\
\hskip 56.9055pt=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{A_{i}}\langle U_{1}(\lambda_{i},s),g\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{A_{i}}\langle U_{2}(\lambda_{i},s),g\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\\

Therefore,

and

Conversely, if

\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{1}=\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{2}
and for all
\lambda\geq 0
,

\lambda\geq 0
. For every

, there exists a set

of

\langle U_{1}(\lambda,s),p\rangle=\langle U_{2}(\lambda,s),p\rangle\quad\text{for all }p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Since

is a dense set in

∎

6.
Rotation Equivariant, Homogeneous and locally determined valuations

In this section, we specialize our main result to obtain classification theorems for valuations with certain geometric properties.

6.1.
Rotation equivariant valuations

Our first aim is to show that the integral representation of Theorem
5.4
has a special form in the rotation equivariant case. In particular, many of the technical steps
in the previous sections can be simplified in this setting.

Recall the definition of
\nu_{\lambda,g}
in (
10
) and the definition of the control measure
\mu
in (
15
). First, the rotation equivariance implies that for every
\phi\in\operatorname{O}(n)
and every

(24)

\nu_{\lambda,g}(A)=\langle\overline{V}(\lambda\chi_{A}),g\rangle=\langle\overline{V}(\lambda\chi_{\phi A}),\phi g\rangle=\nu_{\lambda,\phi g}(\phi A).

In particular, if

, then
\nu_{\lambda,\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}
is a rotation invariant measure. This will also be the case for the control measure
\mu

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
V\colon C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
be a weak
∗
continuous valuation that is rotation equivariant and satisfies
\operatorname{V}(0)=0

defined in (

and every

\mu(A)=\mu(\phi A).

Therefore,
\mu
is a multiple of the Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
on
{S^{n-1}}
.

Proof.

We recall that
\mu
is defined in (
15
) by a series which elements are
\mu_{i,\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}
with
i\in\mathbb{N}
. For every open set

, these are defined by (

\mu_{i,\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(G)=\sup\left\{V_{\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(f):f\prec G,\|f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq i\right\}.

For
\phi\in\operatorname{O}(n)
and every open set

, since
\|\phi f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}=\|f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{i,\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(\phi G)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{V_{\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(f):f\prec\phi G,\|f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq i\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{V_{\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(f):\phi^{-1}f\prec G,\|f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq i\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{V_{\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(f):\phi^{-1}f\prec G,\|\phi^{-1}f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq i\right\}.

Therefore, taking
f^{\prime}=\phi^{-1}f
, using that
\phi
is bijective and
\phi^{-1}\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}=\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}
, we get

\displaystyle\mu_{i,\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(\phi G)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{V_{\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(\phi f^{\prime}):f^{\prime}\prec G,\|f^{\prime}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq i\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{V_{\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(f^{\prime}):f^{\prime}\prec G,\|f^{\prime}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq i\right\}

\displaystyle=\mu_{i,\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}(G).

Thus, by regularity of the measures it follows that
\mu_{i,\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}}

∎

The main advantage in this case is the fact that the function

from Lemma

can be replaced with another function

with a more explicit description. This new function leads to a

which satisfies the main Theorem
5.4
and is rotation equivariant. In order to construct this

, let
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, let

denote the function given by Lemma
4.2
and let

be the set defined in (

).

From the Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem (see
[
15
, 2.8-2.9]
for an extensive discussion), there exists a Borel set

such that
\mu({S^{n-1}}\setminus S_{\lambda,p})=0
and

for every

, where

denotes the closed ball centered at

as that limit.

First, define
S_{\lambda}=\bigcap_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}}S_{\lambda,p}
and let

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\nu_{\lambda,g}(B(s,\rho))}{\mu(B(s,\rho))}=\langle U(\lambda,s),g\rangle

\displaystyle\left|\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\nu_{\lambda,g}(B(s,\rho))}{\mu(B(s,\rho))}-\langle U(\lambda,s),g\rangle\right|\hskip 184.9429pt

\displaystyle=\left|\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\nu_{\lambda,g}(B(s,\rho))}{\mu(B(s,\rho))}-\lim_{i\to\infty}\langle U(\lambda,s),p_{i}\rangle\right|\hskip 45.5244pt

\displaystyle=\left|\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\nu_{\lambda,g}(B(s,\rho))}{\mu(B(s,\rho))}-\lim_{i\to\infty}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\nu_{\lambda,p}(B(s,\rho))}{\mu(B(s,\rho))}\right|\hskip 2.84526pt

\displaystyle=\left|\lim_{i\to\infty}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\nu_{\lambda,g-p_{i}}(B(s,\rho))}{\mu(B(s,\rho))}\right|\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\|g-p_{i}\|\zeta(\lambda).

The limit inferior is treated analogously. Therefore, the limit exists for any
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
and any

. To extend beyond

\mu
-full measure set,

Since the right hand side converges when

and any

U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)(g)=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\nu_{\lambda,g}(B(s,\rho))}{\mu(B(s,\rho))}.

Clearly, the linearity of

In the following subsections, whenever we assume that the valuation is rotation equivariant we will be working with the specific map

Lemma 6.3
.

For every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}

, for every

Proof.

Let

\phi\in\operatorname{O}(n)

\begin{split}\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda,s),g\rangle&=\lim_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\frac{\nu_{\lambda,g}(B(s,\rho))}{\mu(B(s,\rho))}\\
&=\lim_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\frac{\nu_{\lambda,\phi g}(B(\phi(s),\rho))}{\mu(B(\phi(s),\rho))}=\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda,\phi(s)),\phi\,g\rangle.\end{split}

Now, for
B\in\Sigma_{n}
, by the regularity of the measures
U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)
and
U^{\prime}(\lambda,\phi(s))
, given
\varepsilon>0
, there exist compact sets
K_{1}\subset B
and
K_{2}\subset\phi B
such that

U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)(B)\leq U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)(K_{1})+\varepsilon,\quad\quad U^{\prime}(\lambda,\phi(s))(\phi B)\leq U^{\prime}(\lambda,\phi(s))(K_{2})+\varepsilon.

Let
K=K_{1}\cup\phi^{-1}(K_{2})
, and take a decreasing sequence
(f_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
such that
\|f_{j}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq 1
,
f_{j}(t)=1
for
t\in K
and
f_{j}(t)=0
for
\mbox{dist}(t,K)\geq 1/j
. By the Monotone Convergence Theorem, it follows that

U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)(K)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda,s),f_{j}\rangle,\hskip 9.95845ptU^{\prime}(\lambda,\phi(s))(\phi K)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda,\phi(s)),\phi f_{j}\rangle.

Hence, using (
27
),

\displaystyle U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)(K_{1})

\displaystyle\leq U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)(K)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda,s),f_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda,\phi(s)),\phi f_{j}\rangle=U^{\prime}(\lambda,\phi(s))(\phi K).

Thus, we have
U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)(B)\leq U^{\prime}(\lambda,\phi(s))(\phi B)+\varepsilon
.
Similarly, we get

U^{\prime}(\lambda,\phi(s))(\phi B)\leq U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)(B)+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, this shows that
U^{\prime}
is rotation equivariant.
∎

We will make use of these properties to provide the following natural decomposition of
U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)
: Given
e\in S^{n-1}
, we can decompose
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{R}e\times e^{\perp}
, where
e^{\perp}
denotes the hyperplane orthogonal to
e
. This decomposition induces a bijection
\vartheta_{e}\colon{S^{n-1}}\to(-1,1)\times S^{n-2}\cup\{(1,0,\ldots,0)\}\cup\{(-1,0,\ldots,0)\}
, defined by

(28)

(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\mapsto\begin{cases}\Big(s_{1},\displaystyle\frac{s_{2}}{\sqrt{1-s_{1}^{2}}},\ldots,\frac{s_{n}}{\sqrt{1-s_{1}^{2}}}\Big)&\text{ for }s_{1}\neq\pm 1\\
(\pm 1,0,\ldots,0)&\text{ for }s_{1}=\pm 1,\end{cases}

where
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
are the coordinates of the point
s\in{S^{n-1}}
with respect to an orthonormal basis
\{e,e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
. The map
\vartheta_{e}
clearly defines a homeomorphism between
{S^{n-1}}\backslash\{\pm e\}
and the cylinder
(-1,1)\times{S^{n-1}}
.

In the following, we denote the normalized Hausdorff measures with respect to the sphere as
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{k}
, that is, for any
0\leq k\leq n
(with
k
integer), we set

\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{k}(A)=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{k}(A)}{\mathcal{H}^{k}(S^{k})}

for any Borel set
A\subset S^{k}
.

Lemma 6.4
.

For every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, there exists a measure
\sigma_{\lambda}
on the Borel sets in
(-1,1)
such that for every
e\in{S^{n-1}}

U^{\prime}(\lambda,e)\circ\vartheta^{-1}_{e}=\sigma_{\lambda}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{n-2}

on
(-1,1)\times S^{n-2}
with
\vartheta_{e}
defined by (
28
).

Proof.

Let
C\times D
be a rectangle in
(-1,1)\times S^{n-2}
. Lemma
6.3
implies that

(U^{\prime}(\lambda,e)\circ\vartheta_{e}^{-1})(C\times D))=(U^{\prime}(\lambda,e)\circ\vartheta_{e}^{-1})(C\times\tilde{\phi}\,D)

for every
\tilde{\phi}\in\operatorname{O}(n-1)
.
Therefore, for every Borel subset
C\subset(-1,1)
, the measure on
S^{n-2}
defined by
D\mapsto(U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)\circ\vartheta_{e}^{-1})(C\times D)
is a constant multiple of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{n-2}
, say,
\sigma_{\lambda}(C)\,\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{n-2}
. It is now easy to see that the map
C\mapsto\sigma_{\lambda}(C)
defines a Radon measure on
(-1,1)
.

Finally, to prove that this measure is independent of the point, let
e
and
e^{\prime}
be distinct points of
S^{n-1}
. Choose
\psi\in\operatorname{O}(n)
so that
\psi(e)=e^{\prime}
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and a Borel set
A\subset(-1,1)
, it follows from Lemma
6.3
that

(29)

\displaystyle U^{\prime}(\lambda,e)(\vartheta^{-1}_{e}(A\times S^{n-2}))

\displaystyle=U^{\prime}(\lambda,\psi(e))(\psi\vartheta^{-1}_{e}(A\times S^{n-2})))

(30)

\displaystyle=U^{\prime}(\lambda,e^{\prime})(\vartheta^{-1}_{e^{\prime}}(A\times S^{n-2})).

∎

This lemma shows that, in the rotation equivariant case, the push-forward of
U^{\prime}(\lambda,e)\in M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
by
\vartheta_{e}
is an element of
M((-1,1)\times S^{n-2}\cup\{e\}\cup\{-e\})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
of the form

(31)

\sigma_{\lambda}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{n-2}+a_{\lambda}\,\delta_{e}+b_{\lambda}\delta_{-e}

with
a_{\lambda},b_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. In particular, for every
g\in C({S^{n-1}})

(32)

\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda,s),g\rangle=\int_{(-1,1)}\int_{S^{n-2}}g(\vartheta_{s}^{-1}(\alpha,t))\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{n-2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\lambda}(\alpha)+a_{\lambda}g(s)+b_{\lambda}g(-s).

Although this representation can be useful, one cannot ensure continuity in
\lambda
of the individual terms. For instance,
a_{\lambda}
need not be continuous in
\lambda
. Moreover, it is not clear which conditions on
\sigma_{\lambda}
,
a_{\lambda}
and
b_{\lambda}
should be imposed in order for them to define a weak
∗
valuation. To obtain a complete characterization, we consider the three terms together as a measure on the interval
[-1,1]
. To this end, for each
s\in{S^{n-1}}
, we introduce the following operator from
C({S^{n-1}})
to
C([-1,1])
:

\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s,\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g(s)&\text{if }\alpha=1,\\
g(-s)&\text{if }\alpha=-1,\\
\displaystyle\int_{S^{n-2}}g(\vartheta_{s}^{-1}(\alpha,t))\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{n-2}(t)&\text{if }\alpha\in(-1,1).\\
\end{array}\right.

Clearly, the previous mapping is well-defined (the image is a continuous function), is linear, continuous and surjective. Note that it is a normalized Radon transform.

Theorem 6.5
.

The map
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
is a weak
∗
continuous and rotation equivariant valuation with
\operatorname{V}(0)=0
if and only if there exists a weak
∗
continuous map
\tau\colon\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M([-1,1])_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
with
\tau_{0}=0
such that

\langle V(f),g\rangle=\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{[-1,1]}\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s,\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{f(s)}(\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(s)

for any
f\in C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
and
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
.

Proof.

First, we will define
\tau_{\lambda}
by duality. For any
\xi\in C([-1,1])
we define

\tau_{\lambda}(\xi)=\int_{(-1,1)}\xi(\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\lambda}(\alpha)+a_{\lambda}\xi(1)+b_{\lambda}\xi(-1)

using (
31
).
Clearly,
\tau_{\lambda}
is linear and bounded and, therefore,
\tau_{\lambda}
is a measure on
[-1,1]
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\langle\tau_{\lambda},\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s,\cdot)\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{(-1,1)}\left(\int_{S^{n-2}}g(\vartheta_{s}^{-1}(\alpha,t))\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{n-2}(t)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\lambda}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+a_{\lambda}g(s)+b_{\lambda}g(-s)

\displaystyle=\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda,s),g\rangle.

Now, we have to check that
\tau_{\lambda}
is weak
∗
continuous but this is a consequence of two facts, the surjectivity of
g\mapsto\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s,\cdot)
and the weak
∗
continuity of the mapping
\lambda\mapsto U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)
. Indeed, let
\xi\in C([-1,1])
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
and
\{\lambda_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
a sequence that converges to
\lambda
. Since
g\mapsto\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s,\cdot)
is a surjective mapping, there exists
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
such that
\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s,\cdot)=\xi
and, thus,

\langle\tau_{\lambda_{m}},\xi\rangle=\langle\tau_{\lambda_{m}},\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s,\cdot)\rangle=\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda_{m},s),g\rangle\to\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda,s),g\rangle=\langle\tau_{\lambda},\xi\rangle

as
m\to\infty
.

Conversely, let
\tau_{\lambda}\in M([-1,1])_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
and any
s\in{S^{n-1}}
, define the mapping

U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)(g)=\int_{[-1,1]}\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s,\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{\lambda}(\alpha)=\langle\tau_{\lambda},\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s,\cdot)\rangle.

Clearly, this mapping is linear and continuous; therefore, it defines a measure. Observe that that
\|U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)\|\leq\|\tau_{\lambda}\|
; hence, it is uniformly controlled. We now verify that it is
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-strong Carathéodory. First, it follows easily that the mapping

\lambda\mapsto\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda,s),g\rangle

is continuous for any
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
and any
s\in{S^{n-1}}
. For any
\alpha\in(-1,1)
and
e\in{S^{n-1}}
, define

C_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(\{s\in{S^{n-1}}:\langle s,e\rangle=\alpha\})}.

Fix
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. Let
s\in{S^{n-1}}
and let
\{s_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{S^{n-1}}
be a sequence that converges to
s
. Then

\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s_{m},\pm 1)=g(\pm s_{m})

and, for
\alpha\in(-1,1)
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s_{m},\alpha)

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{n-2}}g(\vartheta^{-1}_{s_{m}}(\alpha,t))\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{n-2}(t)

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}\int_{\{r\in{S^{n-1}}:\langle r,s_{m}\rangle=\alpha\}}g(r)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(r).

Since
g
is uniformly continuous, it follows that
\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s_{m},\cdot)
converges uniformly to
\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s,\cdot)
. Hence, the mapping

s\mapsto\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda,s),g\rangle

is continuous and, in particular, it is Borel measurable. Thus, by Theorem
5.4
, the mapping
f\mapsto\operatorname{V}(f)
, given by

\displaystyle\langle\operatorname{V}(f),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{n-1}}\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda,s),g\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{{S^{n-1}}}\int_{[-1,1]}\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s,\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{\lambda}(\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(s),

is a weak
∗
continuous valuation.

Finally, let us check that
\operatorname{V}
is rotation equivariant. Note that it is enough to see that
\operatorname{\overline{R}}(\phi g)(\phi s,\cdot)=\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s,\cdot)
for any
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
and any
\phi\in\operatorname{O}(n)
. This follows easily since

\operatorname{\overline{R}}(\phi g)(\phi s,\pm 1)=\phi g(\phi(\pm s))=g(\pm s),

and for any
\alpha\in(-1,1)
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\overline{R}}(\phi g)(\phi s,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\int_{{S^{n-1}}}\phi g(\vartheta_{\phi(s)}^{-1}(\alpha,t))\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{n-2}(t)

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}\int_{\{r\in{S^{n-1}}:\langle r,\phi(s)\rangle=\alpha\}}\phi g(r)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(r)

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}\int_{\{r\in{S^{n-1}}:\langle s,r\rangle=\alpha\}}g(r)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(r)=\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(s,\alpha).

Therefore, the valuation
\operatorname{V}
is rotation equivariant.
∎

Theorem
1.2
follows immediately from this result.

6.2.
Homogeneous valuations

We derive next the following corollary to Theorem
5.4
. Let
q\in\mathbb{R}
.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
\,\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
be a weak
{}^{\,*}
​ continuous and
q
-homogeneous
valuation. The map
U\colon\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\times{S^{n-1}}\!\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
from Theorem
5.4
can be chosen so that

U(\lambda,s)=\lambda^{q}\,U(1,s)

for every
\lambda>0
and
s\in{S^{n-1}}
. Conversely, if
\,U(\lambda,s)=\lambda^{q}\,U(1,s)
for every
\lambda>0
and
s\in{S^{n-1}}
, then the associated valuation is
q
-homogeneous.

Proof.

First, if
q=0
, then weak
∗
continuity implies that
\operatorname{V}(f)=\operatorname{V}(0)
for every
f\in C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. It is easy to see that the map
U(\lambda,s)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({S^{n-1}})}\operatorname{V}(0)
represents the valuation with a similar integral representation as in Theorem
5.4
, even if
U(0,s)
may not be 0
\mu
-almost everywhere. It is also clear that this map satisfies
U(\lambda,s)=\lambda^{q}\,U(1,s)

If
q\neq 0
, then
q
-homogeneity implies that
\operatorname{V}(0)=\lambda^{q}\operatorname{V}(0)
for every
\lambda>0
, and therefore
\operatorname{V}(0)=0
if
q\neq 0
. Thus, we can apply Theorem
5.4
, and there exists a map
U
as in the statement. Let us consider the extension
\overline{\operatorname{V}}
of
\operatorname{V}
as defined in Corollary
5.5
and let us check that
\overline{\operatorname{V}}
is
q
-homogeneous. To this end, let
C\subset{S^{n-1}}
be closed and
\lambda>0
. For
\tau>0
, let
f_{\tau}\in C({S^{n-1}})
be such that
f_{\tau}(s)=1
for every
s\in C
,
\|f_{\tau}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\leq 1
and
f_{\tau}(s)=0
whenever
\mbox{dist}(s,C)\geq\tau
. By Corollary
5.5
, we have

\langle\overline{\operatorname{V}}(\lambda\chi_{C}),g\rangle=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\langle\operatorname{V}(\lambda f_{\tau}),g\rangle=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\lambda^{q}\langle\operatorname{V}(f_{\tau}),g\rangle=\langle\lambda^{q}\,\overline{\operatorname{V}}(\chi_{C}),g\rangle

for every
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
and
\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{0}]
. Hence,
\overline{\operatorname{V}}(\lambda\chi_{C})=\lambda^{q}\overline{\operatorname{V}}(\chi_{C})
.

Now, we claim that the class of subsets
A
of
{S^{n-1}}
for which
\overline{\operatorname{V}}(\lambda\chi_{A})=\lambda^{q}\overline{\operatorname{V}}(\chi_{A})
for every
\lambda\geq 0
is a
\sigma
-algebra. Indeed, using the valuation property of
\overline{\operatorname{V}}
, we see that this class is closed under finite union and taking complementary sets.
It remains to show that it is closed under countable unions. Let
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a disjoint sequence of subsets in this class. For
A=\bigcup_{i}A_{i}
, the
sequence
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{A_{i}}
converges almost everywhere to
\chi_{A}
. By Corollary
5.5
, we have

(33)

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\operatorname{V}}(\lambda\chi_{A}),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\langle\overline{\operatorname{V}}(\lambda\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{A_{i}}),g\rangle

(34)

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\lambda^{q}\langle\overline{\operatorname{V}}(\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{A_{i}}),g\rangle=\langle\lambda^{q}\overline{\operatorname{V}}(\chi_{A}),g\rangle

for every
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
.

We consider the map
U\colon\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\times{S^{n-1}}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
associated to
\operatorname{V}
.
For
s\in{S^{n-1}}
and
\lambda\geq 0
, we define the map

U^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\times{S^{n-1}}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\tilde{U}(\lambda,s)=\lambda^{q}U(1,s).

It is clear that
\tilde{U}
is a
\mu
-strong Carathéodory and uniformly controlled map. Now, for every
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
and every
A\in\Sigma_{n}
one gets that

\int_{A}\!\langle U(\lambda,s),g\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\langle\overline{\operatorname{V}}(\lambda\chi_{A}),g\rangle=\langle\lambda^{q}\overline{\operatorname{V}}(\chi_{A}),g\rangle=\lambda^{q}\int_{A}\!\langle U(1,s),g\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s).

This implies that for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, there exists a set
S^{\prime}
of
\mu
-full measure where
U(\lambda,s)=\tilde{U}(\lambda,s)
for every
s\in S^{\prime}
. By Corollary
5.6
,
U
and
\tilde{U}
define the same valuation. The converse is clear.
∎

6.3.
Locally determined valuations and dual area measures

We call a valuation
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}

locally determined
if

(35)

f_{1}\wedge\chi_{B}=f_{2}\wedge\chi_{B}\,\text{ implies }\,\operatorname{V}(f_{1})(B)=\operatorname{V}(f_{2})(B)

for every open set
B\subset{S^{n-1}}
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. In fact, an argument similar to
[
25
]
shows that a locally determined map that is weak
∗
continuous is actually a valuation in this context.

Note in particular that locally determined maps satisfy the following

(36)

f\wedge\chi_{B}=0\,\text{ implies }\,\operatorname{V}(f)(B)=V(0)(B)=0

for every open set
B\subset{S^{n-1}}
and
f\in C({S^{n-1}})
. We will use (
36
) in the proof of the following lemma.

Lemma 6.7
.

The map
\,\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
is a weak
∗
continuous, locally determined, rotation equivariant valuation with
\operatorname{V}(0)=0
if and only if there exists a continuous function
\theta\colon\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
with
\theta(0)=0
such that for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
s\in{S^{n-1}}

U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)=\theta(\lambda)\,\delta_{s}.

Proof.

Let
s\in{S^{n-1}}
and
\lambda\geq 0
. Suppose
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
is a function such that
s\not\in\mbox{supp}(g)
, the support of
g
. Since
\mbox{supp}(g)
is a closed set,

B(s,\rho)\cap\mbox{supp}(g)=\emptyset

for every
0<\rho<\mbox{dist}(s,\mbox{supp}(g))
.
In particular,
\langle\operatorname{V}(\lambda f),g\rangle=0
for every
f\prec B(s,\rho)
.
Therefore, we can consider the map
U^{\prime}
constructed in Section
6.1
, and we have

U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)(g)=\lim_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\frac{\langle\operatorname{V}(\lambda B(s,\rho)),g\rangle}{\mu(B(s,\rho))}=0.

Since this holds for every
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
with
s\not\in\mbox{supp}(g)
, it follows that
U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)=u(\lambda,s)\,\delta_{s}
for some
u(\lambda,s)\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
.

Let now
s^{\prime}\not=s
be another point in
{S^{n-1}}
. Choose
\phi\in\operatorname{O}(n)
such that
\phi(s)=s^{\prime}
.
By Lemma
6.3
, we have

u(\lambda,s)=U^{\prime}(\lambda,s)(\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}})=U^{\prime}(\lambda,s^{\prime})(\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}})=u(\lambda,s^{\prime}).

Therefore,
u(\lambda,s)
does not depend on
s
, and we can rename it as
\theta(\lambda)
.

To see that
\theta\colon\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
is continuous, just note that

\langle\operatorname{V}(\lambda\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}),\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle=\int_{{S^{n-1}}}\langle U^{\prime}(\lambda,s),\operatorname{\mathbbm{1}}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\int_{{S^{n-1}}}\theta(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\mu({S^{n-1}})\theta(\lambda).

Hence, the continuity of
\theta
follows from the weak
∗
continuity of
\operatorname{V}
and from
\theta(0)=0
.

The converse statement is immediate.
∎

The following classification result is an immediate consequence of Theorem
6.5
and Lemma
6.7
.

Theorem 6.8
.

A map
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
is a weak
∗
​ continuous, locally determined, rotation equivariant valuation with
\operatorname{V}(0)=0
if and only if there is a continuous function
\theta\colon\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
with

\langle\operatorname{V}(f),g\rangle=\int_{S^{n-1}}\theta(f(s))g(s)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(s)

and

.

Requiring that the valuation is, in addition,
q
-homogeneous with

, by Corollary
6.6
and the above we obtain the following classification.

Theorem 6.9
.

A map
\operatorname{V}\colon C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
is a weak
∗
​ continuous, locally determined, rotation equivariant, and

such that

\langle\operatorname{V}(f),g\rangle=c\,\int_{S^{n-1}}f^{q}(s)g(s)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(s)

for every

and

.

Finally, we return to valuations on star bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We call a valuation
\operatorname{Z}\colon\mathcal{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}^{n}\to M({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}

locally determined
if the valuation
f\mapsto\operatorname{Z}(L_{f})
, as defined in (
3
), is locally determined on
C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
.

For
q\in\mathbb{R}
, Huang, Lutwak, Yang, and Zhang
[
19
]
introduced the
q
-th dual area measures of a convex body

with the origin in the interior as

(37)

\tilde{S}_{q}(K)(B)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{B}\rho_{K}^{q}(s)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(s)

for Borel sets

. Note that the map

is a valuation defined on convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the origin in their interiors and taking values in

. Also, note that this valuation can be extended to all star bodies using the same definition as in (

Theorem

6.4.
A final remark on weak
∗
continuity versus norm continuity

Our purpose here is to exhibit a simple example of a weak
∗
continuous, rotation equivariant valuation that is not norm continuous, i.e. it is not continuous when
M({S^{n-1}})
is endowed with the norm topology. Take
\tau_{\lambda}=\min\{\lambda,1\}\,\delta_{\cos\lambda}
with
\delta_{\lambda}
the Dirac measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Clearly,
\tau_{0}=0
. Let us check that
\lambda\mapsto\tau_{\lambda}
is weak
∗
continuous. For every
\xi\in C([-1,1])
, we have

\langle\tau_{\lambda},\xi\rangle=\min\{\lambda,1\}\xi(\cos\lambda)

which is continuous in
\lambda
, since both
\xi
and
\lambda\mapsto\min\{\lambda,1\}
are continuous. Therefore, by Theorem
6.5
, the measure-valued function
\tau
defines a weak
∗
continuous, rotation equivariant valuation
\operatorname{V}
. Let us show that it is not norm continuous when
n=2
. We will identify
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the complex plane and the unit sphere
S^{1}
with the interval
[0,2\pi]
, that is, any angle
\beta\in[0,2\pi]
is identified with
e^{i\beta}\in S^{1}
. In this case note that for
g\in C(S^{1})
,

\operatorname{\overline{R}}g(e^{is},\cos\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\left(g(e^{i(s+\lambda)})+g(e^{i(s-\lambda)})\right).

Now consider the function

f(e^{is})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-s+2\pi&\text{if }s\in[0,\pi],\\
s&\text{if }s\in[\pi,2\pi],\end{array}\right.

and for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
the functions
f_{m}(e^{is})=f(e^{is})+\frac{1}{m}
which are continuous. Clearly
f_{m}
converges uniformly to
f
and we have
f,f_{m}\geq 1
. Thus,

\langle U^{\prime}(f(e^{is}),e^{is}),g\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(g(e^{0})+g(e^{i(2s)})\right)

and

\displaystyle\langle U^{\prime}(f_{m}(e^{is}),e^{is}),g\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}\big(g(e^{i\frac{1}{m}})+g(e^{i\left(2s-\frac{1}{m}\right)})\big)&\text{if }s\in[0,\pi],\\[4.0pt]
\frac{1}{2}\big(g(e^{i(2s+\frac{1}{m})})+g(e^{-i\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)})\big)&\text{if }s\in[\pi,2\pi],\end{array}\right.

where we used the fact that
g(e^{i(\beta+2\pi)})=g(e^{i\beta})
for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for any
g\in C(S^{1})
,

\displaystyle\langle\operatorname{V}(f),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(g(e^{0})+g(e^{i2s})\right)\,\mathrm{d}s=\pi g(e^{0})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(e^{is})\,\mathrm{d}s,

and

\langle\operatorname{V}(f_{m}),g\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(g(e^{i\frac{1}{m}})+g(e^{i\left(2s-\frac{1}{m}\right)})\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\pi}^{2\pi}\left(g(e^{-i\frac{1}{m}})+g(e^{i\left(2s+\frac{1}{m}\right)})\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
=\frac{\pi}{2}\left(g(e^{i\frac{1}{m}})+g(e^{-i\frac{1}{m}})\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(e^{is})\,\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, for any
m
one can take
g_{m}(s)
such that
g_{m}(e^{0})=1
and
g_{m}(e^{\frac{i}{m}})=g(e^{-\frac{i}{m}})=0
and, we have

\langle\operatorname{V}(f)-\operatorname{V}(f_{m}),g_{m}\rangle=\pi.

Thus,
\operatorname{V}
is not norm continuous.

Proposition A.1

\nu_{f,g}(A)=\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{g}(f\chi_{A})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{g}(h_{m})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{i_{m}}\nu_{\lambda_{i,m},g}(A_{i,m})

with

\displaystyle|\nu_{f,g}(A)|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{i_{m}}|\nu_{\lambda_{i,m},g}(A_{i,m})|\leq\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{i_{m}}\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\zeta(\lambda_{i,m})\mu(A_{i,m})

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\zeta(\|f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty})\lim_{m\to\infty}\mu\left(\bigcup_{i}A_{i,m}\right)\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\zeta(\|f\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty})\mu(A),

since
\mu
is non-negative and countably additive. From this bound, we get that
\nu_{f,g}

\mu

Let

\mu

. For every

Moreover, for every

and it follows that

as elements in

Now, we need to check that

and
\varepsilon>0

Now, given
f\in B({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
and
\varepsilon>0

\delta>0
be as before. Suppose

∎

For every

\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\quad\hskip 71.13188pt+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{A_{i}^{-}}K_{0}(\alpha_{i}^{\prime},s)-K_{0}(\alpha_{i},s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)+\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{g}(\alpha_{i}\chi_{A_{i}^{+}})-\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{g}(\alpha_{i}^{\prime}\chi_{A_{i}^{+}})+\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{g}(\alpha_{i}^{\prime}\chi_{A_{i}^{-}})-\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{g}(\alpha_{i}\chi_{A_{i}^{-}})+\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}

Lemma A.3
.

There exists a Borel set
A_{0}\subset{S^{n-1}}

A(\delta,\varepsilon)\\

Lemma A.4
.

For
g\in C({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, there is
K_{g}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\times{S^{n-1}}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\operatorname{V}_{g}(f)=\int_{{S^{n-1}}}K_{g}(f(s),s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

for every
f\in B({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
. Moreover,
K_{g}
satisfies the following conditions.

•

For each
\lambda\geq 0
, the function
K_{g}(\lambda,\cdot)
coincides with the Radon–Nikodym derivative with respect to
\mu
of the measure
\nu_{\lambda,g}
associated to the scalar-valued valuation
\operatorname{V}_{g}
according to (
8
), and in particular, we have
K_{g}(\lambda,\cdot)\in L_{1}({S^{n-1}},\mu)
.

•

The function
K_{g}(\cdot,s)
is continuous for every
s\in{S^{n-1}}
.

Proof.

Let
K_{0}(\lambda,s)
be defined as in (
38
) and recall that
0\leq K_{0}(\lambda,s)\leq\zeta(\lambda)\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
and any
s\in{S^{n-1}}
. By Lemma
A.3
, we can define

K_{g}(\lambda,s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\lim_{m}K_{0}(\lambda_{m},s)&\text{if }s\not\in A_{0},\text{ and }\lambda_{m}\to\lambda\text{ with }\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{Q}\\
0&\text{if }s\in A_{0}\end{array}\right.

Clearly, for every fixed
s\in{S^{n-1}}
the mapping
\lambda\mapsto K_{g}(\lambda,s)
is continuous and for every
\lambda\geq 0
. If
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, then the mapping
s\mapsto K_{g}(\lambda,s)=K_{0}(\lambda,s)\chi_{A_{0}^{c}}(s)
is Borel, since
s\mapsto K_{0}(\lambda,s)
is Borel and
s\mapsto\chi_{A_{0}^{c}}
is also. If
\lambda\not\in\mathbb{Q}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, the mapping
s\mapsto\lim_{m}K_{0}(\lambda_{m},s)
is Borel, since it is the limit of Borel mappings and, thus,
s\mapsto K_{g}(\lambda,s)
is also Borel.

We check that
s\mapsto K_{g}(\lambda,s)
is the Radon–Nikodym derivative with respect to
\mu
of the measure
\nu_{\lambda,g}
. If
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
,
K_{0}(\lambda,s)=\frac{d\nu_{\lambda,g}}{d\mu}
and since
\mu(A_{0})=0
one has that
K_{0}(\lambda,\cdot)=K_{g}(\lambda,\cdot)
as elements of
L^{1}(\mu)
.

If
\lambda\not\in\mathbb{Q}_{n}
, take
\{\lambda_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
such that
\lambda_{m}\to\lambda
. Since
\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{g}
is continuous,

\nu_{\lambda,g}(A)=\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{g}(\lambda\chi_{A})=\lim_{m}\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{g}(\lambda_{m}\chi_{A})=\lim_{m}\int_{A}K_{g}(\lambda_{m},s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s).

Now,
K_{g}(\lambda_{m},s)\leq K_{0}(\lambda_{m},s)\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\zeta(\lambda_{m})
. As
\zeta
is bounded on bounded sets, the Dominated Convergence Theorem yields

\nu_{\lambda,g}(A)=\int_{A}K_{g}(\lambda,s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s).

Since this holds for every
A\in\Sigma_{n}
,
K_{g}(\lambda,\cdot)
coincides with
\frac{d\nu_{\lambda,g}}{d\mu}
as elements in
L^{1}(\mu)
.

As a final step, we prove the representation. In Proposition
A.1
, we checked that
\Phi_{g}(f\chi_{A})=\Phi_{g}(f)\chi_{A}
, that is,

\int_{S^{n-1}}K_{0}(\lambda\chi_{A}(s),s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\int_{A}K_{0}(\lambda,s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\nu_{\lambda,g}(A)=\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{g}(\lambda\chi_{A}).

Hence,

\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{g}(\lambda\chi_{A})=\int_{S^{n-1}}K_{g}(\lambda\chi_{A}(s),s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

holds for indicator functions with rational coefficients. It is easy to check that it can be extended to simple functions, and these are in turn dense in the set of bounded Borel functions.

Now, to extend the representation for every bounded Borel function we will show that the mapping on the right side of the equation, that is,

f\mapsto\int_{S^{n-1}}K_{g}(f(s),s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s),

is a continuous map. Therefore, since it coincides with
\overline{\operatorname{V}}_{g}
on a dense set, they will coincide on the bounded Borel functions. Let
f\in B({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
and
\{f_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B({S^{n-1}})_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
such that
\|f-f_{m}\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. In particular,
f_{m}(s)\to f(s)
as
m\to\infty
for every
s\in{S^{n-1}}
, which implies that
K_{g}(f_{m}(s),s)\to K_{g}(f(s),s)
as
m\to\infty
. For every
\lambda\geq 0
and every
s\in{S^{n-1}}
we have

K_{g}(\lambda,s)\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\zeta(\lambda).

Hence, taking
\lambda=\sup_{m}\|f_{m}\|
and using the fact that
\zeta
is bounded on bounded sets we get

K_{g}(f_{m}(s),s)\leq\|g\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\sup_{\alpha\leq\lambda}\zeta(\alpha).

Applying the Dominated Convergence Theorem it follows that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{S^{n-1}}K_{g}(f_{m}(s),s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\int_{S^{n-1}}K_{g}(f(s),s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s),

which concludes the proof.
∎

Finally, note that if
g\in C({S^{n-1}})
, then

\operatorname{V}_{g}=\operatorname{V}_{g^{+}}-\operatorname{V}_{g^{-}}.

Therefore, the function defined as

K_{g}(\lambda,s)=K_{g^{+}}(\lambda,s)-K_{g^{-}}(\lambda,s)

will satisfy Lemma
4.2
.

Remark A.5
.

The previous result can also be obtained by an application of the Radon–Nikodym theorem: if a scalar valuation
\operatorname{W}
admits an integral representation with respect to a measure
\sigma
, then for every measure
\eta
such that
\sigma
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\eta
, the valuation
\operatorname{W}
admits a similar integral representation with respect to
\eta
. Finally, one can check that the control measure defined in
[
39
]
is absolutely continuous with respect to the control measure
\mu
defined in Section
4
, which yields the result.
