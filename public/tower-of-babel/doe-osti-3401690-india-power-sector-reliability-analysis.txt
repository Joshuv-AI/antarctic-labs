Study Background

 Scope of Study
Objective: Assess pathways for India to achieve its
2070 power sector goals reliably and cost-effectively
by considering various supply- and demand-side
factors to support planning by national stakeholders,
including the Central Electricity Authority (CEA)
Temporal Resolution:
Annual (2025 – 2070)

Spatial Resolution:
All of India (divided into 36 states & union territories)

Modeling Methodology:
Capacity Expansion Model (ReEDS-India)

Power Sector Components:
Generation, Storage, Transmission, and Demand

Regional Energy Deployment System (ReEDS) India Model
 Figure. ReEDS-India Modeling Regions

ReEDS-India is an open-access tool for mid-
 and long-term capacity expansion modeling
that finds the mix of generation, transmission,
 and storage technologies that meet the
anticipated requirements of the electric sector
 at least cost.

 The objective function is to minimize total system
 cost (including capacity, operations and
maintenance, fuel, operating reserves, substations,
 and transmission).

 The ReEDS-India model contains multiple years of weather data (2014-2024) that can be
 used to create and stress-test different generation, transmission, and storage portfolios,
 thus supporting grid reliability solutions under a range of weather conditions.

 Key Takeaways: Coal and Natural Gas
Role of Coal and Natural Gas on the Supply-Side Figure. 2070 Installed Capacity (MW) of Natural Gas (combined
 Cycle) for India by Region in Demand Scenario for Weather Year
 Coal capacity consists primarily of supercritical coal plants and natural 2015
  gas capacity consists primarily of combined cycle plants.
 In the scenarios with a larger build-out of natural gas capacity (Base
  Case and Demand), its generation is concentrated during the Spring
  and Autumn seasons and during time periods of lower solar and wind
  supply.
 Firm capacity shifts from being primarily provided by coal to being
  primarily provided by combined cycle natural gas across all seasons in
  the Demand scenario.

Synthesized Stakeholder Feedback
 The fuel price of natural gas is a key sensitivity in the model and
  impacts the build out of generation infrastructure.
 Domestic natural gas resources, import facilities, and transportation
  infrastructure are limited.
