Clinical outcomes of a 3-day modified BEP regimen in malignant ovarian germ cell tumors in a single-center real-world study
Authors: Xiuli Zhang, Yedan Ren, Yun Liang, Ling Wang, Yujia Wang, Yaxia Chen, Junfen Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMalignant ovarian germ cell tumors (MOGCTs) mainly affect adolescents and young women and are highly curable with bleomycin, etoposide, and cisplatin (BEP). To reduce toxicity associated with the standard 5-day regimen, we evaluated the efficacy, safety, and fertility outcomes of a 3-day modified BEP regimen in patients with MOGCTs.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective analysis of 81 MOGCT patients with 3-day modified BEP at the Women’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine from 2004 to 2024. The 3-day modified BEP regimen comprised bleomycin (15 U) and etoposide (100 mg/m2) on days 1–3, and cisplatin (75 mg/m2) on day 1, repeated every 4 weeks for 3–6 cycles.ResultsAll 81 MOGCT patients included in our study underwent primary surgery before chemotherapy; 86.4% had early-stage disease, and fertility-sparing surgery was performed in 73 cases. After a median follow-up of over 9 years, the 5-year progression-free survival rate was 97.5% (95% CI: 94.2–100), with no treatment-related deaths. Bleomycin-induced pneumonitis occurred in 4.9% and resolved with supportive care. Among 30 patients attempting conception, 90% achieved pregnancy, resulting in 25 term live births.ConclusionsThe 3-day modified BEP regimen provides excellent survival with reduced toxicity, offering greater convenience and preserved fertility, supporting its use as a safer and more practical treatment alternative for initial chemotherapy in MOGCTs.

Introduction

Malignant ovarian germ cell tumors (MOGCTs) are rare but represent the leading gynecologic malignancy among women under 25 years of age, accounting for up to 80% of malignant ovarian tumors in preadolescents [1]. Despite their rarity, MOGCTs generally have excellent survival outcomes due to their marked chemosensitivity, even in advanced stages [2]. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) classification, MOGCTs encompass dysgerminoma, embryonal carcinoma, yolk sac tumor, non-gestational choriocarcinoma, immature teratoma and mixed germ cell tumor containing the elements mentioned above [3].

Bleomycin, etoposide, cisplatin (BEP) combination chemotherapy remains the standard first-line regimen recommended by both the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) and the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO). The conventional 5-day BEP protocol - bleomycin 30 U intravenously weekly, etoposide 100 mg/m2, and cisplatin 20 mg/m2 on days 1 to 5 every 21 days-has achieved 5-year survival rates of 82%-94% [4–7]. However, treatment-related toxicity remains a major concern. The 5-day BEP regimen is associated with up to 25% incidence of grade 3–4 neutropenia, approximately 10% febrile neutropenia, and treatment delays in nearly one-third of patients [4, 8–14]. Moreover, bleomycin-induced pneumonitis (BIP) and sepsis during the neutropenic phase can be fatal, contributing to a treatment-related mortality of around 5%. The need for weekly bleomycin administration also increases hospital visits, costs, and logistical burdens-particularly for patients living in remote areas-potentially reducing adherence and quality of life.

Given these limitations, modifications to the standard BEP regimen are warranted to minimize toxicity and improve patient convenience without compromising efficacy. Since the early 2000s, our center has implemented a 3-day modified BEP regimen with reduced cumulative doses of bleomycin, etoposide, and cisplatin. This approach aims to maintain the high curative potential of BEP while reducing adverse events and preserving fertility. The present retrospective study evaluates the clinical efficacy, toxicity profile, and fertility outcomes of this 3-day modified BEP regimen in patients with MOGCTs, providing real-world evidence for a potentially safer and more practical treatment alternative.

Materials and methods

Data collection

This retrospective study included patients with MOGCTs treated and followed at the Women’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine, between January 2004 and December 2024. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the ethics committee of Women’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine (IRB-20250257-R). Clinical data were extracted from electronic medical records, including age, tumor characteristics (histology, stage, tumor markers, location, and residual disease), surgery procedures, chemotherapy-related adverse events, treatment response, and fertility outcomes.

Eligible histology types included dysgerminoma, yolk sac tumor, embryonal carcinoma, non-gestational choriocarcinoma, immature teratoma, and mixed germ cell tumors. Inclusion criteria were patients who underwent surgery followed by a 3-day modified BEP regimen. Exclusion criteria were: (i) chemotherapy other than the 3-day modified BEP; (ii) incomplete medical records; (iii) concomitant malignancies; (iv) non-gynecologic primary germ cell tumors, and (v) stage IA immature teratoma grade 1 or stage IA/IB dysgerminoma. Of 136 patients initially identified, 81 met the inclusion criteria for final analysis.

Treatment

Primary surgery included fertility-sparing surgery (FSS) or radical surgery. FSS preserved the uterus and at least one ovary, while radical surgery involved removal of the uterus and both ovaries. Comprehensive staging including peritoneal washings, omentectomy or biopsy, peritoneal biopsies, and optional retroperitoneal lymph node assessment.

Chemotherapy was initiated a median of 8 days postoperatively (range, 4–29). The 3-day modified BEP regimen comprised bleomycin (15 U) and etoposide (100 mg/m2) on days 1–3, and cisplatin (75 mg/m2) on day 1, repeated every 4 weeks for 3–6 cycles [15]. Bleomycin (10 U) was administered to a nine-year-old girl.

Follow-up

Patients were followed every 3 months for 2 years, every 6 months for the next 3 years, and annually thereafter. Follow-up included clinical evaluation, physical examination, and serum HCG and AFP testing. Image (CT or MRI) was performed when recurrence was suspected.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and compared using the chi-squared or Fisher’s exact test. Continuous variables were presented as medians and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the interval from surgery to disease progression or death from any cause. Patients without events were censored at the last follow-up. Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 31, with p-values < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient demographics and clinical characteristics

Between January 2005 and December 2024, 81 patients with MOGCTs met the inclusion criteria (Fig. 1). The median age at diagnosis was 22 years (range, 9–34). Most patients (83%, 67/81) presented with FIGO stage I disease; only three had stage IV disease (one pulmonary and two hepatic metastases). Two patients had recurrent immature teratomas. FSS was performed in 90% (73/81) of cases.

Response and progression

After a median follow-up of 9.8 years, the 5-year PFS was 97.5% (95% CI, 94.2–100.0; Fig. 2A). Patients with early-stage disease (FIGO I–II) demonstrated significantly better 5-year PFS (100%) compared with advanced-stage disease (FIGO III–IV, 81.8%; 95% CI, 59.1–100.0; P = 0.006; Fig. 2B). The overall 5-year disease-free survival (DFS) was 95.1% (Fig. 2C). Among patients with non-dysgerminomatous tumors (FIGO I – III), the 5-year DFS was 96% (95% CI, 90.6–100; Fig. 2D). There were 2 patients progressed and one patient relapsed (Table 2).

Toxicity of the 3-day modified BEP regimen

No treatment-related deaths occurred. The regimen was well tolerated, with no secondary leukemia or myelodysplasia observed. Alopecia was common but reversible. Nausea and vomiting were reported in 96% (78/81) of patients, severe in five. Grade 4 neutropenia (granulocyte counts < 0.5 * 109/L) occurred in 17.3% (14/81) and resolved with granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF). Febrile neutropenia occurred in three cases and responded to antibiotics. Adverse events are compared with those from the standard 5-day BEP regimen [16] in Table 3 .

Fertility outcomes

Among 73 underwent FSS, menstrual recovery occurred in 97% (71/73), except for one case of premature ovarian failure and one premenarchal patient. Of the 30 women who attempted conception, 27 (90%) achieved 40 pregnancies, resulting in 26 term live births among 25 patients, with no congenital anomalies reported (Table 4).

Discussion

Ovarian preservation is a priority in the management of pediatric and adolescent ovarian tumors, particularly in the context of fertility-sparing treatment. Recent evidence has highlighted the value of ultrasonographic identification of normal ovarian tissue surrounding the tumor — the so-called ovarian crescent sign (OCS) — as a key predictor of successful ovarian-sparing surgery [17]. The authors advocate that the presence of OCS should be routinely reported in preoperative imaging to guide surgical decisions.

The introduction of BEP chemotherapy represented a landmark advance in the management of MOGCTs. However, the standard 5-day BEP regimen is often limited by toxicity, including BIP, severe neutropenia, febrile neutropenia, and cisplatin-related nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity [4, 8, 18–20]. These toxicities may cause treatment delays, hospitalization, and long-term morbidity, underscoring the need for safer, more convenient alternatives without compromising efficacy.

In this real-world retrospective study, we evaluated a 3-day modified BEP regimen with reduced cumulative doses of bleomycin (45 U/cycle), etoposide (300 mg/m²), and cisplatin (75 mg/m²). Despite dose reduction and shortened duration, the regimen achieved excellent disease control, with a 5-year PFS of 97.5% and no treatment-related deaths. Relapses were rare and confined to advanced-stage cases. These findings are comparable with Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) data reporting 5-year cause-specific survival rates of approximately 94% for MOGCTs overall, 97% for dysgerminoma, and 92% for non-dysgerminoma [5]. In our cohort, the 5-year PFS was 100% for dysgerminoma and 90.4% for non-dysgerminoma, reaffirming stage as a key prognostic factor.

Importantly, the 3-day regimen demonstrated a favorable toxicity profile relative to the standard 5-day BEP protocol. Grade 4 neutropenia occurred in 17.3% of patients and resolved promptly with granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF). Febrile neutropenia was uncommon and managed successfully with antibiotics. The incidence of severe nausea and vomiting (6%) was lower than reported with conventional cisplatin dosing, reflecting both the reduced dose and improved antiemetic support. No cases of secondary leukemia or myelodysplastic syndrome were observed. Bleomycin-induced pneumonitis remains a major concern, with fatality rates of 1% – 3% at cumulative doses exceeding 300 U [10–14, 21]. Because BIP is dose-dependent, the reduced total bleomycin exposure in this modified regimen likely contributed to the low incidence observed (4.9%), all resolving with supportive care. The inpatient 3-day schedule also eliminated the need for weekly outpatient bleomycin infusions, thereby reducing hospital visits and potential treatment delays during the neutropenic phase.

Fertility preservation is a critical goal in treating young women with MOGCTs. In this study, FSS was feasible in 90% of patients, with restoration of regular menstruation in 97%. Among those attempting conception, 90% achieved pregnancy, resulting in 26 term live births without congenital anomalies. These results align with prior studies indicating that FSS does not compromise oncologic safety and is associated with excellent reproductive outcomes [22–24]. In one series of 105 patients treated with FSS, 42 of 45 women who desired pregnancy conceived, resulting in 56 live births [23]. Similarly, in a study of yolk sac tumors, 75% of women who attempted conception achieved pregnancy [24]. Together, these findings confirm that fertility preservation combined with modified chemotherapy is both safe and effective in this population.

Non-dysgerminomatous MOGCTs generally carry a higher risk of recurrence compared with dysgerminomas [25]. In a Gynecologic Oncology Group (GOG) trial, standard 5-day BEP achieved a disease-free survival (DFS) rate of 96% [4]. Our modified regimen produced comparable results, with a 5-year PFS of 96% (95% CI, 90.6–100) in patients with FIGO stage I-III non-dysgerminomas, supporting equivalent efficacy with reduced toxicity.

This research has inherent limitations. First, its retrospective and single-center nature may introduce bias and restrict the broader application of the results. Additionally, the lack of a direct comparison with standard 5-day BEP regimen prevents definitive conclusions regarding relative efficacy. Although our sample was derived from a single, high-volume care center, potentially limiting the generalizability of our findings, our strict adherence to national guidelines serves to mitigate this bias. Second, the event rate was very low, with only 3 recurrences (3.7%) among 81 patients. This low event count precluded any reliable multivariate analysis to identify independent prognostic factors, and even the univariate comparisons are underpowered. We will continue follow-up and plan to report overall survival (OS) data in a future study.

Our real-world data confirm that the 3-day modified BEP regimen offers favorable efficacy and manageable toxicity in malignant ovarian germ cell tumors (MOGCTs), supporting it as a practical alternative to the standard 5-day schedule. Chemotherapeutic stress accelerates senescence via neuroendocrine–epigenetic pathways, justifying dose-intensity optimization in young patients [26]. Nanoplatforms enable targeted delivery with reduced toxicity [27], benefiting cisplatin regimens [28]. Machine learning improves biotransformation for safer supportive care [29], and precision nutrition complements tumor metabolism modulation [30].

Beyond chemotherapy, epigenetic regulators (KDM1A) drive tumor progression via Wnt/β-catenin signaling; KDM1A inhibition suppresses growth and induces apoptosis [31]. Similar networks may influence MOGCT outcomes. Natural phytochemicals also show promise: Jerantinine A binds CXCR4/HER2 [32], and Emilia sonchifolia constituents inhibit cancer kinases, warranting further study [33].

In conclusion, the 3-day modified BEP regimen appears to be a safe, effective, and more convenient first-line treatment option for MOGCTs. It achieves excellent survival, minimal severe toxicity, and favorable fertility outcomes. By reducing treatment burden without sacrificing efficacy, this regimen represents a promising alternative to the standard 5-day BEP protocol. Future prospective, multicenter studies are warranted to confirm these findings and further define the optimal balance between efficacy, safety, and fertility preservation in this highly curable malignancy.
