The smallest invariant factor of elliptic curves, and coincidences
Alexander Milner, Jack Shotton

Abstract
. For an elliptic curve ℰ \mathcal{E} over ℚ \mathbb{Q} and a natural number j j , Cojocaru has shown that there is an explicit constant 𝒞 ℰ , j \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},j} giving (under GRH) the density of primes p p of good reduction such that the smallest invariant factor of ℰ ⁡ ( 𝔽 p ) \mathcal{E}(\mathbb{F}_{p}) is j j . For ℰ \mathcal{E} without complex multiplication, we study the question of when 𝒞 ℰ , j \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},j} is positive (a necessary and, on GRH, sufficient condition for there to be infinitely many such p p ), strengthening the main result of [ 14 ] . Our arguments are group-theoretic using the image of the adelic Galois representation of ℰ \mathcal{E} . Experimentally, 𝒞 ℰ , j \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},j} appears to vanish only when there is a coincidence of division fields; we document a number of families of such coincidences arising from abelian division fields.

2.
Galois images

Associated to
\mathcal{E}
, we have the
adelic Tate module

T(\mathcal{E})=\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{E}[n].

It is a free
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of rank 2, and we will fix a choice of basis. We then obtain a continuous representation

\rho:\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to GL_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})

where
\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
is the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}
. Let

H=\mathrm{im}(\rho)\subset GL_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}).

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\rho_{n}:\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})

be the mod-
n
reduction of
\rho
. We introduce the following notation:

•

\bmod_{n}:GL_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})\to GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
is the reduction mod
n
map;

•

\Gamma_{n}=\ker(\bmod_{n})
;

•

for
G\subset GL_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
,
G\bmod n=\bmod_{n}(G)
;

If
G\subset GL_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
is an open subset, the
level
of
G
is the least integer
m
such that
\Gamma_{m}\subset G
.
We note now, and use without comment, that if
A,B\subset GL_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
are subsets that both contain the same
\Gamma_{m}
, then
A\cup B\bmod m=A\bmod m\cup B\bmod m
, and similarly for intersections.

As
\mathcal{E}
does not have complex multiplication,
H
is open by
[
19
, Théorème 3]
, and we define the
adelic level

m_{\mathcal{E}}
of
\mathcal{E}
to be the level of
H
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

[\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[k]):\mathbb{Q}]=|\rho_{k}(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}))|=|H\bmod k|=|H/H\cap\Gamma_{k}|.

As in Theorem
1.1
, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
we define

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},j}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(k)}{[\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[jk]):\mathbb{Q}]}.

The sum is absolutely convergent, as Serre’s open image theorem implies that
[\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[k]):\mathbb{Q}]\asymp\psi(k)\asymp k^{4}
for
k
squarefree.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be any integer divisible by the adelic level
m_{\mathcal{E}}
(usually we will have
m=m_{\mathcal{E}}
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
k\in\mathbb{N}
and that
p
is a prime such that
v_{p}(k)\geq v_{p}(m)
. Then

|H\bmod pk|=|H\bmod k|\cdot\begin{cases}p^{4}&\text{ if $p\mid k$}\\
\psi(p)&\text{if $p\nmid k$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

This is the
Claim
in
[
8
]
Section 7.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
S
be the set of prime numbers such that
v_{p}(j)<v_{p}(m)
. Then:

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},j}=\sum_{k\in\left\langle S\right\rangle}\frac{\mu(k)}{|H\bmod jk|}\prod_{p\not\in S,p\mid j}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{4}}\right)\prod_{p\not\in S,p\nmid j}\left(1-\frac{1}{\psi(p)}\right).

Furthermore,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},j}>0
if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},\gcd(j,m)}>0
.

Proof.

If
n\in\mathbb{N}
is squarefree and of the form
kn^{\prime}
, where all the prime factors of
k
are in
S
and all the prime factors of
n^{\prime}
are not in
S
, then by Lemma
2.1
we have

\frac{\mu(n)}{|H\bmod jn|}=\frac{\mu(k)}{|H\bmod jk|}\prod_{p\mid n^{\prime},p\mid j}\frac{-1}{p^{4}}\prod_{p\mid n^{\prime},p\nmid j}\frac{-1}{\psi(p)}.

The factorisation (
1
) follows.

As
\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{\psi(p)}\right)
is easily shown to be positive, we see that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},j}>0
if and only if

\sum_{k\in\left\langle S\right\rangle}\frac{\mu(k)}{|H\bmod jk|}>0.

If
p
is a prime that divides
\frac{j}{\gcd(j,m)}
then
p\not\in S
and so, for any
k\in\left\langle S\right\rangle
,
v_{p}(jk)=v_{p}(j)>v_{p}(m)
. So, by (repeated application of) Lemma
2.1
,

|H\bmod jk|=A|H\bmod\gcd(j,m)k|

for a positive integer
A
independent of
k
. Thus

\sum_{k\in\left\langle S\right\rangle}\frac{\mu(k)}{|H\bmod jk|}=\frac{1}{A}\sum_{k\in\left\langle S\right\rangle}\frac{\mu(k)}{|H\bmod\gcd(j,m)k|}.

Since
S=\{p:v_{p}(j)<v_{p}(m)\}=\{p:v_{p}(\gcd(j,m))<v_{p}(m)\}
, this is positive if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},\gcd(j,m)}>0
and we obtain the last part of the proposition.
∎

In particular, if we are interested in when
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},j}>0
, we may restrict attention to integers
j
that divide the adelic level
m_{\mathcal{E}}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that
j\mid m
and let
S=\{p:p\mid m/j\}
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},j}=0
if and only if

H\cap\Gamma_{j}\bmod m=\bigcup_{p\in S}H\cap\Gamma_{jp}\bmod m.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},j}=0
if and only if

\sum_{k\in\left\langle S\right\rangle}\frac{\mu(k)}{|H\bmod jk|}=0.

Multiplying this by
|H\bmod j|
we see that this is equivalent to

\sum_{k\in\left\langle S\right\rangle}\mu(k)\left|\frac{H\cap\Gamma_{jk}}{H\cap\Gamma_{j}}\right|=0

and, since each
jk\mid m
, this is equivalent to

\sum_{k\in\left\langle S\right\rangle}\mu(k)\frac{|H\cap\Gamma_{jk}\bmod m|}{|H\cap\Gamma_{j}\bmod m|}=0.

For each
k\in\left\langle S\right\rangle
squarefree,
H\cap\Gamma_{jk}\bmod m=\bigcap_{p\mid k}H\cap\Gamma_{jp}\bmod m
and so, by the inclusion-exclusion principle,

\sum_{k\in\left\langle S\right\rangle}\mu(k)\frac{|H\cap\Gamma_{jk}\bmod m|}{|H\cap\Gamma_{j}\bmod m|}=1-\frac{1}{|H\cap\Gamma_{j}\bmod m|}\left|\bigcup_{p\in S}H\cap\Gamma_{jp}\bmod m\right|.

The proposition follows.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose that
m_{\mathcal{E}}\mid j
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},j}>0
.

Proof.

We may assume that
j=m_{\mathcal{E}}
and take
m=m_{\mathcal{E}}
in Proposition
2.3
. Then the set
S
is empty and so

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},j}>0
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let

. If there exists a prime

\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[j])=\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[pj])

Take

Proposition 2.9

Let

(1)

If

.

(2)

If

(1)

If

.

(2)

Suppose that

. Let

We have that

Suppose first that

Therefore

(3)

If

Write
\bar{H}
for

. Suppose, then, that

is coprime to

Then

is a subgroup of

such that

surjects onto each factor and such that

is surjective.
By Goursat’s lemma, there are normal subgroups

and

such that

and

is the graph of an isomorphism

If

3.
A positivity criterion

We now prove our main positivity criterion for
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{E},j}
. To ease notation we redefine
H
to be the image of the mod
m_{\mathcal{E}}
Galois representation:

\rho_{m_{\mathcal{E}}}:\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\twoheadrightarrow H\subset GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbb{Z}).

If
k\mid m_{\mathcal{E}}
, then define

\mathcal{D}_{k}:H\to(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z})^{\times}

to be the composition of determinant map with reduction modulo
k
. Since the determinant of
\rho_{m_{\mathcal{E}}}
is the cyclotomic character, we can also see
\mathcal{D}_{k}
as the restriction map

\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[m_{\mathcal{E}}])/\mathbb{Q})\to\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{k})/\mathbb{Q}).

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
jk\mid m_{\mathcal{E}}
. Then

\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[j])\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{jk})=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{j})

if and only if

\mathcal{D}_{jk}(H\cap\Gamma_{j})=(1+j\mathbb{Z}/jk\mathbb{Z})\subset(\mathbb{Z}/jk\mathbb{Z})^{\times}.

Proof.

The image of the map

\mathcal{D}_{jk}:H\cap\Gamma_{j}=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[m_{\mathcal{E}}])/\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[j]))\to\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{jk})/\mathbb{Q})=(\mathbb{Z}/jk\mathbb{Z})^{\times}

is
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{jk})/(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{jk})\cap\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[j])))
. The lemma follows, as
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{jk})/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{j}))=1+j\mathbb{Z}/jk\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Theorem 3.2

Proposition 4.7
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[3])=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})
and that

, the
squarefree part of the discriminant of
\mathcal{E}

3m=\begin{cases}2|\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}^{sf}|&\text{if $\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}^{sf}\equiv 1\bmod 4$}\\
4|\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}^{sf}|&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[m])=\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[3m])
.

Proof.

Let
\chi
be the primitive Dirichlet character associated to the quadratic extension
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}})
and let

\epsilon
be as in the proof of Proposition
3.11
. We may factor
\chi
as
\chi_{3}\chi^{\prime}
where
\chi_{3}
is
the quadratic character of conductor 3 and
\chi^{\prime}
is a quadratic character of conductor coprime to 3. Then the
character
\epsilon\cdot\chi\circ\det
of
GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbb{Z})
is trivial on the image
H
of
\rho\bmod m_{\mathcal{E}}
. If

, then
\chi^{\prime}(\det(h))=1
and
\epsilon(h)=1
. It follows that

, so
that

. Thus

The following lemma, which is the key to the argument, is proved in

Lemma 5.1

(
[
3
]
, Lemma 6.1)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(F_{q})_{q\in\mathcal{P}}
,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=(F^{\prime}_{q})_{q\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
be two families of finite Galois extensions of a number field
F
, indexed by sets of rational primes
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{P}
. For any square-free integer
k
composed of primes from
\mathcal{P}
, define:

•

fields
F_{k}=\prod_{q|k,q\in\mathcal{P}}F_{q}
and
F^{\prime}_{k}=\prod_{q|k,q\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}F^{\prime}_{q}
, where the product denotes compositum;

•

integers
N_{k}=[F_{k}:F]
and
N^{\prime}_{k}=[F^{\prime}_{k}:F]
where
N_{1}=N^{\prime}_{1}=1
;

•

quantities

\delta(\mathcal{F})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\\
q|k\Rightarrow q\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(k)}{N_{k}},\quad\delta(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\\
q|k\Rightarrow q\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(k)}{N^{\prime}_{k}}.

In addition, suppose that:

•

\mathcal{F}

covers

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. That is: for all
q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, there exists
q\in\mathcal{P}
such that
F^{\prime}_{q^{\prime}}\subseteq F_{q}
, and for all
q\in\mathcal{P}
, there exists
q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
such that
F^{\prime}_{q^{\prime}}\subseteq F_{q}
;

•

The sums defining
\delta(\mathcal{F})
and
\delta(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
are absolutely convergent.

Then
\delta(\mathcal{F})\geq\delta(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
.

Corollary 5.2
.

If, in addition to the hypotheses in Lemma
5.1
, the fields
(F^{\prime}_{q})_{q\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
are linearly disjoint over
F
then

\delta(\mathcal{F})\geq\delta(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\prod_{q\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\left(1-\frac{1}{N^{\prime}_{q}}\right).

In
[
3
]
this is applied as follows. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a non-CM elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[2])\neq\mathbb{Q}
and let
F_{q}=\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{E}[q])
for each prime
q
(with
\mathcal{P}
the set of all primes). Let

be the largest abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
contained in

, which is either quadratic or cubic by assumption. Let

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{q\in\mathcal{P}:\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})\cap K_{2}=\mathbb{Q}\}

and, for

\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})&\text{if $q\mid m_{\mathcal{E}}$, $q\neq 2$}\\

\mathcal{F}=\{F_{q}\}_{q\in\mathcal{P}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k|R}\mu(k)\left(\frac{1}{N^{\prime}_{k}}-\frac{1}{N^{\prime}_{2k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k|R}\mu(k)\left(\frac{1}{\phi(k)}-\frac{1}{d\phi(k)}\right)-\sum_{k|\frac{R}{S}}\mu(Sk)\left(\frac{1}{\phi(Sk)}-\frac{1}{d\phi(Sk)}\right)
