On Quantum Aspects of 1-Form Symmetries I: BV–BRST Cohomology and Anomaly Polynomials
Weizhen Jia, Yi-Nan Wang, Yi Zhang

Abstract
We investigate the quantum aspects of gauging continuous 1-form global symmetries. In this paper, we study the BV–BRST quantization of a U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) 2-form gauge field, described geometrically by a U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gerbe. Starting from the local Čech data of the gerbe, we construct the corresponding infinitesimal symmetry structure in terms of a Lie 2-algebroid, and show that, together with the associated exact Courant algebroid, it provides a natural geometric framework for the BV–BRST complex of this higher-form gauge theory. In this formulation, the field-ghost tower is encoded directly in the local gerbe data, and the higher Russian formula arises naturally from the relations among the connective structure, the curving, and the 3-form curvature. We further show that the resulting Čech–de Rham bicomplex provides a natural setting for anomaly descent for U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) 1-form symmetries, and illustrate the construction with explicit examples in Maxwell theory.

The Russian formula.

The curvature of the connection reform can be defined as

\displaystyle\Omega=\hat{\rm d}\omega+\frac{1}{2}[\omega,\omega]_{L}\,.

(39)

A basic geometric fact is that this curvature is horizontal. Substituting
\omega=b_{i}-\varpi
, one therefore finds

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=({\rm d}+{\rm s})(b_{i}-\varpi)+\frac{1}{2}[b_{i}-\varpi,b_{i}-\varpi]_{L}

\displaystyle={\rm d}b_{i}+\frac{1}{2}[b_{i},b_{i}]_{L}\,.

(40)

Hence, the extended connection
b_{i}-\varpi
has curvature equal to the ordinary gauge field strength

\displaystyle F_{i}={\rm d}b_{i}+\frac{1}{2}[b_{i},b_{i}]_{L}\,.

(41)

This gives precisely the Russian formula in the trivialized algebroid picture. In the Abelian case, which is the one relevant for the higher-form generalization studied later, the Lie bracket on
L
vanishes and the formula reduces to

\displaystyle({\rm d}+{\rm s})(b_{i}-\varpi)=F_{i}\,,\qquad F_{i}={\rm d}b_{i}\,.

(42)

The trivialized Atiyah algebroid therefore furnishes a direct geometric realization of the BRST bicomplex. However, in order to generalize this construction to gerbes, it is more convenient to recast the same structure in Čech language. We now explain this Čech presentation.

2.3
Comparison with the Čech–de Rham Complex

We now explain how the Čech presentation
{\cal L}_{g_{ij}}
is related to the bicomplex of the trivialized Atiyah Lie algebroid reviewed above. In the Abelian pure gauge sector, we show that the Čech–de Rham description established in
[
73
]
agrees with the BRST bicomplex of the trivialized Atiyah Lie algebroid. In this way, the map
\Phi
constructed below provides the bridge between the two descriptions. This sets up the stage for the later generalization to the gerbe case, since the Čech formulation is expressed directly in terms of local descent data and therefore continues to make sense when no ordinary bundle description is available.

In a consistent splitting, the exterior algebra of the Atiyah Lie algebroid takes the form

\displaystyle\Omega^{n}(A)=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}\Omega^{(p,q)}(M,L)\,,

(70)

and the Lie algebroid differential
\hat{\rm d}
decomposes as
\hat{\rm d}={\rm d}+{\rm s}
, with

\displaystyle{\rm d}

\displaystyle:\Omega^{(p,q)}(M,L)\to\Omega^{(p+1,q)}(M,L)\,,

(71)

\displaystyle{\rm s}

\displaystyle:\Omega^{(p,q)}(M,L)\to\Omega^{(p,q+1)}(M,L)\,.

(72)

On the other hand, given an open cover
{\cal U}=\{U_{i}\}
, the Čech–de Rham bicomplex is

\displaystyle C^{\bullet}({\cal U},\Omega^{\bullet}(M))\,,

(73)

with differentials

\displaystyle{\rm d}

\displaystyle:C^{q}({\cal U},\Omega^{p}(M))\to C^{q}({\cal U},\Omega^{p+1}(M))\,,

(74)

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle:C^{q}({\cal U},\Omega^{p}(M))\to C^{q+1}({\cal U},\Omega^{p}(M))\,.

(75)

In the Abelian case there is a natural comparison map

\displaystyle\Phi:\Omega^{(p,q)}(M,L)\longrightarrow C^{q}({\cal U},\Omega^{p}(M))

(76)

defined on generators by

\displaystyle\Phi({\rm d}x^{\mu})={\rm d}x^{\mu},\qquad\Phi(t^{*})=\varpi_{ij}\equiv\ln g_{ij}\,.

(77)

Thus, the generator of Chevalley–Eilenberg degree one is mapped to the Čech 1-cochain determined by the transition functions.

The basic compatibility statement is that, in the pure gauge sector,
\Phi
intertwines the two bicomplex differentials:

\displaystyle\Phi\circ({\rm d}+{\rm s})=({\rm d}+\delta)\circ\Phi\,.

(78)

Since the graded algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(A)
is generated by degree 0 and degree 1 elements, and both
\hat{\rm d}={\rm d}+{\rm s}
and
{\rm d}+\delta
act as degree-one derivations, it suffices to verify (
78
) on
\Omega^{0}(A)
and
\Omega^{1}(A)
.

For a scalar
\beta\in\Omega^{0}(A)
, the statement is immediate. We now consider a degree-one element

\beta\in\Omega^{1}(A)
, which in the consistent splitting can be represented by

\displaystyle\beta=(\Lambda,m)\in\Omega^{(1,0)}(M,L)\oplus\Omega^{(0,1)}(M,L)\,.

(79)

Recalling the decomposition (
25
), under the map
\Phi
one has

\displaystyle\Phi(\beta)=\Lambda_{i}+m\,\varpi_{ij}=(\tilde{\Lambda}_{i}-mb_{i})+m\,\varpi_{ij}\,.

(80)

Since the isotropy algebra is Abelian, the Lie algebroid differential reduces to

\displaystyle({\rm d}+{\rm s})\beta={\rm d}\Lambda+{\rm d}m\wedge t^{*}\,.

(81)

Applying
\Phi
gives

\displaystyle\Phi(({\rm d}+{\rm s})\beta)={\rm d}\Lambda_{i}+{\rm d}m\,\varpi_{ij}\,.

(82)

On the other hand, the Čech–de Rham differential acts by

\displaystyle({\rm d}+\delta)\Phi(\beta)

\displaystyle=({\rm d}+\delta)(\Lambda_{i}+m\,\varpi_{ij})

\displaystyle={\rm d}\Lambda_{i}+\delta\Lambda_{i}+{\rm d}m\,\varpi_{ij}+m\,{\rm d}\varpi_{ij}+m\,(\delta\varpi)_{ijk}\,.

(83)

Using
(\delta\varpi)_{ijk}=0
and
(\delta b)_{ij}={\rm d}\varpi_{ij}
, we obtain

\displaystyle({\rm d}+\delta)\Phi(\beta)={\rm d}\Lambda_{i}+{\rm d}m\,\varpi_{ij}=\Phi(({\rm d}+{\rm s})\beta)\,,

(84)

and hence (
78
) holds for degree-one elements in the pure gauge sector.

The comparison map
\Phi
is to be understood here in the Abelian pure gauge sector, which is the only case needed for the Russian formula and the descent equations discussed in this paper. In that sector, the Čech–de Rham presentation is built entirely from the transition data
g_{ij}
, and the identification of the Chevalley–Eilenberg degree-one generator
t^{*}
with the Čech 1-cochain
\varpi_{ij}=\ln g_{ij}
gives the expected correspondence between the BRST bicomplex and the Čech–de Rham bicomplex.

In the presence of charged matter fields, however, one needs to consider cochains valued in a vector bundle
F
, and the naive extension of
\Phi
is no longer a strict cochain map for
\beta\in\Omega^{1}(A;F)
. The reason is that the twisting coming from the
U(1)
-representation produces additional overlap terms which are absent in the pure gauge case. A satisfactory treatment of such
F
-valued cochains would require a more refined comparison morphism
[
74
,
75
]
, and we shall not pursue this here.

Therefore, in the Abelian pure gauge sector, the map
\Phi
provides the desired chain-level comparison between the BRST bicomplex of the trivialized Atiyah Lie algebroid and the Čech–de Rham bicomplex of its Čech presentation
{\cal L}_{g_{ij}}
. In this sense, the Čech differential
\delta
may be regarded as the Čech realization of the BRST differential
{\rm s}
.

2.4
Russian Formula and the BRST Complex

Physically, one identifies
b_{i}
as the local gauge field and
F_{i}={\rm d}b_{i}
as the local gauge field strength. Recall from (
69
) that the local 2-forms
F_{i}
glue to a globally defined field strength
F\in\Omega^{2}(M)
. We may now reexpress the horizontality of the curvature of the connection reform
\omega=b-\varpi
in the Čech language as

\displaystyle\boxed{({\rm d}+\delta)(b-\varpi)=F}\,.

(85)

This is precisely the Russian formula in the Čech–de Rham bicomplex.

Eq. (
85
) is the image under the comparison map
\Phi
of the corresponding statement (
42
) in the trivialized Atiyah Lie algebroid. Through the chain map (
78
), the BRST differential
{\rm s}
is represented in the Čech language by
\delta
, while the Chevalley–Eilenberg generator
t^{*}
is realized at the level of local cocycle data by the Čech 1-cochain
\varpi_{ij}=\ln g_{ij}
. Thus, the Čech–de Rham bicomplex provides a local realization of the BRST complex.

We identify ghost number with Čech degree in the Čech–de Rham bicomplex. Under the comparison map, the Čech differential
\delta
realizes the BRST operator
{\rm s}
, while
-\varpi
plays the role of the ghost field. At first sight, one might recognize
\delta
as the classical gauge variation and
\ln g_{ij}
as the transformation parameter. The fact that they remain encoded in the structure designed for the quantized theory is nothing mystical: both
(\delta,\varpi)
and
({\rm s},c)
arise from the same underlying cohomological structure associated with gauge symmetry, and that structure is already present at the classical level. In the BRST formalism, one resolves the quotient by gauge orbits by adjoining ghost variables together with a nilpotent differential. In the Atiyah Lie algebroid, this resolution is already built into the exact sequence (
50
) under trivialization, and the geometric fact that the algebroid curvature
\Omega
is horizontal is precisely what gives rise to the Russian formula (
85
). Thus, in the Abelian case,
\delta
plays for gauge directions a role analogous to that of the de Rham differential
{\rm d}
for spacetime directions:
{\rm d}
differentiates along
TM
, while
\delta
probes the gauge directions encoded by the transition functions. The cocycle
\varpi_{ij}=\ln g_{ij}
is the corresponding local datum, and in this sense it may be regarded as the classical antecedent of the ghost field in the BRST formalism.

3
Lie 2-Algebroids Derived from Gerbes

In the previous section we reviewed the Atiyah Lie algebroid and formulated its Čech presentation
{\cal L}_{g_{ij}}

Physically, this higher analogue is required because the background gauge field for a 1-form symmetry is not an ordinary connection on a principal bundle, but a gerbe with connective structure. In the case of a gerbe, there is no ordinary vector bundle whose sections directly play the role of the Atiyah Lie algebroid. More intrinsically, one may formulate the constructions below in terms of sheaves or 2-categories, as developed in
[

(\delta f)_{ijk}=0
, so that
\{f_{ij}\}
defines a Čech
1
-cocycle and is therefore locally exact.

The local bracket on
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
is defined in the same way as (
49
) for the ordinary symmetry case:

[(\underline{X},\{f_{ij}^{X}\}),(\underline{Y},\{f_{ij}^{Y}\})]_{{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}}=\big([\underline{X},\underline{Y}]_{TM},\{\mathcal{L}_{X}f_{ij}^{Y}-\mathcal{L}_{Y}f_{ij}^{X}\}\big)\,.

(95)

One can readily check that the bracketed object again satisfies the cocycle condition (
89
), since we can find by using the Cartan identity that

\displaystyle(\delta(\mathcal{L}_{X}f^{Y}-\mathcal{L}_{Y}f^{X}))_{ijk}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{X}(\delta f^{Y})_{ijk}-\mathcal{L}_{Y}(\delta f^{X})_{ijk}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{X}(i_{Y}{\rm d}\ln g_{ijk})-\mathcal{L}_{Y}(i_{X}{\rm d}\ln g_{ijk})

\displaystyle=i_{[X,Y]}{\rm d}\ln g_{ijk}\,.

(96)

In this way, the algebraic structure carried by
{\cal L}_{g_{ij}}
extends directly to the higher counterpart
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
. For our purposes, this is the sense in which
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
should be regarded as a Lie 2-algebroid: it is a 2-term local symmetry object built from the gerbe cocycle, equipped with an anchor to vector fields and a bracket compatible with the higher cocycle condition, even though we do not have a vector bundle. Therefore, we will refer to
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
as the Čech presentation of a Lie 2-algebroid. We will see that this is precisely the structure needed to organize the infinitesimal symmetries relevant to the BV–BRST complex.

Split and connective structure.

A
U(1)
-gerbe with
connective structure
is described in addition by a collection of 1-forms
C=\{C_{ij}\in\Omega^{1}(U_{ij})\}
defined on double overlaps, and satisfying on triple overlaps

(\delta C)_{ijk}=C_{ij}+C_{jk}-C_{ik}={\rm d}\ln g_{ijk}\,.

(97)

Thus,
C_{ij}
is a Čech 1-cochain whose coboundary reproduces the Čech 2-cocycle
{\rm d}\ln g_{ijk}
at the level of 1-forms, just as a connection
b_{i}
has coboundary
{\rm d}\ln g_{ij}
in the principal bundle case.

We now show that the split of the higher symmetry object
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
is precisely the connective structure of the gerbe. This is the direct analogue of the fact that a split of
{\cal L}_{g_{ij}}
is equivalent to the local gauge field
b_{i}
.

A split of
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
means that, for each vector field
\underline{X}\in\Gamma(TM)
, we choose a distinguished lift through a map
\sigma_{2}:\Gamma(TM)\to{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
:

\sigma_{2}(\underline{X})=(\underline{X},\{f_{ij}^{X^{H}}\})\,.

(98)

The natural higher analogue of the Atiyah formula
f_{i}^{X^{H}}=i_{X}b_{i}
is

f_{ij}^{X^{H}}=i_{X}C_{ij}\,,

(99)

where
C_{ij}\in\Omega^{1}(U_{ij},\mathfrak{u}(1))
is a collection of 1-forms on double overlaps. Substituting (
99
) into the cocycle condition (
89
), we obtain

(\delta C)_{ijk}=C_{ij}+C_{jk}-C_{ik}={\rm d}\ln g_{ijk}\,.

(100)

which is precisely the defining condition for a connective structure on the gerbe. Therefore, a split of
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
is equivalent to the choice of connective structure on the gerbe.

Once a split is chosen, every element of
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
decomposes as

(\underline{X},\{f_{ij}^{X}\})=(\underline{X},\{f_{ij}^{X^{H}}\})+(0,\{f_{ij}^{X}-f_{ij}^{X^{H}}\})\,.

(101)

Hence, we can introduce the higher connection reform

\omega_{2}(\underline{X},\{f_{ij}^{X}\})=\{f_{ij}^{X^{H}}-f_{ij}^{X}\}\,,

(102)

which satisfies
\mathrm{ker}(\omega_{2})=\mathrm{im}(\sigma_{2})
. Then, in exact analogy with the ordinary symmetry case, we have the following exact sequences in both directions:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{1}({\cal U},\Omega^{0}(M,\mathfrak{u}(1)))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma(TM)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0\,.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{2}}

(103)

The maps
\rho_{2}
,
j_{2}
,
\sigma_{2}
and
\omega_{2}
satisfy

\displaystyle\rho_{2}\circ\sigma_{2}(\underline{X})

\displaystyle=\underline{X}\,,\qquad\sigma_{2}\circ\rho_{2}(\underline{X},\{f^{X}_{ij}\})=(\underline{X},\{f^{X^{H}}_{ij}\})\,,

(104)

\displaystyle\omega_{2}\circ j_{2}(\{f_{ij}\})

\displaystyle=\{f_{ij}\}\,,\qquad j_{2}\circ\omega_{2}(\underline{X},\{f^{X}_{ij}\})=(0,\{f^{X^{H}}_{ij}-f^{X}_{ij}\})\,.

(105)

Curvature of the split.

We next compute the obstruction for the split
\sigma_{2}
to preserve the bracket. Given two vector fields
\underline{X},\underline{Y}\in\Gamma(TM)
, the curvature of
\sigma_{2}
is defined as

\displaystyle R^{\sigma_{2}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})

\displaystyle=[\sigma_{2}(\underline{X}),\sigma_{2}(\underline{Y})]_{{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}}-\sigma_{2}([\underline{X},\underline{Y}]_{TM})

\displaystyle=\big(0,\{f_{ij}^{[X^{H},Y^{H}]}-f_{ij}^{[X,Y]^{H}}\}\big)\,.

(106)

Using (
99
) and the Cartan identity, we find

\displaystyle f_{ij}^{[X^{H},Y^{H}]}-f_{ij}^{[X,Y]^{H}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{X}i_{Y}C_{ij}-\mathcal{L}_{Y}i_{X}C_{ij}-i_{[X,Y]}C_{ij}

\displaystyle=i_{Y}i_{X}{\rm d}C_{ij}\,,

(107)

and thus

R^{\sigma_{2}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})=\big(0,\{i_{Y}i_{X}{\rm d}C_{ij}\}\big)\,.

(108)

Equivalently, if one introduces the higher connection reform

\omega_{2}(\underline{X},\{f_{ij}^{X}\})=\{f_{ij}^{X^{H}}-f_{ij}^{X}\}\,,

(109)

then its curvature is represented by the same local data,

R^{\omega_{2}}((\underline{X},\{f_{ij}^{X^{H}}\}),(\underline{Y},\{f_{ij}^{Y^{H}}\}))=\{-i_{Y}i_{X}{\rm d}C_{ij}\}\,,

(110)

since the bracket on the Abelian vertical part vanishes. Thus, the curvatures of the split
\sigma_{2}
and the connection form
\omega_{2}
are encoded by the same Čech 1-cochain
\{i_{Y}i_{X}{\rm d}C_{ij}\}
, exactly as in (
67
) for ordinary gauge symmetry.

At present we restrict attention to the higher gauge sector encoded by
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
. A more complete higher gauge theory would presumably require an analogue of charged matter fields. Since gerbes are naturally associated with extended objects rather than point particles, such fields should be related to string-like charged objects. Correspondingly, one expects that this would involve an appropriate notion of higher representation of the Lie 2-algebroid
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
. We do not develop this here, and leave it as a possible direction for extending the present construction beyond the pure higher gauge sector.

3.2
The Čech–de Rham Complex and a Partial Higher Russian Formula

For the Atiyah Lie algebroid, we began with the algebroid differential
\hat{\rm d}
, then passed through a local trivialization to the BRST form
{\rm d}+{\rm s}
, and finally translated this to the Čech–de Rham differential
{\rm d}+\delta
. In the gerbe case there is no natural vector bundle whose exterior algebra directly supplies such a differential. The higher symmetry structure is therefore most naturally described directly in the total Čech–de Rham bicomplex.

In the Čech presentation, the algebra is exactly parallel to the ordinary symmetry case. There, a split determined by the local connection data
b_{i}
led to the relation (
85
); here, the split is determined by the connective structure
C_{ij}
. Denoting

c_{ijk}\equiv\ln g_{ijk}\,,

(111)

we immediately obtain the following relation:

({\rm d}+\delta)(C-c)={\rm d}C\,,

(112)

where
C=\{C_{ij}\}
is viewed as a Čech 1-cochain of 1-forms, while
c=\{c_{ijk}\}
is a Čech 2-cochain of 0-forms. Thus, the relation (
112
) is a direct higher analogue of the Russian formula (
85
) for ordinary gauge symmetry, with the connective structure
C_{ij}
replacing the local gauge potential
b_{i}
.

Applying
{\rm d}
to
\delta C={\rm d}c
yields
\delta({\rm d}C)_{ijk}=0
, and thus
\{{\rm d}C_{ij}\}
is a Čech 1-cocycle valued in
\Omega^{2}(M)
. Hence, one may choose a collection of local 2-forms
B_{i}\in\Omega^{2}(U_{i},\mathfrak{u}(1))
such that

(\delta B)_{ij}=B_{i}-B_{j}={\rm d}C_{ij}\,.

(113)

Then, the relation (
112
) can be written as

({\rm d}+\delta)(C-c)=\delta B\,.

(114)

Given a gerbe with connective structure, such a local 2-form
B_{i}
is called a
curving
of the connective structure.

There is an important difference here from the ordinary gauge symmetry, where the curvature of the split lands directly in the vertical part of the algebroid. In the gerbe case, the curvature of the split (
108
) may be written as

R^{\sigma_{2}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})=\big(0,\{f_{ij}^{[X^{H},Y^{H}]}-f_{ij}^{[X,Y]^{H}}\}\big)=\big(0,\{i_{Y}i_{X}(B_{i}-B_{j})\}\big)\,.

(115)

Therefore, the transition (
90
) between the lift of the bracket and the bracket of the lifts is represented by

u_{i}=i_{Y}i_{X}B_{i}\,.

(116)

In other words, the collection
\{B_{i}\}
does not define the split of
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
itself, but controls the failure of the split to preserve the bracket.

However, the relation (
114
) is not yet the higher Russian formula itself. At this stage, the local 2-forms
B_{i}
have only appeared as the curving associated to the split of the higher symmetry object
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
. In the next section we will see that these same 2-forms define the split of the associated exact Courant algebroid. Once that interpretation is in place, we will obtain the full higher Russian formula and the geometric meaning of the BV–BRST tower becomes manifest.

4
Exact Courant Algebroids Derived from Gerbes

In the previous section we introduced the Čech Lie 2-algebroid
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
associated to a
U(1)
-gerbe and showed that its split is precisely the connective structure
C_{ij}
. We also found that the local 2-forms
B_{i}
appear naturally in the curvature data of that split. We now explain that these same 2-forms admit a second, equally natural geometric interpretation: they define the split of an exact Courant algebroid associated to the gerbe. This is the higher analogue of the role played by the local gauge field in the ordinary symmetry case. For a brief review of the basics of Courant algebroids, see Appendix
A.2
.

4.1
Exact Courant Algebroids in Čech Language

An exact Courant algebroid is a vector bundle
E\to M
equipped with a Courant bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}
, a nondegenerate symmetric pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{E}
, and an anchor map
\rho_{E}:E\to TM
, fitting into the exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces TM}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0\,.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{E}}

(117)

For an exact Courant algebroid, however, the symmetric pairing determines the inclusion canonically: for each
\alpha\in T^{*}M
, the element
j_{E}(\alpha)\in E
is characterized by

\langle\rho_{E}^{*}(\alpha),e\rangle_{E}=\alpha(\rho_{E}(e))\qquad\forall\alpha\in T^{*}M,\ e\in E\,.

(118)

Thus, the inclusion
j_{E}
is in fact precisely the adjoint map
\rho_{E}^{*}
.

As in the ordinary symmetry case, we can also work in the Čech presentation. Let
\{U_{i}\}
be an open cover of
M
. On each patch
U_{i}
, an exact Courant algebroid is locally trivialized as

E|_{U_{i}}\cong TU_{i}\oplus T^{*}U_{i}\,.

(119)

A local section is therefore represented by a pair
(\underline{X},a_{i})
, with
\underline{X}\in\Gamma(TU_{i})
and
a_{i}\in\Omega^{1}(U_{i})
. To glue these local models into a global exact Courant algebroid, one uses closed 2-forms
{\cal B}_{ij}\in\Omega^{2}(U_{ij})
satisfying

(\delta{\cal B})_{ijk}={\cal B}_{ij}+{\cal B}_{jk}-{\cal B}_{ik}=0\,,\qquad{\rm d}{\cal B}_{ij}=0\,,

(120)

and identifies on overlaps

(\underline{X},a_{i})\sim(\underline{X},a_{j}+i_{X}{\cal B}_{ij})\,.

(121)

We therefore define
{\cal L}_{{\cal B}_{ij}}
to be the collection of pairs

(\underline{X},\{a_{i}^{X}\})\,,

(122)

where
\underline{X}\in\Gamma(TM)
and the local 1-forms
a_{i}^{X}\in\Omega^{1}(U_{i})
satisfy
a_{i}^{X}=a_{j}^{X}+i_{X}{\cal B}_{ij}
. This is the Čech presentation of the exact Courant algebroid associated to the gluing data
\{{\cal B}_{ij}\}
.

At the level of sections, we obtain the exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega^{1}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\cal L}_{{\cal B}_{ij}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma(TM)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0\,,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{E}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{E}}

(123)

where

\rho_{E}(\underline{X},\{a_{i}^{X}\})=\underline{X}\,,\qquad\rho_{E}^{*}(a)=(0,\{a\})\,.

(124)

The local Courant bracket on
TU_{i}\oplus T^{*}U_{i}
induces a bracket on
{\cal L}_{{\cal B}_{ij}}
given by

\displaystyle[(\underline{X},\{a_{i}^{X}\}),(\underline{Y},\{a_{i}^{Y}\})]_{{\cal L}_{{\cal B}_{ij}}}=\Big([\underline{X},\underline{Y}]_{TM},\{\mathcal{L}_{X}a_{i}^{Y}-\mathcal{L}_{Y}a_{i}^{X}-\frac{1}{2}{\rm d}(i_{X}a_{i}^{Y}-i_{Y}a_{i}^{X})\}\Big)\,,

(125)

and the pairing is given by

\langle(\underline{X},\{a_{i}^{X}\}),(\underline{Y},\{a_{i}^{Y}\})\rangle_{{\cal L}_{{\cal B}_{ij}}}=\{i_{X}a_{i}^{Y}+i_{Y}a_{i}^{X}\}\,.

(126)

Using (

), one can check that both the bracket and the pairing are compatible with the identification (

Split, isotropic split, and curvature.

A split of the exact sequence (

\sigma_{E}:\Gamma(TM)\to{\cal L}_{{\cal B}_{ij}}
such that

\sigma_{E}(\underline{X})=(\underline{X},\{a_{i}^{X^{H}}\})\,.

(127)

Equivalently, we may define a connection reform
\omega_{E}:{\cal L}_{{\cal B}_{ij}}\to\Omega^{1}(M)
with

j_{E}(\{a_{i}\})=(0,\{a_{i}\})\,,

(128)

which satisfies
\mathrm{ker}(\omega_{E})=\mathrm{im}(\sigma_{E})
. The maps
\rho_{E}
,
j_{E}
,
\sigma_{E}
and
\omega_{E}
satisfy

\displaystyle\rho_{E}\circ\sigma_{E}(\underline{X})

\displaystyle=\underline{X}\,,\qquad\sigma_{E}\circ\rho_{E}((\underline{X},\{a_{i}^{X}\}))=(\underline{X},\{a_{i}^{X^{H}}\})\,,

(129)

\displaystyle\omega_{E}\circ j_{E}(\{a_{i}\})

\displaystyle=\{a_{i}\}\,,\qquad j_{E}\circ\omega_{E}((\underline{X},\{a_{i}^{X}\}))=(0,\{a_{i}^{X}-a_{i}^{X^{H}}\})\,.

(130)

Note that unlike

and
\rho_{E}

\sigma_{E}

since the image of
\omega_{E}
has only a component in

has both
TM
and
T^{*}M

\langle\sigma_{E}(\underline{X}),\sigma_{E}(\underline{Y})\rangle_{E}=0\qquad\forall\underline{X},\underline{Y}\in\Gamma(TM)\,.

(131)

Then, for any
\underline{X}\in\Gamma(TM)
and
(\underline{Y},\{a_{i}^{Y}\})\in\Gamma({\cal L}_{{\cal B}_{ij}})

\langle\sigma_{E}(\underline{X}),(\underline{Y},\{a_{i}^{Y}\})\rangle=\omega_{E}((\underline{Y},\{a_{i}^{Y}\}))(\underline{X})\,,

(132)

which means
\omega_{E}
can be identified as the pullback
\sigma_{E}^{*}
. Then, given an isotropic split, we have the exact sequences going in both directions:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

and hence

determines a cohomology class

(136)

This determines a class in de Rham cohomology

disappear.

4.2
The Exact Courant Algebroid Associated to a Gerbe

Now suppose we have a
U(1)
-gerbe with connective structure, characterized by the data
(g_{ijk},C_{ij})
. It is obvious that
{\rm d}C_{ij}
is a closed 2
-

(\delta C)_{ijk}={\rm d}\ln g_{ijk}
it follows that
\delta{\rm d}C=0
. Thus, we can define an exact Courant
{\cal L}_{{\rm d}C_{ij}}
by taking

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{ij}={\rm d}C_{ij}\,.

(137)

This is the exact Courant algebroid naturally derived from a gerbe with connective structure, on which the local 2-forms
B_{i}
introduced in the previous section now acquire a second, independent geometric meaning.
11
11

11

Strictly speaking, the forms
C_{ij}
and
B_{i}
are

rather than
T^{*}M
. Since
\mathfrak{u}(1)
is a one-dimensional real vector space, we will suppress this distinction.

Split and 2-form gauge field.

Suppose we are given a collection of local 2-forms
B=\{B_{i}\in\Omega^{2}(U_{i},\mathfrak{u}(1))\}
. We define the horizontal lift of a vector field
\underline{X}
by specifying
a_{i}^{X^{H}}=i_{X}B_{i}
, i.e.,

\sigma_{E}(\underline{X})=(\underline{X},\{i_{X}B_{i}\})\,.

(138)

We can immediately see that the gluing relation (

) requires that

B_{i}-B_{j}={\rm d}C_{ij}\,.

(139)

Thus, the local 2-form

determining a split of the exact Courant algebroid
{\cal L}_{{\rm d}C_{ij}}
is automatically a curving defined in (

). Moreover, due to the antisymmetry of

\langle\sigma_{E}(\underline{X}),\sigma_{E}(\underline{Y})\rangle=(i_{X}i_{Y}B_{i}+i_{Y}i_{X}B_{i})=0\,,

(140)

and hence this split is isotropic. Then, we can write down the curvature 3-form of the 2

as

\displaystyle H_{i}\equiv{\rm d}B_{i}\,,

which can be derived from the curvature of

\displaystyle R^{\sigma_{E}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})=-j_{E}(R^{\omega_{E}}((\underline{X},\{\underline{a}^{X_{H}}_{i}\}),(\underline{Y},\{\underline{a}^{Y_{H}}_{i}\})))=(0,\{i_{Y}i_{X}H_{i}\})\,.

(142)

It is easy to see from

on the intersection of any

and

glue to a global closed 3

as the curvature of the

At this point the geometric role of all local data has been identified. The gerbe cocycle

defines the highest Čech component, the connective structure
C_{ij}
defines the split of the Lie 2-algebroid
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
, and the local 2-forms

define the isotropic split of the associated exact Courant algebroid

. We now collect these ingredients and formulate the higher Russian formula.

5
Higher Russian Formula and the BV–BRST Complex

Given a
U(1)
-gerbe with connective structure and curving, a choice of local trivializations determines local Čech–de Rham data

(B_{i},C_{ij},c_{ijk})\,,

(143)

where

B_{i}\in C^{0}({\cal U},\Omega^{2}(M))\,,\qquad C_{ij}\in C^{1}({\cal U},\Omega^{1}(M))\,,\qquad c_{ijk}\in C^{2}({\cal U},\Omega^{0}(M))\,.

(144)

These local forms arise from the two geometric structures introduced in the previous sections. The connective structure
C_{ij}
induces a split of the Lie 2-algebroid
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
, while the curving
B_{i}
determines an isotropic split of the associated exact Courant algebroid
{\cal L}_{{\rm d}C_{ij}}
.

We have seen that from the split Lie 2-algebroid one obtains the descent relation

({\rm d}+\delta)(C-c)=\delta B\,,

(145)

while from the split exact Courant algebroid one obtains the curvature identity

{\rm d}B=H\,,

(146)

where the local 3-forms
{\rm d}B_{i}
glue to a globally defined closed 3-form curvature. Combining (
145
) and (
146
), we obtain the
higher Russian formula

\boxed{({\rm d}+\delta)(B-C+c)=H}\,.

(147)

It is convenient to introduce the total field

\omega_{\rm tot}\equiv B-C+c

(148)

in the total Čech–de Rham complex. Then (
147
) can be written as

({\rm d}+\delta)\omega_{\rm tot}=H\,.

(149)

This is the direct higher analogue of the ordinary Russian formula (
85
), with
\omega=b-\varpi
replaced by the total field
\omega_{\rm tot}
.

Expanding (
147
) by Čech degree recovers the local relations

(\delta c)_{ijkl}=0\,,\qquad(\delta C)_{ijk}=({\rm d}c)_{ijk}\,,\qquad(\delta B)_{ij}=({\rm d}C)_{ij}\,,\qquad{\rm d}B_{i}=H_{i}\,.

(150)

Thus, the higher Russian formula packages the full hierarchy of local descent relations together with the global curvature into a single identity. From the viewpoint of physics, the first three identities are recognized as the familiar BRST transformation laws of an Abelian reducible gauge system, or equivalently as the minimal BV–BRST differential on the field-ghost tower. In particular, they identify
C
as the 1-form ghost associated to the 2-form gauge field
B
, and
c
as the corresponding ghost-for-ghost. The total field
\omega_{\rm tot}=B-C+c
may therefore be viewed as the extended field
\hat{B}
encoding the full field-ghost hierarchy (see Appendix
B
), while the final identity
{\rm d}B_{i}=H_{i}
expresses the globally defined 3-form curvature.

This also clarifies the relation between the Čech–de Rham bicomplex in the gerbe case and the BV–BRST complex. Recall that in the 0-form symmetry case, the Čech–de Rham bicomplex provides a local model for the BRST complex of an irreducible gauge theory, where the Čech differential
\delta
plays the role of the BRST differential, and the degree-1 Čech cocycle corresponds to the ghost field. In the 1-form symmetry case, the gauge symmetry is reducible, and the corresponding local complex naturally represents the minimal field-ghost sector of the BV–BRST complex. The Čech degree records the ghost hierarchy:
C
is the 1-form ghost and
c
is the 0-form ghost-for-ghost. In this sense, the role of the Čech–de Rham bicomplex in the gerbe case is the precise higher analogue of its role in the ordinary principal bundle case. For a principal bundle it models the BRST complex associated with the Atiyah Lie algebroid; for gerbes it models the corresponding higher field-ghost BV–BRST complex associated with the Lie 2-algebroid, while the curving simultaneously determines the split of the associated exact Courant algebroid encoding the 3-form curvature.

6.1
Descent Equations and Anomaly Polynomials

Building on
[
30
]

In a local trivialization, the algebroid Chern–Weil construction produces characteristic classes and their Chern–Simons transgression forms, whose bi-degree expansion yields the descent equations
[
26

, and hence the Wess–Zumino consistency condition
[

Anomaly descent for ordinary symmetries

For an ordinary gauge symmetry with local gauge field
b
and curvature
F={\rm d}b+\frac{1}{2}[b,b]_{L}
, a characteristic class is a closed form constructed from the curvature

I_{d+2}(F)={\cal Q}(F,\ldots,F)\in\Omega^{d+2}(M)\,,

(151)

where
\cal Q
is a symmetric invariant polynomial valued in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of the symmetry. Locally, one can write

I_{d+2}(F)={\rm d}\mathrm{CS}_{d+1}(b)\,,

(152)

where
\mathrm{CS}_{d+1}(b)
is the corresponding Chern–Simons transgression form.

In the BRST complex coming from the trivialized Atiyah Lie algebroid, the algebroid differential
\hat{\rm d}
decomposes as
{\rm d}+{\rm s}
,
and the connection reform
\omega=b-\varpi
realizes the extended connection, whose total curvature coincides with
F
by the Russian formula. Expanding
\mathrm{CS}_{d+1}(\omega)
by the de Rham form degree
p
and the ghost number
q
yields a sequence of descent equations

{\rm d}\alpha^{(p,q)}+{\rm s}\alpha^{(p+1,q-1)}=0\,,\qquad p+q=d+1\,,

(153)

and in particular the Wess–Zumino consistency condition

{\rm d}\alpha^{(d-1,2)}+{\rm s}\alpha^{(d,1)}=0\,.

(154)

The consistent anomaly of a quantum field theory on a
d

\alpha^{(d,1)}

\alpha^{(d,1)}\neq{\rm d}\alpha^{(d-1,1)}+{\rm s}\alpha^{(d,0)}

H^{d,1}({\rm d}|{\rm s})

provides a topological invariant called the
anomaly polynomial

\alpha^{(d,1)}
is the consistent anomaly descending from it.

Anomaly descent for 1-form symmetries

A key point, already familiar from the Atiyah Lie algebroid description of the BRST complex, is that the Chern–Weil construction depends only on the existence of a closed differential subalgebra generated by a “connection” and its “curvature,” rather than on any particular formulation of the total space of a principal bundle. In the present higher-form setting, the higher Russian formula (
147
) exhibits the 3-form
H
as a
{\rm d}+\delta
-closed, ghost-free curvature associated to the total BV–BRST complex. Consequently, invariant polynomials in
H
define cocycles in the total bicomplex, and admit local Chern–Simons transgression representatives exactly as in ordinary Chern–Weil theory. In particular, the cohomological characterization of anomalies via descent continues to apply in this higher algebroid formulation.

At the purely local/perturbative level, the analogue of the anomaly polynomial is now a closed gauge-invariant
(d+2)
-form constructed from
H
(and possibly gravitational data from the frame bundle or its associated tangent bundle). In the simplest case of a pure 1-form symmetry background, this means a polynomial of the schematic form

I_{d+2}(H)\in\Omega^{d+2}(M)\,,\qquad{\rm d}I_{d+2}(H)=0\,.

(155)

One can also consider cases involving Pontryagin classes of
TM
as polynomials of the spacetime curvature 2-form

I_{d+2}(H,R)\in\Omega^{d+2}(M)\,,\qquad{\rm d}I_{d+2}(H,R)=0\,,

(156)

which give rise to gauge-gravitational mixed anomalies.

Locally,
I_{d+2}(H)
admits a Chern–Simons transgression
(d+1)
-form

built from the BV–BRST data
(B,C,c)

{\rm d}\alpha^{(p,q)}+\delta\alpha^{(p+1,q-1)}=0\,,\qquad p+q=d+1\,,

(157)

including in particular the Wess–Zumino consistency condition

{\rm d}\alpha^{(d-1,2)}+\delta\alpha^{(d,1)}=0\,.

(158)

This naturally makes

with

d

lives in
H^{d,1}({\rm d}|\delta)

Since

is a form of odd degree, we have

7
Conclusions

In this work, we studied the geometry underlying the BV–BRST description of a
U(1)
1-form gauge symmetry. We began by reformulating the Atiyah Lie algebroid, which underlies the BRST complex of ordinary gauge symmetry, in terms of its local Čech presentation
{\cal L}_{g_{ij}}
. This perspective is particularly useful because, for a gerbe, the higher analogue of the Atiyah Lie algebroid is no longer described by an ordinary vector bundle. The natural higher symmetry object therefore appears directly in the Čech description.

Since the background field for a
U(1)
1-form symmetry comes from a gerbe, which is naturally formulated in Čech data, we developed the corresponding higher structure directly in the Čech picture. Starting from the gerbe cocycle, we introduced the local infinitesimal symmetry object
{\cal L}_{g_{ijk}}
, which should be regarded as a Čech presentation of the Lie 2-algebroid associated to the gerbe. From the physical viewpoint, we identify the connective data
C
with the 1-form ghost, the Čech 2-cocycle
c
with the ghost-for-ghost, and the curving
B
with the 2-form gauge field. Given a gerbe with connective structure, one can further construct an associated exact Courant algebroid, where the same gauge field
B
arises from a choice of split.

Using these ingredients, we derived the higher Russian formula (
147
), which packages the local gerbe data and the global 3-form curvature into a single identity. Its expansion by Čech degree reproduces the hierarchy of local descent relations and identifies the resulting Čech–de Rham bicomplex with the minimal BV–BRST field-ghost complex of an Abelian reducible gauge system. We then showed that the curvature
H
plays the role of the ghost-free total curvature in the higher setting, allowing one to construct anomaly polynomials and derive the corresponding consistent anomaly by descent. Finally, we illustrated the formalism in two explicit examples: the mixed anomaly of electric and magnetic
U(1)
1-form symmetries in
4d
Maxwell theory, which can be viewed as an ABJ anomaly, and the mixed gauge-gravitational anomaly in
5d
Maxwell theory, which admits a Green–Schwarz type interpretation.

There are several directions in which the present construction could be developed further. First, from a geometric viewpoint, our treatment of the Lie 2-algebroid is given in a local Čech presentation rather than in a direct analogue of the vector bundle description of the Atiyah Lie algebroid. It would be desirable to identify the precise global geometric object underlying this presentation, possibly in relation to higher principal bundles and their infinitesimal symmetries
[
87
,
88
,
89
,
90
,
91
,
92
,
93
]
. Closely related to this is the fact that, in our derivation of the higher Russian formula, both the Lie 2-algebroid and the associated Courant algebroid play essential roles. An important open question is therefore whether there exists a single geometric structure that unifies the two and from which the higher Russian formula follows directly. If so, it would be natural to ask whether it carries an intrinsic cohomology theory, analogous to the cohomology of the Atiyah Lie algebroid defined by
\hat{\rm d}
, and whether the total field
\omega_{\rm tot}
can be interpreted as a geometric object on it, just as the connection form
\omega
is naturally associated with the Atiyah Lie algebroid.

The recent development in
[
94
]
provides a natural framework in which these questions may be formulated globally. In that work, the Atiyah
L_{\infty}
-algebroid of a principal
\infty
-bundle is introduced,
p
-form connections are interpreted as order-
p
splits, the Čech–Deligne description of connections on higher
U(1)
-bundles is recovered, and derived higher symmetries of higher
U(1)
-bundles are related to higher Courant algebroids. From the perspective of the present paper, this suggests that our local Čech Lie 2-algebroid should arise as a presentation of a global Atiyah
L_{\infty}
-algebroid of a gerbe, while the associated Courant algebroid may encode the additional curvature data entering the higher Russian formula. The present work emphasizes a complementary physical aspect of these structures: the emergence of the BV–BRST field-ghost tower, the higher Russian formula, and anomaly descent for
U(1)
1-form symmetries.

More generally, the present construction should admit a direct higher-form generalization. For a pure
p
-form gauge symmetry, our local Čech construction should naturally extend to a corresponding higher algebroid structure associated with a higher gerbe. However, our construction also depends crucially on the associated exact Courant algebroid, and so a systematic generalization would require the appropriate higher analogue of this structure as well. The framework of
[
94
]
, especially its treatment of
p
-form connections on principal
\infty
-bundles and its relation between higher
U(1)
-bundles and higher Courant algebroids, suggests a promising route toward such a generalization.

Furthermore, the descent data constructed in this paper encode the consistent anomaly, since in the case of ordinary gauge symmetry, the Čech–de Rham complex arises from the consistent splitting of the Atiyah Lie algebroid. It has been shown in
[
30
]
that one may also choose a covariant splitting of the Atiyah Lie algebroid, whose corresponding cohomological description leads directly to the covariant anomaly
[
95
]
. It would be interesting to understand whether an analogous covariant splitting exists in the Lie 2-algebroid setting and, if so, how the corresponding covariant anomaly is represented.

There are also a number of physical extensions. One natural direction is to couple the gerbe sector to an ordinary Yang–Mills theory, where the interplay between 1-form and 0-form symmetries may be organized into a higher-group structure
[
96
,
86
,
97
,
98
,
99
,
100
,
101
,
102
,
103
]
. Another is to incorporate matter fields. In the higher-form setting, this should naturally include string-like charged objects, which may be regarded as a kind of 2-matter and may require an appropriate notion of higher representation for the underlying higher algebroid structure.

, which can be characterized by two bundle maps

and

.
\omega:A\to L

and satisfies

\omega(j(\underline{\mu}))=-\underline{\mu}\,.\qquad\forall\underline{\mu}\in\Gamma(L)\,.

(190)

Once an

. On the other hand, one can define a

(or a

\sigma:TM\to A
satisfying

\rho(\sigma(\underline{X}))=\underline{X}\,,\qquad\forall\underline{X}\in TM\,,

(191)

The image of

(192)

We can see that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces TM}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0\,.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}

(193)

Any transitive Lie algebroid possesses an exterior algebra:

(194)

The coboundary operator of this exterior algebra,

+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\eta([\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{i},\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{j}]_{A},\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{i}},\cdots,\widehat{\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{j}},\cdots,\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{p+1})\,,

(195)

where

The compatibility condition ensures that

Given such a representation, one can modify (

\displaystyle\hat{{\rm d}}^{E}\eta(\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{1},\cdots,\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{p+1})={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i}(-1)^{i+1}\phi_{E}(\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{i})\eta(\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{i}},\cdots,\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{p+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\eta([\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{i},\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{j}]_{A},\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{i}},\cdots,\widehat{\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{j}},\cdots,\underline{\mathfrak{X}}_{p+1})\,.

A.2
Courant Algebroids

A Courant algebroid is a vector bundle
E
over
M
equipped with three structures: ① a bundle map
\rho:E\to TM
called the anchor map; ② a symmetric, non-degenerate, fiber-wise bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:E\otimes E\to\mathbb{R}
, called the
inner product
or
pairing
; ③ a bilinear operation
[\cdot,\cdot]_{E}:\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
called the
Courant bracket
, which is antisymmetric:

\displaystyle[\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2}]_{E}=-[\underline{e}_{2},\underline{e}_{1}]_{E}\,.

(204)

These are required to satisfy, for all
\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2},\underline{e}_{3}\in\Gamma(E)
:

(a)

\rho([\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2}]_{E})=[\rho(\underline{e}_{1}),\rho(\underline{e}_{2})]_{TM}
(anchor preserves brackets),

(b)

[f\underline{e}_{1},g\underline{e}_{2}]_{E}=fg[\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2}]_{E}+f(\rho(\underline{e}_{1})g)\underline{e}_{2}-g(\rho(\underline{e}_{2})f)\underline{e}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\langle\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2}\rangle(gDf-fDg)
,
\forall f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, where
D:C^{\infty}(M)\to\Gamma(E)
is defined by
\langle Df,e\rangle=\rho(e)(f)
,

(c)

\rho(\underline{e}_{1})\langle\underline{e}_{2},\underline{e}_{3}\rangle=\langle[\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2}]_{E},\underline{e}_{3}\rangle+\langle\underline{e}_{2},[\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{3}]_{E}\rangle
,

(d)

[\underline{e}_{1},[\underline{e}_{2},\underline{e}_{3}]_{E}]_{E}+\text{cyclic}=DT(\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2},\underline{e}_{3})
, where

\displaystyle T(\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2},\underline{e}_{3})=\frac{1}{3}(\langle[\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2}]_{E},\underline{e}_{3}\rangle+\text{cyclic})

(205)

is called the Jacobiator.

Condition (d) shows that the Courant bracket does not satisfy the Jacobi identity. This failure motivates the introduction of the Dorfman bracket (also called the Dorfman derivative), which is a bilinear operation
[\cdot\circ\cdot]_{E}:\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
defined by

\displaystyle[\underline{e}_{1}\circ\underline{e}_{2}]_{E}:=[\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2}]_{E}+\frac{1}{2}D\langle\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2}\rangle.

(206)

The Dorfman bracket satisfies a Leibniz identity (making
(\Gamma(E),[\cdot\circ\cdot]_{E})
a Leibniz algebra):

\displaystyle[\underline{e}_{1}\circ[\underline{e}_{2}\circ\underline{e}_{3}]_{E}]_{E}=[[\underline{e}_{1}\circ\underline{e}_{2}]_{E}\circ\underline{e}_{3}]_{E}+[\underline{e}_{2}\circ[\underline{e}_{1}\circ\underline{e}_{3}]_{E}]_{E}\,.

(207)

In this sense, the Dorfman bracket restores a Jacobi-like identity at the cost of ruining the antisymmetry of the bracket, and the Courant bracket is recovered by antisymmetrization

\displaystyle[\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2}]_{E}=\frac{1}{2}([\underline{e}_{1}\circ\underline{e}_{2}]_{E}-[\underline{e}_{2}\circ\underline{e}_{1}]_{E})\,.

(208)

Moreover, the Dorfman bracket is compatible with the anchor and the inner product in a simpler way:

(a)

\rho([\underline{e}_{1}\circ\underline{e}_{2}]_{E})=[\rho(\underline{e}_{1}),\rho(\underline{e}_{2})]_{TM}
,

(b)

[\underline{e}_{1}\circ f\underline{e}_{2}]_{E}=f[\underline{e}_{1}\circ\underline{e}_{2}]_{E}+(\rho(\underline{e}_{1})f)\underline{e}_{2}
,

(c)

[e\circ e]_{E}=D\langle e,e\rangle
,

(d)

\rho(\underline{e}_{1})\langle\underline{e}_{2},\underline{e}_{3}\rangle=\langle[\underline{e}_{1}\circ\underline{e}_{2}]\circ\underline{e}_{3}\rangle+\langle\underline{e}_{2}\circ[\underline{e}_{1}\circ\underline{e}_{3}]\rangle
.

The Courant algebroid can be defined alternatively by replacing the Courant bracket and the corresponding conditions with the Dorfman bracket and the above conditions.

A Courant algebroid is said to be
exact
if the anchor map is surjective and the following exact sequence is satisfied:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces TM}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0\,,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{E}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{E}}

(209)

where
\rho^{*}
is the pullback of
\rho
defined through the inner product. In this case, the Courant algebroid is locally identified as
TM\oplus T^{*}M
. Note that unlike transitive Lie algebroids, the surjectivity of
\rho_{E}
does not automatically imply the existence of the short exact sequence (
209
).

Connection and curvature on
E
.

Suppose
E
is an exact Courant algebroid. As in the transitive Lie algebroid case, there is a canonical vertical subbundle

V:=\mathrm{ker}(\rho_{E})=\mathrm{im}(\rho_{E}^{*})\subset E,

(210)

which is isotropic with respect to the inner product in the sense that
\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle=0
,
\forall v_{1},v_{2}\in V
.

A choice of
connection
(or
split
) is a bundle map
\sigma_{E}:TM\to E
satisfying
\rho_{E}\circ\sigma_{E}=\mathrm{id}_{TM}
. In general
\sigma_{E}
is not unique, and each choice of
\sigma_{E}
defines the horizontal subbundle
H=\mathrm{im}(\sigma)
of
E
, and the Courant algebroid can be decomposed as
E=H\oplus V
. We can also impose the isotropic condition on
\sigma_{E}
by requiring that

\langle\sigma_{E}(\underline{X}),\sigma_{E}(\underline{Y})\rangle=0\,,\qquad\forall\underline{X},\underline{Y}\in TM\,.

(211)

Then, the horizontal subbundle is said to be
maximally isotropic
or
Lagrangian
(maximal in the sense that it is an isotropic subbundle of largest possible rank).

The curvature of
\sigma_{E}
measures the obstruction for horizontal lifts to close under the Courant bracket, defined as

R^{\sigma_{E}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})\equiv[\sigma_{E}(\underline{X}),\sigma_{E}(\underline{Y})]_{E}-\sigma_{E}([\underline{X},\underline{Y}]_{TM})\,.\qquad\forall\underline{X},\underline{Y}\in TM\,.

(212)

Since
\rho_{E}
preserves brackets, we have for all
\underline{X},\underline{Y}\in TM
,

\rho_{E}(R^{\sigma_{E}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y}))=\rho_{E}\big([\sigma_{E}(\underline{X}),\sigma_{E}(\underline{Y})]_{E}-\sigma_{E}([\underline{X},\underline{Y}]_{TM})\big)=0\,,

(213)

and hence
R^{\sigma_{E}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})\in\mathrm{ker}(\rho_{E})=\mathrm{im}(\rho_{E}^{*})
.
Using the inner product, one can package this vertical component into a 3-form
H\in\Omega^{3}(M)
defined by

H(\underline{X},\underline{Y},\underline{Z}):=\big\langle[\sigma_{E}(\underline{X}),\sigma_{E}(\underline{Y})]_{E},\sigma_{E}(\underline{Z})\big\rangle\,,\qquad\forall\underline{X},\underline{Y},\underline{Z}\in TM\,.

(214)

For an isotropic splitting (
211
),
H
is totally antisymmetric and one has the identity

[\sigma_{E}(\underline{X}),\sigma_{E}(\underline{Y})]_{E}=\sigma_{E}([\underline{X},\underline{Y}]_{TM})+\rho_{E}^{*}(i_{\underline{Y}}i_{\underline{X}}H)\,,

(215)

which makes explicit that
H
is the curvature of the exact Courant algebroid. Moreover,
H\in\Omega^{3}(M)
is a closed 3-form. The cohomology class
[H]\in H^{3}(M,\mathbb{R})
is the Ševera class, classifying exact Courant algebroids up to isomorphism.

Appendix B
Batalin–Vilkovisky (BV) Formalism and Quantization of Abelian 2-Form Gauge Fields

Following
[
22
]
, we present the BV quantization of a free theory of the Abelian two-form gauge field
B_{\mu\nu}
in spacetime dimensions
d\geqslant 6
. For
d=4
and
d=5
the free two-form field is dual to compact scalar and
U(1)
gauge field respectively, and it is hence economical to go to the dual frame and perform the quantization there in the conventional manner. For spacetime dimensions
d\leqslant 3
, the free two-form field cannot carry any local physical degrees of freedom.

On a local patch, the free theory of the Abelian 2-form
B_{\mu\nu}
is described by the classical action

S_{0}[B]=-\frac{1}{12}\int{\rm d}^{d}x\,H_{\mu\nu\rho}H^{\mu\nu\rho}\,,

(216)

expressed in terms of its field strength defined by
H_{\mu\nu\rho}=3\partial_{[\mu}B_{\nu\rho]}
.
H
and the action are invariant under the
reducible

[
21
]
gauge transformation

\delta B_{\mu\nu}=2\partial_{[\mu}\Lambda_{\nu]}\,,

(217)

where there are
gauge-for-gauge
transformations
\delta\Lambda_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\lambda
. This indicates that the gauge transformations (
217
) are not linearly independent.
The gauge parameter
\Lambda_{\mu}
has
n=d
components and
\lambda
has
m=1
components, for a total of
n-m=d-1
independent gauge symmetries. We will amply see that the gauge fixing procedure would respect exactly this number.

Since gauge-for-gauge transformations terminate at the first round, this type of gauge symmetry is called
first-stage reducible
according to the Batalin–Vilkovisky (BV) field-antifield formalism
[
20
,
21
]
. Whenever reducible gauge symmetries appear, the traditional BRST quantization is not sufficient and one needs to adopt the BV quantization. In the following we demonstrate how to apply the BV algorithm to
B_{\mu\nu}
.

Extended field space.

We introduce
ghost fields

\{C_{\mu},\,c\}
for gauge parameters
\Lambda_{\mu}
and
\lambda
.

Grassmann parity
for
B_{\mu\nu},\Lambda_{\mu}
and
\lambda
are
\epsilon(B_{\mu\nu})=\epsilon(\Lambda_{\mu})=\epsilon(\lambda)=0\mod 2
and as for the ghosts, they are always parity-opposed to their gauge parameters:
\epsilon(C)=\epsilon(\Lambda)+1
and
\epsilon(c)=\epsilon(\lambda)+1
. We have ghosts from two different stages. To distinguish between them, we introduce the
ghost number
denoted as
\gh(X)
for a field
X
. For our classical fields
X\in\{B_{\mu\nu},\Lambda_{\mu},\lambda\}
, we have
\gh(X)=0
whereas for the ghosts they are just the stage number plus one, i.e.,
\gh(C)=1
and
\gh(c)=2
.

We then need to double the space of field variables by assigning to each field
\Phi^{I}
(collective notation for the set of all fields) its
antifields

\Phi^{*}_{I}
.
The ghost number assignments and Grassmann parities of the antifields are related to their ordinary counterparts

\gh(\Phi^{*}_{I})=-\gh(\Phi^{I})-1\,,\quad\epsilon(\Phi^{*}_{I})=\epsilon(\Phi^{I})+1\quad\mod 2\,.

(218)

In addition, Grassmann parity also equals the ghost number modulo two.
In such a way, we arrive at the
minimal
sector

\{B_{\mu\nu}\,,C_{\mu}\,,c\,;B^{*\mu\nu}\,,C^{*\mu}\,,c^{*}\}\,.

(219)

The action
S_{0}[B]
is then augmented to the minimal BV action
S_{\text{Min}}[\Phi,\Phi^{*}]

depending on the original fields but also on the ghost fields and their antifields. It is a ghost number zero, even functional that should be a proper solution of the
classical master equation

(S_{\text{Min}},S_{\text{Min}})=0\,,

(220)

where the
antibracket

(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
on the extended field spaces is defined as
15
15

15

We use the superscripts “
L
” and “
R
” to indicate whether the functional derivative is acting from the left or from the right, respectively. For any function or functional

\phi

\delta X(\phi)=\delta\phi\frac{\delta^{L}X}{\delta\phi}=\frac{\delta^{R}X}{\delta\phi}\delta\phi
.

(X,Y)=\frac{\delta^{R}X}{\delta\Phi^{I}}\frac{\delta^{L}Y}{\delta\Phi^{*}_{I}}-\frac{\delta^{R}X}{\delta\Phi^{*}_{I}}\frac{\delta^{L}Y}{\delta\Phi^{I}}\,.

(221)

Moreover, it should satisfy the boundary condition:

S_{0}[B]=S_{\text{Min}}[\Phi,\Phi^{*}=0]\,.

(222)

In our case, minimal action reads

S_{\text{Min}}=\int{\rm d}^{d}x\,\left(-\frac{1}{12}H_{\mu\nu\rho}H^{\mu\nu\rho}+2B^{*\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}C_{\nu}+C^{*\mu}\partial_{\mu}c\right)\,.

To gauge-fix the gauge freedom of

and

(224)

Ghost numbers are as follows:

\begin{tabular}[]{c|cccccc|ccc}&$B_{\mu\nu}$&$C_{\mu}$&$\bar{C}^{\mu}$&$c$&$\bar{c}$&$\eta$&$b^{\mu}$&$b$&$\pi$\\

With these rules, the BV

BRST–BV gauge-fixing.

An appropriate

It is odd and of ghost number

\displaystyle S_{\delta}[\Phi^{I}]

\displaystyle=S_{\text{NM}}\left[\Phi^{I},\Phi^{*}_{I}=\frac{\delta\Psi}{\delta\Phi^{I}}\right]

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}^{d}x\Big(-\frac{1}{12}H_{\mu\nu\rho}H^{\mu\nu\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}-\partial_{\mu}\bar{c}\,\partial^{\mu}c

Russian formula and the BV–BRST complex.

A convenient way to encode at once the gauge symmetry (
217
), its first-stage reducibility, and the resulting BRST algebra is through the
Russian formula
. Conceptually, this identity is already the core input of the BRST description of ordinary (irreducible) gauge theories: it states that one can extend the de Rham differential
{\rm d}
by the BRST differential
{\rm s}
so that the curvature is totally horizontal, i.e. it has no ghost components. For a first-stage reducible system such as the Abelian two-form, the same idea persists but requires including the ghost-for-ghost; we may refer to the resulting identity as the
higher Russian formula
.

In the free Abelian case, the differentials
{\rm d}
and
{\rm s}
define a bicomplex

{\rm d}^{2}=0\,,\qquad{\rm s}^{2}=0\,,\qquad{\rm d}{\rm s}+{\rm s}{\rm d}=0\,,

(245)

called the BV–BRST complex. Introduce the extended field

\hat{B}\equiv B-C+c\,,

(246)

with
\gh(B,C,c)=(0,1,2)
. The (higher) Russian formula is the horizontality requirement

({\rm d}+{\rm s})\,(B-C+c)=H\equiv{\rm d}B\,,

(247)

namely the extended differential
{\rm d}+{\rm s}
has no ghost contributions in the curvature.
Expanding (
247
) by form degree (or equivalently by ghost number) gives

{\rm s}B-{\rm d}C+({\rm d}c-{\rm s}C)+{\rm s}c=0\,,

(248)

and since the three bracketed terms have different form degrees, they must vanish separately:

{\rm s}B={\rm d}C\,,\qquad{\rm s}C={\rm d}c\,,\qquad{\rm s}c=0\,.

(249)

Thus, imposing the higher Russian formula (
247
) automatically generates the BRST transformations, while the BV master action constructed above provides their canonical realization via antifields.
