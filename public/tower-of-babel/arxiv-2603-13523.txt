Bogomolov property for modular Galois representations with nontrivial nebentypus
Pietro Piras

Abstract
. A field in which the (logarithmic) Weil height is bounded from below by a strictly positive constant is said to have the Bogomolov property (property (B)). Given a normalized eigenform f ∈ S k ​ ( Γ 0 ​ ( N ) ) f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N)) Amoroso and Terracini [ 3 ] proved (B) for the field “cut out” by the adelic representation associated to f f under some assumptions on f f , generalizing the earlier work of Habegger [ 21 ] on elliptic curves. In this paper we extend this result to the case of normalized eigenforms with nontrivial nebentypus character. We also introduce the notion of ADZ field, inspired by earlier work of [ 1 ] [ 1 ] , exhibiting a class of fields in which property (B) is preserved under (arbitrary) composition.



1.
Introduction

Let
h:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the (absolute, logarithmic) Weil height (see definition
3.1
). In the seminal paper
[
8
]
the authors define the
Bogomolov property
for any set
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
:

A set
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has the Bogomolov property (property (B) for short) if there is a constant
C>0
such that for all nonzero elements
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
that are not roots of unity we have
h(\alpha)>C
.

In the literature property (B) has been extensively investigated when
\mathcal{A}
is a Galois extension of the rationals (but see
[
15
]
for a very recent result of property (B) for non-Galois extensions). The question of whether a field has property (B) is related to an open conjecture of Lehmer which asks to find an constant
C>0
such that for all nonzero
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
that are not roots of unity one has

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{C}{\deg\alpha}.

Fields in which Lehmer’s conjecture is true are said to have the Lehmer property and of course having property (B) is stronger than having the Lehmer property (conjecturally
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has the Lehmer property but it does not have (B) as the sequence
\{2^{1/n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
shows). For a review of Lehmer’s problem see Smyth’s excellent survey
[
36
]
. Lehmer’s conjecture has been generalized by Rémond in the following

Conjecture

(
[
29
]
, Conjecture 3.4)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated subgroup of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}
and let
\Gamma_{div}=\{\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}\mid\gamma^{n}\in\Gamma\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be its division group. There is a positive constant
c
such that

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{c}{[\mathbb{Q}(\Gamma_{\operatorname{div}},\alpha):\mathbb{Q}(\Gamma_{\operatorname{div}})]}.

For more on Rémond’s conjecture see for example
[
29
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
. We now give a list of some fields with property (B).

(1)

By a theorem of Northcott (see
[
7
, Theorem 1.6.8.]
for example), given an integer
d>0
and
h_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, for any number field
\mathbb{K}
there are only a finite number of elements of degree less than
d
and height less than

. This implies that any number field has property (B).

(2)

In
[
32
]
Schinzel
[
32
]
showed that the field of totally real numbers
\mathbb{Q}^{tr}
has (B). This was generalized by Garza in
[

]
to a lower bound on the height of algebraic numbers with at least one real conjugate. Moreover one can prove Schinzel’s result using Bilu’s equidistribution theorem
[
6
]
which roughly states that points of small height equidistribute (in a weak sense) on the unit circle inside the complex numbers.

(3)

In
[
8
]
the authors show that if
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{Q}
is a field with bounded local degree at some nonempty set of rational primes, then
\mathbb{L}
has (B). This is done by proving an inequality on the discriminant of an algebraic number
\alpha\in\mathbb{L}
relating the ramification index, the residual degree and the normalized variance

V_{p}(\alpha;\mathbb{L}):=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\cup\infty}\left(N_{x}-\frac{m}{q+1}\right)

where
N_{x}
is the number of conjugates of
\alpha
that have the same reduction modulo the prime ideal above
p
.

(4)

The maximal abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
in
[
2
]
and more generally for number fields
[
4
]
.

(5)

Galois extensions of number fields with Galois group with finite index center (
[

).

(6)

A celebrated result of Habegger
[

states that the field
\mathbb{Q}(E_{tors})
obtained by adjoining the coordinates of an elliptic curve

\mathbb{Q}
has (B).

(7)

Recently in
[

the authors define property (B) for continuous morphisms of topological groups reframing previous results in the literature (see section
3
). In the same article they show (B) for representations coming from modular forms under some conditions, generalizing
[
21
]
.

(8)

In
[
12
]
the authors prove property (B) for representation

\rho:G_{\mathbb{K}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

with
\mathbb{K}
a number field, such that
\rho
maps an inertia subgroup at a prime above
p
surjectively onto an open subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. With this result they provide many new examples of fields with property (B).

The goal to this paper is to improve the main result of
[
3
]
where the authors show that under some assumptions, given a compatible system of representations
\{\rho_{f,v}\}
associated to a newform
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
with
k\geq 2
the field cut out by the product representation

\widehat{\rho}_{f}=\prod_{v}\rho_{f,v}

has property (B). Here we show an analogous result assuming nontrivial nebentypus. We give now the statement of the main theorem in a qualitative version (see Theorem
4.3
for the precise statement)

Theorem
.

Let
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
a normalized eigenform with
q
-expansion

f=\sum a_{n}q^{n}.

If
p
is a prime outside the level such that
a_{p}=0
and the mod
p
representation associated to
f
is big, then the adelic representation

\widehat{\rho}_{f}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{f}).

has property (B).

The main difficulty to proving this theorem is the existence of inner twists of
f
that complicate the image of the representation. To resolve this issue one uses that, up to passing to a finite extension of the coefficient field of
f
, for big enough
p
the image of the representation at
p
is controlled. In fact one shows that a finite extension of the field cut out by the representation has (B). One could ask for the case
k=1
but in that case one does not have a compatible system of representations but instead one complex representation; as is well known the image is finite so the field cut out by the representation is finite and (B) is guaranteed by Northcott’s theorem.

To study the representation outside of
p
we introduce the notion of ADZ field at a place
v
, inspired by
[
1
]
. Given a number field
\mathbb{K}
, a Galois extension
\mathbb{F}/\mathbb{K}
is ADZ at a place
v
of
\mathbb{K}
if the field fixed by the center of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}/\mathbb{K})
has (uniformly) bounded local degrees over
v
. In
[
1
, Theorem 1.5]
the authors show that ADZ fields have (B)
uniformly
in
v
,
[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{Q}]
and the bound
d_{0}
of the local degrees, but this property has not been further investigated. It turns out that being ADZ is preserved with respect to the compositum i.e. that the compositum of ADZ fields at the same place is again ADZ; this is in stark contrast with the general bad behavior of property (B) under the compositum of fields where even finite extensions of (infinite) fields with (B) need not have (B) (see for example
[
1
, Theorem 5.3]
). The proof is entirely group-theoretic and rests on the fact that one can precisely describe the center of the Galois group of compositum in terms of the centers of the other Galois groups (Lemma
5.6
). Moreover, since there are only a finite number of extension of bounded degree of a local field, property ADZ is preserved under
arbitrary
compositum of ADZ fields with uniformly bounded local degrees (Theorem
5.8
). Using ADZ fields one shows (Proposition
5.9
) that the representation outside of
p
has (B) when restricted to an ADZ field at a place above
p
and in particular to every number field. Fields with ADZ property seem therefore to be a well behaved class of fields with respect to property (B); for example, since every abelian extension
\mathbb{L}
of
\mathbb{K}
is ADZ (at every place of
\mathbb{K}
), the compositum of
\mathbb{L}
with an abelian extension is ADZ and has therefore (B) (see remark
5.10
). Following the previously cited works of
[
2
]
and
[
1
]
we conjecture that the maximal abelian extension of an ADZ field has (B).

2.
The Deligne-Shimura construction

Let

and level

with an action of the absolute Galois group

is the
\ell

. Since

) one gets a homomorphism

G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{K}_{f}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})

i.e. a representation of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
into
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{K}_{f}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
. Since
\mathbb{K}_{f}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\simeq\prod_{\lambda\mid\ell}\mathbb{K}_{f,\lambda}
(
[
13
, Chapter 9.2]
) where
\mathbb{K}_{f,\lambda}
is the completion of
\mathbb{K}_{f}
at the prime
\lambda
above
\ell
, one can then specify to each
\mathbb{K}_{f,\lambda}
to get for each
\lambda

\rho_{f,\lambda}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{K}_{f,\lambda}).

From each
\rho_{f,\lambda}
one constructs representations with value in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f,\lambda})
(see
[
13
, Proposition 9.9.5]
) denoted still by
\rho_{f,\lambda}
. The set
\{\rho_{f,\lambda}\}

is called a

. Let

be a place of
\mathbb{K}_{f}
above the rational prime

(or equivalently a prime in
\mathcal{O}_{f}
above
p_{v}
). The representation
\rho_{f,v}
has the following properties (see
[
13
, Theorem 9.6.5]
)

(1)

It is continuous and unramified at primes
\ell
that do not divide
Np_{v}
(we will say that it is unramified

Np_{v}
).

(2)

For each
\ell

and Frobenius element

\ell

Recall that by the Chebotarev density theorem, for an algebraic Galois extension

the set of Frobenius elements is dense in

(with the Krull topology). As a consequence one can show that if two (continuous) functions defined on the Galois group agree on the set of Frobenii, then they coincide. Let
\varepsilon_{\ell}
be the
\ell

where

denotes the profinite completion of

3.
Property (B) for morphisms

We recall now the fundamental definitions.

Definition 3.1
.

The (logarithmic) Weil height (on
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
) is the function
h:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
defined as

h(\alpha)=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{v\in M_{K}}[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]\log\max\bigl\{|\alpha|_{v},1\bigr\}

for all nonzero algebraic
\alpha
, where
K
is any number field that contains
\alpha
,
M_{K}
is the set of places of
K
and
K_{v}
the completion of
K
with respect to the metric induced by
v
.

We list some properties of the height (see
[
37
]
).

Proposition 3.2
.

For
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
the Weil height satisfies the following properties

(1)

h(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n})\leq h(x_{1})+\cdots+h(x_{n})+\log n
.

(2)

h(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})\leq h(x_{1})+\cdots+h(x_{n})
.

(3)

h(x^{m})=|m|h(x)
for any
x\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
(so
h(1/x)=h(x)
for all nonzero
x\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
).

(4)

For a polynomial
F\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[t]
of degree
m
and any
x\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
h(F(x))=mh(x)+O_{F}(1)
.

(5)

h(\sigma(x))=h(x)
for each
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

(6)

For any real numbers
C_{1},C_{2}>0
there are only finitely many algebraic numbers
x
with
\deg(x)\leq C_{1}
and
h(x)\leq C_{2}
(Northcott’s theorem).

(7)

We have
h(\alpha)=0
if and only if
\alpha
is a root of unity.

We say that an algebraic extension
\mathbb{K}
of
\mathbb{Q}
has the
Bogomolov property
(property
\rm(B)
for short) if the Weil height is bounded from above by a positive constant, outside the roots of unity i.e. there exists some
C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}\setminus\mu(\mathbb{K})
we have

h(x)\geq C

where
\mu(\mathbb{K})
denotes the roots of unity in
\mathbb{K}
. The definition of property
\rm(B)
has been applied to continuous homomorphisms of topological groups.

\widehat{\rho}_{f}
i.e.

\rho^{(p)}_{f}=\prod_{v\nmid p}\rho_{f,v}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\prod_{v\nmid p}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f,v}).

If
f
is CM then
\mathbb{Q}(\widehat{\rho}_{f})
is an abelian extension of a quadratic number field, so it has (B) by
[
4
]
. For this reason
f
will always be assumed without complex multiplication
. The main result of
[
3
]
was conditional on the existence of a prime
p
such that the image of
\rho_{f,p}
is

\widehat{G}(\mathcal{O}_{f}/p\mathcal{O}_{f})=\{u\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f}/p\mathcal{O}_{f})\mid\det u\in(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*})^{k-1}\}.

In particular for that prime
p
the image of
\rho_{f,p}
contains
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f}/p\mathcal{O}_{f})
which is used to prove the “normal closure lemma”
[
3
, Proposition 5.1.]
. In the case of non trivial nebentypus some problems arise due to the existence of
inner twists
of
f
that complicate the image of
\rho_{f,p}
. However, as we will describe, as long as one considers a normal subgroup of finite index of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
corresponding to a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
where the character is trivial, the image is known and has been studied by Momose, Papier and Ribet. We give now a more detailed explanation of this and of the general results about inner twists following the exposition of
[
31
]
.

Inner twists

Let
f
be as before and denote by
\mathbb{E}
be the field generated over
\mathbb{Q}
by the coefficients of the
q
-expansion of
f
. Let
\gamma
be an automorphism of
\mathbb{E}
and consider the newform “twisted” by
\gamma

f^{\gamma}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\gamma(a_{n})q^{n}.

This is again a newform (see
[
30
]
end of page 21) of the same level with nebentypus
\chi^{\gamma}
. There might exist a Dirichlet character
\chi
that satisfies for almost all primes
p
the relation
\gamma(a_{p})=\chi(p)a_{p}
. In general if
\chi
is a Dirichlet character we write
f\otimes\chi
for the modular form twisted by the character as

f\otimes\chi=\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi(n)a_{n}q^{n}.

The existence of the character
\chi_{\gamma}
associated to the automorphism
\gamma
can be rewritten as

(4.1)

f^{\gamma}=f\otimes\chi_{\gamma}.

Given
\gamma
, if this character exists it is unique (recall that in the beginning of section 4 we have assumed that
f
is without complex multiplication) and it will be denoted by
\chi_{\gamma}
. Let now
\Gamma=\{\gamma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{E})\mid\gamma(a_{p})=\chi_{\gamma}(p)a_{p}\text{ for almost all $p$}\}
i.e. the group of automorphisms of
\mathbb{E}
that admit a character
\chi_{\gamma}
as above. Let
\Omega
be the set of Dirichlet characters
\chi_{\gamma}
associated to the automorphisms
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Remark 4.1
.

Recall that if
\chi
is the (nontrivial) nebentypus of
f
, we have that

a_{p}=\overline{a_{p}}\chi(p)

where
\overline{a_{p}}
denotes the complex conjugation of
a_{p}
, for all primes
p
not dividing
N
(see
[
30
, Section 1]
). This means that
\overline{\chi}
(and also
\chi
) belongs in
\Omega
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be the subfield of
\mathbb{E}
fixed by
\Gamma
and
\mathbb{K}
be the abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
fixed by all the Dirichlet characters associated to the twists. Except for finitely many primes the image of
G_{\mathbb{K}}
under
\rho_{f,p}
lies in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p})

[
31
, Theorem 3.1]
and is exactly the group

(4.2)

\widehat{G}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}):=\{\gamma\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p})\mid\det(\gamma)\in(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})^{k-1})\}.

In particular letting
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{O}_{f}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the image of
\rho_{f,p}
mod
p
contains
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{A}/p\mathcal{A})
. This is analogous to what happens in
[
3
, Remark 2.2]
where
\det(\rho(\operatorname{Frob}_{\ell}))=\ell^{k-1}
for all
\ell\nmid Np
since the nebentypus is trivial.

Remark 4.2
.

As written above if
\gamma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{Q})
then
f^{\gamma}
is again a newform of the same level. If
\gamma
is an inner twist then
f^{\gamma}=f\otimes\chi_{\gamma}
and for all places
v
we have

(4.3)

{\rho}_{f^{\gamma},v}\simeq{\rho}_{f,v}\otimes\chi_{\gamma}.

If
v
is above
\ell\nmid Np
the representation at
v
is unramified at
p
. Since both
\rho_{f^{\gamma},v}(I_{p})
and
\rho_{f,v}(I_{p})
are trivial, by (
4.3
) we have that
\chi_{\gamma}(I_{p})
is also trivial i.e.
\chi_{\gamma}
is unramified at
p
. The field
\mathbb{K}
fixed by all the characters associated to the twists is therefore unramified at
p
.

We can now state the main theorem of our paper. Assume now that
f
and
p
satisfy the following

Assumption 1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{O}_{f}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

(P0)

p\nmid N
;

(P1)

a_{p}=0
;

(P2)

The image of
\rho_{f,p}
mod
p
contains
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{A}/p\mathcal{A})
.

(P3)

p\geq 5,\ p\nmid k-1
and
\frac{p+1}{2}\nmid k-1
.

Our main theorem is

Theorem 4.3
.

Under the above conditions the representation

\widehat{\rho}_{f}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{f}).

has property (B).

This theorem improves the main result of
[
3
]
where one assumes that
f
has trivial nebentypus. We now briefly discuss
Assumption 1
. As was noted in the paragraph about the Deligne-Shimura construction, the characteristic polynomial of a Frobenius element at a prime above
p

that does not divide the level
N
has characteristic polynomial
X^{2}-a_{p}(f)X+\chi(p)p^{k-1}
. This motivates (P0) as we have control over the Frobenius elements over the primes outside of
N
. The Assumption (P1) is motivated by the fact that if
a_{p}(f)=0
and
v
a place above
p
, the representation
\rho_{f,v}
restricted to
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is crystalline according to
[
33
]
, so one can use the classification of Breuil of crystalline representations
[
9
, Proposition 3.1.1]
and conclude that it is of type
V_{k,a_{p},\omega}
. Moreover this classification states the representation is induced precisely when
a_{p}=0

[
9
, Proposition 3.1.2]
. Finally (P1) is used in Proposition
5.9
to show, by looking at its characteristic polynomial, that the image of the square of a Frobenius element at
p
under
\rho_{f}^{(p)}
is scalar. In the context of elliptic curves as in
[
21
]
this assumption was the existence of a supersingular prime for an elliptic curve
E
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. This is guaranteed by a theorem of Elkies
[
16
]
that states that for a rational elliptic curve there are actually infinitely many supersingular primes. Elkies improved his theorem to the case of number fields with at least one real embedding (see
[
17
]
) but for a general number field the result is unknown. The assumption on supersingular primes was used by Habegger in combination with an open image theorem of Serre
[
34
]
to guarantee the existence of infinitely many supersingular primes for
E
such that the representation

G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(E[p])

is surjective. The Assumption (P2) has been already discussed in the paragraph about inner twists in section 4. Let us mention that it is the analogue of Serre’s theorem in our context of representations associated to modular forms and as in
[
3
]
it shall be heavily used in the normal closure Lemma
5.19
. The last Assumption (P3) is also used in the proof of the normal closure lemma and to guarantee that the representation
V_{k,0,\omega}
is absolutely irreducible.

5.
Proof of the main result

From now on we fix the prime
p
of assumption
1
and an embedding
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. The proof of Theorem
4.3
is structured in the following way. First one proves that the representation
\rho_{f}^{(p)}
has (B). This is the part “outside of
p
” of
\widehat{\rho}_{f}
and to show (B) one uses the characteristic polynomial to prove that the square of a Frobenius element is central. After that one deals with the part “inside of
p
” i.e. the
p
-adic representation
\rho_{f,p}
. This is more challenging and one has to use the fact that
\rho_{f,p}
is a crystalline representation by a classification due to Breuil. Finally one merges the two representations using Theorem
5.3
which is a slight generalization of
[
3
, Proposition 3.4]
allowing the base field to be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.
We give an explanation of the proof of the “merging” Theorem
5.3
. Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field and
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
a infinite Galois extension. Any element in
\mathbb{L}
is contained in a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{K}
, so if we suppose that
\mathbb{L}
is a union of finite Galois extensions
\mathbb{L}_{n}
such that the height in each
\mathbb{L}_{n}
is bounded from below by a constant that does not depend on
n
, then that bound will also work for the whole field
\mathbb{L}
. The problem is then to give sufficient conditions on each
\mathbb{L}_{n}
to have a uniform lower bound. The starting point is the following slight generalization of
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
be a finite Galois extension of a number field
\mathbb{K}
and let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K})
. Let
a
,
b\geq 1
be rational integers,
\rho\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
S
a set of places of
\mathbb{L}
above a fixed prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{K}
. If for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}
and
v\in S
we have

|\gamma^{a}-\sigma(\gamma)^{b}|_{v}\leq p^{-\rho}\;

then for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{L}
such that
\alpha^{a}\neq\sigma(\alpha)^{b}
we have

(5.1)

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{1}{a+b}\left(|S|\frac{[\mathbb{L}_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{Q}]}\rho\log p-\log 2\right).

where
v
is any place in
S
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{L}
and
v\in S
. By
[
2
, Lemma 1]
there exists an integer
\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}
such that
\alpha\beta
is an integer and

|\beta|_{v}=\max\{1,|\alpha|_{v}\}^{-1}.

Then we have
|(\alpha\beta)^{a}-\sigma(\alpha\beta)^{b}|_{v}\leq p^{-\rho}
and
|\beta^{a}-\sigma(\beta)^{b}|_{v}\leq p^{-\rho}
by our assumptions. Using the ultrametric inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle|\alpha^{a}-\sigma(\alpha)^{b}|_{v}

\displaystyle=|\beta|_{v}^{-a}|(\alpha\beta)^{a}-\sigma(\alpha\beta)^{b}+(\sigma(\beta)^{b}-\beta^{a})\sigma(\alpha)^{b}|_{v}

\displaystyle\leq c(v)\max(1,|\alpha|_{v})^{a}\max(1,|\sigma(\alpha)|_{v})^{b}

with
c(v)=p^{-\rho}
for all
v\in S
. Moreover the last inequality holds for an arbitrary place
w
of
\mathbb{L}
with

c(w)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }w\nmid\infty,w\notin S\\
2&\text{ if }w\mid\infty.\end{cases}

Since
\alpha^{a}-\sigma(\alpha)^{b}
is not zero, by applying the product formula we get:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{v}\frac{[\mathbb{L}_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]}{[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{Q}]}\log|\alpha^{a}-\sigma(\alpha)^{b}|_{v}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v}\frac{[\mathbb{L}_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]}{[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{Q}]}\left(\log c(v)+a\log\max\{1,|\alpha|_{v}\}+b\log\max\{1,|\sigma(\alpha)|_{v}\}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{v\mid\infty}\frac{[\mathbb{L}_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]}{[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{Q}]}\right)\log 2-\left(\sum_{v\in S}\frac{[\mathbb{L}_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{Q}]}\right)\rho\log p+ah(\alpha)+bh(\sigma(\alpha))

\displaystyle=\log 2+(a+b)h(\alpha)-\rho\log p\sum_{v\in S}\frac{[\mathbb{L}_{v}:\mathbb{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}][\mathbb{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{Q}]}.

Since
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
is Galois
[\mathbb{L}_{v}:\mathbb{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}]
is constant for all
v
above
\mathfrak{p}
so

\sum_{v\in S}\frac{[\mathbb{L}_{v}:\mathbb{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}][\mathbb{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{Q}]}=\frac{|S|[\mathbb{L}_{v}:\mathbb{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}][\mathbb{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{Q}]}=\frac{|S|[\mathbb{L}_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{Q}]}.

We have then the bound

\displaystyle h(\alpha)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{a+b}\left(\rho\frac{|S|[\mathbb{L}_{v}:\mathbb{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}][\mathbb{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{Q}]}\log p-\log 2\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a+b}\left(|S|\frac{[\mathbb{L}_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{Q}]}\rho\log p-\log 2\right).

∎

As a corollary we recover
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
by choosing
S
to be the set of all places of
\mathbb{L}
above
\mathfrak{p}
. Note that the previous lemma is a bound only for the special elements
\alpha
such that
\alpha^{a}\neq\sigma(\alpha)^{b}
because otherwise one cannot apply the product formula. This will be circumvented using assumption
2
.
Let us now come back to the previous setting of the infinite extension
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
. We want to write
\mathbb{L}
as a union of finite Galois extensions
\mathbb{L}_{n}
of
\mathbb{K}
with controlled height so that the union
\mathbb{L}
will have (B). Fix now an
\mathbb{L}_{n}
and suppose that there exists a
\sigma_{n}\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{L}_{n}/\mathbb{K})
such that for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}_{n}}
and all places
v_{n}
of
\mathbb{L}_{n}
over a fixed prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{K}
we have

(5.2)

|\gamma^{a}-\sigma_{n}(\gamma)^{b}|_{v_{n}}\leq p^{-{\rho_{n}}}

for some positive integers
a,b
and
\rho_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Putting
\mathbb{L}_{n}
in (
5.1
) with
S
the set of all places
v_{n}
of
\mathbb{L}_{n}
over
\mathfrak{p}
we have for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{L}_{n}
the bound

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{1}{a+b}\left(\frac{[\mathbb{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{Q}]}\rho_{n}\log p-\log 2\right).

holds for all
\alpha
such that
\sigma_{n}(\alpha)^{b}\neq\alpha^{a}
. To have a bound on every element of
\mathbb{L}_{n}
we need to produce for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{L}_{n}
an element
\beta\in\mathbb{L}_{n}
such that
h(\alpha)\geq Ch(\beta)
, for some constant
C>0
independent of
\alpha
, and such that
\beta^{a}\neq\sigma_{n}(\beta)^{b}
. For this we will choose
\beta=\tau(\alpha)/\alpha^{g}
for some special automorphism
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K})
and integer
g>1
. The element
\beta
will also have the property that
\beta^{\prime}/\beta
will never be a
p^{\infty}
-root of unity for each conjugate
\beta^{\prime}
of
\beta
different from
\beta
. Lemma
5.1
provides a bound on the height of
\beta
(and therefore on
\alpha
) that depends on
n
. To eliminate this dependency first notice that if we put
a=b=1
, equation (
5.2
) says that
\sigma_{n}
belongs to some ramification group for the places
v_{n}
of
\mathbb{L}_{n}
above
\mathfrak{p}
; moreover the condition
\beta^{a}\neq\sigma_{n}(\beta)^{b}
becomes
\sigma_{n}(\beta)\neq\beta
. To guarantee the existence of such
\sigma_{n}
we can choose each
\mathbb{L}_{n}
to be the field fixed by the normal closure of some ramification group inside
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{L}_{n+1}/\mathbb{K})
. Note that by choosing the normal closure, each
\mathbb{L}_{n}
will be a Galois extension of
\mathbb{K}
. Taking
\sigma_{n}
in the last nontrivial ramification group
H_{n}
of
\mathbb{L}_{n}/\mathbb{K}
with index
i_{n}
we have the bound

|\sigma_{n}(\gamma)-\gamma|_{v}\leq p^{-\frac{(i_{n}+1)}{e(\mathfrak{p}|p)e_{n}}}

where
e(\mathfrak{p}|p)
and
e_{n}
are the ramification indices of
p
in
\mathbb{K}
and of
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathbb{L}_{n}
. If the quantities
{(i_{n}+1)}/{e_{n}}
and
|\overline{H_{n}}|
are bounded by constants independent of
n
we can use the following “acceleration” lemma (this is why we need that
\beta^{\prime}/\beta
is never a
p^{\infty}
-root of unity).

Lemma 5.2

(Lemma 2.1,
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field,
v
a finite place of
\mathbb{K}
over a rational prime
p
and
\rho>0
. Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
be such that
|\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}|_{v}\leq p^{-\rho}
. Then for any non-negative integer
\lambda
we have
|\gamma_{1}^{p^{\lambda}}-\gamma_{2}^{p^{\lambda}}|_{v}\leq p^{-s_{p,\rho}(\lambda)}
with
s_{p,\rho}(\lambda)\rightarrow+\infty
as
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
.

We find then a big enough positive
\lambda
such that

|\sigma_{n}(\gamma^{p^{\lambda}})-\gamma^{p^{\lambda}}|_{v}\leq p^{-|\overline{H_{n}}|}

for all
n
at once. Taking
\phi\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{L}_{n}/\mathbb{Q})
, since
|\phi^{-1}(x)|_{v}=|x|_{\phi(v)}
the previous bound will work for all the places in the orbit of
v
under the action of any
\phi
commuting with
\sigma_{n}
. Choosing
S
as the orbit of this action and bounding its size, we apply Lemma
5.1
to deduce

h(\beta)\geq\frac{\log(p/2)}{2p^{\lambda}}.

5.1.
Hypotheses and proof of the merging theorem

We state now the assumptions needed to apply the strategy described previously. Consider a general Galois extension
\mathbb{L}
of a number field
\mathbb{K}
.

Assumption 2
.

There exists
\tau\in G_{\mathbb{K}}\cap I
, where
I
is an inertia group over the prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{K}
,
such that

\varepsilon_{p}(\tau)=g\text{ with }g\in\mathbb{Z},g>1

and
\tau_{\mid\mathbb{L}}\in Z(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}))
(this element is called a
central element
).

Assumption 3
.

There exist
C_{1},C_{2}\geq 1
with the following properties. There exists a sequence of finite Galois extensions
\left(\mathbb{L}_{n}/\mathbb{K}\right)_{n\geq 0}

\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{L}_{0}\subsetneq\mathbb{L}_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathbb{L}_{n}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathbb{L},\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{L}=\bigcup_{n}\mathbb{L}_{n}

such that for
n\geq 1
the extension
\mathbb{L}_{n}/\mathbb{K}
is ramified over
\mathfrak{p}
. Moreover, let
e_{n}\geq 1
be the ramification index,
\left(G_{i}^{n}\right)_{i}
the sequence of ramification groups of
\mathbb{L}_{n}/\mathbb{K}
at a place over
\mathfrak{p}
. We further denote by
H_{n}=G_{i_{n}}^{n}
the last nontrivial ramification group and
\overline{H_{n}}
its normal closure in
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{L}_{n}/\mathbb{K}\right)
. Then

\frac{e_{n}}{i_{n}+1}\leq C_{1},\quad|\overline{H_{n}}|\leq C_{2}

and

\mathbb{L}_{n}^{\overline{H_{n}}}=\mathbb{L}_{n-1}.

With these hypotheses we can prove the following

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field and
\mathbb{F}/\mathbb{K}
and
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
two (possibly infinite) Galois extensions and suppose that:

(1)

The field
\mathbb{F}
is unramified at a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{K}
over
p
and satisfies property (B).

(2)

The field
\mathbb{L}
satisfies assumptions
2
and
3
.

The field
\mathbb{FL}
has property (B).

The unramifiedness of
\mathbb{F}
at
\mathfrak{p}
is required to control the ramification of
\mathbb{FL}
. The following proposition states that if
assumption 2
is satisfied then providing a lower bound for some special elements of
\mathbb{L}
is sufficient for a global bound of
\mathbb{L}
. The proof is similar to
[
1
, Proposition 4.2]
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mathbb{L}
be a Galois extension of a number field
\mathbb{K}
that satisfies
assumption 2
. For each nonzero
\alpha\in\mathbb{L}
that is not a root of unity, there exists an element
\beta
such that
h(\alpha)\geq h(\beta)
and for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K})
that do not fix
\beta
, we have that
\sigma(\beta)/\beta
is not a root of unity of order a power of
p
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{L}
not a root of unity and define
\beta=\tau(\alpha)/\alpha^{g}
where
\tau
and
g
are given by
assumption 2
. Observe that if
\beta
were a root of unity then we would have

(5.3)

h(\alpha)=h(\tau(\alpha))=h(\beta\alpha^{g})=h(\alpha^{g})=gh(\alpha)

and since
g>1
the equation (
5.3
) would imply that
h(\alpha)=0
which is a contradiction since
\alpha
is not a root of unity. Observe also that

h(\beta)=h(\tau(\alpha)\alpha^{-g})\leq h(\tau(\alpha))+gh(\alpha)=(g+1)h(\alpha).

This means that
h(\alpha)\geq{h(\beta)}/(g+1)
. Since
\mathbb{K}
is a number field then it has (B) by Northcott’s theorem, so if
\beta\in\mathbb{K}
then
h(\beta)\geq c_{0}
for some constant
c_{0}>0
. If
\beta\notin\mathbb{K}
there exists
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K})
such that
\sigma(\beta)\neq\beta
and since
\tau
is central we have

\frac{\sigma(\beta)}{\beta}=\frac{{\sigma}\tau(\alpha)}{\tau(\alpha)}\left(\frac{{\sigma}(\alpha^{g})}{\alpha^{g}}\right)^{-1}=\frac{\tau(\eta)}{\eta^{g}}

where
\eta={{\sigma}(\alpha)}/{\alpha}\in\mathbb{L}
. Now if
{\sigma(\beta)}/{\beta}
was a
p
-root of unity, its height would be zero and since
g>1
,
\eta
would be a root of unity since
h(\eta)=0
. Writing
\eta=\eta_{1}\eta_{2}
with
\eta_{1}
a
p
-root of unity and
\eta_{2}
with order not divisible by
p
we have
\tau(\eta_{1})/\tau(\eta_{1}^{g})=1
since
\varepsilon_{p}(\tau)=g
. This implies that
\sigma(\beta)/\beta=\tau(\eta_{2})/\tau(\eta_{2}^{g})
has order not divisible by
p
which is a contradiction. This means that
{\sigma(\beta)}/{\beta}
is not a
p
-root of unity.
∎

We are ready now to prove Theorem
5.3
.

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

Let

5.2.
Outside of

In this section we deal with the representation outside of
p
. We start with the following definition which was inspired by
[
1

.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field and
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
a Galois extension of
\mathbb{K}
with Galois group
G
. We say that
\mathbb{L}
has the
ADZ property
or that it is a
ADZ field
if the extension
\mathbb{L}^{Z(G)}/\mathbb{K}
has bounded local degrees over some non Archimedean place
v
of
\mathbb{K}
. We will say that a field has ADZ or that is an ADZ field
at
v
to specify the place.

This definition stems from one of the main results of
[
1
]
where it is shown that property ADZ implies property (B) (see
[
1
, Theorem 1.5]
). In this section we will prove that ADZ is preserved under compositum and therefore we exhibit a new class of fields that have (B) that is closed under compositum i.e. taking the compositum also has (B). Before proving this we first have a useful lemma on the center of the fiber product of groups.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
{G}_{i\in I}
be a family of groups and
\{\phi_{i}:G_{i}\rightarrow H\}_{i\in I}
a family of surjective morphisms onto some fixed group
H
. If

G=G_{1}\times_{H}G_{2}\times_{H}\cdots=\left\{(g_{i})\in\prod_{i\in I}G_{i}\mid\phi_{i}(g_{i})=\phi_{j}(g_{j})\text{ for all }i,j\in I\right\}

is the fiber product over
H
, then

Z(G)=\{(g_{i})\in G\mid g_{i}\in Z(G_{i})\}.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{i}:G\rightarrow G_{i}
be the projection on the
i
-th factor. If an element
g\in G
is such that
\pi_{i}(g)\in Z(G_{i})
for all
i\in I
then it clearly lies in the center of
G
. On the other hand let
g\in Z(G)
. Fix an arbitrary element
h_{i}\in G_{i}
, we will show that
\pi_{i}(g)\in Z(G_{i})
for all
i\in I
by showing that it commutes with
h_{i}
. Since each
\phi_{j}
is surjective onto
H
we can find an element
g^{\prime}\in G
such that
\phi_{j}(\pi_{j}(g^{\prime}))=\phi_{i}(h_{i})
and
\pi_{i}(g^{\prime})=h_{i}
. We have then

g^{\prime}\cdot g\cdot(g^{\prime})^{-1}=g

since
g\in Z(G)
and taking
\pi_{i}
we have that
h_{i}g_{i}h_{i}^{-1}=g_{i}
. The claim follows.
∎

We now state a lemma about the field fixed by the center of a compositum of fields. Roughly it states that “the field fixed by the center of the compositum is the compositum of the fields fixed by the centers”.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\{\mathbb{L}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of Galois extension of a number field
\mathbb{K}
with Galois group
G_{i}
lying in a common algebraic closure. Denote by
\mathbb{L}
the compositum of all the
\mathbb{L}_{i}
and
G
its Galois group over
\mathbb{K}
. We have that
\mathbb{L}^{Z(G)}
equals the compositum of the fixed fields
\mathbb{L}_{i}^{Z(G_{i})}
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{i}:G\rightarrow G_{i}
denote the restriction map and
H=\operatorname{Gal}(\cap_{i\in I}\mathbb{L}_{i}/\mathbb{K})
. Each
\mathbb{L}_{i}
corresponds to the subfield of
\mathbb{L}
fixed by
\pi_{i}^{-1}(G_{i})
i.e. we have
\mathbb{L}_{i}=\mathbb{L}^{\pi_{i}^{-1}(G_{i})}
for all

. Since the compositum of the
\mathbb{L}^{\pi_{i}^{-1}(Z(G_{i}))}
is the smallest field that contains them, the subgroup of

corresponding to it is the intersection of all the
\pi_{i}^{-1}(Z(G_{i}))
. We have

\bigcap_{i\in I}\pi_{i}^{-1}(Z(G_{i}))=\{(g_{j})\in G\mid g_{i}\in Z(G_{i})\text{ for all }i\in I\}=Z(G)

where the last inequality comes from Lemma
5.6
.
∎

We are now ready to prove that the compositum of ADZ fields is again ADZ.

Let

that are all ADZ at a place

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{L}
be the compositum of all the

and put

and

is the compositum of all the

7.
Examples

We discuss now some examples. One of the main difficulties in producing examples is to find
f
such that (P2) holds for some prime
p
. The main tool that we use is an unpublished result of Mascot
[
23
]
that we describe briefly. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime above a rational prime
p
in the ring of integers of the coefficient field of
f
. Given a newform
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
and the associated representation
\rho_{f}
, denote by
\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
the mod
\mathfrak{p}
reduction of
\rho_{f}
. Let
p
be a prime such that
\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
is irreducible. Serre’s modularity conjecture predicts that
\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
arises also as the mod
\mathfrak{p}
representation associated to another newform in
S_{k(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})}(\Gamma_{1}(N(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}))
for some weight
k(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})
and level
N(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})
. Indeed the level is the prime to
p
part of the Artin conductor of
\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}

N(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})=\prod_{r\neq q}r^{n_{r}}

where
n_{r}
is the sum of
\operatorname{codim}(V^{I_{r}})
and the Swan conductor at
r

\operatorname{Swan}_{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{[I_{r}:G_{i,r}]}\operatorname{codim}(V^{G_{i,r}})

where
G_{i,r}
is the
i
-th ramification group at
r
. If
l
is a prime such that
l|N(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})
but
l^{2}\nmid N(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})
then since
N(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})|N
we have that
n_{l}=1
from which it follows that
\operatorname{codim}V^{I_{l}}=1
and
\operatorname{Swan}_{l}=0
. Up to conjugacy we have

\rho_{\mid I_{r}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\xi\\
0&\chi\\
\end{pmatrix}

for some nontrivial morphism
\xi:I_{l}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
so by assuming that the
l
-part of the nebentypus of
f
is trivial we have that
\chi=1
and
\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}(I_{l})
is cyclic of order
l
. By
[
34
, Proposition 15]
this implies that the image of the mod
\mathfrak{p}
representation contains
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. So in summary given a modular form
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N,l))=S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N)\cap\Gamma_{0}(l))
with
l\nmid N,
if the representation at
p
is irreducible,
l|N(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})
but
l^{2}\nmid N(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})
then the image of
\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
contains
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. By choosing
\mathfrak{p}
above
p
with residual degree one and a prime
r\nmid pN
such that the characteristic polynomial of the Frobenius at
r
mod
\mathfrak{p}
is irreducible in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the representation is irreducible. We have

Proposition 7.1

(Theorem 5
[
23
]
)
.

Under the above condition either the representation
\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
comes from another representation associated to a newform with level
N(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})
or the image contains
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

To guarantee that the representation does not come from another newform is a hard task, especially since the bound weight
k(\rho_{f,\mathfrak{p}})
depends on the prime
p
. Mascot gives a computational method to check this but instead we decided to use a theorem of Diamond for the case
k=2
.

Theorem 7.2

(Theorem 4.1
[
14
]
)
.

Suppose that
p
is odd and that
\rho:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is an irreducible representation arising from
S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N,l))
for some prime
l
not dividing
Np
. If
\rho
is unramified at
l
, then
\rho
arises from
S_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
.

Using the above theorem, checking that a representation is not old a at
p
is much easier. For this reason all of our examples will be in weight 2, even thought theoretically there might be examples in any weight, but these are much harder to compute.
