The number of cycles of a given length in dense hamiltonian graphs: proving Hilton’s conjecture
Chengli Li, Leyou Xu, Bo Zhou

Abstract
A classical theorem of Sheehan in 1977 states that every hamiltonian graph G G of order n n satisfying e ⁡ ( G ) > ⌊ n 2 4 ⌋ + 1 e(G)>\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+1 contains at least two cycles of every length ℓ \ell , 3 ≤ ℓ ≤ n 3\leq\ell\leq n . In the same paper, Sheehan recorded a conjecture of Hilton, which strengthens this conclusion by asserting that such a graph contains at least n − ℓ + 2 n-\ell+2 cycles of length ℓ \ell for each 3 ≤ ℓ ≤ n 3\leq\ell\leq n . We prove Hilton’s conjecture for all hamiltonian graphs of order at least 440 440 . Key words: Hilton’s conjecture, hamiltonian graph, pancyclic graph, cycle

Introduction

A graph is hamiltonian if it contains a Hamilton cycle (a cycle that goes through every vertex of the graph). Hamiltonicity has been a central topic in graph theory for many decades. Since deciding whether a graph is hamiltonian is NP-complete, a major line of research has sought structural conditions that guarantee Hamiltonicity. A classical starting point is Dirac’s theorem
[
12
]
: every
n
-vertex graph with minimum degree at least
\frac{n}{2}
is hamiltonian. This theorem has inspired many refinements and variants concerning degree conditions, connectivity, random graphs, decompositions, robustness and related aspects of Hamiltonicity, see, for example,
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
27
,
28
,
30
]
and the survey
[
19
]
.

Pancyclicity is a natural strengthening of Hamiltonicity. An
n
-vertex graph is called pancyclic if it contains cycles of all lengths from
3
to
n
. Bondy’s well-known “meta-conjecture”
[
5
]
in 1973 suggests that, apart from simple extremal exceptions, any non-trivial sufficient conditions for Hamiltonicity should in fact force pancyclicity. This conjecture is illustrated by Bondy’s theorem
[
4
]
, which strengthens Dirac’s theorem by showing that every
n
-vertex graph with minimum degree at least
\frac{n}{2}
is either pancyclic or isomorphic to
K_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}}
. Further evidence was given by Bauer and Schmeichel
[
2
]
, building on work of Schmeichel and Hakimi
[
32
]
, who showed that the hamiltonian conditions of Bondy
[
6
]
, Chvátal
[
8
]
and Fan
[
18
]
also imply pancyclicity with certain exceptional graphs.

This paper considers a counting version of this circle of questions. Instead of asking merely whether each length occurs, we ask how many cycles of a prescribed length must appear in a dense hamiltonian graph. This is a natural strengthening. For a graph
G
, let
e(G)
denote its number of edges. Sheehan
[
33
]
proved in 1977 that if
G
is a hamiltonian graph of order
n
satisfying

e(G)>\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+1,

(1.1)

then
G
contains at least two cycles of every length
\ell
,
3\leq\ell\leq n
.
Such graphs are the dense graphs we study in this paper. Let
c_{\ell}(G)
be the number (or multiplicity) of cycles of length
\ell
in
G
. In the same paper, Sheehan recorded the following conjecture due to A.J.W. Hilton, which proposes a much sharper uniform multiplicity form of pancyclicity.

Conjecture 1.1

(Hilton)
.

Let
G
be a hamiltonian graph of order
n
satisfying (
1.1
). Then, for every integer
\ell
with
3\leq\ell\leq n
,
c_{\ell}(G)\geq n-\ell+2
.

The conjecture is consistent with the extremal behavior at the two ends of the cycle spectrum (the set of cycle lengths of a graph): for
\ell=n
it asks for two Hamilton cycles, a result already proved by Sheehan
[
33
]
, while for
\ell=3
it follows from a result of Erdős
[
14
]
, where it was proved that
every graph of order
n
with
e(G)=\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+\ell
with
\ell=1,2,3
contains at least
\ell\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
triangles.
Despite these endpoint cases, the full conjecture has remained open since Sheehan first posed it in 1977. Our main result confirms this conjecture for sufficiently large order.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a hamiltonian graph of order
n\geq 440
satisfying (
1.1
).
Then, for every integer
\ell
with
3\leq\ell\leq n
,
c_{\ell}(G)\geq n-\ell+2
.

In fact, we will prove a slightly stronger statement in Theorem
3.1
, which leaves only finite exceptional ranges for the lengths
6\leq\ell\leq 10
and confirms Conjecture
1.1
for all other lengths.

We mention that this study also belongs to the problem to determine the number of substructures in a fixed class of
graphs, which received much attention, see
[
1
,
15
,
16
]
. For example, Erdős
[
16
]
conjectured that the
maximum number of cycles of length
5
in an
n
-vertex triangle-free graph is
\left(\frac{n}{5}\right)^{5}
. Through the work
[
21
]
, it was confirmed independently in
[
20
,
24
]
.

If

Every

(ii)

(iii)

Lemma 3.2

Proof.

If

Now let

If

, then
m\geq 3
and (
1.1
) becomes

By Lemma

If

and (

Let

be a spanning subgraph of

Then there is a vertex

whose degree is at most

∎

Having established the lemma, we now proceed to the main result.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
G
be a hamiltonian graph of order
n
satisfying (
1.1
).
Our proof proceeds by contradiction. Suppose to the contrary that
c_{\ell}(G)\leq n-\ell+1
. By Lemma

,
\ell\leq n-1
and
\ell=4
or
\ell\geq 6
.

Firstly, we establish the following fundamental lemma. Part of the lemma is straightforward, but the crucial and nontrivial part is that
H
cannot be bipartite.

Lemma 3.3
.

There is a spanning hamiltonian subgraph
H
of
G
such that
H
is
C_{\ell}
-free and

e(H)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor-n+\ell+1.

(3.1)

Moreover,

cannot be bipartite.

Proof.

Let
C
be a Hamilton cycle of
G
. As
\ell\leq n-1
, every
\ell
-cycle contains at least one edge outside
C
.
For each
\ell
-cycle choose one of its edges outside

. Let
E_{1}
be the set of chosen edges, where it is possible that the same edge is chosen for several cycles. Then
|E_{1}|\leq c_{\ell}(G)\leq n-\ell+1
. Let
H
be the subgraph of

obtained by removing the edges of

. Then

is spanning and still contains the Hamilton cycle

By the construction of

is

-free. Moreover, by (

), we have

e(H)=e(G)-|E_{1}|\geq e(G)-(n-\ell+1)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+2-(n-\ell+1)=\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor-n+\ell+1,

proving (
3.1
).

Next, we show that
H
cannot be bipartite.
Toward a contradiction, suppose that
H
is bipartite. As
H
is a spanning hamiltonian subgraph of

,
n=2m
for some integer

.

Claim 3.4
.

\ell
is odd.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
\ell
is even. Then
\ell=2r
for some integer

, so

e(H)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor-n+\ell+1=m^{2}-2m+2r+1.

Note that

and
m\geq 3
. If
m=3
, then
e(H)\geq 8
, so apart from a Hamilton cycle, there are at least two chords, and from the bipartiteness, it can not be

-free. This shows that

. Then

e(H)\geq m^{2}-2m+2r+1>\frac{m^{2}}{2}

contradicting Lemma
2.5
.
∎

By Claim

,
\ell=2r+1
for some integer

with
3\leq r\leq m-1
.

Let
(X,Y)
be the bipartition of
H
. As

is hamiltonian and bipartite,
|X|=|Y|=m
.
As

is bipartite, all edges of
G
inside

or inside

.
Then
G=B+I
for some

with
H\subseteq B\subseteq K_{m,m}
and
I=E(G[X])\cup E(G[Y])
.
Let

h=m-r,\,e(B)=m^{2}-t\mbox{ and }|I|=p.

Note that
I\subseteq E(G)\setminus E(H)
.
From
G
to
H
, we have deleted at most
n-\ell+1=2m-(2r+1)+1=2h

p\leq 2h
.
On the other hand, from (
1.1
), we have

m^{2}-t+p=e(B)+p=e(G)\geq m^{2}+2,

and hence

. So
t\leq p-2\leq 2h-2
.

Let
uv\in I
. Assume that

. In

, a
(u,v)
-path of length
2r
contains

and
r-1

, and so
the number of
(u,v)
-paths of length

Among these paths, fix an edge

that is incident to
u
or
v
, and denote the other endpoint of

by

. Then
w\in Y
.
Such a path containing

contains

and
r-1
vertices of
X\setminus\{u,v\}
,
so there are at most

(m-1)_{r-1}(m-2)_{r-1}=\frac{T}{m}

(u,v)
-paths of length
2r
containing a fixed edge in

that is incident to
u
or
v
.
Now fix an edge

with

and

. The edge

can occupy any one of the

internal edge positions of the path. Once this position is fixed, the remaining

2(r-1)(m-1)_{r-1}(m-3)_{r-2}=\frac{2(r-1)}{m(m-2)}T

Case 1.

.

In this case,
\frac{2(r-1)}{m(m-2)}T\geq\frac{T}{m}
. Recall that

. Then at most

t\frac{2(r-1)}{m(m-2)}T\leq(2h-2)\frac{2(r-1)}{m(m-2)}T=\frac{4(m-r-1)(r-1)}{m(m-2)}T\leq\left(1-\frac{2}{m}\right)T

(u,v)

(u,v)

. Each remaining path together with the edge

Case 2.

m\geq 2r+1
.

In this case,
\frac{2(r-1)}{m(m-2)}\leq\frac{1}{m}
. At most
\frac{t}{m}T

(u,v)
-paths of length

contain an edge in

, and so at least
T-\frac{t}{m}T

(u,v)
-paths of length
2r
remain in

Note that an edge

If

Let

Claim 3.5
.

Each edge in

is incident with

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that

Suppose first that

We count the number of
(u,v)
-paths of length

in

. First note that for pairs of neighbors of

and

, there are at least
2

B[X_{0},Y_{0}]\cong K_{r+1,s}

is increasing on
[124,\infty)
and is positive.
