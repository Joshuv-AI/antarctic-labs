Birational geometry of Quot schemes on smooth projective curves via stable pairs
Chandranandan Gangopadhyay, Atsushi Ito

Abstract
. Let C C be a smooth projective curve of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 over ℂ \mathbb{C} , and let E 0 E^{0} be a vector bundle on C C . We investigate the birational geometry of the Quot scheme Quot C ⁡ ( E 0 , k , n ) \Quot_{C}(E^{0},k,n) , which parametrizes quotients of E 0 E^{0} of rank k k and degree n n , and its fiber 𝒬 L \mathcal{Q}_{L} over Pic n ⁡ ( C ) \Pic^{n}(C) for n ≫ 0 n\gg 0 . Our main tool is the moduli space of stable pairs, which yields small ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial modifications (SQMs) of Quot C ⁡ ( E 0 , k , n ) \Quot_{C}(E^{0},k,n) and 𝒬 L \mathcal{Q}_{L} . We explicitly describe the nef, movable, and effective cones of each SQM. Consequently, we prove that 𝒬 L \mathcal{Q}_{L} is a Mori dream space and that the determinant morphism Quot C ⁡ ( E 0 , k , n ) → Pic n ⁡ ( C ) \Quot_{C}(E^{0},k,n)\to\Pic^{n}(C) is a Mori dream morphism.

1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
. Let
E^{0}
be a vector bundle on
C
of rank
r
and degree
e
.
For integers
0\leq k\leq r-1
and
n
,
let
{\Quot}_{C}(E^{0},k,n)
be the Quot scheme parametrizing quotients of
E^{0}
of rank
k
and degree
n
.
The Quot scheme has played a fundamental role in the construction and study of moduli spaces of vector bundles (see e.g.
[
DN89
]
,
[
PR03
]
,
[
BGL94
]
,
[
MO07a
]
).
Since the Quot scheme for
E^{0}=\mathcal{O}_{C}^{r}
can be regarded as a compactification of the space of morphisms from
C
to the Grassmannian,
it has been extremely useful in Gromov–Witten theory (see e.g.
[
BDW96
]
,
[
Ber97
]
,
[
MO07b
]
,
[
Opr06
]
).

The geometry of these Quot schemes has been studied extensively, in particular the case
of
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\coloneqq{\Quot}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{O}^{r}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}},k,n)
.
If
k=r-1
or
n=0
,
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is a projective space or a Grassmannian, respectively.
If
0\leq k\leq r-2
and
n>0
,
Strømme in
[
Str87
]
showed that
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is a smooth projective variety with
\Pic(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})=\mathbb{Z}^{2}

and the nef cone
\Nef(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})
is generated by two globally generated line bundles.
In
[
Jow12
]
and
[
Ven11
]
, the effective cone
\Eff(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})
and the movable cone
\Mov(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})
were computed respectively.
In
[
Ito17
]
, the birational geometry of
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
was studied and it was shown that
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is a Mori dream space.
Recall that a normal projective variety
X
is called a
Mori dream space
if

1)

X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and
\Pic(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
,

2)

\Nef(X)
is spanned by finitely many semiample line bundles,

3)

there exist finitely many small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modifications (SQMs)
f_{i}:X\dashrightarrow X_{i}
such that
each
X_{i}
satisfies 2) and
\Mov(X)=\bigcup_{i}f_{i}^{*}(\Nef(X_{i}))
.

Mori dream spaces were introduced by Hu and Keel in
[
HK00
]
and they have nice properties from the perspective of the minimal model program.
In
[
Ito17
]
, it was further shown that all the small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modifications of
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
have moduli-theoretic descriptions.

For
C
with genus
g\geq 2
, the Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}\coloneqq{\Quot}_{C}(E^{0},k,n)
can be reducible and non-reduced.
However, for
n\gg 0
,
\mathcal{Q}
is known to be irreducible and generically smooth for
E^{0}=\mathcal{O}_{C}^{r}
by
[
BDW96
]
, and for arbitrary
E^{0}
by
[
PR03
]
.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{Q}
is locally factorial and its Picard group was computed for
n\gg 0
by
[
GS24
]
.

Note that there is a natural morphism

\displaystyle\det:\mathcal{Q}\to{\Pic}^{n}(C),\quad[E^{0}\twoheadrightarrow B]\mapsto\det(B).

(1.1)

For
[L]\in\Pic^{n}(C)
,
we set
\mathcal{Q}_{L}={\Quot}_{C}(E^{0},k,n)_{L}\coloneqq\det^{-1}([L])
.
If
k=r-1
and
n\gg 0
,
(
1.1
) is a projective bundle and hence
\mathcal{Q}_{L}
is a projective space.
For
0\leq k\leq r-2
and
n\gg 0
, it was shown in
[
GS24
]
that (
1.1
) is flat,
and
\mathcal{Q}_{L}
is also locally factorial with
\Pic(\mathcal{Q}_{L})=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
In
[
GS26
]
, the nef and effective cones of
\mathcal{Q}_{L}
were computed for
E^{0}=\mathcal{O}_{C}^{r}
and
n\gg 0
.
In particular, the nef cone is generated by two globally generated line bundles.

Hence,
\mathcal{Q}_{L}
for
n\gg 0
shares several geometric properties with
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
.
The purpose of this article is to study the birational geometry of
\mathcal{Q}_{L}
for arbitrary
E^{0}
and sufficiently large
n
.
In particular, we show that, similarly to
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
, the space
\mathcal{Q}_{L}
is a Mori dream space.

To produce all the SQMs of
\mathcal{Q}_{L}
, we work with the moduli space

M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
for
E_{0}\coloneqq(E^{0})^{\vee}
and a real number
\delta>0
,
which parametrizes equivalence classes of
\delta
-semistable pairs
(E,\alpha)
with
E
a vector bundle of rank
s
and degree
d
and
\alpha:E_{0}\to E
a non-zero homomorphism.
We refer to §
2
for the definition of
\delta
-(semi)stability.
This semistability condition was first introduced by Bradlow in
[
Bra91
]
for
E_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{C}
.
In
[
BD91
]
, the moduli space of semistable pairs
M_{C,\delta}(\mathcal{O}_{C},s,d)
was constructed as a compact Kähler manifold for certain values of
\delta
. In
[
Tha94
]
, in the case
s=2
, it was shown that the moduli spaces for different values of
\delta
are related by a sequence of flips.
In
[
BDW96
]
,
M_{C,\delta}(\mathcal{O}^{r}_{C},s,d)
was introduced for a certain range of
\delta
and it was shown that for
s=2
, they are again related by a sequence of flips. The moduli of semistable pairs
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
for any
E_{0}
(in fact on any smooth projective variety) were constructed in
[
Wan15
]
and
[
Lin18
]
.
As in the case of Quot schemes, there is a morphism

\det:M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)\to\Pic^{d}(C),\quad[E_{0}\xrightarrow{\alpha}E]\mapsto[\det(E)].

For
[L]\in\Pic^{d}(C)
, we define
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)_{L}\coloneqq\det^{-1}([L])
.

The relevance of the moduli space of stable pairs to our study of Quot schemes is that, for
\delta\gg 0
, there exists an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Quot(E^{0},k,n)\xrightarrow{\sim}M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},r-k,n-e),\quad[E^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}B]\mapsto[E_{0}\to(\ker q)^{\vee}].

(1.2)

Since
\det(\ker q)^{\vee}=\det B\otimes\det E_{0}
,
(
1.2
) induces an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Quot(E^{0},k,n)_{L}\simeq M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},r-k,n-e)_{L\otimes\det E_{0}}.

(1.3)

In the following Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, we work under the setting below.

•

C
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
.

•

E^{0}
is a vector bundle on
C
of rank
r
and degree
e
.

•

0\leq k\leq r-2
is an integer.

•

n
is a sufficiently large integer depending on
C,E^{0},k
.

•

\mathcal{Q}_{L}
is the fiber of the morphism
\det:\Quot(E^{0},k,n)\to{\Pic}^{n}(C)
over
[L]\in\Pic^{n}(C)
.

•

Set
E_{0}\coloneqq(E^{0})^{\vee},s\coloneqq r-k,d\coloneqq n-e
and
\tilde{L}\coloneqq L\otimes\det E_{0}\in\Pic^{d}(C)
.

Under the above setting,
we see that there exists a sequence of rational numbers

\displaystyle 0=\delta_{0}<\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}<\cdots<\delta_{N}

which depends on
E_{0},s,d
,
such that for each
1\leq i\leq N+1
,
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
and
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)_{\tilde{L}}
do not depend on
\delta\in(\delta_{i-1},\delta_{i})
,
where
\delta_{N+1}=\infty
.
In particular,
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)_{\tilde{L}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{Q}_{L}
for
\delta>\delta_{N}
by (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.1
.

For
1\leq i\leq N+1
and
\delta\in(\delta_{i-1},\delta_{i})
,
the following hold.

(1)

M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)_{\tilde{L}}
is irreducible, reduced, normal, locally complete intersection, and locally factorial.

(2)

The rational map defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{L}\dashrightarrow M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)_{\tilde{L}}:[E^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}B]\mapsto[E_{0}\to(\ker q)^{\vee}],

is a SQM of
\mathcal{Q}_{L}
.

If
k\geq 2
,
we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{L}={\Quot}_{C}(E^{0},k,n)_{L}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{\tilde{L}}\coloneqq\Quot_{C}(E_{0},s,d)_{\tilde{L}}:[E^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}B]\mapsto[E_{0}\twoheadrightarrow(\ker q)^{\vee}]

is a SQM as well.
Hence there exists a sequence of rational numbers

\displaystyle 0=\delta^{\prime}_{0}<\delta^{\prime}_{1}<\delta^{\prime}_{2}<\cdots<\delta^{\prime}_{N^{\prime}}

such that for
1\leq j\leq N^{\prime}+1
and
\delta^{\prime}\in(\delta^{\prime}_{j-1},\delta^{\prime}_{j})
,
the composite

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{L}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{\tilde{L}}\dashrightarrow M_{C,\delta^{\prime}}(E^{0},k,n)_{L}:[E^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}B]\mapsto[E^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}B]

is a SQM by applying Theorem
1.1
to
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{\tilde{L}}
.

In the following theorem,
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)_{\tilde{L}}
is denoted by
M_{i,\tilde{L}}
for
\delta\in(\delta_{i-1},\delta_{i})
for simplicity.
Similarly,

M_{C,\delta^{\prime}}(E^{0},k,n)_{L}
is denoted by
M^{\prime}_{j,L}
for
\delta^{\prime}\in(\delta^{\prime}_{j-1},\delta^{\prime}_{j})
.
By (
1.3
), we identify
\mathcal{Q}_{L}
(resp.
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{\tilde{L}}
) with
M_{N+1,\tilde{L}}
(resp.
M^{\prime}_{N^{\prime}+1,L}
).

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a basis
\lambda,\mu
of
N^{1}(\mathcal{Q}_{L})_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}

such that the following hold.

Let
\theta_{i}
be classes in

\displaystyle\theta_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d+\delta_{i}}{s}-(g-1)\right)\lambda-\mu\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq i\leq N.

If

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}_{j}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n+\delta^{\prime}_{j}}{k}+(g-1)\right)\lambda+\mu\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq j\leq N^{\prime}.

in the same way by setting

Then

(1)

It holds that

\displaystyle\Nef(M_{i,\tilde{L}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\theta_{i-1}+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\theta_{i}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq N,

\displaystyle\Nef(\mathcal{Q}_{L})=\Nef(M_{N+1,\tilde{L}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\theta_{N}+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\lambda.

Furthermore, if

\displaystyle\Nef(M^{\prime}_{j,L})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\theta^{\prime}_{j-1}+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\theta^{\prime}_{j}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq j\leq N^{\prime},

\displaystyle\Nef(\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{\tilde{L}})=\Nef(M^{\prime}_{N^{\prime}+1,L})

(2)

If

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,

C
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2

and
E^{0}
is a vector bundle on
C
of rank
r
and degree
e
.
Let
E_{0}\coloneqq(E^{0})^{\vee}
.

2.1.
\delta
-stability

Let
\delta>0
.
In this section, we recall some notions on
\delta
-stability of pairs.
See
[
Wan15
]
,
[
Lin18
]
for details.

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

A
pair

(E,\alpha)
consists of a coherent sheaf
E
and a morphism
\alpha:E_{0}\to E
.
If
\alpha\neq 0
,
(E,\alpha)
is called
nondegenerate
.

(2)

A
subpair
of a pair
(E,\alpha)
is a pair
(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
where
\iota:E^{\prime}\subset E

is a subsheaf and

\iota\circ\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha\quad\text{if }\im\alpha\subset E^{\prime},\qquad\alpha^{\prime}=0\quad\text{otherwise}.

(3)

A
quotient pair
of
(E,\alpha)
is a pair
(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})
where
q:E\to E^{\prime\prime}
is a quotient sheaf and
\alpha^{\prime\prime}=q\circ\alpha:E_{0}\to E^{\prime\prime}
.

(4)

The
\delta
-slope
of a pair
(E,\alpha)
is

\mu_{\delta}(E,\alpha)=\mu(E,\alpha)\coloneqq\frac{\deg E+\varepsilon(\alpha)\delta}{\rank E},

where
\varepsilon(\alpha)\coloneqq 0
if
\alpha=0
and
\varepsilon(\alpha)\coloneqq 1
if
\alpha\neq 0
.

(5)

A
morphism
of pairs
\varphi:(E,\alpha)\to(F,\beta)
is a morphism of sheaves
\varphi:E\to F
such that there exists a constant
c\in\mathbb{C}
with
\varphi\circ\alpha=c\beta
.

(6)

A pair
(E,\alpha)
is
\delta
-
stable
if
E
is locally free and

\mu(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})<\mu(E,\alpha)

(1.1)

for any proper subpair
(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
.
Equivalently,

(E,\alpha)
is
\delta
-stable if
E
is locally free and

\mu(E,\alpha)<\mu(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})

(1.2)

for every proper quotient pair
(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})
.

\delta
-semistability is defined by replacing the strict inequality
<
with
\leq
.

(7)

For a subpair
(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
of a pair
(E,\alpha)
,
the quotient pair
(E,\alpha)/(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})=(E/E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})
is defined by

\alpha^{\prime\prime}=0
if
\alpha^{\prime}\neq 0
, and
\alpha^{\prime\prime}=q\circ\alpha
if
\alpha^{\prime}=0
,
where
q:E\to E/E^{\prime}
is the quotient map.

(8)

For integers
s\geq 1
and
d
,
let
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
be the coarse moduli space of
S
-equivalence classes of (nondegenerate, that is,
\alpha\neq 0
)
\delta
-semistable pairs
(E,\alpha)
with
\rank E=s
,

\deg E=d
.

(9)

The fiber of

\det:M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)\to\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(C):\quad[(E,\alpha)]\mapsto[\det E]

over
[L]\in\Pic^{d}(C)
is denoted by
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)_{L}
.

We note that the moduli space
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
exists by
[
Wan15
, Theorem 3.8]
,
[
Lin18
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Furthermore, the locus
M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
of
\delta
-stable pairs is the fine moduli space.

Lemma 2.2
.

(1)

For a locally free sheaf
E
,
(E,0)
is
\delta
-(semi)stable if and only if
E
is (semi)stable in the usual sense.

(2)

If
f:(E,\alpha)\to(F,\beta)
is a non-zero morphism between
\delta
-stable pairs with
\mu(E,\alpha)=\mu(F,\beta)
,
then
f
is an isomorphism.

(3)

If
F
is semistable and
(E,\alpha)
is nondegenerate and
\delta
-stable with
\mu(F)=\mu(E,\alpha)
, then
\Hom(F,E)=0
.

(4)

Let
0\to(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})\to(E,\alpha)\to(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})\to 0
be exact, that is,
0\to E^{\prime}\to E\to E^{\prime\prime}\to 0
is exact and
(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
is a subpair of
(E,\alpha)
, and
(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})
is the corresponding quotient pair.
If
(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
and
(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})
are
\delta
-semistable and
\mu(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})=\mu(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})
,
then
(E,\alpha)
is
\delta
-semistable.

(5)

Let
0\to(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})\to(E,\alpha)\to(E^{\prime\prime},0)\to 0
be exact with
\alpha\neq 0
.
Assume
(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
is
\delta
-stable and
E^{\prime\prime}
is semistable with
\mu_{\delta}(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})=\mu(E^{\prime\prime})
.
If
(E,\alpha)
is not
(\delta-\varepsilon)
-stable for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists a subsheaf
G
with
G\cap E^{\prime}=0
and
\mu(G)=\mu(E^{\prime\prime})
.

Proof.

(1) follows from the definition.
(2) is a special case of
[
Lin18
, Lemma 2.11]
.

(3) Assume
f:F\to E
is non-zero and let
(f(F),\alpha^{\prime})
be the induced subpair of
(E,\alpha)
.
Then
\mu(F)\leq\mu(f(F))\leq\mu(f(F),\alpha^{\prime})\leq\mu(E,\alpha)=\mu(F)
, where
\mu=\deg/\rank
is the usual slope,
and hence the equality holds in each inequality.
By
\mu(f(F),\alpha^{\prime})=\mu(E,\alpha)
and the
\delta
-stability of
(E,\alpha)
,
we have
(f(F),\alpha^{\prime})=(E,\alpha)
and hence
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha\neq 0
.
On the other hand, we have
\alpha^{\prime}=0
by
\mu(f(F))=\mu(f(F),\alpha^{\prime})
, which is a contradiction.

(4) Note that we have
\mu(E,\alpha)=\mu(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})=\mu(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})\eqqcolon\mu
.

Let
(F,\beta)
be a subpair of
(E,\alpha)
.
If
\beta=0
, then we have
\mu(F,\beta)=\mu(F)\leq\mu
by

\mu(F\cap E^{\prime})\leq\mu(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})=\mu
and
\mu(F/(F\cap E^{\prime}))\leq\mu(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})=\mu
.
If
\beta\neq 0
, let
(F\cap E^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\subset(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
and
(F/(F\cap E^{\prime}),\beta^{\prime\prime})\subset(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})
be the induced subpairs.
Hence we can check that
\mu(F,\beta)\leq\mu
by using

\mu(F\cap E^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\leq\mu(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})=\mu
and
\mu(F/(F\cap E^{\prime}),\beta^{\prime\prime})\leq\mu(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})=\mu
.

(5) By (4),
(E,\alpha)
is
\delta
-semistable.
Since
(E,\alpha)
is not
(\delta-\varepsilon)
-stable,
there exists a subpair
(G,\alpha_{G})\subsetneq(E,\alpha)
such that
\mu_{\delta-\varepsilon}(G,\alpha_{G})\geq\mu_{\delta-\varepsilon}(E,\alpha)
.
Hence we have
\alpha_{G}=0
and
\mu(G)=\mu_{\delta}(E,\alpha)
by
\mu_{\delta}(G,\alpha_{G})\leq\mu_{\delta}(E,\alpha)
.

Then
(G\cap E^{\prime},0)\subsetneq(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
is a subpair and
G/(G\cap E^{\prime})\hookrightarrow E^{\prime\prime}
is a subsheaf.
For
\mu\coloneqq\mu_{\delta}(E,\alpha)=\mu_{\delta}(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})=\mu(E^{\prime\prime})
,
we have

•

G\cap E^{\prime}=0
or
\mu(G\cap E^{\prime})<\mu
, and

•

G/(G\cap E^{\prime})=0
or
\mu(G/(G\cap E^{\prime}))\leq\mu
.

Since
0\to G\cap E^{\prime}\to G\to G/(G\cap E^{\prime})\to 0
is exact and
\mu(G)=\mu
,
we have
G\cap E^{\prime}=0
and
\mu(G)=\mu(G/(G\cap E^{\prime}))\leq\mu
.
∎

2.2.
Mori dream morphisms

Mori dream morphisms
, which are a relative version of Mori dream spaces, were introduced in
[
AW14
]
,
[
Oht22
]
.
We refer the reader to
[
Oht22
]
for the details.

For a morphism
\pi:X\to U
between quasi-projective varieties,

\Pic(X/U)\coloneqq\Pic(X)/\pi^{*}\Pic(U)

denotes the relative Picard group.
We set
\Pic(X/U)_{\mathbb{Q}}\coloneqq\Pic(X/U)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
and

N^{1}(X/U)=\Pic(X/U)_{\mathbb{Q}}/\equiv
,
where
D\equiv D^{\prime}
if
(D.C)=(D^{\prime}.C)
for any complete curve
C\subset X
contracted by
\pi
.
For a Cartier divisor
D
on
X
, let

\alpha_{D}:\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}(D)\to\mathcal{O}(D)

be the natural map.
A
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
is called
\pi
-effective,
\pi
-movable,
\pi
-semiample
if
\codim\Supp(\coker(\alpha_{mD}))\geq 1
,
\codim\Supp(\coker(\alpha_{mD}))\geq 2
,
\coker(\alpha_{mD})=0
for some
m>0
, respectively.

Definition 2.3

(
[
Oht22
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

A projective morphism
\pi:X\to U
between quasi-projective varieties is called a
Mori dream morphism
if it satisfies the following conditions.

1)

X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and
\Pic(X/U)_{\mathbb{Q}}=N^{1}(X/U)_{\mathbb{Q}}
,

2)

\Nef(X/U)
is spanned by finitely many
\pi
-semiample line bundles,

3)

there exist finitely many SQMs
f_{i}:X\dashrightarrow X_{i}
over
U
such that
each
X_{i}
satisfies 2) and
\Mov(X/U)=\bigcup_{i}f_{i}^{*}(\Nef(X_{i}/U))
.

3.
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
for sufficiently large or small
\delta>0

Throughout this section, fix integers
s>0
and
d
.
For a locally free sheaf
F
on
C
, the (usual) slope is defined by
\mu(F)=\deg F/\rank F.

First, we consider the case when
\delta
is sufficiently large.
For each integer
1\leq i\leq r
, set

\displaystyle e_{i}\coloneqq\min\{\deg F\mid E_{0}\twoheadrightarrow F,\rank F=i\}

and

\displaystyle\delta_{\sharp}\coloneqq\inf\left\{\delta\geq 0\mid\frac{d+\delta}{s}<\frac{e_{i}+\delta}{i}\text{ for any }1\leq i\leq\min\{s-1,r\}\right\}.

(3.1)

More explicitly, we can define
\delta_{\sharp}\coloneqq 0
if
s=1
and

\displaystyle\delta_{\sharp}\coloneqq\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq\min\{s-1,r\}}\frac{id-se_{i}}{s-i}

if
s\geq 2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(E,\alpha)
be a locally free pair with
\rank E=s
,
\deg E=d
,
\alpha\neq 0
.
Assume
\delta>\delta_{\sharp}
.
Then
(E,\alpha)
is
\delta
-semistable if and only if
(E,\alpha)
is
\delta
-stable if and only if
\alpha
is generically surjective.

In particular,

(1)

If
s>r
,
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)=\emptyset
.

(2)

If
1\leq s\leq r
,

M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)\to\operatorname{Quot}_{C}(E^{0},r-s,d+e),\quad[(E,\alpha)]\mapsto[E^{0}\to E^{0}/\alpha^{\vee}(E^{\vee})]

is an isomorphism, where
\alpha^{\vee}:E^{\vee}\to E_{0}^{\vee}=E^{0}
is the dual of
\alpha
.

Proof.

Assume
(E,\alpha)
is
\delta
-semistable. If
\alpha
is not generically surjective, then
s^{\prime}\coloneqq\rank\im\alpha\leq\min\{s-1,r\}
.
For the induced morphism
\alpha^{\prime}:E_{0}\to\im\alpha
,

(\im\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
is a subpair of
(E,\alpha)
and hence we have

\frac{d+\delta}{s}=\mu(E,\alpha)\geq\mu(\im\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})=\frac{\deg(\im\alpha)+\delta}{s^{\prime}}\geq\frac{e_{s^{\prime}}+\delta}{s^{\prime}},

which contradicts
\delta>\delta_{\sharp}
.
Hence
\alpha
is generically surjective.

If
\alpha
is generically surjective, so is
\alpha^{\prime\prime}:E_{0}\to E^{\prime\prime}
for any proper quotient
(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})
with locally free
E^{\prime\prime}
. Hence

\mu(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})=\frac{\deg E^{\prime\prime}+\delta}{s^{\prime\prime}}\geq\frac{e_{s^{\prime\prime}}+\delta}{s^{\prime\prime}}>\frac{d+\delta}{s}=\mu(E,\alpha).

Hence
(E,\alpha)
is
\delta
-stable.

Since
\delta
-stability implies
\delta
-semistability, we obtain the equivalence in the statement of this lemma.
The last statements (1), (2) are clear.
∎

Next we consider the case when
\delta>0
is sufficiently small.
Define
\delta_{\flat}>0
by

\frac{d+\delta_{\flat}}{s}=\min\left\{\frac{d^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\,\middle|\,s^{\prime},d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq s^{\prime}\leq s-1,\frac{d^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}>\frac{d}{s}\right\}

if
s\geq 2
.
Define
\delta_{\flat}\coloneqq\infty
if
s=1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
0<\delta<\delta_{\flat}
.
If
(E,\alpha)
is a
\delta
-semistable pair with
\rank E=s
,
\deg E=d
,
\alpha\neq 0
,
then
E
is semistable in the usual sense.
In particular, there exists a morphism

f:M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)\to M_{C}(s,d),\quad[(E,\alpha)]\mapsto[E]

over
\Pic^{d}(C)
,
where
M_{C}(s,d)
is the coarse moduli space of
S
-equivalence classes of semistable vector bundles of rank
s
and degree
d
on
C
.

Proof.

Since any line bundle is stable, the case
s=1
is clear.
Assume
s\geq 2
.

Let
(E,\alpha)
be a
\delta
-semistable pair with
\rank E=s
,
\deg E=d
.
Let
E^{\prime}\subset E
be a subsheaf with
1\leq\rank E^{\prime}\leq s-1
.
For the induced subpair
(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
of
(E,\alpha)
, we have

\mu(E^{\prime})=\frac{\deg E^{\prime}}{\rank E^{\prime}}\leq\frac{\deg E^{\prime}+\varepsilon(\alpha^{\prime})\delta}{\rank E^{\prime}}=\mu(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})\leq\mu(E,\alpha)=\frac{d+\delta}{s}<\frac{d+\delta_{\flat}}{s},

which implies

\mu(E^{\prime})=\frac{\deg E^{\prime}}{\rank E^{\prime}}\leq\frac{d}{s}=\mu(E)

by the definition of
\delta_{\flat}
. Hence
E
is semistable.
The last statement is clear.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Let
E
be a stable locally free sheaf with
\rank E=s,\deg E=d
.
Then any
(E,\alpha)
with non-zero
\alpha:E_{0}\to E
is
\delta
-stable for
0<\delta<\delta_{\flat}
. In fact, for any quotient bundle
q:E\to E^{\prime\prime}
with
1\leq\rank E^{\prime\prime}\leq s-1
, we have

\mu(E^{\prime\prime})>\mu(E)=\frac{d}{s}

which implies

\mu(E^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})\geq\mu(E^{\prime\prime})\geq\frac{d+\delta_{\flat}}{s}>\frac{d+\delta}{s}=\mu(E,\alpha).

Hence the fiber of
f:M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)\to M_{C}(s,d)
over
[E]
is the projective space
\mathbb{P}({\rm Hom}(E_{0},E))
.

Furthermore,
if
\mu(E)=\frac{d}{s}>\mu_{\rm max}(E_{0})+2g-2
, where
\mu_{\rm max}(E_{0})\coloneqq\max\{\mu(F)\mid F\subset E_{0}\}
, then
{\rm ext}^{1}(E_{0},E)={\rm hom}(E,E_{0}\otimes\omega_{C})=0
by the stability of
E
. Hence we have

\mathbb{P}({\rm Hom}(E_{0},E))=\mathbb{P}^{se+r(d-s(g-1))-1}

and
f
is a
\mathbb{P}^{se+r(d-s(g-1))-1}
-bundle over the stable locus
M^{s}_{C}(s,d)\subset M_{C}(s,d)
.

Let
M_{C}(s,L)
be the fiber of
M_{C}(s,d)\to\Pic^{d}(C)
over
[L]
.
By restriction,
we obtain
f_{L}:M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)_{L}\to M_{C}(s,L)
, which is a projective bundle over
M^{s}_{C}(s,L)\coloneqq M_{C}(s,L)\cap M_{C}^{s}(s,d)
.

4.
Estimate of dimensions

Throughout this section, fix integers
s>0
and
d
.

4.1.
Expected dimensions

The following lemma is a consequence of
[
Lin18
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\delta>0
and
[(E,\alpha)]\in M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
be a point in the
\delta
-stable locus.
If
\operatorname{Hom}(E,\omega_{C}\otimes\ker\alpha)=0
,
then

M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
is smooth of dimension
dr+se-s(r-s)(g-1)
at
[(E,\alpha)]
.

Proof.

Let
I^{\bullet}=\{E_{0}\xrightarrow{\alpha}E\}
be a complex concentrated in degrees 0 and 1.
By
[
Lin18
, Theorem 1.2]
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hom(I^{\bullet},E)&=\dim T_{[(E,\alpha)]}M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)\\
&\geq\dim_{[(E,\alpha)]}M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)\geq\hom(I^{\bullet},E)-\ext^{1}(I^{\bullet},E).\end{split}

(4.1)

Since there is a natural triangle

\displaystyle I^{\bullet}\to E_{0}\xrightarrow{\alpha}E\xrightarrow{\beta}I^{\bullet}[1],

we have an exact sequence

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0\to\operatorname{Ext}^{-1}(I^{\bullet},E)&\to\operatorname{Hom}(E,E)\to\operatorname{Hom}(E_{0},E)\to\operatorname{Hom}(I^{\bullet},E)\\
&\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(E,E)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(E_{0},E)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(I^{\bullet},E)\to 0\end{aligned}

(4.2)

and
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(I^{\bullet},E)=0
for
i\neq-1,0,1
. In fact, we have
\operatorname{Ext}^{-1}(I^{\bullet},E)=0
as follows.

Take
\varphi
in the kernel of
\operatorname{Hom}(E,E)\to\operatorname{Hom}(E_{0},E)
, that is,
\varphi:E\to E
such that
\varphi\circ\alpha=0
. Then
\varphi:(E,\alpha)\to(E,\alpha)
is a morphism of stable pairs, hence
\varphi=0
or an isomorphism by
[
Lin18
, Lemma 2.11]
. Since
\alpha\neq 0
, we have
\varphi=0
.
Thus
\operatorname{Hom}(E,E)\to\operatorname{Hom}(E_{0},E)
is injective, and hence
\operatorname{Ext}^{-1}(I^{\bullet},E)=0
.

Then it holds that

\hom(I^{\bullet},E)-\ext^{1}(I^{\bullet},E)=\chi(I^{\bullet},E)=\chi(E_{0},E)-\chi(E,E)=dr+se-s(r-s)(g-1).

Furthermore, by (
4.2
) and Serre duality,

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(I^{\bullet},E)
is the dual of the kernel of

\operatorname{Hom}(E,\omega_{C}\otimes E_{0})\to\operatorname{Hom}(E,\omega_{C}\otimes E),\quad f\mapsto({\rm id}\otimes\alpha)\circ f,

and hence
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(I^{\bullet},E)=\operatorname{Hom}(E,\omega_{C}\otimes\ker\alpha)^{\vee}
.
Then this lemma follows from (
4.1
).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

For example, the condition
\operatorname{Hom}(E,\omega_{C}\otimes\ker\alpha)=0
in Lemma
4.1
holds if

(1)

r=1
, i.e., a Bradlow pair, or

(2)

s=r
and
\delta>\delta_{\sharp}
, i.e.,
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)=\operatorname{Quot}_{C}(E^{0},0,d+e)
, or

(3)

s=1
and
d>2g-2+\mu_{\max}(E_{0})
, or
s\geq 2
and
0<\delta<\frac{d-2s(g-1)-s\mu_{\rm max}(E_{0})}{s-1}
.

In fact,
\ker\alpha=0
in the case (1) since
E_{0}
is a line bundle and
\alpha\neq 0
.
For (2),
\ker\alpha=0
also holds by Lemma
3.1
.
For (3), assume
f:E\to\omega_{C}\otimes E_{0}
is non-zero.
Then
(\im f,f\circ\alpha)
is a quotient pair of
(E,\alpha)
and hence

\displaystyle\frac{d+\delta}{s}=\mu(E,\alpha)\leq\mu(\im f,f\circ\alpha)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\deg(\im f)+\delta}{\rank(\im f)}

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{\max}(\omega_{C}\otimes E_{0})+\delta=2g-2+\mu_{\max}(E_{0})+\delta

since
(E,\alpha)
is
\delta
-stable.
This contradicts the condition of
\delta
in (3) and hence
\operatorname{Hom}(E,\omega_{C}\otimes E_{0})=0
.
In particular,
\operatorname{Hom}(E,\omega_{C}\otimes\ker\alpha)=0
.

Hence
M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
is smooth of dimension
dr+se-s(r-s)(g-1)
in these cases.
We note that this statement for
\delta
in (3) is proved in
[
BDW96
, Theorem 3.20]
for
E_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus r}
.

4.2.
Upper bounds of the dimension of
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)

The purpose of this subsection is to show Lemma
4.11
, which states that

\dim M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)\leq r(d-s\mu_{\min}(E_{0}))+s\max\{s,r\}(g-1)

for
s\geq 1
and
\delta>0
,
where
\mu_{\min}(E_{0})=\min\{\mu(F)\mid E_{0}\twoheadrightarrow F\}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume a pair
(E,\alpha)
with
\alpha\neq 0
is
\delta
-semistable with
\rank E=s,\deg E=d
.
Let

0=(F_{0},\alpha_{0})\subset(F_{1},\alpha_{1})\subset(F_{2},\alpha_{2})\subset\cdots\subset(F_{\ell},\alpha_{\ell})=(E,\alpha)

be a Jordan-Hölder filtration as in
[
Lin18
, Proposition 2.13]
.
If
(E,\alpha)
is
(\delta-\varepsilon)
-semistable for
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, then

\im\alpha\subset F_{1}
. Furthermore, if
\ell\geq 2
, then
F_{1}
is the unique minimal bundle among those with
\im\alpha\subset F
and
\mu(E/F)=\frac{d+\delta}{s}
.

Proof.

By definition,

(F_{i},\alpha_{i})/(F_{i-1},\alpha_{i-1})
is
\delta
-stable with
\mu_{\delta}((F_{i},\alpha_{i})/(F_{i-1},\alpha_{i-1}))=\frac{d+\delta}{s}
.
If
\ell=1
, the claim is trivial, so assume
\ell\geq 2
.

If
\im\alpha\not\subset F_{1}
, then

\mu_{\delta-\varepsilon}(F_{1},\alpha_{1})=\mu(F_{1})=\mu_{\delta}(F_{1},\alpha_{1})=\frac{d+\delta}{s}>\frac{d+\delta-\varepsilon}{s}=\mu_{\delta-\varepsilon}(E,\alpha),

and hence
(E,\alpha)
is not
(\delta-\varepsilon)
-semistable.

For the second statement, suppose
F\subset E
satisfies
\im\alpha\subset F
and
\mu(E/F)=\frac{d+\delta}{s}
. Suppose
F_{1}\not\subset F
.
Then we have

\displaystyle 0\to F_{1}/(F_{1}\cap F)\to E/F\to E/(F+F_{1})\to 0.

(4.3)

Since

(F_{1},\alpha_{1})
is
\delta
-stable with
\alpha_{1}\neq 0
and
(F_{1}/(F_{1}\cap F),0)
is a proper quotient of
(F_{1},\alpha_{1})
, we have

\displaystyle\mu(F_{1}/(F_{1}\cap F))=\mu_{\delta}(F_{1}/(F_{1}\cap F),0)>\mu_{\delta}(F_{1},\alpha_{1})=\frac{d+\delta}{s}.

(4.4)

On the other hand,
E/F_{1}
is semistable since it is obtained as successive extensions by
F_{i}/F_{i-1}
for
2\leq i\leq\ell
and

(F_{i}/F_{i-1},0)=(F_{i},\alpha_{i})/(F_{i-1},\alpha_{i-1})
’s are
\delta
-stable with the same slope.
Since
E/F_{1}\to E/(F+F_{1})
is a quotient,

\mu(E/(F+F_{1}))\geq\mu(E/F_{1})=\mu_{\delta}(E/F_{1},0)=\frac{d+\delta}{s},

contradicting
\mu(E/F)=\frac{d+\delta}{s}
by (
4.3
) and (
4.4
). Hence we get that
F_{1}\subset F
.
∎

We note that
(F_{i},\alpha_{i})/(F_{i-1},\alpha_{i-1})=(F_{i}/F_{i-1},0)
for all but exactly one
1\leq i\leq\ell
for any
\delta
-semistable pair
(E,\alpha)
with
\alpha\neq 0
in the above Jordan-Hölder filtration.
Furthermore,
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}(F_{i},\alpha_{i})/(F_{i-1},\alpha_{i-1})
does not depend on the filtration
[
Lin18
, Proposition 2.13]
.
Hence we obtain the following decomposition of
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Fix
\delta>0
. Let
1\leq s^{\prime}\leq s-1
and
d^{\prime}
be integers such that
\frac{d+\delta}{s}=\frac{d^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}
. Then there exists an embedding

M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s-s^{\prime},d-d^{\prime})\times M_{C}(s^{\prime},d^{\prime})\hookrightarrow M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d),

given by

([(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})],[F])\mapsto[(E^{\prime}\oplus F,\alpha^{\prime}\oplus 0)].

Furthermore,
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
is the disjoint union of these images and
M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
.

Proof.

We can check that
\frac{d-d^{\prime}+\delta}{s-s^{\prime}}
coincides with
\frac{d+\delta}{s}=\frac{d^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}
.
Thus
(E^{\prime}\oplus F,\alpha^{\prime}\oplus 0)
in the statement of this lemma is
\delta
-semistable and hence we obtain the embedding.
The last statement follows from the uniqueness of the associated graded objects
\oplus(F_{i},\alpha_{i})/(F_{i-1},\alpha_{i-1})
in the Jordan-Hölder filtration.
∎

Recall that we fix
s,d
in this section.
Now fix also
\delta
.

We note that
\delta^{\prime}
-stability coincides with
\delta^{\prime}
-semistability if
\frac{d+\delta^{\prime}}{s}\not\in\bigcup_{s^{\prime}=1}^{s-1}\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}
.
Hence, for
0<\varepsilon\ll\delta,s,d
, if
(E,\alpha)
is
(\delta+\varepsilon)
-semistable (resp.
(\delta-\varepsilon)
-semistable), then it is both
(\delta+\varepsilon)
-stable (resp.
(\delta-\varepsilon)
-stable) and
\delta
-semistable.
In particular,
there are natural morphisms

\pi_{\delta}^{\pm}:M_{C,\delta\pm\varepsilon}(E_{0},s,d)\to M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
s^{\prime},d^{\prime}
be as in Lemma
4.4
.
Let

([(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})],[F])\in M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s-s^{\prime},d-d^{\prime})\times M_{C}(s^{\prime},d^{\prime})\subset M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)

and assume that
F
is stable.
Then the fiber of
\pi_{\delta}^{-}:M_{C,\delta-\varepsilon}(E_{0},s,d)\to M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
over
([(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})],[F])
is the projective space

\mathbb{P}({\rm Ext}^{1}(F,E^{\prime}))=\mathbb{P}^{s^{\prime}(\delta+(s-s^{\prime})(g-1))-1}.

Proof.

Let
[(E,\alpha)]\in M_{C,\delta-\varepsilon}(E_{0},s,d)
be a point in the fiber over
([(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})],[F])
. Then there exists an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to E^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\iota}E\to F\to 0

(4.5)

with
\alpha=\iota\circ\alpha^{\prime}

by Lemma
4.3
and the stability of
F
.
Note that this exact sequence is unique up to scalar
since
E^{\prime}
is the unique minimal subsheaf with
\im\alpha\subset E^{\prime}
,

\mu(E/E^{\prime})=\frac{d+\delta}{s}
, and
(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}),(F,0)
are
\delta
-stable.
If (
4.5
) splits, we have an injection
f:F\to E
.
Then
f
is a non-zero morphism
(F,0)\to(E,\alpha)
between
(\delta-\varepsilon)
-stable pairs with

\mu_{\delta-\varepsilon}(F,0)=\mu(F)=\frac{d^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}=\frac{d+\delta}{s}>\frac{d+\delta-\varepsilon}{s}=\mu_{\delta-\varepsilon}(E,\alpha),

a contradiction.
Hence (
4.5
) does not split.
Thus, we have a point in
\mathbb{P}({\rm Ext}^{1}(F,E^{\prime}))
.

Conversely, we have the following claim.

Claim 4.6
.

Let
0\to E^{\prime}\to E\to F\to 0

(3)

∎

6.
Description of
\delta
with

In this section,
we determine when
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)\neq M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
holds for sufficiently large
d
.
By Lemma
4.4
,

M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)\setminus M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
is the union of

M_{C,\delta}^{s}(E_{0},s-s^{\prime},d-d^{\prime})\times M_{C}(s^{\prime},d^{\prime})

with

1\leq s^{\prime}\leq s-1,d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\frac{d+\delta}{s}=\frac{d^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}
.
Since
M_{C}(s^{\prime},d^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
,
it suffices to consider whether
M_{C,\delta}^{s}(E_{0},s-s^{\prime},d-d^{\prime})
is empty or not for such
s^{\prime},d^{\prime}
.

6.1.
The case
s\leq r

In this subsection, we assume
2\leq s\leq r
and

d
is sufficiently large.
In this case,

\displaystyle\delta_{\sharp}=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq s-1}\frac{id-se_{i}}{s-i}=(s-1)d-se_{s-1}.

(6.1)

Proposition 6.1
.

Assume
2\leq s\leq r
and
d
is sufficiently large.
Let

\Gamma=\{\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}<\cdots<\delta_{N}\}\subset(0,\delta_{\sharp}]

be the set of
\delta\in(0,\delta_{\sharp}]
such that

\frac{d+\delta}{s}=\frac{d^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\quad\text{for some integers}\quad 1\leq s^{\prime}\leq s-1\quad\text{and}\quad d^{\prime}\leq d-e_{s-s^{\prime}}.

Then

(1)

For
\delta>0
,
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)\neq M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
holds if and only if
\delta\in\Gamma
.

(2)

\delta_{1}=\delta_{\flat}
.

(3)

\delta_{N}=\delta_{\sharp}=(s-1)d-se_{s-1}
.

Proof.

(1) It suffices to show the following claim.

Claim 6.2
.

For integers
1\leq s^{\prime}\leq s-1,d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\frac{d+\delta}{s}=\frac{d^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}
,
M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s-s^{\prime},d-d^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
holds if and only if
d^{\prime}\leq d-e_{s-s^{\prime}}
.

Proof of Claim
6.2
.

If
s-s^{\prime}=1
,

M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s-s^{\prime},d-d^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
if and only if
there exists a non-zero morphism
\alpha:E_{0}\to E
to a locally free
E
with
\rank E=1,\deg E=d-d^{\prime}
,
which is equivalent to
d-d^{\prime}\geq e_{1}
.

Assume
s-s^{\prime}\geq 2
.
The
\delta_{\sharp}
of
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s-s^{\prime},d-d^{\prime})
is

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq s-s^{\prime}-1}\frac{i(d-d^{\prime})-(s-s^{\prime})e_{i}}{s-s^{\prime}-i}.

(6.2)

Since
\delta=\frac{s}{s^{\prime}}d^{\prime}-d
, we see that

\delta
is greater than (
6.2
) if and only if

d-d^{\prime}<\frac{(s-s^{\prime}-i)d+s^{\prime}e_{i}}{s-i}

for any
1\leq i\leq s-s^{\prime}-1
,
which is equivalent to

\displaystyle d-d^{\prime}<\frac{d+s^{\prime}e_{s-s^{\prime}-1}}{s^{\prime}+1},

(6.3)

since
d
is sufficiently large.

If the condition (
6.3
) holds, by Lemma
3.1
,

M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s-s^{\prime},d-d^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
if and only if
there exists a generically surjective morphism
\alpha:E_{0}\to E
to a locally free
E
with
\rank E=s-s^{\prime},\deg E=d-d^{\prime}
,
which is equivalent to
d-d^{\prime}\geq e_{s-s^{\prime}}
.

Assume that the condition (
6.3
) does not hold.
Since we fix
s
, there are finitely many choices of
s^{\prime}
.
Hence
d-d^{\prime}
is sufficiently large since so is
d
, and
M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s-s^{\prime},d-d^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
by Proposition
5.1
.
In this case,
d-d^{\prime}
is sufficiently large and hence
d^{\prime}\leq d-e_{s-s^{\prime}}
holds.
∎

(2) By the definition of
\delta_{\flat}
, we have
\delta_{\flat}\leq\delta_{1}
.
Let
\frac{d+\delta_{\flat}}{s}=\frac{d^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}
with
1\leq s^{\prime}\leq s-1
and
d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
By the definition of
\delta_{\flat}
,
we have
\frac{d^{\prime}-1}{s^{\prime}}\leq\frac{d}{s}

and hence
d^{\prime}\leq\frac{s^{\prime}}{s}d+1\leq d-e_{s-s^{\prime}}
,
where the last inequality holds by

and
s^{\prime}<s
.
Thus
\delta_{\flat}\in\Gamma
and hence
\delta_{\flat}\geq\delta_{1}
.

(3) In this case,
\delta_{\sharp}=(s-1)d-se_{s-1}
by (
6.1
).
Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{d+\delta_{\sharp}}{s}=d-e_{s-1}=\frac{d^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}

for
s^{\prime}=1,d^{\prime}=d-e_{s-1}
.
Thus
\delta_{\sharp}\in\Gamma
.
∎

6.2.
The case
s>r

In this subsection, we assume
s>r
and

d

In this case,

\displaystyle\delta_{\sharp}=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq r}\frac{id-se_{i}}{s-i}=\frac{rd+se}{s-r}.

Proposition 6.3
.

Assume
s>r
and
d
is sufficiently large.
Let

\Gamma=\{\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}<\cdots<\delta_{N}\}\subset(0,\delta_{\sharp}]

be the set of
\delta\in(0,\delta_{\sharp}]
such that

\frac{d+\delta}{s}=\frac{d^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\quad\text{for some integers}\quad 1\leq s^{\prime}<s-r\quad\text{ or }\quad s-r\leq s^{\prime}\leq s-1,\ d^{\prime}\leq d-e_{s-s^{\prime}}.

Then

(1)

For
\delta>0
,
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)\neq M^{s}_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s,d)
holds if and only if

.

(2)

\delta_{1}=\delta_{\flat}
.

(3)

\delta_{N}=\delta_{\sharp}=\frac{rd+se}{s-r}

Proof.

(1) It suffices to show the following claim.

Claim 6.4
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\delta_{\sharp}]
and

1\leq s^{\prime}\leq s-1,\ d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}

such that
\frac{d+\delta}{s}=\frac{d^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}

Then

if and only if

•

, or

and

.

6.4
.

Assume

.
Then the
\delta_{\sharp}
of

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq r}\frac{i(d-d^{\prime})-(s-s^{\prime})e_{i}}{s-s^{\prime}-i}.

Hence we see that
\delta
is greater than (

\displaystyle d-d^{\prime}<\frac{(s-s^{\prime}-r)d-s^{\prime}e}{s-r}

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} d-d^{\prime}=d-\frac{s^{\prime}}{s}(d+\delta)&\geq d-\frac{s^{\prime}}{s}(d+\delta_{\sharp})\\
&=\frac{s-s^{\prime}}{s}d-\frac{s^{\prime}}{s}\cdot\frac{rd+se}{s-r}=\frac{(s-s^{\prime}-r)d-s^{\prime}e}{s-r}.\end{aligned}

Hence
\delta
is less than or equal to (
6.4
).
Since

is sufficiently large by (
6.5
) and

,
we have

.

If
s-s^{\prime}\leq r
,
we see that
M_{C,\delta}(E_{0},s-s^{\prime},d-d^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
if and only if
d^{\prime}\leq d-e_{s-s^{\prime}}
by the same proof as Claim
6.2
.
∎

(2) By definition,
\delta_{\flat}\leq\delta_{1}
.
Let
\frac{d+\delta_{\flat}}{s}=\frac{d^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}
with
1\leq s^{\prime}\leq s-1
and
d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
s^{\prime}<s-r
, then
\delta_{\flat}\in\Gamma
and hence
\delta_{\flat}\geq\delta_{1}
. If
s^{\prime}\geq s-r
,
by the definition of
\delta_{\flat}
,
we have
\frac{d^{\prime}-1}{s^{\prime}}\leq\frac{d}{s}

and hence
d^{\prime}\leq\frac{s^{\prime}}{s}d+1\leq d-e_{s-s^{\prime}}
.
Thus
\delta_{\flat}\in\Gamma
and hence
\delta_{\flat}\geq\delta_{1}
.

(3)
We have
\frac{d+\delta_{\sharp}}{s}=\frac{d+e}{s-r}
.
Therefore by taking
s^{\prime}=s-r,d^{\prime}=d-e_{r}=d+e
, we have
\delta_{\sharp}\in\Gamma
.
∎
