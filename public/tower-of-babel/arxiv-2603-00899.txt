A strong nullity parameter for rooted graphs
Aida Abiad, Mary Flagg, H. Tracy Hall, Jephian C.-H. Lin, Bryan Shader

Abstract
The inverse eigenvalue problem of a graph G G studies the possible spectra of matrices associated with G G , including as an important subproblem the possible nullities of such a matrix. Much research in this area to date has focused only on the spectrum of the matrix itself, but there are applications of inverse eigenvalue problems that also involve the interaction between that spectrum and the spectrum of some maximal proper principal submatrix, or in other words the interlacing spectrum that results from crossing out any one row and the same column. Motivated by this refined information, given a graph G G on n n vertices with a designated root vertex, we investigate all possible nullity pairs where the first nullity is that of an n × n n\times n symmetric matrix associated to G G and the second nullity is that of the principal submatrix of size ( n − 1 ) × ( n − 1 ) (n-1)\times(n-1) that results from deleting the row and column associated to the root vertex. We define a new parameter ξ ​ ξ ​ ( G , i ) \xi\xi(G,i) for rooted graphs ( G , i ) (G,i) equipped with the strong nullity interlacing property that coordinates the two values of a nullity pair, and we show that this graph parameter is minor monotone. Moreover, we prove a bifurcation lemma for the strong nullity interlacing property. We use these new tools to characterize the rooted graphs with ξ ​ ξ ​ ( G , i ) ≥ s \xi\xi(G,i)\geq s for s ∈ { 0 , 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 } s\in\{0,1,2,3,4,5\} by finding the minimal minors for each of these families. These families turn out to have strong connections to the minimal minors for ξ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ k \xi(G)\geq k .

Extending the root

We say that a rooted graph

Lemma 4.1
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

be a matrix in

Lemma 4.2

5
Schur complement

In this section, we provide some general results about the Schur complement that leads to many applications.

Let
A
be a real symmetric matrix of the form

A=\begin{bmatrix}Q&B^{\top}\\
B&C\end{bmatrix}

such that
Q
is invertible. Then the
Schur complement
of
Q
in
A
is denoted and defined by

A/Q=C-BQ^{-1}B^{\top}.

Note that the notion of the Schur complement is not limited to square matrices in general (though
Q
has to be square to be invertible), but we only focus on real symmetric cases. Also, the intuition behind the Schur complement is row and column operations

\begin{bmatrix}I&O\\
-BQ^{-1}&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}Q&B^{\top}\\
B&C\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&-Q^{-1}B^{\top}\\
O&I\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}Q&O\\
O&A/Q\end{bmatrix}.

Consequently, we have
\operatorname{null}(A)=\operatorname{null}(A/Q)
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
A
be an
n\times n
real symmetric matrix such that
A[\alpha]
is invertible for some
\alpha\subseteq[n]
. Then for any
i\notin\alpha
,
A
and
A/A[\alpha]
have the same
i
-nullity pair.

Proof.

By the property of the Schur complement,
\operatorname{null}(A)=\operatorname{null}(A/A[\alpha])
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
\alpha
are the first few indices and write

A=\begin{bmatrix}A[\alpha]&B^{\top}\\
B&C\end{bmatrix}

and thus
A/A[\alpha]=C-BA[\alpha]^{-1}B^{\top}
. By direct computation,

(A/A[\alpha])(i)=C(i)-(BA[\alpha]^{-1}B^{\top})(i)=A(i)/A[\alpha],

so
\operatorname{null}((A/A[\alpha])(i))=\operatorname{null}(A(i))
. In summary,
A
and
A/A[\alpha]
have the same
i
-nullity pair.
∎

In
[
3
, Lemma 3.5]
and
[
19
, Lemma 3.2]
, it was shown that
A
having SAP implies that
A/A[\alpha]
also has SAP, under some assumptions. Here we make a general statement for this behavior and extend it to
i
-SNIP.

Recall that for a subset
\alpha
of vertices of
G
,

N(\alpha)=\bigcup_{v\in\alpha}N(v)\setminus\alpha.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G
be a graph and
A\in\mathcal{S}(G)
. Suppose
\alpha\subseteq V(G)
is a subset of vertices such that
A[\alpha]
is invertible and
A/A[\alpha]\in\mathcal{S}(H)
for some graph
H
in which
N(\alpha)
is a clique. Then
A
having SAP implies that
A/A[\alpha]
has SAP, and for any given
i\notin\alpha
,
A
having
i
-SNIP implies that
A/A[\alpha]
has
i
-SNIP.

Proof.

Let
a=|\alpha|
and
b=|N(\alpha)|
. Without loss of generality, we may assume the first rows/columns are corresponding to
\alpha
and write

A=\begin{bmatrix}Q&B^{\top}\\
B&C\end{bmatrix}

with
Q=A[\alpha]
. We may further assume the first
b
rows in
B
correspond to
N(\alpha)
, so
B
is zero except for the first
b
rows. By the Schur complement, we have

\begin{bmatrix}I&O\\
-BQ^{-1}&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}Q&B^{\top}\\
B&C\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&-Q^{-1}B^{\top}\\
O&I\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}Q&O\\
O&A/Q\end{bmatrix},

which we write as
E^{\top}AE=Q\oplus(A/Q)
.

Let
X
be a real symmetric matrix such that
A/Q\circ X=I\circ X=O
. Define

\widehat{X}=E(O\oplus X)E^{\top}=\begin{bmatrix}Q^{-1}B^{\top}XBQ^{-1}&-Q^{-1}B^{\top}X\\
-XBQ^{-1}&X\end{bmatrix}.

We claim that
A\circ\widehat{X}=I\circ\widehat{X}=O
.
Since
A/Q\in\mathcal{S}(H)
and
N(\alpha)
is a clique in
H
, we have
X[N(\alpha)]=O
, which further implies
C\circ X=O
as
H
is a supergraph of
G-\alpha
.
Since
B
is zero except for the first
b
rows, each column of
XB
is a linear combination of the first
b
columns of
X
. The first
b
columns of
X
have their first
b
entries zero by
X[N(\alpha)]=O
, so the first
b
rows of
XB
are zero. Consequently, the first
b
rows of
-XBQ^{-1}
and the first
b
columns of
-Q^{-1}B^{\top}X
are zero. Moreover, as
B^{\top}
is zero except for the first
b
columns and
XB
is zero on the first
b
rows, we have
B^{\top}XB=O
and
Q^{-1}B^{\top}XBQ^{-1}=O
. Combining all these observations, we have
A\circ\widehat{X}=I\circ\widehat{X}=O
.

For SAP, it is straightforward to see that if
(A/Q)X=O
, then

(E^{\top}AE)(O\oplus X)=(Q\oplus A/Q)(O\oplus X)=O

and

A\widehat{X}=AE(O\oplus X)E^{\top}=(E^{\top})^{-1}(E^{\top}AE)(O\oplus X)E^{\top}=O.

In summary, if
A/Q\circ X=I\circ X=O
and
(A/Q)X=O
, then
A\circ\widehat{X}=I\circ\widehat{X}=O
and
A\widehat{X}=O
. Suppose
A
has SAP. Then
\widehat{X}=O
, which implies
O\oplus X=O
and
X=O
. Therefore,
A/Q
has SAP.

For
i
-SNIP, we use the notation
\overset{(i,:]}{=}O
to indicate that a matrix is zero except for possibly the
i
-th row. Following a similar argument, if
(A/Q)X\overset{(i,:]}{=}O
, then
(E^{\top}AE)(O\oplus X)\overset{(i,:]}{=}O
, which implies
(E^{\top}AE)(O\oplus X)E^{\top}\overset{(i,:]}{=}O
. Since each row of
(E^{\top})^{-1}
has entry
i
equal to
0
except possibly for the
i
-th row,

A\widehat{X}=AE(O\oplus X)E^{\top}=(E^{\top})^{-1}(E^{\top}AE)(O\oplus X)E^{\top}\overset{(i,:]}{=}O.

Therefore, if
A
has
i
-SNIP, then
A/Q
has
i
-SNIP.
∎

This lemma is particularly useful when
|N(\alpha)|=1
, which implies that

is a clique in

.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let

be a graph such that

are connected graphs on at least

Name the two graphs in the maximum as

Let

k+2=\frac{\xi\xi(G,i)+4}{2}

and the case
k<\ell
gives

\ell+1=\frac{\xi\xi(G,i)+3}{2}.

Along with
e(G)\geq e(G-i)
, we obtain the general bound

e(G)+1\geq\binom{m}{2}\ \ \mbox{ where }\ m=\bigg\lceil\frac{\xi\xi(G,i)+3}{2}\bigg\rceil.

∎

The previous result allows us to derive a Nordhaus–Gaddum type bound, an analogous result of
[
5
, Section 3]
.

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
(G,i)
be a rooted graph on
n
vertices. Then

\xi\xi(G,i)+\xi\xi(\overline{G},i)\leq 2\sqrt{2}n.

Proof.

By
Theorem

7.4
, with
m
as there defined, we have

e(G)+1\geq\binom{m}{2}\geq\ \frac{1}{8}(\xi\xi(G,i)^{2}+4\,\xi\xi(G,i)+3).

Let
x=\xi\xi(G,i)
,
\overline{x}=\xi\xi(\overline{G},i)
, and
s=x+\overline{x}
. Then we may take the sum of

\displaystyle e(G)+1

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8}(x^{2}+4x+3)\text{ and}

\displaystyle e(\overline{G})+1

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8}(\overline{x}^{2}+4\overline{x}+3)

to get

\binom{n}{2}+2\geq\frac{1}{8}(x^{2}+\overline{x}^{2}+4s+6).

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

(x^{2}+\overline{x}^{2})(1^{2}+1^{2})\geq(x+\overline{x})^{2}=s^{2}.

Therefore,

\binom{n}{2}+2\geq\frac{1}{8}(\frac{1}{2}s^{2}+4s+6).

By solving the inequality, we have

s\leq-4+\sqrt{16+8n(n-1)},

which is bounded above by
\sqrt{8n^{2}}=2\sqrt{2}n
if
n\geq 0
.
∎

When the Colin de Verdière parameter
\mu(G)
was defined in
[
11
,
10
]
, SAP was introduced by an equivalent definition through the surjectivity of a certain map.
This was then translated to a null space characterization in
[
2
,
21
]
,
using the span of certain matrices, here called “ingredients”, coming from either the vertices or the edges of the graph
G
.
Since the characterization depends only on the subspace and the graph,
by extension it also allows for a variant of SAP to be defined on a subspace other than the kernel.

Definition 7.6
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices labeled
\{1,\dots,n\}
, and let
L
be a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of dimension
m
. The subspace
L
gives a
full recipe from
G
if, given a matrix
N
whose
m
columns form a basis for
L

and whose
n
rows we call
\mathbf{u}_{1}^{\top},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{n}^{\top}
, the following set of
m\times m
real symmetric
ingredients
spans
the entire space
\operatorname{Sym}_{m}(\mathbb{R})
:

•

\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\phantom{{}^{\top}}}\mspace{-3.5mu}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\mspace{-3mu}\top}
for
j
any vertex of
G
(called
vertex ingredients
) and

•

\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\phantom{{}^{\top}}}\mspace{-3.5mu}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\mspace{-3mu}\top}+\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\phantom{{}^{\top}}}\mspace{-3.5mu}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\mspace{-3mu}\top}
for
\{j,k\}
any edge of
G
(called
edge ingredients
).

We remark that while the definition of a full recipe appears to depend on the choice of a basis matrix for
L
, it is nonetheless well-defined for any
L
.
Suppose that
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are two basis matrices, implying
N_{1}=N_{2}Q
for some
m\times m
invertible matrix

. Let
M\in\operatorname{Sym}_{m}(\mathbb{R})
be given. Observe that
M
is a linear combination of matrices of the form
\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\phantom{{}^{\top}}}\mspace{-3.5mu}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\mspace{-3mu}\top}
and
\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\phantom{{}^{\top}}}\mspace{-3.5mu}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\mspace{-3mu}\top}+\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\phantom{{}^{\top}}}\mspace{-3.5mu}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\mspace{-3mu}\top}
(ingredients from
N_{1}
) if and only if
Q^{\top}\!MQ
is a linear combination of matrices of the form
Q^{\top}\!\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\phantom{{}^{\top}}}\mspace{-3.5mu}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\mspace{-3mu}\top}Q
and
Q^{\top}\!\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\phantom{{}^{\top}}}\mspace{-3.5mu}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\mspace{-3mu}\top}Q+Q^{\top}\!\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\phantom{{}^{\top}}}\mspace{-3.5mu}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\mspace{-3mu}\top}Q
(ingredients from

).
Note that the map

is a bijection from
\operatorname{Sym}_{m}(\mathbb{R})
to itself.
Whether the definition holds or not is thus independent of the choice of basis for the subspace

.

Concluding remarks

In this paper, we introduced a new parameter
\xi\xi(G,i)
as a tool to understand the nullity pair problem. We established various theoretical results for

i
-SNIP.
Using these results we were able to characterize the graphs with
\xi\xi(G,i)\geq s
for
s\in\{4,5\}
.

Question 8.1
.

In the known cases of minimal minors for
\xi\xi(G,i)\geq 2k
, corresponding to values
k\in\{0,1,2\}
, the minimal minors are precisely the minimal minors for
\xi(G)\geq k+1
, rooted at any vertex that is not a cut-vertex.
Does this pattern continue for
k>2
?

We proved that the minimal minors for
\xi\xi(G,i)\geq 2k+1
and the minimal minors for
\xi\xi(G,i)\geq 2k
can be obtained from each other, reducing the cases to be studied by half.
(This implies in particular that any case of an answer to
8.1
would also resolve the case
\xi\xi(G,i)\geq 2k+1
.)

These results fit in the general framework of the inverse eigenvalue problem for a graph
G
as extended to the
(\lambda,\mu)
-problem, which studies the simultaneous spectra both of a matrix described by a graph and of the principal submatrix corresponding to deletion of a single root vertex.
