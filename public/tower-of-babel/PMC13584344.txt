Immune-related adverse events with cancer clinical benefits: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Zhijun Li, Qing Sun, Xueling Yang, Jing Ni, Yao Zhang, Jing Wang, Keyu Ding, Guanlin Wang, Huiran Yang, Qiong Yu, Yan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGrowing research have reported an association of the occurrence of immune-related adverse events (irAEs) and clinical benefits in patients with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) treatments, but the findings remain controversial. The aim of this study is to identify whether the incidence of irAEs and their subtypes undergoing ICIs treatment is associated with enhanced efficacy and survival benefits in all solid tumors.MethodsCochrane Library, Embase, PubMed and Web of Science databases were searched from database inception and December 31, 2023. Two reviewers independently extracted data and reviewed the quality items using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale criteria, and resolved any discrepancies by consensus discussion with third reviewer. We performed the systematic review and meta-analysis in compliance with the guidelines of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analysis and Meta-analysis of Observational Studies in Epidemiology. The main outcomes were irAEs incidence, objective response rate (ORR), overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). Subgroup and sensitivity analyses were performed.ResultsA total of 184 articles involving a total of 63,780 patients met the inclusion criteria. The pooled incidence of irAEs in this meta-analysis was 37.3% (95%CI: 34.4%-40.3%). The occurrence of irAEs was associated with increased ORR (RR, 2.32 [95%CI, 2.02–2.67]; I2 = 80.1%; P < 0.001), and pooled ORRs in irAE+ versus irAE- patients were 39.4% (95%CI: 35.4%-43.5%) versus 16.2% (95%CI: 13.5%-19.4%). Besides, irAEs development presented better weighted average OS (20.03 months versus 10.83 months), and associated with longer OS (HR, 0.55 [95%CI, 0.51–0.59]; I2 = 70.4%; P < 0.001). Similar finding in PFS, weighted average PFS in irAEs patients was 9.73 months versus 4.15 months in without irAEs patients, and irAEs occurrence improved PFS (HR, 0.58 [95%CI, 0.53–0.63]; I2 = 50.8%; P < 0.001) compared with those who did not.ConclusionsThe intimate association was observed between the development of irAEs and increased response rate and improved survival benefits in patients who treated with ICIs, and may have useful prognostic value in ICIs therapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-026-16446-9.Keywords: Immune-related adverse events, Immune checkpoint inhibitors, Cancer, Efficacy, Benefits

Background

Cancer is the leading cause of mortality globally, and the important public health problem worldwide, despite advances in prevention, diagnostic and therapeutic strategies in the past decades [1, 2]. Meanwhile, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has yielded unprecedented clinical success in treating multiple cancer types, which has reshaped the therapeutic landscape of cancer therapy [3–6]. To date, two major monoclonal antibodies in cancer immunotherapies targeted programmed cell death-1 (PD-1) or programmed cell death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen-4 (CTLA-4) axes, respectively; and 9 ICIs drugs were approved and with expansion to different targets and cancers [7–9]. ICIs would reactivate antitumor cytolytic function of the exhausted immune T cells, and may affect peripheral tolerance to autoantigens, resulted with increased even excessive inflammation and immunologic activation, through blocking the aforementioned inhibitory pathway between T lymphocytes and tumor or antigen-presenting cells [10]. Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) are the unique toxicities resembling autoimmune responses in ICIs therapy, which may affect multiple organ systems at any time including organ-specific toxicities and non-organ-specific toxicities [11, 12]. The most commonly manifestations are skin, endocrine, gastrointestinal, liver, lung, renal, muscles and so on. The overall incidence of irAEs ranges from 54% to 76%, most irAEs tend to be mild and self-limited; while 5%-40% of severe irAEs even induce more serious rarer or life-threatening irAEs by affecting cardiovascular and central nervous systems [7, 10, 11, 13–15], if not be early recognized or appropriated treatments. Previous studies suggested that the reinvigorated immune activation of anergized immune cells would attack both tumor and normal tissue cells, which would induce irAEs limiting the use of ICIs in many patients, while might predict a better efficacy and benefits to ICIs treatment [16]. Nevertheless, the association of irAEs occurrence and the efficacy and benefits are yet to be resolved, however the predictive biomarkers identify may be used to properly select the right patients to receive locoregional therapies plus immunotherapy and to provide useful information for disease-monitoring as well as treatment-decision making, which is highly desirable and paramount for refining therapeutic strategies particularly in the era of precision medicine [2, 11, 17–19].

Although numerous recent studies and reviews have investigated the irAEs development and efficacy and survival benefits in patients with ICIs therapy, and supported the hypothesis of favorable response and survival prognosis in various tumor patients who developed several specific types of irAEs undergoing ICIs treatment, and irAEs can serve as an effective clinical biomarker for ICIs treatments in several cancers [15, 20]. IrAEs may predict higher response and longer survival prognosis in patients with renal cell carcinoma (RCC), hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC), melanoma (MM), urothelial carcinoma (UC) and cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) who received PD-1 inhibitors combination treatment, and may serve as an effective clinical biomarker to predict the efficacy of ICIs therapy in various cancer types [15, 18, 21–23]. However, still lack a definite conclusion and exist contradictory findings in the predictive value of irAEs occurrence and development for efficacy and survival benefits, for example, several previous studies and reviews showed that irAEs including pneumonitis, thyroid disruption, myalgia, and mucosal toxicity were not observed with a significant association with survival outcomes [16, 22, 24, 25], which might be due to the paucity of data in specifically subgroup analyses and needed for further validation.

To our knowledge, the robust and precise association between irAEs and efficacy and survival benefits in the context of specific irAE types, irAEs severity, ICIs types, cancer types, or geographic regions remains controversial and required to further evaluate. Meanwhile, importantly there are numbers of high-quality relevant studies occurrence in recent years. Herein, we performed the largest comprehensive and up-to-date systematic review and meta-analysis to determine the association of the development of irAEs and the efficacy and benefits in patients with ICIs treatments, and to investigate the predictive effects of specific irAEs types and grades for improving the novel insight into individualized precision treatment with the growing understanding of irAEs.

Methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis were performed in compliance with the guidelines of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analysis (PRISMA) and Meta-analysis of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (MOOSE) [26, 27]. As for only published studies included in this study, Beihua University deemed which from ethic review and waived informed consent.

Search strategy

We searched the Cochrane Library, Embase, PubMed and Web of Science databases for studies published between database inception and December 31, 2023. The following search terms were used for the initial search: (adverse event OR immune-related adverse event OR irAEs) AND (CTLA-4 OR cytotoxic T lymphocyte associated protein 4 OR PD-1 OR programmed death receptor 1 OR PD-L1 OR programmed death-ligand 1 OR immune-checkpoint inhibitors OR checkpoint blockade OR ICI) AND (efficacy OR benefit OR response OR survival OR prognosis OR outcome OR mortality OR ORR OR OS OR PFS) AND (cancer OR carcinoma OR tumor OR malignancy).

Selection process

Two reviewers (Z.L. and Q.S.) independently performed a rapid screening of all citations from the electronic databases through titles and abstracts, and then conducted the full-text review of all potentially relevant studies to determine the final eligible articles (eTable 1 in Supplement 1). Additionally, any additional eligible studies were identified by screening the citations of the relevant studies. Any disagreements or discrepancies among the process were discussed and resolved by consensus discussion with third reviewer (Y.L.).

Inclusion criteria

The following criteria were considered essential for study inclusion: (1) patients with solid tumors who received ICIs treatment, including CTLA-4, PD-(L)1 inhibitors; (2) prospective, retrospective, or randomized clinical trial of study design; (3) English publication without region restrictions; (4) outcomes included the incidence of irAEs, objective response rates (ORR), overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with and without irAEs; (5) availability data for RR or HRs with 95% CIs of response rate and survival data; and (6) animal studies, case reports, reviews and clinical guidelines were excluded.

Data extraction and study quality

Two reviewers (Z.L. and Q.S.) extracted data independently and resolved any discrepancies by consensus discussion with third reviewer (Y.L.). If articles provided multiple independent cohorts and cancer types and irAEs subtypes, which were all extracted and listed separately and we are presenting them as the separate data. The aforementioned two reviewers (Z.L. and Q.S.) independently reviewed the quality items and potential differences of scoring in the eligible studies using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) criteria [28] (eTable 3 in Supplement 1).

Statistical analysis

Pooled incidence of irAEs with 95%CIs was calculated by weighted averages using the DerSimonian-Laird random-effects model on the number of patients experienced irAEs and patients with ICIs treatment in this meta-analysis. Meanwhile, pooled ORRs in patients with irAEs and without irAEs were conducted to evaluate the treatment response effect of irAEs in ICIs patients, respectively. Weighted averages were performed for studies reporting OS and PFS numeric data in patients with and without irAEs [19]. Besides, pooled RRs and their corresponding 95%CIs were calculated to assess the association of irAEs development with ICIs response, that greater than 1 indicated an increased response. Pooled HRs and their corresponding 95%CIs were calculated to assess the association of irAEs development with survival benefits, that less than 1 indicated a favorable prognosis. We conducted both fixed- and random- effects meta-analyses using the generic inverse-variance method to assess the overall effect size based on the reported available ORR, OS and/or PFS data in patients with or without irAEs of the included studies. I2 statistics were used to estimate the proportion of variance in the data contributed to heterogeneity rather than sampling error, and I2 score of 25% or less indicated a low heterogeneity. Subgroup analyses were performed by irAEs subtype, the severity of irAEs, ICIs type, cancer type and study characteristics, as long as at least two data were available for pooled results to examine the association of irAEs development and efficacy and/or survival benefits. Sensitivity analysis was conducted by sequentially excluding each study. Publication bias was assessed by the visual analysis of funnel plot, Begg test and Egger test. If publication bias presented in the meta-analysis, the trim-and-fill method was used to identify and address potential publication bias through estimate the potentially missing studies and adding these hypothetical data to original analysis comparing the adjusted effect size with original results [29]. All statistical analyses were calculated using R 4.1.3 meta package, and P < 0.05 indicated statistically significant.

Results

Study selection

A total of 11,788 citations were identified in the aforementioned electronic databases, and the overview process of study selection shows in Fig. 1. After removing 2208 duplicate citations, we identified 9580 articles that examined the association between ICIs treatment and irAEs prognostic value through comprehensive analysis of the titles and abstracts, and 237 full-text studies assessed for eligible. Finally, 184 articles involving a total of 63,780 patients met the established criteria and were included in the meta-analysis (eResults and eTable 2 in Supplement 1).

Flowchart of study selection process

Study characteristics

eResults, eTable 2 and eTable 4 in Supplement 1 show an overview of the eligible studies characteristics and clinical outcomes. The sample sizes in the included 184 studies ranged from 15 to 14,016 patients (median:130), and 22,900 experienced irAEs. The majority of studies were retrospective (n = 164), whereas 10 were prospective and 10 were clinical trials. All included studies examined the association of irAEs development with the efficacy and benefit in patients with ICIs treatment, and provided at least one or more of ORR, OS and PFS data. A total of 93 studies provided ORR data, 153 studies included OS data, 132 studies provided PFS data; 14 studies included only ORR data, 27 studies provided only OS data, 10 studies provided only PFS data; 116 studies provided both OS and PFS data and 49 studies provided both ORR, OS and PFS data. In terms of ICIs type, most included studies focused on PD-(L)1 inhibitor (n = 127), 17 studies examined CTLA-4 inhibitors monotherapy and other 40 studies looked at any ICIs. With regard to geographic region, 76 studies were conducted in Asia-Pacific countries, 46 in European countries, 54 in the America countries and 8 in Global. In terms of cancer type, 63 studies focused on lung cancer including 60 of NSCLC, 2 of SCLC, 43 on MM, 11 on RCC, 7 on HNSCC, 6 on UC, 5 on GC, 2 on HCC, 2 on CC, 2 on CCA, 2 on EC,1 on Sarcoma, the remaining 40 included mixed tumor types. The median irAEs onset time ranged from 2.4 to 28 weeks (9.8 ± 5.8), and the follow-up times ranged from 3 to 54.2 months (15.8 ± 9.4). We used NOS criteria to evaluate the quality of each included study in the meta-analysis, and the NOS score allocated for the included studies ranged from 5 to 9 stars (7.54 ± 0.95) (eTable 2 in Supplement 1).

Incidence of irAEs

All 184 eligible studies reported incidence of irAEs in patients who received ICIs treatment, which ranged from 1.91% to 85.29%, and 47 studies of them looked specifically at individual or organ specific irAEs. The pooled incidence of irAEs involving 189 independent data in this meta-analysis was 37.3% (95%CI: 34.4%-40.3%) (eTable 5 and eFigure 1 in Supplement 1), and the pooled incidence of any irAEs was 41.3% (38.1%-44.6%), 32.3% (26.6%-39.2%) of skin irAEs, 18.7% (12.5%-26.9%) of endocrine irAEs, 35.5% (21.2%-52.9%) of gastrointestinal irAEs and 26.6% (15.4%-41.8%) of pneumonitis irAEs. Furthermore, the pooled incidence of irAEs in patients treated with anti-PD-(L)1 was 35.5% (32.3%-38.9%), and 48.6% (38.3%-59.1%) of anti-CTLA-4 therapy (eTable 5 in Supplement 1).

Objective response rate

A total of 81 studies (n = 19533) provided ORR data and 93 studies (n = 21585) provided data for evaluating the association of irAEs occurrence and ORR in patients receiving ICIs treatment. The pooled ORRs involving 81 studies in the meta-analysis were 39.4% (95%CI: 35.4%-43.5%) and 16.2% (95%CI: 13.5%-19.4%) in irAE+ versus irAE- patients who receiving ICIs treatment. Furthermore, the pooled ORRs in irAE+ versus irAE- patients treated with anti-PD-(L)1 were 40.1% (35.8%-44.6%) versus 17.7% (14.7%-21.2%), and 22.9% (17.2%-29.8%) versus 9.9% (8.2%-11.9%) for anti-CTLA-4 therapy (eTable 6 and eFigure 2 in Supplement 1). Our meta-analysis of ORR included 93 studies, and a significant difference between irAEs group and non-irAEs group (RR, 2.32 [95%CI, 2.02–2.67]; I2 = 80.1%; P < 0.001) was observed that patients experienced irAEs with ICIs treatment had higher ORR than those without irAEs patients (Fig. 2 in Supplement 1). In the subgroup meta-analysis based on irAEs subtypes and grade, patients who had the following subtypes and severity of irAEs had a significant higher ORR than patients who did not: endocrine (RR, 1.86 [95%CI, 1.28–2.71]; I2 = 0.0%; P = 0.001), pneumonitis (RR, 2.07 [95%CI, 1.57–2.73]; I2 = 50.1%; P < 0.001), rheumatlogic and/or arthritis (RR, 5.75 [95%CI, 3.16–10.48]; I2 = 21.6%; P < 0.001), single irAEs (RR, 1.92 [95%CI, 1.60–2.30]; I2 = 0.0%; P < 0.001), multi-organ irAEs (RR, 3.63 [95%CI, 1.83–7.18]; I2 = 85.7%; P < 0.001) and severe irAEs (RR, 3.62 [95%CI, 1.69–7.76]; I2 = 19.6%; P < 0.001). Consistent results were also observed across all subgroups stratified by study design, sample size, geographic region, analysis method, cancers type, and ICIs type, aligning with the overall estimate of higher ORR. However, no statistically significant differences were identified in terms of ORR within studies conducted in the CCA, skin-irAE, Gastrointestinal-irAE, and mild irAEs patients (Table 1).

Overall survival

Of the 133 studies (n = 55683) reported OS data, and 95 studies (n = 24918) reported numeric OS data. 81 studies (n = 22311) both reported numeric OS data of irAE + and irAE – group, and the weighted average OS was 20.03 (95%CI: 2.12–37.94) months versus 10.83 (95%CI: 1.24–20.41) months in irAEs+ versus irAEs- patients. Furthermore, the weighted average OS of in irAEs+ versus irAEs- patients treated with anti-PD-(L)1 was 20.86 (95%CI: 4.42–37.29) versus 10.44 (95%CI: 1.97–18.9) months, and 15.62 (95%CI: 0.75–30.48) versus 9.12 (95%CI: 6.19–12.06) months with anti-CTLA-4 therapy (eTable 6 in Supplement 1). Our meta-analysis of OS included 131 studies that an association between irAEs occurrence and longer OS (HR, 0.55 [95%CI, 0.51–0.59]; I2 = 70.4%; P < 0.001) was observed (Fig. 3). Besides, results of landmark analysis were available for 23 studies (n = 25521) with 38 data for OS, and meta-analysis of these included studies revealed patients with irAEs occurrence demonstrated improved OS (HR, 0.63 [95%CI, 0.53–0.75]; I2 = 77.6%; P < 0.001) compared with those who did not (Table 1 and eFigure 3 A in Supplement 1).

Associations between development of immune-related adverse events (irAEs) and progression-free survival

In the stratification analysis on irAEs subtypes, patients who had one of those irAEs subtypes had a significant advantage in OS compared with individuals who did not: skin irAEs (HR, 0.56 [95%CI, 0.49–0.64]; I2 = 66.7%; P < 0.001), endocrine irAEs (HR, 0.59 [95%CI, 0.54–0.64]; I2 = 44.2%; P < 0.001), gastrointestinal irAEs (HR, 0.69 [95%CI, 0.61–0.78]; I2 = 55.6%; P < 0.001), musculoskeletal irAEs (HR, 0.46 [95%CI, 0.26–0.81]; I2 = 0.0%; P = 0.006), rheumatologic and/or arthritis irAEs (HR, 0.46 [95%CI, 0.32–0.66]; I2 = 0.0%; P < 0.001), pancreatitis irAEs (HR, 1.94 [95%CI, 1.21–3.09]; I2 = 44.1%; P = 0.005), thrombocytopenia irAEs (HR, 2.36 [95%CI, 1.48–3.77]; I2 = 0.0%; P < 0.001). Nevertheless, there were no favorable OS were observed in pulmonary (HR, 1.26 [95%CI, 0.98–1.62]; I2 = 63.2%; P = 0.07), hepatobiliary (HR, 0.99 [95%CI, 0.87–1.12]; I2 = 8.2%; P = 0.87), myelosuppression (HR, 0.82 [95%CI, 0.65–1.03]; I2 = 0.0%; P = 0.08), and mucositis (HR, 0.59 [95%CI, 0.02–14.32]; I2 = 77.3%; P = 0.74), due to the paucity of data and need further research. In terms of severity of irAEs, patients who treated with ICIs experiencing single irAEs, multi-organ irAEs and mild irAEs were observed a significantly favorable OS compared with who did not (single irAEs: HR, 0.61 [95%CI, 0.54–0.69]; I2 = 50.3%; P < 0.001; multi-organ: HR, 0.39 [95%CI, 0.34–0.44]; I2 = 0.0%; P < 0.001; mild irAEs: HR, 0.58 [95%CI, 0.51–0.66]; I2 = 15.2%; P < 0.001), but not in severe irAEs (HR, 0.86 [95%CI, 0.65–1.13]; I2 = 72.8%; P = 0.27) (Table 1).

In the stratification analysis by study design, a better survival observed in irAEs group of prospective studies (HR, 0.54 [95%CI, 0.48–0.62]; I2 = 0.0%; P < 0.001), retrospective (HR, 0.55 [95%CI, 0.51–0.60]; I2 = 70.5%; P < 0.001), but not observed in clinical trials (HR, 0.46 [95%CI, 0.20–1.03]; I2 = 79.9%; P = 0.05). Regardless of geographic region, a significant advantage was observed for patients in Asia-Pacific countries (HR, 0.50 [95%CI, 0.45–0.56]; I2 = 57.8%; P < 0.001), Europe (HR, 0.52 [95%CI, 0.46–0.59]; I2 = 64.0%; P < 0.001), and America (HR, 0.67 [95%CI, 0.56–0.79]; I2 = 75.0%; P < 0.001) who developed irAEs with ICIs treatment. Additionally, a noteworthy association between irAEs occurrence and enhanced OS in LC (HR, 0.55 [95%CI, 0.50–0.61]; I2 = 69.3%; P < 0.001; NSCLC: HR, 0.54 [95%CI, 0.50–0.69]; I2 = 65.8%; P < 0.001), MM (HR, 0.53 [95%CI, 0.46–0.62]; I2 = 68.9%; P < 0.001), Digestive system cancers (HR, 0.49 [95%CI, 0.40–0.59]; I2 = 60.3%; P < 0.001) and Urogenital tumors (HR, 0.52 [95%CI, 0.47–0.59]; I2 = 21.9%; P < 0.001) were observed, while not found in GC and CCA patients. In addition, patients experiencing irAEs who treated with anti-PD-(L)1 had a longer OS (HR, 0.52 [95%CI, 0.47–0.56]; I2 = 69.1%; P < 0.001) than those who did not, while no related with irAEs in anti-CTLA-4 treatment (HR, 0.84 [95%CI, 0.64–1.09]; I2 = 62.9%; P = 0.20). Consistent findings for OS were also observed in subgroup meta-analyses on ICIs monotherapy, ICIs combination therapy, study size and analysis model (Table 1).

Progression-free survival

Of the 112 studies (n = 27462) reported PFS data, and 82 studies (n = 17296) reported numeric PFS data. The weighted average PFS including 82 studies was 9.73 (95%CI: -1.44-20.91) months versus 4.15 (95%CI: -1.22-9.52) months in irAEs+ versus irAEs- patients. Furthermore, the weighted average PFS of in irAEs+ versus irAEs- patients treated with anti-PD-(L)1 was 9.47 (95%CI: -1.42-20.37) versus 3.97 (95%CI: -1.04-8.98) months, and 18.75 (95%CI: 6.3-31.43) versus 2.29 (95%CI: 1.4–3.19) months with anti-CTLA-4 therapy (eTable 6 in Supplement 1). Similar to OS, our meta-analysis of PFS including 112 studies observed that patients who experienced irAEs with ICIs treatment had a prolonged PFS (HR, 0.54 [95%CI, 0.50–0.58]; I2 = 70.1%; P < 0.001) than those non-irAEs patients (Fig. 4). Besides, results of landmark analysis were available for 19 studies (n = 5774) with 25 data for PFS, and the meta-analysis of these included studies revealed patients with irAEs occurrence demonstrated improved PFS (HR, 0.58 [95%CI, 0.53–0.63]; I2 = 50.8%; P < 0.001) compared with those who did not (Table 1 and eFigure 3B in Supplement 1).

Associations between development of immune-related adverse events (irAEs) and progression-free survival

Stratification analysis by irAEs subtype showed that evident PFS benefits existed in skin irAEs (HR, 0.52 [95%CI, 0.44–0.61]; I2 = 72.2%; P < 0.001), endocrine irAEs (HR, 0.68 [95%CI, 0.63–0.73]; I2 = 46.3%; P < 0.001), gastrointestinal irAEs (HR, 0.77 [95%CI, 0.64–0.94]; I2 = 56.7%; P = 0.01), pulmonary (HR, 0.90 [95%CI, 0.91–0.99]; I2 = 54.7%; P = 0.03), rheumatologic and/or arthritis irAEs (HR, 0.44 [95%CI, 0.31–0.62]; I2 = 0.0%; P < 0.001), pancreatitis irAEs (HR, 1.58 [95%CI, 1.00-2.48]; I2 = 0.0%; P = 0.04), myelosuppression (HR, 0.72 [95%CI, 0.59–0.89]; I2 = 0.0%; P = 0.002) and musculoskeletal (HR, 0.52 [95%CI, 0.27-1.00]; I2 = 0.0%; P = 0.05). But, no favorable PFS were observed in hepatobiliary (HR, 0.97 [95%CI, 0.86–1.08]; I2 = 50.9%; P = 0.57), due to the paucity of data and need further research. Regardless of the severity of irAEs, patients who experienced single irAEs, multi-organ irAEs, mild irAEs and severe irAEs receiving ICIs treatment presented a significantly favorable PFS compared with who did not (single irAEs: HR, 0.65 [95%CI, 0.50–0.83]; I2 = 84.0%; P < 0.001; multi-organ: HR, 0.56 [95%CI, 0.48–0.65]; I2 = 37.3%; P < 0.001; mild irAEs: HR, 0.52 [95%CI, 0.45–0.61]; I2 = 21.7%; P < 0.001; severe irAEs: HR, 0.71 [95%CI, 0.59–0.84]; I2 = 61.5%; P < 0.001) (Table 1).

Stratification analysis by study design, patients with irAEs had a longer PFS compared with non-irAEs patients in prospective studies (HR, 0.53 [95%CI, 0.42–0.66]; I2 = 52.8%; P < 0.001), retrospective (HR, 0.55 [95%CI, 0.51–0.59]; I2 = 70.6%; P < 0.001), and clinical trials (HR, 0.37 [95%CI, 0.17–0.82]; I2 = 81.0%; P = 0.01). In terms of geographic region, a significant better advantage was observed for patients who developed irAEs with ICIs treatment in Asia-Pacific countries (HR, 0.52 [95%CI, 0.47–0.58]; I2 = 66.0%; P < 0.001), Europe (HR, 0.53 [95%CI, 0.46–0.62]; I2 = 75.1%; P < 0.001), and America (HR, 0.57 [95%CI, 0.48–0.68]; I2 = 66.0%; P < 0.001). Furthermore, irAEs occurrence was positively association with enhanced PFS in LC (HR, 0.57 [95%CI, 0.53–0.62]; I2 = 60.1%; P < 0.001; NSCLC: HR, 0.58 [95%CI, 0.54–0.63]; I2 = 60.0%; P < 0.001), MM (HR, 0.59 [95%CI, 0.49–0.69]; I2 = 72.0%; P < 0.001), Digestive system cancers (HR, 0.50 [95%CI, 0.42–0.60]; I2 = 60.8%; P < 0.001) and Urogenital tumors (HR, 0.44 [95%CI, 0.33–0.58]; I2 = 56.9%; P < 0.001), while not found in HCC and EC patients. In addition, patients who experienced irAEs treated with anti-PD-(L)1 had a longer PFS (HR, 0.53 [95%CI, 0.49–0.57]; I2 = 64.5%; P < 0.001), while no relationship between irAEs and PFS in anti-CTLA-4 treatment (HR, 0.96 [95%CI, 0.63–1.48]; I2 = 72.3%; P = 0.86). Consistent findings for PFS were also observed for the subgroup meta-analyses by ICIs monotherapy, ICIs combination therapy, study size and analysis model, which similar to OS (Table 1).

Sensitivity analysis and publication bias

In the sensitivity analysis, the pooled results for ORR, OS and PFS in this meta-analysis by the sequent omitting every study, all remained significantly consistent demonstrated that the associations of the occurrence of irAEs and the efficacy and benefits in patients who treated with ICIs was reliable and robust. In terms of publication bias, the funnel plot for ORR, OS and PFS displayed asymmetry and revealed publication bias by visual analysis, and observed in both Begg test and Egger test (eFigure 4–8 in Supplement 1). Therefore, we used the trim-and-fill method to evaluate the effect of publication bias to the combined results. Although 35, 47 and 36 studies were imputed for ORR, OS and PFS, the pooled results (ORR: HR, 1.70 [95%CI, 1.46–1.98]; I2 = 81.7%; P < 0.001; OS: HR, 0.67 [95%CI, 0.61–0.72]; I2 = 74.3%; P < 0.001; PFS: HR, 0.65 [95%CI, 0.59–0.70]; I2 = 76.6%; P < 0.001) supported the stability of findings in this meta-analysis that consistent with the original results (eFigure 9–11 in Supplement 1).

Discussion

Identifying reliable predictive biomarkers of the efficacy and clinical benefits to ICIs has always been the focus and hotspot of in-depth research in cancer immunotherapy, and growing researches suggest that irAEs development might be increase the overall response rates and consequently improve prognosis in various tumor patients [7, 19, 30, 31]. However, the precise correlation of them still remains controversial and debate as for our knowledge so far [32, 33], especially for specific irAEs subtypes, ICIs types or cancer types and need further research. Therefore, we performed the largest and most comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis so far, which included 184 articles comprising a total of 63,780 patients, to assess the association between the occurrence of irAEs and response and prognosis in patients receiving ICIs therapy. The findings suggested a significant association between the irAEs in patients experienced ICIs treatment compared with those who did not was associated with prolonged prognosis among tumor. The occurrence of irAEs presented a better efficacy and benefit with substantially increased ORR, longer OS and improved PFS, which might be a feasible clinical predictor of efficacy and survival benefit. Besides, more comprehensive stratified analyses in this study were performed and showed the findings was irrespective of the study design, geographic region, study size and analysis methods.

In terms of irAEs subtype, patients who experienced irAEs had increased response rate only observed in those presenting endocrine, pneumonitis, rheumatologic and/or arthritis irAEs, not in skin, gastrointestinal irAEs. Besides, the survival benefit in patients with irAEs were observed in patients presenting skin, endocrine, gastrointestinal, musculoskeletal, rheumatologic and/or arthritis, pancreatitis, thrombocytopenia, but not observed in hepatobiliary irAEs. While pulmonary and myelosuppression irAEs only presented longer PFS, and mucositis irAEs showed no favorable OS, but due to the paucity of data and need further research. In terms of severity of irAEs, severe irAEs tended to be actively associated with higher ORR, but not observed in mild irAEs. Patients who experienced mild irAEs presented a significantly favorable OS compared with who did not, but not in severe irAEs patients. For PFS, the development of mild irAEs and severe irAEs both enhanced PFS in patients with ICIs treatment. The development of irAEs was significantly associated with a favorable survival benefit in patients with anti-PD-(L)1 treatment but not in anti-CTLA-4 therapy, and increased ORR were observed in both patients with anti-PD(L)1 and anti-CTLA-4 treatment. Unfortunately, due to data deficiency in several subgroup meta-analyses and need to further research.

To date, the mechanism underlying of the association of irAEs and efficacy and benefits is still not completely elucidated. Antigen sharing or tissue cross reactivity between tumor cells and normal tissues as one of the most promising hypotheses, and may be a possible explanation for the association of irAEs and the efficacy of ICIs [19, 34]. Due to the antigen mimicry theory, the T-cell-mediated immune response not only on tumor cells but also to normal cells as well generating both treatment response and immune toxicity, thereby predicting better efficacy and benefits [35]. Based on the potential hypotheses, more severe irAEs indicated the better efficacy and benefit in cancer immunotherapy, however only increased ORR and better PFS observed in patients experienced severe irAEs, but no for OS in this meta-analysis. Consistent findings with previous studies in OS [32, 36, 37], that might be due to the increasing mortality in severe patients, and may counteract the immune efficacy and survival benefit. For example, vitiligo in melanoma patients with ICIs treatment is a unique clinical manifestation of this main hypotheses, and positively associated with efficacy observed in both melanoma and NSCLC patients for now, which might be induced by the increased melanocyte-specific CD8 + T-cell activity to shared antigens between melanocytes and tumor cells [32, 38]. Previous studies suggested that gastrointestinal irAEs enjoyed a significant improved survival benefits may contribute to the diversity of baseline intestinal flora [39, 40]. Consistent findings with previous studies in pulmonary and hepatobiliary irAEs for efficacy and benefit, which might be attributed to the severity of them and the highest incidence of these two irAEs with anti-PD-(L)1 treatment and the basic diseases among advanced or metastatic patients in mostly eligible studies [16, 41], while pulmonary enjoyed a significant longer PFS in this study. In addition, anti-CTLA-4 therapy was associated with increased ORR but not with better prognosis, which might due to the more severe irAEs and higher mortality with anti-CTLA-4 treatment than anti-PD-(L)1 [42–45]. Moreover, another possible explanation for their association between irAEs and ICIs efficacy was the dysregulation of humoral immunity, that B cell activation modulated by the PD-1 signaling pathway through both a T cell-dependent and T cell-independent manner, and anti-thyroid antibodies exists in thyroid dysfunction may partly explanted the association of irAEs and survival prognosis based on clinical evidence [46–49]. Previous studies suggested that anti-CTLA-4 therapy might activate T cells at the earlier stage of their development and might thus directly disrupt central tolerance without affecting the tumor immune response, while anti-PD-(L) major activate T cells in the later effect stage [34, 35]. Meanwhile, several chronic irAEs especially of rheumatologic and/or arthritis irAEs also showed better efficacy and survival benefits presented in those patients which might cause the chronic disease affecting the quality of life and partially lead to the interruption of immunotherapy [24, 40, 41, 50]. Therefore, the mechanism of irAEs in cancer immunotherapy need further research to illustrate and more focus on the quality of life in patients with chronic irAEs.

This study reviewed 184 studies involving 63,780 patients, providing a comprehensive analysis of the relationship between irAEs and survival outcomes, that adds weight to the generalizability of the findings. The findings provided actionable insights into clinical practice, suggesting that the presence of irAEs may be an indicator of favorable prognosis in patients treated with ICIs. Wan G et al. performed a retrospective multi-institutional cohort and a population-level study to investigate the relationship of the multi-organ irAEs among recipients of ICIs and survival outcomes, suggested that the reliably identifying irAEs cluster to which a patient belongs can provide valuable clinical information for prognosticating outcomes of immunotherapy [51]. Cook S et al. suggested developing a clinically meaningful irAE was associated with improved OS based on 803 patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC receiving ICIs [52]. It’s an interesting dilemma that oncologists face that the occurrence of irAEs may predict a better response and survival benefits to ICIs, otherwise indicated lack of efficacy and benefits [19]. Therefore, it’s critical to proper monitor and manage the development of irAEs and patient education for earlier reporting for maximizing the ICIs efficacy and benefits, which can be treated effectively with appropriate use of steroids and other immunosuppressive drugs, so that patients with related irAEs can be treated promptly, prevent and resumed any treatment interruption in times [20, 25, 53]. More well-designed large-scale prospective researches are needed in discovering other predictive biomarkers that may allow us to determine who may develop adverse events and potentially derive greater benefit from ICIs, and to provide useful information for disease-monitoring as well as treatment-decision making.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged in our study. First, the majority of the data were from retrospective studies, adding possible bias such as recall bias, selection bias and information bias. Besides, considering that the research subjects are at different initial disease courses or stages, there may be lead time bias. Second, study and population heterogeneity were extensive that the weight average outcomes should be interpreted accordingly. The significant heterogeneity was observed in the pooled ORR, OS and PFS analysis, which might be contributed to variations in irAEs subtype, severity of irAEs, ICIs type, cancer type and study characteristics, while subgroup meta-analyses results showed robust and supported the findings in the study. Third, limited availability data for specific subgroup meta-analyses, such as the incidence of irAEs in different grades, irAEs subtypes, anti-CTLA-4 treatment and prospective studies, that might yield less conclusive findings. Finally, Besides, visual analysis, Begg’s and Egger’s test revealed publication bias within the meta-analyses of ORR, OS and PFS, which may exaggerate the pooled results. However, the trim and fill method detected the combined results did not substantially deviate from the original results, and support the reliable of our findings. Thereby, limited by sample size and generalisability, well-designed large-scale prospective studies on the co-occurrence patterns and prognostic implications of irAEs, and its potential biological mechanisms across various organs warrants further study.

Conclusions

In the study, an intimate association was observed between the existence of irAEs and increased response rate and improved survival benefits in patients who treated with ICIs, and may have useful prognostic value in ICIs therapy, especially in skin, endocrine, gastrointestinal, musculoskeletal, rheumatologic and/or arthritis, pancreatitis, thrombocytopenia, and severe irAEs resulted in better ORR and PFS not in OS. Early detection and management of irAEs become more crucial for maximizing the treatment duration and minimizing toxicity in patients with ICIs. More large-scale prospective studies are warranted to validate the findings, and integrate into clinical practice resulting for personalized precision decision-making.
