Numerical investigation and optimization of structural and operating parameters for cuttings transport in a bidirectional eccentric micro-underreamer
Authors: Xu Zhang, Mengyu Cao, Li Liu, Yuqi Sun, Wei Li, Qiang Zhang, Fulu Chen, Chaoyu Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cuttings transport behavior plays a crucial role in downhole safety, drilling efficiency, and wellbore cleaning during underreaming operations. Because of the complex local flow passage and eccentric structural characteristics of a bidirectional eccentric micro-underreamer, cuttings migration and discharge are highly sensitive to downhole operating conditions. In this study, a three-dimensional solid–liquid two-phase flow model was established for a bidirectional eccentric micro-underreamer, and an Eulerian–Lagrangian approach was employed to describe the two-way coupling between the drilling fluid and cuttings particles. After the blade structural parameters were optimized, the effects of drill string rotational speed, weight on bit, drilling fluid density, and drilling fluid viscosity on cuttings transport behavior were systematically investigated. The results indicate that drill string rotational speed and weight on bit both exert non-monotonic effects on cuttings discharge efficiency, and relatively favorable cuttings-carrying performance was achieved at 50 r/min and 100 kN, respectively. By contrast, increasing drilling fluid density weakened cuttings transport performance, whereas increasing drilling fluid viscosity enhanced the drag and suspension capacities of the fluid for cuttings particles. Within the investigated range, a drilling fluid density of 1.6 g/cm3 and a viscosity of 50 mPa·s were associated with relatively favorable cuttings-carrying performance. These findings provide a theoretical basis for the optimization of operating parameters and the engineering application of bidirectional eccentric micro-underreamers.

Introduction

Underreaming operations have become increasingly important in deep, ultra-deep, and complex-structure well drilling because they can improve wellbore trajectory, facilitate casing running, and support subsequent completion operations1. During underreaming, efficient transport of bottom-hole cuttings by drilling fluid is essential for downhole safety, drilling efficiency, and wellbore cleaning quality2, 3. Once cuttings accumulate at the bottom hole or within locally enlarged wellbore sections, a series of adverse consequences may arise, including increased torque and drag, reduced rate of penetration, drill string sticking, and wellbore instability. Therefore, understanding cuttings transport behavior during underreaming is of considerable importance for both tool design and field operation optimization4.

Cuttings transport is essentially a complex solid–liquid two-phase flow process governed by the interactions among fluid motion, particle migration, and downhole tool geometry5, 6, 7. Previous studies have demonstrated that drilling fluid flow rate, rheological properties, drill string rotational speed, well inclination, annular eccentricity, and particle size can all significantly affect flow-field distribution, cuttings-bed evolution, and cuttings return efficiency8, 9, 10, 11, 12. In particular, drilling fluid rheology determines the drag and suspension capacities of the fluid, whereas drill string rotation and annular eccentricity alter the local velocity field and tangential disturbance intensity, thereby influencing the formation, disruption, and resuspension of cuttings beds13, 14. Accordingly, cuttings transport behavior is controlled not only by operating parameters, but also by the geometric characteristics of the local flow passage15, 16.

Extensive experimental studies, theoretical modeling, and numerical simulations have been conducted on wellbore cuttings transport. Early studies mainly relied on empirical correlations, layered models, or simplified theoretical models to predict critical return velocity, cuttings-bed height, and wellbore cleaning behavior17, 18. With the development of computational fluid dynamics, numerical methods such as the Eulerian-Eulerian, Eulerian–Lagrangian, and CFD-DEM approaches have been widely applied in cuttings transport research, enabling more detailed characterization of particle trajectories, local concentration distributions, and fluid–solid coupling behavior under complex downhole conditions19, 20, 21. Among these methods, the Eulerian–Lagrangian and CFD-DEM approaches are particularly suitable for flow regimes with relatively low particle concentrations, where detailed resolution of particle migration paths and local force interactions is required. Accordingly, they have been extensively used to evaluate the effects of drill pipe rotation, drilling fluid rheology22, 23, wellbore inclination, and particle properties on cuttings transport efficiency.

Existing studies have identified the major factors governing cuttings transport under conventional annular wellbore conditions24. In general, drill pipe rotation is considered beneficial for disturbing the cuttings bed and enhancing particle removal; however, its effectiveness depends strongly on flow rate, eccentricity, and wellbore inclination. Meanwhile, drilling fluid density, viscosity, and non-Newtonian rheological parameters can significantly affect particle settling velocity, slip behavior, and pressure loss, thereby altering hole-cleaning efficiency25, 26. Nevertheless, most available studies have focused on regular annuli, conventional well sections, or idealized wellbore geometries, and understanding of cuttings transport behavior in flow passages with local geometric discontinuities remains limited27.

Compared with conventional wellbore annuli, the flow passages in the vicinity of underreaming tools exhibit more pronounced geometric irregularity and local structural complexity. Structural features such as blades, edges, and variable cross-section flow channels can alter the local velocity field, pressure field, and particle accumulation locations, thereby affecting the dispersion, transport, and discharge of drill cuttings28. Recent studies have shown that the blade geometry of stabilizers, variable-diameter stabilizers, and local geometric characteristics under enlarged wellbore conditions can significantly influence flow-field organization and cuttings transport efficiency, and that these effects are often coupled with operating parameters such as flow rate and rotational speed29. However, studies on bidirectional eccentric micro-underreamers, which possess asymmetric local flow-passage characteristics, remain limited. In particular, the mechanisms by which key structural parameters, such as the blade helical angle and blade edge angle, affect cuttings transport behavior have not yet been systematically clarified30, 31.

In this study, a three-dimensional solid–liquid two-phase flow model was established for a bidirectional eccentric micro-underreamer to describe its downhole working process. An Eulerian–Lagrangian approach was adopted to simulate and analyze cuttings transport behavior32. After optimization of the blade structural parameters, particular attention was given to the effects of drill string rotational speed, weight on bit, drilling fluid density, and drilling fluid viscosity on cuttings-carrying performance. The aim of this study is to clarify the influence of major operating parameters on cuttings migration and discharge in the complex local flow passage of a bidirectional eccentric micro-underreamer, thereby providing a theoretical basis for operating-parameter optimization and engineering application of this type of tool.

Computational method

Description of the physical problem

To investigate the influence of the blade structural parameters of a bidirectional eccentric micro-underreamer on cuttings transport behavior in the annulus, a numerical model of solid–liquid two-phase flow during rotary drilling was established. The investigated tool incorporates an eccentrically arranged blade assembly, with cutting structures positioned on the lower and upper sides of the blade region to facilitate borehole enlargement during downward drilling and upward backreaming, respectively. During operation, the tool rotates synchronously with the drill string, and the eccentric blade assembly engages the borehole wall and removes the surrounding formation. The rotational sweep of the blades consequently forms an effective cutting envelope larger than the pass-through profile of the tool, thereby achieving localized borehole enlargement. The overall configuration and working principle of the tool are illustrated in Fig. 1. Meanwhile, the cuttings generated at the bottom hole are entrained by the drilling fluid, forming a solid–liquid mixture that is transported upward through the annulus toward the wellhead. Variations in drilling fluid density, rate of penetration, drill string rotational speed, and blade structural parameters may affect the cuttings generation rate, particle motion characteristics, and local flow-field distribution. Therefore, the downhole flow characteristics and cuttings transport behavior of the tool under different structural and operating conditions require systematic investigation.Fig. 1Schematic diagram of the bidirectional eccentric micro-underreamer.

Schematic diagram of the bidirectional eccentric micro-underreamer.

According to the characteristics of downhole solid–liquid two-phase flow, the numerical models commonly used mainly include the Eulerian–Eulerian two-fluid model and the Eulerian–Lagrangian fluid–particle coupled model. The former is suitable for high-concentration particle flow conditions33, 34, whereas the latter is more appropriate for cases with relatively low particle concentration where particle trajectories need to be tracked. According to field drilling data, the particle size of the returned cuttings generally ranges from 0.45 to 3.3 mm, with a density of approximately 2600 kg/m3. Under the conditions of a rate of penetration of 20 mm/s and a drilling fluid injection flow rate of no less than 25 L/s, the cuttings volume fraction is approximately 5.696%, which is significantly lower than the critical concentration of 35%. Therefore, the Eulerian–Lagrangian approach was adopted in this study to solve the solid–liquid two-phase flow in the wellbore, in which the drilling fluid phase was treated as the continuous phase and the cuttings particles were treated as the discrete phase.

To facilitate the numerical calculation and highlight the primary influencing factors, the model was simplified as follows. The drilling fluid was assumed to be incompressible, the cuttings were simplified as spherical particles, and the effects of inter-particle collisions and particle spin were neglected. In addition, the fluid and particle phases were treated using a two-way coupling approach. Based on the above assumptions, a flow field analysis model for the bidirectional eccentric underreamer was established, providing a foundation for subsequently revealing the influence of blade structural parameters on cuttings transport behavior.

Model establishment and experimental design

Geometric model construction and mesh generation

Based on the actual structural dimensions and operating characteristics of the bidirectional eccentric underreamer, a three-dimensional computational model was established to represent its downhole working process. During rotary drilling, the blades contact the borehole wall and scrape the surrounding formation, thereby enlarging the wellbore. Meanwhile, cuttings generated at the bottom hole are entrained by the drilling fluid and transported upward through the annulus as a solid–liquid two-phase flow. To capture the effects of blade structural parameters on cuttings transport behavior, the principal structural components, including the blades, cuttings-discharge grooves, and annular flow passages, were retained in the geometric model.

Because the actual tool contains numerous local geometric details, retaining all of these features would substantially increase the computational cost and reduce the numerical efficiency. Therefore, while preserving the dominant flow characteristics, minor chamfers, small fillets, and non-critical connecting structures were appropriately simplified. The resulting simplified geometric model is shown in Fig. 2. This simplification retains the principal structural features governing the local flow field and cuttings transport behavior while improving computational efficiency. The subsequent flow-field numerical simulations were performed using ANSYS Fluent.Fig. 2Simplified geometric model.

Simplified geometric model.

Based on the geometric model, the computational domain was discretized into meshes. Considering that the flow field varies dramatically in the blade region, near the cuttings discharge grooves, and in the near-wall region, where relatively large velocity and pressure gradients exist, local mesh refinement was applied in these areas. A relatively uniform mesh distribution was adopted in the remaining regions to balance computational accuracy and efficiency. The final mesh contained approximately 1.608 million cells, and the mesh generation result is shown in Fig. 3. Mesh quality inspection indicated that the generated mesh satisfied the requirements of numerical simulation and could therefore be used for subsequent analyses of flow field characteristics and cuttings transport behavior.Fig. 3Mesh generation result.

Grid independence verification

To minimize the influence of mesh resolution on the numerical results, a grid independence test was carried out before the formal simulations. Under identical boundary conditions and solution settings, several computational models with different mesh numbers were established, and the corresponding flow-field characteristics and cuttings transport results were compared. Considering the objective of this study, the axial mean velocity of cuttings particles at the outlet section was selected as the primary evaluation index. In addition, the local velocity distribution of the flow field was also examined for comparison among different mesh schemes.

The results showed that the calculated quantities gradually converged with increasing mesh number. As the mesh was refined from relatively coarse schemes, the variation in the axial mean velocity of cuttings particles at the outlet section progressively decreased, indicating that the mesh discretization error was effectively controlled. After refinement to the mesh scheme adopted in this study, further increase in mesh number produced only negligible changes in the key evaluation index, whereas the computational cost increased significantly. Therefore, the present mesh scheme was selected for subsequent numerical simulations by comprehensively considering computational accuracy and efficiency. The verification results for different mesh numbers are shown in Fig. 5.Fig. 5Grid independence verification.

Grid independence verification.

The grid independence analysis demonstrates that the selected mesh scheme can adequately ensure the computational accuracy required for analyzing the complex local flow field and cuttings transport characteristics inside the bidirectional eccentric underreamer.

Results and discussion

The results are presented in two parts. First, the blade structural parameters were optimized to determine the baseline structural configuration of the bidirectional eccentric micro-underreamer. On this basis, the effects of drill string rotational speed, weight on bit, drilling fluid density, and drilling fluid viscosity on cuttings transport behavior were systematically analyzed. By comparing cuttings distribution, axial velocity at characteristic cross-sections, and cuttings-carrying performance under different operating conditions, the mechanisms by which these parameters affect cuttings transport behavior were further clarified.

Conclusions

A three-dimensional Eulerian–Lagrangian model was established to investigate the effects of blade structural parameters, operating parameters, and drilling fluid properties on cuttings transport in a bidirectional eccentric micro-underreamer. Based on the numerical results obtained within the investigated parameter ranges, the main conclusions are summarized as follows:Blade structural parameters significantly affected the local flow-field organization and cuttings transport performance. Within the investigated ranges, a blade helical angle of 20° provided the most favorable overall cuttings-lifting performance, while the highest average axial velocity of cuttings particles at the outlet section was obtained at a blade edge angle of 60°. Therefore, a blade helical angle of 20° and a blade edge angle of 60° were identified as the favorable structural parameter values and were adopted as the baseline configuration for the subsequent operating-parameter analyses.Under the selected blade configuration, drill string rotational speed and weight on bit exhibited non-monotonic effects on cuttings transport. In the respective single-factor analyses, the average axial velocity of cuttings particles at the outlet section reached its maximum at a drill string rotational speed of 50 r/min and a weight on bit of 100 kN. These results indicate that moderate rotational speed and weight on bit are more favorable for maintaining effective cuttings transport and discharge.Drilling fluid properties also played an important role in regulating cuttings transport. Increasing the drilling fluid density from 1.6 to 2.2 g/cm3 gradually reduced the average axial velocity of cuttings particles at the outlet section, whereas increasing the drilling fluid viscosity from 30 to 50 mPa·s enhanced the suspension and upward transport of cuttings. Based on the respective parametric analyses, the favorable parameter values identified within the investigated ranges were a blade helical angle of 20°, a blade edge angle of 60°, a drill string rotational speed of 50 r/min, a weight on bit of 100 kN, a drilling fluid density of 1.6 g/cm3, and a drilling fluid viscosity of 50 mPa·s.The present study was conducted within a numerical modeling framework and therefore involved several simplifying assumptions. The cuttings were idealized as spherical particles, and particle–particle interactions were not explicitly considered. In addition, the effect of weight on bit was incorporated through its relationship with the rate of penetration and cuttings generation rate, while the principal parameters were mainly evaluated through single-factor analyses. Therefore, the parameter set identified in this study should be regarded as favorable within the investigated ranges rather than as a globally optimal solution. Future work may extend the present model by considering irregular particle shapes, more detailed particle interactions, multiparameter coupling effects, and laboratory and field validation.
