Quasiparticle dynamics and hydrodynamics of 1d hard rod gas on diffusion scale
Anupam Kundu

Abstract
We investigate the stochastic dynamics of a quasiparticle within a gas of hard rods, focusing on the evolution of its mean, variance, and autocorrelation for two choices of initial states: (i) one with long-range (LR) correlations and (ii) the other without it. We derive analytical results for the phase space density correlations in the former case to complement the known results for the latter case. These results enable us to obtain expressions for the mean, variance, and autocorrelation of a quasiparticle, which are applicable to both initial states. The LR correlations introduce a diffusive-scale correction to the mean Euler generalized hydrodynamic (GHD) equations, modifying the standard local equilibrium form, and our findings reveal that the form of the correction term depends on the LR correlations present in the initial state.

The GHD is written in terms of phase-space quasiparticles. In a hard-rod gas, a quasiparticle is a rod tagged with a specific initial position

The derivation of diffusive scale GHD requires understanding the stochastic motion of individual quasiparticle. We computed the mean, variance, and autocorrelation of the position of a quasiparticle at time

[
33
,
42
]
and the one derived in
[
34
,
41
]
for the local generalized Gibbs initial state.

2
Hard rod gas and initial conditions

We consider a 1d system of
N
hard rods, each with unit mass and length
a
. The state of the
i
-th rod is described by its position and momentum
\{\mathtt{X}_{i},v_{i}\}
for
i=1,2,\dots,N
. Given that the mass is unity, the momentum of each rod is equivalent to its velocity. The dynamics is characterized by ballistic motion interrupted by elastic collisions during which the rods exchange their respective momenta.

The rods are initially confined to a region of length
L
and allowed to move on the infinite line subsequently. We consider the thermodynamic limit, defined by
N\to\infty
and
L\to\infty
, such that the initial density profile
\varrho_{0}(\mathtt{X})
remains a finite-valued function across all space. This system is integrable, possessing
N
locally conserved quantities
Q_{\nu}
, which can be chosen as the moments of the individual velocities:

\displaystyle Q_{\nu}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}^{\nu},\quad\nu=1,2,\dots,N.

(1)

In the limit
a\to 0
, the interacting hard-rod system reduces to a non-interacting hard-point gas (HPG). The microscopic dynamics of the hard rods can be formally mapped onto a system of hard-point particles, each of unit-mass, using a specific coordinate transformation
[
31
,
43
,
44
]
. Given a configuration of
N
hard rods
\{\mathtt{X}_{i},v_{i}\}
, the corresponding point-particle configuration
\{\mathtt{x}_{i},v_{i}\}
is constructed as:

\mathtt{x}_{i}=\mathtt{X}_{i}-(i-1)a,\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,N.

(2)

This mapping effectively removes the inaccessible volume occupied by the rods. In this point-particle representation, the dynamics are greatly simplified: particles move ballistically and, upon collision, exchange velocities without the spatial jumps characteristic of hard rods. Consequently, the system can be evolved as a collection of non-interacting particles, with a final relabeling based on their spatial ordering. Since the transformation in Eq. (
2
) is
one-to-one, the hard-rod dynamics are exactly solvable by mapping the system back from the point-particle representation.
The following two types of initial conditions are typically considered in the literature
[
37
,
31
,
45
,
46
]
.

(a) IC ‘factorized’ in hard rod coordinates
({\rm IC}_{\rm fhr})
:

In this case the initial positions and velocities
\{\mathtt{X}_{i},v_{i}\}
of the rods are chosen directly from the following joint probability distribution:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\rm r}(\{\mathtt{X}_{i},v_{i}\})=\frac{1}{Z_{r}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Omega(\mathtt{X}_{i},v_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}\Theta(\mathtt{X}_{i+1}-\mathtt{X}_{i}-a),

(3)

where
\Theta(\mathtt{X})
is the Heaviside theta function,
Z_{\rm r}
is the normalization factor, and
\int d\mathtt{X}\int dv~\Omega(\mathtt{X},v)=1
. Note that the joint distribution is ‘factorized’ except for the constraint
\mathtt{X}_{i+1}\geq\mathtt{X}_{i}+a
. We assume that the initial state varies slowly over space, characterized by the following choice.

\displaystyle\Omega(\mathtt{X},v)=e^{-\omega(\mathtt{X}/\ell,v)},

(4)

with
\omega(\mathtt{X},v)
being some suitable function that goes to infinity for
|\mathtt{X}|\to\infty
and
|v|\to\infty
.

(b) IC ‘factorized’ in hard point particle coordinates
({\rm IC}_{\rm fhp})
):

In this case, we first choose the configuration
\{\mathtt{x}_{i},v_{i}\}
in point particle coordinates from a joint distribution
\mathbb{P}_{\rm p}(\{\mathtt{x}_{i},v_{i}\})
and then transform to hard rod coordinates
\{\mathtt{X}_{i}=\mathtt{x}_{i}+(i-1)a,v_{i}\}
. The joint distribution is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\rm p}(\{\mathtt{x}_{i},v_{i}\},0)=N!\prod_{i=1}^{N}\psi\left(\mathtt{x}_{i},v_{i}\right)~\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}\Theta(\mathtt{x}_{i+1}-\mathtt{x}_{i}),

(5)

where the
\Theta
functions ensure the ordering
\{\mathtt{x}_{i+1}\geq\mathtt{x}_{i}~;~i=1,...,N\}
and
\int d\mathtt{x}\int dv~\psi(\mathtt{x},v)=1
. The average single particle phase space density and mass density are given, respectively, by
\bar{\mathcal{f}}(\mathtt{x},v)=N\psi\left(\mathtt{x},v\right)
and
\bar{\rho}(\mathtt{x})=N\int dv~\phi\left(\mathtt{x},v\right)
. In particular, we choose
\psi(\mathtt{x},v)=\psi_{0}\left(\frac{\mathtt{x}}{\sigma},v\right)
such that
\bar{\rho}(\mathtt{x})
varies over a length scale
\sigma
. For the corresponding hard rod gas (obtained using the transformation in Eq. (
2
)), the initial average mass density profile is expressed in terms of
\bar{\rho}(\mathtt{x})
as
\bar{\varrho}\big(\mathtt{X}(\mathtt{x})\big)=\frac{\bar{\rho}(\mathtt{x})}{1+a\bar{\rho}(\mathtt{x})}

where

\mathtt{X}(\mathtt{x})=x+a\int d\mathtt{y}~\Theta(\mathtt{x}-\mathtt{y})\bar{\rho}(\mathtt{y})
. The transformation
\mathtt{X}(\mathtt{x})
is essentially the same mapping as described in Eq. (
2
), now expressed using the mass density of the point-particles
\bar{\rho}(\mathtt{x}).
It is easy to realize that for large
N
and small
a
with fixed
\frac{Na}{\sigma}
, the average mass density
\bar{\varrho}(\mathtt{X})
varies over a length scale
\ell=O(\sigma)
for large
\sigma
. Note that the densities of the hard-rod gas in the initial state
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
are already correlated over long-distances to start with. However, we believe that after a small time evolution (small compared to the hydrodynamic scale and large compared to the microscopic scale
[
34
]
) the two initial states
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhr}
and
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
become equivalent albeit the structure of long-range correlations are different.

We are interested in the macroscopic scale evolution of system described by hydrodynamic density fields defined by coarse-graining the
microscopic empirical phase space density
\hat{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathtt{Z},v,\mathtt{t})
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\bar{\mathfrak{f}}}(\mathtt{X},v,\mathtt{t})=\frac{1}{\Delta\mathtt{X}}\int_{\mathtt{X}-\Delta\mathtt{X}/2}^{\mathtt{X}+\Delta\mathtt{X}/2}d\mathtt{Z}~\hat{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathtt{Z},v,\mathtt{t}),~~\text{where}~~~\hat{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathtt{Z},v,\mathtt{t})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta(\mathtt{Z}-\mathtt{X}_{i}(\mathtt{t}))\delta(v-v_{i}).\end{split}

(6a)

The coarse graining scale
\Delta\mathtt{X}
can be chosen as
\Delta\mathtt{X}\sim\ell^{\nu}
with
0<\nu<1/2

[
41
]
such that
a\ll\Delta\mathtt{X}\ll\ell
. For initial states varying over large length scales
\ell
as in Eq.
3
, it is expected
[
37
]
using large deviation argument that the coarse-grained density
\hat{\bar{\mathfrak{f}}}(\mathtt{X},v,t)
varies over space-time scale of
O(\ell)
. In other words, one expects the following form

\displaystyle\hat{\bar{\mathfrak{f}}}(\mathtt{X},v,\mathtt{t})=\mathfrak{f}\left(\frac{\mathtt{X}}{\ell},v,\frac{\mathtt{t}}{\ell}\right),

(6b)

where
\mathfrak{f}\left(X,v,t\right)
is the scaling density. Hence, it seems convenient to work entirely in the scaled coordinate
X=\mathtt{X}/\ell
, time
t=\mathtt{t}/\ell
and density
\mathfrak{f}(X,v,t)
.

A similar coarse-grained phase space density of point particles can also be defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{f}(x,v,t)=\frac{1}{\Delta\mathtt{x}}\int_{\ell x-\Delta\mathtt{x}/2}^{\ell x+\Delta\mathtt{x}/2}d\mathtt{z}~\hat{\mathcal{f}}(\mathtt{z},v,\mathtt{t}=\ell t),~~\text{where}~~~\hat{\mathcal{f}}(\mathtt{z},v,\mathtt{t})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta(\mathtt{z}-\mathtt{x}_{i}(\mathtt{t}))\delta(v-v_{i}).\end{split}

(7)

Ideally, coarse-grained fields should carry an additional label
\ell
, however, we drop it to make the notation simple.
For each microscopic configuration
\{X_{i},v_{i}\}
randomly chosen from the ensemble, one can construct a coarse-grained density field
\mathfrak{f}(X,v,t)
which fluctuates randomly. For generic integrable systems, the GHD provides the evolution of the average density field
\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)=\left\langle\mathfrak{f}(X,v,t)\right\rangle
, starting from a mean profile
\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,0)

[
22
,
28
]
. For large
\ell
, the average density evolves according to the Euler GHD equation
[
41
,
37
]

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)+\partial_{X}\big(v_{\rm eff}(X,v,t)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\big)=0,

(8)

on ballistic space-time scale (
X\sim t
), where the effective velocity is

\displaystyle v_{\rm eff}(X,v,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{v-a\int dv~v~\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)}{1-a\bar{\varrho}(X,t)},~~~{\rm with}~~~\bar{\varrho}(X,t)=\int dv~\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t).

(9)

In this paper, we are interested in the diffusive scale correction to the above Euler equation. It has been shown that, for homogeneous equilibrium state the diffusive correction is given by the following Navier-Stokes equation
[
33
,
42
]
6

\displaystyle\partial\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)+\partial_{X}\big(v_{\rm eff}(X,v,t)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\big)=\frac{1}{2\ell}\partial_{X}\int du~\mathscr{D}(v,u)\partial_{X}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,u,t),

(10a)

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}(v,u)=\frac{1}{1-a\bar{\varrho}(X,t)}\left(\delta(u-v)\int dw~|v-w|~\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,w,t)-|u-v|~\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\right).

(10b)

Recently, it has been shown that for an inhomogeneous local equilibrium state, the above diffusive scale equation is modified
[
34
,
41
]
. The usual derivation of the diffusive scale correction under the assumption of local equilibrium fails because of the emergence of the LR correlation on the Euler space-time scale during evolution
[
36
]
. The presence of such long range correlations indicates that the local state of the system is not in equilibrium form, and consequently such correlations modify the evolution conserved densities in integrable systems at the diffusive space-time scale
[
35
]
. Based on a microscopic description, such modifications have been rigorously computed for hard rods for
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhr}
[see Eq. (
3
)] initial state
[
34
,
41
]
. Explicit forms of the correction on diffusive space-time scale have been obtained.

One of the primary goals of this paper is to derive a similar diffusive scale correction for the other initial ensemble
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
described by Eq. (
5
). In order to do so, we need to study the LR correlations and the dynamics of quasiparticles, which we do in secs.
3
and
4
, respectively. In sec.
5
, we present the derivation of the diffusive scale fluctuating hydrodynamics, where we closely follow the procedure in
[
34
,
41
]
and adapt it for
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
. In sec.
6
we provide our conclusion. Several details of the calculations are provided in the Appendix for smoother presentation of the results.

3
Long-range correlation

In this section, we study the correlation of the phase space density on the Euler space-time scale. For that we follow
[
34
]
and define the height fields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varphi(X,v,t)&=\int dY~\Theta(X-Y)\mathfrak{f}(Y,v,t),\\
\phi(x,v,t)&=\int dy~\Theta(x-y)\mathcal{f}(y,v,t),\end{split}

(11)

which evolve according to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\varphi(X,v,t)&=-v_{\rm eff}[\mathfrak{f}](X,v,t)~\partial_{X}\varphi(X,v,t),\\
\partial_{t}\phi(x,v,t)&=-v~\partial_{x}\phi(x,v,t),\end{split}

(12)

where
v_{\rm eff}[\mathfrak{f}]
is given in Eq. (
9
). It is easy to see that these two height fields are related

\displaystyle\phi(x_{t}(X),v,t)=\varphi(X,v,t),~~\text{and}~~\varphi(X_{t}(x),v,t)=\phi(x,v,t),

(13)

where

\displaystyle x_{t}(X)

\displaystyle=X-a\int dv~\varphi(X,v,t)=X-a\int dv\int dY\Theta(X-Y)\mathfrak{f}(Y,v,t),

(14a)

\displaystyle X_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=x+a\int dv~\phi(x,v,t)=x+a\int dv\int dy\Theta(x-y)\mathcal{f}(y,v,t).

(14b)

These coordinates evolve according to

\displaystyle\frac{dx_{t}(X)}{dt}

\displaystyle=a\int dv~v~\mathfrak{f}(X,v,t),~~\frac{dX_{t}(x)}{dt}=a\int dv~v~\mathcal{f}(x,v,t).

(15)

Given the fluctuations of the phase space densities

\mathfrak{f}(X,v,t)=\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)+\delta\mathfrak{f}(X,v,t)
and
\mathcal{f}(x,v,t)=\bar{\mathcal{f}}(x,v,t)+\delta\mathcal{f}(x,v,t)
, the fluctuations of the height fields, at linear order, are given by

\displaystyle\delta\varphi(X,v,t)

\displaystyle=\int du~\left(\delta(v-u)-a\frac{\bar{\mathcal{f}}(\bar{x}_{t}(X),v,t)}{1+a\bar{\rho}(\bar{x}_{t}(X),t)}\right)~\delta\phi(\bar{x}_{t}(X),u,t),

(16)

\displaystyle\delta\phi(x,v,t)

\displaystyle=\int du~\left(\delta(v-u)+a\frac{\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(\bar{X}_{t}(x),v,t)}{1-a\bar{\varrho}(\bar{X}_{t}(x),t)}\right)~\delta\varphi(\bar{X}_{t}(x),u,t),

(17)

where, recall,
\bar{\rho}(x,t)=\langle\rho(x,t)\rangle
,
\bar{\varrho}(x,t)=\langle\varrho(X,t)\rangle
. In terms of the height field fluctuations the correlations of the phase space densities are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\rm r}(\ell X,v,\ell t;\ell Y,u,\ell t^{\prime})=\left\langle\delta\mathfrak{f}(X,v,t)\delta\mathfrak{f}(Y,u,t^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\partial_{X}\partial_{Y}\left\langle\delta\varphi(X,v,t)\delta\varphi(Y,u,t^{\prime})\right\rangle,

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\rm p}(\ell x,v,\ell t;\ell y,u,\ell t^{\prime})=\left\langle\delta\mathcal{f}(x,v,t)\delta\mathcal{f}(y,u,t^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\left\langle\delta\phi(X,v,t)\delta\phi(Y,u,t^{\prime})\right\rangle.

(19)

The equal-time correlation of hard rod density has the following scaling form
[
37
,
38
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\rm r}(\ell X,v,\ell t;\ell Y,u,\ell t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}\mathscr{C}_{\rm r}(X,v;Y,u;t).

(20a)

We will below show that for both choices of initial states, the scaled correlation has two parts – one singular and the other long-ranged:

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{\rm r}(X,v;Y,u;t)=\delta(X-Y)\mathscr{C}^{\rm r}_{\rm gge}(X,u,v)+\mathscr{C}^{\rm r}_{\rm lr}(X,u;Y,v;t).

(20b)

The singular part
\mathscr{C}_{\rm gge}^{\rm r}(X,u,v)

represents the contribution of local GGE approximation of the microscopic distribution in a fluid cell and the long range part
\mathscr{C}_{\rm lr}^{\rm r}(X,u;Y,v;t)
comes from the fact that these distributions in two far apart fluid cells are weakly correlated [at
O(1/\ell)
]
[
36
]
. For point particle gas, on the other hand, the LR part exists only for
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhr}
initial condition and one has
\mathcal{C}_{\rm p}(\ell x,v,\ell t;\ell y,u,\ell t)=\frac{1}{\ell}\mathscr{C}_{\rm p}(x,v;y,u;t)
with
\mathscr{C}_{\rm p}(x,v;y,u;t)=\delta(X-Y)\mathscr{C}^{\rm p}_{\rm gge}(x,u,v)+\mathscr{C}^{\rm p}_{\rm lr}(x,u;y,v;t)
.
For generic integrable systems, such LR correlations have been predicted first in
[
36
]
using ballistic macroscopic fluctuation theory (BMFT) and later have been computed explicitly for hard rod gas in
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
.
In the following, we will use Eqs. (
16
) and (
17
) to compute these correlations in hard-rod densities from point-particle densities and vise versa.

Hard rod correlation from point particle picture

\langle\delta\varphi(X,v,t)\delta\varphi(Y,u,t^{\prime})\rangle
. Using the relation between
\delta\varphi
and

\displaystyle\langle\delta\varphi(X,v,t)\delta\varphi(Y,u,t^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\int dp_{1}\int dp_{2}\left(\delta(v-p_{1})-a\frac{\bar{\mathcal{f}}(\bar{x}_{t}(X),v,t)}{1+a\bar{\rho}(\bar{x}_{t}(X),t)}\right)\left(\delta(u-p_{2})-a\frac{\bar{\mathcal{f}}(\bar{x}_{t^{\prime}}(Y),u,t^{\prime})}{1+a\bar{\rho}(\bar{x}_{t^{\prime}}(Y),t^{\prime})}\right),

Computation of correlation for
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}

For this case,

\displaystyle\langle\delta\mathcal{f}(y,v,0)\delta\mathcal{f}(z,u,0)\rangle=\frac{1}{\ell}\delta(v-u)~\delta(y-z)~\bar{\mathcal{f}}(y,v,0),

which implies

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\delta\phi(x,v,0)\delta\phi(z,u,0)\rangle&=\int dx_{1}\int dx_{2}~\Theta(x-x_{1})\Theta(z-x_{2})~\langle\delta\mathcal{f}(x_{1},v,0)\delta\mathcal{f}(x_{2},u,0)\rangle,\\

&=\frac{1}{\ell}\delta(v-u)\bar{\phi}(\min(y,z),v,0)\end{split}

with

. Using Eq. (

\displaystyle\langle\delta\varphi(Y,v,t)\delta\varphi(Z,u,t^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}\int dp_{1}\left(\delta(v-p_{1})-a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,v,t)\right)\left(\delta(u-p_{1})-a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times~~~~\bar{\phi}(\min(\bar{x}_{t}(Y)-p_{1}t,\bar{x}_{t^{\prime}}(Z)-p_{1}t^{\prime}),p_{1},0),

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}\int dp_{1}\left(\delta(v-p_{1})-a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,v,t)\right)\left(\delta(u-p_{1})-a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times~~~~\bar{\varphi}(\bar{X}_{t}(\min(\bar{x}_{t}(Y),\bar{x}_{t^{\prime}}(Z)-p_{1}(t^{\prime}-t))),p_{1},t),

(24)

where
\bar{X}_{t}(x)
is given in Eq. (
14b
) with
\mathcal{f}(y,v,t)
inside the integrand replaced by
\bar{\mathcal{f}}(y,v,t)
.
Inserting this result in Eq. (
18
) we find the following expression for the unequal time correlation
\mathscr{C}_{\rm r}(Y,v,t;Z,u,t^{\prime})=\ell\left\langle\delta\mathfrak{f}(Y,v,t)\delta\mathfrak{f}(Z,u,t^{\prime})\right\rangle
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathds{C}_{\rm r}(Y,v,t;Z,u,t^{\prime})=\int dw\big[&\delta\left(\bar{x}_{t}(Y)-wt-\bar{x}_{t^{\prime}}(Z)+wt^{\prime}\right)(1-a\bar{\varrho}(Z,t))\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,w,t)\\
&~~\times~\left\{\left(\delta(v-w)-a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,v,t)\right)\left(\delta(u-w)-a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t^{\prime})\right)\right\}\\
&-a\Theta\left(\bar{x}_{t}(Y)-wt-\bar{x}_{t^{\prime}}(Z)+wt^{\prime}\right)\left(\delta(u-w)-a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t^{\prime})\right)\\
&~~~~~\times~~(1-a\bar{\varrho}(Z,t^{\prime}))\bar{\mathcal{f}}(\bar{x}_{t^{\prime}}(Z)-w(t^{\prime}-t),w,t)\partial_{Y}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,v,t)\\
&-a\Theta\left(\bar{x}_{t}(Z)-wt^{\prime}-\bar{x}_{t^{\prime}}(Y)+wt\right)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,w,t)\\
&~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times~~\left(\delta(v-w)-a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,v,t)\right)\partial_{Z}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t^{\prime})\\
&+a^{2}\bar{\phi}(\bar{X}_{t}(\min(\bar{x}_{t}(Y),\bar{x}_{t}(Z)-w(t^{\prime}-t)),w,t)\\
&~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times~~\left(\partial_{Y}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,v,t)\right)\left(\partial_{Z}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t^{\prime})\right)\big].\end{split}

(25)

It is easy to see that for
t=t^{\prime}
the expression simplifies and in fact, it has the structure in Eq. (
20b
) with

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{\rm gge}^{\rm r}(X,v,u)=

\displaystyle\left[\delta(u-v)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)-a(2-a\rho(X,t))\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,u,t)\right]

(26a)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathscr{C}^{\rm r}_{\rm lr}(X,v;Y,u;t)&=~~~a\Theta(X-Y)\Big[\left(\partial_{X}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\right)\left(\partial_{Y}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,u,t)\right)\bar{\mathscr{F}}(Y,t)\\
&~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-\left(\partial_{X}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\right)~(1-a\bar{\varrho}(Y,t))\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,u,t)\Big]\\
&~~~+~a\Theta(Y-X)\Big[\left(\partial_{X}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\right)\left(\partial_{Y}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,u,t)\right)\bar{\mathscr{F}}(X,t)\\
&~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-\left(\partial_{Y}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,u,t)\right)~(1-a\bar{\varrho}(X,t))\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\Big]\end{split}

(26b)

where
\bar{\mathscr{F}}(X,t)=\int dZ\int dv~\Theta(X-Z)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)
.
We observe that the LR part has a jump at
X=Y
. We point out that the mass density-density correlation can be obtained by integrating the phase-space density correlation
\mathds{C}_{\rm r}(Y,v,t;Z,u,t^{\prime})
in Eq. (
25
) over the velocities, which agrees with the result obtained in
[
47
]
using both BMFT and microscopic computations.

In summary, the equal-time correlation
\mathscr{C}_{\rm r}(X,v;Y,u;t)
of the hard rod gas has a singular and a non-singular part, as stated in Eq. (
20b
). The singular part represents the GGE correlation. The non-singular part represents LR correlation, which has a discontinuity at
X=Y
. Writing Eq. (
26b
) in the following form

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{\rm r}_{\rm lr}(X,v;Y,u;t)=\Theta(Y-X)\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,+}_{\rm lr}(X,v,Y,u,t)+\Theta(X-Y)\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,-}_{\rm lr}(X,v,Y,u,t),

(27)

we identify

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,+}_{\rm lr}(X,v,Y,u,t)=a\Big[\left(\partial_{X}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\right)\left(\partial_{Y}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,u,t)\right)\bar{\mathscr{F}}(X,t)-\left(\partial_{Y}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,u,t)\right)~(1-a\bar{\varrho}(X,t))\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\Big],

(28)

and
\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,-}_{\rm lr}(X,v,Y,u,t)=\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,+}_{\rm lr}(Y,u,X,v,t)
.
The presence of such jumps in the equal-time correlation functions was first predicted in
[
35
]
for generic integrable systems and was explicitly demonstrated for hard rod gas in
[
34
]
for
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhr}
. Here we have explicitly demonstrated the presence of such a jump at
X=Y
for initial state
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
.

Near the jump, the correlation function can be split into symmetric and anti-symmetric parts

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{\rm r}_{\rm lr}(X,v;Y,u;t)\overset{X\approx Y}{=}\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,sym}_{\rm lr}(X,v,u,t)+\text{sgn}(X-Y)\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,asym}_{\rm lr}(X,v,u,t),

(29a)

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,sym}_{\rm lr}(X,v,u,t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,+}_{\rm lr}(X,v,X,u,t)+\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,-}_{\rm lr}(X,v,X,u,t)\right),

(29b)

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,asym}_{\rm lr}(X,v,u,t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,+}_{\rm lr}(X,v,X,u,t)-\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,-}_{\rm lr}(X,v,X,u,t)\right).

(29c)

Note that the un-equal time correlation does not have such singularities.
These features of the correlation, both equal and un-equal time, will be useful for deriving diffusive scale hydrodynamics in the later sections.

Computation of correlation for
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhr}
:

As shown in
A
, a similar calculation can also be done for the initial condition (Eq. (
3
)) ‘factorized’ in hard rod coordinates. In this case also the equal time correlation has two parts – a sigular GGE contribution and a discontinuous LR correlation as in Eq. (
20b
). Furthermore, the LR part can again be decomposed into a symmetric and an antisymmetric part near the jump at
X=Y
, as was shown previously in
[
34
,
41
]
.

4
Quasiparticle dynamics

In this section, we study the motion of a quasiparticle rod starting at the location
\mathtt{X}_{q}(0)=\mathtt{X}_{q}
with velocity
v_{q}
. For a given sample of the initial configuration of the position and the velocities of the rods, the location
\mathtt{X}_{q}(\mathtt{t})\equiv\mathtt{X}_{\mathtt{t}}(\mathtt{X}_{q}(0),v_{q})
of the quasiparticle rod at time
t
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathtt{X}_{q}(\mathtt{t})

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathtt{x}}(X_{q}(0))+v_{q}\mathtt{t}+a\sum_{r\neq q}\Theta\left[\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{q}(0))+v_{q}\mathtt{t}-\hat{\mathtt{x}}(X_{r}(0))-v_{r}\mathtt{t}\right],

(30)

\displaystyle\text{where,}~~\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{q})

\displaystyle=\mathtt{X}_{q}-a\sum_{r\neq q}\Theta\left[\mathtt{X}_{q}-\mathtt{X}_{r}\right].

(31)

The above way of defining the dynamics of the quasiparticle is natural for
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhr}
as the coordinates in the initial states are chosen directly in hard-rod coordinates only. For the
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
initial state, the coordinates are first chosen from the point particle picture and then transformed to the hard rod coordinates. In this case, one would like to start with a definition of the location
\mathtt{X}_{q}(t)
of the
q^{\rm th}
quasiparticle at time
t
in terms of the coordinates of the point particles. Formally, it can be written as

\displaystyle\mathtt{X}_{q}(\mathtt{t})=

\displaystyle\mathtt{X}_{q}(0)-a\sum_{r\neq q}\Theta[\mathtt{X}_{q}(0)-\hat{\mathtt{X}}(x_{r}(0))]+v_{q}\mathtt{t}+a\sum_{r\neq q}\Theta[\mathtt{X}_{q}(\mathtt{t})-\hat{\mathtt{X}}(x_{r}(\mathtt{t}))],

(32)

\displaystyle\text{where,}~~\hat{\mathtt{X}}(x_{q})

\displaystyle=\mathtt{x}_{q}+a\sum_{r\neq q}\Theta\left[\mathtt{x}_{q}-\mathtt{x}_{r}\right].

(33)

We would like to show that Eq. (
32
) and Eq. (
30
) are exactly the same.
Since
\sum_{r\neq q}\Theta[\mathtt{X}_{q}-\hat{\mathtt{X}}(x_{r})]=\sum_{r\neq q}\Theta[\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{q})-\mathtt{x}_{r}]
, for any configuration
\hat{\mathtt{X}}(\hat{\mathtt{x}}(X_{q}))=\mathtt{X}_{q}
and
\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\hat{\mathtt{X}}(x_{q}))=\mathtt{x}_{q}
. Hence, Eq. (
32
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathtt{X}_{q}(\mathtt{t})=

\displaystyle\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{q}(0))+v_{q}\mathtt{t}+a\sum_{r\neq q}\Theta[\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{q}(\mathtt{t}))-\mathtt{x}_{r}(\mathtt{t})],

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{q}(0))+v_{q}\mathtt{t}+a\sum_{r\neq q}\Theta[\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{q}(0))+v_{q}\mathtt{t}-\mathtt{x}_{r}(0)-v_{r}\mathtt{t}],

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\mathtt{x}}(X_{q}(0))+v_{q}t+a\sum_{r\neq q}\Theta[\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{q}(0))+v_{q}\mathtt{t}-\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{r}(0))-v_{r}\mathtt{t}],

(34)

where we have used
\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{q}(\mathtt{t}))=\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{q}(0))+v_{q}\mathtt{t}
on the
2^{\rm nd}
line and used
\mathtt{x}_{r}(0)=\hat{\mathtt{x}}(X_{r}(0))
on the
3^{\rm rd}
line. Note that Eq. (
34
) is essentially the same as Eq. (
30
). Hence, from now on we work with the definition in Eq. (
30
) for both choices of the initial states.

In the thermodynamic limit, the Eq. (
30
) can be written in terms of the empirical single particle phase space density
\mathfrak{f}(X,v,t)
, defined in Eq. (
6b
), as follows [see
B
]

\displaystyle X_{t}(X_{q},v_{q})

\displaystyle=x_{0}(X_{q})+v_{q}t+a\int dY\int dv~\mathfrak{f}(Y,v,0)\Theta\left(x_{0}(X_{q})+v_{q}t-x_{0}(Y)-vt\right),

(35)

where
x_{t}(X)
is given in Eq. (
14a
).
Clearly
X_{0}(X_{q},v_{q})=X_{q}
. It is easy to show that [see
C
]

\displaystyle X_{t+dt}(X_{q},v_{q})=X_{dt}(X_{t}(X_{q},v_{q}),v_{q})=X_{t}(X_{q},v_{q})+dX_{t}(X_{t}(X_{q},v_{q}),v_{q})..

(36)

Introducing the shorthand notation
X_{t}^{q}=X_{t}(X_{q},v_{q})
such that
X_{0}^{q}=X_{q}
, the displacement in Eq. (
36
) in time
dt
can be written as

\displaystyle dX_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})=X_{t+dt}^{q}-X_{t}^{q}=X_{dt}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})-X_{t}^{q},

(37a)

where

\displaystyle X_{dt}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})

\displaystyle=x_{t}(X_{t}^{q})+v_{q}dt+a\int dY\int dv~\mathfrak{f}(Y,v,t)\Theta\left(x_{t}(X_{t}^{q})+v_{q}dt-x_{t}(Y)-vdt\right).

(37b)

Since the initial density
\mathfrak{f}(Y,v,0)
is fluctuating, the location
x_{0}(Y)
as well as
X_{t}(X_{q},v_{q})
also fluctuate. One can separate the fluctuations from the mean using

\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}(Y,u,t)

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,u,t)+\delta\mathfrak{f}(Y,u,t),~~\text{and}~~x_{t}(X)=\bar{x}_{t}(X)+\delta x_{t}(X),

(38)

where

\displaystyle~~\bar{x}_{t}(X)=\langle x_{t}(X)\rangle=X-a\int dY~\int du~\Theta(X-Y)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,u,t),

(39)

and

\displaystyle~~\delta x_{t}(X)=-a\int dY\int du~\Theta(X-Y)~\delta\mathfrak{f}(Y,u,t).

(40)

with
\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,v,t)=\langle\mathfrak{f}(Y,v,t)\rangle
.
Expanding to quadratic orders in fluctuations, we get

\displaystyle dX_{t}(Z,v)\approx dX_{t}^{\rm eu}(Z,v)+\delta X_{t}(Z,v)+\widehat{dX}_{t}(Z,v),

(41a)

where

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{\rm eu}(Z,v)=\bar{x}_{t}(Z)+vdt+a\int dY\int du~\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,u,t)\Theta\left(\mathcal{U}^{t}_{dt}(Z,v;Y,u)\right)-X_{t}^{q},

(41b)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{t}_{dt}(Z,v;Y,u)=\bar{x}_{t}(Z)+v{dt}-\bar{x}_{t}(Y)-u{dt},

(41c)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta X_{t}(Z,v)&=\delta x_{t}(Z)+a\int dY\int du~\Theta\left(\mathcal{U}^{t}_{dt}(Z,v;Y,u)\right)\delta\mathfrak{f}(Y,u,t),\\
&+a\int dY\int du~\delta\left(\mathcal{U}^{t}_{dt}(Z,v;Y,u)\right)\big(\delta x_{t}(Z)-\delta x_{t}(Y)\big)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,u,t),\end{split}

(41d)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{dX}_{t}(Z,v)=&~a\int dY\int du~\delta\big(\mathcal{U}^{t}_{dt}(Z,v;Y,u)\big)~\big(\delta x_{t}(Z)-\delta x_{t}(Y)\big)~\delta\mathfrak{f}(Y,u,t)\\
&~+\frac{a}{2}\int dY\int du~\delta^{\prime}\big(\mathcal{U}^{t}_{dt}(Z,v;Y,u)\big)~\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,u,t)~\big(\delta x_{t}(Z)-\delta x_{t}(Y)\big)^{2}.\end{split}

(41e)

Since
\left\langle\delta X_{t}\right\rangle=0
, we have
\left\langle dX_{t}\right\rangle=dX_{t}^{\rm eu}+\left\langle\widehat{dX}_{t}\right\rangle
which allows us to rewrite Eq. (
41a
) as

\displaystyle dX_{t}(Z,v)\approx\left\langle dX_{t}(Z,v)\right\rangle+\delta X_{t}(Z,v).

(42)

However, we are interested in computing the conditional average

\left\langle dX_{t}|X_{t}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle
– the average displacement conditioned on the
q^{\rm th}
particle being at
(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})
at time
t
. To compute this quantity, We first make the following observations: The displacement given in Eq. (
37
) is written in terms of density. Ideally, one should start with a discrete version of Eq. (
37
) as done in
[
34
]
:

\displaystyle d\mathtt{X}_{\mathtt{t}}(\mathtt{X}_{q},v_{q})

\displaystyle=v_{q}d\mathtt{t}+a\sum_{r\neq q}^{N}\big[\Theta\left(\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{q})+v_{q}d\mathtt{t}-\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{r})-v_{r}d\mathtt{t}\right)-\Theta\left(\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{q})-\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{X}_{r})\right)\big],

(43)

where the discrete transformation
\hat{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{Z})
is given in Eq. (
31
). Since there is a density of particles at phase-space point
(Z,v)
at time
t
, the quantity
dX_{t}(z,v)
is actually a displacement per particle. In terms of continuous density functions, a more appropriate definition of the displacement of the
q^{\rm th}
quasiparticle in duration
dt
starting from location
X_{t}^{q}
with bare velocity
v_{q}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{d}X_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})=\frac{\int dZ\int dv~\mathfrak{f}(Z,v,t)dX_{t}(Z,v)\delta(Z-X_{t}^{q})\delta(v-v_{q})}{\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q},t)},

(44)

and
\left\langle dX_{t}|X_{t}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathcal{d}X_{t}(X_{q},v_{q})\right\rangle
.
Using the form of
dX_{t}(Z.v)
in Eq. (
42
) and splitting
\mathfrak{f}(Z,v,t)=\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)+\delta\mathfrak{f}(Z,v,t)
, one finds

\displaystyle\left\langle dX_{t}|X_{t}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathcal{d}(X_{q},v_{q})\right\rangle=dX_{t}^{\rm eu}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})+\overline{dX}_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q}),

(45a)

where
X_{t}^{\rm eu}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})
is given in Eq. (
41b
) and

\displaystyle\overline{dX}_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})=

\displaystyle~\frac{\left\langle\delta\mathfrak{f}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q},t)\delta X_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})\right\rangle}{\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q},t)}+\left\langle\widehat{dX}_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})\right\rangle,

(45b)

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle\widehat{dX}_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})\right\rangle=&a\int dY\int du~\delta\big(\mathcal{U}^{t}_{dt}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q};Y,u)\big)~\big\langle\big(\delta x_{t}(X_{t}^{q})-\delta x_{t}(Y)\big)~\delta\mathfrak{f}(Y,u,t)\big\rangle\\
&+\frac{a}{2}\int dY\int du~\delta^{\prime}\big(\mathcal{U}^{t}_{dt}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q};Y,u)\big)~\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Y,u,t)~\left\langle\big(\delta x_{t}(X_{t}^{q})-\delta x_{t}(Y)\big)^{2}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(45c)

and
\delta^{\prime}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\delta(x)
. Recall,
\delta x_{t}(X)
is given in Eq. (
40
).
Note that
dX_{t}^{\rm eu}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})
in Eq. (
41b
) is the displacement of the quasiparticle in duration
dt
starting from position
X_{t}^{q}
at time
t
, if one considers contribution only from the mean fluid flow evolving according to the Euler equation.
We will see later that the correction
\overline{dX}_{t}
to the Euler displacement in Eq. (
45a
) appears due to the LR correlation of the density fields and hence will be of
O(1/\ell)
. Together,
dX_{t}^{\rm eu}
and
\overline{dX}_{t}
provide the mean displacement of the
q^{\rm th}
quasiparticle in duration
dt
starting from position
X_{t}^{q}
at time
t
.

The noise
\delta X_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})
arises due to the stochastic evolution of the single-particle phase space density
\mathfrak{f}(Y,v,t)
. For the initial state
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhr}
, it has been shown in
[
34
,
41
]
that both
\overline{dX}_{t}
and
\left\langle\delta X_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})^{2}\right\rangle
would contribute to the diffusive (as shown later) correction to the Euler scale displacement
of the quasiparticle at time
t
that started from position
X_{t}^{q}
with velocity
v_{q}
. In the following, we will derive a similar diffusive scale correction to the Euler scale displacement for the initial state
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
defined in Eq. (
5
).

Clearly, for
dt\to 0
one has
dX_{t}^{\rm eu}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})\to 0
. Expanding the right hand side of Eq. (
41b
) to
O(dt)
one finds that

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{\rm eu}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})\simeq v_{\rm eff}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q},t)dt+O(dt^{2}),

(46)

where
v_{\rm eff}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})
is given in Eq. (
9
).

Recall from previous section that for both the initial conditions
\mathscr{C}(X,v;Y,u;t)
[see Eq. (
20b
)] is sum of a singular part representing GGE correlation and a non-singular part containing the LR correlation.
Since
\overline{dX}_{t}
depends linearly on the density correlations, one can write the contributions from
\mathscr{C}^{\rm r}_{\rm gge}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\rm r}_{\rm lr}
separately

\displaystyle\overline{dX}_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})=\overline{dX}_{t}^{\rm gge}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})+\overline{dX}_{t}^{\rm lr}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q}).

(47)

The conditional variance of the infinitesimal displacement
dX_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})
is denoted by
\left\langle\delta\hskip-4.73611pt\raisebox{2.15277pt}{--}X_{t}^{2}|X_{t}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle
and is defined by

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta\hskip-4.73611pt\raisebox{2.15277pt}{--}X_{t}^{2}|X_{t}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle=\bigg\langle\Big(~dX_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})-\left\langle dX_{t}|X_{t}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle\Big)^{2}\Big|X_{t}^{q},v_{q}\bigg\rangle.

(48)

Inserting
dX_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q})
from Eq. (
41a
) and
\left\langle dX_{t}|X_{t}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle
from Eq. (
45
) into the above equation, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta\hskip-4.73611pt\raisebox{2.15277pt}{--}X_{t}^{2}|X_{t}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle=\left\langle\delta X_{t}(Z,v)^{2}\right\rangle_{Z=X_{t}^{q}\atop v=v_{q}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right),

(49)

where
\delta X_{t}(Z,v)
is given in Eq. (
41d
). The
O(1/\ell^{2})
term contains the contribution from higher-order correlations which can be neglected.
As before, one has two contributions to the variance

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle\delta\hskip-4.73611pt\raisebox{2.15277pt}{--}X_{t}^{2}|X_{t}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle&=\left\langle\delta\hskip-4.73611pt\raisebox{2.15277pt}{--}X_{t}^{2}|X_{t}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle^{\rm gge}+\left\langle\delta\hskip-4.73611pt\raisebox{2.15277pt}{--}X_{t}^{2}|X_{t}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle^{\rm lr}\\
&=\left[\left\langle\delta X_{t}^{2}(Z,v)\right\rangle^{\rm gge}+\left\langle\delta X_{t}^{2}(Z,v)\right\rangle^{\rm lr}\right]_{Z=X_{t}^{q}\atop v=v_{q}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right).\end{split}

(50)

It can be shown that for
dt\to 0
, both
\overline{dX}_{t}(Z,v)\to 0
and
\left\langle\delta X_{t}^{2}(Z,v)\right\rangle\to 0
. In fact, to leading order in
dt
one finds (
see
D
and
E
)

\displaystyle\overline{dX}_{t}^{\rm gge}(Z,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}~\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm gge}(Z,v,t)~dt+O(dt^{2}),

(51a)

\displaystyle\overline{dX}_{t}^{\rm lr}(Z,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}~\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr}(Z,v,t)~dt+O(dt^{2}),

(51b)

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta X_{t}^{2}(Z,v)\right\rangle^{\rm gge}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{2}}{\ell}~D_{t}(Z,v)~dt+O(dt^{2}),

(51c)

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta X_{t}^{2}(Z,v)\right\rangle^{\rm lr}

\displaystyle=0~+~O(dt^{2}).

(51d)

Using the structure of GGE and LR correlations, studied in the previous section, one can show that (see Eqs. (
82b
) , (
126
) and (
157
))

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr}(Z,v,t)=

\displaystyle~\frac{a}{\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)(1-\bar{\varrho}(Z,t))}~\int du~\int du^{\prime}\int dw~(v-u)\left[\delta(u-u^{\prime})+\frac{a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X_{t}^{q},u,t)}{1-\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)}\right]

(52a)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~\times~~\left[\delta(v-w)+\frac{a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)}{1-\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)}\right]~\mathscr{C}^{{\rm r,sgn}(v_{q}-u)}_{\rm lr}(Z,w;Z,u^{\prime};t),

(52b)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm gge}(Z,v,t)=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{2}\Bigg[\frac{\partial_{Z}\int du~|v_{q}-u|\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t)}{1-a\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)}+\partial_{Z}\left(\frac{\int du~|v_{q}-u|\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t)}{1-a\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)}\right)\Bigg],

(52c)

\displaystyle D_{t}(Z,v)=

\displaystyle\frac{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1}}{1-a\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)}\int du|v-u|~\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t),

(52d)

where
\mathscr{C}^{{\rm r},\pm}_{\rm lr}
is defined in Eq. (
27
).
Inserting the contributions from Eqs. (
46
), (
51a
) and (
51b
), into Eq. (
45a
), we observe

\displaystyle\left\langle dX_{t}|X_{t}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle=\Big(v_{\rm eff}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q},t)+\frac{1}{\ell}\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q},t)\Big)dt~+O(dt^{2}),

(53)

where

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q},t)=

\displaystyle\left(\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm gge}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q},t)+\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q},t)\right).

(54)

4.1
Mean and variance of the quasiparticle location at time
t
:

We start by writing the position
X_{t}^{q}
of the
q^{\rm th}
quasiparticle in time
t
, starting from
X_{q}
with velocity
v_{q}
, as

\displaystyle X_{t}^{q}=X_{q}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{d}X_{s}(X_{s}^{q},v_{q}),

where, recall from Eq. (
37a
),

dX_{s}(X_{s}^{q},v_{q})=X_{s+ds}^{q}-X_{s}^{q}=X_{ds}(X_{s}^{q},v_{q})-X_{s}^{q}
and
X_{q}=X_{0}^{q}
.
The average position conditioned on the initial location
(X_{q},v_{q})
is

\displaystyle\left\langle X_{t}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=X_{q}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\left\langle dX_{s}|X_{s}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle\right\rangle_{X_{s}^{q}},

(55)

where the average
\left\langle...\right\rangle_{X_{s}^{q}}
represents
\left\langle...\right\rangle_{X_{s}^{q}}=\int dX_{s}^{q}~...\mathcal{P}_{q}(X_{s}^{q},s|X_{q},v_{q})
with
\mathcal{P}_{q}(X_{s}^{q},s|X_{q},v_{q})
being the probability density of finding the
q^{\rm th}
quasiparticle at position
X_{s}^{q}
at time
s
starting from position
X_{q}
at
s=0
. Inserting the expression of
\left\langle dX_{s}|X_{s}^{q},v_{q}\right\rangle
from Eq. (
53
) into Eq. (
55
), we have

\displaystyle\left\langle X_{t}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=X_{q}+\int_{0}^{t}ds~\left\langle v_{\rm eff}(X_{s}^{q},v_{q},s)+\frac{1}{\ell}\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}(X_{s}^{q},v_{q})\right\rangle_{X_{s}^{q}}.

(56)

Separating the mean, we can write

\displaystyle X_{t}=\left\langle X_{t}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle+\Delta X_{t}^{q},

(57)

where
\Delta X_{t}^{q}
represents the fluctuation of
X_{t}^{q}
.
Using this relation at
t=s
inside the integrand and then expanding it to quadratic order in
\Delta X_{s}^{q}
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle X_{t}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle=X_{q}+\int_{0}^{t}ds~\Big[v_{\rm eff}(\left\langle X_{s}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle,v_{q},s)&+\frac{1}{\ell}\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}(\left\langle X_{s}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle,v_{q},s)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}v_{\rm eff}(\left\langle X_{s}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle,v_{q},s)}{\partial\left\langle X_{s}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle^{2}}~\left\langle\big(\Delta X_{s}^{q}\big)^{2}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle\Big],\end{split}

(58)

where
\left\langle\big(\Delta X_{s}^{q}\big)^{2}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle
is the variance of
X_{s}^{q}
conditioned on
X_{0}^{q}=X_{q}
and velocity
v_{q}
.

To compute this variance, we follow a similar procedure as done for the mean. We first note that

\displaystyle\left\langle\big(\Delta X_{t}^{q}\big)^{2}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\bigg\langle\Big(~X_{t}^{q}-\left\langle X_{t}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle\Big)^{2}\Big|X_{q},v_{q}\bigg\rangle,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{t}ds^{\prime}\int dZ\int dZ^{\prime}~\left[\frac{\mathbb{C}_{s,s^{\prime}}(Z,v_{q};Z^{\prime},v_{q})}{{dt}^{2}}\right]_{{dt}\to 0}~\mathcal{P}_{q}(Z,s;Z^{\prime},s^{\prime}),

(59)

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{s,s^{\prime}}(Z,v_{q};Z^{\prime},v_{q})=\left\langle\delta X_{s}(Z,v_{q})\delta X_{s^{\prime}}(Z^{\prime},v_{q})\right\rangle,

(60)

and
\mathcal{P}_{q}(Z,s;Z^{\prime},s^{\prime})
is the joint probability of finding the
q^{\rm th}
quasiparticle at positions
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
at times
s
and
s^{\prime}
respectively.

Note that the variance in Eq. (
59
) is in the form

\displaystyle\left\langle\big(\Delta X_{t}^{q}\big)^{2}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}ds^{\prime}~\mathtt{C}_{vv}^{q}(s,s^{\prime}),

(61)

where
\mathtt{C}_{v,v^{\prime}}(s,s^{\prime})
is the velocity auto-correlation function defined as
\mathtt{C}_{vv}^{q}(s,s^{\prime})=\left\langle\delta v_{s}^{q}\delta v_{s^{\prime}}^{q}\right\rangle,

with
\delta v_{s}=v_{s}^{q}-\left\langle v_{s}^{q}\right\rangle
being the fluctuation in the velocity
v_{s}^{q}=\frac{\mathcal{d}X_{t}^{q}}{dt}
of the
q^{\rm th}
quasiparticle at time
s
. From Eq. (
59
) we identify

\displaystyle\mathtt{C}_{vv}^{q}(s,s^{\prime})=\int dZ\int dZ^{\prime}~\left[\frac{\mathbb{C}_{s,s^{\prime}}(Z,v_{q};Z^{\prime},v_{q})}{{dt}^{2}}\right]_{{dt}\to 0}~\mathcal{P}_{q}(Z,s;Z^{\prime},s^{\prime}).

(62)

As shown in Eq. (
158
) of
E
, in the
{dt}\to 0
limit, the correlation
\mathbb{C}_{s,s^{\prime}}(Z,v_{q};Z^{\prime},v_{q})
is proportional to
\delta(s-s^{\prime})
. On the other hand, for
s=s^{\prime}
the joint probability
\mathcal{P}_{q}(Z,s;Z^{\prime},s^{\prime})=\delta(Z-Z^{\prime})\mathcal{P}_{q}(Z,s|X_{q},v_{q})
. We use these facts to simplify the expressions of
\mathtt{C}_{v,v^{\prime}}(s,s^{\prime})
and consequently of
\left\langle\big(\Delta X_{t}^{q}\big)^{2}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle
. We get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathtt{C}_{vv}^{q}(s,s^{\prime})=&~\delta(s-s^{\prime})~\frac{a^{2}}{\ell}~\int dZ~D_{s}(Z,v_{q})\mathcal{P}(Z,s|X_{q},v_{q}))\\
=&~\delta(s-s^{\prime})~\frac{a^{2}}{\ell}~\left\langle D_{s}(X_{s}^{q},v_{q})|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle_{X_{s}^{q}},\end{split}

(63)

where we have used Eq. (
51c
) and recall
X_{0}^{q}=X_{q}
.
Inserting this result in Eq. (
61
) we get

\displaystyle\left\langle\big(\Delta X_{t}^{q}\big)^{2}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle=\frac{a^{2}}{\ell}\int_{0}^{t}ds~\left\langle D_{s}(X_{s}^{q},v_{q})|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle_{X_{s}^{q}}.

(64)

Once again, replacing
X_{s}^{q}=\left\langle X_{s}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle+\Delta X_{s}^{q}
inside the integrand, we expand it in powers of
\Delta X_{s}^{q}
. Each power can be individually averaged; however, higher order moments would give contributions at order smaller than
O(1/\ell)
– hence we neglect them. Finally, we find

\displaystyle\left\langle\big(\Delta X_{t}^{q}\big)^{2}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{\ell}\int_{0}^{t}ds~D_{s}(\left\langle X_{s}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle,v_{q})+O\left(\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right).

(65)

One can now insert this expression of the variance of
X_{t}^{q}
in Eq. (
58
) to get the following integro- differential equation for the mean

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d\left\langle X_{t}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle}{dt}=v_{\rm eff}&(\left\langle X_{t}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle,v_{q},t)+\frac{1}{\ell}\bigg[\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}(\left\langle X_{t}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle,v_{q},t)\\
&+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}v_{\rm eff}(\left\langle X_{t}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle,v_{q},t)}{\partial\left\langle X_{t}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}dsD_{s}(\left\langle X_{s}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle,v_{q})\bigg]+O\left(\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right),\end{split}

(66)

which must be solved with initial condition
\left\langle X_{0}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle=X_{q}
. From this equation we see that the mean
\left\langle X_{t}^{q}\right\rangle
has the
form
\left\langle X_{t}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle=X_{t}^{\rm eu}(X_{q},v_{q})+O(1/\ell)
, using which in the integrand of Eq. (
65
), we get

\displaystyle\left\langle\big(\Delta X_{t}^{q}\big)^{2}|X_{q},v_{q}\right\rangle=\frac{a^{2}}{\ell}\int_{0}^{t}ds~D_{s}(X_{s}^{\rm eu},v_{q})+O\left(\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right).

(67)

where the Euler location
X_{t}^{\rm eu}
of the
q^{\rm th}
quasiparticle is given by the solution of

\displaystyle\frac{dX_{t}^{\rm eu}}{dt}=v_{\rm eff}(X_{t}^{\rm eu},v_{q},t),

(68)

with initial condition
X_{0}^{\rm eu}=X_{q}
. A small extension of the above calculation provides the following expression for the auto-correlation

\displaystyle\big\langle\Delta X_{t}^{q}\Delta X_{t^{\prime}}^{q}|X_{q},v_{q}\Big\rangle=\frac{a^{2}}{\ell}\int_{0}^{\min(t,t^{\prime})}ds~D_{s}(X_{s}^{\rm eu},v_{q})+O\left(\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right).

(69)

These results agree with previous derivations of variance
[
45
,
47
]
and auto-correlation
[
47
]
for
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
using microscopic approaches.

Remark:

Note, in the above derivation for the mean, variance, and auto-correlation of a quasiparticle, we have never used the explicit form of the LR correlation – we have only used the particular structure of the correlation in Eqs. (
20b
) and (
27
). As discussed in sect.
3
, such a structure is present in
\mathscr{C}(X,v;Y,u;t)
for both choices of initial states
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhr}
and
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
(see Eqs. (
3
) and (
5
)). Hence, these results hold for both initial states.

Our first set of main results are the expressions in Eqs. (
66
), (
67
) and (
69
), respectively, of the mean, variance and autocorrelation of the position of the quasiparticle starting from
X_{q}
with velocity
v_{q}
and the second main result is, they are valid for both
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
and
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhr}
initial states.

4.2
Homogeneous initial state:

The homogeneous initial state is characterized by a space independent average single particle phase space density
\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v)=\bar{\varrho}_{0}\mathcal{h}(v)
, where
\mathcal{h}(v)
is the velocity distribution and
\bar{\varrho}_{0}
is the uniform mass density. For this state, it is easy to see from Eq. (
52
) that there are no drift contributions to the correction to the Euler currents,
i.e.,

\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm gge}=\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr}=0
. Consequently, the mean location of the quasiparticle is given by the Euler location of the particle
\left\langle X_{t}^{q}|X_{q}\right\rangle=X_{t}^{\rm eu}
. Since
\mathcal{j}_{\rm f}^{\rm gge}
becomes space independent, for the autocorrelation we find

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta X_{t}^{q}\Delta X_{t^{\prime}}^{q}\right\rangle=\frac{\min(t,t^{\prime})}{\ell}\frac{a^{2}\bar{\varrho}_{0}}{1-a\bar{\varrho}_{0}}\int du~|v-u|~\mathcal{h}(u),

(70)

which in terms of the microscopic variables (unscaled) reads

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\mathtt{X}_{\mathtt{t}}^{q}\Delta\mathtt{X}_{\mathtt{t}^{\prime}}^{q}\right\rangle=\min(\mathtt{t},\mathtt{t}^{\prime})\frac{a^{2}\bar{\varrho}_{0}}{1-a\bar{\varrho}_{0}}\int du~|v-u|~\mathcal{h}(u),

(71)

as was derived in
[
47
]
using a microscopic approach.

5
Hydrodynamics on diffusion scale

We are interested in computing the diffusive scale correction to the Euler equation (
8
). For a smooth function
\Psi(X,v)
we define

\displaystyle\Psi(t)=\left\langle\sum_{q=1}^{N}\Psi(X_{t}(X_{q},v_{q}),v_{q})\right\rangle=\int dZ\int dv~\Psi(Z,v)~\left\langle\sum_{q=1}^{N}\delta(Z-X_{t}(X_{q},v_{q}))(v-v_{q})\right\rangle.

(72)

Evolving
\Psi(t)
infinitesimally from
t
to
t+dt
we get

\displaystyle\Psi(t+dt)=

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{q=1}^{N}\Psi(X_{t+dt}^{q},v_{q})\right\rangle=\left\langle\sum_{q=1}^{N}\Psi(X_{t}^{q}+dX_{t}(X_{t}^{q},v_{q}))\right\rangle

(73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dZ\int dv~\left\langle\mathfrak{f}(Z,v,t)\Psi(Z+dX_{t}(Z,v),v)\right\rangle

(74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dZ\int dv~\left\langle\left(\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)+\delta\mathfrak{f}(Z,v,t)\right)\Psi(Z+\left\langle dX_{t}(Z,v)\right\rangle+\delta X_{t}(Z,v),v)\right\rangle,

(75)

where recall
\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)=\left\langle\mathfrak{f}(Z,v,t)\right\rangle
.
Expanding the right hand side to quadratic order in
dX_{t}
we get

\displaystyle\Psi(t+dt)

\displaystyle=\int dZ\int dv~\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)\Psi(Z,v,t)

\displaystyle~+\int dZ\int dv\bigg\langle\left(\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)+\delta\mathfrak{f}(Z,v,t)\right)\big[\langle dX_{t}(Z,v)\rangle+\delta X_{t}(Z,v)\big]\bigg\rangle\partial_{Z}\Psi(Z,v)

(76)

\displaystyle~+\int dZ\int dv~\bigg\langle\left(\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)+\delta\mathfrak{f}(Z,v,t)\right)\left[\left\langle dX_{t}(Z,v)\right\rangle+\delta X_{t}(Z,v)\right]^{2}\bigg\rangle\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{Z}\Psi(Z,v)+O(dX_{t}^{3}).

On the other hand, from the definition (
72
) of
\Psi(t+dt)
, we get

\displaystyle\Psi(t+dt)=

\displaystyle\int dZ\int dv~\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t+dt)\Psi(Z,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dZ\int dv~\Psi(Z,v)\Big(\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)+dt\partial_{t}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)+O(dt^{2})\Big)

(77)

Equating the right hand sides of Eqs. (
76
) and (
77
), and taking the limit of small
dt
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)=&-\partial_{Z}\Big[\bar{v}_{t}(Z,v)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)-\frac{a^{2}}{2\ell}\partial_{Z}\left(D_{t}(Z,v)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)\right)\Big],\end{split}

(78)

where

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{t}(Z,v)=

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)\left\langle dX_{t}(Z,v)\right\rangle+\left\langle\delta X_{t}(Z,v)\delta\mathfrak{f}(Z,v,t)\right\rangle}{\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)~dt}\bigg]_{dt\to 0}=\bigg[\frac{\left\langle dX_{t}|Z,v\right\rangle}{dt}\bigg]_{dt\to 0},

(79)

\displaystyle D_{t}(Z,v)=

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{\ell\left\langle\delta X_{t}^{2}(Z,v)\right\rangle}{a^{2}{dt}}\bigg]_{dt\to 0},

(80)

Recall the definition of
\left\langle dX_{t}|Z,v\right\rangle
from Eq. (
45a
).
According to Eq. (
53
) one has
\left\langle dX_{t}|Z,v\right\rangle=v_{\rm eff}(Z,v,t)dt+\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}(Z,v,t)dt

where recall from Eq. (
54
) that
\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}=\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm gge}+\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr}
. For homogeneous initial state
\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr}=0
and the rest of the term simplifies to reproduce the Navier-Stokes equation
(
10
) derived previously in
[
33
,
42
]
making a local equilibrium approximation for the statistical state in a fluid cell. However, for the inhomogeneous initial state, this GHD equation gets modified due to the presence of LR correlation
[
36
,
37
]
that gets generated through coherent transport of initial fluctuations in distant parts of the system through Euler evolution. This fact was first proved in
[
35
]
for generic integrable systems and later demonstrated for the hard-rod gas through explicit microscopic calculations
[
34
,
41
]
for
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhr}
. For this initial state they showed that the precise cancelation below happens

\displaystyle\partial_{Z}\Big[\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm gge}(Z,v,t)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)-\frac{a^{2}}{2}\partial_{Z}\big(D_{t}(Z,v)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)\big)+\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr,asym}(Z,v,t)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)\Big]=0,

(81)

where a further decomposition
\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr}=\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr,sym}+\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr,asym}
has been used. This decomposition is possible because the correlation
\mathscr{C}^{\rm r}_{\rm lr}(X,v;Y,u;t)
can be decomposed in this way as shown in Eq. (
29
). For the initial state
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
, using the explicit expressions of the correlation
\mathscr{C}^{\rm r}_{\rm lr}(X,v;Y,u;t)
in Eq. (
26
), it is also possible to
easily show that the same cancelation occurs as in Eq. (
81
). (see
F
). Hence, for the inhomogeneous case of
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
initial state, the GHD equation (
78
) for
\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)
becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,\bar{t})+\partial_{Z}\left(v_{\rm eff}(Z,v,\bar{t})\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,\bar{t})\right)=-\frac{1}{\ell}\partial_{Z}\Big[\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr,sym}(Z,v,t)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)\Big],

(82a)

where
v_{\rm eff}(Z,v,t)
is given in Eq. (
9
) and

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr,sym}(Z,v,t)=

\displaystyle~\frac{a}{\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)(1-\bar{\varrho}(Z,t))}~\int du~\int du^{\prime}\int dw~(v-u)

\displaystyle~~\times~\left[\delta(u-u^{\prime})+\frac{a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t)}{1-\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)}\right]~\times~\left[\delta(v_{q}-w)+\frac{a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)}{1-\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)}\right]

(82b)

\displaystyle~~\times~\Big[a^{2}\left(\partial_{Z}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t)\right)\left(\partial_{Z}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,w,t)\right)\bar{\mathscr{F}}(Z,t)-\frac{a}{2}(1-a\bar{\varrho}(Z,t))\partial_{Z}\big(\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,w,t)\big)\Big],

with
\bar{\mathscr{F}}(X,t)=\int dZ^{\prime}\int dw^{\prime}~\Theta(Z-Z^{\prime})\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,w^{\prime},t)
. The expression of
\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr,sym}(Z,v,t)
can be further simplied to

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr,sym}

\displaystyle(Z,v,t)=~\frac{a}{\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)(1-\bar{\varrho}(Z,t))}~\int du~(v-u)

(82c)

\displaystyle~\times~\Bigg\{\Big[a^{2}\left(\partial_{Z}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t)\right)\left(\partial_{Z}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\right)\bar{\mathscr{F}}(Z,t)-\frac{a}{2}(1-a\bar{\varrho}(Z,t))\partial_{Z}\big(\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)\big)\Big],

\displaystyle~+~\frac{a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)}{1-\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)}\Big[a^{2}\left(\partial_{Z}\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)\right)\left(\partial_{Z}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t)\right)\bar{\mathscr{F}}(Z,t)-\frac{a}{2}(1-a\bar{\varrho}(Z,t))\partial_{Z}\big(\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t)\big)\Big],

\displaystyle~+~\frac{a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t)}{1-\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)}\Big[a^{2}\left(\partial_{Z}\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)\right)\left(\partial_{Z}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)\right)\bar{\mathscr{F}}(Z,t)-\frac{a}{2}(1-a\bar{\varrho}(Z,t))\partial_{Z}\big(\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)\big)\Big],

\displaystyle~+~\frac{a^{2}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t)}{(1-\bar{\varrho}(Z,t))^{2}}\Big[a^{2}\left(\partial_{Z}\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)\right)\left(\partial_{Z}\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)\right)\bar{\mathscr{F}}(Z,t)-\frac{a}{2}(1-a\bar{\varrho}(Z,t))\partial_{Z}\big(\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)^{2}\big)\Big]\Bigg\}.

The Eq. (
82
) describes HD of hard-rod gas on diffusion scale for
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
initial state and this is our third main result.For
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhr}
[see Eq. (
3
)], the corresponding HD equation for
\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)
was obtained in
[
34
,
41
]
however the structure of the diffusion scale term is different from that of
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
as the long range parts of the correlation are different in these two cases.

6
Conclusion

We study the stochastic motion of a quasiparticle in a gas of hard rods. We computed the mean, variance, and autocorrelation of a quasiparticle for two choices of initial states, one with LR correlation and the other without it. The calculation required us to derive the LR correlations explicitly – while the LR correlations were known for the initial state
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhr}

[
34
,
37
,
41
]
, we provide new results for the initial state
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
. These results for LR correlations are further used to derive expressions of the mean, variance, and autocorrelation of a quasiparticle at any time
t
, valid for both initial states. We observe that the mean location of the quasiparticle at time
t
receives a diffusive scale correction to the prediction from the mean Euler GHD equation through LR correlations. This correction essentially contributes to the diffusion scale term of the GHD equation satisfied by the mean phase space density
\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)
and, along with the fluctuations, modifies it from the LE form. However, as argued in
[
34
]
the diffusive scale equation for
\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)
does not produce any entropy as combined with the evolution of the two-point correlation, the hydro-scale dynamics is still time reversal symmetric.

An interesting and important direction to explore would be to achieve a fluctuating hydrodynamic description of hard-rod system valid at mesoscopic scale. According to recent studies
[
41
,
48
]
the Euler equation (
8
) with
\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)
replaced by a fluctuating density field
\mathfrak{f}(Z,v,t)
provides the correct fluctuating hydrodynamics, including diffusive scale. The same idea has also been used to develop the BMFT
[
37
]
. This essentially means that, hard-rod systems being integrable, there are no bare diffusion and emergent noise at the mesoscopic scale. It would be interesting to demonstrate this more explicitly. Second, the microcopic dynamics of hard rods starting from
{\rm IC}_{\rm fhp}
initial state being analytically tractable, it would be interesting to compare the microscopic computation of the current fluctuation with hydrodynamic predictions.

\mathscr{C}^{\rm r}(X,v;Y,u;t)

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{\rm r}_{\rm lr}(X,v;Y,u;t)\overset{X\approx Y}{=}\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,sym}_{\rm lr}(X,v,u,t)+\text{sgn}(X-Y)\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,asym}_{\rm lr}(X,v,u,t),

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,sym}_{\rm lr}(X,v,u,t)=

\displaystyle~a^{2}\left(\partial_{X}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\right)\left(\partial_{X}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,u,t)\right)\bar{\mathscr{F}}(X,t)-\frac{a}{2}(1-a\bar{\varrho}(X,t))\partial_{X}\big(\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,u,t)\big),

(159b)

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{\rm r,asym}_{\rm lr}(X,v,u,t)=

\displaystyle-\frac{a}{2}(1-a\bar{\varrho}(X,t))\Big[\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,u,t)\partial_{X}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)-\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,v,t)\partial_{X}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(X,u,t)\Big],

(159c)

with
\bar{\mathscr{F}}(X,t)=\int dZ\int dv~\Theta(X-Z)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)

Inserting the asymmetric part into the expression in Eq. (
119
), we get

\displaystyle\mathtt{j}_{\rm lr}^{\rm asym}(Z,v,t)=

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{j}}_{\rm d}^{\rm lr,asym}(Z,v,t)\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)

(160)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{2}~\int du~\int du^{\prime}\int dw~(v-u)

\displaystyle~~\times~~\left[\delta(u-u^{\prime})+\frac{a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u,t)}{1-\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)}\right]~\left[\delta(v-w)+\frac{a\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,v,t)}{1-\bar{\varrho}(Z,t)}\right]

\displaystyle~~~~\times~~{\rm sgn}(v-u)\Big[\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u^{\prime},t)\partial_{X}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,w,t)-\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,w,t)\partial_{X}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}(Z,u^{\prime},t)\Big],
