Regular pullback of generalized cluster structures
Misha Gekhtman, Michael Shapiro, Alek Vainshtein

Abstract
. We consider the problem of lifting a regular cluster structure on a quasi-affine variety to the ambient affine space and a similar problem of defining a regular pullback of a regular cluster structure under a dominant rational map between affine spaces. We provide sufficient conditions for the existence of the corresponding object, called an almost-cluster structure, study its combinatorics, compatible Poisson bracket and the corresponding upper cluster algebra.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
13F60

1.
Introduction

In recent years, we studied cluster structures on simple Lie groups and their homogeneous spaces that are compatible with (homogeneous) Belavin–Drinfeld Poisson–Lie brackets, see, e.g.,
[
6
]
and references therein. While working toward a general construction, we encountered in
[
7
]
the following two situations: (i) a cluster-like structure on a Lie group arises as the pullback of a compatible (generalized) cluster structure along a dominant rational map from a Poisson affine space; (ii) a cluster-like structure on a Poisson affine space arises as the pullback of a compatible (generalized) cluster structure on a Lie group embedded in this affine space as a quasi-affine variety.
It turns out that these two procedures share many common features, and
we expect that this pullback mechanism is useful well beyond the particular example considered in
[
7
]
. For this reason, we present here the general construction of a
regular pullback
.

To explain the idea, we begin with a representative special case. Let

\Psi:{\mathbb{A}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
be a dominant rational map, and assume that
{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
carries a
regular
generalized cluster structure
{\mathcal{C}}
, meaning that every cluster variable in every cluster is a regular function on

{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
. Fix affine coordinate systems
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
and
y_{1},\dots,y_{s}
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
, then every cluster variable

f_{l}
can be rewritten as a rational function in
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
, and is not regular on
{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
.
Our goal is to construct on
{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
an analogue of a regular generalized cluster structure, which we denote

\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}
.
Instead of looking at
\Psi^{*}f_{l}
, we take for cluster variables their numerators, which are again regular functions. Additionally, new frozen cluster variables are irreducible factors from the denominators of
\Psi^{*}f_{l}
.
The exchange relations in
\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}
are obtained by pulling back the exchange relations in
{\mathcal{C}}
, rewriting the result over a common denominator, and then taking the numerator.

The key point is that the resulting pullbacks of generalized exchange relations may differ from standard generalized cluster relations. In both settings, the right-hand side is a polynomial whose monomials are linearly ordered. In a usual generalized cluster relation, the lowest and highest monomials are coprime; in a pullback relation, these extreme monomials need not be coprime.
Although in both cases the mutation of relations can be encoded via mutations of an exchange quiver, the quiver mutation rules arising from cluster pullback can be substantially more involved, see Section
4
for details.

The construction of cluster pullback is given in Section
2
. In Section
3
we formulate a necessary condition for the pullback to be well-defined. Section
4
provides a combinatorial description of the mutation rules. In Section
5
we discuss compatibility of Poisson brackets on
{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
and
{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
. Finally, in Section
6
we give sufficient conditions ensuring that the pullback
\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}
of the upper cluster algebra is regular and complete, that is,
\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}={\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{A}}^{r}]
.

2.
General question

Let
G=\{g_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a finite collection of distinct irreducible polynomials on an
s
-dimensional affine space
{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
with coordinates
y_{1},\dots,y_{s}
. Each
g_{i}
defines an irreducible divisor
D_{i}=\{g_{i}=0\}
. Let
V={\mathbb{A}}^{s}\setminus D_{V}
with
D_{V}=\cup_{i\in I}D_{i}
be the quasi-affine variety defined by the collection
\{D_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, so that any
regular function
f
on
V
can be written in a unique way as

(2.1)

f=\hat{f}\prod_{i\in I}g_{i}^{-\lambda_{i}(f)}

where
\hat{f}\in{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\dots,y_{s}]
is a polynomial not divisible by any of
g_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}(f)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
i\in I
.
Note that here and in what follows we allow for negative values of
\lambda_{i}(f)
.

Further, let
r\geq s
and let
\Psi{:\ }{\mathbb{A}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
be a dominant rational map from an
r
-dimensional affine space
{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
with coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
to
{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
satisfying the following condition: there exists a family of
r-s
irreducible algebraically independent polynomials
{\tt H}=\{{\tt h}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{r}]

such that for every
y_{m}
,
m\in[1,s]
, one can write the pullback of
y_{m}

as
\Psi^{*}y_{m}={\tt y}_{m}\prod_{j\in J}{\tt h}_{j}^{-\kappa_{j}(y_{m})}
with
{\tt y}_{m}\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dots.x_{r}]
not divisible by any of
{\tt h}_{j}
and
\kappa_{j}(y_{m})\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
j\in J
; (it is possible that for some
j\in J
,
\kappa_{j}(y_{m})=0
for all

m\in[1,s]
).
Consequently, for any polynomial
p\in{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\dots,y_{s}]
there exists a unique polynomial
{\tt p}\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{r}]
not divisible by any of
{\tt h}_{j}
such that

(2.2)

\Psi^{*}p={\tt p}\prod_{j\in J}{\tt h}_{j}^{-\kappa_{j}(p)}

with
\kappa_{j}(p)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
j\in J
.
In particular, such a polynomial for
g_{i}
is denoted
{\tt g}_{i}
,
i\in I
.
Polynomials
\{{\tt g}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{{\tt h}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
are called
distinguished
.
In what follows it will be convenient to refer to the family of distinguished polynomials as

\{{\tt q}_{k}\}_{k\in K}
with
K=I\cup J
, so that
{\tt q}_{k}={\tt g}_{k}
for
k\in I
and
{\tt q}_{k}={\tt h}_{k}
for
k\in J
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a regular (generalized) cluster structure on
V
, and
F\cup G=\{f_{l}\}_{l=1}^{s}
is an initial cluster for
{\mathcal{C}}
that includes
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in I}
as frozen variables (there may be other frozen variables in
F
as well). It follows from (
2.1
), (
2.2
) that for
f_{l}\in F

(2.3)

\Psi^{*}f_{l}=\hat{\tt f}_{l}\prod_{k\in K}{\tt q}_{k}^{-\lambda_{k}(f_{l})}.

Here
\hat{\tt f}_{l}\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{r}]
is not divisible by any of
{\tt q}_{k}
, exponents
\lambda_{k}(f_{l})
for
k\in I
are defined
in (
2.1
), and exponents
\lambda_{k}(f_{l})\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for
k\in J
can be written
via
\lambda_{i}(f_{l})
,
\kappa_{j}(g_{i})
, and
\kappa_{j}(\hat{f}_{l})
defined above; however, the explicit
formula for
\lambda_{k}(f_{l})
is not essential for further considerations.

Our goal is to study the behavior of functions
\hat{\tt f}_{l}
under transitions between the clusters of
{\mathcal{C}}
directly,
without tracing the behavior of original functions
f_{l}
.
We will prove that under certain mild restrictions, given an initial seed
\Sigma=(Q,F\cup G,{\mathcal{P}})
for
{\mathcal{C}}
, one can define a seed
\Psi^{*}\widehat{\Sigma}=(\Psi^{*}\widehat{Q},\widehat{\tt F}\cup{\tt G}\cup{\tt H},\Psi^{*}\widehat{\mathcal{P}})
, mutations for the quiver
\Psi^{*}\widehat{Q}
, and exchange relations for the almost-cluster variables in
\widehat{\tt F}=\{\hat{\tt f}_{l}\}
, thus giving rise to a (generalized) almost-cluster structure
\Psi^{*}\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
.
When
G=\varnothing
we occasionally drop the hat notation and speak of

\Psi^{*}{\Sigma}=(\Psi^{*}Q,{\tt F}\cup{\tt H},\Psi^{*}{\mathcal{P}})
with
{\tt F}=\{{\tt f}_{l}\}
, and when
\Psi
is trivial, and hence

{\tt H}=\varnothing
, we occasionally drop the
\Psi
notation and speak of
\widehat{\Sigma}=(\widehat{Q},\widehat{F}\cup G,\widehat{\mathcal{P}})
.

We start from two similarly looking examples.

Example 2.1
.

Assume that
s=4
and
I=\varnothing
, so that
V={\mathbb{A}}^{4}
with coordinates
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}
. Define a cluster structure
{\mathcal{C}}
on
V

by the initial cluster
\{f_{1}=y_{1},f_{2}=y_{2},f_{3}=y_{3},f_{4}=y_{1}y_{4}-y_{2}y_{3}\}
with frozen variables
f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}
and a unique exchange relation
f_{1}f_{1}^{\prime}=f_{2}f_{3}+f_{4}
, so that
f_{1}^{\prime}=y_{4}
. Clearly,
{\mathcal{C}}
has only two distinct seeds and is regular. Further, consider
{\mathbb{A}}^{5}
with coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{5}
and a map
\Psi{:\ }{\mathbb{A}}^{5}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{4}
given by

\Psi^{*}y_{1}=x_{1}/x_{5}
and
\Psi^{*}y_{i}=x_{i}
for
i=2,3,4
. Assume that
|J|=1
, and the only distinguished polynomial is

{\tt h}=x_{5}
, so that
{\tt f}_{i}=x_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
,
{\tt f}_{1}^{\prime}=x_{4}
,
{\tt f}_{4}=x_{1}x_{4}-x_{2}x_{3}x_{5}
and

\lambda(f_{1})=\lambda(f_{4})=1
,
\lambda(f_{2})=\lambda(f_{3})=\lambda(f_{1}^{\prime})=0
. Consequently, the exchange relation for

f_{1}

can be rewritten as
({\tt f}_{1}/{\tt h}){\tt f}_{1}^{\prime}={\tt f}_{2}{\tt f}_{3}+{\tt f}_{4}/{\tt h}
, which yields

{\tt f}_{1}{\tt f}_{1}^{\prime}={\tt f}_{2}{\tt f}_{3}{\tt h}+{\tt f}_{4}
. The latter is a usual exchange relation for a cluster structure

\Psi^{*}{{\mathcal{C}}}
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{5}
with frozen variables
{\tt f}_{2},{\tt f}_{3},{\tt f}_{4}
and
{\tt h}
.

Example 2.2
.

In this example
V
and
{\mathcal{C}}
are the same as in Example
2.1
and the map
\Psi{:\ }{\mathbb{A}}^{5}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{4}
is given by

\Psi^{*}y_{2}=x_{2}/x_{5}
and
\Psi^{*}y_{i}=x_{i}
for
i=1,3,4
. The only distinguished polynomial is the same
{\tt h}=x_{5}
, so that

{\tt f}_{i}=x_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
,
{\tt f}_{1}^{\prime}=x_{4}
,
{\tt f}_{4}=x_{1}x_{4}x_{5}-x_{2}x_{3}
and
\lambda(f_{2})=\lambda(f_{4})=1
,

\lambda(f_{1})=\lambda(f_{3})=\lambda(f_{1}^{\prime})=0
. Consequently, the exchange relation for
f_{1}

can be rewritten as
{\tt f}_{1}{\tt f}_{1}^{\prime}=({\tt f}_{2}/{\tt h}){\tt f}_{3}+{\tt f}_{4}/{\tt h}
, which does not yield any cluster-like exchange relation for
{\tt f}_{1}
and
{\tt f}_{1}^{\prime}
.

One may try to fix this problem by considering
\bar{\tt f}_{1}^{\prime}={\tt h}{\tt f}_{1}^{\prime}
that satisfies the cluster exchange relation

{\tt f}_{1}\bar{\tt f}_{1}^{\prime}={\tt f}_{2}{\tt f}_{3}+{\tt f}_{4}
. However, this construction is not canonical: one can consider instead

\bar{\tt f}_{1}={\tt h}{\tt f}_{1}
that satisfies the cluster exchange relation
\bar{\tt f}_{1}{\tt f}_{1}^{\prime}={\tt f}_{2}{\tt f}_{3}+{\tt f}_{4}
. Note that not only the corresponding cluster structures on
{\mathbb{A}}^{5}
are different, but the corresponding upper cluster algebras are different as well. Indeed, in the first case we have

\displaystyle\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}_{1}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{C}}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{5},x_{1}x_{4}x_{5}-x_{2}x_{3}]\cap{\mathbb{C}}[(x_{4}x_{5})^{\pm 1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{5},x_{1}x_{4}x_{5}-x_{2}x_{3}]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{C}}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{5},x_{4}x_{5}]\cap{\mathbb{C}}[(x_{4}x_{5})^{\pm 1},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{5}]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{5},x_{4}x_{5}],

so that
x_{4}\notin\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}_{1}
, while in the second case a similar computation gives

\displaystyle\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}_{2}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{C}}[(x_{1}x_{5})^{\pm 1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{5},x_{1}x_{4}x_{5}-x_{2}x_{3}]\cap{\mathbb{C}}[x_{4}^{\pm 1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{5},x_{1}x_{4}x_{5}-x_{2}x_{3}]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{C}}[x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5},x_{1}x_{5}],

and hence
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}_{1}\neq\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}_{2}
.

Example
2.2
illustrates a general problem. By definition, an exchange relation for an arbitrary pair of adjacent seeds
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
in
{\mathcal{C}}
reads

f_{m}^{\Sigma}f_{m}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}=P_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}
with
P_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{C}}[\{f_{l}^{\Sigma}\}_{l\neq m},\{g_{i}^{\pm 1}\}_{i\in I}]
, where
f_{m}^{\Sigma}
and
f_{m}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}
are cluster variables in
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
, respectivelly, and
P_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}
is the right hand side of the corresponding exchange relation. This relation yields

(2.4)

\frac{\hat{\tt f}_{m}^{\Sigma}\hat{\tt f}_{m}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}}{\prod_{k\in K}{\tt q}_{k}^{\lambda_{k}(f_{m}^{\Sigma})+\lambda_{k}(f_{m}^{\Sigma^{\prime}})}}=\frac{\tilde{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}}{\prod_{k\in K}{\tt q}_{k}^{\Lambda_{k}(P_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}})}}

with
\tilde{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{C}}\left[\{\hat{\tt f}_{l}^{\Sigma}\}_{l\neq m},\{{\tt g}_{i}\}_{i\in I},\{{\tt h}_{j}\}_{j\in J}\right]
not divisible by any of
{\tt q}_{k}
.
We say that there exists a
coherent
pullback of
{\mathcal{C}}
if for any seed
\Sigma
and any mutable index
m

the Laurent monomial

(2.5)

M=\prod_{k\in K}{\tt q}_{k}^{\lambda_{k}(f_{m}^{\Sigma})+\lambda_{k}(f_{m}^{\Sigma^{\prime}})-\Lambda_{k}(P_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}})}

equals 1 and the family
\widehat{\tt F}^{\Sigma}\cup{\tt G}\cup{\tt H}
is algebraically independent on
{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
.

As is shown in the next example, the notion of coherency is rather subtle: it might happen that
M=1
for all exchange relations in some seed
\Sigma
, and
M\neq 1
for an exchange relation in an adjacent seed
\Sigma^{\prime}
.

Example 2.3
.

Assume that
s=3
and
I=\varnothing
, so that
V={\mathbb{A}}^{3}
with coordinates
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}
.
Define a cluster structure
{\mathcal{C}}
on
V

by the initial cluster
\{f_{1}=y_{1},f_{2}=y_{1}^{2}+y_{1}y_{2}-1,f_{3}=y_{3}f_{2}^{2}-y_{1}-y_{2}\}
with the frozen variable
f_{3}
and exchange relations
f_{1}f_{1}^{\prime}=1+f_{2}
and
f_{2}f_{2}^{\prime}=1+f_{1}f_{3}
, so that
f_{1}^{\prime}=y_{1}+y_{2}
and
f_{2}^{\prime}=y_{1}y_{3}f_{2}-1
. Since
{\mathcal{C}}

is of rank 2 with one frozen variable, it has exactly six distinct cluster variables:
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{1}^{\prime},f_{2}^{\prime}
listed above and one more variable

f_{2}^{\prime\prime}
that satisfies the exchange relation
f_{2}f_{2}^{\prime\prime}=f_{1}^{\prime}+f_{3}
in the cluster adjacent to the initial one in
direction 1, so that
f_{2}^{\prime\prime}=y_{3}f_{2}
. We thus see that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a regular cluster structure on
V
.

Further, consider
{\mathbb{A}}^{4}
with coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{4}
and a map
\Psi{:\ }{\mathbb{A}}^{4}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
given by

\Psi^{*}y_{i}=1+x_{i}x_{4}
for
i=1,2
and
\Psi^{*}y_{3}=x_{3}x_{4}
. Assume that
|J|=1
, and the only distinguished polynomial is

{\tt h}=x_{4}
, so that
\Psi^{*}f_{i}={\tt f}_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
,
\Psi^{*}f_{i}^{\prime}={\tt f}_{i}^{\prime}
for
i=1,2
, and

\Psi^{*}f_{2}^{\prime\prime}={\tt f}_{2}^{\prime\prime}{\tt h}
. The right hand sides of exchange relations in the initial cluster are
P_{1}=1+f_{2}
and

P_{2}=1+f_{1}f_{3}
, so that
\tilde{\tt P}_{1}=1+{\tt f}_{2}
and
\tilde{\tt P}_{2}=1+{\tt f}_{1}{\tt f}_{3}
. Consequently,
M

defined via (
2.5
) equals 1. However, in the cluster adjacent to the initial one in
direction 1, the right hand side of the new exchange relation for
f_{2}
is
P_{2}^{\prime\prime}=f_{1}^{\prime}+f_{3}
, so that

\tilde{\tt P}^{\prime\prime}_{2}={\tt f}_{1}^{\prime}+{\tt f}_{3}
, and hence (
2.5
) yields

M=1/{\tt h}
, and so coherence is violated.

The last example in this section exhibits the situation when
I\neq\varnothing
and the cluster structure on

is generalized. This example is important for the study of (generalized) cluster structures on

, where
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}

are subsets of the set of positive simple roots in the

root system and

is a nilpotent isometry (see
[
6
]
for details). In
[
6
]
we treated the subclass
of Belavin–Drinfeld triples that we called aperiodic, which leads to ordinary cluster structures on
GL_{n}
.
The first instance of a periodic
Belavin–Drinfeld triple is treated in
[
5
, Section 6]
as an application of the theory of periodic staircase matrices. It gives a generalized cluster structure on
GL_{6}
. The example below presents a similar construction in
GL_{3}
.

Let

\hat{f}_{1}

\hat{f}_{1}\hat{f}_{1}^{\prime\prime}=(\hat{f}_{3})^{2}f_{8}+\hat{f}_{9}\hat{f}_{3}\hat{f}_{2}^{\prime}+(\hat{f}_{2}^{\prime})^{2}\hat{f}_{6}\hat{f}_{7}f_{8}.

Note that the first and the last term in the right hand side of this relation are not coprime, as required in the definition of a generalized exchange relation. We are interested in recovering such relations for all clusters
in
{{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{C}}}_{3}
.

3.
Sufficient condition for the existence of a coherent pullback

In this section we obtain the following sufficient condition for the existence of a coherent pullback. Recall that
a family of functions
\{\varphi_{m}\}_{m=1}^{p}
is said to be algebraically dependent on a subvariety
W\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
if there exists a polynomial
R(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{p})
that vanishes identically on
W
, and algebraically independent if such a polynomial does not exist.

Theorem 3.1
.

A coherent pullback of a regular (generalized) cluster structure
{\mathcal{C}}
on
V
with the initial cluster

F=\{f_{l}\}_{l=1}^{s}
exists if for any
k\in K

the family
(\hat{\tt F}\cup{\tt G}\cup{\tt H})\setminus\{{\tt q}_{k}\}

is algebraically independent on
\{{\tt q}_{k}=0\}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
.

Proof.

For a pair of adjacent seeds
\Sigma
,
\Sigma^{\prime}
and
an exchange relation
f_{m}^{\Sigma}f_{m}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}=P_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}
in
{\mathcal{C}}
, write down the evaluation of

\tilde{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}
in the ring
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{r}]
similarly to (
2.3
) as a polynomial not divisible by any of
{\tt q}_{k}
divided by
\prod_{k\in K}{\tt q}_{k}^{\lambda_{k}(\tilde{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}})}
.
Clearly,
\lambda_{k}(\tilde{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}})\leq 0
,
since the evaluation is a polynomial. It follows immediately from (
2.4
) that

(3.1)

\lambda_{k}(\tilde{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}})=\lambda_{k}(f_{m}^{\Sigma})+\lambda_{k}(f_{m}^{\Sigma^{\prime}})-\Lambda_{k}(P_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}})

so that condition
M=1
for
M
given by (
2.5
) is equivalent to
\lambda_{k}(\tilde{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}})=0

for all
k\in K
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that for some
k\in K
and some seed
\Sigma
, the family

(\hat{\tt F}^{\Sigma}\cup{\tt G}\cup{\tt H})\setminus\{{\tt q}_{k}\})

is algebraically independent on
\{{\tt q}_{k}=0\}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
is adjacent to
\Sigma

in direction
m\in[1,s]
. Then
\lambda_{k}(\tilde{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}})=0
.

Proof.

Indeed, condition
\lambda_{k}(\tilde{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}})>0
would mean that
{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}
vanishes identically on
\{{\tt q}_{k}=0\}
. Note that
{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}
is not divisible by
{\tt q}_{k}
in the ring

{\mathbb{C}}\left[\{\hat{\tt f}_{l}^{t}\}_{l\neq m},\{{\tt h}_{j}\}_{j\in J}\right]
. Therefore, the polynomial

{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}\bigr\rvert_{{\tt q}_{k}=0}

certifies an algebraic dependence of the family
(\hat{\tt F}^{\Sigma}\cup{\tt H})\setminus\{{\tt q}_{k}\})

on
\{{\tt q}_{k}=0\}
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that for some seed
\Sigma
the families

(\hat{\tt F}^{\Sigma}\cup{\tt G}\cup{\tt H})\setminus\{{\tt q}_{k}\})

are algebraically independent on
\{{\tt q}_{k}=0\}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
for all
k\in K
.
Then the same holds for the families
(\hat{\tt F}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}\cup{\tt G}\cup{\tt H})\setminus\{{\tt q}_{k}\})

for any
\Sigma^{\prime}
mutationally equivalent to
\Sigma
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the assertion of the lemma for a seed
\Sigma^{\prime}
adjacent to
\Sigma
.
Assume that
\Sigma^{\prime}
is adjacent to
\Sigma
in direction
m
.
It follows from Lemma
3.2
that under the assumptions of the lemma,

\lambda_{k}(\tilde{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}})=0

for all
k\in K
. Consequently, conditions (
3.1
) guarantee that (
2.4
) is satisfied with
M=1
, so that

\hat{\tt f}_{m}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}=\tilde{\tt P}_{m}^{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}/\hat{\tt f}_{m}^{\Sigma}
. Therefore, an algebraic dependence for a family

(\hat{\tt F}^{\Sigma^{\prime}}\cup{\tt H})\setminus\{{\tt q}_{k}\}

on
\{{\tt q}_{k}=0\}
would imply an algebraic dependence of the family

(\hat{\tt F}^{\Sigma}\cup{\tt H})\setminus\{{\tt q}_{k}\}

on the same subvariety, a contradiction.
∎

It remains to prove that the conditions of the theorem imply that the family
\widehat{\tt F}^{\Sigma}\cup{\tt G}\cup{\tt H}
is algebraically independent on
{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
. Indeed, assume the contrary, and let
R
be an irreducible polynomial that certifies the algebraic dependence. Pick an arbitrary
{\tt q}_{k}
and write
R={\tt q}_{k}R_{1}+R_{0}
where
R_{0}
is not divisible by
{\tt q}_{k}
. Since
R
is irreducible,
R_{0}
is a non-trivial polynomial that vanishes identically on
\{{\tt q}_{k}=0\}
, a contradiction.
∎

The following example shows that the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
are not necessary for the existence of the regular pullback.

Example 3.4
.

Assume that
s=3
and
I=\varnothing
, so that
V={\mathbb{A}}^{3}
with coordinates
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}
.
Define a cluster structure
{\mathcal{C}}
of rank 1 on
V

by the initial cluster
\{f_{1}=y_{1},f_{2}=y_{1}^{2}+y_{1}y_{2}-y_{3},f_{3}=y_{3}\}
with frozen variables
f_{2},f_{3}
and the
exchange relation
f_{1}f_{1}^{\prime}=f_{2}+f_{3}
. Clearly,
f_{1}^{\prime}=y_{1}+y_{2}
, so that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a regular cluster structure on
V
.

Further, consider
{\mathbb{A}}^{4}
with coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{4}
and a map
\Psi{:\ }{\mathbb{A}}^{4}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
given by

\Psi^{*}y_{1}=x_{1}
and
\Psi^{*}y_{i}=x_{i}x_{4}
for
i=2,3
. Assume that
|J|=1
, and the only distinguished polynomial is

{\tt h}=x_{4}
, so that
\Psi^{*}f_{i}={\tt f}_{i}
for
i=1,2
,
\Psi^{*}f_{3}={\tt f}_{3}{\tt h}
, and
\Psi^{*}f_{1}^{\prime}={\tt f}_{1}^{\prime}
.
The right hand side of the exchange relation in the initial cluster is
P=f_{2}+f_{3}
, so that

\tilde{{\tt P}}={\tt f}_{2}+{\tt h}{\tt f}_{3}
. Consequently, the evaluation of
\tilde{\tt P}
in
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]

equals
x_{1}^{2}+x_{1}x_{2}x_{4}
, and hence the coherent pullback of
{\mathcal{C}}
to

exists. On the other hand,
the family
\{{\tt f}_{1},{\tt f}_{2},{\tt f}_{3}\}
is algebraically dependent on the

\{{\tt h}=x_{4}=0\}
since

.

4.
Combinatorics of a coherent pullback

4.1.
The pullback of a seed

We assume that the generalized cluster structure
{\mathcal{C}}
on
V
is described with the help of quiver with multiplicities

Q=(Q,d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
, where
n
is the number of mutable vertices, see
[
3
,
4
]
for details; without loss of generality we assume that vertices
1,\dots,n
are mutable and vertices
n+1,\dots,s
are frozen. Recall that the standard definition of the quiver mutation in direction
m
for the quiver with multiplicities is modified as follows: if both vertices
i
and
j

in a path
i\to m\to j
are mutable, then this path contributes
d_{m}
edges
i\to j
to the mutated quiver
Q^{\prime}
; if exactly one of the vertices
i
or
j
is frozen then the path contributes
d_{j}
or
d_{i}
edges
i\to j
to
Q^{\prime}
, respectively. The multiplicities at the vertices do not change. The adjacent cluster variable in direction

m
is given by the generalized exchange relation
f_{m}f^{\prime}_{m}=P_{m}=\sum_{r=0}^{d_{m}}T_{r}
with

(4.1)

T_{r}=p_{mr}\prod_{1\leq l\leq n}f_{l}^{rb_{ml}}\prod_{n<l\leq s}f_{l}^{\lfloor rb_{ml}/d_{m}\rfloor}\prod_{1\leq l\leq n}f_{l}^{(d_{m}-r)b_{lm}}\prod_{n<l\leq s}f_{l}^{\lfloor(d_{m}-r)b_{lm}/d_{m}\rfloor};

here
b_{ml}
is the number of arrows from
m
to
l
,
b_{lm}
is the number of arrows from
l
to
m
, and

p_{mr}
are exchange coefficients that satisfy condition
p_{m0}=p_{md_{m}}=1
and are mutated via

p^{\prime}_{mr}=p_{m,d_{m}-r}
. The
y
-variable

t_{m}
at vertex
m
is

t_{m}=\prod_{1\leq l\leq s}f_{l}^{b_{ml}-b_{lm}},

see
[
3
, Section 6.1]
.

We define the quiver
\Psi^{*}\widehat{Q}
in a two-step procedure. First, take a copy of the quiver
Q
and add a new frozen vertex for every
j\in J
(note that vertices corresponding to
i\in I
are already present in
Q
). The variables assigned to the vertices
k\in K
are
{\tt q}_{k}
, and the variables assigned to other vertices are
\hat{\tt f}_{l}
, so that the set of variables is

\widehat{\tt F}\cup{\tt G}\cup{\tt H}
. All arrows between the mutable vertices of
Q
are retained in

\Psi^{*}\widehat{Q}
. Additionally, for any
k\in K
, the
discrepancy

(4.2)

\delta_{km}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}d_{m}b_{lm}\lambda_{k}(f_{l})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=n+1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{s}b_{lm}\lambda_{k}(f_{l})-\sum_{l=1}^{n}d_{m}b_{ml}\lambda_{k}(f_{l})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=n+1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{s}b_{ml}\lambda_{k}(f_{l})

is computed and
|\delta_{km}|

arrows are added between
m
and
k
. These arrows are directed from
m
to
k
if
\delta_{km}
in (
4.2
) is positive, and from
k
to
m
otherwise. As usual, for
k\in I
, the cycles of length 2 formed by the new arrows and existing arrows between
m
and
k
should be deleted. Finally, the pullback of a string
is defined as
\Psi^{*}(p_{mr})_{r=1}^{d_{m}}=(\hat{\tt p}_{mr})_{r=1}^{d_{m}}
, so that
\Psi^{*}\widehat{\Sigma}=(\Psi^{*}Q,\widehat{\tt F}\cup{\tt G}\cup{\tt H},\Psi^{*}\widehat{\mathcal{P}})
is a pullback of
\Sigma=(Q,F\cup G,{\mathcal{P}})
.
The following statement is immediate.

Proposition 4.1
.

The
y
-variables for the seed
\Psi^{*}\widehat{\Sigma}
coincide with the pullback of
y
-variables for the seed
\Sigma
.

The quiver
\Psi^{*}\widehat{Q}
defined above is enough for an adequate description of the regular pullback of a cluster structure, see Section
4.3
. For the case of generalized cluster structures we will further modify the definition of
\Psi^{*}\widehat{Q}
in
Section
4.4
below.

4.2.
Mutations of a coherent pullback

In what follows we assume that the coherency condition
M=1
for
M
defined in (
2.5
) is satisfied for any seed in

{\mathcal{C}}
. Consequently,

(4.3)

\Lambda_{k}(P_{m})=\max_{0\leq r\leq d_{m}}\lambda_{k}(T_{r}),\quad k\in K,

and the exchange relation (
2.4
) for
\hat{\tt f}_{m}
and
\hat{\tt f}_{m}^{\prime}
reads

(4.4)

\hat{\tt f}_{m}\hat{\tt f}_{m}^{\prime}=\sum_{r=0}^{d_{m}}\hat{\tt T}_{r}\prod_{k\in K}{\tt q}_{k}^{\mu_{km}(r)}

with
\mu_{km}(r)=\Lambda_{k}(P_{m})-\lambda_{k}(T_{r})
;
here and in what follows we suppress explicit mention of adjacent seeds
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
. Further,
by (
4.1
),

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(T_{r})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{k}(p_{mr})+r\sum_{l=1}^{n}b_{ml}\lambda_{k}(f_{l})+\sum_{l=n+1}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{rb_{ml}}{d_{m}}\right\rfloor\lambda_{k}(f_{l})

\displaystyle+(d_{m}-r)\sum_{l=1}^{n}b_{lm}\lambda_{k}(f_{l})+\sum_{l=n+1}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{(d_{m}-r)b_{lm}}{d_{m}}\right\rfloor\lambda_{k}(f_{l}),\quad k\in K,r\in[0,d_{m}].

Denoting
\{a\}=a-\lfloor a\rfloor
for any number
a
, one can rewrite the latter expression as

\lambda_{k}(T_{r})=\chi_{km}(r)+\tau_{km}r+\sigma_{km}
with

(4.5)

\displaystyle\chi_{km}(r)=

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(p_{mr})-\sum_{l=n+1}^{s}\left(\left\{\frac{rb_{ml}}{d_{m}}\right\}+\left\{\frac{(d_{m}-r)b_{lm}}{d_{m}}\right\}\right)\lambda_{k}(f_{l}),

\displaystyle\tau_{km}=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}(b_{ml}-b_{lm})\lambda_{k}(f_{l})+\sum_{l=n+1}^{s}\frac{b_{ml}-b_{lm}}{d_{m}}\lambda_{k}(f_{l}),

\displaystyle\sigma_{km}=

\displaystyle d_{m}\sum_{l=1}^{n}b_{lm}\lambda_{k}(f_{l})+\sum_{l=n+1}^{s}b_{lm}\lambda_{k}(f_{l}).

Taking into account that
\chi_{km}(0)=\chi_{km}(d_{m})=0
, we arrive at

\tau_{km}=\frac{\lambda_{k}(T_{d_{m}})-\lambda_{k}(T_{0})}{d_{m}},\quad\sigma_{km}=\lambda_{k}(T_{0}),

which finally yields

(4.6)

\mu_{km}(r)=-\chi_{km}(r)+r\frac{\mu_{km}(d_{m})-\mu_{km}(0)}{d_{m}}+\mu_{km}(0).

Note that after the mutation at
m
, one has
b^{\prime}_{ml}=b_{lm}
and
b^{\prime}_{lm}=b_{ml}
, so that

\chi^{\prime}_{km}(r)=\chi_{km}(d_{m}-r),\qquad\tau^{\prime}_{km}=-\tau_{km},\qquad\sigma^{\prime}_{km}=d_{m}\tau_{km}+\sigma_{km}.

Let
\chi_{km}^{\Sigma}(r)
denote the analog of
\chi_{km}(r)
written for vertex
m
in an arbitrary seed
\Sigma
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
\Sigma
, either
\chi_{km}^{\Sigma}(r)=\chi_{km}(r)
, or
\chi_{km}^{\Sigma}(r)=\chi^{\prime}_{km}(r)
, depending on the parity of the number of mutations at
m
in a sequence connecting
\Sigma
with the initial seed.

Proof.

The statement is clear for the first term in the expression for
\chi_{km}(r)
. Let us prove that the second term does not change after the mutation at
i\neq m
. First, all vertices
l\in[n+1,s]
are frozen, so

\lambda_{k}(f_{l})
does not change during mutations. Note that at least one of
b_{ml}
and
b_{lm}
vanishes. Without loss of generality we assume that
b_{lm}=0
and
b_{ml}\geq 0
. If there is no path
l\to i\to m
or
m\to i\to l
in the quiver, mutation at
i
does not influence
b_{ml}
and
b_{lm}
. Otherwise, either
b_{ml}^{\prime}=b_{ml}\pm d_{m}\geq 0
and
b^{\prime}_{lm}=0
, or

b^{\prime}_{ml}=0
and
b^{\prime}_{lm}=d_{m}-b_{ml}>0
. In the first case,

\left\{\frac{rb^{\prime}_{ml}}{d_{m}}\right\}=\left\{\frac{rb_{ml}}{d_{m}}\pm r\right\}=\left\{\frac{rb_{ml}}{d_{m}}\right\},\quad\left\{\frac{(d_{m}-r)b^{\prime}_{lm}}{d_{m}}\right\}=\left\{\frac{(d_{m}-r)b_{lm}}{d_{m}}\right\}=0,

while in the second case,

\left\{\frac{rb^{\prime}_{ml}}{d_{m}}\right\}=0,\quad\left\{\frac{(d_{m}-r)b^{\prime}_{lm}}{d_{m}}\right\}=\left\{\frac{(d_{m}-r)(d_{m}-b_{ml})}{d_{m}}\right\}=\left\{\frac{rb_{ml}}{d_{m}}\right\},

hence in both cases the coefficient at
\lambda_{k}(f_{l})
in (
4.5
) is preserved.
∎

It follows from (
4.6
) and Proposition
4.2
that behavior of
\mu_{km}(r)
is defined by the behavior
of
\mu_{km}(0)
and
\mu_{km}(d_{m})
. Denote by
\mu_{km}^{i}(0)
and
\mu_{km}^{i}(d_{m})
the values of
\mu_{km}(0)
and

\mu_{km}(d_{m})
after mutation at
i
. Clearly, if there are no arrows between
i
and
m
then

\mu_{km}^{i}(0)=\mu_{km}(0)
and
\mu_{km}^{i}(d_{m})=\mu_{km}(d_{m})
. Otherwise,
\mu_{km}^{i}(0)
and
\mu_{km}^{i}(d_{m})
are
obtained as follows.

Proposition 4.3
.

(i)
If
b_{mi}>0
then

(4.7)

\begin{gathered}\mu_{km}^{i}(0)=\max_{0\leq r\leq d_{m}}\left\{\chi_{km}(r)-\frac{r}{d_{m}}\left(\mu_{km}(d_{m})-\mu_{km}(0)\right)+rb_{mi}\mu_{ki}(0)\right\},\\
\mu_{km}^{i}(d_{m})-\mu_{km}^{i}(0)=\mu_{km}(d_{m})-\mu_{km}(0)-d_{m}b_{mi}\mu_{ki}(0).\end{gathered}

(ii)
If
b_{im}>0
then

(4.8)

\begin{gathered}\mu_{km}^{i}(d_{m})=\max_{0\leq r\leq d_{m}}\left\{\chi_{km}(d_{m}-r)-\frac{r}{d_{m}}\left(\mu_{km}(0)-\mu_{km}(d_{m})\right)+rb_{im}\mu_{ki}(d_{i})\right\},\\
\mu_{km}^{i}(0)-\mu_{km}^{i}(d_{m})=\mu_{km}(0)-\mu_{km}(d_{m})-d_{m}b_{im}\mu_{ki}(d_{i}).\end{gathered}

Proof.

(i) Recall that
\Lambda_{k}(P_{m})=\mu_{km}(0)+\lambda_{k}(T_{0})=\mu_{km}(d_{m})+\lambda_{k}(T_{d_{m}})
. Equating the two expressions above we get

(4.9)

\mu_{km}(0)+d_{m}b_{mi}\lambda_{k}(f_{i})+\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{out}}=\mu_{km}(d_{m})+\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}

with

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle=d_{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}b_{ml}\lambda_{k}(f_{l})+\sum_{l=n+1}^{s}b_{ml}\lambda_{k}(f_{l}),

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle=d_{m}\sum_{l=1}^{n}b_{lm}\lambda_{k}(f_{l})+\sum_{l=n+1}^{s}b_{lm}\lambda_{k}(f_{l}).

In a similar way, after mutation at
i
we have

(4.10)

\mu_{km}^{i}(0)+\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{out}}+d_{m}\sum_{l=1}^{n}b_{mi}d_{i}b_{il}\lambda_{k}(f_{l})+\sum_{l=n+1}^{s}b_{mi}d_{m}b_{il}\lambda_{k}(f_{l})\\
=\mu_{km}^{i}(d_{m})+\lambda_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}+d_{m}b_{mi}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime}_{i}).

Subtracting (
4.10
) from (

),
we arrive at

\mu_{km}^{i}(d_{m})-\mu_{km}^{i}(0)=\mu_{km}(d_{m})-\mu_{km}(0)\\
+d_{m}b_{mi}\left(d_{i}\sum_{l=1}^{n}b_{il}\lambda_{k}(f_{l})+\sum_{l=n+1}^{s}b_{il}\lambda_{k}(f_{l})-\lambda_{k}(f_{i})-\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime}_{i})\right).

To get the second relation in (

), it remains to notice that the expression in the brackets above equals

-\mu_{ki}(0)

To get the first relation in (

) for
\mu_{km}^{i}(r)

taking into account that

by Proposition

,
and use condition

which is equivalent to (

\mu_{km}^{i}(0)=\max_{0\leq r\leq d_{m}}\left\{\chi_{km}(r)-\frac{r}{d_{m}}\left(\mu_{km}^{i}(d_{m})-\mu_{km}^{i}(0)\right)\right\},

and the first relation in (

(ii) The proof is similar, with (
4.6
) replaced by

\mu_{km}(d_{m}-r)=-\chi_{km}(d_{m}-r)+\frac{r}{d_{m}}(\mu_{km}(0)-\mu_{km}(d_{m}))+\mu_{km}(d_{m}).

∎

and denote

and

is given in

Proposition 5.1
.

If all

are Casimirs of
{\{\cdot,\cdot\}}
,
\{f_{m},t_{m}\}=\omega_{m}f_{m}t_{m}
,
\omega_{m}\neq 0

,
and
\{f_{l},t_{m}\}=0
for
l\neq m
,

,
m\in[1,n]
, then
{\{\cdot,\cdot\}}
is compatible with
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Assume that
{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
is equipped with a Poisson bracket
{\{\cdot,\cdot\}}
that satisfies conditions of
Proposition
5.1
, and that there exists a coherent pullback
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
of
{\mathcal{C}}
. Let
{\{\cdot,\cdot\}}_{\rm p}
be a Poisson bracket on
{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
that makes the map
\Psi{:\ }({\mathbb{A}}^{r},{\{\cdot,\cdot\}}_{\rm p})\to({\mathbb{A}}^{s},{\{\cdot,\cdot\}})
Poisson. Further, let
\tilde{\tt t}_{m}
denote the

of the quiver
\Psi^{*}Q
.

Theorem 5.2
.

If all
{\tt p}_{mr}^{*}
are Casimirs of
{\{\cdot,\cdot\}}_{\rm p}
,
\{{\tt h}_{j},\tilde{\tt t}_{m}\}_{\rm p}=0
for all
j\in J
and

, and the set

{\tt F}\cup{\tt H}
forms a log-canonical basis for

then the

{\{\cdot,\cdot\}}_{\rm p}
is compatible with
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

We claim that the conditions of the Theorem imply conditions of Proposition
5.1
. Indeed,

\{{\tt f}_{l},\tilde{\tt t}_{m}\}_{\rm p}=\{\Psi^{*}f_{l}\prod_{j\in J}{\tt h}_{j}^{\lambda_{j}(f_{l})},\tilde{\tt t}_{m}\}_{\rm p}\\
=\left\{\prod_{j\in J}{\tt h}_{j}^{\lambda_{j}(f_{l})},\tilde{\tt t}_{m}\right\}_{\rm p}\Psi^{*}f_{l}+\{\Psi^{*}f_{l},\tilde{\tt t}_{m}\}_{\rm p}\prod_{j\in J}{\tt h}_{j}^{\lambda_{j}(f_{l})}.

By the conditions of the Theorem, the first term above vanishes. Further,

4.1

\tilde{\tt t}_{m}=\Psi^{*}t_{m}

therefore the second term above vanishes for

∎

6.
Regular pullback of the upper cluster algebra

Assume that there exists a coherent pullback of the (generalized) cluster structure
{\mathcal{C}}
on
V
to
{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
. One can define the regular pullback
\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}
of the
upper cluster algebra
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
as the intersection of the rings of Laurent polynomials in almost-cluster variables
over the ground ring of polynomials in frozen variables
taken over all almost-clusters. The relation between
\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}
and
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
under very mild conditions is given by the following statement.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume that any seed of
{\mathcal{C}}
is coprime, then
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
is the localization of
\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}
by the multiplicative set generated by
G=\{g_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.

Proof.

Denote
R={\mathbb{C}}[\{g_{i}^{-1}\}_{i\in I}]
.
To prove the inclusion of the localization of
\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}
in
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
is enough to check that
zh\in\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
for any

z\in R
and any
h\in\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}
. By definition, in any seed of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
,
h
can be written as a Laurent polynomial in almost-cluster variables, which, in turn, are the cluster variables of the corresponding seed of

{\mathcal{C}}
multiplied by a Laurent monomial in
G
. It follows that
h\in\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
. The claim concerning
zh
follows from the fact that
R
is a subring of the ground ring of
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
.

To prove the opposite inclusion, consider an arbitrary
p\in\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
. It can be written as a Laurent polynomial in the initial seed
\Sigma
of
{\mathbb{C}}
. Consequently, the corresponding Laurent polynomial in the initial seed
\widehat{\Sigma}
of

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
differs from
p
by a Laurent monomial in
G
. It follows that there exists a monomial

M
in
G
such that
Mp
is a Laurent polynomial in
\widehat{\Sigma}
. In a similar way, for any adjacent
seed
\Sigma_{m}
there exists a monomial
M_{m}
such that
M_{m}p
is a Laurent polynomial in
\widehat{\Sigma}_{m}
. Thus, for

\bar{M}=M\prod_{m}M_{m}
,
\bar{M}p
is simultaneously a Laurent polynomial in
\widehat{\Sigma}
and all
\widehat{\Sigma}_{m}
. It remains to note that if
P,Q\in{\mathbb{C}}[\{f_{l}\}_{l=1}^{s},\{g_{i}^{-1}\}_{i\in I}]
are coprime then the corresponding

\tilde{P},\tilde{Q}
defined in (
2.4
) are coprime as well, and hence any seed of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is coprime, so that the upper bound defined by
\widehat{\Sigma}
coincides with the whole
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
, see
[
1
, Theorem 1.5]
.
∎

The following more restrictive conditions guarantee that the regular pullback to
{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
of a complete (generalized) cluster structure on

is complete.

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume that there exists a seed
\Sigma=(Q,F\cup G)
of a regular complete generalized cluster structure
{\mathcal{C}}
on
V
such that for every
i\in I
each

,

(i)
there exists a coherent pullback
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
of
{\mathcal{C}}
to
{\mathbb{A}}^{s}
;

(ii)
rational expressions for
y_{m}
are Laurent polynomials that do not contain frozen variables in
\widehat{F}
in the denominator;

(iii)
if additionally these rational expressions do not contain variables in
G
in the denominator then

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is complete.

Proof.

(i) It is sufficient to check that the conditions of Theorem
3.1
are satisfied. Indeed, since the size of the family
\widehat{F}\cup G\setminus\{g_{i}\}
equals the dimension of
D_{i}
, any algebraic dependence would make impossible to restore
y_{m}
at a generic point of
D_{i}
.

(ii) Since
{\mathcal{C}}
is complete, every
y_{m}
can be written as a Laurent polynomial in any seed
\Sigma
of
{\mathcal{C}}
with coefficients that are polynomials in frozen variables in
F
and Laurent polynomials in
G
. This expression can be rewritten in the corresponding seed
\widehat{\Sigma}
as a Laurent polynomial with coefficients that are polynomials in frozen variables in
\widehat{F}
and
G
divided my a monomial
L
in
G
.

(iii) Assume that
L=1
at some seed
\widehat{\Sigma}
. We claim that the same holds for any seed

\widehat{\Sigma}^{\prime}
. Clearly, it is enough to prove that the Laurent representation in the adjacent seed

\widehat{\Sigma}
obtained by replacing an almost-cluster variable
\hat{f}=\hat{f}_{l}
by
\hat{f}^{\prime}
has this property.
Let
R
and
R^{\prime}
be the rings of polynomials in the variables of
\widehat{\Sigma}
and
\widehat{\Sigma}^{\prime}
, respectively. Assume that
y=QM/\hat{f}^{k}
, where

,

is not divisible by a monomial and
M
is a Laurent monomial in almost-cluster variables of
\widehat{\Sigma}
excluding
\hat{f}
and not containing frozen variables in the denominator. Further, let

is not divisible by a monomial and

is a Laurent monomial in variables of

\widehat{\Sigma}^{\prime}

\hat{f}
and
\hat{f}^{\prime}

\hat{f}\hat{f}^{\prime}=P
, where

Let

be the minimal non-negative integer such that

\tilde{Q}(\hat{f}^{\prime})^{k-m}M=Q^{\prime}M^{\prime}P^{k}

∎
