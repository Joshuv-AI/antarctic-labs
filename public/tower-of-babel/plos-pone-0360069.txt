Hybrid RBF neural network–based sliding mode control for a magnetorheological elastomer isolator under variable frequency and amplitude excitations

Kiran Katari, Umanath R Poojary, Ananda Hegde, Gangadharan K. V.

ABSTRACT
Magnetorheological elastomers have attracted significant attention for semi-active vibration isolation applications due to their ability to rapidly change mechanical properties under an applied magnetic field. However, the nonlinear and hysteretic behavior of Magnetorheological elastomer materials poses challenges for achieving robust vibration suppression under varying excitation conditions. This study presents a hybrid control strategy that integrates a radial basis function neural network with a Sliding Mode Controller to enhance the vibration isolation performance of an MRE-based semi-active isolator. A MRE isolator with orthogonal magnetic flux configuration is designed and fabricated, and its dynamic characteristics are evaluated under harmonic excitation in the frequency range of 15–80 Hz. The proposed hybrid controller adaptively estimates system nonlinearities using the neural network while maintaining robustness through sliding mode control. Experimental and simulation results demonstrate that the proposed controller provides effective vibration attenuation while maintaining stable adaptive performance under varying excitation frequency and amplitude. The results show that the semi-active MRE isolator achieves a maximum vibration reduction of 55.8% near the resonance region under the investigated operating conditions, while maintaining stable performance across varying excitation frequencies. The proposed control framework provides an effective solution for handling nonlinear dynamics, and it offers greater adaptability in smart vibration isolation systems based on magnetorheological elastomers.

1 Introduction Magnetorheological elastomers (MREs) are a class of smart composite materials consisting of ferromagnetic particles embedded in an elastomeric matrix. When subjected to an external magnetic field, the mechanical properties of MREs, such as stiffness and damping, can be rapidly and reversibly tuned. This field-dependent behavior makes MREs highly attractive for applications in vibration isolation, adaptive structures, and smart mechanical systems. Compared with magnetorheological fluids, MREs offer advantages such as structural stability, absence of particle sedimentation, and ease of integration into mechanical components. Consequently, MRE-based devices have been extensively investigated for semi-active vibration control applications in recent years [ 1 – 4 ]. Significant research efforts have been devoted to improving the performance of MRE materials through particle distribution, matrix composition, and fabrication techniques. Early studies demonstrated that the mechanical properties of MREs strongly depend on particle concentration, particle alignment, and the type of elastomer matrix used during fabrication [ 5 – 7 ]. Isotropic and anisotropic MRE structures have been widely investigated, where anisotropic MREs typically exhibit higher magneto-induced stiffness due to the chain-like alignment of magnetic particles during curing under magnetic fields [ 8 – 10 ]. Recent studies have further explored the effects of hybrid particle compositions, microstructural optimization, and advanced fabrication techniques to enhance the magneto-mechanical properties of MREs. Researchers have reported significant improvements in magnetorheological effects through the incorporation of nano-scale additives and optimized curing processes [ 11 – 14 ]. Moreover, advances in material characterization techniques have enabled better understanding of the nonlinear viscoelastic behavior and magnetic field-dependent stiffness variation in MRE materials [ 15 – 18 ]. Along with material development, numerous MRE-based vibration mitigation devices have been proposed and experimentally validated. These include MRE vibration isolators, adaptive mounts, sandwich beams, and tuned vibration absorbers designed to mitigate unwanted vibrations in mechanical and structural systems. The field-dependent stiffness of MRE materials allows such devices to adapt their dynamic properties according to changing excitation conditions [ 19 – 21 ]. Recent studies have demonstrated the feasibility of MRE isolators in applications such as vehicle suspension systems, structural vibration mitigation, and precision engineering platforms. Researchers have also developed multi-layer and shear-mode MRE devices capable of providing enhanced vibration suppression performance over wider frequency ranges [ 22 – 25 ]. These developments highlight the potential of MRE-based devices as effective semi-active vibration isolation solutions. By virtue of elastomer matrix, MRE is a viscoelastic material, additionally it attains attributes of a polymer composite due to the ferromagnetic fillers. Under the influence of magnetic field, MRE exhibits field dependent properties. These factors make MRE a complex engineering material which requires integration of the aforementioned domains when modeling the material behavior. Due to the highly nonlinear and hysteretic behavior of MRE materials, accurate modeling plays a crucial role in the design and control of MRE-based vibration isolation systems. Several phenomenological and physics-based models have been proposed to describe the dynamic behavior of MRE materials under varying magnetic fields. Commonly used models include viscoelastic models, Bouc–Wen hysteresis models, and modified Kelvin–Voigt models [ 26 ]. Among these approaches, the Bouc–Wen model has been widely adopted due to its ability to capture nonlinear hysteresis behavior across different excitation conditions. Researchers have proposed modified and improved Bouc–Wen models capable of representing the dynamic characteristics of MRE materials over wide frequency ranges and strain amplitudes [ 27 , 28 ]. In addition, data-driven modeling approaches and machine learning techniques have recently been explored to improve modeling accuracy for complex smart material systems [ 29 ]. Apart from material model, design and development of MRE based devices require an effective control strategy that can make the system adoptable. A considerable research effort has focused on the development of control strategies for MRE-based vibration isolation systems. Traditional model-based and model-free controllers such as proportional–integral–derivative (PID) controllers have been widely used due to their simplicity and ease of implementation in semi-active vibration control systems [ 30 ]. To address nonlinear system behavior and modeling uncertainties, researchers have proposed intelligent control approaches such as fuzzy logic controllers and neural network-based controllers. These techniques can effectively handle nonlinearities without requiring precise mathematical models of the system [ 31 ]. Neural network-based controllers, in particular, have demonstrated strong capability in learning complex nonlinear system dynamics and improving vibration suppression performance in smart structures [ 32 ]. More advanced control techniques such as sliding mode control (SMC) have attracted significant attention due to their robustness against parameter uncertainties and external disturbances. Adaptive control strategies have also been developed to automatically adjust controller parameters in response to varying operating conditions [ 33 ]. Furthermore, optimization-based algorithms and hybrid intelligent control frameworks combining fuzzy logic, neural networks [ 34 ], and robust nonlinear control techniques [ 32 , 35 – 37 ] have been proposed to further enhance vibration mitigation performance in smart vibration isolation systems. In recent years, intelligent control techniques for smart vibration isolation systems have evolved considerably. Recent studies have increasingly adopted adaptive neural networks, adaptive fuzzy sliding mode control, and hybrid optimization-based approaches to improve the performance of smart dampers and magnetorheological elastomer (MRE) isolators operating under nonlinear and uncertain conditions [ 31 – 33 ]. These intelligent controllers enhance adaptability by learning unknown system dynamics online while simultaneously reducing control chattering and improving robustness against parameter uncertainties. Furthermore, hybrid control architectures integrating artificial intelligence with robust nonlinear control techniques have demonstrated superior vibration attenuation, faster convergence, improved disturbance rejection, and enhanced energy efficiency in semi-active smart vibration isolation systems [ 35 – 38 ]. These recent developments further motivate the present study, where a Hybrid Radial Basis Function Neural Network–Sliding Mode Controller is proposed to exploit both the nonlinear approximation capability of neural networks and the robustness of sliding mode control for MRE-based vibration isolation. Despite significant advances in MRE material development, device design, modeling techniques, and control strategies, several challenges remain in achieving robust vibration suppression across a wide range of operating conditions. First, the nonlinear and hysteretic behavior of MRE materials introduces uncertainties that complicate the modeling and control of vibration isolation systems. Second, many existing vibration isolation devices are limited to specific deformation modes, which restricts their ability to effectively attenuate multi-directional vibrations. Third, conventional control strategies may not provide sufficient adaptability when the system is subjected to varying excitation conditions. To address these challenges, hybrid intelligent control strategies that integrate learning-based algorithms with robust nonlinear control methods have recently attracted increasing attention. Such approaches can improve system adaptability while maintaining stability under uncertain dynamic environments. The scientific contribution of the present work extends beyond the implementation of an intelligent controller. Unlike conventional Sliding Mode Control, which relies heavily on an accurate mathematical representation of the system dynamics, the proposed hybrid framework integrates a Radial Basis Function Neural Network (RBFNN) with a physics-based Bouc–Wen hysteresis model to adaptively compensate for nonlinear modeling uncertainties associated with the hysteretic behavior of the MRE isolator. The Bouc–Wen model preserves the physical interpretation of the hysteresis mechanism, while the RBFNN estimates the residual nonlinear dynamics arising from parameter variations, excitation-dependent characteristics, and unmodeled disturbances. Consequently, the proposed framework combines the robustness of Sliding Mode Control with the adaptive learning capability of the RBFNN, resulting in improved vibration suppression under simultaneously varying excitation frequency and amplitude without requiring an exact nonlinear system model. Therefore, there is a strong motivation to develop advanced hybrid intelligent control strategies capable of handling nonlinear hysteretic dynamics while maintaining robust vibration isolation performance over varying excitation frequencies and amplitudes. To address this challenge, this study proposes a Hybrid Radial Basis Function Neural Network–Sliding Mode Controller (RBSM) for an MRE-based semi-active vibration isolator. The proposed framework combines a physics-based Bouc–Wen hysteresis model with adaptive neural network learning to compensate for nonlinear modeling uncertainties while preserving the robustness of Sliding Mode Control. The amplitude dependent behavior is a typical characteristic often found in the filled elastomers. The MRE also belongs to a class of filled elastomer with additional field induced attributes. At lower excitation amplitudes, the frequency shift is relatively larger compared to the frequency shift registered at higher excitation amplitudes. The application of a magnetic field alters the mechanical properties of the MRE element, and which in turn alters the amplitude dependent variation in the characteristics of the isolator. In the passive state (without magnetic field), the stiffness of the elastomer matrix is relatively low, resulting in a lower natural frequency of the isolator. When a magnetic field is applied, the embedded ferromagnetic particles interact and form chain-like structures along the magnetic field direction. This interaction increases the effective stiffness of the MRE element and shifts the natural frequency of the isolator to a higher value. On the contrary, increased excitation amplitude modifies the interaction between the fillers, which eventually contributes to the variation in the frequency shift (from active to passive state) as a function of excitation amplitude. Due to this stiffness variation, the resonance frequency of the isolator changes between passive and active states. If the excitation frequency is close to the passive state natural frequency, large vibration amplitudes may occur due to resonance. By activating the magnetic field, the stiffness increases and the resonance frequency shifts away from the excitation frequency, thereby reducing the vibration amplitude. However, maintaining the isolator continuously in the active state may also lead to resonance when the excitation frequency approaches the active state natural frequency. Therefore, it is necessary for the controller to switch between passive and active states depending on the excitation frequency. A typical displacement transmissibility plot illustrating this behavior is presented in Fig 6 . According to this plot, the MRE isolator must be under active state to overcome the resonance of the isolator system under passive state. Continually maintaining the active state is not beneficial as it leads to the risk of encountering the resonance of the active state. Thus, a frequency needs to be defined that marks the transition from the active state to the passive state. The “Controller Stopping Frequency” is a critical strategic transition parameter that allows the isolator to cease active control interventions, enabling it to naturally dissipate energy and behave as a passive isolator. The identification of this frequency is pivotal in understanding how the controller dynamically responds to varying operating conditions, ensuring optimal performance in structural vibration mitigation. The importance of the Controller Stopping Frequency becomes particularly evident in the development and testing of MRE isolator controllers. By pinpointing the frequencies at which the controller effectively halts the active phase, designers can tailor the controller settings to strategically engage and disengage based on the system’s dynamic response. This adaptability is crucial in real-world scenarios where structural vibrations may vary in frequency and amplitude. 5.1. Control strategy implications from observed behavior The experimental results reveal important control requirements for effective vibration isolation using MRE-based semi-active isolators. As the excitation amplitude increases, the system’s stiffness and natural frequency tend to decrease, which alters the dynamic response. This necessitates a control strategy that can adapt in real time to varying amplitude conditions. Additionally, the isolator exhibits strong nonlinear and hysteretic behavior, particularly near resonance, requiring the controller to be robust against modeling uncertainties. Furthermore, at higher excitation frequencies beyond the Controller Stopping Frequency the system naturally offers sufficient passive isolation. In such cases, continued actuation becomes unnecessary and energy-inefficient. Therefore, an ideal controller should adapt to amplitude changes, compensate for nonlinearities, and intelligently switch off or reduce control effort when active intervention is no longer beneficial. The proposed RBSM controller fulfills these criteria through its hybrid architecture, combining the robustness of sliding mode control with the adaptability of the Radial Basis Function Neural Network. 5.2. Control framework To address the highly nonlinear and amplitude-dependent behavior of the MRE isolator, the proposed control framework adopts a hybrid architecture combining Sliding Mode Control (SMC) and a Radial Basis Function Neural Network (RBFNN). SMC provides robustness to model uncertainties and external disturbances, making it suitable for nonlinear systems. However, its discontinuous control action may induce chattering and struggles to accommodate unmodeled dynamics. To mitigate these issues, an RBFNN is integrated to learn and compensate for unknown nonlinearities in real time. This combination retains the strengths of both methods, achieving high adaptability and reliable control across varying operating conditions. Within the proposed hybrid framework, the Bouc–Wen model provides the physics-based representation of the MRE hysteresis, whereas the RBF Neural Network functions as an adaptive compensator that estimates the residual nonlinear dynamics arising from hysteretic modeling errors and parameter uncertainties during online operation. 5.2.1. Mathematical modeling of MRE isolator. The Sliding Mode Controller (SMC), in particular, is a model-based control technique that relies on the system’s differential equations to define the control logic. It requires this mathematical model to accurately compute the equivalent control force and ensure convergence of the system states to the desired trajectory, as established through Lyapunov stability criteria. Without this model, the controller cannot determine the corrective action needed to drive the system to the sliding surface and maintain the desired motion. Hence, a precise model is fundamental to the successful implementation of SMC in handling the nonlinear, amplitude- and frequency-dependent behavior of MRE isolators. To develop an effective and robust control strategy, it is crucial to begin with an accurate mathematical representation of the Magnetorheological Elastomer (MRE) isolator. MRE-based systems exhibit complex nonlinear dynamics arising from viscoelastic damping and hysteresis. These characteristics are influenced by magnetic field strength, excitation amplitude, and frequency, making model fidelity essential for control design. The nonlinear hysteretic restoring force z ( t ) in the MRE isolator is modeled using the Bouc–Wen hysteresis formulation, which has been widely adopted for representing nonlinear hysteretic behavior in structural and smart materials systems [ 45 – 47 ]. The Bouc-Wen model introduces an internal hysteretic variable whose evolution governs the shape and amplitude of the hysteresis loop. The model parameters adopted in the present study were selected using the experimentally identified dynamic characteristics of the fabricated MRE isolator. In particular, the equivalent stiffness and damping coefficients were determined from the measured frequency-response characteristics, while the observed current-dependent natural-frequency variation was used to validate the parameter selection of the Bouc–Wen model. Therefore, the mathematical model employed for controller design represents the experimentally measured behavior of the MRE isolator. The evolution equation of the hysteretic variable z ( t ) is given by z ˙ ( t ) = A x ˙ ( t ) − β | x ˙ ( t ) | | z ( t ) | n − 1 z ( t ) − γ x ˙ ( t ) | z ( t ) | n (6) where x ( t ) and x ˙ ( t ) denote the displacement and velocity of the isolated mass, respectively. In this formulation, the parameter A controls the linear contribution to the hysteretic evolution, while β and γ govern the nonlinear hysteretic characteristics and influence the shape and smoothness of the hysteresis loop. The exponent n is a positive constant that determines the sharpness of the transition between the elastic and hysteretic regions of the response. The hysteretic variable z ( t ) represents the nonlinear memory-dependent restoring force of the MRE material and captures t he evolution of the hysteresis loop under varying magnetic field strength and excitation conditions. Consequently, z ( t ) constitutes one of the dominant nonlinear components governing the dynamic response of t he MRE isolator. Although the Bouc–Wen model provides an effective mathematical representation of the hysteretic behavior, discrepancies between the model and the actual MRE response may arise due to parameter variations, material nonlinearities, and operating conditions uncertainties. These uncertainties motivate the incorporation of an adaptive learning mechanism within the proposed control framework. The Bouc-Wen model is particularly suitable for magnetorheological materials because it can effectively capture the nonlinear and rate-dependent hysteresis observed in experimental responses of MRE-based vibration isolation systems under varying excitation amplitudes and frequencies. Several studies have successfully employed Bouc–Wen based models to describe the dynamic behavior of magnetorheological devices and smart vibration control systems [ 13 , 47 ]. The experimental characterization presented in the previous section provides the dynamic parameters required for the controller development and numerical simulations. Specifically, the experimentally measured natural frequencies, equivalent stiffness, damping ratios, and current-dependent frequency-shift characteristics were used to calibrate the dynamic model of the MRE isolator. These experimentally identified properties were incorporated into the Bouc–Wen-based dynamic model to ensure that the simulated system accurately reproduces the measured vibration behavior under different excitation amplitudes and applied current levels. Consequently, the controller simulations are based on experimentally validated system parameters rather than purely theoretical assumptions, thereby establishing a direct link between the experimental characterization and the subsequent control performance evaluation. 5.2.2 Sliding mode controller design. In general, there are differences between the response of the mathematical model and the actual response of the MRE isolator. These inconsistencies are caused by a variety of factors, including variations in MRE property due to magnetisation and excitation amplitude. To overcome these inconsistencies and establish a successful controller strategy, a collection of strong controller designs is offered specifically for the MRE isolator. The Sliding Mode Controller methodology is one of the robust controllers for the MRE isolator. The Sliding Mode Controller is distinguished by its ability to perform efficiently for nonlinear systems while remaining robust in the face of parametric uncertainties and external disruptions. Lyapunov stability analysis. The squared Lyapunov function was selected in this study due to its mathematical simplicity and its effectiveness in analyzing the stability of nonlinear dynamic systems. In sliding mode control and neural network–based adaptive control systems, quadratic Lyapunov candidate functions are commonly used because they ensure positive definiteness and facilitate straightforward derivation of stability conditions. A typical Lyapunov candidate function is defined as [ 49 , 50 ] V = 1 2 s 2 (12) where s represents the sliding surface. This quadratic form guarantees that V > 0 for all s ≠ 0 and V = 0 only when the system state reaches the sliding surface. The derivative of the Lyapunov function can be easily evaluated, which simplifies the stability analysis and enables the derivation of sufficient conditions for asymptotic stability. Furthermore, the squared Lyapunov function provides a convenient framework for integrating neural network adaptation laws and sliding mode control strategies. This choice allows the stability of the closed-loop system to be rigorously proven while maintaining a computationally efficient control design. Furthermore, the adaptive RBFNN continuously estimates the unknown nonlinear dynamics, reducing the magnitude of the uncertainty that must be compensated by the discontinuous switching term. The control law consists of an equivalent control component and a discontinuous switching component. The equivalent control compensates for the nominal system dynamics, whereas the switching term provides robustness against modeling uncertainties and external disturbances. The switching gain η is selected to satisfy the reaching condition while avoiding excessive control activity. Consequently, η should be sufficiently large to overcome bounded disturbances but not excessively high, since unnecessarily large switching gains increase control chattering. This formulation ensures that the sliding surface is attractive and the tracking error converges to zero, thereby achieving robust control under uncertainty. Although the equivalent control law is derived from the mathematical model, its practical implementation relies only on measurable system states and the RBFNN-estimated nonlinear dynamics. Therefore, higher-order state derivatives are not required during online controller execution. In practical implementation, the control input of the MRE isolator is not the control force directly but the electric current supplied to the electromagnetic coil. The applied current generates a magnetic field, which modifies the mechanical properties of the magnetorheological elastomer and consequently alters the controllable force produced by the isolator. The magnetic field intensity inside the MRE element is proportional to the input current applied to the coil and can be expressed as H = N I l m (17) where H is the magnetic field intensity, N is the number of coil turns, I is the applied electric current, and l m is the effective magnetic path length. The magnetic flux density inside the MRE element can then be written as B = μ H (18) where μ is the effective magnetic permeability of the MRE composite. The controllable force generated by the MRE isolator depends on the field-dependent stiffness of the elastomer. Therefore, the control force can be expressed as a function of the applied magnetic field and displacement F c = k ( B ) x (19) where k ( B ) represents the magnetic-field-dependent stiffness of the MRE material. Since the magnetic flux density B is directly related to the applied current I , the control force can be represented as F c = k ( I ) x (20) Thus, the control signal generated by the proposed controller is converted into the required coil current to produce the desired controllable force in the MRE isolator. This relationship allows the semi-active controller to regulate the mechanical response of the isolator through the modulation of the electromagnetic current. 5.2.4. Integration of RBFNN with Sliding Mode Control. In conventional Sliding Mode Control, high switching gains are generally required to compensate for unknown nonlinearities, resulting in undesirable chattering of the control signal. In the proposed controller, the RBF Neural Network adaptively estimates the unknown nonlinear dynamics online. Consequently, the switching component is required to compensate only for the residual approximation error rather than the complete system uncertainty. This substantially reduces the discontinuous switching action and suppresses control chattering while maintaining the robustness characteristics of Sliding Mode Control. Although the Sliding Mode Controller (SMC) provides robust performance against system uncertainties and external disturbances, it suffers from a key limitation known as chattering , which arises due to the discontinuous nature of the control law. Additionally, the effectiveness of the controller depends on precise knowledge of the nonlinear function f ( t ), which encompasses the complex stiffness, damping, and hysteretic forces in the MRE system. However, in real-time applications, such nonlinear dynamics are often unknown or only partially known. To improve robustness against modeling uncertainties and external disturbances, a Radial Basis Function Neural Network (RBFNN) is integrated with the sliding mode controller. The RBF network is used to approximate unknown nonlinear dynamics, while the sliding mode control law guarantees system robustness and stability. Such hybrid intelligent control strategies combining RBF neural networks and sliding mode control have been widely adopted for nonlinear systems due to their strong approximation capability and robustness [ 51 – 53 ]. An RBFNN is a three-layer feedforward network consisting of: An input layer , which receives the state vector (typically the tracking error and its derivative), A hidden layer , which applies nonlinear transformation through Gaussian radial basis functions, An output layer , which produces an estimate of the unknown nonlinear function. The approximation model of the nonlinear function f ( t ) using RBFNN can be expressed as: f ( t ) = W T h ( x ) + ε (21) where: x = [ e ( t ) , e ˙ ( t ) ] T is the input vector consisting of tracking error and its rate, h ( x ) is the vector of radial basis functions, W is the ideal weight vector of the network, ε is the bounded approximation error. The proposed RBF Neural Network adopts a three-layer feedforward architecture consisting of an input layer, a hidden radial basis layer, and a linear output layer. The network input vector is defined as x = [ e ( t ) , e ˙ ( t ) ] T , where e ( t ) and e ˙ ( t ) denote the tracking error and its first derivative, respectively. These quantities are directly obtained from the measured displacement response and the desired reference trajectory. The hidden layer consists of Gaussian radial basis neurons, whose outputs are expressed as h i ( x ) = exp ( − ‖ x − c i ‖ 2 2 σ i 2 ) , where c i and σ i represent the center and width of the i t h radial basis function. The network output is computed as the weighted sum of the hidden-layer responses to approximate the unknown nonlinear dynamics. The neural network’s estimate of f ( t ) is given by: f ^ ( t ) = W ^ T h ( x ) (22) where W ^ is the estimated weight vector. The input vector of the RBF Neural Network consists only of the tracking error and its first derivative, x = [ e ( t ) , e ˙ ( t ) ] T , which are directly obtained from the measured displacement response of the MRE isolator and the desired reference trajectory. Consequently, the proposed controller does not require higher-order state derivatives or direct measurement of the hysteretic internal variable. Instead, the RBFNN utilizes these measurable signals to estimate the unknown nonlinear dynamics online, thereby providing a practical implementation while avoiding the noise amplification commonly associated with numerical differentiation of higher-order derivatives. By replacing the unknown f ( t ) in the sliding mode control law with its neural network estimate f ^ ( t ) , the modified control force becomes: F ( t ) = m ( x ¨ d + η s g n ( s ) + c 1 e ˙ ) − f ^ ( t ) (23) The estimation error is defined as: f ~ ( t ) = f ( t ) − f ^ ( t ) = W ~ T h ( x ) + ε (24) where W ~ = W − W ^ is the weight estimation error. It should be emphasized that the proposed RBF Neural Network does not replace the Bouc-Wen hysteresis model employed in the dynamic formulation of the MRE isolator. Instead, the Bouc-Wen model continues to represent the physical hysteretic restoring force through the internal variable z ( t ), while the RBFNN adaptively estima t es the unknown nonlinear dynamics associated with the modeling uncertainties. Since the hysteretic force represented by z ( t ) is embedded within the nonlinear function f ( t ), any mismatch between the ma t hematical model and the actual hysteretic response is compensated online by the RBFNN. Consequently, the Sliding Mode Controller utilizes the improved estimate f ^ ( t ) to generate a more accurate control action, thereby reducing control chattering and improving robustness against parameter variations, nonlinear hysteresis, and external disturbances. To ensure stability and convergence, a composite Lyapunov function is constructed as: V = 1 2 s 2 + 1 2 λ W ~ T W ~ (25) where λ > 0 is the neural network adaptation gain. To ensure that V ˙ ≤ 0 , the following adaptive update law for the weight vector is used: W ^ ˙ = − 1 m λ s h ( x ) (26) This adaptation law enables the neural network to learn the system’s nonlinear behavior online and improve control accuracy while suppressing chattering. The resulting hybrid controller, termed the Radial Basis Sliding Mode (RBSM) controller, combines the robustness of SMC with the adaptability of RBFNN to handle the nonlinear, amplitude-dependent response of the MRE isolator. The proposed RBFNN operates as an adaptive nonlinear approximator within the RBSM control framework. The initial RBFNN weights are estimated offline using the Levenberg–Marquardt (LM) optimization algorithm based on the available training data. These LM-estimated weights provide the initial network parameters for the RBSM controller. During online controller operation, the network parameters are further adapted according to the Lyapunov-based adaptive law presented in Eq. (26) . Therefore, the LM algorithm provides the initial weight estimation, whereas the Lyapunov-based adaptation law enables real-time adjustment of the RBFNN parameters in response to variations in the system dynamics and excitation conditions. The RBFNN parameters, including the radial basis centers and widths, were selected to provide sufficient approximation capability over the expected operating range of the MRE isolator. The RBFNN employs a 2–10–1 architecture consisting of two input neurons corresponding to the tracking error \(e(t)\) and its derivative \(\dot e(t)\), ten Gaussian radial basis neurons in the hidden layer, and one output neuron representing the estimated nonlinear dynamics \(\hat f(t)\). The number of hidden neurons was selected through preliminary tuning to achieve a suitable compromise between approximation accuracy and computational complexity. During controller operation, the adaptive weight update law continuously minimizes the approximation error of the unknown nonlinear dynamics, enabling real-time compensation of modeling uncertainties. 5.3. Conclusions This study presented a hybrid adaptive control strategy for vibration suppression in a magnetorheological elastomer (MRE)-based vibration isolation system. The proposed controller integrates sliding mode control with a radial basis function (RBF) neural network to address the nonlinear and frequency-dependent characteristics of the MRE isolator. The neural network component estimates unknown nonlinear dynamics in real time, while the sliding mode control framework ensures stable system behavior and effective disturbance rejection. From a scientific perspective, the proposed framework demonstrates how a physics-based hysteresis model and a data-driven adaptive learning algorithm can be effectively integrated within a robust nonlinear control architecture. Rather than replacing the underlying Bouc–Wen hysteresis model, the RBFNN compensates for the residual nonlinear dynamics and modeling uncertainties, thereby improving controller adaptability while preserving the physical interpretability of the system model. This hybrid strategy provides a practical methodology for controlling nonlinear smart-material-based vibration isolation systems operating under varying excitation conditions. The effectiveness of the proposed RBSM controller was evaluated through simulation studies under varying excitation amplitudes and frequencies. The results demonstrate that the proposed controller significantly improves vibration isolation performance across the investigated frequency range of 15–80 Hz. In particular, the controller effectively suppresses vibration near the resonance region where vibration amplification is typically most severe. The proposed RBFNN–SMC controller achieved a maximum vibration reduction of 55.8% at the resonance frequency under the investigated operating conditions. In addition, the adaptive capability of the RBF neural network enables the controller to automatically adjust its control action in response to changes in excitation frequency and amplitude, resulting in improved vibration attenuation over the investigated operating range. Furthermore, the controller maintains stable performance under varying operating conditions, demonstrating its capability to handle the nonlinear behavior of the MRE-based isolation system. The results highlight the potential of integrating intelligent learning techniques with robust control methods for advanced adaptive vibration control applications. Overall, the proposed hybrid RBSM control framework provides an effective and practical solution for adaptive vibration suppression in MRE-based vibration isolation systems. Future work will focus on the experimental implementation of the proposed controller and its evaluation under more complex excitation environments, including multi-frequency, broadband, and random vibration inputs, to further assess its robustness and applicability to real-world engineering systems.
