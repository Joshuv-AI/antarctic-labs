Radius-Flow Entanglement in Hadron States and Gravitational Form Factors
Kiminad A. Mamo

Abstract
We propose a lattice-ready entanglement observable for QCD hadrons: the vacuum-subtracted radius flow of the ball Rényi entropy, 𝔰 n ​ ( R , h ) ≡ R ​ ∂ R Δ ​ S n ​ ( B R , h ) \mathfrak{s}_{n}(R;h)\equiv R\,\partial_{R}\Delta S_{n}(B_{R};h) , defined via the Euclidean replica cut-and-glue construction in a rest-frame momentum-projected one-hadron state, with spin averaging performed at the level of the final flow. In the continuum, varying R R at fixed shape is equivalent to a Weyl rescaling, so the flow is trace selected and admits a surface-plus-remainder organization on the entangling sphere. We use this to formulate a lattice stability test of boundary dominance: fit the measured flow on local R R windows to a low-curvature remainder plus a small template basis built from hadronic gravitational form factors (GFFs). A rest-frame momentum eigenstate on a periodic lattice is spatially delocalized, so one does not take a controlled

The continuum organizing principle is that a size change at fixed shape can be represented (after an appropriate diffeomorphism that holds the cut locus fixed in coordinates) as a Weyl rescaling of the background metric. A Weyl response couples to the trace
T\equiv T^{\mu}{}_{\mu}
, so the radius flow is trace selected. At leading order it admits a modular-correlator (source-detector) representation of the schematic form
\sim\!\int\!\sqrt{g}\,\langle T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}(x)\,K(R)\rangle

IV

is the modular Hamiltonian. This makes clear that the flow is an integrated response functional rather than a probability density, and that the Breit-frame profiles introduced below are used only as response templates for the
R

Miller 2025

R

A quantitative identification of fitted coefficients with continuum-normalized EMT matrix elements requires an explicit lattice EMT/trace renormalization and matching procedure; here we deliberately formulate the comparison at the level of
R

Assuming such a window exists, the main physics question is which element of the spin-0/spin-2 GFF basis controls the leading boundary term. Trace selection constrains the operator channel in the continuum response, but the leading boundary coefficient extracted after renormalization and surface isolation is dynamical.
In particular, the soft-wall holographic QCD computation in App.
L
shows that even for a trace–energy correlator the surviving nonlocal piece closes on the two-function basis
\{A^{S}(t),A(t)\}

\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)}(R)=R^{3}\rho_{S}(R)
built from
A^{S}(t)

A(t)

\{A^{S},A\}
rather than
\{A^{S}\}
alone.

We therefore treat the lattice comparison as a small hypothesis space with two pure endpoints,

\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)}(R)=R^{3}\rho_{S}(R),\qquad\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}(R)=R^{3}\rho_{A}(R),

where the factor

\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{\rm mix}(R;c_{0},c_{2})=c_{0}\,\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)}(R)+c_{2}\,\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}(R),

(5)

equivalently associated in momentum space with

G_{\rm mix}(t;c_{0},c_{2})=c_{0}A^{S}(t)+c_{2}A(t).

(6)

A minimal first measurement is

IV.1
Metric response and the Weyl-flow identity

Following Refs.
Rosenhaus and Smolkin 2014b
;
Rosenhaus and Smolkin 2014a
, define the entanglement response density

\mathcal{T}^{\rm EE}_{\mu\nu}(x\,|\,B_{R};\rho)\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{g(x)}}\,\frac{\delta S_{\rm EE}(B_{R};\rho)}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}(x)},

(18)

so that

\delta S_{\rm EE}(B_{R};\rho)=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\sqrt{g(x)}\;\mathcal{T}^{\rm EE}_{\mu\nu}(x\,|\,B_{R};\rho)\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}(x).

(19)

Define the trace projection

\mathcal{T}^{\rm EE}(x\,|\,B_{R};\rho)\equiv-2\,g^{\mu\nu}(x)\mathcal{T}^{\rm EE}_{\mu\nu}(x\,|\,B_{R};\rho).

(20)

A boundary movement
B_{R}\to B_{R+\delta R}
can be undone by a diffeomorphism that maps the shifted ball back to the original coordinate locus, at the price of pulling back the metric. The resulting change in the pulled-back metric is a uniform Weyl rescaling at fixed cut locus. For an infinitesimal radius change at fixed shape,
R\to R+\delta R
,

\delta g_{\mu\nu}(x)=2\frac{\delta R}{R}\,g_{\mu\nu}(x),\qquad\delta g^{\mu\nu}(x)=-2\frac{\delta R}{R}\,g^{\mu\nu}(x).

(21)

Inserting this into
Eq.

19
yields

R\frac{\mathrm{d}S_{\rm EE}(B_{R};\rho)}{\mathrm{d}R}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\sqrt{g(x)}\;\mathcal{T}^{\rm EE}(x\,|\,B_{R};\rho).

(22)

Because
\delta g^{\mu\nu}\propto g^{\mu\nu}
, a size flow isolates the trace channel.

At integer
n\geq 2
the replica definition gives
S_{n}=(1-n)^{-1}(\ln Z_{n}-n\ln Z_{1})
. A radius change at fixed shape is again organized by a Weyl response on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. In particular,

\displaystyle R\frac{\mathrm{d}S_{n}(B_{R};\rho)}{\mathrm{d}R}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\Bigg[\Big\langle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\sqrt{g}\;T(x)\Big\rangle_{\!\mathcal{M}_{n},\rho}

\displaystyle\qquad-n\,\Big\langle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\sqrt{g}\;T(x)\Big\rangle_{\!\mathcal{M}_{1},\rho}\Bigg]+\mathcal{R}^{(n)}_{\rm flow,ct},

(23)

where
T(x)\equiv T^{\mu}{}_{\mu}(x)
and
\mathcal{R}^{(n)}_{\rm flow,ct}
denotes the UV-local and
\Sigma
-local counterterm completion fixed by the chosen replica/cut prescription and trace-renormalization scheme; see App.
J
. For a fixed prescription,
\mathcal{R}^{(n)}_{\rm flow,ct}
is state independent and therefore cancels in the vacuum subtraction defining
\Delta S_{n}
and
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
.

IV.2
Source-detector correlator

At leading order in the metric perturbation, the Rosenhaus-Smolkin identity gives
Rosenhaus and Smolkin 2014b
;
Rosenhaus and Smolkin 2014a

\displaystyle\delta S_{\rm EE}(B_{R};\rho)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\sqrt{g(x)}\;\Big\langle T_{\mu\nu}(x)\,K_{\rho}(R)\Big\rangle_{\!\rho}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}(x)

(24)

\displaystyle+\delta S_{\rm EE}^{\rm(ho)}+\delta S_{\rm EE}^{\rm(ct)}.

Taking the trace and using
Eq.

22
yields

\displaystyle R\frac{\mathrm{d}S_{\rm EE}(B_{R};\rho)}{\mathrm{d}R}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\sqrt{g(x)}\;\Big\langle T(x)\,K_{\rho}(R)\Big\rangle_{\rho}

(25)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{R}_{\rm flow}^{\rm(ho)}+\mathcal{R}_{\rm flow}^{\rm(ct)},\qquad T(x)\equiv T^{\mu}{}_{\mu}(x).

The modular Hamiltonian acts as a region-supported source tied to the cut, while the local trace insertion acts as a detector scanning the spacetime response.

IV.3
Vacuum-subtracted radius flow

Apply
Eq.

25
to
\rho=\rho_{h,\lambda}
and to
\rho=\rho_{0}
and subtract. This gives

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{\rm EE}(R;h,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\sqrt{g(x)}\;\Big[\left\langle T(x)\,K_{h}(R)\right\rangle_{\rho_{h,\lambda}}-\left\langle T(x)\,K_{\rm vac}(R)\right\rangle_{\rho_{0}}\Big]+\Delta\mathcal{R}_{\rm flow}^{\rm(ho)}+\Delta\mathcal{R}_{\rm flow}^{\rm(ct)},

(26)

with

K_{h}(R)\equiv-\ln\rho_{B_{R},h,\lambda},\qquad K_{\rm vac}(R)\equiv-\ln\rho_{B_{R},0}.

(27)

The exact trace-selected logic is crucial, but it does
not
by itself determine which Breit-frame GFF profile should best approximate the leading boundary coefficient after renormalization, surface isolation, and state preparation are all fixed. That bridge remains dynamical and is therefore tested empirically below. The pure scalar template is the minimal baseline because of trace selection; the spin-2 template is a competing endpoint because the holographic integrated stress-tensor response summarized in App.
L
is not controlled by
A^{S}(t)
alone; and the mixed family closes the natural two-function basis motivated by that appendix.

V
UV reference ball kernel, surface localization, and the boundary-flow bridge

For a CFT vacuum reduced to a ball, the modular Hamiltonian is local
Casini et al. 2011
:

\displaystyle K_{\rm CFT}(R)

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{|\bm{y}|<R}\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{y}\;F_{R}(\bm{y})\;T_{00}(0,\bm{y})+c_{\rm CFT}(R),

(28)

\displaystyle F_{R}(\bm{y})

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{R^{2}-|\bm{y}|^{2}}{2R}.

In QCD this locality is not expected to hold in the IR. The role of
K_{\rm CFT}(R)
here is to organize the near-
\Sigma
singular structure controlling UV-local and
\Sigma
-local counterterms.

Starting from
Eq.

26
, add and subtract
K_{\rm CFT}(R)
inside the modular correlators:

\displaystyle\left\langle T(x)\,K_{h}(R)\right\rangle_{\rho_{h,\lambda}}-\left\langle T(x)\,K_{\rm vac}(R)\right\rangle_{\rho_{0}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle T(x)\,K_{\rm CFT}(R)\right\rangle_{\rho_{h,\lambda}}-\left\langle T(x)\,K_{\rm CFT}(R)\right\rangle_{\rho_{0}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle T(x)\,\big(K_{h}(R)-K_{\rm CFT}(R)\big)\right\rangle_{\rho_{h,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\quad-\left\langle T(x)\,\big(K_{\rm vac}(R)-K_{\rm CFT}(R)\big)\right\rangle_{\rho_{0}}.

(29)

At leading order,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{\rm EE}(R;h,\lambda)

\displaystyle=2\pi\!\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\sqrt{g(x)}\int_{|\bm{y}|<R}\mathrm{d}^{d-1}\bm{y}\;F_{R}(\bm{y})

(30)

\displaystyle\times\Big[\left\langle T(x)\,T_{00}(0,\bm{y})\right\rangle_{\rho_{h,\lambda}}-\left\langle T(x)\,T_{00}(0,\bm{y})\right\rangle_{\rho_{0}}\Big]

\displaystyle+\Delta\mathcal{R}_{\rm ct}(R)+\Delta\mathcal{R}_{\rm IR}(R)+\Delta\mathcal{R}_{\rm fin}(R).

Define the
\tau
-integrated trace response density

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\rm EE}(\bm{x}\,|\,B_{R};\rho)\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\;\mathcal{T}^{\rm EE}(\tau,\bm{x}\,|\,B_{R};\rho),

(31)

so that

R\frac{\mathrm{d}S_{\rm EE}(B_{R};\rho)}{\mathrm{d}R}=\int\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{x}\;\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\rm EE}(\bm{x}\,|\,B_{R};\rho).

(32)

Vacuum subtract and renormalize in the trace channel. A minimal surface organization is

\displaystyle\Delta\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\rm EE,ren}(\bm{x}\,|\,B_{R})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}(R,\Omega;h,\lambda)\,\delta(r-R)

(33)

\displaystyle+\Delta\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\rm EE,ren,reg}(\bm{x}\,|\,B_{R}),

where the second term collects higher-derivative surface distributions together with bulk-regular terms. With this definition,

\mathfrak{s}_{\rm EE}(R;h,\lambda)=c_{\Sigma}(R;h,\lambda)+\mathfrak{s}_{\rm EE}^{\rm reg}(R;h,\lambda),

(34)

with

c_{\Sigma}(R;h,\lambda)\equiv R^{2}\!\int\!\mathrm{d}\Omega\;\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}(R,\Omega;h,\lambda),

(35)

and

\mathfrak{s}_{\rm EE}^{\rm reg}(R;h,\lambda)\equiv\int\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{x}\;\Delta\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\rm EE,ren,reg}(\bm{x}\,|\,B_{R}).

(36)

At integer
n\geq 2
the analogous decomposition reads

\mathfrak{s}_{n}(R;h,\lambda)=c_{\Sigma,n}(R;h,\lambda)+\mathfrak{s}_{n}^{\rm reg}(R;h,\lambda).

(37)

For practical lattice studies one works in the rest frame and averages over ball centers and lattice rotations, so the angle-integrated monopole piece is the natural quantity to compare to isotropic Breit templates. We say that a radius interval
R\in[R_{1},R_{2}]
is
boundary dominated
if the dominant nontrivial
R
dependence of the measured flow is controlled by
c_{\Sigma}(R;h)
(or
c_{\Sigma,n}
), while the packaged remainder is subleading and/or slowly varying.

Status of assumptions and derivations.

To make explicit what is definition-level versus hypothesis-level:

•

Exact on the lattice (definition-level):
for a fixed replica/cut prescription,
\Delta S_{n}(B_{R};h)
and
\mathfrak{s}_{n}(R;h)
are well-defined lattice observables computed from replica ratios.

•

Continuum organizing principle (controlled):
a radius change at fixed shape is a Weyl rescaling, so the flow is trace selected; after trace-channel renormalization, UV structure can be organized into
\Sigma
-supported distributions plus a regular remainder.

•

Empirical hypothesis (to be tested):
on some accessible window
a\ll R\ll L/2
, the leading nontrivial
R
dependence is dominated by the
k=0
surface term and is well described by the small template basis generated by
\{A^{S},A\}
(with a low-curvature remainder accounting for smooth backgrounds and residual contact structure).

To make boundary dominance testable, we advocate the basis fit

\mathfrak{s}_{n}^{\rm lat}(R)=a_{0}^{(n)}\,\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)}(R)+a_{2}^{(n)}\,\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}(R)+r_{M}(R),

(38)

with

r_{M}(R)=\sum_{m=0}^{M}b_{m}R^{m},\qquad M=0,1,2.

(39)

Here
(c_{0},c_{2})
label the abstract mixed template family introduced in Sec.
VI
, whereas
(a_{0}^{(n)},a_{2}^{(n)})
are the coefficients extracted from lattice data on a chosen window. Pure spin-0 and pure spin-2 fits are the nested limits
a_{2}^{(n)}=0
and
a_{0}^{(n)}=0
. If desired, one may repackage the same fit as an overall normalization times the mixed template
\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{\rm mix}(R;c_{0},c_{2})
, with
(c_{0},c_{2})
defined only up to an overall rescaling. The holographic fixed-ratio benchmark corresponds to the further restriction
a_{0}^{(n)}:a_{2}^{(n)}=c_{0}^{\rm holo}:c_{2}^{\rm holo}
.

Boundary dominance is not tied to the polynomial choice in
Eq.

39
; one may equally use an expansion in
(R-R_{\star})
about the window midpoint, a constrained spline with a fixed smoothness prior, or a Gaussian-process smooth prior. What matters is that the extracted coefficients and turning point remain stable under such changes. To quantify the intended “low-curvature remainder” condition, define on a chosen window

c_{\rm temp}(R)\equiv a_{0}^{(n)}\,\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)}(R)+a_{2}^{(n)}\,\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}(R),

(40)

together with the dimensionless curvature measures

\displaystyle\kappa_{\rm reg}

\displaystyle\equiv\max_{R\in[R_{1},R_{2}]}\left|R^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}r_{M}}{\mathrm{d}R^{2}}\right|,

(41)

\displaystyle\kappa_{\rm temp}

\displaystyle\equiv\max_{R\in[R_{1},R_{2}]}\left|R^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}c_{\rm temp}}{\mathrm{d}R^{2}}\right|.

(42)

A boundary-dominated window is one in which the coefficients remain stable under moderate changes of
M
,
R_{1,2}
, and the remainder basis, and in which
\kappa_{\rm reg}\ll\kappa_{\rm temp}
. In that sense the remainder cannot fake a template turning point without developing comparable curvature and destabilizing the fit.

VI
Gravitational form factors, endpoint templates, and the mixed holographic basis

We use a minimal two-channel notation throughout this section:
A^{S}(t)
denotes the scalar/trace GFF,
A(t)
the spin-2/TT GFF, and the superscripts
0
and
2
on
\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)}(R)
and
\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}(R)
refer to the corresponding scalar and spin-2 channels. The real-space templates are simply the associated Breit-frame densities, evaluated at the entangling radius
R
and multiplied by
R^{3}
.

This section defines the Breit-frame GFF template family that will be compared to the boundary observable extracted from the entanglement flow. The scalar/trace template remains the minimal baseline because the continuum flow is trace selected, but the spin-2/TT template is also a bona fide competing endpoint, and the natural closure is the mixed two-function basis generated by
A^{S}(t)
and
A(t)
. Appendix
L
motivates this basis: within soft-wall holographic QCD, the pole-subtracted integrated trace-energy correlator closes on these two GFFs and on no additional low-spin structures in that model.

VI.1
Spin-0 (trace-channel) endpoint

For each partonic sector
i\in\{q,g\}
, define the scalar (trace-channel) GFF
A_{i}^{S}(t)
by the spin-averaged matrix element of the traced symmetric EMT:

\frac{1}{2S+1}\sum_{\lambda=-S}^{S}\langle p^{\prime},\lambda|\,\widehat{T}_{i\,\mu}^{\ \ \mu}(0)\,|p,\lambda\rangle\equiv 2M_{h}^{2}\,A_{i}^{S}(t),

(43)

with
t\equiv(p^{\prime}-p)^{2}
. The entanglement-side boundary observable extracted from lattice
\mathfrak{s}_{n}(R;h)
is tied to the fixed replica prescription and to the chosen renormalized definition of the trace operator
T^{\mu}{}_{\mu}
on the lattice. A quantitative comparison to the continuum GFF inputs therefore requires an EMT/trace renormalization and matching procedure (for example a flowed EMT matched to
\overline{\rm MS}
at a reference scale). In the present paper the comparison is intentionally formulated at the shape level: an overall normalization mismatch is absorbed into the fitted template coefficients, while any residual
\Sigma
-localized contact-term structure is treated as part of the low-curvature remainder sector.

The separate quark/gluon trace operators mix under renormalization; throughout we take the sector-decomposed
A_{i}^{S}(t)
in the same renormalization scheme and scale as the numerical inputs quoted below. In Breit kinematics
\Delta^{0}=0
one has
t=-\bm{\Delta}^{2}
.

Define the Breit-frame three-dimensional density

\rho_{S,i}(r)\equiv\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{\Delta}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\;\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{\Delta}\cdot\bm{r}}\;A^{S}_{i}(-\bm{\Delta}^{2}),\qquad r\equiv|\bm{r}|,

(44)

and sum over
i=q,g
to obtain
\rho_{S}(r)
. The corresponding dimensionless surface-evaluated template is

\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)}(R)\equiv R^{3}\rho_{S}(R).

(45)

This is the most direct Breit-frame benchmark for a trace-selected boundary observable.

VI.2
TT/spin-2 endpoint from the
A
form factor

For a spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}
target of mass
M_{h}
, the symmetric EMT matrix element is commonly decomposed in terms of four invariant form factors
A_{i}(t)
,
B_{i}(t)
,
D_{i}(t)
, and
\overline{C}_{i}(t)
, as in App.
E
. The scalar/trace channel isolates a specific combination of these form factors and leads to
A_{i}^{S}(t)
. However, the TT sector is naturally tied to the tensor part of the stress-tensor correlator, and the simplest Breit-frame TT-inspired endpoint is built from
A_{i}(t)
alone:

A_{i}(t)=\alpha_{A,i}\left(1-\frac{t}{\Lambda_{A,i}^{2}}\right)^{-2},\qquad i\in\{q,g\},

(46)

with the numerical parameters quoted below.

Define

\rho_{A,i}(r)\equiv\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{\Delta}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\;\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{\Delta}\cdot\bm{r}}\;A_{i}(-\bm{\Delta}^{2}),

(47)

and
\rho_{A}(r)=\rho_{A,q}(r)+\rho_{A,g}(r)
. The corresponding surface-evaluated endpoint is

\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}(R)\equiv R^{3}\rho_{A}(R).

(48)

In the present paper we use
\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}(R)
as a compact TT/spin-2 proxy. It is not dictated by trace selection alone, but it is one of the two natural basis elements singled out by the soft-wall analysis of App.
L
.

VI.3
Mixed spin-0/spin-2 template and holographic benchmark

The endpoint templates do not exhaust the natural GFF hypothesis space. Appendix
L
shows that, within soft-wall AdS/QCD and for the pole-subtracted integrated trace-energy correlator, one obtains a definite linear combination of
A^{S}
and
A
. This suggests defining, for each partonic sector,

{G_{\rm mix}}_{i}(t;c_{0},c_{2})\equiv c_{0}\,A_{i}^{S}(t)+c_{2}\,A_{i}(t),

(49)

with coefficients
c_{0}
and
c_{2}
not fixed a priori by the lattice analysis. The corresponding Breit density is

\displaystyle{\rho_{\rm mix}}_{i}(r;c_{0},c_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{\Delta}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\;\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{\Delta}\cdot\bm{r}}\;{G_{\rm mix}}_{i}(-\bm{\Delta}^{2};c_{0},c_{2})

\displaystyle=c_{0}\rho_{S,i}(r)+c_{2}\rho_{A,i}(r),

(50)

and after summing over
i=q,g
one obtains the mixed boundary template

\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{\rm mix}(R;c_{0},c_{2})\equiv R^{3}\rho_{\rm mix}(R;c_{0},c_{2})=c_{0}\,\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)}(R)+c_{2}\,\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}(R).

(51)

Because a common rescaling of
(c_{0},c_{2})
can be absorbed into an overall normalization, only their ratio matters at the level of shape matching. In practice it is therefore cleaner to fit the equivalent linear basis coefficients multiplying
\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)}
and
\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}
directly, as in
Eq.

38

The soft-wall holographic derivation of App.
L
provides a benchmark subfamily of this mixed basis. There one finds, up to the common prefactor
1/(2\kappa_{5}^{2})\sim N_{c}^{2}
,

c_{2}^{\rm holo}(E_{h},M_{h})=1-\frac{1}{3}\frac{M_{h}^{2}}{E_{h}^{2}},\qquad c_{0}^{\rm holo}(E_{h},M_{h})=-\frac{9}{32}\frac{M_{h}^{2}}{E_{h}^{2}},

(52)

so that the fixed-ratio holographic benchmark is

\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{\rm hol}(R;E_{h},M_{h})\equiv\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{\rm mix}\big(R;c_{0}^{\rm holo}(E_{h},M_{h}),c_{2}^{\rm holo}(E_{h},M_{h})\big).

(53)

In the rest/Breit kinematics
E_{h}=M_{h}
, this becomes the specific ratio
c_{2}^{\rm holo}:c_{0}^{\rm holo}=2/3:-9/32
. We emphasize that this ratio is
not
imposed in the lattice comparison: it is only a model-dependent benchmark, while the mixed coefficients are extracted freely from the data.

The logic is diagrammatic. The mixed correlator
\langle O_{h}\,T\,T^{00}\,O_{h}\rangle
decomposes into

-,

Scope of the benchmark.

The soft-wall fixed-ratio benchmark is used only as a

VI.4

On the entanglement side, the boundary observable

entering

surface distribution in the renormalized response. This motivates comparing

VII
Main lattice-facing hypotheses, mixed-basis fits, and falsifiability

Section
V
motivates a boundary-dominated window in which the leading nontrivial
R
dependence is carried by
c_{\Sigma,n}(R;h)
and the packaged remainder is subleading and low curvature.

Mixed-basis hypothesis.

On such a window, the working lattice hypothesis is

c_{\Sigma,n}(R;h)\approx a_{0}^{(n)}\,\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)}(R)+a_{2}^{(n)}\,\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}(R),

(68)

with pure spin-0 and pure spin-2 endpoint models recovered as the nested limits
a_{2}^{(n)}=0
and
a_{0}^{(n)}=0
. A stable mixed fit with both coefficients nonzero is therefore itself a nontrivial physical outcome.

Holographic fixed-ratio benchmark.

A useful one-parameter subfamily is obtained by constraining the ratio of the two basis coefficients to the soft-wall benchmark,

a_{0}^{(n)}:a_{2}^{(n)}=c_{0}^{\rm holo}:c_{2}^{\rm holo}.

(69)

Equivalently, one may fit a single overall amplitude multiplying
\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{\rm hol}(R;E_{h},M_{h})
. This constraint is used only as a benchmark test rather than as an input assumption.

The sharp content is therefore the
R
dependence, not the absolute normalization. Because the fitted coefficients are unconstrained in sign, the corresponding boundary-dominated feature in the data can appear as either a peak or a dip at the turning point of the preferred template.

Define the first positive extrema of the pure endpoints,

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)}(R)}{\mathrm{d}R}\right|_{R=R_{\rm EE}^{(0)}}=0,\qquad\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}(R)}{\mathrm{d}R}\right|_{R=R_{\rm EE}^{(2)}}=0.

(70)

For the representative dipole inputs,

R_{\rm EE}^{(0)}\simeq 0.84~\mathrm{fm},\qquad R_{\rm EE}^{(2)}\simeq 0.43~\mathrm{fm}.

(71)

A stable boundary-dominated lattice signal can therefore discriminate between the two pure endpoints at the level of hadronic radii, while a successful mixed fit can be interpreted relative to these two anchor scales.

Define the sign-sensitive gradient diagnostics for the pure endpoints,

\mathcal{G}_{0}(R)\equiv-\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)}(R)}{\mathrm{d}R},\qquad\mathcal{G}_{2}(R)\equiv-\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}(R)}{\mathrm{d}R}.

(72)

Each changes sign at its own turning point. The corresponding sign change seen in the data is therefore informative only together with the location of the turning point and the stability of the fit under template changes. A sign change near
0.84~\mathrm{fm}
supports the spin-0 endpoint. A sign change near
0.43~\mathrm{fm}
supports the spin-2 endpoint. A stable turning point in between, together with nonzero fitted weights for both basis elements, indicates genuine mixing.

The default lattice-facing fit model on each candidate window is the mixed-basis-plus-remainder ansatz of
Eqs.

38
and
39
. Because the pure endpoints are nested inside this family, standard nested-model comparisons (likelihood-ratio, information criteria, or Bayes factors) are natural diagnostics in addition to the stability tests. A convincing boundary-dominated window is one in which (i) the extracted coefficients and turning point are stable under changes of
M
, (ii) they are stable under moderate shifts of
R_{1,2}
, (iii) they are stable under changes of lattice spacing, volume, and ball discretization, (iv) the residuals show no additional extrema or oscillations beyond statistical fluctuations, and (v) the preferred endpoint/mixed classification is itself stable under these variations.

This turns the physics question into a clean basis-selection problem. Several distinct outcomes are possible:

1.

Pure spin-0 success:
the nested fit prefers
a_{2}^{(n)}=0
within uncertainties, while
a_{0}^{(n)}\neq 0
is stable. This supports the minimal trace-selected endpoint picture.

2.

Pure spin-2 success:
the nested fit prefers
a_{0}^{(n)}=0
within uncertainties, while
a_{2}^{(n)}\neq 0
is stable. This supports a spin-2-dominant boundary response and aligns naturally with the holographic selector logic of App.
L
.

3.

Genuine mixed success:
both
a_{0}^{(n)}
and
a_{2}^{(n)}
are nonzero and stable, and freeing the second coefficient materially improves the fit relative to both pure endpoints. This indicates that the boundary response is genuinely mixed in GFF space.

4.

Holographic-ratio success:
the mixed fit is successful and the extracted ratio
a_{0}^{(n)}:a_{2}^{(n)}
is consistent, within uncertainties, with the soft-wall benchmark. This would be a particularly sharp outcome, though not one assumed anywhere in the analysis.

5.

No stable GFF-basis description:
none of the endpoint or mixed fits survives the window/remainder/systematics tests. Then either the boundary-dominance assumption or the GFF-matching conjecture itself fails in the accessible window.

Concrete falsifiers therefore include:

•

No stable window:
there is no interval where the mixed-basis-plus-low-curvature description is stable.

•

Shape mismatch:
the lattice-inferred boundary contribution exhibits nodes, oscillations, or additional extrema not reproduced by any stable linear combination of
\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)}
and
\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}
.

•

Coefficient instability:
the extracted weights
a_{0}^{(n)}
and
a_{2}^{(n)}
vary strongly under changes of window, remainder basis, or lattice spacing.

•

Incompatible turning point:
the extracted turning point is robust but does not match either endpoint scale and cannot be reproduced by a controlled mixed interpolation.

In that sense the GFF program advocated here is sharper than a one-template-only proposal: the relative success or failure of the scalar endpoint, the spin-2 endpoint, and the genuinely mixed fit is itself an observable statement about the hadron-state entanglement response.

VIII
Conclusions and outlook

We introduced a lattice-facing entanglement observable for QCD hadrons: the vacuum-subtracted radius flow of ball Rényi entropies in a rest-frame momentum-projected hadron state, with polarization averaging performed at the level of the final flow. At fixed replica/cut prescription this definition is exact, and vacuum subtraction removes state-independent UV and
\Sigma
-localized contributions.

A size variation at fixed shape is a Weyl response, so the continuum flow is trace selected. This motivates the modular-correlator representation developed above and, after renormalization, a surface-plus-remainder organization on
\Sigma=\partial B_{R}
. Operationally, boundary dominance is tested by fitting the lattice flow to the mixed-basis ansatz
Eqs.

38
and
39
and checking stability against window choice, remainder choice, lattice spacing, volume, and ball discretization.

The lattice comparison is framed in the two-function GFF basis
\{\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(0)},\mathfrak{t}_{h}^{(2)}\}
. The pure spin-0 and pure spin-2 templates are nested limits of the mixed fit. For the representative nucleon inputs used here, the pure endpoints predict distinct turning-point scales,
R_{\rm EE}^{(0)}\simeq 0.84~\mathrm{fm}
and
R_{\rm EE}^{(2)}\simeq 0.43~\mathrm{fm}
. A stable lattice signal can therefore help distinguish among scalar dominance, spin-2 dominance, and genuine mixing.

The next step is straightforward: measure
\Delta S_{n}(B_{R};h)
and
\mathfrak{s}_{n}(R;h)
, beginning with
n=2
, isolate a candidate boundary-dominated window, and confront the extracted shape with the two pure endpoints and the mixed fit. The outcome is data-driven. What this paper provides is the observable, the response framework, the stability protocol, and a disciplined two-function hypothesis space for lattice QCD to test.

Appendix D

This appendix spells out why the UV-sensitive part of the trace-channel response can be organized by distributions supported on the entangling surface

D.1
Why the trace response localizes on
\Sigma

In the correlator-based representation of the radius flow, the modular insertion associated with the UV reference ball kernel is a ball-weighted insertion of
T_{00}
with the characteristic factor

f_{R}(\bm{y})\equiv F_{R}(\bm{y})\,\Theta(R-|\bm{y}|),\qquad F_{R}(\bm{y})=\frac{R^{2}-|\bm{y}|^{2}}{2R}.

(74)

The only nonsmooth feature of
f_{R}
is the step function
\Theta(R-|\bm{y}|)
. At short spatial separations, stress-tensor correlators admit a distributional/OPE organization. As a distribution in

one expects

\Big\langle T(0,\bm{x})\,T_{00}(0,\bm{y})\Big\rangle\sim\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{K}^{(n)}(\bm{x})\,\partial^{(n)}\delta^{(3)}(\bm{x}-\bm{y}),

(75)

where
\partial^{(n)}
denotes a multi-derivative acting on the delta distribution and the coefficients
\mathcal{K}^{(n)}
are local operator expectation values. When such singular terms are integrated against
f_{R}
, derivatives of the delta distribution are transferred onto
f_{R}
in the sense of distributions. Because
F_{R}
is smooth, the only source of surface support is differentiation of the step function, producing
\delta(r-R)
and its radial derivatives.

D.2

Define the renormalized response tensor by subtracting the UV-local and
\Sigma

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\rm EE,ren}(\bm{x}\,|\,B_{R};\rho)

\displaystyle\equiv-2g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{T}^{\rm EE,ren}_{\mu\nu}.

(77)

Locality and rotational covariance imply that at fixed

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\rm EE,ren}(\bm{x}\,|\,B_{R};\rho)

\mathfrak{s}_{2}(R)
from a correlated smooth fit to
\Delta S_{2}(R)
(or from correlated finite differences with controlled covariance), rather than from pointwise differencing; and (v) fit locally first to the pure endpoints and then to the mixed two-basis model.

The minimal reporting output is:
(a) whether a boundary-dominated window exists,
(b) the presence or absence of a pronounced intermediate-
R
extremum,
(c) the extracted turning point and whether it is closer to
R_{\rm EE}^{(0)}
or
R_{\rm EE}^{(2)}
,
(d) the sign of the slope on either side of the extremum,
(e) whether a second basis coefficient is statistically required, and
(f) the stability of the preferred endpoint/mixed classification under lattice spacing, volume, and cut-discretization changes.

K.3
Excited-state contamination

Unless the source-sink separation
|\tau_{f}-\tau_{i}|
is sufficiently large, excited-state contamination can distort the measured
R
dependence of
\Delta S_{n}(B_{R};h,\lambda)
and therefore of the flow
\mathfrak{s}_{n}(R)
, potentially shifting or broadening the extracted turning point. Accordingly, the same stability checks used in standard lattice two- and three-point analyses should be performed here: vary
|\tau_{f}-\tau_{i}|
(and/or use multistate fits) and require that the inferred window stability, endpoint preference, and mixed-fit coefficient ratio are stable within uncertainties.

Appendix L
Holographic derivation of the momentum-space hadron central charge

Unlike the Euclidean discussion in the main text, this appendix is written in Minkowski signature with the mostly-minus convention

\eta_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(+,-,-,-),\qquad\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3.

(102)

The purpose of this appendix is to show, within a soft-wall AdS/QCD model, why the pole-subtracted integrated mixed stress-tensor correlator closes on the two-function basis
\{A(t),A^{S}(t)\}
and why the benchmark combination quoted in the main text takes the form

\mathfrak{c}_{h}(t;p_{0},m_{0})\propto\left(1-\frac{1}{3}\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)A(t)-\frac{9}{32}\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}A^{S}(t).

(103)

The derivation is for a scalar target. Its role in the main text is limited: it motivates the mixed basis
\{A,A^{S}\}
and supplies a model-dependent benchmark ratio inside that basis; it is
not
a first-principles nucleon derivation.

We start from the reduced four-point amplitude

\langle O_{h}(p_{2})\,T(q_{1})\,T^{00}(q_{2})\,O_{h}(p_{1})\rangle=(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p_{1}+q_{1}-p_{2}-q_{2})\,\mathcal{M}_{T00}(p_{2},q_{2};p_{1},q_{1}),

(104)

where
T\equiv T^{\mu}{}_{\mu}
. The exact spacetime integration of the first insertion imposes

q_{1}=0.

(105)

In that kinematics the uncontracted amplitude still contains the standard elastic external-line poles in the
s
- and
u
-channels. The finite object is therefore the
pole-subtracted
integrated correlator. We write

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}(q_{2})\equiv\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg,exact}(q_{2})-\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(\mathrm{ct})}(q_{2}),}

(106)

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg,exact}
is the exact pole-subtracted integrated correlator and
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(\mathrm{ct})}
is the purely local two-graviton matter seagull. The quantity that carries the nonlocal GFF information is
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}
, not
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(\mathrm{ct})}
.

The main result is obtained in exact integrated Breit kinematics,

q_{1}=0,\qquad q_{2,0}=0,\qquad t\equiv q_{2}^{2}=-Q_{2}^{2}<0,

(107)

and in the window

\kappa^{2}\ll Q_{2}^{2}\ll 4m_{0}^{2}.

(108)

There one finds

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}(q_{2})\approx 2\left[p_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\,m_{0}^{2}\right]\frac{1}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}}\,A(t)-\left(\frac{3}{2}\,m_{0}^{2}\right)\frac{3}{16\kappa_{5}^{2}}\,A^{S}(t),\qquad t=q_{2}^{2},}

(109)

or, equivalently,

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}(q_{2})\approx 2p_{0}^{2}\,\mathfrak{c}_{h}(t;p_{0},m_{0}),

(110)

with

\boxed{\mathfrak{c}_{h}(t;p_{0},m_{0})=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}}\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{3}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)A(t)-\frac{9}{32}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\,A^{S}(t)\right].}

(111)

The logic is straightforward:
(i) decompose the four-point function into Witten-diagram channels;
(ii) subtract the elastic scalar pole from the
s
- and
u
-channels;
(iii) use the exact integrated projection
q_{1}=0
to show that those pole-subtracted
s
- and
u
-channels vanish identically;
(iv) identify the surviving nonlocal
t
-channel TT and spin-0 exchanges with the same spectral data that define
A(t)
and
A^{S}(t)
; and
(v) evaluate the resulting kinematic coefficients.

L.1
Soft-wall ingredients and form-factor definitions

We work on AdS
5
in Poincaré coordinates with

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\bar{g}_{MN}\mathrm{d}X^{M}\mathrm{d}X^{N}=\frac{R^{2}}{z^{2}}\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\mathrm{d}x^{\nu}-\mathrm{d}z^{2}\right),\qquad z\in(0,\infty),

(112)

and soft-wall dilaton profile

\varphi(z)=\kappa^{2}z^{2}.

(113)

For the present argument we only need three ingredients:
the hadron ground-state wavefunction
\Phi_{0}(z)
,
the non-normalizable TT profile
H(t,z)
,
and the non-normalizable trace/scalar profile
F(t,z)
.

The scalar hadron is represented by a charged bulk field
X
dual to a boundary operator of dimension
\Delta_{h}
, with ground-state mode

\Phi_{0}(z)=\mathcal{N}_{0}\,z^{\Delta_{h}},\qquad m_{0}^{2}=2\kappa^{2}\Delta_{h},\qquad\mathcal{N}_{0}^{2}=\frac{2\kappa^{2\Delta_{h}-2}}{R^{3}\Gamma(\Delta_{h}-1)}.

(114)

The universal soft-wall measure appearing in the overlap integrals is

\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)=\frac{R^{3}}{z^{3}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa^{2}z^{2}}.

(115)

The TT and trace source profiles are normalized by

H(t,0)=1,\qquad F(t,0)=1.

(116)

The corresponding three-point form factors are

\boxed{F_{h}^{(2)}(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,\Phi_{0}(z)^{2}\,H(t,z),\qquad F_{h}^{(0)}(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,\Phi_{0}(z)^{2}\,F(t,z).}

(117)

In the scalar-target soft-wall model these are identified with the spin-2 and scalar/trace GFFs,

F_{h}^{(2)}(t)\equiv A(t),\qquad F_{h}^{(0)}(t)\equiv A^{S}(t).

(118)

L.2
Channel decomposition, pole subtraction, and the exact
q_{1}=0
selection

The full reduced amplitude decomposes as

\mathcal{M}_{T00}=\mathcal{M}_{T00}^{(s)}+\mathcal{M}_{T00}^{(u)}+\mathcal{M}_{T00}^{(ct)}+\mathcal{M}_{T00}^{(t,TT)}+\mathcal{M}_{T00}^{(t,0)},

(119)

corresponding to the diagram classes shown in Fig.
10
.

Let
G_{X}(z,z^{\prime};s)
denote the scalar bulk-to-bulk propagator. The pole-subtracted version is obtained by removing the elastic
n=0
mode,

G_{X}^{\rm sub}(z,z^{\prime};s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\Phi_{n}(z)\Phi_{n}(z^{\prime})}{s-m_{n}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}.

(120)

The relevant source overlaps are

\mathcal{A}_{mn}(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,\Phi_{m}(z)\Phi_{n}(z)\,H(t,z),

(121)

and

\mathcal{S}_{mn}(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,\Phi_{m}(z)\Phi_{n}(z)\,F(t,z).

(122)

Because the integrated trace insertion is a zero-momentum Weyl source, the exact
q_{1}=0
projection implies

F(0,z)=1\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\mathcal{S}_{mn}(0)=\delta_{mn}.

(123)

This is the key simplification: the exact integrated source selects the elastic mode only. Since
G_{X}^{\rm sub}
starts at
n=1
, the pole-subtracted integrated
s
- and
u
-channel amplitudes vanish identically,

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(s,{\rm sub})}(q_{2})=0,\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(u,{\rm sub})}(q_{2})=0.}

(124)

Therefore the exact pole-subtracted integrated correlator contains only a local contact term plus the nonlocal TT and spin-0
t
-channel exchanges:

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg,exact}(q_{2})=\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(\mathrm{ct})}(q_{2})+\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(t,TT)}(q_{2})+\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(t,0)}(q_{2}),

(125)

so that

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}(q_{2})=\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(t,TT)}(q_{2})+\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(t,0)}(q_{2}).}

(126)

There is a second exact simplification specific to the integrated kinematics.
For an even-spin TT exchange with
j\geq 4
, every extra top momentum is proportional to

q=\frac{q_{2}}{2}=-\frac{\Delta}{2},

(127)

and is therefore annihilated by TT transversality. Hence

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(t,j,TT)}(q_{2})\Big|_{q_{1}=0}=0,\qquad j=4,6,8,\dots}

(128)

The exact integrated TT sector is therefore a pure spin-2 selector.

L.3
UV factorization onto the
A
and
A^{S}
channels

In the UV-dominated regime the reduced top vertices factorize onto the same spectral sums that appear in the three-point functions, so the nonlocal regular amplitude may be written as

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}(q_{2})=\mathcal{K}_{2}(q_{2},p)\,\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{T}(t)\,A(t)+\mathcal{K}_{0}(q_{2},p)\,\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)\,A^{S}(t),\qquad t=q_{2}^{2}.}

(129)

Here
\mathcal{K}_{2}
and
\mathcal{K}_{0}
are purely kinematic factors, while

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{T}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
depend only on the chosen soft-wall completion.

For the minimal completion used in the main text, analytic continuation to spacelike momentum transfer,

t=-Q_{2}^{2},\qquad Q_{2}^{2}>0,\qquad a_{2}\equiv\frac{Q_{2}^{2}}{4\kappa^{2}},

(130)

gives

\boxed{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{T}(-Q_{2}^{2})=\frac{a_{2}}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}(a_{2}+2)}=\frac{Q_{2}^{2}}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}(Q_{2}^{2}+8\kappa^{2})},}

(131)

and

\boxed{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(-Q_{2}^{2})=\frac{3a_{2}}{16\kappa_{5}^{2}(a_{2}+2)}=\frac{3Q_{2}^{2}}{16\kappa_{5}^{2}(Q_{2}^{2}+8\kappa^{2})}.}

(132)

Hence, for
Q_{2}^{2}\gg\kappa^{2}
,

\boxed{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{T}(-Q_{2}^{2})\approx\frac{1}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}},\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(-Q_{2}^{2})\approx\frac{3}{16\kappa_{5}^{2}}.}

(133)

L.4
Kinematics and the surviving coefficients

We use

p\equiv\frac{p_{1}+p_{2}}{2},\qquad q\equiv\frac{q_{1}+q_{2}}{2},\qquad\Delta\equiv p_{2}-p_{1}=q_{1}-q_{2},\qquad t\equiv\Delta^{2}.

(134)

For equal-mass on-shell external states,

p_{1}^{2}=p_{2}^{2}=m_{0}^{2},\qquad p\cdot\Delta=0,\qquad 4p^{2}=4m_{0}^{2}-t.

(135)

Under the exact integrated projection,

q_{1}=0,\qquad q=\frac{q_{2}}{2},\qquad\Delta=-q_{2},\qquad t=q_{2}^{2}.

(136)

The TT projector is

\Pi^{TT}_{\mu\nu,\alpha\beta}(\Delta)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\theta_{\mu\alpha}\theta_{\nu\beta}+\theta_{\mu\beta}\theta_{\nu\alpha}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\,\theta_{\mu\nu}\theta_{\alpha\beta},\qquad\theta_{\mu\nu}(\Delta)=\eta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\Delta_{\mu}\Delta_{\nu}}{\Delta^{2}}.

(137)

Contracting it with the scalar-target tensor structure gives the exact TT kinematic factor

\boxed{\mathcal{K}_{2}(q_{2},p)=2\left[\left(p_{0}-\frac{\Delta_{0}(\Delta\cdot p)}{t}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\left(1-\frac{\Delta_{0}^{2}}{t}\right)\left(p^{2}-\frac{(\Delta\cdot p)^{2}}{t}\right)\right].}

(138)

In the exact integrated kinematics
\Delta\cdot p=0
, so

\mathcal{K}_{2}(q_{2},p)=2\left[p_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\left(1-\frac{q_{2,0}^{2}}{q_{2}^{2}}\right)p^{2}\right].

(139)

In Breit kinematics
q_{2,0}=0
this reduces to

\boxed{\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\rm Breit}(q_{2},p)=2\left[p_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\,p^{2}\right]=2\left[p_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\,m_{0}^{2}+\frac{t}{12}\right].}

(140)

The scalar/trace channel has a simpler role in the final benchmark. After the same exact
q_{1}=0
projection and on-shell reduction, its leading small-
|t|
prefactor is

\mathcal{K}_{0}(q_{2},p)=-\frac{3}{2}\,m_{0}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(t).

(141)

We do not display the full
\mathcal{O}(t)
correction because it is not needed in the matching window
Eq.

108
; the benchmark ratio follows entirely from the leading term.

Combining
Eqs.

129
,
133
,
140
and
141

and dropping terms suppressed by
|t|/m_{0}^{2}
gives

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}(q_{2})\approx 2\left[p_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\,m_{0}^{2}\right]\frac{1}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}}\,A(t)-\left(\frac{3}{2}\,m_{0}^{2}\right)\frac{3}{16\kappa_{5}^{2}}\,A^{S}(t),\qquad t=q_{2}^{2}.}

(142)

This is the nonlocal integrated trace–energy correlator formula used in the main text.

What is exact and what is asymptotic.

It is useful to separate the logic into three layers:

1.

Exact after pole subtraction:

the integrated pole-subtracted correlator contains only the local seagull plus the TT and spin-0
t
-channel exchanges,
Eqs.

125
and
126
.

2.

Exact after the
q_{1}=0
projection:

the pole-subtracted
s
- and
u
-channels vanish identically,
Eq.

124
, and higher even TT spins also drop out,
Eq.

128
.

3.

Asymptotic in the window
\kappa^{2}\ll Q_{2}^{2}\ll 4m_{0}^{2}
:

the coefficient functions freeze to constants and the
\mathcal{O}(t/m_{0}^{2})
terms may be dropped, yielding
Eq.

142
.

L.5
Momentum-space hadron central charge

Factoring out
2p_{0}^{2}
defines the momentum-space hadron central charge,

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}(q_{2})\approx 2p_{0}^{2}\,\mathfrak{c}_{h}(t;p_{0},m_{0}),

(143)

with

\boxed{\mathfrak{c}_{h}(t;p_{0},m_{0})=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}}\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{3}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)A(t)-\frac{9}{32}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\,A^{S}(t)\right].}

(144)

Using the standard holographic normalization

2\kappa_{5}^{2}=16\pi G_{N}=\frac{8\pi^{2}}{N_{c}^{2}},\qquad\frac{1}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}}=\frac{N_{c}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}},

(145)

this becomes

\boxed{\mathfrak{c}_{h}(t;p_{0},m_{0})=\frac{N_{c}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{3}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)A(t)-\frac{9}{32}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\,A^{S}(t)\right].}

(146)

For separate partonic sectors
i\in\{q,g\}
the same argument gives

\boxed{\mathfrak{c}_{h,i}(t;p_{0},m_{0})\propto\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{3}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)A_{i}(t)-\frac{9}{32}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\,A_{i}^{S}(t)\right].}

(147)

This is the sector-decomposed relation quoted in the main text.

The derivation above was performed for a scalar target, so the nucleon use of the result should be read only as a basis-selection argument and a benchmark-ratio extrapolation. With that caveat, one makes the replacements

m_{0}\to M_{h},\qquad p_{0}\to E_{h},\qquad A(t)\to A_{h}(t),\qquad A^{S}(t)\to A_{h}^{S}(t),

(148)

to obtain

\boxed{\mathfrak{c}_{h,i}(t;E_{h},M_{h})\propto\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{3}\,\frac{M_{h}^{2}}{E_{h}^{2}}\right)A_{i}(t)-\frac{9}{32}\,\frac{M_{h}^{2}}{E_{h}^{2}}\,A_{i}^{S}(t)\right].}

(149)

The large-energy limit is then

\boxed{\mathfrak{c}_{h,i}(t;E_{h}\to\infty,M_{h})\propto A_{i}(t).}

(150)

Thus the integrated holographic response becomes spin-2 dominated at large energy, while the scalar/trace contribution is suppressed by
M_{h}^{2}/E_{h}^{2}
.

Summary.

Within the soft-wall model, the exact integrated projection
q_{1}=0
kills the pole-subtracted
s
- and
u
-channels, leaving only TT and spin-0
t
-channel exchange. Those two channels are precisely the ones that define the GFFs
A(t)
and
A^{S}(t)
, so the nonlocal regular correlator closes on the two-function basis
\{A,A^{S}\}
. After evaluating the mostly-minus kinematics and taking the window
\kappa^{2}\ll Q_{2}^{2}\ll 4m_{0}^{2}
, one arrives at the benchmark combination
Eq.

144

M.1
Five-dimensional effective description

We work on AdS
5
in Poincaré coordinates,

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\bar{g}_{MN}\mathrm{d}X^{M}\mathrm{d}X^{N}=\frac{R^{2}}{z^{2}}\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\mathrm{d}x^{\nu}-\mathrm{d}z^{2}\right),\qquad z\in(0,\infty),

(159)

with soft-wall dilaton profile

\varphi(z)=\kappa^{2}z^{2}.

(160)

The 5D description needed here is effective rather than microscopic:

S_{\rm eff}=S_{\rm grav}+S_{X}+S_{\chi}^{(2)}+S_{\rm bdy}+S_{\rm gf}.

(161)

The three relevant sectors are the TT graviton sector, the charged scalar hadron sector, and an effective spin-0 completion for the trace channel.

TT gravity sector.

For the TT sector it is sufficient to use the standard soft-wall Einstein action in axial gauge as the effective generator of the TT equations,

S_{\rm grav}=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{5}X\,\sqrt{g}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\varphi(z)}\left(\mathcal{R}+\frac{12}{R^{2}}\right)+\cdots,

(162)

where the omitted terms are gauge-fixing and boundary pieces that do not affect the on-shell cubic vertices used below.

We parameterize the four-dimensional metric fluctuation by

g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{R^{2}}{z^{2}}\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}+h^{TT}_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{4}\,\eta_{\mu\nu}f+\cdots\right),\qquad h_{zM}=0.

(163)

The omitted modes are longitudinal/vector fluctuations that do not contribute to the reduced exact
q_{1}=0
amplitudes considered here.

The quadratic TT action may be written as
Mamo and Zahed 2020

S_{TT}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{8\kappa_{5}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\left[\partial_{\rho}h^{TT}_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\rho}h^{TT\,\mu\nu}+\partial_{z}h^{TT}_{\mu\nu}\partial_{z}h^{TT\,\mu\nu}\right],

(164)

with the standard soft-wall measure

\boxed{\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)=\frac{R^{3}}{z^{3}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa^{2}z^{2}}.}

(165)

Charged scalar hadron sector.

The hadron is represented by a charged bulk scalar
X
dual to a boundary operator
O_{h}
of dimension
\Delta_{h}
:

S_{X}=-\int\mathrm{d}^{5}X\,\sqrt{g}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\varphi(z)}\left[g^{MN}\partial_{M}X^{\dagger}\partial_{N}X+m_{5}^{2}|X|^{2}\right],\qquad m_{5}^{2}R^{2}=\Delta_{h}(\Delta_{h}-4).

(166)

For a plane-wave mode
X(x,z)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}p\cdot x}\Phi(z)
, the normalizable solutions are

\Phi_{n}(z)=\mathcal{N}_{n}\,z^{\Delta_{h}}L_{n}^{(\Delta_{h}-2)}(\kappa^{2}z^{2}),\qquad m_{n}^{2}=4\kappa^{2}\left(n+\frac{\Delta_{h}}{2}\right),

(167)

with orthonormality

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,\Phi_{n}(z)\Phi_{m}(z)=\delta_{nm},

(168)

and normalization

\mathcal{N}_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{2\kappa^{2\Delta_{h}-2}}{R^{3}}\frac{n!}{\Gamma(n+\Delta_{h}-1)}}.

(169)

The ground state is

\Phi_{0}(z)=\mathcal{N}_{0}\,z^{\Delta_{h}},\qquad m_{0}^{2}=2\kappa^{2}\Delta_{h}.

(170)

The scalar bulk-to-bulk propagator has the standard mode expansion

G_{X}(z,z^{\prime};s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Phi_{n}(z)\Phi_{n}(z^{\prime})}{s-m_{n}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}.

(171)

Effective spin-0 trace sector.

Unlike the TT sector, the physical trace channel mixes the metric trace, longitudinal metric modes, and the dilaton. A complete diagonalization depends on the precise 5D Einstein-dilaton model. For the present correlator one only needs a normalizable spin-0 exchange and a non-normalizable trace source profile normalized to unity at the boundary, so we use the effective completion
Mamo and Zahed 2020

M.2
TT modes, source profiles, and the GFF building blocks

The TT radial equation is

\partial_{z}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa^{2}z^{2}}}{z^{3}}\partial_{z}\Psi^{(2)}\right)+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa^{2}z^{2}}}{z^{3}}\,t\,\Psi^{(2)}=0.

(178)

Its normalizable solutions are

\Psi_{n}^{(2)}(z)=\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}\,z^{4}L_{n}^{(2)}(\kappa^{2}z^{2}),\qquad m_{2,n}^{2}=4\kappa^{2}(n+2),

(179)

with orthonormality

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,\Psi_{n}^{(2)}(z)\Psi_{m}^{(2)}(z)=\delta_{nm},

(180)

and

\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{2\kappa^{6}}{R^{3}}\frac{n!}{\Gamma(n+3)}}.

(181)

The four-dimensional TT projector is

\boxed{\Pi^{TT}_{\mu\nu,\alpha\beta}(\Delta)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\theta_{\mu\alpha}\theta_{\nu\beta}+\theta_{\mu\beta}\theta_{\nu\alpha}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\,\theta_{\mu\nu}\theta_{\alpha\beta},}

(182)

with

\theta_{\mu\nu}(\Delta)=\eta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\Delta_{\mu}\Delta_{\nu}}{\Delta^{2}}.

(183)

The TT bulk-to-bulk propagator is

G^{(2)}_{\mu\nu,\alpha\beta}(z,z^{\prime};t)=\Pi^{TT}_{\mu\nu,\alpha\beta}(\Delta)\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Psi_{n}^{(2)}(z)\Psi_{n}^{(2)}(z^{\prime})}{t-m_{2,n}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}.

(184)

Let
H(t,z)
denote the non-normalizable TT bulk-to-boundary profile normalized by

H(t,0)=1.

(185)

The corresponding spin-2 hadronic form factor is

\boxed{F_{h}^{(2)}(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,\Phi_{0}(z)^{2}\,H(t,z).}

(186)

In the scalar-target soft-wall model this is identified with the spin-2 GFF,

F_{h}^{(2)}(t)\equiv A(t),

(187)

see Fig.
12
a.

Similarly, the non-normalizable spin-0/trace profile
F(t,z)
defines

\boxed{F_{h}^{(0)}(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,\Phi_{0}(z)^{2}\,F(t,z),}

(188)

which we identify with the scalar/trace GFF,

F_{h}^{(0)}(t)\equiv A^{S}(t),

(189)

see Fig.
12
b.

It is useful to make the corresponding spectral representation explicit. Introducing source residues
f_{n}^{(2)}
and
f_{\ell}^{(0)}
through the near-boundary expansion of the non-normalizable profiles, one may write

\displaystyle H(t,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_{n}^{(2)}\,\Psi_{n}^{(2)}(z)}{t-m_{2,n}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon},

\displaystyle F(t,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_{\ell}^{(0)}\,\Psi_{\ell}^{(0)}(z)}{t-m_{0,\ell}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon},

(190)

so that the two form factors become

\boxed{A(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_{n}^{(2)}\,g_{n}^{(2)}}{t-m_{2,n}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon},\qquad A^{S}(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_{\ell}^{(0)}\,g_{\ell}^{(0)}}{t-m_{0,\ell}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon},}

(191)

where the bottom overlaps
g_{n}^{(2)}
and
g_{\ell}^{(0)}
are defined below in
Eq.

207

M.3

We use

(194)

For the final compact reduction we also choose exact integrated Breit kinematics,

q_{2,0}=0.

M.5
Pole subtraction and exact disappearance of the integrated
s
- and
u
-channels

The key source overlaps are

\mathcal{A}_{mn}(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,\Phi_{m}(z)\Phi_{n}(z)\,H(t,z),

(197)

and

\mathcal{S}_{mn}(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,\Phi_{m}(z)\Phi_{n}(z)\,F(t,z).

(198)

Because the exactly integrated trace insertion is a zero-momentum Weyl rescaling, one has

F(0,z)=1\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\mathcal{S}_{mn}(0)=\delta_{mn}.

(199)

This is the essential selection rule: the exact
q_{1}=0
source projects onto the elastic mode only.

The un-subtracted
s
- and
u
-channel amplitudes contain the standard external-line elastic poles. The correct finite quantity is obtained by subtracting the elastic
n=0
pole from the exchanged scalar propagator,

G_{X}^{\rm sub}(z,z^{\prime};s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\Phi_{n}(z)\Phi_{n}(z^{\prime})}{s-m_{n}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}.

(200)

Then, in the exact integrated limit
q_{1}=0
, the source overlap
Eq.

199
selects the elastic mode
n=0
, while the pole-subtracted propagator starts at
n=1
. Therefore the pole-subtracted integrated
s
- and
u
-channel amplitudes vanish identically:

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(s,{\rm sub})}(q_{2})=0,\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(u,{\rm sub})}(q_{2})=0.}

(201)

The statement is about the integrated amplitudes, not the kernel itself:
G_{X}^{\rm sub}(z,z^{\prime};s)
is nonzero, but its contraction with the exact
q_{1}=0
source vanishes.

The exact pole-subtracted integrated correlator is therefore

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg,exact}(q_{2})=\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(\mathrm{ct})}(q_{2})+\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(t,TT)}(q_{2})+\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(t,0)}(q_{2}),

(202)

and hence

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}(q_{2})=\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(t,TT)}(q_{2})+\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(t,0)}(q_{2}).}

(203)

M.6
Bottom hadron vertices

For the elastic bottom vertices it is convenient to introduce the scalar-target tensor

\boxed{\mathbb{T}^{\alpha\beta}(p_{2},p_{1};m_{0},m_{0})=p_{2}^{\alpha}p_{1}^{\beta}+p_{2}^{\beta}p_{1}^{\alpha}-\eta^{\alpha\beta}\big(p_{1}\!\cdot\!p_{2}-m_{0}^{2}\big).}

(204)

The bottom couplings of the exchanged TT and spin-0 modes to the hadron are then

\mathcal{G}_{n}^{(2)\alpha\beta}(p_{2},p_{1})=g_{n}^{(2)}\,\mathbb{T}^{\alpha\beta}(p_{2},p_{1};m_{0},m_{0}),

(205)

\mathcal{G}_{\ell}^{(0)}(p_{2},p_{1})=g_{\ell}^{(0)},

(206)

with overlaps

g_{n}^{(2)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,\Phi_{0}(z)^{2}\Psi_{n}^{(2)}(z),\qquad g_{\ell}^{(0)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,\Phi_{0}(z)^{2}\Psi_{\ell}^{(0)}(z).

(207)

The TT exchange carries the explicit tensor structure (
204
); the scalar exchange is controlled by the traced scalar form factor and will be parameterized below by a reduced scalar invariant
\mathcal{K}_{0}
after the exact integrated Breit projection.

M.7

This observation makes it possible to obtain the relevant top cubic vertices by varying the quadratic 5D actions once with respect to that Weyl source.

TT top vertex.

Varying the quadratic TT action

\boxed{S_{fTT}^{(3)}=\frac{1}{8\kappa_{5}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,f(x,z)\left[\partial_{\rho}h^{TT}_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\rho}h^{TT\,\mu\nu}+\partial_{z}h^{TT}_{\mu\nu}\partial_{z}h^{TT\,\mu\nu}\right].}

Local matter seagull.

The local two-graviton matter contact term comes from the second variation of the scalar action

g_{MN}=\bar{g}_{MN}+h_{MN},\qquad h\equiv\bar{g}^{MN}h_{MN},

(220)

\sqrt{g}=\sqrt{\bar{g}}\left[1+\frac{1}{2}h+\frac{1}{8}h^{2}-\frac{1}{4}h_{MN}h^{MN}+\mathcal{O}(h^{3})\right],

(221)

g^{MN}=\bar{g}^{MN}-h^{MN}+h^{MP}h_{P}{}^{N}+\mathcal{O}(h^{3}).

(222)

The cross term between two metric insertions

S_{X}^{(2h)}=-\int\mathrm{d}^{5}X\,\sqrt{\bar{g}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\varphi}\left[B^{MN}[h^{(1)},h^{(2)}]\,\partial_{M}X^{\dagger}\partial_{N}X+\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{C}_{\rm cross}[h^{(1)},h^{(2)}]\,\big(\bar{g}^{MN}\partial_{M}X^{\dagger}\partial_{N}X+m_{5}^{2}|X|^{2}\big)\right],

(223)

\displaystyle B^{MN}[h^{(1)},h^{(2)}]

\displaystyle=h^{(1)MP}h^{(2)N}{}_{P}+h^{(2)MP}h^{(1)N}{}_{P}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}h^{(1)}h^{(2)MN}-\frac{1}{2}h^{(2)}h^{(1)MN},

(224)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\rm cross}[h^{(1)},h^{(2)}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}h^{(1)}h^{(2)}-\frac{1}{2}h^{(1)}_{PQ}h^{(2)PQ}.

(225)

For the polarizations relevant here,

h_{\mu\nu}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{4}\,\eta_{\mu\nu},\qquad h_{\mu\nu}^{(2)}=\epsilon_{\mu\nu}^{00}\,\mathcal{H}^{(00)}(q_{2},z)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}q_{2}\cdot x},

(226)

the four-dimensional derivative seagull vanishes, while the radial-kinetic and mass cross terms survive. Evaluated on the hadron ground state, this gives

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(\mathrm{ct})}(q_{2})=-\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;\mathcal{W}_{2}(z)\,\mathcal{H}^{(00)}(q_{2},z)\left[\frac{z^{2}}{R^{2}}\,\Phi_{0}^{\prime}(z)^{2}+\frac{R^{2}}{z^{2}}\,m_{5}^{2}\,\Phi_{0}(z)^{2}\right].}

\boxed{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(-Q_{2}^{2})=\frac{3a_{2}}{16\kappa_{5}^{2}(a_{2}+2)}=\frac{3Q_{2}^{2}}{16\kappa_{5}^{2}(Q_{2}^{2}+8\kappa^{2})}.}

M.10
Exact TT contraction and exact integrated Breit reduction

Using

p_{2}=p+\frac{\Delta}{2},\qquad p_{1}=p-\frac{\Delta}{2},\qquad p_{1}\cdot p_{2}=p^{2}-\frac{t}{4},

(253)

the scalar-target tensor becomes

\mathbb{T}^{\alpha\beta}(p_{2},p_{1};m_{0},m_{0})=2p^{\alpha}p^{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\,\Delta^{\alpha}\Delta^{\beta}-\eta^{\alpha\beta}\left(p^{2}-\frac{t}{4}-m_{0}^{2}\right).

(254)

Because the TT projector is traceless and transverse,

\Pi^{TT}_{\mu\nu,\alpha\beta}(\Delta)\,\eta^{\alpha\beta}=0,\qquad\Pi^{TT}_{\mu\nu,\alpha\beta}(\Delta)\,\Delta^{\alpha}=0,

(255)

only the

term survives in the TT contraction. A short calculation gives

\boxed{\mathcal{K}_{2}(q_{2},p)=2\left[\left(p_{0}-\frac{\Delta_{0}\,(\Delta\!\cdot\!p)}{t}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\left(1-\frac{\Delta_{0}^{2}}{t}\right)\left(p^{2}-\frac{(\Delta\!\cdot\!p)^{2}}{t}\right)\right].}

(256)

In the exact integrated equal-mass kinematics,

\Delta=-q_{2},\qquad p\cdot q_{2}=0,

(257)

so

\boxed{\mathcal{K}_{2}(q_{2},p)\Big|_{q_{1}=0}=2\left[p_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\left(1-\frac{q_{2,0}^{2}}{q_{2}^{2}}\right)p^{2}\right].}

In exact integrated Breit kinematics,
q_{2,0}=0

\boxed{\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\rm Breit}(q_{2},p)=2\left[p_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\,p^{2}\right]=2\left[p_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\,m_{0}^{2}+\frac{t}{12}\right].}

(259)

For the scalar channel, the exact covariant form of
\mathcal{K}_{0}(q_{2},p)
depends on the normalization convention used for the effective spin-0 completion. However, the only form needed in the proof of App.
L
is its exact integrated Breit reduction. Evaluating the scalar exchange generated by
Eqs.

214
and
219
with one trace source and one
T^{00}
source gives

\boxed{\mathcal{K}_{0}^{\rm Breit}(q_{2},p)=-\left(\frac{3}{2}\,m_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,q_{2}^{2}\right)=-\left(\frac{3}{2}\,m_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,t\right).}

(260)

This is the exact integrated Breit scalar invariant entering the final reduction. It is this quantity, rather than a separate pure-trace projector contraction, that controls the scalar exchange in the conventions used here.

Substituting

259
and
260
into the UV-factorized amplitude

237

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}(q_{2})=2\left[p_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\,p^{2}\right]\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{T}(q_{2}^{2})\,A(q_{2}^{2})-\left(\frac{3}{2}\,m_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,q_{2}^{2}\right)\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(q_{2}^{2})\,A^{S}(q_{2}^{2}).}

(261)

Using the on-shell identity
p^{2}=m_{0}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{4}q_{2}^{2}
, this becomes

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}(q_{2})=2\left[p_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{12}(4m_{0}^{2}-q_{2}^{2})\right]\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{T}(q_{2}^{2})\,A(q_{2}^{2})-\left(\frac{3}{2}\,m_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,q_{2}^{2}\right)\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(q_{2}^{2})\,A^{S}(q_{2}^{2}).}

(262)

Since
q_{2}^{2}=t=-Q_{2}^{2}

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}(q_{2})=2\left[p_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{12}(4m_{0}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2})\right]\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{T}(-Q_{2}^{2})\,A(-Q_{2}^{2})-\left(\frac{3}{2}\,m_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\,Q_{2}^{2}\right)\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(-Q_{2}^{2})\,A^{S}(-Q_{2}^{2}).

(263)

M.11
Final reduction in the window
\kappa^{2}\ll Q_{2}^{2}\ll 4m_{0}^{2}

Now impose the hierarchy

\kappa^{2}\ll Q_{2}^{2}\ll 4m_{0}^{2},\qquad q_{2}^{2}=-Q_{2}^{2}.

(264)

In this regime the coefficient functions freeze to the constants in

\frac{3}{2}\,m_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,q_{2}^{2}\approx\frac{3}{2}\,m_{0}^{2}.

(266)

Therefore

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}(q_{2})\approx 2\left[p_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\,m_{0}^{2}\right]\frac{1}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}}\,A(t)-\left(\frac{3}{2}\,m_{0}^{2}\right)\frac{3}{16\kappa_{5}^{2}}\,A^{S}(t),\qquad t=q_{2}^{2}.}

; compare

M.12
Momentum-space hadron central charge

Factoring out
2p_{0}^{2}
from

267

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{\rm reg}(q_{2})\approx 2p_{0}^{2}\,\mathfrak{c}_{h}(t;p_{0},m_{0}),

with

\boxed{\mathfrak{c}_{h}(t;p_{0},m_{0})=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}}\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{3}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)A(t)-\frac{9}{32}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\,A^{S}(t)\right].}

(269)

Using the standard holographic normalization

2\kappa_{5}^{2}=16\pi G_{N}=\frac{8\pi^{2}}{N_{c}^{2}},\qquad\frac{1}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}}=\frac{N_{c}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}},

(270)

this becomes

\boxed{\mathfrak{c}_{h}(t;p_{0},m_{0})=\frac{N_{c}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{3}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)A(t)-\frac{9}{32}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\,A^{S}(t)\right].}

(271)

For separate partonic sectors

\boxed{\mathfrak{c}_{h,i}(t;p_{0},m_{0})\propto\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{3}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\right)A_{i}(t)-\frac{9}{32}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}\,A_{i}^{S}(t)\right].}

(272)

This is the sector-decomposed relation used in the main text.

Although the explicit derivation was carried out for a scalar target, the final answer depends only on the spin-2 and spin-0 hadronic GFFs. The step to the nucleon should therefore be read only as a basis-motivation and benchmark-ratio extrapolation. With that understanding, one makes the replacements

m_{0}\to M_{h},\qquad p_{0}\to E_{h},\qquad A(t)\to A_{h}(t),\qquad A^{S}(t)\to A_{h}^{S}(t),

(273)

to obtain

\boxed{\mathfrak{c}_{h,i}(t;E_{h},M_{h})\propto\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{3}\,\frac{M_{h}^{2}}{E_{h}^{2}}\right)A_{i}(t)-\frac{9}{32}\,\frac{M_{h}^{2}}{E_{h}^{2}}\,A_{i}^{S}(t)\right].}

(274)

The large-energy limit is then

\boxed{\mathfrak{c}_{h,i}(t;E_{h}\to\infty,M_{h})\propto A_{i}(t).}

(275)

Thus the integrated holographic response becomes spin-2 dominated at large energy, while the scalar/trace contribution is suppressed by
M_{h}^{2}/E_{h}^{2}

M.13
Exact spin-2 selection in the integrated TT sector

There is an additional structural simplification in the exact integrated kinematics. Consider a minimal Reggeized TT exchange of even spin
j

\mathcal{Q}_{00}^{(j)\,\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{j}}(q,\Delta)=\text{ST}\!\left[\Pi^{TT\,\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{}_{00}(\Delta)\,q^{\mu_{3}}\cdots q^{\mu_{j}}\right],

(276)

where

q=\frac{q_{2}}{2},\qquad\Delta=-q_{2},\qquad q=-\frac{\Delta}{2}.

(277)

Hence every extra factor of

\Delta

\boxed{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T00}^{(t,j,TT)}(q_{2})\Big|_{q_{1}=0}=0,\qquad j=4,6,8,\dots}

(278)

whereas
j=2
survives because there are no extra
q
-indices to kill. Thus the exact integrated TT sector is a pure spin-2 selector.
