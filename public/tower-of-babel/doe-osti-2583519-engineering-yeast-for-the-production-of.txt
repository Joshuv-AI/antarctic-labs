Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

NREL and Lygos have a mutual interest in engineering yeast for the production of acids and
esters as intermediates towards fuels or bioproducts. In particular, the yeast Pichia kudriavzevii
is a fantastic candidate for the production of acid and ester based products including butyric acid
and ethyl butyrate. However, the strain is relatively uncommon in the biotechnological space and
as such there are not an abundant amount of genetic tools that are publically available. However,
Lygos has been working with this species in particular for several years and has developed
genetic tools and physiological characteristics and are well versed in engineering this strain for
acid based products. While currently focusing on malonic acid-derived products, Lygos is not
currently targeting butyric acid and ethyl butyrate but have a long term interest in these
molecules and derivatives therein. This CRADA proposal seeks to leverage NREL’s immediate
focus on butyric acid and ethyl butyrate, and general bioprocess expertise with Lygos’ specific
knowledge on engineering P. kudriavzevii for the production of acid-based products.

Summary of Research Results:

During the formation of this CRADA, NREL researchers began pivoting to alternative yeast
strains and methods and ultimately decided not to utilize the strains and methods generated by
Lygos. Ultimately no work was performed on this CRADA, no material or money was
exchanged.

Task 1:

NREL will leverage yeast strains and genetic tools provided by Lygos to engineer the yeast for
the production of acid and ester based products. Initial efforts will focus on production of butyric
acid via the incorporation of multi-gene pathways leading to the baseline production of butyric
acid. Additional genetic modifications may be made in order to increase metabolic flux
including, for example, the disruption of genes required for ethanol production. (Estimated 2
years).

No work was performed on task 1.

Task 2:

Following the successful production of butyric acid, strains will be modified to co-produce
butyrylCoA and ethanol, which will serve as precursors to ethyl butyrate.

No work was performed on task 2.

Task 3:

Alcohol acetyltransferases will be next tested for their ability to produce ethyl butyrate from

will be assisted by ongoing technical input of Lygos.

No work was performed on task 3.
