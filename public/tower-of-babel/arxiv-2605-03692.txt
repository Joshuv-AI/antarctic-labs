Cyclicity via weak ∗ sequentially cyclicity in Radially weighted Besov spaces
Anusrika Datta, Stefan Richter

Abstract
A radially weighted Besov space H H is a space of holomorphic functions on the unit ball 𝔹 d ⊆ ℂ d \mathbb{B}_{d}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d} whose N N -th radial derivative is square integrable with respect to a given admissible radial measure. We write M ​ u ​ l ​ t ​ ( H ) Mult(H) for its multiplier algebra. The cyclic vectors in H H are those functions f f whose multiplier multiples are dense in H H . We call a multiplier f ∈ M ​ u ​ l ​ t ​ ( H ) f\in Mult(H) weak ∗ sequentially cyclic if its multiplier multiples are weak ∗ sequentially dense in M ​ u ​ l ​ t ​ ( H ) Mult(H) . It is immediate that every weak ∗ sequentially cyclic multiplier is cyclic, and it turns out that the two notions coincide whenever H H has the complete Pick property. However, in more general radially weighted Besov spaces there may be multipliers that are cyclic, but not weak ∗ sequentially cyclic. For bounded holomorphic functions f f with no zeros in 𝔹 d \mathbb{B}_{d} , we obtain a condition on log ⁡ f \log f that implies the cyclicity of f f in H H and yields invertibility properties for 1 / f 1/f within an associated Smirnov-type class. This condition is formulated in terms of weak ∗ sequentially cyclic multipliers and can often be verified using a comparison principle: if f , g ∈ M ​ u ​ l ​ t ​ ( H ) f,g\in Mult(H) satisfy | f | ≤ | g | |f|\leq|g| and if f f is weak ∗ sequentially cyclic, then g g is also weak ∗ sequentially cyclic. These results provide new insights into cyclicity phenomena in radially weighted Besov spaces in settings, where H H fails to be a complete Pick space.

Introduction

Cyclicity is a fundamental concept in the study of invariant subspaces of analytic function spaces and has deep connections with operator theory, factorization theory, and the function-theoretic properties of multipliers.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space of holomorphic functions on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{d}
, and let
\mathrm{Mult}(H)=\{\phi:\mathbb{B}_{d}\to\mathbb{C};\phi f\in H\text{ for all }f\in H\}
be its multiplier algebra. We have provided definitions of all required concepts in Section 2 and 3. For now just recall that a function
f\in H
is said to be
cyclic
, if the smallest closed multiplier invariant subspace generated by
f
coincides with
H
. The problem of characterizing cyclic vectors has been extensively studied in functional analysis. In the one-dimensional setting of the Hardy space of the unit disc, cyclic functions are precisely the outer functions,
[
7
]
. Extending this type of characterization to other spaces, such as the Bergman space and the Dirichlet space, has been an active area of research, see e.g.
[
12
]
,
[
11
]
. In these settings, cyclicity is related to the zero sets, boundary behavior, and the structure of invariant subspaces. In several variables, fewer results are available. If
d=2
, then in
[
15
]
the authors determined necessary and sufficient conditions for polynomials to be cyclic in weighted Dirichlet type spaces of the unit ball. These results have been generalized to certain to smooth functions and higher dimensions in
[
18
]
. For some general cyclicity conditions for the Drury–Arveson space
H^{2}_{d}
and more general weighted Besov spaces that have the so-called Pick property, we refer the reader to
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
. In this paper we will say that
H
is a Pick space, if there is a Hilbert space norm on
H
such that the corresponding reproducing kernel is a normalized complete Pick kernel, i.e.
k_{w}(z)=\frac{1}{1-\langle u(z),u(w)\rangle}
for some function
u
from
\mathbb{B}_{d}
into the unit ball of
\ell^{2}
. One of the advantages of Pick spaces is that frequently questions about functions in the space can be reduced to questions about functions in the multiplier algebra. In this paper our work will directly focus on the multiplier algebra, and we will establish results for spaces that don’t necesssarily have the Pick property. Thus, while our results are motivated by
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
, our theorems will be valid for weighted Besov spaces
B^{N}_{\omega}
that lie between the Drury–Arveson space
H^{2}_{d}
and the Hardy space
H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d})
a range of spaces that are known to not have the Pick property.

Central to our work is the concept of
weak
∗
sequential cyclicity
.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
\phi\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)
is said to be

weak
∗
sequentially cyclic
if there exists a sequence

\{u_{n}\}\subseteq\mathrm{Mult}(H)
such that

u_{n}\phi\xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{weak}^{\ast}\;}1.

The spaces
H=B^{N}_{\omega}
that we will consider, all contain their multipliers as a dense subset. In that case this notion is formally stronger than cyclicity: if
\phi\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
is
weak
∗
sequentially cyclic, then
\phi
is cyclic in

B^{N}_{\omega}
(see Lemma
4.1
). While the converse holds for complete Pick spaces (see Corollary
3.3
),
in Section
7
we will provide an
example showing that for
d\geq 2
there are
H^{\infty}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d})
-functions that are cyclic in
H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d})
, but
not weak
∗
sequentially cyclic in
H^{\infty}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d})
. Thus, the new concept naturally fits within the existing
framework, and it has the potential for additional structural control.

We will see that weak
∗
sequential cyclicity has many of the same elementary properties that cyclicity has: Under suitable
hypotheses weak
∗
sequential cyclicity is preserved under
products and quotients by weak
∗
sequentially cyclic multipliers.

We will now restrict our discussion to the weighted Besov spaces
H=B^{N}_{\omega}
. In that context we will show the following result, see Theorem
5.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
\phi,\psi\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
with
|\phi(z)|\leq|\psi(z)|
for all
z\in\mathbb{B}_{d}
, and if
\phi
is weak
∗
sequentially cyclic, then so is
\psi
.

If
B^{N}_{\omega}
happens to be a Pick space, then this result also follows from the equivalence of the cyclicity concepts mentioned above and Theorem ??? of
[

In
[

the authors defined the Pick-Smirnov class of a Pick space
H
by

N^{+}(H)=\left\{\frac{u}{v}\;:\;u,v\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega}),\ \text{and }v\text{ is cyclic in }H\right\}.

Analogously, we define

N^{+}_{\ast}(B^{N}_{\omega})=\left\{\frac{u}{v}\;:\;u,v\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega}),\ \text{and }v\text{ is weak${}^{\ast}$ sequentially cyclic}\right\}

(1.1)

Thus,

with equality, whenever

is a Pick space. Since cyclic functions have no zeros, it is clear that functions in

are analytic in

. Furthermore, it is elementary to show that a multiplier

We will see that if

has no zeros in

is a ratio of multipliers with no zeros in

is in the Pick-Nevanlinna class. Additionally, if such

is weak

-sequentially cyclic, then
\log\phi\in N^{+}_{\ast}(B^{N}_{\omega})
.
Our main result is a weak converse of this.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

and assume

has no zeros in

\mathbb{B}_{d}
and has bounded argument.

If

then

is cyclic in

\frac{1}{f}\in N^{+}(B^{N}_{\omega})
and

This will be Theorem

is cyclic in

\log f\in N^{+}(B^{N}_{\omega})
.
In particular, in
[

2
The weighted Besov spaces
B^{N}_{\omega}

Let
d
be a positive integer, and consider the open unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{d}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Let
Hol(\mathbb{B}_{d})
denote the space of holomorphic functions by on
\mathbb{B}_{d}
. We define a
radially weighted Besov space
associated with a radial measure
\omega
and a nonnegative integer
N
by

B^{N}_{\omega}:=\bigl\{f\in Hol(\mathbb{B}_{d}):R^{N}f\in L^{2}(\omega)\bigr\},

where
R
is the radial derivative operator

R=\sum_{j=1}^{d}z_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}

and
\omega
is assumed to be an admissible radial measure on
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{d}}
. This means it can be expressed as

d\omega(z)=d\mu(r)\,d\sigma(w),\quad z=rw,

with
\sigma
the normalized rotation-invariant measure on the sphere
\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}
, and
\mu
a Borel measure on
[0,1]
such that

\mu((r,1])>0\quad\text{for all }0<r<1.

Remark.
If
\mu
has a point mass at
r=1
, then we define the
L^{2}(\omega)
-norm of a function
f\in Hol(\mathbb{B}_{d})
by

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{d}}|f|^{2}\,d\omega+\mu(\{1\})\,\|f\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d})},

where
H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d})
denotes the Hardy space of holomorphic functions
f
satisfying

\|f\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d})}=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}}|f(rz)|^{2}\,d\sigma(z)=\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}}|f(rz)|^{2}\,d\sigma(z)<\infty.

The space
B^{N}_{\omega}
is equipped with the norm

\|f\|^{2}_{B^{N}_{\omega}}=\begin{cases}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)},&N=0,\\[5.0pt]
\omega(\mathbb{B}_{d})\,|f(0)|^{2}+\|R^{N}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)},&N>0.\end{cases}

Under these definitions,
B^{N}_{\omega}
becomes a Hilbert function space over
\mathbb{B}_{d}
, in the sense that point evaluations are continuous linear functionals.

For example if
d\omega=d\sigma\,d\delta_{1}
. Then the scale of spaces

\{B^{N}_{\omega}\}_{N\geq 0}
satisfies the following inclusions,
according to the value of
N
relative to
\frac{d-1}{2}
, see
[
5
]
.

The Pick space case (
N\geq\frac{d-1}{2}
).

In this range,
B^{N}_{\omega}
is a Pick space and we have

B^{N}_{\omega}\subseteq H^{2}_{d}.

The Non-Pick space case (
0\leq N<\frac{d-1}{2}
).

In this case,
B^{N}_{\omega}
is not a Pick space and we obtain

H^{2}_{d}\subseteq B^{N}_{\omega}\subseteq B^{0}_{\omega}=H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}).

In the present work, our main interest is on those spaces which are not Pick spaces, although all of the results we obtain also hold in the setting of Pick spaces. In fact, as mentioned in the Introduction, for Pick spaces more general results have been established in
[
4
,
5
]
.

We will use the following two theorems from
[
5
]
:

Theorem 2.1

(Theorem 4.2 in
[
5
]
)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega
be an admissible radial measure.
There exist constants
C>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that whenever
\phi,\psi\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
with

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\frac{\phi}{\psi}\right)\geq 0,

then

\psi^{n}e^{-\frac{\phi}{\psi}}\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega}),

and

\left\|\psi^{n}e^{-\frac{\phi}{\psi}}\right\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}\leq C\left(\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}+\|\phi\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}\right)^{n}.

Theorem 2.2

(Lemma 4.1(b) in
[
5
]
)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\omega
be an admissible radial measure.

If
\phi\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
is zero-free in
\mathbb{B}_{d}
and
j
is a non-negative integer such that

\phi^{j}\log\phi\in H^{\infty},

then there exists an integer
n\geq j
such that

\phi^{n}\log\phi\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega}).

3
Multipliers and weak
\ast
closed ideals

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{B}_{d}
. It is clear that linear combinations of certain countable sets of reproducing kernels are dense in
H
, hence
H
is separable. Since
H
is separable, the trace class operators on
H
are a separable predual of
\mathcal{B}(H)
, the algebra of all bounded linear transformations on
H
. We write
\mathrm{Mult}(H)
for the multiplier algebra of
H
. Then each element
\phi
of
\mathrm{Mult}(H)
induces a bounded multiplication operator
M_{\phi}\in\mathcal{B}(H)
and
\mathrm{Mult}(H)
becomes a Banach space with norm
\|\phi\|_{Mult(H)}=\|M_{\phi}\|_{\mathcal{B}(H)}=\sup\bigl\{\|\phi f\|:f\in H,\,\|f\|\leq 1\bigr\}.

\mathrm{Mult}(H)
is thus isometrically isomorphic to a weak
\ast
-closed subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(H)
, and it can be identified with the dual of a separable Banach space. As such, it carries a weak
\ast
-topology, which agrees with the weak
\ast
-topology of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
restricted to
\mathrm{Mult}(H)
. One easily checks that point evaluations on
\mathbb{B}_{d}
are weak
\ast
-continuous linear functionals on
\mathrm{Mult}(H)
. Then it is a standard fact that a sequence of multipliers
\phi_{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)
converges in the weak
\ast
-topology to
\phi\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)
, if and only if
\phi_{n}(z)\to\phi(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{B}_{d}
and there is
M>0
such that
\|\phi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(H)}\leq M
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, (
[
8
]
, Proposition-2).

If
\phi\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)
, then
\mathrm{Mult}(H)\phi=\{u\phi:u\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)\}
and we write

[\phi]_{*}=\overline{\mathrm{Mult}(H)\phi}^{wk*}\subseteq\mathrm{Mult}(H)

for the weak
\ast
-closed ideal generated by
\phi
. We have been unable to answer the following question in all generality.

Question 3.1
.

If
\phi,\psi\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)
such that
\psi\in[\phi]_{*}
, then is there a sequence
u_{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)
such that
u_{n}\phi\to\psi
in the weak
\ast
-topology?

If
\mathcal{H}
has a normalized Pick kernel, then the answer is yes. That can be deduced from a result of Davidson, Ramsey, and Shalit,
[
10
]
, Corollary 2.7, which says that for Pick spaces
H
the weak
\ast
-closed ideals in
\mathrm{Mult}(H)
are in 1-1 correspondence with the multiplier invariant subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
. See Corollary
3.3
below for a more direct proof that is based on Lemma 3.3 of
[
3
]
. However, we do not know the answer for example for
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{d})
, if
d\geq 2
. Thus, it remains unclear under which conditions a multiplier
\phi
that satisfies
[\phi]_{*}=\mathrm{Mult}(H)
is weak
\ast
sequentially cyclic.

Let
\mathcal{S}(\phi)
be the weak
\ast
-sequential closure of
\mathrm{Mult}(H)\phi
. Then it follows from the Krein-Smulian Theorem (see
[
9
]
), Corollary V.12.7) that
\mathcal{S}(\phi)
is weak
\ast
-closed, if and only if it is weak
\ast
-sequentially closed. Thus, the above Question is equivalent to the question of whether
\mathcal{S}(\phi)
is weak
\ast
-sequentially closed. The structure of
\mathrm{Mult}(H)
can be used to restate the question.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\phi\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)
. Then

\mathcal{S}(\phi)=[\phi]_{*}

if and only if there is
c>0
such that for all
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\phi)
with
\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(H)}\leq 1
there is a sequence
u_{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)
such that
\|u_{n}\phi\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(H)}\leq c
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
u_{n}(z)\phi(z)\to\psi(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{B}_{d}
.

Proof.

The Lemma is a strengthening of one of the directions of the equivalence of (a) and (c) of Theorem V.12.11 of
[
9
]
. Indeed, if
\mathcal{S}(\phi)=[\phi]_{*}
, then
\mathrm{Mult}(H)\phi
is sequentially dense in
[\phi]_{*}
, hence
(a)\Rightarrow(c)
of Theorem V.12.11 of
[
9
]
implies the conclusion as stated in the Lemma.

Conversely, assume that there is
c>0
such that for all
\psi
in the unit ball of
\mathcal{S}(\phi)
there is a sequence
u_{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)
such that
u_{n}(z)\phi(z)\to\psi(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{B}_{d}
and
\|u_{n}\phi\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(H)}\leq c
for all
n
.

By what was stated before the Lemma, we have to show that
\mathcal{S}(\phi)
is weak
\ast
-sequentially closed. Thus, let
\psi_{n}\in\mathcal{S}(\phi)
and
\psi\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)
such that
\psi_{n}\to\psi
in the weak
\ast
-topology. Thus, there is
M>0
such that
\|\psi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(H)}\leq M
for all
n
and
\psi_{n}\to\psi
pointwise in
\mathbb{B}_{d}
. Choose a sequence of positive reals
1>r_{n}\to 1
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By our hypothesis and a scaling argument there is a sequence
\{u_{nk}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)
such that
\|u_{nk}\phi\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(H)}\leq cM
and
u_{nk}\phi\to\psi_{n}
pointwise in
\mathbb{B}_{d}
as
k\to\infty
.
The unit ball in
\mathrm{Mult}(H)
is a normal family, hence the pointwise convergence in
\mathbb{B}_{d}
for norm bounded sequences is equivalent to local uniform convergence. Thus, there is
k_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|u_{nk_{n}}(z)\phi(z)-\psi_{n}(z)|<1/n
for all
z\in r_{n}\mathbb{B}_{d}
.

Then
u_{nk_{n}}\phi\to\psi
in the weak
\ast
-topology. Indeed, we already noted the uniform boundedness of the norms of
u_{nk_{n}}\phi
, and if
z\in\mathbb{B}_{d}
, then for any
n
such that
|z|<r_{n}
we have

|u_{nk_{n}}\phi(z)-\psi(z)|\leq\frac{1}{n}+|\psi_{n}(z)-\psi(z)|.

Thus,
u_{nk_{n}}\phi\to\psi
pointwise in
\mathbb{B}_{d}
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

If
H\subseteq\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{B}_{d})
has a normalized Pick kernel, then
\mathcal{S}(\phi)=[\phi]_{*}
for all
\phi\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)
. Thus,
\phi
is weak
\ast
sequentially cyclic, if and only if
1\in[\phi]_{\ast}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathrm{Mult}(H)
, then
\phi\in H
since
H
has a normalized Pick kernel. Hence we define
[\phi]
to be the closure in
H
of
\mathrm{Mult}(H)\phi
. Since weak
\ast
-convergent sequences in
\mathrm{Mult}(H)
are weakly convergent in
H
, and
[\phi]
is weakly closed, it follows that
\mathcal{S}(\phi)\subseteq\mathrm{Mult}(H)\cap[\phi]
. Thus, Lemma 3.3 of
[
3
]
implies that
\phi
satisfies the condition of Lemma
3.2
with
c=1
.
∎

Notice that Lemma 3.3 of
[
3
]
also implies that if
H
is a Pick space, then multipliers that are cyclic in
H
must be weak
\ast
sequentially cyclic.

4
Some preliminary results

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\phi\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
. If
1\in[\phi]_{\ast}
, then
\phi
is cyclic in
B^{N}_{\omega}
.

Consequently weak
∗
sequentially cyclic multipliers are cyclic in
B^{N}_{\omega}
.

Proof.

Suppose
1\in[\phi]_{\ast}
, then there is a net of multipliers
\{\psi_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma}
such that
\psi_{\gamma}\phi\to 1
in the weak
\ast
topology of
\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
. If
g\in B^{N}_{\omega}
, then
L(\psi)=\langle\psi,g\rangle_{B^{N}_{\omega}}=\langle M_{\psi}1,g\rangle_{B^{N}_{\omega}}
defines a weak
\ast
-continuous linear functional on
\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
. Hence

\langle\psi_{\gamma}\phi,g\rangle_{B^{N}_{\omega}}=L(\psi_{\gamma}\phi)\to L(1)=\langle 1,g\rangle_{B^{N}_{\omega}},

i.e.
\psi_{\gamma}\phi\to 1
weakly in
B^{N}_{\omega}
. We write
[\phi]
for the smallest invariant subspace of
B^{N}_{\omega}
that contains
\phi
. By standard Banach space theory it agrees with the weak closure of the multiplier multiples of
\phi
, hence
1\in[\phi]
, and we conclude that
\phi
is cyclic in
B^{N}_{\omega}
.
∎

We know the following lemma is true, see
[
8
]
, Proposition-11.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
f\in B^{N}_{\omega}
and
g\in Mult(B^{N}_{\omega})
, then
gf
is cyclic if and only if both
f
and
g
are cyclic.

A similar result holds for weak
∗
sequentially cyclicity.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f,g\in Mult(B^{N}_{\omega})
. Then
fg
is weak
∗
sequentially cyclic if and only if both
f
and
g
are weak
∗
sequentially cyclic.

Proof.

First, assume that
fg
is weak
∗
sequentially cyclic. Then there exists a sequence
\{p_{n}\}\subseteq Mult(B^{N}_{\omega})
such that

p_{n}(fg)\to 1\quad\text{weak$\ast$}.

This implies
(p_{n}f)g\to 1
weak
\ast
. By hypothesis,
p_{n}f\in Mult(B^{N}_{\omega})
, so
g
is weak
∗
sequentially cyclic.

Also, by symmetry,
f
is weak
∗
sequentially cyclic as well.

Conversely, assume that
f
and
g
are both weak
∗
sequentially cyclic in
B^{N}_{\omega}
. Then there exist sequences
(p_{k}),(q_{k})\subseteq Mult(B^{N}_{\omega})
such that

p_{k}f\to 1\quad\text{weak$\ast$},\quad q_{k}g\to 1\quad\text{weak$\ast$}.

There exist constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
such that

\|p_{k}f\|_{Mult(B^{N}_{\omega})}\leq C,\quad\|q_{k}g\|_{Mult(B^{N}_{\omega})}\leq C^{\prime}.

Hence,

(q_{k}p_{k})(fg)\to 1\quad\text{pointwise},\quad\|(q_{k}p_{k})(fg)\|_{Mult(B^{N}_{\omega})}\leq M,

for some
M=CC^{\prime}>0
. Therefore,
fg
is weak
∗
sequentially cyclic.
∎

, hence
\phi
must be sequentially
\ast
-cyclic. The other direction is trivial.∎

The comparison principle

Theorem 5.1
.

If
f,g\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
satisfy
|f|\leq|g|
, and if
f
is weak
∗
sequentially cyclic, then
g
is weak
∗
sequentially cyclic.

Proof.

Since
f,g\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
with
|f|\leq|g|
, by Theorem 3.2 in
[
4
]
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\frac{f^{n}}{g}\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega}).

Because
f
is weak
∗
sequentially cyclic, there exists a sequence
\{\phi_{k}\}\subseteq\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
such that

\phi_{k}f\to 1\quad\text{weak}^{\ast}.

Hence,

(\phi_{k}f)^{n}\to 1\quad\text{weak}^{\ast}.

This implies that

\left(\phi_{k}^{\,n}\frac{f^{n}}{g}\right)g=\phi_{k}^{\,n}f^{n}\to 1\quad\text{weak}^{\ast}.

Observe that

\phi_{k}^{\,n}\frac{f^{n}}{g}\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega}).

Therefore,

is weak

sequentially cyclic.
∎

6
Theorems for
\log f

We are interested in cyclic functions, thus we will always want to assume that
\phi(z)\neq 0
in
\mathbb{B}_{d}
. Then
\phi
has an analytic logarithm in
\mathbb{B}_{d}
and saying that
\phi
has bounded argument is equivalent to saying that
g=\log\phi
has bounded imaginary part. In
[
5
]
it was shown that polynomials without zeros in the ball always have bounded argument. In a single variable we know that functions with bounded real, hence they satisfy an inequality of the type
|g(z)|\leq c\log\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}
(See
[
17
]
, Page 294 - ex:9 and 10). By considering slice functions, the same conclusion holds for functions in
\mathbb{B}_{d}
and we conclude that if
\phi
has bounded argument, then there is
c>0
such that
|\phi(z)|\geq A{(1-|z|)^{c}}
for all
z\in\mathbb{B}_{d}
.

We start with the following observation.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\phi\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
have no zeros in
\mathbb{B}_{d}
and bounded argument.
Then there are
\psi\in\Mult(B^{N}_{\omega})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\log\phi=\frac{\psi}{\phi^{n}}
.

Consequently, if
\phi
is weak
\ast
-sequentially cyclic, then
\log\phi\in N^{+}_{\ast}(B^{N}_{\omega})
.

Proof.

Since multipliers are bounded, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|\phi(z)\log|\phi(z)||\leq C\quad\text{for all }z\in B^{N}_{\omega}.

By hypothesis,
\phi
has bounded argument, so there exists
A>0
with

|\operatorname{Im}(\log\phi(z))|\leq A\quad\text{for all }z\in B^{N}_{\omega}.

Thus,

|\phi(z)\log\phi(z)|\leq|\phi(z)|\log|\phi(z)|+|\phi(z)||\arg\phi(z)|\leq C+A\|\phi\|_{\infty}<C^{\prime},

for some constant
C^{\prime}>0
. This implies
\phi\log\phi\in H^{\infty}
.

Hence by Theorem
2.2
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\psi:=\phi^{n}\log\phi\in Mult(B^{N}_{\omega}).

Thus,
\log\phi=\frac{\psi}{\phi^{n}}
.
Now assume that
\phi
is weak
\ast
-sequentially cyclic, then by Corollary
4.4
the function
\phi^{n}
is weak
\ast
-sequentially cyclic, hence
\log\phi\in N^{+}_{\ast}(B^{N}_{\omega})
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
f\in B^{N}_{\omega}\cap H^{\infty}
and assume that there are
u,v\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
such that

1.

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
,

2.

f=e^{-\frac{u}{v}}
,

3.

f
has bounded argument, and

4.

v
is weak
\ast
sequentially cyclic.

Then there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
v^{n}f\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
and

[v^{n}f]_{*}=Mult(B^{N}_{\omega}).

Proof.

Fix
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
. Since
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, we have

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\alpha\frac{u}{v}\right)\geq 0.

By Theorem 2.1, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
C>0
such that

v^{n}f^{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
with

\displaystyle\|v^{n}f^{\alpha}\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\alpha u\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}+\|v\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})})^{n}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}+\|v\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})})^{n}

(6.1)

Since
v
is weak
∗
sequentially cyclic, there exists a sequence
\{\phi_{j}\}\subseteq\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
such that

\phi_{j}v\to 1\quad\text{pointwise}

and there is
M>0
such that

\|\phi_{j}v\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}\leq M

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, because
f
has bounded argument, there exists
A>0
such that

|\operatorname{Im}\frac{u}{v}|=|\operatorname{Im}\log f|\leq A.

Note that

\|\phi_{j}v\|_{\infty}\leq\|\phi_{j}v\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}\leq M.

Since
\phi_{j}v(z)\to 1
we must have
M\geq 1
.
Set

\gamma=\frac{1}{M}>0.

Claim:

v^{n}f^{\alpha(1-\gamma)}\in[v^{n}f^{\alpha}]_{\ast}
.

Note that
\psi_{j}=e^{\alpha\gamma\phi_{j}u}\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
. We will establish the claim by showing that
\psi_{j}v^{n}f^{\alpha}\to v^{n}f^{\alpha(1-\gamma)}
in the weak
\ast
topology of
\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
.

Since

\psi_{j}f^{\alpha}=e^{-\alpha\frac{u}{v}(1-\gamma\phi_{j}v)}

the pointwise convergence follows from the pointwise convergence of
\phi_{j}v\to 1
. We have to prove that
\|\psi_{j}v^{n}f^{\alpha}\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}
is bounded independently of
j
.

For all
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\operatorname{Re}(1-\gamma\phi_{j}v(z))\geq 0\ \text{ and }\ \ |\operatorname{Im}(1-\gamma\phi_{j}v(z))|\leq 1.

This implies

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\frac{u}{v}(1-\gamma\phi_{j}v)\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)\operatorname{Re}(1-\gamma\phi_{j}v)-\operatorname{Im}\!\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)\operatorname{Im}(1-\gamma\phi_{j}v)

\displaystyle\geq-\,A.

Hence

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\alpha\frac{u}{v}(1-\gamma\phi_{j}v)+\alpha\frac{Av}{v}\right)\geq 0.

Now apply Theorem 2.1 again, this time with

\phi=\alpha u(1-\gamma\phi_{j}v)+\alpha Av\text{ and }\psi=v.

Note that the
n
and
C
in Theorem
2.1
do not depend on
\phi
,
\psi
, or
\alpha
. Thus

\displaystyle\|\psi_{j}f^{\alpha}\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}

\displaystyle=e^{\alpha A}\left\|v^{n}e^{-\frac{\alpha u(1-\gamma\phi_{j}v)+\alpha Av}{v}}\right\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\alpha A}\left(\|v\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}+\|\alpha u(1-\gamma\phi_{j}v)+\alpha Av\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\alpha A}\left(\|v\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}(1+A)+(1+\gamma M)\|u\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}\right)^{n}<\infty.

This establishes the Claim.

For
k=0,1,\dots
let
\alpha_{k}=(1-\gamma)^{k}.
Then the Claim implies that for each
k
we have
v^{n}f^{\alpha_{k+1}}\in[v^{n}f^{\alpha_{k}}]_{\ast}
.
Since for each
\phi\in\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
the subspace
[\phi]_{\ast}
is multiplier invariant, we obtain inductively that
[v^{n}f^{\alpha_{k}}]_{\ast}\subseteq[v^{n}f]_{\ast}
for all
k
. But
\alpha_{k}\to 0
and
\|v^{n}f^{\alpha_{k}}\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})}
is bounded independently of
k
by inequality (
6
). Thus,
v^{n}f^{\alpha_{k}}\to v^{n}
weak
\ast
, hence
[v^{n}]_{\ast}\subseteq[v^{n}f]_{\ast}
. But now Corollary
4.4
implies that
[v^{n}]_{\ast}=\mathrm{Mult}(B^{N}_{\omega})
and this concludes the proof of the Lemma.
∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
f\in B^{N}_{\omega}\cap H^{\infty}
and assume that
f
has no zeros inside
\mathbb{B}_{d}
and has bounded argument. If

\log f\in N^{+}_{*}(B^{N}_{\omega}),

then

is cyclic in

.

f\in N^{+}_{*}(B^{N}_{\omega})\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{f}\in N^{+}(B^{N}_{\omega}).

Let
f
be as in the hypothesis of the theorem. We may assume that

. Then

satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
6.2
and we conclude that there is a

and a weak

7
Example: Cyclicity Does Not Imply
\ast
-Cyclicity

Let
S(z)
be a non-trivial singular inner function that is cyclic in the Bergman space of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
,

L^{2}_{a}=\{f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D}):\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f|^{2}\frac{dxdy}{\pi}<\infty\}.

Such a function exists, see
[
13
,
14
,
16
]
). Note that
\mathrm{Mult}(H^{2}(dB))=H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{d})
.

Theorem 7.1
.

The function
f(z,w)=S(z)\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{2})
is cyclic in
H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{2})
, but it is not weak
\ast
sequentially cyclic in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{2})
.

Proof.

Using multinomial notation the reproducing kernel for
H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{2})
is

k_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{1}{(1-\langle z,\lambda\rangle)^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)\sum_{|\alpha|=n}\frac{n!}{\alpha!}\overline{\lambda}^{\alpha}z^{\alpha}.

Hence if
h\in\mathrm{Hol}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{2})
has Taylor series

h(z,w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=n}\hat{h}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})z^{\alpha_{1}}w^{\alpha_{2}},

then for
g(z)=h(z,0)
we have

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{2})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=n}\frac{\alpha_{1}!\alpha_{2}!}{n!}|\hat{h}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}|\hat{h}(n,0)|^{2}=\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{a}},

and equality holds, whenever
h(z,0)=h(z,w)
for all
(z,w)\in\mathbb{B}_{2}
.

Since
S
is cyclic in
L^{2}_{a}
, there is a sequence of polynomials
\{p_{n}(z)\}
such that

\|p_{n}S-1\|_{L^{2}_{a}}\longrightarrow 0.

Then with
q_{n}(z,w)=p_{n}(z)
we have
\|q_{n}f-1\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{2})}=\|p_{n}S-1\|_{L^{2}_{a}}\longrightarrow 0
, hence
f
is cyclic in
H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{2})
.

Now suppose, for contradiction, that
f
is also weak
\ast
sequentially cyclic in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{2})
. Then there exists a sequence of polynomials
\{q_{n}(z,w)\}
such that

q_{n}f\longrightarrow 1\quad\text{in the weak}^{\ast}\text{ topology of }H^{\infty}(\{|z|^{2}+|w|^{2}<1\}).

Write
p_{n}(z)=q_{n}(z,0)
and observe that

\sup_{|z|^{2}+|w|^{2}<1}|q_{n}(z,w)f(z,w)|\geq\sup_{|z|<1}|q_{n}(z,0)f(z,0)|=\sup_{|z|<1}|p_{n}(z)S(z)|.

Hence,
p_{n}S\to 1
in the weak
\ast
sense on
\mathbb{D}=\{|z|<1\}
.
This implies that
S
is weak
\ast
-sequentially cyclic in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

However, in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
cyclicity and weak
\ast
sequential cyclicity are equivalent notions. Therefore
S
would be cyclic in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. But in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, the cyclic functions are precisely the outer functions, and a non-trivial singular inner function cannot be outer. This is a contradiction.

Thus,
f
is cyclic in
H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{2})
, but it is not weak
\ast
sequentially cyclic.
∎

Similarly, one obtains examples of this type for any dimension
d
. Indeed, the singular inner function
S
used above is cyclic in any weighted Bergman space of the unit disc with standard weights
(1-|z|^{2})^{\gamma}dxdy
,
\gamma>-1
. Then we can define

f(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{d})=S(z_{1}).

Repeating the above argument will show that
f
is cyclic, but not weak
\ast
sequentially cyclic in
H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d})
.

in the norm of

\displaystyle\leq\|\frac{1-b_{n}}{1-r_{n}b_{n}}\psi\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(H)}+\|\frac{a_{n}}{1-r_{n}b_{n}}\|_{\mathrm{Mult}(H)}
