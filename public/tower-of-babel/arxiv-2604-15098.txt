Tangent bundle of punctual Hilbert scheme and distinguishing products of varieties
Supravat Sarkar

Abstract
. We describe the indecomposable components of the tangent bundle of the punctual Hilbert scheme of a smooth projective surface. As an application, we prove a recent conjecture about classification of products of punctual Hilbert schemes of smooth projective surfaces. We also determine when two products of symmetric powers of a smooth variety can be isomorphic.



Keywords
: Punctual Hilbert scheme, tangent bundle, indecomposable vector bundle

MSC Number: 14F06, 14C05

1.
Introduction

We work throughout over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers. Algebraic varieties will always be assumed to be integral. For a smooth projective surface
X
and a positive integer
n
, the punctual Hilbert scheme
X^{[n]}
parametrizing length
n
subschemes of
X
is a widely studied object.
[
11
]
showed
X^{[n]}
is a smooth projective variety of dimension
2n
. For
X
a
K3
surface,
X^{[n]}
becomes a hyperkähler manifold. For
X
an abelian surface, one gets a hyperkähler manifold
Kum_{n}(X)
closely related to
X^{[n]}
.

Several properties of
X^{[n]}
are studied throughout the literature. Automorphisms of
X^{[n]}
are studied by several authors, for example
[
7
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
3
]
. Another direction of exploring
X^{[n]}
is to study vector bundles on
X^{[n]}
. This has been pursued for the so-called tautological bundles in
[
28
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
among others.

For any smooth projective variety
Y
, a very natural vector bundle on
Y
is the tangent bundle, which we will always denote by
T_{Y}
. Study of
T_{Y}
often gives important geometric information about
Y
, as can be seen, for example, from the famous paper
[
19
]
of Mori. Tangent bundles of varieties has been studied from several viewpoints, for example in
[
18
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
27
]
and
[
9
]
.

One of the goals of this article is to study tangent bundle of
X^{[n]}
for a smooth projective surface
X
. By
[
2
, Theorem 3]
, every vector bundle on a projective variety can be written as a direct sum of indecomposable vector bundles, which are uniquely determined upto isomorphism. Here a vector bundle is called
indecomposable
if it is not a direct sum of two nonzero vector bundles. Our result describes these indecomposable components of
T_{X^{[n]}}.
To state the result, we need the following notation: let
\mathcal{C}
be the class of smooth projective surfaces
X
which are of the form
(D\,\times\,Y)/G
, where
D
is an elliptic curve,
Y
a smooth curve of general type, and
G
is a finite subgroup of
\text{Aut}^{0}(D)
acting diagonally on
D\times Y.
This is what was called class
E
in
[
12
, Theorem D]
. In this case,
X
is minimal of Kodaira dimension
1
, and the Iitaka fibration of
X
is an isotrivial elliptic fibration. Call a smooth projective surface
X
to be of class
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
if
X
is either of class
\mathcal{C}
, or a bielliptic surface, or a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over an elliptic curve with
T_{X}
split, that is, direct sum of two line bundles. By
[
13
]
, roughly speaking, this last possibility actually happens very frequently.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface,
n\geq 2
an integer. Then the following holds:

(1)

If
X
is an abelian surface, then

T_{X^{[n]}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X^{[n]}}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathcal{E}

for an indecomposable vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
.

(2)

If
X
is of class
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
., then

T_{X^{[n]}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X^{[n]}}\oplus\mathcal{E}

for an indecomposable vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
.

(3)

In all other cases,
T_{X^{[n]}}
is indecomposable.

Further, in
(1)
and
(2)
, there is a smooth morphism
\pi:X^{[n]}\to F^{\prime}
with
F^{\prime}
an abelian variety such that
\mathcal{E}=\ker\!\left(T_{X^{[n]}}\xrightarrow{d\pi}\pi^{*}T_{F^{\prime}}\right).

The idea of the proof of Theorem
A
is as follows. Let
S_{n}
be the symmetric group on
n
letters, acting on
X^{n}
by permuting the factors. Given a direct sum decomposition of
T_{X^{[n]}}
, we can obtain a direct sum decomposition of
T_{X^{n}}
by
S_{n}
-invariant subbundles, as we explain in the beginning of §5. In §4, we show such direct sum decomposition of
T_{X^{n}}
has very limited possiblities. Finally, in §5, we show most of those possibilities do not arise from a direct sum decomposition of
T_{X^{[n]}}
. Along the way, we need to translate the conditions that
X
is an abelian surface or of class
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
in terms of splitting of
T_{X}
. This we do in Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
in §3, which are interesting in their own right.

Next, we turn to the following question:

Question 1
.

Suppose
X_{1},\cdots,X_{r},Y_{1}\cdots Y_{s}
are varieties such that

\prod_{i}X_{i}\cong\prod_{j}Y_{j}.

Can we conclude that
r=s
and upto renumbering
X_{i}\cong Y_{i}
for all
i
?

Of course without any assumption on the varieties the answer to the question is negative, as the varieties themselves can be products. Even when the varieties are smooth projective and indecomposable, that is, not a nontrivial product, the answer can be negative, as one can see by choosing the varieties to be a suitable abelian varieties. In general when there is no obvious counterexample, this question is very subtle, especially when the varieties are not proper. For example, the famous Zariski cancellation problem is an instance of this question, see
[
14
]
. In the case the varieties are projective, indecomposable with irregularity
0
, the question has an affirmative answer, essentially by the same proof as of
[
15
, Theorem 2.7, Corollary 2.8]
. When the varieties are smooth projective indecomposable with ample canonical class, the question has an affirmative answer due to
[
8
, Theorem 4.2]
.

As an application of Theorem
A
, we give an affirmative answer to Question
1
when the varieties are punctual Hilbert schemes of a fixed smooth projective surface. This proves a recent conjecture in
[
10
]
about classification of products of Hilbert schemes, and extends the corresponding result for Hilbert scheme of points on a smooth projective curve as proved in
[
20
]
.

Theorem B
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface, and
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}
,
b_{1},\dots,b_{s}
positive integers.
Suppose

\prod_{i}X^{[a_{i}]}\cong\prod_{j}X^{[b_{j}]}.

Then
r=s
and

\{a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{s}\}

as multisets.

Next, we give an affirmative answer to question
1
when the varieties are symmetric powers of a fixed smooth variety of any dimension (not necessarily proper).

Theorem C
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety of dimension
\geq 2
, and suppose

\prod_{i}X^{(a_{i})}\cong\prod_{j}X^{(b_{j})}.

Then
r=s
and
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{s}\}

as multisets.

We prove Theorem
A
in §6, and Theorem
B
and
C
in §7.

\pi_{i}^{*}T_{X_{i}}
as a direct summand subbundle of

via the natural isomorphism
T_{Y}\cong\oplus_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}T_{X_{i}}.

•

For a group
G
acting on a complex manifold
Y
, we say the action is a

if
G
is the group of deck transformations of a holomorpic covering space
Y\to X.

•

For a variety
X
, we denote the singular locus of
X
by
\mathrm{Sing}(X).

•

For positive integer
n
,
\mathbb{D}^{n}

\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\big||z_{i}|<1\text{ for all }i\}.
We abbreviate
\mathbb{D}^{1}

\mathbb{D}.

For a smooth projective variety
X
, the canonical bundle of
X
will be denoted by
K_{X}.

3.
Splitting of tangent bundle of a surface

The goal of this section is to prove Theorems
3.2
and
3.3
. We start with the following Lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
C_{1},C_{2},C_{1}^{\prime},C_{2}^{\prime}\in\{\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{D}\}
,
not all equal to
\mathbb{C}
. Let

C_{1}\times C_{2}\xrightarrow{\varphi}C_{1}^{\prime}\times C_{2}^{\prime}

be an isomorphism. Then there exists
i,j\in\{1,2\}
such that

p_{i}^{*}T_{C_{i}}\cong\varphi^{*}q_{j}^{*}T_{C_{j}^{\prime}}

as subbundles of
T_{C_{1}\times C_{2}}
, where
p_{i}:C_{1}\times C_{2}\to C_{i}
and
q_{j}:C_{1}^{\prime}\times C_{2}^{\prime}\to C_{j}^{\prime}
are the projections.

Proof.

Suppose first that there is
i,j
with no nonconstant map
C_{i}\to C_{j}^{\prime}
.
Without loss of generality assume
i=2
,
j=1
. Then it is easy to see that there is an isomorphism
C_{1}\xrightarrow{\phi_{1}}C_{1}^{\prime}

such that the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{1}\times C_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{1}^{\prime}\times C_{2}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{1}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{1}}

.

commutes.

In this case,

p_{2}^{*}T_{C_{2}}\cong\varphi^{*}q_{2}^{*}T_{C_{2}^{\prime}}.

Now suppose for all
i,j
there are nonconstant maps
C_{i}\to C_{j}^{\prime}
and
C_{j}^{\prime}\to C_{i}
(by symmetry). In this case one clearly gets
C_{1}=C_{2}=C_{1}^{\prime}=C_{2}^{\prime}=\mathbb{D}\text{ or }\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Call it
C.

In this case
\varphi
is a composition of permutations of the factors
and automorphisms of each factor. For
C=\mathbb{D}
this is proved in

[
24
, Page 167]
, and for

C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
this is elementary, see
[
5
]
for example. The conclusion follows.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface whose tangent bundle is trivial upto a line bundle twist. Then
X
is an abelian surface.

Proof.

Let

T_{X}=L\oplus M,

where
L,M
are line subbundles of
T_{X}
, and let

\varphi:L\to M

be an isomorphism.

Let
N_{1},N_{2}
be the graphs of
\varphi
and
-\varphi
, respectively.
That is,

N_{1}=\operatorname{im}(L\xrightarrow{(\mathrm{id},\varphi)}L\oplus M),\quad N_{2}=\operatorname{im}(L\xrightarrow{(\mathrm{id},(-\varphi))}L\oplus M).

Then

T_{X}=N_{1}\oplus N_{2},

and
L,M,N_{1},N_{2}
are pairwise distinct line subbundles.

Let
p:Y\to X
be the universal cover. We first show that
Y\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}
. By
[
6
, Theorem C]
, we can assume that
Y=C_{1}\times C_{2}
, where
C_{1},C_{2}\in\{\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{D}\}
, and

p_{1}^{*}T_{C_{1}}=p^{*}L,\quad p_{2}^{*}T_{C_{2}}=p^{*}M,

where
p_{i}:C_{1}\times C_{2}\to C_{i}
are the projections.

Applying
[
6
, Theorem C]
for the decomposition
T_{X}=N_{1}\oplus N_{2}
, we get
C_{3},C_{4}\in\{\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{D}\}
and an isomorphism
\psi:C_{3}\times C_{4}\to C_{1}\times C_{2}

such that

p_{3}^{*}T_{C_{3}}=(p\psi)^{*}N_{1},\quad p_{4}^{*}T_{C_{4}}=(p\psi)^{*}N_{2},

where
p_{j}:C_{3}\times C_{4}\to C_{j}
are the projections.

As
L,M,N_{1},N_{2}
are pairwise distinct, so are

(p\psi)^{*}L,(p\psi)^{*}M,(p\psi)^{*}N_{1},(p\psi)^{*}N_{2},

that is,

\psi^{*}p_{1}^{*}T_{C_{1}},\psi^{*}p_{2}^{*}T_{C_{2}},p_{3}^{*}T_{C_{3}},p_{4}^{*}T_{C_{4}}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have
Y=\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Now by
[
16
]
,
X
is either an abelian surface or a bielliptic surface. But
X
cannot be bielliptic surface as

h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=h^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})=2h^{0}(X,L^{-1}),

is even. So,
X
is an abelian surface.

∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface whose tangent bundle has a trivial direct summand.
Then
X
is an abelian surface or of class
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

We have

T_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L

for a line bundle
L
on
X
.
Taking determinants, we get

L\cong K_{X}^{-1}.

So,

\Omega_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus K_{X}.

As
\Omega_{X}
has a nowhere vanishing section, by
[
23
]
we have
\kappa(X)\leq 1
.

Suppose
\kappa(X)\neq-\infty
. Note that

\dim\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(X)=h^{0}(X,T_{X})>0,

where
\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(X)
is the identity component of the automorphism group scheme of
X.

By
[
12
]
,

X
is of class
\mathcal{C}
or an abelian surface or a bielliptic surface.

Suppose
\kappa(X)=-\infty
. So,
h^{0}(X,K_{X})=0.

Hence

h^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})=h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=1.

By
[
6
, Theorem C]
,
X
has no
(-1)
-curve. So
X
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over an elliptic curve. As
T_{X}
splits,
X
is in class
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
.

∎

4.
S_{n}
-invariant subbundles of
T_{X^{n}}

The goal of this section is to prove Theorems
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
. For a smooth projective surface
X
and an integer
n\geq 2
, the action of
S_{n}
on
X^{n}
induces an action of
S_{n}
on
T_{X^{n}}
, that is, an
S_{n}
-linearization of
T_{X^{n}}
. A subbundle of
T_{X^{n}}
is called
S_{n}
-invariant if it is invariant under this action.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface with
T_{X}
indecomposable, and
n\geq 2
an integer. Then
T_{X^{n}}
can not be written as a direct sum of two nonzero
S_{n}
-invariant subbundles.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{i}:X^{n}\to X
be the projections. Suppose we can write
T_{X^{n}}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2}
, with
\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}
nonzero
S_{n}
-invariant subbundles of
T_{X^{n}}
. Note that
\pi_{i}^{*}T_{X}
is indecomposable, as its restriction to a section of
\pi_{i}
is
T_{X}
, which is indecomposable. As
T_{X}
is nontrivial,
\pi_{i}^{*}T_{X}
’s are pairwise non-isomorphic.

Since

\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}T_{X}\cong\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2},

by
[
2
, Theorem 3]
, there exists
I\subset[n]
such that we have

\mathcal{E}_{1}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\pi_{i}^{*}T_{X},

and

\mathcal{E}_{2}=\bigoplus_{i\not\in I}\pi_{i}^{*}T_{X}.

As
\mathcal{E}_{1}
is
S_{n}
-invariant, we have

\bigoplus_{i\in I}\pi_{i}^{*}T_{X}\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\sigma(I)}\pi_{i}^{*}T_{X}\quad\text{for all }\sigma\in S_{n}.

As
\pi_{i}^{*}T_{X}
’s are pairwise non-isomorphic, by
[
2
, Theorem 3]
we have
I=\sigma(I)
for all
\sigma\in S_{n}
. Hence
I=\emptyset
or
[n]
. This is a contradiction since
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
are nonzero.

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface that is not an abelian surface, and
n\geq 2
an integer. Suppose there are line subbundles
L,M
of
T_{X}
with
T_{X}=L\oplus M.
Suppose
\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}
are
S_{n}
-invariant subbundles of
T_{X^{n}}
with
0<\textrm{rank }\mathcal{E}_{1}\leq\textrm{rank }\mathcal{E}_{2}
and

T_{X^{n}}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2}.

Let
\pi_{i}:X^{n}\to X
be the projections.
Then, after swapping
L,M
if necessary, one of the following holds:

(a)

\mathcal{E}_{2}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}^{*}M.

(b)

L
is trivial, and denoting

\mathcal{F}_{1}=\operatorname{im}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X^{n}}\xlongrightarrow{(\pi_{i}^{*}\xi)_{i}}\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}L\right),

and

\mathcal{F}_{2}=\ker\left(\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}L\xlongrightarrow{(\pi_{i}^{*}\xi^{-1})_{i}}\mathcal{O}_{X^{n}}\right),

where
\xi:\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow L
is a trivialization, we have

either (i)

\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{F}_{k}
for some
k=1,2
, and
h^{0}(X,M)=0
,

or (ii)

\mathcal{E}_{2}=\mathcal{F}_{k}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}M\right)
, for
k=1,2
, and
h^{0}(X,M)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Note that

T_{X^{n}}=\left(\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}L\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j}\pi_{j}^{*}M\right)

is a decomposition into line bundles. By
[
2
, Theorem 3]
, there are
I,J\subseteq[n]
with

\mathcal{E}_{1}\cong\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\pi_{i}^{*}L\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j\in J}\pi_{j}^{*}M\right),

and

\mathcal{E}_{2}\cong\left(\bigoplus_{i\not\in I}\pi_{i}^{*}L\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j\not\in J}\pi_{j}^{*}M\right).

Since
\mathcal{E}_{1}
is
S_{n}
-invariant, we have

(1)

\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\pi_{i}^{*}L\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j\in J}\pi_{j}^{*}M\right)\cong\left(\bigoplus_{i\in\sigma(I)}\pi_{i}^{*}L\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j\in\sigma(J)}\pi_{j}^{*}M\right)

for all
\sigma\in S_{n}.

Since
X
is not an abelian surface, by Lemma
3.2
we have
L\not\cong M
. In particular, at most one of
L,M
can be trivial. We consider the cases separately.

Case 1:

L,M
are both nontrivial. So,
\mathcal{O}_{X},L,M
are pairwise nonisomorphic. It easily follows that
\{\pi_{i}^{*}L\}_{i\in[n]}
and
\{\pi_{j}^{*}M\}_{j\in[n]}
are pairwise nonisomorphic. Using (
1
) and
[
2
, Theorem 3]
, we get
\sigma(I)=I
,
\sigma(J)=J
for all
\sigma\in S_{n}
. Thus each of
I,J
is either
[n]
or
\emptyset
. As
\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}\neq 0
, we see that
(a)
holds, possibly after swapping
L
and
M
.

Case 2:
Exactly one of
L,M
, say
L
, is trivial.

Note that
\mathcal{O}_{X^{n}},\{\pi_{i}^{*}M\}_{i\in[n]}
are pairwise nonisomorphic. By (
1
) and
[
2
, Theorem 3]
, we have
J=\sigma(J)
for all
\sigma\in S_{n}.
So
J=\emptyset
or
[n]
. If
J=[n]
, then
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{E}_{1}\leq\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{E}_{2}
forces
I=\emptyset
. Now swapping
L,M
, we see that
(a)
holds.

Now suppose
J=\emptyset
. So,

(2)

\mathcal{E}_{1}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X^{n}}^{|I|}\text{ and }\mathcal{E}_{2}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X^{n}}^{n-|I|}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j\in[n]}\pi_{j}^{*}M\right)

We first prove the following claim.

Claim:
The only proper nonzero
S_{n}
-invariant subbundles of
\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}L
are
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
.

Proof.

Clearly
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
proper nonzero
S_{n}
-invariant subbundles. we show they are the only ones.

Note that for a projective variety
Y
with an action of a finite group
G
,
G
-linearizations of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{n}
are in one-to-one correspondence with homomorphisms
G\to GL(n,\mathbb{C})
.
If
\xi
is a trivialization of
L
,
\xi
induces a trivialization of
\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}L
, and the
S_{n}
-linearization of
\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}L
coming from the
S_{n}
-linearization of
T_{X^{n}}
corresponds to the permutation representation
\rho:S_{n}\to GL(n,\mathbb{C})
.

So the
S_{n}
-invariant subbundles of
\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}L
are in one-to-one correspondence with subrepresentations of
\rho
. It is well-known that
\rho
has exactly two proper nonzero subrepresentations. This shows
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
are the only ones.
∎

Now we return to the proof of Case
2
.
We analyze two subcases separately.

Subcase 1:

h^{0}(X,M)=0
. Hence

\mathrm{Hom}\!\left(\mathcal{E}_{1},\bigoplus_{j\in[n]}\pi_{j}^{*}M\right)\cong\mathrm{Hom}\!\left(\mathcal{O}_{X^{n}}^{|I|},\bigoplus_{j\in[n]}\pi_{j}^{*}M\right)=0.

So
\mathcal{E}_{1}\subseteq\bigoplus_{i\in[n]}\pi_{i}^{*}L
is
S_{n}
-invariant subbundle, hence by Claim,
(i)
of
(b)
holds.

Subcase 2:

h^{0}(X,M^{-1})\neq 0
. As
M
is nontrivial, we must have
h^{0}(X,M^{-1})=0
. Hence

\mathrm{Hom}\!\left(\bigoplus_{j\in[n]}\pi_{j}^{*}M,\bigoplus_{i\in[n]}\pi_{i}^{*}L\right)\cong\mathrm{Hom}\!\left(\bigoplus_{j\in[n]}\pi_{j}^{*}M,\mathcal{O}_{X^{n}}^{n}\right)=0.

So any subbundle of
T_{X^{n}}
isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{j\in[n]}\pi_{j}^{*}M
must be contained in the factor
\bigoplus_{j\in[n]}\pi_{j}^{*}M
, hence is equal to that factor. Since
\mathcal{E}_{2}
contains a subbundle isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{j\in[n]}\pi_{j}^{*}M
, we see that
\mathcal{E}_{2}\supseteq\bigoplus_{j\in[n]}\pi_{j}^{*}M.
So,
\mathcal{E}_{2}=\mathcal{F}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j\in[n]}\pi_{j}^{*}M\right)
for a proper
S_{n}
-invariant subbundle

\mathcal{F}
of
\bigoplus_{i\in[n]}\pi_{i}^{*}L
. If
\mathcal{F}=0
, then
(a)
holds. If
\mathcal{F}\neq 0
, then by Claim
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{1}
or
\mathcal{F}_{2}
, so
(ii)
of
(b)
holds.

∎

5.
Non-extendability of some subbundle of
T_{X^{n}}
to
X^{[n]}

We set up the following notations which will be followed throughout this section. Let
X
be a smooth projective surface,
n\geq 2
an integer. Let
f:X^{n}\to X^{(n)}
be the Hilbert-Chow morphism, and
q:X^{n}\to X^{(n)}
the quotient map,
Z
the singular locus of
X^{(n)}
,
E=f^{-1}(Z)
, and
\tilde{Z}=q^{-1}(Z)
, the big diagonals, and
j:X^{n}\setminus\tilde{Z}\to X^{n}
the inclusion. So,
f:X^{[n]}\setminus E\cong X^{(n)}\setminus Z
is an isomorphism and
q:X^{n}\setminus\tilde{Z}\to X^{(n)}\setminus Z
is finite étale. Let
\pi_{i}:X^{n}\to X
be the projections.

First we make the following definitions for a smooth projective surface
X
.

(1)

Given a direct summand
\mathcal{E}
of
T_{X^{[n]}}
, we define an
S_{n}
-invariant direct summand
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
of
T_{X^{n}}
as follows:

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a subbundle of
T_{X^{[n]}}
such that
T_{X^{[n]}}=\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{F}.
Let

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}=q^{*}f_{*}\big(\mathcal{E}|_{X^{[n]}\setminus E}\big),\quad\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}=q^{*}f_{*}\big(\mathcal{F}|_{X^{[n]}\setminus E}\big).

Clearly both are subbundles of
T_{X^{n}\setminus\widetilde{Z}}
with direct sum
T_{X^{n}\setminus\widetilde{Z}}
.
Now we define

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}:=j_{*}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime},\quad\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}:=j_{*}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}.

Both are naturally subsheaves of
j_{*}T_{X^{n}\setminus\widetilde{Z}}=T_{X^{n}}
, as
\mathrm{codim}_{X^{n}}(\widetilde{Z})=2
.

Also,
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}\oplus\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=T_{X^{n}}
,
so
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
is a direct summand of
T_{X^{n}}
, in particular it is a subbundle.

Finally,
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
is
S_{n}
-invariant, as
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}|_{X^{n}\setminus\widetilde{Z}}
is
S_{n}
-invariant.

(2)

Given a subbundle
V
of
T_{X^{n}}
, we say
V
is
extendable
if
V=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
for some direct summand
\mathcal{E}
of
T_{X^{[n]}}
.

The following result will be used as a crucial step in the proof of Theorem
A
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface, and let
L,M
be line subbundles of
T_{X}
with
T_{X}=L\oplus M
. Let
Then the subbundle
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}^{*}M

of
T_{X^{n}}
is not extendable.

Proof.

The idea of the proof is the following: since
f^{-1}\circ q:X^{n}\setminus{\widetilde{Z}}\to X^{[n]}\setminus E
is finite étale, given a rank
n

S_{n}
-invariant subbundle
V
of
T_{X^{n}}
,
V|_{X^{n}\setminus{\widetilde{Z}}}
is the pullback of a vector bundle
V^{\prime}
on
X^{[n]}\setminus E
under
f^{-1}\circ q
. Now if
V=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
for a direct summand subbundle
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
of
T_{X^{[n]}}
, then the total space of
\mathcal{E}
must be the closure of the total space
S
of
V^{\prime}
in the total space of
T_{X^{[n]}}
. Since we know
V
, we know
S
. We get a contradiction by showing that a fibre of the projection
S\to X^{[n]}
has dimension
\geq n+1
. This is a local question, so we do the computation in a local chart of
X^{[n]}.
What we might lose after going to the local chart is the description of the subbundles
L
and
M
of
T_{X}
, but if we use
[
6
, Theorem C]
, we can choose charts in
X
in such a way that
L
and
M
correspond to tangents in horizontal and vertical directions, respectively. So we can get back
L
and
M
in the chart.

Now we carry out the idea. For a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on a complex manifold
N
, denote by
\mathrm{Sp}(\mathcal{E})
the total space of
\mathcal{E}
. Thus,
\mathrm{Sp}(\mathcal{E})
is a complex manifold with a map to
N
, whose fibres are naturally vector spaces. Given a map
h:N\to N^{\prime}
, we have an induced map
dh:\mathrm{Sp}(T_{N})\to\mathrm{Sp}(T_{N^{\prime}})
of complex manifolds.

Let
r_{1},r_{2}:\mathbb{D}^{2}\to\mathbb{D}
be the projections. By
[
6
, Theorem C]
, there is open embedding

\phi:\mathbb{D}^{2}\to X

such that

\phi^{*}L=r_{1}^{*}T_{\mathbb{D}},\phi^{*}M=r_{2}^{*}T_{\mathbb{D}}

as subbundles of
T_{\mathbb{D}^{2}}
. Let
\phi_{3},\cdots,\phi_{n}:\mathbb{D}^{2}\to X
be open embeddings with the same property, such that the images of
\phi,\phi_{3},\cdots,\phi_{n}
are disjoint.

Note that given a length
2
subscheme
\mathfrak{t}
of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, there is a unique line
\ell(\mathfrak{t})
containing
\mathfrak{t}
.
For
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}\in\mathbb{C}
, let
\tau(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4})

be the unique
\mathfrak{t}\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{[2]}
such that:

(i)

\ell(\mathfrak{t})=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid z=y_{1}w+y_{2}\}
,

(ii)

If
[\mathfrak{t}]=(z_{1},w_{1})+(z_{2},w_{2})
, then
w_{1}+w_{2}=y_{3}
and
(w_{1}-w_{2})^{2}=y_{4}
.

The map
\mathbb{C}^{4}\xrightarrow{\tau}(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{[2]}

is an open embedding. Let

.

One can locally define

∎

7.

We prove Theorems

Proof of Theorem

:

Proof.

Let

be the projections.

Let

1,&\quad\textnormal{if $X$ is of class $\tilde{\mathcal{C}}$},\\
0,&\quad\textnormal{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For

, let
t_{k}
be the number of summands of rank

in a decomposition of
T_{Y}
as a direct sum of indecomposable vector bundles. By
[
2
, Theorem 3]
,

is well-defined, that is, independent of the decomposition.

On the other hand,

T_{Y}\cong\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}T_{X^{[a_{i}]}},

and Theorem
A
shows

is the number of times
k
occurs in the multiset
\{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{r}\}
. Here we are using the observation: if
\mathcal{E}
is an indecomposable vector bundle on

, then
\pi_{i}^{*}(\mathcal{E})
is indecomposable, as its restriction to a section of

is
\mathcal{E}

is also same as the number of times

occurs in the multiset

. So, for each

,
k
occurs same number of times in each multiset. Now

\sum_{i}a_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\dim Y=\sum_{j}b_{j}

shows this is true for

also. So both multisets are the same.
∎
