Well-posedness and asymptotic behavior of solutions to a second order nonlocal parabolic MEMS equation
Yufei Wei, Yanyan Zhang

Abstract
We consider a second-order nonlocal parabolic MEMS equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions: u t − Δ ​ u = λ ( 1 − u ) 2 ​ ( 1 + ∫ Ω 1 1 − u ​ 𝑑 x ) 2 , x ∈ Ω , t > 0 , u_{t}-\Delta u=\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}\bigl(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,dx\bigr)^{2}},\quad x\in\Omega,\ t>0, where Ω ⊂ ℝ N \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} ( 1 ≤ N ≤ 3 ) (1\leq N\leq 3) is a bounded smooth domain and λ > 0 \lambda>0 . Using operator semigroups and the contraction mapping principle, we prove local existence and give a quenching criterion. Under suitable smallness conditions on λ \lambda and the initial data, global existence and exponential convergence to the minimal steady state are obtained. Assuming the global solution stays uniformly away from the singularity u = 1 u=1 , we show that the system forms a gradient system. By establishing analyticity of the energy and a Lojasiewicz–Simon inequality, we prove that the solution converges to a steady state with either exponential or algebraic rate depending on the Lojasiewicz exponent. Numerical experiments in 1D and 2D illustrate the results and support conjectures on the λ \lambda -dichotomy. Keywords: MEMS equation; nonlocal parabolic equation; global existence; asymptotic behavior; Lojasiewicz–Simon inequality; convergence rate; numerical simulation.

Introduction

1.1
Research background

Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems (MEMS) are devices that integrate miniature mechanical structures, micro-sensors, micro-actuators, electronic circuits, and other devices. Typically ranging in size from micrometers to millimeters, MEMS offer advantages such as compact dimensions, low energy consumption, high integration, and high sensitivity.

The development history of MEMS can be traced back to the 1950s. In 1959, physicist Richard Feynman proposed the possibility of manipulating and controlling objects at a minuscule scale. In 1964, Westinghouse produced the first batch of mass-produced MEMS devices, known as resonant gate transistors. In 1982, Kurt Petersen published a seminal paper titled ”Silicon as a Mechanical Material”, which extensively discussed the material properties of silicon and etching data, greatly advancing the field of MEMS.

Today, amid the rapid growth of digitalization, the Internet of Things (IoT), and artificial intelligence (AI) technologies, MEMS is facing unprecedented opportunities for development. MEMS plays an indispensable role in cutting-edge fields such as industrial inspection, autonomous driving, and smart healthcare. Investigating the mathematical models of MEMS will facilitate an in-depth exploration of their properties, provide a robust theoretical foundation for MEMS advancement, and propel MEMS-related technologies toward more advanced levels.

2.4
Semi-Fredholm Operators

Definition 2.8

(See Definition 2.2.1 in
[

]
)
.

(2)

(See Remark 2.2.2 in

]
)

The finite dimensionality of

implies that there exists a closed subspace

such that

. Furthermore,
R(A)=A(X)
forms a Banach space under
\|\cdot\|_{Z}
.

Theorem 2.5

(See Theorem 2.2.3 in
[
11
]
)
.

Let

and

be Banach spaces,

, and

as defined in Remark
2.1
. If
\ker A
is finite-dimensional, then
A:W\to Z
is a semi-Fredholm operator if and only if there exists a constant

such that for all

,

\|Au\|_{Z}\geq C\|u\|_{W}.

Remark 2.2
.

Since
\|u\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|-\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}
(see formula (1.3.26) in
[

]
), the operator

is semi-Fredholm.

Theorem 2.6

(See Theorem 2.2.5 in
[
11
]
)
.

Let

be a semi-Fredholm operator and

a compact operator. Then
A+G:W\to Z
is also a semi-Fredholm operator.

The existence of solutions to the steady state equation (

Theorem 2.8

(See Theorem 3.2 in

]
)
.

3
Local existence

In this section, we prove the Theorem
1.1
. We will truncate the equation to obtain the auxiliary equation, then construct the function space and use the contraction mapping principle to study the local existence of the solution of the auxiliary equation, and finally get the conclusion from the sobolev embedding theorem.

Denote
A=-\triangle
, then
D(A)=H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and
A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{2}(\Omega)
(see the proof of Theorem 2.7.2 in
[
22
]
), which is denoted as
\{Q(t);t\geq 0\}
. Thus we consider:

u(t)=Q(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}Q(t-s)f(u(s))\mathrm{d}s,

Note that
f(u)
has a singular point
u=1
, so we consider the truncated function

g_{\delta}(u):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{1-u},&\quad u\leq 1-\delta,\\
\delta^{-1},&\quad u>1-\delta,\end{array}\right.

and

f_{\delta}(u):=\lambda\left(g_{\delta}(u)\right)^{2}\left(1+\int_{\Omega}g_{\delta}(u)dx\right)^{-2}.

(3.1)

Thus we have
f_{\delta}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and

\left\|g_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\delta^{-1},\left\|g_{\delta}^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\delta^{-2}.

(3.2)

Now we consider the following auxiliary equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{t}-\triangle u=f_{\delta}(u),&(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
u(t,x)=0,&(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\quad\right.

(3.3)

and operator
B

\begin{array}[]{lll}&L^{2}(\Omega)&\to L^{2}(\Omega),\\
&u&\mapsto Q(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}Q(t-s)f_{\delta}(u(s))\mathrm{d}s.\end{array}

(3.4)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

Y_{M}:=\left\{u\in C\left([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)\right),\|u\|_{Y}\leq M\right\},\quad

where
\|u\|_{Y}=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)\right)}
,
M:=\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+1
. Then
f_{\delta}
(as defined in (
3.1
)) is Lipschitz continuous in
Y_{M}
.

Proof.

Note that

\forall u_{1},u_{2}\in Y_{M}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|f_{\delta}(u_{1})-f_{\delta}\left(u_{2}\right)\right|

(3.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\lambda g_{\delta}^{2}(u_{1})}{\left(1+\int_{\Omega}g_{\delta}(u_{1})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}}-\frac{\lambda g_{\delta}^{2}(u_{2})}{\left(1+\int_{\Omega}g_{\delta}(u_{2})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\lambda\left(g_{\delta}^{2}(u_{1})-g_{\delta}^{2}(u_{2})+g_{\delta}^{2}(u_{2})\right)}{\left(1+\int_{\Omega}g_{\delta}(u_{1})\mathrm{d}x\right)}-\frac{\lambda g_{\delta}^{2}(u_{2})}{\left(1+\int_{\Omega}g_{\delta}(u_{2})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\lambda\left(g_{\delta}(u_{1})+g_{\delta}\left(u_{2}\right)\right)}{\left(1+\int_{\Omega}g_{\delta}(u_{1})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}}\cdot\left(g_{\delta}(u_{1})-g_{\delta}\left(u_{2}\right)\right)\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda g_{\delta}^{2}\left(u_{2}\right)\cdot\int_{\Omega}\left(g_{\delta}(u_{1})-g_{\delta}\left(u_{2}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x}{\left(1+\int_{\Omega}g_{\delta}(u_{1})\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(1+\int_{\Omega}g_{\delta}\left(u_{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\lambda g_{\delta}^{2}\left(u_{2}\right)\cdot\int_{\Omega}\left(g_{\delta}(u_{1})-g_{\delta}\left(u_{2}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x}{\left(1+\int_{\Omega}g_{\delta}(u_{1})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}\left(1+\int_{\Omega}g_{\delta}\left(u_{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\lambda\left(\delta^{-3}\left|u_{1}-u_{2}\right|+\delta^{-4}\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{1}-u_{2}\right|\mathrm{d}x\right),

then

\displaystyle\left\|f_{\delta}(u_{1})-f_{\delta}\left(u_{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|f_{\delta}(u_{1}(s))-f_{\delta}(u_{2}(s))\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|2\lambda\left(\delta^{-3}\left|u_{1}-u_{2}\right|+\delta^{-4}\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{1}-u_{2}\right|\mathrm{d}x\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|2\lambda\delta^{-3}\left|u_{1}-u_{2}\right|\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+\left\|2\lambda\delta^{-4}\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{1}-u_{2}\right|\mathrm{d}x\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\lambda\delta^{-3}\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+2\lambda\delta^{-4}|\Omega|\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{1}-u_{2}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(2\lambda\delta^{-3}+2\lambda\delta^{-4}|\Omega|\right)\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

5
Asymptotic behavior of solutions

In this section, we will use gradient system theory, analyticity theory and Lojasiewicz-Simon method to study the convergence and convergence rate of the global solution to the steady-state solution.

5.1
Gradient system

Definition 5.1

(See Definition 6.3.1 in
[
22
]
)
.

Let
H
be a complete metric space,
S(t)
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
H
, and

be a continuous functional. If the following conditions hold:

(1) For every

is monotonically decreasing in

;

(2)
V(x)\geq C
for all

, where

is a constant.

Then

is called a Lyapunov functional.

Definition 5.2

(See Definition 6.3.2 in
[
22
]
)
.

Let
H
be a complete metric space,
S(t)
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
H
, and
V
be a Lyapunov functional. If the following conditions hold:

(1) For every

, there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
\bigcup_{t\geq t_{0}}S(t)x
is relatively compact in
H
;

(2) If
V(S(t)x)=V(x)
for all

, then
x
is a fixed point of
S(t)
.

Then

is called a gradient system.

To construct a gradient system, we will find a Lyapunov functional and a
C_{0}
-semigroup for the equation and prove the compactness of the trajectories.

Multiply both sides of equation (
1.1
) by
u_{t}
and integrate over
\Omega

\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}u_{t}\Delta u\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda u_{t}}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

By Green’s formula,

\int_{\Omega}u_{t}\Delta u\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{t}\nabla u\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{t}\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\,\mathrm{d}S.

Combining with the Dirichlet boundary conditions, we have
\int_{\Omega}u_{t}\Delta u\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{t}\nabla u\,\mathrm{d}x
. Therefore,

\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(-\lambda\left(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{-1}\right),

which implies

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\lambda}{1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x}\right)=-\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

We define the energy functional for equation (

(5.2)

Note that

E(t)(u)\geq 0,\quad\forall u\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

Therefore,

For every

and

(M_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that:

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|M_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{n}(L^{\infty},\mathbb{R})}r^{n}<\infty,\ \ \text{where}\ \|M_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{n}(L^{\infty},\mathbb{R})}=\sup\{|M_{n}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})|\ \mid\ \|u_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1\},

\forall h\in\bar{B}(0,r)\subset U,\ \ \ S(u+h)=S(u)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M_{n}(h,\cdots,h).

Since
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for
1\leq N\leq 3
, we have

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{2}},\quad\forall u\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

For every
u\in V\subset U
, since

\|M_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{n}(H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1},\mathbb{R})}=\sup\{|M_{n}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})|\ \mid\ \|u_{i}\|_{H^{2}}\leq 1\},

let
v_{i}=\frac{u_{i}}{\|u_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}}\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
so that
\|v_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}=1
. Then

\displaystyle\|M_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{n}(H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1},\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\sup\{|M_{n}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})|\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdots\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\ \mid\ \|u_{i}\|_{H^{2}}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\{\|M_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{n}(L^{\infty},\mathbb{R})}C\|u_{1}\|_{H^{2}}\cdots C\|u_{n}\|_{H^{2}}\ \mid\ \|u_{i}\|_{H^{2}}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle\leq C^{n}\|M_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{n}(L^{\infty},\mathbb{R})}.

Take
r_{1}<\frac{r}{C}
, then

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|M_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{n}(H^{2},\mathbb{R})}r_{1}^{n}<\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|M_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{n}(L^{\infty},\mathbb{R})}r^{n}<\infty,

and thus

\forall h\in\bar{B}(0,r_{1})\subset V,\ \ \ S(u+h)=S(u)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M_{n}(h,\cdots,h).

Therefore,
S(u)
is analytic on
V
.

Define the mapping
g:S(V)\to\mathbb{R}
as:

g(y)=\frac{1}{y}.

For every
y_{0}\in S(V)
, we have

\frac{1}{y}=\frac{1}{y_{0}}\cdot\frac{1}{1+\frac{y-y_{0}}{y_{0}}}=\frac{1}{y_{0}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{y-y_{0}}{y_{0}}\right)^{n},\quad\text{when}\ \left|\frac{y-y_{0}}{y_{0}}\right|<1.

Therefore,
g
is analytic on
S(V)
, and by Theorem
2.3
,
\frac{1}{1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x}
is analytic on
V
.

On the other hand, consider
T(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x:V\to\mathbb{R}
. Its derivatives are:

DT(u)(v)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x,

D^{2}T(u)(v,w)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x.

Thus for every
u\in V
, there exist
r_{1}>0
and
n
-linear continuous symmetric maps
DT(u)
and
D^{2}T(u)
such that

\forall h\in\bar{B}(0,r_{1})\subset V,\ \ \ T(u+h)=T(u)+DT(u)(h)+D^{2}T(u)(h,h).

Therefore,
T(u)
is analytic on
V
.

In conclusion, the energy functional

E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\lambda}{1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x}

is analytic on
V
.
∎

5.3
Lojasiewicz-Simon Inequality

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\psi
be an equilibrium point satisfying (
1.10
). Then the operator
A=D^{2}E(\psi):H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is a semi-Fredholm operator.

Proof.

The energy functional is
E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\lambda}{1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x}
.
By calculation, we obtain:

DE(u)(v)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda v}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}(1-u)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(5.10)

\displaystyle D^{2}E(u)(v,w)=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x+2\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda v}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{3}(1-u)^{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{w}{(1-u)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda v}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\frac{w}{(1-u)^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

For the equilibrium point
\psi
, the operator
A:H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfies

\langle Av,w\rangle=D^{2}E(\psi)(v,w),

and in the weak sense,

Av=\frac{2\int_{\Omega}\frac{v}{(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\cdot\lambda}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{3}(1-\psi)^{2}}-\frac{2\lambda v}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}(1-\psi)^{3}}-\Delta v.

Let

G(v)=\frac{2\int_{\Omega}\frac{v}{(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\cdot\lambda}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{3}(1-\psi)^{2}}-\frac{2\lambda v}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}(1-\psi)^{3}}.

We now prove that
G:H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is a bounded linear operator:

For every
v\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|G(v)(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{4\lambda^{2}(\int_{\Omega}\frac{v}{(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{6}(1-\psi)^{4}}-\frac{8\lambda^{2}v\int_{\Omega}\frac{v}{(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{5}(1-\psi)^{5}}

\displaystyle+\frac{4\lambda^{2}v^{2}}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{4}(1-\psi)^{6}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\psi
satisfies (
1.10
),
\|\frac{1}{1-\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}}
is bounded. Moreover, for
1\leq N\leq 3
,
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, so

\int_{\Omega}\frac{v}{(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq M\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M\|v\|_{H^{2}}.

Thus

\int_{\Omega}|G(v)(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{M\|v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}}{(1-\psi)^{4}}+\frac{M\|v\|_{H^{2}}|v|}{(1-\psi)^{5}}+\frac{Mv^{2}}{(1-\psi)^{6}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\|v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

Furthermore,

\nabla G(v)=\frac{4\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{v}{(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{3}}\frac{\nabla\psi}{(1-\psi)^{3}}-\frac{2\lambda}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\frac{(1-\psi)\nabla v+3v\nabla\psi}{(1-\psi)^{4}},

so

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\Omega}|\nabla G(v)(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{16\lambda^{2}(\int_{\Omega}\frac{v}{(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{6}}\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{(1-\psi)^{6}}-\frac{16\lambda^{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{v}{(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{5}}\frac{(1-\psi)\nabla\psi\nabla v+3v|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{(1-\psi)^{7}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4\lambda^{2}}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{4}}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}(1-\psi)^{2}+6v(1-\psi)\nabla\psi\nabla v+9v^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{(1-\psi)^{8}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}M\|v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{(1-\psi)^{6}}+M\|v\|_{H^{2}}\frac{(1-\psi)|\nabla\psi\nabla v|+3|v||\nabla\psi|^{2}}{(1-\psi)^{7}}

\displaystyle\quad+M\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}(1-\psi)^{2}+6|v|(1-\psi)|\nabla\psi\nabla v|+9v^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{(1-\psi)^{8}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq M\|v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{(1-\psi)^{6}}\,\mathrm{d}x+M\|v\|_{H^{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla\psi\nabla v|}{(1-\psi)^{6}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{3|v||\nabla\psi|^{2}}{(1-\psi)^{7}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\quad+M\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{(1-\psi)^{6}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{6|v\nabla\psi\nabla v|}{(1-\psi)^{7}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{9v^{2}}{(1-\psi)^{8}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq M\|v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+M\|v\|_{H^{2}}\left(\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+M\left(\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}+\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore

\|G(v)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}|G(v)(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla G(v)(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\|v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

On the other hand, the embedding operator
I:H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
,
I(v)=v
is compact. Hence
G:H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is a compact operator.

By Remark
2.2
,
-\Delta:H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is a semi-Fredholm operator. Combined with Theorem
2.6
,
A=G-\Delta
is a semi-Fredholm operator.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be the set of equilibrium points of equation (
1.1
):

\mathscr{E}=\left\{\phi\mid-\Delta\phi=\frac{\lambda}{(1-\phi)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\phi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}},\ \phi|_{\partial\Omega}=0\right\},

and let
\psi\in\mathscr{E}
. Then there exist
\sigma>0
depending on
\psi
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that for every
u\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying
\|u-\psi\|_{H^{2}}\leq\sigma
:

\left\|\Delta u+\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\geq|E(u)-E(\psi)|^{1-\theta}.

(5.11)

Proof.

Consider the linearization near the equilibrium point
\psi
:

\begin{cases}Lw\equiv-\Delta w+\frac{2\int_{\Omega}\frac{w}{(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\cdot\lambda}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{3}(1-\psi)^{2}}-\frac{2\lambda w}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}(1-\psi)^{3}}=0,\ x\in\Omega\\
w|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{cases}

(5.12)

By Proposition
5.3
,
L:H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is a semi-Fredholm operator, so its kernel
\ker(L)
is finite-dimensional.

Let
\dim\ker(L)=m
, and let
(\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{m})
be an orthonormal basis of
\ker(L)
. Let
\Pi:H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to\ker(L)\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
be the projection operator. Define the operator
\tilde{L}:H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
by

\tilde{L}w=\Pi w+Lw,

which is bijective.

Decompose
u
as:

u=v+\psi,\quad v\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

Define the operator
\mathcal{M}:H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
:

\mathcal{M}(v)=-\Delta u-\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}},\quad\text{where}\ u=v+\psi,

(5.13)

so that
D\mathcal{M}(0)=L
.

Let

\mathcal{N}(v)=\mathcal{M}(v)+\Pi v,

(5.14)

then
D\mathcal{N}(0)=\tilde{L}
.

Since
\tilde{L}
is bijective, by the local inverse mapping theorem, there exist a neighborhood
W_{1}(0)\subset H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
of
0
and a neighborhood
W_{2}(0)\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
of
0
, and an inverse mapping

\Psi:W_{2}(0)\to W_{1}(0)

such that

\mathcal{N}(\Psi(g))=g,\quad\forall g\in W_{2}(0),

(5.15)

\Psi(\mathcal{N}(v))=v,\quad\forall v\in W_{1}(0),

(5.16)

with

\|\mathcal{N}(v_{1})-\mathcal{N}(v_{2})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{H^{2}},\quad\forall v_{1},v_{2}\in W_{1}(0),

(5.17)

\|\Psi(g_{1})-\Psi(g_{2})\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|g_{1}-g_{2}\|_{L^{2}},\quad\forall g_{1},g_{2}\in W_{2}(0),

(5.18)

where
C>0
is a constant.

Since
\frac{1}{1-u}
and
\frac{1}{1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x}
are analytic in a neighborhood of
\psi\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{M}
is analytic in a neighborhood of
0\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Thus
\mathcal{N}:W_{1}(0)\to W_{2}(0)
and
\Psi:W_{2}(0)\to W_{1}(0)
are both analytic.

Let

\xi=(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\quad\Pi v=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\xi_{j}\phi_{j}.

Define
\Gamma:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\Gamma(\xi)=E\left(\Psi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\xi_{j}\phi_{j}\right)+\psi\right),\quad\text{for sufficiently small}\ \xi\ \text{such that}\ \sum_{j=1}^{m}\xi_{j}\phi_{j}\in W_{2}(0).

By the analyticity of
E
and
\Psi
,
\Gamma(\xi)
is analytic in a neighborhood of
0\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Differentiating (
5.15
) with respect to
g
gives:

D\mathcal{N}(v)\cdot D\Psi(g)=I,

i.e.,
D\mathcal{N}(v)\in\mathcal{L}(H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1},L^{2})
and
D\Psi(g)\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2},H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1})
.

Partial derivatives of
\Gamma
:

\frac{\partial\Gamma}{\partial\xi_{j}}=DE\left(\Psi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\xi_{j}\phi_{j}\right)+\psi\right)\cdot D\Psi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\xi_{j}\phi_{j}\right)\cdot\phi_{j},\quad j=1,\cdots,m.

(5.19)

where

\displaystyle DE(u)\cdot v

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda v}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}(1-u)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\Delta u-\frac{\lambda}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}(1-u)^{2}}\right)v\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\psi\in\mathscr{E}
,
\xi=0
is a critical point of
\Gamma(\xi)
.

By the Lojasiewicz Inequality, there exist small constants
\sigma>0
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that

|\nabla\Gamma(\xi)|\geq|\Gamma(\xi)-\Gamma(0)|^{1-\theta},

i.e.,

|\nabla\Gamma(\xi)|\geq|\Gamma(\xi)-E(\psi)|^{1-\theta}.

(5.20)

From (
5.13
) and (
5.19
),

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\Gamma}{\partial\xi_{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathcal{M}\left(\Psi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\xi_{j}\phi_{j}\right)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|D\Psi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\xi_{j}\phi_{j}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2},H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1})}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\mathcal{M}\left(\Psi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\xi_{j}\phi_{j}\right)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}},\quad j=1,\dots,m.

Thus

\displaystyle|\nabla\Gamma(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\mathcal{M}\left(\Psi\left(\Pi v\right)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

(5.21)

\displaystyle=C\left\|\mathcal{M}\left(\Psi\left(\Pi v\right)\right)-\mathcal{M}(v)+\mathcal{M}(v)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\mathcal{M}\left(\Psi\left(\Pi v\right)\right)-\mathcal{M}(v)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}\right),\quad v\in W_{1}(0).

From (
5.14
) and (
5.17
),

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}(v_{1})-\mathcal{M}(v_{2})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Pi v_{1}-\Pi v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathcal{N}(v_{1})-\mathcal{N}(v_{2})\|_{L^{2}}

(5.22)

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(C+1)\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{H^{2}},\quad v_{1},v_{2}\in W_{1}(0).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\|\Psi(\Pi v)-v\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\Psi(\Pi v)-\Psi(\mathcal{N}v)\|_{H^{2}}

(5.23)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Pi v-\mathcal{N}v\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=C\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining (
5.21
)-(
5.23
):

\displaystyle|\nabla\Gamma(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left((C+1)\|\Psi(\Pi v)-v\|_{H^{2}}+\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}\right)

(5.24)

\displaystyle\leq C\left((C+1)C\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}.

On the other hand, for
t\in[0,1]
and
v\in W_{1}(0)
, we have
v+t(\Psi(\Pi v)-v)\in W_{1}(0)
. Thus for
u=v+\psi
, using (
5.22
) and (
5.23
):

\displaystyle|E(u)-\Gamma(\xi)|

\displaystyle=|E(u)-E(\Psi(\Pi v)+\psi)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(u+(1-t)(\Psi(\Pi v)-v))\,\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}DE(u+(1-t)(\Psi(\Pi v)-v))\cdot(\Psi(\Pi v)-v)\,\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{0\leq t\leq 1}\left|DE(u+(1-t)(\Psi(\Pi v)-v))\cdot(\Psi(\Pi v)-v)\right|.

Let
z=u+(1-t)(\Psi(\Pi v)-v)
. By (
5.10
):

\displaystyle\quad DE(u+(1-t)(\Psi(\Pi v)-v))\cdot(\Psi(\Pi v)-v)

\displaystyle=DE(z)\cdot(\Psi(\Pi v)-v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla z\nabla(\Psi(\Pi v)-v)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda(\Psi(\Pi v)-v)}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-z}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}(1-z)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\Delta z\cdot(\Psi(\Pi v)-v)-\frac{\lambda(\Psi(\Pi v)-v)}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-z}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}(1-z)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}(\Psi(\Pi v)-v)\left(\Delta z-\frac{\lambda}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-z}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}(1-z)^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}(\Psi(\Pi v)-v)(\mathcal{M}(z-\psi))\,\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore

\displaystyle|E(u)-\Gamma(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{0\leq t\leq 1}\|\mathcal{M}(v+(1-t)(\Psi(\Pi v)-v))\|_{L^{2}}\|\Psi(\Pi v)-v\|_{H^{2}}

(5.25)

\displaystyle=\max_{0\leq t\leq 1}\left\|\mathcal{M}(v+(1-t)(\Psi(\Pi v)-v))-\mathcal{M}(v)+\mathcal{M}(v)\right\|_{L^{2}}\|\Psi(\Pi v)-v\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{0\leq t\leq 1}\left((1-t)(C+1)\|(\Psi(\Pi v)-v)\|_{H^{2}}+\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|\Psi(\Pi v)-v\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}+(C+1)C\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\cdot C\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Substituting (
5.24
)-(
5.25
) into (
5.20
) and using
|a+b|^{1-\theta}\leq 2|a|^{1-\theta}+|b|^{1-\theta}
:

For
v\in W_{1}(0)
,

\displaystyle C\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq|\nabla\Gamma(\xi)|

\displaystyle\geq|\Gamma(\xi)-E(\psi)|^{1-\theta}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(|E(u)-E(\psi)|^{1-\theta}-|E(u)-\Gamma(\xi)|^{1-\theta}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}|E(u)-E(\psi)|^{1-\theta}-\frac{1}{2}C^{1-\theta}\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2(1-\theta)}.

Thus

C\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}C^{1-\theta}\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2(1-\theta)}\geq\frac{1}{2}|E(u)-E(\psi)|^{1-\theta}.

Since
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
2(1-\theta)>1
, and
\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}
is bounded, we obtain:

\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}\geq C|E(u)-E(\psi)|^{1-\theta},\quad v\in W_{1}(0).

Let
\epsilon>0
be a small constant. We can choose
\sigma
sufficiently small so that

C|E(u)-E(\psi)|^{-\epsilon}\geq 1,\quad\|u-\psi\|_{H^{2}}\leq\sigma.

Therefore

\|\mathcal{M}(v)\|_{L^{2}}\geq|E(u)-E(\psi)|^{1-\theta+\epsilon}=|E(u)-E(\psi)|^{1-\theta^{\prime}},

where
\|v\|_{H^{2}}=\|u-\psi\|_{H^{2}}\leq\sigma
.

Hence

\left\|\Delta u+\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\geq|E(u)-E(\psi)|^{1-\theta},

for
\|u-\psi\|_{H^{2}}\leq\sigma,\ \theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. The theorem is proved.
∎

Asymptotic behavior

To prove Theorem

, we first introduce the following lemma:

Lemma 5.2

(See Theorem 6.3.2 in
[

]
)
.

(H,S(t),V)
be a gradient system. Then for every
x\in H
, the
\omega
-limit set
\omega(x)
is a connected compact invariant set consisting entirely of fixed points of

.

Proof of Theorem

.

In Section 5.1, we constructed the gradient system

\omega
-limit set
\omega(u_{0})
consists entirely of equilibrium points, i.e., there exists
\psi\in\omega(u_{0})
satisfying equation (
1.2
), and there exists a sequence
t_{n}\to\infty
such that
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|u(\cdot,t_{n})-\psi\|_{H^{2}}=0
.

We can show that
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Indeed, since for
1\leq N\leq 3
,
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
for
0<\alpha<1
, we have
\psi\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
. Thus there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that
\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1-\delta
. Therefore, for every
x,\ y\in\Omega
,

\left|\frac{1}{1-\psi(x)}-\frac{1}{1-\psi(y)}\right|=\left|\frac{\psi(x)-\psi(y)}{(1-\psi(x))(1-\psi(y))}\right|\leq C|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha},

so
\frac{1}{1-\psi}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
, and hence
f(\psi)=\frac{\lambda}{(1-\psi)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
. By Schauder estimates,
\psi\in C^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)
. Iterating this process, we obtain
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Next, we prove that
\omega(u_{0})
consists of a unique equilibrium point.

From (
5.2
),
E(u(t))
is monotonically decreasing in
t
and
E(u(t))\geq 0
, so

\lim_{t\to\infty}E(u(t))=E_{\infty}.

Moreover, for every
u,\ v\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
with
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}},\ \|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1-\delta
,

\displaystyle|E(u)-E(v)|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}-|\nabla v|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\lambda}{1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x}-\frac{\lambda}{1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-v}\,\mathrm{d}x}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u+v)||\nabla(u-v)|\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{v-u}{(1-u)(1-v)}\,\mathrm{d}x}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-v}\,\mathrm{d}x)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|u+v\|_{H^{2}}\|u-v\|_{H^{2}}+C\|u-v\|_{H^{2}},

hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}|E(u(t_{n}))-E(\psi)|\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}(\|u(t_{n})+\psi\|_{H^{2}}\|u(t_{n})-\psi\|_{H^{2}}+C\|u(t_{n})-\psi\|_{H^{2}})=0.

Therefore,

E(\psi)=E_{\infty}.

Similar to (
5.3
), we also have:

E(\psi)+\int_{t}^{\infty}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=E(u(t)).

If there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
E(u(t_{0}))=E(\psi)
, then
\forall\ t\geq t_{0}
,
u_{t}=0
and
u=\psi
, proving (
1.11
).

Now consider the case where for all
t\geq 0
,
E(u(t))>E(\psi)
.

By Theorem
5.4
, for
\psi\in\omega(u_{0})
, there exist
\sigma>0
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
depending on
\psi
such that when
\|u-\psi\|_{H^{2}}\leq\sigma
:

\left\|\Delta u+\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\geq|E(u)-E(\psi)|^{1-\theta}.

(5.27)

Note that (
5.2
) is equivalent to

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(E(u(t))-E(\psi))+\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\left\|-\Delta u-\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}=0,

(5.28)

and

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(E(u(t))-E(\psi))^{\theta}=\theta(E(u(t))-E(\psi))^{\theta-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(E(u(t))-E(\psi)).

(5.29)

Substituting (
5.28
) into (
5.29
) yields:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(E(u(t))-E(\psi))^{\theta}

(5.30)

\displaystyle=\ -\theta(E(u(t))-E(\psi))^{\theta-1}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\left\|-\Delta u-\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

If there exists a sufficiently large
T>0
such that
\forall\ t>T
,
\|u-\psi\|_{H^{2}}<\sigma
, then by (
5.27
):

\displaystyle\quad\ \ -\frac{1}{\theta}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(E(u(t))-E(\psi))^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\ (E(u(t))-E(\psi))^{\theta-1}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\left\|-\Delta u-\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\ \|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore,

\int_{T}^{+\infty}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau<+\infty.

Moreover, since

\|u(t)-u(s)\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|\int_{s}^{t}u_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\int_{s}^{t}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

we have

\|u(t)-\psi\|_{L^{2}}\to 0,\quad t\to+\infty.

By the relative compactness of
\bigcup_{t\geq T}u(t)
in
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, (
1.11
) is proved.

Now we prove the existence of
T
.

Since in
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
u(\cdot,t_{n})\to\psi
and
E(u(t_{n}))\to E(\psi)
, for every
\epsilon>0
with
\epsilon<\sigma
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that when
n\geq N
:

\|u(\cdot,t_{n})-\psi\|_{H^{2}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2},\quad\frac{1}{\theta}(E(u(t_{n}))-E(\psi))^{\theta}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

(5.31)

For
n\geq N
, let

\overline{t_{n}}=\sup\{t\geq t_{n}\ \mid\ \|u(\cdot,s)-\psi\|_{H^{2}}<\sigma,\quad\forall s\in[t_{n},t]\}.

If there exists
n_{0}\geq N
such that
\overline{t_{n_{0}}}=+\infty
, then take
T=t_{n_{0}}
, proving the existence of
T
.

If
\forall\ n\geq N
,
\overline{t_{n}}<+\infty
,
then by Theorem
5.4
, for
t\in[t_{n},\overline{t_{n}}]
,

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(E(u(t))-E(\psi))^{\theta}

(5.32)

\displaystyle=\ -\theta\frac{\,\mathrm{d}E(u(t))}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(E(u)-E(\psi))^{\theta-1}

\displaystyle=\ \theta\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(E(u)-E(\psi))^{\theta-1}

\displaystyle=\ \theta\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\left\|-\Delta u-\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}(E(u)-E(\psi))^{\theta-1}

\displaystyle\geq\ \theta\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}.

Integrating (
5.32
) with respect to
t
gives:

\int_{t_{n}}^{\overline{t_{n}}}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\frac{1}{\theta}(E(u(t_{n}))-E(\psi))^{\theta}.

(5.33)

Combining (
5.31
)-(
5.33
),
\forall\ n\geq N
:

\displaystyle\|u(\overline{t_{n}})-\psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(\overline{t_{n}})-u(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}+\|u(t_{n})-\psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{n}}^{\overline{t_{n}}}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\|u(t_{n})-\psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\theta}(E(u(t_{n}))-E(\psi))^{\theta}+\|u(t_{n})-\psi\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon.

That is, as
n\to+\infty
,
u(\overline{t_{n}})\to\psi
in
L^{2}
.

By the relative compactness of
\bigcup_{t\geq t_{0}}u(t)
in
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, there exists a subsequence
u(\overline{t_{n}})
(still denoted as
u(\overline{t_{n}})
) such that
u(\overline{t_{n}})\to\psi
in
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Therefore, there exists
N^{\prime}\geq N
such that when
n\geq N^{\prime}
,
\|u(\overline{t_{n}})-\psi\|_{H^{2}}<\frac{\sigma}{2}
, which contradicts the definition of
\overline{t_{n}}
.

Theorem
1.3
is proved.
∎

5.5
Convergence Rate of Solutions

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

First, we prove that the solution
u
of equation (
1.1
) satisfies in
L^{2}(\Omega)
:

•

When
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
:
\|u(\cdot,t)-\psi\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{0}e^{-C_{1}t}
as
t\to+\infty
.

•

When
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
:
\|u(\cdot,t)-\psi\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{\theta}{1-2\theta}}
as

.

Indeed, by Theorem
5.4
and Remark
5.1
, when
t
is sufficiently large:

\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|\Delta u+\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\geq C|E(u)-E(\psi)|^{1-\theta},\quad\theta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right].

(5.34)

Let
y(t)=(E(u)-E(\psi))^{\theta}
. From (
5.30
) and (
5.34
):

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+C\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\leq 0.

(5.35)

Integrating (
5.35
):

\int_{t}^{+\infty}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\frac{1}{C}y(t),

thus

\|u(\cdot,t)-\psi\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|\int_{t}^{+\infty}u_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\int_{t}^{+\infty}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\frac{1}{C}y(t).

(5.36)

Combining (
5.34
) and (
5.35
) yields the differential inequality:

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+Cy^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}\leq 0.

(5.37)

When
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
, exponential decay follows directly from (
5.36
) and (
5.37
):

\|u(\cdot,t)-\psi\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{0}e^{-C_{1}t}.

When
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, consider the equation:

\begin{cases}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}z}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+Cz^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}=0\\
z(0)=y(0)>0\end{cases}

(5.38)

The solution to (
5.38
) is:

z(t)=\left(-C\frac{2\theta-1}{\theta}t+y(0)^{\frac{2\theta-1}{\theta}}\right)^{\frac{\theta}{2\theta-1}}.

Let
w(t)=z(t)-y(t)
. Subtracting (
5.38
) from (
5.37
):

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}w}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+C(z^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}-y^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}})\geq 0.

Applying the mean value theorem:

\begin{cases}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}w}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+C\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}\xi^{\frac{1-2\theta}{\theta}}w\geq 0\\
w(0)=0\end{cases}

(5.39)

where
\xi
lies between
y
and
z
.

Multiplying (
5.39
) by
e^{\int_{0}^{t}C\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}\xi(\tau)^{\frac{1-2\theta}{\theta}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau}
:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(w(t)e^{\int_{0}^{t}C\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}\xi(\tau)^{\frac{1-2\theta}{\theta}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau}\right)\geq 0,

and since
w(0)=0
:

w(t)e^{\int_{0}^{t}C\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}\xi(\tau)^{\frac{1-2\theta}{\theta}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau}\geq 0,

thus

w(t)\geq 0,

and therefore

\|u(\cdot,t)-\psi\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{C}y(t)\leq\frac{1}{C}z(t)=C(1+t)^{-\frac{\theta}{1-2\theta}}.

Next, we prove that in
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) hold for
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
respectively.
Let
v=u-\psi
. Then
v
satisfies:

\begin{cases}v_{t}-\Delta v=\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}-\frac{\lambda}{(1-\psi)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}},&x\in\Omega,t>0\\
v=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,t>0\\
v(x,0)=u_{0}(x)-\psi(x),&x\in\Omega\end{cases}

(5.40)

Multiplying (
5.40
) by
v
and integrating over
\Omega
:

\displaystyle\quad\ \ \frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\ =\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}-\frac{\lambda}{(1-\psi)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right)v\,\mathrm{d}x,

where

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}-\frac{\lambda}{(1-\psi)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}

\displaystyle\ =\lambda\left[\frac{1}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\ \left.+\frac{1}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(1-\psi)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\ =\lambda\left[\frac{1}{(1-u)^{2}}\frac{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}-(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\ \left.+\frac{1}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\frac{(1-\psi)^{2}-(1-u)^{2}}{(1-u)^{2}(1-\psi)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\ =\lambda\left[\frac{1}{(1-u)^{2}}\frac{(2+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)(\int_{\Omega}\frac{-v}{(1-u)(1-\psi)}\,\mathrm{d}x)}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\ \left.+\frac{1}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\frac{(2-u-\psi)v}{(1-u)^{2}(1-\psi)^{2}}\right].

Since
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a steady-state solution and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1-\delta
, we have
\|\frac{1}{1-\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M
and
\|\frac{1}{1-u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M
for all

.

Thus

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\ =\lambda\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{v}{(1-u)^{2}}\frac{(2+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)(\int_{\Omega}\frac{-v}{(1-u)(1-\psi)}\,\mathrm{d}x)}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right.

\displaystyle\quad\ \left.+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\frac{(2-u-\psi)v^{2}}{(1-u)^{2}(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\ \leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v|}{(1-u)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\Omega}\frac{|-v|}{(1-u)(1-\psi)}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{(2-u-\psi)v^{2}}{(1-u)^{2}(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\ \leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

yielding

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(5.41)

Multiplying (
5.40
) by
v_{t}
and integrating over
\Omega
:

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\ =\lambda\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{v_{t}}{(1-u)^{2}}\frac{(2+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)(\int_{\Omega}\frac{-v}{(1-u)(1-\psi)}\,\mathrm{d}x)}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right.

\displaystyle\quad\ \left.+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\frac{(2-u-\psi)v_{t}v}{(1-u)^{2}(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\ \leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v_{t}|}{(1-u)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\Omega}\frac{|-v|}{(1-u)(1-\psi)}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{(2-u-\psi)v_{t}v}{(1-u)^{2}(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\ \leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\frac{(C\|v\|_{L^{2}})^{2}}{2}+\frac{\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{2}.

Yielding

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(5.42)

Differentiating (
5.40
) with respect to
t
:

\displaystyle\quad v_{tt}-\Delta v_{t}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{2\lambda u_{t}}{(1-u)^{3}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}-\frac{2\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{t}}{(1-u)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{3}}-\frac{2\lambda\psi_{t}}{(1-\psi)^{3}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\ +\frac{2\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{\psi_{t}}{(1-\psi)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{(1-\psi)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{3}}.

Since
\psi
is a steady-state solution,
\psi_{t}=0
and

, so

v_{tt}-\Delta v_{t}=\frac{2\lambda v_{t}}{(1-u)^{3}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}-\frac{2\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{v_{t}}{(1-u)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{3}}.

(5.43)

Multiplying (
5.43
) by
v_{t}
and integrating over
\Omega
:

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{2\lambda}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{v_{t}^{2}}{(1-u)^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{2\lambda(\int_{\Omega}\frac{v_{t}}{(1-u)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\lambda}{(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{v_{t}^{2}}{(1-u)^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\frac{1}{4C_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(5.44)

Adding (

thus

(5.45)

When

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{y}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+\alpha\widetilde{y}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{2\theta}{1-2\theta}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}(t)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\alpha t}+Ce^{-\alpha t}\int_{0}^{t}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{2\theta}{1-2\theta}}e^{\alpha\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\alpha t}+Ce^{-\alpha t}\left[\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{2\theta}{1-2\theta}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\ e^{\frac{\alpha t}{2}}+(1+\frac{t}{2})^{-\frac{2\theta}{1-2\theta}}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}e^{\alpha\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{2\theta}{1-2\theta}}.

Hence

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{\theta}{1-2\theta}},\quad\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{\theta}{1-2\theta}},

and therefore

\displaystyle\|v\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-u}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}-\frac{\lambda}{(1-\psi)^{2}(1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{1-\psi}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+C\left\|\frac{1}{(1-u)^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{-v}{(1-u)(1-\psi)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right\|_{L^{2}}+C\left\|\frac{(2-u-\psi)v}{(1-u)^{2}(1-\psi)^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|v\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{\theta}{1-2\theta}}.

When
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
, we have
\|v\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{0}e^{-C_{1}t}
, so

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{y}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+\alpha\widetilde{y}\leq Ce^{-2C_{1}t}.

Thus

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}(t)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\alpha t}+Ce^{-\alpha t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\alpha-2C_{1})\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=Ce^{-\alpha t}+Ce^{-\alpha t}\frac{1}{\alpha-2C_{1}}\left(e^{(\alpha-2C_{1})t}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\alpha t}+Ce^{-2C_{1}t}+Ce^{-\alpha t}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\min\{\alpha,2C_{1}\}t}.

Hence

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-\min\{\frac{\alpha}{2},C_{1}\}t},\quad\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-\min\{\frac{\alpha}{2},C_{1}\}t},

and similarly

\|v\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|v\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-\min\{\frac{\alpha}{2},C_{1}\}t}.

The theorem is proved.
∎

6.3
Conjectures

Based on these numerical experiments, we propose:

Conjecture 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}(1\leq N\leq 3)
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary. For some
u_{0}\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, there exists
\lambda^{*}>0
such that:

•

For
\lambda<\lambda^{*}
, equation (
1.1
) has a unique global solution
u_{\lambda}
converging to a steady state as
t\to\infty
.

•

For
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
, the solution quenches in finite time and the steady-state equation (
1.4
) has no solution.

Conjecture 2
.

For
\lambda<\lambda^{*}
, let
u_{\lambda}
be defined as in Conjecture
1
. Then
\forall\ t<\infty
, the mapping
\lambda\longmapsto u_{\lambda}(\cdot,t)
is monotonically increasing in
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
