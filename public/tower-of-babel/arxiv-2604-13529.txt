Stabilization of finite-energy grid states of a quantum harmonic oscillator by reservoir engineering with two dissipation channels
Rémi Robin, Pierre Rouchon, Lev-Arcady Sellem

Abstract
We propose and analyze an experimentally accessible Lindblad master equation for a quantum harmonic oscillator, simplifying a previous proposal to alleviate implementation constraints. It approximately stabilizes periodic grid states introduced in 2001 by Gottesman, Kitaev and Preskill (GKP), with applications for quantum error correction and quantum metrology. We obtain explicit estimates for the energy of the solutions of the Lindblad master equation. We estimate the convergence rate to the codespace when stabilizing a GKP qubit, and numerically study the effect of noise. We then present simulations illustrating how a modification of parameters allows preparing states of metrological interest in steady-state.

2
Gottesman-Kitav-Preskill spaces

Denote
{\bf q}
and
{\bf p}
the position and impulsion operators of a quantum harmonic oscillator, satisfying
[{\bf q},{\bf p}]=i
.
From Glauber identity, for any
\eta>0
such that
d:=\eta^{2}/2\pi\in\mathbb{N}
, the periodic operators
e^{\pm i\eta{\bf q}}
and
e^{\pm i\eta{\bf p}}
commute, and generate the so-called stabilizer group
\{e^{im\eta{\bf q}}e^{in\eta{\bf p}}\,,(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}
.
Recall that in the
q
-representation,
{\bf p}=-i\frac{d}{dq}
so that
e^{\pm i\eta p}
is a translation operator on
q
. Then, for any wavefunction
|\psi\rangle=(\psi(q))_{q\in\mathbb{R}}
, we have:

e^{im\eta{\bf q}}e^{in\eta{\bf p}}|\psi\rangle=\left(e^{im\eta q}\psi(q+n\eta)\right)_{q\in\mathbb{R}}.

(1)

Solving for
+1
eigenstates of Eq. (
1
), one formally defines the GKP subspace of a qudit as the
d
-dimensionnal joint eigenspace of the stabilizers associated to the eigenvalue
+1
, spanned by the Dirac combs

\psi_{k}(q):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(q-(nd+k)\frac{2\pi}{\eta})
,
where
k\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
and
\delta
stands for the Dirac distribution.
In particular, when
d=2
, one can encode a qubit in the subspace spanned by the two GKP states, protected from the effect of local noise by the distance between the supports of the two logical states in phase space.
When
d=1
, on the other hand, the GKP subspace is
1
-dimensional (the corresponding Dirac comb is also known as a qunaught); while it cannot encode logical information, it has been considered for metrological applications

[
7
,
28
,
15
]
.

The GKP states defined above are not square integrable and thus not valid wavefunctions; several regularized versions, called finite-energy GKP states, coexist in the litterature
[
20
]
. We follow
[
11
]
and introduce a regularizing operator
{\bf E}_{\epsilon}:=e^{-\frac{\epsilon}{2}({\bf q}^{2}+{\bf p}^{2})}
parametrized by
\epsilon>0
. The finite-energy GKP states are then related to their infinite-energy counterpart through

|\psi_{k,\epsilon}\rangle:={\bf E}_{\epsilon}|\psi_{k}\rangle,

(2)

see e.g.
[
25
]
for explicit expressions of the corresponding wavefunctions.
Note that the energy of these states is defined as the expectation value of the photon-number operator
{\bf N}={\bf a}^{\dagger}{\bf a}
(with
{\bf a}=({\bf q}-i{\bf p})/\sqrt{2}
the so-called annihilation operator). For
\epsilon\ll 1
, it grows as
\langle{\bf N}\rangle\propto 1/\epsilon
on GKP states.

3
TWO-DISSIPATORS DYNAMICS

In
[
24
]
,
a Lindblad type dynamics was studied to stabilize a finite-energy square GKP code. It involves

4
dissipators, inspired from the stabilizers of the logical code,

\displaystyle{\bf L}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sin(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\bf q})+i\epsilon\cos(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\bf q}){\bf p},

(3)

\displaystyle{\bf L}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sin(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\bf p})-i\epsilon\cos(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\bf p}){\bf q},

\displaystyle{\bf L}_{3}

\displaystyle=\cos(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\bf q})-i\epsilon\sin(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\bf q}){\bf p}-e^{\epsilon\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}/2}\,{\bf Id},

\displaystyle{\bf L}_{4}

\displaystyle=\cos(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\bf p})+i\epsilon\sin(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}{\bf p}){\bf q}-e^{\epsilon\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}/2}\,{\bf Id},

where
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}=\sqrt{2\pi d}
is the lattice constant of a square GKP qudit for
d\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
: the choice
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}=2\sqrt{\pi}
(
d=2
) stabilizes a qubit, while
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}=\sqrt{2\pi}
(
d=1
) stabilizes a single state.

Here, we study the properties of the following modification of this scheme: we consider only the first two dissipators, but replace
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}
with
\eta=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\square}/2
, for the case of a qubit (
\eta=\sqrt{\pi}
) and a qunaught (
\eta=\sqrt{\pi/2}
), leading to the Lindblad equation

\frac{d}{dt}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=\mathcal{L}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}):=D[{\bf M}_{1}]({\boldsymbol{\rho}})+D[{\bf M}_{2}]({\boldsymbol{\rho}})

(4)

with
D[{\bf M}]({\boldsymbol{\rho}}):={\bf M}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\bf M}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}{\bf M}^{\dagger}{\bf M}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}-\frac{1}{2}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\bf M}^{\dagger}{\bf M}
and

\displaystyle{\bf M}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sin(\eta{\bf q})+i\epsilon\cos(\eta{\bf q}){\bf p}

(5)

\displaystyle{\bf M}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sin(\eta{\bf p})-i\epsilon\cos(\eta{\bf p}){\bf q}.

(6)

The motivation behing these modifications can be understood through the following naive intuition:
understanding the terms in
\epsilon
as perturbations, considering Lindblad operators as constraints applied to a system, and ignoring non-commutativity of operators for the sake of intuition, the constraint
\sin(\theta)=0
is equivalent to
\sin(2\theta)=0;\;\cos(2\theta)=1
.
As we’ll briefly explain in Sec.
8
,
both the reduction of
\eta
and the number of dissipators could significantly alleviate experimental implementation constraints.

4
); initialized in vacuum
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}=|0\rangle\langle 0|

.

, we observe the state converge to a GKP qubit state (for this initial state, the steady state is close to the so-called magic state

).

shows long-time simulations of Eq. (

) starting from the vacuum state
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}=|0\rangle\langle 0|

). It illustrates how this simplified dynamics already allows for the stabilization of GKP qubit or qunaught states.

4
A priori estimates

Direct calculations, adapted from the methodology in
[
26
]
, allow deriving explicit bounds on the energy, defined
as the expectation value
\langle{\bf N}\rangle=\tr({\bf N}{\boldsymbol{\rho}})
of the photon
number operator
{\bf N}={\bf a}^{\dagger} {\bf a}
, along trajectories of a density operator governed by Eq. (
4
).
Here, we obtain a priori estimates by formal computations,
led as if the dimension of the underlying Hilbert space
were finite. We plan to exploit these estimates for a fully
rigorous mathematical analysis in future publications.

Theorem 1
.

For
\eta>0
, there exists
\overline{\epsilon}\in(0,2/\eta)
such that for all
\epsilon<\overline{\epsilon}
and
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}
with
\tr({\bf N}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})<\infty
, for all
t\geq 0
:

\frac{d}{dt}\tr({\bf N}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t})\leq-\lambda(\epsilon,\eta)\tr({\bf N}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t})+\mu(\epsilon,\eta)

(7)

with
\lambda>0,\mu>0
some constants depending on
\epsilon,\eta
.
In particular, the energy along trajectories remains bounded by
\max\left(\tr({\bf N}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}),\mu(\epsilon,\eta)/\lambda(\epsilon,\eta)\right)
at all times. Moreover,
\lambda(\epsilon,\eta)
can be chosen arbitrarily close below
2\epsilon\eta(1-\epsilon\eta/2)
.

The proof of this theorem is mostly technical and deferred to the Appendix.
Note that this result, relating only to the stability of the dynamics, is expressed for arbitrary values
\eta>0
, although only the values
\eta\in\{\sqrt{\pi},\sqrt{\pi/2}\}
are considered in the rest of the paper.

5
Convergence of logical observables

Let us consider the case of a qubit (
d=2
, corresponding to
\eta=\sqrt{\pi}
). Two notions of convergence are of interest. On the one hand, any state should converge to the codespace, that is the two-dimensional subspace spanned by GKP states. On the other hand, adding spurious noise process on top of the stabilizing dynamics can lead to an evolution inside the logical subspace: in the infinite time limit, any state decoheres to a unique mixed state, and the convergence rate of this evolution inside the codespace quantifies the timescale on which logical information decoheres.

To quantify convergence to the codespace, let us recall that GKP states are, up to small errors due to normalization, close to
+1
eigenstates of the GKP stabilizers. Instead of solving for the evolution of states through the Lindblad equation, we can solve for the evolution of periodic observables through its adjoint,
i.e.
in the so-called Heisenberg picture.

For observables of the form
{\bf O}_{f}=f(2\eta{\bf q})
with
f
a smooth
2\pi
-periodic function, one checks
[{\bf M}_{2},{\bf O}_{f}]=0
(using
2\eta^{2}=2\pi
), so that only
{\bf M}_{1}
contributes to the Heisenberg evolution. A direct computation, presented in the Appendix, yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{*}({\bf O}_{f})

\displaystyle=-\big(\epsilon+2\epsilon^{2}\eta\big)\eta\sin(2\eta{\bf q})\,f^{\prime}(2\eta{\bf q})

\displaystyle\quad+4\epsilon^{2}\eta^{2}\cos^{2}(\eta{\bf q})\,f^{\prime\prime}(2\eta{\bf q}),

(8)

where primes denote derivatives of
f
with respect to its argument
\theta=2\eta{\bf q}
. This reduces the study to the one-dimensional operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f(\theta)=\sin(\theta)\,f^{\prime}(\theta)-\sigma\,(1+\cos\theta)\,f^{\prime\prime}(\theta),

(9)

up to a positive prefactor depending on
\epsilon
and
\eta
.

Lemma 1

(Symmetry)
.

For
0<\sigma<2
, the operator
\mathcal{A}
is symmetric and non-negative on
L^{2}(w)
with weight
w(\theta)=(1+\cos\theta)^{1/\sigma-1}
. More precisely, integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\langle f,\mathcal{A}g\rangle_{w}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sigma\,(1+\cos\theta)^{1/\sigma}\,f^{\prime}(\theta)\,g^{\prime}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

(10)

Proof.

Write
w_{2}(\theta)=(1+\cos\theta)^{1/\sigma}
and note that

\displaystyle w_{2}^{\prime}=-\frac{\sin\theta}{\sigma}\,(1+\cos\theta)^{1/\sigma-1}=-\frac{\sin\theta}{\sigma}\,w.

(11)

Integrating by parts,

\displaystyle\langle f,\mathcal{A}g\rangle_{w}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}f\big[\sin\theta\,g^{\prime}-\sigma\,(1+\cos\theta)\,g^{\prime\prime}\big]w\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=-\sigma\int_{0}^{2\pi}f\,g^{\prime\prime}\,w_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}f\,g^{\prime}\,\sin\theta\,w\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\sigma\int_{0}^{2\pi}f^{\prime}\,g^{\prime}\,w_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta+\sigma\int_{0}^{2\pi}f\,g^{\prime}\,w_{2}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{2\pi}f\,g^{\prime}\,\sin\theta\,w\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

(12)

The last two terms cancel since
\sigma\,w_{2}^{\prime}+\sin\theta\,w=0
.
∎

We now establish a weighted Poincaré inequality for
\mathcal{A}
. The difficulty lies in the degeneracy of
w_{2}
at
\theta=\pi
, which we handle via localized Hardy-type estimates. We decompose the torus into
I_{1}^{+}=[\pi,3\pi/2]
,
I_{1}^{-}=[\pi/2,\pi]
,
I_{1}=I_{1}^{+}\cup I_{1}^{-}
and
I_{2}=[0,\pi/2]\cup[3\pi/2,2\pi]
.

Lemma 2

(Localized Hardy inequality)
.

For
0<\sigma<2
and
g\in C^{\infty}(\pi,3\pi/2)
with
g(3\pi/2)=0
,

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{2}(w,\,I_{1}^{+})}^{2}\leq\frac{16\sigma^{2}}{(2-\sigma)^{2}}\,\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(w_{2},\,I_{1}^{+})}^{2}.

(13)

The same holds on
I_{1}^{-}
for
g
with
g(\pi/2)=0
, by symmetry.

Proof.

Set
\phi(\theta)=(1+\cos\theta)^{1/\sigma-1/2}
and differentiate:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\theta}\big(g^{2}\phi\big)

\displaystyle=2g\,g^{\prime}\,\phi

\displaystyle\quad-\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\sin\theta\,g^{2}\,(1+\cos\theta)^{1/\sigma-3/2}.

(14)

Integrating over
[\pi,3\pi/2]
, the boundary terms vanish (
\phi(\pi)=0
and
g(3\pi/2)=0
), giving

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\int_{\pi}^{3\pi/2}g^{2}\,w\,\frac{(-\sin\theta)}{\sqrt{1+\cos\theta}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\quad=-2\int_{\pi}^{3\pi/2}g\,g^{\prime}\,(1+\cos\theta)^{1/\sigma-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

(15)

On
(\pi,3\pi/2)
,
-\sin\theta/\sqrt{1+\cos\theta}\geq 1
, so the left-hand side is at least
(1/\sigma-1/2)\|g\|_{L^{2}(w,I_{1}^{+})}^{2}
. Applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the right-hand side,

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\|g\|_{L^{2}(w,I_{1}^{+})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\|g\|_{L^{2}(w,I_{1}^{+})}\,\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(w_{2},I_{1}^{+})},

(16)

whence

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{2}(w,I_{1}^{+})}\leq\frac{4\sigma}{2-\sigma}\,\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(w_{2},I_{1}^{+})}.

(17)

∎

Lemma 3

(Poincaré estimate on
I_{2}
)
.

On
I_{2}
the weights
w
and
w_{2}
are bounded above and below by positive constants. Hence, for
g
with
g(3\pi/2)=0
(or
g(\pi/2)=0
),

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{2}(w,\,I_{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\sigma}\,\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(w_{2},\,I_{2})}^{2},

(18)

\displaystyle|g(\pi/2)-g(3\pi/2)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\sigma}\,\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(w_{2},\,I_{2})}^{2}.

(19)

Theorem 2

(Spectral gap)
.

For
0<\sigma<1/2
, there exists
C_{\sigma}>0
such that for any smooth
2\pi
-periodic
f
with
\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\theta)\,w(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta=0
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}\leq C_{\sigma}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+\cos\theta)^{1/\sigma}\,|f^{\prime}(\theta)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

(20)

Proof.

We decompose the norm and subtract boundary values to create functions vanishing at the interface points:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(w)}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-f(3\pi/2)\|_{L^{2}(w,\,I_{1}^{+})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|f-f(\pi/2)\|_{L^{2}(w,\,I_{1}^{-})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|f\|_{L^{2}(w,\,I_{2})}

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\|w\|_{L^{1}(I_{1})}}\big(|f(3\pi/2)|+|f(\pi/2)|\big).

(21)

The first two terms are controlled by the Hardy inequality (Lemma
2
). For the
I_{2}
term, we write

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(w,\,I_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-f(3\pi/2)\|_{L^{2}(w,\,I_{2})}

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\|w\|_{L^{1}(I_{2})}}\,|f(3\pi/2)|,

(22)

and the Poincaré bound (Lemma
3
) controls the first term. The same lemma bounds
|f(3\pi/2)-f(\pi/2)|
, so it remains to control a single point value, say
|f(\pi/2)|
.

The zero-mean condition gives

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{1}}\,|f(\pi/2)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\big(f(\pi/2)-f(\theta)\big)\,w(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\|w\|_{L^{1}}}\,\|f-f(\pi/2)\|_{L^{2}(w)}.

(23)

The right-hand side is split over
I_{1}^{-}
(Hardy),
I_{2}
(Poincaré), and
I_{1}^{+}
where we use

\displaystyle\|f-f(\pi/2)\|_{L^{2}(w,\,I_{1}^{+})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|f-f(3\pi/2)\|_{L^{2}(w,\,I_{1}^{+})}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+|f(3\pi/2)-f(\pi/2)|\,\sqrt{\|w\|_{L^{1}(I_{1}^{+})}},

(24)

with both terms already estimated. Collecting all bounds yields the result.
∎

Corollary 1

(Convergence of periodic observables)
.

Recall that
\sigma=\frac{2\epsilon\eta}{1+2\epsilon\eta}
, so
0<\sigma<1/2
is equivalent to
\epsilon\eta<1/2
. Let
C_{\sigma}
be the constant from Theorem
2
. For any smooth
2\pi
-periodic function
f
with
\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\theta)\,w(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta=0
, the Heisenberg evolution of
{\bf O}_{f}=f(2\eta{\bf q})
satisfies

\displaystyle\|f_{t}\|_{L^{2}(w)}\leq e^{-\gamma_{\sigma}\,t}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(w)},\,\gamma_{\sigma}=\frac{(\epsilon+2\epsilon^{2}\eta)\eta\,\sigma}{C_{\sigma}}=\frac{2\epsilon^{2}\eta^{2}}{C_{\sigma}},

(25)

where
f_{t}
denotes the function such that
e^{t\mathcal{L}^{*}}{\bf O}_{f}={\bf O}_{f_{t}}
.

More generally, for any smooth
2\pi
-periodic
f
, the observable
e^{t\mathcal{L}^{*}}{\bf O}_{f}
converges exponentially to
\bar{f}_{w}\cdot{\bf Id}
at rate
\gamma_{\sigma}
, where
\bar{f}_{w}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}f\,w\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,/\,\int_{0}^{2\pi}w\,\mathrm{d}\theta
is the weighted mean of
f
.

6
Effect of photon loss

To study the robustness to noise, we modify the Lindblad equation to add a term modeling photon loss at rate
\kappa>0
:

\frac{d}{dt}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=D[{\bf M}_{1}]({\boldsymbol{\rho}})+D[{\bf M}_{2}]({\boldsymbol{\rho}})+\kappa D[{\bf a}]({\boldsymbol{\rho}}).

(26)

Note that without the stabilization (dissipators in
{\bf M}_{1}
and
{\bf M}_{2}
), any state converges exponentially to the vacuum state
|0\rangle
.
When the stabilization is on, the solution to Eq. (
26
) stays close to the codespace at all times instead. However, as shown Fig.
2
, despite the confinement of solutions to the codespace, the logical coherences inside this space itself decay.

7
Stabilization of a GKP qunaught

Apart from their application for quantum error correction, GKP grid states have also been considered as valuable resources in metrology.
They can allow to measure simultaneously the commuting modular observables derived from

e^{2i\sqrt{\pi}{\bf q}}
and
e^{2i\sqrt{\pi}{\bf p}}
, or equivalently
{\bf q}\textrm{ mod }\sqrt{\pi}
and
{\bf p}\textrm{ mod }\sqrt{\pi}
,
circumventing the Heisenberg uncertainty principle attached to measurements of
{\bf q}
and
{\bf p}
.
These states can be generated through well-known universal control methods for harmonic oscillators, such as the ECD
[
8
]
or SNAP methods
[
13
]

POSSIBLE IMPLEMENTATIONS

In practice, the Lindblad equation (
4
)
models a quantum harmonic oscillator coupled to an unrealistic exotic bath. It can, however, be approximated through reservoir engineering methods as used in previous GKP proposals
[
24
]
or for other bosonic codes
[
17
]
.
In a nutshell, reservoir engineering methods rely on coupling a system of interest to a strongly damped auxiliary system; the complexity of bath engineering then transforms into the complexity of the coupling to engineer.

The complexity of these methods typically scales with the number of dissipation channels to engineer. For instance, each dissipation channel can be approximated by a dedicated ancilla system – translating the number of dissipators to hardware complexity. Alternately, each necessary dissipators can be engineered stroboscopically, which corresponds to a Trotter decomposition of the evolution – the achievable dissipation rate is then inversely proportional to the number of dissipators in this decomposition.
In this regard, stabilizing GKP states with half the numbers of dissipation channels can significantly alleviate implementation constraints.

circuitQED

In the case of circuitQED, both the system of interest and the ancilla are harmonic oscillators, corresponding to electromagnetic modes typically at microwave frequencies. The implementation method proposed in
[
24
]
for the stabilization of a GKP qubit with four dissipators (Eqs.
3
) could straightforwardly be adapted to our new dynamics with only two, since they directly correspond to their first two dissipators but with a different value of the parameter
\eta
.
In addition, in their case
\eta
is a function of the impedance of the mode of interest, which needed to be at twice the resistance quantum, a high value possibly difficult to achieve in practice. Reducing
\eta
alors alleviate this requirement.

Trapped ions

Trapped ions also appear as a particularly appealing platform for GKP experiment, with several demonstration of GKP stabilization reported in the litterature. In this case, the harmonic oscillator is a motional degree of freedom of an ion (or possibly a collective mode of an ensemble of ions), and the auxiliary system is the spin degree of freedom of the same ion.
The
\eta
parameter in this paper depends on a quantity called the Lamb-Dicke parameter in that field
[
29
]
, which can depend both on the ion species and specificities of the trap.
A possibly interesting direction to avoid fine-tuning
\eta
could be to resort to so-called Quantum Signal Processing (QSP) methods. Notably, in the context of trapped ions, the recent preprint
[
21
]
introduced a method to approximate nonlinear Hamiltonian based on their Fourier decomposition, a feature particularly appealing in our context where all relevant operators are perturbations of periodic operators.

9
CONCLUDING REMARKS

In conclusion, we have presented a simplified, two-dissipator Lindblad dynamics that approximately stabilizes finite-energy Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) states, and established its convergence properties. While numerical simulations show that the logical decoherence rates scales qualitatively worse than a previously proposed four-dissipator dynamics in presence of photon loss, it trades robustness for implementation simplicity. Both methods are also based on very similar approaches, so that one can expect technological developments used to realize the stabilization proposed here to also be directly useful for the more robust but complex four-dissipator approach; it can thus appear as a tempting first experimental objective to validate experimental developments.
We also showed that with a slight modification of parameters the same approach allows for the stabilization of GKP qunaught states for quantum metrology.

Note that we focused here on so-called square GKP states. We refer the reader to e.g.
[
6
,
23
]
for a general theory of GKP qudits of arbitrary logical dimension on more general lattices. Similar stabilizing dynamics could be derived for these generalizations, in a similar fashion to this article where Lindblad operators are associated to logical stabilizers of the GKP code.

Finally, we hope to leverage the formal a priori energy estimates
obtained to develop a fully rigorous analysis of the system in
forthcoming publications.

Numerical simulations were run on a laptop GPU with double precision arithmetic (Nvidia Quadro RTX 3000 with 6Go of RAM), using the libraries jaxquantum
[
14
]
for the manipulation of GKP states and dynamiqs
[
12
]
for the numerical integration of Lindblad equations. The related source codes are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Derivation of Eq. (

Here, we obtain a priori estimates by formal computations,
led as if the dimension of the underlying Hilbert space
were finite. We plan to exploit these estimates for a fully
rigorous mathematical analysis in future publications.

Formally, the evolution of the expectation value of an observable (that is a hermitian operator)
{\bf O}
on the solution
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}
to Eq. (
4
) can be obtained by duality through

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tr({\bf O}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t})

\displaystyle=\tr(\mathcal{L}^{*}({\bf O}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t})

(30)

\displaystyle=\tr\left(D^{*}[{\bf M}_{1}]({\bf O})\,{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}+D^{*}[{\bf M}_{2}]({\bf O})\,{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\right)

(31)

with
D^{*}[{\bf M}]({\bf O}):=\frac{1}{2}{\bf M}^{\dagger}[{\bf O},{\bf M}]+h.c.

Using
{\bf N}=\frac{{\bf q}^{2}+{\bf p}^{2}-1}{2}
allows deriving estimates on
\mathcal{L}^{*}({\bf N})
. Below we’ll repeatedly use the relation
[f({\bf A}),{\bf B}]=f^{\prime}({\bf A})[{\bf A},{\bf B}]
valid for
f
analytical and
{\bf A},{\bf B}
such that
[{\bf A},[{\bf A},{\bf B}]]=0
. We’ll also make use of the following operator inequalities, valid for any
\alpha,\beta>0
and
r\in(0,1)
,
as well as their equivalent for
{\bf p}
:

\displaystyle-{\bf q}\sin(2\eta{\bf q})

\displaystyle\leq|{\bf q}|

(32)

\displaystyle\cos(2\eta{\bf q})

\displaystyle\leq{\bf Id}

(33)

\displaystyle-\alpha{\bf q}^{2}+\beta|{\bf q}|

\displaystyle\leq-r\alpha{\bf q}^{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\alpha(1-r)}{\bf Id}.

(34)

We find

\displaystyle[{\bf q}^{2},{\bf M}_{1}]

\displaystyle=[{\bf q}^{2},\sin(\eta{\bf q})+i\epsilon\cos(\eta{\bf q}){\bf p}]

(35)

\displaystyle=i\epsilon\cos(\eta{\bf q})[{\bf q}^{2},{\bf p}]=-2\epsilon{\bf q}\cos(\eta{\bf q})

(36)

\displaystyle{\bf M}_{1}^{\dagger}[{\bf q}^{2},{\bf M}_{1}]

\displaystyle=-2\epsilon{\bf q}\cos(\eta{\bf q})\sin(\eta{\bf q})+2i\epsilon^{2}{\bf p}{\bf q}\cos^{2}(\eta{\bf q})

(37)

\displaystyle=-\epsilon{\bf q}\sin(2\eta{\bf q})+i\epsilon^{2}{\bf p}{\bf q}(\cos(2\eta{\bf q})+{\bf Id})

(38)

hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\bf M}_{1}^{\dagger}[{\bf q}^{2},{\bf M}_{1}]+h.c.

\displaystyle\quad=-\epsilon{\bf q}\sin(2\eta{\bf q})+\frac{i\epsilon^{2}}{2}[{\bf p},{\bf q}(\cos(2\eta{\bf q})+{\bf Id})]

(39)

\displaystyle\quad=-\epsilon{\bf q}\sin(2\eta{\bf q})+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}(\cos(2\eta{\bf q})+{\bf Id})-\eta\epsilon^{2}{\bf q}\sin(2\eta{\bf q})

(40)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\epsilon(1+\epsilon\eta)|{\bf q}|+\epsilon^{2}{\bf Id}.

(41)

Similar but slightly tedious computations lead to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\bf M}_{1}^{\dagger}[{\bf p}^{2},{\bf M}_{1}]+h.c.\leq-2\epsilon\eta(1-\frac{\epsilon\eta}{2}){\bf p}^{2}+3\epsilon\eta^{3}{\bf Id}

(42)

so that all in all, for any
r\in(0,1)
:

\displaystyle D^{*}[{\bf M}_{1}]({\bf N})

\displaystyle\leq-\epsilon\eta(1-\frac{\epsilon\eta}{2}){\bf p}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon(1+\epsilon\eta)|{\bf q}|

(43)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon^{2}+3\epsilon\eta^{3}){\bf Id}

(44)

\displaystyle\leq-r\epsilon\eta(1-\frac{\epsilon\eta}{2}){\bf p}^{2}+C(r,\epsilon,\eta){\bf Id}

(45)

with
C
depending on
r,\epsilon,\eta
from combining the constant terms coming from using Eqs. (
34
) and (
44
).
Combining with the corresponding calculation for
{\bf M}_{2}
, we finally obtain:

\mathcal{L}^{*}({\bf N})\leq-2r\epsilon\eta(1-\frac{\epsilon\eta}{2}){\bf N}+C^{\prime}(r,\epsilon,\eta){\bf Id}.

(46)

Note that here
r
is a free parameter in
(0,1)
required in proof steps, but can be chosen arbitrarily close to
1
.

\displaystyle[{\bf O}_{f},{\bf M}_{1}]

where we used
