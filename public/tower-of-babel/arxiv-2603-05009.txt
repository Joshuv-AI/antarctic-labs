Observational and Thermodynamic aspects of one-dimensional Dark Energy EoS parametrization models
Anirban Chatterjee, Yungui Gong

Abstract
We examine the observational viability and physical implications of the Gong–Zhang (GZ) dark–energy equation–of–state parametrizations using exclusively late–time cosmological probes. Two one-dimensional parametrization models, GZ–Type I and GZ–Type II, are constrained with Type Ia supernovae (Union3, Pantheon+SH0ES, and DES–SN5YR), DESI baryon acoustic oscillations, and cosmic chronometer measurements of H ⁡ ( z ) H(z) . Bayesian inference combined with information–criteria diagnostics shows that both parametrizations provide competitive alternatives to Λ \Lambda CDM, while the GZ–Type II model is consistently favored, exhibiting reduced parameter degeneracy and stronger Jeffreys–scale support. Beyond background expansion tests, we employ configuration entropy as a thermodynamically motivated probe of structure formation. We demonstrate that the entropy–production rate sensitively traces the impact of dynamical dark energy on late–time gravitational clustering while preserving standard early–time behavior. Our results establish the Gong–Zhang framework as a physically transparent and observationally consistent extension of Λ \Lambda CDM, with configuration entropy providing a complementary diagnostic of late–time cosmic acceleration.

A minimal and widely adopted strategy to model dynamical dark energy is through phenomenological parametrizations of the equation–of–state parameter

Among the various proposals, one–dimensional parametrizations play a special role owing to their simplicity, stability, and direct interpretability in terms of observational constraints. In this context, the Gong–Zhang parametrizations

They are explicitly constructed to recover the matter–dominated background at high redshift and to generate smooth, monotonic deviations from

It quantifies the degree of spatial inhomogeneity in the matter distribution and monotonically decreases as gravitational instability amplifies initially small density perturbations. As structures form and the Universe becomes increasingly clumpy, configuration entropy dissipates, encoding the irreversible flow of information associated with structure formation.

II
Observational data, cosmological framework, and statistical methodology

We constrain the Gong–Zhang Type I (GZ1) and Type II (GZ2) dark–energy
parametrizations using a combination of late–time cosmological observations
that probe the background expansion history without relying on any
CMB–derived information or early–Universe physics.
Throughout this work we adopt units
c=1
and assume a spatially flat FLRW
geometry.
The sampled parameter vector is

\theta=\{\Omega_{m},\;h,\;h\,r_{d},\;w_{0}\},

(1)

where
\Omega_{m}
is the present matter density parameter,

h=H_{0}/100
is the dimensionless Hubble constant,

w_{0}
characterizes the dark–energy equation of state,
and the sound–horizon scale
r_{d}
is treated as a phenomenological late–time
parameter absorbed into the combination
h\,r_{d}
.
This setup enables a fully model–independent use of late–time data, avoiding
assumptions about recombination physics or the baryon density.

(a) Baryon acoustic oscillations:

We use BAO measurements from the first year of observations of the DESI survey
DESI:2024mwx
,
covering the redshift range
0.1<z<4.2
.
DESI probes the large–scale clustering of matter through multiple tracers,
including bright galaxies, luminous red galaxies, emission–line galaxies, and
Ly
\alpha
forest quasars.
The BAO information is provided in terms of the compressed observables

\frac{D_{M}(z)}{r_{d}}
and
\frac{D_{H}(z)}{r_{d}}
, where
D_{M}(z)
denotes the transverse comoving distance and
D_{H}(z)=1/H(z)
is the Hubble distance. The sound–horizon scale
r_{d}
is not computed from early–Universe physics but
is instead treated as a free late–time parameter, ensuring a purely
phenomenological and model–independent BAO analysis
DESI:2024mwx
;
Colgain:2024xqj
.

(b) Type Ia supernovae:

We employ three independent SNe Ia compilations:
the Union3 binned dataset
Scolnic:2021amr
,
the Pantheon+SH0ES (PPS) sample
Rubin:2023jdq
,
and the DES–SN5YR compilation
DES:2024jxu
.
Union3 combines 24 supernova samples and spans the redshift range

0.01<z<2.26
, while the PPS dataset covers
0.001<z<2.26
with corrections for
peculiar velocities.
The DES–SN5YR compilation consists of 1635 spectroscopically confirmed SNe Ia
distributed over
0.10<z<1.13
.
All supernova likelihoods are constructed using the full covariance matrices
provided by the respective collaborations, with analytic marginalization over
the nuisance absolute magnitude.

(c) Cosmic chronometers:

Direct measurements of the Hubble parameter
H(z)
are included using the
cosmic chronometer method
Gadbail:2024rpp
, which exploits the differential age evolution of
passively evolving galaxies.
The dataset consists of 32
H(z)
measurements
Gaztanaga:2008xz
;
Oka:2013cba
spanning the redshift range

0.07<z<2.36
and provides an independent probe of the expansion rate that does
not rely on any cosmological distance ladder.

In a spatially flat FLRW spacetime, the luminosity distance is given by
Hu:1995en
,
d_{L}(z)=(1+z)\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dx}{H(x)}=\frac{1+z}{H_{0}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dx}{E(x)}
, where
E(z)=H(z)/H_{0}
.
The transverse comoving distance is
D_{M}(z)=d_{L}(z)/(1+z)
, and the
volume–averaged BAO distance is defined as,
D_{V}(z)=\left[z\,D_{M}^{2}(z)\,D_{H}(z)\right]^{1/3}.

For the standard
\Lambda
CDM model, the normalized Hubble expansion reads

E^{2}(z)=\Omega_{r}(1+z)^{4}+\Omega_{m}(1+z)^{3}+1-\Omega_{m}-\Omega_{r}.

(2)

For the Gong–Zhang Type I (GZ1) dark–energy parametrization,

w(z)=\frac{w_{0}}{1+z},

(3)

the corresponding expansion history is

E^{2}(z)=\Omega_{r}(1+z)^{4}+\Omega_{m}(1+z)^{3}+\bigl(1-\Omega_{m}-\Omega_{r}\bigr)(1+z)^{3}\exp\!\left(\frac{3w_{0}z}{1+z}\right).

(4)

For the Gong–Zhang Type II (GZ2) parametrization,

w(z)=\frac{w_{0}e^{z/(1+z)}}{1+z},

(5)

the normalized Hubble expansion becomes

E^{2}(z)=\Omega_{r}(1+z)^{4}+\Omega_{m}(1+z)^{3}+\bigl(1-\Omega_{m}-\Omega_{r}\bigr)(1+z)^{3}\exp\!\left[\,3w_{0}\!\left(e^{\frac{z}{1+z}}-1\right)\right]

(6)

We perform parameter estimation using Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC)
ForemanMackey:2013

simulations implemented with the publicly available
emcee
sampler
Lewis:2019xzd
.
The total likelihood is constructed as

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm SN}\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm OHD}\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm BAO},

(7)

and sampled over the parameter vector

\theta=\{\Omega_{m},\;h,\;h\,r_{d},\;w_{0}\}.

(8)

Flat priors are imposed on all model parameters.
The resulting posterior distributions and parameter correlations are analyzed
using the
GetDist
package, from which marginalized constraints are
derived.

To quantitatively assess the statistical performance of the Gong–Zhang
dark–energy parametrizations relative to the reference
\Lambda
CDM model,
we employ information–theoretic model selection criteria that penalize model
complexity, namely the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and the Bayesian
Information Criterion (BIC).
These criteria provide complementary diagnostics by balancing goodness of fit
against the number of free parameters.
The AIC is defined as

\mathrm{AIC}=\chi^{2}_{\min}+2m,

(9)

while the BIC is given by

\mathrm{BIC}=\chi^{2}_{\min}+m\ln N,

(10)

where
\chi^{2}_{\min}
denotes the minimum chi–square value of the fit,

m
is the number of independent model parameters, and
N
is the total number
of data points included in the likelihood.

For each dataset combination, we compute the differences

\Delta\mathrm{AIC}=\mathrm{AIC}_{\rm model}-\mathrm{AIC}_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}},\qquad\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=\mathrm{BIC}_{\rm model}-\mathrm{BIC}_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}},

such that negative values indicate a statistical preference for the
corresponding Gong–Zhang parametrization over
\Lambda
CDM.
The strength of the evidence is interpreted using the Jeffreys scale.
Following standard conventions,

|\Delta\mathrm{IC}|<2
corresponds to weak or inconclusive evidence,

2\leq|\Delta\mathrm{IC}|<6
indicates positive evidence,

6\leq|\Delta\mathrm{IC}|<10
implies strong evidence,
and
|\Delta\mathrm{IC}|\geq 10
signifies very strong to decisive evidence
in favor of the model with the lower information criterion value.
These thresholds are applied consistently to both AIC and BIC results. This combined AIC–BIC analysis enables a robust and transparent comparison
between the Gong–Zhang parametrizations and the
\Lambda
CDM model
deCruzPerez:2024shj
, allowing
us to quantify whether the improved fit provided by a dynamical dark–energy
equation of state justifies the introduction of additional model freedom.

III
Results based on observational analysis

We constrain the Gong–Zhang Type I (GZ1) parametrization, and subsequently
compare it with the Type II (GZ2) case, using three independent combinations
of late–time cosmological probes, namely
Union3+BAO+OHD, Pantheon+SH0ES+BAO+OHD, and DES–SN5YR+BAO+OHD.
These datasets jointly probe the background expansion history and the cosmic
distance scale through supernova luminosity distances, cosmic chronometer
measurements of
H(z)
, and DESI DR2 BAO observables
D_{M}(z)/r_{d}
and

D_{H}(z)/r_{d}
.
The resulting marginalized one– and two–dimensional posterior distributions
are shown in the left panel of Fig.
1
.
Across all dataset combinations, the inferred matter density is tightly
constrained in the range
\Omega_{m}\simeq 0.23
–
0.27
, while the Hubble
parameter lies within
h\simeq 0.64
–
0.70
, with the
Pantheon+SH0ES combination systematically favors slightly higher values due
to the SH0ES absolute calibration.
The BAO data impose strong constraints on the distance scale, leading to a
pronounced positive correlation between

and
hr_{d}
.
In the
\Omega_{m}
–
w_{0}
plane, the contours exhibit a clear anti–correlation,
indicating that an increase in the matter density can be partially
compensated by a more negative dark–energy equation of state.
The GZ1 model mildly favors quintessence–like behavior with

,
although the
\Lambda
CDM limit remains allowed within the
1

confidence level.
Consistent with these moderate parameter degeneracies, the Jeffreys–scale
comparison based on
\Delta\mathrm{AIC}
and
\Delta\mathrm{BIC}
indicates
only weak to moderate evidence in favor of GZ1 relative to
\Lambda
CDM for
Union3 and Pantheon+SH0ES, with stronger preference emerging only when

The corresponding MCMC constraints for the Gong–Zhang Type II (GZ2)
parametrization are displayed in the right panel of Fig.

.
While the background parameters
\Omega_{m}
,
h
, and
hr_{d}
are constrained
at a similar level of precision as in GZ1, the posterior structure involving
the equation–of–state parameter
w_{0}
shows important differences.
In particular, the two–dimensional contours involving

are noticeably
tighter and less elongated, reflecting a reduced degeneracy between the
dark–energy sector and the matter–distance parameters.
The GZ2 model consistently favors a negative equation of state in the range

w_{0}\simeq-1
to
-0.6
, with minimal overlap with the
cosmological–constant value.
This behavior persists across all three dataset combinations, including
DES–SN5YR+BAO+OHD, which provides the strongest constraints.
The reduced parameter correlations and narrower posteriors naturally
translate into a superior statistical performance: the Jeffreys–scale
analysis shows strong evidence for GZ2 over
\Lambda
CDM for
Union3+BAO+OHD and decisive evidence for
DES–SN5YR+BAO+OHD.
Overall, the GZ2 parametrization provides a more efficient and less
degenerate description of late–time cosmic acceleration within the
observational data considered.

III.4
Cosmographic and sound-speed analysis of Gong–Zhang models

Cosmography provides a purely kinematical description of the cosmic expansion
history, independent of the underlying gravitational dynamics or the specific
matter content of the Universe.
It relies on a Taylor expansion of the scale factor around the present epoch
and therefore offers a model–independent framework to characterize late–time
cosmic acceleration and possible deviations from the standard
\Lambda
CDM
scenario.
The expansion history is encoded in the hierarchy of cosmographic parameters,
defined through successive time derivatives of the scale factor as

H=\frac{\dot{a}}{a},\qquad q=-\frac{\ddot{a}}{aH^{2}},\qquad j=\frac{\dddot{a}}{aH^{3}},\qquad s=\frac{a^{(4)}}{aH^{4}},

(11)

where
H
is the Hubble parameter,
q
the deceleration parameter,
j
the
jerk, and
s
the snap.

For a general Friedmann expansion history expressed in terms of the normalized
Hubble parameter
E(z)=H(z)/H_{0}
, the cosmographic parameters can be written as
functions of redshift according to

\displaystyle q(z)

\displaystyle=-1+(1+z)\frac{E^{\prime}(z)}{E(z)},

(12)

\displaystyle j(z)

\displaystyle=q(z)\bigl[2q(z)+1\bigr]+(1+z)\,q^{\prime}(z),

(13)

\displaystyle s(z)

IV
Configuration Entropy as a Physical Probe of Structure Growth

The growth of cosmic structure is an inherently irreversible process driven by
gravitational instability, during which the matter distribution progressively
departs from large–scale homogeneity.
While the adiabatic sound speed
c_{s}^{2}
characterizes the microphysical
stability of dark–energy perturbations and regulates the propagation of
pressure fluctuations, its imprint on structure formation is ultimately
realized through the clustering of matter.
Configuration entropy provides an information–theoretic and thermodynamic
framework to quantify this macroscopic outcome by tracking how gravitational
instability redistributes matter and accumulates information over cosmic time.
Unlike conventional growth observables that probe only instantaneous amplitudes
or local rates, configuration entropy encodes the integrated history of
structure formation and is therefore sensitive to both the background expansion
and the perturbative properties of the cosmic fluid.

IV.1
Physical meaning of configuration entropy

For a pressureless matter fluid with density
\rho(\mathbf{x},t)
defined over
a comoving volume
V
, the configuration entropy is introduced as a
measure of the spatial information content of the matter distribution.
To ensure a well–defined entropy functional, we work with the normalized
density contrast

p(\mathbf{x},t)=\rho(\mathbf{x},t)/M
, where

M=\int_{V}\rho(\mathbf{x},t)\,dV
is the conserved total mass.
The configuration entropy is then defined as

S_{c}(t)=-\int_{V}p(\mathbf{x},t)\,\ln p(\mathbf{x},t)\,dV,

(18)

Up to an additive constant set by the total mass, this definition is equivalent to the Shannon entropy of the normalized density field. This quantity provides a global measure of the degree of spatial disorder in
the matter distribution.
In a perfectly homogeneous universe, the density is uniform,

p(\mathbf{x},t)=\text{const.}
, and the configuration entropy attains its
maximum value, remaining constant in time.
As gravitational instability amplifies density perturbations and matter
progressively clusters, the effective configuration space available to the
system is reduced, leading to a monotonic decrease of
S_{c}
.
From a physical perspective, configuration entropy quantifies how efficiently
gravity transforms an initially smooth matter distribution into structured
patterns such as filaments, walls, and halos. Because it encodes the cumulative outcome of nonlinear structure formation,
configuration entropy offers a complementary, thermodynamic description of
cosmic inhomogeneity that goes beyond local or perturbation–based diagnostics.
Unlike standard measures that track the growth of individual modes,

S_{c}
captures the global redistribution of matter and provides a unified
characterization of large–scale structure formation in an expanding universe.

IV.2
Entropy evolution and the role of structure growth

The evolution of configuration entropy follows directly from mass conservation
and the continuity equation governing a pressureless matter fluid in an
expanding universe.
Expressed in terms of the scale factor
a
, the entropy evolution can be written
as

\frac{dS_{c}}{da}=-\frac{3}{a}\bigl(S_{c}-M\bigr)-D(a)\,D^{\prime}(a),

(19)

where
M=\int_{V}\rho(\mathbf{x},t)\,dV
denotes the conserved total mass within
the comoving volume
V
, and
D(a)
is the linear growth factor of matter density
perturbations, with a prime indicating differentiation with respect to
a
.
When the normalized density
p=\rho/M
is adopted in the definition of

S_{c}
, the quantity
M
simply sets the constant reference level of entropy and
does not affect the dynamical evolution. Each term in Eq. (
19
) admits a clear physical interpretation.
The first term represents the dilution effect associated with the cosmological
expansion, reflecting the tendency of the background expansion to preserve
homogeneity.
In contrast, the second term acts as a genuine source term that captures entropy
production driven by the growth of density perturbations.
This contribution encodes the irreversible redistribution of matter induced by
gravitational clustering and establishes a direct connection between structure
formation and the thermodynamic evolution of the cosmic matter distribution. Eq. (
19
) therefore highlights a central physical result:
the evolution of configuration entropy is directly sourced by the growth of
matter perturbations.
Any physical mechanism that alters the growth history—such as a dynamical
dark–energy equation of state or modified expansion rate—will necessarily leave
a characteristic imprint on the entropy evolution.
Configuration entropy thus provides a global, physically motivated diagnostic
that links late–time structure formation to the underlying cosmological
dynamics.

IV.3
Background expansion and growth in Gong–Zhang models

Within the Gong–Zhang framework, the impact of dynamical dark energy on the
evolution of configuration entropy enters through two closely related channels:
the background expansion rate and the linear growth of matter perturbations.
The background evolution is governed by the dimensionless Hubble parameter

E(a)\equiv H(a)/H_{0}
, which can be written in the general form

E^{2}(a)=\Omega_{m0}a^{-3}+\Omega_{r0}a^{-4}+\Omega_{{\rm DE},0}\,\exp\!\left[-3\int_{1}^{a}\bigl(1+w(a^{\prime})\bigr)\,d\ln a^{\prime}\right],

(20)

explicitly encoding the integrated history of the dark–energy equation of
state. We use
w(a)=w[z(a)]
interchangeably, with
a=(1+z)^{-1}
. For the Gong–Zhang Type I (GZ1) parametrization, the equation of state is given
by
w(a)=w_{0}\,a
, ensuring that dark energy is dynamically suppressed at early
times and becomes relevant only at late epochs.
The Type II (GZ2) form,

w(a)=w_{0}\,a\,e^{\,1-a}
, introduces a mild exponential modulation that allows
for richer late–time dynamics while preserving the standard matter–dominated
era at high redshift.
In both cases, the modified expansion history feeds directly into the evolution
of matter perturbations. The growth of linear matter density perturbations, which acts as the direct
source of entropy production, is computed using the Linder–Jenkins growth
equation,

D^{\prime\prime}(a)+\frac{3}{2}\!\left[1-\frac{w(a)}{1+X(a)}\right]\frac{D^{\prime}(a)}{a}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{X(a)}{1+X(a)}\frac{D(a)}{a^{2}}=0,

(21)

where a prime denotes differentiation with respect to the scale factor
a
, and

X(a)=\frac{\Omega_{m}(a)}{1-\Omega_{m}(a)}.

(22)

This equation consistently accounts for the interplay between the evolving
dark–energy equation of state and the clustering of pressureless matter. Together, the background expansion encoded in
E(a)
and the growth factor

D(a)
fully determine the entropy evolution through the source term

D(a)D^{\prime}(a)
appearing in the configuration–entropy equation.
As a consequence, the Gong–Zhang parametrizations naturally generate distinct
and observationally testable entropy–production histories.
At the same time, their construction ensures compatibility with early–time
cosmology, since dark–energy effects are dynamically suppressed during the
radiation– and matter–dominated eras. From the linear growth factor
D(a)
, the (logarithmic) growth rate is defined as

f(a)\equiv\frac{d\ln D}{d\ln a}=\frac{a\,D^{\prime}(a)}{D(a)}.

(23)

To quantify departures from the standard cosmological model, we consider the
relative growth-rate deviation with respect to
\Lambda
CDM,

\Delta f(a)\equiv\frac{f_{\rm model}(a)-f_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(a)}{f_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(a)},

(24)

which directly measures the suppression or enhancement of structure growth
induced by dynamical dark energy.

IV.4
Numerical implementation and entropy diagnostics

The entropy evolution is evaluated by fixing the conserved mass
M
and imposing
initial conditions deep in the matter–dominated era (
a_{i}\ll 1
), where dark–energy
effects are negligible.
We normalize the configuration entropy to
S_{c}(a_{i})=1
and present all results in
terms of the ratios
S_{c}(a)/S_{c}(a_{i})
or
\Delta R(a)
, thereby removing any
dependence on the arbitrary entropy normalization.
The linear growth factor is initialized with
D(a_{i})\propto a_{i}
and normalized to
unity at the present epoch,
D(1)=1
, ensuring standard early–time growth.
With these choices, the entropy diagnostics isolate genuine late–time physical
effects induced by dynamical dark energy.

The coupled evolution of the linear growth factor and the configuration entropy
is solved numerically, with initial conditions imposed deep in the
matter–dominated era to ensure insensitivity to early–time transients.
The growth factor is normalized to unity at the present epoch,
D(a=1)=1
,
and we track the normalized entropy ratio
S_{c}(a)/S_{c}(a_{i})
in order to remove
any dependence on the arbitrary normalization of the initial entropy. A particularly informative diagnostic of the thermodynamic impact of structure
formation is the entropy–production rate,

R(a)\equiv\frac{d\ln S_{c}}{da},

(25)

which quantifies the instantaneous rate at which information is redistributed
by gravitational clustering during cosmic evolution.
To isolate the effect of dynamical dark energy, we further define the relative
entropy–rate deviation with respect to the standard cosmological model,

\Delta R(a)\equiv R_{\rm model}(a)-R_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(a),

(26)

thereby providing a direct, purely thermodynamic comparison between competing
cosmological scenarios.
This quantity highlights deviations from
\Lambda
CDM in the late–time regime
and serves as a sensitive probe of how modifications to the expansion history
and growth of structure imprint themselves on the global entropy evolution of
the Universe.

IV.5

\Delta f(a)

\Lambda

A shallow late–time minimum is followed by a partial recovery toward the present epoch, reflecting the competition between accelerated expansion, which inhibits gravitational clustering, and residual matter growth that persists even in the dark–energy dominated regime. The smooth and monotonic evolution of
\Delta f(a)
across all dataset combinations demonstrates that the Gong–Zhang parametrizations modify late–time structure growth in a controlled and physically consistent manner, without introducing instabilities or unphysical behavior.

V
Conclusion

In this work we have performed a comprehensive observational and physical investigation of the Gong–Zhang Type I (GZ1) and Type II (GZ2) dynamical dark–energy parametrizations using three independent combinations of late–time cosmological probes, namely Union3+BAO+OHD,
Pantheon+SH0ES+BAO+OHD, and DES–SN5YR+BAO+OHD. By marginalizing over the sound–horizon scale and avoiding any CMB–derived priors, we adopted a fully phenomenological framework that isolates late–time cosmic dynamics. The resulting marginalized one– and two–dimensional posterior distributions (Fig.
1
) demonstrate that both parametrizations provide stable, well–behaved fits to all dataset combinations, yielding consistent constraints on the fundamental parameter set
\{\Omega_{m},\,h,\,hr_{d},\,w_{0}\}
. In particular, the inferred values of
\Omega_{m}
and
h
remain fully
compatible with standard late–time cosmology, while the equation–of–state parameter
w_{0}
departs from the cosmological–constant limit in a controlled and physically interpretable manner.

The correlation matrices shown in Fig.
2
reveal the dominant degeneracy directions among the matter sector, distance scale, and dark–energy dynamics. Both models exhibit the expected strong correlation between
h
and
hr_{d}
, reflecting the manner in which BAO data constrain the distance scale, as well as a pronounced anti–correlation between
\Omega_{m}
and
w_{0}
. Importantly, these correlations are systematically weaker for the GZ2 parametrization, indicating reduced parameter degeneracies and improved separability between the matter and dark–energy sectors. This behavior is reflected directly in the information–criteria analysis: the Jeffreys–scale comparison shown in Fig.
3
establishes that GZ1 is weakly to moderately favored over
\Lambda
CDM depending on the dataset, while GZ2 is consistently preferred, with strong evidence for Union3+BAO+OHD and decisive evidence for DES–SN5YR+BAO+OHD. These results demonstrate that a minimal one–dimensional time–dependent equation of state can provide a statistically competitive and, in the case of GZ2, superior description of late–time cosmic acceleration.
The marginalized constraints in the
\Omega_{m}
–
w_{0}
plane and the
corresponding one–dimensional posteriors of
w_{0}
(Fig.
4
) further clarify the distinct phenomenology of the two parametrizations. While GZ1 mildly favors quintessence–like behavior with
w_{0}
clustered around
-1
and remaining consistent with
\Lambda
CDM at the
1
–
2\sigma
level, GZ2 robustly selects a negative equation of state in the range
w_{0}\simeq-0.55
to
-0.35
, with minimal overlap with the cosmological–constant value.
This tighter localization of
w_{0}
explains the enhanced statistical
performance of GZ2 and highlights the physical relevance of the Gong–Zhang construction in capturing late–time deviations from
\Lambda
CDM.
Beyond background expansion, we have examined the kinematic and dynamical implications of the two models through a cosmographic analysis. The cosmographic corner plots in Fig.
5
show that both parametrizations predict a consistent late–time expansion history characterized by negative deceleration and well–constrained higher–order kinematic parameters. These results provide a model–independent validation of the Gong–Zhang dynamics and demonstrate that the inferred cosmic acceleration is smooth and free of pathological behavior. In Fig.
6
, we show the behavior of the adiabatic sound speed squared for the Gong–Zhang dark–energy parametrizations in the
(z,\omega_{0})
plane, highlighting the regions where
0<c_{s}^{2}<1
and hence perturbative stability and causal propagation are maintained across the observable redshift range.
Since the sound speed regulates the propagation of pressure perturbations and controls the response of dark energy to inhomogeneities, these stability domains provide an essential microphysical consistency check on the background dynamics.

Motivated by this, we next investigate the macroscopic imprint of dynamical dark energy on structure formation by analyzing the relative deviation of the linear growth rate with respect to
\Lambda
CDM (Fig.
7
). Both Gong–Zhang parametrizations reproduce the standard early–time behavior during matter domination, while exhibiting a suppressed growth of cosmic structures at late times, thereby linking the perturbative properties inferred from
c_{s}^{2}
to observable signatures in the growth of matter fluctuations. The smooth and monotonic nature of this deviation represents a key
advantage of the Gong–Zhang models: they modify the late–time growth
history without introducing abrupt transitions or instabilities.
This behavior is fully consistent with current large–scale structure
constraints and reflects the physical influence of a time–dependent
equation of state on gravitational clustering. Finally, we have explored the thermodynamic imprint of dynamical dark energy through the evolution of the configuration–entropy production rate. The entropy–production–rate deviation
\Delta R(a)
across different
dataset combinations (Fig.
8
), together with its deviation relative to
\Lambda
CDM for representative initial conditions (Fig.
9
), reveals that both Gong–Zhang parametrizations regulate the dissipation of configuration entropy in a controlled and physically meaningful manner. In particular, the transition to dark–energy domination damps the entropy production rate and drives it toward a stationary regime, reflecting a reduced efficiency of structure formation at late times. This behavior provides a thermodynamic interpretation of cosmic acceleration and establishes configuration entropy as a powerful and complementary diagnostic of dynamical dark energy.

Taken together, our results demonstrate that minimal one–dimensional
equation–of–state parametrizations, when constructed with appropriate high–redshift behavior as in the Gong–Zhang models, can successfully reproduce late–time cosmological observations, improve upon
\Lambda
CDM in a statistically meaningful way, and yield a coherent physical picture of background expansion, structure growth, and entropy flow. Among the two parametrizations, the Type II model emerges as the more viable candidate, exhibiting tighter parameter constraints, reduced degeneracies, stronger statistical preference, and a clearer thermodynamic signature. Looking ahead, several extensions of the present work are of immediate interest. The inclusion of full DESI BAO measurements, weak–lensing and redshift– space–distortion data, and future supernova samples from Rubin–LSST will enable significantly tighter constraints on the Gong–Zhang parametrizations. On the theoretical side, it would be valuable to investigate whether the Gong–Zhang forms can be embedded within concrete scalar–field or modified–gravity realizations, and to explore the implications for nonlinear structure formation and gravitational collapse. More broadly, the configuration–entropy framework introduced here opens a new thermodynamic window onto cosmic acceleration and provides a physically motivated criterion for assessing the viability of dynamical dark–energy models beyond standard background and growth diagnostics.
