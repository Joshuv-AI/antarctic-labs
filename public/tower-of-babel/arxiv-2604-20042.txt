On Threshold Pairwise Compatibility Graphs
Sheikh Azizul Hakim, Md. Shamsuzzoha Bayzid

Abstract
Pairwise Compatibility Graphs ( PCGs ) form a tree-metric graph class that originated in phylogeny and has since attracted sustained interest in graph theory. Several natural generalizations have been proposed in order to overcome the expressive limitations of classical PCGs , including k k -interval-PCGs , k k -OR-PCGs , and k k -AND-PCGs . In this paper, we introduce ( k , t ) (k,t) -threshold-PCGs , a threshold-based framework that unifies these generalized notions: adjacency is determined by whether at least t t among k k underlying PCG predicates accept the vertex pair. We investigate the expressive power of this model from both constructive and asymptotic viewpoints. On the positive side, we show that every graph on n n vertices is a ( n , t ) (n,t) -threshold-PCG for every 1 ≤ t ≤ n 1\leq t\leq n . On the negative side, we prove that for every fixed pair ( k , t ) (k,t) , the class of ( k , t ) (k,t) -threshold-PCGs is asymptotically rare among all graphs. As a consequence, we obtain sharp separations from previously studied models, including a strict expressive gap relative to k k -interval-PCGs . We also study explicit obstruction families through incidence graphs and derive additional structural consequences for the conjunction case, including the strictness of the k k -AND-PCG hierarchy and the failure of closure under complement.

Introduction

Pairwise Compatibility Graphs
(
PCGs
) were introduced by Kearney, Munro, and Phillips in the context of phylogeny, where compatibility relations among taxa are modeled through distances in an underlying edge-weighted tree
[
21
]
. Over time, however, the notion has developed into a graph-theoretic representation model of independent interest. Informally, a graph is a
PCG
if its vertices can be associated with the leaves of an edge-weighted tree so that two vertices are adjacent exactly when their leaf-to-leaf distance lies in a prescribed interval. This definition is easy to state and geometrically appealing, but the class it defines has proved to be surprisingly subtle.

A large body of work has been devoted to understanding the expressive power of
PCGs
. On the positive side, several nontrivial graph classes are known to admit
PCG
representations; for example, graphs of Dilworth number at most two are
PCGs

[
11
,
12
]
, triangle-free outerplanar
3
-graphs are
PCGs

[
25
]
, and further sufficient conditions are known for broader families
[
10
,
20
]
. On the negative side, the class is not universal: it was shown that not every graph is a
PCG

[
29
]
, and subsequent work developed explicit
obstruction families, proof techniques for non-
PCG
ness, and enumerative studies
of minimal non-
PCG
graphs

[
15
,
4
,
5
,
3
]
.
Surveys such as
[
24
,
13
,
14
]
document both the
breadth of the area and the persistence of several basic open problems. Even at small orders, the boundary is delicate: all graphs on at most seven vertices are
PCGs

[
6
]
, while more intricate behavior appears immediately beyond that range.

These limitations have motivated a number of natural generalizations. One direction relaxes the single-interval requirement while retaining a common witness tree, leading to
k
-interval-PCG

[
1
,
19
]
. Another direction combines several
PCG
representations on the same vertex set by Boolean operations, giving rise to classes such as
k
-OR-PCG
and
k
-AND-PCG

[
8
,
14
]
. These models enlarge the expressive scope of classical
PCGs
, but they have largely been studied in parallel rather than within a single framework. As a consequence, some basic comparative questions have remained poorly understood. For example, the recent literature asks for sharper separations between multi-interval and Boolean-combination models, for explicit obstruction families in low-level
OR
/
AND
classes, and for a better understanding of distinguished hard families from the multi-interval literature
[
8
,
14
]
.

In this paper, we unify these directions through a threshold-based framework. Given

k
underlying
PCG
predicates, we declare a pair of vertices adjacent whenever
at least
t
of them accept the pair. This yields the class of

(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
s. The definition subsumes several previously
studied models as special cases: ordinary
PCGs
correspond to
(1,1)
,

k
-OR-PCG
is recovered when
t=1
, and

k
-AND-PCG
is recovered when
t=k
. In this sense,

(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
s provide a single framework for studying
fixed-size combinations of tree-distance graph representations.

This framework leads to a clear dichotomy. If the number of constituent predicates is
allowed to grow with the graph size, then the model becomes universal: every graph on

n
vertices is a
(n,t)
-threshold-PCG
for every
1\leq t\leq n
. In contrast, if the number of predicates is fixed, then even after allowing all thresholds, the resulting classes occupy only an asymptotically vanishing fraction of all graphs. Thus the threshold model is both expressive and structurally restrictive. Moreover, it provides a convenient setting in which several phenomena can be studied uniformly: counting arguments yield separations from multi-interval representations, incidence graphs give explicit obstruction families, and the conjunction case exhibits both a strict hierarchy and failure of complement closure.

Our contributions

This paper initiates the systematic study of

(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
s. Our main contributions are as follows.

•

We prove a universality theorem: every graph on
n
vertices is a

(n,t)
-threshold-PCG
for every
1\leq t\leq n
.

•

We prove an asymptotic sparsity theorem: for every fixed integer
k
, even
after allowing all thresholds
t\in[k]
, the union of the corresponding threshold
classes has asymptotic density zero among all graphs.

•

We compare threshold combinations with multi-interval representations and show
that
(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
s can be substantially more expressive.
In particular, if
f(k)\in o(k^{2}/\log k)
, then for all sufficiently large
k

there exists a graph that is a
(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
but not an

f(k)
-interval-PCG
. As a consequence, for all sufficiently large

k
,

k\textsc{-interval-{PCG}}\subsetneq k\textsc{-OR-{PCG}}.

•

We develop explicit obstruction families via set-system incidence graphs. This
yields concrete lower bounds for threshold representations, explicit infinite
families outside low-level threshold classes, and quantitative bounds for some graph classes.

•

In the conjunction setting, we prove that the hierarchy of

k
-AND-PCG
classes is strict:

k\textsc{-AND-{PCG}}\subsetneq(k+1)\textsc{-AND-{PCG}}\qquad\text{for every }k\geq 1.

•

We also show that the classes
k
-
AND
-
PCG
and k
-interval-PCG
are not closed under complement.

Taken together, these results show that threshold combinations provide a robust and
meaningful extension of the
PCG
paradigm. They are expressive enough to unify
and strengthen several known variants, yet rigid enough to admit strong counting
theorems, explicit obstructions, and nontrivial hierarchy results. We believe this makes

(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
s a natural object of study in their own right and
a useful organizing framework for future work on tree-distance graph representations.

The next proposition records the three basic special cases that justify our threshold viewpoint.

Proposition 6
.

Let

1.

a graph is a

if and only if it is a

-threshold-PCG

2.

a graph is a

-OR-PCG
if and only if it is a
(k,1)
-threshold-PCG
;

3.

a graph is a
k
-AND-PCG
if and only if it is a
(k,k)
-threshold-PCG
.

Proof.

The first statement is immediate from the definitions. For the second statement, a pair
uv

(k,1)
-threshold-PCG
if and only if it belongs to at least one among
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
, that is, if and only if

uv\in E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup E_{k}.

Hence this is exactly
k
-OR-PCG
. Similarly, a pair
uv
is adjacent in a
(k,k)
-threshold-PCG
if and only if it belongs to all of
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
, that is, if and only if

uv\in E_{1}\cap\cdots\cap E_{k}.

Thus this is exactly
k
-AND-PCG
.
∎

6

-threshold-PCG

3
Universality When the Number of Predicates Grows

We now show that the threshold model becomes universal once the number of underlying
PCG
predicates is allowed to grow with the graph size.

We first record two simple padding lemmas. Since both
K_{n}
and
\overline{K_{n}}
are trivially
PCGs
, they may be used as auxiliary predicates in threshold constructions.

Lemma 7
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices. If
G
is a
q
-OR-PCG
, then
G
is a
(n,t)
-threshold-PCG
for every

1\leq t\leq n-q+1.

Proof.

Since
G
is a
q
-OR-PCG
, write

G=G_{1}\cup\cdots\cup G_{q},

where each
G_{i}
is a
PCG
. Add
t-1
copies of
K_{n}
and
n-q-(t-1)
copies of
\overline{K_{n}}
. Then every edge of
G
is accepted by at least one of
G_{1},\dots,G_{q}
and by all
t-1
copies of
K_{n}
, hence by at least
t
predicates in total. Every nonedge is accepted by none of
G_{1},\dots,G_{q}
and only by the
t-1
copies of
K_{n}
, hence by exactly
t-1
predicates. Therefore,
G
is a
(n,t)
-threshold-PCG
.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices. If
G
is a
q
-AND-PCG
, then
G
is a
(n,t)
-threshold-PCG
for every

q\leq t\leq n.

Proof.

Since
G
is a
q
-AND-PCG
, write

G=G_{1}\cap\cdots\cap G_{q},

where each
G_{i}
is a
PCG
. Add
t-q
copies of
K_{n}
and
n-t
copies of
\overline{K_{n}}
. Then every edge of
G
is accepted by all
q
original predicates and by all
t-q
copies of
K_{n}
, hence by at least
t
predicates. Every nonedge is missing from at least one of
G_{1},\dots,G_{q}
, so it is accepted by at most
(q-1)+(t-q)=t-1
predicates. Therefore,
G
is a
(n,t)
-threshold-PCG
.
∎

Theorem 9
.

For every integer
n\geq 1
and every
t\in[n]
, every graph on
n
vertices is a
(n,t)
-threshold-PCG
.

Proof.

Let
G
be any graph on
n
vertices.

If
n\leq 7
, then
G
is a
PCG
by
[
6
]
, hence a
1
-OR-PCG
. Lemma
7
with
q=1
gives the claim for all
t\in[n]
.

Assume now that
n\geq 8
. By the known
OR
bound
[
8
]
, every graph on
n
vertices is a
q_{\mathrm{OR}}
-OR-PCG
with

q_{\mathrm{OR}}=\left\lceil\frac{1}{3}(n-7)\right\rceil+1.

Hence Lemma
7
shows that
G
is a
(n,t)
-threshold-PCG
for every

1\leq t\leq n-\left\lceil\frac{1}{3}(n-7)\right\rceil.

Also, by the known
AND
bound
[
8
]
, every graph on
n
vertices is a
q_{\mathrm{AND}}
-AND-PCG
with

q_{\mathrm{AND}}=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor.

Hence Lemma
8
shows that
G
is a
(n,t)
-threshold-PCG
for every

\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\leq t\leq n.

These two ranges cover all of
[n]
, since

n-\left\lceil\frac{1}{3}(n-7)\right\rceil\geq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor.

Therefore,
G
is a
(n,t)
-threshold-PCG
for every
t\in[n]
.
∎

Theorem
9
shows that the threshold framework becomes fully expressive once the number of predicates is allowed to grow linearly with the number of vertices.

4
Asymptotic Sparsity for Fixed
k

We now consider the opposite regime, where the number of underlying predicates is fixed. Even if the threshold parameter is allowed to vary over all values in
[k]
, the resulting graph classes remain asymptotically negligible.

Lemma 10
.

There exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that the number
P_{n}
of labeled

PCGs
on vertex set
[n]
satisfies
P_{n}\leq e^{Cn\log n}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a labeled
PCG
on vertex set
[n]
. By suppressing degree-
2

internal vertices in a witness tree, we may assume that
G=\textsc{PCG}(T,d_{\min},d_{\max})

for some reduced weighted tree
T
with leaf set
[n]
. Such a tree has at most
2n-3

edges, and the number of leaf-labeled reduced binary tree topologies on
[n]
is

(2n-5)!!=e^{O(n\log n)}

[
16
,
26
]
.

Fix one such topology
T
, and let
w_{1},\dots,w_{m}
be its edge weights, where

m\leq 2n-3
. For each pair of leaves
u,v\in[n]
, the distance
d_{T}(u,v)
is a linear
form in the variables
w_{1},\dots,w_{m}
. Hence, for fixed
T
, the represented graph is
determined by the sign pattern of the
O(n^{2})
linear polynomials

d_{T}(u,v)-d_{\min}
and
d_{T}(u,v)-d_{\max}
in the
m+2=O(n)
variables

w_{1},\dots,w_{m},d_{\min},d_{\max}
. By Theorem 3 of
[
27
]
, the number of such sign
patterns is at most
e^{O(n\log n)}
.

Multiplying by the
e^{O(n\log n)}
possible reduced leaf-labeled tree topologies gives

P_{n}\leq e^{O(n\log n)}
, as required.
∎

Theorem 11
.

Let
k\geq 1
be fixed. Then there exists an integer
n_{0}
such that, for every

n\geq n_{0}
, there exists a graph on
n
vertices that is neither a

(k,t)
-threshold-
PCG
nor the complement of a

(k,t)
-threshold-
PCG
, for every
t\in[k]
.

Proof.

Let
P_{n}
denote the number of labeled
PCGs
on vertex set
[n]
.
By Lemma
10
,

P_{n}\leq e^{Cn\log n}.

For fixed
n,k,t
, let
T_{n,k,t}
denote the number of labeled

(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
s on
[n]
. Any ordered
k
-tuple of labeled

PCGs

(G_{1},\dots,G_{k})
on
[n]
determines, via the threshold rule, at most one
labeled
(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
. Since there are
P_{n}
choices for each

G_{i}
, there are at most
(P_{n})^{k}
such tuples. Hence

T_{n,k,t}\leq(P_{n})^{k}\leq e^{Ck\,n\log n}.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{n,k}
be the family of labeled graphs on
[n]
that are

(k,t)
-threshold-
PCGs
for some
t\in[k]
, together with their complements.
Then

|\mathcal{F}_{n,k}|\leq\sum_{t=1}^{k}2T_{n,k,t}\leq 2k\,e^{Ck\,n\log n}.

On the other hand, the total number of labeled graphs on
[n]
is

2^{\binom{n}{2}}=e^{\Theta(n^{2})}.

Since
k
is fixed,

2k\,e^{Ck\,n\log n}=o\!\left(2^{\binom{n}{2}}\right).

Hence, for all sufficiently large
n
,

2k\,e^{Ck\,n\log n}<2^{\binom{n}{2}}.

Therefore, some labeled graph on
[n]
lies outside
\mathcal{F}_{n,k}
.
By definition, this graph is neither a
(k,t)
-threshold-
PCG
nor the complement of one, for every
t\in[k]
.
∎

It is convenient to record the counting consequence explicitly.

Corollary 12
.

Let
k\geq 1
be fixed, and let
\mathcal{T}_{n,k}
denote the family of labeled graphs on
[n]
that are
(k,t)
-threshold-
PCGs
for some

. Then

\frac{|\mathcal{T}_{n,k}|}{2^{\binom{n}{2}}}\to 0\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty.

In particular, the union over all thresholds

still has asymptotic density zero.

Proof.

By the proof above,

|\mathcal{T}_{n,k}|\leq\sum_{t=1}^{k}T_{n,k,t}\leq k\,e^{Ck\,n\log n}.

Since

and

∎

Theorem
11
and Corollary

stand in sharp contrast both with Section
3
and with the early
optimism surrounding
PCGs
. While the model was introduced by Kearney, Munro,
and Phillips
[
21
]
, it was later conjectured that every graph
might be a
PCG
, a possibility subsequently disproved in

[
29
]
. Our result shows that every fixed threshold extension remains
far from universal in a much stronger asymptotic sense: for fixed
k
, even after allowing
all thresholds
t\in[k]
and even up to complement, these classes occupy only an
asymptotically vanishing corner of the graph universe.

5
Separating Multi-interval and Threshold Models

We now examine more closely the relationship between threshold combinations and
multi-interval representations. These two frameworks arise from different ways of
relaxing the classical
PCG
model. In a
k
-interval-PCG
,
one keeps a single witness tree and increases expressive power by allowing several
admissible distance intervals on that tree
[
1
]
. In a

(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
, by contrast, one allows several independent

PCG
predicates and combines them through a threshold rule. Since both are natural extensions of
PCG
, it is
important to compare their expressive strength. The next theorem shows that, from an
asymptotic viewpoint, threshold combinations can be substantially more powerful than
multi-interval representations.

Theorem 13
.

Let
f(k)\in o\!\left(\frac{k^{2}}{\log k}\right)
, and let
t
be an integer such that
1\leq t\leq k
. Then there exists an integer
k_{0}
such that, for every
k\geq k_{0}
, there exists a graph that is a
(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
but not an
f(k)
-interval-PCG
.

Proof.

Let
I_{k}^{(f)}
denote the number of labeled
f(k)
-interval-PCG
s on
vertex set
[k]
. By Theorem
9
, every graph on
k
vertices is a

(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
. Thus it suffices to show that

I_{k}^{(f)}<2^{\binom{k}{2}}

for all sufficiently large
k
.

Fix a labeled
f(k)
-interval-PCG
on
[k]
. After suppressing
degree-
2
internal vertices in a witness tree, we may assume that its representation
uses a reduced weighted tree with leaf set
[k]
and at most
2k-3
edges. The number of
such leaf-labeled tree topologies is
e^{O(k\log k)}
.

Now fix one such topology. Its representation is determined by at most
2k-3
edge
weights together with at most
2f(k)
interval endpoints, so the relevant parameter space
has dimension

d=O(k+f(k)).

For this fixed topology, the represented graph changes only when the relative order of a
leaf-to-leaf distance and an interval endpoint changes. Since there are
O(k^{2})

leaf-to-leaf distances and
2f(k)
endpoints, the parameter space is cut by a hyperplane
arrangement of size

H=O(k^{2}f(k)+f(k)^{2}).

Because
f(k)\in o(k^{2}/\log k)
, we in particular have
H=O(k^{4})
.

For a fixed reduced leaf-labeled tree topology, let
d=O(k+f(k))
denote the number
of real parameters (the edge weights together with the interval endpoints), and let

H=O(k^{2}f(k)+f(k)^{2})
denote the number of relevant comparison hyperplanes. The
parameter space is therefore partitioned by an arrangement of at most
H
hyperplanes
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since the represented graph is constant on each cell of this
arrangement, the number of distinct graphs realizable from this fixed topology is at most
the number of cells.

By the standard bound on the number of cells in a hyperplane arrangement, this number is
at most

\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{H}{i}

(see, e.g.,
[
30
]
). Using

\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{H}{i}\leq(d+1)\binom{H}{d}\leq(d+1)\left(\frac{eH}{d}\right)^{d},

we obtain

\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{H}{i}=e^{O\!\left(d\log(H/d)\right)}.

Now
d=O(k+f(k))
, and since
f(k)\in o(k^{2}/\log k)
, we have

H=O(k^{4})
,
so

\log(H/d)=O(\log k).

Therefore, the number of realizable graphs for this fixed topology is at most

e^{O((k+f(k))\log k)}.

Finally, the number of reduced leaf-labeled tree topologies on
[k]
is

e^{O(k\log k)}
. Multiplying these bounds, we obtain

I_{k}^{(f)}\leq e^{O(k\log k)}\cdot e^{O((k+f(k))\log k)}=e^{O((k+f(k))\log k)}.

On the other hand, the total number of labeled graphs on
[k]
is

2^{\binom{k}{2}}=e^{\Theta(k^{2})}
. Since
f(k)\in o(k^{2}/\log k)
, we have

(k+f(k))\log k=o(k^{2})
. Therefore

I_{k}^{(f)}=e^{o(k^{2})}=o\!\left(2^{\binom{k}{2}}\right).

So for all sufficiently large
k
, there exists a graph on
k
vertices that is not an

f(k)
-interval-PCG
. Since every graph on
k
vertices is a

(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
, the result follows.
∎

A particularly relevant consequence is obtained by taking
t=1
and
f(k)=k
.

Corollary 14
.

There exists an integer
k_{0}
such that, for every
k\geq k_{0}
, there exists a graph
that is a
k
-OR-PCG
but not a

k
-interval-PCG

Proof.

Apply Theorem

that

Thus, we show that, for all sufficiently large

more expressive than

-interval-PCG

Lemma 17

(Single-shell bound)

There exists an absolute constant

such that the following holds.

Let

Theorem 18
.

There exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that the following holds.

Let
A
be a finite set with
|A|=m
, and let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq 2^{A}
. If

(k,t)
-threshold-PCG

|\mathcal{S}|\leq(C\,m^{3})^{k}.

Proof.

Assume that

(k,t)
-threshold-PCG

on the common vertex set

Fix

. Since

is a

, there exist a weighted tree

\widetilde{T}_{i}
, an interval

such that

for all distinct leaves corresponding to vertices of

Let

be the minimal subtree of

spanning the leaves corresponding

is a reduced weighted tree with

.

For each

Then for every

Define

Now fix

Then

. In each of those two components, choose a shortest cycle of

\overline{P_{i}}
. Each such cycle is chordless. Since the two cycles lie in different
components of
F_{k}
, they are vertex-disjoint and there are no edges between them.

By the necessary condition for
PCG
s proved by Hossain et al., if the complement
of a graph contains two vertex-disjoint chordless cycles such that no vertices on one cycle is adjacent to the vertices on the other cycle, then that graph is not a

PCG

[
20
,
24
]
. Applying this to
P_{i}
, we obtain
that
P_{i}
is not a
PCG
, a contradiction. Hence
G_{k}\notin k\textsc{-AND-{PCG}}
.
\triangleleft

Combining Steps 1 and 2, we obtain

k\textsc{-AND-{PCG}}\subsetneq(k+1)\textsc{-AND-{PCG}}.

∎

Theorem 26
.

For every integer
k\geq 1
, the class
k
-
AND
-
PCG
is not closed
under complement.

Proof.

Let

F_{k}:=\bigsqcup_{t=1}^{k+1}K^{(t)}_{2k,2k}\text{ and }G_{k}:=\overline{F_{k}}.

Each graph
K_{2k,2k}
is a
PCG

[
29
]
, and finite disjoint unions of

PCG
s are again
PCG
s
[
28
]
. Hence

F_{k}\in\textsc{PCG}\subseteq k\textsc{-AND-{PCG}}.

On the other hand, by Theorem
25
,

G_{k}=\overline{F_{k}}\notin k\textsc{-AND-{PCG}}.

Therefore,
k
-
AND
-
PCG
is not closed under complement.
∎

8
Conclusions and Open Problems

We introduced
(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
s as a common framework for several
existing generalizations of
PCGs
. This viewpoint simultaneously recovers
ordinary
PCGs
,
k
-OR-PCG
s, and

k
-AND-PCG
s, while remaining close to the original
tree-distance model.

Our results exhibit a clear dichotomy. When the number of constituent predicates grows
with the graph size, the model becomes universal: every graph on
n
vertices is a

(n,t)
-threshold-PCG
for every
t\in[n]
. In contrast, for
fixed
k
, even after allowing all thresholds
t\in[k]
, the resulting classes
have asymptotic density zero. We also showed that threshold combinations can be much
more expressive than multi-interval representations: if
f(k)\in o(k^{2}/\log k)
, then
for all sufficiently large
k
there exists a graph that is a

(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
but not an

f(k)
-interval-PCG
. In particular, for all sufficiently large
k
,

k\textsc{-interval-{PCG}}\subsetneq k\textsc{-OR-{PCG}}.

Beyond counting arguments, we developed explicit obstruction families via set-system
incidence graphs. These yield concrete lower bounds for threshold representations and, in
particular, explicit infinite families outside low-level threshold classes. The same
approach also gives strong lower bounds for the family
H_{m}
in the multi-interval
setting. Finally, for conjunctions, we proved that

k\textsc{-AND-{PCG}}\subsetneq(k+1)\textsc{-AND-{PCG}}

for every
k\geq 1
, and that
k
-
AND
-
PCG
is not closed under
complement.

The main remaining questions are quantitative. Our results leave a substantial gap
between lower and upper bounds for explicit obstruction families, and they also leave
open the small-parameter behavior of several threshold and multi-interval classes.

Open problems.

1.

Determine the correct order of growth of the minimum threshold width of

H_{y}=I(4y-3,\,2y-1).

Our results give a lower bound of
\Omega(y/\log y)
, while the general universality theorem gives only much weaker upper bounds.

2.

Determine the correct order of growth of the minimum interval number needed to
represent
H_{y}
. We proved the lower bound

\Omega\!\left(\frac{4^{y}}{y^{7/4}}\right),

but the exact asymptotics remain unknown.

3.

Sharpen the incidence-graph bounds. For general
I(p,q)
, determine the true
minimum number of constituent
PCG
s required in a

(k,t)
-threshold-PCG
representation, and compare it to the true
minimum interval number in the multi-interval model.

4.

Determine whether the separation

k\textsc{-interval-{PCG}}\subsetneq k\textsc{-OR-{PCG}}

already holds for every fixed
k\geq 2
, or only asymptotically for sufficiently
large
k
.

5.

Understand the relationship between the PCG parameters such as minimum threshold width or minimum internal number and the standard graph parameters, such as treewidth, pathwidth, rankwidth, twinwidth, branchwidth, etc.

Appendix A
Non-closure of Multi-Interval PCGs under Complement

It has been posed as an open problem in
[
8
,
14
]
to determine whether
k
-interval-PCG
is closed under complement. We resolved this question in the negative, utilizing the hierarchy of generalized leaf powers introduced by Dupré la Tour,
Lafond, and Ndiaye
[
22
]
.

Definition 27

(Leaf power
[
9
]
)
.

A graph is a
leaf power
if it is a
PCG
representable as

\operatorname{PCG}(T,[0,d_{\max}])

for some edge-weighted tree
T
and some
d_{\max}\geq 0
.

Definition 28

(Generalized leaf powers
[
22
]
)
.

Let
q\geq 1
be an integer. A graph
G=(V,E)
is a
\mathrm{GLP}(q)
if there exist
a positively weighted tree
T
with leaf set
V
, and real thresholds

\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<\cdots<\theta_{q},

such that, for every two distinct vertices
u,v\in V
,

uv\in E\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_{T}(u,v)\ \text{is at most an odd number of the thresholds }\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{q}.

To write our proof succintly, we define a new graph operation. For a graph
G
, let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
denote two disjoint copies of
G
, and we write

G^{\prime}:=\overline{G_{1}\cup G_{2}}.

We now state several well-known facts to facilitate our proof.

Proposition 29
.

[
22
]

For every integer
i\geq 1
,

\mathrm{GLP}(1)=\textsc{Leaf Power}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{GLP}(2i)=i\text{-interval-}\textsc{PCG}.

Proposition 30
.

[
22
]

For every integer
q\geq 1
, if
G\in\mathrm{GLP}(q)
, then

G^{\prime}=\overline{G_{1}\cup G_{2}}\in\mathrm{GLP}(q+1).

Proposition 31
.

[
22
]

For every integer
q\geq 1
, if
G\notin\mathrm{GLP}(q)
, then

G^{\prime}=\overline{G_{1}\cup G_{2}}\notin\mathrm{GLP}(q+1).

Proposition 32
.

[
22
]

C_{4}\notin\mathrm{GLP}(1).

We can now prove our theorem.

Theorem 33
.

For every integer
k\geq 1
, the class of
k
-interval-
PCG
s is not closed
under complement.

Proof.

Fix
k\geq 1
. For
r\geq 1
, define graphs
F_{r}
recursively by

F_{1}:=C_{4},\qquad F_{r+1}:=\overline{F_{r}^{(1)}\cup F_{r}^{(2)}},

where
F_{r}^{(1)}
and
F_{r}^{(2)}
are two disjoint copies of
F_{r}
. By Fact
32
, we have
F_{1}\notin\mathrm{GLP}(1)
. Hence,
by repeated application of Fact
31
,

F_{r}\notin\mathrm{GLP}(r)\qquad\text{for every }r\geq 1.

In particular,

F_{2k}\notin\mathrm{GLP}(2k).

Using Fact
29
, this becomes

F_{2k}\notin k\text{-interval-}\textsc{PCG}.

Now suppose, for contradiction, that the class of
k
-interval-
PCG
s is
closed under complement.

Since
C_{4}
is a
PCG

[
6
]
, it is also a

k
-interval-PCG
. Thus
F_{1}
belongs to the class.
We claim that then
F_{r}
is a
k
-interval-PCG
for every
r\geq 1
.

Indeed, assume that
F_{r}
is a
k
-interval-PCG
.
Then
F_{r}^{(1)}
and
F_{r}^{(2)}
are also
k
-interval-
PCG
s.
Moreover, their disjoint union is again a
k
-interval-PCG
: take two
disjoint copies of a witness tree for
F_{r}
and connect them by an edge of
sufficiently large weight, so that every leaf-to-leaf distance between the two copies
lies outside the union of the
k
admissible intervals, while all distances inside
each copy remain unchanged. Therefore,

F_{r}^{(1)}\cup F_{r}^{(2)}

is a
k
-interval-PCG
. By the assumed closure under complement,

F_{r+1}=\overline{F_{r}^{(1)}\cup F_{r}^{(2)}}

is also a
k
-interval-PCG
. This proves the claim by induction.

Applying the claim with
r=2k
, we obtain that
F_{2k}
is a

k
-interval-PCG
, resulting in a contradiction.

Therefore, the class of
k
-interval-
PCG
s is not closed under complement.
∎

Appendix B
Shell Bound Lemma

In this section, we introduce some useful notions that are useful for proving several results on the incidence graph classes.

Lemma 34

(Single-shell bound)
.

There exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that the following holds.

Let
T
be a reduced weighted tree with leaf set
A
, where
|A|=m
, and let

I=[\alpha,\beta]
be an interval of real numbers. Define

\mathcal{F}(T,I):=\Bigl\{\{a\in A:d_{T}(x,a)+\lambda\in I\}:x\in T,\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R}\Bigr\},

where
x\in T
means that
x
is an arbitrary point of the geometric realization of
the tree
T
. Then

|\mathcal{F}(T,I)|\leq C\,m^{3}.

Proof.

Fix an interval
I=[\alpha,\beta]
. For a point
x
on the tree and a real number
\lambda
, define

X(x,\lambda):=\{a\in A:d_{T}(x,a)+\lambda\in I\}.

We want to bound how many different subsets
X(x,\lambda)
can appear. We first keep
x
on one fixed edge
e
of the tree, and count how many subsets can be realized there.

Let
e
be an edge of length
\ell_{e}
, and parametrize its points by

x\in[0,\ell_{e}]
. For each leaf
a\in A
, the restriction of
d_{T}(x,a)
to
e

is affine with slope in
\{+1,-1\}
: as
x
moves along
e
, the distance to
a

either increases at unit rate or decreases at unit rate, depending on which side of
e

the leaf
a
lies. Hence

d_{T}(x,a)=\sigma_{a}x+c_{a}

for suitable constants
\sigma_{a}\in\{+1,-1\}
and
c_{a}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Now fix a leaf
a
. The membership of
a
in
X(x,\lambda)
is determined by whether

\alpha\leq d_{T}(x,a)+\lambda\leq\beta.

As
(x,\lambda)
varies, this truth value can change only when

d_{T}(x,a)+\lambda
hits one of the two endpoints
\alpha
or
\beta
. Thus, the only
possible change points are on the two lines

\lambda=\alpha-(\sigma_{a}x+c_{a})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lambda=\beta-(\sigma_{a}x+c_{a}).

So inside the strip

0\leq x\leq\ell_{e},

the behavior of all leaves is controlled by an arrangement of at most
2m
such lines,
together with the two boundary lines
x=0
and
x=\ell_{e}
.

This arrangement cuts the strip into
O(m^{2})
regions. Inside any one region, no boundary
line is crossed, so for every leaf
a
, the statement

d_{T}(x,a)+\lambda\in I

has the same truth value throughout the whole region.

Therefore, the subset
X(x,\lambda)

is constant on each region. Hence, while
x
stays on the fixed edge
e
, at most
O(m^{2})
different subsets can
be realized.

Finally, since
T
is reduced and has
m
leaves, it has at most
2m-3
edges.
Summing the
O(m^{2})
possibilities over all edges gives

|\mathcal{F}(T,I)|=O(m^{3})
, and the claim follows.
∎

Proposition 35
.

There exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that the following holds.

Let
A
be a finite set with
|A|=m
, and let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq 2^{A}
. If

G(A,\mathcal{S})
is an
\ell
-interval-PCG
, then

|\mathcal{S}|\leq C\,m^{3}\ell^{2}.

Proof.

The proof is the same shell-counting argument as in
Lemma
34
, but with a union of
\ell
disjoint intervals
instead of a single interval. On a fixed edge of the reduced tree, each leaf contributes

2\ell
endpoint lines in the
(x,\lambda)
-plane, so the arrangement has

O(m\ell)
lines and therefore
O(m^{2}\ell^{2})
faces. Summing over the
O(m)

edges of the reduced tree yields
O(m^{3}\ell^{2})
possible neighborhoods on the

B
-side.
∎
