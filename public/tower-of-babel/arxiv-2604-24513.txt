Adjusted connections on non-abelian bundle gerbes
Konrad Waldorf

Abstract
Higher gauge theory for non-abelian structure 2-groups faces significant challenges when extending beyond the fake-flat sector, which suffers from limited applicability in physical models. A promising resolution involves equipping 2-groups with additional structure, known as adjustments. We present a comprehensive theory of adjusted connections on non-abelian bundle gerbes, classified by Saemann’s adjusted version of non-abelian differential cohomology. This theory enables, in particular, a new coordinate-independent formulation of Tellez-Dominguez’ lifting theorem, establishing a correspondence between adjusted connections on non-abelian bundle gerbes and connections on abelian bundle 2-gerbes.

Introduction

Bundle gerbes serve as the most common differential-geometric realization of bundles for categorical Lie groups, also known as Lie 2-groups. Their primary advantages are twofold: (1) the structural simplicity, relying solely on basic differential geometry, and (2) the accessibility of connections through ordinary connections and differential forms. Among bundle gerbes, those associated with the Lie 2-group
B{\mathrm{U}}(1)
(abelian bundle gerbes) are the most prevalent. These were first developed by Murray, Carey, and collaborators
[
murray
,
carey5
,
murray2
,
carey2
]
and were instantly adopted by Gawȩdzki for applications in conformal field theory
[
gawedzki5
,
gawedzki1
,
gawedzki2
]
, where connections on abelian bundle gerbes model the B-field in string theory.

Generalizing this framework to Lie 2-groups beyond
BA
(where
A
is an abelian Lie group) proved challenging. Aschieri, Cantini, and Jurčo pioneered a generalization to a non-abelian Lie group
H
in
[
aschieri
]
. While they also investigated connections, they immediately encountered difficulties in defining them consistently beyond the “fake-flat” regime.

A key conceptual hurdle was to recognize that the adequate generalization of abelian Lie groups is not merely non-abelian Lie groups (which led to the term “non-abelian bundle gerbes”), but rather Lie 2-groups. Non-abelian Lie groups
H
embed into this setting through their automorphism 2-group
\mathscr{A}\mathrm{ut}(H)
. In this article, we model general Lie 2-groups through their Lie crossed modules
\Gamma=(H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t}}{{\to}}G)
.

The above-mentioned fake-flatness is a condition for categorical connections, discovered by Breen and Messing
[
breen1
]
. Locally, such a
\Gamma\!
-connection consists of a pair
(A,B)
comprising a 1-form
A\in\Omega^{1}(U,\mathfrak{g})
and a 2-form
B\in\Omega^{2}(U,\mathfrak{h})
. Their fake-curvature is defined as

\mathrm{fcurv}(A,B):=\mathrm{d}A+\tfrac{1}{2}[A\wedge A]-t_{*}(B)\in\Omega^{2}(U,\mathfrak{g})\text{.}

Here,
t_{*}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
denotes the Lie algebra crossed module associated with
\Gamma
. Fake-flatness means the vanishing of the fake curvature. Imposing this condition simplifies the theory of connections on non-abelian gerbes. For instance, in our joint work with Schreiber
[
schreiber2
,
schreiber6
,
schreiber5
]
, we demonstrated that fake-flat connections admit a well-defined parallel transport along surfaces, and furthermore, that fake-flatness is necessary for this feature. However, in many physical applications, fake-flatness is an undesirable constraint; for example, in string theory, it would restrict the application of bundle gerbes for the string 2-group
\Gamma={\mathrm{String}}(d)
to sigma models on
flat
target spaces.

Naively abandoning fake-flatness, surprisingly, jeopardizes the existence of 2-morphisms in the bicategory of
\Gamma\!
-connections (see
). These 2-morphisms are essential for the gluing conditions on triple intersections.
Fortunately, Saemann et al. resolved this issue by modifying a single detail in the definition of the 1-morphisms (the gauge transformations)
[
Kim2020
,
Rist2022
]
. Specifically, a gauge transformation is a pair
(g,\varphi)
consisting of a function
g:U\to G
and a 1-form
\varphi\in\Omega^{1}(U,\mathfrak{h})
. One can then define a curvature notion,
\mathrm{curv}(g,\varphi)\in\Omega^{2}(U,\mathfrak{h})
. In the fake-flat case, one requires
\mathrm{curv}(g,\varphi)=0
; however, insisting on this condition for non fake-flat
\Gamma\!
-connections leads to the before-mentioned constraints on 2-morphisms.
The crucial insight was to relate the fake-curvature to the curvature of these gauge transformations. This coupling necessitates a new additional structure on Lie 2-groups, termed an
adjustment
. An adjustment is a map

\kappa:G\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}

(1.1)

that is linear in
\mathfrak{g}
and satisfies several further conditions. The corrected condition on gauge transformations reads

\mathrm{curv}(g,\varphi)=\kappa(g,\mathrm{fcurv}(A,B))\text{.}

(1.2)

Saemann et al. utilized this local formalism to establish an adjusted version of non-abelian differential cohomology, denoted
\hat{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(M,\Gamma)^{a\hskip-0.65556ptd\hskip-0.49164ptj}
. It represents the most general possible version of non-abelian higher gauge theory.

In the case of fake-flatness,

1.2
reduces to
\mathrm{curv}(g,\varphi)=0

Moreover, it follows immediately from the setup in

A comprehensive discussion of the existence and classification of adjustments on Lie 2-groups was recently presented in our joint work with Ludewig

Lie 2-groups and connections

In this section, we revisit several foundational concepts of higher gauge theory required for this article. This comprises mostly familiar material, bundled and summarized, with minor extensions and reformulations.

2.1
Lie 2-groups and Lie 2-algebras

For the purposes of this article, a
Lie 2-group
is a crossed module
\Gamma=(H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t}}{{\to}}G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\to}}\mathrm{Aut}(H))
of (possibly Fréchet) Lie groups, where
G
and
H
are Lie groups,
t
is a Lie group homomorphism, and
\alpha:G\times H\to H
is a smooth action of
G
on
H
by Lie group homomorphisms, such that

t(\alpha(g,h))=gt(h)g^{-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\alpha(t(h),h^{\prime})=hh^{\prime}h^{-1}

hold for all
g\in G
and
h,h^{\prime}\in H
.
We denote by
\alpha_{g}\in\mathrm{Aut}(H)
the action of a fixed
g\in G
on
H
. For
h\in H
, we consider the map
\tilde{\alpha}_{h}:G\to H
defined by
\tilde{\alpha}_{h}(g):=h^{-1}\alpha(g,h)
, which is
not
a Lie group homomorphism but satisfies
\tilde{\alpha}_{h}(1)=1
.
We refer to
[
Waldorf2016
, §A]
for a useful collection of formulas for calculations in a crossed module.

For an abelian Lie group
A
, we denote by
BA
the Lie 2-group
(A\to\ast\to\mathrm{Aut}(A))
, and for a Lie group
F
, we denote by
F_{d\hskip-0.40974pti\hskip-0.65556pts}
the Lie 2-group
(\ast\to F\to\ast)
. For another Lie group
H
, we denote by
\mathscr{A}\mathrm{ut}(H)
the Lie 2-group
(H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\to}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{id}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{Aut}(H))
, where

denotes the assignment of inner automorphisms.

Given a Lie 2-group
\Gamma
, we define the
homotopy groups

F:=\pi_{0}\Gamma:=G/t(H)
and
A:=\pi_{1}\Gamma:=\mathrm{ker}(t)\subseteq H
, together with the projection
p:G\to F
and the inclusion

. We note that
A
is abelian and central in
H
, and that the action
\alpha
induces an action
\alpha_{0}
of

such that
\alpha_{0}(p(g),a)=\alpha(g,i(a))
holds for all

and

. The Lie 2-group

is called

is trivial.
If
\Gamma
is central, then
\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(g)=1
for all

; from this, one can verify that the map
\tilde{\alpha}_{h}:G\to H
depends only on

.

Moreover, a Lie 2-group
\Gamma
is called

and

is an exact sequence of Lie groups. In this article, most Lie 2-groups are assumed to be smoothly separable and central; however, e.g.,

is in general neither, depending on

Again for the purposes of this article, a
Lie 2-group homomorphism

f:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
between Lie 2-groups consists of Lie group homomorphisms

and

If
\Gamma
is a smoothly separable Lie 2-group, it comes equipped with two Lie 2-group homomorphisms

, and

[
pries2
]
.
This 2-group extension is central in the sense of
[
pries2
]
if and only if
\Gamma
is central in the sense defined above.

The Lie 2-algebra
\mathfrak{G}
of a Lie 2-group

is the associated crossed module
\mathfrak{G}=(\mathfrak{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t_{*}}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha_{*}}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{der}(\mathfrak{h}))

[

.

We define

, obtaining an exact sequence of Lie algebras

2.2
Adjustments

The theory of adjustments was introduced by Sati, Schreiber, and Stasheff
[
sati1
]

]

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a central Lie 2-group with Lie 2-algebra
\mathfrak{G}
.
An
adjustment
for
\Gamma
is a map

\kappa:G\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}

that is linear and continuous in
\mathfrak{g}
, smooth in
G
, and satisfies the following conditions:

\displaystyle\kappa(g_{1}g_{2},X)

\displaystyle=\kappa(g_{1},\mathrm{Ad}_{g_{2}}(X))+\kappa(g_{2},X)

(2.2.1)

\displaystyle\kappa(t(h),X)

\displaystyle=(\tilde{\alpha}_{h^{-1}})_{*}X

(2.2.2)

\displaystyle\kappa(g,t_{*}Y)

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{g})_{*}(Y)-Y

(2.2.3)

for all
g,g_{1},g_{2}\in G
,
h\in H
,
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, and
Y\in\mathfrak{h}
.
We say that
\kappa
is
adapted
to a splitting
u
of
\mathfrak{G}
if it additionally satisfies

t_{*}\kappa(g,X)=t_{*}u(\mathrm{Ad}_{g}(X)-X)

(2.2.4)

for all
g\in G
and
X\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Two straightforward consequences of the axioms are:

\displaystyle\kappa(g^{-1},X)

\displaystyle=-\kappa(g,\mathrm{Ad}_{g}^{-1}(X))

\displaystyle\kappa(1,X)

\displaystyle=0\text{.}

We note that the adjustments in
[

,
Tellez2023
]
are related to ours by inversion in the first argument, and that
[
Rist2022
]

We consider the groupoid

If

Let
X
be a smooth manifold, let
\Gamma=(H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t}}{{\to}}G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\to}}\mathrm{Aut}(H))
be a Lie 2-group, and let
\mathfrak{G}=(\mathfrak{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t_{*}}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha_{*}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h}))
be the corresponding Lie 2-algebra.

Definition 2.3.1
.

A
\Gamma\!
-connection on
X

is a pair
(A,B)
consisting of a 1-form
A\in\Omega^{1}(X,\mathfrak{g})

and a 2-form
B\in\Omega^{2}(X,\mathfrak{h})
. The 2-form

\mathrm{fcurv}(A,B):=\mathrm{d}A+\tfrac{1}{2}[A\wedge A]-t_{*}(B)\in\Omega^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g})

is called the
fake-curvature
, and the 3-form

\mathrm{curv}(A,B):=\mathrm{d}B+\alpha_{*}(A\wedge B)\in\Omega^{3}(X,\mathfrak{h})

is called the
curvature
. A connection
(A,B)
is called
fake-flat
, if
\mathrm{fcurv}(A,B)=0
, and it is called
flat
, if it is fake-flat and
\mathrm{curv}(A,B)=0
.

Remark 2.3.2
.

Curvature and fake curvature satisfy the identities

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{fcurv}(A,B))

\displaystyle=[\mathrm{d}A\wedge A]-t_{*}(\mathrm{d}B)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{curv}(A,B))

\displaystyle=\alpha_{*}(\mathrm{fcurv}(A,B)\wedge B)-\alpha_{*}(A\wedge\mathrm{curv}(A,B))\text{.}

In case of fake-flatness, we have

t_{*}(\mathrm{curv}(A,B))=0
, so that
\mathrm{curv}(A,B)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-valued.
If
\Gamma
is additionally central, we have
\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{curv}(A,B))=0
.
It is useful to define

F_{A}:=p_{*}(\mathrm{d}A+\tfrac{1}{2}[A\wedge A])=p_{*}(\mathrm{fcurv}(A,B))\in\Omega^{2}(M,\mathfrak{f})\text{;}

then, the first identity implies
\mathrm{d}F_{A}=[F_{A}\wedge p_{*}A]
, a Bianchi identity for
p_{*}A
.

Remark 2.3.3
.

For
\kappa
an adjustment on
\Gamma
, the 3-form

\mathrm{curv}_{\kappa}(A,B):=\mathrm{curv}(A,B)+\kappa_{*}(A\wedge\mathrm{fcurv}(A,B))

is called the

.
One can easily verify that

\mathrm{curv}_{\kappa}(A,B)=\mathrm{d}B+\kappa_{*}(A\wedge\mathrm{d}A)+\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{*}(A\wedge[A\wedge A])\text{,}

revealing a term resembling a Chern-Simons form. This will be relevant later; see, e.g.
and

combines with the factor

We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{curv}_{\kappa}(A,B))

\displaystyle=b_{\kappa}(F_{A}\wedge F_{A})\text{.}

In the case of fake-flatness, curvature and adjusted curvature agree.

Remark 2.3.4

Suppose

-connection
