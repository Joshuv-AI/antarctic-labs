Flat Lorentzian Lie groups: A complete description
Mohamed Boucetta

Abstract
In this paper, we establish a complete structural description of flat Lorentzian Lie groups, i.e., Lie groups endowed with a flat left invariant Lorentzian metric, thereby resolving a long-standing open problem in the theory of pseudo-Riemannian Lie groups. Our main result shows that any flat Lorentzian Lie group either admits a timelike parallel left-invariant vector field or is of Kundt type, and that in both cases the underlying Lie algebra falls into one of six explicit classes. A key ingredient of the proof is a refined analysis of the double extension process, which reveals that all flat Lorentzian Lie algebras arise — directly or in a generalized sense — from flat Euclidean ones. As a consequence, we obtain easily a complete classification in dimensions three and four, recovering and unifying several previously known partial results.

Introduction

A Lie group
G
endowed with a left-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric is called a
pseudo-Riemannian Lie group
. When the metric is positive definite (respectively, of signature
(-,+,\ldots,+)
), we shall refer to it as
Riemannian
(respectively,
Lorentzian
). The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of such a group, equipped with the induced inner product, is called a
pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra
; accordingly, we use the terms
Euclidean
and
Lorentzian
in the corresponding cases.

It is well known that any left-invariant Riemannian metric on a Lie group is geodesically complete. In contrast, in the Lorentzian setting when the metric is flat, geodesic completeness holds if and only if the group is unimodular
[
16
]
.

Let
(G,h)
be a pseudo-Riemannian Lie group, and denote by
\nabla
its Levi-Civita connection. Identifying
\mathfrak{g}
with the space of left-invariant vector fields on
G
, the connection induces a bilinear product
\cdot
on
\mathfrak{g}
defined by

X\cdot Y=\nabla_{X}Y,\qquad X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}.

This is characterized by the two relations:

[X,Y]=X\cdot Y-Y\cdot X\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle X\cdot Y,Z\rangle+\langle X\cdot Z,Y\rangle=0.

It is a classical result that
(G,h)
is flat if and only if
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
is a left-symmetric algebra, that is,

\mathrm{ass}(X,Y,Z)=\mathrm{ass}(Y,X,Z),\qquad\forall X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g},

where

\mathrm{ass}(X,Y,Z)=(X\cdot Y)\cdot Z-X\cdot(Y\cdot Z)

denotes the associator. We call
\mathfrak{g}
a flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra.

The classification of flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie groups is a fundamental and difficult problem in the theory of homogeneous spaces. It sits at the crossroads of Lie theory, differential geometry, and the study of left-symmetric algebras, and has attracted sustained attention over several decades. In the Riemannian case, a complete characterization was obtained by Milnor
[
14
]
, who proved that a Lie group admits a flat left-invariant Riemannian metric if and only if its Lie algebra decomposes orthogonally as
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{b}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
, where
\mathfrak{b}
is an abelian subalgebra,
\mathfrak{u}
is an abelian ideal, and
\mathrm{ad}_{b}
is skew-symmetric for every
b\in\mathfrak{b}
. A refined and more precise version of this result was later obtained in
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
, which will play a central role in the present paper.

In contrast, the Lorentzian case is far from being completely understood, despite numerous partial results. It is known that any flat Lorentzian Lie group is necessarily solvable
[
10
]
. In
[
2
]
, Aubert and Medina introduced the so-called
double extension process
, which provides a systematic way to construct flat Lorentzian Lie algebras from flat Euclidean ones. This construction has led to several important subclasses, including flat nilpotent Lorentzian Lie algebras
[
2
]
, those with degenerate center
[
1
]
and non-unimodular (hence incomplete)
[
6
]
.

On the other hand, it can be shown that the Levi-Civita product of a flat Euclidean Lie algebra is of Novikov type, a condition that places strong algebraic constraints on the algebra (see the definition below). The class of flat Lorentzian Lie algebras whose Levi-Civita product is of Novikov type has been described in
[
5
,
7
,
13
]
. More recently, a classification of flat nilpotent Lorentzian Lie groups has been obtained in
[
3
]
and an algebraic group-theoretical version of this classification was already present in
[
11
, Section 3]
.
In addition, flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie groups of signature
(2,n-2)
have been investigated in
[
12
,
8
]
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\bullet)
be a left-symmetric algebra. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is a
Novikov algebra
if it satisfies

(x\bullet y)\bullet z=(x\bullet z)\bullet y,\qquad\forall x,y,z\in\mathcal{A}.

(1)

Novikov algebras arise naturally in the study of Poisson brackets of hydrodynamical type
[
4
]
.

A flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie group is said to be of
Novikov type
if its Lie algebra, endowed with the Levi-Civita product, is a Novikov algebra. Such Lie algebras admit a useful characterization given in
[
7
,
13
,
5
]
(see Proposition
2.3
).

To summarize the state of the art, two main classes of flat Lorentzian Lie algebras have emerged from the existing literature: those obtained via the double extension process from flat Euclidean Lie algebras, and those whose Levi-Civita product is of Novikov type. This naturally raises the following fundamental question, which has remained open until now:

Do there exist flat Lorentzian Lie algebras that belong to neither of these two classes? And more generally, is it possible to give a complete structural description of all flat Lorentzian Lie algebras?

The main result of this paper provides a definitive and complete answer to this question. We prove that every flat Lorentzian Lie algebra belongs to one of six explicit families, which together cover both the double extension class and the Novikov class, and show that no other flat Lorentzian Lie algebras exist. The key geometric insight behind our approach is the following dichotomy, established in Proposition
2.5
: any flat Lorentzian Lie algebra either admits a timelike parallel left-invariant vector field, or is of Kundt type. This dichotomy, which is of independent geometric interest, organizes the entire classification and drives the structure of the paper.

We recall that Kundt spacetimes play a central role in general relativity and Lorentzian geometry. A Lorentzian manifold
(M,g)
is said to be of Kundt type
[
9
]
if there exist a nowhere-vanishing null vector field
V
and a
1
-form
\alpha
such that

g(V,V)=0,\quad\nabla_{X}V=\alpha(X)V,\quad\nabla_{V}V=0,

for every vector field
X
orthogonal to
V
. In the homogeneous setting studied here, the Kundt condition translates into a precise algebraic condition on the Levi-Civita product of the Lie algebra, which we exploit systematically throughout the paper.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(G,h)
be a flat Lorentzian Lie group. Then

(G,h)
admits either a timelike parallel left-invariant vector field or is of Kundt type
and the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=h(e)

-

They reduce to systems of linear equations that are straightforward to solve explicitly, making it easy to construct concrete examples in arbitrary dimension and to systematically explore the geometry of flat Lorentzian Lie groups far beyond the low-dimensional setting. As an illustration, we derive from our six general families a complete classification of flat Lorentzian Lie algebras in dimensions three and four, presented in Tables

The paper is organized as follows. Section

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we present several useful properties of the Lie algebra of skew-symmetric endomorphisms. We also recall some basic facts concerning non-unimodular flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie groups and Novikov algebras. Finally, we state a refined version of Milnor’s theorem, which will play a central role in the sequel, and we prove the first assertion of Theorem
1.1
.

2.1
The Lie algebra of skew-symmetric endomorphisms

We begin by recalling some standard facts on pseudo-Euclidean vector spaces. A
pseudo-Euclidean vector space
is a finite-dimensional real vector space
V
endowed with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
of signature
(q,n-q)
. The cases
q=0
and
q=1
correspond to
Euclidean
and
Lorentzian
structures, respectively.

Let
(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean vector space. We denote by
\mathfrak{so}(V)
the Lie algebra of skew-symmetric endomorphisms of
V
. It is well known that any totally isotropic subspace of
V
has dimension at most
\min(q,n-q)
. In particular, in the Lorentzian case, any nontrivial totally isotropic subspace is one-dimensional.

Assume now that
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is positive definite. Then
\mathfrak{so}(V)
is naturally endowed with the symmetric bilinear form

\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathfrak{so}}=-\mathrm{tr}(AB),\qquad A,B\in\mathfrak{so}(V),

which is positive definite. Moreover, this form is
quadratic
in the sense that, for all
A,B,C\in\mathfrak{so}(V)
,

\langle[A,B],C\rangle_{\mathfrak{so}}+\langle[A,C],B\rangle_{\mathfrak{so}}=0.

(2)

As a consequence, the nonzero eigenvalues of the operator

\mathrm{ad}_{A}:\mathfrak{so}(V)\to\mathfrak{so}(V),\quad B\mapsto[A,B],

are purely imaginary.

This property extends to the adjoint action of
A
on
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
. Indeed, if
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
are the eigenvalues of
A
, then the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{ad}_{A}
are given by
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}
,
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Since
A
is skew-symmetric, its eigenvalues are purely imaginary, and the conclusion follows.

We will also use the following property: if
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{so}(V)
is an abelian Lie subalgebra and
A\in\mathfrak{so}(V)
satisfies
\mathrm{ad}_{A}(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{A}
, then
\mathrm{ad}_{A}(\mathcal{A})=\{0\}
. Indeed, for all
B,C\in\mathcal{A}
, one has

\langle[A,B],C\rangle_{\mathfrak{so}}=\langle A,[B,C]\rangle_{\mathfrak{so}}=0,

which implies
[A,B]=0
for all
B\in\mathcal{A}
.

The following proposition is a classical consequence of Lie’s theorem.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a Euclidean vector space and
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{so}(V)
a solvable Lie subalgebra. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is abelian, and there exist an orthonormal basis

(e_{1},f_{1},\ldots,e_{r},f_{r},g_{1},\ldots,g_{s})

of
V
and linear forms
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that, for all
A\in\mathfrak{g}
,

A(e_{i})=\lambda_{i}(A)f_{i},\quad A(f_{i})=-\lambda_{i}(A)e_{i},\quad A(g_{j})=0,

for
i=1,\ldots,r
and
j=1,\ldots,s
.

2.2
Completeness of flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie groups and the modular vector

Let
(G,h)
be a pseudo-Riemannian Lie group and let
\mathfrak{g}
denote its Lie algebra, identified with the space of left-invariant vector fields. The metric
h
induces a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathfrak{g}
. The Levi-Civita connection defines a bilinear product on
\mathfrak{g}
, called the
Levi-Civita product
, given by

2\langle u\cdot v,w\rangle=\langle[u,v],w\rangle+\langle[w,u],v\rangle+\langle[w,v],u\rangle.

(3)

For
u\in\mathfrak{g}
, we denote by
\mathrm{L}_{u},\mathrm{R}_{u}\in\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g})
the left and right multiplication operators defined by

\mathrm{L}_{u}(v)=u\cdot v,\qquad\mathrm{R}_{u}(v)=v\cdot u.

Then
\mathrm{L}_{u}
is skew-symmetric with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, and the adjoint representation is given by

\mathrm{ad}_{u}=\mathrm{L}_{u}-\mathrm{R}_{u},\qquad\mathrm{ad}_{u}(v)=[u,v].

The curvature tensor at the identity is

\mathrm{K}(u,v)=\mathrm{L}_{[u,v]}-[\mathrm{L}_{u},\mathrm{L}_{v}].

The metric is flat if and only if
\mathrm{K}=0
. In this case,
(G,h)
is called a
flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie group
.

Since the study of flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie groups essentially reduces to that of their Lie algebras, we introduce the notion of a
flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra
. By this we mean a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
endowed with a pseudo-Euclidean metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
such that, for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\mathrm{L}_{[u,v]}-[\mathrm{L}_{u},\mathrm{L}_{v}]=0,

where
\mathrm{L}_{u}
denotes the left multiplication operator associated with the Levi-Civita product defined by (
3
).

In contrast to the Riemannian case, where left-invariant metrics are always geodesically complete, completeness in the pseudo-Riemannian setting is more subtle, even in the flat case. A flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie group is geodesically complete if and only if it is unimodular
[
16
]
. Equivalently, its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is unimodular, that is,

\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{ad}_{u})=0\quad\text{for all }u\in\mathfrak{g}.

This condition is equivalent to the vanishing of the
modular vector

\mathbf{h}\in\mathfrak{g}
, defined by

\langle u,\mathbf{h}\rangle=\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{ad}_{u})=-\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{R}_{u}),\qquad u\in\mathfrak{g}.

(4)

The modular vector of a flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra enjoys several important properties, summarized in the following proposition proved in
[
6
]
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a
flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra. Then:

1.

The modular vector satisfies
\mathbf{h}\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}
and

\mathrm{R}_{\mathbf{h}}
is symmetric with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
.
In
particular, if
\mathfrak{g}
is non-unimodular then
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is degenerate,
\langle\mathbf{h},\mathbf{h}\rangle=0
and
\mathbf{h}.\mathbf{h}=0
.

2.

If
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is Lorentzian then
H=\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{h}\}
is a two-sided ideal
(
with respect to the Levi-Civita product
)
in
H^{\perp}
.

2.3
Pseudo-Euclidean Novikov algebras as a subclass of flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebras

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra, and denote by
\cdot
its Levi-Civita product. The flatness condition is equivalent to

\mathrm{ass}(u,v,w)=\mathrm{ass}(v,u,w),\qquad u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g},

where
\mathrm{ass}(u,v,w)=(u\cdot v)\cdot w-u\cdot(v\cdot w)
is the associator. Thus,
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
is a left-symmetric algebra.

An important subclass of left-symmetric algebras is given by Novikov algebras. A left-symmetric algebra
(\mathcal{A},\cdot)
is called a
Novikov algebra
if

(x\cdot y)\cdot z=(x\cdot z)\cdot y,\qquad x,y,z\in\mathcal{A}.

(5)

Novikov algebras arise naturally in the study of Poisson brackets of hydrodynamical type
[
4
]
. Flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebras whose Levi-Civita product defines a Novikov structure were investigated in
[
7
,
13
]
. We recall the following characterization.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra and let
\cdot
denote its Levi-Civita product. Then
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
is a Novikov algebra if and only if, for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\mathrm{L}_{u\cdot v}=[\mathrm{L}_{u},\mathrm{L}_{v}]=0.

2.4
A refinement of Milnor’s theorem

We present here a refinement of Milnor’s theorem, proved in
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
, which will play a central role in the sequel.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a Euclidean Lie algebra and
\cdot
its Levi-Civita product. Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is flat if and only if the following conditions hold:

1.

[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{g}\cdot\mathfrak{g}
;

2.

the derived ideal
\mathfrak{d}:=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and its orthogonal complement
\mathfrak{a}:=\mathfrak{d}^{\perp}
are abelian, and
\dim\mathfrak{d}=2r
.

In this case,
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is unimodular and there exists a representation of the abelian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
,

\rho:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{d})

such that
\ker\rho
is the center of
\mathfrak{g}
,

\bigcap_{a\in\mathfrak{a}}\ker\rho(a)=\{0\},

and the Levi-Civita product is given by

u\cdot v=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }u\in\mathfrak{d}\text{ or }v\in\mathfrak{a},\\
\rho(u)(v)&\text{if }u\in\mathfrak{a},\ v\in\mathfrak{d}.\end{cases}

(6)

In particular, for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\mathrm{L}_{[u,v]}=\mathrm{L}_{u\cdot v}=[\mathrm{L}_{u},\mathrm{L}_{v}]=0,

and hence
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
is a Novikov algebra.

Remark 1
.

The condition
\bigcap_{a\in\mathfrak{a}}\ker\rho(a)=\{0\}
is equivalent to
\sum_{a\in\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{Im}\,\rho(a)=\mathfrak{d}
which is equivalent to
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{g}\cdot\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem
2.1
will play a pervasive role throughout the paper. Whenever a flat Euclidean Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{h}},\star,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}})
appears — either in Proposition
3.1
as the full Euclidean part
e^{\perp}
in the timelike case, or as the algebra
({\mathfrak{h}},\star)
arising in the Kundt case (Propositions
4.1
-
4.5
) — its orthogonal decomposition
{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{d}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
and the associated representation
\rho:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{d})
will be the primary tool for determining the remaining data
(E,F,A,u,v,w,\alpha,\lambda)
step by step. In this sense, Theorem
2.1
is the algebraic backbone of the entire classification.

As a consequence of Proposition
2.1
, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{d},\rho)
as in Theorem
2.1
, then there exist a basis
(e_{1},f_{1},\ldots,e_{r},f_{r})
of
\mathfrak{d}
and nonzero vectors
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\in\mathfrak{a}
such that, for all
a\in\mathfrak{a}
,

\rho(a)(e_{i})=\langle u_{i},a\rangle f_{i},\quad\rho(a)(f_{i})=-\langle u_{i},a\rangle e_{i},\quad i=1,\ldots,r.

Moreover, the center of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{d}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
is
Z(\mathfrak{g})=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\}^{\perp}
.

2.5
Three key lemmas

In this subsection, we prove three key lemmas that will play a crucial role in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
E
be a vector space,
(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a Euclidean vector space,
\Lambda:E\to\mathfrak{so}(V)
and
B:E\to V
be two linear maps such that, for all
a,b\in E
,

\bigcap_{a\in E}\ker\Lambda_{a}=\{0\},\quad[\Lambda_{a},\Lambda_{b}]=0\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda_{a}\circ B(b)=\Lambda_{b}\circ B(a).

Then there exists a vector
v\in V
such that, for all
a\in E
,
B(a)=\Lambda_{a}(v)
.

Proof.

Note that the conditions
\bigcap_{a\in E}\ker\Lambda_{a}=\{0\}
and
[\Lambda_{a},\Lambda_{b}]=0
imply that
\dim V=2n
. We prove the result by induction on
n
.

If
\dim V=2
, any nonzero skew-symmetric endomorphism is invertible. Hence, there exists
a\in E
such that
\Lambda_{a}
is invertible. For any
b\in E
, we obtain

B(b)=\Lambda_{b}\circ\Lambda_{a}^{-1}\bigl(B(a)\bigr),

which gives the desired result.

Assume now that the result holds for
n-1
, and let
\dim V=2n
. Choose
a\in E
such that
\Lambda_{a}\neq 0
. If
\Lambda_{a}
is invertible, the conclusion follows as above. Otherwise, set
V_{0}=\ker\Lambda_{a}
. Then
V=V_{0}\oplus V_{0}^{\perp}
.

For any
b\in E
, write

B(b)=B_{1}(b)+B_{2}(b),

where
B_{1}(b)\in V_{0}
and
B_{2}(b)\in V_{0}^{\perp}
. Since
[\Lambda_{a},\Lambda_{b}]=0
, the endomorphism
\Lambda_{b}
preserves both
V_{0}
and
V_{0}^{\perp}
. The relation
\Lambda_{a}\circ B(b)=\Lambda_{b}\circ B(a)
is then equivalent to

\Lambda_{b}\bigl(B_{1}(a)\bigr)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda_{a}\circ B_{2}(b)=\Lambda_{b}\circ B_{2}(a).

Since the restriction of
\Lambda_{a}
to
V_{0}^{\perp}
is invertible, there exists
v\in V_{0}^{\perp}
such that
B_{2}(b)=\Lambda_{b}(v)
for all
b\in E
. On the other hand,
B_{1}(a)\in\bigcap_{b\in E}\ker\Lambda_{b}
, hence
B_{1}(a)=0
.

Finally, consider the restrictions of
\Lambda_{b}
to
V_{0}
. By the induction hypothesis applied to
B_{1}
, there exists
v_{0}\in V_{0}
such that
B_{1}(b)=\Lambda_{b}(v_{0})
for all
b\in E
. Therefore,

B(b)=\Lambda_{b}(v_{0}+v),

which concludes the proof.
∎

The process of double extension of a flat pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{h}},[\cdot,\cdot],\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, described in
[
2
]
, involves two endomorphisms
D,A\in\mathrm{End}({\mathfrak{h}})
and an element
w\in{\mathfrak{h}}
satisfying

Put

Having in mind the relation

(7)

2.6

The following proposition is a key ingredient and give a proof of the first assertion of Theorem

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

It is known from
[
10

that
G

is a representation of the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
decomposes orthogonally as

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{r},

where each
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module. One of these components, say

, is Lorentzian.

Let

denote the restriction of

to
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. Since

is solvable, Lie’s theorem implies the existence of a nontrivial

of dimension

or

. We distinguish the following cases:

1.

. Since

is irreducible Lorentzian then either

and

2.

\dim I=2

is nondegenerate. Then

of

4

By Proposition

5
Conclusion

In this work, we have established a complete structural description of flat Lorentzian Lie groups, thereby resolving a long-standing open problem in the theory of pseudo-Riemannian Lie groups. We have shown that any such group necessarily admits either a left-invariant parallel timelike vector field or is of Kundt type, and that, in both cases, its Lie algebra belongs to one of six explicitly determined models. A notable and practically useful feature of these six models is that all conditions imposed on their parameters — commutativity relations between skew-symmetric endomorphisms, invariance conditions on representations, compatibility relations between linear maps — are of a purely linear-algebraic nature, reducing to systems that are straightforward to solve. This makes it easy to construct explicit examples in arbitrary dimension and to systematically explore the geometry of flat Lorentzian Lie groups well beyond the low-dimensional setting. The present result unifies and extends several previously known partial classifications, while revealing a clear dichotomy between Novikov-type structures and those arising from generalized double extensions of flat Euclidean Lie algebras. We expect these results to serve as a foundation for further investigations, particularly the study of flat pseudo-Riemannian metrics of higher index.

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}e,

\displaystyle(e.e).f

\displaystyle=\alpha(-v-\alpha f),

\displaystyle(e.e).a

\displaystyle=\alpha(Ea+\langle a,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e).

So

.

2.

u=e
,
v=f

\displaystyle(e.f).e

\displaystyle=-v.e=-\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e,

\displaystyle(e.f).f

\displaystyle=-v.f=\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}f+Av,

\displaystyle(e.f).a

\displaystyle=-v.a=-v\star a-\langle Av,a\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e.

So

,

and

.

3.

u=f
,

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle(f.e).f

\displaystyle(f.e).a

and

.

u=e
,

\displaystyle(e.a).e

\displaystyle=(Ea+\langle a,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e).e=\langle Ea,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e,

\displaystyle(e.a).f

\displaystyle=(Ea+\langle a,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e).f=-\langle Ea,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}f-AEa-\langle a,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}v,

\displaystyle(e.a).b

\displaystyle=(Ea+\langle a,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e).b=Ea\star b+\langle AEa,b\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e+\langle a,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}(Eb+\langle v,b\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e).

So
Eu=0
,
AE=-\langle\bullet,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}v
and
{\mathrm{L}}_{Ea}=-\langle a,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E
.

5.

u=a
,
v=e

\displaystyle(a.e).e

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle(a.e).f

\displaystyle=-\langle u,a\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}v

\displaystyle(a.e).b

\displaystyle=\langle u,a\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}(Eb+\langle b,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e).

So
|u|v=0
and
|u|E=0
.

6.

u=f
,
v=f

\displaystyle(f.f).e

\displaystyle=(-\lambda f-w).e=-\lambda^{2}e-\langle w,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e

\displaystyle(f.f).f

\displaystyle=(-\lambda f-w).f=-\lambda(-\lambda f-w)+\langle w,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}f+Aw

\displaystyle(f.f).a

\displaystyle=(-\lambda f-w).a=-\lambda(Fa+\langle a,w\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e)-w\star a-\langle Aw,a\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e.

So
\langle u,w\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}=-\lambda^{2}
,
Aw=-\lambda w
,
{\mathrm{L}}_{w}=-\lambda F
.

7.

u=f
,
v=a\in{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle(f.a).e

\displaystyle=(Fa+\langle a,w\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e).e=\langle Fa,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e,

\displaystyle(f.a).f

\displaystyle=(Fa+\langle a,w\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}e).f=-\langle Fa,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}f-AFa-\langle a,w\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}v

6.3
Verification of the Novikov property in Proposition
4.1

We have seen in the proof of Proposition
4.1
that
\alpha=0
,
E=0
,
v=0
,
{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{d}\oplus{\mathcal{A}}\oplus\hbox{\bb R}u
and

\begin{cases}A_{|\mathfrak{d}}=0,\;Aa={\mathrm{R}}_{\mathfrak{h}}(a)=\rho(a)h,\;a\in{\mathcal{A}},\;Au=m+n-\lambda u,\\
F_{|\mathfrak{d}}=-\frac{1}{|u|^{2}}(\rho(n)-\lambda\rho(u)),\;F_{|{\mathcal{A}}\oplus\hbox{\bb R}u}=0,\\
m=\rho(u)h+|u|^{2}h,\;w=w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3},\\
w_{1}=\lambda h-\frac{1}{|u|^{2}}\rho(n)(h)+\frac{\lambda}{|u|^{2}}\rho(u)h,\;w_{2}=\frac{\lambda n}{|u|^{2}},\;w_{3}=-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{|u|^{2}}u,\end{cases}

where
\rho:\mathfrak{a}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{d})
is a representation of an abelian Lie algebra satisfying
\bigcap_{a\in\mathfrak{a}}\ker\rho(a)=\{0\}
and
\star
is given by (
6
). Let us show that in this case the extension
\mathfrak{g}=\hbox{\bb R}e\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\hbox{\bb R}f
endowed with the Levi-Civita product given by (
10
) is a Novikov algebra, i.e, the data satisfy (
16
).

Note first that (
16
) reduces to

\begin{cases}a\star(b\star c)-b\star(a\star c)=0,\;{\mathrm{L}}_{a\star b}=0,\\
b\star Aa-a\star Ab=\langle a,u\rangle Ab-\langle b,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}Aa,\\
A(a\star b)=0,\;A^{2}a=-\langle a,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}w,\\
[F,{\mathrm{L}}_{a}]=0,\;FA={\mathrm{R}}_{w}-\lambda A+\langle\bullet,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}w,\;AF=0,\\
{\mathrm{L}}_{Fa}=0,\;{\mathrm{L}}_{Aa}=-\langle a,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}F,\\
Fu=0,\\
\;A^{*}u=-\lambda u,\;{\mathrm{R}}_{u}=0,\\
Aw=-\lambda w,\langle u,w\rangle=-\lambda^{2},\;{\mathrm{L}}_{w}=-\lambda F.\end{cases}

(24)

Verification of the identities.

(1)

a\star(b\star c)-b\star(a\star c)=0,\;{\mathrm{L}}_{a\star b}=0.

Since
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a flat Euclidean Lie algebra, by virtue of Theorem
2.1
, it satisfies this two identities.

(2)

A(a\star b)=0
.

We have
\mathfrak{d}={\mathfrak{h}}\star{\mathfrak{h}}
and then
A(a\star b)=0
.

(3)

b\star Aa-a\star Ab=\langle a,u\rangle Ab-\langle b,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}Aa,

This relation holds obviously if
a\in\mathfrak{d}
or
b\in\mathfrak{d}
.

If
a,b\in{\mathcal{A}}
then
\langle u,a\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}=\langle u,b\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}=0
and

b\star Aa-a\star Ab=b\star(a\star h)-a\star(b\star h)=0.

So the relation holds in this case.

Let
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
b=u
. So

\begin{cases}u\star Aa-a\star Au=u\star(a\star h)-a\star m=\rho(u)\rho(a)(h)-\rho(a)\rho(u)h-|u|^{2}\rho(a)h=-|u|^{2}\rho(a)h\\
\langle a,u\rangle Au-\langle u,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}Aa=-|u|^{2}\rho(a)h\end{cases}

and the relation holds.

(4)

A^{2}a=-\langle a,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}w.

For
a\in\mathfrak{d}
or
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
the relation holds obviously. For
a=u
,

\displaystyle A^{2}u

\displaystyle=A(m+n-\lambda u)=\rho(n)h-\lambda(m+n-\lambda u)

\displaystyle=\rho(n)h-\lambda n-\lambda(\rho(u)h+|u|^{2}h)+\lambda^{2}u,

\displaystyle-\langle u,u\rangle w

\displaystyle=-|u|^{2}(\lambda h-\frac{1}{|u|^{2}}\rho(n)(h)+\frac{\lambda}{|u|^{2}}\rho(u)h+\frac{\lambda n}{|u|^{2}}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{|u|^{2}}u)

\displaystyle=-\lambda|u|^{2}h+\rho(n)(h)-\lambda\rho(u)h-{\lambda n}+{\lambda^{2}}u

and the relation holds.

(5)

[F,{\mathrm{L}}_{a}]=0
.

For
a\in\mathfrak{d}
,
{\mathrm{L}}_{a}=0
and for
a\in{\mathcal{A}}\oplus\hbox{\bb R}u
,
({\mathrm{L}}_{a})_{|\mathfrak{d}}=\rho(a)
and
({\mathrm{L}}_{a})_{|{\mathcal{A}}\oplus\hbox{\bb R}u}=0
so
[F,{\mathrm{L}}_{a}]=0
for any
a\in{\mathfrak{h}}
.

(6)

FA={\mathrm{R}}_{w}-\lambda A+\langle\bullet,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}w
.

For
a\in\mathfrak{d}

\displaystyle FAa=0=a\star w-\lambda Aa+\langle a,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}w.

For
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we have

\displaystyle FAa

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|u|^{2}}(\rho(n)\rho(a)h-\lambda\rho(u)\rho(a)h),

\displaystyle\rho(a)w-\lambda Aa

\displaystyle=\rho(a)w_{1}-\lambda\rho(a)h

\displaystyle=\rho(a)\left(\lambda h-\frac{1}{|u|^{2}}\rho(n)(h)+\frac{\lambda}{|u|^{2}}\rho(u)h\right)-\lambda\rho(a)h

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|u|^{2}}(\rho(a)\rho(n)h-\lambda\rho(u)\rho(a)h)

and the relation holds
since
\rho
is a representation of abelian Lie algebra.

For
u
, we have

\displaystyle FAu

\displaystyle=F(m+n-\lambda u)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|u|^{2}}(\rho(n)-\lambda\rho(u))(\rho(u)h+|u|^{2}h),

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|u|^{2}}\rho(n)\rho(u)h-\rho(n)h+\frac{\lambda}{|u|^{2}}\rho(u)^{2}h+\lambda\rho(u)h,

\displaystyle u\star w-\lambda(m+n-\lambda u)+|u|^{2}w

\displaystyle=\rho(u)(\lambda h-\frac{1}{|u|^{2}}\rho(n)(h)+\frac{\lambda}{|u|^{2}}\rho(u)h)-\lambda(\rho(u)h+|u|^{2}h)-\lambda n+\lambda^{2}u

\displaystyle+|u|^{2}\left(\lambda h-\frac{1}{|u|^{2}}\rho(n)(h)+\frac{\lambda}{|u|^{2}}\rho(u)h+\frac{\lambda n}{|u|^{2}}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{|u|^{2}}u\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda\rho(u)h-\frac{1}{|u|^{2}}\rho(u)\rho(n)(h)+\frac{\lambda}{|u|^{2}}\rho(u)^{2}h-\lambda\rho(u)h-\lambda|u|^{2}h-\lambda n+\lambda^{2}u

\displaystyle+\lambda|u|^{2}h-\rho(n)(h)+\lambda\rho(u)h+\lambda n-\lambda^{2}u

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|u|^{2}}\rho(n)\rho(u)h-\rho(n)h+\frac{\lambda}{|u|^{2}}\rho(u)^{2}h+\lambda\rho(u)h

and the relation holds.

(7)

AF=0,\;{\mathrm{L}}_{Fa}=0,\;{\mathrm{L}}_{Aa}=-\langle a,u\rangle F
.

Since
\mathrm{Im}F\subset\mathfrak{d}
, we have
AF=0
and
{\mathrm{L}}_{Fa}=0
. Let us check the relation
{\mathrm{L}}_{Aa}=-\langle a,u\rangle F
. The relation hold obviously if
a\in\mathfrak{d}\oplus{\mathcal{A}}
. For
a=u
, we have

\displaystyle({\mathrm{L}}_{Au})_{|{\mathcal{A}}\oplus\hbox{\bb R}u}

\displaystyle=0=-|u|^{2}F_{|{\mathcal{A}}\oplus\hbox{\bb R}u},

\displaystyle({\mathrm{L}}_{Au})_{|\mathfrak{d}}

\displaystyle=\rho(n)-\lambda\rho(u),

\displaystyle-|u|^{2}F_{|\mathfrak{d}}

\displaystyle=\rho(n)-\lambda\rho(u)

and the relation holds.

(8)

Fu=0,\;\langle w,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}=-\lambda^{2},\;{\mathrm{R}}_{u}=0,\;A^{*}u=-\lambda u,\;{\mathrm{L}}_{w}=-\lambda F,\;Aw=-\lambda w
.

We have obviously
Fu=0
,
\langle w,u\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}=-\lambda^{2}
,
{\mathrm{R}}_{u}=0
and
A^{*}u=-\lambda u
. Moreover,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{L}}_{w}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|u|^{2}}\left(\lambda{\mathrm{L}}_{n}-\lambda^{2}{\mathrm{L}}_{u}\right)=-\lambda F,

\displaystyle Aw

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{|u|^{2}}An-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{|u|^{2}}Au

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{|u|^{2}}\rho(n)h-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{|u|^{2}}(\rho(u)h+|u|^{2}h)-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{|u|^{2}}n+\frac{\lambda^{3}}{|u|^{2}}u

\displaystyle=-\lambda w.

This completes the verification.

6.4
Verification of the Novikov property in Proposition
4.2

Let
({\mathfrak{h}},\star,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a Euclidean algebra together with the data
(E,F,A,u,v,w,\alpha,\lambda)
as in Proposition
4.2
.
Let us check when the extension
\mathfrak{g}
is a Novikov algebra. We have
\alpha=\lambda=0
,
u=v=0
,
E=0
. Moreover,
({\mathfrak{h}},\star,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
and
(F,A,w)
are given in Proposition
4.2
.
In this case, (
16
) reduces to

\begin{cases}a\star(b\star c)-b\star(a\star c)=0,\;{\mathrm{L}}_{a\star b}=0,\\
b\star Aa-a\star Ab=0,\;A(a\star b)=0,\;A^{2}=0,\\
[F,{\mathrm{L}}_{a}]=0,\;FA={\mathrm{R}}_{w},\;AF=0,\\
{\mathrm{L}}_{Fa}=0,\;{\mathrm{L}}_{Aa}=0,\\
Aw=0,{\mathrm{L}}_{w}=0,\\
\end{cases}

Since
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a flat Euclidean Lie algebra, by virtue of Theorem
2.1
, the first relations hold.

Since
\mathfrak{d}={\mathfrak{h}}\star{\mathfrak{h}}
the relation
A(a\star b)=0
is equivalent to
A_{1}=0
and
A^{2}=0
is equivalent to
A_{2}^{2}=0
.

The relations
b\star Aa-a\star Ab=0
and
[F,{\mathrm{L}}_{a}]=0
hold obviously.
The relation
{\mathrm{L}}_{Fa}={\mathrm{L}}_{Aa}=0
is equivalent to
\mathrm{Im}F_{2}\subset\ker\rho
and

\mathrm{Im}A_{2}\subset\ker\rho
. We have
{\mathrm{L}}_{w}={\mathrm{L}}_{w_{2}}=0
if and only if
\rho(w_{2})=0
. Moreover,
A(w)=\rho(w_{2})h+A_{2}w_{2}=0
if and only if
\rho(w_{2})h=0
and
A(w)=0
. We have

FA=\begin{pmatrix}0&F_{1}\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{h}\\
0&F_{2}A_{2}\end{pmatrix},\;AF=\begin{pmatrix}0&{\mathrm{R}}_{h}\circ F_{2}\\
0&A_{2}F_{2}\end{pmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathrm{R}}_{w}=\begin{pmatrix}0&{\mathrm{R}}_{F_{1}(h)}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

So
AF=0
and

are equivalent to

A_{2}F_{2}=F_{2}A_{2}=0,\;\rho(F_{2}(a))h=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad F_{1}(a\star h)=a\star F_{1}(h),\;a\in\mathfrak{a}.

Finally, we get that
\mathfrak{g}
is a Novikov algebra if and only if

[F_{1},\rho(a)]=0,\;A_{1}=0,\;A_{2}^{2}=0,\;\mathrm{Im}F_{2}\subset\ker\rho,\;\mathrm{Im}A_{2}\subset\ker\rho,\;A_{2}F_{2}=F_{2}A_{2}=0,\;\rho(w_{2})h=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad A_{2}w_{2}=0.

Let
({\mathfrak{h}},\star,\langle\;,\;\rangle)

{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}=\mathfrak{d}\oplus\mathfrak{b}

Verification of the identities.

(1)

[{\mathrm{L}}_{a},{\mathrm{L}}_{b}]=0

(2)

{\mathrm{L}}_{a\star b}=-\langle Aa,b\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E

and
\langle Aa,b\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E=0

\displaystyle={\mathrm{L}}_{\rho(a)x}=\langle\rho(a)x,X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{L}}_{x\star a}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\langle Ax,a\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E

\displaystyle=0.

\displaystyle{\mathrm{L}}_{x\star y}

\displaystyle=\langle x,X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}{\mathrm{L}}_{E(y)}=\langle x,X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}\langle E(y),X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E,

\displaystyle\langle Ax,y\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E

\displaystyle=\langle x,X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}\langle E(X),y\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}=-\langle x,X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}\langle E(y),X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E.

(3)

[E,F]=[E,{\mathrm{L}}_{a}]=[F,{\mathrm{L}}_{a}]=0
.

These relation are obviously satisfied.

(4)

EA={\mathrm{R}}_{v},\;FA={\mathrm{R}}_{w}
.

For
a\in\mathfrak{a},x\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}

\displaystyle EAa-{\mathrm{R}}_{v}(a)

\displaystyle=E\rho(a)X-\rho(a)E(v_{0})=[E,\rho(a)]X=0,

\displaystyle FAa-{\mathrm{R}}_{w}(a)

\displaystyle=F\rho(a)X-\rho(a)F(X)=[F,\rho(a)]X=0,

\displaystyle=\langle x,X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E^{2}(X)-x\star E(v_{0})=\langle x,X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E^{2}(X)-x\star E(X)=0,

\displaystyle FAx-{\mathrm{R}}_{w}(x)

\displaystyle=\langle x,X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}FE(X)-x\star F(X)=\langle x,X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}[E,F](X)=0.

(5)

AE=-\langle\bullet,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}v,\;AF=-\langle\bullet,w\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}v
.

Recall that
v=E(v_{0})=E(X)
and
w=F(X)
. For
a\in\mathfrak{a},x\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}
,

\displaystyle AEa+\langle a,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}v

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle AFa+\langle a,w\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}v

\displaystyle=AF_{2}(a)=\rho(F_{2}(a))X=0,

\displaystyle AEx+\langle x,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}v

\displaystyle=\langle Ex,X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E(X)+\langle x,E(X)\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E(X)=0,

\displaystyle AFx+\langle x,w\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}v

\displaystyle=\langle Fx,X\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E(x)+\langle x,F(X)\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E(X)=0.

(6)

{\mathrm{L}}_{Ea}=-\langle a,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E,{\mathrm{L}}_{Fa}=-\langle a,w\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E,\;{\mathrm{L}}_{Aa}=0
.

For
a\in\mathfrak{a},x\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}
,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{L}}_{Ea}+\langle a,v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{L}}_{Fa}+\langle a,w\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}E

\displaystyle={\mathrm{L}}_{F_{2}a}=0,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{L}}_{Aa}
