Recurrence of the Chronic Subdural Hematoma After Burr-Hole Trephination With or Without Intra-Operative Saline Irrigation
Authors: Young Hwan Kim, Hak Sung Kim, Jae Ho Kim, Jong Gyu Kim, Chang Il Ju
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveChronic subdural hematoma (CSDH) is a common condition in the elderly population, usually treated by burr-hole trephination and hematoma drainage. Although various surgical techniques have been described, the optimal surgical method remains controversial. This study aimed to compare the recurrence rate of CSDH after burr-hole drainage with or without intraoperative saline irrigation to identify the more effective surgical approach.MethodsFrom January 2019 to December 2021, a total of 126 patients who underwent burr-hole trephination for CSDH were retrospectively reviewed. Among them, operative records of 145 procedures were analyzed. Patients were divided into two groups according to whether intraoperative saline irrigation was performed (n=91) or not (n=54). Demographic characteristics, medical history, radiologic findings, and recurrence or reoperation rates were compared between the groups.ResultsAmong 145 procedures, recurrence occurred in 33 cases (22.76%), and reoperation was required in 6 cases (4.14%). The recurrence rate was significantly lower in the irrigation group (14.81%) compared to the non-irrigation group (27.47%). The reoperation rate was also lower with irrigation (1.85% vs. 5.49%). Univariate analysis identified male sex, non-homogeneous hematoma type, and anticoagulation use as significant risk factors for recurrence. Multivariate analysis confirmed intraoperative saline irrigation as a significant independent protective factor.ConclusionThis study demonstrates that intraoperative saline irrigation during burr-hole drainage for CSDH significantly reduces the rates of recurrence and reoperation, showing favorable clinical outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

Chronic subdural hematoma (CSDH) is one of the most common neurosurgical diseases that can occur after head injury, especially in elderly patients.214) A history of trauma is frequently present, although it often remains unconfirmed. The prevalence of CSDH is increasing with the aging of society.16) In the past, CSDH was thought to be caused by repetitive hemorrhage owing to rupture of cortical bridging veins from minor head injury.33) However, recent studies suggest that CSDH formation is driven not merely by simple trauma, but by a continuous cycle of complex inflammatory reactions and re-bleeding that occur after trauma.46111528) This complex inflammatory reaction is thought to be the main mechanism of CSDH formation.

Although more than 60% of patients have a history of minor head injury,10) elderly patients often do not recall the traumatic event. A small CSDH can usually be absorbed spontaneously; however, if neurological deficits are present, surgical treatment is required. The treatment of CSDH is successfully performed through simple surgical procedures such as burr-hole trephination without accompanying disease or additional cerebral hemorrhage. Among the neurosurgical treatments, it is considered a relatively simple procedure with a short learning curve. However, the high recurrence rate remains a major complication with reported recurrence rates ranging from 3.1% to 33.3%.22)

Various surgical methods have been introduced so far, but burr-hole trephination is now considered the most effective surgical methods.1835) In addition, it is known that maintaining drainage after surgery has the effect of decreasing the recurrence rate by about half.2535) However, the efficacy of intraoperative saline irrigation in reducing the recurrence rate remains controversial. Based on our surgical experience, we hypothesized that intraoperative saline irrigation could lower the recurrence rate of CSDH.

This study aimed to demonstrate that the procedure with intraoperative saline irrigation can lower the recurrence rate and reoperation rate of CSDH. Furthermore, the effects of the comorbidity and radiologic feature of hematoma on the recurrence rates and the patient’s prognosis were evaluated.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

We retrospectively analyzed the electronic medical records (EMRs) and radiologic findings of 205 patients with CSDH who underwent burr-hole trephination in our hospital from January 2019 to December 2021. In all cases, surgery was performed through a single burr-hole trephination. Preoperative computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) were performed for diagnostic purposes in all patients. To minimize bias, the imaging findings were retrospectively and independently reviewed by two neurosurgeons. Among them, patients with a follow-up period of less than 3 months, patients with vascular malformation such as arteriovenous fistula and intracerebral hemorrhage were excluded. To minimize the ‘surgeon effect’ and ensure procedural consistency, both surgeons adhered to a standardized institutional protocol for all preoperative preparations, surgical steps, and post-operative care. This uniform approach was intended to mitigate potential biases arising from individual surgical proficiency or selection preferences.

A total 126 patients were selected as the population, and a total 145 cases were collected considering the case of bilateral CSDH. Of these, 19 patients (13.1%) presented with bilateral CSDH, and each hematoma cavity was analyzed as an independent case (n=145) based on the site-specific nature of the surgical intervention. The inclusion criteria were: 1) adult patients (age >18 years) diagnosed with CSDH via CT or MRI, and 2) those who underwent their first surgical treatment at our institution. The exclusion criteria were: 1) patients with a prior history of ipsilateral CSDH surgery, 2) traumatic acute subdural hematoma (SDH), and 3) cases with incomplete clinical or radiologic follow-up data. By excluding recurrent cases and prior surgical histories, we aimed to evaluate the primary impact of intraoperative saline irrigation on treatment-naïve CSDH. There were 98 (67.5%) male patients and 47 (32.4%) female patients. The mean age was 70.46 years old and there were patients ranging from 26 to 92 years old. We divided all cases into two groups depending on the intraoperative saline irrigation. Fortunately, the distinct surgical preferences of the two neurosurgeons at our institution naturally allowed us to classify the patients based on the use of intraoperative saline irrigation. The group that did not perform the intraoperative saline irrigation was classified as group A (n=91), and the group that underwent intraoperative saline irrigation was classified as group B (n=54). In group A, we inserted the drain catheter right away and terminated the operation without further treatment. In group B, we performed the saline irrigation through burr-hole using a large amount of saline, and the irrigation through a catheter was also performed using a syringe. Irrigation was continued until the drainage fluid came out in a somewhat clean form. The drainage catheter was connected to the drain bag, and the drainage bag was placed on lower dependent position than the head for smooth drainage. It was maintained for 1 or 2 days and withdrawn in consideration of drain volume and CT findings.

In this study, each hematoma cavity in bilateral cases (n=19, 13.1%) was treated as an independent clinical unit for analysis. This methodology was chosen because the primary outcome—recurrence—is directly influenced by the localized surgical environment and the specific intervention (irrigation vs. non-irrigation) applied to that particular subdural space. To ensure the stability of the logistic regression model and prevent multicollinearity, independent variables were carefully selected based on their distinct clinical and radiologic characteristics.

Surgical technique: irrigation procedure

In the irrigation group, after the hematoma was evacuated through burr-hole trephination, a soft, multi-perforated drainage catheter was inserted into the subdural space. Irrigation was performed manually using a 10mL syringe with warm sterile normal saline. The irrigation was conducted gently to avoid any cortical injury or sudden increase in intracranial pressure. The total volume of saline used for irrigation was not predetermined; rather, the procedure continued until the initially dark-red, old bloody discharge became clear and transparent.

To minimize postoperative pneumocephalus, the subdural space was intentionally and completely filled with saline at the end of the procedure. Saline was injected through the catheter until it overflowed through the burr hole, ensuring that no residual air space remained before the skin was closed.

Data collection

Demographic, clinical and radiographic data `were collected by EMRs. The demographic and clinical data included: gender, age, direct head trauma, smoking history, medical history (hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, liver diseases, renal diseases, stroke), history of anticoagulant or antiplatelet medication, pre or postoperative Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), pre or postoperative modified Rankin Score (mRS), hospitalization days. A GCS and mRS were immediately before and 30 days after surgery through medical records in all patients, and the effect of surgical treatment was evaluated through this.

The radiographic data included: operation side, hematoma thickness (defined as the maximal thickness of the remaining hematoma and fluid within the subdural space on the axial view, excluding pneumocephalus volume on CT or MRI), midline shifting (more than or less than 10 mm), hematoma density characteristics on preoperative CT (FIGURE 1). All hematomas were classified into four types according to hematoma density: homogeneous, laminar, separated, and trabecular types.20) More broadly, hematomas were classified into a homogeneous group and a non-homogeneous group (which included the remaining types).

In this study, recurrence was defined based on both radiologic and clinical criteria. Radiologic recurrence was defined as an increase in the thickness or volume of the SDH on follow-up CT scans compared to the immediate post-operative CT, with or without a significant midline shift. Specifically, a hematoma thickness of 10mm or greater at the 1-month follow-up was utilized as the quantitive threshold for radiologic recurrence. Clinical recurrence was defined as the recurrence or worsening of neurologic symptoms (e.g., headache, hemiparesis, gait disturbance, or altered consciousness) associated with the re-accumulation of the hematoma. For the purpose of statistical analysis, a ‘recurrent case’ was designated as a patient who exhibited both radiologic progression and relevant clinical symptoms. Among these recurrent cases, ‘re-operation’ was specifically defined as a subset of patients who required secondary surgical intervention due to severe neurological deterioration.

Statistical analyses

We used the SPSS version 24.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) software for statistical analysis. Data were collected on demographic features, clinical history, radiologic factors, clinical outcomes, recurrence and re-operation rate. We performed a univariate analysis to study the relationships between each variable and the recurrence rate of CSDH by applying the Mann–Whitney U test and the χ2 test. Logistic regression analysis was used to identify the degree for correlation between the surgical procedure and the recurrence, and significance was prescribed at a p-value of less than 0.05.

To identify true independent predictors of recurrence and adjust for potential baseline confounding factors, a multivariate logistic regression analysis was subsequently conducted (TABLE 4). The multivariable model demonstrated that male sex remained a robust, independent risk factor for CSDH recurrence (odds ratio [OR], 4.001; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.274–12.569; p=0.018). Crucially, intraoperative saline irrigation was confirmed to be a statistically significant independent protective factor, substantially reducing the odds of recurrence by approximately 58% (OR, 0.421; 95% CI, 0.186–0.954; p=0.038). DISCUSSION

Surgical treatment of CSDH is probably one of the first surgical techniques for neurosurgeons. It is usually treated with burr-hole trephination and has a good prognosis. However, it shows a relatively high recurrence rate after surgery, and there are various controversies over detailed surgical methods and risk factors to reduce this. Many risk factors of the recurrence of CSDH have been reported. Reported factors include age, gender, antiplatelet or anticoagulant medication, brain atrophy, direct trauma history, bilateral hematoma, hematoma type, diabetes, postoperative posture, time of surgery, surgical procedure, postoperative pneumocephalus, cytokines, alcohol abuse, smoking history and midline shifting.13819202125263234)

Our study attempted to confirm the difference of detailed surgical methods on the recurrence rate of CSDH and the existing risk factors. In principle, it is required to understand the pathophysiology of CSDH. CSDH is a fluid collection consisting of various stages of bleeding depending on the production time between the dura mater and arachnoid mater in the subdural space.7) Conventionally, the cause of CSDH was a traumatic head injury inducing tearing of the bridging veins on the brain surface.1723) However, this theory is controversial considering that imaging scans performed immediately after head trauma often do not find acute bleeding, that it takes more than four weeks on average to develop neurologic symptoms,930) and that the blood pattern is conflicting with a substance of bridging vein.5) Considering these points, we can assume that acute hemorrhage is not the only source of CSDH formation.

Recently many studies propose there are complex pathways involved in CSDH development. Edlmann et al.7) reported the main causes of CSDH formation are inflammation, angiogenesis and membrane development. The unnatural membrane surrounding the CSDH has been identified as a source of fluid collection and hemorrhage. Angiogenesis promotes the formation of fragile blood vessels within membrane walls and the accompanying fibrinolysis prevents clot formation. This series of processes causes bleeding tendency, which leads to repeated hemorrhage. An abundance of inflammation markers have been found within the membranes and CSDH.1231) It contributes to the inflammatory response and promotes membrane growth and fluid collection.7) Saito et al.24) reported that the risk of CSDH recurrence increased when high concentrations of these inflammation markers remained after surgery. This can be the physiologic rationale for reducing the recurrence rate of CSDH by burr-hole trephination with saline irrigation, and the same results can be confirmed in our study. In our study, most recurrences were confirmed in the intraoperative non-irrigation group, and statistically significant difference was identified.

Moreover, our univariate analysis showed that the recurrence rate was significantly higher in the non-homogeneous hematoma group than in the homogeneous group (p=0.039). Although its statistical independence was attenuated in the multivariate model (p=0.182), the clinical relevance of hematoma density should not be overlooked. Non-homogeneous hematomas are known to harbor higher concentrations of inflammatory markers, which promote membrane growth and fluid collection.24) The loss of statistical independence in the multivariate model suggests a potential confounding effect or interaction with the surgical procedure. Given the baseline imbalance where the non-irrigation group contained a higher proportion of separated-type hematomas (p=0.001), the therapeutic effect of extensive saline irrigation may have actively neutralized the intrinsic recurrence risk of non-homogeneous types. Similarly, anticoagulation history lost strict significance in the multivariate model but exhibited a prominent clinical trend with a high OR of 6.017 (p=0.057). This marginal significance is highly likely due to the small number of patients on anticoagulants in our cohort (n=7), which limited the statistical power.

In this study, the significantly longer hospital stay observed in the recurrent group compared to the non-recurrent group is interpreted as a consequence of additional medical interventions or re-operations necessitated by the recurrence, rather than a causal factor for recurrence itself. Conversely, the non-irrigation group (group A) exhibited a higher recurrence rate but a significantly shorter hospital stay compared to the irrigation group (group B). This discrepancy occurred because the neurosurgeon responsible for the non-irrigation group tended to transfer patients to local hospitals within five days postoperatively. Such early transfer may have acted as a critical confounding factor in our analysis. Considering existing literature which suggests that sufficient postoperative bed rest and intensive early management can positively influence the prevention of CSDH recurrence, the higher recurrence rate in group A might have been affected not only by the absence of intraoperative irrigation but also by the relatively short period of postoperative management. Therefore, standardized postoperative protocols are required to isolate and verify the pure clinical effects of saline irrigation.

Conversely, Kim et al.13) reported a higher recurrence rate in saline irrigation group than in non-irrigation group. The author mentioned that the massive irrigation procedure leaves a large amount of air in the subdural space after surgery, and that the air generated in this way suppresses the expansion of the brain parenchyma. In addition, brain cortex damage that occurs during saline irrigation can cause not only recurrence of CSDH, but also acute cerebral hemorrhage, so it is recommended not to perform the irrigation during burr-hole trephination with closed drainage.2729) However, if intraoperative saline irrigation is performed, we believe that the proper amount of saline injection through drain catheter at the end of surgery can minimize the pneumocephalus and that careful irrigation can prevent brain damage. Based on our findings, a more effective and safe procedure is required to reduce the postoperative pneumocephalus and brain cortex injury.

In our multivariate model, male sex emerged as a statistically significant independent risk factor for CSDH recurrence. While the exact pathophysiological mechanism linking male sex to higher recurrence rates remains to be fully elucidated, it may be associated with differences in physical activity, head trauma patterns, or hormonal factors affecting hematoma membrane permeability. Furthermore, although anticoagulation history did not reach strict statistical significance in our fully adjusted model (p=0.057), the notably high OR (6.017) underscores its potential clinical importance. We interpret this as a strong clinical trend, suggesting that patients on anticoagulants require more stringent postoperative monitoring and perhaps a lower threshold for re-intervention. These independent risk factors, when considered alongside the protective role of saline irrigation, provide a nuanced framework for personalizing surgical and postoperative care strategies to minimize the risk of CSDH recurrence.

Our research has the limitations of retrospective research, research in a single institution, and a relatively small group, and it is thought that it needs to be supplemented. Future multicenter studies with larger patient cohorts are required to validate these findings. Another limitation of this study is that we did not include the individual surgeon as a covariate in the statistical model. Although we mitigated this by following a strictly standardized protocol, the potential for a ‘surgeon effect’ cannot be entirely excluded. However, given that burr-hole trephination is a highly standardized and relatively simple neurosurgical procedure, we expect the impact of individual technical differences on the overall recurrence rate to be minimal. In addition, it seems necessary to study whether there is a clinical improvement in comparing the difference between GCS and mRS before and after surgery. Furthermore, while we suggested that the clearance of high concentrations of the inflammatory markers is a primary mechanism for reducing recurrence in the irrigation group, this study is limited by the lack of quantitative analysis of these markers. Specifically, we did not measure the levels of cytokines or other inflammatory mediators in the drainage fluid of the two groups. Future studies incorporating biochemical assessments would be essential to provide more definitive evidence regarding the pathophysiological impact of intraoperative saline irrigation on the recurrence of CSDH.

Lastly, there is a statistical limitation concerning the independence of data. We included bilateral CSDH cases as independent samples, which may not fully account for intra-patient correlation or systemic factors affecting both sides. Although we focused on the site-specific effect of intraoperative irrigation, this statistical constraint should be considered when interpreting the results. Future studies utilizing more advanced models, such as generalized estimating equations, would be necessary to further refine these findings.

CONCLUSION

CSDH is a common disease in elderly patients and the incidence is increasing with aging. In our study, overall recurrence rate after burr-hole drainage was 22.8%. While anticoagulation history and non-homogeneous hematoma type were significantly associated with recurrence in the univariate analysis, our multivariate model confirmed that intraoperative saline irrigation is a significant independent protective factor against recurrence, whereas male sex is an independent risk factor. Anticoagulants also demonstrated a strong clinical trend toward higher recurrence. Therefore, we carefully recommend the routine implementation of saline irrigation during burr-hole trephination to improve clinical outcomes and mitigate recurrence risks.
