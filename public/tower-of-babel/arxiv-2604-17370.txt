Weighted Automata and Regular Expressions for Financial Systems
Manfred Droste, Vitaly Nürnberg

Abstract
We introduce weighted finite finance automata (WFFA), a formal framework for modeling and analyzing quantitative properties of financial systems driven by uncertain economic variables such as stock prices, interest rates, and exchange rates. The model provides a compositional and language-theoretic approach to scenario-based financial analysis, enabling systematic evaluation of financial instruments and trading strategies. To specify such systems, we introduce weighted finance regular expressions , a declarative language for quantitative financial properties. We establish a Kleene–Schützenberger-type correspondence [ 29 , 20 ] between WFFAs and weighted finance regular expressions, together with effective translation procedures between the two formalisms. On the algorithmic side, we investigate fundamental decision and optimization problems for WFFAs, including the computation of extremal payoffs, and identify expressive yet computationally tractable subclasses. These results provide a foundation for formal, compositional, and efficient analysis of financial systems under multiple market scenarios.



Keywords:
weighted automata, financial modeling, scenario analysis, derivatives, option pricing, formal methods, max-plus algebra, regular expressions

1
Introduction

Financial markets play a central role in modern economic systems and give rise to a wide range of complex financial instruments
[
13
,
19
,
24
,
25
]
.
To support decision-making, a variety of sophisticated mathematical models, in particular stochastic models, and software tools have been developed. However, given the inherent unpredictability of financial markets, these approaches cannot guarantee reliable outcomes. Moreover, they typically lack a compositional and formally analyzable structure that would allow quantitative reasoning across sequences of interacting market events.

A central challenge in computational finance is the efficient evaluation of financial instruments over large collections of possible market scenarios. In practice, such analyses rely on scenario generation, stress testing, and backtesting (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
), which often require ad hoc numerical procedures and offer limited support for compositional modeling. As a result, it remains difficult to reason about complex financial systems in a scalable and interpretable way. These limitations motivate the development of formal frameworks that support both expressive modeling of financial scenarios and efficient quantitative evaluation.

Formal methods provide a natural foundation for such frameworks. By offering mathematically precise models with well-defined semantics, they enable rigorous specification, analysis, and verification of complex systems. In recent years, formal methods have been successfully applied in financial contexts, including the design of exchange mechanisms
[
15
]
and the analysis of DeFi smart contracts
[
3
]
, demonstrating their potential for improving reliability and risk management.

At the core of formal methods lie non-deterministic finite automata
[
26
,
18
]
and regular expressions
[
20
,
18
]
, which serve as fundamental models for representing and analyzing discrete systems. Automata provide intuitive operational models and form the basis for verification techniques such as model checking
[
5
]
, while regular expressions offer concise specification formalisms.

Weighted automata
, introduced by Schützenberger
[
29
]
(see also
[
12
,
22
,
28
,
7
]
), extend classical automata by assigning weights from a semiring to transitions, which are aggregated along runs. They provide a general framework for modeling quantitative properties such as time
[
23
]
, costs and rewards
[
9
]
, probabilities
[
27
]
, and energy consumption
[
4
]
. They have also found important applications in natural language processing and artificial intelligence
[
30
]
.
The algebraic structure of weighted automata yields robust closure and decidability properties, making them a powerful tool for quantitative reasoning.

Weighted regular expressions
provide a complementary specification formalism, and the Kleene–Schützenberger theorem
[
20
,
29
]
establishes their expressive equivalence with weighted automata.

Despite their expressive power, weighted automata have not yet been systematically applied to financial modeling, where system behavior depends on dynamically evolving external variables.
Classical models do not directly capture such data-dependent dynamics.
Bridging this gap enables the transfer of automata-theoretic techniques to financial applications, in particular for scenario-based analysis.

In this paper, we extend the theory of weighted automata and regular expressions to model financial systems driven by external quantitative data. Our framework provides a compositional and formally analyzable approach to scenario-based financial modeling.

Our contributions:

•

We introduce
weighted finite finance automata (WFFA)
over semirings.
WFFAs are non-deterministic finite automata that process finite sequences of valued letters, i.e., pairs consisting of a symbol from a finite alphabet and a value from a (possibly infinite) data domain.
In contrast to classical weighted automata, transition weights are arithmetic expressions with variables (so-called
\mathbb{F}
-expressions
) referring to financial quantities at the time of a transition.

•

We demonstrate the expressiveness of WFFAs through financial examples, including bonds, stocks, European and American options, and limit orders, demonstrating that the framework captures a broad range of financial instruments.

•

We establish closure of WFFAs under standard operations, including sum, Hadamard and Cauchy products, and Kleene star.
To control the growth of transition expressions, we identify subclasses of bounded size. In particular, we show that monomial
expressions suffice to capture all WFFA behaviors, while general
\mathbb{F}
-expressions provide a convenient specification formalism.
For certain primitive expressions, the corresponding constructions can be further optimized, ensuring computational tractability in practical applications.

•

We introduce
weighted finance regular expressions
and prove a Kleene–Schützenberger-type theorem
[
29
,
20
]
establishing expressive equivalence with WFFAs.
We further show that, when restricted to primitive expressions, weighted finance regular expressions are strictly more expressive than WFFAs, and we identify a restricted fragment that exactly matches WFFA expressiveness.

•

We give an algebraic characterization of WFFAs via matrix representations and derive an efficient evaluation procedure running in time quadratic in the number of states.

•

In the max-plus setting, we prove decidability of the support and threshold problems for WFFAs. These problems are fundamental in the theory of weighted automata (cf.
[
22
,
7
]
).
In our setting, they determine, respectively, whether any non-trivial outcome is achievable and whether a given payoff level can be exceeded, thereby providing a basis for the formal analysis of financial strategies.

Related models:

•

Binomial trees

[
6
,
19
]
are a classical tool in financial engineering for modeling asset price evolution and pricing derivatives.
They describe a discrete-time branching structure where, at each step, the asset price moves to one of two possible values.
Such trees can be naturally interpreted as sets of market scenarios, where each path corresponds to a finance word in our setting, while WFFAs provide a mechanism to evaluate quantitative properties along these scenarios.
In contrast to classical binomial models, where price dynamics are fixed in advance, WFFAs allow the use of symbolic transition expressions, enabling more flexible and data-dependent modeling.

•

The
discounted cash flow (DCF) model

[
21
]
estimates the value of an investment by aggregating discounted future cash flows.
Several weighted automata models incorporate discounting (cf.
[
8
,
10
]
), typically assuming fixed discount factors encoded in the model.
In contrast, WFFAs support dynamically determined discount factors that are provided as part of the input data, for instance derived from observed market rates.

•

WFFAs can be viewed as
automata over an infinite alphabet
, since they process symbols paired with values from a potentially infinite domain.
Related models include weighted timed automata
[
23
]
and weighted register automata
[
2
]
, which use clocks or registers to handle data.
While these models achieve high expressiveness, this often comes at a significant computational cost, and classical closure properties may fail.
In contrast, WFFAs remain lightweight and retain key algebraic and algorithmic properties of semiring-weighted automata over finite alphabets.
Moreover, WFFAs are complementary to register-based models: whereas register automata are well-suited for specifying and constraining data-dependent scenarios, WFFAs provide a natural framework for quantitative evaluation of such scenarios.

Recall that a

3
Weighted Finite Finance Automata

Let
\Sigma
be an alphabet and
\mathbb{F}=(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{D},\mathbb{b})
a finance semiring with the underlying commutative semiring
\mathbb{S}=(S,\oplus,\otimes,\mathbb{0},\mathbb{1})

Definition 3.1
.

A (non-deterministic) weighted finite finance automaton (WFFA) over
\Sigma
and
\mathbb{F}
is a tuple
\mathcal{A}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})
where:

•

Q
is a finite set of states,

•

•

T\subseteq Q\times\Sigma\times Q

•

\mathrm{wt}_{I}:I\to S
and
\mathrm{wt}_{F}:F\to S
are an initial weight function resp. a final weight function,

•

\mathrm{wt}_{T}:T\to\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
is a transition weight function assigning an
\mathbb{F}
-expression to every transition.

In financial applications, the states
Q
of a WFFA

encode the possible stages of a contract or financial process.
These may represent, for instance, periods between cash flows, phases of a bond or option contract, or different market or credit states.

Let
|\mathcal{A}|_{Q}=|Q|
, and let

|\mathcal{A}|_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}}=\max\{|\mathrm{wt}_{T}(t)|\mid t\in T\}

denote the maximal size of a transition weight occurring in

. If

, we set

.
We say that
\mathcal{A}
is

if all initial and final weights of

are equal to

, i.e.,
\mathrm{wt}_{I}(I)=\mathrm{wt}_{F}(F)=\{\mathbb{1}\}

\mathbb{F}
.
Let
{\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\tau}\subseteq\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}}

, the

i\in[n]
. For any finance word
w=(a_{1},d_{1})\dots(a_{n},d_{n})\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
, let
\mathrm{Run}_{\mathcal{A}}(w)
be the set of all runs
\varrho\in\mathrm{Run}_{\mathcal{A}}
with label
a_{1}\dots a_{n}
that are valid in run context
d_{1}\dots d_{n}
. The set is always finite, but it may contain exponentially many runs in
|w|
.

The
behavior
of
\mathcal{A}
is the mapping
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S
defined for all finance words
w=(a_{1},d_{1})\dots(a_{n},d_{n})\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
by

{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)=\bigoplus\big(\mathrm{wt}_{\mathcal{A}}(\varrho,d_{1}\dots d_{n})\mid\varrho\in\mathrm{Run}_{\mathcal{A}}(w)\big)

where
\bigoplus\emptyset=\mathbb{0}
. Intuitively, the behavior of
\mathcal{A}
is the value that the automaton assigns to a given finance word by aggregating the contributions of all valid runs.

For any WFFA collection
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
, let
{[\![\mathcal{W}]\!]}=\{{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}\mid\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{W}\}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Since
S\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
, any WFFA
\mathcal{A}
with transition weights in
S
reduces to a standard semiring-weighted automaton. In this case, the behavior of
\mathcal{A}
is independent of the data values, i.e.,
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w^{\prime})
for all finance words
w,w^{\prime}
with the same underlying event sequence. Hence,
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
can be viewed as a function
\Sigma^{*}\to S
, i.e., a formal power series. Thus, WFFAs extend classical weighted automata by allowing data-dependent transition weights.

4
Weighted Finance Regular Expressions

Let
\Sigma
be an alphabet and
\mathbb{F}=(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{D},\mathbb{b})
be a finance semiring, where
\mathbb{S}=(S,\oplus,\otimes,\mathbb{0},\mathbb{1})
is a commutative semiring.
A
quantitative finance language (QFL)
over
\Sigma
and
\mathbb{F}
is a mapping
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S
.
We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
proper
if
\mathcal{F}(\varepsilon)=\mathbb{0}
.
Let
\mathrm{QFL}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
denote the collection of all QFLs over
\Sigma
and
\mathbb{F}
.

From an economic perspective, a QFL
\mathcal{F}
assigns a quantitative outcome to each possible market scenario.
A finance word
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
represents a discrete-time evolution of relevant market variables together with observable events, while the value
\mathcal{F}(w)
corresponds to a financial quantity of interest, such as a payoff, a cost, or a risk measure.
Thus, QFLs provide a formal, scenario-based view of financial systems, capturing how the outcome of a financial instrument or strategy depends on the underlying market trajectory.
This perspective naturally encompasses key tasks in quantitative finance, including scenario analysis, stress testing, and the evaluation of trading strategies under varying market conditions.

Given
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
, the behavior
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
is a QFL over
\Sigma
and
\mathbb{F}
. Hence,

{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}]\!]}\subseteq\mathrm{QFL}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}.

That is, WFFAs define a subclass of QFLs that admit a finite-state, operational representation.

While QFLs provide a semantic view of financial systems, such mappings are typically too complex to be specified explicitly in a compact form.
Instead, one seeks structured and compositional formalisms for defining them.
WFFAs provide one such formalism.
In this section, we introduce an alternative, expression-based approach for defining QFLs, namely
weighted finance regular expressions
.

To define their semantics, we first lift the standard operations on formal power series (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
) to the setting of quantitative finance languages.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\in\mathrm{QFL}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
.

(a)

The sum
(\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2})\in\mathrm{QFL}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
is defined for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
by

(\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2})(w)=\mathcal{F}_{1}(w)\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}(w).

(b)

The Hadamard product
(\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{2})\in\mathrm{QFL}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
is defined for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
by
(\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{2})(w)=\mathcal{F}_{1}(w)\otimes\mathcal{F}_{2}(w)
.

(c)

The Cauchy product
(\mathcal{F}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{2})\in\mathrm{QFL}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
is defined for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
by

{(\mathcal{F}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{2})(w)=\bigoplus\big(\mathcal{F}_{1}(w_{1})\otimes\mathcal{F}_{2}(w_{2})\mid w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\text{ and }w_{1}\cdot w_{2}=w\big)}

where
{w_{1}\cdot w_{2}}
denotes the concatenation of finance words.

(d)

Let
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{QFL}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
be proper. The Kleene star
\mathcal{F}^{*}\in\mathrm{QFL}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
is defined by

{\mathcal{F}^{*}(w)=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{F}(w_{i})\mid w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\text{ and }w_{1}\cdot{\dots}\cdot w_{k}=w\right)}

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
, where the empty product (for
k=0
) is defined to be
\mathbb{1}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that the infinite sum in the equation for
\mathcal{F}^{*}(w)
has only finitely many non-zero terms, since
\mathcal{F}
is proper. Hence,
\mathcal{F}^{*}(w)
is well defined.

Remark 4.3
.

The operations from Definition
4.1
admit a natural interpretation in quantitative finance.
The sum
\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}
models the aggregation of alternative outcomes, for instance selecting the better
payoff in max-plus settings or combining competing strategies.
The Hadamard product
\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{2}
combines the values of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
for the same market scenario.
In the max-plus case, this operation
reduces to additive composition, so that combining with a constant corresponds
to shifting the resulting value, e.g., modeling fixed costs or premiums.
The Cauchy product
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{2}
captures the sequential composition of financial mechanisms: it aggregates all possible decompositions of a scenario into two consecutive phases, reflecting, for example, multi-period contracts or staged investment decisions. Finally, the Kleene star
\mathcal{F}^{*}
models iteration and repetition, allowing for an unbounded number of consecutive applications of a financial mechanism, as in repeated trading strategies or rolling contracts.

Together, these operations provide a compositional toolkit for constructing complex financial models from simpler building blocks.

Definition 4.4
.

A weighted finance regular expression
\mathcal{R}
over
\Sigma
and
\mathbb{F}
is derived from the grammar

\mathcal{R}\;::=\;s_{\varepsilon}\mid e_{a}\mid\mathcal{R}\oplus\mathcal{R}\mid\mathcal{R}\cdot\mathcal{R}\mid\mathcal{R}^{*}

where
s\in S
,
e\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
a\in\Sigma
.

The
semantics
of
\mathcal{R}
is the QFL
{[\![\mathcal{R}]\!]}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S
defined for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
inductively on the structure of
\mathcal{R}
as follows:

\displaystyle{[\![s_{\varepsilon}]\!]}(w)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}s,&\text{if }w=\varepsilon,\\
\mathbb{0},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle{[\![e_{a}]\!]}(w)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}{[\![e]\!]}(d),&\text{if }w=(a,d)\text{ with }a\in\Sigma\text{ and }d\in\mathbb{D}\\
\mathbb{0},&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

\displaystyle{[\![\mathcal{R}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{R}_{2}]\!]}(w)

\displaystyle=({[\![\mathcal{R}_{1}]\!]}\oplus{[\![\mathcal{R}_{2}]\!]})(w)

\displaystyle{[\![\mathcal{R}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{R}_{2}]\!]}(w)

\displaystyle=({[\![\mathcal{R}_{1}]\!]}\cdot{[\![\mathcal{R}_{2}]\!]})(w)

\displaystyle{[\![(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})^{*}]\!]}(w)

\displaystyle={[\![\mathcal{R}^{\prime}]\!]}^{*}(w)

where
s\in S
,
e\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
,
a\in\Sigma
and
\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2},\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
are subexpressions of
\mathcal{R}
. Here, if
{[\![\mathcal{R}^{\prime}]\!]}
is not proper, then the semantics
{[\![\mathcal{R}^{\prime}]\!]}^{*}
may be undefined, since the definition of the star involves infinite sums, which need not exist in general semirings. A regular expression
\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
is called
valid
if, for every subexpression
(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})^{*}
of
\mathcal{R}
,
{[\![\mathcal{R}^{\prime}]\!]}
is a proper QFL. Let
\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
denote the set of all valid weighted finance regular expressions over
\Sigma
and
\mathbb{F}
. In the rest of this section we consider only valid expressions, so that the semantics is well defined.
For any collection of weighted finance regular expressions
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
, let
{[\![\mathcal{W}]\!]}=\{{[\![\mathcal{R}]\!]}\mid\mathcal{R}\in\mathcal{W}\}
.

Remark 4.5
.

In contrast to the classical theory of weighted regular expressions (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
),
the Hadamard product is not included as a primitive syntactic operation in the grammar above, since it can be expressed using the available constructs.
Nevertheless, it is convenient to use the Hadamard product at the semantic level,
as it provides a concise way to express pointwise combinations of quantitative values.

Example 4.6
.

If
E=S
, then
\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
corresponds to the standard semiring-weighted regular expressions of Schützenberger
[
29
]
(cf. also
[
7
,
11
]
).

Example 4.7
.

Consider the bond valuation model from Example
3.3
.
We recall the alphabet

\Sigma=\{\texttt{cpn},\texttt{fin},\texttt{dfl}\},

where
cpn
,
fin
, and
dfl
denote coupon, final, and default events, respectively.
We also recall the finance semiring
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
.
The bond cash-flow structure can be described by the weighted finance regular expression

\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{bond}}=[-P_{0}]_{\varepsilon}\cdot(\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle_{\texttt{cpn}})^{*}\cdot\big(\langle\!\langle C\otimes F\rangle\!\rangle_{\texttt{fin}}\oplus[0.0]_{\texttt{dfl}}\big).

For a finance word
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
of the form (
4
),
we obtain
{[\![\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{bond}}]\!]}(w)=\pi_{\mathrm{bond}}
.

Example 4.8
.

In this example, we construct a regular expression modeling the profit of a long position
in the European call option from Example
3.7
.
Let
\Sigma=\{\bot\}
and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
.
Let
e^{\mathrm{long}}_{\mathrm{eur}}\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
be defined as in Equation (
5
).
The corresponding regular expression

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{long}}_{\mathrm{eur}}=[e^{\mathrm{long}}_{\mathrm{eur}}]_{\bot}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}

directly encodes the payoff function.
An equivalent expression using only primitive
\mathbb{F}
-expressions can be constructed as

\tilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\mathrm{long}}_{\mathrm{eur}}=[-c_{0}]_{\varepsilon}\cdot\bigl(({\langle\!\langle 1.0\rangle\!\rangle}_{\bot}\cdot[-K]_{\varepsilon})\oplus[0.0]_{\bot}\bigr),

where
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\mathrm{long}}_{\mathrm{eur}}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})

For all

Example 4.9
.

Consider the American call option from Example
3.9
.
We construct a weighted finance regular expression over

\Sigma=\{\bot\}
and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
describing the payoff of a long position.
The expression is given by

\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{amer}}=[-c_{0}]_{\varepsilon}\cdot\bigl([0.0]_{\bot})^{*}\cdot\bigl([0.0]_{\varepsilon}\oplus(\langle\!\langle 1.0\rangle\!\rangle_{\bot}\cdot([0.0]_{\bot})^{*})\bigr)\bigr).

Intuitively, the factor
([0.0]_{\bot})^{*}
models waiting, allowing the option holder
to postpone the exercise arbitrarily. The choice

[0.0]_{\varepsilon}\oplus(\langle\!\langle 1.0\rangle\!\rangle_{\bot}\cdot([0.0]_{\bot})^{*})

captures the decision either not to exercise the option or to exercise it at a given time step.
The term
[-c_{0}]_{\varepsilon}
accounts for the initial premium.
For all input words
w=(\bot,S_{1})\dots(\bot,S_{T})\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
,

\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{amer}}
yields the payoff of the American call option.

These examples demonstrate that weighted finance regular expressions
provide a compositional framework for modeling financial payoffs.

Our first main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 4.10

(Kleene–Schützenberger Theorem for QFLs)
.

For every QFL
{\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S}
, the following are equivalent:

(a)

\mathcal{F}
is definable by a weighted finance regular expression, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}]\!]}
;

(b)

\mathcal{F}
is recognizable by a WFFA, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}]\!]}
.

Moreover, the equivalence is effective: from a weighted finance regular expression one can effectively construct a WFFA defining the
same QFL, and conversely, from a WFFA one can effectively construct a weighted finance regular expression defining the same QFL.

Remark 4.11
.

The proof of Theorem
4.10
is deferred to Section
7
, where we present a stronger version of the theorem. This stronger result provides additional technical insights into the constructions, and Theorem
4.10
can be seen as a special case of that more general statement.

Remark 4.12

(Relation to classical Kleene–Schützenberger results)
.

The construction in Theorem
4.10
follows the classical proof scheme of the Kleene–Schützenberger theorem for weighted automata. One may ask whether this result can be derived directly from classical equivalence results between weighted automata and rational expressions over semirings.

Such a reduction can be formulated by encoding WFFAs as weighted automata over suitable semirings of functions. For instance, transition weights may be viewed as local weight functions

f:D\times\Sigma\to S,

describing the contribution of a transition to the weight of a valued symbol. Runs of the automaton then correspond to finite sequences of such functions, whose evaluation along a finance word induces a quantitative finance language

\mathcal{F}:(D\times\Sigma)^{*}\to S.

More generally, one may consider semirings of multisets of finite sequences of such local weight functions. This yields a multiset-based semantics in the spirit of unifying frameworks for weighted automata and weighted logics (see, e.g.,
[
16
,
9
]
). In this way, classical equivalence results between weighted automata and rational expressions over suitable semirings (see, e.g.,
[
7
, Theorem 2.12]
) can be related to the present setting.

However, such encodings lead to rather technically involved semirings whose elements no longer admit a natural
financial interpretation. For this reason, we prefer to give direct constructions tailored to WFFAs.
These constructions are based on standard automata-theoretic techniques, such as product and state-elimination constructions, but are carried out directly within the WFFA model.
Theorem
4.10

therefore provides the conceptual backbone of our framework and serves as a basis for the later study of
large but syntactically restricted classes of finance expressions.

5
Closure Properties of WFFAs

In this section, we study the closure properties of QFLs captured by WFFAs.
These results will be used in Section
7

to prove our Kleene–Schützenberger theorem.
They are also of independent interest, as they provide a compositional view
of financial systems in terms of simpler building blocks.

Thanks to our definition of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions, the automata constructions
establishing these closure properties closely resemble those for standard
semiring-weighted automata.
We present these constructions in detail, as they will also be used in
Section
6
to analyze WFFAs with restricted expressions.

Let
\Sigma
be an alphabet and
\mathbb{F}=(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{D},\mathbb{b})
a finance semiring where
{\mathbb{S}=(S,\oplus,\otimes,\mathbb{0},\mathbb{1})}
is a commutative semiring.

The following theorem establishes that WFFAs share the same closure properties as weighted automata over commutative semirings (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
) with respect to the operations introduced in Definition
4.1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}]\!]}
such that
\mathcal{F}
is proper. Then, the QFLs
\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}
,
\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{2}
,
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{2}
and
\mathcal{F}^{*}
are also in
{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}]\!]}
.

Example 5.2
.

A
bull spread

[
25
]
is constructed by taking a long position in a call option
with strike
K^{\mathrm{low}}
and a short position in a call option
with strike
K^{\mathrm{high}}>K^{\mathrm{low}}
.
Let
\pi_{\mathrm{low}}^{\mathrm{long}}
and
\pi_{\mathrm{high}}^{\mathrm{short}}
denote the corresponding payoffs,
and let
\mathcal{F}(\pi)
be their encoding as QFLs over
\Sigma=\{\bot\}
and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
.
Then the payoff of the bull spread satisfies

\mathcal{F}(\pi_{\mathrm{bull}})=\mathcal{F}(\pi_{\mathrm{low}}^{\mathrm{long}})\otimes\mathcal{F}(\pi_{\mathrm{high}}^{\mathrm{short}}).

Since both components are recognizable by WFFAs (Example
3.7
),
Theorem
5.1
implies that the bull spread is also recognizable.
This illustrates the compositional modeling of financial strategies via closure properties.

In the remainder of this subsection, we present a proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
. Then there exists
\mathcal{A}_{\oplus}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
such that

{[\![\mathcal{A}_{\oplus}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}_{1}]\!]}\oplus{[\![\mathcal{A}_{2}]\!]}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
be WFFAs with state sets
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
, respectively.
We use the standard disjoint union construction.
Assume
Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}=\emptyset
and define
\mathcal{A}_{\oplus}
by taking the union of the components of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
.
Then every run of
\mathcal{A}_{\oplus}
is a run of either
\mathcal{A}_{1}
or
\mathcal{A}_{2}
, with weights preserved.
Hence, for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
,

{[\![\mathcal{A}_{\oplus}]\!]}(w)={[\![\mathcal{A}_{1}]\!]}(w)\oplus{[\![\mathcal{A}_{2}]\!]}(w).

∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
. Then there exists

\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
such that

{[\![\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}_{1}]\!]}\otimes{[\![\mathcal{A}_{2}]\!]}
.

Proof.

We use the standard synchronous product construction for weighted automata
over commutative semirings (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
). Let

\mathcal{A}_{i}=(Q_{i},I_{i},T_{i},F_{i},\mathrm{wt}_{I_{i}},\mathrm{wt}_{T_{i}},\mathrm{wt}_{F_{i}})\;(i\in\{1,2\}).

We define

Q=Q_{1}\times Q_{2},

I=I_{1}\times I_{2},

F=F_{1}\times F_{2}

and let
T
consist of all synchronized transitions

{t=((q_{1},q_{2}),a,(q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime}))}

such that
(q_{1},a,q_{1}^{\prime})\in T_{1}
and
{(q_{2},a,q_{2}^{\prime})\in T_{2}}
.
The initial and final weights are defined componentwise by

{\mathrm{wt}_{I}(i_{1},i_{2})=\mathrm{wt}_{I_{1}}(i_{1})\otimes\mathrm{wt}_{I_{2}}(i_{2})},

for
i_{1}\in I_{1}
and
{i_{2}\in I_{2}}
, and

\mathrm{wt}_{F}(f_{1},f_{2})=\mathrm{wt}_{F_{1}}(f_{1})\otimes\mathrm{wt}_{F_{2}}(f_{2}),

for
{f_{1}\in F_{1}}
,
f_{2}\in F_{2}
.
The weight of a synchronized transition

{t=((q_{1},q_{2}),a,(q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime}))\in T}
is

{\mathrm{wt}_{T}(t)=\mathrm{wt}_{T_{1}}(q_{1},a,q_{1}^{\prime})\otimes\mathrm{wt}_{T_{2}}(q_{2},a,q_{2}^{\prime})},

where
\otimes
denotes the syntactic combination of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions.
This is well defined since
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
is closed under
\otimes
.
Then runs of
\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}
are in one-to-one correspondence with pairs of runs
of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
on the same word, and their weights evaluate to the product
of the corresponding run weights. Hence, for every
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
,

{[\![\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}]\!]}(w)={[\![\mathcal{A}_{1}]\!]}(w)\otimes{[\![\mathcal{A}_{2}]\!]}(w).

∎

Definition 5.5
.

A WFFA
\mathcal{A}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})
is called:

(a)

initially normalized if
I=\{q_{I}\}
,
\mathrm{wt}_{I}(q_{I})=\mathbb{1}
, and no transition enters
q_{I}
;

(b)

finally normalized if
F=\{q_{F}\}
,
\mathrm{wt}_{F}(q_{F})=\mathbb{1}
, and no transition leaves
q_{F}
;

(c)

completely normalized if it is both initially and finally normalized.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
. Then:

(a)

there exists an initially normalized
\mathcal{A}_{I}
with
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{I}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
;

(b)

there exists a finally normalized
\mathcal{A}_{F}
with
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{F}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
;

(c)

if
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
is proper, there exists a completely normalized
\mathcal{A}_{I,F}
with
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{I,F}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
.

Proof.

We use standard normalization constructions (cf., e.g.,
[
7
]
).
The only non-trivial aspect is the redistribution of initial and final weights to transitions.
In our setting, this is achieved by lifting weights to
\mathbb{F}
-expressions: an initial weight
s
can be incorporated into outgoing transitions as
s\otimes w
, and dually for final weights.
Moreover, closure of
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
under
\oplus
allows us to combine contributions from multiple initial (or final) states.
For initial normalization, introduce a fresh state
q_{I}
and redirect all transitions from initial states. If necessary, adjust the final weight of
q_{I}
to preserve the value on
\varepsilon
. Final normalization is dual.
For complete normalization, assuming that the automaton is proper, introduce fresh states
q_{I},q_{F}
and combine the two constructions.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

(a)

Let
\mathcal{A}_{F}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
be finally normalized and
\mathcal{A}_{I}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
be initially normalized.
Then there exists
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Cauchy}}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
such that

{[\![\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Cauchy}}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}_{F}]\!]}\cdot{[\![\mathcal{A}_{I}]\!]}
.

(b)

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
be completely normalized.
Then there exists
\mathcal{A}^{*}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
with
{[\![\mathcal{A}^{*}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}^{*}
.

Proof.

We use standard constructions for weighted automata (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
), adapted to
\mathbb{F}
-expressions.

(a)

Identify the final state of
\mathcal{A}_{F}
with the initial state of
\mathcal{A}_{I}
and take the union of the remaining components.
Then every run of the resulting automaton is obtained by concatenating a run of
\mathcal{A}_{F}
with a run of
\mathcal{A}_{I}
.
The weight of such a run is the product of the corresponding run weights, since the connecting state carries no additional weight.
Hence, the behavior of the constructed automaton coincides with the Cauchy product.

(b)

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is completely normalized, i.e., it has a unique initial state and a unique final state.
Identify the final state with the initial state, and redirect all transitions entering the final state to the initial state, thereby creating loops.
Then every run corresponds to a finite concatenation of runs of
\mathcal{A}
.
The weight of such a run is the product of the weights of the corresponding subruns.
Hence, the behavior of the constructed automaton coincides with the Kleene star of
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
. ∎

As a corollary from Lemmas
5.6
and
5.7
, we obtain:

Corollary 5.8
.

(a)

For all
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
, there exists
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
such that

{[\![\mathcal{A}^{\prime}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}_{1}]\!]}\cdot{[\![\mathcal{A}_{2}]\!]}
.

(b)

For all
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
such that
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
is proper, there exists
\mathcal{A}^{*}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
such that

{[\![\mathcal{A}^{*}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}^{*}
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

The theorem follows by combining Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
with Corollary
5.8
.
∎

Recall that
\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\tau}
denotes the class of purely transition-weighted WFFAs. As an immediate corollary of Lemma
5.6
, we obtain:

Corollary 5.9
.

{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}]\!]}={[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\tau}]\!]}
.

Intuitively, this means that any WFFA can be transformed into an equivalent purely transition-weighted form without changing its behavior.

Remark 5.10
.

While the closure constructions for WFFAs follow the same principles as for weighted automata over semirings, they may lead to a significant increase in the size of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions.
In particular, normalization (Lemma
5.6
) can yield expressions of size
\mathcal{O}((|\mathcal{A}|_{Q})^{2}\cdot|\mathcal{A}|_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}})
, which may be costly to evaluate.

6
Restricted WFFAs

As discussed in Remark
5.10
,
allowing unrestricted
\mathbb{F}
-expressions in WFFAs can lead to a significant increase in the size and evaluation cost of transition-weight expressions.
This becomes particularly problematic when the automaton’s behavior must be evaluated repeatedly or when extremal values are computed.

These observations motivate the study of more disciplined subclasses of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions that control this growth.
In this section, we identify several such subclasses that remain computationally tractable while still preserving useful closure properties of WFFAs.

Throughout all of this section, let
\Sigma
be an alphabet and
\mathbb{F}=(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{D},\mathbb{b})
a finance semiring such that
\mathbb{S}=(S,\oplus,\otimes,\mathbb{0},\mathbb{1})
is a commutative semiring. Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
be an arbitrary collection of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions. We call an automaton

{\mathcal{A}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}}

E
-weighted
if
\mathrm{wt}_{T}(T)\subseteq E
, i.e., all transition weights are drawn from the restricted class
E
.
Intuitively, this means that the quantitative behavior of the automaton is built using only a prescribed set of simple or “admissible” expressions, typically chosen to control the size and computational complexity of the resulting automata.

Let
\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)\subseteq\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
denote the collection of all
E
-weighted WFFAs over
\Sigma
and
\mathbb{F}
.
Let
\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\tau}(E)=\mathrm{WFFA}^{\tau}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}\cap\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
denote the collection of all
E
-weighted WFFAs over
\Sigma
and
\mathbb{F}
, which are purely transition-weighted.

6.1
Closure Properties of Restricted WFFAs

In this subsection, we generalize Theorem
5.1
to
E
-weighted WFFA.

We say that
E
is
\otimes
-closed
if, for every
e_{1},e_{2}\in E
,
{[\![e_{1}\otimes e_{2}]\!]}\in{[\![E]\!]}
. We say that
E
is
(S,\otimes)
-closed
if, for every
s\in S
and
e\in E
,
{[\![s\otimes e]\!]}\in{[\![E]\!]}
.

Example 6.1
.

(a)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
S
are
\otimes
- and
(S,\otimes)
-closed.

(b)

If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
, then
{\langle\!\langle S\rangle\!\rangle}_{\mathbb{F}}
is
\otimes
-closed but not
(S,\otimes)
-closed. This is due to the fact that expressions in
{\langle\!\langle\mathbb{S}\rangle\!\rangle}_{\mathbb{F}}
can define only functions
d\mapsto s\cdot d
for some constant
s\in S
. On the other side, expressions of the form
s\otimes{\langle\!\langle s^{\prime}\rangle\!\rangle}
define functions of the form
d\mapsto s\cdot d+s^{\prime}
.

(c)

For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
, the collection of primitive expressions
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
is neither
\otimes
- nor
(S,\otimes)
-closed. The reason for this is that primitive expressions define either constant functions
d\mapsto s
or functions of the form
d\mapsto s\cdot d
whereas expressions
[\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}]
(with
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
) and
[s\otimes\pi]
(with
s\in S
and
\pi\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
) can define, e.g., the function
d\mapsto d+1
.

(d)

Recall that
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}
denotes the collection of affine
\mathbb{F}
-expressions. If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
, then
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}
is
\otimes
- and
(S,\otimes)
-closed.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
. Then, the following hold.

(a)

(\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2})\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
.

(b)

If
E
is
\otimes
-closed, then
(\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{2})\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
.

(c)

(\mathcal{F}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{2})\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
.

(d)

If
E
is
(S,\otimes)
-closed and
\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
is proper, then its Kleene star
\mathcal{F}^{*}
belongs to
{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
.

Remark 6.3
.

Note that the constructions for
\oplus
and the Cauchy product
\cdot
do not require any closure assumptions on
E
, in contrast to
\otimes
and the star operation.

In the rest of this subsection, we provide a proof of Theorem
6.2
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
be any collection of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions and
{\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}}
. Then their Cauchy product
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{2}
belongs to
{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
.

The proof of this theorem follows from Lemmas
5.7
and
6.5
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
be any collection of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions and
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
.

(a)

There exists an initially normalized
\mathcal{A}_{I}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
such that
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{I}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
.

(b)

There exists a finally normalized
\mathcal{A}_{F}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
such that
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{F}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})
.

(a)

In contrast to Lemma
5.6
, we cannot absorb initial weights into transitions, as
E
is not assumed to be
\oplus
- or
(S,\otimes)
-closed.
Instead, we record the initial state along each run and defer the contribution of
\mathrm{wt}_{I}
to the final step.
Formally, we introduce a fresh state
q_{I}
and extend states
q\in Q
to pairs
(i,q)
with
i\in I
, simulating each run of
\mathcal{A}
while remembering its origin.
Transitions are lifted componentwise, and final weights are defined by

\mathrm{wt}_{F}(i,f)=\mathrm{wt}_{I}(i)\otimes\mathrm{wt}_{F}(f).

for
i\in I
and
f\in F
.
If necessary, the value on
\varepsilon
is handled by making
q_{I}
final.
Then each run of
\mathcal{A}_{I}
corresponds to a run of
\mathcal{A}
with the same accumulated weight, using commutativity of
\mathbb{S}
, hence
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{I}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
.

(b)

Final normalization is dual. ∎

Remark 6.6
.

Given an arbitrary collection
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
, using the normalization constructions of Lemma
6.5
we can construct an
E
-weighted WFFA for
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{1}]\!]}\cdot{[\![\mathcal{A}_{2}]\!]}
with
\mathcal{O}((|\mathcal{A}_{1}|_{Q})^{2}+(|\mathcal{A}_{2}|_{Q})^{2})
states.

For any
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
, let the
\oplus
-closure
of
E
be defined as

\langle E\rangle_{\oplus}=\Bigl\{[e_{1}\oplus{\dots}\oplus e_{k}]\mid k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\text{ and }e_{i}\in E\text{ for all }i\in[k]\Bigr\}\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}.

Here,
[e_{1}\oplus{\dots}\oplus e_{k}]
denotes an expression obtained by choosing some valid way of parenthesizing the summands
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
. While different parenthesizations yield syntactically distinct expressions, their evaluation in the semiring is independent of the grouping, due to the associativity of
\oplus
.

The following lemma shows that closing
E
under
\oplus
does not increase the expressive power of WFFAs.
The key idea is that sums of expressions can be simulated by nondeterministic branching at the automaton level, as formalized in the proof below.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
be any collection of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions. Then

{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\langle E\rangle_{\oplus})]\!]}={[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\langle E\rangle_{\oplus}}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\langle E\rangle_{\oplus})
.
We construct
{\mathcal{A}_{E}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)}
with the same behavior.
For each transition
t=(p,a,q)\in T
with weight

\mathrm{wt}_{T}(t)=[e_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus e_{k}],\qquad e_{i}\in E,

we replace
t
by
k
transitions from copies of
p
to copies of
q
, all labeled by
a
, whose weights are the summands
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
.
Accordingly, the automaton
\mathcal{A}_{E}
is obtained by expanding the state space, replacing each state
q
by copies
(q,i)
, where
i
is an index indicating which summand in the
\oplus
-decomposition of a transition weight has been selected.
Since WFFA semantics aggregates runs using
\oplus
, we obtain

{[\![\mathcal{A}_{E}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}_{\langle E\rangle_{\oplus}}]\!]}.

The converse inclusion is immediate, since
E\subseteq\langle E\rangle_{\oplus}
.
∎

Lemma 6.8
.

(a)

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
be
(S,\otimes)
-closed and let
\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
be proper. Then
\mathcal{F}^{*}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
.

(b)

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
be arbitrary and let
\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}^{\tau}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
be proper. Then
{\mathcal{F}^{*}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}^{\tau}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}}
.

Proof.

(a)

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
with
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}=\mathcal{F}
.
Using the Kleene-star construction from Lemma
5.7
,
we obtain
\mathcal{A}^{*}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}})
with
{[\![\mathcal{A}^{*}]\!]}=\mathcal{F}^{*}
.

The transition weights of
\mathcal{A}^{*}
are built from expressions in
E
by combining them with constants from
S
via
\otimes
and
\oplus
.
Since
E
is
(S,\otimes)
-closed and
\otimes
is commutative, every such expression is equivalent to an element of
\langle E\rangle_{\oplus}
.
Hence, by replacing transition weights with equivalent expressions, we obtain
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\langle E\rangle_{\oplus}}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\langle E\rangle_{\oplus})
with the same behavior.
Finally, Lemma
6.7
yields an equivalent automaton
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{E}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
, so
\mathcal{F}^{*}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
.

(b)

The argument is analogous. In the purely transition-weighted case, the construction only introduces expressions of the form

e
,
\mathbb{1}\otimes e
,
e\otimes\mathbb{1}
, or
\mathbb{1}\otimes e\otimes\mathbb{1}
with
e\in E
, all of which are equivalent to
e
.
Thus all transition weights lie in
\langle E\rangle_{\oplus}
, and applying Lemma
6.7
yields the result. ∎

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

(a)

By Lemma
5.3
, the disjoint union construction preserves
E
-weightedness.

(b)

By Lemma
5.4
, transition weights have the form
[e_{1}\otimes e_{2}]
with

.
Since

is

-closed, these can be replaced by equivalent expressions in

.

Follows from Lemma
6.4
.

(d)

Follows from Lemma

As a corollary from the proof of Theorem

Corollary 6.9
.

Let

be an arbitrary collection of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions. Then, for all QFLs

6.2
Primitive Expressions

In Section
3
we saw that WFFAs with primitive expressions are capable of describing a wide range of practically relevant scenarios.
However, as already illustrated by simple constructions, composing WFFAs (e.g., via sequential or Hadamard composition) quickly leads to increasingly complex
\mathbb{F}
-expressions.
Such expressions tend to grow rapidly in size, making their analysis and interpretation cumbersome.
This raises the question of whether one can remain within a restricted class of simple expressions while still preserving useful closure properties.

In this subsection, we investigate this question and study the closure properties of WFFAs with primitive expressions.
In particular, we demonstrate that the WFFA constructions for the Cauchy product and the Kleene star can be optimized in this restricted setting.

In Example
6.1

(c)
, we showed that, in general, primitive expressions are neither
\otimes
-closed nor
(S,\otimes)
-closed.
We further show that
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
-weighted WFFAs do not, in general, preserve closure under the Hadamard product and the Kleene star.
This justifies the conditions placed on
E
in Theorem
6.2
(b) and (d).

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
\Sigma=\{\bot\}
and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
. Then, the following hold:

(a)

There exist
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
such that
(\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{2})\notin{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
.

(b)

There exists
\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
such that
\mathcal{F}
is proper and
{\mathcal{F}^{*}\notin{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}}
.

For the proof of Theorem
6.10
, we require Lemmas
6.11
and
6.12
.

For
\delta=(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we define the hypercube

{\mathcal{C}(\delta,R)=\{(d_{1}^{\prime},\dots,d_{n}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}\mid d_{i}\leq d_{i}^{\prime}\leq d_{i}+R\text{ for all }i\in[n]\}}.

For
\delta=d_{1}\dots d_{n}\in\mathbb{D}^{*}
, let
\bot_{\delta}
denote the finance data word

(\bot,d_{1})\dots(\bot,d_{n}).

Let
\mathrm{Run}_{\mathcal{A}}^{n}\subseteq\mathrm{Run}_{\mathcal{A}}
be the set of all runs of
\mathcal{A}
of length
n
, which is a finite set.

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
\Sigma=\{\bot\}
, let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
, and let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})
be such that
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)\neq\mathbb{0}
for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
.
Then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
there exist
\varrho\in\mathrm{Run}_{\mathcal{A}}^{n}
,
\delta\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, and
R>0
such that

{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(\bot_{\delta^{\prime}})=\mathrm{wt}_{\mathcal{A}}(\varrho,\delta^{\prime})

for all
\delta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(\delta,R)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})
and fix
n\geq 1
.
Enumerate
\mathrm{Run}_{\mathcal{A}}^{n}=(\varrho_{j})_{j\in[k]}
.
For each run
\varrho_{j}
, the weight
\mathrm{wt}_{\mathcal{A}}(\varrho_{j},\delta)
is an affine function of
\delta\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
.
Hence,

{{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(\bot_{\delta})=\max_{j\in[k]}\mathrm{wt}_{\mathcal{A}}(\varrho_{j},\delta)}

is the pointwise maximum of finitely many affine functions.
For each
j\in[k]
, let

\Delta_{j}=\{\delta\in\mathbb{D}^{n}\mid\mathrm{wt}_{\mathcal{A}}(\varrho_{j},\delta)\geq\mathrm{wt}_{\mathcal{A}}(\varrho_{j^{\prime}},\delta)\text{ for all }j^{\prime}\in[k]\}.

Then each
\Delta_{j}
is a polyhedron defined by linear inequalities, and by assumption
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(\bot_{\delta})\neq\mathbb{0}
for all
\delta
, we have

\mathbb{D}^{n}=\bigcup_{j\in[k]}\Delta_{j}.

Since this is a finite covering of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
by polyhedra, at least one
\Delta_{j}
contains a cube
\mathcal{C}(\delta,R)
for some
\delta\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
R>0
.
For this
j
, we have

{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(\bot_{\delta^{\prime}})=\mathrm{wt}_{\mathcal{A}}(\varrho_{j},\delta^{\prime})

for all
\delta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(\delta,R)
.
∎

Recall that
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}
denotes the collection of affine
\mathbb{F}
-expressions (cf. Section
2
).

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
\Sigma=\{\bot\}
and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
. Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S
be the QFL defined by

\mathcal{F}(\bot_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}})=n+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
.
Then,
\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}\setminus{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
.

Proof.

The QFL
\mathcal{F}
is recognized by the one-state WFFA with a single loop transition

{t=(q,\bot,q)}
with weight

\mathrm{wt}_{T}(t)=[{\langle\!\langle 1.0\rangle\!\rangle}\otimes 1.0],

where both the initial and final weights of
q
are
0.0
.
Thus each input symbol contributes
d_{i}+1
, and therefore

{{[\![\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{F}}]\!]}(\bot_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}})=n+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}}.

Suppose, for contradiction, that
\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
, and let
\mathcal{A}
be such an automaton.
By Lemma
6.11
, for every
n
there exist a run
\varrho\in\mathrm{Run}_{\mathcal{A}}^{n}
, a vector
\delta\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, and
R>0
such that

{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(\bot_{\delta^{\prime}})=\mathrm{wt}_{\mathcal{A}}(\varrho,\delta^{\prime})

for all
\delta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(\delta,R)
.
Since
\mathcal{A}
has primitive weights,
\mathrm{wt}_{\mathcal{A}}(\varrho,\delta^{\prime})
is an affine function

c+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}d_{i}^{\prime}

with
\alpha_{i}\in\{0,s_{i}\}
depending on the transition type.
On the other hand,

{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(\bot_{\delta^{\prime}})=n+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}^{\prime}

on
\mathcal{C}(\delta,R)
.
Hence these affine functions coincide, implying
\alpha_{i}=1
for all
i
and
c=n
.
Thus all transitions along
\varrho
must be
{\langle\!\langle 1.0\rangle\!\rangle}
, and the constant term
c
equals the sum of initial and final weights, which is bounded independently of
n
.
This contradicts
c=n
for arbitrarily large
n
. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}\notin{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.10
.

(a)

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S
be defined as follows.
For
{w=(\bot,d_{1})\dots(\bot,d_{n})\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}}
, let

\mathcal{F}_{1}(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}

and

\mathcal{F}_{2}(w)=n,

and let
{\mathcal{F}_{1}(\varepsilon)=\mathcal{F}_{2}(\varepsilon)=0.0}
.
Both QFLs are recognized by one-state WFFAs:
\mathcal{F}_{1}
via a loop with weight
\langle\!\langle 1.0\rangle\!\rangle
, and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
via a loop with constant weight
1.0
.
Then, for
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{2}
, we have

\mathcal{F}(\bot_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}})=n+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}.

By Lemma
6.12
,
\mathcal{F}\notin{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
.

(b)

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S
be defined by

\mathcal{F}((\bot,d))=d+1

and

\mathcal{F}(w)=\mathbb{0}

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
with
|w|\neq 1
.
Then
\mathcal{F}
is proper and is recognized by a WFFA with a single transition
(q_{0},\bot,q_{1})
of primitive weight
{\langle\!\langle 1.0\rangle\!\rangle}
, initial weight
1.0
at
q_{0}
, and final weight
0.0
at
q_{1}
.
For its Kleene star, we obtain

\mathcal{F}^{*}(\bot_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}})=n+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}.

Again, by Lemma
6.12
,
\mathcal{F}^{*}\notin{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
.
∎

Despite the negative results of Theorem
6.10
,
Corollary
6.9
shows that
purely transition-weighted WFFAs with primitive expressions remain closed under the Kleene star.
Moreover, as we illustrate below, this restricted class admits more efficient constructions for both the Cauchy product and the Kleene star.

To formalize these improvements, we introduce two structural properties.
We say that
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
satisfies the
bounded-sum property

if there exists a constant
B\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}

and all
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\in E
, there exist
b\leq B
and
e_{1}^{\prime},\dots,e_{b}^{\prime}\in E
satisfying

{[\![e_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus e_{k}]\!]}={[\![e_{1}^{\prime}\oplus\dots\oplus e_{b}^{\prime}]\!]}.

Let
B(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})
denote the minimal such constant.
We say that a data-binding function
\mathbb{b}:\mathbb{D}\times S\to S
is
additive
if

{\mathbb{b}(d,s\oplus s^{\prime})=\mathbb{b}(d,s)\oplus\mathbb{b}(d,s^{\prime})}

for all
d\in\mathbb{D}
and
s,s^{\prime}\in S
.

Lemma 6.13
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finance semiring with an additive data-binding function. Then,
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
satisfies the bounded sum property and
B(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})\leq 2
.

Proof.

Let
e=[\pi_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\pi_{k}]
with
\pi_{i}\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
.
Since
\oplus
is commutative, we may group all constant and all binding terms and write

e=[s^{\prime}\oplus{\langle\!\langle s^{\prime\prime}\rangle\!\rangle}]

for some
s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\in S
, where one of the terms may be absent.
By additivity of
\mathbb{b}
, we have

{[\![e]\!]}={[\![s^{\prime}\oplus{\langle\!\langle s^{\prime\prime}\rangle\!\rangle}]\!]}.

Thus every sum of elements of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
is equivalent to a sum of at most two elements of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
, and hence
B(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})\leq 2
.
∎

As an illustration, we can demonstrate that the bounded-sum property holds not only for finance semirings with an additive data-binding function. For example, consider
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
as defined in Example
2.6
(b). Clearly, the data-binding function of
\mathbb{F}
is not additive. For instance,

\mathbb{b}_{\times}^{\mathbb{R}}(-1,1\oplus 2)\neq\mathbb{b}_{\times}^{\mathbb{R}}(-1,1)\oplus\mathbb{b}_{\times}^{\mathbb{R}}(-1,2)
.

Lemma 6.14
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}^{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
satisfies the bounded-sum property with
B(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})\leq 5
.

Proof sketch.

Let
e=[\pi_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\pi_{k}]
with
\pi_{i}\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
.
By separating constant and binding terms, and further splitting binding terms according to the sign of their coefficients,
one can reduce
e
to an equivalent sum of at most five primitive expressions.
The key observation is that, although the data-binding function is not additive,
it is still possible to control the contribution of positive and negative coefficients separately.
A detailed case analysis yields the bound
5
.
∎

Lemma 6.15
.

Assume that
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
satisfies the bounded-sum property.

(a)

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\in\mathrm{WFFA}^{\tau}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})
. Then, there exists
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\tau}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})
such that
{{[\![\mathcal{A}^{\prime}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}_{1}]\!]}\cdot{[\![\mathcal{A}_{2}]\!]}}
, and
|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}|_{Q}=\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{A}_{1}|_{Q}+|\mathcal{A}_{2}|_{Q})
.

(b)

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}^{\tau}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})
such that
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
is proper. Then, there exists
{\mathcal{A}^{*}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\tau}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})}
such that
{[\![\mathcal{A}^{*}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}^{*}
and
|\mathcal{A}^{*}|_{Q}=\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{A}|_{Q})
.

Proof.

(a)

Using the standard construction for the Cauchy product
(Corollary
5.8
),
we obtain an automaton
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
with

|\hat{\mathcal{A}}|_{Q}=|\mathcal{A}_{1}|_{Q}+|\mathcal{A}_{2}|_{Q}+\mathcal{O}(1)
and

{[\![\hat{\mathcal{A}}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}_{1}]\!]}\cdot{[\![\mathcal{A}_{2}]\!]}
.

Since
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
are purely transition-weighted,
all transition weights of
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
lie in
\langle\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}\rangle_{\oplus}
.
By the bounded-sum property, each such weight can be replaced by an equivalent expression of bounded size.
Applying Lemma
6.7
then yields

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{WFFA}^{\tau}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})
with the same behavior.
Since
B(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})
is constant, the number of states remains

\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{A}_{1}|_{Q}+|\mathcal{A}_{2}|_{Q})
.

(b)

The proof is analogous, using the Kleene-star construction from
Corollary
5.8
.
∎

.

6.3
Affine Expressions

Recall that an
\mathbb{F}
-expression
e\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
is
affine
if either
e\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
or
e=[{\langle\!\langle s\rangle\!\rangle}\otimes s^{\prime}]
for some
s,s^{\prime}\in S
.
Let
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}
denote the set of all affine
\mathbb{F}
-expressions. Clearly,

\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}.

We say that
\mathbb{F}
is
multiplicative
if

\mathbb{b}(d,s\otimes s^{\prime})=\mathbb{b}(d,s)\otimes\mathbb{b}(d,s^{\prime})

for all
d\in\mathbb{D}
and
s,s^{\prime}\in S
. For instance,
\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
is multiplicative. It is immediate that
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}
is
(S,\otimes)
-closed, and if
\mathbb{F}
is multiplicative, then
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}
is also
\otimes
-closed.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
6.2
, we obtain the following closure properties of affine
\mathbb{F}
-expressions
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}
.

Corollary 6.17
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
such that
\mathcal{F}
is proper. Then:

(a)

The QFLs
\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{2}
,
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{2}
and
\mathcal{F}^{*}
are also in
{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
.

(b)

If
\mathbb{F}
is multiplicative, then the QFL
\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{2}
is also in
{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
.

Lemma 6.18
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
and
\Sigma=\{\bot\}
. Then,

{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}\subsetneq{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}\subsetneq{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}.

Proof.

The inclusions

{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}\subseteq{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}\subseteq{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}

are immediate.
To show strictness, we argue as follows.
First, define
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S
on words of the form
\bot_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}
, where
n\geq 0
and
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}\in D
, by

\mathcal{F}(\bot_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}})=n+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}.

By Lemma
6.12
,
\mathcal{F}\notin{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
,
while
\mathcal{F}
is recognized by a WFFA with affine expressions. Hence the first inclusion is strict.
Second, define
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S
such that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\bot_{0.5})=1
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(w)=\mathbb{0}
for all other words
w
.
Then
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
.
Suppose, for contradiction, that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
.
Then there exists a WFFA
\mathcal{A}
over
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}
such that

{{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(\bot_{d})=\max_{i\in[k]}(a_{i}d+b_{i})}

for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.
However, a function of this form cannot be equal to
\mathbb{0}
at all points except at a single input.
This contradicts the definition of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Hence
{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\notin{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}}
,
and the second inclusion is strict.
∎

Lemma 6.19
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
. Then
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}
does not satisfy the bounded-sum property.

Proof.

For
i\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, let
\alpha_{i}=[{\langle\!\langle 2i\rangle\!\rangle}\otimes(-i^{2})]
. Then

{[\![\alpha_{i}]\!]}(x)=2ix-i^{2}.

For every
N\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, the function

{[\![\alpha_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\alpha_{N}]\!]}

is the maximum of
N
distinct affine functions, each dominating on a non-empty interval.
Hence it cannot be represented as a maximum of fewer than
N
affine expressions.
Therefore no uniform bound exists.
∎

The failure of the bounded-sum property has concrete consequences for the size of automata constructions.

Lemma 6.20
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
and
\Sigma=\{\bot\}
.
Then there exists a family
(\mathcal{F}_{N})_{N\geq 1}
such that:

(a)

\mathcal{F}_{N}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}^{\tau}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
is recognized by an automaton with
\mathcal{O}(N)
states;

(b)

every
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}^{\tau}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}})
with
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}=\mathcal{F}_{N}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{N}
satisfies

|\mathcal{A}|_{Q}\geq 3\cdot N^{\frac{4}{3}}.

Proof sketch.

For
i\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, define the function
\varphi_{i}:x\mapsto 2ix-i^{2}
, and for
N\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, define
f_{N}:x\mapsto\max_{i\in[N^{2}]}\varphi_{i}(x)
.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{N}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S
be given by
\mathcal{F}_{N}(\bot_{d})=f_{N}(d)
and
\mathcal{F}_{N}(w)=\mathbb{0}
for
|w|\neq 1
.
The function
f_{N}
is realized by a purely transition-weighted WFFA over
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}
with
2N
states: one distributes the
N^{2}
affine expressions
\varphi_{i}
over
N
initial and
N
final states.

For the Cauchy product, we obtain

(\mathcal{F}_{N}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{N})(\bot_{x,y})=\max_{i,j\in[N^{2}]}\bigl(\varphi_{i}(x)+\varphi_{j}(y)\bigr).

Each pair
(i,j)
defines a distinct affine function in two variables, and for every
(i,j)
there is a non-empty region on which this function uniquely attains the maximum. Hence any WFFA recognizing
\mathcal{F}_{N}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{N}
must realize at least
N^{4}
distinct runs of length
2
.

Now let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})
be a purely transition-weighted WFFA over
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}
with
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}=\mathcal{F}_{N}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{N}
, and let

H=Q\setminus(I\cup F).

Since
\mathcal{A}
accepts only words of length
2
,
I\cap F=\emptyset
and every accepting run has the form

q_{I}\to q_{H}\to q_{F},

where
q_{I}\in I
,
q_{H}\in H
, and
q_{F}\in F
. Therefore the number of runs is at most

|I|\cdot|H|\cdot|F|.

Thus

N^{4}\leq|I|\cdot|H|\cdot|F|.

By the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality,

|\mathcal{A}|_{Q}=|I|+|H|+|F|\geq 3(|I|\,|H|\,|F|)^{1/3}\geq 3N^{4/3}.

This proves the claim.
∎

6.4
Monomials

In this subsection, we show that for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
, WFFAs with arbitrary
\mathbb{F}
-expressions can be simulated by WFFAs using expressions of constant size.

An
\mathbb{F}
-constraint
c\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
is called
basic
if
c=[(\pi_{1}\oplus\pi_{2})\bowtie\pi_{3}]
where
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
{\bowtie}\in\{=,\neq\}
. An
\mathbb{F}
-constraint
c\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
is called
simple
if it is either basic or of the form
c=[\beta_{1}\otimes\beta_{2}]
where
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{F}}
are basic
\mathbb{F}
-constraints. An expression
e\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
is called an
\mathbb{F}
-monomial
if it is of the form
e=[c\otimes\alpha]
where
c\in\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{F}}
is a simple
\mathbb{F}
-constraint and
\pi\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}
is a affine
\mathbb{F}
-expression. Let
\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{F}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\mathbb{F}}
denote the collection of all
\mathbb{F}
-monomials.

Remark 6.21
.

Clearly,
{[\![\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}}]\!]}\subsetneq{[\![\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{F}}]\!]}
.
Moreover, all expressions in
\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{F}}
have bounded size, and thus can be evaluated in constant time (cf. Remark
2.8
).

Theorem 6.22
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
. Then

{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}]\!]}={[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}.

Proof.

The statement follows from Lemmas
6.25
and
6.7
.
∎

We briefly recall that a function
f:\mathbb{D}\to S
is
piecewise affine
if there exists a finite partition of
\mathbb{D}
into intervals such that, on each interval, either
f
is affine or
f
is constantly
\mathbb{0}
.

Lemma 6.23
.

For every
e\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
, the function
{[\![e]\!]}:\mathbb{D}\to S
is piecewise affine.

Proof sketch.

The statement follows by structural induction on
e
.
Constants and bindings are affine,
\otimes
preserves affinity, and the operations
\oplus
, equality, and inequality introduce only finitely many breakpoints, yielding a finite partition of
\mathbb{D}
.
∎

Lemma 6.24
.

Every piecewise-affine function
f:\mathbb{D}\to S
can be represented by an expression in
\langle\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{F}}\rangle_{\oplus}
.

Proof sketch.

Let
f:\mathbb{D}\to S
be piecewise affine. Then there exists a finite partition of
\mathbb{D}
into intervals such that
f
is affine on each interval.
Each affine component can be encoded by a monomial together with constraints restricting its domain to the corresponding interval; see Example
2.9
.
Taking the
\oplus
-sum over all components yields an expression in
\langle\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{F}}\rangle_{\oplus}
representing
f
.
∎

Lemma 6.25
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
. Then

{[\![\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}]\!]}={[\![\langle\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{F}}\rangle_{\oplus}]\!]}.

Proof sketch.

By Lemma
6.23
, every expression
e\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
defines a piecewise-affine function
f:\mathbb{D}\to S
.
By Lemma
6.24
, such a function can be represented by an expression in
\langle\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{F}}\rangle_{\oplus}
.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain the following strict hierarchy:

Corollary 6.26
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
. Then:

{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}\subsetneq{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}\subsetneq{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}={[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}]\!]}.

Remark 6.27
.

The reduction result of Theorem
6.22
can be used to derive expressiveness limitations of WFFAs.
In particular, one can show that the class of WFFA-recognizable QFLs is not closed under pointwise negation over the arctic semiring
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{arc}}
.
However, after an appropriate transformation, such negated functions can be represented in the tropical semiring.
We omit the technical details.

7
Proof of the Kleene–Schützenberger Theorem

In this section, we prove a parametric version of the Kleene–Schützenberger theorem for WFFAs. In Section
4
, we stated the theorem for the full class of WFFAs with arbitrary
\mathbb{F}
-expressions as transition weights. Here, we generalize this result to WFFAs whose transitions are restricted to an arbitrary collection
E
of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions. This formulation covers the full class as a special case and provides a flexible framework to study WFFAs with different types of transition expressions.

Let
\Sigma
be an alphabet and
\mathbb{F}=(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{D},\mathbb{b})
a finance semiring with
\mathbb{S}=(S,\oplus,\otimes,\mathbb{0},\mathbb{1})
.

Given a collection of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
, we say that
\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
is
E
-weighted
if, for every subexpression of
\mathcal{R}
of the form
e_{a}
with
e\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
a\in\Sigma
, we have
e\in E
. Let

\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)=\{\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}\mid\mathcal{R}\text{ is }E\text{-weighted}\}.

For any collection of weighted finance regular expressions
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
, let

{[\![\mathcal{W}]\!]}=\{{[\![\mathcal{R}]\!]}\mid\mathcal{R}\in\mathcal{W}\}.

Unfortunately, under a restriction to an arbitrary collection
E
of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions, the expressive correspondence between WFFA and weighted finance regular expressions may fail. For example, the following lemma demonstrates that, already for primitive expressions, weighted finance regular expressions are strictly more expressive than WFFAs.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\Sigma=\{\bot\}
and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
. Then

{[\![\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}\supsetneq{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S
be defined by
\mathcal{F}(\bot_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}})=n+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
for all
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
. Let
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
be the regular expression
([1.0]_{\varepsilon}\cdot{\langle\!\langle 1.0\rangle\!\rangle}_{\bot})^{*}
. Then,
{[\![\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}]\!]}=\mathcal{F}
, so
\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}})]\!]}
.

On the other hand, by Lemma
6.12
,
\mathcal{R}\notin{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
.
∎

Definition 7.2
.

A regular expression
\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
is called
\varepsilon
-free if it can be derived from the grammar

\mathcal{R}\;::=\;e_{a}\mid\mathcal{R}\oplus\mathcal{R}\mid\mathcal{R}\cdot\mathcal{R}\mid\mathcal{R}\cdot\mathcal{R}^{*}\mid\mathcal{R}^{*}\cdot\mathcal{R}

where
e\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
a\in\Sigma
.

For
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
, let
\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\varepsilon\text{-}\mathrm{free}}(E)=\{\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)\mid\mathcal{R}\text{ is }\varepsilon\text{-}\mathrm{free}\}
.

Remark 7.3
.

Note that, for any
\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\varepsilon\text{-}\mathrm{free}}(E)
,
{[\![\mathcal{R}]\!]}
is proper, i.e.,
{[\![\mathcal{R}]\!]}(\varepsilon)=\mathbb{0}
.

Definition 7.4
.

A regular expression
\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
is called restricted if it can be derived from the grammar

\mathcal{R}\;::=\;s_{\varepsilon}\mid e_{a}\mid\mathcal{R}\oplus\mathcal{R}\mid\mathcal{R}\cdot\mathcal{R}\mid(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon\text{-}\mathrm{free}})^{*}

where
s\in S
,
e\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon\text{-}\mathrm{free}}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\varepsilon\text{-}\mathrm{free}}(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}})
.

Clearly,

\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\varepsilon\text{-}\mathrm{free}}(E)\subseteq\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\mathrm{res}}(E)\subseteq\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E).

We now present the main result of this section: a parametric version of the Kleene–Schützenberger theorem for WFFAs, which relates weighted finance regular expressions and WFFAs relative to an arbitrary collection of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions. This formulation captures both the case where the collection is
(S,\otimes)
-closed and the general case for an arbitrary collection
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
.

Let
\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\tau,\mathrm{proper}}(E)
denote the class of all purely transition-weighted automata
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\tau}(E)
such that
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
is a proper QFL.

Theorem 7.5

(Parametric Kleene–Schützenberger theorem)
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
be a collection of
\mathbb{F}
-expressions.

(a)

If
E
is
(S,\otimes)
-closed, then
{[\![\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}={[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
.

(b)

For an arbitrary
E
,
{[\![\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\mathrm{res}}(E)]\!]}={[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
.

(c)

For an arbitrary
E
, then
{[\![\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\varepsilon\text{-}\mathrm{free}}(E)]\!]}={[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\tau,\mathrm{proper}}(E)]\!]}
.

Remark 7.6
.

As mentioned in Example
6.1
(a),
E=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
is
(S,\otimes)
-closed. Therefore, Theorem
4.10
is an immediate consequence of Theorem
7.5
.

The proof of Theorem
7.5
is given below.

The next lemma establishes recognizability of the elementary weighted finance expressions.

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
.

(a)

For every
e\in E
and
a\in\Sigma
,
{[\![e_{a}]\!]}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\tau}(E)]\!]}
.

(b)

For every
s\in S
,
{[\![s_{\varepsilon}]\!]}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
.

Proof.

Both claims are immediate from the one-transition and one-state constructions:

e_{a}
is realized by a purely transition-weighted automaton with a single transition labeled by
a
and weighted by
e
, and
s_{\varepsilon}
is realized by a one-state automaton whose unique state is both initial and final, with weight
s
assigned to either the initial or the final weight function.
∎

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\varepsilon\text{-}\mathrm{free}}(E)
. Then there exists
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\tau}(E)
such that
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{R}]\!]}
.

Proof.

By induction on the structure of
\mathcal{R}
. The base case
\mathcal{R}=e_{a}
follows from Lemma
7.7
(a). The cases
\oplus
and concatenation follow from Corollary
6.9
(a),(b). For expressions of the form
\mathcal{R}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{R}_{2}^{*}
and
\mathcal{R}_{1}^{*}\cdot\mathcal{R}_{2}
, we use Remark
7.3
to ensure that the subexpression under the Kleene star is proper, and then apply Corollary
6.9
(c).
∎

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\mathrm{res}}(E)
. Then there exists
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
such that
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{R}]\!]}
.

Proof.

Again by induction on the structure of
\mathcal{R}
. The cases
e_{a}
and
s_{\varepsilon}
follow from Lemma
7.7
. The cases
\oplus
and concatenation follow from Theorem
6.2
(a),(c). If
\mathcal{R}=(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon\text{-}\mathrm{free}})^{*}
, then Lemma
7.8
yields a purely transition-weighted WFFA for
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon\text{-}\mathrm{free}}
, and Remark
7.3
ensures that its behavior is proper; hence Corollary
6.9
(c) applies.
∎

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
be
(S,\otimes)
-closed and let
\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
. Then there exists
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
such that
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{R}]\!]}
.

Proof.

Immediate from Lemma
7.7
and Theorem
6.2
.
∎

Lemma 7.11
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
. Then there exists
\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\mathrm{res}}(E)
such that
{[\![\mathcal{R}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
.

Proof sketch.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
with
Q=\{1,\dots,m\}
.
For each transition
t=(p,a,r)\in T
, define a restricted expression

\mathcal{R}_{t}:=[\mathrm{wt}_{T}(t)]_{a}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\mathrm{res}}(E).

For all
p,r\in Q
and
k\in\{0,\dots,m\}
, we define expressions

\mathcal{R}_{p,r}^{(k)}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\mathrm{res}}(E)

recursively by

\mathcal{R}_{p,r}^{(0)}=\bigoplus_{t=(p,a,r)\in T}\mathcal{R}_{t},

and

\mathcal{R}_{p,r}^{(k+1)}=\mathcal{R}_{p,r}^{(k)}\oplus\mathcal{R}_{p,k+1}^{(k)}\cdot\bigl(\mathcal{R}_{k+1,k+1}^{(k)}\bigr)^{*}\cdot\mathcal{R}_{k+1,r}^{(k)}.

Since
\mathcal{R}_{k+1,k+1}^{(k)}
is
\varepsilon
-free, its behavior is proper by Remark
7.3
, and thus the star is admissible in the restricted syntax.
As in the usual state-elimination construction for weighted automata, one shows by induction on
k
that
\mathcal{R}_{p,r}^{(k)}
denotes the sum of the weights of all paths from
p
to
r
whose intermediate states lie in
\{1,\dots,k\}
. Hence
\mathcal{R}_{i,f}^{(m)}
captures all paths from
i
to
f
.
Finally, define

\mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{i\in I,\,f\in F}\bigl((\mathrm{wt}_{I}(i))_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathcal{R}_{i,f}^{(m)}\cdot(\mathrm{wt}_{F}(f))_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}^{\mathrm{res}}(E).

Then
{[\![\mathcal{R}]\!]}
coincides with
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
on all non-empty words. If
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(\varepsilon)\neq\mathbb{0}
, we add the term
[{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(\varepsilon)]_{\varepsilon}
.
∎

As a corollary of Theorem
7.5
, we obtain:

Corollary 7.12
.

{[\![\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}]\!]}={[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}]\!]}.

8
Matrix Representation of WFFAs

In this section, we give an algebraic characterization of WFFAs in terms of matrix products.
As a consequence, we obtain a polynomial-time procedure for evaluating their behavior.
More precisely, the evaluation algorithm is quadratic in the number of states and linear in the length of the input word.
This is noteworthy since a WFFA may have exponentially many runs of a given length, whereas the matrix-based evaluation avoids explicit run enumeration.

Throughout this section, let
\Sigma
be an alphabet, let
\mathbb{S}=(S,\oplus,\otimes,\mathbb{0},\mathbb{1})
be a semiring, and let
\mathbb{F}=(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{D},\mathbb{b})
be a finance semiring.

For
k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, a
k\times l
matrix
over a set
X
is a mapping
A:[k]\times[l]\to X
.
For matrices
A\in S^{k\times l}
and
B\in S^{l\times m}
, their product
A\boxtimes B\in S^{k\times m}
is defined by

(A\boxtimes B)_{i,j}=\bigoplus_{x\in[l]}A_{i,x}\otimes B_{x,j}.

Let
\mathbb{U}^{(k)}\in S^{k\times k}
denote the unit matrix.

It is well known that
(S^{k\times k},\boxtimes,\mathbb{U}^{(k)})
is a monoid and that recognizable weighted languages admit a matrix characterization via monoid morphisms (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
).
We now adapt this viewpoint to WFFAs.

Remark 8.1
.

Not every morphism
\mu:\mathbb{D}\Sigma_{\mathrm{free}}^{*}\to S^{k\times k}_{\boxtimes}
corresponds to a WFFA.
For instance, over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
, the QFL
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{D}\{\bot\}^{*}\to S
defined by

\mathcal{F}(\bot_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}^{2}

admits such a matrix representation, but is not WFFA-recognizable, since every
\mathbb{F}
-expression over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
defines a piecewise-affine function.

For an
\mathbb{F}
-expression matrix
X\in(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}})^{k\times k}
, let
{[\![X]\!]}(d)\in S^{k\times k}
be defined entrywise by

({[\![X]\!]}(d))_{i,j}={[\![X_{i,j}]\!]}(d).

Given a mapping
\xi:\Sigma\to(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}})^{k\times k}
, define the morphism

\mu_{\xi}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma_{\mathrm{free}}^{*}\to S^{k\times k}_{\boxtimes}

by
\mu_{\xi}(\varepsilon)=\mathbb{U}^{(k)}
and

\mu_{\xi}((a_{1},d_{1})\dots(a_{n},d_{n}))={[\![\xi(a_{1})]\!]}(d_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{[\![\xi(a_{n})]\!]}(d_{n}).

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
. We say that a morphism
\mu:\mathbb{D}\Sigma_{\mathrm{free}}^{*}\to S^{k\times k}_{\boxtimes}
is
E
-generated
if there exists
\xi:\Sigma\to E^{k\times k}
such that
\mu=\mu_{\xi}
.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
with
{[\![\mathbb{0}]\!]}\in{[\![E]\!]}
, and let
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S
. Then

{\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}}

if and only if there exist
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, vectors
\lambda\in S^{1\times k}
and
\nu\in S^{k\times 1}
, and an
E
-generated morphism

\mu:\mathbb{D}\Sigma_{\mathrm{free}}^{*}\to S^{k\times k}_{\boxtimes}

such that

\mathcal{F}(w)=\lambda\boxtimes\mu(w)\boxtimes\nu

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
.

Proof.

The statement follows from Lemmas
8.3
and
8.4
.
∎

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
with
{[\![\mathbb{0}]\!]}\in{[\![E]\!]}
and let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)
. Then there exist
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, vectors
\lambda\in S^{1\times k}
and
\nu\in S^{k\times 1}
, and an
E
-generated morphism
\mu
such that

{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)=\lambda\boxtimes\mu(w)\boxtimes\nu

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
.

Proof.

Enumerate the states of
\mathcal{A}
as
q_{1},\dots,q_{k}
.
Define
\lambda\in S^{1\times k}
and
\nu\in S^{k\times 1}
by
{\lambda_{i}=\mathrm{wt}_{I}(q_{i})}
and
{\nu_{i}=\mathrm{wt}_{F}(q_{i})}
for all
i
, where missing weights are interpreted as
\mathbb{0}
.
For each
a\in\Sigma
define a matrix
\xi(a)\in E^{k\times k}
by placing the transition weight of
(q_{i},a,q_{j})
in position
(i,j)
, and an expression equivalent to
\mathbb{0}
otherwise.
Then the induced morphism
\mu_{\xi}
satisfies

{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)=\lambda\boxtimes\mu_{\xi}(w)\boxtimes\nu,

as follows directly from the definition of WFFA behavior.
∎

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
,
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
,
\lambda\in S^{1\times k}
,
\nu\in S^{k\times 1}
, and
\xi:\Sigma\to E^{k\times k}
.
If

\mathcal{F}(w)=\lambda\boxtimes\mu_{\xi}(w)\boxtimes\nu

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
, then
\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}
.

Proof.

Construct a WFFA with state set
[k]
, all states both initial and final, initial and final weights given by
\lambda
and
\nu
, and transition weight of
(i,a,j)
equal to
(\xi(a))_{i,j}
.
By construction, its behavior coincides with
\mathcal{F}
.
∎

As a direct consequence, we obtain the following complexity bound.

Corollary 8.5
.

Assume that
\oplus
,
\otimes
, and
\mathbb{b}
can each be computed in
\mathcal{O}(1)
time.
Let
{\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}}
and let
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
. Then
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)
can be computed in

{\mathcal{O}\bigl((|\mathcal{A}|_{Q})^{2}\cdot|\mathcal{A}|_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}}\cdot|w|\bigr)}

time.

Proof.

Let
k=|\mathcal{A}|_{Q}
. By Lemma
8.3
, write

{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)=\lambda\boxtimes\mu_{\xi}(w)\boxtimes\nu.

Assume that
{w=(a_{1},d_{1})\dots(a_{n},d_{n})}
. We compute iteratively

v_{0}=\lambda,\qquad v_{i}=v_{i-1}\boxtimes{[\![\xi(a_{i})]\!]}(d_{i})\quad(i\in[n]).

Each matrix evaluation
{[\![\xi(a_{i})]\!]}(d_{i})
requires
\mathcal{O}(k^{2}|\mathcal{A}|_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}})
time, and each matrix-vector product has the same asymptotic cost.
Thus the total running time is

\mathcal{O}(k^{2}|\mathcal{A}|_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}}|w|).

∎

Remark 8.6
.

If
E=S
,
E=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}
, or
E=\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{F}}
, then
|\mathcal{A}|_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}}=\mathcal{O}(1)
. Hence the behavior can be evaluated in

\mathcal{O}((|\mathcal{A}|_{Q})^{2}\cdot|w|).

In particular, this applies to
\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
when restricted to primitive, affine, or monomial transition weights.

9
Decision Problems for WFFAs

In this section, we do not attempt an exhaustive study of decision problems for WFFAs.
Instead, we illustrate how the reduction to monomials can be used to derive decidability results for two representative verification tasks: the support problem and the
>
-threshold problem.

9.1
Support Problem

The support problem asks whether a given WFFA assigns a value different from
\mathbb{0}
to some finance word.
Equivalently, it asks whether the quantitative finance language recognized by the automaton has non-empty support.

Let
X
be a set and
f:X\to S
a mapping. The
support
of
f
is defined as

{\mathrm{supp}(f)=\{x\in X\mid f(x)\neq\mathbb{0}\}}.

For any finance word
w=(a_{1},d_{1})\dots(a_{n},d_{n})\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
, let

\pi_{\Sigma}(w)=a_{1}\dots a_{n}\in\Sigma^{*}.

For
L\subseteq\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
, let

\pi_{\Sigma}(L)=\{\pi_{\Sigma}(w)\mid w\in L\}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
and let
e\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
. Then it is decidable whether
{\mathrm{supp}({[\![e]\!]})\neq\emptyset}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.23
,
{[\![e]\!]}
is piecewise affine.
Hence there exists a finite partition of
\mathbb{D}
into points and intervals such that
{[\![e]\!]}
is affine on each piece.
It is therefore enough to inspect finitely many pieces. On each piece, one can decide whether the corresponding affine function is non-zero somewhere. Thus it is decidable whether
\mathrm{supp}({[\![e]\!]})\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
and let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
. Then
\pi_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{supp}({[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}))\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
is a regular language.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})
. Define the non-deterministic finite automaton

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(Q,I^{\prime},T^{\prime},F^{\prime})

by

I^{\prime}=\{i\in I\mid\mathrm{wt}_{I}(i)\neq\mathbb{0}\},

F^{\prime}=\{f\in F\mid\mathrm{wt}_{F}(f)\neq\mathbb{0}\},

and

T^{\prime}=\{(p,a,q)\in T\mid\mathrm{supp}({[\![\mathrm{wt}_{T}(p,a,q)]\!]})\neq\emptyset\}.

Then a word
u=a_{1}\dots a_{n}\in\Sigma^{*}
is accepted by
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
if and only if there exists a finance word

w=(a_{1},d_{1})\dots(a_{n},d_{n})\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}

with
\pi_{\Sigma}(w)=u
and
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)\neq\mathbb{0}
. Hence

L(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=\pi_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{supp}({[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]})),

so
\pi_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{supp}({[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}))
is regular.
∎

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
and let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
. Then it is decidable whether
\mathrm{supp}({[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

By Lemma
9.2
, the language
\pi_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{supp}({[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}))
is regular and effectively constructible.
Moreover,

\mathrm{supp}({[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]})\neq\emptyset

if and only if

\pi_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{supp}({[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}))\neq\emptyset.

Since emptiness of regular languages is decidable (see, e.g.,
[
18
]
), the claim follows.
∎

9.2
Threshold Problems

In this subsection, we focus on the finance semiring
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
.
In financial terms, the
>
-threshold problem asks whether a modeled instrument or strategy can exceed a prescribed payoff level under some market scenario.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a scenario language, i.e., a set of finance words representing possible scenarios.

Definition 9.4
.

Let
{\bowtie}\in\{<,>\}
.
The
\bowtie
-threshold problem over
\mathcal{D}
asks, given
{\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, whether there exists a finance word
w\in\mathcal{D}
such that

{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)\bowtie\theta.

If
\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
, we simply speak of the
\bowtie
-threshold problem
.

Remark 9.5
.

For WFFAs with constant transition weights, the threshold problem reduces to the corresponding problem for classical max-plus weighted automata; the relevant decidability results are well known (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
).

Let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathbb{D}
. We denote by
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{I}}\Sigma^{*}
the set of all finance words (scenarios)

w=(a_{1},d_{1})\dots(a_{n},d_{n})\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}

such that
d_{i}\in\mathcal{I}
for all
i\in[n]
.
This defines a basic class of scenario languages with a global constraint on the data values, typically given by an interval
\mathcal{I}=[\ell,h]
.

Theorem 9.6
.

(a)

The
>
-threshold problem for
\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
is decidable.

(b)

Let
\mathcal{I}=[\ell,h]\subseteq\mathbb{D}
be an interval. Then the
>
-threshold problem over
\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{I}}\Sigma^{*}
for
\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
is decidable.

For the proof of Theorem
9.6
, we require the following lemma.

Lemma 9.7
.

Let
e\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
and let either
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{D}
or
\mathcal{I}=[l,h]\subseteq\mathbb{D}
. Then
\sup_{x\in\mathcal{I}}{[\![e]\!]}(x)
is computable.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.23
,
{[\![e]\!]}
is piecewise affine.
Hence there exists a finite partition of
\mathbb{D}
into points and intervals such that
{[\![e]\!]}
is affine on each piece.
The supremum over
\mathbb{D}
or over
[l,h]
is therefore attained either at a breakpoint or at an endpoint of one of the finitely many relevant intervals, and is thus computable.
∎

Remark 9.8
.

Let
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{D}
or
\mathcal{I}=[l,h]\subseteq\mathbb{D}
.

(a)

If, for a class
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
, every
e\in E
defines a piecewise-affine function with
\mathcal{O}(|e|)
pieces, then
\sup_{x\in I}{[\![e]\!]}(x)
can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(|e|)
time.

(b)

In particular, for
E=\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{F}}
, all expressions have constant-size piecewise-affine structure, so
\sup_{x\in\mathcal{I}}{[\![e]\!]}(x)
is computable in
\mathcal{O}(|e|)
time.

Proof of Theorem
9.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
, let
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{D}
or
{\mathcal{I}=[l,h]}
, and let
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.
For each transition
t\in T
, compute

M_{t}=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{I}}\left({[\![\mathrm{wt}_{T}(t)]\!]}(x)\right)

using Lemma
9.7
. If some transition
t
with
M_{t}=\infty
occurs on a path from an initial state to a final state using only transitions with supremum different from
-\infty
, then the answer is positive.
Otherwise, transitions with
M_{t}=-\infty
can be removed, and every remaining transition can be replaced by the constant weight
M_{t}
.
This yields a classical max-plus weighted automaton
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
over
\Sigma
such that

\exists w\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{I}}\Sigma^{*}:\ {[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)>\theta\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists u\in\Sigma^{*}:\ {[\![\mathcal{A}^{\prime}]\!]}(u)>\theta.

The claim now follows from the decidability of the
>
-threshold problem for max-plus weighted automata.
∎

Remark 9.9
.

By Theorem
9.6
and the constant-size representation of monomial expressions, the
>
-threshold problem for
\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{F}})
is decidable in polynomial time.

Since Theorem
4.10
yields an effective translation from weighted finance regular expressions to WFFAs, the preceding decidability results immediately transfer to weighted finance regular expressions.

Corollary 9.10
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
and let
\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{REG}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
. Then the following hold:

(a)

It is decidable whether
\mathrm{supp}({[\![\mathcal{R}]\!]})\neq\emptyset
.

(b)

Given
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, it is decidable whether there exists
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
such that
{[\![\mathcal{R}]\!]}(w)>\theta
.

(c)

Given
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and an interval
\mathcal{I}=[l,h]\subseteq\mathbb{D}
, it is decidable whether there exists
w\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{I}}\Sigma^{*}
such that
{[\![\mathcal{R}]\!]}(w)>\theta
.

Remark 9.11
.

The
<
-threshold problem is closely related to risk analysis, where one asks whether there exists a scenario whose payoff stays below a given risk level.
In the deterministic case (i.e., when each word induces at most one run), the
<
- and
>
-threshold problems are inter-reducible.
In contrast, for nondeterministic WFFA over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
, threshold problems become undecidable, as follows from results on max-plus automata
[
1
]
.

10
Conclusion

We introduced weighted finite finance automata (WFFAs), a novel automata-theoretic framework for modeling quantitative aspects of financial systems.
We established closure properties of the model and proved a Kleene–Schützenberger-type characterization via weighted finance regular expressions, yielding an effective translation between the two formalisms.
Moreover, we provided an algebraic matrix representation of WFFAs, which leads to a polynomial-time evaluation procedure.
Finally, we applied these results to derive decidability results for fundamental verification tasks, including the support problem and the threshold problem.

Several directions for future research remain open.
On the algorithmic side, a systematic complexity analysis of the constructions and decision procedures developed in this paper is required.
In particular, the decidability landscape of WFFAs is not yet fully understood.
Beyond the threshold problem considered here, an important direction is to identify classes of quantitative finance languages that capture realistic market scenarios while preserving decidability.

From a modeling perspective, WFFAs can be extended to incorporate multiple interacting financial variables, enabling the analysis of systems driven by correlated factors such as multiple asset prices, exchange rates, and portfolio dynamics, thereby opening the way to applications in portfolio management.
Another promising direction is the extension to continuous time, in the spirit of weighted timed automata
[
23
]
, which would allow modeling of time-sensitive financial processes.

Another direction for future work concerns the algebraic encoding discussed in Remark
4.12
. While the present paper focuses on direct constructions tailored to WFFAs, it would be interesting to investigate a fully algebraic treatment of the model. Such an approach may provide further structural insight and could be useful for studying extensions of WFFAs with additional features.

Altogether, WFFAs provide a unified automata-theoretic framework for modeling and analyzing quantitative aspects of financial systems, combining expressiveness with decidability and efficient evaluation.

This appendix provides detailed proofs of the main results as well as additional technical arguments omitted from the main text for clarity.

A.1
Closure Properties of WFFAs (Section
5
)

Lemma A.1

(Lemma
5.3
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
. Then there exists
\mathcal{A}_{\oplus}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
such that

{[\![\mathcal{A}_{\oplus}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}_{1}]\!]}\oplus{[\![\mathcal{A}_{2}]\!]}
.

Proof.

We use the standard disjoint union construction for non-deterministic automata.
For
i\in\{1,2\}
, let
\mathcal{A}_{i}=(Q_{i},I_{i},T_{i},F_{i},\mathrm{wt}_{I_{i}},\mathrm{wt}_{T_{i}},\mathrm{wt}_{F_{i}})
. We may assume that
Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}=\emptyset
. Then, we define
\mathcal{A}_{\oplus}
by

\mathcal{A}_{\oplus}=(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2},I_{1}\cup I_{2},T_{1}\cup T_{2},F_{1}\cup F_{2},\mathrm{wt}_{I_{1}}\cup\mathrm{wt}_{I_{2}},\mathrm{wt}_{T_{1}}\cup\mathrm{wt}_{T_{2}},\mathrm{wt}_{F_{1}}\cup\mathrm{wt}_{F_{2}}).

Here, the union of functions is defined as follows. For
i\in\{1,2\}
, let
f_{i}:X_{i}\to Y_{i}
such that
X_{1}\cap X_{2}=\emptyset
. Then,
(f_{1}\cup f_{2}):(X_{1}\cup X_{2})\to(Y_{1}\cup Y_{2})
is defined by
(f_{1}\cup f_{2})(x_{1})=f_{1}(x_{1})
for all
x_{1}\in X_{1}
and by
(f_{1}\cup f_{2})(x_{2})=f_{2}(x_{2})
for all
x_{2}\in X_{2}
.

Since the state sets
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are disjoint, every run of
\mathcal{A}_{\oplus}
lies entirely within either
\mathcal{A}_{1}
or
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. Thus, the set of runs of
\mathcal{A}_{\oplus}
on any word
w
is the disjoint union of the sets of runs of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
on
w
. Moreover, all weights (initial, transition, final) are preserved for the respective components. Therefore,
we obtain

{[\![\mathcal{A}_{\oplus}]\!]}(w)={[\![\mathcal{A}_{1}]\!]}(w)\oplus{[\![\mathcal{A}_{2}]\!]}(w).

∎

Lemma A.2

(Lemma
5.4
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
. Then there exists

\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
such that

{[\![\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}_{1}]\!]}\otimes{[\![\mathcal{A}_{2}]\!]}
.

Proof.

In this case, we can adopt the standard product construction for weighted automata over commutative semirings (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
).
Let

\mathcal{A}_{i}=(Q_{i},I_{i},T_{i},F_{i},\mathrm{wt}_{I_{i}},\mathrm{wt}_{T_{i}},\mathrm{wt}_{F_{i}})
(
i=1,2
).
We construct the product automaton

\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})

where:

•

Q=Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
,
I=I_{1}\times I_{2}
,
F=F_{1}\times F_{2}
;

•

T
contains all transitions
((q_{1},q_{2}),a,(q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime}))
such that
(q_{1},a,q_{1}^{\prime})\in T_{1}
and
(q_{2},a,q_{2}^{\prime})\in T_{2}
;

•

for
(i_{1},i_{2})\in I
,
\mathrm{wt}_{I}(i_{1},i_{2})=\mathrm{wt}_{I_{1}}(i_{1})\otimes\mathrm{wt}_{I_{2}}(i_{2})
;

•

for
(f_{1},f_{2})\in F
,
\mathrm{wt}_{F}(f_{1},f_{2})=\mathrm{wt}_{F_{1}}(f_{1})\otimes\mathrm{wt}_{F_{2}}(i_{2})
;

•

for
t=((q_{1},q_{2}),a,(q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime}))\in T
,
\mathrm{wt}_{T}(t)=[\mathrm{wt}_{T_{1}}(q_{1},a,q_{1}^{\prime})\otimes\mathrm{wt}_{T_{2}}(q_{2},a,q_{2}^{\prime})]
. Note that here
\otimes
is applied to
\mathbb{F}
-expressions.

It can be shown that
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}_{1}]\!]}\otimes{[\![\mathcal{A}_{2}]\!]}
. The idea is that, for each word
w
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the runs of
\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}
and the pairs of runs of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
on the same word
w
. By pairing states and synchronizing transitions on the same input symbol, each accepting run in the product automaton carries the weight of the first run multiplied by the weight of the second run (using the commutativity of the semiring
\mathbb{S}
). Summing over all runs on
w
then yields the Hadamard product of the behaviors of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
.
∎

Lemma A.3

(Lemma
5.6
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
. Then:

(a)

there exists an initially normalized
\mathcal{A}_{I}
with
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{I}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
;

(b)

there exists a finally normalized
\mathcal{A}_{F}
with
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{F}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
;

(c)

if
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
is proper, there exists a completely normalized
\mathcal{A}_{I,F}
with
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{I,F}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})
.

(a)

Let
q_{I}
be an element with
q_{I}\notin Q
. We define the initially normalized WFFA

\mathcal{A}_{I}=(Q\cup\{q_{I}\},\{q_{I}\},T^{\prime},F^{\prime},[q_{I}\mapsto\mathbb{1}],\mathrm{wt}_{T^{\prime}},\mathrm{wt}_{F^{\prime}})

as follows.

•

We define the new transition set

T^{\prime}=T\cup\{(q_{I},a,q)\mid(i,a,q)\in T\text{ and }i\in I\}

and the transition weights
\mathrm{wt}_{T^{\prime}}
by

\mathrm{wt}_{T^{\prime}}(t)=\mathrm{wt}_{T}(t)

for all
t\in T

and

\mathrm{wt}_{T^{\prime}}(q_{I},a,q)=\left[\bigoplus(\mathrm{wt}_{I}(i)\otimes\mathrm{wt}_{T}(i,a,q)\mid(i,a,q)\in T\text{ and }i\in I)\right].

for all
(q_{I},a,q)\in T^{\prime}\setminus T
. Here, formally, we choose any order for the summands and any parenthesizing. Note that the order of the summands and their parenthesizing in
\mathbb{F}

\oplus
-operation in the semiring
\mathbb{S}
.

•

If
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{I}]\!]}(\varepsilon)=\mathbb{0}
, then we let
F^{\prime}=F
and
\mathrm{wt}_{F^{\prime}}=\mathrm{wt}_{F}
.

•

If
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{I}]\!]}(\varepsilon)=s_{\varepsilon}\neq\mathbb{0}

F^{\prime}=F\cup\{q_{I}\}
and
\mathrm{wt}_{F^{\prime}}=\mathrm{wt}_{F}\cup[q_{I}\mapsto s_{\epsilon}]
.

Similarly to the corresponding construction for semiring-weighted automata (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
), it can be shown that
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{I}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
. Every run of
\mathcal{A}
starting from an original initial state
i\in I
is simulated in
\mathcal{A}_{I}
by a run starting from the new state
q_{I}
, whose outgoing transitions encode the initial weights
\mathrm{wt}_{I}(i)
. Likewise, the treatment of
q_{I}
as a final state for
\varepsilon
ensures that the empty-word behavior is preserved, so overall
{[\![\mathcal{A}_{I}]\!]}={[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}
.

(b)

We proceed dually to (a).

(c)

Choose
q_{I},q_{F}\notin Q
such that
q_{I}\neq q_{F}
and define the completely normalized WFFA

\mathcal{A}_{I,F}=(Q\cup\{q_{I},q_{F}\},\{q_{I}\},T^{\prime},\{q_{F}\},[q_{I}\mapsto\mathbb{1}],\mathrm{wt}_{T^{\prime}},[q_{F}\mapsto\mathbb{1}])

where:

•

T^{\prime}=T\cup T_{I}\cup T_{I,F}\cup T_{F}
where:

–

T_{I}=\{(q_{I},a,q)\;|\;(i,a,q)\in T\text{ for some }i\in I\}
;

–

Lemma A.4

(Lemma

(a)

Assume that

•

Lemma A.5

(Lemma

)
.

Let

\mathbb{F}
-expressions and

If

.

•

For all

(b)

A.2.4
Negation Operator (Remark
6.27
)

As an application of Theorem
6.22
, we now illustrate certain expressiveness limitations of WFFAs.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
. For any QFL
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S
, where
S=\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
, define the
negation

(-\mathcal{F}):\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S
by

(-\mathcal{F})(w)=\begin{cases}-\mathcal{F}(w),&\text{if }\mathcal{F}(w)\neq-\infty,\\
-\infty,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

As observed in Example
3.7
, such a negation operator is of practical relevance: it permits switching between the profit functions associated with long and short positions in a given financial instrument by simply reversing the sign of the payoff.
As demonstrated in Example
3.7
, the profit functions of both the long and the short position in a European call option are WFFA-recognizable with respect to the finance semiring
\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
. However, for more complex financial instruments, this property may fail. The underlying intuition is that our model lacks the ability to compare the finance values carried by different transitions.

Let
S_{\mathrm{arc}}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
and
S_{\mathrm{trop}}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
. Consider a QFL
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}\to S_{\mathrm{arc}}
. Define the mapping

\lambda_{i}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{wt}_{I}(q_{i}),&\text{if }q_{i}\in I,\\
\mathbb{0},&\text{otherwise};\end{cases}

•

\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{k})^{T}
where, for each
i\in[k]
,
\nu_{i}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{wt}_{F}(q_{i}),&\text{if }q_{i}\in F,\\
\mathbb{0},&\text{otherwise};\end{cases}

•

\xi:\Sigma\to E^{k\times k}
be defined for all
a\in\Sigma
and
i,j\in[k]
by

(\xi(a))_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{wt}_{T}(q_{i},a,q_{j}),&\text{if }(q_{i},a,q_{j})\in T,\\
e_{\mathbb{0}},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It can be shown that
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)=\lambda\boxtimes\mu_{\xi}(w)\boxtimes\nu
for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
.

∎

Lemma A.29

(Lemma
8.4
)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
,
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
,
\lambda\in S^{1\times k}
,
\nu\in S^{k\times 1}

\xi:\Sigma\to E^{k\times k}

\mathcal{F}(w)=\lambda\boxtimes\mu_{\xi}(w)\boxtimes\nu

\mathcal{F}\in{[\![\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}(E)]\!]}

Let

Lemma A.30

(Lemma
9.1
)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
and
e\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
. Then, it is decidable whether
\mathrm{supp}({[\![e]\!]})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.23
, we can effectively find a representation of
{[\![e]\!]}
as in Equation (
15
).
Then,
\mathrm{supp}({[\![e]\!]})\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\mathrm{supp}\!\left(\alpha_{I}^{c_{e}(J)}\right)\neq\mathbb{0}
for some
J\in\mathcal{P}(\delta_{e})
(i.e.,
J\neq\emptyset
).
For every
J\in\mathcal{P}(\delta_{e})
, let
c_{e}(J)=\langle c_{J},c^{\prime}_{J}\rangle
. Then,
\alpha_{J}^{c_{e}(J)}:J\to S
with

\alpha_{J}^{c_{e}(J)}(d)=c_{J}\cdot d+c^{\prime}_{J}
and hence
\mathrm{supp}(\alpha_{I}^{c_{e}(J)})\neq\mathbb{0}

if and only if
c_{J}\neq\mathbb{0}
and
c^{\prime}_{J}\neq\mathbb{0}
. This means that
\mathrm{supp}({[\![e]\!]})\neq\mathbb{0}
if and only if there exists

I\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)
such that
c_{I}\neq\mathbb{0}
and
c^{\prime}_{J}\neq\mathbb{0}
.
Hence, it is decidable whether
\mathrm{supp}({[\![e]\!]})\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Lemma A.31

(Lemma
9.2
)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{arc},\times}
and
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
. Then,
\pi_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{supp}({[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}))\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
is a regular language.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})
. We define the non-deterministic finite automaton
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(Q,I^{\prime},T^{\prime},F^{\prime})
by letting:

•

I^{\prime}=\{i\in I\mid\mathrm{wt}_{I}(i)\neq\mathbb{0}\}
,
F^{\prime}=\{f\in F\mid\mathrm{wt}_{F}(f)\neq\mathbb{0}\}
;

•

T^{\prime}=\{t\in T\mid\mathrm{supp}({[\![\mathrm{wt}_{T}(t)]\!]})\neq\emptyset\}
.

Then, for every
w\in\mathbb{D}\Sigma^{*}
,
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)\neq\mathbb{0}
if and only if
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
accepts the word
\pi_{\Sigma}(w)
. Hence,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
accepts the language
\pi_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{supp}({[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}))\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
, i.e.,
\pi_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{supp}({[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}))
is regular.
∎

Lemma A.32

(Lemma
9.7
)
.

Let
e\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
and either
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{D}
or
\mathcal{I}=[l,h]\subseteq\mathbb{D}
. Then,
\sup_{x\in\mathcal{I}}{[\![e]\!]}(x)
is computable.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.23
,
{[\![e]\!]}
is a piecewise affine function, i.e., there exist a finite set
\delta=\{d_{1},\dots,d_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{D}
with
0=d_{0}<d_{1}<{\dots}<d_{k}<d_{k+1}=\infty
and a mapping
c:\mathcal{P}(\delta)\to S^{2}
such that
{[\![e]\!]}=\bigoplus_{J\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}\alpha_{J}^{c(J)}
. For every
i\in\{0,\dots,k\}
, let
J_{i}=(d_{i},d_{i+1})
and
c(J_{i})=\langle c_{i},c^{\prime}_{i}\rangle
. Then, for all
i\in\{0,\dots,k\}
,

M_{i}=\sup_{x\in J_{i}}\alpha_{J_{i}}^{c(J_{i})}(x)=c_{i}^{\prime}+\max\{c_{i}\cdot d_{i},c_{i}\cdot d_{i+1}\}

where for
d_{k+1}=\infty
, we assume
c_{i}\cdot\infty=\infty
whenever
c_{i}>0
,

c_{i}\cdot\infty=-\infty
whenever
c_{i}<0
,
0\cdot\infty=0
and
(-\infty)\cdot\infty=-\infty
. Note that
M_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
.
Then,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{D}}{[\![e]\!]}(x)=\max\{{[\![e]\!]}(d_{0}),{\dots},{[\![e]\!]}(d_{k}),M_{0},\dots,M_{k}\}.

Therefore,
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{D}}{[\![e]\!]}(x)
is computable.

For
\mathcal{I}=[l,h]
, we can use Remark
A.18
and assume without loss of generality that
l=d_{p}
and
h=d_{q}
for some
0\leq p<q\leq k
.
Then,

\sup_{x\in[l,h]}{[\![e]\!]}(x)=\max\{{[\![e]\!]}(d_{p}),{\dots},{[\![e]\!]}(d_{q}),M_{p},{\dots},M_{q}\}

and hence
\sup_{x\in[l,h]}{[\![e]\!]}(x)
is also computable.
∎

Now ce can give the proof of Theorem
9.6
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,I,T,F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T},\mathrm{wt}_{F})\in\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
,
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{D}
or
\mathcal{I}=[l,h]
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
a threshold. Our goal is to determine whether there exists a finance word
w\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{I}}\Sigma^{*}
such that
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)>\theta
. For each transition
t\in T
, we use Lemma
9.7
to compute
M_{t}=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{I}}{[\![\mathrm{wt}_{T}(t)]\!]}(x)
.
Define the sets:

•

T_{-\infty}=\{t\in T\mid M_{t}=-\infty\}
,

•

T_{\infty}=\{t\in T\mid M_{t}=\infty\}
.

Observe that for every
t\in T_{-\infty}
and
d\in\mathcal{I}
, we have
{[\![\mathrm{wt}_{T}(t)]\!]}(d)=-\infty
. Consequently, any run of
\mathcal{A}
that includes a transition in
T_{-\infty}
is not valid.

First, for every transition
t=(p,\sigma,q)\in T_{\infty}
check whether:

•

p
is reachable from an initial state from
I
via transitions in
T\setminus T_{-\infty}
, and

•

some final state from
F
is reachable from
q
via valid transitions in
T\setminus T_{-\infty}

If both conditions hold, then for every threshold
\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}
there exists a run
\varrho
of
\mathcal{A}
on some word
w\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{I}}(\Sigma^{*})
such that
\varrho
touches the transition
t
and
\mathrm{wt}_{\mathcal{A}}(\varrho)>\vartheta
. As a consequence, there exists
w\in\mathbb{D}_{I}\Sigma^{*}
such that
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)\geq\mathrm{wt}_{\mathcal{A}}(\varrho)>\theta
.

Otherwise, if no transition in
T_{\infty}
appears in any valid run of
\mathcal{A}
,
we construct a semiring-weighted automaton
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(Q,I,T^{\prime},F,\mathrm{wt}_{I},\mathrm{wt}_{T^{\prime}},\mathrm{wt}_{F})
over
\Sigma
and
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{arc}}
where
T^{\prime}=T\setminus(T_{-\infty}\cup T_{\infty})
and
\mathrm{wt}_{T^{\prime}}:T^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined for all
t\in T^{\prime}
by
\mathrm{wt}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(t)=M_{t}
. Then,
{[\![\mathcal{A}]\!]}(w)>\theta
for some
w\in\mathbb{D}_{I}\Sigma^{*}
if and only if
{[\![\mathcal{A}^{\prime}]\!]}(u)>\theta
for some
u\in\Sigma^{*}
.
Since the
>
-threshold problem for weighted automata over the max-plus semiring is decidable (cf.
[
1
]
), and the present setting can be reduced to this case, the
>
-threshold problem for
\mathrm{WFFA}_{\Sigma,\mathbb{F}}
over
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{I}}\Sigma^{*}
is also decidable.
∎
