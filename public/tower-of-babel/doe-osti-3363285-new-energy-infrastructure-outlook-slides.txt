Introduction

• This report provides a perspective on energy infrastructure in the continental
  United States under development as of December 31, 2025, focusing on projects
  making significant progress toward achieving commercial operation
• The states of the projects vary from “Announced” to “Under Construction”
• The states of “Announced” or “Early Development” are not necessarily strong
  indicators of commitment of major capital and human resources; development
  through operational capacity additions is highly dependent on the market
  environment
 • Entry into permitting and interconnection processes nominally requires some expenditure of
 capital by the project developer; these expenses alone are not sufficient to move a project
 from “Early Development” to “Advanced Development” status because there is still a high
 level of uncertainty, pending approvals, and alone result in no more than project-related
 paper
• Projects in “Advanced Development” or “Under Construction”—progressing
  projects—reflect a more significant financial and human resource commitment to
  completion and offer a better perspective of the new capacity that may be
  forthcoming

 4

Highlights and Takeaways

• Natural gas is the largest section of the generation fleet, followed by coal
  and nuclear
• Hydro is the oldest section of the generating fleet, followed by coal,
  petroleum, and nuclear
• The size and age of different sections of the fleet are reflected in planned
  retirements of coal and nuclear plants over the next decade
• The next few years of planned capacity heavily feature solar and energy
  storage, with natural gas at a distant third, slightly favoring non-NGCC
  (natural gas combined cycle), and nuclear and hydro at an even more
  distant fourth and fifth, respectively
• Most planned additions are in early development, and renewables make
  up the majority of planned additions
• Coal stockpiles have generally increased over the past five years, but
  petroleum stockpiles have remained stable after falling since 2010

New Power Plants

• This report provides comparisons of new power plant capacity from both the
  nameplate and capacity credit perspectives by fuel category using the regional
  capacity credit factors used in the North American Electric Reliability Corporation
  (NERC) assessment process for renewable and energy storage resources
 • These factors are developed by the planning regions based on historical rates of peak-hour
 performance
 • Regions with outlying factors are an artifact of small capacity installations or lack historical
 data upon which to establish a lower credit
• This report assumes interconnection-level average credit for areas that do not
  have a specified credit
• Starting in 2024, energy storage resources are credited, per current NERC
  planning processes
• At present, due to the lack of operational data, industry has not determined
  distinct capacity credits for onshore and offshore wind

Source: NERC 2025 Summer Reliability Assessment

Electric Transmission Projects*
 *Bulk electric system only

• Many generator interconnections require the parallel development of transmission
  infrastructure to achieve operation and grid interconnection; these projects often have
  their own timelines and can lead to delays in generator operation
• If a significant amount of the uncertain capacity fails to develop, the need may arise for
  additional transmission infrastructure to deliver output from existing capacity and to
  maintain reliability
• Like generation, transmission project development duration is impacted by several factors
  including labor availability, component production lead times, permitting duration,

Fuel Stockpiles

• Unlike NG and some other liquid fuels used by the power sector, a large
  quantity of coal is continually maintained in on-site stockpiles at coal-fired
  power generators to ensure reliability and continued plant operation
• While NG is used for power, NG storage is primarily leveraged by pipeline
  operators and local distribution companies (LDCs) to ensure reliable
  pipeline pressures and to meet a regulated LDC utility’s obligation to serve
• Petroleum product stockpiles are primarily used for market balancing
