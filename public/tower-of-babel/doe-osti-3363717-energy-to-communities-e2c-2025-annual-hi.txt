Energy to Communities (E2C)

2025 Annual Highlights

The Energy to Communities (E2C)
program links local governments,
Tribes, utilities, and community
organizations with national
laboratory experts to deliver tailored,
state‑of‑the‑art analysis that supports
energy systems aligned with local and
regional priorities.
The program is funded by the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE),
managed by the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR),
(nlr.gov), and delivered in collaboration with Argonne National Laboratory
(ANL) (anl.gov), Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL) (lbl.gov),
Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) (ornl.gov),
and Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL) (pnnl.gov).

The E2C program stands out for its approach to delivering energy
technical assistance, with three complementary offerings that allow
communities to engage in the way that best meets their needs. Through
In-Depth Partnerships, Peer-Learning Cohorts, and Expert Match, E2C
connects communities with subject matter experts who help them tackle
complex energy challenges, build capacity, and advance local goals.

Moving Forward
As E2C moves forward in 2026, the program is positioned
to build on the momentum of its growing portfolio and
continue evolving to meet communities’ most pressing
and emerging energy challenges. Most notably, as two
in‑depth partnership teams prepare to conclude their
work in 2026, the program will capture lessons learned,
refine approaches, and strengthen models for long‑term
community–laboratory collaboration.
The year ahead also brings new opportunities to leverage
the full breadth of laboratory capabilities across the E2C
portfolio. Communities and lab teams will increasingly
explore advanced tools and platforms—including
microgrid design and analysis, digital twins, and other
ARIES capabilities—to support more sophisticated
planning, resilience strategies, and system‑level decision
making. DOE-funded investments in national laboratory
capabilities will help communities anticipate emerging
issues, test solutions before deployment, and accelerate
implementation with greater confidence.
Together, these developments signal a year of continued
innovation and deeper technical engagement. By
expanding its ability to address evolving energy needs
while advancing Lab capabilities through real‑world
community partnerships, E2C will continue supporting
practical, scalable, and community‑driven energy
solutions in 2026 and beyond.
