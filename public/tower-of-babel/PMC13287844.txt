Publication and Retraction Activity in the Field of Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation: Origins, Concerns, and Perspectives
Authors: Bekzhan A Permenov, Olena Zimba, Darkhan Suigenbayev, Burhan Fatih Kocyigit
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundExtracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) has become a vital life-support tool in critical care, particularly during the COVID-19 pandemic. The rapid growth of ECMO research necessitates bibliometric analysis to identify trends, key contributors, and obstacles, including concerns regarding scientific integrity, such as retractions.MethodsA bibliometric analysis was conducted using the Scopus database (search date: August 25, 2025), with the phrase “extracorporeal membrane oxygenation” searched in the title, abstract, or keywords. All English-language publications from 1958 and 2025 were covered. Data on publication trends, prominent countries, institutions, journals, authors, keywords, and citation/Altmetric Attention Scores were obtained. Retractions were detected using standardized queries on PubMed, and data on publication year, retraction year, country, and reasons were obtained.ResultsA total of 26,786 ECMO-related publications were examined. The number of publications showed a significant upward trend (P < 0.001), peaking in 2022 with 240 articles. The United States was the leading country (n = 11,261), followed by Germany (n = 2,349), Italy (n = 1,756), China (n = 1,660), and the United Kingdom (n = 1,642). The ASAIO Journal was the most prolific source (n = 1,204). Harvard Medical School (n = 585) ranked first among institutions. Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19)-related papers dominated citation and altmetric impact, with the 2020 JAMA Wuhan cohort publication receiving the most citations (n = 17,423). Between 2012 and 2024, nine ECMO-related articles were retracted.ConclusionThis analysis highlights the significant global growth of ECMO research, demonstrating considerable academic and social impact, particularly during the COVID-19 pandemic. The results offer valuable insights into publication trends, major contributors, and research visibility, potentially informing future research and partnerships in this field.Keywords: Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation, ECMO Treatment, COVID-19 Pandemic, Bibliometrics, Retraction of Publication

INTRODUCTION

MeSH defines Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (ECMO) as a life support instrument that circulates blood through an oxygenation system, which can involve a pump, membrane oxygenator, and heat exchanger. This method provides temporary cardiopulmonary support in life-threatening circumstances, such as smoke inhalation injury, respiratory failure, and heart failure.1 ECMO has diverse applications across neonatal, pediatric, and adult demographics.2,3 It has been critically utilized in recent years for conditions such as acute respiratory distress syndrome and cardiogenic shock, and notably during the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic.4,5

The increasing implementation of ECMO is reflected in the growing number of publications in the literature. In the last decade, there has been a notable increase in research on ECMO, corresponding with advancements in technology and clinical expertise. This rapid increase is significant for both clinical applications and research productivity, as well as citation trends and the understanding of scientific impact.6,7 At this point, bibliometric analyses offer a means to assess publication trends, prominent countries, institutions, authors, and journals within a specific discipline using objective criteria. In the rapidly evolving and multifaceted domain of ECMO, bibliometric approaches are crucial for identifying research gaps and guiding future investigations.6,8

The rapid growth of scientific publishing has introduced concerns regarding quality and reliability.9 The limited number of retraction cases in ECMO underscores the importance of data reliability, methodological accuracy, and adherence to ethical standards. Various factors, such as plagiarism, methodological inconsistencies, and issues with the peer-review process, can result in considerable ambiguities in the literature. This presents a risk of misleading in both research settings and clinical practice.10,11

This study presents a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of ECMO publications, revealing trends in research output over time, as well as the leading countries, institutions, journals, and authors. Furthermore, by thoroughly reviewing retractions in the ECMO literature, a crucial aspect of scientific reliability has been assessed. Thus, our analysis aims to assist researchers and editors in identifying future research priorities while also illuminating past advancements and the current state of ECMO research.

METHODS

Search setting

The Scopus database was employed to analyze publications in the field of ECMO in this bibliometric study. A search was conducted on August 25, 2025, to generate the publication list. The search term “extracorporeal membrane oxygenation” was employed, and the title, abstract, and/or keywords (TITLE-ABS-KEY) field were selected by the search strategy.12 No timeframe restrictions were implemented; hence, all publications from the earliest available paper (year: 1958) through 2025 were incorporated into the analysis. The study examined only items published in English.13 The restriction was implemented to maintain consistency in indexing and citation tracking, considering that English is the predominant language in major bibliometric databases. All publication types are included.

The ten articles with the highest citation counts were determined. The titles of the publications, their sources, publication years, citation counts, and Altmetric Attention Score (AAS) values were recorded. AAS is a metric that assesses the online prominence and social media interaction of a paper. This score was derived using the Altmetric tool and quantitatively represents the article’s engagement across social media shares, news outlets, blogs, and various online platforms. AAS is utilized to assess the social and academic visibility of the publication beyond conventional citation measures.14,15

Listing of retracted publications

The PubMed was used to identify retracted publications in the field of ECMO. The search was performed using two different queries:

• “retracted publication”[pt] AND “ECMO”

• “retracted publication”[pt] AND “ECMO”

• “retracted publication”[pt] AND “extracorporeal membrane oxygenation”

• “retracted publication”[pt] AND “extracorporeal membrane oxygenation”

Publications obtained using this search strategy were included in the study, and data, including the year of publication, year of retraction, title, source, country, and retraction reasons, were recorded.

The official retraction notices from the respective journals were reviewed for each retracted publication. No additional classification system was used due to the limited number of retracted works in the ECMO field. The arguments in the official notices were recorded verbatim without any modifications.

Analyses and data presentation

The data were presented as numbers (n). The graphs and visualizations were created using Microsoft Excel. The trend in the number of publications over the years was examined using linear regression analysis with the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences version 20.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

RESULTS

Selection procedure

Based on the search settings outlined in the methods section, 28,965 publications were initially identified. Publications in languages other than English were excluded, and one article labeled as 2026 was removed from the study. After the comprehensive procedure, analyses were performed on 26,786 documents.

Publication timeline and trend

The first article was published in 1958, and the current analysis covers the period from 1958 to 2025. The earliest publication retrieved was by Wright et al. (1958) in The Lancet, entitled ‘Apparent Increase in Blood-Prothrombin During Extracorporeal Bypass and During Slow Coagulation in Siliconed Containers’. This investigation concentrated on extracorporeal bypass techniques in the context of open-heart surgery, which are regarded as conceptual precursors of modern ECMO technology rather than ECMO as it is currently defined. Its inclusion in our dataset is indicative of the historical origins of extracorporeal support research. The number of papers reached its peak in 2022 (n = 240). Linear regression analysis revealed a significant upward trend in publication output over the years (R2 = 0.57, P < 0.001) (Fig. 1).

Retracted publications

The first retracted publication in the field of ECMO was Long-term survival after cardiac retransplantation: a single-center experience (International Heart Journal, retraction year 2012), while the most recent retraction involved Altered Antibiotic Pharmacokinetics during Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation: Cause for Renal Function? (American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine, retraction year 2024). A total of 9 ECMO-related publications had been retracted (Table 6). In chronological order, two articles were retracted in 2012, one in 2013, one in 2022, three in 2023, and two in 2024. The reasons for retraction included plagiarism, duplicate publication, ethical concerns, data validity issues, and multiple methodological and reporting problems, such as discrepancies in scope, inappropriate citations, incoherent content, and peer-review manipulation (Table 6).

DISCUSSION

A significant upward trend was observed in the ECMO literature between 1958 and 2025, with the number of publications reaching a peak in 2022. The United States stood out as the most productive country, followed by Germany, Italy, China, and the United Kingdom. The ASAIO Journal was the journal with the most publications, and Brodie D, Combes A, and Lorusso R were among the most prolific authors. Harvard Medical School, AP-HP Assistance Publique – Hôpitaux de Paris, and Boston Children’s Hospital were recorded as the institutions producing the most publications. Citation and AAS data indicate that ECMO studies, particularly during the COVID-19 period, had high academic and online visibility. A total of 9 publications were retracted in the ECMO literature. The first retraction was in 2012, and the most recent retraction was in 2024.

Throughout the examined timeframe (1958–2025), a notable upward trend in the number of publications was observed. The first publication was in 1958, and the number of publications reached a peak in 2022, with 240 documents. This finding underscores the growing intensity of clinical applications and research endeavors in the field of ECMO, as well as the increasing presence of technological advancements in the literature. The rapid rise in recent years particularly indicates the increasing utilization of ECMO in critical and intensive care environments, the advancement of novel devices and methodologies, and the representation of multidisciplinary collaborations in the literature.16 Furthermore, global health crises, such as the COVID-19 pandemic, have significantly heightened interest in ECMO research, facilitating the swift sharing of clinical experiences and the development of new protocols and clinical guidelines tailored to pandemic conditions.17,18

The United States was the predominant country in ECMO literature. Germany, Italy, China, and the United Kingdom were among the prominent contributing countries following the United States. Japan, France, Canada, Australia, and South Korea were among the ten most productive countries. This distribution indicates that ECMO research is primarily concentrated in countries with advanced healthcare systems and robust research infrastructures. The United States' leadership can be attributed to its extensive critical care capacity, investment in ECMO technology, and accumulated clinical expertise.19,20 This circumstance highlights the importance of global research collaboration and technology transfer, as well as the necessity for future funding of ECMO research in various regions.

The journal with the most publications in the ECMO literature was the ASAIO Journal. Perfusion and The Annals of Thoracic Surgery followed this. Additionally, journals such as Critical Care Medicine, Artificial Organs, and Pediatric Critical Care Medicine have made key contributions to the field. According to 2024 CiteScore data, the journals with the highest scores were Intensive Care Medicine (30.9), Critical Care Medicine (14.1), and The Journal of Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery (10.8). The findings suggest that ECMO research primarily centers on intensive care and cardiothoracic surgery, with particular specialized journals serving as the principal sources of literature.21 The prominence of high-impact factor journals underscores the significance of this field for clinical practice and academic visibility. This situation indicates that a central network of journals influences the quality and visibility of ECMO publications.

The most frequently used keywords were “Human,” “Extracorporeal Oxygenation,” and “Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation,” indicating that the ECMO literature is predominantly based on human-centered and clinically-oriented investigations. Furthermore, demographic emphases involving “adult,” “male,” and “female,” as well as methodological definitions such as “retrospective study” and “priority journal,” suggest that the majority of studies were conducted using clinical observations and retrospective designs.

Harvard Medical School was the most prolific institution in ECMO research. It was followed by Assistance Publique - Hôpitaux de Paris and Boston Children’s Hospital, both known for their expertise in ECMO. It was noteworthy that institutions, including the University of Toronto in Canada and Inserm in France, made substantial contributions.

The concentration of centers in the United States, in particular, reaffirms this country's scientific leadership and research capacity in the field of ECMO.22 France’s high ranking with multiple institutions demonstrates the country’s central role in ECMO research in Europe.6 In addition, Canada’s inclusion on the list, alongside the University of Toronto, indicates that there are strong research centers in North America outside the United States.

Our investigation of the most cited ECMO-related papers reveals two unique tendencies. First, the overwhelming presence of COVID-19-related publications among the most cited and influential documents demonstrates the pandemic’s remarkable impact on ECMO research. Publications, including the Wuhan cohort study and remdesivir clinical trials, acquired unparalleled visibility, both throughout the scientific community and in the general public, as evidenced by their very high AAS. This suggests that ECMO-related literature was often cited in the larger context of COVID-19 treatment and intensive care, broadening its appeal beyond typical cardiothoracic and critical care audience.23,24

Secondly, pivotal ECMO-specific trials, such as the CESAR trial (Lancet, 2009) and prior prospective studies (JAMA, 1979), are among the most cited publications, highlighting their critical influence on ECMO practice and clinical standards. The persistent reference to this landmark research highlights its enduring scientific and clinical significance, even amidst a proliferation of pandemic-related literature.

Although the total number of retractions in ECMO-related publications was limited (n = 9), their existence highlights the importance of scientific integrity in a rapidly growing and highly collaborative academic field. The retraction reasons were diverse, encompassing plagiarism, duplication, serious ethical and data validity issues, and manipulation of peer review procedures. Notably, several retractions in recent years (2022–2024) emerged from publications with broad or multidisciplinary focuses, often beyond conventional cardiovascular and critical care domains. This tendency may reflect both the increased pressure to publish ECMO-related papers during and after the COVID-19 pandemic25 and the susceptibility of specific publishing routes to methodological and editorial flaws. Preserving credibility in ECMO research requires rigorous adherence to research ethics and transparency, along with ongoing initiatives by journals, institutions, and researchers to foster robust peer-review mechanisms and prevent scientific misconduct.

This study has several limitations that should be noted. First, the bibliometric study was based solely on the Scopus database; papers indexed exclusively in other databases may have been overlooked, which could affect the comprehensiveness of the results. Secondly, only English-language papers were included, potentially excluding relevant research from non-English-speaking countries. Third, citation counts and AAS were documented at a single time point, and these metrics are flexible and open to temporal variation. The retraction data were confined to PubMed, potentially excluding retractions indexed in other databases. We did not have access to reliable international data on ECMO infrastructure (e.g., number of ECMO devices per country); therefore, we were unable to perform correlation analyses between research output and equipment availability. Future research that combines bibliometric data with metrics related to healthcare infrastructure could yield important insights into the impact of access to ECMO devices on research productivity.

This bibliometric analysis highlights a significant increase in ECMO research from 1958 to 2025. The number of ECMO-related publications has increased significantly in recent years, primarily due to global healthcare challenges, including the COVID-19 pandemic. The United States continues to lead the field, both in terms of publication volume and the presence of prominent research centers. Citation and AAS data indicate that ECMO research had significant academic and social online prominence, particularly during the pandemic. On the other hand, retractions, however few in number, underscore the significance of scientific ethics and methodological accuracy. These findings offer valuable insights that will guide future research in ECMO studies, both to enhance academic visibility and maintain scientific credibility. The COVID-19 pandemic has brought ECMO to the forefront of treatment options on a global scale. In this context, survey-based studies on ECMO are necessary to gain a deeper understanding of existing practices, priorities, and the perspectives of various stakeholders.

In addition to assessing research productivity, bibliometric and retraction analyses offer significant practical implications. Funding organizations can utilize these insights to prioritize underrepresented regions or topics, thus promoting a more equitable global research landscape. Institutions and researchers can find prospective partners through publication and citation connections, therefore improving collaborative research and information dissemination. Journal editors and publishers may utilize retraction patterns to enhance peer-review procedures, augment editorial supervision, and preserve scientific integrity. Thus, our findings may function not only as a descriptive analysis of ECMO research but also as a resource to guide strategic decisions in research financing, partnership enhancement, and editorial policy formulation.
