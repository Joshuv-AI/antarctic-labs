On the Minimax Bifurcation Formula Journal: Journal of Differential Equations
Y. Sh. Il’yasov

Abstract
We develop a variational minimax method for detecting maximal saddle-node bifurcations in abstract nonlinear equations. Unlike continuation and path-following techniques, the method identifies the critical parameter directly as an extremal value of an extended Rayleigh quotient. We prove an abstract minimax bifurcation principle, establish the existence and characterization of weak saddle-node bifurcation points, and justify finite-dimensional Galerkin approximations. We also obtain perturbation estimates for the bifurcation value. Applications to non-variational systems of nonlinear elliptic equations show that the approach is not restricted to classical variational structures. The resulting framework provides a unified tool for detecting, approximating, and analyzing saddle-node bifurcations.

Introduction

Saddle-node bifurcations, also known as folds or turning points, are among the
basic mechanisms by which solution branches of nonlinear equations appear or
disappear. In nonlinear differential equations they often mark critical
parameter values beyond which no solution exists. Locating such values is
therefore a central problem both in analysis and in numerical computation.

The standard way to detect saddle-node bifurcations is to continue a branch of
solutions and monitor the loss of invertibility of the linearized operator. This
is the basis of classical local and global bifurcation theory, including the
Crandall–Rabinowitz theorem, Rabinowitz’s global theorem, and the
Krasnosel’skiĭ theory, as well as of numerical continuation methods

[
15
,
29
,
31
,
33
,
38
,
36
,
32
]
. These methods
are powerful, but they are essentially indirect: the bifurcation point is
detected through the behaviour of a solution curve.

The aim of this paper is to develop a different approach. We identify maximal
weak saddle-node bifurcation points directly, by means of a minimax variational
formula. The method does not require one to construct or follow a solution
branch. Instead, the critical parameter is characterized as an extremal value of
an extended Rayleigh quotient. This provides both an analytical tool for proving
the existence of singular solutions and a finite-dimensional scheme suitable for
Galerkin and finite-element approximation.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{o}\subset W
be a prescribed cone of admissible
functions in a Banach space
W
. We consider nonlinear equations of the
form

\mathcal{F}(u,\lambda):=\mathcal{A}(u)-\lambda\mathcal{G}(u)=0,\qquad u\in\mathcal{S}^{o},

(1.1)

where
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{G}:W\to W^{*}
are continuously Fréchet
differentiable in an open neighbourhood of
\mathcal{S}^{o}
.

The central object of the paper is the
extended Rayleigh quotient

[
21
,
25
]

\mathcal{R}(u,v):=\frac{\langle\mathcal{A}(u),v\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{G}(u),v\rangle},\qquad(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}^{o}\times\mathcal{S}^{o},

(1.2)

where
\langle\mathcal{G}(u),v\rangle\neq 0
for all

(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}^{o}\times\mathcal{S}^{o}
.
Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between

W^{*}
and
W
.

The basic observation is that stationary points of
\mathcal{R}
encode
singular solutions of (
2.1
). Indeed, assume that

\lambda^{*}=\mathcal{R}(u^{*},v^{*}),\qquad D_{v}\mathcal{R}(u^{*},v^{*})=0,\qquad D_{u}\mathcal{R}(u^{*},v^{*})=0.

The first stationarity condition gives

\big\langle\mathcal{A}(u^{*})-\lambda^{*}\mathcal{G}(u^{*}),v\big\rangle=0\qquad\forall v\in\mathcal{S}^{o}.

Since
\mathcal{S}^{o}
is assumed to generate
W
, this implies

\mathcal{F}(u^{*},\lambda^{*})=0
in
W^{*}
. The second stationarity condition
yields

\big\langle D_{u}\mathcal{F}(u^{*},\lambda^{*})\varphi,v^{*}\big\rangle=0\qquad\forall\varphi\in W.

Thus
v^{*}
is a weak adjoint eigenvector of the linearized operator. In
particular, under the usual Fredholm alternative, the linearized operator is
singular, and
u^{*}
is a singular solution of (
2.1
).

We call
(u^{*},\lambda^{*})
a
weak saddle-node bifurcation point

of (
2.1
) if, in addition, there exist
\varepsilon>0
and a
neighbourhood
V
of
u^{*}
in
W
such that (
2.1
) has no
solutions in
\mathcal{S}^{o}\cap V
for

\lambda\in(\lambda^{*},\lambda^{*}+\varepsilon)
.

Our approach to weak saddle-node bifurcation points in
\mathcal{S}^{o}

is based on the following minimax bifurcation formula:

\lambda^{*}:=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v).

(1.3)

We call
\lambda^{*}
the
extreme value
of (
2.1
) in

\mathcal{S}^{o}
. If
\lambda^{*}<+\infty
, then (
2.1
) has no
solutions in
\mathcal{S}^{o}
for any
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
. Indeed, if

u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}
solves (
2.1
) for some
\lambda
, then

\mathcal{R}(u,v)=\lambda\qquad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{S}^{o},

and therefore

\lambda=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v)\leq\sup_{w\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(w,v)=\lambda^{*}.

Thus (
1.3
) gives a direct variational candidate for the maximal
parameter value at which solutions can exist.

A pair
(u^{*},v^{*})\in\mathcal{S}^{o}\times(\mathcal{S}\setminus\{0\})
is said
to realize the minimax bifurcation formula if

-\infty<\lambda^{*}<+\infty

and

\lambda^{*}=\mathcal{R}(u^{*},v^{*})=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u^{*},v)=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v),

(1.4)

with

D_{u}\mathcal{R}(u^{*},v^{*})=0,\qquad D_{v}\mathcal{R}(u^{*},v^{*})=0.

(1.5)

If, in addition,
v^{*}\in\mathcal{S}^{o}
, then the infimum in (
1.4
)
is attained and may be written as a minimum.

The main results of the paper show that, under suitable compactness,
positivity, and finite-dimensional approximation assumptions, such a
minimax solution yields a
maximal weak saddle-node bifurcation point

of (
2.1
).

This approach was initiated in

[
20
]
–
[
27
]
and developed in several directions,
including applications to nonlinear equations, gradient-type numerical methods,
non-variational systems, and eigenvalue perturbation problems

[
22
,
23
,

,

,
26
,
37
]
.
The present paper gives a unified abstract framework for these ideas. Its
novel point is the combination of three ingredients: the two-variable extended
Rayleigh quotient, the minimax bifurcation formula, and a finite-element
approximation principle for detecting singular solutions.

The paper focuses on three issues: direct identification of maximal weak
saddle-node bifurcation points, convergence of finite-dimensional
approximations, and perturbation estimates for the bifurcation value.

,

, and

2
Minimax bifurcation formula for an abstract problem

Let
W
be a reflexive Banach space and let
\mathcal{C}^{0}
be an ordered
function space continuously embedded into
W
. Let
\mathcal{S}^{o}\subset\mathcal{C}^{0}
be the strict part of a convex cone, and set

\mathcal{S}:=\overline{\mathcal{S}^{o}}^{\,\mathcal{C}^{0}}.

Thus
\mathcal{S}
is the corresponding closed convex cone in

\mathcal{C}^{0}
. We assume that
\mathcal{S}
is weakly sequentially
closed in
W
. We also assume that
\mathcal{S}^{o}
generates
W
, that is,

\operatorname{span}\bigl(\mathcal{S}^{o}\cup(-\mathcal{S}^{o})\bigr)=W.

Whenever the quotient
\mathcal{R}(u,v)
is evaluated on
\mathcal{S}
, the
zero element is understood to be excluded. In particular,

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{R}(u,v)\quad\text{means}\quad\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\{0\}}\mathcal{R}(u,v).

We consider the abstract equation

\mathcal{F}(u,\lambda):=\mathcal{A}(u)-\lambda\mathcal{G}(u)=0,\qquad u\in\mathcal{S}^{o},

(2.1)

where
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{G}:\mathcal{S}\to W^{*}
. We assume that, in a

W
-neighbourhood of
\mathcal{S}^{o}
, these maps admit continuously
Fréchet differentiable extensions. The associated extended Rayleigh quotient is

\mathcal{R}(u,v):=\frac{\langle\mathcal{A}(u),v\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{G}(u),v\rangle},\qquad\langle\mathcal{G}(u),v\rangle\neq 0.

(2.2)

We impose the following
denominator positivity and monotonicity condition
.

(D)

For every

(u,v)\in(\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}^{o})\cup(\mathcal{S}^{o}\times\mathcal{S}),

with the above convention on the zero element, one has

\langle\mathcal{G}(u),v\rangle>0.

Moreover,

\langle D_{u}\mathcal{G}(u)w,v\rangle\geq 0\qquad u\in\mathcal{S}^{o},\quad v,w\in\mathcal{S}.

Here and below,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing
between
W^{*}
and
W
. Condition
{\rm(D)}
ensures that
\mathcal{R}

is well defined on

(\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}^{o})\cup(\mathcal{S}^{o}\times\mathcal{S}),

with the zero element excluded whenever
\mathcal{R}
is evaluated on

\mathcal{S}
. For fixed
u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}
, the map

v\mapsto\mathcal{R}(u,v)
is weakly continuous on
\mathcal{S}
; for fixed

v\in\mathcal{S}
, the map
u\mapsto\mathcal{R}(u,v)
is
C^{1}
on

\mathcal{S}^{o}
.

We study singular and bifurcation points of (
2.1
) in
\mathcal{S}^{o}

through the
minimax bifurcation formula

\lambda^{*}:=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v).

(2.3)

Let
\{W_{r}\}_{r=1}^{\infty}
be finite-dimensional subspaces of
W
, with

\dim W_{r}=r
, and let
\mathcal{I}_{r}:\mathcal{C}^{0}\to W_{r}
be continuous
maps.

We first impose
Galerkin approximation assumptions
on the cone.

(P1)

For every
r\geq 1
, there exists a basis

\{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{r}\}
of
W_{r}
such that

\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}:=\mathcal{I}_{r}\mathcal{S}^{o}=\left\{u=\sum_{i=1}^{r}u^{i}\eta_{i}\in W_{r}:u^{i}>0,\ i=1,\ldots,r\right\}.

We set

\mathcal{S}_{r}:=\overline{\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}}^{\,W_{r}}

and assume that

\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}\subset\mathcal{S}^{o}
,

\mathcal{S}_{r}\subset\mathcal{S}
.

(P2)

For every
u\in\mathcal{S}
,

\|\mathcal{I}_{r}u-u\|_{W}\to 0
as
r\to\infty
.

The Galerkin approximation of (
2.1
) is

\langle\mathcal{F}(u,\lambda),\zeta\rangle=0\qquad\forall\zeta\in W_{r},\quad u\in W_{r}.

(2.4)

For
r=1,2,\ldots
, define

\lambda_{r}^{*}:=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v).

(2.5)

For every
r\geq 1
, set

\lambda_{r}(u):=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v),\qquad u\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}.

We next impose
structural assumptions on the Galerkin minimax problems

associated with equation (
2.1
).

(H1)

For every
r\geq 1
,

\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}}\lambda_{r}(u)>0.

Moreover, for every

\varepsilon>0
, the set

K_{r,\varepsilon}:=\{u\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}:\lambda_{r}(u)\geq\varepsilon\}

is relatively compact in
\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
.

(H2)

For every
r\geq 1
, if
u\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
,
v\in\mathcal{S}_{r}
,
and

D_{u}\langle\mathcal{A}(u),v\rangle(\psi)\geq 0\qquad\forall\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{r},

then
v\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
.

Remark 2.1
.

By
{\rm(D)}
, for every fixed
v\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
, the map

u\mapsto\mathcal{R}(u,v)
is continuous on
\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
. Hence

\lambda_{r}(\cdot)
, as the infimum of continuous functions, is upper semicontinuous.
Therefore
{\rm(H1)}
implies that
\lambda_{r}(\cdot)
attains its positive maximum
in
\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The extended Rayleigh quotient
\mathcal{R}
is said to satisfy the

(PS)_{e}
-condition at level
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
if every sequence

(u_{n},v_{n})\in\mathcal{S}_{n}^{o}\times\mathcal{S}_{n}^{o}
such that

\begin{cases}\mathcal{R}(u_{n},v_{n})\to\lambda,\\
D_{u}\mathcal{R}(u_{n},v_{n})(\xi)=0,\qquad\forall\xi\in W_{n},\\
D_{v}\mathcal{R}(u_{n},v_{n})(\zeta)=0,\qquad\forall\zeta\in W_{n},\end{cases}

(2.6)

has a subsequence and positive numbers
t_{n}>0
such that, after replacing

v_{n}
by
t_{n}v_{n}
, still denoted by
v_{n}
, one has

u_{n}\to\bar{u}\quad\text{strongly in }W,\qquad v_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{v}\quad\text{weakly in }W,

with
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{o}
and

\bar{v}\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\{0\}
. Such a sequence is called a

(PS)_{e}
-sequence.

This rescaling in the second component is natural, since
\mathcal{R}
is
homogeneous with respect to
v
.

In our first main result, we obtain singular solutions of (
2.1
) as
limits of finite-dimensional maximal weak saddle-node bifurcation points.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that the denominator positivity and monotonicity condition

{\rm(D)}
, the Galerkin cone realization assumption
{\rm(P1)}
,
and the structural assumptions on the Galerkin minimax problems

{\rm(H1)}
–
{\rm(H2)}
hold. Suppose also that

0<\lambda_{r}^{*}<+\infty
for all
r\geq 1
.

(1^{\circ})

For every
r\geq 1
, there exist

u_{r}^{*},v_{r}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
such that

\lambda_{r}^{*}=\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},v_{r}^{*})=\min_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},v)=\max_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}}\min_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v).

(2.7)

Moreover,

\langle\mathcal{F}(u_{r}^{*},\lambda_{r}^{*}),\zeta\rangle=0\quad\forall\zeta\in W_{r},\qquad\bigl\langle D_{u}\mathcal{F}(u_{r}^{*},\lambda_{r}^{*})\xi,v_{r}^{*}\bigr\rangle=0\quad\forall\xi\in W_{r}.

Thus
(u_{r}^{*},\lambda_{r}^{*})
is a finite-dimensional weak singular
point; in the terminology of this paper, it is a maximal weak
saddle-node bifurcation point of the Galerkin problem.

(2^{\circ})

Assume, in addition, that the approximation assumption
{\rm(P2)}

holds, and that there
exist
0<a<b<+\infty
and
M\geq 1
such that

\lambda_{r}^{*}\in(a,b)\qquad\text{for all }r\geq M.

Suppose also that
\mathcal{R}
satisfies the
(PS)_{e}
-condition at
every level
\lambda\in[a,b]
. Then, up to a subsequence and a possible
positive rescaling of
v_{r}^{*}
,

\lambda_{r}^{*}\to\hat{\lambda}^{*}\in[a,b],\qquad u_{r}^{*}\to u^{*}\quad\text{strongly in }W,\qquad v_{r}^{*}\rightharpoonup v^{*}\quad\text{weakly in }W,

where
u^{*}\in\mathcal{S}^{o}
and

v^{*}\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\{0\}
. Moreover,

\mathcal{F}(u^{*},\hat{\lambda}^{*})=0\quad\text{in }W^{*},\qquad\bigl\langle D_{u}\mathcal{F}(u^{*},\hat{\lambda}^{*})\xi,v^{*}\bigr\rangle=0\quad\forall\xi\in W.

If
\lambda^{*}<+\infty
, then
\hat{\lambda}^{*}\leq\lambda^{*}
.

For the convergence of the minimax levels we use the following

uniform Rayleigh approximation condition
in the first variable.

(U)

For every
u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}
,

\sup_{v\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\left|\mathcal{R}(u,v)-\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}_{r}u,v)\right|\to 0\qquad\text{as }r\to\infty.

The following theorem proves the existence of a maximal weak saddle-node
bifurcation point and thereby justifies the minimax bifurcation formula
(
2.3
) as a direct variational characterization of the
maximal parameter value.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that the assumptions of
(1^{\circ})
in Theorem
2.1
are
satisfied. Assume, in addition, that the cone approximation assumption

{\rm(P2)}
and the uniform Rayleigh approximation condition
{\rm(U)}

hold. Suppose also that there exist
0<a<b<+\infty
and
M\geq 1
such that

\lambda^{*}\in(a,b),\qquad\lambda_{r}^{*}\in(a,b)\quad\text{for all }r\geq M,

and that
\mathcal{R}
satisfies the
(PS)_{e}
-condition at every level

\lambda\in[a,b]
. Then equation (
2.1
) has a maximal weak saddle-node
bifurcation point
(u^{*},\lambda^{*})\in\mathcal{S}^{o}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Moreover, there exists
v^{*}\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\lambda^{*}=\mathcal{R}(u^{*},v^{*})=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u^{*},v).

(2.8)

In particular,
\mathcal{F}(u^{*},\lambda^{*})=0
in
W^{*}
and

\bigl\langle D_{u}\mathcal{F}(u^{*},\lambda^{*})\xi,v^{*}\bigr\rangle=0

for all
\xi\in W
.

Proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We prove the two assertions separately.

Proof of
(1^{\circ})
.

Fix
r\geq 1
and set

\lambda_{r}(u):=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v)
,

u\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
. By
{\rm(D)}
, the map

u\mapsto\mathcal{R}(u,v)
is continuous for each fixed

v\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
; hence
\lambda_{r}
is upper semicontinuous.

Let
(u_{n})\subset\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
be a maximizing sequence for

\lambda_{r}^{*}
. Since
0<\lambda_{r}^{*}<+\infty
, we may choose

\varepsilon>0
such that
0<\varepsilon<\lambda_{r}^{*}
. Then, for all
large
n
,
u_{n}\in K_{r,\varepsilon}
. By
{\rm(H1)}
, after passing
to a subsequence,
u_{n}\to u_{r}^{*}
in
W_{r}
, with

u_{r}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
. The upper semicontinuity of
\lambda_{r}
gives

\lambda_{r}^{*}=\lambda_{r}(u_{r}^{*})=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},v).

By
{\rm(P1)}
, if
\{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{r}\}
is the positive basis of

\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
, then

\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}=\left\{v=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v^{i}\eta_{i}:\ v^{i}>0\right\}.

Since
\mathcal{R}(u,\cdot)
is homogeneous of degree zero and the
denominator is positive, it is easy to see that

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v)=\min_{1\leq i\leq r}\mathcal{R}(u,\eta_{i}).

Thus
(u_{r}^{*},\lambda_{r}^{*})
maximizes
\lambda
under the inequalities

\mathcal{R}(u,\eta_{i})-\lambda\geq 0
,
i=1,\ldots,r
.

Since
u_{r}^{*}
is an interior point of
\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
, the Fritz John
multiplier rule yields numbers
\mu_{i}\geq 0
,
i=1,\ldots,r
, with

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{i}=1
, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{i}D_{u}\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},\eta_{i})=0,\qquad\mu_{i}\bigl(\lambda_{r}^{*}-\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},\eta_{i})\bigr)=0.

For active indices,
\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},\eta_{i})=\lambda_{r}^{*}
. Define

\kappa_{i}:=\frac{\mu_{i}}{\langle\mathcal{G}(u_{r}^{*}),\eta_{i}\rangle},\qquad v_{r}^{*}:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\kappa_{i}\eta_{i}.

Then
v_{r}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{r}\setminus\{0\}
, and the complementarity
relations immediately give

\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},v_{r}^{*})=\lambda_{r}^{*},\qquad\bigl\langle D_{u}\mathcal{F}(u_{r}^{*},\lambda_{r}^{*})\xi,v_{r}^{*}\bigr\rangle=0\quad\forall\xi\in W_{r}.

It remains only to see that
v_{r}^{*}
actually belongs to

\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
. From the last identity we have, for every

\psi\in W_{r}
,

D_{u}\langle\mathcal{A}(u_{r}^{*}),v_{r}^{*}\rangle(\psi)=\lambda_{r}^{*}D_{u}\langle\mathcal{G}(u_{r}^{*}),v_{r}^{*}\rangle(\psi).

Since
\lambda_{r}^{*}>0
, condition
{\rm(D)}
implies that the right-hand
side is nonnegative for every
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{r}
. Hence
{\rm(H2)}

gives
v_{r}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
.

Thus
v_{r}^{*}
is an interior minimizer of

v\mapsto\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},v)
, and therefore

D_{v}\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},v_{r}^{*})=0
on
W_{r}
. Equivalently,

\langle\mathcal{F}(u_{r}^{*},\lambda_{r}^{*}),\zeta\rangle=0\quad\forall\zeta\in W_{r}.

Together with the adjoint relation above, this proves
(1^{\circ})
.

Proof of
(2^{\circ})
.

By
(1^{\circ})
, for every
r\geq M
there exist

u_{r}^{*},v_{r}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}
satisfying

\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},v_{r}^{*})=\lambda_{r}^{*},\qquad D_{u}\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},v_{r}^{*})=0,\quad D_{v}\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},v_{r}^{*})=0\quad\text{on }W_{r}.

Since
\lambda_{r}^{*}\in(a,b)
, passing to a subsequence if necessary, we have

\lambda_{r}^{*}\to\hat{\lambda}^{*}\in[a,b]
. Hence

(u_{r}^{*},v_{r}^{*})
is a
(PS)_{e}
-sequence at level
\hat{\lambda}^{*}
. By the

(PS)_{e}
-condition, after passing to a further subsequence and, if needed,
after a positive rescaling of
v_{r}^{*}
, we obtain

u_{r}^{*}\to u^{*}\quad\text{strongly in }W,\qquad v_{r}^{*}\rightharpoonup v^{*}\quad\text{weakly in }W,

where
u^{*}\in\mathcal{S}^{o}
and
v^{*}\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\{0\}
.

We now pass to the limit in the Galerkin equations. Let

\zeta\in\mathcal{S}
. By
{\rm(P2)}
,

\mathcal{I}_{r}\zeta\to\zeta
in
W
, and

\mathcal{I}_{r}\zeta\in W_{r}
. Therefore

0=\langle\mathcal{F}(u_{r}^{*},\lambda_{r}^{*}),\mathcal{I}_{r}\zeta\rangle\to\langle\mathcal{F}(u^{*},\hat{\lambda}^{*}),\zeta\rangle.

Since
\mathcal{S}^{o}
generates
W
, this identity extends by linearity to
all
\zeta\in W
. Hence

\mathcal{F}(u^{*},\hat{\lambda}^{*})=0\quad\text{in }W^{*}.

The adjoint singularity relation is obtained in the same way. For

\xi\in\mathcal{S}
, using
\mathcal{I}_{r}\xi\in W_{r}
, we have

0=\bigl\langle D_{u}\mathcal{F}(u_{r}^{*},\lambda_{r}^{*})\mathcal{I}_{r}\xi,v_{r}^{*}\bigr\rangle.

Since
\mathcal{I}_{r}\xi\to\xi
strongly in
W
,
u_{r}^{*}\to u^{*}
strongly,

\lambda_{r}^{*}\to\hat{\lambda}^{*}
, and
v_{r}^{*}\rightharpoonup v^{*}
weakly, we get

\bigl\langle D_{u}\mathcal{F}(u^{*},\hat{\lambda}^{*})\xi,v^{*}\bigr\rangle=0.

Again, by the generating property of
\mathcal{S}^{o}
, this holds for all

\xi\in W
. Thus
(u^{*},\hat{\lambda}^{*})
is a weak singular solution of
(
2.1
).

It remains to compare
\hat{\lambda}^{*}
with
\lambda^{*}
. For any

v\in\mathcal{S}^{o}
, since
\mathcal{I}_{r}v\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}

4
Existence of singular solutions for a system of elliptic equations

We consider the system

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}^{k}u^{k}-f^{k}(x,u)&=\lambda g^{k}(x,u),&&x\in\Omega,\\
u^{k}&\geq 0,&&x\in\Omega,\\
u^{k}|_{\partial\Omega}&=0,&&k=1,\ldots,m.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

Here
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, is a polyhedron,

u=(u^{1},\ldots,u^{m})
, and

\mathcal{L}^{k}u^{k}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\sigma_{ij}^{k}(x)\frac{\partial u^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)+c^{k}(x)u^{k},\qquad k=1,\ldots,m,

where repeated indices
i,j
are summed from
1
to
d
. We assume that

\sigma_{ij}^{k},c^{k}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and that each
\mathcal{L}^{k}

satisfies the ellipticity condition (
9.2
).

We write
\mathbb{W}:=(W_{2})^{m}
,
\mathbb{L}^{p}:=(L^{p})^{m}
, and use the
notation

\mathcal{L}:=(\mathcal{L}^{1},\ldots,\mathcal{L}^{m})^{T},\qquad f(u):=(f^{1}(\cdot,u),\ldots,f^{m}(\cdot,u))^{T},\qquad g(u):=(g^{1}(\cdot,u),\ldots,g^{m}(\cdot,u))^{T}.

Furthermore,

a_{m}(u,v):=\sum_{k=1}^{m}a^{k}(u^{k},v^{k}),\qquad a^{k}(u^{k},v^{k}):=\langle\mathcal{L}^{k}u^{k},v^{k}\rangle,

and

\mathcal{F}(u,\lambda):=\mathcal{L}u-f(u)-\lambda g(u).

For
t=(t^{1},\ldots,t^{m})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
, we impose the following
assumptions.

{\rm(h_{1})}

There exist
a_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
a_{0},a_{1}>0
, and
0<q<1

such that
0<a_{0}\leq a_{k}(x)\leq a_{1}
a.e. in
\Omega
, and

g^{k}(x,t)=a_{k}(x)(t^{k})^{q}.

In particular,

\frac{\partial g^{k}}{\partial t^{l}}=0\quad(l\neq k),\qquad t^{k}\frac{\partial g^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}=qg^{k}.

{\rm(h_{2})}

The functions
f^{k}
are nonnegative and continuous on

\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
,
C^{1}
with respect to

t\in(0,+\infty)^{m}
, and
f^{k}(x,t)>0
for

t\in(0,+\infty)^{m}
. Moreover, for some
C>0
and

1<\gamma_{0}\leq\gamma<2^{*}-1
,

0\leq f^{k}(x,t)\leq C\bigl(|t|^{\gamma_{0}}+|t|^{\gamma}\bigr),\qquad\left|\frac{\partial f^{k}}{\partial t^{l}}(x,t)\right|\leq C\bigl(|t|^{\gamma_{0}-1}+|t|^{\gamma-1}\bigr).

{\rm(h_{3})}

The system is cooperative:

\frac{\partial f^{k}}{\partial t^{l}}(x,t)\geq 0,\qquad k,l=1,\ldots,m,\qquad t\in(0,+\infty)^{m}.

{\rm(h_{4})}

There exists
\theta>1
, with
1<\theta<\gamma_{0}
, such that

t^{k}\frac{\partial f^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}(x,t)\geq\theta f^{k}(x,t),\qquad k=1,\ldots,m.

{\rm(h_{5})}

The reaction terms are degenerate on the boundary of the positive cone:
if
t^{j}=0
for at least one
j
, then

f^{k}(x,t)=0,\qquad\frac{\partial f^{k}}{\partial t^{l}}(x,t)=0,\qquad k,l=1,\ldots,m.

A model example is

g^{k}(x,t)=a_{k}(x)(t^{k})^{q},\qquad f^{k}(x,t)=b_{k}(x)\prod_{j=1}^{m}(t^{j})^{\alpha_{kj}},

where

,
0<b_{0}\leq b_{k}(x)\leq b_{1}
,

,

, and

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{kj}<2^{*}-1.

Then

–
{\rm(h_{5})}
hold, with any

We work in the cone
\mathbb{S}^{o}:=(S^{o})^{m}\subset\mathbb{W}

is defined in Appendix A. Let

.

–

, the quotient
\mathcal{R}
satisfies

{\rm(H1)}
and
{\rm(H2)}
on the finite-dimensional cones. By
Lemma
9.1
, these cones satisfy the Galerkin approximation
assumptions. Finally, Lemma
4.3
gives the normalized

(PS)_{e}
-condition at every positive level.

Theorem
2.1
therefore applies. Hence, for each
r
, there exist

u_{r}^{*},v_{r}^{*}\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
such that

\lambda_{r}^{*}=\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},v_{r}^{*})=\min_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},v)=\max_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}}\min_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v).

Moreover, after passing to a subsequence and allowing the admissible
positive rescaling of
v_{r}^{*}
, we have

\lambda_{r}^{*}\to\hat{\lambda}^{*}\in[\mu^{-1},\mu]
,

u_{r}^{*}\to u^{*}
strongly in
\mathbb{W}
, and

v_{r}^{*}\rightharpoonup v^{*}
weakly in
\mathbb{W}
, where

u^{*}\in\mathbb{S}^{o}
and
v^{*}\in\mathbb{S}\setminus\{0\}
. Passing to the
limit in the Galerkin singularity relations gives

\mathcal{F}(u^{*},\hat{\lambda}^{*})=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{W}^{*},\qquad\bigl\langle D_{u}\mathcal{F}(u^{*},\hat{\lambda}^{*})\xi,v^{*}\bigr\rangle=0\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{W}.

Thus
(u^{*},\hat{\lambda}^{*})
is a weak singular solution of (
5.1
).
∎

5
Existence of singular solutions for elliptic systems

We consider the system

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}^{k}u^{k}-f^{k}(x,u)&=\lambda g^{k}(x,u),&&x\in\Omega,\\
u^{k}&\geq 0,&&x\in\Omega,\\
u^{k}|_{\partial\Omega}&=0,&&k=1,\ldots,m,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, is a polyhedron and

\mathcal{L}^{k}u^{k}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\sigma_{ij}^{k}(x)\frac{\partial u^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)+c^{k}(x)u^{k}.

Here and below, repeated indices
i,j
are summed from
1
to
d
.
We assume that

\sigma_{ij}^{k},c^{k}\in C(\overline{\Omega})

and that the coefficients satisfy the ellipticity condition
(
9.2
). We write

u=(u^{1},\ldots,u^{m}),\qquad\mathcal{L}=(\mathcal{L}^{1},\ldots,\mathcal{L}^{m})^{T},

f(u)=(f^{1}(\cdot,u),\ldots,f^{m}(\cdot,u))^{T},\qquad g(u)=(g^{1}(\cdot,u),\ldots,g^{m}(\cdot,u))^{T},

and

\mathcal{F}(u,\lambda)=\mathcal{L}u-f(u)-\lambda g(u).

Let

\mathbb{W}:=(W_{2})^{m},\qquad\mathbb{L}^{p}:=(L^{p})^{m},

and define

a_{m}(u,v):=\sum_{k=1}^{m}a^{k}(u^{k},v^{k}),\qquad a^{k}(u^{k},v^{k}):=\langle\mathcal{L}^{k}u^{k},v^{k}\rangle,

\langle u,v\rangle_{m}:=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\langle u^{k},v^{k}\rangle.

The positive cone is denoted by

\mathbb{S}^{o}:=(S^{o})^{m},

where
S^{o}
is defined in Appendix A.

For
t=(t^{1},\ldots,t^{m})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
, we impose the following
assumptions.

{\rm(h_{1})}

The parameter term is diagonal and sublinear:

g^{k}(x,t)=a_{k}(x)(t^{k})^{q},\qquad 0<q<1,

where
a_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and

0<a_{0}\leq a_{k}(x)\leq a_{1}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

In particular,

\frac{\partial g^{k}}{\partial t^{l}}=0\quad(l\neq k),\qquad t^{k}\frac{\partial g^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}=qg^{k}.

{\rm(h_{2})}

The functions
f^{k}
are nonnegative, continuous on

\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
, and
C^{1}
with respect to

t\in(0,\infty)^{m}
. Moreover,

f^{k}(x,t)>0\quad\text{for }t\in(0,\infty)^{m},

and there exist constants
C>0
and

1<\gamma_{0}\leq\gamma<2^{*}-1

such that

0\leq f^{k}(x,t)\leq C\bigl(|t|^{\gamma_{0}}+|t|^{\gamma}\bigr),

and

0\leq\frac{\partial f^{k}}{\partial t^{l}}(x,t)\leq C\bigl(|t|^{\gamma_{0}-1}+|t|^{\gamma-1}\bigr),\qquad l=1,\ldots,m.

{\rm(h_{3})}

The system is cooperative:

\frac{\partial f^{k}}{\partial t^{l}}(x,t)\geq 0,\qquad k,l=1,\ldots,m.

{\rm(h_{4})}

There exists
\theta>1
,
1<\theta<\gamma_{0}
, such that

t^{k}\frac{\partial f^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}(x,t)\geq\theta f^{k}(x,t),\qquad k=1,\ldots,m.

{\rm(h_{5})}

The nonlinearities are degenerate on the boundary of the positive cone:
if
t^{j}=0
for at least one
j
, then

f^{k}(x,t)=0,\qquad\frac{\partial f^{k}}{\partial t^{l}}(x,t)=0,\qquad k,l=1,\ldots,m,

where the derivatives are understood as their continuous extensions to the
boundary whenever needed.

Example 1
.

A typical class satisfying
{\rm(h_{1})}
–
{\rm(h_{5})}
is

g^{k}(x,t)=a_{k}(x)(t^{k})^{q},\qquad 0<q<1,

with
0<a_{0}\leq a_{k}(x)\leq a_{1}
, and

f^{k}(x,t)=b_{k}(x)\prod_{j=1}^{m}(t^{j})^{\alpha_{kj}},

where

0<b_{0}\leq b_{k}(x)\leq b_{1},\qquad\alpha_{kj}>1,\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{kj}<2^{*}-1.

Then
f^{k}
is cooperative and vanishes, together with its first derivatives,
on the boundary of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
. Moreover,

t^{k}\frac{\partial f^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}=\alpha_{kk}f^{k},

so
{\rm(h_{4})}
holds with any

1<\theta<\min_{1\leq k\leq m}\alpha_{kk}.

One may take

\gamma_{0}=\min_{1\leq k\leq m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{kj},\qquad\gamma=\max_{1\leq k\leq m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{kj}.

Let
\{\mathcal{T}_{r}\}_{r\geq 1}
be a shape-regular family of meshes
satisfying the discrete maximum principle, and let

\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
be the corresponding nodal basis of
W_{r}
. For

k=1,\ldots,m
, let
A_{r}^{k}
be the stiffness matrix

(A_{r}^{k})_{ij}=\langle\mathcal{L}^{k}\psi_{i},\psi_{j}\rangle.

By Appendix A,
A_{r}^{k}
is an
M
-matrix and satisfies (
9.4
).

We set

\mathbb{W}_{r}:=W_{r}^{m},\qquad\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}:=(S_{r}^{o})^{m}.

Equivalently,

\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}=\left\{u=\sum_{i=1}^{rm}u^{i}\bar{\psi}_{i}\in\mathbb{W}_{r}:u^{i}>0,\ i=1,\ldots,rm\right\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{o},

where
\{\bar{\psi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{rm}
is the natural componentwise nodal basis
of
\mathbb{W}_{r}
. We also put

\mathbb{S}_{r}:=\overline{\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}}^{\,\mathbb{W}_{r}}\setminus\{0\}\subset\mathbb{S}.

We define the extended Rayleigh quotient

\mathcal{R}(u,v):=\frac{a_{m}(u,v)-\langle f(u),v\rangle_{m}}{\langle g(u),v\rangle_{m}},\qquad(u,v)\in(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{S}^{o})\cup(\mathbb{S}^{o}\times\mathbb{S}).

(5.2)

By
{\rm(h_{1})}
, the denominator is strictly positive on this set.
Moreover, by
{\rm(h_{2})}
and Proposition
10.1
in
Appendix B, the quotient
\mathcal{R}
satisfies condition
{\rm(D)}
.

We consider the minimax values

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{o}}\inf_{v\in\mathbb{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v),

(5.3)

\displaystyle\lambda_{r}^{*}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}}\inf_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v),\qquad r=1,2,\ldots.

(5.4)

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
\lambda^{*}<+\infty
. Then system (
5.1
) has no positive
solution
u\in\mathbb{S}^{o}
for any
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
.

Proof.

If
u\in\mathbb{S}^{o}
solves (
5.1
), then, for every

v\in\mathbb{S}^{o}
,

a_{m}(u,v)-\langle f(u),v\rangle_{m}=\lambda\langle g(u),v\rangle_{m}.

Hence

\mathcal{R}(u,v)=\lambda\qquad\forall v\in\mathbb{S}^{o}.

Therefore

\lambda=\inf_{v\in\mathbb{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v)\leq\sup_{w\in\mathbb{S}^{o}}\inf_{v\in\mathbb{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(w,v)=\lambda^{*}.

Thus no positive solution exists for
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Assume that
{\rm(h_{1})}
–
{\rm(h_{5})}
hold. Assume also that the
finite-element meshes satisfy the discrete maximum principle (
9.4
)
for each component operator
\mathcal{L}^{k}
, and that

0<\lambda_{r}^{*}<+\infty\qquad\text{for all }r\geq 1.

Suppose, in addition, that there exist
\mu>0
and
M\geq 1
such that

\mu^{-1}<\lambda_{r}^{*}<\mu\qquad\text{for all }r\geq M.

Then the following assertions hold.

(1^{\circ})

For every
r\geq 1
, the Galerkin problem admits a maximal weak saddle-node
bifurcation point
(u_{r}^{*},\lambda_{r}^{*})
with

u_{r}^{*}\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
. Moreover, there exists

v_{r}^{*}\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
such that

\lambda_{r}^{*}=\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},v_{r}^{*})=\min_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u_{r}^{*},v)=\max_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}}\min_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v).

(2^{\circ})

There exist a subsequence, not relabelled, a number

\hat{\lambda}^{*}\in[\mu^{-1},\mu]
, and elements

u^{*}\in\mathbb{S}^{o},\qquad v^{*}\in\mathbb{S}\setminus\{0\},

such that

\lambda_{r}^{*}\to\hat{\lambda}^{*},\qquad u_{r}^{*}\to u^{*}\quad\text{strongly in }\mathbb{W},\qquad v_{r}^{*}\rightharpoonup v^{*}\quad\text{weakly in }\mathbb{W}.

Moreover,

\mathcal{F}(u^{*},\hat{\lambda}^{*})=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{W}^{*},

and

\bigl\langle D_{u}\mathcal{F}(u^{*},\hat{\lambda}^{*})\xi,v^{*}\bigr\rangle=0\qquad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{W}.

Thus
(u^{*},\hat{\lambda}^{*})
is a weak singular solution of (
5.1
).

Remark 5.1
.

Only Theorem
2.1
is used in this section. Hence the approximation
condition
{\rm(U)}
is not needed, and we do not claim that

\hat{\lambda}^{*}=\lambda^{*}
. Such an identification requires the additional
approximation hypothesis
{\rm(U)}
and Theorem
2.2
.

Remark 5.2
.

The assumptions
{\rm(h_{1})}
–
{\rm(h_{5})}
are used as follows. The
diagonal sublinear structure of
g
gives

t^{k}g^{k}_{t^{k}}=qg^{k},\qquad 0<q<1.

Together with the superlinear structure of
f
, this yields the compactness
of positive superlevel sets of the finite-dimensional functional

\lambda_{r}(u):=\inf_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v).

This verifies the new condition
{\rm(H1)}
. The cooperativity of
f
,
combined with the discrete maximum principle for the transposed stiffness
matrices, gives the adjoint boundary-exclusion condition
{\rm(H2)}
.
Finally, the degeneracy condition
{\rm(h_{5})}
is used in the Picone-type
compactness argument verifying the
(PS)_{e}
-condition.

5.1
Verification of the abstract assumptions

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume
{\rm(h_{1})}
–
{\rm(h_{5})}
. Then the extended Rayleigh quotient

\mathcal{R}
satisfies
{\rm(H1)}
and
{\rm(H2)}
on the
finite-dimensional cones
\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
.

Proof.

For
r\geq 1
, set

\lambda_{r}(u):=\inf_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v),\qquad u\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}.

By condition
{\rm(D)}
, the map
u\mapsto\mathcal{R}(u,v)
is continuous
for every fixed
v\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
. Hence
\lambda_{r}
, being the infimum of
continuous functions, is upper semicontinuous.

We first show that

\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}}\lambda_{r}(u)>0.

Let

\bar{1}=(1,\ldots,1)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{r},\qquad\bar{\omega}_{r}^{k}:=(A_{r}^{k})^{-1}\bar{1},

and set

\omega_{r}^{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\bar{\omega}_{r}^{k})^{i}\psi_{i},\qquad\omega_{r}:=(\omega_{r}^{1},\ldots,\omega_{r}^{m}).

Since
A_{r}^{k}
is an
M
-matrix,
\omega_{r}^{k}\in S_{r}^{o}
, and hence

\omega_{r}\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
. For
t>0
sufficiently small and

v\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
, the sublinear term in the denominator gives

\mathcal{R}(t\omega_{r},v)\geq\frac{ct-C(t^{\gamma_{0}}+t^{\gamma})}{Ct^{q}}.

Since
0<q<1<\gamma_{0}\leq\gamma
, the right-hand side is positive for some

t=t_{0}>0
. Therefore

\lambda_{r}(t_{0}\omega_{r})>0.

We next prove the compactness of positive superlevel sets. Let

K_{r,\varepsilon}:=\{u\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}:\lambda_{r}(u)\geq\varepsilon\},\qquad\varepsilon>0.

Since
W_{r}^{m}
is finite-dimensional, it is enough to show that

K_{r,\varepsilon}
is bounded and separated from

\partial\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
.

We first show that
K_{r,\varepsilon}
is separated from the boundary of

\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
. Suppose, to the contrary, that there exists a sequence

u_{n}\in K_{r,\varepsilon},\qquad u_{n}\to u_{0}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{W}_{r},

where

u_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}.

Writing

u_{n}^{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}u_{n,j}^{k}\psi_{j},\qquad u_{n,j}^{k}>0,

we may assume, after passing to a subsequence, that for some component

k_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and some node
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
,

u_{n,i_{0}}^{k_{0}}\to 0.

Let
\bar{\psi}_{i_{0}}^{k_{0}}\in\mathbb{W}_{r}
denote the vector whose

k_{0}
-th component is
\psi_{i_{0}}
and whose other components are zero.
Although
\bar{\psi}_{i_{0}}^{k_{0}}\notin\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
, it belongs to

\mathbb{S}_{r}
. Since
\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
is dense in
\mathbb{S}_{r}
and

\mathcal{R}(u_{n},\cdot)
is continuous on
\mathbb{S}_{r}
, we have

\lambda_{r}(u_{n})=\inf_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u_{n},v)\leq\mathcal{R}(u_{n},\bar{\psi}_{i_{0}}^{k_{0}}).

Indeed, this follows by testing with

\bar{\psi}_{i_{0}}^{k_{0}}+\delta e_{r}\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o},\qquad\delta>0,

where
e_{r}\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
is fixed, and then letting
\delta\downarrow 0
.

We estimate the last quotient. By definition,

\mathcal{R}(u_{n},\bar{\psi}_{i_{0}}^{k_{0}})=\frac{a^{k_{0}}(u_{n}^{k_{0}},\psi_{i_{0}})-\langle f^{k_{0}}(u_{n}),\psi_{i_{0}}\rangle}{\langle g^{k_{0}}(u_{n}),\psi_{i_{0}}\rangle}.

Since
f^{k_{0}}\geq 0
, it is enough to estimate the linear part from above.
Let
A_{r}^{k_{0}}=(a_{ij}^{k_{0}})
be the stiffness matrix. With our nodal
coordinates,

a^{k_{0}}(u_{n}^{k_{0}},\psi_{i_{0}})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{i_{0}j}^{k_{0}}u_{n,j}^{k_{0}}

(up to replacing
A_{r}^{k_{0}}
by its transpose, depending on the convention
for the matrix entries). Since
A_{r}^{k_{0}}
is an
M
-matrix, its
off-diagonal entries are nonpositive:

a_{i_{0}j}^{k_{0}}\leq 0\qquad j\neq i_{0}.

Therefore

a^{k_{0}}(u_{n}^{k_{0}},\psi_{i_{0}})\leq a_{i_{0}i_{0}}^{k_{0}}u_{n,i_{0}}^{k_{0}}\leq Cu_{n,i_{0}}^{k_{0}}.

On the other hand, by
{\rm(h_{1})}
,

\langle g^{k_{0}}(u_{n}),\psi_{i_{0}}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}a_{k_{0}}(x)(u_{n}^{k_{0}})^{q}\psi_{i_{0}}\,dx.

Since

u_{n}^{k_{0}}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}u_{n,j}^{k_{0}}\psi_{j}(x)\geq u_{n,i_{0}}^{k_{0}}\psi_{i_{0}}(x),

we obtain

\langle g^{k_{0}}(u_{n}),\psi_{i_{0}}\rangle\geq a_{0}(u_{n,i_{0}}^{k_{0}})^{q}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{i_{0}}^{q+1}\,dx\geq c(u_{n,i_{0}}^{k_{0}})^{q}.

Consequently,

\mathcal{R}(u_{n},\bar{\psi}_{i_{0}}^{k_{0}})\leq C\frac{u_{n,i_{0}}^{k_{0}}}{(u_{n,i_{0}}^{k_{0}})^{q}}=C(u_{n,i_{0}}^{k_{0}})^{1-q}\to 0,

because
0<q<1
. Hence

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{r}(u_{n})\leq 0.

This contradicts
u_{n}\in K_{r,\varepsilon}
, since

\lambda_{r}(u_{n})\geq\varepsilon>0.

Therefore
K_{r,\varepsilon}
is separated from

\partial\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
.

It remains to prove that
K_{r,\varepsilon}
is bounded. Suppose, to the
contrary, that

u_{n}\in K_{r,\varepsilon},\qquad\|u_{n}\|_{\mathbb{W}_{r}}\to+\infty.

Since
K_{r,\varepsilon}
is separated from the boundary, there exists

\delta>0
such that

u_{n,j}^{k}\geq\delta\qquad\text{for all }n,\ k=1,\ldots,m,\ j=1,\ldots,r.

Let

T_{n}:=\max_{k,j}u_{n,j}^{k}\to+\infty.

Passing to a subsequence, assume that

T_{n}=u_{n,i_{0}}^{k_{0}}.

Testing again by
\bar{\psi}_{i_{0}}^{k_{0}}
, the
M
-matrix sign pattern gives

a^{k_{0}}(u_{n}^{k_{0}},\psi_{i_{0}})\leq CT_{n}.

On the other hand, by
{\rm(h_{4})}
and the positivity of
f^{k_{0}}
in
the interior of the cone, the separation from the boundary implies a
uniform lower superlinear estimate

\langle f^{k_{0}}(u_{n}),\psi_{i_{0}}\rangle\geq cT_{n}^{\theta},\qquad\theta>1.

Moreover,

\langle g^{k_{0}}(u_{n}),\psi_{i_{0}}\rangle\geq cT_{n}^{q}.

Therefore

\lambda_{r}(u_{n})\leq\mathcal{R}(u_{n},\bar{\psi}_{i_{0}}^{k_{0}})\leq CT_{n}^{1-q}-cT_{n}^{\theta-q}\to-\infty,

because
\theta>1
. This contradicts

\lambda_{r}(u_{n})\geq\varepsilon>0
. Hence
K_{r,\varepsilon}
is bounded.

It remains to verify
{\rm(H2)}
. Let
u,v\in\mathbb{S}_{r}
and suppose
that

D_{u}\langle\mathcal{A}(u),v\rangle(\bar{\psi}_{i})\geq 0,\qquad i=1,\ldots,rm.

Since

D_{u}\langle\mathcal{A}(u),v\rangle(\bar{\psi}_{i})=a_{m}(\bar{\psi}_{i},v)-\langle f_{u}(u)\bar{\psi}_{i},v\rangle_{m},

and
f_{u}(u)\geq 0
by cooperativity, we obtain

a^{k}(\psi_{i},v^{k})\geq 0,\qquad i=1,\ldots,r,\quad k=1,\ldots,m.

Equivalently,

(A_{r}^{k})^{T}\bar{v}^{k}\geq 0.

Since
A_{r}^{k}
is an irreducible nonsingular
M
-matrix, the same is true
for
(A_{r}^{k})^{T}
. Hence
\bar{v}^{k}>0
for every
k
, and therefore

v\in\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}.

Thus
{\rm(H2)}
holds.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume
{\rm(h_{1})}
–
{\rm(h_{5})}
. Then the extended Rayleigh quotient

\mathcal{R}
satisfies the
(PS)_{e}
-condition on

\mathbb{S}^{o}\times\mathbb{S}^{o}
at every level
\lambda>0
.

Proof.

Let

(u_{n},v_{n})\in\mathbb{S}_{n}^{o}\times\mathbb{S}_{n}^{o}

be a
(PS)_{e}
-sequence at a level
\lambda>0
, and set

\lambda_{n}:=\mathcal{R}(u_{n},v_{n})\to\lambda.

The relation
D_{v}\mathcal{R}(u_{n},v_{n})=0
gives

\mathcal{F}_{n}(u_{n},\lambda_{n})=0.

Testing this equation by
u_{n}
, we obtain

a_{m}(u_{n},u_{n})=\lambda_{n}\langle g(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle_{m}+\langle f(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle_{m}.

On the other hand, testing the adjoint relation

D_{u}\mathcal{R}(u_{n},v_{n})=0
componentwise by

(u_{n}^{k})^{2}/v_{n}^{k}
and using Picone’s identity, we get

a_{m}(u_{n},u_{n})\geq\lambda_{n}\left\langle g_{u}(u_{n})v_{n},\frac{u_{n}^{2}}{v_{n}}\right\rangle_{m}+\left\langle f_{u}(u_{n})v_{n},\frac{u_{n}^{2}}{v_{n}}\right\rangle_{m}.

By
{\rm(h_{1})}
,

\left\langle g_{u}(u_{n})v_{n},\frac{u_{n}^{2}}{v_{n}}\right\rangle_{m}=q\langle g(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle_{m},

and by
{\rm(h_{4})}
,

\left\langle f_{u}(u_{n})v_{n},\frac{u_{n}^{2}}{v_{n}}\right\rangle_{m}\geq\theta\langle f(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle_{m}.

Combining these identities yields

(\theta-1)a_{m}(u_{n},u_{n})\leq(\theta-q)\lambda_{n}\langle g(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle_{m}.

Since
\lambda_{n}
is bounded and
q+1<2
, the Sobolev inequality implies

\|u_{n}\|_{\mathbb{W}}\leq C.

Hence, up to a subsequence,

u_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}\quad\text{weakly in }\mathbb{W},\qquad u_{n}\to\bar{u}\quad\text{strongly in }\mathbb{L}^{\beta},\quad 1\leq\beta<2^{*}.

The normalization in the definition of the
(PS)_{e}
-condition gives
boundedness of
(v_{n})
in
\mathbb{W}
. Therefore, after passing to a
subsequence,

v_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{v}\quad\text{weakly in }\mathbb{W},\qquad v_{n}\to\bar{v}\quad\text{strongly in }\mathbb{L}^{\beta},\quad 1\leq\beta<2^{*}.

Passing to the limit in the Galerkin equations gives

\mathcal{F}(\bar{u},\lambda)=0.

By the maximum principle and Hopf’s boundary lemma,

\bar{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{o}.

It remains to show that
\bar{v}\neq 0
. The equation
D_{u}\mathcal{R}(u_{n},v_{n})=0
is tested componentwise by the
finite-dimensional vector whose
k
-th component has nodal coefficients

\left(\frac{(u_{n,i}^{k})^{2}}{v_{n,i}^{k}}\right)_{i}.

Using the componentwise discrete Picone inequality from
Appendix
11
, we obtain

a_{m}(u_{n},u_{n})\geq\lambda_{n}\left\langle g_{u}(u_{n})v_{n},\frac{u_{n}^{2}}{v_{n}}\right\rangle_{m}+\left\langle f_{u}(u_{n})v_{n},\frac{u_{n}^{2}}{v_{n}}\right\rangle_{m}.

Hence, for any
\chi\in(q,1)
,

1-\chi\leq\left\langle f_{u}(u_{n})v_{n},v_{n}\right\rangle_{m}-\chi\left\langle f(u_{n}),\frac{v_{n}^{2}}{u_{n}}\right\rangle_{m}.

If
\bar{v}=0
, then the right-hand side tends to
0
by the growth
assumptions and the degeneracy condition
{\rm(h_{5})}
, contradicting

1-\chi>0
. Hence

\bar{v}\in\mathbb{S}\setminus\{0\}.

Finally, the energy identity and the strong convergence of the nonlinear
terms imply

a_{m}(u_{n},u_{n})\to a_{m}(\bar{u},\bar{u}).

Together with weak convergence and coercivity, this yields

u_{n}\to\bar{u}\quad\text{strongly in }\mathbb{W}.

Passing to the limit in the adjoint linearized relation gives

\bigl\langle D_{u}\mathcal{F}(\bar{u},\lambda)\xi,\bar{v}\bigr\rangle=0\qquad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{W}.

Thus the
(PS)_{e}
-condition is verified.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.5
.

By Lemma
5.1
, the quotient
\mathcal{R}
satisfies

{\rm(H1)}
and
{\rm(H2)}
on the discrete cones

\mathbb{S}_{r}^{o}
. By Lemma

{\rm(P1)}
–
{\rm(P3)}
. By Proposition
10.1

{\rm(D)}
holds. Finally, Lemma

.

Therefore all assumptions of Theorem

6
Minimax bifurcation formula for a one-dimensional elliptic system

In this section we consider the one-dimensional case
d=1
of system
(
5.1
). Namely, we study

\left\{\begin{aligned} -(u^{k})^{\prime\prime}-f^{k}(x,u)&=\lambda g^{k}(x,u),&&x\in\Omega:=(0,1),\\
u^{k}&\geq 0,&&x\in\Omega,\\
u^{k}|_{\partial\Omega}&=0,&&k=1,\ldots,m.\end{aligned}\right.

(6.1)

Let
\mathcal{L}=-d^{2}/dx^{2}
and
0<q<1
. We work with the cones

\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}:=(S_{\mathcal{L}}^{o})^{m},\qquad\mathbb{U}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}:=(U_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q})^{m},

where
S_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}
and
U_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}
are defined in
Appendix A. The corresponding closed cone is denoted by

\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{L}}
.

The extended Rayleigh quotient is

\mathcal{R}(u,v)=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{1}(u^{k})^{\prime}(v^{k})^{\prime}\,dx-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{1}f^{k}(x,u)v^{k}\,dx}{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{1}g^{k}(x,u)v^{k}\,dx},

and we set

\lambda_{\mathcal{L}}^{*}:=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{U}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}}\inf_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v).

(6.2)

We impose the following one-dimensional variants of the structural assumptions
used above. For simplicity, the notation
{\rm(h_{1})}
–
{\rm(h_{5})}
is
kept local to this section. All inequalities below are understood for a.e.

x\in(0,1)
, all
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
, and all relevant
indices.

{\rm(h_{1})}

The parameter term is diagonal and
q
-homogeneous:

g^{k}(x,t)=g^{k}(x,t_{k}),\qquad a_{0}t_{k}^{q}\leq g^{k}(x,t)\leq a_{1}t_{k}^{q},

where
a_{0},a_{1}>0
. Moreover,
g^{k}
is
C^{1}
in
t_{k}>0
,

t_{k}g^{k}_{t_{k}}(x,t)=qg^{k}(x,t)
, and
g^{k}_{t_{j}}(x,t)=0
for
j\neq k
.

{\rm(h_{2})}

The reaction terms are nonnegative, cooperative, and
C^{1}
with respect to

t\in(0,\infty)^{m}
:

f^{k}(x,t)\geq 0,\qquad f^{k}_{t_{j}}(x,t)\geq 0,\qquad j,k=1,\ldots,m.

{\rm(h_{3})}

There exist exponents
1<\gamma_{k}\leq\gamma<+\infty
and constants

c_{0},C_{0}>0
such that

c_{0}t_{k}^{\gamma_{k}}\leq f^{k}(x,t)\leq C_{0}t_{k}^{\gamma_{k}}(1+|t|)^{\gamma-\gamma_{k}}.

{\rm(h_{4})}

There exists
\theta>1
, with

1<\theta<\min_{1\leq k\leq m}\gamma_{k},

such that

t_{k}f^{k}_{t_{k}}(x,t)\geq\theta f^{k}(x,t),\qquad k=1,\ldots,m.

{\rm(h_{5})}

The derivatives of
f
satisfy compatible growth estimates:

0\leq f^{k}_{t_{k}}(x,t)\leq C_{1}t_{k}^{\gamma_{k}-1}(1+|t|)^{\gamma-\gamma_{k}},

and, for
j\neq k
,

0\leq f^{k}_{t_{j}}(x,t)\leq C_{1}t_{k}^{\gamma_{k}}(1+|t|)^{\gamma-\gamma_{k}}.

Example 2
.

A model class satisfying
{\rm(h_{1})}
–
{\rm(h_{5})}
is

g^{k}(x,t)=a_{k}(x)t_{k}^{q},\qquad f^{k}(x,t)=b_{k}(x)t_{k}^{\beta_{k}}\prod_{j\neq k}(1+t_{j})^{\alpha_{kj}},

where
0<a_{0}\leq a_{k}(x)\leq a_{1}
,
0<b_{0}\leq b_{k}(x)\leq b_{1}
,

0<q<1
,
\beta_{k}>1
, and
\alpha_{kj}\geq 0
. In this case one may take

\gamma_{k}=\beta_{k},\qquad\gamma=\max_{1\leq k\leq m}\left(\beta_{k}+\sum_{j\neq k}\alpha_{kj}\right).

Moreover, since
t_{k}f^{k}_{t_{k}}=\beta_{k}f^{k}
, condition
{\rm(h_{4})}

holds with any
1<\theta<\min_{k}\beta_{k}
.

Remark 6.1
.

These assumptions are the one-dimensional counterparts of the structural
hypotheses used above. The diagonal
q
-homogeneity of
g
gives the
sublinear parameter term. The lower superlinear bound in
{\rm(h_{3})}

yields compactness of positive superlevel sets, cooperativity gives the
boundary-exclusion condition
{\rm(H2)}
, and

{\rm(h_{4})}
–
{\rm(h_{5})}
are used in the Picone-type compactness
argument.

Theorem 6.6
.

Assume
d=1
and suppose that
f
and
g
satisfy

{\rm(h_{1})}
–
{\rm(h_{5})}
. Then

0<\lambda_{\mathcal{L}}^{*}<+\infty.

Moreover, the following assertions hold.

(1^{\circ})

System (
6.1
) admits a maximal weak saddle-node bifurcation point

(u^{*},\lambda_{\mathcal{L}}^{*})
in the class of nonnegative weak
solutions. Moreover, (
6.1
) has no nonnegative weak solution for

\lambda>\lambda_{\mathcal{L}}^{*}
.

(2^{\circ})

There exists
v^{*}\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{L}}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\lambda_{\mathcal{L}}^{*}=\mathcal{R}(u^{*},v^{*})=\inf_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u^{*},v)=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{U}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}}\inf_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}}\mathcal{R}(u,v).

(6.3)

If, in addition,
v^{*}\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}

(

Lemma 6.1

(Relative interpolation estimate in dimension one)
.

Let
\Omega=(0,1)
,
d(x):=\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
, and

\mathcal{L}=-d^{2}/dx^{2}
. Assume that
0<q<1
and

u\in U_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}\subset S_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}
. Then, for every
quasi-uniform family of one-dimensional meshes,

\left\|\frac{\mathcal{I}_{r}u-u}{u}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\to 0\qquad\text{as }h_{r}\to 0.

More precisely, there exists
C(u)>0
, independent of
r
, such that

|\mathcal{I}_{r}u(x)-u(x)|\leq C(u)h_{r}^{1+q}d(x),\qquad x\in(0,1),

and consequently

\left\|\frac{\mathcal{I}_{r}u-u}{u}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq C(u)h_{r}^{1+q}.

Proof.

Since
u\in S_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}
, we have
u(x)\geq c_{u}d(x)
on

(0,1)
. Moreover,
u\in U_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}
means that

0\leq-u^{\prime\prime}\leq C_{u}d^{q}
in the sense of distributions, and hence a.e. in

(0,1)
.

Let
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{N}<x_{N+1}=1
be a quasi-uniform mesh, with

h_{i}=x_{i}-x_{i-1}
and
h_{r}=\max_{i}h_{i}
. On

I_{i}=[x_{i-1},x_{i}]
, denote by
\ell_{i}
the affine interpolant of
u
.
On every interior element, the standard one-dimensional interpolation
estimate gives

|u(x)-\ell_{i}(x)|\leq Ch_{i}^{2}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{I_{i}}|u^{\prime\prime}|\leq C(u)h_{i}^{2}\sup_{I_{i}}d^{q}.

By quasi-uniformity and
0<q<1
, it is easy to see that

h_{i}^{2}\sup_{I_{i}}d^{q}\leq Ch_{r}^{1+q}d(x)
for
x\in I_{i}
. Hence

|u(x)-\mathcal{I}_{r}u(x)|\leq C(u)h_{r}^{1+q}d(x)

on all interior elements.

It remains only to check the boundary elements. On
I_{1}=[0,x_{1}]
, since

u(0)=0
, we have
\mathcal{I}_{r}u(x)=xu(x_{1})/x_{1}
. Therefore

|u(x)-\mathcal{I}_{r}u(x)|\leq x\int_{0}^{x_{1}}|u^{\prime\prime}(s)|\,ds\leq C(u)x\int_{0}^{x_{1}}s^{q}\,ds\leq C(u)h_{r}^{1+q}x.

Since
d(x)=x
on
I_{1}
, this gives the required estimate near the left
endpoint. The right boundary element is treated in the same way. Thus

|\mathcal{I}_{r}u(x)-u(x)|\leq C(u)h_{r}^{1+q}d(x),\qquad x\in(0,1).

Dividing by
u(x)\geq c_{u}d(x)
, we obtain the relative estimate and the
proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
u=(u^{1},\ldots,u^{m})\in\mathbb{U}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}:=(U_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q})^{m}
, and let

\mathcal{I}_{r}
act componentwise. Then

\left\|\frac{\mathcal{I}_{r}u-u}{u}\right\|_{(L^{\infty}(0,1))^{m}}:=\max_{1\leq k\leq m}\left\|\frac{\mathcal{I}_{r}u^{k}-u^{k}}{u^{k}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq C(u)h_{r}^{1+q}.

In particular,

\left\|\frac{\mathcal{I}_{r}u-u}{u}\right\|_{(L^{\infty}(0,1))^{m}}\to 0\qquad\text{as }h_{r}\to 0.

Lemma 6.2
.

Condition
{\rm(U)}
holds for the class
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{L}}
.
Equivalently, for every
u\in\mathbb{U}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}
,

\sup_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}}\left|\mathcal{R}(u,v)-\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}_{r}u,v)\right|\to 0\qquad\text{as }r\to\infty.

(6.4)

Proof.

Fix
u\in\mathbb{U}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}
. By
Corollary
6.1
,

\delta_{r}:=\left\|\frac{\mathcal{I}_{r}u-u}{u}\right\|_{(L^{\infty}(0,1))^{m}}\to 0.

In particular, for all large
r
,
\mathcal{I}_{r}u
is comparable with

u
, say
\frac{1}{2}u^{k}\leq\mathcal{I}_{r}u^{k}\leq 2u^{k}
.

We use the elementary identity

\mathcal{R}(u,v)-\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}_{r}u,v)=\frac{a_{m}(u-\mathcal{I}_{r}u,v)+\langle f(\mathcal{I}_{r}u)-f(u),v\rangle_{m}}{\langle g(u),v\rangle_{m}}+\frac{\langle g(\mathcal{I}_{r}u)-g(u),v\rangle_{m}}{\langle g(u),v\rangle_{m}}\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}_{r}u,v).

Thus it is enough to show that the first two quotients tend to zero
uniformly in
v
, and that
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}_{r}u,v)
remains
uniformly bounded.

Let
v\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}
. Since
\mathcal{L}v^{k}\geq 0
and

|u^{k}-\mathcal{I}_{r}u^{k}|\leq\delta_{r}u^{k}
, we have

\left|a^{k}(u^{k}-\mathcal{I}_{r}u^{k},v^{k})\right|=\left|\langle u^{k}-\mathcal{I}_{r}u^{k},\mathcal{L}v^{k}\rangle\right|\leq\delta_{r}\langle u^{k},\mathcal{L}v^{k}\rangle=\delta_{r}\langle\mathcal{L}u^{k},v^{k}\rangle.

As
u\in\mathbb{U}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}
,
0\leq\mathcal{L}u^{k}\leq C(u)d^{q}
.
Since
u^{k}\geq c_{u}d
and
g^{k}(x,u)\geq a_{0}(u^{k})^{q}
, it follows that

d^{q}\leq C(u)g^{k}(x,u)
. Therefore

\frac{|a_{m}(u-\mathcal{I}_{r}u,v)|}{\langle g(u),v\rangle_{m}}\leq C(u)\delta_{r}.

Next, the relative convergence
\mathcal{I}_{r}u/u\to 1
, the

q
-homogeneity of
g
, and the growth assumptions on
f
imply

\max_{k}\left\|\frac{f^{k}(x,\mathcal{I}_{r}u)-f^{k}(x,u)}{g^{k}(x,u)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\max_{k}\left\|\frac{g^{k}(x,\mathcal{I}_{r}u)-g^{k}(x,u)}{g^{k}(x,u)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\to 0.

Indeed, all intermediate points between
u
and
\mathcal{I}_{r}u
are
comparable with
u
, and the derivative bounds in
{\rm(h_{5})}
, together
with
u^{k}\geq c_{u}d
and
g^{k}(x,u)\geq a_{0}(u^{k})^{q}
, give the required
domination; the term involving
g
follows directly from

q
-homogeneity. Hence

\sup_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}}\frac{|\langle f(\mathcal{I}_{r}u)-f(u),v\rangle_{m}|+|\langle g(\mathcal{I}_{r}u)-g(u),v\rangle_{m}|}{\langle g(u),v\rangle_{m}}\to 0.

In particular,
\langle g(\mathcal{I}_{r}u),v\rangle_{m}
and

\langle g(u),v\rangle_{m}
are uniformly equivalent for all large
r
.

Finally, the same estimates give

|a_{m}(\mathcal{I}_{r}u,v)|+\langle f(\mathcal{I}_{r}u),v\rangle_{m}\leq C(u)\langle g(\mathcal{I}_{r}u),v\rangle_{m},

and therefore
|\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I}_{r}u,v)|\leq C(u)
, uniformly in

v\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}
. Substituting these estimates into the
identity above proves (
6.4
).
∎

7
Perturbation estimates for the maximal bifurcation value

We first record a simple perturbation estimate for the minimax value. For an
operator
\mathcal{A}
, set

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}}(u,\lambda):=\mathcal{A}(u)-\lambda\mathcal{G}(u),\qquad\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}(u,v):=\frac{\langle\mathcal{A}(u),v\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{G}(u),v\rangle}.

The same notation will be used for the corresponding extremal values and
critical points.

Proposition 7.1

(Perturbation bounds)
.

Let
\Psi\in C_{W}(\mathcal{S}^{o},W^{*})
, and assume that

-\infty<\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}+\Psi}:=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}+\Psi}(u,v)<+\infty.

(1^{\circ})

If
u^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathcal{S}^{o}
realizes the maximizer

\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}(u^{*}_{\mathcal{A}},v)=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}(u,v),

then

\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}+\Psi}\geq\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}+\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{\langle\Psi(u^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}),v\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{G}(u^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}),v\rangle}.

(7.1)

(2^{\circ})

If
v^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\{0\}
realizes the
minimizer in the dual minimax value,

\Lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}}\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}(u,v)=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}(u,v^{*}_{\mathcal{A}})<+\infty,

then

\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}+\Psi}\leq\Lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}+\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\frac{\langle\Psi(u),v^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{G}(u),v^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle}.

(7.2)

Proof.

For
(1^{\circ})
, we test the outer supremum at

u=u^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Since

\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}+\Psi}(u,v)=\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}(u,v)+\frac{\langle\Psi(u),v\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{G}(u),v\rangle},

the estimate follows at once from

\inf(a+b)\geq\inf a+\inf b
.

For
(2^{\circ})
, we test the inner infimum at

v=v^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}
. This gives

\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}+\Psi}\leq\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}+\Psi}(u,v^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}),

and the desired bound follows from

\sup(a+b)\leq\sup a+\sup b
.
∎

We say that
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies the
minimax principle

on
\mathcal{S}^{o}\times\mathcal{S}
if

\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}(u,v)=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}}\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}(u,v)=:\Lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}.

(7.3)

Corollary 7.1
.

Assume that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}}(u,\lambda)=0
has a maximal weak
saddle-node bifurcation point

(u^{*}_{\mathcal{A}},\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}})\in\mathcal{S}^{o}\times\mathbb{R}
. Suppose also that

\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies the minimax principle and that there
exists
v^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}(u^{*}_{\mathcal{A}},v)=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}(u,v^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}).

Then, whenever
\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}+\Psi}
is finite,

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{\langle\Psi(u^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}),v\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{G}(u^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}),v\rangle}\leq\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}+\Psi}-\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\frac{\langle\Psi(u),v^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{G}(u),v^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle}.

(7.4)

In particular, if

-\infty<\underline{d}_{\Psi}\leq\overline{d}_{\Psi}<+\infty,

where

\underline{d}_{\Psi}:=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{\langle\Psi(u^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}),v\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{G}(u^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}),v\rangle},\qquad\overline{d}_{\Psi}:=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{o}}\frac{\langle\Psi(u),v^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{G}(u),v^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle},

then, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\varepsilon\underline{d}_{\Psi}\leq\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}+\varepsilon\Psi}-\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\varepsilon\overline{d}_{\Psi}.

In particular,

\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}+\varepsilon\Psi}\to\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

7.1
Example

We illustrate the perturbation estimate on a one-dimensional problem. Consider

\left\{\begin{aligned} -(\sigma(x)(u^{k})^{\prime})^{\prime}-f_{1}^{k}(x,u)&=\lambda g^{k}(x,u),&&x\in\Omega:=(0,1),\\
u^{k}&\geq 0,&&x\in\Omega,\\
u^{k}|_{\partial\Omega}&=0,&&k=1,\ldots,m,\end{aligned}\right.

(7.5)

where
\sigma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and

\sigma(x)\geq\nu^{-1}>0
a.e. in
\Omega
. Set

\mathcal{L}u:=-u^{\prime\prime},\qquad\mathcal{L}_{1}u:=-(\sigma u^{\prime})^{\prime},\qquad\delta(u):=(\mathcal{L}_{1}-\mathcal{L})u-\bigl(f_{1}(u)-f(u)\bigr).

Then
\mathcal{L}_{1}-f_{1}=\mathcal{L}-f+\delta
.

Assume that
f_{1}
satisfies the same structural hypotheses as
f
. Then
the perturbed problem has a finite extreme value

\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}-f_{1}}
. If

(u^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f},\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f})
is a maximal weak
saddle-node bifurcation point of the unperturbed problem, then
Corollary
7.1
gives

\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}-f_{1}}\geq\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f}+\inf_{v\in\mathbb{S}}\frac{\langle\delta(u^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f}),v\rangle_{m}}{\langle g(u^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f}),v\rangle_{m}}.

(7.6)

As a scalar example, consider

-u^{\prime\prime}-u^{\gamma}=\lambda u^{q},\qquad u\geq 0,\qquad u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(7.7)

where
0<q<1<\gamma
. By Theorem
6.6
, this problem has a maximal weak
saddle-node bifurcation point

(u^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f},\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f})\in U^{o,q}_{\mathcal{L}}\times(0,+\infty)
.

Now perturb (
7.7
) by

-u^{\prime\prime}-u^{\gamma}-\kappa(x)u^{\gamma_{1}}=\lambda u^{q},\qquad u\geq 0,\qquad u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(7.8)

where
1<\gamma_{1}
,
\kappa\in C(\overline{\Omega})
, and

\kappa\geq 0
. Here

f_{1}(u)=u^{\gamma}+\kappa u^{\gamma_{1}},\qquad\delta(u)=-\kappa u^{\gamma_{1}}.

The perturbed problem also has a maximal weak saddle-node bifurcation point

(u^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f_{1}},\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f_{1}})\in U^{o,q}_{\mathcal{L}}\times(0,+\infty)
.

Applying (
7.6
) first to

\mathcal{L}-f_{1}=\mathcal{L}-f+\delta
, and then to

\mathcal{L}-f=\mathcal{L}-f_{1}+(-\delta)
, we obtain

0\leq\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f}-\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f_{1}}\leq\|\kappa\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\|u^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}^{\gamma_{1}-q}.

(7.9)

More precisely, the lower estimate may be written as

\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f}-\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f_{1}}\geq\inf_{v\in S}\frac{\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(x)(u^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f_{1}})^{\gamma_{1}}v\,dx}{\int_{0}^{1}(u^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f_{1}})^{q}v\,dx}\geq 0.

Thus adding the nonnegative reaction term
\kappa u^{\gamma_{1}}
can only
decrease the maximal parameter value.

Corollary 7.2
.

Under the assumptions above,

\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f_{1}}\to\lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f}\qquad\text{as }\|\kappa\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\to 0.

Proof.

The assertion follows directly from (
7.9
), since

u^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f}
is fixed and belongs to
C(\overline{\Omega})
.
∎

Remark 7.1
.

The same argument applies to perturbations
V(x,s)=\kappa(x)h(s)
,

\kappa\geq 0
, provided
h(s)/s^{q}
is bounded on the range of

u^{*}_{\mathcal{L}-f}
.

8
Concluding remarks

We have shown that, under suitable compactness, positivity, and approximation
assumptions, the maximal weak saddle-node bifurcation point

(u^{*},\lambda^{*})
of (
2.1
) is characterized by the minimax bifurcation
formula (
1.3
). The finite-dimensional scheme developed in the paper
applies to general nonlinear equations and was verified for non-variational
elliptic systems.

Theorems
2.1
and
5.5
show that Galerkin approximations produce
finite-dimensional maximal weak saddle-node points and, after passage to the
limit, singular solutions
(u^{*},\hat{\lambda}^{*})
with

\hat{\lambda}^{*}\leq\lambda^{*}
. Under the stronger assumptions of
Theorem
2.2
, the same scheme recovers the maximal bifurcation value
itself. This is already useful from the numerical point of view: in
bifurcation analysis, the detection of a singular point along a solution
branch is often sufficient to identify the bifurcation event

[
33
,
38
]
.

The finite-dimensional formula also gives a nonlinear analogue of the
Collatz–Wielandt formula. Indeed, if

\{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{r}\}
is the positive basis of the Galerkin cone, then

\lambda_{r}(u)=\min_{1\leq i\leq r}\frac{\langle\mathcal{A}(u),\eta_{i}\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{G}(u),\eta_{i}\rangle},\qquad\lambda_{r}^{*}=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{r}^{o}}\lambda_{r}(u).

This leads to a finite-dimensional nonsmooth maximization problem, which can
be treated by standard tools of nonsmooth optimization

[
3
,
8
,
13
,
16
]
. Such formulas have already proved
effective in related nonlinear bifurcation and eigenvalue problems

[
22
,
23
,
24
,
37
]
.

The convergence results obtained here may be viewed as an extension of the
classical finite-element philosophy for eigenvalue approximation, going back
to Courant and developed further in
[
2
,
12
,
14
]
, to a
class of nonlinear saddle-node bifurcation problems. In a similar spirit,
Theorem
7.1
and Corollaries
7.1
,
7.2
extend the
Rayleigh quotient perturbation method
[
28
]
to the present nonlinear
minimax setting.

Formulas related to (
1.3
) also have a natural connection with the
Birkhoff–Varga variational principle for the spectral radius of essentially
positive matrices
[
39
]
, which is rooted in Perron–Frobenius theory.
Further extensions to general matrices were considered in
[
27
]
.
For elliptic operators, related variational characterizations of principal
eigenvalues appear in the works of Donsker–Varadhan
[
17
]
,
Protter–Weinberger
[
35
]
, Nussbaum–Pinchover
[
34
]
, and
Berestycki–Nirenberg–Varadhan
[
5
]
.

The present approach also gives a direct way to detect solutions of systems
such as (
5.1
) at critical parameter values

\lambda=\lambda^{*}
or
\lambda=\hat{\lambda}^{*}
. It remains an interesting
question whether the existence of solutions to (
5.1
) for noncritical
values of
\lambda
can be obtained by complementary variational or
topological methods.

For example, in the scalar problem

-\Delta u-u^{\gamma}=\lambda u^{q}\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u\geq 0,\qquad u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\qquad 0<q<1<\gamma,

(8.1)

classical sub- and supersolution arguments yield solutions for

\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
, and the extremal solution is obtained as the
limit
u_{\lambda}\to u_{\lambda^{*}}
as

\lambda\to\lambda^{*-}

[
1
,
9
]
. However, this approach
does not readily extend to systems with
m\geq 2
, where the construction of
ordered sub- and supersolutions becomes substantially more delicate.

Finally, the minimax formula (
1.3
) should be regarded as complementary
to classical global bifurcation theory. Rabinowitz’s global theorem

[
36
]
describes continua of solutions emerging from suitable
eigenvalues by topological degree arguments, but it does not locate turning
points. In contrast, (
1.3
) provides a direct quantitative mechanism
for identifying maximal saddle-node values, including in nonlinear and
non-variational systems.

Appendix A. Cones and finite-dimensional approximations

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded polyhedral domain and set

W:=W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{C}^{0}:=C(\overline{\Omega})
. We consider
the uniformly elliptic operator

\mathcal{L}u=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\sigma_{ij}(x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\right)+c(x)u,

(9.1)

where
c,\sigma_{ij}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\sigma_{ij}=\sigma_{ji}
, and

\nu^{-1}|\xi|^{2}\leq\sigma_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\nu|\xi|^{2}\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(9.2)

We write

a_{\mathcal{L}}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\sigma_{ij}(x)u_{x_{j}}v_{x_{i}}+c(x)uv\right)\,dx,\qquad\langle\mathcal{L}u,v\rangle=a_{\mathcal{L}}(u,v),

and assume coercivity of
a_{\mathcal{L}}
whenever it is needed.

Let
d(x):=\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
. We use the strict cone

S^{o}:=\left\{v\in C(\overline{\Omega})\setminus\{0\}:v|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad v(x)\geq c_{v}d(x)\ \text{in }\Omega\text{ for some }c_{v}>0\right\}.

(9.3)

For
q>0
, set

S_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}:=\{v\in S^{o}:\langle\mathcal{L}v,\eta\rangle\geq 0\ \text{for all }\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),\ \eta\geq 0\},

and

U_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}:=\left\{u\in S_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}:\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),\ \eta\geq 0\\
\eta\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\langle\mathcal{L}u,\eta\rangle}{\langle d^{q},\eta\rangle}<+\infty\right\}.

Their closed counterparts are

S:=\overline{S^{o}}^{\,C(\overline{\Omega})},\qquad S_{\mathcal{L}}:=\overline{S_{\mathcal{L}}^{o}}^{\,C(\overline{\Omega})},\qquad U_{\mathcal{L}}^{q}:=\overline{U_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}}^{\,C(\overline{\Omega})}.

Let
\{\mathcal{T}_{r}\}_{r\geq 1}
be a shape-regular family of triangulations,
and let
W_{r}\subset W
be the conforming
P_{1}
-finite element space with
nodal basis
\{\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{N_{r}}\}
. The nodal interpolant is

\mathcal{I}_{r}u:=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}u(B_{i})\psi_{i}.

We shall use the following interpolation assumption:

{\rm(I)}\qquad\mathcal{I}_{r}u\to u\quad\text{strongly in }W\quad\text{for every function to which interpolation is applied.}

The discrete cones are

S_{r}:=\left\{u_{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}u_{i}\psi_{i}\in W_{r}:\ u_{i}\geq 0\right\},\qquad S_{r}^{o}:=\left\{u_{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r}}u_{i}\psi_{i}\in W_{r}:\ u_{i}>0\right\}.

Then
S_{r}^{o}\subset S^{o}
and
S_{r}=\overline{S_{r}^{o}}^{\,W_{r}}
.

Let
A_{r}=(a_{ij})
,
a_{ij}:=\langle\mathcal{L}\psi_{j},\psi_{i}\rangle
. We
shall use the discrete maximum-principle implication

A_{r}\vartheta\geq 0,\quad\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N_{r}}\setminus\{0\}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\vartheta>0.

(9.4)

This holds, for instance, if
A_{r}
is a nonsingular irreducible

M
-matrix.
In the one-dimensional case
\Omega=(0,1)
,
\mathcal{L}=-d^{2}/dx^{2}
, and
for
P_{1}
-meshes, the standard stiffness matrix is a nonsingular irreducible

M
-matrix. Hence (
9.4
) holds. In the one-dimensional application
we also assume quasi-uniformity, that is,

\kappa^{-1}h_{r}\leq h_{i}\leq h_{r},\qquad h_{r}:=\max_{i}h_{i}.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
W=W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{C}^{0}=C(\overline{\Omega})
, and let

\mathcal{I}_{r}
be the nodal interpolation operator.

(i)

For the cone
S^{o}
, the discrete cones
S_{r}^{o}
satisfy

{\rm(P1)}
–
{\rm(P2)}
. Moreover, the approximation condition

{\rm(P3)}
holds for every function satisfying
{\rm(I)}
.

(ii)

Assume
d=1
,
\mathcal{L}=-d^{2}/dx^{2}
,
0<q<1
, and
(
9.4
). For the pair of cones

\mathcal{S}^{o}=S_{\mathcal{L}}^{o},\qquad\mathcal{U}^{o}=U_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q},

one may take

S_{\mathcal{L},r}^{o}:=\{v_{r}\in S_{r}^{o}:A_{r}\bar{v}>0\},\qquad U_{\mathcal{L},r}^{o,q}:=\mathcal{I}_{r}U_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}.

Then
{\rm(P1)}
–
{\rm(P2)}
hold whenever the interpolants are
well defined and belong to the corresponding discrete cones. Moreover,

{\rm(P3)}
holds for all functions satisfying
{\rm(I)}
whose
interpolants eventually remain in the relevant discrete cones.

Proof.

For
(i)
, the statement follows directly from the nodal representation of

S_{r}^{o}
. Positive nodal coefficients imply
v_{r}\geq 0
,
v_{r}=0
on

\partial\Omega
, and
v_{r}\geq c_{v_{r}}d
in
\Omega
; hence

S_{r}^{o}\subset S^{o}
, while
S_{r}
is the closed polyhedral cone generated
by the nodal basis. The approximation statement is precisely
{\rm(I)}
.

For
(ii)
, the implication (
9.4
) gives the required
boundary-exclusion property. If
v_{r}\in S_{\mathcal{L},r}^{o}
, then

v_{r}\in S_{r}^{o}\subset S^{o}
and

\langle\mathcal{L}v_{r},\psi_{i}\rangle=(A_{r}\bar{v})_{i}>0,\qquad i=1,\ldots,N_{r}.

The closure is the polyhedral cone

S_{\mathcal{L},r}=\{v_{r}\in S_{r}:A_{r}\bar{v}\geq 0\}.

Thus the Galerkin cone assumptions follow. The definition

U_{\mathcal{L},r}^{o,q}:=\mathcal{I}_{r}U_{\mathcal{L}}^{o,q}
gives the
corresponding approximation property in the first variable, and the remaining
assertion follows again from
{\rm(I)}
.
∎

Appendix B. Differentiability of the Nemytskii term

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,

W:=W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
, and
d(x):=\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
.
Let
g:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a Carathéodory function
such that
g(x,\cdot)\in C^{1}((0,\infty))
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, and
assume that, for some
0<q<1
,

|g(x,t)|\leq Ct^{q},\qquad|g_{t}(x,t)|\leq Ct^{q-1},\qquad t>0.

We denote by
Q
the corresponding Nemytskii operator,

Q(u)(x):=g(x,u(x))
.

Proposition 10.1

(Bounded linearization and relative differentiability)
.

Let
u\in S^{o}
. Then

\langle DQ(u)\phi,\psi\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}g_{t}(x,u)\phi\psi\,dx

defines a bounded linear operator
DQ(u):W\to W^{*}
. More precisely,

\left|\int_{\Omega}g_{t}(x,u)\phi\psi\,dx\right|\leq C(u)\|\phi\|_{W}\|\psi\|_{W},\qquad\phi,\psi\in W.

Assume, in addition, that
g_{t}
is relatively continuous near positive
rays, namely

\omega(\delta):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Omega,\ t>0\\
|\rho-1|\leq\delta\end{subarray}}\frac{|g_{t}(x,\rho t)-g_{t}(x,t)|}{t^{q-1}}\to 0\qquad\text{as }\delta\to 0.

If
h_{n}\in W
,
u+h_{n}\geq 0
, and

\left\|\frac{h_{n}}{u}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\to 0,

then

\frac{\|Q(u+h_{n})-Q(u)-DQ(u)h_{n}\|_{W^{*}}}{\|h_{n}\|_{W}}\to 0.

In particular, this applies to
g(x,t)=a(x)t^{q}
, where

0<a_{0}\leq a(x)\leq a_{1}
.

Proof.

Since
u\in S^{o}
,
u(x)\geq c_{u}d(x)
in
\Omega
. Hence

|g_{t}(x,u)|\leq Cu^{q-1}\leq C(u)d^{q-1}\leq C(u)d^{-1}.

Therefore, for
\phi,\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

\left|\int_{\Omega}g_{t}(x,u)\phi\psi\,dx\right|\leq C(u)\left\|\frac{\phi}{d}\right\|_{2}\|\psi\|_{2}\leq C(u)\|\phi\|_{W}\|\psi\|_{W},

by Hardy’s inequality and the embedding
W\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
.
The estimate extends to all
\phi,\psi\in W
by density.

For the differentiability assertion, write

Q(u+h_{n})-Q(u)-DQ(u)h_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}\bigl[g_{t}(x,u+sh_{n})-g_{t}(x,u)\bigr]h_{n}\,ds.

If
\delta_{n}:=\|h_{n}/u\|_{L^{\infty}}\to 0
, then

u+sh_{n}=\rho_{n}(x,s)u
, with
|\rho_{n}-1|\leq\delta_{n}
. By the relative
continuity assumption,

|g_{t}(x,u+sh_{n})-g_{t}(x,u)|\leq\omega(\delta_{n})u^{q-1}\leq C(u)\omega(\delta_{n})d^{-1}.

Using again Hardy’s inequality, we obtain

\|Q(u+h_{n})-Q(u)-DQ(u)h_{n}\|_{W^{*}}\leq C(u)\omega(\delta_{n})\|h_{n}\|_{W}=o(\|h_{n}\|_{W}).

For
g(x,t)=a(x)t^{q}
, the relative continuity condition follows from

|(\rho t)^{q-1}-t^{q-1}|=t^{q-1}|\rho^{q-1}-1|,\qquad\rho\to 1.

The proof is complete.
∎

Appendix C. Picone-type inequalities

We record the Picone-type inequalities used in the compactness arguments.

Lemma 11.1

(Continuous Picone inequality)
.

Let
u\geq 0
,
v>0
in
\Omega
, and assume that

u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
are such that
u^{2}/v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then

\langle\mathcal{L}u,u\rangle\geq\left\langle\mathcal{L}v,\frac{u^{2}}{v}\right\rangle.

Proof.

It is enough to argue for smooth positive functions; the general case
follows by approximation. The zero-order term cancels. For the principal
part, a direct computation gives

\sigma_{ij}v_{x_{j}}\left(\frac{u^{2}}{v}\right)_{x_{i}}-\sigma_{ij}u_{x_{j}}u_{x_{i}}=-\sigma_{ij}\left(u_{x_{j}}-\frac{u}{v}v_{x_{j}}\right)\left(u_{x_{i}}-\frac{u}{v}v_{x_{i}}\right)\leq 0.

Here we use the symmetry and ellipticity of
(\sigma_{ij})
. Integration
over
\Omega
gives the claim.
∎

Lemma 11.2

(Discrete Picone inequality)
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{r}
be symmetric and assume that

a_{ij}\leq 0
for
i\neq j
. Then, for every

\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{r}
and every

\mathbf{v}\in(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{r})^{\circ}
,

\mathbf{u}^{T}A\mathbf{u}\geq(A\mathbf{v})\cdot\frac{\mathbf{u}^{2}}{\mathbf{v}},\qquad\frac{\mathbf{u}^{2}}{\mathbf{v}}:=\left(\frac{u_{1}^{2}}{v_{1}},\ldots,\frac{u_{r}^{2}}{v_{r}}\right).

Proof.

Set
z_{i}:=u_{i}/v_{i}
. By symmetry,

\mathbf{u}^{T}A\mathbf{u}-(A\mathbf{v})\cdot\frac{\mathbf{u}^{2}}{\mathbf{v}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{r}a_{ij}v_{i}v_{j}(z_{i}-z_{j})^{2}.

The diagonal terms vanish, while
a_{ij}\leq 0
for
i\neq j
. Hence the
right-hand side is nonnegative.
∎

Corollary 11.1

(Componentwise form)
.

Let
A^{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,m
, be symmetric matrices with nonpositive
off-diagonal entries. Then, for

\mathbf{u}=(\mathbf{u}^{1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{m})\geq 0
and

\mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{v}^{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}^{m})>0
,

\sum_{k=1}^{m}(\mathbf{u}^{k})^{T}A^{k}\mathbf{u}^{k}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{m}(A^{k}\mathbf{v}^{k})\cdot\frac{(\mathbf{u}^{k})^{2}}{\mathbf{v}^{k}}.

Remark 11.1
.

In the Galerkin arguments the discrete Picone inequality is applied to the
nodal coefficient vectors. Thus the vector
(u_{i}^{2}/v_{i})_{i=1}^{r}
belongs
to the finite-dimensional coefficient space, although the pointwise quotient

u_{r}^{2}/v_{r}
need not be a finite-element function.
