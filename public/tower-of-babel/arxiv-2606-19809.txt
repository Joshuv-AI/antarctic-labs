Photon surfaces extension in general spherical dust collapse
Roberto Giambò, Camilla Lucamarini

Abstract
We extend the analysis of photon surfaces in spherical dust collapse to the general, non-marginally bound case, i.e. allowing the energy function k ⁡ ( x ) k(x) of the Lemaître–Tolman–Bondi (LTB) model to be non-zero. Starting from the dynamical-systems formulation developed in our previous work for the marginally bound case [ 10 ] , we derive the photon surface equations for the LTB metric with k ⁡ ( x ) ≠ 0 k(x)\neq 0 , and we recast the geometric condition for a timelike hypersurface to be a photon surface as a non-autonomous first-order dynamical system. Even though the LTB evolution equation does not integrate in closed form when k ⁡ ( x ) ≠ 0 k(x)\neq 0 , the implicit solutions available in the literature, together with comparison theorems for ordinary differential equations, are sufficient to show that the only physically acceptable extension of the exterior photon sphere r = 3 ​ M r=3M into the collapsing dust cloud is a null hypersurface, generated by outgoing radial null geodesics. Combining this fact with the known results on the causal structure of the general LTB model, we then establish that the photon surface reaches the central singularity if and only if the singularity is naked, thereby extending to the general case the picture already known in the marginally bound regime. The structural dichotomy between the naked and covered end states is also discussed in connection with possible observational signatures in the early-time evolution of black-hole shadows.



Keywords:
photon surface, dust collapse, Lemaître–Tolman–Bondi
model, black hole, naked singularity, non-marginally bound case, gravitational
collapse, shadow.

1
Introduction

The notion of photon sphere classically arises in static and spherically symmetric
black-hole spacetimes, such as the Schwarzschild solution, as a particular
timelike hypersurface on which null geodesics initially tangent to the surface
remain tangent throughout their evolution. In the Schwarzschild geometry the
photon sphere coincides with the timelike cylinder
r=3M
, and it plays a
fundamental role in the analysis of the shadow of a black hole as observed by a
distant observer
[
1
,
2
,
23
]
.

A natural geometric generalisation of this notion to spacetimes lacking specific
symmetries or Killing structures is the concept of
photon surface
,
introduced by Claudel, Virbhadra and Ellis
[
4
]
. By their
definition, a photon surface is a timelike, embedded hypersurface such that every
null geodesic initially tangent to it remains tangent. The construction is
intrinsic, makes no reference to the existence of Killing vectors, and is
therefore well suited to fully dynamical settings such as gravitational collapse,
where no global timelike Killing field is available
[
3
]
.

The detection of black holes through their shadows by the Event Horizon
Telescope
[
1
,
2
]
has further motivated the systematic study of
photon surfaces in dynamical spacetimes, since the photon surface is the natural
geometric object underlying the formation of the shadow in non-stationary
backgrounds. One physically rich setting in which photon surfaces can be
investigated dynamically is gravitational collapse, which provides at the same
time a concrete model of black-hole formation and an arena in which the cosmic
censorship conjecture
[
19
]
can be tested.

In a recent companion paper
[
10
]
(hereafter referred to as

Paper I
), we carried out a systematic study of photon surfaces in the
Lemaître–Tolman–Bondi (LTB) dust collapse model in the
marginally
bound
case, i.e. under the assumption that the
energy function

k(x)
of the
collapsing shells vanishes identically.
The main results of that work can be
summarised as follows:

(i)

the condition that a timelike
SO(3)
-invariant hypersurface in a
general spherically symmetric spacetime be a photon surface can be reformulated
as a non-autonomous first-order dynamical system, with a single auxiliary
variable
y(t)
encoding the tilt of the hypersurface relative to the comoving
observer;

(ii)

the only physically acceptable extension of the exterior photon
sphere
r=3M
into the interior of the collapsing dust cloud is a null
hypersurface, generated by outgoing radial null geodesics;

(iii)

the photon surface reaches the central singularity if and only if
the singularity is naked; in the covered (black-hole) case it terminates at
the regular centre, while in the naked case there exist outgoing radial null
geodesics emanating from the central singularity that escape the photon
surface.

The assumption
k\equiv 0
was adopted in
Paper I
mainly because it
allows the LTB evolution equation to be integrated in closed elementary form,
yielding the explicit expression
r(t,x)=x\bigl(1-\mu(x)t\bigr)^{2/3}
for the
areal radius. This explicit solution greatly simplifies the quantitative
analysis, both for the dynamical system governing the photon surface and for the
comparison arguments needed to study its behaviour near the central singularity.
From a physical and mathematical standpoint, however, it is natural to ask
whether the qualitative picture established in
Paper I
survives when
the marginally bound assumption is dropped. This is a non-trivial question: in
the non-marginally bound case the LTB equation does not admit a closed-form
elementary solution, but only either a parametric (cycloidal/hyperbolic) or an implicit one, and the
photon surface ODE acquires additional terms depending on
k(x)
and on its
derivative
k^{\prime}(x)
. A priori, one cannot exclude that these terms qualitatively
modify the structure of the photon surface, in particular its behaviour near the
central singularity.

The aim of the present paper is to show that this is not the case: the
qualitative picture of
Paper I
is fully robust under the relaxation
of the marginally bound assumption. More precisely, we show that the
dynamical-systems framework developed in
Paper I
naturally adapts to
the general LTB model, and that the unique physically acceptable extension of the
exterior photon sphere remains the outgoing null hypersurface, regardless of the
non-zero initial binding energy of the collapsing shells. We then use the
solution of the LTB equation in the implicit and the comparison results for radial
null geodesics in the general LTB model
[
8
,
17
,
22
]

to establish that the photon surface reaches the central singularity if and only
if the singularity is naked, exactly as in the marginally bound case.

The paper is organised as follows. Section
2
reviews the
general LTB model with
k(x)\neq 0
and collects the facts about the parametric
solution and the causal structure of the central singularity that will be needed
in the sequel. Section
3
specialises the general photon
surface equations of
Paper I
to this setting, states the key Lemma
3.1
,
ruling out the spacelike initial data, and proves the null-extension theorem.
Section
4
analyses the resulting null geodesic ODE separately
in the covered and naked singularity cases, by means of sub- and supersolution
comparison arguments. Section
5
summarises the results and
discusses their physical implications, in particular concerning the structural
differences between the early-time shadows of black holes and naked
singularities formed in collapse.

2
Dust collapsing spacetimes: the general case

2.1
The LTB metric and its evolution equation

We consider a spherically symmetric spacetime whose interior is filled by a
pressureless perfect fluid (dust), with energy–momentum tensor

T=-\rho\,dt\otimes\partial_{t}
, where
\rho=\rho(t,x)
is the rest-mass
density
[
12
,
22
]
. The Einstein field equations with this
matter content reduce, in synchronous comoving coordinates
(t,x,\theta,\varphi)
,
to the Lemaître–Tolman–Bondi (LTB) metric

g=-\,dt^{2}+\frac{r_{x}^{2}}{1+k(x)}\,dx^{2}+r^{2}\,d\Omega^{2},

(1)

where
r=r(t,x)
is the areal radius,
r_{x}=\partial r/\partial x
,
k(x)>-1
is
the so-called
energy function
, and

d\Omega^{2}=d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\!\theta\,d\varphi^{2}
is the round metric on the
unit two-sphere. The evolution of the areal radius is governed by the single PDE

r_{t}(t,x)=-\sqrt{k(x)+\frac{2m(x)}{r(t,x)}},

(2)

where
m(x)
is the Misner–Sharp mass function and the minus sign selects the
collapsing branch (
r_{t}<0
). The functions
k(x)
and
m(x)
are freely
prescribable initial data, subject to standard regularity requirements at the
centre and to the physical condition that the energy density
\rho
be
non-negative.

The sign of
k(x)
classifies the collapsing shells:

•

k(x)=0
:
marginally bound
shells, with zero total mechanical
energy. This is the case treated in
Paper I

[
10
]
.

•

k(x)<0
:
bound
shells, with negative total energy; the collapse
is decelerated by the gravitational potential.

•

k(x)>0
:
unbound
shells, with positive total energy; the shell
is asymptotically free at infinity.

The interior LTB region is defined for
x\in[0,x_{b}]
, and it is matched to a
Schwarzschild exterior across the timelike hypersurface

\Sigma=\{x=x_{b}\}
. The Darmois junction conditions
[
5
]

reduce, in this setting, to the requirement that the Misner–Sharp mass be
continuous across
\Sigma
, i.e. that the Schwarzschild mass coincide with the
boundary value
M=m(x_{b})
; this condition is automatically propagated by
equation (
2
). We refer to
[
10
,
14
,
11
,
15
]
for further details on the matching procedure.

2.2
The marginally bound vs. the general case

In the marginally bound case
k\equiv 0
, equation (
2
) integrates
in closed elementary form, yielding

r(t,x)=x\bigl(1-\mu(x)\,t\bigr)^{2/3},\qquad\mu(x):=\tfrac{3}{2}\sqrt{\tfrac{2m(x)}{x^{3}}},

(3)

where the initial condition is
r(0,x)=x
, and the singularity time of the shell
labelled by
x
is
t_{s}(x)=1/\mu(x)
. In particular, the central singularity
forms at
t_{s}(0)=1/\mu(0)
, which we may normalise to unity without loss of
generality.

For
k(x)\neq 0
, the closed-form expression (
3
) is no longer
available; nevertheless,
equation (
2
) can be integrated to give
r(t,x)
in the implicit form (see
[
16
]
or
[
20
, Section 18.3]
)

t_{s}(r)-t=\sqrt{\frac{r^{3}}{2m(x)}}\,\Gamma\left(-\frac{k(x)r}{2m(x)}\right),

(4)

where the (positive) function
\Gamma
is defined as

\Gamma(y)=\frac{\arcsin\sqrt{y}}{y^{3/2}}-\frac{\sqrt{1-y}}{y}.

(5)

Here

\arcsin
and
\sqrt{y}

denote the principal branches of the complex inverse sine and square root, respectively
1
1

1

In other words,
\Gamma(y)=\frac{\operatorname{arcsinh}\sqrt{-y}}{(-y)^{3/2}}-\frac{\sqrt{1-y}}{y}
for
y<0
.
, we restrict
\Gamma(y)

to real arguments
y<1
and extend it by continuity to
y=0
setting
\Gamma(0)=\tfrac{2}{3}
.

The unbound case, where
k(x)>0
, corresponds to the case where
y
is negative, while the bound case, where
k(x)<0
, corresponds to the case where
y
is positive. The common limit,
y=0
, relates to the marginally bound case
k=0
.

In the equation (
4
), the function
t_{s}(r)
is an integration function that can be specified, as usual, assuming the initial condition
r=x
at
t=0
:

t_{s}(r)=\sqrt{\frac{x^{3}}{2m(x)}}\,\Gamma\left(-\frac{k(x)x}{2m(x)}\right).

(6)

Since the righthand side of (
4
) tends to
0
as
r\to 0^{+}
, the function
t_{s}(x)

expresses the comoving time when
the shell labeled by
x
collapses to
r=0
.

In both regimes (bound and unbound), the standard regularity conditions on the initial data
(
m(x)/x^{3}
smooth and positive,
k(x)/x^{2}
smooth) ensure that
r_{x}(t,x)>0

throughout the region where the cloud is regular. The non-central apparent
horizon curve
t_{h}(x)
, defined for
x>0
by

r(t_{h}(x),x)=2m(x)
, is everywhere smooth on
(0,x_{b}]
.

2.3
Causal structure of the central singularity

The causal structure of the general LTB model has been studied in
depth
[
22
,
6
,
17
,
12
]
. We collect here the
results that will play a role in our analysis, as recasted in the framework of
[
8
]
, where a general class of spacetimes, including the present case as a particular situation, was analyzed. There it is considered a class of spherically symmetric spacetimes

g=-e^{2\nu}\,dt^{2}+e^{2\lambda}\,dx^{2}+r^{2}\,d\Omega^{2},

(7)

with
\lambda,\nu,r
functions of
(t,x)
,
where the function

r\,(r_{t}e^{-\nu})^{2}=2m+r\,\left[(r_{x}e^{-\lambda})^{2}-1\right],

(8)

can be seen as a function of
(x,r)
; therefore, it includes the dependence on
t
through
r(t,x)
. In this case, the final state of the central singularity depends on the Taylor expansion of (
8
) in the variables
(x,r)
. In our case, it takes the form
2m(x)+rk(x)
.
We assume the free functions
m(x)
and
k(x)
admit Taylor expansions of the form

\displaystyle m(x)

\displaystyle=x^{3}(m_{0}+m_{p}x^{p}+o(x^{p})),\qquad m_{0},m_{p}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\ p\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\},

(9)

\displaystyle k(x)

\displaystyle=x^{2}(k_{0}+k_{q}x^{q}+o(x^{q})),\qquad k_{0},k_{q}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\ q\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}.

(10)

These requirements imply
[
9
]
that the initial data are regular at the centre,
and similarly to the marginally bound case, non central singularities are covered, whereas the central shell
x=0
becomes trapped at the same comoving time it becomes singular, i.e.
t_{s}(0)=t_{h}(0)
. In view of this, the central singularity may possibly become naked,
yielding the following
classification
[
9
,
8
]
: setting

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=\min\{p,q\},

(11)

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(2m_{3}+k_{3}\tau)\sqrt{\tau}}{2(2m_{0}+k_{0}\tau)^{3/2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m_{0}}}\left[\Gamma\!\left(-\frac{k_{0}}{2m_{0}}\right)\!\left(\frac{m_{3}}{m_{0}}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{k_{3}}{k_{0}}\right)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{k_{0}}{2m_{0}}}}\!\left(\frac{m_{3}}{m_{0}}-\frac{k_{3}}{k_{0}}\right)\right]

we have that:

•

the central singularity is
naked
if either
n\in\{1,2\}
, or

n=3
with the coefficient
a
in (
9
) suitably large:
a>a_{\text{c}}:=\tfrac{26+15\sqrt{3}}{3}m_{0}.

•

It is
covered
(i.e. a black-hole singularity) otherwise,
i.e. for
n\geq 4
or for
n=3
with
a\leq a_{\text{c}}
.

In other words, the causal nature of the central singularity in the general LTB
model is dictated by the same parameters
n
and
a
controlling the leading
behaviour of the mass profile in the marginally bound case, but together with the energy
function
k(x)
. We shall comment
on this case in Section
5
.

A crucial technical tool, which we shall repeatedly use in the sequel, is the
following comparison property
[
8
]
: the apparent
horizon curve
t_{h}(x)
is a
subsolution
of the outgoing radial null
geodesic equation

t^{\prime}(x)=\frac{r_{x}(t(x),x)}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}},\qquad x\in(0,x_{b}),

(12)

in the sense that
t_{h}^{\prime}(x)\leq r_{x}(t_{h}(x),x)/\sqrt{1+k(x)}
. This property,
which holds for the general LTB metric exactly as in the marginally bound
case
[
10
]
, is the cornerstone of the analysis in
Section
4
.

3
Photon surfaces in dust collapse: the general case

3.1
General dynamical-systems formulation

We briefly recall the framework developed in
Paper I

[
10
]
;
the reader is referred to that paper for full derivations and a complete
discussion. For a general spherically symmetric metric of the form (
7
), the condition that an

SO(3)
-invariant timelike hypersurface

\mathcal{S}=\{(t,x(t),\theta,\varphi)\}
be a photon surface in the sense of
Claudel–Virbhadra–Ellis
[
4
]
is equivalent (cf. Proposition 2 of

Paper I
, and the quasi-local characterisation
of
[
3
]
) to the first-order non-autonomous dynamical system

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{\nu-\lambda}\,y(t),

(13)

\displaystyle\dot{y}(t)

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-y(t)^{2}\bigr)\,e^{\nu-\lambda}\,\frac{r_{x}-r\,\nu_{x}}{r}+y(t)\,\frac{r_{t}-r\,\lambda_{t}}{r},

(14)

where
y(t)\in[-1,1]
is an auxiliary variable encoding the direction of the
tangent vector to the hypersurface relative to the comoving observer. The
boundary values
y=\pm 1
are equilibrium points of (
14
), and they
correspond to outgoing (
y=+1
) and ingoing (
y=-1
) radial null geodesics,
which are trivially photon surfaces (being null hypersurfaces). The intermediate
values
y\in(-1,1)
correspond to genuinely timelike photon surfaces tilted with
respect to the comoving frame.

3.2
Specialization to the LTB metric with
k(x)\neq 0

For the LTB metric (
1
) we read off

e^{\nu}=1
and
e^{\lambda}=r_{x}/\sqrt{1+k(x)}
, hence

e^{\nu-\lambda}=\sqrt{1+k(x)}/r_{x}
.
The corresponding strictly equivalent
first-order system reads:

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}{r_{x}}\,y(t),

(15)

\displaystyle\dot{y}(t)

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-y(t)^{2}\bigr)\,\left[\frac{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}{r}+y(t)\,\left(\frac{r_{t}}{r}-\frac{r_{tx}}{r_{x}}\right)\right].

(16)

For
k\equiv 0
the system (
15
)–(
16
) reduces to
system (13a)–(13b) of
Paper I
. The presence of the factor

\sqrt{1+k(x)}
in (
15
) is a genuine effect of the non-marginally
bound regime; as we shall see, in the model studied here it does not alter the qualitative behaviour of
the photon surface near the central singularity.

3.3
Initial conditions and admissibility

The exterior photon surface, in the Schwarzschild geometry, is located at
r=3M
.
Its extension into the LTB interior must therefore start from the boundary

\Sigma=\{x=x_{b}\}
at the comoving time
t_{0}
defined implicitly by

r(t_{0},x_{b})=3\,m(x_{b}).

(17)

The existence and uniqueness of
t_{0}
are guaranteed by the strict monotonicity
of
r(\cdot,x_{b})
in
t
, which follows from
r_{t}<0
.

The second piece of initial data for system (
15
)–(
16
)
is the value
y_{0}=y(t_{0})
, which encodes the tilt of the photon surface at the
matching point. The requirement that the surface be non-spacelike at
t_{0}

forces
|y_{0}|\leq 1
. The boundary cases
y_{0}=\pm 1
correspond, as recalled
above, to outgoing/ingoing null extensions, when the trajectory remains on the
null hypersurface
y\equiv\pm 1
for all
t
.; the case
y_{0}=-1
is physically
irrelevant since the ingoing radial null geodesic does not extend the exterior
photon surface continuously. The remaining question is whether some
intermediate value
y_{0}\in(-1,1)
may yield a geometrically and physically
acceptable timelike extension.

The following lemma, which is the central analytical result of the present
paper, rules out all such intermediate values and forces
y_{0}=1
. If
y_{0}<0
the solution would not live in the interior spacetime for
t<t_{0}
, because by (
15
) the function
x(t)
would be decreasing. Then the only relevant case that must be discussed is when
y_{0}
is positive. This discussion is provided by the following crucial result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(x(t),y(t))
be a solution of system (
15
)–(
16
)
with initial data
\bigl(x(t_{0}),y(t_{0})\bigr)=(x_{b},y_{0})
,
y_{0}\in(0,1)
. Then
there exists
\tilde{t}<t_{0}
such that
x(\tilde{t})=x_{b}
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same strategy as Lemma 1 in

Paper I

[
10
]
, adapted to the presence of the
factor
\sqrt{1+k(x)}
in system (
15
)–(
16
) which prevents to use an explicit expression for
r(t,x)
. We
organise the argument in two steps: first, we establish the existence of an
intermediate time
t_{1}<t_{0}
at which
y(t_{1})=0
and
x(t_{1})>0
; second, we use
this fact to show that the trajectory re-intersects the star boundary at an
earlier comoving time.

Step 1.
As a first and crucial claim, let us establish that

\exists\,t_{1}<t_{0}\ :\ y(t_{1})=0,\quad x(t_{1})>0.

(18)

By contradiction, suppose that (
18
) is false. Then either
y(t)

remains positive for all
t<t_{0}
, or it vanishes only when
x(t)
does as
well. In both cases there exists

t_{\inf}\in[-\infty,t_{0})
such that

\lim_{t\to t_{\inf}^{+}}x(t)=0.

(19)

Indeed, in the first case equation (
15
) would give
x(t)

increasing for
t\in(t_{\inf},t_{0})
, and hence the limit

\lim_{t\to t_{\inf}^{+}}x(t)
exists and must necessarily be zero (otherwise

y
would have to vanish at a positive value of
x
, contradicting the
assumption).

Using (
15
)–(
16
) we obtain, by dividing the two
equations,

\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{\dot{y}}{\dot{x}}=\frac{1-y^{2}}{y}\,\frac{r_{x}}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}\left[\frac{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}{r}+y\left(\frac{r_{t}}{r}-\frac{r_{tx}}{r_{x}}\right)\right],

(20)

whence, separating the variables and integrating between
y(t)
and
y_{0}
on
the left, and between
x(t)
and
x_{b}
on the right,

\int_{y(t)}^{y_{0}}\frac{y}{1-y^{2}}\,dy=\int_{x(t)}^{x_{b}}\left[\frac{r_{x}}{r}\left(1+\frac{y(x)}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}\,r_{t}\right)-\frac{y(x)}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}\,r_{tx}\right]dx.

(21)

The integral on the left-hand side becomes

\frac{1}{2}\int_{y(t)}^{y_{0}}\frac{2y}{1-y^{2}}\,dy=-\frac{1}{2}\ln\!\left(1-y^{2}\right)\bigg|_{y(t)}^{y_{0}}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1-y(t)^{2}}{1-y_{0}^{2}},

(22)

which is bounded from above,

\forall t\in(t_{\inf},t_{0})
.

Let us now evaluate the integral on the right-hand side of (
21
).
For convenience we split the evaluation into two pieces. First, in view
of (
2
), and recalling that
r_{x}/r>0
, we have

\int_{x(t)}^{x_{b}}\frac{r_{x}}{r}\left(1+\frac{y(x)}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}\,r_{t}\right)dx=\int_{x(t)}^{x_{b}}\frac{r_{x}}{r}\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\tfrac{2m(x)}{r}+k(x)}}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}y(x)\right)dx\\
\geq C(t)\int_{x(t)}^{x_{b}}\frac{r_{x}}{r}\,dx=C(t)\,\ln\frac{3m(x_{b})}{r(t,x(t))},

(23)

where

C(t)=\min_{t\leq t_{0}}\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\tfrac{2m(x(t))}{r(t,x(t))}+k(x(t))}}{\sqrt{1+k(x(t))}}y(t)\right).

Observing that we are performing an evaluation
outside the horizon (
2m/r<1
), we have
C(t)\geq 0
. Then, since

r(t,x(t))\to 0
as
t\to t_{\inf}
, the quantity in the right hand side of (
23
) tends to

+\infty
if
C(t)
remains bounded away from zero, or remains positive in
case
\limsup_{t\to t_{\inf}}y(t)=1
, which could possibly imply
C(t)=0
.

We therefore obtain a
contradiction if we show that the remaining part of the integral
in (
21
) is bounded: indeed, if
\limsup_{t\to t_{\inf}}y(t)=1
, and possibly there exists a sequence
t_{k}\to t_{\inf}
such that
C(t_{k})\to 0
, the integral
in the left hand side of (
23
) would remain positive but the right hand side in (
22
) would tend to

-\infty
on
t_{k}
; while if
y(t)
remains bounded away from
1
, then
C(t)
would remain bounded away from zero, the integral
in the left hand side of (
23
) would tend to
+\infty
(by comparison with the right hand side of (
23
)) and that in (
22
) would remain
bounded.

So let us evaluate the remaining integral in (
21
). Thanks
to (
2
), we have, integrating by parts,

\begin{split}-\int_{x(t)}^{x_{b}}\!\frac{y(x)}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}\,r_{tx}\,dx&=\int_{x(t)}^{x_{b}}\!\frac{y(x)}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}\!\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{2m(x)}{r}+k(x)}\right)_{\!x}\!dx\\[2.0pt]
&=\frac{y(x)}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}\,\sqrt{\tfrac{2m(x)}{r}+k(x)}\,\bigg|_{x(t)}^{x_{b}}-\int_{x(t)}^{x_{b}}\!\sqrt{\tfrac{2m(x)}{r}+k(x)}\,d\!\left(\frac{y(x)}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}\right)\!.\end{split}

(24)

The first quantity in the right-hand side of (
24
) is obviously
bounded (again, recall
2m/r<1
), whereas the second integral transforms as

\int_{x(t)}^{x_{b}}\!\sqrt{\tfrac{2m(x)}{r}+k(x)}\,d\!\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}\right)\leq C_{2}\,\frac{y}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}}\bigg|_{t}^{t=t_{0}},

(25)

with
C_{2}>0
existing, again, since the evaluation is performed outside the horizon. Then the integral in (
25
) is bounded as well and, as observed before, we reach a contradiction,
and therefore (
18
) must hold.

Step 2.
From now on we argue similarly as in Lemma 1 of

Paper I
:
x(t)
actually satisfies (see
[
10
, (12)]
) the second order ODE

\begin{split}\ddot{x}(t)&=\frac{k(x)+1}{r\,r_{x}}+\left(\frac{r_{t}}{r}-\frac{2r_{tx}}{r_{x}}\right)\dot{x}\\
&\quad-\left(\frac{r_{x}}{r}+\frac{r_{xx}}{r_{x}}-\frac{k^{\prime}(x)}{2\bigl(k(x)+1\bigr)}\right)\dot{x}^{2}+\frac{r_{x}\bigl(r\,r_{tx}-r_{x}\,r_{t}\bigr)}{r\bigl(k(x)+1\bigr)}\,\dot{x}^{3},\end{split}

(26)

from which it is immediate to conclude, since
\dot{x}(t_{1})=0
, that

t_{1}
must be a minimum for
x(t)
, and therefore for all

t<t_{1}
,
y(t)
remains negative and
x(t)
increases, eventually reaching
again
x_{b}
for some
\tilde{t}<t_{1}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

3.4
The null extension theorem

Lemma
3.1
has the following immediate consequence. Suppose

(x(t),y(t))
is an extension of the exterior photon surface with
|y_{0}|<1
.
By Lemma
3.1
, the trajectory re-intersects
\Sigma=\{x=x_{b}\}
at
some earlier comoving time
\tilde{t}<t_{0}
. At that earlier time, however, we
have

r(\tilde{t},x_{b})>r(t_{0},x_{b})=3M,

(27)

because the collapse is monotonically ingoing (
r_{t}<0
). Hence the extension
does not match the exterior photon sphere
r=3M
at
\tilde{t}
: the matching
would require
r(\tilde{t},x_{b})=3M
, which contradicts the previous inequality.
We conclude that extensions with
|y_{0}|<1
are not physically acceptable.

The case
y_{0}=-1
corresponds to the ingoing radial null geodesic, which is
also incompatible with the matching to the exterior photon sphere
(the ingoing null geodesic moves towards smaller
r
, whereas the photon
sphere
r=3M
is a static hypersurface in the Schwarzschild exterior, matched
at
t_{0}
from outside). Therefore the only physically acceptable extension
corresponds to
y_{0}=+1
, i.e. the outgoing null hypersurface.

Theorem 3.2
.

The general spherical dust collapse model (
1
)–(
2
)
admits an extension of the exterior photon surface
r=3M
into the interior of
the cloud. This is the only physically acceptable extension, and it is given by
the null hypersurface

\mathcal{S}=\bigl\{(t,x(t),\theta,\varphi):\ t<t_{0},\ (\theta,\varphi)\in S^{2}\bigr\},

(28)

where, for each fixed
(\theta,\varphi)
, the curve
(x(t),1)
solves
system (
15
)–(
16
) with initial data

x(t_{0})=x_{b}
and
y(t_{0})=1
.

Remark 3.3
.

Setting
y\equiv 1
in (
15
), the photon surface is generated by
the outgoing radial null geodesic equation

\frac{dt}{dx}=\frac{r_{x}(t(x),x)}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}},\qquad t(x_{b})=t_{0},

(29)

which extends equation (18) of
Paper I
to the case
k(x)\neq 0
. In the
sequel, all the relevant information on the photon surface will be obtained by
analysing this ODE.

Photon surface analysis: black hole vs. naked singularity

By Theorem
3.2
, the qualitative behaviour of the photon
surface is entirely encoded in the outgoing radial null geodesic ODE
(
29
). The analysis splits naturally according to the causal
nature of the central singularity, as classified in
Section
2.3
. In particular, recall that the classification recalled there makes use of the two parameters
n
and
a
defined in (
11
).

4.1
The covered (black-hole) case

Assume that the central singularity is covered, e.g.
n\geq 4

or
n=3
with
a
sufficiently small. Under these
conditions, the results of
[
8
]
imply that
every outgoing radial null geodesic starting in the interior of the cloud with

t<t_{h}
extends backwards to the regular centre
x=0

without
reaching
the central singularity.

The same comparison argument applies, verbatim, to the solution
t(x)
of
(
29
). Since by construction
t(x_{b})=t_{0}<t_{h}(x_{b})
, and since

t_{h}
is a subsolution of (
12
), we obtain

t(x)<t_{h}(x)\qquad\text{for all }x\in(0,x_{b}],

(30)

so that the photon surface stays everywhere below the apparent horizon. The
solution
t(x)
extends backwards from
x_{b}
to
x=0
, with

\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}t(x)=t(0)<t_{s}(0)=1
. Therefore, in the black-hole case,

the photon surface terminates at the regular centre

x=0
, consistently
with the absence of any photon escaping the central singularity.

4.2
The naked singularity case

Assume now that the central singularity is naked, e.g.
n\in\{1,2\}
, or

n=3
with
a
large in (
11
). In principle, following the strategy of

Paper I
, this would mean that a supersolution of (
29
) of the
form

t_{\xi}(x)=t_{s}(0)+\xi\,x^{n},

(31)

exists, with
\xi
chosen so that
t_{\xi}(x)
lies
strictly below the apparent horizon
t_{h}(x)
in a right neighborhood of

x=0
, and at the same time

t_{\xi}^{\prime}(x)\geq\frac{r_{x}(t_{\xi}(x),x)}{\sqrt{1+k(x)}},\qquad 0<x\ll 1.

(32)

The verification of (
32
) should rely on the Taylor expansions of
r

and
r_{x}
derived from the implicit form
(
4
).

However, in these cases, casting the problem into the so-called

supersolution

r_{\xi}=\xi x^{3}

Combining the subsolution
r_{\xi}
with the supersolution
r_{h}

the two-sided estimate

r_{h}(x)=2m(x)\leq r(x)\leq r_{\xi}(x)\qquad\text{for }0<x<x_{b},

(33)

r(x)
of the null geodesic equation is squeezed between

r_{\xi}

x=0

\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}t(x)=t_{s}(0).

(34)

Hence,
the photon surface reaches the central naked singularity

Furthermore, by the very same comparison argument, the outgoing radial null
geodesics

with initial data

to
x=0

below

photon surface, i.e.
t_{g}(x)<t(x)
for all
x\in(0,x_{b}]
; physically, they
correspond to photons that emanate from the central naked singularity and
escape to the exterior, lying outside the photon surface. Therefore, in the
naked singularity case,
the photon surface is insufficient to cover
the central singularity
, and an infinite family of escaping null geodesics
exists.

4.3
Main theorem

The discussion above is summarized by the following result, which extends
Theorem 4 of
Paper I

[
10
]
to the general
non-marginally bound case.

Theorem 4.1
.

Consider the general LTB dust collapse model (
1
)–(
2
)
with mass and energy profiles satisfying (
9
)–(
10
). Let
t(x)
denote the photon surface established in
Theorem
3.2
. Then:

(a)

If the central singularity is covered, the photon surface extends
to the regular centre, with
\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}t(x)<t_{s}(0)
, and no outgoing
null geodesic emanating from the central singularity exists.

(b)

If the central singularity is naked, the photon surface reaches
the singularity, obtaining
\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}t(x)=t_{s}(0)
, and there exists an
infinite family of outgoing radial null geodesics
t_{g}(x)
emanating from
the central naked singularity with
t_{g}(x)<t(x)
for all
x\in(0,x_{b}]
.

In particular, the photon surface reaches the central singularity if and only
if the singularity is naked.

5
Discussion and conclusions

In this paper we have extended the analysis of photon surfaces in spherical
dust collapse, carried out in
Paper I

[
10
]
for
the marginally bound LTB model, to the general non-marginally bound case

k(x)\neq 0
. The main conclusions can be summarised as follows.

1.

By recasting the geometric condition for a timelike hypersurface to be
a photon surface in spherical symmetry as a non-autonomous first-order
dynamical system, we have shown that the unique physically acceptable
extension of the exterior photon sphere
r=3M
into the collapsing dust
cloud is a null hypersurface generated by outgoing radial null geodesics,
irrespective of the initial non-zero binding energy of the shells
(Theorem
3.2
).

2.

By combining this result with the parametric solutions of the LTB
equation and the comparison properties of the apparent horizon, we have
shown that the photon surface reaches the central singularity if and only
if the singularity is naked (Theorem
4.1
).

The extension from
k\equiv 0
to general
k(x)\neq 0
is non-trivial at the
technical level for two main reasons. First, the LTB evolution
equation (
2
) no longer integrates in closed elementary form, so
that the explicit expression (
3
) used in
Paper I
is
unavailable. We have shown that the
solution in the implicit form (
4
), together
with the asymptotic expansions near the centre, provide a sufficient
substitute for the comparison arguments needed to handle both the dynamical
system (
15
)–(
16
) and the null geodesic
ODE (
29
).

Second, the photon surface ODE acquires the factor
1/\sqrt{1+k(x)}
relative
to its marginally bound counterpart. Since
k(0)=0
and
k(x)=O(x^{m})
with

m\geq 2
, these new terms may possibly
alter the dominant terms in the super- and subsolution comparisons, combining with the mass function. But this combination can be treated via the introduction of area-radius coordinates
[
9
,
8
]
, promoting
r
as a new coordinate in place of
t
. In this way the qualitative methods successfully employed in the marginally bound case can be used also for the general LTB collapse - and for a wider class of collapsing solutions, actually.

From a broader perspective, Theorem
4.1
confirms that the link
between the global structure of the photon surface and the local causal nature
of the central singularity is a
robust
feature of the LTB model, not an
artefact of the marginally bound assumption. This structural dichotomy between
the black-hole and the naked-singularity case is consistent with the
expectation, supported by numerical and analytical
studies
[
18
,
21
,
23
,
13
]
, that the
formation and the early-time features of the shadow of a collapsing object are
deeply tied to the causal structure of the entire underlying spacetime,
rather than to any specific choice of initial data or to the asymptotic regime
alone. In particular, the existence of an infinite family of outgoing null
geodesics escaping the photon surface in the naked case, with arbitrarily
large redshift
[
7
]
, suggests that the early-time
shadow of a forming naked singularity should differ substantially from that of
a forming black hole, leaving a potentially distinguishing imprint for
next-generation observational instruments
[
1
,
2
]
.

An immediate natural
extension to be explored concerns the study of photon surfaces in dust collapse models with
non-vanishing tangential stresses
[
14
]
or, more generally, with
non-zero pressure, where the structure of the central singularity and of the
apparent horizon is significantly richer. Finally, the analysis of photon
surfaces in the presence of rotation, although technically considerably more
demanding due to the loss of spherical symmetry, would provide an important
step towards a fully realistic description of the early-time formation of
shadows in astrophysically relevant collapse scenarios.
