# The Discoverie of Witchcraft — Reginald Scot (1584)

**Archive.org:** https://archive.org/details/discoverieofwitc00scot

---

## What Is This?

*The Discoverie of Witchcraft* is the most important skeptical work of the witch trial era. Written by Reginald Scot in 1584, it systematically demolished the case for witchcraft persecution decades before the worst witch panics peaked in Germany and New England.

## Who Was Reginald Scot?

Scot was an English country gentleman and Member of Parliament. He investigated witchcraft claims with a skeptical, empirical eye — rare for his time. His book drew on legal scholarship, classical sources, and firsthand accounts to argue that supposed witches were innocent, mentally vulnerable people caught in a system designed to extract confessions through torture.

## Key Arguments

- **Witches don't exist** — Scot argued the very category was imaginary. There were no actual covens, no pacts with the devil, no supernatural powers.
- **Confessions are torture products** — He documented how witch hunters manufactured confessions through starvation, sleep deprivation, and physical torment.
- **The Malleus Maleficarum is unreliable** — Scot specifically refuted the most influential witch-hunting manual, point by point.
- **Belief in witchcraft is superstition** — He traced the origins of witch beliefs to folklore, not scripture or law.

## Historical Impact

King James VI of Scotland (later James I of England) was so outraged by Scot's book that he ordered all copies burned when he took the English throne in 1603. James himself had written the pro-persecution *Daemonologie* in response to North Berwick witch panic in Scotland. Scot's book went underground but influenced the skeptical tradition in English law.

## Why It Matters## Why It Matters

*The Discoverie* is essential because it's the clearest window into what educated, skeptical people in the 1500s actually thought about witch trials. It's a primary source from inside the era, making it invaluable alongside the pro-persecution treatises it was arguing against.
