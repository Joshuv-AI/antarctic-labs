Scaling Behaviors of Work Cumulants in Slow Isothermal Processes Preprint: APS/123-QED
Ruohan Xu, Yanbo Qiao, H. T. Quan

Abstract
We study the cumulants of work in a slow isothermal process for gapped systems. Using the Martin-Siggia-Rose-De Dominicis-Janssen (MSRDJ) formalism and the properties of connected correlation functions, we show that in this process, the n n -th cumulant of work scales as 1 / T n − 1 1/T^{n-1} , where T T is the time duration. This result holds generally for arbitrary smooth protocols. Furthermore, we derive the coefficients of the cumulants from equilibrium properties. These coefficients are found to be relevant to thermodynamic geometric tensors.

II
Theoretical setup

Suppose the system is simply described by a Langevin equation:

\partial_{t}^{2}\varphi+\gamma_{0}\partial_{t}\varphi+\partial_{\varphi}[V_{0}(\varphi)+V_{t}(\varphi)]=\eta_{t}.

(1)

Here
\gamma_{0}
is the friction coefficient, the noise
\eta_{t}
satisfies
\langle\eta_{t}\eta_{t^{\prime}}\rangle=2D\delta(t-t^{\prime})
where
D
is the diffusion coefficient,
V_{0}
is the confining potential, and
V_{t}
is the driving potential. For a driving process, we can choose
t\in[0,T]
, and
V_{t}(\varphi)|_{t=0}=0
. Also, we specify the initial condition to be the equilibrium distribution. To avoid complex operator algebra, we choose to use MSRDJ field theory. Then the Lagrangian would be

\mathcal{L}_{t}[\varphi,\psi]=\left[-\psi\{\partial_{t}^{2}\varphi+\gamma_{0}\partial_{t}\varphi+\partial_{\varphi}[V_{0}(\varphi)+V_{t}(\varphi)]\}+D\psi^{2}\right].

(2)

We define the static Lagrangian:

\mathcal{L}_{0}[\varphi,\psi]:=\left[-\psi\{\partial_{t}^{2}\varphi+\gamma_{0}\partial_{t}\varphi+\partial_{\varphi}[V_{0}(\varphi)]\}+D\psi^{2}\right].

(3)

Then
\mathcal{L}_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{0}-\psi\partial_{\varphi}[V_{t}(\varphi)]
. The characteristic function of work in field theory is given by
17

\ln\chi(\nu)=\ln\left(\frac{1}{Z}\int\mathcal{D}\varphi\mathcal{D}\psi\exp\left(S+(i\nu)\int_{0}^{T}dt\partial_{t}V_{t}(\varphi)\right)\right).

(4)

Here
S=\int_{-\infty}^{0}dt\mathcal{L}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}dt\mathcal{L}_{t}
. Notice that we extended the time period to ensure the initial condition.

III
Analysis of the work cumulants

III.1
Case 1: linear driving protocol

We first start from the simplest case with linear driving. In this case we have the following result:
For a confined and gapped system with a small linear driving protocol
V_{t}=\frac{t}{T}V_{1}
, the
n
-th cumulant of the work distribution scales as
\kappa_{n}\propto\left(\frac{1}{T}\right)^{n-1}
for large
T
. We will give a detailed derivation in the following.

Using MSRDJ field theory to express the characteristic function of work,

\ln\chi(\nu)=\ln\left(\frac{1}{Z}\int\mathcal{D}\varphi\mathcal{D}\psi\exp\left(S_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}dt\frac{i\nu}{T}V_{1}+\int_{0}^{T}dt\frac{t}{T}\mathcal{V}\right)\right).

(5)

Here
S_{0}=\int_{-\infty}^{T}dt\mathcal{L}_{0}
, and
\mathcal{V}:=-\psi\partial_{\varphi}[V_{1}(\varphi)]
, which is of the same order as
V_{1}
. Using the perturbation theory to expand in orders of
V_{1}
, we have:

\displaystyle\ln\chi

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\nu)^{m}}{m!T^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{T^{n}n!}\int_{0}^{T}dt_{1}\dots dt_{m+n}t_{1}\dots t_{n}\times\langle\mathcal{V}(t_{1})\dots\mathcal{V}(t_{n})V_{1}(t_{n+1})\dots V_{1}(t_{m+n})\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}

(6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\nu)^{m}}{m!n!}\int_{0}^{1}ds_{1}\dots ds_{m+n}Ts_{1}\dots Ts_{n}\times\langle\mathcal{V}(Ts_{1})\dots\mathcal{V}(Ts_{n})V_{1}(Ts_{n+1})\dots V_{1}(Ts_{m+n})\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}.

Because the original system is gapped, the connected correlation function decays exponentially
2
. By defining the maximum temporal separation
\Delta t_{\max}=\max_{i,j}|t_{i}-t_{j}|
and the correlation function

G_{mn}(t_{1},...t_{n};t_{n+1}...t_{n+m})=\langle\mathcal{V}(t_{1})\dots V_{1}(t_{m+n})\rangle_{c}

, we have (by the exponential clustering property of gapped systems)
2
;
32
;
10
;
20

|G_{mn}|<A_{mn}\exp(-\gamma\Delta t_{\max}/n).

(7)

Here
\gamma
is a uniform constant, and
A_{mn}
is the prefactor bounding the amplitude. Then we fix
s_{1}
and integrate out other time coordinates,

\ln\chi=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\nu)^{m}T^{n}}{m!n!}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}ds_{1}d(s_{2}-s_{1})...d(s_{n}-s_{1})ds_{n+1}...ds_{n+m}(s_{1}^{n}+\Delta)G_{mn}\right\}.

(8)

Here the remaining term is defined as
\Delta:=\Pi_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}-s_{1}^{n}
. If we evaluate the integral of this term using a loose bound

|\Delta|\leq\sum_{i=2}^{n}(|s_{i}-s_{1}|)
,

\int_{0}^{1}ds_{1}d(s_{2}-s_{1})...d(s_{n}-s_{1})|\Delta|\times|G_{mn}|\leq\int_{0}^{1}ds_{1}d(s_{2}-s_{1})...d(s_{n}-s_{1})A_{mn}(\sum_{i=2}^{n}|s_{i}-s_{1}|)\exp(-\gamma T \frac{\sum_{i=2}^n|s_i-s_1|}{n^2}).

Integrating the relative time factors
(s_{i}-s_{1})
against the exponential decay
\exp(-\gamma T|s_i - s_1|/n^2)
gives an additional suppression factor of
1/(\gamma T)
compared to the leading term. This leads to the following result

\ln\chi=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\frac{T}{m!n!}\left(\frac{i\nu}{T}\right)^{m}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}dss^{n}C_{mn}(s,T)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma T}\right)\right\}.

(9)

where we define

C_{mn}(s,T):=\int_{0}^{T}dt_{2}...dt_{m+n}G_{mn}(sT,t_{2},...t_{m+n}).

(10)

Because of the exponential decay property, we can replace the integral bound with
\pm\infty
with a
\mathcal{O}(e^{-\gamma\min(s,1-s)T})
error, which can be absorbed into the
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\gamma T})
term for any finite
s\in(0,1)
. Then
C_{mn}
is a constant due to the time-translation symmetry of
S_{0}
. Defining
\mathcal{C}_{m}:=\sum_{n}\frac{1}{n!}\int_{0}^{1}dss^{n}C_{mn}
, we have

\ln\chi=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\left(\frac{i\nu}{T}\right)^{m}T\left(\mathcal{C}_{m}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma T}\right)\right).

(11)

Taking the derivative with respect to
\nu
directly leads to our scaling relation.

Also, noticing that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}s^{n}C_{mn}:=K_{m}\left(s\right)

(12)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt_{2}\dots dt_{m}\langle V_{1}(0)V_{1}(t_{2})\dots V_{1}(t_{m})\rangle_{\mathrm{c},s}

(13)

where
\langle\dots\rangle_{s}
means the average is taken with the static action
S_{0}+s\int dt^{\prime}\mathcal{V}(t^{\prime})
, which is exactly the equilibrium action at time
sT
. This is just a special case of thermodynamic geometric tensors. So we can have a more compact result:

\ln\chi(\nu)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\left(\frac{i\nu}{T}\right)^{m}T\left(\int_{0}^{1}dsK_{m}(s)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma T}\right)\right).

(14)

This result is quite interesting, since it not only gives the scaling of
\kappa_{n}
, but also gives the exact coefficient using
equilibrium
properties:

\kappa_{n}=\left(\frac{1}{T}\right)^{n-1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}dsK_{n}(s)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma T}\right)\right).

(15)

In fact, this result can be proved in a much more general case, as illustrated in the following discussion.

III.2
Case 2: generic protocol

For a confined gapped system with
C^{2,1}
driving
V_{t}=\sum_{i}\lambda^{i}\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)V_{i}
, for large
T
we have

\kappa_{n}=\left(\frac{1}{T}\right)^{n-1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}ds\dot{\lambda}^{\mu_{1}}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu_{2}}\dots\dot{\lambda}^{\mu_{n}}\mathcal{K}_{n(\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n})}(s)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma T}\right)\right).

(16)

Here

\mathcal{K}_{n(\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n})}(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt_{2}\dots dt_{n}\langle{V}_{\mu_{1}}(0){V}_{\mu_{2}}(t_{2})\dots{V}_{\mu_{n}}(t_{n})\rangle_{\mathrm{c},s}.

(17)

The derivation is similar to the linear driving case. Following previous derivation,

\ln\chi=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\nu)^{m}T^{n}}{m!n!}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}ds_{1}\dots ds_{m+n}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\lambda^{\mu_{k}}\left(s_{k}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu_{n+j}}\left(s_{n+j}\right)\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mu_{1}}(s_{1}T)\dots\mathcal{V}_{\mu_{n}}(s_{n}T)\dots V_{\mu_{m+n}}(s_{m+n}T)\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}\right\}.

(18)

Using
\left|\prod_{k=1}^{n}f^{\mu_{k}}\left(s_{k}\right)-\prod_{k=1}^{n}f^{\mu_{k}}\left(s_{1}\right)\right|\leq C\max(|\Delta s|)
for
C^{2,1}
functions on
[0,1]^{n}
,

\displaystyle\ln\chi=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\nu)^{m}T^{n}}{m!n!}\Bigg\{\int_{0}^{1}ds_{1}\dots ds_{m+n}

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\lambda^{\mu_{k}}\left(s_{1}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\max_{i=2}^{n}(|s_{i}-s_{1}|))\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu_{n+j}}\left(s_{1}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\max_{i=n+1}^{n+m}(|s_{i}-s_{1}|))\right)

(19)

\displaystyle\times\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mu_{1}}(Ts_{1})\dots\mathcal{V}_{\mu_{n}}(Ts_{n})V_{\mu_{n+1}}(Ts_{n+1})\dots V_{\mu_{m+n}}(Ts_{m+n})\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}\Bigg\}.

Again, the integration of the deviations is controlled by the exponential decay of the correlations, and results in a correction of order
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma T}\right)
. As before, resummation leads to

\ln\chi(\nu)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\left(\frac{i\nu}{T}\right)^{m}T\left(\int_{0}^{1}ds\dot{\lambda}^{\mu_{1}}\dots\dot{\lambda}^{\mu_{m}}\mathcal{K}_{m(\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{m})}(s)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma T}\right)\right).

(20)

We thus derived the power-law scaling of
\kappa_{n}
conjectured in Ref.
27
for arbitrary smooth protocols.

This result directly shows the geometric structure behind the work cumulants. For the special case of
n=2
, this tensor
\mathcal{K}_{2(\mu\nu)}
is just the thermodynamic metric introduced by Crooks and Sivak
5
;
29
. For higher orders, the geometry is not Riemannian, but a special case of Finsler geometry as in
3
. However, unlike the
C_{\nu_{1}\dots\nu_{n}}
tensors in
3
, all
\mathcal{K}_{n}
tensors here are fully time-independent, which is more well-defined in geometry. This property comes from the fact that high-order cumulants only capture the non-Gaussian and thus nontrivial part of the work distribution, excluding the non-decaying background of correlations.

It should also be noticed that this proof depends on the finite gap condition, but not on the detailed balance condition. It will break down if the gap is closed and the
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma T}\right)
term is no longer a small value. On the other hand, generalization to non-equilibrium steady state without detailed balance would be possible.

IV
Example: The Breathing Oscillator

To explicitly verify the validity of our field-theoretic framework and the generalized geometric tensors, we consider an exactly solvable toy model: the overdamped harmonic oscillator with a time-dependent stiffness. For simplicity, we set the inverse temperature
\beta=1
and the friction coefficient
\gamma_{0}=1
. The potential is given by
V_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\lambda(t)x^{2}
. We choose an inverse-time driving protocol
\lambda(t)=\frac{1}{1+ct}

14
, where
c=\frac{1}{T}
denotes the driving rate.

The generalized force conjugate to the driving parameter is
P:=\partial_{\lambda}{V_{t}}=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}
. In equilibrium at a fixed
\lambda
, the position
x(t)
follows an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, a Gaussian process with the two-point correlation function
C(t)=\langle x(0)x(t)\rangle_{\mathrm{eq}}=\frac{1}{\lambda}e^{-\lambda|t|}
.

According to case 2, the
n
-th cumulant of work is completely determined by the
n
-point connected correlation function of
P
. Using Wick’s theorem, the connected diagram of
n
squared variables forms a single cycle. Taking into account the permutation of vertices and the internal exchange of legs, the symmetry factor is exactly
\frac{(n-1)!}{2}
. The connected correlator thus factorizes into a convolution ring of two-point functions:

\langle P(t_{1})\dots P(t_{n})\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{(n-1)!}{2}\prod_{\mathrm{cycle}}C(t_{i},t_{i+1}).

(21)

To obtain the local geometric tensor
\mathcal{K}_{n}(\lambda)
, we integrate over the
n-1
relative time coordinates. This temporal convolution can be evaluated as a 1-loop integral in the Fourier frequency domain:

I_{n}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\left(\frac{2}{\omega^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right)^{n}=\frac{(2n-3)!!}{(n-1)!\lambda^{2n-1}}.

(22)

Multiplying by the symmetry factor yields the higher-order local metric tensor:

\mathcal{K}_{n}(\lambda)=\frac{(n-1)!}{2}\cdot I_{n}=\frac{(2n-3)!!}{2\lambda^{2n-1}}.

(23)

The
n
-th cumulant
\kappa_{n}
is obtained by integrating this local tensor along the driving manifold. Using the time derivative of our protocol
\dot{\lambda}=-c\lambda^{2}
, the integral simplifies to:

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}dt\,(\dot{\lambda})^{n}\mathcal{K}_{n}(\lambda(t))

(24)

\displaystyle=(-c)^{n}\frac{(2n-3)!!}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{1+ct}dt

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\frac{(2n-3)!!}{2}c^{n-1}\ln(1+cT).

This analytic scaling yields
\kappa_{n}\propto\frac{1}{T^{n-1}}
for all
n\geq 1
.

To verify this field-theoretic asymptotic result, we compare it with the exact solution of the characteristic function of work
\Psi(t)=\mathbb{E}(e^{qW})
, where
q
is the counting field. Following the moment-generating framework driven by an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process
31
, the generating function can be analytically derived as (see Appendix
A
for detailed derivations):

\displaystyle\ln(\mathbb{E}(e^{qW}))=-\frac{1}{2}\ln[

\displaystyle\frac{q-m_{-}}{m_{+}-m_{-}}(1+cT)^{m_{+}}

(25)

\displaystyle+\frac{m_{+}-q}{m_{+}-m_{-}}(1+cT)^{m_{-}}\Bigg],

where the parameters
m_{\pm}
are defined by
m_{\pm}=[-(c+2)\pm\sqrt{(c+2)^{2}+8qc}]/{2c}
.

Comparison between exact (solid) and asymptotic (dashed) results of case 2. The parameters can be found in the first paragraph of Sec.

Expansion of this solution in the slow-driving limit (
c\to 0
with
cT
fixed) reproduces our diagrammatic formula of all cumulants (see Appendix
A
). This agreement, as visualised in Fig.
1
, demonstrates that the field-theoretic framework captures the scaling behaviors of work cumulants in slow driving limit.

V
Conclusion

In conclusion, we have proven the
(1/T)^{n-1}
scaling behavior of work cumulants conjectured in Ref.
27
in a slow isothermal process for gapped systems using MSRDJ field theory. This protocol-independent power-law scaling behavior naturally emerges from the dimensional reduction of time integrals, which is guaranteed by the exponential decay of connected correlation functions in gapped systems.
We also showed that the exact scaling coefficients define a set of higher-order thermodynamic geometric tensors, extending the conventional Riemannian thermodynamic metric
29
to a broader geometric framework.

Finally, we emphasize that this scaling fundamentally relies on the presence of a mass gap. If the system is driven across a continuous phase transition, the correlation time diverges. In such non-adiabatic regimes, the
1/T
expansion breaks down, leading to the emergence of critical scaling exponents
9
;
8
;
36
.

Appendix A
Exact Solution for the Breathing Harmonic Oscillator

In this Appendix, we provide the detailed derivation of the characteristic function of work for the time-dependent breathing oscillator presented in Sec.
IV
.

Let
\Psi(t)=\mathbb{E}(e^{qW})
and
\Phi(t)=\langle x^{2}e^{qW}\rangle
. According to Ref.
31
, their joint time evolution is governed by the following system of ordinary differential equations:

\dot{\Psi}=\frac{q\dot{\lambda}}{2}\Phi,\quad\dot{\Phi}=-2\lambda\Phi+2\Psi+\frac{3q\dot{\lambda}}{2}\frac{\Phi^{2}}{\Psi}.

(26)

By introducing the ratio
y(t)=\Phi(t)/\Psi(t)
, the coupled system reduces to a single nonlinear Riccati equation:

\dot{y}=2-2\lambda y+q\dot{\lambda}y^{2}.

(27)

To linearize this equation, we employ the standard substitution
y(t)=-\frac{1}{q\dot{\lambda}}\frac{\dot{u}}{u}
. Under the protocol
\lambda(t)=\frac{1}{1+ct}

14
, we change the independent variable to
z=1+ct
, which transforms the Riccati equation into a second-order linear Euler-Cauchy equation:

z^{2}u^{\prime\prime}(z)+2\left(1+\frac{1}{c}\right)zu^{\prime}(z)-\frac{2q}{c}u(z)=0.

(28)

The general solution of this equation is a linear combination of power functions
u(z)=Az^{m_{+}}+Bz^{m_{-}}
, where the characteristic roots are given by:

m_{\pm}=\frac{-(c+2)\pm\sqrt{(c+2)^{2}+8qc}}{2c}.

(29)

Imposing the initial condition at the equilibrium state
t=0
, we have
y(0)=\langle x^{2}\rangle_{\text{eq}}=1
. Solving for the integration constants
A
and
B
, and integrating
\dot{\Psi}/\Psi
back, we arrive at the final expression for the generating function presented in the main text. Expanding Eq.(
29
) at
c\to 0
and retaining the
\mathcal{O}(c^{n-1})
order (where
m_{-}
term is neglected) yields
\kappa_{n}=(-1)^{n}\frac{(2n-3)!!}{2}c^{n-1}\ln(1+cT)
. The remainder contributes
\mathcal{O}(c^{n})
and vanishes.
