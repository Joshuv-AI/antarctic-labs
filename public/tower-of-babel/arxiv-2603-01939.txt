Dispersive estimates for a system of tensorial quasilinear wave equations satisfying the weak-null condition
Sari Ghanem

Abstract
We establish both global existence and decay properties for solutions with small data for a general class of coupled system of tensorial quasilinear hyperbolic wave equations in three space dimensions, that covers the dynamical Einstein equations coupled to a class of non-linear matter sources that do not satisfy the null condition of Christodoulou and Klainerman, and have new different non-linearities than the one treated by Lindblad-Rodnianski, for which their celebrated seminal L ∞ L^{\infty} -estimate does not work, to the best of our knowledge. Global existence of solutions for a general class of quasilinear wave equations satisfying the weak-null condition, with small initial data , is largely an open problem at present. There is no known theory to prove decay for the class of non-linear hyperbolic partial differential equations that we treat in this paper. We establish a technique based on novel decoupling of the higher order energy estimates, at the level of the L 2 L^{2} -norm of the Lie derivatives of the tangential components , without involving all the other components, up to some good factor. This generalizes our previous results to include new non-linearities that are not present in the Einstein-Yang-Mills system in the Lorenz gauge.

2
Strategy of the proof

2.1
The continuity argument

We define the following higher order energy norm.

Definition 2.1.1
.

We define the energy norm as follows

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{k}(t)

(2.1)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|\leq k}\big[\|w_{\gamma}^{1/2}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}(\Phi^{(0)}-h^{0})(t,\cdot))\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t}^{ext})}+\sum_{l\in\{1,\ldots,N\}}\|w_{\gamma}^{1/2}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J}}\Phi^{(l)}(t,\cdot))\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t}^{ext})}\big]\;,

where the integration is taken on
\Sigma_{t}^{ext}({\cal K})
with respect to the Lebesgue measure
dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3}
, and where the weight
w_{\gamma}
is given in Definition
1.3.4
.

We are going to run a bootstrap argument on the higher order energy (
2.1
) with the weight
w_{\gamma}
, using the fact that the energy grows continuously in time
t
. We assume that the higher order energy satisfies the following bound, for all time
t
in any interval
[0,T]
, with
T>0
,

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{k}(t)\leq E(k)\cdot\epsilon\cdot(1+t)^{\delta}\;,

(2.2)

for some
\epsilon>0
and
\delta>0
to be determined later.

Notation 2.1.2
.

Here we set always the constant
E(k):=1
for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, but this constant will be used in our estimates to indicate the number of derivatives for which we are using our bootstrap assumption (
2.2
).

Then, we are going to prove that this à priori bound implies for
k=K
large enough, a strictly better bound, say it implies that

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{K}(t)\leq\frac{1}{2}\cdot E(K)\cdot\epsilon\cdot(1+t)^{\delta}\;.

(2.3)

Thus, by continuity of
{\cal E}_{K}(t)
, and by the fact that we start with an initial data
{\cal E}_{K}(0)
satisfying the bound, this implies that bound (
2.2
) is an actual true bound on the whole time interval
[0,\infty)
by well-posedness of the equations (
1.19
)-(
1.20
) in the exterior region.

2.2
The recursive formula of Grönwall type inequalities

The way we are going to prove the strict upgrade of inequality (
2.2
) is by proving a recursive formula of Grönwall type inequalities, such that for initial data small enough, it allows one to upgrade (
2.2
) by Grönwall lemma. More precisely, our goal is to prove an inequality of the following type, for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and most crucially, without using a bootstrap assumption, (
2.2
), on higher derivatives than a certain
k=K
large enough ,

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon\cdot(1+t)^{c_{0}\cdot\epsilon}+\epsilon\cdot{\cal E}_{k}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\epsilon}{(1+\tau)^{1-c\cdot\epsilon}}\cdot{\cal E}_{k-1}(\tau)\cdot d\tau

(2.4)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\epsilon}{(1+\tau)}\cdot{\cal E}_{k}(\tau)\cdot d\tau\;.

Here
{\cal E}_{k-1}=0
when
k=0
. Thus, by choosing
\epsilon
and the initial data
{\cal E}_{k}(0)
small enough, we would be able to upgrade (
2.2
) for a certain
k
large enough, without using an assumption (
2.2
) on higher derivatives than
k
. One recurrent argument, among others, that we use in order to close the bootstrap (i.e. not involve more derivatives than what we upgrade for), is to use the fact that when one differentiates a product, one term does not get more than half of the derivatives, and we can then use the bootstrap assumption on that term, using only half of the derivatives and a certain loss of derivatives.

We note that it is crucial to have the factor
\frac{1}{(1+\tau)}
infront of the leading term with the highest number of derivatives, that is
{\cal E}_{k}(\tau)
. A a factor with an
\epsilon
loss in the decay rate, namely
\frac{\epsilon}{(1+\tau)^{1-c\cdot\epsilon}}
is tolerable only for the lower order derivatives
{\cal E}_{k-1}(\tau)
, so that we could upgrade the bootstrap assumption (
2.2
). Also, it is important to have an
\epsilon
, or quantities that could be chosen arbitrarily small, for the other constants, as this is how we can
upgrade
.
We are going to show that there exists a certain
\delta>0
in (
2.2
), to be determined later, and that there exists a certain
\epsilon>0
that can be chosen arbitrarily small, such that (
2.4
) holds for
k\geq K
, without using the bootstrap assumption (
2.2
) on more than
k
derivatives. Therefore, for
\epsilon
small enough and for initial data
{\cal E}_{k}
small enough, one could upgrade (
2.2
). It is however, crucial that there exists
K
such that (
2.4
) holds for all
k\geq K
without using an assumption on the growth of higher derivatives than
k
. This is simply called closing a bootstrap argument.

2.3
The idea of the proof

In fact, our strategy to establish (
2.4
), is to use the bootstrap assumption and our new decoupled energy estimates from Subsection
2.4
, in order to transform the
\epsilon
factor in (
2.2
) into a power that controls the growth of the energy, i.e. to prove first that (
2.2
) implies a better estimate that is

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{k}(t)\leq E(k+k_{0})\cdot\epsilon\cdot(1+t)^{c\cdot\epsilon}\;.

(2.5)

with a loss of
k_{0}
derivatives, thus not closing yet the bootstrap argument. Here the constant
E(k+k_{o}):=1
is just a notation to tell us that we used our bootstrap assumption (
2.2
) on no more than
k+k_{0}
derivatives, thus, such an estimate is not yet an upgrade since we use more derivatives than what we upgrade for, namely
k

4.1

Now, if we would like, we could start by estimating the products in (

This would be crucially important to get a decoupled energy estimate that does not use more derivatives than what it allows to upgrade for. We have no choice other than to estimate the sources of the wave equations by using the bootstrap assumption on all the derivatives involved, in order to derive à priori estimates. \displaystyle\frac{(1+t)\cdot w_{\gamma^{\prime}}(q)}{\epsilon\cdot(1+t+|q|)^{2}}\cdot|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J^{\prime}}}H|^{2}\cdot{|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}A)|^{2}}

(4.13)

we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}\frac{(1+t)\cdot w_{\gamma^{\prime}}(q)}{\epsilon\cdot(1+t+|q|)^{2}}\cdot|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J^{\prime}}}H|^{2}\cdot{|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi^{(l)})|^{2}}\Big)r^{2}drd\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}E(|J|+2)\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{(1+t+|q|)^{3-4\delta}(1+|q|)^{1+2\delta+2(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})}}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E(|J|+2)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{(1+t)^{1+\epsilon^{\prime}}}\cdot\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{(1+t+|q|)^{2-4\delta-\epsilon^{\prime}}(1+|q|)^{1+2\delta+2(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})}}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E(|J|+2)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{(1+t)^{1+\epsilon^{\prime}}}\cdot\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{(1+|q|)^{3-2\delta-\epsilon^{\prime}+2(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})}}dr\,.

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}\frac{(1+t)\cdot w_{\gamma^{\prime}}(q)}{\epsilon\cdot(1+t+|q|)^{2}}\cdot|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{J^{\prime}}}H|^{2}\cdot{|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}({\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi^{(l)})|^{2}}\Big)r^{2}drd\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E(|J|+2)\cdot\epsilon^{3}\,.

Similarly, we get the result for
\Phi^{(0)}-h^{0}

4.2
The space-time integral of tangential derivatives

We notice that when we use Lemma
4.0.7
for (
4.7
), we get derivatives with a seemingly bad factor that prevents us from obtaining the correct factor of the type
\frac{1}{(1+t)}
in a desired inequality of the form of (

). However, these seemingly wrong decay rates are not factors for any derivative, but for tangential derivatives for which we have a control on the space-time integral in our decoupled higher order energy estimates. Hence, we will show that for
\delta
small enough depending on
\mu
, we can absorb these integrals to the left hand side of our energy estimate (
4.7
) and therefore, they will not appear in a desired inequality of the form of (
2.4
).

We notice that the weight that enters infront of the space-time integral of the tangential derivatives is actually
\widehat{w}_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{\prime}(q)
, which in view of definition of the weight, (
2.8
), is actually
\frac{\widehat{w}(q)}{(1+|q|)}
.

Whereas to the weight that enters infront of (
4.0.7
) in the energy estimate (
4.7
), it is actually
w_{\gamma^{\prime}}(q)
. Thus, for the term (
4.0.7
), we need to absorb to the left hand side a space-time integral prescribed by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}E(3)\cdot\frac{\epsilon}{(1+t+|q|)^{1-\delta}\cdot(1+|q|)}\cdot|{\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}^{(\bf{m})}}\Phi_{V}|^{2}\cdot w_{\gamma^{\prime}}(q)\cdot d^{n}x\ \cdot d\tau\;.

On one hand, for
q<0
, we know that
\frac{\widehat{w}_{\gamma^{\prime}}(q)}{(1+|q|)}=\frac{1}{(1+|q|)^{1-2\mu}}
,
\mu<0
, and
\frac{\widehat{w}_{\gamma^{\prime}}(q)}{(1+|q|)}=\frac{1}{(1+|q|)}
. Thus, we look forward to absorb space-time integral on the right hand side

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}E(3)\cdot\frac{\epsilon}{(1+|q|)^{2-\delta}}\cdot|{\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}^{(\bf{m})}}\Phi_{V}|^{2}\cdot d^{n}x\ \cdot d\tau

(4.14)

into a space-time integral on the left hand side as follows, with
\mu<0
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}\frac{\widehat{w}_{\gamma^{\prime}}(q)}{(1+|q|)}\cdot|{\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}^{(\bf{m})}}\Phi_{V}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+|q|)^{1-2\mu}}\cdot|{\mbox{$\nabla\mkern-13.0mu/$\,}^{(\bf{m})}}\Phi_{V}|^{2}\cdot d^{n}x\ \cdot d\tau\;.

(4.15)

We can choose
\mu
small, and choose

On the other hand, for

Similarly, we deal with the term (

Thus, as discussed above, considering the behaviour of the weights in (

5
Improved à priori bounds on the energy

In this section, we can start by using our decoupled energy estimates from Proposition
4.0.2
, combined with our decoupled commutator estimate for the tangential components (
2.11
), to upgrade the bootstrap assumption (
2.2
) from a
\delta
power to an
c.\epsilon
power, however while requiring the à priori bootstrap assumption on more derivatives than what we upgrade for, thus it is
not yet
an actual upgrade. The way one can achieve this, is by induction on
k
to prove that

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{k}(t)\leq E(k+5)\cdot\epsilon\cdot(1+t)^{c\cdot\epsilon}\;,

(5.1)

by requiring the bootstrap assumption (
2.2
) to hold true for
k+5
derivatives, thus not closing the bootstrap argument yet. This, in a later stage, will be translated into pointwise decay on the fields and applied only to about half of the derivatives, so as to close the bootstrap argument.

Definition 5.0.1
.

We recall Definition
4.0.1
and we then define the decoupled higher order energy norm for full system, including the fields, as

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{\textit{good}\,,\,\gamma^{\prime}}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\{1,\ldots,N\}}{\cal E}^{\textit{good}\,,\,\gamma^{\prime}}_{k}(\Phi^{(l)})(t)+{\cal E}^{\textit{good}\,,\,\gamma^{\prime}}_{k}(\Phi^{(0)}-h^{0})(t)\;,

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{full\,,\,\gamma^{\prime}}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\{1,\ldots,N\}}{\cal E}^{full\,,\,\gamma^{\prime}}_{k}(\Phi^{(l)})(t)+{\cal E}^{full\,,\,\gamma^{\prime}}_{k}(\Phi^{(0)}-h^{0})\,.

The goal of this section is to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 5.0.2
.

Let
M\leq\epsilon
,
\gamma\geq 5\delta
, and
0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{4}
. Then, for
\gamma^{\prime}<1+4\delta
, we have

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{\textit{good}\,,\,\gamma^{\prime}}_{k}(t)\lesssim E(k+3)\cdot\epsilon\cdot(1+t)^{c\cdot\epsilon}\,,

(5.2)

for
{\cal E}^{\textit{good}\,,\,\gamma^{\prime}}_{k}(0)<\epsilon^{2}
, that is an estimate of the type (
2.5
) as outlined in our strategy of the proof (thus, it is not yet an upgrade since there is a loss on the derivatives). And if we additionally use (
5.2
) with a
\gamma^{\prime}>\frac{1}{2}+\gamma
, then we have

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{\textit{full}\,,\,\gamma}_{k}(t)\lesssim E(k+5)\cdot\epsilon\cdot(1+t)^{c\cdot\epsilon}\,.

(5.3)

for
{\cal E}^{\textit{full}\,,\,\gamma}_{k}(0)<\epsilon^{2}
. Thus, this imposes that the initial data must satisfy
{\cal E}^{\textit{good}\,,\,\gamma^{\prime}}_{k}(0)<\epsilon^{2}
for a certain
\gamma^{\prime}
such that
\frac{1}{2}+\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}<1+4\delta
, from which we get (
5.3
) under the bootstrap assumption with the weight
w_{\gamma}
. Consequently, we get the improved pointwise à priori bounds on the fields in Lemma
5.3.1
, for any
\gamma
such that

.

Remark 5.0.3

Recall that the bootstrap assumption was carried out with the weight
\gamma
only . Our goal is ultimately improve (
5.2
) where we would have
E(k)
instead of
E(k+5)
, for
k
large enough. This would be the goal of the next section.

5.1
Improved à priori bounds on the energy of good components

Lemma 5.1.1
.

Let
M\leq\epsilon
,
\gamma\geq 3\delta
, and
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2}
. For
\gamma^{\prime}<1+4\delta
, we have in the exterior region the following estimate on the sources of the good components, for all
l\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma^{ext}_{\tau}}\frac{(1+\tau)}{\epsilon}\cdot|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Big(g^{\alpha\beta}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\alpha}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\beta}\Phi^{(l)}_{{\cal S}^{l}_{1}\ldots{\cal S}^{l}_{k_{l}}}\Big)|^{2}r^{2}\cdot w_{\gamma^{\prime}}(q)drd\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E(|I|+3)\cdot\epsilon^{3}\,.

Proof.

We start by using our à priori decay estimates from Lemma
3.0.2
where we used our bootstrap assumption (
2.2
) with the weight
w_{\gamma}
. Using the structure of the good wave equation (
1.20
), we get depending on whether we have
\Phi^{(0)}
in the sources, or
\Phi^{(l)}
,

, that in the exterior region

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Big(g^{\lambda\mu}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\lambda}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}\Phi^{(l)}_{{\cal S}^{l}_{1}\ldots{\cal S}^{l}_{k_{l}}}\Big)|\leq\begin{cases}&E(|I|+3)\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{(1+t+|q|)^{3-3\delta}(1+|q|)^{1+\gamma}}\;,\\
&E(|I|+3)\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{(1+t+|q|)^{3-3\delta}(1+|q|)^{1+2\delta}}\,.\end{cases}

Hence, for all
l\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
we have in the exterior region,

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Big(g^{\lambda\mu}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\lambda}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\mu}\Phi^{(l)}_{{\cal S}^{l}_{1}\ldots{\cal S}^{l}_{k_{l}}}\Big)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E(|I|+3)\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{(1+t+|q|)^{3-3\delta}(1+|q|)^{1+\min\{2\delta,\gamma\}}}\;,

and consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{(1+t)}{\epsilon}\cdot|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Big(g^{\alpha\beta}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\alpha}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\beta}\Phi^{(l)}_{{\cal S}^{l}_{1}\ldots{\cal S}^{l}_{k_{l}}}\Big)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E(|I|+3)\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{(1+t+|q|)^{5-6\delta}(1+|q|)^{2+2\min\{2\delta,\gamma\}}}\,.

Since
r^{2}\lesssim(1+t+|q|)^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{(1+t)}{\epsilon}\cdot|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Big(g^{\alpha\beta}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\alpha}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\beta}\Phi^{(l)}_{{\cal S}^{l}_{1}\ldots{\cal S}^{l}_{k_{l}}}\Big)|^{2}r^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E(|I|+3)\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{(1+t+|q|)^{3-6\delta}(1+|q|)^{2+2\min\{2\delta,\gamma\}}}\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{(1+t)}{\epsilon}\cdot|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Big(g^{\alpha\beta}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\alpha}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}_{\beta}\Phi^{(l)}_{{\cal S}^{l}_{1}\ldots{\cal S}^{l}_{k_{l}}}\Big)|^{2}\cdot r^{2}\cdot w_{\gamma^{\prime}}(q)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E(|I|+3)\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{3}\cdot(1+|q|)^{1+2\gamma^{\prime}}}{(1+t+|q|)^{3-6\delta}(1+|q|)^{2+2\min\{2\delta,\gamma\}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E(|I|+3)\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{(1+t+|q|)^{3-6\delta}(1+|q|)^{1-2\gamma^{\prime}+2\min\{2\delta,\gamma\}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E(|I|+3)\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{(1+t+|q|)^{1+\epsilon^{\prime}}(1+|q|)^{3-6\delta-\epsilon^{\prime}-2\gamma^{\prime}+2\min\{2\delta,\gamma\}}}

For
\gamma^{\prime}<1+2\min\{2\delta,\gamma\}
, then

and by choosing
\epsilon^{\prime}
and
\delta
small enough, we have

and therefore integrable in
|q|
. For

, we have

translates as the condition
\gamma^{\prime}<1+4\delta
.

Let

,

We can insert the estimate on the sources of the good components from Lemma

We start by estimating the “bad” terms

Once we have an à prior control on

\displaystyle|{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi^{(l_{i})}_{{\cal S}^{l_{i}}_{1}\ldots{\cal S}^{l_{i}}_{k_{l_{i}}}}|\cdot(1+t+|q|)\cdot\big[(1+|q|)\cdot w_{\gamma^{\prime}}(q)\big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\cdot\sum_{|K|\leq|I|+2}\|\big(w_{\gamma^{\prime}}(q)\big)^{1/2}{\nabla^{(\bf{m})}}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{K}}\Phi^{(l_{i})}_{{\cal S}^{l_{i}}_{1}\ldots{\cal S}^{l_{i}}_{k_{l_{i}}}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma^{ext}_{t})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E(k+5)\cdot\sqrt{\epsilon}\cdot(1+t)^{c\cdot\epsilon}\,.

This would in particular imply a control on

and since for all

\displaystyle|\partial_{\underline{L}}{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi^{(l_{i})}_{{\cal S}^{l_{i}}_{1}\ldots{\cal S}^{l_{i}}_{k_{l_{i}}}}|\lesssim E(|I|+5)\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{(1+t+|q|)^{1-c\cdot\epsilon}\cdot(1+|q|)^{1+\gamma^{\prime}}}\;.

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}_{Z^{I}}\Phi^{(l_{i})}_{{\cal S}^{l_{i}}_{1}\ldots{\cal S}^{l_{i}}_{k_{l_{i}}}}|\lesssim E(|I|+5)\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{(1+t+|q|)^{1-c\cdot\epsilon}\cdot(1+|q|)^{\gamma^{\prime}}}\;.

Hence,

6.4
