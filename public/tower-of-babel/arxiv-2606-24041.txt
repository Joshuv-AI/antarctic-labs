Nonrotating and rotating black holes with secondary disformal hair in a ghost-free metric-affine parity-violating scalar-torsion theory
E. Brito, G. Macedo, J. R. Nascimento, A. Yu. Petrov, P. J. Porfírio

Abstract
In this paper, we formulate a ghost-free metric-affine scalar-torsion model in which a pseudoscalar field is coupled to the Nieh–Yan density and derivatively coupled to the Einstein-Palatini tensor. The theory is projectively invariant and, in the shift-symmetric sector, depends on the scalar only through its gradient. After fixing the projective gauge, the independent connection is solved exactly: its symmetric part is the Levi-Civita connection associated with the metric h μ ​ ν h_{\mu\nu} , disformally related to the metric g μ ​ ν g_{\mu\nu} , while its antisymmetric part is a purely axial torsion sourced by v μ = ∂ μ ϑ v_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\vartheta . We identify a degenerate ghost-free branch, α = 6 ​ β 2 ​ κ 2 \alpha=6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2} , for which Ricci-flat geometries in the h h -metric frame solve the metric equations and the Noether current vanishes identically. For example, we consider Schwarzschild and Kerr geometries in the h h -metric frame, which generate disformal deformations on these geometries from the perspective of the g g -metric frame. For the Schwarzschild black hole in the h h -metric frame, we construct static and Babichev–Charmousis-like time-dependent scalar profiles, and discuss horizon regularity in Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates. For the Kerr black hole in the h h -metric frame, we show that the fixed-norm scalar profile is generically axisymmetric, depending on both the radial and angular coordinates, and we construct a stationary future-regular profile in ingoing Kerr coordinates. The Kerr solution with the nontrivial scalar field is stealth only in the h h -metric frame. In the g g -metric frame, it produces disformal corrections stemming from the nontrivial scalar field profile and it represents secondary disformal scalar hair rather than primary Noether-charge hair.

Introduction

Undoubtedly, General Relativity (GR) has been remarkably successful over the last century. Its theoretical predictions have been confirmed by numerous experiments to date – in particular, a notable recent example is the observation of black-hole shadows by
the Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration

Clifton:2011jh

Many of them are based on modifications of GR, or alternative theories of gravity, obtained by including new dynamical fields (non-)minimally coupled to gravity, such as, for example, Brans-Dicke and Horndeski theories

Petrov:2020wgy

Scalar-tensor theories are particularly interesting models of modified theories of gravity. They provide an important arena for testing the robustness of black-hole no-hair arguments. A standard current-based no-hair theorem for shift-symmetric Galileons

Such configurations are usually referred to as stealth black holes (Ricci-flat metrics sourced by a non-trivial scalar field (hair)

Degenerate Higher-Order Scalar-Tensor Theories (DHOST) provide a broad framework for constructing higher-derivative scalar-tensor models that avoid the propagation of undesirable ghost degrees of freedom. The aforementioned scalar-tensor theories have mostly been explored within the metric approach, in which the only dynamical quantities are the metric and the scalar field. A possible natural way to generalize them is to consider such theories within the metric-affine (Palatini) approach, a formalism in which the metric and the connection are treated as independent variables a priori

The purpose of this paper is to construct a ghost-free metric-affine parity-violating model coupling a topological invariant based on the torsion tensor with a (pseudo-)scalar field. The Nieh–Yan invariant is a natural candidate because it is intrinsically tied to torsion and becomes nontrivial only in torsional geometries. Furthermore, by requiring projective invariance, the connection equation can be solved exactly. The symmetric part becomes the Levi-Civita connection of the metric

2
Ghost-free metric-affine parity-violating modified gravity

Our starting point will be to define the proposed modified theory of gravity. The model basically consists of adding two nonminimal couplings – one of which involves a (pseudo)scalar field (
\vartheta
) coupled to the Nieh-Yan density and the other one involving derivatives of the scalar field coupled to the Ricci scalar – to the Einstein-Palatini action. Now, let us write the action explicitly

S=S_{EP}+S_{NM}+S_{\vartheta}+S_{m},

(2)

where the Einstein-Palatini action is defined by

S_{EP}=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,R(\Gamma),

(3)

where
R(\Gamma)=g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}(\Gamma)
is the Ricci scalar and
R_{\mu\nu}(\Gamma)
is the Ricci tensor, both defined in the Palatini framework, and the non-minimal couplings are given by

S_{NM}=\beta\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\vartheta NY+\frac{\gamma}{4\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\left(XR(\Gamma)+2g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}(\partial_{\alpha}\vartheta)(\partial_{\beta}\vartheta)G_{\mu\nu}(\Gamma)\right),

(4)

where
X\equiv g^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\mu}\vartheta)(\partial_{\nu}\vartheta)
. The first term in the action above stands for the non-minimal coupling between the topological Nieh-Yan term and the scalar field, while the second one is the coupling between the Einstein-Palatini tensor,
G_{\mu\nu}(\Gamma)=R_{\mu\nu}(\Gamma)-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R(\Gamma)
, with first-order derivatives of the scalar field. In addition,
\beta
and
\gamma
are coupling constants controlling the non-minimal interactions. The Nieh-Yan term is defined by

NY\equiv\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\left(S^{\rho}_{\,\,\mu\nu}(S_{\rho\alpha\beta}+Q_{\alpha\beta\rho})-\dfrac{1}{2}R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\right),

(5)

where
\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}=\dfrac{\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}}{\sqrt{-g}}
is the Levi-Civita tensor and
\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
is the Holst term
Holst:1995pc
. The minimal scalar sector is defined by

S_{\vartheta}=\alpha\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\bigg[\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\vartheta)(\partial^{\mu}\vartheta)-V(\vartheta)\bigg],

(6)

we have introduced the real constant parameter
\alpha
for the sake of future convenience, the special case
\alpha=1
captures the standard scalar action, while the case
\alpha<0
describes scalar fields with “wrong” sign kinetic term (ghost field). Finally, the contribution coming from matter sources is defined by

S_{m}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{L}_{m}(g_{\mu\nu},\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta},\Psi),

(7)

where
\Psi
stands for matter sources different from
\vartheta
.
Some remarks should be made on Eq.(
4
). First, the Nieh-Yan term is a topological invariant, which means that it can be rewritten as the divergence of a vector (see, for example,
Nieh:1981ww
)

NY=-\nabla^{(g)}_{\mu}\bar{S}^{\mu},

(8)

where
\bar{S}^{\mu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}S_{\nu\alpha\beta}
is the axial piece of the torsion tensor. Taking this into account,
S_{NY}
can be expressed as

S_{NM}=\beta\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,v_{\mu}\bar{S}^{\mu}+\frac{\gamma}{4\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\left(XR(\Gamma)+2g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}(\partial_{\alpha}\vartheta)(\partial_{\beta}\vartheta)G_{\mu\nu}(\Gamma)\right),

(9)

where we have integrated Eq.(
4
) by parts and disregarded boundary terms and defined
v_{\mu}=\nabla^{(g)}_{\mu}\vartheta=\partial_{\mu}\vartheta
. The first term in the action
S_{NM}
, written in the previous shape, represents by itself a contact interaction between two axial vectors:
v_{\mu}
and
\bar{S}^{\mu}
.

In analogy with Chern-Simons modified gravity
Jackiw:2003pm
, one can lay out two different formulations:

•

Non-dynamical formulation (
\alpha=0
and
\gamma=0
). It occurs when
\vartheta
is taken to be a background field which entails that
v_{\mu}
can be interpreted as a local Lorentz-violating background
in such a way that this coupling matches one of the possible terms proposed in the
Standard-Model Extension (SME)
Kostelecky:2003fs
. As a result, we shall see that the theory within this formulation must fulfill a constraint in just the same way as the Pontryagin one in Chern-Simons modified gravity
Jackiw:2003pm
. Moreover, requiring
\vartheta=\mbox{const.}
which is the same as taking
v_{\mu}=0
leads to the vanishing of
S_{NY}
and
S_{\vartheta}
and then the model reduces to the Einstein-Palatini with matter sources.

•

Dynamical formulation (
\alpha\neq 0
and
\gamma\neq 0
). In this case,
\vartheta
is treated as a fundamental field of the model, and is therefore described by its own equation of motion rather than a constraint.

Another striking feature is related to the parity symmetry; notice that the Nieh-Yan term is odd under parity transformation since it is defined in terms of the Levi-Civita tensor; as a result,
\vartheta
must also be odd in order to maintain the invariance of the full action under parity transformations. Conversely, if
\vartheta
is even, the model breaks the parity invariance explicitly.

The full action (
2

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left\{\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\left[R(\Gamma)\left(1+\frac{\gamma}{2}X\right)+\gamma v^{\mu}v^{\nu}G_{\mu\nu}(\Gamma)\right]+\beta v_{\mu}\bar{S}^{\mu}+\alpha\bigg[\frac{1}{2}X-V(\vartheta)\bigg]+\mathcal{L}_{m}\right\}.

(10)

1.

For a trivial potential (
V(\vartheta)=0
),
it is straightforward to check that Eq.(

In addition, the action (

From now on, we shall concentrate our attention on the shift/projective-symmetric model, which corresponds to getting rid of the potential term in Eq.(

Delhom:2019wcm
, that is, using the matrix representation of Eq.(
16
), we can rewrite it as

\sqrt{-h}\hat{h}^{-1}=\sqrt{-g}\hat{g}^{-1}\left(\hat{I}+\gamma\hat{vv}\right).

(38)

Now, taking the determinant of the former equation, one gets the following relation:
\sqrt{-h}=\sqrt{-g}\sqrt{\left(1+\gamma X\right)}
, where we have used the identity
\det(\hat{I}+\gamma\hat{vv})=1+\gamma X
(see
HornJohnson
). Substituting this into Eq.(
16
), we find

h^{\mu\nu}=\frac{g^{\mu\nu}+\gamma v^{\mu}v^{\nu}}{\sqrt{1+\gamma X}},

(39)

and, as a consequence,

h_{\mu\nu}=\sqrt{1+\gamma X}\left(g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma X}v_{\mu}v_{\nu}\right).

(40)

Both metrics are disformally related. By defining the quantity
Y=h^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\mu}\vartheta)(\partial_{\nu}\vartheta)
, it is possible to invert the former equations, i.e.,

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+\gamma X}\,h^{\mu\nu}-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma X}\tilde{v}^{\mu}\tilde{v}^{\nu};

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\gamma X}}\left(h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{1+\gamma X}}v_{\mu}v_{\nu}\right),

(41)

where
X
must be interpreted as a function of
Y\equiv h^{\mu\nu}v_{\mu}v_{\nu}
, which are related by
Y=X\sqrt{1+\gamma X}
and
\tilde{v}^{\mu}\equiv h^{\mu\nu}v_{\nu}
.
With this at hand, we can reverse Eq. (
32
). To do that, let us define
T_{\rho\alpha\lambda}\equiv h_{\rho\nu}\tilde{S}^{\nu}_{\,\,\alpha\lambda}
. Now, multiplying Eq. (
32
) by
h_{\nu\rho}h_{\mu\sigma}
, one finds

T_{\rho\alpha\sigma}-T_{\sigma\alpha\rho}=-2\beta\kappa^{2}v_{\lambda}\frac{\epsilon^{\lambda\phantom{a}\nu\mu}_{\phantom{a}\alpha}}{\sqrt{1+\gamma X}}h_{\nu\rho}h_{\mu\sigma}.

(42)

Defining
F_{\alpha\rho\sigma}=T_{\rho\alpha\sigma}-T_{\sigma\alpha\rho}
, so

\displaystyle F_{\alpha\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\beta\kappa^{2}v_{\lambda}\frac{\epsilon^{\lambda\phantom{a}\nu\mu}_{\phantom{a}\alpha}}{\sqrt{1+\gamma X}}h_{\nu\rho}h_{\mu\sigma}

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\beta\kappa^{2}v_{\lambda}\epsilon^{\lambda\phantom{a}}_{\phantom{a}\alpha\rho\sigma}\sqrt{1+\gamma X}

which satisfies

\displaystyle T_{\sigma\rho\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(F_{\alpha\rho\sigma}+F_{\rho\sigma\alpha}-F_{\sigma\alpha\rho}\right)

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\kappa^{2}v_{\lambda}\frac{\epsilon^{\lambda\phantom{a}\nu\mu}_{\phantom{a}\sigma}}{\sqrt{1+\gamma X}}h_{\nu\rho}h_{\mu\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\kappa^{2}v_{\lambda}\epsilon^{\lambda\phantom{a}}_{\phantom{a}\sigma\rho\alpha}\sqrt{1+\gamma X}

Finally, from Eq. (
32
) and using
\tilde{S}^{\nu}_{\,\,\alpha\lambda}=T_{\rho\alpha\lambda}h^{\rho\nu}
, we arrive at

\tilde{S}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}=-\beta\kappa^{2}v_{\lambda}\epsilon^{\alpha\lambda}_{\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mu\nu},

(45)

or, in terms of the metric
h_{\mu\nu}
, the torsion reads

\tilde{S}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{\beta\kappa^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\gamma X}}v_{\lambda}h^{\alpha\vartheta}h^{\lambda\sigma}\epsilon^{(h)}_{\vartheta\sigma\mu\nu},

(46)

where we have defined
\epsilon^{(h)}_{\vartheta\sigma\mu\nu}=\sqrt{-h}\,\varepsilon_{\vartheta\sigma\mu\nu}=\sqrt{1+\gamma X}\,\epsilon_{\vartheta\sigma\mu\nu}
. Note that the torsion tensor is entirely sourced by the scalar field
\vartheta
and its derivative. As a consequence of Eq. (
45
), the axial piece of the torsion tensor is given by

\bar{S}^{\mu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\tilde{S}_{\nu\alpha\beta}=-6\beta\kappa^{2}v^{\mu}.

(47)

Since the projective modes can be gauged away, the full connection is given by

\tilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}=\,^{(h)}L^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\beta\kappa^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\gamma X}}v_{\lambda}h^{\alpha\vartheta}h^{\lambda\sigma}\epsilon^{(h)}_{\vartheta\sigma\mu\nu}.

(48)

This is a remarkable result since the torsion tensor is entirely sourced by the derivative of the scalar field and its norm,
v_{\mu}
and
X
.

2.1.2
Metric field equation

Using
\tilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}=\tilde{C}^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}+\tilde{S}^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}
, the Riemann tensor can be decomposed into

R^{\alpha}_{\phantom{a}\beta\mu\nu}(\tilde{\Gamma})=R^{\alpha}_{\phantom{a}\beta\mu\nu}(h)+\nabla_{\mu}^{(\tilde{\Gamma})}\tilde{S}^{\alpha}_{\nu\beta}-\nabla_{\nu}^{(\tilde{\Gamma})}\tilde{S}^{\alpha}_{\mu\beta}+\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{\nu\beta}\tilde{S}^{\alpha}_{\mu\lambda}-\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{\mu\beta}\tilde{S}^{\alpha}_{\nu\lambda}.

(49)

With this definition at hand, one can rewrite the field equation (
13
) in the
h
-metric frame. Let us check this step-by-step. First, from the definition (
49
), the Ricci tensor can be cast into the form,

R_{\beta\nu}(\tilde{\Gamma})=R_{\beta\nu}(h)+\nabla_{\lambda}^{(\tilde{\Gamma})}\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{\nu\beta}-\nabla_{\nu}^{(\tilde{\Gamma})}\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{\lambda\beta}+\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{\nu\beta}\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{\lambda\gamma}-\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{\gamma\beta}\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{\nu\lambda}.

(50)

Now, plugging Eq.(
46
) into Eq. (
50
),
it is easy to see that

\begin{split}R_{(\beta\nu)}(\tilde{\Gamma})&=R_{\beta\nu}(h)-\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{\gamma(\beta}\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{\nu)\lambda}\\
&=R_{\beta\nu}(h)+\frac{2\beta^{2}\kappa^{4}}{1+\gamma X}\left(Yh_{\mu\nu}-v_{\mu}v_{\nu}\right),\end{split}

(51)

where
\nabla_{\lambda}^{(\tilde{\Gamma})}\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{(\nu\beta)}=0
and
\tilde{S}^{\lambda}_{\lambda\gamma}=0
, as a result of the antisymmetric properties of the Levi-Civita tensor and the torsion tensor. Second, note that the combination in brackets of Eq.(
13
) can be expressed as

\begin{split}R(\tilde{\Gamma})+\gamma v^{\alpha}v^{\beta}R_{\alpha\beta}(\tilde{\Gamma})&=R_{\alpha\beta}(\tilde{\Gamma})h^{\alpha\beta}\sqrt{1+\gamma X}\\
&=\sqrt{1+\gamma X}\,\tilde{R}(h)+\frac{6\beta^{2}\kappa^{4}Y}{\sqrt{1+\gamma X}},\end{split}

(52)

where
\tilde{R}(h)=h^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}(h)
. The third term on the l.h.s. of Eq.(
13
) reduces to

2\gamma v^{\beta}v_{(\mu}R_{\nu)\beta}(\tilde{\Gamma})=2\gamma v^{\beta}v_{(\mu}R_{\nu)\beta}(h).

(53)

The last term on the left-hand side of Eq.(
13
) can be set into the form

\begin{split}-2\beta\kappa^{2}v_{\lambda}\epsilon^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\beta\gamma(\mu}S_{\nu)}^{\,\,\,\beta\gamma}=\frac{4\beta^{2}\kappa^{4}}{1+\gamma X}\left(Yh_{\mu\nu}-v_{\mu}v_{\nu}\right).\end{split}

(54)

Putting all information together, the field equation in the
h
-metric frame becomes

\begin{split}G_{\mu\nu}(h)-\frac{\gamma v_{\mu}v_{\nu}\tilde{R}(h)}{2\sqrt{1+\gamma X}}+\frac{2\gamma\tilde{v}^{\beta}v_{(\mu}R_{\nu)\beta}(h)}{\sqrt{1+\gamma X}}+\frac{3\beta^{2}\kappa^{4}Yh_{\mu\nu}}{1+\gamma X}-3\beta^{2}\kappa^{4}v_{\mu}v_{\nu}\left(\frac{2+\gamma X}{1+\gamma X}\right)=\kappa^{2}T_{\mu\nu},\end{split}

(55)

where
T_{\mu\nu}=T^{(h)}_{\mu\nu}+T^{(m)}_{\mu\nu}
with

T^{(h)}_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{\alpha}{1+\gamma X}\left[v_{\mu}v_{\nu}\left(1+\frac{\gamma X}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}h_{\mu\nu}Y\right]

(56)

is the
\vartheta
-dependent part of the energy-momentum tensor.

2.1.3
Scalar field equation

By taking into account the gauge fixing Eq.(
28
) and inserting the axial piece of the torsion (
2.1.1
) and the connection (
48
) into Eq. (
15
), the scalar field equation (
15
) becomes

\displaystyle\left(\alpha-6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2}\right)\square^{(g)}\vartheta+\frac{\gamma}{\kappa^{2}}\nabla^{(g)}_{\mu}\left[v_{\sigma}R^{(\mu\sigma)}(h)\right]=0.

(57)

Let us clarify the meaning of the ghost-free condition in the present construction. After
integrating out the connection and the axial torsion, the effective coefficient multiplying
the scalar kinetic term is
\alpha-6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2}
. Therefore, the region

\alpha-6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2}>0
corresponds to the standard healthy sign of the reduced
scalar-torsion kinetic sector, whereas
\alpha-6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2}<0
would lead to a
ghostlike sign. The special branch
\alpha=6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2}

used below is a degenerate branch of the reduced equations: the ordinary scalar kinetic
term is exactly canceled by the torsion-induced contribution. For Ricci-flat geometries in
h
-metric frame, the remaining curvature-dependent part of the above equation also vanishes. In this sense, the branch is ghost-free in the reduced
scalar-torsion sector relevant for the exact solutions considered here. A full perturbative
stability analysis of the physical metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, however, lies beyond the scope of
the present work.

As we have already discussed, we consider the special case corresponding to
V(\vartheta)=0
. In this case, the action becomes invariant under shift transformation,
\vartheta\rightarrow\vartheta+a
, and, as a consequence, it admits a conserved Noether current defined by

J^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S}{\delta(\partial_{\mu}\vartheta)},

(58)

with its explicit form given by Eq. (
24
),(
25
). After integrating out the connection and the axial piece of the torsion tensor, one finds

J^{\mu}=(\alpha-6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2})\frac{\tilde{v}^{\mu}}{\sqrt{1+\gamma X}}+\frac{\gamma}{\kappa^{2}}\left[\tilde{v}^{\beta}\tilde{R}^{\mu}_{\phantom{a}\beta}(h)-\frac{\gamma}{(1+\gamma X)^{3/2}}\tilde{v}^{\mu}\tilde{v}^{\alpha}\tilde{v}^{\beta}R_{\alpha\beta}(h)\right],

(59)

where
\tilde{R}^{\mu}_{\phantom{a}\beta}(h)=h^{\mu\alpha}R_{\alpha\beta}(h)
.
Taking these definitions into account, the scalar field equation reduces to the conservation of the Noether current

\nabla_{\mu}^{(g)}J^{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}J^{\mu}\right)=\nabla^{(h)}_{\mu}\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}=0,

(60)

where
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}\equiv\dfrac{J^{\mu}}{\sqrt{1+\gamma X}}
is the conserved current in the
h
-metric frame.
Such a current plays an important role in global shifted scalar-tensor theories. In this context, one may recall a no-hair theorem
for Galileon theories, where it has been argued
Hui:2012qt
that for static and spherically symmetric spacetimes, the only non-trivial component of the Noether current
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{r}
must vanish everywhere to ensure regularity of the square norm of the Noether current
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{2}=h^{\mu\nu}\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mu}\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\nu}
at the horizon. They also argued that such a setup implies that the scalar field must be constant everywhere. In our particular case, this no-hair theorem is recovered, namely, from Eq.(
59
) it is straightforward to check that
\vartheta=const
implies
J^{\mu}=0
or, equivalently,
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}=0
. Nevertheless, in much the same way as what happens in
Babichev:2013cya
, we have an alternative condition to keep
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{2}
regular at the horizon for Ricci-flat black holes, i.e., by taking

\alpha=6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2},

(61)

one has
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}=0
throughout spacetime without restricting
\vartheta(r)
to a constant everywhere, while still satisfying the no-hair regularity condition. As pointed out in
Babichev:2013cya
, this alternative condition is more convenient because there are no constraints on the scalar field.

In the next section we shall explore static symmetric black holes in more detail following the aforementioned line of reasoning.

3
Applications: Exact Ricci-flat black-hole solutions

In this section, let us study the consistency of some black holes within the shifted symmetric modified theory. Before to begin with, it is worthwhile to emphasize an important assumption that we shall proceed with, namely, in addition to imposing Eq. (
61
), let us also impose regularity of the norm of
v_{\mu}
at the horizon, which is easily reached by the requirement that
Y=h^{\mu\nu}v_{\mu}v_{\nu}=const
, even though
v_{\mu}
does not.
This is not an arbitrary choice; rather, it is technically convenient because the field equations will be rather simplified. Fixed-norm vectors have been explored in the context of spontaneous Lorentz symmetry-breaking models; see, for example,
Kostelecky:1989jw
;
Jacobson:2000xp
. Such a constraint can be incorporated from the very beginning by adding a Lagrange multiplier of the form

S_{L}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\sigma\,(v^{\mu}v_{\mu}-X)=\int d^{4}x\frac{\sqrt{-h}}{(1+\gamma X)}\,\sigma\,(\tilde{v}^{\mu}v_{\mu}-Y),

(62)

in the action (
2
), with
\sigma
being the Lagrange multiplier. Although this procedure is standard, it is more practical to apply the fixed-norm constraint directly to the field equations once we have already found them; as a result, we have a reminiscence of the Einstein-aether theory
Jacobson:2000xp
.

We explore a simple class of exact solutions of the theory. The key observation is that, after solving the connection equation, the field equations take a particularly transparent form in the
h
-metric frame. We use the notation
z\equiv\sqrt{1+\gamma X}
for brevity. Consequently, the two kinetic terms are related by the following relation:

Y=zX,

(63)

or, equivalently, by the cubic equation

z^{3}-z-\gamma Y=0.

(64)

In the
h
-metric frame, the metric field equation can be written as

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}(h)

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2z}v_{\mu}v_{\nu}R(h)+\frac{2\gamma}{z}v_{(\mu}R_{\nu)\rho}(h)\tilde{v}^{\rho}

\displaystyle+\frac{Y}{z^{2}}\left(3\beta^{2}\kappa^{4}-\frac{\alpha\kappa^{2}}{2}\right)h_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1+z^{2}}{z^{2}}\left(3\beta^{2}\kappa^{4}-\frac{\alpha\kappa^{2}}{2}\right)v_{\mu}v_{\nu}=0.

(65)

Therefore, in the ghost-free branch

\alpha=6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2},

(66)

the algebraic scalar-torsion sector cancels identically. In this case, Eq. (
65
) reduces to

G_{\mu\nu}(h)-\frac{\gamma}{2z}v_{\mu}v_{\nu}R(h)+\frac{2\gamma}{z}v_{(\mu}R_{\nu)\rho}(h)\tilde{v}^{\rho}=0.

(67)

It follows immediately that every Ricci-flat geometry in the
h
-metric frame,

R_{\mu\nu}(h)=0,

(68)

solves the metric field equations on the branch (
66
). Furthermore, the Noether current associated with the shift symmetry (
25
) becomes

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}=(\alpha-6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2})\tilde{v}^{\mu}+\frac{\gamma z}{\kappa^{2}}\left[\tilde{R}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}(h)\tilde{v}^{\nu}-\frac{\gamma}{z^{3}}\tilde{v}^{\mu}R_{\alpha\beta}(h)\tilde{v}^{\alpha}\tilde{v}^{\beta}\right].

(69)

Thus, for Ricci-flat
h_{\mu\nu}
, and imposing the regularity condition of the norm of the Noether current

\alpha=6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2}
, one finds

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}=0,

(70)

and the scalar equation is automatically satisfied for a non-trivial scalar field.

3.1
Schwarzschild metric in the
h
-metric frame

As a particular example of a spherically symmetric spacetime, let us take the metric
h_{\mu\nu}
to be the Schwarzschild one,

ds_{h}^{2}=-A(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{A(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},\qquad A(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}.

(71)

Since
R_{\mu\nu}(h)=0
, this geometry solves the field equations, in the
h
-metric frame, for the vanishing of the Noether current,
\alpha=6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2}
. We now present two scalar profiles.

3.1.1
Static fixed-norm profile

A simple static profile is given by

v_{\mu}=\left(0,\sqrt{\frac{q}{A(r)}},0,0\right),

(72)

where
q
is a positive constant.
Then, imposing the fixed-norm condition, one has

Y=h^{\mu\nu}v_{\mu}v_{\nu}=q.

(73)

Plugging Eq. (
71
) into Eq. (
41
), we find the line element in the
g
-metric frame is given by

\mathrm{d}s_{g}^{2}=-\frac{A(r)}{z}\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\frac{z}{A(r)}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{z}\mathrm{d}\Omega^{2}.

(74)

The scalar field is obtained from Eq. (
73
) by substituting the vector (
72
) into the condition (
73
); thus, one finds

\vartheta(r)=\vartheta_{0}\pm\sqrt{q}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\sqrt{1-2M/r}},

(75)

which can be easily integrated to get

\vartheta(r)=\vartheta_{0}\pm\sqrt{q}\left[\sqrt{r(r-2M)}+2M\ln\left(\sqrt{r}+\sqrt{r-2M}\right)\right].

(76)

It is noteworthy that the scalar field is finite at the horizon
r=2M
. Its radial coordinate derivative diverges in Schwarzschild coordinates, but the invariant norms
Y=q
and
X=q/z
are finite.
Let us emphasize the interpretation of this solution. Since the
h_{\mu\nu}
metric is
Schwarzschild,
R_{\mu\nu}(h)=0
, and we are working on the branch

\alpha=6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2}
, the
h
-frame metric equations are satisfied even though the
scalar field is nontrivial. Moreover, the Noether current vanishes identically on this branch.
Therefore the scalar profile is stealth with respect to
h_{\mu\nu}
for a discussion of stealth solutions, see e.g.
Ayon-Beato:2004nzi
:
the scalar is present, but the background remains a Schwarzschild spacetime in the
h
-metric frame.

This interpretation as a stealth solution, however, applies only in the
h
-frame. The metric
g_{\mu\nu}

is disformally related to
h_{\mu\nu}
, namely

g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{z}h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{\gamma}{z^{2}}\partial_{\mu}\vartheta\partial_{\nu}\vartheta,

and therefore the scalar gradient deforms the physical geometry whenever
\gamma\neq 0
.
Thus the solution should not be interpreted as a purely stealth black hole in the physical
frame. Instead, it represents a Schwarzschild background (in the
h
-metric frame) dressed by a secondary
disformal scalar hair in the physical
g
-frame.

The Kretschmann scalar invariant for the metric (
74
) is

K_{g}\equiv R_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}(g)R^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}(g)=\frac{4(z^{2}-1)^{2}}{z^{2}r^{4}}+\frac{16M(z^{2}-1)}{z^{2}r^{5}}+\frac{48M^{2}}{z^{2}r^{6}}.

(77)

It shows that the divergence at
r=2M
of the radial coordinate derivative is only a problem of a bad choice of the coordinate system. Note also that for
z=1
one recovers the Schwarzschild invariant
48M^{2}/r^{6}
.

The physical metric (
74
) can be viewed as a black hole with a deficit solid angle. It can be seen more clearly by proceeding with the following coordinate redefinitions, namely:
\bar{t}\to\dfrac{t}{\sqrt{z}}
,
\bar{r}\to r\sqrt{z}
. With this redefinition, the metric (
74
) becomes

\mathrm{d}s_{g}^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2M_{eff}}{\bar{r}}\right)\mathrm{d}\bar{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{1-\frac{2M_{eff}}{\bar{r}}}\mathrm{d}\bar{r}^{2}+\frac{\bar{r}^{2}}{z^{2}}\mathrm{d}\Omega^{2},

(78)

where we define the effective Schwarzschild mass
M_{eff}=M\sqrt{z}
.
Note that the area of the
2
-sphere is now
4\pi\left(\dfrac{r}{z}\right)^{2}
. The deficit solid angle is then defined by

\Delta\Omega=4\pi\left(1-\frac{1}{z^{2}}\right).

(79)

Thus, the line element in the
g
-metric frame takes the form

\mathrm{d}s_{g}^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2M_{eff}}{\bar{r}}\right)\mathrm{d}\bar{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{1-\frac{2M_{eff}}{\bar{r}}}\mathrm{d}\bar{r}^{2}+(1-\Delta)\bar{r}^{2}\mathrm{d}\Omega^{2},

(80)

where
\Delta=\left(1-\dfrac{1}{z^{2}}\right)
.

Although the physical metric displays a solid angle deficit of the same geometrical type as the Barriola–Vilenkin global monopole spacetime
Barriola:1989hx
, the origin of this deficit is different. In the present model there is no scalar triplet, and no topological charge. The deficit is instead generated by the disformal relation between the physical metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and the Ricci-flat metric
h_{\mu\nu}
, through the scalar-gradient norm entering the algebraic factor
z
.

3.1.2
Time-dependent regular scalar profile

We now allow for the scalar field to be time-dependent. We choose a similar ansatz for the scalar field profile proposed in
Babichev:2013cya
, so

\vartheta(t,r)=qt+\psi(r),

(81)

where
q
is a positive constant. This choice leads to

v_{\mu}=[q,\psi^{\prime}(r),0,0],

(82)

where the prime is for derivative with respect to the radial coordinate.
Now, let us impose the fixed-norm condition,

Y=h^{\mu\nu}v_{\mu}v_{\nu}=-\frac{q^{2}}{A(r)}+A(r)(\psi^{\prime}(r))^{2}=Y_{0},

(83)

where
Y_{0}
is a constant.
To check the regularity condition of the scalar field at the horizon, one rewrites Eq. (
83
) as

\psi^{\prime}(r)=\pm\frac{\sqrt{q^{2}+Y_{0}A(r)}}{A(r)}.

(84)

Note that near the horizon,
A(r)\to 0
, the equation for
\psi^{\prime}(r)
behaves as

\psi^{\prime}(r)=\pm\frac{|q|}{A(r)}+...\,\,,

(85)

where the ellipses stand for subleading terms. At first sight, this seems to diverge at the horizon. However, it reflects the "bad" choice of coordinates. To check regularity at the future horizon, introduce the advanced Eddington–Finkelstein coordinate

v=t+r_{*}=t+r+2M\ln\left(\frac{r}{2M}-1\right),

(86)

where
r_{*}
is the tortoise coordinate. Using the advanced coordinate,

\vartheta(v,r)=q\left(v-r-2M\ln\left(\frac{r}{2M}-1\right)\right)+\psi(r).

(87)

Therefore, for the positive branch, near the horizon, we have

\vartheta_{+}(v,r)=qv+\mbox{finite radial function}.

(88)

This shows that the scalar field is regular at the future horizon.

After using the relation (
41
), the line element in the
g
-metric frame reads

\mathrm{d}s_{g}^{2}=\frac{1}{z}\mathrm{d}s_{h}^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{z^{2}}\left[q\mathrm{d}t+\psi^{\prime}(r)\mathrm{d}r\right]^{2}.

(89)

Thus, explicitly,

\displaystyle g_{tt}

\displaystyle=-\frac{A(r)}{z}+\frac{\gamma q^{2}}{z^{2}},

(90)

\displaystyle g_{tr}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma q\psi^{\prime}}{z^{2}},

(91)

\displaystyle g_{rr}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{zA(r)}+\frac{\gamma\psi^{\prime 2}}{z^{2}},

(92)

\displaystyle g_{\vartheta\vartheta}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{z},\qquad g_{\phi\phi}=\frac{r^{2}}{z}\sin^{2}\vartheta.

(93)

The off-diagonal component
g_{tr}
is a radial shift induced by the time-dependent scalar; it is not a rotating term.

A particularly close analogue of the stealth Schwarzschild black hole found in
Babichev:2013cya
is obtained by choosing

Y_{0}=-q^{2}.

(94)

Substituting it into Eq.(
84
), we obtain

\psi^{\prime}(r)=\pm\frac{q\sqrt{1-A(r)}}{A(r)}=\pm\frac{q\sqrt{2M/r}}{1-2M/r}.

(95)

Now, integrating it and using Eq.(
81
), we arrive at

\vartheta_{\pm}(t,r)=qt\pm q\left[2\sqrt{2Mr}+2M\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{r}-\sqrt{2M}}{\sqrt{r}+\sqrt{2M}}\right)\right]+\vartheta_{0},

(96)

where
\vartheta_{0}
and
q
are constants.
Both solutions above (with plus and minus sign) coincide with that found by Babichev and Charmousis
Babichev:2013cya
with the identification
\mu=2M
. To further verify the regularity at the horizon, let us use the advanced coordinate (
86
); in this case, the positive branch of the solution is

\vartheta_{+}(v,r)=q\left[v-r+2\sqrt{2Mr}-4M\ln\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{r}{2M}}\right)\right]+\vartheta_{0},

(97)

clearly confirming its regularity at
r=2M
. Furthermore, the gradient of the scalar field,
v_{\mu}
, is also regular at the horizon. The line element in the
g
-metric frame, in regular coordinates, reads

\mathrm{d}s_{g}^{2}=\frac{1}{z}\left[-A(r)\mathrm{d}v^{2}+2\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}r+r^{2}\mathrm{d}\Omega^{2}\right]+\frac{\gamma}{z^{2}}\left[q\mathrm{d}v+\left(\psi^{\prime}(r)-\frac{q}{A(r)}\right)\mathrm{d}r\right]^{2}.

(98)

For the future-regular branch,

\psi^{\prime}(r)-\frac{q}{A(r)}=\frac{\sqrt{q^{2}+Y_{0}A(r)}-q}{A(r)}\to\frac{Y_{0}}{2q},\quad\mbox{when}\quad r\to 2M,

(99)

so Eq. (
98
) is regular at the future horizon.

Therefore, from the perspective of the
h
-frame, we constructed a stealth black hole with a non-trivial scalar field (regular everywhere), with
\mathcal{J}^{\mu}=0
and
\mathcal{J}^{2}=0
. On the other hand, from the perspective of the
g
-metric frame, we constructed a black hole setup with secondary hair because matter is assumed to couple to the physical metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, and
g_{\mu\nu}
is disformally related to
h_{\mu\nu}
; the scalar gradient induces a deformation of the physical geometry. Hence the solution (as we have seen before) is better interpreted as carrying secondary disformal scalar hair rather than as a stealth configuration in the physical frame.

3.2
Kerr metric in the
h
-metric frame

We now consider the case in which the metric is a Kerr background in the
h
-metric frame. We also denote the Kerr rotation parameter by
a_{K}
. In
Boyer–Lindquist coordinates, the line element is

\displaystyle ds_{h}^{2}=

\displaystyle-\left(1-\frac{2Mr}{\Sigma}\right)dt^{2}-\frac{4Ma_{K}r\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\,dt\,d\phi+\frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}\,dr^{2}+\Sigma\,d\theta^{2}+\frac{\mathcal{A}\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\,d\phi^{2},

(100)

where

\displaystyle\Sigma=r^{2}+a_{K}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,\qquad\Delta=r^{2}-2Mr+a_{K}^{2},

(101)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=(r^{2}+a_{K}^{2})^{2}-a_{K}^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta.

(102)

Since the Kerr metric is Ricci-flat,

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}(h)=0,

(103)

it solves the
h
-frame metric equations on the ghost-free branch

\displaystyle\alpha=6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2}.

(104)

On the same branch, the Noether current vanishes identically for Ricci-flat

h_{\mu\nu}
, and therefore the scalar field equation is automatically satisfied.
Thus Kerr provides another exact black-hole geometry of the theory in the
h
-metric frame.
It should be emphasized that the Kerr character of the solution refers to the
metric
h_{\mu\nu}
. The metric
g_{\mu\nu}
is obtained through the disformal
map (
41
).
Consequently, for
\gamma\neq 0
and a nontrivial scalar gradient, the physical geometry is
not the Kerr metric, but a Kerr-derived disformal geometry. Therefore, the usual Kerr
horizon, ergosurface, separability properties, and Carter constant cannot be assumed a
priori for
g_{\mu\nu}
. They must instead be determined directly from the physical metric.
In the present work, we establish the existence of the exact Kerr solution in the
h
-metric frame and its
associated disformal physical geometry; a detailed analysis of the causal structure and
geodesic separability of
g_{\mu\nu}
will be carried out elsewhere.

3.2.1
Static axisymmetric fixed-norm profile

Let us first construct the direct analogue of the static Schwarzschild profile. We impose
the constant
h
-norm condition

\displaystyle Y\equiv h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\vartheta\,\partial_{\nu}\vartheta=Y_{0},

(105)

where
Y_{0}
is constant, and take the separable ansatz

\displaystyle\vartheta(r,\theta)=R(r)+\Theta(\theta).

(106)

Using the inverse Kerr metric, the fixed-norm condition becomes

\displaystyle\Delta\left(R^{\prime}(r)\right)^{2}+\left(\Theta^{\prime}(\theta)\right)^{2}=Y_{0}\left(r^{2}+a_{K}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\right).

(107)

Introducing a separation constant
C
, one obtains

\displaystyle\Delta\left(R^{\prime}(r)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=Y_{0}r^{2}+C,

(108)

\displaystyle\left(\Theta^{\prime}(\theta)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=Y_{0}a_{K}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta-C.

(109)

Therefore,

\displaystyle v_{r}=\partial_{r}\vartheta=\sigma_{r}\sqrt{\frac{Y_{0}r^{2}+C}{\Delta}},\qquad v_{\theta}=\partial_{\theta}\vartheta=\sigma_{\theta}\sqrt{Y_{0}a_{K}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta-C},

(110)

where
\sigma_{r},\sigma_{\theta}=\pm 1
label the independent branches. The scalar field is
then given implicitly by

\displaystyle\vartheta(r,\theta)=\vartheta_{0}+\sigma_{r}\int dr\sqrt{\frac{Y_{0}r^{2}+C}{r^{2}-2Mr+a_{K}^{2}}}+\sigma_{\theta}\int d\theta\sqrt{Y_{0}a_{K}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta-C}.

(111)

Reality of the angular profile restricts the allowed range of
C
. In particular, for a
globally real profile on the full angular interval, one must choose
C
such that

Y_{0}a_{K}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta-C\geq 0

in the region of interest.

The metric
g_{\mu\nu}
is obtained from the disformal relation

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{z}h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{\gamma}{z^{2}}\partial_{\mu}\vartheta\,\partial_{\nu}\vartheta.

(112)

Equivalently,

\displaystyle ds_{g}^{2}=\frac{1}{z}ds_{h}^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{z^{2}}\left(v_{r}\,dr+v_{\theta}\,d\theta\right)^{2}.

(113)

Thus the sector involving
t
and
\phi
is simply rescaled,

\displaystyle g_{tt}=\frac{1}{z}h_{tt},\qquad g_{t\phi}=\frac{1}{z}h_{t\phi},\qquad g_{\phi\phi}=\frac{1}{z}h_{\phi\phi},

(114)

whereas the
(r,\theta)
sector is disformally deformed:

\displaystyle g_{rr}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Sigma}{z\Delta}+\frac{\gamma}{z^{2}}\frac{Y_{0}r^{2}+C}{\Delta},

(115)

\displaystyle g_{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Sigma}{z}+\frac{\gamma}{z^{2}}\left(Y_{0}a_{K}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta-C\right),

(116)

\displaystyle g_{r\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{z^{2}}\,\sigma_{r}\sigma_{\theta}\sqrt{\frac{(Y_{0}r^{2}+C)(Y_{0}a_{K}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta-C)}{\Delta}}.

(117)

The off-diagonal component
g_{r\theta}
is not a rotational term. It is generated by
the angular dependence of the scalar-gradient profile. The rotational character of the
geometry is still encoded in the Kerr component
g_{t\phi}
.

A particularly simple branch is obtained by choosing
C=0
. In this case,

\displaystyle v_{r}=\sigma_{r}\sqrt{Y_{0}}\frac{r}{\sqrt{\Delta}},\qquad v_{\theta}=\sigma_{\theta}\sqrt{Y_{0}}\,a_{K}\cos\theta,

(118)

and the scalar field can be written explicitly as

\displaystyle\vartheta(r,\theta)=\vartheta_{0}+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{Y_{0}}\left[\sqrt{\Delta}+M\ln\left|r-M+\sqrt{\Delta}\right|\right]+\sigma_{\theta}\sqrt{Y_{0}}\,a_{K}\sin\theta.

(119)

The corresponding line element in the
g
-metric frame reads

\displaystyle ds_{g}^{2}=\frac{1}{z}ds_{h}^{2}+\frac{\gamma Y_{0}}{z^{2}}\left(\sigma_{r}\frac{r}{\sqrt{\Delta}}\,dr+\sigma_{\theta}a_{K}\cos\theta\,d\theta\right)^{2}.

(120)

In the Schwarzschild limit
a_{K}\to 0
, the angular part of the scalar disappears and
one recovers the static Schwarzschild-derived profile discussed in the previous subsection,
with
Y_{0}
playing the role of the constant scalar-gradient norm.

3.2.2
Stationary future-regular profile

As in the Schwarzschild case, one may construct a scalar profile with linear time
dependence while keeping the background stationary in the
h
-metric frame. This is the Kerr analogue of
the Babichev–Charmousis-type scalar configuration. We take

\displaystyle\vartheta(t,r,\theta)=qt+R(r)+\Theta(\theta),

(121)

where
q
is a constant. Since the theory is shift-symmetric, the physical metric depends
only on the one-form
d\vartheta
. Therefore, although the scalar itself depends linearly
on
t
, the disformally related metric is stationary.

The fixed-norm condition,

\displaystyle Y=h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\vartheta\,\partial_{\nu}\vartheta=Y_{0},

(122)

gives, after multiplying by
\Sigma
,

\displaystyle Y_{0}\Sigma=\Delta\left(R^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(\Theta^{\prime}\right)^{2}-\frac{q^{2}(r^{2}+a_{K}^{2})^{2}}{\Delta}+a_{K}^{2}q^{2}\sin^{2}\theta.

(123)

This equation can be separated. Introducing a separation constant
K
, we write

\displaystyle\Delta\left(R^{\prime}\right)^{2}-\frac{q^{2}(r^{2}+a_{K}^{2})^{2}}{\Delta}-Y_{0}r^{2}

\displaystyle=-K,

(124)

\displaystyle\left(\Theta^{\prime}\right)^{2}+a_{K}^{2}q^{2}\sin^{2}\theta-Y_{0}a_{K}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta

\displaystyle=K.

(125)

Hence,

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{\pm}(r)=\pm\frac{\sqrt{q^{2}(r^{2}+a_{K}^{2})^{2}+\Delta\left(Y_{0}r^{2}-K\right)}}{\Delta},

(126)

and

\displaystyle\Theta^{\prime}_{\pm}(\theta)=\pm\sqrt{K+Y_{0}a_{K}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta-a_{K}^{2}q^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}.

(127)

The reality of the angular profile restricts the allowed values of
K
. In particular, the
quantity

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(\theta)\equiv K+Y_{0}a_{K}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta-a_{K}^{2}q^{2}\sin^{2}\theta

(128)

must be non-negative in the angular domain considered.

Let us now analyze the behavior at the future event horizon. For a non-extremal Kerr
black hole, the outer horizon is located at

\displaystyle r_{+}=M+\sqrt{M^{2}-a_{K}^{2}}.

(129)

The Boyer–Lindquist coordinates are singular at
\Delta=0
, so the regularity of the
scalar must be checked in ingoing Kerr coordinates. We define

\displaystyle dv=dt+\frac{r^{2}+a_{K}^{2}}{\Delta}\,dr,\qquad d\tilde{\phi}=d\phi+\frac{a_{K}}{\Delta}\,dr.

(130)

Then the scalar one-form becomes

\displaystyle d\vartheta=q\,dv+\left[R^{\prime}(r)-q\frac{r^{2}+a_{K}^{2}}{\Delta}\right]dr+\Theta^{\prime}(\theta)d\theta.

(131)

The future-regular branch is selected by choosing the sign of
R^{\prime}
so that the leading
term
q(r^{2}+a_{K}^{2})/\Delta
cancels at
r=r_{+}
. Equivalently, for
q\neq 0
,

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{\rm reg}(r)={\rm sgn}(q)\,\frac{\sqrt{q^{2}(r^{2}+a_{K}^{2})^{2}+\Delta\left(Y_{0}r^{2}-K\right)}}{\Delta}.

(132)

Expanding near the outer horizon, one obtains

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{\rm reg}(r)-q\frac{r^{2}+a_{K}^{2}}{\Delta}\longrightarrow\frac{Y_{0}r_{+}^{2}-K}{2q(r_{+}^{2}+a_{K}^{2})},\qquad r\to r_{+}.

(133)

Thus the potentially divergent contribution cancels, and
d\vartheta
is regular at the
future horizon. In terms of the ingoing coordinate
v
, the regular scalar profile may be
written as

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\rm reg}(v,r,\theta)=qv+\int dr\left[R^{\prime}_{\rm reg}(r)-q\frac{r^{2}+a_{K}^{2}}{\Delta(r)}\right]+\int d\theta\,\Theta^{\prime}(\theta)+\vartheta_{0}.

(134)

The radial integrand is finite at
r=r_{+}
, so the scalar field is regular on the future
horizon. The opposite branch is singular at the future horizon and corresponds instead to
a branch adapted to the past horizon.

In ingoing Kerr coordinates, the Kerr line element, in the
h
-metric frame, takes the regular form

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{h}=

\displaystyle-\left(1-\frac{2Mr}{\Sigma}\right)dv^{2}+2\,dv\,dr+\Sigma\,d\theta^{2}-\frac{4Ma_{K}r\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\,dv\,d\tilde{\phi}

\displaystyle\quad-2a_{K}\sin^{2}\theta\,dr\,d\tilde{\phi}+\frac{\mathcal{A}\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\,d\tilde{\phi}^{\,2}.

(135)

The line element in the
g
-metric frame is, therefore,

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{g}=\frac{1}{z}ds^{2}_{h}+\frac{\gamma}{z^{2}}\left\{q\,dv+\left[R^{\prime}_{\rm reg}(r)-q\frac{r^{2}+a_{K}^{2}}{\Delta}\right]dr+\Theta^{\prime}(\theta)d\theta\right\}^{2}.

(136)

This expression is manifestly regular at the future horizon, provided the angular profile
is real and finite in the angular region considered. The regularity follows from two facts:
first,
ds^{2}_{h}
is the regular ingoing Kerr form in the
h
-metric frame; second,
the scalar one-form entering the disformal term is finite at
r=r_{+}
.

It is also useful to check the Schwarzschild limit. Taking
a_{K}\to 0
and choosing

K=0
, one finds

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{\rm reg}(r)\longrightarrow\frac{\sqrt{q^{2}+Y_{0}A(r)}}{A(r)},\qquad A(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r},

(137)

and

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{\rm reg}(r)-\frac{q}{A(r)}\longrightarrow\frac{\sqrt{q^{2}+Y_{0}A(r)}-q}{A(r)}.

(138)

This is precisely the future-regular branch of the time-dependent Schwarzschild profile
discussed in the previous subsection.

Therefore, as in the Schwarzschild case, the solution is stealth only in the

h
-metric frame. On the branch
\alpha=6\beta^{2}\kappa^{2}
, Kerr is Ricci-flat, the Noether
current vanishes, and the scalar equation is automatically satisfied. In the

g
-frame, however, the scalar gradient enters through a disformal relation and, therefore, deforms the
metric
g_{\mu\nu}
away from Ricci-flat backgrounds in the
h
-metric frame. The resulting background is better interpreted as a Kerr black hole dressed
by secondary disformal scalar hair in the
h
-metric frame, rather than as a purely stealth Kerr black hole in the
g
-metric frame.

4
Conclusions

In this work, we constructed a projectively invariant ghost-free metric-affine scalar-torsion model in
which a pseudoscalar field is coupled to the Nieh–Yan density. After fixing the projective gauge, the independent connection
can be solved exactly. Its symmetric part becomes the Levi-Civita connection associated
with the
h
-metric frame, while its antisymmetric part is a purely axial torsion sourced by
the scalar gradient. The
g
-metric frame is then related to the
h
-metric frame
through a disformal transformation.

We showed that, after integrating out the connection and the axial torsion, the scalar
sector admits a special degenerate branch in which the ordinary scalar kinetic contribution
is canceled by the torsion-induced term. On this branch, Ricci-flat geometries in the

h
-metric frame solve the metric field equations, and the Noether current vanishes
identically. Therefore, the scalar field equation is automatically satisfied without forcing
the scalar profile to be trivial. This provides a simple mechanism for constructing exact
black-hole solutions with nontrivial scalar configurations.

We applied this construction to Schwarzschild and Kerr geometries in the
h
-metric
frame. In the Schwarzschild case, we obtained both a static fixed-norm scalar profile and a
Babichev-Charmousis-like time-dependent profile. The latter is regular on the future
horizon when written in advanced Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates. The corresponding
geometry in the
g
-metric frame is not Schwarzschild when the disformal coupling
is nonzero. In the static case, it can be interpreted as a Schwarzschild-like geometry with a
solid-angle deficit generated by the scalar-gradient sector, rather than by a
topological monopole charge.

In the Kerr case, we found that the scalar profile is generically axisymmetric. In contrast
with the Schwarzschild branch, the scalar field depends not only on the radial coordinate
but also on the angular coordinate
\theta
. This angular dependence is a feature of the
rotating geometries and disappears in the nonrotating limit. It also contributes to the
disformal deformation of the
g
-metric frame, in particular through the angular-radial
sector. We also constructed a stationary future-regular profile in ingoing Kerr coordinates,
where the scalar one-form and the associated
g
-metric remain regular at the future
horizon, provided the angular profile is real and finite in the angular domain considered.

The black hole solutions accompanied by nontrivial scalar field profiles are stealth only in the
h
-metric frame. In the

g
-metric frame, matter couples to the disformally related metric, and the scalar gradient
generically deforms the geometry. The resulting black holes should therefore be interpreted
as carrying secondary disformal scalar hair rather than primary Noether-charge hair. In
the Kerr case, the geometry in the
h
-metric frame is exactly Kerr, but the geometry in
the
g
-metric frame is Kerr-derived and disformally deformed. Its horizon structure,
ergoregion, causal properties, and possible separability should be analyzed directly in the

g
-metric frame. We leave these questions for future work.
