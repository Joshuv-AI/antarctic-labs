Twisted Holographic Superconductors in External Magnetic Field
Jovan Potrebić, Dragoljub Gočanin

Abstract
Among various applications of the AdS/CFT correspondence in condensed matter physics of particular importance is the realization of the phase transition between the normal and superconducting phase in a holographic QFT. After seminal papers on holographic superconductors that introduced the basic setup, one of the main lines of development focused on capturing the Meissner effect with all the relevant parameters, which requires inclusion of an external magnetic field. Although a complete holographic description of a superconductor is still lacking, the basic elements of the gravitational systems dual to what can be most accurately characterized as a charged superfluid have been established. Using holographic setups for describing three- and four-dimensional superconductors, we investigate the effect of noncommutative twist deformation of bulk fields on the phase transition parameters, such as the critical magnetic field and condensate. In a wider context, our results represent a first systematic attempt to elucidate the role of noncommutative gauge field theory as part of the bulk description of condensed matter systems.



I
Introduction

One of the most significant discoveries within string theory, which proved to be a fruitful ground for future investigations, much of which is independent of string theory itself, is the AdS/CFT correspondence
Maldacena 1998
;
Gubser et al. 1998
;
Witten 1998
, which relates string theory in (asymptotically)
AdS_{d+1}
spacetime to a conformal field theory (CFT) defined, in a sense, on its
d
-dimensional boundary. Over time, this correspondence has been generalized into what is now known as gauge–gravity duality, which states that certain quantum field theories (QFTs) are equivalent to higher-dimensional gravitational theories. Of particular interest is the so-called weak limit of duality, in which strongly coupled QFTs on the boundary correspond to classical gravitational theories in higher dimensions.

As shown in
Gubser 2008
, spontaneous
U(1)
symmetry breaking in bulk geometries with a horizon can be used to describe the transition from the normal to the superconducting phase in the dual QFT. This observation led to the seminal work of Hartnoll, Herzog, and Horowitz
Hartnoll et al. 2008a
;
Hartnoll et al. 2008b
;
Herzog et al. 2009
, in which a model of a holographic superconductor was first introduced. Over time, many extensions of this model and various applications of gauge–gravity duality to condensed matter physics have appeared
Horowitz and Roberts 2008
;
Nakano and Wen 2008
;
Amado et al. 2009
;
Koutsoumbas et al. 2009
;
Maeda et al. 2008
;
Sonner 2009
;
Zeng et al. 2009
;
Pang 2009
;
Cai and Zhang 2010
;
Siopsis and Therrien 2010
.

One of the first questions that arose was how to extend the model to incorporate magnetic fields. A realistic holographic model is expected to exhibit the Meissner effect and to allow for the computation of the superconducting coherence length and magnetic penetration depth. To capture these phenomena, dynamical gauge fields on the boundary theory are required. However, the original model of Hartnoll
et al.
considered
U(1)
gauge fields in the bulk, which, according to the holographic dictionary, correspond to a global
U(1)
symmetry on the boundary. Consequently, although this construction was referred to as an AdS/CFT superconductor, it is more accurately described as a
charged superfluid
. The first attempts to incorporate constant magnetic fields appeared shortly thereafter
Nakano and Wen 2008
;
Albash and Johnson 2008
;
Hartnoll 2009
.

The appearance of a phase transition in the
(2+1)
-dimensional case is particularly intriguing, since the Mermin–Wagner-Hohenberg theorem
Mermin and Wagner 1966
;
Hohenberg 1967
forbids spontaneous symmetry breaking due to large fluctuations in low-dimensional systems. However, the holographic realization of superconductivity is fundamentally different. In this setup, the phase transition becomes possible due to the peculiarities of the large-
N
limit, which effectively suppresses fluctuations and thereby allows algebraic long-range order to survive. The question of condensation in higher dimensions was soon addressed in
Gregory et al. 2009
. Shortly thereafter, models of
(3+1)
-dimensional holographic superconductors were constructed with
Ge et al. 2010
and without magnetic fields
Gregory et al. 2009
;
Pan et al. 2010
;
Pan and Wang 2010
. Related studies have addressed flux periodicity and quantum hair in holographic superconductors, magnetic effects in the insulator/superconductor transition, holographic superfluids/superconductors in dilaton-gravity backgrounds, emergent gauge fields, and phase transitions in dilaton holography with global or local symmetries
Montull et al. 2011
;
Montull et al. 2012
;
Salvio 2012
;
Domenech et al. 2010
;
Salvio 2013
.

On the other hand, background magnetic fields (or their generalizations) are known to be linked, in certain setups, with the emergence of the structure of noncommutative (NC) geometry. A typical example is provided by an effective description of a single Landau level (usually LLL) as a NC plane generated by noncommuting guiding-center coordinates of the electron’s
cyclotron motion in a strong orthogonal magnetic field,
[R_{x},R_{y}]=i\ell_{B}^{2}
, with the NC scale set by the magnetic length
\ell_{B}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{eB}}
, see
Girvin et al. 1986
. A similar structure appears in the seminal paper by Dong and Senthil
Dong and Senthil 2020
where composite fermion fields on NC space are used to describe fluctuations in the low-energy regime, see also
Gočanin et al. 2021
.
As a kind of generalization, in the string theory setting, Seiberg and Witten found that, within an appropriate limit, the endpoints of an open string attached to a D-brane emersed in a background NS-NS B-field effectively probe a NC space, with the NC scale inversely proportional to the B-field
Seiberg and Witten 1999
, see also
Ardalan et al. 1999
;
Chu and Ho 1999
. The effective description of the brane is given by NC gauge field theory.

Motivated by a general expectation that the classical structure of spacetime breaks in one way or another at small enough length scales (presumably Planckian), one can promote spacetime coordinates into operators that satisfy some nontrivial commutation relations, the most common of which are the canonical, or
\theta
-constant commutation relations,
[\hat{x}^{\mu},\hat{x}^{\nu}]=i\theta^{\mu\nu}
,
where the NC deformation parameters
\theta^{\mu\nu}
comprise a constant antisymmetric matrix,
\theta^{\mu\nu}=-\theta^{\nu\mu}
, thus introducing a NC geometry of the spacetime itself. In a NC spacetime, uncertainty relations
\Delta x^{\mu}\Delta x^{\nu}\geq\frac{1}{2}|\theta^{\mu\nu}|
render an ideal localization of events (spacetime points) impossible.
An important aspect of having a (canonical) NC spacetime as a background is that it can deform the Landau levels of an electron in a magnetic field by effectively changing the value of the magnetic field,
B_{\text{eff}}=B(1+B\theta)
. It should be noted that spacetime noncommutativity by itself is not enough to replace the external magnetic field; it can only modify an already existing one
Dimitrijević Ćirić et al. 2018
.

In this work, we build on the framework developed in
Albash and Johnson 2008
;
Ge et al. 2010
and investigate the consequences of having NC fields in the bulk on the physics of
(2+1)
- and
(3+1)
-dimensional holographic superconductors in the presence of external magnetic fields. We show that NC gauge field structure in the bulk has a direct effect on the parameters of the dual boundary system, e.g., reducing the critical magnetic field. This is a first step in a systematic investigation of the potential that NC field theory/gravity has for capturing the physics of superfluids/superconductors in the setting of gauge–gravity duality.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
II
, we introduce some basic elements of NC field theory that will be needed in the main part of the paper. In Section
III
, we present a NC model of a holographic superconductor in a homogeneous external magnetic field and, employing numerical methods, determine the impact of noncommutativity on the condensate and the critical magnetic field. In Section
IV
, a model of a NC
(3+1)
-dimensional holographic superconductor is analyzed using both numerical and analytical approaches, and the effect of the NC parameter on the critical magnetic field is obtained. Conclusions and outlook are presented in the final section. The details of the calculations are presented in the Appendices
A
,
B
and
C
.

II
NC Field Theory

The models of holographic superconductors that we are going to use in the following sections involve ordinary dual gravitational descriptions in terms of classical black hole geometries with AdS asymptotics. What we will be interested in is what happens with a holographic superconductor (its critical magnetic field and condensate) when we apply a NC twist of the bulk fields that deforms the classical bulk. Note that in our approach, the NC structure does not pertain to the boundary system, which is, in principle, considered to be some realistic superconductor accessible to observations, but merely to its dual geometric description. In this sense, NC geometry is used as a tool that could potentially broaden the holographic language and perhaps capture some new aspect of a lower-dimensional CMT system.

In general, an (Abelian) twist operator can be defined in a coordinate-free fashion using a set
\{X_{I}\,|\,I=1,\dots,s\leq D\}
of mutually commuting vector fields (note that their number need not be equal to the number of spacetime dimensions
D
) and a constant antysymmetric matrix
\theta^{IJ}
, as

\mathcal{F}=\exp\left(-\frac{i}{2}\theta^{IJ}X_{I}\otimes X_{J}\right).

(1)

The twist operator deforms the ordinary commutative product of functions on spacetime into a NC
\star
-product

\displaystyle f_{1}\star f_{2}=\mu\left(\mathcal{F}^{-1}(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=f_{1}f_{2}+\frac{i}{2}\theta^{IJ}X_{I}[f_{1}]X_{J}[f_{2}]+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{2}),

(2)

where
\mu\left(f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\right)=f_{1}\cdot f_{2}
is the multiplication map and
X_{I}[f]=X^{\mu}_{I}\partial_{\mu}f
.
Since the vector fields
X_{I}
commute, the
\star
-product is associative. The system of commuting vector fields
\{X_{I}\}
that generate the twist operator
are an independent structure on the spacetime manifold that provides a generally covariant formulation of an NC theory. However, there is no universal criterion for determining what set of vector fields we should choose in a particular situation. We can always choose (at least locally) a system of coordinates
x^{\mu}
adapted to
\{X_{I}\}
by setting
X_{I}=\partial_{\mu}
so that (
II
) reduces to the standard Moyal-Weyl-Gronewold
\star
-product

\displaystyle f_{1}\star f_{2}

\displaystyle=f_{1}f_{2}+\frac{i}{2}\theta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}f_{1}\partial_{\nu}f_{2}+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{2}).

(3)

When applied on coordinate functions themselves, it gives us the
\theta
-constant
\star
-commutator relations,

[x^{\mu},x^{\nu}]_{\star}=x^{\mu}\star x^{\nu}-x^{\nu}\star x^{\mu}=i\theta^{\mu\nu}=const.

In the absence of a universal criteria, it seems reasonable to base our choice on the isometries of spacetime. Taking a set of commuting Killing vector fields
\{K_{I}\}
of a given spacetime as generators of an Abelian twist ensures that, while the algebraic structure of the theory gets NC deformed, the coupling with the metric and all the objects built out of it (connection, curvature) remains unchanged.
Namely, by choosing the coordinate system

y^{\alpha}
such that
K_{I}=\delta^{\alpha}_{I}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y^{\alpha}}\equiv\delta^{\alpha}_{I}\partial_{\alpha}
, the Killing equation yields
\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}(y)=0
, ensuring that the coupling with the metric remains commutative as in the classical theory,

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\star(\cdots)

\displaystyle=g_{\mu\nu}\cdot(\cdots)+\frac{i}{2}\theta^{IJ}\delta^{\alpha}_{I}\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}(y)\mathcal{L}_{K_{J}}(\cdots)

\displaystyle=g_{\mu\nu}\cdot(\cdots).

(4)

While it leaves the coupling with spacetime geometry unchanged, a Killing twist can result in NC deformed equations of motion for matter fields.

II.1
Seiberg-Witten Gauge Field Theory

To define an action for NC gauge field theory we first replace the ordinary commutative product by a NC
\star
-product in the classical action. But to maintain gauge invariance of the action, one also has to introduce NC fields (denoted by a hat symbol) that transform in representations of a NC-deformed symmetry group. In the Seiberg-Witten (SW) construction, a variation of a NC matter field (in the fundamental representation) under an infinitesimal NC gauge transformation is given by

\delta_{\hat{\epsilon}}^{\star}\,\hat{\Phi}=i\,\hat{\epsilon}\star\hat{\Phi},

(5)

where
\hat{\epsilon}(x)
is a NC gauge parameter. However, if we assume
\hat{\epsilon}
to be Lie algebra-valued, NC gauge transformations will fail to satisfy the closure axiom. The problem is resolved by taking
\hat{\epsilon}
from the universal enveloping algebra (UEA) of the starting (undeformed) Lie algebra, in which case we have a closure

[\delta_{\hat{\epsilon}_{1}}^{\star},\delta_{\hat{\epsilon}_{2}}^{\star}]_{\star}=\delta^{\star}_{-i[\hat{\epsilon}_{1},\hat{\epsilon}_{2}]_{\star}}.

(6)

In the SW construction, NC covariant derivative and NC field strength inherit the structure of their classical counterparts,

\hat{D}_{\mu}\hat{\Phi}=\partial_{\mu}\hat{\Phi}-i\,\hat{A}_{\mu}\star\hat{\Phi},

(7)

\hat{F}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\hat{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\hat{A}_{\mu}-i[\hat{A}_{\mu},\hat{A}_{\nu}]_{\star}.

(8)

Note that even if we work with an Abelian gauge theory with
U(1)
gauge group, the NC
U(1)_{\star}
field strength still has the non-Abelian character due to
\star
-product. However, the fact that
\hat{F}_{\mu\nu}
is also UEA-valued means that the system has infinitely many new degrees of freedom. To eliminate these unwanted degrees of freedom we use the SW map
Seiberg and Witten 1999
, which allows us to express the NC degrees of freedom in terms of the original commutative ones. The basic principle is that NC gauge transformations are induced by the commutative ones,

\delta^{\star}_{\hat{\epsilon}}\hat{A}_{\mu}(A)=\hat{A}_{\mu}(A+\delta_{\epsilon}A)-\hat{A}_{\mu}(A),

(9)

where
\delta_{\epsilon}A_{\mu}=-\partial_{\mu}\epsilon
and
\delta^{\star}_{\hat{\epsilon}}\hat{A}_{\mu}=-\partial_{\mu}\hat{\epsilon}+i[\hat{\epsilon},\hat{A}_{\mu}]_{\star}
. Now we can solve the differential equation
9
perturbatively and derive the SW representation of the NC field
\hat{A}_{\mu}
as a power series in
\theta^{\mu\nu}
with coefficients built out of classical fields (likewise for
\hat{\Phi}
and
\hat{F}_{\mu\nu}
),

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=A_{\mu}-\frac{1}{4}\,\theta^{\alpha\beta}\{A_{\alpha},\partial_{\beta}A_{\mu}+F_{\beta\mu}\}+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{2}),

(10)

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=F_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\,\theta^{\alpha\beta}\{A_{\alpha},(\partial_{\beta}+D_{\beta})F_{\mu\nu}\}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\,\theta^{\alpha\beta}\{F_{\alpha\mu},F_{\beta\nu}\}+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{2}),

(11)

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\Phi-\frac{1}{4}\,\theta^{\alpha\beta}A_{\alpha}(\partial_{\beta}+D_{\beta})\Phi+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{2}).

(12)

Finally, the NC action can be organized as a perturbative expansion in the powers of
\theta^{\mu\nu}
, maintaining the (undeformed) gauge invariance at each order.

D Twisted Holographic Superconductor in an External Magnetic Field

A model of a
(2+1)
-dimensional holographic superconductor immersed in an external magnetic field is constructed in
Albash and Johnson 2008
and the bulk action is given by

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{4}^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Bigg(R+\frac{6}{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+L^{2}\left[-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-(D_{\mu}\Phi)^{*}D^{\mu}\Phi-V(|\Phi|)\Bigg]\right),

(13)

where
D_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}-iA_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative that involves (when appropriate) the standard symmetric and metric-compatible Christofel connection
\Gamma_{\mu}
of the curved background, and we choose the quadratic potential

(which turns out to be enough) with

Moreover, we will work in the probe limit in which Einstein-Maxwell sector decouples from the scalar, and use a self-consistent, fully backreacted, electrically and magnetically charged, i.e., dyonic Reissner-Nordström (RN) black hole as a fixed background for the dynamical scalar field. This background is a particular (exact) solution of the Einstein-Maxwell field equations with the metric given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{2}\alpha^{2}}{z^{2}}\left(-f(z)\,dt^{2}+dx^{2}+dy^{2}+\frac{dz^{2}}{\alpha^{2}f(z)}\right),

(14)

where

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=1+(h^{2}+q^{2})z^{4}-\big(1+h^{2}+q^{2}\big)z^{3}

\displaystyle=(1-z)\big(z^{2}+z+1-(h^{2}+q^{2})z^{3}\big),

(15)

and the gauge field strength is

F=2h\alpha^{2}\,dx\wedge dy+2q\alpha\,dz\wedge dt.

(16)

In this coordinate system,

is a dimensionless radial coordinate, scaled such that the black hole horizon is located at
z_{h}=1
and the asymptotic boundary at

\alpha
,
q
, and

are related to the mass, electric charge, and magnetic charge of the black hole, respectively, with
\alpha

The temperature of the black hole and its dual field theory is obtained via Bekenstein-Hawking formula

T=\frac{1}{\beta}=\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}(3-h^{2}-q^{2}),

(17)

with

Since we are interested in axially symmetric configurations, we choose a gauge adapted to these symmetries, namely

A=h\alpha^{2}(x\,dy-y\,dx)+2q\alpha(z-1)\,dt.

(18)

Following
Kobayashi et al. 2007

-2q\alpha
so that the gauge potential remains regular at the horizon. This gauge potential produces a constant magnetic field on the boundary theory

B=F_{xy}=2h\alpha^{2}.

(19)

II
on the classical action (

\displaystyle S_{\text{NC}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\kappa_{4}^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\bigg(R+\frac{6}{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle L^{2}\bigg[-\frac{1}{4}g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\hat{F}_{\mu\nu}\star\hat{F}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}(D_{\mu}\hat{\Phi})^{*}\star D_{\nu}\hat{\Phi}-m^{2}\hat{\Phi}^{*}\star\hat{\Phi}\bigg]\bigg),

where
D_{\mu}\hat{\Phi}=\partial_{\mu}\hat{\Phi}-i\hat{A_{\mu}}\star\hat{\Phi}

\hat{\Phi}

which, through the mass–dimension relation, implies that the scaling dimension of the dual boundary operator is also modified. To avoid this shift, we redefine the mass squared as

m^{\prime 2}=-\frac{2}{L^{2}},

(30)

which remains safely above the BF bound. After that (
III
) reduces to

\displaystyle Z^{\prime\prime}(z)

\displaystyle+\Bigg(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-\frac{2}{z}\Bigg)Z^{\prime}(z)+\Bigg(\frac{4q^{2}(z-1)^{2}}{f(z)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{z^{2}f(z)}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha^{2}f(z)}(1+2h{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk})\Bigg)Z(z)=0.

(31)

In order to formulate appropriate boundary conditions, we analyze equation (
III
) both near the horizon and near the conformal boundary. Before doing so, let us introduce a dimensionless function

Z(z)\;\to\;\tilde{Z}(z)=\alpha L^{2}Z(z).

(32)

Near the AdS boundary, equation (
III
) reduces to

\tilde{Z}^{\prime\prime}(z)-\frac{2}{z}\tilde{Z}^{\prime}(z)+\frac{2}{z^{2}}\tilde{Z}(z)=0,

(33)

which is an Euler-type differential equation with solutions

\lim_{z\to 0}\tilde{Z}(z)=\Psi^{(1)}z+\Psi^{(2)}z^{2},

(34)

where
\Psi^{(1)}
and
\Psi^{(2)}
are dimensionless constants. Following
Klebanov and Witten 1999
, both of these modes prove to be normalizable, meaning that neither acts as a source for the other. This forces us to impose one of the following boundary conditions:

\Psi^{(1)}=0\quad\text{or}\quad\Psi^{(2)}=0.

(35)

Here,
\Psi^{(1)}
is proportional to the vacuum expectation value (VEV) of the
\Delta=1
operator
\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}\rangle
in the dual boundary theory, while
\Psi^{(2)}
corresponds to the VEV of the
\Delta=2
operator
\langle\mathcal{O}_{2}\rangle
. Since there are no qualitative differences between the
\Delta=1
and
\Delta=2
cases, in our computations we restrict ourselves to the
\Delta=2
case.

Next, we analyze the near-horizon behavior of (
III
), which can be classified into three distinct regimes:

•

Case 1:

\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}(1+2h{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk})<2
.
In this case, the equation of motion reduces to a Bessel equation

\tilde{Z}^{\prime\prime}(z)-\frac{1}{1-z}\tilde{Z}^{\prime}(z)+\frac{a^{2}}{1-z}\tilde{Z}(z)=0,

(36)

with

a^{2}=\frac{2-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}(1+2h{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk})}{3-h^{2}-q^{2}}>0,

(37)

whose solutions are

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to 1}\tilde{Z}(z)

\displaystyle=\psi_{1}J_{0}\!\left(2a\sqrt{1-z}\right)+\psi_{2}Y_{0}\!\left(2a\sqrt{1-z}\right)

\displaystyle\approx\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\gamma+\frac{1}{\pi}\log\big(a (1-z)\big)\right),

(38)

where
\gamma
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. To avoid divergences, we set
\psi_{2}=0
as the boundary condition.

•

Case 2:

\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}(1+2h{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk})=2
.
Here, the equation simplifies to

\tilde{Z}^{\prime\prime}(z)-\frac{1}{1-z}\tilde{Z}^{\prime}(z)+b^{2}\tilde{Z}(z)=0,

(39)

where

b^{2}=\frac{4q^{2}}{(3-h^{2}-q^{2})^{2}}+\frac{4}{3-h^{2}-q^{2}}>0,

(40)

with solutions

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to 1}\tilde{Z}(z)

\displaystyle=\psi_{1}J_{0}\!\left(b^{2}(1-z)\right)+\psi_{2}Y_{0}\!\left(b^{2}(1-z)\right)

\displaystyle\approx\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\gamma+\frac{2}{\pi}\log\big(b^2 (1-z)\big)\right).

(41)

Again, to avoid divergences, we impose
\psi_{2}=0
.

•

Case 3:

\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}(1+2h{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk})>2
.
In this regime, the equation becomes

\tilde{Z}^{\prime\prime}(z)-\frac{1}{1-z}\tilde{Z}^{\prime}(z)-\frac{a^{2}}{1-z}\tilde{Z}(z)=0,

(42)

with

a^{2}=\frac{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}(1+2h{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk})-2}{3-h^{2}-q^{2}}>0,

(43)

whose solutions are

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to 1}\tilde{Z}(z)

\displaystyle=\psi_{1}I_{0}\!\left(2a\sqrt{1-z}\right)+\psi_{2}K_{0}\!\left(2a\sqrt{1-z}\right)

\displaystyle\approx\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}\left(-\gamma-\log\big(a (1-z)\big)\right).

(44)

To ensure regularity, we again choose
\psi_{2}=0
as the boundary condition.

III.1
Numerical Method and Results

The equation (
III
) cannot be solved analytically, and therefore we must employ numerical methods. In our analysis, we use the shooting method with initial conditions

\tilde{Z}(1)=1,\quad\tilde{Z}^{\prime}(1)=\frac{2-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}(1+2h{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk})}{3-h^{2}-q^{2}}\,.

(45)

The system depends on several parameters,
\{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha^{2}},h,q,{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}\}
. The value of
\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}
was previously chosen as
\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}=\frac{Bn}{\alpha^{2}}=2h
. This leaves us with three free parameters,
{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}
,
h
, and
q
. However, for arbitrary choices of these parameters, solutions do not exhibit the correct asymptotic behavior compatible with (
34
). Consequently, in our method, for a fixed value of
{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}
, we fix the value of
h
and then vary
q
until the boundary conditions at the conformal boundary are satisfied.

We observe two classes of solutions: zero-node and one-node modes, shown in Figure

. Nevertheless, as argued in Ref.
Gubser 2008
, only zero-node modes are physically relevant.

For convenience, we define the rescaled temperature

\tilde{T}=\alpha\frac{3-\tilde{q}^{2}}{4\pi},

(46)

which corresponds to the temperature at zero magnetic field at which non-trivial solutions appear, with the corresponding

denoting the critical value at which condensation sets in.

An important dimensionless quantity of interest is

\frac{B}{\tilde{T}^{2}}=\Bigg(\frac{4\pi}{3-\tilde{q}^{2}}\Bigg)^{2}2h.

We therefore study its dependence on
\tfrac{T}{\tilde{T}}
and compare it to the commutative case reported in

IV
4D Twisted Holographic Superconductor in an External Magnetic Field

In the previous section, we analyzed the effects of an Abelian Killing twist deformation of the bulk on the parameters of the
(2+1)
-dimensional holographic superconductor in an external magnetic field. Such an analysis can, to a certain extent, be extended to higher-dimensional cases. Here, we focus on the
(3+1)
-dimensional case. The topic of holographic superconductors in
(3+1)
-dimensions, both with and without an external magnetic field, has been extensively investigated
Gregory et al. 2009
;
Pan et al. 2010
;
Pan and Wang 2010
;
Liu et al. 2010
;
Ge et al. 2010
. Since in
5
-dimensional bulk the Gauss–Bonnet term is no longer topological, most of these studies include it. In our case, however, the twist effects are of primary interest. As we work with Killing twists, where the background metric remains undeformed, we restrict ourselves to the pure Einstein–Hilbert gravitational action. Therefore, our 5-dimensional bulk action is

S_{\text{NC}}=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{5}^{2}}\int d^{5}x\,\sqrt{-g}\Bigg(R+\frac{12}{L^{2}}\\
+L^{2}\Bigg[-\frac{1}{4}g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\hat{F}_{\mu\nu}\star\hat{F}_{\alpha\beta}\\
-g^{\mu\nu}(D_{\mu}\hat{\Phi})^{*}\star D_{\nu}\hat{\Phi}-m^{2}\hat{\Phi}^{*}\star\hat{\Phi}\Bigg]\Bigg).

(49)

Since in the
5
-dimensional bulk the electric and magnetic fields are no longer dual to each other, the construction of dyonic black branes with AdS asymptotics becomes considerably more involved. Therefore, unlike the
4
-dimensional case where the gravitational and gauge fields constitute a fixed background, here both the scalar and the gauge field are considered dynamical. Consequently, the AdS planar black brane is taken as a fixed geometrical background with the metric

ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+r^{2}(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dw^{2})+\frac{1}{f(r)}dr^{2},

(50)

where
f(r)=\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}(1-\left(\frac{r_{h}}{r}\right)^{4})
.The temperature of this black brane is given by

T=\frac{r_{h}}{\pi L^{2}}.

For subsequent calculations, it is convenient to introduce the Poincaré coordinate

z=\frac{r_{h}}{r},

in terms of which the metric takes the form

ds^{2}=\frac{L^{2}\alpha^{2}}{z^{2}}\left(-f(z)\,dt^{2}+dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dw^{2}\right)+\frac{L^{2}}{z^{2}}\frac{dz^{2}}{f(z)},

(51)

where
\alpha=\frac{r_{h}}{L^{2}}
and
f(z)=1-z^{4}
.
Note that the temperature can now be written as

T=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}.

Among the generators of the
\mathbb{R}\oplus ISO(3)
Killing algebra of the background spacetime, we again consider a twist generated by
\partial_{x}
and
\partial_{y}
,

\mathcal{F}=\exp\left[-\frac{i{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}}{2\alpha^{2}}\left(\partial_{x}\otimes\partial_{y}-\partial_{y}\otimes\partial_{x}\right)\right].

(52)

Using the same procedure as in the previous case, we obtain exactly the same first-order NC correction as
III
, together with the equations of motion, only now in five dimensions.

Following the logic of
Abrikosov et al. 1963
;
Ge et al. 2010
we will further consider separately strong and weak magnetic field limits. In the weak magnetic field limit there are no NC corrections and our analysis reduces completely to that of
Ge et al. 2010
;
Gregory et al. 2009
(for
D=5
and in pure Einstein-Hilbert case). Therefore, we focus on the strong magnetic field limit, where following
Abrikosov et al. 1963
we assume

A_{t}=A_{t}(z),\quad A_{x}=-By,\quad A_{y}=Bx,\quad A_{z}=A_{w}=0,

and
\Phi=\Phi(x,y,z)
. We choose the axially symmetric gauge, since we are interested in axially symmetric configurations. It is therefore convenient to switch to polar coordinates
(x,y)\to(r,\varphi)
, implying
\Phi=\Phi(r,\varphi,z)=\Phi(r,z)
.
Under these assumptions, the equations of motion for
A_{t}
and
\Phi
reduce to:

A_{t}^{\prime\prime}(z)-\frac{1}{z}A_{t}^{\prime}(z)+\frac{2L^{2}A_{t}\Phi}{z^{2}}\left(\Phi-\frac{B{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}}{2}r\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial r}\right)=0,

(53)

\displaystyle z^{2}f^{\prime}(z)\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z}-zf(z)\left(3\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z}-z\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial z^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{z^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}(1+B{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk})\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial r}\right)+\frac{z^{2}A_{t}^{2}(z)}{\alpha^{2}f(z)}\Phi

\displaystyle-m^{2}L^{2}\Phi\left(1-\frac{B{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}}{4}\right)-\frac{B^{2}z^{2}r^{2}}{4\alpha^{2}}(1+B{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk})\Phi=0.

(54)

The scalar field equation can be solved by separation of variables,
\Phi(r,z)=R(r)Z(z)
, which transforms the partial differential equation (
IV
) into a system of two coupled ordinary differential equations:

R^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{1}{r}R^{\prime}(r)-\Big(\frac{Br}{2}\Big)^{2}R(r)=-\lambda^{2}R(r),

(55)

\displaystyle Z^{\prime\prime}(z)

\displaystyle+\Bigg(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-\frac{3}{z}\Bigg)Z^{\prime}(z)+\Bigg(\frac{A_{t}^{2}(z)}{\alpha^{2}f^{2}(z)}

(56)

\displaystyle-\frac{m^{2}L^{2}}{z^{2}f(z)}

\displaystyle\Bigg(1-\frac{B{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}}{4}\Bigg)-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha^{2}f(z)}(1+B{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk})\Bigg)Z(z)=0.

Equation (
56
), similarly to (
26
), admits exact solutions in terms of hypergeometric functions. However, for
\lambda^{2}=Bn
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it reduces to the equation of a two-dimensional harmonic oscillator. Since the lowest modes are expected to condense first, we again choose
n=1
, which implies

R(r)=\exp\left(-\frac{Br^{2}}{4}\right).

(57)

The radial distribution of the condensate is the same as in the
(2+1)
-dimensional case, indicating strong localization and the formation of superfluid droplets.

Note that in equation (
56
) the mass squared receives an NC correction. However, we may again safely impose

m^{2}L^{2}\left(1-\frac{B{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}}{4}\right)=-3,

(58)

which, together with the chosen value of
\lambda^{2}
, reduces (
56
) to

\displaystyle Z^{\prime\prime}(z)

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-\frac{3}{z}\bigg)Z^{\prime}(z)+\bigg(\frac{A_{t}^{2}(z)}{\alpha^{2}f(z)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{z^{2}f(z)}-\frac{B}{\alpha^{2}f(z)}(1+B{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk})\bigg)Z(z)=0.

(59)

In order to solve the coupled system of equations (
53
) and (
IV
), we employ two approaches — one analytical and one numerical — both based on an appropriate approximation.
Since we are in the vicinity of the critical point, the scalar field can be considered negligibly small compared to the gauge field. Therefore, neglecting it in (
53
) renders an analytically solvable equation. Its solution, subject to the appropriate boundary conditions, is given by

A_{t}(z)=\frac{\rho}{r_{h}^{2}}(1-z^{2}).

(60)

Now we tackle the equation for
Z(z)
using analytical and numerical methods.

IV.1
Analytical method

Here we employ the analytical method developed in

Gregory et al. 2009
;
Pan et al. 2010
;
Pan and Wang 2010
;
Liu et al. 2010
;
Ge et al. 2010
,
to determine the temperature dependence of the critical magnetic field. First, we expand
Z(z)
both near the asymptotic boundary (
z=0
),

\displaystyle Z(z)=c_{1}z+c_{3}z^{3}+\mathcal{O}(z^{4}),

(61)

and near the horizon (
z=1
),

\displaystyle Z(z)

\displaystyle=Z(1)-Z^{\prime}(1)(1-z)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}Z^{\prime\prime}(1)(1-z)^{2}+\mathcal{O}((1-z)^{3}).

(62)

Substituting
62
in equation (
IV
) and expanding around the horizon yields

Z^{\prime}(1)=-\frac{\left(B+B^{2}{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}-3\alpha^{2}\right)Z(1)}{4\alpha^{2}},

(63)

together with

\displaystyle Z^{\prime\prime}(1)=\frac{24BZ(1)+24B^{2}{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}Z(1)-168\alpha^{2}Z(1)}{128\alpha^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{36\left(B+B^{2}{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}-3\alpha^{2}\right)Z(1)+\frac{4B\left(B+B^{2}{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}-3\alpha^{2}\right)Z(1)}{\alpha^{2}}}{128\alpha^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\frac{4B^{2}{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}\left(B+B^{2}{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}-3\alpha^{2}\right)Z(1)}{\alpha^{2}}-4Z(1)\left(A_{t}^{\prime}(1)\right)^{2}}{128\alpha^{2}}.

(64)

Thus, the coefficients in the near-horizon expansion are fully determined by
Z(1)
. Near the asymptotic boundary, we impose
c_{1}=0
, corresponding to the standard quantization procedure.

The central idea of this approximate analytical method is to smoothly match the near-horizon and near-boundary expansions at an intermediate point, chosen as
z_{m}=\frac{1}{2}
. This leads to a system of equations relating
Z(1)
,
c_{3}
, and
A_{t}^{\prime}(1)
. Eliminating
Z(1)
and
c_{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 10B^{3}{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}+5B^{4}{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}^{2}+98B\alpha^{2}+309\alpha^{4}

\displaystyle+B^{2}\left(5+98{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}\alpha^{2}\right)-5\alpha^{2}\left(A_{t}^{\prime}(1)\right)^{2}=0.

(65)

Solving this equation for
B
, selecting the positive root, and expanding to the first order in
{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}
, we find the critical magnetic field,

\displaystyle B_{c}=\frac{1}{5}\left(-49\alpha^{2}+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}\left(856\alpha^{2}+25\left(A_{t}^{\prime}(1)\right)^{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{25}\left(-49\alpha^{2}+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}\left(856\alpha^{2}+25\left(A_{t}^{\prime}(1)\right)^{2}\right)}\right)^{2}{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}.

(66)

Using
\alpha=\pi T

A_{t}^{\prime}(1)=\frac{2\rho}{r_{h}^{2}}
, and the critical temperature obtained from the weak magnetic field analysis conducted in
Gregory et al. 2009

T_{c}=\left(\frac{2\rho}{L}\sqrt{\frac{5}{309}}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\frac{1}{\pi L},

(67)

we arrive at the temperature-dependence of the critical magnetic field including the NC correction,

\displaystyle B_{c}=\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{5}\left(-49+\sqrt{856+1545\left(\frac{T_{c}}{T}\right)^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle-\left[\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{5}\left(-49+\sqrt{856+1545\left(\frac{T_{c}}{T}\right)^{6}}\right)\right]^{2}{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}.

(68)

Note that this result can be written as

B_{c}=B_{c}^{(0)}\left(1-B_{c}^{(0)}{\mathchar 22\mkern-9.0muk}\right),

(69)

where

B_{c}^{(0)}=\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{5}\left(-49+\sqrt{856+1545\left(\frac{T_{c}}{T}\right)^{6}}\right)

(70)

represents the temperature dependence of the critical magnetic field in the undeformed theory.

The classical result for

coincides exactly with the result obtained in
Ge et al. 2010
in the pure Einstein–Hilbert limit (i.e., in the absence of the Gauss–Bonnet term), which confirms the consistency of our analysis. Figure

shows us that, due to the presence of the NC deformation, the critical magnetic field is reduced, implying that the NC twist effectively enhances the magnetic field. It is interesting to note that (

from

V
Conclusion and Outlook

In this paper, we initiated a systematic study of the role of NC gauge field theory in a holographic description of condensed matter systems, in particular, holographic superconductors. We focused on
(2+1)
- and
(3+1)
-dimensional models of holographic superconductors (or, more accurately, charged superfluids) with established gravitational bulk actions. Working in the probe limit, we applied a NC twist deformation of the bulk fields and promoted the classical bulk action into a NC action invariant under deformed gauge transformations. We used a special kind of twist adapted to the isometries of the background black hole bulk geometry that only affects the gauge-matter sector, leaving the metric intact. The goal was to see how this Killing twist transformation in the bulk is reflected in the dual boundary system through phase-transition parameters such as the critical magnetic field. Using mainly numerical methods for solving the relevant field equations, we showed that critical behavior indeed depends on the parameter of noncommutativity, e.g. the NC parameter can reduce the value of the critical magnetic field. As can be seen from the numerical results obtained, the modification of the critical relations shows a clear sign of a continuous deformation depending on the strength of the NC parameter.

It should be noted that, in this setup, the boundary system is to be regarded as a model of some potentially realistic superconductor, while the gravitational dual amounts to just an alternative formal description. In that sense, the use of a NC field theory is simply an expansion of the holographic vocabulary, providing additional structure that might capture some relevant aspects of the physics of superconductors and related condensed matter systems in the holographic setting. From this point of view, NC gauge field theory need not be regarded as a physical theory, merely as a part of the holographic language, augmenting the dual gravitational description of lower-dimensional strongly correlated systems.
In this paper, we used a very simple class of twist deformations that introduces only NC gauge and matter fields, but does not affect the bulk geometry. This was only a first step, setting the stage for a more general investigation of the full capacity of twist deformations as a tool in AdS/CMT. Further investigation could involve bulk geometries with NC-deformed metric, and even going into nonperturbative regime of NC field theory/gravity.
An ultimate goal is to identify a concrete physical aspect of some CMT system that has a characteristic dual description in terms of NC bulk fields, including the metric.

Apart from these general considerations regarding the role of NC field theory, we also point out that both bulk models we discussed assume the probe limit. In the
(2+1)
-dimensional model, both the metric and the gauge field constitute a self-consistent Einstein-Maxwell background on which the scalar propagates. In the
(3+1)
-dimensional bulk model, only geometry is fixed while the gauge field and the scalar are both dynamical. Holographic models with dynamical gravity have been studied in the context of AdS/CMT, but the addition of a NC deformation presents a highly non-trivial complication. This interesting but challenging task demands systematic further investigation.

Finally, let us mention that, although there are several papers concerning holographic superconductors that introduce a NC scale to modify the bulk
Pramanik et al. 2014
;
Ghorai and Gangopadhyay 2016
;
Pramanik and Ghosh 2015
;
Gangopadhyay et al. 2018
;
Paul and Gangopadhyay 2025
, their approach is essentially phenomenological, based on smearing the sources (mass and charge distributions of a black hole) with the NC scale as a kind of screening parameter,
and not on a fundamental introduction of a NC-deformed algebra at the field-theory level. However, it is interesting to note that
Pramanik and Ghosh 2015
also observed a reduction of the critical magnetic field due to the presence of the NC scale.
