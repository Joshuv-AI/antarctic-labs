A Mesoscopic Partition Function for Equilibrium Statistical Mechanics
B. Osano

Abstract
We develop a mesoscopic formulation of equilibrium statistical mechanics based on coarse-grained occupation-number sectors of one-particle phase space. A mesoscopic partition function is constructed by averaging the microscopic Hamiltonian over configurations compatible with a given occupation profile. The construction converges to the canonical Gibbs partition function in the fine-graining limit and remains compatible with interacting many-body systems. Within this framework, thermodynamic extensivity is shown to be equivalent to asymptotic factorisation of the mesoscopic partition function, while residual inter-cell correlations generate subextensive corrections. The resulting formalism provides a mathematically consistent bridge between microscopic Gibbs theory and mesoscopic thermodynamics.



I
Introduction

The relationship between microscopic dynamics and macroscopic thermodynamic behaviour has been a central problem since the foundational work of Boltzmann
and Gibbs
boltzmann1872
;
gibbs1902
. A major achievement in this direction is kinetic theory, in which the Boltzmann equation provides a
systematic route from particle dynamics to macroscopic transport laws, recovering continuum descriptions such as the Navier–Stokes equations in
appropriate limits
chapman1970
;
landau1980
. More broadly, coarse-graining and scaling approaches—developed notably by Kadanoff and
Wilson—have clarified how macroscopic, fluid-like behaviour emerges from microscopic interactions
kadanoff1966
;
wilson1971
. Extensions to
nonequilibrium systems, particularly through the work of Onsager and Prigogine, further highlight the interplay between microscopic reversibility
and macroscopic irreversibility
onsager1931
;
prigogine1967
.

Despite these advances, classical thermodynamics remains fundamentally a theory of macroscopic systems. Its formulation relies on key assumptions,
including extensivity and weak coupling between a system and its environment, which ensure that bulk properties dominate over boundary effects and allow for
a consistent definition of thermodynamic variables
sekimoto2010
. When these assumptions are violated—as in small or mesoscopic systems—standard thermodynamic relations require modification. In particular, extensivity may fail, and interaction or surface contributions become significant, invalidating
the weak-coupling approximation
hill1962
;
sekimoto2010
. Hill’s nanothermodynamics
hill1962
and its extension
hill1999

provide a systematic extension of thermodynamic relations to such systems by introducing additional degrees of freedom and modified thermodynamic
potentials, while stochastic thermodynamics generalises these ideas to nonequilibrium regimes characterised by fluctuations and strong
system–environment interactions
seifert2012
;
jarzynski1997
. In these settings, even the distinction between heat and work becomes ambiguous, especially under strong coupling
talkner2016
;
talkner2020
. The complexity increases if magnetic fields are included: magnetically induced interactions
osano2017
change how macroscopic approximations emerge from microscopic considerations. The situation is equally unclear when considering the fluid limit of two interacting fluids
osano2020a
;
osano2020b
;
osano2019
.

Moreover, in nonequilibrium systems, thermodynamic behaviour is often only locally valid: subsystems that admit an effective thermodynamic description
may themselves be small due to strong gradients or interactions
ottinger2023
. This underscores the need for a framework
that systematically connects microscopic structure to mesoscopic and macroscopic thermodynamic behaviour. The present construction is intended as a mesoscopic coarse-grained formulation of equilibrium statistical mechanics. The convergence arguments are therefore understood in the standard asymptotic sense commonly employed in statistical mechanics and coarse-graining theory, rather than as fully rigorous measure-theoretic proofs.

II
A Mathematical Formulation

In the classical Gibbs formulation of statistical mechanics, the canonical partition function is defined directly on the full
N
-particle phase space

\Gamma^{N}.

Macroscopic thermodynamic quantities are then obtained from the microscopic Hamiltonian through phase-space integration
gibbs1902
;
Huang1987
;
Pathria2011
. While this framework is exact, it does not explicitly encode a mesoscopic level of description in which coarse-grained occupation variables emerge dynamically from the microscopic degrees of freedom.

A natural mesoscopic description should satisfy three requirements:

1.

It should retain the microscopic Hamiltonian structure.

2.

It should introduce coarse-grained variables that describe particle populations in mesoscopic phase-space cells.

3.

It should recover the canonical Gibbs ensemble in the fine-graining limit.

To achieve this, we construct a coarse-grained description based on partitions of the one-particle phase space

\Gamma=\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1)

Unlike the standard Gibbs formulation, the mesoscopic variables are not microscopic coordinates themselves, but occupation numbers associated with finite phase-space cells. The resulting framework is therefore intermediate between microscopic statistical mechanics and macroscopic thermodynamics.

The construction is closely related to coarse-graining procedures appearing in kinetic theory, lattice gases, renormalisation-group methods, and local-equilibrium formulations of nonequilibrium statistical mechanics
kadanoff1966
;
wilson1971
;
zwanzig1961
;
deGroot1984
. However, rather than introducing local equilibrium phenomenologically, we define a mesoscopic partition function directly from occupation-number sectors of phase space.

The key idea is to partition the one-particle phase space into cells

\Gamma=\bigcup_{\alpha}\Omega_{\alpha},

and to associate with each microscopic configuration

\gamma=(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})\in\Gamma^{N}

an occupation profile
\{n_{\alpha}\},
where
n_{\alpha}
counts the number of particles occupying the cell
\Omega_{\alpha}.

The mesoscopic free energy then emerges from a statistical sum over occupation sectors, with each sector weighted by the average microscopic energy compatible with the corresponding occupation profile.

This formulation preserves the microscopic interaction structure while providing a mathematically consistent coarse-grained description. In particular, the multinomial combinatorics associated with occupation numbers is naturally compatible with the phase-space partition, since the cells act on the one-particle phase space rather than on the full
N
-particle configuration space. The resulting framework is therefore consistent with the probabilistic structure underlying classical equilibrium statistical mechanics
ruelle1969
;
georgii1988
;
Huang1987
;
Pathria2011
.

II.1
Cell Structure and Mesoscopic State

The standard canonical partition function for a system of
N
classical particles in a domain
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with Hamiltonian

H_{N}:\Gamma^{N}\to\mathbb{R},

(2)

is

Z_{N}(\Lambda,\beta)=\frac{1}{N!}\int_{\Gamma^{N}}e^{-\beta H_{N}(\gamma)}\,d\gamma,

(3)

where

\gamma=(z_{1},\dots,z_{N}),

with

z_{k}=(q_{k},p_{k})\in\Gamma,

and

\Gamma=\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

is the one-particle phase space. To construct a mesoscopic description, we partition the one-particle phase space
\Gamma
into cells

\{\Omega_{\alpha}\},

with cell volumes

\varepsilon_{\alpha}:=|\Omega_{\alpha}|.

The coarse-graining scale
\ell
is assumed to satisfy

\xi\ll\ell\ll L,

(4)

where
\xi
is the correlation length and
L
is the macroscopic system size. The superscript
\ell
appearing in
Z_{N}^{(\ell)}
and related quantities throughout this paper refer to this coarse-graining scale; in particular, the dependence of the mesoscopic partition function on
\ell
encodes the resolution of the phase-space partition, with finer partitions corresponding to smaller
\ell
.

For a microscopic configuration

\gamma=(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})\in\Gamma^{N},

(5)

define the occupation numbers

n_{\alpha}(\gamma):=\sum_{k=1}^{N}1_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(z_{k}),

(6)

where
1_{\Omega_{\alpha}}
denotes the indicator function of the cell
\Omega_{\alpha}
. The occupation numbers satisfy the constraint

\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N.

(7)

We now elevate the description to the cell structure level. For a fixed occupation profile

\{n_{\alpha}\},
we define the compatible configuration

\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\}):=\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma^{N}:n_{\alpha}(\gamma)=n_{\alpha}\ \forall\alpha\right\},

(8)

such that

|\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})|:=\int_{\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})}d\gamma.

(9)

The corresponding coarse-grained Hamiltonian is therefore defined by

\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\}):=\frac{1}{\int_{\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})}d\gamma}\int_{\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})}H_{N}(\gamma)\,d\gamma,

(10)

where

\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\})
represents the mesoscopic energy obtained by averaging the microscopic Hamiltonian over all microstates compatible with the occupation profile.

II.2
The Mesoscopic Partition Function

For notational convenience, we write

\sum_{\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N}

(11)

to denote summation over all collections of nonnegative integers
\{n_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
satisfying
n_{\alpha}\geq 0,
and
\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N.

This allows us define a mesoscopic partition function

Z_{N}^{(\ell)}(\Lambda,\beta)=\sum_{\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N}\frac{1}{\prod_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}!}\left[\prod_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}\right]e^{-\beta\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\})},

(12)

where the global 1/N! is absorbed into the multinomial combinatorics.

The combinatorial structure has a direct interpretation:

•

The factor

\prod_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}

represents the phase-space volume associated with the occupation profile.

•

The factorial factor

\prod_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}!

accounts for the indistinguishability of particles occupying the same cell.

•

The Boltzmann weight

e^{-\beta\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\})}

assigns the coarse-grained energetic contribution of the occupation profile.

Theorem

[Convergence]
Assume that the Hamiltonian
H_{N}
is stable and tempered in the sense of Ruelle
ruelle1969
. Then, as

\max_{\alpha}\operatorname{diam}(\Omega_{\alpha})\to 0,

(13)

the mesoscopic partition function converges to the canonical partition function:

Z_{N}^{(\ell)}(\Lambda,\beta)\to Z_{N}(\Lambda,\beta),

(14)

with convergence uniform on compact subsets of
(\beta,\Lambda)
.

Proof:

Throughout the proof we fix
\beta>0
and write
f(\gamma):=e^{-\beta H_{N}(\gamma)}
for the Boltzmann weight.
All convergence statements are with respect to the product Lebesgue measure
d\gamma=dz_{1}\cdots dz_{N}
on
\Gamma^{N}
.

Step 1: Measurable partition of
\Gamma^{N}
into
occupation sectors.

Let
\Gamma=\bigcup_{\alpha}\Omega_{\alpha}
be a measurable
partition of the one-particle phase space into disjoint cells
satisfying
\Omega_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\beta}=\emptyset
for

\alpha\neq\beta
. For a microscopic configuration

\gamma=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\in\Gamma^{N}
define occupation numbers

n_{\alpha}(\gamma):=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(z_{k}).

(15)

The occupation sectors

\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\}):=\bigl\{\gamma\in\Gamma^{N}:n_{\alpha}(\gamma)=n_{\alpha}\;\forall\,\alpha\bigr\}

(16)

form a measurable partition of
\Gamma^{N}
, so that

\Gamma^{N}=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{n_{\alpha}\}\,:\\
\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N\end{subarray}}\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\}).

(17)

Consequently, the canonical partition function decomposes as

Z_{N}(\Lambda,\beta)=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{n_{\alpha}\}\,:\\
\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})}f(\gamma)\,d\gamma.

(18)

Step 2: Integrability and domination of the
Boltzmann weight.

Since
H_{N}
is
stable
in the sense of Ruelle
ruelle1969
,
there exists
B>0
such that

H_{N}(\gamma)\geq-BN\qquad\forall\,\gamma\in\Gamma^{N},

(19)

whence
f(\gamma)\leq e^{\beta BN}
uniformly.
Since
H_{N}
is
tempered
, the interaction decays sufficiently
rapidly at large separations to ensure that
f\in L^{1}(\Gamma^{N})
, so that
Z_{N}(\Lambda,\beta)<\infty
.
The uniform upper bound
e^{\beta BN}
serves as the dominating
function required for the application of the Dominated Convergence
Theorem below.

Step 3: Sector-wise approximation of the Boltzmann
weight.

Fix an occupation profile
\{n_{\alpha}\}
with

\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N
. For
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})
,
particle
k
lies in the cell
\Omega_{\alpha(k)}
determined by
its assignment to the profile.
Define the
coarse-grained configuration

\bar{Z}_{N}^{(\ell)}(\{n_{\alpha}\})
by replacing each particle
coordinate
z_{k}
with an arbitrary representative

\bar{z}_{\alpha(k)}\in\Omega_{\alpha(k)}
. The coarse-grained
Hamiltonian is

\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\}):=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})|}\,\int_{\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})}H_{N}(\gamma)\,d\gamma,

(20)

so that
e^{-\beta\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\})}
is the sector average of

f
.

For each sector, define the
oscillation of the Boltzmann weight

\omega_{\ell}(\{n_{\alpha}\}):=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})}\bigl|f(\gamma)-f(\gamma^{\prime})\bigr|.

(21)

We claim that, as
\ell:=\max_{\alpha}\operatorname{diam}(\Omega_{\alpha})\to 0
,

\omega_{\ell}(\{n_{\alpha}\})\to 0\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }\gamma\in\Gamma^{N}.

(22)

Justification of (
22
).
For
stable and tempered
pair potentials, the interaction
\phi(r)
may be singular at
r=0
(e.g. hard-core or Lennard-Jones), but by stability (
19
) the set of configurations for which any two particles approach within a distance
\delta
has Lebesgue measure that vanishes with
\delta
.
More precisely, the set

S_{\delta}:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma^{N}:\exists\,k\neq j,\;|q_{k}-q_{j}|<\delta\}

(23)

satisfies
|S_{\delta}|\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
.

Outside
S_{\delta}
, the potential
\phi
is bounded and
Lipschitz on the relevant domain, so
H_{N}
(and therefore
f
)
is uniformly continuous. Specifically, for

\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})\setminus S_{\delta}
, we have

|H_{N}(\gamma)-H_{N}(\gamma^{\prime})|\leq L_{\delta}\max_{k}|z_{k}-z_{k}^{\prime}|\leq L_{\delta}\cdot\ell,

(24)

where
L_{\delta}
is the Lipschitz constant of
H_{N}
outside

S_{\delta}
, and the last inequality uses the fact that, within a
single occupation sector of mesh
\ell
, particles in the same cell
are displaced by at most
\ell
. By the mean-value inequality for

e^{-\beta\,\cdot}
,

|f(\gamma)-f(\gamma^{\prime})|\leq\beta e^{\beta BN}L_{\delta}\cdot\ell.

(25)

Hence
\omega_{\ell}(\{n_{\alpha}\})\to 0
as
\ell\to 0
uniformly
on
\Gamma^{N}\setminus S_{\delta}
, for each fixed
\delta>0
.
Since
\delta>0
is arbitrary and
|S_{\delta}|\to 0
, the claim
(
22
) follows for a.e.
\gamma
.

Step 4: Sector integral approximation.

From Step 3, for a.e.
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})
,

f(\gamma)=e^{-\beta\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\})}+o(1)\quad\text{as }\ell\to 0,

(26)

where the
o(1)
term is bounded uniformly by
2e^{\beta BN}

(from Step 2). Integrating over
\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})
,

\int_{\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})}f(\gamma)\,d\gamma=|\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})|\,e^{-\beta\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\})}+o\!\left(|\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})|\right).

(27)

Step 5: Sector volume.

The occupation sector
\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})
consists of all

N
-tuples in which exactly
n_{\alpha}
particles lie in
\Omega_{\alpha}

for each
\alpha
. Since the cells tile
\Gamma
exactly (disjoint
union), the particles in each cell are independently and uniformly
distributed within that cell. A direct combinatorial computation
gives

|\mathcal{C}(\{n_{\alpha}\})|=\frac{N!}{\prod_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}!}\prod_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}},\qquad\varepsilon_{\alpha}:=|\Omega_{\alpha}|.

(28)

This identity follows from the multinomial expansion of

\left(\sum_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\right)^{N}=|\Gamma|^{N}

restricted to the constraint
\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N
.

Step 6: Reassembly and dominated convergence.

Substituting (
27
) and (
28
) into (
18
),

\displaystyle Z_{N}(\Lambda,\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N}\frac{N!}{\prod_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}!}\left[\prod_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}\right]e^{-\beta\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\})}+o(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N}\frac{1}{\prod_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}!}\left[\prod_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}\right]e^{-\beta\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\})}+o(1)

\displaystyle=Z_{N}^{(\ell)}(\Lambda,\beta)+o(1).

(29)

The interchange of limit and sum is justified by dominated convergence: the summand is bounded above by

\frac{1}{\prod_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}!}\prod_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}\cdot e^{\beta BN},

(30)

and the sum of these bounds over all occupation profiles equals

\frac{|\Gamma|^{N}}{N!}\,e^{\beta BN}<\infty
,
which is the dominating integrable function.

Hence

\lim_{\ell\to 0}Z_{N}^{(\ell)}(\Lambda,\beta)=Z_{N}(\Lambda,\beta).

(31)

The same domination estimate is uniform in
(\beta,\Lambda)
on
compact subsets, yielding uniform convergence on compact subsets
of
(\beta,\Lambda)
.

III
The Mesoscopic Partition Function and Extensivity

We now establish the relationship between factorisation of the mesoscopic partition function and extensivity of the coarse-grained free energy.
The mesoscopic partition function is

Z_{N}^{(\ell)}(\Lambda,\beta)=\sum_{\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N}\frac{1}{\prod_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}!}\prod_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}e^{-\beta\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\})},

(32)

where

\varepsilon_{\alpha}:=|\Omega_{\alpha}|

and

\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\})

is the coarse-grained Hamiltonian associated with the occupation profile.
The corresponding free energy is

F^{(\ell)}(N,\Lambda,T):=-k_{B}T\log Z_{N}^{(\ell)}(\Lambda,\beta).

(33)

III.1
Factorisation and Extensivity

Suppose that the coarse-grained Hamiltonian admits the decomposition

\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\})=\sum_{\alpha}\bar{H}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha})+R(\{n_{\alpha}\}),

(34)

where

R(\{n_{\alpha}\})

(35)

contains the inter-cell correlations.
Then the partition function becomes

Z_{N}^{(\ell)}=\sum_{\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N}\frac{1}{\prod_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}!}\prod_{\alpha}\left(\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}e^{-\beta\bar{H}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha})}\right)e^{-\beta R(\{n_{\alpha}\})}.

(36)

If

R(\{n_{\alpha}\})=o(N)

(37)

in the thermodynamic limit at fixed density, then

e^{-\beta R(\{n_{\alpha}\})}=e^{o(N)}.

(38)

Consequently,

Z_{N}^{(\ell)}=e^{o(N)}\sum_{\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N}\frac{1}{\prod_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}!}\prod_{\alpha}\left(\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}e^{-\beta\bar{H}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha})}\right).

(39)

Define the cell partition functions

Z_{\alpha}^{(\ell)}(n_{\alpha},\beta):=\frac{\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}}{n_{\alpha}!}e^{-\beta\bar{H}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha})}.

(40)

Then

Z_{N}^{(\ell)}=e^{o(N)}\sum_{\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N}\prod_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}^{(\ell)}(n_{\alpha},\beta).

(41)

We evaluate the constrained sum in equation
41
asymptotically in the thermodynamic limit
N\to\infty
at fixed density

\varrho=N/V
.
Write the summand in the form

\prod_{\alpha}Z^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha},\beta)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{\alpha}\log Z^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha},\beta)\right),

(42)

so that the constrained sum becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{n_{\alpha}\}\,:\\
\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N\end{subarray}}\prod_{\alpha}Z^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha},\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{n_{\alpha}\}\,:\\
\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N\end{subarray}}\exp\!\Bigl(\Phi(\{n_{\alpha}\})\Bigr),

\displaystyle\Phi(\{n_{\alpha}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\log Z^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha},\beta).

(43)

To justify a saddle-point evaluation of this sum, we impose the following regularity condition on the cell partition functions.

Assumption:

For each cell
\alpha
, the function
n_{\alpha}\mapsto\log Z^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha},\beta)
is strictly concave in

n_{\alpha}
for
n_{\alpha}\geq 1
.

Strict concavity of each summand implies that
\Phi
is strictly concave on the constraint hypersurface

\{\{n_{\alpha}\}:\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N,\;n_{\alpha}\geq 0\}
,
so there exists a unique maximiser
\{\bar{n}_{\alpha}\}
, determined
by the first-order conditions

\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{\alpha}}\log Z^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha},\beta)\bigg|_{n_{\alpha}=\bar{n}_{\alpha}}=\mu^{*}\qquad\forall\,\alpha,

(44)

where
\mu^{*}
is a Lagrange multiplier enforcing

\sum_{\alpha}\bar{n}_{\alpha}=N
.
This condition is the mesoscopic analogue of the chemical-potential equalisation condition in the grand canonical ensemble.

We now apply the saddle-point (Laplace) approximation to the
sum (
III.1
).
Expand
\Phi
to second order around
\{\bar{n}_{\alpha}\}
:

\displaystyle\Phi(\{n_{\alpha}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(\{\bar{n}_{\alpha}\})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(\bar{n}_{\alpha})(n_{\alpha}-\bar{n}_{\alpha})^{2}

(45)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle O\!\left(\sum_{\alpha}|n_{\alpha}-\bar{n}_{\alpha}|^{3}\right),

where

\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(\bar{n}_{\alpha}):=\frac{d^{2}}{dn_{\alpha}^{2}}\log Z^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha},\beta)\big|_{n_{\alpha}=\bar{n}_{\alpha}}<0

by Assumption
III.1
.
Substituting into (
III.1
) and approximating
the constrained sum by a Gaussian integral over the fluctuations

\delta n_{\alpha}:=n_{\alpha}-\bar{n}_{\alpha}
subject to

\sum_{\alpha}\delta n_{\alpha}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{n_{\alpha}\}\,:\\
\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N\end{subarray}}\prod_{\alpha}Z^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha},\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\Phi(\{\bar{n}_{\alpha}\})}\cdot\left[\frac{(2\pi)^{(M-1)/2}}{\left(\prod_{\alpha}|\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(\bar{n}_{\alpha})|\right)^{1/2}\cdot\Delta_{M}^{1/2}}\right]

(46)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle O(N^{-1/2}),

where
M
denotes the number of cells and
\Delta_{M}
is the determinant of the constraint-projected Hessian
debruyne2020
.
Taking logarithms,

\displaystyle\log\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{n_{\alpha}\}\,:\\
\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}=N\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\prod_{\alpha}Z^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha},\beta)=\Phi(\{\bar{n}_{\alpha}\})+O(\log N)

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\log Z^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(\bar{n}_{\alpha},\beta)+O(\log N).

Since
\Phi(\{\bar{n}_{\alpha}\})=O(N)
while the prefactor
contributes only
O(\log N)
, combining with equation (
41
) gives

\log Z^{(\ell)}_{N}=\sum_{\alpha}\log Z^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(\bar{n}_{\alpha},\beta)+o(N),

(48)

where
\{\bar{n}_{\alpha}\}
is the unique dominant occupation profile
of equation (
44
).
Multiplying through by
-k_{B}T
yields

F^{(\ell)}(N,\Lambda,T)=\sum_{\alpha}F^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(\bar{n}_{\alpha},T)+o(N),

(49)

where
F^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha},T):=-k_{B}T\log Z^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha},\beta)
.
Thus, asymptotic factorisation of the mesoscopic partition function
implies extensivity of the coarse-grained free energy.

Remark:
The assumption holds whenever
Z^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha},\beta)
is log-convex as a function of
n_{\alpha}
, which is guaranteed for non-interacting or weakly interacting cells by the standard theory of cumulant generating functions. For interacting cells, the condition must be verified for the specific interaction model; it is equivalent to the
requirement that the isothermal compressibility of the cell is positive, a standard thermodynamic stability condition.

III.2
Physical Conditions for Factorisation

The factorisation property follows from the decay of correlations between distinct phase-space cells.
Assume:

1.

The Hamiltonian
H_{N}
is stable and tempered in the sense of Ruelle.

2.

Correlations decay sufficiently rapidly:

\langle A_{\alpha}A_{\gamma}\rangle-\langle A_{\alpha}\rangle\langle A_{\gamma}\rangle\to 0

(50)

as
\operatorname{dist}(\Omega_{\alpha},\Omega_{\gamma})\to\infty.

3.

The coarse-graining scale satisfies
\xi\ll\ell,

where
\xi
is the correlation length.

Under these conditions, occupation-number correlations satisfy

\operatorname{Cov}(n_{\alpha},n_{\gamma})\to 0

(51)

for

\alpha\neq\gamma.

Consequently, the induced probability measure on occupation profiles asymptotically factorises:

P(\{n_{\alpha}\})\approx\prod_{\alpha}P_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha}).

(52)

Therefore, the inter-cell correction satisfies

R(\{n_{\alpha}\})=o(N),

(53)

yielding the asymptotic factorisation.

III.3
Extensivity and Asymptotic Factorisation

Suppose now that the free energy satisfies

F^{(\ell)}(N,\Lambda,T)=\sum_{\alpha}F_{\alpha}^{(\ell)}(\bar{n}_{\alpha},T)+o(N).

(54)

Using

F^{(\ell)}=-k_{B}T\log Z_{N}^{(\ell)},

(55)

we obtain

\log Z_{N}^{(\ell)}=\sum_{\alpha}\log Z_{\alpha}^{(\ell)}(\bar{n}_{\alpha},\beta)+o(N),

(56)

and expressing in exponential form gives

Z_{N}^{(\ell)}=\left[\prod_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}^{(\ell)}(\bar{n}_{\alpha},\beta)\right]e^{o(N)}.

(57)

Thus, extensivity is consistent with the asymptotic factorisation property described in equation (
52
) of the mesoscopic partition function. Conversely, asymptotic factorisation directly implies extensivity.

III.4
The Coarse-Graining Defect

When inter-cell correlations remain significant, the correction
term
R(\{n_{\alpha}\})
does not vanish in the thermodynamic limit.
Define the defect functional

\Delta^{(\ell)}:=\bar{H}(\{n_{\alpha}\})-\sum_{\alpha}\bar{H}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha}).

(58)

Then the free energy becomes

F^{(\ell)}(N,\Lambda,T)=\sum_{\alpha}F^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(N,\Lambda,T)+\Delta^{(\ell)}+o(N).

(59)

Thus, non-extensive contributions are controlled directly by the
residual inter-cell correlations encoded in
\Delta^{(\ell)}
.

We now analyse the scaling of
\Delta^{(\ell)}
in the thermodynamic
limit for short-range interactions.

Structure of the defect.

The defect
\Delta^{(\ell)}
receives contributions only from
particle pairs
(k,j)
whose coordinates
z_{k}
and
z_{j}
lie in

different
cells. To see this, write the microscopic
Hamiltonian as

\mathcal{H}_{N}(\Gamma)=\sum_{k}V_{1}(z_{k})+\sum_{k<j}\phi(|q_{k}-q_{j}|),

(60)

where
V_{1}
is a one-body potential and
\phi
is a pair potential.
The sector average
\bar{\mathcal{H}}(\{n_{\alpha}\})
decomposes as

\bar{\mathcal{H}}(\{n_{\alpha}\})=\underbrace{\sum_{\alpha}\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha})}_{\text{intra-cell}}+\underbrace{\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\bar{\phi}_{\alpha\beta}(n_{\alpha},n_{\beta})}_{\text{inter-cell}},

(61)

where
\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}(n_{\alpha})
collects the one-body terms and
intra-cell pair interactions for cell
\Omega_{\alpha}
, and

\bar{\phi}_{\alpha\beta}
is the sector-averaged inter-cell pair
energy between cells
\Omega_{\alpha}
and
\Omega_{\beta}
.
Consequently,

\Delta^{(\ell)}=\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\bar{\phi}_{\alpha\beta}(n_{\alpha},n_{\beta}).

(62)

The defect is therefore entirely determined by inter-cell pair interactions. For two- or higher- body cases see
osano-inprep
.

Scaling for short-range interactions.

Assume that the pair potential
\phi
has finite range
r_{0}
, meaning

\phi(r)=0
for
r>r_{0}
. Under the coarse-graining condition

\ell\gg\xi
of equation (
4
), only cells
\Omega_{\alpha}
and

\Omega_{\beta}
that are
adjacent
(i.e.
\mathrm{dist}(\Omega_{\alpha},\Omega_{\beta})\leq r_{0}
) contribute
non-trivially to the sum in equation (
62
).
Interior cells have
O(\ell^{d})
volume and contribute to

\bar{\phi}_{\alpha\beta}
only through their shared boundary layer
of thickness
r_{0}
. The number of interior cell pairs with
non-vanishing inter-cell interaction is therefore proportional to
the total surface area of the partition, which scales as
V^{(d-1)/d}

for a domain of volume
V
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

More precisely, partitioning
\Lambda
into
M\sim V/\ell^{d}
cells
of side
\ell
, the number of adjacent cell pairs is
O(M^{(d-1)/d})\sim V^{(d-1)/d}/\ell^{d-1}
, and each contributes an inter-cell
energy of order
n_{\alpha}n_{\beta}\|\phi\|_{\infty}\ell^{d-1}r_{0}
.
Summing over adjacent pairs and using
n_{\alpha}\sim\varrho\ell^{d}
,

\Delta^{(\ell)}\sim\varrho^{2}\|\phi\|_{\infty}r_{0}\,V^{(d-1)/d}.

(63)

Hence the defect scales as a surface term:

\frac{\Delta^{(\ell)}}{V}\sim V^{-1/d}\to 0\qquad\text{as }V\to\infty,

(64)

confirming that
\Delta^{(\ell)}=o(N)
in the thermodynamic limit,
consistent with the factorisation condition of Section 3.1.

Subextensive corrections to the free energy.

From equation (
63
), the leading correction to
the extensive free energy is

F^{(\ell)}(N,\Lambda,T)=Nf(\varrho,T)+c(\varrho,T,\ell)\,V^{(d-1)/d}+o\!\left(V^{(d-1)/d}\right),

(65)

where
f(\varrho,T)
is the bulk free energy density and

c(\varrho,T,\ell)
is a surface coefficient depending on the
density, temperature, and coarse-graining scale. The correction

\Sigma^{(\ell)}:=c(\varrho,T,\ell)\,V^{(d-1)/d}
is precisely
the subextensive term appearing in the generalised Euler relation
of Section 4, and satisfies

\frac{\Sigma^{(\ell)}}{V}\to 0\qquad\text{as }V\to\infty,

(66)

recovering the standard Euler relation in the thermodynamic limit.
For long-range interactions (e.g. Coulomb or gravitational
potentials), equation (
63
) no longer holds
and the defect may scale faster than
V^{(d-1)/d}
; the analysis
of such cases is left for future work.

IV
The Generalised Euler Relation

We now derive a generalised Euler relation from the free-energy structure established in equations (
49
) and (
58
).

Derivation of the bulk-plus-surface decomposition.

From Section (
III
), the free energy satisfies

F^{(\ell)}(N,\Lambda,T)=\sum_{\alpha}F^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(\bar{n}_{\alpha},T)+o(N),

(67)

where the sum runs over the dominant occupation profile

\{\bar{n}_{\alpha}\}
.
In the thermodynamic limit at fixed density
\varrho=N/V
, the
dominant profile satisfies
\bar{n}_{\alpha}\approx\varrho\,|\Omega_{\alpha}|
for each cell
\alpha
.
The intra-cell free energies
F^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}
are therefore
functions of
\varrho
,
T
, and the cell volume
|\Omega_{\alpha}|

only.
Summing over cells and passing to the continuum limit in cell
volume, the leading term in equation (
67
) is
extensive:

\sum_{\alpha}F^{(\ell)}_{\alpha}(\bar{n}_{\alpha},T)=Nf(\varrho,T)+o(N),

(68)

where
f(\varrho,T)
is the bulk free energy per particle,
determined by the intra-cell partition functions.

When inter-cell correlations are non-negligible, the full free energy
receives an additional contribution from the coarse-graining defect

\Delta^{(\ell)}
defined in equation (
58
) of
Section 3.4:

F^{(\ell)}(N,V,T)=Nf(\varrho,T)+\Sigma^{(\ell)}(N,V,T)+o(N),

(69)

where we identify

\Sigma^{(\ell)}(N,V,T):=-k_{B}T\,\Delta^{(\ell)},

(70)

with
\Delta^{(\ell)}
the defect functional of
equation (
58
).
The correction
\Sigma^{(\ell)}
therefore encodes precisely the
inter-cell correlation energy that is not captured by the
factorised intra-cell contributions.
For short-range interactions, the analysis of Section 3.4 gives

\Sigma^{(\ell)}\sim V^{(d-1)/d},

(71)

since the defect receives contributions only from adjacent cell
pairs and the number of such pairs scales as the total boundary
area of the partition.

The Euler relation.

Since
Nf(\varrho,T)
is homogeneous of degree one in
(N,V)
,
Euler’s theorem gives

N\frac{\partial(Nf)}{\partial N}+V\frac{\partial(Nf)}{\partial V}=Nf.

(72)

Using the standard thermodynamic identities applied to the full
free energy
F^{(\ell)}
,

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial F^{(\ell)}}{\partial V},

(73)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}\frac{\partial F^{(\ell)}}{\partial N},

(74)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial F^{(\ell)}}{\partial T},

(75)

together with the definition
U=F^{(\ell)}+TS
, we obtain

U=TS-PV+\mu N+\Sigma^{(\ell)}.

(76)

This is the generalised Euler relation for the mesoscopic system.
The correction term
\Sigma^{(\ell)}
is the thermodynamic
signature of inter-cell correlations.

Recovery of the standard Euler relation.

From equation (
71
),

\frac{\Sigma^{(\ell)}}{V}\sim V^{-1/d}\to 0\qquad\text{as }V\to\infty,

(77)

so
\Sigma^{(\ell)}=o(V)=o(N)
in the thermodynamic limit.
Equation (
76
) therefore recovers the
standard Euler relation

U=TS-PV+\mu N

(78)

in the thermodynamic limit, as required for an extensive system.
For long-range interactions the scaling (
71
)
need not hold, and the correction
\Sigma^{(\ell)}
may persist
as a genuinely non-extensive contribution.

V
Conclusion

We have introduced a mesoscopic partition function based on coarse-grained occupation-number sectors of one-particle phase space. The construction provides a mathematically consistent bridge between microscopic statistical mechanics and mesoscopic thermodynamics.
The coarse-grained Hamiltonian is defined by averaging over microscopic configurations compatible with a given occupation profile. This avoids the incompatibility between multinomial occupation-number combinatorics and partitions of the full
N
-particle phase space.

The mesoscopic partition function converges to the canonical Gibbs partition function in the fine-graining limit. Within this framework, extensivity emerges when occupation-sector correlations become asymptotically negligible. Conversely, persistent inter-cell correlations generate non-extensive corrections encoded in the coarse-graining defect functional.
The resulting formulation provides a unified description of factorisation, extensivity, and correlation structure in mesoscopic statistical mechanics, while remaining compatible with interacting many-body Hamiltonians. The occupation-sector decomposition provides a natural mesoscopic bridge between microscopic phase-space dynamics and emergent thermodynamic structure.
