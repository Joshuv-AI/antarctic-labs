Rate-Distortion Theory for Deductive Sources under Closure Fidelity
Jianfeng Xu

Abstract
We study lossy compression of a finite statement source generated in a fixed deductive environment. The source symbols are statements in a knowledge base endowed with a proof system, and reconstruction fidelity is measured by preservation of deductive closure rather than by symbolwise equality. This induces, once the proof system and canonical order are fixed, a decomposition of the source into an irredundant core and redundant stored consequences. Under a natural disjointness condition on zero-distortion reconstruction sets, we show that the minimum zero-distortion rate equals the source mass of the core times the entropy of the source conditioned on that core. For reconstruction alphabets contained in the deductive closure of the source knowledge base, we further prove that the full rate–distortion function depends only on the core, so redundant states are invisible to both rate and distortion. When the decoder is limited to a bounded inference-depth budget (a bounded number of iterations of the immediate-consequence operator), we obtain an exact rate–depth–distortion characterization. Under an additional order-robustness assumption identifying the chosen core with the order-free essential set, this characterization interpolates between classical symbolwise compression and unconstrained deductive compression. These results formulate deductive compression as a structured source-coding problem and quantify how shared inference structure changes the fundamental limits of communication.



I
Introduction

Classical rate–distortion theory studies the minimum description rate required to represent a random source under a prescribed fidelity criterion
[
25
,
7
,
8
]
. In the standard formulation, the source alphabet is treated as a collection of symbols and distortion is imposed directly on source–reconstruction pairs. In many modern communication settings, however, the source is not naturally a flat alphabet but a finite knowledge base equipped with a shared proof system. In such settings, symbolwise reproduction can be unnecessarily stringent: if the receiver can reconstruct the same deductive consequences from a different stored representation, then the communication task has arguably been completed.

This observation suggests a source-coding problem in which fidelity is measured not by exact symbol reproduction but by preservation of deductive closure. We study this problem for a finite source set
S_{O}
endowed with a fixed proof system and its induced closure operator
\text{Cn}(\cdot)
. The resulting source model carries an intrinsic redundancy structure: some states are irredundant premises, while others are stored consequences that can be re-derived once those premises are known. Once the proof system and canonical order are fixed, this induces a
decomposition of the source into an irredundant core

A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
and a redundant part
J:=S_{O}\setminus A
. Under closure fidelity, redundant states need not be transmitted faithfully, so the effective source rate can be strictly smaller than the classical rate.

Operationally, the source studied here is not a random knowledge base
but a
statement-level source generated within a fixed deductive
environment
. A finite knowledge base
S_{O}
, together with its proof
system and closure operator, is fixed as part of the model, and the
random source symbol is a statement
S_{o}\sim P_{O}
drawn from that
environment. Thus a source block models a stream of statement reports,
updates, query answers, or semantic tokens produced relative to the
same local knowledge base. This is why the distortion may depend on
the ambient
S_{O}
while remaining a standard single-letter
distortion function.

semantic-communication and task-oriented communication literature,
including early conceptual proposals

Our formulation is narrower and more Shannon-theoretic: we fix a

Related work and positioning.

Two classical information-theoretic lines are especially relevant.
First, source coding with decoder side information and
computation-oriented compression—from Slepian–Wolf and Wyner–Ziv

connected to zero-error and graph-theoretic information theory.
Shannon’s zero-error framework introduced confusability as an

Our redundant/materialized split is closest to the materialized-view

Finally, our work is also adjacent to recent rate–distortion

deductive environment or exploit the proof-induced decomposition into
an irredundant core and redundant stored consequences that is central
to the present deductive-source model.

This paper asks three basic information-theoretic questions. First, what is the exact zero-distortion rate under closure fidelity? Second, how does the full rate–distortion function depend on the irredundant core? Third, how does this picture change when the decoder has only a limited
inference-depth budget (a limited number of iterations)? We answer these questions in a finite-alphabet setting. Our sharpest converse statements require an explicit disjointness assumption on zero-distortion reconstruction sets; we state this assumption up front because a central goal of the paper is to identify precisely where exact characterization is possible and where additional structure is still needed.

Our main message is that closure fidelity converts deductive redundancy into rate savings. In particular, the zero-distortion problem reduces to coding only the irredundant core, and the full rate–distortion function decomposes into a contribution from that core alone. We further show that when the decoder can perform at most
\delta

iterations of the immediate-consequence operator, the relevant effective source becomes a

\delta
-irredundant core. Under an additional order-robustness
assumption aligning the chosen core with the order-free essential
set, this yields an exact rate–depth–distortion interpolation
between classical symbolwise compression and unconstrained
deductive compression. Source–channel separation, closure-adapted converse bounds, and heterogeneous-receiver extensions then follow as consequences of this source-model characterization.

Main Contributions

•

A source-model formulation for deductive compression.

We formulate a finite-alphabet rate–distortion problem in which the source is a knowledge base endowed with a fixed proof system and the fidelity criterion is preservation of deductive closure. This shifts the emphasis from scheme-specific semantic communication architectures to a source-model-level information-theoretic object.

•

An exact zero-distortion characterization under closure fidelity.

Under Assumption
3.1
, we prove that the minimum zero-distortion rate is

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0)=P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A}),

where
A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
is the irredundant core,
P_{A}
is its source mass, and
\pi_{A}
is the source distribution conditioned on
A
(Theorem
3.1
). Thus the effective zero-distortion rate is determined by the core rather than by the full stored source.

•

A core-only decomposition of the full rate–distortion function.

For reconstruction alphabets contained in
\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, we show that
the entire rate–distortion function decomposes into a contribution
from the irredundant core alone
(Theorem
4.1
). In this sense,
redundant states are invisible to both rate and distortion.

•

An exact rate–depth–distortion law for limited inference.

When the decoder is allowed only a bounded inference-depth budget,
we introduce the
\delta
-irredundant core and prove the exact
zero-distortion rate–depth law

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,\delta)=P_{\delta}\,H(\pi_{\delta}),

together with the corresponding full rate–depth–distortion function

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(D,\delta)
over
(D,\delta)

(Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
).
Under an additional order-robustness assumption ensuring

\text{Atom}(S_{O})=\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O})
, this yields a sharp interpolation between
classical symbolwise coding and unconstrained deductive coding.

•

Information-theoretic consequences and extensions.

The source characterization leads to source–channel separation statements, closure-adapted converse bounds of Fano type, and extensions to heterogeneous receivers with mismatched vocabularies. These results show how shared inference structure affects reliable communication beyond the homogeneous source-coding setting.

II
Deductive Source Model and Closure Fidelity

This section isolates the minimum structure needed for the
source-coding problem studied in this paper.
At this stage no channel model is required.
The basic object is a finite source alphabet of statements,
equipped with a fixed proof system and its induced deductive
closure operator.
This structure induces, once the proof system and canonical order are
fixed, a decomposition of the source into irredundant premises and
redundant stored consequences, which in turn determines the
closure-based fidelity criterion.

II-A

Deductive Source and Irredundant Core

Assumption 2.1

(Fixed effective proof system)
.

We fix a finite ambient universe
\mathbb{S}_{O}
of candidate
statements together with an effective proof system

\mathsf{PS}
.
Hence
\text{Cn}(\Gamma)\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
is finite for every finite
\Gamma
.
For every finite
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
and
every
s\in\mathbb{S}_{O}
, whether

\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm{kb}}s
is decidable.
The induced deductive closure operator is

\text{Cn}(\Gamma)\;:=\;\{s\in\mathbb{S}_{O}:\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm{kb}}s\},

viewed as the standard logical closure operator associated with the
proof system

[
14
]
.
It satisfies:

(Cn1)

Reflexivity:

\Gamma\subseteq\text{Cn}(\Gamma)
.

(Cn2)

Monotonicity:

\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow\text{Cn}(\Gamma)\subseteq\text{Cn}(\Gamma^{\prime})
.

(Cn3)

Idempotence:

\text{Cn}(\text{Cn}(\Gamma))=\text{Cn}(\Gamma)
.

Assumption 2.2

(Finite source alphabet)
.

The transmitted knowledge base
S_{O}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}

is finite and effectively listable under a fixed canonical order.
The reconstruction alphabet
\hat{S}_{O}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}

is also finite and nonempty.

Assumption 2.3

(Decidable redundancy test)
.

For every finite
\Gamma\subseteq S_{O}
and every
s\in S_{O}
,
membership
s\in\text{Cn}(\Gamma)
is decidable.

Remark 2.1

(On Assumption
2.3
)
.

Assumption
2.3
is implied by
Assumption
2.1
; it is stated separately only to
highlight the specific decidability query used in the deletion
procedure of Definition
2.2
.

Definition 2.1

(Deductive source)
.

A
deductive source
is a pair
(S_{O},P_{O})
, where

S_{O}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
is a finite knowledge base and

P_{O}\in\Delta(S_{O})
is a probability distribution on
S_{O}
,
together with the fixed proof system of
Assumption
2.1
.

Definition 2.2

(Irredundant core)
.

Let
S_{O}
be a finite knowledge base.
Define
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
by the following deterministic deletion
procedure:
initialize
A\leftarrow S_{O}
; scan the elements of
S_{O}
in the
fixed canonical order; whenever the current element
s
satisfies

s\in\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{s\})
, remove it from
A
.
The final set is called the
irredundant core
of
S_{O}
.

Definition 2.3

(Core and redundant part)
.

For a deductive source
S_{O}
, write

A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O}),\qquad J:=S_{O}\setminus A.

The set
A
is the
irredundant core
, and
J
is the

redundant part
.

Proposition 2.1

(Core correctness)
.

Under
Assumptions
2.1
–
2.3
,
the set
A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
satisfies:

(i)

\text{Cn}(A)=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
;

(ii)

for every
a\in A
,

a\notin\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{a\})

(irredundancy)
;

(iii)

\text{Atom}(S_{O})
is uniquely determined by

S_{O}
,
Cn
, and the fixed canonical order.

Each deletion preserves closure.
Indeed, if
s\in\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{s\})
, then

A\subseteq\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{s\})
, hence by monotonicity and
idempotence,

\text{Cn}(A)\subseteq\text{Cn}(\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{s\}))=\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{s\}),

while the reverse inclusion is immediate from

A\setminus\{s\}\subseteq A
.
By induction over the scan, the final output satisfies

\text{Cn}(A)=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, proving (i).

For (ii), let
a\in A
and let

A_{\mathrm{scan}}
denote the current set when
a
is scanned.
Since
a
is retained,

a\notin\text{Cn}(A_{\mathrm{scan}}\setminus\{a\})
.
Later deletions only shrink the set, so

A\setminus\{a\}\subseteq A_{\mathrm{scan}}\setminus\{a\}
.
Monotonicity then gives

\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{a\})\subseteq\text{Cn}(A_{\mathrm{scan}}\setminus\{a\}),

hence
a\notin\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{a\})
.

Part (iii) follows from the determinism of the deletion
procedure once
Cn
and the canonical order are fixed.
∎

Remark 2.2

(Proof-system dependence and what is intrinsic)
.

The set
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
is relative to the chosen proof system and, in
general, to the canonical order.
This dependence is not a defect of the model: it reflects the fact
that communication value is measured relative to shared inference
capability.
What is primary in this paper is the induced
rate–distortion quantity, not any one particular realizing core.
Section
III
shows that the scalar
zero-distortion rate is intrinsic to the deductive source model, even
when different canonical orders may produce different irredundant
cores.
Appendix
C-B
then gives a sufficient
condition under which the core itself becomes order-invariant.

II-B

Closure Fidelity and Closure Distortion

The source model above suggests a fidelity criterion stronger than
semantic similarity but weaker than symbolwise equality:
a reconstruction should be considered faithful whenever it
preserves the deductive consequences of the transmitted knowledge
base.

Definition 2.4

(Closure fidelity)
.

For finite
S,\hat{S}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
, define

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S,\hat{S})\;:=\;\frac{|\text{Cn}(S)\cap\text{Cn}(\hat{S})|}{|\text{Cn}(S)\cup\text{Cn}(\hat{S})|},

with
0/0:=1
.
Thus
\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S,\hat{S})=1
if and only if

\text{Cn}(S)=\text{Cn}(\hat{S})
.

Remark 2.3

(Closure fidelity as a Jaccard-type similarity)
.

The quantity
\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S,\hat{S})
is the Jaccard similarity
between the two closure sets
\text{Cn}(S)
and
\text{Cn}(\hat{S})
. Jaccard-
type similarity and the associated Tanimoto/Jaccard distance are
classical set-comparison tools; see, e.g.,

[
3
,
17
]
. Here the comparison is
lifted from raw symbol sets to deductive closures.

Remark 2.4

(Conceptual relation to semantic content)
.

The closure-based viewpoint is conceptually related to the early
Carnap–Bar-Hillel account of semantic information, in which the
informational content of a sentence is tied to the set of possibilities
it excludes
[
4
]
. Our quantity

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S,\hat{S})
is not a possible-world measure; it is
a proof-theoretic comparison of deductive consequence sets. Still, as
a Jaccard-type similarity on closures, it can be viewed as a natural
way to quantify how much inferential content is preserved by a
reconstruction.

Definition 2.5

(Hamming distortion)
.

The Hamming distortion
[
7
, Ch. 10]
is

d_{H}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}):=\mathbf{1}[s_{o}\neq\hat{s}_{o}].

Definition 2.6

(Closure distortion)
.

Fix a reference knowledge base
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}

and write
\Gamma_{-s}:=\Gamma\setminus\{s\}
.
For
s_{o}\in\Gamma
and
\hat{s}_{o}\in\hat{S}_{O}
, define

C_{s_{o}}:=\text{Cn}(\Gamma_{-s_{o}}\cup\{s_{o}\}),\qquad C_{\hat{s}_{o}}:=\text{Cn}(\Gamma_{-s_{o}}\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\}),

and set

d_{\text{Cn}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid\Gamma):=1-\frac{|C_{s_{o}}\cap C_{\hat{s}_{o}}|}{|C_{s_{o}}\cup C_{\hat{s}_{o}}|},

(2.1)

with
0/0:=0
, i.e., zero distortion when both closure sets are empty.
In the sequel we use the single-letter distortion

d_{\text{Cn}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}):=d_{\text{Cn}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})
.

Remark 2.5

(Why closure distortion is the right object here)
.

Under
d_{H}
, every symbol error is costly.
Under
d_{\text{Cn}}
, replacing a statement by a different one may be
free if the deductive closure is preserved.
This is precisely the mechanism by which deductive redundancy
becomes compressible.
The entire zero-distortion theory developed in the next section
rests on this distinction.

Proposition 2.2

(Redundant states are zero-distortion free)
.

Let
A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
and
J=S_{O}\setminus A
.
If
j\in J
and
\hat{s}\in\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, then

d_{\text{Cn}}(j,\hat{s}\mid S_{O})=0.

Because
A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})\subseteq S_{O}\setminus\{j\}
, one has by
Proposition
2.1
(i) and monotonicity that

\text{Cn}(S_{O})=\text{Cn}(A)\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{j\}).

Since also
S_{O}\setminus\{j\}\subseteq S_{O}
, the reverse inclusion

\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{j\})\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
is immediate.
Hence

\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{j\})=\text{Cn}(S_{O}).

Because
\hat{s}\in\text{Cn}(S_{O})=\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{j\})
, one has

(S_{O}\setminus\{j\})\cup\{\hat{s}\}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{j\}).

By monotonicity and idempotence,

\text{Cn}\bigl((S_{O}\setminus\{j\})\cup\{\hat{s}\}\bigr)=\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{j\})=\text{Cn}(S_{O}).

Hence the two closures in
Definition
2.6
coincide, and the
Jaccard distance is zero.
∎

Definition 2.7

(Closure-based rate–distortion function)
.

Let
(S_{O},P_{O})
be a deductive source. Let
\mathsf{S}_{o}\sim P_{O}
and let

\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{o}\in\hat{S}_{O}
be generated via a test channel

P_{\hat{S}\mid S}
as
\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{o}\sim P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid\mathsf{S}_{o})
.
With the single-letter distortion
d_{\text{Cn}}(\cdot,\cdot\mid S_{O})
, define

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(D;\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,P_{O}):=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{\hat{S}\mid S}:\\
\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{Cn}}(\mathsf{S}_{o},\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{o})]\leq D\end{subarray}}I(\mathsf{S}_{o};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{o}).

(2.2)

Remark 2.6

(Operational meaning of the source model)
.

The object being compressed in this paper is a
statement stream
drawn from a fixed deductive environment
, not a random knowledge base.
More precisely, a finite knowledge base
S_{O}
is fixed once and for
all as part of the source model, together with its proof system and
closure operator, and the random source symbol
S_{o}
is drawn from

P_{O}
on
S_{O}
.

Operationally, one may think of
S_{o}
as a statement report, update,
query answer, or symbolic semantic token produced within the same local
knowledge environment
S_{O}
.
A block of length
m
therefore models
m
independent draws from
the same deductive source environment.
Accordingly, the distortion

d_{\text{Cn}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})
is a standard single-letter
distortion function parameterized by the fixed ambient knowledge base

S_{O}
.

We do
not
study in this paper the distinct problem in which the
knowledge base itself is random across blocks.
That would lead to a higher-level hierarchical source model and is left
for future work.

Remark 2.7

(Roadmap)
.

III
studies the case

and

Section
V
later replaces the

III
Exact Zero-Distortion Rate

This section gives the main zero-distortion characterization
behind the paper.
Throughout, let

A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O}),\qquad J:=S_{O}\setminus A,\qquad P_{A}:=P_{O}(A),\qquad P_{J}:=1-P_{A},

and, whenever
P_{A}>0
, let

\pi_{A}(a):=\frac{P_{O}(a)}{P_{A}},\qquad a\in A.

We write
h_{b}(p):=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)
for the binary entropy function,
and adopt the convention
P_{A}H(\pi_{A}):=0
when
P_{A}=0
.

The key technical issue is whether different core symbols can share
the same zero-distortion reconstruction.
When they cannot, the zero-distortion problem admits an exact
single-letter solution.

Assumption 3.1

(Disjoint zero-distortion reconstruction sets)
.

For each
s\in S_{O}
, define its zero-distortion reconstruction set

R_{\text{Cn}}(s):=\{\hat{s}_{o}\in\hat{S}_{O}:\ d_{\text{Cn}}(s,\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0\}.

We assume that for distinct
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,

R_{\text{Cn}}(a_{1})\cap R_{\text{Cn}}(a_{2})=\varnothing.

Remark 3.1

(Fully redundant degenerate case)
.

If
A=\varnothing
and there exists

\hat{s}^{\star}\in\hat{S}_{O}\cap\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, then the constant
reconstruction
\hat{S}_{o}\equiv\hat{s}^{\star}
achieves zero closure
distortion for every source symbol, and hence

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0;\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,P_{O})=0.

The exact theorem below therefore focuses on the nondegenerate case

A\neq\varnothing
, where the core carries positive effective
complexity.

Remark 3.2

(Zero-mass core case)
.

If
A\neq\varnothing
,
P_{A}=0
, and
A\subseteq\hat{S}_{O}
, then
the source is supported on
J=S_{O}\setminus A
. Hence any fixed

a_{0}\in A
used as a constant reconstruction achieves zero closure
distortion almost surely, because
a_{0}\in A\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
and
every symbol in
J
is zero-distortion free against every
reconstruction in
\text{Cn}(S_{O})
by
Proposition
2.2
. Therefore

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0;\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,P_{O})=0.

Accordingly, the only nontrivial case in
Theorem
3.1
is
P_{A}>0
.

Remark 3.3

(Interpretation and naturality of
Assumption
3.1
)
.

Assumption
3.1
is a

zero-distortion nonconfusability condition
on the irredundant
core.
It is the closure-fidelity analogue of requiring pairwise disjoint
zero-error decoding regions: a reconstruction that is closure-correct
for one core symbol cannot simultaneously be closure-correct for a
different core symbol.

Thus the assumption is not an arbitrary technical restriction but the
precise condition under which the exact zero-distortion converse
collapses to a single-letter entropy expression.
When it fails, and still assuming
A\subseteq\hat{S}_{O}
, the
achievability bound

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0)\leq P_{A}H(\pi_{A})
still holds, but the converse
must account for overlap among the sets

\{R_{\text{Cn}}(a):a\in A\}
, leading to a genuine confusability problem
on the core.
Appendix
B
gives concrete Datalog classes in
which the assumption is satisfied by construction.

Proposition 3.1

(Zero-distortion achievability)
.

Assume
P_{A}>0
and
A\subseteq\hat{S}_{O}
. Then

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0;\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,P_{O})\;\leq\;P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A}).

(3.1)

Define the conditional distribution

P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\hat{s}_{o}\mid s_{o}):=\begin{cases}\mathbf{1}[\hat{s}_{o}=s_{o}],&s_{o}\in A,\\[3.0pt]
\pi_{A}(\hat{s}_{o}),&s_{o}\in J,\end{cases}

where
\pi_{A}
is supported on
A\subseteq\hat{S}_{O}
.

For
a\in A
, the reconstruction is exact, so

d_{\text{Cn}}(a,a\mid S_{O})=0
.
For
j\in J
, the reconstruction lies in

A\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, hence

d_{\text{Cn}}(j,\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0
by
Proposition
2.2
.
Therefore the expected distortion is zero.

Let
T
be the auxiliary variable

T(s_{o}):=\begin{cases}s_{o},&s_{o}\in A,\\
*,&s_{o}\in J.\end{cases}

Under the above conditional law,
\hat{S}_{o}
depends on
S_{o}

only through

, and the output marginal is exactly
\pi_{A}
.
Hence

I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o})=I(T;\hat{S}_{o})=H(\hat{S}_{o})-H(\hat{S}_{o}\mid T)=H(\pi_{A})-P_{J}H(\pi_{A})=P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A}).

This proves (
3.1
).
∎

Theorem 3.1

(Exact zero-distortion rate)
.

Assume
A\neq\varnothing
,
A\subseteq\hat{S}_{O}
, and
Assumption
3.1
.
Then the closure-based zero-distortion rate is exactly

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0;\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,P_{O})\;=\;P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A}),

(3.2)

with the convention that the right-hand side is
0
when
P_{A}=0
.

If
P_{A}=0
, the claim follows from
Remark
3.2
. Assume henceforth that

P_{A}>0
.

The achievability part is Proposition
3.1
.
For the converse, collapse the source to the auxiliary variable

T(s_{o}):=\begin{cases}s_{o},&s_{o}\in A,\\
*,&s_{o}\in J.\end{cases}

Then
I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o})\geq I(T;\hat{S}_{o})
by data processing.
Under zero closure distortion, each core symbol
a\in A
must be
reconstructed inside
R_{\text{Cn}}(a)
, and
Assumption
3.1
makes these supports pairwise
disjoint; symbols in
J
impose no identifying constraint by

2.2
. A disjoint-support entropy
decomposition, combined with concavity of entropy, yields

Together with Proposition
3.1
, this proves
(

). Full details are given in
Appendix

.
∎

Remark 3.4

(Canonical-order dependence versus intrinsic rate)
.

The set
A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
may depend on the fixed canonical order used
in Definition
2.2
. This does
not
mean that the
zero-distortion rate itself is order-dependent.
Indeed, the left-hand side of (

),

, is defined entirely in terms
of the source law

and the closure distortion

, and

Consequently, for any canonical order under which

applies, the scalar

must equal the same intrinsic zero-distortion rate.

What may depend on the canonical order is the particular
core/redundant decomposition that realizes this value.
Appendix

gives a sufficient

Corollary 3.1

(Uniform source and strict gain over Hamming fidelity)
.

Assume

, and let

.
Under the hypotheses of

3.1
:

(i)

, then

(3.3)

(ii)

,

For (i), under the uniform source,

, so

.
Substituting into (

) gives

).

For (ii), write

for the source distribution

Using Theorem

∎

Remark 3.5

(Interpretation)
.

Theorem

is the point at which deductive
structure enters the information theory in a decisive way.
Under Hamming fidelity, every source symbol must remain
distinguishable and the zero-distortion rate is

.
Under closure fidelity, only the irredundant core must remain
distinguishable; the redundant part is absorbed into a single
zero-cost component.
This is why the exact answer is

rather than

H(P_{O})
.

Remark 3.6

(The main unresolved issue)
.

Assumption

identifies the main technical

Without disjointness, and still assuming

, the
upper bound

still follows from Proposition

,
but the converse must quantify the overlap structure among the
sets

,
a\in A
.
This leads naturally to a confusability graph on the core.
A graph-entropy-type characterization of that general case is, in
our view, the key next theoretical breakthrough needed to complete
the zero-distortion picture. A related analysis for ambiguous alphabets under asymptotic equipartition is given in
[

]
.

IV
Core Decomposition of the Full Rate–Distortion Function

Section
III
identified the exact
zero-distortion rate under closure fidelity.
We now extend the analysis to arbitrary distortion levels.
The main point is that the redundant part
J=S_{O}\setminus A

remains invisible not only at
D=0
, but across the entire
rate–distortion function.
Thus the full problem decomposes into a classical
rate–distortion problem on the irredundant core, scaled by the
source mass of that core.

Definition 4.1

(Core sub-source rate–distortion function)
.

Let
A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
and let
\pi_{A}
be the source
distribution conditioned on
A
.
Assume
\hat{S}_{O}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})\cap\mathbb{S}_{O}
.
The
core sub-source rate–distortion function
is

R^{(A)}(D^{\prime};\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,\pi_{A}):=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{\hat{S}\mid A}:\\
\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{Cn}}(A_{o},\hat{S}_{o})]\leq D^{\prime}\end{subarray}}I(\mathsf{A}_{o};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{o}),

(4.1)

where
\mathsf{A}_{o}\sim\pi_{A}
, the reconstruction alphabet is

\hat{S}_{O}
, and the distortion is

d_{\text{Cn}}(\cdot,\cdot\mid S_{O})
restricted to inputs in
A
.

Theorem 4.1

(Core decomposition of the full
rate–distortion function)
.

Assume
\hat{S}_{O}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})\cap\mathbb{S}_{O}
.
Then, for every
D\geq 0
,

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(D;\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,P_{O})\;=\;P_{A}\cdot R^{(A)}\!\Bigl(\frac{D}{P_{A}};\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,\pi_{A}\Bigr),

(4.2)

with the convention that the right-hand side is
0
when

P_{A}=0
.

In particular:

(i)

under the additional hypotheses

A\subseteq\hat{S}_{O}
and
Assumption
3.1
,
one has

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0;\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,P_{O})=P_{A}H(\pi_{A})
,
recovering Theorem
3.1
;

(ii)

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(\cdot)
is convex,
non-increasing, and reducible to a finite-alphabet
core problem that is amenable to standard numerical
evaluation;

(iii)

redundant states are invisible to both rate and
distortion.

By Proposition
2.2
, every redundant state contributes
zero closure distortion against every reconstruction in

\hat{S}_{O}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, so both the distortion constraint and the
mutual-information minimization reduce to the irredundant core.
A lower bound follows by introducing the auxiliary variable

T(s_{o})=s_{o}
on
A
and
T(s_{o})=*
on
J
, and then applying
entropy concavity exactly as in the converse of
Theorem
3.1
.
Achievability is obtained by extending an optimal test channel for the
core sub-source to the redundant part using a common output law.
The full proof is given in Appendix
A-B
.
∎

Remark 4.1

(Disjointness is needed only for the exact
zero-distortion formula)
.

The decomposition theorem itself does
not
require
Assumption
3.1
.
That assumption is needed only to identify the
core-only zero-distortion term in closed form as

H(\pi_{A})
.
Thus the structural reduction to the core is more general than
the exact zero-distortion evaluation.

Remark 4.2

(What remains nonclassical at positive distortion)
.

Theorem
4.1
reduces the full source to its
core, but the reduced problem is still nonclassical because the
distortion on the core is the closure distortion

d_{\text{Cn}}(\cdot,\cdot\mid S_{O})
, not a symbolwise distortion.
Thus the theorem isolates the precise source of the semantic
gain: redundancy disappears entirely, while the nontrivial
positive-distortion geometry is concentrated on the core.

V
Limited Inference and the Rate–Depth–Distortion Tradeoff

Sections
III
and
IV

treat the decoder as having unrestricted deductive power.
We now introduce a bounded inference-depth budget.
The resulting fidelity criterion distinguishes statements that are
re-derivable
eventually
from those re-derivable

within
\delta
iterations of
T_{\mathsf{PS}}
.
This yields a natural rate–depth–distortion tradeoff.

V-A

Finite-Step Deduction and Derivation Depth

Assumption 5.1

(Finite-step closure dynamics)
.

For the proof systems considered in this section, there exists a
computable monotone operator
T_{\mathsf{PS}}
on finite subsets
of
\mathbb{S}_{O}
such that, for every finite

B\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
,

(T1)

T_{\mathsf{PS}}(B)\supseteq B
;

(T2)

B\subseteq B^{\prime}\Rightarrow T_{\mathsf{PS}}(B)\subseteq T_{\mathsf{PS}}(B^{\prime})
;

(T3)

with
T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{0}(B):=B
and

T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{n+1}(B):=T_{\mathsf{PS}}(T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{n}(B))
,
one has

\text{Cn}(B)=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{n}(B);

(T4)

the sequence
T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{n}(B)
stabilizes in
finitely many iterations.

This covers standard Datalog and Horn-clause settings over finite
active domains

[
28
,
5
,
1
,
9
]
.

Definition 5.1

(Derivation depth)
.

For finite
B\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
and
s\in\mathbb{S}_{O}
,
define

\text{Dd}(s\mid B):=\min\{n\geq 0:\ s\in T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{n}(B)\},

with
\text{Dd}(s\mid B):=\infty
if
s\notin\text{Cn}(B)
.

Definition 5.2

(Maximum intrinsic derivation depth)
.

Let
A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
.
The
maximum intrinsic derivation depth
of
S_{O}
is

\mathsf{D_{d}}:=\max_{q\in S_{O}}\text{Dd}(q\mid A),

with the convention
\max\varnothing:=0
.

Assumption 5.2

(Order-robust core for the bounded-inference section)
.

Throughout Section
V
, we additionally assume

A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})=\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O}):=\{s\in S_{O}:\ s\notin\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\})\}.

Equivalently, every element of the chosen core is globally
nonderivable from the remaining stored source symbols.
This assumption is satisfied, for example, under the
essential-generation condition of
Proposition
C.1
.

Remark 5.1

(Why the extra assumption is needed here)
.

The family
A_{\delta}
is defined directly from derivability out of

S_{O}\setminus\{s\}
, so as
\delta
increases it converges to the
order-free essential set
\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O})
, not in general to an
arbitrary order-dependent deletion core
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
.
Assumption
5.2
is therefore exactly
what ensures that the large-
\delta
endpoint of the
bounded-inference theory coincides with the zero-distortion law of
Section
III
.

V-B

Depth-Constrained Fidelity

Definition 5.3

(
\delta
-step closure distortion)
.

For integer
\delta\geq 0
, define

d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O}):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }S_{O}\subseteq T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{\delta}\bigl((S_{O}\setminus\{s_{o}\})\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\}\bigr),\\[4.0pt]
1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(5.1)

Remark 5.2

(Interpretation of
d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}
)
.

The distortion
d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}
is a bounded-inference
fidelity criterion.
It asks whether the entire source set
S_{O}
can be
reconstructed within at most
\delta
iterations of the
immediate-consequence operator after replacing
s_{o}
by

\hat{s}_{o}
.

At
\delta=0
, one has

d_{\text{Cn}}^{0}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=\mathbf{1}[s_{o}\neq\hat{s}_{o}]=d_{H}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}).

Thus the zero-inference endpoint recovers classical symbolwise
fidelity.

Definition 5.4

(
\delta
-redundancy and

\delta
-irredundant core)
.

A state
s\in S_{O}
is
\delta
-redundant
if

s\in T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{\delta}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\}).

The corresponding
\delta
-irredundant core
is

A_{\delta}:=\text{Atom}_{\delta}(S_{O}):=\{s\in S_{O}:\ s\notin T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{\delta}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\})\}.

Write

P_{\delta}:=P_{O}(A_{\delta}),\qquad\pi_{\delta}(s):=\frac{P_{O}(s)}{P_{\delta}},\quad s\in A_{\delta},

whenever
P_{\delta}>0
.

Lemma 5.1

(
\delta
-redundant states are distortion-free)
.

If
s\notin A_{\delta}
, then

d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(s,\hat{s}\mid S_{O})=0,\qquad\forall\,\hat{s}\in\hat{S}_{O}.

If
s\notin A_{\delta}
, then by definition

s\in T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{\delta}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\}).

By monotonicity of
T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{\delta}
,

T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{\delta}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\})\subseteq T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{\delta}\bigl((S_{O}\setminus\{s\})\cup\{\hat{s}\}\bigr).

Hence both
S_{O}\setminus\{s\}
and
s
belong to the latter
set, so the whole source set
S_{O}
is recovered within

\delta
iterations.
Therefore
d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(s,\hat{s}\mid S_{O})=0
.
∎

Proposition 5.1

(
\delta
-core filtration)
.

The family
\{A_{\delta}\}_{\delta\geq 0}
satisfies:

(i)

A_{0}=S_{O}
;

(ii)

A_{\delta+1}\subseteq A_{\delta}
for every

\delta\geq 0
;

(iii)

under Assumption
5.2
,

A_{\delta}=A
for every
\delta\geq\mathsf{D_{d}}
.

Part (i) follows from

T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{0}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\})=S_{O}\setminus\{s\}
, which
never contains
s
.

For (ii), monotonicity in the number of iterations gives

T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{\delta}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\})\subseteq T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{\delta+1}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\}),

so once
s
becomes
\delta
-redundant, it remains

(\delta+1)
-redundant.

For (iii), let
j\in J=S_{O}\setminus A
.
Since
\text{Cn}(A)=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, one has
j\in\text{Cn}(A)
, and by the
definition of
\mathsf{D_{d}}
,

j\in T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{\mathsf{D_{d}}}(A).

Because
A\subseteq S_{O}\setminus\{j\}
, monotonicity yields

j\in T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{\mathsf{D_{d}}}(S_{O}\setminus\{j\}),

hence
j\notin A_{\delta}
for every
\delta\geq\mathsf{D_{d}}
.

Conversely, if
a\in A
, then by
Assumption
5.2
,

a\notin\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{a\}).

Therefore
a\notin T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{\delta}(S_{O}\setminus\{a\})
for
every finite
\delta
, because

T_{\mathsf{PS}}^{\delta}(S_{O}\setminus\{a\})\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{a\})
.
Hence
a\in A_{\delta}
for all
\delta
, proving

A_{\delta}=A
when
\delta\geq\mathsf{D_{d}}
.
∎

Assumption 5.3

(Disjoint zero-distortion sets for the

\delta
-core)
.

For each
\delta\geq 0
and each
a\in A_{\delta}
, define

R_{\delta}(a):=\{\hat{s}_{o}\in\hat{S}_{O}:\ d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(a,\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0\}.

We assume that for distinct

,

R_{\delta}(a_{1})\cap R_{\delta}(a_{2})=\varnothing.

V-C

Exact Rate–Depth–Distortion Laws

Definition 5.5

(Rate–depth–distortion function)
.

For
\delta\geq 0
, define

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(D,\delta;\,d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta},\,P_{O}):=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{\hat{S}\mid S}:\\
\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(S_{o},\hat{S}_{o})]\leq D\end{subarray}}I(\mathsf{S}_{o};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{o}).

(5.2)

Theorem 5.1

(Exact zero-distortion rate–depth tradeoff)
.

Fix
\delta\geq 0
.
Assume
A_{\delta}\subseteq\hat{S}_{O}
and that

5.3
holds at this
\delta
.
Then

(5.3)

with the convention that the right-hand side is
0
when

.

The proof is the exact analogue of
Theorem

, with

,
P_{A}
, and
\pi_{A}

A_{\delta}
,

, and

Indeed, define

Corollary 5.1

(Endpoints and monotonicity of the zero-distortion

Assume

VII
Conclusion

This paper studied lossy compression of a finite knowledge source equipped with a fixed proof system, with fidelity measured by preservation of deductive closure rather than by symbolwise equality. The central conclusion is that, under closure fidelity, the effective source complexity is governed by the irredundant core of the knowledge base rather than by the full stored source. Under an explicit disjointness condition on zero-distortion reconstruction sets, we showed that the zero-distortion rate is exactly
P_{A}H(\pi_{A})
, and that the full rate–distortion function decomposes into a contribution from
that core alone whenever the reconstruction alphabet is contained in

\text{Cn}(S_{O})
. We further derived an exact rate–depth–distortion characterization
under a bounded inference-depth budget; under an
additional order-robustness assumption ensuring that the chosen core
coincides with the essential set, this yields a sharp interpolation
between classical symbolwise compression and unconstrained deductive
compression.

From an information-theoretic perspective, the paper contributes a
source-model formulation for deductive compression rather than merely
a semantic communication architecture. Its main insight is that shared inference structure can be treated as a rate-reducing resource: redundant stored states are free under closure fidelity because they can be re-derived at the decoder. Source–channel separation statements, closure-adapted converse bounds, and heterogeneous-receiver extensions follow from this source-model viewpoint.

Several directions remain open. The most important is to remove or weaken the disjointness assumption and obtain a characterization for the general confusable case. Other natural directions are to extend the theory beyond finite-alphabet and i.i.d. models, to study multiterminal and interactive versions of deductive compression, and to allow heterogeneous proof systems across agents. We hope that the formulation developed here provides a useful starting point for a broader information-theoretic theory of structured sources with inference-enabled reconstruction.

Appendix B
Datalog Classes Satisfying the Assumptions

This appendix records concrete Datalog classes under which the
standing assumptions used in the paper are satisfied.
The goal is not to give the most general logical characterization,
but to isolate natural and checkable sufficient conditions.

semantics induced by

.

.

semantics over finite active domains

,
5
,
1
,

.

2.1
–
2.3

and Assumption

the set
\mathbb{S}_{O}
of ground atoms is finite.
Hence every knowledge base

until the least fixpoint is reached

5
,
1
,

and every

,

and

if there exists an injective map

\tau:A\to\mathcal{D}
assigning a unique tag to each core fact,
together with a distinguished argument position in the relevant
predicates, such that:

(TS1)

each
a\in A
appears with tag
\tau(a)
, and no other
stored fact in
S_{O}
carries the tag
\tau(a)
;

(TS2)

every instantiated rule propagates but never creates the
distinguished tag: if all body facts used in a derivation
carry the same tag
t
, then the derived head fact also
carries tag
t
; moreover, a fact carrying tag
t
can be
derived only from premises of which at least one already
carries tag
t
;

(TS3)

for every
a\in A
, there exists at least one fact

w_{a}\in\text{Cn}(S_{O})
carrying tag
\tau(a)
such that

w_{a}\notin\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{a\})
.

Proposition B.2

(Tag seeding implies core-disjointness)
.

Let a finite active-domain Datalog instance be tag-seeded in the
sense of Definition
B.2
, and let the
reconstruction alphabet satisfy

\hat{S}_{O}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})\cap\mathbb{S}_{O}
.
Then Assumption
3.1
holds.

Fix
a\in A
, and let
\hat{s}\in R_{\text{Cn}}(a)
.
Then

\text{Cn}\bigl((S_{O}\setminus\{a\})\cup\{\hat{s}\}\bigr)=\text{Cn}(S_{O}).

In particular, the witness fact
w_{a}
belongs to the left-hand
closure.
By (TS2), any derivation of
w_{a}
must preserve the tag

\tau(a)
.
By (TS1), the knowledge base
S_{O}\setminus\{a\}
contains
no stored fact carrying the tag
\tau(a)
.
Therefore
\hat{s}
itself must carry the tag
\tau(a)
.

Hence every element of
R_{\text{Cn}}(a)
carries the tag
\tau(a)
.
Because the tags are distinct across different core facts by
injectivity of
\tau
, no reconstruction symbol can belong
simultaneously to
R_{\text{Cn}}(a_{1})
and
R_{\text{Cn}}(a_{2})
when

a_{1}\neq a_{2}
.
Thus

R_{\text{Cn}}(a_{1})\cap R_{\text{Cn}}(a_{2})=\varnothing,\qquad a_{1}\neq a_{2},

which is Assumption
3.1
.
∎

Remark B.2

(Finite-depth variants)
.

The tagged-provenance construction above justifies
Assumption
3.1
for the unconstrained
zero-distortion problem.
A finite-depth analogue can also be imposed to justify
Assumption
5.3
, but it requires tagging
the
\delta
-irredundant symbols themselves rather than only the
final core
A
.
Since this extra bookkeeping is orthogonal to the main line of the
paper, we do not formalize the finite-depth syntactic condition here.

Remark B.3

(Scope of the syntactic condition)
.

The tag-seeded class is only a sufficient condition for
Assumptions
3.1
and

5.3
, not a necessary one.
Its role is to show that the disjointness assumptions used in the
main theorems hold on a concrete and checkable Datalog family, rather
than being purely abstract hypotheses.

Appendix C
Extra Examples and Counterexamples

This appendix records supplementary examples illustrating the main
definitions and the limits of the theory.

C-A

Order Dependence of the Irredundant Core

Example C.1

(Order dependence)
.

Let

S_{O}=\{p,q,r\},

and suppose the proof system contains the two rules

p\leftarrow q,\qquad q\leftarrow p.

Then
p
and
q
are deductively interchangeable, while
r
is
independent.

If the canonical order is
p\prec q\prec r
, then
p
is deleted
when scanned because
p\in\text{Cn}(\{q,r\})
, and the resulting core is

\text{Atom}(S_{O})=\{q,r\}.

If the canonical order is
q\prec p\prec r
, then the resulting
core is

\text{Atom}(S_{O})=\{p,r\}.

Thus
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
may depend on the canonical order, even though in
both cases the closure it generates is the same.

C-B

A sufficient condition for order invariance

Definition C.1

(Essential set)
.

The
essential set
of
S_{O}
is

\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O}):=\{s\in S_{O}:\ s\notin\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\})\}.

Proposition C.1

(Order-robust core under essential generation)
.

For every finite knowledge base
S_{O}
:

(i)

\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O})
depends only on
S_{O}
and
Cn
, not on the
canonical order;

(ii)

\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O})\subseteq\text{Atom}(S_{O})
for every canonical order;

(iii)

if
\text{Cn}(\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O}))=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, then

\text{Atom}(S_{O})=\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O})

for every canonical order.

Part (i) is immediate from the definition.

For (ii), let
s\in\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O})
, and let
B
be the current
set when
s
is scanned in the deletion procedure of
Definition
2.2
.
Since
B\setminus\{s\}\subseteq S_{O}\setminus\{s\}
, monotonicity of

Cn
gives

\text{Cn}(B\setminus\{s\})\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\}).

Because
s\notin\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\})
, one also has

s\notin\text{Cn}(B\setminus\{s\})
, so
s
cannot be deleted.
Hence
s\in\text{Atom}(S_{O})
.

For (iii), assume
\text{Cn}(\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O}))=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
and let

s\in\text{Atom}(S_{O})
.
If
s\notin\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O})
, then

s\in\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\})
, while by part (ii) all
essential elements survive every deletion order.
Hence, at the moment
s
is scanned, the current set still contains

\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O})\setminus\{s\}=\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O})
. By monotonicity,

\text{Cn}(\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O}))\subseteq\text{Cn}(B\setminus\{s\}).

Since
\text{Cn}(\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O}))=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
by assumption and
s\in\text{Cn}(S_{O})

by reflexivity, it follows that

s\in\text{Cn}(B\setminus\{s\}),

which would force
s
to be deleted—a contradiction.
Therefore every element of
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
is essential, so together
with part (ii) one gets

\text{Atom}(S_{O})=\operatorname{Ess}(S_{O})
.
∎

Corollary C.1

(Order-invariant materialized EDB/IDB stores)
.

Suppose
S_{O}
consists of EDB facts together with materialized IDB
facts of a function-free Datalog program whose rule heads are all IDB
predicates.
Then no EDB fact is derivable from the remaining stored facts, the EDB
part generates
\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, and therefore
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
equals the
stored EDB part for every canonical order.

Since all rule heads are IDB predicates, no rule derives an EDB fact.
Hence every stored EDB fact is essential.
Moreover, the stored EDB facts generate all materialized IDB facts by
construction, so the EDB subset generates
\text{Cn}(S_{O})
.
Apply Proposition
C.1
(iii).
∎

C-C

A Minimal Exact Zero-Distortion Example

Example C.2

(One redundant consequence)
.

Let

S_{O}=\{a,b,c\},

and suppose
c\in\text{Cn}(\{a,b\})
, while neither
a
nor
b
is
derivable from the other two statements.
Then

A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})=\{a,b\},\qquad J=\{c\}.

Assume further that
S_{O}\subseteq\hat{S}_{O}
and that
Assumption
3.1
holds for this instance.

If
P_{O}
is uniform on
S_{O}
, then

P_{A}=\frac{2}{3},\qquad\pi_{A}(a)=\pi_{A}(b)=\frac{1}{2}.

Hence Theorem
3.1
gives

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0)=P_{A}H(\pi_{A})=\frac{2}{3}\cdot 1=\frac{2}{3}\ \text{bits}.

By contrast, classical zero-distortion coding under Hamming fidelity
requires

H(P_{O})=\log 3

bits. Thus the redundant consequence
c
creates a strict
deductive compression gain.

C-D

A Simple Rate–Depth Example

Example C.3

(A two-step shortcut)
.

Let

S_{O}=\{a,b,d,e\},

and suppose the proof system contains auxiliary statements

c,f\notin S_{O}
and rules

c\leftarrow a,\qquad d\leftarrow c,\qquad f\leftarrow b,\qquad e\leftarrow f.

Then
d
is derivable from
a
with derivation depth
2
, and
e

is derivable from
b
with derivation depth
2
.
The irredundant core is

A=\{a,b\}.

Assume throughout this example that
S_{O}\subseteq\hat{S}_{O}
, that
Assumption
5.2
holds, and that
Assumption
5.3
holds for

\delta=0,1,2
.

However, with depth budget
\delta=1
, neither
d
nor
e

is redundant relative to
S_{O}\setminus\{d\}
or
S_{O}\setminus\{e\}
,
because the intermediate statements
c
and
f
are not stored.
Hence

A_{0}=A_{1}=S_{O}.

At depth budget
\delta=2
, both
d
and
e
become redundant,
so

A_{2}=\{a,b\}=A.

If
P_{O}
is uniform on
S_{O}
, then

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,0)=R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,1)=H(P_{O})=2

bits, whereas

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,2)=P_{A}H(\pi_{A})=\frac{1}{2}\cdot 1=\frac{1}{2}

bit.
This illustrates how additional inference depth can sharply reduce the
effective source rate.

C-E

A Confusable-Core Counterexample

Example C.4

(Failure of Assumption
3.1
)
.

Let

S_{O}=\{a_{1},a_{2},b\},

and let the proof system contain an auxiliary statement
r\notin S_{O}

together with rules

r\leftarrow a_{1},a_{2},\qquad a_{1}\leftarrow b,r,\qquad a_{2}\leftarrow b,r.

Neither
a_{1}
nor
a_{2}
is derivable from the remaining stored
statements alone, so both are core elements.

Now replace
a_{1}
by
r
.
From
\{a_{2},b,r\}
, one can derive
a_{1}
, and the resulting
closure equals
\text{Cn}(S_{O})
.
Similarly, replacing
a_{2}
by
r
also preserves the full closure.
Therefore

r\in R_{\text{Cn}}(a_{1})\cap R_{\text{Cn}}(a_{2}),

so Assumption
3.1
fails.

This example shows that the general zero-distortion problem has a real
core-confusability phenomenon and is not captured by the disjoint case
alone.

C-F

Restricted Alphabet Impossibility

Example C.5

(No representative for a core symbol)
.

Let

S_{O}=\{a,b\},

and suppose the proof system contains no nontrivial derivation rules.
Then

A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})=\{a,b\}.

If the reconstruction alphabet is restricted to

V=\{a\},

then
a
has a zero-distortion representative in
V
, but
b

does not:

R_{\text{Cn}}^{(V)}(b)=\varnothing.

Therefore zero closure distortion is impossible, and
Theorem
6.5
yields

R_{\mathrm{sem}}^{(V)}(0)=\infty.

This is the simplest instance showing that heterogeneous receivers
matter only through the availability of zero-distortion
representatives for core symbols.
