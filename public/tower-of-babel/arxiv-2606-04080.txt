Essential Unitarity for Higher-Order Quantum Computation
Samson Abramsky, Radha Jagadeesan

Abstract
We develop a semantic framework for higher-order quantum computation based on a boundary-centric presentation of compact closed categories, building on Kelly–Laplaza and Abramsky. Morphisms are polarized boundary linkings composed by execution, with a unit-free monoidal sum providing reversible control and branching. We identify a notion of essential unitarity generalizing unitarity from first-order processes to higher-order interfaces; at first order it coincides with standard unitarity, and at higher order it characterizes when information is preserved relative to the boundary. Essential unitarity is the unique predicate compatible with dagger-monoidal structure, coherence reindexing, and currying, and reducing to ordinary unitarity at first order. Every morphism of the quantum core is essentially unitary. The framework realizes the coherent quantum switch and other one-slot, equal-ratio, purity-preserving supermaps as coherent pure-comb dilations. Extended Abstract appears in QPL 2026

Introduction

Since the foundational work of Abramsky and
Coecke
[
1
]
, Categorical Quantum Mechanics has
shown that much of the structural backbone of quantum

[

equipped with a symmetric monoidal

[

. String diagrams give a
representation-free account of process composition, independent of
any Hilbert-space model.

At first order, reversible dynamics is captured by unitarity: a
closed-system evolution

is reversible precisely when

f^{\dagger}f=ff^{\dagger}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
. Higher-order processes such as

under quantum
control, are physically realizable and reversible, yet not
first-order unitary endomorphisms: their inputs are processes, not
states. This raises the question:

what is the correct notion of unitarity for higher-order
quantum processes—one that captures operational reversibility
beyond first-order endomorphisms?

We work from below. The starting point is the free compact closed
category of Kelly–Laplaza
[
17
]
, in the concrete
combinatorial presentation of Abramsky
[
2
]
that
separates the roles of permutations, loops, and polarities. We
extend it with a unit-free monoidal sum

\mathbb{C}
-linear completion for superposition
and quantum amplitudes; quantum evolution arises by exponentiating
Hermitian involutions, starting from the structural involutions
generalizing the Pauli construction. The boundary-centric story is
in §
2

Before linearization, the unit-free monoidal sum
\oplus
is not a
biproduct and has no product projections, so the KL/coproduct
structural fragment admits no copying, erasure, or measurement. Rather than introducing distributivity
via coherence isomorphisms
[
19

normal form: objects are finite disjoint unions of interfaces, and
tensor distributes over sum strictly at the object level. The
ambient source category
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})
, a dagger symmetric monoidal
category with a symmetric semimonoidal sum, is the
\mathbb{C}
-linear
completion of the KL/coproduct structural fragment
(§

The Kelly–Laplaza linking re-expresses each

is two-sided

The boundary semantics gives an exact characterization of the

\mathbb{QC}

The present approach.

We work
from below

higher-order processes and contexts-with-holes. The point of
departure is methodological. Supermap and higher-order process

The Polarized Source Category
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})

We construct the ambient source category

3.1
Kelly–Laplaza base and polarized interfaces

The free dagger compact closed category on one generating object is
described concretely by Kelly and Laplaza
[
2
,
17
]
.
The generator produces the atomic positive interface
(1,0)
;
duality gives
(0,1)
, and tensor builds all signed interfaces

(m,n)\;=\;(1,0)^{\otimes m}\otimes(0,1)^{\otimes n}.

An object of the Kelly–Laplaza base
\mathbf{KL}
is thus a signed
finite set
M=(M^{+},M^{-})
, and a morphism
f:M\to N
is a polarized
bijection

f^{\sharp}\colon M^{+}\sqcup N^{-}\;\cong\;N^{+}\sqcup M^{-}

equipped with a loop count
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
.

Composition is path execution with cycle formation.
Gluing along the shared boundary decomposes each connected component
into either a path contributing to the composite wiring or a closed
cycle; if
\ell(g,f)
denotes the number of new cycles, then

\kappa(g\circ f)\;=\;\kappa(f)+\kappa(g)+\ell(g,f).

Associativity of this composition and correctness of the loop accounting
follow from Kelly–Laplaza coherence
[
2
,
17
]
.
The dagger is inverse wiring; duality is polarity reversal,

M^{*}:=(M^{-},M^{+})
; the tensor unit is

\mathbf{1}:=(\varnothing,\varnothing)
, the empty interface, arising as
the nullary tensor (zero copies of the generator on each side).

3.2
The ambient source category with finite coproducts

\mathbf{FinBij}

4
Boundary Transport

Boundary transport sends each morphism
f:A\to B
of
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})

to a single matrix
T_{f}
on the boundary port sets of the cut object

A^{*}\otimes B

\tau_{f}

permutation matrix

4.1
Boundary ports

For a signed interface

and

For

(Boundary port sets)

For objects

of
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})

4.2
Boundary transport

Let

Definition 4.3

(Boundary matching of a KL morphism)
.

The

\mathbb{C}^{N_{M,N}\times P_{M,N}}

(\underline{\tau_{g}})_{\tau_{g}(p),p}=1

Definition

\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})

via a single KL component and then
\mathbb{C}

Remark 4.4

(Axiom-link extraction and squaring)
.

A KL morphism

is a polarized bijection
g^{\sharp}
(its axiom
links) together with a loop count
\kappa(g)
. The boundary matching

\tau_{g}
extracts the axiom links, repackaging

and its

The construction separates the structural (wiring) and scalar (loop

Remark 4.10

5
Essential Unitarity

The boundary operator
T_{f}
of §
4

assigns, to each morphism
f:A\to B
of
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})
, a single
matrix
T_{f}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{A,B}\times P_{A,B}}
between
finite-dimensional inner-product spaces indexed by the boundary
ports. This section asks a self-contained structural question
about that matrix: is it a two-sided unitary? The predicate
answering the question —
essential unitarity
— is the
natural generalization of ordinary unitarity from square matrices
to the port-typed boundary operators produced by
T
.

5.1
The EU predicate

Definition 5.1

(Essential unitarity)
.

A morphism
f:A\to B
of
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})
is
essentially
unitary (EU)
if its boundary operator
T_{f}
is a two-sided
unitary:

T_{f}^{\dagger}T_{f}=I_{P_{A,B}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad T_{f}T_{f}^{\dagger}=I_{N_{A,B}}.

Equivalently, EU is ordinary unitarity of the squared boundary
operator
T_{f}
on the cut object
A^{*}\otimes B

(Remark
4.4
). The two-sided condition
forces
|P_{A,B}|=|N_{A,B}|
.

A concrete witness: the evaluation morphism

\ev_{A,B}:(A^{*}\otimes B)\otimes A\to B
has no chance of being
unitary as a
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})
-morphism (its source and target are not
even equidimensional as objects), yet
T_{\ev_{A,B}}
is a
permutation matrix on the squared boundary, hence unitary. EU
lives in the matrix world, not in the source-level
\odot
world.

For a single
\mathbf{KL}
basis isomorphism, source unitarity already
forces EU: inverse source wiring gives inverse boundary wiring. This
implication fails in the linear closure
(Appendix
D.3
).

First-order morphisms lack mixed polarity, so the cut

A^{*}\otimes B
trivially identifies
T_{f}
with
f
as a matrix:

Lemma 5.2

(EU at first order coincides with unitarity in
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})
)
.

Let
f:A\to B
be a morphism of
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})
between first-order
single-polarity objects, e.g.
A=(p,0)^{\oplus n}
and

B=(p,0)^{\oplus m}
after the standard first-order presentation.
Then
f
is unitary in
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})
if and only if
f
is
essentially unitary.

Proved in Appendix
C
.

Remark 5.3

(
\eta/\epsilon
scalar loop forces unit-freeness)
.

For non-empty

,
\eta_{M}:\mathbf{1}\to M^{*}\otimes M

and

(

\mathbb{QC}

5.2
Uniqueness of EU

The Uniqueness theorem below rests on a single structural engine.
Currying, distributivity, and coherence together retype any
interface-decomposable morphism down to a first-order block whose
boundary operator is
the same matrix
(on canonically
identified port sets) as the original. Any predicate that is
compatible with those moves is therefore determined by its
behaviour on first-order morphisms. The Uniqueness proof then
reduces to checking the first-order clause.

Definition 5.5

(Uniform type)
.

A distributed object
A=\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i}
of
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})

is
uniform
if all summands share the same
Kelly–Laplaza interface, i.e.
A\cong X^{\oplus n}
for some
single interface
X=(m^{+},m^{-})
and
n\geq 1
.

Definition 5.6

(Interface-decomposable morphism)
.

A morphism
u:A\to B
of
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})
is

interface-decomposable
if it admits a decomposition

u\cong u_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus u_{r}
in which each
u_{k}
has
uniform source and uniform target.

Every first-order object
(p,0)^{\oplus n}
is uniform, and every
object of the form
X^{\oplus n}
is uniform.
Interface-decomposability is preserved by currying (
after distributing to
\oplus/\otimes
normal form, currying acts
entrywise on the resulting structural matrix components), by dagger, and by composition (the
intermediate object’s interface decomposition constrains both
factors). Every morphism of the quantum core
\mathbb{QC}

(§
6
) is interface-decomposable
(Lemma
C.1
).

Proposition 5.7

(Reduction to first order)
.

Let
u_{k}:X^{\oplus n}\to Y^{\oplus m}
be a uniform-to-uniform
block of
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})
with

,

(Remark

). Then

\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})
with

on canonically identified

(i)

\mathrm{EU}(u_{k})\iff\mathrm{EU}(\hat{u})
.

(ii)

For any predicate
R
on
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})
satisfying the block

R(u_{k})\iff R(\hat{u})
.

Applying

(ii)

\hat{u}
with

\tau_{\hat{u}}=\tau_{u_{k}}

(i)
Two-sided unitarity is a property of the

\mathrm{EU}(u_{k})\iff\mathrm{EU}(\hat{u})
.

(ii)
The retyping is a finite composition of

by
(R2)
(for coherence/distributivity)
and
(R3)
(for curry/uncurry), so

.
∎

6.4

A morphism of

The proof therefore proceeds constructor by constructor. The
exponential constructor is handled by ordinary matrix algebra:
if

is a
Hermitian unitary, so

Fairbanks–Selinger feedback unitarity; tensor is handled by the

)
governing

.

Theorem 6.8

(EU closure for
\mathbb{QC}

Every morphism of
\mathbb{QC}

Proof.

The proof rests on three pillars: structural support

We separate loop-freeness from unitarity. The support statement is

We prove the slightly stronger invariant that every morphism produced

\Sigma

We argue by induction on the filtration

(\mathbb{QC}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
after Definition

already-built operands. Separately,

D.8
supplies loop-free tagged
structural support for every
\mathbb{QC}

induction, while the loop-free support hypotheses come from the
support lemmas.

Base case.

The unit-free structural image

Exponentials.

Direct sum and dagger.

7.3
Resource-linear contexts as supermaps

A supermap context picks out an input by a typed slot variable

\Box_{i}
and combines slots and constants under a discipline that
splits resources for the multiplicative connectives (
\circ,\otimes
)
and shares them for the additive connective (
\oplus
). This is the
standard linear/additive-logic shape: multiplicative branches are
formed simultaneously and partition the available slots, while
additive branches are alternatives and so are typed under the same
slot context.

Theorem 7.5

(Resource-linear contexts induce supermaps)
.

Fix a slot
\Box:A\to B
and a class
\mathsf{U}\subseteq\mathbb{QC}
of
constants. The grammar

C[\Box]\;::=\;\Box\;\mid\;C[\Box]\circ M\;\mid\;M\circ C[\Box]\;\mid\;C[\Box]\otimes M\;\mid\;M\otimes C[\Box]\;\mid\;C_{1}[\Box]\oplus C_{2}[\Box]\qquad(M\in\mathsf{U})

defines the
one-slot resource-linear contexts
. Each
multiplicative production places the slot on exactly one side; the
additive production requires the slot in both branches. For every
well-formed term
C[\Box]:E\to F
, substitution

\Phi_{C}:\mathbb{QC}(A,B)\longrightarrow\mathbb{QC}(E,F),\qquad\Phi_{C}(f):=C[f]

is
\mathbb{C}
-linear (extending to the ambient
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})
hom-space)
and
\Phi_{C}(f)
is EU for every
f\in\mathbb{QC}(A,B)
.

Proof in Appendix
E.3
.

Remark 7.6

(Resource discipline as supermap discipline)
.

The resource-linearity rules encode the standard supermap
properties syntactically. The split rule for
\circ,\otimes

blocks
copying
(multiple slot occurrences in a branch
violate disjoint-context). The share rule for
\oplus
blocks

discarding
(a slot missing from one branch violates
context agreement). Slot variables enter only through
substitution, enforcing

. The grammar
admits no

— feeding an output of

The following lemma is structural infrastructure about

Lemma C.1

(

Every morphism of
\mathbb{QC}

5.6
.
In particular, every object of
\mathbb{QC}
is, up to structural
isomorphism in
\mathbb{QC}

\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}X_{k}^{\oplus n_{k}}

Proof.

Put every object in complete
\oplus/\otimes
normal form. Thus

, with each
M_{i}
a single signed interface.
Grouping equal signed interfaces gives the canonical uniform-sector
decomposition of

. Interface-decomposability means that, after
passing to such finite sector decompositions and refinements, the
morphism is block-diagonal with uniform source and uniform target
blocks.

We prove, by induction on the construction of
\mathbb{QC}
, that every
generated morphism is interface-decomposable. We also maintain the
endomorphism invariant needed for exponentials: if
S:A\to A
is a
generated Hermitian involution, then its interface decomposition

For structural generators, after distributing to normal form, a

\mathrm{MLL}
structural components, up to structural

\oplus

). Each

interface-decomposable.

The closure operations preserve interface-decomposability. For

\oplus
, take the direct sum of the block decompositions. For

\otimes
, use distributivity:

\left(\bigoplus_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}\right)\otimes\left(\bigoplus_{\beta}g_{\beta}\right)=\bigoplus_{\alpha,\beta}(f_{\alpha}\otimes g_{\beta}),

and tensor products of uniform blocks are uniform. Dagger swaps
source and target blocks; duality preserves uniformity. Currying
and uncurrying retype the underlying structural matrix components
(Theorem
6.7
); they act entrywise on the

\oplus

uniform-to-uniform.

For composition, refine the two decompositions along the common
intermediate object. If

and

are written over this common
refinement, then

g\circ f=\bigoplus_{\alpha}(g_{\alpha}\circ f_{\alpha}),

and each composite is again uniform-to-uniform.

S

as a signed

after refinement.

be a Hermitian involution already generated
in
\mathbb{QC}
. By the maintained invariant choose an invariant uniform
decomposition

\mathrm{exp}_{i}(\theta,S)=\cos\theta\,\mathrm{id}_{A}+\mathrm{i}\sin\theta\,S=\bigoplus_{\lambda}\bigl(\cos\theta\,\mathrm{id}_{A_{\lambda}}+\mathrm{i}\sin\theta\,S_{\lambda}\bigr)=\bigoplus_{\lambda}\mathrm{exp}_{i}(\theta,S_{\lambda}),

This covers the structural generators, exponentials, and all

\mathbb{QC}

\mathbb{QC}

D.3
Linear closure discriminates source unitarity and EU

The implication from source unitarity to EU holds for a single

\mathbf{KL}
basis isomorphism, where inverse source wiring gives
inverse boundary wiring. It fails after passing to the linear closure.

Work after the fixed evaluation
\delta=d>0
. Let
X
be the
one-positive-port interface and set
A=X\otimes X^{*}
. Write the
source copy of
A
with positive port
p_{s}
and negative port

n_{s}
, and the target copy with positive port
p_{t}
and negative
port
n_{t}
. Thus

P_{A,A}=\{n_{s},p_{t}\},\qquad N_{A,A}=\{p_{s},n_{t}\}.

Let
I=\mathrm{id}_{A}
, and let
E:A\to A
be the turnback endomorphism
whose polarized wiring pairs
p_{s}
with
n_{s}
and pairs
n_{t}

with
p_{t}
. Then
E^{\dagger}=E
, and composing two turnbacks closes
one scalar loop and leaves a turnback:

E\circ E=dE.

Hence
P=d^{-1}E
is a self-adjoint idempotent, and

F:=I-2P=I-\frac{2}{d}E

satisfies

F^{\dagger}=F,\qquad F^{2}=I.

Thus
F
is source-unitary in
\mathrm{Perm}(\mathbb{C})(A,A)
.

Now order the boundary bases as
P_{A,A}=(n_{s},p_{t})
and

N_{A,A}=(p_{s},n_{t})
. From the evaluated boundary operator
(Definition
4.5
),

T_{I}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad T_{E}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

By
\mathbb{C}
-linearity of
T
,

T_{F}=\begin{pmatrix}-2/d&1\\
1&-2/d\end{pmatrix}.

Since this matrix is real symmetric,

T_{F}^{\dagger}T_{F}=\begin{pmatrix}1+4/d^{2}&-4/d\\
-4/d&1+4/d^{2}\end{pmatrix}\neq I.

Therefore
F
is source-unitary but not essentially unitary. The
failure is a linear-closure phenomenon:
F
is a linear combination of
basis linkings, and its source inverse is obtained by cancellation in
the linear category rather than by inverse boundary wiring of a single

\mathbf{KL}
linking.

is unitary on

is unitary as a block matrix,

is unitary.

Proof.

((Q\boxtimes P)^{\dagger}(Q\boxtimes P))_{(i,k),(i^{\prime},k^{\prime})}=\sum_{j,\ell}\overline{q_{\ell k}}\,q_{\ell k^{\prime}}\,P_{ji}^{\dagger}P_{ji^{\prime}}=(Q^{\dagger}Q)_{kk^{\prime}}\,(P^{\dagger}P)_{ii^{\prime}}=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{ii^{\prime}}I,

Lemma D.19

(Layout-shadow calculus)
.

On morphisms with loop-free structural expansions, the assignment

f\mapsto\Sigma_{f}
is
\mathbb{C}
-linear, sends identity to identity, is
dagger compatible, preserves
\oplus
, sends
\otimes
to the
Kronecker product on layouts (Definition
D.17
),
and sends duals to transpose:

\Sigma_{\mathrm{id}}=I,\qquad\Sigma_{f^{\dagger}}=\Sigma_{f}^{\dagger},\qquad\Sigma_{f\oplus g}=\Sigma_{f}\oplus\Sigma_{g},

\Sigma_{f\otimes g}=\Sigma_{g}\boxtimes\Sigma_{f},\qquad\Sigma_{f^{*}}=\Sigma_{f}^{T}.

For loop-free termwise composition,

\Sigma_{g\circ f}=\Sigma_{g}\Sigma_{f}
.

Proof.

Linearity is entrywise. The identity has one diagonal structural
term of coefficient
1
in each layout and zero off the diagonal,
so
\Sigma_{\mathrm{id}}=I
. Dagger conjugates coefficients and transposes
the additive matrix, giving
\Sigma_{f^{\dagger}}=\Sigma_{f}^{\dagger}
.
Direct sum is block diagonal on additive layouts, hence

\Sigma_{f\oplus g}=\Sigma_{f}\oplus\Sigma_{g}
. Duality reverses
source and target layouts without conjugating coefficients, giving

\Sigma_{f^{*}}=\Sigma_{f}^{T}
.

For tensor, write

f_{ji}=\sum_{r}\alpha_{ji,r}s_{ji,r},\qquad g_{\ell k}=\sum_{t}\beta_{\ell k,t}t_{\ell k,t}.

Since tensor is computed entrywise,

(f\otimes g)_{(j,\ell),(i,k)}=\sum_{r,t}\alpha_{ji,r}\beta_{\ell k,t}(s_{ji,r}\otimes t_{\ell k,t}),

so
(\Sigma_{f\otimes g})_{(j,\ell),(i,k)}=(\Sigma_{f})_{ji}(\Sigma_{g})_{\ell k}
,
which is
(\Sigma_{g}\boxtimes\Sigma_{f})_{(j,\ell),(i,k)}
by
Definition
D.17
.

For composition, write

f_{ki}=\sum_{r}\alpha_{ki,r}s_{ki,r},\qquad g_{jk}=\sum_{t}\beta_{jk,t}t_{jk,t}.

By bilinearity of source composition and the loop-free structural
hypothesis on each support composite,

(g\circ f)_{ji}=\sum_{k}\sum_{r,t}\beta_{jk,t}\alpha_{ki,r}(t_{jk,t}\circ s_{ki,r}).

Taking coefficient sums gives

(\Sigma_{g\circ f})_{ji}=\sum_{k}\Bigl(\sum_{t}\beta_{jk,t}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{r}\alpha_{ki,r}\Bigr)=(\Sigma_{g}\Sigma_{f})_{ji}.

∎

Remark D.20

Definition E.1

(Boundary-type realization)

Every unit-free type admits an
\oplus/\otimes
normal form. A

Proposition E.2

(Tensor block algebra)
.

For each

, let

Then the structural endomorphisms of

are exactly
the permutations of the
r

\mathcal{S}_{r}=\operatorname{Im}\bigl(\mathbb{C}[S_{r}]\to\End(\mathbb{C}^{r})\bigr).

Moreover,
\mathcal{S}_{r}\cong\mathbb{C}\oplus M_{r-1}(\mathbb{C})

Finally, every unitary in

is realized by a
morphism of
\mathbb{QC}_{q}^{\mathrm{IMLL}(+)}(q^{\otimes r},q^{\otimes r})
built
from the identity phase and exponentials of tensor transpositions.

Proof.

Paper-specific.

By Mac Lane coherence, every structural endomorphism of

q^{\otimes r}
in the unit-free
\mathrm{IMLL}
fragment is uniquely
determined by the induced permutation of tensor positions, so

\Str_{\mathrm{IMLL}}(q^{\otimes r},q^{\otimes r})\cong S_{r},

and the boundary image is the permutation representation of
S_{r}

on
\mathbb{C}^{r}
. Taking the
\mathbb{C}
-linear span gives

\mathcal{S}_{r}=\operatorname{Im}\bigl(\mathbb{C}[S_{r}]\to\End(\mathbb{C}^{r})\bigr).

The identity and each tensor transposition

are
Hermitian involutions in the structural fragment, so

\mathrm{exp}_{i}(\theta,\mathrm{id}),\mathrm{exp}_{i}(\theta,\tau)\in\mathbb{QC}_{q}^{\mathrm{IMLL}(+)}

The remaining assertions are standard representation theory and

[
14

Lemma E.5

Let
