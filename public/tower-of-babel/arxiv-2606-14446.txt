Abstract
. We consider a hydrodynamic model of flocking-type with pressure on the torus, with integrable interaction kernel and density bounded away from zero. We prove that, if an entropy weak solution exists, then its L 2 L^{2} norm decays exponentially fast in time towards the mean values on the period. The proof relies on the study of a suitable energy functional that combines a strictly convex entropy for the system and a potential term, and this allows us to treat the nonlocal source term for a class of strictly positive convolution kernels in L 1 L^{1} .

1.
Introduction

The mathematical modeling of self-organized systems is a topic that is receiving a lot of attention in the recent years,
and it has brought new challenges in the area of partial differential equations that yield interesting questions in the mathematical community.
Typical examples of self-organized systems can be met in biology: flocks of birds, swarms of bacteria or schools of fishes, but in general,
self-organized system can be found in other sciences as long as emergent behavior is present in the system. Many of these mathematical models
have been derived by the pioneering work of Cucker and Smale
[
9
]
. We refer to
[
4
,
16
]
for general references on the subject.

In this paper we are interested in the Euler-type flocking system that takes the form

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho\mathtt{v})=0,&\\
\partial_{t}(\rho\mathtt{v})+\partial_{x}\left(\rho\mathtt{v}^{2}+p(\rho)\right)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}K(x-x^{\prime})\rho(x)\rho(x^{\prime})\left(\mathtt{v}(x^{\prime})-\mathtt{v}(x)\right)\,dx^{\prime},\end{cases}

(1.1)

on the periodic domain
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/2\mathbb{Z}
, represented by the interval
[-1,1)
.
Here,
(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}\times[0,+\infty)
,
\rho>0
stands for the density,
\mathtt{v}
for the velocity
and
p
for the pressure. Our aim is to study the long term behavior of entropy weak solutions to (
1.1
)
in the non-vacuum regime. We assume that the pressure term satisfies

p\in C^{1}([0,+\infty);[0,+\infty))\,,\hskip 20.00003ptp^{\prime}>0

(1.2)

and the convolution kernel
K
satisfies

K:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptK\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})\,,\hskip 20.00003ptK\mbox{ even}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\bar{K}:=\essinf_{x\in[-1,1)}K(x)>0\,.

(1.3)

A class of interaction kernels
K
that has been studied in
[
6
]
in the context of Euler-alignment system is

K(x)=|x|^{-\beta}\hskip 10.00002pta.e.\ \mbox{on }\mathbb{T},\hskip 10.00002pt\beta>0

and it is easy to check that for
0<\beta<1
the kernels in this class satisfy conditions (
1.3
).
The constant case
K=\bar{K}>0
for all-to-all interaction models is also included in assumption (
1.3
) and the existence of
BV
entropy weak solutions is treated in
[
3
]
. However, for the non-constant case and under the assumption
p(\rho)=\kappa\rho
with
\kappa>0
, the existence is under investigation.

Concerning rigorous limit from kinetic formulation to hydrodynamic, we refer to
[
6
,
15
]

where the authors consider weak solutions to kinetic equations and their limit to suitably regular solutions to the hydrodynamic formulation. In
[
6
]
the authors rigorously derive the isentropic Euler-alignment system with singular communication weights from a BGK-alignment model in a multidimensional setting using a relative entropy approach. In particular the result on the one dimensional torus holds with
p(\rho)=\rho^{3}

and with a singular kernel of the form
K(x)=|x|^{-\beta}
with
\beta\in(0,\tfrac{3}{2})
, while in
[
15
]
the authors rigorously derive the Euler-flocking system with isothermal pressure, and smooth and symmetric kernel, again in a multidimensional setting and by means of relative entropy techniques. For general references on hydrodynamic models, see
[
14
,
17
]
.

Having set
m:=\rho\mathtt{v}
as the momentum, we rewrite the system for the conserved quantities as

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}m=0,\\
\partial_{t}m+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+p(\rho)\right)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}K(x-x^{\prime})(\rho(x)m(x^{\prime})-\rho(x^{\prime})m(x))\,dx^{\prime}\end{cases}

(1.4)

and consider the initial condition

(\rho,m)(x,0)=(\rho_{0}(x),m_{0}(x))\,,\hskip 20.00003ptx\in\mathbb{T}\,.

(1.5)

In view of studying the long term behavior of entropy weak solutions to (
1.4
)-(
1.5
),
we assume that an entropy weak solution
(\rho,m)
to (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) exists up to some time
T^{*}
, with
T^{*}\leq+\infty
and that the density
\rho
is uniformly bounded and away from zero, i.e. there are positive constants
0<\bar{\rho}_{inf}\leq\bar{\rho}_{sup}
such that

\bar{\rho}_{inf}\leq\rho(x,t)\leq\bar{\rho}_{sup}\hskip 20.00003pt(x,t)\in\Omega:=\mathbb{T}\times[0,T^{*})\,.

(1.6)

We remark that, if the convolution kernel is constant, the result in
[
3
]
captures
the global existence of periodic entropy weak solutions, for periodic initial data having finite total variation and initial density bounded away from zero, in the case of linear pressure term
while the long-time behavior is not treated therein.
The existence result to the case of an integrable, positive convolution kernel is treated in a forthcoming publication by the authors. We also refer to
[
1
,
2
]
for other results on the Euler-alignment system with linear pressure. In
[
1
]
the authors study the existence of solutions in the case of a constant convolution kernel, for compactly supported initial data with density uniformly positive away from zero. Moreover, they analyze the large time-behavior of solutions under a suitable condition on the initial data. However, in
[
2
]
, this condition is removed and the unconditional time asymptotic flocking is proved.

In this paper, our approach to study the long-time behavior of weak solutions is inspired by the work of Dafermos to hyperbolic systems of balance laws with dissipative source. In particular we adopt the strategy in
[
11
, Section 3]
to deal with our system, where the presence of the nonlocal source term requires a careful treatment.

Here is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
(\rho_{0},m_{0})\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and that
\rho_{0}(x)\in[\bar{\rho}_{inf},\bar{\rho}_{sup}]
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}
, for some
0<\bar{\rho}_{inf}\leq\bar{\rho}_{sup}
and
suppose that (
1.2
), (
1.3
) hold. Let
T^{*}>0
or

.

If
(\rho,m)
is an entropy weak solution of the problem (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) on

that satisfies (

),
then there exist positive constants
\mu
and

, independent of

, such that

\|\rho(\cdot,t)-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+\|m(\cdot,t)-\bar{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\mu\left[\|\rho_{0}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+\|m_{0}-\bar{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\right]e^{-ct}

(1.7)

for all

, where
\bar{\rho}
and
\bar{m}
are the average values on

of
\rho_{0}
and

We remark that, if

then (

\|\mathtt{v}(\cdot,t)-\bar{\mathtt{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\rightarrow 0,\hskip 20.00003pt\text{as }t\to+\infty,\hskip 20.00003pt\bar{\mathtt{v}}=\frac{\bar{m}}{\bar{\rho}}\,.

(1.8)

Several contributions on the subject are devoted to suitably regular solutions; for instance in
[
5
]
the author studies the well-posedness and the long-time behavior of strong solutions for the compressible isothermal Euler system with nonlocal dissipation on a periodic domain in a multidimensional setting. This result is obtained under suitable smallness and regularity assumptions on the initial data.

Concerning periodic solutions of systems of balance laws, in
[
10
, Section 12.10]
the large-time behavior of solutions to genuinely nonlinear,
2\times 2
systems of conservation laws is studied, and the total variation is shown to decay as
1/t
. In
[
11
,
12
]
strictly hyperbolic systems — including the compressible Euler system with partially dissipative source terms — are analyzed under general pressure laws and small initial data, and exponential decay of the total variation is established.

In addition, systems of conservation laws with a non-local source term have been studied in
[
7
,
8
]
,
where the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for small BV data has been addressed by means of a semigroup approach.

Furthermore, we observe that most of the research on hydrodynamic models for flocking is devoted to the pressureless Euler-alignment system, see
[
16
]
and references therein. In particular, in
[
13
]
the pressureless analogue of (
1.4
) is considered and analysis is conducted by means of an alignment functional which is similar, in structure, to the functional
{\mathcal{H}}(t)
defined at (
2.12
).

The strategy of the paper is as follows. We first rewrite system (
1.1
) in terms of the perturbation variables
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})

that represent the deviation from the average values. We then construct a strictly convex entropy
\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})
inspired by the general structure
of relative entropy, and we introduce the potential functional

\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{x}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dy-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{z}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dy\,dz\;,

which is 2-periodic and with zero average on the period and it can be controlled by the
L^{2}
norm of
\tilde{\rho}
. Using the entropy
\tilde{\eta}
and the potential functional
\Phi
, we define the energy functional
\mathcal{E}(t)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left[\sigma\tilde{\eta}+\Phi^{2}-\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\Phi\tilde{m}\right]\,dx

with
\sigma>0
sufficiently large.

The strict convexity of
\tilde{\eta}
, together with the bound on
\Phi
, allows us to show that
\mathcal{E}(t)
is equivalent to the squared
L^{2}
norm of the perturbations
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})
. We further prove that
\mathcal{E}(t)
decays exponentially in time, from which we deduce the convergence in
L^{2}
of the solution
(\rho,m)
toward the equilibrium state determined by the spatial averages of the initial data
(\rho_{0},m_{0})
.

The structure of the paper is the following.

- In Section
2
, we change the variables (
\rho,m)
to
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})
that are the perturbations of the density and the momentum with respect to their averages
\bar{\rho}
and
\bar{m}
, respectively and rewrite the system for new variables.
After having given the definition of entropy weak solution, we introduce the relative entropy
\tilde{\eta}
in terms of the new variables, the energy functional
\mathcal{E}(t)
and other functionals that are later employed in the proofs. Last, we restate Theorem
1.1
in terms of the new variables, see Theorem
2.1
, and state the decay of the
L^{2}
norm of the solutions for the shifted system.

- In Section
3
, we analyze the energy functional
\mathcal{E}(t)
in more detail.
Thanks to the analysis in Section
2
and more specifically, the estimates on the functionals therein and the relative entropy, we prove the decay in time of the energy functional

\mathcal{E}(t)
and then complete the proof of Theorem
2.1

that implies immediately Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Set Up of the problem

We consider 2-periodic initial data
U_{0}(x)=(\rho_{0}(x),\,m_{0}(x))
and define

\bar{\rho}:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{0}(x)\,dx\,,\hskip 20.00003pt{\bar{m}}:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}m_{0}(x)\,dx

(2.1)

where
\bar{\rho}\in[\bar{\rho}_{inf},\bar{\rho}_{sup}]
. Without loss of generality
\bar{m}
can be assumed to be
zero, since with a change of coordinates we can reduce the problem to zero average momentum.
Indeed one can apply the change of the variables
(x,t)
and
\mathtt{v}
given by

x\mapsto x-\bar{\mathtt{v}}t
,
\mathtt{v}\mapsto\mathtt{v}-\bar{\mathtt{v}}

with
\bar{\mathtt{v}}
the average velocity as given in (
1.8
).

For convenience, we use the following notation for the source term of system (
1.4
):

\mathcal{G}[\rho,m](x):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}K(x-x^{\prime})\left(\rho(x)m(x^{\prime})-\rho(x^{\prime})m(x)\right)\,dx^{\prime},

(2.2)

from here and on to simplify the forthcoming analysis.

We also note that an entropy-entropy flux pair
(\eta,q)
for system (
1.4
)
is a smooth pair of functions
\eta=\eta(\rho,m)
and
q=q(\rho,m)
, with
\eta
convex, that satisfies the equations

\left(-\frac{m^{2}}{\rho^{2}}+p^{\prime}\right)\eta_{m}=q_{\rho},\hskip 20.00003pt\eta_{\rho}+\frac{2m}{\rho}\eta_{m}=q_{m}\,.

Now we provide the definition of an
entropy weak solution

(\rho,m)(x,t)
for
x\in\mathbb{T}
and
t\in[0,T^{*})
.

Definition 2.1
.

Assume that
(\rho_{0},m_{0})\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
with
\rho_{0}(x)>0
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}
.
Assume that
K\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
. Let
T^{*}>0
or
T^{*}=+\infty
.

We say that
(\rho,\,m)
is an entropy weak solution of (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) on
\Omega=\mathbb{T}\times[0,T^{*})
if:

•

(\rho,m)(\cdot,0)=(\rho_{0},m_{0})
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
;

•

L^{1}
-Lipschitz continuous dependence in time holds true, i.e. for
U=(\rho,\,m)
,

\|U(t_{2})-U(t_{1})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\leq L|t_{2}-t_{1}|,\hskip 20.00003ptt_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T^{*})\,,

for some positive constant
L
;

•

(\rho,m)
is a solution to (
1.4
) in the sense of distributions on
\mathbb{T}\times(0,T^{*})
;

•

for every pair of smooth entropy-entropy flux
(\eta,q)
, it holds

\partial_{t}\eta(\rho,m)+\partial_{x}q(\rho,m)\leq\eta_{m}\,\mathcal{G}[\rho,m],

in the sense of distributions on
\mathbb{T}\times(0,T^{*})
.

Using the symmetry of the kernel
K
, it is easy to verify that entropy weak solutions
(\rho,m)
to (
1.4
)
on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T^{*})
conserve mass and momentum, that is,

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(x,t)\,dx=\bar{\rho},\hskip 20.00003pt\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}m(x,t)\,dx=0\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,t\in[0,T^{*}).

It should be pointed out that the sense of distributions in the definition above requires that the corresponding relations hold for every test function
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,T^{*}))
with support contained in
\mathbb{T}\times[T_{1},T_{2}]
for some
0<T_{1}<T_{2}<T^{*}
. In the case of the entropy inequality, one, of course, also needs
\phi\geq 0
.

The analysis lies on studying the evolution equations of the shifted variables
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})
defined by

\tilde{\rho}(x,t):=\rho(x,t)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{0}(x)\,dx\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\tilde{m}(x,t):=m(x,t)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}m_{0}(x)\,dx\,.

(2.3)

The second identity in (
2.1
) yields that
\tilde{m}(x,t)=m(x,t)
.

The new variables
\tilde{\rho}
,
\tilde{m}
have both zero average over
\mathbb{T}
, i.e.

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{\rho}(x,t)\,dx=0,\hskip 20.00003pt\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{m}(x,t)\,dx=0,\hskip 20.00003ptt\in[0,T^{*})\,.

(2.4)

Then direct computations yield the system for the new variables
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})
, that is

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\tilde{\rho}+\partial_{x}\tilde{m}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\tilde{m}+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}+p(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})\right)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}K(x-x^{\prime})\Big((\tilde{\rho}(x)+\bar{\rho})\tilde{m}(x^{\prime})-\tilde{m}(x)(\tilde{\rho}(x^{\prime})+\bar{\rho})\Big)\,dx^{\prime}.\end{cases}

(2.5)

In a similar fashion, we define the shifted kernel
\tilde{K}(x):=K(x)-\bar{K}
and from assumptions (
1.3
), we know that

\tilde{K}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\tilde{K}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\tilde{K}\mbox{ even}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\tilde{K}(x)\geq 0\,.

(2.6)

Hence, the source function from (
2.2
) takes the form

\mathcal{G}[\rho,m](x)=-2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}\,\tilde{m}(x)+\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho},\tilde{m}](x)\,,

with

\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\rho,m](x):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{K}(x-x^{\prime})(\rho(x)m(x^{\prime})-\rho(x^{\prime})m(x))\,dx^{\prime}.

(2.7)

Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}[\rho,m](x)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}K(x-x^{\prime})((\tilde{\rho}(x)+\bar{\rho})\tilde{m}(x^{\prime})-\tilde{m}(x)(\tilde{\rho}(x^{\prime})+\bar{\rho}))\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bar{K}\left[(\tilde{\rho}(x)+\bar{\rho})\tilde{m}(x^{\prime})-\tilde{m}(x)(\tilde{\rho}(x^{\prime})+\bar{\rho})\right]\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{K}(x-x^{\prime})\left[(\tilde{\rho}(x)+\bar{\rho})\tilde{m}(x^{\prime})-\tilde{m}(x)(\tilde{\rho}(x^{\prime})+\bar{\rho})\right]\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bar{K}(\tilde{\rho}(x)+\bar{\rho})\tilde{m}(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}-\tilde{m}(x)\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bar{K}\tilde{\rho}(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}+2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}\right]+\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho},\tilde{m}](x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}\tilde{m}(x)+\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho},\tilde{m}](x)\;,

since (
2.4
) holds true. Using these calculations, system (
2.5
) reads as

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\tilde{\rho}+\partial_{x}\tilde{m}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\tilde{m}+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}+p(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})\right)=-2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}\,\tilde{m}(x)+\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho},\tilde{m}]\end{cases}

(2.8)

and we assign the corresponding initial data

(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})(x,0)=\left(\tilde{\rho}_{0}(x),\tilde{m}_{0}(x)\right)\,\hskip 20.00003ptx\in\mathbb{T}\;,

(2.9)

with
\tilde{\rho}_{0}(x)=\rho_{0}(x)-\bar{\rho}
and
\tilde{m}_{0}(x)=m_{0}(x)
, for
x\in\mathbb{T}
. From (
1.6
), the shifted density
\tilde{\rho}
satisfies

-\bar{\rho}<\bar{\rho}_{inf}-\bar{\rho}\leq\tilde{\rho}(x,t)\leq\bar{\rho}_{sup}-\bar{\rho}

(2.10)

for
x\in\mathbb{T}
and
t\in[0,T^{*})
, and
\bar{\rho}\in[\bar{\rho}_{\inf},\bar{\rho}_{sup}]
is given at (

).

The above change of variables implies that
(\rho,m)
is an entropy weak solution to (
1.4
), (
1.5
) on
\Omega
if and only if

is an entropy entropy weak solution to (

), (
2.9
) on
\Omega
.

2.1.
The functionals

This subsection introduces the functionals used to capture the decay behavior of an entropy weak solution
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})

to (
2.8
)–(
2.9
) on
\Omega
with
\bar{\rho}>0
.

(1)

The functional that measures the contribution of the convolution kernel
\tilde{K}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{K}(x-x^{\prime})\left[\tilde{m}(x,t)\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{\rho}(x^{\prime},t)+\bar{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}(x,t)+\bar{\rho}}}-\tilde{m}(x^{\prime},t)\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{\rho}(x,t)+\bar{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}(x^{\prime},t)+\bar{\rho}}}\,\right]^{2}dx\,dx^{\prime}\,.

(2.12)

(2)

The potential functional
\Phi
is defined by

\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{x}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dy-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{z}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dy\,dz\;.

(2.13)

(3)

The convex entropy
\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})
of the system (
2.8
) is given by

\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})=(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})(\varepsilon(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})-\varepsilon(\bar{\rho}))-\tilde{\rho}\,\bar{\rho}\varepsilon^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}\;,

(2.14)

and it is normalized as follows:

\tilde{\eta}(0,0)=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\nabla\tilde{\eta}(0,0)=0\,.

We will see that it is strictly convex and there are
0<c_{1}<c_{2}
constants such that

c_{1}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}+\tilde{m}^{2})\leq\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})\leq c_{2}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}+\tilde{m}^{2})\,,

see the forthcoming (

(4)

The energy functional
\mathcal{E}(t)
is introduced by means of the

\tilde{\eta}
and the potential functional
\Phi

\mathcal{E}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left[\sigma\tilde{\eta}+\Phi^{2}-\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\Phi\tilde{m}\right]\,dx

(2.15)

where

(5)

Last, we need a modified kinetic energy functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(t)

\displaystyle:=\left(\sigma\kappa-\frac{1}{\bar{K}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}\,dx+\beta\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{\rho}^{2}\,dx,

(2.16)

and the positive constants

and

(2.17)

, which holds due to (

2.2.
Estimates on the potential functional

To begin with, we prove an identity for the functional
\mathcal{H}(t)
given at (
2.12
)
in terms of the nonlocal source term
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
given at (
2.7
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\bar{\rho}>0
and assume
\tilde{K}
satisfies properties (
2.6
). Then for
\mathcal{H}(t)
defined in (
2.12
),
the following identity holds:

\mathcal{H}(t)=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\tilde{m}(x)}{\tilde{\rho}(x)+\bar{\rho}}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho},\tilde{m}]\,dx\geq 0\;,

(2.18)

for all
t\in[0,T^{*})
with
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
.

Proof.

Thanks to the symmetry of the kernel
\tilde{K}
, we perform the following computations

\displaystyle-\mathcal{H}(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{m(x)}{\rho(x)}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\rho,m]\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{K}(x-x^{\prime})\frac{m(x)}{\rho(x)}[\rho(x)m(x^{\prime})-\rho(x^{\prime})m(x)]\,dx^{\prime}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{K}(x-x^{\prime})\frac{m(x^{\prime})}{\rho(x^{\prime})}[\rho(x^{\prime})m(x)-\rho(x)m(x^{\prime})]\,dx\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{K}(x-x^{\prime})\Big[\frac{m(x)}{\rho(x)}(\rho(x)m(x^{\prime})-\rho(x^{\prime})m(x))

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{m(x^{\prime})}{\rho(x^{\prime})}(\rho(x^{\prime})m(x)-\rho(x)m(x^{\prime}))\Big]\,dx\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{K}(x-x^{\prime})\left[2m(x)m(x^{\prime})-m(x)^{2}\frac{\rho(x^{\prime})}{\rho(x)}-m(x^{\prime})^{2}\frac{\rho(x)}{\rho(x^{\prime})}\right]dx\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{K}(x-x^{\prime})\left[m(x)\sqrt{\frac{\rho(x^{\prime})}{\rho(x)}}-m(x^{\prime})\sqrt{\frac{\rho(x)}{\rho(x^{\prime})}}\,\right]^{2}dx\,dx^{\prime}\leq 0.

The last inequality is true since
\tilde{K}
is positive and employing definition (
2.12
) for
\rho=\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}
and
m=\tilde{m}
. The proof is complete.
∎

We proceed with a preliminary analysis of the potential functional
\Phi
given at (
2.13
). For later convenience, we observe that
\Phi
can be rewritten as

\Phi(x,t):=\psi(x,t)-<\psi(\cdot,t)>\,,

(2.19)

where

\psi(x,t):=\int_{0}^{x}\rho(y,t)\,dy\,,\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{T}\,;\hskip 20.00003pt<\psi(\cdot,t)>:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\psi(z,t)\,dz\,.

(2.20)

Then we immediately deduce the equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\Phi(x,t)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\rho}(x,t),

(2.21)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi(x,t)

\displaystyle=-\tilde{m}(x,t).

(2.22)

Since
\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{m}=0
and
\rho
is 2-periodic in space, then also
\Phi
is 2-periodic in space.
Indeed, for any
x\in\mathbb{T}
,

\displaystyle\Phi(x+1,t)-\Phi(x-1,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x+1}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dy-\int_{0}^{x-1}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dy

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{z}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dy\,dz+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{z}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dy\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{x-1}^{x+1}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dy=0.

It is also straightforward to verify that the average of
\Phi

over a period is zero. Furthermore, we claim that

\int_{-1}^{1}\Phi^{2}(x,t)\,dx\leq 2\int_{-1}^{1}\tilde{\rho}^{2}(x,t)\,dx\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\in[0,T^{*})\,.

(2.23)

To prove the claim, we first rewrite
\Phi(x,t)
as

\Phi(x,t)=\int_{\bar{x}}^{x}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dy

with
\bar{x}\in[-1,1)
satisfying

\int_{0}^{\bar{x}}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dy=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{z}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dydz
and then estimate

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\Phi^{2}(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\int_{\bar{x}}^{x}\tilde{\rho}(y,t)\,dy\right)^{2}dx\leq\int_{-1}^{1}\left[\left(\int_{\bar{x}}^{x}1\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\bar{x}}^{x}\tilde{\rho}^{2}(y,t)\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]^{2}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\int_{\bar{x}}^{x}1\,dy\right)\left(\int_{\bar{x}}^{x}\tilde{\rho}^{2}(y,t)\,dy\right)dx\leq\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\int_{\bar{x}}^{x}1\,dy\right|\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\tilde{\rho}^{2}(y,t)\,dy\right)dx

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\tilde{\rho}^{2}(y,t)\,dy\right)\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\int_{\bar{x}}^{x}1\,dy\right|dx=\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\tilde{\rho}^{2}(y,t)\,dy\right)\int_{-1}^{1}\left|x-\bar{x}\right|\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=\left(\bar{x}^{2}+1\right)\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\tilde{\rho}^{2}(y,t)\,dy\right)\,.

Since
|\bar{x}|\leq 1
, then claim (
2.23
) follows.

2.3.
The entropy inequality

In this subsection we consider the entropy functional (
2.14
) and investigate its properties.

By (
1.2
), the system (
2.8
) is endowed with a uniformly convex entropy function
\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})
normalized by
\tilde{\eta}(0,0)=0
and
\nabla\tilde{\eta}(0,0)=0
, given by

\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})=(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})(\varepsilon(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})-\varepsilon(\bar{\rho}))-\tilde{\rho}\,\bar{\rho}\varepsilon^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}\;,

where
\varepsilon(\omega)
is a function that satisfies
\varepsilon^{\prime}(\omega)=\frac{p(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}
.

The gradient of the entropy is equal to

\nabla\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})=\left(\varepsilon(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})-\varepsilon(\bar{\rho})+(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})\varepsilon^{\prime}(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})-\bar{\rho}\varepsilon^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})^{2}},\,\frac{\tilde{m}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}\right),

while the Hessian matrix is equal to

\nabla^{2}{\tilde{\eta}}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})=\begin{bmatrix}(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})p^{\prime}(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})+\dfrac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})^{3}}&-\dfrac{\tilde{m}}{(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})^{2}}\\
-\dfrac{\tilde{m}}{(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})^{2}}&\dfrac{1}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}\end{bmatrix},

with determinant

\det\nabla^{2}{\tilde{\eta}}=p^{\prime}+2\frac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})^{4}}\;.

Using (
1.2
) and (
2.10
), we obtain that
\tilde{\eta}
is strictly convex. Then, there exist two constant values
0<c_{1}<c_{2}
, depending on the range of values of
\tilde{\rho}
and
\tilde{m}
, such that

c_{1}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}+\tilde{m}^{2})\leq\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})\leq c_{2}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}+\tilde{m}^{2})\;.

(2.24)

The constant values can be estimated as follows: rewrite
\tilde{\eta}
as
a sum,
\tilde{\eta}=g(\tilde{\rho})+h(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})
with

g(\tilde{\rho}):=(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})(\varepsilon(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})-\varepsilon(\bar{\rho}))-\tilde{\rho}\,\bar{\rho}\varepsilon^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})\,,\hskip 20.00003pth(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m}):=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}\,.

Noticing that
g(0)=g^{\prime}(0)=0
and
g^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{\rho})=\frac{p^{\prime}(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}
, we can choose

c_{1}=\min\left\{\min_{[\bar{\rho}_{inf},\bar{\rho}_{sup}]}\frac{p^{\prime}(\rho)}{\rho},\,\frac{1}{2\bar{\rho}_{sup}}\right\}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptc_{2}=\max\left\{\max_{[\bar{\rho}_{inf},\bar{\rho}_{sup}]}\frac{p^{\prime}(\rho)}{\rho},\,\frac{1}{2\bar{\rho}_{inf}}\right\}.

(2.25)

By denoting with
\tilde{q}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})
the corresponding entropy flux,
then the entropy weak solutions to (
2.8
) satisfy the inequality in the sense of distributions:

\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})+\partial_{x}\tilde{q}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})\leq\frac{\tilde{m}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}\left(-2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}\,\tilde{m}+\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho},\tilde{m}]\right).

(2.26)

Integrating over the torus
\mathbb{T}
the above inequality, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\{-2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}\frac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}+\frac{\tilde{m}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho},\tilde{m}]\right\}\,dx\leq 0

(2.27)

where, in the last inequality, we employed (
2.18
) for
\rho=\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}
and
m=\tilde{m}
.
Therefore we rewrite inequality (
2.27
) as

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})\,dx+2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{m^{2}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}\,dx+\mathcal{H}(t)\leq 0\,,

that will later yield the time decay of the entropy
\tilde{\eta}
.

This section is mainly devoted to the proof of Theorem

3.1.
The energy functional

We recall the energy functional introduced in (
2.15
):

\mathcal{E}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left[\sigma\tilde{\eta}+\Phi^{2}-\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\Phi\tilde{m}\right]dx\,,

where
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})

\tilde{\eta}
satisfying (
2.24
),
\Phi
is the potential functional given at (
2.13
) and
\sigma
a positive constant to be determined.

In the next lemma we show that, for
\sigma
sufficiently large, the functional
\mathcal{E}(t)
is equivalent to the
L^{2}
norm of
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume (
2.10
), (
2.25
) and

\sigma>\sigma_{1}:=\frac{1}{c_{1}\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\;.

(3.1)

Then the following inequalities hold:

\lambda_{1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}(x,t)+\tilde{m}^{2}(x,t))\,dx\leq\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\lambda_{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}(x,t)+\tilde{m}^{2}(x,t))\,dx

(3.2)

for all
t\in[0,T^{*})
, for some positive constants
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
depending on
\sigma
,
\bar{K}
,
\bar{\rho}
,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
.

Proof.

Using (
2.10
), (
2.23
) and (
2.24
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle=~\sigma\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{\eta}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\{\Phi^{2}-\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\Phi\,\tilde{m}\right\}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq~\sigma c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}+\tilde{m}^{2})\,dx+\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}-\Phi\,\tilde{m}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\sigma c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}+\tilde{m}^{2})\,dx+\frac{1}{2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\{-\Phi^{2}-\tilde{m}^{2}\right\}\,dx

(3.3)

\displaystyle\geq~\sigma c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}+\tilde{m}^{2})\,dx+\frac{1}{2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\{-2\tilde{\rho}^{2}-\tilde{m}^{2}\right\}\,dx

\displaystyle=~\left(\sigma c_{1}-\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{\rho}^{2}\,dx+\left(\sigma c_{1}-\frac{1}{2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{m}^{2}\,dx\,.

(3.4)

Thus, for
\sigma
satisfying (
3.1
), we get the bound of
\mathcal{E}(t)
from below.

Concerning the estimate of
\mathcal{E}(t)
from above, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\sigma\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}+\tilde{m}^{2})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Phi^{2}+\tilde{m}^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\sigma\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}+\tilde{m}^{2})\,dx+\left[2\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{\rho}^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(2\tilde{\rho}^{2}+\tilde{m}^{2})\,dx\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=\left(c_{2}\sigma+2+\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{\rho}^{2}\,dx+\left(c_{2}\sigma+\frac{1}{2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tilde{m}^{2}\,dx

(3.5)

using again (
2.23
) and (
2.24
).
Combining (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we arrive at

\left(\sigma c_{1}-\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}(x,t)+\tilde{m}^{2}(x,t))\,dx\leq\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\left(c_{2}\sigma+2+\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\tilde{\rho}^{2}(x,t)+\tilde{m}^{2}(x,t))\,dx,

\forall\,t\in[0,T^{*})
.
By setting

\lambda_{1}:=\sigma c_{1}-\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda_{2}:=c_{2}\sigma+2+\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\,,

we conclude that

0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
if
\sigma
satisfies (
3.1
), and that (
3.2
) holds. The proof is complete.
∎

Now, we recall the modified kinetic functional
\mathcal{Z}(t)
defined in (

)

\kappa
and

satisfying (

.

Proposition 3.1
.

If (

), (

), (

\sigma>\sigma_{2}:=\frac{1}{\kappa\bar{K}}\,,

(3.6)

(3.7)

3.2.
A Gronwall estimate for the energy

This subsection is devoted in study of the evolution of the energy functional and it is shown that is nonincreasing in time, i.e.,

\mathcal{E}(t_{2})-\mathcal{E}(t_{1})\leq 0,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,t_{2},\,t_{1}\in[0,T^{*})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002ptt_{2}>t_{1}.

The following theorem states the key properties of
\mathcal{E}(t)
that allows us to prove Theorem
2.1
. More precisely, this provides the time decay of the energy functional
\mathcal{E}(t)
in a Gronwall-type setting that would later enable us to extract an uniform bound on the
L^{2}
norm of solutions
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{m})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
, if
\sigma
is chosen sufficiently large and (
2.17
) hold,
then the functionals
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{Z}
given at (
2.12
), (
2.15
) and (
2.16
) respectively, satisfy

\mathcal{E}(t_{2})-\mathcal{E}(t_{1})+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left[\sigma\mathcal{H}(t)+\mathcal{Z}(t)\right]\,dt\leq 0

(3.9)

and

\mathcal{E}(t)+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}(s)e^{-2c(t-s)}\,ds\leq\mathcal{E}(0)e^{-2ct}

(3.10)

for any
t,\,t_{1},\,t_{2}\in[0,T^{*})
with
t_{2}>t_{1}
, where

is given in (
3.8
).

Proof.

To begin with, we multiply the second equation of system (
2.8
) by the potential
\Phi(t)
,
then add the term

\tilde{m}\partial_{t}\Phi+\left(\frac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}+p(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})\right)\partial_{x}\Phi

to get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\Phi\tilde{m})+\partial_{x}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}+p(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\Phi(-2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}\,\tilde{m}+\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho},\tilde{m}])-\tilde{m}^{2}+\tilde{\rho}\left(\frac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}+p(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=-2\Phi\bar{K}\bar{\rho}\tilde{m}+\Phi\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho},\tilde{m}]-\tilde{m}^{2}\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}+\tilde{\rho}\,p(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})\,,

(3.11)

using (
2.21
)–(
2.22
). Furthermore, multiplying (
2.22
) by
2\Phi
, we obtain

\partial_{t}\Phi^{2}=-2\tilde{m}\,\Phi.

(3.12)

Now, we write inequality (
2.26
) and equations (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) in distributional sense.
Indeed, for any nonnegative periodic test function
\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,T^{*}))
with support contained in
\mathbb{T}\times[t_{1},t_{2}]

for some
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<T^{*}
, we combine these three integral relations to reach the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}

\displaystyle\left(\sigma\tilde{\eta}+\Phi^{2}-\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\Phi\,\tilde{m}\right)\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}-\sigma\tilde{q}\partial_{x}\varphi+\sigma\frac{\tilde{m}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}\left(2\bar{K}\bar{\rho}\,\tilde{m}-\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho},\tilde{m}]\right)\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{\tilde{m}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}+p(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})\right)\right)\partial_{x}\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}-\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\left(2\Phi\bar{K}\bar{\rho}\,\tilde{m}-\Phi\,\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho},\tilde{m}]+\tilde{m}^{2}\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}-\tilde{\rho}\,p(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})\right)\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}2\tilde{m}\,\Phi\,\varphi\,dx\,dt\,.

(3.13)

Notice that the right hand side of the inequality above is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\left(\Phi\frac{\tilde{m}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}+\Phi\,p(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})\right)-\sigma\tilde{q}\right]\partial_{x}\varphi\,dx\,dt+\left(2\sigma\bar{K}\bar{\rho}-\frac{1}{\bar{K}}\right)\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\tilde{m}^{2}}{\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho}}\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left[\frac{1}{\bar{K}\bar{\rho}}\left(\Phi\tilde{\mathcal{G}}[\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho},\tilde{m}]+\tilde{\rho}\,p(\tilde{\rho}+\bar{\rho})\right)\right]\,dx\,dt\;,
