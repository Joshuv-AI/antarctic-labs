Safe hepatectomy in patients with constitutional indocyanine green excretory defect: two case reports highlighting multimodal functional assessment
Authors: Toshiki Eryu, Haruki Mori, Hiromitsu Maehira, Nobuhito Nitta, Toru Miyake, Sachiko Kaida
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction and importance:Constitutional indocyanine green (ICG) excretory defect is a rare condition characterized by markedly impaired ICG clearance despite preserved conventional liver function. This discrepancy makes the preoperative assessment of hepatic functional reserve difficult. We report two patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and constitutional ICG excretory defect who underwent hepatectomy based on multimodal functional assessment.Case presentation:Case 1 was a 71-year-old man with a 40-mm HCC involving segments 5 and 8. The indocyanine green retention rate at 15 minutes (ICGR15) was 94.3%, whereas other liver function tests were preserved. Galactosyl human serum albumin (GSA) scintigraphy and fibrosis-related markers supported adequate hepatic reserve, and right anterior sectionectomy was safely performed. Case 2 was an 80-year-old man with a 30-mm recurrent HCC in segment 4, contacting the middle hepatic vein. Although ICGR15 was markedly elevated at 80.0%, conventional liver function tests and GSA scintigraphy suggested acceptable hepatic reserve. Extended left hepatectomy was performed without postoperative liver failure.Clinical discussion:These cases illustrate that markedly elevated ICGR15 does not necessarily indicate impaired hepatic functional reserve in patients with a constitutional ICG excretory defect. When ICGR15 is disproportionately elevated relative to conventional liver function, alternative assessment methods are required. GSA scintigraphy may help guide surgical decision-making in this rare condition.Conclusion:A constitutional ICG excretory defect should be suspected when ICGR15 is markedly elevated despite preserved liver function. In selected patients, hepatectomy may be feasible when the surgical indication is determined using multimodal functional assessment, particularly GSA scintigraphy.

Introduction

Hepatectomy is a mainstay of treatment for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) in patients with well-preserved liver function, such as those with Child-Pugh grade A[1]. The indocyanine green (ICG) test is widely used to evaluate hepatic functional reserve and determine the appropriate extent of hepatectomy[2]. Although several methods for preoperative liver function assessment have been developed, the indocyanine green retention rate at 15 minutes (ICGR15) remains an important parameter in surgical decision-making.

Constitutional ICG excretory defect is a rare condition characterized by markedly elevated ICGR15, despite otherwise preserved liver function[3,4]. This discrepancy makes it difficult to accurately assess hepatic functional reserve and may lead to uncertainty regarding the safety and extent of liver resection. Because only a limited number of hepatectomy cases in patients with constitutional ICG excretory defect have been reported, the optimal strategy for preoperative functional assessment remains unclear.

We herein report two patients with HCC and a constitutional ICG excretory defect who underwent successful hepatectomy based on a multimodal assessment of hepatic functional reserve, including GSA liver scintigraphy.

Methods

This case report has been presented in accordance with the SCARE 2025 checklist[5].

Case 1

A 71-year-old man was admitted to Shiga University of Medical Science Hospital with a liver tumor identified on contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) during a pulmonary fibrosis follow-up. The patient had no history of alcohol consumption or hepatitis B or C virus infection. There was no family history of jaundice or hepatitis. His medical history included pulmonary fibrosis, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and hyperlipidemia. An abdominal CT scan and gadolinium–ethoxybenzyl–diethylenetriamine pentaacetic acid–magnetic resonance imaging (Gd-EOB-DTPA-MRI) demonstrated a single tumor located in segments 5 and 8 of the liver, measuring 40 mm in diameter (Fig. 1A and B). On a preoperative ICG test, the ICGR15 was 94.3%; all other examination results were normal (Table 1). The total bilirubin level was 1.1 mg/dL, and the direct bilirubin level was 0.3 mg/dL. The serum albumin level was 4.3 g/dL, and prothrombin activity was 96%. The Child-Pugh score was 5 points, which indicated grade A. The degree of liver damage was equivalent to grade A in accordance with the scoring system of the Liver Cancer Study Group of Japan. To more accurately evaluate hepatic functional reserve, we determined that the Fibrosis-4 index (FIB-4 index) was 2.62, Mac2 Binding Protein Glucosylation Isomer (M2BPGi) was 0.89, and galactosyl human serum albumin (GSA) on liver scintigraphy (LHL15) was 0.931, while the heart uptake ratio (HH15) was 0.620, all within the range considered safe for surgical procedures (Fig. 1C). Therefore, we diagnosed the patient with a constitutional ICG excretory defect with HCC, and hepatectomy was planned.

Right anterior sectionectomy was performed without complications. Pathological examination of the resected specimen revealed well-differentiated HCC. The liver parenchyma showed mild sinusoidal dilatation, and the non-cancerous liver exhibited cirrhosis. The postoperative course was uneventful, and the patient was discharged 7 days after the hepatectomy.

Case 2

An 80-year-old man was admitted to Shiga University of Medical Science Hospital for treatment of recurrent HCC. He had a history of alcoholic hepatitis and had previously undergone multiple treatments for HCC, including radiofrequency ablation once, transcatheter arterial embolization twice, and percutaneous ethanol injection therapy twice. CT and Gd-EOB-DTPA-MRI revealed a 30-mm tumor in hepatic segment 4, contacting the middle hepatic vein (Fig. 1D and E). The patient had no history of hepatitis B or C virus infection, and there was no family history of jaundice or hepatitis. His medical history included hypertension and diabetes mellitus.

On preoperative ICG testing, the ICGR15 was 80.0%, whereas the results of other liver function tests were preserved (Table 1). The total bilirubin level was 0.6 mg/dL, and the direct bilirubin level was 0.2 mg/dL. The serum albumin level was 4.1 g/dL, and prothrombin activity was 91%. The Child-Pugh score was 5 points, corresponding to grade A. Liver damage was classified as grade A according to the Liver Cancer Study Group of Japan criteria. For a more accurate assessment of hepatic functional reserve, the Fibrosis-4 index was calculated as 2.99, and GSA liver scintigraphy showed an LHL15 of 0.906 and an HH15 of 0.544, values considered acceptable for hepatectomy (Fig. 1F). Intraoperatively, the liver surface was smooth. No ascites, splenomegaly, or portosystemic shunt was observed. Extended left hepatectomy was performed without intraoperative complications. Pathological examination of the resected specimen revealed moderately differentiated HCC. The liver parenchyma showed mild sinusoidal dilatation, and the non-cancerous liver exhibited chronic hepatitis. The patient was discharged 13 days after hepatectomy without postoperative complications.

Discussion

Constitutional indocyanine green (ICG) excretory defect is a rare disorder characterized by markedly impaired ICG clearance despite otherwise preserved liver function, presumably due to impaired active transport of ICG across the hepatocyte membrane[3,4,6]. In affected patients, conventional liver function test results are generally preserved, except for the ICG test[6]. This discrepancy makes preoperative assessment of hepatic functional reserve difficult and may complicate decision-making regarding the extent of hepatectomy. The present cases suggest that a markedly elevated indocyanine green retention rate at 15 minutes (ICGR15) does not necessarily indicate impaired hepatic functional reserve in patients with constitutional ICG excretory defect. In both patients, despite extremely high ICGR15 values, conventional liver function was preserved, and hepatectomy was completed safely without postoperative liver failure.

Constitutional ICG excretory defect was reported by Namihisa et al. in the Japanese literature in 1973 and later described in the English-language literature in 1981[3,4]. Since then, only a limited number of hepatectomy cases have been reported in the English-language literature[6–16]. As summarized in Table 2, most previously reported patients underwent hepatectomy safely after preoperative evaluation using modalities other than the ICG test, most commonly GSA liver scintigraphy[6–16]. These previous reports, together with the present cases, indicate that constitutional ICG excretory defect itself does not necessarily reflect reduced hepatic functional reserve. Rather, the main clinical problem is that the ICG test becomes unreliable as an indicator for determining the extent of liver resection. When this condition is suspected, alternative methods for assessment of hepatic reserve are therefore required. In Japan, the ICG test remains one of the principal preoperative tools for evaluating hepatic functional reserve and determining the appropriate extent of hepatectomy[17–19]. However, ICG clearance is influenced not only by hepatocellular uptake and excretion but also by hepatic blood flow and intrahepatic vascular conditions[20]. Accordingly, the ICG test may not accurately reflect true hepatic functional reserve in patients with a constitutional ICG excretory defect. In contrast, GSA liver scintigraphy provides a quantitative assessment of functioning hepatocyte mass that is independent of the abnormal ICG transport mechanism[21–25]. GSA binds specifically to the asialoglycoprotein receptor on the sinusoidal membrane of hepatocytes, and receptor expression reflects the number of functioning hepatocytes[26,27]. Previous studies have demonstrated significant correlations between the receptor index LHL15 and conventional liver function parameters[21,26,27], and an LHL15 value below 0.9 is generally regarded as indicative of impaired liver function[26]. In addition, several studies have shown that GSA-based assessment is useful for predicting postoperative liver failure and for determining the safety of hepatectomy[28–30].

The present cases support the clinical utility of GSA liver scintigraphy in this setting. In both patients, LHL15 values were within the range generally considered acceptable for hepatectomy, and these findings were consistent with preserved conventional liver function and favorable postoperative outcomes. Notably, relatively extensive hepatectomy, including right anterior sectionectomy and extended left hepatectomy, was feasible without major postoperative complications. These observations are clinically relevant because reliance on the ICGR15 value alone might have led to overestimation of operative risk and possible exclusion from potentially curative surgery. Taken together, these findings suggest that hepatectomy may be feasible in selected patients with constitutional ICG excretory defect when hepatic functional reserve is evaluated using a multimodal approach centered on GSA liver scintigraphy.

The pathophysiology of constitutional ICG excretory defect has not yet been fully elucidated. Recent studies have suggested that organic anion-transporting polypeptide (OATP) transporters, particularly OATP1B3, may play an important role in hepatocellular ICG uptake[31–33]. OATP1B3 has also been implicated in ICG retention in hepatocellular carcinoma cells[32], and reduced expression of this transporter in both tumor and non-tumorous liver tissue has been reported in a patient with constitutional ICG excretory defect[16]. These findings suggest that abnormalities in hepatocellular transporter expression or function may contribute to this disorder, although further investigation is required to clarify the precise mechanism[16,31–33]. From a practical surgical perspective, however, the key point is that markedly abnormal ICG retention does not always correspond to poor hepatic functional reserve.

This report has several limitations. First, as with all case reports, the number of patients is small, and the findings should be interpreted with caution. Second, although GSA liver scintigraphy appeared useful in the present cases and in previous reports, the optimal combination of modalities for preoperative assessment in this rare condition remains uncertain. Nevertheless, because constitutional ICG excretory defect is uncommon and may easily be overlooked, the accumulation of further cases will be important to refine perioperative assessment strategies.

Conclusion

Constitutional ICG excretory defect should be considered when ICGR15 is disproportionately elevated despite otherwise preserved liver function. In such patients, hepatectomy may still be feasible if hepatic functional reserve is assessed using a multimodal approach, particularly GSA liver scintigraphy, together with conventional liver function tests and complementary fibrosis-related markers. Recognition of this rare condition is important to avoid unnecessary exclusion from potentially curative liver resection.
