Introduction

Antiretroviral therapy (ART) based on dolutegravir (DTG) plus lamivudine (3TC) is widely recommended as a preferred first-line regimen for people living with HIV (PLWH) because of its potent antiviral activity, high genetic barrier to resistance, and favorable safety profile [1–4]. The choice of DTG/3TC is also supported by regimen simplification and reduced long-term drug exposure [2]. DTG/3TC has demonstrated non-inferior efficacy compared with traditional three-drug regimens in treatment-naive PLWH and was approved by regulatory agencies in both Europe and the USA [5–8].

Epidemiological data indicate that approximately 6–15% of PLWH present with baseline HIV-1 RNA > 500,000 copies/mL [9, 10]. PLWH with high baseline viral load are at greater risk of opportunistic infections and experience slower clinical improvement [10–12]. In a real-world study from China, 88.8% (7/8) of ART-naive PLWH with high baseline viral load achieved virological suppression at week 48 [13]. Similar results were observed in another study, where 88% (14/16) of participants with baseline viral load > 100,000 copies/mL achieved virological suppression [14]. However, the use of dual regimens in individuals with high baseline viral load remains limited as a result of insufficient clinical evidence [3, 15, 16].

To address this knowledge gap, we conducted a real-world multicenter study among treatment-naive PLWH initiating DTG/3TC in China. We hypothesized that DTG/3TC would achieve comparable virological suppression in treatment-naive PLWH with baseline viral load > 500,000 copies/mL compared with those with lower baseline viral load at week 48.

Methods

Study Design

This study was a multicenter, real-world, observational cohort study evaluating the effectiveness and safety of DTG/3TC in treatment-naive PLWH in China. Both retrospective and prospective data were included. Participants were followed for 48 weeks after ART initiation. The study was registered on the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2300074854). The study protocol was approved by the Shanghai Public Health Clinical Center Ethics Committee.

Setting

This real-world multicenter cohort study was conducted at 18 clinical sites across different regions of China. Participants were enrolled between February 2019 and June 2024 based on routine clinical care documented in the Hospital Information System (HIS) at each site. Individuals initiating DTG/3TC were followed as part of routine clinical practice.

Participants

We retrospectively and prospectively included treatment-naive adults with confirmed HIV-1 infection and available baseline viral load at each site. Participants were categorized into high or low viral load (VL) groups using a 500,000 copies/mL cutoff. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) adults with confirmed HIV-1 infection; (2) ART-naive status, defined as no prior exposure to antiretroviral therapy, with DTG/3TC as the initial regimen; (3) available baseline viral load data measured by a standard method. Exclusion criteria included (1) documented resistance to DTG or 3TC; (2) no available follow-up data during the 48-week follow-up period for retrospectively enrolled participants. Resistance information was extracted from medical records when available; baseline genotypic resistance testing was not mandated by the study protocol and followed routine practice at each participating center. Eligible participants were identified through the electronic medical records of HIS at each site. Clinical and laboratory assessments were recorded at baseline, week 24, and week 48 after initiation of DTG/3TC. Participants who did not complete the week 48 visit were classified as lost to follow-up unless documented clinical outcomes were available through medical record review.

Variables

Baseline HIV-1 RNA served as the primary exposure variable. For the purpose of comparative analyses, participants were categorized into two baseline viral load strata: a high VL group (> 500,000 copies/mL) and a low VL group (≤ 500,000 copies/mL). The primary outcome was virological suppression (defined as HIV-1 RNA < 50 copies/mL) at week 48 using the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) snapshot algorithm. Secondary outcomes included virological suppression at weeks 24 and 48, assessed using the per-protocol (PP) analysis; the incidence of adverse events during follow-up; and changes in CD4+ T cell counts.

Data Collection and Management

Recorded information included demographic characteristics, routine laboratory test results, and HIV-related parameters. Adverse events occurring during treatment were documented throughout the follow-up period as part of routine clinical care. Data from all participating clinical sites were uploaded to an electronic data system. All data were reviewed and exported by the data manager for statistical analysis.

Data Sources and Measurement

Plasma HIV-1 RNA was measured using the Roche COBAS TaqMan HIV-1 test, with a lower limit of detection of 20 copies/mL. CD4+ T cell counts were determined by flow cytometry at local clinical laboratories using standard procedures. Routine hematologic and biochemical parameters were measured according to each site’s certified clinical laboratory protocols, which adhered to national regulatory standards. Laboratory abnormalities were graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), version 5.0, with changes assessed relative to baseline values.

Bias

Several potential sources of bias were considered. Selection bias was minimized by consecutively including all eligible PLWH at each site. Information bias was reduced by the use of standardized laboratory procedures. Follow-up bias was addressed by determining outcomes using the FDA snapshot algorithm; participants were not classified as lost to follow-up if documented clinical outcomes were available through review of electronic medical records.

Study Size

We included all eligible participants with baseline VL ≥ 500,000 copies/mL and enrolled participants with lower baseline VL at a 1.5:1 ratio relative to the high VL group.

Statistical Analysis

Quantitative variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR), and the Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess normality. Depending on the distribution, Student’s t test or Wilcoxon exact test was applied. Categorical variables were summarized as counts and percentages, and analyzed using the chi-square test.

All enrolled participants were included in the intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis, while those who completed follow-up at both weeks 24 and 48 were also included in the PP analysis. Between-group differences in virological suppression rates were evaluated using two-sided chi-square tests. Logistic regression models adjusted for baseline CD4+ T cell counts were performed as sensitivity analyses to evaluate the association between baseline viral load group and virological suppression at weeks 24 and 48. Subgroup analyses were performed for participants with baseline HIV-1 RNA > 1,000,000 copies/mL. All statistical tests were two-sided, with a p value < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Ethics Approval and Consent to Participate

The study protocol was approved by the Shanghai Public Health Clinical Center Ethics Committee (approval number 2023-S084-01), and was performed in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration of 1964 and its later amendments. Signed informed consent was obtained from participants for prospective enrollment.

Virological Suppression at Weeks 24 and 48

At week 24, viral load data were available for 145/150 (96.7%) participants in the high VL group and 214/225 (95.1%) participants in the low VL group. At week 48, viral load data were available for 143/150 (95.3%) participants in the high VL group and 216/225 (96.0%) participants in the low VL group.

According to the FDA snapshot analysis, virological suppression was achieved in 75.8% [95% confidence interval (CI) 69.0–82.7%] and 90.0% (95% CI 85.4–94.6%) of participants in the high and low VL groups, respectively, at week 24 (P = 0.001), and in 88.0% (95% CI 82.8–93.2%) and 92.0% (95% CI 87.9–96.2%) at week 48. No statistically significant between-group difference was observed at week 48 (− 4.0% [95% CI − 2.6 to 10.6%, P = 0.233]). At week 48, 7.5% and 4.0% of participants in the high and low VL groups, respectively, had HIV-1 RNA ≥ 50 copies/mL, while 4.5% and 4.0% had missing virological data (Fig. 2a, b). Participants with High Viral Load at WEEK 48

In our study, two participants in the high VL group had plasma HIV-1 RNA levels remaining above 1000 copies/mL at week 48, whereas no participants in the low VL group met this criterion. Both participants showed no baseline resistance mutations, and initial virological responses were satisfactory.

The first participant had a baseline viral load of > 10,000,000 copies/mL. After 8 weeks of treatment, the viral load decreased markedly to 134 copies/mL and further to 56.8 copies/mL at week 24, with no reported clinical adverse events. However, at week 48, the viral load rebounded to 8260 copies/mL. The participant subsequently received compliance counseling, but died 24 weeks after the last follow-up without returning for further visits.

The second participant had a baseline viral load of 946,000 copies/mL, which declined to 155 copies/mL at week 8. The participant was advised to attend regular follow-ups, but at week 48, the viral load had increased to 1620 copies/mL. According to family members, the participant had episodes of missed doses and refused repeated recommendations for resistance testing.

As shown in Table 2, grade 3 adverse events occurred in one participant in each group. No serious drug-related adverse events were reported. Most adverse events were mild or moderate in severity. The most common adverse events were dermatitis and anemia, while the most common laboratory abnormality was lipid abnormality. Discussion

In this multicenter study, with the largest cohort of participants with high baseline viral load reported to date, we comprehensively evaluated the effectiveness of DTG/3TC in PLWH with different baseline viral load levels, which provides real-world evidence on the use of DTG/3TC in this population.

A significantly lower virological suppression rate at week 24 in the high VL group suggests a delayed virological response among this population (75.8% vs. 90.0%). This finding is clinically relevant, as individuals with a higher baseline viral burden may require a longer time to reach virological suppression [17]. Although the rate of viral suppression at week 24 was significantly lower in the high VL group than in the low VL group, in the ITT analysis, 88.0% of participants in the high VL group and 92.0% in the low VL group achieved virological suppression at week 48, with no significant difference between groups. Consistent findings in the PP analysis further supported the primary endpoint results at week 48. In addition, our subgroup analysis showed that the virological suppression rates were comparable across the three subgroups (> 1,000,000 copies/mL, 500,000–1,000,000 copies/mL, and ≤ 500,000 copies/mL) at week 48, indicating that the long-term efficacy of DTG/3TC may remain stable regardless of baseline viral load strata, even among participants with extremely high viral load. Our results are consistent with previous studies. In the USA, 89.7% (26/29) of the participants with baseline viral load > 500,000 copies/mL achieved suppression at week 48 [18]. In China, 95.5% (19/20) of treatment-naive PLWH with high baseline viral load achieved virological suppression [19]. Furthermore, in individuals with baseline HIV-1 RNA > 500,000 copies/mL, DTG/3TC demonstrated virological efficacy comparable to that of triple regimens in both a Chinese retrospective study (78.3% vs. 76.5% at week 48) and the multicenter DRAGON study (96.0% vs. 94.0% at week 48) [20, 21]. Although current international guidelines remain cautious regarding the use of DTG/3TC in treatment-naive PLWH with high baseline viral load [4, 16], our findings, together with existing evidence, support the potential of DTG/3TC as a treatment option for this population, while highlighting the need for closer viral load monitoring during the early treatment period.

In our study, two participants in the high VL group had plasma HIV-1 RNA levels > 1000 copies/mL after 48 weeks of treatment. Both initially showed favorable therapeutic effects, but subsequent follow-up interruption or missed doses may have contributed to these outcomes. Given that suboptimal adherence is associated with less effective viral suppression, missed doses may explain the viral rebound observed in one of these cases [22]. However, because adherence was not systematically measured, this explanation should be interpreted with caution.

In China, DTG/3TC is generally considered for hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg)-negative individuals because of inadequate anti-hepatitis B virus (HBV) coverage and the risk of emergent resistance-associated HBV variants [3, 23]. More broadly, baseline resistance, HBV status, and opportunistic infections (OIs) are important considerations when interpreting outcomes of DTG/3TC. Although baseline resistance testing was not mandated by the study protocol, recent national surveillance data from China reported low pretreatment resistance rates to DTG and 3TC among newly diagnosed PLWH (0.1% and 0.3%, respectively), suggesting that the potential impact of undetected baseline resistance on our findings may be limited [24]. In addition, baseline HBsAg status, comorbidities, and diagnosed OIs were collected. However, detailed HBV-related data and longitudinal information on these factors were not systematically recorded. Therefore, we were unable to further evaluate whether these factors influenced DTG/3TC selection, treatment outcomes, or delayed virological suppression in the high VL group.

In this study, both groups showed a continuous increase in CD4+ T cell counts during follow-up, indicating effective immune reconstitution after DTG/3TC initiation. The longitudinal comparisons revealed no significant difference between groups in the increases of CD4+ T cell counts from baseline to weeks 24 and 48. These findings suggest that although a higher viral burden may cause more profound immune depletion [25, 26], the immune system of PLWH can recover effectively once ART is initiated, underscoring the importance of timely ART initiation for improved clinical outcomes.

DTG/3TC demonstrated a favorable safety profile during treatment. Dyslipidemia was the most common abnormality, occurring in 18 participants (12.0%) in the high VL group and 23 participants (10.2%) in the low VL group. Although INSTI-based regimens have been associated with weight gain and metabolic disturbances [27], some evidence suggests they may have more favorable lipid effects [28, 29]. In a study comparing DTG/3TC with triple therapy, the dual regimen was associated with less weight gain and more favorable high-density lipoprotein cholesterol changes [21]. In clinical practice, regular monitoring of lipid profiles during ART is essential, and appropriate statin use should be considered in PLWH, given their potential benefits in reducing inflammation and supporting renal protection in PLWH [30].

There are several limitations of our study. First, we did not collect reasons why DTG/3TC was prescribed in the study population, e.g., avoiding nephrotoxicity and minimizing drug–drug interactions. Second, the retrospective and prospective enrollment may have introduced selection bias or potential confounding, which could not be fully excluded without multivariable adjustment. Third, although it is the largest cohort reported to date for this population, the sample size remained limited. Without a concurrent triple-therapy control group, direct comparisons with triple regimens cannot be made. Finally, the predominantly male population and single-country setting may limit the generalizability of our findings.

Conclusions

Although participants with baseline HIV-1 RNA > 500,000 copies/mL showed delayed virological suppression at week 24, DTG/3TC subsequently demonstrated favorable effectiveness and safety in treatment-naive PLWH with high baseline viral load by week 48. These findings support the potential of DTG/3TC as a treatment option for this population, while early virological monitoring remains important.
