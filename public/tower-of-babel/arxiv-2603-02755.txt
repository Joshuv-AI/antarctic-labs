Quaternionic complex manifolds and fixed-point sets of S 1 S^{1} -actions
Kazuyuki Hasegawa

Abstract
In this paper, we study fixed-point sets of S 1 S^{1} -actions and compatible complex structures on quaternionic manifolds. We obtain an equation involving the first Chern classes of the fixed-point set and of a quaternionically flat manifold with compatible complex structure of closed type. In addition, if the first Chern class of the fixed-point set is not trivial, then the quaternionic manifold does not admit hypercomplex structures containing given compatible complex structure on any open set containing the fixed-point set. Moreover, we determine the connected components of the fixed-point set arising from quaternionic S 1 S^{1} -actions on the quaternionic projective space. We apply these results to Pontecorvo’s example SO ∗ ​ ( 2 ​ n + 2 ) / SO ∗ ​ ( 2 ​ n ) × SO ∗ ​ ( 2 ) \mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2n+2)/\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2n)\times\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2) . 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 53C10, Secondary 53C26 Keywords: compatible complex structure, twistor function, quaternionic complex manifold, transversally complex submanifold.

Introduction

A quaternionic manifold
M
is a smooth manifold equipped
with a subbundle
Q
of the endmorphism bundle of
TM

which is locally generated by three almost complex structures satisfying
the quaternionic relations and there exists a torsion-free affine connection
preserving
Q
. Such a connection is called a quaternionic connection.
Note that a quaternionic connection is not unique for
Q
.
Several papers study compatible (almost) complex structures with a given
Q
and
quaternionic manifolds admitting such complex structures (see
[
3
,
8
]
for example).
The fundamental questions are

(1)

Are there compatible (globally defined) complex structures on
(M,Q)
?,

(2)

If there exists a compatible complex structure
I
,
is it possible to extend
I
to a (locally) hypercomplex structure
(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
such that
I_{1}=I
?

In particular, Joyce
[
15
]
introduces a quaternionic complex manifold, defined
as a quaternionic manifold equipped with a nowhere vanishing twistor function.
The twistor function is defined as a section lying in the kernel of the twistor operator.
In this setting, there exists a unique quaternionic connection
preserving both a distinguished compatible complex structure and a volume form.
Consequently, the holonomy group of this connection is contained in

\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{H})\cdot\mathrm{U}(1)
, where
4n=\dim M
.
This group appears in the Berger’s list of possible holonomies of torsion-free connections
(see
[
5
,
17
]
). Hitchin
[
13
]
gives examples generated from
quaternionic Kähler or hyperKähler manifolds with an
S^{1}
-action.

Related to the problems as above,
Pontecorvo
[
20
]
shows that

\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2n+2)/\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2n)\times\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2)(\subset\mathbb{H}P^{n})

is a manifold with the compatible complex structure by using the twistor space. Note that there exists no almost complex structure on
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
.
Hitchin
[
13
]
points out that this manifold is given by the twistor(moment) function on
\mathbb{H}P^{n}

with the
S^{1}
-action.
The fixed-point set of this
S^{1}
-action on
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
is
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
, which is contained in

\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2n+2)/\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2n)\times\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2)
.
Hence, this motivates us to study fixed-point sets of a quaternionic
S^{1}
-action.
In the quaternionic Feix-Kaledin construction
[
7
]
,
the c-projective structure of the complex manifold,
which arises as one of the connected components of the fixed-point set
of the constructed quaternionic manifold under the
S^{1}
-action,
plays an essential role.
From this perspective, the study of the fixed-point sets of

S^{1}
-actions on quaternionic manifolds is also of interest.

We show that a connected component of the fixed-point set
of a quaternionic
S^{1}
-action on a quaternionic manifold is a
quaternionic or complex submanifold (Theorem
2.5
). Note that a quaternionic submanifold
is totally geodesic with respect to any quaternionic connection and a complex submanifold which is the
fixed-point set is totally geodesic with respect to a specific quaternionic connection.
In particular, we prove that a connected component of the fixed-point set
of a quaternionic
S^{1}
-action on
\mathbb{H}P^{n}

is
\mathbb{H}P^{k}
(
k\leq n
) or

\mathbb{C}P^{l}
(
l\leq n
) (Theorem
4.15
).
The proof is done geometrically, and topologically,
by
[
9
, Theorem 5.2 in Chapter VII]
, the fixed-point sets
of a continuous
S^{1}
-actions on
\mathbb{H}P^{n}

have the same integral
cohomology as
\mathbb{H}P^{k}
or as
\mathbb{C}P^{l}
.

In Riemannian submanifold geometry, the fixed-point set of an isometric action is a totally geodesic submanifold.
Although a conformal change of the ambient metric does not preserve the property of being totally geodesic, it does preserve the totally umbilic condition.
For this reason, totally umbilic submanifolds are the most fundamental and important objects in conformal submanifold geometry.
The conformal geometry of
S^{4}
can be viewed as the quaternionic geometry of
\mathbb{H}P^{1}
, and
its higher-dimensional analogue is the quaternionic geometry of
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
.
In quaternionic submanifold geometry (see
[
23
]
),
it is shown that the
(2,0)+(0,2)
-part of the second fundamental form is independent of
the choice of a quaternionic connection.
Moreover,
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
is characterized as
a transversally complex submanifold in
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
with vanishing

(2,0)+(0,2)
-part of the second fundamental form.
Thus,
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
plays a role analogous to that of
a totally umbilic submanifold in conformal geometry.
From this perspective, it is also natural and important to study fixed-point sets of

S^{1}
-actions within the framework of quaternionic submanifold geometry.

We give detailed and elementary descriptions of twistor functions and quaternionic complex manifolds, reflecting their significant roles in this paper, in Sections
3.1
and
4.1
.
As observed in
[
22
,
15
]
, in order to define a twistor operator which is independent of the choice of
a quaternionic connection, one must consider an appropriate tensor product of

Q
with a real line bundle of suitable weight.
We provide an explicit expression for this operator, and in particular, following
[
1
]
, we describe the Ricci curvature of the distinguished quaternionic connection.
Moreover, we express the first Chern class of a quaternionic complex manifold
in terms of this Ricci curvature.
We introduce the compatible complex structure of closed type on a quaternionic manifold
(see Definition
3.28
), which gives
a collection of quaternionic complex manifolds and twistor functions on the quaternionic manifold (Proposition
3.31
).
We obtain an equation involving the first Chern classes of the fixed-point set and of
a quaternionically flat manifold with compatible complex structure of closed type (Theorem
4.9
).
This directly can be applied to the case of
a quaternionic complex manifold.
Indeed, in Section
4.3
, we apply our results to
\mathbb{H}P^{n}

to illustrate Pontecorvo’s example

\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2n+2)/\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2n)\times\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2)

building on Hitchin’s description in
[
14
]
.
As shown in
[
20
, Theorem 3.9]
, there exists no locally hypercomplex structure containing
the distinguished compatible complex structure.
In our paper, it is proven
that no open neighborhood of the fixed-point set admits any hypercomplex structure
(Example
4.18
).

We discuss the complex two-plane Grassmann manifold
Gr(2,n+1)
in Section
4.4
.
There is no compatible almost complex structure on
Gr(2,n+1)
.
Nevertheless, there exists a compatible complex structure on

Gr(2,n+1)\setminus Gr(2,n)
. Indeed, this open manifold carries the quaternionic complex structure
induced by a twistor function. However, this compatible complex structure cannot be extended
to a hypercomplex structure (Example
4.21
).

2
A fixed-point set of a quaternionic
S^{1}
-action

Throughout this paper,
all manifolds are assumed to be smooth and without boundary
and maps are assumed to be smooth
unless otherwise mentioned. The space of sections of a vector bundle
E\rightarrow M

is denoted by
\Gamma(E)
.

2.1
A quaternionic manifold and its complex submanifold

In dimensions greater than four,
we say that
M
is a
quaternionic
manifold
with
quaternionic structure

Q
if

Q
is a subbundle of
\mathrm{End}(TM)

of rank
3
which at every point
x\in M
is spanned by endomorphisms

I_{1}
,
I_{2}
,
I_{3}\in\mathrm{End}(T_{x}M)
satisfying

(2.1)

\displaystyle I_{1}^{2}=I_{2}^{2}=I_{3}^{2}=-\mathrm{id},\,\,I_{1}I_{2}=-I_{2}I_{1}=I_{3},

and there exists a torsion-free connection
\nabla
on
M

such that
\nabla
preserves
Q
, that is,

\nabla_{X}\Gamma(Q)\subset\Gamma(Q)
for all
X\in\Gamma(TM)
.
Such a torsion-free connection

\nabla
is called a
quaternionic connection
and
the triplet
(I_{1}
,
I_{2}
,
I_{3})
is called an

admissible frame
of
Q
at
x
.
Note that we use the same letter
\nabla
for the connection on
{\rm End}(TM)

induced by
\nabla
. In four dimensions, we make the special definition that
a quaternionic manifold is a self-dual conformal manifold.
The dimension of the quaternionic manifold
M
is denoted by
4n
.
Note that a quaternionic connection is not unique, in fact,
there is the following result
[
12
,
2
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\nabla^{1}
and
\nabla^{2}
be quaternionic connections on
(M,Q)
. Then there exists a 1-form
\xi
on
M
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{X}Y=\nabla^{1}_{X}Y+S^{\xi}_{X}Y

for all
X
,
Y\in\Gamma(TM)
, where
S^{\xi}

is defined by

\displaystyle S^{\xi}_{X}Y=\;

\displaystyle\xi(X)Y+\xi(Y)X-\xi(I_{1}X)I_{1}Y-\xi(I_{1}Y)I_{1}X

\displaystyle-\xi(I_{2}X)I_{2}Y-\xi(I_{2}Y)I_{2}X-\xi(I_{3}X)I_{3}Y-\xi(I_{3}Y)I_{3}X.

Conversely, for a given quaternionic connection
\nabla^{1}
, the connection

\nabla^{2}
given by the equation above is also a quaternionic connection.

An
almost hypercomplex manifold
is defined to be a manifold
M
endowed
with 3 almost complex structures

I_{1}
,
I_{2}
,
I_{3}
satisfying the quaternionic relations (
2.1
).
If
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
,
I_{3}
are integrable, then
M
is called
a
hypercomplex manifold
.
There exists a unique torsion-free connection on a hypercomplex manifold
for which the hypercomplex structures are parallel.
It is called the
Obata connection

[
18
]
.
Obviously, hypercomplex manifolds are quaternionic manifolds with

Q=\langle I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\rangle
.
We say that
M
is
locally hypercomplex
if,
for every point
x\in M
,
there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
x

on which a hypercomplex structure
(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
is defined.
Let
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
be a metric on
Q
defined by

(A,B)=-\frac{1}{4n}\mathrm{Tr}AB

for
A,B\in Q
.
We set
\|A\|=\sqrt{(A,A)}
for
A\in Q
.
Note that any admissible frame is orthonormal frame and
any quaternionic connection is a metric connection with respect to this metric.
For a
(0,2)
-tensor
\theta
, we define the
(0,2)
-tensor
\Pi_{h}(\theta)
by

\Pi_{h}(\theta)(X,Y)=\frac{1}{4}\left(\theta(X,Y)+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\theta(I_{\alpha}X,I_{\alpha}Y)\right)

for
X,Y\in TM
. We also define a tensor
B^{\nabla}
on a quaternionic manifold
(M,Q)
by

\displaystyle B^{\nabla}=\frac{1}{4(n+1)}(Ric^{\nabla})^{a}+\frac{1}{4n}(Ric^{\nabla})^{s}-\frac{1}{2n(n+2)}\Pi_{h}(Ric^{\nabla})^{s}

for a quaternionic connection
\nabla
, where
Ric^{\nabla}
is the Ricci tensor of
\nabla
and

(Ric^{\nabla})^{s}
(resp.
(Ric^{\nabla})^{a}
) is the symmetric (resp. skew-symmetric)
part of
Ric^{\nabla}
.
The
Weyl tensor

\displaystyle W:=R^{\nabla}-R^{B^{\nabla}}

does not depend on a particular choice
\nabla

(see
[
2
]
), that is,
it is invariant of
Q
. Here
R^{\nabla}
is the curvature of
\nabla
and
R^{B^{\nabla}}
is defined by

\displaystyle R^{B^{\nabla}}_{X,Y}=S^{B^{\nabla}(Y,\,\cdot\,)}_{X}-S^{B^{\nabla}(X,\,\cdot\,)}_{Y}

for
X
,
Y\in TM
.

A submanifold
N
in a quaternionic manifold
(M,Q)
is said to be an
almost complex submanifold

if there exists a section
I
of
Q|_{N}
such that

I^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,I(TN)\subset TN.

The almost complex structure on
N
can be induced by
I
. In addition, if the
induced almost complex structure is integrable, then
N
is called a
complex submanifold
.
An almost complex submanifold
N
is called a
transversally complex submanifold
if

T_{x}N\cap L(T_{x}N)=\{0\}

at each point
x\in N
for all element
L
of orthogonal complement of

\langle I\rangle
with respect to
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
.
Note that the induced almost complex structure on a transversally complex submanifold
is integrable. We refer to
[
23
]
for transversally complex submanifolds.

2.2
A fixed-point set

We start with the following lemma, which is well known
in the case of an isometric action (see
[
16
]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(M,\nabla)
be a manifold with a torsion-free affine connection
\nabla
, and let

X
be a vector field which generates one-dimensional subgroup
A

of affine transformations of
(M,\nabla)
.
We denote a connected component of the fixed-point set of
A
by
F
.
Then we have

(1)

T_{x}F=\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X)_{x}
for all
x\in F
,

(2)

F
is totally geodesic in
(M,\nabla)
.

Proof.

At first, we show (1). At
x\in F
, take
Y\in T_{x}F
and a curve
\gamma:I\to F
such that

\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=Y
.
Since
X_{\gamma(t)}=0
for all
t\in I
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{Y}X=\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}(0)}X=\left.\frac{d}{dt}(P_{\gamma}|^{t}_{0})^{-1}(X_{\gamma(t)})\right|_{t=0}=0,

where
(P_{\gamma}|^{t}_{0}):T_{\gamma(0)}M\to T_{\gamma(t)}M
is the parallel transformation along

\gamma
with respect to
\nabla
. This shows that
T_{x}F\subset\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X)_{x}
.
Since
L_{X}\nabla=0
, we have
\nabla_{Y}(\nabla X)=-R^{\nabla}_{X,Y}
, hence it vanishes on
F
.
Therefore
\dim\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X)
is constant on
F
, which means that

\cup_{x\in F}\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X)_{x}
is a smooth vector bundle over
F
.
Take
Y\in\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X)_{x}
and extend it to a vector field

\bar{Y}
on a neighborhood
U
of
x
such that
\bar{Y}|_{U\cap F}\in\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X)

and
\bar{Y}_{x}=Y
.
To show that
Y\in T_{x}F
, it is sufficient to prove
\varphi_{s}(\exp(tY))=\exp(tY)
for all
s
,
where
\exp
is the exponential map with respect to
\nabla
and

\{\varphi_{s}\}
is the one parameter family of
X

(Thus it holds that
\exp(tY)\in F
and
Y\in T_{x}F
).
Therefore, we shall show
\varphi_{s\ast}(Y)=Y
.
This follows from

\displaystyle[X,\bar{Y}]_{x}=(\nabla_{X}\bar{Y}-\nabla_{\bar{Y}}X)_{x}=0.

Here we have used
X_{x}=0
and
\bar{Y}_{x}=Y\in\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X)_{x}
at
x\in F
.
Next, we show (2).
Since
X
preserves
\nabla
, we have

\displaystyle 0=(L_{X}\nabla)_{Y}Z=R^{\nabla}_{X,Y}Z-\nabla_{\nabla_{Y}Z}X

for
Y,Z\in\Gamma(TF)
. Hence it holds that
\nabla_{\nabla_{Y}Z}X=0
on
F
, which means that

\nabla_{Y}Z\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X))=\Gamma(TF)
.
This shows that
F
is totally geodesic.
∎

Let
(M,Q)
be a quaternionic manifold.
We consider the normalizer

\displaystyle N(Q)=\{A\in\mathrm{End}(TM)\mid[A,I]\in Q\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,\,I\in Q\}

and the centralizer

\displaystyle Z(Q)=\{A\in\mathrm{End}(TM)\mid[A,I]=0\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,\,I\in Q\}.

Then we see that
N(Q)=Q+Z(Q)
.
If
X\in\Gamma(TM)
generates a quaternionic
S^{1}
-action,
then
\nabla X
is an element of
N(Q)
. Now we decompose

\nabla X=f_{Q}^{\nabla}+f_{Z}^{\nabla}\in\Gamma(Q)+\Gamma(Z(Q)).

On a fixed-point set
F
,
f_{Q}^{\nabla}
and
f_{Z}^{\nabla}
are
independent of the choice of a quaternionic connection. Therefore, we can omit the symbol
\nabla
for

f_{Q}^{\nabla}
and
f_{Z}^{\nabla}
on
F
, that is, we simply write
f_{Q}
and
f_{Z}
on
F
.
Before proving Theorem
2.5
, we establish several lemmas.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(M,Q)
be a quaternionic manifold endowed with an
S^{1}
-action preserving
Q
,
and let
X
denote the vector field generating the
S^{1}
-action.
Let
\nabla
be a quaternionic connection, and let
F
be a connected component
of the fixed-point set of the
S^{1}
-action which is not contained in

f_{Q}^{\nabla-1}(0)
. Then we have

(1)

F
is an almost complex submanifold of
(M\setminus f_{Q}^{\nabla-1}(0),Q)
,

(2)

I_{2}(TF)\subset\mathrm{Im}(\nabla X)
for a local admissible frame

(I_{1}=\frac{f_{Q}}{\|f_{Q}\|},I_{2},I_{3})
on
(M\setminus f_{Q}^{\nabla-1}(0),Q)
,

(3)

TF\cap I_{2}TF=TF\cap I_{3}TF=\{0\}
for such a local admissible frame.

Proof.

We first prove (1).
Set
I:=\frac{f_{Q}}{\|f_{Q}\|}
and decompose

\nabla X=f_{Q}+f_{Z}=\|f_{Q}\|I+f_{Z}\in Q+Z(Q)

on
F
. For
Y\in\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X)_{x}
, we compute

\displaystyle\|f_{Q}\|(\nabla_{IY}X)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\|f_{Q}\|IY}X=\nabla_{f_{Q}Y}X=(\nabla X)(f_{Q}Y)

\displaystyle=(f_{Q}+f_{Z})(f_{Q}Y)=f_{Q}(f_{Q}+f_{Z})(Y)=f_{Q}(\nabla X)(Y)=0.

Hence
T_{x}F=\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X)_{x}
is
I
-invariant,
which proves (1).
Next we prove (2).
Take
Y\in\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X)
. Then

0=\nabla_{Y}X=f_{Q}Y+f_{Z}Y,

and hence

I_{1}Y=\frac{1}{\|f_{Q}\|}f_{Q}Y=-\frac{1}{\|f_{Q}\|}f_{Z}Y.

Since

\frac{1}{2\|f_{Q}\|}(f_{Q}-f_{Z})(Y)=-\frac{1}{\|f_{Q}\|}f_{Z}Y,

we obtain

I_{1}Y=\frac{1}{2\|f_{Q}\|}(f_{Q}-f_{Z})(Y).

Therefore,

(2.3)

\displaystyle I_{2}I_{1}Y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\|f_{Q}\|}(-f_{Q}-f_{Z})(I_{2}Y)=-\frac{1}{2\|f_{Q}\|}(f_{Q}+f_{Z})(I_{2}Y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\|f_{Q}\|}(\nabla X)(I_{2}Y)\in\mathrm{Im}(\nabla X).

Since
\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X)
is
I_{1}
-invariant, we obtain

I_{2}(TF)=I_{2}(\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X))\subset\mathrm{Im}(\nabla X).

Finally, we prove (3).
Set
V:=TF\cap I_{2}TF=TF\cap I_{3}TF
.
Then
V
is
Q
-invariant.
Let
Y\in V
. Then
Y
and
I_{\alpha}Y
belong to
V
.
Substituting
I_{3}Y\in V\subset\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X)

into (
2.3
) in place of
Y
, we obtain

Y=-\frac{1}{2\|f_{Q}\|}(\nabla X)(I_{1}Y).

Since
I_{1}Y\in TF=\mathrm{Ker}(\nabla X)
,
it follows that
Y=0
, which proves (3).
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\nabla
be a quaternionic connection, and let

X
be a vector field preserving
Q
. Then we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{Y}f_{Q}^{\nabla}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\mathrm{Tr}H^{\nabla}_{Y,I_{\alpha}(\,\cdot\,)}X)I_{\alpha}

for
Y\in TM
, where
H^{\nabla}
is the Hessian with respect to
\nabla
. In addition,
if
L_{X}\nabla=0
, then we have

\displaystyle\nabla f_{Q}^{\nabla}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\mathrm{Tr}R^{\nabla}_{X,(\,\cdot\,)}\circ I_{\alpha})I_{\alpha}.

Proof.

Since an admissible frame
(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
is an orthonormal frame with respect to

(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
,
we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{Y}f_{Q}^{\nabla}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}Y(\mathrm{Tr}(\nabla X)I_{\alpha})I_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\mathrm{Tr}(\nabla X)I_{\alpha})(\nabla_{Y}I_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\mathrm{Tr}(\nabla_{Y}(\nabla X))I_{\alpha})I_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\mathrm{Tr}(\nabla X)(\nabla_{Y}I_{\alpha}))I_{\alpha}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\mathrm{Tr}(\nabla X)I_{\alpha})(\nabla_{Y}I_{\alpha}).

The second term and third term are canceled since
\nabla
is a quaternionic connection.
By
\nabla_{Y}(\nabla X)=H^{\nabla}_{Y,(\,\cdot\,)}X
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{Y}f_{Q}^{\nabla}=-\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\mathrm{Tr}H^{\nabla}_{Y,I_{\alpha}(\,\cdot\,)}X)I_{\alpha}.

If
L_{X}\nabla=0
, then we see
R^{\nabla}_{X,Y}Z=-H^{\nabla}_{Y,Z}X
for all
Y,Z\in TM
.
∎

We can now state and prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
(M,Q)
be a quaternionic manifold endowed with an
S^{1}
-action preserving
Q
.
If
F
is a connected component of the fixed-point set of the
S^{1}
-action,
then
\|f_{Q}\|
is constant on
F
.
If
f_{Q}=0
on
F
, then
F
is a quaternionic submanifold. Otherwise,

F
is a transversally complex and totally geodesic submanifold with respect to
a quaternionic connection and its dimension satisfies

(2.4)

\displaystyle\dim F\leq\frac{1}{2}\dim M.

Proof.

Let
X
be a vector field on
M
generating the
S^{1}
-action.
By
[
11
, Corollary 4.3]
, there exists a quaternionic connection
\nabla
such that
L_{X}\nabla=0
.
Since

H^{\nabla}_{Y,Z}X=-R^{\nabla}_{X,Y}Z=0

for all
Y,Z\in\Gamma(TF)
,
Lemma
2.4
implies that
\nabla f_{Q}=0
, and hence
\|f_{Q}\|
is constant on
F
.
If
f_{Q}=0
on
F
, then
\nabla X=f_{Z}\in Z(Q)
along
F
.
It follows that
TF
is
Q
-invariant, and consequently
F
is a quaternionic submanifold.
If
f_{Q}\neq 0
on
F
, then Proposition
2.3
shows that

F
is an almost complex and totally geodesic submanifold.
Moreover, the induced almost complex structure
I_{1}
of
F
is integrable since

(\nabla_{Y}I_{1})(Z)=0

for all
Y,Z\in TF
. By (3) of Lemma
2.3
, it follows that
F
is a transversally complex submanifold.
The inequality (
2.4
) follows from (3) of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

The highest dimensional case for

3
The twistor function and a quaternionic complex manifold

3.1
The twistor operator

Since the twistor operator has a significant role in this paper,
we give a detailed and elementary description for it.
Let
M
be an
m
-dimensional manifold, and set
L:=\Lambda^{m}(T^{\ast}M)
.
Take coordinate systems

\{U_{\alpha},(x^{\alpha}_{1},\dots,x^{\alpha}_{m})\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
of
M
.
Consider a collection of maps
t_{\alpha\beta}:U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}\to\mathbb{R}^{\ast}

defined by

\displaystyle U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}\ni x\mapsto\left|\det\left(\frac{\partial x^{\beta}_{i}}{\partial x^{\alpha}_{j}}\right)_{x}\right|^{-s}

for
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since the collection
\{t_{\alpha\beta}\}
satisfies the cocycle condition,
we can obtain the vector bundle
L^{s}
of
\mathrm{rank}L^{s}=1
.
We have
L^{s}\otimes L^{s^{\prime}}\cong L^{s+s^{\prime}}
.
In particular, it holds that

\underbrace{(L^{\frac{q}{p}})\otimes\cdots\otimes(L^{\frac{q}{p}})}_{p\,\,times}\cong\underbrace{L\otimes\cdots\otimes L}_{q\,\,times}

for
q/p\in\mathbb{Q}^{\ast}
.
Indeed, a transition function of a tensor product of
two line bundles over
M
is given by
\{t_{\alpha\beta}t^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}\}
,
where
\{t_{\alpha\beta}\}
,
\{t^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}\}
are transition functions of
the bundles respectively.
Let
\nabla
be a torsion-free affine connection on
M
.
We denote the connection on
L
induced from
\nabla
by the same symbol
\nabla

and define the connection on
L^{s}
which is denoted by
\nabla^{s}

(
\nabla^{1}=\nabla
).
The connection
\nabla^{\frac{q}{p}}
(
q/p\in\mathbb{Q}^{\ast}
) is also characterized by

\displaystyle\nabla^{q}(f\otimes\cdots\otimes f)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}e\otimes\cdots\otimes(\underbrace{\nabla^{\frac{q}{p}}e}_{i-th})\otimes\cdots\otimes e

for
\underbrace{e\otimes\cdots\otimes e}_{p\,\,times}=\underbrace{f\otimes\cdots\otimes f}_{q\,\,times}
. In fact, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}e\otimes\cdots\otimes(\underbrace{\nabla^{\frac{q}{p}}_{X}e}_{i-th})\otimes\cdots\otimes e

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{p}\,\theta_{L}(X)(p\,(e\otimes\cdots\otimes e))

\displaystyle=q\,\theta_{L}(X)(f\otimes\cdots\otimes f)=\nabla^{q}(f\otimes\cdots\otimes f),

where
\theta_{L}
is defined by
\nabla f=\theta_{L}\otimes f

for a nowhere vanishing section
f\in\Gamma(L)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f
be a nowhere vanishing section of
L
and
\nabla
be a connection such that

\nabla f=0
. If
e\in\Gamma(L^{\frac{q}{p}})

with\underbrace{e\otimes\cdots\otimes e}_{p\,\,times}=\underbrace{f\otimes\cdots\otimes f}_{q\,\,times}
, then we have
\nabla^{\frac{q}{p}}e=0
.

Let
(M,Q)
be a quaternionic manifold, and let
\nabla
,
\tilde{\nabla}
be quaternionic connections.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\tilde{\nabla}=\nabla+S^{\xi}
,
the difference of
\tilde{\nabla}^{s}
and
\nabla^{s}
as connections on
L^{s}
is given by

\tilde{\nabla}^{s}-\nabla^{s}=-4s(n+1)\xi\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{L^{s}}
.

Proof.

Let
(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{4n})
be a tangent frame of
M
.
The connection forms with respect to
(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{4n})
for
quaternionic connections
\tilde{\nabla}
and
\nabla
are denoted by

\tilde{\omega}^{j}_{i}
and
\omega^{j}_{i}
.
Then it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4n}\tilde{\omega}^{i}_{i}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{4n}{\omega}^{i}_{i}(X)+\mathrm{Tr}S^{\xi}_{X}.

The connection forms
\tilde{\theta}_{L}
and
\theta_{L}

of
\tilde{\nabla}
and
\nabla
of
L
with respect to

f=\xi_{1}^{\ast}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{4n}^{\ast}
are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\theta}_{L}=-\sum_{i=1}^{4n}\tilde{\omega}^{i}_{i},\,\,\ \theta_{L}=-\sum_{i=1}^{4n}{\omega}^{i}_{i},

where
(\xi_{1}^{\ast},\dots,\xi_{4n}^{\ast})
is the dual frame of
(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{4n})
.
From
\mathrm{Tr}S^{\xi}_{X}=4(n+1)\xi(X)
(see
[
2
]
), it holds that

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}^{s}_{X}e-\nabla^{s}_{X}e=s(\tilde{\theta}_{L}(X)-\theta_{L}(X))e=-s(\mathrm{Tr}S^{\xi}_{X})e=-4s(n+1)\xi(X)e

for
X\in TM
and
e\in\Gamma(L^{s})
.
∎

The connection on
Q\otimes L^{s}
is denoted by
\nabla^{Q,s}
, which is
induced by
\nabla
and
\nabla^{s}
.
We take a local section
I
of
Q

such that
(I_{1}:=I,I_{2},I_{3})
is an admissible frame of
Q

and
e\in\Gamma(L^{s})
.
By
\tilde{\nabla}_{X}I-\nabla_{X}I=[S^{\xi}_{X},I]
and Lemma
3.2
,
we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}^{Q,s}_{X}(I\otimes e)

\displaystyle=(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}I)\otimes e+I\otimes(\tilde{\nabla}^{s}_{X}e)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{X}I)\otimes e+[S^{\xi}_{X},I]\otimes e+I\otimes(D^{s}_{X}e)-4s(n+1)\xi(X)(I\otimes e)

\displaystyle={\nabla}^{Q,s}_{X}(I\otimes e)+[S^{\xi}_{X},I]\otimes e-4s(n+1)\xi(X)(I\otimes e).

Since
[S^{\xi}_{X},I]=[S^{\xi}_{X},I_{1}]=2\xi(I_{2}X)I_{3}-2\xi(I_{3}X)I_{2}
, we see that

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}^{Q,s}_{X}(I\otimes e)-{\nabla}^{Q,s}_{X}(I\otimes e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[S^{\xi}_{X},I]\otimes e-4s(n+1)\xi(X)(I\otimes e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{-2(\xi\circ I_{1})(I_{3}X)I_{3}-2(\xi\circ I_{1})(I_{2}X)I_{2}+4s(n+1)(\xi\circ I_{1})(I_{1}X)I_{1}\}\otimes e.

Consider

\displaystyle T^{\ast}M_{(1)}:=\left\{A^{\xi}:=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\xi\circ I_{\alpha})\otimes I_{\alpha}\mid\xi\in T^{\ast}M\right\}.

If
4s(n+1)=-2
, then

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}^{Q,s}_{X}(I\otimes e)-{\nabla}^{Q,s}_{X}(I\otimes e)=-2A^{\xi\circ I}(X)\otimes e(\in T^{\ast}M_{(1)}\otimes L^{s}).

By
[
2
, Lemma 1.2 (2)]
, the decomposition

\displaystyle T^{\ast}M\otimes Q=T^{\ast}M_{(1)}\oplus(T^{\ast}M\otimes Q)_{0}

holds, where
(Q\otimes T^{\ast}M)_{0}
is the subspace of tensors with all zero contractions.
Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle T^{\ast}M\otimes Q\otimes L^{s}=(T^{\ast}M_{(1)}\otimes L^{s})\oplus((T^{\ast}M\otimes Q)_{0}\otimes L^{s}).

By viewing
a connection on
Q\otimes L^{s}
as
a map from
\Gamma(Q\otimes L^{s})
to

\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\otimes Q\otimes L^{s})
, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.3

(
[
22
]
)
.

If
s=-\frac{1}{2(n+1)}
, then we have

\displaystyle p\circ\tilde{\nabla}^{Q,s}=p\circ\nabla^{Q,s},

where
p:T^{\ast}M\otimes Q\otimes L^{s}\to(T^{\ast}M\otimes Q)_{0}\otimes L^{s}

is the projection onto the second factor.

Hereafter we set

s_{0}:=-\frac{1}{2(n+1)}.

We can define a operator
D^{s_{0}}:=p\circ\nabla^{Q,s_{0}}:\Gamma(Q\otimes L^{s})\to\Gamma((T^{\ast}M\otimes Q)_{0}\otimes L^{s_{0}})
, which is independent of the choice
of a quaternionic connection.
We call
D^{s_{0}}
the
twistor operator
.
A section of
Q\otimes L^{s_{0}}
in
\mathrm{Ker}\,D^{s_{0}}

is called a
twistor function
(
[
15
]
). Clearly,
\mu
is a twistor function if and only if
there exists
\xi\in T^{\ast}M
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle{\nabla}^{Q,s_{0}}\mu=A^{\xi}\otimes e=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\xi\circ I_{\alpha})\otimes I_{\alpha}\otimes e.

If we choose a quaternionic connection
\nabla
such that

\nabla\nu=0
for some volume element
\nu
on
M
,
the section

e\in\Gamma(L^{s_{0}})
with

\underbrace{e\otimes\cdots\otimes e}_{2(n+1)\,\,times}=\nu^{\ast}\in\Gamma(L^{-1})(=\Gamma(L^{\ast}))

satisfies
\nabla^{s_{0}}e=0
, where
\nu^{\ast}
is the dual frame of
\nu
.
Therefore,
the equation (
3.1
) is equivalent to

(3.2)

\displaystyle\nabla\bar{\mu}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\xi\circ I_{\alpha})\otimes I_{\alpha},

where
\mu=\bar{\mu}\otimes e
.
We say that
\xi
is called a
corresponding one-form
of
\bar{\mu}
(with respect to
\nabla
).
Note that
for a given volume form
\nu

there exists a unique quaternionic connection
\nabla
such that

\nabla\nu=0
.
Hereafter for a section
e\in\Gamma(L^{s_{0}})
satisfying

\underbrace{e\otimes\cdots\otimes e}_{2(n+1)\,\,times}=\nu^{\ast}

for a volume form
\nu
, the pair
(e,\nu)
is called an

associated pair
. Any section
e\in\Gamma(L^{s_{0}})
defines a volume element
\nu

such that
(e,\nu)
is an associated pair.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any volume form
\nu
, there exists
e\in\Gamma(L^{s_{0}})
such that
either
(e,\nu)
or
(e,-\nu)
is an associated pair.

\hat{M}

\hat{M}
onto

\hat{\pi}
.

e_{0}:=1\in{\mathbb{R}}\,(\cong T_{1}{\mathbb{R}}^{>0})

We denote the horizontal lifts relative to the connections

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(M,Q)
be a quaternionic manifold, and let
\hat{M}
be the Swann bundle of

.
For a twistor function
\mu=\bar{\mu}\otimes e
of
(M,Q)

we define a function
\hat{\mu}:\hat{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}

by

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}((r,(I_{2},I_{2},I_{3})))

\displaystyle:=(\hat{\mu}_{1}(r,(I_{2},I_{2},I_{3})),\hat{\mu}_{2}(r,(I_{2},I_{2},I_{3})),\hat{\mu}_{3}(r,(I_{2},I_{2},I_{3})))

\displaystyle:=(r^{\frac{2}{c}}(\bar{\mu},I_{1}),r^{\frac{2}{c}}(\bar{\mu},I_{2}),r^{\frac{2}{c}}(\bar{\mu},I_{3}))

for

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle d\hat{\mu}(Z_{0}^{c})

\displaystyle=(cr\frac{\partial}{\partial r})\hat{\mu}=2\hat{\mu},\,\,\,\mbox{that is,}\,\,\,d\hat{\mu}_{\alpha}(Z_{0})=2\hat{\mu}_{\alpha}.

\displaystyle d\hat{\mu}_{1}\circ\hat{I}_{1}^{\bar{\theta},c}=d\hat{\mu}_{2}\circ\hat{I}_{2}^{\bar{\theta},c}=d\hat{\mu}_{3}\circ\hat{I}_{3}^{\bar{\theta},c}

Lemma 3.7

(3.3)

\displaystyle d\bar{\mu}_{\alpha}

where

3.3
A quaternionic complex manifold

In this subsection, we recall the notion of a quaternionic complex manifold
and its fundamental properties (
[
15
]
),
which play an important role in this paper.

Definition 3.13

(
[
15
]
)
.

A quaternionic manifold
(M,Q)
with a twistor function

\mu\in\Gamma(Q\otimes L^{s_{0}})
which vanishes nowhere on
M

is called a

quaternionic complex manifold
.

For a twistor function
\mu
that vanishes nowhere on
M
, an almost complex structure is defined as

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\|\bar{\mu}\|}

by expressing
\mu=\bar{\mu}\otimes e
for
e\in\Gamma(L^{s_{0}})
.
This is an almost complex structure determined up to sign. In fact, using

\mu=\bar{\mu}\otimes e=\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\otimes e^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\|\bar{\mu}\|}=\pm\frac{\bar{\mu}^{\prime}}{\|\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\|}.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
(M,Q)
be a quaternionic complex manifold with a twistor function

\mu\in\Gamma(Q\otimes L^{s_{0}})
and
\nu
be a volume form on
M
.
If
\nabla
is a quaternionic connection such that
\nabla\nu=0
and

e\in\Gamma(L^{s_{0}})
with
(e,\nu)
an associated pair,
then
\bar{\mu}\in\Gamma(Q)
given by
\mu=\bar{\mu}\otimes e
satisfies

\displaystyle\nabla\left(\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\|\bar{\mu}\|}\right)=\frac{1}{\|\bar{\mu}\|}\left((\xi\circ I_{2})\otimes I_{2}+(\xi\circ I_{3})\otimes I_{3}\right),

where
(I_{1}:=\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\|\bar{\mu}\|},I_{2},I_{2})
is an admissible frame and

\xi
is given by (
3.1
).

Proof.

Since
\mu
is a twistor function, then it holds that

\displaystyle\nabla\bar{\mu}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\xi\circ I_{\alpha})\otimes I_{\alpha}.

Because
\nabla
is a metric connection with respect to
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
,
we have

\displaystyle d\|\bar{\mu}\|^{2}=2\|\bar{\mu}\|d\|\bar{\mu}\|,\,\,\,d\langle\bar{\mu},\bar{\mu}\rangle=2\langle\nabla\bar{\mu},\bar{\mu}\rangle=2\langle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\xi\circ I_{\alpha})\otimes I_{\alpha},\|\bar{\mu}\|I_{1}\rangle=2(\xi\circ I_{1})\|\bar{\mu}\|.

So it holds that
d\|\bar{\mu}\|=\xi\circ I_{1}
.
Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\nabla\left(\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\|\bar{\mu}\|}\right)

\displaystyle=d\left(\frac{1}{\|\bar{\mu}\|}\right)\otimes\bar{\mu}+\frac{1}{\|\bar{\mu}\|}\nabla\bar{\mu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\|\bar{\mu}\|^{2}}(d\|\bar{\mu}\|)\otimes(\|\bar{\mu}\|I_{1})+\frac{1}{\|\bar{\mu}\|}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\xi\circ I_{\alpha})\otimes I_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\|\bar{\mu}\|}(\xi\circ I_{1})\otimes I_{1}+\frac{1}{\|\bar{\mu}\|}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(\xi\circ I_{\alpha})\otimes I_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|\bar{\mu}\|}\left((\xi\circ I_{2})\otimes I_{2}+(\xi\circ I_{3})\otimes I_{3}\right).

∎

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
(M,Q)
be a quaternionic complex manifold with a twistor function
\mu
.
For
\mu=\bar{\mu}\otimes e=\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\otimes e^{\prime}
,
we have

\displaystyle\|\bar{\mu}\|^{-2(n+1)}\nu=\|\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\|^{-2(n+1)}\nu^{\prime},

where
(e,\nu)
,
(e^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})
are associated pairs.

Proof.

Since
(e,\nu)
,
(e^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})
are associated pairs, we have

e\otimes\cdots\otimes e=\nu^{\ast}\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,e^{\prime}\otimes\cdots\otimes e^{\prime}=\nu^{\prime\ast}.

If
e=ae^{\prime}
for a function
a
, we have

\nu^{\ast}=e\otimes\cdots\otimes e=a^{2(n+1)}e^{\prime}\otimes\cdots\otimes e^{\prime}=a^{2(n+1)}\nu^{\prime\ast}
.
On the other hand, since
(\mu=)\bar{\mu}\otimes e=\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\otimes e^{\prime}=(\bar{\mu}^{\prime}/a)\otimes e
,
we have
\bar{\mu}^{\prime}=a\bar{\mu}
.
Therefore,
it holds that

\displaystyle\|\bar{\mu}\|^{-2(n+1)}\nu=|a|^{2(n+1)}\|\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\|^{-2(n+1)}a^{-2(n+1)}\nu^{\prime}=\|\bar{\mu}^{\prime}\|^{-2(n+1)}\nu^{\prime}.

∎

For a quaternionic complex manifold
(M,Q)
with a twistor function
\mu
, we can define
a specific volume form as in Lemma
3.15
, which is denoted by
\nu^{\mu}
.

Proposition 3.16

(
[
15
]
)

Let
(M,Q)
be a quaternionic complex manifold with a twistor function

\mu\in\Gamma(Q\otimes L^{s_{0}})

Denote the almost complex structure defined from

by

Then there exists a unique quaternionic connection

such that

is symmetric and

Proof.

Theorem 2.4 in
[

tells us that

We can write

Lemma 3.18
.

Let

be a quaternionic complex manifold with a twistor function

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}-\nabla=S^{\alpha},\,\,\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}d\log\|\bar{\mu}\|.

The connection

3.4
A compatible complex structure of closed type

For any admissible frame
(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
of
Q
, there are connection forms
\omega^{\nabla}_{1}
,

\omega^{\nabla}_{2}
,
\omega^{\nabla}_{3}
with respect to a quaternionic connection
\nabla
with

(3.9)

\displaystyle\nabla I_{\alpha}=\omega^{\nabla}_{\gamma}\otimes I_{\beta}-\omega^{\nabla}_{\beta}\otimes I_{\gamma},

where
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
is any cyclic permutation of
(1,2,3)
. It is known that

I_{1}
is integrable if and only if
\omega^{\nabla}_{2}\circ I_{2}=\omega^{\nabla}_{3}\circ I_{3}
for any
admissible frame
(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
. The integrability of
I_{1}
is independent of the choice of
a quaternionic connection
\nabla
. Hence
\omega^{\nabla}_{2}\circ I_{2}=\omega^{\nabla}_{3}\circ I_{3}
holds for any quaternionic connection if
I_{1}
is integrable.

Lemma 3.26
.

For admissible frames
(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
and
(\bar{I}_{1},\bar{I}_{2},\bar{I}_{3})
,
if
I_{1}=\bar{I}_{1}
, then we have

\omega^{\nabla}_{2}\circ I_{2}+\omega^{\nabla}_{3}\circ I_{3}=\bar{\omega}^{\nabla}_{2}\circ\bar{I}_{2}+\bar{\omega}^{\nabla}_{3}\circ\bar{I}_{3},

where
\omega^{\nabla}_{\alpha}
(resp.
\bar{\omega}^{\nabla}_{\alpha}
) is
the connection form with respect to
(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
(resp.
(\bar{I}_{1},\bar{I}_{2},\bar{I}_{3})
)
given by (
3.9
).

Proof.

For simplicity, we omit
\nabla
for the subscript of
\omega_{\alpha}^{\nabla}
(
\alpha=2,3
).
Since

\displaystyle\nabla I=\omega_{2}\otimes I_{3}-\omega_{3}\otimes I_{2}=\bar{\omega}_{2}\otimes\bar{I}_{3}-\bar{\omega}_{3}\otimes\bar{I}_{2},

\displaystyle I_{2}=\cos\theta\bar{I}_{2}-\sin\theta\bar{I}_{3},\,\,\,I_{3}=\sin\theta\bar{I}_{2}+\cos\theta\bar{I}_{3},

we have

\displaystyle\omega_{2}=\cos\theta\bar{\omega}_{2}-\sin\theta\bar{\omega}_{3},\,\,\,\omega_{3}=\sin\theta\bar{\omega}_{2}+\cos\theta\bar{\omega}_{3}.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\omega_{2}\circ I_{2}

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}\theta(\bar{\omega}^{\nabla}_{2}\circ\bar{I}_{2})-\sin\theta\cos\theta(\bar{\omega}^{\nabla}_{2}\circ\bar{I}_{3})-\sin\theta\cos\theta(\bar{\omega}^{\nabla}_{3}\circ\bar{I}_{2})+\sin^{2}\theta(\bar{\omega}^{\nabla}_{3}\circ\bar{I}_{3}),

\displaystyle\omega_{3}\circ I_{3}

\displaystyle=\sin^{2}\theta(\bar{\omega}^{\nabla}_{2}\circ\bar{I}_{2})+\sin\theta\cos\theta(\bar{\omega}^{\nabla}_{2}\circ\bar{I}_{3})+\sin\theta\cos\theta(\bar{\omega}^{\nabla}_{3}\circ\bar{I}_{2})+\cos^{2}\theta(\bar{\omega}^{\nabla}_{3}\circ\bar{I}_{3}).

This completes the proof.
∎

By virtue of Lemma

on

As Remark 5.2 in

is exact.
Hence, we conclude as follows.

Any two Ricci symmetric quaternionic connections are related by an exact one-form in

\omega^{\nabla}_{2}\circ I_{2}=\omega^{\nabla}_{3}\circ I_{3}(=(1/2)\eta^{\nabla}_{I})

is also a globally defined one-form. In fact, for
(I_{1}=I,I_{2},I_{3})
(resp. (
\bar{I}_{1}=I,\bar{I}_{2},\bar{I}_{3})
)
is an admissible frame defined on
U
(resp.
V
), we see that

\omega^{\nabla}_{2}\circ I_{2}=\omega^{\nabla}_{3}\circ I_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\nabla}_{I}=\bar{\omega}^{\nabla}_{2}\circ\bar{I}_{2}=\bar{\omega}^{\nabla}_{3}\circ\bar{I}_{3}

on
U\cap V
.

Lemma 3.29
.

Let
(M,Q)
be a quaternionic manifold with a volume form
\nu

and
I
be a complex structure compatible with
Q
.
Let
\nabla
be a quaternionic connection such that
\nabla\nu=0
and

(e,\nu)
be the associated pair.
If a section
\mu=fI\otimes e\in\Gamma(Q\otimes L^{s_{0}})
is
a nowhere vanishing twistor function if and only if
\eta_{I}^{\nabla}=2d(\log|f|)

for a nowhere vanishing function
f
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mu=fI\otimes e
is a twistor function. Then we have

\displaystyle\nabla(fI\otimes e)=df\otimes I+f(\nabla I)=(\xi\circ I)\otimes I+(\xi\circ I_{2})\otimes I_{2}+(\xi\circ I_{3})\otimes I_{3},

where
(I_{1}=I,I_{2},I_{3})
is an admissible frame. This implies that

f\omega_{2}\circ I_{2}=f\omega_{3}\circ I_{3}=df
. Then we have
\eta_{I}^{\nabla}=2d(\log|f|)
.
Conversely, setting
\xi=-df\circ I
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla(fI\otimes e)

\displaystyle=df\otimes I+f(\nabla I)

\displaystyle=df\otimes I+f(\omega^{\nabla}_{3}\otimes I_{2}-\omega^{\nabla}_{2}\otimes I_{3})

\displaystyle=df\otimes I+f\left(\left(-\frac{df}{f}\circ I_{3}\right)\otimes I_{2}+\left(\frac{df}{f}\circ I_{2}\right)\otimes I_{3}\right)

\displaystyle=(\xi\circ I)\otimes I+(\xi\circ I_{2})\otimes I_{2}+(\xi\circ I_{3})\otimes I_{3},

which means that
\mu
is a twistor function.
∎

Lemma 3.30
.

Let
(M,Q)
be a quaternionic manifold with a compatible complex structure
I
.
If
\nabla
and
\nabla^{\prime}
are quaternionic connections such that
\nabla I=\nabla^{\prime}I=0
, then
\nabla=\nabla^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The difference tensor
S^{\xi}
for
\nabla
and
\nabla^{\prime}

in Lemma
2.1
satisfies that
[S^{\xi},I]=0
. This means that
\xi=0
.
∎

The following proposition means that a compatible complex structure

of closed type

Proposition 3.31
.

Let

be a quaternionic manifold, and let

with

. Suppose that an

If

\nabla^{I}

. Moreover, the complex structure

is
induced from a locally defined twistor function

\nabla^{I}

Proof.

Take an associated pair

and
\nabla
be a quaternionic connection on

such that
\nabla\nu=0
.
For any point

\mu^{U}

4
An obstruction to the existence of hypercomplex structures

4.1
Curvatures of the
\mu
-connection

As (1.5.2) in
[
2
]
, we have

\displaystyle[R^{\nabla}_{X,Y},I_{\alpha}]=\Omega^{\nabla}_{\gamma}(X,Y)I_{\beta}-\Omega^{\nabla}_{\beta}(X,Y)I_{\gamma}

for
X
,
Y\in TM
, where
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
is any cyclic permutation of
(1,2,3)
.
From
[
2
, Corollary 1.4 and Remark 1.12]
,
the two-form
\Omega_{\alpha}
is
given by

\displaystyle\Omega^{\nabla}_{\alpha}(X,Y)=2(B^{\nabla}(X,I_{\alpha}Y)-B^{\nabla}(Y,I_{\alpha}X))

for
X
,
Y\in TM
. On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\Omega^{\nabla}_{\alpha}=d\omega^{\nabla}_{\alpha}+\omega^{\nabla}_{\beta}\wedge\omega^{\nabla}_{\gamma}.

We consider the case of a quaternionic complex manifold defined by a nowhere vanishing twistor function
\mu
.
Take an admissible frame
(I_{1}=I,I_{2},I_{3})
, where
I
is a complex structure defined from
\mu
.
Since
\nabla^{\mu}I=0
, we have
\omega^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{2}=\omega^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{3}=0
, and hence

\Omega^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{2}=\Omega^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{3}=0
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The Ricci tensor
Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}
satisfies that

Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Y)=Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{1}X,I_{1}Y)=-Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{2}X,I_{2}Y)=-Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{3}X,I_{3}Y)

for
X
,
Y\in TM
.

Proof.

Since
\Omega^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{2}=0
and
Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}
is symmetric, it holds that

B^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Y)=-B^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{2}X,I_{2}Y)

for any
X
,
Y\in TM
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4n}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2n(n+2)}\Pi_{h}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4n}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{2}X,I_{2}Y)+\frac{1}{2n(n+2)}\Pi_{h}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{2}X,I_{2}Y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4n}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{2}X,I_{2}Y)+\frac{1}{2n(n+2)}\Pi_{h}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Y).

This means that

(4.1)

\displaystyle Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Y)+Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{2}X,I_{2}Y)=\frac{2}{(n+2)}\Pi_{h}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Y).

Similarly, we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Y)+Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{3}X,I_{3}Y)=\frac{2}{(n+2)}\Pi_{h}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Y).

Combining these two equations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), it holds that

\displaystyle Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{2}X,I_{2}Y)=Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{3}X,I_{3}Y),

which also implies that

\displaystyle Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Y)=Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{1}X,I_{1}Y).

Again by (
4.1
), we see that

\displaystyle Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Y)+Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{2}X,I_{2}Y)=\frac{2}{(n+2)}\cdot\frac{1}{4}\left(2Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Y)+2Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{2}X,I_{2}Y)\right).

Therefore, we have
Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Y)=-Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{2}X,I_{2}Y)
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

\Pi_{h}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}=0
and
B^{\nabla^{\mu}}=\frac{1}{4n}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}
.

We set
Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{I_{1}}(X,Y):=Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,I_{1}Y)
for
X
,
Y\in TM
.

Lemma 4.3
.

The non-vanishing coefficient
\Omega^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle\Omega^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{1}=\frac{1}{n}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{I_{1}}.

Proof.

By corollary
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\Omega^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{1}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=2(B^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,I_{1}Y)-B^{\nabla^{\mu}}(Y,I_{1}X))

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{1}{4n}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,I_{1}Y)-\frac{1}{4n}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{1}Y,X)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,I_{1}Y)=\frac{1}{n}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{I_{1}}(X,Y)

for all
X
,
Y\in TM
.
∎

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(I_{1}R^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{X,Y})=-2Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{I_{1}}(X,Y)

since

\Omega^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{1}(X,Y)=-\frac{1}{2n}\mathrm{Tr}(I_{1}R^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{X,Y}).

The following proposition shows that
the Ricci tensor
Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}
of the
\mu
-connection
provides an obstruction to the existence of a local hypercomplex structure
of the form
(I_{1}=I,I_{2},I_{3})
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(M,Q)
be a quaternionic complex manifold with a twistor function
\mu
,
and let
I
be the complex structure induced by
\mu
.
If
(I_{1}=I,I_{2},I_{3})
is a hypercomplex structure on an open set
U\subset M
,
then
Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}=0
on
U
.
In particular, if
Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{x}\neq 0
at a point
x\in M
,
then any neighborhood
V
of

does not admit a hypercomplex structure

(I_{1}=I,I_{2},I_{3})
on

.

Proof.

Let
\nabla^{0}
be the Obata connection on

associated with
the hypercomplex structure
(I_{1}=I,I_{2},I_{3})
.
Since
\nabla^{0}I=\nabla^{\mu}I=0

3.30
implies that
\nabla^{0}=\nabla^{\mu}
.
Hence
Ric^{\nabla^{0}}=Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}
.
Because

is skew-symmetric whereas

it follows that
Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}=0
on

.
∎

\displaystyle c_{1}(M)=\left[-\frac{1}{2\pi}Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}_{I_{1}}\right].

The

\displaystyle\Theta^{\nabla}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\Omega^{\nabla}_{\alpha}\wedge\Omega^{\nabla}_{\alpha}

\nabla

due to
[

.

.

Proof of the main theorem

The following lemma is easy to prove.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(M,(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}))

(M,I_{\alpha})

Proof.

Let

If

\mu

Precisely, we have

Lemma 4.8
.

Let

\displaystyle Ric^{\nabla^{I}}(X,Y)=Ric^{\nabla^{I}}(I_{1}X,I_{1}Y)=-Ric^{\nabla^{I}}(I_{2}X,I_{2}Y)=-Ric^{\nabla^{I}}(I_{3}X,I_{3}Y)

Theorem 4.9

-quaternionic action, and

From the assumption, we have the quaternionic connection

We point out that there is a non quaternionically flat hypercomplex manifold with quaternionic

4.3
The quaternionic projective space–Pontecorvo’s example–

Let
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
be the (right-)quaternionic projective space
with its standard quaternionic structure
Q
.
It is well known that
(\mathbb{H}P^{n},Q)
admits the standard
quaternionic Kähler metric
g
.
We denote by
\mathrm{Aut}(Q)
(resp.
\mathrm{Aut}(g)
)
the group of quaternionic transformations
(resp. isometries) of
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
.
It is known that

\mathrm{Aut}(g)=\mathrm{Sp}(n+1)/\mathbb{Z}_{2}\subsetneq\mathrm{Aut}(Q)=\mathrm{PGL}(n+1,\mathbb{H})=\mathrm{GL}(n+1,\mathbb{H})/\mathbb{R}^{\ast}.

See, for example,
[
1
]
.
Thus, in general, a quaternionic
S^{1}
-action on

(\mathbb{H}P^{n},Q)
is not necessarily isometric
with respect to
g
.
To determine the fixed-point sets of a quaternionic

S^{1}
-action on
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
,
we therefore work independently of the metric
g
.
The following lemma is well known,
however, we include an alternative and more elementary proof for completeness.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
(M,Q)
be a quaternionic manifold.
If
N
is a
Q
-invariant submanifold, then
N
is totally geodesic for any quaternionic connection

\nabla
.

Proof.

Let
\nabla
be any quaternionic connection of
Q
and
set
h_{x}(X,Y):=[(\nabla_{X}Y)_{x}]\in T_{x}M/T_{x}N
at
x\in N
for all
X
,
Y\in\Gamma(TN)
.
We have
h(X,I_{\alpha}Y)=I_{\alpha}h(X,Y)
for any
\alpha\in\{1,2,3\}
.
Here we note that
I_{\alpha}[\xi]:=[I_{\alpha}\xi]\in T_{x}M/T_{x}N
for
\xi\in T_{x}M
.
Since
\nabla
is torsion-free,

h
is symmetric. Therefore, it holds that

h(I_{\alpha}X,I_{\alpha}Y)=-h(X,Y)
for any
\alpha\in\{1,2,3\}
.
This implies that

h(I_{\gamma}X,I_{\gamma}Y)=h(I_{\alpha}I_{\beta}X,I_{\alpha}I_{\beta}Y)=-h(I_{\beta}X,I_{\beta}Y)=h(X,Y)

for any cyclic permutation
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
of
(1,2,3)
.
Hence
h
vanishes, that is,
\nabla_{X}Y\in\Gamma(TN)
for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TN)
.
∎

In particular, the induced quaternionic structure and connection on
N
makes

N
a quaternionic manifold.

Definition 4.13
.

A quaternionic manifold
(M,Q)
is said to be
complete

if there exists a quaternionic connection
\nabla

such that
(M,\nabla)
is geodesically complete.
Here,
(M,\nabla)
is called
\nabla
-geodesically complete
if every
\nabla
-geodesic is defined on the entire real line
\mathbb{R}
.

The quaternionic projective space
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
with standard quaternionic structure
is complete. Indeed, the Levi-Civita connection of the standard Riemannian metric is a quaternionic connection.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are
Q
-invariant submanifolds in a complete quaternionic manifold
(M,Q)
.
Assume that there exists a point
x\in N_{1}\cap N_{2}
such that
T_{x}N_{1}=T_{x}N_{2}
.
If
N_{1}
is connected and
N_{2}
is geodesically complete for the induced quaternionic connection,
then
N_{1}
is an open submanifold in
N_{2}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar as in 14. Lemma in p.105 of
[
18
]
.
By Lemma
4.12
,
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are totally geodesic submanifolds for
any quaternionic connection. Because
(M,Q)
is complete, there exists
a quaternionic connection
\nabla

for which
(M,\nabla)
is
\nabla
-geodesically complete.
Let
\gamma
be a geodesic on
N_{1}
starting from
x
to
y
with respect to the induced connection from

\nabla
.
So
\gamma
is a geodesic on
(M,\nabla)
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)\in T_{x}N_{2}
.
Therefore,
\gamma
is a geodesic on
N_{2}
since
N_{2}
is totally geodesic and
\nabla
-complete.
Since the parallel translation of
T_{x}N_{1}=T_{x}N_{2}
on
(M,\nabla)
along

\gamma
gives
T_{y}N_{1}=T_{y}N_{2}
.
This shows
y\in N_{2}
. By repeating this argument and connected assumption for
N_{1}
,
we have
N_{1}\subset N_{2}
.
∎

Now we can characterize the fixed-point sets of a quaternionic
S^{1}
-action on
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
.

Theorem 4.15
.

If
S^{1}
acts on
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
preserving the quaternionic structure, then
each connected component
F
of the fixed-point set
is one of the following:

(1)

an isolated point,

(2)

a submanifold diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{C}P^{l}
with
f_{Q}\neq 0

which is a transversally totally complex submanifold contained in

\mathbb{H}P^{l}\subset\mathbb{H}P^{n}
,

(3)

a submanifold diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{H}P^{k}
with
f_{Q}=0
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.5
,

F
is either a quaternionic or a transversally complex and totally geodesic submanifold.
We first consider the case where
F
is a quaternionic submanifold in
M
.
We may assume that there exists a point
x\in F
such that

T_{x}F=T_{x}\mathbb{H}P^{k}
, where
4k=\dim F
.
By Lemma
4.14
,
F
is an open submanifold in
\mathbb{H}P^{k}
.
Since
F
is closed, it follows that
F=\mathbb{H}P^{k}
.

Next, we consider
the case where
F
is transversally complex.
Set
V=TF\oplus I_{2}TF(=TF\oplus I_{3}TF)
, which is a
Q
-invariant subbundle of
TM|_{F}
over
F
.
Since
F
is totally geodesic for a quaternionic connection,
V
is parallel with respect to
this connection, and hence with respect to any quaternionic connection (Remark
2.8
).
We may assume that there exists
x\in F
such that

V_{x}=T_{x}\mathbb{H}P^{l}
, where
4l=\mathrm{rank}\,V
.
Take a geodesic
\gamma
on
F
from
x
to
y
.
Then
\gamma
is a geodesic in
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
, and hence,
it is geodesic in
\mathbb{HP}^{l}
, and hence we obtain

V_{y}=T_{y}\mathbb{H}P^{l}
. This shows that
F
is contained in
\mathbb{H}P^{l}
, where
it is a (totally geodesic and) transversally complex submanifold with
\dim F=2l
.
Finally, we show that
F=\mathbb{C}P^{l}
.
We consider the case of
l\geq 2
.
By Theorem
2.5
,

F
is a transversally totally complex submanifold and it is totally geodesic
with respect to a quaternionic connection.
This means that the second fundamental form of
F
with respect to this quaternionic connection
vanishes.
Consequently, the
(2,0)+(0,2)
-part of the second fundamental form of
F
with respect to
the standard quaternionic connection on
\mathbb{H}P^{l}
vanishes.
By Theorem 6.7 in
[
23
]
, it follows that

F
is an open part of
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
.
Since
F
is closed, we conclude that
F=\mathbb{C}P^{l}
.
When
l=1
,
\mathbb{H}P^{1}\cong S^{4}
with the conformal structure
[g_{0}]
, where

g_{0}
is standard metric on
S^{4}
. Note
that a quaternionic structure is a conformal structure for four dimensional cases.
Since
F
is totally geodesic for a metric in
[g_{0}]
,

F
is totally umbilic with respect to the metric
g_{0}
, hence
F
is an open part of
the 2-dimensional sphere of a certain radius, which is isomorphic to

\mathbb{C}P^{1}
. The closedness for
F
implies that
F=\mathbb{C}P^{1}
.
∎

Remark 4.16
.

For a transversally totally complex submanifold
of half dimension in a quaternionic manifold,
the
(2,0)+(0,2)
-part of the second fundamental form
is independent of the choice of quaternionic connection.
See
[
23
, Proposition 3.7]
.

As a consequence of Corollary
4.11
and Theorem
4.15
, we have the following.

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
(\mathbb{H}P^{n},Q)
be the quaternionic projective space with the standard quaternionic structure

Q
and an
S^{1}
-action preserving

, and let
\mu
be a twistor function.
We assume that the complex structure
I
induced from

Let

.

(I_{1}=I,I_{2},I_{3})

on any open set
U
containing

.
In particular, if

, then the

-bundle

It follows from

∎

To illustrate Pontecorvo’s example

\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2n+2)/\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2n)\times\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2)

from our viewpoint, we consider the lifted map of a twistor function to the Swann bundle which carries the hypercomplex structure, which has been studied in Section
3.2
.
Consider the quaternionic
S^{1}
-action on
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
given by

(4.4)

\displaystyle e^{i\theta}[z]:=[e^{i\theta}z],

where
[z]\in\mathbb{H}P^{n}
. Note that
e^{i\theta}(u+jv)=e^{i\theta}u+je^{-i\theta}v

for
u+jv\in\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{H}
.
Take the associated pair
(e,\nu^{g})
,
where
\nu^{g}
is the volume form of the standard metric
g
on

\mathbb{H}P^{n}
.
We consider the twistor function
\mu=f_{Q}^{\nabla^{g}}\otimes e

on
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
, where
\nabla^{g}
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g

and
X
generates the
S^{1}
-action.
The vector field
\hat{X}
which generates
the lifted action to
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{X}_{(z_{1},\dots,z_{n+1})}

\displaystyle=(iz_{1},\dots,iz_{n+1})=(iu_{1},\dots,iu_{n+1},-iv_{1},\dots,-iv_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{n+1}\left(u_{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{l}}-\bar{u}_{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{u}_{l}}-v_{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{l}}+\bar{v}_{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{v}_{l}}\right).

By Lemmas
3.5
,
3.9
and Proposition
3.11
, the zero set of
\hat{\mu}
coincides with
the zeros of

\theta(\hat{X})=(\theta_{1}(\hat{X}),\theta_{2}(\hat{X}),\theta_{2}(\hat{X}))
, where

(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})
is the connection form of the
{\rm SO(3)}
-bundle

induced from
\nabla^{g}

\displaystyle\theta_{1}

\displaystyle=r^{-2}{\rm Im}(\sum_{l=1}^{n+1}\bar{u}_{l}du_{l}+\bar{v}_{l}dv_{l}),

\displaystyle\theta_{2}

\displaystyle=r^{-2}{\rm Re}(\sum_{l=1}^{n+1}{u}_{l}dv_{l}-v_{l}du_{l}),

\displaystyle\theta_{3}

\displaystyle=r^{-2}{\rm Im}(\sum_{l=1}^{n+1}{u}_{l}dv_{l}-v_{l}du_{l}),

where

\hat{\mu}

\hat{\mu}^{-1}(0)=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\mid{}^{t}u\,v=0\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\|u\|=\|v\|\}.

The fixed-point set of the

.
By Lemma
3.12
, a connected component containing

of the compliment of the zeros

\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2n+2)/\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2n)\times\mathrm{SO}^{\ast}(2)

In fact, using the change of coordinates

\displaystyle z_{i}=\frac{1}{2}(u_{i}+v_{i}),\,\,\,z_{n+1+i}=-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}(u_{i}-v_{i})\,\,\,(i=1,\dots,n+1),

we have

\displaystyle{}^{t}z\,z={}^{t}u\,v,\,\,\,\|u\|^{2}=\|z\|^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\mathrm{Im}\,z_{i}\bar{z}_{n+1+i},\,\,\,\|v\|^{2}=\|z\|^{2}-2\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\mathrm{Im}\,z_{i}\bar{z}_{n+i}.

See
[
20

[

Example 4.18

(Pontecorvo’s example)
.

.

,

does not admit a hypercomplex structure

(I_{1}=I,I_{2},I_{3})

,
where

is the

.

]
, it is shown that the holonomy group of the canonical connection

.

4.4
The complex two-plane Grassmann manifold

We present a further example obtained as the quaternionic complex quotient of
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
.
The example given here is not quaternionically flat, but by using the geometry of fixed-point sets
(Proposition
4.19
)
similar to the previous section, we can show the non-existence of a hypercomplex structure.
We refer to
[
15
]
for the quaternionic complex quotient and for
interesting four-dimensional examples.

Consider the
S^{1}
-action
(
4.4
) and
the twistor function
\mu
on
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
as in Example
4.18
.
Then it holds that

\displaystyle\mu^{-1}(0)=\{[z]=[u+jv]\in\mathbb{H}P^{n}\mid{}^{t}u\,v=0\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\|u\|=\|v\|\}.

and

\displaystyle\mu^{-1}(0)/S^{1}=Gr(2,n+1),

where
Gr(2,n+1)
is the complex two-plane Grassmann manifold.
Note that the quaternionic structure on
Gr(2,n+1)
is not flat,
that is,
W\neq 0
,
as follows from the curvature formulas in
[
6
]
.
We consider the twistor function
\mu^{\circ}
on
\mathbb{H}P^{n}

whose lifted map
\hat{\mu}^{\circ}
to the Swann bundle
is given by

\hat{\mu}^{\circ}_{1}=\|u_{1}\|^{2}-\|v_{1}\|^{2}
and

\hat{\mu}^{\circ}_{2}+i\mu^{\circ}_{3}=2iu_{1}v_{1}
.
Since
\mu^{\circ}
is invariant
under the action (
4.4
),
\mu^{\circ}
descends to a twistor function on the quotient space
Gr(2,n+1)
.
Therefore
\mathbb{H}P^{n}\setminus\{[z]\mid z_{1}=0\}
is a quaternionic complex manifold
with the twistor function
\mu^{\circ}
.
In particular, on
\mu^{-1}(0)\setminus\{[z]\in\mathbb{H}P^{n}\mid z_{1}=u_{1}+jv_{1}=0\}
,
the twistor function
\mu^{\circ}
has no zero points.
Note that the quotient of

(\mu^{-1}(0)\setminus\{[z]\in\mathbb{H}P^{n}\mid z_{1}=0\})
by the
S^{1}
-action
(
4.4
)
is a quaternionic complex quotient space
with the twistor function induced by
\mu^{\circ}

in the sense of
[
15
]
.
It is known that there is no compatible almost complex structure on
Gr(2,n+1)
.
However, there exists a compatible complex structure on

\displaystyle Gr(2,n+1)\setminus Gr(2,n).

Indeed, this open subset carries the quaternionic complex structure
induced by
\mu^{\circ}
.
Nevertheless, this compatible complex structure cannot be extended
to a hypercomplex structure as Example
4.21
.
To see this, we show the following proposition in the compact setting
in contrast with the case of
T^{\ast}\mathbb{C}P^{n}

endowed with the Calabi metric (see Section
4.1
).

Proposition 4.19
.

Let
(M,Q,g)
be a compact quaternionic Kähler manifold
with non-zero scalar curvature.
Suppose that
S^{1}
acts on
M
isometrically,
and let
F
be a connected component of the fixed-point set
such that
f_{Q}\neq 0
and
F\cap\mu^{-1}(0)=\emptyset
,
where
\mu
is the quaternionic Kähler moment map associated with the
S^{1}
-action.
Then
M\setminus\mu^{-1}(0)

does not admit a hypercomplex structure
(I_{1}=I,I_{2},I_{3})

on any open set
U
containing
F
.
In particular, if
\dim F\geq 4
, then the
{\rm SO}(3)
-bundle
S(U)
is not trivial
for any open set
U\subset M\setminus\mu^{-1}(0)
with
F\subset U
.

Proof.

Let
\nabla^{g}
be the Levi-Civita connection of
g
.
Since connection
\nabla^{g}
and
\nabla^{\mu}
are quaternionic connections with respect to
Q
, we have

R^{\nabla^{g}}-R^{B^{\nabla^{g}}}=R^{\nabla^{\mu}}-R^{B^{\nabla^{\mu}}}(=W).

By Corollary
3.25
, we see that
\nabla^{g}_{X}Y=\nabla^{\mu}_{X}Y\in\Gamma(TF)
for any

X
,
Y\in\Gamma(TF)
.
Therefore, it holds that

p(R^{B^{\nabla^{g}}}_{X,Y}Z)=p(R^{B^{\nabla^{\mu}}}_{X,Y}Z)

for any
X
,
Y
,
Z\in\Gamma(TF)

from Remark
2.8
, where
p
is the projection onto the tangent component.
Using Corollary
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle 4n\big(Ric^{\nabla^{g}}(Y,Z)X-Ric^{\nabla^{g}}(X,Z)Y-Ric^{\nabla^{g}}(Y,I_{1}Z)I_{1}X+Ric^{\nabla^{g}}(X,I_{1}Z)I_{1}Y

\displaystyle-2Ric^{\nabla^{g}}(I_{1}X,Y)I_{1}Z\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4(n+2)\big(Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(Y,Z)X-Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,Z)Y-2Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(I_{1}X,Y)I_{1}Z

\displaystyle-Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(Y,I_{1}Z)I_{1}X+Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(X,I_{1}Z)I_{1}Y\big)

X
,
Y
,
Z\in\Gamma(TF)
. Taking the trace in the slot of
X
, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle nRic^{\nabla^{g}}(Y,Z)=(n+2)Ric^{\nabla^{\mu}}(Y,Z),

which means that

\iota^{\ast}c_{1}(M\setminus\mu^{-1}(0))=\frac{\tau}{8\pi(n+2)}[\omega_{F}]

where
\tau
is the scalar curvature of
g
,
\omega_{F}
is the Kähler form on
F

and
\iota:F\to M\setminus\mu^{-1}(0)
is the inclusion.
Since
F
is a compact Kähler manifold due to
[
4

, then
c_{1}(M\setminus\mu^{-1}(0))\neq 0
.
∎
