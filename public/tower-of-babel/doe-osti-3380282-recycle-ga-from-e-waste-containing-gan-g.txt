 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 Purdue University plans to use microorganisms to extract Ga from GaN/GaAs chips. This
 method is aimed at replacing traditional hydrometallurgy.

 Summary of Research Results:

 Task 1: Achieve High Leaching efficiency (90%) of Gallium.

 Purdue University will buy raw materials and microorganism and conduct bio-leaching
 experiments.

 Task 1 Results: The University of Utah conducted laboratory-scale bio-leaching experiments
 using gallium-containing waste LED to evaluate the feasibility of microbial gallium recovery.
 Due to the extremely low gallium concentration in the e-waste feedstock, the leaching
 experiments achieved an average gallium recovery efficiency of approximately 20%. The
 results indicate that gallium dilution in heterogeneous e-waste streams poses a fundamental
 limitation to achieving high leaching efficiency under laboratory conditions.

 Subsequent experimental efforts were suspended due to supply constraints associated with
 gallium nitride (GaN) powder. The identified commercial vendor for GaN powder
 (MilliporeSigma) was unable to provide the required material during the project period. As a
 result, further experimental validation and optimization of gallium leaching performance could
 not be completed.

 Task 2: Performance Design of Experiments based on Experiment Data.

 Purdue University will conduct design of experiments research based on experiments data to find
 the optimal condition of leaching and provide the results to NLR.

 Task 2 Results:

 No work was completed for this Task due to experimental challenges encountered in Task 1 and
 the subsequent lack of required feedstock materials.

 Task 3: Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) of the developing Technology.

 Purdue University will perform life cycle assessment of Ga leaching in lab-scale and provide the
 results to NLR. NLR will evaluate the results and recommend additional analysis.

 Task 3 Results:

 Purdue University completed a prospective life cycle assessment (LCA) of the gallium
 bioleaching process developed at the laboratory scale. The assessment followed ISO
 14040/14044 principles. The system boundary was defined on a cradle-to-gate basis, capturing
 upstream material inputs, energy consumption, and process emissions associated with gallium
 recovery via bioleaching. The LCA results indicate that gallium recovered through the
 bioleaching route exhibits substantially lower emissions compared to conventional primary
 gallium production.

 Task 4: Techno-Economic Assessment of the developing Technology (TEA).

 Purdue University will assess the cost of leaching using TEA and provide the results to NLR.
 NLR will evaluate the results and recommend additional analysis.

 Task 4 Results:

 Purdue University completed a comparative techno-economic assessment (TEA) of gallium
 recovery via bioleaching, hydro-annealing, and conventional primary gallium production. The
 results identified that cost reduction for secondary gallium recovery is strongly dependent on
 maintaining a sufficient supply of e-waste feedstock and avoiding excessively low gallium
 concentrations.

 The TEA results were disseminated through conference publications presented at the 2025
 ASME Manufacturing Science and Engineering Conference (MSEC) and the 2026 CIRP Life
 Cycle Engineering (LCE) Conference.
