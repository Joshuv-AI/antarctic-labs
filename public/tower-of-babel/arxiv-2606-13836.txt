Generalized affine spaces, generalized ovoids and generalized quadrangles
J. A. Thas Ghent University

Abstract
. We consider a partition of the projective space 𝐏𝐆 ⁡ ( m + n − 1 , 𝐊 ) \mathbf{PG}(m+n-1,\bf K) , with K any (commutative) field, into one space of dimension m − 1 m-1 while all other spaces of the partition have dimension n − 1 n-1 . Characterizations of particular partitions of this type are given. This research is motivated by some interesting problems on translation generalized quadrangles [ 9 ] . In the paper we apply our results to generalized ovoids and generalized quadrangles. Finally we give some ideas for further research. Keywords: projective space, affine space, spread, generalized ovoid, generalized quadrangle, translation MSC: 05B25, 51A30, 51A45, 51A15, 51A40, 51E12, 51E20, 51E21, 51E22, 51E23

7.
Generalized quadrangles

Here our results will be applied to finite translation generalized quadrangles (TGQ) of order
(s,s^{2}),s\not=1
. Again much of it can be generalized to the infinite case.

Definitions

A (finite)
generalized quadrangle (GQ)
is an incidence structure
\mathcal{S}=(P,B,\bf I)
in which
P
and
B
are disjoint (nonempty) sets of objects called
points
and
lines
(respectively) and for which
I
is a symmetric point-line incidence relation satisfying the following axioms:

(i)

Each point is incident with
1+t
lines
(t\geq 1)
and two distinct points are incident with at most one line.

(ii)

Each line is incident with
1+s
points
(s\geq 1)
and two distinct lines are incident with at most one point.

(iii)

If
x
is a point and
L
is a line not incident with
x
, then there is a unique pair
(y,M)\in P\times B
for which
x\mathbf{I}M\mathbf{I}y\mathbf{I}L
.

The integers
s
and
t
are the
parameters
of the GQ and
\mathcal{S}
is said to have
order

(s,t)
; if
s=t
, then
\mathcal{S}
is said to have
order

s
.

For terminology, notations, results, etc., concerning finite generalized quadrangles and not explicitly given here, see the monograph of Payne and Thas
[
5
]
.

Let
O=\{\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{q^{2n}}\},n\geq 1
, be a generalized ovoid in
\mathbf{PG}(4n-1,q)
. Let
\tau_{i}
be the tangent space of
O
at
\xi_{i},i=0,1,\ldots,q^{2n}
.

Now embed
\mathbf{PG}(4n-1,q)
in a
\mathbf{PG}(4n,q)
and construct a point-line geometry
T(n,2n,q)=T(O)
as follows.

Points are of three types:

(i)

The points of
\mathbf{PG}(4n,q)\setminus\mathbf{PG}(4n-1,q)
.

(ii)

The
3n
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbf{PG}(4n,q)
which intersect
\mathbf{PG}(4n-1,q)
in one of the spaces
\tau_{i}
.

(iii)

The symbol
(\infty)
.

Lines are of two types:

(a)

The
n
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbf{PG}(4n,q)
which intersect
\mathbf{PG}(4n-1,q)
in one of the spaces
\xi_{i}
.

(b)

The elements of
O
.

Incidence in
T(n,2n,q)
is defined as follows. A point of type (i) is incident only with lines of type (a); here the incidence is that of
\mathbf{PG}(4n,q)
. A point of type (ii) is incident with all lines of type (a) contained in it and with the unique element of
O
contained in it. The point
(\infty)
is incident with no line of type (a) and with all lines of type (b).

Theorem 7.1
.

The point-line geometry
T(n,2n,q)=T(O)
is a GQ of order
(q^{n},q^{2n})
.

An important class of GQ is the class of
translation generalized quadrangles
; see Payne and Thas
[
5
]
and Thas, Thas and Van Maldeghem
[
10
]
.

Theorem 7.2

(8.7.1 of Payne and Thas
[
5
]
)
.

The point-line geometry
T(O)
is a TGQ of order
(q^{n},q^{2n})
. Conversely, every TGQ of order
(s,s^{2}),s\not=1

It follows that the theory of the TGQ of order

, with

.

The generalized ovoid

\mathbf{PG}(4n-1,q)
is a pseudo-quadric if and only if the TGQ

is classical, that is, if and only if

In most of the results we have to assume that

To extend our results to the case

Condition (R)
