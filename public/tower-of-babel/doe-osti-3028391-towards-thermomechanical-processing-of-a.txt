 ABSTRACT
 The Advanced Reactor Technologies (ART) Program has established a
multiyear plan to develop Alloy 709 advanced stainless steel, generate the data
package, and develop material-specific design parameters in qualifying it as a
new structural material for Class A construction in the American Society of
Mechanical Engineers (ASME) Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code, Section III,
Division 5, High Temperature Reactors. In collaboration with material vendors,
the Advanced Materials Development activities under ART have successfully
scaled the Alloy 709 plate form production from a laboratory heat of 500 pounds
to commercial heats totaling 133,000 pounds of Alloy 709 plate fabricated from
three heats. The goal of the overall Alloy 709 development program is to
establish the necessary microstructural and mechanical properties relationship for
Alloy 709 to ultimately develop fabrication parameters for other product forms,
such as bars, pipes, and forgings using the available ART Alloy 709 materials.
This study will enable its deployment in the industry as an advanced construction
material for high-temperature components.
 The objective of this Alloy 709 development work in Fiscal Year 2025 is to
experimentally determine the high-temperature deformation-recrystallization
response of the Alloy 709 heats and to experimentally generate true stress–true
strain data for Alloy 709 using the available commercial heat plate materials.
Integral to this work is the previous characterization of the as-rolled materials,
the determination of an effective solution-annealing process, and the evaluation
of the effect of controlled cooling on the resultant precipitation in these
commercial heats. This report summarizes and builds on the results of the
previous reports to assess the high-temperature deformation behavior as a
function of deformation temperature, strain and strain rate using commercial
Alloy 709 Heat 58776-3RB fabricated by G. O. Carlson and Heat 529900-02
fabricated by ATI Specialty Rolled Products.

 1. INTRODUCTION
 The objective of this Alloy 709 development work is to establish thermomechanical processing
(TMP)—microstructure—mechanical properties relationships to develop fabrication parameters that can
be employed for product forms, such as bars, pipes, and forgings in addition to plates. This report presents
the results of the initial exploratory assessment of processing parameters, such as deformation
temperatures (900°C–1100°C), strain (0.1, 0.3, 0.5), and strain rate (1/s, 10/s), to determine the
recrystallization behavior of Alloy 709. Previous work in Fiscal Years 2023 and 2024 concentrated on
identifying the grain coarsening temperature (for determining the required solution-annealing
temperature) and precipitation response during controlled cooling from high temperature for A709 using
the as-rolled commercial heat plate materials. This information was reported by Wang et al. (2023) and
Burke et al. (2023).
 The results of the Alloy 709 grain coarsening temperature (TGC) study (Wang et al., 2023)
demonstrated that a solution-anneal temperature of 1200°C was required for this material. However, the
commercial ASTM specification for solution annealing Alloy 709 states that the solution anneal
temperature must be above 1150°C. Therefore, to assess whether that minimum temperature is adequate,
some samples were annealed at 1175°C prior to tests whereas others were solution-annealed at 1200°C.
This present study was performed by the University of Pittsburgh Ferrous Physical Metallurgy Group,
under the direction of Professor C. Isaac Garcia using the Commercial Heat 58776-3R fabricated by G. O.
Carlson and Heat 529900-02 fabricated by ATI Specialty Rolled Products.
 1.1. Background: Recrystallization
 Recrystallization, including static and dynamic recrystallization, is a process by which a
polycrystalline metal containing a high dislocation density produced via plastic deformation is
transformed into new, strain-free, equiaxed grains, with a significantly reduced dislocation density. The
main driving force for recrystallization is the decrease in stored energy associated with the dislocations
generated due to plastic strain during the deformation process. Some factors that affect recrystallization
include: 1) the extent and type of deformation (strain and strain rate), 2) temperature, 3) time at
temperature, and 4) grain size of the alloy. (Humphreys and Hatherly, 1995)
 During TMP of austenitic steels, larger reheated grains of austenite are refined by static
recrystallization that occurs between the high-temperature roughing passes during hot-rolling. At
temperatures below the recrystallization stop temperature, T5%, no recrystallization of the austenite grains
will occur, and any deformation will lead to a “pancaked” (deformed) grain structure. There is another
temperature, T95%, above which any deformation will lead to full recrystallization. Between T95% and T5%
high-temperature deformation will lead to partial recrystallization of the alloy. This is shown
schematically in Figure 1. (DeArdo, 2001) As the deformation temperature increases, very fine
recrystallized grains can nucleate at the original (deformed) austenite grain boundaries, deformation
bands, and twin boundaries in addition to any nitride or carbide inclusions in the alloy. During high-
temperature deformation, partially-recrystallized microstructures will develop between the stop-stop (T5%)
temperature and the start-stop (T95%) temperature. At high temperatures and high levels of deformation
above the T95%, all grains will be recrystallized. Full recrystallization can also occur during the interpass

times, which is the time between high-temperature deformations. Partially-recrystallized microstructures
develop during high-temperature deformation between T5% and T95%; however, if the alloy is held in that
temperature range, the fine recrystallized grains will grow and consume the deformed grains so that the
microstructure will then be completely recrystallized. (DeArdo, 2001, DeArdo et al., 2009)

 The flow curves and austenite microstructure are directly influenced by the chemical composition,
and these can be described by the Zener–Hollomon parameter Z, which is a temperature-compensated
strain rate. This parameter relates the changes in temperature and strain rate to the stress-strain behavior
of a material.
 For deformations with a low Z value, the dislocations accumulate until the critical point where the
dynamic restoration process occurs. As shown in Figure 2a, before the critical strain for dynamic recovery
(𝜀𝜀𝑐𝑐𝑟𝑟 ) is reached, the dislocations are accumulated through work-hardening. Between the critical strain for

𝜀𝜀𝑐𝑐𝑅𝑅 , dynamic recrystallization occurs with the new recrystallized grains forming at high energy sites (i.e.,
grain or subgrain boundaries, deformation bands, etc.). (DeArdo et al., 2009)
 For deformations with high Z values, a small amount of restoration occurs, and a deformed austenite
microstructure with a high dislocation density is produced. These microstructures have high stored energy
that can trigger static restoration processes. (Cui, 2016)

 2.1. Materials
 The as-received Heat 58776-3R plate, Lot Number 58776-3RBA1 fabricated by G. O. Carlson (the
Carlson heat), had a thickness of 1.125 inch. This material had been solution annealed at 1050°C. The as-
received plate from Heat 529900-02, fabricated by ATI (the ATI heat) with a plate identification of
CG05368, had a thickness of 1.8 inch. This plate was solution-annealed at a minimum of 1150°C and

 Cylindrical compression specimens were machined from these plates, with the specimen axis (length)
aligned along the rolling direction. For the Carlson specimens, a 0.25 inch-thick surface layer was

layers was to ensure the specimens had a uniform as-received microstructure.

 2.2. High-Temperature Compression Tests
 One hundred compression specimens of each commercial Alloy 709 grade (Carlson and ATI) were
received from Oak Ridge National Laboratory. The cylindrical specimens had dimensions of 0.75-inch in
length and 0.5-inch diameter. A small 0.08-inch-diameter hole was drilled at mid-height to insert a
thermocouple for temperature control during the compression test. Figure 3 shows a typical as-received
specimen. These specimens were oriented so that the length was parallel to the rolling direction.
 For this study, the ATI samples were encapsulated under vacuum and solution-annealed at 1200°C
for 1 hour followed by a water quench to produce a uniform and fully-recrystallized austenite grain
structure prior to the high-temperature compression tests. The Carlson samples were also encapsulated
under vacuum and solution-annealed in accordance with ASTM A240-240M for Alloy 709/UNS 31025,
which states that a solution-anneal temperature must be above 1150°C. Thus, the Carlson specimens
were solution-annealed at 1175°C for 30 minutes and water quenched, thereby providing a set of
specimens that fulfilled the ASTM solution-anneal requirement. Note that microstructural data for the
1150°C anneal had been obtained and reported by Wang et al. (2023). All annealing treatments were
conducted with an MTI Corporation tube furnace model GSL 1500X OTF, capable of heating from room
temperature to 1250°C.
 The high-temperature compression deformation tests were performed using a Gleeble Model 3500-
GTC system capable of deforming the specimens at temperatures up to and including 1100°C to acquire
flow stress versus strain curves. The specimens were reheated to 1100ºC for 10 minutes, then cooled to
the relevant deformation temperature, deformed in compression, and immediately cooled with
compressed air, according to specific conditions shown in the test matrix in Table 2. The flow stress at the
deformation temperature was determined, and the true stress and true strain flow curves were produced.
The temperature of each specimen was monitored during the tests.

 2.3. Microstructure Evaluation Techniques
 The as-tested compression specimens were sectioned parallel to the deformation axis, as shown in
Figure 3, and were subsequently prepared for microstructural analysis using standard metallographic
techniques. Specimens were mounted in a 1.5-inch mount using thermoset Copper Bakelite powder
(Struers CitoPress Mounting) and manually ground using SiC papers from 120 to 1200 grit, followed by
manual polishing for 5 minutes using a 0.05 µm Al2O3 suspension and vibratory polishing (Pace
Technologies Giga-S 900) for a minimum of 12 hours using a 0.05 µm deagglomerated Al2O3. After
vibratory polishing, samples were cleaned in an ethanol ultrasonic bath for 15 minutes and dried with
compressed air.
 Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) characterization was performed using an FEI Scios FIB-SEM
Dual Beam system equipped with an EDAX EBSD detector with specimens tilted at 70º towards the
detector at a 14 mm working distance. Data were collected with a 20 kV electron beam and 13nA probe
current. Area scans were acquired at 250× magnification with a 0.7 µm step size and a 500 × 500 µm scan
area. Supplementary microstructural characterization was performed using the Zeiss Sigma Variable
Pressure field emission gun scanning electron microscope (FEG-SEM) operated at 20 kV and equipped
with an Oxford Instruments XMax 100 Silicon Drift Detector and Aztec analysis system.
 For low-magnification light optical microscopy (LOM) using a Keyence VHX 970F microscope,
samples were etched by immersion in a V2A solution for 10 minutes at 40ºC to reveal the austenite
grains. The V2A solution contained 50 mL of hydrochloric acid (HCl), 50 mL of deionized water, and
5 mL of nitric acid (HNO3). Optical micrographs were acquired at 200×, and ImageJ software
(Version 1.54g) was used to manually assess the approximate extent of recrystallization (area percent).

 3. RESULTS
 3.1. High-Temperature Deformation-Recrystallization Behavior of
 Carlson Alloy 709
3.1.1. Microstructural Characterization of the As-Annealed and Deformed
 Carlson Alloy 709 Specimens
 The as-received Carlson Alloy 709 specimen solution-annealed at 1175°C for 30 minutes and water-
quenched was evaluated using LOM to assess the grain structure. Examination of the etched specimen
revealed the presence of very coarse and fine recrystallized grains, as shown in Figure 4. However, the
microstructure was very nonuniform.

 5

Figure 4. Light optical micrographs of the etched as-annealed (1175°C for 0.5 hour) Carlson Alloy 709
sample. Note the inhomogeneous grain structures and the faint vertical topographic bands in the etched
specimen (aligned with the rolling direction of the plate).

 Numerous coarse and fine annealing twins were observed with dispersed regions containing fine
grains. The visible vertical “band-like” features in the micrograph suggest possible microsegregation
from the cast alloy. In addition, numerous blocky inclusions were visible in the material.
3.1.1.1.      900°C Deformation
 The microstructures developed as a function of strain and strain rate at 900°C are presented in the
LOM images of Figure 5. The micrographs in the left column were deformed in compression at a strain
rate of 1/s whereas those in the right column experienced a strain rate of 10/s. The specimens deformed
to a strain of 0.1 (Figure 5a and 5b) showed no pronounced evidence of deformation as determined via
optical metallography whereas some slip bands observed in Figure 5c and 5d deformed to 0.3 strain. The
most pronounced deformation structures were associated with the specimens deformed to 0.5 strain
(Figure 5e and 5f). The slip bands can be clearly seen in the coarse grains of the etched specimens.
Numerous fine grains are also visible, but these grains were present prior to the deformation at 900°C.
Due to the high degree of deformation, the etchant reacted strongly with the emergent slip bands,
resulting in very aggressive attack and dark regions in the sample associated with the fine grains.
 Due to the complexity of the nonuniform initial grain sizes in the as-annealed condition, it was not
possible to conclusively assess whether there had been any recrystallization in the 0.5 strained specimens.
Thus, some polished samples were examined in the FEG-SEM using backscattered electron (BSE) and
secondary electron (SE) imaging. Figure 6 is a BSE montage obtained from the 10/s sample strained to
0.5. The channeling contrast in the BSE image clearly shows a heavily deformed microstructure
containing brightly-imaging, blocky inclusions, which were shown by energy dispersive x-ray
spectroscopy to be Nb-rich. There was no evidence of recrystallization in any of these specimens.

3.1.1.2.      1000°C Deformation
 Figure 7 shows the effect of deformation at 1000°C on the Carlson Alloy 709 annealed material as a

3.1.3. Flow Stress Curves (True Stress–True Strain) for Carlson Alloy 709
 The hot deformation behavior was monitored during the compression tests, and the data are presented
in flow stress curves (true stress–true strain curves). These are summarized in for each strain rate (1/s and
10/sec) and are presented for each deformation temperature in Figure 14.

Figure 14. Flow stress curves for all Carlson compression tests at 1/s and 10/s strain rates.

 At a strain rate of 1/s and 0.5 strain, the stress at 900°C was approximately 380 MPa, whereas at
1000°C, the value decreased to 260 MPa, and at the highest deformation temperature (1100°C), the stress
dropped to ~160 MPa. Similar trends with deformation temperature were noted for the other strains
evaluated.

 14

 At the higher strain rate of 10/s and 0.5 strain, the stress at 900°C was notably higher than at 1/s, with
a value of ~425 MPa, followed by 1000°C (~320 MPa), and the lowest stress (~215 MPa) for deformation
at 1100°C.
 These data show that as the deformation temperature increases, the flow stress decreases. Higher
temperatures reduce the resistance to deformation, and the flow stress curves shift downward as the
temperature increases. In addition, the dislocation mobility increases so that dynamic recovery and
recrystallization processes are enhanced. Both processes soften the material and reduce the flow stress.
 As the strain is increased, the flow stress also increases due to the generation of more dislocations
during deformation, leading to work-hardening. In addition, as the strain rate increases, there is less time
for recovery and recrystallization during deformation. As a result, dislocations accumulate more rapidly,
thereby raising the flow stress.

 4. DISCUSSION
 4.1. Solution-Annealing Temperature
 In this exploratory study to scope out boundaries for TMP “space” (deformation temperature, strain
and strain rate effects), the ASTM specification for this Alloy (ASTM240/240M) states that the annealing
temperature must be greater than 1150°C. Therefore, the Carlson Alloy 709 specimens (0.75 inch long by
0.5 inch diameter) were solution-annealed at 1175°C for 30 minutes and water-quenched prior to
compression deformation tests. The plate from which these specimens were obtained had been previously
annealed at 1050°C by the supplier. The 1175°C solution-annealing treatment resulted in a coarse-
grained recrystallized austenite structure in some areas and a fine-grained austenite structure mixed with
coarse grains in other regions of the sample. Thus, this was not a desirable structure despite following the
ASTM guideline.
 Previous work performed and reported in 2023 documented the TGC for the Carlson Alloy 709 heat,
as this temperature reflects the dissolution of high-temperature Nb-rich carbides/carbonitrides (NbC,
Nb(CN)) that form in this high N–high C austenitic stainless steel. The TGC was determined to be
1200°C. (Wang et al., 2023)
 The initial FEG-SEM evaluation of the Carlson specimens deformed at 1100°C was focused on
identification of recrystallized grains versus deformed grains but also provided important information on

 23

the general microstructure. Figure 24 is an SE image showing the microstructure of the specimen
deformed to 0.5 strain at 1100°C and 10/s. The coarse Nb-rich inclusions (identified via SEM-EDXS
analysis) are visible on the surface of this electropolished sample. In addition, there are very fine
submicron particles on the specimen surface, consistent with the presence of undissolved NbC/NbCN.
Thus, this experiment concerning the solution annealing temperature for Alloy 709 supports the 2023
report results.

annealed at 1175°C for 30 min, water-quenched and deformed at 1100°C at 10/s to a strain of 0.5. Note
the presence of coarse inclusions and very fine submicron particles present in this material.

 The higher flow stresses during deformation will affect the ability to impart higher levels of strain as

recrystallized microstructure in the final product/component. A partially-recrystallized microstructure
will be inhomogeneous and can be extremely difficult to ‘correct’ during subsequent hot deformation or
post-TMP heat treatments in a final component. Such inhomogeneous microstructures may also impact
long-term performance of the material in service.

 5. SUMMARY
 This initial project to assess the deformation-recrystallization behavior as a function of deformation
temperature, strain, and strain rate provided relevant information concerning the lower bound for TMP for
two heats of Alloy 709. The span of strains evaluated in this initial work has highlighted the need to
include higher strains when mapping out the viable TMP “space.” All data generated in this study
pertained to dynamic recrystallization during compression deformation, which is relevant to forging and
hot-rolling processes.
 This study has shown:
• A solution-annealing temperature of 1200°C, identified in the previous report (Wang et al., 2023), is
 necessary for NbC/NbCN dissolution and to promote a more homogeneous microstructure.
• The high-temperature compression test results showed that 900°C is below the recrystallization start
 temperature for strains up to 0.5 for both 1/s and 10/s strain rates.
• The results of the 1000°C and 1100°C compression tests revealed that no recrystallization occurred
 for a strain of 0.1 for either strain rate tested.
• Tests conducted at 1000°C showed that no recrystallization occurred for a strain of 0.3 at a strain rate
 of 1/s, whereas a strain rate of 10/s induced a small amount of dynamic recrystallization at some
 original austenite grain boundaries as measured via EBSD. Some dynamic recrystallization occurred
 in the samples deformed to 0.5 strain, with more dynamic recrystallization observed for the 10/s strain
 rate compared to the 1/s strain rate.
• Dynamic recrystallization occurred in the specimen deformed to 0.3 and 0.5 strain at 1100°C for both
 strain rates, with more recrystallization with the 10/s strain rate.

 6. FUTURE WORK
 Based on the results of this initial study, the work will be extended to include a strain of 0.7 and will
focus on the 1100°C and 1000°C deformations. In addition, selected tests will be performed on the
Carlson Alloy 709 compressions specimens annealed at 1200°C for 1 hour to assess the effect of the
microstructure on flow stress and extent of recrystallization. Selected specimens will be further
characterized and the fraction recrystallized for hot-deformed specimens will be assessed using SEM-
based techniques.
 During forging and rolling operations, the alloy will have an “interpass” time between deformation
passes, during which time some static recrystallization will occur. This will also be examined in the
second phase of this project.
