Robust load frequency control with honeypot-based compensation and AETM under FDI attacks
Authors: ChuanCai Chen, Hao Wu, Shijie Li, Tingwen Yu, Haiguang Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper investigates the load frequency control (LFC) problem of power systems under actuator-side false data injection (FDI) attacks. First, an FDI-aware LFC model is developed to describe corruption in the control input channel. Subsequently, an adaptive event-triggered mechanism (AETM) is introduced to improve communication efficiency, and transmission lag is incorporated into the closed-loop model. Furthermore, a honeypot-gated adaptive compensation mechanism is proposed, where the honeypot detection flag activates the compensation channel and the estimated attack signal reduces the effective malicious component entering the actuator. Based on Lyapunov–Krasovskii functionals, sufficient LMI-based conditions are derived for stability analysis and controller synthesis with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H_{\infty }$$\end{document} performance. Finally, simulation results under different attack scenarios show that the proposed method attenuates FDI-induced frequency regulation errors and reduces unnecessary event-triggered transmissions.

Introduction

Load Frequency Control (LFC) is essential for maintaining frequency stability and ensuring the secure operation of interconnected power systems1–3. By regulating the power imbalance between generation and load demand, LFC suppresses frequency deviations and restores the system to its nominal operating condition under external disturbances4,5. With the increasing integration of renewable energy sources and large-scale interconnected grids, maintaining robust and reliable frequency regulation has become increasingly challenging6,7.

Recent studies have focused on optimizing LFC performance in renewable-integrated and interconnected power systems. Multi-area coordination strategies have been developed to enhance tie-line power regulation under load disturbances8,9. In addition, wind-integrated10 and EV-assisted frequency regulation frameworks11 have been investigated to improve system flexibility and dynamic response capability. Advanced control methodologies, such as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H_\infty$$\end{document} control12, model predictive control13, and event-triggered strategies14, have also been proposed to enhance robustness and communication efficiency.

Beyond power-system applications, adaptive control methods have also been extended to networked nonlinear systems with input delays and parametric uncertainties, such as electro-hydraulic servo systems15,16. However, these studies mainly address trajectory tracking rather than cyber-resilient LFC under actuator-side FDI attacks.

With the increasing dependence on communication networks, LFC systems have become vulnerable to various cyber-attacks17. Typical threats include denial-of-service (DoS) attacks18, deception attacks19, replay attacks20, and false data injection (FDI) attacks21, all of which can disrupt the normal operation of the control loop. To enhance resilience against cyber and network-induced disturbances, various attack-aware LFC strategies have been proposed in recent years. Disturbance-observer-based and sliding-mode approaches were developed to estimate and compensate system uncertainties and malicious perturbations22. In parallel, observer-based \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H_\infty$$\end{document} control frameworks were adopted to attenuate the adverse effects of cyber-attacks, packet losses, and communication delays in networked environments23. With respect to DoS attacks, resilient LFC designs incorporating attack-parameter-dependent time-varying Lyapunov methods were investigated to guarantee closed-loop stability under constrained attack conditions24. Furthermore, event-triggered resilient communication mechanisms were introduced to alleviate network burden while maintaining stability during DoS incidents25. In addition, secure signal extraction techniques based on multi-channel redundancy and filtering schemes were proposed to mitigate FDI attacks by reconstructing intact measurements from corrupted signals26.

Beyond these LFC-oriented studies, recent works have also investigated secure control of broader networked systems under cyber-attacks, delays, and communication constraints. For power-system-oriented problems, DoS-resilient stability analysis of multi-area interconnected systems was studied in27. For general networked control systems, hybrid-triggered control with two-sided looped functionals was developed to handle time-varying delays and DoS attacks28. Truncated predictive control was investigated for delayed networked control systems under dual cyber-physical attacks29. Extended dissipative predictive control was further studied for nonlinear delayed systems affected by cyber-attacks and sensor distortion30. In addition, observer-based secure control was reported for wireless networked control systems under stochastic deception attacks31. Although these studies provide useful references for cyber-resilient control, proactive compensation-oriented defense for actuator-side FDI attacks in LFC systems remains less explored.

In addition to resilient control strategies, event-triggered communication mechanisms have been widely investigated to reduce network burden in LFC systems. Static32,33 and dynamic triggering rules34,35 were developed to balance communication efficiency and control performance, while adaptive threshold designs were introduced to accommodate varying system conditions36. Recently, intelligent event-triggered control and data compression mechanisms supervised by mini-batch machine learning have also been investigated for NCSs under DoS attacks to improve bandwidth utilization and robustness37,38. However, these works mainly focus on general NCSs or autonomous vehicle systems, while the coordinated design of AETM and honeypot-gated compensation for actuator-side FDI attacks in LFC systems remains insufficiently studied.

In the cybersecurity domain, honeypots have been widely adopted as proactive defense mechanisms that emulate vulnerable systems to attract and analyze malicious activities. Honeynet-based architectures have demonstrated their effectiveness in detecting and logging attacker behaviors in cloud environments39, while IoT-oriented honeypot systems have been developed to simulate vulnerable devices and capture adversarial patterns in cyber-physical contexts40. These studies indicate that honeypots can serve as effective tools for early attack awareness and behavioral analysis. However, their integration into LFC systems, especially for improving resilience against FDI attacks, remains insufficiently explored.

In summary, although resilient LFC, event-triggered communication, and secure networked control have been widely studied, the integration of proactive cyber-defense information with compensation-based LFC design remains insufficiently explored. In particular, how to utilize honeypot-based detection information to mitigate actuator-side FDI attacks while preserving event-triggered communication efficiency is still an open issue.

Motivated by the above discussion, this paper investigates the LFC problem under actuator-side FDI attacks and proposes a honeypot-gated adaptive compensation framework combined with an AETM. The main contributions are summarized as follows:

(1) An actuator-side FDI-aware LFC model is established to characterize malicious corruption in the control input channel. By incorporating the AETM and transmission lag, an attack-affected closed-loop model is developed for subsequent stability analysis.

(2) A honeypot-gated adaptive compensation mechanism is proposed to attenuate the influence of FDI attacks. The honeypot mechanism provides a detection flag to activate the compensation channel, and the compensation signal is generated to reduce the effective malicious component entering the actuator channel.

(3) Based on Lyapunov–Krasovskii functionals, sufficient LMI-based conditions are derived for stability analysis and controller synthesis. The proposed method is verified through comparative simulations under attack-free, uncompensated FDI, and compensated FDI cases.

Adaptive event-triggered mechanism and unified modeling with transmission lag

In networked LFC systems, continuous transmission of measurement data may impose a heavy communication burden and even cause network congestion. To reduce unnecessary data transmission, a state-dependent AETM is introduced between the sensor and the controller. Different from periodic transmission, the proposed mechanism updates the transmitted data only when the system state deviation is sufficiently significant.

Remark 1

The AETM enables on-demand data transmission by adapting the triggering threshold to the system operating condition. The exponential term in (7) makes the threshold sensitive to the transmission error \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$e(i_nh)$$\end{document}. When disturbances or oscillations enlarge the error, the triggering condition becomes more responsive, so that timely state updates can be provided during critical transient periods. When the system approaches steady state, redundant transmissions are avoided, thereby reducing communication overhead while preserving the required control performance.

Honeypot-gated adaptive compensation mechanism

To mitigate the adverse influence of stealthy FDI attacks, a honeypot-gated adaptive compensation mechanism is introduced in this subsection. The honeypot is employed as a proactive cyber-defense layer to capture suspicious intrusion behaviors and generate a binary detection signal \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\chi (t)\in \{0,1\}$$\end{document}. Specifically, χ (t)=1 indicates that an intrusion has been detected, while χ (t)=0 corresponds to normal operation. This detection signal is used to determine whether the compensation channel should be activated.

The unknown FDI signal is reconstructed by a honeypot-gated adaptive estimator. Let \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{a}(t)$$\end{document} denote the estimate of the injected FDI signal a(t), and let \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$s_a(t)$$\end{document} denote the attack-related signal captured by the honeypot. The estimator is updated as11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \hat{a}(t^{+})= {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} (1-\beta _a)\hat{a}(t)+\beta _a s_a(t), & \chi (t)=1,\\ (1-\alpha _a)\hat{a}(t), & \chi (t)=0, \end{array}\right. } \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta _a\in (0,1)$$\end{document} is the update gain and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha _a\in (0,1)$$\end{document} is the leakage rate. When an intrusion is detected, the estimate is updated using the honeypot-captured signal. When no intrusion is detected, the estimate gradually decays to avoid estimator drift under normal operation.

To prevent excessive compensation, the estimate is further bounded by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{a}(t)\leftarrow \min \left\{ \max \left\{ \hat{a}(t),-\hat{a}_{\max }\right\} , \hat{a}_{\max } \right\} ,$$\end{document} where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{a}_{\max }>0$$\end{document} is the prescribed estimation bound.

Based on the estimated attack signal, the compensated control command is constructed as\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$u_c(t)=u(t)-\chi (t)\hat{a}(t),$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$u_c(t)$$\end{document} denotes the compensated control command, and u(t) is the nominal event-triggered control command. Since the input channel may still be affected by the injected FDI signal, the actual input applied to the LFC system is expressed as\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \begin{aligned} u_{\textrm{exe}}(t)&=u_c(t)+a(t)\\&=u(t)+a(t)-\chi (t)\hat{a}(t), \end{aligned} \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$u_{\textrm{exe}}(t)$$\end{document} denotes the actually executed input received by the actuator.

For notational convenience, the remaining uncompensated attack component is denoted by\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \tilde{a}(t)=a(t)-\chi (t)\hat{a}(t), \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tilde{a}(t)$$\end{document} represents the residual attack signal after honeypot-gated compensation. Thus, the proposed compensation mechanism reduces the effective malicious component entering the input channel from a(t) to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tilde{a}(t)$$\end{document}. When χ (t)=0, the compensation channel is inactive and the system operates under the original attacked input. When χ (t)=1, the estimated attack signal is used to counteract the injected malicious signal.

Remark 2

The honeypot-gated compensation mechanism acts on the control input channel rather than on the physical plant dynamics. The honeypot provides the binary detection signal χ (t) to determine whether the compensation channel should be activated. When no intrusion is detected, the nominal event-triggered control command is preserved. Once an intrusion is detected, the estimated attack signal is used to compensate the malicious injection. Therefore, this mechanism aims to attenuate the influence of FDI attacks while avoiding unnecessary compensation under normal load variations.

Remark 3

It should be emphasized that the honeypot mechanism is not introduced as an additional physical subsystem of the LFC model. It does not change the state vector, system matrices, or plant dynamics. Conclusion

This paper has investigated the LFC problem of power systems under actuator-side FDI attacks. First, an FDI-aware LFC model has been developed to describe malicious corruption in the control input channel, and an AETM-based closed-loop model with transmission lag has been established. Subsequently, a honeypot-gated adaptive compensation mechanism has been proposed, in which the honeypot detection flag activates the compensation channel and the estimated attack signal is used to reduce the effective malicious component entering the actuator. Based on Lyapunov–Krasovskii functionals, sufficient LMI-based conditions have been derived for stability analysis and controller synthesis with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H_{\infty }$$\end{document} performance. Simulation results under attack-free, uncompensated FDI, and compensated FDI cases have shown that the proposed method attenuates FDI-induced frequency regulation errors and reduces unnecessary event-triggered transmissions.
