Interventions for Silent Brain Infarction and Perioperative Neurocognitive Disorders in Cardiovascular Surgery (INSPIRE): study protocol for a multicentre randomised controlled trial
Authors: Lu Wang, Shu Li, Philip James Devereaux, Hong Lv, Ting Li, Luchuan Zhang, Yi An, Jingli Chen, Changwei Wei, Lei Zhao, Yuming Peng, Jia Shi

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe incidence of silent brain infarction (SBI) and perioperative neurocognitive disorders (PND) is higher in cardiac surgery. However, standard preventive strategies remain unknown due to limited evidence.Methods and analysisThis multicentre, prospective, randomised controlled clinical trial with a 1-year follow-up includes patients undergoing elective cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB). 912 participants are randomly assigned 1:1 into either the intervention group with neuroprotective anaesthesia targets (mean arterial pressure 65–90 mm Hg, bispectral index 40–60, bilateral regional cerebral oxygen saturation ≥60%, arterial inflow temperature <37℃ and rewarming rate <0.5℃/min after exceeding 30℃ during CPB) or the control group maintained by anaesthetists according to routine care. The primary outcome is the 7-day incidence of new-onset SBI. The key secondary outcome is the 30-day incidence of PND. The secondary outcomes include the 5-day incidence of postoperative delirium, the 30-day incidence of new-onset overt stroke, the 1-year incidence of new-onset overt stroke, the 30-day incidence of transient ischaemic attack (TIA), the 1-year incidence of TIA, the 1-year incidence of PND, the 30-day and 1-year composite incidence of major adverse events (renal insufficiency, myocardial infarction, pulmonary embolism, seizure, all-cause mortality), length of stay (LOS) in intensive care unit, LOS in hospital and hospitalisation cost.Ethics and disseminationThe trial was approved by the Institutional Review Board/Independent Ethics Committee of Fuwai Hospital (Approval No. 2024-2445) and all participating centres. Introduction

Stroke continues to be a devastating complication that increases morbidity, readmission risk and mortality in the setting of cardiac surgery, which represents a major concern for patients.1 2 Silent brain infarction (SBI) is defined as the presence of cerebral infarction by neuroimaging in the absence of any clinically apparent stroke symptoms.3 4 Modern advances in MRI have facilitated the detection of SBI, even in healthy people, and the incidence increases within specific populations (eg, patients with hypertension).5 Accumulating evidence on the association of SBI with stroke, impaired cognitive function, dementia and depression has indicated that these silent infarctions are neither silent nor innocuous.6,8

Perioperative SBI occurred in 7% of 1114 patients ≥65 years of age undergoing elective non-cardiac surgery, in an international prospective cohort study, and was associated with an increased risk of cognitive decline 1 year after surgery.9 Another multicentre prospective cohort study demonstrated that among 934 patients who had major non-cardiac surgery, mainly intracranial, 11.9% had experienced covert stroke, which more than doubled the risk of postoperative delirium and long-term neurocognitive decline.10 SBI may not be a rare event after cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB).11 The reported prevalence of SBI varies between 14% and 50%, depending on the cardiac disease and type of cardiac surgery (eg, aortic valve procedures are associated with a higher incidence).12 13

Patients and clinicians now recognise cognitive decline as a patient–important complication after cardiac surgery.14,16 The incidence of perioperative neurocognitive disorders (PND), defined as neurocognitive abnormalities detected during the perioperative periods, including preoperative cognitive impairment, acute delirium after surgery, delayed neurocognitive recovery and postoperative neurocognitive disorders, is high, with more than 50% at discharge.17,19 These adverse neurological outcomes are associated with prolonged length of stay (LOS), long-term morbidity and mortality and increased healthcare expenditure for patients and providers.20 There remains uncertainty regarding the relationship between SBI and neurological cognitive impairment in cardiovascular procedures.21

SBI has been identified as the subclinical stage of symptomatic stroke. Many radiologic and postmortem studies show that perioperative strokes are ischaemic and embolic.22 According to our previous meta-analysis, the possible mechanisms of SBI are embolism and hypoperfusion.23 Currently, there are no standard preventive strategies for SBI or PND in clinical practice. While neuro-first intraoperative anaesthetic management strategies focus on cerebral perfusion and autoregulation, optimal mean arterial pressure (MAP), bispectral index (BIS)-guided anaesthesia, regional cerebral oxygen saturation monitoring (rSO2) and appropriate temperature management seem to be protective against perioperative neurological injury and may affect SBI.24,27 We hypothesise that these combined anaesthetic interventions will be protective for neurological function.

The perfusion pressure (ie, the MAP) may contribute to the occurrence of cerebral infarction. According to a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials, the effect of high (compared with low) blood pressure targets on stroke or cognitive decline after cardiac surgery remains unclear.28 A randomised trial (n=248) concluded that the incidence of cardiac and neurologic complications, including stroke, was significantly lower in the higher MAP group targeting 80–100 versus 50–60 mm Hg during coronary artery bypass graft.29 Another trial (n=197) showed that targeting a higher MAP during CPB did not affect new cerebral infarcts among patients undergoing on-pump cardiac surgery (70–80 vs 40–50 mm Hg).30 An observational study has reported that MAP <55 mm Hg was related to adverse clinical outcomes, including acute kidney injury and myocardial injury.31 Sun et al reported a retrospective cohort study of 7457 patients that defined an intraoperative hypotension threshold in association with stroke in cardiac surgery.32 They found that MAP <64 mm Hg during CPB for every 10 min was strongly related to stroke and suggested that MAP may be an important intraoperative therapeutic haemodynamic target to reduce the incidence of stroke in on-pump surgery. Hence, optimal MAP may influence SBI, and more research is needed to determine this effect. In light of the above published evidence, we set the MAP range at 65–90 mm Hg to evoke a protective effect on neurological complications.

With the development of processed electroencephalographic monitor measures, BIS is applied to individualise the depth of anaesthesia. Some studies explored the effect of anaesthetic depth on complications after major surgery and concluded that deep general anaesthesia was not associated with higher occurrence of stroke or mortality, but higher incidence of delirium (BIS target of 35).33,35 And there was a stronger relationship between deep anaesthesia and complications when blood pressure was also low.36 37 Pierik et al concluded that intraoperative BIS values below 25 and a longer duration of MAP below 60 mm Hg could be used to predict perioperative stroke risk in cardiothoracic surgery.38 Prolonged concurrent hypotension and low BIS may lead to neurological complications, so we assembled a bundle of intraoperative anaesthetic targets to determine their effect on the incidence of SBI.

Beyond MAP or BIS, rSO2 is another important parameter that could impact SBI. Area under the threshold below an absolute value of 50% for cerebral oxygen saturation was shown to be an independent risk factor of adverse outcomes, including stroke, renal failure and deep sternal wound infection, etc, for cardiac surgical patients.39 Optimising cerebral oxygenation (rSO2≥60%) was associated with better neurocognitive outcomes, especially memory.40 Higher rSO2 values may be beneficial, but the neuroprotective role of rSO2-guided interventions in preventing stroke or PND after cardiac surgery remains uncertain.41 42 Additionally, whether the use of hypothermia and slow rewarming during CPB could attenuate central nervous system consequences remains unknown.27 Therefore, more high-quality evidence on these interventions is needed.

The objectives of our trial are to evaluate the efficacy of intraoperative anaesthesia targets (MAP 65–90 mm Hg, BIS 40–60, bilateral rSO2≥60%, arterial inflow temperature <37℃ and rewarming rate <0.5℃/min after exceeding 30℃ during CPB) in reducing neurological complications. The primary outcome is the 7-day incidence of new-onset SBI. The key secondary outcome is the 30-day incidence of PND. Secondary outcomes also include the 5-day incidence of postoperative delirium, the 30-day incidence of new-onset overt stroke, the 1-year incidence of new-onset overt stroke, the 30-day incidence of transient ischaemic attack (TIA), the 1-year incidence of TIA, the 1-year incidence of PND, the 30-day and 1-year composite incidence of major adverse events (renal insufficiency, myocardial infarction, pulmonary embolism, seizure and all-cause mortality), LOS in intensive care unit (ICU), LOS in hospital and hospitalisation cost.

Trial design

The Interventions for Silent Brain Infarction and Perioperative Neurocognitive Disorders in Cardiovascular Surgery (INSPIRE) trial is a multicentre, randomised trial investigating whether an intraoperative neuroprotective anaesthesia bundle reduces the incidence of SBI after cardiac surgery with CPB. Elective patients undergoing cardiac surgery with CPB will be recruited from Fuwai Hospital, Beijing Tiantan Hospital, Xuanwu Hospital, Beijing Chaoyang Hospital and the Central Hospital of Wuhan. Participants, surgeons and outcome assessors will be blinded to treatment allocation. Preoperative interviews will be conducted by specially trained research assistants who will inform patients about the study objectives, potential risks and benefits, and obtain written informed consent from participants or their legal representatives (online supplemental file 3). Study population

Inclusion criteria

Patients aged ≥60 years and scheduled to undergo elective cardiac surgery with CPB will be recruited for screening eligibility at least 1 day before surgery.

Exclusion criteria

Patients fulfilling one or more of the following criteria will be excluded: (1) contraindication to MRI scanning; (2) not suitable for receiving the target bundle intervention; (3) unable to receive neurocognitive evaluation due to language, vision or hearing impairments; (4) breastfeeding or pregnancy; (5) terminal illness with a life expectancy of less than 3 months; (6) mental or legal disability and (7) current enrolment in another interventional study.

Randomisation, allocation and blinding

Patients are randomly allocated from an interactive web-based randomisation system to receive either the investigational neuroprotective bundle or routine care in a 1-to-1 ratio. Designated staff who will not be involved in anaesthesia management or follow-up will perform recruitment and randomisation. To minimise selection and ascertainment biases, the participants (legal representatives), outcomes assessors in the follow-up, care providers in the ICU and surgeons will be blinded to the randomisation until the completion of the trial analysis, unless a serious adverse event (SAE) occurs that requires urgent treatment that could be influenced by knowledge of the treatment allocation. Anaesthesiologists will not be blinded due to the nature of the intervention; however, they will be instructed not to disclose the group allocation to other study personnel or participants. Intraoperative anaesthetic record and postoperative clinical notes will be collected by specific equipment and personnel, and group assignment will not be disclosed throughout the process.

Study intervention and comparator

Patients in the intervention group receive the intraoperative neuroprotective anaesthesia bundle, which includes a target MAP of 65–90 mm Hg, BIS 40–60, bilateral rSO2≥60%, arterial inflow temperature <37℃ and a rewarming rate <0.5℃/min after exceeding 30℃ during CPB. The success of interventions is defined as MAP, BIS, rSO2, temperature during rewarming is within target range throughout the entire surgical procedure (single continuous out-of-range measurements cannot exceed a duration ≥5 min and cumulative out-of-range measurements cannot exceed a duration of 20% of the total operative time) (after excluding definite interferences).

For participants in the intervention group, when MAP <65 mm Hg, anaesthesiologists will thoroughly evaluate as follows. If BIS is <40, the infusion rate of intravenous anaesthetic or the concentration of inhaled anaesthetic may be decreased. If BIS is greater than 40, the preload, myocardial contractility and afterload will be assessed, and one or more methods, such as fluid therapy, inotropic agents or vasopressors will be employed to increase MAP. When MAP exceeds 90 mm Hg, if BIS is greater than 60, the infusion rate of intravenous anaesthetic or the concentration of inhaled anaesthetic may be increased. If the BIS is between 40 and 60, preload, myocardial contractility and afterload will be assessed, and one or more methods, such as diuretics or vasodilators, should be used. When BIS exceeds 60, the infusion rate of intravenous anaesthetic or the concentration of inhaled anaesthetic should be increased, or intravenous midazolam (0.03 mg/kg) should be administered to maintain BIS below 60. When BIS is below 40, the infusion rate of intravenous anaesthetic or the concentration of inhaled anaesthetic should be reduced to maintain BIS above 40. When rSO2 on either side is less than 60, the MAP and BIS need to be maintained in the target ranges, and the arterial blood gas will be adjusted to ensure these ranges (7.35<PH<7.45, PaO2>80 mm Hg, 35<PaCO2<50 mm Hg, haematocrit (Hct)>30%). The arterial conduit temperature during CPB should be set below 37°C. When the nasopharyngeal temperature exceeds 30°C, rewarm slowly at a rate of less than 0.5°C per minute (figure 2).

For participants in the control group, MAP, BIS, rSO2 and temperature will be maintained by anaesthesiologists according to surgical procedures or current clinical consensus to ensure the safety of anaesthesia. The specific anaesthesia management for every parameter is similar to that of the intervention group.

Standard anaesthesia management

All participants will be monitored with standard monitoring including ECG, peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO2), invasive arterial blood pressure (IBP), central venous pressure, cardiac output (CO), stroke volume (SV), systemic vascular resistance, BIS, near infrared spectroscopy -changes from bilateral rSO2 and temperature. Anaesthesia induction will be induced with etomidate (0.3 mg/kg), sufentanil (2 μg/kg) and rocuronium (1 mg/kg) or cis-atracurium (0.3 mg/kg). Anaesthesia maintenance will be performed with propofol, dexmedetomidine, rocuronium or cis-atracurium and sevoflurane.

Magnetic resonance study protocol

Patients will undergo standardised MRI of the brain when they are admitted to the hospital and again between postoperative days 3 and 7, as soon as the patient can tolerate the procedure accompanied by carers who are blinded. The imaging sequence will include a sagittal T1, an axial T2, a coronal fluid-attenuated inversion recovery, and an axial diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI). The MRI sequences will be performed as standard care on the ACUTATM Elfin 0.23 T MR machine, with a horizontal distance of ≤1.3 mm and a vertical distance of ≤1.4 mm. The DWI sequence can detect a new acute covert stroke with diffusion restriction.

MRI will be electronically transferred via a secure connection to the imaging interpretation centre. Two independent neuroradiology experts, blinded to the baseline characteristics and group allocation, will assess the MRI in duplicate and provide a consensus interpretation of the presence of cerebral imaging findings.

Perioperative cognitive dysfunction

Trained research personnel will screen the patients’ cognitive function on four occasions: (1) the day when patients are admitted for their index surgery; (2) within 7 days postoperatively; (3) 30 days after surgery and (4) 1 year after surgery. Delirium will be screened twice daily for the first 5 postoperative days. Study personnel will assess for delirium using the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) and CAM-ICU.20 Study personnel will assess patients’ neurocognitive function using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA). Patients screened and found to have a decreased MoCA score of 2 points or more from preoperative baseline will be considered to have cognitive decline.43 The 30-day and 1-year follow-ups are conducted via a face-to-face visit at the hospital or a remote video interview via social media (WeChat) to assess and confirm the occurrence of PND.

Blood samples

We will collect blood samples at three different time points during the admission: (1) before surgery; (2) 24 hours after surgery and (3) 72 hours after surgery. All samples collected will be venous. At each time point, a total of 4 mL is drawn and stored in heparin-coated tubes. We will centrifuge the samples for 10 min at 3000 rpm, cool to 4℃, then extract and store the plasma at −80°C until examination.

Study end points

The study aims to investigate whether intraoperative anaesthetic interventions that focus on neurologic protection reduce the incidence of SBI after cardiac surgery with CPB.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the 7-day incidence of new-onset SBI. New-onset SBI refers to cerebral infarctions detected on postoperative MRI that are not present on preoperative MRI and are without corresponding clinical symptoms.

Key secondary outcome

The key secondary outcome is the 30-day incidence of PND. The 30-day incidence of PND is defined in this study as the occurrence of postoperative delirium and/or cognitive decline, with cognitive decline defined as a decrease of ≥2 points in MoCA from baseline.

Secondary outcomes

The secondary endpoints include the following:

The 5-day incidence of postoperative delirium

The 5-day incidence of postoperative delirium

It refers to the incidence of delirium evaluated by CAM/CAM-ICU within the first 5 days after surgery.

The 30-day and 1-year incidence of new-onset overt stroke

The 30-day and 1-year incidence of new-onset overt stroke

New-onset overt stroke is defined as any new ischaemic or haemorrhagic cerebrovascular event after surgery with focal or global neurological (motor, sensory or cognitive) dysfunction lasting at least 24 hours. And a broadened stroke definition, also including neurological dysfunction of <24 hours duration with corresponding evidence of central nervous system haemorrhage or infarction (including pathological, imaging, or other objective evidence of cerebral, spinal cord or retinal focal ischaemic injury in a defined vascular distribution).3 We will use the broader definition. In the standardised follow-up questionnaire, the related clinical symptoms and signs include sudden unilateral hemiplegia, sudden syncope, sudden obnubilation and sudden amaurosis. If a patient has any of these symptoms or signs, a highly specific examination (either cerebral CT or MRI) will be performed.

The 30-day and 1-year incidence of TIA

The 30-day and 1-year incidence of TIA

TIA is defined as focal arterial ischaemia with transient symptoms (lasting <24 hours) and without evidence of infarction by pathology or imaging.44

The 1 year incidence of PND

The 1 year incidence of PND

The 1-year incidence of PND refers to the incidence of cognitive decline at 1 year after surgery.

The 30-day and 1-year composite incidence of major adverse events (renal insufficiency, myocardial infarction, pulmonary embolism, seizure and all-cause mortality)

Perioperative renal dysfunction is defined according to the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes criteria for cardiac surgery-associated acute kidney injury.45 In the standardised follow-up questionnaire, the related clinical symptoms and signs include oliguria, increased nocturnal enuresis, and local or systemic oedema. If a patient presents any of these symptoms or signs, specific examinations, including serum creatinine level and 24-hour urine volume measurement, will be performed. A detailed history of any renal replacement therapy will also be collected.

Perioperative myocardial infarction is diagnosed in accordance with the European Association of Cardio-Thoracic Surgery expert consensus statement.46 For 3 days after the operation, all patients undergo daily ECGs and troponin testing. The diagnosis of myocardial infarction is made if (1) high-sensitivity cardiac troponin values (hs-troponin) >500×the upper reference limit (URL), occurring from a normal baseline cardiac troponin value, (2) 35×<hs-troponin <500×URL, associated with the supportive signs of new myocardial ischaemia, (3) 10×<hs-troponin <35×URL, repeat 24 hours postsurgery and hs-troponin >35×associated with the supportive signs of new myocardial ischaemia. The supportive signs of new myocardial ischaemia include (1) new ischaemic ECG changes, (2) angiographic or coronary CT angiography (CTA)-documented graft/coronary occlusion and (3) imaging evidence of new loss of viable myocardium or new regional wall motion abnormality. In the standardised follow-up questionnaire, the related clinical symptoms and signs include chest tightness or pain, dyspnoea, cyanosis, haemoptysis and hacking cough. If a patient presents with any of these symptoms or signs, we obtain troponin levels, an ECG, and an echocardiogram. Coronary CTA or coronary angiography may be subsequently performed.

Pulmonary embolism is defined as thrombosis in the pulmonary vasculature indicated by pulmonary CTA, accompanied by clinical symptoms and signs (chest pain, haemoptysis or dyspnoea).47 In the standardised follow-up questionnaire, the related clinical symptoms and signs include chest tightness or pain, dyspnoea, cyanosis, haemoptysis and hacking cough. The subsequent specific examinations include pulmonary CTA, pulmonary angiography or radionuclide imaging, performed based on symptoms or signs.

Seizures are defined as generalised tonic-clonic events, focal attacks or a mixture of both, persisting for a few minutes, especially within the first 5–8 hours after operation, during the period of weaning from intravenous sedation in the ICU. Combined with electroencephalogram findings, perioperative seizures are diagnosed.48 In the standardised follow-up questionnaire, the related clinical symptoms and signs include sudden generalised, focal or mixed clonic attacks; convulsion with rigidity and myoclonia.

All-cause mortality is defined as death due to any cause.

Reporting of SAEs

The adverse effect of the anaesthesia management bundle will be closely monitored from the start of anaesthesia to discharge. The SAEs, life-threatening or requiring prolongation of existing hospitalisation or resulting in persistent or significant disability/incapacity, will be recorded and reported to the ethics committee within 24 hours. For such events, research personnel will complete an SAE case report form.

Statistical considerations for sample size and power

The sample size calculation is based on the incidence of SBI. The summarised SBI rate after on-pump cardiac surgery was 37% according to our previous meta-analysis.23 Based on the NeuroVISION Cardiac pilot study, 39% of patients experienced perioperative covert stroke.49 We chose a two-sided α level of 0.05 (interim analysis) and a 5% dropout rate; 912 subjects will provide 90% power to detect an absolute 10% reduction of SBI. For the key secondary endpoint, the incidence of cognitive decline was more than 50% at discharge.18 50 636 participants will detect an absolute 12% reduction, based on experts’ experience with a PND incidence of 40% in the control group within 30 days (two-sided α=0.05, β=0.1, dropout rate=5%). The study is powered for both the primary endpoint and the key secondary endpoint. The sample size calculation can be obtained at http://www.cnstat.org/statbox/.

Interim analysis

After approximately 50% of the study participants have completed the assessment of the primary and key secondary outcomes, an interim analysis will be conducted to assess efficacy. The study will be discontinued (early stop) if any of the following occur: (1) the study hypothesis is proved: the incidence of SBI is lower in the intervention group due to anaesthetic neuroprotective bundle compared with control group at the nominal significant level of α two-sided p<0.003 by the O’Brien-Fleming method51; (2) the study hypothesis cannot be proved after study completion based on the current findings: the conditional power is less than 15% even if the remaining 50% of total sample size is enrolled or (3) the study violates the ethical standards for obvious safety concerns (such as increased risk in all-cause mortality).

Statistical analysis plan

Demographic data and baseline characteristics will be summarised as numerical means (SD) or medians (IQRs) for continuous variables, and as the number of patients (percentages) for categorical variables. Group comparisons will be performed by Student’s t-test for normally distributed data or Wilcoxon rank-sum tests for non-parametric data. Categorical variables will be compared using Pearson’s χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

The primary outcome will be compared between groups using Pearson’s χ2 test, and intervention effects will be expressed as risk ratios, along with 95% CIs and associated p values. The data will be analysed according to the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle, and the ITT analysis will include all consenting participants who are randomised to the intervention or control group, regardless of the success of intervention. Alongside the unadjusted analyses presented above, logistic regression will be used to account for potential confounders. Variables with absolute standardised difference >0.130 (=1.96×1456+1456, where 456 is the number of patients in each group), are considered imbalanced and adjusted for in the primary outcome analysis.

A per-protocol (PP) analysis, based on the success of intervention, is planned as a sensitivity analysis between two groups. An as-treated analysis among all participants, based on the success of intervention, will be conducted as additional analysis. We plan to undertake a limited number of prespecified subgroup analyses by using interaction terms in regression models. These analyses assess interactions between primary outcome and variables, including gender, hypertension, diabetes, stroke, TIA, carotid artery disease, type of surgery and MoCA <26. We use post hoc analyses to aid in interpreting the primary outcome. For SBI, assuming missing at random, multiple imputation with chained equations will be performed to address missing data. A two-sided p<0.05 in accordance with the α spending function is considered statistically significant for all primary outcome analyses.

Secondary statistical analyses are intended to be exploratory and will be interpreted accordingly for key secondary and secondary outcomes. A two-sided p≤0.05 will be considered as noticeable without adjustment for multiplicity. All outcomes will be analysed according to the statistical analysis plan with SAS V.9.4.

Data collection

The INSPIRE trial uses a web-based, paperless data submission system (http://ccsr.cvs-china.com) for data collection and management. All five hospitals participating in the study are authorised to access the data submission system. A high-quality Secure Sockets Layer protocol is used for web-data transmission. The study has set 11 major items for in-hospital clinical data including demographic characteristics, medical history, accessory examination, surgical information, adherence to interventions, intraoperative vital signs, postoperative neurological complications (SBI or PND), major clinical events, other adverse events, laboratory examinations and postoperative recovery.

All follow-ups are completed by a special team that is blinded to the intervention. For the 30-day follow-up, a face-to-face visit is conducted in the hospital or via a remote video interview using social media to inquire and confirm the occurrence of PND and major morbidity. All video interviews are recorded and further reviewed by another follow-up team member. At about 20 days after the operation, the team contacts the study participants to confirm the type of follow-up. If confirmation of follow-up is not obtained, the team will contact the participant again 5 days later. Family members of the patients will be contacted to confirm death. The special follow-up team screens for relatively specific symptoms and signs and records the information in the standardised questionnaires. When a patient reports an SAE, the team suggests specific diagnostic examinations based on the related symptoms and signs, and these patients will be contacted daily to ensure the examinations are performed.

The 1-year follow-up is completed via telephone using a similar standardised questionnaire. The interview will also be recorded and reviewed by another member of the follow-up team. The study participants are asked to send the related examination images or reports to the team as soon as possible. The diagnostic results are independently reviewed by two clinicians who are blinded to randomisation. Discussion is conducted to reach consensus in the event of any discrepancies. All the questionnaires, interview videos, and highly specific diagnostic reports are maintained in a highly secure electronic system at Fuwai Hospital. All study participants are blinded to the randomisation during the follow-up period.

Data management

All collected data will be kept strictly confidential for research purposes only. The electronic data collection form will be used for data management. Files containing participants’ information will be stored in a locked filing cabinet. The data will be stored on a research computer, and weekly backups will be performed to another external hard drive.

Study quality control

The study Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) will assess data quality and safety throughout the trial. The DMC has no competing interests in the INSPIRE trial and monitors study implementation and SAEs. If clinically important safety concerns arise and consensus cannot be reached, a process for resolving them should be established, and the DMC should occur only as a joint decision with the principal investigators, including continuing the study with or without modifications or discontinuing the study. The DMC will review the cumulative safety data after approximately 50% of the study participants have completed the assessment of the primary and key secondary outcomes.

An independent Clinical Events Committee comprising experienced experts in the field reviews and adjudicates adverse events to provide consistency in categorising the trial outcomes. Event adjudication is performed per the study’s prespecified event definitions and in accordance with the Clinical Events Committee charter. Additionally, to maintain a high level of protocol adherence, refresher education sessions are conducted for study coordinators. Coordinators check protocol compliance and identify any potential protocol violations.

Protocol amendment

The principal investigator will be responsible for any decision to amend the protocol that may impact the potential safety and benefits to the patients or the conduct of the study. If there are any modifications (eg, changes to the population, sample sizes, study procedures, interventions, outcomes or analyses), the principal investigator will communicate with the relevant parties and obtain approval from the IRB/Independent Ethics Committee of Fuwai Hospital and all participating centres before implementation. The principal investigator will also be responsible for communicating with other relevant parties.

Discussion

The INSPIRE study—a prospective, multicentre, randomised, controlled trial—evaluates the efficacy of intraoperative physiologic targets focused on neurologic protection in 912 patients undergoing elective cardiac surgery with CPB with 1-year follow-up. We included multiple medical centres in the INSPIRE study to help evaluate the efficacy of combined anaesthetic neuroprotective targets (MAP 65–90 mm Hg, BIS 40–60, bilateral rSO2≥60%, arterial inflow temperature <37℃ and rewarming rate <0.5℃/min after exceeding 30℃ during CPB) for Chinese cardiac surgery patients to reduce the occurrence of SBI.

There are some limitations to the trial. To guarantee the maximum neuroprotective effects, the intervention bundle comprises four complex parameters, which would make implementation difficult. To minimise this, we will conduct refresher education sessions as recruitment progresses and monitor adherence to the intervention monthly. Moreover, due to the complexity of the intervention bundle, the anaesthesiologists can’t be blinded. We therefore apply the MR image to detect SBI with back-to-back assessment. Trained anaesthesiologists at different medical centres are involved and inevitably will introduce heterogeneity. Besides, the assessment for cognitive outcomes is limited to a 1-year follow-up period, though the majority of PND occurred within the first 30 days after surgery.

Data sharing

De-identified individual participant data and associated data will be shared after study completion and publication of the main findings. Information will be available on reasonable request to the corresponding author.

Current status of the trial

The trial has recruited 115 participants.

Ethics and dissemination

The study was approved by the IRB/Independent Ethics Committee of Fuwai Hospital (Approval No. 2024-2445) and all participating centres. The study recruited its first participant on 6 July 2025 and is estimated to be completed in 2028. The findings of the study will be disseminated in peer-reviewed journals and will be presented at national or international conferences.
