Abstract
A renormalization-group (RG) improved Schwarzschild-like black hole (BH) is studied here, with a lapse that interpolates between a classical Schwarzschild exterior and a quantum-smoothed interior set by a cutoff scale ξ \xi and an interpolation parameter γ \gamma . The horizon structure is worked out alongside the photon sphere and shadow radius R sh R_{\mathrm{sh}} , the scalar, electromagnetic, and Dirac Regge-Wheeler-Zerilli problems are set up in a single treatment, and fundamental together with overtone quasinormal modes are computed via sixth-order WKB cross-checked against time-domain ringdown. Strong Cosmic Censorship (SCC) is examined at the inner Cauchy horizon – a horizon that the improved geometry generates without charge or rotation – and the ratio β = | Im ​ ω | / κ − \beta=|\mathrm{Im}\,\omega|/\kappa_{-} turns out to be multipole-independent at the 6 % 6\% level, following β ≃ λ L / κ − \beta\simeq\lambda_{L}/\kappa_{-} . A thermodynamic analysis identifies a Davies-type phase transition of the outer horizon and a nontrivial Weinhold-Ruppeiner geometry on the ( S , γ ) (S,\gamma) slice; the Schwarzschild T H ∝ 1 / r + T_{H}\propto 1/r_{+} decay is replaced by a bell-curve profile peaking at T H max ≃ 0.062 T_{H}^{\max}\simeq 0.062 . A scan of the ( ξ , γ ) (\xi,\gamma) plane gathers the joint behavior of the shadow, the scalar barrier, the SCC ratio, and T H T_{H} , and exhibits a thin crescent adjacent to the extremal boundary where Christodoulou-SCC marginally fails. A comparison against Bardeen, Hayward, and Bonanno-Reuter BHs places the improved Schwarzschild BH as the most Schwarzschild-like of the regular-BH family at matched perturbation scale, with shadow-degenerate imaging against Hayward and Bonanno-Reuter at the 1 % 1\% level. The closing analysis takes up the sparsity of the Hawking flux and the energy-emission rate, both depending cleanly on ( ξ , γ ) (\xi,\gamma) through a single auxiliary function tied to the outer-horizon surface gravity. Keywords : RG-improved black hole; asymptotic safety; quasinormal modes; strong cosmic censorship; Hawking temperature

V
SCC at the Cauchy Horizon

The two-horizon structure of Sec.
II
means the improved Schwarzschild geometry possesses an inner Cauchy horizon (CH) at
r=r_{-}
. CHs are the natural arena for Penrose’s SCC conjecture
Penrose 1969
;
Chandrasekhar 1998
;
Dafermos and Luk 2017
: they mark the boundary past which the GR Cauchy problem loses determinism in a regular initial-data formulation. In the modern Christodoulou version
Cardoso et al. 2018
;
Hintz and Vasy 2018
;
Mo et al. 2018
;
Destounis 2019b
;
Dias et al. 2018
;
Cao et al. 2024

r_{-}
that the spacetime cannot be extended past it with square-integrable matter. The relevant dimensionless ratio is

\beta\equiv\frac{|\mathrm{Im}(\omega_{0})|}{\kappa_{-}}\,,

(22)

with SCC respected for
\beta<1/2
and violated for
\beta>1/2
. Recent work has refined the picture across many charge/spin/fermion configurations
Hod 2019
;
Destounis 2019a
;
Casals and Marinho 2022
;
Dias et al. 2018
, with a recurring theme: approach to the extremal limit pushes
\beta
toward (or past)
1/2
.

A noteworthy feature of the improved Schwarzschild BH is that SCC can be examined here
without
a charge, spin, or cosmological-constant source for the inner horizon – the two-horizon structure is purely quantum-gravitational, driven by the RG improvement of the lapse. Table

contains
\beta
for the scalar

. A cross-check shows that

Sparsity of Hawking Radiation and Energy Emission Rate

Two further radiative observables round out the picture: the sparsity of the Hawking flux, which quantifies the discreteness of the emission process, and the energy-emission rate per frequency, which quantifies the total power output in the geometric-optics limit. IX.1
Sparsity of Hawking radiation

Although Hawking radiation has a thermal spectrum, the emission is not continuous in time. It happens through the release of discrete and well-separated quanta, so the Hawking flux is intrinsically sparse
Visser et al. 2017
;
Gray et al. 2016
. A convenient way to quantify this sparsity compares the characteristic thermal wavelength of the emitted particles with the effective emission area of the BH
Visser et al. 2017
.

The sparsity parameter is

\psi=\frac{\mathcal{C}}{\tilde{g}}\,\frac{\lambda^{2}_{\rm th}}{A_{\rm eff}},

(28)

where

is a positive numerical constant,

is the number of spin states of the emitted quanta, and

\lambda_{\rm th}=\frac{2\pi}{T_{H}},\hskip 20.00003ptA_{\rm eff}=\frac{27}{4}\,A_{\rm BH}(r_{+})=27\pi r_{+}^{2}.

(29)

Using temperature given in (
24

\psi=\frac{64\pi^{3}}{27}\,\frac{1}{r_{+}^{2}\,\mathcal{D}^{2}(r_{+})},

(30)

with

(31)
