Comparison of survival outcomes for people with HR+/HER2− metastatic breast cancer who received palbociclib, ribociclib, or abemaciclib with an aromatase inhibitor: a plain language summary
Authors: Hope S Rugo, Rachel M Layman, Filipa Lynce, Xianchen Liu, Benjamin Li, Lynn McRoy, Aaron B Cohen, Melissa Estevez, Giuseppe Curigliano, Adam Brufsky
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Plain Language Summary What is this summary about? This summary describes two original articles from one study that compared health outcomes for people with metastatic breast cancer. The people in the study received one of three different medicines called CDK4/6 inhibitors: palbociclib, ribociclib, or abemaciclib, in combination with an aromatase inhibitor, as their first treatment following a metastatic breast cancer diagnosis. There has never been a clinical trial that directly compares how well these three CDK4/6 inhibitors work for people with metastatic breast cancer. The researchers wanted to know if any of the three medicines, when combined with an aromatase inhibitor, were associated with A) a longer period of time that people’s cancer remained under control after starting treatment or B) longer life for people in the study. The study used information from a large database that contained the medical records of people treated in cancer clinics in the United States. Only people with HR+/HER2− metastatic breast cancer were included in the study. What were the results? Among the three medicines taken in combination with an aromatase inhibitor, there were no significant differences in how long it took for people’s cancer to get worse. There were also no significant differences in how long people lived after starting any of the three medicines with an aromatase inhibitor. What do the results mean? These results support the use of palbociclib, ribociclib, or abemaciclib in combination with an aromatase inhibitor as a first treatment for people with HR+/HER2− metastatic breast cancer.Clinical trial number: NCT06495164KEYWORDS: abemaciclib, aromatase inhibitor, comparative effectiveness, cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitor, hormone therapy, palbociclib, plain language summary, real-world study, ribociclib
