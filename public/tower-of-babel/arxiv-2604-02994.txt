Weight distribution bounds to relate minimum distance, list decoding, and symmetric channel performance
Donald Kougang-Yombi, Jan Hązła

Abstract
We study relationships between worst-case and random-noise properties of error correcting codes. More concretely, we consider connections between minimum distance, list decoding radius, and block error probability on noisy channels. A recent result of Pernice, Sprumont, and Wootters established the tight connection between list decoding radius and symmetric channel performance for linear codes. We extend this result to general codes. The proof proceeds by directly bounding the weight distribution rather than by sharp threshold techniques. We then turn to the relation between minimum distance and symmetric channel performance. The results we just described imply that a q q -ary code of relative distance δ \delta has vanishing error probability on the symmetric channel up to the Johnson radius J q ​ ( δ ) J_{q}(\delta) . We improve upon this bound in the case of linear codes, for a range δ \delta , for q ≥ 4 q\geq 4 . In our proof we consider the erasure properties of codes, and bound their weight distribution through inequalities introduced by Samorodnitsky. The latter part of the proof gives a more general technique that bounds the symmetric channel performance of a linear code with constant relative distance and good erasure channel performance.

Introduction

1.1
List decoding and symmetric channels

An error correcting code with finite input alphabet
\Sigma
and block length
n
is a subset
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\Sigma^{n}
. When

|\Sigma|=q
we say that the code is
q
-ary (and binary for
q=2
).
A code can be thought of as a packing of
|\mathcal{C}|
possible messages
that allows to recover the original message even if the
sent codeword
x\in\mathcal{C}
is corrupted by noise.
Typically, one desires codes which have a good tradeoff
between the rate
R(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq\frac{\log_{q}|\mathcal{C}|}{n}

(which quantifies how many different messages can
be encoded into
\mathcal{C}
) and
the level of noise they can recover from.

The concept of maximum recoverable level of noise can
be made concrete in several meaningfully different ways.
In the
worst-case model
, we consider the
minimum
distance

d(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq\min_{x,y\in\mathcal{C},x\neq y}\Delta(x,y)
,
where
\Delta(x,y)
denotes the
Hamming distance
, that is
the number of coordinates
1\leq i\leq n
such that
x_{i}\neq y_{i}
.
It is well known that for a code with minimum distance
d

there exists a decoder that recovers the original codeword
for every pattern of
\lceil d/2\rceil-1
errors and
every pattern of
d-1
erasures. For a family
of codes with increasing block lengths
\{\mathcal{C}_{n}\}_{n}
,
we can express how well it corrects errors
in the worst-case model by the notion of

relative minimum distance

\delta\coloneqq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\delta(\mathcal{C}_{n})
,
where
\delta(\mathcal{C}_{n})\coloneqq d(\mathcal{C}_{n})/n
.
Accordingly, such a family corrects up to around

\delta/2
fraction of worst case errors
and
\delta
fraction of worst case erasures.

In the
probabilistic (Shannon)
error model,
we consider a (memoryless) communication channel
which independently distorts every coordinate
1\leq i\leq n
of the original codeword according to a fixed probability
distribution. Accordingly, a channel
W
with output
alphabet
\mathcal{Y}
is characterized
by a family of
q
probability distributions on
\mathcal{Y}
,
one each for every input symbol
x\in\Sigma
.
The most natural example
is the
q
-ary symmetric
channel

\qSC_{p}
with crossover probability

0\leq p\leq 1
, which satisfies
W(x|x)=1-p

and
W(x^{\prime}|x)=\frac{p}{q-1}
for
x\neq x^{\prime}
.
Here we will focus on
the range
0\leq p\leq 1-1/q
, as at
p=1-1/q

the channel is fully noisy and input symbols are statistically indistinguishable.
Another important example is the
q
-ary erasure
channel

\qEC_{\lambda}
with erasure probability
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. This channel has output alphabet
\mathcal{Y}=\Sigma\cup\{?\}
and satisfies

W(x|x)=1-\lambda
and
W(?|x)=\lambda
for every
x\in\Sigma
.
In the Shannon model, it is easy to see that one cannot
recover the original message with certainty in any nontrivial scenario. Instead, a code
family
\mathcal{C}_{n}

is considered to successfully correct errors on a channel
W
if
there exists a decoder which recovers the original message
except with probability that goes to
0
as
n
grows
(for every codeword, with respect to the channel noise;
or in expectation over a randomly chosen codeword).
In other words, we want the family
\mathcal{C}_{n}
to
have
vanishing block error probability
.

The notion of
\emph{listdecoding}

is often considered a ’’bridge‘‘ between
the worst-case and Shannon models. For a more detailed
introduction to list decoding, see
[
GRS25
, Chapter 7]
. A code is said to be

(p,L)
-list decodable if for every
y\in\Sigma^{n}

it holds
|\{x\in\mathcal{C}:\Delta(y,x)\leq pn\}|\leq L
. In particular,
the decoder that, given

, outputs a list of
L
closest
codewords to

according to the Hamming distance,
will include the original codeword in the list for any
pattern of at most
pn
errors. One can consider a
family of codes
\mathcal{C}_{n}
to be ’’good‘‘ at list decoding with
p
fraction of errors if it is
(p,L)
-list decodable for some modest
L=L(n)
, for example
L=O(1)
or
L=\poly(n)
.

Several questions can be asked
about general connections between

noise models. Perhaps the most natural is:
What is the largest crossover probability
p

such that every code family
\mathcal{C}_{n}
of relative distance

\delta
has vanishing block error probability on the
\qSC_{p}

We discuss this question

As for the connection between the

]
states that a code

-list decodable for

\displaystyle p<J_{q}(\delta)\coloneqq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{q}{q-1}\delta}\right)\;,

We will call the quantity

The Johnson bound is tight in the sense that there exist linear codes

Recently, Pernice, Sprumont, and Wootters

For

]

generalized this sharp threshold result to linear codes over larger alphabets.
As a result, they established that if a code family

Theorem 1
.

Let

The proof of

Instead, we use a simple double counting

\displaystyle A_{w}(x)\coloneqq\left|\left\{y\in\mathcal{C}:\Delta(x,y)=w\right\}\right|\;.

Let

\nu_{q}(n,t_{1},t_{2},w)\coloneqq|S(0^{n},t_{1})\cap S(1^{w}0^{n-w},t_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n}{t_{1}}(q-1)^{t_{1}}L\;.

(3)

1.2
Symmetric channel decoding
from minimum distance

As mentioned, perhaps
the most natural theoretical question
relating the worst-case and random-noise properties of error correcting
codes is: For a given relative distance
\delta
,
what is the largest crossover probability
p

such that every
family of
q
-ary codes
\{\mathcal{C}_{n}\}_{n}
with relative distance

\delta
has vanishing block error probability on the symmetric channel
\qSC_{p}
?
Accordingly, given a
q
-ary alphabet
\Sigma
and relative minimum distance

0\leq\delta\leq 1-1/q
, let

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{sym}}(q,\delta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup\left\{p\geq 0:\forall\ \{\mathcal{C}_{n}\}_{n},\mathcal{C}_{n}\subseteq\Sigma^{n},\text{ if }\delta(\mathcal{C}_{n})\geq\delta\text{, then }P_{B}\left(\mathcal{C}_{n},\qSC_{p}\right)\to 0\right\}\;.

For

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{Lsym}}(q,\delta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup\left\{p\geq 0:\forall\ \text{linear}\ \{\mathcal{C}_{n}\}_{n},\mathcal{C}_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\text{ if }\delta(\mathcal{C}_{n})\geq\delta\text{, then }P_{B}\left(\mathcal{C}_{n},\qSC_{p}\right)\to 0\right\}\;.

As we are not aware of an extensive discussion of this problem in the existing literature, we proceed to a short survey of what we know about it. Recall the
q
-ary entropy function

H_{q}(x):=x\log_{q}(q-1)-x\log_{q}(x)-(1-x)\log_{q}(1-x)

A random code (as well as a random linear code)

satisfies
\delta(\mathcal{C}_{n})\geq\delta
.
At the same time, by the Shannon‘s channel coding theorem, it cannot
have vanishing error probability on
\qSC_{p}
if

H_{q}(p)>H_{q}(\delta)+\varepsilon

, we have an upper bound

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{sym}}(q,\delta)\leq p_{\mathrm{Lsym}}(q,\delta)\leq\delta\;.

(4)

Since for larger values of

,

]

\qSC_{p}

H_{q}(p)>\delta+\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}-1}

\delta\leq 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}-1}

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{Lsym}}(q,\delta)\leq H_{q}^{-1}\left(\delta+\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}-1}\right)\;.

This improves upon

Let us proceed to a short sketch of our proof.
Since both the Johnson bound and

We proceed in a seemingly roundabout way,

1.3
Symmetric channel decoding from
minimum distance and erasure channel

Consider a binary linear code

\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

Applying the Johnson bound as discussed in

\BSC_{0.05}

On the other hand, consider another linear code that has

\BEC_{0.533}

\displaystyle A_{w}(x)\leq\frac{\binom{n}{t}(q-1)^{t}L}{\nu_{q}(n,t,w)}\leq\exp_{q}\left(n\left(H_{q}(t/n)+o(1)-M^{(q)}(\gamma,t/n)\right)\right)\leq\exp_{q}\left(n\left(F^{(q)}(\gamma,p)+o(1)\right)\right)\;,

(9)

so indeed for
w=\gamma n\leq 2p^{\prime}n+n^{3/4}

it holds
w\leq 2t
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\log_{q}A_{\gamma n}(x)\leq F^{(q)}(\gamma,p)+o(1)=G(\gamma)+o(1)\;,

uniformly in
x
and
\gamma
.
Finally, by
Lemma

16
it holds
that for
p^{\prime}\leq\gamma\leq\min(2p^{\prime},1)
we have

\displaystyle F^{(q)}(\gamma,p^{\prime})-G(\gamma)=F^{(q)}(\gamma,p^{\prime})-F^{(q)}(\gamma,p)>0\;.

Therefore, by
Proposition

18

we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{n},\qSC_{p^{\prime}})=0
.
∎

Symmetric channel decoding
from minimum distance and erasure channel

4.1
Weight distribution bounds

The main technical tool in this section is
a clean bound on the weight distribution of a linear code
in terms of its conditional entropy on the erasure channel.
The bound is taken from
[
AHO25
]
and is a generalization of the binary bound
from
[
HSS21
]
.

Theorem 19

(Theorem 39 in
[
AHO25
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 1
,
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}_{q}
a linear code
and let
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. Let
X
be a uniformly chosen codeword in
\mathcal{C}
and
Y
be the output of transmitting
X
over the channel
\qEC_{\lambda}
. Then, the weight distribution of
\mathcal{C}
satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}\left(\frac{q^{\lambda}-1}{q-1}\right)^{i}\leq 2^{H(X|Y)}\;,

where
H(X|Y)
is the conditional Shannon entropy
(with base-2 logarithm) of

X
given
Y
.

One corollary of
Theorem

19
is a bound on the block error probability of decoding
\mathcal{C}
on a variety of channels.

Corollary 20

(Corollary 44 in
[
AHO25
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 1
,
0<\lambda\leq 1
and
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}_{q}
a linear code with minimum distance
d
. Let
X
be a uniformly chosen codeword in
\mathcal{C}
and
Y
be the output of transmitting
X
over the channel
\qEC_{\lambda}
. Let
W:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a channel, and assume that
c\coloneqq Z(W)\frac{q-1}{q^{\lambda}-1}<1
. Then, it holds,

\displaystyle P_{B}(\mathcal{C},W)\leq\frac{c^{d}}{1-c}\cdot 2^{H(X|Y)}\;.

Our proofs apply
Theorem

19
in a
black box fashion, and our results give a generalization
of
Corollary

20
.

4.2
Further properties of

M^{(q)}
and
F^{(q)}

The function
M^{(q)}(\gamma,\delta)

has more properties that will be useful
in this section. First, it is concave
as a function of
\gamma
.

Lemma 21
.

For fixed
q\geq 2
and
0\leq p\leq 1-1/q
,
the function
\gamma\mapsto-M^{(q)}(\gamma,p)
is a convex function on
[0,\min(2p,1)]

Second, its partial derivative with respect
to
\gamma

is equal to the logarithm of the Bhattacharayya coefficient

. We will use this property in analyzing

Lemma 22
.

Let

,

, then it holds

\displaystyle\frac{\partial M^{(q)}(\gamma,p)}{\partial\gamma}\bigg\rvert_{\gamma=0}

\displaystyle=\log_{q}Z(\qSC_{p})=\log_{q}\left(\frac{q-2}{q-1}p+2\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{q-1}}\right)\;.

The proofs of the lemmas are deferred to
Section

6
. We also state a claim
which is easy to check from the definitions.

Claim 23
.

For every
q\geq 2
and
0\leq p\leq 1-1/q

it holds

.

Proof of

4.5
Binary dual bound

There is another bound for the weight distribution
in terms of the conditional entropy on the
erasure channel of the
dual code

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}\coloneqq\{x:\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}=0\ \forall y\in\mathcal{C}\}
.
A similar result to
Theorem

6
can be obtained
by replacing
Theorem

19

with this dual bound. For simplicity,
in this section we restrict ourselves to
q=2
.

Definition 25
.

Let
0\leq\lambda\leq R<1
. Let

\displaystyle G^{\perp}_{\lambda,R}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}R-\lambda-\min(\gamma,1-\gamma)\log(2^{1-\lambda}-1)&\text{if}\quad 0\leq\min(\gamma,1-\gamma)<1-2^{\lambda-1},\\
h(\gamma)-(1-R)&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then, for
0\leq\delta\leq 1
, let

\displaystyle p^{\perp}_{*}(\lambda,R,\delta)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\delta/2\leq p\leq 1/2:F(\delta,p)\leq G^{\perp}_{\lambda,R}(\delta)\right\}\;.

Theorem 26
.

Let
0<\delta,\lambda<1
, let
\mathcal{C}=\{\mathcal{C}_{n}\}_{n}
be a family of binary linear codes of rate
R=\lim_{n}R(\mathcal{C}_{n})
with
\delta(\mathcal{C}_{n})\geq\delta
, and such that
\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}P_{b}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp}_{n},\BEC_{\lambda})=0.
Then,

\displaystyle\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}P_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{n},\BSC_{p})=0,\ \text{for every}\ p<p^{\perp}_{*}(\lambda,R,\delta).

The function
G^{\perp}_{\lambda,R}
is defined
only for
\lambda\leq R
. This is without
loss of generality, since
\lambda\leq R

follows from the Shannon‘s coding theorem
if the dual code family
has vanishing bit error probability
on the
\BEC_{\lambda}
.
Since
F(\delta,1/2)=0

and
G_{\lambda,R}^{\perp}(\delta)\geq 0

(see
41
), the
value of
p^{\perp}_{*}(\lambda,R,\delta)
is well defined.

We will need two prerequisites before we
prove
Theorem

26
. First,
let us state the dual weight distribution
bound due to
[
Sam19
]
:

Proposition 27

(Corollary 40 in
[
AHO25
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, with weight distribution
(A_{0},\ldots,A_{n})
. Let
0<\lambda\leq 1
and
\tau\coloneqq\frac{q^{\lambda}-1}{q-1}
.

1)

Let
X
be a uniformly chosen random element in
\mathcal{C}
,
Y
be the output of transmitting
X
through
\qEC_{\lambda}
. Then, for every
0\leq w\leq n
, it holds
A_{w}\leq\tau^{-w}2^{H(X|Y)}
.

2)

Similarly, let
X^{\perp}
be a uniformly chosen random element in
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
, and
Y^{\perp}
be the output of transmitting
X^{\perp}
through
\qEC_{\lambda}
. Let
\gamma=\frac{w}{n}
, then, for every
0\leq w\leq n
, it holds

\displaystyle A_{w}\leq|\mathcal{C}|2^{H(X^{\perp}|Y^{\perp})}\begin{cases}(1-\tau)^{-\gamma n}(1+(q-1)\tau)^{-(1-\gamma)n}&\quad\text{if}\ \gamma<\frac{q-1}{q}(1-\tau),\\
q^{-n(1-H_{q}(\gamma))}&\quad\text{if}\ \frac{q-1}{q}(1-\tau)\leq\gamma\leq\frac{q-1}{q}(1+\tau),\\
(1+\tau)^{-\gamma n}(1-(q-1)\tau)^{-(1-\gamma)n}&\quad\text{if}\ \gamma>\frac{q-1}{q}(1+\tau).\end{cases}

Second, we need to check that

G^{\perp}_{\lambda,R}
is a concave function.

Lemma 28
.

The function
\gamma\mapsto G^{\perp}_{\lambda,R}(\gamma)
is a concave function for
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
.

Proof.

Let
G(\gamma)\coloneqq G^{\perp}_{\lambda,R}(\gamma)
,

m\coloneqq\min(\gamma,1-\gamma)
and
m_{0}:=1-2^{\lambda-1}
.
Since
R-\lambda-m_{0}\log(2^{1-\lambda}-1)=h(m_{0})-1-R
,
the function
G(\gamma)
is continuous on
[0,1]
.
Also

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(\gamma)=\begin{cases}\mp\log(2^{1-\lambda}-1)\quad\ \text{if}\ 0\leq m<m_{0}\\
h^{\prime}(\gamma)\quad\ \text{otherwise}\;,\end{cases}

in particular one checks that

G^{\prime-}(m_{0})=-\log(2^{1-\lambda}-1)=h^{\prime}(m_{0})=G^{\prime+}(m_{0})
, and by a symmetric argument

G^{\prime-}(1-m_{0})=G^{\prime+}(1-m_{0})
. Since
G
has continuous first derivative,
to conclude that it is concave
it is sufficient to check that

G^{\prime\prime}(\gamma)\leq 0
for every
m\neq m_{0}
.
However, this is clear since

\displaystyle G^{\prime\prime}(\gamma)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }m<m_{0}\\

In either case,
G^{\prime\prime}(\gamma)\leq 0

is concave.

Claim 29

Let

\displaystyle\frac{q-1}{q}n^{\prime}=\frac{q-1}{q}\rho n\leq\delta n-\frac{q-1}{q}\varepsilon\delta n<\delta n\leq d(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\;.

The Plotkin bound (

\mathcal{C}

In

for

. However,

assuming
(p,L)
-
erasure

Lemma 30
.

Let
\{\mathcal{C}_{n}\}_{n}
be a family of
q
-ary codes and
0\leq\rho\leq 1
.
If
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is
(\rho,L)
-erasure list decodable for some
L=L(n)

satisfying
d(\mathcal{C}_{n})\geq\omega(\log L)
, then for every
\rho^{\prime}<\rho
it
holds

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{n},\qEC_{\rho^{\prime}})=0\;.

Proof.

Take some
\rho^{\prime}<\rho_{0}<\rho
and write
\rho^{\prime}=(1-\varepsilon)\rho_{0}
.
Consider the following two-step procedure.
First, sample a random set
\mathcal{E}_{0}\subseteq[n]
,
such that every
1\leq i\leq n
is placed in
\mathcal{E}_{0}
independently
with probability
\rho_{0}
. Then, conditioned on
\mathcal{E}_{0}
,
choose
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{0}
such that every
i\in\mathcal{E}_{0}
is
placed in
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
independently with probability
1-\varepsilon
.
Note that the overall distribution of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is that of the
erasure pattern on the
\qEC_{\rho^{\prime}}
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
.
Given
\mathcal{E}\subseteq[n]
, let

S(\mathcal{E})\coloneqq\{y\in\mathcal{C}:y_{\bar{\mathcal{E}}}=x_{\bar{\mathcal{E}}}\}
, where

\bar{\mathcal{E}}
denotes the complement of
\mathcal{E}
. The set
S(\mathcal{E})

contains the codewords that cannot be distinguished from
x
given
the erasure pattern
\mathcal{E}
. Since if
|S(\mathcal{E})|=1
, then

S(\mathcal{E})=\{x\}
and the MAP decoder
is guaranteed to decode
x
correctly,
to show vanishing error probability on
\qEC_{\rho^{\prime}}

it is sufficient to upper
bound the probability that
|S(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})|>1
.

If
|\mathcal{E}_{0}|\leq\rho n
, then by the erasure list decoding property
it holds
|S(\mathcal{E}_{0})|\leq L
. Fix some
\mathcal{E}_{0}
such that

|S(\mathcal{E}_{0})|\leq L
and let us write
S(\mathcal{E}_{0})=\{x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\}
for
some
k<L
. Observe that, conditioned on
\mathcal{E}_{0}
,
for every
1\leq j\leq k
it holds

\displaystyle\Pr\left[y_{j}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\;|\;\mathcal{E}_{0}\right]

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon)^{\Delta(x,y_{j})}\leq(1-\varepsilon)^{d(\mathcal{C}_{n})}\;.

Applying the union bound, it holds
\Pr[|S(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})|>1\;|\;\mathcal{E}_{0}]\leq L(1-\varepsilon)^{d(\mathcal{C}_{n})}
. Since
\Pr[|\mathcal{E}_{0}|>\rho n]=o(1)
by the Hoeffding
bound, we have

\displaystyle\Pr\left[|S(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})|>1\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Pr\left[|\mathcal{E}_{0}|>\rho n\right]+\Pr\left[|S(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})>1|\;|\;|\mathcal{E}_{0}|\leq\rho n\right]

\displaystyle\leq o(1)+L(1-\varepsilon)^{d(\mathcal{C}_{n})}=o(1)\;,

where in the last step we invoke the assumption

d(\mathcal{C}_{n})\geq\omega(\log L)
.
∎

and

6.1
’’Almost closed form‘‘
for
M^{(q)}

While the function
M^{(q)}
does not
seem to have a simple closed form for

q\geq 3
, we can give an ’’almost‘‘
closed form for it. This is useful not only for
numerically computing the values of

M^{(q)}
, but also in several of the proofs
that follow.

Lemma 31
.

Let
0\leq p\leq 1-1/q
and
0\leq\gamma\leq\min(2p,1)
.
Let
C_{q}\coloneqq\frac{(q-2)^{2}}{4(q-1)}
,

\beta(\zeta)\coloneqq\frac{1}{1+C_{q}(\frac{1-\zeta}{\zeta})^{2}}
.
Then,

\displaystyle M^{(q)}(\gamma,p)=\gamma\tilde{H}_{q}(\zeta)+(1-\gamma)H_{q}(\beta(\zeta))

for the unique
\zeta
such that
\gamma\frac{\zeta}{2}+(1-\gamma)\beta(\zeta)=p-\frac{\gamma}{2}
.

Since
\beta(\zeta)
is a strictly increasing
function of
\zeta
, it is easy to see that the
value of
\zeta
is unique. It also means that

\zeta
, and consequently
M^{(q)}(\gamma,p)

can be efficiently computed via binary search.
(In principle one can also determine the value
of
\zeta
by reducing the formula
for
\beta(\zeta)
to a polynomial equation
of degree 3. However, this seems more cumbersome.)

Proof.

Let
D\coloneqq D(\gamma,p)

and
T(\zeta,\beta)\coloneqq\gamma\tilde{H}_{q}(\zeta)+(1-\gamma)H_{q}(\beta)
.
Consider a pair of values
(\zeta,\beta)\in D

that realizes the supremum in the definition
of
M^{(q)}(\gamma,p)
.
First, let us argue that we can assume that

\gamma\zeta/2+(1-\gamma)\beta=p-\gamma/2
.
Indeed, note that

\gamma(1-2/q)/2+(1-\gamma)(1-1/q)=1-1/q-\gamma/2\geq p-\gamma/2
. Therefore, if

\gamma\zeta/2+(1-\gamma)\beta<p-\gamma/2
,
then either
\zeta<1-2/q
or
\beta<1-1/q
.
If
\zeta<1-2/q
, then for some small
\varepsilon>0

it holds
(\zeta+\varepsilon,\beta)\in D

and
T(\zeta+\varepsilon,\beta)\geq T(\zeta,\beta)
.
A symmetric argument applies if
\beta<1-1/q
.

To sum up, it holds that

\displaystyle M^{(q)}(\gamma,p)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\zeta,\beta\leq 1\\
\gamma\zeta/2+(1-\gamma)\beta=p-\gamma/2\end{subarray}}T(\zeta,\beta)\;.

(11)

Let us apply the Lagrange multiplier method
to
T(\zeta,\beta)

with the constraint

\gamma\zeta/2+(1-\gamma)\beta=p-\gamma/2
.
The Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\zeta,\beta,\lambda^{\prime})=\gamma\tilde{H}_{q}(\zeta)+(1-\gamma)H_{q}(\beta)+\lambda^{\prime}\Big(p-\frac{\gamma}{2}-\gamma\frac{\zeta}{2}-(1-\gamma)\beta\Big),

and we have

\displaystyle\nabla\mathcal{L}=0\iff\begin{cases}\log_{q}(\frac{1-\zeta}{\zeta})+\log_{q}(\frac{q-2}{2})=\lambda^{\prime}/2&\\
\log_{q}(\frac{1-\beta}{\beta})+\log_{q}(q-1)=\lambda^{\prime}&\\
\gamma\frac{\zeta}{2}+(1-\gamma)\beta=p-\frac{\gamma}{2}\;.\end{cases}

By substituting
\lambda^{\prime}
from the second equation into the first equation, we get
\log\left((\frac{1-\zeta}{\zeta})^{2}\times\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}\right)=\log(\frac{1}{C_{q}})
i.e.,
\beta=\frac{1}{1+C_{q}(\frac{1-\zeta}{\zeta})^{2}}
.

Since the function

\zeta\mapsto\gamma\zeta/2+(1-\gamma)\beta(\zeta)

is strictly increasing from 0 to
1
for

0\leq\zeta\leq 1
, indeed there is unique

\zeta
satisfying

\gamma\zeta/2+(1-\gamma)\beta(\zeta)=p-\gamma/2
. By the Lagrange multiplier
calculation above, this point must achieve
the supremum of
T
. (The only other possibilities
have
\zeta\in\{0,1\}
or

\beta\in\{0,1\}
, but these
are excluded by the fact that

\lim_{\zeta\to 0^{+}}\tilde{H}^{\prime}_{q}(\zeta)=\lim_{\beta\to 0^{+}}H^{\prime}_{q}(\beta)=\infty
,
as well as

\lim_{\zeta\to 1^{-}}\tilde{H}^{\prime}_{q}(\zeta)=\lim_{\beta\to 1^{-}}H^{\prime}_{q}(\beta)=-\infty
.)
∎

Proof of

If

Below we proceed in two cases,

Binary case

Let

\Sigma=\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
For a given

b(y):=|\{w<j\leq n:y_{j}=1\}|
. Then,

\displaystyle y\in B\iff\begin{cases}a(y)+b(y)\leq t\\
w-a(y)+b(y)\leq t\;.\end{cases}

So,

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(n,t,w)=\sum_{a=0}^{w}\sum_{b=0}^{n-w}\mathds{1}[a+b\leq t]\mathds{1}[w-a+b\leq t]\binom{w}{a}\binom{n-w}{b}.

Recall that for every

. Then, substituting
w=\gamma n,a=\zeta\gamma n,b=\beta(1-\gamma)n

as the sum has at most

nonzero terms, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(n,t,w)

\displaystyle=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq a\leq\gamma n\\
0\leq b\leq(1-\gamma)n\end{subarray}}{\sum}\mathds{1}[a+b\leq pn]\mathds{1}[\gamma n-a+b\leq pn]\binom{\gamma n}{\zeta\gamma n}\binom{(1-\gamma)n}{\beta(1-\gamma)n}

\displaystyle\leq n^{2}\sup_{(\zeta,\beta)\in D}\exp_{2}\left(n[\gamma h(\zeta)+(1-\gamma)h(\beta)]\right)\;,

where

. To conclude the proof,
it is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle\sup_{(\zeta,\beta)\in D}\gamma h(\zeta)+(1-\gamma)h(\beta)=\gamma+(1-\gamma)h\left(\frac{p-\gamma/2}{1-\gamma}\right)=M(\gamma,p)\;.

To see that, first note
that

\left(1/2,\frac{p-\gamma/2}{1-\gamma}\right)\in D

therefore
\mathrm{LHS}\geq\mathrm{RHS}
.
On the other hand, if
(\zeta,\beta)\in D
,
then
(1/2,\beta)\in D
, and if

(1/2,\beta)\in D

\beta\leq\frac{p-\gamma/2}{1-\gamma}\leq 1/2
. So, indeed

\displaystyle\gamma h(\zeta)+(1-\gamma)h(\beta)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma+(1-\gamma)h\left(\frac{p-\gamma/2}{1-\gamma}\right)=M(\gamma,p)\;.\qed

Large alphabets
q\geq 3
.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\binom{w}{s}\binom{s}{a}(q-2)^{w-s}\binom{n-w}{b}(q-1)^{n-w-b}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=0}^{w}\sum_{b=0}^{n-w}\mathds{1}\left[n-s/2-b\leq t\right]2^{s}\binom{w}{s}(q-2)^{w-s}\binom{n-w}{b}(q-1)^{n-w-b}\;.

In the last step we used the fact that

Substituting

\displaystyle\mu_{q}(n,t,w)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=0}^{w}{\sum_{b=0}^{n-w}}\mathds{1}\left[n-s/2-b\leq t\right]2^{2\zeta\gamma n}\binom{\gamma n}{2\zeta\gamma n}(q-2)^{(1-2\zeta)\gamma n}\binom{(1-\gamma)n}{\beta(1-\gamma)n}(q-1)^{(1-\beta)(1-\gamma)n}

\displaystyle\leq n^{2}\exp_{2}\left(n\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(\zeta,\beta)\in[0,1/2]\times[0,1]\\
1-\zeta\gamma-\beta(1-\gamma)\leq p\end{subarray}}{\sup}\gamma\left[2\zeta+h(2\zeta)+(1-2\zeta)\log(q-2)\right]+(1-\gamma)\left[h(\beta)+(1-\beta)\log(q-1)\right]\right)\;.

We have

,
and

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(\zeta,\beta)\in[0,1/2]\times[0,1]\\

\displaystyle=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(\zeta,\beta)\in[0,1]^{2}\\

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\zeta,\beta)\in D(\gamma,p)}\gamma\tilde{H}_{q}(\zeta)+(1-\gamma)H_{q}(\beta)=M^{(q)}(\gamma,p)\;,

indeed it holds

\displaystyle\mu_{q}(n,t,w)

\displaystyle\leq n^{2}\exp_{2}{\left(n\log q\sup_{(\zeta,\beta)\in D(\gamma,p)}\gamma\tilde{H}_{q}(\zeta)+(1-\gamma)H_{q}(\beta)\right)}

\displaystyle=n^{2}\exp_{q}\left(nM^{(q)}(\gamma,p)\right)\;.\qed

We follow the exposition in

In the

channel, the input alphabet
is
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and the output distribution for

is given by the standard Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}((-1)^{b},\sigma^{2})

\overline{c}=((-1)^{c_{1}},\ldots,(-1)^{c_{n}})

Note that for two codewords

with

\|\overline{x}-\overline{y}\|_{2}=2\sqrt{k}

.
Furthermore, let

We can now state our main result,
