1.
A sumset intersection problem

Let
\mathbf{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
be the set of positive integers.
A decreasing sequence of sets
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
is

strictly decreasing
if
A_{q}\neq A_{q+1}
for all
q\in\mathbf{N}

and
asymptotically strictly decreasing
if
A_{q}\neq A_{q+1}

for infinitely many
q\in\mathbf{N}
.
A decreasing sequence of sets
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
that is not asymptotically
strictly decreasing is eventually constant.

Let
X
be an additive abelian semigroup and let
A
be a subset of
X
.
The
h
-fold sumset of
A
, denoted
hA
, is the set of all sums of
h
not necessarily
distinct elements of
A
.
Nathanson
[
2
]
posed the following problem:

Let
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
be a strictly decreasing sequence of sets
in an additive abelian semigroup and let

A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}
.
Describe the set

\mathcal{H}(A_{q})=\left\{h\in\mathbf{N}:hA=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}\right\}.

What sets of integers arise in this way?

Of course,
A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}
implies

1\in\mathcal{H}(A_{q})
for all sequences
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
.

For all
q\in\mathbf{N}
, we have
A\subseteq A_{q}
and so
hA\subseteq hA_{q}
.
Therefore,

hA\subseteq\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}.

Thus,
h\in\mathcal{H}(A_{q})
if and only if
x\in hA_{q}
for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
implies
x\in hA
.

If the decreasing sequence
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
is eventually constant,
then there exists
q_{0}\in\mathbf{N}
such that

A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}=A_{q_{0}}

and

hA=hA_{q_{0}}=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}

and so
\mathcal{H}(A_{q})=\mathbf{N}
. Thus, we consider only decreasing sequences
that are asymptotically strictly decreasing.

For
x\in X
, the
representation function

r_{A,h}(x)
counts
the number of
h
-tuples

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A\times\cdots\times A
such that

x=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}
. The representation function determines the sumset:

hA=\{x\in X:r_{A,h}(x)>0\}
.
In the additive semigroup
\mathbf{Z}
, we have
r_{\mathbf{Z},h}(x)=\infty
for all
x\in\mathbf{Z}
and
h\geq 2
.

Theorem 1

(Nathanson
[
2
]
)
.

Let
h\in\mathbf{N}
. Let
X
be an additive abelian semigroup such that
r_{X,h}(x)<\infty

for all
x\in X
. Let
A
be a subset of
X

and let
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
be an asymptotically strictly decreasing sequence
of subsets of
X
such that
A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}
.
Then

hA=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}

and
h\in\mathcal{H}(A_{q})
.
If
r_{X,h}(x)<\infty
for all
x\in X
and
h\geq 2
, then

hA=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}

for all
h\in\mathbf{N}
and
\mathcal{H}(A_{q})=\mathbf{N}
.

Theorem 2
.

If
A
is a set of nonnegative integers and if
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
is a
strictly decreasing sequence
of sets of nonnegative integers such that
A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}
, then

\mathcal{H}(A_{q})=\mathbf{N}
.

Proof.

The additive semigroup
\mathbf{N}_{0}
satisfies
r_{\mathbf{N}_{0},h}(x)<\infty

for all
h\in\mathbf{N}
and
x\in\mathbf{N}_{0}
.
∎

A sequence
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
of sets of integers is
uniformly bounded below

if there is an integer
m_{0}

such that
\min A_{q}\geq m_{0}
for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
.

Theorem 3
.

If
(A_{q})
is a strictly decreasing sequence
of sets of integers that are uniformly bounded below, then
\mathcal{H}(A_{q})=\mathbf{N}
.

Proof.

Because the sequence
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded below,
there is an integer
m_{0}
such that
x\geq m_{0}
for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
and
x\in A_{q}
.
Let
A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}
. For all
h\in\mathbf{N}
,
if
x\in\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}
, then, for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
,
there is an
h
-tuple
(a_{1,q},\ldots,a_{h,q})\in A_{q}^{h}
such that

x=a_{1,q}+\cdots+a_{h,q}
. For all
j=1,\ldots,h
, we have

m_{0}\leq a_{j,q}=x-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{h}a_{i,q}\leq x-(h-1)m_{0}

There are only finitely many
h
-tuples of integers
(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})
such that

m_{0}\leq a^{\prime}_{j}\leq x-(h-1)m_{0}

for all
j=1,\ldots,h
, and so there is an
h
-tuple
(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})
such that

(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})=(a_{1,q},\ldots,a_{h,q})

for infinitely many
q
. For all
j=1,\ldots,h
, we have

a^{\prime}_{j}\in A_{q}
for infinitely many
q
. Because the sets
A_{q}
are decreasing,
we have
a^{\prime}_{j}\in A_{q}
for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
and so
a^{\prime}_{j}\in A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}
.
Therefore,
(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})\in A^{h}
and
x=a^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+a^{\prime}_{h}\in hA
.
It follows that
hA=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}
for all
h\in\mathbf{N}
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
3

is essentially the proof of
Theorem
1
in
[
2
]
.

2.
Arithmetical structure

Let
a+m\mathbf{Z}
be the congruence class of
a
modulo
m
and, for
u,v\in\mathbf{Z}
, let

[u,v]+m\mathbf{Z}=\bigcup_{a=u}^{v}(a+m\mathbf{Z}).

Theorem 4
.

For every integer
h_{0}\geq 3
, there is a strictly
decreasing sequence
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
of sets of integers such that

\mathcal{H}(A_{q})=\{1\}\cup\{h_{0},h_{0}+1,h_{0}+2,\ldots\}.

Proof.

Let
h_{0}\geq 3
.
We shall construct a strictly
decreasing sequence
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
of sets of integers with

(1)

A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}

such that

(2)

hA=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}

for all
h\geq h_{0}
, but

(3)

hA\neq\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}

for all
h\in\{2,\ldots,h_{0}-1\}
.

Let
s
be a positive integer and let

m=(h_{0}-1)s+2

and

A=[0,s]+m\mathbf{Z}.

For all
h\in\mathbf{N}
, we have

hA=[0,hs]+m\mathbf{Z}.

Also, because
0\in A
, the sequence
of sumsets is increasing:

hA\subseteq(h+1)A
for all
h\in\mathbf{N}
.

For
h\in\{1,2,\ldots,h_{0}-1\}
, we have

hA\subseteq(h_{0}-1)A=[0,(h_{0}-1)s]+m\mathbf{Z}=[0,m-2]+m\mathbf{Z}.

Therefore,

(4)

(m-1+m\mathbf{Z})\cap hA=\emptyset\qquad\text{for all $h\in\{1,2,\ldots,h_{0}-1\}$.}

Because
h_{0}s\geq(h_{0}-1)s+1=m-1
, for all
h\geq h_{0}
we have

\mathbf{Z}=[0,m-1]+m\mathbf{Z}=[0,h_{0}s]+m\mathbf{Z}=h_{0}A\subseteq hA\subseteq\mathbf{Z}

and so

(5)

hA=\mathbf{Z}\qquad\text{for all $h\geq h_{0}$.}

For all
q\in\mathbf{N}
, let

B_{q}=\{m-1+mr:r\geq q\}

and

A_{q}=A\cup B_{q}.

The sequences
(B_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
and
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
are strictly decreasing with

\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}B_{q}=\emptyset

and

\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}(A\cup B_{q})=A.

Moreover,

B_{q}\subseteq m-1+m\mathbf{Z}.

Relation (
4
) implies

B_{q}\cap hA=\emptyset

for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
and
h\in\{1,2,\ldots,h_{0}-1\}
.

We have

[0,m-2]+m\mathbf{Z}=[0,(h_{0}-1)s]+m\mathbf{Z}=(h_{0}-1)A\subseteq(h_{0}-1)A_{q}.

Because
m\mathbf{Z}\subseteq A
and
m-1+mq\in B_{q}
, for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
and

h\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle m-1+m\mathbf{Z}

\displaystyle=(m-1+mq)+m\mathbf{Z}\subseteq B_{q}+A

\displaystyle\subseteq 2(A\cup B_{q})=2A_{q}\subseteq hA_{q}

and so

(6)

m-1+m\mathbf{Z}\subseteq\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}\qquad\text{for all $h\geq 2$.}

Comparing (
4
) and (
6
), we see that

hA\neq\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}

and so
h\notin\mathcal{H}(A_{q})
for all
h\in\{2,\ldots,h_{0}-1\}
.

For all
h\geq h_{0}-1\geq 2
and
q\in\mathbf{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}

\displaystyle=[0,m-1]+m\mathbf{Z}=\left([0,m-2]+m\mathbf{Z}\right)\cup\left(m-1+m\mathbf{Z}\right)

\displaystyle=\left([0,(h_{0}-1)s]+m\mathbf{Z}\right)\cup\left(m-1+m\mathbf{Z}\right)

\displaystyle\subseteq(h_{0}-1)A\cup 2A_{q}

\displaystyle\subseteq(h_{0}-1)A_{q}

\displaystyle\subseteq hA_{q}

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathbf{Z}

and so

(7)

\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}=\mathbf{Z}\qquad\text{for all $h\geq h_{0}-1$.}

Comparing (
5
) and (
7
), we see that

hA=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}\qquad\text{for all $h\geq h_{0}$}

and so
h\in\mathcal{H}(A_{q})
for all
h\geq h_{0}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5
.

For every integer
d\geq 2
, there exists a strictly decreasing sequence

(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
of sets of integers with

\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}=\emptyset

such that

\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}=\emptyset

if and only if
d
does not divide
h
.

Corollary 1
.

For every integer
d\geq 2
, there exists a strictly decreasing sequence
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}

of sets of integers such that

\mathcal{H}(A_{q})=\{h\in\mathbf{N}:h\not\equiv 0\pmod{d}\}.

Proof.

Let
(g_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers and let

G_{q}=\prod_{j=1}^{q}g_{j}

for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
. The sequence
(G_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
is strictly increasing
and defines an additive system (Nathanson
[
1
]
).
The integer
G_{q}/G_{k}
is divisible by
g_{k+1}
for all
q\geq k+1
.

For all
q\in\mathbf{N}
, let

A_{q}=\{G_{r},-(d-1)G_{r}:r\geq q\}.

The sequence of sets
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
is strictly decreasing and

A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}=\emptyset.

If
h=d^{\prime}d
, then
h=d^{\prime}(d-1)+d^{\prime}
. For all
q\in\mathbf{N}
, we have
G_{q}\in A_{q}
and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=d^{\prime}\left((d-1)G_{q}-(d-1)G_{q}\right)

\displaystyle=d^{\prime}(d-1)G_{q}+d^{\prime}(-(d-1)G_{q})

\displaystyle\in hA_{q}.

It follows that if
d
divides
h
, then
0\in\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}
.

Conversely, if
0\in\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}
, then, for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
,
we have
0\in hA_{q}
and so there exist sequences of nonnegative integers

(x_{r})_{r=q}^{\infty}
and
(y_{r})_{r=q}^{\infty}
such that

h=\sum_{r=q}^{\infty}(x_{r}+y_{r})

and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=q}^{\infty}(x_{r}G_{r}+y_{r}(-(d-1)G_{r}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=q}^{\infty}(x_{r}-(d-1)y_{r})G_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=q}^{\infty}z_{r}G_{r}

where
z_{r}=x_{r}-(d-1)y_{r}
for all
r\geq q
, and

|z_{r}|=|x_{r}-(d-1)y_{r}|\leq(d-1)(x_{r}+y_{r})\leq(d-1)h.

Suppose that
z_{r}\neq 0
for some
r\geq q
. Let
k
be the smallest integer such that

k\geq q
and
z_{k}\neq 0
. Then

z_{k}G_{k}=-\sum_{r=k+1}^{\infty}z_{r}G_{r}.

Because
g_{k+1}
divides
G_{r}/G_{k}
for all
r\geq k+1
,
there is a nonzero integer
\ell
such that

0\neq z_{k}=-\sum_{r=k+1}^{\infty}z_{r}\left(\frac{G_{r}}{G_{k}}\right)=g_{k+1}\ell.

This implies

g_{q}\leq g_{k}<g_{k+1}\leq|z_{k}|\leq(d-1)h

for all
q
, which is absurd because
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}g_{j}=\infty
.
It follows that, for all
r\geq q
, we have
z_{r}=x_{r}-(d-1)y_{r}=0
and so

h=\sum_{r=q}^{\infty}(x_{r}+y_{r})=\sum_{r=q}^{\infty}((d-1)y_{r}+y_{r})=d\sum_{r=q}^{\infty}y_{r}\equiv 0\pmod{d}.

This proves that
0\in\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}
if and only if

h\equiv 0\pmod{d}
.

If
n\in\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}
and
n\neq 0
, then, for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
,
we have
n\in hA_{q}
and there is a sequence
(z_{r})_{r=q}^{\infty}

of integers such that

n=\sum_{r=q}^{\infty}z_{r}G_{r}=G_{q}\sum_{r=q}^{\infty}z_{r}\left(\frac{G_{r}}{G_{q}}\right)

and so
|n|\geq G_{q}
for all
q
, which is absurd
because
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}G_{q}=\infty
. Therefore,

\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}=\begin{cases}\{0\}&\text{if $h\equiv 0\pmod{d}$}\\
\emptyset&\text{if $h\not\equiv 0\pmod{d}$.}\end{cases}

Because
hA=\emptyset
for all
h\in\mathbf{N}
, we have

\mathcal{H}(A_{q})=\{h\in\mathbf{N}:h\not\equiv 0\pmod{d}\}
.
This completes the proof.
∎
