Abstract
. An old conjecture in non-Kähler geometry states that, if a compact Hermitian manifold has constant holomorphic sectional curvature, then the metric must be Kähler (when the constant is non-zero) or Chern flat (when the constant is zero). It is known to be true in complex dimension 2 2 by the work of Balas and Gauduchon in 1985 (when the constant is negative or zero) and Apostolov, Davidov and Muskarov in 1996 (in general). In dimension 3 3 or higher, the conjecture is only known in some special cases, such as for all twistor spaces by the work of Davidov, Grantcharov, and Muskarov, or for the locally conformally Kähler case (when the constant is negative or zero) by the work of H. Chen, L. Chen and Nie, or for all non-balanced Bismut torsion parallel (BTP) manifolds by the work of S. Chen and Zheng, where they also showed that the conjecture holds for all balanced BTP threefolds, utilizing a classification result by Zhao and Zheng. In this article, we show that the conjecture is valid for all balanced BTP manifolds in complex dimension 4 4 . The interesting part is that while balanced BTP manifolds are highly restrictive, the classification is still lacking in dimensions 4 4 or higher, and this study might shed some light on the structure of balanced BTP manifolds in dimension 4 4 .

1.
Introduction and statement of results

In non-Kähler geometry, a long-standing open conjecture is the following one which aims at understanding the “Hermitian space forms”:

Conjecture 1

(
Constant Holomorphic Sectional Curvature Conjecture).

Any compact Hermitian manifold with constant holomorphic sectional curvature must be either Kähler or Chern flat.

Here for a given Hermitian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, the holomorphic sectional curvature in the direction of
X
is defined by

H(X)=R_{X\bar{X}X\bar{X}}/|X|^{4}
,
where
X
is any non-zero complex tangent vector of
(1,0)
type and
R
is the curvature tensor of the Chern connection
\nabla
of
g
.

Recall that complete Kähler manifolds with constant holomorphic sectional curvature are called
complex space forms
, their universal covers are the complex projective space
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, the complex Euclidean space
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, or the complex hyperbolic space
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, equipped with (a constant multiple of) the standard metrics. On the other hand, by the classic result of Boothby
[
8
]
in 1958, compact Chern flat manifolds are exactly compact quotients of complex Lie groups (equipped with left-invariant Hermitian metrics). Compact Chern flat manifolds can (and often) be non-Kähler when
n\geq 3
(while in the non-compact case there exist non-Kähler Chern flat complete Hermitian surfaces
[
19
]
).

So the above conjecture simply says that if
(M^{n},g)
is a compact Hermitian manifold with
H=c
, then either
g
is Kähler (hence
(M^{n},g)
is a complex space form), or
g
is Chern flat (namely,
R=0
) hence its universal cover is a complex Lie group (equipped with a left-invariant Hermitian metric).

Conjecture
1
is known to be true when
n=2
, by the work of Balas and Gauduchon
[
6
]
(see also
[
5
]
) in 1985 for the
c\leq 0
case, and by
Apostolov, Davidov, Muskarov
[
4
]
in 1996 for all
n=2
cases, as a corollary of their beautiful classification theorem for compact self-dual Hermitian surfaces.

For
n\geq 3
, the conjecture is still largely open, except in some special cases. The first substantial result towards the conjecture is the one obtained by Davidov, Grantcharov, and Muskarov
[
12
]
, in which they showed among other things that the only twistor space with constant holomorphic sectional curvature is the complex space form
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
. More recently, H. Chen, L. Chen and Nie
[
9
]
considered locally conformally Kähler manifolds and confirmed the conjecture in the
c\leq 0
cases. They also pointed out the necessity of the compactness assumption in the conjecture by explicit examples. Tang in
[
17
]
proved the conjecture under the additional assumption that the metric is Chern Kähler-like, meaning that the curvature tensor
R
obeys all the Kähler symmetries. In
[
24
]
, Zhou and Zheng proved that any compact balanced threefold with zero
real bisectional curvature
, a notion introduced in
[
20
]
which is slightly stronger than
H
, must be Chern flat. Also, in
[
14
]
and
[
16
]
, Zheng and collaborators confirmed the conjecture under the additional assumption that either
(M^{n},g)
is a complex nilmanifold with nilpotent complex structure
J
(in the sense of
[
11
]
), or
g
is Bismut Kähler-like (BKL), meaning that the curvature
R^{b}
of the Bismut connection
\nabla^{b}
of
g
(
[
7
]
) obeys all Kähler symmetries.

An interesting class of special Hermitian metrics consists of
Bismut torsion-parallel
(abbreviated as BTP) manifolds, which means
\nabla^{b}T^{b}=0
where
T^{b}
is the torsion tensor of
\nabla^{b}
. All BKL manifolds and all Vaisman manifolds are examples of non-balanced BTP manifolds, by the work of Zhao and Zheng
[
21
]
and by the work of Andrada and Villacampa
[
2
]
, respectively.

Recall that a Hermitian metric
g
is said to be
balanced
if
d(\omega^{n-1})=0
, where
\omega
is the Kähler form of
g
and
n
is the complex dimension of the manifold. BTP metrics are systematically studied in
[
22
]
, where it was shown that the BTP condition is equivalent to some partial symmetry conditions on
R^{b}
, generalizing the AOUV conjecture which states that BKL
\subset
BTP. The conjecture was raised by Angella, Otal, Ugarte and Villacampa in
[
3
]
and was confirmed in
[
21
]
.

The majority of BTP manifolds are non-balanced, but there are also balanced BTP manifolds when the complex dimension
n\geq 3
, though such metrics tend to be highly restrictive. In their recent work
[
23
]
, Zhao and Zheng give a coarse classification result for compact balanced (but non-Kähler) BTP threefolds.

In
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
, S. Chen and Zheng confirmed Conjecture
1
for non-balanced BTP manifolds in all dimensions, and also for balanced BTP threefolds, utilizing the classification results of
[
23
]
. The main purpose of this article is to confirm Conjecture
1
for all compact balanced BTP fourfolds:

Theorem 1
.

Let
(M^{4},g)
be a compact Hermitian manifold of complex dimension
4
so that
g
is balanced and Bismut torsion-parallel (BTP). If
g
has constant Chern holomorphic sectional curvature, then it must be either Kähler or Chern flat.

We remark that for BTP manifolds, when the metric is non-balanced, the vector field
X
dual to Gauduchon’s torsion
1
-form
\eta
(
[
13
]
) is a holomorphic vector field with constant norm, and
X
belongs to the kernel of the Bismut curvature tensor
R^{b}
. This gives a starting point in the study of Conjecture
1
and makes the analysis relatively easy. In the balanced (but non-Kähler) case, on the other hand,
\eta=0
and we do not have a flat direction of
R^{b}
to work with, so the study of Conjecture
1
becomes more challenging. As observed in
[
24
]
, balanced threefolds admit special unitary frames under which the torsion tensor takes a particularly simple form. This is the starting point in the coarse classification for balanced BTP threefolds in
[
23
]
, which enabled Chen and Zheng to prove Conjecture
1
for such threefolds. For dimensions
n\geq 4
, this is no longer the case. In what follows we will give an outline of our strategy in proving Theorem
1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a BTP manifold. Let
e=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be a local unitary frame of type
(1,0)
tangent vectors, and denote by
\varphi=\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n}\}
the coframe of local
(1,0)
-forms dual to
e
(namely,
\varphi_{i}(e_{j})=\delta_{ij}
for any
1\leq i,j\leq n
). Let
T^{j}_{ik}
be the components under
e
of the Chern torsion tensor
T
, and
R^{b}
the curvature tensor of the Bismut connection
\nabla^{b}
. Note that the Bismut torsion
T^{b}
can be expressed in terms of the Chern torsion
T
and vice versa, so the BTP condition
\nabla^{b}T^{b}=0
is equivalent to
\nabla^{b}T=0
(
[
22
]
). Since
\nabla^{b}T=0
implies
(R^{b}\cdot T)^{\ell}_{pq}=0
, so we know that for any BTP manifold and under any local unitary frame
e
it holds that

(1)

(R^{b}_{i\bar{j}}\cdot T)^{\ell}_{pq}:=\sum_{r=1}^{n}\big\{R^{b}_{i\bar{j}r\bar{\ell}}T^{r}_{pq}-R^{b}_{i\bar{j}p\bar{r}}T^{\ell}_{rq}-R^{b}_{i\bar{j}q\bar{r}}T^{\ell}_{pr}\big\}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j,\ell,p,q\leq n.

Next, let us consider the expression

(2)

\sigma_{B}=\sqrt{-1}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}B_{i\bar{j}}\,\varphi_{i}\wedge\overline{\varphi}_{j}=\sqrt{-1}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\big(\sum_{r,s=1}^{n}T^{j}_{rs}\overline{T^{i}_{rs}}\big)\,\varphi_{i}\wedge\overline{\varphi}_{j}.

Clearly, this is independent of the choice of the local unitary frame
e
hence is a globally defined non-negative
(1,1)
-form on
M^{n}
. We will denote by
r_{B}
the number of positive eigenvalues of
\sigma_{B}
, and call it the
rank of the
B
tensor
. Since
\nabla^{b}B=0
,
r_{B}
is a constant.

As observed in
[
10
]
, under the BTP and constant Chern holomorphic sectional curvature assumptions, the Bismut curvature components
R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}
can be explicitly expressed as a quadratic polynomial in
T
. So (
1
) becomes an over-determined system of cubic equations in
T
. When the dimension
n=4
, we will divide the proof into two cases, depending on whether
r_{B}<4
or
r_{B}=4
. The first case is easier as the torsion components
T^{j}_{ik}
have more zero terms. In the second case, which will eventually lead to a contradiction thus cannot occur, we will focus our discussion on the reduced-to-Ricci form of (
1
) after a simultaneous diagonalization process for
B
and
S=Ric^{b}
. When
S
is not identically zero, this reduced form of (
1
) will enable us to rule things out. This is the content of §4. The more technical case when
S
is identically zero will be discussed in the last section.

The proof process along with the classification result of
[
23
]
will also lead to the following explicit description of the Chern flat case:

Corollary 2
.

Let
(M^{4},g)
be a compact, balanced BTP manifold in complex dimension
4
with constant Chern holomorphic sectional curvature. Then one of the following will occur:

(i)

g
is Kähler. In this case
(M^{4},g)
is a complex space form.

(ii)

g
is non-Kähler and Chern flat. In this case the universal cover of
(M^{4},g)
is holomorphically isometric to
(\mbox{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}),\lambda g_{\kappa})\times({\mathbb{C}},g_{0})
. Here
g_{0}
is the flat metric on
{\mathbb{C}}
,
\lambda>0
is a constant, and
g_{\kappa}
is the standard Killing metric.

Let
\{X,Y,Z\}
be a basis of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
so that
[X,Y]=Z
,
[Y,Z]=X
, and
[Z,X]=Y
. Using
\{X,Y,Z\}
as a unitary basis defines a Hermitian inner product on
{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
which is independent of the choice of the basis. This inner product corresponds to a left-invariant Hermitian metric
g_{\kappa}
on the simple complex Lie group
\mbox{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
. The metric is balanced, BTP, and Chern flat.

In general dimensions, Podestà and Zheng characterized compact BTP manifolds that are Chern flat
[
15
, Theorem 1.2, Theorem 3.4]
. Their universal covers are complex reductive Lie groups, the BTP metrics preserve the product structure and are unique up to a Killing-isometry on each simple factor. In particular, in complex dimension
3
and
4
, up to a constant multiple of the metric, the only (non-Kähler) compact, Chern flat BTP manifolds are quotients of
(\mbox{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}),g_{\kappa})
or
(\mbox{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}),g_{\kappa})\times({\mathbb{C}},g_{0})
.

Let us conclude this introduction section by giving a few quick remarks. First, in Conjecture
1
, one can replace the constant Chern holomorphic sectional curvature assumption by the constant Levi-Civita (or Bismut) holomorphic sectional curvature assumption. The proof of Theorem
1
is very likely extendable to such cases. Secondly, a natural question is how to extend Theorem
1
to higher dimensions, namely, to confirm Conjecture
1
for balanced BTP manifolds in dimensions
5
or higher. The proof of Theorem
1
is detailed case-by-case analysis which does not seem to be easily extendable, and new ideas and insight are needed. Thirdly, balanced BTP manifolds form a highly restrictive class of special Hermitian manifolds, though its classification is still wide open in dimensions
4
or higher. A technical difficulty is we do not know where to start, as the
3
-dimensional case was indeed too special. The analysis in this article, although only aimed at a rather special case when the Chern holomorphic sectional curvature is constant, it does allow us to look into the structure of balanced BTP fourfolds in some cases. Hopefully the discussions can be pushed further to generate some useful information which might help lead to some classification result for such manifolds in dimension
4
or higher.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin by fixing some notions. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Hermitian manifold. Denote by
\nabla
,
\nabla^{b}
the Chern and Bismut connection of
g
, and write their torsion and curvature as
T
,
T^{b}
,
R
, and
R^{b}
, respectively. The Chern holomorphic sectional curvature is a constant
c
if and only if

\widehat{R}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}:=\frac{1}{4}\big(R_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}+R_{k\bar{j}i\bar{\ell}}+R_{i\bar{\ell}k\bar{j}}+R_{k\bar{\ell}i\bar{j}}\big)=\frac{c}{2}\big(\delta_{ij}\delta_{k\ell}+\delta_{i\ell}\delta_{kj}\big),

for any
1\leq i,j,k,\ell\leq n
under any local unitary frame
e
. When
g
is BTP and having constant Chern holomorphic sectional curvature,
[
10
, Lemma 8]
gives the expression of
R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}
. Note that the Chern connection corresponds to the
t=0
case in the family of Gauduchon connections, while the
v
,
w
notations were defined in the formula five lines above Lemma 7 in
[
10
]
.

Lemma 1

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a BTP manifold whose Chern holomorphic sectional curvature is equal to a constant
c
. Then under any local unitary frame
e
,

(3)

R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}=\frac{c}{2}\big(\delta_{ij}\delta_{k\ell}+\delta_{i\ell}\delta_{kj}\big)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{n}T^{r}_{ik}\overline{T^{r}_{j\ell}}-\frac{3}{4}\sum_{r=1}^{n}\big(T^{j}_{ir}\overline{T^{k}_{\ell r}}+T^{\ell}_{kr}\overline{T^{i}_{jr}}\big)+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r=1}^{n}\big(T^{\ell}_{ir}\overline{T^{k}_{jr}}+T^{j}_{kr}\overline{T^{i}_{\ell r}}\big),

for any
1\leq i,j,k,\ell\leq n
.

Recall that Gauduchon’s torison
1
-form
\eta
is defined by
d(\omega^{n-1})=-\eta\wedge\omega^{n-1}
where
\omega
is the Kähler form of
g
. By definition, the metric
g
is balanced if
\eta=0
. Write
\eta=\sum_{i}\eta_{i}\varphi_{i}
where
\varphi
is the conframe dual to
e
, then we have
\eta_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}T^{k}_{ki}
for each
i
(see
[
22
]
for instance). Take
k=\ell
and sum it up from
1
to
n
in (
3
), we get the expression for (first) Bismut Ricci tensor
S
:

Lemma 2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a balanced BTP manifold with constant Chern holomorphic sectional curvature
c
. Then under any local unitary frame
e
, the first Bismut Ricci curvature tensor
S
has components

(4)

S_{i\bar{j}}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{k}}=\frac{c}{2}(n+1)\delta_{ij}+\frac{1}{4}\big(B_{i\bar{j}}-A_{i\bar{j}}\big),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j\leq n,

where
A_{i\bar{j}}=\sum_{r,s}T^{r}_{is}\overline{T^{r}_{js}}
,
B_{i\bar{j}}=\sum_{r,s}T^{j}_{rs}\overline{T^{i}_{rs}}
.

Note that both
A
and
B
are independent of the choice of the local unitary frame
e
thus are globally defined
(1,1)
tensors on
M^{n}
. Their traces are clearly equal, so the trace of
S
is given by

(5)

\mbox{tr}(S):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}S_{i\bar{i}}\,=\,\frac{c}{2}n(n+1).

Fix
p\in M
, and write
V=T_{p}M\cong{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
the holomorphic tangent space at
p
. Denote by
\mbox{ker}(B)=\{v\in V\mid B_{v\bar{u}}=0\ \,\forall\,u\in V\}
the kernel of
B
, and by
(\mbox{ker}(B))^{\perp}
its orthogonal complement in
V
. We have the following

Lemma 3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a BTP manifold. Then under any local unitary frame
e
it holds

(6)

R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ e_{k}\in(\mbox{ker}(B))^{\perp}\ \mbox{and}\ \,e_{\ell}\in\mbox{ker}(B),

for any
1\leq i,j\leq n
.

Proof.

Let
e
be a local unitary frame. Under the BTP assumption, the equation (
1
) holds. Now if
e_{k}\in(\mbox{ker}(B))^{\perp}
,
e_{\ell}\in\mbox{ker}(B)
, then by the definition of
B
we have
T^{\ell}_{pq}=0
for any
1\leq p,q\leq n
. So (
1
) becomes

\sum_{r=1}^{n}R^{b}_{i\bar{j}r\bar{\ell}}\,T^{r}_{pq}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j,p,q\leq n.

Multiply
\overline{T^{k}_{pq}}
on both sides of the above equation and sum up
p
and
q
, we get
\sum_{r}R^{b}_{i\bar{j}r\bar{\ell}}\,B_{k\bar{r}}=0
. Since
B
is positive definite in
(\mbox{ker}(B))^{\perp}
, this means that
R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}=0
whenever
e_{k}\in(\mbox{ker}(B))^{\perp}
. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Next we show that under the BTP condition, the tensors
B
and
S
can be simultaneously diagonalized, namely,

Lemma 4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a BTP manifold. Then locally there always exist unitary frames under which both
B
and
S
are diagonal, namely,
S_{i\bar{j}}=B_{i\bar{j}}=0
for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
.

Proof.

Let
e
be any local unitary frame. Under the BTP assumption, the Bismut curvature obeys the symmetry condition
R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}=R^{b}_{k\bar{\ell}i\bar{j}}
for any
1\leq i,j,k,\ell\leq n
. Denote by
\theta^{b}
,
\Theta^{b}
the matrix of connection and curvature for the Bismut connection
\nabla^{b}
under
e
, namely,

\nabla^{b}e_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta^{b}_{kj}\otimes e_{j},\ \ \ \Theta^{b}=d\theta^{b}-\theta^{b}\wedge\theta^{b},\ \ \ \ \Theta^{b}_{k\ell}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}\,\varphi_{i}\wedge\overline{\varphi}_{j},

for any
1\leq k,\ell\leq n
where
\varphi
is coframe dual to
e
. Since
\nabla^{b}T=0
, we get
\nabla^{b}B=0
, that is,

dB_{i\bar{j}}=\sum_{r}\big(\theta^{b}_{ir}B_{r\bar{j}}+B_{i\bar{r}}\overline{\theta^{b}_{jr}}\big),

or
dB=\theta^{b}B-B\,\theta^{b}
in matrix form. Here we used the fact that
\theta^{b}
is skew-Hermitian. Taking the exterior derivative on the last equality and using the structure equation, we obtain

(7)

0\,=\,\Theta^{b}B-B\,\Theta^{b}.

The matrix
B
is Hermitian symmetric, so we may choose a local unitary frame
e
so that the matrix
B=(B_{i\bar{j}})
under
e
is diagonal. Put the equal diagonal elements of
B
into the same blocks, equation (
7
) says that
\Theta^{b}_{ij}=0
whenever
B_{i\bar{i}}\neq B_{j\bar{j}}
, so
\Theta^{b}
is block-diagonal. By the symmetry property of
R^{b}
we have

S_{i\bar{j}}=\sum_{k}R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{k}}=\sum_{k}R^{b}_{k\bar{k}i\bar{j}},

which is the trace of the
(1,1)
-form
\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{b}_{ij}
with respect to the Kähler form
\omega
of
g
. Therefore we conclude that
S=(S_{i\bar{j}})=\mbox{tr}_{\omega}(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{b})
, so
S
is also block diagonal under
e
. Within each block,
B
is a multiple of the identity matrix, so by a suitable unitary change of basis in each block, we may assume that
S
is diagonal, while
B
remains the same. Under the new unitary frame both
B
and
S
are diagonalized simultaneously.
∎

2
and Lemma

, we have the following

Lemma 5
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a balanced BTP manifold with constant Chern holomorphic sectional curvature. Then locally there always exist unitary frames under which

,
A
,

are all diagonal.

Finally, by contracting the first two indices

and

in (

) and utilizing the symmetry condition

Lemma 6
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a BTP manifold, and let
e
be a local unitary frame under which

is diagonal. Then we have

(8)

(S_{i\bar{i}}+S_{k\bar{k}}-S_{j\bar{j}})\,T^{j}_{ik}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j,k\leq n.

3.
Balanced BTP fourfolds with
r_{B}<4

Let
(M^{4},g)
be a balanced BTP manifold such that its Chern holomorphic sectional curvature is equal to a constant
c
. The goal is to show that either
g
is Kähler, in which case
(M^{4},g)
is a complex space form, or
g
is non-Kähler and Chern flat, in which case if we further assume the manifold is compact, then
(M^{4},g)
is a compact quotient of
\mbox{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})\times{\mathbb{C}}
with the product metric
\lambda g_{\kappa}\times g_{0}
, of a constant multiple of the Killing metric with the flat Euclidean metric. In this section, we will deal with the
r_{B}<4
case, and leave the
r_{B}=4
case to the next two sections.

We will separate our discussions according to the values of
r_{B}
, the rank of the
B
tensor. Note that
r_{B}=0
corresponds to the situation
B=0
, in this case
T=0
hence
g
is is Kähler. So we just need to examine the cases
1\leq r_{B}<4
in this section. For the most part, the argument works locally.

First let us consider the
r_{B}=1
case. In this situation the argument works for all dimensions, and we have the following

Proposition 1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a balanced BTP manifold with constant Chern holomorphic sectional curvature, then
r_{B}\neq 1
.

Proof.

Assume
r_{B}=1
on the contrary, we want to derive at a contradiction. We may assume that
n\geq 2
, as for
n=1
all Hermitian metrics are Kähler (hence
r_{B}=0
). Choose local unitary frame
e
so that
\mbox{ker}(B)
is spanned by
\{e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
. So
T^{j}_{ik}=0
for all
i,k
and for all
2\leq j\leq n
. Since
g
is balanced,
T^{1}_{1k}=-T^{2}_{2k}-\cdots-T^{n}_{nk}=0
for any
k
, so the only possibly non-zero Chern torsion components are
T^{1}_{ij}
,
2\leq i,j\leq n
. Write
T^{1}_{ij}
as
E_{ij}
, then
E=(E_{ij})
becomes a skew-symmetric
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix. Since
T^{\ast}_{1\ast}=0
, by (
3
) we have

R^{b}_{1\bar{1}k\bar{\ell}}=\frac{c}{2}(\delta_{k\ell}+\delta_{1k}\delta_{1\ell})+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r=1}^{n}T^{1}_{kr}\overline{T^{1}_{\ell r}},

hence
R^{b}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}=c
