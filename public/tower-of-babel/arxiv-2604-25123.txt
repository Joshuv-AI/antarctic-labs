Implied Volatility Expansions for VIX Options in Forward Variance Models
Y. Liao, A. Agarwal, F. Bourgey

Abstract
We develop closed-form expansions for the implied volatility of VIX options within the class of forward variance models. Our approach builds on weak-approximation techniques for VIX option prices and yields explicit implied volatility expansions with computable correction terms. The resulting formulas enable fast and accurate calibration without requiring numerical root-finding. We illustrate the performance of the proposed expansions in both standard and rough Bergomi-type models, as well as in mixed specifications, and demonstrate their accuracy through numerical experiments.



1
Introduction

Accurate closed-form implied volatility expansions allow industry practitioners to efficiently calibrate their models to market data. This operation is crucial for computing risk sensitivities of derivative trading books based on current market conditions. In this work, we develop implied volatility expansions for options based on S&P500 volatility index (VIX) within the class of forward variance models. Our implied volatility expansions are derived from VIX option price approximations developed by
[
8
]
.

The VIX was first introduced in 1993 by the Chicago Board Options Exchange (CBOE) and is designed to measure the 30-day implied volatility of a log-contract on the S&P 500 index. In practice, the log-contract is replicated using market prices of listed vanilla options across a continuum of strikes. Following
[
2
]
, a natural way to model the joint dynamics of the S&P 500 spot price
S_{t}
and its implied volatility surface is to work directly with the term structure of implied variances of log-contracts, referred to as forward variances. As is well known, these forward variances are driftless under the risk–neutral measure and can be treated as additional state variables alongside
S_{t}
. The resulting models are referred to as
forward variance models
.

In this framework, the instantaneous forward variance curve
(\xi_{t}^{u})_{t\leq u}
is defined by

\displaystyle\xi_{t}^{u}=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}\Bigl((u-t)\,\hat{\sigma}_{t}(u)^{2}\Bigr),

(1)

where
\hat{\sigma}_{t}(u)^{2}
denotes the implied variance of the log-contract with maturity
u
observed at time
t
(equivalently, the variance swap volatility with maturity
u
at time
t
). In this work, we assume that the process
(\xi_{t}^{u})_{t\leq u}
solves the following stochastic differential equation (SDE)

\displaystyle\xi_{t}^{u}=\xi_{0}^{u}+\int_{0}^{t}\xi_{s}^{u}\,K^{u}(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{s},

(2)

where
K
is a deterministic
L^{2}
-kernel function, and
\xi_{0}^{u}
is a given initial forward variance curve. The solution to (
2
) is explicitly given by the log-normal process

\displaystyle\xi_{t}^{u}=\xi_{0}^{u}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}K^{u}(s)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}K^{u}(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{s}\Bigr),

for all
u\geq t
. Equivalently, the instantaneous log-forward variance
X_{t}^{u}:=\ln(\xi_{t}^{u})
, with
u\geq t
, solves the SDE

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{u}=-\frac{1}{2}K^{u}(t)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+K^{u}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{t}.

(3)

Assumption 1
.

The initial instantaneous forward variance curve
u\mapsto\xi_{0}^{u}
is positive, bounded, and bounded away from zero.

Assumption 2
.

The kernel
K
in (
2
) satisfies
\int_{0}^{t}K^{u}(s)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s<\infty
, for every
u\in[T,T+\overline{\Delta}]
, for some
\overline{\Delta}\geq 1
. Moreover, for any
p>0
, there exists a positive constant
C_{p}
such that, for all
\Delta\leq\overline{\Delta}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\mathrm{e}^{\,p\int_{0}^{T}K^{u}(t)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\leq C_{p}.

(4)

This class of models encompasses both the standard Bergomi model
[
3
]
and the rough Bergomi model
[
1
]
. In the standard Bergomi model, the kernel takes the exponential form

\displaystyle K^{u}(t)=\omega\,\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa(u-t)},

(5)

where
\omega>0
is the volatility of forward variances, and
\kappa>0
is the mean-reversion speed. The rough Bergomi model corresponds to a fractional power-law kernel,

\displaystyle K^{u}(t)=\eta\,(u-t)^{H-\frac{1}{2}},

(6)

where the Hurst exponent
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
governs the degree of roughness (or memory decay), and
\eta>0
again represents the volatility of forward variances. Unlike (
5
), the curve
u\mapsto\xi_{t}^{u}
does not admit a finite-dimensional Markovian representation.

1.1
Our contributions

In this work, we consider both specifications: the standard and the rough Bergomi models, as the standard Bergomi model is comparatively more tractable for simulation and calibration, whereas the rough Bergomi model is better aligned with empirical evidence on rough volatility and short-maturity implied volatility skews. Moreover, we consider mixed versions of the two models, which are more suitable than their single kernel counterparts. In particular, using our closed-form implied volatility expansions, we can accurately capture the observed market skew without resorting to numerical root-finding. Through simulation studies, we show that our implied volatility expansions allow rapid calibration across a wide range of model parameters. Only in extreme parameter regimes does the accuracy deteriorate, but our implied volatility expansion still provides reliable initial guesses for standard root-finding procedures.

1.2
Notations and definitions

The Black-Scholes (BS) call and put price formulas, as a function of the log-spot
x\in\mathbb{R}
, log-strike
k\in\mathbb{R}
, and volatility
\sigma>0
, are defined by

\displaystyle C^{\mathrm{BS}}(x,k,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}T+\sigma\sqrt{T}Z}-\mathrm{e}^{k}\bigr)^{+}\Bigr]=\mathrm{e}^{x}\,\Phi(d_{1}(x,k,\sigma))-\mathrm{e}^{k}\,\Phi(d_{2}(x,k,\sigma)),

(7)

\displaystyle P^{\mathrm{BS}}(x,k,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{k}-\mathrm{e}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}T+\sigma\sqrt{T}Z}\bigr)^{+}\Bigr]=\mathrm{e}^{k}\Phi(-d_{2}(x,k,\sigma))-\mathrm{e}^{x}\Phi(-d_{1}(x,k,\sigma)),

(8)

where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and

\displaystyle d_{1}(x,k,\sigma):=\frac{x-k}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}+\frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2},\qquad d_{2}(x,k,\sigma):=d_{1}(x,k,\sigma)-\sigma\sqrt{T}.

(9)

Here,
\Phi(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\phi(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t
denotes the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the standard normal random variable, with probability density function (PDF)
\phi(t):=\mathrm{e}^{-t^{2}/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}
.

The first- and second-order partial derivatives of
C^{\mathrm{BS}}
with respect to the volatility
\sigma
are given by (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Proposition 32]
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vega}(x,k,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\partial_{\sigma}C^{\mathrm{BS}}(x,k,\sigma)=\mathrm{e}^{x}\sqrt{T}\phi\bigl(d_{1}(x,k,\sigma)\bigr),

(10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vomma}(x,k,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\partial_{\sigma}^{2}C^{\mathrm{BS}}(x,k,\sigma)=\mathrm{e}^{x}\sqrt{T}\phi(d_{1}(x,k,\sigma))\biggl(\frac{(x-k)^{2}}{\sigma^{3}T}-\frac{\sigma T}{4}\biggr).

(11)

From
[
6
, Proposition 34]
, the following useful identities involving spatial derivatives hold

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}^{2}-\partial_{x})C^{\mathrm{BS}}(x,k,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{Vega}(x,k,\sigma)}{\sigma T},

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}^{3}-\partial_{x}^{2})C^{\mathrm{BS}}(x,k,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Vega}(x,k,\sigma)\Bigl(-\frac{x-k}{\sigma^{3}T^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma T}\Bigr).

(12)

VIX definition.

The VIX index at time
T
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{VIX}_{T}:=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\xi_{T}^{u}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\xi_{0}^{u}\,\mathrm{e}^{Y_{T}^{u}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u},

(13)

where
\Delta=30/365
corresponds to 30 days, and

\displaystyle Y_{T}^{u}:=X_{T}^{u}-X_{0}^{u}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}K^{u}(t)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}K^{u}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{t},

denotes the increment of the log-forward variance over the interval
[0,T]
.

VIX option prices.

Define the VIX call, put, and futures prices for fixed strike and maturity,
\mathrm{e}^{k}
and
T
, respectively, as

\displaystyle C:=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(\mathrm{VIX}_{T}-\mathrm{e}^{k}\bigr)^{+}\Bigr],\qquad P:=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{k}-\mathrm{VIX}_{T}\bigr)^{+}\Bigr],\qquad F:=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}].

VIX implied volatility.

The implied volatility of the VIX call price
C
with log-strike
k
and maturity
T
, denoted by
\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}(k,T)
, is the unique non-negative solution of

\displaystyle C^{\mathrm{BS}}(\ln(F),k,\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}(k,T))=C.

(14)

Hermite polynomials.

Let
(\mathrm{He}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
denote the probabilist’s Hermite polynomials, defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{He}_{j}(x):=(-1)^{j}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\partial_{x}^{j}\,\Bigl(\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\Bigr),\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

(15)

The rest of the paper is organised as follows.
In Section
2.1
and
3.1
, we provide approximations for VIX option prices under single- and mixed-kernel forward variance models, respectively.
In Section
2.2
, we present explicit implied volatility expansion for VIX options and assess their accuracy through numerical experiments in Section
2.3
for both the standard and the rough Bergomi models. In Section
3.2
, we extend these expansions to mixed exponential forward variance models and report the corresponding numerical results in Section
3.3
. In Section
4
, we calibrate our implied volatility expansions to market VIX smiles under both the single- and mixed-kernel forward variance framework. All code supporting this work is available at
https://github.com/fbourgey/rough-bergomi/
.

2
Single-kernel forward variance models

In this section, we outline the key steps for deriving VIX option price approximations within the single-kernel forward variance models introduced in
[
8
]
. The same steps are used later in Section
3.1
to derive price approximations in mixed-kernel forward variance models. These results form the basis for subsequent expansions of the VIX implied volatility, which are developed in the following sections.

2.1
VIX price approximation

Motivated by the idea of approximating an arithmetic mean by its geometric mean (see
[
8
, Section 2.1]
for the intuition), we introduce a log-normal proxy for the squared VIX, defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}:=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]\exp\,\biggl(\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{u}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}Y_{T}^{u}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\biggr),

(16)

where
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]=\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\xi_{0}^{u}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u
.

Proposition 1
.

(
[
8
, Proposition 2.3]
)

The proxy
\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}
is log-normal, that is,
\ln(\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2})

\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{P},\sigma_{P}^{2})
, where the mean and variance are given by

\displaystyle\mu_{P}:=\ln(\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}])-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\biggl(\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{u}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}K^{u}(t)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\biggr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

(17)

and

\displaystyle\sigma_{P}^{2}:=\int_{0}^{T}\biggl(\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{u}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}K^{u}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\biggr)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

(18)

Assumption 3
.

We require the proxy
\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}
to be non-degenerate. Specifically, for any
p>0
, there exist positive constants
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
c
, and
\Delta_{0}
such that, for all
\Delta\leq\Delta_{0}
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\Delta,T,p}

\displaystyle:=\biggl(\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{u}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl(K^{u}(t)^{2}-\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{v}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}K^{v}(t)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\Bigr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\bigg|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c\Delta^{a_{1}},

(19)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\Delta,T,p}

\displaystyle:=\biggl(\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{u}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl(K^{u}(t)-\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{v}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}K^{v}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\Bigr)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\bigg|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c\Delta^{a_{2}},

(20)

Additionally, we assume

\displaystyle\sup_{\Delta\leq\Delta_{0}}|\mu_{P}|\leq c,\qquad\frac{1}{c}\leq\inf_{\Delta\leq\Delta_{0}}\sigma_{P}\leq\sup_{\Delta\leq\Delta_{0}}\sigma_{P}\leq c.

In view of the proxy, the VIX option price
\mathbb{E}[\varphi(\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2})]
is approximated by a Taylor expansion around
\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}
, under the assumption that the payoff function
\varphi
is sufficiently smooth. In what follows, we present an approximation formula for VIX option prices within single-kernel forward variance models, together with explicit expressions for the standard and rough Bergomi models.

Theorem 1
.

(
[
8
, Theorem 2.7]
)

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be a
\theta
-Hölder continuous function for some
\theta\in(0,1]
. Under Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
, the price of a VIX option with payoff
\varphi(\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2})
admits the following approximation

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\varphi(\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2})\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\varphi(\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2})\bigr]+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\gamma_{i}\,\partial_{\epsilon}^{i}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\varphi\bigl(\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}\,e^{\epsilon}\bigr)\bigr]\big|_{\epsilon=0}+\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},

(21)

where the remainder term
\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}
satisfies the bound
|\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}|\leq C\Delta^{3(a_{1}\land\frac{a_{2}}{2})}
.
The coefficients
(\gamma_{i})_{i=\{1,2,3\}}
are given explicitly by

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{8\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{u}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}\Biggl(\int_{0}^{T}\Biggl(K^{u}(t)^{2}-\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{v}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}K^{v}(t)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\Biggr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Biggr)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\frac{1}{2\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{u}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}\int_{0}^{T}\Biggl(K^{u}(t)-\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{v}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}K^{v}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\Biggr)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u,

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{u}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}\Biggl(\int_{0}^{T}\Biggl(K^{u}(t)^{2}-\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{v}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}K^{v}(t)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\Biggr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Biggr)

(22)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\times\Biggl(\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{v}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}K^{v}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\Biggl(K^{u}(t)-\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{v}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}K^{v}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\Biggr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Biggr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u,

(23)

\displaystyle\gamma_{3}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{u}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}\Biggl(\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{v}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}K^{v}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\Biggl(K^{u}(t)-\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{v}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}K^{v}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\Biggr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Biggr)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u.

(24)

Remark 1
.

The payoff of of a VIX call with log-strike
k
is
\varphi(x)=(\sqrt{x}-\mathrm{e}^{k})^{+}
, a VIX put is
\varphi(x)=(\mathrm{e}^{k}-\sqrt{x})^{+}
, and a VIX futures is
\varphi(x)=\sqrt{x}
. In all these cases, the function
\varphi
is
\frac{1}{2}
-Hölder continuous.

VIX option prices proxy.

Define the proxy prices of a VIX call, put and futures by

\displaystyle C_{P,0}:=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}-\mathrm{e}^{k}\bigr)^{+}\Bigr],\qquad P_{P,0}:=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{k}-\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}\bigr)^{+}\Bigr],\qquad F_{P,0}:=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}],

and the corrected proxy prices by

\displaystyle C_{P}

\displaystyle:=C_{P,0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\gamma_{i}\,\partial_{\epsilon}^{i}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl(\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}-\mathrm{e}^{k}\Bigr)^{+}\Bigr]\Big|_{\epsilon=0},

\displaystyle P_{P}

\displaystyle:=P_{P,0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\gamma_{i}\,\partial_{\epsilon}^{i}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{k}-\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\bigr)^{+}\Bigr]\Big|_{\epsilon=0},

\displaystyle F_{P}

\displaystyle:=F_{P,0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\gamma_{i}\,\partial_{\epsilon}^{i}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\bigr]\big|_{\epsilon=0}.

Remark 2
.

In the standard Bergomi model, there are closed-form expressions for
\mu_{P}
,
\sigma_{P}
, and
(\gamma_{i})_{i=\{1,2,3\}}
, if the initial forward variance curve
u\mapsto\xi_{0}^{u}
is constant on
u\in[T,T+\Delta]
. In contrast, in the rough Bergomi model, explicit expressions exist for
\mu_{P}
and
\sigma_{P}
, while
(\gamma_{i})_{i=\{1,2,3\}}
do not admit an explicit form even when the forward variance curve is flat. See
[
8
, Propositions 2.9 and 2.11]
for details.

2.2
Implied volatility expansion

In view of Remark
1
, the approximations for VIX call, put, and futures prices in (
21
) can be expressed as linear combinations of the BS price formula and its derivatives with respect to the log-spot price. Let
\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}:=\frac{\sigma_{P}}{\sqrt{T}}
and define the effective log-spot
x_{P}:=\frac{\mu_{P}}{2}+\frac{\sigma_{P}^{2}}{8}
. Consider a call payoff
\varphi(x)=(\sqrt{x}-\mathrm{e}^{k})^{+}
.
From Proposition
1
and Theorem
1
, we have

\displaystyle C_{P,0}=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}-\mathrm{e}^{k}\bigr)^{+}\Bigr]=C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}\bigr).

The
i
-th order derivative in (
21
) is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{\epsilon}^{i}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\varphi\bigl(\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{\epsilon}\bigr)\bigr]\big|_{\epsilon=0}=\partial_{\epsilon}^{i}C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{P}+\tfrac{\epsilon}{2},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}\bigr)\big|_{\epsilon=0}=2^{-i}\partial_{x}^{i}C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}\bigr),\quad i\in\{1,2,3\},

where

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{i}C^{\mathrm{BS}}(x,k,\sigma)=\mathrm{e}^{x}\Phi(d_{1}(x,k,\sigma))+\mathds{1}_{i\geq 2}\,\mathrm{e}^{x}\phi(d_{1}(x,k,\sigma))\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\binom{i-1}{j}\,(-1)^{j-1}\frac{\mathrm{He}_{j-1}(d_{1}(x,k,\sigma))}{\sigma^{j}T^{\frac{j}{2}}}.

(25)

Thus, the VIX call price approximation in (
21
) rewrites as

\displaystyle C_{P}=C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}\bigr)+\sum_{j=1}^{3}2^{-j}\gamma_{i}\,\partial_{x}^{i}C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}\bigr).

(26)

We can analogously rewrite the price approximation in (
21
) for VIX put, using the combination of the BS put price formula in (
8
) and its derivatives. The VIX put price approximation formula is then given by

\displaystyle P_{P}=P^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}\bigr)+\sum_{j=1}^{3}2^{-j}\gamma_{i}\,\partial_{x}^{i}P^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}\bigr),

(27)

where

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{i}P^{\mathrm{BS}}(x,k,\sigma)=-\mathrm{e}^{x}\Phi(-d_{1}(x,k,\sigma))+\mathds{1}_{i\geq 2}\,\mathrm{e}^{x}\phi(d_{1}(x,k,\sigma))\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\binom{i-1}{j}\,(-1)^{j-1}\frac{\mathrm{He}_{j-1}(d_{1}(x,k,\sigma))}{\sigma^{j}T^{\frac{j}{2}}}.

(28)

For VIX futures prices, the computation is the same but with
\mathrm{e}^{k}=0
. The expression is given as follows

\displaystyle F_{P}=\mathrm{e}^{x_{P}}\Bigl(1+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{4}+\frac{\gamma_{3}}{8}\Bigr).

(29)

In the above, the put-call parity holds, that is,
C_{P}-P_{P}=F_{P}-\mathrm{e}^{k}
. Consequently, implied volatilities, using the futures price (
29
), computed either from VIX call options (
26
) or VIX put options (
27
) coincide.

Inspired by
[
5
]
, we derive an approximation for the volatility implied by VIX call prices in (
26
) by using relations between BS Greeks and sensitivities of VIX option prices with respect to log-spot
x
and volatility
\sigma
in (
1.2
).

Theorem 2
.

Under Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
, the VIX implied volatility
\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}(k,T)
defined in (
14
) is approximated by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}(k,T)\approx\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}T}+\frac{3\gamma_{3}}{8\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}T}-\frac{\gamma_{3}}{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}^{3}T^{2}}(x_{P}-k),

(30)

where
\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}=\frac{\sigma_{P}}{\sqrt{T}}
,
x_{P}=\frac{\mu_{P}}{2}+\frac{\sigma_{P}^{2}}{8}
, and the coefficients
(\gamma_{i})_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}
are given in (
1
).

Proof.

Let us set
\delta=\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{4}+\frac{\gamma_{3}}{8}
. Using the VIX futures approximation in (
29
),
F\approx F_{P}=e^{x_{p}}(1+\delta)
, we have

\displaystyle\ln(F)\approx\ln(F_{P})=x_{P}+\ln(1+\delta)=x_{P}+\delta+O(\delta^{2}).

Hence, by a first-order Taylor expansion in the first argument,

\displaystyle C=C^{\mathrm{BS}}(\ln(F),k,\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}})\approx C^{\mathrm{BS}}(x_{P},k,\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}})+\delta\,\partial_{x}C^{\mathrm{BS}}(x_{P},k,\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}),

up to terms of order
O(\delta^{2})
. This shows that the additional
\partial_{x}C^{\mathrm{BS}}
term arises from replacing
\ln(F)
by its approximation.

From (
26
) and the identities in (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle C_{P}=C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}\bigr)+\delta\,\partial_{x}C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}\bigr)+\Delta\sigma\,\mathrm{Vega}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}\bigr),

where

\displaystyle\Delta\sigma:=\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}T}+\frac{3\gamma_{3}}{8\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}T}-\frac{\gamma_{3}}{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}^{3}T^{2}}(x_{P}-k).

A first-order Taylor expansion in the volatility argument yields

\displaystyle C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}+\Delta\sigma\bigr)=C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}\bigr)+\Delta\sigma\mathrm{Vega}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}\bigr)+O((\Delta\sigma)^{2}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle C_{P}\approx C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}+\Delta\sigma\bigr)+\delta\,\partial_{x}C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{P},k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P}}{2}\bigr).

∎

3
Mixed-kernel forward variance models

Mixed-kernel forward variance models introduced in

4

are more capable of reproducing observed market VIX smiles than single-kernel forward variance models. In this case, the forward variance process

\displaystyle\xi_{T}^{u}=\xi_{0}^{u}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\lambda_{j}\exp(Y_{T,j}^{u}),\qquad Y_{T,j}^{u}:=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}K_{j}^{u}(t)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}K_{j}^{u}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{t},

The mixed formulation in (

For kernels defined by

When instead

\displaystyle\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\lambda_{j}\mathrm{VIX}_{T,j}^{2},\qquad\mathrm{VIX}_{T,j}^{2}=\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\xi_{0}^{u}\exp(Y_{T,j}^{u})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u.

(32)

), we obtain

\displaystyle C^{j}_{P,N}

\displaystyle=C^{\mathrm{BS}}\!\bigl(x_{\theta,j},k_{\theta,j},\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr)+\delta_{x,j}\partial_{x}C^{\mathrm{BS}}\!\bigl(x_{\theta,j},k_{\theta,j},\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr)+\delta_{k,j}\partial_{k}C^{\mathrm{BS}}\!\bigl(x_{\theta,j},k_{\theta,j},\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\Delta\sigma_{j}\mathrm{Vega}\!\bigl(x_{\theta,j},k_{\theta,j},\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr),

where

\displaystyle\Delta\sigma_{j}:=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\mathrm{e}^{x_{P,j}}}{\mathrm{e}^{x_{\theta,j}}\sqrt{T}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{i=0}^{3}c_{i,j}\,\partial_{\mu_{P,1}}^{i}\omega_{n,j}\mathrm{He}_{n-1}\bigl(A-\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}\sqrt{T}}{2}\bigr).

Since

\displaystyle C^{\mathrm{BS}}(\cdot,\cdot,\sigma+\Delta\sigma)=C^{\mathrm{BS}}(\cdot,\cdot,\sigma)+\Delta\sigma\mathrm{Vega}(\cdot,\cdot,\sigma)+O((\Delta\sigma)^{2}),

we further obtain

\displaystyle C^{j}_{P,N}\approx C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{\theta,j},k_{\theta,j},\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}+\Delta\sigma_{j}\bigr)+\delta_{x,j}\,\partial_{x}C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{\theta,j},k_{\theta,j},\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr)+\delta_{k,j}\,\partial_{k}C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(x_{\theta,j},k_{\theta,j},\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr).

(66)

This representation shows that the VIX call price can be approximated by a BS price with shifted volatility
\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}/2+\Delta\sigma_{j}
, together with first-order corrections in the log-spot and log-strike weighted by
\delta_{x,j}
and
\delta_{k,j}
, respectively.
∎

Remark 4
.

In Proposition
2
and Theorem
4
, we note that both the VIX option price and the VIX implied volatility can be rewritten in two different forms. Although these two representations do not affect the accuracy of VIX call, put, or futures price approximations, they lead to different accuracies for the implied volatility formulas, since the zeroth-order term in (
65
), namely either
\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,1}/2
or
\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,2}/2
, differs across these two forms. Moreover, for each level of log-moneyness, we can also choose the value of
\theta
that provides the best approximation for each
j
.
This is achieved by minimising the first-order correction term in (
65
). More precisely, for
j\in\{1,2\}
, we define

\displaystyle\theta^{*}=\underset{\theta\in[0,1]}{\argmin}\,\Big|\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\,\mathrm{e}^{x_{P,j}}}{\mathrm{e}^{x_{\theta,j}}\sqrt{T}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{i=0}^{3}c_{i,j}\,\partial_{\mu_{P,1}}^{i}\omega_{n,j}\mathrm{He}_{n-1}\bigl(A-\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}\sqrt{T}}{2}\bigr)\Big|.

For a fixed
j
, the only
\theta
-dependence variable in the objective function is
\mathrm{e}^{-x_{\theta,j}}
. Therefore, minimising with respect to
\theta
is equivalent to maximising
x_{\theta,j}
over
[0,1]
, which leads to,

\displaystyle\theta^{*}=\begin{cases}1,&\delta_{j}>0,\\
0,&\delta_{j}<0,\\
\text{any\ }\theta\in[0,1],&\delta_{j}=0.\end{cases}

Remark 5
.

Consider the case of
\theta=0
, which gives

\displaystyle x_{0,j}=\ln(F_{P}),\qquad k_{0,j}=\ln(F_{P})+\frac{A\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}\sqrt{T}}{2}-\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}^{2}T}{8},\qquad j\in\{1,2\}.

Then, we rewrite (
3.2
) as

\displaystyle C^{j}_{P,N}

\displaystyle=C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(\ln(F_{P}),k_{0,j},\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr)+\delta_{k,j}\,\partial_{k}C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(\ln(F_{P}),k_{0,j},\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\,\mathrm{e}^{x_{P,j}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{i=0}^{3}c_{i,j}\,\partial_{\mu_{P,1}}^{i}\omega_{n,j}\mathrm{He}_{n-1}\bigl(A-\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}\sqrt{T}}{2}\bigr)\phi\bigl(A-\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}\sqrt{T}}{2}\bigr),

where the first two terms constitute the first-order Taylor series expansion of
C^{\mathrm{BS}}(\ln(F_{P}),k,\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}/2)
at
k=k_{0,j}
. That is,

\displaystyle C^{j}_{P,N}\approx C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(\ln(F_{P}),k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr)+\Delta\sigma_{j}\,\mathrm{Vega}C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(\ln(F_{P}),k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr),

(67)

where the volatility adjustment
\Delta\sigma
is defined as follows

\displaystyle\Delta\sigma_{j}:=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vega}\bigl(\ln(F_{P}),k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr)}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{i=0}^{3}c_{i,j}\,\partial_{\mu_{P,1}}^{i}\omega_{n,j}\mathrm{He}_{n-1}\bigl(A-\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}\sqrt{T}}{2}\bigr)\phi\bigl(A-\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}\sqrt{T}}{2}\bigr).

Furhtermore, we approximate (
67
) as a first-order Taylor series expansion in
(k,\sigma)=(k,\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}/2)

\displaystyle C_{P,N}\approx C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(\ln(F_{P}),k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr)+\Delta\sigma_{j}\,\mathrm{Vega}C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(\ln(F_{P}),k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr)\approx C^{\mathrm{BS}}\bigl(\ln(F_{P}),k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}+\Delta\sigma_{j}\bigr).

The VIX implied volatility corresponding to this approximation is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}^{j}(\ln(F_{P}),k,T)

(68)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\,\mathrm{e}^{x_{P,j}}}{\mathrm{Vega}\bigl(\ln(F_{P}),k,\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}}{2}\bigr)}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{i=0}^{3}c_{i,j}\partial_{\mu_{P,1}}^{i}\omega_{n,j}\mathrm{He}_{n-1}\bigl(A-\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}\sqrt{T}}{2}\bigr)\phi\bigl(A-\tfrac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{P,j}\sqrt{T}}{2}\bigr).

(69)

Note that the VIX implied volatility expansion obtained in this remark differs from (
65
) in Theorem
4
. This difference arises because the present derivation first specialises to the case
\theta=0
and then successively absorbs the strike correction and the Hermite correction into the BS price formula through two first-order Taylor approximations. While this approach avoids discarding terms as in the proof of Theorem
4
, it introduces additional higher-order and
mixed remainder terms. Consequently, the resulting formula (
68
) is expected to suffer from a larger loss of accuracy compared with (
65
). This loss of accuracy is also observed in the numerical tests reported in Appendix
C.2
.

Remark 6
.

In the mixed-kernel forward variance models, the squared VIX index proxy
\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}
in (
33
) is given by a linear combination of two log-normal random variables
\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P,j}^{2}
,
j\in\{1,2\}
. A natural approximation choice is to use a single log-normal random variable with the first two moments matched with the first two moments of
\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}
. Applying the VIX implied volatility expansion in (
30
) from Theorem
2
, originally derived for the single-kernel case, we then obtain an approximation for the implied volatility of VIX options under the mixed-kernel framework. Numerically, this approach performs significantly worse than approximations obtained from Theorem
4
. Further details are given in Appendix
B
.

(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\lambda,H,\xi_{0}^{T})
to
(\eta,H,\xi_{0}^{T})
. Let us consider the
n=4
, shortest maturities
(T_{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}
from the observation date, each associated with a market VIX futures price
(F_{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}
. We assume that the initial forward variance
\xi_{0}^{T_{i}}
is flat between
T_{i}
and
T_{i+1}
, and introduce a term structure in the parameters
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},H,\lambda)
making them maturity-dependent and piecewise constant in
[T_{i},T_{i+1}]

T_{i}
has the following two steps:

•

For a given choice of
(\eta_{1}^{T_{i}},\eta_{2}^{T_{i}},H,\lambda^{T_{i}})

is set as the unique solution to the
N

61
) of Proposition
3
.

•

With the value found for
\xi_{0}^{T_{i}}
in the first step, we compute the
L^{2}
distance between the model implied volatility and the market implied volatility, and perform a minimisation over
(\eta_{1}^{T_{i}},\eta_{2}^{T_{i}},H,\lambda^{T_{i}})
. We repeat the recursive procedure with updated
(\eta_{1}^{T_{i}},\eta_{2}^{T_{i}},H,\lambda^{T_{i}})
until the minimum is obtained.

In our calibration test, we choose to fix the kernel decay rate
H=0.1
, and assume that the implied volatilities have equal unitary weights for all strikes and maturities while evaluating the
L^{2}
distance used in the calibration procedure described above. The function we use to find the shortest
L^{2}
distance is
scipy.optimize.least_squares
from the
scipy
library. In addition, we calibrate to the same market data using the same calibration procedure under the standard Bergomi and mixed standard Bergomi models, and detail the results in Appendix

).

Appendix B
Moment-matching log-normal approximation

In the mixed-kernel models, as introduced in Section
3.1
, the squared VIX index
\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}
is approximated by a weighted sum of two log-normal random variables given as follows

\displaystyle\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\lambda_{i}\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P,i}^{2},

where
\ln(\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P,j}^{2})\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{P,j},\sigma_{P,j}^{2})
,
j\in\{1,2\}
, with
\mu_{P,j}
and
\sigma_{P,j}^{2}
defined in (
34
) and (
35
). Since
\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P,j}
for
j=1,2,
in the mixed standard and mixed rough Bergomi models are linearly dependent, it allows the proxy
\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}
to be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}=\lambda\mathrm{e}^{\mu_{P,1}+\sigma_{P,1}Z}+(1-\lambda)\mathrm{e}^{\mu_{P,2}+\sigma_{P,2}Z},\qquad Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1).

A natural approximation is to match the first two moments of
\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}
with those of a single log-normal random variable
Y=\mathrm{e}^{\mu_{Y}+\sigma_{Y}Z_{Y}}
. Letting
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
denote the first and second moments of
\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}
, respectively, we get

\displaystyle m_{1}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2}]=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\lambda_{j}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mu_{P,j}+\frac{\sigma_{P,j}^{2}}{2}},

\displaystyle m_{2}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}[(\mathrm{VIX}_{T,P}^{2})^{2}]=\lambda_{1}^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{2\mu_{P,1}+2\sigma_{P,1}^{2}}+\lambda_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{2\mu_{P,2}+2\sigma_{P,2}^{2}}+2\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mu_{P,1}+\mu_{P,2}+\frac{(\sigma_{P,1}+\sigma_{P,2})^{2}}{2}}.

Matching the moments yields

\displaystyle\sigma_{Y}=\sqrt{\ln\Bigl(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}^{2}}\Bigr)},\qquad\mu_{Y}=\ln(m_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{Y}^{2}.

Using the expressions for
\mu_{P}
and
\sigma_{P}
in (
17
) and (
18
), respectively, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{2(\ln\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]-\mu_{Y})}{\int_{0}^{T}(\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{u}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}K_{0}^{u}(t)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}{\int_{0}^{T}(\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{T}^{T+\Delta}\frac{\xi_{0}^{u}}{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{VIX}_{T}^{2}]}K_{0}^{u}(t))^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t},

where
K_{0}^{u}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa(u-t)}
and
K_{0}^{u}(t)=(u-t)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}
under the standard and rough Bergomi models, respectively. Since this equation cannot be solved explicitly for
\kappa
or
H
, even under the assumption of a flat initial forward variance curve, we determine the value of
\kappa
or
H
numerically by a root-finding procedure. We then compute the square root on the left-hand side directly to obtain the corresponding value of
\omega
or
\eta
. Once the parameters in the kernel function are determined, we compute the coefficients
(\gamma_{i})_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}
in (
1
) and apply the implied volatility expansion in (
30
), thereby obtaining an approximation of the VIX implied volatility in the mixed standard and mixed rough Bergomi models.

To check the accuracy of the VIX implied volatility expansion formula derived from this procedure (referred as
Expansion (match)
), we consider three different VIX maturities
T\in\{1,3,6\ \text{months}\}
, and assume the initial forward variance is constant with
\xi_{0}^{u}=\xi_{0}=0.24^{2}
, for all
u\in[T,T+\Delta]
, in the mixed standard and mixed rough Bergomi models. The other parameter values are given in Table
1
and
2
. From the results reported in Figure
12
–
15
, we can see that the VIX smiles obtained via this method (
Expansion (match)
) are almost flat and inaccurate, with signed relative errors exceeding 50% for Scenario 1, 2 and 4. Even in the best-performing case, Scenario 3, the relative errors are more than 10%. The main issue in this approach is that the second moment
m_{2}
is disproportionately large compared to the squared first moment
m_{1}^{2}
, which forces an excessively high matched log-variance and leads to a distorted moment-matched approximation.
