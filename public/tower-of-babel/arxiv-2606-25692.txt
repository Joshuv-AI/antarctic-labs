The weighted L 2 L^{2} -Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities for the curl-free vector fields and second order derivatives: The sharp constants and stability estimates.
Anh Tuan Duong, Van Hoang Nguyen

Abstract
In this paper, we study the weighted L 2 L^{2} -Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities for curl-free vector fields and second order derivatives. Firstly, we prove a family of the sharp weighted second order L 2 L^{2} -Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities that complements the results in [ C. Cazacu, J. Flynn and N. Lam, Calc. Var. Partial Differential Equations 62 (2023), no. 4, Paper No. 118, 26 pp. ] and [ A. T. Duong and V. H. Nguyen, On the sharp second order Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality. Ann. Fenn. Math., 50(1):275–286, 2025 ]. Secondly, we establish a stability version of the sharp weighted L 2 L^{2} -Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities for curl-free vector fields proved by Cazacu, Flynn and Lam. Finally, we prove a stability estimate for the sharp weighted second order L 2 L^{2} -Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities established in this paper. Our approach is based on the spherical harmonic decomposition method, the one dimensional integral inequalities and their improvements.

Introduction

Let us start by recalling the first order interpolation inequalities introduced by Caffarelli, Kohn and Nirenberg in
1stCKN
known as the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (CKN for short) inequalities.

Theorem A. (CKN inequalities)
Let
n\geq 1
and let
p,q,r,\alpha,\beta,\delta
and
\sigma
be real numbers satisfying

p,q\geq 1,\qquad r>0,\qquad\delta\in[0,1],

and

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\alpha}{n}>0,\quad\frac{1}{q}+\frac{\beta}{n}>0,\quad\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\gamma}{n}>0,

where
\gamma=\delta\sigma+(1-\delta)\beta
. Then there exists
C>0
such that the inequality

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{r}|x|^{r\gamma}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq C\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{p}|x|^{\alpha p}dx\Big)^{\frac{\delta}{p}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{q}|x|^{\beta q}dx\Big)^{\frac{1-\delta}{q}}

(1.1)

holds for any function
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if the following relations occur

\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\gamma}{n}=\delta\Big(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\alpha-1}{n}\Big)+(1-\delta)\Big(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{\beta}{n}\Big),

\alpha-\sigma\geq 0\qquad\text{ if }\qquad a>0,

and

\alpha-\sigma\leq 1\qquad\text{ if }a>0\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\alpha-1}{n}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\gamma}{n}.

Furthermore, on any compact set in the space of parameters, the constant
C
above is bounded. Interestingly, the CKN inequalities (
1.1
) contain many well-known and important inequalities in analysis such as Sobolev inequalities, Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities, Hardy inequalities, Hardy-Sobolev inequalities, Nash’s inequalities, Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle, etc. as special cases. The CKN inequalities play the important roles and have many applications in analysis and theory of partial differential equations (see, e.g,
CKNappl
). They have been extensively studied by many mathematicians, especially on the questions about the sharp constant and the classification of extremal functions for the CKN inequalities. In this research direction, we can list some celebrated results of Aubin and Talenti
Aubin
;
Talenti

;

for the sharp Sobolev inequalities, the sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities, and the sharp Nash’s inequalities, respectively. The interest reader may consult the papers

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2b}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx\geq C(n,a,b)^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\right)^{2},\quad u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}).

(1.2)

The sharp constant
C(n,a,b)
and the extremal functions in (
1.2
) are explicitly computed and depend on the parameter regions of
a,b
as follows

\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}_{1}:=\{(a,b)\,|\,a+1>b,a\leq(n-2)/2\}\\
\mathcal{A}_{2}:=\{(a,b)\,|\,a+1<b,a\geq(n-2)/2\}\\
\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}\\
\mathcal{B}_{1}:=\{(a,b)\,|\,a+1<b,a\leq(n-2)/2\}\\
\mathcal{B}_{2}:=\{(a,b)\,|\,a+1>b,a\geq(n-2)/2\}\\
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{1}\cup\mathcal{B}_{2}.\end{cases}

More precisely, it was shown in
CatrinaCosta
that

\bullet
If
(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}
, then the sharp constant
C(n,a,b)=|n-(a+b+1)|/2
and the extremal functions are given by

u(x)=c\exp\Big(-\lambda\frac{|x|^{1+a-b}}{1+a-b}\Big),

where
c,\lambda
are constants with
\lambda(1+a-b)>0
.

\bullet
If
(a,b)\in\mathcal{B}

C(n,a,b)=|n-(3a-b+3)|/2
and the extremal functions are given by

u(x)=c|x|^{2(a+1)-n}\exp\Big(-\lambda\frac{|x|^{1+a-b}}{1+a-b}\Big)

where
c,\lambda
are constants with
\lambda(1+a-b)>0
.

In the case
1+a=b
, the CKN inequality (
1.2

C(n,a,a+1)=|n-2a-2|/2
. In this case, the sharp constant
C(n,a,a+1)
is not attained by non-zero functions. The inequality (
1.2
) was first proved by Costa in
Costa
for a particular range of parameters by using the
e

method, and then was established by Catrina and Costa in
CatrinaCosta
for full range of parameters by using the spherical harmonic decomposition method and the Kelvin transformation. A simple and direct proof of (
1.2
) was given in
CaFlyLa
. Moreover, in
CaFlyLa
, the authors obtained a stronger version of (
1.2
) in which the full gradient
\nabla u
is replaced by the radial derivative
\nabla u\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}
.

It is remarkable that in the case
a=0
and
b=-1
, the CKN inequality (
1.2
) is exactly the famous Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle (see
Heisenberg
;
Weyl
), which asserts that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}|x|^{2}dx\geq\frac{n^{2}}{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{2},\quad u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(1.3)

The constant
n^{2}/4
in (
1.3
) is sharp and is attained by Gaussian functions
u(x)=ce^{-\lambda|x|^{2}}
with
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma>0
. Motivated by questions and applications in hydrodynamics and harmonic analysis, Maz’ya in
(
Mazya
, Section
3.9
)
raised the question on the sharp constant in (
1.3
) when the scalar function
u
is replaced by a divergence-free vector field
U
, here a vector field
U=(U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{n}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called divergence-free if

\text{\rm div}U=\partial_{1}U_{1}+\partial_{2}U_{2}+\cdots+\partial_{n}U_{n}=0.

This question was solved by Cazacu, Flynn and Lam (see
CFL22
) in two dimensional case, i.e,
n=2
. More precisely, in
CFL22
the authors established the following inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}|x|^{2}dx\geq\frac{(n+2)^{2}}{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{2},\quad u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(1.4)

The constant
(n+2)^{2}/4
is sharp and is achieved by Gaussian functions
u(x)=ce^{-\lambda|x|^{2}}
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>0
. A vector field
U
is curl-free if
U=\nabla u
for some scalar function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, the inequality (
1.4
) is equivalent to following inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla U|^{2}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|U|^{2}|x|^{2}dx\geq\frac{(n+2)^{2}}{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|U|^{2}dx\right)^{2}

(1.5)

for any curl-free vector field
U\in(C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{n}
. The constant
(n+2)^{2}/4
is again the best possible and it is attained by the curl-free vector fields of the form
U(x)=cxe^{-\lambda|x|^{2}}
with
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>0
. The inequality (
1.5
) provides an uncertainty principle of Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl type for curl-free vector fields. By an isometrically isomorphic between divergence-free vector fields and curl-free vector fields in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(in fact, if
U
is a divergence-free vector field in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
then
U(x)=(-\partial_{x_{2}}u,\partial_{x_{1}}u)
for some scalar function
u
), the inequality (
1.5
) is equivalent to the following inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla U|^{2}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|U|^{2}|x|^{2}dx\geq 4\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|U|^{2}dx\right)^{2}

(1.6)

for any divergence-free vector fields
U\in(C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))^{2}
. The constant
4
is sharp and is attained by the vector fields of the form
U(x)=c(-x_{2},x_{1})e^{-\lambda|x|^{2}}
, with
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>0
. The inequality (
1.6
) answers affirmatively the question of Maz’ya in the case
n=2
. Recently, this question was completely solved by Hamamoto
HamaMaz
(see also
Hamasimple
for a simpler proof). In fact, by using the poloidal-toroidal decomposition for divergence-free vector fields and an one-dimensional variational problem, he proved that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla U|^{2}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|U|^{2}|x|^{2}dx\geq\frac{1}{4}(\sqrt{n^{2}-4(n-3)}+2)^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|U|^{2}dx\right)^{2}

(1.7)

for any divergence-free vector field
U\in(C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{n}
. The constant
(\sqrt{n^{2}-4(n-3)}+2)^{2}/4
is sharp in (
1.7
) and is achieved by some divergence-free vector fields.

In
CFL23
, Cazacu, Flynn and Lam extended the inequality (
1.5
) to the weighted case and proved a family of sharp CKN inequalities for curl-free vector fields. In order to state their results, let us define
X_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, to be the set of vector fields
U\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})^{n}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla U|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx<\infty,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|U|^{2}}{|x|^{2b}}dx<\infty,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|U|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx<\infty,

\lim_{|x|\to 0,\infty}|x|^{-1+\frac{n}{2}-a}U(x)=0

and

\lim_{|x|\to 0,\infty}|x|^{-b+\frac{n}{2}}U(x)=0.

Let
n\geq 1
and
a,b
be real numbers such that
(n-2a)^{2}>4(n+1)
, it was proved by Cazacu, Flynn and Lam in
CFL23
that for any curl-free vector field
U\in X_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla U|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|U|^{2}}{|x|^{2b}}dx\geq C_{1}(n,a,b)^{2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|U|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{2}.

(1.8)

The sharp constant
C_{1}(n,a,b)
and extremal vector fields
U
are fully described and depend on the parameters
a
and
b
. More precisely, they proved for
n\geq 1
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
(n-2a)^{2}\geq 4(n+1)
that if
a-b+1>0
then

C_{1}(n,a,b)=\frac{a-b+1+\sqrt{(n-2a-2)^{2}+4(n-1)}}{2},

and it is achieved by the curl-free vector fields

U(x)=c|x|^{-\frac{n-2a-\sqrt{(n-2a-2)^{2}+4(n-1)}}{2}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{a-b+1}|x|^{a-b+1}}x,\quad c\in\mathbb{R},\,\lambda>0.

In the case
a-b+1<0
, the sharp constant
C_{1}(n,a,b)
is given by

C_{1}(n,a,b)=-\frac{a-b+1-\sqrt{(n-2a-2)^{2}+4(n-1)}}{2}.

and is attained by the curl-free vectors fields

U(x)=c|x|^{-\frac{n-2a+\sqrt{(n-2a-2)^{2}+4(n-1)}}{2}}e^{\frac{\lambda}{a-b+1}|x|^{a-b+1}}x,\quad c\in\mathbb{R},\,\lambda>0.

The inequality (
1.8
) becomes the sharp weighted Hardy inequality for curl-free vector fields of Hamamoto and Takahashi
HamaTaka1
by letting

(see also
HamaTaka2
for another proof). It also contains the weighted Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle for curl-free vector fields if
b=-a-1
,
a>-1
, and the weighted Hydrogen uncertainty principle if
b=-a
,

. In particular, when
a=0
and

, the Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle and the Hydrogen uncertainty principle for curl-free vector fields were proved in
CFL22
.

Let
u
be the scalar potential of
U
, i.e,
U=\nabla u
. Then, the inequality (
1.8
) is equivalent to the following inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2b}}dx\geq C_{1}(n,a,b)^{2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{2}

for any function
u
with
\nabla u\in X_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Furthermore, the equality holds if

\nabla u(x)=c|x|^{-\frac{n-2a-\sqrt{(n-2a-2)^{2}+4(n-1)}}{2}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{a-b+1}|x|^{a-b+1}}x,\quad c\in\mathbb{R},\,\lambda>0

in the case
a-b+1>0
, and if

\nabla u(x)=c|x|^{-\frac{n-2a+\sqrt{(n-2a-2)^{2}+4(n-1)}}{2}}e^{\frac{\lambda}{a-b+1}|x|^{a-b+1}}x,\quad c\in\mathbb{R},\,\lambda>0

in the case
a-b+1<0
.

As mentioned in
CFL23
, it is not true in general that for
U=\nabla u

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla U|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx.

Hence, it is worth-while to prove a version of (
1.8

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx
instead of
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla U|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2b}}dx\geq C_{2}(n,a,b)^{2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{2}.

(1.9)

This inequality has been only proved in

a,b
:

. The sharp constant is given by

C_{2}(n,a,-a-1)=\frac{n+4a+2}{2},

which is attained by the functions of form

with

and

. In fact, in

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}|^{2}}{|x|^{2b}}dx

Our first goal of this paper is to prove (

and
b

Y_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is the set of all functions in

such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx<\infty,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2b}}dx<\infty,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx<\infty,

\lim_{|x|\to 0,\infty}|x|^{-1-a+\frac{n}{2}}\nabla u(x)=0

and

\lim_{|x|\to 0,\infty}|x|^{-b+\frac{n}{2}}\nabla u(x)=0.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
a,b
be real numbers such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2b}}dx\geq C_{2}(n,a,b)^{2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{2}

The constant

In the paper
BL1985
, Brézis and Lieb posed the question on the stability estimate for the sharp Sobolev inequality by asking that how close the function is to the set of extremals when the equality nearly occurs. This question can be seen as a quantitative version of the celebrated concentration-compactness principle of Lions for the Sobolev inequality

(1.12)

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}dx\right)\inf_{v\in E_{HUP}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u-v|^{2}dx\right)+C_{2}\inf_{v\in E_{HUP}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u-v|^{2}dx\right)^{2}

where
E_{HUP}
is the set of Gaussian functions, i.e,

E_{HUP}=\{ce^{-\alpha|x|^{2}}\,:\,\alpha>0,\,c\in\mathbb{R}\}.

In
Fathi
, Fathi gave a simple proof of the above estimate by using the Poincaré inequality for Gaussian type measures. Moreover, he obtained the explicit constants
C_{1}=\frac{1}{4}
and
C_{2}=\frac{1}{16}
which are not optimal. Later, Cazacu, Flynn, Lam and Lu in
CFLL24
show that the sharp values of these constants must be
C_{1}=n
and
C_{2}=1
. In addition, these authors in
CFLL24
also proved the stability estimates for the CKN inequalities (
1.2
) with parameters
a,b
satisfying the conditions

0\leq a\leq\frac{n-2}{2},\,\,b\leq\frac{na}{n-2},\,\,\text{ and }\,\,a+b+1=\frac{2nb}{n-2}.

The restricted constraints on
a
and
b
are necessary to apply the Poincaré inequality for the log concave measures after making the change of variables. These restrictions on
a
and
b
were removed recently in
DLLN26
, where Do, Lam, Lu and the second author established the sharp stability version of the CKN inequality (
1.2
) for full ranges of
a
and
b
.

Although the stability version of the
L^{2}-
CKN inequalities (
1.2
) was completely characterized, however there are very little results on the stability estimates for the
L^{2}-
CKN inequalities for the curl-free vector fields (
1.8
). In
DNCVPDE
, the authors in this paper proved the following stability estimate for the second order uncertainty principle established in
CFL22
, that is the inequality (
1.8
) with
a=0
and
b=-1
.

Theorem B.
For any
n\geq 2
, it holds for any non-zero function
u\in H^{2}_{0,-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}|x|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{n+2}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\geq\frac{1}{768}\inf_{v\in E_{HUP}}\left\{\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u-\nabla v|^{2}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx}\,:\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right\}.

(1.13)

To prove (
1
), we first established an improvement of the second order uncertainty principle for functions that are orthogonal to all radial functions. With the help of this improvement, if the left hand side of (
1
) is small, then the function
u
is close to an even function. Then, by employing the spectral analysis for Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator associated to Gaussian measure and Hermite polynomials, we obtained the stability result for even functions. The inequality (
1
) follows from these two ingredients. However, the constant
\frac{1}{768}
is far from being sharp. In
DLL26
, Do, Lam, Lu and Zhang gave another proof of the above result by using the spherical harmonic decomposition technique and Fourier transformation for radial functions. They also got a better estimate by showing that the above result holds with constant
\frac{1}{4}(\sqrt{n^{2}+4n-4}-n)
. Later, this constant was proved to be sharp by Huang and Ye in
HuangYe
.

Therefore, the second goal in this paper is to establish the stability version of the
L^{2}-
CKN inequalities for the curl-free vector fields (
1.8
) and for the second order derivative from Theorem
1.1
. To state our next main results, let us introduce some notations. For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}

the curl-free vector field

|x|^{-\frac{n}{2}+a-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(n-2a-2)^{2}+4(n-1)}}e^{\frac{|x|^{a-b+1}}{a-b+1}}x&\mbox{if $1-b+a<0$,}\end{cases}

Note that

) and

is the set of extremal curl-free vector fields. For a curl-free vector field

if

and

if

. Our second main result is a stability version of the

) and is stated as follows

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

and

such that

,

for some positive constant
C
depending only on
n,a,b
.

Remark that the constant

can be computed explicitly from the proof of Theorem

Corollary 1.4

Let

for any curl-free vector field

Let

Put

Let

Finally, let us give some comments on our approach to prove the main results in this paper. The inequality (
1.9
) in the particular case
b=-a-1
was proved in
DNAFM
by using the factorization method and in
CFL22
by using the
expanding-the square
method. These methods are usually used to prove the functional inequalities such as Hardy and CKN inequalities. However, it is difficult to apply these methods to the inequality (
1.9
) for general parameters
a,b
. Instead, our approach in this paper is to apply the spherical harmonic decomposition method to prove Theorem
1.1
(i.e, to prove the inequality (
1.9
) for general parameters
a
and
b
). It is well-known that the spherical harmonic decomposition method is a powerful mathematical tool in studying the functional inequalities, especially in the sharp forms and their stability versions (see, e.g,
TerZoAiM
;
DNCVPDE
;
DLL26
;
CFL22
and the references therein). It enables us to reduce the proof of (
1.10
) for general functions to that for radial functions (on each node). This makes the problem more flexible to handle. More precisely, by using this method, to prove Theorem
1.1
, it is enough to establish the following one-dimensional inequalities

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle(u_{k}^{\prime\prime})^{2}r^{n-2a-1}dr+(n-1)(1+2a)\int_{0}^{\infty}(u_{k}^{\prime})^{2}r^{n-2a-3}dr\Big)\int_{0}^{\infty}(u_{k}^{\prime})^{2}r^{n-2b-1}dr

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}(n,a,b)^{2}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(u_{k}^{\prime})^{2}r^{n-a-b-2}dr\Big)^{2}.

for any
k\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\Big(2\int_{0}^{\infty}(u_{k}^{\prime})^{2}r^{n-2a-3}dr+(c_{k}+2(1+a)(n-2a-4))\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{k}^{2}r^{n-2a-5}dr\Big)\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}(u_{k})^{2}r^{n-2a-3}dr

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}(n,a,b)\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{k}^{2}r^{n-a-b-4}dr\Big)^{2}

for any
k\geq 1
which are consequences of Lemma
2.2
and the one dimensional-Hardy inequality (
2.27
) (see Section
2
). In order to prove Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.7
, we follows the approach in
DNCVPDE
, where we established the stability version of the second order Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle. Employing the spherical harmonic decomposition method and Lemma
2.2
, we will prove that the sharp constants in the weighted
L^{2}-
CKN inequalities for curl-free vector fields and second order derivatives can be improved by larger constants when restricted to curl-free vector fields
U=\nabla u\in X_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
or to functions
u\in Y_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which are orthogonal to all radial functions (see Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
5.1
below). At this step, the conditions
(n-2a)^{2}>4(n+1)
and
(n-2a-2)^{2}>8(1+a)(1+2a)
play the important role. These improvements imply that if
\delta(U)
(or
\delta_{CKN}(u)
) is small then
U
(or
\nabla u
) is close to a curl-free vector field

(or
\nabla v
) in a weighted
L^{2}
space on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
v
is a radial function. Using Lemma
2.3
, we obtain the stability results for such curl-free vector field

(or function

). Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.7
directly follow from these two estimates.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section

, we prepare some ingredients which are used to prove our main results such as the spherical harmonic decomposition method, some integral identities, the one-dimension integral inequalities and their improvements. Section

is devoted to prove Theorem

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we prepare some ingredients for the proof of our main Theorems. Let
U\in X_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be a scalar potential of
U
, i.e,
\nabla u=U
. We then have

\lim_{|x|\to 0,\infty}|x|^{-1+\frac{n}{2}-a}\nabla u(x)=0

(2.1)

and

\lim_{|x|\to 0,\infty}|x|^{\frac{n}{2}-b}\nabla u(x)=0.

We first establish the following identity.

Lemma 2.1
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla U|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\Delta u)^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx-2a(n-2a-3)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2+2a}}dx

\displaystyle\qquad-4a(1+a)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Big(\nabla u\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}\Big)^{2}}{|x|^{2+2a}}dx.

(2.2)

Proof.

Notice that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla U|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx

where
\nabla^{2}u=(\partial_{ij}^{2}u)_{n\times n}
denotes the Hessian matrix of
u
and
|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(\partial^{2}_{ij}u)^{2}
. For
\epsilon<R
, we denote by
A_{\epsilon,R}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\epsilon\leq|x|\leq R\}
, and
\nu(x)
the outward normal vector to
\partial A_{\epsilon,R}
. Let
S
denote the
(n-1)
dimensional surface area measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Using an integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}\frac{|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}|\nabla\partial_{i}u|^{2}|x|^{-2a}dx

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(-\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}\partial_{i}u\Delta\partial_{i}u|x|^{-2a}dx+2a\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}\partial_{i}u\nabla\partial_{i}u\cdot x|x|^{-2a-2}dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}\partial_{i}u\nabla\partial_{i}u\cdot\nu(x)|x|^{-2a}dS\Big)

\displaystyle=-\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta u|x|^{-2a}dx+a\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}\nabla(|\nabla u|^{2})\cdot x|x|^{-2a-2}dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla^{2}u(\nu(x))|x|^{-2a}dS

\displaystyle=\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}(\Delta u)^{2}|x|^{-2a}dx-2a\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}\Delta u\nabla u\cdot x|x|^{-2a-2}dx

\displaystyle\qquad-a(n-2a-2)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2a+2}}dx-\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}\Delta u\,\nabla u\cdot\nu(x)|x|^{-2a}dS

\displaystyle\qquad+a\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}|\nabla u|^{2}x\cdot\nu(x)|x|^{-2a-2}dS+\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla^{2}u(\nu(x))|x|^{-2a}dS.

Using again an integration by parts, we also have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}&\Delta u\nabla u\cdot x|x|^{-2a-2}dx\\
&=-\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(\nabla u\cdot x|x|^{-2a-2})dx\\
&\qquad+\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}\nabla u\cdot\nu(x)\nabla u\cdot x|x|^{-2a-2}dS\\
&=-\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}x\cdot\nabla^{2}u(\nabla u)|x|^{-2a-2}dx-\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}|\nabla u|^{2}|x|^{-2a-2}dx\\
&\qquad+(2a+2)\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}(\nabla u\cdot x)^{2}|x|^{-2a-4}dx+\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}\nabla u\cdot\nu(x)\nabla u\cdot x|x|^{-2a-2}dS\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}\nabla(|\nabla u|^{2})\cdot x|x|^{-2a-2}dx-\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}|\nabla u|^{2}|x|^{-2a-2}dx\\
&\qquad+(2a+2)\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}(\nabla u\cdot x)^{2}|x|^{-2a-4}dx+\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}\nabla u\cdot\nu(x)\nabla u\cdot x|x|^{-2a-2}dS\\
&=\frac{n-2a-2}{2}\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}|\nabla u|^{2}|x|^{-2a-2}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}|\nabla u|^{2}x\cdot\nu(x)|x|^{-2a-2}dS\\
&\qquad-\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}|\nabla u|^{2}|x|^{-2a-2}dx+(2a+2)\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}(\nabla u\cdot x)^{2}|x|^{-2a-4}dx\\
&\qquad+\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}\nabla u\cdot\nu(x)\nabla u\cdot x|x|^{-2a-2}dS\\
&=\frac{n-2a-4}{2}\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2a+2}}dx+(2a+2)\int_{A_{\epsilon,R}}\frac{\Big(\nabla u\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}\Big)^{2}}{|x|^{2a+2}}dx\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}|\nabla u|^{2}x\cdot\nu(x)|x|^{-2a-2}dS+\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}\nabla u\cdot\nu(x)\nabla u\cdot x|x|^{-2a-2}dS.\end{split}

(2.4)

We next treat the integral on the boundary of
A_{\epsilon,R}
. Notice by using the spherical coordinate that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{\{|x|=r\}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dS\Big)r^{-2a}dr=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx<\infty.

Consequently, there exist two sequence
\{\epsilon_{i}\}_{i}
and
\{R_{i}\}_{i}
,
\epsilon_{i}\to 0^{+}
and
R_{i}\to\infty
as
i\to\infty
, such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\epsilon_{i}^{1-2a}\int_{\{|x|=\epsilon_{i}\}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dS=\lim_{i\to\infty}R_{i}^{1-2a}\int_{\{|x|=R_{i}\}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dS=0,

which is equivalent to

\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon_{i},R_{i}}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}|x|^{1-2a}dS=0.

(2.5)

On the other hand, from (
2.1
), we have

\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon_{i},R_{i}}}|\nabla u|^{2}|x|^{-1-2a}dS=0.

(2.6)

In addition,

\Big|\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon_{i},R_{i}}}\Delta u\,\nabla u\cdot\nu(x)|x|^{-2a}dx\Big|\leq\frac{n}{2}\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon_{i},R_{i}}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}|x|^{1-2a}dS+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon_{i},R_{i}}}|\nabla u|^{2}|x|^{-1-2a}dS,

\Big|\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla^{2}u(\nu(x))|x|^{-2a}dx\Big|\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon_{i},R_{i}}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}|x|^{1-2a}dS+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon_{i},R_{i}}}|\nabla u|^{2}|x|^{-1-2a}dS,

\Big|\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}|\nabla u|^{2}x\cdot\nu(x)|x|^{-2a-2}dx\Big|\leq\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}|\nabla u|^{2}|x|^{-2a-1}dS,

and

\Big|\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}\nabla u\cdot\nu(x)\nabla u\cdot x|x|^{-2a-2}dx\Big|\leq\int_{\partial A_{\epsilon,R}}|\nabla u|^{2}|x|^{-2a-1}dS.

It follows from these estimates and (
2.5
), (
2.6
) that all integrals on the boundary
\partial A_{\epsilon_{i},R_{i}}

As mentioned in the introduction, one of the main ingredients in our approach is the spherical harmonic decomposition method. In the following, we shall list some facts on this technique. For
n\geq 2
, and
k\geq 0
is an integer, we denote by
\mathcal{H}_{k}
the eigenspace of the spherical Laplace operator
-\Delta_{S^{n-1}}
on the sphere
S^{n-1}
with respect to the eigenvalue
c_{k}=k(n+k-2)
, that is
-\Delta_{S^{n-1}}\phi=c_{k}\phi
with
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
. For each
k
, let
\phi_{kl}
,
l=1,2,\ldots,\text{ dim }\mathcal{H}_{k}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{k}
in
L^{2}(S^{n-1})
with standard surface area measure on
S^{n-1}
, i.e,

\int_{S^{n-1}}\phi_{kl}(\omega)\phi_{kl^{\prime}}(\omega)d\omega=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $l=l^{\prime}$},\\
0&\mbox{if $l\not=l^{\prime}$}.\end{cases}

It is well-known that such the functions
\phi_{kl},k=0,1,2,\ldots
and
l=1,2,\dots,\text{ dim }\mathcal{H}_{k}
constitute an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(S^{n-1})
. A function

can be decomposed as

u(x)=u(r\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{\text{ dim }\mathcal{H}_{k}}u_{kl}(r)\phi_{kl}(\omega),\quad r=|x|,\,\omega=\frac{x}{|x|}.

(2.7)

In fact
u_{kl}(r)=\int_{S^{n-1}}u(r\omega)\phi_{kl}(\omega)d\omega\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is a radial function. In the sequel, for simplicity of the notations, we will write the decomposition (
2.7
) as

u(x)=u(r\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}u_{k}(r)\phi_{k}(\omega),

where
-\Delta_{S^{n-1}}\phi_{k}=c_{k}\phi_{k}
.

Suppose that

or

. As discussed above, we can decompose

It implies that

\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-V|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\,:\,V\in\mathcal{M}_{a,b}\right\}\leq 2\Big(\frac{1}{\kappa-1}+\frac{2}{1-b+a}\Big)\delta(\nabla u)=2C\delta(\nabla u),

(4.8)

with

C=\frac{1}{\kappa-1}+\frac{2}{1-b+a}.

We now show that the infimum above is attained by a curl-free vector field
V\in\mathcal{M}_{a,b}
if
\delta(\nabla u)<\frac{1}{16C}<\frac{\kappa-1}{2}.
Indeed, let
\varphi_{i}(x)=c_{i}\varphi(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}x)
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\lambda_{i}>0
,
i\geq 2
(
\varphi
is given in (
1.11
)) be a sequence of functions such that
V_{i}=\nabla\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{a,b}
and

\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-\nabla\varphi_{i}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx=\inf_{V\in\mathcal{M}_{a,b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-V|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx<\frac{2C}{16C}=\frac{1}{8}.

The triangle inequality implies that

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-\nabla\varphi_{i}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\Big|\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla\varphi_{i}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}-1\Big|.

Thus for
i
large enough, we get

\frac{1}{4}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla\varphi_{i}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\leq\frac{9}{4}.

A simple computation gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla\varphi_{i}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx=c_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{n}{2}+1+\frac{a+b+1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\varphi^{\prime}(|x|))^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx.

Thus, there are
D_{1},D_{2}>0
such that
D_{1}^{2}\leq c_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{n}{2}+1+\frac{a+b+1}{2}}\leq D_{2}^{2}
for
i
large enough. We also note that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-\nabla\varphi_{i}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\\
=1+c_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{n}{2}+1+\frac{a+b+1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\varphi^{\prime}(|x|))^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx-2c_{i}\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\nabla u(x)\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}\varphi^{\prime}(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|x|)}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx.

(4.9)

We next claim that
(\lambda_{i})
is bounded from above and below by positive constants. Indeed, suppose, up to extracting a subsequence, that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{i}=\infty.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R>0
small enough such that
\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx<\epsilon^{2}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\nabla u(x)\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}\varphi^{\prime}(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|x|)}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big|\leq\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|\nabla u||\varphi^{\prime}(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|x|)|}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx+\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|\nabla u|\varphi^{\prime}(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|x|)}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|\varphi^{\prime}(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|x|)|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\epsilon\Big(\int_{B_{R}}\frac{(|\varphi^{\prime}(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|x|)|)^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{i}^{\frac{-n+a+b+1}{4}}\Big(\int_{B_{R\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}^{c}}\frac{(\varphi^{\prime}(|x|))^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda_{i}^{\frac{-n+a+b+1}{4}}\epsilon\Big(\int_{B_{R\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}}\frac{(\varphi^{\prime}(|x|))^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which implies

\Big|c_{i}\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\nabla u(x)\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}\varphi^{\prime}(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|x|)}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big|\leq D_{2}\Big(\int_{B_{R\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}^{c}}\frac{(\varphi^{\prime}(|x|))^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+D_{2}\epsilon\Big(\int_{B_{R\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}}\frac{(\varphi^{\prime}(|x|))^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let
i\to\infty
, we obtain

\limsup_{i\to\infty}\Big|c_{i}\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\nabla u\cdot(\frac{x}{|x|})\varphi^{\prime}(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|x|)}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big|\leq D_{2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\varphi^{\prime}(|x|))^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we get

\limsup_{i\to\infty}\Big|c_{i}\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\nabla u\cdot(\frac{x}{|x|})\varphi^{\prime}(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|x|)}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big|=0.

This together with (
4.9
) implies

\frac{1}{8}\geq\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-\nabla\varphi_{i}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx>1,

which is impossible. Concerning the lower bound of
\lambda_{i}
, suppose, up to extracting a subsequence, that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{i}=0.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R>0
large enough such that
\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx<\epsilon^{2}
. By using the similar arguments as in the previous case, we also obtain

\limsup_{i\to\infty}\Big|c_{i}\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\nabla u\cdot(\frac{x}{|x|})\varphi^{\prime}(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|x|)}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big|=0.

This together with (
4.9
) implies

\frac{1}{8}\geq\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-\nabla\varphi_{i}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx>1,

which is again impossible. So the claim is proved. As a consequence, there are
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}>0
such that

|c_{i}|\leq a_{1},\qquad 0<a_{2}\leq\lambda_{i}\leq a_{3}

for any
i
. Extracting a subsequence, we can assume that
c_{i}\to c
and
\lambda_{i}\to\lambda\in[a_{2},a_{3}]
. Denote
\varphi_{0}=c\varphi(\lambda x)
and
V_{0}=\nabla\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{a,b}
. We then have
\nabla\varphi_{i}\to\nabla\varphi_{0}
in
L^{2}(|x|^{-\frac{a+b+1}{2}}dx)
and hence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-V_{0}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-\nabla\varphi_{0}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-\nabla\varphi_{i}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx

\displaystyle=\inf_{V\in\mathcal{M}_{a,b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-V|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx.

Thus, the infimum in (
4.8
) is attained.
Remark that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx=1,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-V_{0}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx=\inf_{V\in\mathcal{M}_{a,b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-V|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\leq\frac{1}{8}

then

. So we can choose a positive constant
\bar{a}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\bar{a}V_{0}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx=1

or, equivalently
\bar{a}=(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|V_{0}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. The triangle inequality follows that

\displaystyle\Big|\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|V_{0}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}-1\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|V_{0}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-V_{0}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\big(2C\delta(\nabla u)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where in the last inequality, we have used (
4.8
).
From this estimate and the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-\bar{a}V_{0}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-V_{0}+(1-\bar{a})V_{0}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-V_{0}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx+2(\bar{a}-1)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|V_{0}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\nabla u)+2\Big|\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}-1\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 8C\delta(\nabla u).

This gives

\delta(\nabla u)\geq\frac{1}{8C}\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-V|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx}\,:\,V\in\mathcal{M}_{a,b},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|V|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\right\}

when
\delta(\nabla u)<\frac{1}{16C}
. Hence, Theorem
1.3
is proved for
\delta(\nabla u)<\frac{1}{16C}
.

It remains to prove the theorem when
\delta(\nabla u)\geq\frac{1}{16C}
. Observing that

\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-V|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx}\,:\,V\in\mathcal{M}_{a,b},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|V|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\right\}\leq 4.

Then,

\delta(u)\geq\frac{1}{64C}\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-V|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx}\,:\,V\in\mathcal{M}_{a,b},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|V|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\right\}

as desired.
Thus, we finish the proof of Theorem
1.3
in the case

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
n\geq 2

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx=1
. Then,

\delta_{CKN}(u)\geq\delta_{CKN}(u_{r})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u_{r}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx+(\bar{\kappa}-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u_{o}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx,

where
\bar{\kappa}>1
is the constant from Lemma
5.1
. Consequently,

\frac{|\nabla u_{o}|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\leq\frac{1}{\bar{\kappa}-1}\delta_{CKN}(u)

and if
\delta_{CKN}(u)<\frac{\bar{\kappa}-1}{2}
then

\delta_{CKN}(u_{r})\leq 2\delta_{CKN}(u).

The proof of this lemma is similar to that of Lemma
4.2
, so we omit the details.

Next, we establish the stability estimate for radial functions in
Y_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as follows.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
a,b
be real numbers such that
n-2a-4\not=0
,
(n-2a-2)^{2}>8(1+a)(1+2a)
and
a-b+1>0
. Let
u\in Y_{a,b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a radial function such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx=1
. Then,

\delta_{CKN}(u)\geq(1-b+a)\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u-\nabla v|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx\,:\,v\in\mathcal{N}_{a,b}\right\}.

Proof.

As above, we may assume that

\lim_{r\to 0,\infty}r^{\frac{n}{2}-a-2}u(r)=0

thanks to
n-2a-4\not=0
(see Section
2
). Moreover, since
u
is a radial function, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{|x|^{2a}}dx=n\sigma_{n}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}(u^{\prime\prime})^{2}r^{n-2a-1}dr+(n-1)(1+2a)\int_{0}^{\infty}(u^{\prime})^{2}r^{n-2a-3}dr\Bigg),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2b}}dx=n\sigma_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}(u^{\prime})^{2}r^{n-2b-1}dr

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{a+b+1}}dx=n\sigma_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}(u^{\prime})^{2}r^{n-a-b-2}dr.

The desired estimate follows from the part (i) of Lemma
2.3
with
f=u_{k}^{\prime}
,
A=n-2a
,
B=n-2b
and
C=(n-1)(1+2a)
.
∎

With Lemma
5.2
and Theorem
5.3
at hand, it is sufficient to mimic the last part of the proof of Theorem
1.3
to finish the proof of Theorem
1.7
in the case
1-b+a>0
. The details are also omitted. ∎
