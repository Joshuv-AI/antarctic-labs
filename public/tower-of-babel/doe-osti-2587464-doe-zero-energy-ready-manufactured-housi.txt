 Summary

Manufactured homes offer American consumers an affordable option for decent single-family
detached housing. For working-class American families in many U.S. markets, manufactured
homes are the first step toward home ownership. They now make up 10% of all new homes
constructed in the United States, with higher percentages in the south and in rural communities.
To help encourage the production of homes that are more durable, healthy, efficient, and disaster
resistant, the U.S. Department of Energy is bringing its building science research to the
manufactured housing industry through DOE’s Zero Energy Ready Manufactured Home (ZER-
MH) program, which provides technical assistance and voluntary guidelines to manufactured
home builders. Homes built to these guidelines are better able to handle power outages and less
likely to experience moisture issues, offering a better product option for American families. This
higher quality is evidenced by energy modeling which shows homes manufactured to these
voluntary guidelines will typically use half the energy of manufactured homes built to the current
minimum requirements of the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD)’s
Manufactured Housing and Construction Safety Standard (MHCSS). These homes can also
reduce critical energy demand during the busiest hours of the day, typically late afternoon and
early evening in the summer when air conditioning demand is highest and mornings in the winter
when furnaces and heaters are heating up. Reducing electricity demand during these peak periods
when electricity rates are at their highest reduces costs for American families while freeing up
capacity on overburdened energy distribution networks. Builders participating in the DOE ZER-
MH program are eligible for a tax incentive via the 45L tax credit, which helps to offset the costs
of ZER-MH upgrades, enabling builders to offer these certified homes at no additional cost.
Together these factors enable manufactured homes to offer home buyers a housing option that is
both affordable to finance and affordable to operate, with lower monthly mortgage payments and
lower monthly energy bills.

In Fiscal Year 2022, DOE’s Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) entered into a cooperative
research and development agreement (CRADA) with Clayton Homes to provide technical
assistance to support the participation of Clayton and other manufacturers in DOE’s Zero Energy
Ready Manufactured Housing program (ZER-MH).

In 2023, Clayton partnered with DOE to become the first manufactured home company
committed to constructing its homes to the criteria of DOE’s ZER-MH program. Clayton is a
market leader in the manufactured housing industry and is well positioned to help address the
challenges of designing, producing, marketing, financing, and delivering more energy-efficient,
durable, healthy, and ruggedized zero energy ready manufactured homes. Between July 2023 and
December 2024, the vast majority of Clayton’s manufactured homes were built to the DOE ZER-
MH standard in 39 Clayton home building facilities located throughout the country.

 iii

During this pilot phase of the DOE ZER-MH program, DOE has continued to engage with
Clayton and other stakeholders to solicit feedback that may help improve the program and to
identify innovative methods, products, and training needs to help more members of the industry
participate in the DOE ZERH program to cost-effectively produce high-performance DOE ZER-
MH certified manufactured homes. This report serves to document the technical assistance
activities conducted by ORNL in this effort.

 Introduction

Beginning in Fiscal Year (FY) 2022, Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) provided technical
assistance to the manufactured housing industry on behalf of the U.S. Department of Energy
(DOE) to support the DOE Zero Energy Ready Manufactured Homes program (ZER-MH), a
pilot program encouraging manufactured housing builders to construct manufactured homes to
voluntary above-code standards for improved energy efficiency, durability, and health. Homes
built to the requirements of the DOE ZER-MH program will be significantly more energy
efficient than manufactured homes built to the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban
Development (HUD)’s Manufactured Housing and Construction Safety Standard (MHCSS, also
known as the “HUD code.”)

This report serves to document technical assistance activities conducted by ORNL for the period
from FY 2023 through 2024. These activities included coordinating and facilitating Subject
Matter Expert (SME) meetings to gather input from key stakeholders in various aspects of the
residential construction and manufactured housing industry with the purpose of providing input
to the DOE ZER-MH program. ORNL recruited some of the nations’ top residential building
scientists and stakeholders to participate in four meetings led by principal investigator Michael
Lubliner. Participants at the meetings included representatives from DOE and the manufactured
housing industry as well as SMEs in several facets of residential and manufactured home
construction. A list of key participants is provided in Table 1.

At the time this technical assistance began, the HUD-code building industry was facing many
unique challenges, including the persistence of inefficient building methods, high materials costs,
limited availability of capital, and the inability for consumers to find financing. ORNL technical
assistance activities resulted in production of the publication Smart Connected Manufactured
Housing Solutions through High Performance Design, by Lubliner, Eckman, and Hagerman
(ORNL/SPR-2022/2572). That report focused on leveraging utility resources and other utility
demand-side management (DSM) and distributed energy resource (DER) program partnerships.
This effort began prior to the start of the 45L tax credit builder incentives for DOE ZER-MH
certified homes. The report identifies community-scale opportunities for manufactured home
builders that concurrently balance utility and consumer needs. The report provides builders,
consumers, developers, and public and private stakeholders with new ideas to realize energy-
efficient, cost-effective, healthy, comfortable, affordable, and durable HUD-code homes. The
report offers ways to minimize the delivered costs of these homes through reduced construction
time frames and innovative delivery, installation, and setup, while reducing homeowners’
operational costs through the use of higher efficiency building envelopes, mechanical equipment,
and miscellaneous electric loads (MELS).

 Subject Matter Expert Meeting – Discussion and
 Recommendations

The ORNL SME meetings and technical support has contributed toward the current and future
potential of the DOE program, beyond the pilot improvements. The ORNL SME meeting
discussions focused on the following topic areas.

Quality Assurance/Quality Control (QA/QC)

The DOE ZER-MH criteria require testing in the home-building facility (off-site) and at the
home’s set-up location (on-site). Simple, lower cost QA/QC screening tools and protocols were
identified as a need in the SME meetings with The Energy Conservatory (TEC), Slipstream, and
other SMEs. TEC is working with Clayton to develop new testing equipment packages to help
support Clayton’s ZER-MH deployment efforts. Several factors were identified as contributing
to energy and cost savings: improved envelope air sealing and duct sealing at the factory and at
site set-up. All these factors also contribute to up-front HVAC cost savings because they
decrease heating and cooling loads, allowing mechanical equipment to be downsized and right
sized.

Hot Water

SME discussions were beneficial in helping Clayton understand and adopt new heat pump water
heater technology as well as HVAC energy interactions and plumbing design details to help
ensure their homes provide the best value to homebuyers.

Heating/Cooling

Discussions focused on using central forced-air systems installed at the factory and/or installed
on site. This requires clear HVAC installer and retailer training. Clear guidance on maintenance
and operations for homeowners and tenants and training programs for the retailer and factory
sales forces are also needed.
Discussions with the SMEs focused on the following topics related to HVAC equipment and
testing practices:
  ACCA Manual S and Manual D to size HVAC equipment and ducts for each home.
  In-factory production line QA/QC of the belly, attic, and duct leakage testing.
  QA/QC duct leakage tests after set up.
  QA/QC envelope leakage tests after set up.
  Transitioning from centrally ducted electric furnaces with air conditioners to centrally
 ducted heat pumps.

 5

  Installing ECOBEE ENERGY STAR-labeled connected thermostats in all homes.
  Homes in hot-humid climate zones are either “dehumidifier” ready or have dehumidifiers
 installed in the factory.

Ventilation, IAQ, Humidity

Discussions included climate-appropriate equipment design and controls with maintenance and
operations guidance for homeowners and training for retailer and factory sales forces. Other
SME discussions included the following topics:
  Whole-house supply and/or exhaust ventilation systems designed with air flow
 rate/control commissioning using testing tools per ASHRAE 62.2.
  Assessment of “more balanced” whole house supply and/or exhaust ventilation systems
 that seek to limit the magnitude and frequency of home depressurization in hot-humid
 climates.
  Upgrading homes to ENERGY STAR-labeled, quieter, higher flow range intermittent
 bathroom and kitchen exhaust fans with smart controls per ASHRAE 62.2
  Improved occupant and retailer education and training tools on indoor air quality to help
 occupants and retailers understand the importance of ventilation maintenance and
 operations. QR codes at the retailer/developer point of sale that link to education
 resources is one option.

Recommendations

The SME discussions included future potential areas for stakeholder RD&D consideration:
  Conduct RD&D focused on ZER-MH monitoring, verification, and evaluation (MVE) to
 identify future opportunities and challenges.
  Develop and refine a ZER-MH “constant improvement process” to help assess, identify,
 and reduce root causes of building science-related factory, transport, and/or set-up issues
 identified as part of the ZER-MH QA/QC factory and/or on-site set-up.
  Work with ECOBEE and utilities to provide data for analysis of run-time and electricity
 usage from the tens of thousands of deployed connected thermostats and other internet-
 of-things (IOT)-related technologies. (This was a suggestion made by Phillip Agee from
 Virginia Tech who has been involved with manufactured housing connected thermostats
 and RD&D.)
  Conduct occupant satisfaction/market research surveys, working with utility and DOE
 ZER-MVE stakeholders. For example, identify market drivers and energy-efficiency
 opportunities based on HVAC duct work location in future manufactured homes.

 6

  Conduct engineering and RD&D on improved duct thru-rim designs versus R6-R8
 crawlspace crossover ducts in vented crawlspaces, in multi-section homes.
  Identify energy and non-energy benefits from “plug-n-play” centrally and not centrally
 ducted split-system heat pumps installed at the factory.
  Use SME peer review to improve Clayton’s O&M manuals on HVAC, IAQ/ventilation,
 Heat Pump Water Heaters (HPWH), and plumbing to maximize “cradle to grave” energy
 and non-energy performance.
  Consider a Ventilation/IAQ pilot research study with Pacific Northwest National
 Laboratory and Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory targeting ZER-MH.
  Support RD&D with TEC, AirCyler™, Slipstream, and other SME resources to improve
 energy and non-energy benefits to ZER-MH stakeholders.
  Conduct evaluation research to help ZER-MH maximize energy and non-energy benefits,
 to consumers and IRA taxpayer-funded investments.

Other additional suggestions noted during SME discussions included the following:
  Explore opportunities to implement community-scale research projects with NEXT Step,
 PHAs, tribal housing stakeholders, and Clayton community developers.
  Support opportunities to replace older manufactured homes with new ZER-MH, when
 appropriate, as an alternative to conducting DOE Weatherization Assistance Program
 (WAP-WX), and/or home performance contractor or homeowner retrofits.
  Identify energy and non-energy benefits from solar shingles installed at the factory to
 reduce first costs of adding PV.

 Conclusions

Through four meetings conducted to gather input from SMEs and other stakeholder engagement
activities, ORNL has provided guidance for manufacturers that has helped members of the
manufactured housing industry to successfully participate in the DOE ZER-MH program and
bring to market higher performing manufactured homes. See Appendix A for slides presented at
these meetings.

The following key conclusions can be drawn from the SME meetings and technical assistance
provided to Clayton:
  Clayton has transformed typical HUD-code homes built to MHCSS Thermal Standards,
 to ENERGY STAR and now to the DOE ZER-MH pilot criteria. Clayton should support
 future RD&D with SMEs and other building science partners. Clayton should consider
 continuing to work with individual SMEs (teams) who can assist with future important
 ZER-MH RD&D that continues to help maximize energy and non-energy benefits.

  Homes built to DOE ZER-MH by Clayton can dramatically reduce both annual energy
 usage and energy peak load demands in all climate zones in the continental United States.
 Both energy and non-energy economic benefits will result from the thousands of Clayton
 ZER-MH homes built to date and those anticipated. These homes are affordable to
 purchase, operate, and maintain, offering consumers an economical choice in high-
 performance homes. ORNL recommends that Clayton continue discussions with DOE
 and stakeholders related to SME suggestions, using its own resources.

  Clayton has appreciated the technical assistance provided by ORNL and the SMEs
 through the ORNL CRADA. Guidance from the group was beneficial in helping the
 Clayton engineering team to understand and adopt new ZER-MH technology such as heat
 pump water heaters to help ensure homes provide the best value to homebuyers.
