Project Objective

 The goal of the Kokes award is to increase attendance at the conference by graduate
students and to recognize the excellent, well-rounded, and diverse graduate student community in
catalysis. The Kokes fund only supports students attending North American universities, and any
funds provided by DOE will only be used to support students attending institutions within the
United States. Support from various sponsors (e.g., NSF, DOE) is crucial to ensure that funds are
available for this program.

 Only students and have accepted abstracts to give an oral presentation or poster are eligible
for the Kokes award. The award covers the conference registration and 5 nights in the conference
hotel (Hyatt Regency), and thus helps subsidize a significant portion of the cost of attendance.
Students will be double-booked in each room, so that the total value of the award for NAM29 will
be $1,125 ($550 for student registration and $115 per night for five nights in dual occupancy
rooms). For comparison, the Kokes award was worth $933 at NAM28, held in Providence. With
our targeted funds (see Table 2, or $135,000 total projected), our goal is to provide 100-120
students with Kokes awards. Any students attending from Atlanta area universities will only
receive funds to cover the cost of registration, enabling even more students to attend the
conference. Beyond selecting just the top students for the Kokes award, we will work to ensure
that students from underrepresented groups are given preference. To further enhance the efforts,
additional advertising about the Kokes awards will be sent to faculty at Clark Atlanta University
and Morehouse College (local area HBCUs with chemistry programs), with the hope that several
of their undergraduate or graduate research students will attend the conference. If approved, all
DOE-derived Kokes award funds will be distributed to students who are U.S. Citizens.

Selection Process and Outcomes

 Review and selection of Kokes award winners was overseen by the Kokes chairs (D.
Hibbitts and D. Bruce). Overall, 256 students applied for Kokes awards to NAM29, 234 of which
(91%) were from the United States. Students applied from 88 different colleges and universities
from 38 different states (Alaska, Arizona, Hawaii, Idaho, Maine, Missouri, Nebraska, Nevada,
New Hampshire, South Dakota, Vermont, and West Virginia had no applicants).
 If advisors have multiple applicants from their groups, the advisor is asked to rank their
students (or to submit only one letter of recommendation), and generally only one award is given
per research group, to ensure that award winners come from a variety of research groups, rather
than concentrated from large research groups. Only applicants with accepted conference abstracts
(oral or poster presentations) and recommendation letters are circulated for review, which was 171
applicants for NAM29. Application packages include a student’s CV, presentation abstract, and
their advisor’s letter of recommendation, and reviewers evaluated these packages in the following
categories: academics, leadership, publication/presentation record, and strength of the advisor
letter of recommendation. Ultimately, 53 reviewers (from North American universities and
national laboratories) reviewed 10 applications, each, ensuring that all applicants had 3 reviews of
their application materials. Reviews follow a weighted rubric (Table 3), which most heavily
considers the advisor’s recommendation letter, followed by academic, publication/presentation
record, and leadership qualifications, and the least weighted category being the student’s year in
their studies (with a slight preference toward awarding students further in their PhD studies). The
average total score was 29 out of 45. Among the 171 circulated for review, 52 students were scored
but not considered further, either because they had previously won a Kokes award, or because
another student in their research group was ranked higher by the advisor and/or reviewer
recommendations. Of the remaining 119, 100 were awarded based solely on reviewer
recommendations, while the 19 with the lowest reviewer scores were evaluated based on reviewer
recommendations as well as the advisor’s academic rank with priority given to research groups
whose advisors were at the assistant (pre-tenure) rank. These 19 award decisions were also made
at a later date, once financial commitments were more certain. Ultimately, we were able to award
an additional 14 awards out of these 19 applicants, resulting in 114 awards out of 256 total
applications (45% acceptance rate).

 These awardees received some funds to support their travel to Atlanta, GA; a hotel room (two-
per room) for the duration of the conference; and complementary conference registration.
Awardees were present for the entire conference and had volunteering duties to help with
conference organization. For example, Kokes awardees helped with conference registration,
tracking session attendance, ticketing functions at the opening reception and closing awards
banquet, assisting with the poster session, and other administrative duties.
 Overall, funds from DOE enabled >20 additional Kokes Awardees to attend the conference,
sharing their exciting science and contributing to a successful NAM29 conference. The ongoing
support of sponsors such as DOE-BES for the Kokes Award is critical to maintaining U.S.
leadership in catalysis and to advancing the fundamental understanding of chemical
transformations that enables more selective, energy- and atom-efficient catalytic processes. Such
advances directly support DOE-BES priorities in sustainable feedstock conversion, carbon-neutral
chemistry, energy-efficient transformations, and the development of new catalytic approaches for
the production of fuels and chemicals essential to the global economy.
