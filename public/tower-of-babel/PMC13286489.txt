Reply: Safety Evaluation of the RibXcar Technique Using the Piezotome: An Analysis of Surgical Complications
Authors: Raúl M. Manzaneda Cipriani, Héctor Duran
Published: 2026

We appreciate the interest shown by Drs. Pérez Pachon and Hoyos Ariza1 in our article and welcome the opportunity to address several methodological inaccuracies presented in their correspondence.2 Scientific debate is essential for the advancement of surgical knowledge, particularly in emerging fields such as minimally invasive rib reshaping.3–5 Notably, the authors of the letter recently introduced the “Waistline Aesthetic Slimming by Puncture” (WASP) technique,6 which shares conceptual overlap with RibXcar. As relatively recent contributors to this field, clarification of several impressions presented in their letter is necessary to ensure that the discussion remains grounded in appropriate methodological considerations.

STUDY DESIGN, TITLE, AND SCOPE

The claim that surgeon surveys are unsuitable for evaluating procedural safety is not supported by established surgical methodology. Surveys of experienced practitioners are frequently used to characterize complication profiles and real-world practice patterns, particularly when emerging procedures have not yet reached the stage at which large prospective multicenter trials are feasible.7,8 Accordingly, the title of our article accurately reflects its design as a safety evaluation based on the aggregated experience of surgeons performing the technique.

This criticism is difficult to reconcile with the methodology of the WASP study itself, which relies on a retrospective analysis of 4 surgeons across 3 centers.6 Such a design is susceptible to the same selection and reporting biases attributed to our work. In addition, 96% of their cohort underwent simultaneous high-definition liposuction, making it difficult to isolate outcomes attributable specifically to rib reshaping.

COMPLICATION RATES AND INTERPRETATION

The interpretation of complication rates presented in the letter reflects a methodological conflation. The 2.65% rate reported in our article refers specifically to potentially life-threatening complications, such as pneumothorax and hemothorax. Pooling these events with subjective outcomes, such as prolonged postoperative pain, constitutes a misclassification inconsistent with standard surgical outcome frameworks.

We acknowledge a typographical transposition between Table 3 and the main text regarding pneumothorax and prolonged pain percentages and support publication of a corrigendum.2 Importantly, recalculation from the original survey data confirms that the combined incidence of pneumothorax and hemothorax remains consistent with the rate reported in the abstract.

AESTHETIC OUTCOMES

The absence of validated patient-reported outcome measures was explicitly acknowledged in our article. However, the WASP study relied on the global aesthetic improvement scale, originally validated for facial aesthetic procedures rather than skeletal body contouring, and these assessments were performed by operating surgeons rather than blinded evaluators.

CONTEXT

Scientific discussion benefits from transparency. Dr. Hoyos promotes the WASP technique through the Total Definer educational platform, and the first author of the letter serves as the scientific director of this organization. These affiliations provide relevant context when critiques are directed toward a competing technique.9

CONCLUSIONS

We support correcting the table transcription error through a corrigendum. Nevertheless, the central conclusion of our study remains unchanged: RibXcar, when performed by trained surgeons using a piezotome and ultrasound guidance, demonstrates a low incidence of life-threatening complications. More robust study designs are needed to evaluate outcomes and complications and to validate safety through critical scientific assessment by surgeons without commercial bias.
