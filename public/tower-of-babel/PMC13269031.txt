CHA2DS2-VASc and CHA2DS2-VA Scores as Predictors of Short- and Long-Term Mortality Following Transvenous Lead Extraction
Authors: Celina Wojciechowska, Łukasz Tułecki, Wojciech Jacheć, Dorota Nowosielecka, Paweł Wojciechowski, Anna Polewczyk, Andrzej Kutarski
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMultimorbidity significantly affects prognosis in patients after transvenous lead extraction (TLE). The CHA2DS2-VASc and CHA2DS2-VA scores, widely used for stroke risk stratification in atrial fibrillation, include clinical factors that are also associated with long-term mortality.PurposeTo evaluate the usefulness of the CHA2DS2-VASc and CHA2DS2-VA scores in predicting short- and long-term mortality in patients following non-laser TLE.MethodsThis retrospective study included 3822 patients who underwent non-laser TLE between March 2006 and April 2023 at high-volume centres. The median follow-up was 1848 days (Q1-Q3: 815–3146 days). Patients were stratified into two groups according to CHA2DS2-VASc score (<3 vs. ≥3). 30-day, 1-year, and 3-year, and overall follow-up mortality were assessed. Cox proportional hazards models were used to identify independent predictors of death during short and long follow-up.ResultsPatients with CHA2DS2-VASc ≥3 had significantly higher mortality at 30 days 1 year, 3 years, and during overall follow-up compared with those scoring <3. A cut-off ≥3 showed 80.6% sensitivity and 62.3% specificity for predicting 3-year mortality. Mortality increased progressively with higher CHA2DS2-VASc scores (Spearman’s r = 0.983, p<0.001). The multivariate Cox regression demonstrated that number of points on CHA2DS2-VASc and CHA2DS2-VA scores were independent predictors long-term mortality, alongside Charlson comorbidity index, atrial fibrillation, infective TLE indications, and renal dysfunction. Each 1-point increase in the CHA2DS2VA score was associated with an 11.7% increase in the risk of death. In sex-specific analysis, the risk increased by 33.0% per point in females (HR 1.330; p<0.001) and by 20.6% in males (HR 1.206; p<0.001).ConclusionCHA2DS2-VASc and CHA2DS2-VA score may be a useful tool for predicting the risk of death during long-term observation of patients after TLE. Incorporation of this score into clinical decision-making may improve risk stratification and support individualized management of patients undergoing TLE.Keywords: lead extraction, mortality risk, comorbidity, CHA2DS2-VASc score, cardiac implantable electronic device, risk stratification

Introduction

In recent years, the number of patients qualified for cardiac implantable electronic device (CIED) removal has been increasing because of the constantly increasing number of implantations and simultaneously growing dysfunction and infection complications.1 This trend is also influenced by increasing life expectancy of patients. Lead extraction is used to treat infectious and non-infectious complications associated with CIED. Initially, open heart surgery was more commonly used to remove leads, which is a more invasive and expensive method. Endovascular removal has evolved over the past three decades, and is now considered the leading method in experienced centres with appropriate team presentation when lead extraction is indicated. The latest 2020 European Society of Cardiology guidelines on the assessment of the cardiovascular system and management of patients undergoing non-cardiac surgery broadly discuss the management of the patient before intervention, taking into account the risks of the procedure, including the urgency of its performance and the patient’s condition. Surgical risk (30-day risk of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, or stroke), depending on the type of surgery or intervention without considering the patient’s comorbidities, was divided into 3 groups: low (<1%), intermediate (1–5%), and high (>5%). However, the Transvenous Lead Extraction procedure was not performed in any of these group.2 Until now, few reports revealed that TLE is procedure with intermediate risk (1.4–4.1% at 30 days, with in-hospital mortality approximately 2.5–3.3%).3–7 The endovascular interventions are less invasive compared to median sternotomy, but apart from the risk associated with the procedure, it is important to remember the risk of the patient who usually suffers from several comorbidities. The patient-related risk depends on age, diagnosed cardiovascular disease or the presence of cardiovascular risk factors (eg arterial hypertension, diabetes)8 and comorbidities.9

Currently, there are several surgical perioperative risk assessment scales developed on the basis of multivariate analyses that have been validated over the last decade.10–12 Procedure-related mortality directly attributable to TLE in experienced centers remains relatively low and is generally reported to be below 1%.1 However, short-term mortality, including peri-procedural and in-hospital mortality, and especially long-term mortality, is significantly higher and is largely due to the overall burden of comorbidities in the patient population and the indication for extraction, rather than to procedural complications themselves. Similarly, several risk calculators for TLE have been published.13–15 In recent years, several models and calculators for predicting procedural, short- and long-term risk have been published to estimate outcomes in patients undergoing TLE. These models typically incorporate a variety of clinical and procedural variables. The most commonly considered factors include age, renal dysfunction, heart failure severity, anemia, infection as an indication for extraction, and procedural characteristics such as lead dwell time or the number of extracted leads.7,15–17 Large registry data, including the ESC-EHRA ELECTRa registry, have further highlighted the importance of both patient-related and procedural factors in predicting adverse outcomes after TLE.13,18 In addition, the usefulness of other scales such as the Clinical Frailty Scale (CFS)5–7 in predicting mortality in the TLE cohort has been indicated.19

Despite their predictive value, many currently available risk models focus on short-term survival and require numerous clinical and procedural parameters, making them relatively complex and potentially limiting their routine use in daily clinical practice. Therefore, simple and widely recognized clinical scores may represent a practical alternative for risk stratification in patients undergoing TLE.

One of the most frequently used scores by clinicians in daily practice was the CHA2DS2-VASc score (congestive heart failure, hypertension, age≥75 years (2 points), diabetes mellitus, prior stroke or TIA or thromboembolism (2 points), vascular disease, age 65–74 years, sex category). The recent guidelines recommended using the CHA2DS2-VA score (Congestive heart failure, hypertension, age ≥75 years (2 points), diabetes mellitus, prior stroke/transient ischaemic attack/arterial thromboembolism (2 points), vascular disease, age 65–74 years, without sex category) to indicate an elevated thrombosis risk before introducing anticoagulant treatment in patients with atrial fibrillation for stroke prevention.20 Importantly, most of the variables included in these scores are also well-recognized predictors of adverse outcomes in cardiovascular populations. Consequently, the CHA2DS2-VASc score may potentially serve as a convenient and easily accessible tool for mortality risk stratification in patients undergoing TLE.

The aim of this study was to evaluate the usefulness of the CHA2DS2-VASc and CHA2DS2-VA scores for predicting mortality risk in patients undergoing TLE, with particular emphasis on their prognostic value in 30-days post-procedural and long-term outcomes.

Methods

Study Population

This was retrospective cohort study included 3822 patients who underwent non-laser transvenous lead extraction (TLE) between March 2006 and April 2023. The study population is derived from the TLE database of the reference center, which was previously described.21 The median follow-up for the entire cohort was 1848 days (Q1-Q3: 815–3146 days). Patient’s clinical characteristics, CIED system data, pacing history, procedural efficacy, and mortality at 30 days, 1 year, 3 years, and during the entire follow-up period were analyzed. Data were evaluated for the entire cohort and stratified by sex.

Lead Extraction Procedures Definitions

TLE indications, procedure effectiveness, and complications were estimated according to recent TLE recommendations (2017 Heart Rhythm Society (HRS) consensus and 2018 European Heart Rhythm Association (EHRA) guidelines).22,23 The efficacy of TLE was expressed as the percentage of procedural success which was defined as the removal of all targeted leads and lead material from the vascular space in the absence of any permanently disabling complications or procedure-related deaths. TLE complications were defined as major complications if they were life threatening, led to significant or permanent disability or death, or required surgical intervention. Patient characteristics and comorbidities were obtained from the medical records. Some comorbidities were analysed separately and were also included in the Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) calculation.24 Atrial fibrillation diagnosis included patients with paroxysmal, persistent, or permanent atrial fibrillation. The most recent left ventricle ejection fraction (LVEF) values pre-TLE were used, and creatinine concentration was assessed during hospitalisation.

Dataset and Statistical Methods

Spearman correlation was used to assess the association between the CHA2DS2-VASc score and mortality at 30 days, 1 year, 3 years, and during the entire follow-up period.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to determine the optimal cut-off value of the CHA2DS2-VASc score for mortality prediction. ROC analysis identified ≥3 points as the optimal threshold providing the best balance of sensitivity and specificity for mortality prediction. Therefore, the CHA2DS2-VASc score was dichotomized (<3 and ≥3; groups 1 and 2, respectively) for Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, and differences between survival curves were assessed using the Log rank test.

Differences between groups were assessed using the chi-square test with Yates’ correction for categorical variables and the Mann–Whitney U-test for continuous variables, as appropriate.

To determine the impact of clinical and CIED-related variables on survival after TLE, univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed. Regression analyses were conducted for the entire cohort and separately for female and male subgroups.

Two multivariable regression models were constructed for the entire cohort and for the female subgroup. The first model included the CHA2DS2-VASc score, whereas the second model included the CHA2DS2-VA score, with sex analyzed as an independent variable. Variables with p<0.05 in univariable analysis were included in the multivariable model.

Statistical significance was defined as p<0.05. Statistical analyses were performed using Statistica version 13.3 (TIBCO Software Inc).

Approval of the Bioethics Committee

All patients provided written informed consent for the TLE procedures and for use of anonymized data from their medical records, approved by the Bioethics Committee at the Regional Chamber of Physicians in Lublin no. 288/2018/KB/VII. This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki.

Kaplan–Meier Analysis

The impact of the CHA2DS2-VASc score on short- and long-term mortality was illustrated using Kaplan–Meier curves. The CHA2DS2-VASc score of ≥3 was associated with worse prognosis. Additionally, 7-day mortality including perioperative deaths, was 0.61%, 6 deaths (0.41%) in group 1 and 17 deaths (0.72%) in group 2 (Figure 2).

Kaplan–Meier curves, comparison of the mortality between groups 1 and 2, log rank P<0.001, HR = 1.434; 95% CI (1.391–1.479), P<0.001.

Regardless of the follow-up period, mortality was significantly higher in group 2. In the short-term follow-up (30-day), mortality in group 2 was 2.9-fold higher, whereas during the entire follow-up period it was 2.3-fold higher.

Thirty-Day Follow-Up in the Entire Study Population

In the univariable analysis, both the CHA2DS2-VASc and CHA2DS2-VA score were statistically significant predictors of mortality. Similarly, the HAS-BLED and CCI scores were significantly associated with 30-day mortality. Additionally, age, atrial fibrillation, hemoglobin and creatinine concentration, the need for anticoagulation therapy, CRT-P, lead-related infective endocarditis, and major complications were significant predictors of mortality. Among the components of the CHA2DS2-VASc score, female sex, hypertension, and vascular disease were not correlated with short-term mortality.

In multivariable analysis, independent predictors of 30-day mortality included higher NYHA functional class, lead-related infective endocarditis, and major procedural complications. Conversely, higher LVEF and higher haemoglobin concentration were associated with improved survival. In the multivariable regression analysis of Model 2 based on the CHA2DS2-VA score, similar hazard ratios were observed as in Model 1. Female sex, analysed as an independent variable, was not associated with 30-day mortality Table 4.

Long-Term Follow-Up in the Entire Study Population

In the univariable analysis, most clinical variables were associated with long-term mortality, whereas procedural parameters, including major complications and complete clinical success, were not significant predictors. In Model 1, a higher CHA2DS2-VASc score remained an independent predictor of long-term mortality. Each additional point in CHA2DS2-VASc score was independently associated with 11% increase in mortality rate (HR 1.110; p=0.003) Other independent predictors of worse long-term prognosis included higher CCI, higher NYHA functional class, the presence of atrial fibrillation, higher creatinine concentration, the need for chronic anticoagulation therapy, and the presence of CRT systems. Infectious indications for TLE, including isolated pocket infection and lead-related infective endocarditis, were also associated with increased mortality. In contrast, higher LVEF and higher haemoglobin concentration were associated with better long-term survival. In Model 2 based on the CHA2DS2-VA score, similar associations were observed. Each additional point in the CHA2DS2-VA score was independently associated with a 17% higher risk of death (HR 1.174; p<0.001). In this model, female sex, analysed as a separate variable, was associated with better survival after TLE (Table 5).

Discussion

The main conclusion from our study is that both CHA2DS2-VASc and CHA2DS2-VA scores were independent prognostic factors of long-term mortality in patients after TLE during a median follow-up of 1848 days (Q1-Q3: 815–3146 days). In addition, higher NYHA class and reduced LVEF were consistently associated with worse survival. The female sex was associated with a lower long-term mortality in the overall cohort. In contrast, neither score predicted 30-day perioperative mortality, which was primarily influenced by major TLE complications and infectious indications.

Short- and Long-Term Mortality

The baseline characteristics of our cohort regarding age, sex, and comorbidities were similar to those of previous large TLE studies. In our study, the distribution of TLE indications was characterized by a lower rate of local infection and comparable systemic infection.1,5,17,19,25–27 The 30-day mortality rate (1.87%) confirms that TLE is an intermediate-risk procedure. Long-term all-cause mortality in our cohort was 39.4% at median 5.1 years follow-up. Data on longer follow-up are limited, Deharo JC et al25 reported 35.4% all-cause 5-year mortality in infected patients and 27.0% in controls (median follow-up of 25 months), Narducci M. et al reported, global all-cause mortality 41% at median follow-up of 4.5 years.26 Arabia G. described higher number of all-cause deaths 30.2% in the infection group compared to 9.5% in the non-infection group in median 6.5-year follow-up.27

Risk Factors for 30-Day Mortality

Systemic CIED infection emerged as a strong predictor of short-term mortality similar to prior studies.3,5,19 Other unfavorable predictors included a higher NYHA class, reduced LVEF, and lower hemoglobin concentration. A similar unfavorable trend for lower haemoglobin level was described by Deckx et al5 Among CHA2DS2-VASc/VA components, heart failure (reflected by a higher NYHA class and reduced LVEF) remained the main driver of early mortality risk. Renal failure has been found to be an independent predictor of mortality.3,25 In our study, higher creatinine concentrations showed a trend towards statistical significance.

Risk Factors for Long-Term Mortality

To our knowledge, this is the first study evaluating CHA2DS2-VASc and CHA2DS2-VA scores as predictors of long-term outcomes post-TLE. In addition to these scores, higher CCI, HAS-BLED (model 1), LRIE, renal failure, atrial fibrillation, and CRT before TLE were independently associated with worse prognosis. Azari et al did not prove that CCI is an independent risk factor for long-term mortality; however, they demonstrated the usefulness of the clinical frailty score.19 LRIE predicted worse prognosis, not only for short but also for long-term mortality.3,5,28,29 As in many studies, chronic kidney disease was a predictor of death. In our study, we found an association between increased creatinine and mortality.29,30 In our cohort, decreased ejection fraction was related to higher mortality, and the presence of CRT before TLE, similar to the experience described by Azari,19 was associated with higher mortality.

CHA2DS2-VASc and CHA2DS2-VA Score as Risk Factor of Mortality

Although originally developed to predict thromboembolic risk in AF patients, evidence supports the broader prognostic value of these scores even outside the AF population. In the MADIT-CRT study, increment in CHA2DS2-VASc was associated with an increase in mortality risk.31 Higher CHA2DS2-VASc has been associated with increased all-cause mortality not only in heart failure but also post-myocardial infraction, TAVI, and other populations, even after adjusting for confounders.32–35 Our findings confirm that incremental increases in CHA2DS2-VASc/VA scores correspond to higher long-term mortality risk in the TLE population, emphasizing their utility as simple, widely available prognostic tools.

Conclusion

Our study demonstrates that the CHA2DS2-VASc and CHA2DS2-VA scores are significant predictors of long-term mortality in patients undergoing TLE, reflecting the impact of the patient’s baseline clinical condition rather than procedural complexity. These simple, readily available scores may facilitate rapid risk stratification and help identify patients who could benefit from closer clinical surveillance, more intensive follow-up, and comprehensive cardiovascular management after TLE.
