A Model Companion for Abelian Lattice-Ordered Groups with a Valuation
John Stokes-Waters email :- john.stokes-waters@postgrad.manchester.ac.uk

Abstract
. An abelian lattice-ordered group, or abelian ℓ \ell -group, is an abelian group equipped with a compatible lattice ordering. In this paper, we introduce two multi-sorted extensions of abelian lattice-ordered groups inspired by the zero-set maps for continuous functions into ℝ \mathord{\mathbb{R}} . We demonstrate that this expansion is equivalent to equipping 𝒢 \cal{G} with a spectral subspace X X of ℓ ​ -Spec ​ ( 𝒢 ) \ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G}) , along with the map sending a ∈ 𝒢 a\in\cal{G} to V ⁡ ( a ​ ∧ ⁡ 0 ) ∩ X V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\cap X . Using a classical partial quantifier elimination result originally due to Fuxing Shen and Volker Weispfenning [ 15 ] , we show that one of these expansions admits a model companion, which is complete and has quantifier elimination in a small language expansion.

1.
Discussion

Abelian
\ell
-groups arise frequently throughout mathematics, notably as reducts of rings of real-valued continuous functions on Tychonoff spaces, as well as the value groups of valued fields. In addition, they play an important role in number theory, as
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
are both abelian
\ell
-groups for any choice of
n
. Further, they also play a central role in the study of Łukasiewicz logic, as the category of abelian
\ell
-groups with strong order unit is equivalent to the category of MV algebras.

Our main interests lie in the model-theoretic properties of abelian
\ell
-groups. The class of existentially closed abelian
\ell
-groups is well-understood (see
[
12
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
), however it does not form an elementary class
[
17
, Page 72]
. Our goal then is to find a suitable theory of abelian
\ell
-groups which admits a model companion.

In this paper, we propose a multi-sorted extension to abelian
\ell
-groups, which we call
densely valued

\ell
-groups
. These are inspired by the ring of real-valued continuous functions
C(\mathord{\mathbb{R}})
, and the map
P
which assigns to each
f\in C(\mathord{\mathbb{R}})
the set
\{x\in\mathord{\mathbb{R}}\ |\ f(x)\geqslant 0\}
. As such, a densely valued
\ell
-group is a triple
(\cal{G},\cal{L},P)
where
\cal{G}
is an abelian
\ell
-group,
\cal{L}
is a bounded distributive lattice, and
P
is a lattice morphism from
\cal{G}
to
\cal{L}
satisfying some additional axioms (See Definition
3.3
for details). Further, we show that there is a pair of adjoint functors between the category of abelian
\ell
-groups and the category of densely valued
\ell
-groups (Theorem
3.8
). This adjunction equips each abelian
\ell
-group
\cal{G}
with the lattice of closed constructible sets of its spectrum.

The main importance of densely valued
\ell
-groups is that they are the natural model-theoretic environment for the application of the Shen-Weispfenning theorem - a classical result due to
[
15
]
and stated in Theorem
5.11
, which tells us that if
(\cal{G},\cal{L},P)
is densely valued with
\cal{G}
divisible and satisfying
patching
(see Definition
5.10
), then we can eliminate quantifiers in the group sort.

Using the Shen-Weispfenning theorem, we first characterise the algebraically closed densely valued
\ell
-groups (Theorem
5.13
). In particular, they are those triples
(\cal{G},\cal{L},P)
with patching,
\cal{G}
divisible, and
\cal{L}
Boolean. Then, we use this to characterise existentially closed densely valued
\ell
-groups (Theorem
5.18
) as those which are algebraically closed and with
\cal{L}
atomless.

We investigate the theory
\tvlgrpec
of densely valued
\ell
-groups, and show it is complete (Theorem
6.1
), and has quantifier elimination in the language
((+,-,\leqslant,0,\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}},\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}),(\sqsubseteq,\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcup$}}}},\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcap$}}}},\top,\bot,\lnot),P)
(Theorem
6.6
).

An

\mathord{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
-group

\cal{G}
with a partial order
\leqslant
such that
(\cal{G},\leqslant)
is a lattice, and such that, for

with

, we have that
a+c\leqslant b+c
. From now on, we use “
\ell
-group” to mean “abelian
\ell
-group”. If
\leqslant
is a total order, then we say that
\cal{G}
is an
\mathord{\boldsymbol{o}}
-group
. We note that the class of
\ell
-groups is finitely axiomatisable in the language
\llgrp=(+,-,0,\leqslant,\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}},\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}})
, and we denote the corresponding theory by
\tlgrp
.

For an element
a\in\cal{G}
, we write
\left|a\right|=a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}(-a)
, which is the
absolute value
of
a
. We note that
0\leqslant\left|a\right|
for all
a\in\cal{G}
. Further, we write
\cal{G}^{+}=\{a\in\cal{G}\ |\ 0\leqslant a\}
for the
positive cone
of
\cal{G}
.

An
\mathord{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
-group morphism
is a map
\phi:\cal{G}\to\cal{H}
which is both a group morphism and a lattice morphism. We say
\phi
is an
embedding
(resp.
surjection
) if it is injective (resp. surjective).

An
\ell
-group
\cal{G}
is
divisible
if, for all
a\in\cal{G}
and
n\in\mathord{\mathbb{N}}
, there exists
b\in\cal{G}
with
nb=a
. If
\cal{G}
is a divisible
o
-group, then we call it a
DOAG
. The
divisible hull
of
\cal{G}
, denoted
\divis(\cal{G})
, is the tensor product
\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\cal{G}
. We formally identify
\divis(\cal{G})
with the set of symbols
\left\{\frac{a}{n}\ |\ a\in\cal{G},\ n\in\mathord{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
, with operations given for
a,b\in\cal{G}
and
n,m\in\mathord{\mathbb{N}}
by
\frac{a}{n}-\frac{b}{m}=\frac{a-b}{nm}
,
\frac{a}{n}\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}\frac{b}{m}=\frac{a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{0.75346pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}b}{nm}
, and
\frac{a}{n}\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}\frac{b}{m}=\frac{a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{0.75346pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}b}{nm}
. The map
\iota:\cal{G}\to\divis(\cal{G}):g\mapsto\frac{g}{1}
is an
\ell
-group embedding, and
\divis(\cal{G})
embeds into any divisible extension of
\cal{G}
.

An
\mathord{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
-subgroup
of
\cal{G}
is a subgroup
\cal{H}\subseteq\cal{G}
that is also a sublattice. We denote this by
\cal{H}\leqslant\cal{G}
. An
\ell
-subgroup
\cal{J}\mathbin{\leqslant}\cal{G}
is an
\mathord{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
-ideal
if it is also
convex
- for all
a,b\in\cal{J}
and
c\in\cal{G}
with
a\leqslant c\leqslant b
,
c\in\cal{J}
. We denote this by
\cal{J}\mathbin{\lhd}\cal{G}
, and note that
\ell
-ideals are precisely the kernels of
\ell
-group morphisms. For
S\subseteq\cal{G}
, the
minimal

\mathord{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
-ideal
containing
S
, denoted
\mathop{\textnormal{Ideal}\left(S\right)}
, is the intersection of all
\ell
-ideals containing
S
.

An
\ell
-ideal
\cal{J}\mathbin{\lhd}\cal{G}
is
prime
if any of the following equivalent conditions hold:
(1)\ {\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}}
is totally-ordered;
(2)
For all
a,b\in\cal{G}^{+}
with
a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}b\in\cal{J}
,
a\in\cal{J}
or
b\in\cal{J}
; or
(3)
For all
a,b\in\cal{G}
with
a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}b=0
,
a\in\cal{J}
or
b\in\cal{J}
.

We remark that we adopt the convention that
\cal{G}
is a prime
\ell
-ideal of
\cal{G}
. We write
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
for the collection of prime
\ell
-ideals of
\cal{G}
. This is a spectral space with quasicompact open sets
D(a)=\{\cal{J}\in\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})\ |\ a\notin\cal{J}\}\cup\{\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})\}
. Further, we let
V(a)=\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})\setminus D(a)
. We define both
D(S)
and
V(S)
for arbitrary
S\subseteq\cal{G}
similarly, and note that
\mathop{\textnormal{Ideal}\left(S\right)}=\bigcap V(S)
. This is the
Nullstellensatz
(for
\ell
-groups).

Let
\LGrp
be the category of
\ell
-groups, and
\Spec
the category of spectral spaces. Then, the map
\ell\mathord{\textnormal{-Spec}}:\LGrp\to\Spec
given for an
\ell
-group morphism
\phi:\cal{G}\to\cal{H}
and
\cal{J}\in\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{H})
by
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\phi)(\cal{J})=\phi^{-1}(\cal{J})
is a contravariant functor. Further,
\ell
-Spec sends embeddings to surjections, surjections to embeddings, and for
a\in\cal{G}
, we have
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\phi)^{-1}(V(a))=V(\phi(a))
.

For an
\ell
-group
\cal{G}
and any
X\subseteq\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
with
\bigcap X=\{0\}
, we have an
\ell
-group embedding:

I:\cal{G}\to\prod_{\cal{J}\in X}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}}:a\mapsto(a+\cal{J})_{\cal{J}\in X}.

Further, by taking divisible hulls in each component, we can embed
\cal{G}
in a product of DOAGs. The theory of DOAGs is complete, and so by taking a saturated extension, we get that there is some DOAG
\Gamma
and
\ell
-group embedding
\cal{G}\to\Gamma^{X}
.

Given a set
S\subseteq\cal{G}
, the
polar
of
S
is the set
S^{\perp}=\{g\in\cal{G}\ |\ (\forall s\in S)(g\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}s=0)\}
. For an element
g\in\cal{G}
, we write
g^{\perp}=\{g\}^{\perp}
, and
g^{\perp\perp}=(g^{\perp})^{\perp}
.

Originally due to
[
17
, Page
69
]
, with the below modern treatment due to
[

, an
\ell
-group
\cal{G}
is existentially closed if it satisfies the following:

(1)

\cal{G}
is divisible.

(2)

For all
a,b,c\in\cal{G}^{+}
with
a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}b=0
, there exists
g,h\in\cal{G}
with
c=g+h
, and:

a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}h=g\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}b=g\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}h=0.

(3)

For all
a\in\cal{G}^{+}
non-zero, there exists
g,h\in\cal{G}^{+}
non-zero such that
g,h\leqslant a
and
g\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}h=0
.

(4)

For all
a\in\cal{G}^{+}
, there exists
g\in\cal{G}^{+}
non-zero such that
a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}g=0
.

(5)

\mathop{\textnormal{Ideal}\left(g\right)}=g^{\perp\perp}
for all
g\in\cal{G}
.

If only
(1)
and
(2)
hold, then
\cal{G}
is algebraically closed. We remark that conditions
(1)-(4)
are first-order in
\llgrp
, and so
(5)
isn’t by
[
17
, Page
72
]
and general model theory.

An element

is a
weak order unit
if either of the following equivalent conditions holds:
(1)
For all
a\in\cal{G}^{+}
, if
g\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}a=0
, then
a=0
; or
(2)
The set
D(g)
is dense in
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
.

Further, it is a

if either of the following equivalent conditions holds:
(1)
For all
a\in\cal{G}^{+}
, there exists some
n\in\mathord{\mathbb{N}}
such that
a\leqslant ng
; or
(2)

D(g)=\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})\setminus\{\cal{G}\}
.

We call
\cal{G}
is
Archimedean
if every
g\in\cal{G}^{+}
is a strong order unit, and it is
hyper-Archimedean
if, for all surjective
\ell
-group morphism
\phi:\cal{G}\to\cal{H}
,
\cal{H}
is Archimedean. Equivalently,
\cal{G}
is hyper-Archimedean if every proper
\ell
-ideal is minimal.

3.
Valuations on
\ell
-Groups

3.1.
Motivation

As discussed, the prime ideal structure of
\cal{G}
is of pivotal importance in understanding
\cal{G}
as a whole. Therefore, we want a valuation on an
\ell
-group
\cal{G}
to be a tool we can use to examine this structure in a first-order setting. The following example gives some motivation as to how we will go about doing this.

Example 3.1

Let
C(\mathord{\mathbb{R}})
be the
\ell
-group of continuous functions from
\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
to
\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
, where all operations are carried out pointwise. The set
\cal{J}_{x}=\{f\in C(\mathord{\mathbb{R}})\ |\ f(x)=0\}
is clearly a prime
\ell
-ideal of
C(\mathord{\mathbb{R}})
.

Hence, in order to gain some access to prime
\ell
-ideals in a first-order setting, we would like to construct a notion of valuation that resembles the map
Z
sending a continuous function
f:\mathord{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
to the set
f^{-1}(0)\subseteq\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
. However, we observe that this map doesn’t respect of any
\cal{G}
’s structure. Indeed, we see:

Z(X)\cap Z(X+1)=\emptyset\neq\{0\}=Z(X)=Z(X\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}(X+1)).

If however, we instead consider the map
P
which sends a continuous function
f:\mathord{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
to the set
f^{-1}([0,\infty))\subseteq\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
of points where
f
takes on a positive value, then we observe the following:

(1)

For any
f,g:\mathord{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
continuous, we have that:

\displaystyle 0\leqslant f(x)\text{ and }0\leqslant g(x)\iff

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\min\{f(x),g(x)\}

\displaystyle 0\leqslant f(x)\text{ or }0\leqslant g(x)\iff

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\max\{f(x),g(x)\}.

In particular, the map
P
preserves the lattice structure on
\cal{G}
.

(2)

We can recover
Z
in a first-order way by noticing that:

f(x)=0\iff 0\leqslant(-\left|f\right|)(x).

In general, an
\ell
-group isn’t an
\ell
-group of continuous functions. Instead, we use the following folklore result to provide a general framework for constructing a valuation.

Theorem 3.2

Let
\cal{G}
be an
\ell
-group. Then, there are
\ell
-group embeddings:

\cal{G}\hookrightarrow\prod_{\cal{J}\in\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}}\hookrightarrow\Gamma^{\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})}

for some divisible
o
-group
\Gamma
. We remark that this map isn’t uniquely determined, but for any choice of composition
I
, we have that:

I(f)(\cal{J})\geqslant 0\iff I(f\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)(\cal{J})=0\iff f\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0\in\cal{J}\iff\cal{J}\in V(f\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)

for any
f\in\cal{G}
and
\cal{J}\in\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
.

This justifies asking the question “At what points of its domain is an element of an
\ell
-group positive?” in general. Thus, we construct our definition of a valuation on an
\ell
-group:

Definition 3.3

(Valuation on an
\ell
-Group)

Let
\cal{G}
be an
\ell
-group. Then, a
valuation
on
\cal{G}
is a lattice morphism:

P:\cal{G}\to\cal{L}

where
\cal{L}
is a bounded distributive lattice, satisfying the following:

•

P
is
scaling invariant
-
P(a)=P(na)
for all
a\in\cal{G}
and
n\in\mathord{\mathbb{N}}
.

•

P
is
positivity affirming
-
P(a)=\top
for all
a\in\cal{G}^{+}
.

•

P
is
essentially total
- for all
l\in\cal{L}
with
l\neq\bot
, there exists some
a\in\cal{G}
with
P(a)=l
.

Further, if for all
a\in\cal{G}
, we have that:

P(a)=\top\implies 0\leqslant a

then we say that
P
is
positivity detecting
, and a
dense valuation
.

Example 3.4

Let
\cal{G}
be an
\ell
-group, and
\cal{L}=\cal{P}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G}))
. Then, the map:

P:\cal{G}\to\cal{L}:a\mapsto\{\cal{J}\in\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})\ |\ a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0\in\cal{J}\}

is a dense valuation on
\cal{G}
. Equivalently, by Theorem
3.2
, we have that:

P(a)=\{\cal{J}\in\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})\ |\ I(a)(\cal{J})\geqslant 0\}.

We remark that
P
is not surjective - indeed, for every
a\in\cal{G}
, there is some
\cal{J}\in\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
with
a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0\in\cal{J}
, and so
P(a)\neq\emptyset
.

We can bundle an
\ell

valued

\mathord{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
-group
, or a
densely valued

\mathord{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
-group
if

is a dense valuation. In particular, we view this in the language

\mathord{\mathbb{G}}
and
\mathord{\mathbb{L}}

\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}:\mathord{\mathbb{G}}\times\mathord{\mathbb{G}}\to\mathord{\mathbb{G}}

\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcap$}}}}:\mathord{\mathbb{L}}\times\mathord{\mathbb{L}}\to\mathord{\mathbb{L}}

\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcup$}}}}:\mathord{\mathbb{L}}\times\mathord{\mathbb{L}}\to\mathord{\mathbb{L}}

P:\mathord{\mathbb{G}}\to\mathord{\mathbb{L}}

In this language, there is evidently a theory of both valued and densely
\ell
-groups, which we denote
\tvlgrp
and
\tdvlgrp
respectively. We observe that these theories are
\forall\exists
. This also gives us the notion of a valued
\ell
-group morphism for free.

Definition 3.5

(Valued
\ell
-Group Morphism)

Let
\cal{V}=(\cal{G},\cal{L},P)
and
\cal{W}=(\cal{H},\cal{K},Q)
be valued
\ell
-groups. Then, a
valued

\mathord{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
-group morphism
from
\cal{V}
to
\cal{W}
is a pair
(\phi,\psi)
consisting of an
\ell
-group morphism and a bounded lattice morphism, and such that
Q\circ\phi=\psi\circ P
.

Further,
(\phi,\psi)
is an
embedding
(resp.
surjection
) if both
\phi
and
\psi
are embeddings (resp. surjections).

We write
\ValGrp
for the category of valued
\ell
-groups and valued
\ell
-group morphisms, and
\DValGrp
for the full subcategory whose objects are densely valued
\ell
-groups.

3.2.
The Natural Valuation of an
\ell
-Group

Theorem
3.2
suggests that there is a natural way to associate a valuation to an
\ell
-group, and that this can be done in such a way that the associated valuation looks precisely like taking a function to the set of points where it has a positive value. It turns out that not only is this true, but this construction is functorial.

Lemma 3.6

Let
\cal{G}
be an
\ell
-group. Then:

(1)

The triple
\textnormal{Val}(\cal{G})\coloneqq(\cal{G},{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G}))},V(-\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0))
is a densely valued
\ell
-group.

(2)

If
\cal{H}
is an
\ell
-group, and
\phi:\cal{G}\to\cal{H}
an
\ell
-group morphism, then there is a valued
\ell
-group morphism:

\textnormal{Val}(\cal{\phi})\coloneqq(\phi,{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\phi))}):\textnormal{Val}(\cal{G})\to\textnormal{Val}(\cal{H}).

(3)

If
\phi
is an embedding (resp. surjection), then so is
\textnormal{Val}(\cal{\phi})
.

(4)

Val
is a covariant functor from
\LGrp
to
\DValGrp
.

Proof.

(1)
Obvious from the observation that
\cal{J}\in V(f\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)
if and only if
I(f)(\cal{J})\geqslant 0
, where
I
is an
\ell
-group embedding of
\cal{G}
in
\Gamma^{\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})}
as in Theorem
3.2
.

(2)
It is clear that
{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\phi))}
is a bounded lattice morphism. Hence, it suffices to show that this diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G}))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{H}))}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(-\mathop{\text{\raisebox{0.75346pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\wedge$}}}}0)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(-\mathop{\text{\raisebox{0.75346pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\wedge$}}}}0)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\phi))}}

Thus, let
a\in\cal{G}
and
\cal{J}\in\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{H})
. We see that:

\displaystyle\cal{J}\in{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\phi))}(V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0))\iff

\displaystyle\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\phi)(\cal{J})\in V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0\in\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\phi)(\cal{J})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\phi(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\in\cal{J}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\cal{J}\in V(\phi(a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)

as required.

(3)
Immediate, as the functors
\bar{\cal{K}}
and
\ell
-Spec both have this property.

(4)
As
\bar{\cal{K}}
and
\ell
-Spec are both contravariant functors, then their composition is a covariant functor, and so we are done.
∎

Even stronger than being a functor, we can show that
Val
is a left adjoint to the forgetful functor
\iota:\DValGrp\to\LGrp
. To do this, we first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7

Let
\cal{V}\coloneqq(\cal{G},\cal{L},P)
be a valued
\ell
-group. Then, there is a unique bounded lattice morphism
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}:{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G}))}\to\cal{L}
such that this diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G}))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{L}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(-\mathop{\text{\raisebox{0.75346pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\wedge$}}}}0)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon_{\cal{V}}}

Further,
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}
is surjective and given by
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(a))=P(-\left|a\right|)
and
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(\emptyset)=\bot
.

Proof.

We first show that
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}
is well-defined. Hence, let
a,b\in\cal{G}
such that
V(a)=V(b)
. By the Nullstellensatz, we have that
\mathop{\textnormal{Ideal}\left(a\right)}=\mathop{\textnormal{Ideal}\left(b\right)}
, and so there exists
n,m\in\mathord{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\left|a\right|\leqslant n\left|b\right|
and
\left|b\right|\leqslant m\left|a\right|
. In particular,
-n\left|b\right|\leqslant-\left|a\right|
and
-m\left|a\right|\leqslant-\left|b\right|
, and so:

P(-\left|b\right|)=P(-n\left|b\right|)\sqsubseteq P(-\left|a\right|)=P(-m\left|a\right|)\sqsubseteq P(-\left|a\right|).

Thus,
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(a))=\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(b))
. Next, we show that
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}
is a bounded lattice morphism. Hence, let
a,b\in\cal{G}^{+}
and consider that:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(a)\cup V(b))=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(-(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(-a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcup$}}}}P(-b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(a))\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcup$}}}}\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(b)).

Hence,
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}
respects joins, and mutatis mutandis respects meets. By definition,
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(\emptyset)=\bot
, , and we note that
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(0))=P(-\left|0\right|)=\top
. Thus,
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}
is a bounded lattice morphism. Next, we show that it uniquely makes this diagram commute:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G}))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{L}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(-\mathop{\text{\raisebox{0.75346pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\wedge$}}}}0)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon_{\cal{V}}}

For
a\in\cal{G}
, we see that:

\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0))=P(-\left|a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0\right|)=P(-((-a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}0))=P(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)=P(a).

Hence, the above diagram commutes. Further, if
\gamma:{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G}))}\to\cal{L}
also makes it commute, then we see that for
a\in\cal{G}
:

\gamma(V(a))=\gamma(V(-\left|a\right|))=\gamma(V((-\left|a\right|)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0))=P(-\left|a\right|)=\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(a)).

Hence,
\gamma=\epsilon_{\cal{V}}
, and so
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}
is unique. Finally, let
l\in\cal{L}
. If
l=\bot
, then
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(\emptyset)=l
. Otherwise, as
P
is essentially total, then there exists
a\in\cal{G}
such that
P(a)=l
. By the above, we have that
l=P(a)=\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0))
, as required.
∎

Using this, it is almost immediate that
Val
is left adjoint to
\iota
. Regardless, we include a proof for completeness.

Corollary 3.8

(The Natural Valuation Adjunction)

The functor
Val
is left adjoint to forgetful functors
\iota^{\mathord{\textnormal{d}}}:\DValGrp\to\LGrp
and
\iota:\ValGrp\to\LGrp
.

Proof.

We show the case for
\iota
, with the case for
\iota^{\mathord{\textnormal{d}}}
following similarly. Let
\cal{V}\coloneqq(\cal{G},\cal{L},P)
be a valued
\ell
-group,
\cal{H}
an
\ell
-group, and
(\phi,\psi):\textnormal{Val}(\cal{H})\to\cal{V}
a valued
\ell
-group morphism. We claim that
\phi
is the unique
\ell
-group morphism such that the diagram
(\dagger)
below commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textnormal{Val}(\cal{H})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textnormal{Val}(\cal{G})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{V}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\phi,\psi)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textnormal{Val}(\phi)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbbm{1}_{\cal{G}},\epsilon_{\cal{V}})}

where
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}
is the map per Lemma
3.7
. To show commutativity of
(\dagger)
, it is sufficient to show that these two diagrams commute:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{H}))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G}))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{L}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\phi))}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbbm{1}_{\cal{G}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon_{\cal{V}}}

Clearly, the leftmost diagram commutes. Hence, let
a\in\cal{H}
and consider:

\displaystyle\psi(V(a))=

\displaystyle\psi(V((-\left|a\right|)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(P\circ\phi)(-\left|a\right|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(-\left|\phi(a)\right|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(\phi(a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\epsilon_{\cal{V}}\circ{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\phi))})(V(a)).

Thus,
(\dagger)
commutes. Finally, if the map
\alpha:\cal{H}\to\cal{G}
is another
\ell
-group morphism such that
\textnormal{Val}(\alpha)
makes
(\dagger)
commute, then we remark that it is immediate that, for
a\in\cal{H}
:

\phi(a)=(\mathbbm{1}_{\cal{G}}\circ\alpha)(a)=\alpha(a).

Hence,
\phi=\alpha
, and so by definition,
\textnormal{Val}\mathbin{\dashv}\iota
.
∎

This adjunction mimics the case of valued fields - in particular, it is clear that the map which equips a field with the trivial valuation
\nu:K\to\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}:k\mapsto 0
is left adjoint to the forgetful functor. However, we notice that here the natural valuation of an
\ell
-group is much more interesting - the lattice
{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G}))}
carries non-trivial information about
\cal{G}
that can’t be accessed in a first-order way in
\cal{G}
alone.

3.3.
Topological Representations of Valued
\ell
-Groups

The previous section suggests that valuations on
\ell
-groups are connected to their
\ell
-spectra. Indeed, we can construct a valuation on an
\ell
-group from any spectral subspace of its
\ell
-spectrum, as the next lemma demonstrates.

Definition 3.9

(Dense Set)

Let
X=(X,\tau)
be a topological space, and
D\subseteq X
. Then,
D
is
dense in

\mathord{\boldsymbol{X}}
if
\mathop{\textnormal{Cl}_{\tau}(D)}=X
.

Lemma 3.10

Let
\cal{G}
be an
\ell
-group, and
X\subseteq\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
be a spectral subspace. Then, we have a valuation
P:\cal{G}\to{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(X)}
given by
P(a)=V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\cap X
. Further, if
X
is dense in
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
, then
P
is a dense valuation.

Proof.

As
V(-\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0):\cal{G}\to{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G}))}
is a valuation, it is clear that
P
is a scaling invariant and essentially total lattice morphism. Further, we notice that for
a\in\cal{G}^{+}
, if
V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)=\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
, then
P(a)=X
. Thus,
P
is a valuation.

Now, if
X
is dense, then
X\cap D(a)\neq\emptyset
for any non-zero
a\in\cal{G}
. Thus, if
P(a)=x
, then
X\subseteq V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)
. Hence,
X\cap D(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)=\emptyset
, and so by density of
X
,
a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0=0
. Thus,
0\leqslant a
, and so
P
is a dense valuation.
∎

This is already a powerful tool for producing examples of valued
\ell
-groups. However, the remarkable thing is that every valuation on
\cal{G}
is of this form.

Lemma 3.11

Let
\cal{G}
be an
\ell
-group, and
X\subseteq\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
a spectral subspace. Then,
X
is dense in
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
if and only if
\bigcap X=\{0\}
.

Proof.

(\implies)
Let
a\in\bigcap X
. By definition, we have for all
\cal{J}\in X
that
a\in\cal{J}
. Thus,
X\subseteq V(a)
, and so we see that
X=V(a)\cap X=V((-\left|a\right|)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\cap X
. Therefore,
0\leqslant-\left|a\right|
, and so
a=0
. Thus,
\bigcap X=\{0\}
, as required.

(\impliedby)
Let
a\in\cal{G}
with
D(a)\neq\emptyset
. In particular,
a\neq 0
, and so
a\notin\bigcap X
. It follows that there is some
\cal{J}\in X
such that
a\notin\cal{J}
. Hence,
X\cap D(a)\neq\emptyset
, and so
X
is dense.
∎

Theorem 3.12

(The Topological Representation Theorem)

Let
\cal{V}=(\cal{G},\cal{L},P)
be a valued
\ell
-group, and:

P_{*}:\textnormal{PrimF}(\cal{L})\to\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G}):\cal{F}\mapsto(\nu_{\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})}^{-1}\circ\textnormal{PrimF}(\epsilon_{\cal{V}}))(\cal{F})

with
X=\image(P_{*})
. Then:

(1)

X
is a spectral subspace of
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
, and the map
\Phi:{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(X)}\to\cal{L}
given by
\Phi=\mu_{\cal{L}}^{-1}\circ{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(P_{*})}
is a bounded lattice isomorphism such that
(\mathbbm{1}_{\cal{G}},\Phi)
is an isomorphism of valued
\ell
-groups.

(2)

If
X=\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
, then
\Phi=\epsilon_{\cal{V}}
.

(3)

V(-\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\cap X
is a dense valuation if and only if
X
is dense in
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
.

Proof.

(1)
By Lemma
3.7
, we have that
\epsilon_{\cal{V}}
is a surjective bounded lattice morphism. Hence, by Spectral Duality,
P_{*}
is is a homeomorphism onto its image, and so,
\Phi
is a bounded lattice isomorphism. By
[
7
, Corollary
2.1.4
]
,
X
is a spectral subspace. Now, it remains to check that
(\mathbbm{1}_{\cal{G}},\Phi)
is an isomorphism of valued
\ell
-groups. In particular, we show that this diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{L}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(-\mathop{\text{\raisebox{0.75346pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\scriptstyle\wedge$}}}}0)\cap X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}

First, let
l\in\cal{L}
,
C\in{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(X)}
, and notice that:

\displaystyle\Phi(C)=l\iff

\displaystyle{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(P_{*})}(C)=V(l)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle(\forall\cal{F}\in\textnormal{PrimF}(\cal{L}))(\cal{F}\in{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(P_{*})}(C)\iff\cal{F}\in V(l))

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle(\forall\cal{F}\in\textnormal{PrimF}(\cal{L}))(P_{*}(\cal{F})\in C\iff l\in\cal{F}).

Hence, let
a\in\cal{G}
, and see that for
\cal{F}\in\textnormal{PrimF}(\cal{L})
:

\displaystyle P_{*}(\cal{F})\in V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\iff

\displaystyle V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\in\textnormal{PrimF}(\epsilon_{\cal{V}})(\cal{F})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0))\in\cal{F}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle P(a)\in\cal{F}.

Thus,
\Phi(V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0))=P(a)
, and so the above diagram commutes as required.

(2)
Let
X=\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
. It is clear that
\Phi(\bot)=\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(\bot)
. Then, for
a\in\cal{G}
and
\cal{I}\in\textnormal{PrimF}(\cal{L})
, we see that:

\displaystyle\cal{I}\in{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(P_{*})}(V(a))\iff

\displaystyle P_{*}(\cal{I})\in V(a)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle a\in P_{*}(\cal{I})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle V(a)\in\textnormal{PrimF}(\epsilon_{\cal{V}})(\cal{I})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(a))\in\cal{I}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\cal{I}\in V(\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(a)).

Hence, it follows that
\Phi(V(a))=\mu_{\cal{L}}^{-1}(V(\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(a)))=\epsilon_{\cal{V}}(V(a))
, as required.

(3)
By Lemma
3.11
, we show that
V(-\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\cap X
is a dense valuation if and only if
\bigcap X=\{0\}
. We check both implications separately:

•

(\implies)
Let
a\in\cal{G}
with
D(a)\neq\emptyset
. It is clear that
a\neq 0
, and so
a\notin\bigcap X
. Thus, there is some
\cal{J}\in X
with
a\notin\cal{J}
, and so
X\cap D(a)\neq\emptyset
. In particular,
X
is dense.

•

(\impliedby)
This is Lemma
3.10
.

∎

One quick consequence of the Topological Representation Theorem is the following alternative characterisation of the scaling invariance axiom. In particular, this brings the notion of a valuation on an
\ell
-groups closer in spirit to a valuation on a field.

Lemma 3.13

Let
\cal{G}
be an
\ell
-group, and
\cal{L}
a bounded distributive lattice. Let
P:\cal{G}\to\cal{L}
be a lattice morphism which is positivity affirming and essentially total. Then,
P
is scaling invariant if and only if, for all
a,b\in\cal{G}
:

P(a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcap$}}}}P(b)\sqsubseteq P(a+b)\sqsubseteq P(a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcup$}}}}P(b).

Proof.

(\implies)
By assumption,
\cal{V}\coloneqq(\cal{G},\cal{L},P)
is a valued
\ell
-group. Hence, by the Topological Representation Theorem, let
X\subseteq\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
be a spectral subspace such that
\cal{V}\cong(\cal{G},{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(X)},V(-\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\cap X)
. We assume WLOG that this isomorphism is equality. Now, for
a,b\in\cal{G}
and
\cal{J}\in X
, we see that:

\displaystyle\cal{J}\in V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\cap V(b\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\implies

\displaystyle a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0,b\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0\in\cal{J}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\cal{J}\leqslant a+\cal{J},b+\cal{J}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\cal{J}\leqslant(a+b)+\cal{J}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\cal{J}\in V((a+b)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\ [\dagger].

In particular,
P(a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcap$}}}}P(b)\sqsubseteq P(a+b)
. Now, as
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}}
is totally ordered, then we have that:

\displaystyle[\dagger]\implies

\displaystyle\cal{J}\leqslant(a+\cal{J})+(b+\cal{J})

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\cal{J}\leqslant 2\cdot\max\{a+\cal{J},b+\cal{J}\}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\cal{J}\leqslant(2(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}b))+\cal{J}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\cal{J}\in V((2(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}b))\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0).

In particular,
P(a+b)\sqsubseteq P(2(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}b))=P(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}b)=P(a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcup$}}}}P(b)
, as required.

(\impliedby)
Let
a\in\cal{G}
. We see immediately that:

P(a)=P\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}a\right)\sqsubseteq P(na)\sqsubseteq P\left(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}a\right)=P(a).

∎

The key notion used in the Conrad-Darnel-Nelson construction is that of the valuation lattice.

\displaystyle\Pi(a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcap$}}}}\Pi(b)=

\displaystyle\top=

\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle\bot=

The proof of this is non-trivial (see

We recall that, for a poset

Lemma 4.4

Let
\cal{G}

\ell

\cal{L}=\mathbin{\Pi(\cal{G})^{\textnormal{op}}}

\mathbin{\Pi(\cal{G})^{\textnormal{op}}}

P
is a dense valuation on

Proof.

(1)
We check each property in turn:

•

(Lattice Morphism) Let
a,b\in\cal{G}
. Then, we see that, as
\Pi(g)=\Pi(\left|g\right|)
for all
g\in\cal{G}

\displaystyle P(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}b)=

\displaystyle\Pi((a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}b)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi(\left|(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}b)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0\right|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi(((-a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}0)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}((-b)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi((-a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}0)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcup$}}}}\Pi((-b)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0){\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcap$}}}}}^{\mathord{\textnormal{op}}}\Pi(b\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(a){\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcap$}}}}}^{\mathord{\textnormal{op}}}P(b).

and similarly for
\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}
. Hence,

is a lattice morphism.

•

(Scaling Invariant) Let

and

. We notice then that:

\displaystyle P(a)=

\displaystyle\Pi(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi\cap D(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)^{\max}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi\cap D(n(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0))^{\max}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi\cap D((na)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)^{\max}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi((na)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)

\displaystyle P(na).

(Essentially Surjective) As

(Positivity Affirming and Detecting) First, let

Corollary 4.5

Let
\cal{G}
be an
\ell
-group, and
\Pi\subseteq\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
a plenary set. Let
\tau
be the topology on
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
, and
\tau_{\textnormal{con}}
the patch topology on
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
, and
X=\mathop{\textnormal{Cl}_{\tau_{\textnormal{con}}}(\Pi)}
. Then,
X
is a dense spectral subspace, and the map
\alpha:\mathbin{\Pi(\cal{G})^{\textnormal{op}}}\to{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(X)}
given by
\alpha(\Pi(g))\mapsto V(g)\cap X
is a bounded lattice isomorphism, and induces a valued
\ell
-group isomorphism:

(\mathbbm{1}_{\cal{G}},\alpha):(\cal{G},\mathbin{\Pi(\cal{G})^{\textnormal{op}}},\Pi(-\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0))\to(\cal{G},{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(X)},V(-\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\cap X).

Proof.

First, we show that
X
is a dense spectral subspace of
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
. By
[
7
, Theorem
2.1.3
]
,
X
is a spectral subspace. Then, as
\bigcap X\subseteq\bigcap\Pi=\{0\}
, then
X
is dense by Lemma
3.11
. Now, we show that
\alpha
is a well-defined bounded lattice isomorphism. Hence, consider:

•

(Well-Defined) Let
g,h\in\cal{G}
be such that
\Pi(g)=\Pi(h)
, and suppose
\alpha(\Pi(g))\neq\alpha(\Pi(h))
. In particular (WLOG), let
\cal{J}\in X
be such that
\cal{J}\in V(g)\cap D(h)
. As
\cal{J}\in X=\mathop{\textnormal{Cl}_{\tau_{\textnormal{con}}}(\Pi)}
, then
V(g)\cap D(h)\cap\Pi\neq\emptyset
. Hence, let
\cal{I}\in\Pi\cap V(g)\cap D(h)
. Then, there is some
\cal{I}^{\prime}\in D(h)^{\max}
such that
\cal{I}\subseteq\cal{I}^{\prime}
. As
\Pi
is plenary, then
\cal{I}^{\prime}\in\Pi
. Now, we notice that as
g\in\cal{I}
, then
g\in\cal{I}^{\prime}
. In particular,
\cal{I}^{\prime}\in\Pi(h)
and
\cal{I}^{\prime}\notin\Pi(g)
↯. Thus,
\alpha(\Pi(g))=\alpha(\Pi(h))
, as required.

•

(Lattice Morphism) Let
g,h\in\cal{G}^{+}
. We notice immediately that:

\displaystyle\alpha(\Pi(g)\mathbin{{\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcup$}}}}}^{\textnormal{op}}}\Pi(h))=

\displaystyle\alpha(\Pi(g\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}h))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V(g\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}h)\cap X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(V(g)\cap X)\cup(V(h)\cap X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(\Pi(g))\cup\alpha(\Pi(h))

and similarly for
\mathbin{{\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcap$}}}}}^{\textnormal{op}}}
.

•

(Injective) Let
g,h\in\cal{G}^{+}
be such that
\Pi(g)\neq\Pi(h)
. In particular, there is some
\cal{I}\in\Pi
such that (WLOG)
g\in\cal{I}
and
h\notin\cal{I}
. Then, as
\Pi\subseteq X
, we have that
\alpha(\Pi(g))=V(g)\cap X\neq V(h)\cap X=\alpha(\Pi(h))
.

•

(Surjective) Let
g\in\cal{G}^{+}
. It is immediate that
\alpha(\Pi(g))=V(g)\cap X
, as required.

Now, we show that
(\mathbbm{1}_{\cal{G}},\alpha)
is a valued
\ell
-group isomorphism. However, this follows immediately from the fact that
V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\cap X=\Pi(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)

∎

In particular, every Conrad-Darnel-Nelson valuations is associated to some densely valued
\ell

Example 4.6

Let
\cal{G}=\mathord{\mathbb{R}}\times_{\mathord{\textnormal{lex}}}\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
be the lexicographic product of
\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
with itself, and let
\cal{J}=\{(a,0)\ |\ a\in\mathord{\mathbb{R}}\}
so that
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})=\{\{0\},\cal{J},\cal{G}\}
. It is clear that
X=\{\{0\}\}
is a spectral subspace of
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})\}
, and so by
[
7
, Corollary
4.4.6
]
that is dense in
\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})\}
. However, we notice that
\cal{J}
is maximal amongst
\ell
-ideals not containing
(0,1)
, and so is regular. Hence,

isn’t plenary.

5.
Standard Structures

5.1.
Functional Representations of Densely Valued
\ell
-Groups

The study of
\ell
-groups is intimately related to the study of function spaces of the form
\Gamma^{X}
for
\Gamma
a DOAG, as in particular every
\ell
-group embeds in one of this form. The following section demonstrates that a similar result holds for densely valued
\ell
-groups - namely, we can always embed a densely valued
\ell
-group
\cal{V}
into a
standard structure
(Definition
5.2
).

Lemma 5.1

Let
\cal{V}=(\cal{G},\cal{L},P)
be a densely valued
\ell
-group. Then, there is a DOAG
\Gamma
and
\ell
-group embedding
\iota:\cal{G}\to\Gamma^{\textnormal{PrimF}(\cal{L})}
such that, for
a\in\cal{G}
and
\cal{F}\in\textnormal{PrimF}(\cal{L})
,
\iota(a)(\cal{F})\geqslant 0
if and only if
P(a)\in\cal{F}
.

Proof.

By the Topological Representation Theorem, let
X\subseteq\ell\textnormal{-Spec}(\cal{G})
be a dense spectral subspace such that
\cal{V}\cong(\cal{G},{\bar{\mathcal{K}}(X)},V(-\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\cap X)
. We can assume WLOG that this isomorphism is equality. Now, we define a map:

I_{X}:\cal{G}\to\prod_{\cal{J}\in X}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}}:a\mapsto(a+\cal{J})_{\cal{J}\in X}.

Clearly,
I_{X}
is an
\ell
-group morphism. Further, we note that for
a\in\cal{G}
:

I_{X}(a)=0\iff(\forall\cal{J}\in X)(a\in\cal{J})\iff a\in\bigcap X\iff a=0.

Thus,
\ker(I_{X})=\{0\}
, and so
I_{X}
is an
\ell
-group embedding. Further, for
a\in\cal{G}
and
\cal{J}\in X
, we see that
I_{X}(a)(\cal{J})=0
if and only if
a+\cal{J}=\cal{J}
if and only if
a\in\cal{J}
. Then, as
\nu_{X}
is an isomorphism of spectral spaces, then we have an isomorphism:

\nu_{X}^{*}:\prod_{\cal{J}\in X}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}}\to\prod_{\cal{F}\in\textnormal{PrimF}({\bar{\mathcal{K}}(X)})}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.32pt}{$\displaystyle{\nu_{X}^{-1}(\cal{F})}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.07pt}{$\textstyle{\nu_{X}^{-1}(\cal{F})}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.12001pt}{$\scriptstyle{\nu_{X}^{-1}(\cal{F})}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\nu_{X}^{-1}(\cal{F})}$}}}:(a+\cal{J})_{\cal{J}\in X}\mapsto(a+\nu_{X}^{-1}(\nu_{X}(\cal{J})))_{\cal{J}\in X}.

We then see that, for
a\in\cal{G}
and
\cal{F}\in\textnormal{PrimF}({\bar{\mathcal{K}}(X)})
, we have:

\displaystyle 0\leqslant(\nu_{X}^{*}\circ I_{X})(a)(\cal{F})\iff

\displaystyle\nu_{X}^{-1}(\cal{F})\leqslant a+\nu_{X}^{-1}(\cal{F})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0\in\nu_{X}^{-1}(\cal{F})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\nu_{X}^{-1}(\cal{F})\in V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\cap X

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle V(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}0)\cap X\in\cal{F}.

Finally, by taking divisible hulls in each component and we have that
\cal{G}
embeds into a product of DOAGs. By
[
9
, Exercise
2.8.10
]
, the theory of DOAGs is complete, and so there is a DOAG
\Gamma
with
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{G}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\cal{J}}$}}}\mathbin{\leqslant}\Gamma
for each
\cal{J}\in X
. Hence, there is a valued
\ell
-group embedding
\iota:\cal{G}\to\Gamma^{\textnormal{PrimF}(\cal{L})}
which satisfies
\iota(a)(\cal{F})\geqslant 0\iff P(a)\in\cal{F}
.
∎

We note that, by identifying
\cal{G}
with
\iota(\cal{G})
, we can always assume that
\iota
is the inclusion map.

As alluded to above, this allows us to define an important class of densely valued
\ell
-groups - the standard structures.

Lemma 5.2

(Standard Valuations)

Let
\Gamma
be a divisible
o
-group and
X
a non-empty set. Then, the map:

\{-\geqslant 0\}:\Gamma^{X}\to\cal{P}(X):f\mapsto\{f\geqslant 0\}

where
\{f\geqslant 0\}=\{x\in X\ |\ f(x)\geqslant 0\}
is a valuation on
\Gamma^{X}
, and surjective. We call this the
standard valuation
on
\Gamma^{X}
, and write
\textnormal{Stan}\left(\Gamma^{X}\right)
for the triple
(\Gamma^{X},\cal{P}(X),\{-\geqslant 0\})
, which we call the
standard structure
associated to
\Gamma^{X}
.

Proof.

Immediate from the motivation for the definition of a dense valuation, noting that for any
S\subseteq X
, the map:

f:X\to\Gamma:x\mapsto\begin{cases}g&x\in S\\
-g&x\notin S\end{cases}

is such that
S=\{f\geqslant 0\}
for any choice of
g\in\Gamma^{+}
.
∎

The importance of standard structures follows from the next result, which tells us that every densely valued
\ell
-group can be viewed as a substructure of a standard structure. In particular, this result generalises the classical result for
\ell
-groups, by letting
\cal{V}=\textnormal{Val}(\cal{G})
.

Theorem 5.3

(The Functional Representation Theorem)

Let
\cal{V}=(\cal{G},\cal{L},P)
be a densely valued
\ell
-group. Then, there is a divisible
o
-group
\Gamma
such that
\cal{V}\mathbin{\leqslant}\textnormal{Stan}\left(\Gamma^{\textnormal{PrimF}(\cal{L})}\right)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
, we have an
\ell
-group embedding
I:\cal{G}\to\Gamma^{\textnormal{PrimF}(\cal{L})}
for some divisible
o
-group
\Gamma
such that, for all
a\in\cal{G}
and
\cal{F}\in\textnormal{PrimF}(\cal{L})
,
I(a)(\cal{F})\geqslant 0
if and only if
P(a)\in\cal{F}
. Now, let
X=\textnormal{PrimF}(\cal{L})
, and define a map:

J:\cal{L}\to\{\{f\geqslant 0\}\ |\ f\in\Gamma^{X}\}:\begin{cases}P(a)\mapsto\{I(a)\geqslant 0\}\\
\bot\mapsto\emptyset.\end{cases}

We claim that
J
is a well-defined bounded lattice embedding. Hence, consider:

•

(Well-Defined and Injective) Let
a,b\in\cal{G}
. We see that:

\displaystyle P(a)=P(b)\iff

\displaystyle(\forall\cal{F}\in X)(P(a)\in\cal{F}\iff P(b)\in\cal{F})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle(\forall\cal{F}\in X)(I(a)(\cal{F})\geqslant 0\iff I(b)(\cal{F})\geqslant 0)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle J(P(a))=J(P(b)).

Hence,
J
is well-defined and injective.

•

(Meets and Joins) We see that, for
a,b\in\cal{G}
:

\displaystyle J(P(a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcap$}}}}P(b))=

\displaystyle J(P(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{I(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}b)\geqslant 0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{I(a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}I(b)\geqslant 0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{I(a)\geqslant 0\}\cap\{I(b)\geqslant 0\}

\displaystyle=

6.2.

Another major model-theoretic property enjoyed by atomless Boolean algebras is that of quantifier elimination. However, in our setup, there is some subtlety. Our language omits a symbol for Boolean negation in the lattice sort, and so we will need to expand our language by a new function symbol. However, the Shen-Weispfenning theorem doesn’t guarantee that we can eliminate group quantifiers in this new language. Thus, we need the following lemma first.

Lemma 6.5

\lbalg=\lbdlat\cup\{\lnot\}

\lbalg

Lemma 6.9

Let
\cal{V}=(\cal{G},\cal{L},P)
be an existentially closed densely valued
\ell

(1)

For each

\llgrp

\lvlgrp
-automorphism
(\phi,\psi_{\phi}):\cal{V}\to\cal{V}

\psi_{\phi}:\cal{L}\to\cal{L}:x\mapsto\begin{cases}P(\phi(-a))&a\in\cal{G}^{+}\text{ such that }P(-a)=x\\

\alpha:\mathord{\textnormal{Aut}}_{\llgrp}(\cal{G})\to\mathord{\textnormal{Aut}}_{\lvlgrp}(\cal{V})

Proof.

(1)
First, we check that
\psi_{\phi}
is well-defined. Hence, let
a,b\in\cal{G}^{+}

\displaystyle P(-a)=P(-b)\iff

\displaystyle(\forall c\in\cal{G})(a\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}c=0\iff b\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}c=0)

\displaystyle(\forall c\in\cal{H})(\phi(a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}c=0\iff\phi(b)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}c=0)

\displaystyle P(\phi(-a))=P(\phi(-b))

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\psi_{\phi}(P(-a))=\psi_{\phi}(P(-b)).

Hence,
\psi_{\phi}
is well-defined and injective. Next, we show that
\psi_{\phi}
is a surjective bounded lattice morphism. Thus, consider:

•

For
k\in\cal{L}
, we have that there is some

such that

. As

is an automorphism, let

•

Let

\displaystyle\psi_{\phi}(P(-a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcup$}}}}P(-b))=

\displaystyle\psi_{\phi}(P((-a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}(-b)))

\displaystyle P(\phi((-a)\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}(-b)))

\displaystyle P(\phi(-a))\mathop{\text{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\sqcup$}}}}P(\phi(-b))

Finally, by construction, we have that for
a\in\cal{G}^{+}

(\phi,\psi)
is an
\lvlgrp

(2)
