Charges of supergravity
Remigiusz Durka, Jerzy Kowalski-Glikman, Rene Payne

Abstract
We study conserved charges of 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 supergravity formulated as a constrained BF theory based on the 𝖮𝖲𝗉 ⁡ ( 1 | 4 ) \mathsf{OSp}(1|4) superalgebra. Using the covariant phase space formalism, we derive bulk and boundary contributions to the symplectic structure and construct charges associated with Lorentz transformations, supersymmetry, translations, and diffeomorphisms. We show that the algebra of boundary charges reproduces the expected superalgebra, while translational charges vanish on-shell due to the super-torsion constraint, leaving Lorentz and supersymmetry as the non-trivial generators.



1
Introduction

The idea of describing gravity as a gauge theory has attracted sustained interest over the years, as it offers a promising route toward reconciling General Relativity with the framework of quantum field theory. Among the various proposals, the MacDowell–Mansouri formalism
MacDowell:1977jt
is particularly notable for its conceptual clarity and elegance. In this approach, gravity with a cosmological constant is reformulated as a gauge theory of the (Anti-)de Sitter group
\mathsf{SO}(2,3)
or
\mathsf{SO}(1,4)
. One of its key features is that it unifies the tetrad and the spin connection into a single connection one-form, allowing spacetime symmetries to be treated in a fully gauge-theoretic manner.

A powerful and closely related perspective emerges when this construction is expressed in the language of BF theories. Although pure BF theory is topological and does not contain local degrees of freedom, it can be appropriately modified — either through constraints or deformations — to reproduce General Relativity
Plebanski:1977zz
;
Smolin:2003qu
;
Freidel:2005ak
. This formulation is especially appealing because of its deep connections with spin foam models and Loop Quantum Gravity. Moreover, it naturally accommodates the Barbero–Immirzi parameter, which plays an important role in quantum theory
Holst:1995pc
;
Durka:2011yv
. Extending this framework further to include supersymmetry leads to supergravity, where one gains, among other things, a more systematic and robust definition of conserved charges.

In earlier work
Durka:2009pf
(see also
Eder:2021rgt
), it was demonstrated that
\mathcal{N}=1
supergravity with a negative cosmological constant can be formulated as a constrained BF theory based on the superalgebra
\mathsf{OSp}(1|4)
. This construction can be viewed as a supersymmetric extension of the Freidel–Smolin–Starodubtsev model
Smolin:2003qu
;
Freidel:2005ak
, and it reproduces the standard supergravity action supplemented by additional topological terms, such as the Euler, Pontryagin and Nieh–Yan, and the Holst contribution.

More recently, there has been renewed interest in the role of boundaries in gravitational theories, particularly in light of the “corner symmetry” proposal
Donnelly:2016auv
;
Freidel:2020xyx
;
Freidel:2020svx
;
Freidel:2020ayo
(for reviews, see, e.g.,
Ciambelli:2022vot
;
Assanioussi:2023jyq
;
Speziale:2025lkm
). The central idea behind this approach is that, in the presence of boundaries, gauge redundancies cease to be mere redundancies: instead, they give rise to genuine physical symmetries acting on the boundary phase space. The surface charges associated with these symmetries form an algebra that captures important dynamical and thermodynamical properties of spacetime, including, for instance, aspects related to black hole entropy. While this framework has been successfully developed for purely bosonic gravity
Durka:2021ftc
, its extension to supersymmetric theories remains largely unexplored and constitutes an important open problem.

In this paper, we take a step in this direction by systematically deriving the conserved charges of
\mathsf{OSp}(1|4)
supergravity formulated as a constrained BF theory. Our main objective is to better understand the symplectic structure of the theory in the presence of boundaries and to compute the algebra of charges associated with local Lorentz transformations, diffeomorphisms, and supersymmetry.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we review the formulation of supergravity as a constrained BF theory. We introduce the
\mathsf{OSp}(1|4)
connection and its curvature, construct the action including the Immirzi parameter, and derive the equations of motion for both the fundamental fields and the auxiliary
\mathbb{B}
fields. Section 3 is devoted to a detailed analysis of the symmetries of the theory, including the transformation laws of the component fields under supersymmetry, Lorentz transformations, and diffeomorphisms. In Sections 4 and 5, we apply the covariant phase space formalism to derive the symplectic potential, carefully separating bulk and corner contributions, and thereby identifying the relevant boundary charges. Finally, in Section 6, we compute the algebra of these corner charges and explicitly demonstrate that the Poisson brackets of the supercharges close, reproducing the expected superalgebra at the boundary.

Supergravity as a constrained BF theory

It is well known that gravity can be formulated as a gauge theory of the Poincaré group in flat spacetime and of
\mathsf{SO}(2,3)
or
\mathsf{SO}(1,4)
in the presence of a negative or positive cosmological constant, respectively. Similarly, supergravity

\mathsf{OSp}(1|4)

2.1
Connection, covariant derivative, and curvature

The gauge field
\mathbb{A}
is a one-form valued in a Lie algebra or superalgebra. In the case at hand, when the gauge (super)algebra is
\mathsf{OSp}(1|4)
(see Appendix
A
for this algebra (anti)commutators and Appendix
B
for conventions concerning Dirac
\gamma
-matrices), the gauge field decomposes into the Lorentz connection
\omega^{ab}
associated with Lorentz generators, the tetrad
e^{a}
associated with translations, and the gravitino
\psi
associated with the supersymmetry generators. Because canonically the gauge field (one-form) is dimensionless while the dimension of gravitino is
[-1/2]
we introduce the compensating constant
\kappa
of dimension
[1/2]
defined by

\kappa^{2}=\frac{4\pi G}{\ell}\,,

(1)

to make the dimensions right and get the factor 1/2 in front of the gravitino kinetic term. In this formula
G
is Newton’s constant, and
\ell
is a length scale related to the cosmological constant
-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}=\frac{\Lambda}{3}
. Similarly, with the help of
\ell
, we also assure the correct dimension in the translational part of the connection, the tetrad.

The gauge field
Durka:2012wd
is then written as

\mathbb{A}=\frac{1}{2}A^{IJ}M_{IJ}+\kappa\bar{\psi}^{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\omega^{ab}M_{ab}+\frac{1}{\ell}e^{a}P_{a}+\kappa\bar{\psi}^{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}\,.

(2)

Accordingly the (super)curvature

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}=d\mathbb{A}-\frac{i}{2}[[\mathbb{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}{{,}}\,\mathbb{A}]]\,,

(3)

splits into bosonic and fermionic parts

\mathbb{F}=\frac{1}{2}\,F^{(s)}{}^{IJ}\,M_{IJ}+\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\,F^{(s)}{}^{ab}\,M_{ab}+F^{(s)}{}^{a}\,P_{a}+\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}\,.

(4)

In the formula (
3
) we denote by
[[\ast\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}{{,}}\,\ast]]
the bracket of two
\mathsf{OSp}(1|4)
-valued forms. This bracket becomes anticommutator for two fermions and commutator otherwise.

The bosonic (super)curvatures associated with local Lorentz and translational symmetries are

\displaystyle F^{(s)}{}^{ab}=F^{ab}-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}\,\bar{\psi}\wedge\gamma^{ab}\psi\,,\qquad F^{(s)a}\equiv F^{(s)a4}=F^{a}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}\,\bar{\psi}\wedge\gamma^{a}\psi\,,

(5)

with the AdS curvature
F^{ab}=R^{ab}+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}e^{a}\wedge e^{b}
and the torsion
T^{a}=\ell F^{a}=D^{\omega}e^{a}
, where the Lorentzian covariant differential is

\displaystyle D^{\omega}e^{a}=de^{a}+\omega^{a}{}_{b}\wedge e^{b}\,.

(6)

The fermionic curvature
\mathcal{F}
can be defined with the help of a covariant differential

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{A}\bar{\psi}

\displaystyle=d\bar{\psi}-\frac{1}{4}\omega^{ab}\wedge\bar{\psi}\,\gamma_{ab}-\frac{1}{2\ell}e^{a}\wedge\bar{\psi}\,\gamma_{a}\,,\qquad\mathcal{D}^{A}\psi=d\psi+\frac{1}{4}\omega^{ab}\wedge\gamma_{ab}\,\psi+\frac{1}{2\ell}e^{a}\wedge\gamma_{a}\,\psi\,,

(7)

where we identify

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\omega}\psi

\displaystyle=d\psi+\frac{1}{4}\omega^{ab}\wedge\gamma_{ab}\psi\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\omega}\bar{\psi}

\displaystyle=d\bar{\psi}-\frac{1}{4}\omega^{ab}\wedge\bar{\psi}\gamma_{ab}\,.

(8)

Then the fermionic curvatures are given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=\kappa\mathcal{D}^{A}\psi=\kappa\left(\mathcal{D}^{\omega}\psi+\frac{1}{2\ell}e^{a}\wedge\gamma_{a}\psi\right)\,,

(9)

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=\kappa\mathcal{D}^{A}\bar{\psi}=\kappa\left(\mathcal{D}^{\omega}\bar{\psi}-\frac{1}{2\ell}e^{a}\wedge\bar{\psi}\gamma_{a}\right)\,.

(10)

Below it will be convenient to use the complete covariant differential associated with connection (
2
) that acts on gauge (super)algebra-valued
n
-forms

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\mathbb{A}}(\ast)=d(\ast)-i[[\mathbb{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}{{,}}(\ast)]]\,.

(11)

One can check that

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}=-i[[{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{A}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}{{,}}{\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{A}}]]\,.

(12)

It follows directly from (
11
) and (
12
) that variation of the curvature can be expressed as a covariant derivative of variation of the gauge potential,

\displaystyle\delta\mathbb{F}(\mathbb{A})={\mathbb{D}}^{\mathbb{A}}\delta\mathbb{A}\,.

(13)

It follows also that the curvature satisfies Bianchi identity

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\mathbb{A}}\mathbb{F}=0\,,

(14)

which decomposes into

\displaystyle D^{A}F^{(s)IJ}+\kappa\bar{\psi}\wedge\gamma^{IJ}\mathcal{F}=0\,,

(15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{A}\bar{\mathcal{F}}+\kappa\bar{\psi}\wedge\gamma_{IJ}F^{(s)IJ}=0\,.

(16)

Decomposing the connection into Lorentz, translational and supersymmetric parts (see
Andrianopoli:2014aqa
) we can rewrite these equations as

\displaystyle D^{\omega}F^{(s)ab}+\frac{1}{\ell}\,e^{a}\wedge F^{(s)b}-\frac{1}{\ell}\,e^{b}\wedge F^{(s)a}+\kappa\bar{\psi}\wedge\gamma^{ab}\mathcal{F}=0\,,

(17)

\displaystyle D^{\omega}F^{(s)a}-\frac{1}{\ell}\,e_{b}\wedge F^{(s)ab}-\kappa\bar{\psi}\wedge\gamma^{a}\mathcal{F}=0\,,

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\omega}\bar{\mathcal{F}}-\frac{1}{2\ell}e^{a}\,\wedge\bar{\mathcal{F}}\,\gamma_{a}+\kappa\bar{\psi}\wedge\gamma_{ab}F^{(s)ab}+2\kappa\bar{\psi}\wedge\gamma_{a}F^{(s)a}=0\,.

(19)

Also note the useful identity
\mathcal{D}^{\omega}\mathcal{D}^{\omega}\psi=\frac{1}{4}R^{ab}(\omega)\wedge\gamma_{ab}\psi

With these definitions, we can now proceed to define the BF action for supergravity.

3
Symmetries

The infinitesimal gauge transformations of the gauge field are defined in terms of the covariant derivative

\delta_{\Upsilon}\mathbb{A}_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\Upsilon-i[\mathbb{A}_{\mu},\Upsilon]\equiv\mathbb{D}^{\mathbb{A}}_{\mu}\Upsilon\,,

(36)

where the gauge parameter
\Upsilon
decomposes into parameters of
local Lorentz, translation, and supercharge symmetries

\Upsilon=\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{IJ}M_{IJ}+\bar{\epsilon}^{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{ab}M_{ab}+\zeta^{a}P_{a}+\bar{\epsilon}^{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}\,.

(37)

3.1
Supersymmetry

Using the above formula one can immediately derive the supersymmetry transformations

\delta_{\epsilon}e^{a}=-\ell\kappa\,\bar{\epsilon}\,\gamma^{a}\,\psi\,,\qquad\delta_{\epsilon}\omega^{ab}=\kappa\,\bar{\epsilon}\,\gamma^{ab}\,\psi\,,\qquad\delta_{\epsilon}\bar{\psi}=\frac{1}{\kappa}(\mathcal{D}^{\omega}\bar{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{2\ell}e^{a}\bar{\epsilon}\gamma_{a})\,,

(38)

where
\mathcal{D}^{\omega}\bar{\epsilon}=d\bar{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{4}\omega^{ab}\,\bar{\epsilon}\gamma_{ab}\,.

The supersymmetry transformations of the curvatures can be easily obtained from

\delta\mathbb{F}_{\mu\nu}=\mathbb{D}_{\mu}\delta\mathbb{A}_{\nu}-\mathbb{D}_{\nu}\delta\mathbb{A}_{\mu}=[\mathbb{D}_{\mu},\mathbb{D}_{\nu}]\Upsilon=i[\Upsilon,\mathbb{F}_{\mu\nu}]\,,

(39)

therefore we have

\delta_{\epsilon}F^{(s)a}=-\bar{\epsilon}\gamma^{a}{\mathcal{F}}\,,\qquad\delta_{\epsilon}F^{(s)ab}=\bar{\epsilon}\gamma^{ab}{\mathcal{F}}\,,\qquad\delta_{\epsilon}\bar{\mathcal{F}}=-\frac{1}{4}\bar{\epsilon}\gamma^{ab}F^{(s)}_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\epsilon}\gamma_{a}F^{(s)a}\,.

(40)

One can further show that the first two terms of the Lagrangian (
24
) are invariant under local supersymmetry if the components of the field
{\mathbb{B}}=(B^{(s)},{\mathcal{B}})
transform as

\delta_{\epsilon}B^{(s)a}=-\bar{\epsilon}\gamma^{a}{\mathcal{B}}\,,\qquad\delta_{\epsilon}B^{(s)ab}=\bar{\epsilon}\gamma^{ab}{\mathcal{B}}\,,\qquad\delta_{\epsilon}\bar{\mathcal{B}}=-\frac{1}{4}\bar{\epsilon}\gamma^{ab}B^{(s)}_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\epsilon}\gamma_{a}B^{(s)a}\,.

(41)

However, the gauge breaking term in (
23
),

-\frac{\alpha}{4}\,\epsilon_{abcd}\,B^{(s)ab}\wedge B^{(s)cd}-{\alpha}\,\bar{\mathcal{B}}\wedge\gamma^{5}{\mathcal{B}}\,,

(42)

is not invariant under supersymmetry transformations given in (
41
). Indeed its variation is

\alpha B^{(s)a}\wedge\bar{\epsilon}\gamma_{a}\gamma^{5}{\mathcal{B}}\,,

(43)

and does not vanish. As it was shown in
Durka:2009pf
the action obtained after solving the algebraic field equations for
B
and
\mathcal{B}
is nevertheless invariant under local supersymmetry when one employs the
1.5
formalism. The very reason is that the term (
43

\omega

Translations and Lorentz transformations

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\omega^{ab}

\displaystyle=D^{\omega}\lambda^{ab}\,,\qquad\frac{1}{\ell}\delta_{\lambda}e^{a}=-\frac{1}{\ell}\lambda^{a}_{\;b}\,e^{b}\,,\qquad\delta_{\lambda}\bar{\psi}=-\frac{1}{4}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{ab}\lambda^{ab}\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}(e^{a}\,\zeta^{b}-e^{b}\,\zeta^{a})\,,\qquad\frac{1}{\ell}\delta_{\zeta}e^{a}=D^{\omega}\zeta^{a}\,,\qquad\delta_{\zeta}\bar{\psi}=-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{a}\zeta^{a}\,,

3.3
Diffeomorphisms

We now turn to diffeomorphisms generated by a vector field
\xi^{\mu}
. The transformation laws are given by the action of the Lie derivative,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}(\ast)=\xi\lrcorner(d\,\ast)+d(\xi\lrcorner\,\ast)\,,

(51)

and read

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}A_{\mu}^{IJ}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A^{IJ}_{\mu}\,,

(52)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}\psi_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\psi_{\mu}\,,

(53)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}B_{\mu\nu}^{(s)IJ}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}B_{\mu\nu}^{(s)IJ}=\xi^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}B_{\mu\nu}^{(s)IJ}+B_{\rho\nu}^{(s)IJ}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\rho}+B_{\mu\rho}^{(s)IJ}\partial_{\nu}\xi^{\rho}\,,

(54)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}\mathcal{B}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\mathcal{B}_{\mu\nu}=\xi^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\mathcal{B}_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{B}_{\rho\nu}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\rho}+\mathcal{B}_{\mu\rho}\partial_{\nu}\xi^{\rho}\,.

(55)

The Lorentz connection, tetrad, and gravitino transform as follows

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}\omega_{\mu}^{ab}

\displaystyle=\xi^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\omega_{\mu}^{ab}+\omega_{\nu}^{ab}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\,,

(56)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}e_{\mu}^{a}

\displaystyle=\xi^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}e_{\mu}^{a}+e_{\nu}^{a}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\,,

(57)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}\psi_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\xi^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\psi_{\mu}+\psi_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\,.

(58)

In the differential-form notation, these transformations take the compact form

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}\omega^{ab}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\omega^{ab}

\displaystyle=\xi\lrcorner(d\,\omega^{ab})+d(\xi\lrcorner\,\omega^{ab})\,,

(59)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}e^{a}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}e^{a}

\displaystyle=\xi\lrcorner(d\,e^{a})+d(\xi\lrcorner\,e^{a})\,,

(60)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}\psi=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\psi

\displaystyle=\xi\lrcorner(d\,\psi)+d(\xi\lrcorner\,\psi)\,.

(61)

Later we will use the helpful purely bosonic identity
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A^{IJ}=\xi\lrcorner F(A)^{IJ}+D^{A}(\xi\lrcorner A^{IJ})
extended later to the supersymmetric version and its fermionic analog
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\psi=\xi\lrcorner\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{D}^{A}(\xi\lrcorner\psi)

4
Charges of supergravity

In the following sections, we generalize the results of
Durka:2021ftc
to the case
of supergravity. We first consider the symplectic structure in the bulk and at the corner and then we construct the conserved charges associated with local symmetries of the action, Lorentz symmetry, supersymmetry, and general coordinate invariance. Although the translational symmetry is not, strictly speaking, a symmetry of the action we can still construct the associated charges that, as it will turn out, vanish. Finally, we will discuss the algebra of symmetries and the associated charges.

The symplectic potential density
\theta

16\pi\,\delta S=\int_{M}d\theta+\ldots

(62)

where
"\ldots"
denotes terms that vanish when the field equations are
satisfied. Since the symplectic potential density
\theta

\Sigma

\displaystyle\Theta=\int_{\Sigma}\theta\,.

(63)

In the case of the action (

6
Algebra of charges

Let us now consider the charge algebra. We can compute the algebra of corner charges (
74
)-(
76
) using the fact that by construction the charges generate infinitesimal transformations through the Poisson
bracket (see e.g.,
Harlow:2019yfa
;
Ciambelli:2022vot
;
Speziale:2025lkm
)

\left\{\mathcal{H}^{S}[\Xi],(\ast)\right\}=\delta_{\Xi}(\ast)\,.

(86)

The Poisson bracket of two charges can be expressed as a variation of the charge

\left\{\mathcal{H}^{S}[\Xi_{1}],\mathcal{H}^{S}[\Xi_{2}]\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{\Xi_{1}}\mathcal{H}^{S}[\Xi_{2}]-\delta_{\Xi_{2}}\mathcal{H}^{S}[\Xi_{1}]\right)\,.

(87)

We first consider the algebra of charges associated with local gauge symmetries, Lorentz, translational, and supersymmetry with the infinitesimal parameters (
\lambda^{ab},\zeta^{a},\epsilon_{\alpha}
). We assume that the gauge parameters are field-independent.

We use the following notation for the corner charges with labels referring to: Lorentz (L), translation (T) and supercharge (SUSY):

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{ab}\lambda^{ab}\,,

(88)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{a}\zeta^{a}\,,

(89)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon]

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{16\pi}\int_{S}\bar{\mathcal{B}}\epsilon\,.

(90)

6.1
Lorentz with Lorentz:
\lambda
with
\lambda

For two Lorentz charges we have

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{2}]\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{\lambda_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{2}]-\delta_{\lambda_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}]\right)\,.

(91)

Using

\delta_{\lambda}B^{(s)}_{ab}=-\lambda_{a}{}^{c}B^{(s)}_{cb}-\lambda_{b}{}^{c}B^{(s)}_{ac}\,,

(92)

and

\lambda_{12}^{ab}=\lambda_{1}{}^{a}{}_{c}\lambda_{2}^{cb}-\lambda_{2}{}^{a}{}_{c}\lambda_{1}^{cb}\,,

(93)

one finds

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{2}]\right\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{ab}\,\lambda_{12}^{ab}=\mathcal{H}_{L}\big[\lambda_{12}\big]\,.

(94)

Thus, the Poisson bracket algebra of Lorentz charges reproduces the Lorentz subalgebra.

6.2
Lorentz with translation:
\lambda
with
\zeta

Next, consider the mixed Lorentz–translation bracket,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{2}]\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{\lambda_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{2}]-\delta_{\zeta_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}]\right)\,.

(95)

Using

\delta_{\lambda}B^{(s)}_{a}=-\lambda_{a}{}^{b}B^{(s)}_{b},\qquad\delta_{\zeta}B^{(s)}_{ab}=B^{(s)}_{a}\zeta_{b}-B^{(s)}_{b}\zeta_{a}\,,

(96)

we obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{2}]-\delta_{\zeta_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}]=-\frac{4}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{a}\,\lambda_{1\,b}{}^{a}\zeta_{2}^{b}\,.

(97)

Hence,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{2}]\right\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{a}\,(\lambda_{1\,b}{}^{a}\zeta_{2}^{b})\,.

(98)

This is again of the form of a translation charge, with transformed parameter

\zeta_{12}^{a}=\lambda_{1\,b}{}^{a}\zeta_{2}^{b}\,,

(99)

so that

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{2}]\right\}=-\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{a}\,\zeta_{12}^{a}=\mathcal{H}_{T}\big[\zeta_{12}\big].

(100)

6.3
Lorentz with supersymmetry:
\lambda
with
\epsilon

Finally, for the mixed Lorentz–supersymmetry bracket, we use

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{\lambda_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]-\delta_{\epsilon_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}]\right).

(101)

The relevant transformations are

\delta_{\lambda}\bar{\mathcal{B}}=\frac{1}{4}\,\bar{\mathcal{B}}\gamma_{ab}\lambda^{ab},\qquad\delta_{\epsilon}B^{(s)}_{ab}=\bar{\epsilon}\,\gamma_{ab}\,\mathcal{B}\,.

(102)

A short computation, using the Majorana flip identity, gives

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]-\delta_{\epsilon_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}]=-\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}\bar{\mathcal{B}}\,\gamma_{ab}\lambda_{1}^{ab}\epsilon_{2}\,,

(103)

and therefore

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]\right\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{16\pi}\int_{S}\bar{\mathcal{B}}\,(\frac{1}{4}\gamma_{ab}\lambda_{1}^{ab}\epsilon_{2})\,.

(104)

This is again of the form of a supersymmetry charge, with transformed parameter

\epsilon_{12}=\frac{1}{4}\,\lambda_{1}^{ab}\gamma_{ab}\epsilon_{2},

(105)

so that

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]\right\}=\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}\big[\epsilon_{12}\big].

(106)

6.4
Translation with translation:
\zeta
with
\zeta

For two translational charges we have

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{2}]\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{\zeta_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{2}]-\delta_{\zeta_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{1}]\right)\,,

(107)

and the required transformation is

\delta_{\zeta}B^{(s)}_{a}=B^{(s)}_{ab}\,\zeta^{b}.

(108)

It follows that

\displaystyle\delta_{\zeta_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{2}]-\delta_{\zeta_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{1}]=-\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{ac}(\zeta_{1}^{c}\zeta_{2}^{a}-\zeta_{2}^{c}\zeta_{1}^{a})\,.

(109)

Hence

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{2}]\right\}=-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{ab}\,\left(-(\zeta_{1}^{a}\zeta_{2}^{b}-\zeta_{2}^{a}\zeta_{1}^{b})\right).

(110)

Defining

\lambda_{12}^{ab}:=-(\zeta_{1}^{a}\zeta_{2}^{b}-\zeta_{2}^{a}\zeta_{1}^{b})\,,

(111)

allows us to write

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{2}]\right\}=\mathcal{H}_{L}\big[\lambda_{12}\big].

(112)

6.5
Translation with supersymmetry:
\zeta
with
\epsilon

Let us consider the mixed translation–supersymmetry bracket

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{\zeta_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]-\delta_{\epsilon_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{1}]\right).

(113)

Using

\delta_{\zeta}\bar{\mathcal{B}}=\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{\mathcal{B}}\gamma_{a}\zeta^{a},\qquad\delta_{\epsilon}B^{(s)}_{a}=-\bar{\epsilon}\,\gamma_{a}\mathcal{B},

(114)

we obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{\zeta_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{16\pi}\int_{S}\left(\delta_{\zeta_{1}}\bar{\mathcal{B}}\right)\epsilon_{2}=-\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}\bar{\mathcal{B}}\gamma_{a}\zeta_{1}^{a}\epsilon_{2}\,,

(115)

and

\displaystyle\delta_{\epsilon_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{1}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}\left(\delta_{\epsilon_{2}}B^{(s)}_{a}\right)\zeta_{1}^{a}=\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}\bar{\epsilon}_{2}\gamma_{a}\mathcal{B}\,\zeta_{1}^{a}.

(116)

Using the Majorana flip identity

\bar{\epsilon}_{2}\gamma_{a}\mathcal{B}=-\bar{\mathcal{B}}\gamma_{a}\epsilon_{2}\,,

(117)

we find

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]\right\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{16\pi}\int_{S}\bar{\mathcal{B}}(\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{a}\epsilon_{2}\zeta_{1}^{a})\,.

(118)

This is again of the form of a supersymmetry charge. Defining

\epsilon_{12}=\frac{1}{2}\,\zeta_{1}^{a}\gamma_{a}\epsilon_{2}\,,

(119)

we can write

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]\right\}=\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}\big[\epsilon_{12}\big].

(120)

6.6
Supersymmetry with supersymmetry:
\epsilon
with
\epsilon

Let us now finally consider the bracket of two supersymmetry charges,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{\epsilon_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]-\delta_{\epsilon_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{1}]\right).

(121)

Using

\delta_{\epsilon}\bar{\mathcal{B}}=-\frac{1}{4}\,\bar{\epsilon}\,\gamma^{ab}B^{(s)}_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{\epsilon}\,\gamma^{a}B^{(s)}_{a},

(122)

we obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{\epsilon_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}\left(\bar{\epsilon}_{1}\gamma^{ab}\epsilon_{2}\right)B^{(s)}_{ab}+\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}\left(\bar{\epsilon}_{1}\gamma^{a}\epsilon_{2}\right)B^{(s)}_{a},

(123)

\displaystyle\delta_{\epsilon_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{1}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}\left(\bar{\epsilon}_{2}\gamma^{ab}\epsilon_{1}\right)B^{(s)}_{ab}+\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}\left(\bar{\epsilon}_{2}\gamma^{a}\epsilon_{1}\right)B^{(s)}_{a}.

(124)

After using the standard Majorana identities for the bilinears, the bracket becomes

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]\right\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{ab}\left(\bar{\epsilon}_{1}\gamma^{ab}\epsilon_{2}\right)-\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{a}\left(\bar{\epsilon}_{1}\gamma^{a}\epsilon_{2}\right).

(125)

Therefore

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]\right\}=\mathcal{H}_{L}\big[\lambda_{12}\big]+\mathcal{H}_{T}\big[\zeta_{12}\big],

(126)

with composite parameters

\lambda_{12}^{ab}=\bar{\epsilon}_{1}\gamma^{ab}\epsilon_{2},\qquad\zeta_{12}^{a}=\bar{\epsilon}_{1}\gamma^{a}\epsilon_{2}\,.

(127)

6.7
Summary of algebra of (gauge) charges and the on-shell reduction

By the explicit calculation we thus convinced ourselves that the algebra of Poisson brackets of the Noether charges associated with local gauge symmetries represents the original supersymmetry algebra of
\mathsf{OSp}(1|4)
.

Collecting the results obtained above, the gauge part of the corner charge algebra can be summarized as

\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}\{\cdot,\cdot\}&\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{2}]&\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{2}]&\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]\\
\hline\cr\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}]&\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{12}]&\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{12}]&\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{12}]\\
\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{1}]&-&\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{12}]&\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{12}]\\
\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{1}]&-&-&\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{12}]+\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{12}]\end{array}

where in each entry the composite parameters
\lambda_{12}
,
\zeta_{12}
and
\epsilon_{12}
are the ones defined in the corresponding subsections. The lower-triangular part is omitted since it is determined by antisymmetry of the Poisson bracket.

Before turning to diffeomorphisms, let us comment on the meaning of the above algebra on-shell. From the algebraic field equation for the translational component of the
\mathbb{B}
-field we have

B^{(s)}_{a}=\frac{1}{\beta}F^{(s)}_{a},

(128)

which is the so-called super-torsion. The vanishing of the super-torsion due to field equations (as shown at the end of Appendix
C
) implies that the translational corner charge is weakly vanishing,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta]=-\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{a}\zeta^{a}=-\frac{2}{16\pi\beta}\int_{S}F^{(s)}_{a}\zeta^{a}\thickapprox 0\,.

(129)

Thus, while the translation sector is present in the formal gauge-charge algebra, it does not define an independent non-trivial corner charge on the physical shell. In particular, the translational contribution appearing in the supersymmetry bracket drops out weakly on-shell, and one finds

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{12}]+\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{12}]

\displaystyle\thickapprox\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{12}]\,.

(130)

It is therefore natural to quotient the weakly vanishing translation charge out of the algebra. The remaining non-trivial on-shell corner algebra is then generated by the Lorentz and supersymmetry charges alone and takes the form

\begin{array}[]{c|cc}\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\text{on-shell}}&\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{2}]&\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]\\
\hline\cr\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}]&\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{12}]&\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{12}]\\
\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{1}]&-&\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{12}]\end{array}

which is the non-trivial internal gauge algebra that survives after imposing the equations of motion. In this sense, the translational part of the formal gauge algebra is pure gauge on-shell, whereas the Lorentz and supersymmetry charges remain as genuine corner charges.

As we will see in the next subsection, the brackets involving diffeomorphisms provide the complementary part of the full boundary algebra.

6.8
Algebra of diffeomorphism charges

The diffeomorphism charge we found was

\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi]=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}\left(B^{(s)}_{IJ}\xi\lrcorner A^{IJ}+4\kappa\,\bar{\mathcal{B}}\xi\lrcorner\psi\right),

(131)

where
\xi
is tangent to the corner
S
.

6.8.1
Diffeomorphism with diffeomorphism:
\xi
with
\xi

We first compute the bracket of two diffeomorphism charges. Using

\{\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi_{1}],(\ast)\}=\delta_{\xi_{1}}(\ast)=\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}}(\ast)\,,

(132)

we obtain

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi_{2}]\}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\xi_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi_{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}}B^{(s)}_{IJ}\xi_{2}\lrcorner A^{IJ}+B^{(s)}_{IJ}\xi_{2}\lrcorner\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}}A^{IJ}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{4}{16\pi}\int_{S}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}}\bar{\mathcal{B}}\xi_{2}\lrcorner\psi+\bar{\mathcal{B}}\xi_{2}\lrcorner\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}}\psi\right).

(133)

Using the identity

\xi_{2}\lrcorner\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}}(*)=\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}}(\xi_{2}\lrcorner\,*)-[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]\lrcorner\,(*)\,,

(134)

we can rewrite this as

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi_{2}]\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}}\!\left(B^{(s)}_{IJ}\xi_{2}\lrcorner A^{IJ}+4\,\bar{\mathcal{B}}\xi_{2}\lrcorner\psi\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}\left(B^{(s)}_{IJ}[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]\lrcorner A^{IJ}+4\,\bar{\mathcal{B}}[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]\lrcorner\psi\right).

(135)

The first term vanishes since
S
is closed and
\xi_{1}
is tangent to
S
. Therefore

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi_{1}],\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi_{2}]\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}\left(B^{(s)}_{IJ}[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]\lrcorner A^{IJ}+4\,\bar{\mathcal{B}}[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]\lrcorner\psi\right)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{H}_{D}[[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]]\,.

(136)

Thus, with the present sign convention for
\mathcal{H}_{D}
, the diffeomorphism charges form an anti-representation of the Lie algebra of vector fields tangent to the corner.

6.8.2
Lorentz with diffeomorphism:
\lambda
with
\xi

We now compute the mixed Lorentz–diffeomorphism bracket. Using

\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda]=-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{ab}\lambda^{ab},

(137)

we find

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi],\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda]\}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\xi}\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda]=-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}B^{(s)}_{ab}\lambda^{ab}.

(138)

Since

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\!\left(B^{(s)}_{ab}\lambda^{ab}\right)=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}B^{(s)}_{ab}\lambda^{ab}+B^{(s)}_{ab}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\lambda^{ab},

(139)

and the integral of a total Lie derivative over
S
vanishes, we get

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi],\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda]\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{ab}\ \mathcal{L}_{\xi}\lambda^{ab}=-\mathcal{H}_{L}[\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\lambda].

(140)

By antisymmetry of the Poisson bracket,

\{\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda],\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi]\}=\mathcal{H}_{L}[\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\lambda]\,.

(141)

6.8.3
Translation with diffeomorphism:
\zeta
with
\xi

For the translation charge

\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta]=-\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{a}\zeta^{a},

(142)

one similarly finds

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi],\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta]\}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\xi}\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta]=-\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}B^{(s)}_{a}\zeta^{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{16\pi}\int_{S}B^{(s)}_{a}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\zeta^{a}=-\mathcal{H}_{T}[\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\zeta].

(143)

Therefore,

\{\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta],\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi]\}=\mathcal{H}_{T}[\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\zeta]\,.

(144)

6.8.4
Supersymmetry with diffeomorphism:
\epsilon
with
\xi

Finally, for the supersymmetry charge

\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon]=-\frac{4}{16\pi}\int_{S}\bar{\mathcal{B}}\epsilon\,,

(145)

we obtain

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi],\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon]\}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\xi}\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon]=-\frac{4}{16\pi}\int_{S}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\bar{\mathcal{B}}\epsilon\,.

(146)

Using again that the total Lie derivative integrates to zero on
S
, we get

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi],\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon]\}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{16\pi}\int_{S}\bar{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\epsilon=-\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\epsilon]\,.

(147)

Hence,

\{\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon],\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi]\}=\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\epsilon]\,.

(148)

Here
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\epsilon
denotes the Lie derivative of the spinorial parameter along the corner; more precisely, one should understand it as the corresponding spinorial Lie derivative on
S
.

6.8.5
Summary

Collecting these results together with the internal gauge algebra derived above, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{c|cccc}\{\cdot,\cdot\}&\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{2}]&\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{2}]&\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{2}]&\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi_{2}]\\
\hline\cr\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{1}]&\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{12}]&\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{12}]&\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{12}]&\mathcal{H}_{L}[\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{2}}\lambda_{1}]\\
\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{1}]&-&\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{12}]&\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{12}]&\mathcal{H}_{T}[\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{2}}\zeta_{1}]\\
\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\epsilon_{1}]&-&-&\mathcal{H}_{L}[\lambda_{12}]+\mathcal{H}_{T}[\zeta_{12}]&\mathcal{H}_{SUSY}[\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{2}}\epsilon_{1}]\\
\mathcal{H}_{D}[\xi_{1}]&-&-&-&-\mathcal{H}_{D}[[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]]\end{array}

where, as before, the lower-triangular part is omitted since it is determined by antisymmetry.

On-shell, the translation charge weakly vanishes, and therefore all terms proportional to
\mathcal{H}_{T}
drop out.

7
Conclusions

In this work, we have derived and analyzed the conserved charges of
\mathcal{N}=1
supergravity formulated as a constrained BF theory, placing particular emphasis on boundary and corner contributions. Using the covariant phase space approach, we identified the relevant symplectic structure and showed how conserved charges emerge from local gauge symmetries and diffeomorphisms.

A central result is that the algebra of boundary (corner) charges reproduces the expected superalgebra structure, confirming consistency with the underlying symmetry principles of supergravity. We further demonstrated that translational charges vanish on-shell as a consequence of the super-torsion constraint, and therefore do not contribute to the physical boundary symmetry algebra. The non-trivial on-shell algebra is thus generated by Lorentz and supersymmetry charges.

We also found that incorporating diffeomorphism charges completes the boundary symmetry structure, yielding a representation of the algebra of vector fields on the boundary. Altogether, these results highlight the role of boundary degrees of freedom in supergravity and offer a step toward extending the “corner symmetry” program to supersymmetric settings.

Among the many questions opened by this work, two appear particularly pressing. First, it would be interesting to apply the extended phase space formalism
Ciambelli:2021nmv
;
Ciambelli:2022cfr
to generalize our analysis to arbitrary diffeomorphisms, beyond those tangential to the corner considered here. Second, one may explore whether the ambiguities in the definition of corner charges and the treatment of edge modes can be used to render the gauge translational charge non-vanishing. Both corner-orthogonal diffeomorphisms and gauge translational charges are physically significant, as they are related, for instance, to the energy and momentum of bulk configurations. Clarifying their interpretation, as well as the relation between them, remains an important direction for future work. These questions will be addressed in a forthcoming paper.
