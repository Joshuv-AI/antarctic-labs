 Abstract
 The magnetic flux induced in magnetic bodies of several shapes are examined. In addition the surface
 magnetic field intensity on the exterior surface of conducting objects is estimated since it is the driver
 for narrow slot aperture inductive ports of entry. In this report lower frequencies are the focus, but we
 also include the magnetic field intensity of a conducting cylinder near the first resonance and a reference
 to the high frequency limit.

1 INTRODUCTION
This short report examines the internal magnetic flux enhancements when magnetic materials are placed
in a uniform magnetic field. The magnetic materials act as magnetic antennas concentrating the captured
magnetic flux. It is worth noting that as the magnetic permeability increases the flux saturates at a value
dependent on the aspect ratio of the magnetic antenna. In this large permeability limit the boundary
condition on the exterior surface of the magnetic structure approaches a perfect magnetic conductor with
only the normal component of the magnetic field intensity at the surface.
 We examine the prolate spheroidal shape with arbitrary magnetic permeability and next the spherical
limit (as a check). We also include results for high permeability tubular cylinders using the duality of the
perfectly conducting tubes.
 We then examine the low frequency magnetic field intensity (as well as the normal electric field) on
the surface of conducting objects of several shapes. This field intensity is the driver for the inductive
penetrations by narrow slot apertures at low frequencies. Finally, we include the conductive cylinder at both
low frequencies and at the first length resonance.

4 CONCLUSIONS
This short report gives solutions for the magnetic prolate spheroid, a magnetic sphere, and a perfect magnetic
tubular cylinder. The equatorial magnetic flux, normalized by the free space flux without the object, is given
for several examples. The large permeability limits, which only depend on the geometric aspect ratios, are
compared for the various shapes.
 We also examine enhancements in the low frequency surface the exterior magnetic field intensity of
cylindrical, spherical, prolate and oblate spheroidal conductive objects which is the driver for inductive
penetrations by narrow slot apertures. Finally, we estimate the magnetic field enhancement at the midpoint
of a thin cylinder near its first length resonance and give references to the higher frequency limits.
