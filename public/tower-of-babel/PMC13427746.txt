A chromosome-level genome assembly of the chemosymbiotic species Rugalucina vietnamica (Lucinida: Lucinidae)
Authors: Yansong Wang, Yan Tang, Hao Wang, Junlong Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rugalucina vietnamica is one of the representative symbiotic bivalves in marine chemosynthesis-based ecosystems. The species is concentrated along the Beibu Gulf-Qiongzhou Strait coastal region in the western Pacific, making it an ideal model for studying the evolution and dispersal of coastal benthic fauna in the South China Sea. However, the lack of a high-quality reference genome has limited the identification of genome-wide genetic variation and hindered population genomic investigations. In this study, we report a high-quality chromosome-level genome of R. vietnamica, generated by integrating PacBio, Illumina, and high-resolution chromosome conformation capture sequencing. The final assembled genome spans 1.23 Gb, with a scaffold N50 of 78 Mb, and was successfully anchored onto 17 chromosomes. BUSCO assessment recovered 96.50% of conserved metazoan genes, indicating high completeness. Genome annotation further revealed that transposable elements account for 65.56% of the genome, and a total of 18,649 protein-coding genes were predicted.

Background & Summary

The family Lucinidae (Mollusca: Bivalvia: Lucinida) constitutes one of the most representative lineages of symbiotic bivalves in marine chemosynthesis-based ecosystems. Species within this family establish stable intracellular associations with sulfur-oxidizing bacteria, enabling their widespread occurrence in organic-rich sedimentary habitats. Through these obligate endosymbiotic partnerships, lucinids contribute to nutrient cycling and the maintenance of benthic ecosystem productivity across diverse marine habitats, ranging from shallow coastal environments to deep-sea ecosystems1–4. Extant members of Lucinidae exhibit remarkable species diversity and extensive geographic distribution. Their principal diversity center is concentrated in shallow waters of the Indo-West Pacific region5,6.

Rugalucina vietnamica is one of the representative lucinid species distributed across the northern continental shelf of the South China Sea and adjacent tropical and subtropical waters. It primarily inhabits mangrove-associated muddy intertidal flats and adjacent shallow subtidal sediments. The species is characterized by a relatively small and thin shell, and its vertical distribution spans from the upper intertidal zone to the shallow subtidal zone, exhibiting pronounced adaptation to habitat gradients7,8. The core geographic range of R. vietnamica largely overlaps with the distribution of mangrove forests and seagrass meadows in southern China, particularly along the Beibu Gulf and the Qiongzhou Strait coastal region. This spatial congruence, together with its broad bathymetric tolerance, renders the species an ideal model for investigating evolutionary processes and dispersal dynamics of coastal benthic fauna in the South China Sea.

The South China Sea and its adjacent waters experienced repeated and pronounced sea-level fluctuations during the Pleistocene, particularly the cyclical opening and closing of the Qiongzhou Strait, which exerted profound effects on regional population connectivity and genetic structure of marine organisms. Although marine environments lack conventional terrestrial physical barriers, oceanographic circulation patterns, temperature and salinity gradients, and microhabitat heterogeneity collectively form a dynamic network of biogeographic isolation. These interacting factors have generated discernible signatures of genetic differentiation across multiple benthic invertebrate lineages9,10.

Traditional population genetic analyses based on a limited number of mitochondrial or nuclear markers have been effective in resolving broad scale phylogeographic patterns. However, such marker-based approaches are inherently constrained in their capacity to disentangle complex demographic histories, detect subtle population structure, and elucidate the genomic basis of adaptive evolution. With the rapid advancement of high-throughput sequencing technologies, whole-genome resequencing has emerged as a powerful framework for investigating population genetic structure, patterns of gene flow, and signatures of natural selection in marine organisms. This approach enables the capture of extensive genome-wide variation, encompassing both neutral and non-neutral polymorphisms at an unprecedented resolution11,12. Nevertheless, population genomic inference based on resequencing data is critically dependent on the availability of a high-quality reference genome. Despite representing a major lineage of chemosymbiotic bivalves, genomic resources for Lucinidae remain extremely limited, thereby constraining comprehensive investigations into their population genetic structure and adaptive evolutionary mechanisms.

Against this background, and to establish a robust genomic framework for subsequent population genetic analyses based on whole-genome resequencing, we generated a high-quality reference genome for R. vietnamica sampled from the South China Sea. In this study, we integrated Illumina short-read sequencing, PacBio HiFi long-read sequencing, and Hi-C chromatin conformation capture technologies to produce a chromosome-level genome assembly and annotation of R. vietnamica. The final assembly spans 1.23 Gb and comprises 17 chromosomes, with a scaffold N50 of 78 Mb. Genome completeness assessment using BUSCO recovered 96.50% of conserved orthologs, indicating a high level of assembly completeness. By integrating ab initio prediction, homology-based annotation, and transcriptome evidence, followed by manual curation, we predicted 18,649 protein-coding genes. This high-quality reference genome provides a critical genomic resource for phylogenetic inference, adaptive evolutionary analyses, and the investigation of diverse molecular mechanisms underlying chemosymbiotic and ecological adaptation in Lucinidae.

Methods

Sample Collection and Sequencing

A sample of R. vietnamica was collected in April 2024 from a mangrove mudflat in Beihai, Guangxi Province, China (109.194622° E, 21.402808° N). Immediately after collection, individual was flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and subsequently stored at −80 °C until DNA extraction. The same frozen individual was used for Illumina, PacBio, and Hi-C sequencing to ensure data consistency. DNA was physically sheared into 350 bp fragments to construct a short-insert library. After bridge amplification, the library was sequenced on the Illumina platform, generating 61.3 Gb of 150 bp paired-end reads for genome size estimation. For long-read sequencing, a PacBio library was constructed from genomic DNA and sequenced to produce approximately 86.51 Gb of data, with an average read length of 14.02 kb. To generate chromosome-level scaffolding information, a Hi-C library was prepared from formaldehyde-fixed tissue. Crosslinked chromatin was digested with the restriction enzyme DpnII, followed by end repair, proximity ligation, purification, and fragment enrichment. Library concentration and insert size distribution were assessed using Qubit 2.0 fluorometry and an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer, respectively. Effective library concentration was quantified by qPCR to ensure sequencing quality. Transcriptome sequencing was performed using muscle tissue from R. vietnamica. Library quality control was conducted using Qubit 2.0 and the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer, followed by qPCR quantification. The validated libraries were sequenced on the Illumina platform (Table 1).

Genome Survey Analysis

During the genome survey stage, genomic characteristics were inferred from the distribution of sequencing reads. A 350 bp insert-size Illumina library was used to construct a 21-mer frequency distribution (k = 21). K-mer analysis was performed using Jellyfish v2.1.413. The genome size of R. vietnamica was estimated to be approximately 2.14 Gb.

De novo assembly of PacBio HiFi reads was performed using Hifiasm v0.1914. Among the assemblies generated under different parameters, the optimal assembly was selected based on genome size consistency and contig N50. Potential contaminant sequences were removed through alignment against the NCBI NT database and mitochondrial genome sequences, resulting in a clean nuclear genome assembly with a total contig length of 2.4 Gb. Assembly completeness was assessed using BUSCO v5.2.115 with the metazoa_odb10 dataset, yielding 94.13% complete orthologs (Table 2). In the Hi-C anchoring step, sequencing reads were first filtered using HiC-Pro v2.10.018. The genome sequence was partitioned, ordered, and oriented based on Hi-C data using Juicer v1.619. Specifically, the contig-scale genome was divided into 50 kb segments, Hi-C interaction data were used to correct misassemblies in the draft assembly, and scaffolding was performed to produce the final chromosome-scale assembly. After refinement, the final genome size was 1.23 Gb, BUSCO v5.2.1 indicated an assembly completeness of 96.50% based on the metazoa_odb10 dataset, with 92.80% complete and single-copy BUSCOs, suggesting that the refinement process mainly removed redundant haplotypic sequences rather than true loss of genomic content. The assembly showed a contig N50 of 19 Mb, and a scaffold N50 of 78 Mb (Table 2). Repeat Sequence Annotation

Repetitive elements in the genome were annotated using EarlGrey v7.0.120. EarlGrey is a pipeline that integrates database-based annotation with de novo approaches, making it particularly suitable for non-model organisms with limited reference repeat libraries. The workflow included genome preprocessing, de novo transposable element (TE) identification and library construction, TE sequence extension and consensus generation, genome-wide TE annotation and classification, and result formatting into standardized outputs. Protein-Coding Gene Prediction and Evaluation

Using the repeat-masked genome as input, protein-coding genes were predicted with the BRAKER3 v3.0821 pipeline, which integrates GeneMark-ETP v1.0.222, Augustus v3.1.023, and TSEBRA v1.1.2.524. This workflow combines ab initio prediction, homology-based evidence from related species, and transcriptome-based annotation to generate an integrated gene set. Homology-based annotation was conducted using the Metazoa protein database as the reference25. A total of 18,649 protein-coding genes were predicted. Assessment of genome completeness using BUSCO v5.2.1 indicated that 78.30% of conserved orthologs were recovered (Table 2).

Data Records

The raw Illumina, PacBio, Hi-C, and transcriptome sequencing data have been deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive under accession number SRP68050326. The assembled genome has been deposited in NCBI under accession number GCA_056413925.127. The genome annotation file is available in Figshare28.

Technical Validation

The final genome assembly of Rugalucina vietnamica is 1.23 Gb in size and is anchored to 17 chromosomes, which together cover 99.67% of the genome (Table 3, Fig. 1). The assembly achieved a contig N50 of 19 Mb and a scaffold N50 of 78 Mb (Table 2). Repetitive TEs comprise 65.56% of the genome (Table 4, Fig. 2). To assess assembly and annotation quality, genome completeness was evaluated using BUSCO v5.2.1 with the metazoa_odb10 database. Complete BUSCOs accounted for 96.50% of the genome assembly, whereas 78.30% were recovered in the annotated gene set (Table 2).
