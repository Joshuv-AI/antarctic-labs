Analyzing the influence of variable viscosity and thermal radiation on heat and mass transfer of MHD bioconvective nanofluid flow through surface regression model
Authors: Shantha Sheela J, David Maxim Gururaj A
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The present study explores the impacts of significant parameters on the flow characteristics through sensitivity analysis of magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) bioconvection nanofluid flow considering variable viscosity. Using the Runge-Kutta fourth-order technique, the set of governing equations is transformed into nonlinear coupled ordinary differential equations. The analysis focuses primarily on how various parameters, including thermophoresis, Brownian motion, variable viscosity, magnetic field, thermal radiation parameter, bioconvection Lewis number, bioconvection Peclet number, and others, influence the density profiles of microbes, temperature, and concentration. These profiles are detailed and graphically depicted. Finally, the parameters that have the most significant impact on the desired output are examined. The concluded results are applied in various fields, such as microfluidics and biological systems (like nutrient transport in aquatic environments), bioengineering, bio-nano coolant systems, and biofuel cells.

Introduction

The studies on bioconvection nanofluid flow are mostly applied in industrial and commercial items like biofuel and biofertilizers1. Numerous distinct metabolic characteristics of cyanobacteria and eukaryotic microalgae are beneficial for the production of biofuel2. Therefore, bioconvective phenomena can be used in many scientific and technological fields. Thus, bioconvection in nanofluid flow has attracted a lot of attention from researchers and scientists lately3. One exciting development in fluid mechanics and heat transport is the use of nanofluids. From electronics cooling to energy systems, nanofluids have the potential to improve an extensive range of applications by improving the heat transfer and thermal conductivity characteristics of base fluids. Most of the researchers have observed that the nanofluids have better effectiveness on convective heat transfer than that of base fluids. Hilledson et al.4 and Kuznetsov5 have discussed the concept of nanofluid bioconvection in detail.

The stability of a nanofluid under both oscillatory and non-oscillatory instability conditions of nanofluid flow, including oxytactic microorganisms, is demonstrated by Kuznetsov6. Additionally, magnetohydrodynamics is familiar for its technical applications, such as the cooling of nuclear reactors and cooling and heating chambers, astrophysical flows, open water reservoirs, and other environmental and economic improvements. It finds considerable usage in engineering and physics, particularly in the design of gas turbines and space technologies. Moreover, applications in geophysics, astrophysics, oil drilling, thermal recovery, and underground nuclear waste storage facilities motivated many researchers to work on this concept.

Bakier7 examined the impact of thermal radiation from a vertical surface on mixed convection in a porous material. Hossain and Takhar8 studied the forced and free convection flow and explored how radiation influences flow on issues related to heat transfer. The effects of radiation heat transfer and transverse magnetic fields were examined by Damseh9 in order to perform a numerical analysis of mixed convection in magnetohydrodynamics. In a study, Zahmatkesh10 investigated how thermal radiation causes the temperature to be distributed almost consistently throughout the vertical sections of an enclosure. Hayat et al.11 worked on magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) steady nanofluid flow through a rotating disc. Ahmad et al.12 offered an inclusive analysis of how nanoparticles move through porous medium with gyrotactic organisms over an extensive surface. In comparison to the circumstances of constant properties, viscosity plays a significant role in the flow property of fluids, and the flow characteristics undergo substantial alterations when the effects of variable viscosity are considered.

Literally, analysis of viscosity is quite significant for determining the thermofluidic behavior of heat transfer fluids. Seddeek13 registered the impacts of variable viscosity and radiation on unsteady MHD flow over a semi-infinite flat plate. Soundalgekar et al.14 investigated the impacts of variable viscosity on boundary layer flow with variable surface temperature. Mohammad et al.15 studied heat and mass transfer along a vertical cylinder with variable viscosity effects. MHD boundary layer flow with temperature-varying fluid properties has been examined by H. Konwar et al.16. The impact of mass and heat transmission in mixed convection flow with chemical reactions has been investigated by Zia Ullah17. In a porous media with varying viscosity, the rate of mass and heat transfer has been registered by A.S. Adeyemo et al.18. Mishra et al.19 worked on rotational microorganism magnetohydrodynamic nanofluid flow. Mondal et al.20 studied the entropy generation of variable viscosity and thermal radiation on magneto-nanofluid flow. Mishra et al.21 investigated the entropy optimization of various properties on MHD nanofluid flow. Mishra et al.22 studied the thermal radiation’s effect on a rotating vertical cone’s mixed convective unsteady nanofluid flow. Effects of magnetohydrodynamics on the flow of a three-dimensional nanofluid across a stretched surface in a porous medium: The effects of an unsteady mixed convection nanofluid flow were studied by Mondal et al.23. Mishra et al.24 used Sutterby nanofluid over a stretching surface with Brownian motion and a porous medium to discuss the effects of microbial activity.

Although most of the prominent results on the impact of variable viscosity have been highlighted in the previous studies, they still need more attention. The technique of sensitivity analysis is used to ascertain how changes in a model’s or system’s input parameters might be linked to variations in the model’s or system’s output. By showing which parameters have the most effects on the behavior of the system, this method supports comprehending the relationship between the model’s parameters and the outputs that are produced.

In experimental design, the Response Surface Methodology25,26, also known as the surface regression model, is a useful technique for analyzing problems where a response is influenced by several variables that optimize the response and assists in establishing a list of experimental designs for response prediction. In order to study a particular model term or interaction, it could be appropriate to modify the theoretical constraints.

An important statistical method for determining the variability of specific variables’ performance in relation to the RSM model is ANOVA analysis, often known as the analysis of variance. The ANOVA analysis synthesis criterion is used to evaluate the accuracy of the RSM model using the following computing estimators: degree of freedom (DOF), sum of squares, adjusted mean square, F-value, and P-value. In addition to modeling fitness, an attempt would likely be made to explain the influence of parameters.

Vahedi et al.27 registered sensitivity analysis on MHD forced convection of a Cu-water nanofluid flow across a wedge. Md. Faisal Md. Basir28 examined the sensitivity analysis of MHD nanofluid flow with binary chemical reaction. Priyadharshini M et al.29 examined the numerical and sensitivity study on MHD blood flow. Nonlinear radiative flow of a hybrid nanofluid across a stretching cylinder has been investigated numerically by Mohammed Ismail et al.30. Shantha Sheela J et al.31 studied bioconvection nanofluid flow considering nonlinear thermal radiation using response surface methodology. P MN, N SK32 registered the detailed study of two-dimensional Casson fluid flow on a stretching surface that is stable and embedded with trimetallic nanoparticles suspended in engine oil. S. Znaidia et al.33 examined the sensitivity analysis of optimizing radiative heat transfer in magnified radiative nanofluid flow past a melting surface with the effects of thermal conductivity and effective viscosity. Farooq et al. al.34 studied the flow characteristics and thermal performance in dihydrogen oxide-based nanofluid performance in PTSC. Farooq et al.35 investigates the unsteady flow of a hybrid nanofluid over a spinning disk in a porous medium under the influence of viscous diffusion.

Motivated by the preceding studies, the authors intend to investigate MHD bioconvection nanofluid flow with variable viscosity effects, and sensitivity analysis is utilized to comprehend how different elements affect the system’s performance. Tables and graphs are demonstrated, and the results are discussed in detail for various pertinent parameters. Such research is novel and has not yet been accomplished, according to the best extensive research to date.

Novelty of the research work

The present study’s novelty is the implementation of nanoparticle-volume-fraction-dependent viscosity into the MHD nanofluid bioconvection model. Unlike previous constant-viscosity or temperature-dependent viscosity techniques, the current formulation adopts a ϕ-dependent viscosity model that introduces a direct and nonlinear connection between the momentum and concentration equations.

The impact of parameters such as thermal radiation parameter (Rd), mixed convection parameter (λ), surface temperature parameter (q), Lewis number (Le), magnetic field parameter (M), variable viscosity parameter (Nv), bioconvection Rayleigh number (Rb), bioconvection Lewis number (Lb), and bioconvection Peclet number (Pe) on velocity, temperature, concentration, and microorganism density is extensively investigated, with suitable physical explanations provided by tables and graphs.

The results are calculated using the parameters’ estimated values:

Impact of magnetic field and mixed convection parameter on velocity profile

The velocity declines for the rising value of the magnetic field parameter, and this occurs because a magnetic field generates Lorentz force, which opposes motion and reduces it; hence, a stronger magnetic field results in a lower velocity, as shown in Fig. 2a. The velocity then rises again as bioconvective and buoyant forces become dominant, particularly away from the wall. As the mixed convection parameter increases, the velocity profile also rises. The basis for this phenomenon is that whenever buoyancy on free convection becomes significant, mixed convection happens. The buoyancy becomes increased as the mixed convection parameter value rises. Hence, the velocity of the flow increases. However, when buoyancy becomes too strong, it causes flow stratification and increased resistance; hence, velocity drops again as shown in 2b.Fig. 2Velocity profile for different values of the parameters: (a) Magnetic field and (b) mixed convection.

The Fig. 3a shows that the temperature profile consequently increases for the rising value of the thermophoresis parameter. This occurs as a result of the movement of smaller particles during the thermophoresis process from a hotter to a cooler region, or from a high-temperature segment to a lower-temperature portion. Consequently, heat transfers rapidly from the hotter surface to the fluid, boosting its temperature. Figure 3b displays that for the boosting values of the Brownian motion parameter, which describes the random movement of particles suspended in a fluid, the temperature profile increases.

Impact of the buoyancy ratio parameter and the thermal radiation parameter on the temperature profile

Actually, as thermal radiation increases, the nanofluid’s temperature profiles rise because of the enhanced heat-absorption capabilities of nanoparticles and the increased energy transfer brought on by radiation. Raising the buoyancy ratio in bioconvection nanofluid flow enhances both chemical and biological buoyancy forces, boosts upward convective motion, thickens the nanoparticle boundary layer, and hence increases the concentration profile as shown in Fig. 6a.

The particles migrate outward and build up within the fluid domain as a result of being aggressively pushed away from the wall.

Statistical analysis

Numerical computational simulations have a higher interpretation, which makes an experimental scheme essential. Utilizing computer programming, data collection is created and then applied to actual situations. The Response Surface Methodology (RSM) is a technique also known as the surface regression model that takes into account the possibility that several input factors may influence response and uses experimental design to determine the significant input parameter and the precise quantity of an input parameter that can improve response. The general quadratic model42 explains how the input variables and the output response are related. The quadratic model also has three linear terms, three bilinear terms with two components, and one intercept term. The most well-known experiment for fitting the second-order model is the ”Central Composite Design” (CCD).

Surface regerssion model

The process began by constructing a Central Composite Design matrix to define the specific boundary combinations of the selected independent operational parameters. For each distinct design point, the governing coupled differential equations of the bioconvective nanofluid were solved numerically to obtain the exact output responses, namely the Nusselt number, Sherwood number, and local microorganism density. These numerical outputs were then utilized to fit second-order polynomial equations via the Method of Ordinary Least Squares (OLS), calculating the precise empirical weights for the linear, quadratic, and interaction effects. Finally, Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) was executed to statistically validate the adequacy of the fitted equations, ensuring that the resulting 3D response surface plots reliably and accurately map the physical behavior of the fluid system.
