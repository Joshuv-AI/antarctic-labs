Infinite transitivity and polynomial vector fields
Rafael B. Andrist, Ivan Arzhantsev

Abstract
. We prove that for many pairs H 1 , H 2 H_{1},H_{2} of root subgroups of the automorphism group Aut ⁡ ( ℂ 2 ) \Aut({\mathbb{C}}^{2}) the diagonal action of the group generated by H 1 , H 2 H_{1},H_{2} on ( ℂ 2 ) m ({\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{m} has an open orbit for any positive integer m m . The result is based on the study of the Lie algebra of polynomials in two variables with the standard Poisson bracket.

1.
Introduction

In this work, we study the properties of multiple transitivity for groups acting on the affine plane
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. It follows from the classical Jung theorem that the group of polynomial automorphisms
\Aut({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
is generated by the two-dimensional torus of diagonal matrices and the one-parameter unipotent subgroups of the form

L_{r}\colon(x+\alpha y^{r},y),\quad\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}\quad\text{and}\quad R_{s}\colon(x,y+\beta x^{s}),\quad\beta\in{\mathbb{C}},

where
r
and
s
run through the non-negative integers. Let us consider the group
G_{r,s}
generated by the two subgroups
L_{r}
and
R_{s}
.

We recall that an action of a group
G
on a set
X
is infinitely transitive if for any positive integer
m
and any two
m
-tuples of pairwise distinct points in
X
there is an element
g\in G
that sends the first tuple to the second one. It is easy to see that the group
G_{r,s}
acts on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
with an open orbit. By
[
7
]
, the action of
G_{r,s}
on its open orbit is infinitely transitive only if
rs=2
. It is asked in
[
7
]
whether
rs=2
implies that the action is indeed infinitely transitive. The positive answer is given in
[
13
,
9
]
. We give another proof of this result in Section
4
. This proof connects the property of infinite transitivity of an action with the structure of a tangent algebra, and therefore appears promising for further generalizations.

Let us say that an action of a group
G
on an algebraic variety
X
is generically infinitely transitive if for any positive integer
m
the diagonal action of
G
on
X^{m}:=X\times\ldots\times X
(
m
times) has a Zariski open orbit. Clearly, any infinitely transitive action is generically infinitely transitive.

The main result of this paper (Theorem
18
) claims that the action of
G_{r,s}
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
is generically infinitely transitive if and only if
rs\geqslant 2
. It is easy to see
that the condition
rs\leqslant 1
is equivalent to the fact that
G_{r,s}
is a linear algebraic group.

The proof of Theorem
18
is based on the study of subalgebras of the Lie algebra of polynomials
{\mathbb{C}}[x,y]
with the standard Poisson bracket; see Section
2
.
We show that for the subalgebra
\lie(x^{p},y^{q})
generated by the two monomials
x^{p}
and
y^{q}
we have

\lie(x^{p},y^{q})\cap{\mathbb{C}}[x]=x^{p}{\mathbb{C}}[x^{pq-p-q}]\quad\text{and}\quad\lie(x^{p},y^{q})\cap{\mathbb{C}}[y]=y^{q}{\mathbb{C}}[y^{pq-p-q}],

provided
\min(p,q)\geq 2
and
\max(p,q)\geq 3
; see Proposition
7
. Moreover, the subalgebra
\lie(x^{p},y^{q})
has finite codimension in
{\mathbb{C}}[x,y]
if and only if

(p,q)=(2,3)
or
(3,2)
.

In the same Section
2
we find explicit pairs of derivations that generate the Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
with zero divergence or with constant divergence; see Propositions
1
,
11
and Theorem
10
. These results complement the results of
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, where explicit finite generating sets for many Lie algebras of polynomial vector fields are found.

In order to go from Lie algebras to the corresponding transformation groups, we use Proposition
23
on approximation of polynomial flows. This result goes back to Varolin
[
14
]
, some of its versions were used in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. This approach is applicable not only to the affine plane
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, but also to other affine surfaces; see Proposition
25
for the case of Danielewski surfaces.

Denote by
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
the additive group of the ground field
{\mathbb{C}}
. Any non-trivial regular action
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}\times X\to X
on an algebraic variety
X
gives rise to a subgroup
H
in

\Aut(X)
isomorphic to
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
. Such subgroups are called
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-subgroups of
\Aut(X)
. The subgroups
L_{r}
and
R_{s}
are examples of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-subgroups in
\Aut({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
.
These are precisely the
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-subgroups normalized by the two-dimensional torus of diagonal matrices. Such
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-subgroups are called root subgroups. Some results on transitivity properties for groups generated by
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-subgroups can be found in
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
and references therein.

In Corollary
29
, we use the fact that the Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields with zero divergence on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
is 2-generated and construct
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-subgroups
H_{1},H_{2}
(one them is not root) such that the group
G
generated by
H_{1},H_{2}
is infinitely transitive on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.

It is important to notice that the results on Lie algebras in Section
2
are obtained by algebraic methods and are valid over any field of characteristic zero. At the same time, the results on transitivity properties of group actions are based on Propositions
23
,
27
, the proofs of which use analytical methods, in particular the Implicit Function Theorem. It would be interesting to obtain criteria for infinite transitivity or generic infinite transitivity in terms of the corresponding Lie algebras applying purely algebraic arguments.

2.
Some results on Lie algebras

Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be a field of characteristic zero. Consider the Lie algebra
L
of all polynomial vector fields
f_{1}(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+f_{2}(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
on
{\mathbb{K}}^{2}
with zero divergence, i.e.,

\frac{\partial f_{1}(x,y)}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial f_{2}(x,y)}{\partial y}=0.

well known that
L
coincides with the Lie algebra of all volume-preserving vector fields, all symplectic vector fields, and all Hamiltonian vector fields on
{\mathbb{K}}^{2}
. In particular, vector fields from
L
are precisely vector fields of the form

V_{f}:=\frac{\partial f(x,y)}{\partial y}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial f(x,y)}{\partial x}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

for some
f\in{\mathbb{K}}[x,y]
. As for the commutator, we have

[V_{f},V_{h}]=V_{\{f,h\}},\quad\text{where}\quad\{f,h\}=\frac{\partial f(x,y)}{\partial x}\frac{\partial h(x,y)}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial f(x,y)}{\partial y}\frac{\partial h(x,y)}{\partial x}

is the standard Poisson bracket on
{\mathbb{K}}[x,y]
. We denote by
A
the Lie algebra
({\mathbb{K}}[x,y],\{\cdot,\cdot\})
.
The map
f\mapsto V_{f}
establishes an isomorphism of Lie algebras between
A/I
and
L
, where
I
is the central ideal of constants in
A
.

As the first result, we show that the Lie algebra
L
is 2-generated. We provide a short direct proof.

Proposition 1
.

The Lie algebra
L
is generated by two locally nilpotent derivations

\partial_{1}=y^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-x\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

Proof.

Notice that
\partial_{1}=\frac{1}{3}V_{y^{3}}
and
\partial_{2}=\frac{1}{2}V_{x^{2}+2y}
. So it suffices to show that the polynomials
x^{2}+2y
and
y^{3}
generate the Lie algebra
A
.
Let
A_{0}
be the Lie subalgebra in
A
generated by
x^{2}+2y
and
y^{3}
. We have

\{x^{2}+2y,y^{3}\}=6xy^{2},\ \{x^{2}+2y,xy^{2}\}=4x^{2}y-2y^{2},\ \{x^{2}+2y,2x^{2}y-y^{2}\}=4x^{3}-12xy,

\{x^{2}+2y,x^{3}-4xy\}=6y-12x^{2},\ \ \{x^{2}+2y,y-2x^{2}\}=10x,\ \ \{x^{2}+2y,x\}=-2.

Since the polynomials
x^{2}+2y
and
y-2x^{2}
are not proportional, we conclude
that
1,x,x^{2},y
lie in
A_{0}
. Further, we obtain

\{\{x^{2},y^{l}\},y^{l}\}=2l^{2}y^{2(l-1)}.

This implies that if
y^{l}\in A_{0}
then
y^{2(l-1)}\in A_{0}
and
2(l-1)>l
for
l\geqslant 3
. Since we have

\{x,y^{l}\}=ly^{l-1},

we conclude that
y^{s}\in A_{0}
for all
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
. In the same way we have

\{\{\{y^{3},x^{k}\},x^{k}\},x^{k}\}=6k^{3}x^{3(k-1)}.

This shows that if
x^{k}\in A_{0}
then
y^{3(k-1)}\in A_{0}
and
3(k-1)>k
for
k\geqslant 2
. Since we have

\{x^{k},y\}=kx^{k-1},

we conclude that
x^{m}\in A_{0}
for all
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
. Finally, the equality

\{x^{m},y^{s}\}=msx^{m-1}y^{s-1},

implies that all monomials in
x,y
are in
A_{0}
and so
A_{0}
coincides with
A
.
∎

Remark 2
.

The fact that the Lie algebra
L
is 2-generated can also be deduced from
[
4
, Theorem 28]
with
n=1
.

Let us take a closer look at the structure of the Lie algebra
A=({\mathbb{K}}[x,y],\{\cdot,\cdot\})
.

Lemma 3
.

We have

\{x^{p}y^{q},x^{r}y^{s}\}=(ps-qr)x^{p+r-1}y^{q+s-1}=\begin{vmatrix}p&q\\
r&s\end{vmatrix}x^{p+r-1}y^{q+s-1}.

The proof is straightforward.

Let us define an operator
\adj_{f}\colon A\to A
with
f\in A
by
\adj_{f}(g)=\{f,g\}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
p,q,n
be positive integers with
n\leqslant pq
and
\epsilon
be an element of
{\mathbb{K}}
. Then

\adj_{x^{p}-\varepsilon y}^{n}(y^{q})=\sum_{k=0}^{s}c_{k,n}x^{p(q-k)-n}y^{k},

where
s=\lfloor q-\frac{n}{p}\rfloor
. Moreover, we have
the recursion relation

c_{k,n+1}=\varepsilon(p(q-k)-n)c_{k,n}+p(k+1)c_{k+1,n}

with
c_{k,n}=0
for
k>s
.

Proof.

We use the induction on
n
. With
n=0
we obtain

c_{q,0}=1
and
c_{k,0}=0
for
k\neq q
.
For the induction step we have

\{x^{p}-\varepsilon y,\sum_{k=0}^{s}c_{k,n}x^{p(q-k)-n}y^{k}\}=\sum_{k=0}^{s}c_{k,n}\{x^{p},x^{p(q-k)-n}y^{k}\}-\sum_{k=0}^{s}\varepsilon c_{k,n}\{y,x^{p(q-k)-n}y^{k}\}=

=\sum_{k=0}^{s}kpc_{k,n}x^{p(q-k)-n+p-1}y^{k-1}+\sum_{k=0}^{s}\varepsilon(p(q-k)-n)c_{k,n}x^{p(q-k)-n-1}y^{k}=

=\sum_{k=0}^{s^{\prime}}c_{k,n+1}x^{p(q-k)-(n+1)}y^{k},

where
s^{\prime}=\lfloor q-\frac{n+1}{p}\rfloor
. This leads to the required relation.
∎

If
\varepsilon\neq 0
, we apply the automorphism
(x,y)\mapsto(x,\varepsilon y)
of the algebra
{\mathbb{K}}[x,y]
and assume that
\varepsilon=1
.

Lemma 5
.

We have

\adj_{x^{p}-y}^{p(q-1)}(y^{q})=c_{0,p(q-1)}x^{p}+c_{1,p(q-1)}y\quad\text{and}\quad\adj_{x^{p}-y}^{pq}(y^{q})=c_{0,pq}.

Moreover,
c_{0,pq}\neq 0
and
c_{0,p(q-1)}+c_{1,p(q-1)}\neq 0
.

Proof.

The first two equalities follow directly from Lemma
4
. Since
c_{q,0}=1
and
c_{k,0}=0
for
k\neq q
, we conclude from the recursion formula that

c_{0,i}=0
for
i<q
and
c_{0,q}\neq 0
. We have
c_{0,n+1}=(pq-n)c_{0,n}+pc_{1,n}
, so
c_{0,i}\neq 0
for
q\leqslant i\leqslant pq
as a sum of two non-negative summands
such that at least one of them is positive. So we obtain
c_{0,pq}\neq 0
.

If
c_{0,p(q-1)}+c_{1,p(q-1)}=0
then the element
\adj_{x^{p}-y}^{p(q-1)}(y^{q})
is proportional to
x^{p}-y
. This implies

0=\adj_{x^{p}-y}^{p(q-1)+1}(y^{q})=\ldots=\adj_{x^{p}-y}^{pq}(y^{q})=c_{0,pq},

a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 6
.

We have

\adj_{x^{p}}^{q}(y^{q})=p^{q}q!x^{pq-q}.

Proof.

With
\varepsilon=0
the recursion relation in Lemma
4
has the form
c_{k,n+1}=p(k+1)c_{k+1,n}
. Since
c_{q,0}=1
and
c_{k,0}=0
for
k\neq q
,
we obtain
c_{0,q}=p^{q}q!
and
c_{i,q}=0
for
i\neq 0
.
∎

Let us denote by
\lie(f,g)
the subalgebra of the Lie algebra
A
generated by
f,g\in A
.

Proposition 7
.

If
\min(p,q)\geq 2
and
\max(p,q)\geq 3
then

\lie(x^{p},y^{q})\cap{\mathbb{K}}[x]=x^{p}{\mathbb{K}}[x^{pq-p-q}]

and

\lie(x^{p},y^{q})\cap{\mathbb{K}}[y]=y^{q}{\mathbb{K}}[y^{pq-p-q}].

Proof.

Let
S=\lie(x^{p},y^{q})
.
We first prove the sufficiency of the conditions.

(1)

By Lemma
6
,
S\ni p^{q}q!x^{pq-q}=\adj_{x^{p}}^{q}(y^{q})
.

(2)

We show that
x^{k(pq-q)-(k-1)p}\in S\Longrightarrow x^{(k+1)(pq-q)-kp}\in S
for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
:

\adj_{x^{p}}^{q-1}\adj_{y^{q}}(x^{k(pq-q)-(k-1)p})=\adj_{x^{p}}^{q-1}(-q(k(pq-q)-(k-1)p)x^{k(pq-q)-(k-1)p-1}y^{q-1})=

=-(k(pq-p-q)+p)p^{q-1}q!x^{(k+1)(pq-q)-kp}.

Since
\min(p,q)\geq 2
,
\max(p,q)\geq 3
, all the coefficients and exponents are positive.
We conclude that
S
contains the monomials
x^{k(pq-p-q)+p}
for any non-negative integer
k
.
We can also interchange the roles of
x^{p}
and
y^{q}
.

For the necessity of the conditions, observe that all possible Lie combinations are obtained through a combination of the adjoint actions
\adj_{x^{p}}
and
\adj_{y^{q}}
which map monomials to monomials. Applying the operators
\adj_{x^{p}}
and
\adj_{y^{q}}
to
x^{p}
and
y^{q}
, we obtains monomials with exponents

(p,0)+k(p-1,-1)+l(-1,q-1)\,:\,k,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}

and

(0,q)+k(p-1,-1)+l(-1,q-1)\,:\,k,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0},

respectively. For
(m,0)
, this yields either
m=p+l(pq-p-q)
for the first variant or
m=p+(l+1)(pq-q-p)
for the second variant.
Similarly, for
(0,n)
we have
{n=q+(k+1)(pq-p-q)}
or
n=q+k(pq-p-q)
.
∎

Corollary 8
.

If
p=3
and
q=2
then

{\mathbb{K}}[x,y]=\langle 1,x,y,xy,x^{2}\rangle\oplus\lie(x^{3},y^{2}).

Proof.

We have
pq-p-q=1
and Proposition
7
implies
x^{3}{\mathbb{K}}[x]+y^{2}{\mathbb{K}}[y]\subseteq\lie(x^{3},y^{2})
.
The inclusion of mixed powers
x^{a}y^{b}
follows from Lemma
3
.
∎

Since
pq-p-q=1
is equivalent to
(p-1)(q-1)=2
and

\lie(x,y^{q})=\langle 1,x,y,y^{2},\ldots,y^{q}\rangle,\quad\lie(x^{2},y^{2})=\langle xy,x^{2},y^{2}\rangle,

we obtain

Corollary 9
.

The subalgebra
\lie(x^{p},y^{q})
has finite codimension in
{\mathbb{K}}[x,y]
if and only if
(p,q)=(2,3)
or
(3,2)
.

The following result is a generalization of Proposition
1
.

Theorem 10
.

If
\min(p,q)\geq 2
and
\max(p,q)\geq 3
then

\lie(x^{p}-y,y^{q})={\mathbb{K}}[x,y].

Proof.

Since we have

\adj_{x^{p}-y}^{p(q-1)}(y^{q})=c_{0,p(q-1)}x^{p}+c_{1,p(q-1)}y

and
c_{0,p(q-1)}+c_{1,p(q-1)}\neq 0
(Lemma
5
), we obtain
x^{p},y\in\lie(x^{p}-y,y^{q})
.
Lemma
3
implies

\adj_{y}^{n}(x^{p})=(-1)^{n}p\cdots(p-n+1)x^{p-n}\in\lie(x^{p}-y,y^{q})

and

\adj_{x}^{n}(y^{q})=q\cdots(q-n+1)y^{q-n}\in\lie(x^{p}-y,y^{q}).

We conclude that the elements
x^{p},x^{p-1},\ldots,x
and
y^{q},y^{q-1},\ldots,y
are contained in
{\lie(x^{p}-y,y^{q})}
.
Since
\min(p,q)\geq 2
and
\max(p,q)\geq 3
, either the elements
x^{2}+2y,y^{3}
or the elements
y^{2}+2x,x^{3}
are contained in

\lie(x^{p}-y,y^{q})
. It is shown in the proof of Proposition
1
that every such pair generates the algebra
A
.
∎

Finally, let us give one more result on Lie algebras. Let
\Delta=x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
. Consider the Lie algebra
\widehat{L}

of polynomial vector fields on
{\mathbb{K}}^{2}
with constant divergence. By
[
12
, Proposition 15.7.2]
, the Lie algebra
\widehat{L}
coincides with the tangent
algebra of the group of polynomial automorphisms
\Aut({\mathbb{K}}^{2})
. Clearly, we have

\widehat{L}=\langle\Delta\rangle\oplus L.

Let us show that the Lie algebra
\widehat{L}
is 2-generated, which is a natural extension of Proposition
1
.

Proposition 11
.

The Lie algebra
\widehat{L}
is generated by two derivations

\widehat{\partial_{1}}=\Delta+3y^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-x\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

Proof.

Note that
[\Delta,V_{x^{a}y^{b}}]=(a+b-2)V_{x^{a}y^{b}}
. It is easy to see that the Lie algebra
A=({\mathbb{K}}[x,y],\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is

{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded:

A=\oplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}A_{i},\quad\text{where}\quad A_{i}=\langle x^{a}y^{b}\,;\,a+b-2=i\rangle.

Define the linear operator
\delta
on
A
given by
\delta(x^{a}y^{b})=(a+b-2)x^{a}y^{b}
. We may consider the semidirect product
of Lie algebras:

\widehat{A}:=\langle\delta\rangle\rightthreetimes A,\quad[\delta,x^{a}y^{b}]=(a+b-2)x^{a}y^{b}.

Then we have
[\Delta,V_{f}]=V_{\{\delta,f\}}
for any polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{K}}[x,y]
. Again the projection

\widehat{A}\to\widehat{L},\quad\alpha\delta+f\mapsto\alpha\Delta+V_{f},\quad\alpha\in{\mathbb{K}}

establishes an isomorphism
\widehat{A}/\widehat{I}\cong\widehat{L}
, where
\widehat{I}=0+{\mathbb{K}}
is the ideal of constants. So it suffices to prove that the elements
x^{2}+2y
and
\delta+y^{3}
generate the algebra

\widehat{A}
. We have

\{x^{2}+2y,\delta+y^{3}\}=2y+6xy^{2},\ \{x^{2}+2y,y+3xy^{2}\}=2x+12x^{2}y-6y^{2},

\{x^{2}+2y,x+6x^{2}y-3y^{2}\}=-2+12x^{3}-36xy,\ \{x^{2}+2y,x^{3}-3xy\}=6y-12x^{2},

\ \ \{x^{2}+2y,y-2x^{2}\}=10x,\ \ \{x^{2}+2y,x\}=-2.

Since the polynomials
x^{2}+2y
and
y-2x^{2}
are not proportional, we conclude
that
1,x,x^{2},y
lie in the Lie algebra
\widehat{A}_{0}
generated by
x^{2}+2y
and
\delta+y^{3}
. Further, we have

\{\{x^{2},\delta+y^{3}\},y\}=\{6xy^{2},y\}=6y^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\{xy^{2},y^{2}\}=2y^{3}.

We conclude that the elements
\delta,x^{2}+2y,y^{3}
are in
\widehat{A}_{0}
, and the claim follows from Proposition
1
.
∎

Remark 12
.

While the vector field
\widehat{\partial_{1}}=(x+3y^{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
is not the velocity field of any one-parameter polynomial subgroup of automorphisms of

, over the field of complex numbers it is the velocity field of one-parameter subgroup of holomorphic automorphisms of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
,
namely,

(x,y)\mapsto((x-3y^{2})e^{t}+3y^{2}e^{2t},ye^{t}).

Remark 13
.

Developing
[
1
, Theorem 11]
, it is shown in
[
8
]
that the Lie algebra of all polynomial vector fields on
{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
is 2-generated. Three generators of the Lie algebra of all volume-preserving polynomial vector fields or, equivalently, of the Lie algebra of all polynomial vector fields with divergence zero on
{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
with
n\geqslant 2
are given in
[

. We do not know whether the Lie algebra of all polynomial vector fields with zero divergence and the Lie algebra of all polynomial vector fields with constant divergence on

.

3.
Infinite transitivity and root subgroups

In this section we assume that the ground field
{\mathbb{K}}
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let
X
be an affine algebraic variety of dimension at least 2 and
T

be an algebraic torus with the character lattice
M
. Assume that we have an effective action
T\times X\to X
. Let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}
be
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-subgroups in
\Aut(X)
normalized by
T
.
Such subgroups are called root subgroups. Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\in M
be characters of
T
which correspond to the action of
T
on
H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}
by conjugation. This means that

tH_{i}(a)t^{-1}=H_{i}(e_{i}(t)a)\quad\forall t\in T,a\in{\mathbb{K}}.

Consider the subgroup
G=\langle H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}\rangle
in
\Aut(X)
. Denote by
X^{m}
the direct product
X\times\ldots\times X
(
m
times).

Definition 14
.

An action of a group
G
on an algebraic variety
X
is said to be
generically infinitely transitive
if for any positive integer
m
the diagonal action of
G
on
X^{m}
has an open orbit.

Assume that the group
G
has an open orbit
\mathcal{O}
on
X
. We are interested when
G
acts on
\mathcal{O}
infinitely transitively or, weaker, when the action of
G
on
X

is generically infinitely transitive.

The following result is a conceptual generalization of
[
7
, Remark 5.7]
. Also it proves one implication in
[
9
, Conjecture 2]
.

Proposition 15
.

Assume that the group
G
acts on
X

Lemma 16

Assume that a group

4.
Infinite transitivity on affine surfaces

In this section we assume that the ground field is the field
{\mathbb{C}}
of complex numbers. Our main aim is to prove Theorem
18
.
We begin with the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 20
.

Let
z_{1},\dots,z_{m-1},z_{m}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
d_{0},d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
. If
z_{m}\neq 0
and

z^{d}_{m}\neq z^{d}_{j},\quad j=1,\dots,m-1,

then there exists a polynomial
f\in z^{d_{0}}{\mathbb{C}}[z^{d}]
with
f(z_{1})=\dots=f(z_{m-1})=0
and
f(z_{m})=1
.

Proof.

We choose

f(z)=c_{0}z^{d_{0}}\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(z^{d}-{z_{j}}^{d}).

We can find
c_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
f(z_{m})=1
under the assumptions made on
z_{m}
.
∎

Now let
X
be a connected smooth affine algebraic surface. In this section we call the subgroup
\exp(tD)
,
t\in{\mathbb{C}}
, associated with a locally nilpotent derivation
D
on
{\mathbb{C}}[X]
, the
flow

of
D
.

Lemma 21
.

Assume that there exist two locally nilpotent derivations
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
on
{\mathbb{C}}[X]

that span the tangent space
T_{x}X
in every point
x
of an open subset
\Omega\subseteq X
.
Then there are functions
x_{1},x_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}[X]
such that
\Ker(\Theta_{1})={\mathbb{C}}[x_{1}]
and

\Ker(\Theta_{2})={\mathbb{C}}[x_{2}]
.

Proof.

Since the variety
X
is smooth, the algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[X]
is integrally closed. The subalgebra
\Ker(\Theta_{1})
coincides with the algebra of invariants of a
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-action, so it is
integrally closed as well. By
[
15
, Theorem 1]
,
\Ker(\Theta_{1})
is the algebra of regular functions on a quasi-affine variety
C
. But
C
has dimension
1

[
11
, Principle 11(e)]
, so it is a smooth affine curve. Take any point
x_{0}\in\Omega
. The map

{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\to X,\quad(y_{1},y_{2})\mapsto\exp(y_{1}\Theta_{1})\exp(y_{2}\Theta_{2})x_{0}

is a dominant morphism, so the surface
X
is rational. Since the inclusion
{\Ker(\Theta_{1})\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}[X]}
induces a dominant morphism
X\to C
, the curve
C
is rational as well.

Any invertible regular function
f
on
X
is invariant with respect to the flows of
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
, see
[
11
, Principle 1(b)]
. So the flows preserve the curves where
f
is constant. This contradicts to the fact that

\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
generate the tangent space at a generic point. We conclude that
{\mathbb{C}}[X]
contains no non-constant regular function. The same holds for
{\mathbb{C}}[C]
. So
C
is an affine line and
\Ker(\Theta_{1})
is the algebra of polynomials in one variable. The same arguments work for
\Ker(\Theta_{2})
.
∎

We use notation of Lemma
21
and for any positive integers
m,d
and an open subset
\Omega\subseteq X
we define a subset in
X^{m}
as

\Omega^{m}_{d}=\{(P_{1},\ldots,P_{m})\in\Omega^{m}\,|\,x_{i}^{d}(P_{s})\neq x_{i}^{d}(P_{l})\neq 0,\,i=1,2,\,s\neq l,\,s,l=1,\ldots,m\}.

Lemma 22
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
21
, let
(P_{1},\dots,P_{m})\in\Omega^{m}_{d}
. Then there exist locally nilpotent derivations
V_{j,k}
on
{\mathbb{C}}[X]
with
j=1,\ldots,m
and
i=1,2

of the form

V_{j,i}=f_{j,k}\Theta_{i},\quad f_{j,i}\in x_{i}^{d_{i}}{\mathbb{C}}[x_{i}^{d}],

such that for every
j=1,\ldots,m
we have

\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{(V_{j,i})_{P_{j}}\}_{i=1,2}=T_{P_{j}}X

and for every
l\neq j
and every
i
we have

(V_{j,i})_{P_{l}}=0.

Proof.

By Lemma
20
there exist functions
f_{j,i}\in x_{i}^{d_{i}}{\mathbb{C}}[x_{i}^{d}]
such that

f_{j,i}(x_{k}(P_{l}))=\delta_{jl}.

Then the vector fields
V_{j,i}=f_{j,i}\Theta_{i}
have the desired properties. ∎

Proposition 23
.

Let
X
be a connected smooth affine algebraic surface. Assume that there exist two locally nilpotent derivations
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
on
{\mathbb{C}}[X]
with
\Ker(\Theta_{i})={\mathbb{C}}[x_{i}]
,

i=1,2
, that span the tangent space
T_{x}X
in every point
x
of an open subset
\Omega\subseteq X
. Assume also that there exist locally nilpotent derivations
D_{1},\ldots,D_{k}
on

{\mathbb{C}}[X]
such that

\lie(D_{1},\ldots,D_{k})\supseteq x_{1}^{d_{1}}{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1}^{d}]\Theta_{1}\oplus x_{2}^{d_{2}}{\mathbb{C}}[x_{2}^{d}]\Theta_{2}

for some non-negative integers
d_{1},d_{2}
and some positive integer
d
.
Then for every positive integer
m
the group
G
generated by the flows of
D_{1},\ldots,D_{k}
acts on
X^{m}
with an open orbit
\mathcal{O}_{m}
, and the action of
G
on
\mathcal{O}_{m}
is infinitely transitive. Moreover, the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{m}
contains the subset
\Omega^{m}_{d}
.

Proof.

Throughout this proof, all spaces are endowed with the Euclidean topology or the subspace topology inherited thereof.

Let
P_{1},\dots,P_{m},P_{m+1}\in\Omega^{m+1}_{d}
and
a_{j}=x_{1}(P_{j})
,
b_{j}=x_{2}(P_{j})
. It suffices to construct an automorphism
\Phi\colon X\to X
such that
\Phi(P_{j})=P_{j}
for every

j=1,\dots,m-1
and
\Phi(P_{m})=P_{m+1}
. Indeed, the subset
\Omega^{m}_{d}
is open in
X^{m}
, so the group
G
acts with an open orbit which is the union of translations of
\Omega^{m}_{d}
.

We choose a path
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X
from
P_{m}
to
P_{m+1}
such that its image satisfies

(1)

x_{1}|_{\gamma((0,1))}\colon\gamma((0,1))\to{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{\zeta a_{1},\dots\zeta a_{m+1}\,:\,\zeta^{d}=1\}
;

(2)

x_{2}|_{\gamma((0,1))}\colon\gamma((0,1))\to{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{\zeta b_{1},\dots\zeta b_{m+1}\,:\,\zeta^{d}=1\}
.

Such a path
\gamma
exists since

X\setminus\left(x_{1}^{-1}(\{\zeta a_{1},\dots\zeta a_{m+1}\,:\,\zeta^{d}=1\})\cup x_{2}^{-1}(\{\zeta b_{1},\dots\zeta b_{m+1}\,:\,\zeta^{d}=1\})\right)

is path-connected.

For each
\tau\in[0,1]
we apply Lemma
22
to the points
P_{1},\dots,P_{m-1},\gamma(\tau)
and obtain the vector fields
V_{j,i,\tau}
. Denote their flows by
\varphi_{j,i,\tau}^{t}
, where
t
stands for the flow time. The map
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\to X^{m}
defined by

(t_{j,i})_{j=1,\dots,m;i=1,2}\mapsto\varphi_{m,2,\tau}^{t_{m,2}}\circ\varphi_{m,1,\tau}^{t_{m,1}}\circ\dots\circ\varphi_{1,2,\tau}^{t_{1,2}}\circ\varphi_{1,1,\tau}^{t_{1,1}}(P_{1},\dots P_{m-1},\gamma(\tau))

it is a submersion in a neighborhood of
0
. Indeed, its Jacobian determinant is non-zero.

By assumption, each of the vector fields
V_{j,i,\tau}
is a finite Lie combination of the locally nilpotent derivations
D_{1},\ldots,D_{k}
. By
[
10
, Corollary 4.8.4]
, a finite composition
\Phi_{j,i,\tau}^{t}
of the flows of

D_{1},\ldots,D_{k}
approximates the flow
\varphi_{j,i,\tau}^{t}
of
V_{j,i,\tau}
on a relatively compact neighborhood of
P_{1},\dots,P_{m-1},\gamma(\tau)
and for a small enough flow-time
|t|<\delta
arbitrarily well. Hence, we can also approximate the above composition

\varphi_{m,2,\tau}^{t_{m,2}}\circ\varphi_{m,1,\tau}^{t_{m,1}}\circ\dots\circ\varphi_{1,2,\tau}^{t_{1,2}}\circ\varphi_{1,1,\tau}^{t_{1,1}}(P_{1},\dots P_{m-1},\gamma(\tau))

by a finite composition

\Phi_{m,2,\tau}^{t_{m,2}}\circ\Phi_{m,1,\tau}^{t_{m,1}}\circ\dots\circ\Phi_{1,2,\tau}^{t_{1,2}}\circ\Phi_{1,1,\tau}^{t_{1,1}}(P_{1},\dots P_{m-1},\gamma(\tau))

of flows of the locally nilpotent derivations
D_{1},\ldots,D_{k}
. We denote this composition by
\Phi_{\tau}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\to X^{m}
, considered as a function depending on the flow times
t_{j,k}
. This approximation will eventually become a submersion as well on a neighborhood
U_{\tau}
of
{0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}
. Indeed, if a holomorphic map approximates another holomorphic map uniformly on compacts, then so do their derivatives; In particular, a good enough approximation will need to have non-zero Jacobian determinant. Since
[0,1]
is compact, we can cover
\gamma([0,1])
by finitely many sets of the form
\Phi_{\tau_{1}}(U_{\tau_{1}}),\dots,\Phi_{\tau_{s}}(U_{\tau_{s}})
. By the Implicit Function Theorem, we now find for each of path-times
\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{s}
the parameters in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
such that we can successfully move
P_{m}
to
P_{m+1}
along the path
\gamma
in finitely many steps. The resulting composition is the desired automorphism which is algebraic as a finite composition of flows of locally nilpotent derivations.
∎

Remark 24
.

In
[
7
, Conjecture 5.23]
, the following result is expected. Let
X
be an affine variety,
D_{1},\ldots,D_{k}
be locally nilpotent derivations on
{\mathbb{K}}[X]
, and
H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}
be their flows. Consider one more locally nilpotent derivation

on
{\mathbb{K}}[X]
with flow

. Then the subgroup

lies in the closure of the subgroup

with respect to the ind-topology on

if and only if

. The reasoning used in the proof of Proposition
23
may be considered as analytical arguments in favor of this conjecture.

Now we are ready to prove the main results.

Proof of Theorem
18
.

If
rs\leqslant 1
then
G_{r,s}
is a linear algebraic group, and this group can not act with an open orbit on varieties
({\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{m}
of arbitrarily high dimension. Let us assume further that
rs\geqslant 2
.

In the notation of Proposition
23
take
X={\mathbb{C}}^{2}
with
\Theta_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
and
\Theta_{2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
. Then
\Omega={\mathbb{C}}^{2}
and
x_{1}=y,x_{2}=x
.
Also we let
k=2
and
D_{1}=y^{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
,
D_{2}=x^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
. Under the identification of the Lie algebra
L
with
A/I
in Section
2
,
we identify
D_{1}
with
\frac{1}{r+1}y^{r+1}
and
D_{2}
with
\frac{1}{s+1}y^{s+1}
. By Proposition
7
, we obtain

\lie(D_{1},D_{2})\supseteq x_{1}^{d_{1}}{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1}^{d}]\Theta_{1}\oplus x_{2}^{d_{2}}{\mathbb{C}}[x_{2}^{d}]\Theta_{2}

with

d=(r+1)(s+1)-(r+1)-(s+1)=rs-1,\quad d_{1}=r+1,\quad d_{2}=s+1.

Now Proposition
23
claims that the action of the group
G_{r,s}
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
is generically infinitely transitive provided
rs\geqslant 2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
17
.

By Theorem
18
and Proposition
23
, the group
G_{r,s}
acts on
({\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{m}
with an open orbit for all
m
, and the open orbit contains the subset
\Omega^{m}_{d}
.
With
rs=2
we have
d=1
, so the subset
\Omega^{m}_{1}
consists of collections

((x_{1},y_{1}),\ldots,(x_{m},y_{m}))\quad\text{with}\ x_{i}\neq 0,\ y_{i}\neq 0,\ x_{i}\neq x_{j},\ y_{i}\neq y_{j}\ \text{for\, all}\ 1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant m.

So it remains to show that any point
((x_{1},y_{1}),\ldots,(x_{m},y_{m}))\in({\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{0\})^{m}
can be sent to a point in
\Omega^{m}_{1}
by an element of
G_{r,s}
.
We obtain this by performing four steps. In each step, we achieve the fulfillment of new conditions on a point from
({\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{0\})^{m}
, while maintaining the conditions obtained in previous steps. Without loss of generality, we assume
r=1
and
s=2
.

Step 1:

x_{i}\neq 0
for all
i
. To obtain this, we apply the transformation
(x+\alpha y,y)
with
\alpha\neq-\frac{x_{i}}{y_{i}}
for all
i
with
y_{i}\neq 0
. If
y_{i}=0
then
x_{i}\neq 0
by our assumption.

Step 2:
in the collection, there is no pair of the form
(x_{i},y_{i})
and
(-x_{i},-y_{i})
. To get this, we apply the transformation
(x,y+\beta x^{2})
with
\beta\neq-\frac{y_{i}+y_{j}}{2x_{i}^{2}}
for all
i,j
with
x_{j}=-x_{i}
.

Step 3:

x_{i}\neq 0
for all
i
and
x_{i}^{2}\neq x_{j}^{2}
for all
i\neq j
. To achieve this, we apply the transformation
(x+\alpha y,y)
with

\alpha\neq-\frac{x_{i}}{y_{i}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \alpha\neq\frac{x_{i}-x_{j}}{y_{j}-x_{i}}\ \text{for\, all}\ y_{i}\neq y_{j}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \alpha\neq-\frac{x_{i}+x_{j}}{y_{i}+x_{j}}\ \text{for\, all}\ y_{i}\neq-y_{j}.

If
y_{i}=y_{j}
then
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
and this transformation does not change the inequality. Also if
y_{i}=-y_{j}
then
x_{i}\neq-x_{j}
and this transformation does not change the inequality as well.

Step 4:

y_{i}\neq 0
for all
i
and
y_{i}\neq y_{j}
for all
i\neq j
. To obtain this, we apply the transformation
(x,y+\beta x^{2})
with

\beta\neq-\frac{y_{i}}{x_{i}^{2}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \beta\neq\frac{y_{i}-y_{j}}{x_{j}^{2}-x_{i}^{2}}\ \text{for\, all}\ i\neq j.

The proof of Theorem

is completed.
