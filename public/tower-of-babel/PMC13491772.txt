Comparison of the efficacy of posterior lumbar interbody fusion and transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion in the treatment of double-level lumbar spondylolisthesis combined with spinal stenosis: a retrospective comparative study
Authors: Zekun Ma, Haopeng Luan, Kadeyanmu Abulimiti, Xuyang Xie, Jiyuan Tao, Jitao Yang, Weibin Sheng, Qiang Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo compare the clinical and radiographic outcomes of posterior lumbar interbody fusion (PLIF) and transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF) in the treatment of double-level lumbar spondylolisthesis combined with spinal stenosis.MethodsThis retrospective comparative study included 44 patients with double-level lumbar spondylolisthesis combined with spinal stenosis who underwent surgical treatment between April 2013 and August 2021. Patients were divided into PLIF and TLIF groups. Perioperative parameters, complications, and clinical outcomes, including visual analog scale (VAS) scores for back and leg pain and the Oswestry Disability Index (ODI), were evaluated preoperatively and at 1 week, 6 months, 12 months, and 24 months postoperatively. Radiographic parameters related to sagittal alignment and vertebral slippage were also assessed. Statistical analyses were performed using appropriate parametric or non-parametric tests based on data distribution.ResultsBaseline characteristics were comparable between the two groups (P > 0.05). No major intraoperative complications or implant-related failures were observed. Both groups demonstrated significant improvements in VAS scores and ODI at all postoperative time points compared with preoperative values (P < 0.05), with no significant differences between groups (P > 0.05). Radiographic parameters showed significant postoperative improvement in both groups (P < 0.05). Although the TLIF group demonstrated numerically greater improvements in certain sagittal alignment parameters, these differences did not reach statistical significance.ConclusionBoth PLIF and TLIF are effective surgical options for the treatment of double-level lumbar spondylolisthesis combined with spinal stenosis, resulting in significant clinical and radiographic improvement. No significant differences in clinical outcomes were observed between the two techniques. While TLIF may show a trend toward improved restoration of certain sagittal alignment parameters, these findings should be interpreted with caution.

Introduction

Lumbar spondylolisthesis is a degenerative spinal disorder characterized by the anterior or posterior displacement of a vertebral body relative to the adjacent segment, often resulting from intervertebral disc degeneration and facet joint arthropathy [1]. It is frequently associated with lumbar spinal stenosis and represents a common cause of low back pain, radicular symptoms, and functional limitation in middle-aged and elderly patients [2, 3]. When conservative treatment fails, surgical intervention is generally indicated to achieve adequate neural decompression, restore spinal alignment, and re-establish segmental stability [4].

Compared with single-level disease, double-level lumbar spondylolisthesis is relatively uncommon but often presents with more complex biomechanical alterations and more pronounced sagittal imbalance. The reported incidence of double-level degenerative spondylolisthesis ranges from 5% to 12%, whereas double-level isthmic spondylolisthesis is even less frequent [5]. Due to the cumulative effects of multi-segment instability and neural compression, these patients may experience more severe symptoms and may require more technically demanding surgical strategies. However, the optimal surgical approach for this specific patient population remains a matter of ongoing debate [6].

Posterior lumbar interbody fusion (PLIF) and transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF) are widely used surgical techniques for the treatment of lumbar spondylolisthesis. PLIF allows for bilateral decompression and interbody fusion through a posterior midline approach but typically requires greater retraction of neural elements and more extensive posterior structure disruption. In contrast, TLIF is performed through a unilateral approach, which may reduce neural traction and preserve contralateral posterior elements, potentially facilitating restoration of sagittal alignment while minimizing soft tissue injury. In addition to these posterior approaches, anterior and lateral interbody fusion techniques, such as anterior lumbar interbody fusion (ALIF) and oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF), have also been developed; however, these approaches are associated with specific approach-related risks and may not be suitable for all patients [7].

Although PLIF and TLIF have been extensively studied in the management of lumbar spondylolisthesis, most existing comparative studies have focused on single-level disease. Recent literature has begun to explore surgical strategies for multi-level spondylolisthesis; however, direct comparisons between PLIF and TLIF specifically in double-level cases remain limited, and the impact of these techniques on global sagittal alignment in this subgroup has not been fully elucidated. Therefore, additional evidence is needed to better inform surgical decision-making in patients with double-level lumbar spondylolisthesis [8, 9].

Accordingly, the present study was designed as a retrospective comparative analysis to evaluate and compare the clinical outcomes and radiographic changes in patients with double-level lumbar spondylolisthesis treated with PLIF or TLIF. By focusing on this relatively underrepresented patient population, this study aims to provide supplementary clinical evidence regarding the effectiveness of these two commonly used surgical techniques and their influence on sagittal alignment.

Methods

This retrospective comparative study was conducted at a single center and approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University. The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study.

Study design and patient selection

Consecutive patients who underwent lumbar interbody fusion for double-level lumbar spondylolisthesis combined with spinal stenosis between April 2013 and August 2021 were screened. A total of 53 patients were initially identified, of whom 9 were excluded based on predefined criteria, resulting in 44 patients included in the final analysis.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients presenting with low back pain accompanied by radicular symptoms in one or both lower extremities and intermittent claudication; (2) radiographic confirmation of double-level degenerative lumbar spondylolisthesis with imaging evidence of spinal stenosis; (3) failure of conservative treatment for at least 3 months; (4) treatment with PLIF or TLIF combined with pedicle screw fixation performed by the same surgical team; and (5) a minimum follow-up duration of 12 months. The exclusion criteria were: (1) isthmic spondylolisthesis or spondylolisthesis greater than grade II; (2) severe lumbar scoliosis (Cobb angle > 20°); (3) intervertebral bony fusion; (4) history of prior lumbar surgery; and (5) presence of spinal infection, malignancy, or severe systemic disease.

The choice of surgical technique (PLIF or TLIF) was based on surgeon preference and individual patient characteristics, including the degree and location of neural compression, anatomical considerations, and intraoperative judgment. Surgical procedure

All procedures were performed under general anesthesia with the patient in the prone position. Standard posterior midline exposure was used in both groups.

TLIF

In the TLIF group, pedicle screws were inserted under fluoroscopic guidance, followed by unilateral facetectomy and adequate neural decompression. The intervertebral disc was removed, endplates were prepared, and autologous bone graft was placed. An interbody cage was then inserted through a unilateral transforaminal approach, followed by rod fixation and gradual reduction of the slipped vertebra.

PLIF

In the PLIF group, bilateral decompression was performed by removal of the lamina and ligamentum flavum. After discectomy and endplate preparation, autologous bone graft and interbody cages were placed bilaterally. Rod fixation and reduction were subsequently achieved. A drainage tube was placed before wound closure in all cases.

Postoperatively, all patients received routine antibiotic prophylaxis and standard postoperative care. Patients were mobilized with lumbar support after an initial period of rest. Regular follow-up was conducted for at least 12 months.

Clinical and radiographic evaluation

Baseline demographic data, including age, sex, and body mass index (BMI), were recorded. Clinical outcomes were assessed using the visual analog scale (VAS) for back and leg pain, the Oswestry Disability Index (ODI), and the Japanese Orthopaedic Association (JOA) score. The JOA score was used to assess functional status, with higher scores indicating better neurological function. All assessments were performed at predefined time points, including preoperatively and at postoperative follow-up visits.

Standing anteroposterior and lateral full-spine radiographs were obtained at each time point using a standardized protocol, with patients in an upright position, with arms supported in a standardized position, and knees fully extended to minimize variability in sagittal alignment measurements.

Radiographic parameters were measured using Surgimap software (Nemaris Inc., USA) by two independent spine surgeons. The average values were used for analysis to reduce measurement variability. The evaluated parameters included sagittal vertical axis (SVA), T1 pelvic angle (TPA), T1 spinopelvic inclination (T1-SPi), thoracic kyphosis (TK), lumbar lordosis (LL), pelvic tilt (PT), sacral slope (SS), slip distance (SD), and slip angle (SA). Statistical Methods

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 25.0 (IBM, USA). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The normality of data distribution was assessed using appropriate tests prior to analysis.

Depending on data distribution, either parametric or non-parametric tests were applied. Intergroup comparisons were performed using independent-sample tests, and intragroup comparisons were conducted using paired tests. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Given the relatively small sample size, the statistical power of the study may be limited, and the results should be interpreted with caution.

Results

Baseline characteristics and perioperative outcomes

A total of 44 patients were included in this study, comprising 21 patients in the PLIF group and 23 patients in the TLIF group. The cohort consisted of 14 males and 30 females, with a mean age of 67.6 ± 11.4 years. There were 25 cases involving L3–L4 and 19 cases involving L4–L5 spondylolisthesis. No statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups with respect to baseline characteristics, including age, sex, duration of symptoms, and affected levels (P > 0.05).

All procedures were completed without intraoperative conversion or termination. The mean operative time was 3.27 ± 0.71 h in the PLIF group and 3.83 ± 0.56 h in the TLIF group, with no significant difference between groups (P > 0.05). The mean intraoperative blood loss was 337.5 ± 167.83 mL in the PLIF group and 389.47 ± 166.32 mL in the TLIF group, also without a significant difference (P > 0.05). No major intraoperative complications, such as vascular injury, dural tear, or nerve injury, were observed. During follow-up, no cases of implant failure, loosening, or breakage were reported.

Clinical outcomes

Both groups demonstrated significant improvements in VAS scores for back and leg pain, as well as ODI scores, at all postoperative time points compared with preoperative values (P < 0.05). Further improvements were observed at the final follow-up compared with the early postoperative period (P < 0.01).

However, no statistically significant differences were observed between the PLIF and TLIF groups at any time point with respect to VAS scores, ODI scores, or JOA scores (P > 0.05).

Radiographic outcomes

Preoperative sagittal alignment parameters were comparable between the two groups (P > 0.05). All patients exhibited sagittal imbalance preoperatively, with SVA ranging from 49.5 to 160 mm (mean: 71.06 ± 44.04 mm).

Significant improvements in sagittal alignment parameters, including SVA, TPA, T1-SPi, LL, PT, SS, slip distance (SD), and slip angle (SA), were observed postoperatively and maintained at the final follow-up in both groups (P < 0.05). No significant changes were observed in thoracic kyphosis (TK) or pelvic incidence (PI) (P > 0.05). Furthermore, there were no significant differences in radiographic parameters between the postoperative and final follow-up time points (P > 0.05), indicating maintenance of correction over time.

When comparing between groups, the TLIF group showed numerically greater improvements in certain sagittal parameters, including SVA, TPA, and T1-SPi; however, these differences did not reach statistical significance (P > 0.05) (Tables 2, 3 and 4), (Figs. 3 and 4).

Summary of findings

In summary, both PLIF and TLIF resulted in significant improvements in clinical and radiographic outcomes in patients with double-level lumbar spondylolisthesis. No statistically significant differences were identified between the two techniques in terms of perioperative parameters, clinical outcomes, or radiographic measurements.

Discussion

Degenerative lumbar spondylolisthesis is closely associated with progressive intervertebral disc degeneration and facet joint arthropathy, leading to segmental instability and vertebral translation. In addition, ligamentous hypertrophy and disc degeneration may contribute to spinal canal and lateral recess stenosis, resulting in compression of the dural sac and nerve roots [10]. Therefore, the primary objectives of surgical treatment are adequate neural decompression, restoration of intervertebral height, and re-establishment of spinal stability [11, 12].

Sagittal alignment plays a critical role in maintaining an energy-efficient upright posture. Disruption of normal spinal curvature due to vertebral slippage and degenerative changes often leads to compensatory mechanisms, including pelvic retroversion and anterior trunk inclination (LL) [13, 14]. Previous studies have shown that patients with double-level spondylolisthesis tend to exhibit more pronounced sagittal imbalance compared with single-level disease, which may negatively affect functional outcomes and quality of life [15, 16].

In the present study, both PLIF and TLIF resulted in significant improvements in clinical outcomes and sagittal alignment parameters. Schwab et al. [17] demonstrated that increased PT and SVA are strongly associated with worse functional outcomes in adult spinal deformity. Similarly, Lafage et al. [18] reported that increased PT correlates with decreased quality of life, and that surgical correction of sagittal alignment may improve patient-reported outcomes. VAS scores and ODI values improved significantly after surgery in both groups, which is consistent with previous reports demonstrating that interbody fusion effectively relieves symptoms and improves functional status in patients with lumbar spondylolisthesis. Similarly, significant improvements in SVA, TPA, and T1-SPi were observed postoperatively, indicating effective correction of global sagittal imbalance.

To comprehensively evaluate sagittal alignment, multiple radiographic parameters were analyzed. Among these, TPA is considered a relatively stable parameter that integrates both spinal inclination and pelvic tilt. In the present study, postoperative improvements in SVA, TPA, and T1-SPi suggest that both surgical techniques contribute to the restoration of sagittal balance [19, 20]. However, no significant changes were observed in thoracic kyphosis (TK), which may reflect limited compensatory capacity of the thoracolumbar junction in elderly patients with chronic degenerative changes.

Interbody fusion techniques are widely used in the treatment of lumbar spondylolisthesis. In addition to posterior approaches (PLIF and TLIF), anterior and lateral approaches (ALIF/OLIF) have also been developed [21]. Although ALIF and OLIF allow placement of larger cages and preservation of posterior elements, they are associated with approach-related complications such as vascular or visceral injury [22, 23]. In contrast, PLIF and TLIF provide direct posterior decompression and stabilization and therefore remain commonly used surgical options in clinical practice.

TLIF is performed through a unilateral approach, which may reduce neural retraction and preserve contralateral posterior structures. This technique may decrease the risk of nerve root injury and facilitate restoration of lumbar lordosis and sagittal alignment [24]. In the present study, both TLIF and PLIF achieved significant improvements in slip distance (SD) and slip angle (SA), indicating effective reduction and stabilization. No significant differences were observed between the two techniques in these parameters. In terms of reduction strategy, it has been suggested that excessive pursuit of anatomical reduction may increase the risk of nerve root traction or injury, and therefore a balanced approach to reduction is recommended [25].

Regarding sagittal alignment, TLIF demonstrated more favorable improvement in certain parameters, including SVA, TPA, and T1-SPi, compared with PLIF. These findings suggest that TLIF may be advantageous in restoring global sagittal balance. However, it is important to note that these radiographic differences were not consistently associated with significant differences in clinical outcomes, as reflected by VAS, ODI, and JOA scores. Therefore, the potential advantages of TLIF in sagittal correction should be interpreted with caution.

Restoration of sagittal alignment is an important goal in the surgical management of spinal deformity. According to the SRS-Schwab classification, optimal alignment is defined as LL = PI ± 9°, PT < 20°, and SVA < 50 mm [26]. Failure to achieve these targets has been associated with increased risk of complications, including adjacent segment degeneration and implant-related failure [27]. In this study, postoperative improvements in LL and SS contributed to the correction of global sagittal alignment, which is consistent with previous reports emphasizing the importance of restoring lumbar lordosis to achieve optimal sagittal balance [28]. However, it should be emphasized that pelvic incidence (PI) is a morphological parameter that remains constant in adults. Any observed variation in PI in this study is likely attributable to measurement variability or differences in patient positioning rather than true anatomical change; therefore, PI should be interpreted cautiously and is not an appropriate parameter for assessing postoperative correction.

From a surgical perspective, preservation of posterior elements and minimization of soft tissue disruption are important considerations. TLIF preserves contralateral posterior structures and may better maintain spinal stability, whereas PLIF requires more extensive posterior decompression. This difference in preservation of posterior stabilizing structures may partly explain the observed variations in sagittal alignment restoration. Nevertheless, given the absence of significant differences in clinical outcomes, both techniques can be considered effective treatment options for double-level lumbar spondylolisthesis.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center retrospective study with a relatively small sample size, which may limit statistical power and reduce the ability to detect between-group differences. Second, the choice of surgical technique was not randomized and was influenced by surgeon preference and patient-specific factors, which may introduce selection bias. Third, fusion-related outcomes, such as fusion rate, cage subsidence, and pseudarthrosis, were not evaluated. In addition, the clinical relevance of the observed changes was not further assessed using minimal clinically important difference thresholds. Although radiographic measurements were performed by experienced observers, measurement variability cannot be completely excluded. Finally, the follow-up duration was relatively limited. Future studies with larger sample sizes, multicenter design, and longer follow-up are required to further validate these findings.

Conclusion

In summary, both PLIF and TLIF are effective surgical options for the treatment of double-level lumbar spondylolisthesis combined with spinal stenosis, resulting in significant improvements in clinical outcomes and radiographic parameters. TLIF may provide more favorable restoration of certain sagittal alignment parameters; however, no significant differences in clinical outcomes were observed between the two techniques. Therefore, the potential advantages of TLIF in sagittal alignment correction should be interpreted with caution.
