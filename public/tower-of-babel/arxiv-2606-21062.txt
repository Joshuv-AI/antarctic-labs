1.
Introduction

On a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
, the Ricci form
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})
represents the first Chern class
c_{1}(M)
up to a factor of
2\pi
:

[\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})]=2\pi c_{1}(M)\in H^{1,1}(M,{\mathbb{R}}).

The celebrated solution to the Calabi conjecture by Yau
[
Cal57
,
Yau78
]
establishes the converse–namely, the existence of metrics with prescribed Ricci curvature–forging a definitive link between geometry and topology:

Theorem A
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold. For any closed real
(1,1)
-form
\Omega
representing
2\pi c_{1}(M)
, there exists a unique Kähler metric
\omega\in[\omega_{g}]
such that

(1.1)

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)=\Omega.

Note that, since
\omega
is Kähler, the Calabi-Yau equation (
1.1
) is equivalent to

(1.2)

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{\omega}\right)=\Omega.

The Calabi-Yau theorem has become a cornerstone of modern geometry, profoundly impacting differential geometry, algebraic geometry, and mathematical physics. Its significance extends beyond the Kähler setting to general Hermitian manifolds, where analogous existence results have been developed; see, e.g.,
[
FY08
,
TW10
,
STW17
]
and the extensive references therein.

Let’s recall the classical Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau theorem (
[
Don85
,
UY86
,
Don87
]
) for stable vector bundles (see also the Hermitian analogue in
[
LY86
]
):

Theorem B
.

For a stable holomorphic vector bundle
E
over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
, there exists a unique Hermitian-Einstein metric
h
on
E
up to scaling, satisfying

(1.3)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=\lambda_{E}\cdot h,

where
R^{h}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
is the Chern curvature tensor of
(E,h)
and

\lambda_{E}=\frac{2\pi n\int_{M}c_{1}(E)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1}}{\mathrm{rank}(E)\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}}.

This foundational result established a deep equivalence on compact Kähler manifolds: a holomorphic vector bundle admits a Hermitian–Einstein metric if and only if it is polystable in the sense of Mumford–Takemoto. This forged a critical bridge between differential geometry—the existence of special metrics solving a nonlinear PDE—and algebraic geometry, translating the purely algebraic notion of stability into an analytic criterion. The correspondence not only unified two seemingly disparate fields but also laid the essential groundwork for the development of complex geometry, gauge theory and representation theory. For comprehensive technical details, we refer the reader to
[
NS65
,
Hit87
,
Sim88
,
Sim92
]
and the extensive references cited therein.

In the series of papers
[
WYY26+
,
FWYY26+
,
WYY26b+
]
, the second and third authors, together with M.-W. Wang and J.-X. Fang, established a vector bundle analogue of the Calabi–Yau theorem. This result provides a solution to the prescribed Hermitian–Yang–Mills tensor equation, extending the classical Hermitian–Einstein existence theory to the case of fully prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills curvature tensors.

Theorem C
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that there exists a smooth Hermitian metric
h^{E}
on
E
such that

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h^{E}}\right)>0.

Then, for any positive-definite Hermitian tensor
P\in\Gamma\left(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}\right)
, there exists a unique smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
satisfying

(1.4)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)=P.

The prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor equation (
1.4
) interpolates between the Calabi-Yau equation (
1.2
) and the Hermitian-Einstein equation (
1.3
). By inheriting the geometric and analytic features of both, it serves as a pivotal bridge connecting the geometry of vector bundles to the broader realm of Kähler and Hermitian geometry.
To dynamically construct these canonical metrics, we introduce the prescribed Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow, which serves as the parabolic analogue of the elliptic equation (
1.4
):

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}=-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)+P,\\[10.0pt]
h(0)=h_{0}.\end{cases}

This flow is analogous to the Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow (
[
Don85
]
), the Ricci flow (
[
Ham82
]
), the Kähler-Ricci flow (
[
Cao85
]
), and the Chern-Ricci flow (
[
TW15
]
), providing a heat-flow method to deform an initial metric toward the solution guaranteed by Theorem
C
. More generally, we consider a broader class of flows of the form

(1.6)

\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}=-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)+\mathcal{F}_{0}(h),

where
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is a general map taking values in
\mathrm{Herm}(E)
; in particular, it includes the curvature associated with a Higgs bundle. Specific instances include:

(i)

\mathcal{F}_{0}(h)=P
for some fixed
P\in\mathrm{Herm}(E)
;

(ii)

\mathcal{F}_{0}(h)=\lambda h
for
\lambda\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
;

(iii)

\mathcal{F}_{0}(h)=\lambda h+P+(\mathrm{tr}_{h_{0}}h)h_{0}
for some fixed Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
.

It is well-known that a priori uniform
C^{0}
-estimates are crucial for the geometric flow theory. The first main result of this paper is a parabolic comparison principle, which yields uniform
C^{0}
-estimates for the flows under consideration. For simplicity, we present a basic version here that applies to the prescribed Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow (
1.5
). A general version is established in Theorem
3.4
, providing uniform
C^{0}
-estimates for a broad class of flows of the form (
1.6
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold, and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
M
. Suppose that
\underline{h}(t)
,
h(t)
and
\overline{h}(t)
are smooth time-dependent Hermitian metrics on
E
defined for
t\in[0,T)
with
T\leq\infty
.

(1)

If
\overline{h}(0)\geq h(0)
and

(1.7)

\frac{{\partial}\overline{h}}{{\partial}t}-\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}t}\geq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{\overline{h}}\right),

then for any
t\in[0,T)
, one has

(1.8)

\overline{h}(t)\geq h(t).

(1’)

If
\underline{h}(0)\leq h(0)
and

(1.9)

\frac{{\partial}\underline{h}}{{\partial}t}-\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}t}\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{\underline{h}}\right),

then for any
t\in[0,T)
, one has

(1.10)

\underline{h}(t)\leq h(t).

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we establish the following long-time convergence and existence results, which generalize Theorem
C
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that there exists a smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)

is quasi-positive as a Hermitian tensor in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
.
Then, for any quasi-positive tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
and any initial Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
, the prescribed Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow (
1.5
) exists for all
t\in[0,\infty)
and converges smoothly at an exponential rate to the
unique
smooth Hermitian metric
h_{\infty}
on
E
satisfying

(1.11)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h_{\infty}}\right)=P.

We outline the proof of Theorem
1.2
. First, we construct two smooth Hermitian barrier metrics
\underline{h}
and
\overline{h}
on
E
satisfying

(1.12)

\underline{h}\leq h_{0}\leq\overline{h},\qquad\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{\underline{h}}\right)\leq P\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{\overline{h}}\right).

By applying Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the uniform
C^{0}
-estimate along the flow (
1.5
):

(1.13)

\underline{h}\leq h(t)\leq\overline{h}.

By using the evolution equation, we prove the following exponential decay estimate:

(1.14)

\sup_{M}\left|\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h(t)}\right)-P\right|_{h(t)}\leq C_{2}e^{-C_{1}t}.

Combining these uniform bounds with the exponential decay, we conclude the long-time existence and convergence of the flow to a limiting metric
h_{\infty}
satisfying (
1.11
). A central innovation is that our proof entirely
avoids
Donaldson’s functional, relying solely on direct geometric estimates. Consequently, our results are valid on general Hermitian manifolds, unlike the classical theory which necessitates the Kähler condition.

The following theorem extends the strategy established in the proof of Theorem
1.2
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler (or balanced) manifold

(M,\omega_{g})
. For given Hermitian tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, if there exist two Hermitian metrics
\underline{h}
and
\overline{h}
on
E
such that

(1.15)

\underline{h}\leq\overline{h},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{\underline{h}}\right)\leq P\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{\overline{h}}\right),

then for any initial metric
h_{0}
satisfying
\underline{h}\leq h_{0}\leq\overline{h}
, the
prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow (
1.5
)
exists for all
t\in[0,\infty)
and
\underline{h}\leq h(t)\leq\overline{h}
. Moreover, for any sequence
\{t_{i}\}\rightarrow\infty
, there exists a subsequence
\{t_{i_{m}}\}
such that
\{h(t_{i_{m}})\}
converges smoothly to a Hermitian metric
h_{\infty}
on
E
satisfying

(1.16)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\infty}}\right)=P.

As we pointed out in Example
6.4
, the prescribed Hermitian tensor
P
in Theorem
1.3
may have e a negative
eigenvalue at some point, and
it need not be positive
. The proof of Theorem
1.3
proceeds as follows: The uniform
C^{0}
-estimate along the flow is established by Theorem
1.1
; higher-order estimates and long-time existence are proved in Theorem
2.5
and Theorem
3.7
; finally, the long-time convergence is established via an analog of the Donaldson functional stated in Theorem
5.2
.
We note that the solution to equation (
1.16
) may not be unique and that the limiting metric
h_{\infty}
depends on the initial metric
h_{0}
, which are significantly different from those in Theorem
1.2
. Further generalizations of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
, along with detailed discussions and examples, are presented in Section
6
.

The following result is a direct consequence of Theorem
1.3
, and can be viewed as a
compactness theorem
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler (or balanced) manifold

(M,\omega_{g})
. For given Hermitian tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, if there exist two Hermitian metrics
\underline{h}
and
\overline{h}
on
E
such that

(1.17)

\underline{h}\leq\overline{h},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{\underline{h}}\right)\leq P\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{\overline{h}}\right),

then there exists a smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
which
solves the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor
equation:

(1.18)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=P.

The prescribed Hermitian–Yang–Mills tensor equation (
1.18
) in Corollary
1.4
is solvable for general
P
that need not be non-negative. Furthermore, the solution is not necessarily unique. For instance, if
E
is a unitary flat bundle, the equation
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)=0
admits multiple distinct solutions obtained by rescaling the metric on each indecomposable component of
E
.

Remark 1.5
.

(1)

By Serre duality, the corresponding statements of Theorem
1.3
remain valid for negative bundles; see
[
WYY26+
]
. Building on the analogy with results established in
[
FWYY26+
]
, we demonstrate that these principal results also hold for Higgs bundles over compact Hermitian manifolds.

(2)

In Theorem
3.4
, we established the uniform
C^{0}
-estimate for a general class of flows

(1.19)

\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}=-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)+\mathcal{F}_{0}(h).

The corresponding convergence theory will be addressed in future work. For instance, similar results in this paper hold for the twisted Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow:

(1.20)

\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}=-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)+\lambda h+P,

upon straightforward modification to the arguments, as indicated in
[
WYY26b+
]
.

(3)

Analogous results are expected to hold for the flow defined by RC-positivity and will be pursued in separate projects.

2.
Evolution equations and higher-order estimates

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension

n
, and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
of rank
r
.
Given a smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
, the Chern connection of

(E,h)
is denoted by
\nabla^{h}
. We shall use the natural
decomposition
\nabla^{h}={\partial}^{h}+{\overline{\partial}}
, where
{\partial}^{h}
is the

(1,0)
part and
{\overline{\partial}}
is the
(0,1)
part. Let
R^{h}
be the Chern
curvature tensor of
(E,h)
. In local holomorphic coordinates

\{z^{i}\}
of
M
and local holomorphic frame
\{e_{\alpha}\}
of
E
,

(2.1)

R^{h}=R_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes\overline{e}^{\beta}\in\Gamma\left(M,\wedge^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}\right),

where

(2.2)

R_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}=-\frac{{\partial}^{2}h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}}{{\partial}z^{i}{\partial}\overline{z}^{j}}+h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\frac{{\partial}h_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}}{{\partial}z^{i}}\frac{{\partial}h_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}}{{\partial}\overline{z}^{j}}.

The
Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor

S^{h}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
of
(E,h)
is defined as

(2.3)

S^{h}:=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=\left(g^{i\overline{j}}R_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}\right)e^{\alpha}\otimes\overline{e}^{\beta}.

To make the computations transparent, we adopt the following
notation throughout the paper. By using the Hermitian
h
, there
are natural lifts of
R^{h}
and
S^{h}
:

(2.4)

\Theta^{h}=R^{h}\cdot h^{-1}=R_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\beta}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes{e}_{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes E)

and

(2.5)

K^{h}=S^{h}\cdot h^{-1}=\left(g^{i\overline{j}}R_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\beta}\right)e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E).

We say that
(M,\omega_{g})
is a
Gauduchon manifold
if

\partial\overline{\partial}\,\omega_{g}^{\,n-1}=0.

It is well known that, for any Hermitian metric
\omega
on
M
,
there exists a smooth function
f
such that

\omega_{g}=e^{f}\omega

satisfies
\partial\overline{\partial}\,\omega_{g}^{\,n-1}=0
(
[
Gau84
]
). A Hermitian metric
\omega_{g}
is called
balanced
if
d\omega_{g}^{n-1}=0
. We use the following notations:

•

\mathrm{Herm}(E)
: the space of Hermitian tensors in

\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
;

•

\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
: the subspace consisting of
positive-definite Hermitian tensors;

•

\mathrm{Herm}^{\geq 0}(E)
: the subspace consisting of
non-negative Hermitian tensors.

2.1.
Some evolution equations

The prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow (
1.5
) can be written locally as

(2.6)

\frac{{\partial}h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}}{{\partial}t}=-S^{h}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}+P_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}.

The convergence of the term

S^{h}-P

is the central focus of this paper. For notational simplicity, we define

(2.7)

\widetilde{S}=S^{h}-P.

We also use the notation

\Delta_{{\mathbb{C}}}f=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}{\partial}{\overline{\partial}}f\right)

for
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The evolution equation of
|\widetilde{S}|^{2}_{h}
along the flow (
1.5
) is

(2.8)

\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)|\widetilde{S}|_{h}^{2}=-2\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}P_{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}h^{\alpha\overline{\mu}}h^{\lambda\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\beta}}-\left|\nabla\widetilde{S}\right|_{g,h}^{2},

where
\nabla
denotes the connection on
E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}
induced by
(E,h)
.

Proof.

The flow equation
\frac{{\partial}h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}}{{\partial}t}=-\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}
gives

(2.9)

\frac{{\partial}h^{\alpha\overline{\beta}}}{{\partial}t}=-h^{\alpha\overline{\mu}}\frac{{\partial}h_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}}{{\partial}t}h^{\lambda\overline{\beta}}=h^{\alpha\overline{\mu}}\widetilde{S}_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}h^{\lambda\overline{\beta}}.

Using the Christoffel symbols
\Gamma_{i\alpha}^{\gamma}=h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\frac{{\partial}h_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}}{{\partial}z^{i}}
, we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}\Gamma_{i\alpha}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}}{{\partial}t}\frac{{\partial}h_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}}{{\partial}z^{i}}+h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}z^{i}}\left(\frac{{\partial}h_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}}{{\partial}t}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{\gamma\overline{\eta}}\widetilde{S}_{\lambda\overline{\eta}}h^{\lambda\overline{\delta}}\frac{{\partial}h_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}}{{\partial}z^{i}}-h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\frac{{\partial}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}}{{\partial}z^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\nabla_{i}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}.

The Chern curvature formula

R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}=-\frac{{\partial}\Gamma_{i\alpha}^{\gamma}}{{\partial}\overline{z}^{j}}h_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}

yields

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\overline{z}^{j}}\left(\frac{{\partial}\Gamma_{i\alpha}^{\gamma}}{{\partial}t}\right)h_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}-\frac{{\partial}\Gamma_{i\alpha}^{\gamma}}{{\partial}\overline{z}^{j}}\frac{{\partial}h_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}}{{\partial}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\overline{z}^{j}}\left(h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\nabla_{i}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}\right)h_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}+\frac{{\partial}\Gamma_{i\alpha}^{\gamma}}{{\partial}\overline{z}^{j}}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}-R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\overline{\alpha}\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}.

In particular, we deduce that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}S^{h}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=g^{i\overline{j}}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}-S^{h}_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}-\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\gamma}}h^{\delta\overline{\gamma}}S^{h}_{\delta\overline{\beta}}.

Here the last equality follows from the Ricci identity

(2.13)

\nabla_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}+R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}-R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\delta\overline{\beta}}h^{\delta\overline{\gamma}}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\gamma}}.

Since
|\widetilde{S}|_{h}^{2}=\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\delta}}h^{\alpha\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\beta}}
and
\widetilde{S}
is Hermitian,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}|\widetilde{S}|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}}{{\partial}t}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\delta}}h^{\alpha\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\beta}}+\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\frac{{\partial}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\delta}}}{{\partial}t}h^{\alpha\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\beta}}

\displaystyle+\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\delta}}h^{\alpha\overline{\mu}}\widetilde{S}_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}h^{\lambda\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\beta}}+\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\delta}}h^{\alpha\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\mu}}\widetilde{S}_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}h^{\lambda\overline{\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{\partial}_{t}\widetilde{S},\widetilde{S}\right\rangle_{h}+\left\langle\widetilde{S},{\partial}_{t}\widetilde{S}\right\rangle_{h}+2\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}h^{\alpha\overline{\mu}}h^{\lambda\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\beta}}.

The Ricci identity yields

(2.15)

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}-g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}-g^{i\overline{j}}R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\gamma\overline{\beta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{h}_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}-S^{h}_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-S^{h}_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}P_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}+S^{h}_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}P_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}.

A straightforward computation shows

(2.16)

\left\langle g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\widetilde{S}-g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\widetilde{S},\widetilde{S}\right\rangle_{h}=0.

Therefore,

(2.17)

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\mathbb{C}}}|\widetilde{S}|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\widetilde{S},\widetilde{S}\right\rangle_{h}+\left\langle\widetilde{S},g^{j\overline{i}}\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{j}\widetilde{S}\right\rangle_{h}+\left|\nabla\widetilde{S}\right|_{g,h}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{Re}\left(\left\langle g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\widetilde{S},\widetilde{S}\right\rangle_{h}\right)+\left|\nabla\widetilde{S}\right|_{g,h}^{2}.

Combining (
2.14
) and (
2.17
), we get

(2.18)

\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)|\widetilde{S}|_{h}^{2}=2\mathrm{Re}\left(\left\langle{\partial}_{t}\widetilde{S}-g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\widetilde{S},\widetilde{S}\right\rangle_{h}\right)+2\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}h^{\alpha\overline{\mu}}h^{\lambda\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\beta}}-\left|\nabla\widetilde{S}\right|_{g,h}^{2}.

By the evolution equation (
2.12
), one has

(2.19)

2\mathrm{Re}\left(\left\langle{\partial}_{t}\widetilde{S}-g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\widetilde{S},\widetilde{S}\right\rangle_{h}\right)=-2\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}S^{h}_{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}h^{\alpha\overline{\mu}}h^{\lambda\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\beta}}.

Substituting (
2.19
) into (
2.18
) yields

(2.20)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)|\widetilde{S}|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}S^{h}_{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}h^{\alpha\overline{\mu}}h^{\lambda\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\beta}}+2\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\widetilde{S}_{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}h^{\alpha\overline{\mu}}h^{\lambda\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\beta}}-\left|\nabla\widetilde{S}\right|_{g,h}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}P_{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}h^{\alpha\overline{\mu}}h^{\lambda\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\beta}}-\left|\nabla\widetilde{S}\right|_{g,h}^{2}.

This is (
2.8
).
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

The following estimate holds:

(2.21)

\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)|\widetilde{S}|_{h}^{2}\leq-2\lambda^{P}_{\min}\cdot|\widetilde{S}|_{h}^{2},

where

(2.22)

\lambda^{P}_{\min}(x,t):=\inf_{v\in E_{x},v\neq 0}\frac{P(v,\overline{v})}{h(t)(v,\overline{v})}.

Proof.

Fix a point
q\in M
and a time
t
. Choose an
h(t)
-unitary basis of

E
such that

h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=\delta_{\alpha\beta},\quad\mbox{and}\quad P_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=\lambda_{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha\beta},

where

\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{r}
. One has

\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}P_{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\widetilde{S}_{\lambda\overline{\mu}}h^{\alpha\overline{\mu}}h^{\lambda\overline{\delta}}h^{\gamma\overline{\beta}}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda_{\beta}|\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}|^{2}\geq\lambda_{1}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}|\widetilde{S}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}|^{2}=\lambda^{P}_{\min}|\widetilde{S}|_{h}^{2}.

The desired estimate now follows from Lemma
2.1
by discarding
\left|\nabla\widetilde{S}\right|_{g,h}^{2}
.
∎

The following transformation formula for the
Hermitian-Yang-Mills curvature tensors is essentially well-known
(e.g.
[
FWYY26+
, Proposition 2.8]
)

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be
a compact Hermitian manifold and
E\rightarrow M
be a holomorphic vector

h
and
h_{0}
are smooth Hermitian metrics on

(2.23)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}{\overline{\partial}}\left(({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{-1}\right)

2.2.
The long-time existence under uniform
C^{0}
-estimates

In this subsection, we establish a long-time existence theorem for the flow (
1.5
) on general Hermitian manifolds, under certain assumptions.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold, and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
.
Suppose that
h_{t}
,
0\leq t<T<\infty
, is a smooth solution to the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow (
1.5
). If there exist constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

(2.24)

C_{1}^{-1}h_{0}\leq h_{t}\leq C_{1}h_{0},\qquad\sup_{M}\left|S^{h_{t}}-P\right|_{h_{t}}\leq C_{2}

for all
t\in[0,T)
, then
\{h_{t}\}
converges in
C^{\infty}
to a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{T}
as
t\to T
. In particular, the flow extends smoothly beyond
T
.

The proof of Theorem
2.4
relies on a careful adaptation of the classical theory of elliptic and parabolic equations. While the existing literature primarily addresses the Kähler case with
P=0
(e.g.,
[
Don85
]
,
[
Siu87
]
), we provide an independent proof applicable to the non-Kähler setting, thereby ensuring the exposition remains self-contained.

Let
\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
be the space of Hermitian metrics on
E
. The right-hand side of (
1.5
) defines a smooth second-order differential operator

L:\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Herm}(E).

For
h\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
and
\eta\in\mathrm{Herm}(E)
, the linearization of
L
is given by

(2.25)

DL_{h}(\eta)_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=g^{i\overline{j}}\frac{{\partial}^{2}\eta_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}}{{\partial}z^{i}{\partial}\overline{z}^{j}}+\text{lower order terms},

which is strongly elliptic. By appealing to the short-time existence theory for quasilinear parabolic systems on compact manifolds (e.g.,
[
Lie96
, Chapter XIII, Section 7]
), we obtain a unique smooth solution to (
1.5
) defined on a maximal time interval
[0,T)
with
T>0
.
For higher-order estimates, we adopt the following conventions. On the Hermitian vector bundle
(E,h_{0})
over
(M,\omega_{g})
, we define the
C^{m}
-norm of a smooth section
\varphi\in\Gamma\left(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}\right)
with respect to
\omega_{g}
and
h_{0}
by

(2.26)

\left\|\varphi\right\|_{C^{m}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}:=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sup_{M}\left|\left(\nabla^{h_{0}}\right)^{j}\varphi\right|_{g,h_{0}},

where
\nabla^{h_{0}}
is the covariant derivative induced by Chern connections of
(E,h_{0})
and
(T^{1,0}M,\omega_{g})
. We fix a finite open cover
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{N}
of
M
such that each
U_{\mu}
is relatively compact in a larger domain
\widetilde{U}_{\mu}
, equipped with holomorphic coordinates and a local holomorphic frame
\{e_{1}^{(\mu)},\dots,e_{r}^{(\mu)}\}
of
E
.
With respect to these local frames, the global
C^{m}
-norm (
2.26
) is equivalent to the local coordinate norm

(2.27)

\sum_{\mu}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sum_{|I|\leq m}\sup_{U_{\mu}}\bigl|\partial^{I}\varphi_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\bigr|,

where
I
denotes a multi-index. The constants implicit in this equivalence depend only on
\omega_{g}
,
h_{0}
, and the chosen finite cover. We also employ parabolic norms locally, after fixing the coordinate charts and frames described above. For a cylinder

Q=B_{r}(x_{\mu})\times I\Subset U_{\mu}\times{\mathbb{R}},

we write
x=(x^{1},\cdots,x^{2n})
for the associated real coordinates. For a scalar function
f(x,t)
defined on
Q
and
1<p<\infty
, we define the parabolic
W^{2,p}
-norm by

(2.28)

\left\|f\right\|_{W^{2,p}_{t}(Q)}:=\sum_{|\nu|\leq 2}\left\|{\partial}_{x}^{\nu}f\right\|_{L^{p}(Q)}+\left\|{\partial}_{t}f\right\|_{L^{p}(Q)}.

For
0<\alpha<1
, define

(2.29)

d((x,t),(y,s)):=|x-y|+|t-s|^{1/2},\qquad[f]_{\alpha;Q}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,t),(y,s)\in Q\\
(x,t)\neq(y,s)\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(x,t)-f(y,s)|}{d((x,t),(y,s))^{\alpha}}.

For an integer
k\geq 0
, the parabolic Hölder norm (e.g.,
[
Lie96
, Chapter IV, Section 1]
) is

(2.30)

\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k+\alpha,(k+\alpha)/2}(Q)}:=\sum_{|\nu|+2j\leq k}\left\|{\partial}_{x}^{\nu}{\partial}_{t}^{j}f\right\|_{C^{0}(Q)}+\sum_{|\nu|+2j=k}\left[{\partial}_{x}^{\nu}{\partial}_{t}^{j}f\right]_{\alpha;Q}.

A crucial ingredient in the proof of Theorem
2.4
is the higher-order estimate for the Hermitian metrics
h_{t}
established below.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold, and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
.
Suppose that
h_{t}
,
0\leq t<T\leq\infty
, is a smooth solution to the flow (
1.5
). If there exist constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

(2.31)

C_{1}^{-1}h_{0}\leq h_{t}\leq C_{1}h_{0},\qquad\sup_{M}\left|S^{h_{t}}-P\right|_{h_{t}}\leq C_{2}

for all
t\in[0,T)
, then for every
\tau\in(0,T)
and every integer
m\geq 0
, there exists a positive constant
C_{m,\tau}=C_{m,\tau}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P,C_{1},C_{2})
such that

(2.32)

\left\|h_{t}\right\|_{C^{m}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq C_{m,\tau},\qquad t\in[\tau,T).

Proof.

We first show that if a smooth Hermitian metric
k
satisfies

(2.33)

C_{1}^{-1}h_{0}\leq k\leq C_{1}h_{0},\qquad\sup_{M}\left|S^{k}\right|_{k}\leq C_{2},

then
\left\|k\right\|_{C^{1}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}
is uniformly bounded by a constant depending only on
\omega_{g}
,
h_{0}
,
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. Otherwise, there are Hermitian metrics
k_{i}
satisfying (
2.33
) and points
x_{i}\to x_{\infty}
such that

(2.34)

m_{i}:=\sup_{M}\left|\nabla^{h_{0}}k_{i}\right|_{h_{0}}=\left|\nabla^{h_{0}}k_{i}\right|_{h_{0}}(x_{i})\to+\infty.

We choose holomorphic coordinates
\{z^{A}\}
and a holomorphic frame
\{e_{\alpha}\}
centered at
x_{\infty}
such that
g_{A\overline{B}}(x_{\infty})=\delta_{AB}
and
h_{0,\alpha\overline{\beta}}(x_{\infty})=\delta_{\alpha\beta}
. We select
\rho>0
so that the coordinate chart contains
D_{3\rho}
. After passing to a subsequence, we have
z_{i}=z(x_{i})\in D_{\rho}
.
Since
k_{i}
are uniformly bounded, and

dk_{i}-\nabla^{h_{0}}k_{i}

are zeroth order terms of
k_{i}
, there exists a uniform constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(h_{0},\omega_{g},C_{1})
such that

(2.35)

|dk_{i}|(x_{i})\geq m_{i}-c_{0},\qquad\sup_{D_{2\rho}}|dk_{i}|\leq m_{i}+c_{0}.

Here,
dk_{i}
denotes the Euclidean gradient of the component matrix
k_{i,\alpha\overline{\beta}}
with respect to the spatial coordinates, and its norm is computed via the standard Euclidean norm over all coordinate and bundle indices. Define rescaled tensors
\widetilde{k}_{i}\in C^{\infty}\left(D_{m_{i}\rho},{\mathbb{C}}^{r\times r}\right)
by

(2.36)

\widetilde{k}_{i}(w):=k_{i}(z_{i}+w/m_{i}).

Given any
R>0
,
\widetilde{k}_{i}
is defined on
D_{R}
provided that
i
is sufficiently large, and

(2.37)

\left|d\widetilde{k}_{i}\right|(0)\geq 1/2,\qquad\sup_{D_{R}}\left|d\widetilde{k}_{i}\right|\leq 2.

In the chosen frames,

(2.38)

S^{k_{i}}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=-g^{A\overline{B}}\frac{{\partial}^{2}k_{i,\alpha\overline{\beta}}}{{\partial}z^{A}{\partial}\overline{z}^{B}}+g^{A\overline{B}}k_{i}^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\frac{{\partial}k_{i,\alpha\overline{\delta}}}{{\partial}z^{A}}\frac{{\partial}k_{i,\gamma\overline{\beta}}}{{\partial}\overline{z}^{B}}.

Hence the rescaled metrics solve

(2.39)

g_{i}^{A\overline{B}}\frac{{\partial}^{2}\widetilde{k}_{i,\alpha\overline{\beta}}}{{\partial}w^{A}{\partial}\overline{w}^{B}}=g_{i}^{A\overline{B}}\widetilde{k}_{i}^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\frac{{\partial}\widetilde{k}_{i,\alpha\overline{\delta}}}{{\partial}w^{A}}\frac{{\partial}\widetilde{k}_{i,\gamma\overline{\beta}}}{{\partial}\overline{w}^{B}}-\frac{1}{m_{i}^{2}}S_{i,\alpha\overline{\beta}},

where
g_{i}^{A\overline{B}}(w)=g^{A\overline{B}}(z_{i}+w/m_{i})
and
S_{i}(w)=S^{k_{i}}(z_{i}+w/m_{i})
.
By (
2.37
) and (
2.33
), the right-hand side of (
2.39
) is uniformly bounded on each fixed
D_{R}
. Fix
p>2n
and choose
\alpha\in(0,1-2n/p)
. By the interior elliptic
W^{2,p}
-estimates and the compact embedding
W^{2,p}\hookrightarrow C^{1,\alpha}
, there exists a subsequence
\widetilde{k}_{i_{s}}
of
\widetilde{k}_{i}
and
\widetilde{k}_{\infty}\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{C}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{r\times r})
, such that

(2.40)

\lim\|\widetilde{k}_{i_{s}}-\widetilde{k}_{\infty}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{D_{R}},g_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})}=0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widetilde{k}_{i_{s}}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{k}_{\infty}\quad\text{weakly in }W^{2,p}({D_{R}},g_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}).

In particular, we have
|d\widetilde{k}_{\infty}|(0)\geq 1/2
and
\widetilde{k}_{\infty}
is a local
C^{1,\alpha}
Hermitian metric.
By passing to the weak limit in (
2.39
), we deduce that
\widetilde{k}_{\infty}
satisfies the following elliptic equation on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
in the distribution sense

(2.41)

\Delta_{0}\widetilde{k}_{\infty}=\sum_{A}({\partial}_{A}\widetilde{k}_{\infty})\widetilde{k}_{\infty}^{-1}({\partial}_{\overline{A}}\widetilde{k}_{\infty}),

where
\Delta_{0}=\sum_{A}\frac{{\partial}^{2}}{{\partial}w^{A}{\partial}\overline{w}^{A}}
.
Since the right-hand side of (
2.41
) is locally
C^{\alpha}
, the bootstrap process shows that
\widetilde{k}_{\infty}
is smooth. Moreover, for any fixed
\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}\setminus\{0\}
, the function
u_{\xi}=\log\left(\xi^{*}\widetilde{k}_{\infty}\xi\right)
is bounded and satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}u_{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\xi^{*}\Delta_{0}\widetilde{k}_{\infty}\xi}{\xi^{*}\widetilde{k}_{\infty}\xi}-\frac{\sum_{A}\left|\xi^{*}\left({\partial}_{A}\widetilde{k}_{\infty}\right)\xi\right|^{2}}{\left(\xi^{*}\widetilde{k}_{\infty}\xi\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{A}\xi^{*}\left({\partial}_{A}\widetilde{k}_{\infty}\right)\widetilde{k}_{\infty}^{-1}\left({\partial}_{\overline{A}}\widetilde{k}_{\infty}\right)\xi}{\xi^{*}\widetilde{k}_{\infty}\xi}-\frac{\sum_{A}\left|\xi^{*}\left({\partial}_{A}\widetilde{k}_{\infty}\right)\xi\right|^{2}}{\left(\xi^{*}\widetilde{k}_{\infty}\xi\right)^{2}}\geq 0,

by Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Hence
u_{\xi}
is constant. Since
\xi
is arbitrary,
\widetilde{k}_{\infty}
is constant. This is a contradiction since
|d\widetilde{k}_{\infty}|(0)\geq 1/2
.

For
\{h_{t}\}
along the flow (
1.5
), one has

(2.42)

\sup_{M}\left|S^{h_{t}}\right|_{h_{t}}\leq\sup_{M}\left|S^{h_{t}}-P\right|_{h_{t}}+\sup_{M}|P|_{h_{t}}\leq C_{2}+C_{1}\sup_{M}|P|_{h_{0}}.

Hence, there is a constant
C_{3}=C_{3}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P,C_{1},C_{2})
such that

(2.43)

\left\|h_{t}\right\|_{C^{1}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq C_{3},\qquad t\in[0,T).

For higher-order estimates, we fix
\tau\in(0,T)
,

\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
p>\frac{2n+2}{1-\alpha}
. Fix

\rho=\frac{\sqrt{\tau}}{4}
with
4\rho^{2}=\tau/4<\tau
. Diminishing
\rho
if necessary, we choose
finitely many holomorphic coordinate charts
U_{\mu}
with local
holomorphic frames such that
B_{4\rho}(x_{\mu})\Subset U_{\mu}
and

M=\bigcup_{\mu=1}^{N}B_{\rho/4}(x_{\mu})
. For
t_{0}\in[\tau,T)
, we
set

(2.44)

Q^{\mu}_{r}(t_{0}):=B_{r}(x_{\mu})\times(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0}].

Then

Q^{\mu}_{2\rho}(t_{0})\Subset U_{\mu}\times(0,T)
. In the coordinate
chart
U_{\mu}
, the flow (
1.5
) takes the form

(2.45)

({\partial}_{t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}})h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=F_{\alpha\overline{\beta}},\qquad F_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=-g^{i\overline{j}}h^{\gamma\overline{\delta}}\frac{{\partial}h_{\alpha\overline{\delta}}}{{\partial}z^{i}}\frac{{\partial}h_{\gamma\overline{\beta}}}{{\partial}\overline{z}^{j}}+P_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}.

The operator
{\partial}_{t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}
is fixed and uniformly parabolic.
The uniform
C^{1}
-estimate (
2.43
) gives a uniform
constant
C_{4}
such that, for every
\alpha,\beta,\mu
and every

t_{0}\in[\tau,T)
,

(2.46)

\left\|F_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{\mu}_{2\rho}(t_{0}))}\leq C_{4}.

All constants introduced
henceforth, starting with
C_{4}
, depend (at most) on
\tau
,

\omega_{g}
,
h_{0}
,
p
,
P
,
C_{1}
, and
C_{2}
; additionally,

C_{m,\tau}
may depend on
m
. Crucially, none of these constants
depend on
t_{0}
or
T
, and this independence will not be stated
explicitly again. The interior parabolic
W^{2,p}_{t}
estimate (e.g.,

[
Lie96
, Theorem 7.22]
), applied on
Q^{\mu}_{\rho}(t_{0})\subset Q^{\mu}_{2\rho}(t_{0})
, yields

(2.47)

\left\|h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\right\|_{W^{2,p}_{t}(Q^{\mu}_{\rho}(t_{0}))}\leq C_{5}.

The parabolic Sobolev
embedding theorem (e.g.,
[
Lie96
, Chapter VI, Section 3]
) gives
a uniform
C^{1+\alpha,(1+\alpha)/2}
-bound. Moreover, the expression in
(
2.45
) shows that

(2.48)

\left\|F_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\right\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(Q^{\mu}_{\rho}(t_{0}))}\leq C_{6}.

The
interior parabolic Schauder estimate (e.g.,

[
Lie96
, Theorem 4.9]
) gives

(2.49)

\left\|h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\right\|_{C^{2+\alpha,(2+\alpha)/2}\left(Q^{\mu}_{3\rho/4}(t_{0})\right)}\leq C_{7}.

Repeating the argument on
nested cylinders between
Q^{\mu}_{\rho}(t_{0})
and
Q^{\mu}_{\rho/4}(t_{0})

yields

(2.50)

\left\|h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\right\|_{C^{m+\alpha,(m+\alpha)/2}\left(Q^{\mu}_{\rho/4}(t_{0})\right)}\leq C_{m,\tau}

for
every
m\geq 0
. Setting
t=t_{0}
and using the finite covering of

M
, together with the equivalence between (
2.26
) and (
2.27
), we obtain
(
2.32
).

∎

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Taking
\tau=T/2
in Theorem
2.5
, for every integer
m\geq 0
there exists a constant
C_{m}=C_{m}(C_{1},C_{2},P,\omega_{g},h_{0},T)>0
such that

(2.51)

\sup_{t\in[T/2,T)}\left\|h_{t}\right\|_{C^{m+2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq C_{m}.

In particular, there exists
\widetilde{C}_{m}>0
such that

(2.52)

\|S^{h_{t}}\|_{C^{m}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq\widetilde{C}_{m}.

By the evolution equation
\partial_{t}h_{t}=-S^{h_{t}}+P
, one has

(2.53)

\sup_{t\in[T/2,T)}\left\|\frac{{\partial}h_{t}}{{\partial}t}\right\|_{C^{m}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq C_{m}^{\prime}.

Moreover, for
s<t
and

,

(2.54)

\left\|h_{t}-h_{s}\right\|_{C^{m}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq\int_{s}^{t}\left\|\frac{{\partial}h_{\sigma}}{{\partial}\sigma}\right\|_{C^{m}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}d\sigma\leq C_{m}^{\prime}|t-s|.

Since

, this implies that
\{h_{t}\}_{t\in[T/2,T)}

-norm. In particular,
h_{t}\to h_{T}
in

for some smooth Hermitian tensor

. By (

),

is a Hermitian metric. Therefore, we can extend the flow with initial metric

.
∎

3.
A parabolic comparison principle and proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we establish a parabolic comparison principle that applies to the prescribed Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow (
1.5
), the classical Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow, and various other related flows. In particular, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a Hermitian manifold, let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
M
,
and let
{\mathscr{F}}:\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)\times\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)\to\mathrm{Herm}(E)
be a map.

(1)

We say that
{\mathscr{F}}
satisfies the
upper null-vector condition
if, for any Hermitian metrics
k
and
\ell
such that
k-\ell\geq 0
, one has

(3.1)

{\mathscr{F}}(k,\ell)(v,\overline{v})\geq 0

for all nonzero
v
satisfying
(k-\ell)(v,\overline{v})=0
.

(2)

We say that
{\mathscr{F}}
satisfies the
lower null-vector condition
if, for any Hermitian metrics
k
and
\ell
such that
k-\ell\leq 0
, then

(3.2)

{\mathscr{F}}(k,\ell)(v,\overline{v})\leq 0,

for any nonzero
v
satisfying
(k-\ell)(v,\overline{v})=0
.

(3)

Fix a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
. We say that
{\mathscr{F}}
is
locally Lipschitz continuous
in the first variable if for any bounded set
K\subset\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)\times\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
, there exists a constant
L_{K}=L_{K}(M,h_{0},K,{\mathscr{F}})
such that for all
(k_{1},\ell),(k_{2},\ell)\in K
,

(3.3)

\left|{\mathscr{F}}\left(k_{1},\ell\right)-{\mathscr{F}}\left(k_{2},\ell\right)\right|_{h_{0}}\leq L_{K}\left|k_{1}-k_{2}\right|_{h_{0}}\quad\text{on }M.

Similarly one can define the local Lipschitz continuity in the second variable.

Example 3.2
.

Suppose that
{\mathscr{F}}_{0}(h)=ah+bP+c^{2}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{h_{0}}h\right)h_{0}
where
a,b,c
are smooth functions on
M
. It is straightforward to verify that

(3.4)

{\mathscr{F}}(k,\ell)={\mathscr{F}}_{0}(k)-{\mathscr{F}}_{0}(\ell)=a(k-\ell)+c^{2}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{h_{0}}(k-\ell)\right)h_{0}

is locally Lipschitz continuous in both variables and satisfies the lower and upper null-vector conditions.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
. We begin with the
following lemma which will be essential throughout the proof. Recall
that a matrix-valued function is said to be quasi-positive on

M
, if it is non-negative definite everywhere, and it is
positive-definite at some point of
M
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold and
f
be a
Lipschitz continuous and quasi-positive function on
M
. Then there
exists a positive function
\eta\in C^{2}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
such that

(4.1)

\left(f-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)\eta=1.

Moreover, if
f
is smooth, then
\eta
is smooth.

Proof.

Fix
p>2\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M
. It is well-known that

\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}:W^{2,p}(M)\to L^{p}(M)
is a Fredholm operator of index
zero. Since
W^{2,p}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(M)
is a compact
embedding and
f\in L^{\infty}(M)
, the multiplication map

W^{2,p}(M)\to L^{p}(M)
,
u\mapsto fu
, is compact. In particular,
the operator

(4.2)

\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}-f:W^{2,p}(M)\to L^{p}(M)

is also
Fredholm of index zero. Moreover, if
v\in W^{2,p}(M)
satisfies

(\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}-f)v=0
in the weak sense, then
v\in C^{1,\alpha}(M)
for some
\alpha>0
. Since
f\in C^{0,1}(M)
, by
the Schauder theory for the elliptic equation
\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}v=fv
, one
concludes that
v\in C^{2}(M)
. By the strong maximum principle, we
conclude that
v
is constant. As
f>0
somewhere, this constant
must be zero. Hence the kernel of
\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}-f
is trivial, and the
index zero property implies that
\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}-f
is onto. Suppose that

\eta\in W^{2,p}(M)
solves

(4.3)

\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\eta-f\eta=-1.

We also have
\eta\in C^{2}(M)
. If
x_{0}
is a minimum point of
\eta
, then
\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\eta(x_{0})\geq 0
, and hence

f(x_{0})\eta(x_{0})=1+\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\eta(x_{0})\geq 1
.
This implies
\eta>0
on
M
, and (
4.1
) follows.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle
over a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. If there exists a
smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that

(4.4)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)

is quasi-positive,
then for any quasi-positive
P\in\Gamma\left(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}\right)
, and any Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
, there exist
two Hermitian metrics
\underline{h}
and
\overline{h}
on
E

such that

(4.5)

\underline{h}\leq h_{0}\leq\overline{h},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{\underline{h}}\right)\leq P\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{\overline{h}}\right).

If
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)
is quasi-positive, we show that there exists a conformal metric
h_{E}=e^{-\varphi}h
such that

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h_{E}}\right)>0.

Indeed, it is well-known that there exists some

such that
\widetilde{g}=e^{f}g
and
\omega_{\widetilde{g}}
is a Gauduchon metric, i.e.,
{\partial}{\overline{\partial}}\omega_{\widetilde{g}}^{n-1}=0
. We define

(4.6)

\kappa_{\widetilde{g},h}(x)=\inf_{0\neq v\in E_{x}}\frac{h\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{\widetilde{g}}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,\Theta^{h}\right)v,v\right)}{h(v,v)}.

Recall that
\Theta^{h}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes E)
is the lifting of
R^{h}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})

. By using the quasi-positivity and the conformal structure, there exists some
\lambda_{0}>0

(4.7)

\int_{M}\kappa_{\widetilde{g},h}(x)\omega_{\widetilde{g}}^{n}=\lambda_{0}\int_{M}\omega_{\widetilde{g}}^{n}.

Since
\kappa_{\widetilde{g},h}
is Lipschitz and
\omega_{\widetilde{g}}
is Gauduchon, by Hodge theory, there exists a function
\varphi\in C^{2,\alpha}(M,\mathbb{R})

\kappa_{\widetilde{g},h}+\Lambda_{\omega_{\widetilde{g}}}\left(\sqrt{-1}{\partial}{\overline{\partial}}\varphi\right)=\lambda_{0}.

We define a new metric

. By formula (
2.23

(4.9)

\Lambda_{\omega_{\widetilde{g}}}\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{E}}-\Lambda_{\omega_{\widetilde{g}}}\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h}=\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{\widetilde{g}}}\sqrt{-1}{\partial}{\overline{\partial}}\varphi\right)\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{E},

as tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
.
Moreover, one has

\displaystyle\kappa_{\widetilde{g},h_{E}}(x)=\inf_{0\neq v\in E_{x}}\frac{h_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{\widetilde{g}}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,\Theta^{h_{E}}\right)v,v\right)}{h_{E}(v,v)}=\kappa_{\widetilde{g},h}(x)+\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{\widetilde{g}}}\sqrt{-1}{\partial}{\overline{\partial}}\varphi\right)=\lambda_{0}>0.

In particular, the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor
\Lambda_{\omega_{\widetilde{g}}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,\Theta^{h_{E}}\right)
is positive definite with respect to
h^{E}
. Since
\widetilde{g}=e^{f}g
, we conclude that
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h_{E}}\right)>0

Since

is compact and

C_{1}=C_{1}(\omega_{g},h_{0},h_{E},P)

h_{0}\leq C_{1}h_{E},\qquad P\leq C_{1}S^{h_{E}}.

Set
\overline{h}=C_{1}h_{E}
. Then
S^{\overline{h}}=C_{1}S^{h_{E}}\geq P
. Since
P
is quasi-positive, there exist a non-empty open subset

and a constant
C_{3}=C_{3}(P,h_{E},U)>0

(4.11)

P\geq C_{3}h_{E}\quad\text{on }U.

Choose
C_{4}>0
, depending on
\omega_{g}
and
h_{E}

(4.12)

S^{h_{E}}-C_{4}h_{E}\leq 0\quad\text{on }M\setminus U.

Choose
\chi\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
with
\chi\geq 0
,
\chi\not\equiv 0
and
\supp(\chi)\subset U

, there exists a smooth function

(\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}-\chi)\psi=C_{4}.

Since

(4.14)

Consequently, there exists a constant
C_{5}=C_{5}(\omega_{g},h_{E},\psi)
such that the following holds on

(4.15)

S^{h_{E}}-(\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\psi)h_{E}\leq C_{5}h_{E}.

Furthermore, there exists
C_{6}=C_{6}(h_{E},\psi,C_{5},h_{0},P,U)
such that

(4.16)

C_{6}e^{\psi}h_{E}\leq h_{0}\quad\text{on }M,\qquad C_{6}e^{\psi}C_{5}h_{E}\leq P\quad\text{on }U.

\underline{h}=C_{6}e^{\psi}h_{E}.

The conformal transformation formula (

By construction, it is straightforward to verify that

on

, and

on

. Therefore,
S^{\underline{h}}\leq P
on

\left({\mathcal{E}},\widetilde{h}\right)=\left(E^{*}\otimes E,h^{*}\otimes k\right).

The identity map
{\rm id}_{E}
is a holomorphic section of
{\mathcal{E}}
, and

(4.22)

\mathrm{tr}_{h}k=\left|{\rm id}_{E}\right|_{\widetilde{h}}^{2}.

By the Bochner formula,

(4.23)

\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{tr}_{h}k=\left|\nabla^{{\mathcal{E}}}{\rm id}_{E}\right|_{g,\widetilde{h}}^{2}-\left\langle\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{{\mathcal{E}}}({\rm id}_{E}),{\rm id}_{E}\right\rangle_{\widetilde{h}}.

In a local holomorphic frame
\{e_{\alpha}\}
of
E
, using the fact that

\Theta^{\mathcal{E}}=\Theta^{E^{*},h^{*}}\otimes{\rm id}_{E}+{\rm id}_{E^{*}}\otimes\Theta^{E,k},

we obtain

(4.24)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{{\mathcal{E}}}({\rm id}_{E}),{\rm id}_{E}\right\rangle_{\widetilde{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}\left(-R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\gamma}}h^{\beta\overline{\gamma}}+R^{k}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\gamma}}k^{\beta\overline{\gamma}}\right)h^{\alpha\overline{\delta}}k_{\beta\overline{\delta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle S^{h},k\right\rangle_{h}+\mathrm{tr}_{h}S^{k}.

Therefore

(4.25)

\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{tr}_{h}k=\left|\nabla^{{\mathcal{E}}}{\rm id}_{E}\right|_{g,\widetilde{h}}^{2}+\left\langle S^{h},k\right\rangle_{h}-\mathrm{tr}_{h}S^{k}\geq\left\langle S^{h},k\right\rangle_{h}-\mathrm{tr}_{h}S^{k}.

Similarly,

(4.26)

\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{tr}_{k}h\geq\left\langle S^{k},h\right\rangle_{k}-\mathrm{tr}_{k}S^{h}.

Since
S^{h}=S^{k}=P
, we obtain

(4.27)

\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\sigma\geq\left\langle P,k\right\rangle_{h}-\mathrm{tr}_{h}P+\left\langle P,h\right\rangle_{k}-\mathrm{tr}_{k}P.

We claim that
\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\sigma\geq 0
on
M
. Indeed, fix an arbitrary point

x\in M
. Choose a basis
\{e_{\alpha}\}
of
E_{x}
such that at point
x
,

(4.28)

h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=\delta_{\alpha\beta},\qquad k_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=a_{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha\beta},\qquad a_{\alpha}>0.

Write
p_{\alpha}=P_{\alpha\overline{\alpha}}(x)
. Since
P\geq 0
,
p_{\alpha}\geq 0
. At point
x
, a straightforward
computation gives

(4.29)

\left\langle P,k\right\rangle_{h}-\mathrm{tr}_{h}P=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(a_{\alpha}-1)p_{\alpha},\qquad\left\langle P,h\right\rangle_{k}-\mathrm{tr}_{k}P=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(a_{\alpha}^{-2}-a_{\alpha}^{-1})p_{\alpha}.

Hence, at point
x
,

(4.30)

\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\sigma\geq\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\left((a_{\alpha}-1)+(a_{\alpha}^{-2}-a_{\alpha}^{-1})\right)p_{\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\frac{(a_{\alpha}-1)^{2}(a_{\alpha}+1)}{a_{\alpha}^{2}}p_{\alpha}\geq 0.

Since
x
is arbitrary,
\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\sigma\geq 0
on
M
. By the maximum principle,

\sigma
is constant and
\Delta_{{\mathbb{C}}}\sigma=0
. Let
x\in M
be a point such that
P(x)
is positive definite. By (
4.30
),
a_{\alpha}=1
for all
\alpha
since
P_{\alpha}>0
. This implies
h=k
at
x
and so
\sigma\equiv 2r
. This also implies
h\equiv k
on
M
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Lemma
4.2
, there exist two Hermitian metrics
\underline{h}
and
\overline{h}
on
E
such that

(4.31)

\underline{h}\leq h_{0}\leq\overline{h},\qquad\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{\underline{h}})\leq P\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{\overline{h}}).

By Theorem
3.7
, the solution exists for all
t\geq 0
and satisfies
\underline{h}\leq h_{t}\leq\overline{h}
. Therefore, there exists a constant
C_{8}=C_{8}(h_{0},\underline{h},\overline{h})\geq 1
such that for all
t\geq 0
,

(4.32)

C_{8}^{-1}h_{0}\leq h_{t}\leq C_{8}h_{0}.

We proceed to prove the convergence of the flow. Define

(4.33)

u(x,t):=|S^{h_{t}}-P|_{h_{t}}^{2},\qquad\lambda_{0}(x):=\inf_{v\in E_{x},\,v\neq 0}\frac{P(v,\overline{v})}{h_{0}(v,\overline{v})}.

Then
\lambda_{0}\in C^{0,1}(M)
and
\lambda_{0}
is quasi-positive. From
h_{t}\leq C_{8}h_{0}
, we deduce

(4.34)

\lambda^{P}_{\min}(x,t):=\inf_{v\in E_{x},\,v\neq 0}\frac{P(v,\overline{v})}{h_{t}(v,\overline{v})}\geq C_{8}^{-1}\lambda_{0}(x).

By Corollary
2.2
,

(4.35)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)u+2C_{8}^{-1}\lambda_{0}u\leq 0.

Set
f=2C_{8}^{-1}\lambda_{0}
. Since
f\in C^{0,1}(M)
is quasi-positive, Lemma
4.1
provides a positive function
\eta\in C^{2}(M)
satisfying

(4.36)

(f-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}})\eta=1.

Define

(4.37)

C_{9}:=\left(\sup_{M}\eta\right)^{-1},\qquad C_{10}:=\sup_{M}\frac{u(\cdot,0)}{\eta}.

Consider the auxiliary function

(4.38)

w=u-C_{10}e^{-C_{9}t}\eta.

Clearly
w(\cdot,0)\leq 0
. Moreover, by (
4.35
),

(4.39)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)w+fw\leq C_{10}e^{-C_{9}t}(C_{9}\eta-1)\leq 0.

The parabolic maximum principle implies
w\leq 0
. Consequently, there exists a constant
C_{11}
, depending on
C_{10}
and
\eta
, such that for all
t\geq 0
,

(4.40)

\sup_{M}|S^{h_{t}}-P|_{h_{t}}\leq C_{11}e^{-\frac{C_{9}}{2}t}.

Since
\partial_{t}h_{t}=-(S^{h_{t}}-P)
, estimates (
4.32
) and (
4.40
) imply

(4.41)

\left\|\frac{\partial h_{t}}{\partial t}\right\|_{C^{0}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq C_{8}C_{11}e^{-\frac{C_{9}}{2}t}.

Thus, for

(4.42)

\|h_{t}-h_{s}\|_{C^{0}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq\int_{s}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial h_{\sigma}}{\partial\sigma}\right\|_{C^{0}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}d\sigma\leq 2C_{8}C_{11}C_{9}^{-1}e^{-\frac{C_{9}}{2}s}.

Hence
h_{t}
converges uniformly to a continuous Hermitian tensor
h_{\infty}
. Taking the limit in (
4.32
), we conclude that
h_{\infty}
is a continuous Hermitian metric on
E
.
Finally, utilizing (
4.32
) and (
4.40
), Theorem
2.5
implies that for every integer
m\geq 0
, there exists a constant
\widetilde{C}_{m}=\widetilde{C}_{m}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P,C_{8},C_{11})
such that for all
t\geq 1

(4.43)

\|h_{t}\|_{C^{m}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq\widetilde{C}_{m}.

The Arzelà-Ascoli theorem implies that every sequence
t_{i}\to\infty
admits a subsequence converging in

to a smooth Hermitian metric. Since we have already established
C^{0}
convergence, the limit must be
h_{\infty}
. Thus, the entire flow converges to
h_{\infty}
in
C^{\infty}
. Passing to the limit in (

) yields

(4.44)

S^{h_{\infty}}=P,

which is equivalent to
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\infty}}\right)=P
.
Moreover, for all integers
m\geq 1
, the standard Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality implies that

(4.45)

\left\|h_{t}-h_{\infty}\right\|_{C^{m}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq C_{12}\left\|h_{t}-h_{\infty}\right\|_{C^{0}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|h_{t}-h_{\infty}\right\|_{C^{2m}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C_{12}
depends on
M,\omega_{g},h_{0}
and
m
. Moreover, by (
4.42
) and (
4.43
),

(4.46)

\left\|h_{t}-h_{\infty}\right\|_{C^{m}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq C_{13}e^{-\frac{C_{9}}{4}t},

where
C_{13}
depends on
M,\omega_{g},h_{0},P
and
m
. Hence,
\{h_{t}\}
converges smoothly to
h_{\infty}
at an exponential rate.
The uniqueness of
h_{\infty}
follows from Proposition
4.3
.
This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem
1.3
. A uniform
C^{0}
-estimate is obtained in Corollary
3.6
as a special case of Theorem
1.1
, while long-time existence of the flow is provided by Theorem
3.7
. We shall discuss the long-time convergence in detail. It is ensured by a modified Donaldson functional.
Fix two Hermitian metrics
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
on
E
.
Let

h(s):[0,1]\rightarrow\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)

be a smooth family of Hermitian metrics on
E
with
h(0)=h_{0}
and
h(1)=h_{1}
. In analogy with the Donaldson functional introduced in
[
Don85
]
,
we define a functional associated with a given Hermitian tensor

P\in\mathrm{Herm}(E)
and two Hermitian metrics
h_{0}
,
h_{1}
:

(5.1)

{\mathcal{M}}_{P}(h_{0},h_{1}):=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(S^{h}-P\right)h^{-1}\cdot\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}s}h^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}ds.

Motivated by the approach of
[
Don85
]
, we establish the following result.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact balanced manifold, i.e.,
d\omega^{n-1}_{g}=0
. The quantity
{\mathcal{M}}_{P}(h_{0},h_{1})
is independent of the choice of the path
\{h(s)\}_{0\leq s\leq 1}
connecting
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
.

(1)

For every smooth path
\{h_{s}\}_{0\leq s\leq 1}
starting at
h_{0}
, one has

(5.2)

\frac{d}{ds}{\mathcal{M}}_{P}(h_{0},h_{s})=\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(S^{h_{s}}-P\right)h_{s}^{-1}\cdot\frac{{\partial}h_{s}}{{\partial}s}h_{s}^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

(2)

Along the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow (
1.5
), one has

(5.3)

\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{M}}_{P}(h_{0},h_{t})=-\int_{M}\left|S^{h_{t}}-P\right|_{h_{t}}^{2}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Proof.

Let

h(s,t):[0,1]\times[a,b]\rightarrow\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)

be a two parameter smooth family of Hermitian metrics on
E
with
h(0,t)\equiv h_{0}
and
h(1,t)\equiv h_{1}
. That means, for any fixed
t_{0}\in[a,b]
,
h(s,t_{0}):[0,1]\rightarrow\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
is a smooth family of Hermitian metrics connecting
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
. We set

(5.4)

\Phi_{s}=\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}s}h^{-1},\qquad\Phi_{t}=\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}t}h^{-1}.

We claim that

(5.5)

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}(K^{h}\Phi_{s})\omega_{g}^{n}=\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}s}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}(K^{h}\Phi_{t})\omega_{g}^{n}.

Indeed, one has

(5.6)

\frac{\partial\Phi_{s}}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial\Phi_{t}}{\partial s}=\Phi_{t}\Phi_{s}-\Phi_{s}\Phi_{t}=[\Phi_{t},\Phi_{s}].

The standard variation formula for the Chern curvature gives

(5.7)

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}K^{h}=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}\Theta^{h}\right)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\,{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}{\partial}^{h}\Phi_{t},

where
{\partial}^{h}
and
{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}
denote the
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
parts of the Chern connection on
\mathrm{End}(E)
induced by
(E,h)
. Indeed, let
\Gamma_{i\alpha}^{\beta}
be the Christoffel symbols of the Chern connection of
(E,h)
. By subtracting the Christoffel symbols of a time-independent reference metric
h_{0}
, it is clear that

\Gamma^{\prime}:=\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}\left(\Gamma_{i\alpha}^{\beta}dz^{i}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\right)

is globally defined and

(5.8)

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}K^{h}=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\,{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}\Gamma^{\prime}

On the other hand, it is obvious that

(5.9)

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}\left(\Gamma_{i\alpha}^{\beta}dz^{i}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\right)={\partial}^{h}\Phi_{t}.

Hence, (
5.7
) follows. By Bochner-Kodaira formula, for every
\varphi\in\Gamma\left(M,\mathrm{End}(E)\right)

(5.10)

{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}{\partial}^{h}\varphi+{\partial}^{h}{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}\varphi=\varphi\cdot\Theta^{h}-\Theta^{h}\cdot\varphi.

Hence,

(5.11)

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}s}K^{h}=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\,{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}{\partial}^{h}\Phi_{s}=-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\,{\partial}^{h}{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}\Phi_{s}+[\Phi_{s},K^{h}].

Using (
5.6
), (
5.7
), and (
5.11
), we first compute

(5.12)

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h}\Phi_{s}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}-\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}s}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h}\Phi_{t}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\,{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}{\partial}^{h}\Phi_{t}\right)\Phi_{s}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}+\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\,{\partial}^{h}{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}\Phi_{s}\right)\Phi_{t}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left([\Phi_{s},K^{h}]\Phi_{t}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}+\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h}[\Phi_{t},\Phi_{s}]\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

Since
\omega_{g}
is balanced (
d\omega^{n-1}_{g}=0
), one has

(5.13)

\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\,{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}{\partial}^{h}\Phi_{t}\right)\Phi_{s}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}=n\sqrt{-1}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left({\partial}^{h}\Phi_{t}\wedge{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}\Phi_{s}\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1},

and

(5.14)

\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\,{\partial}^{h}{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}\Phi_{s}\right)\Phi_{t}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}=n\sqrt{-1}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left({\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}\Phi_{s}\wedge{\partial}^{h}\Phi_{t}\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1}.

Therefore, the sum of the first two terms in (
5.12
) is

(5.15)

n\sqrt{-1}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left({\partial}^{h}\Phi_{t}\wedge{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}\Phi_{s}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}\Phi_{s}\wedge{\partial}^{h}\Phi_{t}\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1}=0.

On the other hand,

(5.16)

-\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left([\Phi_{s},K^{h}]\Phi_{t}\right)+\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h}[\Phi_{t},\Phi_{s}]\right)=-\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Phi_{s}K^{h}\Phi_{t}\right)+\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h}\Phi_{t}\Phi_{s}\right)=0.

Combining (
5.12
), (
5.15
),
and (
5.16
), we prove (
5.5
).
By using (
5.5
), we obtain

(5.17)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h}\Phi_{s}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h}\Phi_{s}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}s}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h}\Phi_{t}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left.\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h}\Phi_{t}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}\right)\right|_{s=0}^{s=1}=0,

where the last step holds since
\Phi_{t}\equiv 0
if
s=0
or
s=1
.
On the other hand,

(5.18)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(-Ph^{-1}\cdot\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}s}h^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}s}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(Ph^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(Ph_{1}^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}-\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(Ph_{0}^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

Therefore
{\mathcal{M}}_{P}(h_{0},h_{1})
is independent of the choice of smooth path connecting
h_{0}
to
h_{1}
.
Since (
5.1
) is invariant under scaling the path,

(5.19)

{\mathcal{M}}_{P}(h_{0},h_{s}):=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(S^{h_{\tau}}-P\right)h_{\tau}^{-1}\frac{{\partial}h_{\tau}}{{\partial}\tau}h_{\tau}^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}d\tau.

Differentiating with respect to
s
yields (
5.2
).
In particular, along the flow (
1.5
), we have

(5.20)

\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{M}}_{P}(h_{0},h_{t})=\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(S^{h_{t}}-P\right)h_{t}^{-1}\frac{{\partial}h_{t}}{{\partial}t}h_{t}^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}=-\int_{M}\left|S^{h_{t}}-P\right|_{h_{t}}^{2}\omega_{g}^{n}.

This proves identity (
5.3
).∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold with
d^{*}\omega_{g}=0
. If there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that
C^{-1}h_{0}\leq h_{1}\leq Ch_{0}
, then there exists a constant
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P,C)
such that

(5.21)

{\mathcal{M}}_{P}(h_{0},h_{1})\geq-C^{\prime}.

Proof.

We consider the linear path

(5.22)

h_{s}=sh_{1}+(1-s)h_{0},\qquad 0\leq s\leq 1,

connecting
h_{0}
to
h_{1}
. We also use notations

(5.23)

H:=h_{1}\cdot h_{0}^{-1},\qquad H_{s}:=sH+(1-s){\rm id}_{E},\qquad\Psi_{s}:=\left(H-{\rm id}_{E}\right)H_{s}^{-1}.

Then for all
s\in[0,1]
,

(5.24)

h_{s}=H_{s}\cdot h_{0},\qquad\frac{\partial h_{s}}{\partial s}h_{s}^{-1}=\Psi_{s}.

Then the transformation formula (
2.23
) gives

(5.25)

K^{h_{s}}=\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{s}}\right)=K^{h_{0}}+\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\,{\overline{\partial}}\left(s({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)H_{s}^{-1}\right).

If we set

(5.26)

B_{s}=s({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)H_{s}^{-1},

then

(5.27)

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h_{s}}\Psi_{s}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}ds=\int_{M}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h_{0}}\Psi_{s}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}ds+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\,{\overline{\partial}}B_{s}\right)\Psi_{s}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}ds.

For each
\lambda>0
, one has

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\lambda-1}{(1-s)+s\lambda}\,ds=\log\lambda
. In particular,

(5.28)

\int_{0}^{1}\Psi_{s}\,ds=\log H.

This implies

(5.29)

\int_{M}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h_{0}}\Psi_{s}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}ds=\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h_{0}}\cdot\log H\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

Moreover, integrating by parts yields

(5.30)

\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\,{\overline{\partial}}B_{s}\right)\Psi_{s}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}=\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\left(B_{s}\wedge{\overline{\partial}}\Psi_{s}\right)\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

We show the integrand of the right-hand side is nonnegative. Indeed, fix a point
x
of
M
. Choose holomorphic coordinates
\{z^{i}\}
around
x
such that
g_{i\overline{j}}(x)=\delta_{ij}
, and choose an
h_{0}
-unitary basis
\{e^{\alpha}\}
of
E_{x}
diagonalizing
H
. With this basis, write

(5.31)

H=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}),\qquad H_{s}=\mathrm{diag}(\nu_{1}(s),\ldots,\nu_{r}(s)),\qquad\nu_{\alpha}(s)=s\lambda_{\alpha}+(1-s).

Set
\theta=({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)(x)
. Since
B_{s}=s({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)H_{s}^{-1}
, at point
x
we have

(5.32)

(B_{s})_{i\alpha}^{\beta}=\frac{s}{\nu_{\beta}(s)}\theta_{i\alpha}^{\beta}.

Since
H
is self-adjoint with respect to
h_{0}
, the adjoint of
{\partial}^{h_{0}}H
with respect to
h_{0}
is
{\overline{\partial}}H
.
Therefore, at point
x
we have

(5.33)

{\overline{\partial}}_{i}H_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\overline{\theta_{i\beta}^{\alpha}}.

A straightforward calculation shows that

(5.34)

{\overline{\partial}}\Psi_{s}=\left({\overline{\partial}}H\right)H_{s}^{-1}-s\left(H-{\rm id}_{E}\right)H_{s}^{-1}\left({\overline{\partial}}H\right)H_{s}^{-1}.

Therefore, at point
x
,

(5.35)

{\partial}_{\overline{i}}(\Psi_{s})_{\beta}^{\alpha}=\left(\frac{1}{\nu_{\alpha}(s)}-s\frac{\lambda_{\beta}-1}{\nu_{\alpha}(s)\nu_{\beta}(s)}\right){\partial}_{\overline{i}}H_{\beta}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\nu_{\alpha}(s)\nu_{\beta}(s)}\overline{\theta_{i\alpha}^{\beta}}.

Consequently,

(5.36)

\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\left(B_{s}\wedge{\overline{\partial}}\Psi_{s}\right)\right)=\sum_{i,\alpha,\beta}\frac{s}{\nu_{\alpha}(s)\nu_{\beta}(s)^{2}}|\theta_{i\alpha}^{\beta}|^{2}\geq 0.

It follows from (
5.27
), (
5.29
), (
5.30
) and (
5.36
) that

(5.37)

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h_{s}}\Psi_{s}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}ds\geq\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h_{0}}\cdot\log H\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

Since
C^{-1}h_{0}\leq h_{1}\leq Ch_{0}
, there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P,C)
such that

(5.38)

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h_{s}}\Psi_{s}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}ds\geq\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(K^{h_{0}}\cdot\log H\right)\omega_{g}^{n}\geq-C_{1}.

On the other hand, there exists

C_{2}=C_{2}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P,C)
satisfying

(5.39)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(-Ph_{s}^{-1}\frac{{\partial}h_{s}}{{\partial}s}h_{s}^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}ds=\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(Ph_{1}^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}-\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(Ph_{0}^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}\geq-C_{2}.

Hence, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Theorem
3.7
, the flow (
1.5
) exists for all time and satisfies

(5.40)

\underline{h}\leq h_{t}\leq\overline{h}

for all
t\in[0,\infty)
.
Let

(5.41)

u(x,t)=|S^{h_{t}}-P|_{h_{t}}^{2},\qquad F(t)=\int_{M}u(x,t)\omega_{g}^{n}.

By Lemma
5.1
and Theorem
5.2
, there exists a uniform
C_{0}=C_{0}(P,\underline{h},\overline{h},h_{0},\omega_{g})>0
such that

(5.42)

\int_{0}^{T}F(t)dt={\mathcal{M}}_{P}(h_{0},h_{0})-{\mathcal{M}}_{P}(h_{0},h_{T})\leq C_{0}

for every
T>0
. Since
F\geq 0
,

(5.43)

F\in L^{1}([0,\infty)).

Moreover, by (
5.40
), there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(P,\overline{h},\underline{h})>0
such that

(5.44)

\lambda^{P}_{\min}(x,t)=\inf_{v\in E_{x},v\neq 0}\frac{P(v,\overline{v})}{h(t)(v,\overline{v})}\geq-C_{1}.

By Corollary
2.2
,

(5.45)

\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)u\leq 2C_{1}u.

Integrating over
M
yields

(5.46)

F^{\prime}(t)\leq 2C_{1}F(t).

Since
F\in L^{1}([0,\infty))
, one can easily deduce that

(5.47)

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}F(t)=0.

Fix
t\geq 1
and consider the time interval
[t-1,t]
. By (
5.45
),

(5.48)

\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\tau}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)\left(e^{-2C_{1}(\tau-t+1)}u(x,\tau)\right)\leq 0,\qquad t-1\leq\tau\leq t.

Let
H(x,y,t)
denote the heat kernel of
{\partial}_{t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}
on
(M,\omega_{g})
with respect to the reference measure
\omega_{g}^{n}(y)
.
Then

(5.49)

v(x,\tau):=\int_{M}H(x,y,\tau-t+1)u(y,t-1)\omega_{g}^{n}(y),

solves the heat equation

(5.50)

\left({\partial}_{\tau}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)v(x,\tau)=0,\quad v(x,t-1)=u(x,t-1).

If we set

(5.51)

w(x,\tau)=e^{-2C_{1}(\tau-t+1)}u(x,\tau),

then (
5.48
) gives

(5.52)

\left({\partial}_{\tau}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)w(x,\tau)\leq 0,\quad w(x,t-1)=u(x,t-1).

The parabolic comparison principle gives

(5.53)

w(x,\tau)\leq v(x,\tau)

on
M\times[t-1,t]
. In particular, at
\tau=t
, one has

(5.54)

u(x,t)\leq e^{2C_{1}}\int_{M}H(x,y,1)u(y,t-1)\omega_{g}^{n}(y).

The heat kernel
H(x,y,1)
is bounded on the compact manifold, and hence

(5.55)

\sup_{M}u(\bullet,t)\leq C_{2}F(t-1),

where
C_{2}=C_{2}(C_{1},M,\omega_{g})>0
.
By (
5.47
), one deduces that

(5.56)

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{M}|S^{h_{t}}-P|^{2}_{h_{t}}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{M}u(\bullet,t)=0.

By Theorem
2.5
and the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, for every sequence
\{t_{i}\}
with
t_{i}\to\infty
, there exists a subsequence (still denoted by
\{t_{i}\}
) such that
\{h_{t_{i}}\}\to h_{\infty}
in
C^{\infty}
, where
h_{\infty}
is a smooth Hermitian metric on
E
. Passing to the limit in (
5.56
) yields
S^{h_{\infty}}=P
.
∎

6.
Further generalizations and examples

In this section, we generalize Theorem
1.3
and present several examples related to the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow.
In the following result, we show that if the condition
S^{\underline{h}}\leq P\leq S^{\overline{h}}
in Theorem
1.3
is replaced by the strict inequality
S^{\underline{h}}<P\leq S^{\overline{h}}
, then the flow (
1.5
) converges globally.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler (or balanced) manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
, and let
P\in\mathrm{Herm}(E)
. Suppose that there exist two Hermitian metrics
\underline{h}
and
\overline{h}
on
E
such that

(6.1)

\underline{h}\leq\overline{h},\qquad S^{\underline{h}}<P\leq S^{\overline{h}}.

Then for any initial metric
h_{0}
satisfying

(6.2)

\underline{h}\leq h_{0}\leq\overline{h},

the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow (
1.5
) converges in
C^{\infty}
to a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{\infty}
satisfying

(6.3)

S^{h_{\infty}}=P.

One of the key ingredients is the following convexity property of the Hermitian-Yang-Mills curvature tensors.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
.
If
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
are two Hermitian metrics on
E
, then the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors satisfy

(6.4)

S^{h_{0}+h_{1}}\leq S^{h_{0}}+S^{h_{1}},

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
.

Proof.

Consider the Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle

(6.5)

\bigl(\mathcal{E},\widetilde{h}\bigr):=\bigl(E\oplus E,\,h_{0}\oplus h_{1}\bigr).

Define the holomorphic bundle map

(6.6)

\iota\colon E\longrightarrow\mathcal{E},\qquad\iota(v)=(v,v).

Its image
F:=\iota(E)\subset\mathcal{E}
is a holomorphic subbundle.
The metric induced by
\widetilde{h}
on
F
coincides via
\iota
with

h_{0}+h_{1}
. Indeed, for every
x\in M
and
u,v\in E_{x}
,

(6.7)

\widetilde{h}\bigl(\iota(u),\iota(v)\bigr)=h_{0}(u,v)+h_{1}(u,v)=(h_{0}+h_{1})(u,v).

Consequently, for
\sigma=\iota(v)
we have

(6.8)

S^{F}(\sigma,\overline{\sigma})=S^{\,h_{0}+h_{1}}(v,\overline{v}),

where
S^{F}
denotes the Hermitian–Yang–Mills tensor of

(F,\widetilde{h}|_{F})
.
Let
B
denote the second fundamental form of
F\subset\mathcal{E}
.
By the Chern curvature formula for holomorphic subbundles,

(6.9)

S^{F}(\sigma,\overline{\sigma})=S^{\widetilde{h}}(\sigma,\overline{\sigma})-|B\sigma|^{2}_{g,\widetilde{h}}\leq S^{\widetilde{h}}(\sigma,\overline{\sigma}).

With respect to a product holomorphic frame of
E\oplus E
,
the curvature of
\widetilde{h}=h_{0}\oplus h_{1}
is block diagonal.
Hence, for
\sigma=\iota(v)
,

(6.10)

S^{\widetilde{h}}(\sigma,\overline{\sigma})=S^{h_{0}}(v,\overline{v})+S^{h_{1}}(v,\overline{v}).

Combining (
6.8
),
(
6.9
), and (
6.10
) yields

(6.11)

S^{\,h_{0}+h_{1}}(v,\overline{v})\;\leq\;S^{h_{0}}(v,\overline{v})+S^{h_{1}}(v,\overline{v})

for every
x\in M
and every
v\in E_{x}
. This completes the proof.∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Suppose that
h_{t_{j}}\rightarrow h_{\infty}
is a subsequential
C^{\infty}
-limit
guaranteed by Theorem
1.3
.
From the previous arguments, we already know that

(6.12)

S^{h_{\infty}}=P,\qquad\underline{h}\leq h_{\infty}\leq\overline{h}.

We now prove that the entire flow
\{h_{t}\}
converges to
h_{\infty}
. By compactness, there exists a constant

C_{1}=C_{1}(P,\omega_{g},\underline{h})>0
such that

(6.13)

P-S^{\underline{h}}\geq C_{1}\underline{h}.

Similarly, there exists a constant

C_{2}=C_{2}(\omega_{g},\underline{h})>0
such that

(6.14)

S^{\underline{h}}\geq-C_{2}\underline{h}.

We set

(6.15)

\rho=\min\{C_{1}/C_{2},1/2\},

and define a Hermitian metric

(6.16)

\gamma:=(1-\rho)\,\underline{h}.

Then, by (
6.12
),

(6.17)

h_{\infty}-\gamma=(h_{\infty}-\underline{h})+\rho\underline{h}>0.

Moreover, using (
6.13
),
(
6.14
) and (
6.15
),
we obtain

(6.18)

P-S^{\gamma}=P-S^{\underline{h}}+\rho S^{\underline{h}}\geq C_{1}\underline{h}-\rho C_{2}\underline{h}\geq 0.

Thus

(6.19)

\gamma<h_{\infty},\qquad S^{\gamma}\leq P.

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, define

(6.20)

\displaystyle l_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=h_{\infty}-\varepsilon\,(h_{\infty}-\gamma),

(6.21)

\displaystyle k_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=h_{\infty}+\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\,(h_{\infty}-\gamma).

Since
\gamma<h_{\infty}
, it follows immediately that

(6.22)

l_{\varepsilon}<h_{\infty}<k_{\varepsilon}.

By Lemma
6.2
, (
6.12
) and
(
6.19
),

(6.23)

S^{l_{\varepsilon}}\leq(1-\varepsilon)S^{h_{\infty}}+\varepsilon S^{\gamma}\leq P.

On the other hand, from the definition of
k_{\varepsilon}
,

h_{\infty}=(1-\varepsilon)\,k_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\,\gamma.

Applying Lemma
6.2
again yields

(6.24)

P=S^{h_{\infty}}\leq(1-\varepsilon)S^{k_{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon S^{\gamma}\leq(1-\varepsilon)S^{k_{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon P,

and hence

(6.25)

P\;\leq\;S^{k_{\varepsilon}}.

Combining (
6.23
) and
(
6.25
), we obtain

(6.26)

S^{l_{\varepsilon}}\leq P\leq S^{k_{\varepsilon}}.

Since
h_{t_{j}}\to h_{\infty}
in
C^{\infty}
and the inequalities in
(
6.22
) are strict, for all sufficiently large
j
,

(6.27)

l_{\varepsilon}\leq h_{t_{j}}\leq k_{\varepsilon}.

Applying Theorem
3.7
for the flow (
1.5
) with initial time
t_{j}
,
together with (
6.26
), gives

(6.28)

l_{\varepsilon}\leq h_{t}\leq k_{\varepsilon},\qquad t\geq t_{j}.

Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
and using

(6.29)

l_{\varepsilon}\to h_{\infty},\qquad k_{\varepsilon}\to h_{\infty}\quad\text{in }C^{0},

we conclude that

(6.30)

h_{t}\to h_{\infty}\quad\text{in }C^{0}.

Finally, by the uniform higher-order estimates furnished by Theorem
2.5
,

h_{t}\to h_{\infty}\quad\text{in }C^{\infty}.

Thus the entire flow (
1.5
) converges to
h_{\infty}
.
∎

For a holomorphic vector bundle
E
, let

\mathrm{Herm}^{\geq 0}(E)
denote the set of nonnegative Hermitian tensors in
\mathrm{Herm}(E)
. Theorem
1.3
has the following refined version:

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler (or balanced) manifold

(M,\omega_{g})
. For given
P\in\mathrm{Herm}^{\geq 0}(E)
, if there exist two Hermitian metrics
\underline{h}
and
\overline{h}
on
E
such that

(6.31)

\underline{h}\leq\overline{h},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{\underline{h}}\right)\leq P\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{\overline{h}}\right),

then for any initial metric
h_{0}
satisfying
\underline{h}\leq h_{0}\leq\overline{h}
, the
prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow (
1.5
)
exists for all
t\in[0,\infty)
and converges smoothly to a Hermitian metric
h_{\infty}
on
E
satisfying

(6.32)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\infty}}\right)=P.

Proof.

Let
h_{\infty}
be a subsequential limit obtained above.
For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
t_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(6.33)

(1-\varepsilon)\,h_{\infty}\leq h_{t_{\varepsilon}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\,h_{\infty}.

Since
S^{h_{\infty}}=P
and
P\geq 0
, constant rescalings satisfy

(6.34)

S^{(1-\varepsilon)h_{\infty}}=(1-\varepsilon)\,P,\qquad S^{(1+\varepsilon)h_{\infty}}=(1+\varepsilon)P.

Applying the parabolic comparison principle
(Theorem
3.4
) from time
t_{\varepsilon}
,
with the stationary barriers in (
6.34
), yields

(6.35)

(1-\varepsilon)\,h_{\infty}\leq h_{t}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\,h_{\infty},\qquad t\geq t_{\varepsilon}.

Because the solution to the flow is uniquely determined by its initial data,
the evolution of
h_{t}
is independent of the choice of
t_{\varepsilon}
.
Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
in (
6.35
),
we obtain

(6.36)

h_{t}\rightarrow h_{\infty}\quad\text{in }C^{0}.

Together with higher-order estimates established in Theorem
2.5
,
this
C^{0}
-convergence upgrades to

(6.37)

h_{t}\rightarrow h_{\infty}\quad\text{in }C^{\infty}.

Thus the entire flow (
1.5
) converges smoothly to
h_{\infty}
.
∎

Example 6.4
.

In this example, we show that Theorem
1.3
can be applied to establish
existence of solutions to the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills equation
for parameters
P\notin\operatorname{Herm}^{\geq 0}(E)
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler (or balanced) manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that
E
admits a Hermitian metric
h_{+}
such that

(6.38)

S^{h_{+}}>0.

Fix a point
x_{0}\in M
, and choose a smooth real-valued function
\psi
satisfying

(6.39)

\psi(x_{0})=0,\qquad\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\psi(x_{0})>0.

Choose constants
0<\varepsilon<1
and
A>0
such that

(6.40)

S^{h_{+}}(x_{0})<\varepsilon A\,\bigl(\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\psi(x_{0})\bigr)\,h_{+}(x_{0}).

Define

(6.41)

h_{1}=e^{A\psi}h_{+},\qquad P=(1-\varepsilon)S^{h_{+}}+\varepsilon S^{h_{1}}.

Then there exists a Hermitian metric
h
with
S^{h}=P
, although

(6.42)

P\notin\operatorname{Herm}^{\geq 0}(E).

Indeed, by (
4.9
), one has

(6.43)

S^{h_{1}}=e^{A\psi}\bigl(S^{h_{+}}-A(\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\psi)h_{+}\bigr).

Evaluating at
x_{0}
gives

(6.44)

P(x_{0})=(1-\varepsilon)S^{h_{+}}(x_{0})+\varepsilon S^{h_{1}}(x_{0})=S^{h_{+}}(x_{0})-\varepsilon A\bigl(\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\psi(x_{0})\bigr)h_{+}(x_{0})<0,

which shows that
P\notin\operatorname{Herm}^{\geq 0}(E)
. Define the lower barrier by

(6.45)

\underline{h}=(1-\varepsilon)h_{+}+\varepsilon h_{1}.

By Lemma
6.2
,

(6.46)

S^{\underline{h}}\leq(1-\varepsilon)S^{h_{+}}+\varepsilon S^{h_{1}}=P.

For the upper barrier, choose a constant
C=C(\underline{h},\omega_{g},h_{+},P)>0
sufficiently large so that

(6.47)

\underline{h}\leq Ch_{+},\qquad P\leq CS^{h_{+}}.

Setting
\overline{h}=Ch_{+}
, we obtain

(6.48)

\underline{h}\leq\overline{h},\qquad S^{\underline{h}}\leq P\leq S^{\overline{h}}.

Finally, Theorem
1.3
implies the existence of a Hermitian metric
h
on
E
satisfying
S^{h}=P
.

Example 6.5
.

Let
(E,h_{0})
be a Hermitian vector bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)=0.

(1)

Let
P=h_{0}
. Then the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow (
1.5
)
has the solution

h(t)=(1+t)h_{0}

for
t\in[0,\infty)
.

(2)

Let
P=-h_{0}
. Then the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow (
1.5
) has the solution

h(t)=(1-t)h_{0}

for
t\in[0,1)
; it degenerates at

.
