Plasma Proteomics Identifies Proteins and Pathways Associated with Cataract: A Prospective Cohort Study
Authors: Yuzhou Zhang, Jun Yu, Yu Peng, Charlene C Yim, Ka Wai Kam, Mary Ho, Dong Fang, Xiu Juan Zhang, Mandy PH Ng, Patrick Ip, Chi Pui Pang, Clement C Tham, Li Jia Chen, Jason C Yam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo systematically identify plasma proteins associated with cataract development and explore their potential causal relationships. Design: Prospective population-based cohort study with integrated Mendelian randomization (MR) and colocalization analyses. Participants: Forty-nine thousand, five hundred and eighty-one UK Biobank participants free of cataract at baseline.MethodsCox proportional hazards models were used to assess associations between 2920 plasma proteins and cataract risk, followed by MR to evaluate causal relationships. Colocalization analysis was conducted to examine whether identified protein-cataract associations shared causal genetic variants. Main Outcome Measures: Incident cataract.ResultsOf the 2920 plasma proteins analyzed, 58 demonstrated significant associations with cataract risk (P < 1.71×10–5, Bonferroni-corrected threshold). Beta-crystallin B2 (CRYBB2) showed the most robust association (hazard ratio: 1.60; 95% confidence interval: 1.55–1.66, P = 5.28×10–164). Mendelian randomization analysis provided evidence supporting causal relationships for 4 proteins (CRYBB2, V-set and immunoglobulin domain-containing protein 4, mevalonate kinase, and metalloproteinase inhibitor 1) with cataract, with genetically predicted CRYBB2 levels showing a significant association with cataract risk, which is strongly supported by colocalization evidence. Functional enrichment analyses revealed involvement of biological pathways related to immune activation, cell fate decision, and structural remodeling in cataract development.ConclusionsThis study identified distinct plasma proteomic signatures associated with incident cataract, offering novel insights into cataract pathogenesis and highlighting potential targets for early detection and therapeutic development.Financial Disclosure(s)The author has no/the authors have no proprietary or commercial interest in any materials discussed in this article.Keywords: Proteomics, Cataract, Mendelian randomization, CRYBB2, Biomarkers

Cataract remains the leading cause of visual impairment and blindness worldwide, with its prevalence projected to increase substantially in aging populations.1,2 Although established risk factors such as advanced age, diabetes, and ultraviolet light exposure provide important epidemiological insights,3, 4, 5 the fundamental molecular mechanisms driving cataractogenesis remain incompletely elucidated. Proteins represent the functional output of genomic information and primary determinants of cellular phenotype. Therefore, systematic proteomic characterization offers opportunities to elucidate direct mechanistic insights into disease pathophysiology.6

While several studies have explored the proteomic landscape of cataract, their focus has been predominantly restricted to crystallin proteins, which are the most abundant proteome components of the lens.7, 8, 9, 10, 11 Additionally, most of these investigations have relied on analyses of lens tissue7 or human aqueous humor.8, 9, 10 Although these intraocular samples provide valuable local information, their invasive collection procedures limit feasibility for large-scale prospective studies. In contrast, plasma represents a promising alternative matrix capable of capturing both local and peripheral pathological signals. Importantly, emerging evidence indicates that lens-derived proteins, including crystallins, are detectable in circulating blood,12 supporting the feasibility of using plasma proteomics to gain insight into lens physiology and pathology. Furthermore, most available studies are limited by cross-sectional designs, small sample sizes, restricted proteomic coverage, and the absence of concomitant genetic data to evaluate the causal relevance of observed associations.

To address these research gaps, we present integrated analyses of observational and genetic data evaluating associations between plasma protein levels and incident cataract in a large, prospective cohort of 49 581 participants from the UK Biobank. The study aimed to (1) identify plasma proteins associated with the long-term risk of developing cataract; (2) assess the potential causal relevance of these proteins using 2-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) with cis-protein quantitative trait loci (cis-pQTLs) from the UK Biobank Pharma Proteomics Project; and (3) explore the underlying biological pathways and regulatory networks through functional enrichment analysis of the cataract-associated proteins.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

The UK Biobank is a large-scale, population-based prospective cohort that recruited >0.5 million participants aged 40 to 70 years between 2006 and 2010. At baseline, a wide range of data were collected, including demographic characteristics, health and lifestyle information, physical measurements, and biological samples. For the present analysis, we included 49 581 participants who met the following criteria: availability of baseline plasma protein measurements, complete data on all covariates, and no prior diagnosis of cataract (Figure S1, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

Ethical approval for the UK Biobank was obtained from the North West Multi-Centre Research Ethics Committee (21/NW/0157). The study was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki, and all study participants provided informed consent. The current study was conducted under UK Biobank Application Number 91320.

Plasma Proteomic Measurement

The UK Biobank Pharma Proteomics Project consortium conducted proteomic profiling of plasma samples collected at baseline from 53 026 participants. The antibody-based Olink Explore Proximity Extension Assay was used for standardized quantification. Detailed methodologies about sample handling, protein detection, data processing, and quality control have been previously documented.13 From the initial set of 2923 detected proteins, we included 2920 proteins in this study after excluding those with a missing data rate >60%. A full list of analyzed proteins is provided in Table S1 (available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org). All analyses were performed using normalized protein expression values derived from the raw protein measurements that are log-base-2 transformed. Protein levels were then transformed to z-scores.

Assessment of Cataract

Cataract was defined using the “first occurrence” fields, which provide a unified International Classification of Diseases 10 code and the earliest recorded date for each health outcome, derived from sources including self-report, primary care, hospital inpatient data, and death registers.14 Case ascertainment was supplemented with Classification of Surgical Operations and Procedures 4 codes C71.2 and C75.1. The relevant data-field identifiers are provided in Table S2 (available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org). The onset date of cataract was determined based on the date of earliest occurrence. Person-years were calculated from the date of baseline assessment to the date of onset cataract, death, loss to follow-up, or the end of follow-up (July 1, 2023), whichever came first.

Assessment of Covariates

Sociodemographic and lifestyle characteristics were collected at baseline through a touchscreen questionnaire. The covariates included age, sex (male, female), ethnicity (white, others), Townsend Deprivation Index, assessment center, alcohol consumption frequency (never/special occasions only, others), smoking status (never, previous/current), body mass index, hypertension (yes, no), and diabetes (yes, no). The first 10 genetic principal components were controlled for to mitigate potential population stratification.

Statistical Analysis

Study Participants

Baseline characteristics were presented as means and standard deviations for continuous variables, and frequencies with percentages for categorical variables. Differences between the cataract and noncataract groups were assessed using t-tests for continuous variables and chi-square tests for categorical variables.

Association Analysis

Cox proportional regression models were performed to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for the associations between each of the 2920 plasma proteins and incident cataract. The models were adjusted for age, sex, ethnicity, assessment center, body mass index, Townsend Deprivation Index, smoking status, alcohol consumption frequency, hypertension, diabetes, and the top 10 genetic principal components. The Bonferroni method was used for multiple testing correction, with a significance threshold set as P < 1.71 × 10–5 (0.05/2920).

To test the robustness of our findings, we performed 2 sensitivity analyses: (1) repeating the primary analysis restricted to participants who underwent cataract surgery, and (2) excluding participants who developed cataract within the first 2 years of follow-up to mitigate potential reverse causality.

MR Analysis

The causal relationship between plasma proteins significantly associated with cataract in the primary analysis was further investigated using 2-sample MR. Genetic instruments were constructed using cis-pQTLs obtained from the UK Biobank Pharma Proteomics Project, which is one of the largest genomic studies on protein levels released so far.13 Summary-level genome-wide association studies data for cataract were derived from the FinnGen R10 release, which included 26 758 cases and 189 604 controls (https://opengwas.io/datasets/finn-b-H7_CATARACTSENILE).

Instrumental variables were selected as cis-pQTLs with genome-wide significance (P < 5 × 10–8) and clumped to ensure independence (clumping window: 1000 kb, maximum linkage disequilibrium r2 of 0.001). Causal effects between each protein and cataract were estimated using 5 MR methods: inverse-variance weighted, MR–Egger, weighted median, weighted mode, and simple mode. For proteins with only 1 eligible cis-pQTL, the Wald ratio method was applied. A causal association was considered statistically significant if the inverse-variance weighted method, supplemented by ≥1 of the other MR methods, showed a significant result. Causal estimates are presented as odds ratios with 95% CIs. Multiple testing correction was performed using the false discovery rate method, with a significance threshold of false discovery rate q < 0.05.

To assess robustness and validity of the MR assumptions, we conducted several sensitivity analyses: the MR–Egger intercept test for horizontal pleiotropy, Cochran Q statistic for heterogeneity among instrumental variants, and leave-one-out analysis to evaluate the influence of individual single nucleotide polymorphisms.

Colocalization Analysis

To evaluate whether the observed MR associations between candidate proteins and cataract were confounded by linkage disequilibrium rather than reflecting a shared causal variant, we performed Bayesian colocalization analysis. This approach estimates the posterior probability that a given genomic region contains a single genetic variant influencing both the protein expression level (exposure) and cataract (outcome).15 Using default prior probabilities, we computed posterior probability for 5 competing hypotheses regarding 2 traits (T1: protein level; T2: cataract): (1) H0: no association with either trait; (2) H1: association with T1 only; (3) H2: association with T2 only; (4) H3: association with both T1 and T2, but with different causal variants; and (5) H4: association with both T1 and T2, sharing a single causal variant. A protein was considered to have strong evidence of colocalization when the posterior probability for H4 (PPH4) exceeded 0.8, indicating a high likelihood that the same underlying genetic variant influences both protein abundance and cataract risk.

Pathway Enrichment Analysis

To gain functional insights into the proteins significantly associated with incident cataract, we performed Gene Ontology and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes pathway enrichment analyses using the Metascape platform.16 All genes encoding the 2920 tested proteins were used as the background reference. As described in a previous study,16 terms with a P value <0.01, a minimum of 3 counts, and an enrichment factor >1.5 (representing the ratio of observed counts to expected counts by chance) were selected and subsequently clustered based on membership similarity.

In addition, we utilized the Transcriptional Regulatory Relationships Unraveled by Sentence-based Text mining database, which contains a comprehensive repository of transcription factor (TF) target regulatory relationships, including 8444 TF target interactions involving 800 human TFs.17 By integrating Transcriptional Regulatory Relationships Unraveled by Sentence-based Text mining with Metascape, we analyzed upstream transcriptional regulators to identify potential TFs that may govern the expression of these cataract-associated proteins. All genes encoding the 2920 tested proteins were employed as the background gene set for this analysis.

Results

Participants’ Characteristics

In this study, we included a total of 49 581 participants. During a median follow-up of 14.05 years (interquartile range 13.14–14.93; range: 0.02–17.23), 6199 incident cataract cases were identified. The baseline characteristics of the participants, stratified by cataract status, are summarized in Table 1. Participants who developed cataract were significantly older and had a higher prevalence of diabetes and hypertension compared with those who did not (both P < 0.001). The distributions of sex, body mass index, smoking status, and alcohol consumption frequency also differed significantly between the 2 groups.

Identifying Proteins Associated with Incident Cataract

Of the 2920 plasma proteins analyzed, 58 demonstrated statistically significant associations with incident cataract after adjustment for multiple comparison (Fig 1; Table S3, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org). Among these, 52 proteins were positively associated with increased cataract risk, while 6 exhibited protective associations. The most significant associations were observed for beta-crystallin B2 (CRYBB2) (HR = 1.60, 95% CI: 1.55–1.66; P = 5.28 × 10–164), growth/differentiation factor 15 (HR = 1.23, 95% CI: 1.16–1.29; P = 2.70 × 10–14), WAP 4-disulfide core domain protein 2 (HR = 1.23, 95% CI: 1.16–1.30; P = 8.58 × 10–13), chitinase-3-like protein 1 (HR = 1.10, 95% CI: 1.07–1.13; P = 2.05 × 10–11), and interleukin-15 (HR = 1.23, 95% CI: 1.18–1.35; P = 2.56 × 10–11). In addition, several proteins demonstrated protective effects against cataract development, including A disintegrin and metalloproteinase with thrombospondin motifs 8 (HR = 0.88, 95% CI: 0.84–0.93; P = 1.77 × 10–6), and WAP, Kazal, immunoglobulin, Kunitz and NTR domain-containing protein 1 (HR = 0.87, 95% CI: 0.82–0.92; P = 1.10 × 10–6).

In sensitivity analyses, we restricted the definition of incident cataract to cases who underwent cataract surgery. Among the 49 302 participants included in this analysis, 5920 incident cataract cases were identified. We repeated the primary analysis and the results revealed 58 proteins significantly associated with cataract risk. Of these, 50 overlapped with those identified in the main analysis. The top 5 most significantly associated proteins in the sensitivity analysis were CRYBB2 (HR = 1.61, 95% CI: 1.55–1.67; P = 3.20 × 10–148), growth/differentiation factor 15 (HR = 1.26, 95% CI: 1.19–1.33; P = 3.25 × 10-16), WAP 4-disulfide core domain protein 2 (HR = 1.24, 95% CI: 1.17–1.31; P = 2.70 × 10–12), von Willebrand factor A domain-containing protein 1 (HR = 1.22, 95% CI: 1.15–1.29; P = 2.19 × 10–11), and chitinase-3-like protein 1 (HR = 1.10, 95% CI: 1.07–1.13; P = 2.76 × 10–10) (Fig S2; Table S4, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org). When excluding participants who developed cataract within the first 2 years of follow-up, the results remained largely consistent (Table S5, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

MR and Colocalization Analyses

We conducted 2-sample MR analyses to evaluate the potential causal effects of the 58 significant proteins on cataract. We identified 4 proteins with statistically significant causal relationships with cataract. Among them, CRYBB2, V-set and immunoglobulin domain-containing protein 4 (VSIG4), and mevalonate kinase (MVK) were associated with an increased risk of cataract (odds ratio >1), whereas metalloproteinase inhibitor 1 (TIMP1) was associated with a decreased risk (odds ratio <1) (Fig 2). For other proteins, no causal relationship was found with cataract (Table S6, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

The scatter plots of the MR analyses for these 4 proteins demonstrated a consistent direction of effect across the genetic instruments, with the inverse-variance weighted regression line indicating a significant association between genetically predicted protein levels and cataract risk (Figs S3–S6, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org). Heterogeneity was observed for CRYBB2 and TIMP1. However, the MR–Egger intercept test revealed no significant evidence of horizontal pleiotropy (all P > 0.05; Tables S7 and S8, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org). Leave-one-out sensitivity analysis demonstrated that the causal estimates for these proteins were not driven by any single influential single nucleotide polymorphism (Figs S3–S7).

For the MR-identified proteins in relation to cataract, we implemented colocalization analysis to ensure that the results were not confounded by linkage disequilibrium. Strong evidence for colocalization (PPH4 >0.8) was observed for CRYBB2 (PPH4 = 98.74%), indicating that the same genetic variant likely influences both its plasma level and cataract risk. In contrast, no support for colocalization was found for the other proteins (all PPH4 <0.01) (Table S9, Fig S8, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

Biological Function of Cataract-Associated Proteins

We performed functional enrichment analysis to elucidate the biological processes and pathways associated with the genes encoding the cataract-associated proteins. The Gene Ontology analysis identified significant enrichment for terms spanning immune activation, cell fate decision, and structural remodeling. This included a prominent “response to cytokine,” the molecular function of “death receptor activity,” and biological processes related to tissue restructuring such as “collagen metabolic process” (Fig 3A). The complete list of enriched terms is available in Table S10 (available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org). Concurrently, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes pathway analysis revealed notable involvement of the cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction pathway (Fig 3B).

To further investigate upstream regulatory mechanisms, we analyzed potential TFs targeting the identified proteins. We identified 32 TFs potentially regulating the expression of the corresponding genes (Fig 3C). The top 5 TFs were Jun, Sp1, Sp3, STAT3, and RelA (Table S11, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

Discussion

In this large-scale proteome-wide association study, we identified 58 plasma proteins significantly associated with the risk of incident cataract. Mendelian randomization analyses further supported a causal role for 4 of these proteins (CRYBB2, VSIG4, MVK, and TIMP1) in cataract development. Subsequent colocalization analysis indicated that genetically predicted higher levels of CRYBB2 were associated with an increased risk of cataract. Overall, our findings highlight the potential value of proteomic biomarkers in mitigating cataract onset, offering promising directions for more personalized and effective therapeutic interventions.

This study provides a comprehensive profiling of the blood-based proteomic landscape of cataract within a large population-based Biobank. Although direct comparisons with prior studies are limited due to the scarcity of similar proteomic investigations, our results are consistent with existing clinical and experimental evidence. For instance, the adverse association of growth/differentiation factor 15 aligns with its recognized age-related upregulation and ultraviolet radiation b-induced expression in lens epithelial cells,18,19 while elevated interleukin-15 levels reinforce the documented role of inflammatory cytokines in cataract development.20 Among the most significantly associated proteins, chitinase-3-like protein 1 and WAP 4-disulfide core domain protein 2—key regulators of inflammatory signaling and extracellular matrix remodeling21,22—emerge as previously unreported candidates in cataract pathogenesis. Furthermore, our analysis reveals protective associations for several novel proteins. A disintegrin and metalloproteinase with thrombospondin motifs 8 may confer protection through suppression of pathological angiogenesis and cellular invasion.23 WAP, Kazal, immunoglobulin, Kunitz and NTR domain-containing protein 1 functions as a known inhibitor of specific transforming growth factor-beta superfamily members.24 These findings collectively illuminate new mechanistic dimensions of cataract pathogenesis and identify promising avenues for therapeutic development.

More importantly, our study provides convergent observational and genetic evidence establishing CRYBB2 as a causal factor in cataract pathogenesis. As a major crystallin and one of the most abundant and stable soluble proteins in the vertebrate lens, CRYBB2 plays an essential role in maintaining lens transparency and structural integrity.25 While previous studies have largely focused on CRYBB2 mutations in congenital cataract, primarily attributing pathogenicity to its genetic variants,26, 27, 28, 29, 30 our findings extend this understanding to the protein level by demonstrating that quantitative variation in CRYBB2 abundance contributes to cataract development. Previous work has shown that compromised structural and thermal stability of CRYBB2 can trigger pathological protein aggregation, ultimately leading to cataract formation.31,32 Together, these insights suggest that therapeutic strategies targeting CRYBB2 stability may represent a promising approach to reduce cataract risk. Such strategies could include the development of pharmacological chaperones to prevent its aggregation, or the enhancement of the lens's intrinsic proteostatic capacity to inhibit the formation of opaque aggregates.

Our MR analysis also identified 3 novel protein biomarkers associated with cataract: VSIG4, MVK, and TIMP1. V-set and immunoglobulin domain-containing protein 4, a B7 family protein highly expressed in tissue-resident macrophages, acts as a complement receptor for iC3b and C3b and functions to suppress complement activation and negatively regulate effector T-cell responses.33,34 While VSIG4 has been implicated in various immune-mediated disorders, its association has previously been documented only in diabetic retinopathy,35 with no established link to cataract pathogenesis. Our results thus suggest a previously unrecognized role for potential immune regulation and complement pathway dysregulation in cataract development. Mevalonate kinase catalyzes the phosphorylation of mevalonate in the cholesterol and isoprenoid biosynthesis pathway.36 Although MVK mutations are known to cause mevalonic aciduria, a rare metabolic disorder,37,38 our study provides the first evidence implicating MVK in cataract risk, indicating a potential role for cholesterol homeostasis in lens pathology. Metalloproteinase inhibitor 1, a well-established inhibitor of matrix metalloproteinases, is involved in extracellular matrix maintenance and remodeling. While TIMP1 has been associated with other ocular conditions such as myopia,39,40 glaucoma,41,42 and diabetic retinopathy,43 its relationship with cataract emerged uniquely in our analysis. Collectively, these findings reveal novel protein biomarkers for cataract and highlight potential targets for future pharmaceutical intervention.

Our functional analysis revealed significant enrichment for biological processes spanning immune activation, cell fate decision, and structural remodeling. The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes pathway analysis further substantiated these observations, demonstrating notable involvement of the cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction pathway, which aligns with the immune regulatory functions of identified proteins like VSIG4. The concordance of evidence corroborates the involvement of dysregulated immune signaling and inflammatory responses in cataract pathogenesis. Complementing these findings, we identified 32 TFs potentially regulating the expression of the corresponding genes, with Jun, Sp1, Sp3, STAT3, and RelA emerging as the top regulators. These TFs are known to regulate diverse cellular processes including inflammatory responses, cellular differentiation, and stress adaptation,44, 45, 46, 47, 48 thereby offering plausible upstream regulatory mechanisms for the observed protein alterations. Together, the integration of transcriptional regulatory networks and pathway analyses provides evidence that cataract pathogenesis involves a complex interplay of multiple pathological processes.

Our study has several limitations. First, the diagnosis of cataract primarily relied on medical records and surgical registries, which may lead to underdiagnosis of early-stage or asymptomatic cases. Second, while the UK Biobank provides extensive coverage of circulating proteins, it does not encompass the entire human proteome, potentially omitting relevant protein biomarkers. Third, the participants in our cohort are of European ancestry exclusively, which may limit the generalizability of our findings to other ethnic populations. Fourth, although we employed genetic methods including MR and colocalization analyses to infer causality, the potential for residual confounding, particularly through horizontal pleiotropy, cannot be entirely ruled out for identified protein–cataract associations. Finally, further validation in external cohorts and diverse populations is essential to confirm the robustness and clinical applicability of our findings.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this large-scale proteomic study identifies a comprehensive spectrum of plasma proteins associated with cataract risk. The integration of observational and genetic evidence confirms several novel biomarkers, including VSIG4, MVK, and TIMP1, suggesting their previously unrecognized roles in the development of cataract. These findings advance our understanding of the molecular mechanisms of cataractogenesis and highlight promising avenues for further investigation into the therapeutic potential of these protein biomarkers.
