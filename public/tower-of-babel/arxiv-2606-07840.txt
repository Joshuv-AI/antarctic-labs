A Divergence-Free Scott-Vogelius Finite Element Method for the Surface Stokes Problem Thanks: This work was supported in part by the NSF, grant no. DMS-2309425
Yerim Kone, Michael Neilan, David Poling

Abstract
. We construct and analyze an exactly divergence-free Scott-Vogelius finite element method for the surface Stokes problem. The proposed scheme simultaneously enforces the tangentiality and incompressibility constructs exactly and has the same number of unknowns as the two-dimensional Euclidean discretization. Our construction extends the surface finite element framework of [ 9 , 10 ] to Scott–Vogelius discretizations defined on curved Clough–Tocher triangulations. In contrast to previous isoparametric Scott–Vogelius methods based on macro-element constructions, the present approach defines the finite element spaces directly on the refined surface triangulation, leading to a substantially simpler and more practical implementation. We prove inf-sup stability of the method and derive optimal-order convergence in the isoparametric regime.



1.
Introduction

The stationary surface Stokes problem reads

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\bm{\Pi}{\rm div}_{\gamma}{\rm Def}_{\gamma}\bm{u}+\nabla_{\gamma}p+\bm{u}

\displaystyle={\bm{f}}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on }\gamma,

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{\Gamma}\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on }\gamma,

along with the pressure constant
\int_{\gamma}p=0

and the velocity tangentiality constrant
\bm{u}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}=0
. Here,
we assume
\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a smooth, closed and orientable two-dimensional surface
with outward unit normal
{\bm{\nu}}
.

Due its many applications in, e.g., lipid bilayers,
thin films, and surfactent-laden interfaces
[
13
,
26
,
30
]
,
the construction of
finite element methods for the surface
(Navier)-Stokes (
1.1
)
has become an active and burgeoning area of research.
Similar to the Euclidean setting, numerical schemes
based on the standard velocity-pressure formulation
must satisfy a compatibility criterion of the two discrete spaces
to ensure stability of the method, namely, a discrete inf-sup condition.
However, the direct use of standard Euclidean finite element
pairs is not straightforward due to the tangential velocity
constraint
\bm{u}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}=0
. In particular, this constraint is, in a sense,
incompatible with conventional
\bm{H}^{1}
-conforming vector-valued
spaces, since strongly enforcing tangentiality of a continuous
vector field on a discrete (Lipschitz) geometry may lead to a locking
phenomenon that degrades the approximation
properties of the finite element space.

As such, there are generally two approaches
to construct convergent surface finite element methods
for (
1.1
) based on the velocity-pressure formulation.
In the first class of methods, discrete spaces developed for the Euclidean formulation are adapted to the surface setting, and the tangential constraint
is enforced weakly either by penalization or by introducing Lagrange multipliers.

[
15
,
19
,
27
,
28
]
.
While such schemes may lead to convergent
methods, they often require user-defined penalty parameters and involve
superfluous degrees of freedom, since the normal component of the velocity
approximation remains an unknown. In addition, achieving optimal-order convergence
may require a high-order approximation of the surface, particularly
for low-order discretizations
[
20
]
.
Consequently, this family of schemes
may lead to sub-optimal convergence rates on solution-dependent evolving geometries
or in situations where the surface is only partially known.

The second class of finite element methods
takes the opposite approach and strongly enforce
tangentiality and relax the
\bm{H}^{1}
-conformity
of the velocity approximation. For example,
the methods given in
[
2
,
23
,
4
,
22
]

approximate the velocity field by the
\bm{H}({\rm div})
-conforming
BDM space and use interior penalty or HDG stabilization techniques to compensate
the lack of conformity of the scheme. These methods
exactly enforce both the tangentiality and divergence-free
constraint in (
1.1
),
but they lead to a relatively large algebraic system compared to

\bm{H}^{1}
-conforming schemes. In addition, as far as we are aware,
a complete stability and convergence analysis is missing from the literature.

Another approach within this second class,
and the one adopted in the present article,
is to modify classical Euclidean Stokes pairs so that they
exactly enforce the tangenality constraint at the expense
of
\bm{H}^{1}
-conformity
[
9
,
10
]
,
while still retaining
\bm{H}({\rm div})
-conformity.
In particular, the velocity space is constructed
by utilizing an auxiliary Piola transform to communicate
function values at interelement Lagrange degrees of freedom.
Unlike the BDM-based discretizations, the resuling discrete spaces
possess
inherent weak continuity properties and therefore
do not require ad hoc stabilization or interelement integration
in the discrete bilinear forms. Moreover, these methods
have the same
dimensions as their two-dimensional Euclidean counterpart
and do not require high-order approximations of the outward unit
normal to obtain optimal-order convergence across all polynomial degrees.
The approach given in
[
9
]
and
[
10
]

has been applied to the Euclidean (low-order) MINI element
and the (high-order) Taylor-Hood pair, respectively. These classical
finite element spaces only enforce the divergence-free constraint
weakly and this property is inherited in the corresponding surface
discretizations.

Recently, there has been significant interest
in discretizations that exactly enforce
the divergence-free constraint
in (Navier)-Stokes models,
as such methods lead to several inherent advantages
[
21
,
32
,
6
,
16
,
14
,
17
,
1
]
. In particular, they exactly preserve
conservation laws at the discrete level for any mesh parameter
and are pressure-robust, i.e., the velocity approximation is independent
of the pressure variable with greater robustness with respect
to the model parameters
[
21
]
. While the construction
of exactly divergence–free finite element spaces
is now a flourishing research area for Euclidean fluid models,
substantially less progress has been made in the surface setting.
Aside from the BDM-based methods

[
2
,
23
]
,
all of the aforementioned surface methods impose the incompressibility
constraint only weakly and consequently do not produce exactly divergence–free velocity
fields. This motivates the development of surface finite element methods
that simultaneously enforce both the tangentility and
incompressibility constraints exactly, while still retainining
the approximation and stability properties of classical Euclidean Stokes pairs.

The goal of this paper is to extend
the constructions of
[
9
,
10
]

to divergence-free pairs, with a focus on the Scott-Vogelius
pair
[
31
,
18
]
. In the Euclidean setting
the velocity space consists of continuous, piecewise polynomials
of degree
r
, whereas the pressure space is given by discontinuous
piecewise polynomials of degree
(r-1)
(in the absence of singular vertices).
In the planar case, this pair is stable provided the polynomial degree
satisfies
r\geq 4
and the triangulation does not contain
“nearly singular” vertices. To reduce these restrictive
polynomial-degree and mesh constraints, a common strategy,
and the one adopted here,
is to implement
the scheme on Clough-Tocher triangulations,
i.e., triangulations obtained by connecting the barycenter of
each triangle to its vertices
[
17
,
29
]
.
On such triangulations, the polynomial degree restriction is relaxed
to
r\geq 2
and the presence of (nearly) singular vertices is eliminated.
We extend these results to the surface cases to construct
divergence-free and exactly tangential methods, using
the construction in
[
9
,
10
]
.

While the implementation of Scott-Vogelius pairs
on split (Clough-Tocher) triangulations is
now relatively standard, an alternative and more recent perspective
is to view the spaces as macro-element finite elements

[
25
,
12
]
.
In this approach, the global finite element space is built on
a generic shape-regular simplicial mesh, whereas the local spaces
are defined with respect to a subdivision of each triangle
into subsimplices. Thus, the reference basis functions
used in the implementation are piecewise polynomials
relative to a locally split reference simplex.
On affine meshes, this macro-element viewpoint is equivalent
to the refined-triangulation construction,
in the sense that both yield the same global finite element space.
However, the two approaches differ on meshes with curved edges
or faces.

Scott-Vogelius pairs in the Euclidean isoparametric setting,
based on the macro-element definition, were constructed
and analyzed in
[
25
,
12
]
. This
viewpoint naturally aligns with the underlying stability
theory, which itself is based on a macro-element argument.
However, despite its advantages for the stability analysis,
the macro-element construction leads to relatively
complicated finite element spaces that
are arduous to implement in practice.
In particular, the spaces
are defined by mapping
piecewise
polynomial
basis functions on the reference triangle. As a result,
most standard finite element software does not support this construction,
as it requires specialized quadrature rules and data structures
(but see
[
3
]
for recent progress in this direction).

Unlike the Euclidean isoparametric methods
[
25
,
12
]
,
in this paper we define the Scott-Vogelius pair directly
on a (curved) surface Clough-Tocher triangulation
rather than through a macro-element construction.
This viewpoint leads to a simpler and more practical scheme
than its macro-element counterpart. In particular,
the proposed velocity space is the same as the Taylor-Hood
space given in
[
10
]
(but on a Clough-Tocher
triangulation). The tradeoff, however, is a significantly
more delicate stability analysis.
To establish stability of the proposed method,
we quantify the discrepancy between the macro-element and non-macro
element constructions and then employ a perturbation
argument to transfer stability from the former to the latter.
As a byproduct of the analysis,
we also prove the stability of the corresponding macro Scott-Vogelius pair
on surfaces,
as well as the stability of the surface
\mathbb{P}_{r}-\mathbb{P}_{0}
pair.

Lastly we comment on the issue of pressure robustness.
A common feature of divergence-free methods is
that the velocity error is independent of the
pressure approximation. In the Euclidean setting,
this property is one of the main advantages of exactly
divergence-free discretizations. For the surface Stokes problem,
however, the situation is more subtle, and pressure robustness is not
automatically guaranteed for exactly (surface) divergence-free methods.
The primary obstruction arises from geometric approximation errors
and the required extension of the source function
{\bm{f}}

to the computational surface. We argue and numerically
show that commonly used extensions destroy pressure robustness.
We also provide a framework for constructing
extensions of
{\bm{f}}
that preserves pressure robustness;
see Remark
5.6
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
After setting the notation, we define the triangulations,
and polynomial diffeomorphisms in Section
2
.
Here, we also define pullbacks between the continuous
and discrete surfaces, as well as the Piola transform.
In Section
3
we define the surface Scott-Vogelius
spaces and prove it is a divergence-free pair.
Section
4
provides an inf-sup stability
proof of the surface pair, and as a byproduct proves
stability of the associated macro pair.
The finite element method and its convergence analysis
is given in Section
5
, and numerical examples
are given in Section
6
.
Finally, the proofs of some technical results are presented
in the appendix.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
d
be the signed-distance function of
\gamma
, defined
in a tubular region
U_{\delta}
of
\gamma
.
Set
{\bm{\nu}}=\nabla d
, and note that
{\bm{\nu}}|_{\gamma}
is the outward unit normal of
\gamma
.
The shape operator
is given by
{\bf H}=\nabla{\bm{\nu}}=D^{2}d
, and note that
{\bf H}{\bm{\nu}}=0
since
|{\bm{\nu}}|=1
.
The other two eigenvalues of
{\bf H}
are denoted by
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
, the principal
curvatures of
\gamma
. The closest point projection

\bm{p}:U_{\delta}\to\gamma
is given by
p(x)=x-d(x)\bm{n}(x)
,
and we define the tangential projection
\bm{\Pi}={\bf I}_{3}-{\bm{\nu}}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}
,
where
{\bf I}_{3}
is the
3\times 3
identity matrix.

The surface gradient of a scalar function
p:\gamma\to\mathbb{R}

is given by
\nabla_{\gamma}p=\bm{\Pi}\nabla p^{e}
, where
p^{e}=p\circ\bm{p}

denotes its extension. The surface gradient
of a vector-valued function
\bm{v}:\gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}

is
\nabla_{\gamma}\bm{v}=\bm{\Pi}\nabla\bm{v}^{e}\bm{\Pi}
, whereas
the surface divergence and deformation tensor is

{\rm div}_{\gamma}\bm{v}={\rm tr}(\nabla_{\gamma}\bm{v})

and
{\rm Def_{\gamma}}\bm{v}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{\gamma}\bm{v}+(\nabla_{\gamma}\bm{v})^{\intercal})
,
respectively.

We denote by
\bm{H}^{1}(\gamma)
the Sobolev space
of vector fields whose components lie in
H^{1}(\gamma)
,
and let
\bm{H}^{1}_{T}(\gamma)=\{\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\gamma):\ \bm{v}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}=0\}
.
Let
\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\gamma};\gamma)
denote the space of square integrable
vector fields with (weak) divergence in
L^{2}(\gamma)
, and
let
L^{2}_{0}(\gamma)
be the space
of square integrable functions on
\gamma
with vanishing mean.
For a flat domain
D
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
we denote by
\mathbb{P}_{k}(D)
the space of polynomials defined
on
D
with degree
\leq k
.

2.1.
Affine triangulation

We adopt the construction and notations of the triangulations found in
[
10

Let
\bar{\Gamma}_{h}

\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,

\bar{T}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.
We assume that the distance function
d
is defined on
\bar{\Gamma}_{h}

d(x)=\mathcal{O}(h^{2})

,
where

For each

\bar{\mathcal{V}}_{h}

Let
\hat{T}

(1,0),(0,1),(0,0)
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|\nabla F_{\bar{T}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}\lesssim h_{\bar{T}},\qquad\det(\nabla F_{\bar{T}}^{\intercal}\nabla F_{\bar{T}})\approx h_{\bar{T}}^{4}.

Here,
\nabla F_{\bar{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 2}
is the
(constant) Jacobian of

.
We use the notation

to indicate the existence of a constant

Mappings between

{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathcal{S}_{0}\to\mathcal{S}_{1}
is a
bi-Lipschitz mapping. The Piola transform of a vector field

\bm{v}_{0}:\mathcal{S}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
with respect
to
{\bm{\Phi}}
is the function
(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}}\bm{v}_{0}):\mathcal{S}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
given
by

(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}}\bm{v}_{0}):=(\mu^{-1}\nabla{\bm{\Phi}}\bm{v}_{0})\circ{\bm{\Phi}}^{-1},

where
\mu:\mathcal{S}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\mu d\sigma_{0}=d\sigma_{1}
, and
d\sigma_{i}
is the surface measure
of
\mathcal{S}_{i}
. Likewise, the Piola transform of
a vector field
\bm{v}_{1}:\mathcal{S}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}

with respect to the inverse mapping
{\bm{\Phi}}^{-1}

is given by

(\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}^{-1}}\bm{v}_{1})=\mu(\nabla{\bm{\Phi}}^{-1}\bm{v}_{1})\circ{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathcal{S}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}.

These transforms satisfy

{\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{0}}\bm{v}_{0}=(\mu^{-1}{\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}}\bm{v}_{0})\circ{\bm{\Phi}}^{-1}

and

{\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\bm{v}_{1}=\mu({\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{0}}\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}^{-1}}\bm{v}_{1})\circ{\bm{\Phi}}

for all
\bm{v}_{0}\in\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{0}};\mathcal{S}_{0})
and

\bm{v}_{1}\in\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}};\mathcal{S}_{1})
.
In particular,
\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}}:\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{0}};\mathcal{S}_{0})\to\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}};\mathcal{S}_{1})

and

\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Phi}}^{-1}}:\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}};\mathcal{S}_{1})\to\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\mathcal{S}_{0}};\mathcal{S}_{0})

are bounded operators.

In the case
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\Gamma_{h}
,
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\gamma
,
and
{\bm{\Phi}}=\bm{p}|_{\Gamma_{h}}
, we introduce the notation

\wideparen{\bm{v}}_{0}:=\mathcal{P}_{\bm{p}}\bm{v}_{0}=(\mu_{h}^{-1}\nabla\bm{p}\bm{v}_{0})\circ\bm{p}^{-1}=(\mu_{h}^{-1}\big[\bm{\Pi}-d{\bf H}\big]\bm{v}_{0})\circ\bm{p}^{-1},

where
\mu_{h}
is given by (
2.7
).
Likewise, we set

\breve{\bm{v}}_{1}:=\mathcal{P}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}\bm{v}_{1}=\mu_{h}\left[{\bf I}_{3}-\frac{{\bm{\nu}}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{h}}{{\bm{\nu}}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{h}}\right]\left[{\bf I}_{3}-d{\bf H}\right]^{-1}\bm{v}_{1}\circ\bm{p},

and note that

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v}_{0})q

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}({\rm div}_{\gamma}\wideparen{\bm{v}}_{0})q^{\ell}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall q\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{h}),

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}({\rm div}_{\gamma}\bm{v}_{1})q

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}\breve{\bm{v}}_{1})q^{e}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall q\in L^{2}(\gamma),

and (cf.
[
10
, (2.11)]
)

\|\bm{v}_{0}\|_{H^{\ell}(K)}\approx\|\wideparen{\bm{v}}_{0}\|_{H^{\ell}(\bm{p}(K))}\qquad\forall\bm{v}_{0}\in\bm{H}^{\ell}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct},\ \ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Similarly, if

\bm{v}\in\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}};\Gamma_{h})

and
\bar{\bm{v}}=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}^{-1}}\bm{v}\in\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}};\bar{\Gamma}_{h})
, then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v})q=\int_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}({\rm div}_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}}\bar{\bm{v}})(q\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct})\qquad\forall q\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{h}),

and
[
10
, (2.16)]

\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{\ell}(K)}\approx\|\bar{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{\ell}(\bar{K})}\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{\ell}(K),\ \ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ K={\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}(\bar{K}).

3.
The surface Scott-Vogelius finite element pair

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\geq 2
and
for a curved surface triangle
in the Clough-Tocher triangulation
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct}
, we define the local spaces

\bm{V}(K):=\{\mathcal{P}_{F_{K}}\hat{\bm{v}}:\ \hat{\bm{v}}\in\mathbb{P}_{r}(\hat{T})^{2}\},\qquad Q(K):=\{\hat{q}\circ F_{K}^{-1}:\ \hat{q}\in\mathbb{P}_{r-1}(\hat{T})\},

where we recall that
F_{K}:\hat{T}\to K
is a polynomial
diffeomorphism of degree
k
, and
\mathcal{P}_{F_{K}}
is the Piola
transform with respect to this mapping.

Let
\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}^{\rm ct}
denote the set
of points of Lagrange degrees of freedom (DOFs) of degree
r

with respect to the affine Clough-Tocher triangulation
\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\rm ct}
,
and map these points to the computational triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct}
via
{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}
:

\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\rm ct}:=\{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}(\bar{a}):\ \bar{a}\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}^{\rm ct}\}.

As in
[
10
]
, to control the consistency errors
of the resulting finite element scheme in Section
5
below,
we make the following assumption
about the placement of the edge DOFs.

Assumption 3.1
.

We assume that the edge DOFs in
\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}^{\rm ct}
correspond to the
(r+1)
-point
Gauss-Lobatto rule.

For a DOF
a
, we denote by
\mathcal{T}^{\rm ct}_{a}\subset\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct}

the set of elements abutting
a
.
We then introduce the following Piola transform
used to enforce weak continuity properties
in the (velocity) finite element space (cf.
[
9
,
10
]
).

Definition 3.2
.

For each
a\in\mathcal{N}^{\rm ct}_{h}
, we arbitrarily choose a single fixed
K_{a}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm ct}_{a}
.
For
K\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm ct}_{a}
, we define
\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}{\bm{x}}=\left(({\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}(a)\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)){\bf I}_{3}-{\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}(a)\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)\right){\bm{x}}.

Namely,
\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}{\bm{x}}
is the Piola transform
of the vector
{\bm{x}}
with respect to the inverse of the closest point projection
onto the plane tangent to
K_{a}
at
a
(cf.
[
9
,
10
]
).

The global Scott-Vogelius-type pair
is defined by concatenating the local spaces,
gluing the velocity space at the Lagrange DOFs via the operator
\mathcal{M}^{K}_{a}
:

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{h}

\displaystyle=\{\bm{v}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{h}):\ \bm{v}|_{K}\in\bm{V}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct};\ \bm{v}|_{K}(a)=\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}\bm{v}|_{K_{a}}(a)\ \forall a\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\rm ct}\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{a}^{\rm ct}\},

\displaystyle Q_{h}

\displaystyle=\{q\in L^{2}_{0}(\Gamma_{h}):\ q|_{K}\in Q(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct}\}.

The discrete velocity space
\bm{V}_{h}
is the same
as the one given in
[
10
]
, but defined with respect to a surface Clough-Tocher
triangulation. However, the discrete pressure space
Q_{h}
consists of discontinuous
(mapped) polynomials, whereas continuous (mapped) piecewise polyomials are used
in
[
10
]
.

Remark 3.3
.

It is shown in
[
10
]

that
\bm{V}_{h}\subset\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}};\Gamma_{h})
, i.e.,
functions in
\bm{V}_{h}
have co-normal continuity across edges
in the mesh. In addition, the space has “weak continuity” properties,
as described in Section
5.1
below.

The next lemma shows that

\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
constitutes a divergence-free pair
for the Stokes problem.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}
satisfies

\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v})q=0\qquad\forall q\in Q_{h},

then
{\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v}\equiv 0
on
\Gamma_{h}
.

Proof.

For each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct}
,
write
\bm{v}|_{K}=\mathcal{P}_{F_{K}}\hat{\bm{v}}_{K}
for some
\hat{\bm{v}}_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{r}(\hat{T})^{2}
.
Define
q
such that

q|_{K}=(\hat{\nabla}\cdot\hat{\bm{v}}_{K})\circ F_{K}^{-1}\qquad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm ct}_{h},

and let
c=|\Gamma_{h}|^{-1}\int_{\Gamma_{h}}q\in\mathbb{R}
.
Because
\hat{\nabla}\cdot\hat{\bm{v}}_{K}\in\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{r-1}(\hat{T})
, we have

q|_{K}\in Q(K)
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm ct}_{h}
, and so
(q-c)\in Q_{h}
.

By the divergence theorem, and by divergence-preserving
properties of the Piola transform, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v})(q-c)=\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v})q

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm ct}_{h}}\int_{K}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v})(\hat{\nabla}\cdot\hat{\bm{v}}_{K})\circ F_{K}^{-1}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm ct}_{h}}\int_{K}\sqrt{\det(\nabla F_{K}^{\intercal}\nabla F_{K})\circ F_{K}^{-1}}|{\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v}|^{2}.

From this identity, we conclude
{\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v}\equiv 0
.
∎

One of the main goals in this paper is to prove the following inf-sup
stability result. The next section is devoted to its proof.

Theorem 3.5
.

There exists
\beta>0
independent of the mesh parameter
h
such that

(3.2)

\beta\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma_{h}}\bm{v})q}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h})}}\qquad\forall q\in Q_{h}.

4.
An auxiliary macro surface Scott-Vogelius finite element pair and
proof of Theorem
3.5

To prove the stability result in Theorem
3.5
,
we first establish an analogous stability result for
auxiliary macro finite element spaces. Once this is achieved, we apply a perturbation argument to derive the desired estimate (
3.2
).
These results also show that the auxiliary macro finite element pair provides a stable and divergence-free discretization of the surface Stokes problem and is therefore of independent interest. However, its practical implementation is relatively involved, as the reference basis functions of the finite element spaces are piecewise polynomials.

4.2.
Local macro spaces

Let

\mathbb{P}_{r}(\hat{T}^{ct})
be the space
of (discontinuous) piecewise polynomials of degree
\leq r

with respect to the reference Clough-Tocher split
\hat{T}^{ct}
,
and set
\mathbb{P}_{r}^{c}(\hat{T}^{ct})=\mathbb{P}_{r}(\hat{T}^{ct})\cap H^{1}(\hat{T})

to be the analogous continuous piecewise polynomial space.
For
\bar{T}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and
{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{h}
related via

{T^{\rm m}}={\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}(\bar{T})
, we define the local spaces

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}:

\displaystyle=[\mathbb{P}^{c}_{r}(\hat{T}^{ct})]^{2},\qquad

\displaystyle{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}(\bar{T}):=\{\mathcal{P}_{F_{\bar{T}}}\hat{\bm{w}}:\ \hat{\bm{w}}\in\widehat{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}\},\qquad

\displaystyle{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}({T^{\rm m}}):=\{\mathcal{P}_{F_{{T^{\rm m}}}}\hat{\bm{w}}:\ \hat{\bm{w}}\in\widehat{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}\},

\displaystyle\widehat{Q}^{\rm m}:

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{r-1}(\hat{T}^{ct}),\qquad

\displaystyle Q^{\rm m}(\bar{T}):=\{\hat{r}\circ F_{\bar{T}}^{-1}:\ \hat{r}\in\widehat{Q}^{\rm m}\},\qquad

\displaystyle Q^{\rm m}({T^{\rm m}}):=\{\hat{r}\circ F_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{-1}:\ \hat{r}\in\widehat{Q}^{\rm m}\}.

Because
F_{\bar{T}}
is affine,
\mathcal{P}_{F_{\bar{T}}}
can be identified
with a constant
3\times 2
matrix, and thus
{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}(\bar{T})
simply
consists of (continuous) tangential piecewise polynomials
of degree
r
with respect to the local Clough-Tocher split of
\bar{T}
. Further note that
\mathcal{P}_{F_{{T^{\rm m}}}}=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}\circ F_{\bar{T}}}=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}}\mathcal{P}_{F_{\bar{T}}}
,
and so we have an equivalent definition of
{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}({T^{\rm m}})
and
Q^{\rm m}({T^{\rm m}})
:

\displaystyle{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}({T^{\rm m}})

\displaystyle=\{\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}}\bar{\bm{w}}:\ \bar{\bm{w}}\in{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}(\bar{T})\},

\displaystyle Q^{\rm m}({T^{\rm m}})

\displaystyle=\{\bar{r}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}:\ \bar{r}\in Q^{\rm m}(\bar{T})\}.

We also define the spaces with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions:

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{0}:

\displaystyle=\widehat{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}\cap\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\hat{T}),\qquad

\displaystyle{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{0}(\bar{T}):=\{\mathcal{P}_{F_{\bar{T}}}\hat{\bm{w}}:\ \hat{\bm{w}}\in\widehat{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{0}\},\qquad

\displaystyle{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{0}({T^{\rm m}}):=\{\mathcal{P}_{F_{{T^{\rm m}}}}\hat{\bm{w}}:\ \hat{\bm{w}}\in\widehat{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{0}\},

\displaystyle\widehat{Q}^{\rm m}_{0}:

\displaystyle=\widehat{Q}^{\rm m}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\hat{T}),\qquad

\displaystyle Q^{\rm m}_{0}(\bar{T}):=\{\hat{r}\circ F_{\bar{T}}^{-1}:\ \hat{r}\in\widehat{Q}^{\rm m}_{0}\},\qquad

\displaystyle Q^{\rm m}_{0}({T^{\rm m}}):=\{\hat{r}\circ F_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{-1}:\ \hat{r}\in\widehat{Q}^{\rm m}_{0}\}.

The following result essentially follows from
[
25
, Theorem 3.9]
.
For completeness, we provide its proof.

Lemma 4.4
.

Given
{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{h}
and
r\in Q^{\rm m}_{0}({T^{\rm m}})
, there exists
\bm{w}\in{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{0}({T^{\rm m}})
such that

{\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\bm{w}=\frac{h^{2}_{T}r}{\sqrt{\det((\nabla F_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{\intercal}\nabla F_{{T^{\rm m}}})\circ F_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{-1})}}\quad\text{in }{T^{\rm m}},\quad\text{and}\quad\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}({T^{\rm m}})}\lesssim\|r\|_{L^{2}({T^{\rm m}})}.

Proof.

Let
r\in Q^{\rm m}_{0}({T^{\rm m}})
be arbitrary. Then, there exists
\hat{r}\in\widehat{Q}^{\rm m}_{0}
such that
r=\hat{r}\circ F_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{-1}
. It follows that
h^{2}_{T}\hat{r}\in\widehat{Q}^{\rm m}_{0}
, and so by the surjectivity
of the divergence operator on the local Clough-Tocher split
\hat{T}^{ct}
(cf.
[
17
]
), there exists
\hat{\bm{w}}\in\widehat{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{0}
such that
\widehat{\rm div}\,\hat{\bm{w}}=h_{T}^{2}\hat{r}
and
\|\hat{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{1}(\hat{T})}\lesssim\|h_{T}^{2}\hat{r}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{T})}
. Letting
\bm{w}=\mathcal{P}_{F_{{T^{\rm m}}}}\hat{\bm{w}}
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\bm{w}=\frac{\widehat{\rm div}\hat{\bm{w}}}{\sqrt{\det(\nabla F_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{\intercal}\nabla F_{{T^{\rm m}}})}}\circ F_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{h^{2}_{T}\hat{r}}{\sqrt{\det(\nabla F_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{\intercal}\nabla F_{{T^{\rm m}}})}}\circ F_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{-1}=\frac{h^{2}_{T}r}{\sqrt{\det(\nabla F_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{\intercal}\nabla F_{{T^{\rm m}}})\circ F_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{-1}}}.

Finally, we make a change of variables to obtain

\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}({T^{\rm m}})}\lesssim h^{-1}_{T}\|\hat{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{1}(\hat{T})}\lesssim h_{T}\|\hat{r}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{T})}\lesssim\|r\|_{L^{2}({T^{\rm m}})}.

∎

4.3.
Auxiliary global macro finite element spaces

Similar to the space
\bm{V}_{h}
, defined on the Clough-Tocher triangulation,
we define the global auxiliary Scott-Vogelius velocity
space as the space of functions which
are locally in
{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}({T^{\rm m}})
for each
{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{h}

and have weak continuity properties
at the degrees of freedom.
Let

\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\rm m,ct}=\{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}(\bar{a}):\ \bar{a}\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}^{\rm ct}\}

denote the locations of the
r
th-degree Lagrange degrees of freedom
on
\mathcal{T}^{\rm m,ct}_{h}
.
For each
a\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\rm m,ct}
,
let
\mathcal{T}^{\rm m,ct}_{a}\subset\mathcal{T}^{\rm m,ct}_{h}
be the set of elements
abutting
a
. We then set

K^{\rm m}_{a}={\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}({\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}^{-1}(K_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}({\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}(a))}))

as the selected element associated with the DOF
a
, that is,
the selected element corresponds to the selected element in the
triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct}
.

Similar to (
3.1
), we define the operator (
a\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\rm m,ct}
)

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K^{\rm m}}{\bm{x}}=\left(({\bm{\nu}}_{K^{\rm m}_{a}}(a)\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{K^{\rm m}}(a)){\bf I}_{3}-{\bm{\nu}}_{K^{\rm m}_{a}}(a)\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{K^{\rm m}}(a)\right){\bm{x}}.

Note that if
a\in{\rm int}({T^{\rm m}})
for some
{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{h}
,
then the outward unit normal at
a
is single-valued
because
{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}|_{\bar{T}}
is a
C^{\infty}
mapping (with
{T^{\rm m}}={\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}(\bar{T})
).
Thus in this case
{\bm{\nu}}_{K^{\rm m}_{a}}(a)={\bm{\nu}}_{K^{\rm m}}(a)
,
and so
\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K^{\rm m}}=\bm{\Pi}_{{T^{\rm m}}}(a)
. In particular,

\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K^{\rm m}}{\bm{x}}={\bm{x}}
for all
\bm{x}

tangent to
\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}
at
a
.

The global macro spaces are given by

(4.6)

\begin{split}{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}&=\{\bm{w}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}):\ \bm{w}|_{T^{\rm m}}\in{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}({T^{\rm m}})\ \forall{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{h},\\
&\qquad\bm{w}|_{K^{\rm m}}(a)=\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K^{\rm m}}(\bm{w}|_{K^{\rm m}_{a}}(a))\ \forall K^{\rm m}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m,ct}_{a},\ \forall a\in\mathcal{N}^{\rm m,ct}_{h}\},\\

Remark 4.5
.

Similar to the space
\bm{V}_{h}
(cf. Remark
3.3
),
there holds
{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}\subset\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}};\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})

due to the arguments in

[

. Furthermore, by
the preceding paragraph, there holds the following equivalent definition of

, defined with with respect to the triangulation

(4.7)

\begin{split}{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}&=\{\bm{w}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}):\ \bm{w}|_{T^{\rm m}}\in{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}({T^{\rm m}})\ \forall{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{h},\\
&\qquad\bm{w}|_{T^{\rm m}}(a)=\mathcal{M}_{a}^{T^{\rm m}}(\bm{w}|_{T^{\rm m}_{a}}(a))\ \forall T^{\rm m}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{a},\ \forall a\in\mathcal{N}^{\rm m}_{h}\},\end{split}

where
\mathcal{N}^{\rm m}_{h}=\{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}(\bar{a}):\ \bar{a}\in\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}\}
,

\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}
is the set of
r
th-degree degrees of freedom with respect to
\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,

T^{\rm m}_{a}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm m}
is the unique element with
K^{\rm m}_{a}\subset T^{\rm m}_{a}
,
and
\mathcal{T}_{a}^{\rm m}
are the elements in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm m}
abutting
a
.
Similar to Assumption
3.1
,
we assume that the edge DOFs in
\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{h}
correspond
to the
(r+1)
-point Gauss-Lobatto integration rule.
The original definition (
4.6
) will be used in the perturbation argument in Section
4.5
, while the alternative definition (

) will be used in the stability analysis of the pair

.

4.4.
Inf-sup stability for auxiliary macro pair

Similar to the Euclidean setting (cf.
[
17
]
),
we prove stability of
{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}\times Q^{\rm m}_{h}
via a macro-element
technique, which relies on a local inf-sup stability result
and the stability of the
\mathbb{P}_{r}-\mathbb{P}_{0}
pair to “glue” the local
results together. The local stability result has been established
in Lemma
4.4
, so we now focus
on the inf-sup stability of a surface analogue of the
\mathbb{P}_{r}-\mathbb{P}_{0}
pair.

We define

(4.8)

\begin{split}\widetilde{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}&=\{\bm{w}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}):\ \bm{w}|_{{T^{\rm m}}}=\mathcal{P}_{F_{T^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{w}}\ \exists\hat{\bm{w}}\in[\mathbb{P}_{r}(\hat{T})]^{2};\\
&\qquad\bm{w}|_{T^{\rm m}}(a)=\mathcal{M}^{{T^{\rm m}}}_{a}(\bm{w}|_{T^{\rm m}_{a}}(a))\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{a},\ \forall a\in\mathcal{N}^{\rm m}_{h}\},\\
\widetilde{Q}^{\rm m}_{h}&=\{r\in L^{2}_{0}(\Gamma_{h}):\ r|_{{T^{\rm m}}}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}({T^{\rm m}})\ \forall{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{h}\}.\end{split}

Remark 4.6
.

Because
[\mathbb{P}_{r}(\hat{T})]^{2}\subset\widehat{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}
, we easily see from (
4.7
) that the inclusion

\widetilde{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}\subset{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
holds.

Continuing, we state an approximation property of
\widetilde{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
.
The proof of the next lemma can be found in

[
10
, Lemma 3.9]
.

Lemma 4.7
.

For each
\bm{z}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{T}(\gamma)
,
there exists
{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}\in\widetilde{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
such that

\displaystyle\|\breve{\bm{z}}-{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}\|_{H^{\ell}({T^{\rm m}})}\lesssim h^{1-\ell}\|\bm{z}\|_{H^{1}(\bm{p}(\omega^{\prime}_{T^{\rm m}}))}\ (\ell=0,1)\qquad\forall{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{h},

where
\breve{\bm{z}}=\mathcal{P}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}\bm{z}
and
\omega^{\prime}_{{T^{\rm m}}}=\mathop{\cup_{S\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{h}}}_{{\rm cl}(S)\cap{\rm cl}({T^{\rm m}})\neq\emptyset}\mathop{\cup_{S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{h}}}_{{\rm cl}(S^{\prime})\cap{\rm cl}(S)\neq\emptyset}S^{\prime}

is the two-layered local patch around
{T^{\rm m}}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

There exists
\tilde{\beta}^{\rm m}>0
independent of
h
such that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\tilde{\beta}^{\rm m}\|r\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}\leq\sup_{\bm{w}\in\widetilde{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\bm{w})r}{\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}}\qquad\forall r\in\widetilde{Q}^{\rm m}_{h}.

Proof.

Fix
r\in\widetilde{Q}^{\rm m}_{h}
, and let
\bm{z}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{T}(\gamma)

satisfy
{\rm div}_{\gamma}\bm{z}=(\mu_{h}^{-1}r)^{\ell}

and
\|\bm{z}\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}\lesssim\|(\mu_{h}^{-1}r)^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\lesssim\|r\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}
.
Let
{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}\in\widetilde{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
be the approximation
of
\breve{\bm{z}}=\mathcal{P}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}\bm{z}
defined in Lemma
4.7
.

By the definition of
\widetilde{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
(
4.8
),
any
\bm{w}\in\widetilde{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
is uniquely
determined by the values
\bm{w}_{T^{\rm m}_{a}}(a)

for all
a\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\rm m}
, where we recall

\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\rm m}
is the set of
{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}
-mapped

r
th-degree Lagrange nodes with respect to
\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.
For each
e\in\mathcal{E}^{\rm m}_{h}
, we let
\{b_{e,j}\}_{j=1}^{r-1}\subset\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\rm m}
denote
the
r
th-degree Lagrange degrees of freedom
that lie in the interior of
e
. Likewise, for each
{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm m}
,
we set
\{c_{{T^{\rm m}},j}\}_{j=1}^{M_{r}}\subset\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\rm m}
to denote the
r
th degree Lagrange degrees of freedom that lie in the interior of
{T^{\rm m}}
,
where
M_{r}=\frac{1}{2}(r-1)(r-2)
. The Lagrange degrees of freedom
for
\widetilde{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
then has the decomposition

\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\rm m}=\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\rm m}\cup\{b_{e,j}:\ e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\rm m},\ 1\leq j\leq r-1\}\cup\{c_{{T^{\rm m}},j}:\ {T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm m},\ 1\leq j\leq M_{r}\}.

Next, let
\{\hat{\omega}_{j}\}_{j=0}^{r}
be the weights
of the (closed)
(r+1)
-point Gauss-Lobatto integration rule on
the interval
[0,1]
, where
\omega_{0}
is the weight
for the node
0
, and
\omega_{r}
is the weight
for the node
1
. We then set

(4.10)

\alpha:=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\omega_{j}\right)^{-1}=\mathcal{O}(1),

which is well-defined because all of the Gauss-Lobatto weights
are positive.

We uniquely determine
\bm{w}\in\widetilde{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
such that

(4.11a)

\displaystyle\bm{w}|_{T^{\rm m}_{a}}(a)

\displaystyle=({\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z})|_{T^{\rm m}_{a}}(a)\qquad

\displaystyle\forall a\in\mathcal{V}^{\rm m}_{h},

(4.11b)

\displaystyle\bm{w}|_{T^{\rm m}_{b_{e,j}}}(b_{e,j})

\displaystyle=({\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z})|_{T^{\rm m}_{b_{e,j}}}(b_{e,j})

\displaystyle\qquad+\alpha|\bar{e}|^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}|_{\bar{T}_{b_{e,j}}}(\bar{b}_{e,j})}\int_{\bar{e}}\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}}\left(\breve{\bm{z}}-{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}}\right)|_{\bar{T}_{b_{e,j}}}\quad

\displaystyle\forall e\in\mathcal{E}^{\rm m}_{h},\ j=1,\ldots,r-1,

(4.11c)

\displaystyle\bm{w}(c_{{T^{\rm m}},j})

\displaystyle=({\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z})(c_{{T^{\rm m}},j})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm m},\ j=1,\ldots,M_{r}.

Here,
\bar{T}_{b_{e,j}}={\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}(T^{\rm m}_{b_{e,j}})

\bar{e}={\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}(e)
, and
\bar{b}_{e,j}={\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}(b_{e,j})
.
Note that the right-hand sides of (
4.11
) are tangent
to
\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}
at their respective points,
and therefore
\bm{w}
is well-defined. Furthermore, because
{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}\in\widetilde{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
, the
first assignment (
4.11a
) implies, by the definition of
\widetilde{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
,

\displaystyle\bm{w}|_{{T^{\rm m}}}(a)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{a}^{T^{\rm m}}(\bm{w}|_{T^{\rm m}_{a}}(a))=\mathcal{M}_{a}^{T^{\rm m}}(({\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z})|_{T^{\rm m}_{a}}(a))=({\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z})|_{T^{\rm m}}(a)\qquad\forall{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{a},\ \forall a\in\mathcal{V}^{\rm m}_{h}.

Set
\bar{\bm{w}}=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}}\bm{w}
,

\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}}=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}}({\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z})
, and
\bar{\breve{\bm{z}}}=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}}\breve{\bm{z}}

so that

(4.12a)

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{w}}|_{\bar{T}}(\bar{a})

\displaystyle=(\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}})|_{\bar{T}}(\bar{a})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bar{T}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{\bar{a}},\ \forall\bar{a}\in\bar{\mathcal{V}}_{h},

(4.12b)

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{w}}|_{\bar{T}_{b_{e,j}}}(\bar{b}_{e,j})

\displaystyle=(\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}})|_{\bar{T}_{b_{e,j}}}(\bar{b}_{e,j})

\displaystyle\qquad+\alpha|\bar{e}|^{-1}\int_{\bar{e}}\left(\bar{\breve{\bm{z}}}-\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}}\right)|_{\bar{T}_{b_{e,j}}}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bar{e}\in\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{h},\ j=1,\ldots,r-1,

(4.12c)

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{w}}(\bar{c}_{{T^{\rm m}},j})

\displaystyle=(\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}})(\bar{c}_{{T^{\rm m}},j})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bar{T}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h},\ j=1,\ldots,M_{r}.

Because
\bm{w},{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z},\breve{\bm{z}}\in\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}};\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})
,
there holds
\bar{\bm{w}},\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}},\overline{\breve{\bm{z}}}\in\bm{H}({\rm div}_{\bar{\Gamma}_{h}};\bar{\Gamma}_{h})
by properties of the Piola transform.
In particular, these functions have co-normal continuity across edges,
and so for any co-normal
\bar{\bm{n}}_{e}
at
\bar{e}\in\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
, there holds

(4.13)

\displaystyle(\bar{\bm{w}}|_{\bar{T}}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{e})(\bar{b}_{e,j})

\displaystyle=(\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}}|_{\bar{T}}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{e})(\bar{b}_{e,j})+\alpha|\bar{e}|^{-1}\int_{\bar{e}}\left(\bar{\breve{\bm{z}}}-\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}}\right)|_{\bar{T}}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{e}\quad\forall\bar{e}\in\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{h},\ j=1,\ldots,r-1,

for any
\bar{T}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
with
\bar{e}\subset{\partial}T
.
Consequently, because
\bar{\bm{w}}_{\bar{T}}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{e}

and
(\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}})_{\bar{T}}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{e}

are polynomials of degree
\leq r
on
\bar{e}
,
we have by the Gauss-Lobatto integration rule, (
4.12a
), (
4.13
), and (
4.10
),

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{e}}\bar{\bm{w}}|_{\bar{T}}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{e}=\int_{\bar{e}}(\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}})|_{\bar{T}}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{e}+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\alpha\omega_{j}\int_{\bar{e}}\left(\bar{\breve{\bm{z}}}-\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}}\right)|_{\bar{T}}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{e}=\int_{\bar{e}}\bar{\breve{\bm{z}}}|_{\bar{T}}\cdot\bar{\bm{n}}_{e}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{{\partial}\bar{T}}\bar{\bm{w}}\cdot\bm{n}_{{\partial}\bar{T}}=\int_{{\partial}\bar{T}}\bar{\breve{\bm{z}}}\cdot\bm{n}_{{\partial}\bar{T}}\qquad\forall\bar{T}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h},

where
\bm{n}_{{\partial}\bar{T}}
is the outward unit co-normal of
\bar{T}
.
Using properties of the Piola transform once again, along with the divergence theorem, we find (with
{T^{\rm m}}={\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}(\bar{T})
)

\displaystyle\int_{{T^{\rm m}}}{\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\bm{w}=\int_{{\partial}{T^{\rm m}}}\bm{w}\cdot\bm{n}_{{\partial}{T^{\rm m}}}=\int_{{\partial}\bar{T}}\bar{\bm{w}}\cdot\bm{n}_{{\partial}\bar{T}}=\int_{{\partial}\bar{T}}\bar{\breve{\bm{z}}}\cdot\bm{n}_{{\partial}\bar{T}}=\int_{{\partial}{T^{\rm m}}}\breve{\bm{z}}\cdot\bm{n}_{{\partial}{T^{\rm m}}}=\int_{T^{\rm m}}{\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\breve{\bm{z}}.

Using that
r
is piecewise constant, we conclude

(4.14)

\begin{split}\int_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\bm{w})r&=\sum_{{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{h}}r|_{{T^{\rm m}}}\int_{{T^{\rm m}}}{\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\bm{w}\\
&=\sum_{{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{h}}r|_{{T^{\rm m}}}\int_{{T^{\rm m}}}{\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\breve{\bm{z}}\\
&=\int_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\breve{\bm{z}})r=\int_{\gamma}({\rm div}_{\gamma}\bm{z})r^{\ell}=\int_{\gamma}\mu_{h}^{-1}|r^{\ell}|^{2}=\|r\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}^{2}.\end{split}

Next, we make a change of variables and
a standard scaling argument to obtain

(4.15)

\displaystyle\|\bm{w}-{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}\|_{H^{1}({T^{\rm m}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\bar{\bm{w}}-\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}}\|_{H^{1}(\bar{T})}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\bar{e}\subset{\partial}\bar{T}}\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}|(\bar{\bm{w}}-\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}})_{\bar{T}}(\bar{b}_{e,j})|^{2},

where we used that
\bar{\bm{w}}=\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}}

at vertices and interior DOFs of the affine mesh (cf. (
4.12
)).

Let
e
be a fixed (curved) edge
of
{T^{\rm m}}
, and let
\bar{e}={\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}(e)
be the corresponding
affine edge. Using the definition of the finite element space

\widetilde{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle(\bar{\bm{w}}-\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}})|_{\bar{T}}(\bar{b}_{e,j})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}(\bar{b}_{{e,j}})}\left((\bm{w}-{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z})|_{{T^{\rm m}}}(b_{e,j})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}(\bar{b}_{{e,j}})}\mathcal{M}_{b_{e,j}}^{{T^{\rm m}}}\left(\bm{w}-({\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z})|_{T^{\rm m}_{b_{e,j}}}(b_{e,j})\right),

and therefore,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\left|(\bar{\bm{w}}-\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}})|_{\bar{T}}(\bar{b}_{e,j})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}(\bar{b}_{e,j})}\|\cdot\|\mathcal{M}_{b_{e,j}}^{{T^{\rm m}}}\|\cdot|(\bm{w}-{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z})|_{T^{\rm m}_{b_{e,j}}}(b_{e,j})|.

From
[
10
, eqn. (2.6)]
, we have that
\|\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}(\bar{b}_{{e,j}})}\|\lesssim 1
, and we clearly have
\|\mathcal{M}_{b_{e,j}}^{T}\|\lesssim 1
. Inserting
these estimates into (
4.16
) and using (
4.12
)
yields

\displaystyle\left|(\bar{\bm{w}}-\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}})|_{\bar{T}}(\bar{b}_{e,j})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|(\bm{w}-{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z})|_{T^{\rm m}_{b_{e,j}}}(b_{e,j})|=\alpha|\bar{e}|^{-1}\left|\int_{\bar{e}}(\bar{\breve{\bm{z}}}-\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}})|_{\overline{T}_{b_{e,j}}}\right|\lesssim h_{T}^{-1/2}\|(\overline{\breve{\bm{z}}}-\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}})|_{\overline{T}_{b_{e,j}}}\|_{L^{2}(\bar{e})}.

We then apply standard trace estimates
and Lemma
4.7
to obtain

\displaystyle\left|(\bar{\bm{w}}-\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}})|_{\bar{T}}(\bar{b}_{e,j})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{T}^{-1}\big\|\bar{\breve{\bm{z}}}-\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\bar{T}_{b_{e,j}})}+\big\|\bar{\breve{\bm{z}}}-\overline{{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}}\big\|_{H^{1}(\bar{T}_{b_{e,j}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{T}^{-1}\big\|\breve{\bm{z}}-{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}\big\|_{L^{2}(T^{\rm m}_{b_{e,j}})}+\big\|\breve{\bm{z}}-{\bm{I}}_{h,1}\bm{z}\big\|_{H^{1}(T^{\rm m}_{b_{e,j}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\bm{z}\|_{H^{1}(\bm{p}(\omega_{T_{b_{e,j}}^{\rm m}}^{\prime})}\lesssim\|\bm{z}\|_{H^{1}(\bm{p}(\omega_{T^{\rm m}}^{\prime\prime}))},

where
\omega_{T^{\rm m}}^{\prime\prime}
is a three-layered local patch
around
{T^{\rm m}}
.

Applying this estimate to (
4.15
)
and once again using Lemma
4.7
, we get

\displaystyle\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}({T^{\rm m}})}\lesssim\|\bm{z}\|_{H^{1}(\bm{p}(\omega_{T^{\rm m}}^{\prime\prime})}.

Summing over
T\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm m}_{h}
, we then conclude
\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}\lesssim\|\bm{z}\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}\lesssim\|r\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}
,
which combined with (
4.14
) yields
the desired inf-sup condition (
4.9
).
∎

The local stability result
given in Lemma
4.4
, combined with the stability
of the
\mathbb{P}_{r}-\mathbb{P}_{0}
pair, shows
that the auxiliary pair
{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}\times Q^{\rm m}_{h}

is inf-sup stable.

Theorem 4.9
.

There exists
\beta_{\rm m}>0
independent
of the discretization parameter
h
such that

(4.17)

\displaystyle\beta_{\rm m}\|r\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}\leq\sup_{\bm{w}\in{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\bm{w})r}{\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}}\qquad\forall r\in Q^{\rm m}_{h}.

Proof.

The result essentially follows directly from Lemmas
4.4
and
4.8
, together with the arguments given in
[
25
, Theorem 4.4]
,
[
12
, Theorem 4.9]
, and
[
17
, Proposition 6.1]
. For completeness, we provide the proof.

Let
r\in Q^{\rm m}_{h}
, and let
\tilde{r}_{0}
be the piecewise constant function with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm m}
that satisfies

\int_{\hat{T}}(r-\tilde{r}_{0})\circ F_{{T^{\rm m}}}=\int_{{T^{\rm m}}}J_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{-1}(r-\tilde{r}_{0})=0\qquad\forall{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm m},

where
J_{{T^{\rm m}}}=\sqrt{\det((\nabla F_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{\intercal}\nabla F_{{T^{\rm m}}})\circ F_{{T^{\rm m}}}^{-1})}
.
Then
(r-\tilde{r}_{0})|_{{T^{\rm m}}}\in Q_{0}^{\rm m}({T^{\rm m}})
for each
{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm m}
,
and so, by Lemma
4.4
, there exists
\bm{w}_{0,{T^{\rm m}}}\in\bm{V}_{0}^{\rm m}({T^{\rm m}})
such
that

.
We then set
\bm{w}_{0}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\rm m}
such that

for all

\|\bm{w}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}\lesssim\|r-\tilde{r}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{w}\in{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\bm{w})r}{\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\int_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\bm{w}_{0})(r-\tilde{r}_{0})}{\|\bm{w}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}}=\frac{\sum_{{T^{\rm m}}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm m}}h_{T}^{2}\int_{{T^{\rm m}}}|r-\tilde{r}_{0}|^{2}/J_{T}}{\|\bm{w}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}}\geq\beta_{{\rm m},0}\|r-\tilde{r}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}

Next, we set

\displaystyle\beta_{{\rm m},1}\|r_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\bm{w}\in{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\bm{w})r}{\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}}+\|r-r_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\bm{w}\in{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\bm{w})r}{\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}}+(1+h^{2})\|r-\tilde{r}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\beta_{{\rm m},0}^{-1})\sup_{\bm{w}\in{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\bm{w})r}{\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}}.

\displaystyle\|r\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq\|r-\tilde{r}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}+\|\tilde{r}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq\|r-\tilde{r}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}+\|r_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}+\|\gamma\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|r-\tilde{r}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}+\|r_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\beta_{{\rm m},0}^{-1}+\beta_{{\rm m},1}^{-1}(1+\beta_{{\rm m},0}^{-1})\right)\sup_{\bm{w}\in{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}({\rm div}_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\bm{w})r}{\|\bm{w}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h})}}.

\displaystyle=\big[a(\bm{u},\wideparen{\bm{\varphi}}_{h})-\int_{\gamma}{\bm{f}}\cdot\wideparen{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}\big]+\int_{\gamma}({\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell})\cdot\wideparen{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}-G_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\varphi}_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\rm ct}}\int_{e^{\gamma}}{\rm Def}_{\gamma}\bm{u}:\left[\hskip-1.8063pt\left[\bm{\varphi}_{h}\right]\hskip-1.8063pt\right]+\int_{\gamma}({\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell})\cdot\wideparen{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(G(\breve{\bm{u}},\breve{\bm{\varphi}})+G(\breve{\bm{u}},\bm{\varphi}_{h}-\breve{\bm{\varphi}})+G(\bm{u}_{h}-\breve{\bm{u}},\bm{\varphi}_{h})\right)

where we used that
\wideparen{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}
is divergence free in the last equality.
Thus, applying (
5.2
) (with
m=2
) and the geometric consistency results (
5.3
)–(
5.4
) we have

(5.10)

\begin{split}a(\bm{u}-\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h},\bm{\varphi}_{h})&\lesssim\left((h^{s}+h^{2k-1})\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{s}(\gamma)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}\right)\|\wideparen{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}\|_{H^{2}_{h}(\gamma)}\\
&\hskip-14.22636pt+h^{k+1}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{2}(\gamma)}\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{H^{2}(\gamma)}+h^{k}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}\|\bm{\varphi}-\wideparen{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\gamma)}+h^{k}\|\bm{u}-\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\gamma)}\|\wideparen{\bm{\varphi}}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\gamma)}\\
&\lesssim\left(\big(h^{s}+h^{k+1}+h^{2k-1}\big)\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{s}(\gamma)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}+h^{k}\|\bm{u}-\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\gamma)}\right)\|\bm{u}-\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}.\end{split}

Next, by the exact same arguments as in
[
10
, (5.23)]
,
and by applying (
5.2
) we obtain

\displaystyle a(\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h},\bm{\varphi})-a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\varphi}_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\rm ct}}\int_{e^{\gamma}}{\rm Def}_{\gamma}\bm{\varphi}:\left[\hskip-1.8063pt\left[\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h}\right]\hskip-1.8063pt\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim(h^{s}\|\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{H^{s-1}_{h}(\gamma)}+h^{2k-1}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)})\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{H^{2}(\gamma)}.

Standard arguments show
\|\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{H^{s-1}_{h}(\gamma)}\lesssim\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{s-1}(\gamma)}+h^{2-s}\|\bm{u}-\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\gamma)}
,
and therefore

(5.11)

\displaystyle a(\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h},\bm{\varphi})-a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{\varphi}_{h})

\displaystyle\lesssim(h^{s}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{s-1}(\gamma)}+h^{2}\|\bm{u}-\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\gamma)}+h^{2k-1}\|{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}})\|\bm{u}-\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}.

Applying (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) to (
5.9
) gets

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}-\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h\|\bm{u}-\wideparen{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\gamma)}+(h^{s}+h^{k+1}+h^{2k-1})\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{s}(\gamma)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}+h^{2k-1}\|{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h\left((h^{s-1}+h^{2k-1})\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{s}(\gamma)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}+h^{k}\|{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+(h^{s}+h^{k+1}+h^{2k-1})\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}+h^{2k-1}\|{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(h^{s}+h^{k+1}+h^{2k-1})\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{s}(\gamma)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}+(h^{k+1}+h^{2k-1})\|{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}

∎

Remark 5.6
.

[Pressure Robustness]
A well-known property of divergence-free methods
for the Euclidean Stokes problem is their pressure-robustness, i.e.,
the discrete velocity solution is independent of the pressure variable
[
21
]
.
However, for the surface Stokes problem, pressure-robustness is not guaranteed
due to geometric and source approximation as we now explain.

Assuming the solution
(\bm{u},p)
is sufficiently
smooth, and assuming
{\bm{f}}_{h}={\bm{P}}_{h}{\bm{f}}^{e}
for some linear operator
{\bm{P}}_{h}
, have for all
\bm{v}\in{\bm{X}}_{h}
,

(5.12)

\begin{split}\int_{\gamma}({\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell})\cdot\wideparen{\bm{v}}&=\int_{\gamma}(-(\bm{\Pi}{\rm div}_{\gamma}{\rm Def}_{\gamma}\bm{u}+\bm{u})\cdot\wideparen{\bm{v}}-\int_{\Gamma_{h}}{\bm{f}}_{h}\cdot\bm{v}\\
&=\int_{\gamma}(-(\bm{\Pi}{\rm div}_{\gamma}{\rm Def}_{\gamma}\bm{u}+\bm{u})\cdot\wideparen{\bm{v}}+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}{\bm{P}}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}{\rm div}_{\gamma}{\rm Def}_{\gamma}\bm{u}+\bm{u})^{e}\cdot\bm{v}+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}{\bm{P}}_{h}(\nabla_{\gamma}p)^{e}\cdot\bm{v},\end{split}

where we used the fact
\wideparen{\bm{v}}
is divergence-free (and so, orthogonal to gradients).
Consequently, due to the term
\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}
in the error estimate
(
5.6
) we see that pressure robustness of method (
5.1
) is guaranteed if
the operator
{\bm{P}}_{h}
is chosen such that

\int_{\Gamma_{h}}{\bm{P}}_{h}(\nabla_{\gamma}p)^{e}\cdot\bm{v}=0\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in{\bm{X}}_{h}.

This property holds if, e.g.,
{\bm{P}}_{h}
maps surface gradients (with respect to
\gamma
) to
surface gradients (with respect to
\Gamma_{h}
).

6.
Numerical Experiments

This section contains three numerical experiments: approximations of a divergence-free function and a no-flow function on the unit sphere, as well as a divergence-free function on a torus. For these examples we take the isoparametric case with
k=r
. We use a MATLAB code built on top of the iFEM library
[
5
]
as in
[
9
]
with modifications to employ the Scott-Vogelius pair, notably changing DOFs to make a discontinuous pressure space on the Clough-Tocher split. We run tests with Scott-Vogelius quadratic (
\mathbb{P}_{2}-\mathbb{P}_{1}
), cubic (
\mathbb{P}_{3}-\mathbb{P}_{2})
, and quartic (
\mathbb{P}_{4}-\mathbb{P}_{3}
) elements, with degrees of freedom at standard Lagrange nodes with Gauss-Lobatto nodes as edge degrees of freedom. Note that this is an implementation of
\bm{V}_{h}
and
Q_{h}
, not the macroelement spaces
{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
and
Q_{h}^{\rm m}
.

To compute the error, ideally one can map the true solution
\bm{u}
to the discrete surface
\Gamma_{h}
using a Piola transform with respect to the inverse of the closest point projection. However, an explicit formula for the closest point projection is not available in general.
Assuming
\gamma
is given as the level set of a smooth function
{\bm{\Phi}}
, i.e.,

\gamma=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}:{\bm{\Phi}}(x)=0\}
, we make the following
approximations (cf.
[
8
]
):

\tilde{{\bm{\nu}}}(x)=\frac{\nabla{\bm{\Phi}}(x)}{|\nabla{\bm{\Phi}}(x)|},\quad\tilde{d}(x)=\frac{{\bm{\Phi}}(x)}{|\nabla{\bm{\Phi}}(x)|},\quad\tilde{\bm{p}}(x)=x-\tilde{d}(x)\tilde{{\bm{\nu}}}(x).

Then there holds

|d(x)-\tilde{d}(x)|=\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1}),

and

|{\bm{\nu}}(x)-\tilde{{\bm{\nu}}}(x)|=\left|\frac{\nabla{\bm{\Phi}}(\bm{p}(x))}{|\nabla{\bm{\Phi}}(\bm{p}(x))|}-\frac{\nabla{\bm{\Phi}}(x)}{|\nabla{\bm{\Phi}}(x)}\right|\lesssim|\bm{p}(x)-x|=\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1}).

Note that
\tilde{\bm{p}}
may not lie on the true surface, but
we have
|\bm{p}(x)-\tilde{\bm{p}}(x)|=\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1})
.
A similar calculation shows

(6.1)

|{\bm{\nu}}(x)-\tilde{{\bm{\nu}}}(\tilde{\bm{p}}(x))|=\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1}).

In the numerical experiments
below the expression for the velocity
\bm{u}:\gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}

is well defined in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
and we denote this expression by
\bm{u}^{ce}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
With the approximations above,
we substitute the true Piola transform
\mathcal{P}_{p^{-1}}\bm{u}
with the computable approximation

\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}\bm{u}:=\left({\bf I}_{3}-\frac{(\tilde{{\bm{\nu}}}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}})\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{h}}{(\tilde{{\bm{\nu}}}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}})\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{h}}\right)(\bm{u}^{ce}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}}),

and note that
|\mathcal{P}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}\bm{u}-\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}\bm{u}|=\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1}).

In the set of numerical experiments, we set

\bm{f}_{h}(x)=\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}\bm{f}(x)

, that is, quadrature points on the discrete surface
\Gamma_{h}
are
mapped to an approximation to
\gamma
with
\tilde{\bm{p}}
,
\bm{f}^{ce}
is evaluated, and the result is mapped back to
\Gamma_{h}
with an approximate Piola transform.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}\bm{u}

and
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}{\bm{f}}
be the approximate Piola transforms
of
\bm{u}
and
{\bm{f}}
, respectively. Then in the isoparametric case
k=r
,
there holds for
(\bm{u},p)\in\bm{H}^{r+1}(\gamma)\times H^{r}(\gamma)
,

(6.2)

\begin{split}\|\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h})}&\lesssim h^{r}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{r+1}(\gamma)}+h^{r+1}\|p\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)},\\
\|p\circ\tilde{\bm{p}}-p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}&\lesssim h^{r}(\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{r+1}(\gamma)}+\|p\|_{H^{r}(\gamma)}),\\
\|\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}&\lesssim h^{r+1}(\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{r+1}(\gamma)}+\|p\|_{H^{r}(\gamma)}).\end{split}

Proof.

By a change of variables and Theorems
5.3
and
5.5
,
it suffices to estimate
\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}
,
\|{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}
,
and
\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}
.

Using
d=\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1})
and
|1-{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}|=\mathcal{O}(h^{2k})=\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1})
,

|1-(\tilde{\bm{\nu}}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}})\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{h}|=\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1})
, and

|1-(\tilde{\bm{\nu}}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}})\cdot{\bm{\nu}}|=\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1})

we have (cf. (
5.5
))

\displaystyle{\bm{F}}_{h}

\displaystyle=\big[{\bf I}_{3}-{{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}}\big]{\bm{f}}_{h}+\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1}\|{\bm{f}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})})

\displaystyle=\big[{\bf I}_{3}-{{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}}\big]\big[{\bf I}_{3}-{(\tilde{\bm{\nu}}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}})\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{h}}\big]({\bm{f}}^{ce}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}})+\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1}\|{\bm{f}}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)})

\displaystyle=\big[{\bf I}_{3}-{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}-(\tilde{\bm{\nu}}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}})\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{h}+{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\big]({\bm{f}}^{ce}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}})+\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1}\|{\bm{f}}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)})

\displaystyle=\big[{\bf I}_{3}-{\bm{\nu}}_{h}\otimes({\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{\nu}}_{h})-(\tilde{\bm{\nu}}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}})\otimes({\bm{\nu}}_{h}-{\bm{\nu}})\big]({\bm{f}}^{ce}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}})+\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1}\|{\bm{f}}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}),

where we used
|{\bm{\nu}}\cdot{\bm{f}}^{ce}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}}|=|{\bm{\nu}}\cdot({\bm{f}}^{ce}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}}-{\bm{f}}\circ\bm{p})|=\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1})

in the last equality. It then follows from (
6.1
)
that
\|{\bm{f}}^{ce}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}\lesssim h^{k+1}\|{\bm{f}}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}
, and therefore

(6.3)

\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\lesssim h^{k+1}\|{\bm{f}}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\lesssim h^{k+1}(\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{2}(\gamma)}+\|p\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}).

We also find that, using a slight variant of (
5.12
),

\displaystyle\|{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\bm{f}}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}\lesssim\|{\bm{f}}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}+h^{k+1}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{2}(\gamma)}+\sup_{\bm{v}\in{\bm{X}}_{h}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma_{h}}(\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}\nabla_{\gamma}p)\cdot\bm{v}}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}_{h}(\Gamma_{h})}}

A simple calculation shows that, for divergence-free
\bm{v}\in{\bm{X}}_{h}
,
there holds

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma_{h}}(\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}\nabla_{\gamma}p)\cdot\bm{v}\right|=\left|\int_{\Gamma_{h}}(\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\bm{p}^{-1}}\nabla_{\gamma}p-\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}p^{e})\cdot\bm{v}\right|\lesssim h^{k+1}\|p\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}\|\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})},

and therefore

(6.4)

\displaystyle\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}+\|{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}\lesssim\|{\bm{f}}\|_{X_{h}^{*}}+h^{k+1}(\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{2}(\gamma)}+\|p\|_{H^{1}(\gamma)}).

Finally, we apply the estimates (
6.3
)–(
6.4
)
to Theorems
5.3
and
5.5

with
k=r
and use
r\geq 2
to obtain
(
6.2
).
∎

A.1.
Proof of Lemma
4.1

Proof.

Let
{\bm{I}}^{\rm ct}_{h}:\bm{C}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
denote
the
k
th degree nodal interpolant with respect to
\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\rm ct}
,
and note that, because
\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\rm ct}
is a refinement
of
\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, there holds
{\bm{I}}^{\rm m}_{h}{\bm{I}}^{\rm ct}_{h}={\bm{I}}^{\rm m}_{h}
.

We write
{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}={\bm{I}}^{\rm ct}_{h}\bm{p}+{\bm{r}}
,
where
{\bm{r}}
is a continuous, piecewise polynomial of degree
k

with respect to
\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\rm ct}
. It then follows from (
2.3
)
that
\|{\bm{r}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}\lesssim h^{k+1}
,
and so by inverse estimates,
\|{\bm{r}}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\bar{K})}\lesssim h^{k+1-m}

for all
\bar{K}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\rm ct}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

We then have for
m=0,1,

\displaystyle|{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}-\bm{p}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}

\displaystyle=|{\bm{I}}^{\rm m}_{h}{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}-\bm{p}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|{\bm{I}}^{\rm m}{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}-{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}+h^{k+1-m}

\displaystyle\lesssim|{\bm{I}}^{\rm m}_{h}({\bm{I}}^{\rm ct}_{h}\bm{p}+{\bm{r}})-({\bm{I}}^{\rm ct}_{h}\bm{p}+{\bm{r}})|_{W^{m,\infty}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}+h^{k+1-m}

\displaystyle\lesssim|{\bm{I}}^{\rm m}_{h}{\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}+h^{k+1-m}.

There holds
|{\bm{I}}^{\rm m}_{h}{\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}\lesssim|{\bm{r}}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}+h^{1-m}|{\bm{r}}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h})}\lesssim h^{k+1-m}
,
and the estimates (
4.1
) now follow.

Next, we write
F_{{T^{\rm m}}}=({\bm{I}}^{\rm m}_{h}\bm{p}+{\bm{I}}^{\rm m}_{h}{\bm{r}})\circ F_{\bar{T}}
,
so that

|F_{{T^{\rm m}}}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\hat{T})}\lesssim|{\bm{I}}^{\rm m}_{h}\bm{p}+{\bm{I}}^{\rm m}_{h}{\bm{r}}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\bar{T})}|F_{\bar{T}}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\hat{T})}^{m},

where we used the fact that
F_{\bar{T}}
is affine.
By the stability of the Lagrange interpolant and the smoothness of
\bm{p}
, we have

|{\bm{I}}^{\rm m}_{h}\bm{p}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\bar{T})}\lesssim 1
. We also have
by inverse estimates and and bounds of
{\bm{r}}
, for
m\geq 1
,
|{\bm{I}}^{\rm m}_{h}{\bm{r}}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\bar{T})}\lesssim h^{1-m}|{\bm{I}}^{\rm m}_{h}{\bm{r}}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\bar{T})}\lesssim h^{1-m}|{\bm{r}}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\bar{T})}\lesssim h^{-m}\|{\bm{r}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\bar{T})}\lesssim h^{k-m+1}.

Using this estimate as well as
|F_{\bar{T}}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\hat{T})}\lesssim h
, yields the first estimate in (
4.4
):

|F_{{T^{\rm m}}}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\hat{T})}\lesssim h^{m}_{T}
. The other two
estimates in the first line of (
4.4
) then follow from the first,
and the second row of estimates follow from the exact same arguments.
∎

Proof of Lemma

Proof.

Let
C_{K}=\nabla F_{K}^{\intercal}\nabla F_{K}:\hat{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}

and
C_{{K^{\rm m}}}=\nabla F_{{K^{\rm m}}}^{\intercal}\nabla F_{{K^{\rm m}}}:\hat{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
,
so that

and
J_{{K^{\rm m}}}=\sqrt{\det(C_{{K^{\rm m}}})}
.
We then use the algebraic inequality

\left|a^{-\frac{1}{2}}-b^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right|\leq\frac{|a-b|}{2(\min\{a,b\})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\qquad a,b>0,

the algebraic identity

(A.1)

\det(C_{K})-\det(C_{{K^{\rm m}}})=\frac{1}{2}{\rm cof}(C_{K}+C_{{K^{\rm m}}}):(C_{K}-C_{{K^{\rm m}}}),

and the linearity of
C\to{\rm cof}(C)
for
2\times 2
matrices
to get

\displaystyle|J_{K}^{-1}-J_{{K^{\rm m}}}^{-1}|

\displaystyle=|(\det(C_{K})^{-1/2}-\det(C_{{K^{\rm m}}})^{-1/2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(|C_{T}|+|C_{{K^{\rm m}}}|)|C_{T}-C_{{K^{\rm m}}}|}{(\min\{\det(C_{K}),\det(C_{{K^{\rm m}}})\})^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

We then use
\det(C_{K})\approx\det(C_{{K^{\rm m}}})\approx h^{4}_{K}
and
|C_{K}|,|C_{{K^{\rm m}}}|\lesssim h^{2}_{K}

(cf. (
4.4
))
to get

\displaystyle|J_{K}^{-1}-J_{{K^{\rm m}}}^{-1}|\lesssim h^{-4}_{K}|C_{K}-C_{{K^{\rm m}}}|.

We then write
C_{K}-C_{{K^{\rm m}}}=\nabla F_{K}^{\intercal}(\nabla F_{K}-\nabla F_{{K^{\rm m}}})+(\nabla F_{K}^{\intercal}-\nabla F_{{K^{\rm m}}}^{\intercal})\nabla F_{{K^{\rm m}}}

to conclude

(A.2)

\|C_{K}-C_{{K^{\rm m}}}\|_{W^{\ell,\infty}(\hat{T})}\lesssim h^{k+2}_{K}\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

by (
4.4
) and Lemma
4.2
; thus,

(A.3)

\displaystyle\|J_{K}^{-1}-J_{{K^{\rm m}}}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}\lesssim h_{K}^{k-2},

and so, by Lemma
4.2
once again,

\displaystyle\left\|A_{K}-A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\nabla F_{K}}{J_{K}}-\frac{\nabla F_{{K^{\rm m}}}}{J_{K^{\rm m}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{\nabla F_{K}-\nabla F_{K^{\rm m}}}{J_{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}+\left\|\frac{\nabla F_{{K^{\rm m}}}}{J_{K}}-\frac{\nabla F_{{K^{\rm m}}}}{J_{K^{\rm m}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{k-1}_{K}+\|\nabla F_{{K^{\rm m}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}\|J_{K}^{-1}-J_{K^{\rm m}}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}\lesssim h_{K}^{k-1}.

Next, we compute

(A.4)

\begin{split}|A_{K}-A_{{K^{\rm m}}}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\hat{T})}&\lesssim\left\|\frac{D^{2}F_{K}}{J_{K}}-\frac{D^{2}F_{{K^{\rm m}}}}{J_{K^{\rm m}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}+\left\|\frac{\nabla F_{K}\nabla J_{K}}{J_{K}^{2}}-\frac{\nabla F_{{K^{\rm m}}}\nabla J_{K^{\rm m}}}{J_{K^{\rm m}}^{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}\\

Applying Lemma
4.2
and the estimates
J_{K}\approx J_{{K^{\rm m}}}\approx h^{2}_{K}
,
we obtain

.

To bound

we add and subtract terms to get

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\frac{\nabla F_{K}\nabla(J_{K}-J_{{K^{\rm m}}})}{J_{K}^{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}+\left\|\left(\frac{\nabla F_{K}-\nabla F_{{K^{\rm m}}}}{J_{K}^{2}}\right)\nabla J_{{K^{\rm m}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}+\left\|\left(\frac{\nabla F_{{K^{\rm m}}}}{J_{K}^{2}}-\frac{\nabla F_{{K^{\rm m}}}}{J_{K^{\rm m}}^{2}}\right)\nabla J_{{K^{\rm m}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}

\displaystyle=:I_{2,1}+I_{2,2}.

The arguments in
[
10
, Appendix C]

\displaystyle J_{K}^{-2}-J_{{K^{\rm m}}}^{-2}

We then conclude

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
4.10

The proof of Lemma
4.10
requires a few preliminary
results. First, for an element
K\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm ct}_{h}
, we set

{K^{\rm m}}=\big({\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}^{-1}\big)(K)
.
Let
a\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\rm ct}
be a Lagrange DOF on
K

and
a^{\rm m}=\big({\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}^{-1}\big)(a)

be the corresponding DOF on
{K^{\rm m}}
. We then set

(B.1)

B_{a}:=\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}\big(\mathcal{M}_{a^{\rm m}}^{K^{\rm{m}}}\big)^{-1}-{\bf I}_{3}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},\quad\text{where}\quad\big(\mathcal{M}_{a^{\rm m}}^{K^{\rm{m}}}\big)^{-1}=\frac{1}{{\bm{\nu}}_{{K^{\rm m}}}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{K^{\rm m}_{a}}}\bm{\Pi}_{K^{\rm m}_{a}}(a^{\rm m}).

The next result shows that
B_{a}
is a perturbation of the normal projection onto
\Gamma_{h}
at
a
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
B_{a}
be defined by (
B.1
). There holds

\displaystyle\big|B_{a}+{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)\big|\lesssim h^{k}.

Proof.

From (
2.4
) and (
4.2
), for
{\bm{\nu}}_{1},{\bm{\nu}}_{2}\in\{{\bm{\nu}}_{T},{\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}},{\bm{\nu}}_{{K^{\rm m}}},{\bm{\nu}}_{K^{\rm m}_{a}}\},
we have the following estimates for
h
sufficiently small:

(B.2)

\displaystyle|{\bm{\nu}}_{1}-{\bm{\nu}}_{2}|\lesssim h^{k},\qquad|1-{\bm{\nu}}_{1}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{2}|\lesssim h^{2k},\qquad\frac{1}{{\bm{\nu}}_{1}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{2}}\lesssim 1.

Consequently, by (
3.1
) and (
B.1
) there holds

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}-\big({\bf I}_{3}-{\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}(a)\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)\big)\big|\lesssim h^{2k},\text{ and }\big|\big(\mathcal{M}_{a^{\rm m}}^{{K^{\rm m}}}\big)^{-1}-\bm{\Pi}_{K^{\rm m}_{a}}(a^{\rm m})\big|\lesssim h^{2k}.

Using the algebraic identity
ab-cd=\frac{1}{2}(a-c)(b+d)+\frac{1}{2}(a+c)(b-d)
and the fact that the matrices in these expressions are clearly uniformly bounded, we have

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}\big(\mathcal{M}_{a^{\rm m}}^{{K^{\rm m}}}\big)^{-1}-\big({\bf I}_{3}-{\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}(a)\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)\big)\bm{\Pi}_{K^{\rm m}_{a}}(a^{\rm m})\big|\lesssim h^{2k}.

Next, we write

\displaystyle\big({\bf I}_{3}-{\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}(a)\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)\big)\bm{\Pi}_{K^{\rm m}_{a}}(a^{\rm m})=\big({\bf I}_{3}-{\bm{\nu}}_{K_{a}}(a)\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)\big)\big(\bm{\Pi}_{K^{\rm m}_{a}}(a^{\rm m})-\bm{\Pi}_{K}(a)\big)+\bm{\Pi}_{K}(a),

and use (
B.2
) to conclude

\displaystyle\big|B_{a}+{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)\otimes{\bm{\nu}}_{K}(a)\big|=\big|\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}\big(\mathcal{M}_{a^{\rm m}}^{{K^{\rm m}}}\big)^{-1}-\bm{\Pi}_{K}(a)\big|\lesssim h^{k}.

∎

Lemma B.2
.

Fix
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct}
, and let
\hat{B}_{K}\in\big[\mathbb{P}_{r}(\hat{T})\big]^{3\times 3}

be defined such that
\hat{B}_{K}(\hat{a})=B_{a}
for
all
r
th-degree Lagrange DOFs
\hat{a}\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}
,
where

and
B_{a}
is defined by (
B.1
).
Then there holds for
k\geq 2
,

(B.3)

\displaystyle|A_{K}^{\dagger}\hat{B}_{K}|_{W^{\ell,\infty}(\hat{T})}\lesssim h_{K}^{2+\ell}\qquad\ell=0,1,

where

denotes the Penrose inverse of
A_{K}=\nabla F_{K}/J_{K}
.

Proof.

Note that by
[
10
, Lemma 2.6]
, we have

(B.4)

\displaystyle|A_{K}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\hat{T})}\lesssim h^{m-1}_{K},\qquad|A_{K}^{\dagger}|_{W^{m,p}(\hat{T})}\lesssim h^{m+1}_{K},

and similarly for
A_{{K^{\rm m}}}
.

{\bm{\ell}=0}
:
Let
\hat{I}_{h}(A_{K}^{\dagger}\hat{B}_{K})
be the
r
-degree interpolant of

A_{K}^{\dagger}\hat{B}_{K}
with respect to the Lagrange nodes
\hat{\mathcal{N}}
.
Then by the Bramble-Hilbert lemma, (
B.4
), and using the fact that
\hat{B}_{K}
is a
polynomial of degree
\leq r
, we have

(B.5)

\begin{split}\|A_{K}^{\dagger}\hat{B}_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}&\leq\|\hat{I}_{h}(A_{K}^{\dagger}\hat{B}_{K})\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}+\|A_{K}^{\dagger}\hat{B}_{K}-\hat{I}_{h}(A_{K}^{\dagger}\hat{B}_{K})\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}\\

It remains to bound

where we used (
B.4
) in the last step.

\hat{C}_{K}=({\bm{\nu}}_{K}\circ F_{K})\otimes({\bm{\nu}}_{K}\circ F_{K})

\hat{T}
.

We then add and subtract the
r

\hat{C}_{K}

(B.8)

\displaystyle\big|{\nabla}\big(\hat{B}_{K}+\hat{C}_{K}\big)(\hat{a})\big|\leq\big|{\nabla}\big(\hat{B}_{K}+\hat{I}_{h}\hat{C}_{\hat{K}}\big)(\hat{a})\big|+\big|{\nabla}\big(\hat{C}_{K}-\hat{I}_{h}\hat{C}_{K}\big)(\hat{a})\big|.

(B.9)

Proof of Lemma

Proof.

Let
{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\in{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
be arbitrary and define
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}
such that
\bm{v}_{K_{a}}(a)={\bm{v}}^{\rm m}_{K^{\rm m}_{a^{\rm m}}}(a^{\rm m})
at Lagrange nodes
a\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\rm ct}
, where
a^{\rm m}=({\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}^{-1})(a)
.

Define
\bar{\bm{v}},\bar{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}:\bar{\Gamma}_{h}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
via composition:

\bar{\bm{v}}=\bm{v}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct},\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}={\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}.

Let
\bar{K}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}^{\rm ct}_{h}
be arbitrary and set
K={\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}(\bar{K}),{K^{\rm m}}={\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}(\bar{K})
.
Note that as

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{K_{a}}(a)={\bm{v}}^{\rm m}_{K^{\rm m}_{a^{\rm m}}}(a^{\rm m}),\qquad\bm{v}_{K}(a)=\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}(v_{K_{a}}(a)),\qquad\text{and }\quad{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}_{{K^{\rm m}}}(a^{\rm m})=\mathcal{M}_{a^{\rm m}}^{{K^{\rm m}}}({\bm{v}}^{\rm m}_{K^{\rm m}_{a^{\rm m}}}(a^{\rm m})),

there holds

(B.12)

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{v}}_{\bar{K}}(\bar{a})=\mathcal{M}_{a}^{K}\big(\mathcal{M}_{a^{\rm m}}^{{K^{\rm m}}}\big)^{-1}\bar{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}_{\bar{K}}(\bar{a})=\bar{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}_{\bar{K}}(\bar{a})+\hat{B}_{K}(\hat{a})\bar{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}_{\bar{K}}(\bar{a})

with
\bar{a}={\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}^{-1}(a)={\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}(a^{\rm m})

and
B_{a}
is defined by (
B.1
).

By definition of
\bm{V}_{h}
and
{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
, we can write
\bm{v}|_{K}\circ F_{K}=A_{K}\hat{\bm{v}}
and
{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\circ F_{K^{\rm m}}=A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}
for some
\hat{\bm{v}},\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\in[\mathbb{P}_{r}(\hat{T})]^{2}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{v}}|_{\bar{K}}\circ F_{\bar{K}}=A_{K}\hat{\bm{v}},\qquad\bar{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|_{\bar{K}}\circ F_{\bar{K}}=A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m},

and so by (
B.12
),

(B.13)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{v}}(\hat{a})=A_{K}^{\dagger}(\hat{a})({\bf I}_{3}+\hat{B}_{K}(\hat{a}))A_{{K^{\rm m}}}(\hat{a})\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}(\hat{a}).

Part 1:
We first establish the following estimate:

(B.14)

\displaystyle\big|\hat{\bm{v}}-A_{K}^{\dagger}({\bf I}_{3}+\hat{B}_{K})A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|_{H^{\ell}(\hat{T})}^{2}\lesssim h_{K}^{4}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2},\qquad\ell=0,1.

Note that
{\bf I}_{3}+\hat{B}_{K}\in[\mathbb{P}_{r}(\hat{T})]^{3\times 3}
and
\|{\bf I}_{3}+\hat{B}_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}\lesssim 1
. Also, see that for
\hat{a}\in\hat{\mathcal{N}}
, by definition of

\bm{V}_{h}
,
{\bm{V}}^{\rm m}_{h}
and construction of
\bm{v}
there holds

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{v}}(\hat{a})=A_{K}^{\dagger}(\hat{a})({\bm{I}}_{3}+B(\hat{a}))A_{{K^{\rm m}}}(\hat{a})\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}(\hat{a}).

Applying the Bramble-Hilbert lemma, the product rule, a change of variables, an inverse estimate, and (
B.4
) we have

(B.15)

\begin{split}\big|\hat{\bm{v}}-A_{K}^{\dagger}({\bf I}_{3}+\hat{B}_{K})A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|_{H^{\ell}(\hat{T})}^{2}&\lesssim\big|A_{K}^{\dagger}({\bf I}_{3}+\hat{B}_{K})A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|_{H^{r+1}(\hat{T})}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{n=0}^{r+1}\big|A_{K}^{\dagger}({\bf I}_{3}+\hat{B}_{K})\big|_{W^{n,\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\big|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|_{H^{r+1-n}(\hat{T})}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{2}_{K}\sum_{n=0}^{r+1}h^{2n}_{K}\big|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|_{H^{r+1-n}(\hat{T})}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{2}_{K}\big|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|_{H^{r+1}(\hat{T})}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{r+1}h_{K}^{2r+2}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{r+1-n}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{2}_{K}\big|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|_{H^{r+1}(\hat{T})}^{2}+h^{4}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}.\end{split}

It remains to bound
\big|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|_{H^{r+1}(\hat{T})}^{2}
. Since
\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}
is a piecewise polynomial of degree
r
, we can use the product rule, a change of variables, (
B.4
), and an inverse estimate to find

(B.16)

\begin{split}\big|A_{{T^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|_{H^{r+1}(\hat{T})}&\leq\sum_{n=0}^{r+1}|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}|_{W^{r+1-n,\infty}(\hat{T})}|\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|_{H^{n}(\hat{T})}=\sum_{n=0}^{r}|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}|_{W^{r+1-n,\infty}(\hat{T})}|\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|_{H^{n}(\hat{T})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{n=0}^{r}h^{r-n}_{K}|\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|_{H^{n}(\hat{T})}=\sum_{n=0}^{r}h^{r-n}_{K}\big|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}^{\dagger}A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|_{H^{n}(\hat{T})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{n=0}^{r}h^{r-n}_{K}\sum_{j=0}^{n}|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}^{\dagger}|_{W^{n-j,\infty}(\hat{T})}\big|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|_{H^{j}(\hat{T})}\\
&\lesssim h^{r}_{K}\sum_{j=0}^{r}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{j}({K^{\rm m}})}\lesssim h^{r}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{r}({K^{\rm m}})}\lesssim h_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}.\end{split}

Combining (
B.15
)–(
B.16
) yields (
B.14
).

Part 2:
We will now derive the estimate for
\|\bar{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{L^{2}(\bar{K})}
.
By a change of variables, we have

(B.17)

\displaystyle\|\bar{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{L^{2}(\bar{K})}^{2}\approx h^{2}_{K}\int_{\hat{T}}\big|A_{K}\hat{\bm{v}}-A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|^{2}.

Then, using the triangle inequality, (
B.4
), and Lemma
4.3
yields

(B.18)

\begin{split}h^{2}_{K}\int_{\hat{T}}\big|A_{K}\hat{\bm{v}}-A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|^{2}&\lesssim h^{2}_{K}\int_{\hat{T}}\big|A_{K}(\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m})\big|^{2}+h^{2}_{K}\int_{\hat{T}}\big|(A_{{K^{\rm m}}}-A_{K})\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|^{2}\\
&\lesssim\int_{\hat{T}}|\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}+h^{2}_{K}\|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}-A_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\int_{\hat{T}}|\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}\\
&\lesssim\int_{\hat{T}}|\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}+h^{2k}_{K}\int_{\hat{T}}\big|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}^{\dagger}A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|^{2}\\
&\lesssim\int_{\hat{T}}|\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}+h^{2k+2}_{K}\int_{\hat{T}}\big|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|^{2}\lesssim\int_{\hat{T}}|\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}+h^{2k}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{L^{2}({K^{\rm m}})}.\end{split}

Define
\hat{B}_{K}
as in Lemma
B.2
. Then, using the triangle inequality and then Part 1, Lemma
B.2
, and Lemma
4.3
,

(B.19)

\begin{split}\int_{\hat{T}}|\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}&\lesssim\int_{\hat{T}}\big|\hat{\bm{v}}-A_{K}^{\dagger}({\bf I}_{3}+\hat{B}_{K})A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|^{2}+\int_{\hat{T}}\big|A_{K}^{\dagger}\hat{B}_{K}A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|^{2}+\int_{\hat{T}}\big|({\bf I}_{3}-A_{K}^{\dagger}A_{{K^{\rm m}}})\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{4}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}+\|A_{K}^{\dagger}B\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\int_{\hat{T}}\big|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\big|^{2}+\|A_{K}^{\dagger}(A_{K}-A_{{K^{\rm m}}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\int_{\hat{T}}|\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{4}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}+h^{2}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{L^{2}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}+h^{2k}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{L^{2}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}\lesssim h^{2}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}.\end{split}

Combining this estimate with (
B.17
)–(
B.19
) yields

\displaystyle\|\bar{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{L^{2}(\bar{K})}^{2}\lesssim h^{2}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}.

Part 3:
Next, we establish a similar result in the
H^{1}
semi-norm.
We use the chain rule and a change of variables
to conclude

\displaystyle|\overline{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|_{H^{1}(\bar{K})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\hat{T}}\big|{\nabla}(A_{K}\hat{\bm{v}})-{\nabla}(A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m})\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\hat{T}}\big|{\nabla}(A_{K}(\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}))\big|^{2}+\int_{\hat{T}}\big|{\nabla}((A_{K}-A_{{K^{\rm m}}})\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m})\big|^{2}=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

To bound
J_{1}
, we first use the product rule, (
B.19
),
and (
B.4
):

(B.20)

\begin{split}J_{1}&\lesssim\|{\nabla}A_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\int_{\hat{T}}|\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}+\|A_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\int_{\hat{T}}|{\nabla}(\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m})|^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{2}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}+h^{-2}_{K}\int_{\hat{T}}|{\nabla}(\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m})|^{2}.\end{split}

With a similar chain of inequalities as above, we have

\displaystyle h^{-2}_{K}\int_{\hat{T}}|{\nabla}(\hat{\bm{v}}-\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-2}_{K}\int_{\hat{T}}\big|{\nabla}(\hat{\bm{v}}-A_{K}^{\dagger}({\bf I}_{3}+\hat{B}_{K})A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m})\big|^{2}+h^{-2}_{K}\int_{\hat{T}}\big|{\nabla}(A_{K}^{\dagger}\hat{B}_{K}A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m})\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+h^{-2}_{K}\int_{\hat{T}}\big|{\nabla}(({\bf I}_{3}-A_{K}^{\dagger}A_{{K^{\rm m}}})\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m})\big|^{2}=:L_{1}+L_{2}+L_{3}.

The first term can be bounded from Part 1:

\displaystyle L_{1}\lesssim h^{2}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({T^{\rm m}})}^{2}.

Next, with the product rule, Lemma
B.2
, and a change of variables,

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-2}_{K}\|{\nabla}(A_{K}^{\dagger}\hat{B}_{K})\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\int_{\hat{T}}|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}+h^{-2}_{K}\|A_{K}^{\dagger}\hat{B}_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\int_{\hat{T}}|{\nabla}(A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{L^{2}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}+h^{2}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}.

To bound
L_{3}
, we first apply the product rule:

\displaystyle L_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-2}_{K}\|{\nabla}({\bf I}_{3}-A_{K}^{\dagger}A_{{K^{\rm m}}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\int_{\hat{T}}|\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}+h^{-2}_{K}\|{\bf I}_{3}-A_{K}^{\dagger}A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\int_{\hat{T}}|{\nabla}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}.

Note that with Lemmas
4.3
and
B.2
,

\displaystyle\|{\bf I}_{3}-A_{K}^{\dagger}A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\lesssim\|A_{K}^{\dagger}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{K})}^{2}\|A_{K}-A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\lesssim h^{2k}_{K},

and

\displaystyle\|{\nabla}({\bf I}_{3}-A_{K}^{\dagger}A_{{K^{\rm m}}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\nabla}A_{K}^{\dagger}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\|A_{K}-A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}+\|A_{K}^{\dagger}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\|{\nabla}(A_{K}-A_{{K^{\rm m}}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{K}^{2k}.

Also, with (
B.4
),

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{T}}|{\nabla}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}^{\dagger}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\int_{\hat{T}}|\nabla(A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m})|^{2}+\|{\nabla}A_{{K^{\rm m}}}^{\dagger}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\int_{\hat{T}}|A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}.

With these estimates, we have

\displaystyle L_{3}\lesssim h^{2k}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}.

Combining the estimates for
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
, and
L_{3}
, we obtain
J_{1}\lesssim h^{2}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}
.

For
J_{2}
, we can use the product rule and Lemma
4.3
to find

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\nabla}(A_{K}-A_{{K^{\rm m}}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\int_{\hat{T}}|\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}+\|A_{K}-A_{{K^{\rm m}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{T})}^{2}\int_{\hat{T}}|{\nabla}\hat{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}.

Combining the bounds of
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
yields

\displaystyle|\bar{\bm{v}}-\bar{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}|_{H^{1}(\bar{K})}^{2}\lesssim h^{2}_{K}\|{\bm{v}}^{\rm m}\|_{H^{1}({K^{\rm m}})}^{2}.

For the next claim, define
\tilde{q}:=q\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}
so that
q\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}|_{\overline{T}}=\tilde{q}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}|_{\overline{T}}
for all
\overline{T}\in\overline{\Gamma}_{h}
. To satisfy the mean zero constraint, let
\overline{c}:=\frac{1}{|\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}|}\int_{\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}}\tilde{q}
,
q^{\rm m}=\tilde{q}-\overline{c}
, and note
q^{\rm m}\in Q^{\rm m}
. Now, there holds with a change of variables and the mean zero property of
q
:

\displaystyle\|q\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}-q^{\rm m}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}\|_{L^{2}(\overline{\Gamma}_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\overline{c}\|_{L^{2}(\overline{\Gamma}_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\overline{\Gamma}_{h}|}{|\Gamma^{\rm m}_{h}|^{2}}\left|\sum_{K^{\rm m}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm m,ct}}\int_{K^{\rm m}}q\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}^{-1}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct}}\int_{K}q(J_{F_{K^{m}}\circ F_{K}^{-1}}-1)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct}}h_{K}^{1/2}\|q\|_{L^{2}(K)}\|(J_{F_{K^{m}}}\circ F_{K}^{-1})(J_{F_{K}^{-1}})-1\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\right|^{2},

where
J_{F_{K^{m}}\circ F_{K}^{-1}}
is the product of the nonzero singular values of
\nabla(F_{K^{m}}\circ F_{K}^{-1})
,
and we applied the chain rule in the last inequality.

Continuing, we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
to conclude

(B.21)

\begin{split}\|q\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}-q^{\rm m}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}\|_{L^{2}(\overline{\Gamma}_{h})}^{2}&\lesssim\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct}}h_{K}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct}}h_{K}^{-1}\|(J_{F_{K^{m}}}\circ F_{K}^{-1})(J_{F_{K}^{-1}})-1\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}^{2}\|q\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)\\
&\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm ct}}h_{K}^{-1}\|(J_{F_{K^{m}}}\circ F_{K}^{-1})(J_{F_{K}^{-1}})-1\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}^{2}\|q\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}.\end{split}

We then write

(B.22)

|(J_{F_{K^{m}}}\circ F_{K}^{-1})(J_{F_{K}^{-1}})-1|=|(J_{F_{K^{m}}}-J_{F_{K}})\circ F_{K^{-1}}|\cdot|J_{F_{K}^{-1}}|\lesssim h^{-2}_{K}|(J_{F_{K^{m}}}-J_{F_{K}})\circ F_{K^{-1}}|.

We then proceed as in the proof of Lemma
4.3
, but using the algebraic inequality

|\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b}|\leq\frac{|a-b|}{2\min\{\sqrt{a},\sqrt{b}\}}\qquad a,b>0.

Letting
C_{K}=\nabla F_{K}^{\intercal}\nabla F_{K}
and
C_{K^{m}}=\nabla F_{K^{m}}^{\intercal}\nabla F_{K^{m}}
, there holds with (
A.2
) and Lemma
4.1

\displaystyle|(J_{F_{K^{m}}}-J_{F_{K}})\circ F_{K^{-1}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\det C_{K}-\det C_{K^{m}}|}{\min\{J_{F_{K}},J_{F_{K^{m}}}\}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(|C_{K}|+|C_{K^{m}}|)|C_{K}-C_{K_{m}}|}{h^{2}_{K}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{K}^{k+2}.

Applying this estimate and (
B.22
) to (
B.21
) yields

\|q\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm ct}-q^{\rm m}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\rm m}\|_{L^{2}(\overline{\Gamma}_{h})}\lesssim h^{k-1/2}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq h\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})},

where we used
k\geq 2
in the last equality.
∎
