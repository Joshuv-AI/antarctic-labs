β2-microglobulin is superior to lactate dehydrogenase and bulky disease for risk stratification in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma patients with IPI 1–2
Authors: Hung Chang, Yu-Shin Hung, Ming-Chung Kuo, Tung-Liang Lin, Hsiao-Wen Kao, Hsuan-Jen Shih, Yi-Jiun Su, Yuen-Chin Ong, Ning-Chun Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The International Prognostic Index (IPI) is the classical assessment tool to evaluate prognosis of diffuse large B cell lymphoma (DLBCL). Although the IPI is simple, reproducible, well validated and widely utilized, it is not entirely accurate in some patients with DLBCL. Recently, it was reported that a subgroup of high-risk DLBCL patients with IPI score 1 or 2 can be identified by bulky disease and high lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels. Such classification of high-risk DLBCL has been used in new clinical trials. We analyzed out cohort database to validate such a sub-classification and further evaluated subdivision by serum β2-microglobulin levels. We retrieved the list of patients with new diagnosis of DLBCL between 2015 and 2019 from the Chang Gung Memorial Hospital in Linkou. Consecutive cases were enrolled without a pre-set sample size assessment. For each patient, the IPI was calculated according to published literature. Patients with an IPI score of 1 and 2 were included for this analysis. High-risk DLBCL was defined as bulky tumors (> 7 cm) or high LDH levels (> 1.3-fold upper limit). Serum β2-microglobulin levels were measured in the clinical laboratory. Analyses were done by comparing patients with or without high-risk features as well as patients with or without high β2-microglobulin levels (≥ 3000 µg/L). In 523 patients with DLBCL, 238 had an IPI score of 1–2, and 234 (115 male and 119 female) had sufficient data for high-risk DLBCL sub-classification. The median age was 64 years (range 26 to 90 years). Blood level of β2-microglobulin correlated with that of LDH (r2 = 0.03, p = 0.0136). There was no difference in β2-microglobulin levels between patients with and without bulky tumors (p = 0.3573). Using LDH > 1.3-fold upper limit as the cutoff, patients with high LDH had significantly higher levels of β2-microglobulin (p = 0.0096). Sixty-seven patients had high-risk features, defined by high LDH or bulky disease. These patients have significantly higher β2-microglobulin levels (p = 0.0173). However, in clinical outcome, high-risk and low-risk DLBCL do not differ significantly in progression-free survival (p = 0.8794) or overall survival (p = 0.4464). Using 3000 µg/L as a cutoff, patients with high β2-microglobulin levels had significantly shorter progression-free survival (p = 0.0183) than those with low levels. The difference in overall survival between high and low-level β2-microglobulin did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.2044). Our experience suggests the high-risk sub-classification based on LDH and bulky disease among patients with an IPI score of 1 or 2 does not correlate with clinical outcomes. In contrast, high levels of β2-microglobulin may help identify a sub-group of high-risk patients. This biomarker may be helpful in refining treatment plans or designing future clinical trials. Introduction

Prognosis of diffuse large B cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is affected by many factors, such as age, tumor stage, extranodal lesions, performance status, and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels. The international prognostic index (IPI), a scoring system consisting of the above 5 factors, has been used for several decades as the standard evaluation of DLBCL prognosis at the time of initial diagnosis [1]. Although the IPI is the standard tool of prognostication, modifications were done to refine or simplify the IPI, with variable success and utilities [2]. Among those clinical factors, the LDH level appears to be especially important. Bulky disease, although not included in the IPI, is also a potential prognostic factor. Not all patients with IPI low risk (score 1 and 2) have favorable outcomes. In some studies, inferior prognosis in IPI low risk can be identified by high LDH and bulky disease [3, 4]. The definition of high LDH (1.3-fold) appears somewhat arbitrary, especially when LDH is already included in the IPI. Therefore, the reclassification requires validation. On the other hand, β2-microglobulin can also be used for prognostication of DLBCL. In our previous study, β2-microglobulin can be used to identify a high-risk subgroup of DLBCL from the entire group of intermediate-risk patients [5]. In this study, we aimed to prove the concept of LDH or bulky disease as key markers of subdividing patients with an IPI score of 1–2 and explore the potential role of β2-microglobulin for the same purpose.

Patients and methods

This study is a retrospective study done by retrieving data from medical records. To conduct this study, a protocol was sent for institutional review board approval and the entire study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. After obtaining approval from Institutional Review Board of Chang Gung Memorial Hospital, we retrieved the list of patients receiving new diagnosis of DLBCL between 2015 and 2019 from the Chang Gung Memorial Hospital in Linkou. Consecutive cases were enrolled without a pre-set sample size assessment. For each patient, clinical data, treatment modalities and outcome were obtained from the medical records. The histological diagnoses of DLBCL were reviewed by expert pathologists in the tumor board conferences. Other B cell lymphomas such as high-grade B cell lymphoma, Burkitt’s lymphoma or lymphomas transformed from indolent lymphomas were excluded from this study. The tumor stages were confirmed by image reviews in lymphoma tumor board conferences. The IPI was calculated according to published literature [1]. Patients with an IPI score of 1 and 2 were included for analysis. High-risk DLBCL among this group was defined as bulky tumors or high LDH levels (> 1.3-fold upper limit), as in published literature [3]. Serum β2-microglobulin levels were measured in the clinical laboratory. The statistical test was performed using GraphPad Prism version 10.00 for Windows (GraphPad Software, La Jolla California USA). Analyses were done by comparing patients with or without high-risk features and patients with or without high β2-microglobulin levels (≥ 3000 µg/L). For correlation of serum LDH and β2-microglobulin levels, a linear regression analysis was used. For comparison of numerical variables between groups, the Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon sign-rank test, whichever applicable, was used. The progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) was calculated by the Kaplan-Meier’s analysis. A log-rank test was used to compare the difference. In all analyses, significant statistical difference was defined as p = 0.05.

Results

We found 523 patients with DLBCL in our cancer center database. Two hundred and thirty-eight patients had low-risk disease, defined by an IPI score of 1 or 2. Excluding patients who lost to follow-up or who had no β2-microglobulin data available, there were 234 patients (115 male and 119 female) for analysis of sub-classification. Their median age was 64 (range 26 to 90).

As defined in the literature, LDH > 1.3-fold upper limit was used as the cutoff for identifying high-risk patients. The normal range of LDH in our clinical laboratory was 135–260 U/L. Therefore, a cutoff level of 338 U/L was set for subclassification. We found 60 patients (25.6%) with high LDH levels. Another factor, bulky disease, was defined as largest tumor diameter ≥ 7 cm. We found 13 patients (5.6%) with bulky disease in our cohort. Patients with high LDH levels or bulky disease were classified as the high-risk group whereas patients with neither high LDH nor bulky disease were classified as the low-risk group. In survival analysis, the median PFS was 65 months for the high-risk group and 64 months for the low-risk group. There was no significant statistical difference (p = 0.8794). The median OS was not reached for both high-risk and low-risk groups. There was no significant statistical difference (p = 0.4464). The results were presented in Fig. 1. Six patients had both features of high LDH and bulky disease. Four patients were free from progression or death. One died with survival less than 3 months. One had relapse but survived. Discussions

DLBCL is the most common type of aggressive lymphoma. The classical frontline treatment is rituximab combined chemotherapy (cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine and prednisone, R-CHOP). About 60–70% of patients can be cured by R-CHOP. While R-CHOP had been the standard of care in the frontline setting for decades, new combinations such as polatuzumab vedotin combing rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, and prednisone has shown its superior efficacy in progression free survival (PFS) [6]. When the patients experience refractory diseases or the tumors relapse shortly (e.g. less than 12 months) from transient remission, the treatment of choice is chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cell therapy and various other immunotherapy alone or in combination with chemotherapies if CAR T therapy is not available. When relapses occur relatively late (e.g. longer than 12 months), salvage chemotherapy followed by autologous stem cell transplantation is still treatment of choice [7–9].

Stratifying risk categories is important. Early identification of high-risk patients may help tailor proper treatment and switch to more effective alternative strategies before rapid, irreversible tumor progression. It may also help identify high-risk unmet clinical needs and design of novel clinical trials [3]. Prognostic factors, therefore, should be properly used for this purpose.

Many prognostic factors are proven to predict outcome of DLBCL. Genetic aberrations, for example, are key predictors of treatment outcome. Genetic change has even become a basis of proposed DLBCL classifications [10–12]. As genetic profiles not only predict prognosis but also treatment response to various novel agents, genetic testing may be one of the most important emerging issues of DLBCL [13]. In other prognostic factors, cell of origin, either defined by immunohistochemical stains or by gene expression profiles, clearly distinguishes the clinical features of germinal center B cell from activated B cell types of DLBCL [11, 14–16]. MYC rearrangements, usually translocations with BCL-2 or BCL-6, result in a marked difference so high-grade B-cell lymphoma with MYC and BCL2 rearrangements is now an entity independent from more common type DLBCL, NOS [17, 18]. In pathology, the tumor proliferation index Ki-67, has been shown to affect outcome of DLBCL as in other types of lymphomas [19, 20]. In image survey, the metabolic tumor volume derived from FDG-positron emission tomography (PET) scans is a novel prognostic factor in both Hodgkin’s and non-Hodgkin’s lymphomas, including DLBCL [21]. In addition to measuring the metabolic tumor volume, accurate assessment of tumor involvement is essential in lymphoma prognostication. Recently, the fused PET/contrast-enhanced CT imaging technique has been utilized to detect occult subdiaphragmatic lesions of lymphoma. The impact of such imaging techniques will have important implications [22].

With all the emerging prognostic markers, the IPI remains the gold standard in evaluating prognosis at initial diagnosis of DLBCL. The IPI consists of age, performance status, stage, number of extra-nodal lesions and serum level of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) [1]. After used in a widespread manner, some modifications of the IPI were done to improve the performance of the prognostic index. As the IPI was introduced before rituximab was incorporated into B cell lymphoma treatment regimens, the revised IPI (R-IPI) was designed using the same 5 factors of the IPI but dividing only 3 distinct prognostic groups which are associated with very good, good and poor outcomes [23]. An age-adjusted International Prognostic Index (IPI) has been designed using only three factors: stage, LDH level, and performance status. A stage-modified IPI, has been designed using four factors: age (> 60 years), stage (I or II), LDH level, and performance status. The National Comprehensive Cancer Network IPI uses the same 5 factors but sub-classifies the scores of age, LDH and extranodal involvement for accurate prediction [24]. The advantages and disadvantages of the original IPI and modified IPIs have been compared [25, 26]. Furthermore, as treatment of DLBCL is evolving rapidly, the IPI needs validation in various settings. For example, Picardi M et al. reported liposomal doxorubicin supercharge-containing front-line treatment in patients with advanced DLBCL (70% with IPI score ≥ 3). The treatment resulted in 87% complete response at the end of treatment [27]. These data appear to suggest modern, novel treatment modalities may overcome high-risk DLBCL. The prognostic values of the IPI, will be a new issue in the era of novel treatment.

In the development of recent years, subdivision of the IPI in identifying subgroups among risk groups has been studied. In our previous study, we found patients with an intermediate-risk IPI score and high β2-microglobulin levels have overall survival comparable to patients with high-risk IPI scores, suggesting an important role of β2-microglobulin in subdivision of DLBCL patients. For patient initially classified as IPI low risk, a proposed subdivision by presence of bulky disease or very high LDH levels (defined as 1.3-fold above laboratory upper limit). Maurer et al. discovered these patients, despite in the group of IPI score 1–2, had similar outcomes to patients with IPI 3 disease. They suggested clinical trials enrolling high-risk patients should also enroll subjects who were re-classified as high-risk [3]. Subsequently, Theodoros et al. reported the Hellenic database validation of the LEO Cohort showing the outcome and subclassification were similar to those found by Maurer et al. [4] Inspired by these findings, we analyzed our DLBCL cohort to explore the relationships of the IPI, β2-microglobulin levels and outcome. The initial analysis revealed that β2-microglobulin blood level is only weakly correlated with that of LDH. Different from previous reports, our study revealed that bulky disease or very high LDH levels were not associated with any difference of OS or PFS. β2-microglobulin levels correlate with LDH but not bulky disease. It should be noted that the statistically insignificant difference for bulky and non-bulky disease is based on a small number of patients with bulky disease. The analysis was not powered enough for clinically meaningful comparison. On the other hand, the group of patients with higher β2-microglobulin levels (≥ 3000 µg/L) had worse PFS but similar OS when compared with patients with lower β2-microglobulin levels. While solid conclusions may not be made based on such a retrospective database, our findings suggest once again the prognostic value of β2-microglobulin. Although β2-microglobulin is not a novel biomarker, its clinical utility is undervalued and should be explored in further studies. When more sophisticated scoring systems are developed for designs of clinical trials or more aggressive treatment modalities, β2-microglobulin should be taken into account as an integral part of precision medicine.

The current study is limited by the research scale and retrospective database in nature.

There are many limits in this study. Solid conclusions can not be reached in such a retrospective analysis. Dose intensity of chemotherapy has an important impact on outcome [27]. Dose density could not be accurately presented in the present analysis. In addition, β2-microglobulin may be seriously influenced by kidney functions. These limits should be better address in prospective studies to confirm our findings.

In conclusion, we analyzed a large cohort data and found β2-microglobulin may help identify a subgroup of high-risk DLBCL with worse PFS among patients with an IPI score of 1–2. Sub-grouping based on LDH or bulky disease, on the other hand, does not appear to predict difference of PFS or OS. In addition to the IPI, we suggest considering β2-microglobulin as a useful biomarker for precision medicine or clinical trial designs in the future.
