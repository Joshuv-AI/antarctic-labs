Logarithmic Schrödinger operators
Jorge J. Betancor 1, Estefanía Dalmasso 2, Juan Carlos Fariña 1, Pablo Quijano 2

Abstract
. In this paper we consider the Schrödinger operator ℒ V = − Δ + V \mathcal{L}_{V}=-\Delta+V in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with a non negative potential V V , and V ≢ 0 V\not\equiv 0 . We define the logarithmic Schrödinger operator log ⁡ ℒ V \log\mathcal{L}_{V} proving its main properties. We obtain a pointwise representation of log ⁡ ℒ V \log\mathcal{L}_{V} when V V satisfies a reverse Hölder inequality of exponent q > d 2 q>\frac{d}{2} by using the semigroup of operators { T t V } t > 0 \{T_{t}^{V}\}_{t>0} generated by ℒ V \mathcal{L}_{V} . We consider the Lipschitz function space adapted to the Schrödinger setting to solve the initial value problem { ∂ u ∂ t = − ( log ⁡ ℒ V ) ​ u , in ​ ℝ n × ( 0 , ∞ ) u ⁡ ( x , 0 ) = f ⁡ ( x ) , x ∈ ℝ d \begin{cases}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=-(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})u,&$\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)$\\ u(x,0)=f(x),&$x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$\end{cases} in terms of the fractional integral associated with ℒ V \mathcal{L}_{V} .

Introduction

Assume that
V
is a nonnegative potential in
L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We consider the sesquilinear form
T
defined by

T(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla f\cdot\overline{\nabla g}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Vf\,\overline{g}\,dx,

where
f,g\in D_{T}=\{h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\nabla h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ and }\sqrt{V}h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
. Since
D_{T}
is dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the quadratic form defined by
T
is closed and nonnegative, there exists a self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}_{V}:D_{T}=D(\mathcal{L}_{V})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\langle\mathcal{L}_{V}f,g\rangle=T(f,g)
for all
f,g\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V})

[
37
, Theorem VIII.15]
. Moreover, for every
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the space of
C^{\infty}
functions with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{V}f=-\Delta f+Vf
.

Our objective is to define and study the logarithmic operator
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
. This operator is defined using functional calculus. We prove that, alternatively,
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
can be defined as the first variation of the fractional operator
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}
at
s=0
. We give a pointwise representation of the operator
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
. We also solve the initial value problem
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=-(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})u
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
, and
u(x,0)=f(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, by using fractional integrals and Lipschitz spaces in the Schrödinger setting.

We now define the logarithmic Schrödinger operator
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
, which is the object of our study. The spectrum
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{V})
is contained in
[0,+\infty)
. Moreover,
0
is not an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{V}
. Indeed, suppose that
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V})
is such that
\mathcal{L}_{V}f=0
. Then,

\langle\mathcal{L}_{V}f,f\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla f|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V|f|^{2}\,dx=0.

Hence,
\nabla f=0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where the gradient is understood distributionally, and
f(x)=0
a.e.
x\in\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:V(y)>0\}
. Since
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
f(x)=0
a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

There exists a spectral measure
E_{V}
supported on
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{V})
such that

\mathcal{L}_{V}f=\int_{[0,+\infty)}\lambda\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f,\quad f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}),

and

D(\mathcal{L}_{V})=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty\right\}.

Here, for every
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mu_{f,g}^{V}
is a complex Borel measure given by
\mu_{f,g}^{V}(U)=\langle E_{V}(U)f,g\rangle
, for every Borel measurable set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. Note that
E_{V}(\{0\})=0
because
0
is not an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{V}
.

If
\phi
is a Borel measurable function on
\mathbb{R}
, we define

\phi(\mathcal{L}_{V})f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(\lambda)\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f,\quad\text{for every }f\in D(\phi(\mathcal{L}_{V})),

where

D(\phi(\mathcal{L}_{V}))=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi(\lambda)|^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty\right\}.

The logarithmic operator
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
is therefore defined by

(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(\lambda)\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f,\quad f\in D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V}),

where

D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{0}^{\infty}|\log(\lambda)|^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty\right\}.

When
V(x)=|x|^{2}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{V}
reduces to the harmonic oscillator operator
H
. In this case, the spectrum
\sigma(H)=\{2k+d\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is discrete. Here
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Moreover, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
such that
\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{d}=k
,

Hh_{\alpha}=(2k+d)h_{\alpha},

where
\displaystyle h_{\alpha}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}h_{\alpha_{j}}(x_{j})
,
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, being

h_{\ell}(u)=(-1)^{\ell}(\sqrt{\pi}2^{\ell}\ell!)^{-1/2}\frac{\partial^{\ell}}{\partial u^{\ell}}\left(e^{-u^{2}}\right)e^{u^{2}/2},\quad u\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and }\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

If
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, we denote
s(\alpha)=\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{d}
. We have that

(\log H)f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\log(2k+d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
s(\alpha)=k\end{subarray}}c_{\alpha}(f)h_{\alpha},\quad\text{for every }f\in D(\log H).

Here,

D(\log H)=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
s(\alpha)=k\end{subarray}}|\log(2k+d)|^{2}|c_{\alpha}(f)|^{2}<\infty\right\}

and, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
,

c_{\alpha}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h_{\alpha}(x)f(x)\,dx,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

For
s\in(0,1)
we define the
s
-power of
\mathcal{L}_{V}
by

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{s}\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f,\quad f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}),

where

D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s})=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2s}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty\right\}.

Note that
D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s_{2}})\subseteq D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s_{1}})

provided that
0<s_{1}\leq s_{2}<1
. If
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\beta})
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
, we have that

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f=f\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We define the operator
\LLog\mathcal{L}_{V}
as follows:

(\LLog\mathcal{L}_{V})f=\frac{d}{ds}(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f)\big|_{s=0}=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f-f}{s},

provided that

f\in D(\LLog\mathcal{L}_{V})=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\beta})\text{ for some }\beta\in(0,1)\text{ and }\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f-f}{s}\text{ exists in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}.

We define, for every
t>0
, the operator
T_{t}^{V}
by

T_{t}^{V}f=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The family
\{T_{t}^{V}\}_{t>0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup of operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The operator
\mathcal{L}_{V}
is the infinitesimal generator of
\{T_{t}^{V}\}_{t>0}
. We can write, for every
t>0
,
T_{t}^{V}=\phi_{t}(\mathcal{L}_{V})
, where
\phi_{t}(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda t}
,
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
.

The Frullani integral representation (see
[
26
, p. 363]
) allows us to write

\log(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-\lambda t}}{t}\,dt,\quad\lambda>0.

This formula motivates the following definition:

(\Log\mathcal{L}_{V})f=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{1/m}^{m}\frac{e^{-t}I-T_{t}^{V}}{t}f\,dt,\quad f\in D(\Log\mathcal{L}_{V}),

where the integrals are understood in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-Bochner sense and the limit is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Here

D(\Log\mathcal{L}_{V})
is the set of all functions
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\displaystyle\frac{e^{-t}I-T_{t}^{V}}{t}f
is
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-Bochner integrable on
(1/m,m)
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
and the limit

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{1/m}^{m}\frac{e^{-t}I-T_{t}^{V}}{t}f\,dt

exists in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We recall that
\displaystyle\frac{e^{-t}I-T_{t}^{V}}{t}f
is
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-Bochner integrable on
(1/m,m)
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
when it is
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-strongly measurable on
((1/m,m),dt)
and

\int_{1/m}^{m}\left\|\frac{e^{-t}I-T_{t}^{V}}{t}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,dt<\infty.

The following theorem establishes the equivalence between the different definitions of the logarithmic operator.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We have that

(a)

f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s_{0}})\cap D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
for some
s_{0}\in(0,1)
, if and only if
f\in D(\LLog\mathcal{L}_{V})
and
(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f=(\LLog\mathcal{L}_{V})f
.

(b)

If
f\in D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
, then
f\in D(\Log\mathcal{L}_{V})
and
(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f=(\Log\mathcal{L}_{V})f
.

Our next objective is to obtain a pointwise representation of
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Note that, as will be established in Proposition
2.1
, we have
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
.

A key difference from the classical case arises from the semigroup properties: unlike the classical heat semigroup
\{W_{t}\}_{t>0}
generated by
-\Delta
, which satisfies
W_{t}1=1
for all
t>0
(the Markovian property), the semigroup
\{T_{t}^{V}\}_{t>0}
is not Markovian, that is,
T_{t}^{V}1\neq 1
for
t>0
. This fundamental difference produces significant distinctions between the pointwise representation for
\log(-\Delta)
established in
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
and the one we shall derive for
\log(\mathcal{L}_{V})
.

In order to establish our pointwise representation of
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
, we consider a special class of potentials satisfying reverse Hölder inequalities. For
1<q<\infty
, we say that a locally integrable function

is in the

when there exists

such that

\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w^{q}\,dx\right)^{1/q}\leq\frac{C}{|B|}\int_{B}w\,dx,

for every ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Functions in
\textup{RH}_{q}
classes were first studied by Gehring (
[
25
]
) and Coifman–Fefferman (
[
12
]
). The condition
V\in\textup{RH}_{q}
for some
1<q<\infty
naturally appears in the study of harmonic analysis operators in the Schrödinger setting, such as Riesz transforms, Littlewood–Paley functions, spectral multipliers, commutators, and others (see
[
1
,
2
,
14
,

,

,

,

,

]
,

Suppose that
d\geq 3
and
V\in\textup{RH}_{q}
with
q>d/2
. As in
[
41
, Eq. (0.14)]
, we define the function
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
by

\rho(x)=\sup\left\{r>0:\frac{1}{r^{d-2}}\int_{B(x,r)}V(y)\,dy\leq 1\right\}.

The function
\rho
was introduced in
[
40
]
for potentials
V
satisfying, for some
C>0
,

\max_{x\in B}V(x)\leq\frac{C}{|B|}\int_{B}V(x)\,dx,

for every ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, in the context of studying the Neumann problem for
\mathcal{L}_{V}
. This auxiliary function
\rho
plays a fundamental role in harmonic analysis in the Schrödinger setting.

It is also proved in
[
40
]
that this function
\rho
satisfies the following estimates:

(1.1)

c_{\rho}^{-1}\,\rho(x)\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-N_{0}}\ \leq\ \rho(y)\ \leq\ c_{\rho}\,\rho(x)\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\frac{N_{0}}{N_{0}+1}},

for some positive constants
c_{\rho}
and
N_{0}
independent of
x
and
y
.
As a consequence of (
1.1
), we have in particular that
\rho(x)\sim\rho(y)
whenever
|x-y|\leq\rho(x)
. That is, there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
x
and
y
, such that

C^{-1}\,\rho(x)\leq\rho(y)\leq C\,\rho(x)
whenever
|x-y|\leq\rho(x)
.

For every
t>0
there exists a measurable function
T_{t}^{V}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
such that

(1.2)

T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{t}^{V}(x,y)f(y)\,dy,

for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, (
1.2
) allows us to define a semigroup of contractions in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
{1\leq p<\infty}
. Since
V
is nonnegative, the Feynman–Kac formula implies that

(1.3)

0\leq T_{t}^{V}(x,y)\leq T_{t}(x-y),\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }t>0,

where
T_{t}(z)=(4\pi t)^{-d/2}e^{-|z|^{2}/(4t)}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
, is the classical heat kernel.

Estimate (
1.3
) can be improved using that
V\in\textup{RH}_{q}
with
q>d/2
. As stated in
[
16
, Proposition 2]
(see also
[
18
,
30
]
), for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
C_{N}>0
such that

(1.4)

T_{t}^{V}(x,y)\leq\frac{C_{N}}{t^{d/2}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{5t}}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-N},

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
. Also,
[
18
, Proposition 4.11]
provides a regularity estimate: there exist
\eta,c>0
such that for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a constant
C_{N}>0
for which

(1.5)

|T_{t}^{V}(x+h,y)-T_{t}^{V}(x,y)|\leq\frac{C_{N}}{t^{d/2}}\left(\frac{|h|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\eta}e^{-c\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-N},

whenever
|h|<\sqrt{t}
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
.

The operator
\mathcal{L}_{V}
can be seen as a nice perturbation of the Euclidean Laplacian
-\Delta
in the following sense: there exists a nonnegative Schwartz class function
\omega
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\delta>0
such that

(1.6)

|T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)|\leq\begin{cases}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\delta}\omega_{t}(x-y),&\text{if }\sqrt{t}\leq\rho(x),\\
\omega_{t}(x-y),&\text{if }\sqrt{t}>\rho(x),\end{cases}

for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\omega_{t}(z)=t^{-d/2}\omega(z/\sqrt{t})
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
(see
[
17
, Proposition 2.16]
).
We now establish our pointwise representation for
\log(\mathcal{L}_{V})
. This result extends, in particular,
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
where
V\equiv 1
is considered.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
q>d/2
. Suppose that
V\in\textup{RH}_{q}
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(a)

We have that

(1.7)

\begin{split}(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f(x)&=-\int_{B(x,1)}(f(y)-f(x))\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}\,dt\,dy\\
&\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,1)}f(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}\,dt\,dy-K(x)f(x),\end{split}

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where

\begin{split}K(x)&=2\log\rho(x)+\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)}{t}\,dy\,dt\\
&\quad-\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,1)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}\,dy\,dt\\
&\quad+\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,1)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}\,dy\,dt+\gamma,\end{split}

and
\gamma
denotes the Euler-Mascheroni constant.

(b)

For every
1<p\leq\infty
,

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f-f}{s}=(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f,

in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Note that the function
K
is not constant. This reflects precisely the difference mentioned earlier: the semigroup
\{T_{t}^{V}\}_{t>0}
is not Markovian, unlike the classical heat semigroup. Consequently, the constant term in the pointwise representation of
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
becomes a non-trivial function
K(x)
depending on the auxiliary function
\rho(x)
, which encodes information about the potential
V
. This fact, combined with the lack of an explicit formula for the heat kernel
T_{t}^{V}(x,y)
—we only have the estimates (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
)—makes the proof of Theorem
1.2
substantially more intricate than that of
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
for the Euclidean case.
A small modification of the proof of Theorem
1.2
allows us to see that if
f\in\textup{Lip}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the
\theta
-Lipschitz space on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for some
\theta\in(0,1]
, and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{d}}\,dy<\infty,

then

\begin{split}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}f(x)-f(x)}{t^{s+1}}\,dt-f(x)\right)&=-\int_{B(x,1)}(f(y)-f(x))\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}\,dt\,dy\\
&\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,1)}f(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}\,dt\,dy-K(x)f(x),\end{split}

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus, the domain of
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
can be extended according to the last identity.

Suppose now
V(x)=1
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In this case
T_{t}^{V}=e^{-t}T_{t}
,
t>0
. By manipulating formula (
1.7
) we get for
f
as in Theorem
1.2
:

\begin{split}(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f(x)&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(f(y)-f(x))\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}T_{t}(x-y)}{t}\,dt\,dy\\
&\quad-f(x)\left(2\log\rho(x)+\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\frac{e^{-t}-1}{t}\,dt+\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}\,dt-\Gamma^{\prime}(1)\right).\end{split}

Since

\log(z)+\int_{0}^{z}\frac{e^{-t}-1}{t}\,dt+\int_{z}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}\,dt=-\gamma\quad\text{for }z\in(0,\infty),

and
\Gamma^{\prime}(1)=-\gamma
, we conclude that

(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f(x)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(f(y)-f(x))\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}T_{t}(x-y)\,dt\,dy,\quad\text{a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

On the other hand, we have that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}T_{t}(x-y)\,dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{(4\pi t)^{d/2}t}\,dt=\frac{2}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{2}\right)^{-d/2}\mathcal{K}_{d/2}(|x-y|),\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\mathcal{K}_{\nu}
denotes the modified Bessel function of the second kind of order
\nu
. Thus,
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
is a special case of Theorem
1.2
here.

Fall and Felli (
[
19
]
and
[
20
]
) studied the essential self-adjointness and established unique continuation properties for a relativistic Schrödinger operator with a singular homogeneous potential defined by

H=(-\Delta+m^{2})^{s}-\frac{a(x/|x|)}{|x|^{2s}}-h(x),

where
m\geq 0
and
s\in(0,1)
. In
[
20
, Eq. (1.3)]
the authors give a pointwise representation for the operator
(-\Delta+m^{2})^{s}
. A pointwise representation for the operator
\log(-\Delta+m^{2})
appears here as a special case of Theorem
1.2
.

In
[
3
]
and
[
36
]
, Lipschitz spaces in the Schrödinger setting were introduced as follows: we say that a function
f\in\textup{Lip}_{V}^{\alpha}
with
0<\alpha<1
when

\|\rho^{-\alpha}f\|_{\infty}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{|h|>0}\frac{\|f(\cdot+h)-f(\cdot)\|_{\infty}}{|h|}<\infty.

The definition of
\textup{Lip}_{V}^{\alpha}
was extended to
0<\alpha<2
in
[
13
]
.

For every
\alpha>0
we consider the negative fractional power
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}
of
\mathcal{L}_{V}
defined by

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-\alpha}\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f,\quad f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}),

where

D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha})=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-2\alpha}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty\right\}.

We are going to see in Proposition
2.5
that
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}
can be expressed in terms of the semigroup
\{T_{t}^{V}\}_{t>0}
as

(1.8)

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}f=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{t}^{V}(f)t^{\alpha-1}\,dt,

By using (
1.2
) we can see that the integral in (
1.8
) allows us to extend
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as a bounded operator from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
where
1/q=1/p-2\alpha/d
,
1<p<d/(2\alpha)
, and from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
L^{d/(d-2\alpha),\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. As can be seen in equation (
1.3
), the integral kernel of the operator
T_{t}^{V}
has a better behavior far away from the diagonal than the classical heat kernel
T_{t}
. In
[
16
]
it was proved that
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}
maps
L^{d/(2\alpha)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into a
BMO
-space denoted by
\textup{BMO}_{\mathcal{L}_{V}}
adapted to the Schrödinger setting. In
[
36
, Theorem 1.2]
(see also
[
13
, Theorem 1.6]
) the authors proved that
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}
maps
\textup{Lip}_{V}^{\theta}
into
\textup{Lip}_{V}^{\theta-2\alpha}
when
0<\theta<1
,
\alpha>0
and
\theta+2\alpha<1
.

Suppose now that
0
does not belong to the spectrum
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{V})
. Then, there exists
a>0
such that
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{V})\subseteq[a,+\infty)
. Since the function
\phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{-\alpha}
,
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
is bounded on
[a,+\infty)
,
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for every
\alpha>0
. Moreover,
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}\circ\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\beta}=\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-(\alpha+\beta)}
, for every
\alpha,\beta>0
, and
\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}f=f
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The family
\{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}\}_{\alpha>0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup of operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We will prove in Proposition
2.3
that the infinitesimal generator of
\{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}\}_{\alpha>0}
is the logarithmic operator
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
. We recall here that
\sigma(H)=\{2k+d\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
when
H
represents the harmonic oscillator operator.

Our final objective is to study the following Cauchy problem

(1.9)

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)+(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})(u(\cdot,t))(x)=0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;t>0,\\
u(x,0)=f(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
. If
0\notin\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{V})
, then, for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
u(x,t)=\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}(f)(x)
is an
L^{2}
-solution of (
1.9
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
V\in\textup{RH}_{q}
with
q>d/2
. Suppose that
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\textup{Lip}_{V}^{\theta}\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for some
\theta\in(0,1)
, and
\nabla f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, the function
u
defined by

u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(t)}\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{z}^{V}(f)(x)z^{t-1}\,dz,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }0<t<1-\theta,

satisfies the following properties:

(a)

u
and
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}
are continuous functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,1-\theta)
.

(b)

\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)=-(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})(u(\cdot,t))(x)
, for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,1-\theta)
.

(c)

For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}u(x,t)=f(x)
.

We prove Theorem
1.1
in Section
2
, Theorem
1.2
in Section
3
and Theorem
1.3
in Section
4
. Throughout this paper
C
and
c
always represent positive constants that may change in each occurrence.

2.
About the logarithmic operator of
\mathcal{L}_{V}

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
together with some properties of the logarithmic operator of
\mathcal{L}_{V}
. As we mentioned before, we define

(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(\lambda)\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f,\quad f\in D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V}),

where

D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{0}^{\infty}|\log(\lambda)|^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty\right\}.

Here
E_{V}
denotes the spectral measure of
\mathcal{L}_{V}
and, for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mu_{f,f}^{V}
is the Borel measure defined by
\mu_{f,f}^{V}(A)=\langle E_{V}(A)f,f\rangle
for each Borel set
A\subseteq(0,\infty)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
V\in\textup{RH}_{q}
with
q>d/2
. Then
D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\beta})\cap D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\alpha})\subseteq D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
, for every
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
, and
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
. We can write

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\log\lambda)^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)&\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{-2\beta}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)+\int_{1}^{\infty}\lambda^{2\alpha}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-2\beta}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2\alpha}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)\right).\end{split}

We deduce that
f\in D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
provided that
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\beta})\cap D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\alpha})
.

On the other hand,
D(\mathcal{L}_{V})\subseteq D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\alpha})
. Hence,
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\alpha})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\beta}
is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
when
1/q=1/p-(2\beta)/d
and
1<p<d/(2\beta)
. In particular,
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\beta}
is bounded from
L^{(2d)/(d+2\beta)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, so
L^{(2d)/(d+2\beta)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\beta})
. Since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq L^{(2d)/(d+2\beta)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we conclude that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\beta})\cap D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\alpha})\subseteq D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
.
∎

We define, for every
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
J_{\beta}=\mathcal{L}_{V}^{i\beta}=\phi_{\beta}(\mathcal{L}_{V})
, where
\phi_{\beta}(\lambda)=\lambda^{i\beta}
,
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
. Since this function is bounded for
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, the operator
J_{\beta}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. We have that

J_{\alpha}\circ J_{\beta}=J_{\alpha+\beta},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}.

Hence
\{J_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}
is a group of bounded operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The operator
i\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
is the infinitesimal generator of the group
\{J_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

We denote by
G
the infinitesimal generator of
\{J_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, that is,

Gf=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{J_{h}f-f}{h},\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

for every
\displaystyle f\in D(G)=\left\{g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{J_{h}g-g}{h}\text{ exists in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}
.

Let
f\in D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
. We are going to show that

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{J_{h}f-f}{h}=i(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f,\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

or equivalently that

\left\|\frac{J_{h}f-f}{h}-i(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\lambda^{ih}-1}{h}-i\log(\lambda)\right|^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)\rightarrow 0

as
h\rightarrow 0
.

We note that
\left|\frac{\lambda^{ih}-1}{h}\right|\leq|\log\lambda|
for all
h\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>0
. Then,

\left|\frac{\lambda^{ih}-1}{h}-i\log\lambda\right|\leq 2|\log\lambda|.

Since
f\in D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
, the Dominated Convergence Theorem leads to

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\lambda^{ih}-1}{h}-i\log\lambda\right|^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)=0.

We conclude that
f\in D(G)
and
Gf=i(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f
.

Conversely, suppose that
f\in D(G)
. Then there exists
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
Gf=g
, that is,

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{hi}-1}{h}\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f-g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0.

Let
(h_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq(0,\infty)
be a sequence converging to zero. The sequence

\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{h_{k}i}-1}{h_{k}}\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

is Cauchy in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, or equivalently, the sequence
\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\lambda^{h_{k}i}-1}{h_{k}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy in
L^{2}((0,\infty),d\mu_{f,f}^{V})
. Hence, there exists
h\in L^{2}((0,\infty),d\mu_{f,f}^{V})
such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\lambda^{h_{k}i}-1}{h_{k}}=h(\lambda)\quad\text{in }L^{2}((0,\infty),d\mu_{f,f}^{V}).

On the other hand, for each
\lambda>0
,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\lambda^{h_{k}i}-1}{h_{k}}=i\log\lambda.

Therefore,
h(\lambda)=i\log\lambda
for
\mu_{f,f}^{V}
-almost every
\lambda>0
.

We have proved that

\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{h_{k}i}-1}{h_{k}}\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f-\int_{0}^{\infty}i\log\lambda\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\lambda^{h_{k}i}-1}{h_{k}}-i\log\lambda\right|^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)\rightarrow 0

as
k\rightarrow\infty
. The arbitrariness of the sequence
(h_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
allows us to conclude that
f\in D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
and
g=i(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f
.
∎

By proceeding in a similar way we can prove the following property:

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that
0\notin\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{V})
. Then
-\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
is the infinitesimal generator of the
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}\}_{t>0}
of operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We proceed now with the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We begin proving part
(a)
. Suppose that
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s_{0}})\cap D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
for some
s_{0}\in(0,1)
. Then
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s})
for every
0<s<s_{0}
. We have that

\frac{|\lambda^{s}-1|}{s}\leq C\begin{cases}|\log\lambda|,&\text{if }0<\lambda<1,\\
|\log\lambda|\lambda^{s}\leq C\lambda^{s_{0}},&\text{if }0<s<s_{0}\text{ and }\lambda\geq 1.\end{cases}

Then,

\left|\frac{\lambda^{s}-1}{s}-\log\lambda\right|^{2}\leq C(\lambda^{2s_{0}}+|\log\lambda|^{2})

for
\lambda>0
and
0<s<s_{0}
. Since
\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2s_{0}}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}|\log\lambda|^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty
, the Dominated Convergence Theorem allows us to obtain that

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left\|\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f-f}{s}-(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\lambda^{s}-1}{s}-\log\lambda\right|^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)=0.

Thus, we have proved that
f\in D(\LLog\mathcal{L}_{V})
and
(\LLog\mathcal{L}_{V})f=(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f
.

If
f\in D(\LLog\mathcal{L}_{V})
, by proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
2.2
we can see that
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s_{0}})\cap D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
, for some
s_{0}\in(0,1)
, and
(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f=(\LLog\mathcal{L}_{V})f
.

To prove part
(b)
we consider the function
F
defined as follows:

\displaystyle F:(0,\infty)

\displaystyle\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{e^{-t}I-T_{t}^{V}}{t}f,\text{ where }f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Since
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a separable space, according to
[
42
, p. 131]
, to show that
F
is
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-strongly measurable on
((0,\infty),dt)
it suffices to prove that
F
is
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-weakly measurable.

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We define, for
t>0
,

H(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[F(t)](x)g(x)dx=\frac{1}{t}\left(e^{-t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)g(x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)g(x)dx\right).

Since
\{T_{t}^{V}\}_{t>0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup of operators in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the function
H
is continuous in
(0,\infty)
. Hence,
H
is a measurable function on
((0,\infty),dt)
. Thus, we proved that
F
is weakly measurable in
((0,\infty),dt)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We have that

\int_{1/m}^{m}\|F(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,dt<\infty.

Indeed, we can write

\begin{split}\int_{1/m}^{m}\|F(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,dt&=\int_{1/m}^{m}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\right|^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)\right)^{1/2}dt\\
&\leq 2\int_{1/m}^{m}\frac{1}{t}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)\right)^{1/2}dt\\
&\leq 4\log(m)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

Hence,
F
is
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-Bochner integrable on
(1/m,m)
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Suppose now that
f\in D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
. We are going to show that

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{1/m}^{m}\frac{e^{-t}I-T_{t}^{V}}{t}f\,dt=(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f,

where the integrals are understood in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-Bochner sense and the limit is taken in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It follows that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g(x)|&\int_{1/m}^{m}\left|\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\,dE_{V}(\lambda)\right)f(x)\right|dt\,dx\\
&\leq\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{1/m}^{m}\left\|\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\,dE_{V}(\lambda)\right)f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}dt\\
&\leq\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{1/m}^{m}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\right|^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)\right)^{1/2}dt\\
&\leq 4\log(m)\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,.\end{split}

Then, Fubini’s theorem leads to

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)\int_{1/m}^{m}&\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\,dE_{V}(\lambda)\right)f(x)\,dt\,dx\\
&=\int_{1/m}^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\,dE_{V}(\lambda)\right)f(x)\,dx\,dt\\
&=\int_{1/m}^{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\,d\mu_{f,g}^{V}(\lambda)\,dt\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{1/m}^{m}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\,dt\,d\mu_{f,g}^{V}(\lambda)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{1/m}^{m}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\,dt\,dE_{V}(\lambda)\right)f(x)\,dx.\end{split}

The arbitrariness of
g
leads to

\int_{1/m}^{m}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\,dE_{V}(\lambda)\right)f\,dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{1/m}^{m}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\,dt\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f.

Our objective is to show that

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{1/m}^{m}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\,dt\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f=(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f,

where the limit is taken in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We have that

\begin{split}\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{m}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\,dt\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{m}^{\infty}\left|\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\right|\,dt\right)^{2}d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{m}^{\infty}\left|\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\right|\,dt\right)^{2}d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)\\
&\quad+\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{m}^{\infty}\left|\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\right|\,dt\right)^{2}d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)\\
&=:J_{1}(m)+J_{2}(m),\quad m\in\mathbb{N}.\end{split}

We get, as in the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 2.12]
,

\int_{m}^{\infty}\left|\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\right|dt=\int_{m}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t\lambda}-e^{-t}}{t}\,dt\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t\lambda}-e^{-t}}{t}\,dt\leq C(|\log\lambda|+1),

for
0<\lambda<1
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Since
f\in D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
, we have
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(|\log\lambda|+1)^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty
, and the Dominated Convergence Theorem leads to

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}J_{1}(m)=0.

On the other hand, we have that

\int_{m}^{\infty}\left|\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\right|\,dt\leq 2\int_{m}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}\,dt\leq 2\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}\,dt,\quad\lambda>1\text{ and }m\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, since
\mu_{f,f}^{V}
is a bounded measure and
\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}\,dt<\infty
, we obtain

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}J_{2}(m)=0.

We conclude that

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left(J_{1}(m)+J_{2}(m)\right)=0.

Again, as in the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 2.12]
we obtain

\int_{0}^{1/m}\left|\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\right|\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\right|\,dt\leq C(|\log\lambda|+1),\quad\lambda>0\text{ and }m\in\mathbb{N}.

The Dominated Convergence Theorem leads to

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1/m}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\,dt\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0.

We conclude that
f\in D(\Log\mathcal{L}_{V})
and

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{1/m}^{m}\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-t\lambda}}{t}\,dt\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(\lambda)\,dE_{V}(\lambda)f=(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f,

in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which completes the proof.

∎

We define, for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the operator

\mathcal{L}_{V,\textup{heat}}^{\alpha}f=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-\alpha)}\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{1/m}^{m}\frac{T_{t}^{V}f-f}{t^{1+\alpha}}\,dt,\quad f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V,\textup{heat}}^{\alpha}),

where the integrals are understood in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-Bochner sense and the limit is taken in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Here

\begin{split}D(\mathcal{L}_{V,\textup{heat}}^{\alpha})=&\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\frac{T_{t}^{V}f-f}{t^{1+\alpha}}\text{ is }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{-Bochner integrable on }((1/m,m),dt),\right.\\
&\left.\text{ for every }m\in\mathbb{N},\text{ and }\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{1/m}^{m}\frac{T_{t}^{V}f-f}{t^{1+\alpha}}\,dt\text{ exists in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}.\end{split}

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

(a)

If
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\alpha})
, then
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V,\textup{heat}}^{\alpha})
and
\mathcal{L}_{V,\textup{heat}}^{\alpha}f=\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\alpha}f
.

(b)

If
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\beta})
for some
\alpha<\beta\leq 1
, then

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\alpha}f=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}f-f}{t^{1+\alpha}}\,dt,

where the integral converges in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-Bochner sense.

Proof.

We note first that part
(a)
can be proved proceeding as in the proof of part
(b)
of Theorem
1.1
. To prove part
(b)
of Proposition
2.4
, we consider the function
F_{\alpha}
defined as follows:

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}:(0,\infty)

\displaystyle\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{e^{-t}I-T_{t}^{V}}{t^{1+\alpha}}f,\text{ where }f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Let
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\beta})
for some
\alpha<\beta\leq 1
. We have that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|F_{\alpha}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,dt&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{e^{-t\lambda}-1}{t^{1+\alpha}}\right|^{2}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)\right)^{1/2}dt\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\alpha-\beta}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2\beta}\,d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)\right)^{1/2}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\alpha}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)\right)^{1/2}\right)<\infty,\end{split}

because
\mu_{f,f}^{V}
is a bounded measure and
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\beta})
.

We conclude that
F_{\alpha}
is
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-Bochner integrable on
((0,\infty),dt)
. It follows that
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V,\textup{heat}}^{\alpha})
and that

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\alpha}f=\mathcal{L}_{V,\textup{heat}}^{\alpha}f=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}f-f}{t^{1+\alpha}}\,dt,

which completes the proof.
∎

We define, for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the operator

\mathcal{L}_{V,\textup{heat}}^{-\alpha}f=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{1/m}^{m}\frac{T_{t}^{V}f}{t^{1-\alpha}}\,dt,\quad f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V,\textup{heat}}^{-\alpha}),

where the integrals are understood in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-Bochner sense and the limit is taken in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Here

\begin{split}D(\mathcal{L}_{V,\textup{heat}}^{-\alpha})=&\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\frac{T_{t}^{V}f}{t^{1-\alpha}}\text{ is }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{-Bochner integrable on }((1/m,m),dt),\right.\\
&\left.\text{ for every }m\in\mathbb{N},\text{ and }\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{1/m}^{m}\frac{T_{t}^{V}f}{t^{1-\alpha}}\,dt\text{ exists in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}.\end{split}

By arguing as in Proposition
2.4
we can obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

(a)

If
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha})
, then
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V,\textup{heat}}^{-\alpha})
and
\mathcal{L}_{V,\textup{heat}}^{-\alpha}f=\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}f
.

(b)

If
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\beta})
for some
\alpha<\beta<1
, then

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-\alpha}f=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}f}{t^{1-\alpha}}\,dt,

where the integral converges in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-Bochner sense.

Remark 2.6
.

Note that the properties established in this section can also be proved for a more general class of operators. As far as we know, these properties have not been studied carefully enough in the literature.

3.
Pointwise representation of
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}

In this section we are going to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. According to Theorem
1.1
we have that

(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f-f}{s},\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Then, for a certain sequence
\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq(0,\infty)
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}=0
, we can write

(3.1)

(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s_{n}}f(x)-f(x)}{s_{n}},\quad\text{a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Since
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V})
, it follows that

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)-f}{t^{s+1}}dt,\quad 0<s<1,

where the integral is understood in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-Bochner sense.

Indeed, let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. From (
3.1
) and the properties of the Bochner integrals, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)-f}{t^{s+1}}dt\right)(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-f(x)}{t^{s+1}}g(x)dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-f(x)}{t^{s+1}}g(x)dxdt+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-f(x)}{t^{s+1}}g(x)dxdt.

Since
\sup_{t>0}\|T_{t}^{V}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-f(x)\right|}{t^{s+1}}

\displaystyle|g(x)|dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{t>0}\|T_{t}^{V}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty.

On the other hand, since
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V})
, there exists
C>0
such that

\|T_{t}^{V}(f)-f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq Ct,\quad t\in(0,1).

Then,

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-f(x)|}{t^{s+1}}|g(x)|dxdt\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t^{s}}dt<\infty.

Fubini’s theorem leads to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)-f}{t^{s+1}}dt\right)(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-f(x)}{t^{s+1}}dtdx.

The arbitrariness of
g
implies that

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)-f}{t^{s+1}}dt\right)(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-f(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt,\quad\text{a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We are going to see that, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
,

(3.2)

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}H_{s}(x)=\int_{B(x,r)}(f(x)-f(y))\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dtdy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,r)}f(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dtdy-K(x,r)f(x),

where

\displaystyle K(x,r)

\displaystyle=2\log(\rho(x))+\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)}{t}dydt-\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,r)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dydt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dydt-\gamma,

and

H_{s}(x)=\frac{1}{s}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-f(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt-f(x)\right),\quad s\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}).

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We can write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-f(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)f(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt+f(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)-1}{t^{s+1}}dt

\displaystyle:=J_{1}(s)+J_{2}(s),\quad s\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}).

According to (
1.3
) we have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}|f(x)-f(y)|dydt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)-f(y)|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-c|x-y|^{2}/t}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+s+1}}dtdy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{B(x,1)}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d+2s-1}}+\int_{B^{c}(x,1)}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{du}{u^{2s}}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{du}{u^{2s+1}}\right)<\infty,\quad s\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}).

Let
r>0
. It follows that

\displaystyle J_{1}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(f(y)-f(x))\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dtdy

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x,r)}(f(y)-f(x))\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dtdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,r)}f(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dtdy

\displaystyle\quad-f(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,r)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dtdy

\displaystyle:=J_{1,1}(s)+J_{1,2}(s)+J_{1,3}(s),\quad s\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}).

Since
s\Gamma(-s)=-\Gamma(1-s)
, for
s\in(0,1)
,
\Gamma(-s)\sim-\frac{1}{s}
as
s\to 0^{+}
. We will use this property several times in the sequel.

By proceeding as above, we get

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dt\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-c|x-y|^{2}/t}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+s+1}}dt\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{d+2s}},\quad y\neq x.

If
|x-y|<r
, then
|x-y|^{d+2s}=r^{d+2s}\left|\frac{x-y}{r}\right|^{d+2s}\geq C|x-y|^{d+\frac{1}{2}}
for
s\in(0,1/4)
. Thus, since
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we obtain

|f(x)-f(y)|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dt\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{d-\frac{1}{2}}},\quad 0<|x-y|<r.

Since
\int_{B(x,r)}|x-y|^{-d+\frac{1}{2}}dy<\infty
, the Dominated Convergence Theorem implies that

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}J_{1,1}(s)=\int_{B(x,r)}(f(y)-f(x))\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dtdy.

On the other hand, using again (
1.3
), we get for every
x\neq y
and
s\in(0,1)
that

\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}\leq C\begin{cases}\frac{e^{-c|x-y|^{2}/t}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+\frac{3}{2}}}&$t\in(0,1)$\\
\frac{1}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+1}}&$t\in(1,\infty).$\end{cases}

Therefore, the Dominated Convergence Theorem leads to

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dt,

so we conclude that

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)s}J_{1,1}(s)=-\int_{B(x,r)}(f(y)-f(x))\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dt.

Since
\supp(f)
is compact, by proceeding as before we obtain that

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)s}J_{1,2}(s)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,r)}f(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dt.

We postpone, for the moment, the study of
J_{1,3}
and proceed to estimate
J_{2}
.

We decompose
J_{2}
as follows,

\displaystyle J_{2}(s)

\displaystyle=f(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)-T_{t}(1)(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt

\displaystyle=f(x)\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt+f(x)\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,r)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt

\displaystyle\quad+{f(x)}\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt-f(x)\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}(x-y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt

\displaystyle:=J_{2,1}(s)+J_{2,2}(s)+J_{2,3}(s)+J_{2,4}(s),

for
s\in\left(0,\frac{\delta}{4}\right)
, where
\delta>0
is as in (
1.6
).

Clearly,

J_{2,4}(s)=-\frac{\rho(x)^{-2s}}{s}f(x),\quad s\in\left(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\right).

Then,

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}J_{2,4}(s)-f(x)\right)

\displaystyle=f(x)\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\left(\frac{\rho(x)^{-2s}-1}{s}+\frac{1-\Gamma(1-s)}{s\Gamma(1-s)}\rho(x)^{-2s}\right)

\displaystyle=f(x)\left(-2\log(\rho(x))+\Gamma^{\prime}(1)\right)=f(x)\left(-2\log(\rho(x))-\gamma\right).

To estimate
J_{2,1}
, we consider
0<s<\frac{\delta}{4}
. According to (
1.6
), for any
0<t<\rho^{2}(x)
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\frac{|T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)|}{t^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\delta}\frac{\omega_{t}(x-y)}{t^{s+1}}=\frac{\omega_{t}(x-y)}{\left(\frac{t}{\rho^{2}(x)}\right)^{s+1-\delta/2}(\rho(x))^{2s+2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{t}(x-y)}{\left(\frac{t}{\rho^{2}(x)}\right)^{1-\delta/4}(\rho(x))^{2s+2}}

\displaystyle=(\rho(x))^{-2s-\delta/2}\frac{\omega_{t}(x-y)}{t^{1-\delta/4}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{(\rho(x))^{-\delta},(\rho(x))^{-\delta/2}\right\}\frac{\omega_{t}(x-y)}{t^{1-\delta/4}}.

Since
\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\omega_{t}(x-y)}{t^{1-\delta/4}}dydt<\infty
, it follows that

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s\Gamma(-s)}J_{2,1}(s)=-f(x)\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)}{t}dydt.

We are going to combine now the estimates for
J_{1,3}
and
J_{2,2}
. By using again (
1.3
), if
0<s<\frac{\delta}{4}
,
0<t<\rho^{2}(x)
and
|x-y|>r
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{e^{-c|x-y|^{2}/t}}{t^{d/2+s+1}}\leq C\frac{e^{-\frac{c}{2t}r^{2}}e^{-\frac{c}{2t}|x-y|^{2}}}{\left(\frac{t}{\rho^{2}(x)}\right)^{d/2+s+1}\rho(x)^{d+2s+2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\left\{(\rho(x))^{-d-2},(\rho(x))^{-d-2-\delta/2}\right\}\frac{e^{-\frac{c}{2t}r^{2}}e^{-\frac{c}{2t}|x-y|^{2}}}{t^{d/2+\delta/4+1}}.

Noting that

\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\frac{e^{-\frac{c}{2t}r^{2}}}{t^{d/2+\delta/4+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{c}{2t}|x-y|^{2}}dydt\leq C\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\frac{e^{-\frac{c}{2t}r^{2}}{t^{d/2+s+1}}}{t^{\delta/4+1}}dt<\infty,

it yields

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s\Gamma(-s)}\left(J_{1,3}(s)+J_{2,2}(s)\right)=f(x)\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,r)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dydt.

Finally, for
t>\rho^{2}(x)
and
0<s<\delta/4
, (
1.3
) leads to

\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}\leq C\frac{e^{-c|x-y|^{2}/t}}{t^{d/2+s+1}}\leq\frac{C}{\left(\frac{t}{\rho^{2}(x)}\right)^{d/2+s+1}\rho(x)^{d+2s+2}}\leq\frac{C}{t^{d/2+1}}.

Since
\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,r)}t^{-d/2-1}dt<\infty
, we have

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s\Gamma(-s)}J_{2,3}(s)=-f(x)\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dydt.

By combining all of the above estimates, we conclude that (
1.7
) holds. By taking
r=1
in (
1.7
) we have established part
(a)
of Theorem
1.2
.

Let us now prove
(b)
. We choose
r>1
such that
\supp(f)\subseteq B(0,r)
.

First, assume that
|x|\geq 2r
, so
f(x)=0
. Then,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}f(x)-f(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt=\int_{B(0,r)}f(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dtdy.

Note that for every
y\in B(0,r)
,
|x-y|\geq|x|-|y|\geq\frac{|x|}{2}\geq r>1
. Then, by using (
1.3
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}f(x)-f(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B(0,r)}|f(y)|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-c\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+s+1}}dtdy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B(0,r)}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{|x|^{d}},

for every
s\in(0,1)
. It follows that, for
1<p<\infty

(3.3)

\frac{1}{s|\Gamma(-s)|}\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}f(\cdot)-f(\cdot)}{t^{s+1}}dt\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,2r))}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}r^{-\frac{d}{p^{\prime}}},

and

(3.4)

\frac{1}{s|\Gamma(-s)|}\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}f(\cdot)-f(\cdot)}{t^{s+1}}dt\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,2r))}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}r^{-d},

for every
s\in(0,1)
. Here, the constants are independent of
s
since
s|\Gamma(-s)|\sim 1
as
s\to 0^{+}
.

Suppose now
|x|<2r
. If
|x-y|\geq 3r
, then
|y|\geq|x-y|-|x|>r
. Therefore,
\supp(f)\subseteq B(x,3r)
and we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(f(y)-f(x))\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dtdy

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x,3r)}(f(y)-f(x))\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dtdy

\displaystyle\quad-f(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,3r)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dtdy

\displaystyle:=J_{1,1}(s,x)+J_{1,3}(s,x),\quad s\in(0,1).

We will prove that

(3.5)

\frac{J_{1,1}(s,x)}{s\Gamma(-s)}\underset{s\to 0^{+}}{\longrightarrow}-\int_{B(x,3r)}(f(y)-f(x))\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dtdy

uniformly on
x\in B(0,2r)
. Indeed, by using (
1.3
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\frac{J_{1,1}(s,x)}{s\Gamma(-s)}+\int_{B(x,3r)}(f(y)-f(x))\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dtdy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(x,3r)}|f(y)-f(x)|\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{t}^{V}(x,y)\left|\frac{1}{t^{s+1}}-\frac{1}{t}\right|dtdy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B(x,3r)}|x-y|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-c\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}}\left|\frac{1}{t^{s+1}}-\frac{1}{t}\right|dtdy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{3r}u^{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-c\frac{u^{2}}{t}}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}}\left|\frac{1}{t^{s+1}}-\frac{1}{t}\right|dtdu.

We observe that, for every
t\in(0,\infty)
and
s\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
,

\frac{e^{-c\frac{u^{2}}{t}}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+1}}\left|\frac{1}{t^{s}}-1\right|\leq C\left(\frac{e^{-c\frac{u^{2}}{t}}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+1}}\chi_{[1,\infty)}(t)+\frac{e^{-c\frac{u^{2}}{t}}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+\frac{3}{2}}}\chi_{(0,1)}(t)\right).

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{3r}u^{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-c\frac{u^{2}}{t}}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}}\left|\frac{1}{t^{s+1}}-\frac{1}{t}\right|dtdu

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{3r}u^{d}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-c\frac{u^{2}}{t}}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+1}}dtdu+\int_{0}^{3r}u^{d}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-c\frac{u^{2}}{t}}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+\frac{3}{2}}}dtdu

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{3r}u^{d}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{t}{u^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}\frac{dt}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+1}}du+\int_{0}^{3r}u^{d}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+\frac{3}{2}}}dtdu

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{3r}\frac{du}{u^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{t^{\frac{3}{4}}}\right)+\int_{0}^{3r}u^{d}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}du<\infty.

The Dominated Convergence Theorem leads to

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{3r}u^{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-c\frac{u^{2}}{t}}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}}\left|\frac{1}{t^{s+1}}-\frac{1}{t}\right|dtdu=0.

Thus, (

) is proved.

As before, we proceed to estimate

given below, and leave the study of

for later. We write

\displaystyle J_{2}(s,x)

\displaystyle=f(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)-T_{t}(1)(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt

\displaystyle=f(x)\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt+f(x)\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,r)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt

\displaystyle\quad+f(x)\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt-f(x)\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}(x-y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt

\displaystyle:=J_{2,1}(s,x)+J_{2,2}(s,x)+J_{2,3}(s,x)+J_{2,4}(s,x)

for

.

By (

\displaystyle\left|\frac{J_{2,1}(s,x)}{s\Gamma(-s)}+\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)}{t}dydt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)|\left|\frac{1}{t^{s+1}}-\frac{1}{t}\right|dydt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\left|\frac{1}{t^{s+1}}-\frac{1}{t}\right|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\delta}\omega_{t}(x-y)dydt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\delta}\left|\frac{1}{t^{s}}-1\right|\frac{dt}{t},

for every

.

As it was proved above, since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-f(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt-1\right)\underset{s\to 0^{+}}{\longrightarrow}

uniformly in

.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
V\in\textup{RH}_{q}
with
q>\frac{d}{2}
. If
f\in\textup{Lip}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
\theta\in(0,1]
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|(1+|y|)^{-d}dy<\infty
, then

(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})(f)(x)=-\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{-h}_{V}-I}{h}(f)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

Let
h>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-h}_{V}f(x)

\displaystyle-f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(h)}\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\left(T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)f(x)\right)t^{h-1}dt+\frac{1}{\Gamma(h)}\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)t^{h-1}dt

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{f(x)}{\Gamma(h)}\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\left(T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)-T_{t}(1)(x)\right)t^{h-1}dt+\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(h)}\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}T_{t}(1)(x)t^{h-1}dt-1\right)f(x)

\displaystyle:=A_{1}(x,h)+A_{2}(x,h)+A_{3}(x,h)+A_{4}(x,h).

By using that
f\in\textup{Lip}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and (
1.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle T_{t}^{V}(x,y)|f(y)-f(x)|t^{h-1}

\displaystyle\leq C|x-y|^{\theta}\frac{e^{-c\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{d/2+1-h}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{e^{-\frac{c}{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{d/2}}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}(x)}{t}\right)^{1-h-\theta/2}\rho(x)^{\theta-2+2h}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{e^{-\frac{c}{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{d/2}}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}(x)}{t}\right)^{1-\theta/2}\max\{\rho^{\theta-2}(x),\rho^{\theta}(x)\}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{e^{-\frac{c}{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{1+(d-\theta)/2}}\max\{1,\rho^{2}(x)\},

when
h\in(0,1)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<t<\rho^{2}(x)
.

Since

\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{e^{-\frac{c}{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{(d-\theta)/2+1}}dydt\leq C\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}t^{\theta/2-1}dt\leq C\rho^{\theta}(x),

the Dominated Convergence Theorem leads to

\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{A_{1}(x,h)}{h}=\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)f(x)}{t}dt.

By using that
f\in\textup{Lip}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and (
1.4
) we get

\displaystyle T_{t}^{V}(x,y)|f(y)|t^{h-1}

\displaystyle\leq{C_{N}}\frac{e^{-c\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{1-h+d/2}}\left(\frac{\rho(x)}{\rho(x)+\sqrt{t}}\right)^{{N}}(|x-y|^{\theta}+|f(x)|)

\displaystyle\leq{C_{N}}\rho(x)^{{N}}\frac{e^{-\frac{c}{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{{N/2+1}-h+d/2}}(t^{\theta/2}+|f(x)|)

\displaystyle\leq{C_{N}}\rho(x)^{{N}}\frac{e^{-\frac{c}{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{d/2}}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}(x)}{t}\right)^{{N/2+1}-h}(t^{\theta/2}+|f(x)|)\rho(x)^{2h-{N+2}}

\displaystyle\leq{C_{N}}\rho(x)^{{N}}\frac{e^{-\frac{c}{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{d/2}}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}(x)}{t}\right)^{{N/2}}(t^{\theta/2}+|f(x)|)\rho(x)^{2h-{N+2}}

\displaystyle\leq{C_{N}}\rho(x)^{{N-2}+2h}\frac{e^{-\frac{c}{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{d/2}}\frac{1}{t^{{N/2}}}(t^{\theta/2}+|f(x)|)

for
h\in(0,1)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\geq\rho^{2}(x)
.

By choosing
N>3
,

\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{e^{-\frac{c}{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{d/2}}\frac{1}{t^{{N/2}}}(t^{\theta/2}+|f(x)|)dydt\leq C\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\theta/2}+|f(x)|}{t^{{N/2}}}dt<\infty.

According to Dominated Convergence Theorem we get

\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{A_{2}(x,h)}{h}=\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)}{t}dt.

By (
1.6
) it follows that

\displaystyle|T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)|t^{h-1}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\rho(x)^{\delta}}\frac{\omega_{t}(x-y)}{t^{1-h-\delta/2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\rho(x)^{2h-2}\omega_{t}(x-y)\left(\frac{\rho^{2}(x)}{t}\right)^{1-h-\delta/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\rho(x)^{2h-\delta}\omega_{t}(x-y)t^{-1+\delta/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\{\rho(x)^{2-\delta},\rho(x)^{-\delta}\}\omega_{t}(x-y)t^{-1+\delta/2},

when
h\in(0,1)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<t<\rho^{2}(x)
.

Since

\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{t}(x-y)t^{-1+\delta/2}dydt\leq C\rho(x)^{\delta},

the Dominated Convergence Theorem leads to

\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{A_{3}(x,h)}{h}=f(x)\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)-T_{t}(1)(x)}{t}dt.

Finally, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{A_{4}(x,h)}{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{f(x)}{h}\left(\frac{\rho(x)^{2h}}{h\Gamma(h)}-1\right)=f(x)\left(\frac{\rho(x)^{2h}-1}{\Gamma(h+1)h}+\frac{1-\Gamma(h+1)}{\Gamma(h+1)h}\right),\quad h\in(0,1).

Then,

\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{A_{4}(x,h)}{h}=f(x)(\gamma+2\log\rho(x)).

We conclude that

\displaystyle-\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-h}(f)(x)-f(x)}{h}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(1)f(x)-T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)}{t}dt

\displaystyle-\int_{\rho^{2}(x)}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)}{t}dt

\displaystyle+f(x)\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}(x)}\frac{T_{t}(1)(x)-T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)}{t}dt

\displaystyle-f(x)(\gamma+2\log\rho(x)).

By a rearrangement of the terms and taking into account that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|(1+|y|)^{-d}<\infty
, Theorem
1.2
and the comments after this one allow us to conclude that

-\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{-h}_{V}(f)(x)-f(x)}{h}=\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{h}_{V}(f)(x)-f(x)}{h}=(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})(f)(x).\qed

Proof of Theorem
1.3

We define
u(x,t)=\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}f(x)
,

and

According to the comments after Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
3.1
, we obtain

(4.1)

\Log(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}f)(x)=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-h}-I}{h}\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }t\in(0,1-\theta).

We are going to show that

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-h}(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}f)(x)=\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-(h+t)}f(x),\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }0<h<1-t-\theta.

Assume first that

, with

. We have that

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{u}^{V}(x,y)u^{t-1}\,du\,dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We can write

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|&\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{u}^{V}(z,y)u^{t-1}\,du\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{s}^{V}(x,z)s^{h-1}\,ds\,dy\,dz\\

This justifies the interchange in the order of integration, obtaining

(4.2)

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-h}(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}f)(x)=\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-(t+h)}f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

) we get

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-(t+h)}f(x)-\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}f(x)}{h}=-\log(\mathcal{L}_{V})(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}f)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }t\in(0,1-\theta).

We are going to show that

(4.3)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-(t-h)}f(x)-\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}f(x)}{-h}=-\log(\mathcal{L}_{V})(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}f)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }t\in(0,1-\theta).

4.2

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-(t-h)}f(x)-\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}f(x)=\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-(t-h)}f(x)-\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-h}(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-(t-h)}f)(x)=\frac{I-\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-h}}{h}\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-(t-h)}f(x),

for

and

.

We now prove that

(4.4)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-(t-h)}f(x)=\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-t}f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }0<t<1-\theta.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

. We decompose

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-(t-h)}f(x)&=\frac{1}{\Gamma(t-h)}\left[\int_{0}^{1}u^{t-h-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{u}^{V}(x,y)f(y)\,dy\,du+\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{t-h-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{u}^{V}(x,y)f(y)\,dy\,du\right]\\

Since

, by using (

) we get, for

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}u^{t-h-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{u}^{V}(x,y)|f(y)|\,dy\,du&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}u^{t-h-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{u}(x-y)|f(y)|\,dy\,du\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}u^{t/2-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{u}(x-y)|f(y)|\,dy\,du\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{|x-y|<1}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d-t}}\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|\,dy\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{|x-y|<1}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|+|f(x)|}{|x-y|^{d-t}}\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|\,dy\right)<\infty,\end{split}

where

does not depend on

. Then, the Dominated Convergence Theorem leads to

Since

, by using again (

) we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{t-h-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{u}^{V}(x,y)|f(y)|\,dy\,du&\leq C\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{t-h-1-d/2}\,du\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|\,dy\\
&\leq C\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{t-1-d/2}\,du\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|\,dy<\infty,\quad 0<h<t,\end{split}

where again
C
does not depend on
h
. According to the Dominated Convergence Theorem we get

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}I_{2}(h)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(t)}\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{t-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{u}^{V}(x,y)f(y)\,dy\,du,

and (
4.4
) is proved.
