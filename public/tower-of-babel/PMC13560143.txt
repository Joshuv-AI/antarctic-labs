Analysis of risk factors for post-transarterial chemoembolization syndrome in patients with liver cancer: a systematic review protocol
Authors: Lei Yang, Shanshan Huang, Xiao Sheng, Qianjin Xu, Jinling Ding
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTransarterial chemoembolization (TACE) is the standard treatment for patients with early-stage hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), and post-embolization syndrome frequently occurs after the procedure. Although multiple studies have investigated risk factors for PES in HCC patients after TACE, their findings remain inconsistent and controversial. This systematic review aims to synthesize current evidence and identify risk factors associated with PES in HCC patients undergoing TACE.Methods/designThis study follows a registered protocol for a systematic review and meta-analysis. We will include randomized and non-randomized trials, prospective or retrospective cohort studies, and predictive model development articles that report risk factors for post-embolization syndrome (PES) after transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) in patients with liver cancer. The primary outcome is the risk factors for post-embolization syndrome. A comprehensive literature search will be conducted from database inception across PubMed, MEDLINE, EMBASE, Web of Science, Scopus, CINAHL Plus, OpenGrey, the Cochrane Library, Google Scholar, and the China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI). Two researchers will independently screen titles, abstracts, and full texts. Risk of bias will be assessed using validated tools. Where studies have similar sample characteristics, assessment methods, research design type and corresponding outcomes, we will pool the results and apply various types of meta-analyses such as mean difference, standard mean difference, or Cox regression for continuous outcomes and risk ratio measurement or logistic regression for categorical outcomes depicted in odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Meta-regression will be used to analyze heterogeneity if necessary.DiscussionThis systematic review and meta-analysis will integrate evidence from both randomized and non-randomized studies, providing an updated and comprehensive synthesis of risk factors for PES after TACE in HCC patients. This study will also explore some factors that may explain the possible differences among different studies. Background

Primary liver cancer is the sixth most common cancer globally. In 2022, there were 865,269 new cases (4.3%) of liver cancer patients worldwide, and 757,948 new deaths (7.8%), ranking it as the third leading cause of cancer death [1]. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for approximately 80–85% of primary liver malignancies [2]. Moreover, HCC carries a poor prognosis, with mortality rates rising rapidly. Major etiologies include hepatitis B virus (HBV), hepatitis C virus (HCV), alcoholic hepatitis, metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease, and alterations in the tumor microenvironment [3, 4]. The development of HCC poses new challenges to its treatment and prevention. Currently, the main treatment measures for HCC mainly include curative and non-curative treatments [5]. Non-curative measures include transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) and systemic therapies. TACE is the standard therapy for intermediate-stage HCC according to the Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer classification and is also widely used in early-stage HCC patients unsuitable for curative resection or ablation [5].

TACE involves injecting chemotherapeutic drugs and lipiodol into the tumor blood vessels through a hepatic artery catheter to achieve the dual effects of local chemotherapy and promoting tumor ischemic necrosis [6, 7]. It is categorized into conventional transarterial chemoembolization combined with ethiodized oil transarterial chemoembolization (c-TACE) and drug-eluting bead transarterial chemoembolization (DEB-TACE) [8]. While c-TACE confers significant survival benefits and improves 2-year survival rates with acceptable tolerance, severe complications occur in 5.6% of cases, including acute liver failure, liver abscess, sepsis, and gastrointestinal bleeding [9, 10] and most patients are prone to post-embolization syndrome (PES). DEB-TACE reduces hepatotoxicity and doxorubicin-related adverse effects, with better tolerance and fewer post-procedural complications than c-TACE. However, c-TACE has a higher complete remission rate for local tumor control in the treatment of HCC [8]. Therefore, c-TACE is regarded as the preferred treatment option for patients who can tolerate PES to achieve complete remission.

The definition of PES lacks universal consensus. This article adopts the consensus of the Asian Pacific Association [11] and defines PES as characterized by nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and fever, occurring in more than 50% of patients after the procedure, and it mostly occurs within 48 h after TACE, accompanied by blood and biochemical abnormalities [12]. PES may lead to prolonged hospital stays, increased healthcare costs, complicates clinical management, and imposes a substantial burden on healthcare systems [13]. It may also adversely affect survival outcomes after TACE [14]. Reported risk factors for PES include alcohol consumption, body mass index, serum albumin level, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, complete embolization, multiple tumor lesions, low skeletal muscle index, number of nodules, repeated TACE sessions, microsphere type, embolic agent, and BCLC stage [15–20]. However, Thanakunchai et al. found no association between BCLC stage or tumor size and PES, contradicting previous reports [21]. Given these inconsistencies, a systematic synthesis of evidence is urgently needed to clarify risk factors for PES, explore heterogeneity, and provide evidence-based guidance for clinical practice.

Objective

Identify and systematically analyze the risk factors for post-embolization syndrome in liver cancer patients after TACE.

Methods

Standards

This study follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines [22] and is being reported in accordance with the reporting guidance provided in the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Protocols (PRISMA-P) statement [23] (see checklist in Supplemental file 1). This protocol was registered in the PROSPERO database (www.crd.york.ac.uk/prospero/) under the registration ID of CRD420251218746.

Eligibility criteria

In addition to randomized and non-randomized studies, this study will include all observational studies, including cohort studies, case–control studies, and cross-sectional studies which reported the risk factors for post-embolization syndrome in patients after transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) for liver cancer. We will also include articles on the construction of prediction models that reported the risk factors for post-TACE PES in patients. Reports from organizations, review papers, editorials, conference abstracts, research papers, qualitative studies, interviews, case series, case reports, or studies that did not provide risk factors for PES will be excluded.

Participants

It includes all liver cancer patients who received TACE treatment and developed PES, and the patients are no less than 18 years old.

Disease

This review will only include patients with liver cancer.

Exposure

Risk factors associated with PES.

Comparator

Those who did not develop PES will form the comparison group.

Outcomes

This study aims to identify the risk factors associated with PES. Therefore, the outcome is PES, namely nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and fever.

Patient and public involvement

There will be no patient or public involvement in this study, as it is based on secondary data.

Information sources

A comprehensive search will be conducted with no language restrictions across PubMed, MEDLINE, EMBASE, Web of Science, Scopus, CINAHL Plus, OpenGrey, the Cochrane Library, Google Scholar, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI). A snowball method will be used to search the reference lists of selected articles for reading and reference for a broader literature review. To minimize publication bias, the OpenGrey online database will be used to search gray literature. For each electronic database, a search strategy specific to that database will be developed. Selection process

We will follow a two-stage selection process to finally include studies in the review. In the first stage, two reviewers will assess whether each title is suitable for inclusion in the review. If it is found unsuitable, the article will not be included, and all other articles will be moved to the second-stage selection. In the second stage, the abstracts of the literature will be obtained, and two reviewers will independently review all the abstracts. If both reviewers agree to reject a study, it will be excluded; the remaining studies will be retrieved and independently reviewed by two authors. If both reviewers agree to accept a study, it will be included; if both agree to reject it, it will be excluded. When the reviewers' opinions differ, a third reviewer will arbitrate the screening results. The screening process will be presented through a PRISMA flowchart.

Data extraction

Data will be extracted using a pre-designed and pre-tested form. This form will include the following information: year of publication, authors, country, study design, age, gender, sample size, the risk factors studied and their associations, as well as the association between these risk factors and PES. Data will be extracted from the full-text by one reviewer and checked by a second reviewer. In case of disagreement, consensus will be reached through discussion.

Risk of bias

For the finally selected case–control studies, cohort studies, and cross-sectional studies, the Newcastle–Ottawa scale (NOS) [24] will be used to assess the risk of bias in observational studies. For studies that do not adjust for risk factors of potential confounding variables, they will be judged to have a “high” risk of bias. Two reviewers will independently assess the risk of bias, and disagreements will be resolved by consulting a third reviewer. If interventional studies are obtained (although the possibility is low), the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool [25] will be used for assessment. In addition, we will use PROBAST to assess the risk of bias in the prediction model [26].

Data synthesis and statistical analysis

Narrative synthesis

We will provide a list of all confirmed risk factors mentioned in the included studies in a narrative format. A detailed summary of all included studies will contain the following information: authors, type of study design, characteristics of participants (age, gender), sample size, risk factors and their measures of association, and the main study findings (including limitations).

Assessment of heterogeneity

We will use the I2 statistic to measure heterogeneity between trials in each analysis. We will use these ranges to guide our interpretation of the I2 statistic in accordance with Sect. 10.10.2 of the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions. If I2 = 0%–40%, heterogeneity will not be significant. If I2 = 30%–60%, it may represent moderate heterogeneity. If I2 = 50%–75%, it may represent substantial heterogeneity and if I2 = 75%–100%, it will indicate considerable heterogeneity.

Statistical analysis

Taking into account the impact of confounding factors, we will use the adjusted estimates. Where studies have similar sample characteristics, assessment methods, research design type, and corresponding outcomes, we will pool the results and apply various types of meta-analyses such as mean difference, standard mean difference, or Cox regression for continuous outcomes and risk ratio measurement or logistic regression for categorical outcomes. These results will be presented in the form of odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). To synthesize the data, when the statistical heterogeneity is not significant (I2 < 50%), a fixed effects model will be selected. When the statistical heterogeneity is significant (I2 ≥ 50%), a random effects model will be selected, followed by further analysis to determine the cause of heterogeneity. Subgroup analyses will be performed to identify the sources of heterogeneity. Finally, if the heterogeneity is considerable high (I2 > 75%), a narrative description will be applied.

If the data are insufficient or missing, we will attempt to contact the study authors and record the number and reasons for the missing data. Publication bias will be evaluated using a funnel plot and Egger test only when at least 10 studies are included in the meta-analysis. Considering the different research methods between observational studies and prediction models, we will combine the influencing factors according to the type of research method and conduct a systematic review.

Analysis of subgroups or subsets

When heterogeneity is evident (I2 > 50%), subgroup analyses will be used to explore the source of heterogeneity. Subgroup analysis will include demographic factors, tumor size, Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) staging, and albumin levels. Considering the differences in embolic agents and side effects between c-TACE and DEB-TACE, we will conduct subgroup analysis on these two types of embolization.

Sensitivity analysis

Since the definition of PES has not been unified and the processing methods for different covariates vary, we will conduct a sensitivity analysis on these two aspects. We will gradually weed out the low-quality studies, re-estimate the pooled effect sizes and compare the results with those of the meta-analysis before the exclusion to explore the impact of the study on the pooled effect sizes and the robustness of the results. If there is no significant change in the results after exclusion, it indicates that the sensitivity is low and the results are relatively stable and credible; conversely, if the exclusion yields a largely different or even diametrically opposed conclusion, this indicates a high sensitivity and low robustness of the outcome.

Conclusion

Although current research focuses on the risk factors for post-embolization syndrome after TACE in liver cancer patients, the studies are scattered and the results are contradictory. There is a lack of research to analyze and compare the relationships among the existing risk factors. Therefore, this systematic review will synthesize available data, clarify associations between risk factors and PES, and provide evidence-based recommendations for early intervention and clinical management.
