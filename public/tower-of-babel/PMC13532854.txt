Classification of High‐ and Low‐Risk Patients With Dermal Leiomyosarcoma: An Exploratory Nationwide Cohort Study
Authors: Kiya Abebe, Anders Munch, Anne Lene Wagenblast, Grethe Schmidt, David Hebbelstrup Jensen, Michael M. Petersen, Anand C. Loya, Søren Daugaard, Thomas Mentzel, Mikkel Herly, Peter Vester‐Glowinski, Mathias Ørholt
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundRisk factors for dermal leiomyosarcoma (dLMS) metastasis remain poorly defined because methodological constraints, small sample sizes, and short follow‐up have limited previous studies.ObjectivesTo investigate risk factors for metastasis and local recurrence, and to propose a high‐ and low‐risk classification for dLMS.MethodsAll patients diagnosed with dLMS in Denmark between 1980 and 2022 were included. Absolute 5‑year risks were estimated using the Aalen‐Johansen method, treating all‑cause mortality as a competing risk.ResultsAmong 381 patients (median age 66 years, 71% male), the 5‐year risk of metastasis was 2.4% and 10% for local recurrence. The most important risk factors for metastasis were tumor necrosis, mitotic grade 3, and perineural/intravascular invasion, with 5‐year absolute risks ranging between 9% and 25%. Positive surgical margins and perineural/intravascular invasion were associated with increased 5‐year absolute risks of local recurrence, ranging between 26% and 50%.ConclusionAlthough few distant metastases were observed, high‐risk dLMS features may include tumor necrosis, mitotic grade 3, perineural/intravascular invasion, or positive surgical margins. Cases without these features may be classified as low‐risk dLMS. We propose regular cross‐sectional imaging in the follow‐up of patients with high‐risk dLMS, whereas follow‐up for patients with low‐risk dLMS could be limited to clinical examinations.

Introduction

Cutaneous leiomyosarcoma (LMS) is a rare smooth muscle neoplasm that can be classified as dermal or subcutaneous depending on the depth of origin. Dermal leiomyosarcoma (dLMS) is generally considered an intermediate‐risk neoplasm, characterized by a considerable risk of local recurrence, but a low risk of metastasis [1]. Although dLMS is associated with a low risk of metastasis, the possibility of metastatic potential cannot be completely dismissed, as reported metastasis rates in previous studies vary from 0% to 14% [2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8].

Efforts have been made to subclassify dermal leiomyosarcomas into high‐ and low‐risk groups. The term “atypical intradermal smooth muscle neoplasms” (AISMN) was proposed for leiomyosarcomas limited to the dermis or with minimal subcutaneous extension, suggesting that they represent a low‐risk subgroup compared to dermal leiomyosarcomas exhibiting subcutaneous infiltration [6, 9, 10, 11]. Despite its recent introduction by the WHO [12], our findings indicate no significant difference in the risk of metastasis between AISMN and dLMS, suggesting that these conditions likely represent different stages of the same disease [1]. Alternatively, dLMS may be staged using the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) classification of soft‐tissue sarcomas; however, this staging‐system primarily applies to subfascial sarcomas and has not been validated for patients with cutaneous sarcomas such as dLMS. As a result, there are currently no validated or evidence‐based risk classifications for dLMS.

Further investigation of risk factors for dLMS metastasis is necessary, as it cannot be regarded as entirely benign. Currently, risk factor studies of dLMS have been limited by small sample sizes, pooling of dermal and subcutaneous LMS, and short follow‐up. This study aimed to investigate the risk factors associated with dLMS metastasis and local recurrence, utilizing the comprehensive Danish National Health Registers known for their high validity and completeness over extended follow‐up periods [13, 14, 15]. The primary objective was to identify risk factors that enable stratification of patients with dLMS into high‐risk and low‐risk groups.

Materials and Methods

Approvals

The project has been approved by the Danish Center for Data Registration (VD‐2019‐98) and the Center of Regional Development (R‐22017531). The study has been conducted in accordance with the STROBE guidelines and the Helsinki Declaration [16, 17].

National Registers

The data were extracted from the Danish National Health Registers, namely the Danish National Pathology Register (DNPR) and the National Patient Register (NPR). The registers and individual‐level linkage of data across all registers have been described previously [18, 19].

Study Design, Participants, and Tumor Classification

All patients diagnosed with dLMS in Denmark between 1980 and 2022 were included. We identified patients using SNOMED [20] codes specific to LMS (M889x3/4/9/X) and topography codes for skin (T01–T02). To ensure accurate classification, all pathology reports underwent manual review. Data on patient and tumor characteristics included age at diagnosis, sex, tumor location, margin status, tumor diameter, invasion depth, tumor necrosis, mitotic grade as defined in the FNCLCC grading system and presence of perineural or intravascular infiltration. All tumors were diagnosed following WHO guidelines for sarcomas, and all tumors were treated at specialized sarcoma centers and assessed by pathologists who had the option to refer to a certified soft‐tissue sarcoma pathologist at a tertiary center [21, 22, 23]. Dermal LMS was defined as an LMS arising within the dermis and included both tumors with and without subcutaneous extension, such as atypical intradermal smooth muscle neoplasms (AISMN) [6, 10, 11]. For all cases of metastatic dLMS, pathology reports were manually reviewed to verify diagnostic accuracy and prevent misclassification. A comprehensive panel of immunohistochemical markers was routinely used to differentiate dLMS from other spindle‐cell neoplasms. This panel included markers such as SMA, caldesmon, vimentin, desmin, cytokeratins (pancytokeratin, AE1/AE3, CK5/6, EMA), melanocytic markers (S100, SOX10, HMB‐45), and vascular markers (CD31, CD34, ERG). Patients were only included if they underwent surgical treatment with wide local excision (WLE). Non‐dermal tumors and patients with metastasis at presentation were excluded. Definitions

Local recurrence was defined by the presence of a recurring dLMS diagnosis code at the site of the primary tumor, whereas metastasis was coded as a dLMS diagnosis with a supplemental metastasis code (M889x6). Clinically and radiologically suspected metastases that were non‐biopsied were identified in the national patient register with the ICD‐8/10 codes DC78‐79.

Outcomes and Definitions

The primary objective of this study was to investigate risk factors for metastasis and local recurrence in terms of absolute 5‐year risks. Local recurrence was defined as tumor recurrence at or near the original site of the primary tumor following primary treatment. Metastasis was defined as a biopsy verified or clinically suspected leiomyosarcoma metastasis at a site distinct from the primary tumor with no other competing cancers. A local metastasis was defined as a metastasis near the surgically treated site (e.g., local skin or local soft tissue metastases). A regional metastasis was defined as an in‐transit or nodal metastasis. A distant metastasis was defined as a metastasis occurring in distant sites (e.g., lungs, brain and internal organs).

Statistical Analysis

Baseline characteristics were summarized as medians and interquartile ranges for continuous variables and proportions for categorical variables. Patients were followed from excision of the primary dLMS until emigration, death, or 31st December 2022, whichever occurred first. The evaluated risk factors included age at presentation (< 66 years, ≥ 66 years), microscopic surgical margin status (negative/positive), invasion through the deep fascia (no/yes), mitotic grade as defined by the FNCLCC grade (grade 1–2/grade 3), presence of necrosis (no/yes), perineural/intravascular infiltration (no/yes), anatomical location (extremities and trunk/head and neck), subcutaneous invasion (no/yes), sex (female/male), and tumor T‐stage (T1/T2). Age and tumor diameter were dichotomized due to few events. Age was split at the median, and tumor diameter was split into AJCC T‐stages T1/T2, with T2 defined as a tumor diameter ≥ 20 mm for head and neck dLMS and ≥ 50 mm for the trunk and extremities [24]. FNCLCC grade was not included as dermal LMS is not routinely graded. The results were presented as 5‐year absolute risks and risk differences with 95% confidence intervals. The classification of dLMS into high‐ and low‐risk groups was defined a priori, considering the overall 5‐year risks (2.5% for metastasis and 10% for local recurrence). Relevant risk factors were those associated with an absolute 5‐year risk increase of > 5% for metastasis or > 10% for local recurrence. Due to the exploratory nature of the study, the thresholds for interpretation were chosen a priori to evaluate all predictors on an absolute risk scale which improves clinical relevance instead of relying solely on p‐values. Margin status was classified based on the final excision margin, i.e., after any potential re‐excisions. The metastasis outcome comprised both locoregional and distant metastatic events. The associations between risk factors and metastasis and local recurrence were estimated using the Aalen‐Johansen estimator with all‐cause mortality as a competing risk. We did not attempt multivariable modeling or development of an individual point scoring system due to few events. The median follow‐up time was estimated using the inverse Kaplan‐Meier method. All analyses were performed in R, version 4.3.2 with the packages tidyverse, survival, prodlim, and riskRegression [25, 26, 27, 28].

Results

Patient, Tumor, and Surgical Characteristics

A total of 381 patients with dermal LMS were included. The median age at diagnosis was 66 years, and most of the patients were males (71%). The median follow‐up time was 14 years (IQR 7–21) for patients with dLMS. The median tumor diameter was 15 mm (IQR 10–25), and most of the tumors were localized on the extremities (50%) or trunk (30%). Invasion through the deep fascia or beyond was seen in 13 patients with dLMS (3%), whereas perineural or intravascular invasion was observed in 1%. Tumor necrosis was observed in 11% of the patients and mitotic grade 3 was present in 21% of the patients. Detailed information on treatment characteristics were available in a subset of 105 patients from 2014 to 2022 with accessible medical charts. The median excision margin was 10 mm for dLMS (IQR 10–20), and negative microscopic surgical margins were achieved in 91% of the patients. Most patients with dLMS were treated with WLE to the fascia (35%) or including the fascia (51%). The baseline characteristics of the included patients and tumors are shown in Table 1.

Overall Prognosis

The 5‐year overall risk of metastasis was 2.4% (95% CI 0.9–3.9) and the 5‐year risk of local recurrence was 10% (95% CI 7.0–13). The median time to metastasis was 24 months (IQR 2.1–48), whereas the median time to local recurrence was 7.3 (IQR 5.5–9.6) months. Three late recurrences (one local recurrence and two metastases) were identified at 78, 133, and 146 months following primary treatment, all occurring in patients with negative microscopic surgical margins. The 5‐year risk of disease‐specific mortality was 1.6%, whereas the all‐cause mortality was 25%.

At the initial diagnosis of metastases, locoregional metastases (n = 6) and distant metastases (n = 3) were observed in nine patients. Subsequently, two patients with initially locoregional metastatic disease developed additional distant metastases, and two further patients developed late distant metastases occurring more than 5 years after diagnosis. This resulted in a total of 11 patients with metastatic disease overall and 7 patients with distant metastases. Of these, 7 patients developed metastases in multiple anatomical locations, yielding a total of 7 locoregional and 14 distant metastatic sites. The 5‐year overall survival was 75% (95% CI 71–80). Risk Factors for dLMS Metastasis and Local Recurrence

The risk factors for metastasis with 5‐year risk differences larger than 5% included necrosis, mitotic grade 3 and perineural/intravascular invasion. Presence of necrosis was associated with a 5‐year risk of metastasis of 9.3% (95% CI 0.6–18) and absence of necrosis with 1.5% (95% CI 0.2–2.8) resulting in a risk difference (RD) of 7.8% (95% CI −1 to 17). The 5‐year risk of metastasis for mitotic grade 3 was 6.5% (95% CI 1–12) and 1.4% (95% CI 0–2.8) for mitotic grade 1 and 2, RD = 5.1% (95% CI −0.6 to 11). Perineural/intravascular invasion yielded a 5‐year risk of metastasis of 25% (95% CI 0–67) compared with 2.1% (95% CI 0.7–3.6) in patients without invasion, RD = 23% (95% CI −20 to 65).

Risk factors for local recurrence with 5‐year risk differences larger than 10% were surgical margin status and perineural/intravascular invasion. Patients with positive surgical margins had a 5‐year risk of local recurrence of 26% (95% CI 11–41) in contrast to 8.4% (95% CI 5.5–11) for negative margins, RD = 18% (95% CI 3–33). Perineural/intravascular invasion was associated with a 5‐year risk of local recurrence of 50% (95% CI 0–100) compared with 9.6% (95% CI 6.6–13) in patients without, RD = 40% (95% CI −11 to 92). High‐ and Low‐Risk dLMS Classification

The classification of dLMS based on necrosis, mitotic grade, perineural/intravascular invasion and positive surgical margins resulted in 132 patients (35%) with high‐risk dLMS and 249 (65%) with low‐risk dLMS. Patients with high‐risk dLMS were older compared with low‐risk (69 vs. 64 years), presented with larger tumors (17 vs. 12 mm) and had more cases of subcutaneous invasion or invasion through the deep fascia (53% vs. 40%). All but one instance of the observed metastases occurred in patients with high‐risk dLMS. In the low‐risk dLMS group, only one locoregional skin/soft tissue metastasis (n = 1) was observed. All remaining 10 patients with metastatic disease were in the high‐risk group and included the 2 patients who developed late metastases. Metastatic sites were observed in the local skin or soft tissue (n = 3), regional lymph nodes (n = 2), the parotid gland (n = 1), in the lungs (n = 5), bones (n = 2), liver (n = 2), small intestine (n = 1), brain (n = 1), breast (n = 1), distant soft tissue (n = 1), distant skin (n = 1). Notably, all the patients who died of dermal leiomyosarcoma (n = 7) were in the high‐risk group.

Among the seven patients who developed distant metastases, age varied between 48 and 86 years old at diagnosis. Tumor sizes ranged from 20 to 70 mm and was unknown in one case. Four of the tumors developed in the lower extremity, one in the trunk, one in the upper extremity, and one in the facial region. Five tumors were confined to the dermis, whereas the remaining tumors infiltrated the subcutis or periosteum. Six out of seven tumors exhibited tumor necrosis. Mitotic grades 1, 2, and 3 were seen, and perineural/perivascular invasion was present in one case. Excisions margins were only available in two cases due to complex resections or inaccessible surgical reports. Positive microscopic margins were present in two patients and negative in five.

Four cases developed a local recurrence prior to dissemination. Metastases developed between 2 and 48 months after diagnosis, and between 133 and 146 months for the late recurrences. The metastases were detected incidentally in the lungs (n = 3) and liver (n = 1) in three patients. One patient with locoregional lymph node metastases was later diagnosed with diffuse skeletal metastases following the onset of skeletal pain, whereas another patient developed locoregional soft tissue metastases with subsequent dissemination to the abdominal wall and spine. The first patient with late metastasis reported symptoms of tumor growth in the breast and subsequent biopsy and CT showed dissemination of dLMS in the lungs, liver, distant breast and skin. The second patient with late metastasis developed a local recurrence 6 years after primary excision of dLMS in 2014, after which a new follow‐up program was initiated. During follow‐up, imaging identified a pulmonary nodule 4 years after the local recurrence with subsequent progression, and biopsy 5 years after the local recurrence confirmed metastatic dLMS with dissemination to the lungs. Four patients reported symptoms related to tumor growth at the primary tumor site several months prior to tumor diagnosis, whereas two patients reported such symptoms several years prior to diagnosis. All patients were in the high‐risk group. Overview of the patients with distant metastatic disease are provided in File S5.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first study to explore risk factors for dermal LMS metastasis and local recurrence in a large unselected nationwide cohort with complete long‐term follow‐up. We found that the most important risk factors for metastasis were necrosis, mitotic grade 3 and perineural/intravascular invasion. The most important risk factors for local recurrence were positive surgical margins and perineural/intravascular invasion.

Our results indicate that one‐third of the patients could be classified as high‐risk dLMS due to the presence of tumor necrosis, mitotic grade 3, perineural/intravascular invasion or positive surgical margins. The remaining two‐thirds of the patients with the absence of these features constitute low‐risk dLMS and have a low 5‐year risks of metastasis (1%–2%) and a moderate risk of local recurrence (8%–10%), supporting the WHO‐classification of dLMS as an intermediate‐risk tumor, characterized by locally aggressive behavior but rarely metastasizing.

Prognostic Factors of dLMS Relapse in the Literature

This is the first study to subclassify patients with dLMS into a high‐risk and low‐risk group, suggesting that necrosis, mitotic grade 3, perineural/intravascular invasion and positive surgical margins may serve as risk factors for metastasis and local recurrence. Consistent with previous findings in the literature [4, 10, 29, 30], our study found that positive surgical margins were associated with an increased risk of local recurrence, underscoring the importance of adequate surgical management of the primary tumor site. To our knowledge, seven studies have previously reported metastases in patients with dLMS [2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8]. Previously suggested risk factors for adverse outcomes in patients with dLMS have included tumor differentiation, tumor diameter, FNCLCC grade, mitotic grade, immunosuppression, and soft tissue invasion. However, due to the small sample sizes and limited number of events, many of these findings have primarily been descriptive. As previously reported [1], our current results continue to underline no prognostic impact of the AISMN diagnosis in terms of metastasis or local recurrence. In contrast to the recent WHO substitution of the dLMS diagnosis with AISMN, our findings do not support this reclassification, as we found no evidence that AISMN represents a prognostically distinct entity.

Follow‐Up of Patients With High‐ and Low‐Risk dLMS

The main purpose of the suggested follow‐up program of patients with dLMS should be detection and treatment of local recurrence in low‐risk patients and early detection of metastasis in patients with high‐risk dLMS. Furthermore, to minimize the risk of local recurrence, patients with positive surgical margins after primary treatment should undergo re‐excision until adequate margin clearance is achieved as positive margins were the single most important risk factor for local recurrence. Due to the considerable risk of local recurrence in both high‐ and low‐risk dLMS, the follow‐up should primarily focus on clinical examinations of the treated site to detect local recurrence and loco‐regional metastasis. The clinical visits could be performed twice in the first year, as most of the events occurred within this time point, followed by annual visits for 3 years for a total of 4 years, as no further relapses occurred after this timeframe. For high‐risk patients, additional imaging could be considered in the first 2 years. This could be either PET/CT or local MRI of the treated site to detect small or deep‐seated loco‐regional recurrences and CT of the thorax to detect distant pulmonary metastases. In patients with low‐risk dLMS, follow‐up could be limited to annual clinical examinations for 4 years at a dermatologist or general practitioner. Despite the occurrence of two late metastases (> 5 years), the proposed follow‐up program would likely capture the majority of locoregional and distant metastases. Limitations

There are limitations to this study despite the large sample size and the completeness of the Danish National Pathology register. This study is limited by the small number of events, particularly in terms of metastasis, and consequently only univariable analyses could be performed with the inherent risk of confounding. Consequently, our classification of high‐ and low‐risk dLMS should be considered as exploratory, which necessitates external validation of our findings. However, this is the largest study to date of dermal leiomyosarcomas, and the separate risk estimates in patients with necrosis, mitotic grade 3, and perineural/intravascular invasion showed substantial 5‐year risk differences in metastatic rates. Secondly, prospective studies are needed to assess and validate the impact of our suggested follow‐up program with focus on the number of scans needed to detect early metastases. However, limiting cross‐sectional imaging to high‐risk patients, which only constituted 132/381 patients (35%) with dLMS, could be considered feasible as imaging and unnecessary radiation exposure are avoided in the remaining 249 patients (65%) with low‐risk dLMS.

Conclusions

Overall, the risk of local recurrence was moderate, while the risk of metastasis was low. Despite the few observed events, we found that necrosis, mitotic grade 3, perineural/intravascular invasion, and positive surgical margins could be used to identify high‐risk dLMS. We suggest that all patients with dLMS should be followed for 4 years with semi‐annual clinical visits during the first year and annual visits the next 3 years. Due to the considerable risk of pulmonary metastasis, follow‐up for patients with high‐risk dLMS could be expanded to include either PET/CT or thoracic CT within the first 2 years. The follow‐up of patients with low‐risk dLMS could be limited to clinical examinations with the same number of visits as high‐risk dLMS.

Synopsis

Risk factors for metastasis and local recurrence in patients with dermal leiomyosarcoma remain poorly understood and no previous studies have suggested a high‐ and low‐risk classification. While the 5‐year risk of distant metastasis was low (three patients), tumor necrosis, mitotic grade 3 and perineural/intravascular invasion were important predictors of locoregional and distant metastasis, whereas positive surgical margins and perineural/intravascular invasion were associated with local recurrence. Tumor necrosis, mitotic grade 3, perineural/intravascular invasion and positive surgical margins could therefore potentially be used to identify high‐risk patients. These data may inform surveillance strategies for patients with dermal leiomyosarcoma.
