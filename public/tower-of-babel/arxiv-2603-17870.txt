Remarks on quadratic left Bol algebras
A. Nourou Issa

Abstract
In this paper the notion of a quadratic (left) Bol algebra is discussed. Several examples of quadratic Bol algebras are given and it is observed that the only two-dimensional quadratic real Bol algebras are quadratic Lie triple systems. Dual representations of Bol algebras are investigated with a particular emphasis on coadjoint representations for quadratic Bol algebras. The notion of T ∗ T^{*} -extension of a quadratic Bol algebra is introduced.



keywords:
Quadratic algebra; dual representation;
T^{*}
-extension.

AMS Subject Classification: 17B05, 17B30, 17D99

Introduction

Smooth (or analytic) loops are known to be a nonassociative generalization of Lie groups whose tangent structures are Lie algebras. Therefore many mathematicians considered the problem of the suitable tangent algebra for some local smooth loop (i.e. a setting for a general nonassociative Lie theory). The first attempt in solving this problem seems to be done by M. A. Akivis
[
3
]
who introduced the notion of a
W
-algebra (now termed Akivis algebra
[
9
]
) as a tangent algebra to a local
3
-web. A decade later, L. V. Sabinin and P. O. Mikheev
[
30
]
introduced the class of hyperalgebras (now called Sabinin algebras) as a broad generalization of Akivis algebras, and this finally solved the problem of the extension of the Lie theory up to smooth loops. For a modern treatment of Sabinin algebras, one refers to
[
22
,
23
,
26
,
34
,
35
]
.

It should be observed that the very beginnings towards a generalization of Lie theory were related to some types of smooth loops such as Moufang loops with the pioneer paper by A. I. Maltsev
[
20
]
who introduced the class of Moufang-Lie algebras (now called Maltsev algebras
[
31
]
) as tangent algebras to local smooth Moufang loops. Generalizing the E. Cartan’s study of the differential geometry of Lie groups, L. V. Sabinin and P. O. Mikheev investigated the differential geometry of smooth Bol loops giving rise to the notion of a Bol algebra as a tangent algebra to a local smooth Bol loop (
[
29
,
21
]
). Since their introduction, papers devoted to Bol algebras are relatively scarce in contrast to the case of Lie-Yamaguti algebras (i.e. generalized Lie triple systems
[
38
]
or Lie triple algebras
[
12
]
). Solvability and semisimplicity as well as quadratic structures of Bol algebras were introduced and investigated by E. N. Kuzmin and O. Zaidi in
[
17
]
. For other investigated so far topics on Bol algebras one may refer to
[
1
,
4
,
6
,
8
,
10
,
25
,
42
]
.

For a given left Bol algebra, a representation was defined in
[
10
]
. In this paper we define a coadjoint representation for a quadratic left Bol algebra, and next its coadjoint representation is used to construct a chain of quadratic left Bol algebras. An attempt towards a study of dual representations of (not necessarily quadratic) left Bol algebras is also done. The notion of a
T^{*}
-extension of a quadratic left Bol algebra is introduced.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In section 2 we recall some basic notions and fundamental results on Bol algebras. Examples of quadratic left Bol algebras are given and symmetric bilinear forms induced by a quadratic structure on a Bol algebra are considered. In section 3 we study dual representations of Bol algebras, either quadratic or not. While for a quadratic Bol algebra a representation as defined in
[
10
]
naturally induces a coadjoint representation, for a general Bol algebra
T
(i.e. not necessarily quadratic) the notion of dual representation is subjected to some conditions (we still do not know whether another specific definition of the dual representation maps could avoid resorting to these conditions). In section 4, for a given quadratic Bol algebra, its
T^{*}
-extension is defined as a generalization of the one for Lie triple systems
[
18
]
.

Throughout this paper all vector spaces and algebras are finite-dimensional over
\mathbb{R}
, unless otherwise stated.

2
Definitions, Examples, and Induced Bilinear Forms

In this section we recall some basic notions and constructions on Bol algebras including the notion of an invariant bilinear form as in
[
17
]
for right Bol algebras. Next we give a definition of a quadratic (left) Bol algebra according to the equivalence between the left and the right invariant conditions in Lie triple systems. Several examples of quadratic Bol algebras are given. It is proved that, except two-dimensional Lie triple systems, there does not exist any quadratic two-dimensional real Bol algebra. Finally, symmetric bilinear forms induced by a quadratic structure on a given Bol algebra are investigated.

Definition 2.1.
(
[
21
,
29
]
).
A left Bol algebra is a triple
(T,*,[\![,,]\!])
consisting of a
vector space
T
, a binary operation
*:T\times T\rightarrow T
and a ternary operation
[\![,,]\!]:T\times T\times T\rightarrow T
satisfying the following identities:

(T01)
x*y=-y*x
,

(T02)
[\![x,y,z]\!]=-[\![y,x,z]\!]
,

(T1)
[\![x,y,z]\!]+[\![y,z,x]\!]+[\![z,x,y]\!]=0
,

(T2)
[\![u,v,[\![x,y,z]\!]]\!]=[\![[\![u,v,x]\!],y,z]\!]+[\![x,[\![u,v,y]\!],z]\!]+[\![x,y,[\![u,v,z]\!]]\!]
,

(T3)
[\![u,v,x*y]\!]=[\![u,v,x]\!]*y+x*[\![u,v,y]\!]+[\![x,y,u*v]\!]-xy*uv

for all
u,v,x,y,z\in T
, where
xy*uv:=(x*y)*(u*v)
.

The definition given above is the one of a
left
Bol algebra. Setting
x\cdot y:=-x*y
and
\langle z,x,y\rangle:=-[\![x,y,z]\!]
, one gets a
right
Bol algebra

(T,\cdot,\langle,,\rangle)
. In
[
17
]
only right Bol algebras were considered. Throughout this paper by a Bol algebra we always mean a left Bol algebra.

Remark 2.2.

The identities (T02), (T1), and (T2) define a
Lie triple system

(T,[\![,,]\!])
. Therefore a Bol algebra could be seen as a Lie triple system with an additional binary operation satisfying (T01) and (T3).

Example 2.3.

In
[

Lemma 2.2
.

In a Lie triple system

Proof.
First suppose (
2.3

\displaystyle b([\![x,y,z]\!],u)

\displaystyle=b([\![z,u,x]\!],y)\;\mbox{(see (\ref{eq2.3}))}

\displaystyle=-b([\![u,z,x]\!],y)

\displaystyle=-b([\![x,y,u]\!],z)\;\mbox{(by (\ref{eq2.3}))}

so we get (
2.4
) (see also
[

and (

Conversely, suppose (

From the other hand, (T1) implies

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=b([\![x,y,z]\!],u)+b(z,[\![x,y,u]\!])\;\mbox{(see (\ref{eq2.4}))}

\displaystyle=b([\![z,u,x]\!],y)+b(z,[\![x,y,u]\!])\;\mbox{(by (\ref{eq2.8}))}

\displaystyle=-b([\![u,z,x]\!],u)+b([\![x,y,u]\!],z)

Definition 2.5.

A quadratic Bol algebra is a pair

Example 2.8.

Let
(T,[\![,,]\!])
be the two-dimensional Lie triple system over

with basis

and multiplication table

[\![e_{1},e_{2},e_{1}]\!]=e_{1}

[\![e_{1},e_{2},e_{2}]\!]=-e_{2}

Define on

Theorem 2.4
.

There does not exist any quadratic structure on a real two-dimensional Bol algebra
(T,*,[\![,,]\!])
with nonzero binary operation.

Proof.

We must consider algebras of type II of Theorem
2.1
. We have

b(e_{2},e_{1})=b(-e_{1}*e_{2},e_{1})=-b(e_{1},e_{2}*e_{1})=-b(e_{1},e_{2})=-b(e_{2},e_{1})

b(e_{2},e_{1})=0
, and

b(e_{2},e_{2})=b(-e_{1}*e_{2},e_{2})=-b(e_{1},e_{2}*e_{2})=0
.

Thus

and we conclude that

is degenerate.
\Box

\displaystyle=b([\![\Pi(x),y,z]\!],u)+b([\![x,\Pi(y),z]\!],u)

\displaystyle+b([\![x,y,\Pi(z)]\!],u)-b(z,[\![x,y,\Pi(u)]\!])

2.2
) if and only if

b([\![x,y,\chi]\!]-xy*\chi,z)=-b(y,[\![x,z,\chi]\!]-xz*\chi)

i.e.

b({\mathcal{R}}_{\chi}(x)(z),y)=-b({\mathcal{R}}_{\chi}(x)(y),z)
.

This completes the proof.
\Box

From the proof of the preceding result, the following corollary becomes obvious.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
(T,[\![,,]\!],b)
be a quadratic Lie triple system. For a derivation
D
of
(T,[\![,,]\!])
, define the map
\phi(D):T\times T\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
\phi(D)(x,y)=b(D(x),y)+b(x,D(y))
for all
x,y\in T
. Then
\phi(D)
is a bilinear, symmetric and invariant form.
\Box

In
\widetilde{pder(T)}
denote by

\widetilde{pder_{a}(T)}
the subspace spanned by all
b
-skew-symmetric pseudoderivations of
(T,b)
and by

\mathfrak{B}_{s}(T)
the vector space spanned by all symmetric bilinear forms on
T
. Then, as vector spaces,
\widetilde{pder(T)}
is (isomorphic to) a direct sum of

\widetilde{pder_{a}(T)}
and a subspace in
\mathfrak{B}_{s}(T)
. Specifically, the following result holds.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(T,b)
be a quadratic Bol algebra. Then the short sequence

0\longrightarrow\widetilde{pder_{a}(T)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{pder(T)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Im\phi\longrightarrow 0

is exact, where
i
denotes the inclusion map and
\phi(\Pi)
is defined by (
2.9
) for all

(\Pi,\chi)\in\widetilde{pder(T)}
.

Proof.

A proof follows from (
2.9
) and the definition of a
b
-skew-symmetric pseudoderivation.
\Box

Remark 2.11.

If
(T,b)
reduces to a quadratic Lie triple system
(T,[\![,,]\!],b)
, then Proposition
2.7
implies the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{Der_{a}(T)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}{Der(T)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Im\phi\longrightarrow 0
,

where
\phi(D)(x,y)=b(D(x),y)+b(x,D(y))
for all
D\in Der(T)
, so that
Der(T)\cong Der_{a}(T)\oplus Im\phi
. Observe that, in this case,
\phi(D)(x,y)
is invariant by Corollary
2.6
.

3
Dual Representations

In this section, given the adjoint representation for a Bol algebra
T
, we define its dual in case when a quadratic structure is defined on
T
. For a not necessarily quadratic Bol algebra
T
, our triple of dual maps does not constitute a representation in general as defined in
[
10
]
unless some conditions are fulfilled, although it is still a representation for the underlying Lie triple system.

In
[
10
]
a representation for a Bol algebra
(T,*,[\![,,]\!])
was defined as a quadruple
(V,\rho,\theta,D_{\theta})
, where
V
is a vector space,
\rho:T\rightarrow End(V)
a linear map,
\theta,D_{\theta}:T\times T\rightarrow End(V)
bilinear maps satisfying

(R1)
D_{\theta}(x,y)+\theta(x,y)-\theta(y,x)=0
,

(R21)
[D_{\theta}(x,y),\rho(z)]=\rho([\![x,y,z]\!])+\rho(x*y)\rho(z)-\theta(z,x*y)
,

(R22)
\theta(x,y*z)=\rho(y)\theta(x,z)-\rho(z)\theta(x,y)-(D_{\theta}(y,z)-\rho(y*z))\rho(x)
,

(R31)
[D_{\theta}(u,v),D_{\theta}(x,y)]=D_{\theta}([\![u,v,x]\!],y)+D_{\theta}(x,[\![u,v,y]\!])
,

(R32)
[D_{\theta}(u,v),\theta(x,y)]=\theta([\![u,v,x]\!],y)+\theta(x,[\![u,v,y]\!])
,

(R33)
\theta(u,[\![x,y,z]\!])=\theta(y,z)\theta(u,x)-\theta(x,z)\theta(u,y)+D_{\theta}(x,y)\theta(u,z)

for all
u,v,x,y,z\in T
.

Because of (R1) we can write
(V,\rho,\theta)
in place of
(V,\rho,\theta,D_{\theta})
. Observe that, if set
l(u)(v):=u*v
,
R(u,v)(w):=[\![w,u,v]\!]
and
L(u,v)(w):=[\![u,v,w]\!]
for all
u,v,w\in T
, then the maps
l:T\rightarrow End(T)
,
u\mapsto l(u)
,
R:T\times T\rightarrow End(T)
,
(u,v)\mapsto R(u,v)
, and
L:T\times T\rightarrow End(T)
,
(u,v)\mapsto L(u,v)
define a representation
(T,l,R,L)
of
(T,*,[\![,,]\!])
that is called the
adjoint representation
of
(T,*,[\![,,]\!])
. The map
r(u)
is defined as
r(u)(v):=v*u
,
u,v\in T
; see also Proposition
2.5
.

Now let
V^{*}
be the dual space of
V
and define a linear map
{\rho}^{*}:T\rightarrow End(V^{*})
and bilinear maps
{\theta}^{*},D^{*}_{\theta}:T\times T\rightarrow End(V^{*})
by

(3.1)

{\rho}^{*}(x)(f):=-f\rho(x),

(3.2)

{\theta}^{*}(x,y)(f):=-f\theta(x,y),

(3.3)

D^{*}_{\theta}(x,y)(f):=-fD_{\theta}(x,y).

Next, as in
[
33
]
, define the switching operator
\iota:{\otimes}^{2}T\rightarrow T
by
\iota(x\otimes y):=y\otimes x
,
\forall x\otimes y\in{\otimes}^{2}T
. Then one observes that
D^{*}_{\theta}(x,y)=D_{-{\theta}^{*}\iota}(x,y)
,
\forall x,y\in T
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(V,\theta,D_{\theta})
be a representation of the Lie triple system
(T,[\![,,]\!])
. Then

(V^{*},-{\theta}^{*}\iota)
is a representation of

(T,[\![,,]\!])
.

Proof.
A proof follows by a straightforward computation of (R1), (R31)-(R33) using the fact that
(V,\theta,D_{\theta})
is a representation of
(T,[\![,,]\!])
. See also section 3 in
[
18
]
or Proposition 2.12 in
[
33
]
in case of Lie-Yamaguti algebras.
\Box

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(T,l,R,L)
be the adjoint representation of a quadratic Bol algebra
(T,b)
. Then
(T^{*},l^{*},-R^{*}\iota,L_{-R^{*}\iota})
is a representation of
(T,b)
(the dual of the adjoint representation

(T,l,R,L)
of
(T,b)
that is called the coadjoint representation of

(T,b)
).

Proof.
That
(-R^{*}\iota,L_{-R^{*}\iota})
satisfies (R1), (R31)-(R33) follows from Lemma
3.1
(observe that the quadratic structure of
T
is not needed here). It remains to check (R21) and (R22) for the triple
(l^{*},-R^{*}\iota,L_{-R^{*}\iota})
.

We have

0=b(([L(x,y),l(z)]-l([\![x,y,z]\!])-l(x*y)l(z)+R(z,x*y))(v),u)
(by (R21))

=b([\![x,y,z*v]\!]-z*[\![x,y,v]\!]-[\![x,y,z]\!]*v-(x*y)*(z*v)+[\![v,z,x*y]\!],u)

=-b(z*v,[\![x,y,u]\!])+b([\![x,y,v]\!],z*u)+b(v,[\![x,y,z]\!]*u)

-b(v*z,(x*y)*u)+b(v,[\![u,x*y,z]\!])
(by (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and (
2.4
))

=b(v,z*[\![x,y,u]\!])-b(v,[\![x,y,z*u]\!])+b(v,[\![x,y,z]\!]*u)

-b(v,z*((x*y)*u))+b(v,[\![u,x*y,z]\!]
(by (
2.2
) and (
2.4
))

=b(v,l(z)L(x,y)(u)-L(x,y)l(z)(u)+l([\![x,y,z]\!])(u)

-l(z)l(x*y)(u)+R(x*y,z)(u))

so the nondegeneracy of
b
implies

fl(z)L(x,y)-fL(x,y)l(z)+fl([\![x,y,z]\!])

-fl(z)l(x*y)+fR(x*y,z)=0
,

\forall x,y,z\in T
and
f\in T^{*}
i.e., by (
3.1
)-(
3.3
),

[L_{-R^{*}\iota}(x,y),l^{*}(z)]-l^{*}([\![x,y,z]\!])-l^{*}(x*y)l^{*}(z)+(-R^{*}{\iota})(z,x*y)=0
.

Thus
(T^{*},l^{*},-R^{*}\iota,L_{-R^{*}\iota})
verifies (
2.1
).

Likewise, from the equality

0=b(R(x,y*z)(v)-l(y)R(x,z)(v)+l(z)R(x,y)(v)

+L(y,z)l(x)(v)-l(y*z)l(x)(v),u)
(by (R22)),

using (
2.2
), (
2.3
) or (
2.4
)) whenever applicable and next the nondegeneracy of
b
, one gets

fR(y*z,x)+fR(z,x)l(y)-fR(y,x)l(z)+fl(x)L(y,z)-fl(x)l(y*z)

=0
,

\forall x,y,z\in T
and
f\in T^{*}
i.e.

(-R^{*}\iota)(x,y*z)-l^{*}(y)(-R^{*}\iota)(x,z)+l^{*}(z)(-R^{*}\iota)(x,y)+L_{-R^{*}\iota}(y,z)l^{*}(x)

-l^{*}(y*z)l^{*}(x)=0

which is (R22) for
(l^{*},-R^{*}\iota,L_{-R^{*}\iota})
. This completes the proof.
\Box

Definition 3.1.

Let
T
be a Bol algebra,
(V_{1},{\rho}_{1},{\theta}_{1})
and
(V_{2},{\rho}_{2},{\theta}_{2})
two representations of
T
. A homomorphism from
(V_{1},{\rho}_{1},{\theta}_{1})
to
(V_{2},{\rho}_{2},{\theta}_{2})
is a linear map
\psi:V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}
such that

\psi({\rho}_{1}(x)(v))={\rho}_{2}(x)(\psi(v))
,

\psi({\theta}_{1}(x,y)(v))={\theta}_{2}(x,y)(\psi(v))

for all
x,y\in T
,
v\in V_{1}
. If, moreover,
\psi
is a bijection then
(V_{1},{\rho}_{1},{\theta}_{1})
and
(V_{2},{\rho}_{2},{\theta}_{2})
are said to be isomorphic.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(T,b)
be a quadratic Bol algebra. Then the adjoint representation
(T,l,R,L)
is isomorphic to the coadjoint representation
(T^{*},l^{*},-R^{*}\iota,L_{-R^{*}\iota})
of

(T,b)
.

Proof.
Let
b^{\sharp}:T\rightarrow T^{*}
,
x\mapsto b^{\sharp}(x)
, be the linear map defined by
b^{\sharp}(x)(y):=b(x,y)
for all
x,y\in T
. Then,

\forall u,z\in T
,

b^{\sharp}(l(x)(z))(u)=b^{\sharp}(x*z)(u)=-b(z*x,u)

=-b(z,x*u)=-b^{\sharp}(z)(l(x)(u))=l^{*}(x)(b^{\sharp}(z)(u))

(we used (
2.2
)) and

b^{\sharp}(R(x,y)(z))(u)=b^{\sharp}([\![z,x,y]\!])(u)=b([\![z,x,y]\!],u)=b(z,[\![u,y,x]\!])

=b^{\sharp}(z)(R(y,x)(u))=-R^{*}{\iota}(x,y)(b^{\sharp}(z)(u))

(we used (
2.3
)). This completes the proof.
\Box

For a given Bol algebra
(T,*,[\![,,]\!])
(not necessarily quadratic) with representation
(V,\rho,\theta,D_{\theta})
and maps
{\rho}^{*}
,
{\theta}^{*}
, and
D^{*}_{\theta}
as defined by (
3.1
)-(
3.3
), the quadruple
(V^{*},{\rho}^{*},-{\theta}^{*}\iota,D_{-{\theta}^{*}\iota})
is not a dual representation for
(T,*,[\![,,]\!])
in general (specifically,

({\rho}^{*},-{\theta}^{*}\iota,D_{-{\theta}^{*}\iota})
does not satisfy (R21) and (R22)), in contrast to the case of quadratic Bol algebras with respect to the adjoint representation (see Theorem
3.2
above; we wonder whether another definition of the triple
({\rho}^{*},{\theta}^{*},D^{*}_{\theta})
would generate a dual representation for
T
). However, up to to some additional conditions on the pair
(\rho,\theta)
, we get a dual representation for
(T,*,[\![,,]\!])
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
T
be a Bol algebra and
(V,\rho,\theta,D_{\theta})
its representation. Then
(V^{*},{\rho}^{*},-{\theta}^{*}\iota,D_{-{\theta}^{*}\iota})
is a representation of
T
if and only if the following conditions hold:

(3.4)

\rho(u*v)\rho(w)+\rho(w)\rho(u*v)=\theta(u*v,w)+\theta(w,u*v),

(3.5)

\theta(u,v)\rho(w)-\rho(w)\theta(v,u)=0

for all
u,v,w\in T
.

Proof.
By Lemma
3.1
, (R1), (R31)-(R33) are naturally verified for

(-{\theta}^{*}\iota,D_{-{\theta}^{*}\iota})
. One only needs to check that (R21) and (R22) hold for

({\rho}^{*},-{\theta}^{*}\iota,D_{-{\theta}^{*}\iota})
if and only if (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) are satisfied.

For (R21) we have,
\forall x,y,z\in T
,

([D_{-{\theta}^{*}\iota}(x,y),{\rho}^{*}(z)]-{\rho}^{*}([\![x,y,z]\!])-{\rho}^{*}(x*y){\rho}^{*}(z)+(-{\theta}^{*}\iota)(z,x*y))(f)

=f\rho(z)D_{\theta}(x,y)-fD_{\theta}(x,y)\rho(z)+f\rho([\![x,y,z]\!])-f\rho(z)\rho(x*y)

+f\theta(x*y,z)

=f(-[D_{\theta}(x,y),\rho(z)]+\rho([\![x,y,z]\!])-\rho(z)\rho(x*y)+\theta(x*y,z))

=f(-[D_{\theta}(x,y),\rho(z)]+\rho([\![x,y,z]\!])+\rho(x*y)\rho(z)+\theta(z,x*y))
(if and only (
3.4
) holds)

=0
(by (R21))

so (R21) holds for
({\rho}^{*},-{\theta}^{*}\iota,D_{-{\theta}^{*}\iota})
.

For (R22) we have,
\forall x,y,z\in T
,

({\rho}^{*}(y)(-{\theta}^{*}\iota)(x,z)-{\rho}^{*}(z)(-{\theta}^{*}\iota)(x,y)-D_{-{\theta}^{*}\iota}(y,z){\rho}^{*}(x)

+{\rho}^{*}(y*z){\rho}^{*}(x)-(-{\theta}^{*}\iota)(x,y*z))(f)

=-f(\theta(z,x)\rho(y)-\theta(y,x)\rho(z)+\rho(x)D_{\theta}(y,z)-\rho(x)\rho(y*z)+\theta(y*z,x))

=-f(\rho(y)\theta(x,z)-\rho(z)\theta(x,y)+D_{\theta}(z,y)\rho(x)-\rho(z*y)\rho(x)+\theta(x,z*y))
(if and only if (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) hold)

=-f(\rho(y)\theta(x,z)-\rho(z)\theta(x,y)-(D_{\theta}(y,z)-\rho(y*z))\rho(x)-\theta(x,y*z))

=0
(by (R22))

so (R22) holds for
({\rho}^{*},-{\theta}^{*}\iota,D_{-{\theta}^{*}\iota})
and the proof is completed.
\Box

4
T^{*}
-extensions of quadratic Bol algebras

In this section we define a
T^{*}
-extension of a given quadratic Bol algebra as a generalization of a
T^{*}
-extension of a Lie triple system (
[
18
]
). With this notion, starting from a quadratic Bol algebra, one gets a chain of quadratic Bol algebras.

Given a Bol algebra
(T,*,[\![,,]\!])
and its representation
(V,\rho,\theta,D_{\theta})
, the notion of a
(2,3)
-cocycle
(\nu,\omega)
for
(T,*,[\![,,]\!])
with respect to
(V,\rho,\theta,D_{\theta})
was given in
[
10
]
. In particular the trilinear map

\omega
satisfies

(4.1)

\omega(x,y,z)=-\omega(y,x,z),

(4.2)

{\circlearrowleft}_{x,y,z}\;\;\omega(x,y,z)=0.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(T,*,[\![,,]\!],b)
be a quadratic Bol algebra and
(\nu,\omega)
a
(2,3)
-cocycle for
(T,*,[\![,,]\!])
with respect to its coadjoint representation
(T^{*},l^{*},-R^{*}\iota,L_{-R^{*}\iota})
. Define on the vector space
T\oplus T^{*}
a binary operation
*_{\nu}
and a ternary operation
[\![,,]\!]_{\omega}
by

(4.3)

(x+f)*_{\nu}(y+g)=x*y+\nu(x,y)+fl(y)-gl(x),

(4.4)

[\![x+f,y+g,z+h]\!]_{\omega}=[\![x,y,z]\!]+\omega(x,y,z)+fR(z,y)-gR(z,x)+hL(y,x)

for all
x,y,z\in T
and
f,g,h\in T^{*}
. Then
T_{\nu,\omega}:=(T\oplus T^{*},*_{\nu},[\![,,]\!]_{\omega})
is a Bol algebra. Moreover, the form
\tilde{b}:(T\oplus T^{*})\times(T\oplus T^{*})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

{\tilde{b}}|_{T}=b
,

\tilde{b}(x+f,y+g)=f(y)+g(x)

for all
x,y\in T
and
f,g\in T^{*}
, is bilinear, symmetric and nondegenerate. The form
\tilde{b}
is invariant if and only if

(4.5)

\nu(x,y)(z)=\nu(y,z)(x),

(4.6)

\omega(x,y,z)(u)=\omega(u,z,y)(x).

Proof.
Using (
3.1
)-(
3.3
) we write (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) as

(x+f)*_{\nu}(y+g)=x*y+\nu(x,y)-l^{*}(y)f+l^{*}(x)g

and

[\![x+f,y+g,z+h]\!]_{\omega}=[\![x,y,z]\!]+\omega(x,y,z)+(-R^{*}\iota)(y,z)f

-(-R^{*}\iota)(x,z)g+L_{-R^{*}\iota}(x,y)h

respectively. Then, since
(T^{*},l^{*},-R^{*}\iota,L_{-R^{*}\iota})
is a representation of
(T,b)
(see Theorem
3.2
), Theorem 3.4 in
[
10
]
implies that
T_{\nu,\omega}
is a Bol algebra.

Clearly
\tilde{b}
is bilinear and symmetric. Now let
y+g\in T\oplus T^{*}
be
\tilde{b}
-orthogonal to all
x+f\in T\oplus T^{*}
. Then
0=\tilde{b}(x+f,y+g)=f(y)+g(x)
which implies
f(y)=0=g(x)
, so
y=0
and
g=0
. Thus
\tilde{b}
is nondegenerate.

It remains to check the invariance of
\tilde{b}
. We have

\tilde{b}((x+f)*_{\nu}(y+g),z+h)=\nu(x,y)(z)+f(y*z)-g(x*z)+h(x*y)

and

\tilde{b}(x+f,(y+g)*_{\nu}(z+h))=f(y*z)+\nu(y,z)(x)+g(z*x)-h(y*x)
.

Therefore
\tilde{b}
satisfies (
2.2
) if and only if
\nu
satisfies (
4.5
). Likewise,

\tilde{b}([\![x+f,y+g,z+h]\!]_{\omega},u+k)=\omega(x,y,z)(u)

+f([\![u,z,y]\!])-g([\![u,z,x]\!])+h([\![y,x,u]\!])+k([\![x,y,z]\!])

and

\tilde{b}(x+f,[\![u+k,z+h,y+g]\!]_{\omega})=f([\![u,z,y]\!])+\omega(u,z,y)(x)

+k([\![x,y,z]\!])-h([\![x,y,u]\!])+g([\![z,u,x]\!])

so that
\tilde{b}
satisfies (
2.3
) if and only if
\omega
verifies (
4.6
).
\Box

Definition 4.1.

The quadratic Bol algebra
(T_{\nu,\omega},\tilde{b})
constructed in Theorem
4.1
is called the
T^{*}
-extension of the quadratic Bol algebra
(T,b)
by the
(2,3)
-cocycle
(\nu,\omega)
. If
(\nu,\omega)=(0,0)
then
(T_{0,0},\tilde{b})
is called the trivial extension of
(T,b)
.

Remark 4.2.

If
x*y=0
,
\forall x,y\in T
and
\nu=0
, then
(T_{0,\omega},\tilde{b})
is the
T^{*}
-extension of the Lie triple system
(T,[\![,,]\!])
by the
3
-cocycle
\omega
as defined in
[
18
]
. Theorem
4.1
also implies that any quadratic Bol algebra gives rise to its trivial extension with double dimension, so one gets a chain of quadratic Bol algebras.

In the proof of Theorem
4.1
above we used the right invariant condition (
2.3
). If one uses the left invariant condition (
2.4
), then one must compute

\tilde{b}(z+y,[\![x+f,y+g,u+k]\!]_{\omega})=-h([\![x,y,u]\!])-\omega(x,y,u)(z)

+f([\![u,z,y]\!])-g([\![u,z,x]\!])+k([\![x,y,z]\!])

and so
\tilde{b}
satisfies (
2.4
) if and only if

(4.7)

\omega(x,y,z)(u)=-\omega(x,y,u)(z).

Observe that this condition (
4.7
) is the same as in the case of Lie-Yamaguti algebras (see (3.6) in
[
11
]
).

Proposition 4.2
.

The conditions (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) are equivalent.

Proof.
First suppose (
4.6
). Then

\omega(x,y,z)(u)=\omega(u,z,y)(x)=-\omega(z,u,y)(x)=-\omega(x,y,u)(z)
(by (
4.6
))

so we get (
4.7
).

For the converse we proceed as follows. From (
4.7
), switching
x
and
u
,
y
and
z
, we get

(4.8)

\omega(u,z,y)(x)=-\omega(u,z,x)(y).

Subtracting memberwise (
4.8
) from (
4.7
) we get

\omega(x,y,z)(u)-\omega(u,z,y)(x)=-\omega(x,y,u)(z)+\omega(u,z,x)(y)

=\omega(y,u,x)(z)-\omega(u,x,z)(y)+\omega(u,z,x)(y)
(by (
4.2
) and (
4.7
))

=\omega(y,u,x)(z)-\omega(u,x,z)(y)-\omega(z,x,u)(y)-\omega(x,u,z)(y)
(by (
4.2
))

=\omega(y,u,x)(z)-\omega(z,x,u)(y)
(by (
4.1
))

=\omega(u,y,z)(x)-\omega(x,z,y)(u)
(by (
4.1
) and (
4.7
)).

Thus we obtained

(4.9)

\omega(x,y,z)(u)-\omega(u,z,y)(x)=\omega(u,y,z)(x)-\omega(x,z,y)(u).

From the other hand, applying (
4.2
) to both side of (
4.7
), we get

-\omega(y,z,x)(u)-\omega(z,x,y)(u)=\omega(y,u,x)(z)+\omega(u,x,y)(z)

i.e., by (
4.7
) and (
4.1
),

\omega(y,z,u)(x)+\omega(x,z,y)(u)=\omega(u,y,z)(x)+\omega(u,x,y)(z)
.

Therefore we have

\omega(x,z,y)(u)-\omega(u,y,z)(x)=\omega(u,x,y)(z)-\omega(y,z,u)(x)

=-\omega(x,y,u)(z)-\omega(y,u,x)(z)+\omega(z,u,y)(x)+\omega(u,y,z)(x)
(by (
