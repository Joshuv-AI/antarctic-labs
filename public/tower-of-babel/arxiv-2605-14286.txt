Degeneration Theorems of Connes and Feigin–Tsygan Type in Mixed Characteristic, with q q -Analogues
Keiho Matsumoto

Abstract
. We prove mixed-characteristic analogues of the Connes and Feigin–Tsygan degeneration theorem. Let W = W ⁡ ( k ) W=W(k) be the Witt vectors of a perfect field of characteristic p > 0 p>0 . For a smooth proper variety X X over W W , the de Rham-to- HP \operatorname{HP} spectral sequence is split degenerate under the small-dimension hypothesis dim ( X / W ) < p − 1 \dim(X/W)<p-1 . More generally, if X X is smooth and proper over the ring of integers 𝒪 K \mathcal{O}_{K} of a finite extension of Frac ⁡ ( W ) \mathrm{Frac}(W) with ramification index e e , we prove the corresponding split degeneration under 2 ​ e ​ dim ( X / 𝒪 K ) < p − 1 2e\dim(X/\mathcal{O}_{K})<p-1 . Under the same ramification hypothesis, we also prove split degeneration of the A inf A_{\operatorname{inf}} -to- TP \operatorname{TP} spectral sequence. Finally, after inverting an explicit factorial, we obtain a topological q q -de Rham analogue.

Introduction

For a smooth algebra
A
over a commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
k
, the classical theorem of Connes, Feigin–Tsygan, and Goodwillie identifies periodic cyclic homology
\operatorname{HP}(A/k)
with the
2
-periodization of the de Rham complex
[
15
,
19
,
22
]
. More recently, Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze, in the
p
-complete quasisyntomic setting, and Antieau, for arbitrary commutative base rings, constructed an analogue of this picture in the form of a complete
\mathbb{Z}
-indexed multiplicative filtration on periodic cyclic homology
[
9
, Theorem 1.17]
,
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
. For a smooth
k
-scheme
X
, the
n
-th graded piece of this filtration is given by

R\Gamma(X,\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/k})[2n],

or more precisely, by the Hodge-completed derived de Rham complex, which agrees with the usual de Rham complex in the smooth case. In general, however, the spectral sequence associated with this filtration need not be split degenerate.

In characteristic
p
, the situation improves under suitable dimension and liftability hypotheses. Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
, and let
X
be a smooth proper variety over
k
admitting a lift to
W_{2}(k)
. If
\dim X<p
, then the Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg filtration on
\operatorname{HH}(X/k)
degenerates. Indeed, this follows from the HKR theorem in characteristic
p
, due to Yekutieli and strengthened by Antieau–Vezzosi, together with the Deligne–Illusie theorem on the degeneration of the Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence
[
34
,
3
,
16
]
. On the other hand, Mathew proved that if
T
is a smooth proper dg category over
k
admitting a lift to
W_{2}(k)
and satisfying
\operatorname{HH}_{i}(T/k)=0
for
i\notin[-p,p]
, then the Tate spectral sequence

\operatorname{HH}_{*}(T/k)[u^{\pm 1}]\Longrightarrow\operatorname{HP}_{*}(T/k)

degenerates
[
27
, Theorem 1.2]
. Applying this to
T=\operatorname{perf}(X)
, the degeneration of the HKR filtration yields the required Hochschild amplitude bound, and a comparison of dimensions then shows that the de Rham-to-
\operatorname{HP}
spectral sequence of Antieau also degenerates for
X
. The purpose of this paper is to establish mixed-characteristic analogues of this picture.

Let

be the ring of Witt vectors of a perfect field

(1)

Let

(2)

Let

. Assume

. Then the de Rham-to-

-modules

\displaystyle\operatorname{HP}_{j}(X/\mathcal{O}_{K};\mathbb{Z}_{p})

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{\operatorname{{dR}}}^{-j+2i}(X/\mathcal{O}_{K}).

Remark 1.2
.

The two smallness assumptions in the preceding theorem come from different
arguments. The crystalline-to-
\operatorname{TP}
statement is proved directly from the

-theoretic splitting criterion; it
does not pass through the Breuil–Kisin splitting argument used for the

and

-to-
\operatorname{TP}

in the ramified case, whereas the Breuil–Kisin
arguments below use the

-height range
[0,2\dim(X/\mathcal{O}_{K})]
and hence

2e\dim(X/\mathcal{O}_{K})<p-1
.

The preceding theorem concerns the mixed-characteristic analogue of the de Rham-to-
\operatorname{HP}
filtration. We establish similar split-degeneration results for further filtrations on topological additive invariants. In the
p
-adic setting, Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze constructed complete multiplicative filtrations on

,

, and

-adic formal schemes, the resulting graded pieces are governed by

-cohomology and its Breuil–Kisin twists
[
9

. Thus

carries a mixed-characteristic filtration which may be viewed as a topological analogue of the motivic filtrations discussed above. Our next result shows that, under the same small ramification hypothesis, this filtration is again split degenerate. We also prove a

-de Rham analogue for the

-equivariant even filtration on

]

.

(1)

Let

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we prove an integral form of the Chern character decomposition for reduced complex topological
K
-theory. More precisely, for a finite CW complex of dimension
d
, we show that the Chern character becomes an isomorphism after inverting

. This result will be used later to control the integrality range in the topological
q
-de Rham analogue.

In Section 3, we study the crystalline-to-
\operatorname{TP}

p
-adic Hodge theory needed in the argument. After that, we prove split
degeneration of the crystalline-to-
\operatorname{TP}
spectral sequence.

Section 4 is devoted to Breuil–Kisin modules. We recall the relevant categories of Breuil–Kisin modules and the low-ramification structure theorem for torsion Breuil–Kisin modules. We then prove a variant of this structure theorem which also allows finite free Breuil–Kisin modules. This variant is the key algebraic input needed to control the filtrations appearing in the proof of the main theorems.

In Section 5, we prove the main results. We first prove split degeneration of the Breuil–Kisin-to-
\operatorname{TC}^{-}
spectral sequence. By specializing this result along the maps from the Breuil–Kisin prism to
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and to

\operatorname{HP}

-to-
\operatorname{TP}
spectral sequence. Finally, we prove the topological
q
-de Rham analogue.

2.
Chern character decomposition of
K_{\operatorname{top}}
-theory over
\mathbf{Z}[1/M!]

In this section we work
entirely in reduced theories
. For a (finite) CW complex
Y
we write

\widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{0}(Y)
and
\widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{-1}(Y)
for reduced complex topological
K
-theory, and

\widetilde{H}^{*}(Y;R)
for reduced singular cohomology with coefficients in a ring
R
.
We also set

\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{even}}(Y;R):=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\widetilde{H}^{2n}(Y;R),\qquad\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{odd}}(Y;R):=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\widetilde{H}^{2n+1}(Y;R).

For
r\in\mathbf{Z}
, we also use the parity notation

\widetilde{H}^{[r]}(Y;R):=\begin{cases}\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{even}}(Y;R),&r\equiv 0\pmod{2},\\
\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{odd}}(Y;R),&r\equiv 1\pmod{2}.\end{cases}

Let
X
be a finite CW complex of (cellular) dimension
d=\dim X
, and set

M:=\Bigl\lfloor\frac{d+1}{2}\Bigr\rfloor,\qquad R:=\mathbf{Z}\!\left[\frac{1}{M!}\right].

Over
\mathbf{Q}
, Atiyah showed that complex topological
K
-theory
K_{\operatorname{top}}
splits via the Chern character, i.e. it decomposes in terms of singular cohomology; see
[
6
]
.
In this paper we refine this by localizing at a finite set of primes: more precisely, we work over

R=\mathbf{Z}\bigl[1/M!\bigr]

and show that the same decomposition holds after tensoring
\widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{m}(X)
with
R
.

Remark 2.1
.

The bound
M!
is the elementary denominator bound obtained from the splitting
principle and the usual Chern character. We do not claim that it is optimal:
sharper
p
-local decompositions can be obtained by more refined methods, for
instance using Adams operations. The present form is chosen because it is
explicit and is sufficient for the topological
q
-de Rham application in
Section 5.

Theorem 2.2
.

For every finite CW complex
X
of dimension
d
as above, the Chern character induces natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle{\operatorname{ch}}\colon\widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{0}(X)\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}R

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{even}}(X;R),

\displaystyle{\operatorname{ch}}\colon\widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{-1}(X)\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}R

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{odd}}(X;R).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a finite CW complex of dimension
d
and set
M=\lfloor(d+1)/2\rfloor
,
R=\mathbf{Z}[1/M!]
.
Then the usual rational Chern character on reduced
K
-theory factors through
R
:

\displaystyle{\operatorname{ch}}:\ \widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{0}(X)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{even}}(X;\mathbf{Q})

\displaystyle\text{factors as}

\displaystyle{\operatorname{ch}}:\ \widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{0}(X)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{even}}(X;R).

and similarly

{\operatorname{ch}}:\ \widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{-1}(X)\longrightarrow\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{odd}}(X;\mathbf{Q})\quad\text{factors as}\quad{\operatorname{ch}}:\ \widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{-1}(X)\longrightarrow\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{odd}}(X;R).

The same statement holds for any finite CW complex
Y
of dimension
\leq d
(in particular for any cofiber
X/A

of a CW pair
(X,A)
with
\dim X\leq d
).

Proof.

We first treat
\widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{0}
.
Recall that for a complex vector bundle
E
on
X
the Chern character is defined by

{\operatorname{ch}}(E)=\sum_{n\geq 0}{\operatorname{ch}}_{n}(E),\qquad{\operatorname{ch}}_{n}(E)\in H^{2n}(X;\mathbf{Q}),

and
{\operatorname{ch}}_{n}(E)
is a universal polynomial in
c_{1}(E),\dots,c_{n}(E)
with coefficients in
\mathbf{Z}[1/n!]

(equivalently, the denominators of
{\operatorname{ch}}_{n}
divide
n!
).

To see this in a way suited for denominator control, use the splitting principle as follows.
There exists a map
p\colon X^{\prime}\to X
such that:
(i)
p^{*}\colon H^{*}(X;\mathbf{Z})\to H^{*}(X^{\prime};\mathbf{Z})
is injective, and
(ii)
p^{*}E
splits as a direct sum of line bundles
L_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{r}
.
Write
x_{i}:=c_{1}(L_{i})\in H^{2}(X^{\prime};\mathbf{Z})
for the Chern roots. Then by definition of
{\operatorname{ch}}
for line bundles,

{\operatorname{ch}}(L_{i})=e^{x_{i}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{x_{i}^{n}}{n!},

and hence

{\operatorname{ch}}(p^{*}E)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}e^{x_{i}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{n!}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{n}\Bigr).

Therefore for each
n\geq 0
we have

{\operatorname{ch}}_{n}(p^{*}E)\in H^{2n}\!\left(X^{\prime};\mathbf{Z}\!\left[\frac{1}{n!}\right]\right).

Since
p^{*}
is injective on cohomology, it follows that already on
X
,

{\operatorname{ch}}_{n}(E)\in H^{2n}\!\left(X;\mathbf{Z}\!\left[\frac{1}{n!}\right]\right).

Now
\dim X=d
implies
H^{k}(X;\mathbf{Z})=0
for
k>d
, hence
{\operatorname{ch}}_{n}(E)=0
for
2n>d
.
Thus only the components with
n\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor
can occur, and all denominators are killed by
\bigl\lfloor d/2\bigr\rfloor!\ \mid\ M!
. Consequently
{\operatorname{ch}}(E)
actually lands in
H^{\mathrm{even}}(X;R)
, and passing to reduced groups
(i.e. taking kernels of the maps to the point) gives

{\operatorname{ch}}:\ \widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{0}(X)\longrightarrow\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{even}}(X;R).

For
\widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{-1}
we use the standard identification
\widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{-1}(X)\cong\widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{0}(\Sigma X)
.
Since
\Sigma X
is a finite CW complex of dimension
d+1
, the above argument applied to
\Sigma X
shows
that the denominators needed for
{\operatorname{ch}}
on
\widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{0}(\Sigma X)
divide
\Bigl\lfloor\frac{d+1}{2}\Bigr\rfloor!\ =\ M!
, hence
{\operatorname{ch}}
lands in
\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{even}}(\Sigma X;R)\cong\widetilde{H}^{\mathrm{odd}}(X;R)
.
This yields the desired factorization for
\widetilde{K}_{{\operatorname{top}}}^{-1}(X)
.

Finally, if
Y
is any finite CW complex with
\dim Y\leq d
, the same argument applies verbatim (with
d
replaced by

\dim Y
). In particular, for a CW pair
(X,A)
with
\dim X\leq d
, its cofiber
X/A
has
\dim(X/A)\leq d
,
so the same denominator control applies to
Y=X/A
.
∎

(X,A)
be a CW pair with inclusion

. The quotient

,

For any reduced cohomology theory

.

3.2.
Preliminaries on spectral sequences I

The aim of this subsection is to give spectral analogues of Li’s definitions and
criteria for filtered complexes over a DVR
[
24
, Section 2]
. More precisely, we introduce the
notions of degeneration, saturated degeneration, and split degeneration for a
filtered spectrum, and then prove the corresponding torsion-length criterion. Let
R
be a DVR with uniformizer
\varpi
, fraction field

K=R[1/\varpi]
, and residue field
\kappa=R/\varpi
.
For a finitely generated
R
-module
N
, write

\displaystyle N_{\operatorname{tor}}

\displaystyle\subset N,

\displaystyle N_{\operatorname{tf}}

\displaystyle:=N/N_{\operatorname{tor}}

for its torsion submodule and torsion-free quotient, respectively.

Let
M
be a spectrum equipped with a decreasing filtration

\cdots\longrightarrow{\operatorname{fil}}^{n+1}M\longrightarrow{\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M\longrightarrow{\operatorname{fil}}^{n-1}M\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M.

Set

{\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M:=\operatorname{cofib}\bigl({\operatorname{fil}}^{n+1}M\to{\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M\bigr).

We assume throughout that the filtration is complete and exhaustive. We also assume that the induced spectral sequence

E_{1}^{n,*}=\pi_{*}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M)\Longrightarrow\pi_{*}(M)

is strongly convergent and locally finite in each homotopy degree. More
explicitly, for every
i
, only finitely many
n
contribute to the
filtration on
\pi_{i}(M)
. Finally, assume that all groups
\pi_{i}(M),\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M),\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M)
are finitely generated
R
-modules, and all maps appearing in the exact
couple are
R
-linear.

For a torsion-free inclusion
A\subset B
of finitely generated

R
-modules, we say that
A\subset B
is saturated if

\varpi A=A\cap\varpi B,

or equivalently if
B/A
is torsion-free.

Definition 3.5

(Spectral version of
[
24
, Definition 2.3]
)
.

Let
M
be as above.

(1)

We say that the filtered spectrum
M
, or equivalently its associated
spectral sequence, is
degenerate
if for all
i,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
natural map

\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)\longrightarrow\pi_{i}(M)

is injective.

(2)

We say that
M
is
saturated degenerate
if it is degenerate
and, for all
i,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the induced injection

\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)_{\operatorname{tf}}\longrightarrow\pi_{i}(M)_{\operatorname{tf}}

is saturated.

(3)

We say that
M
is
split degenerate
if it is degenerate and,
for all
i,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the injection

\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)\longrightarrow\pi_{i}(M)

splits as a map of
R
-modules.

Proposition 3.6

(Spectral version of
[
24
, Proposition 2.5]
)
.

Assume that the spectral sequence associated with
M
degenerates after
inverting
\varpi
, i.e. after tensoring all
E_{r}
-terms with
K
.
Then the following hold.

(1)

The filtered spectrum
M
is saturated degenerate if and only if

\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl(\pi_{i}(M)_{\operatorname{tor}}\bigr)=\sum_{n}\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl(\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M)_{\operatorname{tor}}\bigr)

for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

The filtered spectrum
M
is split degenerate if and only if there is
an abstract isomorphism of
R
-modules

\pi_{i}(M)_{\operatorname{tor}}\simeq\bigoplus_{n}\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M)_{\operatorname{tor}}

for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let

F^{n}\pi_{i}(M):=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)\to\pi_{i}(M)\bigr)

be the filtration induced on
\pi_{i}(M)
. Since the spectral sequence is
strongly convergent and locally finite in each degree, its
E_{\infty}
-page
identifies with the associated graded pieces

E_{\infty}^{n,i}\simeq{\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{n}\pi_{i}(M).

Because the spectral sequence degenerates after inverting
\varpi
, every
differential is
\varpi
-power torsion. In particular,

({\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{n}\pi_{i}(M))_{\operatorname{tor}}
is a subquotient of

\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M)_{\operatorname{tor}}
. Hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl(\pi_{i}(M)_{\operatorname{tor}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n}\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl(({\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{n}\pi_{i}(M))_{\operatorname{tor}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n}\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl(\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M)_{\operatorname{tor}}\bigr).

If equality with the right-hand side holds, then all torsion classes on the

E_{1}
-page survive to
E_{\infty}
. Since all differentials are torsion after
inverting
\varpi
, this forces every differential to vanish. Thus the
spectral sequence is degenerate.

Assume now that the spectral sequence is degenerate. Then

\displaystyle F^{n}\pi_{i}(M)

\displaystyle=\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)

\displaystyle\subset\pi_{i}(M),

and the associated graded pieces of this filtration are

{\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{n}\pi_{i}(M)\simeq\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M).

For a finitely generated
R
-module
N
with a finite exhaustive filtration

F^{*}N
, by
[
24
, Lemma 2.6]
,

F^{n}N_{\operatorname{tf}}\subset N_{\operatorname{tf}}\text{ is saturated for all }n

if and only if

\operatorname{length}_{R}(N_{\operatorname{tor}})=\sum_{n}\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl(({\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{n}N)_{\operatorname{tor}}\bigr).

Applying this lemma to
N=\pi_{i}(M)
gives the first assertion.

For the second assertion, first suppose that
M
is split degenerate.
Then each inclusion

\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)\subset\pi_{i}(M)

splits, so the induced filtration on
\pi_{i}(M)_{\operatorname{tor}}
is split. Since
its graded pieces are
\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M)_{\operatorname{tor}}
, we obtain an abstract
isomorphism

\pi_{i}(M)_{\operatorname{tor}}\simeq\bigoplus_{n}\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M)_{\operatorname{tor}}.

Conversely, suppose that such an abstract isomorphism exists for every
i
.
Taking lengths gives the equality in part
(1)
, hence
M
is saturated
degenerate. Therefore

\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)_{\operatorname{tf}}\subset\pi_{i}(M)_{\operatorname{tf}}

is saturated, and hence split, since finitely generated torsion-free modules
over a DVR are free. It remains to split the torsion part. The induced
filtration on
\pi_{i}(M)_{\operatorname{tor}}
has graded pieces

\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M)_{\operatorname{tor}},

and by the abstract isomorphism

\pi_{i}(M)_{\operatorname{tor}}\simeq\bigoplus_{n}\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M)_{\operatorname{tor}}.

By
[
24
, Corollary 2.11]
each inclusion

\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)_{\operatorname{tor}}\subset\pi_{i}(M)_{\operatorname{tor}}

splits. Combining the splitting on torsion parts with the splitting on
torsion-free quotients gives a splitting

\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)\longrightarrow\pi_{i}(M)

for every
i,n
. Thus
M
is split degenerate.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
M
be a filtered spectrum as above, and assume that
M
is saturated degenerate.
Then for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and every integer
n>0
, one has

\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl(\pi_{i}(M)/\varpi^{n}\bigr)\leq\sum_{a}\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl(\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{a}M)/\varpi^{n}\bigr).

Proof.

Set

N:=\pi_{i}(M),

and let

F^{a}N:=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{a}M)\to\pi_{i}(M)\bigr)

be the induced filtration on
N
.
Since the filtration is locally finite in each degree, this is a finite
exhaustive filtration on the finitely generated
R
-module
N
.

Fix
n>0
. The filtration
F^{*}N
induces a filtration on

N/\varpi^{n}N
by

F^{a}(N/\varpi^{n}N):=\operatorname{im}\bigl(F^{a}N\to N/\varpi^{n}N\bigr).

Hence

\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl(N/\varpi^{n}N\bigr)=\sum_{a}\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl({\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{a}(N/\varpi^{n}N)\bigr).

For each
a
, there is a natural surjection

{\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{a}N/\varpi^{n}{\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{a}N\twoheadrightarrow{\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{a}(N/\varpi^{n}N).

Therefore

\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl({\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{a}(N/\varpi^{n}N)\bigr)\leq\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl({\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{a}N/\varpi^{n}{\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{a}N\bigr).

Summing over
a
, we obtain

\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl(N/\varpi^{n}N\bigr)\leq\sum_{a}\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl({\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{a}N/\varpi^{n}{\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{a}N\bigr).

Since
M
is degenerate, we have

F^{a}N=\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{a}M)

and hence

{\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{a}N\simeq\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{a}M).

Thus

\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl(\pi_{i}(M)/\varpi^{n}\bigr)\leq\sum_{a}\operatorname{length}_{R}\bigl(\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{a}M)/\varpi^{n}\bigr),

as claimed.
∎

4.
Preliminaries on Breuil–Kisin modules

In this section we recall the Breuil–Kisin input used later. We first fix
our conventions and the low-ramification structure theorem for torsion
Breuil–Kisin modules. We then record a slightly enlarged version of the
structure theorem, allowing finite free Breuil–Kisin modules in addition to

p
-power torsion ones.

4.1.
Definitions and structure theorems

Let
\mathfrak{S}=W(k)[[z]]
, equipped with the Frobenius
\varphi
extending the
Witt-vector Frobenius and satisfying
\varphi(z)=z^{p}
. Let

be the
Eisenstein polynomial of the chosen uniformizer

\mathcal{O}_{K}

Definition 4.1
.

(1)

A
Breuil–Kisin module
is a finitely generated

\mathfrak{S}
-module
\mathfrak{M}
together with an isomorphism

\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}:\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}[1/E]\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathfrak{M}[1/E],\qquad\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}:=\mathfrak{M}\otimes_{\mathfrak{S},\varphi}\mathfrak{S}.

(2)

A

-module

together with an isomorphism

\Phi_{\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}}:\varphi_{A_{\operatorname{inf}}}^{*}\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}[1/\tilde{\xi}]\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}[1/\tilde{\xi}].

(3)

Let

be a Breuil–Kisin module, and view

\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}
as an
\mathfrak{S}
-linear map

\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}:\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{M}[1/E].

For integers

-height of

For a Breuil–Kisin module

, the base change

[
8

We also recall the standard structure theorem for Breuil–Kisin modules.

(

, Proposition 4.3]

\mathfrak{S}

We next recall the low-ramification structure theorem for torsion

[

Lemma 4.14

Lemma 4.15
.

Let

be a finitely generated
\mathfrak{S}
-module. Suppose that

is finite free over

Let

be the

-power torsion submodule of

is

For

\mathfrak{S}_{1}

\mathfrak{S}
.

The exact sequence

5.
Proof of the main theorems

Throughout this section, we retain the notation of Section 3. We regard
\mathcal{O}_{K}
as an
\mathbb{S}[z]
-algebra via
z\mapsto\pi
. For a smooth scheme
X
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, let
\mathcal{X}
denote its
p
-adic formal completion. Set

\displaystyle X_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}}

\displaystyle:=X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{K})}\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{X}\widehat{\times}_{\operatorname{Spf}(\mathcal{O}_{K})}\operatorname{Spf}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}).

Then
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}}
is canonically identified with the
p
-adic formal completion of

.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that the invariants of the formal schemes below are defined via their quasisyntomic sites. Then there are canonical equivalences

\operatorname{TP}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{TP}(X_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}};\mathbb{Z}_{p}),\qquad\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{TC}^{-}(X_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}};\mathbb{Z}_{p}),

and, respectively,

\operatorname{TP}(\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{S}[z];\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{TP}(X/\mathbb{S}[z];\mathbb{Z}_{p}),\qquad\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{S}[z];\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{TC}^{-}(X/\mathbb{S}[z];\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Proof.

We prove the assertion for
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}}
. The case of
\mathcal{X}
over
\mathbb{S}[z]
is entirely analogous. Choose an affine Zariski hypercover
U_{\bullet}\to X_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}}
such that each

U_{n}
is a disjoint union of affine opens, and let
\mathfrak{U}_{\bullet}
be the termwise

p
-adic formal completion of
U_{\bullet}
. Since
\operatorname{TP}(-;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and

\operatorname{TC}^{-}(-;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
satisfy Zariski descent on schemes, while on the formal side
they are defined as global sections of the corresponding quasisyntomic sheaves, it is
enough to prove the claim for each affine component

U=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
occurring in some
U_{n}
, with formal completion

\mathfrak{U}=\operatorname{Spf}(\widehat{A})
.

For such an affine
U
, by definition of the quasisyntomic site of an affine formal scheme,
we have

\operatorname{TP}(\mathfrak{U};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{TP}(\widehat{A};\mathbb{Z}_{p}),\qquad\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\mathfrak{U};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\widehat{A};\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

On the other hand, applying
[
1
, Proposition 4.2]
first to

\operatorname{Spec}(A)
and then to
\operatorname{Spec}(\widehat{A})
, yields equivalences

\operatorname{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\xrightarrow{\sim}\varprojlim_{n}\operatorname{THH}(A/p^{n}A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\xleftarrow{\sim}\operatorname{THH}(\widehat{A};\mathbb{Z}_{p}),

because
\widehat{A}/p^{n}\widehat{A}\cong A/p^{n}A
for all
n\geq 1
. Hence

\operatorname{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{THH}(\widehat{A};\mathbb{Z}_{p})

as
S^{1}
-equivariant spectra.

Now

\operatorname{TC}^{-}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})=\operatorname{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{hS^{1}},\qquad\operatorname{TP}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})=\operatorname{THH}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{tS^{1}},

and similarly for
\widehat{A}
. Therefore the preceding equivalence implies

\operatorname{TC}^{-}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\widehat{A};\mathbb{Z}_{p}),\qquad\operatorname{TP}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{TP}(\widehat{A};\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Combining this with the identification of the formal side, we obtain

\operatorname{TC}^{-}(U;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\mathfrak{U};\mathbb{Z}_{p}),\qquad\operatorname{TP}(U;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{TP}(\mathfrak{U};\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

These equivalences are functorial in
U
, hence assemble into equivalences of cosimplicial spectra

\operatorname{TC}^{-}(U_{\bullet};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\mathfrak{U}_{\bullet};\mathbb{Z}_{p}),\qquad\operatorname{TP}(U_{\bullet};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{TP}(\mathfrak{U}_{\bullet};\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Taking totalizations and using fpqc-descent
[
9
, Corollary 3.4]
, we get

\operatorname{TC}^{-}(X_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{Tot}\bigl(\operatorname{TC}^{-}(U_{\bullet};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\bigr)\simeq\operatorname{Tot}\bigl(\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\mathfrak{U}_{\bullet};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\bigr)\simeq\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}};\mathbb{Z}_{p}),

and similarly

\operatorname{TP}(X_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\operatorname{Tot}\bigl(\operatorname{TP}(U_{\bullet};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\bigr)\simeq\operatorname{Tot}\bigl(\operatorname{TP}(\mathfrak{U}_{\bullet};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\bigr)\simeq\operatorname{TP}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}};\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Taking inverses of these equivalences gives the displayed maps

\operatorname{TP}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{TP}(X_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}};\mathbb{Z}_{p}),

and

\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{TC}^{-}(X_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}};\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

This proves the claim.
∎

The same argument gives the following result.

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
\operatorname{HP}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O}_{K};\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is defined via the quasisyntomic
site of the formal scheme
\mathcal{X}
. Then there are canonical equivalences

\operatorname{HP}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O}_{K};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\operatorname{HP}(X/\mathcal{O}_{K};\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Proof.

The proof is identical to that of Proposition
5.1
: one chooses an affine Zariski hypercover of
X
, passes to the termwise
p
-adic formal completions, uses
p
-adic continuity for Hochschild homology, and then takes Tate fixed points after passing to periodic cyclic homology. Descent identifies the resulting totalizations, giving the displayed equivalence.
∎

5.1.
Preliminaries on spectral sequences II

In this subsection, we let
R
be a commutative ring and discuss several preliminary facts concerning spectral sequences that will be used later.

Let
M
be a spectrum equipped with a decreasing filtration

\cdots\longrightarrow{\operatorname{fil}}^{n+1}M\longrightarrow{\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M\longrightarrow{\operatorname{fil}}^{n-1}M\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M.

Set

{\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M:=\operatorname{cofib}\bigl({\operatorname{fil}}^{n+1}M\to{\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M\bigr).

We assume throughout that the filtration is complete and exhaustive. We also assume that the induced spectral sequence

E_{1}^{n,*}=\pi_{*}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M)\Longrightarrow\pi_{*}(M)

is strongly convergent and locally finite in each homotopy degree. More explicitly, for every
i
, only finitely many
n
contribute to the filtration on
\pi_{i}(M)
. Finally, we assume that all groups
\pi_{i}(M)
,
\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)
, and
\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M)
are finitely generated
R
-modules, and that all maps appearing in the exact couple are
R
-linear. Let

F^{n}\pi_{i}(M):=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)\to\pi_{i}(M)\bigr)

be the induced filtration on
\pi_{i}(M)
, and set

{\operatorname{gr}}_{F}^{n}(\pi_{i}M):=F^{n}\pi_{i}M/F^{n+1}\pi_{i}M.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
R\to A
be a morphism of commutative rings.

(1)

We say that the filtered spectrum
M
is
degenerate after tensoring with
A
if for all
i,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the natural map

\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)\otimes_{R}A\to\pi_{i}(M)\otimes_{R}A

is injective.

(2)

We say that the filtered spectrum
M
is
split degenerate after tensoring with
A
if for all
i,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the natural map

\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)\otimes_{R}A\to\pi_{i}(M)\otimes_{R}A

is split injective as a map of
A
-modules.

The following proposition is proved by the same argument as Proposition
3.6
(2).

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
R\to A
be a flat morphism of commutative rings such that
A
is a DVR with fraction field
K
. Assume that the spectral sequence associated with
M
degenerates after tensoring with
K
. Then the filtered spectrum
M
is split degenerate after tensoring with
A
if and only if, for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, there is an abstract isomorphism of
A
-modules

\bigl(\pi_{i}(M)\otimes_{R}A\bigr)_{\operatorname{tor}}\simeq\bigoplus_{n}\bigl(\pi_{i}({\operatorname{gr}}^{n}M)\otimes_{R}A\bigr)_{\operatorname{tor}}.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that of Proposition
3.6
(2), applied to the filtered
A
-module

N_{i}:=\pi_{i}(M)\otimes_{R}A

with filtration

F_{A}^{n}N_{i}:=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)\otimes_{R}A\to\pi_{i}(M)\otimes_{R}A\bigr).

Since the associated spectral sequence degenerates after tensoring with
K
, the same length argument as in Proposition
3.6
(2) shows that the filtration
F_{A}^{*}N_{i}
is split if and only if

(N_{i})_{\operatorname{tor}}\simeq\bigoplus_{n}\bigl({\operatorname{gr}}_{F_{A}}^{n}N_{i}\bigr)_{\operatorname{tor}}.

By construction, one has

{\operatorname{gr}}_{F_{A}}^{n}N_{i}\cong\operatorname{im}\bigl(\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n}M)\otimes_{R}A\to\pi_{i}(M)\otimes_{R}A\bigr)\Big/\operatorname{im}\bigl(\pi_{i}({\operatorname{fil}}^{n+1}M)\otimes_{R}A\to\pi_{i}(M)\otimes_{R}A\bigr),

so, under the evident identification of the graded pieces with

, the claim follows.
∎

We also prove the following lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
M
be a filtered spectrum as above, and let
R\to A
be a morphism of commutative rings such that
A
is a DVR with fraction field
K
. Assume that for every

, the natural map

Proof.

Let
E_{r}^{a,b}
be the associated spectral sequence. Since

the map

is injective for every

by assumption.

We prove by induction on

that
d_{r}=0
. Assume that
d_{s}=0
for all

. Then there are canonical identifications

for all

. Since

Therefore

Lemma 5.11
.

Let

be a short exact sequence of Breuil–Kisin modules over
\mathfrak{S}
whose underlying
\mathfrak{S}
-modules are of the form

If, after tensoring with

, the

is split exact as a sequence of
\mathfrak{S}
-modules.

Proof.

We write

\overline{W}:=W(\mathcal{C}^{\flat}).

The map
\mathfrak{S}\to\overline{W}
sends
z
to a unit. In particular, for every

the natural map

\mathfrak{S}/p^{n}\longrightarrow\overline{W}/p^{n}

is injective. We shall use this repeatedly.

First we record an elementary injectivity statement for extension classes.
Let

T=\bigoplus_{i}\mathfrak{S}/p^{a_{i}},\qquad T^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{j}\mathfrak{S}/p^{b_{j}},

Then the natural map

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{S}}(T,T^{\prime})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\overline{W}}(T_{\overline{W}},T^{\prime}_{\overline{W}})

is injective. Indeed, by additivity it is enough to treat the case

and

. The free resolution

Similarly,

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\overline{W}}({\overline{W}}/p^{a},{\overline{W}}/p^{b})\simeq{\overline{W}}/p^{\min(a,b)}.

Under these identifications the base change map is the natural injection

\mathfrak{S}/p^{\min(a,b)}\longrightarrow{\overline{W}}/p^{\min(a,b)}.

Thus the desired map on
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
is injective.

We next prove that the sequence on
p
-power torsion submodules is exact.
The injectivity of

A_{\operatorname{tor}}\longrightarrow B_{\operatorname{tor}}

and the equality

\operatorname{Ker}(B_{\operatorname{tor}}\to C_{\operatorname{tor}})=A_{\operatorname{tor}}

are immediate from the exactness of

It remains to prove that

B_{\operatorname{tor}}\longrightarrow C_{\operatorname{tor}}

is surjective.

Let
D:=\beta^{-1}(C_{\operatorname{tor}})\subset B
. Then we have an exact sequence

0\to A\to D\to C_{\operatorname{tor}}\to 0.

Quotienting by
A_{\operatorname{tor}}
gives

Since

is finite free over
\mathfrak{S}
and
C_{\operatorname{tor}}
is a direct
sum of modules
\mathfrak{S}/p^{c}
, the extension class lies in

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{S}}(C_{\operatorname{tor}},A/A_{\operatorname{tor}}).

After tensoring with
{\overline{W}}
, the original sequence

0\to A_{\overline{W}}\to B_{\overline{W}}\to C_{\overline{W}}\to 0

is split exact by assumption. Pulling this split sequence back along

(C_{\operatorname{tor}})_{\overline{W}}\longrightarrow C_{\overline{W}}

shows that the base change of

0\to A/A_{\operatorname{tor}}\longrightarrow D/A_{\operatorname{tor}}\longrightarrow C_{\operatorname{tor}}\to 0

to
{\overline{W}}
is split exact. By the injectivity of the base change map on

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
, the original extension is already split over
\mathfrak{S}
.

Thus there is an
\mathfrak{S}
-linear section

Choose

and let
\tilde{b}\in D
be a lift of

such that

. Since

is
\mathfrak{S}
-linear, we have

Hence, if
\tilde{b}\in D
is any lift of

Since

is
p
-power torsion, there exists

such that

Thus

, so
\tilde{b}
is
p
-power torsion. Viewing

B
, we obtain

. Moreover, the image of
\tilde{b}
in

is

lifts to an element of

We have therefore obtained a short exact sequence

-module of
H_{\mathfrak{S}}^{2n+2d}(\mathcal{X})\{n\}_{\operatorname{free}}
is finite free, it is in
\overline{\operatorname{Mod}}^{2d,\varphi}_{/\mathfrak{S}_{1}}\subset\overline{\operatorname{Mod}}^{2d,\varphi}_{/\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}
. Since
\overline{\operatorname{Mod}}^{2d,\varphi}_{/\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}
is closed under extension, we obtain that

H_{\mathfrak{S}}^{2n+2d}(\mathcal{X})\{n\}

is in
\overline{\operatorname{Mod}}^{2d,\varphi}_{/\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}
. Using the exact sequence (
5.12
) and induction
n
, we get that
\mathfrak{M}_{-2d,n}
is in
\overline{\operatorname{Mod}}^{2d,\varphi}_{/\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}
. Since

2de<p-1,

Proposition
4.16
applies. Hence, for every
n
,

(5.15)

\mathfrak{M}_{-2d,n}\simeq\mathfrak{S}^{\oplus a}\oplus\bigoplus_{\lambda}\mathfrak{S}/(p^{a_{\lambda}})

for some finite multiset of positive integers
\{a_{\lambda}\}
and a natural number
a
.

For
m=-2d-1
, by the same argument in the case
m=-2d
, for every
n
,

(5.16)

\mathfrak{M}_{-2d-1,n}\simeq\mathfrak{S}^{\oplus b}\oplus\bigoplus_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathfrak{S}/(p^{b_{\lambda^{\prime}}})

for some finite multiset of positive integers
\{b_{\lambda^{\prime}}\}
and a natural number
b
.

By the isomorphism (
5.14
), we obtain that for every
m,n
,

(5.17)

\mathfrak{M}_{m,n}\simeq\mathfrak{S}^{\oplus a}\oplus\bigoplus_{\lambda}\mathfrak{S}/(p^{a_{\lambda}})

for some finite multiset of positive integers
\{a_{\lambda}\}
and a natural number
a
.

We now prove that (
5.12
) splits. Put

H_{m,n}:=H_{\mathfrak{S}}^{2n-m}(\mathcal{X})\{n\}.

Taking
p
-power torsion in (
5.12
) gives a left exact
sequence

0\to\mathfrak{M}_{m,n+1,\operatorname{tor}}\to\mathfrak{M}_{m,n,\operatorname{tor}}\to H_{m,n,\operatorname{tor}}.

By Proposition
5.8
, after tensoring with
W(\mathcal{C}^{\flat})
the
spectral sequence is split degenerate. In particular, the base change of
(
5.12
) to
W(\mathcal{C}^{\flat})
is split exact. Therefore
Lemma
5.11
gives a split exact sequence

(5.18)

0\to\mathfrak{M}_{m,n+1,\operatorname{tor}}\to\mathfrak{M}_{m,n,\operatorname{tor}}\to H_{m,n,\operatorname{tor}}\to 0.

Next, by
[
29
, Theorem 5.11]
, we have an abstract isomorphism

H_{\mathfrak{S}}^{q}(\mathcal{X})\simeq H_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}^{q}(X_{\mathcal{C}},\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathfrak{S}.

Since every finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module is a direct sum of a free

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module and cyclic modules
\mathbb{Z}_{p}/(p^{a})
, it follows that

H_{m,n}\simeq H_{m,n,\operatorname{tor}}\oplus H_{m,n,\operatorname{tf}},

where

H_{m,n,\operatorname{tf}}:=H_{m,n}/H_{m,n,\operatorname{tor}}

is a finite free
\mathfrak{S}
-module (see
[
29
, Theorem 5.11]
). Thus the natural map

H_{m,n}\to H_{m,n,\operatorname{tf}}

is split.

Now apply Lemma
5.12
to the short exact
sequence (
5.12
). The torsion row is split by
(
5.18
), and the right-hand term
H_{m,n}
has a split
torsion/free decomposition with torsion-free quotient finite free over

\mathfrak{S}
. Hence the surjection

\mathfrak{M}_{m,n}\to H_{m,n}

is split. Therefore (
5.12
) is split exact for all

m,n
.
∎

Theorem 5.14
.

be a smooth proper variety over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and put

Proof.

Put
\mathcal{T}:=\operatorname{perf}(X)
. The map
\mathbb{S}[z]\to\mathcal{O}_{K}
sending

to
\pi
induces a morphism

(5.19)

\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\mathcal{T}/\mathbb{S}[z];\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\operatorname{TP}(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathbb{S}[z];\mathbb{Z}_{p})}\operatorname{HP}(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathcal{O}_{K};\mathbb{Z}_{p})\longrightarrow\operatorname{HP}(\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{O}_{K};\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

\mathcal{O}_{K}
-linear dg categories to

]

By Theorem
5.13

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper variety over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and put
d:=\dim(X/\mathcal{O}_{K})
. Assume
2de<p-1
. Then the
A_{\operatorname{inf}}
-to-
\operatorname{TP}
spectral sequence is split degenerate. In
particular, there is an abstract isomorphism of
A_{\operatorname{inf}}
-modules

\operatorname{TP}_{j}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{A_{\operatorname{inf}}}^{-j+2i}(X).

Proof.

By Theorem
5.13
, the Breuil–Kisin spectral sequence

(5.21)

E_{2}^{i,j}=H_{\mathfrak{S}}^{i-j}(\mathcal{X})\{j\}\Longrightarrow\pi_{-i-j}\bigl(\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{S}[z];\mathbb{Z}_{p})[1/u]\bigr)

\pi_{j}{\operatorname{fil}}_{\mathrm{BMS}}^{n}\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{S}[z];\mathbb{Z}_{p})[1/u]\simeq\bigoplus_{i\geq n}H_{\mathfrak{S}}^{2i-j}(\mathcal{X})\{i\}.

{\operatorname{fil}}_{\mathrm{BMS}}^{*}\operatorname{TC}^{-}(\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{S}[z];\mathbb{Z}_{p})[1/u]\longrightarrow{\operatorname{fil}}_{\mathrm{BMS}}^{*}\operatorname{TP}(\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{S}[z];\mathbb{Z}_{p})\longrightarrow{\operatorname{fil}}_{\mathrm{BMS}}^{*}\operatorname{TP}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}};\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

-semilinear with respect to the

) over

\mathfrak{S}
with

Lemma 5.16
.

Let

R=\mathbb{Z}[S^{-1}],\qquad\Lambda=R[[q-1]].

For a prime
\ell\notin S
, put

\Lambda_{\ell}:=\Lambda\widehat{\otimes}_{R}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[[q-1]].

Let
f:M\to N
be a morphism of finitely generated
\Lambda
-modules.
Assume that

f\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda_{\ell}=0

for every prime
\ell\notin S
. Then
f=0
.

Proof.

Let
I:=\operatorname{im}(f)
. It is enough to show that
I=0
.
If
I\neq 0
, then since
\Lambda
is noetherian,
I
has a maximal ideal in
its support. Every maximal ideal of

\Lambda=R[[q-1]]

is of the form

\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}=(\ell,q-1)

for some prime
\ell\notin S
. Hence

I_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}\neq 0

for some
\ell\notin S
.

Let us denote by
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}
the localization of
\Lambda
at
\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}
. Then the natural map

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\ell}

is faithfully flat; indeed
\Lambda_{\ell}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[[q-1]]
is the

\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}
-adic completion of the noetherian local ring

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}
. Therefore

I_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}\otimes_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}}\Lambda_{\ell}\neq 0.

But this is a subquotient of

I\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda_{\ell}=\operatorname{im}(f\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda_{\ell}),

which is zero by assumption. This contradiction proves
I=0
.
∎

Lemma 5.17
.

Let

R=\mathbb{Z}[S^{-1}],\qquad\Lambda=R[[q-1]],

and for each prime
\ell\notin S
put

\Lambda_{\ell}:=\Lambda\widehat{\otimes}_{R}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[[q-1]].

Let

0\to A\to B\to C\to 0

be a short exact sequence of finitely generated
\Lambda
-modules. Assume
that for every prime
\ell\notin S
, the base changed sequence

0\to A\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda_{\ell}\to B\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda_{\ell}\to C\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda_{\ell}\to 0

is split exact. Then the original sequence is split exact.

Proof.

Let

\xi\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(C,A)

be the extension class. Since
\Lambda
is noetherian and
A,C
are
finitely generated,
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(C,A)
is a finitely
generated
\Lambda
-module.

We show that
\xi=0
. It is enough to show that
\xi
vanishes after
localizing at every maximal ideal of
\Lambda
. Every maximal ideal of

\Lambda=R[[q-1]]
is of the form

\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}=(\ell,q-1)

for some
\ell\notin S
.

Fix such an
\ell
. By assumption, the sequence becomes split after base
change to

\Lambda_{\ell}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[[q-1]].

Equivalently, the image of

\xi_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}}(C_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}},A_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}})

vanishes after tensoring with
\Lambda_{\ell}
. Since

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}\to\Lambda_{\ell}

is faithfully flat, this implies

\xi_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}=0.

Thus
\xi
vanishes after localization at every maximal ideal of
\Lambda
.
Therefore
\xi=0
, and the short exact sequence splits.
∎

Theorem 5.18

be a positive even integer. Let

be a smooth proper variety over

Proof.

Put

and

Let

{\operatorname{fil}}_{\operatorname{ev}}^{*}\operatorname{TP}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\operatorname{ku}/\operatorname{ku})

denote the
S^{1}
-equivariant even filtration; see
[
23
]
and
[
31
]
. For fixed

, set

-de Rham cohomology. Hence there are exact triangles whose homotopy

\Lambda

vanish and that the resulting short exact sequences

(5.24)

\ell
be a prime not inverted in

\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}
-algebra via the identity on
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
and sending

to

By the functoriality of

-equivariant even filtration, a

-equivariant morphism
