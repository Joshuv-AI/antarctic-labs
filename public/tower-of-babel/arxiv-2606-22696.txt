NullFlow: One-Step Generative Reconstruction
Xiao Shi, Edward P. Chandler, Chicago Y. Park, [0.3em] Shirin Shoushtari, Ulugbek S. Kamilov, [0.7em] University of Wisconsin--Madison, Washington University in St. Louis[0.5em] {xiao.shi, epchandler, chicago.park, kamilov}@wisc.edus.shirin@wustl.edu, [0.5em] Equal contribution

Abstract
We propose NullFlow, a principled framework for one-step generative image reconstruction. Our key idea is to confine the generative flow to a measurement-consistent subspace. Because the flow never leaves this subspace, NullFlow needs no separate data-fidelity corrections, unlike existing solvers. NullFlow samples in a single network evaluation by learning the flow’s average velocity, avoiding the step-by-step integration of the traditional flow matching methods. We prove that the average velocity of this constrained flow yields a training objective whose global minimizer is a one-step posterior sampler. We show on image inpainting that NullFlow matches state-of-the-art diffusion solvers while cutting inference from hundreds of network evaluations to one.



Index Terms—
Imaging inverse problems, image reconstruction, generative priors, null-space learning, mean flows.

Introduction

Imaging inverse problems seek to recover an unknown image

{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
from measurements
{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
For linear systems this is
modeled as

{\bm{y}}={\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}+{\bm{n}},

(1)

where
{\bm{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is a known forward operator and

{\bm{n}}
is noise. We focus on the ill-posed setting where

\mathrm{null}({\bm{A}})\neq\{{\bm{0}}\}
, which covers image inpainting,
accelerated MRI, and sparse-view CT. In this
setting, the measurements constrain only the row-space of

{\bm{x}}
; the null-space component is invisible to
{\bm{A}}
and must be supplied

entirely
by prior knowledge. Reconstruction is therefore not inversion
of
{\bm{A}}
, but generation of plausible null-space content consistent with the
measurements.

There is growing interest in learning-based methods for supplying this missing content. Early work predicted
{\bm{x}}
directly from
{\bm{y}}

Existing generative solvers are expensive due to their iterative nature. They reconstruct images by simulating a full generative trajectory, alternating prior updates from the model with data-fidelity updates that enforce measurement consistency, and reach a solution only after hundreds or thousands of network evaluations
[

. Mean Flows

]
are a recent class of methods
that remove this cost for unconditional and class-conditional generation. Rather than learning
the instantaneous velocity of a flow and integrating it step by step, a
Mean Flow learns the
average
velocity between any two time points
directly, collapsing the entire trajectory into a single network
evaluation. To date, however, this idea has not been applied to imaging inverse problems.

In this letter, we propose NullFlow, which brings the Mean Flow into the null space of the forward operator, thus enabling a one-step posterior sampler. We make three contributions:

•

Method.
We propose the first one-step solver for imaging inverse
problems based on mean flows. Confining the flow to
\mathrm{null}({\bm{A}})

keeps every state measurement-consistent by construction, and learning its
average velocity yields reconstruction in a single network evaluation.

•

Theory.
We prove that the average
velocity of this constrained flow satisfies a MeanFlow identity restricted
to the null space, yielding a training objective whose global minimizer is
a one-step posterior sampler
{\bm{x}}\sim p({\bm{x}}|{\bm{y}})
.

•

Experiments.
We show on image inpainting that one NullFlow sample gives the best LPIPS in a single step; averaging 100 samples approaches the MMSE estimate and exceeds the MSE-trained network in PSNR and SSIM, without retraining.

Proposed Method

Our method is motivated by the observation that any
{\bm{x}}
can be decomposed into the row space projection and null space projection of an ill-posed
{\bm{A}}
:

{\bm{x}}=\underbrace{{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}\vphantom{({\bm{I}}-{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{A}})}}_{\text{row space projection}}+\underbrace{({\bm{I}}-{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{A}}){\bm{x}}}_{\text{null space projection}},

where
{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}
is the pseudo-inverse of
{\bm{A}}
. We write

{{\bm{P}}}:={\bm{I}}-{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{A}}
for the orthogonal projection onto

\mathrm{null}({\bm{A}})
, which we use throughout. In the noiseless measurement setting, the inverse problem can be viewed as reconstructing the null-space components, conditioned on the measurement
{\bm{y}}
; the set of all images that are measurement consistent is the affine subspace

{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{y}}:=\{{\bm{x}}:{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}={\bm{y}}\}.

(2)

Our objective is to learn a one-step Mean Flow, which we call NullFlow,
that exists completely within
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bm{y}}}

and samples from the conditional image distribution
p({\bm{x}}|{\bm{y}})
(see
Fig.
1
).

With measurement noise,
{\bm{y}}
no longer determines the row-space component
exactly. One may instead anchor it at the least-squares estimate

{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{y}}
and reconstruct only the null-space component
by defining the flow within
\{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}:{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}={\bm{A}}{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{y}}\}
.
This is equivalent to drawing a
posterior sample from the target image distribution
{\bm{w}}\sim p(\cdot|{\bm{y}})
and forming

{\bm{x}}^{\prime}={\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{y}}+{{\bm{P}}}{\bm{w}}
.
For the remainder of the paper,
we assume noiseless measurements,
and use
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{y}}
given in (
2
).

2.1
Subspace-Restricted Flow Matching

We adapt the flow matching of
[
11
]
to the subspace

{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{y}}
, confining every object—path, flow, and velocity—to

{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{y}}
, whose tangent space is
\mathrm{null}({\bm{A}})
. This keeps every
state measurement-consistent without a separate data-fidelity step.

We transport between two distributions supported on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{y}}
. The
source is
{\bm{x}}_{0}\sim q(\cdot|{\bm{y}})
, with

{\bm{x}}_{0}={\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{y}}+{{\bm{P}}}{\bm{z}}
and

{\bm{z}}\sim{\mathcal{N}}({\bm{0}},{\bm{I}})
, which pins the row-space component to the
least-squares estimate
{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{y}}
and fills the null space with
Gaussian noise. The target is
{\bm{x}}_{1}\sim p(\cdot|{\bm{y}})
, the
conditional image distribution. We seek a probability path

p_{t}(\cdot|{\bm{y}})
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{y}}
, with

p_{0}(\cdot|{\bm{y}})=q(\cdot|{\bm{y}})
and

p_{1}(\cdot|{\bm{y}})\approx p(\cdot|{\bm{y}})
, that interpolates
between them.

We take the conditional path toward a target
{\bm{x}}_{1}
to be Gaussian with
covariance confined to
\mathrm{null}({\bm{A}})
,

p_{t}({\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{y}})=\mathcal{N}\big({\bm{x}}\,\big|\,{\bm{\mu}}_{t}({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{y}}),\,\sigma_{t}^{2}({\bm{x}}_{1}){{\bm{P}}}\big),

(3)

with time-dependent mean
{\bm{\mu}}_{t}
and scale
\sigma_{t}>0
subject to the
endpoint conditions
{\bm{\mu}}_{0}={\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{y}},\,\sigma_{0}=1
and

{\bm{\mu}}_{1}={\bm{x}}_{1},\,\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{\mathop{\mathsf{min}}}\ll 1
, so that

p_{0}=q(\cdot|{\bm{y}})
and
p_{1}
concentrates at

{\bm{x}}_{1}
. Since
{\bm{A}}{\bm{\mu}}_{t}={\bm{y}}
and
\sigma_{t}^{2}{{\bm{P}}}
spreads mass only along
\mathrm{null}({\bm{A}})
, the path
is supported on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{y}}
for all
t
.

To realize this path deterministically, we use the location-scale map of the
Gaussian, the conditional flow

{{\bm{\psi}}}_{t}({\bm{x}}\,|\,{\bm{y}})=\sigma_{t}({\bm{x}}_{1})\,{{\bm{P}}}{\bm{x}}+{\bm{\mu}}_{t}({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{y}}),

(4)

which scales the null-space content of
{\bm{x}}
by
\sigma_{t}
and centers it at

{\bm{\mu}}_{t}
. Pushing a reference
{{\bm{P}}}{\bm{x}}\sim{\mathcal{N}}({\bm{0}},{{\bm{P}}})

through
{{\bm{\psi}}}_{t}
reproduces (
3
) at every
t
.

The flow
{{\bm{\phi}}}_{t}(\cdot|{\bm{y}}):{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bm{y}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bm{y}}}
is
generated by the velocity field
{\bm{v}}_{t}
, the time-dependent field whose
integral curves are its trajectories, solving

\frac{{\mathrm{\,d}}}{{\mathrm{\,d}}t}{{\bm{\phi}}}_{t}({\bm{x}}|{\bm{y}})={\bm{v}}_{t}\big({{\bm{\phi}}}_{t}({\bm{x}}|{\bm{y}})\,\big|\,{\bm{y}}\big),\qquad{{\bm{\phi}}}_{0}({\bm{x}}|{\bm{y}})={\bm{x}}.

(5)

The crucial constraint is that
{\bm{v}}_{t}\in\mathrm{null}({\bm{A}})
: a step
along a null-space direction preserves
{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}={\bm{y}}
, so
{{\bm{\phi}}}_{t}
maps

{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{y}}
into itself and every state remains measurement-consistent.

We instantiate the mean and scale to vary linearly in
t
,

{\bm{\mu}}_{t}({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{y}})=t\,{{\bm{P}}}{\bm{x}}_{1}+{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{y}},\qquad\sigma_{t}({\bm{x}}_{1})=1-(1-\sigma_{\text{min}})t,

(6)

so that (
4
) becomes

{{\bm{\psi}}}_{t}({\bm{x}}\,|\,{\bm{y}})=\big(1-(1-\sigma_{\text{min}})t\big)\,{{\bm{P}}}{\bm{x}}+t\,{{\bm{P}}}{\bm{x}}_{1}+{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{y}},

(7)

moving the null-space content from
{{\bm{P}}}{\bm{x}}
to
{{\bm{P}}}{\bm{x}}_{1}
while
holding the anchor
{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{y}}
fixed.
Differentiating (
7
) in
t
and re-expressing at the
current state
{\bm{x}}
yields the velocity field

{\bm{v}}_{t}({\bm{x}}|{\bm{y}})={{\bm{P}}}\big({\bm{x}}_{1}-(1-\sigma_{\text{min}})\,{\bm{x}}\big)\in\mathrm{null}({\bm{A}}).

(8)

Lemmas
1
and
3
in the appendix extend

[
11
]
to show
{\bm{v}}_{t}

generates (
3
) on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{y}}
, so the flow transports

q(\cdot|{\bm{y}})
to
p(\cdot|{\bm{y}})
.

2.2
One-Step Sampling via Mean Flow

Sampling with the flow of Section
2.1
requires integrating
the instantaneous velocity (
8
) from
t=0
to

t=1
, which costs many network evaluations. To reconstruct in a single step,
we instead learn the
average
velocity of the flow, following the Mean
Flow framework
[
29
]
, but restricted to
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{y}}
.

Writing
{\bm{u}}
for the average velocity and
{\bm{v}}
for the
instantaneous velocity, the
average conditional velocity
between two
times
r\leq t
is the time-average of
{\bm{v}}
along the trajectory,

{\bm{u}}_{r,t}({\bm{x}}_{t}|{\bm{y}}):=\frac{1}{t-r}\int_{r}^{t}{\bm{v}}_{\tau}({\bm{x}}_{\tau}|{\bm{y}})\,{\mathrm{\,d}}\tau,

(9)

with
0\leq r\leq t\leq 1
. The value of learning
{\bm{u}}_{r,t}
directly is that it enables production of a sample by a single jump from
r=0
to
t=1
, with no step-by-step integration of
{\bm{v}}
.

We convert (
9
) into a pointwise constraint
relating
{\bm{u}}
and
{\bm{v}}
that is better suited for learning.
Multiplying by
(t-r)
and differentiating in
t
gives the
MeanFlow
identity
over
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{y}}
,

{\bm{u}}_{r,t}({\bm{x}}_{t}|{\bm{y}})={\bm{v}}_{t}({\bm{x}}_{t}|{\bm{y}})-(t-r)\frac{{\mathrm{\,d}}}{{\mathrm{\,d}}t}{\bm{u}}_{r,t}({\bm{x}}_{t}|{\bm{y}}),

(10)

which reduces to

{\bm{u}}_{t,t}={\bm{v}}_{t}
when
r=t
. Crucially,
(
10
) involves only
{\bm{v}}_{t}
and a derivative of

{\bm{u}}_{r,t}
, both available without integration, turning (
9
) into a regression target.

This identity yields a training objective whose minimizer is the desired
average velocity. We parametrize
{\bm{u}}^{{\bm{\theta}}}
by a network and
regress it against the right-hand side of (
10
), with

{\bm{v}}_{t}
supplied in closed form by (
8
).

Proposition 1
.

Let
{\bm{A}}
be a fixed matrix,
{\bm{y}}
the noiseless measurement, and

{{\bm{P}}}:={\bm{I}}-{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{A}}
the null-space projection.
Assume
{\bm{u}}^{{\bm{\theta}}}
is a universal approximator of vector
fields on
\mathrm{null}({\bm{A}})
. If

{\bm{u}}^{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}_{r,t}({\bm{x}}|{\bm{y}})
is the global minimizer
of

\mathbb{E}\left\|{\bm{u}}^{{\bm{\theta}}}_{r,t}({\bm{z}}|{\bm{y}})-{\bm{v}}+(t-r)\frac{{\mathrm{\,d}}}{{\mathrm{\,d}}t}{\bm{u}}^{{\bm{\theta}}}_{r,t}({\bm{z}}|{\bm{y}})\right\|_{2}^{2},

where the expectation is over
r\leq t\sim{\mathcal{U}}[0,1]
,
({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{y}})\sim q({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{y}})
,
\bm{\epsilon}\sim{\mathcal{N}}({\bm{0}},{\bm{I}})
, and

\displaystyle{\bm{z}}

\displaystyle:=(1-(1-\sigma_{\mathop{\mathsf{min}}})t)\,{{\bm{P}}}\bm{\epsilon}+t\,{{\bm{P}}}{\bm{x}}_{1}+{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{y}},

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}

\displaystyle:={{\bm{P}}}({\bm{x}}_{1}-(1-\sigma_{\mathop{\mathsf{min}}})\bm{\epsilon}).

then
{\bm{u}}^{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}_{r,t}({\bm{x}}|{\bm{y}})
is a Mean Flow in the
null space of
{\bm{A}}
that samples from
p({\bm{x}}|{\bm{y}})
.

The proof is given in the Appendix. This is the first theoretical result establishing a one-step posterior sampler for imaging inverse problems, extending
the unconditional Mean Flow of
[
29
]
to the subspace-restricted
flow of Section
2.1
. Since
{\bm{u}}^{{\bm{\theta}}}
is a
universal approximator, the global minimum of the
\ell_{2}
loss enforces the
MeanFlow identity (
10
) pointwise, and the resulting
field is the average velocity of a flow that transports
q(\cdot|{\bm{y}})

to
p(\cdot|{\bm{y}})
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bm{y}}
.
Algorithms
1
and
2
provide the implementation details of NullFlow, where reconstruction is initialized with
{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{y}}+{{\bm{P}}}\bm{\epsilon}
and

{\bm{u}}^{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}_{0,1}
is evaluated once.

We illustrate NullFlow on the problem of image inpainting using the FFHQ dataset.
We consider noiseless center-mask inpainting on
256\times 256
RGB images,
where a fixed
128\times 128
center patch is removed. During training, observed
pixels are fixed and missing pixels are filled with independent Gaussian noise.

Following
[
31
]
, we train the network with the PMF objective

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PMF}}=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\limits_{r,t,{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},\bm{\epsilon}}\left\|{\bm{u}}^{\theta}_{r,t}({\bm{x}}_{t}|{\bm{y}})-{\bm{u}}_{r,t}({\bm{x}}_{t}|{\bm{y}})\right\|_{2}^{2},

(11)

where
{\bm{u}}_{r,t}({\bm{x}}_{t}|{\bm{y}})
is the target defined by
(
10
). The total derivative in this target is evaluated
efficiently using a Jacobian-vector product, avoiding explicit formation of the
network Jacobian. To improve visual fidelity, we augment
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PMF}}
with
VGG-based LPIPS
[
32
]
and frozen ConvNeXt-V2 feature
regularization
[
33
]
:

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PMF}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{p}}\ell_{\mathrm{p}}(\hat{\bm{x}},\bm{x})+\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}\|\phi(\hat{\bm{x}})-\phi(\bm{x})\|_{2}^{2},

(12)

where
\phi
denotes the ConvNeXt-V2 feature extractor. We set

\lambda_{\mathrm{p}}=0.4
and
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}=0.1
.

At inference, we initialize with the null-space-perturbed least-squares
estimate
{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{y}}+{{\bm{P}}}\bm{\epsilon}
: for center-mask
inpainting, this preserves the observed pixels and fills the missing center
with Gaussian noise. The trained model maps this initialization to the final
reconstruction in a single step.

As shown in Table
1
, NullFlow achieves the best LPIPS with
only one function evaluation, while maintaining competitive PSNR and SSIM.
U-Net obtains the highest distortion metrics, as expected from its MSE
objective, but its reconstructions are overly smooth and have worse perceptual
quality. This perception–distortion tradeoff is also visible in
Fig.
2
: a single NullFlow sample gives sharper and
more faithful details, whereas averaging many samples produces a smoother
MSE/MMSE-like estimate. Fig.
3
confirms this trend
quantitatively, showing that sample averaging improves PSNR and SSIM but
degrades LPIPS. NullFlow thus provides both a perceptual one-step reconstruction and, through sample averaging, a distortion-oriented estimate
without retraining.

4
Conclusion

We introduced NullFlow, the first one-step generative solver for imaging
inverse problems. By confining a Mean Flow to the null space of the forward
operator, every state stays measurement-consistent by construction and
NullFlow samples from
p({\bm{x}}|{\bm{y}})
in a single network
evaluation. On image inpainting it matches the perceptual quality of iterative
diffusion and flow solvers at a fraction of the cost. Extending NullFlow to noisy measurements and to sample-varying operators are promising future
directions.
