Counting Frobenius extensions over local function fields
Jürgen Klüners, Raphael Müller

Abstract
. We determine the asymptotic growth of extensions of local function fields of characteristic p p counted by discriminant, where the Galois group is a subgroup of the affine group AGL 1 ⁡ ( p ) \operatorname{AGL}_{1}(p) . More general, we solve the corresponding counting problems for all groups which arise in a tower of a cyclic extension of order p p over a cyclic extension of degree d d coprime to p p . This in particular give answers for certain non-abelian groups including S 3 S_{3} , dihedral groups of order 2 ​ p 2p , and many Frobenius groups.

1.
Introduction

There is a lot of interest to
ask how many extensions with a given Galois group and bounded discriminant exist, and what is the asymptotic behaviour when this bound goes to infinity.
We would like to count non-normal extensions as well, and therefore we define by abuse of notation a Galois group for non-normal extensions.
For this, let
G\leq S_{n}
be a finite transitive permutation group on
n
points and let
k
be a
field. We will write
\operatorname{Gal}(K/k)=G
if
K/k
is a field
extension such that the Galois group of the Galois closure
\hat{K}/k
viewed as
permutation group on the set of embeddings of
K
into
\hat{K}
is permutation-isomorphic to
G
. For example when we consider
S_{3}\leq S_{3}
we count degree 3-extensions, and by looking at
S_{3}(6)\leq S_{6}
we count Galois
S_{3}
-extensions.

For number fields
k
and transitive permutation groups
G\leq S_{n}
, the classical case is to count by the norm of the relative discriminant.

Z(k,G;X):=\#\{K/k~:~\operatorname{Gal}(K/k)=G,\quad N_{k/\mathbb{Q}}(\operatorname{D}(K/k))\leq X\}.

For a given
X\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
Z(k,G;X)<\infty
. When
k
is a
p
-adic field, the total number of extensions is bounded. The same is true when
k
is a local function field of characteristic
p
, and when
p
is coprime to
|G|
.
In all other cases, it is interesting to study the behaviour of
Z(k,G;X)
for
X\to\infty
.

Gunter Malle proposed in
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
a conjecture for the asymptotic behaviour of
Z(k,G;X)
for number fields and
finite transitive permutation groups. The Malle conjecture predicts explicit constants
a(G),b(k,G)
such that

(1)

Z(k,G;X)\sim c(k,G)\cdot X^{a(G)}\log(X)^{b(k,G)-1}

for some constant
c(k,G)>0
.

In this work, we focus on the discriminant distribution over local function fields of characteristic
p
of some non-abelian groups
G
satisfying
p\mid|G|
. Let
F
be a local function field of characteristic
p
, i.e., a Laurent series ring over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q=p^{r}
elements. For
p\nmid d
let
L/F
be a
C_{d}
-extension, where
C_{d}
denotes the cyclic group of order
d
. Note that there are only finitely many such
L
. Now we study the case that
\operatorname{Gal}(M/L)=C_{p}
. Consider the factorisation

X^{d}-1=f_{1}(X)\cdots f_{r}(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X],

and let
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}
, and denote by
\ell(I):=\deg(\prod_{i\in I}f_{i}).

We describe all potential Galois groups
\operatorname{Gal}(M/F)
arising in those towers.
We denote those groups (see (
16
)) of order
dp^{\ell(I)}
by
G_{p}(d,I)
.
We study the corresponding discriminant counting functions, and prove the asymptotic behaviour of such functions.

The main results of this paper are described in the following theorem (see Theorems
4.5
and
5.1
), where we count by bounded discriminant exponent. The first function counts fields of degree
pd
, and the second function counts fields of degree
dp^{\ell(I)}
, i.e. Galois extensions. A precise definition of
Z_{pd}
and
Z_{dp^{\ell(I)}}
is given in (
28
).

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist a
(p-1)pd
-periodic function
\beta_{I}(x)
and a
(p^{\ell(I)}-1)pd
-periodic function
\tilde{\beta}_{I}(x)
such that

(1)

considered as a permutation group over
pd
points, we get for
x\rightarrow\infty
:

Z_{pd}\left(F,G_{p}(d,I);x\right)\sim\beta_{I}(x)q^{ax}\text{, where }a:=\frac{\ell(I)}{pd}.

(2)

considered as a permutation group over
dp^{\ell(I)}
points, we get for
x\rightarrow\infty
:

Z_{dp^{\ell(I)}}\left(F,G_{p}(d,I)\right);x)\sim\tilde{\beta}_{I}(x)q^{\tilde{a}x}\text{, where }\tilde{a}=\frac{p-1}{pd}\frac{\ell(I)}{p^{\ell(I)}-1}.

The first part of this theorem was already proved in the PhD thesis
[
14
]
of the second author. The second part was only proved in the special case that
|I|=1
. When
\ell(I)=1
, some special cases are the dihedral groups of order
2p
, or more general Frobenius groups
C_{p}\rtimes C_{d}
for
d\mid(p-1)
. Other groups in this list for
p=2
are
A_{4}\cong C_{2}^{2}\rtimes C_{3}
or
C_{2}^{3}\rtimes C_{7}
.

We see that the asymptotic behaviour is only depending on the group order
dp^{\ell{(I)}}
. The oscillating function is additionally depending on the chosen
I
.
This means that the subgroups of
\operatorname{AGL}(1,p)\cong C_{p}\rtimes C_{d}
have the same asymptotic behaviour as
C_{p}
on
p
points or
C_{p}\times C_{d}
on
pd
points.

1.1.
History

For Malle’s conjecture, there has been a lot of activity in the number field situation, and in the case of global function fields when the characteristic is coprime to the group order. Let us focus on the case of positive characteristic. Wright
[
18
]
proved equation (
1
)
for every finite abelian group
A
and over every global field

such that
\operatorname{char}(k)\nmid|A|
, prior to the works of Malle. Ellenberg and Venkatesh
[
1
]
gave a heuristic why Malle‘s conjecture should be true in the global function field situation when the group order is coprime to
p
(tame case). In recent works of Landesman and Levy
[
11
, Theorem 10.1.10]
and Santens
[
17
]
the conjecture in the tame case is proved over rational function fields assuming that the constant field size is large enough.

However, the exponent
a(G)
in (
1
) is incorrect if the characteristic
p
divides the group order. Lagemann
[
9
, Prop. 2.4]
proved lower bounds for all elementary-abelian
p
-extensions. For non-cyclic groups, those lower bounds (except possibly for the group
C_{2}\times C_{2}
) grow larger than (
1
) indicates. Potthast
[
16
]
proved the discriminant counting for all elementary abelian
p
-groups in characteristic
p
. The asymptotics of

has one log-factor more than a corresponding Malle conjecture would predict. Other interesting results are given in
[
3
,
2
]
, where the counting (for rational global function fields) is done by the last jump in the upper numbering of the higher ramification groups. In
[
3
]
, the surprising result is that the counting function over rational function fields is a rational function. In
[
2
]
Gundlach and Seguin, deal with some cases of non-abelian
p
-groups over rational global function fields. Already Lagemann
[
9
,
10
]
proved the corresponding asymptotics for counting abelian
p
-groups by conductor over global function fields of characteristic
p
.

It is well known that there are infinitely many extensions for a given
p
-group
G
over a local function field of characteristic
p
. Therefore, it is an interesting problem to ask for the asymptotic behaviour of the number of such extensions.
Lagemann solved the case of counting abelian
p
-groups over local function fields by discriminant in
[
8
]
. The corresponding count over local function fields by conductor is given in
[
7
]
. In the PhD-thesis
[
14

of the second author there are asymptotic results for (generalized) Heisenberg groups of

1.2.
Structure of the paper

We will consider this problem in the situation of a local function field
F
of characteristic
p
and a tower of fields
M/L/F
such that
\operatorname{Gal}(M/L)\cong C_{p}
and

\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)\cong C_{d}
is cyclic with
p\nmid d
.
By Artin-Schreier Theory, the
C_{p}
-extensions of
L
can be described as
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subspaces of the vector space
L/\wp(L)
,
where
\wp(x)=x^{p}-x
is the Artin-Schreier operator.
In this situation,
L
and
L/\wp(L)
have a nice
\mathbb{F}_{p}[C_{d}]
-module structure which we can use to describe the discriminant counting function for all occurring Galois groups in this setting.

In Chapter 2, we collect known results about tamely ramified extensions and Artin-Schreier theory. In Chapter 3, we study
\mathbb{F}_{p}[C_{d}]
-modules and we show in Proposition
3.5
how to compute the Galois group and the discriminant, when we know the Artin-Schreier generator.

In Chapter 4, we prove the asymptotics when we consider our groups
G_{p}(d,I)\leq S_{pd}
on
pd
points. In Chapter 3, we showed that we have to choose our Artin-Schreier generators from some (generalized) eigenspaces. Since there are only finitely many
C_{d}
-extensions
L
, we count all extensions over all possible fixed
L
. In the end, the results follows by a finite summation.

In Chapter 5, we deal with the Galois situation, and the strategy is similar. The major problem is to control the discriminant of the elementary abelian
p
-extension of the Galois closure over
L
. Here we have the same type of problems as in the elementary abelian case which was solved in
[
16
]
, and we use the methods described in that paper.

2.1.
Tamely ramified cyclic extensions

There is a well-known classification of the finitely many (at most) tamely ramified
C_{d}
-extensions of a local field
F
.

Theorem 2.1

(Classification of tamely ramified extensions)
.

Let
F
be a local function field with residue class field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and prime element
\pi_{F}
. Let
L/F
be an at most tamely ramified extension with ramification index
e=e_{L/F}
and inertia degree
f=f_{L/F}
. Let
\left(\mathbb{F}_{q^{f}}\right)^{\times}=\langle\omega\rangle
and
g:=\gcd(e,q^{f}-1)
.

(a)

Then
L
is conjugate to exactly one field
F(\omega,\sqrt[e]{\omega^{r}\pi_{F}})
where
0\leq r<g
.

(b)

L/F
is a Galois extension if and only if
e\mid(q^{f}-1)
and

e\mid r(q-1)
.

Let
\pi_{L}:=\sqrt[e]{\omega^{r}\pi_{F}}
. If
L/F
is Galois, then
\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)=\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle
with

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\omega,

\displaystyle\ \sigma_{1}(\pi_{L})

\displaystyle=\omega^{l}\cdot\pi_{L},

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\omega^{q},

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(\pi_{L})

\displaystyle=\omega^{k}\cdot\pi_{L},

where
k=\frac{r(q-1)}{e}
and
l=\frac{q^{f}-1}{e}
. The Galois group has the finite presentation

\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)=\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}~|~\sigma_{1}^{e}=1,\ \sigma_{1}^{r}=\sigma_{2}^{f},\ \sigma_{1}^{\sigma_{2}}=\sigma_{1}^{q}\rangle.

(c)

The extension
L/F
is abelian if and only if
e\mid(q-1)
.

It is moreover cyclic if and only if
e\mid(q-1)
and
\gcd(e,f,r)=1
. In this case we have a generator
\sigma=\sigma_{1}^{N}\sigma_{2}
, where

(2)

N:=\begin{cases}\prod\limits_{\ell\in\mathbb{P},\ell\mid e,\ell\nmid r}\ell,&\gcd(e,r)>1\\
0,&\gcd(e,r)=1.\end{cases}

In particular, the generator
\sigma
of
\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)
has the property

(3)

\sigma(\omega)=\omega^{q},\quad\sigma(\pi_{L})=\omega^{k+Nl}\cdot\pi_{L}.

Proof.

(a) and (b) are proven in
[
4
, Chapter 16, p. 249ff]
.

In (c) note that
\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{1})=e
and
\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)
is abelian if and only if

\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{1}^{\sigma_{2}}=\sigma_{1}^{q}\iff\sigma_{1}^{q-1}=\operatorname{id}\iff\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{1})=e\mid(q-1).

If
\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)=\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle
is abelian, we have

\exp(\operatorname{Gal}(L/F))=\operatorname{lcm}\left(\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{1}),\ \operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{2})\right).

Concerning
\sigma_{2}
, we use

\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{1})=e
to get

\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{2})\overset{(b)}{=}f\cdot\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{1}^{r})=f\cdot\frac{e}{\gcd(e,r)}.

Thus,
\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)
is cyclic if and only if
\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)
is abelian and

\displaystyle d=\exp(\operatorname{Gal}(L/F))\iff

\displaystyle e\cdot f=\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{1}),\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{2}))=\operatorname{lcm}(e,\ f\frac{e}{\gcd(e,r)})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle e\cdot f=\frac{e}{\gcd(e,r)}\cdot\operatorname{lcm}\left(\gcd(e,r),\ f\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\cdot\ \frac{\gcd(e,r)}{e}}{\iff}

\displaystyle\gcd(e,r)\cdot f=\operatorname{lcm}(\gcd(e,r),\ f)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\gcd(e,r,f)=1.

Now we can assume that
L/F
is cyclic.
We have
\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{1})=e
and
\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{2})=\frac{ef}{\gcd(e,r)}
.
Then
\sigma=\sigma_{2}
is trivially a generator for
\gcd(e,r)=1
which implies
N=0
.

In the case
\gcd(e,r)>1
let
\ell
be a prime with

•

\ell\nmid e
: Then
\nu_{\ell}(ef)=\nu_{\ell}(f)=\nu_{\ell}(\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{2}))=\nu_{\ell}(\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{1}^{N}\sigma_{2}))
.

•

\ell\mid e,\ell\nmid r
: This implies
\ell\mid N
,
\ell\nmid\gcd(e,r)
and therefore

\nu_{\ell}\left(\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{2})\right)=\nu_{\ell}(ef)
. Since
\nu_{\ell}(\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{1}^{N}))<\nu_{\ell}(ef)
we get that

\nu_{\ell}\left(\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{1}^{N}\sigma_{2})\right)=\nu_{\ell}(\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{2}))=\nu_{\ell}(ef)
as wanted.

•

\ell\mid e,\ell\mid r
: This implies
\ell\nmid f
, and
\ell\nmid N
. We therefore get that

\nu_{\ell}\left(\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{2})\right)<\nu_{\ell}(ef)=\nu_{\ell}(e)
and

\nu_{\ell}(\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{1}^{N}\sigma_{2}))=\nu_{\ell}(\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}))=\nu_{\ell}(\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_{1}))=\nu_{\ell}(e)=\nu_{\ell}(ef).

Hence, the element
\sigma:=\sigma_{1}^{N}\sigma_{2}
for
N
defined in (
2
)
has order
ef
and
is a generator of the Galois group.
Finally, for the last assertion we have

\sigma_{2}(\omega)=\omega^{q}=\sigma_{1}^{N}\sigma_{2}(\omega)\ \text{ and }\ \sigma_{2}(\pi_{L})=\omega^{k}\pi_{L}

and

\sigma_{1}^{N}\sigma_{2}(\pi_{L})=\sigma_{1}^{N}(\omega^{k}\pi_{L})=\omega^{k}\omega^{Nl}\pi_{L}.\qed

2.2.
Artin-Schreier Theory

For the moment, let
L
be any field with
\operatorname{char}(L)=p
. We define the Artin-Schreier operator

\wp\colon L\to L,\quad x\mapsto x^{p}-x.

This map
\wp
is
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear with kernel

\ker(\wp)=\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
We moreover define
J(L):=L/\wp(L)
as the cokernel of
\wp
.
We will write
\theta_{a}
for a solution of
X^{p}-X-a
. Note the factorisation
X^{p}-X-a=\prod_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}(X-\theta_{a}+\lambda)
.

By Artin-Schreier theory,
J(L)
is a parameterising space for all elementary
p
-extensions of
L
, e.g. see

[
15
, p. 294f]
:

Theorem 2.2

(Artin-Schreier theory)
.

Let
L
be a field with
\operatorname{char}(L)=p
.

(a)

There is a
1:1
-correspondence

\displaystyle\Delta\colon\{\mathbb{F}_{p}\text{-subspaces }U\leq L/\wp(L)\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\{\text{p-elementary field extensions }L^{\prime}/L\}

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle\longmapsto L(\wp^{-1}(U)).

(b)

For
U\leq L/\wp(L)
and
L^{\prime}=L\left(\wp^{-1}(U)\right)
there is a canonical isomorphism

U\cong\operatorname{Hom}\left(\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/L),\mathbb{F}_{p}\right),\quad a+\wp(F)\mapsto\chi_{a}\quad\text{via }\chi_{a}(\sigma)=(\sigma-1)(\theta_{a}).

(c)

Let
U\leq L/\wp(L)
be finite and
(a_{1}+\wp(L),\ldots,a_{r}+\wp(L))
be an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-basis of
U
. Then
the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}\left(L\left(\wp^{-1}(U)\right)/L\right)\cong\left(C_{p}\right)^{r}
is generated by the automorphisms
\sigma_{i}
with

\sigma_{i}(\theta_{a_{j}})=\theta_{a_{j}}+\delta_{i,j}\quad\text{for }\quad 1\leq i\leq r,\ 1\leq j\leq r,

where
\delta_{i,j}
is the Kronecker-Delta.

We apply Artin-Schreier theory to a field
L
which is given as a cyclic degree
d
extension of a local function field
F
. Let us therefore assume that
L
is given as the Laurent series ring
\mathbb{F}_{q^{f}}((\pi_{L}))
, where
\pi_{L}
is a prime element and the constant field has
q^{f}
elements.

Choosing
\omega_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{f}}\setminus\wp(\mathbb{F}_{q^{f}})
we get
a nice system of representatives of
L/\wp(L)
by, e.g. see
[
16
, Prop 5.2 (b)]

R_{L}(\pi_{L},\omega_{0}):=\left\{\mu_{0}\omega_{0}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<0\\
p\nmid i\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}\pi_{L}^{i}~|~\mu_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p},\ \lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{f}}\right\}.

Usually, we simply write
R_{L}
.
For any
\beta\in L\setminus\wp(L)
we define

\nu_{J(L)}\left(\left[\beta\right]\right):=\max_{y\in L}\nu_{L}\left(\beta+\wp(y)\right)\leq 0.

Independently of the choices of
\pi_{L}
and
\omega_{0}
, the representative system
R_{L}
has the following properties:

Lemma 2.3
.

R_{L}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space with
\nu_{L}(\alpha)\leq 0
for all
0\neq\alpha\in R_{L}
and

\nu_{J(L)}([\alpha])=\nu_{L}(\alpha)\text{ for }0\neq\alpha\in R_{L}.

This lemma is useful to determine the conductor and the discriminant of an Artin-Schreier extension.

Definition 2.4
.

For
\alpha\in L\setminus\wp(L)
we define the conductor exponent via

\operatorname{Cond}_{L}(\alpha):=\begin{cases}0,&\nu_{J(L)}\left(\left[\alpha\right]\right)=0\\
\lvert\nu_{J(L)}\left(\left[\alpha\right]\right)\rvert+1,&\nu_{J(L)}\left(\left[\alpha\right]\right)<0.\end{cases}

For such an
\alpha
the conductor coincides with the usual conductor for cyclic extensions and therefore we get the following formula for the discriminant exponent.

\operatorname{disc}(L^{\prime}/L)=(p-1)\operatorname{Cond}_{L}(\alpha).

For a subgroup
U\leq L/\wp(L)
, we use the abelian conductor discriminant formula (see
[
6
, Thm. 7.15]
): Let
L^{\prime}:=L\left(\wp^{-1}(U)\right)
.
Let
0\neq\chi_{a}\in\operatorname{Hom}\left(\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/L),\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)
,
then
\wp^{-1}(a)\leq\operatorname{Fix}\left(\operatorname{Ker}(\chi_{a})\right)
by definition, hence by comparing degrees, we get

L^{\prime}_{\chi_{a}}=\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Ker}(\chi_{a}))=L\left(\wp^{-1}(a)\right)

and

\mathfrak{f}(\chi_{a})=\mathfrak{f}\left(F(\wp^{-1}(a))/F\right)=\operatorname{Cond}_{L}\left(a\right).

Thus, for the discriminant exponent of an Artin-Schreier extension with respect to
U\leq L/\wp(L)
, we have

(4)

\operatorname{disc}\left(L^{\prime}/L\right)=\operatorname{disc}\left(L(\wp^{-1}(U))/L\right)=\sum_{0\neq a\in U}\operatorname{Cond}_{L}(a).

Finally, for
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(L/F)
holds

(5)

\sigma\circ\wp=\wp\circ\sigma.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
0\neq\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{r}\in Y_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus Y_{r}=R_{L}
.
Let
I:=\left\{i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}~|~\alpha_{i}\neq 0\right\}
.
Then we have

\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]}\cong M_{I}
as
\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]
-modules and

\operatorname{Gal}(L(\wp^{-1}(\alpha))/F)\cong G_{p}(d,I)\leq S_{pd}.

The discriminant equals

(17)

\operatorname{disc}(L\left(\wp^{-1}(\alpha)\right)/F)=pd-f+(p-1)f|\nu_{L}(\alpha)|.

Proof.

The splitting field
L_{\alpha}
(see figure on page
1
) of
L(\wp^{-1}(\alpha))/F
is

\displaystyle L_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=L\left(\wp^{-1}\left(\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]}\right)\right),\text{ where }\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]}=\left\{g(\sigma)(\alpha)~:~g(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]\right\}.

For
g(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
, we have

g(\sigma)(\alpha)=0\iff g(\sigma)(\alpha_{i})=0\ \text{for all }i\in I\iff f_{i}(X)\mid g(X)\text{ for all }i\in I,

hence
\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]/(\prod_{i\in I}f_{i})=M_{I}
.

Finally,
\operatorname{Gal}\left(L_{\alpha}/L\right)
is a normal subgroup of

\operatorname{Gal}\left(L_{\alpha}/F\right)
, since its fixed field
L
is Galois over
F
. With
\gcd(p,d)=1
and the Theorem of Schur-Zassenhaus (see
[
5
, Hauptsatz I.18.1]
) we have that
\operatorname{Gal}\left(L_{\alpha}/F\right)
is a
semi-direct product
with action determined by
\sigma\cdot g(\alpha):=g(\sigma)(\alpha)

which concludes the proof for the Galois group. For the discriminant note

\displaystyle\operatorname{disc}(L\left(\wp^{-1}(\alpha)\right)/F)

\displaystyle=p\operatorname{disc}(L/F)+(p-1)f(|\nu_{L}(\alpha)|+1)

\displaystyle=pf(e-1)+(p-1)f(|\nu_{L}(\alpha)|+1)

\displaystyle=pd-f+(p-1)f|\nu_{L}(\alpha)|.\qed

Now we compute discriminant of the splitting field.
Let
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{r}\in Y_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus Y_{r}
.
For
\nu_{i}:=\nu_{L}(\alpha_{i})
, write
\alpha_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=\nu_{i}}^{0}\eta_{i,j}\pi_{L}^{j},\eta_{i,j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{f}}
and set

Note that
\operatorname{lt}(\alpha_{i})\in V_{\nu_{i}}
which is
\sigma
-invariant. Hence we get

g(\sigma)(\alpha)=0\iff g(\sigma)(\alpha_{i})=0\text{ for all }i\in I\iff g(\sigma)(\operatorname{lt}(\alpha_{i}))=0\text{ for all }i\in I,

as the two last statements are both equivalent to
f_{i}\mid g
for all
i\in I
.
Thus, we have

\nu_{L}\left(g(\sigma)(\alpha_{i})\right)=\begin{cases}0,&f_{i}\mid g\\
\nu_{L}(\alpha_{i}),&\text{else,}\end{cases}

and

(18)

\nu_{L}\left(g(\sigma)(\alpha)\right)=\min\left\{\nu_{L}(\alpha_{i})~:~1\leq i\leq r,\ f_{i}\nmid g\right\}.

Thus, an ordering of the valuations of
\alpha_{i}
basically determines the field discriminant.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{r}\in R_{L}
, let
I:=\{1\leq i\leq r~:~\alpha_{i}\neq 0\}
and let

be the splitting field of

(a)

The splitting field

is the composite field of the according splitting fields

of
\alpha_{i}

(b)

Let
\ell(i):=\deg(f_{i})

and

. Then we get the discriminant exponent

\displaystyle\operatorname{disc}(L_{\alpha}/L)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\#I}p^{\ell(\tau(1))}\cdots p^{\ell(\tau(j-1))}\cdot\left(p^{\ell(\tau(j))}-1\right)\operatorname{Cond}_{L}(\alpha_{\tau(j)}).

(c)

Writing

(19)

\displaystyle\operatorname{disc}(L_{\alpha}/F)

\displaystyle=p^{\ell(I)}f(e-1)+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\#I}f\left(p^{R_{j}}-p^{R_{j-1}}\right)\operatorname{Cond}_{L}(\alpha_{\tau(j)}).

Proof.

As in Proposition
3.5

L_{\alpha}=L\left(\wp^{-1}\left(\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]}\right)\right)
.

On the one hand, we have

which shows one containment in (a).
Moreover, with

we have

for

and

, hence

thus

which concludes the second containment and the equality in (a) is established.

For (b), we have
\operatorname{disc}(L_{\alpha}/L)=\sum\limits_{0\neq\beta\in\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]}}\operatorname{Cond}_{L}(\beta)
by (
4
) and for every

\beta=g(\sigma)(\alpha)=g(\sigma)(\alpha_{1})+\ldots+g(\sigma)(\alpha_{r})\in\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]},

we have

\nu_{L}(\beta)\overset{\eqref{val-lt}}{=}\nu_{L}\left(\alpha_{\tau(\omega_{g})}\right),\quad\omega_{g}=\max\{1\leq j\leq\#I~:~g(\sigma)(\alpha_{\tau(j)})\neq 0\},

where we use the ordering of the valuations.

It remains to compute the number
N_{j}
of elements

such that
\omega_{g}=j
for

.
Using the

defined in (

\delta_{j}(\tau,I):=|M_{\tau(1)}|\cdots|M_{\tau(j-1)}|\cdot\left(|M_{\tau(j)}|-1\right)=p^{\ell(\tau(1))}\cdots p^{\ell(\tau(j-1))}\cdot\left(p^{\ell(\tau(j))}-1\right),

as this corresponds to the polynomials divisible by

for

and non-divisible by

.
Furthermore,
\nu_{L}(\beta_{i})=\nu_{L}(\alpha_{i})
for all

.
We then have

\displaystyle\operatorname{disc}(L_{\alpha}/L)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{ElementaryAbelian-Disc}}{=}\sum\limits_{0\neq\beta\in\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]}}\operatorname{Cond}_{L}(\beta)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\#I}\delta_{j}(\tau,I)\cdot\operatorname{Cond}_{L}(\alpha_{\tau(j)})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{number-MJ}}{=}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\#I}p^{\ell(\tau(1))}\cdots p^{\ell(\tau(j-1))}\cdot\left(p^{\ell(\tau(j))}-1\right)\operatorname{Cond}_{L}(\alpha_{\tau(j)}).

For part (c), use the discriminant tower formula, the discriminant formula for tamely ramified extensions and observe

.
∎

If

is a singleton, we get

. Let us define

. Then we obtain by Proposition

Remark 3.7
.

If

\operatorname{disc}(L_{\alpha}/F)=\frac{p^{\ell(I)}-1}{p-1}\operatorname{disc}(M/F)-f(e-1)\frac{p^{\ell(I)}-p}{p-1}.

Concerning part (b), we use

In the decomposition, we only need to consider

(21)

For a subset

Lemma 5.12

(Potthast)
