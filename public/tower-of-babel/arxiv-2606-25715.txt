Introduction

The companion paper
[
1
]
develops a cone-ray
pipeline for exact nonnegative matrix factorisation (NMF) of
small matrices in the
uniform-support
regime, where the
target inner dimension equals the matrix rank:
r_{+}=r=\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits(M)
. The pipeline combines the truncated SVD, the
polyhedral cone
\mathrm{col}(M)\cap\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\geq 0}
, the Double Description
Method (DDM, via Fukuda’s
cddlib

[
15
]
), an

obtuseness
ranking on
r
-subsets of cone rays, a
closed-form witness
M_{T}=R_{T}^{-1}(R_{K}^{\!\top})^{-1}
, and an
alternating linear programme (alt-LP) on augmented

r\times(r+k)
supports when the closed-form witness fails.

That work is restrictive in three respects. First,
r_{+}=r

excludes the most interesting regime of NMF, where the
nonnegative rank
strictly
exceeds the matrix rank

[
2
,
6
]
. Second, the pipeline
operates on
U_{r}
, the rank-
r
truncation of the left singular
factor, so every recovered
W
has
\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits(W)\leq r
. Third, the
columns of
U_{r}
are determined by

up to sign, so there is
no SVD gauge freedom to consider.

In the gap regime
r_{+}>r

once. We replace

by an

orthonormal
frame

, and symmetrically

V_{r}
by
V_{r_{+}}(K)=[\,V_{r}\mid V_{\!\perp}K\,]
, where

U_{\!\perp},V_{\!\perp}
are orthonormal bases of
\mathrm{col}(M)^{\perp}
and

\operatorname{row}(M)^{\perp}
respectively and

(G,K)\in\St(m{-}r,r_{+}{-}r)\times\St(n{-}r,r_{+}{-}r)

choose orthonormal directions inside each. The factorisation
constraint becomes the square

QP=\mathop{\operator@font diag}\nolimits(S_{r},\,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{+}\times r_{+}}
. Running
the same lcdd / obtuseness / alt-LP machinery on

(U_{r_{+}}(G),V_{r_{+}}(K))
extends the closed-form witness search
and the alt-LP fallback to arbitrary
r_{+}>r
.

The blind SVD picks one specific
(G,K)
for us; the question
this paper asks is
whether that blind choice matters
.

2.1
The gap constraint

Parameterise symmetrically

W=U_{r_{+}}(G)\,Q,\qquad H=P\,V_{r_{+}}(K)^{\!\top},\qquad Q,P\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{+}\times r_{+}}.

(3)

Using the SVD identity

The slack-enclosure phenomenon.

The
100/100
in Table

than the same closed-form witness phase achieves
in the companion paper at
r_{+}=r

of

[

; even with
the augmented alt-LP layer added, the hybrid gets to

. Adding a search dimension — moving
from
r_{+}=r
to
r_{+}>r
— makes the problem

strictly easier
for the obtuseness heuristic. The
explanation is geometric.

At
r_{+}=r
the data cone
\cone(M)
is
r
-dimensional

and fills
the outer cone
\mathcal{O}
in dimension
exactly; an
r
-subset of
\mathcal{O}
’s extreme rays
encloses it only when those rays are in a narrowly constrained
configuration (essentially the data cone’s own facet
structure). The feasible
r
-subsets form a measure-zero
subvariety in subset space; whether the obtuseness heuristic
hits one depends on how well its near-orthogonality criterion
correlates with the data-cone facet structure — and the
companion paper documents the cases when it does not.

At
r_{+}>r
the data cone is codimension-
(r_{+}-r)

in the outer cone
\mathcal{O}(G)
. An
r_{+}
-subset of
extreme rays has
r_{+}-r
free “padding” dimensions to
place additional rays in — almost any subset that happens
to span the
r
-dim data subspace works, and the

(r_{+}-r)
padding dimensions are filled by whatever rays
are nearby. The feasible region in subset space grows from
measure-zero to a positive-measure open set, and the
obtuseness heuristic hits a feasible subset essentially
always.

Geometrically: enclosing a flat triangle with another
triangle in the same plane is a tight algebraic constraint
(narrow feasible set of triangles); enclosing it with a
tetrahedron in
3
D is an open semi-algebraic condition (vast
family of tetrahedra). The gap regime relaxes the enclosure
problem in exactly this way. The phenomenon mirrors the
“over-parameterisation as implicit regularisation” pattern
seen in compressed sensing and deep network training: when
the underlying object is thinner than the search space, the
basin of attraction widens and reasonable heuristics find it
immediately.

the data cone is easy to find. It does not, on its own, tell
us why the blind SVD ever

second ingredient is that for dense

(W^{*},H^{*})

W_{\!*}
has
no exact zeros). Small perturbations

W^{*}\!+\delta_{W}
,
H^{*}\!+\delta_{H}
that preserve the
product
(W^{*}\!+\delta_{W})(H^{*}\!+\delta_{H})=M
stay
nonnegative for small enough
\delta
, because both factors
are bounded away from zero. Hence the set of valid NRFs is
locally an open subset of an affine space, and the induced
map

(W^{*}\!+\delta_{W})\;\longmapsto\;\operatorname{col}(W^{*}\!+\delta_{W})\;\in\;\Gr(r_{+},m)

has positive-dimensional image, and similarly for the

H
-side map

(H^{*}\!+\delta_{H})\mapsto\operatorname{row}(H^{*}\!+\delta_{H})\in\Gr(r_{+},n)
. The set of valid two-sided gauges
— those
(G,K)
for which some valid NRF has

\operatorname{col}(W^{*})=\operatorname{col}(U_{r_{+}}(G))
and

\operatorname{row}(H^{*})=\operatorname{row}(V_{r_{+}}(K))

— is therefore positive-measure on the product Grassmannian

\Gr(r_{+},m)\times\Gr(r_{+},n)
, and the blind SVD
hits it with probability one for dense random draws.

The contrast with
\mathop{\operator@font diag}\nolimits(C,J_{k})
is sharp. Additivity of
nonnegative rank over block-diagonal sums forces every
rank-
r_{+}
NRF to have

\operatorname{col}(W^{*})=\mathbb{R}^{6}_{\mathrm{top}}\,\oplus\,\operatorname{span}(e_{m-1}+e_{m})
— a single specific
subspace, independent of which valid NRF we pick. Perturbing
the top block off
\mathbb{R}^{6}_{\mathrm{top}}
would violate
block-diagonality; perturbing the bottom block off the

(1,\dots,1)
direction would violate

w_{J}h_{J}^{\!\top}=J_{k}
. The valid-NRF variety
degenerates
to a single point
in the Grassmannian, and the blind SVD
almost surely lands somewhere else. Slack enclosure is
irrelevant when there is no valid gauge to enjoy its slack.

So the dense success rests on
two
geometric facts
working together: slack enclosure (extra search dimensions
make
r_{+}
-subsets easy to find given a valid gauge) and
variety thickness (the valid-NRF family covers a
positive-measure subset of the Grassmannian so a valid gauge
exists generically). Together with the interior-perturbation
argument they explain why blind SVD plus the obtuseness
heuristic delivers
100/100
on dense gap matrices without
ever invoking the alt-LP fallback — and why on structured
matrices like
\mathop{\operator@font diag}\nolimits(C,J_{k})
, where only the first of the
three holds, the blind pipeline fails and an entirely
different branch is needed.

6.1
Why uniform sampling cannot work for this family

The geometric reason is sharper than “hard to find”:

the set of feasible gauges has measure zero in

\Gr(r_{+}-r,\,m-r)
.

By additivity of nonnegative rank over block-diagonal sums

[
2
]
every NRF of
M_{k}=\mathop{\operator@font diag}\nolimits(C,\,J_{k})

decomposes block-by-block:
W=\mathop{\operator@font diag}\nolimits(W_{C},w_{J})
,
H=\mathop{\operator@font diag}\nolimits(H_{C},h_{J}^{\!\top})
with
W_{C}H_{C}=C
and
w_{J}h_{J}^{\!\top}=J_{k}
. To
attain
\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits(W)=r_{+}=7
, we need
\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits(W_{C})=6

(since
\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits(w_{J})=1
), hence
\Span W_{C}=\mathbb{R}^{6}_{\text{top}}

(the embedding of
\mathbb{R}^{6}
into the first six coordinates of

\mathbb{R}^{6+k}
). Therefore

\Span W\;=\;\mathbb{R}^{6}_{\text{top}}\,\oplus\,\Span(e_{m-1}+e_{m})

for
every
rank-
r_{+}
NRF of
M_{k}
, where the second
summand reflects the unique direction of
w_{J}
given
w_{J}h_{J}^{\!\top}=J_{k}
. This is a
single point
in

\Gr(r_{+},m)
; equivalently, the orthogonal complement of

\mathrm{col}(M)
inside
\Span W
is a single
(r_{+}-r)=2

dimensional subspace of
\mathrm{col}(M)^{\perp}
, hence a single point in

\Gr(r_{+}-r,\,m-r)
.

A uniform sample from
\St(m-r,r_{+}-r)
hits this point with
probability
0
. Empirically,
50
samples confirm the
prediction; analytically, no finite number of uniform samples
will work.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits_{+}(M)=r_{+}>r=\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits(M)
. Let

\mathcal{F}\subset\Gr(r_{+}-r,\,m-r)\times\Gr(r_{+}-r,\,n-r)

denote the set of two-sided gauges
(G,K)
for which some
valid size-
r_{+}
NRF
(W,H)
has

\Span W\subseteq\Span U_{r_{+}}(G)
and

\Span H^{\!\top}\subseteq\Span V_{r_{+}}(K)
. If

\mathcal{F}
is a proper subvariety of the product
Grassmannian, then uniform Stiefel-product sampling recovers
an NRF with probability
0
per restart.

For block-diagonal

),
\mathcal{F}
is an isolated point (in fact, a finite
orbit under the discrete block-permutation symmetry), hence
proper. For generic dense

has positive

sampling reported in Table

is the

.

•

Derivative-free

(

Note on Stiefel vs. Grassmannian.

The loss is invariant under right-multiplication

G\to G\cdot R
for
R\in O(r_{+}-r)
(and analogously on

\Span(U_{\!\perp}\,G)=\Span(U_{\!\perp}\,G\,R)

the cone rays

unchanged. The proper search space is
therefore the product Grassmannian

\Gr(r_{+}-r,\,m-r)\times\Gr(r_{+}-r,\,n-r)
of dimension

(r_{+}-r)(m-r_{+})+(r_{+}-r)(n-r_{+})=6+6=12
for
the octagon, not the Stiefel product of dimension

construction. For Haar sampling (Experiment 2 above) the
Stiefel/Grassmannian distinction is immaterial because Haar
measure on
\St
pushes forward to Haar measure on
\Gr
, so
the multi-start experiment is already effectively a
Grassmannian sample.

(ii) CG gets worse, not better.
CG’s momentum
amplifies the cell-wall discontinuities the FD gradient picks

(iii) TR matches SD essentially exactly but takes

Pymanopt’s TrustRegions logs

"Detected many consecutive TR- (radius decreases)"
at
the cell walls: it proposes a Newton step using the quadratic
model, the actual loss jumps because of a cell crossing the
model didn’t predict, TR rejects the step and shrinks the
trust radius, eventually shrinking it small enough to stay in
the originating cell — at which point it has effectively
reduced to SD. Quadratic information does not bridge cells.

So none of the local methods (first- or second-order,
Stiefel or Grassmann) approach the random-restart baseline of

1.85
. The combinatorial-cell pathology dominates the local
geometry; bridging cells requires a fundamentally different
mechanism (smoothed surrogate, basin-hopping, or chamber
enumeration).

The diagnosis is the one we predicted: the loss

(G,K)\;\longmapsto\;\min_{\mu,\nu\geq 0}\|R_{W}(G)\,\mu\,\nu\,R_{V}(K)^{\!\top}-\mathop{\operator@font diag}\nolimits(S_{r},0)\|_{F}

is piecewise constant on cells of

(G,K)
-space (the rays
R_{W},R_{V}
returned by
lcdd

are invariant within each combinatorial cell and jump
discretely at cell boundaries). Inside a cell the FD gradient
is zero; at a cell wall it is
O(1/\varepsilon_{\text{FD}})
.
Local Riemannian descent cannot exploit either regime.

Closing the octagon blindly therefore requires either (i) a
smoother surrogate loss that interpolates across cell walls
(unclear what this should be), (ii) a basin-hopping or
simulated-annealing restart scheme that explicitly jumps
between cells, or (iii) a combinatorial enumeration over the
finite set of
(R_{W},R_{V})
pairs realised at the cell
representatives. We leave all three as open problems.

Caveat: “neither” is the blind two-sided gauge
search problem.

The two-sided symmetric parametrisation

W=U_{r_{+}}(G)\,Q
,
H=P\,V_{r_{+}}(K)^{\!\top}
with

imposes no rank constraint on

by the formulation at the right

neither toolkit phase catches is the

for

\St(m{-}r,r_{+}{-}r)\times\St(n{-}r,r_{+}{-}r)

both Haar-uniform sampling and Riemannian gradient descent on

(G,K)

(§

).

Dense random gap matrices live in regime A; block-diagonal
families like
\mathop{\operator@font diag}\nolimits(C,J_{k})
admit NRFs in both A and B and

We leave this as future work.

10
Limitations

Combinatorial enumeration.

The rank-deficient
W

branch enumerates
\binom{n}{r_{+}}
subsets in the worst case.
For
n=10,r_{+}=7
this is
120
and runs in milliseconds;
for
n=100,r_{+}=30
it is
\sim 3\cdot 10^{25}
and is
infeasible. The right scaling strategy is to enumerate only the
extreme rays of the data cone (separable / vertex-NMF

[
10
,
9
]
) rather than
all column subsets. We do not pursue that here.

Gauge optimisation.

Continuous Riemannian
optimisation of the alt-LP residual over the Grassmannian is
the conjectured right tool for matrices where (i) only

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits(W)=r_{+}
NRFs exist and (ii) the feasible gauge is a
positive-dimensional but non-generic subvariety. Our actual
experiments with finite-difference gradient descent and
derivative-free
FindMinimum

(Section
6.2
) both stall above zero — the loss
landscape is piecewise constant within cells of
G
-space and
local methods cannot cross cell walls. The continuous direction
remains open and likely requires a basin-hopping search or a
smoother surrogate loss; we have not characterised when (i)
holds either.

Verifiable small polytope examples with proven gap.

Polytope slack matrices are the canonical family with

\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits_{+}>\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits
. For small instances, however, polytopes
with rigorously established
\operatorname{xc}
strictly above
the trivial lower bound
\dim+1
are scarce. The triangular
prism gives a verifiable rank-
4
,
r_{+}=5
example:

\operatorname{xc}\leq 5
from its five facets, and

\operatorname{xc}\geq 5
since the prism is not a
3
-simplex.
For gap
\geq 2
the Fiorini–Rothvoss–Tiwary result

[
8
]
establishes

\operatorname{xc}(\text{regular }n\text{-gon})=\Theta(\log n)
asymptotically, but exact values for specific
small
n
are not always tight against the trivial

\#\text{facets}
upper bound (the explicit cascade
construction gives
2\lceil\log_{2}n\rceil
, which for

n\in\{4,5,6,7,8\}
matches the facet count). Our
internally certified circulant
C

(Section
5.2
, with
\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits_{+}(C)=6
by
rectangle-covering) is the smallest fully-proven gap-
2
instance
we know of at rank
4
.

Rank-deficient
W
beyond column subsets.

The
octagon analysis (Section
9
) showed an exact
size-
6
NRF with
W^{*}
of rank
5<r_{+}
and
W^{*}

not
a column subset of the slack matrix. Our

rankDefPhase
only enumerates column subsets, so it
misses this regime entirely. The proper extension is a
cone-membership search where each
W
-column ranges over
nonnegative combinations of
M
’s columns; the resulting inner
problem is a bilinear feasibility analogous to the alt-LP but
on the data cone rather than the SVD-extended cone. We have
not implemented this extension.

12
Conclusion

The cone-ray exact-NMF pipeline of
[
1
]

extends naturally to the gap regime
r_{+}>r
with the

two-sided
symmetric parametrisation
W=U_{r_{+}}(G)\,Q
,

H=P\,V_{r_{+}}(K)^{\!\top}
, square consistency

QP=\mathop{\operator@font diag}\nolimits(S_{r},0)
, and DDM on
U_{r_{+}}(G)
(and on

V_{r_{+}}(K)
in the alt-LP fallback). The extension works
unconditionally on dense random gap matrices (
100/100
at

r_{+}=5
and
r_{+}=6
) but exhibits a structural failure
mode — the SVD’s choice of orthonormal completion in

\mathrm{col}(M)^{\perp}
is arbitrary, and for matrices whose only
rank-
r_{+}
NRFs have a fixed measure-zero
\Span W
the blind
choice misses the feasible gauge with probability
1
. Uniform
Stiefel sampling does not fix this. A small additional phase
that searches
r_{+}
-subsets of
M
’s columns directly bypasses
the gauge problem and restores feasibility for the structured
family
\mathop{\operator@font diag}\nolimits(C,J_{k})
in milliseconds. The combined toolkit is
a strict addition: dense matrices stay at
100\%
and structured
matrices — the most interesting NMF instances in practice —
become reachable.

The natural follow-ups are a Riemannian gauge optimiser for the
intermediate regime, a separable / vertex-pruned column
enumeration for large
n
, and a sharper characterisation of
which matrix families admit only
\mathop{\operator@font rank}\nolimits(W)=r_{+}
NRFs
(forcing the gauge issue) vs. which admit rank-deficient
W

NRFs (where the cheap branch wins).
