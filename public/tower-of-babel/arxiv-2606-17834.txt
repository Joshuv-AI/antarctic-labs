Total curvature and length estimates for timelike curves in Lorentzian length spaces
Darius Erös, Felix Rott, Zhe-Feng Xu

Abstract
We introduce and study a synthetic notion of timelike total curvature for curves in Lorentzian length spaces with upper curvature bounds. In particular, we prove that our notion agrees with its smooth counterpart, and we show that timelike curves of finite total curvature are rectifiable. As the main application, we provide a sharp lower bound for the length of timelike curves solely in terms of the time separation between their endpoints and their total curvature. Keywords: Lorentzian length spaces, timelike upper curvature bounds, total curvature, length estimates MSC2020: 53C23, 53C50, 53B30

Introduction

Lorentzian geometry provides the mathematical framework for the study of causality, spacetime structure, and variational problems in general relativity.
In the smooth setting, this heavily relies on the differentiability of the metric, and involves second-order methods for the study of geodesics and curvature.
Many natural geometric and physical situations, however, lie beyond the smooth category, which motivated the development of synthetic frameworks capable of capturing causality and curvature in low-regularity settings.
The theory of Lorentzian length spaces
[
20
]
provides such a framework, extending the philosophy of metric geometry to the Lorentzian world and allowing one to study causality theory and curvature bounds without assuming a smooth manifold structure.
In this work, we propose a synthetic notion of timelike total curvature and use it to show that timelike curves of prescribed total curvature between two fixed points cannot be arbitrarily short in
\ell
-length.
In fact, we derive a sharp length estimate whose extremal curves are shown to be
\ell
-length-preserving images of geodesic bi-segments whose interior angle coincides with the prescribed total curvature.

In positive signature, the total curvature captures the turning behaviour of a curve and records the total deviation from being geodesic.
This concept has been studied extensively, both in smooth and non-smooth settings
[
22
,
18
,
1
,
5
,
14
]
.
Most immediately relevant for us are the results established in
[
21
,
13
]
, which give upper bounds on the lengths of curves in CAT(
k
) spaces based merely on bounds on total curvature and chord length.
The notion of total curvature in CAT(
k
) spaces was originally introduced in
[
2
]
, see also
[
12
]
, and is based on the idea of summing interior angles of approximating inscribed polygons to yield a natural notion of curvature.

The Lorentzian analogue is considerably subtler, as geometric arguments must additionally take into account the
causal structure
of the space.
Furthermore, the time separation function
\ell
, which determines the geometry of the space, satisfies a
reverse
triangle inequality (along causal chains), and geodesics
maximise
their length as opposed to minimising it.
Therefore, our result will give a
lower
bound on lengths of timelike curves.
Similarly, we will show that bounded total curvature prohibits (local) collapse to
null
length, i.e.,
\ell
-rectifiability.

The development of hyperbolic angles
[
10
]
allows us to give a definition of total curvature for timelike curves in purely synthetic terms, akin to the one used in the study of CAT(
k
) spaces.
A crucial ingredient in the proof of the length estimate in
[
21
]
is the Majorisation theorem of Reshetnyak
[
23
]
, which has recently been adapted to the Lorentzian setting
[
9
]
.
For spacetimes, the notion of total curvature has been considered, e.g., in
[
16
,
4
,
11
]
, but, to the best of our knowledge, it has not previously been developed in the non-smooth setting.

Let us state our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Lower bound on the length of timelike curves)
.

Let
X
be a strongly causal and regular Lorentzian pre-length space and let
K\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\gamma
be a timelike curve in a
(\leq K)
-comparison neighbourhood.
Let
x
and
y
be the endpoints of
\gamma
.
Let
r=\ell(x,y)<D_{K}
be the time separation of the endpoints and let
\kappa=\TC(\gamma)\in[0,+\infty)
be the total curvature of
\gamma
.
Then

L(\gamma)\geq\mathcal{L}(K,r,\kappa),

(1.1)

where

\mathcal{L}(K,r,\kappa):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{K}}\arcsin\!\left(\dfrac{\sin(\sqrt{K}r/2)}{\cosh(\kappa/2)}\right),&\mathrm{if}\,\,K>0,\\[5.16663pt]
\dfrac{r}{\cosh(\kappa/2)},&\mathrm{if}\,\,K=0,\\[5.16663pt]
\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{-K}}\operatorname{arsinh}\!\left(\dfrac{\sinh(\sqrt{-K}r/2)}{\cosh(\kappa/2)}\right),&\mathrm{if}\,\,K<0.\end{cases}

If
\gamma
is a timelike poly-segment and attains equality in (
1.1
), then
\gamma
is a timelike geodesic in the case
\kappa=0
, and is the
\ell
-length-preserving image of an isosceles timelike bi-segment in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
with total curvature
\kappa
if
\kappa>0
.

Preliminaries

We first recall a few basic notions from non-smooth Lorentzian geometry. For further details, we refer to
[
20
,
10
,
7
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Lorentzian pre-length space)
.

A
Lorentzian pre-length space
is a quintuple
(X,\mathsf{d},\ll,\leq,\ell)
such that
(X,\mathsf{d})
is a metric space,
\leq
is a reflexive and transitive relation,
\ll
is a transitive relation contained in
\leq
, and the time separation function
\ell\colon X\times X\to[0,+\infty]
satisfies
\ell(x,z)\geq\ell(x,y)+\ell(y,z)
whenever
x\leq y\leq z
, and
\ell(x,y)>0
if and only if
x\ll y
.

Here, the only interplay between the auxiliary metric
\mathsf{d}
and the time separation
\ell
is topological, and our constructions do not rely on this exact axiomatisation. Other proposed axiomatisations of non-smooth Lorentzian spaces would work equally well.

Next, we recall the definition of the length and the notion of rectifiability for a future-directed causal curve, i.e., for a curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
such that
\gamma(s)\leq\gamma(t)
whenever
s<t
. Timelike and past-directed curves are defined analogously.

Definition 2.2

(
\ell
-length, cf.
[
20
, Definition 2.24]
)
.

Let
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
be a future-directed causal curve. Then we define its
\ell
-length
(or just
length
) by

L(\gamma):=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\ell\bigl(\gamma(t_{i}),\gamma(t_{i+1})\bigr)\,:\,N\in\mathbb{N},\;a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=b\right\}.

(2.1)

The definition of
\ell
-length, along with many subsequent ones, evidently applies equally well to past-directed curves.
Unless stated otherwise, we shall therefore take all causal curves to be future-directed.

Definition 2.3

(Rectifiability, cf.
[
20
, Definition 2.29]
)
.

A causal curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
is called
rectifiable
if, for all
a\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq b
, it holds that

L\bigl(\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}\bigr)>0.

This notion of rectifiability should be understood as dual to the one used in metric geometry, where refining a partition can only increase length, and rectifiability thereby provides a control on the oscillations of the curve. In the Lorentzian setting, in which the time separation satisfies the reverse triangle inequality, and refining can only decrease the length, the degenerate behaviour that is excluded by this notion of rectifiability is (local) collapse to null length.

Since some of our results are formulated for (smooth) spacetimes and the word
geodesic
is slightly ambiguous, we try to emphasise the distinction between geodesics (in the smooth sense) and
maximising geodesics
(
\ell
-maximisers).
For a maximising geodesic from
x
to
y
, we also use the notation
[x,y]
, and we may also refer to such a curve as a (geodesic) segment.

The following two properties ensure that the various notions of curvature bounds that we introduce below are, in fact, equivalent.

Definition 2.4

(Causal properties)
.

Let
X
be a Lorentzian pre-length space.

1.

X
is called
strongly causal
if the set of all timelike diamonds forms a subbasis for the topology.

2.

X
is called
regular
if maximising geodesics have a causal character.
That is, if
\ell(x,y)>0
, then any geodesic between
x
and
y
is timelike (and does not contain a null segment).

For the sake of completeness, we restate the definition of triangle comparison here, and refer the reader to
[

9
]
for further equivalent notions.
By
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
we denote the Lorentzian model space of constant curvature

.
The constant

appearing below is given by
\pi/\sqrt{K}
if
K>0
, and by
+\infty
if
K\leq 0
.
We say a triangle
\Delta(x,y,z)

\ell(x,z)<D_{K}

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, we assume all triangles satisfy size bounds.

Definition 2.5

(Triangle comparison)

Let

be a Lorentzian pre-length space. An open subset

-comparison neighbourhood in the sense of triangle comparison if:

(i)

\ell
is continuous on
(U\times U)\cap\ell^{-1}([0,D_{K}))

(ii)

For all
x\ll y
in

\ell(x,y)<D_{K}

(iii)

Let
\Delta(x,y,z)

p,q
two points on the sides of
\Delta(x,y,z)
. Let
\Delta(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z})
be a comparison triangle in

for
\Delta(x,y,z)
and
\bar{p},\bar{q}
comparison points for

\ell(p,q)\geq\ell(\bar{p},\bar{q})\,.

X

(\leq K)
-comparison neighbourhoods.

X

Definition 2.6

(Angles)

Let

Definition 2.7

(Angle comparison and four-point condition)
.

Assume a strongly causal and regular Lorentzian pre-length space

For any

In particular, if

Lemma 2.10

(Gauss–Bonnet Formula)
.

Let

3
Total curvature of timelike curves

In this section, we first recall the smooth definition of the total curvature of (timelike) curves, then propose a corresponding non-smooth analogue, and show their equivalence in strongly causal spacetimes.
We also give an alternative formulation of total curvature for non-positively curved spaces.

Let
(M,g)
be a spacetime with signature
(+,-,\dots,-)
and let
\gamma\colon[a,b]\rightarrow M
be a timelike
C^{2}
curve parametrised by proper time. Then
T(s):=\dot{\gamma}(s)
satisfies

g\big(T(s),T(s)\big)\equiv 1.

Differentiating the above equation along
\gamma
, we obtain
g(\nabla_{T}T(s),T(s))=0
, which implies that
\nabla_{T}T(s)
is spacelike, i.e.,
g(\nabla_{T}T(s),\nabla_{T}T(s))\leq 0
.
Denote by

\kappa(s):=\sqrt{-g\big(\nabla_{T}T(s),\nabla_{T}T(s)\big)}\in[0,+\infty)

the curvature of
\gamma
at the point
\gamma(s)
. The
(smooth) total curvature
of
\gamma
is then computed by

\int_{a}^{b}\kappa(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(3.1)

Definition 3.1

(Timelike poly-segments)
.

A
timelike poly-segment
in a Lorentzian pre-length space
X
is a timelike curve
\sigma\colon[a,b]\to X
together with a partition
a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=b
such that for each
i=1,\dots,n
, the respective restriction
\sigma|_{[t_{i-1},t_{i}]}
is a (non-constant) maximising geodesic from
p_{i-1}
to
p_{i}
, where we have set
p_{i}:=\sigma(t_{i})
.

If
n=2
, then
\sigma
is called a
timelike bi-segment
.

Since we never deal with poly-segments that contain null segments, we will occasionally drop the adjective ‘timelike’ to increase readability.

Definition 3.2

(Total rotation and curvature)
.

Let
X
be a Lorentzian pre-length space and let
\sigma
be a timelike poly-segment as in Definition
3.1
.
The
total rotation

\kappa^{*}(\sigma)\in[0,+\infty)
of
\sigma
is defined as the sum of the angles at the interior vertices:

\kappa^{*}(\sigma):=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\measuredangle_{p_{i}}(p_{i-1},p_{i+1}).

Let
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
be a timelike curve.
A timelike poly-segment
\sigma
is said to be
inscribed
in
\gamma
if there exists a partition
a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=b
and a parametrisation of
\sigma
on
[a,b]
such that
\sigma(t_{i})=\gamma(t_{i})
for
0\leq i\leq n
.
Denote by

|\sigma|:=\max_{0\leq i\leq n-1}(t_{i+1}-t_{i})

the
mesh-size
of
\sigma
.
We define the
(synthetic) total curvature
of
\gamma
by

\mathrm{TC}(\gamma):=\limsup_{|\sigma|\to 0}\kappa^{*}(\sigma)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(\gamma)}\kappa^{*}(\sigma),

(3.2)

where
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(\gamma)
denotes the collection of timelike poly-segments
\sigma
inscribed in
\gamma
with
|\sigma|<\varepsilon
.

Lemma 3.3

(Derivative of the hyperbolic angle)
.

Let

Proof.

Fix

Note that for

Proposition 3.4

(Notions of total curvature agree)
.

Let

Then

\mathrm{TC}(\gamma)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{def}}}{{=}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(\gamma)}\kappa^{*}(\sigma)=\int_{a}^{b}\kappa(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Proof.

Let
\sigma

\gamma

Corollary 3.6

(Total curvature as supremum)
.

(Total curvature and rotation of poly-segments)

4
Length estimates for timelike curves

The goal for this section is to prove our main theorem and to show that timelike curves of finite total curvature are rectifiable, a result of independent interest.
To do so, we start out by providing explicit formulae for the minimal curvature of bi-segments in the respective model spaces and show that this minimum is attained precisely by (geodesics and) isosceles bi-segments.

Lemma 4.1

(Length of bi-segments and rigidity)
.

Let
p\ll q\ll w
be three timelike related points in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
.
Let
\kappa=\measuredangle_{q}(p,w)
be the total curvature of the corresponding bi-segment, and let
r=\ell(p,w)<D_{K}
.
Assume that
\ell(p,q)+\ell(q,w)=l_{0}
is fixed.
Then

\kappa\geq\bm{\kappa}(K,r,l_{0}):=\begin{cases}2\arcosh\left(\dfrac{\sin(\sqrt{K}r/2)}{\sin(\sqrt{K}l_{0}/2)}\right),&\mathrm{if}\,\,K>0,\\[5.16663pt]
2\arcosh\left(\dfrac{r}{l_{0}}\right),&\mathrm{if}\,\,K=0,\\[5.16663pt]
2\arcosh\left(\dfrac{\sinh(\sqrt{-K}r/2)}{\sinh(\sqrt{-K}l_{0}/2)}\right),&\mathrm{if}\,\,K<0.\end{cases}

Equality holds if and only if
\ell(p,q)=\ell(q,w)=l_{0}/2
when
\kappa>0
, and if and only if
p,q,w
lie on a geodesic in the case
\kappa=0
. Moreover, if
\kappa_{0}\in[0,+\infty)
denotes the total curvature of the corresponding isosceles bi-segment, then its length is given by

\mathcal{L}(K,r,\kappa_{0}):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{K}}\arcsin\left(\dfrac{\sin(\sqrt{K}r/2)}{\cosh(\kappa_{0}/2)}\right),&\mathrm{if}\,\,K>0,\\[5.16663pt]
\dfrac{r}{\cosh(\kappa_{0}/2)},&\mathrm{if}\,\,K=0,\\[5.16663pt]
\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{-K}}\operatorname{arsinh}\left(\dfrac{\sinh(\sqrt{-K}r/2)}{\cosh(\kappa_{0}/2)}\right),&\mathrm{if}\,\,K<0.\end{cases}

(4.1)

Proof.

We treat only the cases
K=0
and
K<0
, since the case
K>0
is analogous to the case
K<0
. We set

u:=\ell(p,q)=m+x,\qquad v:=\ell(q,w)=m-x,\qquad m:=\frac{l_{0}}{2},\qquad-m<x<m.

Case 1:
K=0
.

The Law of Cosines gives

\cosh\kappa=\frac{r^{2}-2m^{2}-2x^{2}}{2(m^{2}-x^{2})},

which implies

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\cosh\kappa=\frac{x(r^{2}-4m^{2})}{(m^{2}-x^{2})^{2}}.

This vanishes either if
r=l_{0}=2m
, in which case
p,q
and
w
lie on a geodesic and
\kappa=0
, or if
r>l_{0}
and
x=0
, in which case

\bm{\kappa}(K,r,l_{0})=\arcosh\!\left(\frac{2r^{2}-l_{0}^{2}}{l_{0}^{2}}\right)=2\arcosh\!\left(\frac{r}{l_{0}}\right).

Case 2:
K<0
.

Set
\lambda:=\sqrt{-K}
.
In the non-trivial case, in which the three points do not lie on a geodesic, we have
r>l_{0}=2m
.
By the Law of Cosines,

\cosh\kappa=\frac{\cosh(\lambda r)-\cosh(\lambda u)\cosh(\lambda v)}{\sinh(\lambda u)\sinh(\lambda v)}.

Using
u+v=2m
and
u-v=2x
, this becomes

\cosh\kappa=\frac{\cosh(\lambda r)-\cosh^{2}(\lambda m)-\sinh^{2}(\lambda x)}{\sinh^{2}(\lambda m)-\sinh^{2}(\lambda x)}.

Now set

y(x):=\sinh^{2}(\lambda x).

Then

\cosh\kappa=\frac{\cosh(\lambda r)-\cosh^{2}(\lambda m)-y}{\sinh^{2}(\lambda m)-y}.

Differentiating with respect to
y
gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\cosh\kappa=\frac{\cosh(\lambda r)-\cosh(2\lambda m)}{(\sinh^{2}(\lambda m)-y)^{2}}>0,

because
r>2m
. Hence
\cosh\kappa
, and therefore
\kappa
, is minimised exactly when
y(x)
is minimised. Since

y(x)=\sinh^{2}(\lambda x)

has a unique minimum at
x=0
, the minimum occurs exactly when

u=v=m=\frac{l_{0}}{2}.

Substituting
x=0
gives

\displaystyle\bm{\kappa}(K,r,l_{0})=\arcosh{\left(\frac{\cosh(\sqrt{-K}r)-\cosh^{2}(\sqrt{-K}l_{0}/2)}{\sinh^{2}(\sqrt{-K}l_{0}/2)}\right)}=2\arcosh{\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{-K}r/2)}{\sinh(\sqrt{-K}l_{0}/2)}\right)}.

The second assertion follows by a direct computation via the Law of Cosines.

Lemma 4.4

(Length as limit)

It therefore remains to show that for every

such that for all partitions

. Choose

such that

.
By the definition of

, there exists a partition

.
Refining
\sigma

Proposition 4.5

(Rectifiability)
.

Let
X
be a strongly causal and regular Lorentzian pre-length space and let
K\in\mathbb{R}
.

\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X

Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that
\gamma

Then we can find

\eta
.

and

By Lemma
4.4
, there exists a sequence of partitions whose mesh-sizes tend to zero and such that the corresponding

\eta

This decreases the number of vertices by one while not increasing the total curvature. Denoting
the resulting curve by
\widetilde{\gamma}_{m-1}
, and relabelling its vertices as

\widetilde{p}_{0},\ldots,\widetilde{p}_{m-1}
, this gives

\kappa^{*}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{m})\geq\kappa^{*}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{m-1})
.

Just as in Proposition
4.5
, we can keep applying Lemma
4.2
until we have reduced

\widetilde{\gamma}_{m}
to the poly-segment
\widetilde{\gamma}_{3}
consisting of three segments, whose
vertices we denote by
\widetilde{p}_{0},\ldots,\widetilde{p}_{3}
, where we distinguish the following two cases:
if
K>0
and both

\ell(\widetilde{p}_{0},\widetilde{p}_{1})+\ell(\widetilde{p}_{1},\widetilde{p}_{2})\geq\frac{D_{K}}{2}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\ell(\widetilde{p}_{1},\widetilde{p}_{2})+\ell(\widetilde{p}_{2},\widetilde{p}_{3})\geq\frac{D_{K}}{2},

then we apply Lemma
4.3
directly to obtain an isosceles bi-segment
\tilde{\alpha}
of the same length, with the same time separation between its endpoints, and with no greater total curvature.
Otherwise, we may apply Lemma
4.2
one more time to end up with a bi-segment
\tilde{\gamma}_{2}
of equal length, with the same time separation between its endpoints, and with no greater total curvature.
By Lemma
4.1
, we know
that among all such bi-segments, the isosceles one, still denoted by
\widetilde{\alpha}
, has the smallest total
curvature. That is, in summary, we have

\kappa=\kappa^{*}(\gamma)\geq\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\gamma})=\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\gamma}_{m})\geq\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\gamma}_{m-1})\geq\ldots\geq\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\gamma}_{3})\big[\geq\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\gamma}_{2})\big]\geq\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\alpha}).

(4.8)

Now, the length of
\tilde{\alpha}
, which is equal to that of
\gamma
, as the above procedure preserves lengths, is explicitly given by
L(\tilde{\alpha})=\mathcal{L}(K,r,\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\alpha}))
, cf. (
4.1
).
Since
\mathcal{L}
is decreasing with respect to the total curvature and
\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\alpha})\leq\kappa
, it follows that

L(\gamma)=L(\tilde{\alpha})=\mathcal{L}(K,r,\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\alpha}))\geq\mathcal{L}(K,r,\kappa).

(4.9)

Let now
\gamma
be any timelike curve.
Consider a sequence of inscribed poly-segments
\sigma_{n}
such that

|\sigma_{n}|\to 0\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\kappa^{*}(\sigma_{n})\to\kappa.

By definition, we have
L(\sigma_{n})\geq L(\gamma)
, and each
\sigma_{n}
has endpoints
x
and
y
.
In particular, we also have that
L(\sigma_{n})\leq\ell(x,y)<D_{K}
.
The above arguments give
L(\sigma_{n})\geq\mathcal{L}(K,r,\kappa^{*}(\sigma_{n}))
.
By Lemma
4.4
, we have
L(\sigma_{n})\to L(\gamma)
, and so

L(\gamma)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{L(\sigma_{n})}\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{L}(K,r,\kappa^{*}(\sigma_{n}))=\mathcal{L}(K,r,\kappa),

as
\mathcal{L}
is clearly continuous in the total curvature.

Finally, suppose
\gamma
is a poly-segment and achieves equality in the length bound (
4.7
).
If
\kappa=0
, then the poly-segment
\gamma
is a geodesic and the claim follows immediately, so suppose that
\kappa>0
.
Equality in (
4.7
) implies that we have equality in (
4.9
) and so
\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\alpha})=\kappa
, as
\mathcal{L}
is strictly decreasing in the total curvature.
Therefore, equality holds throughout (
4.8
), implying that
\tilde{\gamma}
must have been a bi-segment to begin with, as otherwise at least one of the steps in the deformation of Lemma
4.2
or Lemma
4.3
would have strictly decreased the curvature (ignoring vertices in the interior of a segment).
Moreover, since
\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\gamma})=\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\alpha})
, equality holds in the application of Lemma
4.1
. By the equality statement in Lemma
4.1
, the bi-segment
\tilde{\gamma}
must therefore be isosceles, and so,
\gamma
is indeed the
\ell
-length-preserving image of an isosceles timelike bi-segment as the boundary map in Theorem
2.8
preserves
\ell
-length.
∎

Remark 4.7

(Asymptotic rigidity)
.

If
\gamma
is an arbitrary timelike curve that achieves equality in the length estimate (
4.7
), one can follow a similar argument as above along a sequence of approximating poly-segments
\sigma_{n}
to conclude that
\gamma=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{n}(\tilde{\sigma}_{n})
where
\tilde{\sigma}_{n}
is a sequence of bi-segments in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
converging to an isosceles bi-segment
\tilde{\alpha}
and the maps
f_{n}
are
\ell
-length preserving.
The full rigidity statement, i.e., the conclusion that
\gamma
is the (
\ell
-length preserving) image of this isosceles bi-segment
\tilde{\alpha}
, would require that the maps
f_{n}\colon\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\rightarrow\sigma_{n}
converge to a map
f\colon\tilde{\alpha}\rightarrow\gamma
, which is not guaranteed by Theorem
2.8
.

Remark 4.8

(On assumptions)
.

Note that, in general, the theorem fails to hold if the curve leaves the
(\leq K)
-comparison neighbourhood.
Indeed, consider the Lorentzian cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
with the induced intrinsic structure from 3-dimensional Minkowski space.
Its curvature is locally bounded from above by zero, but not globally (this is because the cylinder is not future one-connected, cf.
[
15
]
).
Following any null geodesic
\gamma
that winds around the cylinder once, we have connected two timelike related points
x
and
y
(its endpoints) with a curve of zero length.
Slightly deforming this curve, we can produce a family of timelike geodesics
\gamma_{n}
(with total curvature
\kappa=0
) such that
\ell(\gamma_{n}(0),\gamma_{n}(1))\geq\ell(x,y)
and
L(\gamma_{n})\rightarrow L(\gamma)=0
while
\mathcal{L}(K,\ell(x,y),\kappa)=\ell(x,y)>0
.

If the curvature bound on
X
is global, then the only restrictions for the existence of a lower bound on the length are that the time separation of the endpoints be less than
D_{K}
and that the total curvature be finite.
