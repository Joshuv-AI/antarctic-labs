STOCKPILING

21. In the above calculation, we have made no allowance for b uild ing up
strategic stocks. On the evidence set out in paragraph 4 the Bloc woiid have
to have reserves on hand before the onset of any major hostilities in order
to meet the impact of expanded wartime rates of use. These reserve stocks,
whether primary metal or in fabricated farms - e.g. ammunition, weapons,
military vehicles, etc. — are Just as much a military requirement as the
equipment which is now being used. But as indicated above we believe that
copper shortages have made stockpiling impracticable, and indeed that the
Bloo has had to withdraw copper from reserve, particularly in the past two
years.

22. The magnitude of this set- back — measured in tons of copper net placed
in reserve - can be assessed only by assuming that the Bloc has a stockpile
objective which dt would hope to achieve within a reasonable period- If ve
assume that the objective is to provide far two years’ war time consumption,
the amount might be 1,250,000 tons. If the Bloc wished to build up to this
figure from the present level in five years, the annual installment would
have to be about 135,000 tons.
