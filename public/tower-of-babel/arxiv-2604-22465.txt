Segre invariants of principal bundles over a curve
George H. Hitching, Alfonso Zamora

Abstract
. For a vector bundle V V over a curve X X , the Segre invariant s n ​ ( V ) s_{n}(V) encodes the maximal degree attained by rank n n subbundles of V V . The functions s n s_{n} define stratifications on moduli of V V which are well studied. Let G G be a connected reductive algebraic group, and E → X E\to X a principal G G -bundle. For each parabolic subgroup P ⊂ G P\subset G there is a Segre number s P ​ ( E ) s_{P}(E) , generalising s n ​ ( V ) s_{n}(V) . We show that s P s_{P} is semicontinuous in families of G G -bundles, and thus defines stratifications on moduli spaces of G G -bundles over X X . We study the invariance properties of s P s_{P} , relating the behaviour of s P s_{P} and s ϕ ⁡ ( P ) s_{\phi(P)} for a surjective homomorphism ϕ : G → H \phi\colon G\to H and allowing us to compare the Segre stratifications for G G and H H . Finally, we analyse the stratification for the Borel subgroup B B of GL 3 \mathrm{GL}_{3} , identifying patterns in the geometry and proving, in particular, a sharp Hirschowitz-type bound on s B ​ ( E ) s_{B}(E) for certain topological types.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a complex projective smooth curve of genus
g\geq 2
. A vector bundle
V
of rank
r
and degree
d
over
X
is said to be
semistable
if

\mu(W)=\frac{\deg W}{\mathrm{rk}\,W}\ \leq\ \mu(V)=\frac{d}{r}

for all proper nonzero subbundles
W\subset V
. It is
stable
if inequality is strict for all
W
. The relevance of stability for moduli questions and other purposes is very well known: a moduli space
U_{X}(r,d)
exists for stable vector bundles of rank
r
and degree
d
over
X
, which is an irreducible quasiprojective variety of dimension
r^{2}(g-1)+1
. More generally, each vector bundle over
X
admits a unique Harder–Narasimhan filtration
[
HN75
]
, and the topological invariants of this filtration (ranks and degrees) are shown to be semicontinuous
[
Sha77
]
and to define a stratification on any family of bundles
[
Nit11
]
.

A refinement of the notion of stability is the following. For each
1\leq n\leq r-1
, the
Segre invariant

s_{n}(V)
is defined by

(1.1)

s_{n}(V)\ :=\ \min\{nd-r\deg W:W\subset V\hbox{ a subbundle of rank }n\}.

Noting that
nd-r\deg W=rn(\mu(V)-\mu(W))
, we see that
V
is stable if and only if
s_{n}(V)>0
for each
n
, and strictly semistable if
s_{n}(V)\geq 0
for each
n
and there exists a
k
such that
s_{k}(V)=0
. Thus Segre invariants are naturally “degrees of stability”, measuring how far a vector bundle is from being destabilised by a rank
n
subbundle. The term “invariant” is applied to
s_{n}
because for any
V
, for
1\leq n\leq\mathrm{rk}\,V
we have
s_{n}(V\otimes L)=s_{n}(V)
for all line bundles
L
. In other words,
s_{n}
is determined by the projective bundle
{\mathbb{P}}V
.

We recall that stability and semistability are open conditions on families of bundles. Generalising this statement we get: if
s\equiv nd\;(\!\!\mod r)
then the locus

(1.2)

U_{X}(r,d;n,s)\ =\ \{V\in U_{X}(r,d):s_{n}(V)=s\}

is locally closed in
U_{X}(r,d)
, and
U_{X}(r,d;n,s^{\prime})
is contained in the closure of
U_{X}(r,d;n,s)
for
s^{\prime}<s
. Thus the
s_{n}
define stratifications on
U_{X}(r,d)
, which are well understood
[
RT99
,
BL98
]
.

Notice that there are important differences between Segre and Harder–Narasimhan stratifications. As mentioned above, the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of any bundle
V
is unique. In contrast, if
V
is semistable then a proper subbundle of maximal slope and maximal rank may not be unique (consider a direct sum of line bundles of the same degree). This is reflected in the fact that the Jordan–Hölder filtration of a semistable bundle is not in general unique; but only the associated graded bundle. If
V
is stable, then it may have many maximal subbundles of a given rank with mutually nonisomorphic graded bundles. (Indeed, the enumeration of maximal subbundles is a focus of
[
Hol04
]
and
[
LNe03
]
).

Furthermore, although for
r=2
the Segre and Harder–Narasimhan stratifications on any family of bundles coincide, for
r\geq 3
they diverge. There is a single Harder-Narasimhan stratification on any family whose terms have different ranks and degrees. In contrast, the family admits a distinct Segre stratification for
each
rank
n
with
1\leq n\leq r-1
, and the stratum to which a given bundle
V
belongs is determined solely by the maximal degree of subbundles of rank
n
in
V
.

The goal of the present work is to study a generalisation of the Segre invariant for an arbitrary connected reductive group
G
and an arbitrary parabolic subgroup
P
. We begin by recalling the definition of stability for principal
G
-bundles.

Definition 1.1
.

[
Rth75
, Definition 1.1]

Let
G
be a connected reductive linear algebraic group over
{\mathbb{C}}
. A principal
G
-bundle
E\to X

Q\subset G

By

Let

We will see in §

Let us show that

and

\deg\sigma^{*}T_{\pi}\ =\ n\cdot(\deg V-\deg F)-(r-n)\cdot\deg F\ =\ n\cdot\deg V-r\cdot\deg F.

It now follows from (

for vector bundles.

and the number (

The structure and results of the present article are as follows. In section
2
we collect preliminary results on reductive groups and principal
G
bundles. In section
3
, we give some alternative definitions of and some elementary properties of the function
s_{P}
, and prove:

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group and let
P\subset G
be a parabolic subgroup.

(i)

The function
s_{P}
is semicontinuous in families of
G
-bundles of fixed topological type
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)
. Thus it induces a stratification of the moduli space
M_{X}(G,\delta)
into locally closed subsets

M_{X}(G,\delta;P,s)^{\circ}\ :=\ \{E\in M_{X}(G,\delta):s_{P}(E)=s\}.

(ii)

The function
s_{P}
depends only on the conjugacy class of
P\subset G
.

The content of Theorem
A
(i) is proved in Theorem
3.3
and Corollary
3.4
. We then proceed in section
3.3
to study invariance properties of
s_{P}
generalising the invariance properties of the Segre invariant

for vector bundles. We determine the relation between
s_{P}
and
s_{\phi(P)}
where
\phi\colon G\to H
is a surjective homomorphism of connected algebraic groups (Proposition
3.8
). This will justify the term “invariant” and allow us to prove Theorem
A
(ii) in Corollary
3.9
.

This allows us in section
4
to compare the stratifications on certain components of the moduli spaces of principal bundles for two different groups related by a surjective homomorphism.

Theorem B
.

Let
\phi\colon G\to H
be a surjective homomorphism of connected reductive algebraic groups such that
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)_{0}\subseteq Z(G)
.

(i)

For every parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
, the Segre invariants
s_{P}(E)
and
s_{\phi(P)}(E(H))
coincide. In particular, a principal
G
-bundle
E\to X
is (semi)stable if and only if the associated principal
H
-bundle
E(H)
is (semi)stable.

(ii)

Given topological types
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)
and
\phi_{*}\delta\in\pi_{1}(H)
, we obtain a morphism between the moduli spaces
\phi_{\delta}\colon M_{X}(G,\delta)\ \to\ M_{X}(H,\phi_{*}\delta)
induced by extension of structure group which relates the Segre stratifications by

\phi_{\delta}^{-1}M_{X}(H,\phi_{*}\delta;\phi(P),s)^{\circ}=M_{X}(G,\delta;P,s)^{\circ}.

Moreover, whenever
\phi_{\delta}
is surjective, the number of non-empty strata
M_{X}(G,\delta;P,s)^{\circ}
equals the number of non-empty strata
M_{X}(H,\phi_{*}\delta;\phi(P),s)^{\circ}
.

Theorem
B
is proved in the statements of Theorem
4.4
. Examples are given in
\lx@sectionsign

4.2
: by using the adjoint map and the universal covering map we relate stratifications of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
and
\mathrm{SL}_{r}
to
\mathrm{PGL}_{r}

The examples in section

show that the Segre function remains invariant when taking the adjoint group of

and seem to indicate that the information on the Segre stratification is intrinsically contained the moduli space of adjoint bundles, as happens with
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
and

G

For each

Theorem C
.

Let

. Set
s=2(d_{3}-d_{1})
.

(i)

The locus

is nonempty and irreducible of dimension at most

. Moreover,

is contained in the Segre stratum

(ii)

We have

In particular,

.

(iii)

(Hirschowitz bound) For

For certain choices of

, we can show that the dimension bound (i) on

2.
Reductive groups and principal
G
-bundles

In this section, we review necessary facts on reductive algebraic groups and on principal
G
-bundles and their classification.

2.1.
Reductive groups

A reference for the following is
[
Spr09
]
.

Let
G
be a connected algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
. The
unipotent radical

{\mathrm{R_{u}}}G
is the maximal closed connected unipotent subgroup of
G
. If
{\mathrm{R_{u}}}G
is trivial, then
G
is said to be
reductive
. A
Borel
subgroup
B\subseteq G
is a maximal closed and connected solvable algebraic subgroup. Fixing a faithful representation of
G
on a vector space
V
, the quotient
G/B
can be identified with the space of (generalized) full flags on
V
and called the
full-flag variety
; it is a complete variety. A subgroup
P\subseteq G
is said to be
parabolic
if
G/P
is a complete variety or equivalently if
P
contains a Borel subgroup. A Borel subgroup
B
is a minimal parabolic subgroup with respect to inclusion.

A
maximal torus

T\subseteq G
is a connected abelian subgroup that is maximal with respect to the inclusion, and any two maximal tori are conjugate by the action of
G
. We can fix a maximal torus
T
and a Borel subgroup
B
such that
T\subseteq B\subseteq G
, and then each conjugacy class of parabolic subgroups contains a unique representative
P
satisfying
T\subseteq B\subseteq P\subseteq G
.

Let
X^{\ast}(T)=\{\chi:T\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\}
and
X_{\ast}(T)=\{\lambda:{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\rightarrow T\}
be the
lattices of characters
and
cocharacters
(or
1
-parameter subgroups) of
T
. These are dual free abelian groups via the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-pairing

(2.1)

\left\langle\chi,\lambda\right\rangle\ \mapsto\ \deg\left(t\mapsto\chi(\lambda(t))\right)

for
\chi\in X^{\ast}(T)
and
\lambda\in X_{\ast}(T)
.

Given a parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
, we have an exact sequence

(2.2)

1\ \to\ {\mathrm{R_{u}}}P\ \to\ P\ \to\ L\ \to\ 1

where
L
is the maximal reductive quotient of
P
. Then
P
contains
L
as a
Levi subgroup
, and we have the
Levi decomposition

P={\mathrm{R_{u}}}P\rtimes L
. There are no nontrivial algebraic homomorphisms
{\mathrm{R_{u}}}P\to{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, and therefore the characters of
P
and
L
are the same:

(2.3)

X^{*}(P)\ \cong\ X^{*}(L).

2.2.
Principal
G
-bundles

Let us now recall basic facts about principal
G
-bundles. References for the following include
[
Bal09
]
and
[
Gro58
]
.

Let
Y
be a variety over
{\mathbb{C}}
, and let
G
be a complex algebraic group. A
principal
G
-bundle
over
Y
is a variety
E
admitting a free right action of
G
with quotient
Y
, and such that
E\to Y
is locally trivial in the étale topology. In the same way as a vector bundle, a principal bundle
E
may be recovered up to isomorphism from a cocycle of transition functions
g_{\alpha\beta}\colon U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}\ \to\ G
over a suitable open cover
\{U_{\alpha}\}
of
Y
. The cocycle
\{g_{\alpha\beta}\}
determines an element of the nonabelian cohomology set
H^{1}(X,G)
.

2.2.1.
Associated bundles

Let
F
be a variety admitting a left action of
G
via an algebraic representation
\rho\colon G\to\mathrm{Aut}\,(F)
. Given a
G
-bundle
E\to Y
, one may form the
associated bundle

(2.4)

E(\rho,F)\ :=\ \frac{E\times F}{(e,f)\sim(e\cdot g,\rho(g^{-1})\cdot f)}

which is a fibre bundle over
Y
with fibre
F
. If no confusion should arise, we may write
E(\rho)
or
E(F)
for
E(\rho,F)
.

We list some important associated bundles of a principal
G
-bundle
E\to Y
, which will be used throughout.

•

(
Associated vector bundles
) If
\rho\colon G\to\mathrm{GL}_{r}
is a linear representation, then the associated bundle
E(\rho,{\mathbb{C}}^{r})
is a vector bundle of rank
r
. In particular, if
\chi\colon G\to{\mathbb{C}}^{*}=\mathrm{GL}_{1}
is a character, then
E(\chi,{\mathbb{C}})
is a line bundle.

•

(
Extension of structure group
) Given a group homomorphism
G\to H
, we have an action of
G
on
H
by left translations. Then the associated bundle
E(H)
is in fact a principal
H
-bundle via the right
H
-action

(e,h)\cdot\ell\ =\ (e,h\ell)

for
(e,h)
an equivalence class as in (
2.4
). In this case
E(H)
is said to be obtained from
E
by
extension of structure group
.

•

(
Homogeneous space bundles
) Let
Q\subset G
be a closed subgroup. Consider the action of
G
given by left translations of the left cosets
gQ
for
g\in G
. Then the associated bundle
E(G/Q)
is a fibre bundle with fibre
G/Q
, which we will often denote by
\pi\colon E/Q\rightarrow Y
. It is naturally identified with the globalised coset space
\{eQ:e\in E\}
.

2.2.2.
Reductions of structure group

Let
E\to Y
be a principal
G
-bundle. If
Q\subset G
is a closed subgroup, then
E\to E/Q
is a principal
Q
-bundle. Let
\pi\colon E/Q\to Y
be the projection. A section
\sigma\colon Y\to E/Q
of

Q

\sigma^{*}E
over
Y
, which will be denoted by
E_{Q,\sigma}
, or

when

\sigma
may be understood as prescribing a choice of coset in
G/Q
for each

.

Let us recall some key examples of reduction of structure group.

Example 2.1
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive group and
E\to X
a principal
G
-bundle.

(a)

Here we take
G=\mathrm{GL}_{r}
or
\mathrm{SL}_{r}

(b)

More generally, if

. Then a reduction

is equivalent to a flag of subbundles

for

.

(c)

If

, a parabolic

is the stabiliser of a (partial) flag of isotropic subspaces, and a reduction to

is equivalent to a flag of isotropic subbundles of the associated vector bundle

. A similar statement holds for

.

2.3.
Classification of
G
-bundles

Let us recall the correspondence between topological

Proposition 2.2
.

(a)

(b)

Let

We now discuss the notion of degree for principal bundles. This is a slight generalisation of

[

[

Definition 2.3
.

Let

defines an element

of

For

of

. To see this, observe that the determinant generates

, and evaluation at the determinant defines an isomorphism

which sends

to

.

Definition 2.4
.

[
Hol02
, p. 1]

Let

be a parabolic subgroup and and

a reduction of structure group. Then the

of

.

Remark 2.5
.

We include the group

in the notation

for clarity. Let
\phi\colon G\to H
be any homomorphism of algebraic groups. Then there is a natural map

\phi_{*}\colon\mathrm{Hom}(X^{*}(G),{\mathbb{Z}})\ \to\ \mathrm{Hom}(X^{*}(H),{\mathbb{Z}})

given by restriction to the image of
\phi^{*}\colon X^{*}(H)\to X^{*}(G)
. Unwinding definitions, we see that

.

We observe this because in what follows, we may wish to consider the degree
d_{G}(E)\in\mathrm{Hom}(X^{*}(G),{\mathbb{Z}})
or the degree

of a
P
-bundle obtained from

by reduction of structure group; and
\mathrm{Hom}(X^{*}(P),{\mathbb{Z}})
is typically a larger group than
\mathrm{Hom}(X^{*}(G),{\mathbb{Z}})
. This will be illustrated in the following example, which we also consider in order to compare the notions of topological type and degree.

Example 2.6
.

Take
G=\mathrm{GL}_{r}
. In Example
2.1
(a), we considered the parabolic subgroup
P
of matrices of the form

\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&\star&\cdots&\star\\
0&A_{2}&\cdots&\star\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&A_{t}\end{pmatrix}

where
r_{1}+\cdots+r_{t}=r
and
A_{i}\in\mathrm{GL}_{r_{i}}
. Then

X^{*}(P)\ =\ \left\{{\det}_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots{\det}_{t}^{k_{t}}:k_{1},\ldots,k_{t}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}

where
{\det}_{i}
is the character taking the above matrix to
\det A_{i}
, and

\mathrm{Im}\,\left(X^{*}(G)\ \to\ X^{*}(P)\right)\ =\ \left\{\left({\det}_{1}\cdots{\det}_{t}\right)^{k}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}.

Let now
E_{P}\to X
be a principal
P
-bundle and
E:=E_{P}(G)
the associated
G
-bundle. As discussed in Example
2.1
(a), the associated vector bundle

has a filtration
0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\cdots\subset V_{t-1}\subset V_{t}=V
. For
1\leq i\leq t
, write
W_{i}:=V_{i}/V_{i-1}
for the summands of the associated graded bundle. Let
\phi\colon P\hookrightarrow G
be the inclusion. Now

(2.5)

\left\langle\phi_{*}d_{P}(E_{P}),{\det}^{k}\right\rangle\ =\ \left\langle d_{P}(E_{P}),{\det}_{1}^{k}\cdots{\det}_{t}^{k}\right\rangle\ =\ k\cdot\deg W_{1}+\cdots+k\cdot\deg W_{t}\ =\\
k\cdot\left(\deg W_{1}+\cdots+\deg W_{t}\right)\ =\ k\cdot\deg V\ =\ \left\langle d_{G}(E),{\det}^{k}\right\rangle.

Let us relate the above discussion with the topological types of

and

. Recall the exact sequence
1\to{\mathrm{R_{u}}}P\to P\to L\to 1
as in (
2.2
). The unipotent group

is isomorphic as a variety to an affine space, and the Levi

is the product

. As the map

is a fibration with connected and simply connected fibres, Serre’s long exact sequence of homotopy groups shows that the induced map

is an isomorphism. Thus the topological type of a

-bundle is determined by the degrees of the vector bundles appearing along the diagonal. Moreover, the map
\pi_{1}(P)\to\pi_{1}(\mathrm{GL}_{r})
induced by the inclusion

corresponds to adding the degrees of the
\mathrm{GL}_{r_{i}}
-bundles occurring along the diagonal, in analogy with the computation (

).

In this case we have
\pi_{1}(P)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(X^{*}(P),{\mathbb{Z}})
and
\pi_{1}(G)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(X^{*}(G),{\mathbb{Z}})
. However, this need not hold for arbitrary

and

; for example, the groups

and

may have torsion, while

is torsion free for any group

since

is (see Remark

for an example). The precise relation between topological type and degree will be a subject of further investigation.

3.1.
Characterisations of

Here we give some equivalent definitions of the Segre function defined in the introduction

s_{P}(E)\ =\ \min\{\deg\sigma^{*}T_{\pi}:\sigma\colon X\to E/P\hbox{ a reduction of structure group to }P\};

essentially, we are just making explicit some statements in
[
HN01
, p. 322–323]
. Firstly, we show that the above is equivalent to the definition considered in
[
HN01
]
and formalised in
[

, p. 193]
, which we will use in the proof of semicontinuity of

(Theorem

). Abusing notation, given a map of varieties

, we write

both for the induced map

and that

, when these exist. The proof of the following lemma is left to the reader:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\pi\colon F\to Y
be a smooth morphism of smooth varieties. Set

T_{\pi}\ :=\ \mathrm{Ker}\left(T_{F}\to\pi^{*}T_{Y}\right)\ \cong\ \mathrm{Ker}\left(d\pi\colon T_{F}\to T_{Y}\right).

Suppose that
\sigma\colon Y\to F
is such that
\pi\circ\sigma=\mathrm{Id}_{Y}
. Then
\sigma^{*}T_{F}
splits canonically as
\sigma^{*}T_{\pi}\oplus T_{Y}
, and the normal bundle
N_{\sigma}:=N_{\sigma(Y)/F}
is isomorphic to
\sigma^{*}T_{\pi}
.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have

(3.1)

s_{P}(E)\ =\ \min\left\{\deg N_{\sigma}:\sigma\colon X\to E/P\hbox{ a reduction of structure group to }P\right\}.

A variant of the above will also be used in Theorem
3.3
. Firstly, we recall a definition. As the adjoint action of
P
on
\mathfrak{g}
preserves the subspace
\mathfrak{p}
, there is an exact sequence of
P
-modules

(3.2)

0\ \to\ \mathfrak{p}\ \to\ \mathfrak{g}\ \to\ \mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p}\ \to\ 0\;.

Definition 3.2
.

The map
\iota\colon P\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p})
obtained from the above sequence is called the
isotropy representation
of
P
.

Now let
E
and
\pi\colon E/P\to X
be as above. As before, write
E_{P,\sigma}\to X
for the principal
P
-bundle coming from a reduction
\sigma
. As described in
[
Rth75
, § 2]
, the sequence (

) induces an exact sequence of associated vector bundles

(3.3)

0\ \to\ E_{P,\sigma}(\mathfrak{p})\ \to\ E_{P,\sigma}(\mathfrak{g})\ \to\ E_{P,\sigma}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p})\ \to\ 0,

where
E_{P,\sigma}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p})
is identified with
\sigma^{*}T_{\pi}
. Thus

(3.4)

s_{P}(E)\ =\ \min\left\{\deg E_{P,\sigma}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p}):\sigma\colon X\to E/P\hbox{ a reduction of structure group}\right\}.

Observe that the vector bundle

coincides with the adjoint bundle

, since they have the same transition functions.

Lastly, we reformulate (

) in terms of characters, to maintain contact with
[

]
and for use in
\lx@sectionsign

5
. The determinant

of the isotropy representation is an element of

. Given a

with

Thus we have another interpretation of

given by

(3.5)

s_{P}(E)\ =\ \min\left\{\langle[\sigma],\det\iota\rangle:\sigma\colon X\to E/P\hbox{ a reduction of structure group}\right\},

where
\langle\;,\,\rangle
is the natural pairing

.

3.2.
Semicontinuity of the Segre invariant and stratification of the moduli of principal bundles

We prove here Theorem

Theorem 3.3

Let

Proof.

The associated bundle

[

, we consider the family of associated vector bundles

3.3.
Invariance properties of
s_{P}

Let
V\to X
be a vector bundle of rank
r
and let
L\to X
be any line bundle. It is easy to check that for
1\leq n\leq r-1
we have

s_{n}(V\otimes L)\ =\ s_{n}(V)

for all line bundles
L\to X
.
In particular,
s_{n}(V)
depends only on the projective bundle
{\mathbb{P}}V
; and this is an associated fibration of
E(\mathrm{PGL}_{r})
, the principal
\mathrm{PGL}_{r}
-bundle obtained by extension of structure group via the adjoint map
\mathrm{GL}_{r}\to\mathrm{PGL}_{r}=\mathrm{GL}_{r}/Z(\mathrm{GL}_{r})
. We will now generalise this invariance property of
s_{n}
, comparing the functions
s_{P}
and
s_{\phi(P)}
for a surjective homomorphism
\phi
. Firstly, we require a technical lemma. For an algebraic group
K
, we denote by
K_{0}
the connected component containing the identity element.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\phi\colon G\to H
be a surjective homomorphism of connected algebraic groups.

(a)

Let
Q
be a parabolic subgroup of
H
. Then there is an isomorphism of
H
-spaces
G/\phi^{-1}(Q)\xrightarrow{\sim}H/Q
. In particular,
\phi^{-1}(Q)
is parabolic in
G
.

(b)

Let
P\subset G
be a parabolic subgroup containing
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)_{0}
. Then there is an isomorphism of
H
-spaces
G/P\xrightarrow{\sim}H/\phi(P)
.

Proof.

(a) Write
Q^{\prime}:=\phi^{-1}(Q)
. Using surjectivity of
\phi
and normality of
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)
, one checks that
H
acts on
G/Q^{\prime}
by

(h,(g\cdot Q^{\prime}))\ \mapsto\ \tilde{h}g\cdot Q^{\prime}

for any lift
\tilde{h}
of
h
; and that there is a well defined map
G/Q^{\prime}\to H/Q
given by
g\cdot Q^{\prime}\mapsto\phi(g)\cdot Q
. Unwinding definitions, this can be checked to define the desired isomorphism of
H
-spaces.

(b) By
[
Spr09
, Corollary 6.2.8]
, the image
\phi(P)
is a parabolic subgroup of
H
. Therefore, part (a) shows that
P^{\prime}:=\phi^{-1}(\phi(P))
is a parabolic subgroup. Thus (b) will follow if we can show that
P^{\prime}=P
.

Since
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)_{0}\subseteq P
, we have
\dim P^{\prime}=\dim P
, so the coset space
P^{\prime}/P
is a finite set. Since
G
is connected and
P^{\prime}
is a parabolic subgroup,
P^{\prime}
is connected by
[
Spr09
, Corollary 6.4.10]
. Therefore,
P^{\prime}/P
is a single point and
P^{\prime}=P
, as desired.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The proof of (b) shows that if
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)_{0}\subseteq P
, then in fact all components of
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)
belong to
P
, since
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)\subseteq\phi^{-1}(J)
for any subgroup

. Thus in this case we also have
\phi^{-1}(\phi(P))=P
.

Example 3.7
.

Suppose that
G=\mathrm{GL}_{r}
and
H=\mathrm{PGL}_{r}
and
\phi\colon\mathrm{GL}_{r}\to\mathrm{PGL}_{r}
is the adjoint map. Let

be the maximal parabolic subgroup stabilising a subspace

. Then the isomorphism

in Lemma

{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
and that parameterising projective subspaces of dimension

in

.

We can now state the main invariance property of

.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\phi\colon G\to H
be a surjective homomorphism of connected algebraic groups, and let

be a parabolic subgroup containing
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)_{0}

(a)

Let

be a principal

.

(b)

Let

\{b\in B:s_{P}({\mathcal{E}}_{b})=s\}\ =\ \{b\in B:s_{\phi(P)}({\mathcal{E}}_{b}(H))=s\}.

Proof.

(a) By Lemma

, there is an isomorphism of fibrations over

E/P\ \cong\ E(G/P)\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ E(H)\left(H/\phi(P)\right).

Thus there is a bijection (depending on

preserving the isomorphism class of the restricted vertical tangent bundles. Therefore,

\min\left\{\deg\tau^{*}T_{\left(E(H)/\phi(P)\right)/X}:\tau\hbox{ a section of }E(H)\left(H/\phi(P)\right)\to X\right\}\ =\ s_{\phi(P)}\left(E(H)\right),

as desired. Part (b) is immediate from (a).
∎

As a special case, we note that

(ii). This slightly generalises

4.1.
Maps between moduli spaces

Let
\phi\colon G\rightarrow H
be a surjective morphism of connected reductive algebraic groups. Here we will generalise some statements in
[
Rth75
, § 7]
, to relate the stability of a principal
G
-bundle
E
and that of
E(H)
. Firstly, we record an essential technical fact.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be a connected linear algebraic group, and let
P\subset G
be a parabolic subgroup. Then
Z(G)=Z(P)
. (In particular,
Z(G)\subseteq P
.)

Proof.

By Springer
[
Spr09
, Corollary 6.4.10]
, we have
N_{G}(P)=P
. As
Z(G)\subseteq N_{G}(H)
for all subgroups
H\subseteq G
, in particular
Z(G)\subseteq P
; whence
Z(G)\subseteq Z(P)
. For the converse, we observe that the argument of
[
Spr09
, Corollary 6.2.9]
applies to an arbitrary parabolic: If
y\in Z(P)
then the commutator map
g\mapsto ygy^{-1}g^{-1}
defines a map
G\to G
which factorises via
G/P
; for if
p\in P
, then

y(gp)y^{-1}(gp)^{-1}\ =\ yg(py^{-1}p^{-1})g^{-1}\ =\ yg(y^{-1}pp^{-1})g^{-1}\ =\ ygy^{-1}g^{-1}.

But since
G/P
is projective and
G
is affine, the image of this map is constant, and thus equals the point
y\cdot 1_{G}\cdot y^{-1}\cdot 1_{G}^{-1}=1_{G}
. Thus in fact
y\in Z(G)
.
∎

The main result of the section proves Theorem
B
and describes a special situation where all Segre functions
s_{P}
are invariant under a surjective homomorphism, and so we obtain a morphism between certain components of the moduli spaces and a correspondence between the stratifications. In particular, this generalises
[
Rth75
, Proposition 7.1]
and
[
Rth96
, Corollary 3.18]
. Recall the locally closed strata
M_{X}(G,\delta;P,s)^{\circ}
defined in (
3.9
). For each
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)
, define

(4.3)

\Sigma(G,\delta;P)\ :=\ \{s\in{\mathbb{N}}:M_{X}(G,\delta;P,s)^{\circ}\hbox{ is nonempty}\}.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\phi\colon G\to H
be a surjective homomorphism of connected reductive algebraic groups satisfying
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)_{0}\subseteq Z(G)
. Let
E\to X
be a principal
G
-bundle and let
E(H)
be the associated principal
H
-bundle.

(a)

For
all
parabolics
P\subset G
, we have
s_{P}(E)=s_{\phi(P)}(E(H))
.

(b)

E
is (semi)stable if and only if
E(H)
is (semi)stable.

(c)

For each topological type
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)
, the map
\phi_{\delta}\colon M_{X}(G,\delta)\ \to\ M_{X}(H,\phi_{*}\delta)
induced by extension of structure group is a morphism.

(d)

We have
\phi_{\delta}^{-1}M_{X}(H,\phi_{*}\delta;\phi(P),s)^{\circ}=M_{X}(G,\delta;P,s)^{\circ}
.

(e)

If
\phi_{\delta}
is surjective, then
\Sigma(G,\delta;P)=\Sigma(H,\phi_{*}\delta;\phi(P))
.

Proof.

(a) By Lemma
4.3
, for all parabolics
P\subset G
we have
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)_{0}\subseteq P
. Thus
s_{P}(E)=s_{\phi(P)}(E(H))
for all
P
by Proposition
3.8
(a).

(b) Observe that, for each parabolic
P\subset G
, the image
\phi(P)
is parabolic in
H
by
[
Spr09
, Corollary 6.2.8]
; and, for a parabolic subgroup
Q\subset H
, the inverse image
\phi^{-1}(Q)\subset G
is parabolic in
P
by Lemma
3.5
(a). Also, recall that
E
is stable if
s_{P}(E)>0
for every maximal parabolic
P\subset G
and strictly semistable if
s_{P}(E)\geq 0
for every maximal parabolic
P
with equality for at least one
P
.
Then the statement follows from Proposition
3.8
(a) and Remark
3.6
, since

s_{Q}(E(H))\ =\ s_{\phi(\phi^{-1}(Q))}(E(H))\ =\ s_{\phi^{-1}(Q)}(E)\quad\hbox{and}\quad s_{P}(E)\ =\ s_{\phi(P)}(E(H)).

(c) This follows directly from the “only if” of part (b).

(d) Suppose
F\in M_{X}(H,\phi_{*}\delta)
is a principal
H
-bundle with
s_{\phi(P)}(F)=s
, and which is of the form
E(H)
for some
E\in M_{X}(G,\delta)
; that is, lying in the image of
\phi_{\delta}
. By (a), we have
s_{P}(E)=s
. Conversely, if
s_{P}(E)=s
for a
G
-bundle
E
of topological type
\delta
, then
s_{\phi(P)}(E(H))=s
by the same argument.

(e) By (d), we have an inclusion
\Sigma(G,\delta;P)\subseteq\Sigma(H,\phi_{*}\delta;\phi(P))
in any case. When
\phi_{\delta}
is surjective, by part (a) we see that
M_{X}(G,\delta;P,s)^{\circ}
is nonempty whenever
M_{X}(H,\phi_{*}\delta;\phi(P),s)^{\circ}
is nonempty. Thus the inclusion is a bijection.
∎

4.2.1.
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
and

Let

5.2.
Nonemptiness of strata

We give now a pair of lemmas which will be useful both for proving the existence of stable
G

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

be an extension of line bundles. Then there exists an irreducible family
{\mathcal{F}}\to T\times X
containing

Proof.

Suppose firstly that

as defined in (

of rank two and degree

over an irreducible base will suffice. Suppose, then, that

For any line bundle

to be a nontrivial extension.)
∎

Proposition 5.4

5.3.

We are now in a position to construct irreducible components of some Segre strata

5.4.
A Hirschowitz bound for
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
-bundles

We continue to abbreviate
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
to
G
, and write
B
for the Borel subgroup of upper triangular invertible matrices.

By
[
HN01
, Theorem 1.1]
for any reductive group
H
and parabolic
P\subset H
, one has
s_{P}(E)\leq g\cdot\dim(H/P)
. This shows in particular that
s_{B}(E)\leq 3g
for any
G
-bundle
E
. The upper bound
g\cdot\dim(G/P)
has been sharpened in case
P
is a maximal parabolic in
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
in
[
Hir86
]
(see also
[
CH10
]
) and for certain parabolics in
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}
and
\mathrm{SO}_{r}
in
[
CH14
,
CH15
]
. Here we use Proposition
5.7
to obtain a sharp upper bound for
s_{B}
in a similar way.

As the computations become rather tedious in general, we content ourselves with proving the bound (Theorem
C
(iii)) under convenient numerical hypotheses on
\delta
and
g
.

Corollary 5.10

(Hirschowitz bound)
.

Suppose that
\delta\equiv 0\;(\!\!\mod 3)
and
g\equiv 1\;(\!\!\mod 6)
. Then for any
E\in M_{X}(G,\delta)
we have
s_{B}(E)\leq 3(g-1)
.

Proof.

Firstly, by Theorem
5.6
, we have
\dim M({\underline{d}})\leq 6g-5+s
where
s=2(d_{3}-d_{1})
. Thus
M({\underline{d}})
can be dense in
M_{X}(G,\delta)
only if
6g-5+s\geq 9g-8=\dim M_{X}(G,\delta)
; that is,
s\geq 3(g-1)
. Let us show that one
M({\underline{d}})
is indeed dense for the value
s=3(g-1)
.

By hypothesis,
g=6u+1
for some
u\geq 1
. We may take any tuple of the form
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})\ =\ (-5u+k,u+k,4u+k)
, so that

(i)

d_{2}-d_{1}=6u=g-1
, and

(ii)

d_{3}-d_{1}=9u=\frac{3(g-1)}{2}

As
g=6u+1
is odd and
d_{1}+d_{2}=-4u+2k
is even, by Theorem
4.1
a general
F
of degree
d_{1}+d_{2}
satisfies
s_{1}(F)=g-1
. As
d_{2}-d_{1}=g-1
by (i), a general extension
0\to L_{1}\to F\to L_{2}\to 0
represented in
{\mathbb{H}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}
may be taken to be general in moduli. If
L_{3}
is also general, then by Hirschowitz’s Lemma
F^{*}\otimes L_{3}
may be assumed to be Brill–Noether general. Therefore, applying (i) and (ii) we obtain

h^{0}(X,F^{*}\otimes L_{3})\ =\ \chi(X,F^{*}\otimes L_{3})\ =\ 2d_{3}-d_{2}-d_{1}-2(g-1)\ =\\
2(d_{3}-d_{1})-(d_{2}-d_{1})-2(g-1)\ =\ 3(g-1)-(g-1)-2(g-1)\ =\ 0.

By (i), also
h^{0}(X,L_{1}^{-1}L_{2})=0
for general
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
. As
N_{\sigma}
is an extension
0\to L_{1}^{-1}L_{2}\to N_{\sigma}\to F^{*}\otimes L_{3}\to 0
, we have
h^{0}(X,N_{\sigma})=0
. Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
5.7
, we see that a general fibre of
\Phi_{\underline{d}}\colon{\mathbb{B}}_{\underline{d}}\dashrightarrow M_{X}(G,\delta)
has dimension
2
, and the image has dimension

\dim{\mathbb{B}}_{\underline{d}}-2\ =6g-3+2(d_{3}-d_{1})-2=\ 6g-5+3(g-1)\ =\ 9g-8\ =\ \dim M_{X}(G,\delta)\;.

In particular,
\Phi_{\underline{d}}
is dominant, and
s_{B}(E)\leq 3(g-1)
for a general
E\in M_{X}(G,0)
. By the semicontinuity proven in Theorem
3.3
, the same is true for all
G
-bundles
E
of topological type
\delta
.
∎
