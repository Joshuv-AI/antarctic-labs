Search and Rescue

The Search and Rescue (SAR) effort was conducted out of the VIE station

and consisted largely of Air America, Inc. aircraft. A detailed log of

search efforts is available at AAM, Inc. VIE if required. For various
_reasons, the USAF could only offer limited assistance. O-1 and A-1

siheratt did search late the first day but the bulk of their assistance

was through radio relay from control aircraft.

With a few breaks due to weather and one false lead from a native

_ claiming to have seen an aircraft, the SAR continued through 5 January

1972. All efforts to find traces of 293 were unsuccessful.

Due to the extreme hostility of the area, several AAM, Inc. aircraft
received battle damage while on the SAR. Due to the proximity of the
hostile Route 46, the SAR was rather constricted in nature. The final
effort consisted of dropping leaflets offering rewards for information
and/or the aircrew. The SAR, although late in starting, was as thorough
as terrain, the weather and the enemy would permit. Further air search

was deemed fruitless after 5 Jamuary 1972.
