Abstract
This work establishes that an optimal transport (OT) problem regularized by a given f f -divergence admits the same solution as another OT problem regularized by a different g g -divergence, under an appropriate transformation of the cost function. This structural equivalence between OT problems regularized by distinct divergences, in the sense of sharing the same unique minimizer, is demonstrated within the framework of Polish spaces with bounded cost functions.



I
Introduction

Optimal transport (OT) is established as a fundamental tool for comparing probability distributions, with applications ranging from machine learning to computer graphics
[
21
,
2
,
6
,
14
,
19
,
16
]
. The choice of a suitable discrepancy measure between distributions is essential for tasks such as regression, classification, and generative modeling. In this respect, OT-based distances offer a significant advantage over classical divergences, such as the Kullback–Leibler divergence or other
f
-divergences, as demonstrated in
[
2
]
and
[
14
]
. Indeed, the topology induced through Wasserstein distances is weaker and better adapted to comparing measures with non-overlapping supports or those supported on lower-dimensional sets. See
[
2
]
and
[
22
]
. This property makes OT particularly attractive for modern data analysis.

Despite these appealing properties, from a computational perspective, solving the Kantorovich formulation of optimal transport involves tackling a large-scale linear program. This task often becomes intractable for high-dimensional or large-sample problems, as shown in
[
18
]
. To mitigate the computational complexity of the original problem and improve the sample complexity of the estimators, regularization is commonly employed. This ranges from classic entropic regularization to structured
[
6
]
or quadratic regularizers
[
3
]
. In particular, classic entropic regularization leads to the celebrated Sinkhorn algorithm
[
8
]
. However, more recent developments highlight the benefits of broadening this framework to general
f
-divergences
[
13
,
20
]
. These schemes yield desirable properties such as sparsity or robustness to outliers. Parallel to these advances in optimal transport, similar variational problems are extensively studied within the field of statistical learning theory, particularly within the statistical empirical risk minimization (ERM) framework
[
5
,
17
,
10
,
11
]
. Specifically, theoretical equivalence between variational problems regularized with two different
f
-divergences is established in
[
11
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
12
]
. Therein, it is proved that an ERM problem regularized by a specific
f
-divergence is reformulable using a different divergence and a suitably transformed cost function, yielding the exact same minimizer.

This work establishes an equivalence between optimal transport problems with
f
-divergence regularizations, which is analogous to
[
11
, Theorem 2]
in the context of ERM with
f
-divergence regularizations. More specifically, in this work, it is proved that two optimal transport problems regularized by different divergences admit the same unique optimal coupling, up to a suitable transformation of the cost function. This structural equivalence between OT problems is demonstrated within the framework of Polish spaces with bounded cost functions. Under these general assumptions, the existence of optimal potentials for the dual problem and the uniqueness of the optimal coupling for the primal problem are rigorously established. Furthermore, a closed-form expression for the optimal coupling is obtained.

I-B

Assumptions on
\phi
-divergences

The purpose of this section is to define the class of
\phi
-divergences endowed with sufficient regularity to guarantee satisfactory theoretical properties to regularize OT problems.
Let
\phi
be a convex, proper, lower semi-continuous function from
\mathbb{R}
to
(-\infty,+\infty]
. Throughout this work, the real numbers
a_{\phi}
and
b_{\phi}
denote the following quantities

\displaystyle a_{\phi}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\inf\operatorname{dom}(\phi);\ \text{and}

(1)

\displaystyle b_{\phi}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sup\operatorname{dom}(\phi).

(2)

As stated in
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
, the following limits exist

\displaystyle\alpha_{\phi}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{\phi(x)}{x};\ \text{and}

(3)

\displaystyle\beta_{\phi}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\phi(x)}{x}.

(4)

Note that if
\operatorname{dom}(\phi)=(0,+\infty)
, then by convention

It is shown in
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
that
the convex conjugate of
\phi
, which is denoted by
\phi^{*}
, verifies

\displaystyle\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}(\phi^{*}))=(\alpha_{\phi},\beta_{\phi}).

(5)

The class of Legendre functions, characterized by strict convexity and essential smoothness, ensures a smooth one-to-one correspondence between primal and dual variables. The following definition characterizes functions of Legendre type.

Definition 1

(
[
4
]
, Definition 2.5)
.

A function
\phi
is said to be of Legendre type, if it is strictly convex on
\operatorname{dom}(\phi)
; differentiable on
\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}(\phi))
; and its derivative
{\phi}^{\prime}:\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}(\phi))\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to a_{\phi}}\phi^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\infty,\text{ if }a_{\phi}>-\infty;\ \text{and}

(6)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to b_{\phi}}\phi^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+\infty,\text{ if }b_{\phi}<+\infty.

(7)

The specific class of Legendre functions possesses certain properties regarding conjugation, defined in
[
24
, Section 2.3]
, as detailed in the following lemma.

Lemma 1

(
[
4
]
, Lemma 2.6)
.

If
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is of Legendre type, then so is its convex conjugate
\phi^{*}
. Moreover
\phi^{\prime}:(a_{\phi},b_{\phi})\to(\alpha_{\phi},\beta_{\phi})
and
{\phi^{*}}^{\prime}:(\alpha_{\phi},\beta_{\phi})\to(a_{\phi},b_{\phi})
are continuous, strictly increasing and mutually inverse maps.

In the context of this work, the focus is on a specific subset of Legendre functions that are suitable for defining divergences between probability measures, which are defined as follows.

Definition 2

(Legendre-type generator)
.

A Legendre type function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is said to be a Legendre-type generator when
\phi(1)=0
;
a_{\phi}=0
and
b_{\phi}=+\infty
, with
a_{\phi}
in (
1
) and
b_{\phi}
in (
2
); and its derivative satisfies
\phi^{\prime}(1)=0
.

Given a Legendre-type generator
\phi
, it follows from (
5
) that the interior of the domain of
\phi^{*}
is
(-\infty,\beta_{\phi})
, and
{\phi^{*}}^{\prime}:(-\infty,\beta_{\phi})\to(0,+\infty)
is a one-to-one strictly increasing map, the inverse of which is
\phi^{\prime}:(0,+\infty)\to(-\infty,\beta_{\phi})
.
Note that a Legendre-type generator
\phi
is always positive, as it is convex and verifies
\phi^{\prime}(1)=0
, thus its minimum is achieved at
1
, and the function satisfies
\phi(1)=0
. Moreover, note that the condition
\phi(1)=\phi^{\prime}(1)=0
is not restrictive, as
\phi
-divergences are invariant up to an affine transformation
[
23
]
. A comprehensive classification of the common
\phi
-divergences generated by a Legendre-type generator is available in
[
15
, Tables 1 and 2]
. The following definition specifies the notion of
\phi
-divergence adopted throughout this work.

Definition 3
.

Given a Legendre-type generator
\phi
and two probability measures

and

defined on the same measurable space, the
\phi
-divergence of

with respect to

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(P||Q)=\begin{cases}\int\phi(\frac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}Q}(\theta))\mathrm{d}Q(\theta),\ \text{ if }P\ll Q;\\
+\infty,\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

(8)

where
\frac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}Q}
is the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
P
with respect to
Q
.

Definition
3
is not standard when
the function
\phi
is not assumed to be
superlinear (i.e.,
\lim_{x\to+\infty}\phi(x)/x=+\infty
)
.
In the typical definition, certain probability measures may yield a finite
\phi
-divergence value even when they are not absolutely continuous with respect to the reference measure;
see
[
7
,
4
,
1
]
. However, when a

-divergence is used as a regularization term in an optimization problem, allowing non–absolutely continuous measures may lead to situations in which the optimizer no longer admits a closed-form expression. This is due to the possible presence of a non-zero singular part, which typically cannot be expressed in closed form, as illustrated in
[

II
Problem Formulation

This section establishes the mathematical formulation of optimal transport regularized by a
\phi
-divergence.
The primal problem is introduced, as well as its dual counterpart, and the necessary functional analytic tools.
Throughout this section, the function
\phi
is a Legendre-type generator (Definition
2
). The measure
P_{XY}
in
\triangle(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y})
is assumed to exhibit given marginal measures denoted by
P_{X}\in\triangle(\mathcal{X})
and
P_{Y}\in\triangle(\mathcal{Y})
. The constant
\lambda
is a strictly positive real number, and the function

\mathsf{c}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}

is a bounded Borel measurable cost function. These elements constitute the parameters of the optimal transport problem with
\phi
-divergence regularization, as presented in
[
13
]
and
[
20
]
.

II-A

Primal Problem

The OT problem with
\phi
-divergence regularization is formulated in
[
13
]
and
[
20
]
as

\displaystyle\inf_{P_{XY}\in\Pi(P_{X},P_{Y})}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}}\mathsf{c}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}P_{XY}(x,y)

(9)

\displaystyle+\lambda\,D_{\phi}(P_{XY}\,\|\,P_{X}P_{Y}).

From Definition
3
, the divergence is infinite whenever
P_{XY}
is not absolutely continuous with respect to the product of its marginal measures,
P_{X}
and
P_{Y}
.
Consequently, the analysis is restricted to absolutely continuous couplings.
This allows the primal problem to be expressed directly in terms of the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
P_{XY}
with respect to the product measure
P_{X}P_{Y}
, which is a non-negative function in
\operatorname{L}^{1}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{=}}\inf_{r\geq 0}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}}\Big(\mathsf{c}(x,y)\,r(x,y)+\lambda\phi\big(r(x,y)\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}P_{X}P_{Y}

(10a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{Y}}r(\cdot,y)\,\mathrm{d}P_{Y}(y)\displaystyle\mathop{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{=}}}_{P_{X}}1

(10b)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}}r(x,\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}P_{X}(x)\displaystyle\mathop{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{=}}}_{P_{Y}}1,

(10c)

where (
10b
) and (
10c
) enforce the marginal constraints on the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
P_{XY}
with respect to
P_{X}P_{Y}
; and
r
is a non-negative function in
\operatorname{L}^{1}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y})
.

The dual problem is derived in

15

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sup_{f,g}\int_{\mathcal{X}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}P_{X}(x)+\int_{\mathcal{Y}}g(y)\,\mathrm{d}P_{Y}(y)

(11)

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}}\phi^{*}\left(\frac{f(x)+g(y)-\mathsf{c}(x,y)}{\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}P_{X}P_{Y},\ \

Attainment of the primal problem

As shown in

III-D

Sufficient conditions for primal attainment

The validity of Theorem
2
relies on the assumption that there exists a pair of optimal potentials in
\mathcal{A}_{\phi,\mathsf{c}}
.
Intuitively, this condition requires that the optimal potentials stay uniformly bounded away from the singularity of the dual function
{\phi^{*}}^{\prime}

While verifying this property directly for general Polish spaces can be challenging, it holds automatically in several significant scenarios commonly encountered in applications.
This section explores sufficient conditions ensuring this requirement is met.

The first sufficient condition concerns divergences with superlinear growth, such as the Kullback-Leibler divergence.
In this setting, the domain of the conjugate function
\phi^{*}
extends to the entire real line, effectively removing the boundary singularity.

Lemma 3
.

If
\beta_{\phi}
in (
4
) is equal to

, any pair of optimal potentials is in
\mathcal{A}_{\phi,\mathsf{c}}
.

Proof:

The proof is presented in
[
15
]
.
∎The second sufficient condition addresses the case of discrete OT, where the underlying spaces are finite sets.
Here, the finiteness of the space prevents the values of the potentials from accumulating asymptotically against the boundary
\beta_{\phi}
.

Lemma 4
.

If the sets
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are both finite, then any pair of optimal potentials is in
\mathcal{A}_{\phi,\mathsf{c}}
.

Proof:

The proof is presented in
[
15
]
.

IV
Equivalence of the problem to regularization on the family of
\phi
-divergences

IV-A

Main results

The following theorem constitutes the main result of this work. Intuitively, it states that the choice of divergence in regularized OT is not intrinsic to the problem: any effect of the regularization on the optimal coupling can equivalently be attributed to a modification of the transport cost. The result and its proof have been adapted from
[
11
, Theorem 2]
, which develops in the context of empirical risk minimization with
\phi
-divergence regularization. Although there are fundamental technical differences due to the presence of additional constraints in the case of optimal transport, the two results convey the same meaning in their corresponding scenarios.

Theorem 3
.

Assume that the problem in (
11
) admits a pair of optimal potentials

(\hat{f},\hat{g})\in\mathcal{A}_{\phi,\mathsf{c}}
,
and let
\psi
be a Legendre-type generator (Definition
2
).
Then, there exists a bounded function

\mathsf{v}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}

such that the problem in (
9
) is equivalent,
in the sense that both problems admit the same minimizer,
to the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle\inf_{P_{XY}\in\Pi(P_{X},P_{Y})}\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}}\mathsf{v}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}P_{XY}(x,y)

\displaystyle+\lambda D_{\psi}\!\left(P_{XY}\,\middle\|\,P_{X}P_{Y}\right).

(16)

Proof:

Let
(\hat{f},\hat{g})\in\mathcal{A}_{\phi,\mathsf{c}}
be maximizing potentials for the dual problem in (
11
).
Define the cost function
\mathsf{v}
as

\displaystyle\mathsf{v}(x,y)=-\lambda{\psi^{\prime}}\left({\phi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left(\frac{\hat{f}(x)+\hat{g}(y)-\mathsf{c}(x,y)}{\lambda}\right)\right)+1.

(17)

Given that the functions
\mathsf{c}
,
\hat{f}
and
\hat{g}
are bounded and that the range of
(x,y)\mapsto\frac{\hat{f}(x)+\hat{g}(y)-\mathsf{c}(x,y)}{\lambda}
is bounded and contained in some interval of the form
(-\infty,\gamma)
, with
\gamma<\beta_{\phi}
, the range of
(x,y)\mapsto{\phi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left(\frac{\hat{f}(x)+\hat{g}(y)-\mathsf{c}(x,y)}{\lambda}\right)
is bounded and contained in an interval of the form
(\rho,+\infty)
with
\rho>0
.
Since
\psi^{\prime}
is
C^{1}
on
(0,\infty)
, the function

\displaystyle(x,y)\mapsto{\psi^{\prime}}\left({\phi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left(\frac{\hat{f}(x)+\hat{g}(y)-\mathsf{c}(x,y)}{\lambda}\right)\right)

(18)

is also bounded.
Thus, the function
\mathsf{v}
is bounded, ensuring that problem (
3
) fits within the setting established in previous sections.
Let
\check{f}\in\operatorname{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X},P_{X})
and
\check{g}\in\operatorname{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{Y},P_{Y})
be defined by

\displaystyle\check{f}(x)=\frac{1}{2},\ \forall x\in\mathcal{X};

and

\displaystyle\check{g}(y)=\frac{1}{2},\ \forall y\in\mathcal{Y}.

(19)

The proof proceeds by demonstrating that the functions
\check{f},\check{g}
satisfy (
13
) and (
14
) for the problem in (
3
).
Verification proceeds as follows: let

be fixed, and compute the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{Y}}{\psi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left(\frac{\check{f}(x)+\check{g}(y)-\mathsf{v}(x,y)}{\lambda}\right)\mathrm{d}P_{Y}(y)

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{Y}}{\psi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left(\frac{1+\lambda{\psi^{\prime}}({\phi^{\prime}}^{-1}(\frac{\hat{f}(x)+\hat{g}(y)-\mathsf{c}(x,y)}{\lambda}))-1}{\lambda}\right)\mathrm{d}P_{Y}(y)\ \ \ \ \

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{Y}}{\phi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left(\frac{\hat{f}(x)+\hat{g}(y)-\mathsf{c}(x,y)}{\lambda}\right)\mathrm{d}P_{Y}(y).

(21)

Since
(\hat{f},\hat{g})\in\mathcal{A}_{\phi,\mathsf{c}}
is the solution to (
11
), Lemma

Consider the target regularization defined by the reverse KL generator

From KL to Jensen-Shannon regularization

In the case of a transition to Jensen–Shannon regularization, the target generator is

These examples confirm that changing the regularizing divergence manifests as a non-linear adjustment of the cost function, ensuring that the unique minimizer of the regularized optimal transport problem remains unchanged.

V
Conclusions

This work investigates
\phi
-divergence regularized optimal transport in Polish spaces with bounded cost functions. The existence of optimal dual potentials and unique primal couplings is established, providing closed-form expressions for the latter. The central contribution, Theorem
3
, demonstrates a structural equivalence: a change in the regularization divergence can be compensated by a non-linear transformation of the cost function while preserving the same unique minimizer. This result remains primarily theoretical, since the transformed cost
\mathsf{v}
in (
17
) explicitly depends on the dual potentials
(\hat{f},\hat{g})
of the original problem. Thus, it does not reduce the computational complexity of solving regularized OT problems. Nonetheless, it provides a unifying framework for regularized OT and offers new perspectives for the characterization and sampling of optimal couplings.
