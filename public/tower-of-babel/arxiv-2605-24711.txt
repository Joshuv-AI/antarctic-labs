Covering vertices by sequential stars

Abstract
We study the problem of covering the maximum number of vertices in a graph by a collection of vertex-disjoint stars, each with a number of satellites in a given interval [ k , ℓ ] [k,\ell] , where 1 ≤ k < ℓ 1\leq k<\ell and ℓ \ell can be infinity. This is referred to as sequential [ k , ℓ ] [k,\ell] -Star Packing problem. It is solvable in polynomial time when k = 1 k=1 , but becomes strongly NP-hard when k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . In this paper, we propose either the first or an improved approximation algorithm for the following four sequential settings: • a k + 1 2 \frac{k+1}{2} -approximation algorithm when k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 and ℓ = ∞ \ell=\infty , improving the previous best ratio of ( k + 1 ) 2 2 ​ k + 1 \frac{(k+1)^{2}}{2k+1} ; • a 4 3 \frac{4}{3} -approximation algorithm when k = 2 k=2 and ℓ = ∞ \ell=\infty , improving the previous best ratio of 3 2 \frac{3}{2} ; • the first ( 1 + ℓ ℓ + 1 ) (1+\frac{\ell}{\ell+1}) -approximation algorithm when 2 = k < ℓ 2=k<\ell ; • and the first ( 1 + max ⁡ { k − 1 2 , ( k + 1 ) ​ ℓ 3 ​ ( ℓ + 1 ) } ) (1+\max\left\{\frac{k-1}{2},\frac{(k+1)\ell}{3(\ell+1)}\right\}) -approximation algorithm when 3 ≤ k < ℓ 3\leq k<\ell . Introduction

Given an integer
i\geq 1
, an
i
-star
is a complete bipartite graph in which one part contains one vertex, called the
center
of the star,
and the other part contains
i
vertices called the
satellites
.
An
i
-star is also called a
k^{+}
-star if
i\geq k
, or an
\ell^{-}
-star if
i\leq\ell
.
The star packing problem is to find a collection of vertex-disjoint stars in a given graph
G=(V,E)
so that the number of vertices in these stars is maximized.
One sees that, if any star can be used, then the problem is easy, i.e., solvable in polynomial time.
The more interesting case is where there are constraints on the candidate stars,
leading to many variants that have been studied in the literature for their theoretical importance and a wide range of applications such as wireless sensor networking
[
14
]
.

We examine the
sequential
star packing problem in this paper,
here by sequential it means the candidate stars have their numbers of satellites in a given interval
[k,\ell]
, for
1\leq k\leq\ell
and
\ell
can be a fixed integer or infinity.
The problem is denoted as
[k,\ell]
-Star Packing
.
We remark that in the literature, sequential was used to refer to the case where
k=1
only
[
8
]
.
Hell and Kirkpatrick showed both
[1,\infty)
-Star Packing
and
[1,\ell]
-Star Packing
, for any fixed integer
\ell
, to be polynomial time solvable
[
8
]
,
and the star packing problem using any other candidate star family to be strongly NP-hard
[
7
]
.
It follows that
[k,\ell]
-Star Packing
is hard for any
k\geq 2
, and we study it in this paper from the approximation algorithm perspective.

When
k=\ell
, that is, only one candidate star is allowed,
[k,k]
-Star Packing
reduces to the
(k+1)
-Set Packing problem
(by constructing a
(k+1)
-set of vertices whenever these
k+1
vertices can form into a
k
-star, in
O(|V|^{k+1})
time),
the latter of which admits a
(\frac{k+2}{3}+\epsilon)
-approximation algorithm, for any
\epsilon>0

[
4
]
.
It turns out the design and analysis techniques we develop in this paper do not work out a better approximation algorithm, and thus we assume
k<\ell
throughout the paper,
i.e., in each variant we have at least two candidate stars.
We also observe the case where
k=2
to be quite different from the other case where
k\geq 3
.
Therefore, we distinguish four distinct variants, namely,
[2,\infty)
-Star Packing
,
[k,\infty)
-Star Packing
where
k\geq 3
,

[2,\ell]
-Star Packing
where
\ell>2
, and
[k,\ell]
-Star Packing
where
\ell>k\geq 3
.

The
[k,\infty)
-Star Packing
problem is also denoted as
k^{+}
-Star Packing
, and has received several studies.
It is NP-hard for any

even on bipartite graphs
[
12
]
.
Specially,
[2,\infty)
-Star Packing
remains NP-hard on even more restricted graph classes such as bipartite graphs of maximum degree
4

[

]
and cubic graphs
[

]
.
On the inapproximability,

]

since it is equivalent to the maximum distance-

[

.
Recently, Li

proved a lower bound of

on the approximation ratio for

-Star Packing
when

, if P

A number of approximation algorithms have been designed for

-Star Packing

1.1
Other related work

The star packing problem belongs to the general
\mathcal{H}
-packing framework, where
\mathcal{H}
is a family of (non-isomorphic) graphs.
Given an
\mathcal{H}
, an
\mathcal{H}
-packing in an input graph
G=(V,E)
is a set of vertex-disjoint subgraphs of
G
, each isomorphic to a graph in
\mathcal{H}
.
A vertex in
G
is said to be
covered
by the
\mathcal{H}
-packing if it is contained in one of the subgraphs in the packing.
The
\mathcal{H}
-packing problem aims to find an
\mathcal{H}
-packing to cover the maximum number of vertices.

For another example of
\mathcal{H}
, we can look at an
i
-clique, typically denoted as
K_{i}
, which is the complete graph on
i
vertices.
When
\mathcal{H}=\{K_{2}\}
, i.e., it contains only a single
2
-clique, the
\mathcal{H}
-packing problem is equivalent to the maximum matching problem.
A maximum matching can be computed by several well-known algorithms including the Blossom algorithm by Edmonds
[
1
]
.
The Blossom algorithm is iterative, that it starts with an empty matching and in each iteration it looks for an augmenting path (after contracting odd cycles),
which connects two uncovered vertices through an alternating path with edges alternately not in the current matching and in the current matching;
the algorithm terminates when no augmenting path is found, and the achieved matching is shown to be optimal.
The Blossom algorithm runs in
O(|V|^{2}\cdot|E|)
time, that is, the
\mathcal{H}
-packing problem is polynomial solvable.
When
\mathcal{H}=\{K_{2},K_{3}\}
,
Hell and Kirkpatrick
[
7
]
discovered three specific augmenting structures,
and proved that any packing, in which none of the three structures exists, is a packing that covers the maximum number of vertices.
They provided a polynomial time algorithm for computing such an optimal
\mathcal{H}
-packing.
Lastly, they showed if
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\{K_{1},K_{2},\ldots\}
contains
K_{1}
or
K_{2}
,
then the
\mathcal{H}
-packing problem is polynomial solvable,
and the provided algorithm is also based on augmenting structures connecting different uncovered vertices or some other specific local structures
[
7
]
.

In another paper, Hell and Kirkpatrick
[
8
]
did the same for
[1,\ell]
-Star Packing
where
\ell
is a fixed integer,
by discovering certain augmenting structures on the
\ell
-stars in the current packing,
and proving that any packing in which none of structures exists is a packing that covers the maximum number of vertices;
they provided a polynomial time algorithm for computing such a packing.

Our contributions and paper organization

In this paper, we study four NP-hard variants of the star packing problem from the approximation algorithm perspective, namely,

-Star Packing
,

-Star Packing

[2,\ell]
-Star Packing

-Star Packing
where

We continue to develop local search techniques coupled with the amortized analysis,

[

,
8
]

We contribute two approximation algorithms for

-Star Packing

and

, respectively, improving the previous best ones.
Specifically, for

-Star Packing
where

, we present in Section 2 a

-approximation algorithm,

by Hu et al.
[
9
,
10
]
.
Being the first algorithm in the paper, the description and the performance analysis are presented in full details.
Mostly building on top of the first algorithm, in the second half of Section 2, we propose additionally an augmenting configuration for

-Star Packing

to design a

-approximation algorithm, improving the previous best ratio of

.
The

-Star Packing
problem, where

, does not seem to have been studied in the literature.
When

, in Section 3, we design slightly different local improvement operations from those in the first algorithm,
and propose a distinct augmenting configuration, leading to the first

-approximation algorithm.
Section 4 deals with

, where we make full use of the augmenting configuration,
and show that the same algorithm is a

Given that a lower bound of
\frac{12k+19}{12k+18.994}
on the approximation ratio has been proven for
[k,\infty)
-Star Packing
when

,
we prove in Section 5 the APX-hardness for the last remaining case of

-Star Packing
.
We conclude the paper in Section 6.

2
Approximating the
[k,\infty)
-Star Packing
problem when
k\geq 2

The
[k,\infty)
-Star Packing
problem is also referred to as
k^{+}
-star packing in the literature
[
12
,
11
,
9
,
10
]
.
The state-of-the-art approximation algorithms are local search proposed by Hu et al.
[
9
,
10
]
,
which have a ratio of
1+\frac{k^{2}}{2k+1}
when
k\geq 3
and a ratio of
\frac{3}{2}
when
k=2
.
In this section, we present improved, also local search, approximation algorithms
Approx
[k,\infty)
for
k\geq 3
with a ratio of
\frac{k+1}{2}
,
and
Approx
[2,\infty)
for
k=2
with a ratio of
\frac{4}{3}
.
We will highlight the major differences after we introduce the new local improvement operations.

A local search algorithm is typically supported by a number of operations each is designed to improve the current solution through searching a neighborhood in the solution space.
Our algorithm is denoted as
Approx
[k,\infty)
and the operations are
Collect
and
Trade
defined below.
The algorithm is iterative, and during each iteration a feasible solution, referred to as the
current
solution or packing, is assumed at the beginning,
one of the operations is applied to update/improve the solution.
Before the iteration ends, post-processing is often necessary including collecting the solution information, updating the
remainder
graph,
and some other minor tasks to be detailed in each specific algorithm.
If in an iteration none of the operations is applicable, then the algorithm terminates and returns the current solution as the final solution.

Below we define the operations first, and for each operation we estimate its time complexity.
Given a feasible solution
\mathcal{P}
for the input graph
G=(V,E)
, we call each star in
\mathcal{P}
an
internal
star.
Let
V(\mathcal{P})
denote the set of vertices
covered
by (the stars of)
\mathcal{P}
,
then
V-V(\mathcal{P})
is the set of
uncovered
vertices and the graph
R(\mathcal{P})
induced on
V-V(\mathcal{P})
, i.e.,
R(\mathcal{P})=G[V-V(\mathcal{P})]
,
is the

\mathcal{P}
.
A star in the remainder graph is called an
outside
star.

Definition 1
(Operation
Collect
)

[
11
,
9
,
10
]

Given a vertex
v
in the remainder graph, if its degree in the remainder graph is

,
then the operation extracts the
d

v
with the
d

v
as satellites from the remainder graph and adds it to the current solution
\mathcal{P}
.
(Comment: If

The algorithm starts with the empty solution,
and in the first a few iterations

is repeatedly applied on vertices of degree at least
k
in the remainder graph until impossible.
Note that the degrees of all the vertices can be pre-processed in
O(|E|)

and inside the algorithm their degrees in the remainder graph can be updated in

time too after applying a

Each operation

time but the iteration takes

time due to post-processing.
When no

, which is stated in the following Observation
1
.
We remark that an observation typically consists of straightforward statements that do not need rigorous proofs,
though we will provide some sample proofs below.

Observation 1

When no

1.

the maximum degree of the remainder graph is at most
k-1
;

2.

the center of an internal star is not adjacent to any uncovered vertex.

As we define more operations below, we remark that after applying any one of them in an iteration,
during the post-processing operation

in the remainder graph,
or if there is an uncovered vertex adjacent to the center of an internal star then the uncovered vertex is added to the star to become covered.
Such post-processing at the end of each iteration guarantees the two statements in Observation

To find an applicable operation

Therefore, an applicable operation

When operation

More observations can be made when operation

Using the above defined

2.1
The performance analysis for

We fix an optimal star packing denoted as
\mathcal{Q}
for discussion, in which the stars are referred to as
optimal

Recall that the stars in the computed star packing
\mathcal{P}

a vertex is said

Using Observation
1
(1), when the center of an optimal star is uncovered, at most

We categorize the optimal stars into three types as follows:

Definition 3
(Types of optimal stars)

The optimal stars are categorized into three types:

•

In a Type-1 optimal star, the center and
k-1
satellites are uncovered.

•

In a Type-2 optimal star, the center and no more than
k-2
satellites are uncovered.

•

In a Type-3 optimal star, the center is covered.

Such a categorization of the optimal stars into three types is complete.

We associate one token with each uncovered vertex of
V(\mathcal{Q})
, that is, the number of tokens in an optimal star equals to the number of uncovered vertices.
For each optimal star, the tokens are
transferred
, maybe fractionated,
to some of the covered vertices in the same optimal star according to the below
token-transfer rules
:

Definition 4
(Token-transfer rules)

•

A Type-1 optimal star has
k
tokens in total and at least one covered satellite.
We transfer these
k
tokens to one of the covered satellites.

•

A Type-2 optimal star has
x+1
tokens in total, where
0\leq x\leq k-2
is the number of uncovered satellites, and at least
k-x
covered satellites.
We transfer these
x+1
tokens evenly to
k-x
of its covered satellites (each receiving
\frac{x+1}{k-x}\leq\frac{k-1}{2}
tokens).

•

A Type-3 optimal star has
y
tokens in total, where
0\leq y\leq k-1
is the number of uncovered satellites, and at least
k-y
covered satellites.
We transfer these
y
tokens evenly to the center and
k-y
of its covered satellites (each receiving
\frac{y}{k-y+1}\leq\frac{k-1}{2}
tokens).

By Observations
1
(2) and
2

Observation 3

By the token-transfer rules,

1.

the covered vertices receiving tokens in an optimal star and those uncovered vertices induce a connected subgraph inside the optimal star;
(thus there are always a covered vertex and an uncovered vertex adjacent to each other in the optimal star;)

2.

a vertex receives
k

k

3.

for the satellites of internal
(k+1)^{+}

Proof.

The second item can be not so obvious and we provide a proof.
Denote by

Since the center of

That is,

It then follows from Observation

Lastly, if another satellite of

\frac{k-2}{3}
tokens, the total token received by

\tau(S)\leq\ell+\ell\cdot\frac{k-2}{3}=(\ell+1)\cdot\frac{\ell\cdot(k+1)}{3(\ell+1)}=\frac{(k+1)\ell}{3(\ell+1)}\cdot|V(S)|
.

Assume next the center
c
of
S
receives strictly more than
\frac{k-1}{2}
tokens and
a satellite

receives
\frac{k-1}{2}
tokens in an optimal star
S^{*}
and the co-recipient is
v^{\prime}
.
We thus see that
c
is adjacent to an uncovered vertex
u
and receives its token,
and that the vertex
v^{\prime}
is not in
S
since otherwise
Trade-1
would be applicable on
S
, and therefore
v^{\prime}
is in another internal star
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
.
Due to operation
Trade-2
, we conclude that
v^{\prime}
cannot be a satellite if
S^{\prime}
is a
(k+1)^{+}
-star or be the center if
S^{\prime}
is an
(\ell-1)^{-}
-star.
That is, if
v^{\prime}
is the center of
S^{\prime}
then
S^{\prime}
is an
\ell
-star or if
v^{\prime}
is a satellite of
S^{\prime}
then
S^{\prime}
is a
k
-star.

In the former case, we have identified a sequence of
\ell
-stars that can be used to swap
u
and
v
to become covered and uncovered, respectively,
so that replacing operation
Trade-
i
by operation
Trade-along-AC
we can repeat the argument on any satellite of
S^{\prime}
if it receives
\frac{k-1}{2}
tokens.
Or otherwise,
S^{\prime}
is called a
companion
of
S
, that every one of its satellites receives at most
\frac{k-2}{3}
tokens and it is not a companion for any other
\ell
-star.
Due to the finiteness of the input graph, we always reach the latter case, where the
k
-star
S^{\prime}
is identified through one of its satellite
v^{\prime}
receiving
\frac{k-1}{2}
tokens,
and it is also called a
companion
of
S
.
Similarly as the proof of
Lemma

2
, replacing operation
Trade-
i
by operation
Trade-along-AC
,
the companion
S^{\prime}
is not heavy, its center can receive at most
\frac{k-2}{3}
tokens, and it is not a companion for any other
\ell
-star.

We thus have proved that for the satellite
v
of
S
we can identify a unique companion star
S^{\prime}
,
which can take over
\frac{k-1}{2}-\frac{k-2}{3}
tokens of
v
to reach a total of at most
\frac{k-1}{2}\cdot|V(S^{\prime})|
.
That is, every satellite of
S
retains
\frac{k-2}{3}
tokens, and thus the retained total token is at most
\frac{(k+1)\ell}{3(\ell+1)}\cdot|V(S)|
.
Therefore, on average, each internal star
S
has

\tau(S)\leq\max\left\{\frac{k-1}{2}\cdot|V(S)|,\frac{(k+1)\ell}{3(\ell+1)}\cdot|V(S)|\right\}=\max\left\{\frac{k-1}{2},\frac{(k+1)\ell}{3(\ell+1)}\right\}\cdot|V(S)|.

It follows from
|V(\mathcal{Q})|\leq|V(\mathcal{P})|+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{P}}\tau(S)
that

\frac{|V(\mathcal{Q})|}{|V(\mathcal{P})|}\leq 1+\frac{\sum_{S\in\mathcal{P}}\tau(S)}{|V(\mathcal{P})|}\leq 1+\max_{S\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{\tau(S)}{|V(S)|}=1+\max\left\{\frac{k-1}{2},\frac{(k+1)\ell}{3(\ell+1)}\right\}.

This proves the approximation ratio.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we studied four NP-hard sequential variants of the star packing problem from the approximation algorithm perspective,
where the number of satellites in a candidate star is in a given interval
[k,\ell]
, where
2\leq k<\ell
and
\ell
is a fixed integer or infinity.
We contributed four improved or the first approximation algorithms for them, respectively.
Namely, for
[k,\infty)
-Star Packing
where
k\geq 3
, we presented a
\frac{k+1}{2}
-approximation algorithm,
improving the previous best
\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{2k+1}
-approximation algorithm by Hu et al.
[
9
,
10
]
;
for
[2,\infty)
-Star Packing
, we designed a
\frac{4}{3}
-approximation algorithm, improving the previous best ratio of
\frac{3}{2}
.
The
[k,\ell]
-Star Packing
problem, where
2\leq k<\ell
, is first studied by us,
and when
k=2
, we proposed the first
(1+\frac{\ell}{\ell+1})
-approximation algorithm;
when
k\geq 3
, a slight extension leads to a
(1+\max\left\{\frac{k-1}{2},\frac{(k+1)\ell}{3(\ell+1)}\right\})
-approximation algorithm.

The main algorithm design and analysis techniques are local search coupled with amortized analysis;
additionally, inspired by various augmenting structures observed in the past work of Edmonds
[
1
]
and Hell and Kirkpatrick
[
7
,
8
]
,
we explored augmenting configurations that help bridge two distinct local structures for applying a local improvement operation.
It would be interesting to see other algorithm design techniques than local search to design further better approximation algorithms,
or other global packing structures that can be constructed in polynomial time to ensure the packing quality.

On the inapproximability, we proved a lower bound of
\frac{2077}{2076}
for the last remaining case of
[2,\infty)
-Star Packing
.
For
[k,\infty)
-Star Packing
where
k\geq 3
, we can achieve better lower bounds than the previous one of
\frac{12k+19}{12k+18.994}
,
but all these bounds are far away from the state-of-the-art approximation ratios.
It thus requires continuous efforts in narrowing down the gaps for each of these sequential star packing problems.
