Does hot QCD have a conformal manifold in the chiral limit?
Shi Chen, Aleksey Cherman, Robert D. Pisarski

Abstract
Recent lattice evidence suggests the chiral phase transition in QCD is second-order for N f ≥ 2 N_{f}\geq 2 massless flavors. We constrain CFT descriptions of a critical line in temperature T T and imaginary baryon chemical potential θ B = i ​ μ B / T \theta_{B}=i\mu_{B}/T . An ’t Hooft anomaly at general θ B \theta_{B} constrains the transition even at θ B = 0 \theta_{B}=0 , leaving only three minimal scenarios. The best-motivated scenario for N f ≥ 3 N_{f}\geq 3 , and perhaps also N f = 2 N_{f}=2 , is beyond Ginzburg-Landau, featuring a conformal manifold of θ B \theta_{B} -dependent universality classes with an exactly marginal operator related to baryon density.

McKane and Stone 1980

\displaystyle\gamma_{\phi}(g^{2})\ \sim\ \frac{\left(N_{f}^{2}-1\right)}{4N_{f}}\left(g^{2}+\frac{3N_{f}^{2}}{128}g^{6}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(g^{8}\right)\,.

(S4.3)

Note that a vertex operator has the natural engineering dimension
0
, even in
(2+\epsilon)
D.
Namely, our
\gamma_{\phi}
is the analogy to
\frac{1}{2}\zeta
in the
O(N)
nonlinear sigma model of Ref.
(
Henriksson 2023
, Section 4.1.3)
(see especially their Footnote 35).
As a result, the scaling dimension of
\phi_{ij}
at the UV fixed point is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\phi}(\epsilon)\ \equiv\ \gamma_{\phi}(g_{*}^{2})\ \sim\ \left(1-\frac{1}{N_{f}^{2}}\right)\left[\epsilon-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{3}\right]+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{4}\right)\,.

(S4.4)

The fact that the
N_{f}
-dependence factorizes at this order in
perturbation theory is likely to be an accident, and should not hold
generically.

Our goal here is to extrapolate
\Delta_{\phi}(\epsilon)
to
\epsilon=1
. The most naive
extrapolation would neglect
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4})
terms and find

\displaystyle\Delta_{\phi}(1)=1-\frac{1}{N_{f}^{2}}

(S4.5)

This cannot be remotely reliable because of the terrible truncation artifacts of alternating series at
\epsilon=1
, as well as the fact that underlying perturbative series should be asymptotic.
To get more reasonable estimates of
\Delta_{\phi}
in
3
D, we need a sophisticated resummation method.

Here we extrapolate from
(2+\epsilon)
D to
3
D using the Padé-Conformal-Borel (PCB)
resummation
Costin and Dunne 2018
;
Costin and Dunne 2019
;
Costin and Dunne 2020
;
Costin and Dunne 2022
;
Costin and Dunne 2021
;
Costin et al. 2022
, see also
Ref.
Dunne 2025
for a review. Given a truncated series with
K

terms for
K\gg 1
, PCB achieves fractional errors scaling as
K^{-4}
, significantly better
than the
K^{-2}
scaling of standard Padé-Borel summation. Here
K=3
, so
we cannot expect to be in the asymptotic regime where PCB is fully reliable.
Nevertheless, we expect it to be significantly better than a
naive extrapolation.

For convenience, let us introduce

\displaystyle F(\epsilon)\ \equiv\ \frac{\Delta_{\phi}(\epsilon)}{\epsilon\left(1-\frac{1}{N_{f}^{2}}\right)}\ \sim\ 1-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})

(S4.6)

Our goal is to construct an approximation of the actual function

F(\epsilon)
and examine its behavior at
\epsilon=1
.
The first step in this resummation scheme is to pass to
the Borel plane by
replacing
\epsilon^{n}
with
\delta^{n}/n!
in the series, to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\delta)\ \sim\ 1-\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{3}\right)\,.

(S4.7)

Since this series seems to be alternating, the nearest Borel-plane singularity is likely on the negative real axis, but we cannot reliably estimate its location due to the truncation.
Let us therefore assume that the nearest Borel-plane singularity is at
\delta=-p
with
p>0
, and then treat
p
as a free parameter that we will vary to get an estimate of the truncation error in our resummation.

In Padé-Borel resummations, one usually constructs a Padé approximant to the Borel-plane series, and then integrates it against the Borel kernel
\frac{1}{\epsilon}e^{-\delta/\epsilon}
over the domain
[0,\infty)
.
The idea of the PCB resummation is to improve the convergence of this procedure by first performing a conformal transformation
\delta\mapsto z(\delta)
given by

\displaystyle z(\delta)=\frac{\sqrt{1+\frac{\delta}{p}}-1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{\delta}{p}}+1}.

(S4.8)

with the inverse
\delta(z)=\frac{4pz}{(1-z)^{2}}
.
This conformal transformation maps the branch cut
-\infty+i\varepsilon\to-p\to-\infty-i\varepsilon
to the arc
1+i\varepsilon\to-1\to 1-i\varepsilon
on the unit circle.
It also maps the Borel integral domain
[0,\infty)
to
[0,1)
, which then lies within the convergence disk of the conformal-transformed Borel-transformed series

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{F}}(z)\ \equiv\ \mathcal{F}(\delta(z))\ \sim\ 1-2pz+4p\left(p-1\right)z^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(z^{3}\right).

(S4.9)

The PCB resummation then constructs Padé approximants to
\overline{\mathcal{F}}(z)
, instead of to
\mathcal{F}(\delta)
.
An
[m,n]
Padé approximant to
\overline{\mathcal{F}}(z)
is a rational function of the form
P_{m}(z)/Q_{n}(z)

that matches the series expansion of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}(z)
to order
z^{m+n}
.
The
[1,1]
,
[2,1]
and
[1,2]
Padé approximants are then

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{F}}(z)\big|_{[1,1]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-2z}{2(p-1)z+1}\,,

(S4.10)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{F}}(z)\big|_{[2,1]}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2pz\left(4p^{2}z-2p+z-2\right)-2p-3z+2}{p(8z-2)-3z+2}\,,

(S4.11)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{F}}(z)\big|_{[1,2]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(4p^{2}-3\right)z+2}{z\left(2p\left(4p^{2}z+2p+z+2\right)-3\right)+2}\,.

(S4.12)

When
p
takes values in an appropriate range, these Padé approximants do not put spurious poles on the interval
z\in[0,1]
, and are then reasonable to use in our resummation.
This range of values is
p\in(1/2,\infty)
for the
[1,1]
approximant,
p\in(1/6,1]
for the
[2,1]
approximant, and
p\in(p_{*},\infty)
where
p_{*}\approx 0.1478
is the real root of
8p^{3}+4p^{2}+6p-1
for the
[1,2]
approximant.

The final step is to perform the Borel integral to
\mathcal{F}(\delta)|_{[m,n]}=\overline{\mathcal{F}}(z(\delta))|_{[m,n]}
.
We thus obtain PCB-resummed approximants of
F(\epsilon)
, controlled by parameter
p
:

\displaystyle F(\epsilon)\big|_{[m,n]}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\delta\ e^{-\delta/\epsilon}\mathcal{F}(\delta)\big|_{[m,n]}\,.

(S4.13)
