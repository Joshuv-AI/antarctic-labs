Abstract
Cosmic birefringence and the Hubble tension represent compelling challenges to the standard Λ \Lambda CDM model. The early dark energy (EDE) model with potentials V ⁡ ( ϕ ) ∝ [ 1 − cos ⁡ ( ϕ / f ) ] n V(\phi)\propto[1-\cos(\phi/f)]^{n} offer a unified framework to address both anomalies through energy injection near matter-radiation equality and parity-violating Chern–Simons coupling to photons. While previous studies have focused on n = 3 n=3 , the dependence of the birefringence signal on the potential index n n remains largely unexplored. We perform a comprehensive statistical analysis of axion-like EDE models with n = 2 n=2 , n = 3 n=3 , and n = ∞ n=\infty , using E ​ B EB cross-polarization data from Planck- E ​ B EB and ACT- E ​ B EB . The n = 2 n=2 model is severely disadvantaged, displaying extreme coupling values ( g ​ M pl ≈ 69.912 gM_{\rm pl}\approx 69.912 for Planck, − 40.726 -40.726 for ACT), large χ min 2 \chi^{2}_{\rm min} (144.52 and 86.93), and Δ ​ χ 2 < 1 \Delta\chi^{2}<1 with many local minials. Conversely, n = 3 n=3 achieves the best fits ( χ min 2 = 65.70 \chi^{2}_{\rm min}=65.70 and 48.08 48.08 ) with consistent couplings ( g ​ M pl = − 0.210 ± 0.024 gM_{\rm pl}=-0.210\pm 0.024 and − 0.158 ± 0.025 -0.158\pm 0.025 ) that accurately reproduce observations across all angular scales. We checked that the n = 3 n=3 configuration represents the optimal choice for simultaneously addressing the Hubble tension and cosmic birefringence within a theoretically viable framework.

II
Cosmic Birefringence Induced by Early Dark Energy

Cosmic birefringence can naturally arise from parity-violating interactions between photons and a dynamical pseudoscalar field. In a broad class of axion-inspired models, such interactions are described by a Chern–Simons-type coupling between the scalar field
\phi
and the electromagnetic field tensor. The effective Lagrangian takes the form
14
;
81
;
9

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}-V(\phi)-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}g\phi F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu},

(1)

where
V(\phi)
denotes the scalar-field potential,
F_{\mu\nu}
is the electromagnetic field strength tensor, and
\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}
represents its dual. The coupling constant
g
determines the interaction strength between photons and the pseudoscalar background. Such parity-violating operators emerge naturally in axion and string-inspired frameworks and provide a well-motivated mechanism for generating cosmological birefringence.

A direct consequence of the Chern–Simons interaction is the breaking of the degeneracy between left- and right-handed photon helicities. In a homogeneous cosmological background, the modified photon dispersion relation becomes
16
;
77

\omega_{\pm}=k\mp\frac{g}{2}\dot{\phi},

(2)

where
k
is the photon wavenumber and
\dot{\phi}\equiv d\phi/dt
describes the background evolution of the scalar field. The opposite signs associated with the two helicity states imply that photons acquire different propagation phases during their evolution. Consequently, linearly polarized radiation experiences a continuous rotation of its polarization plane, giving rise to the phenomenon of cosmic birefringence.

The rotation angle equals the integral of the helicity-dependent phase difference along the photon trajectory.
Under the WKB approximation, the polarization rotation angle from emission time
t
to the present epoch
t_{0}
is given by
33
;
35

\beta(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{t_{0}}dt^{\prime}(\omega_{+}-\omega_{-})=\frac{g}{2}[\phi(t_{0})-\phi(t)],

(3)

shows that the total rotation angle depends only on the difference between the scalar-field values at the endpoints of photon propagation. Thus, CMB photons record the cosmological evolution of
\phi
, making them sensitive probes of parity-violating physics in the early Universe.

To describe the dynamics of the pseudoscalar field, we consider an axion-like EDE scenario motivated by string-inspired constructions. The scalar potential is parametrized as
42
;
57

V(\phi)=V_{0}\left[1-\cos\left(\frac{\phi}{f}\right)\right]^{n},

(4)

where
V_{0}
characterizes the energy scale of the potential, and the potential index
n
can be 2, 3, …, and
\infty
.
83
;
65
.

The polarization of CMB radiation is conventionally described by the Stokes parameters. In standard cosmology, the polarization state is characterized by the intensity
I
and the linear polarization parameters
Q
and
U
, which can be written as
87
;
86
;
85

I_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}I+Q&U\\
U&I-Q\end{pmatrix},

(5)

where
Q
and
U
describe the magnitude and orientation of linear polarization.

A time-dependent birefringence angle modifies the Boltzmann equation of CMB polarization. In conformal time
\eta
, the spin-
\pm 2
polarization perturbations
{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}
satisfy
48
;
11

{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}^{\prime}+iq\mu{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}=\tau^{\prime}\left[-{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}+\sqrt{\frac{6\pi}{5}}{}_{\pm 2}Y_{2}^{0}(\mu)\Pi(\eta,q)\right]\pm 2i\beta^{\prime}{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P},

(6)

where a prime denotes differentiation with respect to conformal time,
\tau^{\prime}
is the differential optical depth, and
\Pi
is the Thomson-scattering source term. Compared with the standard case, the additional term proportional to
\beta^{\prime}\equiv d\beta/d\eta
rotates linear polarization, mixing
E
and
B
modes and generating nonzero
TB
and
EB
correlations.

In the limit where the birefringence angle varies sufficiently slowly after recombination, the cumulative rotation effect can be approximated by an effective angle
\beta
. In this case, the observed
E
- and
B
-mode polarization are mixed, giving rise to a nonzero
EB
correlation. The corresponding angular power spectrum can be written as
53
;
51

C_{\ell}^{EB}=\frac{1}{2}\sin(4\beta)(\tilde{C}_{\ell}^{EE}-\tilde{C}_{\ell}^{BB}).

(7)

In the standard
\Lambda
CDM model, parity conservation implies
C_{\ell}^{EB}=0
. Therefore, a nonzero
EB
correlation may indicate parity-violating effects in the photon propagation.
For comparison with CMB observations, it is convenient to introduce the dimensionless angular power spectrum
D_{\ell}^{EB}
as

D_{\ell}^{EB}=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2\pi}C_{\ell}^{EB}.

(8)

We will discuss the comparison between theoretical power spectrum values and experimental data (Planck-
EB
and ACT-

and
-0.158\pm 0.025

D_{\ell}^{EB}

The

and 49.67) with coupling strengths approximately an order of magnitude smaller than
n=3

suggests excessive steepening beyond
n=3
provides no additional benefit.
The
n=2

gM_{\rm pl}\approx 69.912
and
-40.726
) exceeding perturbative scales, elevated
\chi^{2}_{\rm min}

These findings demonstrate that
n=3
