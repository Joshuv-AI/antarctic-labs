The Archimedean height pairing for differential forms on degeneration of Riemann surfaces
Junyu Cao

Abstract
. We define the Archimedean height pairing for fiberwise cohomologically trivial differential forms on a one-parameter degeneration of Riemann surfaces, and we study its asymptotic behavior. The proof relies on recent work by Dai–Yoshikawa on the asymptotics of small eigenvalues. As an application, we relate this pairing to the current-valued pairing of Filip–Tosatti, extending their construction to broader geometric settings.



Introduction

Let
\pi\colon X\to S\simeq{\mathbb{D}}
be a proper surjective holomorphic map from a
complex surface
X

to a Riemann surface isomorphic to the unit disk. We assume that
\pi
has connected fibers and
X_{0}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(0)
is
the unique singular fiber. Assume that
X
is equipped with a

Kähler
metric.

For two smooth real
(1,1)
-forms
\alpha,\beta
on
X
that are
cohomologically trivial on smooth fibers
X_{s}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(s)(s\neq 0)
, i.e.,
\int_{X_{s}}\alpha=\int_{X_{s}}\beta=0
, we define
the Archimedean height pairing
between
them.

Definition 0.1

(Archimedean height pairing,
Definition

4.1
)
.

For any
s\neq 0
, let
-\varphi_{s}\in C^{\infty}(X_{s})

be a potential for
\alpha
on
X_{s}
, i.e.,

\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\varphi_{s}+\alpha\mid_{X_{s}}=0
. Then

the Archimedean height pairing

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle
between
\alpha,\beta

is a function on smooth locus
S^{\circ}\coloneqq S\setminus\{0\}
:

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle(s)\coloneqq\int_{X_{s}}\varphi_{s}\beta,\hskip 10.00002pts\neq 0.

Since
\pi
is a submersion on
S^{\circ}
,
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\in C^{\infty}(S^{\circ})
.

We study its asymptotics around the singular fiber of the degeneration.

Theorem 0.2

(
Theorem

4.5
,
Corollary

4.6
)
.

There is a constant
c_{\alpha,\beta}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle-c_{\alpha,\beta}\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}

extends continuously to
S
. In particular,

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\in L^{\infty}(S^{\circ})

if and only if
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\in C^{0}(S)
.

When
X_{0}
is a reduced divisor and
C_{1},\cdots,C_{N}
are its irreducible components, the constant
c_{\alpha,\beta}
is determined in the following
way: Let
M
be the intersection matrix of irreducible components
of
X_{0}
, given by
(M_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}=(C_{i}\cdot C_{j})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
, and
let

M^{+}
be the
Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of
M
. Then
c_{\alpha,\beta}=\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}^{T}(M^{+})^{T}\mathbf{v}_{\beta}
, where

\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}=\left(\int_{C_{1}}\alpha,\dots,\int_{C_{N}}\alpha\right)^{T},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{v}_{\beta}=\left(\int_{C_{1}}\beta,\dots,\int_{C_{N}}\beta\right)^{T}.

Remark 0.3
.

The name
Archimedean height pairing
comes from
Arakelov theory, e.g. in Bost’s work
[
3
]
. But their
pairings are defined for algebraic cycles/divisors.

Precisely, let
D,E
be two divisors
on the one-parameter degeneration
\pi\colon X\to S

of algebraic curves. Suppose
D,E
are of
relative degree
0
and their supports do not intersect. For a divisor
D
, we
define its Green’s current
g_{D_{s}}
on general fibers
X_{s}
following
[
17
]
*Equation (2.1). The
g_{D_{s}}
is a distribution on

X_{s}
solving the equation

\partial\overline{\partial}g_{D_{s}}+\pi i\delta_{D_{s}}=0,

where
D_{s}
is the restriction of
D
on
X_{s}
.
We remark that
g_{D_{s}}
is smooth on
X_{s}
away
from the support of
D_{s}
.
Then the Archimedean height pairing between

D
and
E
is a well-defined function on
S^{\circ}
:

s\mapsto\langle D_{s},E_{s}\rangle_{\infty}\coloneqq g_{D_{s}}([E_{s}])=\sum_{i}a_{i}g_{D_{s}}(q_{i}),

where
E_{s}=\sum_{i}a_{i}q_{i}
.

In Holmes–de Jong’s paper
[
17
]
*Theorem 2.1,
they derive a similar asymptotic result for the Archimedean height pairing
\langle D_{s},E_{s}\rangle_{\infty}
on degenerations of algebraic
curves.

Notation and conventions

On a complex manifold

For a closed smooth differential form

to denote its de Rham cohomology class.

We use

1.A.
Laplacian on singular varieties

Let
X
be an algebraic variety with the induced Fubini-Study metric, and let
X_{\rm reg}
be the regular part of

Following Li-Tian’s work
[
19
]
*Section 4, we define the ordinary Laplacian
\Delta

on compactly supported smooth functions on

and
let
\overline{\Delta}
be its Friedrichs extension. Then we have

Theorem 1.1

(
[
19
]
*Section 4)
.

The Friedrichs extension
\overline{\Delta}
defined
on functions is self-adjoint on a compact variety
X

(without boundary) whose singular set
\Sing X
is at least
of real codimension
2

When

is

, i.e.,

is locally constant.

.

1.B.
Continuity of fiber integral à la Barlet

In
[
1
]
, Barlet derived the asymptotic expansion of the
fiber integral for the degeneration of complex manifolds near singular fibers.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
1
]
*Théorème 1)
.

Let
D=\{s\in\mathbb{C}\mid|s|<1\}
, and let
X
be a reduced and irreducible complex analytic space of dimension
n+1
. Let
\pi:X\to D
be a surjective holomorphic map, and let us denote by
X_{s}
the cycle
\pi^{-1}(s)
. Let
K
be a compact subset of
X
. There exist rational numbers
r_{1}\leq\dots\leq r_{k}
in
[0,2)\cap\mathbb{Q}
such that for every
C^{\infty}
differential form
\varphi
of type
(n,n)
on
X
with support in
K
, the function
F_{\varphi}\coloneqq\int_{X_{s}}\varphi
admits, as
s\to 0
, an asymptotic expansion of the form:

F_{\varphi}(s)\sim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=r_{1},\dots,r_{k}\\
j=0,\dots,n\\
(m,m^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\end{subarray}}T^{r,j}_{m,m^{\prime}}(\varphi)s^{m}\overline{s}^{m^{\prime}}|s|^{r}(\log|s|)^{j}

where
T^{r,j}_{m,m^{\prime}}
is a
(1,1)
-current on
X
.

Since
F_{\varphi}(s)
does not blow up as
s\to 0
, we have

T^{0,j}_{0,0}(\varphi)=0
for
j\geq 1
.
Let
0<r
be the minimum of non-zero
r_{1}\leq\cdots\leq r_{k}
in
[0,2)\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
,
then for
0<a<\min(r,1)
, we have

\left\lvert F_{\varphi}(s)-F_{\varphi}(0)\right\rvert\leq C(\varphi)\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{a}.

Thus
F_{\varphi}(s)
is Hölder continuous around
0
.

Remark 1.4
.

When the base of the fibration
\pi
is of higher
dimension, Takayama extends Barlet’s result in his
recent paper
[

]

Convergence on the singular fiber

\{\rho_{i}\colon U_{i}\to{\mathbb{R}}\}_{i=0}^{N}
(we denote

U_{\epsilon}
by
U_{0}
)
such that

•

\operatorname{supp}(\rho_{i})\subset U_{i}
for each

•

for all

,

•

\sum_{i=0}^{N}\rho_{i}(x)=1
for all

.

Then for
n\gg 1
such that
\left\lvert s_{n}\right\rvert<\epsilon_{0}
, we
have

\int_{X_{s_{n}}}f_{s_{n}}\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\int_{X_{s_{n}}}\rho_{i}f_{s_{n}}\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\int_{X_{s_{n}}\cap U_{i}}\rho_{i}f_{s_{n}}\alpha

And

\int_{X_{0}}f\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\int_{X_{0}}\rho_{i}f\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\int_{X_{0}\cap U_{i}}\rho_{i}f\alpha

Since
f_{s_{n}},f
are zero in
U_{0}=U_{\epsilon}
, we have

\int_{X_{0}\cap U_{i}}\rho_{0}f\alpha=0=\int_{X_{s_{n}}\cap U_{0}}\rho_{0}f_{s_{n}}\alpha

For
1\leq i\leq N
, the fibration
\pi
on

is trivial and
(\rho_{i}f_{s_{i}}\alpha)\mid_{V_{i}}\to(\rho_{i}f\alpha)\mid_{V_{i}}
in

. So

\int_{X_{s_{n}}\cap U_{i}}\rho_{i}f_{s_{n}}\alpha=\int_{V_{i}}(\rho_{i}f_{s_{i}}\alpha)\mid_{V_{i}}\to\int_{V_{i}}(\rho_{i}f\alpha)\mid_{V_{i}}=\int_{X_{0}\cap U_{i}}\rho_{i}f\alpha.

In summary, we have

\int_{X_{s_{n}}}f_{s_{n}}\alpha\to\int_{X_{0}}f\alpha,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }n\to\infty.

∎

We can extend above lemma if the contribution of fiber integral around singularities is small
enough, which is the following lemma.

Lemma 1.10
.

Suppose
f_{s_{n}}

converges to
f
in
C^{0}_{\rm loc}
on
X_{0,\rm reg}
and
X_{0}

is reduced. Let
\omega
be a
Kähler
metric on

For any

is a tubular

\Sing X_{0}

as
\epsilon\to 0
and

L^{1}
-integrable on

, then for any

(q,q)
form

\int_{X_{s_{n}}}f_{s_{n}}\alpha\to\int_{X_{0}}f\alpha,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

For any fixed
\epsilon>0
, we have a cutoff function

\eta_{\epsilon}\in C^{\infty}(X)
such that

•

0\leq\eta_{\epsilon}\leq 1
,

•

\eta_{\epsilon}=1

•

Then by

\int_{X_{s_{n}}}f_{s_{n}}(1-\eta_{\epsilon})\alpha\to\int_{X_{0}}f(1-\eta_{\epsilon})\alpha,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }n\to\infty.

Therefore, we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\lvert\int_{X_{s_{n}}}f_{s_{n}}\alpha-\int_{X_{0}}f\alpha\right\rvert\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\lvert\int_{X_{s_{n}}}f_{s_{n}}\eta_{\epsilon}\alpha\right\rvert+\left\lvert\int_{X_{0}}f\eta_{\epsilon}\alpha\right\rvert.

For the first term, we have

\left\lvert\int_{X_{s_{n}}}f_{s_{n}}\eta_{\epsilon}\alpha\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\frac{\alpha}{\omega^{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{B(\Sing X_{0},\epsilon)}\left\lvert f_{s_{n}}\right\rvert\omega^{q}.

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\lvert\int_{X_{s_{n}}}f_{s_{n}}\eta_{\epsilon}\alpha\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\frac{\alpha}{\omega^{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}I(\varepsilon).

We then have

(1.1)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\lvert\int_{X_{s_{n}}}f_{s_{n}}\alpha-\int_{X_{0}}f\alpha\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\frac{\alpha}{\omega^{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}I(\varepsilon)+\left\lvert\int_{X_{0}}f\eta_{\epsilon}\alpha\right\rvert.

Note that

\left\lvert\int_{X_{0}}f\eta_{\epsilon}\alpha\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\frac{\alpha}{\omega^{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{B(\Sing X_{0},\epsilon)\cap X_{0}}\left\lvert f\right\rvert\omega^{q}\omega^{q}

and
\mathrm{vol}(B(\Sing X_{0},\epsilon)\cap X_{0})\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0

by

1.12
. So
\left\lvert\int_{X_{0}}f\eta_{\epsilon}\alpha\right\rvert\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
by

-integrability of

.

Letting

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\lvert\int_{X_{s_{n}}}f_{s_{n}}\alpha-\int_{X_{0}}f\alpha\right\rvert=0.

We then conclude the result.
∎

We prove the uniform bound for volume of tubular neighborhoods
of singular set, which is used in previous two lemmas and is
standard:

Lemma 1.12

(Volume bound for singular tubular neighborhoods)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to{\mathbb{D}}
be a proper surjective holomorphic map
from a complex manifold
X
of complex dimension

p=q+1
to the unit disk.
Suppose that
\pi
has connected fiber and
X_{0}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(0)

is the unique singular fiber. Suppose
X_{0}

is reduced. Then
\Sing X_{0}
is of complex codimension
\geq 1

in
X_{0}
.

Let
\omega
be a Kähler metric on
X
, and denote by
B(\Sing X_{0},\epsilon)
the
\epsilon
-tubular neighborhood of
\Sing X_{0}
in
X
with respect to the distance induced by
\omega
. Then there exist constants
C>0
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
s\in{\mathbb{D}}
near
0
(including
s=0
) and all
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
, we have

\int_{B(S,\epsilon)\cap X_{s}}\omega^{q}\leq C\epsilon^{2}.

Proof.

Since
\Sing X_{0}
is a compact complex analytic
subvariety of
X_{0}
of complex dimension less than
q-1
,
its
2(q-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure is finite.
Thus, there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that for any sufficiently
small
\epsilon>0
, the compact set
\Sing X_{0}
can be covered by a finite
number of balls
N(\epsilon)
of radius
\epsilon
centered at points

x_{i}\in\Sing X_{0}
, where

N(\epsilon)\leq C_{1}\epsilon^{-2(q-1)}.

The
\epsilon
-tubular neighborhood
B(\Sing X_{0},\epsilon)
is strictly contained in the union of the concentric balls
B(x_{i},2\epsilon)
.

Next, we bound the volume of
X_{s}
within these local balls. For any
s
(including
s=0
),
X_{s}
is a purely
q
-dimensional complex analytic cycle. Because
\omega
is a Kähler metric on
X
, the standard Bishop-Lelong theorem (or monotonicity formulas) guarantees that the volume ratio is monotonically increasing. Thus, we take
R_{0}>2\epsilon_{0}
such that for all
x\in S
and all
2\epsilon<2\epsilon_{0}<R_{0}
, we have

\frac{1}{(2\epsilon)^{2q}}\int_{B(x,2\epsilon)\cap X_{s}}\omega^{q}\leq\frac{1}{R_{0}^{2q}}\int_{B(x,R_{0})\cap X_{s}}\omega^{q}.

Furthermore, because

and the fibers
X_{s}
are homologous in
X
, the total volume of the fibers

is a topological constant independent of
s
. We have a uniform constant

C_{2}
and the following inequality

\int_{B(x,2\epsilon)\cap X_{s}}\omega^{q}\leq\left(\frac{V}{R_{0}^{2q}}\right)(2\epsilon)^{2q}\coloneqq C_{2}\epsilon^{2q}.

Finally, combining the covering estimate and the local volume bound, we obtain, by the subadditivity of the volume,

\int_{B(\Sing X_{0},\epsilon)\cap X_{s}}\omega^{q}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N(\epsilon)}\int_{B(x_{i},2\epsilon)\cap X_{s}}\omega^{q}\leq N(\epsilon)\cdot C_{2}\epsilon^{2q}\leq C_{1}C_{2}\epsilon^{2}.

Setting
C=C_{1}C_{2}
, the bound holds uniformly for all

near
0
, completing the proof.

is
close to an orthogonal matrix with controlled error terms:

\displaystyle(CC^{T})_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}C_{il}C_{jl}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N-1}(\Upsilon^{(i)}_{s},\Phi_{l-1})_{L^{2}(X_{s})}(\Upsilon^{(j)}_{s},\Phi_{l-1})_{L^{2}(X_{s})}

\displaystyle=(\Upsilon^{(i)}_{s},\sum_{l=1}^{N}\Phi_{l-1}(\Upsilon^{(j)}_{s},\Phi_{l-1}))

\displaystyle=(\Upsilon^{(i)}_{s},\Upsilon^{(j)}_{s}-h^{(j)}_{s})

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\Upsilon^{(i)}_{s}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(X_{s})}\delta_{ij}-(\Upsilon^{(i)}_{s},h^{(j)}_{s})

The last equality follows by
Proposition

2.8
(3).

Since
\left\lVert\Upsilon^{(i)}_{s}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(X_{s})}=1+O(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{8\nu}})
(by
Proposition

2.8
(4)) and
\left\lvert(\Upsilon^{(i)}_{s},h^{(j)}_{s})\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert h^{(j)}_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\Upsilon^{(i)}_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=O(\frac{1}{\log(s^{-1})})^{\frac{1}{2}}
(by
Lemma

2.12
), we have

CC^{T}=I+E,

where
E
, the error term, is controlled by
\left\lVert E\right\rVert\leq K_{1}(\frac{1}{\log(s^{-1})})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
So
C^{-1}=C^{T}(I+E)^{-1}
.

Since
\left\lVert E\right\rVert\ll 1

for
\left\lvert s\right\rvert\ll 1
, we have

(I+E)^{-1}=I-E+E^{2}-E^{3}+\cdots\eqqcolon I+E^{\prime},

where
E^{\prime}=\sum_{i\geq 1}(-1)^{i}E^{i}
. We estimate that

\left\lVert E^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert E\right\rVert(1+\sum_{i\geq 1}\left\lVert E\right\rVert^{i})\leq K_{2}(\frac{1}{\log(s^{-1})})^{\frac{1}{2}}

for some uniform constant
K_{2}
. Then

C^{-1}=C^{T}(I+E^{\prime}).

Note that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\Upsilon^{(1)}_{s}-h^{(1)}_{s}\\
\Upsilon^{(2)}_{s}-h^{(2)}_{s}\\
\vdots\\
\Upsilon^{(N)}_{s}-h^{(N)}_{s}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\Upsilon^{(1)}_{s,\mathrm{low}}\\
\Upsilon^{(2)}_{s,\mathrm{low}}\\
\vdots\\
\Upsilon^{(N)}_{s,\mathrm{low}}\end{pmatrix}=C(s)\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{0}\\
\Phi_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\Phi_{N-1}\end{pmatrix}=C(s)\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\Phi_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\Phi_{N-1}\end{pmatrix}

We have

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\Phi_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\Phi_{N-1}\end{pmatrix}=C^{-1}(s)\begin{pmatrix}\Upsilon^{(1)}_{s,\mathrm{low}}\\
\Upsilon^{(2)}_{s,\mathrm{low}}\\
\vdots\\
\Upsilon^{(N)}_{s,\mathrm{low}}\end{pmatrix}=

\displaystyle C^{T}(s)(I+E^{\prime})\begin{pmatrix}\Upsilon^{(1)}_{s,\mathrm{low}}\\
\Upsilon^{(2)}_{s,\mathrm{low}}\\
\vdots\\
\Upsilon^{(N)}_{s,\mathrm{low}}\end{pmatrix}

So for each
1\leq j\leq N-1
, we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Phi_{j}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}C_{l(j+1)}(s)\Upsilon^{(l)}_{s,\mathrm{low}}+R_{j}.

And the remainder term
R_{j}=\left(C^{T}\cdot E^{\prime}\cdot(\Upsilon^{(1)}_{s,\mathrm{low}},\cdots,\Upsilon^{(N)}_{s,\mathrm{low}})^{T}\right)_{j+1}
is controlled by

\left\lvert R_{j}\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert C^{T}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert E^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert(\Upsilon^{(1)}_{s,\mathrm{low}},\cdots,\Upsilon^{(N)}_{s,\mathrm{low}})^{T}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}=O(\frac{1}{\log(s^{-1})})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

So
\left\lVert R_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(X_{s})}\leq C^{2}\frac{1}{\log(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{-1})}
for some constant
C>0
.

To estimate the derivative of the remainder, we take the
derivative of the
Equation

2.5

on
X_{s}
,

\mathrm{d}\Phi_{j}(s)-C_{l(j+1)}(s)\sum_{l=1}^{N}\mathrm{d}\Upsilon^{(l)}_{s,\mathrm{low}}=\mathrm{d}R_{j}.

Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathrm{d}R_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left\lVert C_{l(j+1)}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\mathrm{d}\Upsilon^{(l)}_{s,\mathrm{low}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\mathrm{d}\Phi_{j}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Since
\left\lVert\mathrm{d}\Phi_{j}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=\lambda_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}(s)
and
\left\lVert\mathrm{d}\Upsilon^{(l)}_{s,\mathrm{low}}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}=O\left(\frac{1}{\log(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{-1})}\right)
by

Proposition

2.8
(4),
we have

\left\lVert\mathrm{d}R_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C^{2}\frac{1}{\log(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{-1})}

for some constant
C>0
.

∎

3.
Estimates of preferred potentials

The setup for this section is the same as
Section

2
(see
1
).

Given a smooth real
(1,1)
-form
\alpha

on
X
, we introduce the following two integrability
conditions:

Condition 3.1
.

We say
\alpha
satisfies
the integrability
condition on general fibers
, if

\int_{X_{s}}\alpha=0

for all
s\neq 0
.

Condition 3.2
.

We say
\alpha
satisfies
the integrability
condition on the singular fiber
, if
\alpha
satisfies
the integrability
condition on general fibers (
3.1
)
and

\int_{C_{i}}\alpha=0,

where
C_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq N
) are irreducible components
of the reduced singular fiber
X_{0}
.

Our goal in this section is to study the
preferred potentials
of smooth
(1,1)
-forms on
X
satisfying
3.1
. The notion of
preferred potentials
originates from
[
13
]
*Theorem 3.2.4.

Definition 3.3

(
Preferred potentials).

Let
\alpha
be a smooth real
(1,1)
-form on
(X,\omega)
satisfying
the integrability
condition on general fibers (
3.1
)
.
Then there is a unique
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X\setminus X_{0})

such that

\displaystyle\int_{X_{s}}\varphi\omega_{s}=0,\hskip 10.00002pt(\alpha+\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\varphi)\mid_{X_{s}}=0.

We say that
\varphi
is the
preferred potential
of the differential form
\alpha
.

Since
(X_{s},\omega_{s})
is compact and
\varphi\mid_{X_{s}}\eqqcolon\varphi_{s}

is smooth on it, we can expand it using Fourier series
in terms of eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{\omega_{s}}
:

\varphi_{s}=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}c_{i}(s)\Phi_{i}(s,z)+\sum_{i\geq N}c_{i}(s)\Phi_{i}(s,z).

The coefficients are determined by

\displaystyle c_{i}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{X_{s}}\varphi_{s}\Phi_{i}(s,z)\omega_{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{X_{s}}\varphi_{s}\frac{-\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}_{z}\Phi_{i}(s,z)}{\lambda_{i}(s)}

\displaystyle=\int_{X_{s}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}_{z}(\varphi_{s})\frac{-\Phi_{i}(s,z)}{\lambda_{i}(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}(s)}\int_{X_{s}}\Phi_{i}(s,z)\alpha.

We use
b_{i}(s)\coloneqq\int_{X_{s}}\Phi_{i}(s,z)\alpha
to denote the
integral terms, and the properties of
\lambda_{i}(s)
are known
for
1\leq i\leq N-1
in
Theorem

2.1
.

We then divide
\varphi
into two parts, a low frequency part and a high
frequency part, following the Fourier expansion:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\rm low}=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}c_{i}(s)\Phi_{i}(s,z),\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi_{\rm high}=\sum_{i\geq N}c_{i}(s)\Phi_{i}(s,z).

These two parts are smooth on
X\setminus X_{0}
by
Lemma

2.3
.

Then we introduce the main theorems of this section.

Theorem 3.4
.

We work under
1
.
Suppose
\alpha
is a smooth real
(1,1)
-form on
(X,\omega)
with

3.1
. Let

\varphi
be the preferred potential of
\alpha
.

(1)

We have an
L^{\infty}
bound for
\varphi_{\rm high}
,

\left\lVert\varphi_{\rm high}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}\leq C\left\lVert\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\alpha\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}

for all
s\neq 0
. The constant
C
only depends
on the geometry of the degeneration.

(2)

We have control over the growth order of

\left\lVert\varphi_{\rm low}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}
,

\left\lVert\varphi_{\rm low}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}\leq C\left\lVert\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{0}}\alpha\right\rVert_{L^{1}(X_{0})}\log(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{-1}).

for all
s\neq 0
.
The constant
C
only depends
on the geometry of the degeneration.

When
\alpha
satisfies the integrability condition
on the singular fiber (
3.2
),
the growth order of
\left\lVert\varphi_{\rm low}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}
has a
better control.

Theorem 3.5
.

We use the same assumption and notations in
Theorem

3.4
.
We assume that the smooth real
(1,1)
-form
\alpha

satisfies
3.2
.
Then the low frequency
part
\varphi_{\rm low}
of the preferred potential satisfies

\frac{\left\lVert\varphi_{\rm low}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}}{\log^{1/2}(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{-1})}\to 0,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }s\to 0.

3.A.
Estimate of the high frequency part

We prove the first part of
Theorem

3.4
regarding the high frequency part of the
preferred potential
. The proof
is based on Cheng-Li’s argument
[
7
]

(also seen in Siu’s lecture note
[
24
]
*Appendix (A.2)).

First, we introduce the following high frequency truncated heat kernel and Green’s function on each
(X_{s},\omega_{s})
:

\displaystyle H^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{2},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=N}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{i}(s)t}\Phi_{i}(s,z_{1})\Phi_{i}(s,z_{2})\hskip 10.00002pt(\text{truncated heat kernel})

\displaystyle G^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}(s)}\Phi_{i}(s,z_{1})\Phi_{i}(s,z_{2})\hskip 10.00002pt(\text{truncated Green's function})

Then the high frequency part
\varphi_{\rm high}
in

Equation

3.1
can be represented by

(3.2)

\varphi_{\rm high}(z,s)=\int_{z_{2}\in X_{s}}(\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\alpha)(z_{2})G^{N}_{s}(z,z_{2})\omega_{s}(z_{2})

To obtain the desired bound on
\varphi_{\rm high}
in
Theorem

3.4
, we estimate

G^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{2})
using Cheng-Li’s argument:

Proposition 3.6
.

There is a uniform constant
C>0
independent of
s\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}

such that

G^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{2})\geq-C,\hskip 10.00002ptz_{1},z_{2}\in X_{s},

for all
s\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}
.

Proof.

Let
H_{s}(z_{1},z_{2},t)
be the heat kernel of
(X_{s},\omega_{s})
,
then we have

(3.3)

\frac{1}{V(s)}+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}e^{-\lambda_{i}(s)t}\Phi_{i}(s,z_{1})\Phi_{i}(s,z_{2})+H^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{2},t)=H_{s}(z_{1},z_{2},t)\geq 0,

where
V(s)=\int_{X_{s}}\omega_{s}
is the volume of
X_{s}
. Since

\omega
is closed,
V(s)\equiv V
is constant.

From the lower bound of higher eigenvalues in
Theorem

2.2
, we know that there
exists
\lambda>0
such that

\lambda_{k}(s)\geq\lambda
for all
k\geq N
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H^{N}_{s}(z,z,t)=\sum_{i=N}^{\infty}-\lambda_{i}(s)e^{-\lambda_{i}(s)t}\left\lvert\Phi_{i}(s,z)\right\rvert^{2}\leq-\lambda H^{N}_{s}(z,z,t).

By Grönwall’s inequality, we have

H^{N}_{s}(z,z,t)\leq H^{N}_{s}(z,z,1)e^{-\lambda(t-1)}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for }t\geq 1

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

(3.4)

\begin{split}\left\lvert H^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{2},t)\right\rvert&\leq\sqrt{H^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{1},t)\cdot H^{N}_{s}(z_{2},z_{2},t)}\\
&\leq\sqrt{H^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{1},1)\cdot H^{N}_{s}(z_{2},z_{2},1)}e^{-\lambda(t-1)}\quad\text{for }t\geq 1.\end{split}

To estimate the truncated Green’s function, we note
that

\int_{0}^{\infty}H^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{2},t)\mathrm{d}t=G^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{i=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}(s)}\Phi_{i}(s,z_{1})\Phi_{i}(s,z_{2}).

Then

\displaystyle G^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}H^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{2},t)\mathrm{d}t+\int_{1}^{\infty}H^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{2},t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}H^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{2},t)\mathrm{d}t-\sqrt{H^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{1},1)\cdot H^{N}_{s}(z_{2},z_{2},1)}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda(t-1)}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(H_{s}(z_{1},z_{2},t)-\frac{1}{V}-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}e^{-\lambda_{i}(s)t}\Phi_{i}(s,z_{1})\Phi_{i}(s,z_{2})\bigr)\mathrm{d}t-I_{2}

\displaystyle(\text{Using }\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{eqn:tmp_heat_knl_sum},\text{and }I_{2}\coloneqq\sqrt{H^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{1},1)\cdot H^{N}_{s}(z_{2},z_{2},1)}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda(t-1)}\mathrm{d}t)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(-\frac{1}{V}-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}e^{-\lambda_{i}(s)t}\Phi_{i}(s,z_{1})\Phi_{i}(s,z_{2})\bigr)\mathrm{d}t-I_{2}

\displaystyle(\text{Using the fact that the heat kernel }H_{s}(z_{1},z_{2},t)\geq 0)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{V}-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left\lVert\Phi_{i}(s,z)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}-I_{2}

\displaystyle\geq-(\frac{1}{V}+I_{1}+I_{2})

Here

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left\lVert\Phi_{i}(s,z)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}\geq 0

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{H^{N}_{s}(z_{1},z_{1},1)\cdot H^{N}_{s}(z_{2},z_{2},0.4)}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda(t-1)}\mathrm{d}t\geq 0.

It remains to control
I_{1},I_{2}
by some uniform constants that are
independent of
s
.

We begin with basic results which
relate the Sobolev constants to the heat kernel and
eigenfunction bounds.

Theorem 3.7

(Theorem 2.1,
[
29
]
)
.

For a manifold
(M,g)
of real dimension
2
, the following two inequalities are equivalent:

(1)

For
0<t\leq 1
and
(x,y)\in M\times M
,

H(x,y,t)\leq C_{1}t^{-2}

(2)

For every
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\leq C_{2}(\left\lVert\mathrm{d}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}})

Here
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
depend continuously on each other.

By classical Moser iteration, we have

Theorem 3.8

(
[
20
]
*Theorem 9.2.7)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold, with the following Sobolev inequality

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{2\nu}\leq S\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{2}

where
\nu>1
. Then for
\Delta u=-\lambda u
, we have

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\exp(\frac{S\sqrt{\nu\lambda}}{\sqrt{\nu}-1})\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{2}.

Specifically, if

in the above Sobolev inequality, and
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{2}=1
, then for

, we have

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\exp(\frac{S\sqrt{2\lambda}}{\sqrt{2}-1})

Finally, we introduce the uniform Sobolev inequality for a
Kähler
family.

Theorem 3.9

(
[
27
]
*Lemma 3.2)
.

Let
\pi:(X,\omega)\to{\mathbb{D}}
be a
Kähler
family, i.e.,
\pi
is a proper, surjective holomorphic map from a
Kähler
manifold
(X,\omega)
to the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
. Suppose
that
X_{0}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(0)
is the unique singular fiber.

Assume
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X_{s}=1
. Then for
0<\left\lvert s\right\rvert<0.5
, we have a uniform Sobolev constant

for

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{4}(X_{s})}\leq C(\left\lVert\mathrm{d}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}})\hskip 10.00002pt\forall f\in C^{\infty}(X_{s})

So

is bounded uniformly by
Theorem

and
Theorem

3.8
. And

by

As a corollary, we can show the first part of

3.4
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Under the settings and notation of
Theorem

3.4
,
we have

\left\lVert\varphi_{\rm high}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}\leq C\left\lVert\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\alpha\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}

for all
s\neq 0
. The constant
C
only depends
on the geometry of the degeneration.

Proof.

By
Equation

3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{\rm high}(z,s)

\displaystyle=\int_{z_{2}\in X_{s}}(\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\alpha)(z_{2})G^{N}_{s}(z,z_{2})\omega_{s}(z_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{z_{2}\in X_{s}}(\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\alpha)(z_{2})(G^{N}_{s}(z,z_{2})+C^{\prime})\omega_{s}(z_{2}),

where we take the constant
C^{\prime}

.
∎

3.B.
Estimate of the low frequency (I)

We prove the second part of
Theorem

3.4
here.

First, we
note that

\varphi_{\rm low}=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}c_{i}(s)\Phi_{i}(s,z)

and the
\Phi_{i}(s,z)
are uniformly bounded by
Theorem

3.9
and
Theorem

3.8
.
So it suffices to estimate

c_{i}(s)
.
Recall that

are well-studied by Dai-Yoshikawa in
Theorem

2.1
.
It remains to determine the asymptotic properties of

b_{i}(s)=\int_{X_{s}}\Phi_{i}(s,z)\alpha

around
0
for all
1\leq i\leq N
.

Our tool is the decomposition
of
\Phi_{i}
in
Theorem

2.10
. We recall
the results here: For each
1\leq i\leq N-1
, we have

\Phi_{i}(s,z)=\sum_{l=1}^{N}C_{li}(s)\Upsilon^{(l)}(s,z)+R_{i}(s,z).

And

•

There is a uniform constant
C
such that
\left\lvert C_{li}(s)\right\rvert<C
for all
1\leq l\leq N
and all
s
.

•

The model functions
\Upsilon^{(l)}(s,z)

are discussed in
Proposition

2.8
.

•

The remainder terms

are discussed in
Theorem

2.10
.

Then each

is decomposed into

(3.5)

b_{i}(s)=\sum_{l=1}^{N}C_{li}(s)\int_{X_{s}}\Upsilon^{(l)}_{s}\alpha+\int_{X_{s}}R_{i}(s,z)\alpha.

We have the following lemma

Lemma 3.11
.

Under the preceding notation. For any smooth

(1,1)
-form
\alpha
on
X
,

\int_{X_{s}}\Upsilon^{(l)}_{s}\alpha=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Area}(C_{l})}}\bigl(\int_{C_{l}}\alpha+\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{\infty}O(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{4\nu}})\bigr).

Proof.

(of
Lemma

3.11
) From the construction of
\Upsilon^{(l)}_{s}
in
Proposition

2.8
, it follows that

\int_{X_{s}}\Upsilon^{(l)}_{s}\alpha=\int_{X_{0}}\frac{\chi_{\epsilon(s)}^{(l)}F_{s}^{*}\alpha}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Area}(C_{l})}},

where
\epsilon(s)=2\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{8\nu}}
.

Since
\alpha
is a smooth form on
X
, by

Theorem

2.4
(4) and
Remark

2.5
, we have

\left\lVert F_{s}^{*}\alpha-\alpha_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{0}\setminus\cup_{p\in\Sing X_{0}}B(p,\epsilon(s)))}\leq K_{2}\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}

for a uniform constant
K_{2}>0
. So

(3.6)

\left\lvert\int_{X_{s}}\Upsilon^{(l)}_{s}\alpha-\int_{X_{0}}\frac{\chi_{\epsilon(s)}^{(l)}\alpha}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Area}(C_{l})}}\right\rvert\leq K_{2}\sqrt{\mathrm{Area}(C_{l})}\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}.

\left\lvert\int_{X_{0}}\frac{\chi_{\epsilon(s)}^{(l)}\alpha}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Area}(C_{l})}}-\int_{C_{l}}\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Area}(C_{l})}}\right\rvert=\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{\infty}O(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{4\nu}}).

and

, we obtain

\left\lvert\int_{X_{s}}\Upsilon^{(l)}_{s}\alpha-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Area}(C_{l})}}\int_{C_{l}}\alpha\right\rvert=\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{\infty}O(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{4\nu}}).

∎

On the other hand, from
Theorem

2.10
(2), we have

\left\lvert\int_{X_{s}}R_{i}(s,z)\alpha\right\rvert=\left\lVert\mathrm{tr}_{\omega}\alpha\right\rVert_{L^{2}(X_{s})}O(\log^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{-1})).

Putting all these asymptotic results into

(3.8)

\begin{split}b_{i}(s)&=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\frac{C_{li}(s)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Area}(C_{l})}}\int_{C_{l}}\alpha+\sum_{l=1}^{N}\frac{C_{li}(s)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Area}(C_{l})}}O(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{4\nu}})\\

We then prove the second part of
Theorem

3.4
.

Proof.

(of
Theorem

3.4
.(2))
For
\alpha
whose integral is zero on general fibers, following

, we have

Estimate of the low frequency (II)

For

4.
Asymptotics of Archimedean height pairing

As an application of previous estimates,
we introduce
the Archimedean height pairing for
smooth real
(1,1)
-forms on
X
. We continue to use the setup
from
1
, except in

Corollary

4.6
, where we allow
for general singular fibers of the degeneration.

Definition 4.1

(Archimedean height pairing)
.

Let
\alpha,\beta
be two smooth real
(1,1)
-forms
on
X
with
\int_{X_{s}}\alpha=\int_{X_{s}}\beta=0

for
s\neq 0
(
3.1
). Let
\varphi
be the preferred potential
of
\alpha
. We define
the Archimedean height pairing

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle
to be a function on
{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle(s)\coloneqq\int_{X_{s}}\varphi\beta,\hskip 10.00002pts\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}.

It is smooth on
{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}
since
\pi\colon X^{\circ}(\coloneqq X\setminus X_{0})\to{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}
is a submersion and
\varphi
is smooth.

Remark 4.2
.

We can use any potential for
\alpha
on
X_{s}
to define the
Archimedean height pairing. Actually, for any potential
-\varphi^{\prime}

of
\alpha
on
X_{s}
(i.e.,
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\varphi^{\prime}+\alpha=0
on

X_{s}
), the difference
\varphi^{\prime}-\varphi
is constant on
X_{s}
, where
\varphi

is the preferred potential of
\alpha
. So
\int_{X_{s}}(\varphi^{\prime}-\varphi)\beta=\mathrm{constant}\cdot\int_{X_{s}}\beta=0
and
\int_{X_{s}}\varphi^{\prime}\beta=\int_{X_{s}}\varphi\beta=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle(s)
.

We now state some basic properties of the Archimedean height pairing defined above.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\alpha,\beta
be two forms
as in
Definition

4.1
. We have

(1)

The Archimedean height pairing
is symmetric and bilinear.

(2)

The Archimedean height pairing is
positive definite, i.e.,

\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle
is a non-negative
function on
{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}
, and

\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle=0
if and only if
\alpha\mid_{X_{s}}=0

for
s\neq 0
.

Proof.

(1)

Since the preferred potentials depend
linearly on the differential forms, we have
that the
Archimedean height pairing is bilinear. It is symmetric
because
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle(s)=\int_{X_{s}}\varphi(-\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\varphi^{\prime})=\int_{X_{s}}\varphi^{\prime}(-\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\varphi)=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle(s),

where
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}
are preferred potentials for
\alpha,\beta
respectively.

(2)

We have

\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle(s)=\int_{X_{s}}\varphi(-\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\varphi)=\left\lVert\mathrm{d}\varphi\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(X_{s})}\geq 0
.
If
\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle=0
, then
the preferred potential
\varphi
of
\alpha

is fiberwise constant, so
\alpha|_{X_{s}}=0
for
s\neq 0
.

∎

Theorem 4.4

(Continuity of pairing)
.

Let
\alpha,\beta
be two smooth real
(1,1)
-forms on
X
with
3.1
satisfying

\int_{C_{i}}\alpha=\int_{C_{i}}\beta=0,

where the
C_{i}
are the irreducible components
of the reduced singular fiber
X_{0}
(
3.2
).

Then the pairing function
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\in C^{0}({\mathbb{D}}^{\circ})

(in
Definition

4.1
)
can be continuously extended to
{\mathbb{D}}
.

Proof.

(of
Theorem

4.4
)
Let
\varphi,\rho\in C^{\infty}(X\setminus X_{0})
be preferred potentials of
\alpha
and
\beta
respectively.

By
Lemma

2.3
and shrinking
the base, we decompose
\varphi
into
its low frequency part
\varphi_{\rm low}\in C^{\infty}(X\setminus X_{0})

and high frequency part

\varphi_{\rm high}\in C^{\infty}(X\setminus X_{0})
. Then

\displaystyle\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle(s)

\displaystyle=-\int_{X_{s}}(\varphi_{\rm low}+\varphi_{\rm high})\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\rho

\displaystyle=-\int_{X_{s}}\rho\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\varphi_{\rm low}-\int_{X_{s}}\varphi_{\rm high}\beta

Using the asymptotic results from
Theorem

3.5
, we have

\rho=o(\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{-1}))
and

\varphi_{\rm low}=o(\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{-1}))
.
Since
\varphi_{\rm low}
is in the space spanned by
eigenfunctions with low frequency, we have

\left\lVert\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\varphi_{\rm low}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}=\left\lVert\Delta_{\omega_{s}}\varphi_{\rm low}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}=o(\log^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{-1})).

So
\int_{X_{s}}\rho\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\varphi_{\rm low}=o(1)
and it is
continuous at
0
.

To show that
\int_{X_{s}}\varphi_{\rm high}\beta
can be continuously extended to
{\mathbb{D}}

Corollary

1.14
. It suffices
to check that
\varphi_{\rm high,s}\in C^{\infty}(X_{s})

satisfies the conditions in
Proposition

1.13
. Note that

\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}_{z}\varphi_{\rm high,s}(z)=-\alpha-\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}_{z}\varphi_{\rm low,s}(z)

•

For the first condition, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}_{z}\varphi_{\rm high,s}(z)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\alpha\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}+\left\lVert\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}_{z}\varphi_{\rm low,s}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\alpha\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}+C\log^{-1}(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{-1})\left\lVert\varphi_{\rm low,s}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\alpha\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}+o(\log^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{-1}))

So
\left\lVert\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}_{z}\varphi_{\rm high,s}(z)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}
is uniformly bounded in
s
.

•

Since
\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\alpha
is continuous on
X\setminus\Sing X_{0}
and

\left\lVert\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}_{z}\varphi_{\rm high,s}(z)-\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\alpha\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}=\left\lVert\Delta_{\omega_{s}}\varphi_{\rm{low},s}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}=o(\log^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{-1})).

We have
\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}_{z}\varphi_{\rm high,s}\to\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{0}}\alpha
in
C^{0}_{\rm loc}
on

X_{0,\rm reg}
, which is the second
condition.

•

Since
\left\lVert\varphi_{\rm high}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}
(by
Theorem

3.4
) and
\left\lVert\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}_{z}\varphi_{\rm high,s}(z)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}
are uniformly bounded,

and
\left\lVert\mathrm{d}\varphi_{\rm high}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(X_{s})}
are also uniformly bounded. So the third condition
is verified.

So we can apply
Corollary

1.14

to
\varphi_{\rm high,s}
and
\int_{X_{s}}\varphi_{\rm high}\beta

is then continuous at
0
.

In summary,
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle(s)
can be continuously extended to
{\mathbb{D}}
.
∎

For general smooth classes
\alpha
and

Theorem 4.5

(Asymptotics of pairing)
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be two smooth real
(1,1)
-forms whose integrals on general fibers are
0
.
Then there is a constant
c_{\alpha,\beta}
such that

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle-c_{\alpha,\beta}\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}

extends continuously to
{\mathbb{D}}
.

The constant
c_{\alpha,\beta}
is determined in the following
way: Let
M
be the intersection matrix of irreducible components
of the singular fiber
X_{0}
, given by
M_{ij}=C_{i}\cdot C_{j}
. Let

M^{+}
be the
Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of
M
. Then
c_{\alpha,\beta}=\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}^{T}(M^{+})^{T}\mathbf{v}_{\beta}
, where

\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}=\left(\int_{C_{1}}\alpha,\dots,\int_{C_{N}}\alpha\right)^{T},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{v}_{\beta}=\left(\int_{C_{1}}\beta,\dots,\int_{C_{N}}\beta\right)^{T}.

In particular, if either
\alpha
or
\beta
satisfies the
integrability condition on the singular fiber
(
3.2
), then
c_{\alpha,\beta}=0
because either
\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}
or
\mathbf{v}_{\beta}
is zero.

Proof.

Since the singular fiber is reduced, we have

X_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}C_{i}
,
where
C_{i}
are the irreducible components.

We consider the
N\times N
intersection matrix

M
with entries
M_{ij}=C_{i}\cdot C_{j}
. By
Zariski’s Lemma (see
[
2
]
*III.8.2, p.111), we have

•

The matrix
M
is negative semi-definite.

•

The kernel of
M
is exactly one-dimensional and is spanned by

\mathbf{1}=(1,1,\cdots,1)
.

Let
V_{0}=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}=0\right\}
be the degree-zero subspace. Then
M|_{V_{0}}

is strictly negative definite and is invertible.
To compute the inverse of
M|_{V_{0}}
, we introduce
M^{+}
the
Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of
M
. Then
M^{+}
acts as the
inverse of
M
on
V_{0}
and annihilates
\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{1})
.

For each
C_{i}
, the irreducible component of

X_{0}
, we consider the line bundle

L_{i}=\mathcal{O}(C_{i})
equipped with a
Hermitian metric
h_{i}
. Then we have a closed smooth
(1,1)

\gamma_{i}=-\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\log\left\lVert e\right\rVert^{2}_{h_{i}}

representing the class
[C_{i}]
,
where
e
is a local frame of the holomorphic
line bundle
L_{i}
. Let
\sigma_{i}
be the canonical section of
L_{i}
which vanishes exactly on
C_{i}
. Then the preferred potential
for
\gamma_{i}
on each smooth fiber
X_{s}
is
\log\left\lVert\sigma_{i}\right\rVert^{2}_{h_{i}}
up to a constant.

We define the following map, which maps onto
differential
forms representing cohomology classes spanned by
\{[C_{i}]\}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Form}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{N}

\displaystyle\to\{\text{smooth closed }(1,1)\text{-forms with zero integral on the singular fiber}\}

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}\gamma_{i}\eqqcolon\mathrm{Form}(\mathbf{x})

Furthermore, the preferred potential of
\mathrm{Form}(\mathbf{x})
on
X_{s}
will be
\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}\log\left\lVert\sigma_{i}\right\rVert^{2}_{h_{i}}

s\neq 0
.

For smooth
(1,1)
-forms
\alpha,\beta
on
X
such that
\int_{X_{s}}\alpha=\int_{X_{s}}\beta=0
for

, the vectors of component integrals

\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}=\left(\int_{C_{1}}\alpha,\dots,\int_{C_{N}}\alpha\right)^{T},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{v}_{\beta}=\left(\int_{C_{1}}\beta,\dots,\int_{C_{N}}\beta\right)^{T}.

are in

. We can then decompose

into

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha})+\underbrace{(\alpha-\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}))}_{\eqqcolon\alpha_{0}}

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\beta})+\underbrace{(\beta-\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\beta}))}_{\eqqcolon\beta_{0}}

Note that for each
C_{i}

\displaystyle\int_{C_{i}}\alpha_{0}

\displaystyle=\text{i-th coordinate of }(\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}-MM^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=0\hskip 10.00002pt(\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}\in V_{0},\text{ so }MM^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}=\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}).

Similarly, we have
\int_{C_{i}}\beta_{0}=0
for each

C_{i}
.

Since the pairing
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle

is bilinear and symmetric, we have

\displaystyle\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\rangle+\langle\alpha_{0},\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\beta})\rangle+\langle\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}),\beta_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}),\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\beta})\rangle

By the previous theorem (
Theorem

4.4
),

\langle\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\rangle
can be extended
continuously to

S
.

Since the preferred potential for
\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha})
is

\sum_{i=1}^{N}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha})_{i}\log\left\lVert\sigma_{i}\right\rVert^{2}_{h_{i}}

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}),\beta_{0}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{X_{s}}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha})_{i}\log\left\lVert\sigma_{i}\right\rVert^{2}_{h_{i}}\beta_{0}

on

\langle\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}),\beta_{0}\rangle

to
S
.

Similarly,
\langle\alpha_{0},\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\beta})\rangle

extends Hölder continuously
to
S
.

For the last term, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}),\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\beta})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha})_{i}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\beta})_{j}\int_{X_{s}}\log\left\lVert\sigma_{i}\right\rVert^{2}_{h_{i}}\gamma_{j}.

,
\int_{X_{s}}\log\left\lVert\sigma_{i}\right\rVert^{2}_{h_{i}}\gamma_{j}-\int_{C_{i}}\gamma_{j}\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}

origin, so

\langle\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}),\mathrm{Form}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\beta})\rangle-\underbrace{\left(\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq N}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha})_{i}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\beta})_{j}\int_{C_{i}}\gamma_{j}\right)}_{\eqqcolon c_{\alpha,\beta}}\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}

extends Hölder continuously on

. Here

can be simplified as

\displaystyle c_{\alpha,\beta}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq N}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha})_{i}(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\beta})_{j}M_{ij}

\displaystyle=(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha})^{T}MM^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=(M^{+}\mathbf{v}_{\alpha})^{T}{v}_{\beta}

In summary,
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle-c_{\alpha,\beta}\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}

extends continuously to
{\mathbb{D}}
.
∎

We note that the height pairing
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle

is well-behaved under semi-stable reduction. So we can extend
the asymptotic result in

to the

.

Let

to be non-reduced and non-irreducible.
Let

be a semi-stable reduction of

with the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}}_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

.

be a projective

A
parabolic automorphism

which preserves the fibration
\pi
and is of infinite order.
This yields the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\coloneqq\mathbb{P}^{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

In some literature (e.g.

Compared to

]
, we allow possibly

in the setup of this section.

We use

5.A.
Preliminaries

Before delving deeper, we introduce basic notions and lemmas in
this part.

Let
[E]\in\NS(X)
be the class of a general fiber of
\pi

in the Néron–Severi group of
X
. We consider the group

\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
consisting of automorphisms of
X

preserving the elliptic fibration. The following
filtration of
\NS(X)
is preserved by
\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
:

0\subsetneq[E]\subsetneq[E]^{\perp}\subsetneq\NS(X),

where
[E]^{\perp}
is the orthogonal complement of
[E]

with respect to the intersection form in
\NS(X)
. By
the
Hodge index theorem, the intersection form on
the quotient lattice
L\coloneqq[E]^{\perp}/[E]
is strictly negative definite.

Let
\mathrm{Isom}(L)
be the
group of automorphisms of the lattice
L
that preserve
the induced intersection form. Since the induced intersection form
is strictly negative-definite,
\mathrm{Isom}(L)
is a
discrete subgroup of the compact orthogonal group
O(\mathrm{rank}\text{ }(L))
.
Thus,
\mathrm{Isom}(L)
is finite.

Since
\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
preserves the lattice
L
equipped
with the intersection form, we have the induced group homomorphism:

\rho\colon\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)\to\mathrm{Isom}(L).

We define
\ker(\rho)\eqqcolon\bm{\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ}_{\pi}(X)}
,
which is the subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
whose induced action
on
[E]^{\perp}/[E]
is trivial.
Because
\mathrm{Isom}(L)
is
finite,
\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ}_{\pi}(X)
is a finite index
subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
.

If we work inside
\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
, we have a
similar construction of
\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ}_{\pi}(X)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
[E]^{\perp}_{\mathrm{H}^{1,1}}
denote the orthogonal complement of
[E]

in
\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
. Then the following
filtration is preserved by
\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
:

0\subsetneq[E]_{{\mathbb{R}}}\subsetneq[E]^{\perp}_{\mathrm{H}^{1,1}}\subsetneq\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,{\mathbb{R}}).

We define
\bm{\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ,(1,1)}_{\pi}(X)}
as
the subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
that acts
trivially on the quotient space

[E]^{\perp}_{\mathrm{H}^{1,1}}/[E]_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. Then

\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ,(1,1)}_{\pi}(X)
is a finite
index subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
.

Proof.

Since the intersection form is non-degenerate, we have
the following orthogonal decomposition

\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X,{\mathbb{R}})=\NS(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus T^{1,1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}},

where
T^{1,1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
is the degree
(1,1)
-part of
the real
transcendental classes
T(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
, and

T(X)
is the transcendental lattice of
X

(see
[
18
]
*Chapter 3, Lemma 3.1).

Note that
[E]\in\NS(X)
, so it is perpendicular to
transcendental classes
T^{1,1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Thus,
[E]^{\perp}_{\mathrm{H}^{1,1}}
has a similar decomposition

[E]^{\perp}_{\mathrm{H}^{1,1}}=[E]^{\perp}_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus T^{1,1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}},

which descends to the quotient space as:

[E]^{\perp}_{\mathrm{H}^{1,1}}/[E]_{{\mathbb{R}}}=([E]^{\perp}/[E])_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus T^{1,1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}.

Since
\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
preserves the
transcendental lattice
T(X)
with its Hodge structure,
the image of
the induced group homomorphism:

\rho_{T}\colon\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)\to\mathrm{Isom}(T(X)_{\mathbb{R}})

is a finite group by

[
18
]
*Chapter 3, Corollary 3.4.
Thus
\ker(\rho_{T})
is a finite index subgroup of

\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
.

Since
T^{1,1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\subset T(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\ker(\rho_{T})\cap\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ}_{\pi}(X)\subset\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ,(1,1)}_{\pi}(X).

Since
\ker(\rho_{T})
and

\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ}_{\pi}(X)
are finite
index subgroups,
\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ,(1,1)}_{\pi}(X)

is also a finite index subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Lemma

5.1
also holds for general projective elliptic surfaces. To see this, it suffices to show that the image of the induced homomorphism
\rho_{T}\colon\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)\to\mathrm{Isom}(T(X)_{\mathbb{R}})
is a finite group. This follows from the fact that
\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
preserves the integral lattice
T(X)
along with its Hodge structure, and the intersection form on the
(1,1)
-part
T^{1,1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
is strictly negative definite. (See also the proof of
[
18
]
*Chapter 3, Corollary 3.4.)

Let
r
be the index of
\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ,(1,1)}_{\pi}(X)
inside

\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
. By elementary group theory, we have
T^{r!}\in\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ,(1,1)}_{\pi}(X)
for any
T\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
.

Lastly, we introduce a useful formula on cohomology classes,
which follows directly from the definition of
\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ,(1,1)}_{\pi}(X)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
v
be a real
(1,1)
-class in the cohomology group

\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X)
.
Suppose
v\cdot[E]=0
.
Then for any
T\in\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ,(1,1)}_{\pi}(X)
, we have

T_{*}v-v=c(v,T)[E],

where
c(v,T)\in{\mathbb{R}}
depends linearly on
v
.

5.B.
Current-valued pairing

We recall the construction of
current-valued pairing

from
[
13
]
*Theorem 3.2.14. We relate it to
the
Archimedean height pairing of smooth forms
in
Definition

4.1
.

Let
\alpha
be a closed smooth real
(1,1)
-form on
X
such that

\int_{X_{s}}\alpha=0
for
s\neq 0
. Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X^{\circ})
be the
preferred potential
of
\alpha
.

Cohomologically, we have
[\alpha]\cdot[E]=0
. Using

Lemma

5.3
, we obtain

T_{*}[\alpha]-[\alpha]=c[E],

for
T\in\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ,(1,1)}_{\pi}(X)
.

Note that
[E]
can be represented by the smooth form

\pi^{*}\beta
, where
\beta
is a suitable smooth form on the
base
B=\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We can take an
f\in C^{\infty}(X)
such that

(5.1)

T_{*}\alpha-\alpha=\pi^{*}\beta+\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}f.

Then
f+T_{*}\varphi-\varphi
is constant on each
X_{s}
for

s\in B^{\circ}
. Therefore, there is a function
u
on
B^{\circ}
such that

(5.2)

\pi^{*}u=f+T_{*}\varphi-\varphi.

Let
\omega
be a
Kähler
metric on
X
normalized
such that

[\omega]\cdot[E]=1
. Then

u(s)
can be determined by

(5.3)

\int_{X_{s}}(\pi^{*}u)\omega_{s}=u(s)=\int_{X_{s}}(f+T_{*}\varphi-\varphi)\omega_{s}

We call
u
the
limit potential
. We rewrite

Equation

5.3

using the
Archimedean height pairing

in
Definition

4.1
:

(5.4)

u(s)=\int_{X_{s}}(f+T_{*}\varphi-\varphi)\omega_{s}=\int_{X_{s}}f\omega_{s}+\langle\alpha,(T^{*}-I)\omega\rangle

The
current-valued pairing
is
defined as
\eta_{B}(T,[\alpha])\coloneqq\beta+\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}u
,
which is a current on
B
. It is smooth on
B^{\circ}
since

u\in C^{\infty}(B^{\circ})
.

We now generalize Filip–Tosatti’s current-valued pairing
by allowing arbitrary
singular fibers of the elliptic fibration
\pi\colon X\to B
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
T\in\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ,(1,1)}_{\pi}(X)
, where

\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ,(1,1)}_{\pi}(X)
is a finite index
subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{\pi}(X)
defined in

Lemma

5.1
.
Then the
current-valued pairing

\eta_{B}(T,[\alpha])
has a continuous
potential for any closed smooth real
(1,1)
-form

\alpha
on
X
such that
\int_{X_{s}}\alpha=0
for
s\neq 0
.

Proof.

We first show that
u\in L^{\infty}(B^{\circ})

following Tate’s argument in
[
26
]

(see also
[
13
]
*Remark 3.2.14).

From
Equation

5.2
, for any integer
i\geq 0
, we have

T^{i}_{*}\pi^{*}u=\pi^{*}u=T^{i}_{*}(f+T_{*}\varphi-\varphi)=T^{i}_{*}f+(T^{i+1}_{*}-T_{*})\varphi.

Then for
k\geq 1
the Birkhoff sum

S_{k}(T,f)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}T^{i}_{*}f
satisfies

S_{k}(T,f)=k\pi^{*}u+\varphi-T^{k}_{*}\varphi.

Thus, for
s\in B^{\circ}
and every
k\geq 1
, we have

\left\lvert u(s)\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{k}\sup_{X_{s}}\left\lvert S_{k}(T,f)\right\rvert+\frac{1}{k}\sup_{X_{s}}\left\lvert\varphi-T^{k}_{*}\varphi\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X)}+\frac{2}{k}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}.

Fixing
s\in B^{\circ}
and letting
k\to\infty
, we have

\left\lvert u(s)\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X)}
. Therefore,

u\in L^{\infty}(B^{\circ})
.

On the other hand, from the relation between the potential
u

and the
Archimedean height pairing
in

Equation

5.4
,
the Archimedean height pairing

\langle\alpha,(T^{*}-I)\omega\rangle(s)=u(s)-\int_{X_{s}}f\omega_{s}\in L^{\infty}(B^{\circ})

is bounded.

By
Corollary

4.6
,

\langle\alpha,(T^{*}-I)\omega\rangle
can be extended
continuously on
B
because it is bounded and has no
singularity. Using
Barlet’s result in
Theorem

1.3
,
the function
s\mapsto\int_{X_{s}}f\omega_{s}

is Hölder continuous on
B
.

In summary,
u(s)=\langle\alpha,(T^{*}-I)\omega\rangle(s)+\int_{X_{s}}f\omega_{s}
is continuous on
B
.
So
\eta_{B}(T,[\alpha])
has a continuous
potential.
∎

As a corollary, we have

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\omega
be a
Kähler
metric on
X
. For any

T\in\mathrm{Aut}^{\circ,(1,1)}_{\pi}(X)
, the limit current

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(T^{n})_{*}\omega}{n^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\pi^{*}\eta_{B}(T,[T_{*}\omega-\omega]).

has a
continuous potential.

We note that the convergence of currents
\frac{(T^{n})_{*}\omega}{n^{2}}\to\frac{1}{2}\pi^{*}\eta_{B}(T,[T_{*}\omega-\omega])
cannot be in
C^{0}_{\rm loc}(X\setminus X_{0})
.

In particular, let
\omega
be a Ricci flat
Kähler
metric on the K3 surface

, i.e.,

\omega^{2}=\mathrm{vol}(\omega)\Omega\wedge\overline{\Omega},

where
\Omega
is the normalized holomorphic
(2,0)
-form on

X
. Then
T^{n}_{*}\omega
is also Ricci flat.
Therefore,

\{[\frac{T^{n}_{*}\omega}{n^{2}}]\}

provides a counterexample to
Tosatti’s question
[
28
]
*Question 1.5(b).

Proof.

By
[
13
]
*Corollary 3.2.20, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(T^{n})_{*}\omega}{n^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\pi^{*}\eta_{B}(T,[T_{*}\omega-\omega]).

So
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(T^{n})_{*}\omega}{n^{2}}

has continuous potential by

Proposition

5.4
.

We now prove that the convergence of currents

\frac{(T^{n})_{*}\omega}{n^{2}}\to\frac{1}{2}\pi^{*}\eta_{B}(T,[T_{*}\omega-\omega])
is not a
C^{0}_{\rm loc}(X\setminus X_{0})

convergence. We proceed by contradiction, assuming that

\frac{(T^{n})_{*}\omega}{n^{2}}\to\frac{1}{2}\pi^{*}\eta_{B}(T,[T_{*}\omega-\omega])
in

.

We let
\xi=T_{*}\omega-\omega
. Then by elementary
computation (see also
[
13
]
*Proposition 3.2.15),
we have

T_{*}^{n}\omega=\omega+n\xi+\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}(n\varphi-S_{n}(T,\varphi))+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\pi^{*}\eta_{B}(T,[\xi]),

where
\varphi
is the
preferred potential
of

\xi
and
S_{n}(T,\varphi)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{*}^{k}\varphi
is a Birkhoff sum.

Note that
\frac{1}{n^{2}}(\omega+n\xi+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\pi^{*}\eta_{B}(T,[\xi]))
converges to
\frac{1}{2}\pi^{*}\eta_{B}(T,[\xi])
in
C^{0}_{\rm loc}(X\setminus X_{0})
.
Under our assumption for contradiction, we have

(5.5)

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}(n\varphi-S_{n}(T,\varphi))\to_{n\to\infty}0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }C^{0}_{\rm loc}(X\setminus X_{0}).

It suffices to show that
Equation

5.5

is impossible.
We interpret
\varphi
, the
preferred potential
of
\xi=T_{*}\omega-\omega
, as follows: Since
\pi
is an elliptic fibration, each smooth fiber
X_{s}
is isomorphic to a torus
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda_{s}
, and
T
acts on
X_{s}
by translations.
Let
\omega_{\rm{flat},s}
be the
normalized flat metric on
X_{s}
. There exists
\rho_{s}\in C^{\infty}(X_{s})

such that

\omega_{\rm{flat},s}-\omega_{s}=\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\rho_{s}.

Applying
T_{*}
to this equation, we obtain

\omega_{\rm{flat},s}-T_{*}\omega_{s}=\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}T_{*}\rho_{s}
,
since
\omega_{\rm{flat},s}
is invariant
under translation
T
. Then we have

\xi\mid_{X_{s}}=T_{*}\omega_{s}-\omega_{s}=-\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}(T_{*}\rho_{s}-\rho_{s}).

Thus, the
preferred potential

\varphi
of

\xi
is equal to
T_{*}\rho_{s}-\rho_{s}
up to a constant on
X_{s}
.
By Ehresmann’s theorem and solving equations on the region where the fibration is locally trivial, there exists
\rho\in C^{\infty}(X\setminus X_{0})
such that
\rho\mid_{X_{s}}=\rho_{s}
on the smooth fibers
X_{s}
.
Furthermore,
\varphi=T_{*}\rho-\rho+c(s)
,
where
c(s)

is a smooth function on
B^{\circ}
.

Substituting
\varphi
by
T_{*}\rho-\rho+c(s)

in

5.5
, we have

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}(n(T_{*}\rho-\rho)-(T^{n}_{*}\rho-\rho))\to_{n\to\infty}0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }C^{0}_{\rm loc}(X\setminus X_{0}).

By smoothness of
\rho
on
X\setminus X_{0}
, the above convergence
is equivalent to

(5.6)

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}(T^{n}_{*}\rho)\to_{n\to\infty}0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }C^{0}_{\rm loc}(X\setminus X_{0}).

We will show that

5.6

is impossible.

On a small open set
U\subset B^{\circ}

not containing any singular points, we can trivialize
the fibration
\pi
as
U\times\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\to U
. Moreover,
we can lift the

to its universal covering

, and the fiber-preserving automorphism
T

can also be lifted, yielding the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{s}\times\mathbb{C}_{z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{-1}(U_{s})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces pr_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{T}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}

We can then express the action of
\widetilde{T}
on the covering space
U_{s}\times\mathbb{C}_{z}
as
\widetilde{T}(s,z)=(s,z+T(s))
, where

is a holomorphic function.
Following the argument in
[

*Proposition 2.2 (see also
[

]
*Proposition 6.2.7,

]
*Lemma 3.7),
the function

is non-constant.

Lifting the convergence in

to the covering space, we find it is equivalent to

(5.7)

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}(\widetilde{T}^{n}_{*}\widetilde{\rho})\to_{n\to\infty}0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }C^{0}_{\rm loc}(U_{s}\times\mathbb{C}_{z}),

for all small open neighborhoods of points in
B^{\circ}
and its
corresponding covering, where
\widetilde{\rho}

is the lift of
\rho
to the covering.

Note that
\widetilde{T}^{n}_{*}\widetilde{\rho}=\widetilde{\rho}(s,z-nT(s))
. By the holomorphic chain rule,
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\overline{\partial}_{s}(\widetilde{T}^{n}_{*}\widetilde{\rho})(s,z)

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}\overline{\partial}_{s}(\widetilde{\rho}(s,z-nT(s)))

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}((\overline{\partial}_{s}\widetilde{\rho})(s,z-nT(s))-n\overline{\partial_{s}T}(\overline{\partial}_{z}\widetilde{\rho})(s,z-nT(s)))\hskip 10.00002pt(\overline{\partial}_{s}T(s)=0)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{s}\overline{\partial}_{s}\widetilde{\rho})(s,z-nT(s))-n(\partial_{s}T(s))(\partial_{z}\overline{\partial}_{s}\widetilde{\rho})(s,z-nT(s))

\displaystyle-n\overline{\partial_{s}T}(\partial_{s}\overline{\partial}_{z}\widetilde{\rho})(s,z-nT(s))+{n^{2}\left\lvert\partial_{s}T\right\rvert^{2}(\partial_{z}\overline{\partial}_{z}\widetilde{\rho})(s,z-nT(s))}

Then
Equation

5.7
implies that

\left\lvert\partial_{s}T\right\rvert^{2}(\partial_{z}\overline{\partial}_{z}\widetilde{\rho})(s,z-nT(s))\to_{n\to\infty}0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }C^{0}_{\rm loc}(U_{s}\times\mathbb{C}_{z})

for all small open sets in
B^{\circ}
.

Since
T

is non-constant and holomorphic, we have
\left\lvert\partial_{s}T\right\rvert^{2}>0

except for at most finitely many
s\in U_{s}
.
Therefore, there exists a finite set
\Sigma\subset U_{s}
such that

(\partial_{z}\overline{\partial}_{z}\widetilde{\rho})(s,z-nT(s))\to_{n\to\infty}0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }C^{0}_{\rm loc}((U_{s}\setminus\Sigma)\times\mathbb{C}_{z}).

Since
\widetilde{\rho}
is periodic in
z
, it implies that

\partial_{z}\overline{\partial}_{z}\widetilde{\rho}(s,z)=0
for
s\in U_{s}\setminus\Sigma
. By smoothness of
\widetilde{\rho}
, we have
\partial_{z}\overline{\partial}_{z}\widetilde{\rho}(s,z)\equiv 0
for
s\in S^{\circ}
. Consequently,

\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\rho_{s}=0
on
X_{s}
for
s\in S^{\circ}
.

By definition of
\rho
, we have

\omega_{\rm{flat},s}-\omega_{s}=\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\rho_{s}=0.

Thus,
\omega_{\rm{flat},s}=\omega_{s}
for
s\in S^{\circ}
.

To get a contradiction, we compute the Gauss curvature of
\omega_{\rm{flat},s}
and
\omega
on smooth fibers
X_{s}
, and study them when
X_{s}
is approaching a singular fiber.

By definition, the Gauss curvature

K_{\omega_{\rm{flat},s}}\equiv 0
. On the
other hand, by
[
8
]
*Lemma 10.1,
the minimum of
K_{\omega_{s}}
goes to
-\infty
as

s\to 0
.
This leads to a contradiction, concluding the proof that the convergence is not in
C^{0}_{\rm loc}(X\setminus X_{0})
.

∎

Appendix A
Asymptotics of a fiber integral

We prove a lemma on the asymptotics of the fiber integral of the
log-norm of the canonical section of a divisor. This result also
appears in other works (e.g.,
[
30
]
*Section 4);
however, we provide a proof here in our setting for convenience and
completeness.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to{\mathbb{D}}
be a degeneration of algebraic curves.
Assume
X
is smooth and we allow a general singular fiber
X_{0}

here. Let
C
be one of the irreducible
components of

X_{0}
and
L=\mathcal{O}(C)
the corresponding line bundle with
Hermitian metric
h
.

Let
\sigma
be the canonical section of
L
which vanishes
exactly on
C
. Then, for any smooth
(1,1)
-form
\alpha

on
X
, there exists a constant
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}
such that

\int_{X_{s}}\log\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert^{2}_{h}\alpha-\left(\int_{C}\alpha\right)\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}

extends Hölder continuously through the origin.

Proof.

We consider a semi-stable reduction for the family

\pi\colon(X,X_{0})\to({\mathbb{D}},\{0\})
given by the
following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times_{{\mathbb{D}}}{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}}_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces pr_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces pr_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{d}}

Here,
f_{d}(t)=t^{d}=s
is a base change of degree
d
, and
\mu
is a resolution of singularities such that
Y
is a smooth complex surface. The singular fiber
Y_{0}=p^{-1}(X_{0})
is a reduced divisor with simple normal crossings. Let
\widetilde{\mu}=pr_{1}\circ\mu
.

Let
m
be the multiplicity of
C
in
X_{0}
. Since
\widetilde{\mu}^{*}X_{0}=dY_{0}
and
Y_{0}
is reduced, the strict transform
\widetilde{C}
of
C
appears in
Y_{0}
with multiplicity
1
. Thus, the pullback of
C
as a Cartier divisor is

\widetilde{\mu}^{*}C=\frac{d}{m}\widetilde{C}+\sum_{k}\nu_{k}E_{k}

where
E_{k}
are exceptional divisors.

This induces a decomposition of the log-norm on
Y
:

(A.1)

\log\left\lVert\widetilde{\mu}^{*}\sigma\right\rVert^{2}_{\widetilde{\mu}^{*}h}=\frac{d}{m}\log\left\lVert\sigma_{\widetilde{C}}\right\rVert^{2}_{h_{\widetilde{C}}}+\sum_{k}\nu_{k}\log\left\lVert\sigma_{E_{k}}\right\rVert^{2}_{h_{E_{k}}}+u

where
\sigma_{D}
denotes the canonical section of a component
D\subset Y_{0}
, and
u
is a smooth function on
Y
.

For
t\neq 0
, the map
\widetilde{\mu}_{t}\colon Y_{t}\xrightarrow{\sim}X_{s}
is an isomorphism. Pulling the integral back to the semi-stable model
Y_{t}
, we obtain:

\int_{X_{s}}\log\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert^{2}_{h}\alpha=\int_{Y_{t}}\log\left\lVert\widetilde{\mu}^{*}\sigma\right\rVert^{2}_{\widetilde{\mu}^{*}h}\widetilde{\mu}^{*}\alpha

Substituting our decomposition
Equation

A.1
, this splits into:

(A.2)

\frac{d}{m}\int_{Y_{t}}\log\left\lVert\sigma_{\widetilde{C}}\right\rVert^{2}_{h_{\widetilde{C}}}\widetilde{\mu}^{*}\alpha+\sum_{k}\nu_{k}\int_{Y_{t}}\log\left\lVert\sigma_{E_{k}}\right\rVert^{2}_{h_{E_{k}}}\widetilde{\mu}^{*}\alpha+\int_{Y_{t}}u\widetilde{\mu}^{*}\alpha=\int_{X_{s}}\log\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert^{2}_{h}\alpha

Since
Y_{0}
has strictly simple normal crossings, we apply

Lemma

A.2
to the
first two terms of
Equation

A.2
, which yield:

\displaystyle\int_{Y_{t}}\log\left\lVert\sigma_{\widetilde{C}}\right\rVert^{2}_{h_{\widetilde{C}}}\widetilde{\mu}^{*}\alpha

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\widetilde{C}}\widetilde{\mu}^{*}\alpha\right)\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}+R_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=m\left(\int_{{C}}\alpha\right)\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}+R_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\int_{Y_{t}}\log\left\lVert\sigma_{E_{k}}\right\rVert^{2}_{h_{E_{k}}}\widetilde{\mu}^{*}\alpha

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{E_{k}}\widetilde{\mu}^{*}\alpha\right)\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}+R_{2,k}(t),

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\widetilde{\mu}_{*}[E_{k}]}\alpha\right)\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}+R_{2,k}(t)

\displaystyle=R_{2,k}(t),

where
R_{1}(t),R_{2,k}(t)
are Hölder continuous around
0
.

Since
u
is smooth, by Barlet’s result (
Theorem

1.3
), the third term
(of
Equation

A.2
)
\int_{Y_{t}}u\widetilde{\mu}^{*}\alpha\eqqcolon R_{3}(t)
, is Hölder continuous around
0
. Substituting the previous computations into
Equation

A.2
, we have

\int_{X_{s}}\log\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert^{2}_{h}\alpha=d\left(\int_{{C}}\alpha\right)\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}+\underbrace{\frac{d}{m}R_{1}(t)+\sum_{k}R_{2,k}(t)+R_{3}(t)}_{\text{Hölder continuous}},

Finally, we translate back to the original base parameter
s
. Since
s=t^{d}
, we have
\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}=\frac{1}{d}\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}
. Therefore,

\int_{X_{s}}\log\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert^{2}_{h}\alpha=\left(\int_{C}\alpha\right)\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}+R(s),

and
R(s)
is Hölder continuous.

We conclude that:

\int_{X_{s}}\log\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert^{2}_{h}\alpha-\left(\int_{C}\alpha\right)\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}

extends Hölder continuously through the origin.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{Y}\to{\mathbb{D}}
be a proper
holomorphic map from a smooth complex surface to
the unit disk. Assume the singular fiber

Y_{0}=\pi^{-1}(0)
is a reduced divisor with
strictly simple normal crossings. Let
D
be an
irreducible component of
Y_{0}
,
\sigma_{D}
be its
canonical section, and
h
be a smooth Hermitian
metric on
\mathcal{O}(D)
. For any smooth

(1,1)
-form
\omega
on
\mathcal{Y}
,
the function

t\mapsto\int_{Y_{t}}\log\left\lVert\sigma_{D}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\,\omega-\left(\int_{D}\omega\right)\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}

extends Hölder continuously to
t=0
.

Proof.

Fix a finite open cover
\{U_{j}\}
of
Y_{0}
in

\mathcal{Y}
by coordinate polydisks

U_{j}\cong{\mathbb{D}}^{2}
such that on each
U_{j}
,

\pi
is given either by
t=z_{1}

(smooth points of
Y_{0}
) or
t=z_{1}z_{2}
(nodes of
Y_{0}
).
Let
\{\rho_{j}\}
be a smooth partition of unity subordinate to
\{U_{j}\}
.

On each
U_{j}
, we decompose the metric as

\log\left\lVert\sigma_{D}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}=\log\left\lvert f_{D}\right\rvert^{2}+u_{j}
,
where
f_{D}\in\mathcal{O}(U_{j})
defines
D\cap U_{j}
and
u_{j}
is a smooth function.
By Barlet’s theorem on fiber integral (
Theorem

1.3
),

t\mapsto\int_{Y_{t}\cap U_{j}}\rho_{j}u_{j}\omega

extends Hölder continuously to
t=0
. We analyze
the singular integral
I_{j}(t)=\int_{Y_{t}\cap U_{j}}\rho_{j}\log\left\lvert f_{D}\right\rvert^{2}\omega
case by case:

Case 1:
U_{j}\cap D=\emptyset
.

Here,
f_{D}
is a non-vanishing holomorphic
function on
U_{j}
. Thus
\log\left\lvert f_{D}\right\rvert^{2}
is
smooth, and
I_{j}(t)
is smooth around

t=0
. In this case,

I_{j}(t)=\left(\int_{D\cap U_{j}}\rho_{j}\omega\right)\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}+I_{j}(t).

Case 2:
U_{j}
intersects
D
outside the nodes.

Here,
\pi=z_{1}
and
f_{D}=z_{1}
.
Thus
I_{j}(t)=\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}\int_{Y_{t}\cap U_{j}}\rho_{j}\omega
.
Since
\pi
on
U_{j}

is a submersion, by Ehresmann’s theorem and
Taylor expansion, we have

\int_{Y_{t}\cap U_{j}}\rho_{j}\omega=\int_{\{z_{1}=t\}\cap U_{j}}\rho_{j}\omega=\int_{\{z_{1}=0\}\cap U_{j}}\rho_{j}\omega+O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert)=\int_{D\cap U_{j}}\rho_{j}\omega+O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert)
.
Multiplying by
\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}
, we obtain

I_{j}(t)=\left(\int_{D\cap U_{j}}\rho_{j}\omega\right)\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}+R_{j}(t),

where
R_{j}(t)=O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert)
is Hölder
continuous around
t=0
.

Case 3:
U_{j}
contains a node
D\cap D^{\prime}
.

Here, the map is given by
\pi=z_{1}z_{2}
, with
D
defined by
z_{1}=0
and
D^{\prime}
defined by
z_{2}=0
. Thus
f_{D}=z_{1}
. The fiber
Y_{t}\cap U_{j}
is the restricted annulus
\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in U_{j}\mid z_{1}z_{2}=t\}
.

Let
\eta=\rho_{j}\omega
. Since
\eta
is a smooth form with compact support in
U_{j}\cong{\mathbb{D}}^{2}
, we can write it globally on the chart as
\eta=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{2}\eta_{k\overline{l}}dz_{k}\wedge d\overline{z}_{l}
. We want to evaluate the asymptotic behavior of

I_{j}(t)=\int_{Y_{t}\cap U_{j}}\log\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert^{2}\,\eta

We partition the domain of integration on the annulus
Y_{t}\cap U_{j}
into two regions:
V_{1}(t)=\{\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{1/2}\leq\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert<1\}
and
V_{2}(t)=\{\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{1/2}<\left\lvert z_{2}\right\rvert<1\}
.

On
V_{1}(t)
, we use
z_{1}
as the coordinate. Substituting
z_{2}=t/z_{1}
and
dz_{2}=-(t/z_{1}^{2})dz_{1}
, the restriction of
\eta
is:

\eta|_{Y_{t}}=\left(\eta_{1\overline{1}}-\eta_{1\overline{2}}\frac{\overline{t}}{\overline{z}_{1}^{2}}-\eta_{2\overline{1}}\frac{t}{z_{1}^{2}}+\eta_{2\overline{2}}\frac{\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert^{4}}\right)\frac{i}{2}dz_{1}\wedge d\overline{z}_{1}

where the smooth coefficients
\eta_{k\overline{l}}
are evaluated at
(z_{1},t/z_{1})
. By the smoothness of
\eta
, we can Taylor expand the leading coefficient as
\eta_{1\overline{1}}(z_{1},t/z_{1})=\eta_{1\overline{1}}(z_{1},0)+O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert/\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert)
.

Integrating the cross-term and higher-order errors against
\log\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert^{2}
over
V_{1}(t)
yields bounded remainders. For example, passing to polar coordinates
r=\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert
:

\int_{\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{1/2}}^{1}\left\lvert\log r\right\rvert\cdot O\left(\frac{\left\lvert t\right\rvert}{r^{2}}\right)r\,dr=O\left(\left\lvert t\right\rvert\int_{\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{1/2}}^{1}\frac{\left\lvert\log r\right\rvert}{r}dr\right)=O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert\log^{2}\left\lvert t\right\rvert)

Similar bounds hold for the
O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}/r^{4})
and
O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert/r)
terms. Thus, the integral over
V_{1}(t)
is tightly dominated by the leading term:

\int_{V_{1}(t)}\log\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert^{2}\eta|_{Y_{t}}=\int_{\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{1/2}\leq\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert<1}\log\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert^{2}\eta_{1\overline{1}}(z_{1},0)\frac{i}{2}dz_{1}\wedge d\overline{z}_{1}+O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert\log^{2}\left\lvert t\right\rvert)

Extending the domain of this integral to the full local component
D^{\prime}\cap U_{j}
(where
z_{2}=0
) introduces an error bounded by the integral over the missing inner disk
\{\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert<\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{1/2}\}
. This hole has a volume integral of
\int_{0}^{\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{1/2}}\left\lvert\log r\right\rvert r\,dr=O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert)
. Hence:

\displaystyle I_{j,1}(t)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{D^{\prime}\cap U_{j}}\log\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert^{2}\eta|_{D^{\prime}}}_{\mathrm{constant}}+O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert\log^{2}\left\lvert t\right\rvert).

On
V_{2}(t)
, we switch to the coordinate
z_{2}
. Here, the log-norm splits exactly as
\log\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert^{2}=\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}-\log\left\lvert z_{2}\right\rvert^{2}
.

Similarly, the restriction of
\eta
in the
z_{2}
coordinate is:

\eta|_{Y_{t}}=\left(\eta_{2\overline{2}}-\eta_{1\overline{2}}\frac{t}{z_{2}^{2}}-\eta_{2\overline{1}}\frac{\overline{t}}{\overline{z}_{2}^{2}}+\eta_{1\overline{1}}\frac{\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert z_{2}\right\rvert^{4}}\right)\frac{i}{2}dz_{2}\wedge d\overline{z}_{2}

with coefficients evaluated at
(t/z_{2},z_{2})
.

The term involving
-\log\left\lvert z_{2}\right\rvert^{2}
behaves exactly like the
V_{1}(t)
integral, yielding a constant relative to
t
minus a remainder:

-\int_{V_{2}(t)}\log\left\lvert z_{2}\right\rvert^{2}\eta|_{Y_{t}}=\underbrace{-\int_{D\cap U_{j}}\log\left\lvert z_{2}\right\rvert^{2}\eta|_{D}}_{\mathrm{constant}}+O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert\log^{2}\left\lvert t\right\rvert)

For the
\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}
term, we must integrate the volume-like form
\eta|_{Y_{t}}
over
V_{2}(t)
. Applying the exact same Taylor expansion and error bounds, the dominant term is
\eta_{2\overline{2}}(0,z_{2})
. We have:

\int_{V_{2}(t)}\eta|_{Y_{t}}=\int_{\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{1/2}\leq\left\lvert z_{2}\right\rvert<1}\eta_{2\overline{2}}(0,z_{2})\frac{i}{2}dz_{2}\wedge d\overline{z}_{2}+\int_{\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{1/2}}^{1}O\left(\frac{\left\lvert t\right\rvert}{r^{2}}\right)r\,dr

The error integral evaluates to
O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert\left\lvert\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert\right\rvert)
. The difference between the main integral and the full integral over
D\cap U_{j}
is the missing disk
\{\left\lvert z_{2}\right\rvert<\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{1/2}\}
, which adds a simple
O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert)
error. Thus:

\int_{V_{2}(t)}\eta|_{Y_{t}}=\int_{D\cap U_{j}}\eta|_{D}+O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert)

Multiplying this volume result by
\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}
gives
\left(\int_{D\cap U_{j}}\rho_{j}\omega\right)\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}+O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert\log^{2}\left\lvert t\right\rvert)
.

Combining both regions for the entire chart
U_{j}
, we have

I_{j}(t)=\left(\int_{D\cap U_{j}}\rho_{j}\omega\right)\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}+\widetilde{R}_{j}(t),

where
\widetilde{R}_{j}(t)=\mathrm{constant}+O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert\log^{2}\left\lvert t\right\rvert)
is Hölder continuous for any exponent
0<\alpha<1
around
t=0
.
