α \alpha -Mutual Information for the Gaussian Noise Channel The research at the University of Minnesota was supported in part by the NSF under grant no. CCF-2045237 .
Mohammad Milanian 1 , Alex Dytso 2 , Martina Cardone 1

Abstract
In this paper, we study Sibson’s α \alpha -mutual information, which we refer to as α \alpha -mutual information, in the context of the additive Gaussian noise channel. While the classical case α = 1 \alpha=1 is well understood and admits deep connections to estimation-theoretic quantities, such as the minimum mean-square error (MMSE) and Fisher information, many of the corresponding structural properties for general values of α \alpha remain less explored. Our goal is to develop a systematic understanding of α \alpha -mutual information in the Gaussian noise setting and to identify which properties extend beyond the Shannon case. To this end, we establish several regularity properties, including finiteness conditions, continuity with respect to the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and the input distribution, and strict concavity/convexity properties that guarantee uniqueness in associated optimization problems. A central contribution of this work is the development of an α \alpha -I-MMSE relationship, which generalizes the classical identity by relating the derivative of α \alpha -mutual information with respect to SNR to the MMSE evaluated under appropriately tilted distributions. This connection further leads to a generalized de Bruijn’s identity and new estimation-theoretic representations of Rényi entropy and differential Rényi entropy. We also characterize the low- and high-SNR behaviors of α \alpha -mutual information. In particular, we show that in the low-SNR regime the first-order behavior depends only on the variance of the input, mirroring the classical case. In the high-SNR regime, for discrete distributions, we show that α \alpha -mutual information converges to the Rényi entropy of order 1 / α 1/\alpha of the input. Furthermore, for general distributions, we establish connections between the high-SNR behavior of α \alpha -mutual information and α \alpha -information dimension. Overall, our results demonstrate that many of the fundamental relationships between information measures and estimation-theoretic quantities extend beyond the Shannon setting, albeit in a modified form involving α \alpha -tilted distributions.

Rényi Measures

Definition 1

(Rényi entropy of order
\alpha
)
.

Let

{\rm mmse}(\mathsf{U}|\mathsf{W})=\inf_{f}\mathbb{E}\left[(\mathsf{U}-f(\mathsf{W}))^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[(\mathsf{U}-\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{U}|\mathsf{W}])^{2}\right].

(47)

In this section, following the notation in
[
29
]
, we will study the following quantity:

{\rm mmse}_{\alpha}(\mathsf{X};{\rm{snr}})={\rm mmse}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}|\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}),

(48)

where, as before,
(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha},\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha})
is distributed according to (
19
). Note that (
48
) is well defined only if
f_{\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}}
exists (see Theorem
1
). Depending on the context, we will use both
{\rm mmse}_{\alpha}(\mathsf{X};{\rm{snr}})
and
{\rm mmse}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}|\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha})
.

IV-A

Generalization of Brown’s identity

We show a generalization of the Brown’s identity
[
29
, eq.(58)]
. Toward this end, we will need the notions of score function and Fisher information, which are next formally defined.

Definition 6

(Score function and Fisher Information)
.

For a distribution
P_{\mathsf{Y}}
supported on
\mathcal{Y}
with a PDF
f_{\mathsf{Y}}(\cdot)
, the score function
\rho_{\mathsf{Y}}:\mathcal{Y}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\rho_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\,y}\log f_{\mathsf{Y}}(y).

(49)

and the Fisher information is defined as

J(\mathsf{Y})=\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{\mathsf{Y}}^{2}(\mathsf{Y})\right].

(50)

We now show that the score function for
f_{\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}}
can be related to the score function for
f_{\mathsf{Y}}
but at a different
{\rm snr}

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
f_{\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}}
exists. Then, the score function for
f_{\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}}(\cdot)
in (
17

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}}(y)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\rho_{\mathsf{Y};\alpha\,{\rm snr}}(y),\,y\in\mathbb{R},

(51)

where

f_{\mathsf{Y}}(\cdot;\alpha\;{\rm snr})
in (
15
).
Moreover, under the assumption
\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{X}^{2}]<\infty

To see the expression for the score function, note that in view of (

\rho_{\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}}(y)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\log f_{\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}}(y)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\log f_{\mathsf{Y}}(y;\alpha\;{\rm snr})=\frac{1}{\alpha}\rho_{\mathsf{Y};\alpha\,{\rm snr}}(y).

(53)

For the proof of the Fisher information, we will leverage the fact that

{\rm{Var}}(\mathsf{X}|\mathsf{Y}=y;{\rm snr})=\frac{1}{\rm snr}+\frac{1}{\rm snr^{2}}\rho_{\mathsf{Y};{\rm snr}}^{\prime}(y),

\displaystyle J(\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}}^{2}(y)\;f_{\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}}(y)\;\mathrm{d}y

IV-C

On Generalized de Bruijn’s Identity

The classical I-MMSE relationship is known to be equivalent to the de Bruijn’s identity
[
61
]
, which relates the derivative of the Shannon differential entropy to the Fisher information. This connection is typically established via Brown’s identity.

Equipped with our generalization of the I-MMSE relationship in (
67
), the representation in (
24
), and the generalized Brown identity in (
52
), we obtain the following generalization of the de Bruijn’s identity:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\,{\rm snr}}h_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\mathsf{X}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\,{\rm snr}}}\mathsf{Z}\right)=-\frac{J(\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha})}{2\,{{\rm snr^{2}}}}.

(70)

We note that generalized de Bruijn’s identities have been studied previously. In particular, the authors in
[
69
]
introduced a generalization of the Fisher information, termed the Rényi -Fisher information, defined as

J_{\alpha}(\mathsf{X})=\alpha\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f^{\prime}_{\mathsf{X}}(x)|^{2}f_{\mathsf{X}}^{\alpha-2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int f_{\mathsf{X}}^{\alpha}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x},

(71)

and established the following generalized de Bruijn’s identity:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\,{\rm snr}}h_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\mathsf{X}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\,{\rm snr}}}\mathsf{Z}\right)=-\frac{1}{2\,\alpha\,{\rm snr}^{2}}J_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\mathsf{X}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\,{\rm snr}}}\mathsf{Z}\right).

(72)

The equivalence between the de Bruijn’s identity in (
70
) and the formulation involving the Rényi -Fisher information in (
72
) follows directly from the score-function identity in (
51
). In particular, this identity shows that the classical Fisher information of
\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}
can be related to the Rényi -Fisher information of

\mathsf{Y}=\mathsf{X}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\,{\rm snr}}}\mathsf{Z}

as

\alpha\,J(\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha})=J_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\mathsf{X}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\,{\rm snr}}}\mathsf{Z}\right).

(73)

IV-D

Continuity of the MMSE at
{\rm snr}=0^{+}

Continuity of the MMSE at
\mathrm{snr}=0^{+}
is a subtle but essential property.
Notably, even in the classical I–MMSE framework for
\alpha=1
, the behavior at

\mathrm{snr}=0^{+}
is not immediate and requires a careful analysis, which was
subsequently established in
[
70
]
. We now show continuity of
{\rm mmse}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}|\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha})
at
{\rm snr}=0^{+}
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\alpha>0
and assume that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{X}^{2}]<\infty,&\alpha\in(0,1),\\
\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{X}^{4\alpha}]<\infty,&\alpha\in(1,\infty).\\
\end{array}\right.

(74)

Then, it holds that

\lim_{{\rm{snr}}\to 0}{\rm mmse}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}|\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha})={\rm{Var}}(\mathsf{X}).

(75)

Proof:

We start by noting that, under the assumption in (
74
), Proposition
1
ensures that
f_{\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}}
always exists.
We now prove that, for each fixed
y\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{{\rm{snr}}\to 0}{\rm{Var}}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}|\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}=y)={\rm{Var}}(\mathsf{X}).

(76)

From (
18
), we have that
\mathrm{d}P_{\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}|\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}=y}(x)=\mathrm{d}P_{\mathsf{X}|\mathsf{Y}=y;\alpha\,{\rm snr}}(x)
, which implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha})|\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}=y]=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\xi(\mathsf{X})\;\phi(\sqrt{\alpha\;\rm{snr}}(y-\mathsf{X}))\right]}{\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(\sqrt{\alpha\;\rm{snr}}(y-\mathsf{X}))\right]},

(77)

and hence,

\displaystyle{\rm{Var}}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}|\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha}=y)=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{X}^{2}\;\phi(\sqrt{\alpha\;\rm{snr}}(y-\mathsf{X}))\right]}{\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(\sqrt{\alpha\;\rm{snr}}(y-\mathsf{X}))\right]}-\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{X}\;\phi(\sqrt{\alpha\;\rm{snr}}(y-\mathsf{X}))\right]}{\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(\sqrt{\alpha\;\rm{snr}}(y-\mathsf{X}))\right]}\right)^{2}.

(78)

Now, note that

\displaystyle\lim_{{\rm{snr}}\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(\sqrt{\alpha\;\rm{snr}}(y-\mathsf{X}))\right]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}},

(79)

and, for
\xi(\mathsf{X})\in\{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{X}^{2}\}
, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{{\rm{snr}}\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\xi(\mathsf{X})\;\phi(\sqrt{\alpha\;\rm{snr}}(y-\mathsf{X}))\right]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathbb{E}\left[\xi(\mathsf{X})\right],

(80)

where we have used the dominated convergence theorem which can be applied since

\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{X}^{2}]<\infty
by assumption (and hence, also
\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{X}]<\infty
).
Thus, we arrive at (
76
).
Now, from the definition of MMSE, we have that

\displaystyle\liminf_{{\rm{snr}}\to 0}{\rm mmse}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}|\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{{\rm{snr}}\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm{Var}}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}|\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha})\right]

(81)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\liminf_{{\rm{snr}}\to 0}{\rm{Var}}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}|\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha})\right]

(82)

\displaystyle={\rm{Var}}(\mathsf{X}),

(83)

where (
82
) follows by applying Fatou’s lemma and (
83
) is due to (
76
).
By the law of total variance, we also have that
{\rm mmse}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}|\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha})\leq{\rm{Var}}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha})
and hence, we arrive at

\lim_{{\rm{snr}}\to 0}{\rm mmse}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}|\mathsf{Y}_{\alpha})\leq\lim_{{\rm{snr}}\to 0}{\rm{Var}}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}).

(84)

In Appendix
K
, we show that

\lim_{{\rm{snr}}\to 0}{\rm{Var}}(\mathsf{X}_{\alpha})={\rm{Var}}(\mathsf{X}).

(85)

This concludes the proof of Proposition
4
.

VI
Conclusions and Future Directions

In this paper, we studied Sibson’s
\alpha
-mutual information in the context of the additive Gaussian noise channel and developed a number of structural results that parallel those known for the classical case
\alpha=1
. In particular, we established several regularity properties, derived an
\alpha
-I-MMSE relationship, and showed how this identity leads to generalized de Bruijn-type relations and new estimation-theoretic representations of Rényi entropy and differential Rényi entropy. We further characterized both the low- and high-SNR behaviors of
\alpha
-mutual information, including its connections to the
\alpha
-information dimension.

Several interesting directions for future work emerge from our results. First, the
\alpha
-I-MMSE relationship suggests the possibility of deriving new functional and information-theoretic inequalities, in analogy with the classical I-MMSE identity, which has been used to obtain entropy power inequalities
[
34
,
63
,
30
]
, log-Sobolev inequalities
[
47
,
49
]
, and related bounds
[
18
,
11
,
44
,
33
]
to name a few. It would be interesting to understand whether similar inequalities can be obtained in the
\alpha
-setting and what new phenomena may arise.

Second, while our analysis focuses on finite values of
\alpha
, the limiting case
\alpha=\infty
remains largely unexplored in this context. This regime is closely connected to notions of maximal leakage and privacy
[
23
]
, and it would be of interest to investigate whether the techniques developed here can be extended to provide new insights into privacy-constrained inference problems.

Third, our results on the high-SNR behavior and
\alpha
-information dimension suggest a deeper connection with
\alpha
-rate-distortion theory. In particular, it would be interesting to explore whether analogues of the relationships established in
[
40
]
between information dimension and rate-distortion functions can be extended to the
\alpha
-Rényi setting.

Fourth, the strict concavity (and convexity) properties established in this paper open the door to studying optimization problems involving
\alpha
-mutual information. For example, it would be natural to investigate whether, under amplitude constraints, the maximizing input distributions are discrete and to characterize their structure, in analogy with classical results for Shannon mutual information
[
60
,
21
]
.

Finally, it would be of interest to extend the present analysis to the vector case, see for example
[
62
]
, and also beyond the Gaussian noise channel. In particular, studying
\alpha
-mutual information for other canonical models, such as the Poisson channel, may reveal new connections between information measures and estimation quantities. In this direction, an intriguing question is whether generalized I-MMSE-type relationships can be established that relate
\alpha

Appendix A
Proof Lemma
1

We start by observing that by the Markov’s inequality with
k>0
, we have that

P_{\mathsf{W}}(w)=\mathbb{P}[\mathsf{W}=w]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\mathsf{W}\geq w\right]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{W}^{k}\right]}{w^{k}},

(126)

which implies that

\sum_{w\in{\mathsf{supp}}(\mathsf{W})}P_{\mathsf{W}}^{\alpha}(w)\leq\mathbb{E}^{\alpha}\left[\mathsf{W}^{k}\right]\sum_{w\in{\mathsf{supp}}(\mathsf{W})}\frac{1}{w^{\alpha k}}.

(127)

In particular, if
{\mathsf{supp}}(\mathsf{W})
is a subset of positive integers and
\alpha k>1
, then the sum convergences since it is a p-series, that is,

\sum_{w\in{\mathsf{supp}}(\mathsf{W})}\frac{1}{w^{\alpha k}}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha k}}<\infty.

(128)

This proves the first property.

For the second property, first consider the following random variable: for
n\in\{1,2,\ldots\}
and some
\eta>0
and
q\neq 0

\displaystyle P_{\mathsf{W}}(w_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{n^{1+\eta}},

(129)

\displaystyle w_{n}

\displaystyle=n^{q},

(130)

where
C
is the normalization constant. Then, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{W}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right]=C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{1+\eta-\frac{q}{\alpha}}},

(131)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{\mathsf{W}}^{\alpha}(w_{n})=C^{\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\alpha(1+\eta)}}.

(132)

To have that
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{W}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right]<\infty
and
H_{\alpha}(\mathsf{W})=\infty
, from the above p-series, it suffices to guarantee the existence of
\eta>0
and
q\neq 0
such that

\displaystyle 1+\eta-\frac{q}{\alpha}>1\text{ and }\alpha(1+\eta)\leq 1

(133)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad\eta>\frac{q}{\alpha}\text{ and }\eta\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}-1.

(134)

Such an
\eta
exists provided that

\frac{q}{\alpha}<\frac{1}{\alpha}-1,

(135)

which can be satisfied by choosing any

.

\displaystyle I_{j}(\mathrm{snr})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\{u\in[L_{j},U_{j}]\}}\,\phi(u)\left(p_{j}+\sum_{i\neq j}p_{i}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\,h_{i,j}(u;\mathrm{snr})}\right)^{1/\alpha}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)+\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{\mathsf{X}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}\left(t,\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon\right)\right){\rm d}t\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\left(\mathbb{E}\left[P_{\mathsf{X}}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\mathsf{X},\varepsilon)\right)\right]\right)

(415)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)+\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{\mathsf{X}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}\left(t,\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon\right)\right){\rm d}t\right)+\frac{1}{\alpha}g_{\alpha}\left(\mathsf{X},\varepsilon\right)

(416)

\displaystyle=\log(3)+\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}I_{\alpha}\left(\mathsf{X};\mathsf{Y}_{3\varepsilon}\right)+\frac{1}{\alpha}g_{\alpha}\left(\mathsf{X},\varepsilon\right),

(417)

where: in (
410
), we let
Q_{\mathsf{Y},s}
have density

q_{s}(y)=\frac{P_{\mathsf{X}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(y,s)\right)}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{\mathsf{X}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(t,s)\right){\rm d}t},

(418)

with
s=\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon
; (
412
) follows from Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem; (
414
) follows since, under the assumption
|y-\mathsf{X}|\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}
, we have that
\mathcal{B}_{\infty}\left(y,\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon\right)\supseteq\mathcal{B}_{\infty}\left(\mathsf{X},\varepsilon\right)
, which implies that

P_{\mathsf{X}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}\left(y,\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon\right)\right)\geq P_{\mathsf{X}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\mathsf{X},\varepsilon)\right),

(419)

and using the fact that the mapping
s\mapsto s^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}
, with
s\in(0,1)
, is decreasing for
\alpha\in(1,+\infty)
(and the pre-log factor
\frac{1}{\alpha-1}
is positive) and increasing for
\alpha\in(0,1)
(and the pre-log factor
\frac{1}{\alpha-1}
is negative); and (
417
) is due to (
34
) in Lemma
4
. Rearranging (
417
) proves the upper bound in (
101
).

We now prove the lower bound in (
101
). From (
35
) in Lemma
4
, we have that

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(\mathsf{X};\mathsf{Y}_{\varepsilon})=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{P_{\mathsf{X}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{\varepsilon},\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\right)\right)}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\right]\right).

(420)

The fact that
|\mathsf{U}|\leq\frac{1}{2}
implies that
|\mathsf{Y}_{\varepsilon}-\mathsf{X}|=|\varepsilon\mathsf{U}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and that

\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\mathsf{Y}_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon/2)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\mathsf{X},\varepsilon).

(421)

Consequently,

P_{\mathsf{X}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{\varepsilon},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\right)\leq P_{\mathsf{X}}(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\mathsf{X},\varepsilon)),

(422)

and also:

1.

If
\alpha\in(1,+\infty)
, then the mapping
s\mapsto s^{-\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}
is decreasing for
s\in(0,1)
and hence,

\left(\frac{1}{P_{\mathsf{X}}(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\mathsf{Y}_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon/2))}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\geq\left(\frac{1}{P_{\mathsf{X}}(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\mathsf{X},\varepsilon))}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}};

(423)

2.

If
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then the mapping
s\mapsto s^{-\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}
is increasing for
s\in(0,1)
and hence,

\left(\frac{1}{P_{\mathsf{X}}(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\mathsf{Y}_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon/2))}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\leq\left(\frac{1}{P_{\mathsf{X}}(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\mathsf{X},\varepsilon))}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}.

(424)

Substituting the above inside (

\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}<0

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(\mathsf{X};\mathsf{Y}_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{P_{\mathsf{X}}(\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\mathsf{X},\varepsilon))}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\right]\right)=g_{\alpha}(\mathsf{X},\varepsilon).

(425)

This prove the lower bound in (

) and concludes the proof of Lemma

.

Appendix O
Proof of Lemma
9

We are interested in computing

\lim_{\mathrm{snr}\to\infty}I_{j}(\mathrm{snr})=\lim_{\mathrm{snr}\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{j}(u;\mathrm{snr})\,\mathrm{d}u,

(426)

where

f_{j}(u;\mathrm{snr})=\mathbbm{1}_{\{u\in[L_{j},U_{j}]\}}\phi(u)\left(p_{j}+\sum_{i\neq j}p_{i}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\,h_{i,j}(u;\mathrm{snr})}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

(427)

We next show that we can exchange the limit and the integral in (
426
) by means of the dominated convergence theorem.
Toward this end, we start by proving that

\displaystyle h_{i,j}(u;\mathrm{snr})\geq 0,\quad{\text{for all}}\ u\in[L_{j},U_{j}].

(428)

We consider two cases separately:

Case 1:

i>j
. We have that

\displaystyle h_{i,j}(u;\mathrm{snr})

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\mathrm{snr}\,(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}-2\sqrt{\mathrm{snr}}\;u\,(x_{i}-x_{j})\right)

(429)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}(x_{i}-x_{j})\left(\mathrm{snr}\,(x_{i}-x_{j})-\mathrm{snr}\,(x_{j+1}-x_{j})\right)

(430)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}(x_{i}-x_{j})\left(\mathrm{snr}\,(x_{i}-x_{j})-\mathrm{snr}\,(x_{i}-x_{j})\right)

(431)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

(432)

where in the first inequality we have used the fact that
u\leq U_{j}
and the second inequality follows from the assumption that
x_{j}\leq x_{i}
for
j\leq i
.

Case 2:

i<j
.
We have that

\displaystyle h_{i,j}(u;\mathrm{snr})

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\mathrm{snr}\,(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}-2\sqrt{\mathrm{snr}}\;u\,(x_{i}-x_{j})\right)

(433)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}(x_{j}-x_{i})\left(\mathrm{snr}(x_{j}-x_{i})+2\sqrt{\mathrm{snr}}\;u\right)

(434)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}(x_{j}-x_{i})\left(\mathrm{snr}(x_{j}-x_{i})-\mathrm{snr}\,(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\right)

(435)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

(436)

where in the first inequality we have used the fact that
u\geq L_{j}
and the second inequality follows from the assumption that
x_{j}\geq x_{i}
for
j\geq i
.

The property in (
428
) implies that

f_{j}(u;\mathrm{snr})\leq\phi(u),

(437)

that is,
f_{j}(\cdot;\mathrm{snr})
is dominated by an integrable function. Moreover, for any fixed
u
, we have the following pointwise limit,

\lim_{\mathrm{snr}\to\infty}f_{j}(u;\mathrm{snr})=\phi(u)\ p_{j}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},

(438)

where we have used the fact that for any fixed
u
, from (
372
), it holds that

h_{i,j}(u;\mathrm{snr})\to+\infty,\quad\text{as}\ \mathrm{snr}\to\infty,

(439)

since the leading term increases with
{\rm snr}
.
Hence, by using the dominated convergence theorem, we arrive at

\displaystyle\lim_{\mathrm{snr}\to\infty}I_{j}(\mathrm{snr})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\mathrm{snr}\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{j}(u;\mathrm{snr})\,\mathrm{d}u

(440)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\lim_{\mathrm{snr}\to\infty}f_{j}(u;\mathrm{snr})\,\mathrm{d}u

(441)

\displaystyle=p_{j}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

(442)

This concludes the proof of Lemma
9
.
