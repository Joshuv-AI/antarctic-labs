Sum of the k k Largest Eigenvalues of Symmetric Matrices: Theory and Applications


Abstract
This paper establishes new upper bounds for the sum of the k k largest eigenvalues of symmetric matrices. When applied to the adjacency matrix of a graph, our results improve upon a related bound due to Mohar [On the sum of k largest eigenvalues of graphs and symmetric matrices, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 99 (2009) 306–313] . Furthermore, in the case of the Laplacian matrix, we prove that the well-known Brouwer’s conjecture [Spectra of Graphs, Springer, New York, 2012] holds for small values of k k for almost all graphs, thereby taking a significant step toward its complete resolution. Key Words: Eigenvalue sum, Symmetric matrix, graph, Adjacency matrix, Laplacian matrix 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05C50

Introduction

The spectral theory of symmetric matrices underpins vast areas of
mathematics and its applications in the physical sciences, engineering and data
science. The eigenvalues of a symmetric matrix encode critical information about the fundamental properties of the system it represents.
While computing the exact eigenvalues is a central numerical task, deriving rigorous a priori bounds for these eigenvalues constitutes
a parallel and equally profound strand of research in matrix
theory. In this paper, we will study the sum of the
k
largest
eigenvalues of symmetric matrices.

Let
M
be an
n\times n
symmetric matrix, whose eigenvalues are all real and ordered as
\lambda_{1}(M)\geq\lambda_{2}(M)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(M)
.
Denote by
\nu(M)
(respectively,
\theta(M)
) the number of eigenvalues of
M
greater than (respectively, less than)

\frac{\operatorname{tr}(M)}{n}
, where
\operatorname{tr}(M)
is the trace of
M
. When
\operatorname{tr}(M)=0
, the quantities
\nu(M)
and
\theta(M)
are referred to as the positive and negative inertia of
M
, respectively.
For a graph
G
, we write
\lambda_{i}(G)=\lambda_{i}(A(G))
and
\mu_{i}(G)=\lambda_{i}(L(G))
, where
A(G)
and
L(G)
denote the adjacency matrix and Laplacian matrix of
G
, respectively. We use
S_{M}(G)
to denote the spectrum of the matrix
M
of
G
.

In
[
17
]
, Mohar obtained an upper bound on the sum of
k
largest
eigenvalues of a symmetric matrix via a simple approach. Nikiforov

[
19
]
strengthened Mohar’s result by incorporating the singular values of
the matrix. Inspired by these works, we further investigate upper bounds on the sum of the
k
largest eigenvalues of symmetric matrices.

When we consider a symmetric matrix as a graph matrix, numerous related results have been established regarding the sum of
its
k
largest eigenvalues–for instance, for the adjacency matrix
[
10
,
17
,
19
]
, (signless) Laplacian matrix
[
1
,
14
]
, distance matrix
[
22
]
, and distance signless Laplacian matrix
[
13
]
.
In the case of the Laplacian matrix, a well-known open problem is Brouwer’s conjecture, stated as follows:

Conjecture 1.1
.

(Brouwer’s conjecture)
[
3
]

Let
G
be a graph with order
n
and size
m
. For each
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}\leq m+\binom{k+1}{2}.

This conjecture has been verified in several special cases but remains open in general. For recent progress, we refer to
[
6
]
. A further motivation of this paper is to apply our main
results to the Laplacian matrix, with the aim of contributing to the study of Brouwer’s conjecture.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we
present our main results concerning the sum of the
k
largest
eigenvalues of symmetric matrices. Section 3 is devoted to
applications of these results to various graph matrices. Finally, we
conclude the paper with a summary in Section 4.

2
Sum of the
k
largest eigenvalues of a matrix

In this section, we focus on deriving upper bounds for the sum of the
k
largest eigenvalues of a symmetric matrix. We now present the first result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
M
be an
n\times n
symmetric matrix. Let
s
be an integer such that
\lambda_{s}(M)>\frac{tr(M)}{n}
. Then for any
k\geq s
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(M)\leq\frac{k}{n}\,tr(M)+\sqrt{tr\left(\Big(M-\frac{tr(M)}{n}I\Big)^{2}\right)\left(k-\frac{s^{2}}{\theta+s}\right)}.

(1)

The equality holds if and only if
k=s=\nu
with

\lambda_{1}(M)=\lambda_{2}(M)=\cdots=\lambda_{s}(M)>\frac{tr(M)}{n}\geq\lambda_{s+1}(M)~\mbox{ and }~\lambda_{n-\theta+1}(M)=\lambda_{n-\theta+2}(M)=\cdots=\lambda_{n}(M).

Proof.

Since

\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}\,\lambda_{i}(M)=tr(M)~\mbox{ and }~\lambda_{1}(M)\geq\lambda_{2}(M)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(M).

It follows that
\lambda_{1}(M)\geq\frac{tr(M)}{n}
. If
\lambda_{1}(M)=\frac{tr(M)}{n}
, then all eigenvalues must be equal:

\lambda_{1}(M)=\lambda_{2}(M)=\cdots=\lambda_{n}(M)=\frac{tr(M)}{n}.

This implies that
s
does not exist, which leads to a contradiction. Otherwise,
\lambda_{1}(M)>\frac{tr(M)}{n}
, that is,
M\neq\frac{tr(M)}{n}I
and
s\geq 1
.
Let
P=M-\frac{tr(M)}{n}I
.
It is clear that

\lambda_{i}(P)=\lambda_{i}(M)-\frac{tr(M)}{n}
and
tr(P)=0
. Then we set
\nu=\nu(M)=\nu(P)
and
\theta=\theta(M)=\theta(P)
, which are defined before.
To prove the result in (
1
), we will show the following equivalent result:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(P)\leq\sqrt{tr(P^{2})\left(k-\frac{s^{2}}{\theta+s}\right)}~~~\mbox{for }k\geq s,

(2)

with equality holding if and only if
k=s=\nu
,
\lambda_{1}(P)=\lambda_{2}(P)=\cdots=\lambda_{s}(P)
and
\lambda_{n-\theta+1}(P)=\lambda_{n-\theta+2}(P)=\cdots=\lambda_{n}(P)
.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}^{2}(P)\geq\frac{1}{s}\left(\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(P)\right)^{2}

(3)

with equality if and only if
\lambda_{1}(P)=\lambda_{2}(P)=\cdots=\lambda_{s}(P)
. Since
\nu\geq s
, using the above result, we obtain

\displaystyle tr(P^{2})=\sum\limits^{\nu}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}^{2}(P)+\sum\limits^{\theta}_{i=1}\lambda_{n-i+1}^{2}(P)

\displaystyle\geq\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda^{2}_{i}(P)+\sum\limits^{\theta}_{i=1}\lambda_{n-i+1}^{2}(P)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{s}\left(\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(P)\right)^{2}+\sum\limits^{\theta}_{i=1}\lambda_{n-i+1}^{2}(P)

(4)

with equality if and only if
\nu=s
and
\lambda_{1}(P)=\lambda_{2}(P)=\cdots=\lambda_{s}(P)
.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with the fact that
tr(P)=0
, we have

\sum\limits^{\theta}_{i=1}\lambda_{n-i+1}^{2}(P)\geq\frac{\left(\sum\limits^{\theta}_{i=1}\,|\lambda_{n-i+1}(P)|\right)^{2}}{\theta}=\frac{\left(\sum\limits^{\nu}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(P)\right)^{2}}{\theta}\geq\frac{\left(\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(P)\right)^{2}}{\theta}

(5)

with equality if and only if
\nu=s
and
|\lambda_{n-\theta+1}(P)|=|\lambda_{n-\theta+2}(P)|=\cdots=|\lambda_{n}(P)|
.

Combining the results in (
4
) and (
5
), we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(P)\leq\sqrt{\frac{\theta\,s}{\theta+s}\,tr(P^{2})}.

If
k=s
, then from the above, the result in (
2
) follows. Moreover, the above equality holds if and only if
\nu=s
with
\lambda_{1}(P)=\lambda_{1}(P)=\cdots=\lambda_{s}(P)
and
\lambda_{n-\theta+1}(P)=\lambda_{n-\theta+2}(P)=\cdots=\lambda_{n}(P)
.

Otherwise,
k\geq s+1
. First we can assume that
s+1\leq k\leq\nu
. Next we will prove the inequality in (
2
) holds strictly for
s+1\leq k\leq\nu
. Since
\nu>s
, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with (
3
) and (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(P)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(P)+\sqrt{(k-s)\sum\limits^{k}_{i=s+1}\lambda_{i}^{2}(P)}

(6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(P)+\sqrt{\left(k-s\right)\left(tr(P^{2})-\sum\limits^{\theta}_{i=1}\lambda_{n-i+1}^{2}(P)-\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda^{2}_{i}(P)\right)}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(P)+\sqrt{\left(k-s\right)\left(tr(P^{2})-\frac{1}{\theta}\left(\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(P)\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{s}\left(\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(P)\right)^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(P)+\sqrt{\left(k-s\right)\left(tr(P^{2})-\frac{\theta+s}{\theta\,s}\left(\sum\limits^{s}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(P)\right)^{2}\right)}.

Let us consider a function

f(x)=x+\sqrt{(k-s)\left(tr(P^{2})-\frac{\theta+s}{\theta\,s}x^{2}\right)},\,~~0\leq x\leq\sqrt{\frac{\theta\,s}{\theta+s}\,tr(P^{2})}.

Then
f^{\prime}(x)=0
gives

x_{1}=\theta\,s\sqrt{\frac{tr(P^{2})}{(\theta+s)(k\,\theta+k\,s-s^{2})}}<\sqrt{\frac{\theta\,s}{\theta+s}\,tr(P^{2})},

which implies that
f(x)
is increasing for
x\leq x_{1}
, and decreasing for
x\geq x_{1}
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle f(x)\leq f(x_{1})=\sqrt{tr(P^{2})\left(k-\frac{s^{2}}{\theta+s}\right)}.

Combining the above result with (
6
), we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(P)<\sqrt{tr(P^{2})\left(k-\frac{s^{2}}{\theta+s}\right)}

(7)

for
s+1\leq k\leq\nu
. The result in (
2
) holds strictly.

Next we assume that
k\geq\nu+1
. Since
\lambda_{i}(P)\leq 0
for
i\geq\nu+1
, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(P)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\lambda_{i}(P)<\sqrt{tr(P^{2})\left(\nu-\frac{s^{2}}{\theta+s}\right)}<\sqrt{tr(P^{2})\left(k-\frac{s^{2}}{\theta+s}\right)}

because (
7
) holds for
k=\nu
. Consequently, the inequality in (
2
) holds strictly. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Note that the condition of
s
in the above theorem is
s\leq\nu
. By the above theorem, we obtain a stronger result in which the upper bound on
s
is replaced by a larger number as follows:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
M
be an
n\times n
symmetric matrix. Let
t
be an integer such that
t\leq n-\theta
. Then for any
k\geq t
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(M)\leq\frac{k}{n}\,tr(M)+\sqrt{tr\left(\Big(M-\frac{tr(M)}{n}I\Big)^{2}\right)\left(k-\frac{t^{2}}{\theta+t}\right)}.

The equality holds if and only if
M=\frac{tr(M)}{n}I
or
k=t
with

\lambda_{1}(M)=\lambda_{1}(M)=\cdots=\lambda_{t}(M)>\frac{tr(M)}{n}\geq\lambda_{t+1}(M)~\mbox{ and }~\lambda_{n-\theta+1}(M)=\lambda_{n-\theta+2}(M)=\cdots=\lambda_{n}(M).

Proof.

It is trivial if
M=\frac{tr(M)}{n}I
. Thus, we assume that
M\neq\frac{tr(M)}{n}I
. Then we have
\lambda_{1}(M)>\frac{tr(M)}{n}
as
M
is symmetric.
If
t\leq\nu
, Theorem
2.1
directly implies the required result together with the equality cases.
Otherwise,
\nu+1\leq t\leq n-\theta
.
Setting
s=\nu
and
k=\nu
in (
1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\lambda_{i}(M)\leq\frac{\nu}{n}\,tr(M)+\sqrt{tr\left(\Big(M-\frac{tr(M)}{n}I\Big)^{2}\right)\,\frac{\theta\nu}{\theta+\nu}}\,.

Using the above result with the fact that
\lambda_{i}=\frac{tr(M)}{n}
for
\nu+1\leq i\leq t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(M)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k-t}{n}\,tr(M)+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\lambda_{i}(M)=\frac{k-\nu}{n}\,tr(M)+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\lambda_{i}(M)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k}{n}\,tr(M)+\sqrt{tr\left(\Big(M-\frac{tr(M)}{n}I\Big)^{2}\right)\frac{\theta\nu}{\theta+\nu}}

\displaystyle<\frac{k}{n}\,tr(M)+\sqrt{tr\left(\Big(M-\frac{tr(M)}{n}I\Big)^{2}\right)\frac{\theta\,t}{\theta+t}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k}{n}\,tr(M)+\sqrt{tr\left(\Big(M-\frac{tr(M)}{n}I\Big)^{2}\right)\left(k-\frac{t^{2}}{\theta+t}\right)}

as
k\geq t
. The result holds strictly. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Next we give a more simple upper bound on the sum of the
k
largest eigenvalues of a matrix.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
M
be an
n\times n
symmetric matrix. Let
s
be an integer such that

\lambda_{s}(M)>\frac{tr(M)}{n}~\mbox{ and }~\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\frac{\lambda_{i}(M)}{s}\geq\frac{tr(M^{2})+tr(M)}{n}-\frac{(tr(M))^{2}}{n^{2}}.

Then for any
k\geq s
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(M)\leq\frac{k}{n}\,tr(M)+\frac{n\theta}{2(\theta+s)}+\frac{n}{2(\theta+s)}\sqrt{\theta\big(k\theta+ks-s^{2}\big)}.

(8)

The equality holds if and only if
k=s=1
with

\lambda_{1}(M)=\frac{tr(M^{2})+tr(M)}{n}-\frac{(tr(M))^{2}}{n^{2}}>\frac{tr(M)}{n}\geq\lambda_{2}(M)

and

\lambda_{n-\theta+1}(M)=\lambda_{n-\theta+2}(M)=\cdots=\lambda_{n}(M).

Proof.

If
\lambda_{1}(M)=\frac{tr(M)}{n}
, then
\lambda_{2}(M)=\lambda_{n}(M)=\frac{tr(M)}{n}
, which means the
s
does not exist.
Otherwise,
\lambda_{1}(M)>\frac{tr(M)}{n}
, that is,
M\neq\frac{tr(M)}{n}I
and
s\geq 1
. Let
P=M-\frac{tr(M)}{n}I
. Then we set
\nu=\nu(M)=\nu(P)
and
\theta=\theta(M)=\theta(P)
.
Since
tr(P^{2})=tr(M^{2})-\frac{(tr(M))^{2}}{n}
, it is equivalent to prove that for
k\geq s
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(P)\leq\frac{n\theta}{2(\theta+s)}+\frac{n}{2(\theta+s)}\sqrt{\theta\big(k\,\theta+k\,s-s^{2}\big)}~~~~\mbox{for }\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\frac{\lambda_{i}(P)}{s}\geq\frac{tr(P^{2})}{n},

(9)

with equality holding if and only if
k=s=1
with
\lambda_{1}(P)=\frac{tr(P^{2})}{n}>0\geq\lambda_{2}(P)
and

\lambda_{n-\theta+1}(P)=\lambda_{n-\theta+2}(P)=\cdots=\lambda_{n}(P)
.

Combining Theorem
2.1
on
k=s
with the condition that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\frac{1}{s}\lambda_{i}(P)\geq\frac{tr(P^{2})}{n}
, we get

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}(P)\leq\frac{n\theta}{\theta+s}\leq\frac{n\theta}{2(\theta+s)}+\frac{n}{2(\theta+s)}\sqrt{\theta\big(k\,\theta+k\,s-s^{2}\big)}.

The first equality holds if and only if
s=\nu
,
\lambda_{1}(P)=\lambda_{2}(P)=\cdots=\lambda_{s}(P)=\frac{tr(P^{2})}{n}
and

\lambda_{n-\theta+1}(P)=\lambda_{n-\theta+2}(P)=\cdots=\lambda_{n}(P)
and the second equality holds only for
k=s=1
.
Hence we prove the case
k=s
and together with equality cases.
Next we will prove the inequality in (
9
) strictly holds for
s+1\leq k\leq\nu
as
\lambda_{i}(P)\leq 0
for
i>\nu
. For this, we consider the following
two case:

{\bf Case\,1.}

tr(P^{2})\geq n\,\theta\,\sqrt{\displaystyle{\frac{tr(P^{2})}{(\theta+s)(k\,\theta+k\,s-s^{2})}}}=\displaystyle{\frac{n\,x_{1}}{s}}

(x_{1}
is defined in the proof of Theorem
2.1
)
.

In this case,
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}(P)\geq\frac{s}{n}tr(P^{2})\geq x_{1}
. Recall that the function
f(x)
defined in the proof of Theorem
2.1
,
f(x)
is decreasing on
x\geq x_{1}
. Then from (
6
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(P)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle f\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}(P)\right)\leq f\left(\frac{s}{n}tr(P^{2})\right)

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s}{n}tr(P^{2})+\sqrt{(k-s)\left(tr(P^{2})-\frac{(tr(P^{2}))^{2}(\theta+s)\,s}{n^{2}\theta}\right)}\,.

We now consider a function

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s\,x}{n}+\sqrt{(k-s)\left(x-\frac{s\,(\theta+s)x^{2}}{n^{2}\theta}\right)}\,,~~~~0\leq x\leq\frac{n^{2}\theta}{s\,(\theta+s)}.

Note that
g(x)
can be rewriten as

g(x)=\frac{s\,x}{n}+\sqrt{\frac{(s-k)(\theta+s)s}{\theta}\left(\frac{x}{n}-\frac{\theta\,n}{2s\,(\theta+s)}\right)^{2}+\frac{(k-s)\ n^{2}\theta}{4s(\theta+s)}}.

(11)

Then we have

g^{\prime}(x)=\frac{s}{n}+\frac{\sqrt{k-s}}{2}\times\frac{\displaystyle{1-\frac{2s(\theta+s)}{n^{2}\theta}x}}{\sqrt{\displaystyle{x-\frac{s(\theta+s)x^{2}}{n^{2}\theta}}}}\,,~~~0<x<\frac{n^{2}\theta}{s\,(\theta+s)}.

From
g^{\prime}(x)=0
, one can easily get

\displaystyle x_{3}=\frac{n^{2}\theta}{2s(\theta+s)}-\frac{n^{2}\theta}{2s(\theta+s)}\sqrt{\frac{\theta\,s}{k\theta+ks-s^{2}}}

and

\displaystyle x_{4}=\frac{n^{2}\theta}{2s(\theta+s)}+\frac{n^{2}\theta}{2s(\theta+s)}\sqrt{\frac{\theta\,s}{k\theta+ks-s^{2}}}\,.

For convenience, let
x_{5}=\displaystyle{\frac{n^{2}\theta}{s(\theta+s)}}
. By the closed interval method on the continuous function
g(x)
for
x\in\left[0,x_{5}\right]
, we have

g(x)\leq\max\Big\{g(0),\,g(x_{3}),\,g(x_{4}),\,g_{1}(x_{5})\Big\}.

After simple calculation, we get
g(0)=0
and
g(x_{5})=\frac{n\theta}{\theta+s}
. By (
11
), one can easily obtain that

g(x_{3})<g(x_{4})=\frac{n\theta}{2(\theta+s)}+\frac{n}{2(\theta+s)}\sqrt{\frac{\theta(k\theta+ks-s^{2})}{s}}.

As
k>s
, then
\frac{k\theta+ks-s^{2}}{s}>\theta
, which follows
g(x_{5})<g(x_{4})
. Hence
g(x)\leq g(x_{4})
.
Applying the above results in (
10
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(P)<g(tr(P^{2}))\leq g(x_{4})\leq\frac{n\theta}{2(\theta+s)}+\frac{n}{2(\theta+s)}\sqrt{\theta\big(k\,\theta+k\,s-s^{2}\big)},

which implies that the inequality in (
9
) strictly holds.

{\bf Case\,2.}

tr(P^{2})<n\,\theta\,\sqrt{\displaystyle{\frac{tr(P^{2})}{(\theta+s)(k\,\theta+k\,s-s^{2})}}}=\displaystyle{\frac{n\,x_{1}}{s}}
.

As
x_{1}=\theta\,s\sqrt{\displaystyle{\frac{tr(P^{2})}{(\theta+s)(k\,\theta+k\,s-s^{2})}}}
, we have

\displaystyle k\times tr(P^{2})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}{\theta}^{2}k}{(\theta+s)(k\,\theta+k\,s-s^{2})}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}{\theta}^{2}}{(\theta+s)(\theta+s-s^{2}/k)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}{\theta}^{2}}{(\theta+s)(\theta+s/(s+1))}

as
k\geq s+1
.

Now let

h(x)=\left(\frac{n\,x}{2(x+s)}+\frac{n}{2(x+s)}\sqrt{x\big(s\,x+x+s\big)}\right)^{2}-\frac{n^{2}x(2x-1)}{2(x+s)(x+s/(s+1))}.

Claim 1
.

h(x)>0
for
x>0
.

Proof of Claim
1
.
Now we can rewrite
h(x)
as

h(x)=\frac{n^{2}}{4(x+s)^{2}(sx+x+s)}p(x),

where

p(x)=(s^{2}-s-2)x^{3}-(2s^{2}-s-2)x^{2}+(3s^{2}+2s)x+2\sqrt{((s+1)x^{2}+sx)^{3}}.

Since
\Big((s+1)x^{2}+sx\Big)^{3}>(s+1)^{2}x^{6}
, we get

p(x)>(s^{2}+s)x^{3}-(2s^{2}-s-2)x^{2}+(3s^{2}+2s)x.

Due to the fact that

(2s^{2}-s-2)^{2}-4(s^{2}+s)(3s^{2}+2s)=-8s^{4}-24s^{3}-15s^{2}+4s+4<0

for
s\geq 1
, we conclude
(s^{2}+s)x^{2}-(2s^{2}-s-2)x+(3s^{2}+2s)
has no real roots. By combining the above results, we obtain that
p(x)>0
for
s\geq 1
and
x>0
.
Hence
h(x)>0
for
x>0
, which completes the proof of
{\bf Claim\,\ref{c1}}
.

From
{\bf Claim\,\ref{c1}}
with (
12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{n\,\theta}{2(\theta+s)}+\frac{n}{2(\theta+s)}\sqrt{\theta\,\Big(s\,\theta+\theta+s\Big)}

\displaystyle>\sqrt{\frac{n^{2}\theta(2\theta-1)\,(s+1)}{2(\theta+s)\Big(\theta\,(s+1)+s\Big)}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{k\,\left(1-\frac{1}{2\theta}\right)\,tr(P^{2})}.

(13)

Since

(tr(P))^{2}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(P)\right)^{2}=tr(P^{2})+2\sum_{i<j}\lambda_{i}(P)\lambda_{j}(P)~\mbox{ and }~tr(P)=0,

we obtain

tr(P^{2})=-2\sum_{i<j}\lambda_{i}(P)\lambda_{j}(P)
.
Then

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}(P)|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tr(P^{2})+2\sum_{i<j}|\lambda_{i}(P)\lambda_{j}(P)|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle tr(P^{2})+\left|2\sum_{i<j}\lambda_{i}(P)\lambda_{j}(P)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,tr(P^{2}).

Using this result, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{\theta}\lambda_{n-i+1}^{2}(P)\geq\frac{\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\theta}|\lambda_{n-i+1}(P)|\right)^{2}}{\theta}=\frac{\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}(P)|\right)^{2}}{4\,\theta}\geq\frac{tr(P^{2})}{2\,\theta}\,.

(14)

Using the above result with (
13
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(P)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{k\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}^{2}(P)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{k\left(tr(P^{2})-\sum_{i=1}^{\theta}\lambda_{n-i+1}^{2}(P)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{k\left(1-\frac{1}{2\,\theta}\right)\,tr(P^{2})}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{n\,\theta}{2(\theta+s)}+\frac{n}{2(\theta+s)}\sqrt{\theta\big(s\,\theta+\theta+s\big)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n\,\theta}{2(\theta+s)}+\frac{n}{2(\theta+s)}\sqrt{\theta(k\,\theta+k\,s-s^{2})}

as
k\geq s+1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
M
be an
n\times n
symmetric matrix. If

\lambda_{2}(M)>\frac{tr(M)}{n}~\mbox{ and }~\lambda_{1}(M)+\lambda_{2}(M)\geq\frac{2\,tr(M^{2})+2\,tr(M)}{n}-\frac{2(tr(M))^{2}}{n^{2}},

then for any
k\geq 2
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(M)\leq\frac{k}{n}\,tr(M)+\frac{n\theta}{2(\theta+2)}+\frac{n}{2(\theta+2)}\sqrt{\frac{\theta\big(k\theta+2k-4\big)}{2}}.

(15)

The equality holds if and only if
k=2
with

\lambda_{1}(M)=\lambda_{2}(M)=\frac{tr(M^{2})+tr(M)}{n}-\frac{(tr(M))^{2}}{n^{2}}>\frac{tr(M)}{n}\geq\lambda_{3}(M)

and
\lambda_{n-\theta+1}(M)=\lambda_{n-\theta+2}(M)=\cdots=\lambda_{n}(M)
.

Proof.

Let
P=M-\frac{tr(M)}{n}I
. It is equivalent to prove that for
k\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(P)\leq\frac{n\theta}{2(\theta+2)}+\frac{n}{2(\theta+2)}\sqrt{\frac{\theta\big(k\,\theta+2k\,-4\big)}{2}}~~~~\mbox{for }\frac{\lambda_{1}(P)+\lambda_{2}(P)}{2}\geq\frac{tr(P^{2})}{n},

(16)

with equality holding if and only if
k=2
with
\lambda_{1}(P)=\lambda_{2}(P)=\frac{tr(P^{2})}{n}>0\geq\lambda_{3}(P)
and

\lambda_{n-\theta+1}(P)=\lambda_{n-\theta+2}(P)=\cdots=\lambda_{n}(P)
.
The proof of the above result is similar as the proof of Theorem
2.3
by taking
s=2
. Now we explain it in detail. By a similar way as the proof of Theorem
2.3
with
s=2
(before
Case 1
), we obtain that the result in (
16
) holds for
k=2
, together with the equality cases. It remains to show the inequality in (
16
) strictly holds
for
3\leq k\leq\nu
. If
tr(P^{2})\geq\frac{nx_{1}}{2}
, by the result of
Case 1
in the proof of Theorem
2.3
, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(P)<g(x_{4})=\frac{n\theta}{2(\theta+2)}+\frac{n}{2(\theta+2)}\sqrt{\frac{\theta\big(k\,\theta+2k\,-4\big)}{2}}.

Otherwise,
tr(P^{2})<\frac{nx_{1}}{2}
. By the result of
Case 2
in the proof of Theorem
2.3
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(P)\leq\sqrt{k\,tr(P^{2})\left(1-\frac{1}{2\,\theta}\right)}.

Appying the result in (
12
) with
s=2
to the above result, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(P)\leq\sqrt{\frac{n^{2}\theta(2\theta-1)}{2(\theta+2)(\theta+2/3)}}.

(17)

Let

\displaystyle R(x)=\left(\frac{n\,x}{2(x+2)}+\frac{n}{2(x+2)}\sqrt{\frac{x\big(3x+2\big)}{2}}\right)^{2}-\frac{n^{2}x(2x-1)}{2(x+2)(x+\frac{2}{3})}~~\mbox{for }x>0.

We can rewrite
R(x)
as
R(x)=\frac{n^{2}}{4(x+2)(3x+2)}S(x)
, where

S(x)=-\frac{9}{2}x^{3}-10x^{2}+14x+2\sqrt{\frac{(3x^{2}+2x)^{3}}{2}}.

As
(3x^{2}+2x)^{3}>9x^{4}(\sqrt{3}x+\frac{3}{2})^{2}
, we get
S(x)>(3\sqrt{6}-\frac{9}{2})x^{3}-(10-\frac{9}{2}\sqrt{2})x^{2}+14x>0
for
x>0
. Hence
R(x)>0
for
x>0
.
Using this fact in (
17
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(P)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{n\,\theta}{2(\theta+2)}+\frac{n}{2(\theta+2)}\sqrt{\frac{\theta\big(3\,\theta+2\big)}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n\,\theta}{2(\theta+2)}+\frac{n}{2(\theta+2)}\sqrt{\frac{\theta(k\,\theta+2k\,-4)}{2}}~~\mbox{ as }k\geq 3\,.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Note that Theorem
2.4
is better than Theorem
2.3
by taking

s=2
. However, they are incomparable in general.

3.2
Adjacency matrix

In this subsection, we will apply the results obtained in last
section to adjacency matrix of a graph.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
G
be a complete multipartite graph. Then G has three distinct eigenvalues if and only if
G
is complete bipartite or
G\cong K_{\underbrace{t,t,\dots,t}_{p}}
with
p\geq 3
and
t\geq 2
.

Proof.

We first prove the sufficiency. Let
G
be a complete multipartite graph with three distinct eigenvalues. If the negative inertia
\theta
of
G
is
1
, then the rank of
G
is
2
as
\lambda_{1}(G)=\max_{i}{|\lambda_{i}(G)|}
and
\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}\,\lambda_{i}(G)=0
. It follows that
G
is complete bipartite.
Otherwise,
\theta(G)\geq 2
. Without loss of generality, we
assume that
G\cong K_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{p}}
, where
n_{1}=\dots=n_{a_{1}}>n_{a_{1}+1}=\dots=n_{a_{1}+a_{2}}>\dots>n_{a_{1}+\dots+a_{s-1}+1}=\dots=n_{a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}}
,
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}n_{i}=n
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}=p
with
3\leq p\leq n-1
.
The characteristic polynomial of a complete multipartite graph is
given in
[
7
]
, that is,

\displaystyle\Phi(G,x)=x^{n-p}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{p}(x+n_{j})-\sum_{i=1}^{p}\,n_{i}\left(\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}(x+n_{j})\right)\right).

For convenience, let
m_{i}=n_{\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{j}}
.
From the above result, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(G,x)=x^{n-p}\prod_{i=1}^{s}(x+m_{i})^{a_{i}-1}\mathcal{F}(x),

(18)

where
\mathcal{F}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{s}(x+m_{j})-\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}m_{i}\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{s}(x+m_{j})
.
For
s=1
, the required result follows immediately.
For
s=2
, then
a_{1}a_{2}\geq 2
as
p\geq 3
. It follows that
-m_{1}
or
-m_{2}
(or possible both) must be an eigenvalue of
G
. Now we have

\mathcal{F}(-m_{1})=a_{1}m_{1}(m_{1}-m_{2})>0
and

\mathcal{F}(-m_{2})=a_{2}m_{2}(m_{2}-m_{1})<0
. This implies that
G
has
an eigenvalue between
-m_{2}
and
-m_{1}
. In addition,
G
has at
least a positive eigenvalue and an eigenvalue zero. Combining these
results,
G
has at least four distinct eigenvalues, a contradiction.
For
s\geq 3
, we have

\mathcal{F}(-m_{i})=-a_{i}m_{i}\sum\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{s}(m_{j}-m_{i}),

which implies that
\mathcal{F}(-m_{i})\mathcal{F}(-m_{i+1})<0
for
1\leq i\leq s-1
.
Then
G
has at least one eigenvalue between
-m_{i}
and
-m_{i+1}
for

1\leq i\leq 2
. In addition,
G
has at
least a positive eigenvalue and an eigenvalue zero. This
contradicts to the condition that
G
has three distinct
eigenvalue.

Next we prove the necessity. By (
18
), we have

S_{A}(K_{\underbrace{t,t,\dots,t}_{p}})=\Big\{t(p-1),\,\underbrace{0,\,\ldots,\,0}_{tp-p},\,\underbrace{-t,\,\ldots,-t}_{p-1}\Big\}.

(19)

From this, one can see that
K_{\underbrace{t,t,\dots,t}_{p}}
has
three distinct eigenvalues if
p\geq 3
and
t\geq 2
. Moveover, it is well known that a complete bipartite graph has three distinct eigenvalues. This completes the proof.
∎

By taking
t=1
in Theorem
2.2
with Lemma
3.11
, we get the following result:

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 2
with negative inertia
\theta
. Then for any integer
k
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(G)\leq\sqrt{2\,m\left(k-\frac{1}{\theta+1}\right)}

with equality holding if and only if
G\cong K_{p,\,q}
with
n=p+q
and
k=1
or
G\cong K_{\underbrace{t,\,t,\ldots,\,t}_{p}}

(n=p\,t,\,p\geq 3)
with
k=1
.

Proof.

By setting
t=1
and
M=A(G)
in Theorem
2.2
with the facts that
tr(A)=0
and
tr(A^{2})=2m
, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(G)\leq\sqrt{2m\left(k-\frac{1}{\theta+1}\right)}

with equality holding if and only if

with
\lambda_{1}(G)>0\geq\lambda_{2}(G)
, and

\lambda_{n-\theta+1}(G)=\lambda_{n-\theta+2}(G)=\cdots=\lambda_{n}(G)
, that is,

has two or three distinct eigenvalues with the positive inertia 1.
By Lemmas
3.2
and

∎

By setting

in Theorem

Theorem 3.13

Let

be a graph of order

with negative inertia
\theta
. If

, then for every integer

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(G)\leq\sqrt{2\,m\left(k-\frac{4}{\theta+2}\right)}

with equality holding if and only if

with

and

or

(n=2\,p\,t,\,p\geq 3)
with

.

Proof.

If
\theta=n-1
, it is clear that

, which is not in our consideration. Otherwise,
\theta\leq n-2
.
Then we set
t=2
and

in Theorem

, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(G)\leq\sqrt{2m\,\Big(k-\frac{4}{\theta+2}\Big)}~~\mbox{for }k\geq 2.

The equality holds if and only if

with
\lambda_{1}(G)=\lambda_{2}(G)>0\geq\lambda_{3}(G)
and
\lambda_{n-\theta+1}(G)=\lambda_{n-\theta+2}(G)=\cdots=\lambda_{n}(G)
.
Now it remains to characterize the graphs with
\lambda_{1}(G)=\lambda_{2}(G)>0\geq\lambda_{3}(G)

\lambda_{n-\theta+1}(G)=\lambda_{n-\theta+2}(G)=\cdots=\lambda_{n}(G)

By Perron-Frobenius theory, then

is an eigenvalue of
G
with multiplicity

is connected.
As
\lambda_{1}(G)=\lambda_{2}(G)>0\geq\lambda_{3}(G)
, we conclude that

has two connected

. Then both

have two

. Then by Lemmas

As

), we get the required result after

[

3.3
Laplacian matrix

This subsection is dedicated to applying Theorem
2.2
to the Laplacian matrix, yielding several new bounds on the sum of the
k
largest eigenvalues. These results advance the resolution of Brouwer’s conjecture by offering partial solutions through different theoretical avenues.
Now we give the first result on Laplacian spectrum by applying Theorem
2.2
.

Theorem 3.19
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
. For every integer
k
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}(G)\leq\frac{2mk}{n}+\sqrt{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}^{2}+2m-\frac{4m^{2}}{n}\right)\,\left(k-\frac{1}{\theta+1}\right)}

(20)

with equality holding if and only if
G\cong K_{n/2,\,n/2}

(n
is even
)
and

k=1
.

Proof:

By setting
t=1
and
M=L(G)
in Theorem
2.2
with the facts that

tr(L(G))=2m
and
tr((L(G)-\frac{2m}{n}I)^{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}^{2}+2m-\frac{4m^{2}}{n}
, we get the result in (
20
) with equality holding if and only if
k=1
with
\mu_{1}(G)>\frac{2m}{n}=\mu_{2}(G)=\cdots=\mu_{n-\theta}
and

\mu_{n-\theta+1}(G)=\mu_{n-\theta+2}(G)=\cdots=\mu_{n}(G)
, that is,
G
has three distinct
Laplacian eigenvalues with
\mu_{2}(G)=\frac{2m}{n}
.
By Lemma
3.5
, we conclude that
G\cong K_{n/2,\,n/2}
(
n
is even), which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.20
.

One can easily confirm that

tr\left(\Big(L(G)-\frac{tr(L(G))}{n}I\Big)^{2}\right)=tr\left(\Big(Q(G)-\frac{tr(Q(G))}{n}I\Big)^{2}\right),

where
Q(G)
is the signless Laplacian matrix of
G
. Thus, the result in (
20
) also holds for
Q(G)
. Moreover, the result in (
20
) can be improved by setting
t=2
(Lemma
3.5
ensures that
t
can be
2
) in Theorem
2.2
except star graphs.

Next we present the results on the characterization of graphs with three or four distinct Laplacian eigenvalues, subject to specified conditions.

Lemma 3.21
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 3
and size
m
. Then
G
has three distinct Laplacian eigenvalues with
\mu_{1}=n
and
\mu_{2}=\mu_{n-1}<\frac{2m+n}{n}

if and only if

G\cong K_{1,\,n-1}
or
G\cong K_{\frac{n}{2},\,\frac{n}{2}}
with even
n
.

Proof:

We first prove the sufficiency. From the given condition with Lemma
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle S_{L}(\overline{G})=\Big\{\underbrace{n-\mu_{n-1}(G),\,\ldots,\,n-\mu_{n-1}(G)}_{n-2},\,0,\,0\Big\}

(21)

with
\mu_{2}(G)=\mu_{n-1}(G)<\frac{2m+n}{n}
. If
G\cong K_{n}
, then
S_{L}(G)=\Big\{\underbrace{n,\ldots,\,n}_{n-1},\,0\Big\}
, a contradiction as
G
has three distinct Laplacian eigenvalues. Otherwise,
G\ncong K_{n}
. Thus

\mu_{n-1}(G)<n
and hence
n-\mu_{n-1}(G)>0
. From (
21
), we conclude that
\overline{G}
is disconnected and has exactly two connected
complements, say
H_{1}
of order
n_{1}
and
H_{2}
of order
n_{2}
such
that
n_{1}+n_{2}=n
, where
n_{1}\geq n_{2}
. Then

S_{L}(\overline{G})=S_{L}(H_{1})\cup S_{L}(H_{2})
. Then
H_{1}\cong K_{n_{1}}

and
H_{2}\cong K_{n_{2}}
due to the result in (
21
) and the well-known fact that a connected graph with exactly two distinct
Laplacian eigenvalues is a complete graph. For
n_{2}=1
, we obtain
H_{2}\cong K_{1}
and
H_{1}\cong K_{n-1}
, that is,
G\cong K_{1,n-1}
.
We now assume that
n_{2}\geq 2
. Therefore,
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
both have two distinct Laplacian eigenvalues whose nonzero eigenvalues are the same.
Due to the above results, we have
H_{1}\cong K_{n/2}
and
H_{2}\cong K_{n/2}

(n
is even
)
, which follows that
G\cong K_{n/2,\,n/2}

(n
is even
)
.

Next we prove the necessity. If
G\cong K_{1,\,n-1}
, then
\frac{2m+n}{n}=\frac{3n-2}{n}>1

and
S_{L}(G)=\Big\{n,\,\underbrace{1,\ldots,\,1}_{n-2},\,0\Big\}
, which
satisfies the required conditions. Otherwise,
G\cong K_{\frac{n}{2},\,\frac{n}{2}}
. Then
\frac{2m+n}{n}=\frac{n+2}{2}>\frac{n}{2}

and
S_{L}(G)=\Big\{n,\,\underbrace{\frac{n}{2},\ldots,\,\frac{n}{2}}_{n-2},\,0\Big\}
,
again satisfying the required conditions.
∎

Next we define a set of graphs as follows:

•

For positive integers
n
and
t
,
\mathcal{Q}(n,t)
denotes the class of connected graphs of order
n
and size
\frac{(n+1)t}{2}
with exactly three distinct Laplacian eigenvalues and algebraic connectivity equal to
t
.

One can easily confirm that
\mathcal{Q}(n,\,n-2)=\{K_{n-2}\vee 2K_{1}\}
.
Moreover, let
G\cong\overline{aK_{b}\cup cK_{1}}
, where
a,\,b,\,c
are positive integers,
a\geq 2
and
b\geq 3
. By considering the Laplacian spectrum of
\overline{G}
, we obtain that
G
has two distinct nonzero Laplacian eigenvalues:
ab+c
and
(a-1)b+c
. In order to make the graph
G
belongs to

\mathcal{Q}(ab+c,\,(a-1)b+c)
, then
a,\,b,\,c
must satisfy the following equation:

(ab+c)(a+c-1)+(ab+c-b)(ab-a)=(ab+c+1)(ab+c-b),

that is,

Theorem 3.23
.

Let

\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\lambda_{i}(M)\leq\frac{(2m+n)(k+1)}{n}+\sqrt{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}d^{2}_{i}+n^{2}-n-2m-\frac{4m^{2}}{n}\right)\left(k+1-\frac{4}{\theta(M)+2}\right)}

the result in (

Theorem 3.24

Let
