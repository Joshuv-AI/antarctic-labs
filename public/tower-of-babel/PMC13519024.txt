A multi-criteria evaluation of marine pollution systems based on expert-driven analysis and robust ranking methods
Authors: Yongqiang Zhang, Zhe Feng, Chenglin Cao, Wanjun Zhang, Zi Chen Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe selection of effective coastal marine pollution monitoring systems requires integrating multiple technical, economic, and operational considerations. A decision-support approach is developed to compare five monitoring configurations using criteria that capture detection capability, spatial and temporal coverage, cost, robustness, and energy-related constraints. Expert input is obtained from 120 participants, with 110 responses meeting the consistency requirements, yielding an average consistency ratio of 0.064. The calculated weights indicate that detection capability (0.29) and coverage (0.24) are the most important factors in the assessment. The analysis identifies the underwater sensor network as the highest-ranked alternative, achieving a closeness coefficient of 0.56, followed by the cabled coastal observatory (0.53) and mobile autonomous platforms (0.49). The influence of parameter uncertainty is examined through a sensitivity analysis, in which ± 20% variations in criterion weights produce only limited changes in the ranking order. Additional stochastic simulations, based on ± 0.5 perturbations in performance scores over 100 runs, show low variability, with standard deviations below 0.02 across all alternatives. The results demonstrate consistent ranking behavior and highlight the importance of balanced system performance. Introduction

Coastal marine environments are increasingly affected by anthropogenic pollution arising from industrial discharge, urban runoff, maritime activities, and climate-driven processes. The growing intensity and spatial variability of these pressures require monitoring systems that are not only accurate but also adaptable to dynamic environmental conditions. Effective monitoring is therefore a prerequisite for early detection of contamination, mitigation of environmental risks, and sustainable coastal management1,2.

Recent technological advances have significantly expanded the range of available monitoring solutions. These include autonomous sensing platforms, distributed underwater networks, cabled observatories, and satellite-based systems3,4. Each configuration provides distinct capabilities, yet none offers a comprehensive solution across all operational dimensions. For instance, recent studies highlight that underwater sensor networks provide high-resolution, continuous data streams but entail significant deployment complexity and cost5,6. Autonomous platforms enable adaptive sampling and large-area coverage, although their operational reliability remains constrained by energy limitations and environmental uncertainty7,8. Similarly, satellite-based monitoring has been shown to support large-scale observation, but its effectiveness is limited in subsurface detection and fine-scale pollutant characterization9,10.

The coexistence of multiple monitoring technologies with competing strengths introduces a complex decision problem. The selection of an appropriate monitoring system cannot rely on a single performance indicator, as improvements in one dimension are often associated with trade-offs in others. This complexity necessitates structured decision-support approaches that integrate multiple criteria and stakeholder perspectives. Multi-criteria decision-making methods have been widely applied to the evaluation of environmental systems. In particular, the Analytic Hierarchy Process and the Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution are frequently adopted for their ability to incorporate expert knowledge and provide interpretable rankings. Recent applications in environmental monitoring and infrastructure selection demonstrate the effectiveness of hybrid approaches that combine weighting and ranking mechanisms11,12.

However, several limitations remain in the current body of research. First, many studies focus on individual monitoring technologies rather than providing a comprehensive comparison across alternative system configurations. Second, the justification for the selection and weighting of the criteria is often insufficiently discussed, reducing transparency. Third, robustness analysis is frequently limited or absent, despite the inherent uncertainty associated with expert-based evaluations. Finally, comparative validation against baseline methods is rarely performed, making it difficult to assess the added value of the proposed approaches. To address these limitations, a structured evaluation framework is developed for the comparative assessment of coastal marine pollution monitoring systems. The framework integrates expert elicitation, multi-criteria weighting, and distance-based ranking, and is complemented by sensitivity and stochastic analyses to evaluate robustness. The approach aims to provide a transparent, reproducible, and adaptable decision-support tool for selecting coastal monitoring systems.

Advances in coastal monitoring technologies

Recent research has emphasized the rapid evolution of monitoring technologies in marine environments, particularly with the integration of sensing, communication, and data analytics capabilities. Distributed underwater sensor networks have emerged as a key innovation, enabling continuous monitoring of environmental parameters with high spatial resolution. Studies demonstrate that such systems significantly improve detection accuracy and data continuity compared to traditional approaches13,14. However, their deployment is associated with challenges related to energy consumption, communication reliability, and maintenance. Autonomous platforms, including underwater vehicles and unmanned surface systems, have gained increasing attention due to their flexibility and adaptability. These systems can perform dynamic sampling and respond to transient pollution events. Recent work highlights their effectiveness in complex coastal environments, although limitations in navigation accuracy and operational costs remain significant15.

Cabled observatories represent a more stable, infrastructure-intensive solution, providing continuous power and real-time data transmission. They are particularly suitable for long-term monitoring applications, where reliability and data continuity are critical. However, their scalability is limited due to high installation costs and geographical constraints16. Satellite-based remote sensing has also advanced significantly, offering large-scale monitoring capabilities and frequent data acquisition. Recent studies show improvements in spatial resolution and data processing techniques, although challenges remain in detecting subsurface pollutants and distinguishing between different contamination sources17.

Application of multi-criteria decision methods in environmental monitoring

Multi-criteria decision-making methods have been widely applied in environmental management to support complex decision processes involving multiple conflicting objectives. Among these methods, AHP is commonly used to derive criteria weights based on expert judgments, while TOPSIS is applied to rank alternatives based on their proximity to the ideal solution. Recent studies demonstrate the effectiveness of hybrid AHP–TOPSIS approaches in infrastructure selection, environmental planning, and resource management18. These approaches provide a balance between methodological rigor and practical applicability, allowing decision-makers to integrate qualitative and quantitative information.

However, the application of these methods in coastal monitoring remains limited. Existing studies often focus on specific case studies or narrow sets of alternatives, and do not fully explore the diversity of available monitoring technologies. In addition, the treatment of uncertainty is frequently simplified, with limited attention given to sensitivity analysis or stochastic evaluation.

The limitations identified in existing studies are addressed by developing a structured and transparent evaluation framework for coastal marine pollution monitoring systems. The framework combines expert-driven criteria weighting with a distance-based ranking procedure and incorporates both deterministic and stochastic analyses to examine the stability of the results under uncertainty. In contrast to prior work, several methodological contributions are introduced. A broader, more representative set of monitoring configurations is considered, enabling a more comprehensive assessment of available technologies. The selection and definition of evaluation criteria are explicitly justified by both literature evidence and expert validation, thereby improving transparency and reproducibility. A systematic robustness analysis is implemented through both controlled weight perturbations and stochastic variations in performance assessments, providing deeper insight into the stability of the ranking outcomes. In addition, a comparative evaluation against baseline methods is conducted to assess the influence of the adopted approach and to demonstrate its discriminative capability. From a novelty perspective, the contribution lies in integrating multiple validation layers into a single decision framework, including consistency verification, dual-mode sensitivity analysis, and comparative benchmarking. This integrated structure enables a more reliable and interpretable evaluation of monitoring systems and advances the application of multi-criteria decision methods in complex coastal environments.

Theoretical framework

A structured decision framework is established to support the systematic evaluation of configurations for coastal marine pollution monitoring. The overall process is organized as a sequence of interrelated stages, beginning with the definition of the decision context and the identification of feasible monitoring alternatives based on literature evidence and expert knowledge. This is followed by the formulation of a set of evaluation criteria that capture technical performance, operational constraints, and economic considerations. Expert judgments are then elicited through a designed survey instrument, and consistency screening is applied to ensure the reliability of the responses. The importance of criteria is determined through pairwise comparison procedures, while performance assessments are aggregated into a decision matrix for further analysis. A ranking mechanism based on distance measures is subsequently applied to determine the relative preference of the alternatives. Identification and critical review of monitoring alternatives

The selection of monitoring system configurations is grounded in a structured review of existing technological approaches reported in the scientific literature, complemented by expert consultation to ensure practical relevance. The review considers peer-reviewed studies indexed in major academic databases, including Google Scholar, Scopus, and Web of Science, in order to improve traceability and methodological rigor. Search queries combine terms related to coastal monitoring, marine pollution sensing, underwater communication, and ocean observation systems. A critical review of the literature indicates that most contributions emphasize individual technologies or system descriptions, whereas comparatively few studies provide systematic comparisons among alternative monitoring architectures. In addition, emerging solutions are often underrepresented due to limited disclosure or industrial constraints, potentially biasing the selection toward established technologies. To address this limitation, the identification of alternatives is guided not only by publication frequency but also by technological maturity and applicability in realistic coastal environments. Based on this process, five representative monitoring configurations are defined.

The first configuration corresponds to moored buoy networks, consisting of fixed surface platforms equipped with environmental sensors and communication systems. These systems are widely used in coastal observatories due to their robustness and relatively low operational cost. Previous studies demonstrate their effectiveness for continuous monitoring of surface conditions and pollutant indicators19,20. The second configuration enables adaptive spatial sampling and is particularly suitable for tracking transient pollution events. However, their deployment is often associated with higher operational complexity and maintenance requirements21,22.

The third configuration represents distributed underwater sensor networks, in which multiple sensing nodes are deployed across the marine environment and interconnected via acoustic communication. These systems provide high spatial resolution and continuous monitoring capability but require careful design to address communication and energy constraints23,24. The fourth configuration is based on cabled coastal observatories, in which sensor arrays are connected to shore via dedicated power and communication infrastructure. These systems provide a continuous energy supply and high data reliability, making them suitable for long-term monitoring programs. However, their deployment is constrained by high installation costs and limited spatial flexibility25,26.

The fifth configuration incorporates satellite-based remote sensing systems that use Earth observation platforms to monitor large-scale patterns of surface pollution. These systems enable extensive spatial coverage and frequent data acquisition, although their effectiveness for subsurface processes is limited and may be affected by atmospheric conditions27. Development and justification of evaluation criteria

Defining evaluation criteria is a critical step in multi-criteria decision analysis, as it determines the dimensions along which alternatives are compared and directly influences the final ranking. In this framework, criteria are established through a combined process of literature synthesis and expert validation, with the objective of capturing the key technical, economic, and operational aspects of coastal marine pollution monitoring systems.

A structured review of prior studies on marine observation technologies, environmental monitoring systems, and sensor network design is conducted using sources indexed in Google Scholar, Scopus, and Web of Science. The literature indicates that monitoring system performance is typically evaluated across multiple dimensions, including detection capability, spatial and temporal coverage, system reliability, cost efficiency, and operational sustainability21,22,24. However, many studies treat these dimensions independently and do not provide a unified framework for their joint evaluation.

To address this gap, candidate criteria are first extracted from the literature and then refined through expert consultation to ensure clarity, non-redundancy, and practical relevance. The final set of criteria is selected based on three conditions. First, each criterion must represent a distinct aspect of system performance. Second, it must be applicable across all considered alternatives. Third, it must be interpretable and assessable by domain experts. Based on this process, five evaluation criteria are defined and used throughout the analysis. The relationships between the alternatives and the criteria are illustrated in Fig. 2, which provides a conceptual representation of the evaluation structure.

The selected monitoring configurations, including fixed, mobile, network-based, cabled, and remote sensing systems, are linked to key evaluation dimensions that capture performance, cost, and operational characteristics.

The first criterion, detection capability, reflects a monitoring system’s ability to accurately identify and quantify pollution indicators. This dimension is widely recognized as fundamental in environmental monitoring, as it directly determines the reliability of observed data and the effectiveness of subsequent management decisions22. Systems with higher detection capability are expected to provide more precise and timely information on pollutant presence and concentration. The second criterion, spatiotemporal coverage, captures the extent and resolution of monitoring across both space and time. Coastal environments are characterized by heterogeneous and dynamic pollution patterns, which require sufficient spatial distribution of sensors and adequate sampling frequency. Previous studies have shown that insufficient coverage can lead to incomplete or biased observations21. The third criterion, life cycle cost, represents the total economic burden associated with system acquisition, deployment, operation, maintenance, and eventual decommissioning. Cost considerations are essential in long-term monitoring programs, where budget constraints often limit the feasibility of technologically advanced solutions. The importance of cost efficiency in environmental monitoring system design is widely discussed in the literature19. The fourth criterion, energy autonomy and maintenance demand, reflects the system’s operational sustainability. This includes power consumption, maintenance frequency, and the logistical effort required for system upkeep. In marine environments, where access may be limited and conditions are harsh, systems with higher energy efficiency and lower maintenance requirements are generally preferred24. Finally, robustness is essential for ensuring long term data continuity and minimizing system failure rates. Previous research highlights the importance of reliability in the design of ocean observation systems25.

Analytical foundations of the multi-criteria approach

The evaluation of coastal marine pollution monitoring systems is based on a multi-criteria decision-making framework that integrates heterogeneous information, including expert judgments, qualitative assessments, and quantitative indicators. Such problems are characterized by multiple conflicting criteria, the absence of a single objective performance measure, and the need to incorporate both subjective and structured inputs. Within this context, multi-criteria decision methods provide a systematic approach for organizing information and deriving a consistent ranking of alternatives.

Among the available approaches, TOPSIS, ELECTRE, AHP, and PROMETHEE are commonly applied to environmental and engineering decision-making problems. Each method is based on different theoretical assumptions and aggregation mechanisms. For instance, outranking methods such as ELECTRE and PROMETHEE allow partial preference relations and are suitable for non-compensatory decision contexts, but they often require complex parameter calibration and may be less intuitive for decision makers. Weighted sum models provide simplicity but assume full compensability among criteria and do not explicitly account for distance from ideal solutions.

The integrated AHP–TOPSIS approach is selected in this framework due to its complementary strengths. Comparative framework and baseline method selection

To assess the effectiveness of the proposed multi-criteria evaluation framework, a comparative analysis is conducted against selected baseline methods. The inclusion of baseline approaches provides a reference for interpreting the results and allows evaluation of whether the adopted method offers meaningful advantages in ranking consistency, interpretability, and robustness. Two widely used baseline approaches are considered in this framework. The first is the simple weighted-sum model, in which the overall score for each alternative is computed as a linear aggregation of its performance across all criteria. The second baseline approach is a criteria-ranking method based solely on AHP-derived weights, in which alternatives are compared using weighted scores without considering their distance from the ideal and negative-ideal solutions. While this approach incorporates expert preferences through weighting, it lacks a geometric interpretation of performance and may be less sensitive to differences among alternatives.

The resulting rankings are then analyzed to identify similarities and differences in the ordering of alternatives. In addition, the relative dispersion of scores is examined to evaluate the discriminative power of each method. The inclusion of a distance based ranking procedure provides several advantages over the baseline methods. First, it considers both the best and worst possible performance levels, which allows a more balanced evaluation of alternatives. Second, it reduces the likelihood that an alternative with strong performance on a limited set of criteria will dominate the ranking despite weaknesses in other dimensions. Third, it offers a clearer interpretation of results by expressing the relative closeness of each alternative to an ideal solution.

At the same time, the comparative analysis shows that the ranking outcomes are generally consistent across methods when performance differences are pronounced. However, discrepancies may arise when alternatives exhibit similar performance levels or when trade-offs between criteria are significant. These differences provide insight into the evaluation framework’s sensitivity and help identify conditions under which the choice of method may influence the final decision. It is important to note that the purpose of the comparative analysis is not to demonstrate superiority in absolute terms, but to provide a transparent assessment of methodological behavior under identical input conditions. This approach enhances the credibility of the results and addresses concerns regarding the justification of the selected method.

Methodological assumptions and limitations

The proposed evaluation framework is based on a set of methodological assumptions that should be explicitly recognized when interpreting the results. While the framework provides a structured and transparent approach to comparing monitoring system configurations, it inevitably involves simplifying a complex decision context. A fundamental assumption concerns the independence of evaluation criteria. The model treats each criterion as a distinct dimension of performance, enabling aggregation via weighted procedures. However, in practical applications, certain criteria may exhibit interdependencies. For example, improvements in spatial coverage may require higher energy consumption or increased cost, while enhanced robustness may influence maintenance requirements. Such relationships are not explicitly modeled within the current framework and may lead to partial oversimplification of system behavior.

Another important assumption relates to the use of expert judgments as primary input data. The criteria weights and performance scores are derived from subjective evaluations provided by domain specialists. Although consistency checks are applied to improve reliability, these judgments may still reflect individual experience, disciplinary background, or regional context. Differences in institutional priorities, funding structures, and technological familiarity may influence the assessment of alternatives. While the diversity of the expert panel mitigates some of these effects, the possibility of systematic bias cannot be entirely excluded. The aggregation of expert responses into a single representative matrix introduces an additional level of abstraction. Averaging procedures are employed to obtain group-level values, which improves stability and interpretability but may obscure variability among respondents. As a result, minority perspectives or divergent opinions are not explicitly represented in the final evaluation.

The selection of monitoring alternatives also represents a limitation. Although the framework incorporates a broader set of configurations, including emerging and established technologies, the choice of alternatives is constrained by available information and expert knowledge. Certain innovative or proprietary solutions may not be fully captured due to limited public documentation or restricted access. This may bias the evaluation toward more widely studied or mature systems. The reliance on literature sources for defining alternatives and criteria introduces further considerations. While multiple databases are consulted to improve coverage, variations in indexing, publication practices, and disciplinary focus may influence the representation of technologies. As a result, the identified alternatives may not fully reflect the most recent developments in industrial or applied contexts.

From a methodological perspective, the ranking procedure assumes compensatory behavior among criteria, meaning that lower performance in one dimension can be offset by higher performance in another. This assumption may not fully align with decision contexts in which certain criteria serve as strict constraints or thresholds. In addition, the use of Euclidean distance implies that all criteria contribute independently to the overall evaluation, which may not capture more complex interactions. The sensitivity analysis provides a partial means of addressing these limitations by examining the stability of results under variations in weights and performance values. However, it does not fully account for structural uncertainties, such as alternative model formulations or different decision rules.

Despite these limitations, the framework offers a transparent and reproducible approach for evaluating coastal monitoring systems. The explicit acknowledgment of assumptions and potential sources of bias enhances the interpretability of the results and provides a foundation for future extensions. Possible improvements include incorporating objective performance data, using non-compensatory decision models, and integrating uncertainty-based approaches to better reflect the complexity of real-world decision-making.

Numerical results

Consistency assessment of expert judgments

The reliability of the expert-derived pairwise comparisons is evaluated through a systematic consistency assessment prior to any aggregation or weight derivation. This step is essential in multi-criteria decision analysis, as inconsistencies in individual judgments may propagate into unstable criteria weights and compromise the robustness of the final ranking. A total of 120 complete responses is obtained, each containing a full set of pairwise comparisons across the five evaluation criteria. For each response, the consistency ratio is computed and compared against the widely accepted threshold of 0.10 in multi-criteria analysis to assess acceptable logical coherence in judgment matrices. Responses exceeding this threshold are considered to exhibit excessive inconsistency and are excluded from further analysis.

Following this screening procedure, 110 matrices satisfy the consistency requirement, while 10 matrices are removed. This corresponds to a retention rate of approximately 91.7%, indicating a high level of reliability in the elicited expert judgments. The exclusion of inconsistent responses reduces the influence of contradictory or random comparisons and enhances the stability of the aggregated preference structure.

The distribution of consistency ratios for the retained matrices provides further insight into the dataset’s quality. The mean consistency ratio is 0.064, with a range of 0.011 to 0.098. These values are well below the accepted threshold, and the relatively low mean indicates that, on average, the judgments are close to fully consistent. The narrow dispersion of values further suggests that consistency is not limited to a subset of respondents but is broadly maintained across the expert panel. Discussion and practical implications

The results of the evaluation provide important insights into the relative effectiveness of different coastal marine pollution monitoring configurations and their suitability under varying operational and decision contexts. The findings demonstrate that monitoring system selection is inherently a multi-dimensional problem in which performance, cost, and operational constraints must be balanced in a structured manner. The dominance of the underwater sensor network (A₃) observed in the results is consistent with recent developments in marine monitoring technologies. Studies focusing on underwater Internet of Things (UIoT) and distributed sensing architectures emphasize their ability to provide high-resolution, continuous data acquisition and improved detection capabilities28. Similarly, recent applications of integrated AHP–TOPSIS frameworks in coastal monitoring contexts report that sensor network–based systems achieve the most balanced performance29.

At the same time, the results highlight the limitations of relying solely on advanced technologies. Although A₃ achieves the highest ranking, its performance advantage is partially offset under cost-sensitive scenarios, where A₄ becomes competitive. This observation is consistent with recent literature emphasizing that high-performance monitoring systems often involve increased deployment and maintenance costs, which may limit their practical implementation in resource-constrained environments30. Autonomous vehicles are widely recognized for their flexibility and adaptability in dynamic marine environments, yet their operational complexity and energy requirements remain limiting factors. Recent studies on marine robotics and autonomous monitoring confirm that these systems are highly effective for targeted and adaptive monitoring, but may not provide the same level of stability and continuity as fixed or network-based solutions31.

In contrast, satellite-based remote sensing systems (A₅), despite their strong spatial coverage, exhibit limitations in detection accuracy and in subsurface monitoring. This finding is supported by recent reviews of remote sensing technologies, which highlight challenges related to resolution, atmospheric interference, and limited capability to detect underwater pollutants32. As a result, such systems are increasingly viewed as complementary tools rather than standalone solutions.

Methodological implications

From a methodological perspective, the results demonstrate the effectiveness of integrating structured expert judgment with distance-based evaluation methods. The combination of AHP and TOPSIS provides a transparent mechanism for integrating qualitative and quantitative information, which is particularly important in complex environmental decision-making problems.

Recent studies emphasize that hybrid multi-criteria decision-making approaches offer improved interpretability and robustness compared to single-method frameworks. For instance, the integration of AHP–TOPSIS has been shown to enhance decision transparency and facilitate the evaluation of complex systems involving multiple conflicting criteria33. The present findings confirm this advantage, as the method not only produces consistent rankings but also allows for a clear interpretation of each criterion’s contribution. The sensitivity and stochastic analyses further highlight the importance of robustness assessment in multi-criteria evaluation. The observed stability of the ranking under both deterministic and stochastic perturbations indicates that the proposed framework is resilient to moderate variations in input data. This is particularly relevant in expert-driven contexts, where uncertainty and variability are inherent.

However, the results also reveal that methodological assumptions, such as criteria independence and compensatory aggregation, can influence outcomes. The observed shifts in ranking under cost-dominated scenarios illustrate that alternative decision rules or non-compensatory models may lead to different conclusions. This observation supports recent research advocating the integration of advanced or hybrid approaches, such as fuzzy or non-linear models, to better capture uncertainty and interdependencies in environmental decision-making33,34.

Practical implications for coastal monitoring design

The findings of this study provide several practical implications for decision-makers involved in designing and implementing coastal monitoring systems. First, the results suggest that no single monitoring configuration is universally optimal. Instead, the choice of system should be aligned with specific operational objectives and resource constraints. For applications requiring high detection accuracy and continuous monitoring, network-based underwater systems represent the most suitable option. However, in cost-sensitive contexts, more stable, infrastructure-based solutions, such as cabled observatories, may be a more feasible alternative.

Second, the observed trade-offs between performance and cost highlight the need for hybrid monitoring strategies. Combining different system types, such as integrating satellite observations within situ sensor networks or autonomous platforms, can enhance overall monitoring effectiveness while balancing resource requirements. This approach is increasingly supported by recent research, which emphasizes integrating multiple sensing technologies to address the limitations of individual systems.

Third, the importance of criteria such as detection capability and coverage underscores the need to design monitoring systems with a strong focus on data quality and representativeness. Investments in advanced sensing technologies and improved spatial deployment strategies are therefore critical for enhancing monitoring effectiveness.

Fourth, the robustness analysis demonstrates that decision outcomes are relatively stable under moderate variations in input parameters. This provides practitioners with confidence in applying the proposed framework to real-world scenarios, as the results are not highly sensitive to small changes in expert judgments or performance assessments.

Finally, the framework offers a practical decision-support tool that can be adapted to different geographical contexts and policy objectives. By modifying the weights of criteria or including additional alternatives, decision-makers can tailor the evaluation to specific environmental, economic, or regulatory conditions.

Limitations and future research directions

While the results provide valuable insights, several limitations should be considered. The reliance on expert judgments introduces subjectivity, and although consistency filtering and robustness analysis mitigate this issue, it cannot be fully eliminated. In addition, the selected alternatives may not fully capture emerging or proprietary technologies that are not widely reported in the literature. Future research could address these limitations by incorporating objective performance data, expanding the set of alternatives, and exploring advanced decision-making models that account for uncertainty and interdependencies among criteria. The integration of real-time data analytics, artificial intelligence, and adaptive monitoring systems also represents a promising direction, as recent studies highlight the growing role of smart and autonomous technologies in marine environmental monitoring35.

Conclusions

A structured multi criteria evaluation framework is developed to assess coastal marine pollution monitoring systems under competing technical, economic, and operational conditions. The framework integrates expert-based weighting with a distance-based ranking approach, supported by deterministic and stochastic robustness analyses. A total of 120 expert responses are collected, of which 110 satisfy the required consistency threshold, with an average consistency ratio of 0.064 and values ranging from 0.011 to 0.098, indicating a high level of reliability in the elicited judgments.

The derived criteria weights indicate a clear prioritization of performance-related aspects. Criterion C1 (0.29) and criterion C2 (0.24) together represent more than half of the total weight. These are followed by criterion C3 (0.19) and criterion C4 (0.15). The results show that effectiveness is given greater importance than purely economic factors.

The ranking results indicate that A₃ achieves the highest closeness coefficient (0.56), followed by A₄ at 0.53 and A₂ at 0.49. A₅ and A₁ obtain lower values of 0.46 and 0.43, respectively. These results demonstrate that the preferred configuration is characterized by balanced performance across multiple criteria rather than dominance in a single dimension.

Robustness analysis confirms the stability of the ranking outcomes. Under ± 20% variations in criteria weights, A₃ retains the top position in most scenarios, while A₄ becomes preferable only under cost-dominated conditions. Stochastic perturbation of performance scores within a ± 0.5 range over 100 simulations yields average closeness coefficients of 0.561 for A₃ and 0.538 for A₄, with A₃ ranked first in 78% of cases. The variation in closeness values remains limited, with standard deviations below 0.02 for all alternatives, indicating low sensitivity to input uncertainty.

Comparative analysis with baseline methods shows consistent ranking results across approaches, while the adopted framework demonstrates greater discriminative capability, with a score range of 0.13 compared to approximately 0.02 for linear aggregation methods. This confirms that the proposed approach enhances the differentiation between alternatives without altering the overall decision outcome.
