A Trust-Region Interior-Point Stochastic Sequential Quadratic Programming Method
Yuchen Fang, Jihun Kim, Sen Na, James Demmel, Javad Lavaei

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a trust-region interior-point stochastic sequential quadratic programming (TR-IP-SSQP) method for solving optimization problems with a stochastic objective and deterministic nonlinear equality and inequality constraints. In this setting, exact evaluations of the objective function and its gradient are unavailable, but their stochastic estimates can be constructed. In particular, at each iteration our method builds stochastic oracles, which estimate the objective value and gradient to satisfy proper adaptive accuracy conditions with a fixed probability. To handle inequality constraints, we adopt an interior-point method (IPM), in which the barrier parameter follows a prescribed decaying sequence. Under standard assumptions, we establish global almost-sure convergence of the proposed method to first-order stationary points. We implement the method on a subset of problems from the CUTEst test set, as well as on logistic regression problems, to demonstrate its practical performance.



keywords

constrained stochastic optimization, nonlinear optimization, trust-region method, interior-point method, sequential quadratic programming

Introduction

We consider solving optimization problems of the form

\min_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\;f({\boldsymbol{x}})=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathcal{P}}}[F({\boldsymbol{x}};\xi)],\quad\;\text{s.t.}\;\;c({\boldsymbol{x}})={\boldsymbol{0}},\quad h({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq{\boldsymbol{0}},

(1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuously differentiable and defined as the expectation of a stochastic realization
F({\boldsymbol{x}};\xi)
. The functions
c:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
represent continuously differentiable deterministic equality and inequality constraints, respectively. The random variable
\xi
follows distribution
{\mathcal{P}}
. We assume that neither the objective value
f({\boldsymbol{x}})
nor its gradient
\nabla f({\boldsymbol{x}})
can be evaluated exactly, but must instead be estimated via sampling. Problem (
1
) arises in a wide range of applications, including optimal control, constrained machine learning, and safe reinforcement learning
[
8
,
15
,
1
,
17
]
.

Interior-point methods (IPMs) constitute one of the most effective frameworks for solving inequality-constrained optimization problems. They have demonstrated remarkable success in deterministic optimization and form the foundation of widely used solvers such as Ipopt
[
43
]
, Knitro
[
12
]
, and LOQO
[
42
]
. Numerous studies have integrated interior-point techniques within deterministic optimization frameworks
[
11
,
10
,
23
,
43
]
, as well as in deterministic settings with noisy function evaluations
[
18
]
.

Extensions to stochastic optimization have also emerged in recent years. For example,
[
2
,
36
]
developed IPMs for minimizing linear functions over convex sets. Subsequent work has expanded settings to nonconvex stochastic optimization. In particular,
[
20
]
investigated bound-constrained problems, which was later extended in
[
19
]
to incorporate linear equality and nonlinear inequality constraints. Under suitable decay conditions on the barrier parameter, these methods establish liminf-type almost-sure convergence guarantees and demonstrate favorable empirical performance.

Despite these advances, existing approaches
[
20
,
19
]
still leave room for further refinement. As gradient-based methods, they may face challenges in effectively handling nonlinear constraints; and their analyses also rely on unbiased gradient estimators with bounded variance, which restricts the range of admissible sampling mechanisms. Moreover, these methods involve multiple interdependent parameter sequences, potentially increasing the effort of practical tuning; and they also enforce strict feasibility at every iteration, thereby necessitating auxiliary procedures to obtain a feasible initial point. Such requirements may introduce additional complexity in both theoretical analysis and implementation, as noted in deterministic optimization settings
[
44
]
.

To address nonlinear constrained optimization, Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) and its variants are among the most effective techniques in the deterministic setting
[
9
,
13
,
30
]
. By performing local linearizations of the constraints at each iteration, SQP methods exhibit a strong capability for handling nonlinear constraints. Motivated by the large scale and intrinsic uncertainty of modern applications, there has been substantial interest in Stochastic SQP (SSQP) methods. In particular, recent adaptive-sampling SSQP methods
[
33
,
34
,
40
,
27
,
41
,
5
,
25
]
eliminate the need for unbiased objective estimates. Instead, they construct
stochastic oracles
that enforce objective estimates to satisfy adaptive accuracy conditions with a fixed probability. Such oracles encompass a broader range of sampling mechanisms and problem settings.

Despite significant progress for equality-constrained problems, SSQP methods for nonlinear inequality-constrained optimization are still relatively limited. Existing approaches are predominantly built on line-search methods
[
22
,
34
,
40
,
3
]
, while the designs built on trust-region methods remain largely open.
Compared with line-search methods, trust-region methods offer several advantages: the step direction and step length are computed simultaneously, often enhancing robustness
[
37
, Chapter 4]
. Moreover, trust-region methods naturally accommodate indefinite Hessian approximations, enabling the direct utilization of curvature information. In contrast, line-search methods typically require positive definiteness of the Hessian approximation, thereby necessitating explicit Hessian modifications.

In this work, we develop a trust-region interior-point SSQP (TR-IP-SSQP) method with adaptive sampling to solve nonlinear inequality-constrained optimization problems. Our contributions are summarized as follows:

1.

We extend the trust-region SSQP method in
[
27
]
to nonlinear inequality-constrained optimization. This extension is nontrivial. Interior-point methods require that the slack variables must remain
deterministically
positive, whereas their updates are
stochastic
. To address this issue, we modify the step computation to explicitly incorporate the update of slack variables and introduce a fraction-to-boundary condition to guarantee the positivity requirements. While such a condition is standard in deterministic interior-point methods
[
11
,
10
]
, to the best of our knowledge, this is its first incorporation within stochastic optimization.

2.

Compared with existing stochastic interior-point methods for nonlinear optimization
[
19
,
20
]
, our method adopts an adaptive sampling strategy, thereby permitting biased objective estimates and allowing gradient estimates with unbounded variance. Furthermore, our method is developed within a
relaxed-feasibility
interior-point framework: strict feasibility of the constraints is not enforced at every iteration, and thus no auxiliary procedure is required to compute a feasible initial point. In addition, the method eliminates the need for multiple interdependent input sequences and imposes no conditions on the decay rate of the barrier parameter, while the approaches in
[
19
,
20
]
require carefully controlled barrier-parameter decay to guarantee convergence.

3.

Our algorithm is built upon a trust-region SSQP framework rather than line-search-based SSQP methods
[
34
,
40
]
or gradient-based approaches
[
19
,
20
]
. The integration of trust-region and SSQP techniques enables the direct use of second-order (Hessian) information without explicit matrix modifications and provides improved handling of nonlinear objective and constraint structures. The availability of second-order information can also lead to enhanced practical performance, particularly in nonconvex optimization settings.

Under the assumption that the objective estimates satisfy proper adaptive accuracy conditions with a high but fixed probability, we establish
global almost-sure convergence
, showing that a subsequence of iterates converges almost surely to first-order stationary points. This result aligns with existing guarantees for inequality-constrained stochastic optimization
[
34
,
19
,
20
]
. We implement our algorithm on a subset of CUTEst datasets
[
28
]
and logistics regression problems to demonstrate its empirical performance.

2
Preliminaries

The Lagrangian of Problem (
1
) is given by
{\mathcal{L}}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}})=f({\boldsymbol{x}})+{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{T}c({\boldsymbol{x}})+{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{T}h({\boldsymbol{x}})
, where
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
denote the Lagrange multipliers associated with constraints
c({\boldsymbol{x}})
and
h({\boldsymbol{x}})
, respectively. Under standard constraint qualifications, such as linear independence constraint qualification (LICQ), finding a first-order stationary point of Problem (
1
) is equivalent to finding a triple
({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{*},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{*})
satisfying the KKT conditions:

\displaystyle\nabla f({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*})+G({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*})^{T}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{*}+J({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*})^{T}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{*}

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{0}},\quad c({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*})={\boldsymbol{0}},

(2)

\displaystyle h({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*})\leq{\boldsymbol{0}},\quad{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{*}

\displaystyle\geq{\boldsymbol{0}},\quad{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{*}^{(i)}h^{(i)}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*})=0,\;i=1,2,\cdots,n.

Following the interior-point paradigm, we associate Problem (
1
) with a barrier problem by introducing a slack variable
{\boldsymbol{s}}=({\boldsymbol{s}}^{(1)},{\boldsymbol{s}}^{(2)},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{s}}^{(n)})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and augmenting the objective with a log-barrier term, where
\theta>0
is a barrier parameter:

\displaystyle\min_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},{\boldsymbol{s}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\theta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln{\boldsymbol{s}}^{(i)}

(3)

\displaystyle\text{s.t. }

\displaystyle c({\boldsymbol{x}})=0,\quad h({\boldsymbol{x}})+{\boldsymbol{s}}=0,\quad{\boldsymbol{s}}^{(i)}>0,\;\forall i=1,2,\cdots,n.

Our algorithm repeatedly solves the barrier problem (
3
) approximately, while driving the barrier parameter
\theta
to zero. The Lagrangian of Problem (
3
) is
{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{s}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}})=f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\theta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln{\boldsymbol{s}}^{(i)}+{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{T}c({\boldsymbol{x}})+{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{T}(h({\boldsymbol{x}})+{\boldsymbol{s}})
. For a given barrier parameter
\theta
, a first-order stationary point of (
3
) is a quadruple
({\boldsymbol{x}}_{\theta},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{\theta},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\theta},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\theta})
satisfying

\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{\theta},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{\theta},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\theta},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\theta})=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla f({\boldsymbol{x}}_{\theta})+G({\boldsymbol{x}}_{\theta})^{T}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\theta}+J({\boldsymbol{x}}_{\theta})^{T}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\theta}\\
-\theta S_{\theta}^{-1}\boldsymbol{e}+{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\theta}\\
c({\boldsymbol{x}}_{\theta})\\
h({\boldsymbol{x}}_{\theta})+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{\theta}\end{pmatrix}={\boldsymbol{0}},

(4)

where
\boldsymbol{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the all-one vector and
S_{\theta}=\text{diag}({\boldsymbol{s}}_{\theta}^{(1)},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{\theta}^{(2)},\cdots,{\boldsymbol{s}}_{\theta}^{(n)})
.

In deterministic optimization, interior-point methods are typically implemented using a nested-loop structure
[
11
,
10
,
38
,
44
]
. The outer loop reduces the barrier parameter, while the inner loop approximately solves (
3
) for a fixed
\theta
until a stationarity condition, such as
\|\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{s}},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\|\leq\epsilon
, is satisfied. In the stochastic setting, however, exact evaluations of the objective, gradient, and KKT residuals are unavailable, rendering such termination criteria unreliable and complicating the nested-loop design. To address this issue, we adopt a single-loop framework in which the barrier parameter follows a predetermined diminishing sequence
\{\theta_{k}\}\downarrow 0
.

At iteration
k
, we denote
\nabla f_{k}=\nabla f({\boldsymbol{x}}_{k})
,
{\bar{g}}_{k}={\bar{g}}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{k})
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{k}}={\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{k}}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k})
, where
\theta_{k}
is the current barrier parameter. We define
c_{k},h_{k},G_{k},J_{k},S_{k}
analogously. Throughout the paper, we assume that
G_{k}=\nabla c({\boldsymbol{x}}_{k})^{T}
has full row rank, as stated in Assumption
6
, and define the
true
Lagrange multipliers at iteration
k
as

{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k}=\theta_{k}S_{k}^{-1}\boldsymbol{e},\qquad{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}=-[G_{k}G_{k}^{T}]^{-1}G_{k}\bigl(\nabla f_{k}+\theta_{k}J_{k}^{T}S_{k}^{-1}\boldsymbol{e}\bigr).

(5)

2.1
TR-IP-SSQP subproblem

At iteration
k
, given the current iterate
({\boldsymbol{x}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k})
, the barrier parameter
\theta_{k}
, and the trust-region radius
\Delta_{k}
, we formulate a TR-IP-SSQP subproblem for Problem (
3
) as

\displaystyle\min_{\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bar{g}}_{k}^{T}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}-\theta_{k}\boldsymbol{e}^{T}S_{k}^{-1}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}^{T}{\bar{H}}_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\theta_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{T}S_{k}^{-2}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}

(6)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle c_{k}+G_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}={\boldsymbol{0}},\quad h_{k}+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}+J_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}+\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}={\boldsymbol{0}},

\displaystyle\|(\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k};S_{k}^{-1}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k})\|\leq\Delta_{k},\quad{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}+\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}\geq(1-\epsilon_{s}){\boldsymbol{s}}_{k},

where
{\bar{H}}_{k}
is a (symmetric) approximation to the Hessian
\nabla^{2}_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}{\mathcal{L}}_{k}
.

The formulation of the subproblem follows that used in deterministic interior-point methods
[
11
,
10
,
23
]
. In contrast to recent stochastic interior-point methods that impose a user-specified lower-bounding sequence on the slack variables
[
19
,
20
]
, we employ the fraction-to-boundary condition
{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}+\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}\geq(1-\epsilon_{s}){\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}
to maintain positivity of the slack variables. This design reduces the number of tuning parameters and aligns the design with deterministic interior-point methods.
By the development of our algorithm in Section
3
, when iteration
k
is successful, the next iterate satisfies
{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k+1}={\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}+\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}
, so the condition can equivalently be written as
{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k+1}\geq(1-\epsilon_{s}){\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}
. This mechanism prevents the iterates from approaching the boundary of the feasible region too aggressively.
In practice,
\epsilon_{s}
is chosen close to one, e.g.,
\epsilon_{s}=0.9
or
0.99
as suggested in
[
10
]
.

2.2
Computing the (rescaled) trial step

We now describe how to compute the (rescaled) trial step. For notational convenience, we denote the rescaled trial step as

\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}\coloneqq(\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k};\tilde{\Delta}{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k})=(\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k};S_{k}^{-1}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}).

Using this notation, the TR-IP-SSQP subproblem (
6
) can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle\min_{\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+n}}\bar{\psi}_{k}^{T}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}^{T}\bar{W}_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}\;\;\text{s.t. }\;\vartheta_{k}+A_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}={\boldsymbol{0}},\;\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}\|\leq\Delta_{k},\;\tilde{\Delta}{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}\geq-\epsilon_{s}\boldsymbol{e},

(7)

where
\bar{\psi}_{k}=({\bar{g}}_{k};-\theta_{k}\boldsymbol{e}),\vartheta_{k}=(c_{k};h_{k}+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k})
,
\bar{W}_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}{\bar{H}}_{k}&0\\
0&\theta_{k}I\end{pmatrix}
, and
A_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}G_{k}&0\\
J_{k}&S_{k}\end{pmatrix}
.
To solve the subproblem (
7
), a potential difficulty is the infeasibility issue, i.e.,
\{\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}:\vartheta_{k}+A_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}={\boldsymbol{0}}\}\cap\{\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}:\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}\|\leq\Delta_{k}\}=\emptyset
. To address this issue, we decompose the trial step into a
normal step
and a
tangential step
. Specifically, we write

\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}\coloneqq\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}+\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}{\boldsymbol{w}}^{x}_{k}\\
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}^{s}_{k}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}{\boldsymbol{t}}^{x}_{k}\\
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}^{s}_{k}\end{pmatrix},

where
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}\in\operatorname{im}(A_{k}^{T})
is the normal step and
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}\in\ker(A_{k})
is the tangential step. We use
{\boldsymbol{w}}^{x}_{k}
,
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}^{s}_{k}
and
{\boldsymbol{t}}^{x}_{k}
,
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}^{s}_{k}
to denote the components of the normal and tangential steps corresponding to
\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}
and
\tilde{\Delta}{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}
, respectively.

To enforce the trust-region constraint, we decompose the trust-region radius
\Delta_{k}
into two components that separately control the lengths of the normal and tangential steps. Specifically, we require

\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}\|\leq\breve{\Delta}_{k}\coloneqq\zeta\Delta_{k}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}\|\leq\hat{\Delta}_{k}\coloneqq\sqrt{\Delta_{k}^{2}-\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}\|^{2}},

(8)

where
\zeta\in(0,1)
is a user-specified parameter. Similarly, the fraction-to-boundary condition is decomposed as

\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}^{s}_{k}\geq-\zeta\epsilon_{s}\boldsymbol{e}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}^{s}_{k}\geq-\epsilon_{s}\boldsymbol{e}-\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}^{s}.

(9)

Here we use the same parameter
\zeta
in the above two decompositions for simplicity.

We first compute the normal step
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}
. Suppose that
A_{k}
has full row rank (as in Assumption
6
), we define

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}:=-A_{k}^{T}[A_{k}A_{k}^{T}]^{-1}\vartheta_{k}.

(10)

In the absence of trust-region and fraction-to-boundary constraints, one would set
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}={\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}
to satisfy
\vartheta_{k}+A_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}={\boldsymbol{0}}
. However, to enforce trust-region and fraction-to-boundary constraints, we scale
{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}
by a factor
{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\in(0,1]
:

\begin{pmatrix}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{k}^{x}\\
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}^{s}\end{pmatrix}=\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}\coloneqq{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}^{x}\\
{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{k}^{s}\end{pmatrix}.

The scaling parameter
{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}
is chosen to ensure feasibility of both (
8
) and (
9
), yielding

{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}=\min\left\{\frac{\zeta\epsilon_{s}}{\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{k}^{s}\|},\frac{\zeta\Delta_{k}}{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|},1\right\}.

(11)

Given the normal step, the tangential step
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}
is obtained by approximately solving

\displaystyle\min_{\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+n}}

\displaystyle m(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k})=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}^{T}\bar{W}_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}+(\bar{\psi}_{k}+{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\bar{W}_{k}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k})^{T}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}

(12)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle A_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}={\boldsymbol{0}},\;\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}\|\leq\hat{\Delta}_{k},\;\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}^{s}\geq-\epsilon_{s}\boldsymbol{e}-\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}^{s}.

As in existing trust-region SSQP methods
[
27
,
25
]
, an exact solution of (
12
) is not required. It suffices that the computed tangential step achieves a fixed fraction
\kappa_{fcd}\in(0,1]
of the Cauchy reduction
[
37
]
, which can be obtained using a projected conjugate gradient method
[
11
]
.

For the convergence analysis, we derive an explicit upper bound on the Cauchy reduction. To this end, following
[
10
]
, we introduce a strengthened constraint. Since
{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}
ensures
\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}^{s}\|\leq\zeta\epsilon_{s}
, the condition

\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}^{s}\geq-\epsilon_{s}\boldsymbol{e}-\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}^{s}

is satisfied whenever

\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}^{s}\|\leq\epsilon_{s}-\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}^{s}\|
.
Using
\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}^{s}\|\leq\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}\|
, we strengthen the constraints in (
12
) to

\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}\|\leq\min\{\hat{\Delta}_{k},\epsilon_{s}-\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}^{s}\|\}.

(13)

Since
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}\in\ker(A_{k})
, it can be written as
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}=Z_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{k}
, where
Z_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{(d+n)\times(d-m)}
forms a basis of the null space of
A_{k}
satisfying
Z_{k}^{T}Z_{k}=I,Z_{k}Z_{k}^{T}=P_{k}
, with
P_{k}=I-A_{k}^{T}[A_{k}A_{k}^{T}]^{-1}A_{k}
denoting the projection matrix. Under this parametrization, the subproblem (
12
) with the constraint (
13
) becomes

\displaystyle\min_{\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-m}}

\displaystyle\tilde{m}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{k})=\frac{1}{2}(Z_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{k})^{T}\bar{W}_{k}Z_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{k}+({\bar{\psi}}_{k}+{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\bar{W}_{k}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k})^{T}Z_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{k}

(14)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{k}\|\leq\min\{\hat{\Delta}_{k},\epsilon_{s}-\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}^{s}\|\},

where we have used the relations
\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}\|^{2}=\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{k}^{T}Z_{k}^{T}Z_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{k}=\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{k}\|^{2}
.
Let
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}^{C}=Z_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{k}^{C}
denote the Cauchy point (see
[
37
, (4.11)]
for the formula), we require the computed tangential step to satisfy (see
[
37
, Lemma 4.3]
for the last inequality)

\displaystyle m(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k})

\displaystyle-m({\boldsymbol{0}})\leq\kappa_{fcd}(m(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}^{C})-m({\boldsymbol{0}}))=\kappa_{fcd}(\tilde{m}(\tilde{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{k}^{C})-m({\boldsymbol{0}}))

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|Z_{k}^{T}({\bar{\psi}}_{k}+{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\bar{W}_{k}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k})\|\min\left\{\hat{\Delta}_{k},\epsilon_{s}-\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}^{s}\|,\frac{\|Z_{k}^{T}({\bar{\psi}}_{k}+{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\bar{W}_{k}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k})\|}{\|Z_{k}^{T}\bar{W}_{k}Z_{k}\|}\right\}.

(15)

The strengthened constraint (
13
) is more restrictive than the original constraints in (
12
), and thus the bound in (
2.2
) is conservative. Nevertheless, it is sufficient for establishing global convergence. Importantly, the strengthened constraint is introduced solely for theoretical purposes. In practice, the algorithm solves only the original subproblem (
12
), and neither the explicit construction of the null-space basis
Z_{k}
nor the constraint modification is required.

3
TR-IP-SSQP Method

In this section, we describe our algorithm design, which is summarized in Algorithm
1
. We first introduce the probabilistic oracles constructed in every iteration.

3.1
Probabilistic oracles

In each iteration, the zeroth- and first-order probabilistic oracles generate estimates of the objective value
\bar{f}({\boldsymbol{x}},\xi)
and the objective gradient
\bar{g}({\boldsymbol{x}},\xi)
, respectively, where
\xi
denotes a random variable defined on some probability space. These estimates are required to satisfy certain adaptive accuracy conditions with a high but fixed probability.

Let
\kappa_{g},\kappa_{f}>0
and
p_{g},p_{f}\in(0,1)
be user-specified parameters. In the
k
-th iteration,
\Delta_{k}
denotes the trust-region radius and
{\bar{\epsilon}}_{k}
denotes the reliability parameter, both of which are updated from the
(k-1)
-th iteration.

The probabilistic first-order oracle is defined at
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}
as follows, which indicates that the noise of the gradient estimate is
{\mathcal{O}}(\Delta_{k})
with probability at least
1-p_{g}
.

Definition 1
(Probabilistic first-order oracle).

Given
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}
, the oracle computes
{\bar{g}}_{k}\coloneqq{\bar{g}}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{k},\xi_{k}^{g})
, an estimate of the objective gradient
\nabla f_{k}
, such that

{\mathcal{A}}_{k}=\{\|{\bar{g}}_{k}-\nabla f_{k}\|\leq\kappa_{g}\Delta_{k}\}\quad\quad\text{satisfies}\quad\quad P({\mathcal{A}}_{k}\mid{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k})\geq 1-p_{g}.

(16)

The zeroth-order probabilistic oracle is defined at both the current iterate
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}
and the trial iterate
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{s_{k}}={\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}+\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}
as follows.

Definition 2
(Probabilistic zeroth-order oracle).

Given
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}
and
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{s_{k}}
, the oracle computes
{\bar{f}}_{k}\coloneqq{\bar{f}}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{k},\xi_{k}^{f})
and
{\bar{f}}_{s_{k}}\coloneqq{\bar{f}}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{s_{k}},\xi_{s_{k}}^{f})
, the estimates of the objective values
f_{k}\coloneqq f({\boldsymbol{x}}_{k})
and
f_{s_{k}}\coloneqq f({\boldsymbol{x}}_{s_{k}})
, such that the following two conditions hold.

\bullet
(i)
The absolute errors
e_{k}\coloneqq|{\bar{f}}_{k}-f_{k}|
and
e_{s_{k}}\coloneqq|{\bar{f}}_{s_{k}}-f_{s_{k}}|
are sufficiently small with a fixed probability:

{\mathcal{B}}_{k}=\left\{\max\left(e_{k},e_{s_{k}}\right)\leq\kappa_{f}\Delta_{k}^{2}\right\}\quad\text{ satisfies }\quad P({\mathcal{B}}_{k}\mid{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k},\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k})\geq 1-p_{f}.

(17)

\bullet
(ii)

The estimates
{\bar{f}}_{k},{\bar{f}}_{s_{k}}
are sufficiently reliable so that they have controlled variance:

\max\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[e_{k}^{2}\mid{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\right],\mathbb{E}\left[e_{s_{k}}^{2}\mid{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k},\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\right]\right\}\leq{\bar{\epsilon}}_{k}^{2}.

(18)

We adopt the exact oracle design proposed in
[
27
]
with
\alpha=0
(in their notation), which was developed for trust-region SSQP methods for solving equality-constrained problems. This unified oracle design allows equality- and inequality-constrained problems to be treated consistently, without requiring any modifications in sampling mechanisms to accommodate inequality constraints.

Estimates satisfying the above probabilistic oracle conditions can be constructed via a variety of techniques, including sample averaging, median-of-means estimators, or finite-difference approximations in gradient-free settings. We refer the interested reader to Section 3.2 of
[
25
]
and the references therein for a detailed discussion of estimator construction and the associated sample size requirements.

Remark 3
.

Our oracle design permits biased estimates and imposes no parametric assumptions on the noise; in particular, the gradient noise may possess infinite variance. These conditions are substantially weaker than those adopted in fixed-sampling frameworks
[
7
,
26
,
19
,
20
]
, where gradient estimates are required to be unbiased and their variance either uniformly bounded or subject to a growth condition. Recent non-asymptotic analyses
[
31
,
14
,
5
]
also employ probabilistic oracle designs but assume that the noise in objective-value estimation is sub-exponential at each iteration, implying the existence of infinite orders of moments and therefore restricting attention to light-tailed noise distributions. Recent literature
[
25
,
41
]
has aimed to relax the sub-exponential noise to heavy-tailed noise for equality-constrained or unconstrained problems.

Remark 4
.

Since the trust-region subproblem is formulated and solved in terms of the rescaled trial step
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}=(\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k},S_{k}^{-1}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k})
, we compute the predicted reduction directly using
\tilde{{\boldsymbol{d}}}_{k}
(cf. (

In the convergence analysis, however, it is more convenient to work with the unscaled trial step
(\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k},\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k})
. Accordingly, we rewrite the predicted reduction as follows, yielding an expression equivalent to (

\displaystyle\text{Pred}_{k}

\displaystyle={\bar{g}}_{k}^{T}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}^{T}{\bar{H}}_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}-\theta_{k}\boldsymbol{e}^{T}S_{k}^{-1}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\theta_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{T}S_{k}^{-2}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+{\bar{\mu}}_{k}\left\{\|(c_{k}+G_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k};h_{k}+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}+J_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}+\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k})\|-\|(c_{k};h_{k}+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k})\|\right\}.

(26)

Remark 5
.

Similar to interior-point methods in deterministic optimization
[
11
,
10
,
23
,
18
]
, our algorithm design involves an estimated stationarity measure
\bar{Q}_{k}
.
Our convergence analysis focuses on both
\bar{Q}_{k}
and its deterministic counterpart,

Q_{k}\coloneqq(P_{k}\psi_{k};\vartheta_{k})\quad\text{where }\;\;\psi_{k}=(\nabla f_{k};-\theta_{k}\boldsymbol{e}).

(27)

In fact,
\|Q_{k}\|
is closely related to the KKT residual
\|\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{k}}\|
of the barrier problem (
3
). With the Lagrangian multipliers defined as in (
5
), there exists a constant
\Upsilon_{L}>0
such that

\|\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{k}}\|\leq\Upsilon_{L}\|Q_{k}\|
for all
k\geq 0
. Therefore, to establish global convergence, it suffices to show that
\|Q_{k}\|\rightarrow 0
almost surely.

We conclude this section by formalizing the randomness of the algorithm. We define
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{2}\cdots
as a filtration of
\sigma
-algebras. The
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}
is generated by
\{\xi^{g}_{j},\xi^{f}_{j}\}_{j=0}^{k-1}
and thus contains the randomness before the
k
-th iteration. We also define
{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1/2}
as the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\{\xi^{g}_{j},\xi^{f}_{j}\}_{j=0}^{k-1}\cup\{\xi^{g}_{k}\}
, which contains the randomness of
{\bar{g}}_{k}
in addition to the randomness in
{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}
. From these definitions, we have
{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1/2}\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
. Letting
{\mathcal{F}}_{-1}=\sigma({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{0})
, we have for all
k\geq 0
,

\sigma({\boldsymbol{x}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k},\Delta_{k},{\bar{\epsilon}}_{k})\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\sigma(\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k},\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k},{\bar{\mu}}_{k})\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1/2}.

4
Convergence Analysis

We study the convergence of the proposed algorithm in this section. We first state the assumptions.

Assumption 6
.

The iterates and trial points,
\{{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}\}
and
\{{\boldsymbol{x}}_{s_{k}},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{s_{k}}\}
, are contained within an open convex set
\Omega
. The objective function
f({\boldsymbol{x}})
is continuously differentiable and bounded below by
f_{\inf}
, and its gradient
\nabla f({\boldsymbol{x}})
is Lipschitz continuous on
\Omega
with constant
L_{\nabla f}
. The constraints
c({\boldsymbol{x}}),h({\boldsymbol{x}})
are continuously differentiable, and their respective Jacobians
G({\boldsymbol{x}}),J({\boldsymbol{x}})
are Lipschitz continuous on
\Omega
with constants
L_{G},L_{J}
, respectively.
There exist constants
\kappa_{c},\kappa_{h}
,
\kappa_{\nabla f}
such that
\|c_{k}\|\leq\kappa_{c}
,
\|h_{k}\|\leq\kappa_{h}
, and
\|\nabla f_{k}\|\leq\kappa_{\nabla f}
for all
k\geq 0
. There exist
\kappa_{1,A},\kappa_{2,A}>0
such that
\kappa_{1,A}\cdot I\preceq A_{k}A_{k}^{T}\preceq\kappa_{2,A}\cdot I
. Finally, there exists a constant
\kappa_{B}\geq 1
such that
\|{\bar{H}}_{k}\|\leq\kappa_{B}
.

Assumption
6
is standard in both deterministic and stochastic SQP literature
[
13
,
39
,
24
,
16
,
11
,
10
,
6
,
20
,
26
,
27
]
. In particular, it implies that
G_{k}
has full row rank,

\displaystyle\max\{\|G_{k}\|,\|J_{k}\|,\|S_{k}\|\}\leq\sqrt{\kappa_{2,A}},\;\;\;\max\{\|G_{k}^{T}[G_{k}G_{k}^{T}]^{-1}\|,\|S_{k}^{-1}\|\}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa_{1,A}}},

(28)

and
\|{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}\|\leq\sqrt{n\cdot\kappa_{2,A}}\eqqcolon\Upsilon_{s}
.

To establish global convergence, we also need the merit parameter to be stabilized, thus we impose the assumption below.

Assumption 7
.

There exist a (potentially stochastic)
{\bar{K}}<\infty
and a deterministic constant
\hat{\mu}
, such that for all
k\geq{\bar{K}}
,
{\bar{\mu}}_{k}={\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}}\leq{\hat{\mu}}
.

Assuming stabilization of the merit parameter is standard in the literature for both line-search-based methods
[
7
,
6
,
4
,
20
]
and trust-region-based methods
[
26
,
27
]
. However, unlike line-search-based methods, our approach does not require the merit parameter to be sufficiently large, which typically necessitates either stronger assumptions on the noise distribution (e.g., the symmetry assumption in
[
6
,
7
]
) or more complicated algorithm design (e.g., addition condition (19) in
[
33
]
). We revisit Assumption
7
in Section
4.3
, where we show that it holds when the gradient estimates are bounded.

4.1
Fundamental lemmas

In the following lemma, we demonstrate that when the estimates of gradients are accurate and the trust-region radius is
sufficiently small compared to
Q_{k}
, then Line 4 of the algorithm will not be triggered.

Lemma 8
.

Under Assumption
6

{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
, if

\|Q_{k}\|\geq\{\kappa_{g}+\eta(\kappa_{B}+\theta_{0})\}\cdot\Delta_{k}

(29)

is satisfied, then Line 4 of Algorithm

Proof.

By the relationship
\|Q_{k}\|-\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|\leq\|Q_{k}-\bar{Q}_{k}\|=\|P_{k}(\psi_{k}-\bar{\psi}_{k})\|\leq\|\psi_{k}-\bar{\psi}_{k}\|=\|\nabla f_{k}-{\bar{g}}_{k}\|
, we have
\|Q_{k}\|-\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|\leq\|\nabla f_{k}-{\bar{g}}_{k}\|\leq\kappa_{g}\Delta_{k}
on the event
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
. In addition, by Assumption
6
, we have
\|{\bar{H}}_{k}\|\leq\kappa_{B}
, which combined with
\theta_{k}\leq\theta_{0}
leads to
\|\bar{W}_{k}\|=\|\text{diag}({\bar{H}}_{k},\theta_{k}I)\|\leq\kappa_{B}+\theta_{0}
. Combining these results, we find that (
29
) implies
\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|/\max\{1,\|\bar{W}_{k}\|\}\geq\eta\Delta_{k}
, thus Line 4 will not be triggered.

In the next lemma, we investigate the difference between the predicted reduction
\text{Pred}_{k}
in (
20
) and the true actual reduction
{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{s_{k}}-{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}
computed using the
\ell_{2}
merit function (
22
). We show that their difference can be upper bounded by
{\mathcal{O}}(\Delta_{k}^{2})
.

Lemma 9
.

Under Assumptions
6
,
7
, and the event
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
, for all
k\geq{\bar{K}}
,

\displaystyle\big|{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{s_{k}}-{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}-\text{Pred}_{k}\big|\leq\Upsilon_{1}\Delta_{k}^{2},

(30)

where
\Upsilon_{1}=\kappa_{g}+\max\{\frac{1}{2}(L_{\nabla f}+\kappa_{B}+{\hat{\mu}}(L_{G}+L_{J})),\theta_{0}(\frac{1}{1-\epsilon_{s}}+\frac{1}{2})\}
.

Proof.

In this proof, we use the formula of
\text{Pred}_{k}
defined in (
4
), which is based on unscaled trial steps. Combining (
22
) and (
4
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}},\theta_{k}}}^{s_{k}}

\displaystyle-{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}},\theta_{k}}}^{k}-\text{Pred}_{k}=f_{s_{k}}-f_{k}-{\bar{g}}_{k}^{T}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}^{T}{\bar{H}}_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}

\displaystyle\quad-\theta_{k}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln({\boldsymbol{s}}_{s_{k}}^{(i)})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln({\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)})-\boldsymbol{e}^{T}S_{k}^{-1}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{T}S_{k}^{-2}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad-{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}}\left\{\|(c_{k}+G_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k};h_{k}+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}+J_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}+\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k})\|-\|(c_{s_{k}};h_{s_{k}}+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{s_{k}})\|\right\}.

Noticing that
\boldsymbol{e}^{T}S_{k}^{-1}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}{{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}
and
\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{T}S_{k}^{-2}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\frac{\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}{{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}})^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}},\theta_{k}}}^{s_{k}}-{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}},\theta_{k}}}^{k}-\text{Pred}_{k}\right|\leq\left|f_{s_{k}}-f_{k}-{\bar{g}}_{k}^{T}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}^{T}{\bar{H}}_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\theta_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\ln({\boldsymbol{s}}_{s_{k}}^{(i)})-\ln({\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)})-\frac{\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}{{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}{{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}\right)^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}}\left|\|(c_{k}+G_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k};h_{k}+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}+J_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}+\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k})\|-\|(c_{s_{k}};h_{s_{k}}+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{s_{k}})\|\right|.

By the Taylor expansion of
f({\boldsymbol{x}})
, the Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla f({\boldsymbol{x}})
(Assumption
6
), and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\left|f_{s_{k}}-f_{k}-{\bar{g}}_{k}^{T}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}^{T}{\bar{H}}_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\right|\leq\|\nabla f_{k}-{\bar{g}}_{k}\|\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\|+\frac{1}{2}(L_{\nabla f}+\kappa_{B})\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\|^{2}.

Using the same derivations and recalling that
{\boldsymbol{s}}_{s_{k}}={\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}+\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\|(c_{s_{k}};h_{s_{k}}+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{s_{k}})\|-\|(c_{k}+G_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k};h_{k}+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}+J_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}+\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k})\|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|c_{s_{k}}-c_{k}-G_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\|+\|h_{s_{k}}+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{s_{k}}-h_{k}-{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}-J_{k}\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}-\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}\|\leq\frac{L_{G}+L_{J}}{2}\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\|^{2}.

Furthermore, for any scalars
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
satisfying
\sigma>0
,
\sigma^{\prime}>-\epsilon_{s}\sigma
, we have

\left|\ln(\sigma+\sigma^{\prime})-\ln(\sigma)-\frac{\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma}\right|\leq\sup_{t\in[\sigma,\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}]}\left|\frac{\sigma^{\prime}}{t}-\frac{\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma}\right|=\frac{\sigma}{\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{1-\epsilon_{s}}\left(\frac{\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma}\right)^{2}.

(31)

Therefore, setting
\sigma={\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}
and
\sigma^{\prime}=\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}
for
i=1,\dots,n
, we obtain

\displaystyle\theta_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\ln({\boldsymbol{s}}_{s_{k}}^{(i)})-\ln({\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)})-\frac{\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}{{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}{{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}\right)^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\theta_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\ln({\boldsymbol{s}}_{s_{k}}^{(i)})-\ln({\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)})-\frac{\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}{{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}\right|+\frac{1}{2}\theta_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}{{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:sigma}}}}{{\leq}}\theta_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{1-\epsilon_{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}{{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}^{(i)}}\right)^{2}=\theta_{k}\left(\frac{1}{1-\epsilon_{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\|S_{k}^{-1}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}\|^{2}.

Since
{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}}\leq{\hat{\mu}}
(Assumption
7
),
\|{\bar{H}}_{k}\|\leq\kappa_{B}
(Assumption
6
) and
\theta_{k}\leq\theta_{0}
, we combine the above displays and obtain

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{s_{k}}-{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}-\text{Pred}_{k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f_{k}-\bar{g}_{k}\|\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\|+\frac{L_{\nabla f}+\kappa_{B}+{\hat{\mu}}(L_{G}+L_{J})}{2}\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\|^{2}+\theta_{0}\left(\frac{1}{1-\epsilon_{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\|S_{k}^{-1}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f_{k}-\bar{g}_{k}\|\Delta_{k}+\max\left\{\frac{L_{\nabla f}+\kappa_{B}+{\hat{\mu}}(L_{G}+L_{J})}{2},\theta_{0}\left(\frac{1}{1-\epsilon_{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right\}\Delta_{k}^{2},

where the last inequality follows from the trust-region constraint; that is,
\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\|^{2}+\|S_{k}^{-1}\Delta{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}\|^{2}\leq\Delta_{k}^{2}
. Combining the above display with
\|\nabla f_{k}-\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\kappa_{g}\Delta_{k}
on the event
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
, we complete the proof.

In the next lemma, we demonstrate that if the estimates of the objective models are accurate and the trust-region radius is sufficiently small compared to
\|Q_{k}\|
and
\epsilon_{s}
, the
k
-th iteration is guaranteed to be successful.

Lemma 10
.

Under Assumptions
6
,
7
, and the event
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{k}
, if for

\Delta_{k}\leq\min\left\{\frac{(1-\eta)\kappa_{fcd}\|Q_{k}\|}{4\kappa_{f}+8\Upsilon_{1}+\kappa_{g}\kappa_{fcd}(1-\eta)},\frac{\|Q_{k}\|}{\kappa_{g}+\kappa_{B}+\theta_{0}},\epsilon_{s}\right\},

(32)

then the
k
-th iteration is successful.

Proof.

To prove that the
k
-th iteration is successful, it suffices to show that Line 4 in Algorithm
1
is not triggered and
\text{Ared}_{k}/\text{Pred}_{k}\geq\eta
, i.e., (
24
a), is satisfied. Since
\eta<1
, we find (
32
) implies (
29
), thus the conclusion of Lemma
8
indicates that Line 4 is not triggered. Next, we show
\text{Ared}_{k}/\text{Pred}_{k}\geq\eta
holds. By the definition of
\text{Ared}_{k}
in (
23
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\text{Ared}_{k}}{\text{Pred}_{k}}-1\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{s_{k}}-\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}}{\text{Pred}_{k}}-1\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{s_{k}}-{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{s_{k}}|+|{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{s_{k}}-{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}-\text{Pred}_{k}|+|{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}-\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}|}{|\text{Pred}_{k}|}.

Note that (
32
) implies
\|Q_{k}\|\geq(\kappa_{g}+\kappa_{B}+\theta_{0})\Delta_{k}
. On the event
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
, we also have
\|Q_{k}\|-\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|\leq\kappa_{g}\Delta_{k}
. Thus,
\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|\geq(\kappa_{B}+\theta_{0})\Delta_{k}
.
By the algorithm design and the fact that
\max\{1,\|\bar{W}_{k}\|\}\leq\kappa_{B}+\theta_{0}
, we have

\text{Pred}_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:threshold_Predk}}}{{\leq}}-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\cdot\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s},\frac{\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|}{\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}\right\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{delta:lemma_guarantee_succ_step_KKT}}}{{=}}-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|\Delta_{k}.

(33)

Since
|{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{\bar{K}},\theta_{k}}^{s_{k}}-{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{\bar{K}},\theta_{k}}^{s_{k}}|=|f_{s_{k}}-{\bar{f}}_{s_{k}}|
and
|{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{\bar{K}},\theta_{k}}^{k}-{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{\bar{K}},\theta_{k}}^{k}|=|f_{k}-{\bar{f}}_{k}|
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\text{Ared}_{k}}{\text{Pred}_{k}}-1\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left|f_{s_{k}}-{\bar{f}}_{s_{k}}\right|+\left|{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{s_{k}}-{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}-\text{Pred}_{k}\right|+\left|f_{k}-{\bar{f}}_{k}\right|}{\left|\text{Pred}_{k}\right|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left|{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{s_{k}}-{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}-\text{Pred}_{k}\right|+2\kappa_{f}\Delta_{k}^{2}}{\left|\text{Pred}_{k}\right|}\qquad(\text{since }{\mathcal{B}}_{k}\text{ holds})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:ared-pred}}}}{{\leq}}\;\frac{\Upsilon_{1}\Delta_{k}^{2}+2\kappa_{f}\Delta_{k}^{2}}{\left|\text{Pred}_{k}\right|}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:lemma6_pred}}}{{\leq}}\frac{(4\kappa_{f}+2\Upsilon_{1})\Delta_{k}}{\kappa_{fcd}\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{delta:lemma_guarantee_succ_step_KKT}}}{{\leq}}1-\eta.

This is equivalent to
\text{Ared}_{k}/\text{Pred}_{k}\geq\eta
; thus, the
k
-th iteration must be successful.

The next lemma shows that if the iteration is successful and objective value estimates are accurate, then the reduction in the
true
merit function is
\mathcal{O}(\Delta_{k}^{2})
.

Lemma 11
.

Under Assumptions
6
,
7
, and the event
{\mathcal{B}}_{k}
, if the
k
-th iteration is successful for
k\geq{\bar{K}}
, then

{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k+1}-{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}\leq-\frac{3\kappa_{fcd}}{8}\min\left\{1,\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{\Delta_{\max}}\right\}\eta^{3}\Delta_{k}^{2}.

(34)

Proof.

For a successful iteration, Line 4 of the algorithm cannot be triggered, thus we must have

\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|/\max\{1,\|\bar{W}_{k}\|\}\geq\eta\Delta_{k}
. This relation implies

\displaystyle\text{Pred}_{k}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:threshold_Predk}}}}{{\leq}}-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s},\frac{\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|}{\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}\right\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{def:acc1}}}}{{\leq}}-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\eta\Delta_{k}\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s},\eta\Delta_{k}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\eta\Delta_{k}\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{\Delta_{\max}}\Delta_{k},\eta\Delta_{k}\right\}\leq-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\min\left\{1,\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{\Delta_{\max}}\right\}\eta^{2}\Delta_{k}^{2},

(35)

since
\eta<1
and
\Delta_{k}\leq\Delta_{\max}
. On the event
{\mathcal{B}}_{k}
, we have
\left|f_{s_{k}}-{\bar{f}}_{s_{k}}\right|+\left|f_{k}-{\bar{f}}_{k}\right|\leq 2\kappa_{f}\Delta_{k}^{2}
. Recalling that
\text{Ared}_{k}=\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k+1}-\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}
and
\text{Ared}_{k}/\text{Pred}_{k}\geq\eta
for a successful iteration, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k+1}

\displaystyle-{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}\leq\left|{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k+1}-\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k+1}\right|+\text{Ared}_{k}+\left|\bar{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}-{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|f_{s_{k}}-{\bar{f}}_{s_{k}}\right|+\eta\cdot\text{Pred}_{k}+\left|f_{k}-{\bar{f}}_{k}\right|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:pred_1st_order}}}}{{\leq}}\;2\kappa_{f}\Delta_{k}^{2}-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\min\left\{1,\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{\Delta_{\max}}\right\}\eta^{3}\Delta_{k}^{2}\leq-\frac{3\kappa_{fcd}}{8}\min\left\{1,\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{\Delta_{\max}}\right\}\eta^{3}\Delta_{k}^{2},

(36)

where we use the upper bound of
\kappa_{f}
in the last inequality.
This completes the proof.

To further the analysis, we define a rescaled
\ell_{2}
merit function:

\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}},\theta}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{s}})\coloneqq f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\theta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln\left(\frac{{\boldsymbol{s}}^{(i)}}{\Upsilon_{s}}\right)+{\bar{\mu}}\|(c({\boldsymbol{x}});h({\boldsymbol{x}})+{\boldsymbol{s}})\|={\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}},\theta}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{s}})+n\theta\ln(\Upsilon_{s}),

which shifts the
\ell_{2}
merit function in (
22
) by a constant. Thus, the reduction in the merit function is preserved. Specifically, for a fixed barrier parameter
\theta
and any two points
({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{s}})
and
({\boldsymbol{x}}^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{s}}^{\prime})
, we have

\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}},\theta}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{s}})-\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}},\theta}({\boldsymbol{x}}^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{s}}^{\prime})={\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}},\theta}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{s}})-{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bar{\mu}},\theta}({\boldsymbol{x}}^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{s}}^{\prime}).

(37)

As a result, the conclusions of Lemmas
9
,
10
, and
11
extend directly to the rescaled merit function.

Rescaling equips the merit function with two features. First, since
\ln({\boldsymbol{s}}^{(i)}/\Upsilon_{s})\leq 0
, we have
f_{\inf}\leq\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}},\theta}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{s}})
. Second, the rescaled merit function is monotone increasing in the barrier parameter
\theta
. Specifically, for any
\widehat{\theta}\leq\theta
, we have
\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}},\widehat{\theta}}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{s}})\leq\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}},\theta}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{s}})
. This property is crucial as our barrier parameters indeed satisfy
\theta_{k+1}\leq\theta_{k}
, leading to

\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k+1}}^{k+1}-\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}\leq\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k+1}-\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k},

(38)

which allows us to accumulate all the reductions over
k\geq\bar{K}
.

To establish global convergence, we further leverage a Lyapunov function, defined based on the rescaled merit function for
k\geq{\bar{K}}
:

\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}=\nu\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}+\frac{1-\nu}{2}\Delta_{k}^{2}+\frac{1-\nu}{2}{\bar{\epsilon}}_{k},

where
\nu\in(0,1)
is a constant satisfying

\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}\geq\max\left\{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{\eta},\frac{4\gamma^{2}}{\kappa_{fcd}\eta^{3}\min\{1,\epsilon_{s}/\Delta_{\max}\}}\right\}.

(39)

We investigate the global convergence of Algorithm
1
by examining the reduction in the Lyapunov function.

Let us first consider the case when the estimates of objective values are accurate.

Lemma 12
.

Under Assumptions

We first fix the barrier parameter and investigate

\bullet

where the upper bound of

\bullet

4.1
), and (

\frac{1}{4}\nu\eta{\bar{\epsilon}}_{k}\geq\frac{1-\nu}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\gamma}\right){\bar{\epsilon}}_{k}
and

-\frac{\nu\kappa_{fcd}}{4}\min\left\{1,\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{\Delta_{\max}}\right\}\eta^{3}\Delta_{k}^{2}+\frac{1-\nu}{2}(\gamma^{2}-1)\Delta_{k}^{2}\leq\frac{1-\nu}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}-1\right)\Delta_{k}^{2},

(48)

and using the inequality
\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k+1}}^{k+1}-\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}\leq\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k+1}-\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}
, we complete the proof.

Lemma
12
shows that when objective value estimates are accurate, a decrease in the Lyapunov function is guaranteed. Lemma
13
shows that when objective value estimates are inaccurate, the value of the Lyapunov function may increase. In the next lemma, we show that as long as the probability of obtaining an inaccurate objective value estimate is below a deterministic threshold, a reduction in the Lyapunov function is guaranteed in expectation.

Lemma 14
.

Suppose that Assumptions
6
and
7
hold,
\nu
satisfies (
39
), and
k\geq{\bar{K}}
.
If
p_{f}\leq\frac{(1-\nu)^{2}}{16\nu^{2}}(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})^{2}
, then we have

\mathbb{E}[\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k+1}}^{k+1}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}]-\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}\leq\frac{1-\nu}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}-1\right)\Delta_{k}^{2}.

(49)

Proof.

By the law of total expectation, we have

\mathbb{E}[\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k+1}}^{k+1}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}]-\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}=\mathbb{E}[(\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k+1}}^{k+1}-\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k})\boldsymbol{1}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}]\\
+\mathbb{E}[(\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k+1}}^{k+1}-\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k})\boldsymbol{1}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}^{c}}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}].

Combining the above display with the conclusions of Lemmas
12
and
13
, and
P({\mathcal{B}}_{k}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1})\geq 1-p_{f}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[

\displaystyle\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k+1}}^{k+1}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}]-\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-\nu}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}-1\right)\Delta_{k}^{2}+\frac{1-\nu}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-1\right){\bar{\epsilon}}_{k}+\nu\cdot\mathbb{E}[(e_{s_{k}}+e_{k})\boldsymbol{1}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}^{c}}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-\nu}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}-1\right)\Delta_{k}^{2}+\frac{1-\nu}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-1\right){\bar{\epsilon}}_{k}+2\nu\sqrt{p_{f}}\cdot{\bar{\epsilon}}_{k},

where the last inequality is due to the Hölder’s inequality. Since
p_{f}\leq\frac{(1-\nu)^{2}}{16\nu^{2}}(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})^{2}
leads to
\frac{1-\nu}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-1\right){\bar{\epsilon}}_{k}+2\nu\sqrt{p_{f}}{\bar{\epsilon}}_{k}\leq 0
, we complete the proof.

The following result follows immediately from Lemma
14
.

Corollary 15
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
14
,
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Delta_{k}=0
a.s.

Proof.

Taking the expectation conditional on
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\bar{K}}-1}
on both sides of (
49
), we have

\mathbb{E}[\Phi^{k+1}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k+1}}-\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{{\bar{K}}-1}]\leq\frac{1-\nu}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}-1\right)\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{{\bar{K}}-1}].

Summing over
k\geq{\bar{K}}
, noting that
\mathbb{E}[\Phi^{k}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{{\bar{K}}-1}]
is monotonically decreasing and bounded below by
\nu\cdot f_{\inf}
(cf. Assumption
6
and the definition of
\Phi^{k}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}
), we have

-\infty<\sum_{k={\bar{K}}}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\Phi^{k+1}_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k+1}}-\Phi_{{\bar{\mu}}_{{\bar{K}}},\theta_{k}}^{k}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{{\bar{K}}-1}]\leq\frac{1-\nu}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}-1\right)\sum_{k={\bar{K}}}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{{\bar{K}}-1}].

Since
\Delta_{k}\geq 0
, by Tonelli’s Theorem, we have
\mathbb{E}[\sum_{k={\bar{K}}}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{{\bar{K}}-1}]<\infty
, which implies
P[\sum_{k={\bar{K}}}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}^{2}<\infty\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{{\bar{K}}-1}]=1
. Since the conclusion holds for an arbitrarily given
{\bar{K}}
, we have
P[\sum_{k={\bar{K}}}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}^{2}<\infty]=1
, which implies that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Delta_{k}=0
almost surely.

4.2
Global convergence

We now establish the global convergence guarantee of Algorithm
1
. We first prove a liminf-type almost-sure convergence for the first-order stationarity. Then, we show that a subsequence of iterates converges to a KKT point of Problem (
1
).

Theorem 16
.

Suppose that the conditions of Lemma
14
hold and
p_{g}+p_{f}<1/2
. Then we have
\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{k}}\|=0
a.s. Moreover, for any subsequence
\|\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{k_{l}}}\|\rightarrow 0
as
l\rightarrow\infty
, any limit point
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{*}
of
\{{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k_{l}}\}_{l}
that satisfies the linear independence constraint qualification (LICQ) for Problem (
1
) (that is, the set of gradients
\{\nabla c^{(i)}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*})\}_{i=1}^{m}\cup\{\nabla h^{(i)}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*})\}_{i\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}}
is linearly independent, with
\bar{\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq\{i\in[n]:h^{(i)}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*})=0\}
being the index set of active inequality constraints) is a KKT point of Problem (
1
). In particular, there exists a pair of Lagrange multipliers
({\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{*},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{*},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{*})
satisfies (
2
).

Proof.

We first prove
\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|Q_{k}\|=0
almost surely.
We note that
\tilde{w}_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(\boldsymbol{1}_{({\mathcal{A}}_{i}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{i})}-\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{1}_{({\mathcal{A}}_{i}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{i})}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}])
is a martingale since

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{w}_{k+1}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}]=\tilde{w}_{k}+\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{1}_{({\mathcal{A}}_{k}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{k})}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}]-\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{1}_{({\mathcal{A}}_{k}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{k})}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}]=\tilde{w}_{k}.

Using the fact that
\boldsymbol{1}_{({\mathcal{A}}_{k}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{k})}\leq 1
and
[
29
, Theorem 2.19]
, we know
\tilde{w}_{k}/k\rightarrow 0
a.s. Define
w_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(2\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{({\mathcal{A}}_{i}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{i})}-1)
, then since
\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{1}_{({\mathcal{A}}_{i}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{i})}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]\geq 1-p_{g}-p_{f}
, we have

\frac{w_{k}}{k}=\frac{2\tilde{w}_{k}}{k}+\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(2\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{1}_{({\mathcal{A}}_{i}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{i})}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]-1)\geq\frac{2\tilde{w}_{k}}{k}+1-2(p_{g}+p_{f}).

As
p_{g}+p_{f}<0.5
, we know from the above display that
w_{k}\rightarrow\infty
almost surely. Next, we prove
\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|Q_{k}\|=0
by contradiction.

Suppose there exist
\epsilon_{1}>0
and
K_{1}\geq{\bar{K}}
such that for all
k\geq K_{1}
,
\|Q_{k}\|\geq\epsilon_{1}
. Since
\Delta_{k}\rightarrow 0
by Corollary
15
, there exists
K_{1}^{\prime}\geq K_{1}
such that for all
k\geq K_{1}^{\prime}
,
\Delta_{k}\leq a\coloneqq\min\{\frac{\Delta_{\max}}{\gamma},\varphi\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{s}\}
with
\varphi\coloneqq\min\{\frac{(1-\eta)\kappa_{fcd}}{4\kappa_{f}+8\Upsilon_{1}+(1-\eta)\kappa_{g}\kappa_{fcd}},\frac{1}{\kappa_{g}+\kappa_{B}+\theta_{0}}\}
.
Therefore, for all
k\geq K_{1}^{\prime}
, we have
\Delta_{k}\leq\min\{\varphi\|Q_{k}\|,\epsilon_{s}\}
, which combined with Lemma
10
shows that if
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{k}
holds, the iteration must be successful. Since
\Delta_{k}\leq\Delta_{\max}/\gamma
, we have
\Delta_{k+1}=\gamma\Delta_{k}
. On the other hand, if
({\mathcal{A}}_{k}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{k})^{c}
holds, the iteration can be successful or not, in which case we have
\Delta_{k+1}\geq\Delta_{k}/\gamma
. Let
b_{k}=\log_{\gamma}\left(\frac{\Delta_{k}}{a}\right)
, which satisfies
b_{k}\leq 0
for all
k\geq K_{1}^{\prime}
. By this definition, we know for
k\geq K_{1}^{\prime}
, if
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{k}
holds, then
b_{k+1}=b_{k}+1
; otherwise,
b_{k+1}\geq b_{k}-1
. Comparing this jumping process with
\{w_{k}\}
, we know
b_{k}-b_{K_{1}^{\prime}}\geq w_{k}-w_{K_{1}^{\prime}}
for all
k\geq K_{1}^{\prime}
. Thus,
b_{k}\rightarrow\infty
, which contradicts
b_{k}\leq 0
for all
k\geq K_{1}^{\prime}
. This contradiction concludes
\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|Q_{k}\|=0
.

To prove
\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{k}}\|=0
almost surely, it suffices to show
\|\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{k}}\|\leq\Upsilon_{L}\|Q_{k}\|
for some constant
\Upsilon_{L}
. Note that the definition of
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k}=\theta_{k}S_{k}^{-1}\boldsymbol{e}
(cf. (
5
)) implies
-\theta_{k}S_{k}^{-1}\boldsymbol{e}+{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k}=0
. Since

\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{k}}=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla f_{k}+G_{k}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}+J_{k}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k}\\
-\theta_{k}S_{k}^{-1}\boldsymbol{e}+{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k}\\
c_{k}\\
h_{k}+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}\end{pmatrix},\quad\quad\quad Q_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}P_{k}\psi_{k}\\
c_{k}\\
h_{k}+{\boldsymbol{s}}_{k}\end{pmatrix},

(50)

it suffices to show that
\|\nabla f_{k}+G_{k}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}+J_{k}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k}\|\leq\Upsilon_{L}\|P_{k}\psi_{k}\|
for some
\Upsilon_{L}>1
.

With the definitions in (
5
), if defining
P_{G,k}=I-G_{k}^{T}[G_{k}G_{k}^{T}]^{-1}G_{k}
, we have
\nabla f_{k}+G_{k}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}+J_{k}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k}=(P_{G,k}\;\;-P_{G,k}J_{k}^{T}S_{k}^{-1})\psi_{k}
. Since
(P_{G,k}\;\;-P_{G,k}J_{k}^{T}S_{k}^{-1})^{T}
is a basis of the null space of
A_{k}
, we know
\nabla f_{k}+G_{k}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}+J_{k}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k}=(P_{G,k}\;\;-P_{G,k}J_{k}^{T}S_{k}^{-1})P_{k}\psi_{k}
, where we use the fact that
P_{k}=I-A_{k}^{T}[A_{k}A_{k}^{T}]^{-1}A_{k}
denotes the orthogonal projection matrix to the null space of
A_{k}
. By the relation
P_{k}=Z_{k}Z_{k}^{T}
, we further have
\nabla f_{k}+G_{k}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}+J_{k}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k}=(P_{G,k}\;\;-P_{G,k}J_{k}^{T}S_{k}^{-1})Z_{k}Z_{k}^{T}\psi_{k}
, which leads to

\displaystyle\|\nabla f_{k}+G_{k}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}+J_{k}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\begin{pmatrix}P_{G,k}&-P_{G,k}J_{k}^{T}S_{k}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\|\|Z_{k}\|\|Z_{k}^{T}\psi_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq(\|P_{G,k}\|+\|P_{G,k}J_{k}^{T}S_{k}^{-1}\|)\|Z_{k}^{T}\psi_{k}\|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:kappa_G_J}}}}{{\leq}}\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{2,A}}{\kappa_{1,A}}}\right)\|Z_{k}^{T}\psi_{k}\|=\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{2,A}}{\kappa_{1,A}}}\right)\|P_{k}\psi_{k}\|,

where the last equality follows from
Z_{k}Z_{k}^{T}=P_{k}=P_{k}^{2}
. This result, combined with
\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|Q_{k}\|=0
, implies that
\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{k}}\|=0
almost surely.

Now consider the subsequence indexed by
k_{l}
with infinite cardinality described in the theorem. With the definition
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k_{l}}=\theta_{k_{l}}S_{k_{l}}^{-1}\boldsymbol{e}
, it follows that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k_{l}}\geq 0
for all
k_{l}
. Let
\bar{\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq\{i\in[n]:h^{(i)}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*})=0\}
. Since
S_{k_{l}}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k_{l}}=\theta_{k_{l}}\boldsymbol{e}
for all
k_{l}
and
\theta_{k_{l}}\rightarrow 0
, we have
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k_{l}}^{(i)}\rightarrow 0
for all
i\notin\bar{\mathcal{A}}
. Combining this result with
\|\nabla{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{k_{l}}}\|\rightarrow 0
yields that
\big\|\nabla f_{k_{l}}+G_{k_{l}}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k_{l}}+\sum_{i\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}}\nabla h^{(i)}_{k_{l}}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k_{l}}^{(i)}\big\|\rightarrow 0
. Let
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{*}
be any limit point of
\{{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k_{l}}\}_{l}
. Then there exists a subsequence
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k_{l_{j}}}\rightarrow{\boldsymbol{x}}_{*}
as
j\rightarrow\infty
with
\big\|\nabla f_{k_{l_{j}}}+G_{k_{l_{j}}}^{T}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k_{l_{j}}}+\sum_{i\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}}\nabla h^{(i)}_{k_{l_{j}}}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k_{l_{j}}}^{(i)}\big\|\rightarrow 0
. This indicates that
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{*}
is a KKT point of Problem (
1
) under LICQ. Note that
\{{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k_{l_{j}}}\}_{j}
is bounded, implying
({\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k_{l_{j}}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k_{l_{j}}})
are bounded under LICQ. Consequently,
({\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k_{l_{j}}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k_{l_{j}}})
admit limit points, denoted by
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{*}
and
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{*}
, respectively, such that
({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*},{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{*},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{*})
satisfies (
2
).

In this section, we re-examine Assumption

Assumption 17
.

For all

, there exists

, such that

.

Lemma 18
.

Suppose that Assumptions

Proof.

It suffices to show that (
21

\displaystyle=({\bar{\psi}}_{k}+{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\bar{W}_{k}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k})^{T}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}^{T}\bar{W}_{k}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{t}}}_{k}+{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}^{T}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}^{2}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}^{T}\bar{W}_{k}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}-{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|Z_{k}^{T}({\bar{\psi}}_{k}+{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\bar{W}_{k}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k})\|\min\left\{\hat{\Delta}_{k},\epsilon_{s}-\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{w}}}_{k}^{s}\|,\frac{\|Z_{k}^{T}({\bar{\psi}}_{k}+{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\bar{W}_{k}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k})\|}{\|Z_{k}^{T}\bar{W}_{k}Z_{k}\|}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k}-{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|,\epsilon_{s},\frac{\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|}{\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}\right\}-2{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|-{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\bar{W}_{k}\|\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|\hat{\Delta}_{k}

\displaystyle\geq\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s},\frac{\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|}{\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}\right\}-3{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|-{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\bar{W}_{k}\|\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|\hat{\Delta}_{k},

where we use the relation
\Delta_{k}-{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|\leq\hat{\Delta}_{k}
in the second inequality. Using the facts that
\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa_{1,A}}}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa_{1,A}}}(\kappa_{c}+\kappa_{h}+\Upsilon_{s})

),

, the above two displays lead to

\displaystyle\text{Pred}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s},\frac{\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|}{\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}\right\}+\frac{3}{2}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|+{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\bar{W}_{k}\|\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|\hat{\Delta}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\bar{W}_{k}\|\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|^{2}-{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{1}{2}\hat{\Delta}_{k}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\kappa_{1,A}}}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\right){\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\bar{W}_{k}\|\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|-{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s},\frac{\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|}{\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}\right\}+\frac{2.5}{\sqrt{\kappa_{1,A}}}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\|\vartheta_{k}\|

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{\Delta_{\max}}{2\sqrt{\kappa_{1,A}}}+\frac{\kappa_{c}+\kappa_{h}+\Upsilon_{s}}{2\kappa_{1,A}}\right){\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\bar{W}_{k}\|\|\vartheta_{k}\|-{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|.

where

\bullet

Case 1.

\min\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\}\leq\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|/\|\bar{W}_{k}\|

\displaystyle\text{Pred}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\right\}-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\right\}+\Upsilon_{3}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|-{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\right\}+\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\right\}+\Upsilon_{3}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|-{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s},\frac{\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|}{\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}\right\}+\frac{\|\vartheta_{k}\|}{2}\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\right\}+\Upsilon_{3}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|-{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|,

since
\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|+\|\vartheta_{k}\|\geq\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|
and
\kappa_{fcd}\leq 1
. Therefore, (
21
) holds provided
{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\geq 0.5\|\vartheta_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\right\}+\Upsilon_{3}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|
, equivalently,
{\bar{\mu}}_{k}\geq\frac{\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\right\}}{2{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}}+\Upsilon_{3}
. By the definition of
{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}
in (
11
), if
{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}=1
, it suffices that
{\bar{\mu}}_{k}\geq 0.5\min\{\Delta_{\max},\epsilon_{s}\}+\Upsilon_{3}
. While if
{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}=\min\{\frac{\zeta\epsilon_{s}}{\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{k}^{s}\|},\frac{\zeta\Delta_{k}}{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|}\}
, since
\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{k}^{s}\|\leq\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|
, we have
{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\geq\min\{\frac{\zeta\epsilon_{s}}{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|},\frac{\zeta\Delta_{k}}{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|}\}=\zeta\frac{\min\{\epsilon_{s},\Delta_{k}\}}{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|}
. Thus, it suffices that
{\bar{\mu}}_{k}\geq\frac{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|}{2\zeta}+\Upsilon_{3}
. Noting that
\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa_{1,A}}}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\leq\frac{\kappa_{c}+\kappa_{h}+\Upsilon_{s}}{\sqrt{\kappa_{1,A}}}
, we further strengthen the condition to
{\bar{\mu}}_{k}\geq\frac{\kappa_{c}+\kappa_{h}+\Upsilon_{s}}{2\zeta\sqrt{\kappa_{1,A}}}+\Upsilon_{3}
. Combining the above two cases, it is sufficient to have
{\bar{\mu}}_{k}\geq 0.5\min\{\Delta_{\max},\epsilon_{s}\}+\frac{\kappa_{c}+\kappa_{h}+\Upsilon_{s}}{2\zeta\sqrt{\kappa_{1,A}}}+\Upsilon_{3}
.

\bullet

Case 2.

\min\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\}>\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|/\|\bar{W}_{k}\|
. In this case, (
51
) leads to

\displaystyle\text{Pred}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\frac{\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|^{2}}{\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}+\Upsilon_{3}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|-{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|(\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|+\|\vartheta_{k}\|)}{2\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}+\frac{\kappa_{fcd}\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\|\vartheta_{k}\|}{2\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}+\Upsilon_{3}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|-{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|.

Rearranging the terms and using
\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|+\|\vartheta_{k}\|\geq\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|
and
\kappa_{fcd}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\text{Pred}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\frac{\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|}{\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}+\frac{\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\|\vartheta_{k}\|}{2\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\min\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\min\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\}+\Upsilon_{3}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|-{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s},\frac{\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|}{\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}\right\}-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s},\frac{\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|}{\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\|\vartheta_{k}\|}{2\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}+\frac{1}{2}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\min\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\}+\Upsilon_{3}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|-{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\kappa_{fcd}}{2}\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s},\frac{\|\bar{Q}_{k}\|}{\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\|\vartheta_{k}\|}{2\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}+\frac{1}{2}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\min\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\}+\Upsilon_{3}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|-{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|.

Therefore, we only need
{\bar{\mu}}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\geq\frac{\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|\|\vartheta_{k}\|}{2\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}+\frac{1}{2}\|\vartheta_{k}\|\min\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\}+\Upsilon_{3}{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\vartheta_{k}\|
,
equivalently,
{\bar{\mu}}_{k}\geq\frac{\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|}{2{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\|\bar{W}_{k}\|}+\frac{\min\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\}}{2{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}}+\Upsilon_{3}
.
Since
\min\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\}>\|Z_{k}^{T}{\bar{\psi}}_{k}\|/\|\bar{W}_{k}\|
in this case, it suffices to have

{\bar{\mu}}_{k}\geq\frac{\min\{\Delta_{k},\epsilon_{s}\}}{{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}}+\Upsilon_{3}
. As in
Case 1
, this holds provided
{\bar{\mu}}_{k}\geq\min\{\Delta_{\max},\epsilon_{s}\}+\frac{\kappa_{c}+\kappa_{h}+\Upsilon_{s}}{\zeta\sqrt{\kappa_{1,A}}}+\Upsilon_{3}
.

Combining the conclusions in
Cases 1
and
2
, we know that there exists
{\bar{\mu}}=\min\{\Delta_{\max},\epsilon_{s}\}+\frac{\kappa_{c}+\kappa_{h}+\Upsilon_{s}}{\zeta\sqrt{\kappa_{1,A}}}+\Upsilon_{3}
such that (
21
) holds as long as
{\bar{\mu}}_{k}\geq{\bar{\mu}}
. By the updating scheme of the merit parameter, we can set
\hat{\mu}=\rho{\bar{\mu}}
and this completes the proof.

5.1
Algorithm setup

For TR-IP-SSQP, batches of samples are generated at each iteration to estimate the objective gradient and value. We use
|\cdot|

\displaystyle|\xi_{k}^{g}|=\frac{C_{g}}{p_{g}\kappa_{g}^{2}\Delta_{k}^{2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad|\xi_{k}^{f}|=|\xi_{s_{k}}^{f}|=\frac{C_{f}}{\min\{p_{f}\kappa_{f}^{2}\Delta_{k}^{4},\bar{\epsilon}_{k}^{2}\}},

where

The parameters for TR-IP-SSQP are set as follows:

Since trust-region methods permit indefinite Hessian matrices, we try four different

6
Conclusion

We proposed a trust-region interior-point stochastic sequential quadratic programming (TR-IP-SSQP) method for identifying first-order stationary points of optimization problems with stochastic objectives and deterministic nonlinear equality and inequality constraints. At each iteration, we constructed stochastic oracles that enforce proper adaptive accuracy conditions with a fixed probability. The method adopts a single-loop interior-point framework in which the barrier parameter follows a user-specified diminishing sequence. Under reasonable assumptions, we established liminf-type almost-sure convergence to first-order stationary points. We demonstrated the practical performance of our method on a subset of problems from the CUTEst test set and on logistic regression problems.
