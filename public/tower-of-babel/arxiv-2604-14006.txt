Coloring powers of random graphs
Alan Frieze, Ross J. Kang, Aditya Raut, Michelle Sweering, Hilde Verbeek

Abstract
Given a graph G G and an integer r ≥ 1 r\geq 1 , the r r th power G r G^{r} of G G is the graph obtained from G G by adding edges for all pairs of distinct vertices at distance at most r r from each other. We focus on two basic structural properties of the r r th power of the binomial random graph G n , p G_{n,p} , namely, the maximum degree Δ ⁡ ( G n , p r ) \Delta(G_{n,p}^{r}) and the chromatic number χ ⁡ ( G n , p r ) \chi(G_{n,p}^{r}) , and give with high probability (w.h.p.) bounds. In the sparse case that p = d / n p=d/n for some fixed constant d > 0 d>0 , we prove the following. We prove that w.h.p. Δ ⁡ ( G n , p r ) ∼ log ⁡ n log ( r + 1 ) ⁡ n \Delta(G_{n,p}^{r})\sim\frac{\log n}{\log_{(r+1)}n} (where log ( 1 ) ⁡ n = log ⁡ n \log_{(1)}n=\log n and log ( r + 1 ) ⁡ n = log ⁡ log ( r ) ⁡ n \log_{(r+1)}n=\log\log_{(r)}n ) and that w.h.p. Δ ⁡ ( G n , p ⌊ r / 2 ⌋ ) + 1 ≤ χ ⁡ ( G n , p r ) ≤ Δ ⁡ ( G n , p r − 1 ) + 1 \Delta(G_{n,p}^{{\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor}})+1\leq\chi(G_{n,p}^{r})\leq\Delta(G_{n,p}^{r-1})+1 . For r = 2 r=2 , we show the upper bound holds with equality. For denser cases, for d d satisfying d = ω ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) d=\omega(\log n) and d ≤ n 1 / r − Ω ⁡ ( 1 ) d\leq n^{1/r-\Omega(1)} as n → ∞ n\to\infty , we have χ ⁡ ( G n , p r ) = Θ ⁡ ( d r / log ⁡ d ) \chi(G_{n,p}^{r})=\Theta(d^{r}/\log d) w.h.p.

Introduction

The
r
th power
G^{r}
of a graph
G
is obtained from
G
by adding edges between all pairs of distinct vertices that are connected by a path of
r
or fewer edges in
G
. Powers of graphs arise naturally in various contexts, such as, for instance, number theory
[
28
]
, ad hoc communication networks
[
3
]
, and distributed computing
[
25
]
.
Note that
G
has diameter at most
r
if and only if
G^{r}
is a complete graph. We consider the
r
th power
G_{n,p}^{r}
of the binomial random graph
G_{n,p}
, which is the probability space of graphs on vertex set
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
in which each of the
\binom{n}{2}
pairs of vertices is included independently at random with probability
p
as an edge.
In particular, we focus on two basic properties of
G_{n,p}^{r}
, namely, its maximum degree
\Delta(G_{n,p}^{r})
and its chromatic number
\chi(G_{n,p}^{r})
.

(Recall that the maximum degree
\Delta(G)
of some graph
G
is the maximum number of neighbours a vertex in
G
has, while the chromatic number
\chi(G)
is the least number of colors needed in a coloring of all vertices so that no two endpoints of a vertex have the same color. We have that
\chi(G)\leq\Delta(G)+1
always.)

We seek good bounds on these parameters that hold with probability tending to
1
as
n\to\infty
, that is,
with high probability (w.h.p.)
.
Let
\log_{(k)}n
indicate the repeated application of the log-function
k
times. So, for example,
\log_{(3)}n=\log\log\log n
.

In the case
r=1
, we understand quite a lot about these parameters. For example, since the degree of any vertex is a binomial random variable, it is straightforward to show that, if
p=d/n
for some fixed constant
d>0
, then the maximum degree
\Delta(G_{n,p})
of
G_{n,p}
satisfies
\Delta(G_{n,p})\sim\frac{\log n}{\log_{(2)}n}
w.h.p. (see e.g.
[
15
, Thm. 3.4]
and
[
10
]
).
Because it is a much more ‘global’ parameter, the chromatic number
\chi(G_{n,p})
of
G_{n,p}
has been more difficult to pin down. Indeed, in the sparse regime, despite remarkable advances, see e.g.
[
1
,
12
,
8
]
, it remains an open problem of Erdős and Rényi
[
14
]
to determine the sharp threshold for
k
-colorability, if it exists. Nevertheless, due to celebrated work
[
26
,
9
,
27
,
30
]
(see
[
20
]
for further background), we know for instance that w.h.p.

\chi(G_{n,p})\sim\frac{n}{\alpha(G_{n,p})}\sim\frac{n\log(1/(1-p))}{\log n},

provided
np=\omega(1)
(where
\alpha(G)
denotes the independence number of
G
, the size of a largest independent set in
G
).

For
r>1
, it is intuitive that taking into consideration paths of length at most
r
could introduce additional ‘non-local’ complications.
We note that the problem of determining the minimum
r
for which
G_{n,p}^{r}
is guaranteed w.h.p. to be a clique is finely understood, see e.g.
[
11
,
24
,
29
,
2
]
.
There has also been much attention for coloring powers of not-necessarily-random graphs, see e.g.
[
5
,
3
,
18
,
6
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
.
It might be surprising how comparatively little study of
\Delta(G_{n,p}^{r})
and
\chi(G_{n,p}^{r})
for
r>1
there is in the literature
[
7
,
17
,
16
]
.

For the maximum degree, Garapaty, Lokshtanov, Maji and Pothen
[
17
]
proved that if
p=d/n
where
d>0
is constant, then w.h.p. the maximum degree of
G_{n,p}^{r}
satisfies
\Delta(G_{n,p}^{r})=\Theta_{r}\left(\frac{\log n}{\log_{(r+1)}n}\right)
. They estimated the implicit multiplicative factor in the range
[0.05\cdot 2^{-r},6]
. We strengthen this in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
p=d/n
, where
d>0
is a constant and let
r\geq 1
be a fixed positive integer. Then, w.h.p.
\Delta(G_{n,p}^{r})\sim\frac{\log n}{\log_{(r+1)}n}
.

We prove Theorem
1
in Section
2
.

For the chromatic number, we show in the sparse regime, with
p=d/n
for some fixed constant
d
, that
\chi(G_{n,p}^{2})
is essentially determined by the clique in
G_{n,p}^{2}
induced by the vertex of maximum degree in
G_{n,p}
and all its direct neighbours.

Theorem 2
.

Let
p=d/n
, where
d>0
is a constant. Then, w.h.p.
\chi(G_{n,p}^{2})=\Delta(G_{n,p})+1
.

We prove this in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we partially generalize the result to
r\geq 2
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
p=d/n
, where
d>0
is a constant and let
r\geq 2
be a fixed positive integer. Then, w.h.p.
\Delta(G_{n,p}^{{\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor}})\leq\chi(G_{n,p}^{r})\leq\Delta(G_{n,p}^{r-1})+1
.

Within the context of Theorem
3
, it was earlier shown by Garapaty, Lokshtanov, Maji and Pothen
[
17
]
that
\chi(G_{n,p}^{2})=\Theta(\Delta(G_{n,p}))
w.h.p. Frieze and Raut
[
16
]
later proved that the
list chromatic number
of
G_{n,p}^{2}
satisfies
\chi_{\ell}(G_{n,p}^{2})\sim\Delta(G_{n,p})
w.h.p.

We will also show at the other end for a reasonably large range of more moderate densities — namely, those
d=np
satisfying both
d=\omega(\log n)
and
d=n^{1/r-\Omega(1)}
— that
\chi(G_{n,p}^{r})
is essentially determined by the independence number
\alpha(G_{n,p}^{r})
of
G_{n,p}^{r}
. (Recall that the independence number
\alpha(G)
of a graph
G
is the maximum cardinality of an independent set of
G
, a vertex subset spanning no edges.) In this density regime, Alon and Mohar
[
5
]
proved that
\alpha(G_{n,p}^{r})=O((n\log d)/d^{r})
w.h.p. In Section
5
we will prove the following.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\varepsilon
be an arbitrarily small positive constant and let
r\geq 1
be a fixed positive integer. Let
d=d(n)
satisfy
d=\omega\log n
where
\omega\to\infty
and
d\leq n^{1/r-\varepsilon}
as
n\to\infty
, and set
p=p(n)=d/n
. Then
\chi(G_{n,p}^{r})=\Theta(d^{r}/\log d)
w.h.p.

One can compare this to the general bound
\chi(G^{r})\leq\Delta(G)^{r}+1
for any
G
, keeping in mind that
\Delta(G_{n,p})\sim d
w.h.p. in the range of edge densities prescribed in
Theorem

4
.
If
d
satisfies
d=\omega((n\log n)^{1/r})
, then w.h.p.
G_{n,p}^{r}
is a clique
[
24
]
, in which case
\chi(G_{n,p}^{r})=n
, and so the upper bound in the range for
p
above is nearly best possible.
Alon and Mohar
[
5
]
obtained similar estimates for
\chi(G^{r})
when
G
has sufficiently large girth; see also
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
.

rather than
r=2
. Given

where
G=G_{n,p}
, define

as the set of vertices at distance at most

from

in
G
. Let
N_{r}(S)=\bigcup\limits_{v\in S}N_{r}(v)
. Also, let

denote the maximum degree in
G^{s}
.

Claim 1
.

If
G[S\cup N_{r}(S)]
is a forest, then
G^{r}
can be vertex-colored with
\Delta_{r-1}+1
colors.

Proof.

We first claim that
S
can be colored in
G^{r}
with
\Delta_{r-1}+1

S
in
G^{r}
are precisely
N_{r}(S)
, hence while coloring

we can restrict our attention to the forest
G[S\cup N_{r}(S)]
. Assign a root to each tree in the forest. We color the
S
vertices in this forest in a top-down approach. Assume that the top
k
layers in a Breadth First Search starting with the root of a tree in
G[S\cup N_{r}(S)]
are colored such that the coloring is proper in
G^{r}
. Consider a vertex
v\in S
in the
(k+1)
-th layer. All the colored vertices which are up to distance
r
from
v
must be up to distance
r-1
from its parent
u
in the
k
-th layer. Since
\Delta_{r-1}
is the maximum degree in
G^{r-1}
,
u
has at most
\Delta_{r-1}-1
already colored neighbors other than
v
within distance
r-1
in
G
. Thus,
v
has at most
\Delta_{r-1}
already colored neighbors among
S\cup N_{r}(S)
in
G^{r}
and we have an available color among
\Delta_{r-1}+1
colors to assign to
v
. We thus conclude that
S
can be colored with
\Delta_{r-1}+1
colors in
G^{r}
. Now by the definition of
S
, all vertices outside
S
have degree at most
\Delta_{r-1}
in
G^{r}
. We can thus greedily color all the remaining vertices with
\Delta_{r-1}+1
colors.
∎

Claim 2
.

G[S\cup N(S)]
is a forest w.h.p.

Proof.

Suppose that
s,t=O(1)
. The number
Z_{s,t}
of vertices within distance
s
of a cycle of length at most
t
in
G
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Z_{s,t})\leq\sum_{i=0}^{s+t}n^{i}i!p^{i}=O(d^{s+t})=O(1).

(5)

So Markov’s inequality implies that
Z_{s,t}\leq\log\log n
w.h.p. This implies that w.h.p. any vertex on a cycle of length at most 6 has degree at most
\log\log n
.

We will compute an upper bound on the probability of a cycle occurring in
G[S\cup N(S)]
. Recall that vertices in
S
have more than
\Delta
vertices in their distance-2 neighbourhood. Thus, if a vertex is in
S

or
in
N(S)
, it must have at least
\Delta
vertices in its distance-3 neighbourhood in
G[S\cup N(S)]
.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that
S\cup N(S)
does not induce a forest. Then it must induce some cycle. By what we have shown, this cycle must be longer than
6
w.h.p.

Consider an induced cycle in the induced graph
G[S\cup N(S)]
and let
\ell
be its length. Note that at every vertex there must be at least
\Delta
vertices in the distance-3 neighbourhood on the induced graph. By the minimality of the cycle, it has
\lfloor\ell/6\rfloor
vertices
S^{\prime}
at distance at least 6 from each other in the induced graph. Note that in the induced graph, any vertex can only be distance 3 from at most one vertex in
S^{\prime}
.

Next let
q
denote the probability that vertex 1 has at least
\Delta_{1}=\Delta-6
vertices at distance at most 3 in
G
. Then, let
k_{i},i=1,2,3
is the number of vertices in level
i
of the breadth first search tree that contains these
\Delta_{1}
vertices. Note that the absence of a 6-cycle implies that
k_{3}\geq(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3})/3
. Then

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K=\Delta_{1}}^{3\Delta_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=K\\
k_{3}\geq\Delta_{1}/3\end{subarray}}\binom{n}{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},n-1-K}\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)^{k_{1}}\left(1-\left(1-\frac{d}{n}\right)^{k_{1}}\right)^{k_{2}}\left(1-\left(1-\frac{d}{n}\right)^{k_{2}}\right)^{k_{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K=\Delta_{1}}^{3\Delta_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=K\\
k_{3}\geq\Delta_{1}/3\end{subarray}}\frac{n^{K}}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)^{k_{1}}\left(\frac{dk_{1}}{n}\right)^{k_{2}}\left(\frac{dk_{2}}{n}\right)^{k_{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K=\Delta_{1}}^{3\Delta_{1}}(de)^{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=K\\
k_{3}\geq\Delta_{1}/3\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{e^{k_{3}}k_{3}!}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K=\Delta_{1}}^{3\Delta_{1}}(de)^{K}\binom{K-1}{2}\frac{1}{e^{K/3}(K/3)!}=o(1),

since the term
(K/3)!
grows faster than
(de)^{K}
.

We conclude that the probability that
G[S\cup N(S)]
contains a cycle and the shortest cycle has length at least 6 is at most

\sum_{6\leq\ell\leq n}\mathbb{P}(S\cup N(S)\text{ contains an induced $\ell$-cycle})\leq\sum_{6\leq\ell\leq n}n^{\ell}\cdot\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)^{\ell}\cdot q^{{\left\lfloor\ell/6\right\rfloor}}\leq d^{6}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(d^{6}q)^{m}=o(1).

The factor
q^{{\left\lfloor\ell/6\right\rfloor}}
arises from the fact that the vertices in
S^{\prime}
are at distance at least 6.
∎

The combination of Claim
1
(with
r=2
) and Claim
2
completes the proof of the upper bound.
∎

Proof of Theorem

We follow the same path as in Section
3
, though the calculations are slightly more involved. Let
S
be the set of all vertices that have degree greater than
\Delta_{r-1}
in
G^{r}
. For the lower bound we observe that the set of vertices at distance at most
{\left\lfloor r/2\right\rfloor}
from a vertex
v
form a clique in
G_{n,p}^{r}
. The upper bound in Theorem
3
follows from Claim
1
and the following lemma.

Lemma 6
.

G[S\cup N_{r}(S)]
is a forest w.h.p.

Proof.

Putting
s=r,t=8r
in (
5
) we see that w.h.p. any vertex within distance
r
of a cycle of length at most
8r
has degree at most
\log\log n\ll\Delta_{r-1}
, by Theorem
1
. This establishes that w.h.p. the short cycles in
G
(length up to
8r
) are at distance greater than
r
from
S\cup N_{r}(S)
, the set of high degree vertices and their neighbors up to distance
r
.

We will now compute an upper bound on the probability of a cycle occurring in
G[S\cup N_{r}(S)]
. Recall that vertices in
S
have more than
\Delta_{r-1}
vertices in their distance-
r
neighborhood. Thus, if a vertex is in
S\cup N_{r}(S)
, it must have at least
\Delta_{r-1}
vertices in its distance-
2r
neighborhood in
G[S\cup N_{r}(S)]
. Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that
S\cup N_{r}(S)
does not induce a forest. Then it must induce a cycle, which must be longer than
8r
w.h.p. Consider a cycle
C
of length
\ell>8r
in the induced graph
G[S\cup N_{r}(S)]
, on vertices
u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{\ell}
. For each
u_{i}
, let
v_{i}
denote a vertex of
S
which is closest to
u_{i}
in
G[S\cup N_{r}(S)]
. For each
i=1,2,..,\ell
, let
A_{i}
denote the set of vertices in
C
within distance
2r
of
u_{i}
along
C
, namely
A_{i}=\{u_{i-2r},...,u_{i},...,u_{i+2r}\}
where indices are modulo
\ell
. If
N_{r}(v_{i})\cap N_{r}(v_{j})\neq\varnothing
for some
u_{j}\notin A_{i}
, then we have a path of length at most
4r
from
u_{i}
to
u_{j}
in
G[S\cup N_{r}(S)]
, namely
u_{i}\rightsquigarrow v_{i}\rightsquigarrow v_{j}\rightsquigarrow u_{j}
. Since
\ell>8r
, we can replace the larger part of the cycle between
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
(strictly greater than
4r
in length) with this path to reduce the length of the cycle. Since
G[S\cup N_{r}(S)]
cannot have short cycles, eventually we obtain a cycle
C
such that for any
u_{i}\in C
,
N_{r}(v_{i})
is disjoint from
N_{r}(v_{j})
for all
u_{j}\in C\setminus A_{i}
. In this way we can divide
C
into
\lfloor\frac{\ell}{4r}\rfloor
disjoint arcs of length
4r
. The set
T
of central vertices of these arcs satisfies the property that their respective closest neighbors from
S
have disjoint neighborhoods up to distance
r
in the induced graph
G[S\cup N_{r}(S)]
. Thus any vertex from
S\cup N_{r}(S)
can be distance at most
2r
away from at most one vertex from
T
. For any
u_{i}\in T
, call the path
u_{i}\rightsquigarrow v_{i}
along with the induced graph on the neighborhood
N_{r}(v_{i})\setminus C
the
neighborhood structure
of
u_{i}
. The neighborhood structures of all vertices in
T
are pairwise disjoint.

Now we obtain an upper bound on the probability that
G[S\cup N_{r}(S)]
contains such a cycle of length
\ell>8r
along with the disjoint neighborhood structures at
\lfloor\frac{\ell}{4r}\rfloor
vertices of
T
. Note that vertices in
T
are at distance multiples of
4r
along
C
from other vertices in
T
. Hence, fixing which of
\{u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor}\}
appears in
T
fixes the other vertices of
T
.
We have

\displaystyle\sum_{8r\leq\ell\leq n}\mathbb{P}\left(\text{$S\cup N_{r}(S)$ contains a cycle $C$ of length $\ell$ with $\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor$ disjoint nbhd structures}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{8r\leq\ell\leq n}\binom{n}{\ell}\frac{(\ell-1)!}{2}\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)^{\ell}\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\text{disjoint nbhd structures at $\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor$ vertices $u_{i}\in C$}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{8r\leq\ell\leq n}d^{\ell}\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\text{disjoint nbhd structures exactly at $u_{1+4r\cdot i}$, $\forall i=0,...,\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor-1$}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{8r\leq\ell\leq n}d^{\ell}\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\forall i=0,...,\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor-1,\begin{aligned} &\text{ $\exists$ path $P_{i}:u_{1+4ri}\rightsquigarrow v_{1+4ri}$ of length $t_{i}\leq r$}\\
&\text{and $|N_{r}(v_{1+4ri})|\geq\Delta_{r-1}-2r+t_{i}$}\\
&\text{outside $C\cup P_{i}\cup\bigcup\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}N_{r}(v_{j})$}\end{aligned}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{8r\leq\ell\leq n}d^{\ell}\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor-1}\left(\begin{aligned} &\sum_{t_{i}=1}^{r}\binom{n-\ell-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{i-1}|N_{r}(v_{1+4rk})\setminus C|}{t_{i}}t_{i}!\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)^{t_{i}}\times\\
&\mathbb{P}\left(\text{$|N_{r}(v_{1+4ri})|\geq\Delta_{r-1}-2r+t_{i}$ outside $C\cup P_{i}\cup\bigcup\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}N_{r}(v_{j})$}\right)\end{aligned}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{8r\leq\ell\leq n}d^{\ell}\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor-1}\left(\sum_{t_{i}=1}^{r}\binom{n}{t_{i}}t_{i}!\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)^{t_{i}}\times\mathbb{P}\left(\text{$|N_{r}(v_{1+4ri})|\geq\Delta_{r-1}-2r+t_{i}$}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{8r\leq\ell\leq n}d^{\ell}\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor-1}\left(\left(\frac{d^{r+1}-d}{d-1}\right)\times\frac{\sum\limits_{d=\Delta_{r-1}-2r}^{\Delta_{r}-r}d^{r}}{n^{1-\varepsilon/2}}\right),\qquad\text{$n^{1-\varepsilon/2}$ is from~\eqref{extra}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{8r\leq\ell\leq n}d^{\ell}\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor\cdot\left(\left(\frac{d^{r+1}-d}{d-1}\right)\times\frac{\Delta_{r}^{r+1}}{n^{1-\varepsilon/2}}\right)^{\lfloor\ell/4r\rfloor}

\displaystyle\leq 8r\cdot d^{4r-1}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}m\left(d^{4r}\cdot\left(\frac{d^{r+1}-d}{d-1}\right)\cdot\frac{\Delta_{r}^{r+1}}{n^{1-\varepsilon/2}}\right)^{m}.

This approaches 0 as
n\rightarrow\infty
, since
\Delta_{r-1}\ll\log n\implies\frac{\Delta_{r}^{r+1}}{n^{1-\varepsilon/2}}\rightarrow 0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4

In the proof, we will need to bound the density of vertex neighbourhoods in
G_{n,p}^{r}
and apply the following theorem by Alon, Krivelevich and Sudakov
[
4
]
. We note a recent improvement in the leading constant for this result in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 7

(
[
4
]
, cf.
[
13
]
)
.

There exists a constant
c
such that for every graph
H
of maximum degree
\Delta
and real number
t
it holds that if

•

2\leq t\leq\Delta
, and

•

for all
v\in V(H)
the neighbourhood of
v
spans at most
\Delta^{2}/t
edges,

then
\chi(H)\leq c\Delta/\log t
.

Again, we denote
G_{n,p}
by
G
and
\Delta(G_{n,p})
by
\Delta
for simplicity.

It is known that w.h.p.
p=\omega\log n
implies that w.h.p. all degrees in
G
are
\sim np
, see for example Theorem 3.4(ii) of
[
15
]
. (Here
A\sim B
means that
A=(1+o(1))B
as
n\to\infty
and
A\lesssim B
means
A\leq(1+o(1))B
.)

5.1
Upper bound

Fix
v\in[n]
and let
N_{i}=N_{i}(v)
denote the vertices at distance
i
from
v
in
G
and let
N(v)
denote the neighbors of
v
in
G^{r}
. Then,

\displaystyle|N(v)|=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|N_{i}(v)|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Delta^{i}\sim\Delta^{r}.

(6)

On the other hand, we have from the Chernoff bounds, that with
\theta=1/(np)^{1/2}
and
i\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|N_{i}(v)|\leq(1-\theta)np|N_{i-1}(v)|\;\bigg|\;|N_{i-1}(v)|\geq\left(\frac{np}{2}\right)^{i-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{\theta^{2}(n-\Delta^{i-1})(np/2)^{i-1}p}{4}\right\}=o(n^{-1}).

(7)

So w.hp., the upper bound in (
6
) is also an asymptotic lower bound. We need two more facts. Let
\nu_{0}=d^{1-\varepsilon}
.

W.h.p., for all
v\in[n],1\leq i\leq r
and
w\notin N_{i}(v)
,
|\left\{u\in N_{i}(v):\left\{u,w\right\}\in E(G)\right\}|\leq\nu_{0}.

(8)

W.h.p., for all
v\in[n]
and
w\in N(v)
,
|\left\{u\in N(v):\left\{u,w\right\}\in E(G)\right\}|\leq\nu_{0}
.

(9)

Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\neg\eqref{up3})

\displaystyle\leq n^{2}\binom{\Delta^{i}}{\nu_{0}}p^{\nu_{0}}\leq n^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta^{i}pe}{\nu_{0}}\right)^{\nu_{0}}\leq n^{2}\left(\frac{2d^{r+\varepsilon}e}{n}\right)^{d^{1-\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq n^{2}\left(\frac{2en^{(r+\varepsilon)(1/r-\varepsilon)}}{n}\right)^{d^{1-\varepsilon}}=o(1).

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\neg\eqref{up4})

\displaystyle\leq n^{2}\binom{\Delta^{r}}{\nu_{0}}^{\nu_{0}}p^{\nu_{0}}\leq n^{2}\left(\frac{2d^{r+\varepsilon}e}{n}\right)^{\nu_{0}}=o(1).

It follows from (
8
) and (
9
) that the number of paths of length at most
r
in
G^{r}
that join pairs of vertices in
N(v)
is at most
\Delta^{2r-1}\nu_{0}
. Note that when we trace a path between a pair of vertices, there are always at most
\Delta
choices for the next vertex and there is at least one time where the number of choices is at most
\nu_{0}
. Because
\Delta/\nu_{0}\gtrsim d^{\varepsilon/2}
, we can apply Theorem
7
with
t\sim d^{\varepsilon/2}
. Thus w.h.p.,

\chi(G^{r})\lesssim\frac{2cd^{r}}{\varepsilon\log d}.

5.2
Lower bound

Let
X_{k}
denote the number of
k
-sets in
G^{r}
for which no pair of vertices are connected by a path of length
r
. We show that
X_{k}=0
w.h.p., for
k\geq k_{0}=\alpha n\log d/d^{r}
where
\alpha=10r!
. Now

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X_{k_{0}})\leq\binom{n}{k_{0}}\exp\left\{-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2(D+\mu)}\right\}

(10)

where, given a fixed
k_{0}
-set, the expected number of number of paths
\mu
satisfies

\mu=\binom{k_{0}}{2}\binom{n-2}{r-1}p^{r}\sim\frac{\alpha d^{r}\log d}{2(r-1)!}\text{ and }D=\sum_{P\sim Q}\mathbb{P}(P,Q),

where
P,Q
run over all distinct pairs of paths of length
r
that (i) join a pair of vertices in
[k_{0}]
and (ii) share at least one edge.

Inequality (
10
) is an example of Janson’s inequality
[
19
]
. Now denote by
{\mathcal{Q}}
, the set of paths
Q
that share an edge with the path
P_{0}=(1,2,\ldots,r+1)
in
K_{n}
. Then, where in the following,
i
denotes the number of edges that a path
Q
shares with
P_{0}
and
j
denotes the number of common vertices,

D=\mu\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{Q}}}\mathbb{P}(Q\mid P_{0})\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{r}\binom{r}{i}\binom{k_{0}}{r+1-j}p^{r-i}\leq\frac{r\mu}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\binom{k_{0}}{r-i}p^{r-i}\leq r\mu k_{0}p\leq\mu.

Applying (
10
),

\mathbb{E}(X_{k_{0}})\leq\left(\frac{ne}{k_{0}}\right)^{k_{0}}\exp\left\{-\frac{\mu}{4}\right\}\leq\left(\frac{ed^{r}}{\alpha\log d}\cdot d^{-\alpha/4}\right)^{k_{0}}=o(1).

Thus, w.h.p., there are no independent sets of size
k_{0}
in
G^{r}
, in which case we have that w.h.p.

\chi(G^{r})\geq\frac{n}{k_{0}}=\frac{d^{r}}{4r\log d}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
4
. ∎

6
Concluding remarks

As hinted at in the discussion preceding their statements, Theorems
3
and
4
might be representative of two nontrivial regimes in the behaviour of
G_{n,p}^{r}
, one in which the chromatic number is locally determined, and the other in which it is more globally determined.

Writing
\omega(G)
for the clique number of a graph
G
, we pose the following conjecture to make concrete this allusion.

Conjecture 8
.

Let
p=d/n
, where
d>0
is a constant and let
r\geq 1
be a fixed positive integer. Then, w.h.p.
\chi(G_{n,p}^{r})=\Theta(\max\{\omega(G_{n,p}^{r}),n/\alpha(G_{n,p}^{r})\})
.

The second term in the maximization coincides with the bound of Theorem
4
. The first corresponds with the bound in Theorem
3
, as well as the (higher) densities for which
G_{n,p}
has diameter at most
r
w.h.p. The main challenge in this conjecture is to determine the transitions between these three density regimes, which we leave to future investigations. It would also be interesting to find the leading constant implicit in the
\Theta
(assuming the conjecture holds), in particular whether it could be (asymptotically) 1.

Our conjecture suggests that, at least in the sparse regime, the correct value is closer to the lower bound in Theorem
3
, due to the following observation.

Lemma 9
.

Let
p=d/n
, where
d>0
is a constant and let
r\geq 1
be a fixed positive integer. Then, w.h.p.
\omega(G_{n,p}^{r})\leq\Delta(G_{n,p}^{{\left\lceil r/2\right\rceil}})+1
.

Proof.

Let
W
denote a maximum clique in
G^{r}_{n,p}
. Note that
\Delta(G^{\left\lceil(r-1)/2\right\rceil}_{n,p})\leq|W|\leq\Delta(G^{r}_{n,p})+1=o(\log n)
. We argue now that since
|W|=s_{0}=o(\log n)
, w.h.p.
W
contains at most
s_{0}
edges. The probability that our assertion fails to be true is at most

\displaystyle\binom{n}{s_{0}}\binom{\binom{s_{0}}{2}}{s_{0}+1}\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)^{s_{0}+1}\leq\left(\frac{ne}{s_{0}}\right)^{s_{0}}\left(\frac{s_{0}e}{2}\right)^{s_{0}+1}\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)^{s_{0}+1}=\frac{e^{2s_{0}+1}d^{s_{0}+1}s_{0}}{2^{s_{0}+1}n}=o(1).

(11)

We next observe that
W
must contain at least one vertex of degree at least

s_{1}=\Delta(G^{\left\lceil(r-1)/2\right\rceil}_{n,p})^{1/r}/2.

Then we claim that
G_{n,p}
contains no vertex
v
of degree at least
s_{1}
that is within distance
r
of a cycle of length at most
2r
. The probability that our assertion fails to be true is at most

\displaystyle n\cdot\sum_{s\geq s_{1}}\binom{n-1}{s}p^{s}\cdot s\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{r}n^{k}p^{k}\cdot\sum_{\ell=3}^{2r}n^{\ell-1}p^{\ell}\leq 2rd^{3r}\sum_{s\geq s_{1}}\frac{d^{s}}{(s-1)!}=o(1).

(12)

We reason for this as follows:
n
choices for
v
; then sum over
s
a bound on the probability of
s
neighbors; then
s
choices for the start of a path to a cycle
C
; then sum over
k
a bound on the probability of there being a path of length
k
to
C
; then sum over
\ell
a bound on the probability of a cycle
C
.

It follows from (
11
) and (
12
) that
W
induces a tree in
G_{n,p}
. Following this we can use the fact that in a tree, the radius is
{\left\lceil D/2\right\rceil}
, where
D
is the diameter. So, we let
T
be any spanning tree of
W
and take
D=r
.
∎
