Bounds on the radius of black hole shadows in n-dimensional Einstein gravity
Jiaqi Fu, Yong Song

Abstract
The dark shadow cast by a black hole, determined by the outermost unstable null circular geodesics (the photon sphere), provides a direct probe of strong-field gravity. In this work, we derive model-independent lower and upper bounds on the shadow radius r sh r_{\mathrm{sh}} for static, spherically symmetric, asymptotically flat black holes in n n -dimensional ( n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 ) Einstein gravity, supported by an anisotropic matter field. For the lower bound, assuming the matter satisfies the Weak Energy Condition (WEC), we prove r sh ≥ ( n − 1 2 ) 1 n − 3 ​ n − 1 n − 3 ​ r H r_{\mathrm{sh}}\geq\bigl(\frac{n-1}{2}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{n-3}}\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n-3}}\,r_{H} , where r H r_{H} is the horizon radius. For the upper bound, under the WEC and the Strong Energy Condition (SEC), together with an asymptotic decay condition on the matter fields, we prove r sh ≤ n − 1 n − 3 ​ [ ( n − 1 ) ​ M ] 1 n − 3 r_{\mathrm{sh}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n-3}}\bigl[(n-1)M\bigr]^{\frac{1}{n-3}} , where M M is the ADM mass. These results reduce to the known four-dimensional bounds and are saturated by the vacuum Schwarzschild-Tangherlini black hole. Our results generalize the four-dimensional shadow bounds to an arbitrary number of dimensions and provide model-independent geometric constraints on the observable shadow of higher-dimensional black hole spacetimes.



I
Introduction

Black holes, as fundamental predictions of general relativity and key astrophysical objects, continue to be a central focus of theoretical and observational research. The recent direct imaging of black hole shadows by the Event Horizon Telescope (EHT)
EventHorizonTelescope:2019dse
;
EventHorizonTelescope:2022wkp
has provided unprecedented insight into the strong-field regime of gravity and firmly established black hole shadows as powerful probes of spacetime geometry near compact objects.

A central geometric feature determining the apparent size of the shadow is the photon sphere—a null circular geodesic on which photons can orbit the black hole
Bardeen:1972fi
;
Cardoso:2008bp
. More precisely, the shadow radius
r_{\mathrm{sh}}
is set by the outermost unstable photon sphere, whose radius
r_{\gamma}
is directly related to
r_{\mathrm{sh}}
via gravitational lensing relations
Virbhadra:1999nm
;
Stefanov:2010xz
. As demonstrated in Ref.
Guo:2022ghl
, black hole spacetimes can possess multiple photon spheres, including stable ones sandwiched between unstable ones. In such configurations, the observed shadow is determined exclusively by the outermost unstable photon sphere; inner unstable spheres and stable spheres are hidden from asymptotic observers by the outer photon sphere. Therefore, any physically meaningful bound on the shadow radius must refer to this outermost unstable photon sphere.

For the four-dimensional Schwarzschild black hole, the shadow radius satisfies
r_{\mathrm{sh}}=3\sqrt{3}M
, and the photon sphere is located at
r_{\gamma}=3M

Yang:2019zcn
. For more general static, spherically symmetric, asymptotically flat black holes surrounded by matter fields (so-called “hairy” black holes)
Nunez:1996xv
;
Hod:2011aa
, it has been shown that the Weak Energy Condition (WEC) leads to a lower bound on the shadow-to-horizon ratio,
r_{\mathrm{sh}}/r_{H}\geq 3\sqrt{3}/2

Hod:2026jtr
, which is saturated by the vacuum Schwarzschild solution. Moreover, the WEC and the Strong Energy Condition (SEC) together lead to an upper bound
r_{\mathrm{sh}}\leq 3\sqrt{3}M

Yang:2019zcn
, where
M
is the ADM mass. Upper bounds on the shadow radius have also been discussed in four-dimensional spacetimes under various energy conditions
Lu:2019zxb
;
Feng:2019zzn
;
Ma:2019ybz
.

With growing interest in higher-dimensional theories of gravity—motivated by string theory, brane-world scenarios, and the gauge/gravity duality—it is natural and important to ask whether analogous universal bounds exist for the black hole shadow radius in
n
-dimensional (
n\geq 4
) Einstein gravity. Previous studies have addressed bounds on innermost photon spheres in higher dimensions
Song:2026cci
, yet a systematic derivation of both lower and upper bounds on the shadow radius, applicable to general hairy higher-dimensional black holes and relying only on physically reasonable energy conditions, remains lacking.

In this work, we fill this gap by deriving universal lower and upper bounds on the shadow radius
r_{\mathrm{sh}}
for static, spherically symmetric, asymptotically flat black holes in
n
-dimensional Einstein gravity, supported by an anisotropic matter field satisfying the Weak Energy Condition (WEC) and the Strong Energy Condition (SEC). For the lower bound, using the WEC, we prove that

\displaystyle\frac{r_{\mathrm{sh}}}{r_{H}}\geq\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-3}}\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n-3}}\;,

(1)

which reduces in four dimensions to Hod’s result
r_{\mathrm{sh}}\geq\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}r_{H}
. For the upper bound, under the WEC and the SEC, supplemented by an asymptotic decay condition on the matter fields, and by comparing the photon sphere effective potential with that of the vacuum Schwarzschild-Tangherlini spacetime, we show that the shadow radius is bounded from above by the vacuum value,

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{sh}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n-3}}\,\bigl[(n-1)M\bigr]^{\frac{1}{n-3}}\;,

(2)

which recovers
r_{\mathrm{sh}}\leq 3\sqrt{3}M
when
n=4
. These bounds are purely geometric and model-independent, depending only on the dimension, the horizon radius, and the ADM mass, and they are saturated by the vacuum higher-dimensional Schwarzschild-Tangherlini black hole.

The present paper is organized as follows: In Sec.
II
we describe the general
n
-dimensional spherically symmetric black hole spacetime and present the Einstein field equations for an anisotropic fluid. In Sec.
III
we derive the lower bound on the shadow radius under the WEC. In Sec.
IV

V

G=c=1
.

Description of the system in higher-dimensional black holes

Consider a static, spherically symmetric, asymptotically flat black hole configuration in
n
-dimensional spacetime. The
n

\displaystyle S=\int d^{n}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{R}{16\pi}+\mathcal{L}_{M}\right)\;,

(3)

where
R

g

The spacetime metric can be written in Schwarzschild-like coordinates as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-e^{-2\delta(r)}\mu(r)\,dt^{2}+\mu(r)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega_{n-2}^{2}\;,

(4)

where the metric functions
\delta(r)
and
\mu(r)

r

\displaystyle d\Omega_{n-2}^{2}=d\theta_{1}^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}^{2}+\cdots+\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-3}\sin^{2}\theta_{i}\right)d\theta_{n-2}^{2}

(5)

(n-2)

The regularity of the event horizon at

requires that

\displaystyle\mu(r_{H})=0\quad\text{with}\quad\mu^{\prime}(r_{H})\geq 0\;.

(6)

Asymptotic flatness imposes

\displaystyle\mu(r\rightarrow\infty)\rightarrow 1\quad\text{and}\quad\delta(r\rightarrow\infty)\rightarrow 0\;.

(7)

Here we do not assume
\delta(r)\equiv 0
, so our results are also applicable to hairy black hole configurations

Volkov:2016ehx

The energy-momentum tensor of the matter field is diagonal in the static orthonormal frame,

\displaystyle T^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(-\rho(r),p_{r}(r),\underbrace{p_{t}(r),p_{t}(r),\ldots,p_{t}(r)}_{n-2\ \text{terms}}\bigr)\;,

(8)

The Einstein equations
G^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=8\pi T^{\mu}{}_{\nu}

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-3)(1-\mu)}{r}-\frac{16\pi r\rho}{n-2}\;,

(9)

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{8\pi r\left(\rho+p_{r}\right)}{(n-2)\mu}\;,

(10)

where the prime denotes differentiation with respect to
r
. Substituting Eq. (

) and (

\displaystyle\rho(r_{H})\leq\frac{(n-3)(n-2)}{16\pi r_{H}^{2}}\;,\qquad p_{r}(r_{H})=-\rho(r_{H})\;.

(11)

The mass function
m(r)
enclosed within a sphere of radius
r
is defined by

\displaystyle m(r)=\frac{1}{2}r_{H}^{n-3}+\frac{8\pi}{n-2}\int_{r_{H}}^{r}\chi^{n-2}\rho(\chi)\,d\chi\;,

(12)

where
\frac{1}{2}r_{H}^{n-3}
is the horizon mass
m(r_{H})
. The ADM mass of the spacetime is
M\equiv m(r\to\infty)
. From Eqs. (
9
) and (

), the relation between

in
n
-dimensional spacetime takes the form

\displaystyle\mu(r)=1-\frac{2m(r)}{r^{n-3}}\;.

(13)

The requirement of a finite mass configuration implies

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}r^{n-1}\rho(r)=0\;.

(14)

Substituting Eqs. (
9
) and (
10
) into the energy-momentum conservation equation
\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}{}_{r}=0
yields

\displaystyle p_{r}^{\prime}=\frac{2T+(n-1)\rho-(n+1)p_{r}}{2r}-\left(\frac{n-3}{2r}+\frac{8\pi rp_{r}}{n-2}\right)\frac{\rho+p_{r}}{\mu}\;,

\displaystyle T=-\rho+p_{r}+(n-2)p_{t}

(16)

is the trace of the energy-momentum tensor
T^{\mu}{}_{\nu}

and Eq. (

(1)

\displaystyle\rho\geq 0\;,

(17)

and it bounds the pressures, which implies the inequalities

\displaystyle\rho+p_{r}\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\rho+p_{t}\geq 0\;.

(18)

\displaystyle(n-3)\rho+p_{r}+(n-2)p_{t}\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\rho+p_{r}\geq 0,\quad\rho+p_{t}\geq 0\;.

(19)

III
Lower Bound on the Shadow Radius of Black Holes

In this section, we study the lower bound on the dimensionless ratio
r_{\mathrm{sh}}/r_{H}
for hairy black holes satisfying the WEC.

The shadow radius
r_{\mathrm{sh}}
measured by an asymptotic observer is set by the outermost unstable photon sphere through the relation
Hod:2026jtr
;
Lu:2019zxb

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{sh}}=\frac{r_{\gamma}}{\sqrt{-g_{tt}(r_{\gamma})}}\;,

(20)

where
r_{\gamma}
is the radius of the outermost unstable photon sphere.

Using Eqs. (
4
), (
13
), and (
20
), one obtains the functional expression

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{sh}}=\frac{r_{\gamma}}{\sqrt{1-\frac{2m(r_{\gamma})}{r_{\gamma}^{n-3}}}}\,e^{\delta(r_{\gamma})}\;.

(21)

Assume the components of the energy-momentum tensor satisfy the WEC. From Eqs. (
10
) and (
18
), we see that the metric function
\delta(r)
is monotonically decreasing:

\displaystyle\frac{d\delta(r)}{dr}\leq 0\quad\text{for}\quad r\in[r_{H},\infty]\;.

(22)

Subsequently, using Eqs. (
7
) and (
22
), we obtain

\displaystyle\delta(r)\geq 0\quad\text{for}\quad r\in[r_{H},\infty]\;.

(23)

Introduce the algebraic variable
x
defined by

\displaystyle x=\frac{m(r_{\gamma})}{r_{\gamma}^{n-3}}\;.

(24)

Using the relations (
23
) and (
24
), Eq. (
21
) can be expressed in the form

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{sh}}\geq\frac{r_{\gamma}}{\sqrt{1-2x}}\;.

(25)

Eq. (
12
) yields specific conditions at the horizon. At
r=r_{H}
, it follows that

\displaystyle r_{H}^{n-3}=2m(r_{H})\leq 2m(r_{\gamma})=2xr_{\gamma}^{n-3}\;.

(26)

Therefore

\displaystyle r_{H}\leq(2x)^{\frac{1}{n-3}}r_{\gamma}\;.

(27)

By combining Eqs. (
25
) and (
27
), we find a manifest inequality between the shadow radius, the horizon radius, and
x
:

\displaystyle\frac{r_{\mathrm{sh}}}{r_{H}}\geq\frac{r_{\gamma}}{r_{H}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2x}}\geq\frac{1}{(2x)^{\frac{1}{n-3}}\sqrt{1-2x}}\;.

(28)

Let

\displaystyle h(x)=(2x)^{\frac{1}{n-3}}(1-2x)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad x\in(0,0.5)\;.

(29)

Note that the condition
\mu(r_{\gamma})>0
outside the horizon guarantees

for all non-extremal configurations. Taking the derivative of
h(x)
and setting
\partial h(x)/\partial x=0
, one easily finds that
h(x)
attains its extremum at
x=\frac{1}{n-1}
. It is straightforward to verify that this extremum is a maximum, whose value is

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{max}}=\left(\frac{2}{n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-3}}\cdot\sqrt{1-\frac{2}{n-1}}=\left(\frac{2}{n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-3}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{n-3}{n-1}}\;.

(30)

Substituting Eq. (
30
) into Eq. (
28
), we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{r_{\mathrm{sh}}}{r_{H}}\geq\frac{1}{h_{\mathrm{max}}}=\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-3}}\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n-3}}\;.

(31)

In four dimensions this reduces to
r_{\mathrm{sh}}/r_{H}\geq\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}
, which is consistent with Hod’s result
Hod:2026jtr
. Notably, this lower bound is saturated by the vacuum Schwarzschild-Tangherlini black hole; however, the presence of matter fields modifies this radius, rendering it larger than that of the “hairless” black hole. It is worth emphasizing that only the WEC has been employed in this section to derive the lower bound on the shadow radius, demonstrating that this bound for higher-dimensional black holes can be established solely under the assumption of the WEC.

IV
Upper Bound on the Shadow Radius of Black Holes

To establish the upper bound of the black hole shadow, we assume the components of the energy-momentum tensor satisfy the WEC and the SEC.

Denote the effective potential function for the photon sphere as
U(r)
, whose expression is given by
Yang:2019zcn

\displaystyle U(r)=\frac{\mu(r)e^{-2\delta(r)}}{r^{2}}\;.

(32)

The relation in Eq. (
20
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{sh}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{U(r_{\gamma})}}\;.

(33)

To facilitate the following derivation, we define an auxiliary function
G(r)
as

\displaystyle G(r)=r^{n-1}U=r^{n-3}\mu e^{-2\delta}\;.

(34)

Taking the derivative of
G(r)
and inserting Eqs. (
9
) and (
10
) yields

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(r)=r^{n-4}e^{-2\delta}\biggl[(n-3)+\frac{16\pi r^{2}p_{r}}{n-2}\biggr]\;.

(35)

Introduce a new auxiliary function
W(r)
defined as

\displaystyle W(r)=e^{-2\delta}\biggl[1+\frac{16\pi r^{2}p_{r}}{(n-2)(n-3)}\biggr]\;,

(36)

so that

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(r)=(n-3)r^{n-4}W(r)\;.

(37)

Outside the outermost photon sphere,
p_{r}
can be either non-positive or non-negative. To analyze the behavior of
W(r)
, we follow Ref.
Yang:2019zcn
and divide the interval
r\in[r_{\gamma},\infty)
into two groups:
I_{n}^{+}=\{I_{1}^{+},I_{2}^{+},\dots\}
where
p_{r}\geq 0
, and
I_{n}^{-}=\{I_{1}^{-},I_{2}^{-},\dots\}
where
p_{r}\leq 0
.

(1)

If
r\in I_{n}^{+}
, i.e.,
p_{r}\geq 0
, by differentiating the auxiliary function
W(r)
, and using Eqs. (
10
) and (
15
), one gets

\displaystyle W^{\prime}(r)=e^{-2\delta}\biggl\{\frac{16\pi r(\rho+p_{r})}{(n-2)(n-3)\mu}\biggl(\frac{n-3}{2}+\frac{8\pi r^{2}p_{r}}{n-2}\biggr)+\frac{16\pi r}{(n-2)(n-3)}\biggl[\frac{n-3}{2}\rho+\frac{5-n}{2}p_{r}+(n-2)p_{t}\biggr]\biggr\}\;.

(38)

From Eq. (
13
) and the WEC, one has
\mu(r)\leq 1
. Then we have

\displaystyle W^{\prime}(r)\geq e^{-2\delta}\biggl[\frac{16\pi r(\rho+p_{r})}{(n-2)(n-3)}\biggl(\frac{8\pi r^{2}p_{r}}{n-2}\biggr)+\frac{16\pi r}{(n-2)(n-3)}\bigl[(n-3)\rho+p_{r}+(n-2)p_{t}\bigr]\biggr]\;.

(39)

From
p_{r}\geq 0
and the SEC, we conclude that
W^{\prime}(r)
is always non-negative on
I_{n}^{+}
. This ensures that
W(r)
is non-decreasing on each interval
I_{n}^{+}
. Consequently, the maximum value of
W(r)
on each interval
I_{n}^{+}
occurs at the endpoint where
p_{r}=0
. Thus, we have
W(r)\leq 1
on
I_{n}^{+}
.

(2)

If
r\in I_{n}^{-}
, i.e.,
p_{r}\leq 0
, from Eq. (
36
) we obtain directly

\displaystyle W(r)\leq e^{-2\delta}\leq 1\;.

(40)

In summary, regardless of the sign of
p_{r}
, we always have
W(r)\leq 1
outside the outermost photon sphere.

Now construct a vacuum potential

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{vac}}=\frac{1-\frac{2M}{r^{n-3}}}{r^{2}}\;,

(41)

and define the corresponding auxiliary function
G_{\mathrm{vac}}=r^{n-1}U_{\mathrm{vac}}
, whose derivative is

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\prime}=(n-3)r^{n-4}\;.

(42)

When
W(r)\leq 1
, the following relation holds

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\prime}\geq G^{\prime}=(n-3)r^{n-4}W(r)\;.

(43)

To compare
G
and
G_{\mathrm{vac}}
, we consider their difference
H(r)=G_{\mathrm{vac}}(r)-G(r)
. The derivative satisfies
H^{\prime}(r)\geq 0
. To determine the integration constant we examine the asymptotic behavior of
H(r)
. Follow Ref.
Yang:2019zcn
, we assume that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}r^{n-1}p_{r}(r)=0\;,

(44)

and from the condition (
14
), the matter fields to decay at least in the following ways:

\displaystyle\rho(r)=\mathcal{O}\bigl(r^{-(n-1+\varepsilon_{1})}\bigr),\qquad p_{r}(r)=\mathcal{O}\bigl(r^{-(n-1+\varepsilon_{2})}\bigr)\quad\text{as }r\to\infty,

(45)

where
\varepsilon_{1}>0
and
\varepsilon_{2}>0
.

From Eq. (
10
) and the bounds on
\rho+p_{r}
, we have for large
r

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}(r)=-\frac{8\pi r(\rho+p_{r})}{(n-2)\mu}=\mathcal{O}\bigl(r^{-(n-2+\varepsilon)}\bigr),\qquad\varepsilon=\min(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})>0\;.

(46)

Integrating from
r
to
\infty
gives

\displaystyle\delta(r)=\int_{r}^{\infty}(-\delta^{\prime}(x))\,dx=\mathcal{O}\bigl(r^{-(n-3+\varepsilon)}\bigr)\;.

(47)

Hence

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{n-3}\delta(r)=0,\qquad e^{-2\delta(r)}=1+\mathcal{O}\bigl(r^{-(n-3+\varepsilon)}\bigr)\;.

(48)

The mass function (
12
) can be rewritten in the following form through the ADM mass

\displaystyle m(r)=M-\frac{8\pi}{n-2}\int_{r}^{\infty}\chi^{n-2}\rho(\chi)d\chi\;.

(49)

With
\rho(\chi)=\mathcal{O}(\chi^{-(n-1+\varepsilon_{1})})
, the integral is
\mathcal{O}(r^{-\varepsilon_{1}})
, so

\displaystyle\mu(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r^{n-3}}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(r^{-(n-3+\varepsilon_{1})}\bigr)\;.

(50)

Combining these, we find

\displaystyle G(r)

\displaystyle=r^{n-3}\mu(r)e^{-2\delta(r)}

\displaystyle=r^{n-3}\Bigl(1-\frac{2M}{r^{n-3}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-(n-3+\varepsilon_{1})})\Bigr)\Bigl(1+\mathcal{O}(r^{-(n-3+\varepsilon)})\Bigr)

\displaystyle=r^{n-3}-2M+\mathcal{O}\bigl(r^{-\epsilon}\bigr).

(51)

Therefore,

\displaystyle H(r)=G_{\mathrm{vac}}(r)-G(r)=\mathcal{O}\bigl(r^{-\epsilon}\bigr)\to 0\quad\text{as }r\to\infty.

(52)

Because
H(r)
is monotonically increasing (since
H^{\prime}(r)\geq 0
) and vanishes at infinity, it must be non-positive for all finite
r
:

\displaystyle H(r)\leq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad G_{\mathrm{vac}}(r)\leq G(r)\;.

(53)

From the definitions this implies
U_{\mathrm{vac}}(r)\leq U(r)
for all
r\geq r_{\gamma}
.

We now relate the position of the photon sphere to its vacuum counterpart. The photon sphere condition
U^{\prime}(r_{\gamma})=0
is equivalent to
r_{\gamma}G^{\prime}(r_{\gamma})=(n-1)G(r_{\gamma})
. Using
G^{\prime}(r)\leq G_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\prime}(r)=(n-3)r^{n-4}
and
G(r)\geq G_{\mathrm{vac}}(r)=r^{n-3}-2M
, we obtain

\displaystyle(n-1)(r_{\gamma}^{n-3}-2M)\leq(n-1)G(r_{\gamma})=r_{\gamma}G^{\prime}(r_{\gamma})\leq r_{\gamma}G_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\prime}(r_{\gamma})=(n-3)r_{\gamma}^{n-3}\;.

(54)

Rearranging yields
2r_{\gamma}^{n-3}\leq 2(n-1)M
, i.e.

\displaystyle r_{\gamma}\leq\bigl[(n-1)M\bigr]^{\frac{1}{n-3}}\equiv r_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{vac}}\;.

(55)

Thus the vacuum photon sphere lies outside the hairy photon sphere,
r_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{vac}}\geq r_{\gamma}
, and therefore belongs to the interval
[r_{\gamma},\infty)
.

Since
U(r)\geq U_{\mathrm{vac}}(r)
holds for all
r\geq r_{\gamma}
, the maximum of
U
is bounded from below by the maximum of
U_{\mathrm{vac}}
on the same interval. Because
r_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{vac}}\in[r_{\gamma},\infty)
, the global maximum of
U_{\mathrm{vac}}
on this interval is attained at
r_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{vac}}
, namely

\displaystyle\max_{[r_{\gamma},\infty)}U_{\mathrm{vac}}=U_{\mathrm{vac}}(r_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{vac}})=\frac{n-3}{n-1}\frac{1}{\bigl[(n-1)M\bigr]^{\frac{2}{n-3}}}.

(56)

Consequently,

\displaystyle U(r_{\gamma})=\max_{[r_{\gamma},\infty)}U\geq\max_{[r_{\gamma},\infty)}U_{\mathrm{vac}}=U_{\mathrm{vac}}(r_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{vac}}),

(57)

and therefore

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{sh}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{U(r_{\gamma})}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n-3}}\,\bigl[(n-1)M\bigr]^{\frac{1}{n-3}}.

(58)

Here we obtain an upper bound for the black hole shadow radius, which is consistent with the four-dimensional result
r_{\mathrm{sh}}\leq 3\sqrt{3}M
. When the inequality is saturated, the radius reduces to that of the vacuum solution.

V
Discussion and conclusion

In this work, we have derived the lower and upper bounds on the shadow radius of static, spherically symmetric, asymptotically flat higher-dimensional black holes within
n
-dimensional Einstein gravity (
n\geq 4
), supported by an anisotropic matter field. Under the assumption of the Weak Energy Condition (WEC), we proved the dimension-dependent lower bound

\displaystyle\frac{r_{\mathrm{sh}}}{r_{H}}\geq\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-3}}\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n-3}}.

(59)

For the four-dimensional case
n=4
, this reduces to
r_{\mathrm{sh}}\geq\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}r_{H}
, which exactly reproduces Hod’s result and is saturated by the vacuum Schwarzschild-Tangherlini black hole. This demonstrates that the presence of matter fields (hair) can only enlarge the shadow relative to the vacuum case, without requiring any additional assumptions on the matter content.

By further imposing the Strong Energy Condition (SEC) and the natural asymptotic decay conditions (
45
) on the matter fields, and comparing the photon-sphere effective potential with that of the vacuum Schwarzschild-Tangherlini spacetime, we established the upper bound

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{sh}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n-3}}\,\bigl[(n-1)M\bigr]^{\frac{1}{n-3}},

(60)

where
M
is the ADM mass of the spacetime. In four dimensions this recovers the well-known bound
r_{\mathrm{sh}}\leq 3\sqrt{3}M
, again saturated by the vacuum solution. The decay conditions (
45
) are physically mild and are satisfied by generic localized matter distributions, such as those arising from compact sources or fields with power-law or exponential fall-off. And they play a central role in fixing the integration constant in the comparison argument. Together, these two inequalities fully constrain the possible size of the black-hole shadow for this class of spacetimes.

It is important to clarify the scope and limitations of our results. The derivation relies on the Tangherlini-type metric ansatz with a direct-product structure and strict asymptotic flatness in all
n
dimensions. As such, our bounds do not apply to warped spacetimes, brane-world scenarios with induced gravity, or models with non-compact/large extra dimensions. In such frameworks, the photon-sphere structure and shadow size may deviate significantly from our predictions.

Several promising directions remain for future work. First, extending the analysis to rotating (axisymmetric) higher-dimensional black holes would be highly desirable, especially given the EHT observations of spinning supermassive black holes. Second, generalizing the bounds to asymptotically de Sitter or anti-de Sitter spacetimes would enable applications to gauge/gravity duality and cosmological black holes. Finally, exploring whether quantum-gravity corrections to the energy conditions or to the asymptotic decay conditions modify these bounds represents an interesting open problem at the interface of gravity and quantum theory.
