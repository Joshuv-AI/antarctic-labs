FOXK1: a multifaceted regulator in metabolic reprogramming and disease progression
Authors: Tingting He, Guangzhi Ma, Gang Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Forkhead box K1 (FOXK1) is an important member of the FOX transcription factor family and functions as a signal-responsive regulator of cellular metabolism, proliferation, differentiation, and stress adaptation. FOXK1 activity is tightly controlled by a multilayered regulatory network, including genetic and epigenetic alterations, transcriptional regulation, non-coding RNAs, and diverse post-translational modifications. These regulatory networks collectively coordinate FOXK1 stability, subcellular localization, and transcriptional activity in a context-dependent manner. Disruption of this tightly balanced regulatory system can lead to sustained FOXK1 activation and contribute to disease pathogenesis.In cancer, FOXK1 predominantly functions as an oncogenic transcription factor that promotes metabolic reprogramming, maintenance of cancer stem cell properties, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, invasion, metastasis, vasculogenic mimicry, and therapeutic resistance. Beyond oncology, emerging evidence further implicates FOXK1 in metabolic disorders, inflammatory diseases, reproductive dysfunction, and neurological conditions, suggesting broader context-dependent pathological functions. Given its central role in multiple disease-associated signaling networks, FOXK1 has emerged as a potential therapeutic target. In this review, we systematically summarize the structural characteristics and regulatory mechanisms of FOXK1, and comprehensively discuss its roles in physiological and pathological processes. We further highlight current advances in FOXK1-targeted therapeutic strategies and address the key challenges for clinical translation. This review provides a conceptual framework for future therapeutic strategies and rational drug development targeting FOXK1.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13062-026-00862-9.

Introduction

The FOXK subfamily comprises two closely related members, FOXK1 and FOXK2, which share substantial sequence homology within the forkhead domain and partially overlapping transcriptional properties. Despite this structural similarity, emerging evidence indicates that FOXK1 displays more dynamic regulation and stronger disease association, particularly in pathological contexts [1, 2]. Initially identified as a modulator of metabolic pathways and cell cycle progression, FOXK1 has since been recognized as a pivotal player in multiple diseases, particularly cancer, where it integrates complex regulatory networks to drive key oncogenic hallmarks [2–4]. The dysregulation of FOXK1 is driven by multiple mechanisms, including genetic alterations, epigenetic modifications, and ncRNA-mediated regulation, all of which collectively contribute to its aberrant activation in diseases [2]. Unlike other FOX family members that focus on specific biological processes, FOXK1 uniquely regulates three core cancer hallmarks: metabolic reprogramming, cancer stem cell (CSC) stemness, and therapeutic resistance, making it a potential hub for therapeutic intervention [1, 5–10]. Moreover, FOXK1 is involved in non-cancerous diseases such as metabolic disorders and inflammatory conditions further expands its therapeutic relevance [1, 11, 12].

However, the mechanisms by which distinct post-translational modifications (PTMs) coordinately regulate the context-dependent functions of FOXK1 remain incompletely understood, and the translational potential of therapeutically targeting FOXK1-centered regulatory networks has not yet been systematically evaluated. In this review, we summarize current advances in the structural features, regulatory mechanisms, and pathological functions of FOXK1, with particular emphasis on PTM-mediated signal integration. We further discuss emerging pharmacological strategies and the major challenges associated with targeting FOXK1-related pathways, thereby providing a mechanistic framework for the development of FOXK1-oriented therapeutic interventions.

Structural features of FOXK1: foundations of its biological functions

The biological functions of FOXK1 are determined by its modular structural organization, which integrates sequence-specific DNA binding, phospho-dependent protein interactions, and dynamic regulation by PTMs (Fig. 1). The human FOXK1 gene is located on chromosome 7p22.1 and encodes a 733-amino acid protein composed of several conserved functional domains [13]. These domains collectively enable signal-responsive transcriptional regulation.

The central determinant of FOXK1 transcriptional activity is the forkhead DNA-binding (FH) domain, a winged-helix motif that recognizes the consensus sequence 5’- [G/T]T [G/A]TTGTT-3’ within target gene promoters [4, 14, 15]. Through this domain, FOXK1 directly regulates genes involved in metabolic reprogramming and tumor progression [16–20]. Beyond DNA binding, FOXK1 functions as a transcriptional scaffold through its N-terminal forkhead-associated (FHA) domain, which mediates phospho-dependent protein-protein interactions and facilitates the recruitment of chromatin-associated corepressor complexes, including Sin3-associated factors such as Sds3 [21]. In addition to its structured domains, FOXK1 contains intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) that contribute to transcriptional regulation [16]. These regions facilitate the assembly of transcriptional regulatory complexes, including through liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS), thereby promoting efficient transcription of genes associated with glycolysis and proliferation. Moreover, FOXK1 activity is dynamically modulated by multiple PTMs, including phosphorylation by glycogen synthase kinase 3 (GSK3) and mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) [4, 22], ubiquitination mediated by ubiquitin-specific protease 28 (USP28) [23], and acetylation regulated by HDAC3 and propionate [12]. Collectively, these structural and regulatory features enable FOXK1 to function as a signal-responsive transcription factor. Through the integration of domain architecture and PTMs, FOXK1 translates upstream environmental and oncogenic cues into context-dependent gene expression programs.

Upstream regulatory networks of FOXK1: from genetic to post-translational control

FOXK1 expression is governed by multilayered regulatory networks that extend from genomic alterations to post-transcriptional modulation. These hierarchical mechanisms integrate developmental cues and oncogenic signaling, thereby determining the amplitude and context specificity of FOXK1 activity. At the genetic level, copy number alterations constitute an important determinant of FOXK1 dysregulation. Analyses of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) datasets have identified recurrent FOXK1 copy number gains in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). This genomic amplification correlates strongly with increased FOXK1 mRNA abundance, suggesting that elevated gene dosage directly enhances transcriptional output in malignant tissues [24].

Epigenetic regulation further modulates FOXK1 transcriptional output. DNA methylation is a fundamental epigenetic modification that regulates gene activity without altering DNA sequence and plays key roles in normal development, genome stability, and carcinogenesis [25–30]. Methylation of cytosine residues within promoter regions generally suppresses gene transcription by restricting chromatin accessibility. Consistently, promoter hypomethylation has been reported to correlate with increased FOXK1 expression in both hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [31] and malignant pleural mesothelioma (MPM) [32]. Genome-wide methylation analyses have further identified FOXK1 as one of the most significantly hypomethylated genes in MPM, suggesting that loss of promoter methylation may represent an important epigenetic mechanism contributing to FOXK1 transcriptional activation in malignancy.

In addition to genetic and epigenetic regulation, FOXK1 transcription is directly controlled by multiple transcription factors that link extracellular cues to nuclear gene expression. FOXK1 contains a Sox-binding element (SBE) within its promoter region, enabling transcriptional activation by SOX15 [33]. SOX15 forms a complex with four-and-a-half LIM domains protein 3 (FHL3), which directly binds to the SBE and activates FOXK1 transcription; Mutagenesis of this element markedly attenuates FOXK1 expression in muscle myoblasts. FOXK1 is also a direct transcriptional target of the oncogenic factor c-Jun, which binds to the FOXK1 promoter and activates its expression, and has been implicated in tumorigenesis in gastric cancer [34–36].

Post-transcriptional regulation by non-coding RNAs

Non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) represent a critical layer of post-transcriptional gene regulation. NcRNAs constitute a major regulatory layer that modulates mRNA stability, translational efficiency, and RNA-RNA interactions [37–40]. Accumulating evidence indicates that FOXK1 expression is tightly controlled by ncRNA-mediated mechanisms, particularly through competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks (Table 1).

Long non-coding RNAs (LncRNAs) are structurally diverse transcripts that influence gene expression through chromatin remodeling, transcriptional control, and post-transcriptional mechanisms. In cancer, many lncRNAs function as ceRNAs by sequestering miRNAs, thereby relieving repression of shared target mRNAs [41]. In this context, several lncRNAs have been identified as upstream regulators of FOXK1 in a cancer type-specific manner. In pancreatic cancer, MCM3AP-AS1 has been reported to bind miR-138-5p and is associated with increased FOXK1 expression, accompanied by enhanced cell proliferation and migration [42]. In hepatocellular carcinoma, SNHG1 similarly upregulates FOXK1 expression via sequestration of miR-376a [43]. Comparable regulatory interactions have been described in glioma and non-small cell lung cancer, where CASC11 and RNASEH1-AS1 competitively bind miR-498 and miR-516a-5p, respectively, leading to FOXK1 derepression and increased tumor aggressiveness [44, 45]. Notably, ceRNA-mediated regulation of FOXK1 frequently converges on epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) and metastatic progression. In colorectal cancer, LINC01503 and XIST enhance FOXK1-associated EMT programs through sequestration of miR-4492 and miR-497, respectively [46, 47]. In addition, LINC00460 has been reported to sustain FOXK1 expression via interaction with miR-612 and is correlated with invasive phenotypes [48].

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) repress gene expression through sequence-specific binding to complementary elements within the 3′ untranslated region (3′ UTR) of target mRNAs, leading to mRNA degradation or translational inhibition [49, 50]. In bladder carcinoma, loss of miR-612 has been associated with increased FOXK1 expression and enhanced invasiveness [48], whereas in gastric cancer, miR-646 has been reported to restrain FOXK1-associated epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) [51]. Similarly, miR-137 directly targets FOXK1 in glioma and is associated with reduced tumor cell proliferation, with its downregulation correlating with FOXK1-associated malignant progression [52]. In summary, FOXK1 expression is shaped by coordinated genetic amplification, promoter hypomethylation, transcription factor activation, and ncRNA-mediated post-transcriptional regulation. Disruption of these interconnected regulatory layers may lead to sustained FOXK1 activation and is associated with tumor development and progression.

Post-translational modifications

PTMs represent a conserved regulatory layer across the Forkhead box (FOX) transcription factor family. These chemical modifications govern protein stability, subcellular localization, DNA-binding capacity, and transcriptional specificity in response to metabolic, stress-related, and oncogenic stimuli. Emerging evidence indicates that FOXK1 is similarly regulated by multiple PTMs. However, in contrast to canonical FOXO factors, which are primarily inactivated through AKT-mediated nuclear exclusion, FOXK1 appears to integrate PTM signals in a manner that often promotes nuclear retention and transcriptional activation. This distinction may underlie its context-dependent roles in metabolic adaptation, genome maintenance, and tumor progression (Fig. 2, Supplementary Table 1).

Ubiquitination and acetylation

Ubiquitination and acetylation are key determinants of transcription factor turnover and activity. Ubiquitin-specific protease 28 (USP28) removes polyubiquitin chains from FOXK1, thereby preventing proteasomal degradation and enhancing protein stability [23]. Stabilized FOXK1 has been reported to cooperate with the YAP signaling pathway, contributing to increased proliferative capacity and radioresistance, particularly in lung cancer models.Acetylation provides an additional layer of regulation. HDAC3-mediated deacetylation suppresses FOXK1 lysosomal degradation and helps maintain protein stability, whereas inhibition of HDAC3 increases FOXK1 acetylation and promotes its turnover. Notably, the gut microbial metabolite propionate has been shown to inhibit HDAC3, linking microbial metabolism to FOXK1 protein stability and transcriptional regulation [12].

O-GlcNAcylation

O-GlcNAcylation is a nutrient-sensitive post-translational modification (PTM) catalyzed by O-GlcNAc transferase (OGT). It regulates multiple transcription factors, including c-Myc, NF-κB, and FOXO proteins [53–55]. This modification often interacts with phosphorylation to modulate protein stability, nuclear localization, and transcriptional activity, thereby linking glucose availability to gene expression programs.

FOXK1 exhibits a strong functional dependence on O-GlcNAcylation. OGT-mediated modification at the C-terminus has been reported to promote the recruitment of the BAP1 complex to chromatin. This interaction is associated with reduced repressive H2AK119ub marks and activation of E2F-dependent transcriptional programs linked to tumor cell proliferation [56]. O-GlcNAcylation also participates in environmental and metabolic stress responses. Exposure to perfluorooctane sulfonate (PFOS) has been shown to inhibit O-GlcNAcase (OGA) activity and induce hyper-O-GlcNAcylation of FOXK1 at Thr573. This modification enhances its interaction with PES1 and is associated with impaired ovarian steroidogenesis and embryonic development [57]. Under insulin-resistant conditions, elevated O-GlcNAcylation promotes FOXK1 nuclear retention and has been linked to disruption of circadian gene regulation, potentially facilitating tumorigenic progression [58].

Phosphorylation

Phosphorylation represents a central regulatory mechanism within the FOX transcription factor family. In FOXO proteins, AKT-dependent phosphorylation promotes 14-3-3 binding and nuclear export, resulting in transcriptional repression [14]. In contrast, FOXK1 phosphorylation is integrated into a distinct signaling framework centered on nutrient and growth factor sensing.

The mTORC1–GSK3 axis constitutes a key regulatory pathway controlling FOXK1 phosphorylation, subcellular localization, and transcriptional activity. Glycogen synthase kinase 3 (GSK3) acts as a primary kinase that phosphorylates FOXK1 at multiple serine residues, including S402, S406, S427, and S431, thereby promoting its interaction with 14-3-3 proteins and inducing cytoplasmic sequestration, which limits transcriptional activity. Under nutrient-rich or insulin-stimulated conditions, suppression of GSK3 activity mediated by mTORC1 signaling reduces FOXK1 phosphorylation and facilitates its nuclear retention [4, 59]. Upstream PI3K-AKT signaling further reinforces this process by sustaining mTORC1 activity, thereby supporting FOXK1 nuclear localization and transcriptional output [14]. Phosphorylation also modulates FOXK1 in response to genotoxic and therapeutic stress. CHK2 phosphorylates FOXK1 at S130 following DNA damage, which induces cytoplasmic redistribution and relieves repression of autophagy-related genes, and has been associated with chemotherapy resistance [60]. In addition, ATM-mediated phosphorylation alters FOXK1 function at DNA double-strand breaks. This modification impairs 53BP1 recruitment and shifts DNA repair toward homologous recombination, a process linked to PARP inhibitor resistance in BRCA1-deficient tumors [61]. Additional context-dependent phosphorylation events mediated by cyclin-dependent kinases and protein kinase C beta II (PKCβII) further connect FOXK1 to cell cycle regulation, immunometabolism, and ferroptosis sensitivity [62].

Collectively, PTMs constitute a conserved regulatory framework within the Forkhead family. However, FOXK1 exhibits PTM-dependent features that appear to favor nuclear localization and transcriptional activation. These properties are associated with metabolic reprogramming, genome maintenance, and therapeutic resistance. FOXK1-associated PTM networks may therefore represent selective and potentially druggable regulatory nodes in precision oncology.

Physiological functions of FOXK1: maintaining homeostasis

Under physiological conditions, FOXK1 functions as a central transcriptional regulator that integrates metabolic cues, growth factor signaling, and cell cycle control to maintain cellular and tissue homeostasis. As a member of the Forkhead box (FOX) family, FOXK1 regulates gene expression through sequence-specific DNA binding, thereby coordinating metabolic balance, controlled proliferation, and tissue development. These physiological functions are tightly regulated by multilayered regulatory networks, ensuring that FOXK1 activity remains adaptive and context dependent.

Metabolic homeostasis

FOXK1 plays a central role in maintaining metabolic homeostasis by coordinating transcriptional programs that balance glucose and lipid metabolism in response to nutrient availability. Rather than acting as a simple regulator of individual metabolic enzymes, FOXK1 functions as a nutrient-responsive transcription factor that integrates extracellular signals with adaptive metabolic gene expression. In glucose metabolism, FOXK1 contributes to the regulation of both glucose utilization and production. Under insulin-stimulated conditions, FOXK1 accumulates in the nucleus and suppresses the expression of key gluconeogenic enzymes, including phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase (Pck1) and glucose-6-phosphatase (G6pc), thereby limiting hepatic glucose output during the fed state and helping maintain systemic glucose balance [63]. In parallel, FOXK1 has been reported to support glycolytic capacity by promoting transcriptional programs associated with glucose utilization, enabling cells to adjust metabolic flux in accordance with nutrient conditions [19, 20]. Through this dual role, FOXK1 may contribute to the dynamic balance between glucose production and consumption.

In addition, FOXK1 participates in the regulation of lipid metabolism in a tissue-dependent manner. Through mTORC1-dependent signaling, FOXK1 suppresses hepatic fatty acid oxidation, thereby influencing lipid storage and energy balance [14, 64]. In adipose tissue, peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma 2 (PPARγ2) acts as a master transcriptional regulator of adipocyte differentiation and lipid accumulation and is essential for systemic energy balance [65]. FOXK1 has been reported to promote adipogenic programs by activating PPARγ2 transcription, thereby linking metabolic signaling to adipose tissue development and lipid storage.

Cell cycle regulation and tissue development

Beyond its metabolic functions, FOXK1 serves as an important regulator of cell cycle progression and tissue development. The cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor p21 functions as a central checkpoint controlling the G1/S cell cycle transition in response to developmental and stress cues [66, 67]. FOXK1 represses p21 transcription, thereby permitting controlled cell cycle progression required for tissue growth and renewal [21]. FOXK1 further supports proliferative capacity by modulating cyclins and cyclin-dependent kinases involved in the G1/S transition, helping maintain balanced proliferation under physiological conditions [56]. Through these mechanisms, FOXK1 maintains the equilibrium between cell cycle progression and restraint, which is essential for tissue integrity.

During skeletal muscle development and regeneration, FOXK1 plays a critical role in balancing progenitor proliferation and differentiation. Foxo4 and Mef2 are key transcription factors that promote myogenic differentiation and cell cycle exit [68, 69]. FOXK1 represses Foxo4 and Mef2, thereby maintaining muscle progenitor cells in a proliferative state and supporting efficient skeletal muscle regeneration [70]. FOXK1 also contributes to embryonic development through modulation of signaling pathways involved in organogenesis. Wnt/β-catenin signaling is a fundamental pathway governing embryonic patterning and cardiogenesis [71–75]. FOXK1 has been reported to regulate components of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, linking forkhead-dependent transcriptional control to cardiac development and morphogenesis [76].

Collectively, the physiological functions of FOXK1 are maintained by tightly coordinated regulatory networks that balance metabolism, proliferation, and differentiation. Disruption of this balance may shift FOXK1 activity from homeostatic regulation toward pathological processes.

Pathophysiological roles of FOXK1 in cancer

Disruption of FOXK1 expression, structural stability, or upstream regulatory signaling can perturb its tightly controlled physiological functions and contribute to pathological states. Altered transcriptional regulation, aberrant PTMs, or imbalances within regulatory networks may result in sustained FOXK1 activation. Among disease contexts, cancer is the most extensively characterized. In multiple malignancies, dysregulated FOXK1 activity has been associated with increased cell proliferation, metabolic reprogramming, enhanced cellular plasticity, and resistance to anticancer therapies (Fig. 3). Collectively, these findings suggest that FOXK1 may function as an active contributor to tumor progression rather than merely a passive downstream effector.

Cell proliferation and cell cycle progression

Unrestrained cell cycle progression is a defining hallmark of cancer and is driven by dysregulated programs governing DNA replication and cell division. has been implicated in tumor cell proliferation through modulation of transcriptional networks associated with G1/S transition and S-phase entry [56]. A central feature of this regulation involves activation of E2F-dependent gene expression networks, which control the transcription of genes required for DNA synthesis, replication licensing, and mitotic progression [77–80]. Mechanistically, FOXK1 has been reported to facilitate chromatin accessibility and cofactor recruitment at E2F target gene loci, thereby establishing a transcriptionally permissive environment has been reported to facilitate. suggest that FOXK1-mediated regulation of E2F signaling is dynamically modulated by PTMs, particularly O-GlcNAcylation, which integrates metabolic status with transcriptional output [56]. Through this mechanism, FOXK1 may link nutrient availability and metabolic signaling to cell cycle progression, potentially supporting tumor growth under conditions of metabolic stress.

Maintenance of cancer stem cell properties

CSCs contribute to tumor initiation, recurrence, and metastasis through their self-renewal capacity and phenotypic plasticity. FOXK1 has been implicated in transcriptional programs that maintain stem-like states across multiple tumor types. In hepatocellular carcinoma, elevated FOXK1 expression correlates with increased levels of stemness-associated markers such as EpCAM and ALDH1. Genetic depletion of FOXK1 reduces tumor sphere formation and impairs self-renewal capacity [5]. In glioma, FOXK1 participates in multilayered regulatory networks involving RNA-binding proteins and long non-coding RNAs, thereby contributing to cancer stem cell (CSC) maintenance and vasculogenic mimicry, a process in which tumor cells form vessel-like structures independently of endothelial cells [18].FOXK1 has been reported to interact with Wnt/β-catenin signaling, a pathway essential for intestinal stem cell renewal. This interaction is associated with reinforcement of self-renewal transcriptional programs and maintenance of tumor-initiating potential [81]. Collectively, these findings suggest that FOXK1 may promote stem-like transcriptional states in a context-dependent manner, favoring cellular plasticity over terminal differentiation and thereby contributing to tumor persistence and adaptability.

Cancer metabolic reprogramming

Metabolic reprogramming is a hallmark of cancer that enables malignant cells to meet the energetic and biosynthetic demands required for rapid proliferation and survival within hostile microenvironments. FOXK1 contributes to this process through transcriptional regulation of genes involved in glucose and lipid metabolism.

In hepatocellular carcinoma, FOXK1 has been reported to promote metabolic reprogramming by enhancing glycolytic activity and supporting anabolic metabolism. Rather than functioning solely through regulation of individual metabolic enzymes, FOXK1 facilitates glucose utilization and lactate production, thereby contributing to tumor cell growth and survival. Functional studies have shown that FOXK1 depletion impairs glycolytic capacity and reduces tumor cell viability, partially through disruption of AKT/mTOR-associated metabolic signaling [20]. In ovarian cancer, FOXK1 cooperates with the Aurora-A/SOX8 signaling axis to regulate glucose metabolism, further linking cell cycle control with metabolic adaptation [19]. In nonalcoholic fatty liver disease-associated hepatocellular carcinoma, FOXK1 mediates mTORC1-dependent suppression of fatty acid oxidation, which is associated with lipid accumulation and the establishment of a metabolic environment permissive for tumor development [64]. This suppression promotes lipid accumulation and supports tumor development.

Vasculogenic mimicry, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance

Advanced tumors frequently exhibit invasive growth, alternative vascularization strategies, and resistance to therapy. FOXK1 has been implicated in the regulation of these aggressive phenotypes in a tumor-specific manner. In glioma, FOXK1 induces expression of genes associated with vasculogenic mimicry, including MMP1, MMP9, and VE-cadherin. These factors contribute to extracellular matrix remodeling and the formation of vascular-like structures independent of endothelial cells [18]. In melanoma, FOXK1 cooperates with deleted in liver cancer 1 (DLC1) to activate MMP9 expression, thereby promoting invasive behavior and metastatic dissemination [17]. FOXK1 also promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in gastric and colorectal cancers. EMT is characterized by reduced epithelial markers and increased mesenchymal traits, which facilitate migration and invasion [34, 51, 82–85].

In addition, FOXK1 contributes to therapeutic resistance through multiple mechanisms. Stabilization of FOXK1 by USP28 has been linked to increased resistance to radiotherapy, whereas DNA damage response pathways further support tumor cell survival under chemotherapeutic stress [23, 61]. Notably, FOXK1-driven oncogenic programs do not function in isolation but are integrated within broader FOX transcription factor networks. Among these, FOXO1 serves as a critical functional counterpart in non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC), particularly in regulating metabolic reprogramming and therapeutic response [86]. Mechanistically, FOXK1 and FOXO1 exhibit opposing yet coordinated roles. At the metabolic level, FOXK1 promotes glycolytic flux by inducing key enzymes such as Hexokinase 2 (HK2) and activating AKT/mTOR signaling [87, 88], whereas FOXO1 suppresses glycolysis through antagonism of PI3K/AKT signaling and activation of stress-responsive transcriptional programs. This interplay may contribute to a dynamic regulatory balance governing tumor metabolic state. In the context of radiotherapy, FOXK1 and FOXO1 also converge on stress response pathways while driving divergent cellular outcomes. FOXK1 has been linked to enhanced radioresistance through activation of Hippo signaling, whereas FOXO1 facilitates stress-induced apoptosis in a context-dependent manner [23, 86]. Dysregulation of upstream signaling pathways, particularly hyperactivation of PI3K/AKT signaling, may simultaneously enhance FOXK1 activity while suppressing FOXO1, thereby shifting this balance toward a therapy-resistant phenotype. Together, the FOXK1-FOXO1 axis may represent a context-dependent regulatory module linking metabolic control with stress responses and influencing therapeutic sensitivity in NSCLC [87].

Collectively, aberrant FOXK1 activity is associated with multiple interconnected processes in cancer, including uncontrolled proliferation, metabolic adaptation, maintenance of stem-like properties, invasive behavior, and therapeutic resistance. These pathological functions may represent an amplified and dysregulated extension of FOXK1’s physiological roles. Context-dependent regulatory networks likely determine the magnitude and direction of FOXK1-driven disease phenotypes.

Pathophysiological roles of FOXK1 in non-cancerous diseases

FOXK1 has been implicated in a range of non-cancerous disorders through its involvement in transcriptional programs that regulate metabolism, inflammation, and cellular stress responses (Fig. 4). Many FOXK1-associated pathways, including mTORC1 signaling, interferon responses, and JAK/STAT activation, are well-established contributors to disease pathogenesis. However, current evidence linking FOXK1 to these conditions remains largely indirect and context dependent, suggesting that FOXK1 may function as a modulatory component within broader regulatory networks rather than as an independent disease driver.

Metabolic diseases

Metabolic disorders arise from disrupted nutrient sensing and dysregulated transcriptional control of metabolic pathways. FOXK1 functions as a signal-responsive transcription factor downstream of the AKT-mTORC1 axis, linking nutrient status to metabolic gene expression in a context-dependent manner. In hepatic metabolism, nutrient-induced mTORC1 activation promotes FOXK1 nuclear activity, leading to repression of lipid metabolism-related genes, including Ppara, Acadm, and Decr1, together with activation of lipogenic transcriptional programs(Fig. 4A). These changes are consistent with a metabolic shift that may contribute to hepatic steatosis and disease progression from nonalcoholic fatty liver disease to nonalcoholic steatohepatitis and hepatocellular carcinoma. Notably, hepatocyte-specific deletion of FOXK1 alleviates these pathological features [64], supporting a functional role in this context.

Beyond hepatic metabolism, FOXK1 has also been implicated in systemic metabolic regulation. In chronic kidney disease (CKD), FOXK1 is upregulated in renal proximal tubular epithelial cells (TECs) in a TGF-β1-dependent manner. Mechanistically, FOXK1 appears to promote a shift from fatty acid oxidation to aerobic glycolysis, at least in part through the formation of transcriptional condensates that facilitate RNA polymerase II recruitment and activation of glycolytic gene expression (Fig. 4B). This glycolytic reprogramming is associated with lactate accumulation, tubular injury, and extracellular matrix deposition, potentially contributing to fibrotic remodeling. Consistently, tubule-specific inhibition of FOXK1 attenuates glycolytic activation and fibrosis-related changes, further supporting its involvement in CKD-associated metabolic dysfunction [16]. In contrast, FOXK1 may exert adaptive functions under metabolic stress in cerebrovascular contexts. Activation of PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling has been reported to induce FOXK1-dependent transcription of Dio2, enhancing local triiodothyronine (T3) production and activating downstream antioxidant pathways. These effects are proposed to support mitochondrial function and vascular metabolic homeostasis [88].

Collectively, FOXK1 regulates metabolic homeostasis in a tissue- and context-dependent mannerthrough coordination of lipid metabolism, glycolytic reprogramming, and hormone-associated metabolic pathways. Rather than functioning as an independent disease driver, FOXK1 appears to act as a regulatory node within broader metabolic signaling networks, linking upstream nutrient and stress signals to disease-associated metabolic remodeling. Although these findings highlight FOXK1 as a potential therapeutic target in metabolic disorders, further studies are required to establish its causal role and evaluate its translational potential.

Inflammatory and immune diseases

FOXK1 appears to function as a versatile transcriptional regulator that coordinates inflammatory and immune responses across diverse pathological contexts in a highly context-dependent manner, exerting both pro- and anti-inflammatory effects through distinct molecular mechanisms.

In antiviral innate immunity, FOXK1 can be co-opted by viral factors to suppress host defense. For example, adenovirus E1A forms a complex with FOXK1 and associated cofactors, leading to reduced stabilization of Interferon Regulatory Factor 3 (IRF3) and impaired activation of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs), thereby attenuating antiviral responses [89]. In innate immune cells, FOXK1 has also been implicated in the metabolic regulation of inflammation. In Toll-like receptor (TLR)-activated macrophages, Cyclin J-CDK-mediated phosphorylation of FOXK1 promotes its cytoplasmic retention, suppresses glycolytic reprogramming and HIF-1α activity, and is associated with reduced proinflammatory cytokine production (Fig. 4C) [90].

The interferon signaling pathway represents a central axis linking antiviral defense and inflammatory pathology. As discussed above, FOXK1 cooperates with HDAC3 to maintain its protein stability and transcriptional activity. In antiviral contexts, this regulatory axis supports basal expression of interferon-related genes such as STAT1 and STAT2, thereby facilitating efficient ISG activation and host defense (Fig. 4C) [91]. In contrast, in rheumatoid arthritis, sustained activation of the HDAC3-FOXK1 axis in fibroblast-like synoviocytes reinforces interferon-driven transcriptional programs, which may contribute to aberrant cell activation, proliferation, and proinflammatory responses. Notably, gut microbiota-derived metabolites such as propionate have been reported to attenuate this pathway by disrupting the HDAC3–FOXK1 interaction, potentially limiting excessive interferon signaling and inflammatory responses [12].

In other chronic inflammatory conditions, FOXK1 exhibits tissue-specific immunomodulatory functions. For instance, in atopic dermatitis, FOXK1 accumulates in the nucleus of epidermal keratinocytes in chronic lesions and acts as a negative regulator by restraining IL-13–induced expression of proinflammatory mediators such as CCL5 and IL32 [92]. Collectively, FOXK1 may function as a context-dependent immunoregulatory node that integrates antiviral signaling, inflammatory metabolism, and epigenetic regulation to modulate immune responses, and may contribute to both the initiation and resolution of inflammation across diverse disease settings.

Reproductive and neurological diseases

FOXK1 has been increasingly implicated in reproductive and neurological disorders as a context-dependent transcriptional regulator that integrates post-transcriptional, post-translational, and signaling inputs to modulate cellular homeostasis.

In the reproductive system, ovarian function depends on tightly controlled transcriptional programs governing granulosa cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival [89–91]. Within this framework, FOXK1 has been reported to participate in the regulation of genes associated with cell cycle progression and cellular homeostasis. In polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) models, FOXK1 expression is elevated in granulosa cells, where its dysregulation has been associated with impaired cell proliferation, enhanced apoptosis and autophagy, and disruption of ovarian function, partly through AMPK/mTOR-related signaling pathways [92]. In addition, post-translational modification of FOXK1, particularly O-GlcNAcylation at Thr573, has been linked to altered steroidogenic capacity, reduced progesterone levels, and defects in follicular development and oocyte maturation. Environmental factors such as perfluorooctane sulfonate (PFOS) may exacerbate these effects by enhancing FOXK1 O-GlcNAcylation, may further contribute to reproductive dysfunction [57].

In the nervous system, FOXK1 appears to be involved in stress-adaptive signaling that supports neuronal survival. AKT signaling plays a central role in protecting neurons from oxidative damage, and FOXK1 has been implicated in this pathway in neurodegenerative contexts [93, 94]. In Parkinson’s disease (PD) models, a circRNA-mediated regulatory axis involving circSV2b and miR-5107-5p has been shown to modulate FOXK1 expression, thereby influencing AKT1 transcription and downstream oxidative stress responses (Fig. 4D). Disruption of this axis is associated with increased reactive oxygen species accumulation and dopaminergic neuron loss, whereas restoration of FOXK1 expression correlates with improved neuronal resilience [95]. Collectively, current evidence suggests that FOXK1 may function as a context-dependent regulatory node linking metabolic signaling, epigenetic modification, and environmental inputs to cellular stress responses in reproductive and neurological systems.

Taken together, FOXK1 should be considered as a potential regulatory factor that may contribute to the pathogenesis of these non-cancer diseases, rather than a definitively established driver. Future studies employing genetic models, loss- and gain-of-function approaches, and clinical validation will be essential to clarify its precise mechanistic roles.

Pharmacological targeting of FOXK1: strategies and progress

FOXK1 has emerged as a transcriptional regulator involved in tumor metabolism, proliferation, and inflammatory signaling. Despite its clear pathological relevance, FOXK1 remains a challenging pharmacological target due to the lack of FOXK1-specific small-molecule inhibitors. Consequently, current therapeutic strategies primarily foucs on indirect pharmacological strategies, including modulation of PTMs, regulation of FOXK1-associated epigenetic complexes, and inhibition of upstream signaling pathways that converge on FOXK1-driven transcriptional programs (Fig. 5). Accumulating preclinical and translational evidence supports the potential feasibility of these approaches across multiple disease contexts.

Targeting FOXK1 via post-translational modification modifiers

PTMs play a decisive role in controlling FOXK1 protein stability, nuclear localization, and transcriptional activity. Pharmacological targeting of enzymes responsible for these modifications represents one of the most well-supported strategies for modulating FOXK1 function.

FOXK1 forms transcriptional repression complexes with HDAC3, thereby regulating gene programs involved in inflammation, metabolism, and cell proliferation [12]. HDAC inhibitors such as trichostatin A, a pan-HDAC inhibitor widely used as a pharmacological tool, as well as the microbiota-derived short-chain fatty acid propionate, disrupt the FOXK1–HDAC3 interaction [12, 58]. This disruption promotes FOXK1 acetylation and proteasomal degradation, leading to attenuation of FOXK1-dependent transcription. In inflammatory disease models, particularly rheumatoid arthritis, propionate exhibits anti-inflammatory effects and has shown synergistic efficacy when combined with anti-TNF therapy, highlighting a context-dependent epigenetic intervention strategy.

Ubiquitin-mediated turnover represents an additional regulatory layer governing FOXK1 stability. As discussed above, the deubiquitinase USP28 contributes to FOXK1 stabilization, suggesting that this pathway may influence FOXK1-dependent functions. Notably, pharmacological inhibition of USP28, such as with AZ1, has been shown to promote proteasomal degradation of oncogenic substrates such as c-MYC and enhance sensitivity to platinum-based chemotherapy in non-small-cell lung cancer [96]. In this context, USP28 inhibition may indirectly facilitate FOXK1 destabilization and attenuate FOXK1-associated resistance phenotypes. However, direct mechanistic evidence supporting this link remains limited and requires further validation.

Emerging evidence further indicates that FOXK1 activity is sensitive to metabolic status through O-GlcNAcylation. Experimental modulation of O-GlcNAc cycling enzymes using OGT inhibitors, such as OSMI-4, alters FOXK1 transcriptional activity in cellular models, highlighting nutrient-sensitive PTMs as an additional regulatory axis [56–58]. Collectively, these findings identify PTM-modifying enzymes as pharmacologically accessible regulators of FOXK1-associated pathological processes.

Targeting FOXK1-associated signaling pathways

FOXK1 operates within a complex signaling network that integrates metabolic and oncogenic cues. Targeting pathways upstream or downstream of FOXK1 provides a clinically tractable means to indirectly suppress FOXK1-mediated effects.

FOXK1 activity is closely linked to the AKT/mTOR pathway, which governs metabolic reprogramming and cell growth. Clinically approved mTOR inhibitors, such as rapamycin and its analog, suppress FOXK1-driven metabolic programs and may synergize with FOXK1-directed strategies [64, 97]. Notably, metformin, a first-line insulin-sensitizing agent [98], has emerged as a key metabolic modulator of FOXK1 activity. Beyond its canonical effects on mitochondrial complex I and AMPK activation, metformin reduces FOXK1 expression and impairs its nuclear localization, particularly under insulin-resistant conditions [58]. In breast cancer, FOXK1 represses circadian clock genes through recruitment of HDAC-containing corepressor complexes, a process enhanced by OGT-mediated metabolic signaling. Metformin counteracts this axis and, when combined with HDAC inhibitors such as vorinostat A, restores circadian gene expression and synergistically suppresses tumor progression in FOXK1-high tumors [58]. These observations highlight the potential value of combining metabolic and epigenetic therapeutic approaches.

Pharmacological inhibition of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, for instance with PRI-724 or LGK974, has been shown to suppress tumor cell proliferation and enhance therapeutic sensitivity [99–102]. Mechanistically, FOXK1 functions as a positive regulator of Wnt/β-catenin signaling by facilitating nuclear translocation of key components such as Dishevelled and sustaining β-catenin-dependent transcriptional programs [81]. Conversely, FOXK1 depletion leads to downregulation of β-catenin and its downstream targets, including c-MYC and cyclin D1, thereby impairing tumor growth [103, 104]. Furthermore, inhibition of Wnt/β-catenin signaling has been associated with diminished FOXK1-driven transcriptional outputs in cancer contexts [105]. Collectively, these findings suggest that pharmacological blockade of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway may disrupt this functional interplay, thereby potentially attenuating FOXK1-associated oncogenic programs.

Emerging perspectives: transcriptional condensates and FOXK1

Beyond established therapeutic strategies, recent insights into LLPS of transcription factors provide a conceptual framework for future FOXK1-targeted interventions. LLPS is a fundamental biochemical process through which intracellular biomolecules concentrate into dynamic condensates [106]. Increasing evidence indicates that LLPS is essential for transcription factors to exert transcriptional activity and regulate diverse cellular functions [107]. Consequently, targeting biomolecular condensates and protein aggregation to disrupt LLPS has emerged as a potential therapeutic strategy for cancer and neurodegenerative diseases [108]. Studies on FOXM1 have demonstrate that disruption of transcriptional condensates through kinase modulation or epigenetic interference can effectively suppress oncogenic transcriptional programs [109]. FOXK1 shares several structural and functional features with FOXM1, including intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) and chromatin-associated transcriptional activity. In addition, recent evidence suggests that the FOXK1 IDR (amino acids 210–303) contributes to liquid droplet formation and facilitates transcriptional activation [16]. Whether similar condensate-targeting strategies can be applied to FOXK1 remains to be determined and will require further biophysical and mechanistic investigation.

In summary, FOXK1 can currently be targeted indirectly through modulation of PTMs, epigenetic regulators, metabolic interventions, and pathway-level inhibitors (Table 2). Direct FOXK1-specific inhibitors are not yet available. Nevertheless, rational combination strategies, particularly those integrating metabolic agents such as metformin with epigenetic modulators, show promising therapeutic potential. Future progress will depend on defining FOXK1-specific regulatory complexes, tissue-specific dependencies, and predictive biomarkers. Such advances will be essential for translating FOXK1-centered therapeutic strategies into precise and effective clinical applications.

Conclusion and future perspectives

FOXK1 is a signal-responsive transcription factor that integrates metabolic sensing, chromatin remodeling, and oncogenic transcription. Although tightly regulated under physiological conditions, can lead to sustained nuclear activation, contributing to metabolic reprogramming, stem-like plasticity, invasion, and therapeutic resistance in cancer.Distinct from canonical FOXO proteins, FOXK1 is predominantly regulated by nutrient-sensitive post-translational modifications, which influence its stability, subcellular localization, and transcriptional activity, underscoring its unique functional positioning within the FOX family.

FOXK1 operates within interconnected signaling networks, including AKT/mTOR, Hippo-YAP/TAZ, Wnt/β-catenin, and JAK/STAT pathways, thereby linking metabolic cues to proliferative and inflammatory transcriptional outputs. Functional overlap and divergence with FOXK2 further highlight the need for systematic dissection of FOXK subfamily-specific regulatory mechanisms to improve therapeutic selectivity.

Despite its emerging importance, direct pharmacological targeting of FOXK1 remains challenging. Current strategies rely largely on indirect modulation, including inhibition of upstream signaling pathways (e.g., mTOR inhibitors and metformin) or enzymes controlling PTMs. However, pathway redundancy and compensatory signaling may limit therapeutic efficacy, emphasizing the need for more selective and mechanism-based approaches.

Future research should prioritize several key directions. First, systematic mapping of FOXK1-centered post-translational modification networks using quantitative proteomics and site-specific mutagenesis may help identify critical regulatory nodes that control FOXK1 activity. Second, integrated multi-omics approaches-including ChIP-seq, RNA-seq, and single-cell transcriptomics-should be applied to define context-specific FOXK1 transcriptional circuits and cellular heterogeneity. Third, structural and biophysical characterization of FOXK1, particularly its IDRs and protein-protein interaction interfaces, may facilitate the rational development of novel therapeutic strategies, including proteolysis-targeting chimeras (PROTACs) or IDR-targeting compounds. A testable hypothesis is that disruption of FOXK1-dependent phase separation or cofactor interactions may impair its transcriptional activity and tumor-promoting functions. Fourth, the development of physiologically relevant models, including conditional knockout systems, patient-derived organoids, and in vivo xenograft models, will be essential for evaluating the functional consequences of FOXK1 targeting. Finally, identification of predictive biomarkers-such as FOXK1 expression levels, PTM signatures, or downstream transcriptional programs-may facilitate patient stratification and improve clinical translation.

Collectively, these efforts may help to establish FOXK1 as a context-dependent and potentially targetable regulatory node in cancer, although substantial mechanistic and translational challenges remain.
