Prognostic role of hemoglobin, albumin, lymphocyte and platelet score in esophageal cancer: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Huanhuan Yang, Yonghong Luo, Yang Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe association of hemoglobin, albumin, lymphocyte and platelet (HALP) score with clinical outcomes in esophageal cancer patients remains unclear. A comprehensive and systematic review and meta-analysis are needed to investigate on this topic.MethodsPubMed, Web of Science and Chinese National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI) databases were searched up to December 7, 2025. Primary prognostic indicator included the overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), disease-free survival (DFS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS). Secondary prognostic indicators consisted of the therapeutic effect including the major pathological response (MPR), objective response rate (ORR) and complete response rate (CRR) and risk of postoperative complications.ResultsEleven studies were included. Pooled results demonstrated that lower HALP score was related to poorer OS [hazard ratio (HR) =1.76, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.34–2.31, P<0.001], DFS (HR =1.61, 95% CI: 1.03–2.53, P=0.04) and CSS (HR =2.023, 95% CI: 1.549–2.644, P<0.001), as well as the PFS (HR =1.54, 95% CI: 0.98–2.41, P=0.06). Besides, lower HALP score was associated with significantly better MPR [odds ratio (OR) =0.335, 95% CI: 0.125–0.807, P=0.02] and ORR (OR =0.443, 95% CI: 0.234–0.841, P=0.006), indicating inferior treatment response. No significant association between HALP score and risk of postoperative complications was observed.ConclusionsHALP score was associated with the survival and therapeutic effect of anti-tumor treatment and lower HALP might indicate worse prognosis in esophageal cancer patients. More high-quality studies are needed to further identify above findings.

Highlight box

Key findings

• This meta-analysis of 11 studies (n=2,574) evaluated the prognostic value of the pretreatment hemoglobin, albumin, lymphocyte and platelet (HALP) score in esophageal cancer. A low HALP score was associated with significantly worse overall survival, disease-free survival, and cancer-specific survival, and showed a non-significant trend toward poorer progression-free survival.

• Low HALP was also associated with a lower likelihood of achieving favorable treatment responses (major pathological response and objective response rate). Evidence for several secondary outcomes was limited (often 1–3 studies).

What is known and what is new?

• Single blood-based markers of nutrition, inflammation, and immunity are linked to outcomes in esophageal cancer, but their clinical utility is limited by variability. Composite indices such as HALP have shown prognostic relevance in multiple solid tumors.

• This study provides an updated, esophageal cancer-specific synthesis showing that pretreatment HALP is consistently associated with survival and treatment response, supporting HALP as an integrated immuno-nutritional prognostic indicator. Most available evidence comes from East Asian cohorts and predominantly esophageal squamous cell carcinoma.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• HALP is inexpensive and routinely available, and may help pretreatment risk stratification and identification of patients who may benefit from intensified monitoring and supportive optimization (e.g., nutrition/anemia management).

• Future research should prioritize multicenter prospective validation, standardized HALP cutoffs/measurement timing, and dedicated analyses in Western populations and esophageal adenocarcinoma to improve generalizability.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer remains a major global health burden. Worldwide estimates show that esophageal cancer accounted for over half a million deaths and several hundred thousand new cases in recent global cancer assessments, and it continues to rank among the most lethal malignancies with large regional differences in incidence and mortality (1,2). Standard treatment approaches depend on stage and histology and include surgical resection for resectable disease, multimodality therapy (neoadjuvant or definitive chemoradiotherapy) for locally advanced tumors, and systemic therapies—recently including chemo-immunotherapy options—for advanced or metastatic disease as recommended in contemporary clinical guidelines (3,4).

In recent years, numerous peripheral blood biomarkers reflecting nutritional status, systemic inflammation and immune competence—such as serum albumin, hemoglobin (or anemia status), lymphocyte counts and platelet-related indices—have been reported to correlate with survival and treatment response in patients with esophageal cancer (5). However, single markers are often limited by variability and insufficient robustness for routine clinical decision-making. To overcome these limitations, composite indices that combine multiple blood parameters have been proposed. One recently developed composite score is the HALP (hemoglobin, albumin, lymphocyte and platelet) score, which integrates markers of nutrition (albumin, hemoglobin), host immune status (lymphocytes) and systemic/inflammatory response (platelets) (6). A growing body of evidence and pooled analyses suggest that HALP has prognostic value across a variety of solid tumors, but its prognostic significance specifically in esophageal cancer remains incompletely defined and is based on a limited number of single-center studies (6,7).

Therefore, we performed the present meta-analysis to comprehensively evaluate the association between pretreatment HALP score and survival and treatment-related outcomes in patients with esophageal cancer based on available evidence. We present this article in accordance with the PRISMA reporting checklist (8) (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1186/rc).

Methods

Literature search

PubMed, Web of Science and Chinese National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI) databases were searched up to December 7, 2025 with these terms: hemoglobin, albumin, lymphocyte and platelet, HALP, esophagus, esophageal, tumor, cancer, carcinoma and neoplasm. Specific search strategy for PubMed database was presented in Figure S1.

Study selection

Studies meeting these criteria were included: (I) esophageal cancer patients; (II) the HALP score was evaluated before anti-tumor treatment according to previously published formula (9,10); (III) the association between HALP score and prognostic indicators was explored with enough relevant data provided; (IV) available full texts; (V) studies were published in Chinese or English.

Studies meeting these criteria were excluded: (I) insufficient or duplicated data; and (II) letters, editorials, reviews, case reports, animal studies or conference abstracts.

Data extraction

Following information was collected: first author, year, country, sample size, pathological type [esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) vs. esophageal cancer], treatment (surgery, chemoradiotherapy, radiotherapy, chemotherapy and others), cutoff value of HALP score, endpoint, hazard ratio (HR) and odds ratio (OR) with corresponding 95% confidence interval (CI).

In our meta-analysis, primary prognostic indicator was the survival including the overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), disease-free survival (DFS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS). Secondary prognostic indicators consisted of the therapeutic effect including the major pathological response (MPR), objective response rate (ORR) and complete response rate (CRR) and risk of postoperative complications such as the pulmonary infection and anastomotic leakage.

Quality evaluation

Methodological quality was assessed by Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS), and studies with NOS scores ≥6 were defined as high-quality studies (11).

Statistical analysis

All analyses were conducted through STATA 17.0 software. Heterogeneity between studies was calculated by Q test and I2 statistic. When significant heterogeneity was detected, represented as I2 >50% and/or P<0.10, the random effects model was used; otherwise, the fixed effects model was applied (12). HRs and ORs with 95% CIs were combined to assess the relationship of HALP score with primary and secondary outcomes among esophageal cancer patients. Subgroup analysis by treatment (surgery vs. non-surgery) for the OS was further performed. Sensitivity analysis was conducted to assess the stability of the pooled results. Begg’s funnel plot and Egger’s test were used to detect publication bias, and significant publication bias was defined as P<0.05 (13,14).

Results

Literature selection

As shown in Figure 1, 54 records were searched. After reviewing titles, abstracts and full texts, 43 publications were excluded. Eleven studies were included eventually (7,15-24).

Association of HALP score with primary prognostic indicators in esophageal cancer

In our meta-analysis, the OS, PFS, DFS and CSS were involved for the prognostic role of HALP score in esophageal cancer. Pooled results demonstrated that lower HALP score predicted worse OS (HR =1.76, 95% CI: 1.34–2.31, P<0.001; I2 =55.0%, P=0.03) (Figure 2), PFS (HR =1.54, 95% CI: 0.98–2.41, P=0.06; I2 =64.4%, P=0.06) (Figure 3A), DFS (HR =1.61, 95% CI: 1.03–2.53, P=0.04; I2 =63.2%, P=0.07) (Figure 3B) and CSS (HR =2.023, 95% CI: 1.549–2.644, P<0.001) (Table 2). Meanwhile, the subgroup analysis based on the treatment for the OS indicated similar results (non-surgery: HR =1.84, 95% CI: 1.01–3.35, P=0.048; surgery: HR =1.69, 95% CI: 1.36–2.09, P<0.001) (Figure S2). Notably, the evidence base for some survival endpoints was limited: PFS and DFS were each reported in only three studies, and CSS was reported in a single study. Therefore, these pooled estimates should be regarded as preliminary and interpreted with caution.

Association of HALP score with secondary prognostic indicators in esophageal cancer

The therapeutic effect of anti-tumor treatment and risk of postoperative complications were also explored for the prognostic role of HALP score. Pooled results manifested that lower HALP score predicted poorer MPR (OR =0.335, 95% CI: 0.125–0.807, P=0.02) and ORR (OR =0.443, 95% CI: 0.234–0.841, P=0.006), suggesting that patients with lower HALP levels were less likely to respond favorably to treatment. Meanwhile, an obvious statistical trend about the association between HALP score and CRR was also observed (OR =0.151, 95% CI: 0.020–1.141, P=0.07) (Table 3). However, MPR, ORR and CRR were each available from only one included study. Therefore, the findings regarding treatment response should be considered exploratory and require further validation.

Discussion

In this comprehensive meta-analysis involving 2,574 patients from 11 studies, we found that a lower HALP score was significantly associated with poorer long-term survival outcomes, including OS, DFS and CSS, in patients with esophageal cancer. In addition, lower HALP levels were correlated with inferior treatment response, as reflected by reduced MPR and ORR. These findings suggest that the HALP score, an easily obtainable and low-cost biomarker derived from routine blood tests, may serve as a useful tool for pretreatment risk stratification and clinical decision-making in patients with esophageal cancer. Early identification of individuals with low HALP may help clinicians recognize high-risk patients who require closer monitoring or more intensive multimodal treatment strategies.

The prognostic significance of HALP is biologically plausible, as it integrates four parameters reflecting nutritional reserve, systemic inflammation and immune competence. Hemoglobin and albumin are well-established indicators of oxygen-carrying capacity, nutritional status and systemic inflammation, all of which influence tumor progression and treatment tolerance. Hypoalbuminemia, for example, has been associated with systemic inflammatory response and poor survival in esophageal cancer and other malignancies (25). Lymphocyte count reflects host immune function, and lymphopenia has been linked to impaired anti-tumor immunity and inferior prognosis (26-28). Platelets can promote tumor growth, angiogenesis and metastasis through multiple mechanisms, and elevated platelet counts have been associated with worse outcomes in gastrointestinal cancers (29). Therefore, the HALP score captures a multidimensional profile of the tumor-host interaction, which may explain its predictive value not only for survival but also for response to chemoradiotherapy or perioperative therapy, consistent with findings reported in other solid tumors such as gastric, colorectal and lung cancers (6,30-32).

Interestingly, our analysis indicated no significant association between HALP score and postoperative complications in esophageal cancer. However, this result should be interpreted with caution because only one to two included studies reported such outcomes, resulting in insufficient statistical power. Evidence from other malignancies suggests that HALP may have predictive value for postoperative morbidity. For instance, low HALP levels were associated with increased postoperative complications in colorectal cancer (33) and were reported as a risk factor for surgical site infection and overall complication burden in gastric cancer surgery (34). These findings imply that HALP may reflect perioperative physiological resilience and inflammation-related surgical vulnerability. Given that the current evidence in esophageal cancer is extremely limited, well-designed prospective studies with larger sample sizes are still required to determine whether HALP can reliably predict postoperative risks in this patient population.

Clinically, the HALP score could be translated into several practical applications for the management of esophageal cancer. First, as a pretreatment, easily obtained composite biomarker, HALP can be used for risk stratification to identify patients at higher oncologic risk who may benefit from intensified surveillance or more aggressive multimodal therapy (e.g., consideration of neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy or closer post-treatment follow-up) (31). Second, HALP may help tailor peri-treatment supportive measures: patients with low HALP—reflecting poor nutritional reserves, anemia and impaired immune status—could be referred early for nutritional optimization, anemia management (iron/erythropoiesis support where appropriate) and immunonutrition or prehabilitation programs to improve surgical tolerance and chemo-/radio-therapy tolerance (16). Third, HALP could be incorporated into multivariable prognostic models or nomograms alongside clinical and pathological factors to improve individualized prognostication and guide decisions such as candidacy for adjuvant therapy or enrollment into clinical trials; recent studies have demonstrated that adding HALP to prediction models increases discriminatory performance in digestive cancers (35). Fourth, because several reports link pretreatment HALP with treatment response (for example, response to chemoradiotherapy in ESCC), dynamic monitoring of HALP during therapy may provide an inexpensive surrogate for treatment efficacy and help identify patients who fail to mount a favorable immuno-nutritional response and thus may need early modification of therapy .Finally, implementation requires standardization—uniform HALP cutoffs, timing of measurement (pre-treatment window), and prospective validation in multicenter cohorts—before routine clinical adoption; ongoing systematic reviews and meta-analyses support promising prognostic value but call for prospective trials to confirm clinical utility (31).

There are several limitations in this meta-analysis. First, the overall number of included studies and the sample sizes of some individual studies were relatively small, which may reduce the precision of the pooled estimates. Second, all included studies were conducted in East Asia (China or Japan); given geographic differences in esophageal cancer epidemiology (e.g., histologic distribution such as ESCC vs. adenocarcinoma), risk-factor profiles, baseline nutritional/inflammatory status, and treatment patterns, the generalizability of our findings to Western populations and other regions may be limited. Third, although factors such as age, tumor stage/histology, treatment strategy, and the cutoff value (and timing/units) used to define low vs. high HALP may influence the prognostic effect, we were unable to perform detailed subgroup analyses or meta-regression because of insufficient reporting and lack of individual patient data. Fourth, the evidence for several endpoints was sparse (often only 1–3 studies per outcome), which limits statistical power, reduces the robustness and generalizability of these findings, and precludes reliable assessment of heterogeneity and publication bias for those outcomes.

Conclusions

Based on current evidence, HALP score was associated with the survival and therapeutic effect of anti-tumor treatment and lower HALP might indicate worse prognosis in esophageal cancer patients. However, more high-quality studies were warranted to further identify above findings.
