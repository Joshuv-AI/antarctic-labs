Variable density and chemical reaction effects on heat and mass transfer of magnetic second-grade nanofluid flow under slip conditions
Authors: Mhamed Benaissa, Zia Ullah, Feyisa Edosa Merga, Asmaa Farouk, Mohamed S Hamdy, Noureddine Elboughdiri, Djamel Ghernaout, Alsamani A M Salih, Nidhal Ben Khedher
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSlip flow, exponential thermal-density and second-grade nanofluid over horizontal stretching circular cylinder has many applications in heat exchangers, pipelines, rocket bodies, aircraft fuselages, submarine hulls, offshore structures, and flow bodies of industrial sectors. This research presents the thermal dependent density and chemical reaction effect on MHD second-grade nanofluid motion of heat and mass transmission over horizontal stretching circular cylinder with thermal-concentration slip. The importance of applied magnetic field is used for maximum increment of heat transport. The coupled partial differential equations of second-grade nanofluid are transformed into ordinary differential equations with help of well-defined similarity variable and stream functions. The average and central difference formula of finite-difference method with Newton–Raphson scheme are employed to find numerical outcomes of nonlinear ordinary differential equations through MATLAB software. Several physical parameters such as magnetic field (), second grade parameter (K1), chemical reaction parameter (Cr), density number (n), thermal slip (BT), and concentration slip (BC) are used for temperature, concentration, and velocity variations. The skin friction rate, local Nusselt number, and Sherwood number are numerical and graphically presented using different physical parameters. It is noted that the velocity profile decreases with increasing magnetic parameter because stronger magnetic field opposes the fluid motion and acts like a braking mechanism. The stronger slip performance in fluid temperature and fluid concentration is observed at each value of thermal slip and concentration slip parameter. The skin friction rate is higher with increasing Prandtl number but Nusselt number decreases with increasing Brownian motion. The magnitude of isothermal contours enhances as chemical reaction and magnetic field enhance under slip conditions.Keywords: MHD second-grade nanofluid, Thermal-concentration slip, Exponential variable density, Chemical reaction, Heat and mass transport, Chemically-reactive cylinder

Introduction

The second-grade nanofluid is the combined form of complex strain–stress properties of second-grade liquids with enhancing characteristics of thermal conductivity which refers the prominent application of heat and mass transfer in engineering and nanotechnology. With normal stress difference and non-Newtonian liquids, the second-grade nanofluid is more suitable for lubricants, polymers, biological liquids, and crude oil in the presence of shear thickening and shear thinning properties. Choi [1] introduced nanofluid with suspended nanoparticles that plays a significant role in the field of engineering and technology. Applications of nanofluids include cooling nuclear reactors and automobiles, accelerating heat transfer in computer micro-chips, the preparation of food, fuel cells, and modes of transport. Nanofluids improve the thermal conductivity and heat transfer rate of conventional base liquids (water, glycol, ethylene, and oil) when a small number of nanoparticles (usually composed of Ag, Au, Al, or their oxides) with sizes ranging from 1 to 100 nm are added. A substantial number of investigations were performed relative to nanofluids. Shehzad et al. [2] examined the three-dimensional Jeffrey nanofluid motion with radiating energy within heat production. Kakac and Yener [3] investigated the convective heat transfer through conduction, convection and radiation. Khan et al. [4] explored the three-dimensional flow of a spinning nanofluid through convection mechanism over a stretching surface. They examined nanofluid flows under the effect of heat transmission using two different models. Authors [5] used the Duan-Rach methodology of generalized Adomic Decomposition method to study MHD radiating nanofluid movement between stretching and shrinking surface. Ramzan and Bilal [6] introduced a chemical reaction-based solution for MHD multidimensional viscoelastic nanofluid flow for thermal enhancement and flow characteristics. To determine an appropriate series solution, they employed HAM technique for numerical results of heat transfer. By applying the Buongiorno model, the MHD double-diffusive nanofluid motion under the impact of natural circulation was evaluated by Akbar et al. [7] over the stretched surface. Salem et al. [8] explored the heat transfer and temperature distribution of magnetic non-Newtonian fluid with mixed convection and variable density effects. Noor et al. [9] investigated the slip performance of temperature distribution over vertically expanding surface using micro-polar nanofluid flow. Siddiqa et al. [10, 11] used the influence of thermal radiation and variable density effects in natural and mixed convection flow over circular and vertical surface numerically. The influence of magneto-hydrodynamic, Soret/Dufour, Newtonian heating, and viscous dissipation on nanofluid flow for heat and mass transfer was examined [12, 13]. Further recent studies of nanofluid flow and heat transfer in this area are included in [14]. Saini and Agrawal [15] studied the influence of radiation, heat generation and slip conditions on nanofluid flow for temperature and concentration performance over shrinking sheet.

The investigation of non-Newtonian fluids has garnered significant attention from scientists and researchers in recent years due to its diverse geophysical and technological uses, including oils, blood at extremely low shear rates, paints, shampoos, polymer compounds, colloidal form liquids, cosmetic products, pasta, suspending liquids, ice cream, and dough flour. The natural and synthetic nanofluid can be utilized in biotechnology, food manufacturing, drilling procedures, and red blood cells, particles, and extending chain polymers. These fluids have a nonlinear relationship between variable viscosity and shear rate, and the resultant products have a variety of rheological properties. The whole properties of non-Newtonian liquids cannot be uncovered by an individual governing model. For technical and industrial-focused manufacturing processes like hot rolling, papermaking, fiber optics, and metal extrusion, the flow of extensible boundary layer motion is becoming more and more significant. The works of [16, 17] inspired other scholars to submit more creative concepts. Mustafa et al. [18] addressed the three-dimensional rotational motion of ferro-fluid caused by a radiated layer under the effect of magneto-hydrodynamics. A novel concept of radiating heat flow in nonlinear form was examined in relation to a mixture of nanoparticles. The impacts of Soret/Dufour on MHD second-grade viscous fluid flow across a porous oscillatory stretching sheet for heat radiation were discussed in reference [19]. The effects of ohmic dissipation and thermal radiation on MHD nanofluid flow over nonlinear porous stretching sheet were explored in references [20, 21]. The performance of heat and mass transfer of second-grade nanofluid flow over permeable stretching surface was examined by Abbas et al. [22]. Several authors [23, 24] examined the slip boundary effects on temperature and concentration profiles of second grade and ternary hybrid nanofluid flow numerically. The exothermic catalytic chemical reaction effect on magnetic-driven nanofluid flow for heat transfer and temperature performance was computed in references [25, 26]. The steady free convective flow of nanofluid and magnetic boundary layers of radiative nanofluid were computed in references [27, 28]. The radiating heat transfer and natural convection flow of fluid over wavy cylindrical wall were calculated in references [29, 30]. Agrawal and Saini [31] performed mathematical analysis for thermal transport and slip performance of temperature distribution over stretching surface in the presence of thermal radiation. Saini et al. [32] used the effects of viscous dissipation and chemical reaction on unsteady nanofluid flow for heat and mass transfer performance within the magnetic field.

The novelty of current analysis presents the influence of temperature dependent density, chemical reaction, and magnetic field on heat and mass transfer performance of second-grade nanofluid. The flow of nanofluid is performed over tow-dimensional horizontal cylinder with thermophoresis and Brownian motion effects. In above literature, studies are performed at small temperature difference between the surface and ambient fluid. In thermal engineering, the circumstances may arise where temperature difference is large. In this situation, the density of fluid is assumed as exponential function of temperature which clearly describes research gap between present and existing studies. By following [8, 10, 11], the nanofluid model is solved using appropriate similarity variables and stream functions in the presence of thermal and concentration slip effects. The main objective of this study signifies the slip performance in temperature and concentration profiles at large temperature difference. The numerical outcomes of physical properties are calculated through using Newton Raphson technique with central difference formula of finite difference method. The steady performance of skin friction, Nusselt number and Sherwood number is computed in MATLAB tool. The important research questions about current analysis are discussed below.

Newton’s technique

Keller box method converts the governing nonlinear ODEs into system of nonlinear algebraic equations after discretization. The solution of nonlinear equation is not possible directly using ordinary matric techniques. Therefore, the Newton Raphson method is employed to linearize the nonlinear algebraic equations at each iteration step. Several mesh sizes N = 1001, 201, 301, and 401 are performed for grid independence test to compute accuracy of results. It is noticed that the variations in physical quantities such as skin friction, Nusselt number and Sherwood number are negligible at N = 401. Therefore, the mesh N = 401 with and tolerance 10–15 are used for numerical outcomes. Moreover, Newton Raphson method provides rapid quadratic convergence, stability and accuracy in boundary layers. Conclusion

Impact of second-grade nanofluid flow over horizontal stretching circular cylinder has many applications in heat exchangers, cooling towers and chimneys, pipelines, rocket bodies, aircraft fuselages, submarine hulls, offshore structures, and flow bodies of industrial sectors. The current study shows the heat and mass transport of second-grade nanofluid over horizontal stretching circular cylinder with thermal-concentration slip, variable density and chemical reactions. The objective of this study signifies the flow characteristics and thermal performance of second-grade nanofluid in large temperature differences between the surface and ambient fluid. In this situation, the density is assumed as exponential function of temperature. Using an established stream function and similarities modification, the coupled partial differential equations are converted into ordinary differential equations with important physical parameters. The resulting ordinary model is solved by applying average and central formula of finite difference approach with Newton–Raphson method for numerical outputs.
