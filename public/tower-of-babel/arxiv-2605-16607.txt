Introduction

For positive integers
k<t_{1},\dots,t_{q}
, we denote by

k
-uniform

-color Ramsey number of

contains a copy of

the edges of which all receive color

c\in[q]
.
The study of these numbers goes back to the seminal paper of Ramsey
[
13
]
, but the problem of finding effective bounds for Ramsey numbers was first studied by Erdős and Rado
[
7

, who
implicitly proved a recursive bound of the form

r_{k}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{q})\leq 2^{\binom{r_{k-1}(t_{1}-1,\ldots,t_{q}-1)}{k-1}}.

(1)

As a corollary, they gave a tower-type upper bound for such hypergraph Ramsey numbers,
greatly improving over the bound from Ramsey’s proof, which was not even primitive recursive.
More precisely, we recursively define the
tower function

T_{i}(x)
by setting
T_{0}(x)=x
and
T_{i+1}(x)=2^{T_{i}(x)}
(thus the subscript is the number of 2’s in the tower); the recurrence in (
1
) for two colors, e.g., then gives that
r_{k}(s,t)\leq T_{k-1}((2+o(1))(s+t))
.

In this note, we investigate upper bounds for another hypergraph Ramsey problem, namely the
k
-uniform vertex online Ramsey game
.
This was introduced by Conlon, Fox, and Sudakov in 2010
[
6
]
for graphs (i.e.,

) in order to give a quantitative improvement over the bound given by (

) for three-uniform hypergraphs.
A hypergraph analogue of the game was first described by Fox, Pach, Sudakov, and Suk
[
8
]
, who studied it for monotone paths,
and by Gasarch, Parrish, and Sinai
[

]
, who studied it for cliques.
Mubayi and Suk

also introduced another variant in their work on the Erdős–Hajnal hypergraph Ramsey problem.

The

-uniform game is played by two players, builder and painter, starting on the ordered vertex set

At the beginning of round

i_{1}<\dots<i_{k-1}<i

•

Whenever builder decides to build an edge, painter must immediately assign it a color from

•

In each round

, builder must build at least one new edge

Builder’s goal is to force painter to create a monochromatic

Since builder always has a winning strategy, namely, to build a clique on

It is worth mentioning that the more general

and Kurek and Ruciński

For more information on this version, we refer the interested reader to the papers

]
and the references therein.

Conlon, Fox, and Sudakov

proved that for the graph vertex online Ramsey game, builder always has a strategy which uses at most

vertices and at most

In particular,

\tilde{r}_{2}(s,t)\leq(s+t-4)\cdot\binom{s+t-2}{s-1},

(2)

and they subsequently use this to give an upper bound
2

2

For off-diagonal Ramsey numbers where

r_{3}(s,t)\leq 2^{\tilde{r}_{2}(s-1,t-1)}+1\leq 2^{(s+t-4)\cdot\binom{s+t-2}{s-1}}+1.

(3)

After finishing the first draft of our paper, we became aware of an exposition by Gasarch, Parrish, and Sinai
[

]
. In this work, they describe a generalization of the Conlon-Fox-Sudakov method for hypergraphs of higher uniformity, but did not obtain an improvement for hypergraph Ramsey numbers. They subsequently raised the question of whether the vertex online Ramsey game for hypergraphs could be used to obtain a further improvement for

Our main result and its first corollary below address this question, giving a recursive bound for

Theorem 1

Let

\begin{split}\tilde{r}_{k}(s,t)&\leq\tilde{r}_{k-1}(s-1,t-1)\cdot 2^{\tilde{r}_{k-1}(s-1,t-1)}\\[4.30554pt]
&=2^{(1+o(1))\tilde{r}_{k-1}(s-1,t-1)},\end{split}

It is worth pointing out that bounds of the form (

8

9
]

We decided to include it in the statement of the theorem because it is an immediate consequence of our proof, which takes a slightly different route from this prior work (wherein it was proved by a modification of the Erdős–Rado argument), and it will be used in the proofs of our corollaries.
On the other hand, (

This sharper recurrence for hypergraph vertex online Ramsey numbers quickly yields two corollaries.
First of all, we obtain a quantitative improvement to the best upper bound for diagonal hypergraph Ramsey numbers

Let

It also extends more generally to give an improvement for certain regimes of multicolor hypergraph Ramsey numbers, but we omit these details.

This matches the current best upper bound for

In light of exciting recent progress on two-uniform diagonal Ramsey numbers

e=\{v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}\}
was built by builder with
i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}
and painter assigned
\Delta(e)=c
, then the letter
c
appears in the word
\Phi(v_{i_{k}})
at the point when builder’s
(k-1)
-uniform strategy picked the edge
e\setminus\{v_{i_{k}}\}
during round
i_{k}
of the
k
-uniform game.
Thus, the number of edges built by builder in the
k
-uniform game is exactly

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\Phi(v_{j})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}i\cdot q^{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m\cdot q^{m},

as required.
∎

3.
Proofs of the Corollaries

For the reader’s convenience we restate Corollary
2
.

Corollary
.

Let
3\leq k<t
. Then

r_{k}(t,t)\leq T_{k-2}\left((1+o(1))2t\binom{2(t-k+1)}{t-k+1}\right).

Proof.

We apply (
4
) and then iterate (
5
)
k-3
times to get

\displaystyle r_{k}(t,t)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq: ramsey-bound}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle 2^{(1+o(1))\tilde{r}_{k-1}(t-1,t-1)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq: online bound}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle 2^{T_{k-3}((1+o(1))\tilde{r}_{2}(t-k+2,t-k+2))}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq: CFS-2-uniform-online}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle T_{k-2}\left((1+o(1))2t\binom{2(t-k+1)}{t-k+1}\right).

∎

Finally, we state and prove Corollary
5
in full generality, for
q
colors.

Corollary
.

For all
\delta\in(0,1]
and
2\leq k<t_{1},\dots,t_{q}
, at least one of the following two inequalities holds:

r_{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{q})\leq q^{\frac{\binom{r_{k-1}(t_{1}-1,\ldots,t_{q}-1)}{k-1}}{\delta k}}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{or}\hskip 28.45274ptr_{k+1}(t_{1}+1,\dots,t_{q}+1)\leq q^{r_{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{q})^{(1+o(1))\delta k}},

where the
o(1)
term goes to
0
as
\max_{i}\{t_{i}\}\to\infty
.

Proof.

The two cases depend on whether
\tilde{r}_{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{q})\leq r_{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{q})^{\mu k}
, where

\mu=\delta\left(1+\frac{\log_{q}\tilde{r}_{k-1}(t_{1}-1,\dots,t_{q}-1)}{\tilde{r}_{k-1}(t_{1}-1,\dots,t_{q}-1)}\right)=(1+o(1))\delta.

If this inequality holds, then

\displaystyle r_{k+1}(t_{1}+1,\dots,t_{q}+1)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq: q-color ramsey bound}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle q^{\tilde{r}_{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{q})}+k-1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q^{r_{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{q})^{(1+o(1))\delta k}}.

Otherwise, we have
\tilde{r}_{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{q})>r_{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{q})^{\mu k}
, and so

\displaystyle r_{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{q})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{q})^{1/\mu k}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq: q-color online bound}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle q^{\left(\tilde{r}_{k-1}(t_{1}-1,\dots,t_{q}-1)+\log_{q}\tilde{r}_{k-1}(t_{1}-1,\dots,t_{q}-1)\right)/\mu k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\frac{\tilde{r}_{k-1}(t_{1}-1,\dots,t_{q}-1)}{\delta k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q^{\frac{\binom{r_{k-1}(t_{1}-1,\ldots,t_{q}-1)}{k-1}}{\delta k}},

where in the last line we used the trivial bound
\tilde{r}_{k-1}(t_{1}-1,\dots,t_{q}-1)\leq\binom{r_{k-1}(t_{1}-1,\ldots,t_{q}-1)}{k-1}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Concluding remarks

In this note, we showed that

r_{k}(s,t)\leq 2^{\tilde{r}_{k-1}(s-1,t-1)}+k-2\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{r}_{k}(s,t)\leq 2^{(1+o(1))\tilde{r}_{k-1}(s-1,t-1)}.

It would of course be desirable to improve on the trivial bound
\tilde{r}_{k-1}(s,t)\leq\binom{r_{k-1}(s,t))}{k-1}
in such a way that Theorem
1
gave a genuine improvement over (
1
) for all combinations
k<s\leq t
.
We believe that even something a bit stronger should be true (cf. the main result in
[
4
]
).

Problem 10
.

Show that for
2\leq k<t
, we have that
\tilde{r}_{k}(t,t)=o\left(\binom{r_{k}(t,t)}{k}\right)
as
t\to\infty
.

Another natural question is whether our recursive bound for vertex online Ramsey numbers is sharp.
For example, is it true that
\tilde{r}_{k}(t,t)=2^{\Theta(\tilde{r}_{k-1}(t-1,t-1))}
?
The stepping-up lemma does not adapt in an obvious way to provide a matching lower bound, and so a proof of such a statement appears to require some new ideas.
