Plausibility Is Not Prediction: Contrastive Evidence for LLM-Based Cellular Perturbation Reasoning
Xinyu Yuan, Xixian Liu, Jianan Zhao, Yashi Zhang, Hongyu Guo, Jian Tang

Abstract
Perturbation experiments are central to understanding cellular mechanisms, but remain costly and sparse, motivating prediction of gene expression responses for unobserved conditions. A promising recent direction leverages large language models (LLMs) as “virtual cell” simulators—using stepwise, knowledge-grounded mechanistic reasoning to infer differential expression—pointing toward an interpretable, knowledge-driven paradigm that transcends purely data-driven approaches. However, we find that plausibility is not prediction: despite producing biologically plausible explanations, these methods fail to capture perturbation-specific effects: systematically overestimating differential expression, often underperforming a simple gene - frequency baseline in aggregate evaluations, and collapsing to chance-level performance at the per-gene level. This reveals a reliance on intrinsic gene response tendencies rather than true perturbation reasoning. We trace this failure to how evidence is presented: existing methods evaluate perturbation–gene pairs in isolation, without exposing how related perturbations differ in their effects on the same gene. To address this limitation, we introduce CORE ( C ontrastive O rganization of R elational E vidence), which reframes prediction as a comparison task by organizing evidence into positive and negative outcomes from related perturbations. Using a biomedical knowledge graph for evidence retrieval, CORE improves calibration and substantially boosts perturbation-specific prediction in both LLM-based and non-LLM settings: for example, on drug-perturbation data, CORE-Reasoning improves Qwen3.5-9B aggregate metrics by up to 28.6%, while on generic perturbation data, CORE-Voting raises macro-per-gene AUROC from chance to 0.703 in average across four cell lines. This highlights contrastive evidence organization as essential to reliable LLM-based perturbation reasoning.

Preliminaries

2.1
Perturbation Differential-Expression Prediction

Task definition.
We study binary differential-expression (DE) prediction under perturbation. Each example is a triple
(c,p,g)
, where
c
denotes a cellular context (e.g., C32 melanoma cells or K562 leukemia cells),
p
is a perturbation such as a drug or genetic intervention, and
g
is a target gene. The label
y_{c,p,g}\in\{0,1\}
indicates whether
g
is called differentially expressed after applying
p
in context
c
. Since our benchmarks evaluate a fixed cellular context at a time
wu2025perturbqa
;
zhang2025tahoe100m
, we omit
c
when unambiguous and write
y_{p,g}
.

Limited-support held-out perturbations.
We focus on an out-of-distribution setting in which test perturbations are held out from the observed support perturbations. Let
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{sup}}
denote support perturbations and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{test}}
denote held-out test perturbations. Prediction uses only a finite collection of observed DE outcomes, reflecting the practical setting where profiling covers a limited panel of perturbations and genes. A support budget
(k,m)
provides labels for
k
support perturbations and
m
measured genes per support perturbation:

\mathcal{S}_{k,m}=\big\{(p_{i},g_{j},y_{p_{i},g_{j}}):p_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{sup}}^{(k)},\,g_{j}\in\mathcal{G}_{p_{i}}^{(m)}\big\},

(1)

where
\mathcal{G}_{p_{i}}^{(m)}
denotes the set of measured genes selected for perturbation
p_{i}
.

2.2
Perturbation-Specific Effects Evaluation

Aggregate metrics.
Following prior perturbation-reasoning benchmarks
wu2025perturbqa
;
wei2026vcworld
, aggregate metrics are computed over all test perturbation–gene pairs. These metrics summarize global predictive performance, but they can be dominated by gene-level response frequencies and therefore do not by themselves show whether a model distinguishes among perturbations for the same target gene.

Per-gene metrics.
To measure perturbation-specific discrimination, we also report macro-per-gene metrics. For each gene
g
, metrics are computed over the held-out set
\{(p,g):p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{test}}\}
, considering only genes with both positive and negative labels; the resulting scores are then averaged across genes. Because
g
is fixed within each group, these metrics require the model to rank perturbations differently for the same gene, matching the practical question of which perturbations are likely to affect a given target. They are therefore better aligned with perturbation prioritization than aggregate metrics dominated by broadly responsive or unresponsive genes.

3
Motivation: Why Plausibility Fails

Recent LLM-based virtual-cell methods cast perturbation prediction as a natural-language reasoning problem
wei2026vcworld
;
wu2025perturbqa
. Given a cellular context, a perturbation, and a target gene, the model is prompted to combine biomedical knowledge, retrieved examples, and step-by-step mechanistic reasoning to decide whether the gene is differentially expressed. This formulation is appealing because it produces interpretable rationales and can incorporate semantic biological knowledge that is difficult to represent in purely data-driven models.

However, a plausible mechanistic explanation is not necessarily predictive evidence. Existing prompt formulations largely evaluate each perturbation–gene pair in isolation. They encourage the model to explain whether the pair is biologically plausible, but they do not require the model to compare the query against observed outcomes for related perturbations on the same target gene. Consequently, the model may produce a convincing positive rationale whenever a drug, pathway, or disease association can be connected to the gene, even if the measured cellular response is negative. We next show that this mismatch between plausibility and prediction leads to three empirical failure modes.

3.1
Systematic Overestimation of Positive Predictions

Figure 2
:
Predicted positive-rate on Tahoe100M C32, showing VCWorld’s yes bias and CORE’s closer calibration to the true yes ratio.

We first examine whether isolated virtual-cell prompts yield calibrated binary decisions. Across VCWorld-style prompts using different base LLMs, we observe a consistent positive-rate bias (Fig.
2
): on the drug-perturbation data (Tahoe100M C32), where the empirical test positive rate is
29.0\%
, several VCWorld runs predict “yes” for most queries, with some yes-rates exceeding
80\%
. This suggests that isolated biological reasoning can be over-permissive: when prompted for a mechanistic rationale, LLMs often find a plausible story and treat it as evidence for differential expression.

This calibration failure is especially problematic for perturbation prediction because negative labels are not merely missing evidence. A gene can be biologically connected to a perturbation through broad stress, pathway, or disease context, yet fail to pass the differential-expression threshold in a specific cell line. Reliable prediction thus requires evidence that distinguishes measured responses, not only evidence that a connection could exist.

3.2
Aggregate Metrics Are Confounded by Gene Response Frequency

The second failure mode is more subtle: high aggregate performance can be achieved without perturbation-specific reasoning. Many genes have stable background response tendencies across perturbations. Some genes are differentially expressed under many conditions, whereas others are rarely called differentially expressed. A predictor can exploit this marginal gene frequency while ignoring the identity of the test perturbation.

To make this confound explicit, we define a diagnostic baseline, Gene-prior Voting, using only the support set
\mathcal{S}_{k,m}
. For each gene
g
with at least one observed support label, the baseline estimates

\hat{\pi}(g)=\frac{\sum\limits_{(p^{\prime},g^{\prime},y_{p^{\prime},g^{\prime}})\in\mathcal{S}_{k,m}}\mathbf{1}\{g^{\prime}=g\}\,y_{p^{\prime},g^{\prime}}}{\sum\limits_{(p^{\prime},g^{\prime},y_{p^{\prime},g^{\prime}})\in\mathcal{S}_{k,m}}\mathbf{1}\{g^{\prime}=g\}}.

(2)

For every held-out perturbation
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{test}}
, it assigns the score

s_{\mathrm{prior}}(p,g)=\hat{\pi}(g).

(3)

Gene-prior Voting is deliberately blind to perturbation identity: all test perturbations receive the same score for a fixed gene. Yet comparison across Fig.
4(a)
and Fig.
5
shows that it can match or exceed isolated LLM prompting on standard aggregate metrics. This further exposes an evaluation confound. Aggregate AUROC and AUPRC reward a model for ranking broadly responsive genes above rarely responsive genes, even when the model does not learn which perturbations affect a given gene.

3.3
Per-Gene Discrimination Remains Near Chance

Third, when we remove the gene-frequency shortcut by evaluating within each gene, isolated LLM reasoning provides little perturbation-specific discrimination as analyzed below. Macro-per-gene metrics compute performance over held-out perturbations for a fixed target gene and then average across genes with both positive and negative labels. Because the gene is fixed, success requires assigning different scores to different perturbations for the same gene.

Under this evaluation, Gene-prior Voting is uninformative by construction, yielding chance-level per-gene AUROC. More importantly, VCWorld-style LLM prompting also remains near chance across model variants (Fig.
5
). The generated rationales may identify plausible biological links, but they do not reliably determine whether this perturbation changes this gene in the measured context.

Summary of motivation.
Together, these observations indicate that the bottleneck is not only biomedical knowledge coverage or model capacity. The central issue is how evidence is organized before reasoning. Existing prompts present an isolated query and encourage plausibility assessment; they do not expose the empirical contrast that matters for prediction: among biologically related perturbations, which ones did and did not alter the same target gene? This motivates CORE, which reframes perturbation prediction as evidence-grounded comparison by retrieving related support perturbations and organizing their same-gene outcomes into positive and negative evidence.

Method: CORE

Figure 3
:

CORE is a knowledge-grounded framework for perturbation differential-expression prediction. Its central design choice is to replace isolated pairwise plausibility judgments with
contrastive same-gene evidence
. For a held-out query
(p,g)
, where
p
is a perturbation and
g
is the query gene, CORE retrieves support perturbations related to
p
, collects their observed labels for the same gene
g
, and separates the evidence into positive and negative support cases. Since
g
is fixed throughout this comparison, the model must distinguish among perturbations rather than relying solely on abstract mechanistic plausibility.

CORE is model-agnostic. We instantiate it in two complementary settings to
validate the framework (Fig.
3
). CORE-Voting is a non-LLM deterministic evidence aggregator designed to test
whether the contrastive support evidence itself contains perturbation-specific signal,
independent of LLM reasoning (Sec.
4.3
). CORE-Reasoning reformulates the same evidence
into a structured prompt
for LLMs, enabling us to evaluate
whether LLMs can leverage
contrastive evidence more effectively than standard prompting strategies (Sec.
4.4
).

Next, we describe the biomedical knowledge graph, the construction of the contrastive evidence sets, and the two framework instantiations in detail.

4.1
ReasonKG: A Biomedical Knowledge Graph for Contrastive Perturbation Reasoning

CORE requires knowledge-grounded evidence: support perturbations should be biologically related to the query
perturbation via structured biomedical relationships rather than surface textual similarity alone.
However, we find that existing knowledge graphs used in this setting are
often sparse or fragmented, limiting their ability to support gene-conditioned perturbation comparison.
For example, in VCWorld, many drug and gene entities lack traversable paths that can
connect perturbations through shared biological context (Fig.
10
). We therefore construct a biomedical
knowledge graph
\mathcal{G}
, termed ReasonKG. ReasonKG is used to identify support perturbations related to the query
perturbation (Sec.
4.2
) and to provide interpretable relational paths for downstream reasoning (Sec.
4.4
).

ReasonKG integrates and harmonizes
multiple biomedical resources
wei2026vcworld
;
chandak2023building
;
himmelstein2017systematic
;
ni2024pertkge
;
walsh2020biokg
,
providing broad and biologically actionable coverage. Most nodes are centered on genes, proteins,
pathways, GO terms, and diseases (Fig.
1
), while multi-source merged nodes create
reliable anchors for connecting heterogeneous biomedical knowledge. Its diverse relation vocabulary
links perturbations to targets, pathways, functions, and biological contexts,
enabling biologically grounded relational reasoning.
Additional analyses of node and relation distributions, graph connectivity, and comparisons with the VCWorld knowledge graph are provided in App.
B
.

4.2
Construction of Contrastive Evidence Sets

Given a query
(c,p,g)
, CORE retrieves perturbations biologically related to
p
using the biomedical knowledge graph introduced in Sec.
4.1
, collects their observed outcomes for the target gene
g
, and organizes the retrieved evidence into positive and negative support sets, as detailed below.

KG-derived perturbation signatures.
For each KG-covered perturbation
p
, CORE constructs a sparse signature
\phi(p)
. The signature summarizes the perturbation’s relation to KG-covered genes using shortest-path distance buckets and training-estimated proximity statistics (details in App.
C.2
).

Retrieving structurally similar perturbations.
Given a query perturbation
p
and a support perturbation
p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{sup}}
, CORE computes their perturbation-side similarity as clipped cosine similarity:

\mathrm{sim}(p,p^{\prime})=\max\{0,\cos(\phi(p),\phi(p^{\prime}))\}.

(4)

The top-k most similar support perturbations are then retrieved:

\mathcal{R}_{k}(p)=\mathrm{TopK}_{p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{sup}}}\mathrm{sim}(p,p^{\prime}).

(5)

To quantify support strength, similarity scores are
converted into a nonnegative voting weight by a monotone power transformation:

w(p,p^{\prime})=\mathrm{sim}(p,p^{\prime})^{\eta}.

(6)

Contrastive organization of same-gene evidence.
CORE partitions retrieved support perturbations by their observed outcome for the same gene:

\mathcal{H}^{+}_{p,g}=\{p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{k}(p):y_{p^{\prime},g}=1\},\qquad\mathcal{H}^{-}_{p,g}=\{p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{k}(p):y_{p^{\prime},g}=0\}.

(7)

Positive support cases contain related perturbations that affected
g
, while negative cases contain related perturbations that did not. This same-gene contrast is central to CORE. Because
g
is fixed, the evidence compares perturbations rather than genes. Both positive and negative evidence
are necessary because biological relatedness does not imply identical transcriptional response.

Finally, CORE summarizes the retrieved evidence using positive and negative vote masses:

V^{+}_{p,g}=\sum_{p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{+}_{p,g}}w(p,p^{\prime}),\qquad V^{-}_{p,g}=\sum_{p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{-}_{p,g}}w(p,p^{\prime}).

(8)

The resulting evidence packet contains the query perturbation, positive and negative support sets, and the corresponding
vote masses. CORE-Voting uses this packet numerically while CORE-Reasoning serializes it into an LLM prompt for reasoning,
as detail next.

4.3
CORE-Voting

CORE-Voting is a non-LLM, deterministic predictor that directly aggregates the contrastive support evidence, to test whether the contrastive support evidences inherently contains perturbation-specific signals. We define the weighted positive support fraction as a contrast score for the same fixed gene:

s_{\mathrm{contrast}}(p,g)=\begin{cases}\frac{V^{+}_{p,g}}{V^{+}_{p,g}+V^{-}_{p,g}},&V^{+}_{p,g}+V^{-}_{p,g}>0,\\[5.16663pt]
\hat{\pi}(g),&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(9)

This score transfers evidence from observed support examples
y_{p^{\prime},g}
for the same target gene
g
. A high value means that under perturbations similar to
p
, relevant support evidence predicts
g
to be more likely differentially expressed. When no same-gene support examples are observed, we fall back to the support gene prior defined in Eqn.
2
.

Gene-anchor smoothing.
To improve robustness under limited support, CORE also applies gene-anchor smoothing to borrow insights from genes similar to the query gene
g
. A signed support response profile
r_{g}
over support perturbations is defined as

r_{g}(p^{\prime})=\begin{cases}2y_{p^{\prime},g}-1,&\text{if }(p^{\prime},g,y_{p^{\prime},g})\in\mathcal{S}_{k,m},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(10)

Gene-gene similarity is computed by clipped cosine similarity:

\mathrm{sim}(g,g^{\prime})=\max\{0,\cos(r_{g},r_{g^{\prime}})\}.

(11)

We identify top
H
response-similar anchor genes

\mathcal{A}_{H}(g)=\mathrm{TopH}_{g^{\prime}}\mathrm{sim}(g,g^{\prime}).

(12)

The anchor-smoothed score averages the same-gene contrast scores over the anchor genes:

s_{\mathrm{anchor}}(p,g)=\frac{\sum_{g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{H}(g)}\mathrm{sim}(g,g^{\prime})s_{\mathrm{base}}(p,g^{\prime})}{\sum_{g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{H}(g)}\mathrm{sim}(g,g^{\prime})}.

(13)

Final CORE-Voting score
. We compute a convex combination of the same-gene contrast score and the anchor-smoothed score:

s_{\text{{CORE-Voting}}}(p,g)=(1-\alpha)s_{\mathrm{contrast}}(p,g)+\alpha s_{\mathrm{anchor}}(p,g).

(14)

4.4
CORE-Reasoning

CORE-Reasoning turns CORE evidence into a structured LLM prompt. For each query
(p,g)
, the evidence contains:

(i)
the gene prior
\hat{\pi}(g)
, marked as a bias to avoid over-reliance;

(ii)
same-gene contrast evidence, including
s_{\mathrm{contrast}}(p,g)
, positive and negative support perturbations (
\mathcal{H}^{+}_{p,q}
,
\mathcal{H}^{-}_{p,q}
), and the corresponding vote masses (
V^{+}_{p,q}
,
V^{-}_{p,q}
);

(iii)
shortest KG paths between the grounded or mapped query perturbation and gene (path retrieval detailed in App.
C.7
); and

(iv)
gene-anchor smoothing evidence, including
s_{\mathrm{anchor}}(p,g)
, top anchor genes
\mathcal{A}_{H}(g)
, and the final score
s_{\text{CORE-Voting }}(p,g)
.

The LLM then adjudicates evidence quality: whether positive and negative supports differ in biological relevance, whether KG paths provide specific biological mechanistic bridges or only generic connectivity, whether endpoint mappings are reliable, and whether anchor-gene smoothing is consistent with the same-gene contrast.
Thus, CORE-Reasoning uses the LLM for structured evidence interpretation—resolving conflicts, assessing path quality, and explaining query-specific deviations—instead of unconstrained plausibility generation.

two common failure modes of LLM-based perturbation reasoning: high aggregate

yes-biased predictions from isolated biological plausibility judgments. Our
experiments address four questions:
(1)
how strong is the gene response
prior under standard aggregate metrics?
(2)
does contrastive same-gene
evidence contain perturbation-specific signal even without an LLM?
(3)
can

support budget?
(4)
do the results transfer from drug perturbations to a
generic perturbations?

Related Work

Single-cell perturbation response prediction.

Perturbation prediction methods such as CPA
lotfollahi2023predicting
, GEARS
roohani2024predicting
, scGPT
cui2024scgpt
, and State
adduri2025state
model cellular responses to genetic or chemical interventions. Their objectives typically emphasize expression reconstruction, distributional shifts, or aggregate metrics rather than perturbation–gene discrimination. Recent benchmarks show that strong perturbation and foundation models can underperform simple baselines
ahlmanneltze2025deep
, while binary perturbation reasoning over perturbation–gene pairs remains challenging
wu2025perturbqa
. CORE instead organizes relational evidence contrastively at this level.

LLM-based perturbation reasoning.

Recent LLM methods cast perturbation prediction as knowledge-grounded reasoning: PerturbQA defines differential-expression, direction, and enrichment QA tasks
wu2025perturbqa
; CellVerse extends language-centric evaluation to single-cell multi-omics
zhang2025cellverse
; SUMMER combines biological summaries, retrieved evidence, and prompting
wu2025perturbqa
; and VCWorld integrates structured knowledge with iterative virtual-cell reasoning
wei2026vcworld
. These methods largely assess perturbation–gene pairs in isolation; CORE compares related perturbations with positive and negative outcomes for the same target gene.

In-context learning in LLMs.

In-context learning (ICL) enables task adaptation through instructions and demonstrations without parameter updates
brown2020language
. Prior work shows that performance depends strongly on demonstration relevance, retrieval, ordering, and label or format structure
liu2022makes
;
rubin2022learning
;
lu2022fantastically
;
min2022rethinking
. For perturbation reasoning, examples should define the relevant comparison, not merely provide plausible support. CORE therefore uses ICL contrastively by presenting matched same-gene positive and negative outcomes, encouraging comparative rather than plausibility-only reasoning.

7
Conclusion, Limitations and Broader Impact

We show that biologically plausible reasoning is not sufficient for reliable perturbation prediction. By organizing retrieved relational evidence into same-gene positive and negative support sets, CORE reframes prediction as evidence-grounded comparison rather than isolated plausibility assessment. Across LLM-based and non-LLM settings, this contrastive organization improves calibration and perturbation-specific discrimination, suggesting that reliable virtual-cell reasoning depends as much on evidence structure as on biological knowledge coverage.

Limitations.

This study focuses on binary differential-expression prediction in fixed cellular contexts, leaving dose, time, protocol variation, expression magnitude, and DEG uncertainty for future work. CORE also depends on knowledge-graph and support-set coverage, so sparse or biased evidence can weaken retrieval and reasoning.

Broader impact.

CORE may help prioritize experiments and make LLM-assisted perturbation reasoning more auditable by exposing positive and negative support evidence. Its predictions should be used for hypothesis generation, not as a substitute for expert review, experimental validation, or clinical decision making.

KG sources and harmonization.

CORE uses a harmonized biomedical knowledge graph assembled from VCWorld,

Handling missing KG evidence.

Missing KG evidence is represented explicitly rather than imputed as negative
biological evidence. If the gene endpoint is not grounded, CORE omits
query-pair path evidence for that gene and relies on same-gene support evidence
when available, gene-anchor smoothing through response-similar grounded genes,
and the support-set gene prior. If the perturbation endpoint is not grounded,
CORE does not fabricate a direct KG path. The evidence packet marks the missing
endpoint and the LLM prompt states that absent KG connectivity should be treated
as missing information, not as evidence that the perturbation cannot affect the
gene.

Figure 9
:

Source and type distribution of isolated nodes in ReasonKG.
Most isolated nodes originate from source-specific entries that lack cross-resource bridging edges, and the majority are genes or pathways.
This audit identifies regions where KG-based retrieval may be sparse and motivates reporting coverage alongside perturbation predictions.

Handling disconnected or unmapped perturbations.

Some perturbation names do not resolve to a canonical KG drug node, especially
when salts, hydrates, stereochemical qualifiers, or benchmark-specific naming
variants are present. For these cases, CORE uses a weak name-based fallback:
the query perturbation is mapped to the five most similar KG-covered evaluation
perturbations using character
n
-gram TF-IDF similarity, with weights
proportional to the fourth power of the lexical similarity. This fallback is
used only to transfer CORE scores from nearby covered perturbations and is
flagged as indirect evidence in the prompt. It is not treated as a curated
drug-target or mechanism edge.

ReasonKG resolves this by merging VCWorld with additional sources such as PrimeKG, HetioNet, BioKG, and PertKGE, which introduce bridging edges across drugs, diseases, proteins, pathways, GO terms, anatomy, and genes. As a result, ReasonKG connects many query pairs: 326 rows have direct drug–gene links, 12,032 have two-hop links, and 35,724 lie in the same connected component, while VCWorld alone has zero connected query rows. This shows that the integrated graph provides the transferable graph space needed for evidence retrieval and perturbation-specific reasoning.

Appendix C
Method Details

Notation and inputs.

For a fixed cell context, let

\mathcal{S}_{k,m}=\{(p,g,y_{p,g})\}
denote the labeled support set under
support budget
(k,m)
, where
p
is a perturbation,
g
is a target gene,
and
y_{p,g}\in\{0,1\}
indicates whether
g
is differentially expressed
under
p
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{sup}}
be the set of support
perturbations,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{eval}}
be the set of held-out
perturbations to score, and
\mathcal{G}
be the benchmark gene set. CORE uses
ReasonKG only as an evidence-organizing structure: KG paths, distances, and
entity mappings are not treated as labels. The gold label for a held-out query
is never included in the evidence packet.

C.1
ReasonKG Grounding and Distance Audit

For every support or evaluation perturbation that can be grounded in ReasonKG,
we audit its graph distance to each grounded benchmark gene. Let

d(p,h)\in\{1,\ldots,6,\infty\}
denote the shortest audited path length from
perturbation
p
to gene
h
, with
d(p,h)=\infty
when no path is found
within depth six. The audit is performed separately for support and evaluation
splits so that held-out perturbations remain perturbation-disjoint from the
support labels. These distances serve two purposes: they define sparse
perturbation signatures for support transfer, and they provide compact
shortest-path evidence for CORE-Reasoning prompts.

The empirical association between distance and support labels is estimated only
from the support split:

\rho(d)=\frac{\sum_{(p,h)\in\mathcal{S}_{k,m}}\mathbf{1}[d(p,h)=d]\,y_{p,h}}{\sum_{(p,h)\in\mathcal{S}_{k,m}}\mathbf{1}[d(p,h)=d]}.

If a distance bucket is absent in the support split, CORE uses a fixed fallback
bucket value rather than estimating from held-out labels.

C.2
KG-Derived Perturbation Signatures

For each KG-covered perturbation
p
, CORE constructs a sparse signature

\phi(p)
over grounded genes. For each grounded gene

h\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{KG}}
, the signature includes a distance-identity
feature and a proximity-rate feature:

\phi(p)=\left\{\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{dist}:h:d(p,h)],\quad\rho(d(p,h))\,\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{prox}:h]\right\}_{h\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{KG}}}.

In the implementation these are dictionary features such as

\texttt{dist:HES1:2}=1
and
\texttt{prox:HES1}=\rho(2)
, vectorized into
a sparse feature matrix. This representation lets perturbations be similar
when they have comparable graph-distance patterns to response-relevant genes,
not merely when their names are textually similar.

C.3
Perturbation Similarity and Contrastive Retrieval

Given a held-out perturbation
p
and a support perturbation

p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{sup}}
, CORE computes a clipped cosine similarity
between KG-derived signatures:

\mathrm{sim}(p,p^{\prime})=\max\{0,\cos(\phi(p),\phi(p^{\prime}))\}.

The similarity is converted into a nonnegative vote weight

w(p,p^{\prime})=\mathrm{sim}(p,p^{\prime})^{\eta},

with
\eta=0.5
in the reported experiments. The main text denotes the
retrieved perturbation set by
\mathcal{R}_{k}(p)
. Operationally, the numerical
CORE-Voting score aggregates all support perturbations with observed labels and
nonzero weights; the prompt displays only the top
L=3
positive and top

L=3
negative cases for readability.

For a query
(p,g)
, CORE keeps the target gene fixed and partitions the
weighted support evidence by the observed outcome for that same gene:

\mathcal{H}^{+}_{p,g}=\{p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{sup}}:m_{p^{\prime},g}=1,\;y_{p^{\prime},g}=1,\;w(p,p^{\prime})>0\},

\mathcal{H}^{-}_{p,g}=\{p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{sup}}:m_{p^{\prime},g}=1,\;y_{p^{\prime},g}=0,\;w(p,p^{\prime})>0\},

where
m_{p^{\prime},g}
indicates whether the support label for
(p^{\prime},g)
is
observed. This fixed-gene partition is the key contrastive step: positive cases
show related perturbations that affected
g
, while negative cases show
related perturbations that did not.

C.4
CORE-Voting

CORE summarizes the contrastive support evidence through positive and negative
vote masses:

V^{+}_{p,g}=\sum_{p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{+}_{p,g}}w(p,p^{\prime}),\qquad V^{-}_{p,g}=\sum_{p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{-}_{p,g}}w(p,p^{\prime}).

The same-gene contrast score is the weighted positive fraction:

s_{\mathrm{contrast}}(p,g)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{V^{+}_{p,g}}{V^{+}_{p,g}+V^{-}_{p,g}},&V^{+}_{p,g}+V^{-}_{p,g}>0,\\[5.16663pt]
\pi_{g},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Here
\pi_{g}
is the support-set gene prior,

\pi_{g}=\frac{\sum_{p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{sup}}}m_{p^{\prime},g}y_{p^{\prime},g}}{\sum_{p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{sup}}}m_{p^{\prime},g}},

with a global support positive rate used only when
g
has no observed
support labels. The evidence packet records
V^{+}_{p,g}
,
V^{-}_{p,g}
, and
their sum as the observed vote mass, because low vote mass indicates that the
same-gene contrast score is weakly supported.

C.5
Gene-Anchor Smoothing

To improve robustness when same-gene support is sparse, CORE also borrows
evidence from response-similar genes. For each gene
g
, define a signed
support response profile over support perturbations:

r_{g}(p^{\prime})=\begin{cases}2y_{p^{\prime},g}-1,&\text{if }(p^{\prime},g,y_{p^{\prime},g})\in\mathcal{S}_{k,m},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Gene-gene similarity is computed as clipped cosine similarity:

\mathrm{sim}(g,h)=\max\{0,\cos(r_{g},r_{h})\}.

For each target gene, CORE selects the top
H
grounded anchor genes,

\mathcal{A}_{H}(g)=\operatorname{TopH}_{h\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{KG}}}\mathrm{sim}(g,h).

We use
H=10
for the numerical anchor score. If the target gene itself is
KG-grounded, it is forced to be the strongest anchor with similarity
1
.

The anchor-smoothed score is

s_{\mathrm{anchor}}(p,g)=\frac{\sum_{h\in\mathcal{A}_{H}(g)}\mathrm{sim}(g,h)\,s_{\mathrm{contrast}}(p,h)}{\sum_{h\in\mathcal{A}_{H}(g)}\mathrm{sim}(g,h)}.

If the anchor weights sum to zero, CORE sets

s_{\mathrm{anchor}}(p,g)=s_{\mathrm{contrast}}(p,g)
. The final
CORE-Voting score is a convex combination:

s_{\mathrm{CORE}}(p,g)=(1-\alpha)s_{\mathrm{contrast}}(p,g)+\alpha s_{\mathrm{anchor}}(p,g).

We use
\alpha=0.5
in the reported experiments. For prompting, CORE displays
the top three anchor genes, while the numerical anchor score uses all
H=10

anchors.

C.6
Fallback for Unmapped Perturbations

If a raw evaluation perturbation
p
cannot be grounded directly in ReasonKG,
CORE cannot construct
\phi(p)
. In this case, it uses a weak name-based
fallback to transfer scores from KG-covered evaluation perturbations rather
than inventing KG paths for
p
. Character
n
-gram TF-IDF similarity is
used to find the top five KG-covered evaluation perturbations:

\mathcal{M}_{5}(p)=\operatorname{Top5}_{q\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{eval}}^{\mathrm{KG}}}\mathrm{sim}_{\mathrm{char}}(p,q).

The mapped perturbations receive sharpened lexical weights

\beta(p,q)=\frac{\max\{\mathrm{sim}_{\mathrm{char}}(p,q),10^{-6}\}^{4}}{\sum_{q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{5}(p)}\max\{\mathrm{sim}_{\mathrm{char}}(p,q^{\prime}),10^{-6}\}^{4}}.

The fallback contrast score is then

s_{\mathrm{contrast}}(p,g)=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{M}_{5}(p)}\beta(p,q)s_{\mathrm{contrast}}(q,g),

and the same weighted transfer is applied to the anchor and final CORE scores.
This mapping is explicitly marked in CORE-Reasoning prompts as weak indirect
evidence: character similarity is not treated as chemical similarity,
target-profile similarity, or a curated KG edge.

C.7
CORE-Reasoning Path Evidence

CORE-Reasoning uses the same CORE evidence packet but serializes it for an LLM.
KG paths are retrieved for the query pair
(p,g)
, not for each support
perturbation. When both endpoints are grounded, the path module searches for
shortest paths between the query perturbation endpoint and the target gene
endpoint. If an endpoint is not directly available, the prompt may display a
path through a mapped perturbation endpoint or an anchor-gene endpoint; such
paths are labeled as indirect evidence.

Each displayed path
\gamma
receives a compactness score

\mathrm{pathscore}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{|\gamma|},

where
|\gamma|
is the number of edges. This value is used only to rank
paths for display. It is not calibrated as a probability, is not part of the
CORE-Voting score, and the prompt explicitly warns the LLM not to treat shorter
paths as sufficient positive evidence.

Thus, CORE-Voting and CORE-Reasoning share evidence construction but use it
differently. CORE-Voting directly aggregates the support evidence numerically,
whereas CORE-Reasoning asks the LLM to interpret the same contrastive evidence,
judge path specificity, and make only query-specific calibrated adjustments to
the default CORE score.

C.9
Prompt Design for Evidence Serialization

CORE-Reasoning uses a structured evidence packet rather than a free-form
retrieval dump. The packet contains the following fields.

1.

Task and query context.
The prompt states the fixed cell
context, query perturbation, and query gene, together with textual
descriptions of the perturbation and gene when available.

2.

Gene prior.
The support-set positive rate for the target gene
is displayed as a calibration reference, followed by an explicit warning
that the prior alone is insufficient because the prediction must distinguish
perturbations for the same gene.

3.

Same-gene support evidence.
The packet reports the
same-gene contrast score, positive vote mass, negative vote mass, and
observed vote mass. It then lists up to three top-weighted positive and up
to three top-weighted negative support perturbations for the same target
gene, including their KG similarity and vote weight.

4.

KG path evidence.
The packet includes up to five shortest KG
paths between the grounded or mapped query endpoints. Mapped endpoints are
explicitly marked so that the LLM can down-weight indirect evidence.

5.

Gene-anchor evidence.
The packet displays the anchor score,
the final blended score, and the top displayed anchor genes. In the main
experiments, the prompt displays three anchor genes, while the numerical
anchor score is computed from the top ten anchors.

6.

Output contract.
Before the final deterministic yes/no
sentence, the model must output a one-line JSON object with fields

probability
,
label
,
default_final_score
,

support_score
,
anchor_score
,

probability_adjustment
, and
decisive_evidence
.

The prompt asks the model to compare positive and negative same-gene cases
before constructing a biological rationale. This design is intended to prevent
the model from treating any plausible perturbation–gene path as sufficient
positive evidence. It also asks the model to preserve the within-gene ordering
implied by the CORE final score unless the evidence packet gives a clear reason
for a small calibrated adjustment.

C.10
CORE-Reasoning Evidence Packet Example

For the query Bestatin (hydrochloride)–HSP90B1, the evidence packet illustrates
how CORE separates numerical support transfer from KG-path explanation. The
same-gene contrast score is
0.882
, with positive vote mass
6.277
and
negative vote mass
0.839
. The gene-anchor score is
0.977
, giving a final
blended score of
0.929
. The displayed positive same-gene supports include
Ponatinib and Cytarabine; the displayed negative supports include
Hydroxyfasudil and LY-2584702 (tosylate salt). The anchor block lists HSP90B1
itself and response-similar genes such as TPX2 and LINC01320. Because Bestatin
is not directly covered by the KG audit in this case, the KG path block uses a
mapped perturbation endpoint and explicitly marks the path as indirect evidence.

The resulting prompt asks the LLM to first decide whether the query perturbation
is closer to the positive or negative same-gene supports, then judge whether the
KG paths are mechanism-bearing or generic connectivity, and finally report a
calibrated probability. Thus, the LLM receives a constrained reasoning problem:
it may provide mechanistic interpretation, but the default probability and the
contrastive support cases are fixed before generation.

Appendix D
Experimental Details

D.1
Benchmarking Settings

Prediction score calculation.
For CORE-Voting, the reported
score is the deterministic blended probability. For CORE-Reasoning, the
reported score is the parsed probability emitted by the model, with the
CORE-Voting score retained separately as the default calibration value in the
prompt record.

Train/Test split setting.
For the Tahoe100M C32 support-budget sweep, all rows use the same held-out perturbation
set and differ only in the support budget
(k,m)
. For PerturbQA K562 data, the
benchmark train/test split is fixed and the full training split is used as the
support pool. Appendix
A
provides the preprocessing details.

D.2
LLM Inference Protocol

Prompt generation and sharding.

For each dataset split and prompt variant, we first materialize all query
prompts before running model inference. The sharding procedure is label-safe:
every shard contains the full support set and a disjoint subset of held-out
test queries, so all queries in a run condition on the same support pool. The
primary C32 CORE-Reasoning run uses the
k=50,m=200
support budget, the
test split only for evaluation queries. The random
seed is fixed to 42 for support sampling, retrieval-side sampling, and prompt
construction.

Models and backend.

The main local LLM results use Qwen3.5-9B
[
17
]
with SGLang
inference
[
27
]
. Unless otherwise stated, each prompt is decoded once. The
SGLang engine is initialized with automatic weight dtype selection, tensor
parallel size 1, static memory fraction 0.80, and maximum context length
16,386 tokens. The submitted C32 runs use the Triton attention backend and
PyTorch sampling backend, with CUDA graph capture disabled for both full and
piecewise graphs. Each inference shard runs on one GPU with batch size 8.
Prompts are rendered with the model chat template, a generation prompt is
appended, and rendered inputs are truncated to at most 8,192 input tokens.

Decoding parameters.

For the sharded SGLang runs, we use temperature
0.6
, top-
p=0.9
, and a
maximum of 8,192 generated tokens. These settings are shared across the
standard VCWorld-style prompt and the CORE-Reasoning prompt unless explicitly
reported otherwise. CORE-Voting does not invoke an LLM; it directly evaluates
the deterministic blended score described in the method details.

Output format and parsing.

CORE-Reasoning prompts require the model to emit a one-line structured
prediction before the final deterministic answer. For binary DE prediction, the
valid labels are
yes
,
no
, and
insufficient
. The
parser first searches for a JSON object in the marked structured-prediction
block, fenced JSON blocks, or the final raw response. It then reads the

label
field and a continuous
probability
field when present.
Probabilities reported on the
0
–
100
scale are normalized to

[0,1]
; probabilities outside the valid range are ignored. If structured
JSON is absent or malformed, the parser falls back to the final-answer text and
then to explicit probability phrases in the response. A
yes
label is
mapped to prediction 1, a
no
label is mapped to prediction 0, and an

insufficient
or unparsable response is treated as unanswered. When a
valid continuous probability is unavailable but a valid binary label is
available, the binary label is used as the score for thresholded metrics.

D.3
Evaluation Metrics

Instance-level binary metrics.

Let
y_{i}\in\{0,1\}
be the held-out binary DE label for query pair
i
,

\hat{y}_{i}\in\{0,1\}
be the parsed discrete prediction, and
s_{i}\in[0,1]

be the parsed or deterministic continuous score. CORE-Voting uses

s_{i}=\hat{y}_{\mathrm{CORE}}(p_{i},g_{i})
. CORE-Reasoning uses the parsed LLM
probability when available. Accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 are computed on
answered examples only:

\mathrm{precision}=\frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP}},\qquad\mathrm{recall}=\frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}},\qquad\mathrm{F1}=\frac{2\,\mathrm{precision}\,\mathrm{recall}}{\mathrm{precision}+\mathrm{recall}}.

When a denominator is zero, the corresponding precision, recall, or F1 term is
set to zero. We also report balanced accuracy when thresholded probabilistic
predictions are evaluated.

Ranking metrics.

AUROC and AUPRC are computed from the continuous scores
s_{i}
on answered
examples. AUROC is the probability that a randomly selected positive example is
ranked above a randomly selected negative example, with ties counted as one
half. AUPRC is computed as average precision over the score-ranked test set.
These ranking metrics are reported only when the answered subset contains both
positive and negative labels. For deterministic voting methods, all held-out
examples are answered, so the ranking metrics are computed over the complete
test split.

Coverage and effective metrics.

Because LLM outputs can be malformed or explicitly abstain, we report the
answered rate

r_{\mathrm{ans}}=\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{N},

where
N
is the number of labeled evaluation examples and
\mathcal{A}
is
the answered subset. The complementary abstention rate is

q=1-r_{\mathrm{ans}}.

For metrics computed on answered examples, we additionally report effective
metrics by multiplying the answered-only value by
r_{\mathrm{ans}}
. This
prevents a method from appearing strong by answering only a small, easy subset
of queries.

Macro per-gene metrics.

In addition to micro-averaged instance metrics, we compute per-gene
discrimination metrics by grouping held-out examples by target gene. For each
gene with at least one positive and one negative answered example, AUROC, AUPRC,
accuracy, balanced accuracy, precision, recall, F1, specificity, predicted
positive rate, and mean probability are computed within that gene. Macro
per-gene values are the unweighted averages over evaluable genes. Genes with
only one class in the answered subset are excluded from per-gene discrimination
metrics and counted in the skipped-gene statistic.
