Introduction

Cutting plane methods for convex minimization maintain a localization region and repeatedly refine it using separating halfspaces
(
9
;
11
)
. Many progress bounds for such methods are stated in terms of volume decrease or related volume based potentials
(
15
)
. This motivates geometric depth guarantees: a halfspace containing a specified point should capture a constant fraction of the volume. Grünbaum’s inequality provides such a guarantee for the centroid: any hyperplane through the centroid of a convex body splits it so that each closed side contains at least a
1/e
fraction of the volume
(
8
)
.

We study a mixed-integer generalization of this depth guarantee. Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
be a convex body and let
S=C\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Following
12
, we measure the size of
S
by its mixed-integer volume
\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)
, the
d
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
S
as a subset of the mixed-integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
; or equivalently, the sum
\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\operatorname{vol}_{d}\bigl(S\cap(\{\mathbf{z}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\bigr)
, where
\operatorname{vol}_{d}(\cdot)
denotes the usual
d
-dimensional volume. Oertel conjectured that there exists a point
\mathbf{y}\in S
such that every closed halfspace
H
containing
\mathbf{y}
satisfies
\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}e}\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)
. The factor
2^{n}
is unavoidable in general. In the pure integer case
d=0
, taking
C=[0,1]^{n}
gives
S=\{0,1\}^{n}
, and for every
\mathbf{y}\in S
there exists a closed halfspace containing
\mathbf{y}
that captures only one point of
S
, so
\mathcal{H}_{0}(S\cap H)\leq 2^{-n}\mathcal{H}_{0}(S)
. This scale is also consistent with the combinatorial complexity of the integer lattice: Doignon’s theorem shows that the Helly number of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
equals
2^{n}

(
6
)
, and related Helly type bounds appear throughout integer optimization
(
1
)
. Oertel’s conjecture is closely connected to complexity bounds in cutting plane methods and to the information complexity of mixed-integer convex optimization
(
3
;
2
)
.

Existing results verify the conjecture (stated precisely below as Conjecture
1
) for sets whose projection
K=\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C)
is large in a quantitative sense.
3
established it when
K
has lattice width at least
\Omega((n+d)^{5/2}n^{n+2})
, a threshold that grows exponentially in
n
.
5
reduced this to a polynomial threshold by assuming that
K
contains a Euclidean ball of radius at least
1178\,d^{2}n^{3/2}
.

Our main result, Theorem
2.2
, establishes a linear threshold. If
K
contains a translate of
k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
with
k\geq\frac{3e}{2}(n+d)
, then there exists
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in S
such that every closed halfspace
H
containing
\mathbf{y}^{\star}
satisfies

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)\geq\Bigl(\frac{1}{e}-\frac{3(n+d)}{2k}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}_{d}(S).

When
k\geq 3e(n+d)
, the bound becomes
\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)\geq\frac{1}{2e}\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)
, which implies the conjectured
\frac{1}{2^{n}e}
bound for all
n\geq 1
. Corollary
1
states the corresponding centerpoint guarantee. We also show that a linear threshold is necessary in a precise quantitative sense: Theorem
5.1
and Corollary
3
prove that any dimension independent constant fraction guarantee based solely on the existence of a translate of
k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
in
K
requires
k
to scale linearly in
n+d
.

We compare our
\ell_{\infty}
ball condition with the Euclidean ball condition in
5
. A Euclidean ball of radius
R
contains the
\ell_{\infty}
ball
(R/\sqrt{n})\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
. Hence,
R\geq\frac{3e}{2}\sqrt{n}\,(n+d)
suffices to apply Theorem
2.2
, and
R\geq 3e\sqrt{n}\,(n+d)
suffices for the
\frac{1}{2e}
-depth guarantee in Corollary
1
. Compared to the threshold
R\geq 1178\,d^{2}n^{3/2}
in
5
, our Corollary threshold replaces the scaling
d^{2}n^{3/2}
by
\sqrt{n}(n+d)
, which corresponds to dropping the sum of exponents from
3.5
to
1.5
. It also lowers the leading constant from
1178
to
3e\approx 8.15
and replaces quadratic dependence on
d
by linear dependence. At a high level, our proof compares mixed-integer volume and Lebesgue volume via Minkowski thickening and erosion (Lemma
7
). This comparison exploits slice concavity from the Brunn–Minkowski theory
(
7
;
14
)
together with a symmetrization argument
(
10
)
. We then perform a centroid rounding step and conclude using Grünbaum’s inequality
(
8
)
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
fixes notation and states our main results. Section
3
develops the volume comparison tools. Section
4
proves the main theorem and corollary. Section
5
establishes the necessity of a linear threshold.

2
Background and main results

2.1
Basic notation

Throughout,
n,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. We write points in
\mathbb{R}^{n+d}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
as

\mathbf{y}=(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x}),\qquad\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ \mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and let
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\mathbb{R}^{n+d}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the projection onto the first factor, i.e.,
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{z}
.
For a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
, we write

\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(A):=\left\{\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbf{y}):\ \mathbf{y}\in A\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the
\mathbf{z}
-fiber of
A
by

A_{\mathbf{z}}:=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ (\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\in A\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For the remainder of this paper, fix a convex body
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
, i.e., a compact convex set with
\intset(C)\neq\emptyset
, and define the mixed-integer lattice and the feasible mixed-integer set

\Lambda:=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad S:=C\cap\Lambda,\qquad K:=\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For any convex body
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
, we write

S_{D}:=D\cap\Lambda,\qquad K_{D}:=\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(D)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We will use the cube

Q:=[-1/2,1/2]^{n},\qquad\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}:=\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ \|\mathbf{u}\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\},

so that
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}=2Q
.
For
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define the
Minkowski sum
and
erosion
by

A+Q:=\left\{a+\mathbf{q}:\ a\in A,\ \mathbf{q}\in Q\right\},\qquad A\ominus Q:=\left\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ \mathbf{z}+Q\subseteq A\right\}.

2.2
Mixed-integer volume

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we write
\operatorname{vol}_{m}
for the
m
dimensional Lebesgue measure (with the ambient space clear from context).

For a measurable set
A\subseteq\Lambda
, we define its
mixed-integer volume
as

\mathcal{H}_{d}(A):=\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\operatorname{vol}_{d}\bigl(A_{\mathbf{z}}\bigr).

(1)

If
A
is bounded, then only finitely many
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
yield a nonempty fiber, and hence
\mathcal{H}_{d}(A)<\infty
.
In particular,
\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)<\infty
since
C
is bounded.

2.3
Halfspace depth and centerpoints

A (closed) halfspace in
\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
is a set of the form

H=\left\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+d}:\ \mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}\geq c\right\},\qquad\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+d}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\},\ c\in\mathbb{R}.

Definition 1
(Halfspace depth and centerpoints)

For
\mathbf{y}\in S
, define the halfspace depth of
\mathbf{y}
with respect to
S
by

h_{S}(\mathbf{y}):=\inf\left\{\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H):\ H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf{y}\in H\right\}.

The set of centerpoints of
S
is

\mathcal{C}(S):=\argmax_{\mathbf{y}\in S}h_{S}(\mathbf{y}).

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and a convex body
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we denote its centroid by

\mathfrak{c}(D):=\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}_{m}(D)}\int_{D}\mathbf{u}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{u}.

We will use the classical Grünbaum inequality
(
8
)
in the following form.

Theorem 2.1
(Grünbaum)

Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a convex body with centroid
\mathfrak{c}(D)
.
For every closed halfspace
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\mathfrak{c}(D)\in H
,

\operatorname{vol}_{m}(D\cap H)\ \geq\ \Bigl(\frac{m}{m+1}\Bigr)^{m}\operatorname{vol}_{m}(D)\ \geq\ \frac{1}{e}\operatorname{vol}_{m}(D).

12
conjectured the following mixed-integer analogue, with the additional factor
1/2^{n}
reflecting the Helly number of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Conjecture 1
(Oertel’s conjecture)

For any convex body
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
, there exists a point
\mathbf{y}\in S=C\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that for every closed halfspace
H
containing
\mathbf{y}
,

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}e}\mathcal{H}_{d}(S).

2.4
Main results

Our main result verifies Conjecture
1
under a linear threshold condition on the
\ell_{\infty}
radius of the projection.

Theorem 2.2

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
be a convex body, and let
S=C\cap\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
. Write
K=\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Assume that there exist
\mathbf{z}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
k>0
such that

\mathbf{z}_{0}+k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}\subseteq K,\qquad k\geq\frac{3e}{2}(n+d).

(2)

Then there exists
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in S
such that for every closed halfspace
H\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
with
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in H
,

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)\ \geq\ \Bigl(\frac{1}{e}-\frac{3(n+d)}{2k}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}_{d}(S).

(3)

If in addition
k\geq 3e(n+d)
, then the coefficient in (
3
) satisfies
\frac{1}{e}-\frac{3(n+d)}{2k}\geq\frac{1}{2e}
, and hence (
3
) yields
\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)\geq\frac{1}{2e}\,\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)
for every closed halfspace
H\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
with
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in H
.
As
\frac{1}{2e}\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}e}
for all
n\geq 1
, this verifies Conjecture
1
for all sets satisfying (
2
) with
k\geq 3e(n+d)
.
We summarize the corresponding centerpoint implication in Corollary
1
.

Corollary 1

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
2.2
, and suppose in addition that
k\geq 3e(n+d)
.
Then
\mathcal{C}(S)\neq\emptyset
, and every
\hat{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathcal{C}(S)
satisfies

h_{S}(\hat{\mathbf{y}})\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2e}\,\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2^{n}e}\,\mathcal{H}_{d}(S).

(4)

We also show that a linear
\ell_{\infty}
radius in the total dimension
n+d
is necessary if one seeks a dimension-free constant fraction guarantee based only on the existence of a translate of
k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
in the projection.
See Theorem
5.1
and Corollary
3
.

Theorem
2.2
remains valid if the axis aligned cube
k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
is replaced by a unimodular image
kU\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
. This relaxes (
2
) by allowing the cube to be aligned with an arbitrary integer basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Corollary 2

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
be a convex body with
S:=C\cap\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and
K:=\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C)
.
Assume that there exist
\mathbf{z}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
k\geq\frac{3e}{2}(n+d)
, and an integer matrix
U\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
with
|\det U|=1
such that

\mathbf{z}_{0}+k\,U\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}\subseteq K.

(5)

Then there exists
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in S
such that for every closed halfspace
H\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
with
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in H
,

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)\ \geq\ \Bigl(\frac{1}{e}-\frac{3(n+d)}{2k}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}_{d}(S).

3
A comparison lemma for mixed-integer volume

The goal of this section is to prove the following comparison inequality, which relates the mixed-integer volume
\mathcal{H}_{d}(S_{D})
to the Lebesgue volume
\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(D)
when the projection of
D
contains a large
\ell_{\infty}
ball.

Lemma 1
(Comparison inequality)

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
be a convex body and recall
K_{D}:=\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(D)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
S_{D}:=D\cap\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
.
Assume that there exist
\mathbf{z}_{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
k>1/2
such that

\mathbf{z}_{D}+k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}\subseteq K_{D}.

(6)

Then we have

\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(D)\ \leq\ \mathcal{H}_{d}(S_{D})\ \leq\ \Bigl(1+\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(D).

(7)

The key idea is to express both volumes via layer cake decompositions (see, for example,
(
13
, Theorem 8.16)
) over the superlevel sets
L_{s}:=\left\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:g(\mathbf{z})\geq s\right\}
, where
g(\mathbf{z}):=\operatorname{vol}_{d}(D_{\mathbf{z}})^{1/d}
is the “slice radius”:

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S_{D})=d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\#(L_{s}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(D)=d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s.

The second equality is the usual layer cake formula (
9
), and the first identity follows from (
1
) with the same layer cake argument for counting measure (derived in (
26
)).
A lattice sandwich lemma (Lemma
2
) then bounds the lattice point count by the Lebesgue measure of thickened and eroded level sets, where we recall that
Q=[-1/2,1/2]^{n}
:

\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s}\ominus Q)\ \leq\ \#(L_{s}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})\ \leq\ \operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s}+Q).

The main technical ingredient (Lemma
7
) controls the effect of thickening and erosion on these volumes via a Brunn–Minkowski based concavity argument for
g
and a scaling argument:

d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s}+Q)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{n+d}d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s,

with an analogous lower bound for erosion. Combining these ingredients yields the comparison inequality in Lemma
1
.

The remainder of this section develops these tools. Section
3.1
establishes the layer cake representations and the lattice sandwich lemma. Section
3.2
proves the concavity of slice radii via Brunn–Minkowski. Section
3.3
proves the integrated thickening/erosion bounds. Section
3.4
assembles the proof of Lemma
1
.

3.1
Layer cake decomposition

Lemma 2
(Lattice sandwich)

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be closed. Then

\operatorname{vol}_{n}(A\ominus Q)\ \leq\ \#(A\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})\ \leq\ \operatorname{vol}_{n}(A+Q).

(8)

Proof

Since
A
is closed and
Q
is compact, the sets
A\ominus Q
and
A+Q
are closed and hence Borel measurable.

For the left inequality, consider the union

U:=\bigcup_{\mathbf{z}\in A\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(\mathbf{z}+Q).

We claim that
A\ominus Q\subseteq U
. Fix
\zeta\in A\ominus Q
. Then
\zeta+Q\subseteq A
.
Since
Q=[-1/2,1/2]^{n}
, there exists
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
\mathbf{z}\in\zeta+Q
(choose
\mathbf{z}_{i}
by rounding
\zeta_{i}
to the nearest integer point).
As
\zeta+Q\subseteq A
, we obtain
\mathbf{z}\in A\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and also
\zeta\in\mathbf{z}+Q
. This shows
\zeta\in U
.

By subadditivity of Lebesgue measure and the fact that
\operatorname{vol}_{n}(Q)=1
,

\operatorname{vol}_{n}(A\ominus Q)\leq\operatorname{vol}_{n}(U)\leq\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in A\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(\mathbf{z}+Q)=\#(A\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}).

For the right inequality, note that for every
\mathbf{z}\in A\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,

\mathbf{z}+\intset(Q)\subseteq A+Q,

since
\mathbf{z}\in A
and
\intset(Q)\subseteq Q
. The sets
\{\mathbf{z}+\intset(Q):\ \mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}
are pairwise disjoint.
Therefore,

\#(A\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in A\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(\intset(Q))=\operatorname{vol}_{n}\Bigl(\bigcup_{\mathbf{z}\in A\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(\mathbf{z}+\intset(Q))\Bigr)\leq\operatorname{vol}_{n}(A+Q),

which completes the proof.

Lemma
2
is the bridge between lattice point counting and continuous volume. The Minkowski sum
A+Q
and erosion
A\ominus Q
provide upper and lower bounds on the lattice point count in terms of Lebesgue measure. When
A
is sufficiently large compared to
Q
, the difference between
\operatorname{vol}_{n}(A+Q)
and
\operatorname{vol}_{n}(A\ominus Q)
is small relative to
\operatorname{vol}_{n}(A)
, making the bounds tight.
See, for example,
4
.

Lemma 3
(Layer cake)

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
be Lebesgue measurable and let
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(\mathbf{z})^{d}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}\bigl(\left\{\mathbf{z}:\ g(\mathbf{z})\geq s\right\}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(9)

Proof

Fix Lebesgue measurable
g\geq 0
and an integer
d\geq 1
. For any
a\in[0,\infty]
we have

a=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{a\geq t\}}\,\mathrm{d}t
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(\mathbf{z})^{d}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{g(\mathbf{z})^{d}\geq t\}}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbf{1}_{\{g(\mathbf{z})\geq t^{1/d}\}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{vol}_{n}\bigl(\left\{\mathbf{z}:\ g(\mathbf{z})\geq t^{1/d}\right\}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}\bigl(\left\{\mathbf{z}:\ g(\mathbf{z})\geq s\right\}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where the second line uses Tonelli’s theorem and the last uses the substitution
t=s^{d}
.

The power of the layer cake decomposition lies in the following observation. For a nonnegative function
g
on
K
, both the continuous integral
\int_{K}g(\mathbf{z})^{d}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}
and the discrete sum
\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in K\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}g(\mathbf{z})^{d}
admit parallel representations via the superlevel sets
L_{s}:=\{g\geq s\}
. Indeed, Lemma
3
gives

\int_{K}g(\mathbf{z})^{d}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s,

and the same calculation applied to the counting measure yields (as we will derive in (
26
))

\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in K\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}g(\mathbf{z})^{d}=d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\#(L_{s}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})\,\mathrm{d}s.

This structural similarity reduces the comparison of continuous and discrete volumes to a purely geometric problem: relating the lattice point count
\#(L_{s}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})
to the Lebesgue measure
\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s})
for each level set
L_{s}
, via Lemma
2
.

3.2
Concavity of slice radii

For
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define

f(\mathbf{z}):=\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C_{\mathbf{z}}).

(10)

Since
C_{\mathbf{z}}=\emptyset
for
\mathbf{z}\notin K
, we have
f(\mathbf{z})=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus K
.

Lemma 4
(Upper semicontinuity of slice volumes)

The function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
is upper semicontinuous. In particular, for every
s>0
the set

\left\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ f(\mathbf{z})\geq s\right\}

is closed in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and contained in
K
.

Proof

Fix
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\mathbf{z}_{k}\to\mathbf{z}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We show
\limsup_{k\to\infty}f(\mathbf{z}_{k})\leq f(\mathbf{z})
.

If
\mathbf{z}\notin K
, then
\delta:=\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{z},K)>0
since
K
is closed.
For all large
k
,
\|\mathbf{z}_{k}-\mathbf{z}\|_{2}<\delta/2
, hence
\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{z}_{k},K)\geq\delta/2>0
and
\mathbf{z}_{k}\notin K
.
Therefore
f(\mathbf{z}_{k})=0=f(\mathbf{z})
for all large
k
, so the claim follows.

Now assume
\mathbf{z}\in K
. Let
\epsilon>0
and set
U:=C_{\mathbf{z}}+\epsilon\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}<1\right\}
. The set
U
is open in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and contains
C_{\mathbf{z}}
.
We claim that
C_{\mathbf{z}_{k}}\subseteq U
for all large
k
such that
\mathbf{z}_{k}\in K
.
If not, then there exist a subsequence
\mathbf{z}_{k_{j}}
and points
\mathbf{x}_{k_{j}}\in C_{\mathbf{z}_{k_{j}}}
with
\mathbf{x}_{k_{j}}\notin U
.
Since
C
is compact, the sequence
(\mathbf{z}_{k_{j}},\mathbf{x}_{k_{j}})\in C
has a convergent subsequence to some
(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\in C
.
Then
\mathbf{x}\in C_{\mathbf{z}}\subseteq U
, and since
U
is open,
\mathbf{x}_{k_{j}}\in U
for all large
j
, a contradiction.

Therefore, for all large
k
, we have
C_{\mathbf{z}_{k}}\subseteq C_{\mathbf{z}}+\epsilon\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}
(if
\mathbf{z}_{k}\not\in K
, then
C_{\mathbf{z}_{k}}=\emptyset
), and thus

f(\mathbf{z}_{k})=\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C_{\mathbf{z}_{k}})\leq\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C_{\mathbf{z}}+\epsilon\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}).

Taking
\limsup_{k\to\infty}
gives
\limsup_{k\to\infty}f(\mathbf{z}_{k})\leq\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C_{\mathbf{z}}+\epsilon\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d})
.
Now take
\epsilon_{m}:=1/m
and set
E_{m}:=C_{\mathbf{z}}+\epsilon_{m}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}
.
Then
(E_{m})
is a decreasing sequence of closed sets with
\bigcap_{m\geq 1}E_{m}=C_{\mathbf{z}}
.
Since
C
is compact,
E_{1}
is bounded and hence
\operatorname{vol}_{d}(E_{1})<\infty
.
By continuity from above for Lebesgue measure,
\operatorname{vol}_{d}(E_{m})\downarrow\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C_{\mathbf{z}})
.
Therefore,
\limsup_{k\to\infty}f(\mathbf{z}_{k})\leq\inf_{m\geq 1}\operatorname{vol}_{d}(E_{m})=\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C_{\mathbf{z}})=f(\mathbf{z})
.
Since
f(\mathbf{z})=0
for
\mathbf{z}\notin K
, we also have
\left\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ f(\mathbf{z})\geq s\right\}\subseteq K
for every
s>0
.

We will use the Brunn–Minkowski inequality in the following standard form (see, e.g.,
14
).

Theorem 3.1
(Brunn–Minkowski)

Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be Lebesgue measurable sets with finite volume. For every
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

\operatorname{vol}_{d}\bigl((1-\lambda)A+\lambda B\bigr)^{1/d}\geq(1-\lambda)\operatorname{vol}_{d}(A)^{1/d}+\lambda\operatorname{vol}_{d}(B)^{1/d}.

Lemma 5
(Brunn concavity)

Define
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
by

g(\mathbf{z}):=f(\mathbf{z})^{1/d}.

(11)

Then
g
is concave on
K
and upper semicontinuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof

Let
\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{2}\in K
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Put
\mathbf{z}_{\lambda}:=(1-\lambda)\mathbf{z}_{1}+\lambda\mathbf{z}_{2}
.
By convexity of
C
,

(1-\lambda)C_{\mathbf{z}_{1}}+\lambda C_{\mathbf{z}_{2}}\subseteq C_{\mathbf{z}_{\lambda}}.

Applying Theorem
3.1
and taking
d
th roots yields

\displaystyle g(\mathbf{z}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C_{\mathbf{z}_{\lambda}})^{1/d}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\lambda)\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C_{\mathbf{z}_{1}})^{1/d}+\lambda\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C_{\mathbf{z}_{2}})^{1/d}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)g(\mathbf{z}_{1})+\lambda g(\mathbf{z}_{2}),

which is concavity.

Upper semicontinuity follows from Lemma
4
and continuity/monotonicity of
t\mapsto t^{1/d}
on
[0,\infty)
.

Lemma 7
(Integral thickening and erosion)

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
be as defined in (
11
) and let
L_{s}
be as in (
12
).
Assume that there exist
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
k>0
such that

\mathbf{c}+2kQ\subseteq K.

(15)

Then for every integer
d\geq 1
,

d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s}+Q)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{n+d}d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s.

(16)

If in addition
k>1/2
, then

d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s}\ominus Q)\,\mathrm{d}s\geq\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{n+d}d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s.

(17)

Proof

Translate coordinates so that
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{0}
. This does not change the integrals in (
16
) and (
17
).
Thus we assume
2kQ\subseteq K
.

Define the set

E:=\left\{(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \mathbf{z}\in K,\ \|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq g(\mathbf{z})\right\}.

Each
\mathbf{z}
fiber is replaced by a centered Euclidean ball of radius
g(\mathbf{z})
, a fiberwise Schwarz symmetrization
(
10
)
.
The set
E
is convex: if
(\mathbf{z}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{i})\in E
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
, then

\mathbf{z}_{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)\mathbf{z}_{1}+\lambda\mathbf{z}_{2}\in K
by convexity of
K
, and

\|\,(1-\lambda)\mathbf{x}_{1}+\lambda\mathbf{x}_{2}\,\|_{2}\leq(1-\lambda)\|\mathbf{x}_{1}\|_{2}+\lambda\|\mathbf{x}_{2}\|_{2}\leq(1-\lambda)g(\mathbf{z}_{1})+\lambda g(\mathbf{z}_{2})\leq g(\mathbf{z}_{\lambda}),

where the last inequality uses concavity of
g
on
K
(Lemma
5
). Hence
(\mathbf{z}_{\lambda},(1-\lambda)\mathbf{x}_{1}+\lambda\mathbf{x}_{2})\in E
.

Let
v_{d}:=\operatorname{vol}_{d}(\left\{\mathbf{x}:\ \|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq 1\right\})
, the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of the Euclidean unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For each
\mathbf{z}\in K
, the
\mathbf{x}
fiber
\left\{\mathbf{x}:\ \|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq g(\mathbf{z})\right\}
equals the dilation
g(\mathbf{z})\left\{\mathbf{x}:\ \|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq 1\right\}
, so

\operatorname{vol}_{d}(\left\{\mathbf{x}:\ \|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq g(\mathbf{z})\right\})=v_{d}\,g(\mathbf{z})^{d}.

Since
g(\mathbf{z})=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus K
, we have
\int_{K}g(\mathbf{z})^{d}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(\mathbf{z})^{d}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}
.
For each
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
E_{\mathbf{z}}:=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ (\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\in E\right\}
.
Then
E_{\mathbf{z}}=\left\{\mathbf{x}:\ \|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq g(\mathbf{z})\right\}
if
\mathbf{z}\in K
, and
E_{\mathbf{z}}=\emptyset
otherwise.
In particular,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{1}_{E}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=\operatorname{vol}_{d}(E_{\mathbf{z}})
for every
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
By Tonelli’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(E)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+d}}\mathbf{1}_{E}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{1}_{E}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{vol}_{d}(E_{\mathbf{z}})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=\int_{\mathbf{z}\in K}\operatorname{vol}_{d}(\left\{\mathbf{x}:\ \|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq g(\mathbf{z})\right\})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}.

Combining with the identities above yields

\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(E)=v_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(\mathbf{z})^{d}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}.

(18)

Since
g
is upper semicontinuous by Lemma
5
, it is Lebesgue measurable. Applying Lemma
3
to
g^{d}
and using
\left\{g\geq s\right\}=L_{s}
gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(\mathbf{z})^{d}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s.

(19)

Next consider
E^{+}:=E+\left(Q\times\{\mathbf{0}\}\right)
.
For each
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, write
E^{+}_{\mathbf{z}}
for the
\mathbf{z}
-fiber of
E^{+}
(i.e., the
\mathbf{x}
-section at
\mathbf{z}
).
Then
\mathbf{x}\in E^{+}_{\mathbf{z}}
if and only if
(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\in E^{+}
, i.e., there exists
\mathbf{q}\in Q
such that
(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x})\in E
. This is equivalent to saying there exists
\mathbf{q}\in Q
with
\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{q}\in K
and
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq g(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{q})
.
Thus,

E^{+}_{\mathbf{z}}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in Q\\
\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{q}\in K\end{subarray}}g(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{q})\,\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}.

If the index set is empty, then
E^{+}_{\mathbf{z}}
is empty and has Lebesgue measure
0
.
Otherwise, the index set is compact and the map
\mathbf{q}\mapsto g(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{q})
is upper semicontinuous, so it attains its maximum at some
\mathbf{q}_{\mathbf{z}}\in Q
with
\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{q}_{\mathbf{z}}\in K
.
The ball
\left\{\mathbf{x}:\ \|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq g(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{q}_{\mathbf{z}})\right\}
contains all other balls in the union, and hence the union equals this ball.
Its radius satisfies
g(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{q}_{\mathbf{z}})=\max_{\mathbf{q}\in Q}g(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{q})=g_{+}(\mathbf{z})
, so the fiber
E^{+}_{\mathbf{z}}
has
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
v_{d}\,g_{+}(\mathbf{z})^{d}
.
Applying Tonelli’s theorem yields

\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(E^{+})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{vol}_{d}(E^{+}_{\mathbf{z}})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=v_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g_{+}(\mathbf{z})^{d}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}.

By Lemma
6
,
\left\{\mathbf{z}:\ g_{+}(\mathbf{z})\geq s\right\}=L_{s}+Q
for every
s\geq 0
, and
g_{+}
is Lebesgue measurable.
Applying Lemma
3
to
g_{+}^{d}
yields

\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(E^{+})=v_{d}\,d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s}+Q)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(20)

Now consider the erosion
E^{-}:=E\ominus\left(Q\times\{\mathbf{0}\}\right)
.
For each
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, write
E^{-}_{\mathbf{z}}
for the
\mathbf{z}
-fiber of
E^{-}
(i.e., the
\mathbf{x}
-section at
\mathbf{z}

By definition of Minkowski erosion,
\mathbf{x}\in E^{-}_{\mathbf{z}}

\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq g(\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{q})

\mathbf{q}\in Q
.

\displaystyle E^{-}_{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\mathbf{q}\in Q}\left\{\mathbf{x}:\ \|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq g(\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{q})\right\}

Since all balls are centered at the origin, the intersection is again a ball, whose radius is the infimum of the radii.

(of Lemma

is Lebesgue measurable.

By Fubini’s theorem,

\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(D)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{vol}_{d}(D_{\mathbf{z}})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=\int_{K_{D}}f(\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}.

Since
g^{d}=f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we also have
\int_{K_{D}}f=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g^{d}
.
Applying Lemma
3
to
g^{d}
gives

\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(D)=\int_{K_{D}}f(\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(\mathbf{z})^{d}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s.

(24)

On the other hand, since
D
is bounded, only finitely many
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
yield a nonempty fiber
D\cap\left(\{\mathbf{z}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, and thus

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S_{D})=\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\operatorname{vol}_{d}\bigl(D\cap\left(\{\mathbf{z}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\bigr)=\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}g(\mathbf{z})^{d}.

(25)

For each
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we use the elementary identity

g(\mathbf{z})^{d}=d\int_{0}^{g(\mathbf{z})}s^{d-1}\,\mathrm{d}s=d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{g(\mathbf{z})\geq s\}}\,\mathrm{d}s,

where the second equality rewrites the integral over
[0,g(\mathbf{z})]
using an indicator function.
Since the integrand is nonnegative, Tonelli’s theorem allows us to exchange the sum and the integral in (
25
).
Moreover, for each
s>0
we have

\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathbf{1}_{\{g(\mathbf{z})\geq s\}}=\#\left\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:\ g(\mathbf{z})\geq s\right\}=\#(L_{s}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})
.
This quantity is finite since
L_{s}\subseteq K_{D}
for
s>0
and
K_{D}
is compact.
Moreover, the value at
s=0
does not affect the integral.
This yields

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S_{D})=d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathbf{1}_{\{g(\mathbf{z})\geq s\}}\,\mathrm{d}s=d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\#(L_{s}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})\,\mathrm{d}s.

(26)

For each
s>0
, the set
L_{s}
is closed (Lemma
5
), so Lemma
2
applies and yields

\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s}\ominus Q)\leq\#(L_{s}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})\leq\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s}+Q).

Multiplying by
ds^{d-1}
and integrating in
s
gives

d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s}\ominus Q)\,\mathrm{d}s\ \leq\ \mathcal{H}_{d}(S_{D})\ \leq\ d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s}+Q)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(27)

Assumption (
6
) is equivalent to
\mathbf{z}_{D}+2kQ\subseteq K_{D}
.
Thus Lemma
7
applies to
K_{D}
with center
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{z}_{D}
and parameter
k
.
Combining (
27
) with (
16
) and (
24
) gives

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S_{D})\leq\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{n+d}d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s=\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(D).

Similarly, combining (
27
) with (
17
) and (
24
) gives

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S_{D})\geq\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{n+d}d\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n}(L_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s=\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(D).

This proves (
7
).

4
Proof of main results

We now prove Theorem
2.2
and Corollary
1
.
Recall that
S:=C\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We build a mixed-integer point
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in S
by shrinking
C
to a body
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
whose centroid has an integer
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-projection (Lemma
9
), and we set
\mathbf{y}^{\star}:=\mathfrak{c}(C^{\prime})\in S
. Fixing a halfspace
H
with
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in H
, Grünbaum’s inequality (Theorem
2.1
) controls the Lebesgue volumes of the two pieces of
C^{\prime}
cut by
\partial H
. A geometric bisection argument (Lemma
10
) guarantees that one of the two pieces contains a translate of
\tfrac{1}{2}C^{\prime}
, hence still has a sufficiently large projection. We then invoke the comparison lemma (Lemma
1
) to convert these Lebesgue-volume bounds into mixed-integer volume bounds for
S
. A short case analysis handles whether the “thick” piece coincides with
C^{\prime}\cap H
or with its complement, and a Bernoulli-type estimate yields (
3
).

4.1
Auxiliary lemmas

The first lemma allows us to restrict attention to halfspaces whose boundary passes through the candidate centerpoint.

Lemma 8
(Boundary reduction for depth)

For every
\mathbf{y}\in S
,

h_{S}(\mathbf{y})=\inf\left\{\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H):\ H\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf{y}\in\partial H\right\}.

Proof

Fix
\mathbf{y}\in S
, and let
H
be a closed halfspace with
\mathbf{y}\in H
.
Write
H=\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+d}:\ \mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{u}\geq c\right\}
with
\mathbf{a}\neq\mathbf{0}
.
If
\mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}=c
, then
\mathbf{y}\in\partial H
and there is nothing to show.
Otherwise,
\mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}>c
.
Set
c_{0}:=\mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}
and let

H_{0}:=\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+d}:\ \mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{u}\geq c_{0}\right\}.

Then
\mathbf{y}\in\partial H_{0}
and
H_{0}\subseteq H
.
By monotonicity of
\mathcal{H}_{d}
,
\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H_{0})\leq\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)
.
Taking the infimum over all
H
gives the claim.

A natural candidate for the centerpoint
\mathbf{y}^{\star}
is the centroid
\mathfrak{c}(C)
of the convex body
C
, since Grünbaum’s inequality guarantees a halfspace depth of at least
1/e
for the
(n+d)
dimensional Lebesgue measure. However, the centroid may not belong to the mixed-integer lattice
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
because its
\mathbf{z}
component is typically not an integer vector. The following lemma addresses this by shrinking
C
toward a suitably chosen point
\mathbf{w}^{\star}
, producing a smaller body
C^{\prime}
whose centroid has an integer
\mathbf{z}
component while retaining most of the original volume.

Lemma 9
(Centroid rounding)

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
be a convex body and set
K=\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C)
.
Assume that there exist
\mathbf{z}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
k>1/2
such that
\mathbf{z}_{0}+k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}\subseteq K
.
Let
\epsilon:=1/(2k)
.
Then there exists
\mathbf{w}^{\star}\in C
such that the set
C^{\prime}:=(1-\epsilon)C+\epsilon\mathbf{w}^{\star}
satisfies:

1.

C^{\prime}\subseteq C
and
\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime})=(1-\epsilon)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C)
.

2.

\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathfrak{c}(C^{\prime}))\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

3.

\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C^{\prime})
contains a translate of
(k-\tfrac{1}{2})\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
.

Proof

For any
\mathbf{w}\in C
, define

T_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{y}):=(1-\epsilon)\mathbf{y}+\epsilon\mathbf{w}.

For any
\mathbf{y}\in C
, the point
T_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{y})
is a convex combination of
\mathbf{y}
and
\mathbf{w}
. Since
C
is convex and
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{w}\in C
, we have
T_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{y})\in C
.
Therefore,
T_{\mathbf{w}}(C)\subseteq C
.

Let
DT_{\mathbf{w}}
denote the Jacobian matrix of
T_{\mathbf{w}}
. Since
T_{\mathbf{w}}
is affine,
DT_{\mathbf{w}}
is constant and equals its linear part. Thus
DT_{\mathbf{w}}=(1-\epsilon)I
, so
|\det DT_{\mathbf{w}}|=(1-\epsilon)^{n+d}
. Hence

\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(T_{\mathbf{w}}(C))=(1-\epsilon)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C),

proving the first item for
T_{\mathbf{w}}(C)
.

Write
\mathfrak{c}(C)=(\mathbf{z}_{C},\mathbf{x}_{C})
. By the change of variables formula, centroids are affine equivariant, i.e.,

\mathfrak{c}(T(C))=T(\mathfrak{c}(C))
for any affine
T
. In particular,

\mathfrak{c}(T_{\mathbf{w}}(C))=(1-\epsilon)\mathfrak{c}(C)+\epsilon\mathbf{w},\qquad\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathfrak{c}(T_{\mathbf{w}}(C)))=(1-\epsilon)\mathbf{z}_{C}+\epsilon\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbf{w}).

We now choose
\mathbf{w}^{\star}
so that the right hand side in the second equality is an integer vector.

The assumption
\mathbf{z}_{0}+k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}\subseteq K
is equivalent to
\mathbf{z}_{0}+2kQ\subseteq K
.
Multiplying by
\epsilon=1/(2k)
gives
\epsilon\mathbf{z}_{0}+Q\subseteq\epsilon K
, and therefore

(1-\epsilon)\mathbf{z}_{C}+\epsilon\mathbf{z}_{0}+Q\subseteq(1-\epsilon)\mathbf{z}_{C}+\epsilon K.

The left hand side is a translate of
Q
, so it contains an integer vector
\mathbf{z}^{\star}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
By the inclusion above,
\mathbf{z}^{\star}\in(1-\epsilon)\mathbf{z}_{C}+\epsilon K
, and hence there exists
\mathbf{u}\in K
such that

(1-\epsilon)\mathbf{z}_{C}+\epsilon\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{z}^{\star}.

Choose
\mathbf{w}^{\star}\in C
with
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbf{w}^{\star})=\mathbf{u}
(such a
\mathbf{w}^{\star}
exists since
\mathbf{u}\in\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C)=K
).
Now fix this choice and set
C^{\prime}:=T_{\mathbf{w}^{\star}}(C)
.
Then

\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathfrak{c}(C^{\prime}))=\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathfrak{c}(T_{\mathbf{w}^{\star}}(C)))=\mathbf{z}^{\star}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},

proving the second item.

Finally, since
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is linear, we have

\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C^{\prime})=\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(T_{\mathbf{w}^{\star}}(C))=(1-\epsilon)\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C)+\epsilon\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbf{w}^{\star})=(1-\epsilon)K+\epsilon\mathbf{u}.

If
K
contains a translate of
k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
, then
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C^{\prime})
contains a translate of
(1-\epsilon)k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
.
Since
(1-\epsilon)k=k-\tfrac{1}{2}
, this proves the third item.

The next lemma ensures that after cutting a convex body by a hyperplane through a specified point, at least one side remains “geometrically large” in the sense that it contains a half-scale copy of the original body. This is exactly what is needed to apply Lemma
1
to one of the pieces.

Lemma 10
(Halfspace bisection)

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
be a convex body and let
H
and
\bar{H}
be two complementary closed halfspaces sharing the same boundary hyperplane.
Then there exists
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
such that either

\mathbf{t}+\frac{1}{2}D\subseteq D\cap H\qquad\text{or}\qquad\mathbf{t}+\frac{1}{2}D\subseteq D\cap\bar{H}.

Proof

Let
H=\left\{\mathbf{y}:\ \mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}\geq c\right\}
and
\bar{H}=\left\{\mathbf{y}:\ \mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}\leq c\right\}
with
\mathbf{a}\neq\mathbf{0}
.
Choose points
\mathbf{y}^{+},\mathbf{y}^{-}\in D
attaining the maximum and minimum of
\mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}
over
D
.
Define the two translates of
\frac{1}{2}D
by

D^{+}:=\frac{1}{2}D+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{y}^{+},\qquad D^{-}:=\frac{1}{2}D+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{y}^{-}.

Since
D
is convex, for any
\mathbf{y}\in D
we have
\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{y}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{y}^{+}\in D
, hence
D^{+}\subseteq D
.
Similarly,
D^{-}\subseteq D
.

Let
\theta:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}^{+}+\mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}^{-}\right)
.
If
c\leq\theta
, we claim that
D^{+}\subseteq H
. Indeed, for
\mathbf{y}\in D
,

\mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{y}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{y}^{+}\Bigr)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}^{+}\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}^{-}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}^{+}=\theta\geq c,

so
\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{y}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{y}^{+}\in H
and hence
D^{+}\subseteq D\cap H
.

If
c\geq\theta
, a symmetric argument shows
D^{-}\subseteq\bar{H}
, hence
D^{-}\subseteq D\cap\bar{H}
.
In both cases we obtain the desired translate of
\frac{1}{2}D
.

4.2
Proof of Theorem
2.2

We begin by applying Lemma
9
to shrink
C
toward a suitable point
\mathbf{w}^{\star}
, producing a smaller body
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
whose centroid
\mathbf{y}^{\star}:=\mathfrak{c}(C^{\prime})
has an integer
\mathbf{z}
component and therefore lies in
S
. For any closed halfspace
H
containing
\mathbf{y}^{\star}
, Grünbaum’s inequality bounds the
(n+d)
dimensional volumes of
C^{\prime}\cap H
and
C^{\prime}\cap\bar{H}
. Meanwhile, Lemma
10
guarantees that one of these two pieces contains a translate of
\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}
, which in turn contains a sufficiently large box in its projection and hence satisfies the hypotheses of the comparison inequality (Lemma
1
). We then convert between continuous and discrete volumes using Lemma
1
and combine the bounds to obtain (
3
).

Proof
(of Theorem
2.2
)

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
2.2
.
Since
k\geq\frac{3e}{2}(n+d)
, we have
k>1/2
, so Lemmas
1
and
9
apply.

We first construct a point
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in S
with an integer projection.
Let
\epsilon:=1/(2k)
. By Lemma
9
, there exist
\mathbf{w}^{\star}\in C
and a set

C^{\prime}:=(1-\epsilon)C+\epsilon\mathbf{w}^{\star}\subseteq C

such that
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathfrak{c}(C^{\prime}))\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C^{\prime})
contains a translate of

(k-\tfrac{1}{2})\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
.
Define

\mathbf{y}^{\star}:=\mathfrak{c}(C^{\prime}).

Then
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in C^{\prime}\subseteq C
, and its
\mathbf{z}
component is an integer vector, hence
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in\Lambda
.
Therefore,
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in S
.

Let
H
be a closed halfspace with
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in H
.
By Lemma
8
, it suffices to prove (
3
) under the additional assumption
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in\partial H
.
Fix such an
H
and write it as

H=\left\{\mathbf{y}:\ \mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\star})\geq 0\right\}\qquad(\mathbf{a}\neq\mathbf{0}).

Let
\bar{H}=\left\{\mathbf{y}:\ \mathbf{a}^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\star})\leq 0\right\}
be the complementary closed halfspace.

A:=C^{\prime}\cap H,\qquad B:=C^{\prime}\cap\bar{H}.

Since
\mathbf{y}^{\star}=\mathfrak{c}(C^{\prime})\in H\cap\bar{H}
, Grünbaum’s inequality (Theorem
2.1
) gives

\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(A)\geq\frac{1}{e}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime}),\qquad\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(B)\geq\frac{1}{e}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime}).

(28)

Moreover,
C^{\prime}=A\cup B
and
A\cap B=C^{\prime}\cap\partial H
is contained in the boundary hyperplane
\partial H
, which has
(n+d)
dimensional Lebesgue measure
0
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime})=\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(A)+\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(B)
, and we obtain

\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(B)=\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime})-\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(A)\leq\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{e}\Bigr)\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime}).

(29)

Next, Lemma
10
applied to
D=C^{\prime}
and the split
(H,\bar{H})
shows that one of
A
or
B

contains a translate of
\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}
. Since
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C^{\prime})
contains a translate of
(k-\tfrac{1}{2})\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
, this implies that one of
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(A)
or
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(B)
contains a translate of
\frac{k-\tfrac{1}{2}}{2}\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
.
Since
k>3/2
, we have
\frac{k-\tfrac{1}{2}}{2}>1/2
, so Lemma
1
is applicable in either case.

We write
S^{\prime}:=C^{\prime}\cap\Lambda
. Since
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
, we have
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
and hence

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)\geq\mathcal{H}_{d}(S^{\prime}\cap H).

(30)

We now distinguish the two cases, depending on whether the large projection occurs on
A
or on
B
.

Case 1: the large projection occurs on
A
.

Since
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(A)
contains a translate of
\frac{k-\tfrac{1}{2}}{2}\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
, Lemma
1
yields

\mathcal{H}_{d}(A\cap\Lambda)\geq\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{k-\tfrac{1}{2}}\Bigr)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(A)=\Bigl(\frac{2k-3}{2k-1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(A).

Since
S^{\prime}=C^{\prime}\cap\Lambda
and
A=C^{\prime}\cap H
, we have
S^{\prime}\cap H=A\cap\Lambda
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{H}_{d}(S^{\prime}\cap H)=\mathcal{H}_{d}(A\cap\Lambda)
, and hence, using (
28
),

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S^{\prime}\cap H)\geq\frac{1}{e}\Bigl(\frac{2k-3}{2k-1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime}).

(31)

We relate
\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime})
to
\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)
. Applying Lemma
1
to
C
with parameter
k
gives

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)\leq\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C),\qquad\text{so}\qquad\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C)\geq\Bigl(\frac{2k}{2k+1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\mathcal{H}_{d}(S).

Since
\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime})=(1-\frac{1}{2k})^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C)
, we obtain

\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime})\geq\Bigl(\frac{2k-1}{2k}\Bigr)^{n+d}\Bigl(\frac{2k}{2k+1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)=\Bigl(\frac{2k-1}{2k+1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\mathcal{H}_{d}(S).

(32)

Combining (
31
), (
32
), and (
30
) gives

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)\geq\frac{1}{e}\Bigl(\frac{2k-3}{2k+1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\mathcal{H}_{d}(S).

(33)

Case 2: the large projection occurs on
B
.

Since
S^{\prime}=C^{\prime}\cap\Lambda
,
B\cap\Lambda=(C^{\prime}\cap\bar{H})\cap\Lambda=S^{\prime}\cap\bar{H}
, and
H\cup\bar{H}=\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
, we have

S^{\prime}=(S^{\prime}\cap H)\cup(S^{\prime}\cap\bar{H})=(S^{\prime}\cap H)\cup(B\cap\Lambda)
.
By subadditivity of
\mathcal{H}_{d}
,

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S^{\prime}\cap H)\geq\mathcal{H}_{d}(S^{\prime})-\mathcal{H}_{d}(B\cap\Lambda).

(34)

We now bound the two terms on the right hand side.
First, since
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C^{\prime})
contains a translate of
(k-\tfrac{1}{2})\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
, Lemma
1
yields

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S^{\prime})\geq\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2k-1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime})=\Bigl(\frac{2k-2}{2k-1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime}).

(35)

Second, since
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(B)
contains a translate of
\frac{k-\tfrac{1}{2}}{2}\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
, Lemma
1
yields

\mathcal{H}_{d}(B\cap\Lambda)\leq\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{k-\tfrac{1}{2}}\Bigr)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(B)=\Bigl(\frac{2k+1}{2k-1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(B).

Using (
29
), we obtain

\mathcal{H}_{d}(B\cap\Lambda)\leq\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{e}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{2k+1}{2k-1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime}).

(36)

Substituting (
35
) and (
36
) into (
34
) gives

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S^{\prime}\cap H)\geq\left[\Bigl(\frac{2k-2}{2k-1}\Bigr)^{n+d}-\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{e}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{2k+1}{2k-1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\right]\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C^{\prime}).

Using (
32
) and
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
yields

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)\geq\left[\Bigl(\frac{2k-2}{2k+1}\Bigr)^{n+d}-\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{e}\Bigr)\right]\mathcal{H}_{d}(S).

(37)

A uniform bound.

From (
33
) and (
37
), for every closed halfspace
H
with
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in\partial H
,

\frac{\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)}{\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)}\geq\min\left\{\frac{1}{e}\Bigl(\frac{2k-3}{2k+1}\Bigr)^{n+d},\ \Bigl(\frac{2k-2}{2k+1}\Bigr)^{n+d}-\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{e}\Bigr)\right\}.

We bound both terms from below using Bernoulli’s inequality
(1-t)^{n+d}\geq 1-(n+d)t
for
t\in[0,1]
.

For the first term,

\Bigl(\frac{2k-3}{2k+1}\Bigr)^{n+d}=\Bigl(1-\frac{4}{2k+1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\geq 1-\frac{4(n+d)}{2k+1}\geq 1-\frac{2(n+d)}{k},

and hence

\frac{1}{e}\Bigl(\frac{2k-3}{2k+1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\geq\frac{1}{e}-\frac{2(n+d)}{ek}\geq\frac{1}{e}-\frac{3(n+d)}{2k},

where the last inequality uses
\frac{2}{e}\leq\frac{3}{2}
.

For the second term,

\Bigl(\frac{2k-2}{2k+1}\Bigr)^{n+d}=\Bigl(1-\frac{3}{2k+1}\Bigr)^{n+d}\geq 1-\frac{3(n+d)}{2k+1}\geq 1-\frac{3(n+d)}{2k},

and therefore

\Bigl(\frac{2k-2}{2k+1}\Bigr)^{n+d}-\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{e}\Bigr)\geq\frac{1}{e}-\frac{3(n+d)}{2k}.

Combining the two bounds shows that for every closed halfspace
H
with
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in\partial H
,

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)\geq\Bigl(\frac{1}{e}-\frac{3(n+d)}{2k}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}_{d}(S).

By Lemma
8
, the same inequality holds for every closed halfspace
H
with
\mathbf{y}^{\star}\in H
.
This proves (
3
) and completes the proof of Theorem
2.2
.

5
Sharpness of the linear threshold

This section shows that the linear scaling in Theorem
2.2
cannot, in general, be improved if one only assumes that
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C)
contains a translate of
k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
.
We construct a family of convex bodies whose mixed-integer volume is concentrated on
2^{n}
“central” integer fibers, while for every point in
S
one can choose a halfspace containing it that intersects this central family in at most one fiber.
Choosing the continuous dimension
d
large relative to
kn
makes the remaining fibers negligible, leading to an exponentially small depth ratio of order
\exp(-\Theta((n+d)/k))
. Thus, if
k
is sublinear in
n+d
, then we will have exponentially small halfspace depth for any point in
S
.

Theorem 5.1
(Necessity of linear
\ell_{\infty}
radius)

For any constant
\alpha>\log 2
, there exist constants
\gamma_{\alpha},M_{\alpha}>0
(depending only on
\alpha

For every integer
k\geq 2
and every integer
m\geq 2(1+\alpha k)
, there exist integers
n,d\geq 1
with
n+d=m
and a convex body

C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}

K:=\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C)
contains a translate of
k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
and, writing
S:=C\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, one has the quantitative upper bound

\sup_{\mathbf{y}\in S}\frac{h_{S}(\mathbf{y})}{\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)}\ \leq\ M_{\alpha}\exp\!\Bigl(-\gamma_{\alpha}\,\frac{m}{k}\Bigr).

Proof

Fix
\alpha>\log 2
, an integer
k\geq 2
, and an integer
m\geq 2(1+\alpha k)
.
Define

n:=\Bigl\lfloor\frac{m}{1+\alpha k}\Bigr\rfloor,\qquad d:=m-n.

Then
n\geq 2
and
d\geq 1
.
In fact,
(1+\alpha k)n\leq m
implies
d=m-n\geq\alpha kn
.

Define the center
\mathbf{c}:=(k+\tfrac{1}{2})(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the box

K:=\mathbf{c}+k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}=[\tfrac{1}{2},\,2k+\tfrac{1}{2}]^{n}.

Define the function
t:K\to[0,1]
by

t(\mathbf{z}):=1-\frac{1}{k}\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{c}\|_{\infty}.

Finally, define the convex body
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
by

C:=\left\{(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \mathbf{z}\in K,\ \|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq t(\mathbf{z})\right\}.

Since
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is convex, the map
\mathbf{z}\mapsto-\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{c}\|_{\infty}
is concave, hence
t
is concave.
Thus
C
is convex: if
(\mathbf{z}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{i})\in C
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
, then
\mathbf{z}_{\lambda}:=(1-\lambda)\mathbf{z}_{1}+\lambda\mathbf{z}_{2}\in K
and

\|\,(1-\lambda)\mathbf{x}_{1}+\lambda\mathbf{x}_{2}\,\|_{2}\leq(1-\lambda)\|\mathbf{x}_{1}\|_{2}+\lambda\|\mathbf{x}_{2}\|_{2}\leq(1-\lambda)t(\mathbf{z}_{1})+\lambda t(\mathbf{z}_{2})\leq t(\mathbf{z}_{\lambda}),

so
\bigl(\mathbf{z}_{\lambda},(1-\lambda)\mathbf{x}_{1}+\lambda\mathbf{x}_{2}\bigr)\in C
.
Moreover, since
K
is compact and
t
is continuous with
0\leq t\leq 1
, the set
C
is compact, and since
t(\mathbf{c})=1
and
\mathbf{c}\in\intset(K)
, the set
C
has nonempty interior.
Clearly
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C)=K
, so
K
contains a translate of
k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
.

Let
S:=C\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Write
v_{d}:=\operatorname{vol}_{d}(\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq 1\right\})
.
For each
\mathbf{z}\in K
, the fiber
C_{\mathbf{z}}:=\left\{\mathbf{x}:(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\in C\right\}
is a Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of radius
t(\mathbf{z})
, hence
\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C_{\mathbf{z}})=v_{d}t(\mathbf{z})^{d}
.
Since
K\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\left\{1,2,\dots,2k\right\}^{n}
, the mixed-integer volume is

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)=\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in K\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C_{\mathbf{z}})=v_{d}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in K\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}t(\mathbf{z})^{d}.

Set

V_{0}:=\left\{k,k+1\right\}^{n}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n},\qquad\#V_{0}=2^{n},\qquad T:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{z}\in K\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
\mathbf{z}\notin V_{0}\end{subarray}}t(\mathbf{z})^{d}.

For
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, each coordinate
z_{i}-(k+\tfrac{1}{2})
is an integer plus
\tfrac{1}{2}
, so
\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{c}\|_{\infty}\in\{\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{2},\tfrac{5}{2},\dots\}
.
The points of
K\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{c}\|_{\infty}=\tfrac{1}{2}
are exactly
V_{0}
, and for all
\mathbf{z}\in V_{0}
one has
t(\mathbf{z})=1-\frac{1}{2k}
.
Therefore,

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)=v_{d}\left(2^{n}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{d}+T\right).

(39)

Fix
\mathbf{y}=(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\in S
. We construct a closed halfspace
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
with
\mathbf{y}\in H
such that
H
intersects
V_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
in at most one integer fiber.
If
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{v}\in V_{0}
, define
\sigma(\mathbf{v})\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
by
\sigma(\mathbf{v})_{i}:=2(\mathbf{v}_{i}-k)-1
for
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and let

H:=H_{\mathbf{v}}:=\left\{(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+d}:\ \sigma(\mathbf{v})^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{z}\geq\sigma(\mathbf{v})^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{v}\right\}.

Then
\mathbf{y}\in H
. Since
\sigma(\mathbf{v})_{i}=+1
when
\mathbf{v}_{i}=k+1
and
\sigma(\mathbf{v})_{i}=-1
when
\mathbf{v}_{i}=k
, the point
\mathbf{v}
is the unique maximizer of
\mathbf{z}\mapsto\sigma(\mathbf{v})^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{z}
over
V_{0}
. Hence
H
intersects
V_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
in exactly one fiber and

\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)\leq v_{d}\left(\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{d}+T\right).

If instead
\mathbf{z}\notin V_{0}
, then some coordinate
\mathbf{z}_{i}\notin\{k,k+1\}
, so
\mathbf{z}_{i}\leq k-1
or
\mathbf{z}_{i}\geq k+2
.
In the first case take
H:=\left\{(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+d}:\ \mathbf{z}_{i}\leq k-1\right\}
and in the second take
H:=\left\{(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+d}:\ \mathbf{z}_{i}\geq k+2\right\}
.
In either case
\mathbf{y}\in H
and
H\cap(V_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})=\emptyset
, hence
\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)\leq v_{d}T
.
Since
h_{S}(\mathbf{y})\leq\mathcal{H}_{d}(S\cap H)
by definition, it follows that for all
\mathbf{y}\in S
,

h_{S}(\mathbf{y})\ \leq\ v_{d}\left(\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{d}+T\right).

(40)

Combining (
39
) and (
40
) yields

\sup_{\mathbf{y}\in S}\frac{h_{S}(\mathbf{y})}{\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)}\leq\frac{\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{d}+T}{2^{n}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{d}+T}\leq 2^{-n}+\frac{T}{2^{n}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2k}\Bigr)^{d}}.

To bound
\frac{T}{2^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{2k}\right)^{d}}
, for
j\in\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}
define the shells

V_{j}:=\Bigl\{\mathbf{z}\in K\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}:\ \|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{c}\|_{\infty}=\frac{2j+1}{2}\Bigr\}.

Then
(K\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})\setminus V_{0}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k-1}V_{j}
and for
\mathbf{z}\in V_{j}
one has

t(\mathbf{z})=1-\frac{2j+1}{2k}
, hence

\frac{t(\mathbf{z})}{1-\frac{1}{2k}}=\frac{1-\frac{2j+1}{2k}}{1-\frac{1}{2k}}=\frac{2k-2j-1}{2k-1}=1-\frac{2j}{2k-1}.

Moreover,
V_{j}
is contained in the cube
\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:\ \|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{c}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{2j+1}{2}\}
, whose cardinality is at most
(2j+2)^{n}
.
Therefore,
\#V_{j}/2^{n}\leq(j+1)^{n}
.
Using
1-x\leq e^{-x}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
d\geq\alpha kn
, we obtain

\Bigl(\frac{t(\mathbf{z})}{1-\frac{1}{2k}}\Bigr)^{d}\leq\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{2j}{2k-1}\,d\Bigr)\leq\exp(-\alpha jn),\qquad\mathbf{z}\in V_{j}.

Therefore,

\frac{T}{2^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{2k}\right)^{d}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{\#V_{j}}{2^{n}}\left(1-\frac{2j}{2k-1}\right)^{d}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(j+1)^{n}e^{-\alpha jn}.

Since
j+1\leq 2^{j}
for all
j\geq 1
, the summand is at most
2^{jn}e^{-\alpha jn}=e^{-(\alpha-\log 2)jn}
.
Thus

\frac{T}{2^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{2k}\right)^{d}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-(\alpha-\log 2)jn}=\frac{e^{-(\alpha-\log 2)n}}{1-e^{-(\alpha-\log 2)n}}\leq\frac{e^{-(\alpha-\log 2)n}}{1-e^{-(\alpha-\log 2)}}.

(41)

It follows that

\sup_{\mathbf{y}\in S}\frac{h_{S}(\mathbf{y})}{\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)}\leq 2^{-n}+\frac{e^{-(\alpha-\log 2)n}}{1-e^{-(\alpha-\log 2)}}.

(42)

To rewrite (
42
) in terms of
m
and
k
, note that
m\geq 2(1+\alpha k)
implies

n=\Bigl\lfloor\frac{m}{1+\alpha k}\Bigr\rfloor\geq\frac{m}{1+\alpha k}-1\geq\frac{m}{2(1+\alpha k)}.

Set
M_{\alpha}:=1+\frac{1}{1-e^{-(\alpha-\log 2)}}
.
Then (
42
) gives

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{y}\in S}\frac{h_{S}(\mathbf{y})}{\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)}

\displaystyle\leq M_{\alpha}\exp\!\bigl(-\min\{\log 2,\ \alpha-\log 2\}\,n\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq M_{\alpha}\exp\!\Bigl(-\min\{\log 2,\ \alpha-\log 2\}\,\frac{m}{2(1+\alpha k)}\Bigr).

Since
1+\alpha k\leq(\alpha+1)k
for
k\geq 1
, the right hand side is at most

M_{\alpha}\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\min\{\log 2,\ \alpha-\log 2\}}{2(\alpha+1)}\,\frac{m}{k}\Bigr).

This proves (
38
) with

\gamma_{\alpha}:=\frac{\min\{\log 2,\ \alpha-\log 2\}}{2(\alpha+1)},\qquad M_{\alpha}:=1+\frac{1}{1-e^{-(\alpha-\log 2)}}.

Corollary 3

Let
\eta\in(0,1)
and let
\kappa:\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\to(0,\infty)
satisfy
\kappa(m)=o(m)
.
Then there exists an integer
m_{0}\geq 1
such that for every integer
m\geq m_{0}
there exist integers
n,d\geq 1
with
n+d=m
and a convex body
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
such that, writing
S:=C\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the projection
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C)
contains a translate of
\kappa(m)\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
but

\sup_{\mathbf{y}\in S}h_{S}(\mathbf{y})\ \leq\ \eta\,\mathcal{H}_{d}(S).

Equivalently, there is no sublinear function
\kappa(m)=o(m)
that can serve as a threshold based only on the dimension that guarantees a dimension-free constant fraction lower bound for mixed-integer halfspace depth.

Proof

Choose any
\alpha>\log 2
and let
\gamma_{\alpha},M_{\alpha}
be the constants from Theorem
5.1
.
Since
\kappa(m)=o(m)
, we have
m/\kappa(m)\to\infty
.
Thus, there exists an integer
m_{0}\geq 1
such that for every integer
m\geq m_{0}
, the integer
k:=\max\{2,\lceil\kappa(m)\rceil\}
satisfies
m\geq 2(1+\alpha k)
and
M_{\alpha}\exp(-\gamma_{\alpha}m/k)\leq\eta
.
Apply Theorem
5.1
with this
(m,k)
to obtain integers
n,d\geq 1
with
n+d=m
and a convex body
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
such that
\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C)
contains a translate of
k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
.
Since
\kappa(m)\leq k
, the projection also contains a translate of
\kappa(m)\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
.
Finally, (
38
) implies
\sup_{\mathbf{y}\in S}h_{S}(\mathbf{y})/\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)\leq\eta
.

Together with Theorem
2.2
, this shows that the dependence on the total dimension in the box-radius assumption cannot be reduced to
o(n+d)
under this type of hypothesis.

6
Conclusion

The main contribution of this paper is a linear threshold for Oertel’s conjecture on mixed-integer halfspace depth. Under the assumption that the projection
K=\proj_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(C)
contains a translate of
k\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
with
k\geq 3e(n+d)
, we have shown the existence of a point in
S=C\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
achieving depth at least
\frac{1}{2e}\mathcal{H}_{d}(S)
, which implies the conjectured
\frac{1}{2^{n}e}
bound. Our condition scales linearly in the total dimension and linearly in
d
, improving upon prior thresholds that depend exponentially on
n
or polynomially as
d^{2}n^{3/2}
. We have also shown that a linear threshold cannot be relaxed: no sublinear radius
\kappa(m)=o(m)
can guarantee a dimension independent constant fraction lower bound.

The most natural direction is to investigate whether Conjecture
1
holds for every convex body
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
without any largeness assumption on the projection. The authors do not see an obvious approach to this question and it seems to require fundamentally new ideas. There also seems to be scope for improving the quantitative bounds: Theorem
2.2
guarantees depth
\frac{1}{e}-\Theta((n+d)/k)
for large
k
, while Theorem
5.1
constructs sets with depth
\exp(-\Theta(m/k))
. Closing this gap and determining the exact asymptotic dependence on
(n,d,k)
remain open.

Finally, the point
\mathbf{y}^{\star}
in Theorem
2.2
arises from centroid rounding and its efficient computation is unclear. Can one approximate such a point from standard oracles for
C
? More broadly, can one design a polynomial time cutting plane procedure that provably removes a constant fraction of mixed-integer volume per iteration? Progress on these questions would have direct implications for the complexity theory of mixed-integer convex optimization.
