DREAMS. JWST Spectroscopy of a z = 8.3 z=8.3 Galaxy with an ALMA Dust Continuum Detection: Early Dust, Very High T dust T_{\rm dust} , and a Multi-wavelength [O III] Ratio Discrepancy Facilities: JWST , ALMA, HST
Kana Takechi, Masami Ouchi, Kimihiko Nakajima, Tomokazu Kiyota, Yoichi Tamura, Yuichi Harikane, Yurina Nakazato, Tom J. L. C. Bakx, Akio K. Inoue, Hidenobu Yajima, Masato Hagimoto, Yoshiaki Ono, Yi Xu

Abstract
We present a deep DREAMS JWST /NIRSpec MSA medium-grating spectrum of MACS0416-Y1, a galaxy at z = 8.312 z=8.312 with the highest-redshift ALMA dust continuum detection to date, in order to characterize its properties together with archival IFU and ALMA data. The deep NIRSpec spectrum reveals a broad H β \beta line with a width of ∼ 1100 \sim 1100 km s -1 . We interpret it as a broad-line AGN whose line diagnostics are consistent with AGN activity across its clumpy structure, given the absence of little red dot signatures. MACS0416-Y1 clearly shows [ Oiii ] 4363 emission, suggesting a moderately low metallicity of 12 + log ⁡ ( O / H ) = 7.86 − 0.08 + 0.09 12+\log(\mathrm{O/H})=7.86^{+0.09}_{-0.08} ( 0.15 ​ Z ⊙ 0.15~Z_{\odot} ). The combination of [ Cii ] 158 μ \mu m and dust continuum emission indicates low dust mass ratios of log ⁡ ( M dust / M gas ) = − 3.60 − 0.22 + 0.29 \log(M_{\rm dust}/M_{\rm gas})=-3.60^{+0.29}_{-0.22} and log ⁡ ( M dust / M metal ) = − 0.95 − 0.20 + 0.29 \log(M_{\rm dust}/M_{\rm metal})=-0.95^{+0.29}_{-0.20} . Because the metallicity of MACS0416-Y1 is around the critical metallicity of 0.1 ​ – ​ 0.2 ​ Z ⊙ 0.1\textrm{--}0.2~Z_{\odot} , the system is expected to undergo dust growth, explaining these low dust mass ratios as well as its small dust mass, M dust ∼ 10 6 ​ M ⊙ M_{\rm dust}\sim 10^{6}~M_{\odot} . The intense UV radiation from the AGN may contribute to a high dust temperature of T dust ≃ 91 − 35 + 62 T_{\rm dust}\simeq 91^{+62}_{-35} K, boosting the dust-continuum emission above the ALMA detection limit despite the small M dust M_{\rm dust} at z > 8 z>8 . We find a very high total flux ratio of [ Oiii ] 88 μ \mu m/[ Oiii ] 5007 = 0.26 ± 0.06 0.26\pm 0.06 in MACS0416-Y1, above predictions from single ionized nebular models at any electron density. This discrepancy suggests that the [ Oiii ] 88 μ \mu m and [ Oiii ] 5007 trace largely distinct regions, with the optical line suppressed in dusty nebulae, and thus requires careful interpretation when combining optical and infrared emission lines in JWST+ALMA studies.

I
Introduction

Dust is a fundamental component of the interstellar medium (ISM) in galaxies, influencing the observational properties, energy budget, and physical conditions. In the local
Universe
, dust heated by interstellar radiation fields typically reaches equilibrium temperatures of
\sim
20–40 K
(
Dunne et al. 2000
;
Casey 2012
)
. However, recent far-infrared (FIR) and submillimeter observations have revealed that dust in high-redshift galaxies, particularly at
z>6
, exhibits unexpectedly high temperatures of 50–100 K,
likely due to intense radiation fields associated with compact star formation and/or conditions that enhance dust heating, including low dust mass or active galactic nucleus (AGN) activity

(
Nakazato et al. 2026
;
Viero et al. 2022
;
Sommovigo et al. 2022
;
Mitsuhashi et al. 2024
;
Tsukui et al. 2023
)
.

Understanding the origin of these high dust temperatures and the physical conditions of the ISM requires combining dust continuum measurements with constraints on the gas properties.

A multi-wavelength approach that combines FIR/submillimeter continuum observations with near-infrared spectroscopy is therefore essential for characterizing dust and ISM properties in early galaxies. FIR and submillimeter observations probe the emission from dust and
FIR
fine-structure emission lines, while near-infrared spectroscopy provides emission line diagnostics that trace gas properties including electron temperature, density, ionization state, and chemical composition
(
Osterbrock & Ferland 2006
;
Draine 2011
)
.
The combination of these datasets enables a more comprehensive understanding of the physical conditions in high-
z
galaxies. For example, measurements of the gas-phase metallicity
can
provide constraints on grain growth processes in the early universe
(
Asano et al. 2013
;
Popping et al. 2017
;
Toyouchi et al. 2025
,
e.g.,
)
.

MACS J0416-Y1 (hereafter Y1) is a star-forming galaxy at
z=8.31
that exhibits an elevated dust temperature and a complex ISM structure
characterized by three emission peaks in the rest-frame UV and two emission peaks in the rest-frame FIR continuum
. It was originally identified as a
z>8
Lyman break galaxy candidate based on multiband
Hubble Space Telescope
Wide Field Camera 3 (
HST
/WFC3) observations
(
Infante et al. 2015
;
Laporte et al. 2015
)
in the Hubble Frontier Fields
(
Lotz et al. 2017
, HFF,)
survey of the MACS J-0416.1-2403 cluster. Follow up observations
with

the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)
spectroscopically confirmed its redshift,
z=8.312
, through detections of the [
Oiii
] 88
\mu
m
(
Tamura et al. 2019
)
and [
Cii
] 158
\mu
m
(
Bakx et al. 2020
)
emission lines.
Y1 exhibits a high [
Oiii
] 88
\mu
m/[
Cii
] 158
\mu
m luminosity ratio of
\sim 9

(
Bakx et al. 2020
)
.
Hagimoto et al. 2025
suggested that this can be explained by a porous ISM geometry with a low neutral-gas covering fraction.

Dust continuum emission was first reported in ALMA Band 7
(
Tamura et al. 2019
)
, representing the highest-redshift dust continuum detection to date.
Dust continuum emission has subsequently been detected across multiple ALMA bands
(
Tamura et al. 2019
;
Harshan et al. 2024
;
Bakx et al. 2025
)
. By combining these detections with upper limits from nondetections
(
Bakx et al. 2020
;
Jones et al. 2024
;
Bakx et al. 2025
)
, the dust temperature has been constrained to 91
{}^{+62}_{-35}
K
(
Bakx et al. 2025
)
, significantly
warmer
than typical values observed in local galaxies
(
Schreiber et al. 2018
,
\sim 25
K; e.g.,)
. The origin of the high dust temperature and the detection of dust continuum at such a high redshift remain under debate.
Tamura et al. 2019
suggested that the observed dust mass requires an earlier episode of star formation to pre-enrich the system, rather than production in a single young burst.
Bakx et al. 2020
argue that the high inferred dust temperature itself reduces the required dust mass, alleviating the tension with rapid dust production.

More recently, observations with the James Webb Space Telescope/Near Infrared Spectrograph (
JWST
/NIRSpec) and
JWST
/Near Infrared Camera (NIRCam) have revealed the clumpy morphology, ionized gas properties, and stellar populations
(
Ma et al. 2024
;
Harshan et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.

Harshan et al. 2024
proposed that merger-driven star formation and/or a possible AGN contribution inferred from emission line diagnostics could account for the strong dust heating.

Despite these advances, several key questions remain unresolved, including the origin of the dust heating, the mechanism responsible for the dust enrichment, and the connection between the rest-frame UV/optical-emitting regions observed with
JWST
and the rest-frame FIR-emitting regions observed with ALMA. Addressing these questions requires a multiwavelength analysis of the ionized gas, cold gas, and dust components using both
JWST
and ALMA observations. In this study, we present new deep
JWST
/NIRSpec Micro-Shutter Assembly (MSA) spectroscopy of Y1, combined with archival NIRSpec Integral Field Unit (IFU) and ALMA data, to investigate its ISM properties and the mechanism of dust production and heating.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section
II
describes the observations and datasets used in this study. Section
III
outlines the data reduction and analysis methods. In Section
IV
, we present the main results, including emission-line properties and ISM diagnostics. Section
V
discusses the implications for dust heating, production, and ISM structure. Finally, Section
VI
summarizes our conclusions.

Throughout this paper, we assume a flat
\Lambda
CDM cosmology with
H_{0}=67.66
km s
-1
Mpc
-1
,
\Omega_{\rm m}=0.3111
, and
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.6889

(
Planck Collaboration et al. 2020
)
.

III.1
Data Reduction

III.1.1
JWST
/NIRSpec MSA

We reduced the data using the

III.1.2
JWST
/NIRSpec IFU

We reduced the raw data retrieved from the Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST) portal using the
JWST
Science Calibration Pipeline with the CRDS context file
jwst_1364.pmap

Early Release Science (ERS)
Targeting Extremely Magnified Panchromatic Lensed Arcs and their Extended Star formation
(

team through stage 1 and stage 2, and replace stage 3 with custom processing using the

and
reproject_interp

calwebb_detector1

\farcs
05. Finally, we construct the median-stacked 3D data cube using the
reproject
and
reproject_interp

III.3
Line Flux and Profile Measurements

We fit the emission lines in the NIRSpec/MSA and IFU spectra using a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method with
emcee

(
Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013
)
.
Flux uncertainties correspond to the 16th and 84th percentiles of the posterior distributions.
The flux-density uncertainties were estimated as
1\sigma
of the flux density in nearby line-free spectral windows within the same extraction region.

The emission-line FWHMs
were
constrained to be larger than the FWHM of the instrumental line spread function (LSF) measured from planetary nebula spectra in
Isobe et al. 2023
.

The intrinsic emission-line velocity widths presented in this paper were derived by deconvolving a Gaussian profile whose FWHM matches that of the LSF measured by
Isobe et al. 2023
.

Y1 has been observed with ALMA Bands 3–9, with continuum emission detection in Bands 5–9
(
Tamura et al. 2019
;
Tamura et al. 2023
;
Bakx et al. 2020
;
Bakx et al. 2025
;

;

.
Bakx et al. 2025
report dust temperature and dust mass of 91
{}^{+62}_{-35}
K and 1.4
{}^{+1.3}_{-0.5}\times 10^{6}~M_{\odot}
, respectively, based on SED fitting of these observations.
This temperature is higher than typical values inferred for galaxies at
z\gtrsim 7

(
Sommovigo et al. 2022
;
Mitsuhashi et al. 2024
, 40–60 K;)
.

The origin of such elevated dust temperatures remains under debate.
A redshift evolution of dust temperature has been reported, with
T_{\rm dust}
increasing toward higher redshift
(
Béthermin et al. 2015
;
Sommovigo et al. 2022
;
Mitsuhashi et al. 2024
)
.
Extrapolation of empirical relations derived at
z\lesssim 7
predicts dust temperatures of
\sim 40
–
60~\mathrm{K}
at
z\sim 8.3
.

This trend has been interpreted as a consequence of the lower metallicity and dust mass at high-
z
, which reduce dust shielding and enable more efficient absorption of UV radiation
(
Sommovigo et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
. These effects are expected to be relevant for Y1, which exhibits both low metallicity and low dust mass.

Theoretical studies have shown that high dust temperatures can be achieved in compact star-forming regions
(
Nakazato et al. 2026
, e.g.,)
.

Kano et al. 2026
suggest that the high dust temperature in Y1 may be explained by an extremely dense cold neutral medium (
\sim 10^{3-4}
cm
-3
), which enhances UV photon trapping and increases the efficiency of dust heating.

Although current observations do not indicate an elevated star-formation rate surface density for Y1 on global scales
(
Harshan et al. 2024
)
, compact star-forming regions on unresolved scales may still contribute to the dust heating.

On the other hand, we detect a broad H
\beta
line (Section
IV.2.1
), indicating the presence of an AGN. The spatial coincidence between the broad-line emission and one of the dust continuum peaks suggests that AGN radiation may contribute locally to dust heating.

Kirkpatrick et al. 2012
show that AGN-host galaxies exhibit higher dust temperatures than star-forming galaxies based on stacked SED analyses.
They interpret this as evidence that UV–optical radiation from the AGN is absorbed by surrounding dust and re-emitted in the infrared, producing a warm dust component in addition to that expected from star formation alone.
Figure
14
illustrates this trend, where AGN-host galaxies (orange) are offset toward higher dust temperatures by
\sim 20
K compared to star-forming galaxies (blue).
Spatially resolved observations
(
Tsukui et al. 2023
;
Villanueva et al. 2024
;
Meyer et al. 2025
;
Fernández Aranda et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
and theoretical studies
(
Schneider et al. 2015
, e.g., radiative transfer modeling of a quasar at
z\sim 6
;)
further suggest that AGN can heat dust and contribute to the infrared emission on sub-galactic scales.
In particular, the
z\sim 6.9
quasar host galaxy J2348
-
3054 exhibits a dust temperature of
88\pm 2
K within the central
r<216
pc region
(
Fernández Aranda et al. 2025
)
. A similar AGN-driven heating mechanism may contribute to the elevated dust temperature observed in Y1.

We therefore interpret the high dust temperature of Y1 as the result of multiple contributing factors, including redshift-dependent ISM conditions (e.g., low metallicity and low dust mass), compact star formation, and AGN heating
.

Next, we consider the dust production mechanisms.

Dust production mechanisms are broadly categorized into stellar sources (e.g., supernovae and AGB stars) and grain growth in the ISM through accretion of gas-phase metals onto pre-existing dust grains
(
Dwek 1998
;
Asano et al. 2013
)
.

Chemical evolution models suggest that stellar sources
can dominate dust production in low-metallicity galaxies, while grain growth in the ISM becomes dominant as galaxies evolve and enrich metals
(
Mancini et al. 2015
)
.

Previous studies have shown that the efficiency of ISM grain growth is dependent on metallicity
(
Asano et al. 2013
;
Burgarella et al. 2025
)
.
In particular, grain growth is expected to become the dominant source of dust production above a critical metallicity,
Z_{\rm crit}

(
Asano et al. 2013
;
Burgarella et al. 2025
)
.
Although
Z_{\rm crit}
depends on model assumptions and physical conditions, such as the star formation timescale and the dust grain size distribution, it is typically estimated to be in the range of
\sim 0.1
–
0.2~Z_{\odot}

(
Asano et al. 2013
;
Burgarella et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.
Figure
15
shows the relation between
M_{\rm dust}
and gas-phase metallicity, where the range of
Z_{\rm crit}
is indicated by
the gray
shaded region.
Y1 has a metallicity of
Z\sim 0.15~Z_{\odot}
(red diamond), placing it around the critical metallicity and in the low-metallicity regime along the sequence traced by the Cosmic Evolution Early Release Science Survey
(
Burgarella et al. 2025
, CEERS;)
, the ALMA survey of LABOCA Extended Chandra Deep Field South Survey sources
(
da Cunha et al. 2015
, ALESS;
z\sim 1
–6;)
, and the Reionization Era Bright Emission Line Survey
(
Algera et al. 2025
, REBELS;
z\sim
7;)

This suggests that grain growth in the ISM is becoming efficient in Y1.

To further investigate the dust content, we examine the dust-to-gas and dust-to-metal ratios as a function of metallicity, following
Kiyota et al. 2025
(see also
Palla et al. 2024

Algera et al. 2025

(3)

(
Zanella et al. 2018

. Adopting a conversion factor
M_{\rm gas}=\xi(M_{\rm HI}+M_{\rm H_{2}})
with a metallicity-dependent factor of

M_{\rm HI}\ll M_{\rm H_{2}}

M_{\mathrm{gas}}=5.56_{-0.61}^{+0.61}\times 10^{9}~M_{\odot}

The total metal mass is then estimated as

Here,

From these values, we obtain

We note that [

VI
Summary

We have presented a comprehensive multi-wavelength analysis of MACS0416-Y1, a galaxy at
z=8.312
with the highest-redshift ALMA dust continuum detection to date. By combining deep
JWST
/NIRSpec MSA spectroscopy with archival
JWST
/
NIRSpec
IFU and ALMA data, we investigate the AGN activity, dust properties, and ISM conditions of Y1. Our main findings are summarized as follows.

1.

The deep NIRSpec spectrum reveals a broad H
\beta
component with a velocity width of
\sim 1100
km s
-1
, indicating the presence of a broad-line AGN. In addition, all clumps lie in the AGN regime of the MEx diagram, consistent with high ionization conditions across the clumps, potentially associated with AGN activity. We also perform a Little Red Dot test using the NIRCam photometry and find that none of the individual clumps or the integrated photometry of Y1 satisfy the LRD selection criteria.

2.

Using the direct method, we derive a gas-phase metallicity of
12+\log({\rm O/H})=7.86^{+0.09}_{-0.08}
(
\sim 0.15~Z\odot
). Strong-line metallicity estimate based on the
R3
index is consistent with this result. The metallicity places Y1 near the critical metallicity (
\sim 0.1
–
0.2~Z_{\odot}
), where dust grain growth in the ISM is becoming efficient.

3.

Combining the metallicity, ALMA dust continuum measurements, and the gas mass inferred from [
Cii
], we derive low dust mass ratios of
\log(M_{\rm dust}/M_{\rm gas})=-3.60^{+0.29}_{-0.22}
and
\log(M_{\rm dust}/M_{\rm metal})=-0.95^{+0.29}_{-0.20}
. These values indicate that Y1 is relatively dust-poor despite its dust continuum detection, consistent with an early stage of dust enrichment where grain growth is becoming efficient.

4.

The high dust temperature of
T_{\rm dust}\simeq 91^{+62}_{-35}
K may be driven by multiple factors, including low metallicity and dust mass, compact star formation, and AGN heating. Previous studies have shown that AGN can produce elevated dust temperature. The spatial coincidence between the broad line emission and one of the dust continuum peaks is consistent with such AGN-driven dust heating in Y1, suggesting that AGN may contribute to the high dust temperature in Y1.

5.

We find a high integrated line ratio of [
Oiii
] 88
\mu
m/[
Oiii
]
5007=0.26\pm 0.06
, which cannot be reproduced by a single homogeneous ionized nebula model for any electron density.
Explaining this ratio requires dust attenuation, whereas the Balmer decrement indicates little or no attenuation in the observed optical emission. We propose that dust attenuation causes the observed optical and FIR lines to preferentially trace different regions within the galaxy, with observed optical emission dominated by low-extinction regions and FIR emission tracing more obscured regions. This implies that joint analysis of
JWST
optical spectroscopy and ALMA FIR line observations should be treated with caution, particularly when assuming single-zone ISM.
