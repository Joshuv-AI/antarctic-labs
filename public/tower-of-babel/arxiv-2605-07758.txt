SMT-Based Active Learning of Weighted Automata
Tiago Ferreira (✉), Kevin Batz, Alexandra Silva

Abstract
We present an SMT-based active learning algorithm for nondeterministic weighted automata (WFAs) as a practical and robust alternative to Hankel/ 𝖫 ⋆ \mathsf{L}^{\star} -style methods. Our algorithm is parametric in a given semiring and, if it terminates, guaranteed to produce minimal WFAs. We prove partial correctness and provide a sufficient termination condition, which in particular implies termination for all finite semirings. Our extensive experimental evaluation shows that our algorithm is capable of learning numerous minimal WFAs over both finite and infinite semirings, vastly outperforms a naive baseline, and is competitive with a state-of-the-art algorithm while producing significantly smaller automata and requiring less interaction with the teacher.

Introduction

Active automata learning, pioneered by Angluin’s
\mathsf{L}^{\star}
algorithm
[
4
]
, has become widely popular in formal verification
[
34
]
.
\mathsf{L}^{\star}
-style algorithms are routinely used to infer models of unknown components from queries, spanning applications in networking
[
21
,
20
]
, software verification
[
12
]
, and real-world systems such as passports
[
2
,
25
]
or bank cards
[
1
]
. A key appeal of these algorithms is their tight integration with verification backends: counterexamples produced by model checkers naturally serve as equivalence-query answers
[
30
]
, while the learned automata act as compact, explainable abstractions of system behavior. Most of the work currently used in verification is based on Boolean automata: from the simpler deterministic finite-state model Angluin pioneered
[
4
]
to Mealy automata (input-output)
[
36
]
to automata with finite memory (with restricted access)
[
17
]
.

Weighted finite automata (WFAs) provide a uniform and expressive formalism for modeling and verifying quantitative system properties such as cost, probability, and resource consumption, making them a central abstraction in quantitative verification. They shift from Boolean to quantitative transitions, allowing these to carry weights from a semiring. This allows WFAs to model important quantities, and then serve as a basis to answer a range of important questions in quantitative verification and performance analysis:
What is the minimal cost of reaching an error state?
,
Is the probability of failure below a threshold?
,
Does every execution respect a given resource bound?
. The question whether
\mathsf{L}^{\star}
-style algorithms can be extended to WFA was a natural one to tackle and the last decade saw a range of theoretical results, mostly showing termination conditions for different classes of semirings: starting from fields
[
7
]
to principal ideal domains
[
35
]
or number rings
[
5
]
. Maybe not surprisingly, learning WFAs turned out to be substantially more challenging than learning deterministic finite automata. Classical Hankel- and table-based generalizations of
\mathsf{L}^{\star}
must reason about algebraic structure in the weight domain (the semiring), and proving termination turned out to be very subtle over many natural semirings, often relying on indirect linear-algebraic arguments. In practice, these challenges limit both the applicability and the scalability of existing WFA learning algorithms.

This paper takes a different, algorithmically-driven approach to learning weighted automata. Rather than refining a Hankel matrix or observation table, we cast learning directly as a constraint-solving problem. Building on a coalgebraic view of WFAs, we derive a system of equations over the given semiring whose solutions correspond exactly to nondeterministic WFAs that agree with the teacher on all observed queries. Learning then amounts to incrementally strengthening this equation system in response to counterexamples and invoking an SMT solver to search for a solution. This perspective yields an algorithm that is parametric in the semiring, naturally supports nondeterminism, and — when it terminates — produces minimal weighted automata by construction.

A central advantage of this formulation is its compatibility with modern SMT technology. We show that the resulting equation systems fall into decidable arithmetic theories for several practically relevant semirings, including all finite semirings and infinite ones such as the tropical semiring. We further identify sufficient conditions guaranteeing termination, which in particular ensure termination for all finite semirings. An extensive experimental evaluation demonstrates that our SMT-based learner can infer numerous minimal WFAs over both finite and infinite semirings, outperforming a naive baseline and competing favorably with an existing algorithm while producing significantly smaller automata and requiring fewer interactions with the teacher. Taken together, our results position constraint-based learning as a practical and robust alternative to Hankel/
\mathsf{L}^{\star}
-style methods for weighted automata in verification-oriented settings.

from the variables in
\Phi
to values in
\mathbb{S}
, representing a satisfying

\Phi

Every finite semiring is trivially decidable by brute-forcing all possible solutions to
\Phi
. Interestingly, various

We will provide two distinct ways of obtaining the following result:

Theorem 4.1

For every decidable semiring, there is a sound and complete implementation of
\mathsf{getAutomaton}
.

For the semirings considered in this paper, we employ SMT solving over decidable theories to find solutions to said systems of equations, or for proving their absence. The respective encodings are presented in
Section

.

4.1

4.2
An Optimized Implementation via State Languages

The central idea for our main approach is to exploit a coalgebraic perspective on the semantics of WFAs
[
10
]
which enables a compositional view of the language of an automaton as a linear combination of languages of its individual states. Formally, for
\mathcal{A}=\langle Q,\iota,\delta,\lambda\rangle
, we define
\llbracket\mathcal{A},q\rrbracket
, the
state language
of
q\in Q
as

\llbracket\mathcal{A},q\rrbracket(\varepsilon)\triangleq\lambda(q)~\text{and}~\llbracket\mathcal{A},q\rrbracket(aw)\triangleq\delta^{a}_{q,q_{1}}\odot\llbracket\mathcal{A},q_{1}\rrbracket(w)\oplus\ldots\oplus\delta^{a}_{q,q_{n}}\odot\llbracket\mathcal{A},q_{n}\rrbracket(w)~.

This yields the following relationship with the language
\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket
of
\mathcal{A}
:

Theorem 4.2

Let
\mathcal{A}=\langle Q,\iota,\delta,\lambda\rangle
be a WFA with
Q=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\}
. Then:

\forall w\in\Sigma^{\star}\colon\quad\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket(w)=\iota(q_{1})\odot\llbracket\mathcal{A},q_{1}\rrbracket(w)\oplus\ldots\oplus\iota(q_{n})\odot\llbracket\mathcal{A},q_{n}\rrbracket(w)~.

State languages are naturally captured recursively, i.e., if
w=\varepsilon
, then
\llbracket\mathcal{A},q\rrbracket(\varepsilon)
is
q
’s output weight
\lambda(q)
since there are no symbols to be consumed. Otherwise, i.e., if
w=aw^{\prime}
, then

\llbracket\mathcal{A},q\rrbracket(aw^{\prime})
is obtained from
“consuming”

a
, and proceeding by running
\mathcal{A}
on
w^{\prime}
from the successors of
q
. Given the state languages
\llbracket\mathcal{A},q\rrbracket(w)
at every state
q\in Q
, we obtain the weight
\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket(w)
by taking the initial weights given by
\iota
into account.

5
Deciding Equation Systems via SMT Solving

Next, we discuss how to solve the equation systems, as defined in the beginning of
Section

4
, for the semirings considered in this paper. We do this by reducing the problem of computing
\mathsf{SAT}\left(\Phi\right)
to satisfiability problems over decidable theories, which enables using SMT solvers. Every system
\Phi
of equations over the Boolean semiring directly corresponds to a propositional satisfiability problem. The encoding is thus straightforward. Let us consider the remaining semirings:

5.1
The Tropical Semiring

Solving equation systems
\Phi
over the tropical semiring

\mathbb{T}~{}\triangleq{}~\langle\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\},\min,+,\infty,0\rangle

boils down to a satisfiability problem over the decidable theory QF-LIA of quantifier-free linear integer arithmetic. Variables are encoded from
\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
into
\{-1,0,1,2,\dots\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, where
-1
is used as a special value representing
\infty
.
The semiring operations are then encoded as:

\displaystyle t\oplus t^{\prime}

\displaystyle~{}\triangleq{}~\mathbf{if}\;\underbrace{t={-1}\vee t^{\prime}={-1}}_{\text{$t$ or $t^{\prime}$ equals $\infty$}}\;\mathbf{then}\;\underbrace{\mathit{max}(t,t^{\prime})}_{{}\text{$t$ if $t^{\prime}=\infty$ else $t^{\prime}$}}\;\mathbf{else}\;\underbrace{\mathit{min}(t,t^{\prime})}_{\text{usual minimum in $\mathbb{N}$}}

\displaystyle t\odot t^{\prime}

\displaystyle~{}\triangleq{}~\mathbf{if}\;\underbrace{t={-1}\vee t^{\prime}={-1}}_{\text{$t$ or $t^{\prime}$ equals $\infty$}}\;\mathbf{then}\;\underbrace{{-1}}_{t+t^{\prime}=\infty}\;\mathbf{else}\;\underbrace{(t+t^{\prime})}_{\text{usual sum in $\mathbb{N}$}}~,

where both
\mathit{max}
and
\mathit{min}
are encoded via if-then-else in the obvious way.

5.2
The Bounded Tropical Semirings

Let
b\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the
b
-bounded tropical semiring

\mathbb{T}_{b}~{}\triangleq{}~\langle\{0,\ldots,b\}\cup\{\infty\},\min,+_{b},\infty,0\rangle~.

We consider two encodings for solving systems
\Phi
of equations over that semiring: A QF-LIA encoding similar to the previous section and an encoding
QF-BV
into the quantifier-free theory of fixed-size bit vectors. Both encode the domain
\{0,\ldots,b\}\cup\{\infty\}
into the finite set
\{0,\ldots,b,b+1\}
, where
b+1
represents
\infty
.

The QF-LIA Encoding.

Since
b+1
represents
\infty
, we straightforwardly let

\displaystyle t\oplus t^{\prime}~{}\triangleq{}~\mathit{min}(t,t^{\prime})\qquad\quad t\odot t^{\prime}~{}\triangleq{}~\mathit{min}((t+t^{\prime}),b+1)~.

The
QF-BV
Encoding.

We map the integer domain
\{0,\dots,b,b+1\}
into its (unsigned) binary number representation in
fixed-size bit vectors
of appropriate size
l
, and use fixed-sized bit vector addition and if-then-else to define

\displaystyle t\oplus t^{\prime}~{}\triangleq{}~\mathit{min}(t,t^{\prime})\qquad\quad t\odot t^{\prime}~{}\triangleq{}~\mathit{min}((t+t^{\prime}),b+1)

analogously to the QF-LIA encoding. We must, however, take care: While just
\lceil\mathit{log}_{2}(b+2)\rceil
bits are sufficient to represent
\{0,\dots,b,b+1\}
,
intermediate
results from the non-truncated addition
t+t^{\prime}
might overflow. The biggest value we must account for is thus
(b+1)+(b+1)=2(b+1)
, therefore needing to represent
2(b+1)+1=2b+3
values, and so
l=\lceil\mathit{log}_{2}(2b+3)\rceil
bits are sufficient to represent all values while avoiding overflows.

5.3
The Bottleneck Semiring

Consider the
bottleneck
semiring

\mathbb{Bot}~{}\triangleq{}~\langle\mathbb{N}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\},\max,\min,-\infty,\infty\rangle.

We encode the carrier set of
\mathbb{N}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
into
\{-2,-1,0,1,2,\dots\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, using
-2
to represent
-\infty
and
-1
for
\infty
. The operations are then encoded in a similar fashion as the tropical operations, accounting for the special infinity values, and minimizing/maximizing otherwise:

\displaystyle t\oplus t^{\prime}~{}\triangleq{}~

\displaystyle\mathbf{if}\;\underbrace{t={-1}\vee t^{\prime}={-1}}_{\text{$t$ or $t^{\prime}$ equals $\infty$}}\;

\displaystyle\mathbf{then}\;\underbrace{-1}_{\text{$\max(t,t^{\prime})=\infty$}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{else}\;\mathbf{if}\;\underbrace{t={-2}}_{\text{$t$ equals $-\infty$}}\;

\displaystyle\mathbf{then}\;\underbrace{t^{\prime}}_{\text{$\max(-\infty,t^{\prime})=t^{\prime}$}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{else}\;\mathbf{if}\;\underbrace{t^{\prime}={-2}}_{\text{$t^{\prime}$ equals $-\infty$}}\;

\displaystyle\mathbf{then}\;\underbrace{t}_{\text{$\max(t,-\infty)=t$}}\;

\displaystyle\mathbf{else}\;\underbrace{\max(t,t^{\prime})}_{\text{$\max$ in $\mathbb{N}$}}

\displaystyle t\odot t^{\prime}~{}\triangleq{}~

\displaystyle\mathbf{if}\;\underbrace{t={-2}\vee t^{\prime}={-2}}_{\text{$t$ or $t^{\prime}$ equals $-\infty$}}\;

\displaystyle\mathbf{then}\;\underbrace{-2}_{\text{$\min(t,t^{\prime})=-\infty$}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{else}\;\mathbf{if}\;\underbrace{t={-1}}_{\text{$t$ equals $\infty$}}\;

\displaystyle\mathbf{then}\;\underbrace{t^{\prime}}_{\text{$\min(\infty,t^{\prime})=t^{\prime}$}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{else}\;\mathbf{if}\;\underbrace{t^{\prime}={-1}}_{\text{$t^{\prime}$ equals $\infty$}}\;

\displaystyle\mathbf{then}\;\underbrace{t}_{\text{$\min(t,\infty)=t$}}\;

\displaystyle\mathbf{else}\;\underbrace{\min(t,t^{\prime})}_{\text{$\min$ in $\mathbb{N}$}}

perform compared to the
\mathsf{WL}^{\star}
algorithm?

RQ1
forms an
ablation study
aiming at determining the impact of incremental SMT solving and alternative encodings.
RQ2
enables evaluating whether
\mathsf{SWAL}
is indeed superior to the naive baseline
\mathsf{Naive}
. In
RQ3
,
\mathsf{WL}^{\star}
refers to a state-of-the-art WFA learning algorithm
[
35
]
for finite semirings, or principal ideal domains. For the semirings considered in this paper,
\mathsf{WL}^{\star}
thus produces (not necessarily minimal) WFAs over the Boolean and bounded Tropical semirings.

Benchmarks.

Answering the above-mentioned research questions requires a diverse set of benchmarks, covering a range of different target WFAs for all considered semirings.
Classically, one would rely on existing datasets of realistic targets
[
27
]
for automata learning evaluations.
The line of work in weighted automata learning has not yet contributed such a dataset.
Therefore, we have devised over 5000 randomly generated benchmarks.

Each run was allocated a memory limit of 8GB, and a time limit of 2 hours. Below we describe the range of parameters considered for these experiments. For all semirings considered in this paper, we generate target automata with state sizes ranging from 1 to 12, and alphabet sizes ranging from 2 to 50 symbols.

For
\mathbb{B}
-WFAs, we use a well-studied family of minimal NFAs
[
16
, Proposition 6.2]
, which is parametric in the number of states and the alphabet size. This yields our scalable set of target WFAs over the Boolean semiring.

For
\mathbb{T}
-WFAs (both unbounded and bounded) and
\mathbb{Bot}
-WFAs, we generated the target WFAs randomly.
Generating random
minimal
automata is, however, famously hard
[
32
]
, with heuristics only known for DFAs and NFAs over the Boolean semiring. Exact algorithms would rely on generating random automata, and then minimizing them, which is known to be undecidable for
\mathbb{T}
-WFAs. Therefore, we adapt a heuristic from
[
32
]
: For fixed number of states and alphabet size, we generate transitions in such a way that, for each state
q
and symbol
a
, the
expected transition density
— the number of outgoing transitions from
q
via
a
with non-
\mathbb{0}
weight — is
1.25
. For the numeric semirings, these non-
\mathbb{0}
weights are sampled uniformly at random from
\{0,\ldots,\min(1000,b)\}
, where
b
is either the bound for the bounded Tropical semiring or
\infty
for the infinite semirings.

7
Related Work

Active automata learning traces its roots back to the late 1980s with Angluin’s seminal
\mathsf{L}^{\star}
algorithm
[
4
]
. While
\mathsf{L}^{\star}
was tailored for learning deterministic finite automata (DFAs), it is the foundation of a long line of adaptations for learning other types of automata, including weighted automata. Bergadano et al.
[
7
]
presented an
\mathsf{L}^{\star}
-based algorithm for learning (minimal) weighted automata over fields such as the rationals. Bollig et al. designed the
\mathsf{NL}^{\star}
algorithm
[
9
]
to learn nondeterministic finite automata (NFAs), which correspond to weighted automata over the Boolean semiring, however the class of learnable automata was restricted (so-called residual automata) and not necessarily minimal. This procedure was generalized by van Heerdt et al. with the
\mathsf{WL}^{\star}
algorithm
[
35
]
, which was proved to terminate for all principal ideal domains (yet no minimality guarantee). Weighted automata learning has generated interest in recent years, with new proofs of termination for the
\mathsf{WL}^{\star}
algorithm for integer-valued automata over the rationals
[
11
]
, automata over number fields with almost minimality
[
5
]
, and the subclass of deterministic weighted automata over semifields
[
29
]
. Daviaud et al.
[
14
]
also provided a more formal language overview of the general feasibility of
\mathsf{L}^{\star}
-style learning of weighted automata, however learnability of semirings such as the naturals or tropical semirings remains unknown. In the aforementioned works, only the algorithms from
[
7
,
11
,
5
]
have minimality arguments. The focus is often on using the algebraic properties of the semiring to characterize termination (ignoring minimality) of
\mathsf{WL}^{\star}
by restricting the semiring or the class of weighted languages the WFA can recognize. Our algorithm instead always guarantees minimality, though this comes at the cost of not having an easy way to explore the same algebraic properties for provable guarantees on termination.

Though we are the first to use SMT for active learning of weighted automata, there is previous work on using satisfiability solvers for inference of deterministic finite automata from finite positive and negative examples (in a passive learning setting)
[
28
,
8
,
24
]
and in active learning in the context of an incomplete teacher
[
26
]
. Oliveira and Silva
[
28
]
, extending the approach in
[
8
]
, use constraint solvers to find a mapping from the states of a prefix-tree automaton (PTA, essentially a partial DFA) to an automaton of a fixed size. If the search fails, the target size is
increased until an automaton is found. The work in
[
24
]
takes inspiration from graph-coloring to encode a SAT problem to directly infer DFAs from examples. In the active setting, Tappler and collaborators use SMT encodings to learn timed automata
[
33
]
, and Moeller and others
[
26
]
formulate a SAT encoding to infer missing entries in an observation table for
\mathsf{L}^{\star}
– the motivation being that the teacher might be imperfect or incomplete and not provide answers to all membership queries. All these works are restricted to deterministic automata over the Booleans, whereas our work provides an algorithm in a richer setting of weighted automata over semirings requiring more general solvers.

8
Conclusion and Future Work

We have presented a novel SMT-based active automata learning algorithm for WFAs as a practical alternative to Hankel/
\mathsf{L}^{\star}
-style methods. The evaluation of our implementation shows promise: It learns numerous minimal WFAs over both finite and infinite semirings, many of which are out of scope for existing
\mathsf{L}^{\star}
-style algorithms, and is competitive with a state-of-the-art algorithm while producing significantly smaller automata and requiring less interaction with the teacher.

As future work, we plan to investigate a more incremental version of our algorithm, where new hypothesis automata are more strongly related to previous ones. Similarly to
\mathsf{L}^{\star}
-based characterizations of termination, this might yield (i) performance increases and (ii) generalizations of
Theorem

3.2
for termination guarantees for a broader class of semirings. Moreover, we plan to extend our constraint-based approach for learning a broader class of automata including, e.g., probabilistic automata. The existence of active learning for probabilistic languages is a longstanding open question that is closely related to convex optimization problems and it would be interesting to investigate if an SMT approach would offer interesting trade-offs when compared to other approaches. Finally, we plan to apply our technique to the quantitative verification of networks by learning models expressed in Weighted NetKAT
[
31
]
.
