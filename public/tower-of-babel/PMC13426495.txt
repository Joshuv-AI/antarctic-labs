Epidural Analgesia Duration and Postpartum Blood Pressure Trajectory in Hypertensive Pregnancies: A Time-to-Event Analysis
Authors: Jiteng Hu, Yongle Li, Yuxuan Deng, Jiao Duan, Jianxiong Huang, Junjian Wen, Zurong Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionPostpartum hypertension (PPHTN) drives severe maternal morbidity in women with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP). The uncharacterized effect of labor epidural analgesia (LEA) on postpartum blood pressure trajectories may explain its protective association with severe maternal morbidity. We aim to investigate whether LEA exposure is associated with a delayed onset of PPHTN during hospitalization in women with HDP.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective cohort study of women with HDP from 2018 to 2023. Participants were categorized based on LEA duration, with non-recipients assigned a duration of zero. An optimal LEA duration cut-off was identified using the maximally selected log-rank statistic to facilitate group comparison. The primary outcome was in-hospital PPHTN, defined as ≥2 postpartum blood pressure measurement meeting standard hypertensive criteria. Time-to-event analyses were performed using Kaplan-Meier estimates and the Log rank test. Multivariable Cox regression with DAG-guided covariate selection was used to adjust for potential confounders. Secondary outcomes included postpartum antihypertensive treatment patterns, specifically combination therapy use and daily labetalol dose. Three prespecified sensitivity analyses assessed the robustness of results.ResultsOf 1,069 women included in the final analysis, 823 (77.0%) received LEA. Longer LEA duration was significantly associated with a reduced hazard of PPHTN (HR = 0.96 per hour; 95% CI: 0.93–0.98; P = 0.002). The optimal duration cut-off for discriminating PPHTN risk was 3.5 hours. Women with LEA duration >3.5 hours had lower in-hospital PPHTN incidence, a longer median time to onset, and reduced postpartum antihypertensive requirements, including lower rates of combination therapy and labetalol dosing, while nifedipine use was similar between groups. After multivariable adjustment, LEA >3.5 hours was independently associated with a reduced risk of PPHTN (HR = 0.72; 95% CI: 0.58–0.88; P = 0.001). Sensitivity analyses yielded consistent results in women not requiring postpartum antihypertensive therapy and across gestational hypertension and pre-eclampsia subgroups. In the cause-specific Cox model, LEA >3.5 hours was associated with a lower hazard of PPHTN, consistent with the primary model. Among women receiving postpartum antihypertensives, LEA duration was not significantly associated with PPHTN risk but was linked to lower labetalol doses.ConclusionLonger LEA duration was associated with a reduced risk of postpartum hypertension and lower antihypertensive use in women with HDP. These observational findings indicate that prolonged analgesia may be associated with improved postpartum hemodynamic stability, suggesting consideration for its continuation in eligible patients; prospective studies are needed to confirm causality and guide clinical practice.

Introduction

Although delivery is the definitive treatment for hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), blood pressure recovery often extends into the postpartum period, with a median time to normalization of up to 6 weeks.1 This prolonged hemodynamic transition places a substantial number of women at risk for postpartum hypertension (PPHTN) during hospitalization.2,3 Available evidence indicates that most PPHTN cases are diagnosed before discharge, coinciding with the peak incidence of specific severe maternal morbidity (SMM) events such as eclampsia and stroke, which occur most frequently between 2 and 5 days postpartum.4 Given that uncontrolled PPHTN is a well-established, modifiable risk factor for these complications, interventions that promote hemodynamic stability during this vulnerable window may reduce the risk of SMM.5,6

Recent evidence suggests that labor epidural analgesia (LEA) is associated with a 35% lower risk of SMM, with a stronger association observed among women with medical comorbidities such as pre-eclampsia (PE).7 While the underlying mechanisms are not fully understood, the prevailing hypothesis attributes this protective effect to attenuation of labor-related physiological stress.8,9 However, this may not be the sole mechanism involved. LEA induces segmental sympathetic blockade, reducing systemic vascular resistance and improving hemodynamic stability during labor.10 Notably, with prolonged administration, cumulative local anesthetic concentrations in the epidural space may sustain pharmacological effects beyond delivery.11 This persistent sympathetic modulation during the intrapartum-to-postpartum transition could attenuate catecholamine-driven hemodynamic lability, thereby delaying the onset of postpartum hypertension in women with HDP. We therefore hypothesized that longer LEA delays the onset of PPHTN in parturient with HDP, potentially reducing its incidence during hospitalization. This mechanism may contribute to the observed reduction in SMM associated with LEA in high-risk populations.

Methods

Design and Ethics Statement

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at Guangdong Women and Children’s Hospital, one of the largest specialized obstetrics and gynaecology hospitals in southern China. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Guangdong Women and Children’s Hospital (Approval No. 202301320). The Institutional Review Board waived the requirement for informed consent due to the retrospective nature of the study and the use of anonymized data collected during routine clinical care. All procedures were carried out in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and applicable institutional guidelines.

Participants

All adult participants who had a singleton vaginal delivery at ≥28 weeks of gestation and were diagnosed with gestational hypertension (GH) or PE between January 2018 and September 2023 were identified through retrospective review of medical records.

Exclusion criteria included discharge diagnoses of chronic hypertension, heart failure, arrhythmia, severe pre-eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, placenta previa, placental abruption, postpartum hemorrhage (blood loss greater than 500 mL within the first 24 hours postpartum); postpartum severe maternal complications, such as the development of eclampsia, stroke, or other conditions necessitating transfer to the intensive care unit; clinical factors such as prolonged prenatal hospitalization (>7 days) or short postnatal hospitalization (≤1 day); and insufficient blood pressure data, defined as absence of measurements during pregnancy or postpartum, or fewer than six postpartum readings.

Data Collection

Maternal demographic and clinical data were extracted from electronic medical records via the clinical database system. All personal identifiers were anonymized, and participant confidentiality was maintained throughout the study. Original data of this study are available at Mendeley Data (doi: 10.17632/jjgzhnmpxp.1). Variables were selected based on established associations with PPHTN,12–15 and included maternal age, body mass index (BMI), parity, gestational age at delivery, presence of diabetes mellitus, type of HDP, oxytocin dose, use of antihypertensive medications, intravenous fluid administration. The study period was divided into two phases: antepartum (from admission to labor onset) and postpartum (from delivery to discharge). Blood pressure measurements and antihypertensive administrations were assigned to the respective phase by timing. Advanced maternal age was defined as ≥35 years, null parity as no prior live birth, preterm birth as <37 weeks gestation, and obesity as BMI ≥30.0 kg/m2. Antihypertensive medication was categorized as none, single-agent, or combination therapy based on prescriptions.

Labor Epidural Analgesia

A standardized LEA protocol was implemented and offered to parturient who elected to receive it. LEA was initiated at approximately 3 cm cervical dilation, with catheter placement performed at the L2–L3 or L3–L4 interspace. Following administration of an initial bolus of 8–10 mL ropivacaine (1 mg/mL) with sufentanil (0.5 μg/mL), analgesia was maintained by continuous epidural infusion at 8–10 mL/h using an electronic patient-controlled analgesia pump, programmed to deliver a demand dose of 5 mL with a 20-minute lockout interval.

Analgesic efficacy was evaluated 30 minutes after the bolus. Successful analgesia was defined as a visual analog scale score <3 during uterine contractions and a sensory block level at or below T8. In the event of inadequate pain relief, a rescue bolus of 5–10 mL of the initial anesthetic solution was administered. If analgesia remained insufficient after the rescue dose, an additional 5 mL of 2% lidocaine was given.

LEA was discontinued after delivery. The duration of LEA was defined as the time from the administration of the initial epidural bolus to delivery. For parturient who did not receive LEA, the LEA duration was recorded as zero. All parturient received standardized postpartum analgesia immediately after delivery, with acetaminophen or diclofenac sodium administered regardless of LEA exposure.

Postpartum Hypertension

The primary outcome was the incidence of PPHTN, defined as at least two documented blood pressure measurement ≥140 mmHg systolic or ≥90 mmHg diastolic during the postpartum hospitalization.16 The onset of PPHTN was defined as the time interval from delivery to the first blood pressure reading meeting hypertensive criteria.

All HDP patients follow a standardized monitoring protocol (every 4–6 hours). Management of PPHTN in this study basically followed the Chinese Expert Consensus on Blood Pressure Management During Pregnancy, which aligns closely with international guidelines and expert recommendations.17 Labetalol was used as the first-line antihypertensive agent. If blood pressure remained uncontrolled, nifedipine or nicardipine was added as second-line therapy. Women with postpartum blood pressure ≥140/90 mmHg continued antihypertensive treatment, with dosages gradually tapered and ultimately discontinued based on blood pressure trends and clinical stability.

Sample Size Calculation

We hypothesized that a longer duration of LEA is associated with a protective effect against the development of PPHTN during hospitalization. Retrospective data indicated that the incidence of PPHTN within three days was approximately 45% among hypertensive HDP parturient who did not receive LEA2. We hypothesized that prolonged LEA exposure would reduce the absolute incidence of PPHTN by 10 percentage points and be associated with a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.7 for time to PPHTN onset during hospitalization. Given that women with longer LEA duration are more likely to be multiparous, and multiparty is estimated to account for approximately 30% of the overall population, a power analysis was conducted using PASS 15.0 for a Cox proportional hazards model (two-sided α = 0.05, 90% power). A total of 840 subjects would provide sufficient power to detect a significant effect, with an estimated 345 PPHTN events required.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are summarized as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range [IQR]), depending on distribution, as assessed by the Shapiro–Wilk test. Categorical variables are expressed as n (%). A univariate Cox proportional hazards model was used to evaluate the association between LEA duration and the risk of developing PPHTN during hospitalization. The optimal cut-off value for LEA duration was determined using the maximally selected log-rank statistic to facilitate group-based analysis.

Group comparisons were performed using the Mann–Whitney U or χ2-test, with Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons. Survival analysis was conducted using Kaplan–Meier curves and the Log rank test to compare time to PPHTN onset. The time-to-event was defined as the interval from delivery to either diagnosis of PPHTN or censoring. Individuals who did not develop PPHTN were censored at the time of their last recorded postpartum blood pressure measurement. Covariates included in the multivariable Cox proportional hazards model were selected based on directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) to minimize over adjustment and appropriately control for confounding (Figure S1). The proportional hazards assumption was evaluated for each covariate using both Schoenfeld residual tests and visual inspection of scaled residuals plots. Results are presented as HRs with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

Three sets of sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the robustness of the primary findings. First, to assess the potential influence of postpartum antihypertensive treatment, we conducted stratified analyses by receipt of antihypertensive medication after delivery. Second, subgroup analyses were conducted according to HDP subtype to assess the consistency and potential heterogeneity of the association between LEA duration and PPHTN. Third, given that 95% of women were discharged within 5 days postpartum and early discharge may limit blood pressure monitoring, we performed a competing risks analysis with early discharge treated as a competing event for the occurrence of PPHTN within 5 days. In this analysis, PPHTN and early discharge were considered mutually exclusive time-to-event outcomes. Cause-specific HRs with 95% CIs were estimated using cause-specific Cox proportional hazards models.

Statistical analyses were performed using Jamovi (version 2.7.6). A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Characteristics

Of 2,059 women screened, 1,069 were included in the final analysis (Figure 1). The incidence of PPHTN during hospitalization was 47.8%, with a median time to onset of 50.4 hours (95% CI: 42.9–57.6). Among these women, 823 (77.0%) received LEA, and LEA duration was significantly associated with a lower hazard of PPHTN (HR= 0.96, 95% CI: 0.93–0.98, P=0.002). PPHTN During Hospitalization

Women with LEA duration >3.5 hours had a significantly lower incidence of PPHTN during hospitalization compared with those with LEA duration ≤3.5 hours (42.3% vs 51.4%, χ2=8.486, P=0.004). Cumulative incidence of PPHTN was significantly lower in the longer LEA duration group on postpartum day 1 (32.3% [95% CI: 27.5–36.7] vs 43.2% [95% CI: 39.2–47.0]) and day 3 (48.9% [95% CI: 42.3–54.8] vs 63.2% [95% CI: 57.0–68.5]), but the difference attenuated and the curves converged by day 5 (Figure 2). The median time to PPHTN onset was longer in women with LEA duration >3.5 hour than in those with LEA duration ≤3.5 hour (73.5 hours [95% CI: 55.3–79.8] vs 40.6 hours [95% CI: 33.1–48.7], χ2=12.543, P<0.001). After adjustment for advanced maternal age, preterm birth, null parity, and antepartum antihypertensive therapy, LEA duration >3.5 hours was independently associated with a reduced hazard of PPHTN in women with HDP (HR=0.72, 95% CI: 0.58–0.88, P=0.001).Figure 2Cumulative Incidence of Postpartum Hypertension Stratified by Labor Epidural Analgesia Duration among women with Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy.Line graph showing cumulative events probability over time for labor epidural analgesia duration groups.

During the postpartum period, women with LEA duration >3.5 hours were less likely to require combination antihypertensive therapy (9.5% vs 13.6%, χ2=4.029, P=0.045) and received lower daily cumulative labetalol doses postpartum than those with ≤3.5 hours, while nifedipine dose was comparable between groups (Table 1). In a sensitivity analysis restricted to women who did not receive postpartum antihypertensive therapy, those with an LEA duration >3.5 hours exhibited a significantly lower hazard of PPHTN compared to those with an LEA duration ≤ 3.5 hours (Table 2). In the PE subgroup, the incidence of PPHTN was lower among women with an LEA duration >3.5 hours than among those with ≤3.5 hours, but the difference was not statistically significant. Discussion

Principal Findings

Among women with HDP, longer duration of LEA was associated with a significantly delayed onset of PPHTN during hospitalization. In multivariable Cox proportional hazards analysis, women with longer LEA exposure had a 28% lower hazard of developing PPHTN compared to those with shorter duration (adjusted HR, 0.72, 95% CI: 0.58–0.88, P=0.001). This temporal delay contributed to a lower cumulative incidence of PPHTN observed during the index hospitalization.

Main Results

In this retrospective cohort study, the cumulative incidence of PPHTN during hospitalization aligned with prior reports and reflects the well-established pattern of progressive onset in the immediate postpartum period.18 In the absence of clinically validated thresholds for LEA duration, we used the maximally selected log-rank statistic to categorize LEA exposure into two groups for comparative analysis. Given the observational nature of our study, LEA duration was not assigned or manipulated but rather determined by the natural course of labor. Consequently, women with longer LEA exposure were more likely to be younger, term-delivering primiparas – a profile associated with prolonged labor.

Notably, prior studies have linked advanced maternal age, multiparity, preterm delivery, and antepartum antihypertensive use to an elevated risk of PPHTN, with the latter two often reflecting greater severity of antepartum hypertensive disease. To account for these potential confounders, we adjusted for all aforementioned variables in our multivariable models. After multivariable adjustment, LEA duration >3.5 hours remained independently associated with a significantly lower risk of PPHTN. This association was consistent across subgroup analyses stratified by HDP type, further supporting that the observed protective effect is unlikely to be attributable solely to baseline differences in maternal or obstetric characteristics.

In addition, women with longer LEA exposure required combination antihypertensive therapy less frequently and had lower daily cumulative labetalol doses postpartum. As antihypertensive use may be a consequence of PPHTN, we did not adjust for it in the primary model. In a sensitivity analysis restricted to women not receiving postpartum antihypertensive, the association between longer LEA duration and reduced PPHTN risk remained consistent with the primary analysis. Among treated women, the association was attenuated. Nevertheless, those with longer LEA exposure still required lower labetalol doses. These findings collectively suggest that extended LEA exposure may promote postpartum hemodynamic stability, thereby reducing reliance on antihypertensive medications.

Our findings further show that women with longer LEA duration had shorter postpartum hospital stays, consistent with a more stable clinical course and reduced PPHTN risk-factors that may facilitate earlier discharge. However, because PPHTN was defined based on in-hospital diagnoses, discharge precludes further detection and thus acts as a competing event. In this context, Kaplan-Meier estimation may underestimate the observable cumulative incidence of PPHTN by prematurely censoring patients. We therefore used a cause-specific Cox model within a competing risks framework, modeling discharge as a competing outcome. The consistency of this analysis with our primary results supports the robustness of the association between prolonged LEA and reduced PPHTN risk, independent of differential follow-up time.

Clinical Implications

Growing evidence supports hemodynamic dysregulation as a central pathophysiological mechanism in HDP, characterized by two primary phenotypes – volume expansion and elevated systemic vascular resistance – for which tailored therapies may improve outcomes.19,20 Given the persistence of these abnormalities postpartum, early PPHTN could be viewed as a continuation of HDP.21 In GH and late-onset PE, volume expansion is a key hemodynamic characteristic. LEA-induced sympathetic blockade can increase vascular capacitance and reduce venous return, potentially attenuating cardiac preload.22 The association between longer LEA duration and lower labetalol use may reflect, in part, these hemodynamic modifications.23

In contrast, early-onset PE is typically associated with elevated systemic vascular resistance. LEA-induced sympathetic blockade has the potential to reduce systemic vascular resistance, which may contribute to blood pressure control in this phenotype.23,24 Given the limited efficacy of beta-blockers alone, vasodilators such as nifedipine are commonly required.25 Notably, women with longer LEA exposure were less likely to receive combination therapy, and among those with PE, nifedipine doses were lower with longer LEA duration – findings that are compatible with a partial physiological overlap between LEA and pharmacologic vasodilation.

Research Implications

The postpartum period is marked by hemodynamic instability due to intrapartum fluid loading, abrupt loss of placental vasodilators, and auto transfusion from interstitial spaces.4 Together, these changes promote volume overload and elevated systemic vascular resistance, potentially triggering PPHTN even in women with normotensive pregnancies.3,26 Despite the widespread use of LEA, its impact on PPHTN in women with HDP remains understudied.27 Among the findings, the cumulative incidence curves show a time-dependent attenuation of the association between LEA duration and PPHTN risk, with the greatest separation within the first 72 hours postpartum. Thereafter, the curves converge, and cumulative incidence becomes comparable by day 5. This pattern may reflect a prolonged offset of LEA effects, potentially due to accumulation of local anesthetic in the epidural space during extended infusion. The reduced in-hospital PPHTN incidence associated with longer LEA may not represent a true reduction in overall risk, but rather a temporal delay in onset. These observations raise the hypothesis that sustained epidural analgesia into the early postpartum period, such as continued epidural analgesia after cesarean delivery, could potentially contribute to hemodynamic stability in women with HDP, warranting further investigation.

Strengths and Limitations

This study has several strengths. It is among the first to examine the association between LEA duration and the timing of PPHTN onset in women with HDP, using time-to-event methods to capture dynamic risk patterns. We leveraged a large, single-center retrospective cohort with detailed clinical documentation, enabling precise exposure and outcome assessment. To ensure accurate PPHTN ascertainment, we rigorously curated blood pressure data from electronic records – excluding measurements without exact timestamps and patients with fewer than six valid postpartum readings. Furthermore, we accounted for the potential confounding effect of antihypertensive therapy by distinguishing pre- from postpartum medication use and conducting stratified sensitivity analyses, which strengthened the robustness of our findings.

However, several limitations warrant consideration. First, the single-center design may limit generalizability, and the LEA duration threshold used to define exposure groups was derived from our cohort and lacks external validation. As an observational study, our findings indicate association rather than causation; the observed link may reflect differences in underlying disease severity or clinical management rather than a direct effect of LEA, and thus should inform – rather than dictate – clinical decisions pending prospective confirmation. Second, PPHTN diagnosis relied on inpatient blood pressure monitoring, which was more frequent in hypertensive women and less so in normotensive individuals. Although all HDP patients follow a standardized monitoring protocol (every 4–6 hours), differential assessment intensity may lead to under-ascertainment of PPHTN and introduce surveillance bias. Finally, residual confounding by unmeasured factors, such as the additional bolus doses of epidural anesthesia, postpartum pain, may influence stress pathways and blood pressure regulation.

Conclusion

Longer LEA duration was associated with a reduced risk of postpartum hypertension and lower antihypertensive use in women with HDP. These observational findings indicate that prolonged analgesia may be associated with improved postpartum hemodynamic stability, suggesting consideration for its continuation in eligible patients; prospective studies are needed to confirm causality and guide clinical practice.
