Clone and characterization of a cytochrome P450 gene for drought tolerance in rice
Authors: Wenqiang Liu, Zuwu Chen, Zheng Dong, Liang Guo, Biaoren Yang, Licheng Liu, Sanxiong Liu, Xiaowu Pan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDrought is a major abiotic stress limiting rice production worldwide. Identifying genes that enhance drought tolerance is essential for breeding resilient varieties.ResultsIn this study, we report the map-based cloning and functional characterization of DT1, a novel cytochrome P450 gene conferring drought tolerance in rice. Using near isogenic lines (NILs) derived from a cross between Xiang743 and Katy, we delimited DT1 into a 115 kb interval on chromosome 3, where contains 18 open read frames (ORFs). Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) analysis identified Os03g55250 as the candidate gene. Clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats-associated nuclease 9 (CRISPR/Cas9) knockout mutants of Os03g55250 exhibited increased drought sensitivity, while overexpression lines showed enhanced drought tolerance, confirming that Os03g55250 was the target gene and positively regulates drought resistance. DT1 was mainly expressed in stems, leaves, and leaf sheaths, and the DT1 protein localized in the endoplasmic reticulum. Haplotype analysis identified Hap1 as a favorable allele in japonica rice.ConclusionsOur findings provide a promising genetic resource for breeding drought-resistant rice varieties and offers new insights into the role of P450 genes in abiotic stress adaptation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12870-026-09247-6.

Introduction

Drought is one of the main abiotic stress limiting rice production worldwide, particularly under the rising global temperatures and increasing water shortage. As a staple food for more than half of the global population, rice is highly vulnerable to water deficit. Over the past few decades, drought has affected 3.4–16.3 million hectares of cropland annually in China alone, causing substantial economic losses [1]. Therefore, developing drought-tolerant rice varieties has become a priority for ensuring global food security, and a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying drought adaptation is essential for breeding efforts.

Considerable progress has been made in recent decades toward dissecting the genetic basis of drought tolerance in rice. Numerous quantitative trait loci (QTLs) and genes associated with drought-related traits have been identified through linkage mapping and genome-wide association studies (GWAS). Among these, several key genes have been successfully cloned and functionally characterized. For instance, DRO1 (Drought1) was cloned using an integrative approach that combined GWAS with analyses of introgression lines (ILs) and transcriptomic profiles. DRO1 encodes a COBRA-like protein and enhances drought resistance by modifying cell wall structure-specifically by increasing cellulose content and maintaining cellulose crystallinity [2]. OsRINGzf1 (Really Interesting New Gene Zinc Finger) is the candidate gene underlying a drought-related QTL on the long arm of chromosome 4 in rice, it encodes a RING zinc finger protein 1 and regulates drought resistance by controlling the turnover of OsPIP2;1 [3]. DRG9 (Drought Resistance Gene 9) was cloned through GWAS and encodes a double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) binding protein, it enhances drought resistance via protein phase separation in rice [4]. RoLe1 (Root Length 1) was identified by GWAS and controls root length and drought resistance in rice [5].

In addition, the molecular mechanism underlying drought resistance have been explored across various processes ranging from root architecture remodeling to osmotic regulation and reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavenging—that collectively contribute to drought resilience [6–8]. The cytochrome P450 monooxygenase superfamily is one of the largest and most functionally diverse enzyme families in plants, participating in the biosynthesis of secondary metabolites, hormone metabolism, and detoxification of xenobiotics [9–11]. Emerging evidence has implicated P450 proteins in abiotic stress responses, including drought and salt tolerance. For instance, OsDSS1, a cytochrome P450 gene, is involved in growth and drought stress responses in rice by fine tuning gibberellin (GA)- to- abscisic acid (ABA) balance [12]. The CYP94D subclade encodes enzymes related to hormone homeostasis response in response to wounding and salt stress in rice [13]. Additionally, OsCYP71D8L, a member of the CYP71 clan in rice, participates in the regulation of multiple agronomic traits and abiotic stress responses by affecting GA and cytokinin (CK) homeostasis [14]. Despite these advances, the specific functions of most rice P450 genes in drought tolerance remain poorly understood, and their potential for crop improvement remains largely unexplored.

Here, we report the map-based cloning and functional characterization of a novel cytochrome P450 gene that confers enhanced drought tolerance in rice. Our findings not only identify a new component in rice drought tolerance network but also provide a valuable genetic resource for the molecular breeding of drought-resistant rice varieties.

Materials and methods

Rice materials

An F2 population and a recombinant inbred line (RIL) population derived from the cross between Xiang743 and Katy were previously used to map stress-related genes [15–17]. Based on the flanking markers of mapping genes, numerous near isogenic lines (NILs) were developed by crossing Xiang743 and Katy, followed by three consecutive backcrossing generations and one generation of selfing using marker-assisted selection. Among these, a pair of NILs, one carrying the Xiang743 genotype (NIL-X ) and the other carrying the Katy genotype (NIL-K) in the interval of RM520-RM130 on chromosome 3, showed contrasting drought resistance phenotypes.

NIL-X was crossed with NIL-K to developed an F2 population consisted of 2465 individual plants. Based on the genotypes of the F2 population, five recombinant F2 individuals in the interval of RM520-RM130 were selected and self-pollinated to generate the corresponding F3 populations.

All populations, along with the parents, were planted in Changsha, China, with a row spacing of 16.5 cm and an intra-row spacing of 16.5 cm. At maturity, seeds were harvested from each individual plant for evaluating drought tolerance. For evaluating agronomic traits, the border plants of each plot were removed to avoid marginal effect during sampling, each material for more than 10 plants was harvested. Field management was the same as that used for routine fields.

Drought tolerance evaluation

To evaluate drought tolerance at the seedling stage, seeds were surface-steriled.

with 0.6% sodium hypochlorite solution, rinsed repeatedly and finally soaked in germination trays at 30 °C for 3 day. After that, the germinated seeds were picked out in culture chamber at 30 °C without light with sterile distilled water to promote root growth for 48 h. Then seedlings were transferred and hydroponically cultured in Yoshida nutrient solution in an artificial climate chamber (28 °C light /25°C dark, 14 h/10 h, 70–80% humidity) until the two- and a- half-leaf stage. About 40 uniform seedlings for each line were then treated with 20% PEG6000 concentration for 6–8 days. After which they were transferred back to normal nutrient solution for 7 days of recover growth. Three biological replications were performed. Survival rate was calculated and used as the drought tolerance index at the seedling stages. Survival rate was expressed as survival seedlings/total seedlings drought-treated at the seedling stage. A seedling was classified as dead when the majority (> 75%) of its leaves were no longer green after recovery.

Extraction of DNAs and development of markers

DNA was extracted from the populations and parental lines using the cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) method as described by Liu et al. [15]. Primers were designed based on sequence comparison between Nipponbare and 93 − 11 and were amplified following the protocol used in the previous study [15].

Vector construction and generation of transgenic plants

To construct the overexpression vector, the full-length coding sequence of DT1 was amplified from the cDNA of the NIL-X, fused with FLAG tag at C-terminus, and cloned into the pC1300 vector under the control of 35 S promoter. To construct clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats-associated nuclease 9 (CRISPR/Cas9) vector, two single guide RNAs (sgRNAs) targeting DT1 were separately designed, and two expression cassettes, OsU3-DT1-sgRNAs, were generated. All plasmids were transformed into calli of Nipponbare via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation using the strain EHA105.

All primers used for vector construction are listed in Supplemental Table S1, and the amplified fragments were verified by sequencing.

RNA extraction and qRT-PCR analysis

Total RNA was extracted from the NILs at the seedling stages using Trizol reagent (Sangon). mRNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using M-MLV (RNase H-) reverse transcriptase according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Vazyme). Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed using chamQ universal SYBR qRT-PCR master mix (Vazyme), with at least three biological replicates. OsActin was used as a reference gene. All primers used for qRT-PCR are listed in Supplemental Table S1.

Subcellular localization

The full-length cDNA of DT1 was amplified from NIL-X and cloned into the pAN580 vector with green fluorescent protein (GFP) coding sequence fused to its N-terminus. The construct was then transformed into rice protoplasts via polyethylene glycol (PEG) mediated transformation. Green fluorescence signal was detected using a laser scanning confocal microscope (LSM880, Germany). The primers used are listed in Supplemental Table S1.

Results

Identification and fine mapping of DT1

NIL-X exhibited a survival rate of 63.39% after drought stress at the seedling stage, whereas NIL-K showed a survival rate of 46.57% (Fig. 1A, B). Given the difference between NIL-X and NIL-K in the RM520-RM130 interval, together with the overlap of an unpublished drought tolerance locus (from our laboratory) with this region, we propose that a gene controlling drought resistance is likely present in the RM520-RM130 interval. We designated the drought tolerance gene as DT1. In order to fine map DT1, polymorphic markers were developed within the interval. Using these markers, five recombinant individuals (R3, R7, R34, R35, R37) were selected from the F2 population. For each recombinant, two kinds of homozygous genotypes (one carrying the Xiang743 allele and the other carrying the Katy allele) were selected from its self-pollinated progeny. Five lines of each genotype were used for evaluating drought resistance.

Firstly, we compared the phenotype of drought resistance between NIL-X and NIL-K, and identified repeatedly the presence of DT1 in the interval of RM520-RM130 (Fig. 2). Then DT1 was further confirmed by R3, R34, and R37 subgroups. Finally, the DT1 was delimited into an interval of 115 kb by substitution mapping on the basis of comparing all overlapping lines from subgroups (Fig. 2). Based on the Nipponbare reference genome, 18 open read frames (ORFs) are annotated in this region (Supplemental Table S2).

Candidate analysis and map-based clone of DT1

To find out the target gene responsible for drought tolerance, qRT-PCR analysis was performed for the 18 ORFs between NIL-X and NIL-K under drought stress at 0, 3 and 12 h, respectively. The results showed that the expression patterns of most genes were similar between the NILs, no significant expression differences were observed at 0, 3 and 12 h between NILs except for Os03g55100, Os03g55250 and Os03g55260. Differential expressions of Os03g55100 and Os03g55260 were detected at early stage, suggesting they might be involved in early signaling under drought stress. While Os03g55250 exhibited a significant difference in expression level at 12 h between the NILs (Fig. 3). Combining their protein annotations and expression profiles, we focused on Os03g55250 as the candidate gene. On the basis of annotation from NCBI, Os03g55250 contains two exons and belongs to a cytochrome P450 gene.

To further confirm that Os03g55250 is responsible for drought resistance, we designed two target sites at the beginning and middle of the first exon of Os03g55250 for knockout using CRISPR/Cas9 in the Nipponbare background, respectively. Sequencing identified two homozygous edited lines, DT1KO−1 and DT1KO−2. DT1KO−1 had a single base deletion at the beginning of the first exon of Os03g55250, DT1KO−2 had a two-base deletion in middle of the first exon of Os03g55250 (Fig. 4A). DT1KO−1 and DT1KO−2 both exhibited enhanced sensitivity for drought stress compared with Nipponbare (Fig. 4B, C). These results confirmed that Os03g55250 is DT1. We then performed overexpression assays. The overexpression construct carried the DT1 coding sequence driven by the 35 S promoter. Overexpression lines OE−1 and OE−2, which showed 55- and 75-fold higher expression of DT1, respectively, displayed enhanced drought tolerance compared with Nipponbare (Fig. 4D-F). Together, these results demonstrate that DT1 acts as a positive regulator of drought resistance in rice.

Expression pattern and subcellular localization

To analyze the expression pattern of DT1, we performed qRT-PCR and examined its transcript levels at the seedling, tillering, and booting stages. The results showed that DT1 was mainly expressed in stems, leaves and leaf sheaths (Fig. 5A), consistent with the data for MH63 (http://crep.ncpgr.cn/) (Supplementary Fig. S1 ).

To determine the subcellular localization of DT1, we transiently expressed a GFP-DT1 fusion protein in rice protoplasts. The GFP signal colocalized with endoplasmic reticulum marker, indicating that DT1 is localized in endoplasmic reticulum (Fig. 5B).

Allelic variation and haplotype analysis of DT1

To further investigate how DT1 functions in drought resistance, we cloned and sequenced the coding region of DT1 alleles from NIL-X and NIL-K. Sequence comparison between NILs identified three nucleotide variations, including at positions 31,448,717 bp (C→A), 31,449,187 bp (T→C), and 31,450,834 bp (G→C), leading to two amino acid changes at 31,448,717 bp from R to S and at 31,450,834 bp from R to S, neither of which fell within the conserved domains (Fig. 6).

Haplotype analysis of DT1 using its coding region according to the data from the website (http://ricevarmap.ncpgr.cn/)

To further clarify the haplotypes of DT1, we used natural variations in the coding region of the gene for haplotype network analysis via the RiceVarMap website (http://ricevarmap.ncpgr.cn/). A total of 9 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), including the 3 SNPs between NILs, were identified in 4689 accessions. As a result, 4689 accessions were group into seven haplotypes. Among these, Hap1 and Hap2 are advantageous haplotypes, accounting for 47.96% and 40.05% of the total resources, respectively. Notably, japonica rice accounts for 64.16% in Hap1, indica rice accounts for 98.03% in Hap2 (Fig. 6). The DT1 allele from NIL-X belongs to Hap1, while that from NIL-K is classified as Hap2. These findings suggest that Hap1 might be a favorable haplotype and could be utilized to improve drought resistance in rice.

Discussion

DT1 was located within a tandem P450 cluster and had evolutionary divergence

Plants have evolved various drought resistance mechanisms, such as drought avoidance, drought tolerance, and drought escape. Drought avoidance enhances water uptake and reduces water loss through root system development, stomatal regulation, and cuticle formation. Drought tolerance maintains cellular integrity under low water content through osmotic adjustment and antioxidant systems. Drought escape allows plants to complete their life cycle early, thereby avoiding drought through accelerated flowering [18]. Numerous genes associated with drought resistance have been identified [19–22]. In this study, we delimited DT1 to a 115 kb region containing 18 ORFs. Further qRT-PCR analysis indicated that three genes—Os03g55100, Os03g55250, and Os03g55260—showed expression differences between NIL-X and NIL-K after drought treatment. Among them, Os03g55100 and Os03g55260 exhibited differential expression only at the early stage of treatment, suggesting that they may be involved in the initial response to drought stress. In contrast, Os03g55250 began to show differential expression as the treatment progressed. Taking these expression patterns together with their protein annotations, we prioritized Os03g55250 as the candidate gene for further study. As a results, overexpression and knockout experiments of Os03g55250 confirmed its association with drought tolerance. In addition, the delimited region contains four tandem P450 genes (Supplemental Table S2). Except for Os03g55260, the other three showed nearly identical expression patterns during drought treatment. Protein sequence alignment revealed that Os03g55250 shares high similarity with three other P450 proteins, ranging from 32.48% to 40.00% (Supplementary Fig. S2). To further clarify the relationships among these four genes, we performed an evolutionary analysis using P450 gene encoded protein in rice. The results indicated that the four genes share a common origin (Supplementary Fig. S3). Among them, Os03g55240, named CYP81A6, has been cloned and shown to confer resistance to herbicides [9]. Collectively, these results suggest although the four genes originated from the same ancestor, they likely have undergone functional divergence during evolution. However, we can not rule out the possibility that some of these genes, including Os03g55250, may act redundantly or cooperatively in drought tolerance. Further investigation is needed to dissect their individual and collective roles in response to drought stress.

Promoter allelic variations rather than conserved domain mutations in DT1 may underlie drought tolerance differences

P450 genes typically contain conserved domains, including the heme-binding domain (FxxGxRxCxG) and the helix K (ExxR) motif [23]. In this study, we compared the coding sequences of DT1 alleles from the NILs. Three SNPs were identified, resulting in two amino acid changes, but neither fell within the conserved domains. This raised the question of whether the promoter activity of the DT1 allele, rather than the encoded protein, contributes to the drought resistance phenotype. To explore this, we analyzed approximately 2 kb of the promoter regions of the DT1 alleles from the NILs. Sequence alignment revealed 39 SNPs, four single-base deletions, as well as a 6-bp and a 141-bp deletion. These variations collectively led to alterations in the predicted binding sites for GA, ABA, and transcription factors including MYB and MYC (Supplementary Fig. S4). These findings were consistent with previous reports that P450 genes are involved in plant hormone signaling pathways [13]. Together, these results suggest that allelic variations in the DT1 promoter region may contribute to genetic variation in drought tolerance. However, further research is needed to determine whether the difference in drought resistance between the two NILs is due to variations in the DT1 protein itself or differences in its promoter activity.

DT1 is a potential target for drought resistance breeding in rice

P450 genes play important roles in chemical defense, hormone regulation, and detoxification under abiotic stress [23]. In this study, the DT1 protein was localized in the endoplasmic reticulum. Agronomic trait evaluation, including heading date, panicle length, panicle number per plant, seed setting, 1000-grain weight and yield per plant, showed that overexpression and knockout of DT1 did not affect yield-related traits (Supplementary Fig. S5), suggesting that DT1 is non-essential for yield under normal conditions and likely plays a role only under specific abiotic stress conditions. In rice drought-resistant breeding, the utilization of drought-resistant genes can enhance plant survival ability and yield stability under water deficit conditions. However, due to insufficient research on drought-resistant genes and their underlying mechanisms, very few genes are currently being utilized in rice breeding programs. DT1 may therefore be a promising candidate gene for improving drought resistance in rice.

Conclusion

In this study, we cloned and characterized DT1, a cytochrome P450 gene that positively regulates drought tolerance in rice. Fine mapping and functional analyses demonstrated that DT1 enhances drought resistance without affecting yield-related traits under normal conditions. The DT1 protein localizes in the endoplasmic reticulum, and allelic variations between the two NILs suggest possible promoter-driven regulatory differences. Haplotype analysis identified Hap1 as a favorable allele enriched in japonica rice. Our work provides a valuable genetic resource for breeding drought-resistant rice varieties and offers new insights into the role of P450 genes in abiotic stress adaptation.
