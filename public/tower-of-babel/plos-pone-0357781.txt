FAnet: A lightweight end-to-end framework for Fructus Aurantii identification and quality grading

LeTian Wu, KeKe Liao, BingWei Song, ShenBo Guo, XingDong Gao, HuiFeng Shi

ABSTRACT
The quality grade of Fructus Aurantii(FA) is closely related to its clinical efficacy and medication safety; therefore, a standardized grading method is needed. However, FA samples show considerable morphological variation, and conventional grading depends largely on manual visual assessment, which is subjective and inefficient. This study proposes FAnet, a lightweight instance segmentation framework based on YOLOv8n-seg, for estimating peel thickness and outer diameter during FA quality grading. FAnet integrates three task-adapted components: C2Star, which combines StarNet and C2f to improve multi-scale feature extraction; the Simple Parameter-Free Attention Module (SimAM), which enhances spatial-channel feature weighting without introducing additional trainable parameters; and a multi-scale dynamic fitting module (MSDFM), which maps segmentation-derived areas to physical dimensions for grading. Using a self-built dataset containing 1,093 original images and 5,035 augmented training images, FAnet achieved 96.12% precision, 98.77% recall, 98.60% mAP@50, and 77.08% mAP@95, with 2.97 M parameters and 7.9 GFLOPs under the present experimental setting. The results indicate that FAnet can support automated FA segmentation and preliminary grading under controlled imaging conditions. Broader validation across larger and more diverse sample populations is still required before deployment-oriented application.

Introduction Fructus Aurantii (FA), commonly known as Zhiqiao, is a traditional Chinese medicine recorded in the Chinese Pharmacopoeia. It is used for regulating Qi, reducing abdominal distension, and promoting digestion [ 1 ]. FA quality affects clinical efficacy and medication safety. Conventional identification and grading rely on manual evaluation of morphological characteristics, color, and texture by experienced personnel. These approaches are limited by low efficiency, high subjectivity, and insufficient standardization. In recent years, machine-vision-based intelligent detection methods have been increasingly applied to agricultural product sorting and industrial quality inspection, offering a potential route for automated identification and grading of traditional Chinese medicines. However, FA has complex surface texture, variable morphology, and background interference, which can reduce feature extraction accuracy, model generalization, and computational efficiency. Therefore, an automated FA grading method needs to balance accuracy, robustness, and computational cost [ 2 ]. In recent years, deep learning technology has introduced a new technical paradigm for the intelligent detection and grading of Chinese herbal medicines. In target detection, methods based on convolutional neural networks have demonstrated notable advantages. Chang et al. (2024) applied an improved ResNet50-based architecture to achieve precise localization of herbal materials [ 3 ]. Zhou et al. (2024) developed a hybrid model combining RGB, HOG, and LBP features with AlexNet for herbal classification [ 4 ]. However, the manual features and heterogeneous deep feature fusion introduced increased inference delays. Liu et al. (2021) applied the SSD algorithm and reported an accuracy of 92.7%; however, limitations in detecting small objects were observed [ 5 ]. In fine grained classification, Wang et al. (2020) reported a 5.3% improvement in classification accuracy using a multichannel color space attention mechanism [ 6 ]. Zhuang et al. (2018) developed a deep convolutional network to recognize 98 herbal medicine types, reaching 95.8% accuracy [ 7 ]. Nonetheless, the model’s large size (320M parameters) limits its deployment on edge devices. The YOLO model series has become prominent due to efficiency and flexibility. Dong et al. (2023) added Coordinate Attention (CA) and a lightweight GhostNet module to YOLOv5s, reducing computation by 37% but decreasing detection recall for overlapping herbs by 9.2% [ 8 ]. Tao et al. (2023) introduced a Feature Enhancement Module (FEM) to recalibrate multi-scale features, mitigating information loss from small targets and increasing the mAP of overlapping licorice samples by 6.5% [ 9 ]. Li et al. (2023) introduced a redundant detection head, which reduced false detections but also decreased sensitivity to large-scale objects [ 10 ]. Luo et al. (2025) integrated Content Aware ReAssembly of Features (CARAFE) up-sampling and Multimatrix Factorization Attention (MFA) into YOLOv8 for Tibetan medicinal herb detection, achieving 89.4% mAP but increasing 23% computational overhead [ 11 ]. In segmentation and lightweight research, Tan et al. (2024) embedded Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) in U-Net, attaining 86.2% IoU in Sichuan safflower semantic segmentation [ 12 ]. Feng et al. (2024) improved YOLOv8 by replacing standard convolutions with FasterNet’s PConv, reducing parameters by 21% and increasing maturity recognition accuracy of yellow essence seeds [ 13 ]. Deng et al. (2023) developed an ECANet dual-path fusion model based on Inception-ResNet-v2 with MSRCR-CLAHE image enhancement, achieving a 97.1% recognition rate for Angelica leaf spot disease [ 14 ]. Despite substantial advancements, persistent challenges remain. First, rigid multi-scale feature fusion mechanisms inadequately adapt to nonlinear characteristics of FA, particularly varying peel thickness. Second, balancing lightweight design with accuracy remains challenging. Third, morphological modeling is insufficient, and quantitative compensation strategies addressing peel texture deformation due to placement angles in static images are lacking. In response to these challenges, this study proposes FAnet, an intelligent identification and grading framework tailored to FA. The framework integrates fusion feature extraction, attention enhancement, and multi-scale dynamic fitting to improve segmentation accuracy and grading efficiency. First, YOLOv8n-seg is used as the baseline model for rapid localization and instance segmentation of FA targets. Second, the C2f module is adapted by integrating StarNet operations to form C2Star, thereby improving the representation of fine surface and contour features. Third, SimAM is introduced to enhance feature weighting while maintaining a lightweight structure. Finally, the MSDFM is used as a post-processing module to establish a nonlinear mapping between pixel-level measurements and real-world geometric attributes, enabling quantitative grading based on estimated outer diameter and peel thickness. The main contribution of this study is the task-specific integration and validation of these components for FA grading, rather than the proposal of a general-purpose segmentation architecture. Materials Data collection In this study, FA samples were dried immature fruits of the cultivated stinking orange variant Fructus Aurantii ‘Xiucheng’ (Rutaceae). All samples were collected in July 2024 from the Chinese Herbal Medicine Plantation Specialized Cooperative of Guangde Shuangkeng Village, Zhangshu City, Jiangxi Province, China (115.193551623 degrees E, 27.966390045 degrees N). The samples were dried by sun drying and oven drying [ 15 ]. During image acquisition, a tripod was used to maintain a fixed camera-to-object distance of 15 cm. The outer diameter of each FA sample was measured using a vernier caliper. Specifically, one equatorial diameter was selected as the initial reference, and four diameters were measured successively by rotating the sample by 45 degrees each time. The arithmetic mean of the four measurements was used as the final transverse diameter of the sample. Data calibration Labelme, an open-source annotation tool, was used as the primary annotation platform and was combined with the Segment Anything Model (SAM) proposed by Meta AI for semi-automatic annotation [ 16 , 17 ]. The annotation target was the cross-sectional image of FA, including two key morphological features associated with grading requirements ( Fig 1 ). The first feature was the inner flesh region, which mainly consisted of white spongy tissue separated by irregular polygonal boundaries between locules. The second feature was the outer peel contour, representing the external boundary of the fruit. During annotation, fruit stalks and shadowed regions were excluded where necessary so that the complete circular or oval contour was retained. All annotations were manually generated and then reviewed for quality control. Inner and outer boundaries were visually inspected and corrected, particularly in difficult cases with weak or ambiguous inner-boundary regions. Consistency checks were performed before model training to reduce labeling uncertainty. 10.1371/journal.pone.0357781.g001 Fig 1 Fructus Aurantii image data: (a) AI labeling; (b) Label fusion. Data enhancement To improve model adaptability to real-world image acquisition, this study used an algorithm-based augmentation strategy designed for FA images captured by mobile phones and cameras. The operations included random rotation within +/-25 degrees, horizontal flipping with a probability of 50%, random brightness and saturation adjustment in the HSV color space (+/-15% and +/-10%, respectively), color-temperature shifting within +/-500 K to simulate warm and cool illumination, generation of semi-transparent bright spots using Gaussian filtering (5–15 pixels, 30–60% transparency), random directional motion blur (5–15 pixels, 0–360 degrees), and ISO 800–3200 equivalent Poisson-Gaussian mixed noise [ 18 ]. Representative augmented images are shown in Fig 2 , and the final dataset sizes are presented in Table 1 . To prevent data leakage, all augmentation procedures were conducted only after the original train/validation/test split. Method Overall architecture of FAnet To ensure clarity and reproducibility, FAnet is defined as a unified framework based on YOLOv8n-seg. The final architecture incorporates three task-adapted components: the C2Star module, the SimAM attention mechanism, and the multi-scale dynamic fitting module (MSDFM). The baseline model adopts the anchor-free YOLOv8n-seg architecture, which integrates object detection and instance segmentation into a unified pipeline. The mask generation mechanism follows a prototype-based strategy similar to YOLACT, but no additional YOLACT structural components are introduced [ 19 , 20 ]. Within this framework, the modifications are applied consistently as follows: (1) the original C2f modules in the backbone and neck are replaced by C2Star modules to enhance multi-scale feature extraction through implicit high-dimensional feature expansion [ 21 ]; (2) SimAM is inserted at three fixed locations, namely after each C2Star module in the backbone, after the feature fusion outputs of the feature pyramid network (FPN), and before the segmentation head [ 22 , 23 ]; and (3) MSDFM is used as a post-processing module that maps normalized pixel areas of the inner and outer regions to real-world dimensions through polynomial regression. Peel thickness is then calculated from the predicted diameters. The final architecture is illustrated in Fig 3 . 10.1371/journal.pone.0357781.g003 Fig 3 FAnet structure. Fusion feature extraction module C2Star To improve the representation of fine-grained morphological features such as peel thickness and contour variation, this study uses C2Star as a replacement for the standard C2f module. The C2Star module integrates the StarNet mechanism into the CSP-based structure. Specifically, Bottleneck blocks in C2f are replaced by Star blocks, which use element-wise multiplicative operations for nonlinear feature transformation. This design implicitly expands feature dimensionality without introducing substantial additional parameters. Through recursive star operations, the module maps input features into a higher-dimensional implicit space, thereby improving the representation of subtle texture and structural variation. Compared with conventional convolution-based feature fusion, C2Star increases feature diversity while reducing computational redundancy. Addressing the question of how to accurately identify subtle variations in FA peel thickness and surface texture, this research proposes the fused feature extraction module C2Star. Accurate detection of such small morphological differences requires networks capable of effectively capturing nuanced local features. While the original YOLOv8n-seg model employs the C2f module (derived from CSPDarknet53) to fuse low level and high level feature maps, thereby improving multi-scale detection accuracy. However, it greatly increases the computational complexity and prolongs the training time and computational resource requirements. To overcome these limitations, this research introduces the StarNet module into the C2Star structure. StarNet utilises star operations (element multiplication) for computational process optimisation. Through nonlinear feature transformation and extended implicit feature dimensions enhanced texture characterisation. Finally, achieving layer-by-layer extraction of FA features. Integration of SimAM lightweight attention module When traditional vision methods deal with FA features, there are low contrast features between the rind edge and the flesh and nonlinear spatial variability in rind thickness. They often need to rely on complex multi-scale filtering. They also need morphological preprocessing processes. These lead to an exponential increase in computational complexity. As a result, it becomes difficult to meet the demand for efficient lightweight detection. In this research, we propose to embed SimAM (Simple Parameter Free Attention Module) 3D attention mechanism in YOLOv8n-seg ( Fig 5 ) [ 26 ]. The modeling is done through the null domain channel synergetic attention without parameterization and reduces computational and storage overhead. The mean and variance of the feature map itself are directly utilized to construct the energy function. Feature calibration is accomplished in a single forward propagation. Reduce the iterative computation than the traditional method. 10.1371/journal.pone.0357781.g005 Fig 5 SimAM Lightweight Attention Module: (a) Channel-wise attention; (b) Spatial-wise attention; (c) Full 3-Dweights for attention. The mechanism synchronizes feature enhancement and computational optimization by strengthening the high frequency response of the rind thickness mutation region. Existing channel or spatial attention modules are different. The SimAM requires no additional parameters. It can adaptively learn similarity information between targets. Also, it can weight the importance of the adjusted features. To better realize attention, the importance of each neuron needs to be evaluated. In neural networks, activating neurons inhibit surrounding neurons (null domain inhibition). Neurons with a null domain inhibition effect should be given higher priority in visual processing. The lower the energy, the more the neuron t is distinguished from the surrounding neurons and the higher the importance. The SimAM is placed at three key locations in the FAnet framework: (1) immediately after the C2Star module in the backbone, enhancing low level texture extraction; (2) at the output of the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN), optimising multi-scale feature fusion; and (3) at the segmentation header input, improving mask generation quality. Multi-scale dynamic fitting module To precisely calculate FA peel thickness, the multi-scale dynamic fitting module is integrated into the FAnet framework. Initially, the pixel areas representing the inner (flesh) and outer (peel) regions of FA fruits are extracted using the YOLOv8n-seg model [ 27 ]. Subsequently, the peel thickness is derived by calculating half the difference between the inner and outer diameters. To ensure reproducibility, the regression procedure in MSDFM is described in detail as follows. The normalized pixel area is defined as the ratio between the segmented region area and the total image area, which reduces the influence of scale variations. Separate polynomial regression models are trained for the estimation of inner diameter and outer diameter. In this study, a second-order (quadratic) polynomial regression is adopted, which provides a balance between fitting accuracy and model simplicity. The dataset used for regression is divided into training and testing subsets with a ratio of 8:2. During training, a five-fold cross-validation strategy is applied to improve robustness, and the final model parameters are selected based on the average validation performance. To prevent overfitting, the fitting performance is evaluated using the coefficient of determination (R²) and residual analysis. Models with unstable fitting behavior are discarded. In addition, for targets of different size ranges, scale-specific regression functions are selected based on empirical error minimization, enabling more accurate mapping across multiple scales. II. Graded experiment A total of 35 samples were included in the grading validation experiment, representing the full independent grading test set rather than an illustrative subset. All available samples with manually measured OD, ID, and peel thickness reference values were used for grading validation. The grading set covered all three grades and included boundary-sensitive samples relevant to threshold-based classification. In the FA grading experiment, the grading standard based on ID, OD, and peel thickness was validated through systematic measurement and manual labeling. As shown in Fig 9 , the FAnet predicted FA’s ID, OD and calculated the peel thickness. Evaluation grades were then given based on the grading standard. Each predicted image was labeled with the OD, ID, peel thickness, and the grading was determined according to the double threshold condition described in Section 3.3. The sensitivity analysis further suggests that current grading limitations arise primarily from threshold effects in derived peel-thickness estimation, rather than from instability in the segmentation framework itself. This distinction indicates that future improvements may be achieved through refinement of thickness measurement or adaptive threshold strategies, without requiring fundamental changes to the segmentation architecture. Conclusions (1) The proposed FAnet framework demonstrates strong segmentation performance and preliminary feasibility for automated quality grading of Fructus Aurantii. While outer-diameter prediction achieved high accuracy, grading reliability remains constrained by uncertainty in peel-thickness estimation, particularly near threshold boundaries. Furthermore, the present grading validation was conducted using the full available independent test set of 35 samples, which supports feasibility assessment but remains limited for establishing deployment-level robustness. Therefore, the present study supports the feasibility rather than definitive robustness of practical grading, and further refinement of thickness measurement together with expanded validation is required before deployment-oriented applications. (2) The present research has three major limitations that need to be addressed. First, model validation is restricted to a single variety of Fructus Aurantii, and its generalizability to other herbal varieties (e.g., Chenpi and Qingpi) remains unconfirmed. Second, although the lightweight architecture of FAnet suggests potential suitability for future implementation on mobile or edge devices, deployment capability has not yet been validated through hardware-level benchmarking. In particular, actual inference speed, memory usage, and end-to-end runtime have not been evaluated on target hardware such as Raspberry Pi. Therefore, the present study supports deployment-oriented feasibility rather than verified hardware-level deployment performance. Third, the MSDFM is developed under controlled imaging conditions, with all images acquired at a fixed distance. Therefore, variations in imaging distance and viewpoint are not explicitly modeled. Future work will explore more robust geometric modeling strategies to improve adaptability under diverse imaging conditions. Finally, the indirect method adopted in Fructus Aurantii grading experiments to calculate peel thickness introduces inherent bias, which may lead to erroneous grade predictions especially when the fruit flesh is irregularly shaped. Additionally, the use of fixed grading thresholds increases the risk of classification errors near threshold boundaries. (3) To address the above limitations, the following targeted research directions are proposed. First, incorporate hyperspectral imaging or laser ranging technology. This technology can realize direct, non destructive measurement of peel thickness. In this way, it can eliminate the bias caused by indirect calculation methods. Second, develop a dynamic threshold adjustment mechanism. Base it on fuzzy logic or other probabilistic models. Integrate confidence scores. This will enhance the flexibility and robustness of the grading system. It’s especially beneficial for samples near threshold values. Although leakage-control and annotation quality-control procedures were implemented to improve reliability, future work may further strengthen annotation consistency through multi-annotator agreement analysis and expanded validation across larger datasets. Finally, expand the validation dataset. Include multiple varieties of citrus related herbs like Chenpi and Qingpi. This is to verify the cross variety generalizability of the model. Optimize the model for future edge-oriented implementation using techniques such as model quantization and hardware acceleration, and conduct hardware-level timing validation on edge devices such as Raspberry Pi.
