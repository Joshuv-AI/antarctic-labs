10 January 1954

Memorandum for: Deputy Director of Central Intelligence

Subject: DDCI's luncheon with the Texas Congressional

delegation on 12 January 1955.

1. In connection with your luncheon meeting with the Texas Congres-
sional delegation on 12 January 1955, your attention is called to the
biographic data on the members of that delegation commencing on page

137 of the February 1954 edition of the Congressional Directory.

2. J, Frank Wilson, of the 5th District (Dallas), did not seek re-
election, and his seat was won by Bruce Alger, a Republican. It is
believed that Mr. Alger is only the fifth Republican elected to Congress
from Texas.

3. Wingate Lucas, of the 12th District, has been succeeded by
Jim Wright.

4. John Lyle, Jr. , of the 14th District, has been succeeded by
John J. Bell.

5. Lloyd Bentsen, Jr. , of the 15th District, has been succeeded by
Joseph M. Kilgore. Kilgore is a lawyer, with four terms in the Texas
Legislature. He was born in 1918 in Brown County and moved to Hidalgo
County in 1949. He attended Westmoreland College in San Antonio and
took his law at the University of Texas. In July 1941 Kilgore joined the
Air Force and graduated from the West Coast flying school. He partici-
pated in raids in North Africa, Greece and Crete in support of the British
8th Army and commanded the second American air raid on Naples in
December 1942. His decorations include the Silver Star, DFC, Air
Medal with two Oak Leaf Clusters, two unit citations and four personal
citations. He is still active as a lieutenant colonel with the 9811th Air
Force Reserve Squadron in the Rio Grande Valley, His home is in
McAllen, and he was considered the unofficial floor leader of the Texas
House.

6. In the 16th District Kenneth Regan has been replaced by J. T,
Rutherford.

7. Senator Lyndon Johnson is now the Senate Majority Leader.

He has never received a thorough CIA briefing, but was invited down for
a Saturday morning briefing here on one or two occasions toward the
close of the 83rd Congress. He was never able to fix a date, but we
should take steps to arrange for such a briefing with him together with
his Majority Whip, Senator Clements of Kentucky. Senator Johnson is

a member of the Senate Armed Services Committee.

8. Senator Price Daniel was the Attorney General of Texas and
was allied with Governor Shivers in supporting President Eisenhower
in 1952.

9. Cong. Martin Dies is the Congressman at Large from Texas.
He was a member of Congress from 1931 to 1945, when he voluntarily
retired. In 1938 he introduced the resolution creating, and became the
first Chairman of, the House Un-A merican Activities Committee. He
is no longer a member of that Committee, and is on the Merchant
Marine and Fisheries Committee.

10. Cong. Wright Patman is largely interested in economic
affairs and anti-trust matters. He is a member of the Banking and
Currency Committee.

11. Sam Rayburn is again the Speaker of the House, having held
that position longer than any other member in history. He has never
received any briefing on CIA activities and it is not believed that he is
desirous of one. CIA matters in the Democratic leadership have always
been handled by the Majority Whip, John McCormack.

12. Olin Teague will presumably become the Chairman of the
Veterans Affairs Committee as soon as the new assignments are
announced. He is the ranking Democratic member of the Committee.

14. Frank Ikard has been selected as a member of the House
Ways and Means Committee which will handle tax matters and the
President's reciprocal trade program. The Democratic membership
of this Committee also serves as a "Committee on Committees"
selecting the Democratic membership of the various Congressional
Committees.

15. Omar Burleson is a member of the Foreign Affairs Com-
mittee and is the ranking Democrat on its Inter -American Affairs
Subcommittee. He is also a member of its Near East and Africa Sub-
committee.

16. George Mahon is the second ranking member of the House
Appropriations Committee and serves as the Chairman of its Armed
Services Appropriations Subcommittee handling the CIA budget. He
does not normally accept luncheon engagements, but likes to stop in
in the morning on the way to work. Such a morning meeting will be
advisable in the near future so that we can ascertain how he wishes
to handle the CIA budget during the coming year, as well as the pro-
blem of financing the construction of a CIA building.

17. Paul Kilday is the third ranking member of the House Armed
Services Committee and has not had much contact with CIA in recent
years; however, he has always been friendly and helpful.

18. Clark Fisher is also a member of the Armed Services Com-
mittee and was debriefed here last Fall following his return from a trip
to the Soviet Union.
