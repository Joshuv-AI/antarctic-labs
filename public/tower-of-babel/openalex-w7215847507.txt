Abstract
This article aims to present an experience report on the implementation of suspended gardens using PET bottles in
municipal schools in Capanema-PA, Brazil, in collaboration with the Municipal Secretariat of Environment (SEMMA)
and the Municipal Secretariat of Education (SEMED). Environmental education in the school context represents a
fundamental strategy for the formation of conscious and responsible citizens. The initiative integrated principles of
sustainability, solid waste reuse, and food security within a practical pedagogical context. The activities, carried out
between March and September 2025, involved informative lectures, collection of PET bottles by students, assembly of
structures, and transplanting of lettuce seedlings. The reception of the activity was extremely positive, with high student
engagement, demonstrating the potential of practical activities with an environmental interface to motivate experiential
learning and environmental education. It is expected that the gardens will be incorporated as an interdisciplinary
pedagogical resource and that the harvested products will contribute to school meals, strengthening the connection
between conscious production and consumption. The experience validates the partnership model between
environmental agencies and educational institutions in promoting environmental education and sustainability,
configuring a replicable and low-cost approach with an impact on the school and family community.
Keywords: Environmental education; School garden; Sustainability.

1. Introduction
The growing concern over global environmental challenges has driven the search for educational strategies that promote
awareness and action toward sustainability (Santos et al., 2011; Ziemmer, 2025). Within this scenario, environmental education
(EE) in the school context emerges as an essential pillar for the development of critical citizenship engaged with socioenvironmental issues (Brasil, 1999; Kopeginski & Lindino, 2019). The school, as a privileged space for formation, has the
potential to integrate theoretical and practical knowledge, stimulating reflection and behavioral change in relation to the
environment (Nepomuceno et al., 2024; Cavalcante et al., 2025).
School gardens have become established as an effective pedagogical tool for EE, providing students with a practical
understanding of natural cycles, food production, and the importance of sustainability (Penz et al., 2023; Soares, 2024). In
addition to promoting connection with nature, these spaces contribute to the development of practical skills, the strengthening
of school identity, and the promotion of food security (Silva et al., 2023; Pantoja, 2025). The integration of gardens into the
school curriculum allows for an interdisciplinary approach, connecting different areas of knowledge and making learning more
meaningful and contextualized (Silva et al., 2021; Silva; Moura & Santos, 2021).
This article aims to present an experience report on the implementation of suspended gardens using PET bottles in
municipal schools in Capanema-PA, Brazil. The initiative sought not only the reuse of solid waste but also the promotion of EE
and sustainability in a practical and participatory manner. The reuse of recyclable materials, such as PET bottles, in educational
projects is a strategy that directly addresses the problem of inadequate disposal and its environmental impacts, while stimulating
creativity and ecological awareness (Carvalho et al., 2022; Rodrigues, 2026). The experience sought to demonstrate how
partnerships between educational institutions and environmental agencies can generate positive outcomes in the formation of a
more conscious and engaged school community (Silva et al., 2020; Morgado, 2008).

3. Theoretical-conceptual framework
3.1 Environmental Education in the School Context
EE is recognized as a continuous and permanent process aimed at forming individuals and communities aware of their
environment, capable of acting individually and collectively in the search for solutions to environmental problems (Rigonat,
2002; Brasil, 1999). Within the school environment, EE transcends the mere transmission of information, seeking to promote
critical reflection, changes in values and behaviors, and the development of active and responsible citizenship (Santos et al.,
2011; Kopeginski; Lindino, 2019). The school, as a space for socialization and knowledge construction, plays a crucial role in
raising students' awareness of socio-environmental issues, encouraging the adoption of sustainable practices and understanding
of the interdependence between natural and social systems (Nepomuceno et al., 2024; Cavalcante et al., 2025).
3.2 School Gardens as a Pedagogical Tool
School gardens have stood out as a powerful pedagogical tool for implementing EE, providing a practical and
experiential learning environment (Penz et al., 2023; Ziemmer, 2025). By growing food, students develop practical skills and
understand the cycles of nature, the importance of biodiversity, and the principles of agroecology (Soares, 2024; Silva et al.,
2023). In addition, the school garden fosters interdisciplinarity, integrating content from different areas of knowledge such as
Science, Mathematics, Geography, and Portuguese Language, making learning more meaningful and contextualized (Silva et
al., 2021; Silva; Moura & Santos, 2021). The active participation of students in the cultivation process stimulates engagement,
collaboration, and a sense of responsibility, turning them into protagonists of their own learning (Nepomuceno et al., 2024; Genc,
2015).
3.3 Sustainability and Reuse of Solid Waste
Sustainability, in essence, seeks to balance present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet
their own needs (United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO), 2012; World Commission on
Environment and Development, 1988). In this context, EE is fundamental to promoting awareness of the importance of proper
solid waste management and conscious consumption (Serrano, 2003; IBGE, 2017). The reuse of materials such as PET bottles
in projects like the suspended gardens exemplifies a sustainable practice that minimizes improper disposal, reduces demand for
new resources, and stimulates the circular economy (Carvalho et al., 2022; Rodrigues, 2026). Such initiatives demonstrate that
sustainability can be addressed in a practical and innovative way in the school environment, generating positive impacts both on
the environment and on students' civic formation (Maurício, 2020; Riguetto; Rotatori & Cosenza, 2020).

4. Results and Discussion

remarkable engagement on the part of the students. Active participation in collecting PET bottles, assembling the structures, and
transplanting the seedlings generated significant enthusiasm, turning the students into protagonists of the learning process
(Nepomuceno et al., 2024). This high level of involvement corroborates the literature pointing to the motivating potential of
practical, experientially rich activities in the school context, especially when addressing environmental themes (Genc, 2015;

Furthermore, the results of the experience indicated that school gardens have great potential as an interdisciplinary
pedagogical resource. It is expected that teachers at the participating schools will incorporate the gardens into the teaching of
various subjects, such as Science, Mathematics, Geography, and Environmental Education. This interdisciplinary approach is
fundamental to building more holistic and contextualized knowledge, allowing students to understand the interconnections
between different fields of knowledge and the challenges of sustainability (Silva et al., 2023; Pombo, 2005). Several studies
show that the school garden can function as a living laboratory, where abstract concepts become concrete and meaningful for
students (Morais, 2017; Moreira et al., 2018).
In addition to the pedagogical impact, the initiative anticipates that the products harvested from the gardens will be used
in school meals, as occurred in one of the schools, where a snack was held in which the students themselves harvested the lettuce
and made their own sandwiches (Figure 7). This seeks to contribute to diversifying the menu offered in school cafeterias and to
reinforce the connection between conscious food production and consumption (Brasil, 1996).

The promotion of food and nutritional security in the school environment, combined with EE, is a strategy aimed not
only at students' health but also at forming healthier and more sustainable eating habits (Regis; Bernard & Boff, 2020; Danelon;
Danelon & Silva, 2006). The experience of growing one's own food allows students to value the production process and make
more informed food choices (Domene, 2008; Brandani et al., 2014).
The experience in Capanema-PA also highlights the potential of partnerships between municipal environmental
agencies (SEMMA) and educational institutions (SEMED) in promoting EE and sustainability. Coordination between different
sectors of society is crucial to the success of socio-environmental projects, as it enables the optimization of resources, the
exchange of knowledge, and the expansion of the reach of actions (Jacobi, 2005; United Nations, 2015). This collaborative
model, which integrates waste reuse, food production, and civic formation, constitutes a replicable, low-cost approach with a
direct impact on the school community and its family extension (Riguetto; Rotatori & Cosenza, 2020; Freitas et al., 2013). The
simplicity and effectiveness of the methodology employed make it accessible to other localities and contexts, promoting the
dissemination of sustainable practices (Carvalho, 2007; Cunha, 1985).
Furthermore, the experience reported here directly relates to the 2030 Agenda and its Sustainable Development Goals
(SDGs), established by the UN (United Nations, 2015). The reuse of PET bottles in building the suspended gardens relates to
SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production), by promoting the reuse of solid waste in the school environment; the
expectation that the harvested produce will be incorporated into meals served in the cafeterias brings the action closer to SDG 2
(Zero Hunger and Sustainable Agriculture) and SDG 3 (Good Health and Well-Being), insofar as it strengthens access to fresh
and nutritious food. The pedagogical and interdisciplinary nature of the activity, in turn, reaches SDG 4 (Quality Education), by
integrating formal content with a practical sustainability experience. Finally, the coordination between the Municipal
Government of Capanema, through SEMMA and SEMED, and the participating schools exemplifies SDG 17 (Partnerships for
the Goals), highlighting the potential of low-cost cross-sector actions for the simultaneous advancement of multiple sustainable
development targets.

5. Final Considerations
The experience of implementing suspended gardens with PET bottles in the municipal schools of Capanema-PA
demonstrates the success of an initiative that transcends mere pedagogical activity, establishing itself as an effective model of
EE and sustainability (Penz et al., 2023; Ziemmer, 2025). The combination of solid waste reuse, food production, and civic
formation not only enriched the school environment but also promoted a deep connection between students and sustainable
practices (Silva et al., 2023; Pantoja, 2025).
The results obtained reinforce the importance of experiential learning and interdisciplinarity as pillars for a
transformative EE. Student engagement, the diversification of the school menu, and the strengthening of institutional partnerships
are clear indicators of the project's positive impact (Nepomuceno et al., 2024; Santos et al., 2011). The ability to replicate this
model in other contexts, given its low cost and simplicity of execution, makes it a valuable tool for promoting environmental
awareness in various communities (Carvalho et al., 2022; Rodrigues, 2026).
In short, the suspended garden project in Capanema-PA not only contributes to forming more conscious and responsible
citizens but also serves as an inspiring example of how education can be an agent of social and environmental change, preparing
future generations for the challenges of a constantly changing world (United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural
Organization (UNESCO), 2012; World Commission on Environment and Development, 1988).
