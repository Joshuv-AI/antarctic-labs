Skyrmion Excitations in the ν = − 1 \nu=-1 Quantum Hall state in Monolayer Graphene
Jincheng An, Ajit C. Balram, Ganpathy Murthy

Abstract
We investigate the skyrmion excited states atop the S ​ U ​ ( 4 ) SU(4) quantum Hall ferromagnetic ground state at filling factor ν = − 1 \nu=-1 in monolayer graphene. The competition among short-range anisotropic interactions, the Zeeman coupling, and sublattice symmetry-breaking potential gives rise to four distinct spin-valley-ordered ground-state phases. Combining effective field theory with the Hartree–Fock approximation, we develop a variational framework that incorporates both the long-range Coulomb interaction and the symmetry-breaking terms on an equal footing. Variational minimization determines the optimal skyrmion texture, including both its spatial radial profile and internal S ​ U ​ ( 4 ) SU(4) spinor structure. We establish the phase diagram of skyrmion excitations, identify thirteen distinct skyrmion phases under different external-field conditions, and determine their spin-valley textures, energies, and characteristic sizes. The robustness of the results against different radial ansätze demonstrates the reliability of our variational approach. Our work provides a systematic framework for studying skyrmion excitations in multicomponent quantum Hall ferromagnets and can be naturally extended to more general S ​ U ​ ( 4 ) SU(4) quantum Hall systems.

QH states are the first discovered topological insulators, characterized by an incompressible bulk gap for charged excitations and gapless chiral edge modes that govern electrical and thermal transport.

The isolation of monolayer graphene in 2004

Owing to its remarkable properties, including ultrahigh carrier mobility, linear/Dirac dispersion near charge neutrality, and exceptional tunability, graphene has become the prototype of a broad family of atomically thin materials, including transition-metal dichalcogenides and various van der Waals heterostructures

8

Over the past two decades, these systems have provided an unprecedented platform for exploring QH physics. The interplay of internal degrees of freedom, interaction-driven symmetry breaking, and topological order displayed by these materials has significantly advanced our understanding of both topological phases and correlated electron phenomena. Among them, graphene has played a particularly prominent role in revealing novel QH ferromagnetic phases

Near charge neutrality, graphene’s low-energy electronic structure is governed by two inequivalent valleys,
K

K^{\prime}
, located at opposite corners of the Brillouin zone and related by time-reversal symmetry
[
7
]
. In each valley, the carriers possess a linear, Dirac-like dispersion characteristic of massless relativistic fermions. When a perpendicular magnetic field

Each LL manifold in graphene is (nearly) fourfold degenerate, reflecting the combination of two valley flavors and two spin states. Importantly, the Zeeman splitting

[

,
11
,
12

\nu=0

Motivated by the discrepancy between the theoretical prediction of a quantum spin Hall state

Although this framework was originally developed for charge neutrality, the same symmetry constraints also apply at filling factor
\nu=-1

A hallmark of QHFMs is that charged excitations may no longer be the simple particle–like or hole-like quasiparticles of Hartree–Fock (HF) theory. Instead, the lowest-energy charged objects may be skyrmions

The competition between Coulomb exchange (which favors a large, smoothly varying texture) and the Zeeman coupling (which favors a localized spin flip) determines the optimal skyrmion size and the number of spin reversals. This balance can be tuned by varying the ratio of these two energy scales and has been extensively studied using HF approaches

In graphene, the approximate

In this work, we study the skyrmion excitations above the QHFM ground states at filling factor
\nu=-1
in monolayer graphene. At
\nu=-1

E_{Z}
(Zeeman) and
E_{V}

,
29

30

. The even relative angular momentum Haldane pseudopotentials do not enter, because the single-flavor forces spatial antisymmetry. The competition between the one-body terms and the anisotropic interactions gives rise to a ground-state phase diagram comprising four distinct QHFM phases
[
29
,
30
]
. By combining effective field theory with the HF approximation, we incorporate the long-range Coulomb interaction, short-range anisotropic interactions, and external fields into a unified variational energy functional for the skyrmion order parameter. The minimization of this functional determines the optimal
SU(4)
spinor profile of the skyrmions. A systematic scan over the interaction parameter space then yields the phase diagram of the competing skyrmion states.

Consistent with previous nomenclature, we will call the region in parameter space where a particular type of skyrmion has the lowest energy a skyrmion “phase.” Note that the same ground state phase can host multiple skyrmion phases.

This system has been studied before
[
29
,
30
]
. Our purpose in revisiting skyrmions at
\nu=-1
is twofold: (i) To examine the effect of sublattice potentials on the skyrmions more carefully. It turns out that some of the skyrmion phases we find at nonzero
E_{V}
were missed in previous work
[
29
,
30
]
. Additionally, some skyrmion phases found to be distinct in previous work are found to be continuously connected in the presence a nonzero
E_{V}
. (ii) To propose and test more general ansätze for skyrmions in graphene, which we plan to use in the future at
\nu=0
. These ansätze remove infrared divergences in the variational energy, and allow us to analytically evaluate it. One of our findings is that the energy of the skyrmions is remarkably insensitive to the particular ansatz used.

This work is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we introduce the model Hamiltonian, which consists of the long-range Coulomb interaction, the short-range
SU(4)
symmetry-breaking interactions, and anisotropic external fields. In Sec.
III

\nu=-1
phase diagram obtained previously
[
29
,
30

. In Sec.
IV

V

VI

VII

II
The Hamiltonian of Monolayer Graphene

The interacting part of the Hamiltonian for monolayer graphene within the ZLL is
[
24
]

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{\mathrm{Coul}}+\hat{H}_{\text{an}},

(1)

where

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{Coul}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{e^{2}}{\varepsilon}\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{1}d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad:\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\dfrac{1}{|{\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2}|}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{r}}_{2}):

(2)

is the
SU(4)
invariant long-range Coulomb interaction. More importantly,
\hat{H}_{\text{an}}
contains the lattice-scale residual anisotropic interaction which, in conjunction with the one-body couplings, will determine the orientation of the spin-valley QHFM. Parameterized by two independent radial functions
V_{z}(r)
and
V_{x}(r)=V_{y}(r)=V_{\perp}(r)
, the anisotropic interactions take the following form

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{an}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{a=x,y,z}\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{1}d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{2}:\hat{\bm{\tau}}^{a}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})V_{a}({\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2})\hat{\bm{\tau}}^{a}({\mathbf{r}}_{2}):,

\displaystyle\quad\hat{\bm{\tau}}^{a}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})=\sum_{\alpha\gamma}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\tau^{a}_{\alpha\gamma}\hat{\psi}_{\gamma}({\mathbf{r}}_{1}),

(3)

where
\tau^{a}\equiv\tau^{a}_{\text{valley}}\otimes\sigma^{0}_{\text{spin}}
, with
a=x,y,z
, are the direct products of Pauli matrices acting on the four-dimensional spin/valley space and
\alpha,\ \gamma=K\uparrow,\ K\downarrow,\ K^{\prime}\uparrow,\ K^{\prime}\downarrow
. After expanding the creation and destruction operators in terms of the ZLL eigenstates of the non-interacting Hamiltonian in the symmetric gauge

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}})=\sum_{m}\phi_{m}({\mathbf{r}})\hat{c}_{m,\alpha},\ \phi_{m}(z)=\dfrac{(z/\ell)^{m}}{\sqrt{2\pi 2^{m}m!}\ell}e^{-\frac{z\bar{z}}{4\ell^{2}}},

(4)

the interaction can be expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{an}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{a}V^{a}_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}}:\hat{\tau}^{a}_{m_{1}m_{4}}\hat{\tau}^{a}_{m_{2}m_{3}}:,

\displaystyle V^{a}_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}}=\langle m_{1},m_{2}|V_{a}({\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2})|m_{3},m_{4}\rangle,

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}^{a}_{m_{1}m_{4}}=\sum_{\alpha\gamma}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{m_{1},\alpha}\tau^{a}_{\alpha\gamma}\hat{c}_{m_{4},\gamma}.

(5)

We Fourier transform the interaction and expand in terms of Haldane pseudopotentials
[
52
]
as

\displaystyle V_{a}({\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int d^{2}{\mathbf{q}}~e^{-i{\mathbf{q}}\cdot({\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2})}v_{a}({\mathbf{q}}),

\displaystyle v_{a}({\mathbf{q}})=\sum_{m}4\pi\ell^{2}u^{(m)}_{a}L_{m}(q^{2}\ell^{2}).

(6)

Here
u^{(m)}_{a}
are the Haldane pseudopotentials in the relative angular momentum channel
m
and
L_{m}
are Laguerre polynomials.

The interaction matrix elements
V^{a}_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}}
can be decomposed in terms of center-of-mass and relative angular momenta as
[
53
]

\displaystyle V^{a}_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}}=\sum_{m}u^{(m)}_{a}{\mathbf{U}}^{(m)}_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}},

\displaystyle{\mathbf{U}}^{(m)}_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}}=\sum_{M}\langle m_{1},m_{2}|M,m\rangle\langle M,m|m_{3},m_{4}\rangle.

(7)

For illustration, the coefficients that combine two individual angular momenta
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
into the center-of-mass
M
and relative
m
angular momenta for
m=0,1
are
[
53
]

\displaystyle\langle m,m^{\prime}\lvert m+m^{\prime},0\rangle=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2^{m+m^{\prime}}}}\sqrt{\dfrac{(m+m^{\prime})!}{m!m^{\prime}!}}

\displaystyle\langle m,m^{\prime}\lvert m+m^{\prime}-1,1\rangle=\dfrac{m-m^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2^{m+m^{\prime}}}}\sqrt{\dfrac{(m+m^{\prime}-1)!}{m!m^{\prime}!}}.

(8)

Next, we include the Zeeman and valley Zeeman terms in the Hamiltonian.

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{Z}=-E_{Z}\sum_{m}\hat{\sigma}_{m}^{z},\quad\hat{H}_{V}=-E_{V}\sum_{m}\hat{\tau}_{m}^{z},

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{m}^{z}=\sum_{\alpha\gamma}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{m,\alpha}(\tau^{0}_{\text{valley}}\otimes\sigma_{\text{spin}}^{z})_{\alpha\gamma}\hat{c}_{m,\gamma},

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}_{m}^{z}=\sum_{\alpha\gamma}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{m,\alpha}(\tau^{z}_{\text{valley}}\otimes\sigma_{\text{spin}}^{0})_{\alpha\gamma}\hat{c}_{m,\gamma}.

(9)

Throughout this work, we choose to fix the magnetic field at
B_{\perp}{=}10~\text{Tesla}
, implying a Zeeman energy of
e_{Z}{=}(1/2)g_{B}\mu_{B}B_{\perp}{=}0.58~\text{meV}
[the
g
-factor
g_{B}{=}2
for graphene]. In later phase diagrams, we will take the
e_{Z}
as the energy unit, i.e.,
e_{Z}{=}1
. The entire Hamiltonian is

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{\mathrm{Coul}}+\hat{H}_{\text{an}}+\hat{H}_{Z}+\hat{H}_{V}

(10)

III
Quantum Hall Ferromagnetic Ground States

We assume that the
\nu=-1
ground state
|\Phi\rangle
is a single Slater determinant (SSD) with translational invariance up to intervalley coherence. Any SSD is uniquely specified by its one-body averages,

\displaystyle\langle\Phi|\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{m,\beta}\hat{c}_{m^{\prime},\eta}|\Phi\rangle=\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}\Delta_{\eta\beta},

(11)

The matrix
\Delta
is simply the projector to the occupied subspace in the four-dimensional spin/valley space.
Since the Coulomb interaction is ineffective in selecting the ground state, we will ignore it in this section. Using the HF approximation, we obtain the ground state energy functional

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptE_{\text{g.s.}}[\Delta]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{N_{\phi}}\langle\Phi|\hat{H}_{\text{an}}+\hat{H}_{Z}|\Phi\rangle

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{a}\Big[u_{a,H}\text{Tr}\big(\tau^{a}\Delta\big)^{2}-u_{a,F}\text{Tr}\big(\tau^{a}\Delta\tau^{a}\Delta\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\ -E_{Z}\text{Tr}\big(\sigma^{z}\Delta\big)-E_{V}\text{Tr}\big(\tau^{z}\Delta\big),

with the Hartree and Fock couplings
[
54
]

\displaystyle u_{a,H}=\dfrac{1}{N_{\phi}}\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}}V^{a}_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{2}m_{1}}=2\sum_{m}u^{(m)}_{a},

\displaystyle u_{a,F}=\dfrac{1}{N_{\phi}}\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}}V^{a}_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{1}m_{2}}=2\sum_{m}(-1)^{m}u_{a}^{(m)},

(13)

where we have used the following properties of the interaction matrix elements
{\mathbf{U}}^{(m)}_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}}

[
28
,
27
,
54
]
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{U}}^{(m)}_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{2}m_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{m}{\mathbf{U}}^{(m)}_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{1}m_{2}},

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}}{\mathbf{U}}^{(m)}_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{2}m_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2N_{\phi}.

(14)

In the USR limit
[
24
]
,
V_{a}({\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2})\propto\delta({\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2})
, and only the lowest Haldane pseudopotential
u^{(0)}_{a}
is nonzero, thus, we will have
u_{a,H}=u_{a,F}
by Eq. (
13
) while for a generic interaction, the parameter space for the anisotropic interaction is four-dimensional
(u_{\perp,H},\ u_{\perp,F},\ u_{z,H},\ u_{z,F})
.

At filling
\nu=-1
, we need to select a particular linear combination of the four basis spinors to be occupied. In this situation, the following ansatz for the four spinors furnishes a complete parametrization of the mean-field ground-state manifold, capturing all possible competing ordered states.

\displaystyle\lvert f_{1}\rangle=\cos\dfrac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\lvert\bm{\tau},{\mathbf{s}}_{a}\rangle+\sin\dfrac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\lvert-\bm{\tau},-{\mathbf{s}}_{b}\rangle,

\displaystyle\lvert f_{2}\rangle=\cos\dfrac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\lvert\bm{\tau},-{\mathbf{s}}_{a}\rangle+\sin\dfrac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\lvert-\bm{\tau},{\mathbf{s}}_{b}\rangle,

\displaystyle\lvert f_{3}\rangle=\sin\dfrac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\lvert\bm{\tau},{\mathbf{s}}_{a}\rangle-\cos\dfrac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\lvert-\bm{\tau},-{\mathbf{s}}_{b}\rangle,

\displaystyle\lvert f_{4}\rangle=\sin\dfrac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\lvert\bm{\tau},-{\mathbf{s}}_{a}\rangle-\cos\dfrac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\lvert-\bm{\tau},{\mathbf{s}}_{b}\rangle

\displaystyle\lvert\bm{\tau}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\frac{\theta_{\tau}}{2}\\
e^{i\phi_{\tau}}\sin\frac{\theta_{\tau}}{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\lvert{\mathbf{s}}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\frac{\theta_{s}}{2}\\
e^{i\phi_{s}}\sin\frac{\theta_{s}}{2}\end{pmatrix},

(15)

where
|\bm{\tau},{\mathbf{s}}\rangle{\equiv}|\bm{\tau}\rangle{\otimes}|{\mathbf{s}}\rangle
with
\bm{\tau}
(
{\mathbf{s}}
) being unit vectors on the valley (spin) Bloch spheres. The
U(1)_{\text{valley}}
symmetry permits the choice
\phi_{\tau}=0,\pi
, which means we are working with real valley spinors. Likewise, the
U(1)_{\text{spin}}
symmetry of spin rotations around the direction of total field allows us to choose
\phi_{s}=0,\pi
, which means we can work with real spin spinors. Thus, all four four-dimensional spinors can be chosen to be real.
The projector for filling
\nu=-1
can be constructed as

\displaystyle\Delta=|f_{1}\rangle\langle f_{1}|.

(16)

With
\text{Tr}\big(\tau^{a}\Delta\tau^{a}\Delta\big)=\text{Tr}\big(\tau^{a}\Delta\big)^{2}=\langle f_{1}|\tau^{a}|f_{1}\rangle^{2}\equiv\big(\tau^{a}_{11}\big)^{2}
, the ground state energy functional of Eq. (
12
) will simplify to

\displaystyle E_{\text{g.s.}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{a}\big(u_{a,H}-u_{a,F}\big)\big(\tau^{a}_{11}\big)^{2}-E_{Z}\sigma^{z}_{11}-E_{V}\tau^{z}_{11},

(17)

which, by Eq. (

), indicates that
\nu=-1
QHFM state only responds to the interacting channels
u^{(m)}_{a}
with odd

odd. Thus, the minimal construction of the anisotropic interaction for the
\nu=-1
QHFM ground states keeps the lowest odd Haldane pseudopotential
u_{a}^{(1)}\neq 0,\ u^{(m>1)}_{a}=0

]

\displaystyle v_{a}({\mathbf{q}})=g_{a}L_{1}(q^{2}\ell^{2}).

\displaystyle u_{\perp,H}=-u_{\perp,F}=\dfrac{g_{\perp}}{2\pi\ell^{2}},\quad u_{z,H}=-u_{z,F}=\dfrac{g_{z}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}.

\nu=-1
QHFM

Now we are ready to study the skyrmion excited states variationally. We start with the following general ansatz for a skyrmion above the
\nu=-1
ground state, the projector for which is given by:

\displaystyle P^{S}({\mathbf{r}})=|\chi({\mathbf{r}})\rangle\langle\chi({\mathbf{r}})|,

\displaystyle|\chi({\mathbf{r}})\rangle=\sqrt{1-W(r)}\dfrac{x+\text{i}y}{r}|f_{1}\rangle+\sqrt{W(r)}|f_{2}\rangle,

(24)

with
W(r)
being an arbitrary smooth radial profile satisfying

\displaystyle 0\leq W(r)\leq 1,\quad W(0)=1,\quad W(\infty)=0.

(25)

This ansatz makes sure that the skyrmion reverts to ground state spinor
|f_{1}\rangle
as
r\to\infty
. A schematic representation of this skyrmion state is shown in Fig.
2
. Note that the variational parameters are the components of the spinor
|f_{2}\rangle
occupied at the center, which has to be orthogonal to
|f_{1}\rangle
, and the function
W(r)
.

The nonzero matrix elements of
P^{S}({\mathbf{r}})
in the basis
|f_{1}\rangle,\ |f_{2}\rangle
are given by

\displaystyle P^{S}_{11}({\mathbf{r}})=1-W(r),\quad P^{S}_{22}({\mathbf{r}})=W(r),

\displaystyle P^{S}_{12}({\mathbf{r}})=P^{S*}_{21}({\mathbf{r}})=\sqrt{1-W(r)}\sqrt{W(r)}\dfrac{x+\text{i}y}{r}.

(26)

The topological charge density is

\displaystyle{\varrho}({\mathbf{r}})=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\text{Tr}\big[P^{S}\partial_{\mu}P^{S}\partial_{\nu}P^{S}\big]=-\dfrac{W^{\prime}(r)}{2\pi r},

(27)

leading to the topological charge

\displaystyle Q=\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}{\varrho}({\mathbf{r}})=-W(r)|_{0}^{\infty}=1,

(28)

as required for a unit-charge skyrmion.

Figure 2:
Schematic representation of a skyrmion excited state upon the
\nu=-1
QHFM ground state. The skyrmion spinor
|\chi({\mathbf{r}})\rangle
is a linear combination between the occupied spinor
|f_{1}\rangle
and an orthogonal spinor
|f_{2}\rangle
, which is unoccupied in the ground state, but occupied at the core of the skyrmion.

We will evaluate the energy of the skyrmion using the nonlinear sigma model (NLSM), which is a long-wavelength effective theory of QHFM
[
10
]
. The total variational energy in this approach is given by

E=E_{\mathrm{stiff}}+E_{\mathrm{Coul}}+E_{\mathrm{SB}},

(29)

where
E_{\mathrm{stiff}}
is the spin/valley stiffness energy which penalizes changes in the order parameter,
E_{\mathrm{Coul}}
is the Coulomb energy, and
E_{\mathrm{SB}}
is the energy of symmetry-breaking terms including the one-body fields and the short-range anisotropic interactions. All these contributions turn out to be functionals of the NLSM order parameter
{\mathbf{P}}({\mathbf{r}})
, which is simply the projector to the occupied state at
{\mathbf{r}}
.

Since
E_{\mathrm{stiff}}
and
E_{\mathrm{Coul}}
are typically much larger than
E_{\mathrm{an}}
, let us evaluate them first.

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{stiff}}=\rho_{s}\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}\text{Tr}\big[\partial_{\mu}{\mathbf{P}}\partial_{\mu}{\mathbf{P}}\big],

(30)

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{Coul}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{e^{2}}{\varepsilon}\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{1}d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{2}\varrho({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\dfrac{1}{|{\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2}|}\varrho({\mathbf{r}}_{2}).

(31)

where
\rho_{s}=1/(16\sqrt{2\pi})~e^{2}/(\varepsilon\ell)
is the spin stiffness
[
12
]
with
\varepsilon\approx 6
being the dielectric constant of encapsulated graphene
[
55
]
.

Let us define the shape of the skyrmion that minimizes the stiffness energy to be
W_{0}(r)
. The calculation of
W_{0}(r)
is outlined in Appendix
A
, where it is shown that
[
56
,
57
,
51
,
30
,
58
]

\displaystyle W_{0}(r)=\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{r^{2}+\lambda^{2}},

(32)

where
\lambda
is the characteristic skyrmion size. This profile saturates the Bogomolńyi–Prasad–Sommerfield (BPS) bound
[
56
,
57
]
and yields the minimum NLSM stiffness energy,
E_{\mathrm{stiff}}=4\pi\rho_{s}
.

Due to the slow falloff of
W(r)
with
r
, some of the terms in the one-body and anisotropic energies are divergent in the infrared. For example, it is evident by inspection that the one-body contributions to
E_{\mathrm{an}}
are divergent in the size of the system. To remove this feature, we introduce a one-parameter family of ansätze inspired by
W_{0}(r)
,

\displaystyle W_{\xi}(r)=\bigg(\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}+r^{2}}\bigg)^{1+\xi},

(33)

where
\xi\geq 0
is a dimensionless variational parameter characterizing the deviation of our ansatz from the BPS solution. For any
\xi>0
, all the terms in the variational energy converge, whereas, by construction, at
\xi=0
we have
W_{\xi=0}(r)=W_{0}(r)
. The corresponding topological charge density is Eq. (
27
), becomes

\displaystyle\varrho_{\xi}({\mathbf{r}})=\dfrac{1+\xi}{\pi\lambda^{2}}\bigg(\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}+r^{2}}\bigg)^{2+\xi}.

(34)

In what follows, we will drop the subscript
\xi
from
W_{\xi}(r)
and
\varrho_{\xi}({\mathbf{r}})
for brevity of notation.
Substituting the order parameter in Eq. (
24
) into Eq. (
30
), the stiffness energy is

\displaystyle\quad E_{\text{stiff}}

\displaystyle=\rho_{s}\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}\bigg[\dfrac{2W(r)\Big(1-W(r)\Big)}{r^{2}}+\dfrac{W^{\prime}(r)^{2}}{2W(r)\Big(1-W(r)\Big)}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\pi\rho_{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\dfrac{2}{u}\left(\frac{1}{u+1}\right)^{\xi+2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\left(\frac{(\xi+1)^{2}u^{2}}{u+1-\left(\frac{1}{u+1}\right)^{\xi}}+u+1-\left(\frac{1}{u+1}\right)^{\xi}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\pi\rho_{s}F(\xi),

(35)

where, in the second equality, we have substituted the variational ansatz in Eq. (
33
), introduced the variable
u=r^{2}
, and set
\lambda=1
since this integral is independent of the characteristic length
\lambda
. The integral
F(\xi)
can be evaluated analytically only for integer values of
\xi
, yielding

\displaystyle F(0)=4,\quad F(1)=29/3-8\log 2,

\displaystyle F(2)=587/30-\sqrt{3}\pi-9\log 3,

\displaystyle F(3)=7039/210-4\pi-24\log 2.

(36)

For general
\xi
,
F(\xi)
is evaluated numerically and is accurately approximated by the polynomial fit

\displaystyle F(\xi)=4+0.129109\xi+0.01007\xi^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad-0.008012\xi^{3}+0.000853\xi^{4},

(37)

which, as shown in Fig.
3
, is monotonically increasing with
\xi
in the range we consider.

Figure 3:
Dependence of the dimensionless functions
F(\xi)
and
X(\xi)
on
\xi
, defined in Eqs. (
35
) and (
39
), respectively. The monotonic increase of both functions indicates that increasing
\xi
raises both the stiffness energy of the nonlinear sigma model and the Coulomb interaction energy of the skyrmion.

To evaluate the Coulomb self-energy
E_{\text{Coul}}
, we first Fourier transform the topological charge density,

\displaystyle\tilde{\varrho}({\mathbf{q}})=\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}\varrho({\mathbf{r}})e^{i{\mathbf{q}}\cdot{\mathbf{r}}}=\dfrac{(q\lambda)^{1+\xi}K_{1+\xi}(q\lambda)}{2^{\xi}\Gamma(1+\xi)},

(38)

which satisfies
\tilde{\varrho}(-{\mathbf{q}})=\tilde{\varrho}({\mathbf{q}})
. Then we have

\displaystyle E_{\text{Coul}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{e^{2}}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\tilde{\varrho}({\mathbf{q}})\tilde{\varrho}(-{\mathbf{q}})=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{e^{2}}{\varepsilon\lambda}X(\xi),

\displaystyle X(\xi)=\dfrac{\pi\Gamma(3/2+\xi)\Gamma(5/2+2\xi)}{4^{1+\xi}\Gamma^{2}(1+\xi)\Gamma(2+\xi)}.

(39)

The dimensionless function
X(\xi)
increases monotonically with
\xi
, as shown in Fig.
3
, and satisfies
X(0)=3\pi^{2}/32
.

We finally turn to the evaluation of
E_{\text{SB}}
in the context of the NLSM. Recall that
since the long-range Coulomb interaction is
SU(4)
invariant, it is unable to select the optimal occupied spinor at the center of the skrmion
|\chi(0)\rangle=|f_{2}\rangle
. We need to compute

\displaystyle E_{\text{SB}}=\langle\Psi_{s}|\hat{H}_{\text{an}}+\hat{H}_{Z}+\hat{H}_{V}|\Psi_{s}\rangle.

(40)

Furthermore, we need to express this as a functional of
{\mathbf{P}}({\mathbf{r}})
. This can be done, subject to the following conditions: (i) The scale of variation of the skyrmion should be much larger than the magnetic length, i.e.,
\lambda\gg\ell
, and (ii) The anisotropic interactions should be small
u_{a}\left(\frac{\ell}{\lambda}\right)^{2}\ll\rho_{s}
. The details are presented
in Appendix.
B
. We reproduce the relevant equations from Appendix
B
[Eq. (
A34
)] here:

\displaystyle E_{\text{SB}}=\dfrac{A}{2\pi\ell^{2}}E_{\text{g.s.}}+\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\bigg[\dfrac{{\mathcal{E}}_{1}}{2\xi}+\dfrac{{\mathcal{E}}_{2}}{2(1+2\xi)}\bigg].

(41)

Note that
A
is the area of the system. The first term is divergent but harmless, since it represents the ground state energy, which will be subtracted from the total energy to find the excitation energy of the skyrmion.
{\mathcal{E}}_{1},\ {\mathcal{E}}_{2}
are functionals of
|f_{2}\rangle
, their explicit forms are derived in Appendix
B
[see Eqs. (
A36
) and (
A37
)], and reproduced here.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{1}=\big(u_{a,H}-u_{a,F}\big)\Big[\tau^{a}_{12}\tau^{a}_{21}+\tau^{a}_{11}\tau^{a}_{22}-\big(\tau^{a}_{11}\big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\ +E_{Z}\big(\sigma^{z}_{11}-\sigma^{z}_{22}\big)+E_{V}\big(\tau^{z}_{11}-\tau^{z}_{22}\big),

(42)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\big(u_{a,H}-u_{a,F}\big)\Big[\big(\tau^{a}_{11}-\tau^{a}_{22}\big)^{2}-2\tau^{a}_{12}\tau^{a}_{21}\Big],

(43)

where
\tau^{a}_{ij}=\langle f_{i}|\tau^{a}|f_{j}\rangle
. Note that the appearance of
u_{a,H}-u_{a,F}
as prefactors in these expressions shows that the even angular momentum Haldane pseudopotentials do not affect the energy of the skyrmions.

After collecting all the contributions, we find the energy of the variational skyrmion.

\displaystyle E_{\text{skyr}}=\pi\rho_{s}F(\xi)+\dfrac{e^{2}X(\xi)}{2\epsilon\lambda}+\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\bigg[\dfrac{{\mathcal{E}}_{1}}{2\xi}+\dfrac{{\mathcal{E}}_{2}}{2(1+2\xi)}\bigg],

(44)

The excitation energy of the skyrmion has no infrared divergence for
\xi>0
, as promised.

Qualitatively, the stiffness and Coulomb energies increase with
\xi
, whereas the anisotropic contribution decreases with
\xi
. Thus, the optimal configuration with the lowest energy will have a nonzero
\xi
, as we will see in the next section.

Skyrmion Phases

) are hatched with red ‘+’ symbols, phases with

are hatched with blue ‘x’ symbols, and phases with

are hatched with green ‘\\’ symbols. The unhatched phases at

do not have quantized

. In such skyrmion phases, the value of

varies continuously as couplings are varied.

By minimizing the skyrmion energy functional of Eq. (
44
) over the parameter space
(g_{\perp},g_{z})
, we get all possible skyrmion excited states above the various
\nu=-1
QHFM ground states. It should be noted that a particular QHFM ground state can have multiple types of skyrmions, depending on the
SU(4)
orientation of the central spinor
|f_{2}\rangle
. We choose the lowest energy skyrmion out of these possibilities to build the skyrmion phase diagrams shown in Fig.
4
.
To distinguish the various skyrmion phases quantitatively with a simple measure, we use the discriminator proposed previously
[
29
,
30
]
.
\Xi
is constructed from the relative orientations of the spin and valley order parameters at the center of the skyrmion and at infinity. It is defined as follows

\displaystyle\ \Xi=\dfrac{\vec{\tau}_{0}\cdot\vec{\tau}_{\infty}}{|\vec{\tau}_{0}||\vec{\tau}_{\infty}|}-\dfrac{\vec{\sigma}_{0}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{\infty}}{|\vec{\sigma}_{0}||\vec{\sigma}_{\infty}|},

\displaystyle\vec{\tau}_{\infty}=\langle\chi(\infty)|\vec{\tau}|\chi(\infty)\rangle=\big(\tau^{x}_{11},\tau^{y}_{11},\tau^{z}_{11}\big),

\displaystyle\vec{\tau}_{0}=\langle\chi(0)|\vec{\tau}|\chi(0)\rangle=\big(\tau^{x}_{22},\tau^{y}_{22},\tau^{z}_{22}\big),

\displaystyle\vec{\sigma}_{\infty}=\langle\chi(\infty)|\vec{\sigma}|\chi(\infty)\rangle=\big(\sigma^{x}_{11},\sigma^{y}_{11},\sigma^{z}_{11}\big),

\displaystyle\vec{\sigma}_{0}=\langle\chi(0)|\vec{\sigma}|\chi(0)\rangle=\big(\sigma^{x}_{22},\sigma^{y}_{22},\sigma^{z}_{22}\big)

(45)

As can be seen later, in the case that both
|f_{1}\rangle\ \&\ |f_{2}\rangle
are direct product states of valley and spin Bloch vectors,
\Xi=-2
corresponds to a complete valley flip,
\Xi=2
corresponds to a complete spin flip, while
\Xi=0
corresponds to completely flipping both valley and spin.

V.1
Skyrmions in Phase A

We first consider the phase-A QHFM background of Eq. (
20
), with the spinor
|K,\uparrow\rangle
occupied, which implies that the spin and valley polarizations at infinity are

\displaystyle\vec{\tau}^{A}_{\infty}=\vec{\sigma}^{A}_{\infty}=\big(0,\ 0,\ 1\big).

(46)

We find four different skyrmions in phase A, labeled by A-S1, A-S2, A-S3, & A-S4, each with a different central spinor. As exhibited in Fig.
1
(a)&(b), skyrmions A-S1, A-S2, and A-S3 are the lowest energy skyrmions in certain regions of the couplings even when
E_{V}=0
. However, it is only when
E_{V}>0
that the A-S4 skyrmion becomes the lowest energy configuration in a tiny region of couplings. Here are the central spinors corresponding to each type of skyrmion for the QHFM ground state A.

•

A-S1 has the central spinor

\displaystyle|f_{2}^{\text{A-S1}}\rangle=|K^{\prime},\uparrow\rangle.

(47)

This indicates a complete valley flip (as labeled by
\Xi^{\text{A-S1}}=-2
in Fig.
1
).

•

A-S2 has the central spinor

\displaystyle|f_{2}^{\text{A-S2}}\rangle=|K,\downarrow\rangle.

(48)

This indicates a complete spin flip (as labeled by
\Xi^{\text{A-S2}}=2
in Fig.
1
).

•

A-S3 has the central spinor

\displaystyle|f_{2}^{\text{A-S3}}\rangle=|K^{\prime},\downarrow\rangle.

(49)

This indicates a complete flip in both valley and spin (as labeled by
\Xi^{\text{A-S3}}=0
in Fig.
1
).

•

A-S4 only appears at
E_{V}\neq 0
and has the central spinor

\displaystyle|f_{2}^{\text{A-S4}}\rangle=\cos\dfrac{\alpha}{2}|K,\downarrow\rangle+\sin\dfrac{\alpha}{2}|K^{\prime},\uparrow\rangle,

(50)

where the nontrivial angle
\alpha
can only be obtained numerically. The resulting valley and spin polarizations are

\displaystyle\vec{\tau}_{0}^{\text{A-S4}}=-\vec{\sigma}_{0}^{\text{A-S4}}=\big(0,\ 0,\ \cos\alpha\big),

(51)

leading to
\Xi^{\text{A-S4}}=2\cos\alpha/|\cos\alpha|=\pm 2
depending on the sign of
\cos\alpha
. The skyrmion phase A-S4 appears to have been missed in previous work
[
29
,
30
]
.

V.2
Skyrmions in Phase B

Now we consider the QHFM background to be phase B, Eq. (
21
), with the occupied spinor
|\bm{\tau},\uparrow\rangle
. This leads to the following spin and valley polarizations at infinity

\displaystyle\vec{\tau}^{B}_{\infty}=\big(\sin\theta_{\tau},\ 0,\ \cos\theta_{\tau}\big),\quad\vec{\sigma}^{B}_{\infty}=\big(0,\ 0,\ 1\big),

(52)

we find four different skyrmions labeled by B-S1, B-S2, B-S3 and B-S4. As seen in Fig.
1
(a)&(b), the first three skyrmion phases are seen even for
E_{V}=0
, whereas the skyrmion phase B-S4 is only stabilized for
E_{V}>0
. Here are the central spinors of the various skyrmion phases:

•

B-S1 has the central spinor

\displaystyle|f_{2}^{\text{B-S1}}\rangle=|-\bm{\tau},\uparrow\rangle.

(53)

This represents a complete valley flip (labeled by
\Xi^{\text{B-S1}}=-2
in Fig.
1
).

•

B-S2 has the central spinor

\displaystyle|f_{2}^{\text{B-S2}}\rangle=|\bm{\tau},\downarrow\rangle,

(54)

which indicates a complete valley flip (as labeled by
\Xi^{\text{B-S2}}=2
in Fig.
1
).

•

B-S3 has the central spinor

\displaystyle|f_{2}^{\text{B-S3}}\rangle=|-\bm{\tau}^{\prime},\downarrow\rangle,\ \lvert-\bm{\tau}^{\prime}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\sin\frac{\theta_{\tau^{\prime}}}{2}\\
-\cos\frac{\theta_{\tau^{\prime}}}{2}\end{pmatrix},

(55)

where
\theta_{\tau^{\prime}}
can only be determined numerically. We find that
\theta_{\tau^{\prime}}=\theta_{\tau}=\frac{\pi}{2}
at
E_{V}=0
. The resulting valley and spin polarizations are

\displaystyle\vec{\tau}_{0}^{\text{B-S3}}=-\big(\sin\theta_{\tau^{\prime}},\ 0,\ \cos\theta_{\tau^{\prime}}\big),

\displaystyle\vec{\sigma}_{0}^{\text{B-S3}}=\big(0,\ 0,\ -1\big),

leading to

. When

as shown in Fig.

(a), representing a complete flip in both valley and spin.

B-S4 only appears at

and has the central spinor

\displaystyle|f_{2}^{\text{B-S4}}\rangle=\cos\dfrac{\alpha}{2}|-\bm{\tau},\uparrow\rangle+\sin\dfrac{\alpha}{2}|\bm{\tau}^{\prime},\downarrow\rangle,

(57)

where
\alpha
and
\theta_{\tau^{\prime}}
can only be determined numerically. The resulting valley and spin polarizations are

\displaystyle\vec{\tau}_{0}^{\text{B-S4}}=\big(\sin^{2}\dfrac{\alpha}{2}\sin\theta_{\tau^{\prime}}-\cos^{2}\dfrac{\alpha}{2}\sin\theta_{\tau},\ 0,

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \sin^{2}\dfrac{\alpha}{2}\cos\theta_{\tau^{\prime}}-\cos^{2}\dfrac{\alpha}{2}\cos\theta_{\tau}\big),

\displaystyle\vec{\sigma}_{0}^{\text{B-S4}}=\big(\sin\alpha\sin(\theta_{\tau}-\theta_{\tau^{\prime}}),\ 0,\ \cos\alpha\big).

(58)

We will not present the expression for

[

30
]

V.3
Skyrmions in Phase C

Next, we consider skyrmion excitations above phase C of the QHFM, with the occupied spinor
\cos\dfrac{\alpha}{2}|\bm{\tau},\uparrow\rangle-\sin\dfrac{\alpha}{2}|-\bm{\tau},\downarrow\rangle
[Eq. (
22
)]. The spin and valley polarizations at infinity are

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\vec{\tau}^{C}_{\infty}=\cos\alpha\big(\sin\theta_{\tau},\ 0,\ \cos\theta_{\tau}\big),\ \vec{\sigma}^{C}_{\infty}=\big(0,\ 0,\ \cos\alpha\big),

(59)

where
\alpha
and
\theta_{\tau}
in

are given in Eq. (

The lowest energy skyrmion has the central spinor

\displaystyle|f_{2}^{\text{C-S}}\rangle=\cos\dfrac{\theta}{2}\Big(\sin\dfrac{\alpha}{2}|\bm{\tau},\uparrow\rangle+\cos\dfrac{\alpha}{2}|-\bm{\tau},\downarrow\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\sin\dfrac{\theta}{2}\Big(\sin\dfrac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}|-\bm{\tau},\uparrow\rangle+\cos\dfrac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}|\bm{\tau},\downarrow\rangle\Big),

(60)

where

can only be determined numerically. This is generically true when

, the angles acquire special values, and the central spinors become simpler. This leads to the skyrmion phases C-S1 and C-S2 shown in Fig.

(a) and (b).

•

C-S1 only occurs at
E_{V}=0
, with the angles taking the values

\displaystyle|f_{2}^{\text{C-S1}}\rangle=\sin\dfrac{\alpha}{2}|\hat{e}_{x},\uparrow\rangle+\cos\dfrac{\alpha}{2}|-\hat{e}_{x},\downarrow\rangle.

(61)

This implies the valley and spin polarizations at the center

\displaystyle\vec{\tau}_{0}^{\text{C-S1}}=\big(-\cos\alpha,\ 0,\ 0\big),\ \vec{\sigma}_{0}^{\text{C-S1}}=\big(0,\ 0,\ -\cos\alpha\big),

(62)

thus the resulting
\Xi^{\text{C-S1}}=0
.

•

C-S2 also only occurs at
E_{V}=0
, with the angles taking the special values
\theta=\pi,\ \bm{\tau}=\hat{e}_{x}
, leading to the central spinor

\displaystyle|f_{2}^{\text{C-S2}}\rangle=\sin\dfrac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}|-\hat{e}_{x},\uparrow\rangle+\cos\dfrac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}|\hat{e}_{x},\downarrow\rangle.

(63)

This implies the valley and spin polarizations at the center

\displaystyle\vec{\tau}_{0}^{\text{C-S1}}=\big(\cos\alpha^{\prime},\ 0,\ 0\big),\ \vec{\sigma}_{0}^{\text{C-S1}}=\big(0,\ 0,\ -\cos\alpha^{\prime}\big),

(64)

resulting in
\Xi^{\text{C-S2}}=-2
.

It should be noted that there are other skyrmions in phase C. However, since they never have the lowest energy for any set of couplings, we ignore them.

VI
Robustness with respect to the function
W(r)

The specific form of the radial profile
W(r)
is not unique. Indeed, the variational ansatz introduced in the main text represents a particular one-parameter family of skyrmion textures that satisfy the boundary conditions in Eq. (
25
). More generally, any radial profile that interpolates smoothly between
W(0)=1
and
W(\infty)=0
is physically admissible. The detailed functional form of
W(r)
is expected to be determined by the competition among the various energy contributions. Since one needs an infinite number of parameters to specify a function, it is not possible to do the calculation for a completely general
W(r)
. However, we can formulate the variational problem in a form that is independent of any particular choice of ansatz. Since
W(r)
is dimensionless, whereas
r
carries the dimension of length, dimensional analysis implies that the most general implicit form of
W(r)
can always be expressed as

\displaystyle W(r)=W(r;\lambda,\vec{\xi}),

where
\lambda
is the characteristic length needed to balance the dimension of
r
, while
\vec{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},...)
denotes an, in general, infinite set of dimensionless variational parameters characterizing the function
W(r)
. Regardless of the specific functional form of
W(r)
, dimensional analysis determines the scaling of each contribution to the skyrmion energy functional,

Stiffness and Coulomb energies:

\displaystyle\quad\quad E_{\text{stiff}}\sim\rho_{s}F(\vec{\xi}),\quad E_{\text{Coul}}\sim\dfrac{e^{2}}{\varepsilon\lambda}X(\vec{\xi}),

Symmetry breaking energy:

\displaystyle\quad\quad E_{\text{SB}}\sim\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\bigg[{\mathcal{E}}_{1}Y_{1}(\vec{\xi})+{\mathcal{E}}_{2}Y_{2}(\vec{\xi})\bigg].

(74)

where all functions about
\vec{\xi}
are dimensionless and are given by

\displaystyle\pi F(\vec{\xi})=\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}\bigg[\dfrac{2W\big(1-W\big)}{r^{2}}+\dfrac{W^{\prime 2}}{2W\big(1-W\big)}\bigg],

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\lambda}X(\vec{\xi})=\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{1}d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{2}\dfrac{W^{\prime}(r_{1})}{2\pi r_{1}}\dfrac{1}{|{\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2}|}\dfrac{W^{\prime}(r_{2})}{2\pi r_{2}},

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}Y_{1}(\vec{\xi})=\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}W,\quad\lambda^{2}Y_{2}(\vec{\xi})=\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}W^{2}.

(75)

We find that the optimal skyrmion is relatively insensitive to the choice of different functional forms for the radial ansatz
W(r)
. To illustrate this, we compare the results obtained using Eq. (
33
) with integer values of
\xi
, as well as those obtained from other functional forms of
W(r)
not contained in the one-parameter family
W_{\xi}(r)
.

VII
Conclusion and Outlook

Quantum Hall ferromagnetism
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
is a spontaneous symmetry-breaking phenomenon which occurs whenever there are internal degrees of freedom, and whenever a manifold of Landau levels is partially full. Initially discovered in 2D electron gases confined in semiconductor heterostructures, in graphene, it leads to a plethora of possible phases due to the approximate
SU(4)
symmetry of each Landau level manifold.

A characteristic of quantum Hall ferromagnets is that they can support extended spin/valley textured excitations called skyrmions. Due to the spin-charge relation in a Landau level, their charge is related to their topological winding number. In addition to being of intrinsic interest, skyrmions can be detected by experimental probes
[
31
,
32
,
33
,
59
,
60
]
, and can be used to distinguish ground state phases.

In this work, we have investigated skyrmion excitations above the
SU(4)
quantum Hall ferromagnetic ground states at filling factor
\nu=-1
in monolayer graphene
[
29
,
30
]
. At this filling, the fourfold nearly-degenerate zeroth Landau level supports a rich set of spin-valley-ordered phases generated by the competition among short-range anisotropic interactions, the Zeeman coupling, and sublattice symmetry-breaking fields. To describe the associated charged topological excitations, we combine an effective field-theory treatment of the long-range Coulomb interaction with a Hartree–Fock description of the symmetry-breaking terms. This approach yields a unified variational energy functional for skyrmion textures, incorporating the spin stiffness, electrostatic self-energy, anisotropy energy, and external-field contributions. By minimizing this functional over the unconstrained variational parameters and the internal
SU(4)
spinor structure, we determine the energetically favored skyrmion configurations and establish their phase diagram across the anisotropic-interaction parameter space.

In the variational calculation, we parameterize the skyrmion by the spatially varying spinor

\displaystyle|\chi({\mathbf{r}})\rangle=\sqrt{1-W(r)}\dfrac{x+\text{i}y}{r}|f_{1}\rangle+\sqrt{W(r)}|f_{2}\rangle.

The variational degrees of freedom naturally separate into radial and internal components. The radial profile
W(r)
, satisfying the boundary conditions in Eq. (
25
), controls the spatial profile of the skyrmion and hence its characteristic size. The spinors
|f_{1}\rangle\&|f_{2}\rangle
determine the orientation of the skyrmion in the internal valley-spin
SU(4)
space.

To construct a physically motivated variational ansatz for
W(r)
, we exploit the fact that the nonlinear sigma model (NLSM) contribution
E_{\text{stiff}}
is the dominant and
SU(4)
-invariant component of the skyrmion energy. We first determine analytically the radial profile
W_{0}(r)
that minimizes
E_{\text{stiff}}
[
29
,
30
,
61
]
(see Appendix
A
). Since this solution saturates the Bogomolńyi–Prasad–Sommerfield (BPS) bound, we refer to it as the BPS profile.

A disadvantage of the BPS profile is that it has certain anisotropic contributions to the skyrmion energy that diverge with the system size. To solve this problem, and to give us an additional variational parameter, we deform the BPS profile and generate a one-parameter family of radial profiles

\displaystyle W_{\text{0}}(r)=\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{r^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\quad\rightarrow\quad W_{\xi}(r)=\bigg(\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}+r^{2}}\bigg)^{1+\xi},

where the deformation parameter
\xi
accounts for the effects of the Coulomb interaction and the symmetry-breaking terms beyond the NLSM.

By variationally minimizing the skyrmion energy functional atop the four different
\nu=-1
QHFM ground state phases
[
29
,
30
]
, we identify thirteen distinct skyrmion states across the anisotropic-interaction parameter space and under different external-field conditions, which are summarized in Table
1
. For some of these skyrmion configurations, the ground state spinor
|f_{1}\rangle
, and the spinor at the skyrmion core
|f_{2}\rangle
can be expressed as direct products of a valley Bloch vector and a spin Bloch vector. In these cases, the skyrmion texture can be readily interpreted in terms of valley flips, spin flips, or simultaneous flips of both. Such skyrmions are characterized by quantized values of the quantity
\Xi
defined in Eq. (
45
), which measures the relative orientations of the spin and valley polarizations between
|f_{1}\rangle
and
|f_{2}\rangle

[
29
,
30
]
. More generally, however,
|f_{1}\rangle
and
|f_{2}\rangle
take spin-valley-entangled forms that cannot be decomposed into direct products of valley and spin states. In this regime, the valley and spin sectors are no longer independent, and the skyrmion texture represents a genuine
SU(4)
excitation involving coherent spin-valley mixing. We find that this occurs when the sublattice potential
E_{V}
is nonzero. In this case, we also find two new skyrmion phases (A-S4 and B-S4) that seem to have been missed in previous works
[
29
,
30
]
.

We have also examined the robustness of the variational approach with respect to the choice of the radial profile
W(r)
. Besides the continuous parametrization
W_{\xi}(r)
adopted in Eq. (
33
), we considered both its restriction to integer-valued deformation parameters and an alternative ansatz proposed in Refs.
[
29
,
30
]
. In all cases, the resulting skyrmion energies, optimal spinor configurations, and phase diagrams are in excellent quantitative agreement over the entire parameter space, with energy differences below 1% and only negligible shifts of the phase boundaries. These results demonstrate that the physical properties of the variationally optimized skyrmions are insensitive to the specific choice of radial ansatz, thereby establishing the robustness and generality of our variational framework.

Although the variational framework developed in this work is tailored to skyrmion excitations above the
\nu=-1
QHFM ground states in monolayer graphene, the overall strategy for constructing the variational energy functional is considerably more general. In particular, it can be naturally extended to the
\nu=0
quantum Hall regime, where the competition among anisotropic interactions gives rise to a much richer family of spin-valley-entangled QHFM phases
[
25
,
62
,
63
,
64
]
. The more intricate internal structure of these ground states is expected to support a wider variety of skyrmion textures with novel topological and symmetry properties beyond those identified here.

An even more intriguing direction is the extension to fractional QHFM phases in graphene
[
65
,
27
,
28
]
. In this case, the long-range Coulomb interaction, strong correlation effects, and internal
SU(4)
symmetry are expected to compete and cooperate in a highly nontrivial manner, potentially leading to fractional skyrmions with rich internal structures and unconventional properties. The variational framework developed here provides a promising starting point for systematically investigating these topological excitations and their phase diagrams in multicomponent fractional quantum Hall systems
[
66
,
67
,
68
,
69
]
.

Appendix A
Minimization of
E_{\text{stiff}}

Recall that the total variational energy of a skyrmion configuration has three parts,

E_{\text{skyr}}=E_{\text{stiff}}+E_{\text{Coul}}+E_{\text{an}},

(A1)

the nonlinear sigma model stiffness energy
E_{\text{stiff}}
, the Coulomb energy
E_{\text{C}}
, and the energy of the
SU(4)
symmetry-breaking parts of the Hamiltonian
E_{\text{an}}
, which contain the one-body fields as well as the anisotropic short-range interactions.
Typically,
E_{\text{stiff}}
provides the leading contribution to the total skyrmion energy. Following previous work, we first find the optimal skyrmion configuration that minimizes
E_{\text{stiff}}

[
51
]
, and subsequently consider deformations away from this optimal configuration to minimize other terms. Since the projector to the occupied state (also the order parameter)
{\mathbf{P}}({\mathbf{r}})
is positive semi-definite, we construct the following operator

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}({\mathbf{r}})=\sqrt{{\mathbf{P}}({\mathbf{r}})}\partial{\mathbf{P}}({\mathbf{r}}),\ \Rightarrow\ {\mathbf{A}}^{\dagger}({\mathbf{r}})=\bar{\partial}{\mathbf{P}}({\mathbf{r}})\sqrt{{\mathbf{P}}({\mathbf{r}})},

(A2)

where
\partial=\partial_{x}-i\partial_{y},\ \bar{\partial}=\partial_{x}+i\partial_{y}
. By construction, one obtains the inequality

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\big[{\mathbf{A}}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{A}}\big]=\text{Tr}\big[\bar{\partial}{\mathbf{P}}{\mathbf{P}}\partial{\mathbf{P}}\big]\geq 0.

(A3)

By virtue of the relation
{\mathbf{P}}={\mathbf{P}}^{2}
, valid for
\nu=-1
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}{\mathbf{P}}=\partial_{\mu}{\mathbf{P}}^{2}={\mathbf{P}}\partial_{\mu}{\mathbf{P}}+\big(\partial_{\mu}{\mathbf{P}}\big){\mathbf{P}}.

(A4)

Then the inequality stated in Eq. (
A3
) can be explicitly expressed as

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\big[\partial_{\mu}{\mathbf{P}}\partial_{\mu}{\mathbf{P}}\big]+2i\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\text{Tr}\big[{\mathbf{P}}\partial_{\mu}{\mathbf{P}}\partial_{\nu}{\mathbf{P}}\big]\geq 0.

(A5)

Upon integration over the two-dimensional space, this inequality yields the NLSM energy,
E_{\text{stiff}}
of Eq. (
30
), and the associated topological charge given in Eqs. (
27
)-(
28
), leading to the celebrated Bogomolńyi–Prasad–Sommerfield (BPS) bound
[
51
,
30
,
58
,
56
,
57
]
,

\displaystyle E_{\text{stiff}}\geq 4\pi\rho_{s}|Q|.

(A6)

E_{\text{stiff}}
reaches its minimum,
E_{\text{stiff}}=4\pi\rho_{s}|Q|
when

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}\partial{\mathbf{P}}=0\ \Leftrightarrow\ \bar{\partial}{\mathbf{P}}{\mathbf{P}}=0.

(A7)

The simplest skyrmionic solution with
Q=1
to Eq. (
A7
) is given by
[
51
,
70
]

\displaystyle P^{S}({\mathbf{r}})=|\chi({\mathbf{r}})\rangle\langle\chi({\mathbf{r}})|,\quad|\chi({\mathbf{r}})\rangle=\dfrac{z|f_{1}\rangle+\lambda|f_{2}\rangle}{\sqrt{r^{2}+\lambda^{2}}},

(A8)

with
\lambda
being the characteristic length of the skyrmion. The function
W(r)
defined in Eq. (
24
) for the BPS state/solution is

\displaystyle W_{0}(r)=\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{r^{2}+\lambda^{2}}.

(A9)

Appendix B
Evaluation of Symmetry-Breaking Terms

In this Appendix, we evaluate the contributions to the skyrmion excited energy from the symmetry-breaking terms. When performing the Hartree-Fock approximation for the two-body anisotropic interacting terms in Eq. (
3
) with an SSD state
|\Psi\rangle
, we decompose the average of four-fermi terms as the product of two-fermi averages:

\displaystyle\langle\Psi|\hat{H}_{\text{an}}|\Psi\rangle

(A10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{a=x,y,z}\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{1}d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{2}V_{a}({\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2})\tau^{a}_{\alpha\gamma}\tau^{a}_{\beta\eta}

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\langle\Psi|\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\hat{\psi}_{\gamma}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\beta}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})\hat{\psi}_{\eta}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})|\Psi\rangle,

\displaystyle\quad-\langle\Psi|\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\hat{\psi}_{\eta}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\beta}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})\hat{\psi}_{\gamma}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})|\Psi\rangle\bigg].

To carry this out, one needs to evaluate the general one-body density matrix in the resulting Fock terms,

\displaystyle\quad\langle\Psi|\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\hat{\psi}_{\eta}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}\phi^{*}_{m}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\phi_{m^{\prime}}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})\underbrace{\langle\Psi|\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{m,\alpha}\hat{c}_{m^{\prime},\eta}|\Psi\rangle}_{\equiv{\mathcal{M}}_{m,\alpha;m^{\prime},\eta}}.

(A11)

To proceed, we construct a one-body operator in the LLL using the matrix elements
{\mathcal{M}}
defined above

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha\eta}=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}|m\rangle{\mathcal{M}}_{m,\alpha;m^{\prime},\eta}\langle m^{\prime}|.

(A12)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\langle\Psi|\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\hat{\psi}_{\eta}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})|\Psi\rangle=\langle{\mathbf{r}}_{1}|\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha\eta}|{\mathbf{r}}_{2}\rangle.

(A13)

Let us introduce the LLL coherent state
[
71
]
,

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{R}}\rangle=\sum_{m}\sqrt{2\pi\ell^{2}}\phi^{*}_{m}({\mathbf{R}})|m\rangle.

(A14)

Using this, one can construct the LLL projector,

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}=\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}|{\mathbf{R}}\rangle\langle{\mathbf{R}}|,

(A15)

the matrix elements of which are given by

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{r}}_{1}|\hat{\Pi}|{\mathbf{r}}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}\langle{\mathbf{r}}_{1}|{\mathbf{R}}\rangle\langle{\mathbf{R}}|{\mathbf{r}}_{2}\rangle

(A16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{mm^{\prime}}\int d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}\phi_{m}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\phi^{*}_{m}({\mathbf{R}})\phi_{m^{\prime}}({\mathbf{R}})\phi^{*}_{m^{\prime}}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\phi_{m}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\phi^{*}_{m}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2\pi\ell^{2}}\text{Exp}\Big[-\dfrac{|{\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2}|^{2}}{4\ell^{2}}+i\dfrac{({\mathbf{r}}_{1}\times{\mathbf{r}}_{2})_{z}}{2\ell^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Pi({\mathbf{r}}_{1},{\mathbf{r}}_{2}).

Accordingly, the wave function of the coherent state can be expressed as

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt\langle{\mathbf{r}}|{\mathbf{R}}\rangle=\sqrt{2\pi\ell^{2}}\sum_{m}\phi_{m}({\mathbf{r}})\phi^{*}_{m}({\mathbf{R}})=\sqrt{2\pi\ell^{2}}\Pi({\mathbf{r}},{\mathbf{R}}).

(A17)

Since the operator
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
acts within the LLL, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{M}}=\hat{\Pi}\hat{\mathcal{M}}\hat{\Pi},

(A18)

or more explicitly,

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{r}}_{1}|\hat{\mathcal{M}}|{\mathbf{r}}_{2}\rangle

(A19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}^{\prime}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}\langle{\mathbf{r}}_{1}|{\mathbf{R}}\rangle\langle{\mathbf{R}}|\hat{\mathcal{M}}|{\mathbf{R}}^{\prime}\rangle\langle{\mathbf{R}}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}^{\prime}\Pi({\mathbf{r}}_{1},{\mathbf{R}})\dfrac{\langle{\mathbf{R}}|\hat{\mathcal{M}}|{\mathbf{R}}^{\prime}\rangle}{2\pi\ell^{2}}\Pi({\mathbf{R}}^{\prime},{\mathbf{r}}_{2}).

Given that the interactions in Eq. (
A10
) are short ranged, namely,
|{\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2}|\approx\ell
and the LLL kernels
\Pi({\mathbf{r}}_{1},{\mathbf{R}})\ \&\ \Pi({\mathbf{R}}^{\prime},{\mathbf{r}}_{2})
are localized within approximately a magnetic length around
{\mathbf{r}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{r}}_{2}
respectively, the double convolution is dominated by
{\mathbf{R}}\approx{\mathbf{R}}^{\prime}
. We can therefore ignore the spatial dependence of the matrix elements of
{\mathcal{M}}
in the coherent state basis, i.e.

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{r}}_{1}|\hat{\mathcal{M}}|{\mathbf{r}}_{2}\rangle=\int d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}\Pi({\mathbf{r}}_{1},{\mathbf{R}}){\mathbf{P}}({\mathbf{R}})\Pi({\mathbf{R}},{\mathbf{r}}_{2}),

(A20)

where

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}({\mathbf{R}})=\langle{\mathbf{R}}|\hat{\mathcal{M}}|{\mathbf{R}}\rangle

(A21)

is the local order parameter. We are assuming that
{\mathbf{P}}
varies very slowly on the scale of the magnetic length when it comes to evaluating the anisotropic energy, that is,
\ell\ll\lambda
. The terms we drop will come with derivatives of
{\mathbf{P}}
, and in principle will correct the stiffness energy, but since the anisotropic couplings are small, we ignore these corrections. Since first derivatives in space cannot occur due to rotational invariance, we are assuming that

u_{a}\left(\frac{\ell}{\lambda}\right)^{2}\ll\rho_{s},

(A22)

a condition easily satisfied for realistic samples.

Now we go to the center-of-mass(COM) and relative coordinates,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{R}}_{c}=\dfrac{{\mathbf{r}}_{1}+{\mathbf{r}}_{2}}{2},\ {\mathbf{s}}={\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2},

(A23)

whence we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\Psi|\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\hat{\psi}_{\eta}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}\Pi({\mathbf{R}}_{c}+\dfrac{{\mathbf{s}}}{2},{\mathbf{R}}){\mathbf{P}}_{\eta\alpha}({\mathbf{R}})\Pi({\mathbf{R}},{\mathbf{R}}_{c}-\dfrac{{\mathbf{s}}}{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(2\pi\ell^{2})^{2}}\text{Exp}\Big[-\dfrac{|{\mathbf{s}}|^{2}}{8\ell^{2}}+i\dfrac{({\mathbf{s}}\times{\mathbf{R}}_{c})_{z}}{2\ell^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\times\int d^{2}{\mathbf{u}}{\mathbf{P}}_{\eta\alpha}({\mathbf{R}}_{c}+{\mathbf{u}})\text{Exp}\Big[-\dfrac{|{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}}{2\ell^{2}}+i\dfrac{({\mathbf{s}}\times{\mathbf{u}})_{z}}{2\ell^{2}}\Big].

Note that we have made the replacement
{\mathbf{u}}\equiv{\mathbf{R}}-{\mathbf{R}}_{c}
.
As mentioned above, assuming
\ell\ll\lambda
, we approximate
{\mathbf{P}}_{\eta\alpha}({\mathbf{R}}_{c}+{\mathbf{u}})={\mathbf{P}}_{\eta\alpha}({\mathbf{R}}_{c})
, which leads to

\displaystyle\quad\langle\Psi|\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\hat{\psi}_{\eta}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi\ell^{2}}{\mathbf{P}}_{\eta\alpha}({\mathbf{R}}_{c})\text{Exp}\bigg[{-\dfrac{|{\mathbf{s}}|^{2}}{4\ell^{2}}}+i\dfrac{({\mathbf{s}}\times{\mathbf{R}}_{c})_{z}}{2\ell^{2}}\bigg].

(A24)

The point of this exercise is to express the expectation value
\langle\Psi|\hat{H}_{\text{an}}|\Psi\rangle
as a functional of the local order parameter
{\mathbf{P}}
.

More explicitly, given a skyrmion state with a local order parameter

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{s}|\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}})\hat{\psi}_{\gamma}({\mathbf{r}})|\Psi_{s}\rangle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi\ell^{2}}P^{S}_{\gamma\alpha}({\mathbf{r}}),

(A25)

the expectation value of the anisotropic interaction Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle E_{\text{an}}=\langle\Psi_{s}|\hat{H}_{\text{an}}|\Psi_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\dfrac{1}{2}\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{1}d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}_{2}V_{a}({\mathbf{r}}_{1}-{\mathbf{r}}_{2})\tau^{a}_{\alpha\gamma}\tau^{a}_{\beta\eta}\Big[\dfrac{P^{S}_{\gamma\alpha}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})}{2\pi\ell^{2}}\dfrac{P^{S}_{\eta\beta}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})}{2\pi\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\ -\langle\Psi_{s}|\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})\hat{\psi}_{\eta}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})|\Psi_{s}\rangle\langle\Psi_{s}|\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\beta}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})\hat{\psi}_{\gamma}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})|\Psi_{s}\rangle\Big].

In the Hartree term above, using the fact that the anisotropic interactions are nonzero only up to a few
\ell
and that
\ell\ll\lambda
, we can replace the density-density interacting term
P^{S}({\mathbf{r}}_{1})P^{S}({\mathbf{r}}_{2})
by
P^{S}({\mathbf{R}}_{c})^{2}
. Similarly, in the Fock term, we use Eq. (
A24
) for
|\Psi_{s}\rangle
. Consequently,

\displaystyle E_{\text{an}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{s}}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}V_{a}({\mathbf{s}})\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}_{c}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}\text{Tr}\Big[P^{S}({\mathbf{R}}_{c})\tau^{a}\Big]^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{s}}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}V_{a}({\mathbf{s}})e^{-\frac{|{\mathbf{s}}|^{2}}{2\ell^{2}}}\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}_{c}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}\text{Tr}\Big[P^{S}({\mathbf{R}}_{c})\tau^{a}P^{S}({\mathbf{R}}_{c})\tau^{a}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\dfrac{1}{2}u_{a,H}\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}_{c}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}\Big[\big(P^{S}_{11}\tau^{a}_{11}\big)^{2}+2P^{S}_{12}\tau^{a}_{21}P^{S}_{21}\tau^{a}_{12}+2P^{S}_{11}\tau^{a}_{11}P^{S}_{22}\tau^{a}_{22}+\big(P^{S}_{22}\tau^{a}_{22}\big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\ -\dfrac{1}{2}u_{a,F}\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}_{c}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}\Big[\big(P^{S}_{11}\tau^{a}_{11}\big)^{2}+2P^{S}_{12}\tau^{a}_{22}P^{S}_{21}\tau^{a}_{11}+2P^{S}_{11}\tau^{a}_{12}P^{S}_{22}\tau^{a}_{21}+\big(P^{S}_{22}\tau^{a}_{22}\big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\dfrac{1}{2}\big(u_{a,H}-u_{a,F}\big)\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{R}}_{c}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}\bigg[(\tau^{a}_{11})^{2}\big(1-W(R_{c})\big)^{2}+2(\tau^{a}_{11}\tau^{a}_{22}+\tau^{a}_{12}\tau^{a}_{21})W(R_{c})\big(1-W(R_{c})\big)+(\tau^{a}_{22})^{2}W^{2}(R_{c})\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\dfrac{1}{2}\big(u_{a,H}-u_{a,F}\big)\big(\tau^{a}_{11}\big)^{2}\dfrac{\pi\Lambda^{2}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}+\big(u_{a,H}-u_{a,F}\big)\Big[\tau^{a}_{11}\tau^{a}_{22}+\tau^{a}_{12}\tau^{a}_{21}-\big(\tau^{a}_{11}\big)^{2}\Big]\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\ell}\Big)^{2}Y_{1}(\xi)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\dfrac{1}{2}\big(u_{a,H}-u_{a,F}\big)\Big[\big(\tau^{a}_{11}-\tau^{a}_{22}\big)^{2}-2\tau^{a}_{12}\tau^{a}_{21}\Big]\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\ell}\Big)^{2}Y_{2}(\xi),

(A26)

where we have introduced a cutoff for the divergent integral
\int\frac{d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}=\frac{\pi\Lambda^{2}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}\equiv\frac{A}{2\pi\ell^{2}}=N_{\phi}
, where
A
is the area of the system. We have also defined

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\ell}\Big)^{2}Y_{1}(\xi)=\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}W(r)=\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\dfrac{1}{2\xi},

(A27)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\ell}\Big)^{2}Y_{2}(\xi)=\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}W^{2}(r)=\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\dfrac{1}{2(1+2\xi)}.

(A28)

In the first equality of both the equations above,
W(r)
is generic, whereas in the second equality we assume it is the
W_{\xi}(r)
defined in Eq. (
33
).

Recall that the Hartree and Fock couplings are given by

\displaystyle u_{a,H}\equiv\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{s}}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}V_{a}({\mathbf{s}})=\dfrac{v_{a}(0)}{2\pi\ell^{2}},

\displaystyle u_{a,F}\equiv\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{s}}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}V_{a}({\mathbf{s}})e^{-\frac{|{\mathbf{s}}|^{2}}{2\ell^{2}}}=\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{q}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}v_{a}({\mathbf{q}})e^{-\frac{q^{2}\ell^{2}}{2}},

(A29)

which agrees with Eq. (
13
) for the explicit form of
v_{a}({\mathbf{q}})
in Eq. (
6
). As in the ground-state energy functional, Eq. (
17
), the anisotropic-interaction contribution to the skyrmion energy functional depends only on the difference between the Hartree and Fock couplings,
(u_{a,H}-u_{a,F})
, indicating the interactions in the even angular momentum channels do not contribute to the skyrmion energy
[
12
,
10
,
36
,
37
]
. In particular, the USR contact interaction does not contribute.
The 1-body Hamiltonian representing the external fields takes the following form in real space

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{Z}=-E_{Z}\int{d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}})\big(\tau^{0}_{\text{valley}}\otimes\sigma^{z}_{\text{spin}}\big)_{\alpha\gamma}\hat{\psi}_{\gamma}({\mathbf{r}}),

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{V}=-E_{V}\int{d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}})\big(\tau^{z}_{\text{valley}}\otimes\sigma^{0}_{\text{spin}}\big)_{\alpha\gamma}\hat{\psi}_{\gamma}({\mathbf{r}}).

(A30)

The Zeeman energy of the skyrmion is

\displaystyle\quad\langle\Psi_{s}|\hat{H}_{Z}|\Psi_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle=-E_{Z}\int\dfrac{d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}\Big[P^{S}_{11}({\mathbf{r}})\sigma^{z}_{11}+P^{S}_{22}({\mathbf{r}})\sigma^{z}_{22}\Big].

(A31)

Using the explicit ansatz of Eq. (
33
) for
W_{\xi}(r)
, we get

\displaystyle\quad\langle\Psi_{s}|\hat{H}_{Z}|\Psi_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle=-E_{Z}\sigma^{z}_{11}\dfrac{\pi\Lambda^{2}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}+E_{Z}\big(\sigma^{z}_{11}-\sigma^{z}_{22}\big)\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\ell}\Big)^{2}Y_{1}(\xi),

(A32)

where
Y_{1}(\xi)
is given in Eq. (
A27
).

Similarly, the valley Zeeman energy is

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{s}|\hat{H}_{V}|\Psi_{s}\rangle=-E_{V}\tau^{z}_{11}\dfrac{\pi\Lambda^{2}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}+E_{V}\big(\tau^{z}_{11}-\tau^{z}_{22}\big)\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\ell}\Big)^{2}Y_{1}(\xi).

The expressions in Eq. (
A31
) and (
B
) are valid for generic
W(r)
as indicated in Eq. (
75
).

After collecting Eqs. (
A26
), (
A31
) and (
B
), we arrive at the expression for the evaluation of all
SU(4)
symmetry-breaking terms,

\displaystyle E_{\text{SB}}=\langle\Psi_{s}|\hat{H}_{\text{an}}+\hat{H}_{Z}+\hat{H}_{V}|\Psi_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\dfrac{\pi\Lambda^{2}}{2\pi\ell^{2}}E_{\text{quad}}+\dfrac{\lambda^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\bigg[\dfrac{{\mathcal{E}}_{1}}{2\xi}+\dfrac{{\mathcal{E}}_{2}}{2(1+2\xi)}\bigg],

(A34)

where

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07182ptE_{\text{quad}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\big(u_{a,H}-u_{a,F}\big)\big(\tau^{a}_{11}\big)^{2}-E_{Z}\sigma^{z}_{11}-E_{V}\tau^{z}_{11},

(A35)

which is exactly
E_{\text{g.s.}}
in Eq. (
17
) and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{1}=\big(u_{a,H}-u_{a,F}\big)\Big[\tau^{a}_{12}\tau^{a}_{21}+\tau^{a}_{11}\tau^{a}_{22}-\big(\tau^{a}_{11}\big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\ +E_{Z}\big(\sigma^{z}_{11}-\sigma^{z}_{22}\big)+E_{V}\big(\tau^{z}_{11}-\tau^{z}_{22}\big),

(A36)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\big(u_{a,H}-u_{a,F}\big)\Big[\big(\tau^{a}_{11}-\tau^{a}_{22}\big)^{2}-2\tau^{a}_{12}\tau^{a}_{21}\Big],

(A37)

which are functionals of the spinor in the skyrmion core
|\chi(0)\rangle=|f_{2}\rangle
.
