Design of a slotted bowtie dual-polarized antenna for Sub-6 GHz multi-service wireless applications
Authors: Saranya Matta, Sambhudutta Nanda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The manuscript presents the design and experimental validation of a dual-band, dual-polarized slotted bowtie antenna for sub-6 GHz wireless applications. The proposed antenna utilizes a 2-stage work approach, where stage-1 features a trapezoidal slot-engineered bowtie radiators arranged orthogonally on a FR4 substrate and excited through a balanced feeding configuration, along with a 100 mm × 100 mm finite metallic reflector placed at an optimized distance of 28.4 mm in stage-2, facilitating reflector-assisted dual-band operation with improved radiation performance. The proposed antenna operates at frequencies of 2.52 GHz and 4.42 GHz, with measured impedance bandwidths of approximately 220 MHz and 420 MHz, respectively. The proposed antenna achieves high port isolation levels of 40 dB and 33.5 dB, with isolation surpassing 30 dB throughout the operational bands. The design sustains cross-polarization levels beneath − 15.8 dB and a front-to-back ratio exceeding 23 dB. The incorporation of the reflector significantly enhances the antenna gain from an average of 2 dBi to 7.14 dBi, with a peak measured gain of 7.6 dBi and 6.63 dBi at the two operating frequencies. The observations show that the proposed antenna is appropriate for sub-6 GHz multi-service wireless systems since it offers a compact and efficient solution for dual-band, dual-polarized operation with high isolation and gain, along with enhanced radiation characteristics.

Introduction

The demand for compact, lightweight, dual-polarized antennas with high isolation, low cross-polarization, stable radiation patterns, and enhanced forward gain has grown with the expansion of sub-6 GHz wireless access systems. These requirements are particularly critical for access points, small cells, and infrastructure platforms supporting multiple coexisting services. Bowtie antennas remain attractive among planar radiators due to their simple geometry, ease of fabrication, and suitability for polarization diversity.

The proposed antenna, though not tailored to a specific 5G NR band, exhibits an upper resonance near 4.42 GHz within the sub-6 GHz spectrum relevant to enhanced mobile broadband and private cellular networks. Its lower resonance around 2.52 GHz aligns closely with the widely used 2.4 GHz ISM band, enabling support for WLAN, IoT applications and small-cell or access-point deployments requiring dual-band and dual-polarized operation. This dual-band response makes it suitable for multi-standard sub-6 GHz architectures. Furthermore, slot-loading techniques in antenna design have proven effective for modifying current distribution, introducing additional resonant modes, and enhancing design flexibility without increasing antenna size.

Recent studies have observed advanced antenna design techniques to improve performance in modern wireless systems. Hybrid optimization and deep learning-based approaches have been explored for effective resource allocation in MIMO-enabled networks1. Furthermore, it has been claimed that antenna designs with metamaterial-based structures and frequency selective surfaces (FSS) increase gain and decrease mutual coupling2,3. CPW-fed structures and circularly polarized MIMO antennas have been designed for millimetre-wave and wideband applications4–6. In the meanwhile, slot-based and reflector-backed antenna topologies have been investigated to improve radiation characteristics and facilitate multiband behaviour7,8. Nevertheless, the majority of these studies concentrate on millimetre-wave or broadband systems that have greater structural complexity. In contrast, the suggested approach uses a straightforward planar slot-engineered bowtie radiator with reflector-assisted resonance to accomplish regulated dual-band dual-polarized functioning.

Various methods have been explored to enhance antenna bandwidth. Approaches such as slot-loaded radiators, multilayer parasitic elements, bent microstrip lines, and defective ground structures9–12 improve impedance bandwidth but often lead to multilayer designs, structural complexity, or fabrication challenges. Similarly, techniques including hybrid couplers, stacked patches, orthogonal feeding networks, and multi-element designs13–17 have been proposed to improve port isolation, though most require intricate feeding arrangements or achieve only moderate isolation (14–22 dB).

Gain enhancement strategies for bowtie and dipole antennas have also been reported12,15,18–21, with typical gains ranging from 1.5 dBi to 3.3 dBi. Radiation efficiency improvements using multilayer substrates, coplanar waveguide feeds, or embedded structures14,16,22,23 generally remain below 70%, often compromising antenna profile, fabrication simplicity, or scalability for wireless applications deployment.

Cross-polarization suppression is another critical requirement for dual-polarized antennas. Techniques such as cavity-backed structures, parasitic loading, balun-based feeding, and magneto-electric dipoles24–28 have achieved levels below − 20 dB, but typically rely on complex feed networks or bulky cavity structures.

Slotted bowtie antennas have attracted significant interest for their broadband characteristics and planar design29–32. Slot loading increases effective electrical length and aperture area, thereby improving bandwidth and radiation performance without enlarging the physical size. However, most reported designs support only single linear polarization, limiting their suitability for modern sub-6 GHz 5G wireless systems.

Reflector integration is a well-established method to reduce backward radiation and enhance gain, front-to-back ratio, and isolation. Cavity-backed and reflector-assisted designs13,33–39 demonstrate improved directionality and isolation, though often at the expense of bandwidth or structural simplicity.

Most reported dual-polarized bowtie, loop, and crossed-dipole antennas achieve enhanced performance through complex multilayer structures, intricate feeding networks, or bulky cavity-backed arrangements. However, there remains a need for a structurally simple and easily fabricated dual-polarized antenna that can simultaneously provide adequate impedance bandwidth, high port isolation, enhanced front-to-back ratio, low cross-polarization, and stable gain for sub-6 GHz multi-service wireless systems. To address this need, this work presents a systematic slot-engineered modification of a bowtie-based radiator. The proposed antenna uses a reflector-assisted resonance mechanism in conjunction with trapezoidal slot-engineered bowtie elements, enabling controlled dual-band operation while preserving a simple planar configuration.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

Unlike conventional loop cross-dipole antennas, which are typically designed to achieve broadband behaviour through loop-based resonators, the proposed antenna structure (Novelty) achieves controlled dual-band operation through the interaction between slot-tuned bowtie radiators and an optimally spaced reflector, a reflector-assisted mechanism that introduces a higher-order resonance while improving gain and front-to-back ratio, a balanced feeding configuration achieving high port isolation and reduced cross-polarization, and comprehensive experimental validation and parametric analysis demonstrating stable radiation characteristics.

Antenna design

Antenna design methodology and evolution

The proposed dual-port, dual-band sub-6 GHz planar slotted bowtie dipole antenna is developed through a two-stage methodology: a developmental stage followed by a concluding stage. Final dimensions are determined via systematic parametric sweeps and manual refinement based on electromagnetic insight, rather than automated numerical optimization.

During the developmental stage, fabrication proceeds in two phases. In the primary phase, Antenna-A—a planar slotted bowtie printed on a substrate with permittivity 4.4 and thickness 0.8 mm—is realized. The trapezoidal slot configuration is selected to achieve resonance at 2.6 GHz, the principal operating band of the radiator.

The slotted bowtie represents an advanced form of the traditional dipole, incorporating slots within its triangular elements. One coaxial line is placed (C1)/2 from the patch centre, and the second at (C2)/2, both parallel to the centreline. One arm of the bowtie dipole connects to the coaxial feed’s outer conductor, while the other connects to the shorting pin. By linking the inner conductor to two shorting pins, enhanced port isolation is achieved. This balanced feed is implemented using two symmetric feed points along with shorting pins, which ensure equal and opposite current distribution on the bowtie arms. This balanced excitation suppresses common-mode currents and improves polarization purity, impedance matching, radiation efficiency, and pattern symmetry.

Antenna-C is realized by coupling a square metal reflector to Antenna-B with an optimized gap (H-dimension). Design sensitivity analysis and parametric study

A detailed parametric analysis was performed utilizing full-wave electromagnetic simulations to examine the effect of critical geometrical parameters on the impedance response, gain characteristics, and dual-band functionality of the proposed antenna. This study investigates the impact of ground-plane dimensions, reflector spacing, slot tapering, and dielectric substrate properties on antenna design. The findings offer valuable insights into the operating mechanisms and optimization strategies, contributing to a more effective approach to antenna development.

Effect of ground size (G)

By changing the ground dimensions from 20 to 100 mm, as illustrated in Fig. 11, the impact of ground plane size on antenna gain is investigated. Smaller ground diameters (20 mm to 40 mm) result in poorer gain throughout the operational bands because they do not adequately control backward radiation. Gain and forward radiation are improved by increasing the ground size to 60 mm. Further enlargement to 80 mm and 100 mm only produces a slight improvement; the 100 mm × 100 mm ground plane offers the greatest and most consistent gain. This suggests that a ground size of 100 mm is the best compromise between compactness and gain increment.Fig. 11Comparison of gain versus frequency for different ground plane (G) sizes.

Dielectric substrate impact

An examination of the dielectric substrate was conducted to investigate the effect of substrate permittivity on antenna parameters, as depicted in Fig. 13. It was observed that substrates with lower dielectric constants, such as Teflon and Rogers RT5880, shifted the resonant frequencies toward higher-frequency regions, whereas higher-permittivity substrates such as FR4 moved the resonances toward lower frequencies. Enhanced impedance matching was noted for substrates with higher permittivity, with FR4 displaying the most pronounced resonance features. Despite these variations, all investigated substrates maintained dual-band behaviour, indicating that the primary operating mechanism is predominantly governed by the radiator–reflector interaction rather than substrate material alone. Therefore, the dielectric substrate mainly influences frequency tuning and impedance matching characteristics.Fig. 13Comparison of S11 versus frequency for different dielectric materials (plot created by the authors).

Effect of reflector spacing (H)

A parametric examination of the influence of radiator-reflector spacing (H) on impedance characteristics was conducted by altering the distance from 5 to 30 mm, as depicted in Fig. 14. At smaller spacing values (5–10 mm), strong electromagnetic coupling suppressed the higher-order resonance, resulting in single-band behaviour around 2.5 GHz. As the spacing increased to 15–20 mm, the second resonant mode gradually appeared, indicating the onset of dual-band operation. The achievement of clear dual-band characteristics with enhanced impedance matching was observed at spacing values ranging from 25 to 30 mm. Furthermore, it was noted that as the height (H) increases, the upper resonant frequency shifts toward lower frequencies. This phenomenon can be attributed to variations in the effective current path and the coupling mechanism. The optimum performance was obtained at H = 28.4 mm, where resonances occurred at 2.52 GHz and 4.42 GHz with enhanced impedance matching and stable radiation characteristics.Fig. 14Comparison of S11 versus frequency for different heights (H) between radiator and reflector (plot created by the authors).

The radiation characteristics were evaluated under far-field conditions in an anechoic chamber to minimize environmental reflections. During the measurements, a standard horn antenna was used as the transmitting antenna, while the proposed antenna served as the receiving antenna. The separation between the transmitting and receiving antennas was maintained at 2.2 m to satisfy far-field conditions. The antenna under test was mounted on a rotating positioner, and measurements were carried out over 360° to obtain radiation patterns in the principal planes.

The introduction of the reflector significantly influences the impedance bandwidth of the antenna. For Antenna-B (without reflector), the − 10 dB bandwidth is approximately 439.91 MHz, corresponding to a fractional bandwidth of about 17.6% at 2.52 GHz. In contrast, for Antenna-C (with reflector), the bandwidth reduces to approximately 220 MHz, resulting in a fractional bandwidth of about 8.7%.

This reduction in bandwidth is primarily due to the increased electromagnetic coupling between the radiator and the reflector, which enhances radiation directivity and front-to-back ratio but also leads to stronger resonance confinement. Despite the reduced bandwidth, the antenna maintains adequate coverage for sub-6 GHz wireless applications, where improved gain, high isolation, and stable radiation characteristics are critical. This reflects a typical design trade-off between bandwidth and radiation performance.

There is a discernible difference between the measured and simulated values especially in the return-loss magnitude and gain. The main causes of this phenomenon, which is frequently observed in slot-loaded antennas, include fabrication tolerances in the slot geometry, feed-connector parasitics, FR4 substrate property uncertainties, measurement errors pertaining to chamber conditions and antenna alignment could affect the outcomes. Additionally, Large trapezoidal slots and an ideal radiator-to-reflector separation are two features of the suggested antenna that are extremely sensitive to minute dimensional changes. However, the measured findings verify that the antenna meets the impedance matching requirements for realistic wireless applications within the intended frequency bands.

While aluminium sheets were utilized for manufacturing because they were more readily available, copper was used to represent the reflector in simulations. Both materials have very little effect on antenna radiation characteristics because of their strong electrical conductivity at sub-6 GHz frequencies. The strong similarity between the measured and simulated results attests to this.

Conclusion

A dual-band, dual-polarized slotted bowtie antenna has been proposed and experimentally validated and well-suited for sub-6 GHz multi-service wireless systems, such as WLAN, IoT networks, and access-point platforms, particularly in environments where polarization diversity, interference mitigation, high isolation, stable radiation patterns, and dual-polarized operation are essential, such as dense wireless deployments. The antenna employs two orthogonally arranged slotted bowtie dipoles with a balanced feeding structure, along with a finite 100 mm × 100 mm metallic reflector to enhance radiation performance and enable dual-band operation. The proposed antenna achieves resonances at 2.52 GHz and 4.42 GHz, with high port isolation (40 dB and 33.5 dB), maintains cross-polarization levels below − 15.8 dB, and a front-to-back ratio exceeding 23 dB. The incorporation of the reflector significantly improves the antenna gain from approximately 2 dBi to a peak value of 7.6 dBi, while maintaining stable radiation characteristics. The outcomes verify that the proposed design provides a successful trade-off between dual-band operation, performance, and simplicity, making it suitable for practical sub-6 GHz wireless communication systems.
