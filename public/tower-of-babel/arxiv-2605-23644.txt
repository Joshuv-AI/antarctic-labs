Balanced intersection size distributions in projective planes
Zoltán Lóránt Nagy, Zsuzsa Weiner

Abstract
Given a point set S S in a projective plane Π q \Pi_{q} of order q q , each line ℓ \ell determines a secant size | S ∩ ℓ | |S\cap\ell| . We study how balanced the secant-size distribution can be for the line set ℒ \mathcal{L} of the plane, in other words, how many lines must share the same secant size. We show that min S ⊆ Π q ⁡ max k | { ℓ ∈ ℒ : | ℓ ∩ S | = k } | = Θ ⁡ ( q 3 / 2 ) . \min_{S\subseteq\Pi_{q}}\max_{k}|\{\ell\in\mathcal{L}:|\ell\cap S|=k\}|=\Theta(q^{3/2}). This shows a large contrast with the case of real projective (or affine) plane, where max k > 1 ⁡ | { ℓ ∈ ℒ : | ℓ ∩ S | = k } | \max_{k>1}|\{\ell\in~\mathcal{L}:|\ell\cap S|=k\}| is always at least the third of | { ℓ ∈ ℒ : | ℓ ∩ S | > 1 } | |\{\ell\in\mathcal{L}:|\ell\cap S|>1\}| . We also discuss explicit constructions in addition to randomized point sets, that are asymptotically close to be optimal, and point out a link between the constructions and character-sum estimates. Finally, we explore the relation between balanced secant size distributions and legitimate colorings, studied by Alon and Füredi, and prove a result that might resemble the Erdős-Faber-Lovász conjecture.



1
Introduction

A recurring theme in combinatorics and incidence geometry is to understand how a set is distributed
with respect to a family of test objects, such as lines, subspaces or in general, hyperedges of a hypergraph defined on the groundset.
In this paper, we consider finite point sets of finite projective planes and investigate how evenly the sizes of their intersection with the lines of the plane can be distributed.

Let
S
be a finite point set in an affine or projective plane with point set
\mathcal{P}
and line set
\mathcal{L}
. For each
k
, let
L_{k}
denote the set of lines intersecting
S
in exactly
k
points, that is,

L_{k}=L_{k}(S):=\{\ell\in\mathcal{L}:|\ell\cap S|=k\}.

First, we briefly discuss the Euclidean setting for comparison. Since we consider finite point sets, this is equivalent to the projective setting over the reals.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
S
be a finite non-collinear set of points in the real projective plane.
For each integer
k\geq 2
, let
us write

x_{k}:=|L_{k}|,

then

\max\left\{\frac{x_{2}}{\sum_{k\geq 2}x_{k}.},\frac{x_{3}}{\sum_{k\geq 2}x_{k}.}\right\}>\frac{1}{3}.

Proof.

We use Melchior’s inequality for real line arrangements. In its dual formulation
(for point sets in the real projective plane), it asserts that

x_{2}\;\geq\;3+\sum_{k\geq 4}(k-3)x_{k},

(1)

see
[
20
,
26
,
29
]
.
Note that this in turn resolved the famous question of Sylvester and Gallai on the number of
2
-secants.
Since
k-3\geq 1
for all
k\geq 4
, it follows immediately that

x_{2}\;\geq\;\sum_{k\geq 4}x_{k}.

Thus either
x_{2}>x_{3}
and then
x_{2}/\sum x_{k}>1/3
, or
x_{2}\leq x_{3}
and then
x_{3}/\sum x_{k}>1/3
.
∎

As a consequence, this setting always forces very strong concentration concerning the intersection sizes.
Note that a closely related problem is to minimize the number of
2
-secants, which is the subject of the celebrated Dirac-Motzkin conjecture, resolved by Green and Tao
[
15
]
.

We will point out a different kind of concentration in the finite projective setting. In order to do this, we introduce some definitions.

Definition 1.2

(
k
-secants)
.

Let
\Pi_{q}
denote a projective plane of order
q
with point set
\mathcal{P}
and line set
\mathcal{L}
.
A line
\ell
in
\mathcal{L}
is called a

k
-secant of a set

S
if
|\ell\cap S|=k
. In the cases
k=0
and
k=1
, these lines are usually called
skew
lines and
tangent
lines (to
S
), respectively.

Let us denote by
n_{\ell}:=|S\cap\ell|
the intersection size of
\ell
with
S
, i.e., the secant size w.r.t.
\ell
, and let
L_{k}(S)
denote the set of
k
-secant lines, i.e.,
L_{k}(S)=\{\ell\in\mathcal{L}:n_{\ell}=k\}
.

Problem 1.3
.

Determine

\min_{S\subseteq\Pi_{q}}\max_{k}\{|L_{k}(S)|\},

in other words, determine the best possible lower bound on the maximum frequency of secant sizes for point sets of a projective plane of order
q
.

Our main result is

Theorem 1.4
.

For any set
S\subseteq\Pi_{q}
, the most common secant size appears at least
\Omega(q^{3/2})
times. This bound is best possible, in particular,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}q^{3/2}-3q\leq\min_{S\subseteq\Pi_{q}}\max_{k}\{|L_{k}(S)|\}\leq(1+o(q^{-\frac{1}{2}}))\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}q^{3/2}.

Observe that the lower and upper bounds differ in a multiplicative constant smaller than
1.382
.

Our main problem investigates the discrepancy of the secant size distribution; more precisely, it measures how close the secant size distribution can be to the uniform distribution over
[0,q+1]
.
The distribution of secant sizes is closely related to various central problems in finite geometry, or in a broader context, combinatorics. The most notable examples include the characterizations of blocking sets or
k
-fold blocking sets, where
0
-secants, and all
t
-secants for
t<k
are forbidden, respectively, and
(k,n)
-arcs, where all
t
-secants for
t>k
are forbidden. Various configurations can be characterized by having only
very few
secant sizes, such as subplanes or unitals, which have many important applications in extremal graph theory or Ramsey theory
[
11
,
25
]
. In fact, avoiding a single secant size can raise challenges, we refer to the theory of untouchable (or tangent-free) sets
[
6
,
7
]
or
[
19
]
for the general case. In a sequel paper, we also discuss the largest possible number of secants with the same size, in terms of the size of the set
[
28
]
.

Determining the smallest possible maximum frequency of secant sizes is also related to the concept of legitimate colorings, introduced by Rosa, and studied by Alon and Füredi
[
3
]
. A
legitimate coloring of a hypergraph
is a coloring of the vertex set by
k
colors such that all hyperedges are distinguishable by their color distribution, in other words, by their multiplicity list
c_{1}(H),c_{2}(H),\ldots c_{n}(H)
, where
c_{i}(H)
denotes the number of vertices in hyperedge
H
colored with the
i
-th color. The minimum number of colors for which a legitimate coloring of the hypergraph exists is the
legitimate coloring number
. Alon and Füredi initiated the study of this coloring number by considering the hypergraph defined by the lines of a projective plane. Observe that a natural obstacle to having a small legitimate coloring number is when the distribution of
c_{i}
is very unbalanced in every coloring. This corresponds exactly to the distribution of secant sizes when one considers projective planes. We will revisit this link in Section
4
.

The celebrated direction problem
[
4
,
8
]
is also closely related to the question posed above. Indeed,
the non-determined directions of a point set in
\Pi_{q}
of size
q
correspond to slopes on which the point set is equidistributed. For the case of point sets of arbitrary size, we refer to

[
14
,
23
]
.

Our paper is organised as follows. In Section 2 we prove our main result, Theorem 1.4. To prove the lower bound, we point out how strongly the standard equations influence the distribution of secant sizes, preventing it from being very uniform. The upper bound relies on a randomised construction. Section 3 is devoted to the presentation of explicit constructions using low-degree polynomials: parabolas and elliptic curves. These point sets will not be extremal w.r.t.
\min_{S\subseteq\Pi_{q}}\max_{k}\{|L_{k}(S)|\}
, but are expected to be not far off, and have links to intensively studied problems in number theory, namely character sum estimates. Finally, we discuss the connection between the legitimate colorings of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
and the main result of the paper and prove a result which can be viewed as the legitimate coloring version of the Erdős-Faber-Lovász conjecture.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.4

We prove the lower and upper bounds separately.

2.1
Proof of the lower bound of Theorem
1.4

We start by proving a variance formula for the secant sizes of an arbitrary point set. This essentially originates from Bruen
[
10
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Pi_{q}
be a projective plane of order
q
and let
S\subset\Pi_{q}
be a set of points.
For each line
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
, let
n_{\ell}
denote the number of points of
S
on
\ell
and let
\mu
be the average number of points on a line, i.e.,

\mu:=\frac{1}{q^{2}+q+1}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}n_{\ell}
.

Then

\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}(n_{\ell}-\mu)^{2}=q|S|\left(1-\frac{|S|}{q^{2}+q+1}\right).

Proof.

Let
N:=q^{2}+q+1
denote the total number of lines.
Let us recall the standard equations:

\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}n_{\ell}=|S|(q+1),\ \ \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}n_{\ell}(n_{\ell}-1)=|S|(|S|-1).

(2)

It follows that

\mu=\frac{\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}n_{\ell}}{N}=\frac{|S|(q+1)}{N}

and

\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}n_{\ell}^{2}=|S|(|S|-1)+|S|(q+1)=|S|(|S|+q),

hence

\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}(n_{\ell}-\mu)^{2}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}n_{\ell}^{2}-2\mu\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}n_{\ell}+N\mu^{2}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}n_{\ell}^{2}-N\mu^{2}=|S|(|S|+q)-\frac{|S|^{2}\,(q+1)^{2}}{N}.

(3)

∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Pi_{q}
be a projective plane of order
q
,
N:=q^{2}+q+1
denote the total number of lines, and let
S
be a set of points in
\Pi_{q}
. Then there exists an integer
k
such that

\frac{N^{3/2}}{\sqrt{12q|S|(1-\frac{|S|}{N})+N}}\leq|L_{k}(S)|.

Proof.

Let
M
denote the maximum cardinality of the sets of
k
-secants,

M:=\max_{k\in\{0,\dots,q+1\}}|L_{k}(S)|.

Let us bound from below the variance

V:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}(n_{\ell}-\mu)^{2}=q|S|\left(1-\frac{|S|}{N}\right),

where
\mu=\frac{\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}n_{\ell}}{N}=\frac{|S|(q+1)}{N}
is the average intersection size.
Write
\mu=\lfloor\mu\rfloor+\alpha
with
\alpha\in[0,1)
.
For each integer
j\geq 0
, consider the pairs

U_{j}:=\{\lfloor\mu\rfloor-j,\lfloor\mu\rfloor+1+j\}.

Their squared distances from
\mu
satisfy

(\alpha+j)^{2}+(1-\alpha+j)^{2}=2\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+2\left(\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\geq 2\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}.

Define
t
and
r
by the Euclidean division

N=2Mt+r,\qquad 0\leq r<2M.

Then the variance is minimized when
|L_{k}|=M
for
k\in\bigcup_{j=0}^{t-1}U_{j}

and
\sum_{k\in U_{t}}|L_{k}|=r
. Observe that
(\alpha+t)^{2}
and
(1-\alpha+t)^{2}
are both at least
t^{2}
.
Hence

2M\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+rt^{2}\leq V.

Using

2\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}(2j+1)^{2}=\frac{t(4t^{2}-1)}{6},

we get

\frac{Mt(4t^{2}-1)}{6}+rt^{2}\leq V.

Recalling that
2M>r
, a straightforward calculation shows that

\frac{Mt(4t^{2}-1)}{6}+rt^{2}-\left(\frac{N^{3}}{12M^{2}}-\frac{13N}{12}\right)=\frac{6Mt(4M^{2}-r^{2})+r(13M^{2}-r^{2})}{12M^{2}}\geq 0.

Therefore

\frac{N^{3}}{12M^{2}}-\frac{13N}{12}\leq V,

which implies

\frac{N^{3/2}}{\sqrt{12V+13N}}\leq M.

Substituting
V=q|S|\left(1-\frac{|S|}{N}\right)

gives the required bound.∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\Pi_{q}
be a projective plane of order
q
and let
S
be a set of points in
\Pi_{q}
. Then there exists an integer
k
such that

\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{\sqrt{3q+13}}\leq|L_{k}(S)|

Proof.

The proof follows from Proposition
2.2
after applying the AM-GM inequality

N\frac{|S|}{N}(1-\frac{|S|}{N})\leq N\frac{1}{4},

which in turn gives

\frac{N^{3/2}}{\sqrt{3qN+13N}}\leq|L_{k}(S)|.

∎

From this, the simplified form of the lower bound of Theorem
1.4
follows. ∎

2.2
Proof of the upper bound of Theorem
1.4

Next, we present a random construction which shows that the bound in Corollary
2.3
gives the right order of magnitude.

Proof.

We construct
S\subseteq\mathcal{P}
by selecting each point independently at random with probability
p=1/2
.
For each line
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
, define the random variable

X_{\ell}:=|\ell\cap S|.

Then

X_{\ell}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(q+1,1/2).

For
k\in\{0,\dots,q+1\}
let

L_{k}:=\{\ell\in\mathcal{L}:X_{\ell}=k\},\qquad|L_{k}|=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}I_{\ell},

where
I_{\ell}:=\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{\ell}=k\}}
.
Note that the variables
I_{\ell}
are not independent, since two lines intersect in one point of the plane.
Nevertheless the dependence is weak, and can be controlled.

Let
N=q^{2}+q+1
denote the number of lines.
For every
\ell
we have

\mathbb{E}[I_{\ell}]=\mathbb{P}(X_{\ell}=k).

Hence we have

\mathbb{E}[|L_{k}|]=N\mathbb{P}(X_{\ell}=k).

For
k\approx(q+1)/2
, Stirling’s formula yields

\mathbb{P}(X_{\ell}=k)=\binom{q+1}{k}2^{-(q+1)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}(q+1)}}(1+o(1)).

Therefore

\mathbb{E}[|L_{k}|]\leq(q^{2}+q+1)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}(q+1)}}{(1+o(1))}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,q^{3/2}+o(q^{3/2}).

We now estimate the variance of
|L_{k}|
.
Since

|L_{k}|=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}I_{\ell},

we have

\mathrm{Var}(|L_{k}|)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\mathrm{Var}(I_{\ell})+\sum_{\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}}\mathrm{Cov}(I_{\ell},I_{\ell^{\prime}}).

The first term satisfies

\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\mathrm{Var}(I_{\ell})\leq N\mathbb{P}(X_{\ell}=k)=\mathbb{E}[|L_{k}|].

To estimate the covariance term, observe that two distinct lines

\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
intersect in exactly one point.
Write

X_{\ell}=X_{P}+X_{\ell^{-}},\qquad X_{\ell^{\prime}}=X_{P}+X_{\ell^{\prime-}},

where
X_{P}
is the Bernoulli variable corresponding to the common point
P

and
X_{\ell^{-}},X_{\ell^{\prime-}}
are independent
\mathrm{Bin}(q,1/2)
variables corresponding
to the remaining points on the two lines
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
.
A direct computation shows that

\mathbb{P}(X_{\ell}=k,X_{\ell^{\prime}}=k)=\mathbb{P}(X_{\ell}=k)^{2}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)\mathbb{P}(X_{\ell}=k)^{2},

which implies

\mathrm{Cov}(I_{\ell},I_{\ell^{\prime}})=O\!\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)\mathbb{P}(X_{\ell}=k)^{2}.

Since there are
N(N-1)
ordered pairs of distinct lines, we obtain

\sum_{\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}}\mathrm{Cov}(I_{\ell},I_{\ell^{\prime}})=O\!\left(\frac{N^{2}}{q}\mathbb{P}(X_{\ell}=k)^{2}\right).

For all
k
we have
\mathbb{P}(X_{\ell}=k)=O(q^{-1/2})
, hence

\sum_{\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}}\mathrm{Cov}(I_{\ell},I_{\ell^{\prime}})=O(q^{2}).

Combining the estimates yields

\mathrm{Var}(|L_{k}|)=O(q^{2}).

Fix
k
satisfying

\left|k-\frac{q+1}{2}\right|\leq C\sqrt{q}.

From the variance estimate obtained above,

\operatorname{Var}(|L_{k}|)=O(q^{2}).

Let
\lambda>0
. By Chebyshev’s inequality,

\Pr\!\left(\,||L_{k}|-\mathbb{E}[|L_{k}|]|>\lambda q^{3/2}\right)\leq\frac{\operatorname{Var}(|L_{k}|)}{\lambda^{2}q^{3}}\leq\frac{C_{1}q^{2}}{\lambda^{2}q^{3}}=\frac{C_{1}}{\lambda^{2}q}

for some absolute constant
C_{1}
.

There are only
O(\sqrt{q})
integers
k
satisfying

\left|k-\frac{q+1}{2}\right|\leq C\sqrt{q}.

Applying the union bound over these values of
k
gives

\Pr\!\left(\exists\,k:\left|k-\frac{n}{2}\right|\leq C\sqrt{q}\ \text{and}\ ||L_{k}|-\mathbb{E}[|L_{k}|]|>\lambda q^{3/2}\right)\leq O\!\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}\sqrt{q}}\right)=o(1).

Hence with probability
1-o(1)
we have simultaneously for all

\left|k-\frac{q+1}{2}\right|\leq C\sqrt{q}
that

|L_{k}|\leq\mathbb{E}[|L_{k}|]+\lambda q^{3/2}.

For values of
k
outside the interval

[(q+1)/2-\sqrt{q},(q+1)/2+\sqrt{q}]

the expectation
\mathbb{E}[|L_{k}|]
is exponentially smaller
(by standard binomial tail estimates), so

\mathbb{P}(|L_{k}|\geq\mathbb{E}[|L_{(q+1)/2}|])=o(q^{-2}).

Taking the union bound over all
k
we conclude that with positive
probability

\max_{k}|L_{k}|\leq(1+o(1))\mathbb{E}[|L_{(q+1)/2}|],

and therefore there exists a set
S
such that

\max_{k}|L_{k}(S)|\leq(1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,q^{3/2}.

∎

3
Explicit constructions

In this section we present three explicit constructions, which although not as strong as the random construction, are still not far from optimal.

3.1
Constructions via parabolas and relation to character sum estimates

In a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of order
p
where
p
is a prime, every element uniquely corresponds to an integer in the interval
[0,p-1]
. This naturally induces an order relation
<
on
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

In our constructions below, we will assume that
p>3
.

Construction 3.1

(Points under the parabola)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
,
\beta
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and let
S
be defined as

S:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}:\alpha x^{2}+\beta x+\gamma<y\}.

In
[
1
]
, Adriaensen and Weiner studied the above point set and proved the next result. They defined the projection function of
S
from direction
(d)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
to be
\pr_{S,d}:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to{\mathbb{N}}
that maps
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
to the number of points of
S
on the line
Y=dX+b
, i.e., the secant size:
\pr_{S,d}(b)=|S\cap\ell[Y=dX+b]|
.
Also, let
\chi(x)
denote the Legendre symbol.

Result 3.2
.

Let
p>3
prime. For the set
S
of Construction
3.1
and for any
d\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
,

(1)

the projection function
\pr_{S,d}
is a cyclic shift of
\pr_{S,1}
;

(2)

the image of
\pr_{S,d}
is an interval whose length lies between
\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2\pi}
and
\sqrt{p}\ln(p)
;

(3)

\pr_{S,d}(b+1)-\pr_{S,d}(b)=-\chi((\beta-1)^{2}+4\alpha(b+1-\gamma))
.
In particular,
|\pr_{S,d}(b+1)-\pr_{S,d}(b)|\leq 1
.

Note that this result implies that apart from the number of points of
S
on the line at infinity and the vertical and horizontal lines, the possible intersection sizes on the remaining lines form an interval of length
O(\sqrt{p}\log p)
. This is consistent with the behavior suggested by the lower bound argument in Theorem
1.4
. In order to bound the most frequent intersection number, we need to understand the frequency pattern of the lines of one parallel class. Hence, let us define prefix sums

\Psi(a,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{t}\chi(a+j)

for
t\in\{0,1,\ldots,q-1\}
.

For now, let
\alpha=1/4
,
\beta=\gamma=1
; and so let
S
be the points ”under” the parabola
y=\frac{1}{4}x^{2}+x+1
and let us take a closer look at the intersection numbers of the parallel class with slope
d
. Result
3.2
gives that

\pr_{S,d}(b+1)-\pr_{S,d}(b)=-\chi(b)

holds in this case. In particular, for
d=1
, iterating this identity gives

\pr_{S,1}(t+1)=\pr_{S,1}(0)-\sum_{j=0}^{t}\chi(j)=\pr_{S,1}(0)-\Psi(0,t)

for
0\leq t\leq p-1
. Here
t=p-1
corresponds to the intercept

b=0
modulo
p
, since
\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\chi(j)=0
.
Since
\pr_{S,d}
is a cyclic shift of
\pr_{S,1}
, in particular,
\pr_{S,d}(b)=\pr_{S,1}(b+(d-1)^{2})
in our choice for the parabola, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
S
be the point set defined by the parabola
y=\frac{1}{4}x^{2}+x+1
in
\AG(2,p)
as in Construction 3.1.
For each
k
, let
L_{k}^{*}(S)
denote the set of affine

k
-secants of
S
that are neither vertical nor horizontal. Then

|L_{k}^{*}(S)|=(p-1)\cdot|\{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:\Psi(0,t)=\pr_{S,1}(0)-k\}|,

where
\Psi(a,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{t}\chi(a+j)
is the prefix sum of the Legendre symbols.

The Pólya–Vinogradov inequality (see e.g.
[
27
]
) provides the
\sqrt{p}\ln p
upper bound on the range of this character sum, while the results of Sárközy
[
31
]
and Sokolovskiĭ
[
32
]
provide the
\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2\pi}
lower bound; Result
3.2

(3)
is a consequence of these bounds.
One may view the prefix sums as ”random” walk on the integers, except that the increments are prescribed by the Legendre symbol.
The above results bound the range of the walk, expressing that character sums behave rather similarly to random walks in some aspects. However, for our purposes we need to control how often the walk attains a given value, since this frequency determines the most common secant size. This leads us to the following conjecture:

Conjecture 3.1

(Prefix sum recurrences)
.

Let
\Psi(a,t)
be defined via the Legendre symbol modulo a prime
p
.
Then

|\{t:\Psi(a,t)=0\}|=O(\sqrt{p}\log^{A}p),

independent of the value of
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
for an absolute constant
A>0
.
A stronger related question is whether

\max_{h\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl|\{\,0\leq t\leq p-1:\Psi(0,t)=h\,\}\bigr|=O(\sqrt{p}\,(\log p)^{A})

for some absolute constant
A>0
?

If Conjecture
3.1
holds, it shows that Construction
3.1
is only a logarithm factor off from the lower bound. For further details on the statistical behavior of non-principal Dirichlet characters and their sums, we refer to
[
9
]
.

The next construction was suggested by Tamás Szőnyi. Interestingly, bounding its most frequent intersection size again leads to the study of prefix-sum recurrences.

Construction 3.4

(Family of parabolas)
.

Let
p>2
prime,
c\in(0,1)
a constant
a=\lfloor cp\rfloor\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
and let
S
be the following set in
\AG(2,p)
.

S:=\{(x,x^{2}+t):x\in\mathbb{F}_{p},\ 0\leq t<a\}\subset\AG(2,p).

For any non-vertical line
\ell:y=mx+b
, the number of its intersection points with a single parabola
y=x^{2}+t
is given by
1+\chi(m^{2}-4(t-b))
, which corresponds to the number of solutions to the quadratic equation
x^{2}-mx+(t-b)=0
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
c\in(0,1)
be a fixed constant (independent of
p
). Summing over all
t<a=cp
, the total intersection size
n_{\ell}
is

n_{\ell}=\sum_{t=0}^{a-1}(1+\chi(m^{2}+4b-4t))=a+\sum_{t=0}^{a-1}\chi((m^{2}+4b)-4t).

(4)

Let us introduce

\Phi(u,a):=\sum_{t=0}^{a-1}\chi(u-t).

For fixed slope
m
, varying the intercept
b
shifts the initial point of the character sum in the above equation. Hence, after a suitable transformation, we get that within a given parallel class, the multiplicity of an intersection size
k
is determined by how often the prefix sum
\Phi
attains the value
k-a
. So again, the most frequent intersection size of Construction
3.4
depends on the distribution of character sums, this time of fixed length
a
,
\Phi(u,a)=\sum_{t=0}^{a-1}\chi(u-t)
for
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. For recent results on such character sums over moving intervals, see
[
16
,
21
]
.

3.2
Explicit constructions via elliptic curves

In this subsection, we present explicit constructions that are almost as good as the random construction. To bound the appearance of the most frequent intersection multiplicity for
S
, we first recall some key results on elliptic curves; for more details see Lenstra
[
24
]
. For
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
satisfying
4a^{3}+27b^{2}\neq 0
, let
E_{a,b}
(or simply
E
) denote the elliptic curve given by the Weierstrass equation

Y^{2}=X^{3}+aX+b.

The set of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-rational points of
E
, that is
E(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, is the set of points of PG
(2,p)
satisfying the homogeneous equation:

E(\mathbb{F}_{p})=\left\{(x,y,z)\in\mathrm{PG}(2,p):y^{2}z=x^{3}+axz^{2}+bz^{3}\right\}

Note that
E(\mathbb{F}_{p})
always contains exactly one point with
z=0
. By Hasse’s Theorem
[
17
,
18
]
, the number of points is given by
|E(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p+1-t
, where
|t|\leq 2\sqrt{p}
.
Two elliptic curves
E_{a,b}
and
E_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}
over
F_{p}
are isomorphic if there exists
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
such that
a^{\prime}=u^{4}a
and
b^{\prime}=u^{6}b
. Following
[
24
]
, we use the notation
\#^{\prime}
to denote a sum over isomorphism classes where each class
E
is weighted by
1/|\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(E)|
, where
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(E)
denotes the group of automorphisms of
E
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Result 3.5

(
[
24
]
, Proposition 1.9 (a))
.

Let
p>3
be a prime. There exists a constant
c
such that for any set of integers
N
where
|n-(p+1)|\leq 2\sqrt{p}
for all
n\in N
, we have:

\#^{\prime}\{\text{isomorphism classes }E:|E(\mathbb{F}_{p})|\in N\}\leq c\cdot|N|\cdot\sqrt{p}\log p(\log\log p)^{2}.

Since
2\leq|\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(E)|\leq 6
for
p>3
, each weight is at least
1/6
. Furthermore, each isomorphism class corresponds to at most
(p-1)/2
distinct pairs
(a,b)
. This leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
p>3
be a prime and let
N
be a set of integers such that

|n-(p+1)|\leq 2\sqrt{p}

for all
n\in N
. Then the number of elliptic curves
E_{a,b}
for which
|E_{a,b}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|\in N
is

O(|N|p^{3/2}\log p(\log\log p)^{2}).

Finally, we can prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
p>3
be a prime. In
\AG(2,p)
, let
S
be as follows:

S:=\left\{(x,v)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}:\exists y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\text{ such that }x^{3}-v=y^{2}\right\}.

Then the number of occurrences of the most frequent intersection multiplicity of
S
with lines is
O(p^{3/2}\log p(\log\log p)^{2})
.

Proof.

Let
\ell
be a non-vertical line
v=mx+b
with
-4m^{3}+27b^{2}\neq 0
. The intersection multiplicity
n_{\ell}=|S\cap\ell|
is the number of
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
for which
x^{3}-mx-b
is a quadratic residue. This relates to the rational points of the elliptic curve
E:Y^{2}=X^{3}-mX-b
as follows:

|E(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=2n_{\ell}+1-Z_{m,b},

where
1
accounts for the point at infinity and
Z_{m,b}\in\{0,1,2,3\}
is the number of roots of
X^{3}-mX-b=0
. So a fixed intersection multiplicity
n_{\ell}
can only occur if the
associated elliptic curve has a total number of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-rational points:
2n+1,2n,2n-1,2n-2
.
Therefore the result now follows by Corollary
3.6
, since the
O(p)
cases of vertical or singular lines are negligible.
∎

4
Applications and related problems

Recall that the
legitimate coloring of a hypergraph
is a coloring of the vertex set by
k
colors such that all hyperedges are distinquisable by their color distribution, in other words, by their multiplicity list
c_{1}(H),c_{2}(H),\ldots c_{n}(H)
, where
c_{i}(H)
denotes the number of vertices in hyperedge
H
colored with the
i
-th color. Let
\chi_{L}(H)
denote the minimum integer
k
, such that there exists a legitimate
coloring of a hypergraph
H
by
k
colors.
Alon and Füredi proved that
5\leq\chi_{L}(\Pi_{q})\leq 8
, provided that
q
is sufficiently large
[
3
]
.

A naive lower bound on the legitimate coloring number would follow from the following argument. Let us consider the line set
\mathcal{L}
of the projective plane
\Pi_{q}
. Then by Theorem
1.4
, we find at least
\min_{S\subseteq\Pi_{q}}\max_{k}\{|L_{k}(S)|\}
lines which are identical in the first coordinate of the color multiplicity list. In order to distinguish all the lines, we will need more colors, thus let us consider the largest non-distinguished line set
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{L}
and taking into consideration the next color. We should keep introducing new colors at least up to the point when the largest undistingushed line set by the previously applied colors has more than one lines.

The next proposition would yield the last step of this procedure and can be viewed as the legitimate version of the celebrated Erdős-Faber-Lovász conjecture. Recall that it states the following.

Conjecture 4.1

(Erdős-Faber-Lovász,
[
13
]
)
.

If a linear hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(V,\mathcal{E})
has
n
edges, each of size at most
n
, then
\mathcal{H}
can be colored properly by
n
colors.

This famous conjecture was fairly recently resolved in the case when
n
is sufficiently large
[
22
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote an arbitrarily chosen
n
-uniform linear hypergraph with
n
edges. Then the legitimate coloring number of
\mathcal{H}
is
2
.

Proof.

Let the edges of
\mathcal{H}
be ordered arbitrarily as

F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{n}
. We provide a suitable blue-red coloring of the vertex set in two phases.
In the first phase we first construct a preliminary red-blue coloring. Color all vertices of

F_{1}
blue. Suppose that the vertices of
F_{1},\dots,F_{i-1}
have already been colored.
If
i
is odd, color every previously uncolored vertex of
F_{i}
blue. If

i
is even, color every previously uncolored vertex of
F_{i}
red.

We claim that after this first phase, every odd-indexed edge has blue
majority and every even-indexed edge has red majority, in the following
stronger sense:

|F_{i}\cap B|\geq n-\lfloor i/2\rfloor\quad\text{if }i\text{ is odd},

and

|F_{i}\cap B|\leq i/2\quad\text{if }i\text{ is even},

where
B
indicates the set of blue vertices.
Indeed, if
i
is odd, then the only red vertices of
F_{i}
can come from
intersections with previous even-indexed edges. There are only

\lfloor i/2\rfloor
such edges, and by linearity each contributes at most
one vertex to
F_{i}
. Hence
F_{i}
contains at most
\lfloor i/2\rfloor
red
vertices, and so at least
n-\lfloor i/2\rfloor
blue vertices. The even
case is analogous: if
i
is even, then the only blue vertices of
F_{i}

can come from intersections with previous odd-indexed edges, of which
there are
i/2
, so
F_{i}
contains at most
i/2
blue vertices.

In the second phase we wish to recolor a subset of the vertices to obtain the required property. For an edge

F_{i}
, we call a vertex
v\in F_{i}

private
for
F_{i}
if
v
belongs to no
edge of
\mathcal{H}
other than
F_{i}
.
We now modify the coloring so that

|F_{i}\cap B|=\begin{cases}n-\lfloor i/2\rfloor,&i\text{ odd},\\
i/2,&i\text{ even}\end{cases}

holds for the number of blue vertices of the edges.
We do this successively for
i=1,2,\dots,n
. We show that this can be done via recoloring only private vertices in

F_{i}
.

Let us call
F_{j}

captured w.r.t.
F_{i}
, if
\exists k<j
, such that
(F_{i}\cap F_{j})\in F_{k}
. In other words, if
F_{i}\cap F_{j}=\{v\}
, then
F_{j}
is captured with respect
to
F_{i}
when the intersection vertex
v
already appeared in an earlier
edge.

Let
R_{i}
denote the set of private vertices of
F_{i}
, let

C_{i}:=\{j<i:F_{j}\text{ is captured with respect to }F_{i}\},

and let us consider the disjoint edges w.r.t.
F_{i}
,

D_{i}:=\{j:F_{i}\cap F_{j}=\emptyset\}.

We claim that

|R_{i}|\geq|C_{i}|+|D_{i}|+1
holds. Indeed, Since
\mathcal{H}
is linear, every edge
F_{j}\neq F_{i}
is either disjoint from
F_{i}
or meets
F_{i}
and
contributes exactly one incidence with a vertex of
F_{i}
. Therefore

n-1=|\{j\in[1,n]:j\neq i\}|=|D_{i}|+(n-|R_{i}|)+|\{j:F_{j}\text{ is captured with respect to }F_{i}\}|.

(5)

As
C_{i}\subseteq\{j:F_{j}\text{ is captured with respect to }F_{i}\}
, the result in turn follows.

Suppose first that
i
is odd. After the first phase,
F_{i}
has at least

n-\lfloor i/2\rfloor
blue vertices. If it has
n-\lfloor i/2\rfloor+t_{i}
blue vertices
(t_{i}\geq 0)
, then it has
\lfloor i/2\rfloor-t_{i}

red vertices. The red vertices of
F_{i}
must lie in previous even-indexed
edges, and the reason for a previous even edge
F_{j}
failing to contribute a red
vertex to
F_{i}
is that either it is disjoint from
F_{i}
, or its
intersection with
F_{i}
has already been captured by an earlier edge. Hence the number of additional red vertices needed in
F_{i}
is at most
|D_{i}|+|C_{i}|.
Since
i
is odd, all private vertices of
F_{i}
were
colored blue in the first phase. We may therefore recolor the required
number of them from blue to red, as
|R_{i}|\geq|C_{i}|+|D_{i}|+1
holds.

The case where
i
is even is similar.
∎
