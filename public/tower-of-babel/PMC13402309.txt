Hybrid auxetic metamaterial platforms enabling multiscale isotropic expansion for distortion-free stretchable displays
Authors: Su-Bon Kim, Junho Kim, Sejin Kim, Dongho Choi, Chang-Yeon Gu, Taek-Soo Kim, Hanul Moon, Seunghyup Yoo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractStretchable displays suffer image distortion due to transverse compression of substrates under uniaxial stretching. Although auxetic frameworks with negative Poisson’s ratios, which expand vertically during horizontal stretching, offer promising solutions, their void-rich structures are unsuitable for displays, requiring hybrid integration with continuous elastomeric substrates. This integration is challenging because rotational motions essential to auxetic unit cells can induce local substrate deformation, while the substrate can also restrict rotation, undermining auxetic performance. Here we show that design methodologies for hybrid auxetic metamaterial platforms, in which auxetic frameworks and elastomeric substrates are bonded at selective sites optimized for both horizontal and vertical stretching, enable isotropic expansion across local-to-global scales. To this end, the isotropic expansion factor is introduced as a statistical metric for quantifying image distortion. With the proposed hybrid platforms, we demonstrate stretchable displays exhibiting uniform, distortion-free expansion and stable performance under repeated stretching.Subject terms: Electronic devices, Displays, Optoelectronic devices and components, Metamaterials

Introduction

Stretchable optoelectronic devices have emerged as a promising platform for diverse applications, including wearable displays1–4, skin electronics5–9, soft robotics10–13, and bioelectronics14–17. A central requirement for such devices is the ability to retain stable performance without functional degradation under stretching, bending, or flexural strains4,18–20. To meet this requirement, several strategies have been developed, including the use of intrinsically stretchable electronic materials19,21–25 and integration architectures that combine performance-preserving rigid islands with compliant interconnectors26–31, enabling efficient stress relaxation even under nonlinear mechanical deformations. For substrates, most stretchable displays developed to date rely on elastomeric materials capable of dissipating mechanical stress under tensile deformation. These materials, like most substances found in nature, possess a positive Poisson’s ratio. Consequently, when stretched, they undergo compression in the direction transverse to the applied tensile axis, inevitably leading to image distortion19,21,32,33. In stretchable displays, such distortion significantly compromises visual clarity and the accuracy of information delivery and therefore must be minimized as much as possible.

To overcome the inherent limitations of natural materials, recent research has explored kirigami-based metamaterial structures with a negative Poisson’s ratio of −1, in which stretching along one axis induces expansion along the perpendicular axis by the same magnitude, thereby suppressing image distortion34–38. Despite this strong advantage, auxetic metamaterials face fundamental limitations when applied to stretchable displays. Crucially, although the overall structure exhibits a negative Poisson’s ratio, only certain regions contribute to isotropic expansion at the local scale, due to the rotation-based mechanism of the unit cell that most auxetic metamaterial structures rely on; when the framework is fully bonded with an elastomer, the local rotational behavior does not support isotropic expansion, leaving regions susceptible to image distortion even if the substrate, in its overall dimensions, appears to expand uniformly in both axes34,38. When the auxetic metamaterial is used as a stand-alone substrate without being coupled to an elastomer, display pixels could be placed in discrete locations less subject to anisotropic expansion to mitigate distortion, but it could make pixel distributions spatially non-uniform and eventually limit their resolution35–37. In this context, image distortion has so far been evaluated mainly qualitatively, lacking a quantitative framework for guiding the design of distortion-free stretchable platforms. Furthermore, the auxetic effect may not work as designed near gripping parts, as they tend to hinder the rotational motion of the auxetic unit cells37,38. Note that gripping parts or fixtures are essential in mechanically actuated configurations for controlled tensile deformation. Such limitations reduce the usable active device area, ultimately constituting a fundamental limitation in applying auxetic metamaterials to stretchable displays.

Here, we propose a rational design methodology based on a hybrid auxetic metamaterial platform—an auxetic framework integrated with an elastomeric substrate—for distortion-free stretchable displays while maximizing the active display area and achieving isotropic expansion. The methodology consists of two key elements: (i) designing an auxetic framework that can achieve the uniform auxetic effect in the presence of mechanical gripping components, and (ii) engineering connection strategies between the framework and an elastomeric substrate to realize continuous isotropic expansion—both locally and globally—on the substrate. To enable quantitative evaluation of image distortion, the ‘isotropic expansion factor’ is introduced as a statistical metric that measures the uniformity of display-image expansion and provides a basis for identifying optimal architectures. The proposed hybrid platform is then realized experimentally by integrating an auxetic framework, fabricated through a soft-mold replication process, with an elastomeric substrate via designated connection points to guide isotropic expansion. Both a spray-printed high-resolution pattern and a passive-matrix (PM) LED array are prepared on the proposed platform and evaluated, verifying the reliable, distortion-free isotropic expansion under uniaxial stretching in both horizontal and vertical directions. The results confirm that the proposed approach advances both the structural design of auxetic frameworks and the interface engineering for the connection of elastomeric substrates, demonstrating its strong potential for adoption across a wide range of stretchable display technologies.

Although auxetic frameworks based on rotating squares can be employed to keep the aspect ratio of the overall rectangular displays for mitigation of image distortion during uniaxial stretching39,40, not all surface regions contribute to isotropic expansion, as can be seen in Fig. 1c. Under 15% uniaxial stretching applied along the horizontal and the vertical directions, respectively, the scatter of (uy/y) versus (ux/x) for points within the auxetic framework, derived from finite-element analysis (FEA), exhibits substantial dispersion, indicating that fully affixing the auxetic framework to the elastomeric substrate results in inevitable local image distortion in stretchable displays (Fig. 1d). To illustrate the local image distortion that can occur in a stretchable display implemented on an elastomer fully bonded with the auxetic framework, FEA results of the kinematic trajectories and principal strain were numerically applied to display images (Supplementary Fig. 1 for image transformation method from FEA predictions). The results clearly illustrate that local distortion is still visible when compared to the initial (unstretched) state (e.g., the horizontal grid lines and soccer-ball patterns), although it is not as severe as in the case of conventional stretchable displays that do not employ an auxetic framework. Furthermore, local deformation remains unavoidable even at downsized length scales—when the size of unit cells is reduced to target higher pixels-per-inch (PPI) values (Supplementary Fig. 2 for the size reduction effect of a fully-bonded auxetic framework with elastomer). These observations call for an alternative design strategy to realize stretchable displays based on auxetic frameworks, suppressing image distortion.

In this regard, this work proposes a stretchable platform that enables isotropic expansion throughout the display region, where an elastomeric substrate that incorporates an edge-stiffening layer at the perimeter is connected to an auxetic framework that incorporates designated auxetic–elastomer connection points (Fig. 1e). The edge-stiffening layer is fabricated thicker than the central region of the elastomer to minimize perimeter deformation. The quantitatively chosen connection configuration between the auxetic framework and the elastomeric substrate reduces the loss of display area available for device placement on the substrate and enables uniform isotropic expansion across the designated display area (Fig. 1f).

The methodology for determining auxetic–elastomer connection sites begins with the requirement that isotropic expansion be preserved under both horizontal and vertical deformations (Fig. 3c). Accordingly, every connection point should closely satisfy the relation u⃗i°=kr⃗i°, where the displacement vector scales proportionally to its position vector, and thus both vectors are parallel to each other. Nevertheless, it is challenging to find cases where all the positions satisfy the exact isotropic expansion condition at the same time. To circumvent this issue, we define the so-called ‘semi-linearity factor’ as KijD=MijDNijD, wherein ‘D’ in superscript is ‘H’ or ‘V’, corresponding to the values obtained for horizontal and vertical stretching, respectively. MijD is a binary variable defined as ‘1’ for the regions where the angle α(xi,yj) between r(xi,yj) and u(xi,yj) is less than 10° and ‘0’ otherwise. NijD denotes the radial projection of u(xi,yj) onto r(xi,yj).

For visualization, KijH and KijV are mapped in blue and green, respectively, on the initial, non-stretched configuration, each normalized to its own minimum–maximum range (0–255) (K~ijH and K~ijV). The spatial map of KijH was obtained at the strain (ε = 3%), where △kab(H) is largest, and the spatial green map of KijV at the strain (ε = 15%), where △kab(V) is largest. Defining connection points at the strain with the smallest △kab provides a high degree of isotropic expansion across the entire strain range. Rotating-square frameworks were adopted, given the rectangular form factor commonly used in stretchable display implementations. This superposition-based methodology for identifying connection sites for auxetic framework–elastomer integration can be extended to other types of auxetic framework geometries (see Supplementary Fig. 9 for design results for auxetic frameworks with different geometries).

Experimental validation of multiscale distortion-free isotropic expansion of images

The proposed stretchable platforms were experimentally realized and compared with the fully-bonded case. Both the proposed and fully-bonded stretchable platforms were fabricated via adhesive bonding of pre-produced 50 µm-thick elastomeric substrate (Dragon Skin 10, Smooth-On Inc.) onto auxetic frameworks replicated via a soft mold derived from 3D-printed masters (see Supplementary Fig. 10a and ‘Methods’ for fabrication details). It is noted that the fully-bonded elastomer was entirely constrained to the underlying auxetic framework, thereby following its deformation as-is, including the rotational behavior of the auxetic units. By contrast, the proposed platform relies on spatially localized bonding through anchors; thus, maintaining mechanical integrity against interfacial debonding at the anchor–elastomer interface is critical under practical operations such as auxetic stretching and user touch. Accordingly, the adhesive lap shear strength was evaluated at the anchor–elastomer interface, which represents the most mechanically vulnerable bonding region (Supplementary Fig. 11). The interfacial shear stresses, including those associated with the anchor radius and those induced by touch-mimicking indentation, were found to be substantially lower than the measured lap shear strength of the adhesive (1.518 MPa), thereby confirming robust resistance to interfacial debonding during operation.

Digital image correlation (DIC) study, carried out to obtain the spatial distribution of u under 15% uniaxial stretching in both horizontal and vertical directions, showed a good match to FEA data and, moreover, confirmed that the proposed approach can indeed realize a stretchable platform featuring continuous, uniform isotropic expansion throughout the substrate (Fig. 4a and ‘Methods’ for measurement setup). In contrast to the fully-bonded cases that revealed specific directionality and patterns in the distribution of u, the hybrid platform adopting the proposed connection strategy between an auxetic framework and an elastomer substrate exhibited a spatial distribution in which u increases linearly with r in all directions, being consistent with the proportional relationship u=kr. In addition, when the stretchable platforms were stretched from 3% to 15% by 3% increments, the isotropic expansion factor (ρ) extracted from DIC analysis was found to be consistently higher and closer to unity (ρ≈ 1) for the proposed platform than for the fully-bonded cases, demonstrating their suitability for distortion-free stretchable displays (Fig. 4b).

To provide a visible and intuitive demonstration of image distortion suppression, letters and motifs were spray-printed on top of the elastomeric substrates (Fig. 4c–h, Supplementary Fig. 10b and ‘Methods’). Substrates with (nH, nV = (5,4) and (5,5) were fabricated using optimized parameters (W, L, D) = (14, 0.5, 0.4) (Fig. 4c–e) and (17, 0.5, 0.5) (Fig. 4f–h), respectively (Fig. 2b and Supplementary Fig. 3). The substrates were then stretched to ε = 15% in both vertical and horizontal directions, respectively, using a customized stretching stage (Fig. 4c, 4f). At 15% strain, both the letter- and motif-patterns on the fully-bonded platforms exhibited significant local distortions (Fig. 4d, g). In contrast, the proposed platforms clearly preserved patterns and their local aspect ratios, providing direct visual evidence for the validity of our design methodology for uniform, isotropic expansion (Fig. 4e, h) and illustrating the importance of (i) optimization of the underlying auxetic framework and (ii) the proposed discrete connection schemes with the elastomer for distortion-free stretchable displays.

Demonstration of distortion-free stretchable auxetic LED displays

Figure 5a shows the mechanical behavior of an auxetic display fabricated by integrating a 7 × 5 stretchable LED array onto the proposed hybrid auxetic platform (Supplementary Fig. 10c and ‘Methods’ for fabrication details). The system setup was established using a customized flexible printed circuit board (FPCB) in a rigid-island configuration, where each LED was electrically connected to the driver circuit via silver paste. The LED array was fabricated on a polyimide substrate that serves as an electrically insulating layer and as a mechanical support. The device architecture incorporated serpentine interconnects defined by laser patterning, with the cathode and anode lines placed on the top and bottom surfaces of the polyimide, respectively, to ensure electrical isolation. While this mesh-like design inherently affords the array an effective Poisson’s ratio of near zero, such arrays cannot function as free-standing devices and must be integrated onto elastomeric substrates28,29. Once bonded, however, this LED array passively conforms to the displacement of the underlying elastomer. Since the hybrid auxetic platform proposed in this study enables isotropic expansion of the elastomeric substrate, the LED array integrated on its surface undergoes equivalent isotropic expansion. The devices were subjected to uniaxial stretching on a customized stage under horizontal (εH = 10%, 15%) and vertical (εV = 10%, 15%) stretching conditions (Supplementary Fig. 12 for system set-up configuration). The experimental results demonstrate that the auxetic substrate guides the LED array to expand isotropically, thereby minimizing image distortion. To evaluate device stability, current–voltage (I–V) characteristics were measured under incremental strains from 0% to 15% at 3% intervals (Fig. 5b, c). The optoelectrical performance of the LEDs exhibited only a small variation across both horizontal and vertical stretching (εH, εV) from 0% to 15%, confirming their robustness under mechanical deformation. In addition, the normalized luminance—defined as the ratio of luminance measured after each stretching cycle to the initial luminance—was measured under cyclic stretching. Even after 500 stretching cycles along both horizontal and vertical directions at ε = 15%, the luminance degradation remained below 2%, ensuring the mechanical reliability of both the auxetic platform and the LED array.

Large-area applicability was examined by integrating a 10 × 10 passive-matrix (PM) LED array onto the proposed platform with unit cell numbers (nH, nV) = (5,5) (Fig. 5d and Supplementary Fig. 13 for the system setup of PM operation). The PM LEDs exhibited programmed arrow-shaped patterns. The patterns retained their structural accuracy without image distortion even under 20% uniaxial stretching in both horizontal and vertical directions, providing direct experimental evidence that the proposed hybrid platform enables isotropic expansion of integrated LED arrays. The device functionality of the system was further validated through programming the PM LEDs to display a dynamic ‘AUXETIC’ text animation (Fig. 5e and Supplementary Movie 1). The animated letters preserved their shape without image distortion during 20% stretching. Along with the above demonstrations of static and dynamic emission, these findings establish the suitability of the proposed auxetic substrate for distortion-free stretchable displays. Note that, in stretchable displays that place light-emitting elements directly on auxetic unit cells, the pixel density is inherently limited by the downscaling limit of the auxetic structures35,37. It may be resolved upon combinatorial use of continuous elastomeric substrates and auxetic frameworks, but those adopting fully bonded auxetic–elastomer integrations are still subject to local distortion transferred from that of rotating auxetic unit cells to the elastomer surface38. In contrast, the selective connection scheme proposed in this work provides devices with a continuous elastomeric surface exhibiting local-to-global isotropic expansion, as quantified by the isotropic expansion factor ρ (Supplementary Table 1).

Discussion

In summary, we proposed a rational design methodology for hybrid auxetic metamaterial platforms based on an auxetic framework integrated with an elastomeric substrate to resolve the challenge of image distortion in stretchable displays with polymeric substrates. Although auxetic structures inherently exhibit a negative Poisson’s ratio, they typically rely on rotational deformation; when fully bonded to elastomeric substrates, this behavior inevitably induces local deformation in the coupled elastomers. To overcome this limitation, we introduced a design strategy that employs an auxetic geometry tailored to minimize the regional imbalance of the auxetic effect within the framework. The approach consisted of two key steps: (i) identifying geometrical parameters of the auxetic framework that minimize regional imbalance of the auxetic effect and yield the lowest distortion under horizontal and vertical uniaxial stretching, even in the presence of mechanical gripping components; and (ii) establishing optimal framework–elastomer connection strategies to achieve continuous, uniform isotropic expansion in the elastomeric substrate across the working area. Unlike previous auxetic designs in mechanical and structural systems, which have mainly been optimized for energy absorption and impact mitigation41 or for enhanced mechanical robustness in engineering components42, and architected lattices that primarily target programmable or global-scale control of effective Poisson’s ratio43, as well as rotating-square tessellation platforms that secure mechanical reliability in auxetic meta-display platforms37,44, our design is driven by the requirement of pixel-wise, field-level isotropic expansion in a hybrid auxetic–elastomer platform. In this context, the auxetic geometry and the discrete connection scheme are co-optimized to achieve a single kinematic target: the displacement vectors remain proportional to the position vectors throughout the active display area.

The design was optimized using an isotropic expansion factor, a statistical metric devised to quantitatively evaluate image distortion. Finite element analysis (FEA) and digital image correlation (DIC) validated that the proposed auxetic–framework–guided approach enables isotropic expansion without image distortion, whereas a fully bonded substrate exhibits deformation-induced distortion. Furthermore, distortion-free expansion of continuous images formed on the proposed auxetic metamaterial platform was verified experimentally, demonstrating its potential for high-resolution stretchable displays. While downsizing the auxetic unit cells could be an intuitive alternative route to achieving distortion-free stretchable displays, one should note that it still encounters fundamental physical and fabrication barriers in fully-bonded systems. Suppose one builds a stretchable display with the pixel density of 300 PPI and downsizes the rotating square unit cells accordingly to have its diagonal length and hinge width be 85 µm and 1.7 µm, respectively. At this micro-scale, interfacial forces from the elastomer often dominate the diminished structural stiffness, which can potentially lead to kinematic locking rather than auxetic behavior. Furthermore, reducing the substrate thickness along with lateral downscaling could induce out-of-plane buckling, compromising the planarity required for display applications, whereas maintaining the thickness results in high-aspect-ratio structures prone to fabrication failure. From an application standpoint, restricting pixels to the center of micro-units severely limits the fill factor, while edge placement causes zig–zag misalignment and visual artifacts. Additionally, interconnects on micro-hinges may suffer from excessive rotational stress, often necessitating use of the non-standard materials like liquid metal. In contrast, the proposed hybrid platform enables seamless integration of high-resolution displays without the need for extreme structural downsizing of auxetic unit cells or non-standard materials (see Supplementary Note 2). Building upon this structural advantage, its broad applicability was further illustrated by integrating rigid-island–interconnector LED arrays, which were confirmed to maintain stable optoelectrical performance under repeated stretching-and-releasing cycles and to support passive-matrix (PM) operation. While macro-scale LEDs were utilized in this demonstration for visual clarity, the underlying mechanical mechanism—pure isotropic expansion driven by kinematic constraints through the auxetic framework and discrete connection points—is expected to be scale-invariant. As verified by our finite element analysis (FEA) (Supplementary Figs. 14 and 15), the induced strain is localized at the serpentine interconnects and the peripheral edges of the islands, leaving the central region under near-zero strain. By leveraging this strain-isolation behavior, pixel density can be substantially increased by arranging multiple micro-pixels as a cluster within a larger rigid island. FEA performed for a 30 μm × 30 μm island geometry shows that the central 26 μm × 26 μm region remains mechanically protected, with the strain kept below the fracture limit of a brittle inorganic layer such as aluminum oxide up to 30% system strain (Supplementary Fig. 14). Given that this strain-isolation behavior is governed primarily by the stiffness contrast between the island and the interconnects, proportional scaling should preserve the mechanically protected central region. Accordingly, a 300 μm × 300 μm island would provide a central low-strain area of approximately 240 μm × 240 μm, sufficient to accommodate a 16 × 16 micro-pixel array fabricated using fine metal mask processes. Even when accounting for approximately 100 μm gaps for stretchable interconnects, this configuration would correspond to an effective system-level resolution exceeding 1000 PPI. These results show that the proposed approach suppresses image distortion at both local and global scales, establishing a versatile platform for next-generation high-quality stretchable displays and optoelectronic devices. We expect that the design methodology and integration strategy presented here will offer valuable insights for developing distortion-free stretchable and wearable systems that must meet various mechanical and optical requirements in diverse application scenarios.

Methods

Fabrication of hybrid auxetic metamaterial platforms and stretchable passive-matrix LED displays made thereon

Fabrication of the auxetic framework and the elastomeric substrate

Initially, the 3D-printed positive mold was filled with elastomer (Dragon Skin 20, Smooth-On Inc.). To eliminate entrapped air, the filled mold was subjected to degassing in a vacuum chamber and cured for 24 h at room temperature. Hard resin (TASK-15, Smooth-On Inc.) was then cast into the demolded elastomeric mold, subsequently being degassed in a vacuum chamber. Before the resin was fully cured, the top surface was carefully scraped to define the precise final thickness and establish the designed perforated geometry of the auxetic framework. Following this, the resin was allowed to complete its curing for 24 h, after which the rigid auxetic framework was successfully demolded. The elastomeric substrate, which forms the soft layer, was prepared using an identical casting method. For this component, Dragon Skin 10 (Smooth-On Inc.) was utilized as the base material (Supplementary Fig. 10a). Here, a mold release agent (Ease Release 200, Smooth-On Inc.) and a cure inhibitor neutralizer (Inhibit X, Smooth-On Inc.) were applied to the 3D-printed molds to ensure proper curing and facile demolding of the elastomeric mold.

Stencil printing of patterns for verification of distortion-free expansion

The coating solution was formulated by mixing Silc Pig, Psycho Paint, and NOVOCS (all products from Smooth-On Inc.) at a precise weight ratio of 1:30:100. The combined ink was subjected to continuous stirring until no residual components remained. Then, spray coating of the ink was performed by tightly securing a custom stencil mask onto the surface of the demolded elastomeric substrate (Supplementary Fig. 10b). Following the application of the coating, the elastomeric substrate was bonded to the rigid auxetic framework using an adhesive (AXIA 2700, AXIA).

Transfer process of the LED array and assembly of the auxetic display

After curing the elastomeric substrate in a 3D-printed mold, a thin adhesive layer (DOWSIL™ SE 9186) was formed by spin-coating at 500 rpm for 40 s. A prefabricated light-emitting diode array (LM102A, Samsung Semiconductor Inc.), prepared on a polyimide supported by a temporary low-adhesion carrier film, was brought into conformal contact with the elastomeric substrate, with the polyimide surface of the array contacting the elastomer. After conformal contact, the carrier film was peeled to complete the transfer, leaving the array adhered via the spin-coated adhesive. The LED-integrated elastomeric substrate was then demolded from the 3D-printed mold. Cylindrical anchors were employed to implement the connection points on the auxetic framework. These anchors were incorporated into the 3D-printed mold design itself to define their position precisely. Then, the top surfaces of the anchors were coated with an adhesive (AXIA 2700, AXIA), and the substrate was aligned to these anchor sites and bonded to the framework (Supplementary Fig. 10c).

As for process scalability and manufacturability, the selective anchoring process requires both a precise definition of anchor geometry and controlled alignment during bonding. Two-photon polymerization (2PP)–based 3D microprinting provides sub-micrometer feature resolution, with demonstrated capability down to the sub-100 nm regime under optimized conditions45, enabling anchor geometries at the required dimensions to be fabricated with sufficient process margin. In addition, the literature reports ~1 µm pre-bond alignment accuracy for wafer-level bonding using state-of-the-art alignment tools46. These reported fabrication and alignment capabilities support the feasibility of implementing the proposed anchoring scheme within practical manufacturing tolerances for most applications under consideration in this work.

Finite element analysis of the auxetic framework and the hybrid auxetic metamaterial platform under uniaxial stretching

The mechanical response of an auxetic framework and the hybrid auxetic metamaterial platform under uniaxial stretching was analyzed using COMSOL Multiphysics. A geometrically nonlinear static study was used with a tetrahedral solid mesh whose characteristic element size was less than 500 µm. The elastic modulus was set to 868.74 MPa for the auxetic framework and 0.125 MPa for the elastomer. Two interface conditions in auxetic-elastomer integration were employed: (i) proposed platform, where the elastomer and framework were tied only at the optimized anchor sites and otherwise traction-free along the interface; and (ii) fully bonded control, where the elastomer was tied over the entire contact area.

Digital image correlation for displacement mapping

Digital image correlation (DIC) was performed on auxetic platforms (TASK-15, Smooth-On Inc.) with a thickness of 3 mm and bonded to an elastomeric substrate (Dragon Skin 10, Smooth-On Inc.) with a thickness of 50 µm. The measurement surface was spray-coated with a matte white base and lightly over-sprayed with a fine black speckle to produce a high-contrast random pattern. A stereo pair of charge-coupled device (CCD) cameras was calibrated for the field of view prior to testing. Specimens were mounted on a custom stretching stage and loaded uniaxially in the horizontal direction and, in separate runs, in the vertical direction in 3% strain steps from 0% to 15%. At each increment, images were acquired after load stabilization. Image sequences were exported as individual frames and processed using a commercial DIC program (ARAMIS, Gesellschaft für Optische Messtechnik mbH). Facet size and point distance parameters were chosen from preliminary convergence tests and kept constant throughout the measurements. The analysis provided in-plane displacement/strain maps, from which the isotropic expansion factor was computed using ux, uy and x, y coordinates.

The electroluminescent measurements of the stretchable LEDs on the auxetic platform under uniaxial stretching

The optoelectrical performance of the stretchable LEDs, including current (I) and luminance (L), was measured using CS 2000 equipment (KONICA MINOLTA, Inc.) and a source meter (Keithley 2400, Keithley Inc.) under ambient conditions. A system uniaxial strain of 15% was applied to the auxetic display during the electroluminescent measurement, using the uniaxial stretching test setup.
