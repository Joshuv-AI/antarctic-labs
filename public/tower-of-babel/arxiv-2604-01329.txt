Model Merging via Data-Free Covariance Estimation
Marawan Gamal Abdel Hameed, Derek Tam, Pascal Jr Tikeng Notsawo, Colin Raffel, Guillaume Rabusseau

Abstract
Model merging provides a way of cheaply combining individual models to produce a model that inherits each individual’s capabilities. While some merging methods can approach the performance of multitask training, they are often heuristically motivated and lack theoretical justification. A principled alternative is to pose model merging as a layer-wise optimization problem that directly minimizes interference between tasks. However, this formulation requires estimating per-layer covariance matrices from data, which may not be available when performing merging. In contrast, many of the heuristically-motivated methods do not require auxiliary data, making them practically advantageous. In this work, we revisit the interference minimization framework and show that, under certain conditions, covariance matrices can be estimated directly from difference matrices , eliminating the need for data while also reducing computational costs. We validate our approach across vision and language benchmarks on models ranging from 86 ​ M 86M parameters to 7 ​ B 7B parameters, outperforming previous data-free state-of-the-art merging methods 1 1 1 Code available at github.com/marawangamal/actmat .

Related Work

Mode Connectivity.

10

While the aforementioned works focus on models trained on the same data,

Model Merging.

Many merging methods rely on the models being merged having shared a training trajectory, thus avoiding the need to account for permutation symmetries
(
47
;
29
;
20
)
.

18

task vectors
, defined as the parameter-space difference between a fine-tuned model and its pretrained initialization.
Reinterpreting model merging within this framework has fostered theoretical analysis highlighting
weight disentanglement
as a necessary condition for effective task arithmetic
(
34
)
. Interestingly, fine-tuning in the linear regime has been shown to promote weight disentanglement
(
34
;
51

Merging Methods.

Beyond linear combinations of parameters
(
47
;
18
)
, a number of methods have been proposed based on the principle of
interference minimization
. TIES
(
49
)
reduces interference at the parameter level, by trimming low-magnitude parameters, resolving sign conflicts across task vectors, and merging only sign-consistent updates.
Similarly, DARE
(
52
)
resets a fraction of fine-tuned parameters to their original weights at random, effectively reducing parameter interference by merging sparse task vectors.
Another family of methods leverages the matrix structure of linear layers via the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD).
In Task Singular Vectors (TSV)
(
14
)
, the authors observe that task matrices are inherently low-rank and reduce “Singular Task Interference” by decorrelating the singular vectors of different tasks before merging.
Iso-C
(
27
)
flattens the spectrum of the merged matrix to balance out dominant directions in weight space with underrepresented ones. KnOTS
(
40
)
finds that models fine-tuned with LoRA exhibit a significantly lower centered kernel alignment score
(
22
)
, compared with full fine-tuning and propose to merge models in an aligned space via the SVD.
Notably, methods such as TSV and Iso-C offer data-free settings, as they have been shown to be relatively robust to scaling coefficients.
Meanwhile, methods such as RegMean
(
20
)
, LOT
(
42
)
, WUDI
(
6
)
, and AdaMerging
(
50
)
require the use of auxiliary data either for optimization of data-dependent objectives or for hyper-parameter tuning.
Similarly to TSV and Iso-C, ACTMat is entirely data-free.

Kronecker-Factored Approximate Curvature (KFAC).
KFAC
(
28
)
provides a tractable approximation of the Fisher information matrix by assuming (i) that the Fisher matrix is block-diagonal across layers and (ii) that layer-wise activations and activation gradients are uncorrelated. In our work, we leverage a variation on the latter assumption to derive the ACTMat estimator.

3.3
Analyzing the Impact of the ACTMat Scaling Factor

The previous section shows that the covariance matrices can be recovered from parameter difference matrices, but only up to a scaling factor. That is,
{\bm{C}}_{t}=\kappa_{t}{\bm{\Delta}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{\Delta}}_{t}
when all error terms vanish. In this section, we examine to what extent these scaling factors pose a problem in the context of model merging via the interference minimization merge rule. Recall that the minimizer of the interference objective has the closed-form solution

{\bm{W}}^{\star}=\textstyle\sum_{t}{\bm{W}}_{t}{\bm{C}}_{t}(\sum_{t^{\prime}}{\bm{C}}_{t^{\prime}})^{\dagger}.

When all scaling factors are equal, using the ACTMat estimates
\widehat{{\bm{C}}}_{t}={\bm{\Delta}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{\Delta}}_{t}
in place of the true covariances yields the same minimizer.
This is due to the scale invariance of the minimizer (i.e., multiplying all the covariances by the same value will not affect the minimizer).
We formalize this statement in the following proposition (proof in Appendix
B.4
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
{\bm{C}}_{t}=\kappa_{t}\widehat{{\bm{C}}}
for
\kappa_{t}\in\mathbb{R}
, and define
the true and approximate minimizers as

{\bm{W}}^{\star}=\textstyle\sum_{t}{\bm{W}}_{t}{\bm{C}}_{t}\left(\sum_{t^{\prime}}{\bm{C}}_{t^{\prime}}\right)^{\dagger}\text{ and }\widehat{{\bm{W}}}=\textstyle\sum_{t}{\bm{W}}_{t}\widehat{{\bm{C}}}_{t}(\textstyle\sum_{t^{\prime}}\widehat{{\bm{C}}}_{t^{\prime}})^{\dagger}.

If all
\kappa_{t}
are equal, then
{\bm{W}}^{\star}=\widehat{{\bm{W}}}
.

Assuming
{\bm{C}}_{t}=\kappa_{t}{\bm{\Delta}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{\Delta}}_{t}

3.4
Theoretical Guarantees of Layer-wise Interference Minimization

The layer-wise objective in Equation (

Remarkably,

5
Conclusion

In this work, we presented ACTMat, a principled approach to data-free model merging that combines covariance estimates derived from difference matrices with the interference minimization framework of RegMean. Our approach leverages three empirical findings that make covariance approximation from difference matrices possible. Namely, cross term error cancellations, uncorrelatedness of activations and activation gradients, and stationarity of covariances throughout fine-tuning.

An interesting direction for future work is developing a theoretical understanding of why these three properties hold in practice, and characterizing the regimes
under which each approximation is most or least accurate.
More broadly, the availability of cheap, data-free covariance estimates may prove useful well beyond the merging setting, and we believe investigating such applications is a fruitful direction for future work

B.1
Layer-wise Interference Minimization

Lemma B.1
.

Let
{\bm{W}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{o}\times D_{i}}
and
{\bm{C}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{i}\times D_{i}}
for
t=1\dots T
. Define
{\bm{A}}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{C}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{i}\times D_{i}}
and
{\bm{B}}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{W}}_{t}{\bm{C}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{o}\times D_{i}}
.
If the matrices
{\bm{C}}_{t}
are symmetric positive semidefinite (denoted from now on by
{\bm{C}}_{t}\succeq 0
), then the matrix equation
{\bm{W}}{\bm{A}}={\bm{B}}
always admits at least one solution, and the set of solutions is

\left\{{\bm{W}}^{\star}+{\bm{Z}}\left(\mathbb{I}_{D_{i}}-{\bm{A}}{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}\right)\mid{\bm{Z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{o}\times D_{i}}\right\},

(9)

with
{\bm{W}}^{\star}={\bm{B}}{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}
being the minimum Frobenius norm solution.
Moreover, the solution is unique iff
{\bm{A}}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{C}}_{t}
is invertible, which is equivalent to
\bigcap_{t=1}^{T}\ker({\bm{C}}_{t})=\{0\}
.

Proof.

A solution to the equation
{\bm{W}}{\bm{A}}={\bm{B}}
exists if and only if each row of
{\bm{B}}
belongs to the row space of
{\bm{A}}
, i.e.,
{\bm{B}}={\bm{B}}{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}{\bm{A}}
.
We solve the equation row by row. Let
{\bm{w}}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{i}}
be a row of
{\bm{W}}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{o}\times D_{i}}
and
{\bm{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{i}}
the corresponding row of
{\bm{B}}
. The equation
{\bm{W}}{\bm{A}}={\bm{B}}
is equivalent to
{\bm{A}}{\bm{w}}={\bm{b}}
for all
({\bm{w}},{\bm{b}})
.
Thus, the existence of a solution is equivalent to
{\bm{b}}\in\operatorname{Im}({\bm{A}})=\ker({\bm{A}})^{\perp}
for all row
{\bm{b}}
of
{\bm{B}}
, where we use the fact that
\operatorname{Im}({\bm{A}})=\ker({\bm{A}})^{\perp}
since
{\bm{A}}
is symmetric.
Therefore,
{\bm{b}}\in\operatorname{Im}({\bm{A}})
if and only if
{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{b}}=0
for all
{\bm{x}}\in\ker({\bm{A}})
. We now prove this condition.

Let
{\bm{x}}\in\ker({\bm{A}})
, so that
{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}=0
.
Then
0={\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}_{t}{\bm{x}}
, which is equivalent to
{\bm{x}}^{\top}C_{t}{\bm{x}}=0\ \forall t
since
{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}_{t}{\bm{x}}\geq 0\forall t
(by the positive semidefinitness of each
{\bm{C}}_{t}
).
Since each
{\bm{C}}_{t}\succeq 0
, there exists
{\bm{C}}_{t}^{1/2}
such that
{\bm{C}}_{t}={\bm{C}}_{t}^{1/2}{\bm{C}}_{t}^{1/2}
.
Thus

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}_{t}{\bm{x}}=0\quad\forall t

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\|{\bm{C}}_{t}^{1/2}{\bm{x}}\|^{2}=0\quad\forall t

(10)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow{\bm{C}}_{t}^{1/2}{\bm{x}}=0\quad\forall t

(11)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow{\bm{C}}_{t}{\bm{x}}=0\quad\forall t,

(12)

So

\displaystyle{\bm{B}}{\bm{x}}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{W}}_{t}{\bm{C}}_{t}{\bm{x}}=0.

(13)

Thus, for each row
{\bm{b}}
of
{\bm{B}}
,

{\bm{b}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}=0\quad\forall{\bm{x}}\in\ker({\bm{A}}),

(14)

which implies
{\bm{b}}\in\operatorname{Im}({\bm{A}})
.
Therefore, the system
{\bm{A}}{\bm{w}}={\bm{b}}
admits a solution for every row, and hence
{\bm{W}}{\bm{A}}={\bm{B}}
admits a solution.
It is easy to check that all solutions are of the form

{\bm{W}}={\bm{W}}^{\star}+{\bm{Z}}\left(\mathbb{I}_{D_{i}}-{\bm{A}}{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}\right)\ \forall{\bm{Z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{o}\times D_{i}}.

(15)

So the solution is unique iff the free term always vanishes, that is, iff
{\bm{A}}{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}=\mathbb{I}_{D_{i}}
.
Since
{\bm{A}}{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}
is the orthogonal projector onto
\operatorname{Im}({\bm{A}})
, this happens iff
{\bm{A}}
is invertible, or equivalently, if
{\bm{A}}
is positive definite (since
{\bm{A}}\succeq 0
as a sum of positive semidefinite matrices), or equivalently, if
\{0\}=\ker\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{C}}_{t}\right)=\bigcap_{t=1}^{T}\ker({\bm{C}}_{t})
.
∎

Lemma B.2
.

Let
{\bm{W}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{o}\times D_{i}}
for
t=1\dots T
. Define
{\bm{C}}_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{z}}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}[{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}]\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{i}\times D_{i}}
for each
t=1\dots T
, where
{\bm{z}}
denote a
D_{i}
-dimensional random vector distributed according to
\mathcal{D}_{t}
. Then the matrix
{\bm{W}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{o}\times D_{i}}
defined by

{\bm{W}}^{\star}=\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{W}}_{t}{\bm{C}}_{t}\right)\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{C}}_{t}\right)^{\dagger},

(16)

is the minimum Frobenius norm solution to the problem

\min_{{\bm{W}}}g({\bm{W}}),\quad g({\bm{W}})=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{z}}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}\bigl[\|{\bm{W}}{\bm{z}}-{\bm{W}}_{t}{\bm{z}}\|_{2}^{2}\bigr].

(17)

Proof.

Expanding
g({\bm{W}})
and using the cyclic property of the trace, we get

\displaystyle g({\bm{W}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{z}}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}\left[{\bm{z}}^{\top}({\bm{W}}-{\bm{W}}_{t})^{\top}({\bm{W}}-{\bm{W}}_{t}){\bm{z}}\right]

(18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{z}}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}\tr\left(({\bm{W}}-{\bm{W}}_{t})^{\top}({\bm{W}}-{\bm{W}}_{t}){\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\right)

(19)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tr\left(({\bm{W}}-{\bm{W}}_{t})^{\top}({\bm{W}}-{\bm{W}}_{t}){\bm{C}}_{t}\right).

(20)

So

\nabla_{{\bm{W}}}g({\bm{W}})=\sum_{t=1}^{T}({\bm{W}}-{\bm{W}}_{t})({\bm{C}}_{t}+{\bm{C}}_{t}^{\top})=2\sum_{t=1}^{T}({\bm{W}}-{\bm{W}}_{t}){\bm{C}}_{t},

(21)

where the last equality uses the fact that
{\bm{C}}_{t}
is symmetric.
Setting the gradient to zero and rearranging the terms leads to

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\bm{W}}}g({\bm{W}})=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow{\bm{W}}{\bm{A}}={\bm{B}},

(22)

with
{\bm{A}}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{C}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{i}\times D_{i}}
and
{\bm{B}}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{W}}_{t}{\bm{C}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{o}\times D_{i}}
.
By Lemma
B.1
, this equation always admits a solution since each
{\bm{C}}_{t}
is a covariance matrix, and thus symmetric positive semidefinite.
The minimum-Frobenius-norm solution is

{\bm{W}}={\bm{B}}{\bm{A}}^{\dagger}=\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{W}}_{t}{\bm{C}}_{t}\right)\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{C}}_{t}\right)^{\dagger}={\bm{W}}^{\star}

(23)

∎

Proof.

After
K
iterations of full-batch gradient descent with learning rate
\eta
, the difference matrix can be written as

{\bm{\Delta}}_{t}={\bm{W}}^{(K+1)}-{\bm{W}}^{(0)}=-\eta\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{x}}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}\!\left[{\bm{g}}^{(k)}{\bm{z}}^{(k)\top}\right]=-\eta\,\overline{{\bm{G}}},

and therefore
{\bm{\Delta}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{\Delta}}_{t}=\eta^{2}\,\overline{{\bm{G}}}^{\top}\overline{{\bm{G}}}
.
Successively applying the triangle inequality for angular distance,

\theta\!\big({\bm{\Delta}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{\Delta}}_{t},\;{\bm{C}}_{t}^{(K)}\big)\;\leq\;\theta\!\big({\bm{\Delta}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{\Delta}}_{t},\;\overline{{\bm{G}}}^{\top}\overline{{\bm{G}}}\big)\;+\;\theta\!\big(\overline{{\bm{G}}}^{\top}\overline{{\bm{G}}},\;\overline{{\bm{S}}}\big)\;+\;\theta\!\big(\overline{{\bm{S}}},\;\widetilde{{\bm{S}}}\big)\;+\;\theta\!\big(\widetilde{{\bm{S}}},\;{\bm{C}}_{t}^{(K)}\big).

Since
{\bm{\Delta}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{\Delta}}_{t}
and
\overline{{\bm{G}}}^{\top}\overline{{\bm{G}}}
are collinear, the first term vanishes, giving

\theta\!\big({\bm{\Delta}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{\Delta}}_{t},\;{\bm{C}}_{t}^{(K)}\big)\;\leq\;{\epsilon}^{(\mathrm{cross})}+{\epsilon}^{(\mathrm{corr})}+{\epsilon}^{(\mathrm{drift})}.

When all three errors vanish,
\measuredangle({\bm{\Delta}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{\Delta}}_{t},\;{\bm{C}}_{t}^{(K)})=0
, which implies
{\bm{\Delta}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{\Delta}}_{t}\propto{\bm{C}}_{t}^{(K)}
.
∎

B.3
Negative Transfer Upper Bound

The layer-wise objective in Equation (
1
) is desirable as it admits a tractable closed-form solution.
However, this comes at the cost of considering each layer independently, ignoring cross-layer interactions and nonlinearities.
Thus, a natural concern is whether minimizing this layer-wise interference objective also minimizes some global objective that we ultimately care about.
Remarkably,
41
showed that layer-wise interference provides an upper bound on the
negative transfer
, which is defined as

\Delta\ell_{t}({\bm{x}})=|\ell\left(f\left({\bm{x}};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{m}\right)\right)-\ell\left(f\left({\bm{x}};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{t}\right)\right)|,

(24)

where
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{D}\to\mathbb{R}
is a loss function applied to the output of network
f
and
{\bm{\theta}}_{m}
is the merged parameter vector. In this section, we extend their result and show that negative transfer can be upper-bounded by a summation of layer-wise interference errors and covariance estimation errors.

Suppose neural network
f
decomposes layer-wise as
f=g^{(L)}\circ\cdots\circ g^{(1)}
, where
g^{(l)}({\bm{z}};\,{\bm{\theta}}^{(l)})
is the function computed by layer
l
on input
{\bm{z}}
. Denoting the composed output at layer
l
by
f^{(l)}
, we have that

f^{(0)}({\bm{x}};\,{\bm{\theta}})={\bm{x}},\;f^{(l)}({\bm{x}};\,{\bm{\theta}})=g^{(l)}(f^{(l-1)}({\bm{x}};\,{\bm{\theta}});\;{\bm{\theta}}^{(l)}).

Define the local error at layer
l
as

\displaystyle\Delta f^{(l)}_{t}({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\|f^{(l)}({\bm{x}};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{m})-f^{(l)}({\bm{x}};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{t})\|,

(25)

\displaystyle\Delta g^{(l)}_{t}({\bm{z}})

\displaystyle=\|g^{(l)}({\bm{z}};\;{\bm{\theta}}_{m}^{(l)})-g^{(l)}({\bm{z}};\;{\bm{\theta}}_{t}^{(l)})\|.

(26)

Intuitively,
\Delta g^{(l)}_{t}
measures the change in the output of layer
l
when only that layer’s parameters are replaced by the merged parameters.
We show in the following theorem that the expected negative transfer can be upper bounded by a summation of these local errors and covariance estimation errors.

See
3.3

Proof.

Let
{\bm{z}}^{(l)}=f^{(l-1)}({\bm{x}};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{t})
and
\tilde{{\bm{z}}}^{(l)}=f^{(l-1)}({\bm{x}};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{m})
denote the input to layer
l
under the fine-tuned and merged parameters, respectively. By the
\beta
-Lipschitz continuity of
\ell
,

\Delta\ell_{t}({\bm{x}})=\big|\ell\big(f^{(L)}({\bm{x}};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{m})\big)-\ell\big(f^{(L)}({\bm{x}};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{t})\big)\big|\leq\beta\,\Delta f_{t}^{(L)}({\bm{x}}),

(27)

where
\Delta f_{t}^{(L)}({\bm{x}})
is the propagated error as defined in Equation (
25
).
We can bound
\Delta f_{t}^{(L)}({\bm{x}})
recursively. For each layer
l
, we have that

\displaystyle\Delta f_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\big\|g^{(l)}(\tilde{{\bm{z}}}^{(l)};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{m}^{(l)})-g^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{t}^{(l)})\big\|

\displaystyle=\big\|g^{(l)}(\tilde{{\bm{z}}}^{(l)};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{m}^{(l)}){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\,-\,g^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{m}^{(l)})}

\displaystyle\qquad{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}+\,g^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{m}^{(l)})}-g^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{t}^{(l)})\big\|

(28)

\displaystyle\leq\big\|g^{(l)}(\tilde{{\bm{z}}}^{(l)};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{m}^{(l)})-g^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{m}^{(l)})\big\|

\displaystyle\qquad+\big\|g^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{m}^{(l)})-g^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{t}^{(l)})\big\|

(29)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{(l)}\,\big\|\tilde{{\bm{z}}}^{(l)}-{\bm{z}}^{(l)}\big\|+\Delta g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)})

(30)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{(l)}\,\Delta f_{t}^{(l-1)}({\bm{x}})+\Delta g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)}),

(31)

where Equation (
28
) adds and subtracts
g^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};\,{\bm{\theta}}_{m}^{(l)})
, Equation (
29
) applies the triangle inequality and Equation (
30
) uses
\gamma^{(l)}
-Lipschitz continuity of
g^{(l)}
in its first argument. Unrolling Equation (
31
) gives

\displaystyle\Delta f_{t}^{(L)}({\bm{x}})\leq\left(\prod_{l=1}^{L}\gamma^{(l)}\right)\Delta f_{t}^{(0)}(x)+\sum_{l=1}^{L}\left(\prod_{m=l+1}^{L}\gamma^{(m)}\right)\Delta g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)}).

(32)

with the convention
\prod_{m=l+1}^{L}\gamma^{(m)}=1
for
l=L
.
Combining this with Equation (
27
) and using
\Delta f_{t}^{(0)}({\bm{x}})=0
leads to

\Delta\ell_{t}({\bm{x}})\leq\beta\sum_{l=1}^{L}\left(\prod_{m=l+1}^{L}\gamma^{(m)}\right)\Delta g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)})=\sum_{l=1}^{L}\widetilde{\gamma}^{(l)}\,\Delta g^{(l)}_{t}({\bm{z}}^{(l)}).

(33)

with

\widetilde{\gamma}^{(l)}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\beta\prod_{m=l+1}^{L}\gamma^{(m)}&\mbox{for }l<L\\
\beta&\mbox{for }l=L\end{array}\right.

(34)

Using the triangle inequality, we can bound
\Delta g^{(l)}_{t}({\bm{z}}^{(l)})
as follows:

\displaystyle\Delta g^{(l)}_{t}({\bm{z}}^{(l)})

\displaystyle=\|g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};{\bm{\theta}}_{m}^{(l)})-g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};{\bm{\theta}}_{t}^{(l)})\|

\displaystyle=\|g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};{\bm{\theta}}_{m}^{(l)}){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};{\bm{\theta}}^{\star(l)})+g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};{\bm{\theta}}^{\star(l)})}-g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};{\bm{\theta}}_{t}^{(l)})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};{\bm{\theta}}_{m}^{(l)})-g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};{\bm{\theta}}^{\star(l)})\|+\|g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};{\bm{\theta}}^{\star(l)})-g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};{\bm{\theta}}_{t}^{(l)})\|

\displaystyle=\Delta g_{t}^{\star(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)})+\|g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};{\bm{\theta}}^{\star(l)})-g_{t}^{(l)}({\bm{z}}^{(l)};{\bm{\theta}}_{m}^{(l)})\|
