Ratio of Left Atrial and Ventricular Volume as New Marker of Atrial Cardiopathy and Stroke Risk
Authors: Julian Deseoe, Martin Hänsel, Lisa Herzog, Neda Davoudi, Andreas R. Luft, Catherine Gebhard, Bjoern Menze, Beate Sick, Susanne Wegener
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Atrial cardiopathy is an important cause of embolic stroke and a potential cause of cognitive impairment. Increased left atrial volume indexed to body surface area (LAVi) has been widely used as a marker for atrial cardiopathy. However, because physiological remodeling, for example, due to exercise, may also increase LAVi, it lacks specificity. Left atrial to ventricular volume (LA:LV) ratio has been suggested as an improved marker of atrial cardiopathy, allowing detection of imbalanced, pathological atrial remodeling. We investigated if LA:LV ratio is associated with different sequelae of atrial cardiopathy.METHODS:We analyzed data from 2 cohorts, the population-based UK Biobank cohort (n=38 848) and a cohort of patients with ischemic stroke from the University Hospital Zürich (n=1273). In the UK Biobank cohort, we compared the association of LAVi and LA:LV ratio with risk of incident ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack ascertained from linked health records, using competing risks survival analysis. We also investigated the association with cognitive function using linear regression models. In the ischemic stroke patient cohort, we compared LAVi and LA:LV ratio for identifying atrial fibrillation/flutter as a cause of stroke.RESULTS:While LAVi was not significantly associated with risk of ischemic stroke/transient ischemic attack (adjusted hazard ratio, 1.11 [95% CI, 0.97–1.26]; P=0.14), a larger LA:LV ratio was (adjusted hazard ratio, 1.15 [95% CI, 1.01–1.30]; P=0.04). Besides, LA:LV ratio was more strongly associated with worse cognitive function. In a stroke patient cohort, LA:LV ratio significantly outperformed LAVi at identifying atrial fibrillation/flutter as underlying cause of ischemic stroke compared with LAVi (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, 0.856 [95% CI, 0.803–0.908] versus 0.808 [95% CI, 0.750–0.866]; P=0.03).CONCLUSIONS:We provide evidence that LA:LV ratio is a strong, novel marker of atrial cardiopathy. Hence, LA:LV ratio has the potential to improve the diagnosis of atrial cardiopathy, facilitating the prophylaxis of ischemic stroke and maintaining brain health.

Atrial cardiopathy is a structural and functional disorder of the atria, which is seen as both a potential cause and consequence of atrial fibrillation/flutter (AF).1 It has also been associated with a higher risk of stroke,2 as well as lower cognitive performance,3 even in the absence of AF. One leading explanatory hypothesis is that due to remodeling in atrial cardiopathy, thrombogenicity of the atria increases, leading to increased risk of stroke.4 Similarly, cerebral microembolism due to atrial cardiopathy may lead to cognitive decline even in the absence of ischemic stroke although the mechanisms linking atrial cardiopathy and cognitive function are not yet clear.3,5,6 Atrial cardiopathy has also been linked to cortical atrophy.3 There is evidence that anticoagulation may reduce future ischemic strokes in patients with atrial cardiopathy, even in the absence of AF.7 However, the diagnosis of atrial cardiopathy in the absence of AF is not yet reliable enough to justify anticoagulation.

Although the importance of atrial cardiopathy is increasingly apparent, reliable methods for its diagnosis are still sparse.4 Large left atrial volume indexed to body surface area (LAVi) has been commonly used as an indicator of atrial cardiopathy.8 Increased LAVi has been associated with increased risk of ischemic stroke9–11 and poorer cognitive function3,12,13 although there has been variation between studies. In clinical practice, increased LAVi can indicate patients with ischemic stroke with a high likelihood of AF as the underlying stroke cause.14

However, the use of LAVi for the detection of atrial cardiopathy has important limitations. Physiological remodeling of the left atrium, for example, due to physical activity, can also lead to increased LAVi, complicating its use as a marker of pathological remodeling.15–19 Recently, the left atrial to ventricular volume (LA:LV) ratio, defined as the ratio between the maximal left atrial volume at end systole and left ventricular end diastolic volume, has been proposed to better differentiate between balanced, physiological, and imbalanced, pathological left atrial enlargement.19–21 This is based on the observation that physiological remodeling, such as that induced by exercise, typically results in proportional increases in both left atrial volume and left ventricular volume, thereby maintaining a relatively stable LA:LV ratio.15–17,19 However, associations of LA:LV ratio with known sequelae of atrial cardiopathy impacting brain health have never been systematically studied.

We aimed to compare LA:LV ratio and LAVi as markers of atrial cardiopathy by investigating their associations with different sequelae of atrial cardiopathy. First, we evaluated the associations of LAVi and LA:LV ratio with the risk of ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA), cognitive function, and cortical atrophy in subpopulations of the population-based UK Biobank (UKBB) cohort. Second, we examined the predictive performance of LAVi and LA:LV ratio for identifying AF as the underlying cause of stroke in a clinical cohort of patients with ischemic stroke from the University Hospital Zürich (USZ).

Methods

In this study, we analyzed data from 2 distinct cohorts: the population-based UKBB cohort and a cohort of patients with ischemic stroke from the USZ.

Association Between Left Atrial Indices and Risk of Ischemic Stroke/TIA, Cognitive Function, and Cortical Atrophy in the UKBB

The UKBB is a large population-based cohort study, which holds data of 502 128 participants from the UK. The recruitment protocol is publicly available.23 The UKBB imaging study contains, among other assessments, cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging for over 50 000 participants.24 We worked with the data release from March 2025 (version 19). From the CMR imaging, cardiac chamber volumes have been previously extracted using deep learning and made accessible to researchers.25 LAVi and LA:LV ratio were standardized to have a mean of zero and an SD of 1 to facilitate comparison of effect sizes. Cognitive function was ascertained from 9 cognitive tests performed as part of the UKBB assessments. These include the tower rearranging test, the trail making test, the reaction time test, the digit symbol substitution test, the numeric memory test, the paired associate learning test, the prospective memory test, the fluid intelligence test, and the matrix pattern completion test.

Most patients undergoing CMR imaging also underwent brain magnetic resonance imaging as part of UKBB assessments. From brain magnetic resonance imaging, volumes of cortical parcellations were automatically extracted and made available to researchers. Details on the ascertainment of all end points can be found in the Supplemental Methods.

Participant Selection

Data of all participants with available information on LA volume were considered for analysis. For assessment of associations with risk of stroke/TIA, patients with AF diagnosed before the imaging date, as well as patients with heart failure, were excluded. Participants with AF were excluded as quantification of cardiac volumes with CMR in these patients can be imprecise.26 Patients with heart failure were excluded as the interplay between left atrial volume and left ventricular volume changes in these patients.27 Hence, these likely warrant a separate investigation. In addition, participants with prior stroke or TIA were excluded. All participants with incomplete data were excluded. Details of how participants with AF and heart failure were identified can be found in the Supplemental Methods.

For assessment of association with cognitive function, the same inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied with 2 exceptions. We excluded all participants who did not undergo complete cognitive testing, including all 9 tests. No imputation of cognitive scores was done. Patients with prior TIA were not excluded, while those with preexisting dementia were excluded. An overview of the inclusion and exclusion of participants can be found in Figure 1.

Statistical Analysis

All analyses were performed using R Studio with R, version 4.4.0. For assessment of associations of LAVi and LA:LV with ischemic stroke/TIA risk, a competing risks analysis treating ischemic stroke/TIA and death as competing events was performed. We calculated cause-specific and subdistribution hazard models28 and performed the same regressions for the competing event, death. We used both unadjusted models and models adjusted for the covariates shown in Table 1. Details on ascertainment of covariates can be found in the Supplemental Material and Table S1. We tested for violation of the proportional hazard assumption by assessing Schoenfeld residuals.29 We investigated sex differences by including an interaction term between LA:LV ratio and sex, and LAVi and sex, respectively, in separate models.

To increase clinical interpretability of our findings, we calculated sex-specific cutoffs for increased LAVi and LA:LV ratio, setting the cutoff at 2 SDs above the mean for male and female participants separately. We then plotted cumulative incidence functions and calculated 5- and 10-year cumulative incidence for stroke/TIA stratified by binary LAVi and LA:LV ratio size, respectively.

Linear regression models were calculated to assess the association of LAVi and LA:LV ratio with cognitive function, both in unadjusted and fully adjusted models. We investigated a potential nonlinear association between LAVi or LA:LV ratio and cognitive function using a restricted cubic spline with 3 nodes.30 Significance of nonlinear components was assessed using the partial F test. Finally, we investigated whether there was an interaction between sex and LAVi or sex and LA:LV ratio for the association with cognitive function.

We then assessed the associations of LAVi and LA:LV ratio with magnetic resonance image–measured volumes of 66 cortical parcellations according to Desikan-Killiany parcellation31 using linear regression models adjusted for age, sex, and intracranial volume. We did not adjust for total cortical gray matter volume. We adjusted for multiple testing using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure.32 The temporal pole parcellation is not available in the UKBB data due to its unreliable segmentation and was thus excluded. Details can be found in the Supplemental Methods. We again excluded all participants with previous stroke, AF, heart failure, or missing data.

Left Atrial Indices for Detection of AF as Cause of Stroke in Patients With Ischemic Stroke From the USZ

Data of all patients treated for ischemic stroke at the USZ in the years 2023 and 2024 were retrospectively collected. We received permission from the Cantonal Ethics Committee Zürich to perform this data collection (PREDICT, BASEC-ID: PB_2016_01751). All patients who had given consent for the use of their routine medical data for research were included. For patients who had not refused use of their routine clinical data but had never given consent, the need for consent was waived by the Cantonal Ethics Committee Zürich. Cardiac volumes were extracted from echocardiography reports, routinely performed after ischemic stroke. For the values to be included in the study, the echocardiography had to be performed after the ischemic stroke diagnosis and before the outpatient checkup ≈3 months after the stroke. Left atrial volume was measured using the biplane method, at the end of systole.33,34 Patients with heart failure were once again excluded. Details can be found in the Supplemental Methods.

The cause of stroke was identified from the 3-month checkup report if available or the discharge report. Similar to previous studies,14 stroke causes were categorized into 3 subgroups: (1) AF, (2) other determined cause, and (3) unknown cause/incomplete workup. Detailed criteria are shown in Table S2. Patients with an unknown cause or incomplete workup were excluded from the analysis. An overview can be found in Figure 1. Excluding patients with an unknown cause or incomplete workup allowed us to compare patients in whom atrial cardiopathy manifesting as AF was the likely stroke cause to patients in which it was unlikely to be the cause of stroke as another plausible cause had been found. In contrast, in patients with an unknown cause despite a complete workup, which patients have atrial cardiopathy as stroke cause is more difficult to define as atrial cardiopathy might lead to ischemic stroke even in the absence of AF.2

Statistical Analysis

Logistic regression models for the association of standardized LAVi and LA:LV ratio with AF being identified as a stroke cause were calculated for LAVi and LA:LV ratio, both in unadjusted models and adjusting for age and sex. Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC-AUC) was calculated for LAVi and LA:LV ratio for classifying stroke cause as either AF or other determined stroke cause and compared using the DeLong test.35 Then, the ideal cutoff point in our sample for both parameters was calculated by maximizing the Youden index.36 Using 10-fold cross-validation, sensitivity and specificity, as well as positive and negative likelihood ratios, were calculated for the 2 parameters. We also evaluated predictive performance in male and female patients separately. As a sensitivity analysis, we excluded all patients with a diagnosis of AF before the stroke and again compared ROC-AUC values for LAVi and LA:LV ratio.

Finally, we investigated if the LA:LV ratio held complementary information to MR proANP, a blood biomarker, which is also used to indicate atrial cardiopathy.37 Details can be found in the Supplemental Material.

Results

Figure 1 shows the inclusion/exclusion flow chart for participants included in this study. For the analysis of atrial indices and risk of ischemic stroke/TIA, 26 955 participants were included; 7551 (19%) of the participants had missing covariates. For analysis of cognitive function, 17 133 participants were included. Again, 7551 (19%) of the participants had missing covariates. In addition, 11 686 (30%) of the participants did not undergo complete cognitive testing. Hence, although both analyses started with the same initial UKBB data set, a substantial number of participants lacked complete cognitive assessments, resulting in a smaller final sample size. However, characteristics between the UKBB ischemic stroke/TIA cohort and the UKBB cognitive function cohort were similar. A summary of cohort characteristics is shown in Table 1.

Cardiac chamber volumes as measured by echocardiography in the clinical cohort were smaller compared with the UKBB cohort (LAVi: 38 versus 32 mL/m2; left ventricular end diastolic volume indexed to body surface area [LVEDVi]: 78 versus 48 mL/m2), which is consistent with the known tendency of echocardiography, to underestimate chamber volumes compared with CMR.38

We visually investigated the association of LAVi and LA:LV ratio with age, sex, and everyday physical activity (Figure 2). LAVi showed a positive association with physical activity. Women had larger mean LA:LV ratios than men, while no such sex difference was observed for LAVi. LAVi has been shown to be similar between sexes, while men typically have larger LVEDVi, explaining our findings.39 These results were also confirmed using linear regression models (Tables S3 and S4). To account for potential sex differences, all subsequent analyses were controlled for sex and investigated for sex-specific effects. Thus, cardiac volumes reported in this study confirmed findings from existing literature.

We then investigated the association of LA:LV ratio and LAVi with risk of ischemic stroke/TIA in participants without prior diagnosis of stroke, TIA, AF, and heart failure. In the UKBB cohort, LAVi was not significantly associated with incident ischemic stroke both in unadjusted (Table S5) and fully adjusted models (Table 3). In contrast, a larger LA:LV ratio was significantly associated with an increased risk of future ischemic stroke/TIA (adjusted hazard ratio, 1.15 [95% CI, 1.01–1.30]] P=0.04). When additionally including interaction terms with sex to the adjusted models, we observed no significant interactions between sex and LAVi (P=0.14) and sex and LA:LV ratio (P=0.83), meaning that there is no significant evidence for sex differences for these associations. No significant associations were found between cardiac volumes and mortality (Table S6). Results for subdistribution hazard models showed similar results (Tables S7 and S8).

The cutoff for increased LAVi size, defined as 2 SDs above the mean for each sex, was similar in men (59 mL/m2) and women (58 mL/m2). The cutoff for the LA:LV ratio was higher in women (0.76) compared with men (0.69), which is consistent with our previous finding that women tend to have a larger LA:LV ratio. Increased LA:LV ratio showed better risk stratification for incident ischemic stroke/TIA compared with increased LAVi with 5- and 10-year incidence being around twice as high for participants with increased LA:LV ratio compared with those with normal LA:LV ratio (5 years: 1.5% [95% CI, 0.9%–2.7%] versus 0.8% [95% CI, 0.7%–0.9%]; 10 years: 2.2% [95% CI, 1.3%–4.0%] versus 1.0% [95% CI, 0.9%–1.1%]; Figure S1; Table S9).

Atrial cardiopathy was previously found to be associated with cognitive decline and cortical atrophy, potentially caused by cerebral microembolism.3 Therefore, we tested for associations of LAVi and LA:LV ratio with cognitive function in participants without prior diagnosis of stroke, AF, dementia, or heart failure. Larger LA:LV ratio was more strongly associated with poorer cognitive function (regression coefficient, −0.024 [95% CI, − 0.032 to − 0.015]) than LAVi (regression coefficient, −0.010 [95% CI, −0.019 to −0.002]) in fully adjusted (Table 3) and unadjusted models (Table S5). No significant interactions for sex and LAVi (P=0.83) or LA:LV ratio (P=0.70) were observed. Using restricted cubic splines, we found no evidence of nonlinearity (Figure S2).

For the analysis of associations of LAVi and LA:LV ratio with cortical volumes, starting with 33 059 participants with both heart volume and cortical parcellations available, 301 were excluded due to prior stroke, 9 due to prior diagnosis of dementia, 931 due to prior diagnosis of AF, and 2864 due to signs of heart failure, leaving 28 954 patients in the final analysis. After adjustment for multiple testing, 3 significant associations between LAVi and volumes of cortical parcellations remained, while 24 significant associations were found for the association of LA:LV ratio and volumes of cortical parcellations. These associations showed atrophy especially in the temporal lobe, insula, and cingulate cortex (Figure 3; Table S10).

Next, we examined the association of standardized LAVi and LA:LV ratio with the probability of AF being identified as the cause of stroke in a cohort of 289 patients with ischemic stroke from the USZ. All the patients included in the analysis in which a non-AF cause of the stroke had been identified had undergone a minimum of 48 hours of rhythm monitoring; 191 of the 213 patients without AF underwent 48 hours of rhythm monitoring, 1 patient underwent 72 hours of rhythm monitoring, 3 patients underwent 5 days of rhythm monitoring, 12 patients underwent 7 days of rhythm monitoring, 5 patients underwent 30 days of rhythm monitoring, and 1 patient had an loop recorder implanted before the stroke. Of the patients in whom AF was identified as the cause of stroke, 26 had a prior diagnosis of AF, 29 were newly diagnosed with AF on a 12-lead ECG before long-term rhythm monitoring, and 21 were newly diagnosed during long-term rhythm monitoring. Results of the analysis are shown in Table 3. Both standardized LAVi and LA:LV ratio were strongly associated with AF as the stroke cause, with the effect size of LA:LV ratio being slightly larger in adjusted models (LA:LV ratio: log odds ratio, 1.7 [95% CI, 1.1–2.3]; P<0.001; LAVi: log odds ratio, 1.4 [95% CI, 0.9–1.9]; P<0.001) and unadjusted models (Table S4). The LA:LV ratio showed a significantly higher ROC-AUC compared with the LAVi for identifying AF as the stroke cause (0.856 [95% CI, 0.803–0.908] versus 0.808 [95% CI, 0.750–0.866]; P=0.03; Figure 4). LA:LV ratio outperformed LAVi on all calculated test metrics (Table 3).

Area under the curve (AUC) with 95% CI is shown in the bottom right. CIs were calculated based on 1000 bootstrap samples. P value was calculated with the DeLong test.

A sex-stratified analysis showed that the LA:LV ratio showed higher ROC-AUC for identifying AF as the stroke cause than LAVi in both sexes. The ideal cutoff as calculated by the Youden Index was higher for women compared with men (women: 0.85 [95% CI, 0.69–0.96]; men: 0.71 [95% CI, 0.55–0.86]; Table S11), which aligns with findings from the UKBB; women showed a higher average LA:LV ratio. For LAVi, cutoffs were lower than the ones calculated in the UKBB cohort, which can be explained by the differing imaging modalities. Cutoffs were similar for the LA:LV ratio between the USZ stroke cohort and the UKBB cohort.

When excluding all patients with diagnosis of AF before the stroke, LA:LV ratio continued to show a significantly better ROC-AUC compared with LAVi (LA:LV ratio, 0.847 [95% CI, 0.785–0.909]; LAVi, 0.776 [95% CI, 0.702–0.850]; P=0.01).

Finally, we aimed to capture the effect of combining LA-LV with current clinical and blood biomarkers for the prediction of atrial cardiopathy. In a logistic regression model including LA:LV ratio, MR proANP, age, and sex, both higher MR proANP and higher LA:LV ratio were significantly associated with an increased likelihood of AF as a stroke cause (Table S12). The multivariate model showed an improved ROC-AUC for predicting AF as stroke cause (0.897 [95% CI, 0.849–0.945]) compared with LA:LV ratio alone although the difference was not statistically significant (P=0.28; Figure S3).

Discussion

We found that in different cohorts and independent of assessment modality (echocardiography versus CMR), the LA:LV ratio consistently showed stronger associations than LAVi with clinically relevant manifestations of atrial cardiopathy. In the UKBB, higher LA:LV ratios were linked to a greater risk of incident ischemic stroke/TIA in participants free of prior diagnosis of stroke, TIA, AF, or heart failure. We further calculate sex-specific cutoffs for increased LA:LV ratio and show that participants with increased LA:LV ratio have around double the risk of incident stroke/TIA (5-year risk, 1.5% [95% CI, 0.9%–2.7%] versus 0.8% [95% CI, 0.7%–0.9%]; 10-year risk, 2.2% [95% CI, 1.3%–4.0%] versus 1.0% [95% CI, 0.9%–1.1%]). Furthermore, the LA:LV ratio was associated with poorer cognitive function. In addition, a larger LA:LV ratio was significantly associated with cortical atrophy, especially in the temporal lobe, insula, and cingulate cortex, providing a potential link between a larger LA:LV ratio and worse cognitive function. Interestingly, atrophy in the temporal lobe, insula, and cingulate cortex has been previously linked to AF and atrial cardiopathy40,41 so that the cortex-specific associations found with LA:LV ratio could potentially represent an atrial cardiopathy–related brain imaging phenotype.

In patients with stroke, the LA:LV ratio showed improved diagnostic performance for detecting AF as the stroke cause, with higher ROC-AUCs in both sexes and again sex-specific optimal cutoffs.

Our findings align with our hypothesis that the LA:LV ratio is more strongly associated with sequelae of atrial cardiopathy than LAVi and show its potential as a marker for atrial cardiopathy. We hypothesize that this is because it addresses key limitations of LAVi for assessing atrial cardiopathy as it allows improved detection of imbalanced, pathological cardiac remodeling.

There is abundant evidence that left atrial and left ventricular function are interdependent.42 For example, changes in left atrial function influence left ventricular filling pressure and vice versa.42,43 The complex interplay of the left atrium and ventricle is not captured by measuring LAVi alone. In contrast, while no single marker could ever fully characterize these interactions, the LA:LV ratio is a straightforward way of assessing the left atrium while incorporating information from the left ventricle.

We hypothesize that, as it allows joint assessment of the left atrium and ventricle, the LA:LV ratio can better distinguish between balanced and unbalanced cardiac remodeling. While there is little literature directly comparing the effects of balanced and unbalanced atrial remodeling on the risk of cardiovascular events, there is some evidence comparing the effects of left atrial enlargement in physically active and inactive people. Participants with left atrial enlargement who were physically active had a significantly lower risk of AF compared with inactive participants with left atrial enlargement.44 Furthermore, physical activity has been associated with reduced risk of AF and stroke.45,46 Therefore, an increased LAVi with unchanged or decreased LVEDVi may better represent an atrial cardiopathy phenotype, which would be associated with increased risk of stroke, worse cognitive function, and increased probability of AF as a stroke cause. Large LAVi coupled with increased LVEDVi, on the other hand, may be more representative of physiological changes, for example, induced by increased physical activity and, thus, less indicative of adverse cardiovascular events. However, more, especially longitudinal, studies are needed to further investigate this hypothesis.

The LA:LV ratio could easily be incorporated into clinical practice because the required measures LAVi and LVEDVi are routinely obtained in echocardiography or cardiac imaging. We demonstrate this by validating the LA:LV ratio in a clinical cohort. In patients with ischemic stroke, early detection of atrial cardiopathy is essential, as patients with atrial fibrillation require anticoagulation to be adequately protected from stroke recurrence.47 The LA:LV ratio could improve the selection of patients for long-term rhythm monitoring to detect underlying AF. Furthermore, the LA:LV ratio could be evaluated in prospective clinical trials for its potential to identify patients with embolic stroke of undetermined source, which might benefit from anticoagulation, even in the absence of AF.7 Hence, this study should inspire prospective multicenter trials to facilitate the establishment of the LA:LV ratio in clinical practice, especially as part of the diagnostic workup of ischemic stroke. Based on our findings, optimal cutoffs will likely be sex- and modality-specific.

Limitations

Due to the large sample size of the UKBB, statistically significant but not clinically relevant associations might be detected. This is especially a potential limitation for associations of the LA:LV ratio with ischemic stroke/TIA in the UKBB cohort, where the effect size was only borderline significant. Due to the exploratory nature of this study, we did not define a priori minimum effect sizes for differences in associations of LAVi and LA:LV ratio with the different end points. In addition, the overall incidence of ischemic stroke/TIA was low in the UK Biobank, as it is a population-based cohort, without selection of patients with increased cardiovascular risk. Furthermore, the associations we report with cognitive function are only cross-sectional, which does not allow for inferring a causal relationship. While longitudinal studies have established a link between atrial cardiopathy and cognitive dysfunction,5,6 the mechanisms behind this association and the clinical implications of these findings need further investigation. It is also important to note that we did not adjust our analysis on associations of LAVi and LA:LV ratio with cortical volumes for total gray matter volume as this would have masked associations with global cortical atrophy. Another limitation of our study is that we deliberately excluded subjects with heart failure, reduced ejection fraction, and LV dilation, as interaction between LAVi and LVEDVi is different in these patients. These subjects should be studied in dedicated cohorts. Finally, although longitudinal studies have been conducted on effects of exercise on heart volumes,15–17,19 supporting our mechanistic hypothesis, establishing causality is not possible with an observational, cross-sectional approach. Furthermore, it would have been interesting to correlate and compare the LA:LV ratio with other proposed imaging markers of atrial cardiopathy, such as left atrial strain.48 However, left atrial strain was not measured in the UKBB imaging protocol and is not routinely measured in clinical practice at the USZ.

The USZ stroke cohort also has its limitations. Importantly, to evaluate LAVi and LA:LV ratio for patients with AF identified as stroke cause versus those with known other cause, we excluded patients with embolic stroke of undetermined source although predicting AF and recurrent stroke in these patients is an important clinical need. We decided to exclude patients with embolic stroke of undetermined source, as defining which of these patients have a stroke due to atrial cardiopathy is challenging, as atrial cardiopathy can potentially lead to stroke even in the absence of AF.2 In these patients, the LA:LV ratio should be evaluated for the detection of atrial cardiopathy in prospective clinical trials, including a complete, standardized long-term rhythm monitoring and outcome assessment. Furthermore, most patients without the detection of AF only underwent 48 hours of rhythm monitoring. While this reflects standard clinical practice, longer rhythm monitoring might have led to the detection of AF in some of these patients.

Conclusions

We show that LA:LV is a promising new marker of atrial cardiopathy and demonstrate its associations with clinically relevant manifestations of atrial cardiopathy. Furthermore, we show its potential value for guiding diagnostic workup post-ischemic stroke. Prospective, multicenter studies are needed to further validate our findings and establish sex-specific reference values to facilitate the use of the LA:LV ratio in clinical practice.
