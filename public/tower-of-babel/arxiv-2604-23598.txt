1.
Introduction

In this article, we aim to study the relation between Sobolev extension properties of a given bounded open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the relation between Sobolev and fractional Sobolev spaces. The problem of finding a function
Ef:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, for a given function
f:\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
Ef|_{\Omega}=f
and
Ef
preserves the ‘regularity’ of
f
is a fundamental theme in analysis.

Formally, given
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s\in(0,1]
, we say that
\Omega
is a
(1,s,p)
-extension domain if there exists a bounded operator
E:\dot{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)\to\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
Ef|_{\Omega}=f
for all
f\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
– the homogeneous Sobolev space; for
s\in(0,1)
, the homogeneous fractional Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)
can be viewed as the fractional counterpart of
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. One of the main goals of this paper is to characterise bounded
(1,1,p)
-extension domains (written as
(1,p)
-extension domains for brevity) in terms of the relation between
\dot{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
\dot{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)
(or
\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) –the fractional order homogeneous Sobolev spaces. The reason behind restricting ourselves to homogeneous spaces is the fact that a bounded domain is a nonhomogeneous Sobolev extension domain if and only if it is a homogeneous Sobolev extension domain (see
[
14
, Corollary 4.9]
).

The search for characterisations of
(1,p)
-extension domains has spanned several decades, evolving from simple boundary smoothness conditions to deep connections with geometric measure theory and functional inequalities. Calderón
[
6
]
(for
1<p<\infty
) and Stein
[
29
]
(for
p=1
and
p=\infty
) have shown that Lipschitz domains are
(1,p)
-extension domains for
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Later, Jones
[
16
]
proved that the more general
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-domains are also
(1,p)
-extension domains. Hajłasz-Koskela-Tuominen
[
12
]
showed that a domain
\Omega
is a
(1,p)
-extension domain if and only if it satisfies Ahlfors regularity and
W^{1,p}(\Omega)=M^{1,p}(\Omega)
(see
Section

2
for definitions). Some other related characterisations were given by the same authors in
[
13
]
. Some geometric characterisations of
(1,p)
-extension domains can be found in
[
16
,
18
,
28
,
17
]
. For more discussion regarding examples of extension domains, see
[
22
]
.

In their seminal work, Bourgain, Brezis, and Mironescu
[
2
]
established a fundamental connection between nonlocal functionals and classical local derivatives (see also subsequent works such as
[
7
,
5
,
3
]
). Specifically, they showed that for a smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, the following limit holds:

\lim_{s\to 1-}(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx=K(n,p)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|^{p}\,dx.

Its primary significance lies in its ability to recover recover the
W^{1,p}
-seminorm as a limit of fractional Gagliardo-type seminorms, effectively bridging the gap between nonlocal and local theories. This identity, commonly referred to as the
BBM formula
in literature, has become a cornerstone in the study of Sobolev spaces and nonlocal operators
[
25
,
1
,
24
,
20
,
9
,
4
]
.

A key ingredient in the proof of the BBM formula is the accompanying
BBM inequality
: For a fixed
s_{0}\in(0,1)
,
s\in(s_{0},1)
, and
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we have

(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\leq C(n,p,\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|^{p}\,dx.

While the original formulation in
[
2
]
includes an additional
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
term on the right-hand side, it can be ignored in view of
[
14
, Corollary 4.9]
.

While the original result of Bourgain, Brezis, and Mironescu was stated for smooth domains, the role of the domain’s geometry remained a subject of significant interest. For instance, it was later observed by Drelichman and Duran
[
10
]
that the BBM-type limit can be recovered for any arbitrary bounded domain if the nonlocal seminorm is suitably restricted to a neighborhood of the diagonal (
x=y
), thereby bypassing the need for the extension property. These developments lead to a fundamental question: to what extent does the validity of the BBM inequality characterise the geometry of the domain? Specifically, one might ask whether the support of the BBM inequality necessarily implies that
\Omega
is a
(1,p)
-extension domain. While this turns out to be false for general domains (see
Sections

1
and
3
below), we show that the BBM inequality provides a sharp characterisation within the class of Ahlfors regular domains.

We now proceed to state our first main result, which states that, on Ahlfors regular domains, the validity of BBM inequality characterises the Sobolev extension property. More precisely, we have

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set,
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then the following are equivalent

(1)

\Omega
is a
(1,p)
-extension domain.

(2)

\Omega
is Ahlfors regular (see
Section

2
) and there exists
s_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

(1.1)

(1-s)[f]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{p}\lesssim[f]_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p},

for any
s\in(s_{0},1)
and
f\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

(3)

\Omega
is Ahlfors regular (see
Section

2
) and, there is some
s_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

(1.2)

(1-s)[f]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p},

for any
s\in(s_{0},1)
and
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Remark 1.2
.

In
Theorem

1.1
, in the implication
(2)

\implies

(1)
, neither of the two conditions Ahlfors regularity
eq.

2.1
and BBM inequality
eq.

1.2
can be dropped. To support this claim, we look at two examples: the interior cusp and the exterior cusp. We know that both are non-extension domains. In
[
8
]
, it is shown that the interior cusp does not support
eq.

1.2
(for
n=2
and
p>2
), but it can be checked that it satisfies
eq.

2.1
. On the other hand, we have shown in
Section

3
that the exterior cusp does not support Ahlfors regularity
eq.

2.1
, but it supports BBM inequality
eq.

1.2
.

A key ingredient in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
is our next result,
Theorem

1.3
, which can be compared to a result by Ponce
[
26

. Both

1.3
and
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
have a similar conclusion, but in
[

]
the hypothesis requires the domain to be a
(1,p)
-extension domain, whereas we require it to be only Ahlfors regular. Recall that there are Ahlfors regular domains which are not
(1,p)
-extension domains like a slit-disk
B(0,1)\setminus(\{0\}\times[0,1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Thus,
Theorem

1.3
can be seen as an improvement of
[

, with a proof without using heavy machinery. Also, we prove the result in
W^{s,p}_{q}
-space setup, which is a generalisation (along the Triebel-Lizorkin scales ) of fractional Sobolev spaces

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Ahlfors regular domain,
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
, and
s_{0}\in\left(\max{\{\frac{q-n}{q},0\}},1\right)
. Then, for any
s\in(s_{0},1)
and any

(1.3)

\fint_{B\cap\Omega}|f(x)-f_{B\cap\Omega}|^{p}\,dx\leq C(n,p,q)(1-s)^{\frac{p}{q}}\mathrm{diam}(B)^{sp}\fint_{B\cap\Omega}\left(\int_{B\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{n+sq}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

for any ball
B
.

As a direct consequence of
Theorem

1.3
, we can deduce the following, which we list here as it may be of interest to the readers.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
s_{0}\in\left(\max\left\{\frac{p-n}{p},0\right\},1\right)
, and
s\in(s_{0},1)
.

(1)

For a bounded domain
\Omega
, for any
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\fint_{y\in\Omega}

\displaystyle\left|f(y)-f_{\Omega}\right|^{p}\,dy\leq C(n,p)\frac{(1-s)\text{diam}(\Omega)^{sp+n}}{|\Omega|}\fint_{y\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{p}}{|y-z|^{n+sp}}\,dz\,dy.

(2)

We get, for any

,

\displaystyle\fint_{y\in B(x,\delta_{x})}

\displaystyle\left|f(y)-f_{B(x,\delta_{x})}\right|^{p}\,dy\leq C(n,p)\delta_{x}^{sp}(1-s)\fint_{y\in B(x,\delta_{x})}\int_{B(x,\delta_{x})}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{p}}{|y-z|^{n+sp}}\,dz\,dy.

Next, we want to provide a class of examples of (possibly non-Ahlfors regular) open sets supporting the BBM inequality. We do it in our next result using the notion of Hajłasz Sobolev spaces (see

Proposition 1.5

Let
1<p<\infty

(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\leq C(n,p,\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|^{p}.

This result allows us to construct specific examples of domains where the BBM inequality holds despite the failure of the

-extension property, as discussed in

In

, the characterisation requires a family of inequalities as

Preliminaries

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
\Omega\neq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By a cube, we shall understand a set of the form
c+\lambda[-1,1]^{n}
. For a cube
Q
, its diameter is denoted by
\mathrm{diam}{Q}
. We denote by
C
a generic positive constant that may vary from appearance to appearance. Whenever necessary, we shall emphasise the dependencies of
C
by writing
C(\cdots)
.

Definition 2.1
.

A set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be Ahlfors regular if

(2.1)

|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\geq C(\Omega,n)r^{n},\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega\ \text{and }r\in(0,\text{diam}(\Omega)/2),

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s\leq 1
. Given
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, the (fractional, when
s<1
) Sobolev seminorm is given by

[f]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla f(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\qquad&\text{ when }s=1\\
\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\ \right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\qquad&\text{ when }s<1.\end{cases}

We also define the norm, for
0<s\leq 1
,

\|f\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\left(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+[f]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We denote, by
\dot{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, the collection of all
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
such that
[f]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
is finite. The space
\dot{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, equipped with
[\cdot]_{W^{s,p}}
, is then called a homogeneous (fractional, when
s<1
) Sobolev space. The (fractional, when
s<1
) Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is defined to be
\dot{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{p}(\Omega)
, and it is equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{s,p}}(\Omega)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
and let
1<p<\infty
be given. A bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be a
(1,s,p)
-extension domain if there exists a bounded extension operator

E:\dot{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)\to\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

that is,
Ef_{|\Omega}=f
and there exist
C>0
such that

[Ef]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C[f]_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

We call a
(1,1,p)
-extension domain simply a
(1,p)
-extension domain.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The maximal function
\mathcal{M}f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
is defined by

\mathcal{M}f(x):=\sup_{Q\ni x}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|\,dy,

where
Q
denotes a cube.

Remark 2.5
.

In the above definition, one could replace the cubes
Q
by balls
B(y,r)
and still get the following result.

Theorem 2.6

(see
[
29
]
)
.

Let
1<p\leq\infty
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,p)>0
, such that

\|\mathcal{M}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let

\lambda\in(0,\infty)
and
\delta\in(0,\infty]
. Given a set

, a
\delta
-cover of

is any countable family of sets
\{B_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

and
\mathrm{diam}(B_{j})\leq\delta

The
\lambda

\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}_{\delta}(A):=\inf\{\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{diam}(B_{j})^{\lambda}:A\subset\cup_{j\geq 1}B_{j},\mathrm{diam}(B_{j})\leq\delta\}.

The
\lambda

\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}(A):=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}_{\delta}(A).

Definition 2.8
.

A function

is said to be an absolutely continuous function if for each

there exists a

\sum_{i=1}^{k}|f(b_{i})-f(a_{i})|<\varepsilon,

.

Let

.

Let

,

f\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)
. Then, there exists a Cartesian coordinate system such that, for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-almost every line
L
parallel to the axes,
f\in W^{s,p}(L\cap\Omega)
.

Next, we state the ACL characterisation of Sobolev spaces.

Lemma 2.17

(ACL characterisation)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and let
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

f\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
;

(2)

There exists
g\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
such that for each
i=1,\dots,n
,
for almost every line
\ell
parallel to the
x_{i}
-axis, the restriction

f|_{\ell\cap\Omega}
is absolutely continuous and

\frac{d}{dt}f(x+te_{i})=g_{i}(x+te_{i})\quad\text{for a.e. }t,

where
g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
.

In this case,
g_{i}=\partial_{i}f
in the weak sense.

Lemma 2.18

(Frostman Lemma, see
[
21
]
)
.

Let
A
be a Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
t>0
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{H}^{t}(A)>0
.

(2)

There is an unsigned Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
\mu(A)>0
, such that

\mu(B(x,r))\leq r^{t}

holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
.

Proof of the

Observe that

\int_{t=0}^{r}t^{q-sq-1}\,dt=\frac{r^{q-sq}}{q(1-s)}.

Hence, for every

and

,

\displaystyle\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\fint_{z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{q}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

\displaystyle\quad=\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\frac{q(1-s)}{r^{q-sq}}\int_{t=0}^{r}t^{q-sq-1}\fint_{z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{q}}\,dz\,dt\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}}{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{r^{q-sq}}\int_{t=0}^{r}t^{q-sq-1}\int_{z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{q}}\,dz\,dt\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}}{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{r^{q-sq}}\int_{t=0}^{r}t^{q-sq-1}\int_{z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega\cap B(y,t)}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{q}}\,dz\,dt\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}}{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{r^{q-sq}}\int_{t=0}^{r}t^{q-sq-1}\int_{z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega\setminus B(y,t)}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{q}}\,dz\,dt\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

\displaystyle\quad=I_{1}+I_{2}.

We evaluate
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
separately below. For
I_{1}

\Omega
:

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}}{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\frac{r^{n}}{r^{q-sq}}\int_{t=0}^{r}t^{q-sq-n-1}\int_{z\in B(y,t)\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{q}}\,dz\,dt\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}}{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\frac{r^{n}}{r^{q-sq}}\int_{z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\int_{t=|y-z|}^{r}t^{q-sq-n-1}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{q}}\,dt\,dz\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}}{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\frac{r^{n}}{r^{q-sq}}\int_{z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\int_{t=|y-z|}^{\infty}t^{q-sq-n-1}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{q}}\,dt\,dz\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}r^{(\frac{np}{q}+sp-p)}}{(n-q+sq)^{\frac{p}{q}}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\int_{z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{n+sq}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}r^{(sp-p)}}{(n-q+sq)^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\int_{z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{n+sq}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

For
I_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}}{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{r^{q-sq}}\int_{t=0}^{r}t^{q-sq-1}\int_{z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega\setminus B(y,t)}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{q}}\,dz\,dt\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}}{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{r^{q-sq}}\int_{t=0}^{r}t^{p-sp-1}\int_{z\in[B(x,r)\cap\Omega]\setminus B(y,t)}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{n+sq}}t^{n+sq-q}\,dt\,dz\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}}{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{r^{q-sq}}\int_{t=0}^{r}t^{n-1}\,dt\int_{z\in[B(x,r)\cap\Omega]\setminus B(y,t)}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{n+sq}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}r^{(sp-p)}}{(n-q+sq)^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\int_{z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{n+sq}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy

Adding
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, from our initial estimate, we get

(3.1)

\begin{split}&\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\fint_{z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{q}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}r^{(sp-p)}}{(n-q+sq)^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\int_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{n+sq}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy.\end{split}

For
y,z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega
implies

. So,

(3.2)

\begin{split}(2r)^{-p}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}&\left|f(y)-f_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\right|^{p}\,dy\\
&\leq\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\fint_{z\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{p}}{(2r)^{p}}\,dz\,dy\\
&\leq\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\fint_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{p}}{|y-z|^{p}}\,dz\,dy\\
&\leq\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\fint_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{q}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy\end{split}

By using
eq.

3.1
, from

3.2
we get that

\begin{split}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}&\left|f(y)-f_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\right|^{p}\,dy\\
&\leq\frac{(q(1-s))^{\frac{p}{q}}r^{sp}}{2^{p}(n-q+sq)^{\frac{p}{q}}}\fint_{y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\left(\int_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|^{q}}{|y-z|^{n+sq}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dy.\end{split}

∎

The following lemma establishes the fractional extension estimates. Our approach utilises the refined estimates from

1.3
to achieve the sharp

-dependence as

, which is essential for our main characterisation. This provides a more precise control over the constants than what is available in

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{B}:=B(0,R)
be an Ahlfors regular (see

2.1
) open set and

. Then there exists

such that for any

,

(3.3)

\int_{\mathbb{B}}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{|Ef(x)-Ef(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\leq C(n,p,\Omega,R)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume

, that is
\mathbb{B}=B(0,4)
as we can always move to the general case by a scaling. We also recall that an Ahlfors regular open set has a boundary of Lebesgue measure zero (see
[
12
, Lemma 3.5]
), so we need not be careful about

when dealing with integrals.

Consider
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
.
For each

, let

be as in
Section

2
. We define

For

, denote by
\mathcal{W}_{x}
the collection of all

such that
x\in Q
. Then from
Section

2
,
\#\mathcal{W}_{x}\leq M
for some
M\in\mathbb{N}
.

Note that, if
Q\in\mathcal{W}
be such that

, then, for
z\in 2Q,d(z,\Omega)\geq 8\mathrm{diam}(Q)>8
, which is not possible since
\mathbb{B}=B(0,4)
. Hence, we have

\mathrm{diam}(Q)<1\quad\text{ for all }Q\in\mathcal{W}.

Also, for
Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}
, by
Section

2
,

(3.4)

B(x_{Q}^{\star},\mathrm{diam}(Q))\subseteq 20Q,

and consequently
|B_{Q}^{\star}|\approx|Q|
. Now

2.1
gives

(3.5)

|B_{Q}^{\star}|\approx|20Q\cap\Omega|\approx|20Q|.

For
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, we define
E
by setting

(3.6)

Ef(x):=\begin{cases}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}}f_{B_{Q}^{\star}}\varphi_{Q}(x),&\ {\rm if}\ x\in\mathbb{B}\setminus\overline{\Omega},\\
f(x),&\ {\rm if}\ x\in\Omega.\par\end{cases}

We write

(3.7)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{|Ef(x)-Ef(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx&\leq\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\ \,dy\,dx\\
&\quad+2\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|Ef(x)-Ef(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega}\frac{|Ef(x)-Ef(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
&=I_{1}+2I_{2}+I_{3}.\end{split}

We will estimate
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
separately below.

Estimate for
I_{2}
:

Let
x\in\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega
and
y\in\Omega
. Fix
Q_{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{x}
. From
eq.

3.6
, we get

(3.8)

\begin{split}|Ef(x)-f(y)|&\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}}\varphi_{Q}(x)|f_{B_{Q}^{\star}}-f(y)|\\
&\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}}\varphi_{Q}(x)\fint_{B_{Q}^{\star}}|f(z)-f(y)|\,dz.\end{split}

Combining
eqs.

3.8
,
3.4
and
3.5
with
Sections

2
and
2
, we get

(3.9)

|Ef(x)-f(y)|\leq C(n)\fint_{20Q_{0}\cap\Omega}|f(z)-f(y)|\,dz.

For
y\in\Omega
,
z\in 20Q_{0}\cap\Omega
, since
|x-y|\geq d(x,\Omega)
, we always have

|z-y|\lesssim|x-y|.

Hence

3.9
gives

(3.10)

\begin{split}\frac{|Ef(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{p}+s}}&\leq C(n,p)\fint_{20Q_{0}\cap\Omega}\frac{|f(z)-f(y)|}{|z-y|^{\frac{n}{p}+s}}\,dz\\
&\leq C(n,p)\fint_{20Q_{0}}\frac{|f(z)-f(y)|}{|z-y|^{\frac{n}{p}+s}}\chi_{\Omega}(z)\,dz\\
&\leq C(n,p)\mathcal{M}\left(\frac{|f(\cdot)-f(y)|}{|(\cdot)-y|^{\frac{n}{p}+s}}\chi_{\Omega}(\cdot)\right)(x).\end{split}

Raising both sides to power
p
and integrating, and then using

2.6
we get, for any

,

(3.11)

\begin{split}I_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega}\frac{|Ef(x)-Ef(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\mathcal{M}\left(\frac{|f(\cdot)-f(y)|}{|(\cdot)-y|^{\frac{n}{p}+s}}\chi_{\Omega}(\cdot)\right)(x)\right]^{p}\,dx\\
&\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx.\end{split}

Integrating again over
\Omega
,

(3.12)

I_{2}\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy.

Estimate for
I_{3}
:
For
x\in\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega

arbitrarily so that
Q_{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{x}

respectively. Set

We write

(3.13)

\begin{split}I_{3}=\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega}\frac{|Ef(x)-Ef(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx&=\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega}\int_{X_{1}(x)}\frac{|Ef(x)-Ef(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega}\int_{X_{2}(x)}\frac{|Ef(x)-Ef(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx.\end{split}

For a

and
Q_{1}\in\mathcal{W}_{y}

(3.14)

\begin{split}Ef(x)-Ef(y)&=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}}f_{B_{Q}^{\star}}\varphi_{Q}(x)-\sum_{R\in\mathcal{W}_{y}}f_{B_{R}^{\star}}\varphi_{R}(y)\\
&=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{W}_{y}}\varphi_{R}(y)\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}}f_{B_{Q}^{\star}}\varphi_{Q}(x)-\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}}\varphi_{Q}(x)\sum_{R\in\mathcal{W}_{y}}f_{B_{R}^{\star}}\varphi_{R}(y)\\

Now,

3.5
and
3.4

\begin{split}|(f_{B_{Q}^{\star}}-f_{B_{R}^{\star}})|&\leq\fint_{B_{Q}^{\star}}\fint_{B_{R}^{\star}}|f(z)-f(w)|\,dz\ dw\\

Section

2

2

\begin{split}|Ef(x)-Ef(y)|^{p}&\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}\cup\mathcal{W}_{y}}\frac{|x-y|^{p}}{\mathrm{diam}{(Q)^{p}}}\left(\fint_{20Q_{0}\cap\Omega}|f(z)-f_{20Q_{0}\cap\Omega}|\,dz\right)^{p}\\
&\lesssim\frac{|x-y|^{p}}{\mathrm{diam}{(Q)^{p}}}\left(\fint_{20Q_{0}\cap\Omega}|f(z)-f_{20Q_{0}\cap\Omega}|\,dz\right)^{p}.\end{split}

For all

2.1

\begin{split}\int_{Q_{0}}&\int_{X_{2}(x)}\frac{|Ef(x)-Ef(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\

Since

, summing over

(3.18)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega}&\int_{X_{2}(x)}\frac{|Ef(x)-Ef(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
&=\sum_{Q_{0}\in\mathcal{W}}\int_{Q_{0}}\int_{X_{2}(x)}\frac{|Ef(x)-Ef(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\\
&\lesssim\sum_{Q_{0}\in\mathcal{W}}\int_{Q_{0}}\left[\mathcal{M}\left(\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(z)-f(\cdot)|^{p}}{|z-w|^{n+sp}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\chi_{\Omega}(\cdot)\right)(x)\right]^{p}\,dx\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\mathcal{M}\left(\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(z)-f(\cdot)|^{p}}{|z-w|^{n+sp}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\chi_{\Omega}(\cdot)\right)(x)\right]^{p}\,dx.\end{split}

Theorem

2.6
and

4.
Self-improvement of extension

Throughout this section, we shall assume that
\Omega\subseteq\bar{\Omega}\subseteq B(0,1)
. Note that, even if we prove
Theorem

1.6
with this additional restriction, the general result follows immediately by a scaling argument. Consider
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
. For each
Q\in\mathcal{W}
, we define
\tilde{Q}
to be a cube having the same centre with
\mathrm{diam}(\tilde{Q})=\mathrm{diam}(Q)^{\frac{1}{s}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set with
|\partial\Omega|=0
. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
, and
s\in(0,1)
. Assume further that
E_{1}:\dot{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)\to\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a bounded extension operator. Define another extension operator
E_{2}
by setting

(4.1)

E_{2}f(x):=\begin{cases}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}}(E_{1}f)_{\tilde{Q}}\varphi_{Q}(x),&\ {\rm if}\ x\in B(0,1)\setminus\overline{\Omega},\\
f(x),&\ {\rm if}\ x\in\Omega,\\
E_{1}f(x),&\ {\rm if}\ x\in\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

If

E_{2}\left(C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\dot{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)\right)\subseteq\dot{W}^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B(0,1)),

then
E_{2}:\dot{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)\to\dot{W}^{1,p}(B(0,1))
is bounded.

Proof.

We use the hypothesis

E_{2}\left(C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\dot{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)\right)\subseteq\dot{W}^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B(0,1)),

to conclude that
E_{2}f
has a weak gradient
\nabla E_{2}f
, which is defined in
B(0,1)
. Thus, we only need to show the seminorm inequality.

Let
x\in\mathbb{B}\setminus\bar{\Omega}
and
Q_{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{x}
. Then we have

\begin{split}\nabla E_{2}f(x)&=\nabla\big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}}(E_{1}f)_{\tilde{Q}}\varphi_{Q}(x)\big)\\
&=\nabla\big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}}\big((E_{1}f)_{\tilde{Q}}-(E_{1}f)_{\tilde{Q}_{0}}\big)\varphi_{Q}(x)\big)\end{split}

From
Section

2
, we know, for
Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}
,
|\nabla\varphi_{Q}(x)|\approx\mathrm{diam}(Q)^{-1}
. Hence,

|\nabla E_{2}f(x)|^{p}\lesssim\left[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{diam}(Q)}\fint_{\tilde{Q}}\fint_{\tilde{Q}_{0}}|(E_{1}f)(y)-(E_{1}f)(z)|\,dz\,dy\right]^{p}.

By Hölder inequality, we get that

\begin{split}|\nabla E_{2}f(x)|^{p}&\lesssim\left[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{diam}(Q)}\fint_{\tilde{Q}}\left(\fint_{\tilde{Q}_{0}}|(E_{1}f)(y)-(E_{1}f)(z)|^{p}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy\right]^{p}\\
&\lesssim\left[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{diam}(Q)}\fint_{\tilde{Q}}\left(\int_{\tilde{Q}_{0}}\frac{|(E_{1}f)(y)-(E_{1}f)(z)|^{p}}{|y-z|^{n}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy\right]^{p}.\end{split}

This implies

|\nabla E_{2}f(x)|^{p}\lesssim\left[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}}\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\tilde{Q}_{0})^{s}}{\mathrm{diam}(Q)}\fint_{\tilde{Q}}\left(\int_{\tilde{Q}_{0}}\frac{|(E_{1}f)(y)-(E_{1}f)(z)|^{p}}{|y-z|^{n+sp}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy\right]^{p}.

Now
\mathrm{diam}(\tilde{Q})=\mathrm{diam}(Q)^{\frac{1}{s}}
and, since
Q,Q_{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{x}
, we have that
\mathrm{diam}(Q_{0})\approx\mathrm{diam}(Q)
and that there exist a
C>0
such that
Q\subseteq CQ_{0}
. Thus, the above inequality implies

|\nabla E_{2}f(x)|^{p}\lesssim\left[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}}\fint_{CQ_{0}}\left(\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{|(E_{1}f)(y)-(E_{1}f)(z)|^{p}}{|y-z|^{n+sp}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy\right]^{p}.

We use (5) of
Section

2
and then take supremum over all cubes containing
x
,

\begin{split}|\nabla E_{2}f(x)|^{p}\lesssim\left[\mathcal{M}\left(\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{|(E_{1}f)(\cdot)-(E_{1}f)(z)|^{p}}{|\cdot-z|^{n+sp}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\right]^{p}.\end{split}

Now, by the boundedness of
E_{1}
and
Theorem

2.6
, we get that

\|\nabla E_{2}f\|_{L^{p}(B(0,1)\setminus\bar{\Omega})}\lesssim\|\nabla f(x)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

The proof follows.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in(0,1)
. Assume that
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\dot{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
E_{1}f\in\dot{W}^{s,p}(B(0,1))
is an extension of
f
, and that
E_{2}f
is defined by
eq.

4.1
. Then
E_{2}f\in\dot{W}^{s,p}(B(0,1))
.

Proof.

Set
\mathbb{B}:=B(0,1)
. Since
E_{2}f=E_{1}f=f
on
\Omega
, we write

[E_{2}f]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{B})}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{B}}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{|E_{2}f(x)-E_{2}f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy=:I_{\Omega\Omega}+I_{\Omega c}+I_{cc},

where the three terms correspond to
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega
,

\Omega\times(\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega)
, and

(\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega)\times(\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega)
.

For
I_{\Omega\Omega}
, we simply have

I_{\Omega\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|E_{1}f(x)-E_{1}f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy\leq[E_{1}f]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{B})}^{p}.

To estimate
I_{\Omega c}
, fix
y\in\Omega
and
x\in\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega
.
Using the definition of
E_{2}
, the Lipschitz bounds for the partition of
unity
\{\varphi_{Q}\}_{Q\in\mathcal{W}}
, and the usual Whitney geometry, one
obtains the standard pointwise estimate

|E_{2}f(x)-f(y)|\leq C\,\mathcal{M}\!\left(\frac{|E_{1}f(\cdot)-E_{1}f(y)|}{|\cdot-y|^{\frac{n}{p}+s}}\chi_{\Omega}(\cdot)\right)(x),

where
\mathcal{M}
is the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(after extending the displayed function by
0
outside
\Omega
).
Hence, by
Theorem

2.6
,

\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega}\frac{|E_{2}f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}\frac{|E_{1}f(x)-E_{1}f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx.

Integrating in
y\in\Omega
yields

I_{\Omega c}\leq C\,[E_{1}f]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{B})}^{p}.

It remains to estimate
I_{cc}
. Let
x,y\in\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega
.
Write

E_{2}f(x)=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{x}}(E_{1}f)_{\widetilde{Q}}\varphi_{Q}(x),\qquad E_{2}f(y)=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{W}_{y}}(E_{1}f)_{\widetilde{R}}\varphi_{R}(y),

and choose a reference cube
Q_{x}\in\mathcal{W}_{x}
.
Set

c_{x}:=(E_{1}f)_{C\widetilde{Q}_{x}},\qquad G(u):=\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{|E_{1}f(u)-E_{1}f(v)|^{p}}{|u-v|^{n+sp}}\,dv\right)^{1/p},\quad u\in\mathbb{B}.

Since
\operatorname{diam}(\widetilde{Q})=\operatorname{diam}(Q)^{1/s}
, we have

\operatorname{diam}(\widetilde{Q})^{s}=\operatorname{diam}(Q)
.
Using cancellation
\sum_{Q}(\varphi_{Q}(x)-\varphi_{Q}(y))=0
, the Lipschitz bound

|\varphi_{Q}(x)-\varphi_{Q}(y)|\lesssim\frac{|x-y|}{\operatorname{diam}(Q)},

and a fractional Poincaré estimate on the enlarged Whitney cubes, we get

|(E_{1}f)_{\widetilde{Q}}-c_{x}|\lesssim\operatorname{diam}(\widetilde{Q})^{s}\,\mathcal{M}(G)(x)=\operatorname{diam}(Q)\,\mathcal{M}(G)(x).

Therefore,

|E_{2}f(x)-E_{2}f(y)|\lesssim|x-y|\bigl(\mathcal{M}(G)(x)+\mathcal{M}(G)(y)\bigr).

Hence

I_{cc}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\bigl(\mathcal{M}(G)(x)+\mathcal{M}(G)(y)\bigr)^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp-p}}\,dx\,dy.

Since
\mathbb{B}
is bounded and
p>sp
, with a change of variables, Fubini’s theorem, symmetry and
Theorem

2.6

I_{cc}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{B}}(\mathcal{M}(G)(x))^{p}\,dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{B}}G(x)^{p}\,dx,

by the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
\mathcal{M}
. Finally,

\int_{\mathbb{B}}G(x)^{p}\,dx=\iint_{\mathbb{B}\times\mathbb{B}}\frac{|E_{1}f(x)-E_{1}f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy=[E_{1}f]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{B})}^{p}.

Combining the estimates for
I_{\Omega\Omega}
,
I_{\Omega c}
, and
I_{cc}
gives

[E_{2}f]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{B})}\lesssim\,[E_{1}f]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{B})},

as claimed.
∎

The following result is motivated by classical Banach-Zarskiĭ theorem (see
[
21
]
). We give a self-contained treatment here.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}
and let
f\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus E)

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Assume that
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(E)=0
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus E}|f^{\prime}(x)|\,\,dx<\infty.

Then
f
is absolutely continuous on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let
K>0
be a Hölder constant of
f
, i.e.

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq K|x-y|^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(E)=0
, there exists a countable
collection of open intervals
\{U_{i}\}_{i}
covering
E
such that

\sum_{i}|U_{i}|^{\alpha}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2K}.

Let
\delta_{1}:=\frac{\varepsilon}{2K}
.

Also, since
|f^{\prime}|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus E)
, there exists
\delta_{2}>0

such that for every measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}\setminus E
with

|A|<\delta_{2}
,

\int_{A}|f^{\prime}(x)|\,\,dx<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Set
\delta:=\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}
.

Now let
\{(a_{j},b_{j})\}_{j=1}^{N}
be a finite collection of pairwise disjoint
intervals with

\sum_{j=1}^{N}(b_{j}-a_{j})<\delta.

We estimate

\sum_{j=1}^{N}|f(b_{j})-f(a_{j})|.

Split each interval into two parts:

(a_{j},b_{j})=\big((a_{j},b_{j})\cap E\big)\ \cup\ \big((a_{j},b_{j})\setminus E\big).

Contribution from
E
.

For each
j
,

|f(b_{j})-f(a_{j})|\leq K(b_{j}-a_{j})^{\alpha}.

Hence,

\sum_{j=1}^{N}|f(b_{j})-f(a_{j})|\leq K\sum_{j=1}^{N}(b_{j}-a_{j})^{\alpha}.

Since the intervals intersecting
E
are contained in
\bigcup_{i}U_{i}
, we get

\sum_{j:\,(a_{j},b_{j})\cap E\neq\emptyset}(b_{j}-a_{j})^{\alpha}\leq\sum_{i}|U_{i}|^{\alpha}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2K}.

Thus,

\sum_{j:\,(a_{j},b_{j})\cap E\neq\emptyset}|f(b_{j})-f(a_{j})|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Contribution from
\mathbb{R}\setminus E
.

On each interval
(a_{j},b_{j})\subset\mathbb{R}\setminus E
, the function is
C^{1}
,
so by the fundamental theorem of calculus,

|f(b_{j})-f(a_{j})|\leq\int_{a_{j}}^{b_{j}}|f^{\prime}(x)|\,\,dx.

Summing over those intervals,

\sum_{j:\,(a_{j},b_{j})\subset\mathbb{R}\setminus E}|f(b_{j})-f(a_{j})|\leq\int_{\bigcup_{j}(a_{j},b_{j})\setminus E}|f^{\prime}(x)|\,\,dx.

Since the total length is
<\delta\leq\delta_{2}
, this is
<\varepsilon/2
.

Combining both parts,

\sum_{j=1}^{N}|f(b_{j})-f(a_{j})|<\varepsilon.

This proves that
f
is absolutely continuous.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

be a fixed
(n-1)
-dimensional hyperplane. For

.

Suppose there exists a set

\mathcal{H}^{n-1+t}(E)>0.

By

2
, for each

Thus, we have

Therefore, by

Some known results

The following two are slightly modified versions of results in
[
5
]

Theorem A.1
.

Let
R>0
,
\mathbb{B}=B(0,R)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
. If
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{B})
, then
there is a constant
K=K(n,p)
such that, for any
x\in\mathbb{B}
,

\lim_{s\to 1-}(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy=K(n,p)|\nabla f|^{p}.

Proof.

Fix
y\in\mathbb{B}
for now. Note that,

\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|}\leq\sup_{z\in B(x,R)}|\nabla f(z)|=C_{x}.

From the Lipschitz continuity of
t\mapsto t^{p}
in
(0,2C_{x})
and by Taylor’s
theorem, we get

\begin{split}\left|\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{p}}-\frac{|\nabla f(x)\cdot(x-y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{p}}\right|&\leq pC_{x}^{p-1}|x-y|^{-1}\left|f(x)-f(y)-\nabla f(x)\cdot(x-y)\right|\\
&\leq pC_{x}^{p-1}|x-y|.\end{split}

This implies

(A.1)

\begin{split}&(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{B}}\left|\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{p}}-\frac{|\nabla f(x)\cdot(x-y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{p}}\right|\frac{\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+sp-p}}\\
&\leq pC_{x}^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+sp-p-1}}\\
&\leq C(n,p)C_{x}^{p-1}(1-s)\int_{r=0}^{2R}r^{p-sp}\,dr\\
&\leq C(n,p)C_{x}^{p-1}\frac{1-s}{p-sp+1}R^{p-sp+1}\to 0,\qquad\text{as }s\to 1-.\end{split}

Again, using the shorthand notation
\gamma_{x}:=\frac{\nabla f(x)}{|\nabla f(x)|}
,

(A.2)

\begin{split}(1-s)&\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{|\nabla f(x)\cdot(x-y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{p}}\frac{\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+sp-p}}\\
&=(1-s)|\nabla f(x)|^{p}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\left|\gamma_{x}\cdot\frac{(x-y)}{|x-y|}\right|^{p}\frac{\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+sp-p}}\\
&=C(n)(1-s)|\nabla f(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\gamma_{x}\cdot\sigma\right|^{p}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma)\right)\left(\int_{t=0}^{2R}t^{p-sp-1}\,dt\right)\\
&=C(n,p)R^{p-sp}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\gamma_{x}\cdot\sigma\right|^{p}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma)\right)|\nabla f(x)|^{p}.\end{split}

It can be checked that the last integral remains the same
if we replace
\gamma_{x}
by any unit vector. Hence, combining
eqs.

A.1
and
A.2
and taking the limit as
s\to 1-
, the result follows. ∎

Theorem A.2
.

Let
R>0
,
\mathbb{B}=B(0,R)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
. If
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{B})
is
such that

L_{n,p,R}(f):=\liminf_{s\to 1-}(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{B}}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx<\infty,

then
f
has a weak derivative and
\nabla f
such that

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq L_{n,p,R}(f)<\infty.

Proof.

Extend
f
by
0
, outside
\mathbb{B}
and yet, call it
f
. Consider the
smooth approximation of
f
, in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, given by

f_{\varepsilon}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x+h)\eta_{\varepsilon}(h)dh=\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}f(x+h)\eta_{\varepsilon}(h)dh,

where
\{\eta_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
is a smooth mollifiers with
\text{supp}(\eta_{\varepsilon})\subseteq B(0,\varepsilon)
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(h)dh=1
. We have

(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx=(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\left|\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\left[f(x+h)-f(y+h)\right]\eta_{\varepsilon}(h)dh\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx.

Using Jensen inequality, with the measure
\eta_{\varepsilon}(h)dh
followed by Fubini and properties of the mollifiers,

\displaystyle(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\frac{\left|f(x+h)-f(y+h)\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(h)dh\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(1-s)\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\eta_{\varepsilon}(h)dh

\displaystyle\leq(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{B}}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dx\leq L_{n,p,R}(f).

In the above equation, we take the lower limit as
s\to 1-
in both the
sides, use Fatou’s lemma along with
Theorem

A.1
, to conclude

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla f_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}\,dx\leq L_{n,p,R}(f).

Again, considering limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
and using properties
of convolution, as
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
,

\int_{\mathbb{B}}|\nabla f|^{p}\,dx\leq L_{n,p,R}(f)<\infty.

∎
