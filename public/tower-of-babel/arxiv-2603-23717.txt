1.
Introduction

Two long standing problems in modern low-dimensional topology are the
Slice-Ribbon Conjecture
and the
Smooth
4
-dimensional Poincaré Conjecture
. We describe both conjectures briefly, and how approaches to the first can relate to the second.

A knot
K\subset S^{3}
is
slice
in the standard
4
-ball
B^{4}
if it bounds a smooth properly embedded disk
D\subset B^{4}
such that
\partial(B^{4},D)=(S^{3},K)
. The disk
D
is called a
slice disk
for
K
. A slice disk
D
for
K
is called
ribbon
if
D
can be isotoped (rel. boundary) so that the restriction of the height function on
B^{4}=B^{3}\times I\to I
to
D
has no index
2
critical points. A knot
K
in
S^{3}
is called
ribbon
if it bounds a ribbon disk in
B^{4}
. The Slice-Ribbon Conjecture, originally stated in
[
13
]
, posits that every slice knot must be ribbon. There are two primary strategies one can use to determine whether or not a given knot is slice. The first strategy is to obstruct sliceness. There are classical invariants which can obstruct
topological sliceness
(i.e.
K
bounds a
topological disk
in
B^{4}
). These classical invariants include the
signature
of
K
, where
\sigma(K)=0
if
K
is topologically slice. Another obstruction to topological sliceness comes from the Fox–Milnor condition of
[
14
]
, which states that the Alexander polynomial of a topologically slice knot must be of the form
f(t)f(t^{-1})
for some Laurent polynomial
f(t)
. Obstructions to smooth sliceness arise from knot Floer homology
[
30
]
and Khovanov homology
[
32
]
. Conversely, one can show that a given knot
K
in
S^{3}
is slice either by explicitly constructing a ribbon disk for
K
(by finding bands which surger
K
to an unlink) or by constructing a specific
0
-trace embedding of
K
into
S^{4}
as in the classical Trace Embedding Lemma (see Lemma
2.2
for more details).

By work of Freedman in
[
15
]
, it is known that any homotopy
S^{4}
is
homeomorphic
to
S^{4}
. The Smooth
4
-dimensional Poincaré Conjecture posits that any smooth
4
-manifold
X
which is homotopy-equivalent to
S^{4}
must actually be
diffeomorphic
to
S^{4}
. If there exists a homotopy
4
-sphere
X
not diffeomorphic to
S^{4}
, such an
X
would be called an
exotic
S^{4}
. Despite many examples of exotic
4
-manifolds
[
15
,
33
,
5
]
, numerous attempts to construct an exotic
S^{4}
(
[
7
,
6
,
23
]
) have so far been unsuccessful (
[
3
,
19
,
21
,
25
,
9
,
29
]
). One of the hurdles in trying to prove (or find a counterexample to) the Smooth Poincaré Conjecture is that many invariants known to distinguish smooth structures on
4
-manifolds vanish on homotopy
4
-spheres. A modern strategy posed by Manolescu and Piccirillo in
[
23
]
tries to circumvent this problem. We describe their strategy briefly.

The Manolescu–Piccirillo strategy is to seek a pair of knots
K,K^{\prime}\subset S^{3}
with homeomorphic
0
-surgeries where
K^{\prime}
is slice through a smooth disk
D\subset B^{4}
, but
K
is not slice. One could then take the disk complement
B^{4}\setminus\eta(D)
and glue it to the
0
-trace of
K
. The result would be a homotopy
4
-sphere
X
where
K
is slice in
X\setminus\eta(B^{4})
but not in the standard
4
-ball. This could only occur if
X
is not diffeomorphic to
S^{4}
.

Recently in
[
12
]
, Dunfield and Gong used computational methods to find pairs of knots on which to apply the Manolescu–Piccirillo strategy. Dunfield and Gong constructed a pair of knots
K_{G}
and
K_{B}
sharing the same zero surgery, where
K_{B}
is ribbon and the knot
K_{G}
(known as
18_{\text{nh}00000601}
in
[
12
]
) is not obviously slice. We depict these knots in Figure

.

Since

share the same

-sphere,

is of unknown sliceness,

is a potential counterexample to the Smooth

-dimensional Poincaré Conjecture. Specifically, Theorem

of
[

states that if

is not smoohtly slice in the standard

is smoothly slice in the standard

has evaded detection,

would be a potential counterexample to the Slice-Ribbon Conjecture.

In this article, we standardize the homotopy

-sphere

constructed by Dunfield and Gong using Kirby calculus. As an immediate corollary, it follows that indeed
K_{G}
is smoothly slice in the
4
-ball. Hence
K_{G}
is a potential counterexample to the slice-ribbon conjecture.

Our main theorem is:

Theorem 1.1
.

The homotopy
4
-sphere
X_{DG}=E_{D_{K_{B}}}\cup_{\psi_{RBG}}\overline{X_{0}(K_{G})}
is diffeomorphic to
S^{4}
.

Corollary 1.1.1
.

K_{G}
is smoothly slice in
B^{4}
.

As
K_{G}
is smoothly slice, but not obviously ribbon, we are left with the following corollary.

Corollary 1.1.2
.

K_{G}
is a potential counterexample to the Slice-Ribbon Conjecture.

Using work of Casson–Gordon, Meier–Zupan, and Miller–Zupan
[

,
25
,

]
, we are able to show that

bounds a fibered

disk in the standard
4
-ball.

Theorem 1.2
.

The knot
K_{G}
bounds a fibered handle-ribbon disk in
B^{4}
.

2.1.
Conventions and notation

Unless otherwise stated, all manifolds are smooth, compact, and oriented.
S^{n}
denotes the standard smooth
n
-sphere and
B^{n}
denotes the standard smooth
n
-ball.
I
is the unit interval
[0,1]
. If

denote an open regular neighborhood of
Y
inside
X
. Given a slice disk

of

in

, we denote the

of

by

. Given a handle decomposition of a smooth
n
-manifold

is a manifold with boundary with a given handle decomposition, then

will correspond to a relative handle decomposition (see Section

]

and a solid torus

where the slope

is replaced by an
n
-tuple of boundary slopes. Related to surgery is the notion of a

. Given an integer surgery on a knot

, the

-trace of

-manifold obtained by attaching an

-framed

-dimensional

-handle to

along

the

of
K
. We note that

is
S_{n}^{3}(K)
. To distinguish between a Dehn surgery and a
4
-dimensional
2
-handle attachment, we will use bracketed coefficients to denote a surgery on a link and unbracketed coefficients to denote a handle attachment along a link. We will use the word
handlebody
to mean a copy of the
3
-manifold
\natural^{n}S^{1}\times B^{2}
for some
n
; otherwise we will specify a dimension. Given a framed link

with at least two-components

and

with framings

and

, a

of

is replaced with a link

, where

is a framed knot obtained by connecting

to

with a band. The framing on the component

is given by the formula
n_{1}+n_{2}\pm 2\text{lk}(L_{1},L_{2})
, where

denotes the algebraic linking number between

and
L_{2}
. Treating a surgery on a link as a framed

-dimensional
2
-handle attachment, a handleslide of one component over another does not change the diffeomorphism type of the resulting
4
-manifold (See Section
5
of
[
18
]
for details). In all figures, we let a boxed integer
k
indicate a full twist on
n
-strands; if
k>0
, then we use a positive full twist; if

, then we use a negative full twist. We also let a boxed
\pm\frac{1}{2}
indicate a positive (resp. negative) half-twist on

2.2.
The Manolescu–Piccirillo strategy

In order to obtain a diagram of
X_{DG}
, we must first review the general RBG construction of
[
23
]
by recalling the definition of an RBG link.

Definition 2.1
.

An
RBG link

R\cup B\cup G\subset S^{3}
is a three-component rationally framed link, with respective framings
r,b,
and
g
such that
H_{1}(S^{3}_{r,b,g}(R\cup B\cup G))=\mathbb{Z}
, which comes equipped with homeomorphisms
\psi_{B}:S_{r,g}^{3}(R\cup G)\to S^{3}
and
\psi_{G}:S_{r,g}^{3}(R\cup B)\to S^{3}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Theorem
1.2
of
[
23
]
shows that RBG links can be used to describe homeomorphisms of
3
-manifolds arising as
0
-surgeries on knots
K_{B}
and
K_{G}
, where
K_{B}
is the image of
B
under the homeomorphism
\psi_{B}
and
K_{G}
is the image of
G
under the homeomorphism
\psi_{G}
. In practice,
K_{B}
(resp.
K_{G}
) is obtained by sliding
B
(resp.
G
) over
R
to unlink
B
(resp.
G
) from
G
(resp.
B
) in the link
R\cup G
(resp.
R\cup B
). The same theorem of
[
23
]
shows that given a homeomorphism
\psi:S_{0}^{3}(K)\to S_{0}^{3}(K^{\prime})
, one can construct an RBG link with
K_{B}=K^{\prime}
and
K_{G}=K
. The process is as follows. Given a homeomorphism
\psi:S_{0}^{3}(K)\to S_{0}^{3}(K^{\prime})
, take the
0
-framed meridian
(\mu_{K^{\prime}},0)
of
K
. Now, let
(R,r)
be the framed knot
\psi^{-1}(\mu_{K^{\prime}},0)
resulting from pulling the meridian of
K^{\prime}
back along our
0
-surgery homeomorphism. This gives a picture of the dual to
K^{\prime}
inside
S_{0}^{3}(K)
. Next, define the framed link
R\cup K\cup\mu_{R}\subset S^{3}
with respective framings
r,0
and
0
. The homeomorphism
\psi_{B}
is given by slam-dunking
R\cup\mu_{R}
, and
\psi_{G}
is given by pushing
R
and
\mu_{R}
through
\psi
, followed by a slam-dunk.

In
[
23
]
, Manolescu and Piccirillo propose the following strategy to construct exotic
S^{4}

-surgery homeomorphisms:

Consider pairs of knots
K
and
K^{\prime}
with a homeomorphism
\psi
of their
0
-surgeries. If

is slice in

and
K^{\prime}
is not, take a slice disk

for

. Then remove a regular neighborhood of

from

to obtain
E_{D_{K}}=B^{4}\setminus\eta(D_{K})
. The boundary of this slice disk complement is precisely
S_{0}^{3}(K)
. Since
K
and
K^{\prime}
have homeomorphic
0
-surgeries, one can then form the manifold
X_{\psi}
by turning the
0
-trace of
K^{\prime}
upside-down and gluing it to

along the
0
-surgery homeomorphism
\psi

X_{\psi}=E_{D_{K}}\cup_{\psi}\overline{X_{0}(K^{\prime})}.

One can check that

is a homotopy
4
-sphere. By construction,
K^{\prime}
is smoothly slice in
X\setminus\eta(\{pt.\})
, but not in the standard
4
-ball. Hence,

is an exotic

.

3.1.
Fibered, homotopy ribbon knots

Definition 3.1

Let

\partial D=K

is surjective. We call such a disk a

In addition to homotopy ribbonness, we also have the notion of

as in
[
27
]
(also known as

in other literature
[
26
,

]
).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
V
be a homotopy
4
-ball and let

be a knot. We say

is

if there is a smooth properly embedded disk

in

\partial D=K
and

admits a handle decomposition without any
4
-dimensional

in

The notions of homotopy ribbonness and handle-ribbonness serve as alternative notions of ribbonness in the setting of more general homotopy
4
-balls, where a canonical Morse function is not obvious. However, when we take our ambient homotopy
4
-ball to be the standard

, we can stratify these notions of sliceness for knots into the following set containments:

\{\text{ribbon knots}\}\subset\{\text{handle-ribbon knots}\}\subset\{\text{homotopy ribbon knots}\}\subset\{\text{slice knots}\}.

The Slice-Ribbon Conjecture implies that all the above containments are equalities.

The next notion of importance to us is
fiberedness
. If
X
is a compact connected smooth manifold, and
\varphi:X\to X
is some diffeomorphism of

, we can form the

X\times_{\varphi}S^{1}=(X\times I)/\sim,

where

and
\sim
is an equivalence relation defined by
(x,0)\sim(\varphi(x),1)
for all
x\in X
. The map
\varphi
is called the
monodromy
of the mapping torus and a copy of
X\times\{\theta\}
for

is called a
fiber
of the mapping torus. A fibered knot is as follows:

Definition 3.3
.

A knot
K\subset S^{3}

is a minimal genus Seifert surface for

\varphi:F\to F
is a diffeomorphism of

which restricts to the identity on
\partial F=K
. The map

is called the
monodromy
of

. For
\theta\in I
, a copy of
F\times\{\theta\}\subset S^{3}\setminus\eta(K)
is called a
fiber
of
K
.

Performing
0
-surgery on a fibered knot

in

yields a closed fibered
3
-manifold,

, with closed fiber
\hat{F}=F\cup D^{2}
obtained by capping off each fiber of

with a meridinal disk of the surgery solid torus. The monodromy of

can be taken to be the map
\hat{\varphi}:\hat{F}\to\hat{F}
defined by

\hat{\varphi}=\begin{cases}\varphi,\ x\in F\\
\text{id},\ x\in D^{2}\ .\end{cases}

We call

the

.

We introduce the notion of a

as in Section

of
[
8
]

Definition 3.4
.

Let

be a fibered knot with monodromy
\varphi
,

, closed fiber
\hat{F}
, and closed monodromy

. We say that

extends over a handlebody
if there is an abstract handlebody

such that

, and a diffeomorphism
\Phi:H\to H
such that
\Phi|_{\partial H}=\hat{\varphi}
.

In addition to fiberedness of knots, we can similarly introduce the notion of a fibered disk:

Definition 3.5
.

Let

be a smooth properly embedded disk in a homotopy
4
-ball

. We say

is

if

admits the structure of a mapping torus

for some compact

-manifold

acts as projection onto the second factor.

Casson and Gordon characterized fibered homotopy ribbon knots in terms of monodromy extensions over handlebodies. We recount this theorem:

Theorem 3.1

(Theorem

[
8
]
)

A fibered knot

is homotopy ribbon in some homotopy

The Casson–Gordon theorem also gives the following corollary:

Corollary 3.1.1

(Corollary

[

]
)

Suppose

is a fibered knot in

with closed monodromy
\varphi
. If

is homotopy ribbon in some homotopy

-ball

, then there is an extension

of

, a homotopy

-ball

, and a fibered homotopy ribbon disk

in

such that

.

To aid in the study of fibered homotopy ribbon knots and monodromy extensions, we recall the notion of

and
derivatives
(see

Definition 3.6
.

Let
L\subset S^{3}
be an n-component link. We say
L
is an
R-link
if
0
-framed surgery on

gives
\#^{n}(S^{1}\times S^{2})
.

We emphasize that it is crucial in the definition of an R-link that the number of components is equal to the number of summands of
S^{1}\times S^{2}
. If
L
is an
n
-component R-link, then
L
gives rise to a closed
4
-manifold

built from a single
4
-dimensional
0
-handle,
n

4
-dimensional
2
-handles,
n

4
-dimensional
3
-handles, and a single
4
-handle. As

was built with no
1
-handles,

is simply-connected. In addition, a Mayer–Vietoris argument shows that the
3
-handles homologically cancel the
2
-handles, leaving us with a homotopy
S^{4}
. By work of Freedman
[
15
]
, we conclude that

is
homeomorphic
to
S^{4}
, but the two are not obviously diffeomorphic.

Given an R-link
L
in
S^{3}
, there are two types of operations we can perform on

which do not change the diffeomorphism type of the resulting homotopy
4
-sphere
X
. The first move allows us to potentially change the isotopy class of a framed component of
L
inside
S^{3}
. This is accomplished by treating our surgery on
L
as a
4
-dimensional
2
-handle attachment and performing
handleslides
on the components of
L
. This move, in turn, gives rise to the Generalized Property R Conjecture (Problem
1.82
of
[
22
]
)):

Conjecture 3.1

(Generalized Property R Conjecture)
.

Every
n
-component R-link is handleslide equivalent to an
n
-component
0
-framed unlink.

The significance of the Generalized Property R Conjecture is that it implies any homotopy
4
-sphere associated to an R-link can be standardized without adding canceling handle-pairs.

The second operation allows us to potentially change the number of components of
L
inside
S^{3}
. If
\mathcal{U}\subset S^{3}
is an
m
component unlink, then
0
-surgery on
\mathcal{U}
yields
\#^{m}(S^{1}\times S^{2})
. If we are given an R-link

, then performing
0
-framed surgery on

gives us

; hence,

is an R-link as well. Letting

be the smooth homotopy
4
-sphere defined by our R-link

, and

the homotopy
4
-sphere defined by

, there are
m
canceling

-handle pairs coming from

. This gives a diffeomorphism from

to
X
. This discussion leads us to our next definition.

Definition 3.7
.

Two R-links

and

in

are

if there are two unlinks

and

of unknotted components in

such that

is handleslide equivalent to

.

Another notion of importance to us will be
