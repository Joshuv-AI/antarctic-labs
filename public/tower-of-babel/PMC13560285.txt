Machine learning-driven prediction of healthcare resource trends and optimal allocation strategies: a data-driven approach
Authors: Mingkai Zhao, Shiqi Pan, Yuanming Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWith the advancement of hierarchical diagnosis and treatment systems in China, primary healthcare institutions have become pivotal in delivering basic medical services. Accurate prediction and optimal allocation of healthcare resources are indispensable for improving service quality and ensuring the effective operation of the healthcare system.MethodsThis research utilizes the Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA) to optimize the hyperparameters of Backpropagation Neural Network (BPNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models, aiming to enhance their predictive performance for primary healthcare resource planning.ResultsThe findings demonstrate that the SSA-LSTM model significantly outperforms the SSA-BPNN model. Specifically, in predicting the number of primary healthcare institutions, the SSA-LSTM model reduces the root mean squared error (RMSE) by 45% (from 1.429 to 0.78765) and the mean absolute error (MAE) by 36.4% (from 0.99575 to 0.63306) on the test set. Across all prediction tasks, including personnel quantity and total health costs, the SSA-LSTM model achieves an average RMSE reduction of 23.5% and MAE reduction of 17.1% on the test set compared with SSA-BPNN. Similar improvements are evident in the training set, with RMSE and MAE decreasing by 19.2% and 15.4%, respectively.ConclusionThe SSA-LSTM model offers robust data-driven decision support for healthcare policymakers. Its superior predictive accuracy enables dynamic adjustments to resource allocation, which is essential for bridging regional disparities in China’s primary healthcare system. By accurately forecasting key healthcare indicators, the model facilitates optimized staffing, institutional planning, and budget distribution, thereby laying a solid foundation for evidence-based resource optimization and enhancing the overall efficiency and equity of primary care services.Supplementary informationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-026-15002-2.Keywords: Machine Learning, Primary Healthcare Institutions, Influencing Factors, Correlation

Introduction

Against the backdrop of China’s rapid economic growth and demographic changes, primary healthcare institutions play a pivotal role in delivering basic medical and public health services. However, the uneven distribution of healthcare resources poses significant challenges to service accessibility and quality [1].

However, there is currently a significant imbalance between the distribution of primary healthcare resources and their supply capacity, which not only affects the fairness of healthcare services, but also restricts the accessibility and quality of services. Therefore, how to enhance the service capacity of primary healthcare institutions through effective resource allocation has become a key issue to be addressed. In recent years, with the rapid development of data analysis technology, the application of machine learning in healthcare has made significant progress, especially in the fields of resource optimization and cost prediction [2].

In the existing literature, the prediction of healthcare costs has been an important research area in healthcare. Traditionally, statistical models such as linear regression have been widely used for healthcare cost prediction [3]. However, these models show some limitations when dealing with the nonlinear, heterogeneous and complex features of medical cost data [4]. Traditional Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Backpropagation Neural Networks (BPNN) have significant limitations in this context. Conventional LSTM, though effective for sequential data, often experiences slow training convergence, particularly with the noisy, non-stationary nature of healthcare personnel data. BPNN, meanwhile, is highly susceptible to local optima, resulting in suboptimal prediction accuracy and unstable performance in dynamic healthcare settings. Additionally, neither model adequately addresses the unique temporal fluctuations and multifactor interactions inherent in primary healthcare workforce dynamics, such as the combined effects of aging populations, policy interventions, and disease outbreaks on personnel demand. In particular, medical cost data usually show left-skewed distributions and are accompanied by thick-tailed phenomena, i.e., most patients have low costs, but a small number of patients have extremely high costs. As a result, tradipoor interpretabilitytional linear models often face the problem of underfitting when capturing complex healthcare cost distributions [5].

In recent years, with the rapid development of big data and machine learning technologies, researchers have begun to introduce machine learning models into medical cost prediction, achieving significant progress. In an analysis of micro-level medical big data, the predictive performance of a linear regression model was compared with that of several machine learning models, including random forest, gradient boosting, and feed-forward neural networks. These machine learning models outperformed the traditional linear regression model in predicting individual medical expenses, with the gradient boosting decision tree (GBDT) model performing best: for annual medical expense prediction, itsout-of-sample R2 improved by 9.52% and was able to effectively reduce the financial risk faced by healthcare institutions [6]. Machine learning models have significant potential to be applied in healthcare cost prediction, not only to predict healthcare costs more accurately, but also to provide technical support for the reform of healthcare insurance payment methods [7]. In traditional research on healthcare cost prediction and resource allocation, time series models are mostly used, and such models predict future trends by analyzing historical data. Zhang Shaoqian et al. constructed an ARIMA model using the health cost data of Gansu Province from 1995 to 2017 and successfully predicted the future total health costs [8]. Similarly, Guo Qing et al. predicted the per capita hospitalization cost and drug ratio by ARIMA model based on patient data from 2002 to 2018 in public hospitals across China [9]. These models perform well in dealing with time-dependent data, but their ability to analyze nonlinear and multidimensional factors is limited, making it difficult to cope with the complex resource allocation problems in the healthcare field [10].

Gray prediction models have also been widely used in the case of scarce resources and high data uncertainty. Dai Taoming et al. successfully predicted future hospitalization costs using medical cost data from public hospitals in Shandong Province, proving the effectiveness of the gray model in small sample data sets [11]. However, with the increase of data dimensions and complexity, the applicability of the gray prediction model is limited and it is difficult to deal with complex multivariate associations [12].

Machine learning models show greater potential when dealing with complex nonlinear data and high-dimensional information. In particular, neural network models, such as Back Propagation Neural Networks (BPNN) and Long Short-Term Memory Neural Networks (LSTM), are able to capture complex relationships in healthcare resource allocation due to their powerful nonlinear mapping capabilities [13]. Kong accurately predicted the hospitalization cost of patients with bronchopneumonia through BPNN, and the results showed that the model outperformed the traditional Support Vector Machine (SVM) [14]. Nevertheless, neural network models also face the problem of poor interpretability [15]. Rui Chen et al. introduced the Local Interpretability Algorithm (LIME) [16], which enhances the interpretability of the BPNN model and provides more informative decision support for healthcare administrators. Dynamic prediction models, such as the multitask framework for mechanical ventilation mortality, are crucial for addressing such evolving demands in primary care [17]

On this basis, integrated learning models have been widely used in medical cost prediction and resource allocation in recent years. Yannan Zhou combined Random Forest (RF) and XGBoost model to predict the medical cost of diabetic patients and successfully improved the prediction accuracy [18]. Xia Tao, on the other hand, predicted multiple single costs for patients with coronary heart disease by integrating RF and XGBoost models, verifying the effectiveness of the model in dealing with complex cost structures [19]. These integrated models can effectively combine the advantages of multiple underlying models to improve the overall prediction performance.

Although the application of machine learning models in healthcare cost prediction has achieved some results, the performance of the models depends largely on the optimization of parameters. In recent years, bionic intelligent optimization algorithms, such as Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA), have been gradually applied to optimize the hyperparameters of machine learning models [20]. Similar metaheuristic approaches, such as the hybrid sine cosine and grey wolf optimizer in wheat image analysis [21], have demonstrated effectiveness in improving model generalization, which inspired the adoption of SSA in this study. SSA achieves a dynamic balance between global exploration and local exploitation. This balance mirrors the archive-based multi-objective arithmetic optimization algorithm (MAOA) in industrial engineering problems, which demonstrates superior convergence speed compared to traditional methods [22]. By combining SSA and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Fan et al. proposed a hybrid optimization algorithm [23], which significantly improves the global search ability of the model and successfully avoids falling into the dilemma of local optimal solutions. SSA is proved to be able to significantly improve the prediction accuracy and stability of the deep learning model, especially when dealing with nonlinear and heterogeneous data, and exhibits a better adaptability [24]. Specifically, in the context of healthcare resource prediction, medical data often exhibit complex nonlinear patterns and high-dimensional heterogeneity and SSA’s balance between exploration and exploitation makes it well suited to handling such complexity.

In summary, this study uses SSA-optimized LSTM and BPNN models to forecast the number of personnel, number of institutions, and total health costs of primary healthcare institutions, which have been proven effective in handling dynamic medical data. By introducing Pearson correlation analysis, variables that are highly correlated with the relevant indicators are screened as model inputs, further enhancing model performance [25]. Compared with traditional time series models and gray prediction models, SSA-LSTM and SSA-BPNN show significant advantages in handling dynamic nonlinear data, coping with data sparsity and predicting in real time. Through the comparative analysis of different models, this paper verifies the application value of SSA-LSTM model in the optimization of primary healthcare resources, which provides data support and scientific basis for the decision-making of relevant departments.

Data sources

The data for this study are mainly derived from the 2010–2022 China Statistical Yearbook and China Health and Wellness Statistical Yearbook and local statistical yearbook data of 17 provinces and cities, including Tianjin and Hebei. The statistical yearbooks are compiled by the National Bureau of Statistics and provincial and municipal statistical bureaus, and the data are of high quality, authoritative, comprehensive and representative, which provide solid support for this study. This study mainly collects statistical data on the field of primary healthcare from the China Statistical Yearbook and the China Health and Healthcare Statistical Yearbook, as well as historical data from 17 provincial and municipal statistical yearbooks involving the number of personnel in primary healthcare organizations, the number of primary healthcare institutions, the number of primary healthcare institutions, and the total cost of health and other key indicators. Sparrow optimization algorithm

In recent years, a Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA) inspired by sparrow’s group foraging and anti-predation behaviors has been proposed, which can effectively improve the global and local search capabilities and balance exploration and development [29]. Similar nature-inspired algorithms, such as the Greylag Goose Optimization (GGO) model, mimic avian group behaviors to optimize search strategies [30]. Unlike GGO, which relies on hierarchical leadership dynamics in goose flocks, SSA’s producer-forager role division demonstrates higher adaptability to high-dimensional medical data. The algorithm performs well in optimizing complex problems and is applied in this study to optimize two deep learning models: a BP neural network and an LSTM network [31]. Specifically, SSA is used to optimize the weights and bias parameters in BP neural networks; in LSTM networks, SSA is used to adjust the regularization coefficients, the initial learning rate, and the optimal number of nodes in the hidden layer in order to improve the model’s generalization ability and learning efficiency.

The core principle of the sparrow search algorithm is based on the role division of labor and group collaboration in sparrow foraging behavior. The entire sparrow population is divided into two main roles: “producers” and “foragers”. Producers usually have higher energy levels, greater foraging ability and strategies, and are responsible for leading the population to higher quality food sources. When an enemy is present, the producer has the task of leading the entire population away from danger and shows a higher survival advantage [32]. Foragers, on the other hand, have lower energy levels and tend to rely on the producer for food and follow the producer’s lead during the foraging process. In the algorithm implementation, 80% of the sparrows are designated as producers based on their energy levels, and the remaining 20% are food seekers, forming a cooperative working mechanism. During the sparrow search process, when the producer detects a natural enemy (i.e., a local optimum solution or a search trap), it will notify the group through an alarm signal, triggering a global search to prevent falling into a local optimum. The entire sparrow flock will quickly change the search direction to avoid “predators” (i.e., local minima in the search space). This mechanism ensures a dynamic balance between local exploitation and global exploration, and avoids the problem that traditional optimization methods tend to fall into local optimality.

SHAP

To enhance the interpretability of the model prediction results, this paper introduces the SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) model to explain the outputs of the SSA-LSTM and SSA-BPNN models. SHAP is a model interpretation method based on the concept of Shapley values in game theory. It achieves the transparent interpretation of “black-box” models by calculating the marginal contributions of each input feature to the model prediction results.

The bionic sparrow optimization algorithm is used to optimize the hyperparameters in the BPNN and LSTM neural network models to accurately predict the number of primary health care personnel, the number of primary health care facilities, and the total health costs. The prediction of the number of primary healthcare insititutions helps to rationally plan the allocation of resources, improve the coverage and quality of healthcare services, and ensure that every region has access to timely and effective health care services [40]. SSA-BPNN and SSA-LSTM models were used to predict the number of primary healthcare institutions respectively. According to the results in Fig. 5, the SSA-LSTM model outperforms the SSA-BPNN in primary care organization quantity prediction. Therefore, the SSA-LSTM model has better prediction accuracy and stability in the prediction of the number of primary care organizations. The SSA-LSTM model’s test set MAE of 0.63306 for primary healthcare institution numbers translates to an average prediction error of 0.63 institutions per region. This margin of error is acceptable for macro-level resource planning, as healthcare administrators typically adjust institutional numbers in increments of 5–10 per administrative division.

Analysis and discussion

Optimization model prediction algorithm

This study uses Pearson correlation analysis to interpret key influencing factors based on statistical yearbook data from 17 provinces and cities. Although regions such as Tianjin and Hebei are covered, the sample size in underdeveloped western regions (e.g., Qinghai, Ningxia) may be insufficient, leading to inadequate capture of primary healthcare characteristics in economically weak areas. While variables with strong linear correlations can be effectively identified, such as the number of beds and primary healthcare institutions (0.9949), and regional GDP and total healthcare costs (0.9941), inherent limitations exist in handling complex medical data. To address this, the study introduces the SHAP method, which quantifies the marginal contributions of features in the SSA-LSTM model to reveal differences in feature impacts within nonlinear relationships, providing a clinical basis for model interpretation. Subsequently, a Long Short-Term Memory network optimized by the bionic Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA-LSTM) is constructed to predict trends in the number of personnel, institutions, and total healthcare costs of China’s primary healthcare institutions. The SSA-LSTM model demonstrates outstanding performance in handling dynamic nonlinear medical resource data, with a test set MAE of 0.633 and RMSE of 0.787 for institution number prediction—significantly outperforming traditional SSA-BPNN models and time-series models. In this study, the long- and short-term memory neural network (LSTM) and back-propagation neural network (BPNN) were hyper-parameter optimized by the bionic sparrow optimization algorithm (SSA). The results show that the SSA-optimized LSTM model outperforms the SSA-BPNN in terms of both prediction accuracy and generalization ability. Specifically, the SSA-LSTM model is able to better handle nonlinear relationships, especially with complex, multidimensional medical data where it shows strong adaptability. This adaptability is consistent with EEG-based eye state classification using modified-BER algorithms, where metaheuristics optimize feature extraction for nonlinear brainwave data [43]. Which provides a new technical path for research and practice in related fields. Compared with the existing literature, the innovation of this study is the introduction of the SSA algorithm to optimize the LSTM model, which significantly improves the prediction effect of the model. In contrast, the traditional literature relies more on linear regression, decision trees and other methods, which are effective in some scenarios but have major limitations when dealing with complex, nonlinear data [44]. Especially in the study of primary care resource allocation, the influencing factors of resource allocation are multidimensional and dynamic [45], and traditional models often fail to adequately capture the complex relationships.

In the future, we aim to: Integrate hospital-level electronic health records, real-time epidemic monitoring data, and urban-rural stratified samples to construct a more representative multi-source heterogeneous dataset, enhancing model applicability to rural and underdeveloped areas. Introduce a hybrid framework of recursive feature elimination and SHAP-Pearson to optimize nonlinear feature screening, and combine spatio-temporal networks to handle the dynamic spatio-temporal characteristics of resource allocation. Develop policy simulation tools to quantify the impact of different resource investment strategies on service quality, promoting the transformation of research results into practical applications for primary healthcare resource planning. This study performs hyperparameter optimization on Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Backpropagation Neural Networks (BPNN) using the bionic Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA), ensuring more efficient use and fair distribution of medical resources under limited conditions. It provides more scientific and rational suggestions and decision support for regional healthcare development and lays a foundation for universal health [46].

Scientific and rational optimization of resource allocation

Based on Pearson correlation analysis, parameters such as different regions, the number of beds, gross regional product, the number of births and population density have significant correlation with the number of primary healthcare personnel, the number of primary healthcare institutions and the total cost of health. These factors largely influence the size and expenditure of primary healthcare institutions. In contrast, time, total health expenditure as a share of GDP and birth rate have lower correlations with the factors we are interested in, suggesting that these indicators have a more limited impact on the allocation of healthcare resources [47]. It is worth noting that elements such as total primary healtcare costs, number of institutions, and number of personnel are strongly negatively correlated with population density, a finding that breaks with traditional misconceptions. In exploring the reasons for this, we believe that it may be due to the fact that the improper design or implementation of mechanisms in certain aspects of the implementation of the hierarchical diagnosis and treatment system has had an inhibitory effect on the development of primary healtcare institutions to a certain extent. In areas with high population density, there are more high-level and larger-scale medical institutions, and the government invests more in such institutions, neglecting the investment in primary healthcare institutions, while the uneven distribution of resources may lead to the fact that primary healthcare institutions are facing a serious shortage of equipment, technology, and talents [48], making it difficult for them to provide high-quality healthcare services. Moreover, as patients and their families, they have a higher degree of trust in large-scale medical institutions, and this preference for medical care further exacerbates the plight of primary healthcare institutions.

The Third Plenary Session of the Twentieth Central Committee of the Communist Party of China (CPC) considered and adopted the Decision of the Central Committee of the CPC on Further Comprehensively Deepening Reforms and Advancing Chinese-Style Modernization, which explicitly proposed to promote the expansion and sinking of high-quality healthcare resources and a balanced regional layout, to accelerate the construction of a hierarchical diagnostic and treatment system, and to promote the construction of close-knit healthcare associations. In recent years, through the efforts of the Chinese government, the country’s medical service level has improved significantly as a whole, but objectively the problem of imbalance in the distribution of medical resources in different regions is still serious, especially in the western region, where the supply of healthcare personnel is still insufficient, and the allocation of medical resources is weak [49], especially in economically underdeveloped regions and rural grassroots medical and healthcare institutions where staffing, medical equipment, and financial support is chronically [50]. Therefore, upgrading the level of medical services and narrowing the regional gap remain important tasks, especially in the western and less developed regions, and it is particularly urgent to increase investment [51].

Future challenges facing China’s primary healthcare undertakings

From the perspective of China’s health development, although the country has entered a period of low birth rates, this does not mean healthcare investment can be reduced. On the contrary, with the acceleration of an aging society, the demand for medical services from the elderly has surged. Healthcare challenges posed by aging are not merely quantitative—they are currently concentrated in areas such as chronic disease management, long-term care, and rehabilitation, with more urgent needs. Meanwhile, stricter requirements are placed on the structure and quality of medical resources to better meet the special physiological and psychological needs of the elderly. Facing the new social development situation, unswervingly promoting aging-friendly reforms ensures that China’s healthcare system can demonstrate stronger resilience and adaptability in addressing future challenges. Therefore, medical institutions must actively respond by integrating aging-friendly concepts into medical services, carrying out aging-friendly reforms, and enhancing the convenience, comfort, and safety of medical services.

The National Healthcare Security Administration’s recently published guidelines for establishing medical service price items emphasize the importance of “aging-friendly” reforms and have set up a series of related items, such as “home hospital bed establishment fee,” “home visit service fee,” and the addition of “escort-free care service,” they are currently more concentrated in the areas of chronic disease management, long-term care and rehabilitation, where the demand is even more urgent [52]. The setting of service prices guides medical institutions, especially primary healthcare institutions, to carry out related services, as they undertake a large number of aging-friendly service projects.

In the future, we believe primary healthcare institutions need to expand service-oriented projects, such as door-to-door services, home care, and regular care, increase the number of geriatric care staff, and improve the medical environment. The state promotes comprehensive adaptive reforms in primary healthcare institutions—from environmental design, equipment configuration, and service processes to staff training—through new policies to ensure better meeting of the health needs of the elderly.

Challenges in integrating primary healthcare systems and machine learning models

Integrating machine learning models into bureaucratic healthcare systems faces multiple challenges. The data collection and management systems of primary healthcare institutions are fragmented, with inconsistent formats of electronic medical records and resource statistics across regions and departments, hindering the construction of standardized datasets required for model training. Meanwhile, decision-making processes in bureaucratic systems often rely on empiricism and traditional statistical methods, creating a barrier to understanding the technical principles and prediction logic of machine learning models, which may disconnect model outputs from actual resource allocation decisions.

Medical data involves patient privacy and sensitive information, creating a contradiction between strict data security regulations and the large-scale data training required by machine learning models—especially in primary institutions with limited technical protection capabilities, where data compliance risks significantly increase. The performance evaluation mechanisms of the existing primary healthcare system prioritize short-term effectiveness, while the deployment and optimization of machine learning models require long-term technical investment and talent support. This conflict between short-term goals and long-term construction may hinder the practical application of models.

To break through these bottlenecks, we propose establishing cross-departmental data collaboration mechanisms at the institutional design level, guiding primary institutions to incorporate technical investment into long-term assessment systems through policy incentives, and cultivating local technical teams relying on regional medical centers to gradually build an intelligent primary healthcare ecosystem that balances data security and model effectiveness.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study employs machine learning to conduct a comprehensive analysis of the factors influencing the development of primary healthcare. Our findings yield valuable insights into the dynamics of resource allocation and provide a robust theoretical foundation for future policy formulation and implementation. The prediction model proposed herein facilitates accurate identification, enables rational allocation of medical resources, and effectively enhances the quality and efficiency of primary care services. This, in turn, better addresses the growing healthcare needs of the population and contributes to the overall health and well-being of society.

Specifically, the SSA-LSTM model serves as a data-driven decision-making tool for healthcare administrators. This aligns with recent advances in groundwater resource prediction using random forest regression [53] and waste management for sustainable energy [54], highlighting the cross-disciplinary value of metaheuristic optimization. By integrating model predictions—such as the number of primary healthcare institutions (MAE = 0.63306) and total health costs (RMSE = 0.3668)—with Pearson correlation analysis (e.g., bed counts r = 0.9989, regional GDP r = 0.9775), managers can dynamically adjust resource allocation strategies. Personnel quotas can be optimized based on regional economic growth and facility expansion; institutional layouts can be rationalized by consolidating resources in densely populated areas; and budget distributions can prioritize technology investments in high-correlation regions. This approach directly supports China’s hierarchical diagnosis policy and enhances resource efficiency, particularly in aging societies with increasing chronic care demands.

However, limitations include potential bias in the dataset, such as the overrepresentation of eastern provinces, and the absence of real-time clinical data. Future research may integrate electronic health records (EHRs) for dynamic predictions and explore hybrid optimization algorithms—similar to the breadth-first search framework used in orthopedic disease classification [55], to enhance feature selection for multi-dimensional medical data, to manage high-dimensional medical data. We will continue to investigate innovative methods and technologies to better support the reform and development of China’s primary healthcare system.
