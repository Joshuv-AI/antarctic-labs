 During the project period, the PI’s work [1–10] focused on applying lattice methods to
problems in theoretical high energy physics. Most of these works were carried out with
members of the RBC and UKQCD collaborations.

1 QED correction to meson leptonic decay
 The ratio of electronic to muonic leptonic decay widths of the light mesons,
                                    Γ (P → eν̄e (γ))
                           Re/µ ≡                    ,   P = π, K,                        (1)
                                    Γ (P → µν̄µ (γ))
provides a clean test of lepton flavor universality (LFU) and a sensitive probe of physics
beyond the Standard Model. Because the electronic channel is helicity suppressed in the
Standard Model, scalar or pseudoscalar new-physics interactions, which are not helicity sup-
pressed, can have relatively enhanced effects on Re/µ , allowing Re/µ to probe mass scales
beyond the direct reach of colliders. At the same time, Re/µ admits an exceptionally clean
theoretical prediction: the leading uncertainty comes from the structure-dependent (SD) part
of the O(α) radiative corrections, whose contribution is suppressed to the O(0.1%) level by
the cancellation of hadronic effects between the electron and muon channels. With the PIO-
NEER experiment [11–13] aiming for a precision of 0.01% in the pion channel and NA62 [14]
pursuing an updated kaon measurement with about 0.2% precision, this SD correction has
become an essential ingredient in precision experiment–theory comparisons.
 In our work [1], we presented the first lattice QCD+QED determination of the SD radia-
tive correction to Re/µ at the physical pion mass and in the continuum limit. The calculation
is organized in a perturbative QED expansion,

where Re/µ is the result without electromagnetic corrections, ∆α,pt is the analytically known
point-like O(α) correction, ∆α,SD is the O(α) SD correction beyond the point-like approxi-
mation, and ∆αn≥2 collects higher-order QED effects, whose leading logarithms are known.
The leading theoretical uncertainty comes from ∆α,SD , which is nonperturbative and was
previously estimated using chiral perturbation theory (ChPT) [15, 16], where the domi-
nant error arises from truncating the chiral expansion. Our work provides a first-principles
determination of this quantity and an independent check of the ChPT prediction.
 The SD correction receives contributions from virtual-photon exchange between the me-
son and the final-state lepton and from SD real-photon emission (Figure 1). The real-emission
part is obtained by phase-space integration over the vector and axial-vector form factors FV
and FA computed in our earlier lattice study [2]. For the virtual part, we employed the
infinite-volume reconstruction (IVR) method, which removes the power-law finite-volume
effects associated with the massless photon, leaving only exponentially suppressed resid-
ual effects. A key difficulty is that the virtual correction is dominated by the point-like
contribution, so the point-like subtraction strongly amplifies statistical and finite-volume
errors relative to the SD signal. To mitigate this, we employed the IVR method with
Coulomb-gauge photons. In Coulomb gauge, the relevant loop diagrams are individually
free of infrared divergences, which suppresses the point-like contribution by a factor of 2–6
and reduces the statistical error after subtraction by about a factor of two. The calcula-
tion is performed on the Nf = 2 + 1 domain-wall fermion ensembles at the physical pion
mass generated by the RBC and UKQCD collaborations [17], using two lattice spacings
for the continuum extrapolation and two volumes to estimate residual finite-volume effects.
An error-cancellation technique based on the correlation between the decay constant and
the axial-vector transverse-photon contribution further reduces statistical and finite-volume
errors, and the consistency between the independent Coulomb- and Feynman-gauge results
provides a nontrivial cross-check.

 In our work [2], we presented a lattice QCD calculation of the radiative leptonic decays
P → ℓνℓ γ (P = π, K), in which the emission of a real photon probes the internal structure of
the meson beyond what is accessible through the decay constant alone. The decay amplitude
consists of an inner-bremsstrahlung contribution, proportional to the decay constant fP , and
a structure-dependent contribution described by the vector and axial-vector form factors FV
and FA . Using the Nf = 2 + 1 domain-wall fermion ensembles at the physical pion mass and
two lattice spacings, and employing the IVR method to reconstruct infinite-volume hadronic
matrix elements from finite-volume lattice data, we determined the momentum dependence
of FV and FA over the full kinematic region (Figure 3) and computed the branching ratios
in the phase-space regions accessed by experiments. Despite the different lattice action and
computational strategy, our form factors and branching ratios are statistically consistent

with the earlier Rome–Southampton results obtained with twisted boundary conditions [19–
21], providing an independent cross-check of lattice QCD calculations of these decays.
 A main finding of this study is the importance of the O(α2 ) collinear radiative correc-

rections reach the O(10%) level in experimentally relevant phase-space regions and cannot
be neglected at the current level of precision. Once they are included, our prediction for
π → eνe γ agrees with the PIBETA measurement [23], resolving a discrepancy present in
earlier lattice results that omitted these corrections. For K → eνe γ with the experimental

2 Neutrinoless double-beta decay
 Neutrinoless double-beta (0νββ) decay is the most sensitive probe of whether neutri-
nos are Majorana particles; its observation would establish lepton-number violation, fix the
neutrino mass scale, and bear on baryogenesis. Besides the long-range contribution from
light-neutrino exchange, 0νββ receives short-range contributions from higher-dimensional
operators, which are unsuppressed if the new physics lies at the TeV scale. In chiral effec-
tive theory [29], the short-range pion-level matrix elements enter the nn → pp ee amplitude
at leading order. In our work [3], we performed the first lattice QCD calculation of the
short-range π − → π + ee matrix elements of the five relevant dimension-9 four-quark opera-
tors directly at the physical pion mass and in the continuum limit, using two Nf = 2 + 1
domain-wall fermion ensembles at a−1 = 1.73 GeV and 2.36 GeV. This eliminates the chiral-
extrapolation systematics of the two previous lattice studies [30, 31], which disagree with
each other by about a factor of two.

3 False-vacuum decay
 False-vacuum decay, namely the quantum tunneling of a metastable state through a large
energy barrier, is relevant to first-order phase transitions in the early universe, the possible
metastability of the Standard Model electroweak vacuum, and nonperturbative phenomena
such as the Schwinger mechanism. The standard approach is the semiclassical Callan–
Coleman bounce method [33, 34], supplemented by perturbative corrections. This approach
is insufficient for strongly coupled theories, in which quantum effects can even generate a

 7

false vacuum that is absent from the classical Lagrangian. A previous lattice Monte Carlo
study by Shen, Draper, and El-Khadra [35] demonstrated how to extract vacuum-decay
rates in simple quantum-mechanical systems using Euclidean-time simulations, while also
illustrating the limitations of that approach for very small rates. A direct lattice Monte Carlo
treatment faces three obstacles: false-vacuum configurations are exponentially suppressed in
the Euclidean path integral, Markov chains can lose ergodicity between well-separated regions
of configuration space, and the decay rate is a real-time observable while the Monte Carlo
simulations have to be performed in imaginary time.
 Building on this prior lattice method, our work [4] developed and demonstrated a new,
fully nonperturbative lattice Monte Carlo method, the “implicit decay amplitude” method.
The method addresses these obstacles without relying on a semiclassical approximation and
rests on three ingredients. First, it uses a formula constructed in the spirit of Fermi’s golden
rule but without requiring an explicit interaction term in the full Hamiltonian,
                  Γ = 2π ⟨FV| (HFV − H) δ(HTV − EFV ) (HFV − H) |FV⟩,                       (8)
where auxiliary Hamiltonians HFV and HTV separate the false- and true-vacuum regions
by means of added barrier potentials. The resulting “difference state,” (HFV − H)|FV⟩, is
localized near the false-vacuum boundary. Second, the energy delta function is implemented
through spectral reconstruction from Euclidean correlators. This ill-posed inverse problem
is handled here with a simple ansatz, but can be improved using established reconstruction
techniques. Third, the “intermediate ratios” method evaluates the exponentially suppressed
ratio of partition functions as a telescoping product over a family of interpolating actions,
with each factor measured in a well-behaved ensemble.

4 Lattice chiral effective theory
 Chiral effective field theory (ChEFT) is the standard tool for describing low-energy QCD,
and a nonperturbative lattice formulation of ChEFT should in principle be able to predict the
chiral, infinite-volume, and physical-mass extrapolations of lattice QCD within the effective
theory itself, allowing direct comparison even at the level of correlation functions. In our
work [5], we carried out the first test of this idea for ππ scattering, simulating the leading-
order SU (2) ChEFT, namely the O(4) nonlinear sigma model realized as the strong-coupling

 9

limit of the O(4) linear sigma model. We used hybrid Monte Carlo (HMC) and generalized
eigenvalue problem (GEVP) to extract the finite-volume two-pion spectrum, and compared
directly with the RBC-UKQCD lattice QCD calculation [36] of the same process in matched
physical volumes. The parameters are tuned to the physical point mπ /Fπ ≈ 135/92.

 The main finding is negative and instructive: leading-order lattice ChEFT does not
reproduce the low-energy spectrum of QCD (Figure 8). In the I = 2 channel the spectra
agree reasonably well, but in the I = 0 channel they differ drastically: the sigma model
supports two states near 2mπ , mixtures of the two-pion and σ operators, corresponding to
an anomalously light, nearly stable σ state with mσ ≈ 2mπ that is absent in lattice QCD.
We trace this failure to the lattice regularization itself: power divergences destroy the usual
ChEFT power counting. For example, the one-loop pion-mass correction carries a quadratic
