 Exercise #1
 Types of Tamper Indicating Devices – Familiarization and Application

Exercise #1 is designed to provide participants with hands-on experience choosing from a variety of
TIDs, and applying them to a variety of closure mechanisms, containers, and envelopes.
This activity is meant to illustrate the applicability of specific TIDs to different types of
closure mechanisms

 1. Understand the practical operation of a variety of TID types and their intended use.
 2. Determine the most effective TID type for a given closure mechanism through
 hands-on application.
 3. Identify additional considerations to determine the suitability of a specific TID type
 (i.e., the variables included in an engineering analysis).

• Tamper Indicating Device Procedure can be reviewed, however, this activity is guided by the
 instructors and does not require participants to follow a procedure.

Instruct the groups to discuss and select a suitable TID for their apparatus’ closing
mechanism, as well as the envelope and Tupperware container. Ask them to note any
important considerations or observations they make during the activity. These notes should
be included in Participation Guide Exercise 1- Participant Feedback Form and should also

Once each group has completed applying TIDs to their first hinged apparatus, and the
envelope and Tupperware container, the instructor will facilitate the rotation of
apparatuses. Prior to the rotation, the instructor will use wire snips to remove any applied
non-adhesive TIDs (except after the final rotation). Note: If adhesive TIDs were applied to
any of the apparatuses, refer back to the primary distinction between TID application
types:
 • Hasp or Hole
 • Non-Hasp and Non-Hole
 • The apparatus closing mechanisms are not conducive for the adhesive type TIDs
The rotations should be conducted until each group has handled each closure mechanism.
Prior to each rotation, the previous non-adhesive TID should be removed by the instructor.
Additionally, the instructor should reiterate that each participant within the group should
have the chance to apply a TID.
Once complete, the instructor will collect the apparatuses, envelopes, and Tupperware
containers and places them to the side. The instructor will then lead a dialogue among the
participants in which they discuss their main observations, interesting notes, and
considerations for the selection of TIDs (see Discussion Questions).
The instructor will show one apparatus to the participants that has had a TID applied to it
and ask the following questions:
 • Who applied the TID?
 • Who witnessed it?
 • When was it applied/removed?
 • Was this TID applied correctly or was it removed and reapplied by an adversary?
The point of these questions is to highlight that the application of TIDs is only a small part
of their importance. Following a procedure to ensure application is consistent, and
recording many details about the applications (e.g., who was involved, what was the date,
etc.) is the piece of a TID program that provides continuity of knowledge.

9. EXERCISE BACKSTORY

You work at a facility that stores large amounts of nuclear material in varying
containerizations. Management has just approved the establishment of a tamper indicating
device program in order to cut down on the effort required to conduct inventories, and to
enhance overall control of nuclear materials within the site boundary. You have been
assigned, along with your colleagues, to conduct an initial assessment to determine the
ideal TID types to be used at your facility. You will apply TIDs to a variety of apparatuses
that are representative of the closure mechanisms on different containers and entry points
to rooms and vaults onsite.
While determining which TIDs to apply to each apparatus, note any important
considerations, concerns, or questions that you or your teammates identify. You will be
asked to provide a debrief on your findings following this initial assessment.

10. DISCUSSION QUESTIONS
( Discussion Questions are to be provided to participants prior to this discussion, which
follows the conclusion of the activity)

1) What were the adhesive TIDs primarily used for?
 • The adhesive TIDs should have been applied to the envelopes and Tupperware
 containers because they do not contain a hasp or hole that can accommodate a bolt
 seal, wire lock seal, etc.
 • TIDs are applicable for doors. Considerations in both TID selection or door
 application/modification to ensure proper functionality is essential.

2) An engineering analysis should be required prior to making a final determination on TID
 selection, rather than just selecting a TID that can be applied. Why is this the case?
 • Engineering analyses are essential prior to selecting TIDs for use in order to
 determine that the TID will be capable of surviving the environment of application
 without prematurely degrading or breaking. Considerations include humidity, sun
 exposure, radiation exposure, potential for rough handling (i.e., damage due to
 frequent transport), etc.

3) Should you consider surface contact and properties when selecting adhesive TIDs? If
 so, why?
 • Yes, surface conditions will impact the reliability of adhesion. There is a case study
 detailing how the use of a specific kind of adhesive TID on a Teflon-coated container
 allowed for the TID to be removed without being voided.

4) How could humidity and background radiation other variables affect selection of the
 proper TID? If so, why?

 5

 • Yes, these and other environmental factors can lead to impacts to the adhesive
 material on adhesive TIDs, degrade the physical status of the TID such that voided
 TIDs cannot be definitively attributed to adversarial actions, etc.

5) What are the three main factors that should guide how TIDs are selected?
 • Premature degradation or inadvertent damage
 o If TIDs degrade because of a high radiation background, or are easily
 damaged during the transport of containers, they will lead to the incorrect
 assumption that unauthorized access has occurred or been attempted.
 • Tamper indication
 o If a TID is not sufficiently taught or permits a certain degree of flexibility,
 unauthorized access can occur without voiding or otherwise noticeably
 damaging the TID.
 o Consider a TID on a container that does not void if the container lid is
 minimally opened. Material (especially in oxide form) can be poured from the
 opening without the TID indicating material has been removed.
 • Substitution
 o If an adhesive TID does not adhere to a specific surface coating, or the
 adhesion is diminished due to high humidity, the TID may be removed and
 replaced without voiding.
 o This may lead to a TID being removed to be counterfeited or allow for a TID
 to be reapplied to conceal unauthorized access and theft.

6) How do these factors influence the conduct of an engineering analysis?
 • Engineering analysis should include the assessment of variables that correspond to
 TID resiliency and robustness in the face of adversarial (exposure to solvents and
 chemicals, peeling, counterfeiting, etc.) and environmental (direct sunlight,
 radiation, vibration, impact, etc.) effects.

7) What should be done with discarded/removed TIDs? Why?
 • To prevent unauthorized reapplication, adversarial substitution, or reverse
 engineering for the purposes of identifying vulnerabilities and determining the style
 and unique characteristics of TIDs in use at a facility, discarded/removed TIDs need
 to be securely stored, with proper chain of custody, and eventually destroyed such
 that they cannot be reapplied or studied.

8) Why is residual residue from removed adhesive TIDs a concern?
 • Residual residue on the surface of a container, or wherever a TID will be applied,
 degrades the strength of adhesion of a new TID. These can allow for the removal of
 an adhesive TID, without voiding, such that it could be reapplied by an adversary
 and would not provide indications of unauthorized access.

 Exercise #4
 Entering and Leaving a Vault, TID Application, Simple Material Weighing

LA-UR

 Exercise #4
 Entering and Leaving a Vault, TID Application, Simple Material Weighing

The intention of Exercise 4 is to both highlight the importance of and give participants hands-on
experience with operating an electronic balance, applying/removing a tamper indicating device (TID)
and entering/exiting a vault. The exercise is set up to allow participants to conduct these activities in
small groups following three separate procedures and a set of instructions via a work authorizing
document. At the end, participants should be able to discuss the relevance of performing accurate
balance measurements in relation to maintaining an accurate accounting inventory, the formality of
TID usage and entering and exiting a vault. A discussion is recommended to highlight how different
