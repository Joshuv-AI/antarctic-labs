Association of lung perfusion single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) with postoperative lung function decline after segmentectomy
Authors: Lucas Wilhelm, Saifeddine Melki, Laetitia Imbert, Achraf Bahloul, Ulrick Gerhard, Timothée Zaragori, Pierre-Yves Marie, Stéphane Renaud, Antoine Verger
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLung perfusion scintigraphy is helpful for predicting a decrease in lung function after lobectomy; however, little is known after segmentectomy. Our study aimed to evaluate the performance of perfusion single-photon emission computed tomography with computed tomography (SPECT/CT) for predicting significantly decreased lung function after segmentectomy.MethodsThis retrospective observational cohort study included consecutive patients who underwent lung perfusion scintigraphy, recorded using 360° semiconductor SPECT/CT, before a planned segmentectomy for lung nodules. The difference in forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1) from the presurgery measurement to the 6-month follow-up was predicted using presurgical lung perfusion SPECT/CT, in addition to clinical factors, presurgical pulmonary function tests, lung CT and anatomical segment counting methods. A minimal clinically important difference (MCID) of 100 milliliters in the FEV1 was used.ResultsThis study included 51 patients [aged 67.0 years (IQR: 60–72)], 21 women, 1 segment resected (IQR: 1–2) who were referred for lung SPECT/CT before segmentectomy. Twenty-three (45%) experienced a functional decrease after 6 months that met the MCID threshold. Lung perfusion SPECT/CT was the only multivariate predictor of the change in the 6-month FEV1 (beta-coefficient 1.532, 95% CI: 0.757–2.308, P<0.001) and of a decrease in FEV1 corresponding to the MCID threshold (odds ratio <0.001, 95% CI: <0.001–0.052, P=0.004). The SPECT-based lung perfusion prediction of a decrease in the FEV1 of more than 192 mL had a sensitivity of 83% and a specificity of 64% for predicting an actual 6-month MCID decrease.ConclusionsA post-segmentectomy decrease in lung function was significantly associated with segmental lung perfusion using lung perfusion SPECT.

Introduction

Background

Pulmonary nodules are common and increase with age (1), with those suspected of causing bronchopulmonary cancer requiring monitoring or treatment (2). When indicated, surgery is the treatment of choice. For patients with nodules less than two centimeters in size, recent large studies have demonstrated the value of segmentectomy, with survival outcomes identical to those of patients treated by lobectomy (3).

Rationale and knowledge gap

According to the European Respiratory Society (ERS) and European Society of Thoracic Surgery (ESTS) guidelines, preoperative lung function must be assessed and postoperative lung function must be predicted before surgery (4,5). However, biomarkers that can predict which patients are likely to experience a clinically significant decline in lung volume are lacking. Several methods exist, including lung perfusion scintigraphy, which can predict a decrease in lung function following surgical lobectomy. However, little is known about the context of segmentectomy because most studies include only a few patients who underwent segmentectomies among large cohorts of patients who underwent lobectomies or pneumectomies (5-7).

Pre- and postoperative lung functions are typically determined before and most frequently at 6 months after surgery using pulmonary function tests (PFTs). Among the tests evaluated, forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1) is the most studied parameter according to ERS/ESTS guidelines (4,5). In pulmonology, several authors have identified a minimal clinically important difference (MCID) for FEV1, with a decrease equal to or greater than 100 mL in postoperative FEV1 compared with preoperative FEV1, resulting in significant clinical manifestations (8-10). Even if MCID was established in pharmacological chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) studies, the latter is not necessarily directly transferable to thoracic surgery. However, it represents patient perception of lung function decrease, exceeding the benefit provided by trials using placebo (9-11).

Objective

Our study aimed to evaluate whether presurgical lung perfusion single-photon emission computed tomography with computed tomography (SPECT/CT) of the segments planned for resection can predict a significant decrease in lung function after segmentectomy. Additionally, we compared these predictions with those based on the number and volume of the resected segments. We hypothesized that SPECT may provide clinically useful predictive accuracy at the segment level and that its use could help identify patients with a significant decrease in lung function after segmentectomy to intensify respiratory rehabilitation and adapt postoperative follow-up. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-0552/rc).

Methods

Population

In this retrospective single-center prognostic cohort study, we retrospectively identified patients who underwent planned segmentectomy for pulmonary nodules in the thoracic surgery department of Nancy CHRU and who had undergone SPECT/CT lung perfusion in the nuclear medicine department from April 2021 to June 2023. The inclusion criteria were patients who had undergone segmentectomy of one or more segments, with a complete preoperative assessment (SPECT/CT, volumetric CT, PFT) in the month prior to surgery and a postoperative assessment of FEV1 at approximately 6 months. Minor patients, pregnant women or persons under protection were ineligible and therefore were not included. We excluded patients for whom the surgeon decided on another type of resection (lobectomy, atypical resection, biopsy) or if the operative indication was ultimately not retained and patients who underwent further thoracic surgery during the follow-up period (6 months postoperatively). Eligible patients in whom segmentectomy involved one or more segments of the basal pyramid were excluded because of technical limitations of the software dedicated to the SPECT/CT images. Patients whose CT implementation was unsuccessful before surgery were also excluded for the same reasons.

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This study was approved by the Nancy CHRU’s Research and Innovation Department and its Ethics Committee (referral No. 468). Informed consent was taken from all the patients.

Lung perfusion SPECT/CT

SPECT/CT lung perfusion scintigraphy was performed immediately after the mean administration of 222 MBq 99mTc macroaggregated albumin (MAA) with the patient in the supine position. SPECT/CT images were obtained with a 360° CZT camera (VERITON-CT, Spectrum Dynamics Medical) with the patient breathing freely.

CT images were obtained using the following parameters: a tube voltage of 120 kV, 110 effective mAs, 0.5 s rotation time, a pitch of 1.0, and a collimation of 40×0.625 mm. CT images were reconstructed with a 1.25 mm slice thickness using a lung filter and an iterative reconstruction algorithm (LISA, Analogic Corporation®). SPECT/CT images were acquired for 360 seconds immediately after CT, and the parameters included an energy window centered at 140.0±10.5 keV. SPECT/CT images were reconstructed using an iterative ordered subset expectation maximization (OSEM) algorithm with 4 iterations, 8 subsets, a median post filter and no CT-based attenuation correction.

The SPECT/CT fusion images obtained were processed on MIM software (MIM Software Inc., Cleveland, USA) via a dedicated workflow. Processing was performed manually together by two experienced nuclear physicians (L.W., S.M.) with a consensual analysis. Volumetric quantification CT

Volumetric CT was performed under apnea, according to the standard protocol in radiology departments during the period of interest. The images, in DICOM format, were retrieved and implemented in planning software (Synapse 3D, Fuji Film Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). The software located the tumor, the interlobar and intersegmental planes, and the anatomy of the vascular-bronchial tree (and any variations), modeling the segment(s) to be resected and thus defining the volume of the segment(s) to be resected, as well as the total lung volume.

Anatomical segment counting (ASC) method

The ASC method considers that all 19 lung segments contribute equally to total lung function; therefore, each resected segment removes 1/19th of the total lung function (6).

PFTs

Preoperative PFTs consisted of spirometry, plethysmography and diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide (DLCO) measurements, and postoperative PFTs consisted of spirometry for FEV1. PFTs were performed according to standard PFT departmental protocols during the inclusion period and were performed by experienced specialists, in line with good clinical practice recommendations (11).

The three PFT parameters measured, namely, the FEV1, forced vital capacity (FVC) and DLCO, were absolute values.

Preoperative staging, surgery and rehabilitation

All patients underwent preoperative staging, including a chest and brain CT scan (or brain MRI), as well as [18F]-FDG-PET and fiberoptic bronchoscopy. The decision for surgical intervention was made by a tumor board, including a certified thoracic surgeon. Segmentectomies were considered only in patients with tumors of less than two cm, with a cN0 status on preoperative imaging, and without evidence of metastasis. The surgeries were performed by experienced thoracic surgeons (S.R. and team) via a video-assisted thoracoscopic approach and consisted of segmentectomy of one or more pulmonary segments, according to margins. After surgery, all patients were prescribed the same respiratory rehabilitation program in accordance with current practices in the thoracic surgery department (prescription for physical therapy sessions at home or in a private practice, with a focus on bronchial drainage and forced/restricted breathing exercises), in accordance with current recommendations (12).

Prediction of postoperative FEV1 variation and definition of MCID

The predicted postoperative FEV1 variations were calculated for each of the three prediction methods using the preoperative FEV1 data as follows:

❖ Predicted variation (SPECT) = − measured preoperative FEV1 × (% counts of the segment(s) to be resected)

❖ Predicted variation (SPECT) = − measured preoperative FEV1 × (% counts of the segment(s) to be resected)

❖ Predicted variation (volumetric CT) = − measured preoperative FEV1 × (% volume of the segment(s) to be resected)

❖ Predicted variation (volumetric CT) = − measured preoperative FEV1 × (% volume of the segment(s) to be resected)

❖ Predicted variation (ASC) = − measured preoperative FEV1 × (n/19 of the segment(s) to be resected)

❖ Predicted variation (ASC) = − measured preoperative FEV1 × (n/19 of the segment(s) to be resected)

MCID in the FEV1 was defined as a decrease ≥−100 mL between the pre- and 6-month postoperative values in the FEV1 (8-10).

Statistical analyses

Categorical variables are expressed as effects and percentages, and continuous variables are expressed as medians and interquartile ranges. As this is a retrospective single-center prognostic cohort study, no sample size was initially determined, and it resulted from data collected (Flowchart in Figure 2). Uni- and multivariate analyses using forward stepwise selection were applied to identify factors predicting the difference in FEV1 from the preoperative measurement to the 6-month follow-up using linear regression. Standard linear regression assumptions were verified, including linearity of continuous predictors, normality of residuals, homoscedasticity, and absence of multicollinearity. The same analyses were conducted with data discretization based on the MCID in FEV1 using logistic regression. Finally, we plotted the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve based on the performance of lung perfusion SPECT/CT to predict a decrease in FEV1 that reached an actual 6-month MCID with the best sensitivity–specificity trade-off. A P value <0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 25.0 software (IBM®).

Results

Population and descriptive data

Among the 167 patients who underwent SPECT/CT to evaluate lung function before planned segmentectomy, 51 patients [aged 67.0 years (IQR: 60–72), 21 women, and 1 segment resected (IQR: 1–2)] meeting the inclusion criteria were included. The median variation in FEV1 at 6 months (i.e., postoperative FEV1 minus preoperative FEV1) was −80 mL (IQR: −200–120]. Thirty-three patients (65%) experienced a functional decrease after 6 months, with 23 (45%) meeting the 100 mL MCID threshold. Eighteen (35%) patients had increased postoperative FEV1 levels, which translated to positive results. Descriptive data are available in Table 1. Details of the flow chart are provided in Figure 2.

Discussion

Key findings

Among the multiple parameters tested in patients who underwent segmentectomy, lung perfusion SPECT/CT was the only individual factor selected by the multivariate analysis for the prediction of the change in the 6-month FEV1. Similarly, lung perfusion SPECT/CT was the only selected factor for the prediction of a decrease in the 6-month FEV1 beyond the MCID (≥100 mL). Moreover, the lung perfusion SPECT/CT-based prediction of a decrease in the FEV1 of more than 192 mL had good performance for predicting an actual decrease in the 6-month MCID.

Strengths and limitations

We have included the largest studied series of patients with segmentectomies who underwent a lung perfusion scintigraphy (6,7,13). In their 2019 systematic review and meta-analysis, Oswald et al. selected 17 studies with a low risk of bias, of which only 4 (23 patients) involved segmentectomy, and of these 23 patients, only 7 underwent SPECT/CT (6). This is the first study to employ a latest-generation 360° CZT SPECT/CT camera, enabling high-precision counting and segment-scale tracing with a low irradiation dose (median effective dose of 7.0 mSv; results not shown). Furthermore, our hybrid CZT SPECT/CT system with dedicated latest-generation CT enabled a combined SPECT/CT procedure including lung CT in patients with apnea. We developed a novel method of tracing segments by tracing the bisecting lines of segmental bronchi, enabling us to approach the anatomical reality and the planned surgical procedure as closely as possible (Figure 1). An advantage of SPECT is that apnea is not required during acquisition, making this examination more accessible for patients with a fragile pulmonary condition (86% of patients in our study had pulmonary pathology).

Limitations are firstly inherent to the retrospective nature of the study and the fact that no external validation is provided. The population included in this study represents patients with small lung cancers rather than high-risk patients as only 23.5% of patients had previous thoracic surgery and the pre-surgical FEV1 was 2,200 mL. It is nevertheless consistent with real-world data of patients addressed for lung perfusion scintigraphy in a context of planned segmentectomy. Even if all included patients received the same standard prescription for the rehabilitation program, no information is available on compliance with and follow-up of this program. However, patients who underwent PFTs 6 months post-surgery should represent the most compliant patients. Although PFTs are performed in accordance with good clinical practice recommendations, PFTs are known to require both patient and physician motivation, as well as full cooperation. Moreover, we could not use percentages of theoretical values, because the reference frames changed during the period studied. Regarding SPECT/CT segmentation, interobserver analysis of segmental perfusion quantification is not available in the current study but it is offset by the specific workflow developed and presented in Figure 1, which assesses the robustness and the reproducibility of the methodology provided. Lastly, eligible patients in whom segmentectomy involved one or more segments of the basal pyramid were excluded due to technical limitations of the software dedicated to the SPECT/CT images. Even if the majority of lung nodules involved upper lung segments (5,14), this latter statement limits the potential generalization of our data.

Comparison with similar research

These results show that the predictive capacity of lung perfusion SPECT/CT also works for patients who have undergone segmentectomy, whereas this had previously been demonstrated only at the suprasegmental level (lobe, lung). SPECT/CT is a powerful tool for predicting postoperative lung function after lobectomy (5-7). Notably, a significant proportion of patients (18 patients, i.e., 35%) improved their postoperative FEV1 at 6 months; i.e., their postoperative FEV1 at 6 months was higher than their preoperative FEV1. Some studies of lung segmentectomy surgery have reported compensation for lung resection and preserved postoperative lung function after surgery, particularly after segmentectomy (15,16). Other assumptions must be considered, such as lung volume reduction effects that occur in patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and/or emphysema, which alter lung function before surgery, particularly in the upper lobe segments where emphysema predominates and lung nodules are most common (13,14,16-19). Other hypotheses include hyperinflation, compensatory lung growth, obstructive atelectasis, mass effect, peritumoral non-functional zone or tumour invasion of micro-vessels, or stapling (15,17). Pre-surgical predictive factors to identify this subgroup of population are provided in Table S1. A higher preoperative FEV1 was associated with a lower probability of improvement in respiratory function after surgery, suggesting a ceiling effect in patients with preserved baseline respiratory function.

Explanations of findings

Among the numerous factors tested, lung perfusion SPECT/CT was the only predictor of 6-month FEV1 changes in the multivariate analysis; factors that were not predictors included clinical factors (age, sex, smoking status, body mass index, number of segments removed, history of pulmonary disease, and history of thoracic surgery), paraclinical factors (preoperative PFT results with measured preoperative FVC, FEV1, and DLCO), and imaging factors (volumetric CT and ASC). The correlation between SPECT/CT of lung perfusion prediction and changes in the 6-month FEV1 was nevertheless weak (R2 at 0.22, Figure 4), suggesting potential other confounding factors and requiring taking this correlation with caution. The fact that the number of segments removed was not predictive of 6-month FEV1 changes, even in univariate analysis, is not surprising since in our population most of the patients (n=31; 61%) benefited from only one pulmonary segment surgery. This “paradoxical” postoperative increase in FEV1 in 35% of our population is reflected by the intercept shown in Figure 4 at −192 mL for lung perfusion SPECT/CT prediction, which is in favor of an overestimation of the predicted SPECT/CT impairment. Lung perfusion SPECT/CT can only predict a decrease in FEV1, which is also the case for the other imaging modalities that are used (volumetric CT or ASC methods).

Implications and actions needed

However, its importance in routine clinical practice is to identify patients who will not recover spontaneously, i.e., those whose respiratory capacity decreases after surgery, to intensify respiratory rehabilitation measures and monitor vigilantly. In our study, 33 (65%) patients in this population experienced a functional decrease in FEV1 after 6 months, with 23 (45%) meeting the MCID threshold.

MCID is a widely accepted concept that reflects not only the patient’s perception of dyspnea or the risk of relapse after acute exacerbation of COPD (8) but also the gain after treatment in pharmacological studies (8-10). Multivariate analysis revealed that only lung perfusion SPECT/CT was a predictive factor for a decrease in 6-month postoperative FEV1 reaching the MCID. In greater detail, the threshold of −192 mL in terms of the predictive value of lung SPECT/CT was associated with a sensitivity of 83% and a specificity of 64% for predicting an actual 6-month MCID decrease. This threshold for lung perfusion SPECT/CT should therefore be of particular clinical interest preoperatively.

Conclusions

In patients who undergo segmentectomy, preoperative lung perfusion SPECT/CT is associated with a 6-month postoperative decrease in FEV1 reaching the widely clinically used MCID cutoff of ≥100 mL. A SPECT/CT-based prediction of a decrease in the FEV1 of more than 192 mL has good performance for predicting an actual decrease in the 6-month MCID. SPECT/CT shows promising predictive utility and warrants prospective multicenter validation before routine clinical implementation.
