Abstract
. We prove Sobolev interpolation inequalities on extension domains that have a form reminiscent of the corresponding whole-space inequalities. This form is crucial in certain applications, which we discuss as well. The technical key ingredient is the notion of a Lebesgue W 1 , p W^{1,p} - extension domain, which we introduce here, and our proof that, for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , any W 1 , p W^{1,p} -extension domain is a Lebesgue W 1 , p W^{1,p} -extension domain.

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
A Sobolev interpolation inequality

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain (a connected open set) and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Recall that the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
consists of
all
L^{p}
-integrable functions on
\Omega
whose first order
distributional derivatives belong to
L^{p}(\Omega)
. the norm We say that
E
is a
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-extension operator if

(1)

E:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

i.e., for every
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
there exists an
E(u)\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
E(u)\big|_{\Omega}\equiv u
. We say that the extension
operator is bounded if there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that

\|E(u)\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\qquad\text{for all}\ u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\,.

A domain
\Omega
is said to be a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain if there
is a bounded
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-extension operator. We emphasize that we do
not assume that
E
is linear.

We are interested in Sobolev interpolation inequalities for extension
domains. The model inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is of the form

(2)

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\theta}

for
u\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{1}_{\rm loc\,}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\nabla u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Here the exponents satisfy
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<\infty
and

(3)

\begin{cases}s\leq r\leq\frac{np}{n-p}&\text{if}\ p<n\,,\\
s\leq r<\infty&\text{if}\ p=n>1\,,\\
s\leq r\leq\infty&\text{if}\ p>n\ \text{or}\ p=n=1\,,\end{cases}

and
\theta\in[0,1]
satisfies

(4)

\theta\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{n}\right)+\left(1-\theta\right)\,\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{r}\,.

Moreover, the constant
C
in (
2
) depends only on

n
,
p
,
s
and
r
.

A feature of (
2
) that is crucial in several applications is that
it involves a
\theta
-geometric mean of
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

and
\|u\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. Intuitively, this means that only a fraction
\theta
of the gradient, and not the whole gradient, is necessary to
control an
L^{r}
-norm.

In view of the elementary inequality
a^{\theta}b^{1-\theta}\leq\theta a+(1-\theta)b
, inequality (
2
) with the
\theta
-geometric mean
implies a corresponding inequality with the arithmetic mean. Moreover, in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a simple scaling argument shows that the inequality with the
\theta
-geometric mean and with the arithmetic mean are equivalent. This
scaling argument, however, is no longer available when the whole space is
replaced by an extension domain and a straightforward application of the
extension property only yields the interpolation inequality with the
arithmetic mean, viz.

(5)

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\right).

This, however, is not enough in certain applications, some of which we
discuss below.

The following is our main result. It provides a Sobolev interpolation
inequality with the
\theta
-geometric mean on extension domains.

Theorem 1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain
and let
0<s<\infty
. Then for all
r
as in (
3
) one has

(6)

W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{s}(\Omega)\subset L^{r}(\Omega)

and for all
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{s}(\Omega)
we have

(7)

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}\,,

where
\theta\in[0,1]
is given by (
4
) and the constant
C
only depends on
\Omega
,
p
,
s
and
r
.

Remark 2
.

(a) The embedding (
6
) is well-known,
but a direct proof based on the extension property only gives the
‘arithmetic mean’ bound (
5
), see e.g.
[
12
]
. The thrust of our
result is the ‘
\theta
-geometric mean’ bound (
7
). This
\theta
-geometric mean bound is well-known when
\Omega
has uniformly
Lipschitz continuous boundary, based on the explicit construction of an
extension operator in this case. In contrast, our theorem is valid for

any
extension domain, irrespectively of how the extension operator
is constructed.

(b) The specific value of
\theta
in Theorem
1
is the
same as in the whole-space inequality (
2
) and the latter is
uniquely determined by a scaling argument. Note that if the equality

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{\delta}\|u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}^{1-\delta}

holds with
some exponent
\delta\in[0,1]
and
r>s
, then necessarily

\delta\geq\theta\,.

Indeed, take
u\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and apply the assumed
inequality to
u((\cdot-x_{0})/\varepsilon)
for some point
x_{0}\in\Omega
.
Comparing the powers of both sides as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we find

\delta\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{n}\right)+\left(1-\delta\right)\,\frac{1}{s}\leq\frac{1}{r}\,,

from which we easily conclude the claimed inequality
\delta\geq\theta
.

(c) The proof of Theorem
1
is easy when
p<n
, but when

p\geq n

it relies on our new Theorem
4
below about Lebesgue
W^{1,p}
-extension operators. While the proof that we give for
p\geq n

also works for
p<n
, we now present a simple direct argument in the
latter case and
explain the difficulty of extending it to
p\geq n
.
For
p<n
we set
p^{*}:=np/(n-p)
. Then the extension property implies
that

\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\,.

(This is the limiting case of Theorem
1
, corresponding to
\theta=1
.) Now given
r,s,\theta
as in the theorem we have, by Hölder’s inequality,

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}\,.

Combining this with the
bound on
\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}
implies the inequality in the
theorem.
For comparison, let us see how a corresponding argument fails to
prove the
theorem in case
p\geq n
. For simplicity we give restrict ourselves
to

p>n
. By the extension property, we have

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\,.

Combining this with the bound

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{1-s/r}\|u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}^{s/r}

gives

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{1-s/r}\|u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}^{s/r}\,.

This
is a bound
as in the theorem, but with the (optimal) power
\theta
replaced by
the larger power
1-s/r
. The argument for
p=n
is similar, but
only leads to
powers even greater than
1-s/r
.

(d) In the case
p=n>1
our proof (based on
[
1
, Theorem 3.4]
)
also gives exponential integrability of
u
and it yields a bound on the
behavior of the constant
C
as
r\to\infty
. For the sake of conciseness
we refrain from stating this explicitly.

Applying the inequality in Theorem
1
to the function
u-u_{\Omega}
and using the Poincaré inequality, we arrive at the following
consequence.

Corollary 3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain
of finite measure and let
0<s<\infty
. Then, for
r
and
\theta
as in
Theorem
1
and any
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{s}(\Omega)
,

\|u-u_{\Omega}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\theta}\|u-u_{\Omega}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}\,.

1.2.
Lebesgue and Lebesgue–Dirichlet extension operators

We now present the technical core of the proof of Theorem
1
.
We say that a bounded
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-extension operator
E
is a
Lebesgue
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-extension operator
if for every
1<q\leq\infty
there is a constant
C_{q}
such that

(8)

\|E(u)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{q}\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\qquad\text{for all}\ u\in L^{q}(\Omega)\,.

We say that a bounded
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-extension operator
E
is a
Lebesgue–Dirichlet
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-extension operator
if it is a
Lebesgue
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-extension operator and if there is a constant
C
such that

(9)

\|\nabla E(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\text{for all}\ u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\,.

We say that
\Omega
is a Lebesgue–Dirichlet
W^{1,p}
-extension domain
if it admits a Lebesgue-Dirichlet
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-extension operator.

The
W^{1,p}
-extension operators constructed in
[
11
]
for
bounded Lipschitz domains are linear Lebesgue–Dirichlet
W^{1,p}
-
extension operators. Moreover, a half space admits a Lebesgue–Dirichlet

W^{1,p}
-extension operator.

Theorem 4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then every
W^{1,p}
-extension domain admits a linear
Lebesgue
W^{1,p}
-extension operator.

However, there are no bounded Lebesgue–Dirichlet
W^{1,p}
-extension
domains.

An unbounded domain
\Omega
admits a Lebesgue–Dirichlet
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-extension operator if and only if there is an extension operator

(10)

E:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to W^{1,p}_{{\rm loc\,}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

such that there is a constant
C
with

(11)

\|\nabla E(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\text{for all}\ u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\,.

The non-existence of bounded Lebesgue-Dirichlet extension domains is
obvious: simply consider the function
u\equiv 1
. Then
\nabla u\equiv 0
on
\Omega
and a Lebesgue-Dirichlet extension operator would extend
u
to the function
Eu\equiv 1
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For examples of unbounded domains with an extension operator satisfying
(
10
) and (
11
) we refer to the reader
[
6
]
.

Remark 5
.

One could, equivalently, consider domains of finite/infinite measure
instead of bounded/unbounded domains above. This is because an unbounded
extension domain necessarily is of infinite measure by the measure density
properties from Proposition
9
and Lemma
10
below.

The following theorem shows that we actually can extend from
\Omega
to
any ball containing
\Omega
with both Lebesgue and Dirichlet control.

Theorem 6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded

W^{1,p}
-extension domain and fix a ball
B
with
\Omega\subset B
.
Then there is a linear operator

(12)

E:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to W^{1,p}(B)

so that

(13)

\|\nabla E(u)\|_{L^{p}(B)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

(14)

E(u)|_{\Omega}=u

and also for all
1<q\leq\infty
,

(15)

\|Eu\|_{L^{q}(B)}\leq C_{q}\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)},\hskip 5.69054pt\text{when}\hskip 5.69054ptu\in L^{q}(\Omega).

1.3.
First application: Heat Kernel Bounds

We end this introduction by presenting two applications where the version
(
7
) of the Sobolev interpolation inequality with the
\theta
-geometric mean is crucial and where the version (
5
) with the arithmetic mean is insufficient.

The first one of these applications concerns a bound on the heat kernel
of the Neumann Laplacian on a
W^{1,2}
-extension domain.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
W^{1,2}
-extension domain. Then the Neumann
heat semigroup
e^{t\Delta_{\Omega}^{\rm N}}
with
t>0
, originally defined
on
L^{2}(\Omega)
, extends to a bounded operator
L^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfying

\|e^{t\Delta_{\Omega}^{\rm N}}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\max\{t^{-n/2},1\}

with a constant
C
depending only on
\Omega
.

The power
-n/2
of
t
is best possible for small
t
. As we will explain
in what follows, this is a consequence of having the optimal power of
\theta
in Theorem
1
.

This theorem is not new. It appears as Theorem 2.4.4 in
[
2
]
. The
theorem is stated there only for
t\leq 1
, but the bound for
t>1

follows from a simple monotonicity argument; see Theorem 3.2.9 in
[
2
]
. The proof of the theorem given in
[
2
]
for
n\geq 3
is rather
direct
and is based on the Sobolev inequality with limiting exponent
2n/(n-2)
.
For
n\leq 2
the proof given in
[
2
]
is not long, but somewhat
unnatural: The author observes that
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
is a

W^{1,2}
-extension domain and uses the Sobolev inequality on that set to
get a bound on the heat kernel in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. This implies the
corresponding heat kernel bound on
\Omega
.

Our Theorem
1
allows us to give a unified proof in all
dimensions. Indeed, in the special case
p=r=2
,
s=1
we obtain the Nash
inequality

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+2/n}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2/n}\,,

and, by Theorem 2.4.7 in
[
2
]
, this
implies the claimed heat kernel bound.

We emphasize that we do not claim that our proof is shorter than that given in
[
2
]
. (Indeed, it probably is not, given the moderately deep
facts – Whitney decomposition, maximal theorem – that enter our proof of
Theorem
4
.) But we find it interesting to know that Nash’s method works in any dimension.
In fact, since a heat kernel bound is equivalent to a Nash inequality, the
‘unnatural proof’ of the heat kernel inequality mentioned above already
implies the Nash inequality. This, however, is a rather convoluted proof
of the Nash inequality and motivated us to find a more direct one.

Finally, we emphasize that it is the case
s=1
in Theorem
1

that is necessary to get heat kernel bounds. This is the case that is not
covered by our Theorem
4
, but requires the truncation machinery
of
[
1
]
, as explained in the proof of Theorem
1
.

1.4.
Second application: Keller–Lieb–Thirring inequalities

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
W^{1,p}
-extension
domain. Given a nonnegative measurable function
W
on
\Omega
, we
consider

E(W):=\inf_{0\not\equiv u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla u|^{p}-W|u|^{p})\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,dx}\,.

When

p=2
, this is the ground state energy of the Schrödinger operator
-\Delta-W
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with Neumann boundary conditions.
In the case
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
p=2
, Keller
[
7
]
asked about
the minimum value
of
E(W)
among all
W
with fixed
L^{q}
norm and gave some heuristic
arguments showing that this should be related to a semilinear elliptic
equation.
Later, Lieb and Thirring
[
10
]
arrived independently at
the same minimization
problem and showed that it is equivalent to a Sobolev interpolation
inequality.
We show that a similar statement applies for arbitrary
1<p<\infty
and
for arbitrary extension domains
\Omega
. Let

S_{r}^{\Omega}:=\inf_{0\neq u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\frac{\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}}{\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}}

with
\theta=\theta(r)
given by
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\theta}{n}=\frac{1}{r}
. (This is
the relation (
4
) when
s=p
.)

Theorem 8
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain. Let
\gamma>0
if
n\geq p
and
\gamma\geq 1-n/p
if
n<p
. Then

\inf_{0\neq W\in L^{\gamma+n/p}(\Omega)}\frac{E(W)+1}{(\int_{\Omega}W^{\gamma+n/p}\,dx)^{1/\gamma}}=-c_{n,p,\gamma}\left(S_{r}^{\Omega}\right)^{-\frac{p(\gamma+n/p)}{\gamma}}.

Here
r
is given by

\frac{1}{\gamma+n/p}+\frac{p}{r}=1

and

c_{n,p,\gamma}:=\frac{(n/(p\gamma))^{n/(p\gamma)}}{(1+n/(p\gamma))^{1+n/(p\gamma)}}\,.

The assumed conditions on
\gamma
are equivalent to
r<np/(n-p)
if
p\leq n
and
for
r\leq\infty
if
p>n
, so, by Theorem
1
, under these conditions we have

S_{r}^{\Omega}>0
.

In particular, Theorem
8
yields the lower bound

(16)

E(W)\geq-1-c_{n,p,\gamma}\left(S_{r}^{\Omega}\right)^{-\frac{p(\gamma+n/p)}{\gamma}}\left(\int_{\Omega}W^{\gamma+n/p}\,dx\right)^{1/\gamma}.

It is easy to construct, for each given
\gamma
as in the theorem, a
sequence
(W_{j})

satisfying
\int_{\Omega}W_{j}^{\gamma+n/p}\,dx\to\infty
and

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{E(W_{j})}{\left(\int_{\Omega}W_{j}^{\gamma+n/p}\,dx\right)^{1/\gamma}}<\infty\,.

(Indeed, we can take
W_{j}
as an optimizer for the
\mathbb{R}^{n}
problem,
truncated
at infinity and rescaled to a sufficiently small ball contained in
\Omega
.) This means that the power
1/\gamma
in the lower bound (
16
) on
E(W)
in
terms of
\int_{\Omega}W^{\gamma+n/p}\,dx
is best possible. Our proof of
Theorem
8
shows that this power is a direct consequence of the
corresponding power
\theta
in Theorem
1
(with
s=p
). Had we
obtained the bound in Theorem
1
with a larger power of
\theta
, we would have gotten the lower bound on
E(W)
only with a power
larger than
1/\gamma
, which would not have been
optimal.

We provide the simple proof of Theorem
8
in Section
5
below.

2.
Measure density condition

The following result appeared in
[
4
, Proposition 1]
.

Proposition 9
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
0<r\leq 1
one has

|\Omega\cap B(x,r)|\geq\delta\,|B(x,r)|\,.

In our proof of Theorem
4
we need the following variation of this
result.

Lemma 10
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an unbounded
domain such that there is an extension operator

E:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to W^{1,p}_{{\rm loc\,}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with

(17)

\|\nabla E(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\text{for all}\ u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\,.

Then there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}

and
r>0
one has

(18)

|\Omega\cap B(x,r)|\geq\delta\,|B(x,r)|\,.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three cases according to whether
p
is
smaller, equal or larger than
n
.

Case
1\leq p<n
.

We begin by proving that
|\Omega|=\infty
. Suppose to the contrary
that
|\Omega|<\infty
.

Since
r\mapsto|\Omega\setminus B(0,r)|
is continuous and
nonincreasing, for given
j\in\mathbb{N}
we can pick
r_{j}
such that

(19)

|\Omega\setminus B(0,r_{j})|=2^{-j}|\Omega|.

Fix
j
and define

(20)

u(x):=\begin{cases}1-\frac{d(y,B(x,r_{j}))}{r_{j+1}-r_{j}}&\text{if}\ y\in B(0,r_{j+1})\cap\Omega\,,\\
0&\text{if}\ y\in\Omega\setminus B(0,r_{j+1})\,.\end{cases}

Then
u
is
\frac{1}{r_{j+1}-r_{j}}
-Lipschitz.

By using the assumption (
17
) on
E
and (
20
) we get
that

(21)

\|\nabla E(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\frac{1}{r_{j+1}-r_{j}}|[B(0,r_{j+1})\cap\Omega]\setminus B(0,r_{j})|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We shall make use of the Sobolev–Poincaré inequality (see, e.g.,

[
9
, Theorem 8.12]
)

(22)

\left(\int_{B(0,r_{j+2})}|E(u)(x)-(E(u))_{B(0,r_{j+2})}|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C\left(\int_{B(0,r_{j+2})}|\nabla E(u)(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The integrand on the left side is
\geq|1-a|
on
B(0,r_{j})\cap\Omega
and
\geq|a|
on
[B(0,r_{j+2})\cap\Omega]\setminus B(0,r_{j+1})
,
where
a=(E(u))_{B(0,r_{j+2})}
. Since
\max\{|1-a|,|a|\}\geq 1/2
, by
combining (
20
) and (
22
), we conclude that

(23)

\begin{split}&\left(\min\{|B(0,r_{j})\cap\Omega|,|[B(0,r_{j+2})\cap\Omega]\setminus B(0,r_{j+1})|\}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\\
&\quad\leq C\frac{1}{r_{j+1}-r_{j}}|[B(0,r_{j+1})\cap\Omega]\setminus B(0,r_{j})|^{\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

By recalling the construction of
r_{j}
in (
19
), we can
express all measures that appear in this inequality in terms of
j
and we
arrive at the bound

(24)

r_{j+1}-r_{j}\leq C\frac{(2^{-j}|\Omega|-2^{-(j+1)}|\Omega|)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(2^{-(j+1)}|\Omega|-2^{-(j+2)}|\Omega|)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}}\,,

which implies that

(25)

r_{j+1}-r_{j}\leq C|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{n}}2^{-\frac{j}{n}}.

This contradicts
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(r_{j+1}-r_{j})=\lim_{j\to\infty}r_{j}-r_{1}=\infty
and therefore proves that
|\Omega|=\infty
.

We now turn to the proof of the measure density bound. We fix
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
r>0
and note that
|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|>0
. Let

r_{0}:=r
and choose a decreasing sequence of
(r_{j})
such that

(26)

|B(x,r_{j})\cap\Omega|=\frac{1}{2^{j}}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|.

Since
|\Omega|=\infty,
we may further choose
r_{-1}>r
so that

(27)

|B(x,r_{-1})\cap\Omega|=2|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|.

Fix
j
and define

(28)

u(y):=\begin{cases}1-\frac{1}{r_{j}-r_{j+1}}\,d(y,B(x,r_{j+1}))&\text{if}\ y\in B(x,r_{j})\cap\Omega\,,\\
0&\text{if}\ y\in\Omega\setminus B(x,r_{j})\,.\end{cases}

Then
u
is
\frac{1}{r_{j}-r_{j+1}}
-Lipschitz.

By using the assumption (
17
) on

and (
28
) we get
that

(29)

\|\nabla E(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\frac{|[B(x,r_{j})\cap\Omega]\setminus B(x,r_{j+1})|^{\frac{1}{p}}}{r_{j}-r_{j+1}}.

Once again we use the Sobolev–Poincaré inequality (
22
),
this time for
j\geq 0
in the form

(30)

\left(\int_{B(x,r_{j-1})}|E(u)(y)-(E(u))_{B(x,r_{j-1})}|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}dy\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C\left(\int_{B(x,r_{j-1})}|\nabla E(u)(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The integrand on the left side is
\geq|1-a|^{np/(n-p)}
on
B(x,r_{j+1})\cap\Omega
and
\geq|a|^{np/(n-p)}
on
[B(x,r_{j-1})\cap\Omega]\setminus B(x,r_{j})
, where
a:=(E(u))_{B(x,r_{j-1})}
. Since
\max\{|1-a|,|a|\}\geq 1/2
, by combining (
29
) and (
30
), we conclude that

(\min\{|B(x,r_{j+1})\cap\Omega|,|[B(x,r_{j-1})\cap\Omega]\setminus B(x,r_{j})|\})^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C\frac{|[B(x,r_{j})\cap\Omega]\setminus B(x,r_{j+1})|^{\frac{1}{p}}}{r_{j}-r_{j+1}}.

According to (
27
), we have

|[B(x,r_{j-1})\cap\Omega]\setminus B(x,r_{j})|=|B(x,r_{j-1})\cap\Omega|-|B(x,r_{j})\cap\Omega|=\frac{1}{2^{j}}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\,,

so the previous inequality implies

r_{j}-r_{j+1}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{2^{j}}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

As
r_{j}\to 0
for
j\to\infty
, we obtain

r=r_{0}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(r_{j}-r_{j+1})\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2^{j}}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Hence

(31)

r^{n}\leq C|\Omega\cap B(x,r)|,

which is the claimed inequality.

Case
p=n
.

We fix
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
r>0
and abbreviate

.
Define

(32)

u(y):=\begin{cases}1&\ {\rm if}\ y\in\frac{1}{3}B\cap\Omega,\\
0&\ {\rm if}\ y\in\Omega\setminus\frac{2}{3}B,\\
2-\frac{3|y-x|}{r}&\ {\rm if}\ y\in(\frac{2}{3}B\setminus\frac{1}{3}B)\cap\Omega.\end{cases}

\frac{3}{r}
-Lipschitz.

By our assumption (
17
) on

and (

(33)

\|\nabla E(u)\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{n}\leq C\|\nabla u\|^{n}_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\leq C\frac{3^{n}}{r^{n}}|[\tfrac{2}{3}B\setminus\tfrac{1}{3}B]\cap\Omega|\leq 3^{n}C\frac{|B\cap\Omega|}{r^{n}}.

Meanwhile, we claim that there exists
C>0
such that

(34)

\int_{10B}|\nabla E(u)|^{n}dx\geq C.

To prove this, we proceed as in the proof of
[

, Proposition 13]
. We recall that the Hausdorff 1-content of a set

is

where the infimum is taken over all countable covers of

and

.
Then, as shown in the proof of

. Hence by

), there exists

)

Combining (

) and (

), we find

(35)

\frac{|B\cap\Omega|}{r^{n}}\geq C,

which is the claimed inequality.

.

We fix

and

and abbreviate

.
Let

(36)

\frac{1}{r}
-Lipschitz.

) on

By Morrey’s inequality (see, e.g.,
[
8

), we
have, for all

(38)

Choosing

Combining (

\frac{1}{2}\leq C\cdot r^{1-\frac{n}{p}}\frac{|B\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}}{r}

Our goal in this section is to prove Theorems

3.1.
Whitney decomposition

Our proof of Theorem

will rely on the Whitney decomposition. We
begin with a reminder of this decomposition; see, e.g.,
[

]
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
U\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. Then there exists a collection
of closed cubes
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
so that

(1)

\bigcup_{i}Q_{i}=U
,

(2)

Then interior of
Q_{i}
are mutually disjoint,

(3)

if
x\in 5Q_{i}
, then
2\operatorname{diam}{Q_{i}}<{\rm dist\,}(x,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus U)<8\operatorname{diam}{Q_{i}}

(4)

For each
Q_{i}
there is
x_{i}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus U

such that
{\rm dist\,}(x_{i}^{\star},Q_{i})<15\operatorname{diam}{Q_{i}}
,

(5)

There is
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\chi_{15Q_{i}}(x)\leq M
for all
x\in U
.

We refer the above collection
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
as a Whitney
covering of
U
. With such a Whitney covering of
U
, we can associate a
Lipschitz partition of unity.

Lemma 12
.

Given a Whitney covering
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of
U
, there
exists a partition of unity
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
so that

(1)

{\rm supp\,}\varphi_{i}\subset 2Q_{i}
,

(2)

\varphi_{i}(x)\leq M^{-1}
for all
x\in Q_{i}
,

(3)

there is a constant
K
such that each
\varphi_{i}
is
K(\operatorname{diam}{Q_{i}})^{-1}
-Lipschitz,

(4)

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{i}(x)=\chi_{U}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
4
. First part.

Let
\Omega
be a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain. We are going to construct a
linear Lebesgue
W^{1,p}
-extension operator.
Let
\mathcal{W}=\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a Whitney covering of the open set

as in Lemma
11
and let
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the Lipschitz partition of unity as in Lemma
12
. We abbreviate

r_{i}:=\operatorname{diam}{Q}_{i}\,.

Let

J:=\{i\in I:\ r_{i}\leq 1\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in J}\,.

Next, let

define

Then

It follows from Proposition

such that

Let

Then from Lemma

is bounded

Indeed, if

For

For fixed

(44)

\begin{split}|(Eu)_{B_{i}^{\star}}-(Eu)_{B_{0}^{\star}}|&=|\rule[2.60138pt]{8.67204pt}{0.65062pt}\kern-11.56346pt\displaystyle\int_{B_{i}^{\star}}Eu(x)dx-\rule[2.60138pt]{8.67204pt}{0.65062pt}\kern-11.56346pt\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}^{\star}}Eu(y)dy|\\

|Eu(x)-Eu(y)|\leq C|x-y|(M(|\nabla Eu|)(x)+M(|\nabla Eu|)(y))

Using (

), (
45

(46)

\displaystyle|(Eu)_{B_{i}^{\star}}-(Eu)_{B_{0}^{\star}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rule[2.60138pt]{8.67204pt}{0.65062pt}\kern-11.56346pt\displaystyle\int_{B_{i}^{\star}}\rule[2.60138pt]{8.67204pt}{0.65062pt}\kern-11.56346pt\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}^{\star}}C|x-y|(M(|\nabla Eu|)(x)+M(|\nabla Eu|)(y))dxdy

\begin{split}\|\sum_{i\in J}(Eu)_{B_{i}^{\star}}-(Eu)_{B_{0}^{\star}})\nabla\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{p}(Q_{0})}^{p}&\leq\sum_{2Q_{i}\cap Q_{0}\neq\varphi}\frac{C}{(\operatorname{diam}(Q_{i}))^{p}}\int_{Q_{0}}|(Eu)_{B_{i}^{\star}}-(Eu)_{B_{0}^{\star}}|^{p}dx\\

Combining (

Now for

(49)

\begin{split}|\sum_{i\in J}(Eu)_{B_{i}^{\star}}\varphi_{i}(x)|&\leq\sum_{2Q_{i}\cap Q_{0}\neq\varphi}\rule[2.60138pt]{8.67204pt}{0.65062pt}\kern-11.56346pt\displaystyle\int_{B_{i}^{\star}}|Eu(y)|dy\\

Hence,

(50)

\displaystyle\|\sum_{i\in J}(Eu)_{Q_{i}^{\star}}\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{p}(Q_{0})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q_{0}}|M(v)(x)|^{p}dx.

By summing over the cubes
Q_{0}
, we conclude that

(51)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}|\nabla E_{1}u(x)|^{p}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}|E_{1}u(x)|^{p}dx\leq C&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|M(v)(x)|^{p}dx\\

(52)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}|\nabla E_{1}u(x)|^{p}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}|E_{1}u(x)|^{p}dx\leq C&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v(x)|^{p}dx\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla Eu(x)|^{p}dx.\end{split}

Combining the definition of

, (

|\partial\Omega|=0

we conclude that

(53)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{\Omega}}|\nabla E_{1}u(x)|^{p}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{\Omega}}|\nabla E_{1}u(x)|^{p}dx\leq C&\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}dx\\
&+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx.\end{split}

Moreover, since

3.4.

. Third part.

Now assume that

in

, we conclude that

is a linear

-extension operator.

Proof of the Sobolev interpolation inequality

In this section we prove our main result, a Sobolev interpolation
inequality with the
\theta
-geometric mean on extension domains.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

be as in the theorem, but assume initially that

.
Let

and let

The key observation is that we have not only

\|\nabla Eu\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\,,

but also

\|Eu\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\,.

This is the content of Theorem
4
. Therefore, the whole-space

2

.

follows from the result of

]

5.
Proof of the generalized Keller–Lieb–Thirring inequality

In this section we show, in the setting of Sobolev extension domains, that
the generalized Keller–Lieb–Thirring inequality is equivalent to a
Sobolev interpolation inequality.

Proof of Theorem
8
.

Interchanging the infima over
u
and
W
, we find

\displaystyle\inf_{0\neq W\in L^{\gamma+n/p}(\Omega)}\frac{E(W)+1}{(\int_{\Omega}W^{\gamma+n/p}\,dx)^{1/\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{0\not\equiv u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\inf_{w>0}w^{-\frac{\gamma+n/p}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}-\sup_{\|W\|_{L^{\gamma+n/p}(\Omega)}=w}\frac{\int_{\Omega}W|u|^{p}\,dx}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}\right).

By Hölder’s equality and its sharpness, we have

\sup_{\|W\|_{L^{\gamma+n/p}(\Omega)}=w}\int_{\Omega}W|u|^{p}\,dx=w\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{p}\,.

Thus, we have shown that

\inf_{0\neq W\in L^{\gamma+n/p}(\Omega)}\frac{E(W)+1}{(\int_{\Omega}W^{\gamma+n/p}\,dx)^{1/\gamma}}=\inf_{0\not\equiv u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\inf_{w>0}w^{-\frac{\gamma+n/p}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}-w\frac{\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}\right).

An elementary computation shows that, for any
A,B>0
,

\inf_{w>0}w^{-\frac{\gamma+n/p}{\gamma}}\left(A-wB\right)=-\frac{(n/(p\gamma))^{n/(p\gamma)}}{(1+n/(p\gamma))^{1+n/(p\gamma)}}A^{-\frac{n/p}{\gamma}}B^{\frac{\gamma+n/p}{\gamma}}

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\inf_{0\neq W\in L^{\gamma+n/p}(\Omega)}\frac{E(W)+1}{(\int_{\Omega}W^{\gamma+n/p}\,dx)^{1/\gamma}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n/(p\gamma))^{n/(p\gamma)}}{(1+n/(p\gamma))^{1+n/(p\gamma)}}\sup_{0\not\equiv u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}\right)^{-\frac{n/p}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma+n/p}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n/(p\gamma))^{n/(p\gamma)}}{(1+n/(p\gamma))^{1+n/(p\gamma)}}\left(S_{r}^{\Omega}\right)^{-\frac{p(\gamma+n/p)}{\gamma}}.

This proves the claimed bound.
∎
