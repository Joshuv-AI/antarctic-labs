Abstract

1.
Introduction

Hardy spaces on finite bordered Riemann surfaces with indefinite inner
products were introduced and systematically studied in

[
1
]
.
The main construction there replaces the usual
unit-disk model by the double
X
of a bordered surface
S
, uses Cauchy
kernels for vector bundles on compact Riemann surfaces, and builds an
explicit isometric isomorphism to a Hardy-Kreĭn space over
\mathbb{D}
.
In
[
16
,
17
]
it was shown that an unramified covering

F\colon S_{1}\to S_{2}
induces a canonical isometric isomorphism between the
associated Hardy-Kreĭn spaces.
The present paper addresses the remaining and more subtle case of ramified coverings.
Ramification introduces zeroes of the derivative of
F
, equivalently, poles of the
reciprocal Jacobian, which invalidate the
change-of-variables argument used in the unramified case and necessitate
a careful correction at the ramification locus.
The central new difficulty compared to the unramified case is that the
half-order differential
\Delta_{1}
satisfying
\Delta_{1}^{\otimes 2}\cong K_{X_{1}}

and the compatibility
\Delta_{1}\cong F^{*}\Delta_{2}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}(\tfrac{1}{2}R_{F})

requires the existence of a spin structure on
X_{1}
compatible with

F
and
\Delta_{2}
; this is a genuine cohomological obstruction
(see Section
3
and Assumption
3.1
below).
We make this hypothesis explicit throughout and show that under it all
constructions are globally well-defined.
A second hypothesis (Assumption
5.1
)
ensures that the branch locus is disjoint from
\partial S_{2}
; without
it several formulas require modification.

2.1.
Finite bordered Riemann surfaces and their doubles

Let
S
be an open Riemann surface such that
S\cup\partial S
is a finite
bordered Riemann surface with boundary
\partial S=X_{0}\cup\cdots\cup X_{k-1}

consisting of
k\geq 1
smooth Jordan curves.
The double
X
of
S
is the
compact Riemann surface of genus
g=2s+k-1
, where
s
is the genus of
S
,
obtained by gluing
S
to its mirror image
S^{\prime}
along
\partial S
.
There is a
canonical anti-holomorphic involution
\tau\colon X\to X
with fixed-point set

X_{f}=\partial S
and
X\setminus X_{f}=S\sqcup S^{\prime}
.

2.2.
Flat vector bundles and signature matrices

Fix a homomorphism
\chi\colon\pi_{1}(S,p_{0})\to U(m)
. The corresponding flat
unitary vector bundle on
S
is denoted
V_{\chi}
.
An analytic section
f
of

V_{\chi}
on the universal cover
\widetilde{X}
satisfies

f(T\widetilde{p})=\chi(T)f(\widetilde{p})
for all deck transformations
T
.
A signature matrix for
V_{\chi}
is a locally constant
m\times m

matrix function
J(\widetilde{p})
on
\partial\widetilde{X}
satisfying
J(\widetilde{p})^{*}=J(\widetilde{p})
and

\chi(T)^{*}J(T\widetilde{p})\chi(T)=J(\widetilde{p})
.
The Hardy-Kreĭn space

H^{2,J(p)}(S,V_{\chi}\otimes\Delta)
is the Hardy space
H^{2}(S,V_{\chi}\otimes\Delta)

equipped with the indefinite inner product

(1)

[\hat{f},\hat{g}]_{J(p)}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\int_{X_{i}}\hat{g}(\widetilde{p})^{*}J_{i}\hat{f}(\widetilde{p}),

where
J_{i}
are the constant values of
J(\widetilde{p})
on the components of

\partial\widetilde{X}
over
X_{i}
(see
[
1
]
for the full
definition).
Under the non-degeneracy condition
h^{0}(X,V_{\chi}\otimes\Delta)=0

this space is a Kreĭn space.

2.3.
Extension to the double and Cauchy kernels

Given
\chi
,
J_{0},\ldots,J_{k-1}
, the bundle
V^{S}_{\chi}
extends uniquely to
a flat unitary vector bundle
V^{X}_{\chi}
on
X
satisfying

(2)

\displaystyle G(\widetilde{p}^{\tau})^{*}

\displaystyle=G(\widetilde{p}),

(3)

\displaystyle\chi(T^{\tau})^{*}G(T\widetilde{p})\chi(T)

\displaystyle=G(\widetilde{p}),

where
G\colon\widetilde{X}\to GL(m,\mathbb{C})
is the everywhere non-singular holomorphic
matrix function encoding the parahermitian pairing

V^{X}_{\chi}\otimes(V^{X}_{\chi})^{\tau}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}

[
1
]
,
Proposition 2.1).
Under

, the bundle

V^{X}_{\chi}\otimes\Delta
on

admits a Cauchy kernel

K_{J(p)}\colon S\times S\to\Hom(\mathbb{C}^{m},\mathbb{C}^{m})
, which is the reproducing
kernel for
H^{2,J(p)}(S,V_{\chi}\otimes\Delta)
.

3.
Spin structures and the ramified direct image

3.1.
Setup and notation for the ramified cover

Let
F\colon S_{1}\to S_{2}
be a finite analytic map of finite bordered Riemann
surfaces, continuous up to the boundary and mapping
\partial S_{1}
to

\partial S_{2}
.
We assume that
F
extends to an analytic map

F\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
between the doubles that is equivariant with respect to
the anti-holomorphic involutions

F\circ\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}\circ F
.
Let
n=\deg F
denote the (topological) degree.
The ramification locus is the finite set

\mathrm{Ram}(F)=\{p\in X_{1}:e_{p}(F)>1\}

where
e_{p}(F)
is the local degree of
F
at
p
.
The ramification divisor and branch divisor are

R_{F}=\sum_{p\in X_{1}}(e_{p}(F)-1)p
,
B_{F}=F_{*}R_{F}=\sum_{q\in X_{2}}(r_{q}-1)q

where
r_{q}=\sum_{F(p)=q}(e_{p}(F)-1)+1\leq n
for each
q
.
By the Riemann-Hurwitz formula,

(4)

2g_{1}-2=n(2g_{2}-2)+\deg R_{F}.

We fix basepoints
p_{0}\in X_{2}
not in the branch locus and

p_{0}^{\prime}\in F^{-1}(p_{0})\subset X_{1}
.

3.2.
The spin-structure hypothesis

The correct analogue of the unramified condition
\Delta_{1}=F^{*}\Delta_{2}

in the ramified case involves a square root of
\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}(R_{F})
.
Since
R_{F}
may have odd multiplicities, such a square root is an
obstruction-theoretic datum, not automatic.
Recall that the canonical bundle satisfies

(5)

K_{X_{1}}\cong F^{*}K_{X_{2}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}(R_{F}).

A spin structure on
X_{1}
is a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
with

\mathcal{L}^{\otimes 2}\cong K_{X_{1}}
.
Given a spin structure
\Delta_{2}

on
X_{2}
, i.e.,
\Delta_{2}^{\otimes 2}\cong K_{X_{2}}
, the condition we
need is

(6)

\Delta_{1}^{\otimes 2}\cong F^{*}\bigl(\Delta_{2}^{\otimes 2}\bigr)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}(R_{F}).

This is equivalent to asking for a spin structure
\Delta_{1}
on
X_{1}

together with an isomorphism
\Delta_{1}\cong F^{*}\Delta_{2}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1/2}
,
where
\mathcal{L}_{1/2}
is a square root of
\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}(R_{F})
.
The existence of a holomorphic line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{1/2}
satisfying

\mathcal{L}_{1/2}^{\otimes 2}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}(R_{F})
is equivalent to
the condition that the divisor class
[\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}(R_{F})]
belongs to
the subgroup
2\mathrm{Pic}(X_{1})\subset\mathrm{Pic}(X_{1})
. Since

\mathrm{Pic}(X_{1})\cong\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X_{1})\times\mathbb{Z}
(degree) and

\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X_{1})
is a divisible group, a complex torus, this
reduces to the single numerical condition that
\deg R_{F}=\sum_{p}(e_{p}(F)-1)

be even. Requiring all ramification indices
e_{p}(F)
to be odd is
a convenient sufficient condition for this, since each

e_{p}(F)-1
is then even, but it is strictly stronger than necessary: for
instance two ramification points each with even index
e_{p}=2
already
give
\deg R_{F}=2
, an even number, thus a square root exists even though no

e_{p}
is odd.

Assumption 3.1

(Spin compatibility)
.

We assume that there exist spin structures
\Delta_{1}
on
X_{1}
and

\Delta_{2}
on
X_{2}
satisfying (
6

(a)

\tau_{i}
-equivariance
\Delta_{i}^{\tau_{i}}\cong\Delta_{i}
with
symmetric transition functions with respect to
\tau_{i}

;

(b)

of

\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}(R_{F})

\mathcal{L}_{1/2}^{\otimes 2}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}(R_{F})
,
such that
\Delta_{1}\cong F^{*}\Delta_{2}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1/2}

Remark 3.2
.

By the discussion above, Assumption

(b) holds whenever

\deg R_{F}
is even; this is the general criterion. It is satisfied, in
particular, in the following cases

(i): all ramification indices

are odd.
Then each

is even, thus

is
even, and a square root

exists as a holomorphic

(ii):

(iii): when all ramification indices are even and

is odd,
thus no square root of

becomes divisible by

When Assumption

is only locally defined, modulo a sign cocycle in

and all subsequent formulas are conditional on a choice of global trivialisation
of this cocycle. We do not pursue this generality here.

to a global analytic section of a holomorphic

Proof.

Coordinate independence.

Let

\tilde{t}_{2}=\beta(t_{2})

which is invariant.
The factor

(a flat bundle) and transforms equivariantly. Hence

is

Holomorphic extension across

Near a ramification point

, the

5.
Inner product on the target Hardy space

5.1.
Boundary hypothesis

We require the following assumption throughout the rest of the paper.

Assumption 5.1

(Boundary transversality)
.

The branch locus
B_{F}
is disjoint from
\partial S_{2}

(12)

\partial S_{2}\cap B_{F}=\left\{\varnothing\right\}.

Assumption
5.1
is not automatic from the condition

F(\partial S_{1})=\partial S_{2}
alone.
Indeed, an analytic map of bordered
surfaces may have ramification points lying on the boundary
\partial S_{1}
,
whose images then lie on
\partial S_{2}\subset B_{F}
.
For maps with boundary
ramification, the inner product formula (
14
) would require
additional correction terms at the boundary ramification points.
Assumption
5.1
excludes this possibility. It is satisfied in
all examples of Section
10
. In Example
10.1
,
the unique ramification point is
z=0\in S_{1}
, which maps to
0\in S_{2}=\mathbb{D}
,
an interior point.

5.2.
Signature matrices for
S_{2}
in the ramified case

The signature matrix
J_{2}(\widetilde{p})
for
(S_{2},\chi_{2},G_{2})
is defined by

(13)

\bigl[J_{2}(\widetilde{p})\bigr]_{kj}=J_{1}(g_{k}\;\widetilde{p})\delta_{kj},

for
\widetilde{p}\in\widetilde{X}
lying over a point of
X_{2,f}=\partial S_{2}
.
One verifies directly that
J_{2}(\widetilde{p})^{*}=J_{2}(\widetilde{p})
and

\chi_{2}(T)^{*}J_{2}(T\widetilde{p})\chi_{2}(T)=J_{2}(\widetilde{p})
for all
T\in\pi_{1}(X_{2},p_{0})
.

5.3.
The indefinite inner product

For sections
\hat{f}^{2}
,
\hat{g}^{2}
of

on
S_{2}

with boundary values in

, define

\bigl[\hat{f}^{2},\hat{g}^{2}\bigr]_{H^{2,J_{2}(p)}(S_{2},V^{S_{2}}_{\chi_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{2})}=\int_{\tilde{X}_{2,f}}\hat{g}^{2}(\widetilde{p}^{\tau_{2}})^{*}G_{2}(\widetilde{p})\;\hat{f}^{2}(\widetilde{p}).

When

is unramified this coincides with the formula in

[

]
, equation (20).
Under Assumption
5.1
,

does not meet

Combined with Lemma

6.
Main Theorem: isometric isomorphism for ramified coverings

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
F\colon S_{1}\to S_{2}
be a finite
n
-sheeted analytic map of finite
bordered Riemann surfaces, continuous up to the boundary, and let

F\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
be the induced map of doubles equivariant with
respect to
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}
.
Let
\chi_{1}\colon\pi_{1}(S_{1},p_{0}^{\prime})\to U(m)

be a unitary representation,
J_{1}(\widetilde{p})
be signature matrices for
V_{\chi_{1}}
,
and assume

(15)

h^{0}\;\left(X_{1},V^{X_{1}}_{\chi_{1}}\otimes\Delta_{1}\right)=0.

Suppose Assumptions
3.1
and
5.1
hold.
Define
\chi_{2}
,
G_{2}
,
J_{2}
by equations (
10
)-(
13
),
and the map
\phi_{F}
by formula (
9
).
Then

(i)

F_{*}^{\rm ram}(V^{X_{1}}_{\chi_{1}}\otimes\Delta_{1})\cong V^{X_{2}}_{\chi_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{2}

as holomorphic vector bundles, in particular, as coherent sheaves,
on all of
X_{2}
;

(ii)

h^{0}(X_{2},V^{X_{2}}_{\chi_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{2})=0
, thus the space

H^{2,J_{2}(p)}(S_{2},V^{S_{2}}_{\chi_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{2})
is non-degenerate
and hence a Kreĭn space;

(iii)

The map
\phi_{F}\colon\hat{f}^{1}\mapsto\hat{f}^{2}
defined
by (
9
) is an isometric isomorphism

\phi_{F}\colon H^{2,J_{1}(p)}\;\left(S_{1},V^{S_{1}}_{\chi_{1}}\otimes\Delta_{1}\right)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{2,J_{2}(p)}\;\left(S_{2},V^{S_{2}}_{\chi_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{2}\right).

Proof.

Part
(i)
.

By Proposition
3.4
the map
\hat{f}^{1}\mapsto\hat{f}^{2}

is a well-defined holomorphic bundle map on
X_{2}
.
It is a morphism of flat vector bundles
the equivariance property (
10
) of
\chi_{2}
ensures
that
\hat{f}^{2}(T\widetilde{p})=\chi_{2}(T)\hat{f}^{2}(\widetilde{p})
for all
T\in\pi_{1}(X_{2},p_{0})
.
It remains to show the map is an isomorphism of coherent sheaves, i.e., that
it has trivial kernel and is surjective as a map of stalks.
The kernel is trivial because at any
q\notin B_{F}
the map is a direct sum of
n
evaluations
of
\hat{f}^{1}
along the
n
branches, and at
q\in B_{F}
holomorphic extension
(Proposition
3.4
) shows that the kernel condition forces

\hat{f}^{1}
to vanish at each preimage with its ramification multiplicity, hence

\hat{f}^{1}=0
globally.
For surjectivity: given any holomorphic section
\hat{f}^{2}

of
V^{X_{2}}_{\chi_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{2}
, the formula (
9
) is inverted
by restriction to any one branch
g_{i}
away from
B_{F}
and holomorphic
continuation;
the
V^{X_{1}}_{\chi_{1}}
-equivariance condition is then verified using the
definition of
\chi_{2}
.
The compatibility
F_{*}^{\rm ram}(\Delta_{1})\cong\Delta_{2}
follows from the
local computation in the proof of Proposition
3.4

the combination
\ell_{1}/(\ell_{2}\sqrt{\varphi^{\prime}})
is non-vanishing holomorphic,
confirming that the half-order differentials match under the direct image.

Part
(ii)
.

Since
F_{*}^{\rm ram}(V^{X_{1}}_{\chi_{1}}\otimes\Delta_{1})\cong V^{X_{2}}_{\chi_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{2}

as coherent sheaves (part
(i)
), and for a finite morphism

F\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
one has

H^{0}(X_{2},F_{*}^{\rm ram}(V^{X_{1}}_{\chi_{1}}\otimes\Delta_{1}))\cong H^{0}(X_{1},V^{X_{1}}_{\chi_{1}}\otimes\Delta_{1})

by the Leray spectral sequence, using finite morphism and coherence,
the assumption (
15
) gives

h^{0}(X_{2},V^{X_{2}}_{\chi_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{2})=h^{0}(X_{1},V^{X_{1}}_{\chi_{1}}\otimes\Delta_{1})=0.

Part
(iii)
.

We have to show (a)
\hat{f}^{1}\in H^{2,J_{1}}(S_{1},V^{S_{1}}_{\chi_{1}}\otimes\Delta_{1})
if and only
if
\hat{f}^{2}\in H^{2,J_{2}}(S_{2},V^{S_{2}}_{\chi_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{2})
;
(b) the inner products satisfy

[\hat{f}^{2},\hat{h}^{2}]_{J_{2}}=[\hat{f}^{1},\hat{h}^{1}]_{J_{1}}
.

For (a): let
X_{2,i}
be a boundary component of
X_{2}
and let

X_{1,ij}
,
j=1,\ldots,n_{i}
, be its preimages in
X_{1}
.
By Assumption
5.1
, the boundary components
X_{2,i}
do
not contain branch values, thus the covering
F
restricts to an honest
(unramified) cover of each
X_{2,i}
by components of
\partial S_{1}
.
Near each

the map

is locally a degree-

-fold map,
but since
X_{1,ij}
is a boundary component and
F(X_{1,ij})\subseteq\partial S_{2}
while
\partial S_{2}\cap B_{F}=\left\{\varnothing\right\}
, in fact
e_{ij}=1

on
\partial S_{1}
with the ramification is in the interior.
Therefore the change of variables on the boundary is non-degenerate, and one has

(16)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k_{2}-1}\int_{X_{2,i}(r)}\hat{f}^{2}(p)^{*}\hat{f}^{2}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k_{2}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\int_{\tilde{X}_{1,ij}(r)}\hat{f}^{1}(\widetilde{p})^{*}\hat{f}^{1}(\widetilde{p})=\sum_{i=0}^{k_{1}-1}\int_{X_{1,i}(r)}\hat{f}^{1}(p)^{*}\hat{f}^{1}(p).

The
\sqrt{e_{ij}}
factor in (
9
) is trivial here since

e_{ij}=1
on
\partial S_{1}
; the formula (
9
) recovers
the unramified boundary behaviour. Taking the supremum over
r\in(1-\varepsilon,1)

shows that
\hat{f}^{1}\in H^{2}(S_{1},V^{S_{1}}_{\chi_{1}}\otimes\Delta_{1})
iff

\hat{f}^{2}\in H^{2}(S_{2},V^{S_{2}}_{\chi_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{2})
.

For (b): using (
14
) and the boundary change-of-variables
identity (
16
) applied to the integrand

\hat{g}^{2}(\widetilde{p}^{\tau_{2}})^{*}G_{2}(\widetilde{p})\hat{f}^{2}(\widetilde{p})
, one computes

\displaystyle\bigl[\hat{f}^{2},\hat{h}^{2}\bigr]_{J_{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{X}_{2,f}}\hat{h}^{2}(\widetilde{p}^{\tau_{2}})^{*}G_{2}(\widetilde{p})\hat{f}^{2}(\widetilde{p})

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{X}_{2,f}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{h}^{1}\;\left((g_{i}^{\tau}\widetilde{p})^{\tau_{1}}\right)^{*}G_{1}(g_{i}^{\tau}\widetilde{p})\hat{f}^{1}(g_{i}^{\tau}\widetilde{p})\frac{|d\ell_{2}(\widetilde{p})|^{2}}{|\ell_{1,i}(g_{i}\widetilde{p})|^{2}\;|\varphi_{i}^{\prime}(g_{i}\widetilde{p})|\;e_{i}(g_{i}\widetilde{p})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{X}_{1,f}}\hat{h}^{1}(\widetilde{p}^{\tau_{1}})^{*}G_{1}(\widetilde{p})\hat{f}^{1}(\widetilde{p})=\bigl[\hat{f}^{1},\hat{h}^{1}\bigr]_{J_{1}},

where the second equality uses (
16
) applied to the
integrand, with the
\tau_{1}
-invariance of sections of
\Delta_{1}
and the
symmetry of its transition functions, Assumption
3.1
(a), together
with the fact that on the boundary

(Assumption
5.1
).
∎

.

7.1.

Definition 7.1
.

A (triangular) vessel over

,

\displaystyle A_{1}^{[*]}A_{1}-A_{1}A_{1}^{[*]}

\displaystyle=B_{1}\Phi B_{1}^{[*]},

(19)

\displaystyle A_{2}^{[*]}A_{2}-A_{2}A_{2}^{[*]}

\displaystyle=B_{2}\Phi B_{2}^{[*]},

Remark 7.2
.

Written as literal operator compositions,

requires

’s

to match

’s domain
\mathcal{H}_{2}
,

’s codomain

to match

’s domain
\mathcal{H}_{2}
.
Neither composition is explicitly
defined, and inserting a single linking operator

and

, using the adjoint-pairing identity

)

(21)

\displaystyle\bigl[B_{2}^{[*]}(\phi_{F}A_{1}h_{1}),e\bigr]_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=\bigl[\phi_{F}B_{1}e,\;A_{2}\phi_{F}h_{1}\bigr]_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=\bigl[B_{2}e,A_{2}\phi_{F}h_{1}\bigr]_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}.

Remark 7.3

8.
Bezoutian operators

8.1.
Interior evaluation functionals

Since
\phi_{F}
is an isometry (Theorem
6.1
), the operator

\phi_{F}\phi_{F}^{[*]}-\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=0
is trivially zero.
To obtain a non-trivial Bezoutian, we use the ramification data of
F

to define a geometrically motivated rank perturbation of the identity.

Remark 8.1
.

For any point
q\in S_{2}
(interior or boundary), the point-evaluation functional

\mathrm{ev}_{q}\colon

\mathcal{H}_{2}

\longrightarrow

\mathbb{C}^{nm}
,
\mathrm{ev}_{q}(h)=h(q)
,
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, with Kreĭn adjoint

\mathrm{ev}_{q}^{[*]}\colon\mathbb{C}^{nm}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
given by

(\mathrm{ev}_{q}^{[*]}v)(p)=K^{(2)}(p,q)v
, where
K^{(2)}
is the
Cauchy (reproducing) kernel of
\mathcal{H}_{2}
introduced in
Section
2
. This is the standard reproducing-kernel
identity: for each fixed
q\in S_{2}
, the section

K^{(2)}(\cdot,q)
is by definition a genuine element of
\mathcal{H}_{2}
.
This is precisely what it means for
\mathcal{H}_{2}
to be a
reproducing-kernel space. The only place a singularity of
K^{(2)}(p,q)

as a bivariate kernel could matter is on the diagonal
p=q
, which
is irrelevant here since
q
is held fixed throughout and we never
evaluate the resulting section of
p
at
p=q
within the inner product.

The rank-one operator

(28)

T_{q}=\mathrm{ev}_{q}^{[*]}\Phi\mathrm{ev}_{q}\colon\mathcal{H}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2},\qquad(T_{q}h)(p)=K^{(2)}(p,q)\Phi h(q),

is therefore a well-defined bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}_{2}
for any
q\in S_{2}
,
with
\mathrm{rank}T_{q}\leq nm
.

8.2.
The ramification Bezoutian

Definition 8.2
.

Let
\mathrm{Ram}(F)\cap S_{1}=\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{N}\}\subset S_{1}
be the
finite set of interior ramification points of
F
, with indices

e_{\nu}=e_{r_{\nu}}(F)\geq 2
and images
q_{\nu}=F(r_{\nu})\in S_{2}
(all

5.1
).
The ramification Bezoutian of the vessel
\mathfrak{V}_{F}
is

(29)

\mathcal{B}_{F}^{\rm ram}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}(e_{\nu}-1)T_{q_{\nu}}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}(e_{\nu}-1)\mathrm{ev}_{q_{\nu}}^{[*]}\Phi\mathrm{ev}_{q_{\nu}}\colon\mathcal{H}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2}.

This is a well-defined, bounded, finite-rank, self-adjoint operator in the
Kreĭn metric. When
F
is unramified,
N=0
and
\mathcal{B}_{F}^{\rm ram}=0
.

The weight
(e_{\nu}-1)
at each ramification point counts the number of
extra preimages that collapse to
r_{\nu}
under
F
. The local degree
is
e_{\nu}
, thus there are
e_{\nu}-1
extra sheets.
The operator

T_{q_{\nu}}=\mathrm{ev}_{q_{\nu}}^{[*]}\Phi\mathrm{ev}_{q_{\nu}}
encodes
the contribution of the branch value
q_{\nu}
to the deviation of
\phi_{F}^{\rm ram}

from an isometry.

8.3.
Livsic-Bezoutian identity

Theorem 8.3
.

In the vessel
\mathfrak{V}_{F}
with Bezoutian
\mathcal{B}_{F}^{\rm ram}
, the following
Livsic-Bezoutian identity holds

(30)

A_{2}^{[*]}\mathcal{B}_{F}^{\rm ram}-\mathcal{B}_{F}^{\rm ram}A_{2}=\Omega_{F},

where
\Omega_{F}\colon\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
is the finite-rank
self-adjoint operator

(31)

\Omega_{F}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}(e_{\nu}-1)\bigl[A_{2}^{[*]},T_{q_{\nu}}\bigr]=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}(e_{\nu}-1)\Bigl(A_{2}^{[*]}K^{(2)}(\cdot,q_{\nu})\Phi\mathrm{ev}_{q_{\nu}}-K^{(2)}(\cdot,q_{\nu})\Phi\mathrm{ev}_{q_{\nu}}A_{2}\Bigr).

In particular, when
F
is unramified
N=0
,
\mathcal{B}_{F}^{\rm ram}=0
, and
both sides of (
30
) vanish identically.
This is genuinely
different in form from the model-operator identity (
19
),
which governs
A_{2}^{[*]}A_{2}-A_{2}A_{2}^{[*]}
for the identity
compression, because here
\mathcal{B}_{F}^{\rm ram}
is the finite-rank
perturbation (
29

term, thus no
B_{2}\Phi B_{2}^{[*]}
contribution arises.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{B}_{F}^{\rm ram}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}(e_{\nu}-1)T_{q_{\nu}}
exactly by
Definition
8.2
, with no identity term present, the
commutator with
A_{2}^{[*]}
is computed termwise using linearity

\bigl[A_{2}^{[*]},\mathcal{B}_{F}^{\rm ram}\bigr]=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}(e_{\nu}-1)\bigl[A_{2}^{[*]},T_{q_{\nu}}\bigr]=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}(e_{\nu}-1)\bigl(A_{2}^{[*]}T_{q_{\nu}}-T_{q_{\nu}}A_{2}\bigr),

and substituting
T_{q_{\nu}}=K^{(2)}(\cdot,q_{\nu})\Phi\mathrm{ev}_{q_{\nu}}

from Remark

gives exactly (
31

Each summand is rank
\leq nm
, and there are finitely many ramification points,
thus
\Omega_{F}
has finite rank. Self-adjointness in the Kreĭn metric
follows since each
T_{q_{\nu}}
is self-adjoint and
A_{2}^{[*]}
is the
genuine Kreĭn adjoint of
A_{2}
.
∎

. This depends on the exact placement of the branch points in

\mathrm{Pic}(X_{1})[2]
and must be confirmed individually.

Conclusion.

Given that Assumption
3.1
holds for the specific arrangement, Theorem
6.1
gives a full isometric isomorphism between
H^{2,J_{1}(p)}(S_{1},V^{S_{1}}_{\chi_{1}}\otimes\Delta_{1})

and
H^{2,J_{2}(p)}(S_{2},V^{S_{2}}_{\chi_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{2})
.
The Bezoutian

\mathcal{B}_{F}^{\rm ram}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{3}(e_{\nu}-1)\mathrm{ev}_{q_{\nu}}^{[*]}\Phi\mathrm{ev}_{q_{\nu}}

is a well-defined finite-rank operator, providing the precise interior corrections via evaluating the proper Szegő reproducing kernels to form
\Omega_{F}
within the Livsic-Bezoutian identity.
