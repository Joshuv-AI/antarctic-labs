In talking by telephone today with

have learned that you are out of the city and will not be
back into the office until Monday noon. Therefore, I am
having this letter delivered to you by courier with the
hope that we can talk by telephone upon your return.

The purpose of this letter is to extend to you, on behalf
of Dean Rusk and the other Directors of this Institute, an
invitation to be our honored Dinner Speaker on the occasion
of our Annual Meeting to be convened December 3-4 in the
State Department. More specifically, we hope that you can
give the after-dinner remarks on Monday evening, December 3,
in the Benjamin Franklin Dining Room on the eighth floor
of the State Department.

Enclosed is the copy of the two-day agenda. You will note
that a number of your associates are on the program, in-
cluding Bill Miller who will lead off the discussions on
the second day. Note also, please, our theme for the two-
day meeting: " Strategic Planning for the 1980s: How Busi-
ness Can Cope with the New International Realities."

We see this as a major event and believe that all would
benefit enormously by having you deliver the keynote evening
remarks. IMDI is entering its tenth year — and our theme
for this first Annual Meeting will be looking ahead at the
next ten years. We would leave the direction and substance
of your remarks to your own discretion. And, we would be
pleased to work with your professional staff in all ways
appropriate in the planning process.

As you are aware from having participated in one of our
Joint Council Quarterly Meetings two years ago this next
December as Dinner Speaker in the Kennedy Center, our
audience is comprised of approximately 250 senior members
of the international corporate, U.S. Government, and foreign
diplomatic communities. Our joint roster appears on page 6
of the enclosed Annual Report .
