Bilateral Treewidth for QBF: Where Strategies and Resolution Meet
Robert Ganian, Marlene Gründel

Abstract
Treewidth is a well-studied decompositional parameter to measure the tree-likeness of a graph. While the propositional satisfiability problem ( Sat ) is known to be tractable when parameterized by the treewidth of the underlying primal graph, the evaluation of quantified Boolean formulas (QBFs) remains 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤 \mathsf{PSPACE} -complete even on formulas of constant treewidth. Intuitively, this is because ordinary treewidth does not take into account the prefix of the QBF: it neither distinguishes between existential and universal variables, nor accounts for the order in which they are quantified. In the past, several weaker variants of treewidth have been devised to incorporate prefix-sensitive information. To establish tractability for QBFs under these notions, prior work has employed either strategy- or resolution-based techniques, thereby dividing the parameterized complexity landscape of QBF into two regimes that are incomparable in strength. We establish fixed-parameter tractability with respect to bilateral treewidth, a novel and strictly more powerful decompositional parameter that combines these rivaling approaches by simultaneously allowing for branching on strategies and performing Q-resolution. As in previous works in this direction, our algorithm assumes that a suitable tree decomposition is provided on the input.

Introduction

The language of
quantified Boolean formulas
(QBFs)
[
10
]
extends propositional logic with existential and universal quantification over variables. While this enables succincter formalizations, which is highly desirable for tasks in planning and verification
[
6
,
12
,
52
,
20
]
—see also
[
46
]
for a recent survey on further applications—it comes at the price of increased complexity: the satisfiability problem for QBFs (
QSat
) is
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete
[
49
]
.

When tasked with solving
QSat
, existential variables can often be handled analogously to the propositional setting (
Sat
), whereas genuinely new approaches are needed for their universal counterparts.
Over the past decades, two conceptually different paradigms have emerged, operating either
top-down
or
bottom-up
, respectively. In the top-down approach,
universal expansion
explicitly branches over all (partial) assignments to universal variables, creating a (possibly exponentially large) conjunction of formulas in which existential variables are replaced by annotated copies to indicate the respective branch.
In practical solving, the (partial) propositional formula that is obtained by universal expansion can then be handed to a
Sat
solver
[
3
,
11
,
35
,
29
]
. From a proof-theoretic perspective, calculi like
\mathsf{\forall Exp+Res}

[
30
]
and further generalizations
[
8
]
combine universal expansion with well-known propositional proof systems such as resolution
[
42
]
.

Universal reduction
, the second approach towards eliminating universal variables, processes QBFs in a bottom-up fashion. Here, universal variables can be dropped from a clause if they are not blocked by a co-occurring existential variable that is quantified later. Combining universal reduction with different variants of resolution gave rise to proof systems like
\mathsf{Q}
-
\mathsf{Res}

[
32
]
and further generalizations
[
27
,
4
,
47
]
. The system
\mathsf{Q}
-
\mathsf{Res}
in particular models practical
QSat
solving based on
conflict-driven clause learning
(CDCL)
[
51
,
28
,
36
]
.
Research in proof complexity has revealed that the paradigms of universal expansion and universal reduction are incomparable
[
30
,
7
]
, that is, their underlying proof systems do not simulate each other. As a consequence, the more sophisticated systems
\mathsf{IR}
-
\mathsf{calc}
and
\mathsf{IRM}
-
\mathsf{calc}

[
9
]
have been introduced to incorporate both paradigms simultaneously.

The
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-hardness of
QSat
has motivated the question of whether structural properties of instances could be employed to achieve tractability. At the foundational level, this study is typically pursued through the lens of
parameterized complexity

[
19
,
17
]
. There, one analyzes the running time of algorithms not only in terms of the input size
|I|
, but also with respect to a specified numerical parameter
k
, which can be seen as a measure of how “well-structured” an instance is. The standard notion of tractability used in this setting is tied to algorithms that run in time
f(k)\cdot|I|^{\mathcal{O}({1})}
for some computable function
f
; problems admitting such algorithms are called
fixed-parameter tractable
(FPT).

The most ubiquitous structural measure employed for such investigations is the
treewidth
of the instance, typically measured in terms of its
primal graph
, thus capturing the tree-likeness of its variable-to-variable interactions. But while treewidth has been instrumental in the design of fixed-parameter algorithms for
Sat

[
18
,
26
,
50
]
,
CSP

[
44
]
,
Integer Programming

[
31
,
21
]
and many other related problems,
QSat
is known to remain
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete even on instances which are “path-like” in the sense of having not only constant treewidth but also constant pathwidth
[
2
]
—see also the related works
[
40
,
25
,
24
]
for refined lower bounds.
Intuitively, this is because treewidth does not take into account the prefix of a QBF: it neither distinguishes between existential and universal variables, nor accounts for the order in which they are quantified. Consequently, several weaker variants of pathwidth and treewidth have been proposed to incorporate prefix-sensitive information. These notions split into two competing realms that remarkably echo the two paradigms encountered in proof complexity and practical solving.

The first realm is represented by
prefix pathwidth

[
23
]
, a variant of pathwidth that places an additional restriction on the order in which variables appear in a path decomposition. More specifically, a variable can only be forgotten when all variables on which it depends have already been introduced in the decomposition. Fixed-parameter tractability with respect to prefix pathwidth is then established in a top-down fashion that essentially eliminates variables from left to right, maintaining at each time step the sets of QBFs that an
existential strategy
could have produced under all universal plays so far. This procedure closely resembles incremental universal expansion with a subsequent explicit instantiation of some existential variables, as will be discussed in Subsection
3.3
.
In contrast,
respectful treewidth

[
16
,
2
]
enforces variables to be forgotten in an order that reverses their occurrence in the prefix. Fixed-parameter tractability with respect to this parameter is proved bottom-up by producing variants of
\mathsf{Q}
-
\mathsf{Res}
proofs.
Dependency treewidth

[
22
]
generalizes respectful treewidth by incorporating
dependency schemes

[
43
,
36
,
47
,
48
]
, thereby allowing greater flexibility in the order of variable elimination. Here, the produced derivations are certain types of
\mathsf{Q}(\mathcal{D})\text{-}\mathsf{Res}
proofs, where
\mathcal{D}
is the deployed dependency scheme. Prior work has shown that respectful treewidth and dependency treewidth are incomparable to prefix pathwidth
[
22
]
, see Figure
1
.

Our Contributions.
In this work, we introduce
bilateral treewidth
, a new decompositional parameter for
QSat
that unifies the two realms discussed above. As our main contribution, we prove that
QSat
is fixed-parameter tractable with respect to bilateral treewidth when a suitable tree decomposition is provided as part of the input. Solving

(a)

by a local branching on partial existential strategies that resembles universal expansion or

(b)

by employing variants of
\mathsf{Q}(\mathcal{D})\text{-}\mathsf{Res}

[

Preliminaries

For a positive integer
i
, we use
[i]
to denote the set
\{1,\dots,i\}
. We assume familiarity with basic graph terminology.

Quantified Boolean Formulas.
Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a countable set of Boolean variables. A
literal
is a Boolean variable
v\in\mathcal{Z}
or its negation
\overline{v}
, a
clause

\mathcal{C}
is a set of literals, and a
CNF formula

\phi
is a set of clauses. A clause
\mathcal{C}
is
tautological
if
\{v,\overline{v}\}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
for some
v\in\mathcal{Z}
and
empty
, denoted by
\bot
, if it does not contain any literals.
We write
\mathrm{Var}({\phi})
(
\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{C}})
, respectively) to denote the set of variables that occur in
\phi
(in
\mathcal{C}
, resp.). For each variable
x\in\mathrm{Var}({\phi})
, we denote by
\phi_{x}
the set of all clauses in
\phi
that contain the literal
x
.

A
quantified Boolean formula
(
QBF
)
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
consists of (1) a
prefix

\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}_{1}\mathcal{Z}_{1}\cdots\mathcal{Q}_{n}\mathcal{Z}_{n}
, in which all
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
are pairwise disjoint finite sets of Boolean variables,
\mathcal{Q}_{i}\in\{\exists,\forall\}
for each
i\in[n]
, and
\mathcal{Q}_{i}\neq\mathcal{Q}_{i+1}
for each
i\in[n-1]
, and (2) a
matrix

\phi
, which is a CNF formula with
\mathrm{Var}({\phi})\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in[n]}\mathcal{Z}_{i}
.
We define
\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}})=\bigcup_{i\in[n]}\mathcal{Z}_{i}
and
the set of
existential
(
universal
, respectively) variables of
\Phi
as
\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi})=\bigcup_{i\in[n],\mathcal{Q}_{i}=\exists}\mathcal{Z}_{i}
(as
\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi})=\bigcup_{i\in[n],\mathcal{Q}_{i}=\forall}\mathcal{Z}_{i}
, resp.), and let
\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})=\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi})\cup\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi})
.
For
V\subseteq\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})
, we write
\mathcal{Q}\setminus V
to denote the prefix obtained from
\mathcal{Q}
after removing all variables in
V
and their respective quantifiers.

An
assignment
to a set
\mathcal{Z}
of Boolean variables is a function
\delta:\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
. Given some
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}
we denote by
\delta{\upharpoonright}_{\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}
the restriction of
\delta
to
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
. The set of all assignments to
\mathcal{Z}
is denoted by
\langle\mathcal{Z}\rangle
.
Given a QBF
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
and an assignment
\delta\in\langle Z\rangle
for some
Z\subseteq\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})
, we denote by
\phi\rvert_{\delta}
the matrix that is obtained when
\delta
is applied to
\phi
.

The semantics of a QBF
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
are neatly described by a
two-player game
. Over the course of a game, the variables of
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
are assigned to 0 or 1 in the order they appear in the prefix, with the
existential player
(
universal player
, respectively) choosing the value for
\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi})
(
\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi})
, resp.).
When the game ends, the players have constructed a total assignment
\delta\in\langle\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})\rangle
. The existential player wins if and only if
\phi\rvert_{\delta}\equiv\top
. For every
x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi})
, let
L_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)
be the set of all universal variables that are quantified to the left of
x
in
\mathcal{Q}
.
An
existential strategy for
\Phi
is a set
\tau=\{\tau_{x}\mid x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi})\}
of functions
\tau_{x}:\langle L_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)\rangle\rightarrow\{0,1\}
that specify the assignment to be used for each existential variable
x
based on the assignment to all universal variables preceding
x
.
An existential strategy
\tau
is a
winning existential strategy
if for every universal assignment
\beta\in\langle\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi})\rangle
, we have
\phi\rvert_{\beta\cup\{x\mapsto\tau_{x}(\beta{\upharpoonright}_{L_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)})\mid x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi})\}}\equiv\top
.
A QBF is
true
if and only if it admits a winning existential strategy; otherwise we say the QBF is
false
. If a QBF contains no variables, it is considered to be true if its matrix is empty, and false if its matrix contains the empty clause. Two QBFs are
equisatisfiable
if and only if they are either both true or both false.

We remark that the notion of existential strategies defined above can also be lifted to account for the presence of dependency schemes; we elaborate on this in the next subsection.

Dependency Schemes and Posets for QBFs.
A
partially ordered set
(
poset
)
\mathcal{D}
is a pair
(V,\preceq_{\mathcal{D}})
where
V
is a set and
\preceq_{\mathcal{D}}
is a reflexive, antisymmetric, and transitive binary relation over
V
. A
linear extension
of a partially ordered set
\mathcal{D}=(V,\preceq_{\mathcal{D}})
is a relation
\leq_{O}
over
V
such that
x\leq_{O}y
whenever
x\preceq_{\mathcal{D}}y
and
(V,\leq_{O})
is a linear order. We use
dependency posets

[
22
,
23
]
to provide a general and formal way to speak about the various
dependency schemes
that have been devised for QBFs
[
43
,
36
,
47
,
48
]
.
Not all dependency schemes give rise to sound proof systems, and not all are easy to compute.
Consequently, we focus solely on the class of
polynomial-time computable permutation dependency schemes

[
48
]
, which can be efficiently deployed in resolution-style proof systems without compromising their soundness. Informally, a permutation dependency scheme assigns to each QBF
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
a binary relation
\mathcal{R}
on its variables such that permuting
\mathcal{Q}
with respect to any order that is consistent with
\mathcal{R}
yields an equisatisfiable QBF.
Based on these dependency schemes, we define the following notion.

Given a QBF
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
, a
(permutation) dependency poset
\mathcal{D}=(\mathrm{Var}({\Phi}),\preceq_{\mathcal{D}})
of
\Phi
is a partially ordered set over
\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})
with the following properties:

1.

for all
x,y\in\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})
, if
x\preceq_{\mathcal{D}}y
, then
x=y
or
x
is quantified to the left of
y
in
\mathcal{Q}
and

2.

for every linear extension
\leq_{O}
of
\mathcal{D}
,
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
is true if and only if
\mathcal{Q}_{\leq_{O}}.\phi
is true, where
\mathcal{Q}_{\leq_{O}}
is obtained from
\mathcal{Q}
by rearranging variables together with their respective quantifiers such that their order agrees with
\leq_{O}
.

Given a polynomial-time computable permutation dependency scheme, the respective dependency poset can be obtained in polynomial time by computing the transitive closure.
The
trivial dependency scheme
gives rise to the
trivial dependency poset
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{trv}}
of
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
, a special type of poset that for all
x,y\in\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})
, enforces
x\preceq_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{trv}}}y
, whenever
x=y
or
x
is quantified to the left of
y
in
\mathcal{Q}
. Other examples of polynomial-time computable permutation dependency schemes include
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tree}}
,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{res}}
,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rrs}}
, and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{std}}

[
37
,
48
]
.

Given a QBF
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
with dependency poset
\mathcal{D}
we define for each
v\in\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})
the set
\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v})=\{v^{\prime}\mid v^{\prime}\preceq_{\mathcal{D}}v\text{ and }v^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})\}
of variables that
v
depends on
. Due to the properties of
\mathcal{D}
, the definition of existential strategies for
\Phi
can be refined by replacing
L_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)
by
\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x})
.
By
(\beta,\tau)
, we abbreviate the assignment
\beta\cup\{\tau_{x}(\beta{\upharpoonright}_{\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x})})\mid x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi})\}
that is created when the existential strategy
\tau
is applied on a universal assignment
\beta
. Thus, for a winning existential strategy
\tau
, we have that
\phi\rvert_{(\beta,\tau)}\equiv\top
for all
\beta\in\langle\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi})\rangle
.

Decompositions, Elimination Orderings, and Cops-and-Robbers.
Let
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
be a QBF. The
primal graph

G_{\Phi}=(V,E)
of
\Phi

is the graph whose vertices are the variables of
\Phi
and where two variables are adjacent if and only if
\phi
has a clause containing both variables. When
\mathcal{Q}
is clear from context, we will sometimes use
G_{\phi}
as shorthand for
G_{\mathcal{Q}.\phi}
.

A
nice tree decomposition of
\Phi
is a pair
\mathcal{T}=(\mathbf{T},{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}})
, where
\mathbf{T}
is a tree (whose vertices are called

) rooted at a node
\mathrm{root}(\mathbf{T})
and
\textstyle\chi
is a function that assigns to each node

a set
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t)\subseteq\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})
, called the
bag of
t
, such that:

(T1)

For every
\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E(G_{\Phi})
, there is a node
t
such that
\{v,v^{\prime}\}\subseteq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t)
.

(T2)

For every
v\in\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})
, the set of nodes
t
satisfying
v\in{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t)
forms a subtree of
\mathbf{T}
.

(T3)

If
t
is a
leaf
of
\mathbf{T}
or

, then
|{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t)|=0
.

(T4)

There are only three kinds of non-leaf nodes in

:

•

introduce
: A node
t
with exactly one child

and
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t)={\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t^{\prime})\cup\{v\}
for a
v\notin{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t^{\prime})
.

•

forget
: A node
t
with exactly one child

and
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t)={\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t^{\prime})\setminus\{v\}
for a
v\in{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t^{\prime})
.

•

: A node

with two children

such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t)={\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t_{1})={\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t_{2})

For simplicity, we will refer to nice tree decompositions simply as

in the following.
The
width
of a tree decomposition is the size of its largest bag minus 1, and the

\Phi

is the minimum width over all tree decompositions of
\Phi
.

For each node
t
, we let
\mathbf{T}_{t}
be the subtree of
\mathbf{T}
that is rooted in

. Further, we let
\mathbf{T}_{\leq t}=\bigcup_{t^{\prime}\in\mathbf{T}_{t}}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t^{\prime})

and let
\mathbf{T}_{<t}=\mathbf{T}_{\leq t}\setminus{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t)

For each two distinct nodes

we write

if and only if
t^{\prime}
lies on the unique path from
t
to
\mathrm{root}(\mathbf{T})
. For each

, we let
\mathrm{forget}({v})
be the unique node
t
for which
v\in{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t)
, but
v\notin{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(t^{\prime})
for all nodes

In our later sections, we will make use of an alternative characterization of treewidth via elimination orderings. An
elimination ordering of
G_{\Phi}
is a linear order
\mathcal{V}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
of its vertices, where
n=|V(G_{\Phi})|
. Given
G_{\Phi}
and an elimination ordering
\mathcal{V}
of
G_{\Phi}
, the
fill-in graph
of
G_{\Phi}
with respect to
\mathcal{V}
is the unique minimal graph
G_{\Phi}^{\mathcal{V}}=(V,E)
such that

V(G_{\Phi}^{\mathcal{V}})=V(G_{\Phi})
,
E(G_{\Phi}^{\mathcal{V}})\supseteq E(G_{\Phi})
, and if
1\leq k<i<j\leq n
and

\{\{v_{k},v_{i}\},\{v_{k},v_{j}\}\}\subseteq E(G_{\Phi}^{\mathcal{V}})
, then
\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E(G_{\Phi}^{\mathcal{V}})
—that is, whenever we “eliminate” a vertex, we turn its remaining neighbors into a clique. The width of an elimination ordering
\mathcal{V}
is the maximum number of neighbors of that any vertex in
E(G_{\Phi}^{\mathcal{V}})
has to its right.
It is known that a graph has treewidth
k
if and only if it admits an elimination ordering of width
k

[
33
]
.

A
path decomposition of
\Phi
is a tree decomposition
\mathcal{P}=(\mathbf{P},{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}})
, where
\mathbf{P}
is a path, and the
pathwidth of
\Phi
is the minimum width over all path decompositions of
\Phi
. Since a path decomposition
\mathcal{P}
can be fully characterized by the order of appearance of its bags along
<_{\mathbf{P}}
, we can succinctly represent it as a sequence
\mathcal{P}=(P_{1},\dots,P_{d})
of bags.
When comparing to previous work in Section
4.2
, we will need a directed variant of path decompositions
[
5
]
.
A
directed path decomposition

\mathcal{P}=(P_{1},\dots,P_{d})
of a directed graph
D
is a sequence of subsets of
V(D)
such that (1)
\bigcup_{i\in[n]}P_{i}=V(D)
, (2) for each vertex
v\in V(D)
, the set
I_{v}=\{i\in[n]\mid v\in P_{i}\}
induces an interval, and (3) for each directed edge
(u,v)\in E(D)
, there are
i,j\in[n]
with
i\leq j
such that
u\in P_{i}
and
v\in P_{j}
. The
width
of
\mathcal{P}
and the
directed pathwidth of
D
, denoted by
\mathrm{dpw}({D})
, are then defined analogously as for path- and treewidth.

Directed pathwidth is closely related to
a directed version of a
cops-and-robber game

[
38
,
45
]
which will facilitate our lower-bound arguments in Lemma
8
—we refer to Barát
[
5
]
for a full description. Essentially, when given a directed graph
D
, the
robber
occupies one of its vertices
v
and can run at infinite speed to any other vertex that is reachable from
v
via a directed path on which no vertex is occupied by a cop. The
cops
also occupy one vertex each, and in each move the cops player can lift a cop and announce a new location for it, which can be any vertex of the graph. The game is played in rounds as follows: the cops lift and announce a move, the robber responds by moving, and the cops complete the move.
Crucially,
in this version the cops cannot see which vertex the robber is occupying. We say that the cops
capture
the robber if a cop moves to the vertex
v
that is currently occupied by the robber and every vertex that the robber can reach from
v
is also occupied by a cop. The
cop number of
D
, denoted by
\mathrm{cn}({D})
, is the minimum number of cops that suffice to guarantee a capture of the robber on
D
. It is known that
\mathrm{dpw}({D})\leq k-1
implies
\mathrm{cn}({D})\leq k

[
5
]
.

3
Derivation Rules: Q(
\mathcal{D}
)-Resolution and Strategy Extension

In this section, we introduce the derivation rules that will be used in Section
5
to prove fixed-parameter tractability of
QSat
with respect to bilateral treewidth. Our first two rules,
Resolution
and
Reduction
, are tightly linked to the proof system
\mathsf{Q}
-
\mathsf{Res}
that was introduced by Kleine Büning
[
32
]
and its dependency-aware variants
[
47
]
. Our adaptation of these rules, however, eliminates variables in an exhaustive way that is more akin to
multi-resolution proofs

[
15
,
39
,
2
]
. Crucially, in contrast to these prior works, we handle tautologies differently to allow for the sound application of an additional rule that we call
Strategy Extension
.

3.1
Eliminating Existential Variables via Resolution

Let
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
be a QBF that does not contain any tautological clauses. We define the following rule.

\phi

Resolution over

x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi}):\quad

\mathrm{Res}({\phi},{x})=\phi\setminus(\phi_{x}\cup\phi_{\overline{x}})\cup\mathrm{R}({\phi},{x})
,

where
\mathrm{R}({\phi},{x})=\{\mathcal{C}\mid\mathcal{C}\text{ is not tautological and there exist }\mathcal{C}_{1}\in\phi_{x},\mathcal{C}_{2}\in\phi_{\overline{x}}\text{ such that }\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{C}_{1}\setminus\{x\})\cup(\mathcal{C}_{2}\setminus\{\overline{x}\})\}
.
We say that we
resolve over
x
in
\phi
whenever we compute
\mathrm{Res}({\phi},{x})
and call
x
the
pivot variable
of this resolution step.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
be a QBF that contains no tautological clause, let
x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi})
, and
\mathcal{D}
be a dependency poset of
\Phi
. Suppose that for all
u\in\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi})
with
x\preceq_{\mathcal{D}}u
, there is no clause
\mathcal{C}\in\phi
such that
\{x,u\}\subseteq\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{C}})
. Then
\Phi
is true if and only if
\mathcal{Q}\setminus\{x\}.\mathrm{Res}({\phi},{x})
is true. Moreover, no clause in
\mathrm{Res}({\phi},{x})
is tautological or contains a literal of
x
.

Proof.

The second statement is readily observed since neither
\phi
nor
\mathrm{R}({\phi},{x})
contain any tautological clauses and no clause in
\mathrm{R}({\phi},{x})
contains any literal in
x
.

Towards the first statement, suppose that
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
is true, that is, there exists a winning existential strategy
\tau
for
\Phi
. We will prove that
\tau\setminus\{\tau_{x}\}
is a winning existential strategy for
\mathcal{Q}\setminus\{x\}.\mathrm{Res}({\phi},{x})
.
Assume that there exists an assignment
\beta\in\langle\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi})\rangle
such that some clause
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Res}({\phi},{x})
is falsified by
(\beta,\tau\setminus\{\tau_{x}\})
.
Clearly,
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{R}({\phi},{x})
, as otherwise
\mathcal{C}\in\phi
and
\tau
would not be winning. Thus, there exist
\mathcal{C}_{1}\in\phi_{x}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}\in\phi_{\overline{x}}
such that
\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{C}_{1}\setminus\{x\})\cup(\mathcal{C}_{2}\setminus\{\overline{x}\})
is falsified by
(\beta,\tau\setminus\{\tau_{x}\})
. Assume w.l.o.g. that
\tau_{x}(\beta{\upharpoonright}_{\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x})})=1
.
Then, since
\mathcal{C}_{2}\in\phi
is satisfied by
(\beta,\tau)
, we have that
\mathcal{C}_{2}\setminus\{\overline{x}\}
is satisfied by
(\beta,\tau\setminus\{\tau_{x}\})
. But then
\mathcal{C}
is also satisfied by
(\beta,\tau\setminus\{\tau_{x}\})
, which contradicts our assumption.

Suppose now
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
is false. Then for each partial existential strategy
\tau
on
X=(\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x}))\setminus\{x\}
, there exists an assignment
\beta_{\tau}\in\langle Y\rangle
with
Y=\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x})
, such that
\Phi^{\beta_{\tau}}=\mathcal{Q}\setminus(X\cup Y).\phi^{\beta_{\tau}}
with
\phi^{\beta_{\tau}}=\phi\rvert_{(\beta_{\tau},\tau)}
is false. Fix such a
\tau
arbitrarily and let
\mathrm{Res}({\phi},{x})^{\beta_{\tau}}:=\mathrm{Res}({\phi},{x})\rvert_{(\beta_{\tau},\tau)}
.
We will prove that
\mathcal{Q}\setminus(X\cup Y\cup\{x\}).\mathrm{Res}({\phi},{x})^{\beta_{\tau}}
is false, which entails that
\mathcal{Q}\setminus\{x\}.\mathrm{Res}({\phi},{x})
is false since
\tau
was chosen arbitrarily.
If for all existential strategies
\tau^{\prime}
on
\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi^{\beta_{\tau}}})
, there exists an assignment
\beta^{\prime}_{\tau^{\prime}}\in\langle\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi^{\beta_{\tau}}})\rangle
such that
(\beta^{\prime}_{\tau^{\prime}},\tau^{\prime})
falsifies a clause
\mathcal{C}\in\phi^{\beta_{\tau}}\setminus((\phi^{\beta_{\tau}})_{x}\cup(\phi^{\beta_{\tau}})_{\overline{x}})
, then this clause will also be present in the resolvent, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Res}({\phi},{x})^{\beta_{\tau}}
and hence
\mathcal{Q}\setminus(X\cup Y).\mathrm{Res}({\phi},{x})^{\beta_{\tau}}
is false.
Let us therefore focus on all existential strategies
\tau^{\prime}
for which all clauses in
\phi^{\beta_{\tau}}\setminus((\phi^{\beta_{\tau}})_{x}\cup(\phi^{\beta_{\tau}})_{\overline{x}})
are satisfied by
(\beta^{\prime}_{\tau^{\prime}},\tau^{\prime})
for all
\beta^{\prime}_{\tau^{\prime}}
. Since
\Phi^{\beta_{\tau}}
is false, we can find for each such
\tau^{\prime}
some
\beta^{\prime}_{\tau^{\prime}}
such that some clause in
(\phi^{\beta_{\tau}})_{x}\cup(\phi^{\beta_{\tau}})_{\overline{x}}
is falsified by
(\beta^{\prime}_{\tau^{\prime}},\tau^{\prime})
. Recall from the statement of the Lemma that no clause in
(\phi^{\beta_{\tau}})_{x}\cup(\phi^{\beta_{\tau}})_{\overline{x}}
contains any universal variable that depends on
x
. Hence there exists some
\beta^{\prime}_{\tau^{\prime}}
such that
(\beta^{\prime}_{\tau^{\prime}},\tau^{\prime})
falsifies
\mathcal{C}_{1}\setminus\{x\}
for some clause
\mathcal{C}_{1}\in(\phi^{\beta_{\tau}})_{x}
and also falsifies
\mathcal{C}_{2}\setminus\{\overline{x}\}
for some clause
\mathcal{C}_{2}\in(\phi^{\beta_{\tau}})_{\{\overline{x}\}}
, as
\beta^{\prime}_{\tau^{\prime}}
has to guarantee a falsified clause for each possible play on
x
.
But then, the clause
\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{C}_{1}\setminus\{x\}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2}\setminus\{\overline{x}\})\in\mathrm{Res}({\phi},{x})^{\beta_{\tau}}
is falsified by
(\beta^{\prime}_{\tau^{\prime}},\tau^{\prime}\setminus\{\tau_{x}\})
.

∎

3.2
Eliminating Universal Variables via Reduction

Let
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
be a QBF that does not contain any tautological clauses. We define the following rule.

\phi

Reduction of

u\in\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi}):\quad

\mathrm{Red}({\phi},{u})=\{\mathcal{C}\setminus\{u,\overline{u}\}\mid\mathcal{C}\in\phi\}
.

We say that we
reduce
u
from
\phi
whenever we compute
\mathrm{Red}({\phi},{u})
. Note that if
\phi
contains the clause
\{u\}
or
\{\overline{u}\}
, then
\mathrm{Red}({\phi},{u})
contains the empty clause, indicating a false instance.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
be a QBF that contains no tautological clause, let
u\in\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi})
, and
\mathcal{D}
be a dependency poset of
\Phi
. Suppose that for all
x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi})
with
u\preceq_{\mathcal{D}}x
, there is no clause
\mathcal{C}\in\phi
such that
\{x,u\}\subseteq\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{C}})
.
Then
\Phi
is true if and only if
\mathcal{Q}\setminus\{u\}.\mathrm{Red}({\phi},{u})
is true.
Moreover, no clause in
\mathrm{Red}({\phi},{u})
is tautological or contains a literal of
u
.

Proof.

The latter claim follows from the fact that
\phi
does not contain any tautological clauses and that both
u
and
\overline{u}
are exhaustively eliminated.

Towards the former claim, suppose that
\Phi
is true, that is, there exists a winning existential strategy
\tau
for
\Phi
. Let
\tau^{\prime}
be the existential strategy that copies
\tau
on all
x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi})
for which
u\not\in\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x})
and for all remaining
x
copies
\tau
on the partial assignment
u=0
. We claim that
\tau^{\prime}
is a winning existential strategy for
\mathcal{Q}\setminus\{u\}.\mathrm{Red}({\phi},{u})
.
Suppose not, that is, there exists a universal assignment
\beta\in\langle\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi})\setminus\{u\}\rangle
such that some clause
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Red}({\phi},{u})
is falsified by
(\beta,\tau^{\prime})
. If
\mathcal{C}\in\phi
, then
\mathcal{C}
is falsified by
(\beta\cup\{u=0\},\tau)
, which contradicts the fact that
\tau
is winning on
\Phi
.
Since by assumption,
(\mathcal{C}\cup\{u,\overline{u}\})\not\in\phi
, we have that
(\mathcal{C}\cup\{u\})\in\phi
or
(\mathcal{C}\cup\{\overline{u}\})\in\phi
. In the first case,
\phi
is falsified by
(\beta\cup\{u=0\},\tau)
. In the latter,
\phi
is falsified by
(\beta\cup\{u=1\},\tau)
, since by assumption,
\mathcal{C}
does not contain any
x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\Phi})
for which
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x})
and therefore,
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
agree on all variables in
\mathcal{C}
. Both cases contradict the fact that
\tau
is a winning existential strategy.

For the other direction, note that all clauses in
\mathrm{Red}({\phi},{u})
are subclauses of clauses in
\phi
. Therefore, every winning existential strategy
\tau^{\prime}
for
\mathcal{Q}\setminus\{u\}.\mathrm{Red}({\phi},{u})
can be extended to a winning existential strategy
\tau
for
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
that copies
\tau^{\prime}
on all existential variables, irrespective of the assignment to
u
.
∎

3.3
Eliminating Variables via Strategy Extension

Strategy Extension
is a rule based on a branching technique deployed in
[
23
]
. In contrast to resolution and reduction, this rule operates on multiple QBFs simultaneously. Intuitively, these QBFs correspond to every possible formula that a specific partial existential strategy could have produced when responding to every possible universal play thus far. We will show in Section
5
that the number of strategies and QBFs that need to be considered in parallel at each time step is bounded by a function of the bilateral treewidth.

Let
\pi=\{\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{\ell}\}
be a set of matrices that all share the same prefix
\mathcal{Q}
, that is,
\bigcup_{j\in[\ell]}\mathrm{Var}({\psi_{j}})\subseteq\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}})
and let no
\psi_{j}
contain a tautological clause.
For some
v\in\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}})
up to which we wish to perform strategy extension, we define
\mathcal{B}=\langle\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\mathcal{Q}})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v})\rangle
to be the set of all universal assignments to the set of variables that
v
depends on. Moreover, we let
\mathcal{A}
be the set of all (partial) existential strategies restricted to the variable set
\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\mathcal{Q}})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v})
, that is, each such strategy specifies a function
\tau_{x}:\mathcal{B}{\upharpoonright}_{\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x})}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
for all
x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\mathcal{Q}})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v})
.
We define the following rule.

\pi=\{\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{\ell}\}

Strategy Extension up to

v\in\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}}):\quad

\mathrm{Ext}({\pi},{v})=\{\pi_{(\tau^{1},\dots,\tau^{\ell})}\mid(\tau^{1},\dots,\tau^{\ell})\in\mathcal{A}^{\ell}\}
,

where
\pi_{(\tau^{1},\dots,\tau^{\ell})}=\bigcup_{j\in[\ell]}\pi_{\psi_{j}}^{\tau^{j}}
with
\pi^{\tau^{j}}_{\psi_{j}}=\{\psi_{j}\rvert_{(\beta,\tau^{j})}\mid\beta\in\mathcal{B}\}
. Recall from the previous section that
(\beta,\tau^{j})
abbreviates the assignment
\beta\cup\{\tau^{j}_{x}(\beta{\upharpoonright}_{\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x})})\mid x\in(\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\mathcal{Q}})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v}))\}
.
Essentially,
\mathrm{Ext}({\pi},{v})
enumerates all
\ell
-tuples of partial strategies—one tuple entry for each of the
\ell
initial QBFs—and for each such tuple, outputs the set of all QBFs that can arise from the initial QBF by having these strategies respond to universal plays. Note that while a single tuple from
\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
results in a set of QBF instances,
\mathrm{Ext}({\pi},{v})
is a set of such sets.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\pi=\{\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{\ell}\}
be a set of matrices that share a prefix
\mathcal{Q}
and contain no tautological clauses, let
v\in\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}})
and let
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}=\mathcal{Q}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v})
.
Then all QBFs in
\{\mathcal{Q}.\psi_{j}~|~j\in[\ell]\}
are true if and only if there
exists some
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Ext}({\pi},{v})
such that all QBFs in
\{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}.\psi^{\prime}~|~\psi^{\prime}\in\pi^{\prime}\}
are true.
Moreover, all matrices in all
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Ext}({\pi},{v})
neither contain a tautological clause nor a clause that contains a literal of any variable in
\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v})
.

Proof.

The latter claim follows from the fact that each clause which occurs in a matrix in some
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Ext}({\pi},{v})
is a subclause of a clause in some
\psi_{j}\in\pi
, which does not contain any tautological clauses by definition. Moreover, each variable in
\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v})
either gets assigned universally (if it is contained in
\mathcal{B}
) or is set by a partial strategy in
\mathcal{A}
.

Towards the former claim, let
\mathcal{B}
be defined as above.
Suppose all QBFs in
\{\mathcal{Q}.\psi_{j}~|~j\in[\ell]\}
are true, that is, for every
\mathcal{Q}.\psi_{j}
, there exists an existential strategy
\rho^{j}=\{\rho^{j}_{x}\mid x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\mathcal{Q}.\psi_{j}})\}
with
\rho^{j}_{x}:\langle\mathcal{B}{\upharpoonright}_{\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x})}\rangle\rightarrow\{0,1\}
, such that for all
\beta^{\prime}\in\langle\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\mathcal{Q}.\psi_{j}})\rangle
it holds that
\psi_{j}\rvert_{(\beta^{\prime},\rho^{j})}\equiv\top
. For each
j\in[\ell]
, let
\tau^{j}=\{\tau^{j}_{x}\mid\tau^{j}_{x}=\rho^{j}_{x},x\in(\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\mathcal{Q}.\psi_{j}})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v}))\}
be the restriction of
\rho^{j}
to existential variables that
v
depends on. Then for all
j\in[\ell]
and all
\beta\in\mathcal{B}
, the QBF
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}.\psi_{j}\rvert_{(\beta,\tau^{j})}
is true. Note that by definition,
\pi_{(\tau^{1},\dots,\tau^{\ell})}=\bigcup_{j\in[\ell]}\{\psi_{j}\rvert_{(\beta,\tau^{j})}\mid\beta\in\mathcal{B}\}
.
Consequently,
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}.\psi^{\prime}
is true for all
\psi^{\prime}\in\pi_{(\tau^{1},\dots,\tau^{\ell})}
.

For the reverse direction, suppose
\mathcal{Q}.\psi_{j}
is false for some
j\in[\ell]
, that is, for every existential strategy
\rho^{j}=\{\rho^{j}_{x}\mid x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\mathcal{Q}.\psi_{j}})\}
with
\rho^{j}_{x}:\langle\mathcal{B}{\upharpoonright}_{\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x})}\rangle\rightarrow\{0,1\}
, there is a
\beta_{\rho^{j}}\in\langle\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\mathcal{Q}.\psi_{j}})\rangle
with
\psi_{j}\rvert_{(\beta_{\rho^{j}},\rho^{j})}\equiv\bot
. For each
\tau\in\mathcal{A}
, find some
\rho^{j}
such that for its restriction
\tau^{j}
(defined as above), it holds that
\tau^{j}=\tau
. Then, for every
\tau\in\mathcal{A}
, there is a partial universal assignment (namely
\beta=\beta_{\rho^{j}}{\upharpoonright}_{\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v})}
) such that
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}.\psi_{j}\rvert_{(\beta,\tau)}
is false. Consequently, for each choice
\tau\in\mathcal{A}
, there is some
\psi^{\prime}\in\pi_{(\tau^{1},\dots,\tau^{j-1},\tau,\tau^{j+1},\dots,\tau^{\ell})}
for which
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}.\psi^{\prime}
is false.
∎

3.4
Relation of Strategy Extension to Universal Expansion

As noted, the first two rules are global variants of the resolution and reduction rule that are employed by various QBF proof systems, such as
\mathsf{Q}(\mathcal{D})\text{-}\mathsf{Res}
. Below, we show that strategy extension can likewise be linked to an existing rule, known as
universal expansion

[
30
]
. This rule is formally defined for a QBF
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
as follows.

\phi

Universal Expansion up to

v\in\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi}):\quad

\mathrm{Exp}({\phi},{v})=\bigwedge_{\beta\in\mathcal{B}}\phi\rvert_{\beta\cup\{x\mapsto x_{\beta{\upharpoonright}_{L_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)}}\mid x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\phi})\}}
,

where
\mathcal{B}
is defined as above and
x_{\beta{\upharpoonright}_{L_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)}}
is a new variable that is
annotated
with the universal assignment
\beta{\upharpoonright}_{L_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)}
. The relation between strategy extension and universal expansion can be understood through two key observations.

•

Both rules branch on all assignments in
\mathcal{B}
, but employ different formalizations:
strategy extension handles the formulas that arise from different universal assignments separately, whereas universal expansion maintains a single formula and uses annotated copies of variables to account for the universal branching. This difference is purely syntactic.

•

Strategy extension goes beyond universal expansion by explicitly assigning all existential variables up to
v
, whereas universal expansion just keeps their annotated counterparts.

Based on these observations, strategy extension up to a variable
v
can be simulated by first performing universal expansion up to
v
, and then instantiating all annotated existential variables up to
v
.
To simplify the exposition, we consider only the case where strategy extension is applied to a single matrix.

Observation A
.

Let
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
be a QBF,
v\in\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\Phi})
, and
\mathcal{E}=\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\mathcal{Q}})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v})
, and let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
be defined as above.
There is a
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Ext}({\{\phi\}},{v})
such that all QBFs in
\{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}.\psi^{\prime}~|~\psi^{\prime}\in\pi^{\prime}\}
are true if and only if

\mathcal{Q}^{\prime\prime}.\bigvee_{\tau^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}\mathrm{Exp}({\phi},{v})\rvert_{\tau^{\prime}}
is true, where for each
\tau\in\mathcal{A}
, the set
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
contains the constant partial existential strategy
\tau^{\prime}=\{\tau_{x_{\sigma}}^{\prime}\mid x_{\sigma}\in\mathrm{Var}({\mathrm{Exp}({\phi},{v})}),x\in\mathcal{E}\}
with
\tau_{x_{\sigma}}^{\prime}=\tau_{x}(\sigma{\upharpoonright}_{\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x})})
, that is,
\tau^{\prime}
sets each annotated variable
x_{\sigma}
to whichever value the strategy
\tau
chooses for
x
when given the partial universal assignment
\sigma{\upharpoonright}_{\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x})}
. Here,
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime\prime}
is obtained from
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
by replacing each
x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}})
with the set of variables
\{x_{\beta{\upharpoonright}_{L_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)}}\mid\beta\in\mathcal{B}\}
.

Proof.

Suppose, there exists a
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Ext}({\{\phi\}},{v})=\{\{\phi\rvert_{(\beta,\tau)}\mid\beta\in\mathcal{B}\}\mid\tau\in\mathcal{A}\}
such that all QBFs in
\{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}.\psi^{\prime}~|~\psi^{\prime}\in\pi^{\prime}\}
are true. In other words, there is a
\tau\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}.\phi\rvert_{(\beta,\tau)}=\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}.\phi\rvert_{\beta\cup\{\tau_{x}(\beta{\upharpoonright}_{\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({x})})\mid x\in\mathcal{E}\}}
is true for all
\beta\in\mathcal{B}
. We can express this property as a single formula by—for each formula that arises under a certain
\beta\in\mathcal{B}
—annotating all existential variables in this formula with
\beta
. We can now merge the individual formulas, since the sets of existential variables that they contain are all pairwise disjoint.
Formally, we conclude from the above that
\Phi^{\prime}:=\mathcal{Q}^{\prime\prime}.\bigvee_{\tau\in\mathcal{A}}\bigwedge_{\beta\in\mathcal{B}}\phi\rvert_{(\beta,\tau)\cup\{x\mapsto x_{\beta{\upharpoonright}_{L_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)}}\mid x\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}})\}}
is true. Observe that
\Phi^{\prime}
does not contain any variable in
\mathcal{E}
since they are assigned by each
\tau\in\mathcal{A}
. Hence, the same formula could have been produced by applying universal expansion up to
v
first and then assigning each annotated variable
x_{\sigma}
that was derived from an
x\in\mathcal{E}
according to
\tau^{\prime}
, that is, by treating its annotation as an input to the strategy function.
Formally, we have
\Phi^{\prime}\equiv\mathcal{Q}^{\prime\prime}.\bigvee_{\tau^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}\mathrm{Exp}({\phi},{v})\rvert_{\tau^{\prime}}
.
The reverse direction can be proved analogously.
∎

4
Tree Decompositions for QBFs

Recall that
QSat
is
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete even on instances of constant treewidth
[
2
]
.
In this section, we adjust the general notion of tree decompositions to enable fixed-parameter tractability of
QSat
via the careful application of the rules introduced in Section
3
.
We call the resulting parameter bilateral treewidth and demonstrate that it strictly dominates all previous measures of tree-likeness considered for QBFs.

4.1
Trunk-Aligned Tree Decompositions and Bilateral Treewidth

Let
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
be a QBF with dependency poset
\mathcal{D}
and let
\mathcal{T}=(\mathbf{T},{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}})
be a tree decomposition of
\Phi
.
For some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathrm{Tr}_{<_{\mathbf{T}}}=(t_{0},\dots,t_{\ell})
be a designated sequence of nodes
t_{0},\dots,t_{\ell}
such that
t_{0}
is a leaf,
t_{\ell}=\mathrm{root}(\mathbf{T})
, and
t_{i-1}
is the child of
t_{i}
for all
i\in[\ell]
, that is,
\mathrm{Tr}_{<_{\mathbf{T}}}
is a leaf-to-root path. We refer to
\mathrm{Tr}_{<_{\mathbf{T}}}
as the
trunk
of
\mathcal{T}
.
We call
\mathcal{T}
a
trunk-aligned tree decomposition
of
\Phi
with respect to
\mathcal{D}
if it satisfies the following property imposed on the order in which variables appear in bags:
For all
u\in\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})
, we have that

(P1)

for all
v\in\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})
with
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v})
, it is the case that
v\notin{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\mathrm{forget}({u}))
or

(P2)

\mathrm{forget}({u})\in\mathrm{Tr}_{<_{\mathbf{T}}}
and for all
v\in\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({u})
, it is the case that
v\in\mathbf{T}_{\leq\mathrm{forget}({u})}
.

Informally, Property
(P1)
requires all variables that depend on
u
to avoid the bag where
u
is forgotten—that is, they are forgotten inside
\mathbf{T}_{u}
or only occur outside of that subtree. Inside the trunk, Property
(P2)
allows for an alternative: all variables that
u
depends on are introduced “before”
u
is forgotten.
The
width
of a trunk-aligned tree decomposition is defined analogously as for standard tree decompositions.
The
bilateral treewidth
of
\Phi
with respect to
\mathcal{D}
, denoted by
\mathrm{btw}({\Phi,\mathcal{D}})
, is the minimal width over all trunk-aligned tree decompositions of
\Phi
with respect to
\mathcal{D}
.

In Section
5
, we will use trunk-aligned tree decompositions to construct proof-like derivation sequences.
Intuitively, whenever a variable is forgotten in a trunk-aligned tree decomposition, we will eliminate it from all QBFs that we currently maintain in our derivation sequence by using one of the rules from Section
3
. As we will prove in Section
5
, Property
(P1)
ensures that we invoke the reduction rule (the resolution rule, respectively) only on universal (existential, resp.) variables that are not blocked by a co-occurring existential (universal, resp.) variable that is quantified later. Similarly, Property
(P2)
guarantees that eliminating variables via strategy extension is tractable.
As a result, our approach not only solves
QSat
, but also produces concise proofs; previously, dependency treewidth was known to admit such proof extractions, whereas prefix pathwidth was not.
Before proceeding to Section
5
, we briefly discuss these measures and their relationship to bilateral treewidth.

4.2
Comparison of Decompositional Parameters for QSAT

For the following considerations, it will be useful to recall Figure
1
. Our aim here is not only to provide an overview of previously introduced measures for the tree-likeness of QBFs, but also to establish that bilateral treewidth generalizes all of them.

Dependency Tree Decompositions.
Dependency tree decompositions were introduced in
[
22
]
and are defined as follows:
Let
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
be a QBF with dependency poset
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{T}=(\mathbf{T},{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}})
be a tree decomposition of
\Phi
.
We call
\mathcal{T}
a
dependency tree decomposition
of
(\Phi,\mathcal{D})
if it satisfies the following property: For all
u,v\in\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})
with
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v})
, it is
not
the case that
\mathrm{forget}({u})<_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{forget}({v})
. Note that this condition resembles
(P1)
. However, the condition imposed on dependency tree decompositions is more restrictive since
v\in{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\mathrm{forget}({u}))
implies that
\mathrm{forget}({u})<_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{forget}({v})
.
Therefore, we conclude the following.

Corollary 4
.

Every dependency tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(\mathbf{T},{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}})
is a trunk-aligned tree decomposition where
\mathrm{Tr}_{<_{\mathbf{T}}}
is an arbitrary leaf-to-root-path in
\mathcal{T}
.

The
dependency treewidth
of
\Phi
with respect to a dependency poset
\mathcal{D}
, denoted by
\mathrm{dtw}({\Phi,\mathcal{D}})
, is the minimal width of all dependency tree decompositions of
(\Phi,\mathcal{D})
.

Dependency treewidth can be characterized in terms of elimination orderings. More precisely, we define the
\mathcal{D}
-elimination ordering
of
G_{\Phi}
to be an elimination ordering of
G_{\Phi}
that is a linear extension of the reverse of
\mathcal{D}
. It is known that
\mathrm{dtw}({\Phi,\mathcal{D}})
is equal to the minimal width among all
\mathcal{D}
-elimination orderings of
G_{\Phi}

[
22
]
.

Respectful Tree Decompositions.
Chen and Dalmau
[
16
]
introduced a concept that is equivalent to respectful tree decompositions, but is phrased in terms of elimination orderings.
Atserias and Oliva
[
2
]
re-stated it in terms of tree decompositions and coined the name. Having introduced the notion of dependency tree decompositions first—although respectful tree decompositions appeared earlier in the literature—we can now neatly define the latter as a special case of the former: A
respectful tree decomposition
is a dependency tree decomposition with respect to the trivial dependency poset
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{trv}}
. The
respectful treewidth
of some
\Phi
is the minimal width of all respectful tree decompositions of
\Phi
.

Prefix Path Decompositions.
Prefix path decompositions
[
23
]
are conceptually different from the previous two measures.
Let
\Phi
be a QBF with dependency poset
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{P}=(\mathbf{P},{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}})
be a path decomposition of
\Phi
.
We call
\mathcal{P}
a
prefix path decomposition
of
(\Phi,\mathcal{D})
if for all
u\in\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})
and all
v\in\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({u})
, we have
v\in\mathbf{P}_{\leq\mathrm{forget}({u})}
.
The resemblance to
(P2)
is evident. Consequently, we obtain:

Corollary 5
.

Every prefix path decomposition
\mathcal{P}
with
\mathcal{P}=(P_{1},\dots,P_{d})
is a trunk-aligned tree decomposition with
\mathrm{Tr}_{<_{\mathbf{T}}}=(P_{1},\dots,P_{d})
.

The
prefix pathwidth
of
\Phi
with respect to dependency poset
\mathcal{D}
, denoted by
\mathrm{ppw}({\Phi,\mathcal{D}})
, is the minimal width of all prefix path decompositions of
(\Phi,\mathcal{D})
.
Eiben, Ganian, and Ordyniak
[
23
]
provided a characterization of prefix pathwidth in terms of the directed pathwidth of a graph

D_{\Phi}^{\mathcal{D}}
which can be obtained from
G_{\Phi}
as follows. We replace every edge
\{u,v\}
with the two directed edges
(u,v)
and
(v,u)
, and add the directed edge
(u,v)
for each two vertices
u,v\in V(G_{\Phi})
with
u\preceq_{\mathcal{D}}v
. Then,
\mathrm{ppw}({\Phi,\mathcal{D}})=\mathrm{dpw}({\Phi,\mathcal{D}})
, where
\mathrm{dpw}({\Phi,\mathcal{D}})
is the directed pathwidth of
D^{\mathcal{D}}_{\Phi}
as introduced at the end of Section
2
.

The Bilateral Treewidth of QParity is Small.
While Corollaries
4
and
5
suffice to show that bilateral treewidth subsumes dependency treewidth and prefix pathwidth, it is not yet clear whether it is genuinely stronger, that is, whether it captures tractable instances which the previous parameters did not. We answer this question in the positive.

For each

Lemma 7
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
, we have
\mathrm{dtw}({\emph{\textsc{QParity}_{n}},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{trv}}})\geq n+1
.

Proof.

Note that for each
i\in[n]
, we have
x_{i}\preceq_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{trv}}}u
and
u\preceq_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{trv}}}z_{i}
.
Since a
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{trv}}
-elimination ordering must be a linear extension of the reverse of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{trv}}
, we know every such ordering
\mathcal{V}
must consist of a sequence of the variables in
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
, followed by
u

\mathcal{X}_{n}
. Since
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
is connected and its neighborhood in

\mathcal{X}_{n}\cup\{u\}

\mathcal{X}_{n}\cup\{u\}

\mathcal{Z}_{n}
, all of its current neighbors are transferred to its not-yet-eliminated neighbors in
\mathcal{Z}_{n}

In particular, the width of
\mathcal{V}
is at least

\mathrm{dtw}({\textsc{QParity}_{n},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{trv}}})\geq n+1
.

Lemma 8
.

For each

\mathrm{ppw}({\emph{\textsc{QParity}_{n}},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{trv}}})\geq n
.

Proof.

Due the characterization of prefix pathwidth via directed pathwidth, it suffices to show
\mathrm{dpw}({\textsc{QParity}_{n},\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{trv}}})\geq n

(T1)
to

\mathcal{X}_{n}

and

5
Using Trunk-Aligned Tree Decompositions to Solve QBF

In this section, we show how the decompositions underlying bilateral treewidth yield fixed-parameter tractability for
QSat
. More precisely, we establish:

Theorem 10
.

There is a computable function
f
and an algorithm that given

1.

a QBF
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
,

2.

a dependency poset
\mathcal{D}
, and

3.

a trunk-aligned tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(\mathbf{T},{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}})
of
(\Phi,\mathcal{D})
with width
\mathrm{btw}({\Phi,\mathcal{D}})

decides whether
\Phi
is true in time
f(\mathrm{btw}({\Phi,\mathcal{D}}))\cdot|\Phi|\cdot|\mathcal{T}|
.

Below, we establish several smaller lemmas that will set up the proof of Theorem
10
.
Broadly speaking, we will follow the following proof strategy. Based on a given trunk-aligned tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}
, we define a proof-like structure that maintains sets of sets of QBFs and that we will refer to as the
derivation sequence

\Pi
. We then prove that
\Pi
satisfies specific properties, namely: the variable-co-occurrences in QBFs within
\Pi
can be tightly linked to the content of bags in
\mathcal{T}
(Lemma
11
) and
\Pi
preserves the truth values of the QBFs it maintains (Lemma
12
), while gradually eliminating all variables from
them (Observation
B
).
Moreover, the number of QBFs that is maintained in parallel at each time step is bounded by a function in the bilateral treewidth of
\Phi
(Lemma
14
) from which we will infer that
\Pi
can be efficiently computed (Lemma
17
). The proof of Theorem
10
then concludes this section.
In order to apply Lemmas
1
,
2
and
3
,
we will assume that the given QBF
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
does not contain any tautological clauses; this is without loss of generality as such clauses can be removed in a pre-processing step without altering the truth value of
\Phi
.

As a first step towards obtaining a derivation sequence, our proposed algorithm takes the partial order
<_{\mathbf{T}}
imposed by the trunk-aligned tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}
and extends it to a full linear order
<_{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}}
, that is, for each two distinct
t,t^{\prime}\in V(\mathbf{T})
we have
t<_{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}}t^{\prime}
or
t^{\prime}<_{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}}t
, and if
t<_{\mathbf{T}}t^{\prime}
, then also
t<_{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}}t^{\prime}
. Now, let
\mathcal{V}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
be the sequence of variables in
\mathrm{Var}({\Phi})
such that
\mathrm{forget}({v_{1}})<_{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}}\mathrm{forget}({v_{2}})<_{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}}\cdots<_{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}}\mathrm{forget}({v_{n}})
. We call
\mathcal{V}
an
elimination ordering of
\mathrm{Var}({\phi})
that agrees with
\mathcal{T}
.

Based on the elimination ordering
\mathcal{V}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
, our algorithm constructs a
derivation sequence

\Pi=\Pi_{0},\Pi_{1},\dots,\Pi_{n}
of
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
, where each
\Pi_{i}=(\mathcal{Q}^{(i)},\mathcal{F}^{(i)})
is a tuple that consists of a prefix
\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}
and a set
\mathcal{F}^{(i)}
of sets of matrices as follows. We initialize
\mathcal{Q}^{(0)}=\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(0)}=\{\{\phi\}\}
. For each
i\in[n]
, we compute
\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(i)}
according to the following rules:

(R1)

If
v_{i}\notin\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})
, then set
\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}=\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(i)}=\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}
.

(R2)

If
v_{i}\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})
and
\{v\in\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})\mid v_{i}\preceq_{\mathcal{D}}v\}\cap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}}))=\emptyset
, then for each set
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}
of matrices, we construct
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{(i)}
such that
\pi^{\prime}
contains the matrix
\mathrm{Res}({\psi},{v_{i}})
if and only if
\pi
contains the matrix
\psi
. Moreover, we set
\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}=\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}\setminus\{v_{i}\}
.

(R3)

If
v_{i}\in\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})
and
\{v\in\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})\mid v_{i}\preceq_{\mathcal{D}}v\}\cap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}}))=\emptyset
, then for each set
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}
of matrices, we construct
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{(i)}
such that
\pi^{\prime}
contains the matrix
\mathrm{Red}({\psi},{v_{i}})
if and only if
\pi
contains the matrix
\psi
. We set
\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}=\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}\setminus\{v_{i}\}
.

(R4)

Otherwise, we let
\mathcal{F}^{(i)}=\bigcup_{\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}}\mathrm{Ext}({\pi},{v_{i}})
and
\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}=\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v_{i}})
.

Intuitively, we alter sets of matrices by gradually eliminating all variables in the order specified by
\mathcal{V}
. Variables are eliminated either by applying the resolution rule to each QBF (Rule
(R2)
), by applying the reduction rule to each QBF (Rule
(R3)
), or by performing strategy extension on each current set of matrices (Rule
(R4)
). Rule
(R1)
merely deals with a case that may arise from the earlier application of strategy extension.

To prove the correctness of the Rules
(R2)
and
(R3)

v_{i}
is eliminated by this rule, all variables that it is neighbored to in any matrix
\psi\in\pi
with
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}
are contained in
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}}))
. We prove this statement in the following.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
be a QBF,
\mathcal{T}=(\mathbf{T},{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}})
be a trunk-aligned tree decomposition of
(\Phi,\mathcal{D})
with width

and
\mathcal{V}
be an elimination ordering that agrees with
\mathcal{T}

\Pi=\Pi_{0},\Pi_{1},\dots,\Pi_{n}
be a derivation sequence of
\Phi

Then for each
i\in[n]
, we have that
N_{G_{\psi}}(v_{i})\subseteq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}}))
for every matrix
\psi\in\pi
in every set
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}
, where
N_{G_{\psi}}(v_{i})
is the open neighborhood of
v_{i}
in the primal graph of
\psi
.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by induction on
i
.
For
i=1
, we have
\mathcal{F}^{(0)}=\{\{\phi\}\}
. From the definitions of
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{V}
and the fact that
v_{1}
is eliminated first in

(R4)
, then there is some
\tilde{\psi}
in some
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(j-1)}
and some
\delta\in\langle\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}^{(j-1)}\cap\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v_{i}})})\rangle
such that for all
C\in\mathfrak{C}
with
C\notin\tilde{\psi}
, there is some
C^{(C)}\in\tilde{\psi}
for which
C=C^{(C)}\rvert_{\delta}
. Extend
D
to the left by all clauses in
E=\{C^{(C)}\mid C\in\mathfrak{C},C\notin\tilde{\psi}\}
, set
M=\tilde{\psi}
and update
\mathfrak{C}=(\mathfrak{C}\setminus\{C\mid C\notin\tilde{\psi}\})\cup E
. Finally, we reduce
j
by one.

When
j=0
, extend
D
to the left by all remaining clauses in
\mathfrak{C}
.

Note that a certifying sequence
D
is minimal in the sense that each clause
C_{j}
with
j\in[t-1]
is used to derive a subsequent clause in
D
, either by resolution, reduction, or partial assignment.
We now prove that certifying sequences obey a certain separation property that we will exploit in the proof of Claim
16
.

Claim 15
.

Let
i\in[n]
,
p
be the parent of
\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}})
, and
\mathcal{C}
be a clause that occurs in some matrix
\psi\in\pi
for some
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i)}
.
Let
D=(C_{1},\dots,C_{t})
be a certifying sequence of
\mathcal{C}
that was produced by the partial derivation sequence
\Pi_{0},\Pi_{1},\dots,\Pi_{i}
.
If
\mathcal{C}
contains a variable in
S=\mathrm{Var}({\phi})\setminus\mathbf{T}_{\leq p}
, then
\bigcup_{j\in[t]}\mathrm{Var}({C_{j}})\subseteq\mathrm{Var}({\phi})\setminus\mathbf{T}_{<p}
, that is, no clause in
D
contains a variable from
\mathbf{T}_{<p}
.

{claimproof}

We prove this claim by carefully analyzing the properties of
D
.

Observation 1

No clause in
D
contains only variables in
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(p)
.
Suppose not and let
C^{\prime}
be the last clause in
D
that contains only variables in
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(p)
. Since
C_{t}=\mathcal{C}
contains a variable from
S
,
C^{\prime}
is to the left of
C_{t}
in
D
, and since
D
is minimal,
C^{\prime}
has been used to eventually obtain
C_{t}
. However, note that no clause that occurs behind
C^{\prime}
in
D
has been derived from
C^{\prime}
by performing resolution or reduction, since
C^{\prime}
contains only variables in
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(p)
, that is, variables that have not yet been forgotten. On the other hand, no clause that occurs behind
C^{\prime}
in
D
has been derived from
C^{\prime}
by applying a partial assignment to
C^{\prime}
since this clause would be either empty (and therefore not useful to derive
C_{t}
, contradicting the minimality of
D
) or contain only variables in
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(p)
(contradicting the fact that
C^{\prime}
is the last clause in
D
with this property).

Observation 2

No clause in
D
contains a variable in
\mathbf{T}_{<p}
and a variable in
S
.
We show the statement via induction. Clearly, all original clauses in
\phi
satisfy the property, since
\mathcal{T}
is a trunk-aligned tree decomposition of
G_{\Phi}
, which means that the node
p
acts as a separator in
\phi
. Let us now assume that there was a clause in
D
that contained variables of both sets,
\mathbf{T}_{<p}
and
S
, and let
C^{\prime}
be the first such clause in
D
. Since neither a reduction nor an assignment could have introduced this new neighborhood, there must have been two clauses,
C_{\ell}
and
C_{m}
, left of
C^{\prime}
in
D
whose resolvent is
C^{\prime}
and, without loss of generality,
C_{\ell}
contained a variable in
\mathbf{T}_{<p}
and
C_{m}
contained a variable in
S
. The variable that we resolved over must have been in
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(p)
as otherwise,
C^{\prime}
would not have been the first clause in
D
to contain a variable from both sets,
\mathbf{T}_{<p}
and
S
. But this is contradicting the fact that no variable in
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(p)
has been forgotten yet.

Equipped with these observations, we now proceed to prove that no clause in
D
contains a variable from
\mathbf{T}_{<p}
. Suppose not and let
C^{\prime}
be the right-most clause in
D
that contains a variable
v
from
\mathbf{T}_{<p}
. Note that
C^{\prime}\neq C_{t}
since, by definition,
C_{t}
contains a variable in
S
and Observation 2 holds. Hence, by the minimality of
D
,
C^{\prime}
is used to derive
C_{t}
. If
v
was dropped from
C^{\prime}
via reduction or by assigning it, we would have obtained a clause that is either empty or contains only variables in
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(p)
(since
C^{\prime}
was assumed to be the last clause in
D
that contains a variable in
\mathbf{T}_{<p}
). The first case contradicts the minimality of
D
, the latter case contradicts Observation 1. Thus,
C^{\prime}
was used in a resolution step and suppose for a moment that the respective pivot variable was not
v
. Then, since
C^{\prime}
is the last clause in
D
that contains
v
, the resolvent must have been tautological and thus got dropped, which contradicts the minimality of
D
. Hence, the pivot variable was
v
, implying that the clause
C^{\prime\prime}
we resolved
C^{\prime}
with must have contained
v
in opposite polarity. Note that their respective resolvent does not contain any variable in
S
, since, by Observation 2, neither did
C^{\prime}
nor
C^{\prime\prime}
.
Moreover, the resolvent does not contain any variable from
\mathbf{T}_{<p}
since we assumed
C^{\prime}
to be the right-most clause for which this is the case. Consequently, the resolvent of
C^{\prime}
and
C^{\prime\prime}
contains only variables in
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(p)
, contradicting Observation 1.

As a consequence of Claim
15
, the existence of a clause
\mathcal{C}
that contains some variable in
S
does not depend on the assignment that was chosen for the variables in
V\cap\mathbf{T}_{<p}
, since none of these variables were used to derive
\mathcal{C}
.
Equipped with this insight, we now proceed to prove the aforementioned sufficient condition for the equivalence of
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
.

Claim 16
.

If
U_{\psi}=U_{\psi^{\prime}}
and
\alpha_{1}^{\prime}=\alpha_{2}^{\prime}
, then
\psi\equiv\psi^{\prime}
.

{claimproof}

We need to show that under the stated assumptions,
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
consist of the same set of clauses. Observe that all clauses in
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
contain only variables in
\mathrm{Var}({\phi})\setminus(\mathbf{T}_{<p}\cup V)
; in particular, each clause either only contains variables in
\mathrm{Var}({U})
or it contains at least one variable from
S=\mathrm{Var}({\phi})\setminus\mathbf{T}_{\leq p}
; on the former,
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
agree, since
U_{\psi}=U_{\psi^{\prime}}
. Without loss of generality, let us now consider an arbitrary clause
\mathcal{C}
that occurs in
\psi
and that contains a variable from
S
.
By Claim
15
, no variable in
\mathbf{T}_{<p}
was used to derive
\mathcal{C}
; hence, every partial assignment that was applied to derive
\mathcal{C}
was only with respect to variables in
V\setminus\mathbf{T}_{<p}=V\cap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(p)
. Since
\alpha_{1}^{\prime}=\alpha_{2}^{\prime}
by assumption, and reduction and resolution steps are applied uniformly across all matrices at each time step
i
, we conclude that the same certifying sequence must have produced
\mathcal{C}
in
\psi^{\prime}
. Consequently,
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
also agree on clauses that contain a variable from
S
. Thus,
\psi\equiv\psi^{\prime}
which concludes the proof.

We conclude the proof of Lemma
14
by using Claim
16
to bound the number of matrices in each
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i)}
.
Since there are at most
2^{|U|}\leq 2^{3^{k+1}}
distinct choices for
U_{\psi}
and at most
2^{|V\cap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(p)|}\leq 2^{k+1}
distinct choices for an assignment
\alpha^{\prime}\in\langle V\cap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(p)\rangle
, we conclude from Claim
16
that
|\pi|\leq 2^{3^{k+1}}\cdot 2^{k+1}\in 2^{2^{\mathcal{O}({k})}}
for any
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i)}
. Consequently, there are at most
2^{2^{2^{\mathcal{O}({k})}}}
possible choices for
\pi
, that is,
|\mathcal{F}^{(i)}|\leq 2^{2^{2^{\mathcal{O}({k})}}}
.
∎

We are now ready to show that the Rules
(R1)
to
(R4)
can be applied efficiently.

Lemma 17
.

There is a computable function
f
with the following property. Given a partial derivation sequence
\Pi^{\prime}=\Pi_{0},\dots,\Pi_{i-1}
of
\Phi=\mathcal{Q}.\phi
and a trunk-aligned tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(\mathbf{T},{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}})
of
(\Phi,\mathcal{D})
with width
k=\mathrm{btw}({\Phi,\mathcal{D}})
, we can derive
\Pi_{i}
in time
|\mathcal{T}|+f(k)\cdot|\Phi|
.

Proof.

We perform a case analysis based on the type of
v_{i}\in\mathcal{V}
that is forgotten to obtain
\Pi_{i}
. Note that the relevant case can be identified in time
|\mathcal{T}|
.

Case 1

If
v_{i}\notin\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})
, then Rule
(R1)
sets
\Pi_{i}=\Pi_{i-1}
in constant time.

Case 2

If
v_{i}\in\mathrm{Var}_{\exists}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})
with
\{v\in\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})\mid v_{i}\preceq_{\mathcal{D}}v\}\cap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}}))=\emptyset
, then we apply Rule
(R2)
, that is, we resolve over
v_{i}
in all matrices
\psi
that are contained in some
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}
.
By Lemma
11
,
v_{i}
shares a clause with at most
k
other variables in each such
\psi
. Since these variables can occur either positively, negatively, or not at all, both
\psi_{v_{i}}
and
\psi_{\overline{v_{i}}}
contain at most
3^{k}
clauses and each of these clauses is of size at most
k+1
. Consequently, the resolvent
\mathrm{R}({\psi},{v_{i}})
replaces the clauses in
\psi_{v_{i}}\cup\psi_{\overline{v_{i}}}
by at most
3^{k}
clauses
(the set of all clauses that the
k
neighbors of
v_{i}
can possibly form), and computing
\mathrm{R}({\psi},{v_{i}})
takes time
\mathcal{O}({{(3^{k})}^{2}k})
. Moreover, by Lemma
14
, we can afford to compute
\mathrm{R}({\psi},{v_{i}})
for each matrix
\psi
that is contained in some
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}
.

Case 3

If
v_{i}\in\mathrm{Var}_{\forall}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})
with
\{v\in\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})\mid v_{i}\preceq_{\mathcal{D}}v\}\cap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}}))=\emptyset
, then, applying Rule
(R3)
, we reduce
v_{i}
from all matrices
\psi
that are contained in some
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}
.
This may be done by a linear pass over all matrices in all sets of
\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}
. At the same time, we remark that by Lemma
11
,
v_{i}
shares a clause with at most
k
other variables in each such
\psi
. Hence, in each
\psi
, the variable
v_{i}
will only be reduced from at most
2\cdot 3^{k}
clauses. Thus, computing
\mathrm{Red}({\psi},{v_{i}})
can be done in time
\mathcal{O}({3^{k}})
under the assumption that variable-clause adjacencies can be looked up in constant time, and by Lemma
14
we can afford to do so for each matrix
\psi
that is contained in some
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}
.

Case 4

If
v_{i}\in\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})
with
S\neq\emptyset
for
S=\{v\in\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})\mid v_{i}\preceq_{\mathcal{D}}v\}\cap{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}}))
, then we apply Rule
(R4)
. Since

is not empty, there is at least one

with
v_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v})
such that
v\in{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}}))
, that is,
v_{i}
violates Property
(P1)
. Hence, by Property
(P2)
,
\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}})\in\mathrm{Tr}_{<_{\mathbf{T}}}
and for all
v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v_{i}})
, we have
v^{\prime}\in\mathbf{T}_{\leq\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}})}
.
Observe that each
v^{\prime}\in(\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v_{i}}))
has not yet been forgotten, that is,
v^{\prime}\in{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}}))
. From the definition of strategy extension in Section
3
, it follows that computing
\mathrm{Ext}({\pi},{v_{i}})
for a single
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}
takes time
(|\mathcal{A}|)^{|\pi|}\cdot|\mathcal{B}|\cdot{|\Phi|}
. Since
|\mathrm{Var}({\mathcal{Q}^{(i-1)}})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\preceq}({v_{i}})|\leq|{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}}))|\leq k+1
, we have

and
|\mathcal{A}|\leq 2^{2^{\mathcal{O}({k})}}
by the definition of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
in strategy extension. Finally, recall that Lemma

bounds
|\pi|\leq 2^{2^{\mathcal{O}({k})}}
and
|\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}|\leq 2^{2^{2^{\mathcal{O}({k})}}}
. Consequently, we can compute
\mathrm{Ext}({\pi},{v_{i}})
in time
2^{2^{2^{\mathcal{O}({k})}}}\cdot|\Phi|
and can afford to do so for each
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(i-1)}
.
∎

With this final piece in hand, we are now ready to establish Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
10
.

First, we compute an elimination ordering
\mathcal{V}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
that agrees with
\mathcal{T}
and store
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\mathrm{forget}({v_{i}}))
for each
i\in[n]
. This can be done in time
\mathcal{O}({|\mathcal{T}|})
. Next, we initialize
\Pi_{0}=(\mathcal{Q}^{(0)},\mathcal{F}^{(0)})
with
\mathcal{Q}^{(0)}=\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(0)}=\{\{\phi\}\}
. Now, for each
i\in[n]
, we compute
\Pi_{i}
by using one of the Rules
(R1)
to
(R4)
depending on the variable type of
v_{i}\in\mathcal{V}
. By Lemma
17
, this sequence of sets up to
\Pi_{n}
can be computed in time
f(k)\cdot|\Phi|\cdot|\mathcal{T}|
.
It follows from Lemma
12
that
\Phi
is true if and only if there exists a set
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(n)}
of matrices such that each matrix
\psi\in\pi
is true.
Moreover, by Observation
B
, each
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(n)}
consists only of matrices
\psi
that contain the empty clause
\bot
or are themselves empty.
By Lemma
14
, the number of QBFs and sets of QBFs in

is bounded by a function of

there exists a set
\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{(n)}
such that no matrix

If so, we return TRUE, otherwise we return FALSE.
∎

6
Concluding Remarks

Our work closes the previous rift between prefix pathwidth and dependency treewidth, two incomparable measures for the tree-likeness of QBFs. Bilateral treewidth provides not only a unified explanation for why prefix pathwidth and dependency treewidth enable fixed-parameter QBF evaluation, but genuinely extends them by covering instances that are beyond the reach of any of the previously developed width parameters for QBF. Moreover, our evaluation algorithm produces fixed-parameter sized proofs as a witness and thus preserves a key advantage of dependency treewidth. In future work, it would be interesting to investigate whether the asymptotic running time of the presented QBF evaluation algorithm can be improved, or whether it is tight under the Exponential Time Hypothesis.

From the viewpoint of proof complexity, it may be of interest to formally analyze these proofs and, if possible, link them to existing proof systems.
From an empirical perspective, it may be interesting to see whether any of the insights employed in our proofs can be adapted to speed up the solution of instances which are expected to be “tree-like”. We remark that—similarly as for the preceding work on prefix pathwidth
[
23
]
—the theoretical upper bound in the function
f
is triple-exponential in the bilateral treewidth (cf. Lemma
14
), and hence is not of practical significance.

Moreover, a major question left for future work is whether the decompositions underlying bilateral treewidth can be computed in fixed-parameter time. None of the known techniques for computing or approximating treewidth
[
1
,
41
,
14
,
13
,
34
]
seem directly applicable to bilateral treewidth, and in fact the question remains open even for the previously studied notions of prefix pathwidth and dependency treewidth. Nevertheless, as illustrated in the proof of Theorem
6
, there are natural classes of QBFs which remained out-of-reach for previous width measures but where constant-width decompositions can be computed easily. A natural stepping stone towards resolving this question would be to first obtain an
\mathsf{XP}
algorithm for computing trunk-aligned tree decompositions of minimum width.
