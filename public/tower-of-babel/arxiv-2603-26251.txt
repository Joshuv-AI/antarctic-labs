Effect of edge-stretching on Steklov eigenvalues and sharp Steklov eigenvalue bounds on leaf–boundary trees
Jiangdong Ai Yizhe Ji Xiaopan Lian Kun Yang

Abstract
Let T T be a finite tree with leaf set δ ​ Ω \delta\Omega as the boundary and let λ 2 \lambda_{2} be the first nontrivial Steklov eigenvalue. Let D D and ℓ \ell be the maximum vertex degree and the number of leaves, respectively. Motivated by the spectral influence of neck-stretching on Riemannian manifolds, we investigate a discrete counterpart–edge-stretching–and its effect on the Steklov eigenvalues of graphs. We prove that Steklov eigenvalues decrease monotonically under the edge–stretching operation. As a consequence, we prove that λ 2 ≤ D / ℓ \lambda_{2}\leq D/\ell , with equality if and only if T T is a star. This fundamentally improves the constant in He–Hua’s bound λ 2 ≤ 4 ​ ( D − 1 ) / ℓ \lambda_{2}\leq 4(D-1)/\ell to the optimal value 1 1 . We also provide a closed-form diagonalization of the Steklov problem on level–regular trees, yielding explicit eigenvalues and multiplicities. In addition, we provide a general upper bound λ k ≤ min ⁡ { 1 , 16 ​ D ​ k / ℓ } \lambda_{k}\leq\min\{1,\,16Dk/\ell\} for higher eigenvalues. Systematic numerical experiments verify the sharp bound and provide evidence for the extremal conjecture of Lin–Zhao on balanced minimum–height trees.

The bound

Such inequalities involve a squaring step: one bounds

A key novelty of our approach is that it bypasses the Cheeger inequality entirely, thereby avoiding the multiplicative constants inherent in Cheeger-type estimates. Instead, we work directly with the Rayleigh quotient at a leaf centroid. By constructing an explicit two-value test function whose energy can be optimized in closed form (Lemma

The key observation is that on a tree, removing a single vertex splits the boundary into components whose sizes are controlled by the centroid property; combining this with the convexity of the energy functional at a vertex yields the sharp constant without any intermediate inequality.

2.1
Preliminaries

Throughout the general graph discussion, we assume that no two boundary vertices are adjacent.

For
f\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
, the Dirichlet energy is

\mathcal{E}_{G}(f):=\sum_{(x,y)\in E}\bigl(f(x)-f(y)\bigr)^{2}.

If
g\in\mathbb{R}^{\delta\Omega}
is boundary data, its harmonic extension
\widehat{g}
is the unique function on
V
with
\widehat{g}|_{\delta\Omega}=g
and
\Delta\widehat{g}=0
on
\Omega
.

Lemma 2.1

(Harmonic extension minimizes energy)
.

Fix
g\in\mathbb{R}^{\delta\Omega}
.
Among all
f\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
with
f|_{\delta\Omega}=g
, the energy
\mathcal{E}_{G}(f)
is minimized by the harmonic extension
\widehat{g}
.

Proof.

Write
f=\widehat{g}+h
with
h|_{\delta\Omega}=0
, expand the energy, and use
\Delta\widehat{g}=0
on
\Omega
.
∎

The Steklov Rayleigh quotient of nonzero boundary data
g\in\mathbb{R}^{\delta\Omega}
is

\mathcal{R}_{G,\delta\Omega}(g):=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{G}(\widehat{g})}{\sum_{x\in\delta\Omega}g(x)^{2}}.

(10)

The min–max principle then gives, for
k=1,\dots,|\delta\Omega|
,

\lambda_{k}(G,\delta\Omega)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}W\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\delta\Omega}\\
\dim W=k\end{subarray}}\ \max_{0\neq g\in W}\ \mathcal{R}_{G,\delta\Omega}(g).

(11)

In several proofs below it is convenient to construct vertex functions
f\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
with prescribed boundary values
g=f|_{\delta\Omega}
;
by Lemma
2.1
,
\mathcal{R}_{G,\delta\Omega}(g)\leq\mathcal{E}_{G}(f)/\sum_{\delta\Omega}g^{2}
for any such extension
f
, so any extension provides a valid upper bound for the Rayleigh quotient.

Let
L
be the Laplacian matrix of
G
ordered so that boundary vertices come first:

L=\begin{pmatrix}L_{\delta\Omega\delta\Omega}&L_{\delta\Omega\Omega}\\[2.0pt]
L_{\Omega\delta\Omega}&L_{\Omega\Omega}\end{pmatrix}.

Proposition 2.2

(Dirichlet–to–Neumann operator)
.

Assume
G
is connected and
\delta\Omega\neq\varnothing
.
Then
L_{\Omega\Omega}
is invertible and the Dirichlet–to–Neumann operator
\Lambda:\mathbb{R}^{\delta\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{\delta\Omega}
is the symmetric matrix

\Lambda=L_{\delta\Omega\delta\Omega}-L_{\delta\Omega\Omega}L_{\Omega\Omega}^{-1}L_{\Omega\delta\Omega}.

(12)

The Steklov eigenvalues of
(G,\delta\Omega)
coincide with the eigenvalues of
\Lambda
.

Proof.

This is the standard Schur complement formula (also known as Kron reduction).
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
T
be a tree with leaves as boundary
\delta\Omega
.
Then
\lambda_{k}(T,\delta\Omega)\leq 1
for every
k
.

Proof.

For a tree with leaf boundary, no two boundary vertices are adjacent and each has degree
1
.
Hence
L_{\delta\Omega\delta\Omega}=I
, and
\Lambda=I-L_{\delta\Omega\Omega}L_{\Omega\Omega}^{-1}L_{\Omega\delta\Omega}
, where the subtracted term is positive semidefinite.
Thus
0\preceq\Lambda\preceq I
.
∎

2.2
Edge stretching

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
L\geq 1
.
Among all sequences
(t_{0},\dots,t_{L})
with
t_{0}=a
and
t_{L}=b

\sum_{i=0}^{L-1}(t_{i+1}-t_{i})^{2}

is minimized by linear interpolation

Proof.

Let

, so
\sum_{i}d_{i}=b-a
.
By Cauchy–Schwarz,
\sum_{i}d_{i}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{L}\bigl(\sum_{i}d_{i}\bigr)^{2}=(a-b)^{2}/L
, with equality if and only if all

are equal.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Fix any
k

.
For

, let
\widehat{g}
be the harmonic extension on

.
Define an extension

\widetilde{g}=\widehat{g}
on the original vertices, and

Remark 2.5

(Comparison with existing monotonicity results)
.

He–Hua
[
6
]
proved that
\lambda_{2}
is monotone non-decreasing under taking subtrees (with leaf boundary),
and Yu–Yu
[
16
]
extended this to all
\lambda_{k}
in the more general setting of graphs with combs.
Both results concern

: removing an edge from a graph decreases the Rayleigh quotient.
Edge stretching is a different operation—it does not remove the edge but

it by inserting new interior vertices.
Theorem
1.2
is complementary to these results: subdivision increases effective resistance along the edge (from

to
L

Proposition 2.6

(Strict decrease for simple eigenvalues)
.

Suppose

The proof of Theorem

with multiplicity
(3-1)\cdot 2=4
and
\mu_{2}=1/(1+3)=1/4
with multiplicity
(2-1)\cdot 1=1
.
Thus
\lambda_{2}(T(3,2))=1/3>1/4=\lambda_{2}(T(2,3))
: placing more branching closer to the leaves increases
\lambda_{2}
.

2.4
Sharp upper bound for
\lambda_{2}

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(G,\delta\Omega)
be connected and let
S\subseteq V
.
Write
p:=|\delta\Omega\cap S|/|\delta\Omega|
and assume
0<p<1
.
Then

\lambda_{2}(G,\delta\Omega)\leq\frac{|E(S,V\setminus S)|}{p(1-p)\,|\delta\Omega|}.

(14)

Proof.

Define
f\equiv(1-p)
on
S
and
f\equiv-p
on
V\setminus S
.
Then
\sum_{\delta\Omega}f=0
,
\sum_{\delta\Omega}f^{2}=p(1-p)|\delta\Omega|
, and
\mathcal{E}_{G}(f)=|E(S,V\setminus S)|
.
Harmonic extension can only decrease energy, so the Rayleigh quotient is at most the computed value.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
T
be a tree and
\delta\Omega
its set of leaves.
There exists a vertex
v
such that each component
C
of
T\setminus\{v\}
satisfies
|\delta\Omega\cap C|\leq|\delta\Omega|/2
.

Proof.

Assign weight
1
to each leaf and
0
to each interior vertex.
A standard weighted centroid argument for trees applies.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
T
be a tree with leaf boundary
\delta\Omega
and
|\delta\Omega|=\ell
.
Fix a vertex
v
of degree
r\geq 2
and let
C_{1},\dots,C_{r}
be the components of
T\setminus\{v\}
.
Write
b_{i}:=|\delta\Omega\cap C_{i}|
and fix an index
i_{0}
.
Set
s:=b_{i_{0}}
and assume
0<s\leq\ell/2
.
Then

\lambda_{2}(T,\delta\Omega)\leq\frac{r-1}{r}\cdot\frac{\ell}{s(\ell-s)}.

(15)

Proof.

Let
\alpha:=s/(\ell-s)
and define the boundary data
g\in\mathbb{R}^{\delta\Omega}
by
g\equiv 1
on
\delta\Omega\cap C_{i_{0}}
and
g\equiv-\alpha
on
\delta\Omega\setminus C_{i_{0}}
.
Then
\sum_{\delta\Omega}g=s-(\ell-s)\alpha=0
, so
g\perp\mathbf{1}
.

Extend
g
to
f\in\mathbb{R}^{V(T)}
by setting
f\equiv 1
on
C_{i_{0}}
,
f\equiv-\alpha
on each
C_{i}
for
i\neq i_{0}
, and choosing
f(v)
to minimize the energy across the
r
edges incident to
v
.
A direct minimization gives
f(v)=\frac{1-(r-1)\alpha}{r}
and

\mathcal{E}_{T}(f)=(f(v)-1)^{2}+(r-1)(f(v)+\alpha)^{2}=\frac{r-1}{r}\,(1+\alpha)^{2}.

Moreover,

\sum_{x\in\delta\Omega}g(x)^{2}=s+(\ell-s)\alpha^{2}=\frac{s\ell}{\ell-s}.

By Lemma
2.1
, the harmonic extension has energy at most
\mathcal{E}_{T}(f)
.
Using
1+\alpha=\ell/(\ell-s)
, we obtain

\mathcal{R}_{T,\delta\Omega}(g)\leq\frac{\frac{r-1}{r}\cdot\frac{\ell^{2}}{(\ell-s)^{2}}}{\frac{s\ell}{\ell-s}}=\frac{r-1}{r}\cdot\frac{\ell}{s(\ell-s)}.

By the min–max principle,
\lambda_{2}\leq\mathcal{R}_{T,\delta\Omega}(g)
, proving (
15
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
v
be a leaf centroid as in Lemma
2.10
.
Let
C_{1},\dots,C_{r}
be the components of
T\setminus\{v\}
, so
r=\deg(v)\leq D
.
Set
b_{i}:=|\delta\Omega\cap C_{i}|
.
By the centroid property, each
b_{i}\leq\ell/2
.
Let
i_{0}
maximize
b_{i}
and write
s:=b_{i_{0}}
.
By pigeonhole,
s\geq\ell/r
.

Apply Lemma
2.11
at
v
and
C_{i_{0}}
to obtain

\lambda_{2}\leq\frac{r-1}{r}\cdot\frac{\ell}{s(\ell-s)}.

Since
s\leq\ell/2
, the function
x\mapsto\ell/(x(\ell-x))
is decreasing on
(0,\ell/2]
, hence

\frac{\ell}{s(\ell-s)}\leq\frac{\ell}{(\ell/r)(\ell-\ell/r)}=\frac{r^{2}}{\ell(r-1)}.

Therefore
\lambda_{2}\leq\frac{r-1}{r}\cdot\frac{r^{2}}{\ell(r-1)}=\frac{r}{\ell}\leq\frac{D}{\ell}
.

For the equality statement, equality throughout forces

r=D,s=\frac{\ell}{r},

and the harmonic extension must have the same energy as the piecewise–constant extension used in
Lemma
2.11
. Hence the function
f
constructed in Lemma
2.11
, which is constant on each component

C_{i}
, must itself be harmonic on
\Omega
.

We claim that each component
C_{i}
contains no interior vertex. Indeed, suppose that some
C_{i}

contains an interior vertex. Since
C_{i}
is a component of
T\setminus\{v\}
, there is a unique vertex

w\in C_{i}
adjacent to
v
. This vertex
w
lies in
\Omega
, because all boundary vertices are leaves
and
w
has the neighbor
v
together with at least one neighbor inside
C_{i}
. By construction,
f

is constant on
C_{i}
, so every neighbor of
w
inside
C_{i}
has the same value as
f(w)
. On the other
hand, the value at
v
is different from the constant value on
C_{i}
. Therefore

\sum_{w^{\prime}\sim w}\bigl(f(w)-f(w^{\prime})\bigr)=f(w)-f(v)\neq 0,

so
f
is not harmonic at
w
, a contradiction.

Thus every
C_{i}
contains no interior vertex, and hence every
C_{i}
consists of a single boundary
leaf. Therefore
T
is a star. Conversely, for the star
K_{1,\ell}
we have

\lambda_{2}=1=\frac{D}{\ell},

since
D=\ell
. Hence equality holds in (
6
).
∎

2.5
General
\lambda_{k}
bound

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

The bound
\lambda_{k}\leq 1
holds by Corollary
2.3
.
Assume
\ell\geq 4Dk
. Let
t=\lfloor\ell/(4Dk)\rfloor\geq 1
and
M:=4k
.
We recursively construct
M
pairwise disjoint connected components
C_{1},\dots,C_{M}
.
Let
U_{1}:=T
, rooted at an arbitrary vertex. For the
m
-step (
1\leq m\leq M-1
), assume the remaining tree is
U_{m}
and that the previously removed components
C_{1},\dots,C_{m-1}
each strictly contain fewer than
Dt
boundary leaves. Under this inductive assumption, the number of boundary leaves in
U_{m}
satisfies

|\delta\Omega\cap U_{m}|>\ell-(m-1)Dt\geq\ell-(M-2)Dt\geq\ell-(4k-2)\frac{\ell}{4k}=\frac{\ell}{2k}\geq 2Dt>Dt.

Hence the root of
U_{m}
must have a child-subtree containing at least
t

boundary leaves, so the greedy downward walk cannot stop at the root. Continuing downward while the current vertex has a child-subtree containing at least
t
boundary
leaves, we eventually terminate at a vertex
u_{m}
since the tree is finite.
Let
u_{m}
be the vertex where the walk stops; equivalently,
the descendant subtree
(U_{m})_{u_{m}}
contains at least
t
boundary leaves,
but every child-subtree of
u_{m}
in
U_{m}
contains fewer than
t
boundary leaves.
Then
u_{m}
is a proper descendant, so
(U_{m})_{u_{m}}
is attached to

U_{m}\setminus(U_{m})_{u_{m}}
by exactly one edge, and the latter is connected.
Moreover,

t\leq|\delta\Omega\cap(U_{m})_{u_{m}}|<Dt.

Indeed, if
u_{m}
is a leaf then
|\delta\Omega\cap(U_{m})_{u_{m}}|=1<Dt
.
Otherwise
u_{m}
has at most
D-1
children in
U_{m}
, and each child-subtree
contains fewer than
t
boundary leaves. Let

C_{m}:=(U_{m})_{u_{m}},\qquad U_{m+1}:=U_{m}\setminus C_{m}.

After
M-1=4k-1
steps, the remaining connected component
U_{M}
satisfies

|\delta\Omega\cap U_{M}|\geq\ell-(M-1)Dt\geq\frac{\ell}{4k}\geq t.

Let
C_{M}:=U_{M}
. Thus we obtain a partition of
T
into
M=4k
disjoint connected components
C_{1},\dots,C_{M}
, each containing at least
t

boundary leaves.

Let
Q
be a quotient graph obtained by contracting each
C_{i}
to a vertex. Since
T
is a tree,
Q
is also a tree with
M
vertices.
Let
n_{i}
be the number of vertices in
Q
with degree of
i
. Since the sum of degrees is
2M-2
, we have

2M-2=n_{1}+2n_{2}+\sum_{i\geq 3}in_{i}\geq n_{1}+2n_{2}+3(M-n_{1}-n_{2}).

Rearranging gives
2n_{1}+n_{2}\geq M+2
, which strictly implies
n_{1}+n_{2}>\frac{M}{2}
. Let
S
be the set of vertices in
Q
with degree at most
2
. We have
|S|\geq M/2=2k
.
The subgraph
Q[S]
induced by
S
has maximum degree at most
2
, so it is a collection of paths and isolated vertices. In any acyclic graph with maximum degree at most
2
, the independence number is at least half the number of its vertices. Thus,
Q[S]
contains an independent set
I
of size at least
|S|/2\geq k
. We select exactly
k
components corresponding to the vertices in
I
. By definition, these
k
components are mutually non-adjacent in
Q
, and each has degree at most
2
in
Q
.

For each
j\in I
, define boundary data
g_{j}\equiv 1
on
\delta\Omega\cap C_{j}
and
g_{j}\equiv 0
elsewhere. Define its extension
f_{j}\equiv 1
on
C_{j}
and
0
elsewhere. Let
W=\mathrm{span}\{g_{j}\}_{j\in I}
which has dimension
k
. For any
g=\sum_{j\in I}a_{j}g_{j}\in W
, consider the extension
f=\sum_{j\in I}a_{j}f_{j}
. Since
I
is an independent set, no two selected components share an edge. The Dirichlet energy of
f
is exclusively supported on edges connecting selected components to unselected components. Since each selected
C_{j}
has degree at most
2
in
Q
, it is connected to at most
2
unselected components. Thus, cross-terms completely vanish, yielding:

\mathcal{E}_{T}(f)=\sum_{j\in I}\deg_{C_{j}}(a_{j}-0)^{2}\leq 2\sum_{j\in I}a_{j}^{2}.

The denominator of the Rayleigh quotient is
\sum_{j\in I}a_{j}^{2}|\delta\Omega\cap C_{j}|\geq t\sum_{j\in I}a_{j}^{2}
. By Lemma
2.1
, the Steklov Rayleigh quotient is bounded by:

\mathcal{R}_{T,\delta\Omega}(g)\leq\frac{\mathcal{E}_{T}(f)}{t\sum_{j\in I}a_{j}^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{t}

Using
\lfloor x\rfloor\geq x/2
for all
x\geq 1
, we have
1/t\leq 8Dk/\ell
. Therefore,
\mathcal{R}_{T,\delta\Omega}(g)\leq 16Dk/\ell
. By the min–max principle,
\lambda_{k}\leq 16Dk/\ell
.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

For
k=2
, Theorem
1.8
yields
\lambda_{2}\leq 32D/\ell
when
\ell\geq 8D
.
The sharp estimate in Theorem
1.4
improves this to
\lambda_{2}\leq D/\ell
for all leaf–boundary trees.

Remark 2.13

(On the constant for higher eigenvalues)
.

The factor
16
in the bound
\lambda_{k}\leq 16Dk/\ell
arises from the
4k
–partition and independent set argument.
For
k=2
, the leaf–centroid method of Theorem
1.4
achieves the sharp constant
1
, but it does not generalize directly to
k\geq 3
because one would need to partition the boundary into
k
balanced parts with few separating edges.
It would be interesting to determine whether the constant
16
can be reduced for specific values of
k
, or whether there exist families of trees showing that it is tight (see Problem
4.2
).

4
Concluding remarks

We have proved that Steklov eigenvalues decrease monotonically under the edge–stretching operation. Moreover, we established a sharp upper bound
\lambda_{2}\leq D/\ell
for trees with leaf boundaries, derived explicit spectra for level-regular trees, and provided a general bound for higher eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
.
These results fit into the extremal program for
\lambda_{2}
on trees with constraints on leaf count and maximum degree:

•

Corollary
1.3
reduces the search for extremizers to homeomorphically irreducible trees.

•

Theorem
1.6
provides a closed formula for an important candidate family, allowing an exact comparison.

•

Theorems
1.4
and
1.8
give upper benchmarks that any candidate must respect.

A natural next step is to combine these tools with structural transformations that move leaves between branches while controlling the DtN form, in order to approach conjectured extremizers such as balanced minimum–height trees
[
11
]
.

It is natural to ask similar questions on other graph families.
Monotonicity of Steklov eigenvalues with respect to the addition of edges (see
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
) justifies the consideration of the problem on subgraph–closed graph families.
