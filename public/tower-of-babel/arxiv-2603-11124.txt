On the energy dissipation rate of ensemble eddy viscosity models of turbulence: Shear flows Thanks: The research herein was partially supported by NSF grant DMS 2410893
William Layton

Abstract
. Classical eddy viscosity models add a viscosity term with turbulent viscosity coefficient developed beginning with the Kolmogorov-Prandtl parameterization. Approximations of unknown accuracy of the unknown mixing lengths and turbulent kinetic energy are typically constructed by solving associated systems of nonlinear convection-diffusion-reaction equations with nonlinear boundary conditions. Often these over-diffuse so additional fixes are added such as wall laws or using different approximations in different regions (which must also be specified). Alternately, one can solve an ensemble of NSE’s with perturbed data, compute the ensemble mean and fluctuation and simply compute directly the turbulent viscosity parameterization. This idea is recent, seems to be of lower complexity and greater accuracy and produces parameterizations with the correct near wall asymptotic behavior. The question then arises: Does this ensemble eddy viscosity approach over-diffuse solutions? This question is addressed herein.

This function

is piecewise linear, continuous, divergence free and
satisfies the boundary conditions. The following are easily calculated values

\begin{array}[c]{cc}||\,\phi\,||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=U,&||\,\nabla\phi\,||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\frac{U}{\beta\,L},\text{ }\\
||\,\phi\,||^{2}=\frac{1}{3}\,U^{2}\,\beta\,L^{3},&\text{ }||\,\nabla\,\phi\,||^{2}=\frac{U^{2}\,L}{\beta}.\end{array}

With this choice of
\phi
, time averaging the energy inequality
(
3.2
) over
[0,T]
and normalizing by
|\Omega|=L^{3}

gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2TL^{3}}||v(T)||^{2}+\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{L^{3}}\int_{\Omega}[\nu+\nu_{turb}]|\,\nabla{v}|^{2}dx\right)dt\leq\frac{1}{2TL^{3}}||v(0)||^{2}

(3.4)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{TL^{3}}(v(T)-v(0),\phi)+\left\langle\frac{1}{L^{3}}(v\cdot\nabla v,\phi)\right\rangle_{T}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{L^{3}}\int_{\Omega}[\nu+\,\nu_{turb}]\nabla{v}:\nabla\phi dx\right)dt.

Due to the above uniform in
T
bounds, the time averaged energy inequality
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varepsilon_{\text{model}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{T})+\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{L^{3}}(v\cdot\nabla v,\phi)\right)dt+

(3.5)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{L^{3}}\int_{\Omega}[\nu+\,\nu_{turb}]\nabla{v}:\nabla\phi dx\right)dt.

The main issue is the RHS model term,
\int\,\nu_{turb}\nabla{v}:\nabla\phi dx
. Before treating that, we recall the analysis of Doering and Constantin

[
4
]
and Wang
[
34
]
for the two terms shared by the NSE,

(v\cdot\nabla v,\phi)
and
\int\nu\nabla{v}:\nabla\phi dx
. For the
nonlinear term,
\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{L^{3}}(v\cdot\nabla v,\phi)dt=:NLT
, we have

\displaystyle NLT=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{L^{3}}([v-\phi]\cdot\nabla v,\phi)dt+\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{L^{3}}(\phi\cdot\nabla v,\phi)dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{L^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}|v-\phi||\nabla v||\phi|+|\phi|^{2}|\nabla v|dx\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{L^{3}}\left\|\frac{v-\phi}{L-z}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}||(L-z)\phi||_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}\right)dt+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{L^{3}}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}||\phi||_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}^{2}||\nabla v||_{L^{1}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}dt.

On the RHS,
||\phi||_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}^{2}=\phi(L)^{2}=U^{2}

and
||(L-z)\phi||_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}=\frac{1}{4}\beta LU.

Since
v-\phi
vanishes on the
z=L
boundary of
\partial\mathcal{S}_{\beta}
, Hardy’s inequality, (
2.2
) in Section 2, the triangle
inequality and a calculation imply

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{v-\phi}{L-z}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|\nabla(v-\phi)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}\leq 2\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}+2\left\|\nabla\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}+2U\sqrt{\frac{L}{\beta}}.

Thus we have the estimate

(3.6)

\displaystyle NLT

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta LU}{4}\frac{1}{L^{3}}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(2||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}^{2}+2U\sqrt{\frac{L}{\beta}}||v||_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}\right)dt+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{L^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{U^{2}}{L^{3}}||\nabla v||_{L^{1}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}dt\right).

For the last term on the RHS, Hölders inequality in space then in time
implies

\displaystyle\frac{U^{2}}{L^{3}}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}|\nabla v|\cdot 1dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{U^{2}}{L^{3}}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx}\sqrt{\beta L^{3}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{U^{2}\sqrt{\beta}}{L^{3/2}}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}1\cdot\sqrt{\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{U^{2}\sqrt{\beta}}{L^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}|\nabla v|^{2}dxdt\right)^{1/2}.

Increase the integral from
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}
to
\Omega
, and use

\beta=\frac{1}{8}\mathcal{R}e_{eff}^{-1}.
Rearranging and using the
arithmetic-geometric inequality gives an estimate useful for the last term in
(
3.6
)

\displaystyle\frac{U^{2}}{L^{3}}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}||\nabla v||_{L^{1}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}dt\leq U^{2}\sqrt{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{L^{3}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}dxdt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq U^{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{8}\frac{1}{LU}}\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{L^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\nu_{eff}|\nabla v|^{2}dxdt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{U^{3}}{L}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1}{8}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{L^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\nu_{eff}|\nabla v|^{2}dxdt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{U^{3}}{L}+\frac{1}{16}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{L^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\nu_{eff}|\nabla^{s}v|^{2}dx\right)dt.

Similar manipulations (using
\beta=\frac{1}{8}\frac{\nu_{eff}}{LU}
) yield an
estimate for the second term on the RHS in (
3.6
):

\displaystyle\frac{\beta LU}{4}\frac{1}{L^{3}}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(2U\sqrt{\frac{L}{\beta}}||v||_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}\right)dt\leq\frac{\beta LU}{2}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{L^{3}}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}^{2}dt+\frac{1}{8}\frac{U^{3}}{L}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{L^{3}}\nu_{eff}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt+\frac{1}{8}\frac{U^{3}}{L}.

The first term on the RHS is simplest:

\displaystyle\frac{\beta LU}{4}\frac{1}{L^{3}}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}2||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16}\frac{1}{L^{3}}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\nu_{eff}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{16}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{eff}}{L^{3}}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt.

Using the last three estimates in the
NLT
upper bound (
3.6
), we
obtain (term by term)

\displaystyle NLT\leq\frac{1}{16}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{eff}}{L^{3}}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt+\frac{1}{8}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{eff}}{L^{3}}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8}\frac{U^{3}}{L}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{U^{3}}{L}+\frac{1}{16}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{eff}}{L^{3}}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt

\displaystyle or:\text{ \ \ }NLT\leq\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{eff}}{L^{3}}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt+\frac{5}{8}\frac{U^{3}}{L}.

Thus, from (
3.5
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varepsilon_{\text{model}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{T})+\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{eff}}{L^{3}}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt+\frac{5}{8}\frac{U^{3}}{L}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{L^{3}}\int_{\Omega}[\nu+\,\nu_{turb}]\nabla{v}:\nabla\phi dx\right)dt.

Consider now the last term on the RHS. Since
\phi
is zero off
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}
and
\nabla\,\phi\,=\frac{U}{\beta L}
on
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}
we
have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{L^{3}}\int_{\Omega}[\nu+\,\nu_{turb}]\nabla{v}:\nabla\phi dxdt=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{L^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}[\nu+\,\nu_{turb}]\nabla{v}:\nabla\phi dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varepsilon_{\text{model}}dt+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{L^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}[\nu+\,\nu_{turb}]\left(\frac{U}{\beta L}\right)^{2}dx\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varepsilon_{\text{model}}dt+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{U}{\beta L}\right)^{2}\beta\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{\beta L^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\nu+\,\nu_{turb}dx\right)dt.

Thus, as
\beta=\frac{1}{8}\mathcal{R}e_{eff}^{-1}
implies
2\beta\mathcal{R}e_{eff}=1/4
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varepsilon_{\text{model}}dt\leq\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{T})+\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{eff}}{L^{3}}||\nabla v||^{2}dt+

\displaystyle+\frac{5}{8}\frac{U^{3}}{L}+\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\frac{U}{\beta L}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\beta L^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\nu+\,\nu_{turb}dxdt.

As a subsequence
T_{j}\rightarrow\infty

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{eff}}{L^{3}}||\nabla v||^{2}dt\rightarrow\left\langle\varepsilon_{\text{model}}\right\rangle_{\infty},

and we calculate

\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\frac{U}{\beta L}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\beta L^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\nu dxdt=\nu\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\frac{U}{\beta L}\right)^{2}=4\frac{\nu}{\nu_{eff}}\frac{U^{3}}{L}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varepsilon_{\text{model}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{T})+\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\nu_{eff}}{L^{3}}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt

\displaystyle+(4\frac{\nu}{\nu_{eff}}+\frac{5}{8})\frac{U^{3}}{L}+\beta\left(\frac{U}{\beta L}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\beta L^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,\nu_{turb}dxdt.

The last term on the RHS is rearranged to be

\beta\left(\frac{U}{\beta L}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\beta L^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,\nu_{turb}dxdt=8\left[\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\beta}|}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,\frac{\nu_{turb}}{\nu_{eff}}dxdt\right]\frac{U^{3}}{L},

The proof is completed by taking the limit superior as
T\rightarrow\infty
.
This gives

\left\langle\varepsilon_{\text{model}}\right\rangle_{\infty}\leq\left(\frac{5}{2}+16\frac{\nu}{\nu_{eff}}+32\left\langle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\beta}|}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,\frac{\nu_{turb}}{\nu_{eff}}dx\right\rangle_{\infty}\right)\frac{U^{3}}{L}.

∎

The above theorem shows that the behavior of the turbulent viscosity in the
near wall region is a critical factor in model dissipation. The near wall term
will be estimated by a precise use of the following lemma which is an
application of the
L^{p}-L^{q}
Hardy inequality (
2.3
).

Lemma 2
.

For
F(x,y,z)\in H^{1}(\mathcal{S}_{\beta})
with
F(x,y,L)=0
there holds

(3.7)

\left(\int_{(1-\beta)L}^{L}|L-z|^{-4}|F(x,y,z)|^{6}dz\right)^{1/6}\leq C_{26}\left(\int_{(1-\beta)L}^{L}|F_{z}(x,y,z)|^{2}dz\right)^{1/2}

Proof.

First note that for
F(x)
vanishing at
x=0
there holds

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{F(x)}{x^{2/3}}\right|^{6}dx\right)^{1/6}\leq C_{26}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|F_{x}(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}.

This follows from (
2.3
) by letting
F(x)=\int_{0}^{x}f(t)dt

and choosing
q=6,p=2,

\alpha=

-4
. These choices satisfy
\left(\alpha+1\right)/6=(1/2)-1
and
1<p\leq q<\infty
as required. We will apply
this result where
F
is a function of
x,y,z
defined on
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}
, the variable
x
is replaced by
z
and a linear change of variables is
made so the zero boundary condition is imposed at
z=L
rather than
z=0
. The
result is

\left(\int_{(1-\beta)L}^{L}|L-z|^{-4}|F(x,y,z)|^{6}dz\right)^{1/6}\leq C_{26}\left(\int_{(1-\beta)L}^{L}|F_{z}(x,y,z)|^{2}dz\right)^{1/2}

∎

This lemma will be used to estimate the integral

\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,\nu_{turb}dx=\mu\tau\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,|u^{\prime}(x,t)|_{e}^{2}dx.

Lemma 3
.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,|u^{\prime}(x,y,z,t;\omega_{j})|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq 3^{-2/3}C_{26}^{2}\beta^{2}L^{2}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\left|\frac{\partial u^{\prime}}{\partial z}(x,y,z,t;\omega_{j})\right|^{2}dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,|u^{\prime}(x,y,z,t)|_{e}^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq 3^{-2/3}C_{26}^{2}\beta^{2}L^{2}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\left\langle\,\left|\frac{\partial u^{\prime}}{\partial z}(x,y,z,t;\omega_{j})\right|^{2}\right\rangle_{e}dx

and thus

\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,\nu_{turb}dx\leq\mu\tau 3^{-2/3}C_{26}^{2}\beta^{2}L^{2}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\left\langle\,\left|\frac{\partial u^{\prime}}{\partial z}(x,y,z,t;\omega_{j})\right|^{2}\right\rangle_{e}dx

Proof.

We have for each
\omega_{j}
, by Holders inequality in the
z
integral,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx=\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\left[\int_{(1-\beta)L}^{L}\left(L-z\right)^{4/3}\,\left|\frac{u^{\prime}(x,y,z,t;\omega_{j})}{\left(L-z\right)^{2/3}}\right|^{2}dz\right]dxdy\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\left[\int_{(1-\beta)L}^{L}\left(\left(L-z\right)^{4/3}\right)^{3/2}\,dz\right]^{\frac{2}{3}}\left[\int_{(1-\beta)L}^{L}\,\left|\frac{u^{\prime}(x,y,z,t;\omega_{j})}{\left(L-z\right)^{2/3}}\right|^{6}dz\right]^{\frac{1}{3}}dxdy

\displaystyle:=\int\int A\cdot Bdxdy.

The first integral (
A
) in the RHS is

A=\left[\int_{(1-\beta)L}^{L}\left(L-z\right)^{2}\,dz\right]^{2/3}=\left[\frac{\left(\beta L\right)^{3}}{3}\right]^{2/3}=3^{-2/3}\beta^{2}L^{2}.

For the second integral (
B
) we apply (
3.7
) from the last
Lemma:

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{(1-\beta)L}^{L}\left(L-z\right)^{-4}\left|u^{\prime}(x,y,z,t;\omega_{j})\right|^{6}dz\right]^{1/3}

\displaystyle\leq C_{26}^{2}\int_{(1-\beta)L}^{L}\,\left|\frac{\partial u^{\prime}}{\partial z}(x,y,z,t;\omega_{j})\right|^{2}dz,

In combination we have

\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,|u^{\prime}(x,t;\omega_{j})|^{2}dx\leq\left(3^{-2/3}\beta^{2}L^{2}\right)C_{26}^{2}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{(1-\beta)L}^{L}\,\left|\frac{\partial u^{\prime}}{\partial z}(x,t;\omega_{j})\right|^{2}dzdxdy

which is the first estimate. The second follows by taking the ensemble average
of the first.
∎

We can now estimate the last term on the RHS of (
3.3
).

Proposition 2
.

We have

\displaystyle 32\left\langle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\beta}|}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,\frac{\nu_{turb}}{\nu_{eff}}dx\right\rangle_{\infty}\frac{U^{3}}{L}\leq

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt[3]{3}}{6}C_{26}^{2}\mu\frac{\tau}{T^{\ast}}\left\langle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\beta}|}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\nu_{eff}\left\langle\,\left|\frac{\partial u^{\prime}}{\partial z}\right|^{2}\right\rangle_{e}dx\right\rangle_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt[3]{3}}{6}C_{26}^{2}\mu\frac{\tau}{T^{\ast}}\left\langle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\beta}|}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\nu_{eff}\left\langle\,\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\right\rangle_{e}dx\right\rangle_{\infty}.

Proof.

From the above estimates we have

\displaystyle 32\left\langle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\beta}|}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,\frac{\nu_{turb}}{\nu_{eff}}dx\right\rangle_{\infty}\frac{U^{3}}{L}\leq

\displaystyle\leq\left(32\cdot 3^{-2/3}C_{26}^{2}\right)\beta^{2}L^{2}\frac{\mu\tau}{\nu_{eff}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\beta}|}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\left\langle\,\left|\frac{\partial u^{\prime}}{\partial z}\right|^{2}\right\rangle_{e}dx\frac{U^{3}}{L}.

Algebraic rearrangement of various multipliers in the above (using turnover
time
T^{\ast}=L/U
) gives

\beta^{2}L^{2}\frac{\mu\tau}{\nu_{eff}}\frac{U^{3}}{L}=\left(\frac{\nu_{eff}}{8LU}\right)^{2}L^{2}\frac{1}{\nu_{eff}}\frac{\mu\tau}{T^{\ast}}\frac{L}{U}\frac{U^{3}}{L}=\frac{1}{64}\nu_{eff}\frac{\mu\tau}{T^{\ast}}.

Thus

\displaystyle 32\left\langle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\beta}|}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\,\frac{\nu_{turb}}{\nu_{eff}}dx\right\rangle_{\infty}\frac{U^{3}}{L}\leq

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{32}{64}\cdot 3^{-2/3}C_{26}^{2}\right)\mu\frac{\tau}{T^{\ast}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\beta}|}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\nu_{eff}\left\langle\,\left|\frac{\partial u^{\prime}}{\partial z}\right|^{2}\right\rangle_{e}dx.

The result now follows from

\left\langle\,\left|\frac{\partial u^{\prime}}{\partial z}\right|^{2}\right\rangle_{e}\leq\left\langle\,\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}\right|^{2}\right\rangle_{e}\leq\left\langle\,\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\right\rangle_{e}.

∎

To develop the numerical value for
\frac{\sqrt[3]{3}}{6}C_{26}^{2}\simeq

\allowbreak 0.230\,92
we begin with
\frac{\sqrt[3]{3}}{6}\simeq\allowbreak 0.240\,37
and, from Bliss
[
1
]
, with
p=p^{\prime}=2,q=6

(3.8)

C_{pq}=\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{\frac{q-p}{p}\Gamma\left(\frac{pq}{q-p}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{q-p}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{p(q-1)}{q-p}\right)}\right)

so that

C_{26}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{2\Gamma\left(3\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)}\right)\simeq 0.980\,14.

We can now give another result for the time averaged energy dissipation rate.

Theorem 5
.

Any weak solution of the eddy viscosity model (

)
satisfying the energy inequality (

\displaystyle\left\langle\varepsilon_{\text{model}}\right\rangle_{\infty}\leq\left(\frac{5}{2}+16\frac{\nu}{\nu_{eff}}\right)\frac{U^{3}}{L}+

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\sqrt[3]{3}}{6}C_{26}^{2}\right)\mu\frac{\tau}{T^{\ast}}\left\langle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\beta}|}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\nu_{eff}\left\langle\,\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\right\rangle_{e}dx\right\rangle_{\infty}

\displaystyle and

\displaystyle\left\langle\varepsilon_{\text{model}}\right\rangle_{\infty}\leq\left(\frac{5}{2}+16\frac{\nu}{\nu_{eff}}\right)\frac{U^{3}}{L}+

(3.10)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\sqrt[3]{3}}{6}C_{26}^{2}\right)\mu\frac{\tau}{T^{\ast}}\left\langle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\beta}|}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\nu_{eff}\left\langle\,\left|\frac{\partial u^{\prime}}{\partial z}\right|^{2}\right\rangle_{e}dx\right\rangle_{\infty}

Theorem 6
.

Suppose
\mu
is one constant value in the flow interior and
\mu=\mu_{\beta}

in

\left\langle\varepsilon_{\text{model}}\right\rangle_{\infty}\leq\left(5+32\frac{\nu}{\nu_{eff}}\right)\frac{U^{3}}{L}.

.

Proof.

To begin note that if
\mu

\mu(x)
inside the volume integral or the volume integral split
into sub-regions
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}
and
\Omega-\mathcal{S}_{\beta}

estimate the last term on the RHS of (

\Omega

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sqrt[3]{3}}{6}C_{26}^{2}\right)\mu_{\beta}\frac{\tau}{T^{\ast}}\left\langle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\beta}|}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}}\nu_{eff}\left\langle\,\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\right\rangle_{e}dx\right\rangle_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{8\sqrt[3]{3}}{6}C_{26}^{2}\right)\mu_{\beta}\frac{\tau}{T^{\ast}}\mathcal{R}e_{eff}\left\langle\frac{1}{L^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{\Omega}}\nu_{eff}\left\langle\,\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\right\rangle_{e}dx\right\rangle_{\infty}

4.
Conclusions

We have analyzed wall effects on energy dissipation rates for the ensemble
eddy viscosity model. The results herein are only a first step. There are a
number of important mathematical challenges (open problems) that remain:

•

The constant multipliers in the results are large as a result of the
number of estimates employed. Significant reduction would bring the analytical
estimates closer to experimental data.

•

The model is based on a turbulence length scale
l(x,t)=|u^{\prime}(x,t)|_{e}\tau
. Since this is an
L^{2}
function, there is no mathematical

account for intermittent transfer of energy from unresolved fluctuations back
to the mean velocity. Attempts have been made based on negative viscosities
but these cannot be correct in principle. A more correct approach is through
