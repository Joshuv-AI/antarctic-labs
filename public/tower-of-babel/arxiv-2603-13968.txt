Dimension reduction of fractional Sobolev seminorms on thin domains
Andrea Braides, Andrea Pinamonti, Margherita Solci

Abstract

Introduction

Dimension-reduction problems arise naturally in the study of variational models defined on thin structures. In many physical situations one considers a family of domains whose thickness tends to zero and investigates the corresponding asymptotic behaviour of the associated energy functionals. Classical examples include thin films, membranes, and plates in nonlinear elasticity, where three-dimensional models give rise to effective lower-dimensional theories. The rigorous derivation of such reduced models has been extensively studied using
\Gamma
-convergence methods, starting from the seminal work of Le Dret and Raoult
[
28
]
and later developments summarized e.g. in the references
[
6
,
7
,
9
,
23
,
22
]
.

Most results in this area concern local energies, typically involving gradients. The simplest example is the Dirichlet integral

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx.

(1)

defined on thin domains

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\omega\times(0,\varepsilon)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\omega
is a (bounded) open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and
\varepsilon
represents the thickness of the film. In this classical setting the asymptotic analysis reveals a separation between derivatives along the thin direction and derivatives along the planar variables, leading to effective lower-dimensional energies defined on
\omega
. This is done through an asymptotic analysis of functionals (
1
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
. In order to provide a common analytical framework, it is customary to scale the variable
u
to a function
v\in H^{1}(\omega\times(0,1))
as follows

v(x^{\prime},x_{d})=u(x^{\prime},\varepsilon x_{d}),\qquad x^{\prime}\in\omega,\ x_{d}\in(0,1),\ \varepsilon>0.

(2)

Here the prime indicates quantities depending on (or operations with respect to) the variable
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
. This change of variables allows one to view functionals (
1
) as defined on
H^{1}(\omega\times(0,1))
. Properly scaling the energies (
dimension-reduction scaling
; for the functional (
1
) simply dividing by
\varepsilon
), it is shown that their limits are actually finite on functions depending only on
x^{\prime}
, thus understood as functions in
H^{1}(\omega)
.

In contrast, much less is known when the energy is nonlocal. Nonlocal variational energies arise naturally in many contexts, including anomalous diffusion, peridynamics, and fractional phase-transition models. In particular, fractional Sobolev seminorms play a fundamental role in the theory of fractional Laplacians and nonlocal operators. A comprehensive overview of fractional Sobolev spaces can be found in the survey of Di Nezza, Palatucci, and Valdinoci
[
20
]
. A key structural result is the celebrated formula of Bourgain, Brezis, and Mironescu
[
5
]
, which shows that fractional seminorms converge to the Dirichlet energy as the fractional exponent tends to one. This paper had a major impact and it was later generalized in many directions (see e.g.
[
36
,
14
,
13
,
12
,
27
,
24
,
19
,
34
,
26
]
) and to different functional spaces (see e.g.
[
11
,
17
,
30
,
31
,
36
]
).

Despite the extensive literature on fractional Sobolev spaces and nonlocal energies, the interaction between nonlocal kernels and geometric constraints remains comparatively unexplored. In particular, the asymptotic behaviour of fractional energies on thin domains presents new difficulties. Unlike the classical Sobolev case, fractional seminorms couple interactions across all length scales, and therefore do not naturally decouple into derivatives along different directions. As a consequence, the derivation of effective lower-dimensional models requires a more delicate analysis.

The aim of this paper is to study the asymptotic behaviour of fractional Sobolev seminorms on thin domains, of the form

\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s}(\Omega_{\epsilon})=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dxdy,

(3)

with
s\in(0,1)
. Our goal is to identify the relevant scaling regimes and to compute the
corresponding
\Gamma
-limits of the associated energies. A key feature of the analysis is that the asymptotic behaviour depends crucially on the integrability of the interaction kernel, which is governed by the fractional exponent.

Our results reveal the existence of two qualitatively different dimension-reduction
regimes separated by the critical exponent
s=1/2
. Before analyzing the dimension-reduction
process, we preliminarily highlight the first relevant scaling, which
is of order
\varepsilon^{1-2s}
. At this scaling, the dominant contribution to the energy arises from oscillations along the thin direction. The corresponding
\Gamma
-limit captures the fractional oscillations of the rescaled functions along one-dimensional vertical sections. In particular, the limit energy can be expressed as the integral over
\omega
of one-dimensional fractional seminorms.

At the next scaling order, the behaviour depends on the value of
s
. When
s<1/2
, long-
range interactions dominate and the resulting effective energy remains nonlocal. In this
regime the limit functional corresponds to a fractional seminorm defined on the planar
domain
\omega
, with an
effective gain of
1/2
in the differentiability index
. When
s>1/2

instead, the dominant interactions occur at small scales of order
\varepsilon
, and the effective
energy becomes local. In this case the
\Gamma
-limit reduces to a Dirichlet-type functional on

\omega
. The exponent
s=1/2
represents a critical threshold at which the two mechanisms
balance and a logarithmic correction appears in the scaling.

These results show that the dimension-reduction behaviour of fractional energies is
governed by the competition between the thickness of the domain and the
characteristic interaction length scale of the kernel. In particular, the analysis reveals a
transition from nonlocal effective energies to local ones depending on the integrability
of the kernel.

Finally, we also consider the regime in which the fractional exponent approaches one
simultaneously with the thickness parameter. In this case, we recover, through a suitable
renormalization, a Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu-type limit that connects the present
analysis with the classical thin-film asymptotics for Dirichlet energies.

The results are complemented by compactness results, which suggest the relevant dimension-reduction convergence of the scaled functions
v
for the computation of the
\Gamma
-limits.
We briefly summarize (and slightly simplify for the sake of exposition) the results below:

Vertical slicing (Theorem
3.3
)

Let
s\in(0,1)
, and define

E^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1-2s}}\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}).

Then we have

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}E^{s}_{\varepsilon}(v)=C_{s,d}\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|v(x^{\prime},x_{d})-v(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}.

(4)

The
\Gamma
-limit is taken with respect to the weak convergence in
L^{2}(\omega\times(0,1))
of the rescaled functions
v
.

This result, in particular, shows that the limit energy describes the fractional oscillations of the function along one-dimensional vertical sections.
Remarkably, the explicit constant
C_{s,d}
,
arising from the change of variables that allows for a Fubini-type argument, can be written in terms of a
hypergeometric
function, which itself can be expressed in terms of Euler’s Gamma function.
The result can also be extended to varying
s_{\varepsilon}
, taking into account the one-dimensional version of the Bourgain-Brezis-Mironescu result if
s_{\varepsilon}\to 1
.

At the next order of the asymptotic expansion, the behaviour changes depending on the
value of the fractional exponent
s
. The following theorem characterizes the resulting
dimension-reduction regimes.

Dimension Reduction (Theorems

4.13
,
4.7
, and
4.4
)

1. Low-integrability regimes.
Let
s<1/2
. At scale
\varepsilon^{2}
we have

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\int_{\omega}\int_{\omega}\frac{|v(x^{\prime})-v(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s}}\,dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}=\lfloor v\rfloor^{2}_{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\omega).

(5)

This limit is calculated with respect to the weak topology in
L^{2}(\omega\times(0,1))
.

2. Critical regime.
For
s\geq 1/2
we have an improved dimension-reduction coerciveness result in the strong topology of
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\omega\times(0,1))
. With respect to this convergence, for
s=1/2
the correct scaling is
\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|
, and we have

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{1/2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2(d-1)}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}v|^{2}dx^{\prime}.

(6)

3. High-integrability regimes.
Let
s>1/2
. At scale
\varepsilon^{3-2s}

we have

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{1-s}K_{s,d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}v|^{2}dx^{\prime}.

(7)

The coefficient
K_{s,d}

in (
7
) can also be expressed as a hypergeometric integral, diverging as
s\to 1/2+
. Note that by the Bourgain-Brezis-Mironescu result, also the functional on the right-hand side of (
5
) diverges as
s\to 1/2^{-}
.

The dimension-reduction regimes can also be analyzed for varying
s_{\varepsilon}
. In particular for
s_{\varepsilon}\to 1^{-}
we recover an analog of the Bourgain-Brezis-Mironescu result, which implies the following
separation of scale
phenomenon at both the first and the second scaling.

Bourgain–Brezis-Mironescu-type Expansion (Theorem
5.1
).

Let
\varepsilon^{1-s_{\varepsilon}}\to 1
; that is, let

1-s_{\varepsilon}<\!<\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}.

Then, we have

(1-s_{\varepsilon})\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\ \mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\Gamma}}\ \frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{d}}\Big|^{2}dx_{d}\,dx^{\prime}+\varepsilon\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}v|^{2}dx^{\prime}+o(\varepsilon)

in the sense of
\Gamma
-expansions

[
10
]
.

These results show that the dimension-reduction behaviour of fractional Sobolev
energies depends crucially on the integrability of the kernel. In particular, the critical exponent
s=1/2
separates regimes in which the effective lower-dimensional energy is nonlocal from those in which it becomes local. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first systematic asymptotic analysis describing the dimension reduction of fractional Sobolev energies in thin domains, including the critical transition at
s=1/2
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we introduce the notation and recall basic facts on fractional Sobolev spaces and dimension-reduction convergence. Section 3 is devoted to the analysis of the first scaling regime and the corresponding
\Gamma
-limit in
the thin direction. In Section 4 we study the dimension-reduction regimes, identify the relevant scaling laws depending on
s
, and compute the associated
\Gamma
-limits. Finally, in Section 5 we reformulate the results in terms of
\Gamma
-expansions, highlighting the analogy with the classical asymptotic expansions for local thin-film energies.

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then we write
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-1})

.
We also write

With a slight abuse of notation, this is done both when
u=u(x^{\prime})
and
u=u(x)
. In the second case, we also write the usual gradient as
\nabla u=(\nabla^{\prime}u,\tfrac{\partial u}{\partial x_{d}})
.

The symbol

\sigma_{k}=\mathcal{H}^{k-1}(S^{k-1})

(8)

denotes the surface measure of the unit sphere in

(in our case, it will be either
k=d

a

2.1
Dimension-reduction convergence

In the following,
\omega
denotes a bounded connected open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
with Lipschitz boundary, and for all
\varepsilon>0

we define the
thin film

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\omega\times(0,\varepsilon)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In order to define a notion of convergence for functions
u_{\varepsilon}
defined on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we rescale them onto a fixed domain. To this end, we define functions
v_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, with
\Omega=\omega\times(0,1)
, by

v_{\varepsilon}(x)=v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d}):=u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},\varepsilon x_{d}).

(9)

Besides strong and weak convergence for
v_{\varepsilon}
in the spaces
L^{p}(\omega\times(0,1))
, we will also use a notion of convergence for functions
u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
toward a dimensionally reduced function
u\in L^{1}(\omega)
. It is customary to proceed as follows
[
28
,
9
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Dimension-reduction convergence)
.

We say that
u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
(dimension-reduction) converge in
L^{p}
to
u\in L^{p}(\omega)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and we use the notation
u_{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\hbox{\rm\scriptsize DR }\,\,}}u
in
L^{p}
, if:

1)
the scaled functions
v_{\varepsilon}
defined by (
9
) converge in
L^{p}(\Omega)
to some
v=v(x^{\prime})
; that is, to some
v
is independent of
x_{d}
;

2)
the limit
u\in L^{p}(\omega)
is defined by the equality
u(x^{\prime})=v(x^{\prime})
.

We will use a similar terminology if the scaled functions
v_{\varepsilon}
converge to
v
in a different topology; in particular, in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
or in
L^{p}
-weak. Moreover, we will often use equivalently
v
and
u
to denote the limit, even though they formally belong to different function spaces.

In the case of thin films modeled by local energies in Sobolev spaces, this convergence is ensured by the following compactness result (see, for example,
[
6
]
, Section 14.1). The proof relies on a simple application of Fubini’s theorem, which allows one to decouple the “vertical” and “planar” components of the gradient.

Lemma 2.2

(Dimension-reduction compactness for local functionals)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
be a sequence with
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
, and suppose that

\sup_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx<+\infty.

Then, up to the addition of constants,
u_{\varepsilon}
is precompact; that is, there exists
c_{\varepsilon}
such that
u_{\varepsilon}+c_{\varepsilon}
is precompact, with respect to the dimension-reduction convergence in
L^{2}
, the limit
u
belongs to
H^{1}(\omega)
and

\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx.

2.2
Fractional Sobolev spaces

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded connected open set, the fractional Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\Omega)
are defined as the set of functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
such that their
Gagliardo seminorm

\lfloor u\rfloor_{s}(\Omega)=\bigg(\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy\bigg)^{1/2}

is finite (see
[
29
,
20
]
, to which we refer for notation and results).

The space
H^{1}(\Omega)
is a singular limit of the spaces
H^{s}(\Omega)
in the following sense.

Theorem 2.3

(Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu limit theorem
[
5
,
36
]
)
.

If
u_{s}
is a family of functions with
u_{s}\in H^{s}(\Omega)
and
\sup_{s}(1-s)\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{H^{s}(\Omega)}<+\infty
,
then, up to subsequences and the addition of constants,
u_{s}
converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
s\to 1
to a function
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, for
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
we have

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{s\to 1}(1-s)\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy=\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx,

with
\sigma_{d}
defined in (
8
). Furthermore, the limit holds also pointwise.

3
\Gamma
-limit in the vertical direction

We now start our analysis of the squared Gagliardo seminorms

\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy

by considering the scaling for which the asymptotic behaviour is governed by the ‘thin direction’
x_{d}
. To give a heuristic idea of this scaling,
we consider a function
u=u(x_{d})
and write the corresponding Gagliardo seminorm in terms of the function
v
defined by
v(x_{d})=u(\varepsilon x_{d})
. After the change of variables
x=\varepsilon z
and
y=\varepsilon w
, we have

\lfloor u\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon^{d-2s}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(v(z_{d})-v(w_{d}))^{2}\Big(\int_{\frac{\omega}{\varepsilon}\times\frac{\omega}{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|z-w|^{d+2s}}dz^{\prime}dw^{\prime}\Big)dz_{d}dw_{d}.

(10)

With
z_{d}-w_{d}\neq 0
fixed, using the change of variable
\xi=z^{\prime}-w^{\prime}
and taking into account the integrability of the kernel, we estimate

\int_{\frac{\omega}{\varepsilon}\times\frac{\omega}{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|z-w|^{d+2s}}dz^{\prime}dw^{\prime}\sim\frac{|\omega|}{\varepsilon^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{1}{(|z_{d}-w_{d}|^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}d\xi.

We can then integrate out the dependence on
|z_{d}-w_{d}|
by the change of variable
\xi=|z_{d}-w_{d}|\zeta
, which gives

\int_{\frac{\omega}{\varepsilon}\times\frac{\omega}{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|z-w|^{d+2s}}dz^{\prime}dw^{\prime}\sim\left(\frac{|\omega|}{\varepsilon^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{1}{(1+|\zeta|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}d\zeta\right)\frac{1}{|z_{d}-w_{d}|^{1+2s}}.

Plugging this relation back into (
10
) we obtain

\lfloor u\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\ \sim\ \varepsilon^{1-2s}|\omega|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{1}{(1+|\zeta^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}d\zeta^{\prime}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(v(z_{d})-v(w_{d}))^{2}}{|z_{d}-w_{d}|^{1+2s}}dz_{d}dw_{d}.

(11)

This formula suggests that

i) the first scaling is
\varepsilon^{1-2s}
;

ii) the limit can be expressed as the integral over
\omega
of the one-dimensional Gagliardo

s
-seminorms along the vertical sections, multiplied by an appropriate constant.;

iii) The coefficient is given by a hypergeometric integral that encodes the interaction between the planar and the vertical variables.

To justify this argument, we first establish a preliminary result that allows us to extend the previous reasoning to functions depending also on the planar variable. More precisely, we prove matching lower and upper bounds for the

s
-seminorm in terms of the integral of the one-dimensional

s
-seminorm taken along the thin direction.

In the following, for
\tau>0
small enough, we define the sets

\omega_{\tau}=\{x^{\prime}\in\omega:\ \hbox{\rm dist}(x^{\prime},\partial\omega)>\tau\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\ \Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\tau}=\omega_{\tau}\times(0,\varepsilon).

Lemma 3.1

(Estimate of the seminorm in the thin direction)
.

Let
s_{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
, and let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence in
H^{s_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})

such that the following properties hold:

\bullet
(equiboundedness)
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}=L_{\infty}<+\infty
;

\bullet
(equi-planar Lipschitz condition) there exists
L>0
such that for all
\varepsilon>0

|u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},x_{d})|\leq L|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\ \ \hbox{\rm for all }x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\omega,x_{d}\in(0,\varepsilon).

Let
\tau>0
and define

V_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}):=\int_{\omega_{\tau}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}.

(12)

For every
\eta>0

\displaystyle(1+\eta)[u_{\varepsilon}]_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})+\frac{\varepsilon\phi(\varepsilon)}{\eta}

\displaystyle\geq C_{s_{\varepsilon},d}\,V_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}),

(13)

\displaystyle[u_{\varepsilon}]_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\tau})-\frac{\varepsilon\phi(\varepsilon)}{\eta}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\eta)C_{s_{\varepsilon},d}\,V_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}).

(14)

The constant

C_{s,d}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}\,d\xi.

(15)

Moreover, the function

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\phi(\varepsilon)=0.

Proof.

\varepsilon\to 0

We extend

\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times(0,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon,\tau}(A)=\int_{\omega_{\tau}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{A}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{(|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{2}+|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dy^{\prime}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle I^{\rm v}_{\varepsilon,\tau}(A)=\int_{\omega_{\tau}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{A}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{(|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{2}+|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dy^{\prime}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle I^{\rm h}_{\varepsilon,\tau}(A)=\int_{\omega_{\tau}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{A}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},y_{d})-u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{(|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{2}+|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dy^{\prime}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}.

|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>\tau

.

Since the change of variable

(16)

for all

4
The dimension-reduction regimes

We now describe the behaviour of Gagliardo seminorms in the dimension-reduction regimes. We first determine two different scaling laws for
s<1/2
and
s>1/2
, and then prove dimension-reduction compactness, in the first case with respect to a weak convergence, and in the second case with respect to a strong convergence, and compute the
\Gamma
-limits with respect to those convergences. The case
s=1/2
is critical and is obtained as a limit case of both other regimes.

4.1
Determination of the scalings

The heuristic argument used to identify the next scaling after

\varepsilon^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}
in the asymptotic analysis—namely,
the scaling at which a dimension-reduction effect appears—is the following.
Interactions between points at distance of order
\varepsilon
or smaller
can be regarded as genuinely
d
-dimensional, whereas interactions between
points at distance larger than
\varepsilon
are essentially
(d-1)
-dimensional.

These two types of interaction scale differently depending on whether

s>\tfrac{1}{2}
(the high-integrability regime) or
s<\tfrac{1}{2}

(the low-integrability regime). In the former case, the dominant
contributions come from interactions at scales not larger than

\varepsilon
, while in the latter case the dominant interactions
occur at scales greater than or equal to
\varepsilon
.

We now illustrate the heuristic argument, giving an estimate of the pointwise limit of
\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
when

u(x^{\prime},x_{d})=v(x^{\prime})
with
v
a Lipschitz function and
s_{\varepsilon}\to s_{0}
, where
s_{0}\in[0,1]
is fixed. We can limit our analysis to
v(x^{\prime})=x_{1}
. In order to give an upper bound, for fixed
x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
we can subdivide interactions inside the ball
B_{K\varepsilon}(x)
, where
K>1
is any constant, and those outside the cylinder
\{y\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}\ |\ |x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|<(K-1)\varepsilon\}
, since

\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset B_{K\varepsilon}(x)\times C_{(K-1)\varepsilon}(x),\qquad C_{(K-1)\varepsilon}(x)=\{y\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>(K-1)\varepsilon\}.

We then have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\!\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}(x)}\!\frac{|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dy\,\,\leq\,\,\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}-2}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}-2}}d\xi\,\,=\,\,C\int_{0}^{K\varepsilon}t^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CK^{2-2s_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{1-s_{\varepsilon}},

so that

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dy\leq CK^{2-2s_{\varepsilon}}|\omega|\frac{\varepsilon^{3-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{1-s_{\varepsilon}},

(25)

with
C
depending only on
d
. Note that the term
K^{2-2s_{\varepsilon}}
is largely suboptimal for
K
large.

As far as the interactions outside the cylinder are concerned, if
R
denotes the diameter of
\omega
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{C_{(K-1)\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{C_{(K-1)\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}-2}}dy

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\int_{\{(K-1)\varepsilon<|\xi|<R\}}\frac{1}{|\xi^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}-2}}d\xi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\int_{(K-1)\varepsilon}^{R}t^{-2s_{\varepsilon}}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\frac{((K-1)\varepsilon)^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-R^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}.

The high-integrability regime.

We now consider the case
s_{0}>1/2
,
so that, from (
26
), we have

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{C_{(K-1)\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dy\leq C(K-1)^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}|\omega|\frac{\varepsilon^{3-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1},

(27)

with
C
depending only on
d
, and, together with (
25
), that

\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\leq C|\omega|\varepsilon^{3-2s_{\varepsilon}}\Big(\frac{K^{2-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{1-s_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{(K-1)^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\Big).

(28)

On the other hand, we can give a lower bound by considering only
y\in B_{\varepsilon/4}(x)
for
x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
with dist
(x^{\prime},\partial\omega)>\varepsilon/2
and
x_{d}\in[\varepsilon/4,3\varepsilon/4]
, so that

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\int_{B_{\varepsilon/4}(x)\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dy=\int_{B_{\varepsilon/4}(x)}\frac{|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\int_{B_{\varepsilon/4}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}-2}}dy=\frac{1}{d}\int_{B_{\varepsilon/4}}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}-2}}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{d}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon/2}t^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}dt=C\frac{\varepsilon^{2-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{1-s_{\varepsilon}},

which shows that

\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\geq C|\omega|\frac{\varepsilon^{3-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{1-s_{\varepsilon}},

(29)

with
C
depending only on
d
.

Estimates (
28
) and (
29
) suggest that the relevant scaling of the energy be

1)
\varepsilon^{3-2s_{\varepsilon}}
for
s_{0}\in(1/2,1)

2)
\frac{\varepsilon^{3-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{1-s_{\varepsilon}}

for
s_{0}=1
.

The low-integrability regime.

We now turn to the case
s_{0}<1/2
,
in which the sign of the denominator in (
26
) changes, so that we have

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{C_{(K-1)\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dy\leq C\varepsilon^{2}|\omega|\frac{R^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}

(30)

with
C
depending only on
d
. This estimate, together with (
25
), implies that

\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\leq C|\omega|\Big(\varepsilon^{2}\frac{R^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon^{3-2s_{\varepsilon}}\frac{K^{2-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{1-s_{\varepsilon}}\Big)\leq C|\omega|R^{1-2s_{0}}\varepsilon^{2},

where this last constant
C
also depends on
s_{0}
.

To check that the scaling is sharp, with fixed
\delta>0
, for all
x^{\prime}\in\omega
with dist
(x^{\prime},\partial\omega)>\delta
we estimate

\int_{\{y\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:K\varepsilon<|x-y|<\delta\}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dy\geq C\varepsilon\int_{K\varepsilon}^{\delta}t^{-2s_{\varepsilon}}dt=C\varepsilon\frac{\delta^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}-(K\varepsilon)^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{1-2s_{\varepsilon}},

so that
\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\geq C\varepsilon^{2}
,
with this last constant also depending on
\delta
. Hence, in this regime the relevant scaling is
\varepsilon^{2}
.

The critical regime.
At
s=1/2
the interactions at scale up to
\varepsilon
and larger than any fixed
\delta>0
are negligible. As a consequence, the correct scaling turns out to be
\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|
, and the limit is local. Note that, compared with the other regimes, when
s=1/2
we have
\varepsilon^{2}=\varepsilon^{3-2s}
; however, at this scaling the functionals diverge. The
\Gamma
-limit is obtained by optimizing the arguments from the cases
s\neq 1/2
.

4.2
\Gamma
-limit in the high-integrability case

In the case where
s_{\varepsilon}\to s_{0}>\tfrac{1}{2}
, we prove a dimension-reduction theorem with respect to strong convergence in
L^{1}
for sequences of functions such that

\frac{1-s_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon^{3-2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})

is equibounded, even though the factor
1-s_{\varepsilon}
is actually redundant unless
s_{\varepsilon}\to 1
.
We show that the cluster points of such sequences belong to
H^{1}(\omega)
, and that the corresponding
\Gamma
-limit is a multiple of the Dirichlet integral.

4.2.1
Strong dimension-reduction compactness

Lemma
2.2

is based on the fact that, in the notation of Definition
2.1
,
we may write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx=\int_{\omega\times(0,1)}|\nabla^{\prime}v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\omega\times(0,1)}\Big|\frac{\partial v_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{d}}\Big|^{2}dx\geq\int_{\omega\times(0,1)}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx,

which at the same time proves compactness for
v_{\varepsilon}
in
H^{1}(\omega\times(0,1))
and that
\frac{\partial v_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{d}}
tends to
0
.
The decoupling of the “horizontal” and “vertical” derivatives is not straightforward for Gagliardo seminorms.
To overcome this difficulty, in the following result we adopt a discretization procedure for the Gagliardo seminorm, which allows us to construct sequences in local Sobolev spaces.
The scaling argument can then be applied to these sequences.

Theorem 4.1

(Non-local dimension-reduction compactness)
.

Let
s=s_{\varepsilon}\to s_{0}>1/2
and let
u_{\varepsilon}
be such that

\sup_{\varepsilon}\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\leq S<+\infty.

Then there exist
u\in H^{1}(\omega)
and a subsequence
\{u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\}_{j}
such that, up to the addition of constants,

u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\hbox{\rm\scriptsize DR }\,\,}}u
locally in
L^{1}
.

Proof.

To make the proof easier to follow, we divide it into several steps.

Step 1.

A first change of variables with a bound on the range of interactions.

We fix
r\in(0,1/2)
and, in view of the heuristic computation in Section
4.1
, we limit our analysis to interactions with
|x-y|<\varepsilon r
; that is, using the change of variables
\xi=y-x
, we estimate

\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\geq\int_{\{|\xi|<\varepsilon r\}}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{d+2s-2}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{r}}\frac{|u(x+\xi)-u(x)|^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}}dxd\xi,

(31)

where
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{r}=\{x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon})>r\varepsilon\}
.

Step 2.

Parameterization on the Stiefel manifold.

The inner integral in (
31
) can be interpreted as a difference quotient, which will yield suitable piecewise-affine interpolations.

Before addressing the general case
d\geq 2
, we first illustrate the strategy in the simpler case
d=2
. In this situation, each vector
\xi
can be completed to the orthogonal basis
\{\xi,\xi^{\perp}\}
. By symmetry, we obtain that

\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\geq\int_{\{|\xi|<\varepsilon r\}}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{d+2s-2}}F_{\varepsilon}^{\xi}(u_{\varepsilon})\,d\xi,

where

F_{\varepsilon}^{\xi}(u_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{r}}\frac{|u(x+\xi)-u(x)|^{2}+|u(x+\xi^{\perp})-u(x)|^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}}dx.

The functionals
F_{\varepsilon}^{\xi}
can be discretized on suitable square lattices oriented with
\xi
.

In dimension larger than two, the extension of
\xi
to an orthogonal basis is performed using the

set of orthonormal bases
(Stiefel manifold) of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

V:=\{\overline{\nu}=(\nu_{1},...,\nu_{d}):\nu_{j}\in S^{d-1}\text{ such that }\langle\nu_{i},\nu_{j}\rangle=0\text{ for }i\neq j\}.

We observe that
V
has Hausdorff dimension equal to
k_{d}:=d(d-1)/2
.

Following the notation in
[
38
, page 3445]
(see also
[
8
, Remark 5]
), we write
\xi=\varepsilon r\rho\,\nu_{n}
for some basis element
\nu_{n}
of
\overline{\nu}\in V
, and rewrite (
31
) as

\displaystyle\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{(\varepsilon r)^{2-2s}}{\mathcal{H}^{k_{d}}(V)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sigma_{d}}{\rho^{-1+2s}}\int_{V}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{r}}\frac{\bigl|u_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon r\rho\,\nu_{n})-u_{\varepsilon}(x)\bigr|^{2}}{|\varepsilon r\rho|^{2}}\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{k_{d}}(\overline{\nu})\,d\rho.

This follows from a standard change of variables; the same argument (with full details) is given in
[
8
, Section 3.1]
.

Step 3.

Discretization on lattices with lattice size up to
\varepsilon r
.

Given
\rho>0
and
\overline{\nu}\in V
, we define

\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}:=\{z_{1}\rho\nu_{1}+z_{2}\rho\nu_{2}+...+z_{d}\rho\nu_{d}:(z_{1},...,z_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}

and
Q_{\rho\overline{\nu}}
as the cube generated by the orthogonal basis
\{\rho\nu_{1},...,\rho\nu_{d}\}
. With fixed
\rho\in(0,1)
, we set

\mathcal{I}^{\varepsilon r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}(\Omega):=\{k\in\varepsilon r\mathbb{Z}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}^{d}:k+\varepsilon rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}\subset\subset\Omega\},

and for every
k\in\mathcal{I}^{\varepsilon r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}(\Omega)
we define

u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}(k)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\varepsilon r\rho|^{d}}\int_{k+\varepsilon rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}u_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

By Jensen’s inequality, we then have

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{r}}\sum_{n=1}^{d}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon r\rho\nu_{n})-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|\varepsilon r\rho|^{2}}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\varepsilon r\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}\sum_{n=1}^{d}|\varepsilon r\rho|^{d}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}(k+\nu_{n})-u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}(k)|^{2}}{|\varepsilon r\rho|^{2}}dx.

Step 4.

Estimate with piecewise-affine interpolations.

We still let
u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}
denote the piecewise-affine interpolation of the discrete function
u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}
defined on
\mathcal{I}^{\varepsilon r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}(\Omega)
, extended through a standard Kuhn decomposition (see, e.g.
[
8
, Remark 6]
). This function is well defined on a slightly smaller set of the form
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{cr}
for some
c
depending only on
d
. We then have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\varepsilon r\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}\sum_{n=1}^{d}|\varepsilon r\rho|^{d}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}(k+\nu_{n})-u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}(k)|^{2}}{|\varepsilon r\rho|^{2}}dx\geq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{cr}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}|^{2}dx.

From (
4.2.1
) we then obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{r^{2(1-s)}}{\varepsilon}\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}\int_{(0,1)\times V}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{cr}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}|^{2}dxd\mu_{\varepsilon},

(33)

where
\mu_{\varepsilon}
denotes the probability measure on
(0,1)\times V
defined as follows

d\mu_{\varepsilon}=\frac{2(1-s)}{\mathcal{H}^{k_{d}}(V)}\frac{1}{\rho^{2s-1}}d\mathcal{H}^{k_{d}}(\overline{\nu})d\rho.

Step 5.

Estimate with a sequence in
H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
.

We define the functions

u^{r}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{(0,1)\times V}u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}(x)d\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\overline{\nu}),

where we have highlighted the dependence on
r
of the interpolations, and note that

\nabla u^{r}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{(0,1)\times V}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}(x)d\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\overline{\nu})

in the sense of distributions.
An application of Jensen’s inequality in (
33
) then gives

\displaystyle\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{r^{2(1-s)}}{\varepsilon}\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{cr}}|\nabla u^{r}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx.

(34)

Step 6.

Compactness of comparison functions.

We can apply compactness Lemma
2.2
with
\omega
replaced by any
\omega^{\prime}
compactly contained in
\omega
and with
(0,1)
replaced by
(cr,1-cr)
. We then obtain that the scaled functions
v_{\varepsilon}^{r}(x)=u_{\varepsilon}^{r}(x^{\prime},\varepsilon x_{d})
, up to subsequences and addition of constants, converge in
L^{2}(\omega^{\prime}\times(cr,1-cr))
to a function
v^{r}(x)=u^{r}(x^{\prime})
with
u^{r}\in H^{1}(\omega^{\prime})
.

Step 7.

Compactness of the original sequence.

We have

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\omega^{\prime}\times(\varepsilon cr,\varepsilon(1-cr))}|u_{\varepsilon}-u^{r}_{\varepsilon}|dx\leq I^{1}_{\varepsilon}+I^{2}_{\varepsilon},

(35)

where

\displaystyle I^{1}_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{[0,1]\times V}\sum_{{k\in{\mathring{\mathcal{I}}}^{\varepsilon r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}\int_{k+{\varepsilon r}Q_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u^{\rho\overline{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}(k)|\,dx\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\overline{\nu})

\displaystyle I^{2}_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{[0,1]\times V}\sum_{{k\in{\mathring{\mathcal{I}}}^{\varepsilon r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}\int_{k+{\varepsilon r}Q_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|u^{\rho\overline{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}(x)-u^{\rho\overline{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}(k)|\,dx\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\overline{\nu}),

and

{\mathring{\mathcal{I}}}^{\varepsilon r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}(\Omega):=\{k\in\varepsilon r\mathbb{Z}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}^{d}:k+\varepsilon rcQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}\subset\subset\Omega\}.

To give a bound on
I^{1}_{\varepsilon}
and
I_{\varepsilon}^{2}
, we can proceed as in
[
8
, Section 3.1]
, using the refined lower estimate in (
4.2.1
).

Using a scaled Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality (see, e.g.

[
29
, Theorem 6.33]
), we have that

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\sum_{{k\in{\mathring{\mathcal{I}}}^{\varepsilon r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}}\int_{k+{\varepsilon r}Q_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}(k)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P|{\varepsilon r}\rho|^{\frac{d}{2}+s}\sum_{k\in{\mathring{\mathcal{I}}}^{\varepsilon r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\bigg(\int_{(k+{\varepsilon r}Q_{\rho\overline{\nu}})^{2}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dxdy\bigg)^{1/2},

where
P
is the Poincaré–Wirtinger constant for the
d
-dimensional unit cube. By this estimate,
using the concavity of the square root and that
\#{\mathring{\mathcal{I}}}^{\varepsilon r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\sim\frac{\varepsilon|\omega|}{\varepsilon^{d}\rho^{d}r^{d}}
, we then have

\displaystyle I^{1}_{\varepsilon}\leq P\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\varepsilon^{s}r^{s}\,2^{1-\frac{d}{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-s)\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\frac{1}{2-s}\ =Pr^{s}\varepsilon\sqrt{1-s}\,2^{1-\frac{d}{2}}|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{2-s}\sqrt{S}.

(36)

Regarding
I^{2}_{\varepsilon}
, we note that

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{k+{\varepsilon r}Q_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|u^{\rho\overline{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}(k)|\,dx\leq r\rho\sqrt{d}\int_{k+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|\nabla u^{\rho\overline{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}(x)|\,dx.

This implies, using (
4.2.1
), that

\displaystyle I^{2}_{\varepsilon}\leq r\sqrt{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\frac{d}{2^{d}}|\omega|\int_{[0,1]\times V}\sum_{k\in{\mathring{\mathcal{I}}}^{\varepsilon r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\int_{k+\varepsilon rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}(x)|^{2}dx\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\overline{\nu})}\leq\varepsilon\,r^{2s-1}\sqrt{\frac{d|\omega|}{2^{d}{c_{d}}}S}.

(37)

From (
35
), (
36
), and (
37
) we obtain that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u^{r}
in
L^{1}
. In particular, we see that
u^{r}
is independent of
r
, and that the convergence of the corresponding
v_{\varepsilon}
is in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\omega\times(0,1))
.
∎

Remark 4.2

(‘Sub-optimal’ lower bound)
.

Let
s=s_{\varepsilon}\to s_{0}>1/2
and let
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
L^{1}
, with
u\in H^{1}(\omega)
. Then from (
34
) and by the arbitrariness of
\omega^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\geq r^{2(1-s_{0})}(1-2cr)\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime}

(38)

for all
r\in(0,1/2c)
, where
c
is the dimensional constant in Step 4 of the proof of Theorem
4.1
. This lower bound will be improved in the case
s_{0}<1
.

4.2.2
A Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu-type result for
s\to 1^{-}

The lower bound obtained in the previous section turns out to be sharp in the case
s_{\varepsilon}\to 1
.
As a consequence, we have the following Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu-type result.

Theorem 4.3

(Dimension-reduction Gamma-limit for
s\to 1
)
.

Let
s=s_{\varepsilon}\to 1^{-}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Then we have

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\lfloor u\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime}

for all
u\in H^{1}(\omega)
, where the
\Gamma
-limit is computed with respect to the dimension-reduction convergence in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
.

Proof.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\hbox{\rm\scriptsize DR }\,\,}}u
with respect to the local-
L^{1}
dimension-reduction convergence. From Remark
4.2
we then obtain

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\geq(1-2cr)\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime},

which gives the desired lower bound by letting
r\to 0
.

As for the upper bound, by a density argument it suffices to consider
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\omega})
.
Let then
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\omega})
, and with an abuse of notation, let
u
also denote the function
u=u(x^{\prime})
, independent of the
d
-th variable, which we view as an element of
H^{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
.
We first note that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:|x-y|>r\varepsilon\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy=0

(39)

for all
r>0
.
Indeed, if
L
is such that
|u(x)-u(y)|\leq L|x-y|
, then,
noting that if
|x-y|\geq 2\varepsilon
then
|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}:|x-y|>r\varepsilon\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}:|x-y|>r\varepsilon\}}L^{2}|x-y|^{2-d-2s}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\{r\varepsilon<|\xi|<2\varepsilon\}}|\xi|^{2-d-2s}d\xi\,dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\{y\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>\varepsilon\}}|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{2-d-2s}dx\,dy\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S^{d-1})\varepsilon|\omega|\Big(\int_{r\varepsilon}^{2\varepsilon}t^{1-2s}dt\Big)^{+}+\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(S^{d-2})\varepsilon^{2}|\omega|\int_{2\varepsilon}^{+\infty}t^{-2s}dt\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{2(1-s)}((r\varepsilon)^{2-2s}-(2\varepsilon)^{2-2s})^{+}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2s-1}(2\varepsilon)^{1-2s}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}{1-s}(r^{2-2s}-2^{2-2s})^{+}+\varepsilon^{3-2s}\Big).

Note that we have implicitly used the fact that
s>1/2
.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:|x-y|>r\varepsilon\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy

(40)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big((r^{2-2s}-2^{2-2s})^{+}+1-s\big).

Letting
s\to 1
, we have (
39
).

From (
39
), we obtain that the asymptotic behaviour of
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}F_{\varepsilon,s}(u)
is the same as that
of

\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:|x-y|<r\varepsilon\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy

with truncated range of interactions.

We now simplify the asymptotic analysis when
|x-y|<r\varepsilon
. We can write

u(x)-u(y)=\langle\nabla u(x),x-y\rangle+O(|x-y|^{2})

uniformly in
x
, so that, with fixed
\eta>0

\displaystyle\big||u(x)-u(y)|^{2}-|\langle\nabla u(x),x-y\rangle|^{2}\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta|\langle\nabla u(x),x-y\rangle|^{2}+C_{\eta}|x-y|^{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta C|x-y|^{2}+C_{\eta}|x-y|^{4},

and

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt\bigg|\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:|x-y|<r\varepsilon\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy

(41)

\displaystyle-\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:|x-y|<r\varepsilon\}}\frac{|\langle\nabla u(x),x-y\rangle|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta C\varepsilon|\omega|\int_{|\xi|<r\varepsilon\}}{|\xi|^{2-d-2s}}d\xi+C_{\eta}\varepsilon|\omega|\int_{|\xi|<r\varepsilon\}}{|\xi|^{4-d-2s}}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\eta\frac{1}{1-s}\varepsilon^{3-2s}+C_{\eta}\varepsilon^{5-2s}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\frac{1}{1-s}\varepsilon^{3-2s}\Big(\eta+C_{\eta}(1-s)\varepsilon^{2}\Big).

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
first, by the arbitrariness of
\eta
and this estimate, together with (
39
), we also have that the asymptotic behaviour of
\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\lfloor u\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
is the same as that
of

\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:|x-y|<r\varepsilon\}}\frac{|\langle\nabla u(x),x-y\rangle|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy.

We now take
r<\frac{1}{2}
, so that

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:|x-y|<r\varepsilon\}}\frac{|\langle\nabla u(x),x-y\rangle|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\omega\times(r\varepsilon,(1-r)\varepsilon)}\int_{B_{r\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{|\langle\nabla u(x),x-y\rangle|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\omega\times(r\varepsilon,(1-r)\varepsilon)}\int_{B_{r\varepsilon}}\frac{|\langle\nabla u(x),\xi\rangle|^{2}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}\,d\xi\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\omega\times(r\varepsilon,(1-r)\varepsilon)}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\frac{1}{d}\int_{B_{r\varepsilon}}|\xi|^{2-d-2s}\,d\xi\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\omega\times(r\varepsilon,(1-r)\varepsilon)}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}c_{d}(r\varepsilon)^{2-2s}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-2r)r^{2-2s}\frac{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}{1-s}\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u(x^{\prime})|^{2}dx^{\prime}.

Between the third and fourth line of the previous formula, we have used the remark that, by the symmetry of the domain of integration, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r\varepsilon}}\frac{|\langle\nabla u(x),\xi\rangle|^{2}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}\,d\xi=|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\int_{B_{r\varepsilon}}\frac{|\langle e_{j},\xi\rangle|^{2}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}\,d\xi=|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\frac{1}{d}\int_{B_{r\varepsilon}}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}\,d\xi

for all elements of the canonical basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}
.
Hence,

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\lfloor u\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\geq(1-2r)\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u(x^{\prime})|^{2}dx^{\prime}

(42)

for all
r<\frac{1}{2}
. Conversely, for all
r>0
we have

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:|x-y|<r\varepsilon\}}\frac{|\langle\nabla u(x),x-y\rangle|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\omega\times(0,\varepsilon)}\int_{B_{r\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{|\langle\nabla u(x),x-y\rangle|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy\,dx=r^{2-2s}\frac{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}{1-s}\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u(x^{\prime})|^{2}dx^{\prime},

repeating the same computations as above, so that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}F_{\varepsilon,s}(u)\leq\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u(x^{\prime})|^{2}dx^{\prime}.

(43)

The desired limsup ineuality follows from (
42
) and (
43
) by letting
r\to 0
.∎

4.2.3
Convergence for
s_{0}\in(1/2,1)

We complete our computations of
\Gamma
-limits in the case
s_{0}>1/2
.

Theorem 4.4

(Dimension-reduction Gamma-limit in the super-critical regime)
.

Let
s=s_{\varepsilon}\to s_{0}\in(1/2,1)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Then we have

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3-2s_{\varepsilon}}}\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{1-s_{0}}K_{s_{0},d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime}

for all
u\in H^{1}(\omega)
, where the
\Gamma
-limit is computed with respect to the dimension-reduction convergence in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
, and

K_{s,d}=\frac{1}{(3-2s)(d-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}{(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}d\xi^{\prime}.

(44)

The form of the coefficient
K_{s,d}
highlights a combination of planar interactions, which give the integral in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, and of interactions in the vertical directions weighted by
s
, whose integral gives the coefficient
1/(3-2s)
.

Remark 4.5

(Comparison with
C_{s,d}
)
.

The coefficients
C_{s,d}
and
K_{s,d}
are related as follows

K_{s,d}=\frac{1}{(2s-1)(3-2s)}C_{s,d}.

(45)

For the sake of readability, the proof is postponed to the Appendix.

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

We use the notation

F^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\lfloor u\rfloor_{s}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})

Lower bound.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\hbox{\rm\scriptsize DR }\,\,}}u\in H^{1}(\omega)
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
. Up to restricting the lower bound to a slightly smaller thin film, we can suppose that the convergence of the functions
v_{\varepsilon}
, where
v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})=u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},\varepsilon x_{d})
, to
u
is in
L^{2}(\omega\times(0,\varepsilon))
. With fixed
K>0
for each
\omega^{\prime}
compact subset of
\omega
we can estimate

\displaystyle F^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}}\int_{\omega^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime}+\xi^{\prime},y_{d})-u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})|^{2}}{((x_{d}-y_{d})^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}dx^{\prime}dx_{d}dy_{d}d\xi^{\prime}

(46)

for
\varepsilon
small enough.
For every
\xi^{\prime}\neq 0
we introduce the probability measures

d\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\xi^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{C_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime})((x_{d}-y_{d})^{2}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}dx_{d}dy_{d},

where

C_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{((x_{d}-y_{d})^{2}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}dx_{d}dy_{d},

(47)

and the averaged functions

u^{\xi^{\prime}}_{\varepsilon}(z)=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(z,x_{d})d\mu_{\varepsilon}(x_{d},y_{d})=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(z,y_{d})d\mu_{\varepsilon}(x_{d},y_{d}).

Note that we have
u^{\xi^{\prime}}_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
L^{2}(\omega)
. From Jensen’s inequality we then deduce that

\displaystyle F^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}C_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime})\int_{\omega^{\prime}}\frac{|u^{\xi^{\prime}}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime}+\xi^{\prime})-u^{\xi^{\prime}}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})|^{2}}{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}dx^{\prime}d\xi^{\prime}.

We use the notation
\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\xi^{\prime}}
for the piecewise-affine interpolations on lattices aligned with the vector
\xi^{\prime}
(in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
instead of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
), we then obtain

\displaystyle F^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}(d-1)}\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}C_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime})\int_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}|\nabla^{\prime}\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\xi^{\prime}}|^{2}dx^{\prime}d\xi^{\prime},

(48)

up to restricting to a slightly smaller
\omega^{\prime\prime}
. Up to a further average, analogous to that in Step 5 of the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we can suppose that
\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\xi^{\prime}}\to u
weakly in
H^{1}(\omega^{\prime\prime})
,
so that

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}(d-1)}\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}C_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime}\int_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime}.

(49)

By a change of variable and Fubini’s Theorem, we can compute

\displaystyle\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}C_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}}\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}{((x_{d}-y_{d})^{2}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}d\xi^{\prime}dx_{d}dy_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{3-2s}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|t-\tau|^{1-2s}\int_{B_{\frac{K}{|t-\tau|}}}\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}{(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}d\xi^{\prime}dtd\tau.

Noting that, by Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem,

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\lim_{K\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|t-\tau|^{1-2s}\int_{B_{\frac{K}{|t-\tau|}}}\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}{(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}d\xi^{\prime}dtd\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|t-\tau|^{1-2s}dtd\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}{(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}d\xi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-s)(3-2s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}{(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}d\xi^{\prime},

we obtain

\lim_{K\to+\infty}\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}C_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime}=\frac{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}{(1-s)(3-2s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}{(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}d\xi^{\prime}.

(50)

Eventually, we then have the estimate

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{1}{1-s_{0}}K_{s_{0},d}\int_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime},

and we can finally let
\omega^{\prime\prime}
invade
\omega
.

Pointwise convergence and upper bound.

We show a pointwise convergence result for
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})
.

Note that, if we fix
K>0
arbitrary, then the contribution of the integral in the set
\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon K\}
is negligible as
\varepsilon\to 0
with respect to the contribution in the complementary set. Indeed,

\displaystyle\int_{\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon K\}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy\leq L^{2}|\omega|\int_{\{\varepsilon K\leq|\xi^{\prime}|\}}C_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime})|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}d\xi^{\prime},

where
L
is a Lipschitz constant for
u
in
\omega
, and
C_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime})
is defined in (
47
).
Since
C_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime})\leq\varepsilon^{2}|\xi^{\prime}|^{-d-2s}
and
s>\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\int_{\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon K\}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1-2s}}\int_{\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon K\}}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2-d-2s}d\xi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1-2s}}\sigma_{d-1}\int_{\varepsilon K}^{+\infty}t^{-2s}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\frac{1}{2s-1}\sigma_{d-1}K^{1-2s},

where
C>0
does not depend on
K
,
\varepsilon
and
s
.
Since
K
can be chosen arbitrarily large and
1-2s<0
, we obtain that we can consider the
s
-seminorm only in the set
\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|<\varepsilon K\}
.
Then, we can estimate

\displaystyle{F_{\varepsilon}^{s}(u)}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}}\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}}C_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime})\int_{\omega}|u(x^{\prime}+\xi^{\prime})-u(x^{\prime})|^{2}dx^{\prime}d\xi^{\prime}+o(1)_{K\to+\infty},

obtaining by symmetry

{F_{\varepsilon}^{s}(u)}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3-2s}(d-1)}\int_{B_{K\varepsilon}}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}C_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime}\int_{\omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx^{\prime}+o(1)_{K\to+\infty}.

(51)

Since (
50
) holds, the upper bound follows
by letting
K\to+\infty
in (
51
), and recalling the lower-bound estimate we obtain the pointwise convergence of
F_{\varepsilon}^{s}(u)
.
∎

We note that, after scaling by
1-s_{0}

4.3
The critical scaling
s=1/2

The behaviour at
s=1/2
can be described by refining the case
s_{\varepsilon}>1/2
, which must be adjusted with a logarithmic correction. The corresponding result is the following.

Theorem 4.7

(Dimension-reduction Gamma-limit in the critical regime)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
be such that

\sup_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{1/2}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})<+\infty.

Then, up to subsequences and addition of constants,
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
converges to
u\in H^{1}(\omega)
with respect to the dimension-reduction convergence in
L^{2}
-weak.
Furthermore, we have

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\lfloor u\rfloor_{1/2}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{d-1}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime}

for all
u\in H^{1}(\omega)
, where the
\Gamma
-limit is computed with respect to the dimension-reduction convergence in
L^{2}
-weak.

Proof.

We now use the notation

F^{1/2}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\lfloor u\rfloor_{1/2}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+1}}dxdy.

In order to prove the equi-coerciveness of
F^{s}_{\varepsilon}
and the lower bound we follow the line of the proof of Theorem
4.4
. In this case, though, the relevant interactions are between the scale
\varepsilon
and the scale
1
.
We then modify the argument in the proof of Theorem
4.4
accordingly. In particular, inequality (
46
) can be changed to

\displaystyle F^{1/2}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{B_{\delta}\setminus B_{K\varepsilon}}\int_{\omega^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime}+\xi^{\prime},y_{d})-u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})|^{2}}{((x_{d}-y_{d})^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}dx^{\prime}dx_{d}dy_{d}d\xi^{\prime}

(53)

for
\varepsilon
small enough, where
\delta>0
is any fixed positive number not exceeding the distance of
\omega^{\prime}
from
\partial\omega
.
The arguments following (
46
) in the proof of Theorem
4.4
can then be repeated word for word, arguing that, up to subsequences and addition of constants we can suppose that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
with
u\in H^{1}(\omega^{\prime\prime})
. Correspondingly, inequality (
49
) becomes

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F^{1/2}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|(d-1)}C_{\varepsilon,K,\delta}\int_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime},

(54)

where

C_{\varepsilon,K,\delta}=\int_{B_{\delta}\setminus B_{K\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}{((x_{d}-y_{d})^{2}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}dx_{d}dy_{d}d\xi^{\prime}

in analogy with (
47
).
Changing variables, we obtain

C_{\varepsilon,K,\delta}=\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{\delta/(\varepsilon|x_{d}-y_{d}|)}\setminus B_{K/|x_{d}-y_{d}|}}\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}{(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}d\xi^{\prime}dx_{d}dy_{d}.

For fixed
t\neq 0
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta/(\varepsilon t)}\setminus B_{K/t}}\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}{(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}d\xi^{\prime}}{\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta/(\varepsilon t)}\setminus B_{K/t}}\frac{1}{|\xi^{\prime}|^{d-1}}d\xi^{\prime}}=1;

hence, in (
54
) we can substitute
C_{\varepsilon,K,\delta}
with

\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{\delta/(\varepsilon|x_{d}-y_{d}|)}\setminus B_{K/|x_{d}-y_{d}|}}\frac{1}{|\xi^{\prime}|^{d-1}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi^{\prime}dx_{d}dy_{d}=\sigma_{d-1}(S^{d-2})\varepsilon^{2}(\log\delta-\log\varepsilon-\log K).

Using this expression in (
54
), we obtain

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F^{1/2}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{d-1}\int_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime}.

The liminf inequality then follows by letting
\omega^{\prime\prime}
tend to
\omega
.

In order to prove the upper bound, as usual, it suffices to show it for piecewise-affine functions and argue by density. Furthermore, since the
\Gamma
-limit is local, it suffices to construct a recovery sequence for an affine map
u
. Thanks to its Lipschitz continuity, for fixed
K>0
we have

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\leq K\varepsilon\}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+1}}dxdy\leq C\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}.

Hence, we can limit ourselves to considering pairs with
|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>K\varepsilon
, for which the computation is the same as that performed for the lower bound.
∎

Remark 4.8

(asymptotic behaviour as
s_{\varepsilon}\to 1/2
)
.

The asymptotic analysis described in the previous theorem can be extended to
s_{\varepsilon}\to 1/2
as

. In this case, the correct scaling depends on

and the scalings
\varepsilon^{2}/(\frac{1}{2}-s_{\varepsilon})
for

and
\varepsilon^{3-2s_{\varepsilon}}/(s_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2})

s_{\varepsilon}>1/2

[
35
]
for a similar analysis in the context of fractional phase transitions (see also
[
37
,
39
]
).

4.4
\Gamma
-limit in the low-integrability case

In the case
s_{\varepsilon}\to s_{0}<1/2
we only have a dimension-reduction compactness result for the weak
L^{2}
convergence. The corresponding
\Gamma
-limits are still given by lower-dimensional Gagliardo seminorms.

4.4.1
A weak dimension-reduction compactness result

We prove a dimension-reduction compactness result with respect to the weak
L^{2}
convergence. It is based on the following general slicing result valid for all
s\in(0,1)
. The lemma is a version adapted to thin films of part of a slicing result in
[
29
, Lemma 6.35]
, following the line of the proof therein. It provides an estimate only for the ‘vertical’ part of the seminorm, a complete slicing result not being available. Note that the hypothesis that
\omega
being bounded is not necessary, but some regularity of its boundary is used.

Lemma 4.9

(Slicing on the ‘thin’ direction)
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
,
s\in(0,1)
, and
u\in H^{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
we have

\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

(55)

Proof.

Given
x^{\prime}\in\omega
and
x_{d}\neq y_{d}\in(0,\varepsilon)
, we consider the ball
B=B(x^{\prime},x_{d},y_{d})
centered at
z^{\ast}=(x^{\prime},\frac{x_{d}+y_{d}}{2})
with radius
\frac{|x_{d}-y_{d}|}{4}
.
Note that, for
\varepsilon
small enough (with respect to the dimensions of
\omega
),

|B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}|\geq c_{\omega}|B|
with
c_{\omega}>0
by the Lipschitz assumption on
\omega
. Hence,

\frac{|u(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\leq\frac{2}{c_{\omega}|B|}\int_{B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u(z)|^{2}+|u(z)-u(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\,dz.

By Fubini’s Theorem and by exchanging the role of
x_{d}
and
y_{d}
in second term of the sum, we then get

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\chi_{B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(z)\frac{1}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{d+1+2s}}\,dy_{d}\,dx\,dz,

(56)

with
C>0
only depending on
c_{\omega}
and the dimension
d
.
If
\chi_{B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(z)=1
, then
|z-z^{\ast}|\leq\frac{|x_{d}-y_{d}|}{4}
, and we can estimate

|x-z|\leq|z-z^{\ast}|+|x-z^{\ast}|\leq\frac{|x_{d}-y_{d}|}{4}+\frac{|x_{d}-y_{d}|}{2};

We obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\chi_{B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(z)\frac{1}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{d+1+2s}}\,dy_{d}\leq 2\int_{\frac{4|x-z|}{3}}^{+\infty}t^{-d-1-2s}\,dt=\frac{2}{d+2s}\Big(\frac{3}{4}\Big)^{d+2s}\frac{1}{|x-z|^{d+2s}}.

From (
4.4.1
), the claim follows with
C>0
depending only on
\omega
and the dimension
d
.
∎

Note that, in terms of the scaled function
v\colon\omega\times(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

v(x^{\prime},t)=u(x^{\prime},\varepsilon t)
, estimate (
55
) becomes

\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|v(x^{\prime},x_{d})-v(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}\leq C\varepsilon^{2s-1}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

(57)

Remark 4.10

(Rigidity)
.

Lemma
4.9
implies a rigidity result for ‘pointwise’ convergence.
More precisely, given
u\colon\omega\times(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
and defining

u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})=u(x^{\prime},\frac{x_{d}}{\varepsilon})
,
by (
57
) we obtain

\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}\leq C\varepsilon^{2s-1}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}).

Then, if
\varepsilon^{2s-1}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=o(1)_{\varepsilon\to 0}
, the function
u
does not depend on the
d
-th variable; that is,
u(x^{\prime},x_{d})=v(x^{\prime})
.

Lemma
4.9
allows us to prove a compactness result, in the sense of the weak dimension-reduction convergence in
L^{2}
(see Definition
2.1
).
We start by showing a compactness result for sequences bounded in
L^{2}
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
be a bounded sequence in
L^{2}(\omega\times(0,1))
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=0.

Then
there exists
u\in L^{2}(\omega)
such that, up to the addition of constants and up to subsequences,

u_{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\hbox{\rm\scriptsize DR }\,\,}}u
weakly in
L^{2}
in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

Proof.

Up to the addition of constants and up to subsequences, we have the weak convergence
v_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup v
.
To show that
v
does not depend on the ‘thin’ variable, we apply the Poincaré inequality and Lemma
4.9
, obtaining

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{1}|v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})-\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{d}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})-v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}

(58)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\ \leq\ C\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}+1}=o(1)_{\varepsilon},

where
\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{1}v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},t)\,dt
. Since
\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\overline{v}
in
L^{2}(\omega)
,
where
\overline{v}(x^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{1}v(x^{\prime},t)\,dt
, by (
58
) and the lower semicontinuity of the norm we find that the weak limit
v
does not depend on
x_{d}
.
∎

Theorem 4.12

(Dimension-reduction compactness)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
be such that

\sup_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})<+\infty.

Then
there exists
u\in L^{2}(\omega)
such that, up to the addition of constants and up to subsequences,

u_{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\hbox{\rm\scriptsize DR }\,\,}}u
weakly in
L^{2}
in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

Proof.

By the Poincaré inequality (see
[
29
, Theorem 6.33]
), we have

\|u_{\varepsilon}-\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}),

where
\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{|\Omega_{\varepsilon}|}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx
.
Then

\|v_{\varepsilon}-\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega\times(0,1))}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}),

which is bounded by assumption. The conclusion follows from Lemma
4.11
.
∎

4.4.2
Increase of integrability in dimension reduction

Lemma
4.12
suggests the scaling for a dimension reduction limit of the Gagliardo seminorms. This is confirmed by the following theorem, which shows a gain of
1/2
in the Gagliardo seminorm.

Theorem 4.13

(Dimension-reduction Gamma-limit in the sub-critical regime)
.

Let
s=s_{\varepsilon}\to s_{0}<\frac{1}{2}
; then the
\Gamma
-limit with respect to the dimension-reduction convergence in
L^{2}
of

F_{\varepsilon}^{s}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dxdy

(59)

is given on
L^{2}(\omega)
by the
H^{\frac{1}{2}+s_{0}}
-seminorm squared in
\omega
; that is, noting that

d-1+2(\frac{1}{2}+s_{0})=d+2s_{0}
, by

F^{s_{0}}(u)=\int_{\omega}\int_{\omega}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{0}}}dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}.

Proof.

Lower bound.

By compactness, the limit is independent of
x_{d}
.
If
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
; that is,
v_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}(\omega\times(0,1))
, then for all fixed
\delta>0
and
s=s_{\varepsilon}\to 0

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}^{s}(u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>\delta\}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{(\omega\times\omega)\cap\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>\delta\}}\frac{|v_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{(\omega\times\omega)\cap\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>\delta\}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{0}}}dxdy

The lower bound is optimized by letting
\delta\to 0
.

Upper bound.
For
u
Lipschitz, note that

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|<\delta\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big(\int_{(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap\{|x-y|<2\varepsilon\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int_{(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap\{2\varepsilon<|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|<\delta\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big(\int_{(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap\{|x-y|<2\varepsilon\}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d-2+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{(\omega\times\omega)\cap\{2\varepsilon<|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|<\delta\}}\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d-2+2s}}dxdy\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big(\varepsilon\int_{0}^{2\varepsilon}t^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}dt+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}t^{-2s_{\varepsilon}}dt\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\varepsilon^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}+\delta^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}\big),

while

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>\delta\}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int_{(\omega\times\omega)\cap\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>\delta\}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{0}}}dxdy.

Hence,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}^{s}(u)\leq\int_{\omega\times\omega}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{0}}}dxdy,

by the arbitrariness of
\delta
.

For
u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+s_{0}}(\omega)

∎

Remark 4.14

(Beyond Gagliardo seminorms)

, with the same proof. If

4.5
Asymptotic behaviour for
s_{0}\to 1/2

We now analyze the behaviour of the
\Gamma
-limits obtained for
s_{0}\neq 1/2
at the point

. We recall that, for
s_{0}<1/2
and
s_{\varepsilon}\to s_{0}
, we have

F^{s_{0}}(u)=\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\int_{\omega}\int_{\omega}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{0}}}dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}.

For

and

F^{s_{0}}(u)=\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3-2s_{\varepsilon}}}\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{1-s_{0}}K_{s_{0},d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime}.

Proposition 4.15

(Blow-up at the critical regime)
.

Let
F^{s_{0}}
be defined as above for
s_{0}\neq 1/2
. Then we have

\lim_{s_{0}\to 1/2}\Big|s_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\Big|F^{s_{0}}(u)=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2(d-1)}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime}.

Proof.

For the right-hand side limit we only have to compute the asymptotic behaviour of
K_{s_{0},d}
.
For the sake of notational simplicity, we rename
s=s_{0}
,
and check that

\lim_{s\to 1/2^{+}}(2s-1)K_{s,d}=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2(d-1)}.

For
s\to 1/2^{+}
the behaviour of
K_{s,d}
is the same as that of

\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2(d-1)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{d}}{(1+z^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}dz.

We directly estimate this integral. A less direct way would be to resort to its representation in terms of the function
\Gamma
.

Let
N\geq 1
be fixed.
Then, for all
z\geq N

\Big(\frac{N^{2}}{N^{2}+1}\Big)^{\frac{d}{2}+s}z^{-2s}\leq\frac{z^{d}}{(1+z^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}\leq z^{-2s},

and we estimate

\Big(\frac{N^{2}}{N^{2}+1}\Big)^{\frac{d}{2}+s}\frac{N^{1-2s}}{2s-1}\leq\int_{N}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{d}}{(1+z^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}\,dz\leq\frac{N^{1-2s}}{2s-1}.

Since
N
is arbitrary, it follows that

\lim_{s\to\frac{1}{2}+}(2s-1)\int_{N}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{d}}{(1+z^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}\,dz=1.

Since the integral in
(0,N)
is bounded by
N^{d+1}
independently of
s
, we can conclude.

If
s_{0}<1/2
then
F^{s_{0}}(u)=\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s_{0}+\frac{1}{2}}(\omega)
. From Theorem

2.3
in the
d-1
setting, we have

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s}(\omega)=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2(d-1)}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime}.

We can conclude by taking
s=s_{0}+\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

The case

It is worth noting that for

This is due to the increase of differentiability in the dimension-reduction process. We briefly state and proof this fact in the following result.

Theorem 4.16
.

The limit dimension-reduction
\Gamma
-convergence theorems in the previous sections hold with respect to the corresponding dimension-reduction convergences of
u_{\varepsilon}
to
u
locally in
\omega
.

Proof.

The lower bound is achieved by the trivial estimate

\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\geq\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s}((\omega\cap B_{T})\times(0,\varepsilon)).

We can then apply the corresponding theorem replacing

Conversely, to prove the upper bound, by density, it suffices to consider functions

. Let

be such that the support of

\displaystyle\int_{B_{T}\times(0,\varepsilon)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\setminus B_{T})\times(0,\varepsilon)}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sigma_{d-1}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\omega}|u(x^{\prime})|^{2}dx^{\prime}\int_{T}^{+\infty}t^{-2-2s}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{d-1}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\omega}|u(x^{\prime})|^{2}dx^{\prime}\frac{1}{(1+2s)T^{1+2s}}.

If

7
Concluding remarks

We have examined the behaviour of Gagliardo seminorms in
H^{s}
on thin films of thickness
\varepsilon
. The interplay between thickness and the derivation exponent
s
results in a variety of limiting behaviour at the dimension-reduction scaling. For low-integrability regimes
s<1/2
, the relevant interactions are those at finite distance and the energy scaling is
\varepsilon^{2}
. After rescaling, the limit energy is still a Gagliardo seminorm of the same form. Due to the reduced dimension of the space, this form highlights an effective gain of
1/2
in the differentiability exponent. At the critical exponent
s=1/2
the energy scales as
\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|
, while for
1/2<s<1
it scales as
\varepsilon^{3-2s}
. The corresponding rescaling acts as if producing a Bourgain-Brezis-Mironescu kernel leading to a dimensionally-reduced Dirichlet integral in the limit. The same holds as
s\to 1
after scaling by the singular factor
1-s
.

We finally make some comments in the direction of the derivation of lower-dimensional elasticity theories in the spirit of Le Dret and Raoult
[
28
]
. The present analysis can be compared with a recent work on the derivation of variational membrane models in the context of anisotropic nonlocal hyperelasticity in
[
21
]
. There, nonlocal gradients with anisotropic kernels are considered, and the behaviour of the limit functionals depends on suitable dimensionally reduced nonlocal or local gradients according to the scaling of the anisotropic kernels with
\varepsilon
. In contrast to our case, the possible behaviour is not determined by
s
. Conversely, for given convolution kernels, which are related to the Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu approach (see
[
2
]
), the dimensionally reduced theories are always local. In that case, the scaling of the energies depends on the ratio between the scaling of the kernel and the thickness of the film (see
[
3
]
).

C_{s,d}
and

Proposition 8.1

(Alternative expression for
C_{s,d}
(Proposition
3.2
))
.

We have

\displaystyle C_{s,d}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}\,d\xi=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+s)}\,\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}=\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+s)}.

Proof.

In order to carry out the computation, we introduce the quantity

J_{d}^{s}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{d-2}}{(1+z^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}\,dz<+\infty.

Integrating in radial coordinates, we obtain

C_{s,d}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}\,d\xi=\sigma_{d-1}J_{d}^{s}.

For all
d\geq 4
and
s\in(0,1)
a simple integration by parts provides the iterative relation

J^{s}_{d}=\frac{d-3}{d-2+2s}J^{s}_{d-2}.

(64)

To obtain an explicit formula for
J^{s}_{d}
, we then have to compute the products
(d-2+2s)(d-4+2s)\cdots(2s)
if
d
is even, and
(d-2+2s)(d-4+2s)\cdots(1+2s)
if
d
is odd. By recursively using the property of the Euler
\Gamma
-function that
\Gamma(1+\alpha)=\alpha\Gamma(\alpha)
,
it follows that

\displaystyle(d-2+2s)(d-4+2s)\cdots(2s)=2^{\frac{d}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(s)}

if
d
is even

\displaystyle(d-2+2s)(d-4+2s)\cdots(1+2s)=2^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+s)}\ \

\displaystyle\hbox{\rm if $d$ is odd}.

Applying the previous formula with
d-2
in place of
d
and
s=\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(d-3)!!=2^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\,\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}\qquad\text{if $d$ is even},

\displaystyle(d-3)!!=2^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\,\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(1)}\qquad\text{if $d$ is odd}.

here
(d-3)!!
denotes the product of all the positive integers up to
d-3
that have the same parity (odd or even) as
d-3
.
Hence, recalling that
\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})=\sqrt{\pi}
and
\Gamma(1)=1
, we obtain the formula

J_{d}^{s}=\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d-1}{2})\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+s)}\,2sJ^{s}_{2}&\hbox{\rm if $d$ even}\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d-1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+s)}\,(2s+1)J^{s}_{3}&\hbox{\rm if $d$ odd.}\end{cases}

It remains to compute

\displaystyle J^{s}_{2}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{(1+z^{2})^{1+s}}\,dz=\sqrt{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+s)}{2\Gamma(1+s)},\quad J^{s}_{3}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{z}{(1+z^{2})^{1+s}}\,dz=\frac{1}{1+2s},

which give the explicit formula for
J_{d}^{s}

J_{d}^{s}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d-1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+s)}

(65)

in terms of the function
\Gamma
.
Recalling that
\sigma_{d-1}=2\frac{\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{d-1}{2})}
and using (
65
) we can write

C_{s,d}=\sigma_{d-1}J_{d}^{s}=\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+s)}.

and, using the relation
\frac{\sigma_{d}}{\sigma_{d-1}}=\frac{d}{d-1}\ \frac{\Gamma(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)}\,\sqrt{\pi}
,
we also have

C_{s,d}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+s)}\,\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d},

and the proof is completed.
∎

Remark 8.2

(Relation between
K_{s,d}
and
C_{s,d}
(Remark
4.5
))
.

The coefficients
C_{s,d}
and
K_{s,d}
are related by

K_{s,d}=\frac{1}{(2s-1)(3-2s)}C_{s,d}.

We write

K_{s,d}=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{(3-2s)(d-1)}I_{s}^{d},\hbox{ where }I_{d}^{s}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{d}}{(1+z^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}\,dz.

Since an iterative formula corresponding to (
64
) also holds for
I_{s}^{d}
with
d-1
in the place of
d-3
; that is,

I^{s}_{d}=\frac{d-1}{d-2+2s}I^{s}_{d-2}

for all
d\geq 2
and
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1),
following the computations in Proposition
8.1
, we obtain

I_{s}^{d}=\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{d-1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d-1}{2})\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+\frac{d}{2})}\,I^{s}_{0}=\frac{d-1}{4}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d-1}{2}))}{\Gamma(s+\frac{d}{2})}\,\Gamma(s-\frac{1}{2})&\hbox{\rm if $d$ even}\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{d-1}{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d-1}{2})\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(s+\frac{d}{2})}\,I^{s}_{1}=\frac{d-1}{4}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d-1}{2})}{\Gamma(s+\frac{d}{2})}\,\frac{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}{s-\frac{1}{2}}&\hbox{\rm if $d$ odd.}\end{cases}

Noting that
(s-\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(s-\frac{1}{2})=\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})
, we have the following representation formula for
K_{s,d}
in terms of the Gamma function

K_{s,d}=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2(2s-1)(3-2s)}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d-1}{2})\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(s+\frac{d}{2})}.

Recalling the expression of the constant
C_{s,d}
in (
24
),
the desired relation
follows for all
d\geq 2
and
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
.

Remark 8.3
.

We have

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}K_{s,d}=\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2d}.

We can check this directly from the definition of
K_{s,d}
, without using its rewriting in Remark
8.2
. We write

K_{s,d}=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{(3-2s)(d-1)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{d}}{(1+z^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}dz=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{(3-2s)(d-1)}I_{d}^{1},

and note that

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}K_{s,d}=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{d-1}I^{1}_{d}.

(66)

The general recursive formula obtained by integration by parts
in the case
s=1
simply reduces to

I^{1}_{d}=\frac{d-1}{d}I_{d-2}
,
so that

I^{1}_{d}=\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{(d-1)!!}{d!!}I^{1}_{0}=\frac{(d-1)!!}{d!!}\frac{\pi}{2}&\hbox{\rm if $d$ even}\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{(d-1)!!}{d!!}I^{1}_{1}=\frac{(d-1)!!}{d!!}&\hbox{\rm if $d$ odd.}\end{cases}

Recalling the expression

\frac{\sigma_{d}}{\sigma_{d-1}}=\frac{d}{d-1}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)}\sqrt{\pi}=\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{d}{d-1}\frac{(d-1)!!}{d!!}\pi&\hbox{\rm if $d$ even}\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{2d}{d-1}\frac{(d-1)!!}{d!!}&\hbox{\rm if $d$ odd,}\end{cases}

used in the proof of Proposition
3.2
,
we then have

I^{1}_{d}=\frac{d-1}{2d}\frac{\sigma_{d}}{\sigma_{d-1}}

for all
d\geq 2
.
Hence, recalling (
66
), we complete the proof.
