 ABSTRACT

The BOILER project, sponsored by the Nuclear Science User Facilities program, evaluates the effects of
neutron irradiation and liquid Pb exposure on GA05-20Ni and GA05-25Ni alumina-forming austenitic
steels. Twenty-four SS-J3 tensile specimens and 16 SiC temperature monitors were irradiated in eight
High Flux Isotope Reactor (HFIR) rabbit capsules at nominal target temperatures of 400 and 650 °C.

During FY 2026, initial mechanical-testing and in-cell post-irradiation examination activities were
completed in support of the project. The eight capsules were transferred to Building 3025E and
disassembled, and all 24 tensile specimens and 16 SiC temperature monitors were recovered and
inventoried. The temperature monitors were transferred to the Low Activation Materials Development
and Analysis Laboratory (LAMDA), photographed, sectioned, and evaluated using dilatometry and
differential scanning calorimetry when sufficient material was available. Analysis of the thermometry
data remains in progress and will establish the conditions for elevated-temperature tensile testing.

The tensile specimens were photographed, surveyed for dose rate, and subjected to an extended chemical-
cleaning process to reduce residual Pb. Pre-cleaning and final post-cleaning mass measurements were
completed using a calibrated in-cell balance. Vickers hardness testing was subsequently completed in
Building 3025E on all 24 irradiated tensile specimens. Thermometry and hardness results are not
presented in this milestone report, and the reported mass measurements are not interpreted.

The remaining experimental activity is tensile testing following finalization of the irradiation-temperature
determination. After testing, selected specimen sections will be shipped to LAMDA for microscopy and
additional characterization.

 1. INTRODUCTION AND FY 2026 SCOPE

The BOILER experiment evaluates the response of pre-oxidized alumina-forming austenitic (AFA) steels
irradiated in contact with static liquid lead (Pb). The experiment includes two developmental alloy
compositions, GA05-20Ni and GA05-25Ni. Eight HFIR rabbit capsules, designated BLR01 through
BLR08, were fabricated and irradiated. Each capsule contained three SS-J3 tensile specimens and two
silicon carbide (SiC) temperature monitors, resulting in a total experimental inventory of 24 tensile
specimens and 16 temperature monitors. Each capsule was filled with nominally 16 g (1.4 cm3) of Pb.

The irradiation matrix included nominal capsule design temperatures of 400 °C and 650 °C and a target
irradiation dose of approximately 3 dpa. BLR01 through BLR04 contained GA05-20Ni specimens, while
BLR05 through BLR08 contained GA05-25Ni specimens. For each alloy, two capsules were designed for
irradiation at each nominal temperature. The capsule assignments and specimen matrix are summarized in
Table 1. Specimens designated with identifiers beginning G0 correspond to specimen material GA05-
20Ni while conversely beginning with G5 corresponds to specimen material GA05-25Ni.

 2. COMPLETION OF HFIR IRRADIATION

All eight BOILER capsules completed their planned HFIR irradiation exposure. Six capsules, BLR02
through BLR06 and BLR08, were irradiated during HFIR Cycle 511 and the three recorded portions of
Cycle 512. BLR01 and BLR07 entered HFIR one cycle later and were irradiated during Cycle 512 and
Cycle 513.

The HFIR EXSITE records system reports irradiation exposure in megawatt-days (MWD). The six capsules
irradiated during Cycles 511 and 512 accumulated 3,970.10 MWD over 47.081 recorded irradiation days.
BLR01 and BLR07 accumulated 4,064.04 MWD during Cycles 512 and 513 over 48.131 recorded
irradiation days. The capsule-specific exposures are provided in Table 2.

 3. CAPSULE TRANSFER, DISASSEMBLY, AND SPECIMEN RECOVERY

Following completion of irradiation, all eight BOILER capsules were transferred from HFIR to Building
3025E during the first quarter of FY 2026. Capsule-opening preparations included review of the planned
work sequence, configuration of the in-cell equipment, and completion of the required pre-job briefing.
Disassembly of all eight capsules and recovery of their experimental contents were completed during the
second quarter of FY 2026.

 3

The disassembly process consisted of five primary steps. First, the outer rabbit housing was cut using an
in-cell low-speed saw and removed to expose the internal molybdenum (Mo) holder. The Mo holder was
then heated to melt the Pb and relocate it away from the specimen-containing end. After the Pb had
shifted and resolidified, the Mo holder was opened near the specimen end using a pipe cutter. The end cap
and base plate assembly containing three tensile specimens and two SiC temperature monitors was then
removed. Finally, a push-pin tool was used to release the individual specimens and temperature monitors
from the base plate. Figure 1 shows the principal equipment and component-handling steps used during
capsule disassembly, from removal of the outer rabbit housing through release of the specimens and
temperature monitors from the internal carrier.

Figure 1. Representative equipment and workflow used for BOILER capsule disassembly in Building 3025E:
(a) cutting of the outer rabbit housing using the low-speed saw; (b) initial heater-block configuration used to heat
the exposed Mo holders during development of the Pb-relocation step; (c) opening of a cooled Mo holder using the
pipe cutter; (d) specimen and temperature-monitor carrier assembly with the retaining pin identified; and (e) pin-
removal tool used to release the three tensile specimens and two SiC temperature monitors from the carrier.

The heating configuration required adjustment during the early stages of disassembly. Initial attempts to
heat the Mo holders using machined heater blocks did not provide adequate heat transfer to relocate the
Pb. The holders were subsequently placed horizontally in direct contact with the hot plate. While hot, the
holders were inverted allowing the Pb to move. This configuration successfully melted the Pb and
allowed it to move toward the end of the holder opposite the specimens. Center-of-gravity checks were
used to confirm that the Pb had shifted before the holders were opened, and the revised approach was
applied to the remaining capsules.

Most specimen assemblies were removed directly after the Mo holders were opened. In some cases, the
pipe cutter caused localized splitting or fracture of the irradiated Mo holder, requiring removal of residual
holder fragments before the base plate could be placed in the push-pin tool. Two end cap assemblies also

 4

required additional controlled heating to support complete recovery. Residual Pb occasionally retained
individual components within the base plate. These conditions were managed during the recovery process
and did not prevent recovery of the experimental inventory.

All 24 irradiated tensile specimens were recovered, identified, and photographed. Figure 2 provides a
representative view of the three tensile specimens recovered from BLR01. Images documenting the
complete inventory of 24 recovered tensile specimens are provided in Appendix A. Localized residual Pb
remained visible on some specimen surfaces following thermal disassembly, but all specimens were in
suitable physical condition for the planned hardness and tensile testing.

Figure 2. Tensile specimens recovered from BLR01 following capsule disassembly.

All 16 SiC temperature monitors were also accounted for. Some monitors fractured during capsule
opening or removal, but the recovered pieces remained available for thermometry analysis. The intact
monitors and recovered fragments were packaged and transferred to LAMDA for inventory, imaging,
sectioning, and thermal analysis. Appendix B documents the complete temperature-monitor inventory
received by LAMDA and the resulting monitor sections.

Completion of capsule disassembly established the full irradiated specimen inventory required for the
remaining PIE activities. The SiC temperature monitors proceeded through thermometry testing in
LAMDA, while the tensile specimens remained in Building 3025E for imaging, chemical removal of
residual Pb, final mass measurements, and hardness testing.

 4. SiC THERMOMETRY RECOVERY AND LAMDA PROCESSING

All 16 recovered SiC temperature monitors were transferred to LAMDA for post-irradiation thermometry.
Following receipt, the monitors were inventoried and photographed to document their condition before
sectioning. The inventory included both intact monitors and monitors that had fractured during capsule
disassembly and recovery. Images of the complete pre-sectioning inventory are provided in Appendix B.
A monitor-specific sectioning plan was developed based on the recovered length and condition of each TM.
A section at least 10 mm long was allocated for the primary dilatometry measurement when the recovered
monitor length permitted. When sufficient material was available, an additional short section was harvested
for a single differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) measurement. Two approximately 5.5 mm dilatometry

 5

specimens were also harvested from upper and lower axial portions of the monitor when possible.
Comparison of these shorter sections was intended to provide an exploratory indication of whether a
temperature gradient existed within the capsule. The preferred sectioning approach is illustrated in Figure
3, while images documenting the resulting sections are provided in Appendix B.

Figure 3. Representative sectioning plan for BLR06 recovered SiC temperature monitors. A section
approximately 5 mm long near one end was allocated for DSC. The remaining material was divided to provide a
primary dilatometry specimen at least 10 mm long and, when sufficient material was available, two approximately
5.5 mm dilatometry specimens representing upper and lower axial portions of the monitor. Actual cuts were adjusted
based on the recovered length and condition of each monitor.

Fourteen monitors were processed under the initial sectioning instructions. BLR04 TM 24-08 and BLR08
TM 24-16 required follow-on instructions because their recovered lengths were uncertain in the original
pre-job briefing. Specific cuts were subsequently assigned to obtain dilatometry and DSC material from
these monitors.
Dilatometry was used as the primary method for determining the irradiation temperatures recorded by the
SiC monitors. DSC provided supporting thermal-response measurements and an additional basis for
evaluating temperature monitors. All planned dilatometry and DSC measurements were completed by the
time of this report.
Data reduction and determination of the capsule irradiation temperatures remain in progress. Thermometry
results are therefore not presented in this report. The completed measurements provide the basis for
assigning the achieved irradiation temperatures and selecting conditions for the subsequent elevated-
temperature tensile-testing campaign.

 5. TENSILE-SPECIMEN PB REMOVAL AND MASS MEASUREMENTS

Following capsule disassembly, residual Pb remained attached to portions of the tensile-specimen surfaces.
All 24 specimens were initially weighed in-cell to document their post-disassembly, pre-etch condition and
estimate the amount of residual material requiring removal. These measurements supported development
of an in-cell chemical-cleaning process and provided a reference for evaluating its progress.
Dose-rate measurements were also collected from four representative full-length tensile specimens before
chemical cleaning. As summarized in Table 3, measured contact dose rates ranged from 2.3 to 3.25 R/h,
while measurements at 30 cm ranged from 233 to 350 mR/h. The background during the measurements
was approximately 100 mR/h, introducing uncertainty into the net contribution from each specimen. Even
with this limitation, the measurements established that the intact tensile specimens required continued
handling and testing within the Building 3025E hot cells.

Development of the Pb-removal process began during the third quarter of FY 2026. A peroxide-free
solution consisting of acetic acid and ethanol in a 1:2 ratio was selected following supporting bench-scale
evaluations and review of the requirements for in-cell use. Each tensile specimen was placed in an
individual 3 mL vial containing freshly prepared etchant. The vials were capped to limit evaporation, and
the specimens were periodically removed for visual inspection and selected mass measurements.
The initial procedure anticipated approximately 48 hours of exposure, with additional treatment applied as
needed based on the estimated residual Pb mass. Actual Pb removal was substantially slower than predicted
by the supporting bench-scale work. The specimens therefore remained in the original etchant for
approximately two weeks, except when removed for inspection and weighing. After this extended in-cell
effort, the project team agreed that the process had sufficiently reduced the residual Pb and that the
specimens should proceed to the remaining PIE activities.
Post-etch images documented visible residual Pb on six specimens, indicating that the treatment did not
remove all Pb in every case. However, the available in-cell camera system was not sufficient for detailed
evaluation of specimen surfaces, and no conclusions regarding corrosion, oxide condition, or surface
damage are made in this report. More detailed surface examination is planned in LAMDA after mechanical
testing and confirmation that the resulting specimen sections meet facility dose-rate requirements.
Before the final mass-measurement campaign, the in-cell balance was recertified and calibrated during the
fourth quarter of FY 2026. Check-weight measurements collected on the calibration date provided the
reference for monitoring balance performance during the subsequent measurements. For each specimen,
three specimen-plus-cup measurements were collected and corrected using associated cup measurements.
The reported total standard deviation accounts for the repeatability of the specimen measurements and
variability in the cup measurements.

Following tensile testing, the fractured specimen halves will be resurveyed at 30 cm. The reduced specimen

 6. HARDNESS TESTING

Following completion of Pb removal and final mass measurements, the Buehler VH3100 Vickers hardness
tester was installed in Cell 4 of Building 3025E. Instrument operability was confirmed before the cell was
closed. At the beginning of each testing day, instrument performance was verified using a SUN-TEC
hardness reference standard, and the verification results were retained with the test records.
Hardness testing was performed using a 1 kgf load (HV 1) and a 15 s dwell time. The standard measurement
pattern included 20 indentations per specimen, with ten measurements distributed across each specimen
tab. Measurements on the unperforated tab were designated “Full,” while measurements on the opposite

 8

tab were designated “Hole” to identify the region containing the loading hole. A representative
measurement layout for specimen G004 is shown in Figure 4. The test output recorded individual hardness
values, indentation diagonals, and summary statistics for each region. An alternate 17-indentation pattern
was available when specimen geometry or indentation spacing did not permit the standard pattern.
In-cell hardness testing was completed on all 24 irradiated BOILER tensile specimens during Q4 of FY
2026. Limited residual Pb observed on some specimens did not prevent completion of the planned
measurements. Hardness results will be evaluated after the irradiation temperatures are determined and the
tensile-test results are available.

 7. FY 2026 PROGRESS SUMMARY AND REMAINING WORK

During FY 2026, the project completed the planned hardness testing and nearly all preparatory in-cell PIE
activities. The completed mechanical testing consisted primarily of Vickers hardness testing on all 24
irradiated tensile specimens. The completed in-cell activities included receipt of the eight irradiated
capsules from HFIR, capsule disassembly, recovery and inventory of all tensile specimens and SiC
temperature monitors, imaging of the recovered specimens, transfer of the temperature monitors to
LAMDA, chemical treatment to reduce residual Pb on the tensile specimens, and pre- and post-cleaning
mass measurements.

All 24 tensile specimens were recovered in suitable condition for the planned mechanical testing. All 16
SiC temperature monitors, including recovered fragments, were accounted for and transferred to

 9

LAMDA. The temperature monitors were inventoried, photographed, sectioned, and evaluated using
dilatometry and, where sufficient material was available, differential scanning calorimetry. The
thermometry data are being analyzed to determine the irradiation temperatures and select the elevated-
temperature tensile-test conditions.

The remaining mechanical test is elevated temperature tensile testing. Two associated hot-cell activities
remain in Building 3025E: completion of the tensile tests after the irradiation-temperature determination
is finalized and subsequent packaging and shipment of the tested specimen sections to LAMDA. The
transferred material will support the planned microscopy and additional characterization activities.

The FY 2026 work completed the capsule-recovery, specimen-preparation, mass-measurement,
thermometry, and hardness-testing stages of the PIE workflow. The project is positioned to proceed directly
into tensile testing and follow-on LAMDA characterization.
