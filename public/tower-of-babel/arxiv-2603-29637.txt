The continuum limit of the Poland-Scheraga DNA denaturation model
R. Dengler

Abstract
Using a field theory equivalent to a lattice version of the Poland-Scheraga (PS) model, the phase diagram for a long DNA molecule is derived in closed form. A one-loop renormalization group calculation for the generalized PS model with excluded volume interactions shows that there are two stable fixed points. At both fixed points, the excluded volume effect plays a role. At the fixed point reached when the original excluded volume effect is weak, the phase transition is continuous. At the other fixed point, the phase transition is first order.



1
Introduction

The subject of the Poland-Scheraga (PS)
[
12
]
model
is a long DNA molecule in a good solvent. When the temperature is
increased, the DNA molecule denatures. It is assumed that the pairing
energy does not depend on the type of nucleotides and that nucleotides
of two complementary DNA single strands can pair only when their length
indices along the two strands agree. This suggests measuring the length
of the template strand from 3´ to 5´
and the length of the other strand from 5´ to 3´
(see Fig.(
1
)). The two DNA single strands
can then form a sequence of double strands and loops.

Figure 1:
Two DNA single strands can pair only at
same distance
s
from the endpoints. The length is measured from

3^{\prime}
to
5^{\prime}
on the template strand and from
5^{\prime}
to
3^{\prime}
on the
other strand. The only allowed conformations then are a sequence of
loops.

The model captures essential biological and physical aspects of denaturation
in a semi-quantitative way. In the original PS model, excluded volume
effects are ignored. The model and its generalized (gPS) versions
are the subject of new studies, often with a mathematical background.
An introduction can be found in
[
13
]
. Generalized
versions take excluded volume effects into account
[
10
,
6
]
,
allow a length-dependent pairing energy
[
2
]
, or permit
some pairing at not exactly complementary length indices
[
7
,
8
]
.

The purpose of this work is to examine the continuum limit of the
PS model and of a gPS model with excluded volume effect. The term
"continuum limit" here means that the
polymers are long and that stiffness effects can be ignored. The correlation
length of long, dilute linear polymers in a good solvent usually diverges
as
\xi\sim\ell^{\nu}
with polymer length
\ell
, where
\nu

is a universal exponent
[
3
,
11
,
4
]
.
The large correlation length is a hallmark of critical phenomena,
and it is of interest to identify the universality classes and the
universal properties of such polymer solutions.

The central tool is the field theory corresponding to the gPS model
in the continuum limit, which can be derived from a lattice model

[
5
]
. A formal derivation, however, is not really necessary.
The only reasonable option is that polymer conformations and Feynman
diagrams have the same topology. This condition fixes the general
structure of the action integral. The rather rigid connection between
lattice models and field theories for linear polymers is illustrated
in a more direct way in appendix
A.1
. The action
integral corresponding to the PS model has the form

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\int_{x,s}\tilde{\varphi}\left(r_{0}-\nabla^{2}+\partial_{s}\right)\varphi+\int_{x,s}\tilde{\psi}\left[\tau_{0}-\nabla^{2}+W\partial_{s}\right]\psi-\tfrac{\lambda}{\sqrt{K_{d}}}\int_{x,s}\left(\psi\tilde{\varphi}^{2}+\varphi^{2}\tilde{\psi}\right)

(1)

\displaystyle\qquad+\tfrac{u_{\varphi}}{K_{d}}\int_{x}\left(\int_{s}\tilde{\varphi}\varphi\right)^{2}+\tfrac{u_{\psi}}{K_{d}}\int_{x}\left(\int_{s}\tilde{\psi}\psi\right)^{2}+2\tfrac{u_{\varphi\psi}}{K_{d}}\int_{x}\left(\int_{s}\tilde{\varphi}\varphi\right)\left(\int_{s}\tilde{\psi}\psi\right).

The variable
s
measures the length along the polymer strands. The
integrals are abbreviations for
\int_{x,s}\ldots=\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x\mathrm{d}s\ldots
,
the factors
K_{d}=2^{1-d}\pi^{-d/2}/\Gamma\left(d/2\right)
eliminate
complicated factors later on.

A double strand field
\psi\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)
usually
depends on two length variables, but one of them is redundant in the
PS model and has been omitted. The single strand field
\varphi\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)

has one length variable. The nonlinear parts with coupling constant

\lambda
describe denaturation of a double strand
\psi
to two
single strands
\varphi
and the reverse process. In the lattice
version of the model
\lambda
is the weight with which conformations
in which three endpoints coincide actually combine to a vertex.

It is a peculiarity of the PS model that all interactions take place
at matching length variables. The first line of (
1
) accordingly
is local in
s
, and the length variable could also be interpreted
as time. The direction implied by
s
enforces pairs of fields, polymer
sources (
\tilde{\varphi},\tilde{\psi}
) and a polymer sinks (
\varphi,\psi
).
The action (
1
) with
u=0
resembles that of directed
percolation, and formally also is a model of critical dynamics
[
9
]
.
It suggests itself to interpret the model as a reaction-diffusion
process of two types of diffusing particles, with reaction formulas

P\rightarrow 2F
and
2F\rightarrow P
. However, such a process
generates additional marginal and relevant terms.

The nonlinear parts with coupling constant
u
describe the excluded
volume effect, are nonlocal in
s
and not part of the standard PS
model.

If one attributes a Boltzmann factors
e^{\beta E_{\varphi}}
to
each
\varphi
monomer and
e^{\beta E_{\psi}}
to each
\psi

monomer then the lattice model generates the parameters

\displaystyle r_{0}

\displaystyle=c_{\varphi}-\beta E_{\varphi},

(2)

\displaystyle\tau_{0}

\displaystyle=c_{\psi}-\beta E_{\psi},

(3)

where
c
are non-universal constants. A positive energy
E_{j}

favors the respective polymer type, and the parameters
r_{0}
and

\tau_{0}
thus control the degree of denaturation. Since
m_{\varphi}=\int_{x,s}\tilde{\varphi}\varphi

is the
\varphi
-mass the weight
e^{-S}
in a natural way contains
the factor
e^{\beta E_{\varphi}m_{\varphi}}
, and likewise for
E_{\psi}
.

Instead of starting with the action (
1
), one could use
a field theory of the type introduced by Edwards, based on polymer
paths, and manually add length-dependent Boltzmann factors and interactions.
Alternatively, one could simply combine Gaussian functions for the
noninteracting polymer segments. The advantage of an action like (
1
)
is that it follows from a lattice model and makes available the field
theoretic tools and concepts.

2
Physical quantities

The propagator
G\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)=\left\langle\tilde{\psi}\left(0,0\right)\psi\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)\right\rangle

is the statistical weight generated by the action (
1
)
for a double strand polymer starting at the origin with length
0

and terminating at position
\boldsymbol{x}
with length
s
. The
Fourier transform
G\left(\boldsymbol{k}=0,s\right)=\int_{x}\left\langle\tilde{\psi}\left(0,0\right)\psi\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)\right\rangle

is the statistical weight generated by the action (
1
)
for a double strand polymer of length
s
starting at the origin
and terminating anywhere. The average single strand mass
m_{\varphi}

in the latter case is

\left\langle m_{\varphi}\right\rangle=\left\langle\int_{x,s}\tilde{\varphi}\varphi\right\rangle=-\partial G\left(\boldsymbol{k}=0,s\right)/\partial r_{0},

and analogously for
m_{\psi}
and
\tau_{0}
. This, however, are
not yet physical quantities. The correlation function
G\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)

in its lattice model version is the sum over all conformations
C\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)

with length
s
and given endpoints of Boltzmann factors for the
polymer masses
m_{\varphi}
and
m_{\psi}
,

G\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in C\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)}e^{-m_{\varphi}^{(\alpha)}r_{0}-m_{\psi}^{(\alpha)}\tau_{0}}=e^{-m\tau_{0}}\sum_{\alpha\in C\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)}e^{-m_{\varphi}^{(\alpha)}\left(r_{0}-\tau_{0}\right)}.

Here it was used that the given total length
s
enforces a constant
total mass
m=m_{\varphi}^{(\alpha)}+m_{\psi}^{(\alpha)}
. The Boltzmann
factors in the sum are the correct weight for the conformations according
to some pairing energy. The unphysical overall factor
e^{-m\tau_{0}}

drops out from the normalized probability distribution

P\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)=G\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)/\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}}G\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right).

The properly normalized average single strand mass is

\overline{m_{\varphi}}\left(s,r_{0},\tau_{0}\right)=-\partial\ln G\left(\boldsymbol{k}=0,s\right)/\partial r_{0}.

(4)

3
The connection with the PS nomenclature

To make contact with the usual terms of
the discrete variant of the PS model we write down the
\varphi
-propagator

g_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)=\left\langle\tilde{\varphi}\left(\boldsymbol{0},0\right)\varphi\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)\right\rangle_{0}

in two forms

\displaystyle g_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)

\displaystyle=2^{-d}\pi^{-d/2}\theta\left(s\right)s^{-d/2}e^{-r_{0}s-x^{2}/4s},

(5)

\displaystyle g_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{k},\omega\right)

\displaystyle=1/\left(r_{0}+k^{2}-i\omega\right).

(6)

The first line is a normalized diffusion curve with an additional
Boltzmann factor
e^{-r_{0}s}.
It measures the probability to find
the end of a single strand polymer of length
s
at
\boldsymbol{x}

when it has started with length variable
s=0
at the origin. The
second line is the Fourier transform
g\left(\boldsymbol{k},\omega\right)=\int_{x,s}e^{-i\boldsymbol{kx}+i\omega s}g_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)
,
where the “frequency”
\omega
is the variable conjugate to
s
.

The statistical weight
\Omega\left(s\right)
of a
\varphi
-loop
of length
s
of the type shown in Fig.
1

is given by
\Omega\left(s\right)=\int_{x}g_{0}^{2}\left(\boldsymbol{x},s\right)\propto e^{-2r_{0}s}s^{-d/2}
.
This “loop weight” plays a central role in the discrete formulation
of the PS model, and for large
s
it is assumed
\Omega\left(s\right)\sim K^{s}s^{-c}

with some constant
K
and a “loop exponent”
c
. For the PS
model variant with
u=0
it follows
c=d/2
. According to a central
PS theorem valid also for more generic variants of the PS model the
denaturation is a continuous phase transition for
1\leq c<2
.

4
Exact solution without excluded volume effects

The single strand propagator
\left\langle\tilde{\varphi}\varphi\right\rangle

is exactly given by (
5
). The double strand propagator is
determined by the one-loop vertex function

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\tilde{\psi}\psi}^{(1)}\left(\boldsymbol{k},\omega\right)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{2\lambda^{2}}{K_{d}\left(2\pi\right)^{d}}\int^{\Lambda}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}^{d}p}{p^{2}+\left(k-p\right)^{2}+2r_{0}-i\omega}

(7)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\left(\tfrac{\pi/2}{\sin\pi\epsilon/2}\left(r_{0}+\tfrac{k^{2}}{4}-\tfrac{i\omega}{2}\right)^{1-\epsilon/2}-\tfrac{\Lambda^{2-\epsilon}}{2-\epsilon}-\tfrac{\Lambda^{-\epsilon}}{\epsilon}\left(r_{0}+\tfrac{k^{2}}{4}-\tfrac{i\omega}{2}\right)+\ldots\right).

In the first line the internal
\omega
-integral has already been
performed, and
\Lambda=a^{-1}
is the UV cutoff, where
a
is the
lattice spacing of the original lattice model. It often is convenient
to use
a
as length unit. As usual
\epsilon=4-d
.

The vertex function (
7
) is a sum of a singular contribution,
a constant, and non-universal contributions regular in the parameters

r_{0}
,
k
and
\omega.
The constant is uninteresting, it only
shifts the parameter
\tau_{0}
. The regular contributions of higher
order contain additional powers of
r_{0}/\Lambda^{2}
,
k^{2}/\Lambda^{2}

and
i\omega/\Lambda^{2}
and thus are negligible in the continuum
limit (for lengths much larger than the lattice constant
1/\Lambda
).
The double strand propagator
\left\langle\tilde{\psi}\psi\right\rangle

follows as

\displaystyle G\left(k,\omega\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\tau_{0}+k^{2}-Wi\omega+\Gamma_{\tilde{\psi}\psi}^{(1)}\right)^{-1}

(8)

\displaystyle\cong\left[\tau_{0}+k^{2}-Wi\omega-\lambda^{2}\tfrac{\Lambda^{-\epsilon}}{\epsilon}\left(r_{0}+\tfrac{k^{2}}{4}-\tfrac{i\omega}{2}\right)+\lambda^{2}\tfrac{\pi/2}{\sin\pi\epsilon/2}\left(r_{0}+\tfrac{k^{2}}{4}-\tfrac{i\omega}{2}\right)^{1-\epsilon/2}\right]^{-1}.

The geometric series in the coupling constant
\lambda^{2}
is typical
for the PS model. For long polymers the typical values of
\tau_{0}
,

r_{0},

k^{2}
and
\omega
are small, and the singular term
is larger by a factor
k^{-\epsilon}\sim\xi^{\epsilon}
.

5.1
Standard PS model

We now use the exact solution (
7
) to introduce the renormalization
group (RG) formalism. This does not lead to new results, but it is
a good a preparation for the more complicated case with
u\neq 0.

The usual arguments are as follows. The action integral (
1
)
in combination with different cutoffs
\Lambda
represents instances
of the same universality class, with same critical exponents. The

amplitudes
of vertex functions, however - such as
\Gamma_{\tilde{\varphi}\varphi}=r_{0}+k^{2}-i\omega

and
\Gamma_{\tilde{\psi}\psi}=\tau_{0}+k^{2}-i\omega+\Gamma_{\tilde{\psi}\psi}^{(1)}

are non-universal. To eliminate the non-universal aspects one rescales
the fields and coordinates according to
\varphi=Z_{\varphi}\varphi_{\mathrm{R}},

\tilde{\varphi}=Z\tilde{\varphi}_{\mathrm{R}},
\psi=Z_{\psi}\psi,

\tilde{\psi}=Z_{\psi}\tilde{\psi}_{\mathrm{R}}
and
s=Z_{s}s_{\mathrm{R}}

and imposes normalization conditions at some arbitrary small wavevector

\mu

\displaystyle\partial\Gamma_{\tilde{\varphi}\varphi}^{\mathrm{R}}/\partial k^{2}|_{k=\mu,\omega=0}

\displaystyle=1,

(10)

\displaystyle\partial\Gamma_{\tilde{\varphi}\varphi}^{\mathrm{R}}/\partial\left(-i\omega\right)|_{k=\mu,\omega=0}

\displaystyle=1,

(11)

\displaystyle\partial\Gamma_{\tilde{\psi}\psi}^{\mathrm{R}}/\partial k^{2}|_{k=2\mu,\omega=0}

\displaystyle=1.

(12)

The symbol
\mathrm{R}
denotes renormalized quantities. The renormalized
vertex functions get their

-factors from the attached fields and
thus (using dimensional regularization)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\tilde{\varphi}\varphi}^{\mathrm{R}}\left(k,\omega\right)

\displaystyle=Z_{\varphi}^{2}Z_{s}\left[k^{2}-Z_{s}^{-1}i\omega\right],

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\tilde{\psi}\psi}^{\mathrm{R}}\left(k,\omega\right)

\displaystyle=Z_{\psi}^{2}Z_{s}\left[k^{2}-Z_{s}^{-1}i\omega+\lambda^{2}\tfrac{\pi/2}{\sin\pi\epsilon/2}\left(\tfrac{k^{2}}{4}-Z_{s}^{-1}\tfrac{i\omega}{2}\right)^{1-\epsilon/2}\right].

The renormalization conditions lead to

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=Z_{\varphi}=Z_{s},

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=Z_{\psi}^{2}Z_{s}\left[1+\tfrac{\bar{\lambda}^{2}}{4}\tfrac{\pi/2}{\sin\pi\epsilon/2}\left(1-\tfrac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right],

where
\bar{\lambda}=\lambda\mu^{-\epsilon/2}
is the dimensionless
bare coupling constant. With the given
Z
factors the dimensionless
renormalized coupling constant
\lambda_{R}
follows as

\lambda_{R}\left(\bar{\lambda}\right)=\mu^{-\epsilon/2}\Gamma_{\varphi\varphi\tilde{\psi}}^{R}\left(\mu,\lambda\right)=Z_{\varphi}^{2}Z_{\psi}Z_{s}\bar{\lambda}.

Inserting the factors yields

\lambda_{R}^{2}\left(\bar{\lambda}\right)=Z_{\psi}^{2}\bar{\lambda}^{2}=\tfrac{\bar{\lambda}^{2}}{1+\tfrac{\bar{\lambda}^{2}}{4}\tfrac{\pi/2}{\sin\pi\epsilon/2}\left(1-\tfrac{\epsilon}{2}\right)}\overset{\mu\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}4\tfrac{\sin\pi\epsilon/2}{\left(\pi/2\right)\left(1-\epsilon/2\right)}.

(13)

We thus have found a stable fixed point
\lambda_{\mathrm{R}*}^{2}=4\epsilon+O\left(\epsilon^{2}\right).

This critical point is reached when
\varphi
and
\psi
are “massless”,
that is if both types of polymer strands are long. The

remains finite also for

The conditions (

12

5.3
Loop exponent

The loop exponent can be deduced from the solution of the RG differential
equation
[
1

for the vertex function
\Gamma_{\tilde{\psi}\psi}^{R}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\tilde{\psi}\psi}^{R}\left(k,\omega\right)

\displaystyle=Z_{\psi}^{-2}\left(\mu\right)Z_{s}^{-1}\left(\mu\right)\mu^{2}F\left(k/\mu,Z_{s}\left(\mu\right)\omega/\mu^{2},u_{R}\left(\mu\right),\lambda_{R}\left(\mu\right),W_{R}\left(\mu\right)\right)

\displaystyle\sim\mu^{1/\nu-2\eta_{\psi}}F\left(k/\mu,X\omega/\mu^{1/\nu},u_{R}\left(\mu\right),\lambda_{R}\left(\mu\right),W_{R}\left(\mu\right)\right).

6
Conclusion

The field theory ([eq:Act]) captures the long-distance physics
of the PS model, both with and without excluded volume interactions.

In the standard PS model the central quantity is a single denaturation
loop in a DNA molecule. In field theory terms, this corresponds to
a one-loop Feynman diagram, which directly leads to an exact solution.
In three dimensions the degree of denaturation can even be obtained
in closed form (Fig.
2
).

When excluded volume effects are included, a one-loop RG calculation
shows that there are two stable fixed points. At the fixed point reached
when the excluded volume interaction is strong, the phase transition
is first order.

Field theory on a lattice

The action (

\psi

\displaystyle Z_{\psi}

\displaystyle=\int_{-i\infty}^{i\infty}\mathrm{D}\tilde{\psi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{D}\psi e^{-S},

\displaystyle S_{\psi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}\left[\sum_{ij}\tilde{\psi_{i}}^{\mu}e^{-\beta E}v_{ij}^{-1}\psi_{j}^{\mu+1}-r_{2}\sum_{i}\tilde{\psi}_{i}^{\mu}\psi_{i}^{\mu}\right]+O\left(\psi^{4}\right).

The fields
\psi

and
a length index
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}

v_{ij}
is the
next-neighbor matrix. Of primary interest is the bilinear part of

S

G

with length indexes
\mu

\nu

\ell

The power of the next neighbor matrix

\sqrt{\ell/d}

\ell

continuum limit propagator for a given distance
\ell

Gaussian function in space with a weight proportional to
\ell^{-d/2}e^{-r_{0}\ell-\boldsymbol{x}^{2}/2\ell}
