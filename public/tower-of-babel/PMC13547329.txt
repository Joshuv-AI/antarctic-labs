Synthesis of chitosan-starch@Fe2O3 nanocomposite for efficient adsorption of methyl orange and methylene blue dyes from aqueous solutions
Authors: Aliakbar Dehno Khalaji, Kasim Sakran Abass
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractModified chitosan-based materials have gained increasing attention for their high adsorption efficiency in removing diverse pollutants from aqueous solutions. Herein, to enhance the removal capability of chitosan-based compounds towards organic dyes, a new composite CS-ST@Fe2O3 was synthesized via a one-pot reaction and applied for the removal of methyl orange (MO) and methylene blue (MB) from simulated wastewater using batch experiments. Under optimal adsorption conditions, the maximum removal of MO and MB was achieved to 98.63% and 97.56%, respectively. The adsorption results demonstrated that the adsorption mechanism obeyed the Pseudo-second-order (PSO) kinetic model and the Langmuir isotherm model, as a mainly monolayer chemisorption. Reuse experiments after 5 cycles predicted that the removal efficiency for MO and MB dyes in water is maintained at > 92%.Keywords: Chitosan-based compounds, Dye removal, Langmuir monolayer adsorptionSubject terms: Chemistry, Environmental sciences, Materials science

Introduction

Water pollutants contain organic dyes1,2 and heavy metal ions3,4 used in various industries that enter the environment through the discharge of wastewater. Among them, organic dyes have become the focus because they are very toxic and stable1,5–7. Uncontrolled discharge of wastewater into the environment leads to its accumulation in all water resources, which will seriously threaten human health. Therefore, it is urgent to remove different pollutants, especially organic dyes, before discharge1,2. Currently, environmental researchers are committed to removing different pollutants from water resources using different physical and chemical routes8–10. Some of these techniques are expensive, low efficiency, produce secondary pollutants, and are not eco-friendly11–13. These disadvantages lead to limited application. Among different techniques in wastewater treatment, the adsorption process has received much attention in recent years due to its simple operation process, high efficiency, excellent regeneration capacity, and low cost14–17. In this technique, the type of adsorbent is an important parameter in the removal of different pollutants from wastewater18–21. Chitosan (CS) and its modified compounds, due to their large number of hydroxyls, amines, and other groups, can effectively adsorb organic dyes through different interactions such as n–π, π–π, dipole-dipole, H-bonds, Yoshida bonds, and electrostatic attractions22–24. Chitosan is non-toxic, bio-degradable, abundant, and easily modified22–24. Therefore, chitosan-based composites can be prepared by physical or chemical modification and widely used as new adsorbents with high and stable adsorption properties25–29. During the modification of chitosan, the two and three-dimensional network structure formed by cross-linking or grafting may increase the removal efficiency and also enhance its mechanical strength30–32. Recently, different polymers have been used to prepare new chitosan-based composites to increase their sorption sites and enhance the adsorption efficiency33–37. Herein, in order to improve the applicability of chitosan-based composites as new adsorbents for organic dyes uptake, the present study focused on the preparation of a new chitosan-based composite CS-ST@Fe2O3 (Scheme 1) to increase the active sorption sites for MO and MB dyes molecules.

Experimental

Materials

Chitosan with medium molecular weight (CAS No. 9012-76-4), starch (CAS No. 9005-84-9, Extra pure), acetic acid glacial (100%, CAS No. 64-19-7), KOH pellets (CAS 1310-58-3 for analysis), NaOH pellets (CAS 1310-73-2 for analysis), ethanol (96%, CAS No. 64-17-5 for analysis), FeCl2·4H2O (CAS No. 13487-10-9, for analysis), and FeCl3·6H2O (ACS reagent, 97%, CAS No. 10025-77-1) used in this work were purchased and used as received.

Characterization

The as-synthesized compounds were characterized using a variety of analytical techniques. To examine the surface morphology, scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (FE-SEM; JSM-7800 F, JEOL Ltd., Japan) was carried out to provide insights into surface roughness and porosity. X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis was carried out using PXRD; Miniflex, Rigaku, Japan, with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5418 Å) to determine the crystallographic structure within the 2θ range of 10° to 80°. Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy was performed to determine the functional groups. The N2 adsorption–desorption isotherm was obtained at 77 K and 120 °C to estimate surface areas according to the Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) equation using a Belsorp Max II, Microtrac, USA. The thermal behaviour (TG/DTA thermogram) was analysed using a PerkinElmer TGA 4000 thermo-gravimetric analyser (TGA) within the temperature range of 25 °C to 800 °C.

Synthesis of Fe2O3

FeCl2·4H2O (1.98 g) and FeCl3·6H2O (5.4 g) were dissolved in 20 mL of DI water and stirred for 0.5 h to prepare a clear solution. Then, 0.5 M NaOH solution was added dropwise until the pH of the solution reached 12. The mixture was stirred for 3 h at 80 °C. The brown precipitates were calcined at 550 °C for 3 h. The final product was collected, washed with cold DI water and ethanol, and dried at 80 °C for 3 h.

Synthesis of CS-ST@Fe2O3

A mixture of chitosan (2.0 g) as the main matrix and starch (1.0 g) as a crosslinking agent between two chitosan chains in an aqueous glacial acetic acid (5% v/v) was stirred for 1 h at 50 °C. After that, 10 mL of KOH solution (0.5 M) was added dropwise, and the mixture was stirred for 0.5 h. The preparation process of chitosan-starch hydrogels was simplified by using KOH at room temperature. Finally, 0.3 g of Fe2O3 was added, and the mixture was vigorously stirred at 450 rpm at 80 °C for 6 h. The final grey-black powders were collected, washed with cold DI water and ethanol, and dried at 80 °C for 3 h.

Removal of dyes using CS-ST@Fe2O3 in batch adsorption

A series of batch experiments was carried out to determine the adsorption efficiency of CS-ST@Fe2O3. With these experiments, the effect of initial pH solution (2–10), contact time (0–120 min), adsorbent usage (4–24 mg), initial dyes concentration (10–90 mg/L), and initial temperature of dyes solutions (25–45 °C) was studied and discussed. Reusability studies of CS-ST@Fe2O3

To determine the practical applicability of CS-ST@Fe2O3, reusability studies are crucial and necessary. The dye desorption was carried out in the same procedure reported in the previous paper, with minor corrections9,38. The dyes adsorbed using the surface of CS-ST@Fe2O3 were agitated in 10 mL of NaOH (1 M) at 350 rpm and room temperature for a duration of 2 h. The desorbed adsorbent precipitates were then washed in DI water and dried at 80 °C. Then, the CS-ST@Fe2O3 was subsequently used for dye adsorption, as outlined in ’Removal of dyes using CS-ST@Fe2O3 in batch adsorption”.

Results and discussion

characterization

FT-IR

The FTIR spectra of Fe2O3 and CS-ST@Fe2O3 are presented in Fig. 1a,c, respectively. The spectral characteristic peaks of Fe2O3 (Fig. 1a) showed the stretching vibrations of the Fe-O bonds at about 452 cm− 1 and 530 cm− 125,30,39. These vibrations are shown in the FT-IR spectrum of CS-ST@Fe2O3 (Fig. 1c). In addition, the weak broad peak at about 3417 cm− 1 was seen and is assigned to the vibrations of O-H bonds concerning the water molecules adsorbed on the surface of Fe2O3 nanoparticles [26,31,40]. In the FT-IR spectrum of CS-ST@Fe2O3, different bands are seen concerning the –OH (3395 cm− 1), –CH (2857 cm− 1), –C=O (1723 cm− 1), –NH (1649 cm− 1), C–O (1063 cm− 1), –C–O–C– (1019 cm− 1) bonds4,22,24,25,30,32,34,36,39,40.

FT-IR spectrum and XRD patterns of Fe2O3 (a,b) and CS-ST@Fe2O3 (c,d).

XRD patterns

XRD patterns of Fe2O3 and CS-ST@Fe2O3 were shown in Fig. 1b,d, respectively. The XRD pattern of Fe2O3 shows the diffraction peaks were assigned to the hematite Fe2O3 structure phase with JCPDS No. 96-154-6384 at 2θ values of about 24.21° (012), 33.22° (104), 35.68° (110), 40.93° (006), 49.52° (024), 54.11° (116), 57.64° (122), 62.47° (214) and 64.04° (300)25,30,39. No other impurity peaks are observed, suggesting a pure α-Fe2O3 phase. Similar characteristic peaks are seen in the XRD pattern of CS-ST@Fe2O3, which indicates the presence of Fe2O3 loaded on the CS-ST composite. Furthermore, the characteristic peaks assigned to the mixture of CS-PVAc are seen at 2θ values of 14.48° and 20.78°25,30,39,41–43.

TG/DTA thermogram

The TG/DTA curves of CS-ST@Fe2O3 (Fig. 2a) predicted that weight loss occurs in three stages. The 1st stage (50–150 °C) is due to the evaporation of solvent molecules adsorbed on the surface of CS-ST@Fe2O3. The 2nd stage (150 to 350 °C) is assigned to the degradation of the C-O-C in the polymer chain25,30,39. After that, the weight loss is very low, and at 825 °C, about 43% of the mass remained.

(a) TG/DTA curves and (b) N2 adsorption/desorption isotherm of CS-ST@Fe2O3.

BET analysis

The BET result of CS-ST@Fe2O3 is shown in Fig. 2b, and shows that it was classified as a type IV, with an H1 hysteresis loop25,30,39,44, confirming the presence of a mesoporous structure in the CS-ST@Fe2O3. According to the BET analysis, the specific surface area, pore volume, and pore size of CS-ST@Fe2O3 are 4.179 m2/g, 0.0114 cm3/g and 10.88 nm.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)

Figure 3 displays the SEM images of Fe2O3 and CS-ST@Fe2O3. Hematite Fe2O3 displays a rough surface with spherical particles and high agglomeration (Fig. 3a). The irregular surface is marked by a unique crystalline structure. Pure chitosan has a smooth surface, while the surface of CS-ST@Fe2O3 (Fig. 3b) is very rough, non-uniform, and irregularly arranged with various pores. Also, the spherical particles of hematite were seen.

Dye adsorption studies

The effect of the initial pH solution

The removal of anionic and cationic dyes basically depends on the initial pH of the solutions45–52, because the pH of the solution can influence the surface charges of the chitosan-based adsorbent48–52. The pH of dye solutions is adjusted between 1 and 11 by adding 0.5 M NaOH and HCl solutions. The effect of pH on the removal efficiency of MO and MB using the CS-ST@Fe2O3 is presented in Fig. 4a. It can be seen that, in acidic media, the positive charge of the adsorbent enhances the attractions between the surface of CS-ST@Fe2O3 and the negative functional groups of MO molecules. Then, the maximum removal efficiency was seen at a pH solution of 4 (98.63%)45,46,48,49. While for MB, maximum removal efficiency was seen at a pH solution of 10 (97.56%), due to the negative charge of the adsorbent surface50–52. At this pH, the maximum attraction between the negative charge of the adsorbent and the positive charge of MB occurred. According to these results, for the investigation of other important parameters on the removal efficiency of MO and MB dye, the pH solutions of 4 and 10 were selected for MO and MB, respectively.

The effect of (a) initial pH and (b) adsorbent dose in 50 mL dye solution on the removal efficiency of MO and MB dyes adsorption at a temperature of 45 °C and initial concentration of 70 mg/L after 70 min contact time.

The effect of adsorbent dose

At constant of initial dyes concentration, volume, time, and temperature, the effect of initial adsorbent dose (4–24 mg) on the removal efficiency of dyes was investigated and the results are shown in Fig. 4b. The results predicted that the increases of removal efficiency with increases of CS-ST@Fe2O3 dose was happened, due to increases of active sorption surface sites of CS-ST@Fe2O3 for adsorbed dye molecules53–58. Maximum removal efficiency was seen at an adsorbent dose usage of 20 mg, and selected for investigation of other parameters such as time, initial concentration, and temperature.

Effect of initial dye concentration

The effect of initial dye concentrations (10 to 110 mg/L) on the removal efficiency of MO and MB was investigated, and the results are shown in Fig. 5a. The removal efficiency was almost constant with the increase in initial concentrations from 10 mg/L to 70 mg/L. The amount of adsorbent used (0.02 g) in dye solutions with a concentration of ≤ 70 mg/L almost completely removes the dye molecules. In this case, the time to reach equilibrium is faster for lower concentrations. Therefore, the selected optimum concentration is 70 mg/L with R = 98.63% for MO and 97.56% for MB. At dye concentrations > 70 mg/L, the removal efficiency is decreased slowly due to the occupancy of active sites45,46,48,51,52.

The effect of (a) initial dye concentration and (b) contact time in 50 mL dye solution on the removal efficiency of MO and MB dyes adsorption at a temperature of 45 °C, the amount of adsorbent = 20 mg at pH solution of 4 for MO and 10 for MB.

The effect of contact time

The effect of time was carried out and the results are shown in Fig. 5b. The results predicted that the removal efficiency of MO and MB dyes increased rapidly and reached 59.63% for MO and 51.24% respectively during the first 20 min because of many active sorption sites on the surface of CS-ST@Fe2O3 were available and empty, leading to a faster adsorption at the beginning 20 min22,34,36,46,48,52. After this time, the adsorption continues to increase slowly, because of the decrease and the occupation of some sorption surface sites by previous dye molecules. Finally, the dye adsorption reached equilibrium after 70 min with maximum removal values of 98.63% and 67.56% for MO and MB, respectively22,34,36,46,48,52.

The effect of temperature

The effect of temperature on the removal efficiency of MO and MB dyes was explored at 70 min and 50 mg/L dye solutions, and the results are shown in Fig. 6a. The removal efficiency increased from 25 to 40 °C and reached a maximum of 98.63% for MO and 97.56% for MB46,48. This result predicted that the increased temperature can disrupt the different hydrogen bonds, exposing the hidden sorption sites and enhancing various attractions between dye molecules and the adsorbent. These results show that the absorption process of these dyes by the synthetic adsorbent CS-ST@Fe2O3 is an endothermic process.

The effect of (a) temperature and (b) reusability in 50 mL dye solution with an initial concentration of 70 mg/L on the removal percentage of MO and MB dyes adsorption at pH solution of 4 for MO and 10 for MB, the amount of adsorbent = 20 mg after 70 min contact time.

Reusability

Herein, the reusability of CS-ST@Fe2O3 for the removal efficiency of MO and MB after 10 cycles was investigated, and the results are depicted in Fig. 6b. Initially, the composite CS-ST@Fe2O3 exhibited a high removal efficiency of 98.63% for MO and 97.56% for MB. After the 6 cycles, the removal efficiency decreased to 87.63% for MO and 83.84% for MB, indicating a significant reduction in performance22,28,44–46,48,51. This reduction is related to the partial degradation of the composite’s structure, agglomeration or irreversibility of dyes adsorption, leading to a decrease in the active surface sorption sites. The rate of yield reduction of MB is higher than for MO. It seems that the absorption of MB is stronger than that of MO, and more of the dye molecules still remain on the active sites of CS-ST@Fe2O3 after washing. However, this composite retains significant removal efficiency even after 10 uses, indicating its potential for practical applications in water purification. Future work should focus on enhancing the regeneration process to minimize capacity loss, ensure longer lifetime, and greater efficiency in real-world scenarios.

Adsorption mechanism

Given that the adsorbent and adsorbed surface are very sensitive to pH, it is suggested that the pH of the solution is very important for the adsorption efficiency. The composite CS-ST@Fe2O3 shows maximum adsorption for MO in an acidic environment and for MB in an alkaline environment. Therefore, due to the different nature of the two dyes and having different groups, they show different interactions with the surface adsorbent groups of the type of n–π, π–π, electrostatic interactions, Yoshida, and H-bonding22,24,46,49,59,60. The synthetic adsorbent CS-ST@Fe2O3 has many hydroxyl and amine groups on its surface. These groups will easily bond with the pair of non-bonded electrons on the N atoms of MO and MB through hydrogen interactions (O–H···N). Also, these electrons will have n–π interaction with the phenyl rings of the dyes. Due to the protonation of the adsorbent surface in the acidic medium, it easily creates electrostatic attractions with the negative SO3− groups of MO. For methylene blue, this attraction is created in alkaline solutions by using negative groups on the adsorbent and positive S+ groups of MB. Conclusions

A novel composite CS-ST@Fe2O3 was facilely fabricated from the reaction of chitosan and starch in the presence of Fe2O3 nanoparticles. The composite CS-ST@Fe2O3 was characterized using IR, TG/DTA, BET, SEM, and XRD tools. Additionally, the composite CS-ST@Fe2O3 was applied as an efficient adsorbent for removing MO and MB dyes from aqueous solution. The highest removal efficiency is 98.63% (172.6 mg.g− 1) and 97.56% (170.7 mg.g− 1) for MO and MB dyes, respectively. Also, the results showed that the Langmuir isotherm and the pseudo-second-order model well fitted the dye removal by CS-ST@Fe2O3. The high removal efficiency of the composite suggests its potential practical applications in industrial wastewater treatment. The good results of this research help us to investigate the regeneration and reuse of synthetic nanocomposites in real industrial wastewater, which contains multiple pollutants, for future work to increase their economic feasibility and sustainability. Furthermore, modification of the composite to target the removal of other organic pollutants could expand its application in environmental cleanup. With excellent removal performance, the nanocomposite shows strong promise for real-world industrial wastewater applications. Building on these findings, subsequent studies will focus on regeneration and reuse cycles using complex, multi-pollutant industrial effluents to enhance cost-effectiveness and sustainability. Further functionalization of the material may also enable targeted removal of additional organic pollutants, expanding its role in environmental cleanup.
