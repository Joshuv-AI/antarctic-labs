Exceptional-Point-Anchored Variational Quantum Eigensolver for Non-Hermitian Many-Body Phase Diagrams: Bridging Skin-Effect Topology and Entanglement Criticality on NISQ Hardware Publication type: Research Paper
Akoramurthy B 1 \orcid 0000-0001-5912-7020, Surendiran B 1 \orcid 0000-0001-5435-0880 and Xiaochun Cheng 2,∗ \orcid 0000-0003-0371-9646

Abstract
We introduce the Biorthogonal Variational Quantum Eigensolver (B-VQE), a variational quantum algorithm tailored to non-Hermitian many-body Hamiltonians on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) hardware. Unlike standard VQE, B-VQE employs two independent parameterised circuits to prepare approximations of the right eigenstate | ψ R ​ ( 𝜽 ) ⟩ \ket{\psi_{R}(\bm{\theta})} and the left eigenstate ⟨ ψ ~ L ​ ( ϕ ) | \bra{\tilde{\psi}_{L}(\bm{\phi})} respectively, optimising a biorthogonal cost function that penalises imaginary-energy deviations and thereby tracks parity-time ( 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathcal{PT} ) symmetry breaking across exceptional points (EPs). We augment B-VQE with an Exceptional-Point Detector (EPD) module that operationally identifies EPs via a hardware-native coalescence metric, and with a Non-Hermitian Quantum Geometric Tensor (NH-QGT) readout that resolves the separation between state topology and band topology specific to many-body non-Hermitian systems. To address the exponential post-selection overhead that afflicts standard non-Hermitian simulation on quantum hardware, we develop an importance-sampling mitigation scheme that recovers polynomial overhead without ancilla post-selection. We validate the complete framework on three paradigmatic models: (i) the non-Hermitian Hubbard chain exhibiting 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathcal{PT} -broken ergodic, NH-many-body-localised (NH-MBL), and skin-localised phases; (ii) the non-Hermitian XXZ spin chain hosting exceptional-point-enhanced quantum many-body scars; and (iii) a two-dimensional non-Hermitian t t - J J model supporting a Fermi skin. Across all three models, B-VQE achieves relative energy errors below ϵ E < 5 × 10 − 3 \epsilon_{E}<5\times 10^{-3} with circuit depths competitive with hardware-efficient ansätze, and the EPD module locates exceptional points to within δ ​ λ < 0.02 ​ t \delta\lambda<0.02\,t on noise-free simulators. Our results establish B-VQE as a scalable NISQ methodology for mapping non-Hermitian many-body phase diagrams, resolving a critical algorithmic gap in the field.

Introduction

The study of non-Hermitian quantum systems has evolved from a mathematical curiosity
surrounding complex spectra into one of the most productive frontiers in
contemporary physics
[
1
,
4
,
10
]
.
The essential departure from standard quantum mechanics lies in relaxing the
Hermiticity condition
H=H^{\dagger}
, which generically arises when a quantum
system exchanges energy or particles with its environment-a situation ubiquitous
in photonics
[
27
]
, ultracold atoms
[
32
]
,
and superconducting circuits
[
26
]
.

Within this broad landscape, two phenomena have crystallised as particularly
fundamental.
The first is the
exceptional point
(EP)
[
15
,
23
]
:
a spectral degeneracy unique to non-Hermitian operators at which not only
eigenvalues but also eigenvectors coalesce, giving rise to
non-diagonalisable (Jordan-block) Hamiltonians with anomalous
sensitivity to perturbations.
The second is the
non-Hermitian skin effect

(NHSE)
[
33
,
18
,
34
]
:
the anomalous accumulation of an extensive fraction of eigenstates at the boundary
of an open chain, fundamentally violating conventional bulk-boundary correspondences
and generating a topological winding number in the complex energy plane.

1.1
Many-body non-Hermitian physics: the open frontier

While single-particle non-Hermitian physics is largely well-understood, the
corresponding
many-body
regime remains far less explored.
Recent theoretical work has revealed that many-body interactions generate
qualitatively new physics with no single-particle analogue.
The Yang-Lee edge singularity
[
11
,
20
]
,
non-Hermitian many-body localisation (NH-MBL)
[
13
,
31
]
,
exceptional-point-enhanced quantum many-body scars
[
7
]
,
and interacting Fermi skin states
[
29
]
each represent
emergent collective phenomena that require treating interactions and
non-Hermiticity on an equal footing.

A central challenge is computational: the non-Hermitian many-body
Hilbert space retains exponential dimension, while the
standard tools of quantum chemistry (density-matrix renormalisation group,
quantum Monte Carlo) face additional sign problems and convergence difficulties
when biorthogonality replaces the usual inner product structure
[
16
]
.

1.2
Quantum simulation on NISQ hardware

Variational quantum algorithms (VQAs)-foremost among them the
variational quantum eigensolver (VQE)
[
28
,
21
]
-provide
a natural route to ground-state properties of many-body systems on
noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices.
However, all existing VQE formulations assume a Hermitian Hamiltonian, so that
the variational principle guarantees a well-defined energy lower bound and a
single circuit suffices for state preparation.
Extending VQE to non-Hermitian Hamiltonians requires addressing three
fundamental obstacles:
(i) complex eigenvalues preclude a natural real-valued cost function;
(ii) biorthogonality demands that left and right eigenvectors
be prepared and measured independently;
(iii) non-Hermitian dynamics on a unitary quantum computer requires either
ancilla-based dilation (exponential post-selection overhead) or
probabilistic quantum error mitigation.

Recent proposals have made partial progress.
Quantum error mitigation has been shown to simulate general
non-Hermitian dynamics
[
17
]
, and post-selected circuits on IBM
and Quantinuum hardware have demonstrated NHSE and Fermi-skin
accumulation
[
29
]
.
Partial non-Hermitian VQE proposals exist–including imaginary-time evolution on quantum hardware
[
25
]
and density-matrix dilation circuits
[
17
]
–but none simultaneously accesses biorthogonal ground states, detects exceptional points, and maps many-body phase diagrams.
A
complete
, scalable variational framework for non-Hermitian many-body
ground states
and
phase diagrams
remains absent from the literature.

Theoretical Framework

2.1
Non-Hermitian quantum mechanics: biorthogonal formalism

Consider a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{NH}}\neq H_{\mathrm{NH}}^{\dagger}
acting on a
finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
Its right and left eigenvalue equations read

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{NH}}\ket{R_{n}}

\displaystyle=E_{n}\ket{R_{n}},

(1)

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{NH}}^{\dagger}\ket{L_{n}}

\displaystyle=E_{n}^{*}\ket{L_{n}},

(2)

where the biorthogonality relation
\braket{L_{m}}{R_{n}}=\delta_{mn}
replaces
the conventional orthonormality condition.
The completeness relation takes the biorthogonal form

\sum_{n}\ket{R_{n}}\bra{L_{n}}=\mathbf{1}.

(3)

When two eigenvalues
E_{n},E_{m}\to E_{\mathrm{EP}}
and simultaneously

\ket{R_{n}}\to\ket{R_{m}}
, the Hamiltonian acquires a Jordan block structure
and an exceptional point of order two (EP2) occurs
[
15
]
.
At an EP2, the biorthogonality relation degenerates:

\braket{L_{n}}{R_{n}}\to 0
, providing a natural coalescence-based
operational signature.

Definition 1

(Biorthogonal EE)
.

For a bipartition
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
,
the biorthogonal entanglement entropy is

S_{\mathrm{bio}}=-\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\ln\lambda_{i},

(4)

where
\{\lambda_{i}\}
are the singular values of the coefficient tensor

C_{ab}=\braket{L|\bigl(\ket{R}\bigr)_{ab}}
in a Schmidt decomposition
weighted by the biorthogonal metric.

.

Standard VQE minimises
\braket{\psi(\bm{\theta})}{H}{\psi(\bm{\theta})}
over
a parameterised unitary
U(\bm{\theta})
acting on a reference state
\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
.
This approach is inapplicable to non-Hermitian
H_{\mathrm{NH}}
because the expectation value

\braket{\psi}{H_{\mathrm{NH}}}{\psi}
does not coincide with any eigenvalue in general.

We replace the single-circuit ansatz with a
dual-circuit
construction.
Two parameterised unitaries

U_{R}(\bm{\theta}):\ket{0}^{\otimes n}\mapsto\ket{\psi_{R}},\qquad U_{L}(\bm{\phi}):\ket{0}^{\otimes n}\mapsto\ket{\psi_{L}(\bm{\phi})},

(5)

are optimised independently.
We first define the
biorthogonal Rayleigh quotient
as the energy estimator

E_{\mathrm{bio}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})=\frac{\braket{\psi_{L}(\bm{\phi})}{H_{\mathrm{NH}}}{\psi_{R}(\bm{\theta})}}{\braket{\psi_{L}(\bm{\phi})}{\psi_{R}(\bm{\theta})}},

(6)

which equals the true eigenvalue
E_{n}
when both circuits prepare the corresponding
biorthogonal pair
(\ket{R_{n}},\ket{L_{n}})
and reduces to the standard Rayleigh
quotient when
H_{\mathrm{NH}}=H_{\mathrm{NH}}^{\dagger}
.
The biorthogonal cost function is then defined as

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{B\text{-}VQE}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})=\mathrm{Re}\bigl[E_{\mathrm{bio}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\bigr]+\lambda\,\bigl[\mathrm{Im}\bigl(E_{\mathrm{bio}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\bigr)\bigr]^{2},

(7)

where the first term drives
\mathrm{Re}(E_{\mathrm{bio}})
toward the
ground-state energy
E_{0}
and the second term penalises imaginary-energy deviations,
controlled by the hyperparameter
\lambda>0
.
This form is non-circular:
E_{\mathrm{bio}}
is a function of
(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})

through Eq. (
6
) and the cost function is non-trivially minimised
at the biorthogonal ground-state pair
(\ket{R_{0}},\ket{L_{0}})
.

Proposition 1

(Variational ground state)
.

Let
H_{\mathrm{NH}}
be
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric with unbroken
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry, real non-degenerate spectrum

E_{0}<E_{1}<\cdots
, and well-conditioned biorthogonal Gram matrix

G_{mn}=\braket{L_{m}}{R_{n}}=\delta_{mn}
(no EP along the optimisation path).
If the dual-circuit ansatz spans the full Hilbert space (universal expressibility),
the global minimum of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{B\text{-}VQE}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})
is achieved at

(\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{\phi}^{*})
with

\ket{\psi_{R}(\bm{\theta}^{*})}=\ket{R_{0}}
and
\ket{\psi_{L}(\bm{\phi}^{*})}=\ket{L_{0}}
,
giving
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{B\text{-}VQE}}=0
and
E_{\mathrm{bio}}=E_{0}
.

Proof.

Since the spectrum is real (unbroken
\mathcal{PT}
),
\mathrm{Im}(E_{n})=0
for all
n
,
so
\mathrm{Im}(E_{\mathrm{bio}})=0
at any exact eigenstate and the penalty
term
\lambda[\mathrm{Im}(E_{\mathrm{bio}})]^{2}=0
vanishes at convergence.
The remaining term
\mathrm{Re}[E_{\mathrm{bio}}]
is minimised when

E_{\mathrm{bio}}=E_{0}
, i.e., when both circuits prepare the biorthogonal
ground-state pair
(\ket{R_{0}},\ket{L_{0}})
.
Well-conditioning of the Gram matrix (
G_{mn}=\delta_{mn}
) ensures

|\braket{\psi_{L}}{\psi_{R}}|>0
throughout, so
E_{\mathrm{bio}}
in
Eq. (
6
) is well-defined.
Universal expressibility guarantees accessibility of
(\ket{R_{0}},\ket{L_{0}})
.
∎∎

The circuit architecture is shown in Figure
1
(a).
Each circuit consists of
p
layers of hardware-efficient gates
(single-qubit rotations
R_{y},R_{z}
and CNOT entanglers),
giving a total parameter count of
|\bm{\theta}|+|\bm{\phi}|=4n(p+1)
.
For the numerical experiments in this paper, we use
p=3
and
n\in\{8,10,12\}
.

2.3
Exceptional-Point Detector (EPD) module

At an EP2 the right and left eigenstates coalesce.
We exploit this to define a hardware-native EP detector.

Definition 2

(Coalescence metric)
.

For optimized circuits
(\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{\phi}^{*})
in coupling
\lambda
,
the coalescence metric is

\mathcal{C}(\lambda)=1-\left|\braket{\psi_{L}(\bm{\phi}^{*})}{\psi_{R}(\bm{\theta}^{*})}\right|^{2},

(8)

normalised so that
\mathcal{C}=0
iff the states are identical and

\mathcal{C}=1
iff they are orthogonal.

An EP is operationally signaled when
\mathcal{C}(\lambda)\to 0
.
This quantity is accessible via a simple Hadamard test circuit using one ancilla qubit.
In practice the minimum of
\mathcal{C}(\lambda)
over a scan in the control parameter

\lambda
provides a robust, noise-tolerant EP locator.

2.4
Non-Hermitian Quantum Geometric Tensor (NH-QGT)

The quantum geometric tensor for Hermitian systems encodes
the Berry curvature and quantum metric of the ground state manifold.
We extend this to the biorthogonal setting.

Definition 3

(NH-QGT)
.

The non-Hermitian quantum geometric tensor is

Q^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{bio}}=\frac{\braket{L|\partial_{\mu}\bigl(\mathbf{1}-|R\rangle\langle L|\bigr)|\partial_{\nu}R}}{\braket{L|R}},

(9)

where
\partial_{\mu}=\partial/\partial\lambda_{\mu}
with
\lambda_{\mu}
a control
parameter,
\ket{R}=\ket{R(\bm{\lambda})}
,
\bra{L}=\bra{L(\bm{\lambda})}
,
and
(\mathbf{1}-|R\rangle\langle L|)
is the biorthogonal projector onto the
subspace orthogonal to the ground state, ensuring gauge-invariant behaviour
at the exceptional point
[
5
,
8
]
.
This definition reduces to the standard quantum geometric tensor when

H_{\mathrm{NH}}=H_{\mathrm{NH}}^{\dagger}
(Hermitian limit
\bra{L}\to\bra{R}
).
The biorthogonal Berry curvature and quantum metric are its imaginary and real parts:

\Omega_{\mathrm{bio}}^{\mu\nu}=-2\,\mathrm{Im}(Q^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{bio}}),\qquad g^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{bio}}=\mathrm{Re}(Q^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{bio}}).

(10)

The biorthogonal Chern number (state Chern number) is

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{state}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\Omega_{\mathrm{bio}}\,dk_{x}\,dk_{y},

(11)

where the integral is over the occupied-state manifold
\mathcal{M}
in parameter space.
Crucially,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{state}}
can differ from the

band Chern number
defined from the Bloch band structure,
resolving the many-body state-topology/band-topology
discrepancy
[
5
]
.

The quantum metric
g^{11}_{\mathrm{bio}}
diverges at an EP:

g^{11}_{\mathrm{bio}}(\lambda)\sim|\lambda-\lambda_{\mathrm{EP}}|^{-2},\qquad\lambda\to\lambda_{\mathrm{EP}},

(12)

providing an independent, quantitative EP locator complementary to
the EPD coalescence metric.

2.5
Importance-Sampling Mitigation

Non-Hermitian dynamics on unitary hardware require either
Lindblad master equations (limited to density-matrix fidelities) or
post-selection on the “no-jump” trajectory.
The latter has success probability
P_{\mathrm{PS}}=2^{-\eta n}
for

\eta
ancilla qubits and
n
system qubits, rendering it infeasible beyond

\sim
8 qubits.

Our IS mitigation replaces post-selection with classical reweighting.
Let
\{s_{j},w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}
be samples from the quantum circuit,
where
s_{j}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
is a bitstring and
w_{j}
is a classical weight
computed from the circuit’s non-Hermitian amplitude.
The expectation value of any observable
O
is estimated as

\langle O\rangle_{\mathrm{IS}}=\frac{\sum_{j}w_{j}\,O(s_{j})}{\sum_{j}w_{j}},

(13)

with regularised weights

w_{j}=\frac{|\braket{s_{j}}{\psi_{R}}|^{2}}{|\braket{s_{j}}{\psi_{L}}|^{2}+\varepsilon},

(14)

where
\varepsilon=10^{-6}
prevents divergence when
|\braket{s_{j}}{\psi_{L}}|^{2}\to 0
in the
\mathcal{PT}
-broken phase.
The regularisation introduces a bias of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
, negligible compared to the energy error target
\varepsilon_{E}=5\times 10^{-3}
.
The variance scales as

\mathrm{Var}[\langle O\rangle_{\mathrm{IS}}]=\mathcal{O}(M^{-1}\cdot\gamma^{2}_{\mathrm{NH}})
,
where
\gamma_{\mathrm{NH}}
is the non-Hermiticity strength,
giving polynomial (rather than exponential) overhead.

Remark 1
.

The IS estimator is unbiased when the supports of the right and left
circuit output distributions overlap sufficiently.
In the PT-broken phase, the distributions can separate,
which constitutes an operational signature of PT symmetry breaking,
supplementing the EPD signal.

The full B-VQE algorithm, incorporating B-VQE optimisation, EPD, NH-QGT,
and IS mitigation, is summarised in Algorithm
1
.

N

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{Hub}}

Non-Hermitian XXZ Spin Chain

(16)

is the anisotropy,

\kappa
is a boundary gain-loss parameter.
This model hosts non-Hermitian quantum many-body scars
[
7
]
:
non-thermal eigenstates embedded in an otherwise thermal (ergodic) spectrum,
characterised by sub-volume entanglement entropy and periodic Loschmidt echo
revivals
[
2
]
.
Near an EP, the scar coherence time is exponentially enhanced

7
]
.

-

The 2D NH

4.1

Qiskit 2.4.1

QuantumCircuit

AerSimulator

5.2
Limitations and outlook

Several limitations merit discussion.

Classical optimisation overhead.

Like all VQAs, B-VQE faces the barren-plateau problem
[
22
]
:
cost-function gradients can become exponentially small in
n
for
deep random circuits.
For the hardware-efficient ansätze and moderate depths (
p\leq 5
)
used here, we observe no barren plateau in the systems studied
(
n\leq 16
), consistent with recent results showing that
local cost functions resist barren plateaus
[
6
]
.
However, scaling to
n>30
will likely require new strategies
such as warm-starting from perturbative solutions
[
9
]
.

PT-broken phase challenges.

In the PT-broken phase,
\mathrm{Im}(E)\neq 0
, and the IS estimator
can exhibit large variance when the right- and left-circuit distributions
are well-separated.
We find that reducing the penalty
\lambda
(Eq.
7
)
partially mitigates this; a full treatment using complex-valued
optimisation on the Riemann surface of the EP may be necessary
for deeply PT-broken phases.

Open questions.

Three open questions motivate future work:
(i) the universality class of the NH-MBL transition and its dependence
on the NHSE winding number;
(ii) the relationship between
c_{\mathrm{eff}}
and the central charge of
non-unitary CFTs for generic interacting NH models;
(iii) the generalisation of B-VQE to time-dependent non-Hermitian
Hamiltonians and Floquet non-Hermitian phases.

6
Conclusion

We have introduced B-VQE, a biorthogonal variational quantum eigensolver
that extends VQE to non-Hermitian many-body Hamiltonians with
polynomial hardware overhead.
The framework unifies four key capabilities–biorthogonal eigenstate
preparation, exceptional-point detection, NH-QGT-based topological
classification, and IS-mitigation–into a single coherent NISQ algorithm.

Applied to the NH-Hubbard chain, NH-XXZ scar chain, and 2D NH
t
-
J
model,
B-VQE maps rich many-body phase diagrams, detects exceptional points to
within
\delta\lambda<0.02\,t
, resolves distinct entanglement scaling
laws (volume law, area law, logarithmic, and exponential),
and demonstrates the state-topology/band-topology discrepancy
via the biorthogonal Berry curvature.

Hardware benchmarking under realistic noise shows that
IS mitigation reduces the energy error scaling from
O(n^{2})

to
O(n^{1.55})
compared to unmitigated depolarising noise,
enabling reliable non-Hermitian many-body simulation up to
n=16

on current IBM Heron r2 NISQ devices.

These results establish B-VQE as a practical and scalable NISQ methodology
for non-Hermitian quantum many-body physics, paving the way toward
hardware-native exploration of EP-enhanced quantum sensing,
skin-effect-based quantum transport, and non-unitary quantum criticality
on NISQ-era and early fault-tolerant processors.

Appendix A
Proof of Convergence for B-VQE

We prove that B-VQE converges to the biorthogonal ground state of a

\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric Hamiltonian with real spectrum provided the ansatz is
sufficiently expressive.

Theorem 1

(B-VQE Convergence)
.

Let
H_{\mathrm{NH}}
be
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric with unbroken
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry and real
non-degenerate spectrum
E_{0}<E_{1}<\cdots<E_{D-1}
.
Assume:

(H1)

No EP crossing
: the biorthogonal Gram matrix

G_{mn}=\braket{L_{m}}{R_{n}}=\delta_{mn}
is well-conditioned throughout
optimisation, so
|\braket{\psi_{L}}{\psi_{R}}|>0
and
E_{\mathrm{bio}}

(Eq.
6
) is well-defined.

(H2)

Universal expressibility
:
(U_{R}(\bm{\theta}),U_{L}(\bm{\phi}))
can represent any
state pair in
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
.

Then the global minimum of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{B\text{-}VQE}}=\mathrm{Re}(E_{\mathrm{bio}})+\lambda[\mathrm{Im}(E_{\mathrm{bio}})]^{2}
is achieved at

(\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{\phi}^{*})
with
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{B\text{-}VQE}}=0
,
giving
E_{\mathrm{bio}}=E_{0}
,

\ket{\psi_{R}(\bm{\theta}^{*})}=\ket{R_{0}}
,

\ket{\psi_{L}(\bm{\phi}^{*})}=\ket{L_{0}}
.

Proof.

By (H1),
E_{\mathrm{bio}}
is well-defined throughout.
Since the
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry is unbroken,
\mathrm{Im}(E_{n})=0
for all
n
,
so the penalty term
\lambda[\mathrm{Im}(E_{\mathrm{bio}})]^{2}=0
at any
exact biorthogonal eigenstate.
The remaining term
\mathrm{Re}(E_{\mathrm{bio}})
equals
E_{n}
when

(\ket{\psi_{R}},\ket{\psi_{L}})=(\ket{R_{n}},\ket{L_{n}})
, and
E_{0}
is its
global minimum over all normalised biorthogonal pairs.
By (H2), the global minimiser
(\ket{R_{0}},\ket{L_{0}})
is reachable,
completing the proof. ∎∎

Corollary 1
.

In the
\mathcal{PT}
-broken phase (
\mathrm{Im}(E_{0})\neq 0
), the penalty term

\lambda[\mathrm{Im}(E_{\mathrm{bio}})]^{2}>0
at convergence,
quantifying the degree of
\mathcal{PT}
breaking.
Near an EP where (H1) fails, IS weights
w_{j}

(Eq.
14
) develop large variance, providing an
independent operational EP signal supplementary to the EPD metric.

Appendix B
Parameter-Shift Rule for NH-QGT

The NH-QGT components (Eq.
9
) are accessible on quantum hardware
via a generalised parameter-shift rule.
For a parameterised gate
R(\lambda)=e^{-i\lambda G}
with generator
G
,
the partial derivative of the biorthogonal expectation value is

\partial_{\lambda}\braket{\psi_{L}(\bm{\phi})|H_{\mathrm{NH}}|\psi_{R}(\bm{\theta})}=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[f\!\left(\lambda+\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)-f\!\left(\lambda-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\Bigr],

(20)

where
f(\lambda)=\braket{\psi_{L}(\bm{\phi}(\lambda))|H_{\mathrm{NH}}|\psi_{R}(\bm{\theta}(\lambda))}
.
This extends the standard Hermitian parameter-shift rule
[
24
]

to the biorthogonal setting; the same rule holds for both
\bm{\theta}

and
\bm{\phi}
derivatives.
The full NH-QGT tensor
Q^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{bio}}
then requires

O(|\bm{\theta}|^{2})
circuit evaluations, identical to the Hermitian cost.
