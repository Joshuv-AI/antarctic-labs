Existence of minimal Del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 with conic bundles over finite fields
Manoy T. Trip

Abstract
. We study minimal del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 with a conic bundle over a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} according to the action of the absolute Galois group on the singular fibers (which is known as their type). We give a lower bound on the size of the field over which they exist, and determine values of q q for which certain types cannot exist. In particular, we solve the inverse Galois problem for certain types of minimal del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 over finite fields with a conic bundle structure. Additionally, we give bounds on the values of q q for which del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 of index 8 exist over 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} .

1.
Introduction

A del Pezzo surface
X
over a field
k
is a smooth, projective, geometrically integral surface with an ample anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
. Its degree
d
is the self-intersection number
K_{X}^{2}
, and we have
1\leq d\leq 9
. All
k
-minimal rational surfaces are either del Pezzo surfaces or standard conic bundles
[
iskovskih
, Theorem 1]
, which motivates the study of these two types of minimal surfaces.

Let
k
be a perfect field,
\overline{k}
a fixed algebraic closure of
k
, and let
\overline{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X\times_{\Spec k}\Spec\overline{k}
. Del Pezzo surfaces over
k
can be classified based on the action of the absolute Galois group of
k
on
\overline{X}
, which induces automorphisms on
\Pic(\overline{X})
that preserve
K_{\overline{X}}
and the intersection pairing. The subgroup of such automorphisms is isomorphic to the Weyl group of a root system
R_{9-d}
, which only depends on the degree
d
of
X
.

Let us restrict ourselves to the case where
k
is a finite field. Let
X
be a del Pezzo surface over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, of degree
d\leq 6
. We consider the action of
\Gal(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
on
\Pic(\overline{X})
, which can be identified with a cyclic subgroup in
W(R_{9-d})
, generated by the image of the Frobenius automorphism. Let
C(X)
denote the conjugacy class of this image of the Frobenius automorphism in
W(R_{9-d})
. We call this class the
type
of
X
. We consider the following problem:

The inverse Galois problem for del Pezzo surfaces over finite fields.

Let
q
be a prime power, and
1\leq d\leq 6
. For each conjugacy class
C
of
W(R_{9-d})
, does there exist a del Pezzo surface
X/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of degree
d
such that
C(X)=C
?

1.3.
Methods in relation to the literature

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we use two distinct strategies. For the first strategy, which is treated in Section
4
, we use the existence of conic bundles of a certain type to construct corresponding del Pezzo surfaces with conic bundle structure. This method is modeled after
[
trepalindeg2
]
, which deals with the analogous problem for del Pezzo surfaces of degree 2. We prove Proposition
4.6
, which sorts minimal standard conic bundles of degree 1 into five possible cases. This is analogous to
[
trepalindeg2
, Proposition 2.3]
for degree 2. Each of the seven types of minimal del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 with conic bundle structure is uniquely determined by the Galois action on the singular fibers of the conic bundle. We use
[
rybakov
, Theorem 2.11]
, which guarantees the existence of a minimal conic bundle with the corresponding Galois action for each type (except for some very small values of
q
, see Lemma
3.14
). We then construct birational maps to transform it into a del Pezzo surface of the corresponding type.

The second strategy (Section
5
) uses that for any del Pezzo surface of degree 1, there exists a quadratic twist, called the Bertini twist, which is again a del Pezzo surface of degree 1. For surfaces over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of types 1, 2 and 4, the corresponding twist is no longer a minimal surface, and in fact it contains an exceptional curve defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, which can be contracted to obtain a del Pezzo surface of degree 2 of a specific type. In
[
LoughranTrepalin
, Theorem 1.2]
, we find for which
q
such a del Pezzo surface of degree 2 exists. If such a surface
Y
exists, we show for large enough
q
that we can find an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-point
P
on
Y
such that the blow-up of
Y
in
P
results in a del Pezzo surface of degree 1 of the required type. For the twist of type 1, this is already done in
[
1
]
, and we adapt their strategy for all other types of del Pezzo surfaces which contain an exceptional curve defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, which includes in particular the twists of type 2 and 4. For small values of
q
and index 8, we also adapt Magma code from
[
1
]
to computationally confirm existence or non-existence, completing the solution in those cases.

1.4.
Organization of the paper

We start this paper by giving a background of the relevant theory of del Pezzo surfaces (§
2.1
) and conic bundles (§
2.2
). In §
2.3
, we introduce the notion of the type of a del Pezzo surface over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. We also define the index, trace and Picard rank of a del Pezzo surface, properties which are uniquely determined by its type. In §
2.4
, we recall an explicit description of the Picard group of a standard conic bundle over an algebraically closed field, and describe the Galois action on this group. We explain a result by Rybakov
[
rybakov
]
about the existence of minimal standard conic bundles in §
3.1
.

In Section
3
, we use Theorem
3.2
to obtain an explicit characterization of minimal standard conic bundles over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
in the degree 1 case, based on the Galois action on their singular fibers. In §
3.2
and §
3.3
we link this characterization to the classification of minimal del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 with conic bundles, and demonstrate a correspondence between these two classifications. This allows us in §
3.4
to give, for a surface
X
of each type, an explicit element in the conjugacy class of the action of the Frobenius automorphism on
\Pic(\overline{X})
. This gives us a first result about the non-existence of certain types of del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 (§
3.5
).

In Section
4
, we get into the first method used to partially prove Theorem
1.1
. To construct del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1, we use elementary transformations of conic bundles, which are introduced in §
4.1
. We divide minimal standard conic bundles of degree 1 in 5 different cases, numbered (1), (2a), (2b), (2c) and (3), depending on which curves with negative self-intersection they contain (§
4.2
, Proposition
4.6
). Case (3) is the case where the surface is a del Pezzo surface. We treat the other cases one by one in §
4.3
to §
4.7
. For conic bundle surfaces
X
satisfying each of the cases, we find an explicit description of the classes in
\Pic(\overline{X})
of curves with negative self-intersection. We determine lower bounds for
q
above which there is a point on a smooth fiber over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
that allows an elementary transformation resulting in a conic bundle satisfying a different case in Proposition
4.6
, eventually transforming the surface into a del Pezzo surface. This results in Theorem
4.36
, which is a partial result to Theorem
1.1
, and also allows us to prove Theorem
1.2
.

In Section
5
, we lay out a different proof strategy, where we show the existence of del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of types that contain a
(-1)
-curve defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. The types 1, 2 and 4 have quadratic twists of this form, which allows us to improve the result of Theorem
4.36
and conclude our main result in Theorem
1.1
. Using this method, we also get a complete solution for the inverse Galois problem for all but one of the types of index 8. This results in Theorem
1.3
.

Background

We will call a smooth, projective, geometrically integral surface a
nice

Minimal rational surfaces can be classified as follows.

Theorem 2.1

(
[

)

Let

is a del Pezzo surface (see §

(2)

\Pic(X)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
and it admits the structure of a standard conic bundle (see §

Any nice surface defined over a field

is birational over

[

, Proposition II.16]
. Several arithmetic properties of varieties, such as unirationality and the Hilbert property
[

implies that it is enough to consider questions about such properties for minimal del Pezzo surfaces and minimal conic bundles.

Recall that any birational morphism of smooth projective surfaces over

Definition 2.2
.

Let

2.1.
Del Pezzo surfaces

Recall that a del Pezzo surface
X

is a nice surface with an ample anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
such that

. Over an algebraically closed field, del Pezzo surfaces can be described as follows.

Theorem 2.3

(
[

)
.

Let

, and we have the following characterization.

(i)

If

(ii)

If

{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

(iii)

is a blow-up of

in

(i.e. no three points lie on a line, no six points lie on a conic, and no eight points lie on a singular cubic with one of them being the singularity).

If

(

Let

In this paper, we consider del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 of types 1 to 7, which are the minimal del Pezzo surfaces with a conic bundle structure. 2.5.
The inverse Galois problem for del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1

In this paper, we consider the inverse Galois problem for del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 over finite fields, the only degree in which it is still largely unsolved. The 112 conjugacy classes of
W(E_{8})
, corresponding to the types of del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 over finite fields, are tabulated in
[
19
, Table 2]
, together with a number of corresponding properties.

For a smooth projective surface

, we denote by
a(X)
the trace of the Frobenius action on
\Pic(\overline{X})
, and call this the
trace of
X
. The trace is uniquely determined by the type of
X
. The number of rational points on

is determined by this trace, given by the following formula.

Theorem 2.17

(
[
manin
, Theorem 27.1]
)
.

Let

be a smooth projective geometrically rational surface over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Let

be the trace of the Frobenius action on
\Pic(\overline{X})
. Then

\#X({\mathbb{F}}_{q})=q^{2}+a(X)q+1.

In particular,
X({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\neq\emptyset
.

In
[
1
, Theorem 1.5]
, Banwait, Fité and Loughran determine the set of values of
q
for which a del Pezzo surface of degree 1 of a given trace exists. Because the trace is uniquely determined for a given type, classifying by type gives a finer classification of del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 than classifying by trace. The proof in
[
1
]
involves, for each trace value, explicitly choosing a type of surface which has that trace, and demonstrating the existence of a surface of this type. In this paper, we treat a number of types which were not treated in
[
1
]
.

The index of a surface, defined as follows, is a second invariant of a del Pezzo surface determined by its type.

Definition 2.18
.

Let
X
be a nice surface defined over a perfect field
k
. The

of
X
is the size of the largest set of pairwise skew
(-1)
-curves which is stable under the action of the absolute Galois group of

.

It follows from Castelnuovo’s contractibility criterion
[
13
, Theorem 9.3.3]
that a surface
X
is minimal if and only if it has index 0. Table 2 in
[
19
]
lists the index for each type of del Pezzo surface of degree 1 over finite fields. Following the numbering in the first column of Urabe’s table, we note that the minimal del Pezzo surfaces are the ones of types 1 to 37.

The Picard rank of a del Pezzo surface
X
, denoted by
\rho(X)
, is another property that is determined by its type. Among the minimal surfaces, we find using a Magma
[
magma
]
computation (Magma code can be found on GitHub page
[
18
]
) that surfaces of types 1 to 7 have Picard rank 2, and surfaces of types 8 to 37 have Picard rank 1. Using Theorem

and Proposition

, we conclude that surfaces of type 1 to 7 have a conic bundle structure, and surfaces of types 8 to 37 do not have a conic bundle structure.

3.
Classification of minimal standard conic bundles of degree 1

In what follows, we present a result by Rybakov which characterizes the singular fibers of minimal standard rational conic bundles over

(Theorem

4.1.
Elementary transformations

We will repeatedly use the following type of transformation (see for example

). In this subsection, let

Definition 4.1
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow B

of

Let

By construction, the inverse of an elementary transformation is again an elementary transformation.

Definition 4.3

Let

is not a (component of a) fiber of

-section

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow B
be a standard rational conic bundle over
k
, and let
P
be a rational point on a smooth fiber
F_{1}
of

. Consider the elementary transformation
\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle P}=\pi_{F_{1}}\circ\pi_{P}^{-1}\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
at

. Let
D
be an
n
-section on

. Let

denote its strict transform under the morphism

Let

be the multiplicity of

at

. Let

with self-intersection
-2

(3)

The surface
X
is a del Pezzo surface of degree 1 admitting exactly two conic bundle structures.

To prove this proposition, we separately consider the cases where
-K_{X}
is not nef (Lemma

Lemma 4.7
.

Let

be a minimal standard rational conic bundle of degree

over a perfect field

on

Proof.

In
[
9

, it is shown that

, where

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow B
be a minimal standard rational conic bundle of degree

over a perfect field

such that
-K_{X}
is nef, but not ample. Then there is precisely one integral curve
A
on

-K_{X}\cdot[A]=0

for some geometrically integral

for

is the class of a fiber of

(1)

and

(2)

C_{i}\cdot F=1

and

(3)

, and

.

on
\overline{X}

.

Proof.

Because
-K_{X}
is nef but not ample, there is an integral curve

on

by the Nakai–Moishezon Criterion

Lemma 4.14

Let

Proof.

The surface

[

Del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 of index 8

If
X
has index 8, the surface over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is obtained as a blow-up of a collection of closed points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, the sum of the degrees of which is
8
, which lie in general position (Theorem
2.3
). Each type is uniquely determined by the degree of the closed points that are blown up. The characterization can be derived from the Galois orbits of the
(-1)
-curves on
X
, and is given in Table
7
in the
Appendix
. We can computationally demonstrate existence or non-existence of each of these types over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
for small values of
q
(i.e. the values not mentioned in Table
6
for those types), by showing closed points of the appropriate degrees in general position exist in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, using code presented in
[
1
]
, where a number of cases are already treated (one for each trace). The Magma code can be found in
[
18
]
. Together with the result in Table
6
, we can obtain a complete classification of the existence of types of del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 of index 8 which contain a
(-1)
-curve defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. The result is given in Table
8
(
Appendix
). It is moreover shown in
[
1
, 18]
that type 112 exists for all values of
q
. Together with the results in Table
8
, this solves the inverse Galois problem for del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 and index 8 over finite fields for all types except for type 108, which corresponds to a blow-up of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
in two closed points of degree 4 in general position.

Theorem 5.2
.

(1)

Del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 of type 91 exist over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
if and only if
q=16
or
q\geq 19
.

(2)

Del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 of type 92 exist over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
if and only if
q\geq 11
.

(3)

Del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 of type 93, 94 and 95 exist over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
if and only if
q\geq 7
.

(4)

Del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 of type 97 exist over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
if and only if
q\geq 5
.

(5)

Del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 of type 96, 98 and 99 exist over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
if and only if
q\geq 4
.

(6)

Del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 of type 100, 101, 102, 103, 104, 105 and 107 exist over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
if and only if
q\geq 3
.

(7)

Del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 of type 106, 109, 110, 111, 112 exist over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
for all
q
.

Proof.

Part of this result is shown in
[
1
]
, and the rest is completed by Magma computations
[
18
]
. In column 3 of Table
8
the references to
[
1
]
per type are given, where applicable.
∎

Now we have all the results necessary to obtain the statements of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Most of the result is proven in Theorem
4.36
. For types 1, 2 and 4, we use that their Bertini twists are of types 97, 105 and 73, respectively. The result is completed using Magma as described above (see Magma code in
[
18
]
), see Table
8
for types 97 and 105, and Table
6
for type 73.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We know that a del Pezzo surface of degree 1 of a given type exists if and only if its Bertini twist exists. The result follows from Theorem
5.2
, matching each type with its Bertini twist. The pairing can be found in Table
4
.
∎
