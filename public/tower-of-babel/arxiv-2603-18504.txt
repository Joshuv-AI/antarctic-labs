Philip Schrader, Glen Wheeler, Valentina-Mira Wheeler

Abstract
. We study the gradient flow of the length functional on the space of planar immersed closed curves, where the gradient is taken with respect to a family of homogeneous Sobolev H 1 H^{1} -type Riemannian metrics depending on parameters λ > 0 \lambda>0 and a ∈ ℝ a\in\mathbb{R} . The gradient can be written explicitly in terms of arc-length convolution with the periodic Green’s function for the second-order operator associated with the H 1 H^{1} metric, and then the gradient flow is a reparametrisation-invariant nonlocal ODE. Working in the optimal low-regularity setting W 1 , 1 ​ ( 𝕊 , ℝ 2 ) W^{1,1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2}) , we show that the gradient is locally Lipschitz to obtain local well-posedness via the Picard–Lindelöf theorem in Banach spaces. A time-reparametrisation reduces the analysis for general a a to the model case a = 2 {a=2} , for which we obtain exponential decay of the length and global existence with uniform convergence in W 1 , 1 W^{1,1} to a constant map. For C 1 C^{1} immersed initial data we show that immersion is preserved for all time, and we further prove that if the initial curve bounds a convex set then convexity is also preserved by the flow.

1.
Introduction

Let
\imm^{1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2})
denote the space of immersions of the circle into the Euclidean plane, i.e. continuously differentiable maps
\gamma:\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
|\gamma^{\prime}|\neq 0
, where
\mathbb{S}=[0,1]/{\sim}
. The length functional:

(1)

\mathcal{L}(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{1}{\left|{\gamma^{\prime}(u)}\right|}du

is differentiable on
\imm^{1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2})
with the derivative of
\mathcal{L}
at
\gamma\in\imm^{1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2})
in the direction of a variation
v:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by

(2)

\begin{split}D\mathcal{L}(\gamma)v={\left.\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\langle\partial_{\varepsilon}\partial_{u}(\gamma+\varepsilon v),\partial_{u}(\gamma+\varepsilon v)\rangle}{|\partial_{u}(\gamma+\varepsilon v)|}du\right|}_{\varepsilon=0}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\langle\gamma^{\prime}(u),v^{\prime}(u)\rangle}{\left|{\gamma^{\prime}(u)}\right|}du,\end{split}

where the inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the Euclidean one.

A gradient direction
\nabla\mathcal{L}(\gamma)
for the length functional at
\gamma
is determined by a choice of inner product or Riemannian metric
{\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle}_{\gamma}
on
\imm^{1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2})
according to
D\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}={\left\langle{\nabla\mathcal{L}(\gamma),\cdot}\right\rangle}_{\gamma}
. For example, in the parametrisation invariant
L^{2}
metric defined by

{\left\langle{v,w}\right\rangle}_{L^{2}(\gamma)}:=\int_{\mathbb{S}}{\left\langle{v(u),w(u)}\right\rangle}\,|\gamma^{\prime}(u)|du,

after reparametrisation and integration by parts in (
2
) the gradient is
kN
, where
k
the curvature scalar and
N
the normal vector along
\gamma
. The corresponding gradient flow is the well-known curve shortening flow (see the classical papers
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
, and the more recent book
[
1
]
Chapters 2-3).

Motivated by results of Michor and Mumford showing that the Riemannian distance in the
L^{2}(\gamma)
metric is always zero
[
8
]
, and that Sobolev
H^{1}
type metrics do not have this pathology, the authors previously studied
[
9
]
the gradient flow of
\mathcal{L}
with respect to the metric

{{\left\langle{v,w}\right\rangle}_{L^{2}(\gamma)}+{\left\langle{\tfrac{1}{\left|{\gamma^{\prime}}\right|}v^{\prime},\tfrac{1}{\left|{\gamma^{\prime}}\right|}w^{\prime}}\right\rangle}_{L^{2}(\gamma)}}.

This flow exhibits scale dependent behaviour because the two summands in the metric have different scaling under dilations of the plane.

In this paper we consider instead the gradients of
\mathcal{L}
corresponding to the Riemannian metrics:

(3)

\displaystyle{\left\langle{v,w}\right\rangle}_{H^{1}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{a}}{\left\langle{v,w}\right\rangle}_{L^{2}(\gamma)}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{a-2}}{\left\langle{\tfrac{1}{\left|{\gamma^{\prime}}\right|}v^{\prime},\tfrac{1}{\left|{\gamma^{\prime}}\right|}w^{\prime}}\right\rangle}_{L^{2}(\gamma)},

where
\lambda>0,a\in\mathbb{R}
are parameters.
In (
3
) the powers of
\mathcal{L}
ensure that both summands have the same scaling. Indeed if we dilate by a factor of
\rho
we find that

{\left\langle{\rho v,\rho w}\right\rangle}_{H^{1}_{\lambda,a}(\rho\gamma)}=\rho^{3-a}{\left\langle{v,w}\right\rangle}_{H^{1}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)}

and so these metrics are
(3-a)
-homogeneous. Two cases are particularly natural: if
a=3
then the induced map
\rho:\imm^{1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\imm^{1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2})
is an isometry, and if
a=1
the induced Riemannian distance scales by the same factor
\rho
. The former is most interesting from the point of view of shape analysis (see e.g.
[
8
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
2
]
) , while the latter seems very natural from a geometric point of view.

In Lemma
3
we derive an explicit formula (
4
) for the gradients of
\mathcal{L}
with respect to the metrics (
3
). The kernel
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(x)
in (
5
) is the periodic Green’s function solving
{(\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}-1)\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(x)=\delta(x)}
, and the gradient reads

\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)=\frac{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{a-2}}{\lambda^{2}}\Big(\gamma+\gamma\ast_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}\Big),

where the
\ast_{\gamma}
convolution is defined by (
6
). Although it is derived under the assumption that
\gamma
is an immersion,
\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)
is well-defined for any
\gamma\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\mathcal{L}(\gamma)\neq 0
. We therefore define:

Definition
.

Let
I
be an interval containing
0
.
We say
X:\mathbb{S}\times I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is an
H^{1}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)
-gradient flow of the length functional if it satisfies

(GFa)

\partial_{t}X(u,t)=F_{\lambda,a}(X):=\begin{cases}-\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(X(u,t)),&\mathcal{L}(X)\neq 0\\
0&\mathcal{L}(X)=0\end{cases}

An illuminating exact solution is given by round circles: under the flow, a circle of radius
r(t)
remains round and
r
solves an autonomous ODE whose decay rate depends on
a
and
\lambda
(Section
3
). In particular, circles shrink self-similarly to a point, with extinction in finite time for
a<2
and in infinite time for
a\geq 2
.

From (
5
) and (
6
) we observe that under dilation by a factor of
\rho
the gradient satisfies
\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\rho\gamma)=\rho^{a-1}\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)
. That is, the gradient and therefore also the gradient flow are
(a-1)
-homogeneous. It follows that when
a=2
the flow is
equivariant
under Euclidean scaling: if
X
solves (
GF2
) then so does
\rho X
for any
\rho>0
.

We treat (
GFa
) as an ODE on the optimal low-regularity space
W^{1,1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2})
: this space is
the largest in which
\mathcal{L}
is well-defined and

satisfies the continuity properties needed for the Picard-Lindelöf theorem. It turns out that the case
a=2
is easiest to analyse, and, as we show in Proposition
4
, solutions of (
GFa
) are equivalent to solutions of (
GF2
) via a time reparametrisation. This observation allows us to focus for the most part on (
GF2
), and then extend existence and qualitative results to (
GFa
).

Our main results are as follows.

.

Let

(Local well-posedness)

and
X\in C^{1}([-T_{0},T_{0}];W^{1,1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2}))

(ii)

(Global existence via
a=2
)
After the time reparametrisation provided by Proposition

, the solution of (

) corresponds to a solution of (

) that exists for all

.

(iii)

(Exponential decay of length)

.

(v)

Numerics.
The explicit periodic Green’s function makes the right-hand side a convolution-type nonlocal operator with a rapidly decaying kernel for small
\lambda

u
uniformly, evaluate arc length by discrete cumulative sums of
|X^{\prime}|
, and approximate the convolution by a circulant matrix multiplication. The formulation (
17
) with

in the integrand suppresses round-off in the small-

In Figure

) with

and

2.
Formulation

In many of the computations which follow we find it convenient to work in the constant speed parametrisation:

\hat{\gamma}(x):=\gamma\circ\xi_{\gamma}^{-1}(x),\quad\text{where}\quad\xi_{\gamma}(u):=\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{u}\left|{\gamma^{\prime}(\tau)}\right|d\tau.

It will also be convenient to make use of the following abbreviations:

\hat{\partial}_{u}:=\frac{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)}{\left|{\gamma^{\prime}(u)}\right|}\frac{d}{du}\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{d}u:=\frac{\left|{\gamma^{\prime}(u)}\right|}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)}du,

which are related to the constant speed parameter
x
by
\frac{d}{dx}=\hat{\partial}_{u}
and
dx=\hat{d}u
.

Lemma 3
.

The gradient of
\mathcal{L}
with respect to
H^{1}_{\lambda,a}
is given by

(4)

\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)=\frac{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{a-2}}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\gamma+\gamma\ast_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}\right),

where
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(5)

\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(x):=-\frac{\cosh\left(\frac{\left|{x}\right|-1/2}{\lambda}\right)}{2\lambda\sinh\left(\frac{1}{2\lambda}\right)},

and the
\gamma
-convolution by

(6)

(f\ast_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda})(u):=\int_{0}^{1}f(\upsilon)\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(\xi_{\gamma}(\upsilon)-\xi_{\gamma}(u))\hat{d}\upsilon.

Proof.

To find the gradient of length with respect to the metrics (
3
) we need to solve for
\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)
in

(7)

D\mathcal{L}(\gamma)v={\left\langle{\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma),v}\right\rangle}_{H^{1}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)}.

Arguing as in
[
9
]
Section 3, the reparametrisation invariance of
\mathcal{L}
and the
H^{1}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)
metrics ensure that for any fixed reparametrisation
\phi:\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{S}
we have
\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma\circ\phi)=\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)\circ\phi
. In particular

(8)

\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\hat{\gamma})(x)=\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)\circ\xi_{\gamma}^{-1}(x)

where as above
\hat{\gamma}:=\gamma\circ\xi_{\gamma}^{-1}
. We therefore begin by calculating
\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\hat{\gamma})
.

In the constant speed paremetrisation (
2
) becomes:

D\mathcal{L}(\gamma)v=\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{1}{\left\langle{\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}(x),\hat{v}^{\prime}(x)}\right\rangle}dx,

and

{\left\langle{v,w}\right\rangle}_{H^{1}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{a-1}}\int_{0}^{1}{\left\langle{\hat{v}(x),\hat{w}(x)}\right\rangle}+\lambda^{2}{\left\langle{\hat{v}^{\prime}(x),\hat{w}^{\prime}(x)}\right\rangle}dx

Then after integrating by parts, (
7
) says that as distributions

\lambda^{2}(\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\hat{\gamma}))^{\prime\prime}(x)-\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\hat{\gamma})(x)=\mathcal{L}^{a-2}\hat{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(x).

Using the Green’s function method

(9)

\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\hat{\gamma})(x)=\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{a-2}\,\int_{0}^{1}\hat{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(\tau-x)\,d\tau

where
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is the solution to

(10)

\left(\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}-1\right)\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(x)=\delta(x).

with periodic boundary conditions and the appropriate jump discontinuity in the first derivative.
The solution is

\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(x)=-\frac{\cosh\left(\frac{\left|{x}\right|-1/2}{\lambda}\right)}{2\lambda\sinh\left(\frac{1}{2\lambda}\right)}.

We will use the same notation
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
for the periodic extension.

Integrating by parts (again in the distributional sense) in (
9
) and using (
10
) gives

\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\hat{\gamma})(x)=\frac{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{a-2}}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\hat{\gamma}(x)+(\hat{\gamma}\ast\mathcal{G}^{\lambda})(x)\right)

and then converting back to arbitrary parametrisation using (
8
):

(11)

\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)(u)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{a-2}\left(\gamma(u)+\int_{0}^{1}\gamma(\upsilon)\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}\big(\xi_{\gamma}(\upsilon)-\xi_{\gamma}(u)\big)\hat{d}\upsilon\right).

Using (
6
), we can write (
11
) more compactly as (
4
).
∎

We conclude this section with some additional useful formulas. Converting back to arbitrary parametrisation in (
9
) yields an equivalent expression for the gradient:

(12)

\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,a}(\gamma)=\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{a-2}(\hat{\partial}^{2}\gamma\ast_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}).

Integrating (
10
) against a constant function and recalling that the second derivative of
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
is a distributional derivative, we find that

(13)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(x)dx=-1=-\int_{0}^{1}\left|{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(x)}\right|dx

where the second equality holds because
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(x)<0
for all
x\in[0,1]
.

3.
Evolution of a circle

With the ansatz
X(t,u)=r(t)(\cos 2\pi u,\sin 2\pi u)
, direct calculation gives
\hat{\partial}^{2}X=-(2\pi)^{2}X
and
X_{t}=\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}X
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{L}(X)^{a-2}\left(X+X\ast_{X}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}(2\pi r)^{a-2}\left(X-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\hat{\partial}^{2}X\ast_{X}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}(2\pi r)^{a-2}\left(X+\frac{(2\pi r)^{2-a}}{(2\pi)^{2}}X_{t}\right)

where we have used (
12
). Rearranging:

X_{t}=-\frac{(2\pi)^{a}}{1+(2\pi\lambda)^{2}}r^{a-2}X

from which it follows that

r^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{(2\pi)^{a}}{1+(2\pi\lambda)^{2}}r^{a-1}.

Setting
b_{\lambda,a}:=\frac{(2\pi)^{a}}{1+(2\pi\lambda)^{2}}
and
r_{0}=r(0)
, this has solution:

r(t)=\begin{cases}\left(r_{0}^{2-a}-(2-a)b_{\lambda,a}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2-a}}&\quad a\neq 2\\
r_{0}e^{-b_{\lambda,a}t}&\quad a=2\\
\end{cases}

which decays to zero in finite time if
a<2
and infinite time if
a\geq 2
.

4.
Existence of solutions

Before proving existence of solutions we observe that solutions of (
GFa
) are related by time-reparametrisation to solutions of (
GFa
) with
a=2
, i.e. solutions of:

(GF2)

\partial_{t}X(u,t)=F_{\lambda,2}(X)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(X+X\ast_{X}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}\right)&\mathcal{L}(X)\neq 0\\
0&\mathcal{L}(X)=0\end{cases}

Proposition 4
.

Let
I
be an open interval containing
0
and
X:\mathbb{S}\times I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
a solution to (
GFa
) with
X(\cdot,0)=X_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}(X(\cdot,t))>0
. Then there is a local time-reparametrisation of
X
which satisfies (
GF2
) with initial value
X_{0}
. Conversely, if
Y:\mathbb{S}\times I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a solution to (
GF2
) with
Y(\cdot,0)=Y_{0}
, then there is a time-reparametrisation of
Y
which satisfies (
GFa
) with initial value
Y_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
X(u,t)
be a solution to (
GFa
) with
\ell(t):=\mathcal{L}(X(u,t))>0
, then
\ell^{\prime}(t)=-{\left\|{\partial_{t}X(u,t)}\right\|}^{2}_{H^{1}_{\lambda,a}}
is continuous in
t
and
\ell
is Lipschitz. Let
\theta:I\to\mathbb{R}
be the solution to

\theta^{\prime}(\tau)=\ell(\theta(\tau))^{2-a},\quad\theta(0)=0,

then
\theta(\tau)
is strictly increasing and the reparametrisation
Y(u,\tau):=X(u,\theta(\tau))
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}Y(u,\tau)

\displaystyle=\partial_{2}X(u,\theta(\tau))\ell(\theta(\tau))^{2-a}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(X(u,\theta(\tau))+(X\ast_{X}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda})(u,\theta(\tau))\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(Y(u,\tau)+(Y\ast_{Y}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda})(u,\tau)\right)

which is (
GF2
). Conversely, if
Y(u,t)
is a solution of (
GF2
), we write
\ell(t):=\mathcal{L}(Y(u,t))
and let
\phi(\tau)
be the solution to

(14)

\phi^{\prime}(\tau)=\ell(\phi(\tau))^{a-2},\quad\phi(0)=0.

Then defining
X(u,\tau):=Y(u,\phi(\tau))
we find that
X
satisfies:

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}X(u,\tau)

\displaystyle=\partial_{2}Y(u,\phi(\tau))\ell(\phi(\tau))^{a-2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathcal{L}(X(u,\tau))^{a-2}}{\lambda^{2}}\left(X(u,\tau)+(X\ast_{X}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda})(u,\tau)\right)

which is (
GFa
).
∎

It follows that for existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
GFa
) it is sufficient to consider (
GF2
). From now on we abbreviate
\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,2}
to
\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
and
F_{\lambda,2}
to
F_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 5
.

For any
\gamma\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2})
:

(15)

\displaystyle{\left\|{F_{\lambda}(\gamma)}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{L}(\gamma),\quad\text{and}

(16)

\displaystyle{\left\|{F_{\lambda}(\gamma)^{\prime}}\right\|}_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{L}(\gamma).

Proof.

Assuming
\mathcal{L}(\gamma)
is non zero (otherwise the estimates are trivial), and recalling (
13
), we can rewrite
F_{\lambda}
as

(17)

F_{\lambda}(\gamma)(u)=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}(\gamma(v)-\gamma(u))\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}\big(\xi_{\gamma}(\upsilon)-\xi_{\gamma}(u)\big)\hat{d}\upsilon

and then

\left|{F_{\lambda}(\gamma)(u)}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\frac{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left|{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}\big(\xi_{\gamma}(\upsilon)-\xi_{\gamma}(u)\big)}\right|\hat{d}\upsilon

which, using (
13
) again, gives (
15
).
For the second estimate, again we define
\hat{\gamma}(x):=\gamma\circ\xi_{\gamma}^{-1}(x)
and also

\hat{F}(x):=F_{\lambda}(\gamma)(\xi_{\gamma}^{-1}(x))=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\hat{\gamma}(x)+\int_{0}^{1}\hat{\gamma}(\tau)\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(\tau-x)\,d\tau\ \right)

From this we compute

\displaystyle{\hat{F}}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}(x)-\int_{0}^{1}\hat{\gamma}(\tau)\partial_{\tau}\big(\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(\tau-x)\big)\,d\tau\right)

(18)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}(x)+\int_{0}^{1}{\hat{\gamma}}^{\prime}(\tau)\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(\tau-x)\,d\tau\right),

and, noting that
\left|{\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}(x)}\right|=\mathcal{L}(\gamma)
, estimate
|\hat{F}^{\prime}(x)|\leq\frac{2\mathcal{L}(\gamma)}{\lambda^{2}}
. Then since
F_{\lambda}(\gamma)^{\prime}(u)=\frac{\left|{\gamma^{\prime}(u)}\right|}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)}\hat{F}^{\prime}(\xi_{\gamma}(u))
we have

(19)

\left|{F_{\lambda}(\gamma)^{\prime}(u)}\right|\leq\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}}\left|{\gamma^{\prime}(u)}\right|,

hence (
16
).

∎

Corollary 6
.

F_{\lambda}
is continuous on
W^{1,1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{n}\to\gamma
in
W^{1,1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\mathcal{L}(\gamma)\neq 0
; then

Lemma

\displaystyle{\left\|{F_{\lambda}(\gamma)-F_{\lambda}(\beta)}\right\|}_{L^{1}}

5.
Convergence

We collect the two basic estimates that provide information about long-time behaviour: exponential decay of the length and a uniform
L^{\infty}
–bound that is nonincreasing along (
GF2
).

Lemma 9
.

Let
I
be an open interval containing
0
and
X\in C^{1}(I,W^{1,1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2}))
a solution to (
GF2
) with
X(u,0)=X_{0}.
Abbreviate
\mathcal{L}(t)=\mathcal{L}(X(u,t))
and
\mathcal{L}_{0}=\mathcal{L}(0)
, then

(25)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(t)\leq\mathcal{L}_{0}e^{-4t/(1+8\lambda^{2})}\,.

Proof.

Write
\hat{X}(x,t):=X(\xi_{X(t)}^{-1}(x),t)
.
From (
2
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(t)=D\mathcal{L}(X)\cdot\partial_{t}X

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(X)}\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\hat{\partial}_{u}X,\partial_{u}\partial_{t}X\right\rangle du

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}\mathcal{L}(X)}\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\partial_{x}\hat{X},\partial_{x}\left(\hat{X}+\hat{X}*\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}\right)\right\rangle dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathcal{L}}{\lambda^{2}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}\mathcal{L}(X)}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\partial_{x}\hat{X},\partial_{x}(\hat{X}\ast\mathcal{G}^{\lambda})\rangle dx.

We define
\bar{X}:=\int_{0}^{1}Xdu
, then using the fact that
{\partial}_{x}(\hat{X}-\bar{X})=\partial_{x}\hat{X}
and

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}(\hat{X}\ast\mathcal{G}^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\hat{X}\ast(\delta+\mathcal{G}^{\lambda})=-\partial_{t}\hat{X}

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathcal{L}}{\lambda^{2}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}\mathcal{L}(X)}\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle X-\bar{X},\partial_{t}X\right\rangle\hat{d}u.

Then using
{\left\|{X-\bar{X}}\right\|}{\left\|{\partial_{t}X}\right\|}\leq\varepsilon{\left\|{X-\bar{X}}\right\|}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}{\left\|{\partial_{t}X}\right\|}^{2}
, and also
{\left\|{X-\bar{X}}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}\leq\mathcal{L}/2

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\mathcal{L}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon\mathcal{L}}{4\lambda^{2}}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon\lambda^{2}\mathcal{L}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\left\|{\partial_{t}X}\right\|}^{2}\left|{\partial_{u}X}\right|du.

Recalling (
3
), since
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(t)=-{\left\|{\partial_{t}X}\right\|}_{H^{1}_{\lambda}(X)}^{2}
, it now follows that

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(t)\leq-\frac{\mathcal{L}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon\mathcal{L}}{4\lambda^{2}}-\frac{1}{4\varepsilon\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(t),

hence

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(t)\leq-\frac{1-\varepsilon/4}{1/(4\varepsilon)+\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{L}

and setting
\varepsilon=2
and integrating gives the stated result.
∎

Lemma 10
.

If
X:\mathbb{S}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a solution to (
GF2
), then
{\left\|{X(\cdot,t)}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}
is non-increasing.

Proof.

With
V(t):={\left\|{X(\cdot,t)}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}
:

\displaystyle|V(t^{\prime})-V(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}}\left|X(u,t^{\prime})-X(u,t)\right|=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}}\left|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\partial_{t}X(u,\tau)d\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}}|\partial_{t}X(u,\tau)|d\tau

Then by (
15
) and (
25
),
V
is Lipschitz, and by Rademacher’s theorem
V
is differentiable almost everywhere.
By Danskin’s theorem
[
4
]
, since it exists a.e.
V^{\prime}(t)
satisfies

V^{\prime}(t)=\max_{u^{*}\in U^{*}}\partial_{t}|X(u^{*},t)|,\quad\text{where}\quad U^{*}(t):=\{u\in\mathbb{S}:|X(u,t)|=V(t)\}.

For any
u\in\mathbb{S}
, using (
13

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|X(u,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}}\langle X(u,t),X(u,t)+(X\ast_{X}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda})(u,t)\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}}|X(u,t)|^{2}-\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}}\langle X(u,t),\int X(\upsilon,t)\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(\xi_{X}(u)-\xi_{X}(\upsilon))\hat{d}\upsilon

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}}|X(u,t)|^{2}+\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}}|X(u,t)|{\left\|{X(t)}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}

and then for all

∎

First we prove that if the initial curve is continuously differentiable and evolves by (

For each
X_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{R}^{2})

GF2

15

.

7.

In this section we show that the flow preserves convexity of planar curves,

Lemma 15
.

For each

Proof.

Differentiating (
21

\displaystyle-\lambda^{2}F_{\lambda}(\gamma)^{\prime\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\gamma^{\prime\prime}(u)+\frac{{\left\langle{\gamma^{\prime\prime}(u),\gamma^{\prime}(u)}\right\rangle}}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)\left|{\gamma^{\prime}(u)}\right|}\int_{0}^{1}\hat{\partial}\gamma(\upsilon)\big(\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}(\xi_{\gamma}(\upsilon)-\xi_{\gamma}(u))\big)\hat{d}\upsilon\\

{\left\|{F_{\lambda}(\gamma)^{\prime\prime}(u)}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{3}{\lambda^{2}}{\left\|{\gamma^{\prime\prime}(u)}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}.

Where we have used (

Proposition 16
.

is the boundary of a strictly convex domain in

Proof.

First we compute the time evolution of curvature. Using the notation
\partial_{s}=\frac{1}{\left|{X_{u}}\right|}\partial_{u}

and
kN=\partial_{s}T

\partial_{t}\partial_{s}=\partial_{s}\partial_{t}-\langle\partial_{s}\partial_{t}X,T\rangle\partial_{s}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(kN)

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}\partial_{t}T-{\left\langle{\partial_{s}\partial_{t}X,T}\right\rangle}kN

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}\big(\partial_{s}\partial_{t}X-{\left\langle{\partial_{s}\partial_{t}X,T}\right\rangle}T\big)-{\left\langle{\partial_{s}\partial_{t}X,T}\right\rangle}kN

and then

\partial_{t}(k^{2})=2\langle\partial_{t}(kN),kN\rangle=2\langle\partial_{s}^{2}\partial_{t}X,kN\rangle-4k^{2}\langle\partial_{s}\partial_{t}X,T\rangle.

From (

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\partial_{t}X

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(T+T\ast_{X}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}\partial_{t}X

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(kN+\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(X)^{2}}X\ast_{X}(\hat{\partial}^{2}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda})\right)=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(kN+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}\mathcal{L}(X)^{2}}\left(X+X\ast_{X}\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}\right)\right),

is strictly convex, by continuity there exists

remains strictly convex while
