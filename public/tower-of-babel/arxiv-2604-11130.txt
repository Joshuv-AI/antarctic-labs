Rigidity of codimension-1 isometric immersions in complete manifolds
Mert Baştuğ

Abstract
We establish an asymptotic rigidity result for isometric immersions of codimension-1. Specifically, we consider a sequence of immersions from a compact d d -dimensional manifold into a complete ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -dimensional manifold whose elastic energies vanish asymptotically, where the elastic energy quantifies both stretching and bending. We show that such a sequence admits a subsequence converging to an isometric immersion. This extends a result of Alpern, Kupferman, and Maor [ 2 ] to the case of complete target manifolds, where the lack of compactness introduces additional analytical difficulties. The proof is based on an approach using local quantitative rigidity estimates, obtained via a reduction to the Euclidean setting. This method avoids the use of Young measures and provides a flexible framework that may be of independent interest.



1
Introduction

According to Liouville’s theorem, if a map
u\in C^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, defined on the open and connected set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, satisfies
Du(x)\in\SO(d)
for all
x\in U
, then
u
is, in fact, a rigid motion:
u(x)=Rx+b
for some
R\in\SO(d)
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This classical result has been the starting point for various extensions and refinements in the study of rigidity. Reshetnyak
[
19
,
20
]
generalized it to an asymptotic rigidity result for maps of lower regularity: If
u_{k}\in W^{1,p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\dist(Du,\SO(d))\to 0
in
L^{p}
for
p\in[1,\infty)
, then, up to a subsequence, there exists
R\in\SO(d)
such that
Du_{k}\to R
in
L^{p}
. More recently, Friesecke, James and Müller obtained their celebrated quantitative rigidity estimate
[
10
]
(see also
[
5
, Section 2.4]
), which states that for
p\in(1,\infty)
and for every
u\in W^{1,p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined on a Lipschitz domain
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists
R\in\SO(d)
such that

\|Du-R\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\dist(Du,\SO(d))\|_{L^{p}},

(1.1)

where
C
depends only on
U
and
p
. The quantitative rigidity estimate plays a central role in nonlinear elasticity, in particular in the rigorous derivation of lower-dimensional models such as plate and shell theories via
\Gamma
-convergence
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
18
]
.

Due to recent interest in non-Euclidean elasticity, rigidity results similar to those stated above have been generalized to the Riemannian setting. Let
(M,g)
and
(N,h)
be
d
-dimensional compact, oriented Riemannian manifolds. Kupferman, Maor and Schachar
[
13
]
showed that if
u_{k}\in W^{1,p}(M;N)
and

\int_{M}\dist_{g,h}^{p}(d(u_{k})_{x},\SO((T_{x}M,g_{x}),(T_{u_{k}(x)}N,h_{u_{k}(x)}))\,d\vol_{g}(x)\to 0

(see Section
2.1
for an explanation of the notation), then, up to a subsequence,
(u_{k})
converges in
W^{1,p}
to a smooth isometric immersion. Furthermore, Conti, Dolzmann and Müller
[
4
]
extended the quantitative rigidity estimate (
1.1
) to maps in
W^{1,p}(M;M)
. Roughly speaking, they prove that for every
u\in W^{1,p}(M;M)
, there exists an orientation preserving isometry
\phi
such that the
W^{1,p}
-distance between
u
and
\phi
is bounded by the “elastic energy”

\int_{M}\dist_{g}^{p}(du_{x},\SO(T_{x}M,g_{x}))\,d\vol_{g}(x).

See also
[
2
, Figure 1]
for a summary of the existing results.

Such rigidity statements do not hold for codimension-1 maps, that is when the codomain of the map has 1 dimension more than its domain. For example, for any smooth unit-speed curve
u:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the derivative
du_{x}:T_{x}\mathbb{R}\rightarrow T_{u(x)}\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a linear isometry for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, yet
u^{\prime}
is not constant. The reason for the gain in flexibility in higher codimension is that the elastic energy we have considered so far does not restrict bending, which is the source of oscillations. To remedy this issue, we augment the elastic energy by adding a term to account for the bending. Suppose
M
and
N
are
d
and
(d+1)
-dimensional, respectively. Given an immersion
u:M\rightarrow N
and
x\in M
, let
\nu_{u}(x)\in T_{u(x)}N
be the unit normal to
du_{x}(T_{x}M)
that is consistent with the orientation induced by
du_{x}
. The variation of
\nu_{u}
in the ambient space
N
is measured by a map
S_{u}:TM\rightarrow TM
called the shape operator induced by
u
(we give a more precise definition in Section
2.3.2
). Fix
p\in[1,\infty)
. To each
u
we assign the sum of a stretching and a bending energy:

E(u):=\int_{M}\dist_{g,h}^{p}(du_{x},\Ort((T_{x}M,g_{x}),(T_{u(x)}N,h_{u(x)})))\,d\vol_{g}(x)+\int_{M}|du_{x}\circ(S_{u}(x)-S(x))|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}(x),

where
S:TM\rightarrow TM
is a reference shape operator on
M
. The energy is well-defined on the space of Sobolev immersions given by

\Imm_{p}(M;N):=\{u\in W^{1,p}(M;N):\rank du_{x}=d\text{ for a.e. }x\in M,\,\nu_{u}\in W^{1,p}(M;TN)\}.

In
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
, Alpern, Kupferman and Maor prove that if
M
and
N
are compact and
E(u_{k})\to 0
for
u_{k}\in\Imm_{p}(M;N)
, then there exists a smooth isometric immersion
u\in\Imm_{p}(M;N)
such that, up to a subsequence,
(u_{k})
and
(\nu_{u_{k}})
converge in
W^{1,p}(M;N)
to
u
and
\nu_{u}
, respectively, and
S_{u}=S
. Furthermore, the result in
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
extends a similar result proved by the same authors in
[
1
]
, where the additional assumption of constant sectional curvature in
N
was imposed.

The purpose of this paper is to extend the results of Alpern, Kupferman and Maor to the case when
N
is a complete, not necessarily compact manifold. Our main result can be summarized as follows:

Theorem
.

Assume
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
(M,g)
and
(N,h)
be oriented, connected, complete Riemannian manifolds of dimensions
d
and
d+1
, respectively. Let
M
be compact. Assume
(u_{k})\subset\Imm_{p}(M;N)
satisfies
E(u_{k})\to 0
and

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{M}d_{h}^{p}(u_{k},q_{0})\,d\vol_{g}<\infty

for some
q_{0}\in N
. Then, up to a subsequence,
u_{k}
converges in
W^{1,p}
to an isometric immersion
u\in\Imm_{p}(M;N)
. If
|S|_{g}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(M)
, where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
, then
S=S_{u}
.

See Theorem
4.1
for a more detailed statement. In comparison to the Young measure approach employed in
[
2
]
, our proof is based on local quantitative rigidity estimates for Sobolev immersions, which we derive by a reduction to the Euclidean rigidity estimate (
1.1
). The rigidity estimates are of independent interest.

The main idea behind our rigidity estimate can be explained easily for a map
u\in\Imm_{p}(M;\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. Consider a sufficiently small chart
(W,\psi)
on
M
on which the metric
g
is close to being constant. If the unit normal
\nu_{u}
is approximately constant in
W
, then
u(W)
is close to a
d
-dimensional hyperplane. If we denote by
P
the projection onto this hyperplane, then the equidimensional map
v:=P\circ u\circ\psi^{-1}
is a close approximation to
u
. Since the metric is almost constant, we can apply the FJM estimate (
1.1
) to
v
to measure the deviation of
du
from a rotation in terms of its stretching energy in
W
. Our assumption that the normal vector
\nu_{u}
does not vary too much in small regions is not true in general. However, thanks to the Poincaré inequality and the control on the variation of
\nu_{u}
provided by the bending energy, these regions cannot be too big.

In order to extend these considerations to Sobolev immersions in
\Imm_{p}(M;N)
, we could embed
N
isometrically into a large Euclidean space. However, this approach has the drawback that the results depend on the embedding of
N
. This turns out not to be a problem when
N
is a compact manifold. Indeed, in a companion paper
[
3
]
, we follow this approach to deliver a simpler proof for compact target manifolds. Instead, in the current work, we localize in the codomain as well. However, a major difficulty arises due to the fact that an arbitrary Sobolev map in
W^{1,p}(M;N)
is, in general, discontinuous. Hence, the image of a small region in
M
might never be contained in a single chart on
N
. To deal with this problem we follow
[
6
]
and use extended charts on
N
, that is, globally defined maps that take
N
into
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
that are diffeomorphisms only in a certain region of
N
(see also
[
6
, Lemma 1.6]
). For the final result, we refer the reader to Theorem
3.7
.

We believe that our methods are quite flexible and elementary apart from the reference to the Euclidean quantitative rigidity estimate. The approach can also be extended easily to higher codimensions. However, we note that, in contrast to
[
1
,
2
]
, we do not prove the smoothness of the limiting isometric immersion.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary definitions on Riemannian structures on vector bundles and weakly differentiable maps between manifolds. Since we do not embed
N
isometrically into Euclidean space, we use the intrinsic definition of manifold-valued Sobolev maps throughout the work, which is based on the notion of colocal weak differentiability (see Definition
2.5
) introduced by Convent and Van Schaftingen
[
6
]
. In Section
3
, we define
\varepsilon
-isometric charts and extensions of charts via cut-offs (see Definitions
3.1
and
3.3
). In the end, we prove the local quantitative rigidity theorem (Theorem
3.7
). In Section
4
, we state our main result (Theorem
4.1
) and prove some preliminary results. Finally, Section
5
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we give a brief but mostly self-contained introduction to Riemannian structures on vector bundles and weakly differentiable maps between manifolds, which already appear in the approach by Alpern, Kupferman and Maor
[
1
,
2
]
. After a subsection on notation, we recall the definition of a metric connection on a vector bundle
(E,\pi,N)
and define the connector operator, which allows us to construct an isomorphism between
TE
and
TN\oplus E
. With the help of this isomorphism, we define the Sasaki metric on the tangent bundle
TE
. For
E=T^{*}M\otimes TN
, the Sasaki metric allows us to define a distance function between differentials of maps from
M
to
N
. At the end, we also express the connector operator in coordinates, since this will be useful in later estimates. In the last subsection, we give a brief account of colocally weakly differentiable functions introduced by Convent and Van Schaftingen
[
6
]
. Later, we use these functions to generalize Sobolev spaces to Riemannian manifolds. Finally, we define Sobolev immersions based on the paper
[
1
]
.

2.1
Notation

Let
V
be a vector space endowed with a constant metric
g_{0}
. For
v,v^{\prime}\in V
, we write
(v,v^{\prime})_{g_{0}}
in place of
g_{0}(v,v^{\prime})
and set
|v|_{g_{0}}:=\sqrt{(v,v)_{g_{0}}}
. Let
W
be another vector space endowed with a constant metric
h_{0}
. We denote by
\mathcal{L}(V,W)
the space of linear maps from
V
to
W
. The subset of isometries in
\mathcal{L}(V,W)
is denoted by
\Ort((V,g_{0}),(W,h_{0}))
, while the orientation preserving ones are written as
\SO((V,g_{0}),(W,h_{0}))
.

Given an orthonormal basis
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})
of
V
, We define the Frobenius norm of
T\in\mathcal{L}(V,W)
by

|T|_{g_{0},h_{0}}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|Tv_{i}|_{h_{0}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This definition does not depend on the choice of orthonormal basis. In the case
V=W
and
g_{0}=h_{0}
, we abbreviate the notation to
|T|_{g_{0}}
. For subsets
\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{L}(V,W)
, their distance is defined by

\dist_{g_{0},h_{0}}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S}):=\inf\{|T-S|_{g_{0},h_{0}}:T\in\mathcal{T},S\in\mathcal{S}\}.

We write
\e_{d}
for the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Whenever all spaces involved are Euclidean, we omit the metric from the notation and simply write
(v,v^{\prime})
,
|T|
,
\dist(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S})
etc. For two constant metrics
g_{0}
,
g_{0}^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define

|g_{0}-g_{0}^{\prime}|:=\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|(e_{i},e_{j})_{g_{0}}-(e_{i},e_{j})_{g^{\prime}}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
(e_{1},\dots,e_{d})
is the standard basis. If
h_{0}
is a constant metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we use the shorthand
\Ort(g_{0},h_{0})
and
\SO(g_{0},h_{0})
for
\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{0}),(\mathbb{R}^{n},h_{0}))
and
\SO((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{0}),(\mathbb{R}^{n},h_{0}))
, respectively. If
g_{0}=h_{0}
, we further abbreviate to
\Ort(g_{0})
and
\SO(g_{0})
.

Let
(M,g)
and
(N,h)
be Riemannian manifolds. For a smooth map
f:M\rightarrow N
, we denote its differential by
df:TM\rightarrow TN
. In the special case
N=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we identify
T\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. Thus, for every
x\in M
, there exists a map
Df(x):T_{x}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

df_{x}(v)=(f(x),Df(x)v).

If
f
is only weakly differentiable, the symbols
df
and
Df
are used the same sense as in the smooth case. Finally, we denote by
\vol_{g}
the volume form on
M
.

2.2
Riemannian structures on vector bundles

We recall the definition of a connection on a vector bundle. For details, we refer the reader to
[
14
, Chapter 12]
and
[
15
, Chapters 4,5]
. Let
(N,h)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and let
(E,\pi,N)
be a smooth vector bundle of rank
k
equipped with a bundle metric, that is, for every
q\in N
there exists an inner product
\kappa_{q}
on
E_{q}=\pi^{-1}(q)
that depends smoothly on
q
. We denote the set of all sections of
E
by
\Gamma(E)
. A map
\nabla:\Gamma(TN)\times\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
, written
(X,Y)\mapsto\nabla_{X}Y
, is called a connection if

1.

For all
X_{1},X_{2}\in\Gamma(TN)
,
Y\in\Gamma(E)
, and
f_{1},f_{2}\in C^{\infty}(N)
,

\nabla_{f_{1}X_{1}+f_{2}X_{2}}Y=f_{1}\nabla_{X_{1}}Y+f_{2}\nabla_{X_{2}}Y.

2.

For all
X\in\Gamma(TN)
,
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\Gamma(E)
and
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\nabla_{X}(a_{1}Y_{1}+a_{2}Y_{2})=a_{1}\nabla_{X}Y_{1}+a_{2}\nabla_{X}Y_{2}.

3.

For all
X\in\Gamma(TN)
,
Y\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(N)
,

\nabla_{X}(fY)=f\nabla_{X}Y+X(f)Y.

Moreover,
\nabla
is called metric if for all
X\in\Gamma(TN)
,
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\Gamma(E)
,

X((Y_{1},Y_{2})_{\kappa})=(\nabla_{X}Y_{1},Y_{2})_{\kappa}+(Y_{1},\nabla_{X}Y_{2})_{\kappa}.

Given a metric connection
\nabla
on
E
, we sketch the construction of a natural Riemannian metric on
E
called the Sasaki metric. The interested reader can refer to
[
7
, Chapter 3, Ex. 2]
,
[
12
, Chapter 2.B.6]
or
[
21
]
for more details. We start with a decomposition of the tangent bundle
TE
. Let
e\in E_{q}
and let
\alpha:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\rightarrow E
be a smooth curve with
\alpha(0)=e
. We set
\gamma:=\pi\circ\alpha
, and we view
\alpha
as a vector field along
\gamma
. Intuitively, the local behavior of
\alpha
at
0
is determined by the two vectors
\gamma^{\prime}(0)
and
\frac{D\alpha}{dt}(0)
, where
\frac{D}{dt}
is the covariant derivative determined by
\nabla
. Formally, this is reflected in the fact that if
\beta:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\rightarrow E
is another smooth curve with
\beta(0)=e
and
\eta=\pi\circ\beta
, then

\alpha^{\prime}(0)=\beta^{\prime}(0)\iff\left(\gamma^{\prime}(0),\frac{D\alpha}{dt}(0)\right)=\left(\eta^{\prime}(0),\frac{D\beta}{dt}(0)\right).

(2.1)

This observation yields a natural decomposition of
TE
as we now show.

Let
\xi\in TE
. Then
\xi=\alpha^{\prime}(0)
for a smooth curve
\alpha:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\rightarrow E
. We define the so called connector operator (see also
[
17
, Chapter 4]
)

\displaystyle K_{E}:TE

\displaystyle\rightarrow E,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{D\alpha}{dt}(0).

By (
2.1
), the connector operator is well-defined. We remark that the connector operator is simply another way of expressing the covariant derivative. If
X\in\Gamma(TN)
and
Y\in\Gamma(E)
, then
K_{E}\circ dY\circ X=\nabla_{X}Y
as the reader can easily verify.

If we denote by
TN\oplus E
the Whitney sum of
TN
and
E
, then it follows from (
2.1
) and the relation
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=d\pi(\xi)
that the following map is an isomorphism:

\displaystyle TE

\displaystyle\rightarrow TN\oplus E,

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle\mapsto(d\pi(\xi),K_{E}(\xi)).

Identifying
TE
with
TN\oplus E
according to the isomorphism and declaring
TN
orthogonal to
E
yields the inner product

(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\mapsto(d\pi(\xi_{1}),d\pi(\xi_{2}))_{h}+(K_{E}(\xi_{1}),K_{E}(\xi_{2}))_{\kappa},\quad\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in T_{e}E.

(2.3)

As a result, we obtain a Riemannian metric on
E
that we call the Sasaki metric. We denote it by
\sigma
. The Sasaki metric enables us to define a distance on the vector bundle
E
. The corresponding Riemannian distance function is called the Sasaki distance.

In the following proposition, we give an explicit characterization of the Sasaki distance
[
21
, Section 2]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
N
be connected. Let
q_{1},q_{2}\in N
and let
e_{1}\in E_{q_{1}}
,
e_{2}\in E_{q_{2}}
. Then

d_{\sigma}(e_{1},e_{2})^{2}=\inf_{\gamma}|e_{1}-P^{\gamma}(e_{2})|_{\kappa}^{2}+\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\prime}|_{h}\,dt\right)^{2},

(2.4)

where the infimum is taken over all piecewise regular curves
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow N
with
\gamma(0)=q_{1},\gamma(1)=q_{2}
, and
P^{\gamma}:E_{q_{2}}\rightarrow E_{q_{1}}
is the parallel transport along
\gamma
with respect to
\nabla
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:[0,1]\rightarrow E
be a piecewise regular curve with
\alpha(0)=e_{1}
and
\alpha(1)=e_{2}
. Set
\gamma:=\pi\circ\alpha
. Let
(X_{i}(t))_{i=1}^{k}
be an orthonormal basis of
E_{\gamma(t)}
for
t\in[0,1]
, and assume that
X_{i}
is parallel along
\gamma
, that is,
\frac{DX_{i}}{dt}=0
. Then there exists a function
a:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that

\alpha(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}(t)X_{i}(t).

Since
\frac{DX_{i}}{dt}=0
, we get

\frac{D\alpha}{dt}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a^{\prime}_{i}(t)X_{i}(t).

Therefore,

|\alpha^{\prime}(t)|_{\sigma}^{2}=|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|_{h}^{2}+\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{\prime}(t)X_{i}(t)\right|_{\kappa}^{2}=|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|_{h}^{2}+|a^{\prime}(t)|^{2}.

We set

l_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\prime}|_{h}\,dt,\quad l_{2}=\int_{0}^{1}|a^{\prime}|\,dt.

By the concavity of the square root function, we obtain

\int_{0}^{1}|\alpha^{\prime}|_{\sigma}\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}(|\gamma^{\prime}|_{h}^{2}+|a^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dt\geq\frac{l_{1}}{(l_{1}^{2}+l_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\prime}|_{h}\,dt+\frac{l_{2}}{(l_{1}^{2}+l_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{1}|a^{\prime}|\,dt=(l_{1}^{2}+l_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Notice that
l_{2}\geq|a(0)-a(1)|=|e_{1}-P^{\gamma}(e_{2})|_{\kappa}
. This proves that the left-hand side is greater than or equal to the right-hand side in (
2.4
). To show the reverse inequality let
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow N
be piecewise regular curve with
\gamma(0)=q_{1},\gamma(1)=q_{2}
. Define
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
\alpha:[0,1]\to E
by

e_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}X_{i}(0),\quad e_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}X_{i}(1),\quad\alpha(t):=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(a_{i}+t(b_{i}-a_{i}))X_{i}(t).

Then
\alpha(0)=e_{1}
,
\alpha(1)=e_{2}
and

d_{\sigma}(e_{1},e_{2})^{2}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\alpha^{\prime}|_{\sigma}\,dt\right)^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\alpha^{\prime}|_{\sigma}^{2}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{1}|a-b|^{2}+|\gamma^{\prime}|_{h}^{2}\,dt=|e_{1}-P^{\gamma}e_{2}|_{\kappa}^{2}+\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\prime}|_{h}\,dt.

∎

We will use the following simple facts throughout the article. The zero vector in
E_{q}
is denoted by
0_{q}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
q_{1},q_{2}\in N
and let
e_{0},e_{1}\in E_{q_{1}}
,
e_{2}\in E_{q_{2}}
. Then

\displaystyle d_{\sigma}(e_{0},e_{1})=|e_{0}-e_{1}|_{\kappa},

\displaystyle d_{\sigma}(0_{q_{1}},0_{q_{2}})=d_{h}(q_{1},q_{2}),

\displaystyle d_{\sigma}(e_{1},e_{2})\leq|e_{1}|_{\kappa}+|e_{2}|_{\kappa}+d_{h}(q_{1},q_{2}).

Proof.

The first and second equalities follow immediately from
2.4
. To prove the third one we use the triangle inequality:

d_{\sigma}(e_{1},e_{2})\leq d_{\sigma}(e_{1},0_{q_{1}})+d_{\sigma}(0_{q_{1}},0_{q_{2}})+d_{\sigma}(0_{q_{2}},e_{2})=|e_{1}|_{\kappa}+|e_{2}|_{\kappa}+d_{h}(q_{1},q_{2}).

∎

Let
(M,g)
be a connected
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. In this paper, we employ the Sasaki metric in order to define a distance between differentials of maps from
M
to
N
. To this end, we set
E:=T^{*}M\otimes TN
, and for
L\in T_{x}^{*}M\otimes T_{q}N\subset T^{*}M\otimes TN
, we define
\pi(L):=(x,q)
. Then
(E,\pi,M\times N)
is a smooth vector bundle. Furthermore, we can endow each fiber
T_{p}^{*}M\otimes T_{q}N
with the inner product
g_{x}^{*}\otimes h_{q}
. In order to define a metric connection on
T^{*}M\otimes TN
, we let
\nabla^{g}
and
\nabla^{h}
be the Levi-Civita connections on
M
and
N
, respectively. Then there exists a unique metric connection
\nabla
satisfying

\nabla_{(Y,Z)}(\omega\otimes X)=(\nabla_{Y}^{g}\omega)\otimes X+\omega\otimes(\nabla_{Z}^{h}X)

(2.5)

for all
\omega\in\Gamma(T^{*}M)
,
X\in\Gamma(TN)
,
Y\in\Gamma(TM)
and
Z\in\Gamma(TN)
.

Using Proposition
2.1

Corollary 2.3

Let

(2.6)

Lemma 2.4

Proof.

Let

2.3
Weakly differentiable maps between manifolds

In this section we give an intrinsic definition of weak differentiability for maps between manifolds using the notions developed by Convent and Van Schaftingen
[
6
]
. We start the section by recalling the corresponding definitions for real valued functions on manifolds. Throughout the paper, we do not identify functions that agree almost everywhere.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold. We say that
A\subset M
is negligible, if for every
x\in A
, there exists a chart
(W,\psi)
with
x\in W
such that
|\psi(W\cap A)|=0
. If a property holds outside of a negligible subset of
M
, then we say that it holds almost everywhere in
M
. We call
u:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
weakly differentiable if for every chart
(W,\psi)
the function
u\circ\psi^{-1}
is weakly differentiable. We can define a local weak derivative
du:TW\rightarrow T\mathbb{R}
by setting
du:=d(u\circ\psi^{-1})\circ d\psi
. If
(\tilde{W},\tilde{\psi})
is another chart, then the chain rule for weakly differentiable functions yields

d(u\circ\psi^{-1})_{\psi(x)}\circ d\psi_{x}=d(u\circ\tilde{\psi}^{-1})_{\tilde{\psi}(x)}\circ d\tilde{\psi}_{x}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in W\cap\tilde{W}.

Hence, we can glue the local weak derivatives together to obtain a map
du:TM\rightarrow T\mathbb{R}
.

Let
N
be a smooth manifold and let
u:M\rightarrow N
. If
(U,\varphi)
is a chart on
N
, then the domain of
\varphi\circ u
is not an open set in general. Hence, it does not make sense to define the weak differentiability of
u
in terms of its compositions with charts on
N
. An alternative definition was provided by Convent and Van Schaftingen
[
6
]
. Let
\pi_{M}:TM\rightarrow M
and
\pi_{N}:TN\rightarrow N
be the canonical projections. The idea is to consider compositions of
u
with compactly supported smooth functions.

Definition 2.5

(
[
6
, Definition 1.1.]
)
.

A map
u:M\rightarrow N
is called colocally weakly differentiable (c.w.d.) if
f\circ u
is weakly differentiable for all
f\in C_{c}^{1}(N)
. We call
du:TM\rightarrow TN
a colocal weak derivative of
u
if
\pi_{N}\circ du=u\circ\pi_{M}
and

d(f\circ u)_{x}=df_{u(x)}\circ du_{x}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in M.

All colocally weakly differentiable functions possess a unique colocal weak derivative.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
6
, Proposition 1.5]
)
.

If
u:M\rightarrow N
is c.w.d., then it has a unique colocal weak derivative
du:TM\rightarrow TN
. Moreover, if
f\in C^{1}(N)
and
f\circ u
is weakly differentiable, then

d(f\circ u)_{p}=df_{u(x)}\circ du_{x}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in M.

Let
\tilde{N}
be a smooth manifold. The next two propositions state that compositions and Cartesian products of c.w.d. maps can be computed in the expected manner.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
6
, Proposition 1.8]
)
.

Let
u:M\rightarrow N
be c.w.d. and let
v\in C^{1}(N;\tilde{N})
. If
v\circ u
is c.w.d., then

d(v\circ u)_{x}=dv_{u(x)}\circ du_{x}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in M.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
6
, Proposition 1.10]
)
.

If
u_{1}:M\rightarrow N
and
u_{2}:M\rightarrow\tilde{N}
are c.w.d., then so is
u:=(u_{1},u_{2}):M\rightarrow N\times\tilde{N}
, and

du_{x}=(d(u_{1})_{x},d(u_{2})_{x})\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in M.

2.3.1
Homogeneous Sobolev spaces

Let
(M,g)
and
(N,h)
be connected Riemannian manifolds. The Riemannian metric allows us to generalize
L^{p}
-integrability to maps between manifolds.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. We say that a c.w.d. map
u:M\rightarrow N
belongs to the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{1,p}(M;N)
if
|du|_{g,h}\in L^{p}(M)
.

Let
\sigma
denote the Sasaki metric on
(T^{*}M\otimes TN,\pi,M\times N)
. We define

d_{\sigma,p}(u,v):=\left(\int_{M}d_{\sigma}(du_{x},dv_{x})^{p}\,d\vol_{g}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad u,v\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(M;N).

If
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(M;N)
, then we say that
(u_{k})
converges to
u
in
\dot{W}^{1,p}(M;N)
if
d_{\sigma,p}(u_{k},u)\to 0
.

Proposition 2.10

(
[
6
, Proposition 4.2]
)
.

If
N
is complete, then the metric space
(\dot{W}^{1,p}(M;N),d_{\sigma,p})
is complete.

For homogeneous Sobolev spaces, we can prove a compactness result similar to the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem in the Euclidean setting. To this end, we require two preliminary results that are of independent interest as well. The following lemma and its proof is adapted from
[
6
, Proposition 3.8]
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
(u_{k})\subset\dot{W}^{1,p}(M;N)
be a sequence. Assume that there exists
C>0
such that

\int_{M}|du_{k}|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}\leq C\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.14)

Furthermore, assume that
u_{k}\to u
pointwise a.e. in
M
. Then
u\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(M;N)
and

\int_{M}|du|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{M}|du_{k}|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}.

Proof.

Let
f\in C_{c}^{1}(N)
. We show that
f\circ u
is weakly differentiable on
M
. Let
(W,\psi)
be a chart on
M
and set
\tilde{u}_{k}:=f\circ u_{k}\circ\psi^{-1}
,
\tilde{u}:=f\circ u\circ\psi^{-1}

\tilde{u}_{k}\in W_{\text{loc}}^{1,p}(W)

is bounded and

M

\tilde{u}_{k}\to\tilde{u}
in

f\circ u_{k}\to f\circ u
in
L^{p}(M)

W_{\text{loc}}^{1,p}(W)
is closed under weak convergence, it follows from (
2.14

\tilde{u}\in W_{\text{loc}}^{1,p}(W)

f\circ u
is weakly differentiable.

Let
(u_{k_{l}})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
be a subsequence such that

\lim_{l\to\infty}\int_{M}|du_{k_{l}}|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{M}|du_{k}|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g},

and assume without loss of generality that
|du_{k_{l}}|_{g,h}\rightharpoonup w
in
L^{p}(M)
for some

F\in C_{c}^{1}(N;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M;TM),\quad w\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

We observe that

\int_{M}(D(F\circ u)(v),w)\,d\vol_{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{M}D(F_{i}\circ u)(w_{i}v)\,d\vol_{g}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{M}(F_{i}\circ u)\operatorname{div}(w_{i}v)\,d\vol_{g}.

(2.15)

Clearly, the equalities hold for
u_{k}
as well. Therefore, replacing
u
by
u_{k_{l}}
on the right-hand side of (
2.15
) and taking

\int_{M}(D(F\circ u)(v),w)\,d\vol_{g}=\lim_{l\to\infty}\int_{M}(D(F\circ u_{k_{l}})(v),w)\,d\vol_{g}.

(2.16)

If
L\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M;T^{*}M\otimes\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then it similarly follows from (
2.16
) that

\int_{M}(D(F\circ u),L)_{g,\e_{n}}\,d\vol_{g}=\lim_{l\to\infty}\int_{M}(D(F\circ u_{k_{l}}),L)_{g,\e_{n}}\,d\vol_{g}.

(2.17)

Define the Lipschitz constant
\operatorname{Lip}(F)
by

\operatorname{Lip}(F):=\inf\left\{\frac{|F(x)-F(z)|}{d_{g}(x,z)}:x,z\in M,x\neq z\right\}.

Then
|(D(F\circ u_{k_{l}}),L)_{g,\e_{n}}|\leq\operatorname{Lip}(F)|du_{k_{l}}|_{g,h}|L|_{g,\e_{n}}
, and by the weak convergence of
(|du_{k_{l}}|_{g,h})
, we have

\left|\int_{M}(D(F\circ u),L)_{g,\e_{n}}\,d\vol_{g}\right|\leq\operatorname{Lip}(F)\int_{M}w|L|_{g,\e_{n}}\,d\vol_{g}.

Since
L
is arbitrary, we conclude that
|D(F\circ u)|_{g,\e_{n}}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(F)w
a.e. in
M
. At this point, we refer to Proposition 2.2. in
[
6
]
to deduce that
|du|_{g,h}\leq w
a.e. in
M
. Therefore,

\int_{M}|du|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}\leq\int_{M}w\,d\vol_{g}\leq\liminf_{l\to\infty}\int_{M}|du_{k_{l}}|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{M}|du_{k}|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}

This concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.12

6

Let

,

2.

(\nu_{u}(x),du_{x}(v))_{h}=0
for all
v\in T_{x}M
,

3.

if
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})
is a positively oriented basis of
T_{x}M
, then
(du_{x}(v_{1}),\dots,du_{x}(v_{d}),\nu_{u}(x))
is a positively oriented basis of
T_{u(x)}N
.

We call
\nu_{u}(x)
the oriented unit normal at
x
. If
\rank du_{x}<d
, we set
\nu_{u}(x):=0\in T_{u(x)}N
. We define the space of Sobolev immersions by

\Imm_{p}(M;N):=\{u\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(M;N):\rank du_{x}=d\text{ for a.e. }x\in M,\,\nu_{u}\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(M;TN)\}.

Given
u\in\Imm_{p}(M;N)
, we can consider it as a deformation of
M
. We introduce the following functionals that quantify its stretching and bending energies:

\displaystyle E_{s}(u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{M}\dist_{g,h}^{p}(du_{x},\Ort((T_{x}M,g_{x}),(T_{u(x)}N,h_{u(x)})))\,d\vol_{g}(x),

(2.23)

\displaystyle E_{b}(u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{M}|K_{TN}\circ d(\nu_{u})_{x}|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}(x).

(2.24)

We recall that if
u
is smooth,
K_{TN}\circ d(\nu_{u})
gives the covariant derivative of
\nu_{u}
. Since
du_{x}
is injective for a.e.
x\in M
, there exists a unique linear map
S_{u}(x):T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{x}M
satisfying

du_{x}\circ S_{u}(x)=K_{TN}\circ d(\nu_{u})_{x}

for a.e.
x\in M
. We call
S_{u}:TM\rightarrow TM
the induced shape operator. If
M
has a pre-assigned “shape”, then we can also measure the deviation from the original shape after the deformation. Assume that
M
is equipped with a smooth symmetric 2-tensor field
b
. For each
x\in M
we let
S(x):T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{x}M
be the unique linear map defined by

(S(x)v,w)_{g_{x}}=b(v,w)\quad\text{for all }v,w\in T_{x}M.

(2.25)

We call the resulting map
S:TM\rightarrow TM
the reference shape operator. The modified bending energy is given by

E_{b}^{S}(u):=\int_{M}|du_{x}\circ(S_{u}(x)-S(x))|_{g_{x},h_{u(x)}}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}(x).

(2.26)

Clearly,
E_{b}(u)=E_{b}^{S}(u)
if
S\equiv 0
.

3
The quantitative rigidity estimate on
\varepsilon
-isometric charts

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and let
(N,h)
be an oriented
(d+1)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. In this section, we prove a local quantitative rigidity result for Sobolev immersions defined on a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and taking values in
N
. This is achieved in Theorem
3.7
. Our strategy will be to put coordinates on
N
to work with vector valued functions to which we can apply rigidity results in Euclidean spaces. In order to preserve as much metric information as possible in the transition to coordinates, we need to work with close-to-isometric charts, which we introduce in the next definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(U,\varphi)
be chart on
N
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Denote the pushforward of the metric
h
by
\varphi
by the same letter and let
\Gamma_{i,j}^{k}:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
for
i,j,k=1,\dots,d+1
be the Christoffel symbols for
h
. The chart
(U,\varphi)
is called
\varepsilon
-isometric if

\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\e_{d+1}<h<(1+\varepsilon)\e_{d+1}\text{ in }\varphi(U),\quad\|\Gamma_{i,j}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq\varepsilon\text{ for all }i,j,k\in\{1,\dots,d+1\}.

(3.1)

For all
q\in N
and
\varepsilon>0
, the restriction of the exponential map on
T_{q}N
to a sufficiently small ball centered at
0
gives an
\varepsilon
-isometric chart. In the next lemma, we bound quantities that appear in Euclidean rigidity estimates in terms of the corresponding quantities on the manifold.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(U,\varphi)
be an
\varepsilon
-isometric chart on
N
. Let
q\in U
and set
y:=\varphi(q)
.

1.

Let
g_{0}
be a constant metric on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If
L\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},T_{q}N)
, then

\dist_{g_{0},\e_{d+1}}(D\varphi(q)\circ L,\Ort(g_{0},\e_{d+1})\leq\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}\dist_{g_{0},h_{q}}(L,\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{0}),(T_{q}N,h_{q}))+C\varepsilon.

(3.2)

2.

Let
\Pi\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be a
d
-dimensional subspace. Let
n\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be normal to
\Pi
and let
\nu\in T_{q}N
be normal to
d(\varphi^{-1})_{y}(\Pi)
. Assume that
|n|=1
,
|\nu|_{h}=1
and assume that
n
and
\tilde{n}:=D\varphi(q)(\nu)
induce the same orientation on
\Pi
. Then
|n-\tilde{n}|\leq C\varepsilon
.

Both constants
C
depend only on
d
.

Proof.

We denote by
h_{y}
the pushforward of the metric
h_{q}
by
\varphi
. Define the matrix
H_{y}
by

(H_{y})_{ij}:=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\bigg|_{y},\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\bigg|_{y}\right)_{h_{y}},\quad i,j=1,\dots,d+1.

Let
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d+1})
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
consisting of eigenvectors of
H_{y}
. Set
\mu_{i}:=(v_{i},v_{i})_{h_{y}}
and
\tilde{v}_{i}:=v_{i}/\sqrt{\mu_{i}}
. By (
3.1
), we have
(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}<\mu_{i}<1+\varepsilon
. Define
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
by
S\tilde{v}_{i}:=v_{i}
, so that
S\in\Ort(h_{y},e_{d+1})
. Then

|\Id_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}-S|_{h_{y},\e_{d+1}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}|\tilde{v}_{i}-S\tilde{v_{i}}|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{i}}}-1\right|^{2}\leq 4\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}|1-\mu_{i}|^{2}=4(d+1)\varepsilon^{2}.

(3.3)

Now, we choose
R\in\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{0}),(T_{q}N,h_{q}))
such that

|L-R|_{g_{0},h_{q}}=\dist_{g_{0},h_{q}}(L,\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{0}),(T_{q}N,h_{q})).

Then
S\circ D\varphi(q)\circ R\in\Ort(g_{0},\e_{d+1})
and

\displaystyle\dist

{}_{g_{0},\e_{d+1}}(D\varphi(q)\circ L,\Ort(g_{0},\e_{d+1}))

\displaystyle\leq|D\varphi(q)\circ L-S\circ D\varphi(q)\circ R|_{g_{0},\e_{d+1}}\leq|D\varphi(q)\circ(L-R)|_{g_{0},\e_{d+1}}+|(\Id_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}-S)\circ D\varphi(q)\circ R|_{g_{0},\e_{d+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}|D\varphi(q)\circ(L-R)|_{g_{0},h_{y}}+|\Id_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}-S|_{h_{y},\e_{d+1}}\leq\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}|L-R|_{g_{0},h_{q}}+C\varepsilon.

Hence,

\dist_{g_{0},\e_{d+1}}(D\varphi(q)\circ L,\Ort(g_{0},\e_{d+1}))\leq\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}\dist_{g_{0},h_{q}}(L,\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{0}),(T_{q}N,h_{q}))+C\varepsilon.

We move on to the second claim. If
v,\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and

v=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}a_{i}v_{i},\quad\tilde{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}b_{i}v_{i},

then

|(v,\tilde{v})-(v,\tilde{v})_{h_{y}}|=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}a_{i}b_{i}(1-\mu_{i})\right|\leq\varepsilon|v||\tilde{v}|.

(3.4)

An orthogonal decomposition of
\tilde{n}
yields a unit vector
w\in\Pi
such that
\tilde{n}=(\tilde{n},n)n+(\tilde{n},w)w
. By (
3.4
), we get

|(\tilde{n},\tilde{n})-1|=|(\tilde{n},\tilde{n})-(\tilde{n},\tilde{n})_{h_{y}}|\leq\varepsilon|\tilde{n}|^{2}\leq 4\varepsilon,\quad|(\tilde{n},w)|=|(\tilde{n},w)-(\tilde{n},w)_{h_{y}}|\leq\varepsilon|\tilde{n}||w|\leq 2\varepsilon.

Therefore,
|(\tilde{n},n)|>|\tilde{n}|-|(\tilde{n},w)|\geq 1-6\varepsilon
. However,
(\tilde{n},n)>0
, since
\tilde{n}
and
n
induce the same orientation on
\Pi
. Thus, we conclude that
|\tilde{n}-n|\leq C\varepsilon
.
∎

The composition of a Sobolev immersion and a coordinate chart is not weakly differentiable in general, since its domain is not necessarily open. In the next definition, we extend the domain of a chart to all of
N
by a cut-off procedure. This allows us to obtain compactly supported coordinate maps that are diffeomorphic on an open subset of
N
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(U,\varphi)
be a chart on
N
centered at
q
, that is,
\varphi(q)=0
. Let
\theta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1};\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
and assume that
\theta=\Id_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}
in
B(0,1)
and
\theta\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\setminus B(0,2)
. If
B(0,2r)\subset\varphi(U)
, then we set
\varphi^{(r)}(x):=r\theta(\varphi(x)/r)
for
x\in U
and
\varphi^{(r)}(x):=0
for
x\in N\setminus U
. We say that
\varphi^{(r)}
extends
(U,\varphi)
by
\theta
.

If
\varphi^{(r)}
extends
(U,\varphi)
by
\theta
, then we note that
\supp(\varphi^{(r)})\subset U
and
\varphi^{(r)}=\varphi
in
\varphi^{-1}(B(0,r))
. If
u
is a Sobolev immersion taking values in
N
, then
\varphi^{(r)}\circ u
and
D\varphi^{(r)}\circ\nu_{u}
are almost isometric coordinate representations of
u
and the unit normal vector field
\nu_{u}
, respectively, for
x\in u^{-1}(\varphi^{-1}(B(0,r)))
. Our next goal is to bound the derivative of
D\varphi^{(r)}\circ\nu_{u}
in terms of the covariant derivative
K_{TN}\circ d\nu_{u}
. We begin with a lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(U,\varphi)
be an
\varepsilon
-isometric chart on
N
and let
\xi\in T_{(q,v)}TN
with
q\in U
. Then

|K_{T\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(d^{2}\varphi_{(q,v)}(\xi))|\leq C(|K_{TN}(\xi)|_{h}+|v|_{h}|d(\pi_{N})_{(q,v)}(\xi)|_{h}),

where the constant
C
depends only on
d
.

Proof.

Denote the local coordinates on
U
by
(y_{1},\dots,y_{d+1})
. We define coordinates on
TU
by

(y,w)\in U\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}w_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\bigg|_{\varphi^{-1}(y)}\in TU.

Then

\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}Y_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\bigg|_{(q,v)}+\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}W_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{i}}\bigg|_{(q,v)}

for some
Y,W\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. By (
2.9
) and (
2.12
), we have

\displaystyle d\varphi_{q}(K_{TN}(\xi))=\sum_{k=1}^{d+1}\left(W_{k}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d+1}w_{j}Y_{i}\Gamma_{i,j}^{k}(q)\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\bigg|_{y},

\displaystyle K_{T\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(d^{2}\varphi_{(q,v)}(\xi))=\sum_{k=1}^{d+1}W_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\bigg|_{y},

where
(y,w)
are the coordinates of
(q,v)
. We know that

d\varphi_{q}(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}w_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\bigg|_{y},\quad d(\varphi\circ\pi_{N})_{(q,v)}(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}Y_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\bigg|_{y}.

Consequently,

|d\varphi_{q}(K_{TN}(\xi))-K_{T\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(d^{2}\varphi_{(q,v)}(\xi))|\leq|d\varphi_{q}(v)||d(\varphi\circ\pi_{N})_{(q,v)}(\xi)|\leq 4|v|_{h}|d(\pi_{N})_{(q,v)}(\xi)|_{h}.

Since
|d\varphi_{q}(K_{TN}(\xi))|\leq 2|K_{TN}(\xi)|_{h}
, we are done.

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(U,\varphi)
be an
\varepsilon
-isometric chart on
N
and let
\varphi^{(r)}
be its extension by
\theta
. Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open and bounded cube and let
u\in\Imm_{p}(Q;N)
. Define
\tilde{n}(x)
by

d\varphi^{(r)}_{u(x)}(\nu_{u}(x))=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\tilde{n}_{i}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\bigg|_{\varphi^{(r)}(u(x))},\quad x\in Q.

(3.5)

In other words,
\tilde{n}(x)=D\varphi^{(r)}(u(x))(\nu_{u}(x))
. Set
F:=u^{-1}(\varphi^{-1}(B(0,r)))
. Then
\tilde{n}\in W^{1,p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
and

\int_{Q}|D\tilde{n}|^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(\frac{1}{r^{p}}\int_{Q\setminus F}|du|_{\e_{d},h}^{p}\,dx+\int_{Q}|du|_{\e_{d},h}^{p}\,dx+E_{b}(u)\right).

The constant
C
depends only on
d
,
p
and
\theta
.

Proof.

We start by showing that
\tilde{n}
is a c.w.d. map. Let
O
be a bounded and open set such that
\supp(\varphi^{(r)})\subset O\subset\joinrel\subset U
. We define the compact set

G:=\{(q,v)\in TN:q\in\bar{O},|v|_{h}\leq 2\}.

We choose
\Phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(TN)
such that
\Phi\equiv 1
in
G
. Since
|\nu_{u}|_{h}=1
a.e. in
Q
, we have

\tilde{n}=D\varphi^{(r)}\circ\nu_{u}=(D\varphi^{(r)}\circ(\Phi\cdot\Id_{TN}))\circ\nu_{u}.

(3.6)

Clearly,
D\varphi^{(r)}\circ(\Phi\cdot\Id_{TN})\in C_{c}^{\infty}(TN;\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. Since
\nu_{u}
is a c.w.d., this implies that
\tilde{n}
is a c.w.d. map. Consequently,
\tilde{n}
is c.w.d. as well. However,
\tilde{n}\in L^{\infty}(Q)
, so that it is actually weakly differentiable.

Next, we compute
d\tilde{n}
. It follows from (
3.5
) and the second claim in Theorem
2.6
that

d\tilde{n}=K_{T\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\circ d(d\varphi^{(r)}\circ\nu_{u})=K_{T\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\circ d^{2}\varphi^{(r)}\circ d\nu_{u}\quad\text{a.e. in }Q.

(3.7)

We define
\theta^{(r)}(y):=r\theta(y/r)
. Then
\varphi^{(r)}=\theta^{(r)}\circ\varphi
in
U
. If we identify
TT\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with
\mathbb{R}^{4(d+1)}
as in (
2.11
), then a routine computation yields for all
(y,w,Y,W)\in TT\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
,

\displaystyle(K_{T\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\circ d^{2}\theta^{(r)})(y,w,Y,W)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\left(D\theta_{i}^{(r)}(y)(W)+(H\theta_{i}^{(r)}(y)w,Y)\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\bigg|_{y}

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\left(D\theta_{i}\left(\frac{y}{r}\right)(W)+\frac{1}{r}\left(H\theta_{i}\left(\frac{y}{r}\right)w,Y\right)\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\bigg|_{y},

where
H
stands for the Hessian matrix. For
(x,v)\in T\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with
x\in u^{-1}(U)
, we define
\tilde{y}(x)
,
\tilde{w}(x)
,
\tilde{Y}(x,v)
and
\tilde{W}(x,v)
by

\displaystyle\tilde{y}(x):=\varphi(u(x)),\quad\tilde{w}(x):=d\varphi_{u(x)}(\nu_{u}(x)),

\displaystyle d^{2}\varphi_{\nu_{u}(x)}(d(\nu_{u})_{x}(v))=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\tilde{Y}_{i}(x,v)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\bigg|_{(\tilde{y}(x),\tilde{w}(x))}+\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\tilde{W}_{i}(x,v)\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{i}}\bigg|_{(\tilde{y}(x),\tilde{w}(x))}.

Hence, (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) imply

d\tilde{n}_{x}=\sum_{k=1}^{d+1}\left(D\theta_{k}\left(\frac{\tilde{y}(x)}{r}\right)(\tilde{W}(x,\cdot))+\frac{1}{r}\left(H\theta_{k}\left(\frac{\tilde{y}(x)}{r}\right)\tilde{w}(x),\tilde{Y}(x,\cdot)\right)\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\bigg|_{\tilde{y}(x)}

(3.9)

for a.e.
x\in u^{-1}(U)
. We proceed to estimate
|d\tilde{n}_{x}|
. First of all, we note that

\tilde{Y}=d\pi_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\circ d^{2}\varphi\circ d\nu_{u},\quad\tilde{W}=K_{T\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\circ d^{2}\varphi\circ d\nu_{u}.

We can simplify the expression for
\tilde{Y}
by observing that
u=\pi_{N}\circ\nu_{u}
and
\pi_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\circ d\varphi=\varphi\circ\pi_{N}
, so that

d\pi_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\circ d^{2}\varphi\circ d\nu_{u}=d(\pi_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\circ d\varphi)\circ d\nu_{u}=d(\varphi\circ\pi_{N})\circ d\nu_{u}=d\varphi\circ du\quad\text{a.e. in }u^{-1}(U).

Therefore

\tilde{Y}(x,\cdot)=d\varphi_{u(x)}\circ du_{x}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in u^{-1}(U).

(3.10)

On the other hand, we can use Lemma
3.4
to obtain

|K_{T\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\circ d^{2}\varphi_{\nu_{u}(x)}\circ d(\nu_{u})_{x}|\leq C(|K_{TN}\circ d(\nu_{u})_{x}|_{\e_{d},h}+|\nu_{u}(x)|_{h}|d(\pi_{N})_{\nu_{u}(x)}\circ d(\nu_{u})_{x}|_{\e_{d},h})

for
x\in u^{-1}(U)
. Since
|\nu_{u}(x)|_{h}=1
a.e. in
Q
and
\pi_{N}\circ\nu_{u}=u
, we have

|\tilde{W}(x,\cdot)|\leq C(|K_{TN}\circ d(\nu_{u})_{x}|_{\e_{d},h}+|du_{x}|_{\e_{d},h})\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in u^{-1}(U).

(3.11)

We can now estimate
|d\tilde{n}_{x}|
for a.e.
x\in F=u^{-1}(\varphi^{-1}(B(0,r))
. Recall that
\theta\equiv\Id_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}
in
B(0,1)
. Hence, by (
3.9
),

d\tilde{n}_{x}=\sum_{k=1}^{d+1}\tilde{W}_{k}(x,\cdot)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\bigg|_{\tilde{y}(x)}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in F.

Therefore, (
3.11
) yields

|d\tilde{n}_{x}|\leq C(|K_{TN}\circ d(\nu_{u})_{x}|_{\e_{d},h}+|du_{x}|_{\e_{d},h})\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in F.

(3.12)

To give a bound in
u^{-1}(U)\setminus F
, we simply apply the triangle inequality to (
3.9
) and use (
3.10
) and (
3.11
):

\displaystyle|d\tilde{n}_{x}|

\displaystyle\leq\|D\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}|\tilde{W}(x,\cdot)|+\frac{C}{r}|\tilde{w}(x)||\tilde{Y}(x,\cdot)|\sum_{k=1}^{d+1}\|H\theta_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|K_{TN}\circ d(\nu_{u})_{x}|_{\e_{d},h}+|du_{x}|_{\e_{d},h}\right)+\frac{C}{r}|d\varphi_{u(x)}(\nu_{u}(x))||d\varphi_{u(x)}\circ du_{x}|

\displaystyle\leq C|K_{TN}\circ d(\nu_{u})_{x}|_{\e_{d},h}+\frac{C}{r}|du_{x}|_{\e_{d},h}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in u^{-1}(U)\setminus F,

where the last constant depends on
\theta
. Thus, to conclude, the previous estimate together with (
3.12
) gives the desired inequality:

\int_{Q}|d\tilde{n}|^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(\frac{1}{r^{p}}\int_{Q\setminus F}|du|_{\e_{d},h}^{p}\,dx+\int_{Q}|K_{TN}\circ d\nu_{u}|_{e_{d},h}^{p}+|du|_{\e_{d},h}^{p}\,dx\right).

This also proves that
\tilde{n}\in W^{1,p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
.
∎

For the remainder of this section, we fix an open and bounded cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
endowed with a metric
g
such that

\frac{1}{\lambda}\e_{d}\leq g\leq\lambda\e_{d}\quad\text{in }Q

for some
\lambda>0
. In the proof of our local quantitative rigidity result for Sobolev immersions, we rely on a generalization of the Friesecke-James-Müller rigidity estimate (
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
, see also
[
5
, Section 2.4]
) to domains endowed with a non-Euclidean metric. Compare the following result with
[
16
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Theorem 3.6
.

For every
u\in W^{1,p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
x_{0}\in Q
, there exists
R\in\SO(g_{x_{0}},\e_{d})
such that

\int_{Q}|Du-R|_{g,\e_{d}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(|Q|\left(\osc_{Q}g\right)^{p}+\int_{Q}\dist_{g,\e_{d}}^{p}(Du,\SO(g,\e_{d}))\,dx\right).

The constant
C
depends only on
p
,
d
and
\lambda
.

A proof of the theorem above is given in
[
3
]
. Finally, we state and prove the local quantitative rigidity result for Sobolev immersions.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(U,\varphi)
be an
\varepsilon
-isometric chart on
N
and let
\varphi^{(r)}
be its extension by
\theta
. Let
u\in\Imm_{p}(Q;N)
. Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and assume that
F\subset Q
is a Lebesgue measurable set such that

u(F)\subset\varphi^{-1}(B(0,r)),\quad\frac{|Q\setminus F|}{|Q|}\leq\delta^{p}.

Then there exists
x_{0}\in Q
and
R\in\Ort(g_{x},\e_{d+1})
such that

\int_{Q}|D(\varphi^{(r)}\circ u)-R|_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(|Q|\left(\delta^{p}+\varepsilon^{p}+\left(\osc_{Q}g\right)^{p}\right)+E_{s}(u)+\frac{\diam^{p}(Q)}{1-\delta^{p}}\mathcal{E}(u)\right),

where

\mathcal{E}(u):=\frac{1}{r^{p}}\int_{Q\setminus F}|du|_{\e_{d},h}^{p}\,dx+\int_{Q}|du|_{\e_{d},h}^{p}\,dx+E_{b}(u).

The constant
C
depends only on
d
,
p
,
\lambda
and
\theta
.

Proof.

Set
\tilde{u}:=\varphi^{(r)}\circ u
. Then
\tilde{u}
is weakly differentiable, since
u
is a c.w.d. map. In fact,
\tilde{u}\in W^{1,p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. Moreover,
\rank D\tilde{u}=d
a.e. in
F
because the restriction of
\varphi^{(r)}
to
\varphi^{-1}(B(0,r))
is a diffeomorphism. Thus, for a.e.
x\in F
, there exists a unit vector
n(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
such that
n(x)
is orthogonal to
D\tilde{u}(x)(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
(\partial_{x_{1}}\tilde{u}(x),\dots,\partial_{x_{d}}\tilde{u}(x),n(x))
is positively oriented. We also define
\tilde{n}
to be the pushforward of
\nu_{u}
under
\varphi^{(r)}
as in (
3.5
). Our strategy to prove the estimate is to project
\tilde{u}
onto a
d
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and to apply the non-Euclidean rigidity estimate in Theorem
3.6
. We choose a
d
-dimensional subspace such that the error in projection is bounded by the
L^{p}
-norm of
D\tilde{n}
. Since
u
is represented almost isometrically by
\tilde{u}
in
F
and
F
has a relatively large measure in
Q
, the final estimate will be sharp up to some truncation error in
Q\setminus F
.

By Lemma
3.5
,
\tilde{n}\in W^{1,p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, so that we can apply Poincaré’s inequality to get

\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|\tilde{n}(x)-\tilde{n}(z)|^{p}\,dx\,dz\leq C\diam^{p}(Q)|Q|\int_{Q}|D\tilde{n}(x)|^{p}\,dx.

Treating the inner integral on the left-hand side as a function of
z
and applying Chebyshev’s inequality to it, we see that the set of all
z\in Q
satisfying

\int_{Q}|\tilde{n}(x)-\tilde{n}(z)|^{p}\,dx\leq\frac{2C}{1-\delta^{p}}\diam^{p}(Q)\int_{Q}|D\tilde{n}(x)|^{p}\,dx.

(3.13)

is at least
|Q|(1-\delta^{p})/2
. We observe that for a.e.
x\in F
,
n(x)
and
\nu_{u}(x)
satisfy the hypotheses of part 2 of Lemma
3.2
with
\Pi=D\tilde{u}(x)(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence,

|n(x)-\tilde{n}(x)|\leq C\varepsilon\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in F.

(3.14)

Therefore, we can find
x_{0}\in F
at which both (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) are satisfed. Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{F}|n(x)-n(x_{0})|^{p}\,dx\leq C\varepsilon^{p}|F|+C\int_{F}|\tilde{n}(x)-

\displaystyle\tilde{n}(x_{0})|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{p}|Q|+C\frac{\diam^{p}(Q)}{1-\delta^{p}}\int_{Q}|D\tilde{n}(x)|^{p}\,dx.

Then by Lemma
3.5
, we obtain

\int_{F}|n(x)-n(x_{0})|^{p}\,dx\leq C\varepsilon^{p}|Q|+C\frac{\diam^{p}(Q)}{1-\delta^{p}}\mathcal{E}(u).

(3.15)

We denote
\lspan(n(x_{0}))^{\perp}
briefly by
\Pi_{0}
. Let
P
be the orthogonal projection from
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
onto
\Pi_{0}
. Let
T\in\SO((\mathbb{R}^{d},\e_{d}),(\Pi_{0},\e_{d+1}))
and set
v:=T^{-1}\circ P\circ\tilde{u}
. Then
v\in W^{1,p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and we can apply Theorem
3.6
to obtain
\bar{R}\in\SO(g_{x_{0}},\e_{d})
such that

\int_{Q}|Dv-\bar{R}|_{g,\e_{d}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(|Q|\left(\osc_{Q}g\right)^{p}+\int_{Q}\dist_{g,\e_{d}}^{p}(Dv,\SO(g,\e_{d}))\,dx\right).

(3.16)

Set
R:=T\circ\bar{R}
. Then
R\in\SO((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{x_{0}}),(\Pi_{0},\e_{d+1}))
, and by (
3.21
) in Lemma
3.8
(see below), we have for a.e.
x\in F

\displaystyle|D\tilde{u}(x)-R|_{g,\e_{d+1}}

\displaystyle\leq|D\tilde{u}(x)-PD\tilde{u}(x)|_{g,\e_{d+1}}+|PD\tilde{u}(x)-R|_{g,\e_{d+1}}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq|D\tilde{u}(x)|_{g,\e_{d+1}}|n(x)-n(x_{0})|+|Dv(x)-\bar{R}|_{g,\e_{d}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{d}|n(x)-n(x_{0})|+2\dist_{g,\e_{d+1}}(D\tilde{u}(x),\Ort(g_{x},\e_{d+1}))+|Dv(x)-\bar{R}|_{g,\e_{d}}

Furthermore, (
3.22
) gives

\dist_{g,\e_{d}}(Dv(x),\SO(g_{x},\e_{d}))=\dist_{g,\e_{d+1}}(PD\tilde{u}(x),\SO((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{x}),(\Pi_{0},\e_{d+1}))\\
\leq\dist_{g,\e_{d+1}}(D\tilde{u}(x),\Ort(g_{x},\e_{d+1}))+C|n(x)-n(x_{0})|.

for a.e.
x\in F
. Hence, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{F}|D\tilde{u}-R

\displaystyle|_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}\,dx

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|Q|\left(\osc_{Q}g\right)^{p}+\int_{F}\dist_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}(D\tilde{u},\Ort(g_{,}\e_{d+1}))\,dx+\int_{F}|n(x)-n(x_{0})|^{p}\,dx\right).

If we identify
T_{x}\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
D\tilde{u}(x)=D\varphi(u(x))\circ du_{x}
for a.e.
x\in F
. Thus, part 1 of Lemma
3.2
implies

\dist_{g,\e_{d+1}}(D\tilde{u}(x),\Ort(g_{x},\e_{d+1}))\leq\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}\dist_{g,h}(du_{x},\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{x}),(T_{u(x)}N,h_{u(x)})))+C\varepsilon

for a.e.
x\in F
. Consequently, we obtain from (

\int_{F}|D\tilde{u}-R|_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(|Q|\left(\varepsilon^{p}+\left(\osc_{Q}g\right)^{p}\right)+E_{s}(u)+\int_{F}|n(x)-n(x_{0})|^{p}\,dx\right).

(3.19)

To conclude the proof, we apply the triangle inequality in

\int_{Q\setminus F}|D\tilde{u}-R|_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(\int_{Q\setminus F}(1+\varepsilon)^{\frac{p}{2}}|du|_{g,h}^{p}+1\,dx\right)\leq C(E_{s}(u)+\delta^{p}|Q|).

(3.20)

Here

\theta
. Finally, combining (

∎

4
Asymptotic rigidity

In the next theorem, we state our main result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(M,g)
and
(N,h)
be oriented, connected, complete Riemannian manifolds of dimensions
d
and
d+1
, respectively. Let
M
be compact. Assume
(u_{k})\subset\Imm_{p}(M;N)
satisfies

\lim_{k\to\infty}E_{s}(u_{k})=0,\quad\limsup_{k\to\infty}E_{b}(u_{k})<\infty

(4.1)

and

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{M}d_{h}^{p}(u_{k},q_{0})\,d\vol_{g}<\infty

(4.2)

for some
q_{0}\in N
. Then there exists a subsequence
(u_{k_{j}})
and
u\in\Imm_{p}(M;N)
such that

u_{k_{j}}\to u\text{ in }\dot{W}^{1,p}(M;N),\quad du_{x}\in\Ort((T_{x}M,g_{x}),(T_{u(x)}N,h_{u(x)}))\text{ for a.e. }x\in M.

(4.3)

Furthermore, if
S:TM\rightarrow TM
is the reference shape operator with
|S|_{g}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(M)
, where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
, and

\lim_{k\to\infty}E_{b}^{S}(u_{k})=0,

(4.4)

then
S=S_{u}
a.e. in
M
.

We prove Theorem
4.1
in Section
5
. In this section, we collect results that we will need in the proof. As before, we fix an open and bounded cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
endowed with a metric
g
such that

\frac{1}{\lambda}\e_{d}\leq g\leq\lambda\e_{d}\quad\text{in }Q

for some
\lambda>0
. Since
g
is comparable to the Euclidean metric, we will often bound quantities measured with respect to
g
by those measured with respect to the Euclidean metric and vice versa. The result is summarized in the next lemma, which we will use implicitly most of the time.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
f:Q\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is measurable, then

\frac{1}{\lambda^{d/2}}\int_{Q}f\,dx\leq\int_{Q}f\,d\vol_{g}\leq\lambda^{d/2}\int_{Q}f\,dx.

If
V
is a vector space endowed with a constant metric
h_{0}
and
L\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
, then

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}|L|_{g_{x},h_{0}}\leq|L|_{\e_{d},h_{0}}\leq\sqrt{\lambda}|L|_{g_{x},h_{0}}\quad\text{for all }x\in Q.

Proof.

The proof of the first claim is elementary. The second claim follows from Lemma 3.4 in
[
3
]
.
∎

Our main goal in this section is to prove that two Sobolev immersions are close in the
\dot{W}^{1,p}
-sense on small scales if their
L^{p}
-distance and stretching energies are small. We begin with a lemma that bounds the norm of a linear map by its “
L^{p}
-variation”.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
R\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. Then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
p
and
d
such that

|R|\leq C|Q|^{-\frac{1}{d}-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|R(x-y)|^{p}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
Q
is the unit cube centered at
0
. For more general cubes, the inequality follows by a scaling argument. Define
\Psi:\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\Psi(R)=\left(\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|R(x-y)|^{p}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Clearly,
\Psi
is a continuous function on
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. Set
m:=\min\{\Psi(R):|R|=1\}
. Since
\Psi(R)=0
if and only if
R=0
, we have
m>0
. Noting that both
|R|
and
\Psi(R)
are positively homogeneous of degree 1, we have

|R|\leq\frac{1}{m}\left(\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|R(x-y)|^{p}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\text{for all }R\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d+1}).

∎

Let
(N,h)
be a connected, oriented Riemannian manifold. We denote the Sasaki metric on
(T^{*}Q\otimes TN,\pi,Q\times N)
by
\sigma
. In the next lemma, we prove an estimate for the Sasaki distance of linear maps in terms of their distance in coordinates.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(U,\varphi)
be an
\varepsilon
-isometric chart on
N
. Assume that
B(0,r)\subset\varphi(U)
. Let
x\in Q
and let
q_{i}\in\varphi^{-1}(B(0,r))
,
L_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(T_{x}\mathbb{R}^{d},T_{q_{i}}N)
for
i=1,2
. Then

d_{\sigma}(L_{1},L_{2})\leq C(|D\varphi(q_{1})\circ L_{1}-d\varphi(q_{2})\circ L_{2}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}+d_{h}(q_{1},q_{2})(1+|L_{2}|_{g,h})),

where
C
depends only on
d
.

Proof.

Define
y(t):=\varphi(q_{1})+t(\varphi(q_{2})-\varphi(q_{1}))
for
t\in[0,1]
and set
\gamma(t):=\varphi^{-1}(y(t))
. By Proposition
2.1
we know that

d_{\sigma}(L_{1},L_{2})^{2}\leq|L_{1}-L_{\gamma}|_{g,h}^{2}+\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\prime}|_{h}\,dt\right)^{2},

(4.5)

where
L_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{L}(T_{x}\mathbb{R}^{d},T_{q_{1}}N)
is defined as in (
2.7
). We can bound the second term on the right-hand side simply by

\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|_{h}\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}|d(\varphi^{-1})_{y(t)}(\varphi(q_{2})-\varphi(q_{1}))|_{h}\,dt\leq 2|\varphi(q_{2})-\varphi(q_{1})|.

(4.6)

Let
v\in T_{x}\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We define
\alpha(t)\in T_{\gamma(t)}N
to be the vector parallel to
L_{2}v
along
\gamma
. We shall estimate
|(L_{1}-L_{\gamma})v|_{h}=|L_{1}v-\alpha(0)|_{h}
. If we represent
\alpha
in coordinates by

\alpha(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}a_{k}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\bigg|_{\gamma(t)},

then
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}
satisfy the following system of ordinary differential equations:

a_{k}^{\prime}(t)=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{i}(t)y_{j}^{\prime}(t)\Gamma_{i,j}^{k}(\gamma(t)),\quad k=1,\dots,d,

(4.7)

where
\Gamma_{i,j}^{k}:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are the Christoffel symbols. The triangle inequality gives

|L_{1}v-\alpha(0)|_{h}\leq 2|D\varphi(q_{1})(L_{1}v-\alpha(0))|\leq 2|D\varphi(q_{1})(L_{1}v)-D\varphi(q_{2})(L_{2}v)|+2|a(1)-a(0)|.

(4.8)

By (
3.1
) and (
4.7
), we have

|a(1)-a(0)|\leq\int_{0}^{1}|a^{\prime}(t)|\,dt\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\max_{i,j,k}\|\Gamma_{i,j}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}|a(t)||y^{\prime}(t)|\,dt\\
\leq C\int_{0}^{1}|a(t)||y^{\prime}(t)|\,dt\leq C|\varphi(q_{2})-\varphi(q_{1})|\int_{0}^{1}|\alpha(t)|_{h}\,dt.

Since parallel transport preserves length, we know that
|\alpha(t)|_{h}=|L_{2}v|_{h}
. Hence,

|a(1)-a(0)|\leq C|\varphi(q_{2})-\varphi(q_{1})||L_{2}v|_{h}.

Thus, by (
4.8
), we conclude that

|(L_{1}-L_{\gamma})v|_{h}\leq C(|D\varphi(q_{1})(L_{1}v)-D\varphi(q_{2})(L_{2}v)|+|\varphi(q_{1})-\varphi(q_{2})||L_{2}v|_{h}).

Consequently, we obtain

|L_{1}-L_{\gamma}|_{g,h}\leq C(|D\varphi(q_{1})\circ L_{1}-D\varphi(q_{2})\circ L_{2}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}+|\varphi(q_{1})-\varphi(q_{2})||L_{2}|_{g,h})

Finally, (
4.5
), (
4.6
) and the inequality
|\varphi(q_{1})-\varphi(q_{2})|\leq 2d_{h}(q_{1},q_{2})
yield the claim.
∎

We come to the main result of this section.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(U,\varphi)
be an
\varepsilon
-isometric chart on
N
and let
\varphi^{(r)}
be its extension by
\theta
. Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in\Imm_{p}(Q;N)
. Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and assume that
F_{k}\subset Q
is a Lebesgue measurable set such that

u_{k}(F_{k})\subset\varphi^{-1}(B(0,r)),\quad\frac{|Q\setminus F_{k}|}{|Q|}\leq\delta^{p}\quad\text{for }k=1,2.

Then

\int_{Q}d_{\sigma}^{p}(u_{1},u_{2})\,dx\leq C|Q|\left(\delta^{p}+\varepsilon^{p}+\left(\osc_{Q}g\right)^{p}+\frac{\diam^{p}(Q)}{1-\delta^{p}}\left(1+\frac{\delta^{p}}{r^{p}}\right)\right)\\
+C\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{r^{p}}\right)\max_{k=1,2}E_{s}(u_{k})+\frac{\diam^{p}(Q)}{1-\delta^{p}}\max_{k=1,2}E_{b}(u_{k})+\left(1+|Q|^{-\frac{p}{d}}\right)\int_{Q}d_{h}^{p}(u_{1},u_{2})\,dx\right).

(4.9)

The constant
C
depends only on
d
,
p
,
\lambda
and
\theta
.

Proof.

We briefly summarize the content and the proof method of the lemma. The reader should consider
E_{s}(u_{1})
,
E_{s}(u_{2})
and
|Q|
as sufficiently small quantities, so that (
4.9
) implies that the
\dot{W}^{1,p}
distance of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
is controlled by their
L^{p}
distance. We shall prove this by using Theorem
3.7
to show that
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are close to some rotations
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
in coordinates. Then knowing the
L^{p}
distance of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
gives us control on
|R_{1}-R_{2}|
, which controls the distance of
du_{1}
and
du_{2}
thanks to the rigidity theorem.

Set
\tilde{u}_{k}:=\varphi^{(r)}\circ u_{k}
. By the quantitative rigidity estimate in Theorem
3.7
, there exist
x_{k}\in Q
and
R_{k}\in\Ort(g_{x},\e_{d+1})
for
k=1,2
such that

\int_{Q}|D\tilde{u}_{k}-R_{k}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(|Q|\left(\delta^{p}+\varepsilon^{p}+\left(\osc_{Q}g\right)^{p}\right)+E_{s}(u_{k})+\frac{\diam^{p}(Q)}{1-\delta^{p}}\mathcal{E}(u_{k})\right).

(4.10)

For
x\in F_{k}
, we define
L_{k}(x)\in\mathcal{L}(T_{x}\mathbb{R}^{d},T_{u_{k}(x)}N)
by
L_{k}(x):=d(\varphi^{-1})_{\tilde{u}_{k}(x)}\circ R_{k}
, where we identify
T_{\tilde{u}_{k}(x)}\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Since
D\varphi\circ du_{k}=D\tilde{u}_{k}
a.e. in
F_{k}
, we have

|d(u_{k})_{x}-L_{k}(x)|_{g,h}\leq 2|D\varphi(u_{k}(x))(d(u_{k})_{x}-L_{k}(x))|_{g,\e_{d+1}}=2|D\tilde{u}_{k}(x)-R_{k}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}.

(4.11)

for a.e.
x\in F_{k}
. Furthermore, it follows from Lemma
4.4
that

d_{\sigma}(L_{1}(x),L_{2}(x))\leq C(|D\varphi(u_{1}(x))\circ L_{1}(x)-D\varphi(u_{2}(x))\circ L_{2}(x)|_{g,\e_{d+1}}+d_{h}(u_{1}(x),u_{2}(x))(1+|L_{2}(x)|_{g,h}))\\
\leq C(|R_{1}-R_{2}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}+d_{h}(u_{1}(x),u_{2}(x))(1+2|R_{2}|_{g,h}))\leq C(|R_{1}-R_{2}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}+d_{h}(u_{1}(x),u_{2}(x))),

where the last constant depends on
d
and
\lambda
. Using the triangle inequality and Proposition
2.2
, we obtain for a.e.
x\in F_{1}\cap F_{2}
,

d_{\sigma}(d(u_{1})_{x},d(u_{2})_{x})\leq d_{\sigma}(d(u_{1})_{x},L_{1}(x))+d_{\sigma}(L_{1}(x),L_{2}(x))+d_{\sigma}(L_{2}(x),d(u_{2})_{x})\\
\leq|d(u_{1})_{x}-L_{1}(x)|_{g,h}+|d(u_{2})_{x}-L_{2}(x)|_{g,h}+C(|R_{1}-R_{2}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}+d_{h}(u_{1}(x),u_{2}(x))).

(4.12)

Integrating (
4.12
) over
F_{1}\cap F_{2}
and using (
4.11
) gives

\int_{F_{1}\cap F_{2}}d_{\sigma}^{p}(du_{1},du_{2})\,dx\leq C\int_{Q}|D\tilde{u}_{1}-R_{1}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}+|D\tilde{u}_{2}-R_{2}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}+|R_{1}-R_{2}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}+d_{h}^{p}(u_{1},u_{2})\,dx.

(4.13)

Next, we estimate
|R_{1}-R_{2}|
. By Poincaré’s inequality applied to
\tilde{u}_{k}-R_{k}
, we have

\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|\tilde{u}_{k}(x)-\tilde{u}_{k}(y)-R_{k}(x-y)|^{p}\,dx\,dy\leq C\diam^{p}(Q)|Q|\int_{Q}|D\tilde{u}_{k}(x)-R_{k}|^{p}\,dx\quad\text{for }k=1,2.

(4.14)

Since

|(R_{1}-R_{2})(x-y)|\leq|\tilde{u}_{1}(x)-\tilde{u}_{1}(y)-R_{1}(x-y)|+|\tilde{u}_{2}(x)-\tilde{u}_{2}(y)-R_{2}(x-y)|+|\tilde{u}_{1}(x)-\tilde{u}_{2}(x)|+|\tilde{u}_{1}(y)-\tilde{u}_{2}(y)|,

the inequality (
4.14
) implies

\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|(R_{1}-R_{2})(x-y)|^{p}\,dx\,dy\\
\leq C|Q|\left(\diam^{p}(Q)\int_{Q}|D\tilde{u}_{1}(x)-R_{1}|^{p}+|D\tilde{u}_{2}(x)-R_{2}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{Q}|\tilde{u}_{1}(x)-\tilde{u}_{2}(x)|^{p}\,dx\right).

Finally, Lemma
4.3
yields

\displaystyle|R_{1}-R_{2}|^{p}\leq|Q|^{-\frac{p}{d}-2}\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|(R_{1}-R_{2})(x-y)|^{p}\,dx\,dy

(4.15)

\displaystyle\leq C|Q|^{-\frac{p}{d}-1}\left(\diam^{p}(Q)\int_{Q}|D\tilde{u}_{1}(x)-R_{1}|^{p}+|D\tilde{u}_{2}(x)-R_{2}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{Q}|\tilde{u}_{1}(x)-\tilde{u}_{2}(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq C|Q|^{-1}\left(\int_{Q}|D\tilde{u}_{1}(x)-R_{1}|^{p}+|D\tilde{u}_{2}(x)-R_{2}|^{p}\,dx+|Q|^{-\frac{p}{d}}\int_{Q}|\tilde{u}_{1}(x)-\tilde{u}_{2}(x)|^{p}\,dx\right).

We observe that there exists a constant
C
depending only on
\theta
such that

\|\tilde{u}_{1}-\tilde{u}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\leq C\|d_{h}(u_{1},u_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\quad\text{ for all }k,l\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence, we obtain

\int_{Q}|R_{1}-R_{2}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}\,dx\leq C|Q||R_{1}-R_{2}|^{p}\\
\leq C\left(\int_{Q}|D\tilde{u}_{1}-R_{1}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}+|D\tilde{u}_{2}-R_{2}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}\,dx+|Q|^{-\frac{p}{d}}\int_{Q}d_{h}^{p}(u_{1},u_{2})\,dx\right)

The previous estimate, in combination with (
4.13
), implies

\int_{F_{1}\cap F_{2}}d_{\sigma}^{p}(du_{1},du_{2})\,dx\leq C\int_{Q}|D\tilde{u}_{1}-R_{1}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}+|D\tilde{u}_{2}-R_{2}|_{g,\e_{d+1}}^{p}+(1+|Q|^{-\frac{p}{d}})d_{h}^{p}(u_{1},u_{2})\,dx.

(4.16)

Therefore, (

\int_{F_{1}\cap F_{2}}d_{\sigma}^{p}(du_{1},du_{2})\,dx\leq C|Q|\left(\delta^{p}+\varepsilon^{p}+\left(\osc_{Q}g\right)^{p}+\right)+C\max_{k=1,2}E_{s}(u_{k})\\
+C\left(\frac{\diam^{p}(Q)}{1-\delta^{p}}\max_{k=1,2}\mathcal{E}(u_{k})+\left(1+|Q|^{-\frac{p}{d}}\right)\int_{Q}d_{h}^{p}(u_{1},u_{2})\,dx\right).

Since,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u_{k})

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{r^{p}}|Q\setminus F_{k}|+|Q|+\left(1+\frac{1}{r^{p}}\right)E_{s}(u_{k})+E_{b}(u_{k})\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left(1+\frac{\delta^{p}}{r^{p}}\right)|Q|+\left(1+\frac{1}{r^{p}}\right)E_{s}(u_{k})+E_{b}(u_{k})\right)\quad\text{for }k=1,2,

we arrive at

\int_{F_{1}\cap F_{2}}d_{\sigma}^{p}(du_{1},du_{2})\,dx\leq C|Q|\left(\delta^{p}+\varepsilon^{p}+\left(\osc_{Q}g\right)^{p}+\frac{\diam^{p}(Q)}{1-\delta^{p}}\left(1+\frac{\delta^{p}}{r^{p}}\right)\right)\\
+C\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{r^{p}}\right)\max_{k=1,2}E_{s}(u_{k})+\frac{\diam^{p}(Q)}{1-\delta^{p}}\max_{k=1,2}E_{b}(u_{k})+\left(1+|Q|^{-\frac{p}{d}}\right)\int_{Q}d_{h}^{p}(u_{1},u_{2})\,dx\right).

(4.17)

On the other hand, we can apply the last point in Proposition
2.2
outside of
F_{1}\cap F_{2}
to get

\int_{Q\setminus(F_{1}\cap F_{2})}d_{\sigma}^{p}(du_{1},du_{2})\,dx\leq C\int_{Q\setminus(F_{1}\cap F_{2})}|du_{1}|_{g,h}^{p}+d_{h}^{p}(u_{1},u_{2})+|du_{2}|_{g,h}^{p}\,dx\\
\leq C\left(\delta^{p}|Q|+E_{s}(u_{1})+E_{s}(u_{2})+\int_{Q}d_{h}^{p}(u_{1},u_{2})\,dx\right).

(4.18)

To conclude the proof, we simply add (
4.17
) and (
4.18
).

(5.4)

Step 2:
We fix
