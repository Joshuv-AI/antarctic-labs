Dear Stan:-

First, I was terribly sorry to hear about the death of
your father several weeks ago. I know that his final illness was
a sad additional burden for you over a long summer when you did
not need additional burdens. Anne and I moved to Kentucky in the
summer of 1971, and both of her parents and my parents were alive
and well in Tennessee only 200 miles away. We had several good
visits back and forth in that 1971-72 year and then, within three
years, we had lost three out of the four. Now, only my mother
survives. In short, we know your grief, and can in this sense
share your burden.

Second, and on an only somewhat happier note, you are
aware that the University of Kentucky has filed a law suit against
the Iranian students who were arrested here for the disturbances
in connection with your visit on 12 April. My initial hunch was
that the Iranians would try to convert it into a showcase trial, \

complete with national media hoopla and maybe a defense attorney
such as William Kuntsler. I may still be correct on the general
point, but the Iranian students (i.e., some of them are students)
have hired a couple of inexperienced young local female lawyers to
represent them. Both of these women, Barbara Sutherland and Pam
Goldman, have been up to talk to me. I got the impression that
they are somewhat strident "women's liberation" types. I suppose
that one element in their legal case will be the usual First Amend-
ment business, but they apparently also plan to claim that the
Iranians created merely a "commotion" as contrasted to a "disturb-
ance" (I have no idea how the law may differentiate between these
two concepts). I am further guessing that the defense attorneys
will attempt to serve a subpoena on at least one member of your
staff who was in your group here on 12 April. The defense managed
to have subpoenas served today on a number of witnesses--primarily
from among those VIP guests who were first with us at the pre-talk
dinner at the hotel, all of whom are now calling me with this news.

This Strikes me as an absurd defense tactic, in view of the fact
that these VIP friends of ours are distinctly unsympathetic to
the Iranians, and are more likely to testify that the events of
12 April resembled a riot rather than a disturbance or commotion.
Furthermore, the defense attorneys apparently plan to introduce,
in evidence a copy of the official University archives tape-re-
cording of the whole evening. That tape makes it very clear
that something considerably more than a "commotion" was going on.
Well, I'm no lawyer, but it ought to be fairly interesting if the
defense calls witnesses such as former U.S. Senator Albert Gore,
former Lexington Mayor Foster Pettit, the daughter of former Go-
vernor A. B. "Happy” Chandler, and incumbent Federal Judge E. E.
"Gene" Siler, all of whom were more than a little distressed over
the behavior of the Iranians, and none of whom is famous for con-
nections with radical causes.

I am not aware that a trial date has been set. /v Given the
possibility of U.S. foreign policy overtones, I myself would think
it perhaps useful if certain officials in Washington used whatever
channels might be available to them to seek a delay until at least
8 November- -since there will be more than enough public distortions
leading up to 7 November. Or maybe the best tactic would simply be
to get the thing over with and done, as soon as possible. Perhaps
I am exaggerating the implications but, as you know, for a period
of many months now I have been attaching more importance to problems
pertaining to Iran than most other people whom I know.

If you are already receiving all of the information that
you need about this probable courtroom trial in Lexington, let me
know and I will then cease and desist from sending you whatever
I may know and think about the situation. It's practically certain,
I believe, that I will be subpoenaed as a witness--indeed, probably
getting a subpoena from both the prosecution and the defense.

This wraps up my immediate agenda. I'll be back on the
line with you later, on other matters of shared interest.

Warmest personal regards,

Vincent Davis

*Belay that. The subpoenas for the plaintiff (i.e., the Common-
wealth of Kentucky) indicate a trial date of 26. September, at 1000
EDT, on the 4th Floor of our Fayette County Courthouse in midtown
Lexington.
