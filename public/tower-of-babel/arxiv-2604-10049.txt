The Pareto principle in Sports and Economics in view of Runs Scored by Batters in the Indian Premier League
Soumendra Nath Ruz, Asim Ghosh

Abstract
The analysis of income and wealth inequality is often constrained by the lack of reliable data. In this work, we introduce a proxy-based approach in which sports performance data are used to mimic economic distributions. In particular, the total runs scored by a batter in a single T20 season are treated as an analogue of annual income, while the cumulative runs scored across all seasons are taken to represent individual wealth. Using run distributions from the Indian Premier League (IPL), we explore how inequality evolves and estimate its upper limits in the absence of policy intervention. Our findings indicate that inequality derived from seasonal runs closely resembles the highest levels of income inequality observed in real-world data. In addition, the distribution of cumulative runs evolves over time and gradually approaches a limiting value consistent with global patterns of wealth inequality, in line with the Pareto principle. Overall, this study shows that proxy systems can capture essential features of economic inequality and offer a useful way to understand its inherent limits.

IV
Income and wealth inequality in terms of IPL data

We have discussed about income and wealth in the introduction. Income is necessary to meet our daily needs. It acts as a foundation for achieving long-term goals, i.e. making wealth. On the other hand, wealth gives stability to a family/individual to sustain in the society. Wealthy people do not bother much, even if, at present their income is low and have time to grow.

let us try to find what income and wealth resemble in terms of run scored by batters in IPL. We have discussed key features of IPL in the previous section. Around 10 teams teams participate in recent sessions of IPL. Each team competes in a round-robin in the league stages, followed by 4 playoffs and a final. Thus, every team plays several matches in a season. Every match consists of two innings. Batters from either teams get the chance to bat in one of the innings. As it is a 20 over innings match, batters of each team try to score more runs as quickly as possible. Moreover, various prizes lures the batters to play every innings in a spectacular way. In the process, a batter can play several innings if he stays on the team for the entire season. Each team generally retains good batters in the next sessions also. In the process, some batters with good performance report play several innings.

We have segregated the runs scored by players in IPL into two categories:

•

Individual run scored by players in each innings.

•

Cumulative individual run scored in all the past sessions till date.

Now let us compare the role of these two types of runs in the carrier of a batter in IPL. Cumulative run count gives a batter the stability to sustain in the team for longer period. If one batter has played earlier innings well, then his cumulative run will be higher. It is well known that a player can not perform good in every innings. At the same time, IPL matches are very competitive and team management can replace any player in the team if his performance is not satisfactory. Suppose a batter scores low in a particular innings but his cumulative run count is good till date. Then, organizers will not remove him abruptly in the next match and give him chances to do well in the future matches. However, if his cumulative run is low and do not perform well in an innings, team management may remove him. In a nutshell, cumulative run acts as wealth for a player while the runs scored by him in each innings act like his income which grows wealth over the time. If past income is good wealth is gets accumulated and for a certain period even if income become low he has time to survive.

Now let us concentrate on how to calculate the inequality in individual run scored by batters in each innings, i.e., income inequality. For this, one has to list which player has made how many runs in each innings for a particular IPL season. At the same time, we have to keep in mind that cumulative run scored bay batters are related to wealth. So, while calculating income inequality we have to judiciously discard cumulative runs. In this scenario, we have calculated income inequality in the following way.

Conclusion

In the present article, we have viewed income and wealth from a completely different aspect and measured the inequality in them from data which are accurate and open to all.
