Safety of zinc l‐carnosine as a novel food pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 and bioavailability of zinc from this source in the context of Directive 2002/46/EC
Authors: EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA), Dominique Turck, Torsten Bohn, Montaña Cámara, Jacqueline Castenmiller, Stefaan De Henauw, Ángeles Jos, Alexandre Maciuk, Inge Mangelsdorf, Breige McNulty, Androniki Naska, Kristina Pentieva, Alfonso Siani, Frank Thies, Francesco Cubadda, Thomas Frenzel, Ursula Gundert‐Remy, Francesca Marcon, Harry J McArdle, Monika Neuhäuser‐Berthold, Matthew Wright, Irene Nuin Garciarena, Ruth Roldán‐Torres, Areti Favata, Karen Ildico Hirsch‐Ernst
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, the EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA) was asked to deliver an opinion on zinc l‐carnosine as a novel food (NF) pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 and as a source of zinc for use in food supplements. The NF is produced by chemical synthesis. The target population is individuals above the age of 12 years, excluding pregnant and lactating women. The NF which is the subject of the application is a chelate complex, formed between Zn2+ and l‐carnosine. The material is a powder with a particulate nature and is insoluble in water at neutral pH. The Panel considers that the available data do not allow to exclude the presence of a fraction of small particles including nanoparticles in the NF under assessment and do not support its quick dissolution in gastric conditions. In addition, due to the limitations of the studies provided, the actual bioavailability of the zinc provided by the NF at the proposed use levels remains uncharacterised. The Panel concludes that the NF is insufficiently characterised and neither its bioavailability nor its safety can be established.Keywords: bioavailability, food supplement, novel foods, nutrient source, safety, zinc l‐carnosine

On 2 July 2024, the company Hamari Chemicals Ltd. submitted an application to the European Commission in accordance with Article 10 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 to authorise the placing on the Union market of zinc L‐carnosine as a novel food. The applicant requests the authorisation of the use of zinc L‐carnosine as a novel food in food supplements as defined by Directive 2002/46/EC for the general population above 12 years of age, excluding pregnant and lactating women.

Information on existing evaluations and authorisations

In 2003, the Scientific Committee on Food (SCF) established tolerable upper intake levels (UL) for zinc (SCF, 2003). An UL for zinc of 25 mg/day was established for adults, including pregnant and lactating women. For children and adolescents, ULs were established (by extrapolation from the UL for adults) at 7 mg/day for 1–3 years, 10 mg/day for 4–6 years, 13 mg/day for 7–10 years, 18 mg/day for 11–14 years and 22 mg/day for 15–17 years (SCF, 2003).

In 2022, the EFSA NDA Panel (2022) assessed the safety of zinc l‐carnosine as a novel food in accordance with Article 10(3) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, including the bioavailability of zinc from this source in the context of Directive 2002/46/EC. The material is a powder, has particulate nature and is insoluble in water at neutral pH. No relevant data using an existing zinc source as comparator were provided by the applicant and the actual bioavailability of the zinc by the NF at the proposed use levels remained uncharacterised. The Panel concluded that owing to the lack of information on the size distribution and the physicochemical properties of the particles constituting the NF, it was not possible to confirm whether the absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion (ADME) studies and the toxicological studies provided by the applicant were appropriate to assess the safety of the NF. The Panel therefore concluded that the NF is absorbed and provides zinc, but as it is in an insufficiently characterised particulate form, its safety has not been established and the bioavailability has not been determined (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022).

As of the date of this scientific opinion, zinc l‐carnosine has not been authorised for use in food supplements in the EU under Directive 2002/46/EC.

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The safety assessment of this NF is based on data supplied in the application and information submitted by the applicant following a request by EFSA for supplementary information.

Administrative and scientific requirements for NF applications referred to in Article 10 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 are listed in Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2469. 3

A common and structured format on the presentation of NF applications is described in the EFSA guidance on the preparation and presentation of a NF application (EFSA NDA Panel, 2021). As indicated in this guidance, it is the duty of the applicant to provide all of the available (proprietary, confidential and published) scientific data, including both data in favour and not in favour that are pertinent to the safety of the NF.

Methodologies

The assessment follows the methodology set out in the EFSA guidance on NF applications (EFSA NDA Panel, 2021) and the principles described in the relevant existing guidance documents from the EFSA Scientific Committee. The legal provisions for the assessment are laid down in Article 11 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 and in Article 7 of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2469.

This assessment concerns only the risks that might be associated with consumption of the NF under the proposed conditions of use, and is not an assessment of the efficacy of the NF with regard to any claimed benefit.

The evaluation of bioavailability of the nutrient (zinc) from the NF was conducted in line with the principles contained in the ‘Guidance on safety evaluation of sources of nutrients and bioavailability of nutrient from the sources’ (EFSA ANS Panel, 2021).

The assessment of small particles in the NF was conducted by EFSA in line with the principles of the ‘Guidance on technical requirements (TR) for regulated food and feed product applications to establish the presence of small particles including nanoparticles’ (‘Guidance on TR’) (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2021).

ASSESSMENT

Introduction

The NF which is the subject of the application is zinc l‐carnosine. The NF is produced by chemical synthesis and is proposed to be used in food supplements as a source of zinc. The target population is individuals above 12 years of age, excluding pregnant and lactating women.

The NF falls under the following categories, as defined in Article 3 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283: (i) food with a new or intentionally modified molecular structure, where that structure was not used as, or in, a food within the Union before 15 May 1997; (ix) vitamins, minerals and other substances used in accordance with Directive 2002/46/EC, Regulation (EC) No 1925/2006 or Regulation (EU) No 609/2013.

Identity of the NF

The NF is zinc l‐carnosine, a chelate complex formed between Zn2+ and l‐carnosine. l‐carnosine is a naturally occurring dipeptide consisting of l‐histidine and the non‐proteinogenic amino acid β‐alanine. The identity of the NF has already been assessed by EFSA in its previous opinion (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022).

The NF is a solid powder material, with a particulate nature, insoluble in water at pH 7. Following the concerns on the characterisation of the particle size distribution raised in the previously published opinion (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022), and upon EFSA's request, the applicant provided new particle size distribution data obtained by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) to characterise and quantify the fraction of small/nano particles in line with the appraisal route described in Section 3 of the Guidance on TR (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2021). However, the reliability of the data provided was deemed insufficient. Major deviations from the provisions of Section 3 of the Guidance on TR and the relevant Annexes C and D for proper reporting of SEM‐based data were identified (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2021). The magnification levels used were too low to visualise particles in the nano range and the image quality did not allow to distinguish between constituent particles and agglomerates/aggregates. In addition, original reports and accreditation of the laboratory which performed the SEM measurements were not made available. A detailed appraisal of the SEM data is provided in Appendix A.

The NDA Panel notes that appropriate studies on particle size distribution were not provided and that the concerns raised in the previous opinion therefore remain unaddressed.

Production process

The NF is produced by chemical synthesis, as described and assessed by the NDA Panel previously. The Panel considers that the production process was sufficiently described (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022).

Stability

In 2022, the Panel assessed the stability of the NF on the basis of the results of stability testing for three batches of the NF (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022). In the present submission, the applicant provided the analysis of two additional batches stored under normal conditions at 25°C ± 2°C, 60 ± 5% relative humidity (RH) up to 36 months. The batches were analysed for appearance, optical rotation, related substances (l‐histidine), moisture content and zinc l‐carnosine measured according to the Japanese Pharmacopoeia's methods.

Specifications

The proposed specifications are those indicated in the 2022 opinion (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022). Owing to the lack of proper characterisation of the fraction of small particles, including nanoparticles, the Panel is not in the position to establish specifications.

History of use of the NF

According to the applicant, the NF is used outside the European Union (e.g. Japan, South Korea) as a medicinal product for gastric ulcers. The applicant also provided information that zinc l‐carnosine is registered in the U.S. as ‘New Dietary Ingredient’, in Canada as a ‘Natural Health Product’ and in Australia as a ‘Type 2 Simple Complementary Medicine Substance’.

Proposed uses and use levels and anticipated intake

Target population

The target population proposed by the applicant is individuals above the age of 12 years, and according to the applicant the NF ‘is not intended for use in pregnant or lactating women, adults with reduced liver functions and poorly nourished copper deficient adults’.

Proposed uses and use levels

The applicant intends to market the NF for use in food supplements, at a maximum use level of 112.5 mg per day, corresponding to approximately 25 mg of zinc and 87 mg of l‐carnosine.

Absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion (ADME)

Following the concerns raised in the previous opinion (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022) regarding the lack of appropriate data supporting quick dissolution of the NF in line with Section 2.3.2 of the Guidance on TR (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2021), the applicant provided a study on the dissolution rate of the NF in water 7 at pH 3 and a dissociation study performed at pH 2 using ultrafiltration, which included zinc oxide (ZnO) as a comparator.

The Panel noted that the studies provided were not performed according to the Guidance on TR (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2021). Ultrafiltration was not performed at the timepoints indicated in the guidance and the methodology used to measure the dissolved fraction in the filtrate was not validated for this purpose. The total concentration of Zn should have been determined and demonstration of the potential dissociation of the dissolved Zn into Zn ions and l‐carnosine using validated methodology should have been provided. In addition to pH 3, the dissociation rate of the NF at pH 5 should also have been investigated to cover consumers receiving the material in a matrix with high buffering potential (e.g. milk) (see Annex A of the Guidance on TR). Moreover, evidence that the comparator used (ZnO) was food‐grade (i.e. in compliance with the EU regulation on nutrient sources) should have been provided. Finally, the applicant did not provide documentation to demonstrate that the facility that performed the studies was qualified to conduct these analyses [e.g. Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) certificate, accreditation certificate].

Upon EFSA's request, the applicant provided a new study on the dissolution rate in water and dissociation at pH 3 and pH 5, using ZnO (food‐grade) as a comparator.

Dissolution of the NF at pH 5 was negligible, whereas at pH 3 ca. 80% dissolution of the NF was reported at t = 0. However, the study presented several limitations and the analytical methodology used did not appear sufficiently reliable. This was reflected by the large variability within the reported analytical results and by the fact that the sampling timepoints did not correspond to the actual time after which the analysis of suspended particles versus the dissolved fraction was expected to be completed (i.e. ultrafiltration time was not considered). It was also noted that centrifugal ultrafiltration was carried out longer than required to obtain the minimum volume for subsequent analysis, as appropriate for kinetic experiments such as dissolution rate testing.

Additionally, a report from the public literature (Furuta et al., 1995) suggests that, owing to its physicochemical properties, zinc l‐carnosine persists in the gastric environment in vivo and shows a mean residence time double that of soluble ZnSO4 or l‐carnosine.

The rapid dissolution reported in dissolution rate testing at pH 3 is not supported by the SEM imaging (see Section 3.2 and Appendix A), which, in addition to a majority of small particles, shows the presence of several large particles (up to 100 μm in size). These particles dominate the mass‐based distribution of the material and require longer times for dissolving as compared to the smaller particles (including nanoparticles). Therefore, the combined evidence does not support the reported dissolution value at t = 0.

As with the dissolution rate test, no evidence of analytical quality control to demonstrate measurements' accuracy and traceability was provided for the dissociation study. For both studies, the laboratory performing the measurements was not accredited and did not release original study reports.

Due to the limitations of the studies, information on the bioavailability of the NF cannot be obtained.

In addition, the Panel considers that the data available on dissolution are not sufficient to alleviate concerns related to the presence of small particles.

Nutritional information

The NF is proposed to be used as a source of zinc. The nutritional information of the NF cannot be assessed because of the limitations on the identity of the NF.

Toxicological information

The Panel notes that studies performed with the NF were not provided but reference was made to studies available in literature for subchronic and reproductive and developmental toxicity (Matsuda, Mera, et al., 1991; Matsuda, Nishi, et al., 1991; Yamaguchi et al., 1996), summarised in EFSA NDA Panel (2022).

In its previous submission, the applicant included genotoxicity data from the literature (Shibata et al., 1991), as well as an in vitro micronucleus assay performed with the NF (Unpublished, 2021).

Owing to the lack of a correct characterisation of the fraction of small particles, including nanoparticles, of the NF, as well as of their fate in gastrointestinal tract (GIT) conditions, the Panel considers that the provided toxicological information is not appropriate to assess the safety of the NF.

Additionally, the Panel notes that full study reports and documentation on compliance of the studies with the Organisation for Economic Co‐operation and Development (OECD) test guidelines or the principles of GLP (OECD, 1998) were not provided.

Human data

The human data provided (Kashimura et al., 1999; Mahmood et al., 2007; Tan et al., 2017; Watanabe et al., 2010) were already assessed by the EFSA NDA Panel in 2022, and deemed not relevant for the safety assessment of the NF.

Allergenicity

In 2022, the Panel assessed the allergenicity potential of the NF and considered that the likelihood of adverse allergenic reactions to the NF in the target population under the proposed conditions of use is low (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022).

DISCUSSION

The NF which is the subject of the application is a chelate complex, formed between Zn2+ and l‐carnosine. The material is a powder with a particulate nature and is insoluble in water at pH 7. The NF is produced by chemical synthesis and is proposed for use in food supplements as a source of zinc.

Following the concerns regarding the characterisation of the particle size distribution raised in the previously published opinion (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022), the new data submitted do not exclude the presence of a fraction of small particles, including nanoparticles, in the NF and do not support its rapid dissolution under normal gastric conditions at pH 3.

The bioavailability of zinc provided by the NF at the proposed use levels remains uncharacterised.

Owing to the lack of a correct characterisation of the fraction of small particles, including nanoparticles, in the NF, as well as of their fate in GIT conditions, the Panel considers that the provided toxicological information is not appropriate to assess the safety of the NF.

Furthermore, the applicant did not provide the full study reports for any of the published toxicity studies and no documentation on adherence to OECD guidelines and GLP compliance was provided.

CONCLUSIONS

The Panel concludes that the NF, zinc l‐carnosine, is not sufficiently characterised and that neither its bioavailability nor its safety can be established under the proposed conditions of use.

Limitations of the SEM analysis for the measurement of the number‐based constituent particle size distribution

The following limitations have been identified in the submitted SEM analytical data:

While information related to the Laboratory Sample and part of the Methodology section are reported according to the reporting template supplementing the Guidance on TR (Annex D), the method used for quantitative analysis and the results are not reported in line with the template and thus important metadata are missing (e.g. pixel size and lower limit of detection).

Even though the material appears to be well dispersed on the support, due to lack of high‐quality and high‐magnification images, it cannot be concluded with certainty that the measured particles are the constituent particles. In fact, the larger particles identified in the SEM images might actually be agglomerates.
