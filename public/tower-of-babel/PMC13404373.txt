Bibliometric Trends in Inflammasome‑Driven Pyroptosis and Cardiovascular Disease
Authors: Yupei Tan, Wei Wang, Rui Qiang, Hua Guo, Lixin Liang, Chen Wang, Shule Xu, Jidong Wang, Xinping Qiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and PurposeThis bibliometric study provides the first comprehensive synthesis of inflammasome‑driven pyroptosis research in cardiovascular disease (CVD), systematically mapping its evolution. Pyroptosis, an inflammatory form of programmed cell death triggered by inflammasome activation, plays a critical role in various CVDs, including hypertension, ischemia‑reperfusion injury (I/R injury), atherosclerosis, and heart failure (HF). Despite rapid growth of the literature, no bibliometric analysis has specifically focused on this area.Patients and MethodsData were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (1998–April 27, 2025). Bibliometric and visual analyses were performed using CiteSpace and VOSviewer to examine publication trends, country/region, funding agency, institution, author, journal, subject category, co‑cited reference, keyword co‑occurrence, and emerging hotspots.ResultsA total of 4,511 documents (2,918 original articles and 1,593 reviews) were included. China contributed 2,259 publications (50.1% of total) with 56,326 citations; the United States contributed 1,022 publications (22.7%) with 79,057 citations and the highest country‑level h‑index (147); and Italy ranked third with 290 publications (6.4%). Harvard University and its affiliated institutions led in both publication quantity and impact (h‑index, citations per article). Keyword co‑occurrence identified four clusters: pyroptosis mechanisms, NLRP3 inflammasome, signaling pathways, and CVDs. Recent bursts (2024‑2025) highlight “pyroptosis” and “ferroptosis” as emerging frontiers.ConclusionThis bibliometric study identifies NLRP3 as the central research focus in inflammasome‑driven pyroptosis research, with the strongest citation burst. The findings reveal a shift from basic mechanistic studies toward translational research, highlighting emerging priorities such as the crosstalk between pyroptosis and ferroptosis and the need for patient stratification in future clinical trials.

Introduction

Triggered by inflammasome activation, pyroptosis is a recently identified type of programmed cell death that differs from other forms such as apoptosis, necroptosis, and ferroptosis, and is also associated with autophagy.1,2 Initially observed in Shigella‑infected macrophages, pyroptosis was mistakenly classified as apoptosis because of similar morphological features.3 A comparable phenomenon was later reported in Salmonella‑infected macrophages.4 The term “pyroptosis” was introduced in 2000 to describe caspase‑1‑mediated inflammatory programmed cell death.5 Following the 2015 discovery of Gasdermin‑D (GSDMD) as a critical executor, pyroptosis has been redefined as gasdermin‑mediated programmed necrosis.6,7

Inflammasomes are cytoplasmic complexes that detect pathogen‑ or danger‑associated signals, leading to caspase‑1 activation.8,9 Active caspase‑1 cleaves GSDMD, generating an N‑terminal fragment (GSDMD‑NT) that forms membrane pores and induces pyroptotic cell death, accompanied by release of IL‑1β and IL‑18.6,7,10,11

Role of pyroptosis in major CVDs: Pyroptosis contributes to several CVDs, including atherosclerosis, myocardial infarction (MI), heart failure (HF), ischemia‑reperfusion injury (I/R injury), hypertension, and diabetic cardiomyopathy.12–18 In atherosclerosis, pyroptosis of endothelial cells and macrophages promotes plaque formation and instability.12 During MI and I/R injury, cardiomyocyte pyroptosis exacerbates tissue damage and inflammatory infiltration.13,15 In HF, persistent pyroptosis contributes to adverse cardiac remodeling and dysfunction.14 For hypertension, elevated salt activates NLRP3 inflammasome and induces pyroptosis in renal and vascular tissues, and central blockade of NLRP3 lowers blood pressure in salt‑sensitive rats.19 Thus, understanding pyroptosis in CVDs may reveal new therapeutic targets.

Bibliometric analysis offers a quantitative framework to map knowledge structures and emerging trends. This gap is urgent because the exponential growth of literature makes it difficult to identify key contributors, research hotspots, and future directions. Several bibliometric studies have examined pyroptosis in other diseases (eg, acute lung injury, pancreatic cancer);20–23 however, despite a rapidly growing body of literature, no bibliometric analysis has specifically focused on inflammasome‑driven pyroptosis in CVD.

Recent systematic reviews have summarized the roles and mechanisms of pyroptosis in CVDs, including a meta‑analysis on NLRP3 inflammasome in atherosclerosis24 and a review on pyroptosis in myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury,25 as well as a comprehensive overview.26 To complement these reviews, the present study applies a systematic bibliometric approach. Understanding the molecular mechanisms is essential, but a quantitative bibliometric analysis is required to map the overall research landscape.

Therefore, this study utilizes CiteSpace and VOSviewer to map the evolution and key themes in inflammasome‑driven pyroptosis research in CVD from 1998 to 2025, aiming to provide an objective knowledge map for researchers and funding agencies.

Methods

Search Strategies

Publications related to inflammasomes and pyroptosis in cardiovascular research were retrieved from the science citation index expanded database within the WoSCC27,28 (https://www.webofscience.com/wos/woscc/advanced-search). Using MeSH terms (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/mesh), we identified relevant keywords including “pyroptosis” and “inflammasome,” as well as variations such as “pyroptotic” and “pyroptosome.” The search strategy was as follows: #1 TS: (“inflammasome” OR “pyroptotic” OR “pyroptosis” OR “pyroptosome” OR “inflammatory apoptosis” OR “inflammatory apoptoses” OR “caspase 1 dependent cell death”); #2 TS: (“heart” OR “circulatory” OR “*cardi*”); #3 TS: #1 AND #2. “TS” denotes the topic search field. The asterisk (*) is a wildcard representing any sequence of characters (including none); thus “cardi*” matches terms such as “cardiac” and “cardiopathy”.29 The time span covered from the inception of the database to April 27, 2025. The search strategy was independently tested by two authors (YT and RQ). A recall test using 10 landmark papers in the field confirmed 100% retrieval of these papers. Because WoSCC does not directly support MeSH term searching, we used an equivalent approach combining topic fields with wildcards. Only publications in English were included, and the document types were restricted to original articles and reviews. A total of 4,511 publications were included in the final analysis. The literature selection process, which followed the PRISMA 2020 guidelines where applicable (adhering to items related to literature identification, screening, and data extraction, but not risk of bias assessment or meta‑analysis), is illustrated in the PRISMA 2020 flow diagram (Figure 1). Data Collection and Analysis

Of the 4,511 documents meeting the search criteria, 64.69% (2,918) were original articles, while 35.31% (1,593) were reviews. Comprehensive data records from the WoSCC, including annual publication counts, numbers of countries/regions, funding agencies, institutions, authors, journals, subject categories, co-cited references, Hirsch index (H-index), average citations per item (ACI), 2024 impact factors (IF), 2024 Journal Citation Reports (JCR) partitions, citations, and total times cited, were independently extracted by two authors, YT and RQ. In this study, H-index values were obtained at multiple levels: (1) at the country/region, institution, and journal levels using the “Analyze Results” function of WoSCC; (2) at the author level (presented in Table 1) using the “Create Citation Report” function for each author. The H-index is defined as the number of publications (h) that have received at least h citations. Any discrepancies were resolved through consensus. Big data was analyzed and visualized with the help of VOSviewer (v1.6.20) and CiteSpace (v6.4.R1), and Microsoft Excel 2019. Prior to analysis, data refinement included reclassifying source locations; for example, publications from Taiwan were categorized under China,30 while those from England, Scotland, Northern Ireland, and Wales were categorized as the United Kingdom.31 Additionally, synonyms such as “IL-1,” “interleukin-1,” and “interleukin-1 receptor antagonist” were consolidated in the keyword evolution analysis.

VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) was used for bibliometric mapping.32,33 In this study, we performed: (1) co‑authorship analysis of countries/regions (Figure 2D) and authors (Figure 3A); (2) co‑citation analysis of cited authors (Figure 3B) and cited journals (Figure 4A); and (3) co‑occurrence analysis of keywords (Figures 5A and B). The key parameters were as follows. For keyword co‑occurrence, the minimum occurrence threshold was set to 50, counting method was “full counting”, normalization method was “association strength”, and clustering resolution was 1.0. For co‑authorship analysis, the minimum number of publications per country/region was 10, and per author was 10. For co‑citation analysis of journals, the minimum number of co‑citations was set to 1,000. For co‑citation analysis of authors, the threshold was set to include the 30 most frequently cited authors (as shown in Figure 3B). Visualization maps included network views and an overlay view based on average appearance year (AAY). CiteSpace (version 6.4.R1), developed by professor Chaomei Chen at Drexel university, is a Java-based software tool designed for visual citation analysis.34 Using this software, we analyzed institutions, journals, subject categories, and keywords to generate comprehensive visualization maps. Additionally, CiteSpace enabled us to perform journal dual-map overlay analysis and identify keywords exhibiting strong citation bursts within the field of cardiovascular inflammasome and pyroptosis research. The parameters were configured as follows: time span from 2004 to 2025, with 1‑year time slices; node types included institutions, journals, subject categories, and keywords; selection criteria based on the g‑index with k=25. To enhance network interpretability and consistency with the visualizations presented (Figures 6D, 7B and 8), we applied the following additional settings: pruning method= “Pathfinder + Pruning sliced networks”, clustering algorithm=LLR (log‑likelihood ratio), and burst detection with γ= 0.8. The modularity Q and mean silhouette values were used to evaluate cluster quality (as shown in Figure 7B). All other settings were maintained at default values. Several landmark papers have shaped the field, including those by Martinon, Schroder, and others, with high co‑citation counts (see Table 5 for details). In a 2011 study by Eleonora Mezzaroma et al, inflammasomes were identified in experimental AMI mouse models, suggesting their potential therapeutic significance in limiting cell death and preventing HF following AMI.42 Additionally, Masanori Kawaguchi et al (2011) demonstrated that inflammasomes are essential in the pathophysiology of myocardial I/R injury. Their studies using mice lacking ASC and caspase-1 showed that cardiac fibroblasts mediated inflammatory response to I/R injury through the inflammasome pathway, emphasizing inflammasome as potential therapeutic targets for preventing myocardial I/R injury.53

In the overview of 2019, Karen V. Swanson et al explored the core molecules, cells and biochemical mechanisms for the activation and regulation of NLRP3 inflammasome. They stressed that this growing understanding is paving the way for potential therapies targeting NLRP3.55 Previously, Øystein Sandanger et al (2013) confirmed that the expression of NLRP3 inflammasome (a nucleotide-binding domain receptor that can be oligomerized) increased in cardiac fibroblasts and participated in myocardial I/R injury. Their findings were supported by in vitro experiments (using adult C57BL/6 mice and Wistar rats) and in vivo adult mouse cell experiments.55 Peter Duewell et al (2010) found that crystalline cholesterol, as an internal dangerous signal, can cause early inflammation when deposited in arteries or other tissues. Their research showed that cholesterol crystals can activate NLRP3 inflammasome in phagocytes in vitro, thereby damaging phagocytic lysosomes. This discovery provided a new perspective on the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis and highlights emerging molecular targets for treatment interventions for a variety of related diseases.49

In another highly cited paper published in the journal Nature (total citations: 4293), Rongbin Zhou et al (2011) confirmed that when mitophagy or autophagy is damaged, the mitochondria of reactive oxygen species (ROS) damage accumulates, thus triggering the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome. After activation, NLRP3 and its connecting protein ASC, which were originally located in the endoplasmic reticulum, will migrate to the perinuclear space and aggregate with endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondrial organelles. This study clarified the common association between mitochondrial damage and inflammatory diseases.51 Additionally, another Nature article detailed the canonical and non-canonical pyroptosis pathways induced by inflammasomes, mediated by inflammatory caspases including caspase-1, --4, -5, and -11.6

Keyword co-occurrence analysis is commonly utilized to accurately depict the knowledge structure and thematic evolution within a specific research domain.41 Using VOSviewer software, we constructed and visualized a keyword co-occurrence network based on 13,193 extracted keywords from 4,511 articles, after merging synonyms and manually filtering out generic terms. For visualization, only keywords with at least 50 simultaneous occurrences were included.

As shown in the density visualization (Figure 5A), 150 keywords met this threshold. The keywords most commonly used: “NLRP3 inflammasome,” “inflammation,” “activation,” “pyroptosis,” and “oxidative stress,” among others. Many of these keywords also appear prominently in highly co-cited references. Additionally, VOSviewer’s clustering function separated the network into multiple clusters, with keywords in the same cluster sharing strong correlations and represented by the same color.41,43

Keyword outbreak refers to keywords with a significant frequency surge in a specific period of time, which is widely regarded as a key indicator for identifying research trends and emerging topics.57,58 We used CiteSpace to analyze the significant outbreak keywords associated with inflammasome and pyroptosis between 1998 and 2025. Notably, “pyroptosis” (2024–2025, strength 10.02) and “ferroptosis” (2024–2025, strength 7.13) have seen increased citation frequency over the past two years, indicating that these subjects are attracting significant interest and are emerging as current research trends in CVDs. The burst strength of “NLRP3 inflammasome” is nearly four times that of “pyroptosis” and more than five times that of “ferroptosis”, indicating that NLRP3 has been a much more intense research focus historically. However, the recent emergence of “pyroptosis” and “ferroptosis” as bursts (both starting in 2024) suggests a shift toward broader cell death mechanisms. The longer burst duration of NLRP3 (2009–2019) reflects its established role, while shorter but recent bursts signal emerging frontiers. The recent burst of “pyroptosis” reflects the growing recognition of gasdermin‑mediated inflammatory cell death as a central mechanism in CVDs, particularly after the discovery of GSDMD as the executioner of pyroptosis. The concurrent burst of “ferroptosis”—an iron‑dependent, non‑apoptotic cell death pathway driven by lipid peroxidation—suggests an emerging interest in the crosstalk between different regulated cell death modalities. Future studies may explore whether pyroptosis and ferroptosis synergistically contribute to myocardial injury and whether combined inhibition offers therapeutic advantages.

Discussion

By analyzing prominent and influential literature, this paper enhances understanding of key research findings on inflammasome and pyroptosis in the context of CVDs.

Implications for Cardiovascular Researchers

The bibliometric findings of this study offer several practical implications for researchers in the cardiovascular field. First, the NLRP3 inflammasome‑pyroptosis pathway has emerged as a central mechanism in CVD pathogenesis, and targeting this pathway with small‑molecule inhibitors (eg, MCC950, CY‑09, canakinumab) holds therapeutic potential.24,59 Second, keyword clustering reveals that the crosstalk between pyroptosis and other programmed cell death pathways—particularly autophagy and ferroptosis—is an emerging research hotspot. Future studies should investigate the regulatory networks among these cell death modalities.60–62 Third, international collaboration (especially between China‑USA and China‑Germany) has accelerated progress in this field, and researchers are encouraged to build cross‑border teams to leverage complementary expertise.63

Comparison of Research Output and Impact Between China and the USA

Although China leads in publication volume, its average citations per article is substantially lower than that of the United States (as shown in Table 2). This disparity may be attributed to several factors: (1) language barrier—non‑English papers are often less cited internationally; (2) citation habits—domestic journals and domestic citations may dominate Chinese publications; (3) differences in research evaluation systems—China’s emphasis on publication quantity may lead to a higher proportion of incremental studies, whereas the US system traditionally prioritizes novelty and impact. These factors are not mutually exclusive and warrant further investigation.

Biological Interpretation of Keyword Clusters (Figure 5A)

The four clusters identified in Figure 5A correspond to distinct biological themes in inflammasome‑mediated pyroptosis research: Red cluster (pyroptosis mechanisms): Focuses on GSDMD pore formation, caspase‑1 activation, and cell death execution; Blue cluster (NLRP3 inflammasome): Centers on IL‑1β/IL‑18 maturation and release, forming a pro‑inflammatory signaling network; Yellow cluster (signaling pathways): Covers canonical and non‑canonical inflammasome pathways, including crosstalk with other signaling cascades; Green cluster (CVDs): Links pyroptosis to atherosclerosis, myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury, and heart failure, highlighting specific disease targets. These clusters are discussed in detail in the following subsections.

The Cluster 1: The Study of the Inflammasomes Involving in Pyroptosis

Inflammasomes are widely expressed in monocytes and macrophages. Among these, the NLRP1, NLRP3, NLRC4, and AIM2 inflammasomes are primary focuses in pyroptosis research (Figure 10).9 These inflammasomes assemble with ASC and caspase-1 to form active complexes, triggering downstream inflammatory responses.9 For further structural details, readers are referred to previous reviews. The Cluster 2: The Extensively Investigated NLRP3 Inflammasome

Within innate immunity, the NLRP3 inflammasome acts as a crucial cellular mediator that initiates inflammatory responses and triggers gasdermin D-mediated pyroptosis, while enhancing the production and secretion of proinflammatory cytokines like IL-1β and IL-18.64 The NLRP3 inflammasome is composed of three main components: the NLRP3 innate immune receptor, the ASC adaptor protein, and pro-caspase-1 (Figure 11).9 Activation occurs in two distinct steps: (1) “priming” (recognition of pathogen-associated molecular components) and (2) “activation” or “sensing” (often triggered by extracellular ATP).65 Upon activation, NLRP3 leads to caspase-1 cleavage and IL-1β maturation, facilitating downstream inflammatory signaling. The Cluster 3: The Canonical Inflammasome-Induced Pyroptosis Pathway

The activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, which leads to canonical pyroptosis, occurs in two stages: initiation and assembly (Figure 12). Prior to activation, Toll-like receptor (TLR) agonists trigger signaling cascades mainly through the NF-κB pathway, enhancing the transcription of pro-inflammatory mediators such as pro-IL-1β. During the initiation stage, a large molecular complex forms in reaction to PAMPs or DAMPs, leading to the activation of inflammatory caspases and processing of pro-IL-1β. GSDMD is a critical executor: caspase-1 cleaves GSDMD, releasing its N-terminal domain, which forms pores in the cell membrane.6,10 Activated caspase-1 also converts IL-1β and IL-18 precursors into mature cytokines. These pores allow the release of inflammatory mediators, leading to cell swelling and death. The cleavage of GSDMD by caspase-1 is a key component of the canonical pyroptotic pathway. However, key questions remain, such as the specific function of the gasdermin family in subsequent stages of pyroptosis. The Cluster 4: The Non-Canonical Inflammasome-Induced Pyroptosis Pathway

Unlike the canonical pathway, caspases 4 and 5 (in humans) and caspase-11 (in mice) are key mediators of the non-canonical pathway (Figure 13). Intracellular LPS can activate this pathway independently of TLR4.7,66 Activated caspases 4/5/11 cleave GSDMD, generating the N-terminal fragment (GSDMD-NT), which induces pyroptosis by forming membrane pores, similar to the canonical pathway. Activation of the non-canonical inflammasome can also subsequently trigger the canonical inflammasome and caspase-1, resulting in the maturation and release of proinflammatory cytokines. Recent discoveries have further elucidated cross‑regulation between different cell death pathways (eg, GSDME-mediated pyroptosis and Pannexin‑1 activation), but these details are beyond the scope of this bibliometric analysis. Role in Atherosclerosis

Atherosclerosis is a pathological process characterized by: (a) endothelial damage; (b) accumulation of lipoproteins on the vessel walls; (c) adhesion, migration, and transformation of mononuclear cells into macrophages, eventually forming foam cells; and (d) proliferation of smooth muscle cells.68 Vascular endothelial damage marks the beginning of atherosclerosis, in which activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in endothelial cells plays a crucial role.67,69 For example, nicotine increases NLRP3 and ASC expression, triggering inflammasome activation and endothelial pyroptosis.70,71 Exposure to fine particles (PM2.5) increases serum IL‑1β and IL‑18 levels, activates NLRP3 inflammasome in the aorta, and aggravates atherosclerotic plaque formation.67,69,72 Environmental pollutants such as acrylaldehyde and cadmium also cause endothelial cell damage through NLRP3 inflammasome pathways.69 Endothelial dysfunction further promotes mononuclear cell adhesion, differentiation into macrophages, lipoprotein uptake, and foam cell transformation via the NLRP3 pathway.49,67 Microorganisms and neutrophils can influence atherosclerosis progression by regulating these processes.67,69 A recent systematic review and meta‑analysis confirmed the role of NLRP3 inflammasome in atherosclerosis.24

Role in Myocardial I/R Injury

With the progression of atherosclerosis, cardiac I/R injury involves NLRP3 inflammasome activation in multiple cell types, including fibroblasts, cardiomyocytes, endothelial cells, and macrophages.67,73,74 The initial activation of NLRP3 inflammasome in cardiac tissue occurs mainly in fibroblasts, which play a crucial role in the development of myocardial I/R injury.53,75 Hypoxia/reoxygenation significantly increases NLRP3 inflammasome activity and IL‑1β levels in cardiac fibroblasts, while ROS generation and K⁺ efflux activate the NLRP3 inflammasome, exacerbating I/R injury.55,76 Activated NLRP3 promotes neutrophil recruitment and aggravates tissue damage.67,69 The therapeutic implications of pyroptosis in I/R injury have been recently summarized.25

Role in HF

HF, the terminal stage of many CVDs, is closely associated with NLRP3 inflammasome activation.26,64,67 Under stress conditions such as glucose/fructose overload, LPS, or mechanical pressure, NLRP3 is mainly activated in fibroblasts and cardiomyocytes.67,69 Coxsackievirus B3‑induced myocarditis activates NLRP3 in macrophages and myocardial cells.67,69 Blocking the inflammasome pathway has potential clinical significance for delaying the progression of CVDs.26,64 Many NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors have shown efficacy in CVD‑related animal models and cell culture studies.50,64 However, it remains to be clarified whether these inhibitors can effectively influence the occurrence and development of human CVDs. Small‑scale clinical trials may help translate these findings into clinical applications. Further research to discover new therapeutic targets is crucial for developing novel cardiovascular treatments.

Species Differences and Translational Risks

It is important to note that most mechanistic studies on inflammasome‑mediated pyroptosis have been conducted in mouse models, which may not fully recapitulate human pathophysiology. Species differences in inflammasome components (eg, caspase‑11 in mice vs caspase‑4/5 in humans)77,78 and immune responses could affect translational outcomes. Therefore, preclinical findings should be validated in human‑relevant systems (eg, human iPSC‑derived cardiomyocytes or organoids) before clinical application. The failure of some NLRP3 inhibitors in clinical trials to demonstrate consistent cardiovascular benefits underscores the need for caution.

Conflicting Clinical Trial Evidence

While the CANTOS trial showed a reduction in recurrent cardiovascular events with canakinumab,50 other NLRP3 inhibitors (eg, MCC950) have faced safety concerns or failed to meet primary endpoints in clinical trials.79 These mixed results highlight the gap between preclinical promise and clinical reality, emphasizing the need for better patient stratification, biomarker development, and longer‑term safety studies.

Factors Contributing to the Publication Surge (2020–2025)

The sharp increase in publications after 2020 can be attributed to several factors. First, the COVID‑19 pandemic: SARS‑CoV‑2 infection activates the NLRP3 inflammasome and induces pyroptosis,80,81 leading many researchers to pivot to inflammation‑related studies, which partially explains the post‑2020 surge. Second, funding policy: During this period, major funding agencies (eg, the NSFC in China and the NIH in the USA) substantially increased their support for cross‑disciplinary research in inflammation, immunity, and cardiovascular diseases. Third, technological breakthroughs: The discovery of key pyroptosis execution molecules such as GSDMD and GSDME after 2019 accelerated translational research, triggering a wave of publications.6,82 These factors collectively contributed to the exponential growth observed in our dataset.

Additional Limitations and Strategic Recommendations

Beyond the limitations listed in the Strengths and Limitations section, we acknowledge that citation counts are influenced by factors such as journal prestige, self‑citation practices, publication language, and research networks, rather than solely by scientific merit.83 Therefore, highly cited papers or influential authors should not be equated with methodological rigor. To further accelerate progress and diversify the research landscape, we recommend fostering international collaborations that include emerging economies. Countries such as India, Turkey, and Brazil show rapidly growing publication activity (Figure 2D).84 Encouraging high‑quality publications from these regions through training programs, targeted funding, and equitable partnerships will help address region‑specific CVD burdens and reduce global disparities in cardiovascular research. Future bibliometric studies could further stratify the analysis by document type (original articles vs reviews) and by specific CVD subtypes (eg, hypertension, atherosclerosis, myocardial infarction) to uncover distinct research trends and publication patterns.

Summary and Outlook

The knowledge structure of inflammasome‑mediated pyroptosis in CVD is summarized in Figures 12–14. These figures collectively illustrate the core pathways (canonical and non‑canonical), the central role of NLRP3, and the downstream pathological consequences in various CVDs. Briefly, pyroptosis is characterized by inflammation and activated by the activation of the inflammasome. It has been regarded as the core mechanism of the progression of a variety of CVDs, including atherosclerosis, I/R injury, HF, myocardial infarction and hypertension. This unique type of cell death is mainly driven by inflammatory cysteine (especially caspase-1), which is characterized by cell membrane rupture, release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and escalation of the inflammatory process. Among different inflammatory bodies, NLRP3 inflammasome are the most deeply studied in CVDs. It plays a central role in responding to a variety of stress signals such as PAMPs and DAMPs to trigger pyroptosis.

Recent literature measurement analysis shows that the research on inflammation and pyroptosis has increased significantly, among which China and the United States are leading in terms of the influence and number of papers. In recent years, with the promotion of famous institutions such as Harvard University and the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences, this field has experienced exponential growth. Important funding institutions, including the national NSFC and the US NIH, have played a key role in supporting this constantly evolving field of research.

To provide context, we compared the growth trend observed in our field with published bibliometric studies on pyroptosis in other disease areas.20–23,85 Those studies consistently report exponential growth in pyroptosis‑related publications over the past decade,20,23 which is similar to the trend observed in our cardiovascular‑focused dataset. This consistency suggests that the increasing interest in inflammasome‑mediated pyroptosis is not limited to cardiovascular research but reflects a broader scientific trend. Nevertheless, the rising proportion of cardiovascular‑related papers within the pyroptosis literature (as indicated by our data) highlights the growing recognition of this mechanism in CVD pathogenesis.86

Using advanced literature measurement tools such as VOSviewer and CiteSpace, researchers found the evolutionary trend that emphasized the key role of NLRP3 inflammasome in heart disease. A comprehensive keyword co-occurrence analysis shows that the research focus is shifting to clarifying the canonical and non-canonical pathways of inflammasome-induced pyroptosis, and exploring potential treatment strategies aimed at regulating these pathways.

Looking to the future, with the discovery of new molecular targets, especially the targets in the NLRP3 inflammasome signal cascading, the research field of inflammasome and pyroptosis in CVDs is expected to achieve further development. In diseases such as myocardial infarction, HF and atherosclerosis, pyroptosis has attracted more and more attention, which shows that the targeted inflammasome-mediated cell death pathway has great therapeutic potential. In addition, emerging studies on ferroptosis (a unique form of programmed cell death) also show that synergistic strategies can be used to treat both pyroptosis and ferroptosis.

It is increasingly recognized that inflammasomes and pyroptosis are key factors in the pathogenesis of CVDs, which highlights the need for in-depth study of their mechanisms. It is also important to transform the results of preclinical research into clinical trials to develop targeted therapies for CVDs and although most studies remain preclinical, several NLRP3 inhibitors have already entered clinical trials (eg, canakinumab in the CANTOS trial50). We anticipate translation from target discovery to clinical application for myocardial infarction, heart failure, and atherosclerosis within the next 5‑10 years.

Future research directions and clinical implications: Based on our bibliometric findings, we propose the following specific directions: (1) Investigate the regulatory crosstalk between pyroptosis and other programmed cell death pathways, particularly autophagy and ferroptosis, to uncover potential synergistic mechanisms. (2) Conduct small‑scale clinical trials to evaluate the safety and efficacy of NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors (eg, MCC950, canakinumab) in patients with myocardial infarction, heart failure, or atherosclerosis.87 (3) Strengthen international collaborations, especially between China‑USA and China‑Germany, to accelerate data sharing and multi‑center studies. Strengthening international collaborations, as evidenced by robust global partnerships, will be essential to accelerate progress in understanding and treating CVDs driven by inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis.

Strengths and Limitations

In this study, there are still some inherent limitations, which deserve additional consideration when interpreting these results. (1) Firstly, this research data were only from WoSCC database, thus omitting other large authoritative databases such as PubMed, Embase, etc. This is owing to the limitations of current softwares, and direct merging of two or more databases is laborious. (2) Secondly, topic searching was used, TS: (“inflammasome” OR “pyroptotic” OR “pyroptosis” OR “pyroptosome” OR “inflammatory apoptosis” OR “inflammatory apoptoses” OR “caspase 1 dependent cell death”) AND TS: (“heart” OR “circulatory” OR “*cardi*”), yet each article is confined to a range of 3 to 10 keywords, there may be incomplete keyword extraction, which affects the accuracy of bibliometric analysis. (3) Thirdly, due to the continuous updating of the WOSCC database, there may be newly published high-quality research results whose significance may have been overlooked due to their low frequency of citations. (4) Moreover, owing to inherent limitations of bibliometric methods, only English‑language articles and reviews were included, while non‑English articles, meeting abstracts, Early Access papers, editorial materials, letters, and other document types were excluded, which may lead to source bias. In particular, the exclusive reliance on a single database (WoSCC) and the language restriction may affect the comprehensiveness of the literature coverage. (5) Overall, although there are some deficiencies, this work could still represent the overall trend of the field and help scholars quickly understand the development frontier of “pyroptosis initiated by inflammasome in cardiovascular field”. (6) No inferential statistical analyses (eg, t‑tests, ANOVA, regression) were performed because bibliometric data are not derived from random samples and do not meet the assumptions of parametric tests. The descriptive and comparative analyses presented here are appropriate for mapping research trends and patterns. Results should be interpreted as trend descriptions rather than causal inferences. Given the descriptive nature of bibliometric analysis, a formal risk‑of‑bias assessment of individual studies (as required by systematic reviews) was not performed. However, we implemented several measures to minimize bias during data extraction and analysis. First, the search strategy was independently tested by two authors to ensure recall of landmark papers. Second, literature screening and data extraction were performed independently by two authors (YT and RQ), with discrepancies resolved by consensus. Third, we used two distinct software tools (CiteSpace and VOSviewer) to cross‑validate clustering results. We also acknowledge inherent limitations of bibliometric methods that may affect result interpretation: (a) citation bias (highly cited papers are overrepresented, while newly published high‑quality papers may have low citation counts); (b) time lag bias (recent publications have less time to accumulate citations); and (c) database coverage bias (reliance on WoSCC excludes some regional or non‑indexed journals). These biases are considered when interpreting the findings.

Conclusion

This bibliometric study systematically maps the evolution of inflammasome‑driven pyroptosis research in CVD. The key bibliometric findings are as follows:

Publication Trends and Global Collaboration

Research output has grown exponentially over the past two decades. China leads in publication volume, while the United States dominates in citation impact (h‑index and citations per article). International collaboration networks are centered on the USA, China, and Germany, with Harvard University and its affiliates playing a leading role.

Core Research Focus

Keyword co‑occurrence analysis identifies the NLRP3 inflammasome as the central research topic, with the highest citation burst strength and duration (2009–2019).

Emerging Hotspots

Recent keyword bursts (2024–2025) highlight “pyroptosis” and “ferroptosis” as emerging frontiers, indicating a shift from basic mechanism exploration toward translational research.

Thematic Structure

Four major research clusters are identified: pyroptosis mechanisms, NLRP3 inflammasome, signaling pathways (canonical/non‑canonical), and CVDs (atherosclerosis, myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury, HF).

Unresolved Key Scientific Questions for Future Research (Derived from Bibliometric Gaps)

1. How do pyroptosis and ferroptosis interact in the pathogenesis of specific CVDs? 2. Can the regulatory crosstalk between multiple cell death pathways (pyroptosis, autophagy, ferroptosis) be therapeutically targeted? 3. What are the translational barriers between animal models and human patients that limit the clinical application of NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors?

Take‑home Messages for Researchers

For basic researchers: Focus on the crosstalk between pyroptosis, autophagy, and ferroptosis as an emerging mechanistic network. For clinical researchers: Prioritize patient stratification and biomarker development in future trials of NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors. For funding agencies: Support interdisciplinary projects at the intersection of inflammation, immunity, and cardiovascular research to accelerate progress.

Based on these bibliometric findings, small‑scale clinical trials are warranted to evaluate NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors (eg, MCC950, canakinumab) in patients with myocardial infarction, HF, or atherosclerosis. The crosstalk between pyroptosis and ferroptosis, identified as an emerging hotspot, warrants priority investigation.

This bibliometric study maps the evolution of pyroptosis and inflammasome research in CVD, highlighting NLRP3 as a central focus and identifying future directions in translational and therapeutic research.
