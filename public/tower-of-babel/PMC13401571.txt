German translation performance of ChatGPT-4o for laser tattoo removal patient instructions
Authors: Mounika Vattigunta, Sarah Antonevich, Jonas Klausmann, Lorenzo Belotti, Mascha Korsch, Fabian Riske, Ryan Scheinkman, Keyvan Nouri
Published: 2026

To The Editor,

Artificial Intelligence (AI) shows great potential in the modern medical landscape as it aims to simplify medical instructions and encourage patients to make more informed clinical decisions [1]. While AI can be a powerful tool to aid patients in translating medical information, it is necessary to confirm the accuracy of accessible AI models, such as ChatGPT, to ensure patients are adhering to proper medical instruction. We assessed the translational accuracy of ChatGPT for laser tattoo removal patient instructions. ChatGPT was selected due to its user-friendly interface, free cost, and broad accessibility. Prior studies have investigated the translational capabilities of popular AI models for Spanish translation [2]. Our goal was to determine the translational accuracy of ChatGPT to assess if procedural instructions obtained are suitable for German-speaking patients.

Fourteen laser tattoo removal patient instructional documents were sourced from Google from a mixture of private practice and academic sources using the search phrase “laser tattoo removal patient instructions.” The documents were uploaded into ChatGPT-4o as PDF files and translated using the prompt “Translate the attached file into German.” The translated files were output as word documents. Two native German speakers (J.K. & L.B.) evaluated the instruction sets analyzing the number of mistranslated sentences (the meaning of the sentence was lost due to sufficient number of words mistranslated) and number of mistranslated words (word has lost its original meaning). Both evaluators have no prior experience with laser tattoo removal to truly represent a “blank slate” patient perspective.

Sentence-level and word-level accuracy were calculated. The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) for the sentence and word accuracy was also computed to assess inter-rater reliability.

Our findings demonstrate moderate reliability (Sentence Accuracy ICC = 0.70 and Word Accuracy ICC = 0.59) between translators. The average sentence accuracy and word accuracy was found to be 97.9% and 99.6%, respectively for both evaluators (Tables 1 and 2). For one sentence, ChatGPT translated the phrase “keep the area exposed after the first 3 days to “den Bereich nach den ersten 3 Tagen der Behandlung freizulegen,” which was interpreted as “to make the area free after the first three days of treatment by one translator, potentially giving misconceived instructions to readers.

Medical instructions require high accuracy to ensure proper post-procedural recovery, where common problems are likely to be avoided with proper post-operative care [3]. It is important to use these accessible AI models with caution, as their role in medical decisions can influence patient outcomes. Using medically trained, in-person, professional translators has been shown to provide greatest satisfaction and best communication for patients compared with other translational methods [4]. This study has several limitations: a small sample size of documents which may not properly reflect the range of translational challenges present in laser tattoo removal instructions, moderate inter-rater reliability which may affect reproducibility, the quality of source documents was not initially assessed, and only one AI model was used. Moderate inter-rater reliability may be due to differences in knowledge in medical terminology or variations in reginal dialect, both affecting rater’s threshold for correctness.

In conclusion, while the use of AI models provides fast, easy translation of medical documents, it is important to consider the possible downsides, potentially leading to worse outcomes and longer recovery times. Patients and medical professionals should use AI models as a tool to aid in patient care while also incorporating established medical professionals to help with translation of post-procedural instructions and care.
