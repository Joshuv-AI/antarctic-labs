Semaglutide for prevention of type 2 diabetes in women with postpartum prediabetes after gestational diabetes: protocol for a Belgian multicentre double-blind randomised placebo-controlled trial
Authors: Yana Vanlaer, Nina Embo, Niels Bochanen, Nancy Van Wilder, Gertjan Vereecke, Katrien Wierckx, Ann Verhaegen, Peter Coremans, Jean-Christophe Philips, Maria Lytrivi, Philippe Oriot, Sara Vandewalle, Joke Cuypers, Barbara Deconinck, Annouschka Laenen, Chantal Mathieu, Katrien Benhalima
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionWomen with postpartum pre-diabetes following gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) are at particularly high risk of developing type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). While the implementation of effective lifestyle interventions in the early postpartum period is often difficult and has in general limited efficacy to prevent T2DM after GDM, effective preventive strategies, including medical therapies, are needed in this population. The ‘semaglutide for the treatment of pre-diabetes in women with prior gestational diabetes (SERENA) study’ aims to evaluate the efficacy, safety and cost-effectiveness of semaglutide in preventing progression to T2DM during the early postpartum period.Methods and analysisSERENA is a Belgian multicentre, double-blind randomised controlled trial conducted in 13 hospitals. A total of 252 women with a recent history of GDM and postpartum pre-diabetes will be randomised in a 1:1 ratio to receive once-weekly subcutaneous injections of semaglutide 1 mg or placebo in addition to standardised lifestyle intervention. Randomisation is stratified by body mass index at early postpartum assessment (<25, 25–29.9 and ≥30 kg/m²). Participants will be treated for up to 160 weeks (3 years), with a total follow-up period of up to 184 weeks (3 years and 6 months). Study visits are scheduled every 6 months and include blood sampling, clinical examinations and completion of self-administered questionnaires. The primary outcome is the development of T2DM based on the American Diabetes Association criteria. Secondary outcomes include need for diabetes rescue therapy, regression to normoglycaemia, weight loss, insulin sensitivity, beta-cell function, cardiometabolic risk profile, patient-reported outcomes and cost-effectiveness.Ethics and disseminationThe study was approved by the Federal Agency for Medicines and Health Products and the relevant Ethics Committees (2022–502082-22-00) (S66967). Recruitment started in September 2023. Study results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and presentations at scientific conferences. Individual participant data will be made available on reasonable request after study completion, database lock, unblinding and publication of the primary results, subject to applicable ethical, legal and data protection requirements.Trial registration numberNCT05569772.

Introduction

Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is a very prevalent complication affecting about 14% of pregnancies and defined as hyperglycaemia first detected during pregnancy, provided that overt diabetes has been excluded.1 GDM significantly increases the risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes, including large-for-gestational-age infants (LGA), caesarean deliveries, pre-eclampsia and neonatal hypoglycaemia.2 Therefore, effective screening and timely treatment of GDM are essential to reduce maternal and neonatal complications.3 4 Current guidelines recommend in general universal screening using the one-step approach according to the WHO 2013 diagnostic criteria, based on the International Association of the Diabetes and Pregnancy Study Groups (IADPSG) thresholds.5 6 Traditionally, screening is performed between 24 and 28 weeks of gestation; however, increasing evidence supports the potential value of early GDM screening before 20 weeks of gestation in high-risk populations.7 In Belgium, a strategy of universal screening is widely applied, with early screening recommended in women with predefined risk factors such as obesity and a previous history of GDM.8 Initial management consists of lifestyle interventions, including dietary modification, glucose monitoring and physical activity, with pharmacological treatment, most commonly insulin, initiated when glycaemic targets are not achieved.9

The incidence of GDM is increasing, along with rates of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and obesity. While glucose levels often normalise after delivery, women with GDM are at a 7 to 10-fold increased risk of developing T2DM, with approximately 30%–50% developing the condition within 10 years.10–13 Women with persistent pre-diabetes, such as impaired fasting glycaemia or impaired glucose tolerance in the early postpartum period, are particularly at high risk, with up to 50% progressing to T2DM within 5 years.14 Implementing effective interventions postpartum to prevent T2DM after GDM could, therefore, significantly impact population health.

Evidence shows that women with a prior history of GDM progress to T2DM more rapidly than women without GDM, even at similar levels of dysglycaemia.15 Data from the Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) demonstrated a higher cumulative incidence of T2DM in women with previous GDM compared with those without, suggesting that standard lifestyle interventions may be less effective in this high-risk group.16 17 Moreover, the intensive nature of the DPP lifestyle programme limits its feasibility in routine postpartum care, and several postpartum lifestyle interventions have shown modest metabolic effects, likely due to low adherence and limited behavioural change.16 17

A Belgian study indicates that a substantial proportion of women with recent GDM already have pre-diabetes within 3 months postpartum, highlighting the importance of early testing via an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT), as fasting glucose and HbA1c alone fail to detect the majority with pre-diabetes.18 Women with GDM face also increased long-term risks of metabolic syndrome and cardiovascular disease, while their offspring are more likely to develop obesity and T2DM.19 Despite this, attendance at postpartum screening and prevention programmes remains poor.20

Given the limited effectiveness of lifestyle measures and metformin in women with recent GDM, additional therapeutic strategies are needed, particularly for those with early postpartum pre-diabetes.21 Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists offer multiple metabolic benefits, including improved glucose regulation, weight loss and cardiovascular risk reduction in patients with T2DM.22–24 However, evidence for their role after GDM is currently restricted to small, short-term trials that were not designed to assess diabetes prevention.25 26

To address these gaps, the semaglutide for the treatment of pre-diabetes in women with prior gestational diabetes (SERENA) study was designed as the first large multicentre, randomised, placebo-controlled trial (RCT) to evaluate the effect of once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide (a GLP-1 receptor agonist with evidence suggesting potential benefits on glucose regulation, weight reduction and cardiometabolic risk factors)22–24 in women with early postpartum pre-diabetes following a recent history of GDM. By combining pharmacological therapy with a structured lifestyle intervention, the SERENA study adopts a pragmatic approach that bridges the gap between clinical trials in high-risk populations and real-world postpartum care. Stratification by baseline body mass index (BMI) ensures balance of BMI categories between treatment arms. With a maximum follow-up of 184 weeks, including a post-treatment evaluation, the study will also provide unique insights into the durability of intervention effects after cessation of therapy.

Methods and analysis

Study design and setting

The SERENA trial is a Belgian multicentre, double-blind, RCT conducted in 13 hospitals. The study evaluates the effect of once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide 1 mg compared with placebo, in addition to standardised lifestyle intervention, in women with a recent history of GDM who develop pre-diabetes in the early postpartum period.

As part of routine postpartum care, women with a history of GDM undergo a 75 g OGTT between 6 weeks and 12 months postpartum. This OGTT serves as the primary screening tool for study eligibility. When an OGTT cannot be performed, pre-diabetes may alternatively be diagnosed based on FPG and/or HbA1c levels according to the ADA criteria.27 Only women diagnosed with pre-diabetes during this postpartum period, who meet the inclusion criteria and who provide written informed consent (ICF), are eligible for inclusion.

The first study visit (SV1) takes place between 2 weeks and 10 months after the baseline OGTT. At this visit, participants are randomised to receive either semaglutide or placebo. Study medication is self-administered once weekly by subcutaneous injection, starting at a dose of 0.25 mg and gradually titrated, as tolerated, to a maximum of 1.0 mg weekly. The placebo solution for injection is identical in composition to semaglutide 1.34 mg/mL solution for injection, except that it does not contain the active ingredient, semaglutide. The placebo is supplied in a 1.5 mL cartridge assembled into a disposable prefilled FlexTouch pen-injector (Novo Nordisk, Bagsværd, Denmark) and is administered according to the same dosing schedule as the active treatment to ensure blinding. During the first 3 months of treatment, monthly telephone follow-ups are conducted to assess treatment tolerance and adherence.

At SV1, participants undergo a fasting blood test, clinical examination, body composition assessment and complete self-administered questionnaires, either during the visit or online at home. Additional blood samples are collected for long-term storage in the UZ Leuven biobank, and participants are asked to complete a 3-day food diary at home; a detailed description of these procedures is provided under section ‘Data collection’.

Subsequent study visits occur every 6 months and include fasting blood sampling, clinical assessments, questionnaires and body composition measurements. A 75 g OGTT is performed annually. Treatment continues for a maximum of 160 weeks (approximately 3 years). The selected semaglutide dose of 1.0 mg corresponds to the approved maintenance dose for T2DM treatment and was chosen to balance efficacy, tolerability and long-term adherence in a young postpartum population. The 3-year follow-up duration was selected based on evidence that progression to T2DM occurs predominantly in the early postpartum years among women with persistent pre-diabetes, while also balancing feasibility, expected attrition and statistical power in a placebo-controlled trial. To evaluate remission of T2DM after treatment cessation, a final OGTT is performed 3–6 months postintervention, resulting in a maximum total follow-up duration of 184 weeks (3 years and 6 months), in line with current consensus guidelines on T2DM remission.29 The post-treatment follow-up window was selected to evaluate the durability of metabolic effects after discontinuation of semaglutide while allowing sufficient washout time after treatment cessation.

Recruitment and eligibility

Participants are recruited from five university hospitals (UZ Leuven, UZ Antwerpen, UZ Brussel, Erasme and CHU Liège) and eight non-university hospitals (AZORG, AZ Groeninge Kortrijk, ZAS Jan Palfijn, VITAZ Sint-Niklaas, Centre Hospitalier de Mouscron, AZ Sint-Jan Brugge, Jan Yperman Hospital and AZ Turnhout).

Eligible participants are women aged ≥18 years, with a history of GDM diagnosed according to the 2013 WHO criteria (at 24–32 weeks gestation or <24 weeks for early GDM).8 Participants must have pre-diabetes diagnosed between 6 weeks and 12 months postpartum according to ADA criteria (FPG 5.6–6.9 mmol/L, 2-hour OGTT glucose 7.8–11.0 mmol/L and/or HbA1c 39–46 mmol/mol (5.7%–6.4%)).27

Additional inclusion criteria include cessation of breastfeeding, no intention to become pregnant within the next year, use of effective contraception and no use of medication affecting glucose metabolism. Written ICF is obtained prior to any study-related procedures.

Exclusion criteria include established diabetes, presence of autoantibodies suggestive of type 1 diabetes, normal glucose tolerance, history of pancreatitis, previous bariatric surgery or planned surgery within 2 years, unable to understand and speak Dutch, French or English and current pregnant or planning to become pregnant within 1 year after participating in the study. Examples of the ICF forms are provided in online supplemental materials 1 and 2.

Patient and public involvement

Patient representatives were actively involved in the study design. Women with a recent diagnosis of GDM contributed lived experience to inform follow-up and postpartum prevention strategies. Additional input from a survey among >30 women with recent GDM informed the choice of a once-weekly injectable semaglutide formulation and helped refine lifestyle education materials. Patient representatives will also support dissemination by highlighting patient-relevant outcomes and implementation considerations.

Modifications to the study protocol

Three protocol amendments were implemented to improve feasibility and recruitment while maintaining study integrity.

Amendment 1 extended the allowable postpartum OGTT window to 6 weeks–12 months and introduced flexibility to support breastfeeding for up to 14 months. Women with early pregnancy GDM diagnosed before 24 weeks (based on WHO 2013 criteria or ADA HbA1c thresholds) can also be included.27 AZ Sint-Jan Brugge was added as a study site, and Dutch-speaking participants were advised to follow free online dietary counselling sessions via the Flemish Diabetes Liga (‘Zoet Zwanger’).

Amendment 2 expanded the allowable timing of SV1 to between 2 weeks and 10 months after the postpartum OGTT to support breastfeeding for up to 22 months. Furthermore, Jan Yperman Hospital and AZ Turnhout were added as participating sites.

Amendment 3 introduced GDPR-compliant recruitment via (KU Leuven) social media channels and discontinued the centre AZ Delta Roeselare due to recruitment challenges.

Randomisation and blinding

Eligible women are randomised in a 1:1 ratio to semaglutide or placebo, stratified by BMI (<25, 25–29.9 and ≥30 kg/m²). Both groups receive the same lifestyle education. Allocation is performed using a secure, password-protected system (Randomize.net, Interrand, Ottawa, Ontario, Canada) to ensure concealment.30 The randomisation list is provided by an independent statistician who is not involved in recruitment, clinical care or follow-up. Investigators and participants remain blinded to group allocation throughout the study.

Blood collection

Laboratory assessments are performed at study visits with a fasting blood test and at study visits including a 75 g OGTT (table 1).

At fasting study visits, glucose is measured locally. The following analyses are performed centrally at the laboratory of UZ Leuven: HbA1c, lipid profile, aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), C-peptide, insulin and plasma samples for storage. In addition, pregnancy status is assessed by measurement of β-human chorionic gonadotropin (β-HCG) in blood or by a urine pregnancy test.

At study visits including a 75 g OGTT, glucose is measured locally at 0, 30, 60 and 120 min as well as lipase. All participating laboratories are accredited according to national quality standards and use standardised procedures for glucose analysis. Blood samples for glucose measurements are collected in fluoride oxalate tubes and transported immediately to the laboratory to minimise glycolysis and intersite variability.

The following analyses are performed centrally at UZ Leuven: lipid profile, AST, ALT, HbA1c, C-peptide and insulin measured at 0, 30, 60 and 120 min. Additional plasma samples are collected centrally at 0, 30, 60 and 120 min for storage. Pregnancy status is assessed by measurement of β-HCG in blood or by a urine pregnancy test.

Blood samples for glucose analysis are collected in fluoride oxalate tubes and transported immediately to the laboratory to minimise glycolysis. For consistency, all central laboratory analyses are performed at the laboratory of UZ Leuven.

Plasma samples collected at fasting and OGTT visits are stored for future biomarker and metabolomics analyses. Long-term storage (up to 10 years) takes place at −80°C in the UZ Leuven biobank. Samples are transported from participating centres to the biobank by courier at least every 3 months.

For the 75 g OGTT, participants are instructed to fast for at least 10 hours and to refrain from smoking, caffeine intake and physical activity during the test; only water is permitted. A fasting blood sample is collected, followed by ingestion of a 75 g glucose solution (Glucomedics, Lambra, Madrid, Spain) within 5 min. Blood samples are subsequently obtained at 30, 60 and 120 min. All other study assessments are completed during the 2-hour waiting period.

For fasting blood tests without OGTT, participants are instructed to fast for at least 8 hours and to drink water only.

Clinical examinations

Height is measured at SV1 using a calibrated wall-mounted stadiometer. At each study visit, blood pressure, heart rate and weight are measured using calibrated devices. Blood pressure is measured two times at 5 min intervals. BMI is calculated as kg/m². Waist circumference is measured midway between the iliac crest and the lowest rib, and hip circumference at the widest part of the gluteal region. Body composition is measured by bioelectrical impedance analysis with Bodystat 1500 (Bodystat, Douglas, Isle of Man, UK).

Self-administered questionnaires

A self-designed questionnaire on general habits (including smoking and alcohol habits) and socioeconomic factors, as used in the Belgian Diabetes in Pregnancy study (BEDIP-N)31; Frequency Food Questionnaire validated for the multiethnic Belgian population32; the International Questionnaire on Physical Activity validated for use in the Belgian population31 33; a self-designed questionnaire on breastfeeding and anticonception as was previously used in the BEDIP-N study31; the validated health-related quality of life by SF-3634 and the EuroQol 5-Dimension 5-Level (EQ-5D-5L) questionnaire,35 the validated 20-item Centre for Epidemiologic Studies-Depression (CES-D) questionnaire,36 the validated 6-item short-form the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI) questionnaire on anxiety,37 a self-designed treatment satisfaction questionnaire which measures satisfaction with the treatment (based on the Diabetes Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire)38; the Diabetes Risk perception questionnaire, a validated questionnaire to evaluate the perception to develop diabetes39; the validated Pittsburgh sleep quality index to evaluate sleep quality as a risk factor to develop glucose intolerance40 and the short version of the USA Household Food Security Survey Module (HFSSM) to evaluate food security.41

The questionnaires are administered at each study visit and are completed during the study visit (online), or at home.

Standardised lifestyle education

All participants receive standardised lifestyle education during the face-to-face study visits, irrespective of treatment allocation. The lifestyle education follows international (ADA) and national Belgian guidelines and includes support for exclusive breastfeeding up to 6 months,42 weight loss and maintenance aiming to return to prepregnancy weight and ≥5% additional weight loss in women with prepregnancy overweight or obesity18 and encouragement of physical activity by reducing sedentary behaviour and achieving ≥150 min of moderate-intensity activity per week.43

Dietary recommendations include ≤30%–35% of total energy from fat, ≤10% from saturated fat (with <8% from atherogenic saturated fatty acids), ≥15 g fibre per 1000 kcal (Finnish DPP)44 and <10% of energy from added sugars (Belgian Superior Health Council). Practical dietary advice (including healthy recipes) and exercise examples are provided. Diet and physical activity are monitored using questionnaires. Baseline dietary intake is evaluated using a 3-day food diary, providing a reliable estimate of habitual eating patterns while minimising misreporting. Participants record their intake on non-consecutive days, ideally including a weekend day, following study visits 1, 3, 5, 7 and 8.45 Dutch-speaking participants use the Digitaal Dagboek mobile app, developed by UCLL (University of Applied Sciences, Leuven, Limburg, Belgium) for research purposes. Each participant receives a personal study login linked to their unique subject ID, ensuring that only study-relevant data are accessible to the research team. The app allows logging of both home-cooked and store-bought meals, automatically calculating daily macronutrient and micronutrient intake. Non-Dutch-speaking participants, or those unable to use the app, complete a paper-based food diary instead.

In addition, women also have the opportunity to follow six online sessions with a certified dietician (1-hour 30 min duration, only for Dutch-speaking participants), provided by the Flemish Diabetes Liga (the association representing people living with diabetes). During these sessions, practical guidance is provided on their eating habits and on how to eat healthier. These sessions are completely free of charge and can take place during the day or in the evening. Participation in these sessions is optional (and is in line with normal routine) and is not considered part of the core study intervention. All participants, irrespective of language or treatment allocation, receive the same standardised lifestyle education and written study materials during study visits. Attendance to optional online sessions will be recorded and may be explored descriptively in additional analyses.

Safety outcomes

Adverse events that are specifically collected include gastrointestinal adverse events (nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea), hypoglycaemia (<54 mg/dL),50 pancreatitis (defined by the presence of at least two of the following criteria: severe acute abdominal pain; lipase and/or amylase activity ≥3 times the upper limit of normal or characteristic imaging findings of acute pancreatitis. In case pancreatitis is suspected, study medication is discontinued and permanently stopped if the diagnosis is confirmed), cholecystitis, injection-site reactions and hypersensitivity reactions.

Pregnancy history and delivery characteristics (collected from the electronic medical record)

Maternal data: parity; prepregnancy weight; gestational week of GDM diagnosis; results of the 50 g glucose challenge test and 75 g OGTT during pregnancy; treatment of GDM during pregnancy; pregnancy complications such as preeclampsia (BP≥140/90 mm Hg >20 weeks with proteinuria or signs of end-organ dysfunction), eclampsia (one or more generalised convulsions and/or coma in the setting of pre-eclampsia and in the absence of other neurologic conditions), gestational hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mm Hg >20 weeks), pre-existing hypertension and pregnancy-induced cholestasis.

Delivery data: pregnancy duration; type of labour (spontaneous, induced or caesarean before labour) and indications; type of delivery (spontaneous vaginal, forceps or vacuum, caesarean section during labour or planned caesarean section) and indications.

Neonatal data: macrosomia (>4 Kg), LGA (birth weight >90 percentile according to standardised Flemish birth charts adjusted for sex of the baby and parity),51 small for gestational age (birth weight <10 percentile according to standardised Flemish birth charts adjusted for sex of the baby and parity),51 preterm delivery (<37 completed weeks), 10 min Apgar score, shoulder dystocia, birth trauma, neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (at least 4 hours of respiratory support with supplemental oxygen, continuous positive airway pressure or intermittent positive-pressure ventilation during the first 24 hours after delivery), neonatal hypoglycaemia (glycaemia <2.2 mmol/L or <40 mg/dL or need for intravenous dextrose), neonatal jaundice (hyperbilirubinaemia, need for phototherapy), duration and indication for admission on the neonatal intensive care unit (admission defined as >24 hour).

Novel biomarkers

As part of the study, metabolomics analyses will be performed in collaboration with the Steno Diabetes Centre to explore novel biomarkers associated with long-term cardiometabolic risk after GDM. Metabolomic profiling may help identify metabolic signatures predictive of postpartum progression to dysglycaemia and potential responsiveness to semaglutide.52 53 These analyses aim to provide insights into underlying metabolic pathways and may support the development of more individualised follow-up and treatment strategies after GDM.

Premature termination

Participants may discontinue trial treatment and/or withdraw from the study at any time and for any reason, without consequences for their future medical care. Trial treatment may also be temporarily interrupted or permanently discontinued by the Investigator if continuation is not in the participant’s best interest. The sponsor, EC, or regulatory authorities may suspend or terminate the trial if participant safety, trial integrity or scientific validity can no longer be ensured.

Reasons for treatment interruption or discontinuation include, but are not limited to, safety concerns or unacceptable intolerability of the study medication, violation of inclusion or exclusion criteria, pregnancy or intention to become pregnant and (ongoing) treatment with glucose-lowering medication prior to the diagnosis of T2DM.

In all cases of early treatment interruption or discontinuation, appropriate medical care and safety monitoring will be ensured; a detailed description of these procedures is provided under the Trial management section.

Follow-up after premature discontinuation

Including those who develop T2DM and require rescue therapy will continue follow-up according to the study schedule until week 184. For the primary endpoint, the intention-to-treat (IAA) analysis will follow a treatment-policy estimand strategy, whereby participants are analysed according to randomised treatment allocation regardless of treatment discontinuation, rescue medication or protocol deviations. Per-protocol (PP) analyses will estimate the treatment effect for participants that remained on assigned treatment without rescue medication. In participants who develop T2DM, annual visits including an OGTT will be replaced by visits with fasting blood sampling only.

Unplanned pregnancy

Trial medication will be stopped immediately and treatment allocation will be unblinded. Participants exposed to semaglutide will be informed of the limited human pregnancy data and potential foetal risks based on animal studies.54–56 Counselling by a high-risk obstetric care unit will be provided, with regular expert ultrasound examinations as needed. Pregnancy and birth outcomes will be recorded in the electronic case report form (eCRF) (after signing an additional ICF), and safety concerns will be reported to the Ethics Committee (EC) as required.

Power calculation and statistical analysis

Sample size

Based on subanalyses of the DPP in women with a history of GDM, a 3-year T2DM incidence of 38% is assumed in the control group.16 A 50% relative risk reduction in the intervention group is expected, based on evidence from a large RCT demonstrating a 79% reduction in T2DM incidence with liraglutide in obese individuals with pre-diabetes, and on the approximately 50% risk reduction observed with intensive lifestyle intervention in the DPP.15 Given the greater potency of semaglutide compared with liraglutide and its use in combination with lifestyle intervention in the present study, a 50% reduction is considered realistic.

The sample size was calculated for a two-sided χ2 test to detect a difference in 3-year T2DM incidence between treatment groups with 80% power at a 5% significance level, assuming a 30% dropout rate over 3 years (based on the best available evidence from high-risk pre-diabetes populations and our previous mobile-based postpartum lifestyle intervention study conducted in a similar study population).18 Based on these assumptions, a total sample size of 252 participants is required. Furthermore, a blinded interim sample size reassessment is planned to account for lower-than-expected event rates or higher dropout rates.

Recruitment and study feasibility

To avoid bias in recruitment, 13 centres were selected to facilitate the recruitment of a representative multiethnic population with recent history of GDM. The collaborating centres in the SERENA trial are centres who have participated in previous collaborations on GDM (BEDIP-N and/or MELINDA study)18 31 or have extensive experience with clinical trials in diabetes and GDM. The established network (including university and non-university hospitals from different regions in Belgium) will facilitate recruitment. In addition, with the collaboration of many different centres attending to a diverse ethnic population, we will be able to recruit a large multiethnic population. Recruitment is ongoing. Several challenges have been encountered during recruitment, mainly related to prolonged breastfeeding, lack of interest in study participation, concerns about medication use, fear of injections, the 50% chance of randomisation to semaglutide or placebo, a short-term desire for a new pregnancy and limited motivation to continue study participation when no effect on body weight was perceived.

These barriers may affect generalisability, as women willing to participate in a long-term pharmacological trial may differ from the broader population of women with recent GDM and postpartum pre-diabetes. However, recruitment across both university and non-university hospitals in different regions of Belgium was intended to improve representativeness of routine clinical practice.

In response to the challenges, several mitigation strategies were implemented and amendments were submitted and approved. Following these amendments, recruitment has progressed more smoothly. A detailed description of these amendments is provided under the section Modifications to the study protocol.

On approval by the funding body, the recruitment period was extended. Recruitment is currently expected to be completed by mid-2026.

Primary endpoint

The primary analysis will be performed using logistic regression to model T2DM as a binary outcome, with treatment arm and stratification group as explanatory factors. ORs for the treatment effect will be presented with 95% CIs. A logistic regression approach was selected because the primary endpoint is defined as cumulative incidence of T2DM at 160 weeks. In addition, diabetes onset is assessed at discrete study visits rather than continuously, resulting in interval-censored event timing. This approach aligns with the predefined trial estimand and sample size calculation. Exploratory time-to-event analyses may additionally be performed to further assess timing of progression to T2DM. Multiple imputation by treatment arm is applied to handle missing data using earlier clinical measurements as predictors. The imputation models will include treatment allocation, stratification factors, baseline covariates, prior outcome measurements and relevant clinical variables associated with missingness and outcome risk. Missingness patterns will be explored, and sensitivity analyses under alternative assumptions regarding missing data mechanisms may be considered where appropriate. The analysis will follow an ITT approach, estimating the average treatment effect regardless of adherence or initiation of rescue interventions. A secondary PP analysis will target the effect assuming that patients remained on treatment without rescue medication, with postviolation measurements treated as missing and handled by multiple imputation.

Secondary endpoints

Continuous and longitudinal secondary outcomes will be analysed using linear mixed models for repeated measurements, with fixed effects for study arm, time and their interaction, and random effects to account for clustering. Sensitivity analyses adjusting for time since delivery and time since the qualifying postpartum OGTT may additionally be performed to explore potential heterogeneity in baseline risk and treatment response related to postpartum timing. Results will be reported as mean differences with 95% CIs. Binary longitudinal outcomes are analysed using random-effects logistic regression, with ORs and 95% CIs. Given the broad range of secondary and exploratory outcomes, these analyses are primarily considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating. No formal adjustment for multiplicity is planned, and findings from secondary analyses will, therefore, be interpreted cautiously.

Exploratory analyses

Prespecified biomarker analyses will be performed after data collection, with statistical procedures finalised prior to database lock to ensure reproducibility. Multivariate models will identify metabolites or metabolite combinations associated with clinical outcomes.

Interim analysis and database lock

A blinded interim analysis will be conducted once a substantial proportion of participants have completed the full 3-year follow-up, which is expected to be approximately 50 participants. This interim analysis will be performed solely for sample size re-estimation. No efficacy analyses will be conducted, and no multiplicity adjustments will be applied at this stage. The sample size may be increased if observed event rates are lower or dropout rates higher than anticipated, but will not be decreased. Blinding will be maintained to preserve study integrity.

Trial management

This is an academic research trial. University Hospitals Gasthuisberg Leuven (UZ Leuven) is the sponsor and has responsibility for the overall management of the study.

Data management

Every participating site is opened after a first site initiation visit. Monitoring visit (MV) 1 is always conducted within 6 months, except if 5 patients are recruited very quickly, then MV1 is conducted after 4 months. MV2, MV3, MV4 are conducted at intervals of 18 months. Those visits are done by an independent monitor of the clinical trial centre (CTC) of UZ Leuven to check adherence of the participating sites to the protocol and completeness of data collection forms. Each participant gets a subject identification number to ensure confidentiality of the data. All data collected in this study are referred to by subject identification number only. All obtained data are entered in the Good Clinical Practice (GCP) compliant to the eCRF ‘REDCap’.

Trial Steering Committee

A formal Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) is not established, as the investigational medicinal product (IMP) is commonly used for T2DM with well-known side effects. Given the approved use of semaglutide, the known class-specific adverse event profile of GLP-1 receptor agonists, and the low frequency of severe hypoglycaemia associated with these agents, a separate DSMB was not considered mandatory by the sponsor and ethics committees.

Safety oversight is nevertheless ensured through several complementary procedures. The study is conducted according to GCP standards and includes regular independent monitoring visits performed by the CTC of UZ Leuven to ensure protocol adherence, data quality and appropriate safety reporting. In addition, the study is supported by an independent Advisory Board providing external scientific and safety oversight throughout trial conduct.

Overall trial supervision is provided by the Trial Steering Committee (TSC), composed of several physicians (some affiliated with participating study sites and some independent) together with an independent statistician.

The TSC meets at least three times per year, via videoconference, and is responsible for monitoring recruitment and lost-to-follow-up patients, reviewing safety data including serious and other adverse events related to the IMP, ensuring trial data confidentiality, and overseeing the ethical conduct of the study. The TSC recommends administrative changes, modifications to inclusion/exclusion criteria or safety monitoring procedures, or temporary suspension of recruitment if safety data indicate significant adverse effects. Criteria for trial modification or termination include high dropout rates (>40%), substantial treatment discontinuation due to severe adverse events (>20%) or multiple hospitalisations for severe gastrointestinal side effects (>15) or hypoglycaemia (>10).50

Ethics and dissemination

The study was approved by the Federal Agency for Medicines and Health Products and the relevant Ethics Committees (approval number 2022–502082-22-00) and is conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Recruitment started in September 2023. All participants receive written and verbal information about the study objectives, procedures, potential risks and their rights prior to participation. Written ICF is obtained from all participants before inclusion in the study. Participation is voluntary, and participants are informed of their right to withdraw at any time without any consequences for their medical care.

Participant data are handled in accordance with applicable data protection regulations. Personal identifying information is stored separately from study data, and all data are pseudonymised to ensure confidentiality.

Study results will be disseminated through publication in peer-reviewed scientific journals and presentation at national and international scientific conferences.
