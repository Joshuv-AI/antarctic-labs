Abstract
. We study long-time behaviors for branching-diffusion process corresponding to the drifted Schrödinger operator ℒ = 1 2 ​ Δ + ⟨ ∇ V , ∇ ⟩ − K {\mathscr{L}}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\langle\nabla V,\nabla\rangle-K , where K K represents the reduction rate of a population dynamics and ∇ V \nabla V is a given drift term. In particular, we establish exponential convergence rates for the total mass of this process and characterize its quasi-stationary distribution. The proof is based on a novel transformation in spectral analysis, and heat kernel estimates for Schrödinger operators with unbounded potentials. The result is new even in the one-dimensional setting, which especially improves the recent work [ 10 ] . Keywords: branching-diffusion process; total mass; quasi-stationary distribution; Schrödinger operator; heat kernel estimate MSC 2020: 60J85; 60J60; 47D08; 35K08



1.
Introduction

Branching-diffusion processes and their applications to population dynamics have been an active subject in probability and mathematical biology.
In the setting of our paper, we suppose that each individual is described by a trait (or position)
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, with birth and death occurring in continuous time. More precisely, we study a branching-diffusion process
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
defined as follows: individuals experience trait variations modeled by the following diffusion process on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\rm d}X_{t}=\nabla V(X_{t})\,{\rm d}t+{\rm d}B_{t},

where
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion, and
\nabla V
is a given drift term such that
V\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
; an individual with trait
x
gives birth to an individual with the same trait at rate
b(x)
and dies at rate
d(x)
.
We denote

K(x):=d(x)-b(x)

by the reduction rate with trait
x
;
equivalently,
-K(x)
is the growth rate.
In this paper, we assume that
K(x)
is locally bounded.
Let
\mathcal{A}_{t}
be the set of individuals alive at time
t
. Then, the branching-diffusion process
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the empirical measure on the trait space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, which is defined, for all
t\geq 0
, by

(1.2)

\displaystyle Z_{t}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\delta_{X_{t}^{i}},

where
X_{t}^{i}
is the trait of individual
i
at time
t
and is determined by the diffusion process given by (
1.1
).
The total mass of the branching-diffusion process is given by

N_{t}=\#\mathcal{A}_{t}=\langle Z_{t},1\rangle.

The main objective of this note is to study the long-time behavior for
N_{t}
.

Denote by

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\langle{\nabla V,\nabla}\rangle-K

the generator of the branching-diffusion process
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
It is well known that the operator
{\mathscr{L}}
is symmetric with respect to
\mu({\rm d}x)=e^{2V(x)}{\rm d}x
.
The following assumption is always imposed throughout the paper.

Assumption (A0)
.

(i)

(1.4)

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\widetilde{K}(x):=\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\left(K(x)+\frac{1}{2}\Delta V(x)+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla V(x)|^{2}\right)=\infty,

and for any
\theta>0
,

(1.5)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{-\theta\widetilde{K}(x)}\,{\rm d}x<\infty.

(ii)

(1.6)

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{V_{-}(x)}{|x|^{2}}=0,\quad\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{V_{-}(x)}{\inf_{z\in B(x,|x|/2)}\widetilde{K}(z)}<\infty;

or

(1.7)

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{|x|^{2}}{\inf_{z\in B(x,|x|/2)}\widetilde{K}(z)}<\infty,\quad\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{V_{-}(x)}{|x|\inf_{z\in B(x,|x|/2)}\widetilde{K}(z)^{1/2}}=0.

We will see that under (
1.4
), the operator
-{\mathscr{L}}
has discrete spectrum in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\rm d}\mu)
, and there exists an orthonormal basis in

associated with the corresponding eigenvalues

Moreover, there exists a version of

which is continuous and strictly positive, e.g. see the proof of

For

large enough

H_{c,c_{0}}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\exp\left(-V_{+}(x)\right)\left[\exp\left(-c\inf_{z\in B(x,|x|/2)}\widetilde{K}(z)\right)+\exp(-c_{0}|x|^{2})\right],&\quad\hbox{ if }\eqref{e:ess}\hbox{ holds;}\\

The main contribution of this note is as follows.

Theorem 1.1

Under Assumption

According to (

Specifically, if

Suppose that there exist constants

Example

is interesting, and it can be directly applied to the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (O-U) branching-diffusion process (e.g. see
[
15
]
). That is, the diffusion process (
1.1
) is given by

(1.12)

{\rm d}X_{t}=cX_{t}\,{\rm d}t+{\rm d}B_{t},

where

is a non-zero constant. The process (

) corresponds to
V(x)={c}|x|^{2}/2
in our paper. We shall note that the framework of
[

]
excludes the O-U branching-diffusion process, since it requires the term

to have at most linear growth. In particular, for (

K(x)\geq c_{1}|x|^{\beta}-c_{2}

with

and

We point out that a more recent preprint
[

, Remark, p.8]
points out on the possibility of relaxing such kind linear growth condition in one-dimensional case. Nevertheless, our work presents a rigorous proof for this relaxation even in the high-dimensional setting.

The novelty of our approach is by introducing a new transformation of the generator
{\mathscr{L}}
. More explicitly, the importance of this transformation lies in its capacity to absorb the original drift term of
{\mathscr{L}}
into the new potential
\widetilde{K}(x)
, thereby converting the operator
{\mathscr{L}}
into a canonical Schrödinger operator
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}
without drift. With aid of this, we then can make full use of heat kernel estimates for Schrödinger operators with unbounded potentials developed in
[
6
]
. The idea of our approach is completely different from that of
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
, where the probabilistic ideas such as the Girsanov transform are adopted. Roughly speaking, the proof of Theorem
1.1
mainly arises from the analytical perspective.
Compared with the Girsanov transform used in
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
, our method here does not require any martingale-related hypotheses (for instance, Novikov’s condition), which extensively improves
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
.

As an application of our approach, we can characterize the existence and uniqueness of the quasi-stationary distribution for the branching-diffusion system.

(

That is, for any

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}K(X_{s})\,{\rm d}s\right)\phi(X_{t})\right]\nu({\rm d}x)=e^{-\lambda_{0}t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(x)\,\nu({\rm d}x).

In particular,

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}K(X_{s})\,{\rm d}s\right)\phi(X_{t})\right]\,\nu({\rm d}x)}{\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}K(X_{s})\,{\rm d}s\right)\right]\,\nu({\rm d}x)}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(x)\,\nu({\rm d}x).

2.
Preliminaries

This section is devoted to presenting some preliminaries for the proof of Theorem
1.1
, and it contains two parts. In Subsection
2.1
, we introduce a key transformation, which reduces the generator
{\mathscr{L}}
into a Schrödinger operator
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}
without drift, but keeps the associated spectral unchanged.
In Subsection
2.2
, we apply heat kernel estimates for Schrödinger operators with unbounded potentials, and get explicit decay properties of the eigenfunctions associated with the operator
{\mathscr{L}}
.
For simplicity, in what follows, for any
p\in[1,\infty]
,
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\rm d}x)
and
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\rm d}\mu)
are denoted by
L^{p}({\rm d}x)
and
L^{p}(\mu)
respectively.
We will use
c
and
C
, with or without subscripts, to denote strictly positive finite constants whose values are insignificant and may change from line to line.

2.1.
A key transformation

Let
C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the class of twice differentiable functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with compact support. Let
{\mathscr{L}}
be defined by (
1.3
).
For
f\in C_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, define

(2.1)

\begin{split}\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}f:&=e^{V}{\mathscr{L}}(e^{-V}f)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\Delta f-\left(K+\frac{1}{2}\Delta V+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla V|^{2}\right)f\\
&=:\frac{1}{2}\Delta f-\widetilde{K}f.\end{split}

Indeed,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}(e^{-V}f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Delta(e^{-V}f)+\langle\nabla V,\nabla(e^{-V}f)\rangle-K(e^{-V}f)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}e^{-V}f\Delta V+\frac{1}{2}e^{-V}f\left|\nabla V\right|^{2}-e^{-V}\langle\nabla V,\nabla f\rangle+\frac{1}{2}e^{-V}\Delta f

\displaystyle\quad\penalty\ -e^{-V}f\left|\nabla V\right|^{2}+e^{-V}\langle\nabla V,\nabla f\rangle-K(e^{-V}f)

\displaystyle=e^{-V}\left[\frac{1}{2}\Delta f-\left(K+\frac{1}{2}\Delta V+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla V|^{2}\right)f\right].

The advantage of the operator
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}
is that it is the canonical Schrödinger operator with the potential
\widetilde{K}
(without drift) on
L^{2}({\rm d}x)
.
It directly follows from (
2.1
) that
{\mathscr{L}}f(x)=\lambda f(x)
holds for
f\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
, if and only if
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}\tilde{f}(x)=\lambda\tilde{f}(x)
, where
\tilde{f}(x)=e^{V(x)}f(x)
. In particular, the operators
{\mathscr{L}}
and
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}
have the same eigenvalues, and the associated eigenfunctions differ by a factor
e^{V(x)}
.

Next, we assume that (
1.4
) holds. Then, it is well known (e.g. see
[
3
, Chapter 2, Theorem 3.1, p. 57]
) that the operator
-\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}
has discrete spectrum in
L^{2}({\rm d}x)
, and there exists an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}({\rm d}x)
of
C^{2}
-eigenfunctions
\{\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
associated with the corresponding eigenvalues
\{{\lambda}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
so that

\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}=\infty.

Hence,
-{\mathscr{L}}
also has discrete spectrum consisting of eigenvalues
\{{\lambda}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. Moreover, let
\{{\varphi}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be the associated eigenfunctions of
-{\mathscr{L}}
. Then,
{\varphi}_{n}(x)=e^{-V(x)}\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}(x)
. In particular,
\|{\varphi}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}\|_{L^{2}({\rm d}x)}=1
. The first eigenfunction
{\varphi}_{0}
is called ground state in the literature.

2.2.

\{{\varphi}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}

Let

, and

3.
General result and proofs of results in Section

In this section, we will establish a general result for long-time behaviors of branching-diffusion process
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, which immediately yields Theorem
1.1
.
For this, we will consider long-time behaviors of the linear functional
\langle Z_{t},\phi\rangle
for suitable functions
\phi
, which in turn is based on the relationship between the branching-diffusion process
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and the following Feynman-Kac formula for the Schrödinger semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
:

(3.1)

P_{t}\phi(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}(\langle Z_{t},\phi\rangle)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}-K(X_{s})\,{\rm d}s\right)\phi(X_{t})\right],\quad\phi\in C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the diffusion process defined by (
1.1
).

Recall that under (
1.4
), the generator
-{\mathscr{L}}
of the Schrödinger semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has discrete spectrum consisting of eigenvalues
\{{\lambda}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, which correspond to the eigenfunctions
\{{\varphi}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
.
Let
\Pi
denote the projection onto the space generated by the first eigenfunction
{\varphi}_{0}
; that is, for any measurable function
g
such that
g\varphi_{0}\in L^{1}(\mu)
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Pi(g)(x)={\varphi}_{0}(x)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}g(y){\varphi}_{0}(y)\,\mu({\rm d}y),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Let
H_{c,c_{0}}(x)
be a positive, bounded and continuous function defined in (
1.8
); that is, for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
|x|
large enough,

H_{c,c_{0}}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\exp\left(-V_{+}(x)\right)\left[\exp\left(-c\inf_{z\in B(x,|x|/2)}\widetilde{K}(z)\right)+\exp(-c_{0}|x|^{2})\right],&\quad\hbox{ if }\eqref{e:ess}\hbox{ holds;}\\
\displaystyle\exp\left(-V_{+}(x)\right)\exp\left(-c_{0}|x|\inf_{z\in B(x,|x|/2)}\widetilde{K}(z)^{1/2}\right),&\quad\hbox{ if }\eqref{e:ess2}\hbox{ holds.}\end{cases}

In particular, according to Proposition
2.1
, under Assumption
(A0)
, there is a constant
C_{0}>0
such that for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
n\geq 0
,

(3.3)

|{\varphi}_{n}(x)|\leq C_{0}e^{\lambda_{n}T_{0}/2}H_{c,c_{0}}(x)

for some proper
T_{0}\geq 1
and
c,c_{0}>0
. Here, we should mention that when (
1.6
) holds, one can take
c>0
large enough in the definition of
H_{c,c_{0}}(x)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under Assumption
(A0)
, for any measurable function
\phi
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
satisfying

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}H_{c,c_{0}}(y)|\phi(y)|\,\mu({\rm d}y)<\infty

large enough and

and

such that all

,

\displaystyle\|e^{\lambda_{0}t}P_{t}(\phi)-\Pi(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\leq C_{0}e^{-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{0})t}.

As an application of Theorem
3.1
, we can give the

Proof of Theorem

.

1.9

) is satisfied with

Next, we will present the

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Assume that Assumption
(A0)
holds. Recall that
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}=e^{V}{\mathscr{L}}(e^{-V}\cdot)
and
\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\widetilde{p}(t,x,x)\,{\rm d}x<\infty
for any
t>0
.
Then, it holds that

(3.6)

\widetilde{P}_{t}f=e^{V}P_{t}(e^{-V}f),\quad f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\,t>0.

Thus, for any
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
t>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle P_{t}f(x)

\displaystyle=e^{-V(x)}\widetilde{P}_{t}(e^{V(\cdot)}f(\cdot))(x)

\displaystyle=e^{-V(x)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\widetilde{p}(t,x,y)f(y)e^{V(y)}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\widetilde{p}(t,x,y)e^{-V(x)}e^{-V(y)}f(y)\,\mu({\rm d}y),

which implies the heat kernel associated with
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
exists (with respect to the measure
\mu
), denoted by
p(t,x,y)
. That is, for all
t>0
and
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

p(t,x,y)=\widetilde{p}(t,x,y)e^{-V(x)}e^{-V(y)}.

Furthermore, for all
t>0
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}p(t,x,x)\,\mu({\rm d}x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\widetilde{p}(t,x,x)e^{-2V(x)}\,\mu({\rm d}x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\widetilde{p}(t,x,x)\,{\rm d}x<\infty.

Therefore, with aid of this and the remark in the end of Subsection
2.1
, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle p(t,x,y)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{n}t}{\varphi}_{n}(x){\varphi}_{n}(y),\quad t>0,\penalty\ x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where
\{{\lambda}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
are the associated eigenvalues corresponding to the eigenfunctions
\{{\varphi}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
.

For any measurable function
\phi
, any
t>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle P_{t}(\phi-\Pi(\phi))(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}t}{\varphi}_{n}(x)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\varphi}_{n}(y)\left[\phi(y)-\Pi(\phi)(y)\right]\,\mu({\rm d}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-{\lambda}_{0}t}{\varphi}_{0}(x)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\varphi}_{0}(y)\left[\phi(y)-\Pi(\phi)(y)\right]\,\mu({\rm d}y)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}t}{\varphi}_{n}(x)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\varphi}_{n}(y)\left[\phi(y)-\Pi(\phi)(y)\right]\,\mu({\rm d}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}t}{\varphi}_{n}(x)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\varphi}_{n}(y)\phi(y)\,\mu({\rm d}y),

where we used the definition of
\Pi(\phi)(x)
and the facts that
\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\varphi}_{n}(x){\varphi}_{0}(x)\,\mu({\rm d}x)=0
for all
n\geq 1
and
\|{\varphi}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=1
.
Hence, for any
t>T_{0}

\displaystyle\|P_{t}(\phi-\Pi(\phi))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}t}{\varphi}_{n}(\cdot)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\varphi}_{n}(y)\phi(y)\,\mu({\rm d}y)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}t}\|{\varphi}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|{\varphi}_{n}(y)\phi(y)|\,\mu({\rm d}y)

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}t}e^{{\lambda}_{n}T_{0}/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{{\lambda}_{n}T_{0}/2}H_{c,c_{0}}(y)|\phi(y)|\,\mu({\rm d}y)

\displaystyle=c_{1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}t}e^{{\lambda}_{n}T_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}H_{c,c_{0}}(y)|\phi(y)|\,\mu({\rm d}y)\leq c_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}(t-T_{0})},

where in both inequalities above we used (
3.3
) and (
3.4
).
This, along with the fact that for all

,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}(t-T_{0})}=e^{-{\lambda}_{1}t}e^{{\lambda}_{1}T_{0}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-({\lambda}_{n}-{\lambda}_{1})(t-T_{0})}=:c_{3}e^{-{\lambda}_{1}t},

(thanks to (

Furthermore, according to the fact that

for all

and

, we have

\|e^{\lambda_{0}t}P_{t}(\phi)-\Pi(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}=e^{\lambda_{0}t}\|P_{t}(\phi-\Pi(\phi))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}.

Therefore, the proof is complete.

Remark 3.2
.

We add a comment on the proof of Theorem
3.1
. We start from the following computation.
Assume that (
1.4
) holds. For any measurable function
\phi
,
t>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|P_{t}(\phi-\Pi(\phi))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}t}\|{\varphi}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\varphi}_{n}(y)\phi(y)\,\mu({\rm d}y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}t}\|{\varphi}_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\|\phi\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}

and

\displaystyle\|P_{t}(\phi-\Pi(\phi))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}t}\|{\varphi}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\varphi}_{n}(y)\phi(y)\,\mu({\rm d}y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}t}\|{\varphi}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\|{\varphi}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-{\lambda}_{n}t}\|{\varphi}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Then, if there is
t_{0}>0
so that
\|P_{t_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{\infty}(\mu)}<\infty
, it follows from
P_{t}{\varphi}_{n}(x)=e^{-{\lambda}_{n}t}{\varphi}_{n}(x)
for all
t>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
that

\displaystyle\|{\varphi}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq e^{{\lambda}_{n}t_{0}}\|P_{t_{0}}{\varphi}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq e^{{\lambda}_{n}t_{0}}\|P_{t_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{\infty}(\mu)}\|{\varphi}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq e^{{\lambda}_{n}t_{0}}\|P_{t_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{\infty}(\mu)}.

Hence, for any
\phi\in L^{2}(\mu)\cup L^{1}(\mu)
, there exist
T_{0}>0
and
c_{1}>0
such that for all
t\geq T_{0}
,

\|P_{t}(\phi-\Pi(\phi))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\leq c_{1}e^{-{\lambda}_{1}t};

that is,

\|e^{\lambda_{0}t}P_{t}(\phi)-\Pi(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\leq c_{1}e^{-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{0})t}.

However, it is non-trivial to show that
\|P_{t_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{\infty}(\mu)}<\infty
for some
t_{0}>0
, which is related to the so-called ultracontractivity of the semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Moreover, the arguments above also require that

. In particular, taking
\phi\equiv 1
yields the assertion of Theorem
1.1
. Nevertheless, this
implies that
\mu
is a finite measure, which is a strong restriction to study long-time behaviors of the total mass
N_{t}
. Indeed, the key ingredient to obtain Theorem
3.1
is to establish explicit decay properties of the eigenfunctions
\{{\varphi}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
.
