Can an MLP Absorb Its Own Skip Connection?
Antonij Mijoski antonijmijo@gmail.com, antonij.mijoski@unistra.fr, Marko Karbevski marko.karbevski@gmail.com

Abstract
We study when a skip connection around a single-hidden-layer MLP can be absorbed into a residual-free MLP of the same width. We first show that for any architecture whose skip branch is an invertible linear map (including Hyper-Connections and their manifold-constrained variants), the problem reduces to the identity skip case. For homogeneous activations of degree k ≠ 1 k\neq 1 , such as ReLU 2 \operatorname{ReLU}^{2} and ReGLU, absorption is unconditionally impossible by a degree argument. For gated activations whose gate is differentiable at the origin with g ⁡ ( 0 ) = 0 g(0)=0 , including SwiGLU and GeGLU, a linearization argument gives the same conclusion. These impossibility results extend to arbitrary depth: a composition of L L residual blocks using such activations cannot be replicated by any composition of L L residual-free blocks of the same width. For ungated ReLU and GELU, the situation is richer. For generic weight matrices, absorption holds at the single-block level if and only if there exists an index set S S of size at least d d such that W down [ : , S ] W up [ S , : ] = − I d W_{\mathrm{down}}[:,S]\,W_{\mathrm{up}}[S,:]=-I_{d} . This condition is non-generic (it fails with probability one under continuous weight distributions), so skip-connected and residual-free MLPs of the same width represent generically disjoint function classes. Whether this disjointness persists for deep compositions of ReLU or GELU blocks remains open.

Introduction

Skip connections were the breakthrough that enabled systematic training of
deep neural networks. Since their introduction by He et al. (
10
,
11
), they have become present
in virtually every facet of state-of-the-art deep learning: the
Transformer
(
31
)
, Vision Transformers

(
4
)
, ConvNeXt
(
18
)
, LLaMA

(
30
;
5
)
, DeepSeek
(
3
)
, and Gemma

(
7
)
all wrap every attention and MLP sub-layer in a branch

x\mapsto x+\mathrm{MLP}(x)
. Recent work has extended this pattern
beyond the identity, replacing it with learnable mixing matrices

(
34
;
32
)
. The prevailing understanding is that skip
connections smooth the loss landscape
(
17
)
and help gradients
flow
(
11
;
33
)
. We ask a different question: do
skip connections change what functions an MLP can represent?

Given a fixed
\mathrm{MLP}_{2}(x)=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)
, when does there exist an

\mathrm{MLP}_{1}(x)=V_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)
of
the same width such that
x+\mathrm{MLP}_{2}(x)=\mathrm{MLP}_{1}(x)
for
all inputs
x
? If such absorption is possible, the skip connection is

representationally redundant
at that width. If not, the skip
connection grants access to a different region of function space. The
converse question is equally natural: can a residual-free MLP be rewritten
as a skip-connected one? Since the equation
\mathrm{MLP}_{1}(x)=x+\mathrm{MLP}_{2}(x)
rearranges to
(-I_{d})\,x+\mathrm{MLP}_{1}(x)=\mathrm{MLP}_{2}(x)
, both directions are instances of the same absorption
problem, and our results settle both.

Contributions.

We show that when the skip branch is an
invertible linear map, the absorption problem reduces to the identity skip
case, covering Hyper-Connections and their variants
(Lemma
4
). For homogeneous activations of degree

k\neq 1
(
\operatorname{ReLU}^{2}
, ReGLU), we prove absorption is unconditionally
impossible (Proposition
5
), and we extend this to
gated activations whose gate is differentiable at the origin with
g(0)=0

(SwiGLU, GeGLU;
28
) via a linearization argument
(Proposition
6
). These impossibility results extend to
compositions of arbitrary depth (Corollary
7
), settling the
question for the activation functions used in current state-of-the-art
models. For ungated ReLU and GELU, we give if-and-only-if
characterizations for generic weights at the single-block
level (Theorems
8
and
10
), both reducing to the
same submatrix condition

W_{\mathrm{down}}[:,S]\,W_{\mathrm{up}}[S,:]=-I_{d}
. A corollary shows
that skip-connected and residual-free MLPs are generically disjoint
function classes (Corollary
11
); we leave the
multi-layer case for ReLU and GELU as an open problem.

Related Work

Residual networks and reparametrization.

He et al. (
10
) introduced residual connections and
observed that they ease optimization in deep networks. A follow-up study
(He et al.,
11
) showed that placing the identity
path outside all nonlinearities is critical for training very deep
ResNets. Subsequent work has studied the effect of skip connections on the
loss landscape
(
17
)
, on representational power
(
19
)
,
and on implicit regularization
(
9
)
. Our results complement
this line by characterizing exactly when the residual structure is
algebraically redundant. Graef (
8
) and Karbevski and
Mijoski (
16
) studied weight elimination in skipless
and skip-connected transformers respectively; the latter provided a proof
of the absorption characterization for one activation. The present paper
provides complete proofs for both ReLU and GELU, the impossibility results
for homogeneous and gated activations, and the general reduction to the
identity skip case.

The role of skip connections beyond optimization.

Ji et al. (
12
) showed that self-attention is uniquely
dependent on skip connections for trainability: removing them from the
attention block causes catastrophic failure, while MLP blocks degrade more
gracefully. This asymmetry suggests that skip connections play different
algebraic roles around different sub-layers, a theme our work makes
precise for the MLP case. On the analysis side, Elhage et al. (
6
) and Olsson et al. (
22
) have
studied the computational role of the residual stream in transformers.
Hyper-Connections
(
34
)
and their manifold-constrained
variants
(
32
)
generalize the skip to a learnable mixing
matrix; since these matrices are generically invertible
(Section
4
), all our results apply.

Geometry of ReLU networks.

Arora et al. (
1
) studied the reparametrization
symmetries of deep ReLU networks, showing that positive rescaling of
neurons preserves the computed function. Montufar et al. (
20
) analyzed the number of linear regions in deep
ReLU networks. Our necessity proof for the ReLU case builds on similar
geometric ideas, using the arrangement of kink hyperplanes to constrain
the relationship between weight matrices. For further background on
functional equivalence of ReLU networks, see Phuong and Lampert
(
23
).

Ridge functions and neural networks.

The uniqueness theorem for ridge function representations (Pinkus,

25
, Chapter 3; Buhmann and Pinkus,

2
) provides the key linear independence result
used in our GELU proof. The connection between ridge function theory and
neural network expressivity has been explored by several authors; see,
e.g., Pinkus (
24
) and Kainen and Kůrková
(
15
).

Preliminaries and Notation

We work with column vectors throughout. For matrices
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
, we write
A[:,S]
for the submatrix of
columns indexed by
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
B[S,:]
for the
submatrix of rows indexed by
S
. The coordinate projector onto
S
is the
diagonal matrix
\Pi_{S}\in\{0,1\}^{N\times N}
with
(\Pi_{S})_{ii}=1

if and only if
i\in S
, so that
A\,\Pi_{S}\,B=A[:,S]\,B[S,:]
. We
denote by
I_{d}
the
d\times d
identity matrix.

Definition 1

(Pairwise non-collinearity)
.

A collection of vectors
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is

pairwise non-collinear
if for all
i\neq j
, the vector
a_{i}
is
not a scalar multiple of
a_{j}
.

Definition 2

(Ridge function)
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a univariate
function. The function
\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
x\mapsto f(a^{\top}x)
is
called a
ridge function
with direction
a
.

Both the ReLU,
\operatorname{ReLU}(z)=\max(0,z)
, and the GELU,
\operatorname{GELU}(z)=z\,\Phi(z)
where
\Phi
denotes the standard normal CDF, are univariate
functions. Throughout this paper, if
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is univariate and

v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we write
f(v)
for the vector obtained by applying
f
to
each coordinate of
v
.

We consider single-hidden-layer MLPs of the form

\mathrm{MLP}(x)=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x),\qquad W_{\mathrm{up}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d},\;W_{\mathrm{down}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N},

(1)

where
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is an activation function and
N\geq d
.
Sections
6
and
7
treat this form with
\sigma=\operatorname{ReLU}
and
\sigma=\operatorname{GELU}
respectively.

Problem setup.

The absorption question asks when a skip-connected MLP can be rewritten as
a residual-free MLP of the same width. The answer differs sharply between
gated and ungated architectures: for gated MLPs, absorption is
unconditionally impossible for all weight matrices, so no measure-theoretic
framework is needed. For ungated MLPs, exact absorption is possible for
special weight configurations but fails generically, and stating this
precisely requires equipping the parameter space with a measure.

Gated MLPs.

Section
5
considers a gated architecture.

Definition 3

(Gated MLP)
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
N\geq d
, and let
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a univariate
function (the
gate activation
). A
single-hidden-layer gated
MLP
is a map
\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the form

x\mapsto W_{\mathrm{down}}\bigl(g(W_{\mathrm{gate}}\,x)\odot(W_{\mathrm{val}}\,x)\bigr),

where
W_{\mathrm{gate}},W_{\mathrm{val}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
,

W_{\mathrm{down}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
, and
\odot
denotes the
coordinatewise (Hadamard) product. This form involves three weight
matrices and does not reduce to (
1
).

SwiGLU (
g=\mathrm{SiLU}
) and GeGLU (
g=\operatorname{GELU}
), both introduced
by Shazeer (
28
), are instances of this definition.
The impossibility results for gated MLPs
(Propositions
5
and
6
,
Corollary
7
) hold for
every
choice of weight
matrices; no genericity assumption is required.

Ungated MLPs.

For ungated activations (
\sigma=\operatorname{ReLU}
or
\operatorname{GELU}
), the situation is
more delicate: absorption is possible for certain weight configurations and
impossible for others. To make this precise, the weight matrices

(W_{\mathrm{up}},W_{\mathrm{down}})
live in the parameter space

\Theta:=\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times N},

which we equip with Lebesgue measure. For a fixed activation
\sigma
,

\Theta
parametrizes two families of functions
\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{skip}}

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{x\mapsto W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)+x:(W_{\mathrm{up}},W_{\mathrm{down}})\in\Theta\bigr\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ff}}

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{x\mapsto V_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x):(V_{\mathrm{up}},V_{\mathrm{down}})\in\Theta\bigr\}.

The absorption question asks whether

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{skip}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ff}}
.
Theorems
8
and
10
give an exact characterization
for generic parameters, and Corollary
11
shows
that the set of parameters for which absorption holds has Lebesgue measure
zero in
\Theta
.

Parity decompositions.

Both main proofs rely on decomposing the activation into even and odd
parts. Writing
\operatorname{ReLU}(z)=\tfrac{1}{2}(z+|z|)
, we obtain

\operatorname{ReLU}(z)=\underbrace{|z|/2}_{\text{even}}\;+\;\underbrace{z/2}_{\text{odd}}.

(2)

For the GELU,
\operatorname{GELU}(z)=z\,\Phi(z)
, the identity
\Phi(-z)=1-\Phi(z)
gives
\operatorname{GELU}(z)-\operatorname{GELU}(-z)=z
. Defining the even component

E(z):=z(\Phi(z)-\tfrac{1}{2})
,

\operatorname{GELU}(z)=\underbrace{E(z)}_{\text{even}}+\underbrace{z/2}_{\text{odd}}.

(3)

In both cases, the odd part is exactly
z/2
. This shared structure
produces the identical algebraic condition in Theorems
8

and
10
.

4
Reduction to the Identity Skip

Modern architectures do not always use a pure identity skip. In
Hyper-Connections (Zhu et al.,
34
) and their
manifold-constrained variants (Xie et al.,
32
), the
skip branch is replaced by a learnable linear map
M
, yielding blocks of
the form

M\,x+W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x).

(4)

The following lemma shows that when
M
is invertible, the absorption
problem for (
4
) reduces to the standard identity skip
case.

Lemma 4

(Invertible reduction)
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
N\geq d
,
M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
invertible, and

\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
applied coordinatewise. For any
W_{\mathrm{up}},V_{\mathrm{up}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
and
W_{\mathrm{down}},V_{\mathrm{down}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
,

M\,x+W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)=V_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

if and only if

x+(M^{-1}W_{\mathrm{down}})\,\sigma(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)=(M^{-1}V_{\mathrm{down}})\,\sigma(V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

Left-multiply both sides by
M^{-1}
.
∎

For HC, the mixing matrices are unconstrained learnable parameters and
therefore generically invertible (the set of singular
d\times d

matrices has Lebesgue measure zero). For mHC, they are constrained to the
Birkhoff polytope of doubly stochastic matrices; since

\det
is a non-trivial polynomial on this polytope (the identity is doubly
stochastic with
\det=1
), the singular locus has measure zero, so mHC
matrices are also generically invertible.
Consequently, all results in this paper apply to these architectures with
the down-projection matrices replaced by
M^{-1}W_{\mathrm{down}}
and

M^{-1}V_{\mathrm{down}}
.

We therefore study, without loss of generality for the invertible case,
the identity skip absorption problem

x+W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)=V_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(5)

which is the object of the remainder of the paper.

5
Impossibility for Modern Activations

For the activation functions used in current state-of-the-art models,
absorption is unconditionally impossible. We establish this through two
complementary arguments: a homogeneity argument covering
\operatorname{ReLU}^{2}

(So et al.,
29
), used in the Nemotron family

(
21
)
and the modded-nanogpt codebase

(
14
)
; and a Taylor expansion argument
covering the gated activations SwiGLU and GeGLU

(
28
)
: SwiGLU is used in LLaMA

(
30
;
5
)
, DeepSeek
(
3
)
, Mistral

(
13
)
, and Qwen
(
26
)
; GeGLU in Gemma

(
7
)
.

Proposition 5

(Impossibility for homogeneous activations of degree
k\neq 1
)
.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be homogeneous of degree
k>0

with
k\neq 1
, i.e.,
\sigma(\lambda z)=\lambda^{k}\sigma(z)
for all

\lambda>0
. Then for any
N\geq d\geq 1
, any matrices

W_{\mathrm{up}},V_{\mathrm{up}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
and

W_{\mathrm{down}},V_{\mathrm{down}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
, and any

M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with
M\neq 0
,

M\,x+W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)\neq V_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)

for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction the identity holds for all
x
. Evaluating at

\lambda x
for
\lambda>0
:

\lambda\,M\,x+\lambda^{k}\,W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)=\lambda^{k}\,V_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x).

When
k>1
, dividing by
\lambda
and letting
\lambda\to 0
gives

Mx=0
for all
x
, contradicting
M\neq 0
. When
0<k<1
,
dividing by
\lambda^{k}
and letting
\lambda\to\infty
gives the same
contradiction.
∎

A degree-
k
MLP (
k\neq 1
) and a linear map live in incompatible
homogeneity classes, so their sum can never equal a pure degree-
k

expression. This covers
\operatorname{ReLU}^{2}
and also ReGLU, whose neurons compute

\operatorname{ReLU}(a_{i}^{\top}x)\cdot(b_{i}^{\top}x)
and are therefore homogeneous of
degree 2.

The dominant modern activations, SwiGLU and GeGLU, are not homogeneous,
but a linearization argument yields the same conclusion.

Proposition 6

(Impossibility for gated activations)
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
N\geq d
, and let
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be differentiable at
the origin with
g(0)=0
. Then for any
M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with

M\neq 0
, and any single-hidden-layer gated MLPs
(Definition
3
) with weight matrices

W_{\mathrm{down}},W_{\mathrm{gate}},W_{\mathrm{val}}
and

V_{\mathrm{down}},V_{\mathrm{gate}},V_{\mathrm{val}}
,

M\,x+W_{\mathrm{down}}\bigl(g(W_{\mathrm{gate}}\,x)\odot(W_{\mathrm{val}}\,x)\bigr)\neq V_{\mathrm{down}}\bigl(g(V_{\mathrm{gate}}\,x)\odot(V_{\mathrm{val}}\,x)\bigr)

for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Since
g(0)=0
and
g
is differentiable at
0
, we have
g(z)=g^{\prime}(0)\,z+o(z)
as
z\to 0
. Each gated neuron satisfies

g(w_{\mathrm{gate},i}^{\top}x)\cdot(w_{\mathrm{val},i}^{\top}x)=g^{\prime}(0)\,(w_{\mathrm{gate},i}^{\top}x)(w_{\mathrm{val},i}^{\top}x)+o(\|x\|)\cdot O(\|x\|),

so the full gated MLP output is
O(\|x\|^{2})
as
x\to 0
. In the
absorption equation, both MLP terms are
O(\|x\|^{2})
, hence
M\,x=O(\|x\|^{2})
. Since
M\,x
is linear in
x
, this forces
M=0
.
∎

Both impossibility results extend from single blocks to compositions of
arbitrary depth.

Corollary 7

(Depth extension)
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
N\geq d
, and
L\geq 1
. Let
\sigma
be either
homogeneous of degree
k>1
(applied coordinatewise) or a gated
activation with
g
differentiable at the origin and
g(0)=0
. Consider
a composition of
L
residual blocks

F(x)=f_{L}\circ\cdots\circ f_{1}(x),\qquad f_{i}(x)=x+\mathrm{MLP}_{i}(x),

and a composition of
L
residual-free blocks

H(x)=h_{L}\circ\cdots\circ h_{1}(x),\qquad h_{i}(x)=\mathrm{MLP}_{i}^{\prime}(x),

all of width
N
. Then
F\neq H
.

Proof.

In both cases, the MLP output satisfies
\mathrm{MLP}(x)=O(\|x\|^{2})

near the origin. For the residual composition, the degree-1 Taylor term
passes through every block unchanged: the Jacobian of
f_{i}
at the origin
is
I_{d}
, so the Jacobian of
F
at the origin is
I_{d}
. For the
residual-free composition, the Jacobian of each
h_{i}
at the origin is

0
(since the MLP has no linear term), so the Jacobian of
H
at the
origin is
0
. These cannot agree.
∎

This settles the absorption question at arbitrary depth for every
activation function used in current state-of-the-art models:
\operatorname{ReLU}^{2}
,
ReGLU, SwiGLU, and GeGLU. A deep residual network using any of these
activations cannot be replicated by a deep residual-free network of the
same depth and width.

The remainder of the paper addresses the case where absorption is

sometimes
possible: ungated activations whose parity structure is
compatible with degree 1.

6
The ReLU Case

Theorem 8

(Identity absorption for ReLU MLPs)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
N\geq d
. Let
W_{\mathrm{up}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}

and
W_{\mathrm{down}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
be given matrices such that

W_{\mathrm{up}}
has pairwise non-collinear, non-zero rows and

W_{\mathrm{down}}
has non-zero columns. There exist matrices

V_{\mathrm{up}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
and
V_{\mathrm{down}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
such that

W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\operatorname{ReLU}(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)+x=V_{\mathrm{down}}\,\operatorname{ReLU}(V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(6)

if and only if there exists
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}
with
|S|\geq d

such that

W_{\mathrm{down}}[:,S]\,W_{\mathrm{up}}[S,:]=-I_{d}.

(7)

Proof.

We write
w_{i}^{\top}
for the
i
-th row of
W_{\mathrm{up}}
and

\hat{w}_{i}
for the
i
-th column of
W_{\mathrm{down}}
.

Sufficiency.

Suppose
S
satisfies (
7
). Define

D:=I_{N}-2\Pi_{S}
(a diagonal matrix of signs
\pm 1
) and set

V_{\mathrm{up}}:=D\,W_{\mathrm{up}}
,
V_{\mathrm{down}}:=W_{\mathrm{down}}
. Since
D
has entries
\pm 1
, we have

|V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x|=|W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x|
for all
x
. Using the
decomposition (
2
):

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{down}}\,\operatorname{ReLU}(V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,W_{\mathrm{down}}\bigl(D\,W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x+|W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x|\bigr),

\displaystyle W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\operatorname{ReLU}(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)+x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,W_{\mathrm{down}}\bigl(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x+|W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x|\bigr)+x.

These are equal if and only if

\tfrac{1}{2}W_{\mathrm{down}}(D-I_{N})W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x=x
for all

x
. Since
D-I_{N}=-2\Pi_{S}
, this reduces to

-W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\Pi_{S}\,W_{\mathrm{up}}=I_{d}
, which holds by
assumption.

Necessity.

Suppose (
6
) holds. Using
\operatorname{ReLU}(y)=\tfrac{1}{2}(y+|y|)
and rearranging:

\bigl(W_{\mathrm{down}}\,W_{\mathrm{up}}+2I_{d}-V_{\mathrm{down}}\,V_{\mathrm{up}}\bigr)\,x=V_{\mathrm{down}}\,|V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x|-W_{\mathrm{down}}\,|W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x|.

The left side is odd in
x
and the right side is even. Both must
therefore vanish identically, yielding:

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{down}}\,V_{\mathrm{up}}

\displaystyle=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,W_{\mathrm{up}}+2I_{d},

(R1)

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{down}}\,|V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x|

\displaystyle=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,|W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x|\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(R2)

Step 1: Hyperplane alignment.

The map
x\mapsto W_{\mathrm{down}}\,|W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x|
is
non-differentiable on the hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}_{W}=\bigcup_{i}\{x:w_{i}^{\top}x=0\}
. Since
W_{\mathrm{down}}
has
non-zero columns and
W_{\mathrm{up}}
has pairwise non-collinear rows,
these hyperplanes are all distinct and each genuinely contributes a kink.
By (
R2
), the non-differentiability loci must agree:

\mathcal{H}_{V}=\mathcal{H}_{W}
. Therefore each row of
V_{\mathrm{up}}

is collinear with some row of
W_{\mathrm{up}}
, and since both matrices
have
N
rows with pairwise non-collinear directions, the correspondence
is a bijection. After relabeling,
V_{\mathrm{up}}=C\,W_{\mathrm{up}}

where
C=\operatorname{diag}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{N})
with each
c_{i}\neq 0
.

Step 2: Coefficient matching.

Substituting
V_{\mathrm{up}}=C\,W_{\mathrm{up}}
into (
R2
)
and using
|C\,W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x|=|C|\,|W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x|
(since

|c_{i}\cdot z|=|c_{i}|\cdot|z|
), we obtain

V_{\mathrm{down}}\,|C|\,|W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x|=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,|W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x|\qquad\forall\,x.

Since
d\geq 2
and the rows of
W_{\mathrm{up}}
are pairwise
non-collinear, the functions
\{|w_{i}^{\top}x|\}_{i=1}^{N}
are linearly
independent over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
modulo linear functions (their kink hyperplanes are
distinct, so no nontrivial linear combination can be smooth). Therefore

V_{\mathrm{down}}\,|C|=W_{\mathrm{down}}
, giving
V_{\mathrm{down}}=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,|C|^{-1}
.

Step 3: Sign extraction.

Substituting both expressions into (
R1
):

W_{\mathrm{down}}\,|C|^{-1}\,C\,W_{\mathrm{up}}=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,W_{\mathrm{up}}+2I_{d}.

Let
\Sigma:=|C|^{-1}C=\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{sign}(c_{i}))\in\{\pm 1\}^{N\times N}
.
Then

W_{\mathrm{down}}\,(\Sigma-I_{N})\,W_{\mathrm{up}}=2I_{d}.

The diagonal of
\Sigma-I_{N}
has entries
0
(when
c_{i}>0
) or
-2

(when
c_{i}<0
). Setting
S=\{i:c_{i}<0\}
gives
\Sigma-I_{N}=-2\Pi_{S}
, so

W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\Pi_{S}\,W_{\mathrm{up}}=-I_{d}.

By Sylvester’s rank inequality,
d=\operatorname{rank}(I_{d})\leq|S|
.
∎

7
The GELU Case

The GELU proof requires one additional tool: a linear independence result
for scaled analytic functions.

Lemma 9

(Independence of scaled analytic functions)
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be analytic and not a polynomial, and let

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}>0
be pairwise distinct. If

\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}\,\psi(\alpha_{k}\,t)=p(t)\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}

for some polynomial
p
and constants
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
, then

c_{k}=0
for all
k
.

Proof.

Write
\psi(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\,t^{n}
. Since
\psi
is not a
polynomial,
a_{n}\neq 0
for infinitely many
n
. Then
\sum_{k}c_{k}\,\psi(\alpha_{k}\,t)=\sum_{n}a_{n}\bigl(\sum_{k}c_{k}\,\alpha_{k}^{n}\bigr)t^{n}
.
For this to equal a polynomial of degree at most
D
, we need

a_{n}\sum_{k}c_{k}\,\alpha_{k}^{n}=0
for all
n>D
. Since
a_{n}\neq 0
for
infinitely many such
n
, we obtain
\sum_{k}c_{k}\,\alpha_{k}^{n}=0
for
infinitely many
n
. Choosing any
m
such values
n_{1}<\cdots<n_{m}

gives the linear system
\sum_{k}c_{k}\,\alpha_{k}^{n_{j}}=0
for
j=1,\ldots,m
. The coefficient matrix
[\alpha_{k}^{n_{j}}]_{j,k}
is a
generalized Vandermonde matrix with distinct positive bases
\alpha_{k}
,
hence invertible. Therefore
c_{k}=0
for all
k
.
∎

Theorem 10

(Identity absorption for GELU MLPs)
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
N\geq d
. Let
W_{\mathrm{up}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}

and
W_{\mathrm{down}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
be given matrices such that

W_{\mathrm{up}}
has pairwise non-collinear, non-zero rows and
W_{\mathrm{down}}

has non-zero columns. There exist matrices
V_{\mathrm{up}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
and
V_{\mathrm{down}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
such that

W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\operatorname{GELU}(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)+x=V_{\mathrm{down}}\,\operatorname{GELU}(V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(8)

if and only if there exists
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}
with
|S|\geq d

such that

W_{\mathrm{down}}[:,S]\,W_{\mathrm{up}}[S,:]=-I_{d}.

(9)

Proof.

Sufficiency.

Suppose
S
satisfies (
9
). Set
D:=I_{N}-2\Pi_{S}

and define
V_{\mathrm{up}}:=D\,W_{\mathrm{up}}
,
V_{\mathrm{down}}:=W_{\mathrm{down}}
. Since
D
has entries
\pm 1
and
E(z)=z(\Phi(z)-\tfrac{1}{2})
is an even function, i.e.,
E(-z)=E(z)
, the parity
decomposition (
3
) gives:

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{down}}\,\operatorname{GELU}(V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)

\displaystyle=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,E(D\,W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)+\tfrac{1}{2}\,W_{\mathrm{down}}\,D\,W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x

\displaystyle=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,E(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)+\tfrac{1}{2}\,W_{\mathrm{down}}\,(I_{N}-2\Pi_{S})\,W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x,

where the first equality uses the evenness of
E
, so
E(s_{i}\,w_{i}^{\top}x)=E(w_{i}^{\top}x)
for
s_{i}=\pm 1
. Expanding:

\displaystyle=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,E(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)+\tfrac{1}{2}\,W_{\mathrm{down}}\,W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x-W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\Pi_{S}\,W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x

\displaystyle=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\operatorname{GELU}(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)+I_{d}\,x=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\operatorname{GELU}(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)+x.

Necessity.

Suppose (
8
) holds. Applying the parity
decomposition to both sides and separating even and odd parts (which are
uniquely determined):

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{down}}\,E(V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)

\displaystyle=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,E(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x),

(G1)

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{down}}\,V_{\mathrm{up}}

\displaystyle=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,W_{\mathrm{up}}+2I_{d}.

(G2)

Steps 1–2: Direction alignment, scaling, and coefficient
matching.
We show that, after relabeling,
V_{\mathrm{up}}=C\,W_{\mathrm{up}}
with
C=\operatorname{diag}(\pm 1)
and
V_{\mathrm{down}}=W_{\mathrm{down}}
.

We use the Pinkus uniqueness theorem (Pinkus,

25
, Chapter 3; see also Buhmann and Pinkus,

2
): if
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
are
pairwise non-collinear and
\sum_{i=1}^{M}f_{i}(a_{i}^{\top}x)=0
for all

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with each
f_{i}
analytic, then every
f_{i}
is a polynomial of
degree at most
M-2
.

Projecting (
G1
) onto the
\ell
-th output coordinate and
rearranging:

\sum_{j=1}^{N}(V_{\mathrm{down}})_{\ell,j}\,E(v_{j}^{\top}x)-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(W_{\mathrm{down}})_{\ell,i}\,E(w_{i}^{\top}x)=0.

(10)

Since
E
is even, group the
2N
terms by collinearity class of their
directions. The
w_{i}
contribute
N
distinct classes, and each
v_{j}
falls
into one of these classes or introduces a new one. For each class, define
the combined univariate function
F(t)
as the sum of all terms in that
class (with their coefficients). Then (
10
) becomes

\sum_{k}F_{k}(a_{k}^{\top}x)=0
, where
a_{k}
ranges over pairwise
non-collinear class representatives. By the Pinkus theorem, each
F_{k}
is
a polynomial. Applying Lemma
9
to each
F_{k}
:
within each class, the functions
E(\alpha\,t)
for distinct positive

\alpha
are linearly independent modulo polynomials, so the coefficient of
each distinct scaling must vanish separately.

We now draw three consequences.

First, every
v_{j}
must be collinear with some
w_{i}
. Indeed, if a
collinearity class contains only
v_{j}
’s, its combined function is
polynomial, and Lemma
9

forces
(V_{\mathrm{down}})_{\ell,j}=0
for all
\ell
and all
j
in
that class. Such
v_{j}
’s contribute nothing to either (
G1
) or
(
G2
) and may be discarded.

Second, for each
w_{i}
, there exists a
v_{j}
collinear with
w_{i}

satisfying
v_{j}=\pm w_{i}
, and

(V_{\mathrm{down}})_{\ell,j}=(W_{\mathrm{down}})_{\ell,i}
for all

\ell
. To see this, note that the
w_{i}
are pairwise non-collinear, so
exactly one
w_{i}
lies in each class, contributing

-(W_{\mathrm{down}})_{\ell,i}\,E(\|w_{i}\|\,t)
. Since

W_{\mathrm{down}}
has non-zero columns, this coefficient is nonzero for
some
\ell
. By the scaling independence, some
v_{j}
in the same class must
have
\|v_{j}\|=\|w_{i}\|
, i.e.,
v_{j}=\pm w_{i}
, with

(V_{\mathrm{down}})_{\ell,j}=(W_{\mathrm{down}})_{\ell,i}
. If
multiple
v_{j}
’s share the same scaling
\|v_{j}\|=\|w_{i}\|
, their
contributions merge into a single term, so without loss of generality
there is one contributing
v_{j}
per class. Any
v_{j}
’s in the class with
different scalings are forced to have

(V_{\mathrm{down}})_{\ell,j}=0
for all
\ell
; since the entire

j
-th column of
V_{\mathrm{down}}
vanishes, such
v_{j}
’s contribute
nothing to either (
G1
) or (
G2
) and may be discarded.

Third, the matching is a bijection. Each of the
N
classes for
w_{i}

acquires exactly one contributing
v_{j}
, giving
N
contributing
v_{j}
’s
distributed among
N
classes. After relabeling,
V_{\mathrm{up}}=C\,W_{\mathrm{up}}
with
C=\operatorname{diag}(\pm 1)
and
V_{\mathrm{down}}=W_{\mathrm{down}}
.

Step 3: Sign extraction (identical to the ReLU case).

Substituting
V_{\mathrm{up}}=C\,W_{\mathrm{up}}
and
V_{\mathrm{down}}=W_{\mathrm{down}}
into (
G2
):

W_{\mathrm{down}}\,C\,W_{\mathrm{up}}=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,W_{\mathrm{up}}+2I_{d},

hence
W_{\mathrm{down}}\,(C-I_{N})\,W_{\mathrm{up}}=2I_{d}
. Since

C=\operatorname{diag}(\pm 1)
, the matrix
C-I_{N}
has diagonal entries
0
or

-2
. Setting
S=\{i:c_{i}=-1\}
gives
C-I_{N}=-2\Pi_{S}
, so

W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\Pi_{S}\,W_{\mathrm{up}}=-I_{d}.

By Sylvester’s rank inequality,
|S|\geq d
.
∎

8
Generic Disjointness of Function Classes

The submatrix condition
W_{\mathrm{down}}[:,S]\,W_{\mathrm{up}}[S,:]=-I_{d}
imposes
d^{2}
polynomial constraints on the weights. Since there are
only finitely many subsets
S
, the set of parameters in
\Theta

satisfying the condition for some
S
has Lebesgue measure zero. This
yields the following.

Corollary 11

(Generic disjointness)
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
N\geq d
, and
\sigma
be
\operatorname{ReLU}
or
\operatorname{GELU}
. For
Lebesgue-almost every
(W_{\mathrm{up}},W_{\mathrm{down}})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
, there
exist no matrices
V_{\mathrm{up}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
and

V_{\mathrm{down}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
such that

W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)+x=V_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(V_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In other words, for Lebesgue-almost every parameter in
\Theta
, the
corresponding element of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{skip}}
does not belong to

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ff}}
, and vice versa. The two families are
generically disjoint, intersecting only on a measure-zero set in parameter
space.

The following corollary extends the disjointness result to general linear
perturbations of the skip connection.

Corollary 12

(Uniqueness under invertible residual perturbations)
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
N\geq d
, and
\sigma
be
\operatorname{ReLU}
or
\operatorname{GELU}
. For matrices

W_{\mathrm{up}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
,
W_{\mathrm{down}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
, and
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, define

\phi_{W_{\mathrm{up}},W_{\mathrm{down}},Z}(x):=W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)+Z\,x.

For Lebesgue-almost every
(W_{\mathrm{up}},W_{\mathrm{down}},Z)
: if

Z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is such that
Z-Z^{\prime}
is invertible, then for
all
W_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
and
W_{\mathrm{down}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
,

\phi_{W_{\mathrm{up}},W_{\mathrm{down}},Z}\neq\phi_{W_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime},W_{\mathrm{down}}^{\prime},Z^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
\phi_{W_{\mathrm{up}},W_{\mathrm{down}},Z}=\phi_{W_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime},W_{\mathrm{down}}^{\prime},Z^{\prime}}
. Then for all
x
:

W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,x)+(Z-Z^{\prime})\,x=W_{\mathrm{down}}^{\prime}\,\sigma(W_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime}\,x).

Since
Z-Z^{\prime}
is invertible, substituting
y=(Z-Z^{\prime})\,x
:

W_{\mathrm{down}}\,\sigma\bigl(W_{\mathrm{up}}\,(Z-Z^{\prime})^{-1}\,y\bigr)+y=W_{\mathrm{down}}^{\prime}\,\sigma\bigl(W_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime}\,(Z-Z^{\prime})^{-1}\,y\bigr).

Setting
A:=W_{\mathrm{down}}
and
B:=W_{\mathrm{up}}\,(Z-Z^{\prime})^{-1}
,
the left side has the form
A\,\sigma(B\,y)+y
. By the necessity
direction of Theorem
8
(or Theorem
10
), there
must exist
S
with
|S|\geq d
such that
A[:,S]\,B[S,:]=-I_{d}
.
Right-multiplying by
Z-Z^{\prime}
:

W_{\mathrm{down}}[:,S]\,W_{\mathrm{up}}[S,:]=Z^{\prime}-Z.

For fixed
S
, this imposes
d^{2}
algebraic constraints on

(W_{\mathrm{up}},W_{\mathrm{down}},Z)
. The set of parameters
satisfying these constraints for some
S
(finitely many choices) has
Lebesgue measure zero. For generic parameters, no such
S
exists, giving
the desired contradiction.
∎

Remark 13
.

All results in this paper (Theorems
8
and
10
,
Corollaries
11

and
12
) hold for any
N\geq d
.

9
Discussion

Ungated activations and the parity condition.

The identical algebraic condition in Theorems
8

and
10
is not a coincidence. Both ReLU and GELU admit parity
decompositions whose odd part is exactly
z/2
. Any ungated activation

\sigma(z)
with the property
\sigma(z)-\sigma(-z)=z
will yield the
same submatrix condition for identity absorption, provided the even part

E(z)=(\sigma(z)+\sigma(-z))/2
generates linearly independent ridge
functions for non-collinear directions. Ungated SiLU/Swish
\sigma(z)=z/(1+e^{-z})
satisfies this condition, though it is rarely used without
gating in practice.

Gated activations.

The dominant modern architecture uses gated MLPs: SwiGLU in LLaMA

(
30
;
5
)
, DeepSeek
(
3
)
, Mistral

(
13
)
, and Qwen
(
26
)
; GeGLU in Gemma
(
7
)
.
Proposition
6
shows that absorption is unconditionally
impossible for all of these. Combined with
Proposition
5
(which covers
\operatorname{ReLU}^{2}
and ReGLU),
this means that among activations deployed at scale, only ungated GELU
(GPT-2; Radford et al.,
27
) and ungated ReLU admit
non-trivial absorption, and even then only non-generically.

Open problem: deep compositions with ReLU and GELU.

Corollary
7
settles the multi-layer question for
homogeneous and gated activations. For ungated ReLU and GELU, the
single-block characterization (Theorems
8

and
10
) does not extend to depth in the same way. The
degree argument fails because ungated ReLU and GELU have a linear term
(
z/2
) in their Taylor expansion, so each residual-free block has a
nonzero Jacobian at the origin, and a composition of
L
such blocks can
produce a nontrivial linear map. Whether a composition of
L
residual
ReLU or GELU blocks can always be matched by a composition of
L

residual-free blocks of the same width remains open.

Rank-deficient mixing matrices.

Lemma
4
assumes
M
is invertible. If
\operatorname{rank}(M)=r<d
, write the SVD
M=U\,\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r},0,\ldots,0)\,V^{\top}
. In rotated coordinates
y=V^{\top}x
, the equation
decomposes: on the first
r
coordinates, the skip has nonzero
coefficients
\sigma_{i}
and can be normalized to identity by absorbing

\sigma_{i}^{-1}
into the output projections, reducing to the standard
form. On the remaining
d-r
coordinates, the skip connection vanishes
and the equation asks when two MLPs compute the same function on that
subspace. For homogeneous activations of degree
k\neq 1

(Proposition
5
), the degree argument applies to any

x\notin\ker(M)
, so absorption remains impossible whenever
M\neq 0
.

Structural implications.

The condition
W_{\mathrm{down}}[:,S]\,W_{\mathrm{up}}[S,:]=-I_{d}

requires
|S|\geq d
neurons whose weights produce a rank-
d

factorization of
-I_{d}
. In networks with large hidden dimension
(
N\gg d
), the number of candidate subsets
S
grows, but the condition
remains a measure-zero event under continuous weight distributions. This
means that a residual MLP block generically cannot be rewritten as a
single feedforward map of the same width: the skip connection accesses a
different region of function space, not merely a reparametrization of the
same region. Any method that attempts to represent a skip-connected block
as a residual-free network must either increase width or accept
approximation error.

Approximate absorption and future work.

Since exact absorption is non-generic for ReLU and GELU, and impossible
for all other activations considered here, a natural question is how well

approximate
absorption can perform: given a skip-connected MLP, how
closely can a residual-free MLP of the same width approximate it?
Karbevski and Mijoski (
16
) present experiments in
this direction for the GELU activation. A systematic study of the
approximation error as a function of width, depth, activation, and weight
structure remains an important direction for future work.
