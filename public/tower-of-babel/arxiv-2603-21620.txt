Abstract
. In 2014, Juul, Kurlberg, Madhu and Tucker asked the following: given K K a number field and f f a rational function with coefficients in K K , if f 𝔭 f_{\mathfrak{p}} denotes the reduction of f f modulo a prime ideal 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} in the ring of integers of K K , what is the limit inferior of the proportion of periodic points of f 𝔭 f_{\mathfrak{p}} when the norm of 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} goes to infinity? Recent results of Fariña-Asategui and the author show that when f f is a polynomial of degree d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 non-linearly conjugate over ℂ \mathbb{C} to a Chebyshev polynomial then the limit is zero. In this article, we address the remaining cases to give a complete classification of the problem in the case of polynomials.

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a number field,
\mathcal{O}_{K}
its ring of integers and
f
a polynomial with coefficients in
K
. For all but finitely many prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, we can consider the reduction map
f_{\mathfrak{p}}
acting on the residue field
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}
.

As
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a finite field, a point
x\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is either periodic or strictly preperiodic for
f_{\mathfrak{p}}
. How does the proportion of periodic points varies as the norm of the prime
\mathfrak{p}
goes to infinity?

The first problem in this direction was formulated by Schur in 1923, who asked for a classification of all polynomials with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
whose reduction is a bijection for infinitely many primes
Schur1923
(note that if the reduction is a bijection, every point must be periodic and consequently the proportion of periodic points is
1
). The question was answered in 1970 by Fried over any number field
K
, showing that a polynomial whose reduction is a bijection for infinitely many primes must be a composition of linear polynomials with coefficients in
K
and Dickson polynomials, where Dickson polynomials are defined by
f(x+a/x)=x^{n}+a^{n}/x^{n}
with
a\in K
; see
Fried1970
. In 2002, Guralnick, Müller and Saxl generalized Fried’s result to rational functions over any number field, using the theory of arithmetic and geometric iterated Galois groups
GuralnickMullerSaxl2003
. These maps are referred in
GuralnickMullerSaxl2003
as arithmetically exceptional.

Although we find infinitely many primes where the proportion of periodic points is
1
for arithmetically exceptional maps, the proportion of periodic points for the other primes might be arbitrarily small. In fact, this is what we expect to happen in a random map. In
FlajoletOdlyzko1990
, Flajolet and Odlyzko proved that if
f:S\rightarrow S
is a random map, where
S
is finite, the number of periodic points is asymptotically

\sqrt{\frac{\pi\cdot\#S}{2}}

when
\#S\rightarrow+\infty
. Therefore, based on this heuristic, we would expect to find prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
where the reduction
f_{\mathfrak{p}}
has a proportion of periodic points arbitrarily small. Unfortunately, the assumption that the reductions of a polynomial behave randomly is far from being true. This leads to the following question:

Question 1.1

(
(
Juul2014
, Question 1.1)
)
.

Given
K
a number field and
f\in K[x]
a polynomial with coefficients in
K
. Denote
\Per(f,\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}):={\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}:\alpha\text{ is periodic for }f_{\mathfrak{p}}\right\}}
. What is the value of

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(f,K):=\liminf_{N(\mathfrak{p})\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\#\Per(f,\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})}{N(\mathfrak{p})}?

1.1
was firstly formulated in 2014 by Juul, Kurlberg, Madhu and Tucker
Juul2014
, who asked for a classification of the values of
\Per_{\inf}(f,K)
for every number field
K
and any rational function
f\in K(x)
;
(
Juul2014
, Question 1.4)
.

In
Juul2014
, the authors proved that

\Per_{\inf}(x^{d}+c,K)=0

for any
d\geq 2
and
c\in K
such that
0
is not preperiodic, allowing them to give a complete classification of the problem in the case of quadratic polynomials over
\mathbb{Q}
;
(
Juul2014
, Theorem 1.5)
. In
(
Juul2014
, Theorem 1.3)
, they also proved that if
f
has two critical points whose forward orbits do not intersect, then
\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=0
.

It is expected that in general
\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=0
and this has been confirmed in most of the examples known. In accordance with this, in
(
Juul2014
, Theorem 1.2)
authors showed that given
K
a number field and numbers
\epsilon>0
and
d\geq 2
, there is an open Zariski dense set
U_{d,\epsilon}
in the moduli space of rational functions such that

\Per_{\inf}(f,K)\leq\epsilon

for all
f\in U_{d,\epsilon}
.

The only cases known where
\Per_{\inf}(f,K)>0
correspond to the Chebyshev polynomials. Recall that the Chebyshev polynomial of degree
d
, denoted
T_{d}
, is defined as the unique polynomial with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle T_{d}\left(x+\frac{1}{x}\right)=x^{d}+\frac{1}{x^{d}}.

In
(
Juul2014
, Example 7.2)
is proved that if
d\geq 2
then

(2)

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},\mathbb{Q})=\left\{\begin{matrix}1/2&\mbox{if $d$ is an odd prime power,}\\
1/4&\mbox{if $d$ is an even prime power,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{matrix}\right.

In this article, we give a complete answer to
1.1
. The cases where
f
is a polynomial of degree
d=0
or
1
are straightforward and will be solved in
Lemma

3.1
.
When
d\geq 2
, we rely on the tools of arboreal representations, particularly, the results related to iterated Galois groups and fixed-point proportion. In 1985, Odoni initiated the study of iterated Galois groups and fixed-point proportion to solve problems in number theory
Odoni1980
. In his pioneering article, he developed these tools to prove that the Dirichlet density of prime numbers dividing at least one term in the Sylvester sequence (defined recursively as
w_{0}=2
and
w_{n+1}=1+w_{1}\dots w_{n}
) is zero. His idea was then generalized to solve other problems in Number theory. Specifically, in 2014 Juul, Kurlberg, Madhu and Tucker proved that

(3)

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(f,K)\leq\FPP(G_{\infty}(\mathbb{C},f,t)),

where
\FPP
is the fixed-point proportion,
G_{\infty}(\mathbb{C},f,t)
is the iterated Galois group of
f
over
\mathbb{C}
and
t
is transcendental over
\mathbb{C}
(see
Section

3
for the unexplained terms here and
Jones2014
for a survey about the topic).

The recent breakthrough of Fariña-Asategui and the author completely classifies the fixed-point proportion for geometric iterated Galois groups of polynomials
(
Fari~naRadi2026FPP
, Theorem 2)
. As
\mathbb{C}
is algebraically closed, the result applies to this case obtaining that

\FPP(G_{\infty}(\mathbb{C},f,t))=0
if
f
is not linearly conjugate over
\mathbb{C}
to
\pm T_{d}
. Therefore

\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=0

if
f
is not linearly conjugate over
\mathbb{C}
to
\pm T_{d}
.

In the case where
f
is linearly conjugate over
\mathbb{C}
to
\pm T_{d}
, then the fixed-point proportion of
G_{\infty}(\mathbb{C},f,t)
is positive and therefore the upper bound in
Equation

3
is not enough to calculate
\Per_{\inf}(f,K)
. For these cases, we will work out
\Per_{\inf}(f,K)
directly. We will prove that if
f
is linearly conjugate over
\mathbb{C}
to
\pm T_{d}
then

(4)

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K).

Although this step can look obvious, it is not obvious if the conjugation does not have coefficients in
K
. We shall see in
Section

5
that when the conjugation is not in
K
, it lies in a field extension
K^{\prime}/K
of degree
2
. If
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
that is inert in
\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime}}
, then the periodic points of
f
in
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
are in bijection with the periodic points of
T_{d}
. But if
\mathfrak{p}
is split in
\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime}}
, then the periodic points of
f
are in bijection with the periodic points of
T_{d}
in a 2-degree extension of
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and some work needs to be done to prove that
\Per_{\inf}(f,K)
does not change when we restrict to the periodic points of
f
in
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
(see
Proposition

5.2
for a proof of this fact).

Once we know
Equation

4
, we reduce the problem to calculate
\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)
. The calculation is based on the ideas used by the authors in
(
Juul2014
, Example 7.2)
for the case of
K=\mathbb{Q}
, obtaining that

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)=\frac{1}{2}\liminf_{N(\mathfrak{p})\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{r(N(\mathfrak{p})-1,d)+r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)}{N(\mathfrak{p})},

where
r(a,b)
is defined as the largest divisor of
a
coprime to
b
.

So, in order to calculate
\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)
we need to find infinitely many prime ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
N(\mathfrak{p})-1
and
N(\mathfrak{p})+1
have large divisors that are not coprime to
d
, since this will make

r(N(\mathfrak{p})-1,d)
and
r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)
relatively small compared to
N(\mathfrak{p})
. The main tool to find such prime ideals will be Cebotarev’s theorem. However, the existence of roots of unity in
K
will impose restrictions of the prime factors dividing
N(\mathfrak{p})-1
for all but finitely many prime ideals. Concretely, if
m_{K}
is the maximal positive number such that
K
contains a
m_{K}
th root of unity, then for all but finitely many prime ideals we have
m_{K}
divides
N(\mathfrak{p})-1
. The relation between the prime factors of
m_{K}
and
d
will allow to reduce more or less
r(N(\mathfrak{p})-1,d)
and
r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)
, yielding to cases where
\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)
is zero and others where
\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)
is positive.

In order to state the main theorem, recall that the radical of a number
d
, denoted
\rad(d)
, is the product of the prime divisors of
d
. Therefore, note that when we say that
\rad(d)\mid\rad(m)
, this means that all prime divisors of
d
also divide
m
. Contrarily, when we say
\rad(d)\nmid\rad(m)
, this means that there exists one prime
p
such that
p\mid d
and
p\nmid m
. Given a prime
p
, the valuation of
m
at
p
will be denoted
v_{p}(m)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
m_{K}
denote the greatest positive number such that
K
contains a
m_{K}
th primitive root of unity. Let
f
be a polynomial with coefficients in
K
and degree
d
.

(1)

If
d=0
then

\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=0.

(2)

If
d=1
then

\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=1.

(3)

If
d\geq 2
and
f
is not linearly conjugate over
\mathbb{C}
to
\pm T_{d}
, then

\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=0.

(4)

If
d\geq 2
and
f
is linearly conjugate over
\mathbb{C}
to
\pm T_{d}
, then

\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K).

(5)

If
d
has at least two prime factors and
\rad(d)\nmid\rad(m_{K})
, then

\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)=0.

(6)

If
d
is odd, has at least two prime factors and
\rad(d)\mid\rad(m_{K})
, then

\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)=\frac{1}{2}.

(7)

If
d
is even, has at least two prime factors,
\rad(d)\mid\rad(m_{K})
and
v_{2}(m_{K})\geq 2
, then

\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)=\frac{1}{4}.

(8)

If
d
is even, has at least two prime factors,
\rad(d)\mid\rad(m_{K})
and
v_{2}(m_{K})=1
, then

\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)=0.

(9)

If
d
is an odd prime power, then

\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)=\frac{1}{2}.

(10)

If
d
is an even prime power, then

\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)=\frac{1}{4}.

In the case that
K=\mathbb{Q}
then
m_{K}=2
and only the cases (1), (2), (3), (4), (5), (9) and (10) apply. Moreover, the results obtained in
1
for the case
K=\mathbb{Q}
are consistent with
2
.

Corollary 2
.

If
f
is a polynomial with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
and degree
d\geq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(f,\mathbb{Q})=\left\{\begin{matrix}1&\mbox{if $d=1$,}\\
1/2&\mbox{if $d$ odd prime power and $f$ conjugate over $\mathbb{C}$ to $\pm T_{d}$,}\\
1/4&\mbox{if $d$ even prime power and $f$ conjugate over $\mathbb{C}$ to $\pm T_{d}$ ,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{matrix}\right.

Although
Equation

3
also holds in the case of rational functions, there is not a complete classification of the fixed-point proportion of
G_{\infty}(\mathbb{C},f,t)
in the case of rational maps. In
Fari~naRadi2026FPP
, the authors proved that
\FPP(G_{\infty}(\mathbb{C},f,t))=0
for rational maps whose critically exceptional set does not have more than one element (see
(
Fari~naRadi2026FPP
, Theorem 6)
) and conjectured that the cases of positive fixed-point proportion should come from rational maps with euclidean orbifold of type different to
(\infty,\infty)
(see
(
Fari~naRadi2026FPP
, Conjecture 7)
). This latter family includes Lattès maps
L
, although the value of
\FPP(G_{\infty}(\mathbb{C},L,t))
is not known for any of them. Regarding
\Per_{\inf}(L,K)
, authors in
(
Juul2014
, Example 7.3)
proved that in many cases, Lattès maps
L
induced by multiplication by a prime
\ell
in a elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
must have
\Per_{\inf}(L,K)=0
. Unlike Chebyshev polynomials, there are not known cases of Lattès maps such that
\Per_{\inf}(L,K)>0
. Therefore, it still remains open a whole classification in the case of rational functions:

Question 1.2
.

What are the values of
\Per_{\inf}(f,K)
for every number field
K
and every rational function
f\in K(x)
?

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Iteration of maps and local degree

Given a set
S
and a function
f:S\rightarrow S
, we use the notation
f^{n}
to refer to the
n
th composition of
f
with itself. By convention,
f^{0}
equals the identity map in
S
.

Given
\alpha\in S
, we say that
\alpha
is
preperiodic
if there exist
m>n\geq 0
such that
f^{n}(\alpha)=f^{m}(\alpha)
. By the pigeonhole principle, if
S
is finite every point is preperiodic. We say that
\alpha
is
periodic
if
n
can be taken zero in the definition of preperiodic. We denote

\Per(f,S):={\left\{\alpha\in S:\text{ $\alpha$ is periodic for $f$}\right\}}.

We say that a point
\alpha
is
strictly preperiodic
if it is PREperiodic but not periodic.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
F
be a field,
\overline{F}
an algebraic closure of
F
and
f,g\in\overline{F}(x)
. We say that
f
and
g
are
linearly conjugate
over
F
if there exists
L\in F(x)
of degree
1
such that
g=L^{-1}\circ f\circ L
.

Given
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
and
f\in F(x)
, the
local degree
of
\alpha
at
f
is

e_{f}(\alpha):=\min{\left\{k\geq 1:f^{(k)}(\alpha)\neq 0\right\}}

where
f^{(k)}
corresponds to the
k
th derivative of
f
. We say that
\alpha
is a
critical point
for
f
if
e_{f}(\alpha)>1
. We say that
\alpha
is
totally ramified
for
f
if
e_{f}(\alpha)=\deg(f)
. If
f
is a polynomial, then
\infty
is a totally ramified point.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
F
is a field, we have polynomials
f,g\in F[x]
linearly conjugate over
\overline{F}
by a function
L
and
f
has only
\infty
as a totally ramified point, then
L
is a polynomial.

Proof.

Write
g=L^{-1}\circ f\circ L
and suppose that
L
is not a polynomial. Then
L(\infty)\neq\infty
. By multiplicativity of the local degree and the fact that
e_{L}(\alpha)=1
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
, then

\deg(f)=e_{g}(\infty)=e_{L^{-1}}(f(L(\infty)))\cdot e_{f}(L(\infty))\cdot e_{L}(\infty)=e_{f}(L(\infty)),

which implies that
L(\infty)
is totally ramified for
f
giving a contradiction.
∎

2.2.
Algebraic number theory

In this subsection we summarize some results from algebraic number theory that we will use in the article. Given
n\geq 1
, let us denote
\xi_{n}
for a primitive
n
th root of unity.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K
be a number field,
n,m\geq 1
and suppose that
K
contains the primitive roots of unity
\xi_{m}
and
\xi_{n}
. Then
K
contains
\xi_{\lcm(m,n)}
.

Proof.

We use exponential notation. Taking powers if necessary, we may assume that

\xi_{m}=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{m}}\text{ and }\xi_{n}=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n}}.

By Bezout’s theorem there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
am+bn=\gcd(m,n)
. Then

\xi_{m}^{b}\xi_{n}^{a}=e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{b}{m}+\frac{a}{n}\right)}=e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{am+bn}{mn}\right)}=e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{\gcd(m,n)}{mn}\right)}=e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{1}{\lcm(m,n)}\right)}.\qed

As
K
is a number field, then
K
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and therefore
Lemma

2.3
justifies the existence of a maximal number
m_{K}\geq 1
such that
\xi_{m_{K}}
is in
K
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
K
a number field such that
\xi_{m_{K}}
is the maximal primitive root of unity contained in
K
. Then

\displaystyle\Gal\left(K(\xi_{n})/K\right)\cong{\left\{r\in(\mathbb{Z}/\lcm(n,m_{K})\mathbb{Z})^{\times}:r\equiv 1\pmod{m_{K}}\right\}}.

Proof.

By
Lemma

2.3

K(\xi_{n})=K(\xi_{\lcm(m_{K},n)})
. If
\sigma\in\Gal(K(\xi_{n})/K)
then

\sigma(\xi_{\lcm(m_{K},n)})=(\xi_{\lcm(m_{K},n)})^{\varphi(\sigma)}

for some
\varphi(\sigma)\in(\mathbb{Z}/\lcm(n,m_{K})\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Moreover, if
\lcm(m_{K},n)/c=m_{K}
then

\displaystyle\sigma(\xi_{m_{K}})=\sigma((\xi_{\lcm(m_{K},n)})^{c})=(\xi_{\lcm(m_{K},n)})^{\varphi(\sigma)c}=(\xi_{m_{K}})^{\varphi(\sigma)}.

But
\sigma
must fix
\xi_{m_{K}}
so
\varphi(\sigma)\equiv 1\pmod{m_{k}}
. By maximality of
m_{K}
, we obtain the bijection.
∎

The following result will be crucial in
Section

6
and is based on Cebotarev’s theorem:

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
K
be a number field and
\xi_{m_{K}}
the maximal primitive root of unity contained in K. Let
{\left\{n_{i}\right\}}_{i=1}^{k}
be a set of positive numbers and
{\left\{a_{i}\right\}}_{i=1}^{k}
another set of numbers such that
a_{i}\in(\mathbb{Z}/n_{i}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. If the system of congruences

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}x&\equiv&1\pmod{m_{K}}\\
x&\equiv&a_{1}\pmod{n_{1}}\\
&\vdots&\\
x&\equiv&a_{k}\pmod{n_{k}}\\
\end{matrix}\right.

has a solution in
\mathbb{Z}
, then for infinitely many prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
we have

n_{i}\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})-a_{i})

for all
i=1,\dots,k
.

Proof.

Let
r
be a solution and write
n=\lcm(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
. By the Chinese remainder theorem we have
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/\lcm(m_{K},n)\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. By
Lemma

2.4
, we have that
r
corresponds to an element
\sigma\in\Gal(K(\xi_{n})/K)
. Then, by Cebotarev’s theorem, there exist infinitely many prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that

\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}(\xi_{\lcm(m_{K},n)})=(\xi_{\lcm(m_{K},n)})^{r}.

Write
n_{i}=\lcm(m_{K},n)/c_{i}
. Then,

\displaystyle\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}(\xi_{n_{i}})=\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}((\xi_{\lcm(m_{K},n)})^{c_{i}})=(\xi_{\lcm(m_{K},n)})^{r\cdot c_{i}}=(\xi_{n_{i}})^{r}=(\xi_{n_{i}})^{a_{i}}.

On the other hand,

\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}(\xi_{n_{i}})=(\xi_{n_{i}})^{N(\mathfrak{p})},

so combining both equations we obtain that

N(\mathfrak{p})\equiv a_{i}\pmod{n_{i}}

which implies that

n_{i}\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})-a_{i}).\qed

2.3.
Chebyshev polynomials

Given
d\geq 0
, a field
F
and
\zeta\in\overline{F}
we define the
twisted Chebyshev polynomial
of degree
d
with parameter
\zeta
as

\displaystyle T_{d,\zeta}\left(x+\frac{\zeta}{x}\right):=x^{d}+\frac{\zeta}{x^{d}}.

Note that when
\zeta=1
, this corresponds to the standard Chebyshev polynomial defined in
Equation

1
.

When
d\geq 1
, then
\zeta
must be a
(d-1)
th root of unity. Indeed, replacing
x
by
1/x
we have:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^{d}}+\zeta x^{d}=f\left(\frac{1}{x}+\zeta x\right)=f\left(\frac{\zeta}{\zeta x}+\zeta x\right)=(\zeta x)^{d}+\frac{\zeta}{(\zeta x)^{d}}=\zeta^{d}x^{d}+\frac{1}{\zeta^{d-1}x^{d}}

which yields
\zeta^{d-1}=1
.

In
Adams2025
, Adams considered twisted Chebyshev polynomials as another family of polynomials having iterated Galois groups with bad properties. However, these polynomials are just linearly conjugate to the standard Chebyshev polynomials:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
F
be a field,
F_{p}
its prime field and
d\geq 1
. Then, the twisted Chebyshev polynomial
T_{d,\zeta}
is linearly conjugate over
F_{p}(\sqrt{\zeta})
to
\pm T_{d}
. Moreover, if
d
is even
T_{d}
and
-T_{d}
are linearly conjugate over
F_{p}
.

Proof.

Let
\xi_{d-1}
be a
(d-1)
th primitive root of unity. Then
\zeta=\xi_{d-1}^{s}
for some
s\in{\left\{0,\dots,d-2\right\}}
. Consider
a
such that
a^{2}=\zeta
. If
d
is even, we can take
a=\xi_{d-1}^{t}
for some
t\in{\left\{0,\dots,d-2\right\}}
. This is because if
a^{2}=\zeta
then we need to solve
2t\equiv s\pmod{d-1}
which has a solution since
2
is invertible modulo
d-1
. In particular,
a^{d-1}=1
. On the other hand, if
d
is odd, then

a^{d-1}=\left((\xi_{d-1})^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\right)^{s}=(-1)^{s}.

Consider
L(x)=ax
. Then

\displaystyle L^{-1}\circ T_{d,\zeta}\circ L\left(x+\frac{1}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=L^{-1}\circ T_{d,\zeta}\left(ax+\frac{a^{2}}{ax}\right)

\displaystyle=L^{-1}\left(a^{d}x^{d}+\frac{\zeta}{a^{d}x^{d}}\right)

\displaystyle=a^{d-1}x^{d}+\frac{1}{a^{d-1}x^{d}}.

If
d
is even, we obtain
L^{-1}\circ T_{d,\zeta}\circ L=T_{d}
. If
d
is odd, then
L^{-1}\circ T_{d,\zeta}\circ L=(-1)^{s}T_{d}
.

Finally, observe that when
d
is even, the maps
-T_{d}
and
T_{d}
are linearly conjugate by
L(x)=-x
.
∎

3.
Polynomials not linearly conjugate over
\mathbb{C}
to
\pm T_{d}

We start with the points (1) and (2) in
1
. Although it is not of our interest in this article, note that the following proof also applies if

is an invertible rational function of degree

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
K
be a number field and
f\in K[x]
a polynomial.

(1)

If
f
has degree
0
, then

\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=0.

(2)

If
f
has degree
1
, then

\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=1.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of good reduction for
f
. If
f
has degree zero then
f_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)=\alpha
at only one point, so

\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=0.

If
f
is a polynomial of degree
1
, then
f_{\mathfrak{p}}
is invertible. Therefore, if
x_{0}
is a strictly preperiodic point, there exist minimal numbers
n>m\geq 1
such that
f_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}(x_{0})=f_{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}(x_{0})
. But then, as
m
and
n
are minimal, this implies that
f_{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}(x_{0})
has two different preimages:
f_{\mathfrak{p}}^{m-1}(x_{0})
and
f_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n-1}(x_{0})
, contradicting that
f_{\mathfrak{p}}
is invertible.

Therefore, every point is periodic and
\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=1
.
∎

The next step is to prove point (3) in
1
, namely, that
\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=0
for every polynomial
f
that is not linearly conjugate over
\mathbb{C}
to
\pm T_{d}
. As it was mentioned in the introduction, the proof is a simple corollary from the theory of iterated Galois groups and fixed-point proportion that we explain in the following.

Let
F
be a field,
t
transcendental over
F
and fix
F(t)^{\mathrm{sep}}
an separable closure of
F(t)
. If
f\in F(x)
is a rational function with degree
d\geq 2
, we denote
f^{-n}(t)
the set of preimages of
t
under
f^{n}
. Let

F_{\infty}(f,t):=F\left(\bigcup_{n\geq 0}f^{-n}(t)\right)

be the field extension of

obtained by adding all the preimages of
t
. It can be proved that
F_{\infty}(f,t)/F(t)
is a Galois extension. Its Galois group, called the
iterated Galois group
of

is

G_{\infty}(F,f,t):=\Gal(F_{\infty}(f,t)/F(t)).

The Galois group

is profinite and acts transitively on

for every

; see
Jones2014
;
Radi2025FPP
. Given

and

As
G_{\infty}(F,f,t)
is profinite, we can consider
\mu
its unique Haar probability measure. The
fixed-point proportion
of
G_{\infty}(F,f,t)
is defined as

\FPP(G_{\infty}(F,f,t)):=\mu({\left\{g\in G_{\infty}(F,f,t):\#X_{n}(g)>0\text{ for all $n\geq 1$}\right\}}).

It has been observed since the eighties that this number has several applications to problems in arithmetic dynamics

;
ManesThompson2019
;
Odoni1980
;
Odoni1985
and for this reason is one of the areas strongly studied currently; see
Adams2025
;
AdamsHyde2025
;
Fari~na2026
;
Tucker2013ABC
;
Jones2008
;
Jones2012
and
(

, Section 5)
.

One of the applications of the fixed-point proportion concerns

.

Proposition 3.2

(
(

, Proof of Theorem 6.5)

(

where

A recent result of Fariña-Asategui and the author classifies the values of the fixed-point proportion for iterated Galois groups over algebraically closed fields:

(
(

, Theorem 2)
)

Let

As it was proved in

Let

its algebraic closure. Given

and

. Then

(5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)}

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
F
be any field of
\car(F)\neq 2
. Then,

(1)

the map
\pi:\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{F})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{F})
is surjective.

(2)

We have
\pi(x)=\pi(y)
if and only if

.

(3)

The only critical points of
\pi

\pm 1
with local degree

Proof.

Let

. If
w=\infty

\pi(0)=\pi(\infty)=\infty
. If

, then
\pi(x)=w
if and only if

This has a solution since we are working over

. Moreover, since it is a quadratic polynomial it has at most two solutions. As

, this implies that
\pi(x)=\pi(y)
if and only if

.

Finally, the derivative of

We summarize well-known properties about the Chebyshev polynomials in the following proposition:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
F
a field of characteristic zero.

(1)

T_{0}(x)=2
,
T_{1}(x)=x
and then we have the recursion

T_{d+1}(x)=xT_{d}(x)-T_{d-1}(x).

(2)

If
T_{d}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i,d}x^{i}
, then
a_{i,d}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a_{i,d}=0
if and only if

.

(3)

If
\alpha\in\overline{F}
then
e_{\pm T_{d}}(\alpha)
is either
1
or
2
. In particular, if
d\geq 3
then
\pm T_{d}
is only totally ramified at
\infty
.

Proof.

For (1), the first cases are obvious from
Equation

1
. Then

\displaystyle\left(x+\frac{1}{x}\right)T_{d}\left(x+\frac{1}{x}\right)=\left(x+\frac{1}{x}\right)\left(x^{d}+\frac{1}{x^{d}}\right)=T_{d+1}\left(x+\frac{1}{x}\right)+T_{d-1}\left(x+\frac{1}{x}\right).

For (2), using previous point we have the relation

a_{d+1-j,d+1}=a_{d-j,d}-a_{d+1-j,d-1}.

Then by induction it follows that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}a_{d-j,d}>0&\mbox{if $j\equiv 0\pmod{4}$,}\\
a_{d-j,d}=0&\mbox{if $j\equiv 1\pmod{2}$,}\\
a_{d-j,d}<0&\mbox{if $j\equiv 2\pmod{4}$.}\end{matrix}\right.

Finally for (3), using

1
we obtain

\displaystyle T_{d}^{\prime}(\pi(x))=\frac{d}{x^{d-1}}\frac{(x^{2})^{d}-1}{x^{2}-1}.

So, if
\pi(x)\neq\infty
and we let
\xi_{2d}
be a primitive
(2d)
th root of unity, then

at the points

with

. By multiplicativity of the local degree and

we have

e_{T_{d}}(\pi(x_{k}))\cdot e_{\pi}(x_{k})=e_{\pi}(g(x_{k}))\cdot e_{g}(x_{k}).

Then

and
e_{g}(x_{k})=1
. Moreover, by

4.1

e_{\pi}(x_{k})=1
and

, so
e_{T_{d}}(\pi(x_{k}))=2
. The critical points of

and its corresponding local degrees are the same.
∎

In principle we need to find the periodic points of

Lemma 4.3

Given

If

The next lemma shows that the periodic points of

If

In this section we prove point (4) in

Let

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=\Per_{\inf}(g,K).

Let

of good reduction for

So, we can take

Suppose

\displaystyle L^{-1}\circ(\pm T_{2})\circ L(x)=\pm\frac{ax^{2}}{a^{2}-2x^{2}},

and therefore

. Write

and
\pm T_{d}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}x^{i}

. Then

\displaystyle f(x)=L^{-1}\circ(\pm T_{d})\circ L(x)=a^{d-1}\cdot a_{d}\cdot x^{d}+a^{d-2}\cdot a_{d}\cdot d\cdot b\cdot x^{d-1}+\cdots

which implies that

as we are assuming that the
(d-1)
th coefficient of

\displaystyle f(x)=L^{-1}\circ(\pm T_{d})\circ L(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a^{i-1}\cdot a_{i}\cdot x^{i}.

If

is even, then

by
Proposition

4.2
, then
a\in K
. By
Remark

4.10
and
Lemma

5.1
we conclude that

(8)

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=\frac{1}{2}\liminf_{N(\mathfrak{p})\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{r(N(\mathfrak{p})-1,d)+r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)}{N(\mathfrak{p})}.

If
d
is odd, then
a_{2}=0
but
a_{3}\neq 0
, so we conclude that
a^{2}\cdot a_{3}\in K
and thus
a^{2}\in K
. If
a\in K
then
\Per_{\inf}(f,K)
is calculated as in
Equation

8
, so, let us assume that
a\notin K
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
K
be a number field, an odd number
d\geq 2
and consider
f=L^{-1}\circ(\pm T_{d})\circ L\in K[x]
with
L(x)=ax
and
a\notin K
. Then,

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=\frac{1}{2}\liminf_{N(\mathfrak{p})\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{r(N(\mathfrak{p})-1,d)+r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)}{N(\mathfrak{p})}.

Proof.

Call
K^{\prime}=K(a)
. By hypothesis
K^{\prime}/K
is an extension of degree
2
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal such that
\car(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})\neq 2
and
\mathfrak{p}
does not ramify at
K^{\prime}
. Notice that we are excluding only finitely many prime ideals. Let
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
be a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime}}
such that
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\mid\mathfrak{p}
.

As
f
and
\pm T_{d}
are linearly conjugate over
K^{\prime}
, then
L
induces a bijection

(9)

\displaystyle\Per(f_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}},\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Per((\pm T_{d})_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}},\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto a\alpha.

Moreover, since
d
is odd by
Lemma

4.3
we have
\Per((\pm T_{d})_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}},\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}})=\Per((T_{d})_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}},\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}})
.

We have two cases for
\mathfrak{p}
. If
\mathfrak{p}
is split, then
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and therefore by
Lemma

4.6
,

(10)

\displaystyle\#\Per(f_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})=\frac{r(N(\mathfrak{p})-1,d)+r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)}{2}.

If
\mathfrak{p}
is inert, then
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}/\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an extension of degree
2
and consequently the Frobenius map sends
a
to
-a
in
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Recall the trace is (in this case) the surjective additive group homomorphism

\displaystyle\tr:\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}},

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle\mapsto y+\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}(y).

As it is surjective we have

\#\ker(\tr)=N(\mathfrak{p}).

We claim that

\displaystyle a\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\ker(\tr).

Indeed, if
y\in a\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
then
y=ax
for some
x\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\tr(y)=ax-ax^{N(\mathfrak{p})}=0

as
x\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. This proves that
a\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\ker(\tr)
, but since they have the same cardinality, then
a\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\ker(\tr)
.

Let
F_{2}
be the extension of degree
2
of
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}
and recall the map
\pi:\mathbb{P}^{1}(F_{2})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(F_{2})
. Then, if
\beta\in F_{2}^{\times}
,

\displaystyle\tr(\pi(\beta))=\beta+\beta^{-1}+\beta^{N(\mathfrak{p})}+\beta^{-N(\mathfrak{p})}=\frac{(\beta^{N(\mathfrak{p})-1}+1)(\beta^{N(\mathfrak{p})+1}+1)}{\beta^{N(\mathfrak{p})}}.

We are interested in calculating
\#\Per(f_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Using the bijection in
Equation

9
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\#\Per(f_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})=\#(a\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap\Per((T_{d})_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}},\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}})).

Using
Lemma

4.6
, we reduce the previous problem to calculate the number of elements of

\displaystyle V_{\gamma}:={\left\{\pi(\beta)\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}:\tr(\pi(\beta))=0,\,\,\gcd(\left|{\beta}\right|,d)=1\text{ and }\left|{\beta}\right|\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}+\gamma)\right\}}

for
\gamma\in{\left\{-1,1\right\}}
.

Start with
\gamma=1
. We claim that

{\left\{\beta\in F_{2}^{\times}:\tr(\pi(\beta))=0\text{ and }\left|{\beta}\right|\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}+1)\right\}}=\emptyset,

which in particular implies that
V_{1}=\emptyset
. Indeed, if
\tr(\pi(\beta))=0
then
\beta^{N(\mathfrak{p})\pm 1}=-1
for either
+
or
-
in the exponent. Therefore

\left|{\beta}\right|\mid 2(N(\mathfrak{p})\pm 1).

Notice that as
N(\mathfrak{p})
is odd, the previous formula implies that

\left|{\beta}\right|\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}-1).

On the other hand if
\left|{\beta}\right|\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}+1)
, subtracting we conclude that
\left|{\beta}\right|\mid 2
and therefore
\beta=\pm 1
. However
\tr(\pi(\pm 1))\neq 0
.

If
\gamma=-1
consider

H_{-1}:={\left\{\beta\in F_{2}^{\times}:\tr(\pi(\beta))=0\text{ and }\left|{\beta}\right|\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}-1)\right\}}.

By the calculation done before if
\tr(\pi(\beta))=0
then
\left|{\beta}\right|\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}-1)
, so

H_{-1}:={\left\{\beta\in F_{2}^{\times}:\tr(\pi(\beta))=0\right\}}.

Let

G_{a}:={\left\{\beta\in F_{2}^{\times}:\beta^{a}=1\right\}}.

Then

H_{-1}=\bigcup_{\gamma\in{\left\{-1,1\right\}}}(G_{2(N(\mathfrak{p})+\gamma)}\setminus G_{N(\mathfrak{p})+\gamma})

as
tr(\pi(\beta))=0
if and only if
\beta^{N(\mathfrak{p})+\gamma}=-1
for either
\gamma=1
or
\gamma=-1
.

Moreover

\beta\in\bigcap_{\gamma\in{\left\{-1,1\right\}}}(G_{2(N(\mathfrak{p})+\gamma)}\setminus G_{N(\mathfrak{p})+\gamma})

if and only if
\left|{\beta}\right|=4
.

Notice that as
\pm 1\notin H_{-1}
, if
\pi(\beta)\in V_{-1}
then
\pi(\beta)
has two different preimages in
H_{-1}
. Using this observation and
Lemma

4.8
we obtain

\displaystyle\#V_{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in{\left\{-1,1\right\}}}(r(2(N(\mathfrak{p})+\gamma),d)-r(N(\mathfrak{p})+\gamma,d))\right)-\frac{\#{\left\{\beta\in F_{2}^{\times}:\left|{\beta}\right|=4\right\}}}{2}.

As
d
is odd then
r(2(N(\mathfrak{p})+\gamma),d)=2r(N(\mathfrak{p})+\gamma,d)
and therefore

\displaystyle\#V_{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in{\left\{-1,1\right\}}}r(N(\mathfrak{p})+\gamma,d)\right)-\frac{\#{\left\{\beta\in F_{2}^{\times}:\left|{\beta}\right|=4\right\}}}{2}.

As
V_{1}
was empty, then
\#\Per(f_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})=\#V_{-1}
allowing us to conclude

(11)

\displaystyle\#\Per(f_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in{\left\{-1,1\right\}}}r(N(\mathfrak{p})+\gamma,d)\right)-\frac{\#{\left\{\beta\in F_{2}^{\times}:\left|{\beta}\right|=4\right\}}}{2}.

Dividing
Equation

10
and
Equation

11
by
N(\mathfrak{p})
and taking limit, we obtain

\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=\frac{1}{2}\liminf_{N(\mathfrak{p})\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{r(N(\mathfrak{p})-1,d)+r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)}{N(\mathfrak{p})}

as desired.
∎

6.
Analysis of
\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)

In this last section, we prove the points from (5) to (10) in
1
completing the classification.

Fix
K
a number field and recall
m_{K}
is the greatest positive number such that
K
contains a
m_{K}
th primitive root of unity. By
Remark

4.10
and
Proposition

5.2
we have that if
f
is linearly conjugate to
\pm T_{d}
over
\mathbb{C}
then

\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=\frac{1}{2}\liminf_{N(\mathfrak{p})\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{r(N(\mathfrak{p})-1,d)+r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)}{N(\mathfrak{p})},

so we just need to analyze the possible values of this limit in the different cases considered in
1
. For this task, we will use
Proposition

2.5
many times.

Since we are taking a limit, we may assume that if
\mathfrak{p}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, then the norm of
\mathfrak{p}
is coprime to
2\cdot m_{K}
. Under this assumption, the polynomial

x^{m_{K}}-1

is separable in
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and therefore all the
m_{K}
th root of unity are different in
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. As
\xi_{m_{K}}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
this implies that

(12)

m_{K}\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})-1).

6.1.
d
has at least two prime factors and
\rad(d)\nmid\rad(m_{K})

Let
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
be two different prime factors of
d
such that
p_{2}\nmid m_{K}
. Let
m_{1}\geq v_{p_{1}}(m_{K})
and
m_{2}\geq 1
. Consider the system of congruences

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}x&\equiv&1\pmod{m_{K}}\\
x&\equiv&1\pmod{p_{1}^{m_{1}}}\\
x&\equiv&-1\pmod{p_{2}^{m_{2}}}\\
\end{matrix}\right..

As
p_{2}\nmid m_{K}
, the system has a solution. By
Proposition

2.5
there are infinitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that

p_{1}^{m_{1}}\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})-1)\text{ and }p_{2}^{m_{2}}\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})+1).

As
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are factors of
d
then

r(N(\mathfrak{p})-1,d)\leq\frac{N(\mathfrak{p})-1}{p_{1}^{m_{1}}}\text{ and }r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)\leq\frac{N(\mathfrak{p})+1}{p_{2}^{m_{2}}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(f,K)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{p_{1}^{m_{1}}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}^{m_{2}}}\right).

As
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are arbitrary, then

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=0.

6.2.
d
is odd and
\rad(d)\mid\rad(m_{K})

We claim that

r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)=N(\mathfrak{p})+1.

Indeed, suppose that
r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)<N(\mathfrak{p})+1
. Then, there exists
p
prime such that

\displaystyle p\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})+1)\text{ and }p\mid d.

As
d
is odd, then
p
is odd.

On the other hand, if
p
divides
d
, then
p
also divides
m_{K}
by hypothesis so by
Equation

12

p\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})-1).

Therefore
p\mid 2
, leading to a contradiction since
p
is odd.

Let
p
be any prime divisor of
d
. For any
m\geq 1
, consider the system of congruences

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}x&\equiv&1\pmod{m_{K}}\\
x&\equiv&1\pmod{p^{m}}\\
\end{matrix}\right..

As the system has a solution by
Proposition

2.5
there are infinitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that

p^{m}\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})-1)

and consequently

r(N(\mathfrak{p})-1,d)\leq\frac{N(\mathfrak{p})-1}{p^{m}}.

for any
m\geq 1
.
In conclusion,

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=\frac{1}{2}.

6.3.
d
is even,
\rad(d)\mid\rad(m_{K})
and
v_{2}(m_{K})\geq 2

We claim that

r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)=\frac{N(\mathfrak{p})+1}{2}.

If not there exists
p
prime such that

\displaystyle p\mid\frac{N(\mathfrak{p})+1}{2}\text{ and }p\mid d.

Since
p
divides
d
, as in the previous case we obtain that

p\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})-1)

and so
p=2
. But now if
p=2
then

4\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})+1)

and since
4\mid m_{K}
by hypothesis, then

4\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})-1),

leading to a contradiction.

Now taking
p
a prime divisor of
d
, considering the same system of congruences as in the previous case and arguing the same way we obtain

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=\frac{1}{4}.

6.4
d
is even, has at least two prime factors,
\rad(d)\mid\rad(m_{K})
and
v_{2}(m_{K})=1

Let
p
be an odd prime dividing
d
. Let
m_{1}\geq v_{p}(m_{K})
and
m_{2}\geq v_{2}(m_{K})
. Consider the system of congruences

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}x&\equiv&1\pmod{m_{K}}\\
x&\equiv&1\pmod{p^{m_{1}}}\\
x&\equiv&-1\pmod{2^{m_{2}}}\\
\end{matrix}\right..

As
\gcd(m_{K},2^{m_{2}})=2
the system has a solution and so by
Proposition

2.5
there are infinitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that

p^{m_{1}}\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})-1)\text{ and }2^{m_{2}}\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})+1).

Proceeding as in the first case we obtain

\displaystyle\Per_{\inf}(f,K)=0.

6.5
d
is an odd prime power

Let
p
be the prime dividing
d
. Observe that either
r(N(\mathfrak{p})-1,d)=N(\mathfrak{p})-1
or
r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)=N(\mathfrak{p})+1
. Indeed, if not

\displaystyle p\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})-1)\text{ and }p\mid(N(\mathfrak{p})+1),

which contradicts that
p
is odd. Then, arguing as in the second case we obtain

\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)=\frac{1}{2}.

6.6
d
is an even prime power

As before, we have either

r(N(\mathfrak{p})-1,d)=\frac{N(\mathfrak{p})-1}{2}\text{ or }r(N(\mathfrak{p})+1,d)=\frac{N(\mathfrak{p})+1}{2}.

If not, then
4
divides
N(\mathfrak{p})-1
and
N(\mathfrak{p})+1
which leads to a contradiction.

Finally, taking
m\geq v_{2}(m_{K})
and consider the system of congruences

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}x&\equiv&1\pmod{m_{K}}\\
x&\equiv&1\pmod{2^{m}}\\
\end{matrix}\right..

we conclude that

\Per_{\inf}(T_{d},K)=\frac{1}{4}.
