The Very General Verra Fourfold is Irrational
Aideen Fay

Abstract
. We show that the very general Verra fourfold is irrational, using the Hodge atom framework of Katzarkov–Kontsevich–Pantev–Yu. Two novel points are: a refined analysis of Hodge atoms, based on the involution on the cohomology of X X , and a derivation of the quantum multiplication matrix from the quantum differential operator. This gives the first successful application of the method of Hodge atoms to a space with Picard rank greater than one.



1.
Introduction

A Verra fourfold is a double cover

\pi:X\to\mathbf{P}^{2}\times\mathbf{P}^{2}

branched along a smooth divisor of bidegree
(2,2)
. This is a Fano fourfold of index two. Although certain special Verra fourfolds are proven to be rational, there are no known irrational examples
(
3
)
. In this paper, we prove:

Theorem 1
.

A very general Verra fourfold is irrational.

The study of rationality for fourfolds has been revolutionised by recent work of Katzarkov–Kontsevich–Pantev–Yu, who introduced new invariants called Hodge atoms
9
. Hodge atoms
arise from the spectral decomposition of the maximal
A
-model

F
-bundle under quantum multiplication by the Euler vector field

\kappa:=\mathrm{Eu}\star(-)
. The eigenvalues of
\kappa
trace
out a branched cover of the base, and the corresponding generalized eigenspaces form flat subbundles over the Hodge locus, which carry Hodge-theoretic data
that define the Hodge atoms. By the blowup decomposition theorem of
9
, these flat subbundles and their Hodge atom invariants behave additively under blowups. This allows us to identify spaces that cannot be birational to projective space, via the weak factorization theorem
(
1
)
.

Hodge atoms have already been applied to prove irrationality for the very general cubic fourfold
9
and the very general Gushel–Mukai fourfold
2
, the latter providing the first known example in its family. In both cases the Picard rank is one, and Katzarkov et al.’s basic Hodge atom invariants – the number of Hodge classes and the Hodge types present – give a direct obstruction to rationality. The Verra fourfolds considered in this paper present an additional challenge: the “ambient part” of the cohomology is larger, with Picard rank two, and therefore the basic invariants of Hodge atoms do not on their own give an obstruction to rationality. Our proof proceeds by analyzing the canonical involution on the cohomology of a Verra fourfold, which provides us with a more refined Hodge atom decomposition within the generalized zero eigenspace. This isolates the birational obstruction, allowing us to conclude irrationality.

The Cohomology of Verra Fourfolds

As discussed, a
Verra fourfold
is a double cover
\pi\colon X\longrightarrow\mathbf{P}^{2}\times\mathbf{P}^{2}

2.1.
Ambient cohomology

Consider now the ambient part

On the base space
\mathbf{P}^{2}\times\mathbf{P}^{2}

This

3.
Hodge atoms and birational obstructions

3.1.
Quantum cohomology and the
A
-model
F
-bundle

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. The

small quantum cohomology
of
X
is a deformation of the
classical cup product on
H^{\bullet}(X,\mathbf{Q})
, and is parametrised by a point

b
in the complexified Kähler moduli space
B_{X}

11
. The small quantum product
\star_{b}
is given by

\langle\alpha\star_{b}\beta,\,\gamma\rangle\;=\;\sum_{d\in H_{2}(X,\mathbf{Z})}\langle\alpha,\beta,\gamma\rangle_{0,3,d}\,q^{d},

where
\langle\alpha,\beta,\gamma\rangle_{0,3,d}
are
genus-zero, three-point Gromov–Witten invariants that give a virtual count of rational curves of degree
d
meeting cycles Poincaré dual to
\alpha
,

\beta
, and
\gamma
. Here
q^{d}\in\mathbf{Q}[H_{2}(X)]
is the corresponding Novikov variable on
B_{X}
; when
q=0
the small quantum product
\star_{q}
coincides with the classical cup product.

The
A
-model
F
-bundle

(\mathcal{H},\nabla)\to B_{X}

packages this data into a flat vector bundle whose fibre over each
point
b\in B_{X}
is a copy of
H^{\bullet}(X,\mathbf{Q})
equipped with the
quantum product
\star_{b}
. The flat connection on the A-model
F
-bundle, called the
Dubrovin connection
, is defined in terms of
\star_{b}
.

3.2.
Hodge atom invariants

Each Hodge atom
\alpha
carries two basic invariants that encode information about the algebraic and transcendental parts of the corresponding flat subbundle
E_{\alpha}
.

(1)

The
rational Hodge class count

\rho_{\alpha}
is the dimension of the space of Hodge classes in the atom

\rho_{\alpha}:=\dim_{\mathbf{Q}}\bigl(E^{\alpha}\bigr)^{\mathrm{Hdg}}.

(2)

The
Hodge polynomial

P_{\alpha}(t)\in\mathbf{Z}[t,t^{-1}]
records the Hodge types that are present within the atom. The
t^{k}
coefficient is the dimension of the subspace where
p-q=k
. For us, this means that a nonzero coefficient at
t^{2}
signifies the presence of a
(3,1)
-class.

The blowup decomposition theorem of
9

states that the
A
-model
F
-bundle of a blowup splits as a direct
sum

\mathcal{H}_{\widehat{X}}\;\cong\;\mathcal{H}_{X}\;\oplus\;\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}\mathcal{H}_{Z},

where
\widehat{X}=\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X
and
Z
has codimension
r
. This relies on Iritani’s fundamental work on the quantum cohomology of
blowups
8
, which describes how genus-zero
Gromov–Witten invariants of
\widehat{X}
decompose in terms of

X
and
Z
. Since
\rho_{\alpha}
and
P_{\alpha}
both respect direct
sums of Hodge structures

\rho(\widehat{X})=\rho(X)+(r-1)\,\rho(Z),\qquad P_{\widehat{X}}(t)=P_{X}(t)+(r-1)\,P_{Z}(t).

Given that any birational map between smooth projective varieties factors
into blowups by weak factorisation
1
,
the invariants
\rho
and
P
change in a controlled way under birational equivalence.

3.3.
Birational obstructions in Fourfolds

If
X
is a smooth fourfold, the weak
factorization theorem
1
implies that any
birational map
X\dashrightarrow\mathbf{P}^{4}
decomposes into a sequence of blowups and blowdowns along smooth centres of codimension at least two. In
dimension four, these are points, curves, and surfaces. The Hodge atom invariants each type can contribute are restricted as follows:

(1)

A point contributes a single Hodge class of type
(0,0)
and no class with
p\neq q
.

(2)

A smooth curve has Hodge types satisfying
|p-q|\leq 1
and therefore contributes nothing with
|p-q|=2
.

(3)

A smooth projective surface always contributes at least three independent rational Hodge classes: the unit class
1\in H^{0}
, the hyperplane class
\ell\in H^{2}
, and the point class
\ell^{2}\in H^{4}
. This means that any atom that arises from a surface satisfies
\rho_{\alpha}\geq 3
.

A fourfold
X
is therefore irrational if it contains a Hodge atom
\alpha
that satisfies both
\mathrm{Coeff}_{t^{2}}(P_{\alpha})\neq 0
and
\rho_{\alpha}<3
. No combination of points and curves can produce a class with
p-q=2
, since each individual centre contributes nothing with
|p-q|>1
and
\rho_{\alpha}
is additive under the blowup decomposition. The same additivity tells us that any combination involving at least one surface must satisfy
\rho_{\alpha}\geq 3
, as every surface contributes at least three rational Hodge classes.

4.
Ambient Quantum Cohomology

In this section we compute the matrix of quantum multiplication by the anticanonical class for the very general Verra fourfold. Rather than computing it directly using mirror theorems, as in
9

2

We then solve for the unknown matrix entries by deriving the associated Picard–Fuchs operator and using the fact that this must annihilate the deregularised quantum period of the Verra fourfold (
3

The zero generalized eigenspace and Hodge atoms

The zero generalized eigenspace of the ambient part decomposes under the involution as

A_{0}\;=\;A_{0}^{+}\;\oplus\;A_{0}^{-},\qquad\dim A_{0}^{+}=2,\quad\dim A_{0}^{-}=1.

The transcendental lattice
T(X)\subset H^{4}(X)
lies in
\ker(m_{H})
, and the full zero generalized eigenspace is therefore

E_{0}\;=\;T(X)\;\oplus\;A_{0}^{+}\;\oplus\;A_{0}^{-},\qquad\dim E_{0}\;=\;\dim T(X)+3.

The algebraic rank of
E_{0}
is
\rho(E_{0})=3
, so the basic Hodge class count obstruction doesn’t rule out the possibility that
E_{0}
comes from a surface contribution. We need a finer analysis of the zero generalized eigenspace.

4.10.
The involutive decomposition of the zero generalized eigenspace

Let
\varepsilon
denote the canonical involution on
H^{\bullet}(X)
.

Lemma 7
.

We have
[\varepsilon,\kappa]=0
.

Proof.

This is evidently true for symmetric Verra fourfolds, where the involution
\varepsilon
on cohomology arises from a genuine involution on
X
. It is therefore true for any Verra fourfold, because quantum cohomology is deformation invariant.
∎

The involution
\varepsilon
therefore preserves the zero generalized eigenspace
E_{0}=\ker(\kappa)
.
The transcendental part
T\subset H^{4}(X;\mathbf{Q})
is the orthogonal complement to
A_{\text{mid}}=\langle H_{1}^{2},H_{1}H_{2},H_{2}^{2}\rangle
. Since
A_{\text{mid}}
is
\varepsilon
-invariant, so is
T
.

Lemma 8
.

For a very general Verra fourfold, the transcendental lattice
T
lies entirely in either
E_{0}^{+}
or
E_{0}^{-}
.

Proof.

Since
X
is very general,
T
is simple and
T\subset\ker m_{H}\subset E_{0}
. The involution
\varepsilon
decomposes
T
as a direct sum of
\pm 1
eigenspaces. However, if either of these subspaces is proper, we will get a proper sub-Hodge structure of
T
, contradicting simplicity.
∎

We can now track where the components of
E_{0}=T\oplus A_{0}^{+}\oplus A_{0}^{-}
live:

•

A_{0}^{+}
is contained in
E_{0}^{+}
.

•

A_{0}^{-}
is contained in
E_{0}^{-}
.

•

T
must lie entirely in either
E_{0}^{+}
or
E_{0}^{-}
.

As we will see, regardless of the sign of
\varepsilon|_{T}
, the rank of the algebraic part of the positive eigenspace is exactly two:

\rho(E_{0}^{+})=\rho(A_{0}^{+})+\rho(T|_{E_{0}^{+}})=2

4.11.
Obstructions from refined Hodge atom invariants

Lemma 9

(Hodge type obstruction)
.

The transcendental part
T
lies in
E_{0}
, and gives rise to a Hodge atom
\alpha
such that the coefficient of
t^{2}
in
P_{\alpha}(t)
is non-zero.

Proof.

Lemma
8
shows that
T\subset E_{0}
. Since
h^{3,1}(X)=1
, the space
H^{3,1}(X)
is one-dimensional and contained in
T\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{C}
.
∎

Lemma 10

(Hodge class count obstruction)
.

The symmetric part
E_{0}^{+}
carries exactly two rational Hodge classes:
\rho(E_{0}^{+})=2
.

Proof.

The ambient zero eigenspace
A_{0}^{+}
contributes exactly two rational Hodge classes. The transcendental component
T

contributes zero, as a nonzero rational Hodge class in
T
would be a one-dimensional rational sub-Hodge structure and contradict the simplicity of
T
. Hence
\rho(E_{0}^{+})=2
, independently of
whether
T
lies in
E_{0}^{+}
or
E_{0}^{-}
.
∎

4.12.
Excluding birational centres

Proposition 11

(Exclusion of points and curves)
.

No combination of points and curves can account for the Hodge
atom
\alpha
from Lemma
9
.

Proof.

A point contributes a single class of Hodge type
(0,0)
. A smooth curve of genus
g
contributes classes with
|p-q|\leq 1
. Neither can produce a class with
p-q=2
, and hence neither can produce a Hodge polynomial with non-zero coefficient of
t^{2}
.
∎

Proposition 12

(Exclusion of surfaces)
.

No surface can account for the Hodge atom
\alpha
from Lemma
9
.

Proof.

Every algebraic class that comes from a birational centre lives in the symmetric part
E_{0}^{+}
. A surface atom would contribute at least the three Hodge classes
1,\,\ell,\,\ell^{2}
, which would force
\rho(E_{0}^{+})\geq 3
. But Lemma
10
shows that
\rho(E_{0}^{+})=2
.
∎

4.13.
Proof of Theorem
1

Suppose that
X
is rational. By weak factorization, a birational map
X\dashrightarrow\mathbf{P}^{4}
decomposes into blowups and blowdowns along smooth centres. This means that the Hodge atom
\alpha
from Lemma
9
must arise from some combination of points, curves, and surfaces.
Proposition
11
excludes points and curves, and Proposition
12
excludes surfaces. Therefore
X
is irrational.
