Robust adaptive fault-tolerant learning control for human height-weight prediction based on DNN
Authors: Zhifang Wang, Quanzhen Huang, Jianguo Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this paper, a distributed robust adaptive confined fault-tolerant optimal control method based on deep neural networks is proposed, aiming to solve the complexity and uncertainty problems in human height and weight prediction. Note that the term ‘control’ in this work refers to feedback regulation of the iterative learning/optimization dynamics of the predictor (iteration domain), rather than controlling the physical time evolution of human height/weight. In the field of public security technology, accurate prediction of individual physiological characteristics has important application value, especially in crime prevention, individual identification, and behavior analysis. Traditional prediction methods often perform erratically in the face of data noise, environmental changes, and outliers. To this end, this paper combines deep learning and fault-tolerant control theory to propose an efficient and reliable prediction framework by optimizing the robustness and adaptive ability of the predictor. By introducing a limited fault-tolerant mechanism, it can maintain high prediction accuracy and stability under various perturbations and incomplete data conditions. Moreover, we evaluate the proposed framework from three complementary dimensions—statistical similarity, overall predictive performance, and minority-class detection ability—and explicitly acknowledge that these criteria may exhibit trade-offs: improved distributional similarity does not necessarily translate into better decision boundaries, and optimizing overall performance can conflict with minority detection (e.g., recall/F1). Simulation and experimental results show that after 2000 rounds of iterative optimization, the normal and fault-tolerant prediction accuracies of human height for finger length of left and right hands are 98.4% and 97.7%, respectively, and the normal and fault-tolerant prediction accuracies of human body weight are 98.2% and 97.5%, respectively, by combining the 372 sets of data with 30% of data loss caused by human. The accuracy of normal and fault-tolerant prediction of human height was 90.8% and 89.2% for the finger length of the left hand, and the accuracy of normal and fault-tolerant prediction of human weight was 85.6% and 83.3%, respectively. The normal and fault-tolerant prediction accuracies of human height for the finger length of the right hand were 96% and 95.3%, and the normal and fault-tolerant prediction accuracies of human weight were 94.4% and 93.5%, respectively. These findings are most directly applicable to small-to-medium tabular datasets with moderate class imbalance and limited minority samples, which matches the regimes evaluated in this study. This study provides a new idea and technical path for biometric prediction and analysis in the field of public security technology, which has important theoretical significance and practical value.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-57686-w.

Introduction

With the rapid development of social informatization, the field of public security faces increasingly complex security challenges. Accurately obtaining and analyzing human characteristic information, such as height and weight, is of great significance for suspect identification, missing person search, and public security monitoring1,2. However, traditional human feature prediction methods are limited by problems such as data quality, environmental noise, and system fault tolerance, which make it difficult to meet the demand for high accuracy and high reliability in practical applications.

DNN, as a powerful machine learning method, has achieved remarkable results in the fields of biometric feature prediction, pattern recognition, and image processing3,4. In recent years, the wide application of deep learning technology has changed the traditional methods of biometric feature extraction and prediction, especially in the field of public security technology. Biometric feature recognition and prediction based on deep neural networks has become an important research direction. Zheng Wang et al. proposed an autoencoder model based on the baseline deep network to generate deeply hidden attributes for the newly discovered annotation increment and an autoencoder model was reformed by adding an orthogonal generation module as well as identity preservation and sparsity constraints, which can be inserted into any deep network to mine potential information in an unsupervised way5, but the method is more dependent on the data quality and has insufficient ability to adapt to dynamically changing data. Robust Adaptive Control theory is widely used in the control of dynamic systems, especially in environments where uncertainties and perturbations exist6–8. In recent years, RAC theory has gained wide attention in the fields of intelligent transportation, unmanned driving, and robotics. Introducing RAC into deep neural networks can enhance the robustness of the model in complex and dynamic environments, especially in the face of noise or incomplete data9,10. Mou Chen et al. proposed variable structure control combining backstepping and Lyapunov synthesis for asymmetric input nonlinearities such as dead zone and saturation, which guarantees semi-global uniformity of all the signals in a closed-loop system11. D. Wang et al. developed A class of nonlinear systems with arbitrary uncertainty in the form of strict feedback and developed a backstepping-based control design that guarantees uniform ultimate boundedness of the solution of the closed-loop system and makes the tracking error arbitrarily small12. Derong Liu et al. developed a neural-network-based Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) control design for the closed-loop system13. The Jacobi-Bellman equations online solution method studied the infinite horizon optimal robust guaranteed cost control problem for continuous-time uncertain nonlinear systems and transformed the optimal robust guaranteed cost control problem into an optimal control problem by establishing appropriate bounded functions and defining modified cost functions13. The above method relies on the accuracy of the initial model, and if the model error is large, the performance will be significantly degraded, in addition, the above method cannot obtain the accurate regulation parameters in real-time, and if the parameters are not reasonably designed, it will lead to the gradual dispersion of the system error function. In many practical applications, sensor data or input data are often missing or corrupted14,15. To cope with this challenge, Fault-Tolerant Optimization Control mechanisms have been applied in the fields of machine learning, automatic control, and data processing, especially in guaranteeing the system performance while improving the fault-tolerance16,17. Haowei Lin et al. constructed a particle swarm optimization-based neural network (NN) identifier to simulate unknown system dynamics. Using the estimated system states, a particle swarm optimization critic neural network was used to solve the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equations more efficiently18, but the method is more sensitive to whether the fault model is accurately modeled or not, and the control effect is drastically reduced if the model variability is high. Distributed Control is an important branch of modern control theory, especially widely used in MIMO systems19,20. In recent years, Distributed Control technology has been introduced into deep learning models, especially when large-scale data and computations need to be processed, which can effectively reduce the computational complexity and improve the real-time performance of the system. In the field of public security technology, distributed control strategies can be applied in multi-source data fusion, intelligent monitoring, and behavioral analysis21,22. Rudai Yan et al. proposed a data-driven distributed frequency control method for islanded microgrids based on multi-agent quantum deep reinforcement learning, which combines the traditional DRL framework with quantum machine learning and can adaptively obtain an optimal cooperative control strategy23, but the method lacks sufficient global regulation capability in the face of unpredictable large-scale system failures. In addition, with the development of artificial intelligence and biometrics, the demand for biometric prediction in the field of public security technology in the areas of identity verification, behavioral analysis, security monitoring, and so on is gradually increasing. Especially, the prediction of biometric features such as height and weight has a wide range of applications in crime prevention, behavioral identification, and personnel location. For example, Sam Stockwell et al. explored the future development of biometrics in policing and law enforcement in the UK24. We note that this underperformance is mainly observed under our small-sample regime and constrained tuning budget (bounded hyperparameter search), and should not be interpreted as evidence that deep generative models are generally unsuitable for tabular data augmentation.

This paper combines the advantages and complements the shortcomings of cutting-edge technologies such as deep neural networks, distributed control, robust adaptive control, and fault-tolerant optimal control to provide a new way of thinking for the prediction of human height and weight. The prediction model proposed in this paper has important application value in public security technology applications in crime prevention and control, behavior analysis, and intelligent monitoring. Beyond the proposed robust and fault-tolerant prediction framework, continued progress in intelligent methods provides clear directions to further improve performance under practical constraints. First, recent advances in high-fidelity data synthesis under scarcity, such as adaptive fused domain-cycling variational generative adversarial networks, demonstrate that multi-domain feature fusion and cycle-consistent generation can significantly enhance learning when labeled data are limited, which is potentially beneficial for biometric prediction scenarios with small sample sizes27,28. Second, domain shift across acquisition devices and environments remains a major obstacle for real-world deployment; dynamic collaborative adversarial domain adaptation networks offer a principled way to align feature distributions and improve generalization without requiring target labels, which can be extended to cross-device/cross-scene physiological prediction29. Third, multimodal learning has shown strong robustness in challenging engineering sensing tasks, such as arcing detection in electrified railway systems under complex conditions and limited data, suggesting that fusing heterogeneous cues and leveraging robust representation learning can improve reliability in field environments30. Motivated by these advances, future work will explore integrating data synthesis, domain adaptation, and multimodal fusion into our iteration-domain robust learning framework to further enhance generalization under noisy, low-resolution, and cross-domain conditions.

Special note:

(1) Statistical learning-theoretic mechanism of class imbalance: Consider binary classification \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$y \in \left\{ { - 1, + 1} \right\}$$\end{document} with hypothesis f and loss ℓ. The population risk is shown as Eq.(45).45\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R\left( f \right) = E_{{\left( {x,y} \right) \sim P}} \left[ {\ell \left( {yf\left( x \right)} \right)} \right] = \pi_{ + } E_{{x \sim P\left( {x| + } \right)}} \left[ {\ell \left( {f\left( x \right)} \right)} \right] + \pi_{ - } E_{{x \sim P\left( {x| - } \right)}} \left[ {\ell \left( { - f\left( x \right)} \right)} \right]$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pi_{ + } = P\left( {y = + 1} \right)$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pi_{ - } = P\left( {y = - 1} \right)$$\end{document} are class priors. Under imbalanced sampling, empirical risk minimization (ERM) approximates a prior-skewed risk because the empirical class proportions \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{\pi}_{ + } ,\hat{\pi}_{ - }$$\end{document} differ from the target priors. As a consequence, the learned decision rule tends to favor the majority class in order to minimize average loss, which manifests as a shifted decision boundary.

From a Bayes decision perspective, the optimal boundary satisfies a likelihood-ratio test:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\log \frac{{p\left( {x| + } \right)}}{{p\left( {x| - } \right)}} \ge \log \frac{{\pi_{ - } }}{{\pi_{ + } }}$$\end{document}, implying that changing priors (or effective class weights) directly moves the threshold and thus shifts the decision boundary in feature space. Therefore, imbalance is not merely a data-count issue; it changes the effective objective being optimized, unless corrected by cost-sensitive reweighting or calibrated decision thresholds.

(2) Theoretical effect of synthetic samples on feature-space interval distribution: Let \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$z = \phi \left( x \right)$$\end{document} denote the representation in feature space (either handcrafted features or the learned embedding of a DNN). Interpolation-based oversampling generates synthetic minority samples along line segments between minority neighbors, e.g., as shown in Eq.(46).46\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta z = z_{i} + \lambda \left( {z_{j} - z_{i} } \right),\lambda \in \left[ {0,1} \right],z_{i} ,z_{j} \in S_{ + }$$\end{document}

This procedure modifies the empirical minority distribution in two coupled ways. First, it reduces sparsity by filling the convex hull (or local neighborhood) of minority points, which shortens the expected nearest-neighbor distance and compresses the tail of the feature-space interval distribution (i.e., fewer large gaps between minority samples). Second, it performs local smoothing of the minority density (a vicinal distribution around observed samples), which reduces estimation variance of the decision boundary and can increase minority margins. However, if interpolation is performed near the true boundary or across heterogeneous minority modes, synthetic points may fall into regions with high majority density, increasing class overlap and potentially introducing bias (boundary distortion). This explains why oversampling tends to be beneficial in “safe” minority regions (well inside the minority manifold) but can be harmful near boundary regions. In practice, boundary-aware or safe-level synthesis strategies can mitigate this risk by restricting neighbors to locally consistent minority clusters and avoiding generating samples in high-overlap regions.

conclusion

This paper proposes a distributed robust adaptive constrained fault-tolerant optimization control method based on deep neural networks, focusing on human height and weight prediction tasks. It conducts in-depth research addressing practical requirements in the field of public security technology. By designing a distributed model architecture, this work achieves efficient processing of multi-source biometric data, overcoming the limitations of traditional centralized methods in computational complexity and real-time performance. Furthermore, by integrating robust adaptive control theory with bounded fault-tolerant optimization techniques, the model demonstrates remarkable robustness and high fault tolerance when confronted with data gaps, outliers, and noise interference. This research provides an efficient and reliable solution for biometric analysis in public security applications, holding significant theoretical implications and practical value. The future development directions of this paper are summarized as follows: (1) Explore the deep integration of multimodal biometric features, such as combining fingerprint, iris, and behavioral characteristics, to enhance the comprehensiveness and robustness of predictive models, thereby providing more precise individual identification support for public security applications. (2) To address the real-time processing and deployment flexibility requirements of public security applications, edge computing technologies can be integrated to enable efficient model inference on resource-constrained devices, thereby improving practical application performance. (3) To adapt to changes in data distribution and system perturbations within dynamic environments, adaptive algorithms based on online learning and meta-learning can be investigated to ensure model stability and reliability during long-term operation. In future work, we will incorporate noise/quantization-aware training (data augmentation) and uncertainty estimation to further improve robustness under extremely low-quality sensing.

The conclusions in this paper are based on the imbalance ratios, feature distributions, and dataset scale evaluated in our experiments-particularly small-to-medium tabular datasets with limited minority samples. In regimes with extreme imbalance, substantially larger datasets, or different minority manifold complexity, the relative effectiveness of oversampling and generative augmentation methods may change. Moreover, deeper generative models can be more competitive given sufficient data and tuning budget. Therefore, our findings should be interpreted as scenario-specific guidance rather than universal rankings.
