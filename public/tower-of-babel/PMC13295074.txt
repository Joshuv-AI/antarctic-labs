Dose-response relationship between physical activity and sarcopenia in peritoneal dialysis patients
Authors: Yuxin Xiong, Jiao Huang, Yutong Wu, Xuan Xu, Yuanhua Zheng, Honghong Liu, Chunyan Lang, Xiaotian Zhang, Xin Sun, Kaiying Wu, Qin Zheng, Huyan Zhou, Jun Yuan, Hongyan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPhysical activity (PA) is of great importance for peritoneal dialysis (PD) patients with sarcopenia. However, the optimal PA dose for sarcopenia in PD patients remains unclear. This study adopted a cross-sectional design and enrolled PD patients from two large general hospitals in China between March 2023 and February 2025. Sarcopenia was diagnosed in accordance with the 2019 criteria of the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia. The Chinese Low Physical Activity Questionnaire was used to assess PA levels. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) models were applied to examine the dose-response relationships of total PA consumption, total walking time, leisure walking time, light physical activity time, and inactive time with sarcopenia and its three parameters in PD patients. A total of 881 participants were included in the final analysis. Non-linear dose-response relationships were observed between total PA consumption and the prevalence of sarcopenia (overall p < 0.05, non-linear p < 0.05), as well as the five-repetition sit-to-stand (5-STS) (overall p < 0.01, non-linear p < 0.05). Total PA consumption was associated with handgrip strength, but no significant non-linear relationship was detected (overall p < 0.05, non-linear p > 0.05). No significant association was found between PA consumption and skeletal muscle index (overall p > 0.05, non-linear p > 0.05). A protective effect against sarcopenia was observed when PA consumption exceeded 33 MET-h/week, and its protective effect on 5-STS tended to plateau after reaching 43 MET-h/week. These findings indicate that there may be an optimal range of PA for simultaneously reducing sarcopenia risk and improving physical function in PD patients.

Introduction

Peritoneal dialysis (PD) is widely used as an important form of renal replacement therapy for patients with end-stage renal disease. However, PD patients are limited in physical activity (PA) due to dialysate retention in the body, fatigue and other factors. In addition, PD patients commonly suffer from uremic toxin accumulation, metabolic acidosis, malnutrition, and chronic microinflammatory status. These factors collectively contribute to the onset and progression of sarcopenia [1,2]. Sarcopenia is an age-related systemic skeletal muscle disease characterized by the loss of muscle mass, muscle strength and physical function, which severely impairs patients’ quality of life and clinical prognosis. PD patients complicated with sarcopenia are prone to falls, fractures and mobility impairment, which further lead to increased dependence, reduced quality of life and elevated mortality [1].

Levels of PA are closely associated with the development of sarcopenia, and regular exercise can reduce the risk of sarcopenia onset. Yao et al. [3] found that the risk of sarcopenia in people aged >60 years with irregular exercise was 1.56 times that of those who exercised regularly; the risk in people aged >60 years with daily sedentary time exceeding 4 h was 1.677 times that of those with sedentary time within 2 h; and performing daily heavy physical housework could reduce the risk of sarcopenia by 0.538 times. However, PA of different types and intensities exert varying effects on sarcopenia-related parameters in dialysis patients. Multiple studies [4–6] have indicated that light physical activity (LPA) is a risk factor for sarcopenia. In addition, systematic reviews by Steffl et al. [7] and Oliveira et al. [8] both pointed out that the amount of PA is correlated with the development of sarcopenia, and vigorous physical activity (VPA) acts as a protective factor against sarcopenia onset.

Although studies have confirmed a close relationship between PA levels and sarcopenia development, excessive PA may increase the basal metabolic rate, fatigue, and risk of injury in dialysis patients. For dialysis patients, an excessively high basal metabolic rate can lead to excessive energy consumption caused by PA, thereby impairing muscle growth and maintenance. This is particularly true for patients with poor nutritional status, in whom excessive physical exertion may instead increase the likelihood of sarcopenia. In young women with low energy intake, high PA is associated with an elevated risk of sarcopenia [9]. Therefore, it is of great importance to identify the optimal threshold of exercise dose for preventing sarcopenia in PD patients.

Linear correlation can only reflect the average change in sarcopenia risk corresponding to per unit change in PA energy consumption, but cannot identify potential thresholds or inflection points of PA. The Restricted Cubic Spline (RCS) model is a statistical approach for exploring nonlinear relationships between two variables. It integrates the simplicity of linear regression and the flexibility of nonparametric methods, and has become one of the most commonly used analytical tools for dose–response relationships [10,11]. Therefore, to further investigate the optimal PA dose threshold for sarcopenia in PD patients, this study will adopt the RCS model to analyze the dose-response relationships between the PA consumption and the prevalence of sarcopenia as well as its three parameters in PD patients, so as to identify the optimal PA range for reducing the prevalence of sarcopenia.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This was a cross-sectional study. Based on the incidence of sarcopenia in Chinese PD patients of approximately 13% [12], the calculated minimum sample size was 643 cases. A convenient sampling method was adopted, and a total of 881 patients who received regular PD treatment and were available for routine follow-up were recruited from the Peritoneal Dialysis Centers of two large general hospitals in China, from March 2023 to February 2025. Participants aged ≥ 18 years, receiving regular peritoneal dialysis for ≥ 3 month, with clear consciousness and normal verbal communication ability, and who were able to cooperate with handgrip strength (HGS) measurement, the five-repetition sit-to-stand test (5-STS) and body composition analysis were included in this study. (i) Participants receiving concurrent hemodialysis treatment or (ii) those with severe cardiac insufficiency, hepatic insufficiency, respiratory failure, or infection within the past month were excluded. This study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Nanchang University (Approval No.: NCUREC202401001). All participants signed the informed consent forms. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Charlson comorbidity index

The Age-adjusted Charlson Comorbidity Index (ACCI) was used to assess the comorbidity status of patients. This scale is a scoring system constructed by integrating the age-equivalent index on the basis of the Charlson Comorbidity Index [13,14]. The total ACCI score was calculated by summing the age-related comorbidity risk score and the Charlson comorbidity score for each patient, with a higher ACCI score indicating a higher severity of illness [13].

Physical activity assessment

The Chinese Low Physical Activity Questionnaire (C-LoPAQ), originally developed by Johansen et al. [15] and localized into Chinese by Huang et al. [16] was adopted in this study. This questionnaire investigates the frequency and duration of three types of walking activity (transport, fitness, and leisure) performed for at least 10 min per session over the past 30 days, as well as the frequency and duration of PA at three intensity levels (light, moderate, and vigorous). LPA refers to exercise that causes a slight increase in breathing or heart rate, during which one is able to talk or sing. Moderate physical activity (MPA) refers to exercise that leads to a moderate rise in breathing or heart rate; one can talk but cannot sing while performing such activity. VPA refers to exercise that results in a marked increase in breathing and heart rate, making it impossible to carry on a conversation continuously. This questionnaire identifies walking as LPA and quantifies the calorie expenditure of all leisure activities. Sedentary time and daytime napping time (recumbency) are defined as daytime inactive time, which is regarded as one of the negative evaluation criteria for PA.

The energy consumption of different physical activities is quantified in Metabolic Equivalent of Task (MET). The energy consumption per minute for each type of PA is defined as follows: all forms of walking are assigned 3.0 MET; LPA is 2.0 MET; MPA is 4.0 MET; and VPA is 6.0 MET [16]. PA consumption is calculated based on the MET value, activity type, weekly frequency, and duration. The formula is expressed as: PA consumption (MET-h/w) = MET × weekly frequency of each PA × daily activity duration. Total PA consumption is defined as the sum of the consumption of all types of PA.

Diagnostic assessments for sarcopenia

Sarcopenia was diagnosed in accordance with the diagnostic criteria formulated by the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS) in 2019 [17], which is defined as low muscle mass accompanied by low muscle strength and/or low physical performance. The Skeletal Muscle Index (SMI) was measured using a body composition analyzer (Inbody270, South Korea) [18]. PD patients underwent bioelectrical impedance analysis after an overnight fast. The HGS was measured with an electronic dynamometer (CAMRY EH101, China). The 5-STS was defined as the time recorded to complete five consecutive sit-to-stand movements as quickly as possible [19].

Covariates

All RCS models were adjusted for sex, age, dialysis vintage, body mass index (BMI), and ACCI.

Statistical analysis

In this study, RCS analysis was used to investigate the dose-response relationships between the continuous changes in PA consumption, different types of walking (transport walking, fitness walking, leisure walking, total walking), physical activities of different intensities (LPA, MPA, VPA), daytime inactive time and the association strength with sarcopenia and its three diagnostic parameters in PD patients. The 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles were set as the knots for PA levels. After adjusting for relevant confounding factors, the dose-response relationships and statistical parameters between the continuous changes in various PA levels and the association strength with sarcopenia as well as its three diagnostic indicators were finally obtained. If the overall p > 0.05 and nonlinear p > 0.05, it indicates that there is no significant association between the independent variable and the outcome index, nor a nonlinear relationship; if the overall p < 0.05 and nonlinear p > 0.05, it suggests a significant association between the independent variable and the outcome index without a nonlinear relationship; if the overall p < 0.05 and nonlinear p < 0.05, it demonstrates the existence of a nonlinear relationship between the independent variable and the outcome index. Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS 26.0 and Zstats v1.0 software, with a two-tailed p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 881 PD patients were included in this study, among whom 92 cases (10.44%) were diagnosed with sarcopenia. The majority of PD patients engaged in leisure walking (69.47%), followed by transport walking (12.03%), and the smallest proportion participated in fitness walking (8.85%). In terms of PA intensity, most patients mainly took part in LPA (70.72%), followed by MPA (17.14%), and only 0.79% (Seven patients with PD) of patients engaged in VPA (See Table 1).

Discussion

Ensuring adequate PA is the primary strategy for reducing the prevalence of sarcopenia in PD patients

This study found that the RCS curve approximately presented an inverted U-shaped pattern, with an inflection point identified at 33 MET-h/w of weekly total PA consumption. This finding is inconsistent with those in the general study populations (e.g. people aged >60 years) [20]. Qi et al. [21] reported an approximately U-shaped nonlinear relationship between PA levels and the risk of sarcopenia. The discrepancy in results may be attributed to the unique study population of PD patients in this research. PD patients are often limited from engaging in VPA due to fatigue, physical pain, comorbidities, and other factors [22]. Meanwhile, the initial upward trend of the RCS curve in Figure 1a may be attributed to insufficient baseline PA among these patients. Such low activity levels fail to stimulate muscle growth, potentially leading to disuse muscular atrophy and an elevated risk of sarcopenia. Moreover, sarcopenia itself impairs PA capacity, making it difficult for patients to reach the recommended exercise dose. This further accelerates muscle loss and ultimately forms a vicious cycle. When PA consumption exceeds 33 MET-h/w, the activity consumption exerts a definite protective effect against sarcopenia. Studies have confirmed that high-intensity PA can reduce the risk of sarcopenia more significantly, and maintaining regular PA also plays a positive role in sarcopenia prevention [23]. Resistance exercise can increase muscle fiber density, muscle cross-sectional area, and muscle mass [24,25].

Clinical guidelines recommend that PD patients engage in at least 150 min/w of walking activity [26], which equals 450 MET-min/w. Compared with the critical value of 33 MET-h/w (1980 MET-min/w) identified in this study, walking alone is far from sufficient for the prevention and management of sarcopenia, and it is necessary to combine walking with moderate amounts of LPA or MVPA. Further decomposition analysis of total PA consumption showed that none of the individual exercise types were significantly associated with sarcopenia. However, total PA consumption was significantly associated with the prevalence of sarcopenia. This indicates that regardless of the form of exercise, as long as the total PA consumption reaches a certain level, it may exert a protective effect on the prevention and management of sarcopenia in PD patients.

Therefore, given that physical discomfort and fatigue experienced by PD patients during dialysis can further restrict their exercise capacity [27], we recommend 2 h of daily low-to-moderate PA. Notably, sarcopenia may further limit patients’ PA ability. To ensure the prescribed activity consumption can be accomplished, a fragmented exercise strategy is suggested, allowing the 2-h daily activity to be completed in multiple separate sessions. Furthermore, even LPA is beneficial for reducing the risk of sarcopenia in PD patients [28]. For individuals who are unable to perform MPA, MPA can be replaced with LPA or walking activity instead.

Sole PA is insufficient to improve SMI levels in PD patients

The study results confirmed that there was no significant association between total PA consumption and SMI. However, Previous studies [29–32] have indicated that PA exerts a significant beneficial effect on muscle mass, with higher PA levels correlating with a lower risk of low muscle mass, strength and quality. This may be attributable to the fact that the present study analyzed the relationship between weekly total PA consumption and SMI, in which walking and LPA accounted for a large proportion, while MVPA constituted only a small fraction. In contrast, previous evidence has indicated that MVPA or resistance training is significantly associated with SMI.

Further decomposition analysis of total PA consumption showed that none of the individual exercise types were significantly associated with SMI. This may be due to the complex effects of different types of PA on muscle mass, as well as potential synergistic effects between various PA modalities—for example, walking trains lower limb muscle mass while resistance band exercise improves upper limb muscle strength. Additionally, clinical practice guidelines for sarcopenia [33] recommend combining aerobic exercise with resistance band training, which can effectively enhance muscle strength and endurance. Thus, considering a single type of activity in isolation may not accurately reflect its impact on muscle mass. It is suggested that clinical interventions to improve SMI in PD patients should abandon the single-type PA mindset and emphasize the synergistic effects of total PA consumption and PA types. A combined exercise strategy prioritizing resistance training supplemented by aerobic activity is recommended, rather than merely increasing daily PA levels.

Sustaining LPA is an effective Strategy for improving HGS in PD patients

The results showed that HGS presented an increasing trend with the rise in weekly total PA consumption (See Figure 2c). This finding is consistent with the study by Chen et al. [34], who reported that resistance band training increased HGS by 19% in pre-frail older adults within the first 4 weeks of intervention and by 30% after 8 weeks, indicating that HGS increases with the elevation of PA levels. Studies by Olvera-Soto et al. [35] and Wu et al. [36] both demonstrated that 12 weeks of resistance exercise or aerobic exercise (Baduanjin) could improve HGS in dialysis patients. This is because PA, especially activities involving the hands or upper limbs, can directly stimulate the contraction of muscle fibers, maintain the basic functions of muscles, and promote the improvement of muscle strength. By observing the RCS curve in Figure 2c, HGS shows an upward trend with the increase in LPA duration. A systematic review by Ramsey et al. [37] also found that higher levels of total PA and LPA, as well as lower levels of sedentary behavior, were associated with better upper limb strength. When PA is sufficient, LPA helps maintain basic muscle function, promote blood circulation, and is more conducive to improving upper limb muscle strength in patients. In addition, compared with MVPA, LPA is more acceptable to PD patients, thus improving their exercise compliance.

Therefore, healthcare providers should prioritize encouraging and guiding PD patients to maintain LPA as a fundamental intervention, and gradually introduce targeted upper limb resistance training (e.g. resistance band exercises) on this basis, so as to effectively improve and maintain patients’ HGS.

Rational arrangement of PA is a key step in improving the 5-STS performance in PD patients

This study identified that the RCS curve exhibited an approximate L-shaped pattern. PA consumption of 43 MET-h/w yielded the maximum improvement in 5‑STS, and the protective effect remained stable when PA consumption exceeded 43 MET-h/w. Based on the calculation: 43 MET-h/w (equivalent to 369 MET-min/d) divided by 3 MET equals 123 min/d, indicating that PD patients who engage in at least 2 h of walking or light-to-moderate PA per day can achieve the maximum improvement in physical function. The results revealed correlations among total walking time, LPA time, total inactive time, and 5-STS performance. A decreasing trend in 5-STS completion time with the increase in total walking time (See Figure 2d). 5-STS completion time also decreased with the extension of LPA duration, demonstrating that even LPA can potentially improve physical function in PD patients. The findings of Cao et al. [38] indicated that progressive resistance training could improve lower limb strength, whole-body balance and motor coordination, thereby shortening sit-to-stand test time. A Meta-analysis [39] also showed that traditional Chinese exercises could enhance balance function, knee flexion and extension muscle strength, and reduce chair sit-to-stand time in middle-aged and elderly patients with sarcopenia, which is consistent with the results of the present study. PA, especially lower limb exercise, can enhance the strength and endurance of leg muscles, as well as improve patients’ balance and aerobic capacity [40]. Individuals who engage in regular PA have relatively stronger lower limb muscle strength and endurance, with improved muscle coordination and flexibility, enabling them to complete the 5-STS more quickly. In addition, 5-STS completion time showed little change when weekly daytime inactive time was less than 28 h. However, the completion time increased significantly when weekly daytime inactive time exceeded 28 h. This implies that sedentary behavior or napping for less than 4 h per day has a negligible impact on 5-STS performance, while periods exceeding 4 h may lead to prolonged 5-STS completion time and potential decline in physical function. It is therefore recommended that PD patients change their inactive behaviors and interrupt prolonged sedentary periods with walking or LPA.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, it adopted a cross-sectional study design, which results in a weak exploration of the causal relationship between PA and sarcopenia in PD patients. Longitudinal studies are warranted in future research to investigate this causal relationship. Second, PA levels were assessed using a questionnaire survey, which may be subject to recall bias. Wearable devices could be employed to collect objective PA data of patients in subsequent studies. Third, due to the small number of patients engaging in transport walking, fitness walking, MPA and VPA, no dose-response relationship between these activities and sarcopenia was identified in this study. Future research needs to expand the sample size to verify the potential dose-response relationships. In addition, subsequent studies should further control for confounding factors such as nutritional status to more accurately evaluate the effect of PA on sarcopenia.

Implementations

Based on the comprehensive study findings, a weekly PA consumption of at least 33 MET-h/w in PD patients is significantly associated with a lower incidence of sarcopenia. Further increasing weekly PA consumption to 45 MET-h/w can not only more effectively reduce the risk of sarcopenia but also achieve optimal 5-STS performance. Accordingly, we propose the following five PA strategies tailored to different physical conditions of PD patients.For PD patients with good physical fitness who are capable of performing MPA, it is recommended that patients perform 60 minutes of walking, 30 minutes of LPA, and 30 minutes of MPA per day. This strategy yields an approximate PA consumption of 42 MET-h/w.For PD patients with poor physical condition who cannot tolerate MPA, it is recommended that patients perform 60 minutes of walking plus 60 minutes of daily LPA (including household activities). This regimen corresponds to approximately 35 MET-h/w.For patients already diagnosed with sarcopenia, sarcopenia may impair exercise capacity. To improve PA adherence, it is recommended that patients perform 45 minutes of walking and 75 minutes of daily LPA (including household activities), with an approximate PA consumption of 33.25 MET-h/w.For PD patients whose nutritional indicators, such as serum albumin, are within the normal range, PA can be arranged according to the above three strategies. For those with nutritional deficiency, nutritional supplementation should be prioritized before initiating structured PA.Notably, to enhance patients’ adherence and target achievement rate, it is recommended that PD patients complete the 2-hour daily PA via a fragmented exercise pattern.

Conclusion

The study found a nonlinear dose-response relationship between total PA consumption and both the prevalence of sarcopenia and 5-STS performance. This suggests the existence of an optimal range of PA consumption, which is associated with reducing the incidence of sarcopenia and improving physical function. Given the unique characteristics of PD patients, it is recommended that they engage in 2 h of light-to-moderate PA daily (including walking and household chores) and combine this with resistance training to maximize improvements in muscle health.
