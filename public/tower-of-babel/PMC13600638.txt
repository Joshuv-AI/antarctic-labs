Endothelium-dependent Coronary Microvascular Dysfunction – A Diagnosis by Integrated Non-invasive Imaging
Authors: Nicola Gaibazzi, Jiri Sklenar, Attila Kardos, Benedetta Albiani, Sergio Suma, Rebecca Navacchi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) is a common but often underdiagnosed cause of ischemia and angina in patients with angina and no obstructive coronary artery disease (ANOCA). The reliance on invasive testing contributes to delayed or missed diagnosis. Exercise-induced ST-segment abnormalities in ANOCA patients may serve as a non-invasive marker, particularly reflecting impaired endothelium-dependent vasodilation. We report the case of a middle-aged man undergoing a treadmill exercise test for competitive sports eligibility. The patient developed atypical chest pain with significant (>2 mm) down-sloping ST depression in precordial and inferior leads during exertion. Coronary computed tomography angiography revealed normal coronary arteries, raising suspicion for CMD. Further assessment with dipyridamole stress echocardiography demonstrated normal coronary flow velocity reserve (CFVR) in the left anterior descending artery = 3.2, indicating preserved endothelium-independent vasodilation. However, contrast myocardial perfusion imaging revealed patchy areas of delayed replenishment–indicative of CMD and suggestive of impaired endothelium-dependent function. Exercise-induced ST depression was reproducible during stress echocardiography. In patients with ANOCA, patchy perfusion abnormalities during vasodilator stress and preserved CFVR may reflect CMD due to endothelium-dependent dysfunction. Ecostress may offer valuable diagnostic insights and support early identification and management of CMD.

Coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) is a frequent cause of ischemia and angina in patients presenting with chest pain or breathlessness on effort and is often encountered in patients without angina and no obstructive coronary artery disease (ANOCA). The need for invasive functional tests is a significant limitation that leads to underdiagnosis and undertreatment of CMD.

Exercise-induced ST abnormalities in ANOCA are highly specific for CMD, most often subtended by an impaired endothelium-dependent pathway.[1]

We describe a case of a middle-aged man, referred for a treadmill exercise test for competitive sport eligibility assessment, who developed atypical chest pain with >2 mm down-sloping ST depression in the precordial and inferior leads during exercise. Subsequent non-invasive investigations were performed for further diagnostics.

As shown in Figure 1a, coronary computed tomography angiography showed normal coronary arteries (left anterior descending and right coronary arteries shown), without plaques, normal coronary origin, raising the suspicion of CMD.

Multimodal assessment of coronary microvascular function: (a) CT showing no significant atherosclerosis; (b) Stress echocardiography demonstrating normal coronary flow reserve; (c) Myocardial contrast ultrasound perfusion imaging showing patchy perfusion defects; (d) ECG during dipyridamole-echocardiography revealing ST-segment depression

Vasodilator stress-echocardiography with high-dose dipyridamole - 0.84mg/kg over 6 min was performed. Regional wall motion abnormality and Doppler measurement of coronary flow reserve in the left anterior descending artery (CFVR-LAD), as well as myocardial contrast-perfusion imaging, were assessed.

Figure 1b shows normal CFVR-LAD (diastolic stress/rest velocity ratio = 3.2), confirming intact endothelium-independent vasodilation.

As shown in Figure 1c, myocardial perfusion imaging at peak stress was assessed by the flash-replenishment method using parametric imaging (narnar, LLC). Time to myocardial replenishment is color-coded; green pixels represent normal, purple pixels demonstrate patchy areas of slow flow (white arrows) not following the typical endo-epicardial distribution of ischemia, typical to CMD.

As shown in Figure 1d, down-sloping ST depression was reproducible on the 12-lead ECG during stress echocardiography (red arrows).

A pattern of patchy myocardial perfusion at peak vasodilation during contrast stress-echocardiography may be indicative of CMD; notably, stress or acetylcholine-induced heterogeneous myocardial perfusion has been described in CMD patients.[234] In the presence of normal CFVR-LAD by stress-echocardiography, such a pattern may present an impaired endothelium-dependent pathway.
