Residual characteristics and chronic health risk assessment of multiple pesticides in 23 daily-consumed vegetables from Shandong, China: A 4-year investigation
Authors: Da Cai, Hongshuai Sun, Yemin Guo, Yanjie Dong, Lei Wang, Xia Sun, Shancang Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPesticide residues in vegetables have raised an extensive concern. However, the residues and integrated risks of multiple pesticides in various vegetables from Shandong province, a leading vegetable producer in China, remain unclear thus far. Herein, we performed a 4-year investigation on residues of 33 commonly-used pesticides in 23 daily-consumed vegetables from Shandong province during 2018–2021, and comprehensively assessed their chronic health risks for different age groups using both deterministic and probabilistic approaches. Among the 1067 samples, procymidone, carbendazim, and thiamethoxam were the top three detected pesticides. Notably, 6 prohibited pesticides, including carbofuran, omethoate, phorate, methamidophos, chlorpyrifos, and fipronil, were also found in vegetables. Based on the maximum residue limits (MRLs) stipulated in GB 2763, carbofuran, omethoate, and avermectins exhibited the highest over-MRL rates. Moreover, 34.7% of the samples contained pesticide residues. Multiple pesticides were simultaneously detected in 10.2% of the samples. Among multi-residue samples, the percentage of samples containing 2 pesticides was the highest, and the most common combination of residues was procymidone + carbendazim. Additionally, 2.3% of the samples were found with pesticides above the MRLs. More attention should be paid to leaf lettuce, kidney bean, leek, and celery, given the relatively higher over-MRL rates. Although we found that both the individual and cumulative exposure risks of these pesticides for different age groups were within established safety limits (below 1), the dietary exposure risks for children were higher than that for the other three age groups. Omethoate, carbofuran, phorate, and avermectins were the key contributors to the potential health risk for inhabitants. Given the ubiquity of pesticide residues as well as the detection and exceeding MRLs of banned pesticides, the potential health hazards of pesticides in vegetables should be continuously assessed. These findings provide a valuable reference for future pesticide management and risk assessment programs.Keywords: Multi-residue analysis, Vegetables, Dietary exposure, Chronic risk, Cumulative exposure assessment, Probabilistic analysis, UPLC-MS/MS, GC-MS/MS

Introduction

Vegetables are an essential part of a healthy diet for humans and important sources of vitamins, minerals, phytochemicals, and dietary fiber (Zhang et al., 2021). To protect vegetable crops from pests, diseases, and weeds, and improve the quality and yields of vegetables, pesticides have been widely used in the vegetable growth process in past decades (Khazaal et al., 2022). However, excessive use of pesticides may pose serious health risks to consumers due to their toxicity, as well as adverse effects on the environment (Hu et al., 2019). A growing body of evidence indicates a relationship between exposure to pesticides and the incidence of human diseases, such as acute neurotoxicity, neurodevelopmental disorders, allergies, birth defects, diabetes, and cancer (Valcke et al., 2017; Chowdhury et al., 2013; Lv et al., 2022). In fact, dietary intake of vegetables is considered a major exposure pathway to pesticide residues for the general consumers, especially when they are eaten raw (Li et al., 2022). Hence, a systematic investigation into the presence and potential health risks of pesticide residues in vegetables is extremely necessary.

To protect healthy diets of consumers, national pesticide residue monitoring programs have been established in numerous countries to monitor the levels of pesticide residues in foods (Jardim and Caldas, 2012; Jardim and Caldas, 2024; Poulsen et al., 2017; Chen et al., 2019; EFSA, 2021; EFSA, 2022; FAO & WHO, 2013; FAO & WHO, 2025). There have also been many studies on the occurrences of pesticide residues in vegetables from specific regions. Osaili et al. found that acephate, methamidophos and profenofos were the most frequently detected pesticides in vegetables imported into the United Arab Emirates (Osaili et al., 2022). Wang et al. found that the most detected insecticide was chlorpyrifos with a detection rate of 16.00%, by analyzing pesticide residues in eight minor vegetables from Guizhou province in Southwest China (Wang et al., 2022a). Qin et al. reported that difenoconazole and procymidone were pesticides exceeding the maximum residue limits (MRLs) in vegetables, while carbendazim was the most common pesticide in vegetables in the mid-western region of China (Qin et al., 2021). Due to the diversity and complexity of cropping systems, cultivation patterns, climate conditions, and geographic distributions among different regions, the occurrence of insect pests, varieties of pesticide use, frequency of pesticide application, and dissipation of pesticides in crops are different among regions (Wang et al., 2022b). Therefore, owing to these differences, the incidence and severity of pesticide residues would vary substantially across regions. Shandong province, located on the east coast of China, is among the most important agricultural regions in the country. It ranks first in both vegetable production (>80 million tons over eight consecutive years) and export (>27 billion yuan over five consecutive years) among the 31 provinces or cities in China. Zhang et al. monitored chlorothalonil residues in vegetables from Shandong province, and found that the health risk of chlorothalonil to consumers was low (Zhang et al., 2016). Jiang et al. analyzed 26 pesticide residues in radishes in Shandong province, and found that the most frequently detected pesticides were organophosphorus pesticides (Jiang et al., 2022). These available data are focused on specific pesticides or particular vegetables. However, to the best of our knowledge, the comprehensive research on residues and cumulative exposure risks of multiple pesticides in a variety of daily-consumed vegetables from Shandong province has rarely been reported thus far, which, undoubtedly, is required.

For quantitative exposure assessment, food consumption and pesticide occurrence data are usually combined applying either a deterministic (point estimate) or probabilistic (stochastic) approach (Nougadère et al., 2012; Chen et al., 2019; Radulović et al., 2023; Lučić and Onjia, 2025a; Lučić and Onjia, 2025b). The deterministic method calculates the risk quotient by comparing the exposure level to the threshold value at which the hazard is generated, which is a simple, rapid, and inexpensive approach and is often used for screening-level risk assessments (Chen et al., 2019). The probabilistic methodology is preferred for evaluating the cumulative dietary exposure, which requires mathematical modeling to produce an exposure distribution. This method is most reflective of the realistic exposure, and yet the probabilistic models are complex and difficult to generate (FAO, 2006; Yao et al., 2024). Thus, the combination of the deterministic and probabilistic approaches offers the distinct advantage of both capturing the exposure risk in the worst-case scenario and accounting for variability in potential exposure. Nevertheless, the relevant reports regarding the combined application of these two methods to assess dietary exposure risk of pesticide residues are relatively limited, and only Duan et al., Radulović et al., Yao et al., and Lučić et al. evaluated the exposure risks of pesticide residues in cowpeas (Duan et al., 2016), citrus fruits (Radulović et al., 2023), Agrocybe aegerita (Yao et al., 2024), root and tuber vegetables (Lučić and Onjia, 2025a), and peppers (Lučić and Onjia, 2025b), respectively, by combining two methods.

The primary purpose of this study is to monitor residues of 33 commonly-used pesticides in various vegetables from Shandong province, China, and further evaluate whether daily dietary exposure to the pesticides through vegetable consumption could pose cumulative risks to the health of different age groups. To achieve the aim, a four-year investigation on the residue status and integrated risks of the 33 pesticides in 23 daily-consumed vegetables from 6 cities (major vegetable producing areas) in Shandong province was conducted. A total of 1067 samples were analyzed by ultra-performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC-MS/MS), gas chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (GC-MS/MS), and gas chromatography (GC). Both deterministic and probabilistic approaches were applied to assess the individual and cumulative dietary exposure risks of these pesticides for different age groups, including children, teenagers, adults, and elderly people. These results will help to master the types of pesticide residues and pollution levels, and provide a scientific basis for safe production, market supervision, consumption guidance, standard revision, and risk assessment.

The novelty of this work in terms of study scale, monitoring duration, vegetable diversity, banned pesticide focus, and assessment method was highlighted quantitatively by a structured comparison with recent relevant research (Lučić and Onjia, 2025a; Lučić and Onjia, 2025b; Park et al., 2023; Kottadiyil et al., 2023; Dong et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2022a; Ping et al., 2022). As shown in Table S1, the sample sizes of the previous studies ranged from 197 to 580, covered at most 1–2 years, included 1–33 kinds of vegetables (only 1–3 categories), targeted at most 2 banned pesticides (except one study with 8), analyzed 20–339 pesticides, and mostly used deterministic assessment. In contrast, the present work comprises 1067 samples, spans four consecutive years, covers 23 kinds of vegetables across 7 categories, focuses on 13 banned/restricted pesticides, monitors 33 pesticides in total, and combines deterministic with probabilistic assessment. Thus, this research is the first to simultaneously integrate such large scale, long multi-year temporal scope, high vegetable diversity, broad banned pesticide focus, and dual assessment approaches.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and reagents

The 33 certified standards of individual pesticides were obtained from the Agro-Environmental Protection Institute of the Ministry of Agriculture (Tianjin, China). The purity of all pesticide standards was 99.00%∼99.90%. Individual standard stock solutions (1000 mg/L) were prepared in HPLC-grade acetonitrile, methanol, or acetone, based on solubility. All the standard solutions were stored at −20 °C and away from sunlight. HPLC-grade acetonitrile (CH3CN), methanol (CH3OH), formic acid (HCOOH), and acetone (CH3COCH3) were purchased from Merck Co. (Darmstadt, Germany). Analytical-grade anhydrous magnesium sulfate (MgSO4), sodium chloride (NaCl), sodium citrate tribasic dihydrate (C6H5Na3O7·2H2O), sodium citrate dibasic sesquihydrate (C6H6Na2O7·1.5H2O), and ammonium acetate (CH3COONH4) were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent (Beijing, China). Primary secondary amine (PSA) and graphitized carbon black (GCB) were obtained from Agela Technologies (Tianjin, China). All solutions were prepared using ultra-pure water (18.2 MΩ cm at 25 °C) obtained from a Milli-Q system (Millipore, MA, USA).

Sample collection

A total of 1067 vegetable samples were randomly collected from local farms, farmers’ markets, vegetable companies, supermarkets, and wholesale markets in six cities in Shandong province (Jinan, Weifang, Zibo, Linyi, Liaocheng, and Rizhao) (Fig. 1) from 2018 to 2021 (Table S2). In total, 23 vegetable species were selected to ensure a representative selection of vegetables that are commonly consumed daily by local residents and often contaminated by pesticides, including zucchini (n = 39), leek (n = 64), capsicum (n = 85), tomato (n = 71), eggplant (n = 102), cucumber (n = 130), leaf lettuce (n = 28), sweet pepper (n = 31), garland chrysanthemum (n = 27), garlic sprout (n = 12), lettuce (n = 26), broccolis (n = 14), cabbage (n = 38), bitter gourd (n = 14), kidney bean (n = 35), cauliflower (n = 26), celery (n = 60), pak-choi (n = 77), endive (n = 7), Chinese cabbage (n = 69), rape (n = 41), spinach (n = 40), and cowpea (n = 31). The availability and distribution of vegetable samples were the main factors contributing to the unequal distribution of sample sizes among the vegetables. Certain specialty minor vegetables, such as endive, are cultivated on a small scale and in a dispersed manner, and consequently exhibit limited market presence. Yet a larger number of cucumber, eggplant, capsicum, and tomato samples were intentionally collected, as these major vegetables are widely consumed, making them a key focus for assessing potential dietary exposure to pesticide residues. A minimum of 3.0 kg of each sample was collected, placed in an ice chest at 4 °C, and transported to the laboratory, where the samples were stored at 4 °C and promptly processed within 24 h. The quartering method was used to reduce the samples, and 250∼500 g of each sample was homogenized for further extraction and analysis (Agricultural Industry Standard of China NY/T 789-2004, 2004). Pesticide residue analysis was based on fresh weight.

Location of sampling in Shandong province, China.

Sample preparation

Chlorothalonil was extracted from various samples according to NY/T 761-2008 (Agricultural Industry Standard of China NY/T 761-2008, 2008). The other 32 pesticides were extracted from vegetable samples using a modified QuEChERS (Quick, Easy, Cheap, Effective, Rugged, and Safe) method (Balkan and Yılmaz, 2022; Durak et al., 2020; Narenderan et al., 2019). Briefly, 10 g (±0.01 g) of each homogenized sample was placed into a 50-mL centrifuge tube. Next, 10 mL of acetonitrile was added and shaken vigorously for 1 min. Then, 4 g of anhydrous MgSO4, 1 g of NaCl, 1 g of C6H5Na3O7·2H2O, and 0.5 g of C6H6Na2O7·1.5H2O were added to each of these samples. The sample tubes were shaken vigorously and then centrifuged at 1830 g for 5 min. After that, 6 mL of the extracting solution was transferred to a centrifuge tube containing 900 mg of anhydrous MgSO4 and 150 mg of PSA. For dark-colored vegetables, 15 mg of GCB was added. The tubes were then vortexed and centrifuged at 1500 g for 5 min. Thereafter, each supernatant was divided into two parts. A portion of the supernatant was filtered by a 0.22 μm syringe filter for UPLC-MS/MS analysis. Another 2 mL was evaporated to almost dryness under dry nitrogen flow at 40 °C, then redissolved to 1 mL using acetone, and filtered by a 0.22 μm syringe filter for GC-MS/MS analysis.

Instrumental analysis

Chlorothalonil was analyzed by a Shimadzu 2010 plus gas chromatograph (Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan) with an electron capture detector (ECD). An SH-Rtx-5 column (30 m × 0.25 mm, 0.25 μm) (Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan) was used. Nitrogen (purity 99.999%) was applied as carrier gas and auxiliary gas. The inlet temperature and the detector temperature were set at 250 °C and 300 °C, respectively. Samples (1 μL) were injected in the split mode (10: 1). The column temperature program was as follows: the initial temperature was maintained at 50 °C for 1 min, increased to 200 °C at a rate of 30 °C/min and held for 8 min, and then raised to 290 °C at a rate of 30 °C/min and held there for 3 min. The total run time was 20 min.

An ultra-performance liquid chromatography system coupled to an 8050 triple-quadrupole mass spectrometer (Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan) was used to analyze 21 pesticides (Table 1). The separation was performed on an Agilent Zorbax Eclipse plus C18 column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 1.8 μm) with a gradient elution at a flow rate of 0.35 mL/min. The mobile phase consisted of solvent A (0.1% formic acid and 5 mmol/L ammonium acetate in water) and solvent B (methanol). The mobile phase was run with the following program: 0 min 5% B, 1 min 5% B, 4 min 55% B, 12 min 95% B, 14 min 95% B, 14.01 min 5% B, and 15 min 5% B. The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C. The injection volume was 2 μL. The mass spectrometer was operated with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source in multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode. The MRM parameters of the 21 pesticides are shown in Table S3. The interface temperature, desolvation line (DL) temperature, and heat block temperature were set to 300 °C, 250 °C, and 400 °C, respectively. Nitrogen was used as the nebulizer gas and drying gas with flow rates of 3 L/min and 10 L/min, respectively.

The selection of the 33 target pesticides in the analytical scope was based on official data from Shandong province (Reference Limits for Pesticide Residues in Routine Monitoring of Vegetables and Fruits in Shandong Province, http://nyncj.rizhao.gov.cn/art/2020/8/27/art_30847_9696556.html), which explicitly list these 33 pesticides and their MRLs, providing the official regulatory basis for routine monitoring in Shandong. These pesticides were prioritized in relation to high frequency of application and high toxicity (banned/restricted pesticides). These high-frequency applications correspond to major pest and disease pressures in Shandong vegetable production, including downy mildew on cucumber, gray mold on tomato, and lepidopteran pests (e.g., pod borers) on beans. A positive residue finding or residues exceeding MRLs in prior provincial monitoring programs were also criteria for including the pesticides. Given budget constraints, it was not feasible to measure all pesticides registered for vegetable use in China; however, the most commonly applied pesticides in Shandong, as reflected in the above document, were included.

Calculation of target hazard quotient (THQ)

THQ was the ratio of the level of exposure of each individual pesticide to the maximum acceptable level for each pesticide, representing the long-term risk of a pesticide. The THQ of each pesticide consumed through vegetables could be evaluated based on the acceptable daily intake (ADI), which was obtained from the National Standard of China GB 2763 (National Standard of China GB 2763-2021, 2021). The deterministic assessment and probabilistic assessment were carried out. The Monte Carlo simulation method was used for probabilistic assessment by the @RISK 7.6 software. Distribution fitting was conducted for all detected pesticide residues to obtain the optimal fitting distributions and fitting parameters (Table S5). The input variables without assigned probability distributions were treated as fixed-point values throughout the Monte Carlo simulations. As the available literature provided only average values for vegetable consumption and body weight, rather than detailed data, these variables were therefore represented by their mean values (Wu et al., 2018). For left-censored residue data (below the limit of detection, LOD), a value of LOD was assigned as a worst-case scenario, according to WHO/GEMS-Food (1995). The Monte Carlo simulations were performed with 10000 iterations to ensure the robustness and reliability of the results.

Results and discussion

Method validation

Method validation has been performed in the LC-MS/MS, GC-MS/MS, and GC analytical systems to verify reliability of the multi-residue analysis method for the 33 pesticide analytes. As indicated in Table S6, good linearity was obtained with regression coefficients (R2) over 0.99 for all the tested pesticides. The limit of detection (LOD) and limit of quantification (LOQ) were calculated using signal-to-noise ratios of 3 and 10, respectively (Wang et al., 2022a). For all target compounds, the LODs were in the range of 0.1 μg/kg to 1.2 μg/kg, and LOQs ranged from 0.3 μg/kg to 4.1 μg/kg (Table S6). The LODs and LOQs demonstrate the satisfactory sensitivity of the method described herein. The recoveries of the spiked standards were in the range of 80.1%–112.8%, with relative standard deviation (RSD) values of 0.7%–13.4% in different matrix samples (Table S7), thereby meeting the requirements for analyzing and monitoring of pesticide residues in vegetable samples. As shown in Table S8, matrix effect (ME) values varied between −19.7% and 16.6%, which was acceptable (Wang et al., 2022a; Ping et al., 2022; Lučić and Onjia, 2025b). Furthermore, based on these satisfactory validation results, the expanded measurement uncertainty (k = 2) for all reported values was determined to be less than 50%, in accordance with the SANTE/11312/2021 guideline (European Commission, 2021). Consequently, this default uncertainty was applied throughout the study. Hence, these results showed that the method was reliable and could be applied on the determination of pesticide residues in vegetables.

Occurrences of different pesticide residues in vegetables

Residues of 33 pesticides were analyzed by LC-MS/MS, GC-MS/MS, and GC in 23 highly consumed vegetables. A total of 24 (72.7%) compounds were detected and quantified in 1067 samples (Table 2). The most common occurring pesticide was procymidone which was detected in 11.4% of the samples, followed by carbendazim (9.9%), thiamethoxam (4.3%), dimethomorph (4.1%), and imidacloprid (3.1%). Specifically, procymidone showed the highest detection rates in sweet pepper (32.3%, 10/31), celery (23.3%, 14/60), cucumber (23.1%, 30/130), and tomato (21.1%, 15/71), as shown in Fig. 2. Carbendazim was detected most frequently in endive samples, with a detection rate of 42.9% (3/7), followed by cowpea (25.8%, 8/31), leaf lettuce (25%, 7/28), and bitter gourd (21.4%, 3/14). Thiamethoxam had the highest detection rate in garlic sprout (75%, 9/12).

The high prevalence of procymidone and carbendazim found in our study has been reported in other areas. A previous study showed that procymidone was the most frequently detected pesticide in vegetables from South Korea (Park et al., 2016), which was consistent with our observations. Another study revealed that the detection rate of procymidone was 8.9% for vegetables from the mid-western region of China (Qin et al., 2021). The higher detection rate of procymidone observed in our study compared to the 8.9% reported by Qin et al. may be attributable to Shandong's predominance of greenhouse cultivation, where the highly humid and poorly ventilated microenvironment favors Botrytis cinerea outbreaks and consequently necessitates more frequent procymidone applications. Procymidone is a low-toxicity fungicide, often used as a seed dressing, pre-harvest spray or post-harvest dip, and is therefore likely to be commonly used in many regions (Wang et al., 2023). Carbendazim is a broad-spectrum benzimidazole fungicide. Jardim et al. found that carbendazim was the most detected pesticide in food of plant origin in Brazil (Jardim and Caldas, 2024; Jardim and Caldas, 2012). Qin et al. reported that carbendazim was the most common pesticide in vegetables in the mid-western region of China (Qin et al., 2021). Tan et al. also found that carbendazim was the most prevailing pesticide residue in crops in the southernmost province of China (Hainan), with a detection frequency of 56.1% (Tan et al., 2023). The considerably higher detection rate reported by Tan et al. relative to the present study may result from Hainan's tropical climate, which sustains year-round pathogen activity with uninterrupted plant disease pressure, thereby necessitating more frequent carbendazim applications at a higher usage dosage. The above studies and our results all showed that carbendazim residue was common in vegetables, although its detection rate differed among different studies. Various factors may affect the persistence of pesticide residues in vegetables, including crop species, pesticide varieties, climate conditions, cultivation patterns, and frequency of pesticide application, among other factors (Fantke and Juraske, 2013).

Out of the 24 detected pesticides, 12 (50%) were above their existing MRLs, as established by the Chinese government, including carbofuran (0.8%), omethoate (0.3%), and avermectins (0.3%) with the highest over-MRL rates in the 1067 samples (Table 2). More specifically, carbofuran residue in kidney bean was found with the highest over-MRL rate of 11.4%, followed by Chinese cabbage (4.3%) and leek (3.1%), as shown in Fig. 3. Omethoate showed the highest over-MRL rate in leaf lettuce (3.6%), followed by leek (3.1%). Avermectins also had the highest over-MRL rate in leaf lettuce (7.1%). It has been reported that exceeded MRL is a strong indicator of violation of Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) (Nasreddine and Parent-Massin, 2002). The relatively high number of pesticides exceeding MRLs could be due to inappropriate application of pesticides by farmers (Khazaal et al., 2022). Thus, the supervision of pesticide application and vegetable production should be strengthened in the future.

It is worth noting that 6 pesticides that are strictly prohibited to be used for vegetables in China (carbofuran, omethoate, phorate, methamidophos, chlorpyrifos, and fipronil) were found in the collected samples, with detection rates of 1.3%, 0.5%, 0.1%, 0.1%, 1.7%, and 0.1%, respectively (Table 2). More importantly, of the 6 banned pesticides, 4 were above their MRLs, including carbofuran, omethoate, phorate, and chlorpyrifos, with over-MRL rates of 0.8%, 0.3%, 0.1%, and 0.2%, respectively.

The presence of carbofuran, omethoate, and chlorpyrifos observed in this study has also been documented in other regions, indicating that the use of these pesticides may not be an isolated issue confined to the present study area. For instance, a prior report showed that carbofuran residues were detectable in 2.3% of vegetables collected from 31 provinces in China (Lv et al., 2022). Additionally, carbofuran was also detected in other countries like Cameroon where 12 carbofuran-positive samples were detected with a detection rate of 7.5% (Galani et al., 2020), and Canada where carbofuran was found in 6% of the vegetable and fruit samples (Valcke et al., 2017). Carbofuran is among the most toxic carbamate pesticides (Lv et al., 2022), classified by WHO in the category of highly hazardous insecticides, class Ib (WHO, 2010). It is extremely toxic to humans and animals through cholinesterase inhibition (Lv et al., 2022), with teratogenic and embryotoxic effects (Saini et al., 2017). As such, the application of carbofuran on vegetables has been strictly prohibited (MOA, 2002). However, carbofuran was still detected in the collected vegetable samples, with some even exceeding the MRL, which was primarily attributed to its illegal use. Also, as carbofuran is widely used in soil, it may ultimately be detectable in crops (Lv et al., 2022). Besides, as to the banned pesticide omethoate, similarly to our results, previous studies showed that the detection rate of omethoate was 0.9% for vegetables and fruits in China (Li et al., 2021), and that omethoate residues were detected in 129/5560 analyzed vegetable samples that imported into the United Arab Emirates with a detection rate of 2.3% (Osaili et al., 2022). Notably, the residues of omethoate and methamidophos may arise from metabolites of dimethoate and acephate, respectively, while illegal use may be another possible reason (Yu et al., 2016). Moreover, regarding chlorpyrifos, Ping et al. reported a detection rate of 3.1% in greenhouse vegetable samples from Beijing, China (Ping et al., 2022). Of note, chlorpyrifos residues may stem from the persistence and accumulation of this pesticide in soil (Ping et al., 2022). In addition, as chlorpyrifos remains registered for non-vegetable crops in China, the detection of chlorpyrifos in the tested samples may also result from illicit application beyond the approved scope. Overall, the detection and exceeding MRLs of banned pesticides mean that the government should strengthen pesticide management and take severe penalties for the illegal use of prohibited pesticides on vegetables.

Contamination of vegetables with pesticide residues

In total, 370 (34.7%) samples of 23 different vegetable species were contaminated with pesticides. The detection rates of 23 kinds of vegetables were in the range of 7.1%–91.7% (Fig. 4A). Garlic sprout showed the highest detection rate of 91.7%, followed by celery (58.3%), garland chrysanthemum (55.6%), and sweet pepper (51.6%). Previous studies from other regions of China also showed that the detection rate of pesticides in celery was relatively higher, such as the mid-western region of China where the detection in celery was up to 78.6% (Qin et al., 2021), and Changchun city where the detection rate of celery was the highest (69.2%) (Jiang et al., 2021), which was almost similar to our observations.

Detection rates and over-MRL rates of 23 kinds of vegetables (A) and 7 types of vegetables (B).

Among these analyzed samples, 25 (2.3%) samples corresponding to 6 different vegetables were found with pesticides above the existing MRLs. The vegetable with the highest over-MRL rate was leaf lettuce (17.9%), followed by kidney bean (14.3%), leek (9.4%), celery (8.3%), Chinese cabbage (4.3%), and cucumber (0.8%) (Fig. 4A), suggesting that leaf lettuce, kidney bean, leek, and celery were of greatest concern. Yet no pesticide above its MRL was found in the other 17 vegetables. Similarly, a previous study in Korea showed that leek had a high over-MRL rate of 10.0% (Park et al., 2023). Additionally, the elevated over-MRL rate observed for kidney bean in this study can be ascribed to its nature as a typical multiple-harvest vegetable, which is characterized by successive harvests within a single growth cycle and by short harvesting intervals. These production traits substantially heighten the likelihood of conflict between the pre-harvest intervals of pesticides and the frequency of harvesting cycles. Also, this conflict is further amplified by the economic incentives for farmers to secure continuous high yields, thereby increasing pesticide residue levels in kidney bean.

According to NY/T 3177-2018 Classification and Codes of Agro-products (Agricultural Industry Standard of China NY/T 3177-2018, 2018), the 23 kinds of vegetables that were analyzed were classified into 7 categories, including allium vegetables, solanceous fruits, gourd vegetables, vegetable legumes, leafy vegetables, Chinese cabbages, and cabbages. The detection rates of 7 vegetable categories ranged from 9.0% to 43.1% (Fig. 4B). Leafy vegetables had the highest detection rate of 43.1%, followed by vegetable legumes (42.4%). The result was consistent with the observations reported by Jiang et al. who found that the detection rate of pesticides in leafy vegetables was the highest (49.0%) (Jiang et al., 2021). Also, Yu et al. found that leafy vegetables were more contaminated with organophosphorus pesticides than root and fruit vegetables and the detection rate of leafy vegetables was 95.6% (Yu et al., 2016). The markedly lower detection rate in our study (43.1%) compared to the 95.6% reported by Yu et al. (2016) in Changchun may be attributable to differences in regional cultivation patterns and vegetable traits. The Changchun samples were collected entirely from open-field vegetable plots, whereas Shandong's vegetable production is dominated by greenhouse cultivation, which generally experiences lower insect pest pressure and may rely more heavily on fungicides like procymidone and carbendazim rather than organophosphates. Additionally, the Changchun study only collected autumn vegetables destined for long-term winter storage (Yu et al., 2016). Growers may apply pesticides closer to harvest for storage-bound crops to prevent storage decay, resulting in transiently elevated residue levels during this narrow sampling window. Yet Shandong vegetables are typically marketed fresh with shorter pre-harvest intervals, reducing the likelihood of residue accumulation compared to storage-bound crops. Moreover, temporal changes in China's pesticide regulatory framework between the 2014 Changchun study and our more recent monitoring period may have reduced the availability and use of high-toxicity organophosphates in major vegetable-producing provinces including Shandong. Notwithstanding these regional differences, the higher detection rates observed in leafy vegetables compared to other vegetable types in our dataset warrant further interpretation. Leafy vegetables are more prone to insects, which necessitates more frequent pesticide applications at higher concentrations to achieve the desired insecticidal effect (Qin et al., 2015). Also, leafy vegetables, with their short growth cycles and larger surface area, readily accumulate surface-applied pesticides (Ping et al., 2022). Noticeably, the vegetable category with the highest over-MRL rate was allium vegetables (7.9%), followed by vegetable legumes (7.6%) and leafy vegetables (5.3%), whereas no pesticide above its MRL was found in solanceous fruits and cabbages. Furthermore, to investigate the statistical significance of differences in pesticide residue contamination among different categories of vegetables, the Chi-square test and Fisher's exact test were carried out to assess the detection rate and over-MRL rate, respectively. Both the detection rate (χ2 = 51.18, P < 0.001) and over-MRL rate (P < 0.001) showed statistically significant differences among 7 vegetable categories.

In vegetable production, multiple pesticides are often simultaneously applied to control various diseases and pests, however the subsequent multi-residue risk cannot be ignored. Among all samples, 261 samples (24.5%) were contaminated with one pesticide, and 109 samples (10.2%) contained two or more pesticide residues. Specifically, 79 samples (7.4%) contained two pesticides, 16 samples (1.5%) contained three pesticides, 5 samples (0.5%) contained four pesticides, 8 samples (0.7%) contained five pesticides, and 1 sample (leaf lettuce) contained six pesticides. As for the co-occurrence of multi-residues, the most prevalent combination of pesticides was procymidone + carbendazim, accounting for 17.4% of the multi-residue samples. For the 23 kinds of vegetables, except that the co-occurrence of multiple pesticides (≥2 pesticides) was not observed in 5 vegetables, namely garlic sprout, zucchini, cowpea, broccolis, and cauliflower, for the other 18 vegetables, the proportions of co-occurrence of multiple pesticide residues ranged from 11.1% to 56.3% (Fig. 5). The percentages of co-occurrence of multi-residues for different vegetable species were in the following order (the top seven): sweet pepper (56.3%) > rape (55.6%) > lettuce (44.4%) > leek (42.1%) > leaf lettuce (41.7%) > tomato (37.9%) > celery (37.1%).

Percentages of co-occurrence of multiple pesticide residues in the 23 kinds of vegetables. The pink columns represent the percentages of co-occurrence of multiple pesticide residues in vegetables (values in red). The blue columns represent the percentages of single pesticide residue in vegetables (values in blue).

In recent years, multi-residues have been often found in vegetables from many countries. A study from Brazil reported that 60.5% of positive samples contained multiple residues and that sweet pepper had the highest percentage of samples with multi-residues (Jardim and Caldas, 2024), which was consistent with our observations. European data from 2018 to 2020 revealed the presence of multi-residues in 27%–29% of all samples (EFSA, 2021; EFSA, 2022). Chen et al. found that two or more pesticide residues were detected in 14.6% of analyzed vegetable and fruit samples from Xiamen city, China (Chen et al., 2011). The presence of multiple pesticide residues in one single vegetable sample may result from the use of different pesticides or the composition of multiple pesticide ingredients into one formulation, which may aim at avoiding the development of resistance of pests or diseases, as well as may be attributed to the uptake of persistent residues from soil, spray/dust drift to fields adjacent to treated fields, and/or poor agricultural practices (EFSA, 2022).

Dietary exposure and health risk assessment

Based on the data for pesticide residues, vegetable intake and human body weight, the deterministic assessment and probabilistic assessment were performed. Firstly, EDI, THQ and HI values of different age groups were calculated by the deterministic assessment method, as shown in Table 3. THQ values ranged from 1.091 × 10−5 to 0.058 for children, 7.829 × 10−6 to 0.042 for teenagers, 8.286 × 10−6 to 0.044 for adults, and 8.078 × 10−6 to 0.043 for elderly people, i.e., much less than 1. This indicated that the exposure risks of a single type of pesticides in the 23 surveyed vegetables were well within the acceptable limits for the four different age groups. For all age groups (children, teenagers, adults, and elderly people), omethoate had the highest THQ value (0.058, 0.042, 0.044, and 0.043, respectively), followed by carbofuran (0.022, 0.016, 0.017, and 0.016, respectively), phorate (0.014, 0.010, 0.011, and 0.010, respectively), and avermectins (0.012, 0.008, 0.009, and 0.009, respectively). Notably, among the different age groups, children had the highest THQ values, followed by adults and elderly people, and teenagers showed the lowest THQ values.

Despite the fact that the risks of exposure to a single type of pesticides in vegetables were acceptable for the four age groups, many vegetables generally contained multiple pesticide residues simultaneously. Therefore, it was extremely necessary to assess the cumulative risk from a mixture of pesticides in vegetables using the HI method. The HI values for the four age groups were in the following order: children (0.188) > adults (0.143) > elderly people (0.139) > teenagers (0.135) (Table 3). All the HI values were below 1, indicating that the cumulative risks of exposure to pesticides for different age groups were acceptable. The top four contributors to the HI values for inhabitants were omethoate (31.1%), carbofuran (11.8%), phorate (7.5%), and avermectins (6.2%). Consequently, omethoate, carbofuran, phorate, and avermectins were the key components for the potential health risk for inhabitants.

Similar observations were shown in other studies. Findings by Li et al. indicated that in vegetables from 31 regions of China, omethoate, carbofuran, and phorate were the major contributors to the HI for the inhabitants (Li et al., 2021). Duan et al. reported that pesticides with relatively high exposure risks (THQ values) included carbofuran (0.2745) and omethoate (0.0915), both of which were regarded as important residues in cowpea in Hainan, China (Duan et al., 2016). Chen et al. found that the pesticide that contributed most to the HI was omethoate (32%) (Chen et al., 2011). Additionally, it is noteworthy that children exhibited the highest dietary exposure risk among the different age groups, suggesting that children were the most vulnerable group, due to their higher food consumption levels per unit body weight. It has been reported that children do not have adult levels of PON1 enzyme to detoxify organophosphorus pesticides such as omethoate and phorate until after age 7 (Huen et al., 2009). Also, lower maternal PON1 enzyme levels during pregnancy may increase susceptibility of children to neurotoxicity from organophosphorus pesticide exposure (Eskenazi et al., 2014). Our results were almost similar to the observations of Ping et al. who found that children (7-13 years old) had a higher health risk than adults (Ping et al., 2022). However, a comprehensive health risk assessment for all different age groups has been rarely reported in previous research, and only Yuan et al. and Chen et al. evaluated the health risks of two pesticide residues (chlorpyrifos and cypermethrin) (Yuan et al., 2014), and organophosphorus pesticides (Chen et al., 2019), respectively, in vegetables for all age groups thus far.

The deterministic approach revealed the dietary exposure risk in the worst-case scenario, whereas it could not consider information on variability in potential exposure. Thus, the probabilistic model was further used to describe more clearly variability and uncertainties. Details on THQ values at 50%, 75%, 97.5%, and 99% confidence level for the four age groups were presented in Table 4 and Fig. 6A. THQ values at the 50th, 75th, 97.5th, and 99th percentile ranged from 2.703 × 10−6 to 0.042 for children, 1.939 × 10−6 to 0.030 for teenagers, 2.053 × 10−6 to 0.032 for adults, and 2.001 × 10−6 to 0.031 for elderly people, i.e., much less than 1, illustrating acceptable exposure risks. Among the different age groups, the order of THQ values at the 50th, 75th, 97.5th, and 99th percentile was as follows: children > adults > elderly people > teenagers, which was in line with the result obtained by the deterministic approach. In terms of THQ values at the 99th percentile, for individual pesticides, 39.4% of THQ values < 0.001, 54.5% ranged from 0.001 to 0.01, and 6.1% ranged from 0.01 to 0.1 for children, while 45.5% of THQ values < 0.001, 48.5% ranged from 0.001 to 0.01, and 6.1% ranged from 0.01 to 0.1 for the other three age groups.

To assess the cumulative risks of pesticides by the probabilistic model, HI values at the 50th, 75th, 97.5th, and 99th percentile were calculated, as shown in Fig. 6B. Children had the highest cumulative risk, and the HI values at the 50th, 75th, 97.5th, and 99th percentile were 0.118, 0.119, 0.121, and 0.122, respectively. Adults and elderly people had relatively lower HI values than children. Teenagers showed the lowest cumulative risk, with HI values at the 50th, 75th, 97.5th, and 99th percentile of 0.085, 0.086, 0.087, and 0.088, respectively. Overall, these results indicated that the cumulative exposure risks posed by pesticides were acceptable for different age groups. Remarkably, the exposure risks obtained by the probabilistic assessment were relatively lower compared with the results of the deterministic assessment, indicating that the deterministic assessment was conservative.

Sensitivity analysis indicated that for the total long-term health risk, the most influential factors were omethoate and carbofuran. Both omethoate and carbofuran positively affected the chronic health risk. Omethoate exhibited the highest correlation with the chronic health risk, with a correlation coefficient of 0.60, followed by carbofuran with a correlation coefficient of 0.58. The effects of the other parameters were small. The same pattern was observed for all population groups. Furthermore, a targeted distributional sensitivity analysis focusing on these two most influential pesticides, conducted by replacing the fitted Pareto distribution with the empirical distribution, confirmed that the risk estimates were robust regardless of the distributional assumption.

Although we have thoroughly discussed the potential health risks of dietary exposure to pesticides from 23 daily-consumed vegetables for different age groups, some limitations should be acknowledged for our present work. Firstly, the risk assessment was conducted exclusively using Chinese regulatory values (GB 2763). Although it is appropriate for evaluating domestic dietary safety, many countries apply their own ADIs and MRLs, which may differ from Chinese standards. Therefore, future studies should incorporate a structured comparison with Codex Alimentarius Commission/EU/US benchmarks, which will help to fully interpret export risks. Secondly, people are not only exposed to vegetables but also other sources in daily life, such as fruits, cereals, and even drinking water. To obtain a comprehensive understanding of the actual health hazards posed by dietary intake of these pesticides, a total diet study should be further carried out. Thirdly, many vegetables in China are processed in some way, such as washing, peeling, juicing, cooking, among other processes, but the processing factors were not considered in our analysis. Lastly, the high detection rates in small samples for certain minor crops may be a concern, such as carbendazim in endive and thiamethoxam in garlic sprout. Future research should expand both the scope and volume of sampling specifically for these characteristic minor vegetables, including endive and garlic sprout, to conduct more in-depth risk assessment of pesticide residues.

Conclusions

This study is the first to systematically characterize the residue profiles and chronic health risks of 33 pesticides in 23 vegetables from Shandong province, a leading vegetable producer in China. Procymidone, carbendazim, and thiamethoxam were the most frequently detected pesticides, while six prohibited pesticides including carbofuran, omethoate, phorate, methamidophos, chlorpyrifos, and fipronil were still found. Leaf lettuce, kidney bean, leek, and celery exhibited the highest over-MRL rates, and were thus identified as the vegetables of greatest concern. Reassuringly, both deterministic and probabilistic assessments showed that cumulative dietary exposure risks for all age groups remained below the health risk threshold, although the exposure risks for children were higher than those for other age groups. Notably, omethoate, carbofuran, phorate, and avermectins were the key contributors to the potential health risk.

Comparison of these findings with previous studies reveals both shared and region-specific features. The detection of the six prohibited pesticides is a phenomenon also well documented in other regions, indicating that illegal use of banned compounds remains a widespread regulatory challenge rather than a localized problem. In terms of regional characteristics, the prominent role of procymidone and carbendazim in Shandong's residue profiles is closely linked to greenhouse cultivation, where high humidity and limited ventilation favor fungal disease development and necessitate frequent fungicide applications.

Based on these observations, some targeted recommendations are proposed for Shandong's vegetable industry. Firstly, for these vegetables of highest concern, crop-specific lists of permitted pesticides and application protocols should be established to guide farmers on proper use of pesticides, combined with strict enforcement of pre-harvest intervals and adherence to GAP. Secondly, since six prohibited pesticides were still detected, stricter enforcement of the ban is warranted through enhanced unannounced inspections, mandatory electronic tracking of highly toxic pesticide sales, and severe penalties for illegal use, together with farmer education programs. Thirdly, given the high detection rates of procymidone and carbendazim, non-chemical disease management alternatives, such as biocontrol agents, sulfur vaporization, and improved ventilation, should be promoted and incorporated into local technical standards for greenhouse cultivation. Lastly, routine monitoring programs in Shandong should maintain particular vigilance on the six prohibited pesticides, the vegetables of primary concern, and the higher dietary exposure levels observed in children, so as to ensure food safety and minimize risks to human health.
