1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in the interior gradient Hölder continuity of viscosity solutions to nonlocal double phase equations of the type

\displaystyle\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}+a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}\right)\,dy=0

(1.1)

for
x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, whose leading operator switches abruptly between two distinct fractional elliptic phases depending on whether the modulating coefficient
a
is zero or not. Here P.V. stands for the Cauchy principal value and

s,t\in(0,1),\quad 1<p\leq q<\infty,\quad a(x,y)\geq 0.

Such equations, first introduced by De Filippis and Palatucci
[
21
]
, could be regarded as the nonlocal counterpart of classical double phase problems given by

\operatorname{div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du+a(x)|Du|^{q-2}Du)=0,\quad 1<p\leq q,\ a(x)\geq 0.

(1.2)

This class of equations was originally proposed by Zhikov
[
49
]
as one of the models designed to describe strongly anisotropic materials in the context of homogenisation. Hereafter, we simply denote Eq. (
1.1
) by

\mathcal{L}u(x)=0\quad\text{for }x\in\Omega.

More precise structural conditions for inferring the local
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of Eq. (
1.1
) will be presented in the subsequent sections.

1.1.
Overview of related literature

Over the past two decades, the regularity theory for nonlocal problems has emerged as an active research area. For the fractional Laplace equation, Caffarelli and Silvestre
[
10
]
established the Harnack estimate using an extension argument; see
[
48
]
for results on Hölder continuity and
[
9
,
36
]
for those concerning the parabolic version. In the nonlinear framework, Di Castro, Kuusi and Palatucci
[
22
,
23
]
extended the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory to nonlocal
p
-Laplacian equations

\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))K(x,y)\,dy=0,\ \ K(x,y)\approx|x-y|^{-d-sp},

while investigating local behaviors of weak solutions, such as Hölder continuity and Harnack inequalities; see
[
16
]
for the stability of these results as the differentiability order
s
approaches to
1
.

On the other hand, the concept of viscosity solutions to fractional
p
-Laplace equations was considered in 2010 by Ishii and Nakamura
[
35
]
, where the authors prvoed the solvability of solutions via Perron’s method. Subsequently, Lindgren
[
44
]
verified that viscosity solutions are Hölder continuous. The inner relationship between these two different solutions above was established in
[
39
]
. Very recently, Giovagnoli, Jesus and Silvestre
[
31
]
achived a major breakthrough in the regularity area for the fractional
p
-Laplacian. Specially, in the case
p\in[2,\frac{2}{1-s})
, the authors showed the
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity for viscosity solutions and then extended this result to weak solutions by leveraging the equivalence between the two solution notions, advancing beyond the previously known gradient boundedness result in
[
4
]
. For more topics, including potential estimates, the nonlocal Wiener criterion, fractional
(s,p)
-harmonic functions and higher Sobolev regularity among others, the readers can refer to
[
42
,
38
,
41
,
40
,
5
]
.

In recent years, significant progress has been made in the regularity theory of nonlocal problems with non-standard growth. For instance, the methods developed in
[
22
,
23
]
were applied to demonstrate the local boundedness, Hölder regularity and Harnack inequalities for nonlocal
G
-Laplace equations with Orlicz growth in
[
6
,
28
,
7
]
; alternative approaches can be found in
[
11
,
13
]
. Additionally, De Filippis and Mingione
[
19
]
established the interior gradient Hölder continuity for mixed local and nonlocal equations with nonuniform growth modeled by

-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u=0,\quad 1<p\leq q,

(1.3)

through exploiting a perturbation argument, which benefits from the direct regularity refinement provided by classical
p
-Laplace operator.

Turning to the object of study in this article, De Filippis and Palatucci
[
21
]
first explored the regularity theory for (
1.1
), proving the
C^{0,\alpha}
property of its viscosity solution in the spirit of Krylov-Safonov. In terms of distinct solution notions, Fang and Zhang
[
28
]
discussed the Hölder continuity for weak solutions and showed that weak solutions are viscosity solutions. The reverse implication has been claimed in
[
29
]
. Moreover, under suitable assumptions, the Hölder continuity of viscosity solutions was enhanced to Lipschitz in
[
3
]
. It is worth pointing out that the aforementioned papers addressed the case where
s\geq t
, treating the
q
-growth term as a lower-order term to some extent. Conversely, Byun, Ok and Song
[
8
]
considered the case
s\leq t
, and obtained local behaviours of weak solutions. In particular, the authors concluded that solutions to (
1.1
) belong to the class
C^{0,\alpha}
if the coefficient
a
is bounded and satisfies

\begin{cases}&|a(x_{1},y_{1})-a(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq[a]_{\alpha}(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|y_{1}-y_{2}|)^{\alpha}\\[5.69054pt]
&tq\leq sp+\alpha\end{cases}

with
\alpha\in(0,1]
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Furthermore, weak Harnack inequalities were established in
[
25
]
for both cases
s>t
and
s\leq t
; additionally, the authors in
[
43
]
proved the Harnack estimate in the special situation

(where only the differentiability orders differ). More results on the nonlocal problem (
1.1
) can be found in
[
30
,
34
,
46
,
47
]
and references therein.

In contrast to local double phase problems, the development of regularity theory for the nonlocal version remains far from complete; in particular, no results are yet available concerning gradient Hölder continuity or the Harnack inequality. For the local equation (
1.2
), Colombo and Mingione, in their pioneering works
[
14
,
15
]
, established the

regularity of weak solutions under the following sharp conditions:

\begin{cases}&a\in C^{0,\alpha},\ \frac{q}{p}\leq 1+\frac{\alpha}{d}\ \ \ \Rightarrow u\in C^{1,\alpha}\\[5.69054pt]
&u\in L^{\infty},\ a\in C^{0,\alpha},\ q\leq p+\alpha\ \ \ \Rightarrow u\in C^{1,\alpha}\end{cases}

(1.4)

with
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Moreover, Fang, Rădulescu and Zhang
[
26
]
proved the equivalence between weak and viscosity solutions to (
1.2
) , which implies that viscosity solutions are also
C^{1,\alpha}
regular. Remarkably, De Filippis and Mingione
[
20
]
obtained a striking gradient Hölder regularity result for minimizers of nonuniformly elliptic functionals, even in cases where an Euler-Lagrange equation may not exist. Further developments and related results on Eq. (
1.2
) can be found in
[
2
,
17
,
18
,

,

]
and references therein.

1.2.
Our main result

The main goal of this paper is to resolve the open problem raised in
[
21
]
by establishing interior
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity for solutions to Eq. (
1.1
), thus developing a regularity theory that parallels its local counterpart.

Our approach is influenced by the ideas introduced in
[
31
]
in the context of fractional
p
-Laplace equations. However, the techniques there for the fractional
p
-Laplacian cannot be directly applied to (
1.1
) due to the nonstandard (
p,q
)-growth and the mixed differentiability orders. To be precise, this nonuniform ellipticity, combined with such mixed orders, prevents the natural scaling properties inherent to fractional
p
-Laplace equations. Furthermore, addintional efforts are required to deal with the interaction between the structural exponents and the nonnegative coefficient
a(\cdot)
. To deal with this challenge, specific quantitative relationships among these quantities will be presented in the assumptions below. These assumptions are primarily motivated by Lemma
3.2
, Lemma
3.13
, Proposition
4.7
, and Section
5
.

Before stating the main contributions of this work, we elaborate on the preconditions on the coefficient
a(\cdot)
, differentiability orders
s,t
and summability exponents
p,q
. For the measurable coefficient
a:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we make the following assumptions:

(
A_{1}
)

Symmetry:
a(x,y)=a(y,x)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

(
A_{2}
)

Translation invariance:
a(x+z,y+z)=a(x,y)
for all
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(
A_{3}
)

Boundedness:
0\leq a(x,y)\leq\|a\|_{\infty}
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

(
A_{4}
)

Lipschitz continuity:

|a(x_{1},y_{1})-a(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq[a]_{\rm lip}(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|y_{1}-y_{2}|)

for
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We denote
\|a\|_{\rm lip}:=\|a\|_{\infty}+[a]_{\rm lip}
. The basic assumptions on
s,t,p,q
are given as follows:

(
H_{1}
)

0<s\leq t<1
,
2\leq p<q<\infty
;

(
H_{2}
)

p\in\left[2,\frac{2}{1-s}\right)
,
q\in\left[2,\frac{1}{1-t}\right)
;

(
H_{3}
)

tq\leq sp+\min\{1,q-p\}
.

We are now in a position to state our main result: the
a priori

C^{1,\alpha}
estimate for solutions to (
1.1
). Here and in what follows, we refer to
C^{1}
-regular solutions simply as smooth solutions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u
be a smooth solution to Eq. (
1.1
) in
B_{2}
. Suppose that the conditions that (
A_{1}
)–(
A_{4}
) and (
H_{1}
)–(
H_{3}
) stated above are satisfied. Then there exist two universal constants
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C\geq 1
, both depending on
d,s,t,p,q,\|a\|_{\rm lip}
, such that

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(B_{1})}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{{\rm lip}(B_{2})}+\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;2)\right).

We note that while we begin with the
C^{1}
regularity of solutions as a starting point to show the
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity, this step is in fact the core of the matter, in light of some existing results. As stated in
[
31
]
, the
C^{1}
regularity of
u
areves merely as a qualitative condition for the proof (indeed, for Lemma
3.12
below). The estimates we derive depend only on the Lipschitz norm of
u
in the
C^{0,1}
space. Meanwhile, with the aid of our Section
5
, the Lipschitz continuity of
u
can be deduced without much difficulty using the Ishii-Lions method. Futhermover, for the case
s\geq t
, one can refer to
[
3
]
, where Lipschitz estimates for solutions to (
1.1
) were obtained; see also
[
4
, Section 5]
. Notably, this result can be proved even without requiring the coefficient
a(\cdot)
to be Lipschitz continuous, and the associated Lipschitz (semi-) norm on
u
is bounded in terms of the supremum norm of
u
and its tail. For the sake of rigor, we will bridge the gap between the
C^{0,1}
and
C^{1}
regularity of
u
in our forthcoming manuscript
[
24
]
. Therein, we overcome the
C^{1}
regularity assumption on
u
in Lemma
3.12
by means of an approximation argument analogous to
[
31
, Section 9]
, and we also prove the equivalence of weak and viscosity solutions to (
1.1
) for the range
s\leq t
. This process is lengthy, and including it in the present paper would make the key points less prominent. Thus we organize it into a separate paper.

Remark 1.2
.

A few observations regarding the conditions on
a(\cdot)
are in order. The symmetry enables Eq. (
1.1
) to possess a variational structure for the De Giorgi iteration in Section
3
. If
a(\cdot)
is not translation-invariant, in the processes of linearizing Eq. (
1.1
) (Lemma
2.6
), we require
q>1/(1-t)
to ensure the integral

\int_{B_{R}(x)}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d+q(t-1)+1}}<\infty.

This, together with
p\in\left[2,2/(1-s)\right)
, is not ideal when
t
is close to 1 and
s
is away from 1. Moreover, it is known from
[
28
]
that equivalence of weak and viscosity solutions to Eq. (
1.1
) for
s\geq t
does need translation invariance of
a(\cdot)
. When we linearize Eq. (
1.1
), we will differentiate the coefficient
a(\cdot)
; Hence, the Lipschitz continuity is imposed instead of Hölder continuity.

Remark 1.3
.

The requirement
q<1/(1-t)
in (
H_{2}
), rather than
q<2/(1-t)
, stems from the fact that we differentiate
a(\cdot)
with respect to the variable
x
; this avoids the loss of power of
|x-y|^{-d-tq}
when differentiating this power function. The assumption (
H_{3}
) is composed of
tq\leq sp+1
and
tq\leq sp+q-p
. The former corresponds to the condition
tq\leq sp+\alpha
in
[
8
]
, where the authors proved that the solutions to (
1.1
) are Hölder continuous if
a(\cdot)
is
C^{0,\alpha}
-regular. The latter is indeed
p(1-s)\leq q(1-t)
, which ensures that in Section
4
, the integral

\int_{B_{\rho}}\frac{|z|^{q}}{|z|^{d+tq}}\,dz\quad\text{is controlled by}\quad\int_{B_{\rho}}\frac{|z|^{p}}{|z|^{d+sp}}\,dz.

Intuitively, if we let
s,t\rightarrow 1
in the condition
tq\leq sp+\min\{1,q-p\}
from (
H_{3}
), it reduces to
q\leq p+1
, where the number
1
originates from the
C^{0,1}
assumption on
a(\cdot)
. This condition corresponds to the condition
\eqref{1-1-2}_{2}
for local double phase problems.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

).

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give some concepts, notations, and auxiliary results. Throughout this manuscript,
C
or
c
denotes a positive constant that may vary from line to line. Relevant dependencies on parameters are emphasized by using parentheses; that is,
C(p,q,d)
means
C
depends on
p,q,d
. A universal constant means that it depends at most on the structural parameters
d,s,t,p,q
and
\|a\|_{\rm lip}
(or
\|a\|_{\infty},[a]_{\rm lip}
). Let
B_{r}(x_{0}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}||x-x_{0}|<r\}
represent a ball with radius
r>0
and center
x_{0}
. If not important or clear from the context, we will omit the center of ball as
B_{r}=B_{r}(x_{0})
.

We now give the notion of viscosity solutions to (
1.1
) in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
2\leq p\leq q
.

Definition 2.1
.

A lower semicontinuous function
u\in L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{tq}^{q-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is called a viscosity supersolution to Eq. (
1.1
) in
\Omega
, if whenever there is
\psi\in C^{2}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
for some
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
such that
\psi(x_{0})=u(x_{0})
and
\psi(x)\leq u(x)
in
B_{r}(x_{0})
, then one has

\mathcal{L}\psi_{r}(x_{0})\geq 0,

where

\psi_{r}(x)=\begin{cases}\psi(x),&\text{{for }}x\in B_{r}(x_{0}),\\[5.69054pt]
u(x),&\text{{for }}x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0}).\end{cases}

If
-u
is a viscosity supersolution, we call
u
a viscosity subsolution. A function
u
is a viscosity solution if and only if it is super- and subsolution.

For
s\in(0,1)
, the fractional Sobolev space
H^{s}(\Omega)
represents
W^{s,2}(\Omega)
equipped with the norm
\|u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+[u]_{H^{s}(\Omega)}
. Taking the nonlocal characteristic of Eq. (
1.1
) into account, we introduce the tail space given as

L^{k}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u(x)|^{k}}{(1+|x|)^{d+\gamma}}\,dx<\infty\right\}.

for
k\geq 1,\gamma>0
and the corresponding tail function

\mathrm{Tail}_{k,\gamma}(u;x_{0},R):=\left(\int_{B^{c}_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(y)|^{k}}{|x_{0}-y|^{d+\gamma}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}.

We can find the tail here is finite according to the definition of tail space. For
x_{0}=0
, let plainly
\mathrm{Tail}_{k,\gamma}(u;R)=\mathrm{Tail}_{k,\gamma}(u;0,R)
. Particularly, the quantities such as
\mathrm{Tail}_{p-2,sp}(u;R)
,
\mathrm{Tail}_{p-1,sp+1}(u;R)
,
\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq+1}(u;R)
will naturally occur in our forthcoming procedures. The properties of them shall be considered in Lemma
2.3
. In this paper, for simplicity, we denote

J_{k}(t)=|t|^{k-2}t\quad\text{for }t\in\mathbb{R}.

We now present the following basic estimates for the function
J_{k}(t)
and the tail, which will be used repeatedly throughout the subsequent analysis.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
k>1
and
t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

J_{k}(t)-J_{k}(\tau)=(k-1)\int^{1}_{0}|\tau+s(t-\tau)|^{k-2}(t-\tau)\,ds

and

C^{-1}_{k}(|\tau|+|t-\tau|)^{k-2}\leq\int^{1}_{0}|\tau+s(t-\tau)|^{k-2}\,ds\leq C_{k}(|\tau|+|t-\tau|)^{k-2}

with
C_{k}>0
depending only upon
k
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
u\in L^{\infty}(B_{2R})\cap L^{q-1}_{tq}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
q>1,R>0
. The inequalities below hold for a universal constant
C>0
:

(1)

\mathrm{Tail}_{q,\gamma}(u;R)^{q}\leq CR^{-\gamma}\|u\|^{q}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}+\mathrm{Tail}_{q,\gamma}(u;2R)^{q}
;

(2)

\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq+1}(u;R)\leq R^{\frac{-1}{q-1}}\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;R)
;

(3)

If
tq\leq sp+1
, then

\mathrm{Tail}_{p-1,sp+1}(u;R)^{p-1}\leq CR^{-\left(sp+1-tq\frac{p-1}{q-1}\right)}\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;R)^{p-1};

(4)

For
p\in\left[2,\frac{2}{1-s}\right)
,

\mathrm{Tail}_{p-2,sp}(u;R)^{p-2}\leq CR^{\frac{p(1-s)-2}{p-1}}\mathrm{Tail}_{p-1,sp+1}(u;R)^{p-2}.

Proof.

Estimates (1) and (2) are immediate, and (4) follows directly from Hölder inequality. For (3), applying Hölder inequality and
tq\leq sp+1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B^{c}_{R}}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|y|^{d+sp+1}}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{c}_{R}}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|y|^{(d+tq)\frac{p-1}{q-1}+(d+tq)\frac{q-p}{q-1}+sp+1-tq}}dy

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B^{c}_{R}}\frac{|u(y)|^{q-1}}{|y|^{d+tq}}dy\right)^{\frac{p-1}{q-1}}\left(\int_{B^{c}_{R}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{d+tq+(sp+1-tq)\frac{q-1}{q-p}}}dy\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q-1}}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-\left(tq+(sp+1-tq)\frac{q-1}{q-p}\right)\frac{q-p}{q-1}}\left(\int_{B^{c}_{R}}\frac{|u(y)|^{q-1}}{|y|^{d+tq}}dy\right)^{\frac{p-1}{q-1}},

where
C>0
depends on
d,s,t,p,q
. Here we would like to mention that in the second line, we just require
tq+(sp+1-tq)\frac{q-1}{q-p}>0
to ensure the convergence of the integral, which is equivalent to
tq<\frac{q-1}{p-1}(sp+1)
. Clearly, the condition
tq\leq sp+1
meets this requirement.
∎

The following geometric convergence lemma shall play a crucial role in the De Giorgi iteration scheme in Section

.

Lemma 2.4

(
[

)

Let

We now write

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
p\in\big[2,\frac{2}{1-s}\big)
,
tq\leq sp+q-p
and the assumptions
(A_{1}),(A_{3})
of

be in force. Suppose that

for some

is in

\beta=2-p(1-s)-\alpha

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in H^{\beta}_{0}(B_{r})
. Employing the Lipschitz property of

(A_{1}),(A_{3})
of
a(\cdot)
, we can treat the bilinear form

\displaystyle\quad\left|\int_{B_{1}}\mathcal{L}^{a}_{u}v(x)\varphi(x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}(v(x)-v(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B^{c}_{1}}(v(x)-v(y))\varphi(x)K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dydx\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+sp-p+2}}+\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+tq-q+2}}\right)|v(x)-v(y)||\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\,dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+C_{2}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\left(\frac{(|u(x)|+|u(y)|)^{p-2}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}+\frac{(|u(x)|+|u(y)|)^{q-2}}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}\right)(|v(x)|+|v(y)|)|\varphi(x)|\,dydx

\displaystyle=:C_{1}I_{1}+C_{2}I_{2},

where
C_{2}>0
depends on

, and
C_{1}>0
depends additionally on
[u]_{{\rm lip}(B_{1})}
. Let us first consider

. In view of

Then by Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)||\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{d+sp-p+2}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=C\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{d}{2}+sp-p+2-\alpha}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{d}{2}+\alpha}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq C[v]_{H^{\alpha}(B_{1})}[\varphi]_{H^{\beta}(B_{1})}.

For

, we note
(1-r)|y|\leq|x-y|
with
x\in B_{r}
and
y\in B^{c}_{1}
, and thus by Hölder inequality again, we get

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-r)^{d+tq}}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\bigg(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{p-2}+|u(y)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{d+sp}}\left(|v(x)|+|v(y)|\right)\left|\varphi(x)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{q-2}+|u(y)|^{q-2}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\left(|v(x)|+|v(y)|\right)\left|\varphi(x)\right|\bigg)\,dydx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1-r)^{d+tq}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})},

where
C>0
depends on
d,s,t,p,q,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}
and
\mathrm{Tail}_{p-1,sp}(u;1)
,
\mathrm{Tail}_{p-1,sp}(v;1)
,
\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;1)
,
\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(v;1)
.

As a consequence, it yields that

\left|\int_{B_{1}}\mathcal{L}^{a}_{u}v(x)\varphi(x)\,dx\right|\leq C\|\varphi\|_{H^{\beta}(B_{1})}

for all
\varphi\in H^{\beta}_{0}(B_{r})
, which means that
\mathcal{L}^{a}_{u}v
is well-defined in the space
H^{-\beta}(B_{r})
.
∎

Let
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
be any unit vector. We are going to localize the directional derivative

, and compute the linearization of Eq. (

) applied to this. Let

be a smooth radially-symmetric cut-off function satisfying

For an

and a

, introduce a function

Proposition 2.6

(Linearization of (

))
.

Assume that the coefficient

satisfies
(A_{2})
–
(A_{4})
, and
2\leq p\leq q
,

be a smooth solution to (

) in
B_{2R}
. Then the localized function

solves the following equation

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{u}^{a}v_{e}(x)|\leq C\Bigg(

\displaystyle\frac{\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}}{R^{sp+1}}+\frac{\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}}{R^{tq}}+\frac{\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}}{R^{tq+1}}

\displaystyle+(1+R^{-1})\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;2R)^{q-1}+R^{-\left(sp+1-\frac{p-1}{q-1}tq\right)}\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;2R)^{p-1}\Bigg),

where the universal constant
C
depends on
d,s,t,p,q
and
\|a\|_{\rm lip}
.

Proof.

We rewrite
\mathcal{L}u(x)
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u(x)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}+a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}\right)(u(x)-u(y))\eta_{R}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}+a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}\right)(u(x)-u(y))(1-\eta_{R}(y))\,dy

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\,F_{1p}(x)+F_{1q}(x)+F_{2p}(x)+F_{2q}(x)

with
\eta_{R}(x)=\eta\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)
. We know from (
1.1
) that
F_{1p}(x)+F_{1q}(x)+F_{2p}(x)+F_{2q}(x)=0
, and then differentiate this equation to get

e\cdot\nabla F_{1p}(x)+e\cdot\nabla F_{1q}(x)+e\cdot\nabla F_{2p}(x)+e\cdot\nabla F_{2q}(x)=0.

(2.2)

Note the translation invariance of
a(\cdot)
, i.e.,
a(x,x+h)=a(0,h)
for
x,h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Now for
x\in B_{R}
we formally calculate

\displaystyle e\cdot\nabla F_{1q}(x)=

\displaystyle\,e\cdot\nabla\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(0,h)\frac{|u(x)-u(x+h)|^{q-2}}{|h|^{d+tq}}(u(x)-u(x+h))\eta_{R}(x+h)\,dh

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(q-1)a(0,h)\frac{|u(x)-u(x+h)|^{q-2}}{|h|^{d+tq}}(e\cdot\nabla u(x)-e\cdot\nabla u(x+h))\eta_{R}(x+h)\,dh

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(0,h)\frac{|u(x)-u(x+h)|^{q-2}}{|h|^{d+tq}}(u(x)-u(x+h))e\cdot\nabla\eta_{R}(x+h)\,dh,

and similarly, we can get an equality of

with
a(\cdot)\equiv 1
and

here. As a result, it yields that

\displaystyle e\cdot\nabla F_{1p}(x)+e\cdot\nabla F_{1q}(x)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K^{a}_{u}(x,x+h)(e\cdot\nabla u(x)-e\cdot\nabla u(x+h))\eta_{R}(x+h)\,dh

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{h}(x,x+h)e\cdot\nabla\eta_{R}(x+h)\,dh

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K^{a}_{u}(x,y)(e\cdot\nabla u(x)-e\cdot\nabla u(y))\eta_{R}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{h}(x,y)e\cdot\nabla\eta_{R}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{L}_{u}^{a}v_{e}(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K^{a}_{u}(x,y)e\cdot\nabla u(x)(\eta_{R}(y)-\eta_{R}(x))\,dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{h}(x,y)e\cdot\nabla\eta_{R}(y)\,dy,

(2.3)

where the notation

\widehat{h}(x,y):=\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}+a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}.

On the other hand, we compute

\displaystyle e\cdot\nabla F_{2q}(x)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(q-1)a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}e\cdot\nabla u(x)(1-\eta_{R}(y))\,dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e\cdot\nabla_{x}a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}(1-\eta_{R}(y))\,dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(x,y)|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(y))e\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{1-\eta_{R}(y)}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}\right)\,dy

and an analogous equality of
e\cdot\nabla F_{2p}(x)
also is obtained. Thus, it holds that

\displaystyle\quad e\cdot\nabla F_{2p}(x)+e\cdot\nabla F_{2q}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K^{a}_{u}(x,y)e\cdot\nabla u(x)(1-\eta_{R}(y))\,dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e\cdot\nabla_{x}a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}(1-\eta_{R}(y))\,dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))e\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{1-\eta_{R}(y)}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\right)\,dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(x,y)|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(y))e\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{1-\eta_{R}(y)}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}\right)\,dy.

(2.4)

Combining inequalities (
2
), (
2
) with (
2.2
) and noticing

for

, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{u}^{a}v_{e}(x)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))e\cdot\nabla_{y}\left(\frac{1-\eta_{R}(y)}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\right)\,dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(x,y)|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(y))e\cdot\nabla_{y}\left(\frac{1-\eta_{R}(y)}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}\right)\,dy

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e\cdot\nabla_{x}a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}(1-\eta_{R}(y))\,dy

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\ I_{1}+I_{2}-I_{3}.

In the sequel, we first treat the integral

. Recall the Lipschitz continuity of

, we get

|e\cdot\nabla_{x}a(x,y)|\leq[a]_{\rm lip}.

For

Thereby, via the fact

for

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq[a]_{\rm lip}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-1}}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}(1-\eta_{R}(y))\,dy

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}}{R^{tq+1}}+\frac{C\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}}{R}\int_{B_{\frac{7R}{4}}\setminus B_{\frac{3R}{2}}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{d+tq}}+C\int_{B^{c}_{\frac{3R}{2}}}\frac{|u(y)|^{q-1}}{|y|^{d+tq+1}}\,dy

.

All in all, we infer

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{u}^{a}v_{e}(x)|\leq C\Bigg(

\displaystyle\frac{\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}}{R^{sp+1}}+\frac{\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}}{R^{tq}}+\frac{\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}}{R^{tq+1}}+\mathrm{Tail}_{p-1,sp+1}(u;3R/2)^{p-1}

\displaystyle+\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;3R/2)^{q-1}+\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq+1}(u;3R/2)^{q-1}\Bigg),

where

∎

3.
Improvement of gradient norm

This section aims at establishing reduction of the gradient norm for viscosity solutions to Eq. (
1.1
). We mainly follow the ideas developed in
[
31
]
to derive a type of improvement of oscillation lemma for the nonlocal double phase equation. However, there are some nontrivial treatment owing to the nonstandard (
p,q
)-growth. First, we force the linearized equation in Proposition

to fall into a smallness regime under some hypotheses. This is our main tool for improving the gradient norm in this section.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
2\leq p\leq q
and
v_{e}(x)=\eta\left(\frac{x}{2^{K_{0}}}\right)(e\cdot\nabla u(x))
be the localized directional derivative of
u
. Suppose
u
is a solution to Eq. (
1.1
) in a ball

, such that

(1)

and
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2^{k}})}\leq(1-\delta)^{-k}

(2)

\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;2^{K_{0}+1})\leq\varepsilon_{1}

Then under the conditions that

\begin{cases}&tq\leq sp+1\\[5.69054pt]
&p<\frac{2}{1-s},q<\frac{1}{1-t},\end{cases}

(3.1)

we conclude

|\mathcal{L}^{a}_{u}v_{e}(x)|\leq\varepsilon_{0},\quad x\in B_{1},

(3.2)

where
\varepsilon_{0}
is arbitrarily small when we take
\varepsilon_{1},\delta
small and
K_{0}
large.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.6
with
R=2^{K_{0}}
, we can discover

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}^{a}_{u}v_{e}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(\frac{\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2^{K_{0}+1}})}}{2^{K_{0}(sp+1)}}+\frac{\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2^{K_{0}+1}})}}{2^{K_{0}tq}}+\frac{\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2^{K_{0}+1}})}}{2^{K_{0}(tq+1)}}+(1+2^{-K_{0}})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;2^{K_{0}+1})^{q-1}+2^{-K_{0}\left(sp+1-\frac{p-1}{q-1}tq\right)}\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;2^{K_{0}+1})^{p-1}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2^{K_{0}+1}})}}{2^{K_{0}(sp+1)}}+\frac{\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2^{K_{0}+1}})}}{2^{K_{0}tq}}+2\varepsilon_{1}^{q-1}+\varepsilon_{1}^{p-1}\right),

where in the last line we have used
\eqref{3-1-1}_{1}
, and

depends only on

. Observe that by (1),

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}^{a}_{u}v_{e}(x)|\leq C\Big[

\displaystyle\left((1-\delta)^{-(p-1)}2^{p(1-s)-2}\right)^{K_{0}+1}+\left((1-\delta)^{-(q-1)}2^{q(1-t)-1}\right)^{K_{0}+1}

\displaystyle+2\varepsilon_{1}^{q-1}+\varepsilon_{1}^{p-1}\Big]=:\varepsilon_{0}.

In the sequel, based on Eq. (

Lemma 3.2

–
(A_{4})

B_{1}
to arrive at

\int_{B_{1}}[\mathcal{L}^{a}_{u}v_{e}(x)]v_{k}(x)\,dx<\varepsilon_{0}\int_{B_{1}}v_{k}(x)\,dx,

(3.3)

where we can find that the integral on the left-hand side makes sense whenever
u\in L^{p-1}_{sp}\cap L^{q-1}_{tq}
is Lipschitz in
B_{1}
, and
v_{e}
(and thus also
v_{k}
) is in
H^{1-p(1-s)/2}
.

We shall divide the integral on the left-hand side into four parts. Through the definition of
K^{a}_{u}(x,y)
and the symmetry of
a(\cdot)
, we can know that the kernel
K^{a}_{u}(x,y)
also is symmetric, i.e.,
K^{a}_{u}(x,y)=K^{a}_{u}(y,x)
. So the operator
\mathcal{L}^{a}_{u}
has a variational structure. Thus by simple manipulations we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}[\mathcal{L}^{a}_{u}v_{e}(x)]v_{k}(x)\,dx\geq\,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}(v_{k}(x)-v_{k}(y))v_{k}(x)K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}(\varphi_{k}(x)-\varphi_{k}(y))v_{k}(x)K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}[\varphi_{k}(y)-v_{e}(y)]_{+}v_{k}(x)K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}(\varphi_{k}(x)-v_{e}(y))v_{k}(x)K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dxdy

\displaystyle=:\,

\displaystyle J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}.

In the sequel, we aim at evaluating the lower bounds on these four integrals term by term. Then merging theses estimates with (
3.3
) will leads to a recursive inequality on the measure of the sets
A_{k}
, so that we can discuss the limit of
|A_{k}|
.

First, we are going to deal with the nonlocal integral
J_{4}
. Now let us check properties of the kernel
K^{a}_{u}(x,y)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
u
fulfills the conditions given by Lemma
3.1
. Then under the assumption (
3.1
), if
\delta,\varepsilon_{1}>0
are small enough, there holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dy\leq C\qquad\text{for }x\in B_{\frac{3}{4}},

where
C>0
is a universal constant.

Proof.

For
x\in B_{\frac{3}{4}}
, via (1) in Lemma
3.1
, it holds
|u(x)|\leq 1
. Taking into account boundedness of
a
, Hölder inequality and Lemma
2.3
(3) and (4), we could estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}\left(\frac{1+|u(y)|^{p-2}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}+\frac{1+|u(y)|^{q-2}}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}\right)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}\left(\frac{1+|u(y)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{d+sp}}+\frac{1+|u(y)|^{q-2}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\right)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\left[1+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|y|^{d+sp+1}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u(y)|^{q-1}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;1)^{p-2}+\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;1)^{q-2}\right),

(3.4)

where
C>0
depends upon
d,s,t,p,q
and
\|a\|_{\infty}
. When
y\in B_{2^{k+1}}\setminus B_{2^{k}}
with
k\leq K_{0}
, from Lemma
3.1
(1), we have

|u(y)|\leq(1-\delta)^{-(k+1)}2^{k+1}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{B_{2^{k+1}}\setminus B_{2^{k}}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{d+tq}}\leq C2^{-(k+1)tq}.

We can further evaluate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u(y)|^{q-1}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\,dy

\displaystyle=\sum^{K_{0}}_{k=0}\int_{B_{2^{k+1}}\setminus B_{2^{k}}}\frac{|u(y)|^{q-1}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\,dy+\int_{B^{c}_{2^{K_{0}+1}}}\frac{|u(y)|^{q-1}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\sum^{K_{0}}_{k=0}\left[(1-\delta)^{-(q-1)}2^{q-tq-1}\right]^{k+1}+\varepsilon_{1}^{q-1}.

(3.5)

Let
\theta_{0}:=2^{q(1-t)-1}<1
via
q<\frac{1}{1-t}
. Taking
\delta<1
small enough yields that

\theta:=(1-\delta)^{-(q-1)}2^{q(1-t)-1}<\frac{1+\theta_{0}}{2}<1.

Then,

\sum^{K_{0}}_{k=0}\left[(1-\delta)^{-(q-1)}2^{q-tq-1}\right]^{k+1}=\sum^{K_{0}}_{k=0}\theta^{k+1}\leq\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}.

That is, if
\varepsilon_{1}<1
is small, the display (
3
) becomes

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u(y)|^{q-1}}{|y|^{d+tq}}dy\leq C_{1}

(3.6)

with
C_{1}>0
depending on
d,t,q
. Combining the inequalities (
3
) and (
3.6
), the desired result is proved now.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that
u
satisfies the conditions given by Lemma
3.1
. Then under condition (
3.1
), if
\delta,\varepsilon_{1}>0
are small enough, then for
x\in B_{\frac{3}{4}}
there holds that

\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\int_{B_{2^{n+1}}\setminus B_{2^{n}}}(1-\delta)^{-(n+1)}K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dy\leq C

(3.7)

and

\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\int_{B_{2^{n+1}}\setminus B_{2^{n}}}\left((1-\delta)^{-(n+1)}-1\right)K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dy\leq C(\delta),

(3.8)

where
C>0
depends on
d,s,t,p,q,\|a\|_{\infty}
, and
C(\delta)>0
depends also on
\delta
, and
C(\delta)\rightarrow 0
as
\delta\rightarrow 0
.

Proof.

For
x\in B_{\frac{3}{4}}
and
y\in B_{2^{n+1}}\setminus B_{2^{n}}
, it holds
|u(x)|\leq 1
and

|y|\leq|y-x|\left(1+\frac{|x|}{|y-x|}\right)\leq 4|y-x|

and

(1-\delta)^{-(n+1)}=(1-\delta)^{-1}2^{\log_{2}(1-\delta)^{-n}}=(1-\delta)^{-1}2^{n\alpha_{0}}\leq(1-\delta)^{-1}|y|^{\alpha_{0}}\leq 2|y|^{\alpha_{0}},

where
\alpha_{0}:=\log_{2}(1-\delta)^{-1}
and
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
. We proceed to select
\delta
small enough such that
\alpha_{0}<sp(\leq tq)
, and then we can treat

\displaystyle\quad\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\int_{B_{2^{n+1}}\setminus B_{2^{n}}}(1-\delta)^{-(n+1)}K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\int_{B_{2^{n+1}}\setminus B_{2^{n}}}(1-\delta)^{-(n+1)}\left(\frac{1+|u(y)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{d+sp}}+\frac{1+|u(y)|^{q-2}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\right)dy

\displaystyle\leq C\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\int_{B_{2^{n+1}}\setminus B_{2^{n}}}\frac{1+|u(y)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{d+sp-\alpha_{0}}}+\frac{1+|u(y)|^{q-2}}{|y|^{d+tq-\alpha_{0}}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{d+sp-\alpha_{0}}}dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u(y)|^{q-2}}{|y|^{d+tq-\alpha_{0}}}\,dy\right).

Now we can pick
\delta
so small that
\alpha_{0}<\frac{tq}{q-1}
, and use Hölder inequality to get

\displaystyle\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\frac{|u(y)|^{q-2}}{|y|^{d+tq-\alpha_{0}}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\frac{|u(y)|^{q-1}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q-1}}\left(\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{d+tq-\alpha_{0}(q-1)}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}

\displaystyle=C\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;1)^{q-2}.

By means of the facts that
tq\leq sp+1
and
p<\frac{2}{1-s}
, we can find such small
\delta
that

\displaystyle\quad 0<tq-(tq-sp+\alpha_{0})\frac{q-1}{q-p+1}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(sp-\alpha_{0})\frac{q-1}{q-p+1}+tq\frac{2-p}{q-p+1}>0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(sp-\alpha_{0})(q-1)+tq(2-p)>0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow sp-tq\frac{p-2}{q-1}>\alpha_{0}.

(3.9)

In fact, it follows from
tq\leq sp+1
and
p<\frac{2}{1-s}
that
sp-tq\frac{p-2}{q-1}>0
, as can be seen from the following

\displaystyle sp-tq\frac{p-2}{q-1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{q-1}[(q-1)sp-(sp+1)(p-2)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}[(q-p+1)sp-(p-2)]

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{q-1}[(q-p+1)sp-sp]=\frac{q-p}{q-1}sp\geq 0.

So if only
\delta
is sufficiently small, the inequality (
3
) could be achieved. At this stage, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{d+sp-\alpha_{0}}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\frac{|u(y)|^{q-1}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-2}{q-1}}\left(\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{d+tq-(tq-sp+\alpha_{0})\frac{q-1}{q-p+1}}}\right)^{\frac{q-p+1}{q-1}}

\displaystyle=C\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;1)^{p-2}.

Analogous to (
3
), we prove (
3.7
) from the displays above.

We next focus on (
3.8

y\in B_{2^{n+1}}\setminus B_{2^{n}}
, we have
(1-\delta)^{-(n+1)}=2^{(n+1)\alpha_{0}}\leq(2|y|)^{\alpha_{0}}
. Then,

\displaystyle\quad\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\int_{B_{2^{n+1}}\setminus B_{2^{n}}}\left((1-\delta)^{-n-1}-1\right)K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\int_{B_{2^{n+1}}\setminus B_{2^{n}}}\left((2|y|)^{\alpha_{0}}-1\right)\left(\frac{1+|u(y)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{d+sp}}+\frac{1+|u(y)|^{q-2}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\right)dy

\displaystyle=C\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\left(\frac{(2|y|)^{\alpha_{0}}-1}{|y|^{d+sp}}+\frac{(2|y|)^{\alpha_{0}}-1}{|y|^{d+tq}}\right)dy

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\left((2|y|)^{\alpha_{0}}-1\right)\left(\frac{|u(y)|^{p-2}}{|y|^{d+sp}}+\frac{|u(y)|^{q-2}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\right)dy

\displaystyle\leq CI^{1}+C\mathrm{Tail}_{p-1,sp+1}(u;1)^{p-2}\left(\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\frac{\big((2|y|)^{\alpha_{0}}-1\big)^{p-1}}{|y|^{d+sp-p+2}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;1)^{q-2}\left(\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\frac{\big((2|y|)^{\alpha_{0}}-1\big)^{q-1}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}.

Here
I^{1}:=\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\left(\frac{(2|y|)^{\alpha_{0}}-1}{|y|^{d+sp}}+\frac{(2|y|)^{\alpha_{0}}-1}{|y|^{d+tq}}\right)dy
. Via the dominant convergence theorem, it holds that the integrals

I^{1},\quad\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\frac{\big((2|y|)^{\alpha}_{0}-1\big)^{p-1}}{|y|^{d+sp-p+2}}\,dy,\quad\int_{B^{c}_{1}}\frac{\big((2|y|)^{\alpha_{0}}-1\big)^{q-1}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\,dy

\alpha_{0}\rightarrow 0

\mathrm{Tail}_{p-1,sp+1}(u;1)\leq C\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;1)
by Lemma
2.3
, and
\alpha_{0}\rightarrow 0
as
\delta\rightarrow 0
. The inequality (
3.8
) has been deduced from above.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

With the hypotheses of Lemma

J_{4}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}(\varphi_{k}(x)-v_{e}(y))v_{k}(x)K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dxdy\geq-C(\delta)\int_{B_{1}}v_{k}(x)\,dx,

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}[(1-\delta_{k}\eta_{k}(x))-v_{e}(y)]v_{k}(x)K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dxdy

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{1}}v_{k}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}[(v_{e}(y)-1)+\delta_{k}\eta_{k}(x)]K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dydx

\displaystyle\geq-\int_{B_{1}}v_{k}(x)\,dx\times\sup_{x\in{\rm supp}\,v_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{1}}[(v_{e}(y)-1)+\delta_{k}\eta_{k}(x)]K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\,dy

There holds that

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{k}\|\eta_{k}\|_{{\rm lip}(B_{1})}\left(\int_{B_{1}}\chi_{\{v_{k}(x)>0\}}\,dx\int_{B_{2}(x)}\bigg(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+p(s-1)}}+\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+q(t-1)}}\bigg)\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(3)

\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;2^{K_{0}+1})\leq\varepsilon_{1}
,

for any

Proof.

For

Suppose this conclusion holds true for

in

. Obviously,

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2^{n}})}

\displaystyle=(1-\delta)^{-n_{0}}\left\|\nabla u\left(2^{-n_{0}}x\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2^{n}})}

\displaystyle=(1-\delta)^{-n_{0}}\|\nabla u(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2^{n-n_{0}}})}\leq(1-\delta)^{-n}

for

. Next, we justify

. It is easy to see that

Thus by changing variables there holds

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(v;2^{n_{0}+K_{0}+1})^{q-1}

\displaystyle=[(1-\delta)^{-(q-1)}2^{q(1-t)-1}]^{n_{0}}\int_{B^{c}_{2^{K_{0}+1}}}\frac{|u(x)|^{q-1}}{|x|^{d+tq}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\theta^{n_{0}}\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;2^{K_{0}+1})^{q-1}

with

\displaystyle=\sum^{n_{0}}_{n=1}\int_{B_{2^{n+K_{0}+1}}\setminus B_{2^{n+K_{0}}}}\frac{|v|^{q-1}}{|y|^{d+tq}}\,dy+\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(v;2^{n_{0}+K_{0}+1})^{q-1}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum^{n_{0}}_{n=1}(2^{q-1}(1-\delta)^{-(q-1)})^{n+K_{0}+1}2^{-tq(n+K_{0})}+\theta^{n_{0}}\varepsilon_{1}^{q-1}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\theta^{K_{0}+2}}{1-\theta}+\theta^{n_{0}}\varepsilon_{1}^{q-1}\leq\varepsilon_{1}^{q-1},

where in the last line we select

sufficiently large that depends on

.

In addition, we need show

Since

, for

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\frac{J_{p}(u(2^{-n_{0}}x)-u(y))}{|2^{-n_{0}}x-y|^{d+sp}}+a(2^{-n_{0}}x,y)\frac{J_{q}(u(2^{-n_{0}}x)-u(y))}{|2^{-n_{0}}x-y|^{d+tq}}\right]\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigg[\frac{((1-\delta)^{p-1}2^{-(p-1)})^{n_{0}}}{2^{-n_{0}sp}}\frac{J_{p}(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{((1-\delta)^{q-1}2^{-(q-1)})^{n_{0}}}{2^{-n_{0}tq}}a(2^{-n_{0}}x,2^{-n_{0}}y)\frac{J_{q}(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{d+tq}}\Bigg]\,dy.

Then

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that there are such two constants

Then there is

and

\int_{B_{2}}\int_{B_{2}}K(x,y)(w(x)-w(y))^{2}\,dy\,dx\geq c\lambda[w]_{H^{\gamma}(B_{1})}^{2}.

[

.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

be a solution of

Proposition 4.7
.

Let the assumptions
(A_{1})
–
(A_{4})
and
(H_{1})
–
(H_{3})
be in force. Assume
u

) in

\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;1)\leq C_{1}
. If there is a vector

satisfying

then
\nabla u
is locally Hölder continuous, that is, there a universal constant
\alpha>0
such that

\|\nabla u\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1/16})}\leq C.

Here
C>0
is a universal constant depending also on
C_{1}
.

Proof.

Define a function

w_{\sigma}(x)=\eta(12x)(\sigma\cdot\nabla u(x)),

where the direction
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
\eta
is a smooth cut-off function satisfying
\eta(12x)>0
for
|x|<\frac{7}{48}
as well as
\eta(12x)=1
for
|x|\leq\frac{1}{8}
. From Proposition
2.6
, there holds

\left|\mathcal{L}^{a}_{u}w_{\sigma}(x)\right|\leq C\quad\text{for any }x\in B_{\frac{1}{12}}.

(4.4)

In view of the condition
|\nabla u-e|<\frac{1}{4}
in
B_{1}
, for any vector
|\tau|\leq 1
with
|\tau\cdot e|>\frac{1}{2}
, we know that

\left|\nabla u(x)\cdot\tau\right|>\frac{1}{4}\quad\text{for each }x\in B_{1}.

(4.5)

This indicates that for all
x\in B_{\frac{1}{12}}
, the kernel
K^{a}_{u}(x,y)
is uniformly elliptic for
y
in the cone of directions
\mathcal{C}(x):=\left\{x+z\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}:|z\cdot e|>\frac{|z|}{2}\right\}
. That is, for
t\in\big(0,\frac{1}{2}\big)
and
|\nu\cdot e|>\frac{1}{2}
, by virtue of (
4.5
) and
a(\cdot)\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle K^{a}_{u}(x,x+t\nu)

\displaystyle=(p-1)\frac{|u(x)-u(x+t\nu)|^{p-2}}{t^{d+sp}}+(q-1)a(0,t\nu)\frac{|u(x)-u(x+t\nu)|^{q-2}}{t^{d+tq}}

\displaystyle\geq(p-1)\frac{\big(\frac{1}{4}t\big)^{p-2}}{t^{d+sp}}=ct^{-d+p(1-s)-2}.

(4.6)

In order to utilize Lemmas
4.5
and
4.6
, we have to modify the kernel and so the related equation. Now from (
4.4
) and the fact that
x+z\notin B_{\frac{7}{48}}
for
x\in B_{\frac{1}{12}}
and
z\notin B_{\frac{1}{2}}
, we can find

\displaystyle-C-w_{\sigma}(x)\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}}K^{a}_{u}(x,x+z)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}(w_{\sigma}(x)-w_{\sigma}(x+z))K^{a}_{u}(x,x+z)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C-w_{\sigma}(x)\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}}K^{a}_{u}(x,x+z)\,dz.

(4.7)

Recall the bounds on
a(\cdot),\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}
and
\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;1)
together with the relation
tq\leq sp+1
. We derive

\left|w_{\sigma}(x)\int_{B_{1/2}^{c}}K^{a}_{u}(x,x+z)\,dz\right|\leq C,

(4.8)

which is similar to Lemma
3.5
.

Introduce a new kernel

K(x,x+z)=K^{a}_{u}(x,x+z)\chi_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}(z)+\overline{K}(z)\chi_{B^{c}_{\frac{1}{2}}}(z),

where

\overline{K}(z)=c|z|^{-d+p(1-s)-2}

with the

identical to that in (
4
). As a consequence, it yields that

-C\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(w_{\sigma}(x)-w_{\sigma}(x+z))K(x,x+z)\,dz\leq C,

which is the objective we will apply Lemma
4.6
to.

Let us examine that
K
meets the assumptions (i)-(iii) in Lemma
4.6
properly scaled with
x_{0}\in B_{\frac{1}{12}}
and
0<\rho<\frac{1}{12}
. First, the symmetry of
K
in (i) is trivial, because we can see

K(x,y)=K^{a}_{u}(x,y)\chi_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x)}(y-x)+\overline{K}(y-x)\chi_{B^{c}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x)}(y-x).

Second, owing to
B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\subset B_{\frac{1}{2}}
, the boundedness of
a(\cdot)
and the local Lipschitz property of
u
, it holds

\displaystyle\rho^{-2}\int_{B_{\rho}}|z|^{2}K(x_{0},x_{0}+z)\,dz

\displaystyle=\rho^{-2}\int_{B_{\rho}}|z|^{2}K_{u}^{a}(x_{0},x_{0}+z)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{-2}\int_{B_{\rho}}|z|^{2}\left(\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{|z|^{d+sp}}+\|a\|_{\infty}\frac{|z|^{q-2}}{|z|^{d+tq}}\right)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|a\|_{\infty})\rho^{-2}\big(\rho^{p(1-s)}+\rho^{q(1-t)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\rho^{p(1-s)-2},

where in the last inequality we have used
\rho<1
and the condition
(H_{3})
implying
(1-s)p\leq(1-t)q
. On the other hand, noticing again
\rho<1
,
(1-s)p\leq(1-t)q
and
p(1-s)-2<0
,
q(1-t)-2<0
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{B^{c}_{\rho}}K(x_{0},x_{0}+z)\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}\setminus B_{\rho}}K(x_{0},x_{0}+z)\,dz+\int_{B^{c}_{\frac{1}{2}}}K(x_{0},x_{0}+z)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}\setminus B_{\rho}}\left(\frac{|z|^{p-2}}{|z|^{d+sp}}+\|a\|_{\infty}\frac{|z|^{q-2}}{|z|^{d+tq}}\right)\,dz+C

At this moment, we could make use of Lemma

Proof of Theorem

Let

At this point, we may apply Corollary

|\nabla u(x)|\leq C|x|^{\alpha_{0}}\quad\text{for }x\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}

5.
Proof of Lemma

In this section, we are going to exploit the Ishii-Lions methods to complete the proof of Lemma
4.3
, which is of independent interest for nonlocal double phase equations. Although this method has been applied to fractional
p
-Laplace equation to derive the bound on
|\nabla u-e|
, there are some new difficulties that we need carefully balance the subtle interaction between the pointwise behaviour of the coefficient and the exponents
s,t,p,q
, due to the nonstandard growth and mixed differentiability orders as well as the variable coefficient
a(\cdot)
.

Our aim is to employ the Ishii-Lions argument to infer the Lipschitz estimate for the function
u(x)-e\cdot x
in
B_{\frac{1}{2}}
, more precisely, to get

\|u-e\cdot x\|_{\rm lip{(B_{1/2})}}\leq L\quad\text{i.e., }|\nabla u(x)-e|\leq L\quad\text{ for }x\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let the function
u
satisfy the conditions of Lemma
4.3
. Denote

\Psi(x,y)=u(x)-u(y)-e\cdot(x-y)-L\omega(|x-y|)-\kappa\epsilon_{0}\psi(x)

and

\phi(x,y)=e\cdot(x-y)+L\omega(|x-y|)+\kappa\epsilon_{0}\psi(x).

The number
\kappa>0
is to be determined soon, and the function
\omega
is nonnegative, strictly concave and Lipschitz continuous, which is defined as

\omega(\rho)=\rho+\frac{\rho}{20\log(\rho/4)}.

Through simple calculations, we know that
\omega^{\prime}(\rho)\in(\frac{1}{4},1)
for
\rho\in(0,2]
, and

-\frac{C}{\rho\log^{2}\rho}\leq\omega^{\prime\prime}(\rho)\leq-\frac{c}{\rho\log^{2}\rho}\quad\text{for }\rho\in\Big(0,\frac{1}{2}\Big]

with
c,C>0
being universal constants. Let
\psi_{0}(x)\geq 0
be a smooth function that is equal to 0 in
\overline{B}_{\frac{1}{2}}
and is strictly positive in
\overline{B}_{1}\setminus\overline{B}_{\frac{1}{2}}
. We demand that the function
\psi(x)
fulfills
\psi(x)=\psi_{0}(x)^{m}
and

|\nabla\psi(x)|\leq C\psi(x)^{\frac{m-1}{m}}\quad\text{ for }x\in B_{1},

where
C=m\max{|\nabla\psi_{0}(x)|}
and
m>2
is a large integer to be selected later. Specifically, the function
(|x|^{2}-\frac{1}{4})_{+}
can be taken as an example of
\psi_{0}(x)
, so
[(|x|^{2}-\frac{1}{4})_{+}]^{m}
provides a model for
\psi(x)
.

In what follows, we are going to show that
u-e\cdot x
has
L\omega
as a modulus of continuity in
B_{\frac{1}{2}}
. To this end, we verify
\Psi(x,y)\leq 0
for all
x,y\in B_{1}
. Then this indicates the desired modulus of continuity in
B_{\frac{1}{2}}
, because
\psi
is zero in
B_{\frac{1}{2}}
. This thrives on contradiction. Suppose there are
\overline{x},\overline{y}\in B_{1}
such that

\Psi(\overline{x},\overline{y})=\sup_{(x,y)\in B_{1}\times B_{1}}\Psi(x,y)>0,

(5.1)

that is,

u(\overline{x})-u(\overline{y})-e\cdot(\overline{x}-\overline{y})>L\omega(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)+\kappa\epsilon_{0}\psi(\overline{x}).

It is known that the left-hand side is bounded by
\epsilon_{0}
. Thus we could take
\kappa
so large that
\overline{x}
is in
B_{\frac{5}{8}}
by the second term at the right-hand side. Let

\overline{a}:=\overline{x}-\overline{y}.

With the help of
L\omega(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)\leq\epsilon_{0}
, we can make
\overline{a}
arbitrarily small by selecting
\epsilon_{0}
small enough (depending on

), so that
\overline{x},\overline{y}
both belong to

Given
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[\Omega]u(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\frac{J_{p}(u(x)-u(x+z))}{|z|^{d+sp}}\,dz+\int_{\Omega}a(x,x+z)\frac{J_{q}(u(x)-u(x+z))}{|z|^{d+tq}}\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{J_{p}(u(x)-u(x+z))}{|z|^{d+sp}}\,dz+\int_{\Omega}a(0,z)\frac{J_{q}(u(x)-u(x+z))}{|z|^{d+tq}}\,dz

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{L}_{p}[\Omega]u(x)+\mathcal{L}_{q}[\Omega]u(x),

where we have used the translation invariance of
a(\cdot)
. Since

is a solution to Eq. (
1.1

\mathcal{L}u(\overline{x})-\mathcal{L}u(\overline{y})=0

and further

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}[\mathcal{C}]u(\overline{x})-\mathcal{L}[\mathcal{C}]u(\overline{y})+\mathcal{L}[\mathcal{D}]u(\overline{x})-\mathcal{L}[\mathcal{D}]u(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{L}[\mathcal{E}]u(\overline{x})-\mathcal{L}[\mathcal{E}]u(\overline{y})+\mathcal{L}\big[B^{c}_{\frac{1}{16}}\big]u(\overline{x})-\mathcal{L}\big[B^{c}_{\frac{1}{16}}\big]u(\overline{y})

Proof.

Define

F(x)=\int_{B_{R}^{c}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(z))}{|x-z|^{d+sp}}+a(x,z)\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(z))}{|x-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let the conditions
(A_{3}),(A_{4})
on the coefficient
a(\cdot)
hold. Assume that
u\in L^{q-1}_{tq}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is Lipschitz in
B_{r}
and bounded in
B_{R+r}
with
0<r\leq R
. Then for
x,y\in B_{r}
, it holds that

\displaystyle|F(x)-F(y)|\leq C|x-y|,

where the constant
C>0
depends on
d,s,t,p,q,\|a\|_{\rm lip}
and
R^{-1},\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+r})}
,
[u]_{{\rm lip}(B_{r})}
,
\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;R+r)
.

Proof.

We rewrite
F(x)
as

F(x)=F_{p}(x)+F_{q}(x):=\int_{B_{R}^{c}(x)}\frac{J_{p}(u(x)-u(z))}{|x-z|^{d+sp}}\,dz+\int_{B_{R}^{c}(x)}a(x,z)\frac{J_{q}(u(x)-u(z))}{|x-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz.

We just consider
F_{q}(x)
. Let
x,y\in B_{r}
fulfill
|x-y|<\frac{R}{2}
. We evaluate

\displaystyle\quad|F_{q}(x)-F_{q}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R}^{c}(x)}a(x,z)|J_{q}(u(x)-u(z))|\left||x-z|^{-d-tq}-|y-z|^{-d-tq}\right|\,dz

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{B_{R}^{c}(x)}a(x,z)\frac{|J_{q}(u(x)-u(z))|}{|y-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz-\int_{B_{R}^{c}(y)}a(x,z)\frac{|J_{q}(u(x)-u(z))|}{|y-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{R}^{c}(y)}a(x,z)\frac{|J_{q}(u(x)-u(z))-J_{q}(u(y)-u(z))|}{|y-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{R}^{c}(y)}|a(x,z)-a(y,z)|\frac{|J_{q}(u(y)-u(z))|}{|y-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\|a\|_{\infty}\int_{B_{R}^{c}(x)}|u(x)-u(z)|^{q-1}\left||x-z|^{-d-tq}-|y-z|^{-d-tq}\right|\,dz

\displaystyle\quad+\|a\|_{\infty}\int_{B_{R}^{c}(x)\triangle B_{R}^{c}(y)}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{q-1}}{|x-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz

\displaystyle\quad+\|a\|_{\infty}\int_{B_{R}^{c}(y)}\frac{|J_{q}(u(x)-u(z))-J_{q}(u(y)-u(z))|}{|y-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{R}^{c}(y)}|a(x,z)-a(y,z)|\frac{|u(y)-u(z)|^{q-1}}{|y-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz

\displaystyle=:\|a\|_{\infty}I_{1}+\|a\|_{\infty}I_{2}+\|a\|_{\infty}I_{3}+I_{4},

where
B_{R}^{c}(x)\triangle B_{R}^{c}(y)=\Big(B_{R}^{c}(x)\setminus B_{R}^{c}(y)\Big)\cup\Big(B_{R}^{c}(y)\setminus B_{R}^{c}(x)\Big)
stands for the symmetric difference. Using the Lipschitz continuity of
a(\cdot)
, we handle
I_{4}
as

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q-1}[a]_{\rm lip}\int_{B_{R}^{c}(y)}|x-y|\frac{|u(y)|^{q-1}+|u(z)|^{q-1}}{|y-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C[a]_{\rm lip}|x-y|\left(R^{-tq}\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}+\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;y,R)^{q-1}\right).

The treatment of
I_{1}-I_{3}
is similar to that of
J_{1}-J_{3}
in
[
31
, Proof of Lemma 6.9]
, so we directly write

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}\leq C|x-y|\Big(

\displaystyle R^{-tq-1}\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+r})}+\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq+1}(u;x,R)^{q-1}

\displaystyle+[u]_{{\rm lip}(B_{r})}\left(R^{-tq}\|u\|^{q-2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}+\mathrm{Tail}_{q-2,tq}(u;y,R)^{q-2}\right)\Big).

Then we further have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq+1}(u;x,R)^{q-1}\leq R^{-1}\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;x,R)^{q-1}

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}_{q-2,tq}(u;y,R)^{q-2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}(y)}\frac{|u(z)|^{q-1}}{|y-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q-1}}\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}(y)}\frac{dz}{|y-z|^{d+tq}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}

\displaystyle=CR^{-\frac{tq}{q-1}}\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;y,R)^{q-2}.

For
x\in B_{r}
and
z\in B^{c}_{R}(x)
, we get

|z|\leq|z-x|\left(1+\frac{|x|}{|z-x|}\right)\leq|z-x|\left(1+\frac{r}{R}\right)\leq 2|z-x|,

and
B_{R}(x)\subset B_{R+r}
as well as

B^{c}_{R}(x)=B_{R+r}^{c}\cup\big(B_{R+r}\setminus B_{R}(x)\big)\subset B_{R+r}^{c}\cup\big(B_{R+3r}(x)\setminus B_{R}(x)\big).

Thus there holds that

\int_{B_{R}^{c}(x)}\frac{|u(z)|^{q-1}}{|x-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz\leq 2^{d+tq}\int_{B_{R+r}^{c}}\frac{|u(z)|^{q-1}}{|z|^{d+tq}}\,dz+\int_{B_{R+r}\setminus B_{R}(x)}\frac{|u(z)|^{q-1}}{|x-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz

and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R+r}\setminus B_{R}(x)}\frac{|u(z)|^{q-1}}{|x-z|^{d+tq}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+r})}\int_{B_{R+r}\setminus B_{R}(x)}\frac{dz}{|x-z|^{d+tq}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+r})}\int_{B_{R+3r}(x)\setminus B_{R}(x)}\frac{dz}{|x-z|^{d+tq}}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-tq}\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+r})}.

Then,

\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;x,R)^{q-1}\leq C\big(\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;R+r)^{q-1}+R^{-tq}\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+r})}\big)

and analogously,

\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;y,R)^{q-1}\leq C\big(\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;R+r)^{q-1}+R^{-tq}\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+r})}\big).

Here the constant
C>0
depends only on
d,t,q
.

At this stage, combing these preceding displays we obtain

\displaystyle\quad|F_{q}(x)-F_{q}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq C(d,t,q,\|a\|_{\rm lip})|x-y|\Big((R^{-tq}+R^{-tq-1})\|u\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+r})}+R^{-tq}[u]_{{\rm lip}(B_{r})}\|u\|^{q-2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+r})}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+R^{-1})\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;R+r)^{q-1}+[u]_{{\rm lip}(B_{r})}R^{-\frac{tq}{q-1}}\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;R+r)^{q-2}\Big)

\displaystyle=:C|x-y|,

where the positive constant
C
depends on
d,t,q,\|a\|_{\rm lip},[u]_{{\rm lip}(B_{r})}
,
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+r})}
and
R^{-1}
,
\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;R+r)
. The evaluation of
|F_{p}(x)-F_{p}(y)|
is easier, so we directly write

|F_{p}(x)-F_{p}(y)|\leq C|x-y|.

Here the constant
C>0
depends on
d,s,p
,
[u]_{{\rm lip}(B_{r})}
,
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+r})}
and
R^{-1}
,
\mathrm{Tail}_{q-1,tq}(u;R+r)
. Hence we have

|F(x)-F(y)|\leq C|x-y|\quad\text{for }|x-y|<\frac{R}{2}.

In addition, if
x,y\in B_{r}
satisfies
|x-y|\geq\frac{R}{2}
, by noting
|x-y|<2R
, we connect them with three intermediate points
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
in
B_{r}
such that
|x-x_{1}|<\frac{R}{2}
,
|x_{i}-x_{i+1}|<\frac{R}{2}
,
|x_{3}-y|<\frac{R}{2}
(
i=1,2
). Then we can repeat the previous processes for these situations and get further the desired conclusion.
∎

Proof of Lemma

.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq CL\delta_{0}^{d+p-1}|\overline{a}|^{p(1-s)-1}+a(0,0)CL\delta_{0}^{d+q-1}|\overline{a}|^{q(1-t)-1}-CL[a]_{\rm lip}\delta_{0}^{d+q-1}|\overline{a}|^{q(1-t)}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\bigg[CL\delta_{0}^{d+p-1}|\overline{a}|^{p(1-s)-1}-CL\delta_{1}^{p(1-s)}|\overline{a}|^{p(1-s)-1}-C|\overline{a}|^{\frac{p(1-s)}{2}}-C|\overline{a}|

\displaystyle\quad+a(0,0)\left(CL\delta_{0}^{d+q-1}|\overline{a}|^{q(1-t)-1}-CL\delta_{1}^{q(1-t)}|\overline{a}|^{q(1-t)-1}-C|\overline{a}|^{\frac{q(1-t)}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

We shall justify the right-hand side is larger than 0 by choose suitable exponent
\nu

\delta_{1}
and taking
\epsilon_{0}
small enough. Remember

Thus we get

On the other hand, similarly, by picking such large

that
\nu p(1-s)>d+p-1
and the fact
p<\frac{2}{1-s}
, we know

\delta_{0}^{d+p-1}|\overline{a}|^{p(1-s)-1}\gg\delta_{1}^{p(1-s)}|\overline{a}|^{p(1-s)-1}+|\overline{a}|^{\frac{p(1-s)}{2}}+|\overline{a}|.

The remaining task is to compare
\delta_{0}^{d+p-1}|\overline{a}|^{p(1-s)-1}
with the last three terms in
I_{1}
. We first observe that

\delta_{0}^{d+q-1}|\overline{a}|^{q(1-t)}\gg\delta_{1}^{q(1-t)+1}|\overline{a}|^{q(1-t)}

by choosing sufficiently large
\nu
. Taking into the preconditions
p\leq q
and
tq\leq sp+1
, we have
d+p-1\leq d+q-1
and
p(1-s)-1<q(1-t)
, and moreover get

\delta_{0}^{d+p-1}|\overline{a}|^{p(1-s)-1}\gg\delta_{0}^{d+q-1}|\overline{a}|^{q(1-t)}.

Finally, let us justify the relation
p(1-s)-1\leq\frac{q(1-t)+1}{2}
so that we derive

\delta_{0}^{d+p-1}|\overline{a}|^{p(1-s)-1}\gg|\overline{a}|^{\frac{q(1-t)+1}{2}}.

Indeed, resorting to
p\leq q,tq\leq sp+1
and
p<\frac{2}{1-s}
, we can see

\displaystyle tq\leq sp+q-p+(s-1)\frac{2}{1-s}+3\leq sp+q-p+(s-1)p+3

\displaystyle\qquad\Rightarrow\ p(1-s)-1\leq\frac{q(1-t)+1}{2}.

At this time, we get
I_{1}>0
and so arrive at

0=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+T_{4}>0,

which is a contradiction. This end the proof.
∎
