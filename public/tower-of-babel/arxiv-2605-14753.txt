Radiative correction to the charge asymmetry in e + ​ e − → μ + ​ μ − e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-} process.
Roman E. Gerasimov, Petr A. Krachkov, and Roman N. Lee

Abstract
We calculate the next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) QED corrections to the C C -odd part of the differential cross section of the e + ​ e − → μ + ​ μ − e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-} process. This part contributes to the angular and forward-backward asymmetry. Together with our earlier paper 5 , this work completes the analytical calculation of e + ​ e − → μ + ​ μ − e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-} differential cross section at NNLO.

Introduction

Muon pair production in electron-positron annihilation stands as one of the most fundamental processes in quantum electrodynamics (QED). Its simplicity in the Born approximation makes it the “Hello, world!” example in many quantum field theory textbooks. Nevertheless, this process also has a great importance for the experiments. It serves not only as a key process for luminosity determination at
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders but also constitutes a significant background for searches of rare events and potential New Physics manifestations. The latter provides a strong motivation to make the theoretical predictions as precise as possible.

The experimental precision achieved at modern colliders necessitates theoretical predictions beyond the leading order. The next-to-leading order (NLO) QED corrections to this process have been known for decades
2
;
3
;
8
. The pursuit of NNLO precision is now motivated by the requirements of ongoing and future experiments, as highlighted in recent reviews
1

5

C
-even part of the cross section exactly in the muon mass.

Although the muon mass
m_{\mu}
can often be considered as small compared to the
\sqrt{s}

dependence precise bearing in mind the applicability of the obtained results to the production of taus (with some reservations). The exact account of muon mass is also essential when this process serves as a background for the measurement of specific observables like the pion form factor contributing to the hadronic vacuum polarization correction to
(g-2)_{\mu}

m_{e}

3.1
Asymptotics

Using our exact results, we calculate the threshold asymptotics and high-energy asymptotics.
The calculation of the threshold asymptotics is straightforward as it is reduced to the expansion of Goncharov’s polylogarithms at small argument.
The calculation of the high-energy asymptotics is more involved. First, we pass from polylogarithms of argument
\beta
to those of argument
1-\beta
. When we do so, the coefficients in front of
G(\ldots|1-\beta)
are expressed in terms of
G(\boldsymbol{w}|1)
where weights belong to the same alphabet (
17
). Then the functions
G(\ldots|1-\beta)
can be easily expanded in
1-\beta
or, equivalently, in
1/s=(1-\beta^{2})/4
. Expanding up to
1/s^{4}
, we collect the terms by the inverse powers of
s
and by linearly independent rational functions of
c
. Specifically, for our purpose it is sufficient to take the functions
(1\pm c)^{-n}
with
n\in\{0,1,2,3\}
. These functions are multiplied by the linear combinations with integer coefficients of
G(\boldsymbol{w}|1)
with
w_{k}
from (
17
). In order to express these combinations in terms of the classical polylogarithms with argument depending on
c
, it is possible to use the symbol map. However, we find it simpler to use the following practical approach. We determine a conjectural basis of polylogarithmic functions of
c
which may be involved. We use the basis of functions which appeared in the high-energy asymptotics of the
C
-even part of the cross section obtained in our paper
5
. It is constructed of (the products of) the functions

\ln\tfrac{1\pm c}{2},\,\ln 2,\,\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(\tfrac{1+c}{2}\right),\,\pi^{2},\mathrm{Li}_{3}\left(\tfrac{1\pm c}{2}\right),\,\zeta_{3},\,\mathrm{Li}_{4}\left(\tfrac{1\pm c}{2}\right),\,\mathrm{Li}_{4}\left(\tfrac{1-c}{1+c}\right)\,.

(18)

Then we choose
\theta
so as to make
c=\cos{\theta}
and
e^{i\theta}
sufficiently transcendental (e.g., we can take
\theta=1
) and use PSLQ algorithm to recognize the required linear combinations of
G(\ldots|1)
in terms of linear combinations of the described basis of functions with rational coefficients. For more safety, we numerically check the obtained
analytic
expressions in several other points with high precision.

Asymmetry

The differential angular asymmetry is defined via

\mathrm{A}(c)=\frac{d\sigma_{C\text{-odd}}(\omega_{0})}{d\sigma_{C\text{-even}}(\omega_{0})},

(39)

so that
\mathrm{A}(c)=-\mathrm{A}(-c)
.
In the leading approximation the denominator in Eq. (
39
) can be replaced by
d\sigma_{0}
and the asymmetry coincides with the
C
-odd relative correction
\delta_{\text{C-odd}}^{(1)}
, Eq. (
36
),

\mathrm{A}^{(1)}(c)=\delta_{\text{C-odd}}^{(1)}=a\Bigg[\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}_{2\gamma}^{(1)}\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)*}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}+\mathcal{U}_{IF}\Bigg]\,.

(40)

In particular,
\mathrm{A}^{(1)}(c)
depends on
\omega_{0}
via
\mathcal{U}_{IF}
in Eq. (
36
). In order to find the
O(a^{2})
correction to the asymmetry, we need to take into account the expansion of the denominator up to
a^{1}
.
The
C
-even cross section with the required accuracy reads

\frac{d\sigma_{C\text{-even}}(\omega_{0})}{d\Omega}=\frac{d\sigma_{0}}{d\Omega}\left[1+\delta^{(1)}_{C\text{-even}}+\delta^{(1)}_{C\text{-even},\text{(had)}}+O(a^{2})\right]\,.

(41)

where

\delta_{\text{C-even}}^{(1)}=a\left[\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(1)}\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)*}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}+\mathcal{U}_{II}+\mathcal{U}_{FF}\right],\qquad\quad\delta_{\text{C-even},\text{(had)}}^{(1)}=a\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma,\text{(had)}}^{(1)}\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)*}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}\,.

(42)

Then we obtain

\mathrm{A}^{(2)}(c)+\mathrm{A}_{\text{(had)}}^{(2)}(c)=\left[\delta_{\text{C-odd}}^{(2)}-\delta_{\text{C-odd}}^{(1)}\delta_{\text{C-even}}^{(1)}\right]+\left[\delta_{\text{C-odd},\text{(had)}}^{(2)}-\delta_{\text{C-odd}}^{(1)}\delta_{\text{C-even},\text{(had)}}^{(1)}\right]\,.

(43)

Substituting Eqs. (
36
), (
37
), (
38
), and (
42
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}^{(2)}(c)

\displaystyle=a^{2}\Bigg[\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{2\gamma}\mathcal{H}^{(0)*}_{1\gamma}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}+\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{2\gamma}\mathcal{H}^{(1)*}_{1\gamma}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}-\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{2\gamma}\mathcal{H}^{(0)*}_{1\gamma}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{1\gamma}\mathcal{H}^{(0)*}_{1\gamma}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}\Bigg]\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}_{\text{(had)}}^{(2)}(c)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{A}^{(2)}\bigg|_{\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(1)}\to\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma,\text{(had)}}^{(1)},\ \mathcal{H}_{2\gamma}^{(2)}\to\mathcal{H}_{2\gamma,\text{(had)}}^{(2)}}

(44)

Remarkably, the terms depending on
\mathcal{U}
canceled and
\mathrm{A}^{(2)}
and
\mathrm{A}_{\text{(had)}}^{(2)}
do not depend on
\omega_{0}
.

Another conventional experimental quantity is the
forward-backward asymmetry

\mathrm{A}_{\text{FB}}
defined as

\mathrm{A}_{\text{FB}}=\frac{\int\limits_{c>0}d\sigma-\int\limits_{c<0}d\sigma}{\int\limits_{c>0}d\sigma+\int\limits_{c<0}d\sigma}=\frac{\int\limits_{c>0}d\sigma_{C\text{-odd}}(\omega_{0})}{\int\limits_{c>0}d\sigma_{C\text{-even}}(\omega_{0})}\,.

(45)

At first glance, it may seem that
\mathrm{A}_{\text{FB}}
at one and two loops can be given by wrapping each quadratic in
\mathcal{H}
combination in (
40
) and (
44
) with the integration
\intop_{0}^{1}dc[\ldots]
. Introducing weighted angle averaging
\left\langle\ldots\right\rangle=\int_{c>0}\frac{2d\sigma_{0}}{\sigma_{0}}
we obtain at one loop

A_{\text{FB}}^{(1)}=\left\langle A^{(1)}(c)\right\rangle=A_{\text{FB}}^{(1,\text{hard})}+A_{\text{FB}}^{(1,\text{soft})}=a\left\langle\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}_{2\gamma}^{(1)}\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)*}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}\right\rangle+a\left\langle\mathcal{U}_{IF}\right\rangle.

(46)

In particular, we have

A_{\text{FB}}^{(1,\text{soft})}=\frac{3a}{2(3-\beta^{2})}\int_{0}^{1}(2-\beta^{2}(1-c^{2}))\,\mathcal{U}_{IF}\,dc

(47)

For two loops the same prescription gives

\mathrm{A}_{\text{FB}}^{(2,\text{hard})}=a^{2}\Bigg[\left\langle\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{2\gamma}\mathcal{H}^{(0)*}_{1\gamma}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}\right\rangle+\left\langle\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{2\gamma}\mathcal{H}^{(1)*}_{1\gamma}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}\right\rangle-\left\langle\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{2\gamma}\mathcal{H}^{(0)*}_{1\gamma}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}\right\rangle\left\langle\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{1\gamma}\mathcal{H}^{(0)*}_{1\gamma}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}\right\rangle\Bigg]

(48)

However, this is not the only contribution to
\mathrm{A}_{\text{FB}}^{(2)}
because the functions
\mathcal{U}_{IF}
depend on
c
and enter the integrals over
c
in the numerator and denominator of Eq. (
45
) with different integration weights and, thus, their contribution does not vanish. The correct expression for
\mathrm{A}_{\text{FB}}^{(2)}
is

\mathrm{A}_{\text{FB}}^{(2)}=\mathrm{A}_{\text{FB}}^{(2,\text{hard})}+\mathrm{A}_{\text{FB}}^{(2,\text{soft})},

(49)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}_{\text{FB}}^{(2,\text{soft})}

\displaystyle=a^{2}\left[\left\langle\mathcal{U}_{IF}\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(1)}\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)*}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathcal{U}_{IF}\right\rangle\left\langle\tfrac{2\Re\left[\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(1)}\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)*}\right]}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(0)}\right|^{2}}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{3a^{2}\beta(1-\beta^{2})}{(3-\beta^{2})^{2}}\ \ln\left(\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}\right)\ \int_{0}^{1}(3c^{2}-1)\,\mathcal{U}_{IF}\,dc

(50)

The contributions to forward-backward asymmetry related to the hadronic vacuum polarization are obtained by the same replacement
\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(1)}\to\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma,\text{(had)}}^{(1)},\ \mathcal{H}_{2\gamma}^{(2)}\to\mathcal{H}_{2\gamma,\text{(had)}}^{(2)}
as in Eq. (
44
) followed by deletion of the terms which do not depend on
\mathcal{H}_{n\gamma,\text{(had)}}^{(L)}
. In particular, we have

\begin{split}&A_{\text{FB},\text{(had)}}^{(1)}=0~,\quad A_{\text{FB},\text{(had)}}^{(2,\text{soft})}=0~,\\
&A_{\text{FB},\text{(had)}}^{(2,\text{hard})}=A_{\text{FB}}^{(2,\text{hard})}\bigg|_{\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma}^{(1)}\to\mathcal{H}_{1\gamma,\text{(had)}}^{(1)},\ \mathcal{H}_{2\gamma}^{(2)}\to\mathcal{H}_{2\gamma,\text{(had)}}^{(2)}}~.\end{split}

(51)

Conclusion

To conclude, we have calculated all QED and NNLO contributions to the
C
-odd part of the differential cross section, including the hadronic vacuum polarization insertion, for
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
process. This part of the cross section corresponds to the angular asymmetry in comparison to
\theta\to\pi-\theta
. We used the fact that the ratio
m^{2}/M^{2}=m_{e}^{2}/m_{\mu}^{2}
is tiny and neglected the power corrections in this parameter. Our results can be used for arbitrary polarization of all involved particles.

Using

9

42
master integrals,

10
are additional master integrals for the second family. The differential equations have the form:

where

We use

10

\epsilon

\beta

and get the transformation matrix

that reduce both systems to
\epsilon
-form:

\partial_{\beta}\vec{J}=\epsilon M_{\beta}\vec{J}~,\quad\partial_{c}\vec{J}=\epsilon M_{c}\vec{J}~.

(58)

Any specific solution has the form
\vec{J}=U\vec{J}_{0}
, where
U
is an evolution matrix and

are boundary constants. The matrix
U
is defined by the concrete evolution path from the boundary. Using matrices

and

\epsilon

\vec{J}_{0}=L\vec{c}_{0}

\vec{J}_{0}=UL_{0}\vec{c}_{0}~.

(59)

It is possible to set boundary conditions at different points and perform evolution to the necessary point
\{\beta,~c\}
. The threshold point
\beta=0

on

The remaining
8
