Large values of shifted mixed character sums
Néo Tardy

Abstract
. We consider sums of the form F χ ​ ( α , β , θ ) := ∑ α ​ p < n ≤ β ​ p χ ⁡ ( n ) ​ e ​ ( n ​ θ ) , F_{\chi}(\alpha,\beta;\theta)\vcentcolon=\sum_{\alpha p<n\leq\beta p}\chi(n)e(n\theta), where χ \chi is a non-principal Dirichlet character modulo a prime number p p . We prove that p ​ log ⁡ log ⁡ p ≪ max 0 ≤ θ < 1 ⁡ | F χ ​ ( α , β , θ ) | ≪ p ​ log ⁡ p , \sqrt{p}\log\log p\ll\max_{0\leq\theta<1}{\left|F_{\chi}(\alpha,\beta;\theta)\right|}\ll\sqrt{p}\log p, generalizing an old result of Montgomery as well as a recent result of Iggidr in two aspects: we allow general non-principal characters χ \chi , and we consider incomplete mixed character sums.



1.
Introduction

The distribution of
mixed character sums
of the form

\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)e(n\theta),

where
\chi
is a Dirichlet character and
e(t)\vcentcolon=e^{2\pi it}
, is a well-studied problem that has given rise to many recent developments. In this paper, we are interested in the case where one fixes
x
and varies
\theta
as in the case of the
Fekete polynomials

F_{p}(\theta)\vcentcolon=\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}\left(\frac{n}{p}\right)e(n\theta),

where
p
is prime. This particular case can be regarded as the simplest case to analyze, and its distribution has been widely studied (see e.g.
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
5
]
for various bounds on the
L^{q}
-norms (
0<q<\infty
) and on the Mahler measure of
F_{p}
). When
q=2k
is an even positive integer, the asymptotics of the
L^{2k}
-norms of the Fekete polynomials as
p\to+\infty
were determined by Günther and Schmidt
[
8
]
to be
\|F_{p}\|_{2k}\sim\phi_{k}\sqrt{p}
, where
\phi_{k}
only depends on
k
. This was later generalized by Klurman, Lamzouri and Munsch
[
13
]
for all
q>0
. Their method is based on a randomization of
F_{p}(\theta)
, where they prove that, if
F_{p}(\theta)
is viewed as a random continuous function defined on a suitable probability space, then it converges in distribution to a random function that can be computed explicitly. In particular, they were able to determine the asymptotic behaviour of the Mahler measure
M_{0}(F_{p})=\lim\limits_{q\to 0^{+}}\|F_{p}\|_{q}
of the Fekete polynomials as
p\to+\infty
, proving that
M_{0}(F_{p})\sim\phi_{0}\sqrt{p}
for some absolute constant
\phi_{0}\approx 0.749
.

Possible generalizations of the Fekete polynomials are
Turyn polynomials

F_{p}^{r}(\theta)\vcentcolon=\sum_{n=1}^{p}\left(\frac{n+r}{p}\right)e(n\theta),

where
r
is an integer, which we typically assume to be of the form
\lfloor\alpha p\rfloor
for some fixed
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, and
shifted mixed character sums

F_{\chi}(\alpha,\beta;\theta)\vcentcolon=\sum_{\alpha p<n\leq\beta p}\chi(n)e(n\theta).

Note that if
r=\lfloor\alpha p\rfloor
, we have
F_{p}^{r}(\theta)=F_{\left(\frac{\bullet}{p}\right)}(\alpha,\alpha+1;\theta)
. In
[
8
]
, the authors extended their study of the
L^{2k}
-norms to the asymptotic behaviour of
\|F_{p}^{r}\|_{2k}
when
\frac{r}{p}\to\alpha
. More generally, the distribution of
F_{\chi}(\alpha,\beta;\theta)
when
\theta
varies was understood in a recent paper by Bober, Klurman and Shala
[
2
]
using a similar randomization trick as in
[
13
]
.

A natural question that arises in connection with the distribution concerns large values, which provide information about the tail of the distribution of the values of
F_{\chi}(\alpha,\beta;\theta)
as
\theta
varies. The first result in this direction is due to Montgomery
[
14
]
and was proved for Fekete polynomials. He showed that for all large enough
p
, we have

(1.1)

\sqrt{p}\log\log p\ll\max\limits_{0\leq\theta<1}|F_{p}(\theta)|\ll\sqrt{p}\log p,

and conjectured that

\max\limits_{0\leq\theta<1}|F_{p}(\theta)|\asymp\sqrt{p}\log\log p.

Later, Conrey, Granville, Poonen and Soundararajan
[
4
]
gave an alternative proof of (
1.1
). Very recently, Iggidr
[
11
, Corollary 1.4]
generalized the lower bound of (
1.1
) to all non-principal Dirichlet characters
\chi
mod
p
by proving that

\max\limits_{0\leq\theta<1}|F_{\chi}(0,1;\theta)|\gg\sqrt{p}\log\log p,

and claimed that his method can provide the same bound for Turyn polynomials
F_{\chi}(\alpha,\alpha+1;\theta)
. In fact, the result in
[
11
]
is more general, and describes the exact distribution of the large values of
F_{\chi}(0,1;\theta)
.

Since a naive heuristic suggests that Dirichlet characters behave like random variables uniformly distributed on their support, it is interesting to compare (
1.1
) to known bounds for the random analogue

Q_{N}(\theta)\vcentcolon=\sum_{n=1}^{N}X_{n}e(n\theta),

where the
X_{n}
are independent Rademacher random variables. In this setting, it was proved by Salem and Zygmund
[
15
]
that, almost surely, for
N
large enough,

\max\limits_{0\leq\theta<1}|Q_{N}(\theta)|\asymp\sqrt{N\log N},

later improved by Halász
[
9
]
to
\sim\sqrt{N\log N}
. A more precise model would take into account the multiplicativity of characters, and we set

P_{N}(\theta)\vcentcolon=\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n)e(n\theta),

where
f
is a random multiplicative function. If
f
is a Rademacher or a Steinhaus random multiplicative function, it was proved by Benatar, Nishry and Rodgers
[
1
]
that, almost surely, for
N
large enough,

\sqrt{N}\left(\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}\right)^{1/6}\ll\max\limits_{0\leq\theta<1}|P_{N}(\theta)|\ll\sqrt{N}\exp\left(3\sqrt{\log N\log\log N}\right).

This was recently improved by Hardy
[
10
]
, who proved that, almost surely, for
N
large enough,

\max\limits_{0\leq\theta<1}|P_{N}(\theta)|\gg\sqrt{N\log N},

and conjectured that this lower bound is of the correct order of magnitude.
Surprisingly, these bounds are inconsistent with the conjectured size of
\max\limits_{0\leq\theta<1}|F_{p}(\theta)|
, which suggests that Dirichlet characters exhibit more cancellation than a typical random multiplicative function. Indeed,
P_{N}(\theta)
is expected to behave like a complex Gaussian random variable when
\theta
varies (see
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
). However, the limiting distribution of
F_{p}(\theta)
(or more generally of
F_{\chi}(\alpha,\beta;\theta)
) discovered in
[
2
,
13
]
is not Gaussian. This may be explained by the fact that Dirichlet characters exhibit analytic structures that are absent from random multiplicative functions (see Proposition
1.2
).

In this paper, we generalize Montgomery’s bounds (
1.1
) to all shifted mixed character sums.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p
be a sufficiently large prime,
\chi
a non-principal Dirichlet character mod
p
, and
\beta>\alpha\geq 0
. Then,

\sqrt{p}\log\log p\ll_{\alpha,\beta}\max_{0\leq\theta<1}{\left|F_{\chi}(\alpha,\beta;\theta)\right|}\ll_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p}\log p.

Our first step in the proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on the following randomization trick introduced by Klurman, Lamzouri and Munsch
[
13
]
and then used by Bober, Klurman and Shala
[
2
]
and Iggidr
[
11
]
to study mixed character sums. The key idea is to introduce randomness by looking at
F_{\chi}(\alpha,\beta;\theta)
as the continuous random process

k\mapsto F_{\chi}\left(\alpha,\beta;\frac{k+t}{p}\right),

where
0\leq t<1
. Using probabilistic methods, they study its limiting distribution as
p\to+\infty
, which takes the form of an infinite sum of independent random variables. In the present case, we do not need to invoke the probabilistic interpretation, but we use the aforementioned idea by writing

(1.2)

\max_{0\leq\theta<1}{\left|F_{\chi}(\alpha,\beta;\theta)\right|}=\max_{0\leq k\leq p-1}\max_{0\leq t<1}\left|F_{\chi}\left(\alpha,\beta;\frac{k+t}{p}\right)\right|.

This allows us to use the following proposition due to Bober, Klurman and Shala
[
2
, Proposition 3]
, which provides a formula for handling incomplete sums.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
p
be a prime,
\chi
a non-principal Dirichlet character mod
p
,
\beta>\alpha\geq 0
,
0\leq k\leq p-1
,
t\in(0,1)
and
K\geq 1
. Then,

(1.3)

\begin{multlined}F_{\chi}\left(\alpha,\beta;\frac{k+t}{p}\right)=\frac{\tau(\chi)e(\alpha k)}{2\pi i}\sum_{\left|l\right|<K}{\frac{e\left(\beta\left(l+t\right)\right)-e\left(\alpha\left(l+t\right)\right)}{l+t}\overline{\chi}\left(k-l\right)}\\
+O\left(\frac{p\log{p}}{K}+1\right),\end{multlined}

where
\tau(\chi)
is the Gauss sum associated with
\chi
.

Note that, since a non-principal character
\chi
modulo a prime number
p
is a primitive character, we have
|\tau(\chi)|=\sqrt{p}
. Thus, the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
follows immediately from Proposition
1.2
with
K=p
.

Outline of the proof

Our proof uses similar ideas to those of Montgomery
[
14
]
and Conrey, Granville, Poonen and Soundararajan
[
4
]
, incorporating Proposition
1.2
as a new ingredient. We observe in Montgomery’s proof that the value of
t\in(0,1)
does not affect the size of the maximum in (
1.2
). Thus, for the lower bound, we view
t
as being fixed. Then, we shall prove that there exists an integer
k\in[0,p-1]
such that
F_{\chi}\left(\alpha,\beta;\frac{k+t}{p}\right)\gg_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p}\log\log p
. To do so, we will prove that there is a set
\mathcal{S}
of values of
k
for which we can prescribe the values of
\chi(k-l)
for all
|l|\leq K_{0}
for some
K_{0}\geq 1
. With a suitable choice of the values, we will be able to prove that the main term of (
1.3
) has size
\gg\sqrt{p}\log K_{0}
. Thus, in the first instance, we shall view
K_{0}
as a bounded power of
\log p
. We will construct the set
\mathcal{S}
in Lemma
2.1
, and the key ingredient for that is the following proposition (see e.g.
[
12
, Theorem 11.23]
).

Proposition 1.3

(Weil’s bound for character sums)
.

Let
p
be a prime and
\chi
a non-principal Dirichlet character mod
p
of order
d
. Suppose
P\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\left[X\right]
has
m
distinct roots and
P
is not a
d
-th power. Then,

\left|\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\chi\left(P(k)\right)\right|\leq\left(m-1\right)\sqrt{p}.

With the above choice of
K=K_{0}
, the error term in Proposition
1.2
would be too large compared to the main term. Nevertheless, we will see that this can be made smaller after averaging over
k\in\mathcal{S}
.

Notation

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor
its fractional part and
\|x\|
its minimal distance to an integer. We also use the standard notation
e(x)\vcentcolon=e^{2\pi ix}
. For
d\geq 1
, we set
\mu_{d}\vcentcolon=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:z^{d}=1\}
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We have seen that the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
is a direct consequence of Proposition
1.2
. Thus, we now turn to the proof of the lower bound.

Let
t\in[0.1,0.9]
be fixed. Let us take a large prime
p
and
\beta>\alpha\geq 0
. Take
K_{0}\vcentcolon=\left\lfloor\frac{\log{p}}{\left(\log{\log{p}}\right)^{2}}\right\rfloor
and define for all integers
k\in[0,p-1]
and Dirichlet characters
\chi
mod
p
,

F_{\chi,k}(\alpha,\beta)\vcentcolon=F_{\chi}\left(\alpha,\beta;\frac{k+t}{p}\right)

and

{\widetilde{F}}_{\chi,k}(\alpha,\beta)\vcentcolon=\frac{\tau(\chi)e(\alpha k)}{2\pi i}\sum_{\left|l\right|\leq K_{0}}{\frac{e\left(\beta\left(l+t\right)\right)-e\left(\alpha\left(l+t\right)\right)}{l+t}\overline{\chi}\left(k-l\right)}.

In the next lemma, we prove that there exists a choice of
k
for which the values of
\chi(k-l)
can be prescribed for all
|l|\leq K_{0}
, and for which
F_{\chi,k}(\alpha,\beta)
is well approximated by
{\widetilde{F}}_{\chi,k}(\alpha,\beta)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\chi
be a non-principal Dirichlet character mod
p
of order
d
and consider a collection
\left(\xi_{l}\right)_{\left|l\right|\leq K_{0}}
of elements of
\mu_{d}
. Let

\mathcal{S}\vcentcolon=\bigcap_{\left|l\right|\leq K_{0}}\bigcup_{|a|\leq\frac{d}{20}}\left\{0\leq k\leq p-1:\chi\left(k-l\right)=\xi_{l}e\left(\frac{a}{d}\right)\right\}.

Then, there exists
k\in\mathcal{S}
such that

(2.1)

F_{\chi,k}(\alpha,\beta)={\widetilde{F}}_{\chi,k}(\alpha,\beta)+O(\sqrt{p}).

Proof.

Define

W(k)\vcentcolon=\prod_{\left|l\right|\leq K_{0}}\sum_{\left|a\right|\leq\frac{d}{20}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\left(\overline{\chi}\left(k-l\right)\xi_{l}e\left(\frac{a}{d}\right)\right)^{j}.

Since the union in the definition of
\mathcal{S}
is disjoint, by orthogonality in
\mu_{d}
, we have

(2.2)

W(k)=d^{2K_{0}+1}\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{S}}(k)+O\left(d^{2K_{0}+1}\mathbb{1}_{[0,K_{0}]\cup[p-K_{0},p-1]}(k)\right).

Next, expand the product that defines
W(k)
to obtain

W(k)=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\overline{\chi}\left(P(k)\right)\prod_{\left|l\right|\leq K_{0}}{\xi_{l}^{j_{l,P}}\sum_{\left|a\right|\leq\frac{d}{20}}e\left(\frac{aj_{l,P}}{d}\right)},

where
\mathcal{P}
is the set of all polynomials of the form
P(x)=\prod_{|l|\leq K_{0}}(x-l)^{j_{l,P}}
with
0\leq j_{l,P}\leq d-1
for all
|l|\leq K_{0}
. It follows from Weil’s bound (Proposition
1.3
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}W(k)

\displaystyle=p\left(2\left\lfloor\frac{d}{20}\right\rfloor+1\right)^{2K_{0}+1}+O\left(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}\backslash\{1\}}{\left|\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}{\overline{\chi}\left(P(k)\right)}\right|\prod_{\left|l\right|\leq K_{0}}\left|\sum_{\left|a\right|\leq\frac{d}{20}}e\left(\frac{aj_{l,P}}{d}\right)\right|}\right)

\displaystyle=p\left(2\left\lfloor\frac{d}{20}\right\rfloor+1\right)^{2K_{0}+1}+O\left(K_{0}\sqrt{p}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}\backslash\{1\}}\prod_{\left|l\right|\leq K_{0}}\left|\sum_{\left|a\right|\leq\frac{d}{20}}e\left(\frac{aj_{l,P}}{d}\right)\right|\right).

By the inequality
\sum_{|a|\leq x}e(a\theta)\ll\frac{1}{\|\theta\|}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}\backslash\{1\}}\prod_{\left|l\right|\leq K_{0}}\left|\sum_{\left|a\right|\leq\frac{d}{20}}e\left(\frac{aj_{l,P}}{d}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j_{l})_{|l|\leq K_{0}}\\
\forall l,0\leq j_{l}\leq d-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{\left|l\right|\leq K_{0}}\left|\sum_{\left|a\right|\leq\frac{d}{20}}e\left(\frac{aj_{l}}{d}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\prod_{\left|l\right|\leq K_{0}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\left|\sum_{\left|a\right|\leq\frac{d}{20}}e\left(\frac{aj}{d}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\ll\left(C_{1}\left(d+\sum_{1\leq j\leq\frac{d}{2}}\frac{d}{j}+\sum_{\frac{d}{2}<j\leq d-1}\frac{d}{d-j}\right)\right)^{2K_{0}+1}

\displaystyle\ll\left(C_{2}d\log{d}\right)^{2K_{0}+1}

for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}W(k)

\displaystyle=p\left(2\left\lfloor\frac{d}{20}\right\rfloor+1\right)^{2K_{0}+1}+O\left(K_{0}\sqrt{p}\left(C_{2}d\log{d}\right)^{2K_{0}+1}\right)

(2.3)

\displaystyle\asymp p\left(2\left\lfloor\frac{d}{20}\right\rfloor+1\right)^{2K_{0}+1},

since
d\leq p
and by our choice of
K_{0}
. In particular, it follows from (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{2K_{0}+1}}\sum_{K_{0}<k<p-K_{0}}W(k)=\frac{1}{d^{2K_{0}+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}W(k)+O(K_{0})

(2.4)

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{p}{d^{2K_{0}+1}}\left(2\left\lfloor\frac{d}{20}\right\rfloor+1\right)^{2K_{0}+1}.

Now, we shall prove that

(2.5)

\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{S}\right|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{S}}\left|F_{\chi,k}(\alpha,\beta)-{\widetilde{F}}_{\chi,k}(\alpha,\beta)\right|\ll\sqrt{p}.

Let
K>K_{0}
be an integer, by the triangle inequality, we have for all integers
k\in[0,p-1]
,

\begin{multlined}\left|F_{\chi,k}(\alpha,\beta)-{\widetilde{F}}_{\chi,k}(\alpha,\beta)\right|\leq\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2\pi}\left|\sum_{K_{0}<\left|l\right|\leq K}{\frac{e\left(\beta\left(l+t\right)\right)-e\left(\alpha\left(l+t\right)\right)}{l+t}\overline{\chi}\left(k-l\right)}\right|\\
+\left|F_{\chi,k}(\alpha,\beta)-\frac{\tau(\chi)e(\alpha k)}{2\pi i}\sum_{\left|l\right|\leq K}{\frac{e\left(\beta\left(l+t\right)\right)-e\left(\alpha\left(l+t\right)\right)}{l+t}\overline{\chi}\left(k-l\right)}\right|.\end{multlined}

Take
K=p-K_{0}-1
. Then, the second sum is
\ll\log{p}
by Proposition
1.2
and the first sum is

\ll\sqrt{p}\left(\left|\sum_{K_{0}<\left|l\right|<p-K_{0}}{\frac{e(\beta l)}{l+t}\overline{\chi}\left(k-l\right)}\right|+\left|\sum_{K_{0}<\left|l\right|<p-K_{0}}{\frac{e(\alpha l)}{l+t}\overline{\chi}\left(k-l\right)}\right|\right),

hence it suffices to prove that for
\epsilon\in\left\{-1,+1\right\}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(2.6)

\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{S}\right|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{S}}\left|\sum_{K_{0}<\epsilon l<p-K_{0}}{\frac{e(lx)}{l}\overline{\chi}(k-l)}\right|\ll 1.

Expressing the indicator function of
\mathcal{S}
as in (
2.2
) and using (
2
), we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{S}\right|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{S}}\left|\sum_{K_{0}<\epsilon l<p-K_{0}}{\frac{e(lx)}{l}\overline{\chi}(k-l)}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{K_{0}<\epsilon l<p-K_{0}}\frac{1}{l^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{p\left(2\left\lfloor\frac{d}{20}\right\rfloor+1\right)^{2K_{0}+1}}\sum_{K_{0}<\epsilon l_{1}\neq\epsilon l_{2}<p-K_{0}}{\frac{1}{l_{1}l_{2}}\left|\sum_{K_{0}<k<p-K_{0}}W(k)\overline{\chi}(k-l_{1})\chi(k-l_{2})\right|}.

Similarly to (
2
), when
l_{1}\neq l_{2}
with
K_{0}<\left|l_{1}\right|,\left|l_{2}\right|<p-K_{0}
, we have

(2.8)

\begin{multlined}\left|\sum_{K_{0}<k<p-K_{0}}W(k)\overline{\chi}(k-l_{1})\chi(k-l_{2})\right|\\
=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}W(k)\overline{\chi}(k-l_{1})\overline{\chi}^{d-1}(k-l_{2})\right|+O\left(K_{0}d^{2K_{0}+1}\right)\ll K_{0}\sqrt{p}\left(Cd\log{d}\right)^{2K_{0}+1}\end{multlined}

for some constant
C>0
. Indeed, one may replace the set
\mathcal{P}
by the set
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of all polynomials of the form
P(x)=(x-l_{1})(x-l_{2})^{d-1}\prod_{|l|\leq K_{0}}(x-l)^{j_{l,P}}
with
0\leq j_{l,P}\leq d-1
for all
|l|\leq K_{0}
. By assumption on
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
, elements of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\backslash\{1\}
are not
d
-th powers in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
, hence Weil’s bound may be applied. Inserting (
2.8
) into (
2.7
) and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain (
2.6
), and consequently (
2.5
). It follows that there exists
k\in\mathcal{S}
that satisfies (
2.1
).
∎

We now state another technical lemma before proving Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Fix
u_{1},u_{2},c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
with
c_{1},c_{2}\notin\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. Then, for all
K\geq 2
, we have

\sum_{1\leq l\leq K}\frac{\left|\sin{\left(\pi\left(u_{1}l+c_{1}\right)\right)}\sin{\left(\pi\left(u_{2}l+c_{2}\right)\right)}\right|}{l}\gg\log{K},

where the implicit constant only depends on the fractional parts of
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
0\leq u_{1},u_{2},c_{1},c_{2}<1
. Let
x\geq 1
be large enough depending on
u_{1}
and
c_{1}
. Then, one can observe that the number of integers
l\in[x,2x]
such that
\|u_{1}l+c_{1}\|\geq\frac{\|2c_{1}\|}{20}
is
\geq 0.6x
, since the sequence
(u_{1}l+c_{1})_{l}
is equidistributed on the closure of its image mod
1
. Since a similar statement holds with
u_{2}l+c_{2}
, we deduce that for
x
large enough depending on
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
, we have
\left|\sin{\left(\pi\left(u_{1}l+c_{1}\right)\right)}\right|\gg_{c_{1}}1
and
\left|\sin{\left(\pi\left(u_{2}l+c_{2}\right)\right)}\right|\gg_{c_{2}}1
for
\geq 0.2x
values of
l\in\left[x,2x\right]
, and the lemma follows by dyadic decomposition.
∎

Proof of the lower bound in Theorem
1.1
.

It suffices to prove that there exists an integer
k\in[0,p-1]
such that (
2.1
) holds and

\left|{\widetilde{F}}_{\chi,k}(\alpha,\beta)\right|\gg_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt{p}\log{K_{0}},

for some choice of
t\in[0.1,0.9]
depending at most on
\alpha
and
\beta
.

First, assume that
d\geq 3
. Then, for all
\left|l\right|\leq K_{0}
, choose
\xi_{l}\in\mu_{d}
such that

-\frac{\pi}{d}\leq\arg{\left(\frac{\left(e\left(\beta\left(l+t\right)\right)-e\left(\alpha\left(l+t\right)\right)\right)\overline{\xi_{l}}}{l+t}\right)}\leq\frac{\pi}{d},

with the convention that
\arg{0}=0
. Then, let
k
be given by Lemma
2.1
, since
\left|\tau(\chi)\right|=\sqrt{p}
