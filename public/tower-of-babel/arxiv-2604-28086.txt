Nonlinear evolution equations with a non-Lipschitz perturbation: convergence of successive approximations and uniqueness of solutions
G. Díaz & J.I. Díaz

Abstract
This paper investigates the existence and uniqueness of solutions for a nonlinear evolution equation governed by an m-accretive operator 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} in a Banach space, presenting a perturbation term F ⁡ ( t , ⋅ ) {\rm F}(t,\cdot) that does not satisfy the Lipschitz condition. Motivated by nonlinear diffusion models in climatology, we first establish the validity of the Variation of Constants Formula in this nonlinear framework, thereby reformulating the problem as a fixed-point problem for an integral operator. Under structural, boundedness, and unique continuation conditions on an associated scalar integral equation, we prove the convergence of the method of successive approximations towards a unique mild solution. This constructive approach extends previous uniqueness results for semilinear partial differential equations with non-Lipschitz perturbations to the more general setting of nonlinear operators in Banach spaces, which need not be reflexive.

2
Extension of the Constant Variation Formula to nonlinear operators on Banach spaces

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be a Banach space.
Let

{\mathcal{A}}:{\rm D}({\mathcal{A}})\subset{\mathcal{X}}\to 2^{\mathcal{X}}

be an
m
-accretive operator,
i.e.
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\|(x-\widehat{x})+\lambda(y-\widehat{y})\|\geq\|x-\widehat{x}\|,\quad(x,y),~(\widehat{x},\widehat{y})\in{\mathcal{A}}),~\lambda\geq 0\\[5.69046pt]
\hbox{Rank }({\rm I}+{\mathcal{A}})={\mathbb{X}},\end{array}\right.

hold.
We assume throughout that

\overline{{\rm D}({\mathcal{A}})}={\mathcal{X}}.

Let
f\in L^{1}(0,T;X)
. We consider the evolution problem

\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)+{\mathcal{A}}u(t)\ni f(t),\\
u(0)=u_{0}.\end{cases}

For
\lambda>0
define the resolvent

{\rm J}_{\lambda}=({\rm I}+\lambda{\mathcal{A}})^{-1}.

Since
{\mathcal{A}}
is
m
-accretive,

{\rm J}_{\lambda}:{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow{\rm D}({\mathcal{A}})

is well defined and nonexpansive:

\|{\rm J}_{\lambda}x-{\rm J}_{\lambda}y\|\leq\|x-y\|.

The nonlinear semigroup generated by
{\mathcal{A}}
is defined by

{\rm S}(t)x=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left({\rm I}+\frac{t}{n}{\mathcal{A}}\right)^{-n}x.

The Crandall–Liggett Theorem (
[
14
]
) guarantees that the limit exists for every
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
and defines a contraction semigroup. Moreover, for the
non-homogeneous problem we can use the implicit Euler scheme to solve the
problem. Let

\lambda=\frac{T}{n},~t_{k}=k\lambda.

From
u_{0}
, we consider the approximation

u_{k+1}={\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{k}+\lambda f(t_{k}))

Define the piecewise constant interpolation

u_{\lambda}(t)=u_{k},\quad t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}).

By the Crandall–Liggett theorem we know the convergence of
u_{\lambda}(t)

to the unique solution
u(t)
of the problem. Our goal is to extend the
representation formula

u(t)={\rm S}(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}{\rm S}(t-s)f(s)\,ds

which is well-known for linear operators, to the case of nonlinear operators

{\mathcal{A}}
, where now
{\rm S}(t)
is the nonlinear semigroup generated by
{\mathcal{A}}
.

Theorem 2

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an
m
-accretive operator on a Banach space
{\mathcal{X}}
with dense domain. Let
f\in{\rm L}^{1}(0,T;{\mathcal{X}})
. Then the associated implicit Euler scheme converges to a limit function
u(t)
which satisfies

u(t)={\rm S}(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}{\rm S}(t-s)f(s)\,ds.

The proof will be a consequence of several general properties. We start with
the comparison with the homogeneous scheme. Let

v_{k+1}={\rm J}_{\lambda}v_{k},\qquad v_{0}=u_{0}.

Then

v_{k}={\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k}u_{0}.

Recall that by the Crandall–Liggett Theorem (
[
14
]
),
v_{k}\rightarrow{\rm S}(t_{k})u_{0}.

Lemma 1

The following estimate holds

\|u_{k+1}-v_{k+1}\|\leq\|u_{k}-v_{k}\|+\lambda\|f(t_{k})\|.

Proof.
Using the nonexpansiveness of
{\rm J}_{\lambda}
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|u_{k+1}-v_{k+1}\|&\hskip-5.69046pt=\|{\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{k}+\lambda f(t_{k}))-{\rm J}_{\lambda}v_{k}\|\\[2.84544pt]
&\hskip-5.69046pt\leq\|u_{k}+\lambda f(t_{k})-v_{k}\|\\[2.84544pt]
&\hskip-5.69046pt\leq\|u_{k}-v_{k}\|+\lambda\|f(t_{k})\|.\end{array}

\quad{}_{\Box}

Iterating this inequality gives

\|u_{k}\|-\|v_{k}\|\leq\|u_{k}-v_{k}\|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lambda\|f(x_{i})\|.

It will be useful to prove the boundedness of
u_{k}.

Lemma 2

The sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k}
is bounded.

Proof.
Using the previous estimate,

\|u_{k}\|\leq\|v_{k}\|+\sum_{{\rm J}=0}^{k-1}\lambda\|f(t_{\rm J})\|.

Since
{\rm S}(t)
is a contraction,

\|v_{k}\|\leq\|u_{0}\|.

Thus

\|u_{k}\|\leq\|u_{0}\|+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(s)\|ds.

\quad{}_{\Box}

Let us prove now a consequence of the a priori bounds of the implicit Euler scheme and the nonexpansiveness of the resolvent: a property that is consistent with the classical proof of the Crandall–Liggett Theorem (see (
[
14
]
)).

Lemma 3

There exists a constant
{\rm C}>0
, independent of
\lambda
, such that the following estimate holds

\|u-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u\|\leq\lambda{\rm C},

(10)

Proof.
From the definition of the resolvent we have

u-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u\in\lambda{\mathcal{A}}({\rm J}_{\lambda}u).

Thus, there exists
w_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{A}}({\rm J}_{\lambda}u)
such that

u-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u=\lambda w_{\lambda}\quad\text{and hence}\quad\|u-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u\|=\lambda\|w_{\lambda}\|.

Therefore, it suffices to obtain a bound on
\|w_{\lambda}\|
independent of
\lambda
. To this end, we use the implicit Euler scheme

u_{k+1}={\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{k}+\lambda f(t_{k})).

From Lemma
2
, the sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k}
is bounded:

\|u_{k}\|\leq{\rm M}\doteq\|u_{0}\|+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(s)\|\,ds.

Since
{\rm J}_{\lambda}
is nonexpansive, it follows that

\|{\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{k}+\lambda f(t_{k}))\|\leq\|u_{k}+\lambda f(t_{k})\|\leq{\rm M}+\lambda\|f(t_{k})\|,

so the sequence
\{u_{k+1}\}_{k}
also remains in a bounded subset of
{\mathcal{X}}
, uniformly with respect to
\lambda
. Moreover, from the scheme we have

u_{k}+\lambda f(t_{k})-u_{k+1}\in\lambda{\mathcal{A}}(u_{k+1}),

so there exists
z_{k}\in{\mathcal{A}}(u_{k+1})
such that

u_{k}+\lambda f(t_{k})-u_{k+1}=\lambda z_{k}.

Hence,

\|z_{k}\|\leq\frac{\|u_{k}-u_{k+1}\|}{\lambda}+\|f(t_{k})\|.

Using again, the contractivity of
{\rm J}_{\lambda}
,

\|u_{k+1}-u_{k}\|=\|{\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{k}+\lambda f(t_{k}))-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u_{k}\|\leq\lambda\|f(t_{k})\|,

and therefore

\|z_{k}\|\leq 2\|f(t_{k})\|.

This shows that the elements of
{\mathcal{A}}(u_{k+1})
selected by the scheme are uniformly bounded in terms of
\|f\|_{L^{1}}
, independently on
\lambda
. Since
{\rm J}_{\lambda}u
belongs to the same bounded region (by nonexpansiveness and the boundedness of the data), we conclude that there exists a constant
{\rm C}>0
, independent of
\lambda
, such that

\|w_{\lambda}\|\leq{\rm C}.

This proves (
10
).
\quad{}_{\Box}

Now, let us prove a discrete and nonlinear version of the so-called
Variation of Constants Formula (in the framework of linear operators)

Proposition 1

We have

u_{k}={\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k}u_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}{\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k-i-1}\lambda f(t_{i})+r_{k}

with
\|r_{k}\|\rightarrow 0.

Proof.
Let

u_{k+1}={\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{k}+\lambda f(t_{k})),\qquad u_{0}\in X.

Let the homogeneous scheme be

v_{k+1}={\rm J}_{\lambda}v_{k},\qquad v_{0}=u_{0}.

Then
v_{k}={\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k}u_{0}.
Define

w_{k}=u_{k}-v_{k}.

Using the recursion we have

w_{k+1}={\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{k}+\lambda f(t_{k}))-{\rm J}_{\lambda}v_{k}.

Introduce

z_{k,{\rm J}}\doteq{\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k-i-1}\left(u_{i+1}-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u_{i}\right)

and observe that

u_{k}-v_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}{\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k-i-1}\left(u_{i+1}-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u_{i}\right).

Indeed this follows from the telescoping identity

u_{k}={\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k}u_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}{\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k-i-1}\big(u_{i+1}-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u_{i}\big).

From the scheme

u_{{\rm J}+1}={\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{i}+\lambda f(t_{i}))

we define the local error

e_{i}=u_{i+1}-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u_{i}.

Thus

u_{k}={\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k}u_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}{\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k-i-1}e_{i}.

The key point here is that

e_{i}={\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{i}+\lambda f(t_{i}))-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u_{i}.

Since
{\rm J}_{\lambda}
is nonexpansive,

\|e_{i}\|\leq\lambda\|f(t_{i})\|.

Denote
g_{i}\doteq\lambda f(t_{i}),

and consider the approximation

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}{\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k-i-1}g_{i}.

We compare this with the true discrete contribution

\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}{\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k-i-1}e_{i}.

Define the difference

r_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}{\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k-i-1}(e_{i}-g_{i}).

This is the nonlinear error term. Using the nonexpansiveness of
{\rm J}_{\lambda}
we obtain

\|r_{k}\|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\|e_{i}-g_{i}\|.

Now

e_{i}-g_{i}={\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{i}+\lambda f(t_{i}))-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u_{i}-\lambda f(t_{i}).

Adding and subtracting
{\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{i})+\lambda f(t_{i})
we get

e_{i}-g_{i}=\big({\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{i}+\lambda f(t_{i}))-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u_{i}\big)-\lambda f(t_{i}).

Using the Lipschitz property of
{\rm J}_{\lambda}

\|{\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{i}+\lambda f)-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u_{i}\|\leq\lambda\|f\|.

Therefore

\|e_{i}-g_{i}\|\leq\|{\rm J}_{\lambda}(u_{i}+\lambda f(t_{i}))-(u_{i}+\lambda f(t_{i}))\|+\|{\rm J}_{\lambda}u_{i}-u_{i}\|.

By the resolvent identity, we know that

u-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u\in\lambda{\mathcal{A}}({\rm J}_{\lambda}u).

By Lemma
3
, one knows that

\|u-{\rm J}_{\lambda}u\|\leq\lambda{\rm C},

for some constant
{\rm C}>0
independent of
\lambda
. Since the sequence
u_{i}
is bounded, we obtain

\|e_{i}-g_{i}\|\leq{\rm C}\lambda^{2}.

Thus

\|r_{k}\|\leq{\rm C}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lambda^{2}={\rm C}k\lambda^{2}.

But

k\lambda=t_{k}\leq T
,
hence

\|r_{k}\|\leq{\rm C}T\lambda,

and therefore

r_{k}\rightarrow 0~(\lambda\rightarrow 0).\hfill\mbox{$\quad{}_{\Box}$}

Proof of the Theorem
2
)
. We proceed in two steps.
Step 1: The case

f\in{\mathcal{C}}([0,{\rm T}];{\mathcal{X}})
. Let
t_{k}\rightarrow t.
By the Crandall–Liggett Theorem (
[
14
]
),
{\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k}u_{0}\rightarrow{\rm S}(t)u_{0}.
Moreover,

{\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k-i-1}x\rightarrow{\rm S}(t-t_{i})x.

Therefore

\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lambda{\rm J}_{\lambda}^{k-i-1}f(t_{i})\rightarrow\int_{0}^{t}{\rm S}(t-s)f(s)\,ds.

This is a Riemann sum for the Bochner integral. Combining the convergence of
the homogeneous part and the Riemann convergence of the forcing term gives
the desired result.

Step 2: Extension to

f\in{\rm L}^{1}(0,{\rm T};{\mathcal{X}}).

Choose a sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n}\subset{\mathcal{C}}([0,{\rm T}];{\mathcal{X}})
such that
f_{n}\rightarrow f
in
{\rm L}^{1}
. For each
n
, let
u_{k}^{(n)}
be the
scheme with forcing
f_{n}
, and let
u^{(n)}(t)
be the limit from Step 1

u^{(n)}(t)={\rm S}(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}{\rm S}(t-s)f_{n}(s)\,ds.

By the discrete Duhamel estimate,

\|u_{k}-u_{k}^{(n)}\|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lambda\|f(t_{i})-f_{n}(t_{i})\|.

As
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, the right-hand side converges to
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)-f_{n}(s)\|\,ds
. Hence,

\limsup_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\|u_{k}-u^{(n)}(t)\|\leq\limsup_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\big\|u_{k}-u_{k}^{(n)}\big\|+\limsup_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\big\|u_{k}^{(n)}-u^{(n)}(t)\big\|\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)-f_{n}(s)\|\,ds.

Taking
n\rightarrow\infty
, the right-hand side tends to
0
, so

u_{k}\rightarrow u(t)\doteq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}u^{(n)}(t).

Finally, we identify
u(t)
. By the continuous dependence estimate for mild
solutions,

\left\|u^{(n)}(t)-\left({\rm S}(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}{\rm S}(t-s)f(s)\,ds\right)\right\|\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|f_{n}(s)-f(s)\|\,ds\rightarrow 0.

Thus,

u(t)={\rm S}(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}{\rm S}(t-s)f(s)\,ds.

\quad{}_{\Box}
