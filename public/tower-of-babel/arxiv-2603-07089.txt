The adiabatic nuclear SE [Eq. (

\psi^{\rm{ad}}=A\psi^{\rm{dia}}

\displaystyle\left(-\dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla_{R}^{2}+W-E\right)\psi^{\rm{dia}}=0,

(7)

where

\displaystyle W=A^{\dagger}UA.

In order to obtain such adiabatic-to-diabatic representation of SE [Eq. (

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{R}A+\boldsymbol{\tau}A=0.

III.A.1. SA-MCSCF

For the first two variants of CAS [(12e,9o) and (18e,12o)] with AVDZ basis set, 3S-SA-MCSCF to 7S-SA-MCSCF calculations depict that the higher CAS(18e,12o) could able to provide not only improved molecular parameters at different geometries but also quite smooth adiabatic PESs both at the interaction and asymptotic regions. The additional improvement obtained by using a larger active space CAS(24e,15o) with the same basis set (AVDZ) and upto 7S-SA-MCSCF calculation is negligibly “small” while being computationally more demanding. On the process of averaging over increasing number of states, it appears that both 7S-SA-MCSCF and 13S-SA-MCSCF calculations with CAS(18e,12o) using AVDZ basis set provide smooth adiabatic PESs, but the energetics at specific geometries are not improved significantly. \rightarrow

CONCLUSION

The “accuracy” of the molecular parameters as well as adiabatic PESs is one of the most important aspect for constructing the diabatic Hamiltonian. On the other hand, the location of CIs as well as the “accuracy” of associated NACTs are equally important, if not more, to determine the lifetime of the species from the excited electronic states. Moreover, the kinetic process for this chemical reaction will depend on the “accurate” adiabatic PESs as well as NACTs for different/between electronic states.

While exploring the details on the process of constructing diabatic Hamiltonian, it is important to follow the mechanism of the chemical reaction from the BBO perspective of structural (electronic) aspects of O
3
: (a) location and nature of symmetry driven and accidental Jahn-Teller CIs to understand radiationless energy transfer; (b) the “accuracy” of non-adiabatic coupling elements through quantization of NACTs and their topological implications on the reaction attributes; (c) existence of SHS by examining the validaty of Curl Condition for low energy collision process, which varies from one reactive process to another; (d) construction of multi-state diabatic Hamiltonian matrix, which is, at present, four (N = 4) for O
3
case. Once such diabatic Hamiltonians are constructed involving even with singularities of NACTs, reaction attributes will be calculated and compared with experimental data as well as other theoretical results.

In this work, we present lowest four singlet adiabatic PESs (
\tilde{X}^{1}A^{\prime}
,

and

Convergence of geometrical parameters, energetics and locations of minimum energy separation are also described with increasing basis set size. The NACTs are calculated employing 7S-SA-MCSCF method followed by CP-MCSCF approach along carefully chosen circular contours. A symmetry-driven CI involving the 2-3 electronic states at the

However, this extended state-averaging does not yield any improvement in the accuracy of the adiabatic PES compared to the singlet-only approach.

In our future study, we aim to explicitly incorporate SO interactions, which are crucial for capturing intersystem crossing effects and achieving a more complete representation of the nonadiabatic dynamics of ozone. Overall, the methodologies and insights presented in this study lay a basic foundation for the construction of global diabatic PES matrix for the application of BBO based scattering dynamics in ozone.

Diabatic PES matrices constructed along different paths of integration

The 2D rectangular paths along which the ADT equations are solved.

Let us choose two different 2D contour integration of ADT equations along
\theta
and
\phi
for a fixed
\rho
as shown in Figures
S1
(a) and
S1
(b) and such ADT matrices are termed as
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, where those matrices are orthonormal by construction at any point in configuration space:

\displaystyle\mathbf{A_{1}{{}^{\dagger}}}(\theta,\phi)\,\mathbf{A_{1}}(\theta,\phi)=\mathbf{I}

(S6a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A_{2}{{}^{\dagger}}}(\theta,\phi)\,\mathbf{A_{2}}(\theta,\phi)=\mathbf{I}

(S6b)

Furthermore, we consider the following product matrix:

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}(\theta,\phi)=\mathbf{A_{1}{{}^{\dagger}}}(\theta,\phi)\mathbf{A_{2}}(\theta,\phi),

(S7)

which also appears as an orthogonal matrix:

\displaystyle\mathbf{B{{}^{\dagger}}}\,\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{A_{1}{{}^{\dagger}}}\,\mathbf{A_{2}}\right)^{\dagger}\,\left(\mathbf{A_{1}{{}^{\dagger}}}\,\mathbf{A_{2}}\right)

(S8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{A_{2}{{}^{\dagger}}}\left(\mathbf{A_{1}}\,\mathbf{A_{1}{{}^{\dagger}}}\right)\,\mathbf{A_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}

From Equation
S7
, one can get the following relation:

\displaystyle\mathbf{A_{1}}\,\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{A_{2}},

(S9)

which indicates that the ADT matrices belonging to two different paths are related through another orthogonal matrix (
B
).

On the other hand, the diabatic PESs matrices along two different paths of integration takes the following form:

\displaystyle\mathbf{W_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{A_{1}{{}^{\dagger}}}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{A_{1}}

(S10a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{W_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{A_{2}{{}^{\dagger}}}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{A_{2}},\qquad\text{$\mathbf{U}$ is adiabatic PESs matrix (diagonal)}

(S10b)

which leads to

\displaystyle\mathbf{A_{1}}\,\mathbf{W_{1}}\,\mathbf{A_{1}{{}^{\dagger}}}=\mathbf{U}

(S11a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A_{2}}\,\mathbf{W_{2}}\,\mathbf{A_{2}{{}^{\dagger}}}=\mathbf{U}

(S11b)

Comparing Equation
S11
(a) and
S11
(b), we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbf{A_{1}}\,\mathbf{W_{1}}\,\mathbf{A_{1}{{}^{\dagger}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{A_{2}}\,\mathbf{W_{2}}\,\mathbf{A_{2}{{}^{\dagger}}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{A_{1}{{}^{\dagger}}}\,\mathbf{A_{1}}\,\mathbf{W_{1}}\,\mathbf{A_{1}{{}^{\dagger}}}\,\mathbf{A_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{A_{1}{{}^{\dagger}}}\,\mathbf{A_{2}}\,\mathbf{W_{2}}\,\mathbf{A_{2}{{}^{\dagger}}}\,\mathbf{A_{1}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{W_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{W_{2}}\,\mathbf{B}^{\dagger}

(S12)

Therefore, the diabatic PESs matrices along two different paths are related through another orthogonal matrix (B) and thus, calculated observables should be path independent in theoretical consideration.
