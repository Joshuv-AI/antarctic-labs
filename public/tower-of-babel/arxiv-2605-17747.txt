On transposed Poisson conformal superalgebras
Hao Fang, Lamei Yuan

Abstract
. We introduce and study transposed Poisson conformal superalgebras, the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -graded conformal analogues of transposed Poisson algebras, as well as their noncommutative variants. We derive a family of identities forced by the transposed conformal super-Leibniz rule and prove that the tensor product over ℂ [ ∂ ] \mathbb{C}[\partial] of two such superalgebras again carries a natural transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra structure. Moreover, we display a close relationship between transposed Poisson conformal superalgebras and Hom-Lie conformal superalgebras, and give the compatibility conditions between a Poisson conformal superalgebra and a transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra. In addition, several constructions are obtained from modified Lie conformal brackets and from Novikov-Poisson, pre-Lie commutative, differential Novikov-Poisson, and pre-Lie Poisson conformal superalgebras. Finally, using the known classification of Lie conformal superalgebras of rank (1+1), we determine all compatible transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra structures on such superalgebras.

1.
Introduction

The notion of a Lie conformal superalgebra, introduced by Kac
[
16
]
, encodes the singular part of the operator product expansion of chiral fields in two-dimensional quantum field theory.
Closely connected to vertex algebras and formal distribution Lie superalgebras
[
5
,
18
]
, Lie conformal superalgebras also provide an effective framework for studying algebraic structures governed by locality
[
15
,
19
]
.
The structure theory and representation theory of finite Lie conformal superalgebras have been systematically developed in
[
4
,
3
,
11
,
10
]
, while the cohomology theory of conformal (super)algebras was investigated in
[
2
,
8
]
.
Significantly, the notion of a Poisson conformal superalgebra was introduced by Kolesnikov et al.
[
17
]
, which consists of a Lie conformal superalgebra and a commutative associative conformal superalgebra satisfying certain compatibility conditions.
This compatibility provides a natural point of departure for studying variants in which the relation between the associative conformal product and the Lie conformal bracket is modified.

The present paper is concerned with the conformal super version of transposed Poisson algebras.
Recall that a Poisson algebra consists of a commutative associative product and a Lie bracket related by the usual Leibniz rule.
Transposed Poisson algebras, introduced by Bai et al.
[
1
]
, arise by reversing the roles of the two bilinear operations in the Leibniz rule.
They are closely related to Novikov-Poisson algebras, pre-Lie Poisson algebras, and

\frac{1}{2}
-derivations of Lie algebras
[
1
,
12
]
.
The even conformal analogue of transposed Poisson algebras was considered in
[
21
]
.
It is therefore natural to ask for a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded conformal theory in which the transposed compatibility is governed
simultaneously by conformal sesquilinearity and parity.

In this paper, we introduce the notion of transposed Poisson conformal superalgebras.
Such an object is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module carrying a
commutative associative conformal superalgebra structure and a Lie
conformal superalgebra structure, subject to the transposed conformal
super-Leibniz rule: for homogeneous elements
a,b,c
,

2a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]=[(a\circ_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c]+(-1)^{|a||b|}[b_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)].

When the odd part is zero, this recovers the transposed Poisson conformal algebras of
[
21
]
.
If one drops the commutativity requirement on the associative conformal product, the resulting structure will be called a noncommutative
transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra.

Our first goal is to develop the basic structure theory of these
superalgebras. We derive several identities forced by the transposed
conformal super-Leibniz rule, including cyclic identities and mixed
identities involving conformal products and brackets.
These identities are useful not only as computational tools but also as structural constraints.
As an application, we prove that the tensor product over
\mathbb{C}[\partial]

of two transposed Poisson conformal superalgebras again carries a natural
transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra structure.
We also show that, for every even element
h
, the operator
h\circ_{(0)}

gives rise to a Hom-Lie conformal
superalgebra, and we give necessary and sufficient conditions for a fixed
commutative associative conformal superalgebra and a fixed Lie conformal
superalgebra to form both a Poisson conformal superalgebra and a
transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra.

The second part of the paper is devoted to constructions. Starting from a given
transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra and an even element, we construct
a new transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra structure by modifying the Lie conformal bracket through the 0-th
product with that element.
We then show that conformal super analogues of Novikov-Poisson, pre-Lie
commutative, differential Novikov-Poisson, and pre-Lie Poisson structures
give rise to transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra structures, extending the
corresponding constructions in the purely even conformal setting

[
21
]
. Furthermore, we also establish a tensor product construction for
pre-Lie Poisson conformal superalgebras.

Finally, we classify compatible transposed Poisson conformal
superalgebra structures on Lie conformal superalgebras of rank (1+1).
Using the classification of such Lie conformal superalgebras in

[
22
]
, we reduce the problem to the five types

R_{1},\ldots,R_{5}
. For each type, we determine all even conformal products
compatible with the given Lie conformal superalgebra structure.
The classification shows that the transposed conformal super-Leibniz rule
imposes strong restrictions on compatible products: except for the
abelian degenerations and a few explicitly determined nontrivial cases,
the compatible product is necessarily trivial.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the
preliminaries on conformal superalgebras, Lie conformal superalgebras,
Poisson conformal superalgebras, and related structures.
In Section
3
, we introduce transposed Poisson conformal superalgebras and establish their fundamental
identities, tensor product construction, relation with Hom-Lie conformal superalgebras, and
compatibility with Poisson conformal superalgebras.
Section
4
is devoted to constructions
from Novikov-Poisson, pre-Lie commutative, differential
Novikov-Poisson, and pre-Lie Poisson conformal superalgebras.
In Section
5
, we present a complete classification of compatible transposed Poisson conformal
superalgebra structures on Lie conformal superalgebras of rank (1+1).

Throughout this paper, we denote by
\mathbb{C}
the set of complex numbers,
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
the set of nonnegative integers. All vector spaces, linear maps, and tensor products are considered over the complex field
\mathbb{C}
.
For any vector space
A
, the space of polynomials of
\lambda
with coefficients in
A
is denoted by
A[\lambda]
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basic notions and results concerning Lie conformal superalgebras and Poisson conformal superalgebras.

Let

\mathbb{Z}_{2}

The elements of

The parity of a homogeneous element

\lvert 0\rvert=\bar{0}

Throughout what follows, if

A

\mathbb{Z}_{2}

,

A
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
A=A_{\bar{0}}\oplus A_{\bar{1}}
such that
\partial A_{i}\subseteq A_{i}

.
The tensor product of graded spaces is graded by
\lvert a\otimes b\rvert=\lvert a\rvert+\lvert b\rvert
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
8

A

(A,\circ_{\lambda})

\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module compatible with parity,

\lambda
-product, i.e., a linear map
A\otimes A\to\mathbb{C}[\lambda]\otimes A

\displaystyle(\partial a)\circ_{\lambda}b=-\lambda a\circ_{\lambda}b,\quad a\circ_{\lambda}(\partial b)=(\partial+\lambda)a\circ_{\lambda}b.

(2.1)

A conformal superalgebra
(A,\circ_{\lambda})
is called
commutative
, if it satisfies

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}b=(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}b\circ_{-\partial-\lambda}a,\quad\forall~a,b\in{A}.

(2.2)

Definition 2.2

(
[
10

An

\mathbb{C}
-bilinear map

is a conformal superalgebra and satisfies the associativity relations:

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}\left(b\circ_{\mu}c\right)=\left(a\circ_{\lambda}b\right)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c,\quad\forall~a,b,c\in{A}.

(2.3)

Remark 2.4

In every commutative associative conformal superalgebra

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}\left(b\circ_{\mu}c\right)=(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}b\circ_{\mu}\left(a\circ_{\lambda}c\right),\quad\forall~a,b,c\in{A}.

Definition 2.5

(
[

)

A
Lie conformal superalgebra

A=A_{\bar{0}}\oplus A_{\bar{1}}

-graded

3.
Transposed Poisson conformal superalgebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of a transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra, which is a conformal analogue of the transposed Poisson superalgebra. Then we present some identities and properties of transposed Poisson conformal superalgebras.

3.1.
Definition of transposed Poisson conformal superalgebras

Definition 3.1
.

A
transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra
(TPCSA) is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{\bar{1}}
endowed with two
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear maps

\circ_{\lambda},\,[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}]:\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}[\lambda],

such that
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda})
is a commutative associative conformal superalgebra,
(\mathcal{L},[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])
is a Lie conformal superalgebra and the following
transposed conformal super-Leibniz rule
holds:

\displaystyle 2(a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c])=[(a\circ_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c]+(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}[b_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)],\quad\forall~a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}.

(3.1)

When
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{1}}=\{0\}
, this definition reduces to that of a transposed Poisson conformal algebra.
If the associative
\lambda
-product
\circ_{\lambda}
in the transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra
\mathcal{L}
is noncommutative, we call it a
noncommutative TPCSA
.

Remark 3.2
.

The relation (
3.1
) can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle 2([a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c)=[a_{{\lambda}}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)]-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}[b_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)],\quad\forall~a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}.

(3.2)

Remark 3.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])

be a TPCSA and let
a
be a homogeneous element from
\mathcal{L}
.
Then the left multiplication
L(a)
in the associative commutative superalgebra
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda})
, defined by

L(a)_{\lambda}(b)=a\circ_{\lambda}b,\quad\forall~b\in\mathcal{L},

gives a conformal

\frac{1}{2}
-derivation of the Lie conformal superalgebra
(\mathcal{L},[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])
be a (noncommutative) TPCSA.
Define the
n
-th products on the (commutative) associative conformal superalgebra
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda})
and the Lie conformal superalgebra
(\mathcal{L},[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, respectively, by

a\circ_{\lambda}b=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\lambda^{(n)}(a_{(n)}b),\quad[a_{\lambda}b]=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\lambda^{(n)}(a_{[n]}b),

where
\lambda^{(n)}=\lambda^{n}/{n!}
and
a,b\in\mathcal{L}
. Then, the notions of (commutative) associative conformal superalgebra and Lie conformal superalgebra can be equivalently expressed in terms of their respective
n
-th products.
Moreover, the transposed conformal super-Leibniz rule of the (noncommutative) TPCSA can be rewritten in terms of
n
-th products of associative conformal superalgebras and Lie conformal superalgebras as follows:

2(a_{(n)}(b_{[m]}c))=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(a_{(j)}b)_{[n+m-j]}c+(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}b_{{[m]}}(a_{(n)}c),\quad\forall~a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}.

(3.3)

Example 3.4
.

Let
(A,\circ,[\cdot,\cdot])
be an ordinary transposed Poisson superalgebra. Then
\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{C}[\partial]\otimes A
equipped with

a\circ_{\lambda}b=a\circ b,\quad[{a_{\lambda}b}]=[a,b],\quad\forall~a,b\in A

Example 3.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{L}_{1},\circ_{1\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]_{1},\partial_{1})
and
(\mathcal{L}_{2},\circ_{2\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]_{2},\partial_{2})
be two TPCSAs,
where
\mathcal{L}_{1}=(\mathcal{L}_{1})_{\bar{0}}\oplus(\mathcal{L}_{1})_{\bar{1}}
,
\mathcal{L}_{2}=(\mathcal{L}_{2})_{\bar{0}}\oplus(\mathcal{L}_{2})_{\bar{1}}
.
Define a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading on
\mathcal{L}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{2}
by

(\mathcal{L}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{2})_{j}=(\mathcal{L}_{1})_{j}\oplus(\mathcal{L}_{2})_{j},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

For all
x_{1},y_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
,
x_{2},y_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{2},
define

\displaystyle(x_{1}+x_{2})\circ_{\lambda}(y_{1}+y_{2})

\displaystyle=x_{1}\circ_{1\lambda}y_{1}+x_{2}\circ_{2\lambda}y_{2},

\displaystyle[(x_{1}+x_{2})_{\lambda}(y_{1}+y_{2})]

Then
(\mathcal{L}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{2},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\partial=\partial_{1}\oplus\partial_{2})
is a TPCSA.

3.3.
Tensor products of transposed Poisson conformal superalgebras

Now we define the tensor product of TPCSAs as follows:

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{L}_{1},\circ^{1}_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1})
and
(\mathcal{L}_{2},\circ^{2}_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
be two TPCSAs.
Set

\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\partial]}\mathcal{L}_{2},\quad\lvert a\otimes b\rvert=\lvert a\rvert+\lvert b\rvert

and the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module structure on
\mathcal{L}
is given by

\displaystyle\partial(a\otimes b):=(\partial a)\otimes b=a\otimes(\partial b),\quad a\in\mathcal{L}_{1},\,b\in\mathcal{L}_{2}.

Define the
\lambda
-product
\circ_{\lambda}
and the
\lambda
-bracket
[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]
on
\mathcal{L}
respectively by

\displaystyle(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})\circ_{\lambda}(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})=(-1)^{\lvert a_{2}\rvert\lvert b_{1}\rvert}(a_{1}\circ^{1}_{\lambda}b_{1})\otimes(a_{2}\circ^{2}_{\lambda}b_{2}),

\displaystyle[(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})_{\lambda}(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})]=(-1)^{\lvert a_{2}\rvert\lvert b_{1}\rvert}\big([{a_{1}}_{\lambda}b_{1}]^{1}\otimes(a_{2}\circ^{2}_{\lambda}b_{2})+(a_{1}\circ^{1}_{\lambda}b_{1})\otimes[{a_{2}}_{\lambda}b_{2}]^{2}\big),

for all
a_{1},b_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
,
a_{2},b_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}
.
Then
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a TPCSA.

Proof.

Let
a_{1},b_{1},c_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
,
a_{2},b_{2},c_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}
. It is easy to verify that
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda})
is a commutative associative conformal superalgebra.
Then we show that
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a Lie conformal superalgebra.

(a)

Conformal sesquilinearity:

\displaystyle[\partial(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})_{\lambda}(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})]=[((\partial a_{1})\otimes a_{2})_{\lambda}(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\lvert a_{2}\rvert\lvert b_{1}\rvert}\big([{(\partial a_{1})}_{\lambda}b_{1}]^{1}\otimes(a_{2}\circ^{2}_{\lambda}b_{2})+((\partial a_{1})\circ^{1}_{\lambda}b_{1})\otimes[{a_{2}}_{\lambda}b_{2}]^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\lvert a_{2}\rvert\lvert b_{1}\rvert}\big(-\lambda([{a_{1}}_{\lambda}b_{1}]^{1}\otimes(a_{2}\circ^{2}_{\lambda}b_{2}))-\lambda((a_{1}\circ^{1}_{\lambda}b_{1})\otimes[{a_{2}}_{\lambda}b_{2}]^{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)^{\lvert a_{2}\rvert\lvert b_{1}\rvert}\lambda\big([{a_{1}}_{\lambda}b_{1}]^{1}\otimes(a_{2}\circ^{2}_{\lambda}b_{2})+(a_{1}\circ^{1}_{\lambda}b_{1})\otimes[{a_{2}}_{\lambda}b_{2}]^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda[(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})_{\lambda}(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})].

(b)

Skew-symmetry:

\displaystyle[(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})_{-\partial-\lambda}(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\lvert b_{2}\rvert\lvert a_{1}\rvert}\big([{b_{1}}_{-\partial-\lambda}a_{1}]^{1}\otimes(b_{2}\circ^{2}_{-\partial-\lambda}a_{2})+(b_{1}\circ^{1}_{-\partial-\lambda}a_{1})\otimes[{b_{2}}_{-\partial-\lambda}a_{2}]^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)^{\lvert a_{1}\rvert\lvert b_{1}\rvert+\lvert a_{2}\rvert\lvert b_{2}\rvert+\lvert b_{2}\rvert\lvert a_{1}\rvert}\big([{a_{1}}_{\lambda}b_{1}]^{1}\otimes(a_{2}\circ^{2}_{\lambda}b_{2})+(a_{1}\circ^{1}_{\lambda}b_{1})\otimes[{a_{2}}_{\lambda}b_{2}]^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)^{(\lvert a_{1}\rvert+\lvert a_{2}\rvert)(\lvert b_{1}\rvert+\lvert b_{2}\rvert)+\lvert a_{2}\rvert\lvert b_{1}\rvert}\big([{a_{1}}_{\lambda}b_{1}]^{1}\otimes(a_{2}\circ^{2}_{\lambda}b_{2})+(a_{1}\circ^{1}_{\lambda}b_{1})\otimes[{a_{2}}_{\lambda}b_{2}]^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)^{(\lvert a_{1}\rvert+\lvert a_{2}\rvert)(\lvert b_{1}\rvert+\lvert b_{2}\rvert)}[(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})_{\lambda}(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})].

(c)

\displaystyle-(a_{1}\circ^{1}_{\lambda}(b_{1}\circ^{1}_{\mu}c_{1}))\otimes[{a_{2}}_{\lambda}[{b_{2}}_{\mu}c_{2}]^{2}]^{2},

\displaystyle h_{0}(Q1)

3.4.
Relation with Hom-Lie conformal superalgebras

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
be a TPCSA. For any

, define a linear map

\alpha_{h}
:
\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}
by

\displaystyle\alpha_{h}(x):=(h\circ_{\lambda}x)|_{\lambda=0}=h\circ_{(0)}x,\quad\forall~x\in\mathcal{L}.

Then
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\alpha_{h})
is a Hom-Lie conformal superalgebra.

Proof.

Since
h
is even,
\alpha_{h}
is an even linear map. Moreover,
the axioms of conformal sesquilinearity and skew-symmetry are naturally satisfied since

(\mathcal{L},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a Lie conformal superalgebra. For all

,

\displaystyle\alpha_{h}(\partial x)=(h\circ_{\lambda}\partial x)|_{\lambda=0}=(\partial+\lambda)(h\circ_{\lambda}x)|_{\lambda=0}=\partial(h\circ_{(0)}x)=\partial(\alpha_{h}x).

Hence,
\alpha_{h}\partial=\partial\alpha_{h}
. By taking
\gamma:=\lambda,~\mu:=\lambda+\mu,~\lambda:=0
in Eq. (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle[[x_{\lambda}y]_{\lambda+\mu}(h\circ_{(0)}z)]+(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert\lvert y\rvert}[(h\circ_{(0)}y)_{\mu}[x_{\lambda}z]]=[(h\circ_{(0)}x)_{\lambda}[y_{\mu}z]].

which is exactly the Hom-Jacobi identity. Therefore,
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\alpha_{h})

∎

3.5.
Compatibility conditions between PCSAs and TPCSAs

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda})
be a commutative associative conformal superalgebra and
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
be a Lie conformal superalgebra.
Then
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is both a Poisson conformal superalgebra and a transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra if and only if

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]=[b_{\mu-\lambda}a]\circ_{\mu}c=[(a\circ_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c]=0,\quad\forall~a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}.

Proof.

The “if” part can be easily verified. For the “only if” part, let
a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}
. By Eq. (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle 2[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c=[a_{{\lambda}}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)]-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}[b_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)].

By Eq. (
2.20
), we obtain

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)]=[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c+(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}[a_{\lambda}c],

\displaystyle[b_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)]=[b_{\mu-\lambda}a]\circ_{\mu}c+(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}c].

Thus, we have

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c+a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}c]-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}[a_{\lambda}c]+(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}[b_{\mu-\lambda}a]\circ_{\mu}c=0.

By Eq. (
3.5
), we get

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c+a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}c]-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}[a_{\lambda}c]=0.

Hence,
[b_{\mu-\lambda}a]\circ_{\mu}c=0
. From this and Eq. (
3.4
), we get
a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]=(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}b\circ_{\mu}[a_{\lambda}c]

3.1

\displaystyle 2a\circ_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]=[(a\circ_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c]+(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}[b_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)].

By Eqs. (

4.
Constructions of transposed Poisson conformal superalgebras

In this section, we present several constructions of transposed Poisson conformal superalgebras (TPCSAs). We first show how to obtain a new TPCSA from a given one by introducing a suitable binary operation. We then introduce Novikov-Poisson conformal superalgebras, pre-Lie commutative conformal superalgebras, differential Novikov-Poisson conformal superalgebras, and pre-Lie Poisson conformal superalgebras, which are the super counterparts of the corresponding conformal algebras considered in
[
21
]
. Finally, we construct several classes of TPCSAs from these structures.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
be a TPCSA. For any
h\in\mathcal{L}_{\bar{0}}
, define a new binary operation
[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{h}
on
\mathcal{L}
by

\displaystyle[x_{\lambda}y]^{h}:=(h\circ_{\mu}[x_{\lambda}y])|_{\mu=0}=h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}y],\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathcal{L}.

Then
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{h})
is a TPCSA.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z\in\mathcal{L},h\in\mathcal{L}_{\bar{0}}
.
Since
h
is even, the operation
[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{h}
is even.
We first show that
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{h})
is a Lie conformal superalgebra.

(a)
Conformal sesquilinearity:

\displaystyle[(\partial x)_{\lambda}y]^{h}=h\circ_{(0)}[(\partial x)_{\lambda}y]=h\circ_{(0)}(-\lambda[x_{\lambda}y])=-\lambda(h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}y])=-\lambda[x_{\lambda}y]^{h}.

(b)
Skew-symmetry:

\displaystyle[x_{\lambda}y]^{h}

\displaystyle=h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}y]=h\circ_{(0)}(-(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert\lvert y\rvert}[y_{-\partial-\lambda}x])

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert\lvert y\rvert}h\circ_{(0)}[y_{-\partial-\lambda}x]=-(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert\lvert y\rvert}[y_{-\partial-\lambda}x]^{h}.

(c)
Jacobi identity:
By taking
\gamma:=\lambda,~\mu:=\lambda+\mu,~\lambda:=0
in Eq. (
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle[(h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}y])_{\lambda+\mu}z]+(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert\lvert y\rvert}[y_{\mu}(h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}z])]-[x_{\lambda}(h\circ_{(0)}[y_{\mu}z])]=0

Thus, we have

h\circ_{(0)}([(h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}y])_{\lambda+\mu}z]+(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert\lvert y\rvert}[y_{\mu}(h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}z])]-[x_{\lambda}(h\circ_{(0)}[y_{\mu}z])])=0.

That is,

\displaystyle[{[x_{\lambda}y]^{h}}_{\lambda+\mu}z]^{h}+(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert\lvert y\rvert}[y_{\mu}{[x_{\lambda}z]^{h}}]^{h}-[x_{\lambda}{[y_{\mu}z]^{h}}]^{h}=0.

Next we show that transposed conformal super-Leibniz rule holds. By Eq. (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle 2([x_{\lambda}y]^{h}\circ_{\mu}z)=2((h\circ_{(0)}[x_{\lambda}y])\circ_{\mu}z)=h\circ_{(0)}(2([x_{\lambda}y]\circ_{\mu}z))

\displaystyle=h\circ_{(0)}([x_{{\lambda}}(y\circ_{\mu-\lambda}z)]-(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert\lvert y\rvert}[y_{\mu-\lambda}(x\circ_{\lambda}z)])

\displaystyle=h\circ_{(0)}[x_{{\lambda}}(y\circ_{\mu-\lambda}z)]-(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert\lvert y\rvert}h\circ_{(0)}[y_{\mu-\lambda}(x\circ_{\lambda}z)]

\displaystyle=[x_{{\lambda}}(y\circ_{\mu-\lambda}z)]^{h}-(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert\lvert y\rvert}[y_{\mu-\lambda}(x\circ_{\lambda}z)]^{h}.

Therefore,
(\mathcal{L},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{h})
is a transposed Poisson conformal superalgebra.
∎

Definition 4.2
.

A
Novikov-Poisson conformal superalgebra

\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathcal{N}_{\bar{1}}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module endowed with two
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear maps
\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathcal{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}[\lambda]
, denoted by
a\otimes b\mapsto a\circ_{\lambda}b
and
a\otimes b\mapsto a\ast_{\lambda}b
, respectively,
such that
(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda})
is a commutative associative conformal superalgebra,
(\mathcal{N},\ast_{\lambda})
is a Novikov conformal superalgebra and the following equations hold for all
a,b,c\in\mathcal{N}
:

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}c=a\circ_{\lambda}(b\ast_{\mu}c),

(4.1)

\displaystyle(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c-a\ast_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}c)=(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}((b\ast_{\mu}a)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c-b\ast_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)).

(4.2)

Remark 4.3
.

In analogy with Remark
2.3
, the following properties always hold in a Novikov-Poisson conformal superalgebra
(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda},\ast_{\lambda})
:

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}(b\ast_{-\partial-\mu}c)

\displaystyle=(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\ast_{-\partial-\mu}c,

\displaystyle a\circ_{-\partial-\lambda}(b\ast_{\mu}c)

\displaystyle=(a\circ_{-\partial-\mu}b)\ast_{-\partial+\mu-\lambda}c,

\displaystyle a\circ_{-\partial-\lambda}(b\ast_{-\partial-\mu}c)

\displaystyle=(a\circ_{-\partial+\mu-\lambda}b)\ast_{-\partial-\mu}c.

Taking the commutator in a Novikov-Poisson conformal superalgebra, we obtain

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda},\ast_{\lambda})
be a Novikov-Poisson conformal superalgebra. Define

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}b]=a\ast_{\lambda}b-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}(b\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a),\quad\forall~a,b\in\mathcal{N}.

(4.3)

Then
(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a TPCSA.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.11
,
(\mathcal{N},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a Lie conformal superalgebra. For all
a,b,c\in\mathcal{N}
, we have

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c=(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\mu}c-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}(b\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a)\circ_{\mu}c,

\displaystyle[a_{{\lambda}}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)]=a\ast_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert(\lvert b\rvert+\lvert c\rvert)}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a,

\displaystyle[b_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)]=b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)-(-1)^{\lvert b\rvert(\lvert a\rvert+\lvert c\rvert)}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)\ast_{-\partial-\mu+\lambda}b.

By Remark
4.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\mu}c

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lvert c\rvert(\lvert a\rvert+\lvert b\rvert)}c\circ_{-\partial-\mu}(a\ast_{\lambda}b)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lvert c\rvert(\lvert a\rvert+\lvert b\rvert)}(c\circ_{-\partial-\lambda}a)\ast_{-\partial+\lambda-\mu}b=(-1)^{\lvert b\rvert\lvert c\rvert}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)\ast_{-\partial-\mu+\lambda}b,

\displaystyle(b\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a)\circ_{\mu}c

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lvert c\rvert(\lvert a\rvert+\lvert b\rvert)}c\circ_{-\partial-\mu}(b\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lvert c\rvert(\lvert a\rvert+\lvert b\rvert)}(c\circ_{-\partial+\lambda-\mu}b)\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a=(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert c\rvert}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle[a_{{\lambda}}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)]-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}[b_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)]=a\ast_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)+[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c.

in Eq. (

\displaystyle(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\mu}c-a\ast_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)=(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}((b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}a)\circ_{\mu}c-b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)).

Since
(b\ast_{-\partial-\lambda}a)\circ_{\mu}c=(b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}a)\circ_{\mu}c
, it follows further that

\displaystyle(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\mu}c-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}(b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}a)\circ_{\mu}c

\displaystyle=a\ast_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}b\ast_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)

\displaystyle=[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c.

\displaystyle[a_{{\lambda}}(b\circ_{\mu-\lambda}c)]-(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}[b_{\mu-\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)]=2[a_{\lambda}b]\circ_{\mu}c,

so relation (
3.2
) holds. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda})
be a commutative associative conformal superalgebra and
D
be an even derivation. Define a binary operation
\ast_{\lambda}
on
\mathcal{N}
by

\displaystyle a\ast_{\lambda}b=a\circ_{\lambda}D(b),\quad\forall~a,b\in\mathcal{N}.

(4.4)

Then the following conclusions hold:

(1)

(\mathcal{N},\ast_{\lambda})
is a Novikov conformal superalgebra,

(2)

(\mathcal{N},\circ_{\lambda},\ast_{\lambda})
is a Novikov-Poisson conformal superalgebra.

Proof.

(1) Let
a,b,c\in\mathcal{N}
. We first show that
(\mathcal{N},\ast_{\lambda})
is a conformal superalgebra.

\displaystyle(\partial a)\ast_{\lambda}b=(\partial a)\circ_{\lambda}D(b)=-\lambda(a\circ_{\lambda}D(b))=-\lambda(a\ast_{\lambda}b),

\displaystyle a\ast_{\lambda}(\partial b)=a\circ_{\lambda}D(\partial b)=a\circ_{\lambda}(\partial D(b))=(\partial+\lambda)(a\circ_{\lambda}D(b))=(\partial+\lambda)(a\ast_{\lambda}b).

Next we show that
(\mathcal{N},\ast_{\lambda})
is a left-symmetric conformal superalgebra. Since

\displaystyle(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}c-a\ast_{\lambda}(b\ast_{\mu}c)=(a\circ_{\lambda}D(b))\circ_{\lambda+\mu}D(c)-a\circ_{\lambda}D(b\circ_{\mu}D(c))

\displaystyle=a\circ_{\lambda}(D(b)\circ_{\mu}D(c))-a\circ_{\lambda}(D(b)\circ_{\mu}D(c))-a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}D^{2}(c))=-a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}D^{2}(c)),

we similarly have

\displaystyle(b\ast_{\mu}a)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}c-b\ast_{\mu}(a\ast_{\lambda}c)=-b\circ_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}D^{2}(c)).

By Remark
2.4
, we obtain
a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}D^{2}(c))=(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}b\circ_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}D^{2}(c)).
Thus, we get

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lvert b\rvert\lvert c\rvert}a\circ_{\lambda}(D(b)\circ_{\mu}D(c))=(-1)^{\lvert b\rvert\lvert c\rvert}(a\circ_{\lambda}D(b))\circ_{\lambda+\mu}D(c)=(-1)^{\lvert b\rvert\lvert c\rvert}(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}c.

Hence,

is a Novikov conformal superalgebra.

(2) Let

. Relation (

) holds, since

\displaystyle=a\circ_{\lambda}(D(b)\circ_{\mu}c)-a\circ_{\lambda}(D(b)\circ_{\mu}c)-a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}D(c))=-a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}D(c)),

By Remark

a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}D(c))=(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}b\circ_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}D(c))
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle(a\ast_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c-a\ast_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}c)=(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}((b\ast_{\mu}a)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c-b\ast_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)).

. Up to isomorphism, such a Lie conformal superalgebra is one of the following five types:

\displaystyle R_{1}:~[x_{\lambda}x]=0,\quad[x_{\lambda}y]=0,\quad[y_{\lambda}y]=p(\partial)x,\quad\forall~p(\partial)\in\mathbb{C}[\partial];

\displaystyle R_{2}:~[x_{\lambda}x]=0,\quad[y_{\lambda}y]=0,\quad[x_{\lambda}y]=q(\lambda)y,\quad\forall~q(\lambda)\in\mathbb{C}[\lambda];

\displaystyle R_{3}:~[x_{\lambda}x]=(\partial+2\lambda)x,\quad[x_{\lambda}y]=0,\quad[y_{\lambda}y]=0;

\displaystyle R_{4}:~[x_{\lambda}x]=(\partial+2\lambda)x,\quad[x_{\lambda}y]=(\partial+\beta\lambda+\gamma)y,\quad[y_{\lambda}y]=0,\quad\forall~\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C};

\displaystyle R_{5}:~[x_{\lambda}x]=(\partial+2\lambda)x,\quad[x_{\lambda}y]=(\partial+\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda)y,\quad[y_{\lambda}y]=\alpha x,\quad\forall~\alpha\in\mathbb{C}.

We begin with the following definition.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(A,[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])
be a Lie conformal superalgebra. A compatible TPCSA structure over
A
is an even
\lambda
-product
\circ_{\lambda}
on
A
, such that
(A,\circ_{\lambda},[{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot}])
forms a TPCSA.

Obviously, the
\lambda
-product
a\circ_{\lambda}b=0
defines a compatible TPCSA structure over the Lie conformal superalgebra
A
. This is referred to as the
trivial
compatible TPCSA structure over
A
. Consequently, our primary task is to determine the existence of nontrivial compatible TPCSA structures on

A
, and to classify such structures if they exist.

Let
\circ_{\lambda}
be an even
\lambda
-product on
R
. By the definition of a TPCSA, we can assume that

x\circ_{\lambda}x=f(\partial,\lambda)x,\quad x\circ_{\lambda}y=g(\partial,\lambda)y,\quad y\circ_{\lambda}y=h(\partial,\lambda)x,

(5.1)

where
f(\partial,\lambda),g(\partial,\lambda),h(\partial,\lambda)\in\mathbb{C}[\partial,\lambda]
. By commutativity, we get

f(\partial,\lambda)=f(\partial,-\partial-\lambda),\quad y\circ_{\lambda}x=g(\partial,-\partial-\lambda)y,\quad h(\partial,\lambda)=-h(\partial,-\partial-\lambda).

(5.2)

By associativity, we have
x\circ_{\lambda}(x\circ_{\mu}x)=(x\circ_{\lambda}x)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}x
, this is equivalent to

\displaystyle f(\partial+\lambda,\mu)f(\partial,\lambda)=f(-\lambda-\mu,\lambda)f(\partial,\lambda+\mu),

(5.3)

which implies
deg_{\partial}f(\partial,\lambda)+deg_{\partial}f(\partial,\lambda)=deg_{\partial}f(\partial,\lambda)
,
where we use the notation
deg_{\partial}f(\partial,\lambda)
to denote the highest degree of
\partial
in
f(\partial,\lambda)
. Hence,
deg_{\partial}f(\partial,\lambda)=0
.
Now we can suppose
f(\partial,\lambda)=f(\lambda)
for some
f(\lambda)\in\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
. Then Eq. (
5.3
) reduces to

\displaystyle f(\mu)f(\lambda)=f(\lambda)f(\lambda+\mu),

(5.4)

which implies
f(\lambda)=c
for some
c\in\mathbb{C}
.

Moreover, we have
x\circ_{\lambda}(x\circ_{\mu}y)=(x\circ_{\lambda}x)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}y
, hence

\displaystyle g(\partial+\lambda,\mu)g(\partial,\lambda)=cg(\partial,\lambda+\mu).

(5.5)

(1) If
c=0
, then
g(\partial,\lambda)=0
.
(2) If
c\neq 0
, considering the highest degree of
\partial
on both sides,
we get
deg_{\partial}g(\partial,\lambda)=0
, leading to
g(\partial,\lambda)=g(\lambda)
for some
g(\lambda)\in\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
.
It follows that
g(\mu)g(\lambda)=cg(\lambda+\mu)
, which implies
g(\lambda)=0
or
g(\lambda)=c
.

The above elementary computations suggest that the possible forms of

f(\partial,\lambda)
and
g(\partial,\lambda)
are restricted to the following two cases:

\displaystyle(i)~f(\partial,\lambda)=c_{1},\quad g(\partial,\lambda)=0,\quad c_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\};

(5.6)

\displaystyle(ii)~f(\partial,\lambda)=c_{2},\quad g(\partial,\lambda)=c_{2},\quad c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}.

(5.7)

Next we proceed to analyze the five types
R_{1},\dots,R_{5}
separately.

Case 1:
Type
R_{1}
.
Assume first that
p(\partial)\neq 0
, taking
(a,b,c)=(y,y,y)
in Eq. (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle 2y\circ_{\lambda}[y_{\mu}y]=[(y\circ_{\lambda}y)_{\lambda+\mu}y]-[y_{\mu}(y\circ_{\lambda}y)].

Since
y\circ_{\lambda}y\in\mathbb{C}[\partial]x
and all mixed
brackets between
x
and
y
vanish, the right-hand side is zero. Hence

2p(\partial+\lambda)g(\partial,-\partial-\lambda)y=0.

As
\mathbb{C}[\partial,\lambda]
is an integral domain and
p(\partial+\lambda)\neq 0
, we get
g(\partial,-\partial-\lambda)=0
, i.e.,
g(\partial,\lambda)=0
.

Next, taking
(a,b,c)=(x,y,y)
in Eq. (
3.1
) gives

2x\circ_{\lambda}[y_{\mu}y]=[(x\circ_{\lambda}y)_{\lambda+\mu}y]+[y_{\mu}(x\circ_{\lambda}y)].

Since
g=0
, the right-hand side vanishes, and therefore
2p(\partial+\lambda)f(\partial,\lambda)x=0
.
It follows that
f(\partial,\lambda)=0
.

Consequently, the only possible nonzero
\lambda
-product is
y\circ_{\lambda}y=h(\partial,\lambda)x
.
By commutativity,
h
must satisfy
h(\partial,\lambda)=-h(\partial,-\partial-\lambda)
.
Equivalently, writing
T=\partial+2\lambda
, the polynomial
h
is odd with respect to
T
.
Hence there exists a polynomial
\Phi(s,t)\in\mathbb{C}[s,t]
such that

h(\partial,\lambda)=(\partial+2\lambda)\Phi\bigl(\partial,(\partial+2\lambda)^{2}\bigr).

Conversely, one can verify that any such polynomial
h
defines an even commutative associative
\lambda
-product on
R_{1}
.

If
p(\partial)=0
, then the Lie conformal superalgebra
R_{1}
is abelian, and every even commutative associative
\lambda
-product gives a TPCSA structure.

Case 2:
Type
R_{2}
. If
q(\lambda)=0
, then the Lie conformal superalgebra
R_{2}
is abelian, and the argument is the same as in the case
p(\partial)=0
above.

Assume that
q(\lambda)\neq 0
, taking
(a,b,c)=(y,x,y)
in Eq. (
3.1
), we obtain

2y\circ_{\lambda}[x_{\mu}y]=[(y\circ_{\lambda}x)_{\lambda+\mu}y]+[x_{\mu}(y\circ_{\lambda}y)].

Since
[y_{\lambda}y]=0
and
[x_{\lambda}x]=0
, we get
2q(\mu)h(\partial,\lambda)x=0
.
Hence
h(\partial,\lambda)=0
.

Taking
(a,b,c)=(x,x,y)
in Eq. (
3.1
), we get

2x\circ_{\lambda}[x_{\mu}y]=[(x\circ_{\lambda}x)_{\lambda+\mu}y]+[x_{\mu}(x\circ_{\lambda}y)].

This is equivalent to

q(\mu)\bigl(2g(\partial,\lambda)-g(\partial+\mu,\lambda)\bigr)=f(-\lambda-\mu,\lambda)q(\lambda+\mu).

Since
q(\lambda)\neq 0
, the pair
(f,g)
cannot be of the form (
5.6
). Indeed,
if
g(\partial,\lambda)=0
and
f(\partial,\lambda)=c_{1}\neq 0
, then the above identity gives

0=c_{1}q(\lambda+\mu)
, a contradiction. Thus
(f,g)
must be of the form (
5.7
).
Therefore,
c_{2}(q(\mu)-q(\lambda+\mu))=0
.

(1) If
q(\lambda)
is a nonzero constant, then
c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
.
(2) If
q(\lambda)
is nonconstant, then
c_{2}=0
.

Case 3:
Type
R_{3}
.
Taking
(a,b,c)=(y,x,x)
in Eq. (
3.1
), we get

2y\circ_{\lambda}[x_{\mu}x]=[(y\circ_{\lambda}x)_{\lambda+\mu}x]+[x_{\mu}(y\circ_{\lambda}x)].

Since all brackets involving both
x
and
y
are zero, the right-hand side is zero. Hence

2(\partial+\lambda+2\mu)g(\partial,-\partial-\lambda)y=0,

and therefore
g(\partial,-\partial-\lambda)=0
, i.e.,
g(\partial,\lambda)=0
.

Similarly, taking
(a,b,c)=(y,x,y)
in Eq. (
3.1
) gives
[x_{\mu}(y\circ_{\lambda}y)]=0
, that is

(\partial+2\mu)h(\partial+\mu,\lambda)x=0.

Hence
h(\partial+\mu,\lambda)=0
, i.e.,
h(\partial,\lambda)=0
.

As previously mentioned,
f(\partial,\lambda)=c
for some
c\in\mathbb{C}
.
Thus the compatible TPCSA structures on
R_{3}
are precisely

x\circ_{\lambda}x=cx,\quad x\circ_{\lambda}y=0,\quad y\circ_{\lambda}y=0,\quad c\in\mathbb{C}.

Case 4:
Type
R_{4}
.
Taking
(a,b,c)=(x,x,y)
in Eq. (
3.1
), we obtain

2x\circ_{\lambda}[x_{\mu}y]=[(x\circ_{\lambda}x)_{\lambda+\mu}y]+[x_{\mu}(x\circ_{\lambda}y)].

Since
f(\partial,\lambda)=c
for some
c\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

2(\partial+\lambda+\beta\mu+\gamma)g(\partial,\lambda)=c(\partial+\beta\lambda+\beta\mu+\gamma)+(\partial+\beta\mu+\gamma)g(\partial+\mu,\lambda).

(5.8)

Setting
\mu=0
in Eq. (
5.8
), we get

(\partial+2\lambda+\gamma)g(\partial,\lambda)=c(\partial+\beta\lambda+\gamma).

(5.9)

If
\beta=2
, then
(\partial+2\lambda+\gamma)(g(\partial,\lambda)-c)=0
, hence
g(\partial,\lambda)=c
.
If
\beta\neq 2
, by comparing the similar terms in Eq. (
5.9
), one can obtain
g(\partial,\lambda)=c=0
.

It remains to determine polynomial
h
. Taking
(a,b,c)=(y,x,y)
in Eq. (
3.1
) gives

2y\circ_{\lambda}[x_{\mu}y]=[(y\circ_{\lambda}x)_{\lambda+\mu}y]+[x_{\mu}(y\circ_{\lambda}y)].

Since
[y_{\lambda}y]=0
, we obtain

2(\partial+\lambda+\beta\mu+\gamma)h(\partial,\lambda)=(\partial+2\mu)h(\partial+\mu,\lambda).

(5.10)

Setting
\mu=0
in (
5.10
), we get
(\partial+2\lambda+2\gamma)h(\partial,\lambda)=0
, leading to
h(\partial,\lambda)=0
.

Consequently, if
\beta=2
, then

x\circ_{\lambda}x=cx,\quad x\circ_{\lambda}y=cy,\quad y\circ_{\lambda}y=0,\quad c\in\mathbb{C},

whereas if
\beta\neq 2
, then the
\lambda
-product is trivial.

Case 5:
Type
R_{5}
.
Taking
(a,b,c)=(x,x,y)
in Eq. (
3.1
), we obtain

2x\circ_{\lambda}[x_{\mu}y]=[(x\circ_{\lambda}x)_{\lambda+\mu}y]+[x_{\mu}(x\circ_{\lambda}y)].

Since
f(\partial,\lambda)=c
for some
c\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

2(\partial+\lambda+\tfrac{3}{2}\mu)g(\partial,\lambda)=c(\partial+\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda+\tfrac{3}{2}\mu)+(\partial+\tfrac{3}{2}\mu)g(\partial+\mu,\lambda).

(5.11)

Setting
\mu=0
in Eq. (
5.11
), we get

(\partial+2\lambda)g(\partial,\lambda)=c(\partial+\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda).

Since
\partial+\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda
is not divisible by
\partial+2\lambda
, we must have
c=0
.
Thus
f(\partial,\lambda)=g(\partial,\lambda)=0
.

Moreover, taking
(a,b,c)=(y,x,y)
in Eq. (
3.1
) gives

2y\circ_{\lambda}[x_{\mu}y]=[(y\circ_{\lambda}x)_{\lambda+\mu}y]+[x_{\mu}(y\circ_{\lambda}y)].

Since
y\circ_{\lambda}x=0
, we obtain

2(\partial+\lambda+\tfrac{3}{2}\mu)h(\partial,\lambda)=(\partial+2\mu)h(\partial+\mu,\lambda).

(5.12)

Setting
\mu=0
in Eq. (
5.12
), we get
(\partial+2\lambda)h(\partial,\lambda)=0
, hence
h(\partial,\lambda)=0
.

Therefore, the Lie conformal superalgebra
R_{5}
admits only the trivial TPCSA structure.

The preceding case-by-case computations give all necessary forms of the

\lambda
-product, we summarize the discussions above in the following theorem:

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
R
be one of the Lie conformal superalgebras
R_{1},\ldots,R_{5}
listed
above, and let
\circ_{\lambda}
be an even
\lambda
-product on
R
. Then

\circ_{\lambda}
defines a compatible TPCSA
structure on
R
if and only if it is one of the following:

(1)

For
R_{1}
: If
p(\partial)\neq 0
, then

x\circ_{\lambda}x=0,\quad x\circ_{\lambda}y=0,

and

y\circ_{\lambda}y=(\partial+2\lambda)\Phi\bigl(\partial,(\partial+2\lambda)^{2}\bigr)x,\quad\Phi(s,t)\in\mathbb{C}[s,t].

(Each polynomial
\Phi(s,t)\in\mathbb{C}[s,t]
defines such a structure.)

If
p(\partial)=0
, then
R_{1}
is abelian, and every even commutative
associative
\lambda
-product gives such a structure.

(2)

For
R_{2}
: If
q(\lambda)=0
, then
R_{2}
is abelian, and every even
commutative associative
\lambda
-product gives such a structure.

If
q(\lambda)
is a nonzero constant, then

x\circ_{\lambda}x=cx,\quad x\circ_{\lambda}y=cy,\quad y\circ_{\lambda}y=0,\quad c\in\mathbb{C}.

If
q(\lambda)
is nonconstant, then only the trivial structure exists:

x\circ_{\lambda}x=x\circ_{\lambda}y=y\circ_{\lambda}y=0.

(3)

For
R_{3}
: Every structure is given by

x\circ_{\lambda}x=cx,\quad x\circ_{\lambda}y=0,\quad y\circ_{\lambda}y=0,\quad c\in\mathbb{C}.

(4)

For
R_{4}
: If
\beta=2
, then

x\circ_{\lambda}x=cx,\quad x\circ_{\lambda}y=cy,\quad y\circ_{\lambda}y=0,\quad c\in\mathbb{C}.

If
\beta\neq 2
, then only the trivial structure exists:

x\circ_{\lambda}x=x\circ_{\lambda}y=y\circ_{\lambda}y=0.

(5)

For
R_{5}
: Only the trivial structure exists:

x\circ_{\lambda}x=x\circ_{\lambda}y=y\circ_{\lambda}y=0.

Proof.

The necessity is precisely the content of the five cases considered above.
Conversely, for each
\lambda
-product listed in the theorem, evenness and
commutativity are immediate from the construction, and associativity
follows directly from the displayed formulas. A direct substitution of these

\lambda
-products into Eq. (
3.1
) verifies the transposed super-Leibniz rule
in each case. Hence the listed necessary forms are also sufficient.

∎
