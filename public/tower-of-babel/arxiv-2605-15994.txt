Large- N N scaling of Tan’s contact for the harmonically trapped Tonks–Girardeau gas at finite temperature
Felipe Taha Sant’Ana

Abstract
We derive the canonical-ensemble scaling of Tan’s contact for N N harmonically trapped Tonks–Girardeau bosons at finite temperature in the large- N N limit. The leading scaling coefficient reproduces the local-density-approximation result and is obtained from a contour-integral representation of the canonical partition function followed by a saddle-point reduction to a phase-space integral with a self-consistent scaled chemical potential. The subleading coefficient is the central new object of this work: it admits an explicit representation in terms of universal phase-space integrals of the Fermi factor, has closed-form Sommerfeld and virial limits, and is identified with the canonical-versus-grand-canonical ensemble difference at fixed mean particle number. In the high-temperature Boltzmann regime the ratio of subleading to leading coefficients collapses to a universal value, traceable to the Poissonian particle-number statistics of the dilute grand-canonical gas. We construct Padé approximants for both scaling functions that interpolate uniformly between the low-temperature Sommerfeld and high-temperature virial regimes; for the subleading coefficient we report a form that is uniformly accurate on our working range of temperatures and asymptotically correct beyond. The scaling law is verified against canonical contour-integration data across the full temperature range.

On the theoretical side, the

II
The model

We consider
N
identical bosons of mass
m
on the line, confined by an
harmonic potential of frequency
\omega
and interacting
through a contact potential of coupling strength
g>0
.
The Hamiltonian is

H=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}x_{i}^{2}\right)+g\sum_{i<j}\delta(x_{i}-x_{j}).

(1)

Throughout this paper we work in harmonic-oscillator units, setting

\hbar=m=\omega=k_{B}=1
, so that lengths are measured in units of the
oscillator length
a_{0}=\sqrt{\hbar/m\omega}
, energies in units of

\hbar\omega
, and the single-particle eigenstates of the trap are the
familiar Hermite functions

\phi_{n}(x)=\frac{e^{-x^{2}/2}H_{n}(x)}{\pi^{1/4}\sqrt{2^{n}n!}},\qquad\varepsilon_{n}=n+\frac{1}{2},\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots

(2)

We restrict our analysis to the Tonks–Girardeau (TG) limit

g\to\infty
, in which the two-body interaction reduces to
the impenetrability constraint

\Psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=0
whenever
x_{i}=x_{j}
for any
i\neq j
.
In this limit the Bose–Fermi mapping of Girardeau
10

expresses every bosonic eigenstate as a Slater determinant of
single-particle Hermite functions multiplied by an antisymmetrising
sign factor,

\Psi^{(\mathrm{b})}_{\alpha}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=\prod_{i<j}\mathrm{sgn}(x_{i}-x_{j})\;\Psi^{(\mathrm{f})}_{\alpha}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}),

(3)

where
\Psi^{(\mathrm{f})}_{\alpha}
is the noninteracting fermionic
Slater determinant labelled by the occupied set

\alpha=\{n_{1},\dots,n_{N}\}
.
Since

\left|\Psi^{(\mathrm{b})}\right|^{2}=\left|\Psi^{(\mathrm{f})}\right|^{2}
, all local one-body
observables of the TG gas (in particular the spatial density and the
energy) coincide with those of
N
noninteracting spinless fermions in
the same trap. Off-diagonal correlations, however, do not: the
Bose–Fermi map preserves probability density but not phase, so
quantities sensitive to the off-diagonal structure of the one-body
density matrix—most notably the momentum distribution—differ
remarkably between the two systems
23
;
26
.

The central object of this work is Tan’s contact, defined through the
universal large-momentum tail of the single-particle momentum
distribution,

\mathcal{C}=\lim_{k\to\infty}k^{4}\,n(k),\qquad n(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int dx\,dx^{\prime}\,e^{ik(x-x^{\prime})}\,\rho^{(1)}(x,x^{\prime}),

(4)

with
\rho^{(1)}
the thermal one-body density matrix. The
contact admits the formulation as a one-dimensional integral
over a positive integrand
F_{N}(x)
1
1

1

See Appendix
A
for the detailed derivation.
,

\mathcal{C}_{N}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\,F_{N}(x),\qquad F_{N}(x)=\left\langle\rho(x)\,\kappa(x)-S(x)^{2}\right\rangle_{N}^{(\mathrm{CE})},

(5)

where the local objects
\rho
,
S
,
\kappa
are diagonal and
near-diagonal values of the fermionic kernel and

\langle\cdots\rangle_{N}^{(\mathrm{CE})}
denotes the canonical thermal
average at fixed particle number
N
. The representation
(
5
) is the starting point for the saddle-point
analysis of the following sections.

To compare different particle numbers and temperatures on a common
footing, we parametrise the temperature by the dimensionless ratio

\tau:=\frac{T}{T_{F}},\qquad T_{F}=N\hbar\omega,

(6)

where
T_{F}
is the Fermi temperature of the equivalent noninteracting
gas (the energy of the highest occupied harmonic level at
T=0
). In
units with
\hbar=\omega=k_{B}=1
this reads
T_{F}=N
. At fixed

\tau
, the inverse temperature scales as

\beta:=1/T=1/(\tau N)
, so that the thermal de Broglie wavelength

\lambda_{T}=\sqrt{2\pi\beta}
is parametrically smaller than both the
oscillator length and the Thomas-Fermi cloud size
R_{\mathrm{TF}}=\sqrt{2N}

in the relevant scaling regime. This separation of scales is what
makes the large-
N
expansion at fixed
\tau
a genuine semiclassical
limit and underlies the
N^{5/2}
leading scaling of the contact
established in Sec.
III.1
.

The two physically distinct regimes of
\tau
correspond to the
familiar quantum statistics of a noninteracting Fermi gas:
\tau\ll 1

is the deeply degenerate regime in which the Fermi–Dirac distribution
is sharp and Sommerfeld-type expansions apply, while
\tau\gg 1
is the
classical (Boltzmann) regime in which the occupation probabilities are
small and the partition function admits a virial expansion. Both
limits are accessible analytically and serve as anchors for the
finite-
\tau
analysis that follows. In the intermediate regime

\tau\sim 1
we resort to numerical evaluation of the universal
scaling functions, supplemented by Padé approximants that
interpolate between the two asymptotic forms.

II.1
Kernel representation

Let
\Xi(z)
be the grand partition function of the trapped ideal Fermi gas,

\Xi(z)=\prod_{n\geq 0}\left(1+ze^{-\beta\varepsilon_{n}}\right).

The canonical partition function

can be extracted through a coefficient

in

Z_{N}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{dz}{z^{N+1}}\,\Xi(z).

(8)

The integrand

entering the contact (

where the kernel

\displaystyle K_{z}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}(z)\,\phi_{n}(x)\phi_{n}(y),\qquad f_{n}(z)=\frac{ze^{-\beta\varepsilon_{n}}}{1+ze^{-\beta\varepsilon_{n}}},

\displaystyle\rho_{z}(x)

\displaystyle=K_{z}(x,x),\qquad S_{z}(x)=\partial_{y}K_{z}(x,y)\big|_{y=x},\qquad\kappa_{z}(x)=\partial_{x}\partial_{y}K_{z}(x,y)\big|_{y=x}.

III
Canonical contact scaling

In this section we derive the canonical-ensemble large-
N
scaling law

\mathcal{C}_{N}(\tau)=A(\tau)\,N^{5/2}+B(\tau)\,N^{3/2},

(12)

through a contour-integral representation of the canonical partition
function followed by saddle-point reduction. The leading coefficient

A(\tau)
and its asymptotic limits are obtained in
Sec.
III.1
; the subleading coefficient

B(\tau)
, together with its explicit Sommerfeld and virial
expansions, is the subject of Sec.

III.1
Leading term

In order to extract the leading large-
N
behaviour, we proceed to evaluate both contour integrals
in (
8
) and (
9
) by the saddle-point method.
We begin by writing

\frac{\Xi(z)}{z^{N+1}}=\exp\left[\Phi_{N}(z)\right],\qquad\Phi_{N}(z):=\log\Xi(z)-(N+1)\log z.

(13)

For large
N
the
-\log z
term is
sub-leading and may be dropped at the saddle order: the saddle

z=z_{\star}
is determined by

\frac{d}{dz}\left[\log\Xi(z)-N\log z\right]\Big|_{z_{\star}}=0\quad\Longrightarrow N=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{z_{\star}e^{-\beta\varepsilon_{n}}}{1+z_{\star}e^{-\beta\varepsilon_{n}}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}(z_{\star}).

(14)

It is convenient to parametrize
z_{\star}=e^{\beta\mu}
, so that

f_{n}(z_{\star})=\frac{1}{e^{\beta(\varepsilon_{n}-\mu)}+1},

(15)

where
\mu=\mu(N,T)
is the chemical potential.

Since both numerator and denominator of (
9
) are dominated by
the same saddle
z_{\star}
, the leading contribution to their ratio is
obtained by evaluating the kernel functional at
z_{\star}
:

F_{N}(x)\simeq\rho_{z_{\star}}(x)\,\kappa_{z_{\star}}(x)-S_{z_{\star}}(x)^{2}+\cdots,

(16)

where the displayed equality is asymptotic (the omitted terms are
the Gaussian saddle-fluctuation corrections, which we treat
systematically in Sec.
III.2
).

We now evaluate
\rho_{z_{\star}}(x)
,
\kappa_{z_{\star}}(x)
and
S_{z_{\star}}(x)

in the large-
N
, semiclassical limit at fixed
\tau
.
In the Wigner, or phase-space, approximation, the kernel becomes local in
(x,p)

with Fermi occupation

f(p,x)=\frac{1}{e^{\beta\left(\frac{p^{2}}{2}+V(x)-\mu\right)}+1},\qquad V(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{2}.

(17)

Then the local objects defined in (
11
) reduce to the
moments

\displaystyle\rho_{z_{\star}}(x)\simeq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dp}{2\pi}\,f(p,x),

(18)

\displaystyle S_{z_{\star}}(x)\simeq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dp}{2\pi}\,(ip)\,f(p,x)=0,

(19)

\displaystyle\kappa_{z_{\star}}(x)\simeq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dp}{2\pi}\,p^{2}\,f(p,x).

(20)

The vanishing of
S(x)
follows from the parity
p\mapsto-p
of
f(p,x)
.

Using (
16
) and (
19
), we obtain

F_{N}(x)\simeq\rho(x)\,\kappa(x).

(21)

We now show that (
21
) implies
\mathcal{C}_{N}(\tau)\propto N^{5/2}
.
At fixed
\tau
we have
\beta\sim 1/(\tau N)
, and the saddle chemical
potential
\mu(N,\tau)
is fixed by the particle-number constraint
(
14
). Anticipating the scaling

\mu=\xi(\tau)\,N
that will be made explicit shortly,
we introduce the standard large-
N
scaling of coordinates and momenta,

x=\sqrt{2N}\,u,\qquad p=\sqrt{2N}\,q.

(22)

Then

\frac{p^{2}}{2}+V(x)-\mu=N(q^{2}+u^{2}-\xi),

(23)

so that, using
\beta=1/(\tau N)
,

\beta\left(\frac{p^{2}}{2}+V(x)-\mu\right)=\frac{q^{2}+u^{2}-\xi}{\tau}.

(24)

Therefore the occupation (
17
) becomes
N
-independent at
leading order,

f(p,x)\ \longrightarrow\ f_{\tau}(q,u):=\frac{1}{e^{(q^{2}+u^{2}-\xi)/\tau}+1},\qquad\xi=\xi(\tau).

(25)

The scaled chemical potential
\xi(\tau)
is determined self-consistently
by the large-
N
limit of the number constraint
(
14
). Replacing the discrete sum over
single-particle levels by an integral against the 1D harmonic
oscillator density of states, and using

\beta=1/(\tau N)
,
\mu=\xi N
,
\varepsilon_{n}=NE
recasts
(
14

\tau\,\log\left(1+e^{\xi/\tau}\right)=1\,,\qquad\xi=\xi(\tau).

(26)

Equation (
26

\xi(\tau\ll 1)=1-\tau\,e^{-1/\tau}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2/\tau})
at
low temperature and
\xi(\tau\gg 1)\sim-\tau\log\tau
at high
temperature. The zero-temperature limit
\xi(0)=1
follows directly
from (
26

\tau\to 0^{+}
,
\log(1+e^{\xi/\tau})\to\max(\xi/\tau,0)
. Thus the
chemical potential coincides with
N
at
T=0

small
\tau

the Sommerfeld series, while at
\tau\gtrsim 1

polynomially and must be retained. For all numerical results below we use the
self-consistent
\xi(\tau)
from (

\xi=1

\tau

\mathcal{O}(\tau\log\tau)

\tau\gtrsim 1

Let us begin by defining the dimensionless moments

I_{0}(u;\tau):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dq\,f_{\tau}(q,u),\qquad I_{2}(u;\tau):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dq\,q^{2}\,f_{\tau}(q,u).

Using

\displaystyle\rho(x)

\displaystyle\simeq\int\frac{dp}{2\pi}f(p,x)=\frac{\sqrt{2N}}{2\pi}\,I_{0}(u;\tau),

(28)

\displaystyle\kappa(x)

\displaystyle\simeq\int\frac{dp}{2\pi}p^{2}f(p,x)=\frac{(2N)^{3/2}}{2\pi}\,I_{2}(u;\tau).

(29)

Inserting (

F_{N}(x)\simeq\rho(x)\kappa(x)=\frac{(2N)^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\,I_{0}(u;\tau)\,I_{2}(u;\tau)=\frac{N^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\,I_{0}(u;\tau)\,I_{2}(u;\tau).

(30)

Finally, using the definition of the contact (

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{N}(\tau)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\,\frac{N^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\,I_{0}(u;\tau)\,I_{2}(u;\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\,\sqrt{2N}\,\frac{N^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}du\,I_{0}(u;\tau)\,I_{2}(u;\tau)

\displaystyle=A(\tau)\,N^{5/2},

III.1.1
Low-temperature

In the zero-temperature limit,
f_{\tau}(q,u)\to\Theta(1-u^{2}-q^{2})
.
Then for
|u|<1

I_{0}(u;0)=2\sqrt{1-u^{2}},\qquad I_{2}(u;0)=\frac{2}{3}(1-u^{2})^{3/2},\qquad I_{0}I_{2}=\frac{4}{3}(1-u^{2})^{2},

For

IV
Ensemble correspondence

The canonical analysis of Sec.
III.1
–
III.2

established the scaling law (
12
)
through a contour-integral representation followed by saddle reduction.
The same machinery, evaluated at the saddle alone, is essentially the
grand-canonical calculation. Examining what survives—and what
disappears—when the contour is removed clarifies the physical origin
of the subleading coefficient
B(\tau)
as an ensemble-correspondence
effect.

In the GCE the occupations
\eta_{n}\in\{0,1\}
are independent Bernoulli
variables with means

\bar{n}_{n}(z)=\left(e^{\beta(\varepsilon_{n}-\mu)}+1\right)^{-1}
and joint moments

\langle\eta_{m}\eta_{n}\rangle_{\mathrm{GCE}}=\bar{n}_{m}\bar{n}_{n}
for
m\neq n
.
Inserting this factorisation into the pair representation of the
integrand (cf. Appendix B) and using
A_{nn}(x):=0
collapses the
double sum to the same kernel functional that appears under the
contour in (
158
), but evaluated directly
at the GCE fugacity
z=e^{\beta\mu}
:

F^{\mathrm{GCE}}(x;z)=\rho_{z}(x)\,\kappa_{z}(x)-S_{z}(x)^{2},\qquad\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{GCE}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int dx\,F^{\mathrm{GCE}}(x;z),

(105)

with
\rho_{z},\kappa_{z},S_{z}
defined as in (
11
). No
contour integration is required: the right-hand side of
(
105
) is the GCE expectation.

To match
\langle N\rangle_{\mathrm{GCE}}=N
we impose the same number condition
(
14
) as the canonical saddle, which in the
fixed-
\tau
scaling limit (with
\beta=1/(\tau N)
and
\mu=\xi(\tau)\,N
)
reduces to the self-consistent transcendental equation (
26
)
already introduced in Sec.
III.1
. The
phase-space Fermi factor is the one in (
25
), evaluated with
the self-consistent
\xi(\tau)
.

The LDA reduction of (
105
) is identical to that
performed in Sec.
III.1
; setting
S_{z}:=0
by
parity and using the scaling variables (
22
),

F^{\mathrm{GCE}}(x;z)=\frac{N^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\,g_{0}(u;\tau)+\mathcal{O}(1),\qquad x=\sqrt{2N}\,u,

(106)

with
g_{0}(u;\tau)=I_{0}(u;\tau)\,I_{2}(u;\tau)
as in
(
56
). The crucial difference with respect to
the canonical decomposition (
64
) is the absence
of the
\mathcal{O}(N)
saddle-fluctuation term: (
105
) contains
no contour integral, and therefore no Gaussian fluctuations to expand
around. Integrating (
106
) yields

\;\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{GCE}}_{\langle N\rangle=N}(\tau)=A(\tau)\,N^{5/2}+\mathcal{O}(N^{1/2}),\;

(107)

with the same
A(\tau)
as in (
32
), now
understood with the self-consistent
\xi(\tau)
, and no

N^{3/2}
correction at the LDA order. The leading scaling
function
A(\tau)
is therefore an ensemble-independent universal
quantity.

It is important to distinguish two senses in which
(
107
) holds. At the LDA level, the absence of

N^{3/2}
corrections is true by construction: the only
N
-dependence
in
F^{\mathrm{GCE}}(x;z_{\star})
enters through
\sqrt{2N}
in the
rescaled coordinate and through the Fermi factor at the
LDA-saddle fugacity, both of which produce only even powers of

N^{1/2}
.

Subtracting (
107
) from the canonical expansion
(
12
) yields the central observation of this
section,

\Delta\mathcal{C}(\tau):=\mathcal{C}_{N}^{\mathrm{CE}}(\tau)-\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{GCE}}_{\langle N\rangle=N}(\tau)=B(\tau)\,N^{3/2}+\mathcal{O}(N^{1/2}),\;

(108)

with
B(\tau)
given explicitly by (
67
). Equation
(
108
) provides the physical interpretation of the
subleading scaling function:
B(\tau)\,N^{3/2}
is the
ensemble correction to the contact—the part of
\mathcal{C}_{N}^{\mathrm{CE}}

sourced by the constraint of fixed
N
and therefore absent from the
GCE. Equivalently, the GCE delivers the leading
A(\tau)\,N^{5/2}

directly from the local kernel functional, while the CE pays the
price of a fluctuation correction of magnitude exactly

B(\tau)\,N^{3/2}
.

The result (
108
) can be derived independently from the
standard ensemble-correspondence formula (see e.g. the textbook
discussion in
17
or the modern review of
large-deviation methods in statistical mechanics

35
): for any extensive observable
O
,

\langle O\rangle_{\mathrm{CE}}-\langle O\rangle_{\mathrm{GCE}}\simeq\frac{1}{2\kappa_{2}}\,\partial_{N}^{2}\langle O\rangle_{\mathrm{GCE}}-\frac{\kappa_{3}}{2\kappa_{2}^{\,2}}\,\partial_{N}\langle O\rangle_{\mathrm{GCE}},

(109)

with
\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3}
the GCE particle-number cumulants. In the
fixed-
\tau
scaling limit, the cumulants reduce as in
(
62
)–(
63
), exhibiting the
global functions
V_{2},V_{3}
as nothing more than rescaled
particle-number cumulants of the GCE. Setting
O=\mathcal{C}
with

\langle\mathcal{C}\rangle_{\mathrm{GCE}}=A(\tau)\,N^{5/2}
, each derivative drops a
power of
N
while each
1/\kappa
factor restores
1/N
, so both
terms in (
109
) contribute at order
N^{3/2}
.
Reconstructing the coefficients with the explicit Wigner integrands
recovers (
67
) term by term, and confirms that the
structure of
B(\tau)
—a local part proportional to

\partial_{t}^{2}g_{0}/V_{2}
and a global part proportional to

V_{3}/V_{2}^{\,2}
—is dictated by the curvature and skewness of the GCE
particle-number distribution.

In the dilute Boltzmann regime
\tau\gg 1
, the GCE distribution of

N
becomes Poissonian,
\kappa_{2}\simeq\langle N\rangle
and hence

V_{2}(\tau)\to\pi
as in (
96
). The ratio
B/A
then
collapses to the universal value
-1
as in
(
97
), in agreement with
(
103
).

V
Conclusions

We have derived the canonical-ensemble large-
N
scaling law
(
12
) of Tan’s contact for the harmonically
trapped Tonks–Girardeau gas at fixed reduced temperature.
The leading coefficient
A(\tau)
coincides with the
local-density-approximation result and reproduces the known
zero-temperature value
A(0)=128\sqrt{2}/(45\pi^{3})
. The subleading
coefficient
B(\tau)
, the central new object of this work, admits a
universal first-principles representation (
67
) in
terms of phase-space integrals of the Fermi factor, has explicit
Sommerfeld and virial limits given in
(
91
) and
(
103
), and is identified through
(
108
) with the canonical-versus-grand-canonical ensemble
difference at fixed mean particle number. In the Boltzmann limit, the
universal asymptotic coefficient ratio
B/A\to-1
— equivalently

B(\tau\gg 1)\simeq-A(\tau)
— emerges from the Poissonian
particle-number statistics of the GCE: the canonical contact lies
below the corresponding grand-canonical contact at the
canonical-saddle fugacity, with the relative correction

|B/A|/N=1/N
in the dilute classical regime. We construct compact closed-form
Padé approximants for
A(\tau)
and
B(\tau)
, valid uniformly on

\tau\in[0,10]
, and the
scaling law is verified numerically against canonical
contour-integration data for
N
up to
100
across the full
temperature range.

The expansion derived in this manuscript is for fixed positive
\tau
followed by

N\to\infty
. The strictly zero-temperature limit
(
\tau=0
) has a qualitatively different
finite-
N
hierarchy: the thermal Fermi-surface smearing that
broadens the turning-point layer to width
\tau
collapses to the
Airy width
N^{-2/3}
, and the resulting edge corrections produce
distinct
N^{3/4}
and
N^{1/4}
contributions

36
;
28
. Since (
85
) vanishes linearly as

\tau\to 0
, the
N^{3/2}
term derived here becomes subdominant
to the zero-temperature edge corrections in the strict

\tau\to 0
limit, and the two limits do not commute. The crossover
between the two regimes occurs at
\tau\sim N^{-2/3}
, where the
thermal width matches the Airy width. The present analysis is valid
for
\tau\gg N^{-2/3}
, which at
N=100
corresponds to

\tau\gtrsim 0.05
.

The result extends the canonical analysis of
Ref.
30
to many particles through the kernel representation
and identifies the precise origin of the subleading
N^{3/2}
term as a finite-
N

ensemble-correspondence effect. In light of recent direct contact
measurements in trapped one-dimensional Bose gases

12
, the explicit scaling functions
A(\tau)
and

B(\tau)
provide a quantitative target against which
finite-temperature trapped-geometry data can be compared.

Several extensions are natural. The boundary-layer (Airy) corrections
to the contact, which produce additional subleading terms scaling as

N^{3/4}
and
N^{1/4}
at zero temperature

28
, persist at finite temperature but are
expected to be exponentially suppressed by the thermal smoothing of
the Fermi surface; their explicit finite-
T
form is the natural
sequel to the present work. The same contour-integral framework can
also be applied to multi-component fermionic mixtures

8
;
7
and to the trapped Lieb–Liniger gas at
finite coupling
43
, where similar
canonical-vs-grand-canonical distinctions are expected to control the
subleading scaling.

Appendix A
Derivation of the contact

We start with the
j
-body density matrix of
N
particles at temperature
T
,

\displaystyle\varrho^{(j)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{j};x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{N!}{(N-j)!}Z^{-1}\sum_{\alpha}e^{-\beta E_{\alpha}}\int dx_{j+1}\dots dx_{N}\Psi_{\alpha}^{(b)*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})

(110)

\displaystyle\times\Psi_{\alpha}^{(b)}(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j},x_{j+1},\dots,x_{N}),

where
Z=\sum_{\alpha}e^{-\beta E_{\alpha}}
is the partition function and
the system’s total energy is simply the summation of all the individual single-particle energies,
i.e.
,

E_{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n_{i}}
, with
\varepsilon_{n_{i}}=(n_{i}+1/2)\hbar\omega
.
Invoking the Bose–Fermi mapping

\Psi_{\alpha}^{(b)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=\Theta(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\Psi_{\alpha}^{(f)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}),

(111)

where
\Theta(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}):=\prod_{i<j}\mathrm{sgn}(x_{i}-x_{j})
is either
+1
or
-1
, in order to compensate the anti-symmetrization of the fermionic wave function
\Psi^{(f)}_{\alpha}
, and
\alpha
is the quantum number describing the particles in a respective set of individual quantum numbers
\{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{N}\}
, and the fermionic many-body wave function is given by the Slater determinant

\Psi_{\alpha}^{(f)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=\left(N!\right)^{-1/2}\det[\phi_{n_{i}}(x_{j})]_{n_{i}\in\{n_{1},\dots,n_{N}\};\,x_{j}\in\{x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\}},

(112)

with
\phi_{n}(x)
being the solutions of the harmonic oscillator single-particle solutions,
eq. (
110
) reads

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\varrho^{(j)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{j};x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j})=\frac{N!}{(N-j)!}Z^{-1}\sum_{\alpha}e^{-\beta E_{\alpha}}\int dx_{j+1}\dots dx_{N}\Theta(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})

(113)

\displaystyle\times\Psi_{\alpha}^{(f)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\Theta(x^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,x^{\prime}_{j},x_{j+1},\dots,x_{N})\Psi_{\alpha}^{(f)}(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j},x_{j+1},\dots,x_{N}).

Now we turn our focus to the integrand. It is possible to rewrite the product
of the
\Theta
’s as

\displaystyle\Theta(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\Theta(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j},x_{j+1},\dots,x_{N})=

\displaystyle\Theta(x_{1},\dots,x_{j})\Theta(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j})

(114)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=j+1}^{N}\prod_{l=1}^{2j}\mathrm{sgn}(x_{i}-y_{l}),

with
y_{1}=x_{1}<y_{2}=x_{2}<\dots<y_{j}=x_{j}<y_{j+1}=x^{\prime}_{1}<\dots<y_{2j}=x^{\prime}_{j}
.
Now let us consider the set
\mathfrak{S}=\{(y_{1},y_{2})\cup(y_{3},y_{4})\cup\dots\cup(y_{2j-1},y_{2j})\}
.
It is straightforward to observe that

\prod_{i=1}^{2j}\mathrm{sgn}(x-y_{i})=\begin{cases}-1,&x\in\mathfrak{S}\\
+1,&x\notin\mathfrak{S}\end{cases}.

(115)

Denoting the number of variables among
x_{j+1},\dots,x_{N}
which are in
\mathfrak{S}
by
M_{\mathfrak{S}}
, we have that

\Theta(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\Theta(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j},x_{j+1},\dots,x_{N})=\Theta(x_{1},\dots,x_{j})\Theta(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j})(-1)^{M_{\mathfrak{S}}}.

(116)

Consequently, eq. (
113
) results in

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\varrho^{(j)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{j};x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j})=\frac{N!}{(N-j)!}Z^{-1}\Theta(x_{1},\dots,x_{j})\Theta(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j})\sum_{\alpha}e^{-\beta E_{\alpha}}

(117)

\displaystyle\times\int dx_{j+1}\dots dx_{N}(-1)^{M_{\mathfrak{S}}}\Psi_{\alpha}^{(f)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\Psi_{\alpha}^{(f)}(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j},x_{j+1},\dots,x_{N}).

Now, considering any integral of the form

I=\int dx_{1}\dots\int(-1)^{M_{\mathfrak{S}}}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{j}),

(118)

where
M_{\mathfrak{S}}
is the number of integration variables inside
the subdomain
\mathfrak{S}
and
f
is a symmetric function,
it is possible to write

I=\sum_{m=0}^{j}\binom{j}{m}(-1)^{m}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}dx_{1}\dots dx_{m}\int_{\real-\mathfrak{S}}dx_{m+1}\dots dx_{j}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{j}).

(119)

Making use of
\int_{\real-\mathfrak{S}}dx=\int_{\real}dx-\int_{\mathfrak{S}}dx
,
we have

I=\sum_{m=0}^{j}\binom{j}{m}(-1)^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{j-m}\binom{j-m}{n}(-1)^{n}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}dx_{1}\dots dx_{m+n}\int_{\real}dx_{m+n+1}\dots dx_{j}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{j}).

(120)

Performing the summation for
m+n=i
, (
120
) reduces to

I=\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{j}{i}(-2)^{i}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}dx_{1}\dots dx_{i}\int dx_{i+1}\dots dx_{j}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{j}).

(121)

Thence, we have that the
j
-body density matrix (
117
)
can be written as

\displaystyle\varrho^{(j)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{j};x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j})=

\displaystyle\frac{N!}{(N-j)!}Z^{-1}\Theta(x_{1},\dots,x_{j})\Theta(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j})\sum_{\alpha}e^{-\beta E_{\alpha}}

(122)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{i=0}^{N-j}\binom{N-j}{i}(-2)^{i}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}dx_{j+1}\dots dx_{j+i}\int dx_{j+i+1}\dots dx_{N}

\displaystyle\times\Psi_{\alpha}^{(f)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\Psi_{\alpha}^{(f)}(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{j},x_{j+1},\dots,x_{N}).

The one-body density matrix gives the Lenard series

\displaystyle\varrho^{(1)}(x,x^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\frac{N}{Z}\sum_{\alpha}e^{-\beta E_{\alpha}}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\binom{N-1}{j}(-2)^{j}[\mathrm{sgn}(x-x^{\prime})]^{j}\int_{x}^{x^{\prime}}dx_{2}\dots dx_{j+1}

(123)

\displaystyle\times\int dx_{j+2}\dots dx_{N}\Psi_{\alpha}^{(f)}(x,x_{2},\dots,x_{N})\Psi_{\alpha}^{(f)}(x^{\prime},x_{2},\dots,x_{N}).

The sum runs from
j=1
rather than
j=0
: the
j=0
term would
give the noninteracting fermionic one-body density matrix

\varrho^{(1)}_{f}(x,x^{\prime})
, which is analytic in
x^{\prime}-x
at short
distances and therefore contributes neither to the universal

|x^{\prime}-x|^{3}
behaviour nor to the
k^{-4}
tail of the momentum
distribution. The contact is sourced entirely by the non-analytic

|x^{\prime}-x|^{3}
piece of
\varrho^{(1)}
, generated by the

[\mathrm{sgn}(x-x^{\prime})]^{j}
factors at
j\geq 1
.
Here it is possible to recognize the
j
-body fermionic correlator as

\varrho^{(1)}(x,x^{\prime})=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{(-2)^{j}}{j!}[\mathrm{sgn}(x-x^{\prime})]^{j}\int_{x}^{x^{\prime}}dx_{2}\dots dx_{j+1}\varrho^{(j+1)}_{f}(x,x_{2},\dots,x_{j+1};x^{\prime},x_{2},\dots,x_{j+1}),

(124)

where

\displaystyle\varrho^{(j)}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{j};x_{1}^{\prime},\dots,x_{j}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{N!}{(N-j)!}Z^{-1}\sum_{\alpha}e^{-\beta E_{\alpha}}

(125)

\displaystyle\times\int dx_{j+1}\dots dx_{N}\Psi_{\alpha}^{(f)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\Psi_{\alpha}^{(f)}(x_{1}^{\prime},\dots,x_{j}^{\prime},x_{j+1},\dots,x_{N}).

As we are interested in the contact, we are going to restrict ourselves to small distances,
|x^{\prime}-x|\ll 1
.
Therefore, we consider only the term
j=1
, because the terms
j>1
produce
negligible results in the small distance approximation:

\displaystyle\varrho^{(1)}(x,x^{\prime})\underset{x\to x^{\prime}}{\sim}

\displaystyle 2\,\mathrm{sgn}(x^{\prime}-x)\int_{x}^{x^{\prime}}dx_{2}\,\varrho^{(2)}_{f}(x,x_{2};x^{\prime},x_{2})

(126)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 2\,\mathrm{sgn}(x^{\prime}-x)\varrho^{(2)}_{f}(x,R;x^{\prime},R)\delta x,

where
R:=(x+x^{\prime})/2
and
\delta x:=x^{\prime}-x
.

Now we proceed with the explicit evaluation of
\varrho^{(2)}

making use of (
125
) together with (
112
).

N=2 particles

\displaystyle\varrho^{(2)}_{f}(x,R;x^{\prime},R)=

\displaystyle Z^{-1}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}}e^{-\beta(\varepsilon_{n_{1}}+\varepsilon_{n_{2}})}\begin{vmatrix}\phi_{n_{1}}(x)&\phi_{n_{2}}(x)\\
\phi_{n_{1}}(R)&\phi_{n_{2}}(R)\\
\end{vmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}\phi_{n_{1}}(x^{\prime})&\phi_{n_{2}}(x^{\prime})\\
\phi_{n_{1}}(R)&\phi_{n_{2}}(R)\\
\end{vmatrix}

(127)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z^{-1}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}}e^{-\beta(\varepsilon_{n_{1}}+\varepsilon_{n_{2}})}\left[\phi_{n_{1}}\left(R-\delta x/2\right)\phi_{n_{2}}(R)-\phi_{n_{2}}(R-\delta x/2)\phi_{n_{1}}(R)\right]

\displaystyle\times\left[\phi_{n_{1}}(R+\delta x/2)\phi_{n_{2}}(R)-\phi_{n_{2}}(R+\delta x/2)\phi_{n_{1}}(R)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z^{-1}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}}e^{-\beta(\varepsilon_{n_{1}}+\varepsilon_{n_{2}})}\left[\left(\phi_{n_{1}}-\frac{\delta x}{2}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{1}}\right)\phi_{n_{2}}-\left(\phi_{n_{2}}-\frac{\delta x}{2}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{2}}\right)\phi_{n_{1}}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left[\left(\phi_{n_{1}}+\frac{\delta x}{2}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{1}}\right)\phi_{n_{2}}-\left(\phi_{n_{2}}+\frac{\delta x}{2}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{2}}\right)\phi_{n_{1}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z^{-1}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}}e^{-\beta(\varepsilon_{n_{1}}+\varepsilon_{n_{2}})}\frac{\delta x^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle\times\left[(\phi_{n_{2}}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{1}})^{2}+(\phi_{n_{1}}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{2}})^{2}-2\phi_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{2}}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{1}}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{2}}\right].

N=3 particles

\displaystyle\varrho^{(2)}_{f}(x,R;x^{\prime},R)=

\displaystyle Z^{-1}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}e^{-\beta(\varepsilon_{n_{1}}+\varepsilon_{n_{2}}+\varepsilon_{n_{3}})}

(128)

\displaystyle\times\int dx_{3}\,\begin{vmatrix}\phi_{n_{1}}(x)&\phi_{n_{2}}(x)&\phi_{n_{3}}(x)\\
\phi_{n_{1}}(R)&\phi_{n_{2}}(R)&\phi_{n_{3}}(R)\\
\phi_{n_{1}}(x_{3})&\phi_{n_{2}}(x_{3})&\phi_{n_{3}}(x_{3})\\
\end{vmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}\phi_{n_{1}}(x^{\prime})&\phi_{n_{2}}(x^{\prime})&\phi_{n_{3}}(x^{\prime})\\
\phi_{n_{1}}(R)&\phi_{n_{2}}(R)&\phi_{n_{3}}(R)\\
\phi_{n_{1}}(x_{3})&\phi_{n_{2}}(x_{3})&\phi_{n_{3}}(x_{3})\\
\end{vmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z^{-1}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}e^{-\beta(\varepsilon_{n_{1}}+\varepsilon_{n_{2}}+\varepsilon_{n_{3}})}\left[\phi_{n_{1}}^{2}\left(\frac{\delta x}{2}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{2}}\right)^{2}+\phi_{n_{1}}^{2}\left(\frac{\delta x}{2}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{3}}\right)^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\phi_{n_{2}}^{2}\left(\frac{\delta x}{2}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{1}}\right)^{2}+\phi_{n_{2}}^{2}\left(\frac{\delta x}{2}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{3}}\right)^{2}+\phi_{n_{3}}^{2}\left(\frac{\delta x}{2}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{1}}\right)^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\phi_{n_{3}}^{2}\left(\frac{\delta x}{2}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{2}}\right)^{2}-2\phi_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{2}}\frac{\delta x^{2}}{4}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{1}}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-2\phi_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{3}}\frac{\delta x^{2}}{4}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{1}}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{3}}-2\phi_{n_{2}}\phi_{n_{3}}\frac{\delta x^{2}}{4}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{2}}\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{3}}\right].

In the steps above we have used the differentiation relation

\partial_{R}\phi(R)=\frac{\phi(R)-\phi(R-\delta x/2)}{\delta x/2},

(129)

and the orthogonality of the
\phi
’s

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx\,\phi_{m}(x)\phi_{n}(x)=\delta_{m,n}.

(130)

Therefore, from the explicit evaluations for
N=2
and 3 particles, we can generalize the fermionic two-body density matrix for
N
particles as

\displaystyle\varrho^{(2)}_{f}(x,R;x^{\prime},R)=

\displaystyle\frac{(x^{\prime}-x)^{2}}{4}Z^{-1}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{N}}e^{-\beta\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n_{i}}}

(131)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j\neq k}\left\{\left[\phi_{n_{j}}(R)\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{k}}(R)\right]^{2}-\phi_{n_{j}}(R)\phi_{n_{k}}(R)\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{j}}(R)\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{k}}(R)\right\}.

Consequently, we have

\varrho^{(1)}(x,x^{\prime})\approx\frac{|x^{\prime}-x|^{3}}{3}F(R),

(132)

with the definition

\displaystyle F(R):=

\displaystyle Z^{-1}\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{N}}e^{-\beta\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n_{i}}}\sum_{j\neq k}\left\{\left[\phi_{n_{j}}(R)\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{k}}(R)\right]^{2}-\phi_{n_{j}}(R)\phi_{n_{k}}(R)\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{j}}(R)\partial_{R}\phi_{n_{k}}(R)\right\}.

(133)

Now we will use our analysis of the one-body density matrix in order to inspect the momentum distribution

n(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx^{\prime}\;e^{ik(x-x^{\prime})}\rho^{(1)}(x,x^{\prime}).

(134)

Let

R=\frac{x+x^{\prime}}{2},\qquad s=x-x^{\prime},\qquad dx\,dx^{\prime}=dR\,ds,

so that

n(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dR\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ds\;e^{iks}\,\rho^{(1)}\left(R+\frac{s}{2},\,R-\frac{s}{2}\right).

(135)

The short-distance condition,
|s|\to 0
, gives

\rho^{(1)}\left(R+\frac{s}{2},R-\frac{s}{2}\right)\simeq\frac{|s|^{3}}{3}\,F(R).

Hence, for large
|k|
,

\displaystyle n(k)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int dR\,\frac{F(R)}{3}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ds\;e^{iks}|s|^{3}.

(136)

Now we make use of the asymptotic behaviour of the Fourier transform of
|x-x_{0}|^{a-1}f(x)
,

\int dx\,e^{-ik(x-x_{0})}|x-x_{0}|^{a-1}f(x)=\frac{2}{k^{a}}f(x_{0})\cos\left(\frac{\pi a}{2}\right)\Gamma(a).

(137)

Taking
a=4
,
f:=1
,
x_{0}=0
, we get

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ds\;e^{iks}|s|^{3}=\frac{2}{k^{4}}\cos(2\pi)\Gamma(4)=\frac{12}{k^{4}}.

(138)

Therefore,

\displaystyle n(k)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int dR\,\frac{F(R)}{3}\,\frac{12}{k^{4}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{1}{k^{4}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dR\,F(R).

(139)

Finally, with
\mathcal{C}=\lim_{k\to\infty}k^{4}n(k)
,

\mathcal{C}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dR\,F(R).

(140)

Appendix B
Kernel representation

Here we derive the kernel representation for the contact.
For that, we work with our integrand

\displaystyle F_{N}(x)

\displaystyle:=Z_{N}^{-1}\sum_{n_{1}<\cdots<n_{N}}e^{-\beta\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n_{i}}}\sum_{j\neq k}\left\{\left[\phi_{n_{j}}(x)\,\partial_{x}\phi_{n_{k}}(x)\right]^{2}-\phi_{n_{j}}(x)\phi_{n_{k}}(x)\,\partial_{x}\phi_{n_{j}}(x)\partial_{x}\phi_{n_{k}}(x)\right\},

(141)

with the canonical partition function

Z_{N}=\sum_{n_{1}<\cdots<n_{N}}\exp\left(-\beta\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{n_{i}}\right).

(142)

We start by rewriting the canonical sum using occupation variables. For that,
let us introduce occupation numbers
\eta_{n}\in\{0,1\}
with the fixed-
N
constraint

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\eta_{n}=N,\qquad E[\eta]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\eta_{n}\varepsilon_{n}.

(143)

Then the canonical partition function becomes

Z_{N}=\sum_{\{\eta\}}e^{-\beta E[\eta]}\,\delta_{\sum_{n}\eta_{n},\,N},

(144)

and the sum over occupied levels can be rewritten as

\sum_{j\neq k}(\cdots)=\sum_{m\neq n}\eta_{m}\eta_{n}\,(\cdots)_{m,n},

where
(\cdots)_{m,n}
means: replace
(n_{j},n_{k})
by
(m,n)
.
We define the symmetric kernel-like building block

A_{mn}(x):=\left[\phi_{m}(x)\phi_{n}^{\prime}(x)\right]^{2}-\phi_{m}(x)\phi_{n}(x)\phi_{m}^{\prime}(x)\phi_{n}^{\prime}(x).

(145)

Then (
141
) becomes

F_{N}(x)=\sum_{m\neq n}\left\langle\eta_{m}\eta_{n}\right\rangle_{N}\,A_{mn}(x),

(146)

with the canonical expectation

\langle\mathcal{O}(\eta)\rangle_{N}:=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\sum_{\{\eta\}}e^{-\beta E[\eta]}\,\delta_{\sum_{n}\eta_{n},\,N}\,\mathcal{O}(\eta).

(147)

Note that we could also include the
m=n
terms in (
146
)
since
A_{nn}(x)=0
identically.

The grand partition function
\Xi(z)
is defined in
(
7
), with the canonical
Z_{N}
recovered through
the contour representation (
8
). Similarly, for any
function of occupations
\mathcal{O}(\eta)
,

\sum_{\{\eta\}}e^{-\beta E[\eta]}\,\delta_{\sum\eta,N}\,\mathcal{O}(\eta)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{dz}{z^{N+1}}\;\sum_{\{\eta\}}e^{-\beta E[\eta]}z^{\sum_{n}\eta_{n}}\,\mathcal{O}(\eta).

(148)

Now, for fixed
z
, the occupations are independent Bernoulli random variables with

\mathbb{P}_{z}(\eta_{n}=1)=f_{n}(z),\qquad f_{n}(z):=\frac{ze^{-\beta\varepsilon_{n}}}{1+ze^{-\beta\varepsilon_{n}}}.

Equivalently, for fixed
z
,

\sum_{\{\eta\}}e^{-\beta E[\eta]}z^{\sum\eta}=\Xi(z),\qquad\sum_{\{\eta\}}e^{-\beta E[\eta]}z^{\sum\eta}\,\eta_{m}\eta_{n}=\Xi(z)\,f_{m}(z)f_{n}(z)\quad(m\neq n).

(149)

Therefore, for
m\neq n
,

\langle\eta_{m}\eta_{n}\rangle_{N}=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{dz}{z^{N+1}}\;\Xi(z)\,f_{m}(z)f_{n}(z).

(150)

Plugging (
150
) into (
146
) yields an exact contour representation:

\displaystyle F_{N}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\neq n}A_{mn}(x)\,\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{dz}{z^{N+1}}\;\Xi(z)\,f_{m}(z)f_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{dz}{z^{N+1}}\;\Xi(z)\,\sum_{m\neq n}f_{m}(z)f_{n}(z)\,A_{mn}(x).

(151)

The final step is to define the
z
-kernel

K_{z}(x,y):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}(z)\,\phi_{n}(x)\phi_{n}(y),

(152)

together with its associated local quantities

\displaystyle\rho_{z}(x)

\displaystyle:=K_{z}(x,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}(z)\,\phi_{n}(x)^{2},

(153)

\displaystyle S_{z}(x)

\displaystyle:=\partial_{y}K_{z}(x,y)\big|_{y=x}=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}(z)\,\phi_{n}(x)\phi_{n}^{\prime}(x),

(154)

\displaystyle\kappa_{z}(x)

\displaystyle:=\partial_{x}\partial_{y}K_{z}(x,y)\big|_{y=x}=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}(z)\,\phi_{n}^{\prime}(x)^{2}.

(155)

Expanding
\rho_{z}\kappa_{z}-S_{z}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\rho_{z}(x)\kappa_{z}(x)-S_{z}(x)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}f_{m}(z)f_{n}(z)\left[\phi_{m}(x)^{2}\phi_{n}^{\prime}(x)^{2}-\phi_{m}(x)\phi_{m}^{\prime}(x)\phi_{n}(x)\phi_{n}^{\prime}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}f_{m}(z)f_{n}(z)\,A_{mn}(x).

(156)

But
A_{nn}(x)=0
, so

\sum_{m\neq n}f_{m}(z)f_{n}(z)\,A_{mn}(x)=\rho_{z}(x)\kappa_{z}(x)-S_{z}(x)^{2}.

(157)

Therefore, the inner sum in (
151
) collapses to a kernel functional:

F_{N}(x)=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{dz}{z^{N+1}}\;\Xi(z)\,\left[\rho_{z}(x)\kappa_{z}(x)-S_{z}(x)^{2}\right].

(158)

Equivalently, entirely in terms of
K_{z}
,

F_{N}(x)=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{dz}{z^{N+1}}\;\Xi(z)\,\left[K_{z}(x,x)\,\partial_{x}\partial_{y}K_{z}(x,y)\big|_{y=x}-\left(\partial_{y}K_{z}(x,y)\big|_{y=x}\right)^{2}\right].

(159)

B.1
Pair representation

For a fixed Slater determinant built from orbitals

\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{N}\}
, define the configuration kernel

K_{\mathrm{config}}(x,y)=\sum_{a:\,n_{a}=1}\phi_{a}(x)\,\phi_{a}(y),

(160)

and the local quantities

\rho=K(x,x),\qquad S=\partial_{y}K(x,y)\big|_{y=x},\qquad\kappa=\partial_{x}\partial_{y}K(x,y)\big|_{y=x}.

(161)

For any set of occupation numbers
\{n_{a}\}\in\{0,1\}
,

\sum_{j\neq k}A_{n_{j},n_{k}}=\rho_{\mathrm{config}}\,\kappa_{\mathrm{config}}-S_{\mathrm{config}}^{2},

(162)

where
A_{a,b}=(\phi_{a}\,\phi_{b}^{\prime})^{2}-\phi_{a}\,\phi_{b}\,\phi_{a}^{\prime}\,\phi_{b}^{\prime}
.
Expanding the right-hand side we have

\displaystyle\rho\,\kappa-S^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{a}n_{a}\,\phi_{a}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{b}n_{b}\,(\phi_{b}^{\prime})^{2}\right)-\left(\sum_{a}n_{a}\,\phi_{a}\,\phi_{a}^{\prime}\right)^{2}

(163)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b}n_{a}\,n_{b}\left[\phi_{a}^{2}\,(\phi_{b}^{\prime})^{2}-\phi_{a}\,\phi_{a}^{\prime}\,\phi_{b}\,\phi_{b}^{\prime}\right]

(164)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b}n_{a}\,n_{b}\,A_{a,b}(x).

(165)

Since
A_{a,a}=\phi_{a}^{2}\,(\phi_{a}^{\prime})^{2}-\phi_{a}^{2}\,(\phi_{a}^{\prime})^{2}=0
, the diagonal terms
vanish and the double sum reduces to

\sum_{a\neq b}n_{a}\,n_{b}\,A_{a,b}
.

Symmetrising,
A_{a,b}+A_{b,a}=(\phi_{a}\,\phi_{b}^{\prime}-\phi_{b}\,\phi_{a}^{\prime})^{2}
, so the
contribution from each unordered pair
(a,b)
with
a<b
is
the squared Wronskian. Defining the pair integral

J_{ab}=\int dx\,\left(\phi_{a}\,\phi_{b}^{\prime}-\phi_{b}\,\phi_{a}^{\prime}\right)^{2}.

(166)

Averaging (
162
) over the canonical ensemble,

\mathcal{C}_{N}^{\mathrm{CE}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{a<b}\langle n_{a}\,n_{b}\rangle_{\mathrm{CE}}\,J_{ab}.

(167)

If levels are statistically independent
(as in the GCE), then for
a\neq b
,

\langle n_{a}\,n_{b}\rangle_{\mathrm{GCE}}=\bar{n}_{a}\,\bar{n}_{b}

where
\bar{n}_{a}=(1+e^{\beta(\varepsilon_{a}-\mu)})^{-1}
.
The contact takes the kernel form

\mathcal{C}_{N}^{\mathrm{GCE}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\bar{\mathbf{n}}^{T}J\,\bar{\mathbf{n}}.

(168)

The fixed-
N
constraint introduces correlations:

\langle n_{a}\,n_{b}\rangle_{\mathrm{CE}}=\bar{n}_{a}^{\mathrm{CE}}\,\bar{n}_{b}^{\mathrm{CE}}+\mathrm{Cov}_{\mathrm{CE}}(n_{a},n_{b}).

(169)

The covariance is negative (anti-bunching from the
particle-number constraint) and scales as

\mathrm{Cov}_{\mathrm{CE}}(n_{a},n_{b})\sim-\bar{n}_{a}(1-\bar{n}_{a})\,\bar{n}_{b}(1-\bar{n}_{b})/\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{GCE}}(N)
.
At finite
\tau
, the total ensemble difference

\Delta\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{C}_{N}^{\mathrm{CE}}-\mathcal{C}_{N}^{\mathrm{GCE}}
scales as

\sim N^{3/2}
, and therefore cannot be neglected
at subleading order.

An exact canonical computation that avoids enumerating
all
\binom{M}{N}
configurations uses the fugacity
contour integral. Define the grand partition function

\Xi(z)=\prod_{n=0}^{M-1}(1+z\,q_{n})
with

q_{n}=e^{-\beta\varepsilon_{n}}
, and the fugacity-dependent
occupation
\bar{n}_{a}(z)=z\,q_{a}/(1+z\,q_{a})
. Then

\mathcal{C}_{N}^{\mathrm{CE}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\,\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\,\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{dz}{z^{N+1}}\,\Xi(z)\,G(z),

(170)

where

G(z)=\sum_{a,b}\bar{n}_{a}(z)\,\bar{n}_{b}(z)\,R_{ab},\qquad R_{ab}=\int dx\,\left[\phi_{a}^{2}\,(\phi_{b}^{\prime})^{2}-\phi_{a}\,\phi_{a}^{\prime}\,\phi_{b}\,\phi_{b}^{\prime}\right].

(171)

Note that
J_{ab}=R_{ab}+R_{ba}
.

At fixed complex fugacity
z
, the occupation numbers are
independent (the kernel form is exact), so the integrand
is well-defined. The contour integral projects onto the

N
-particle sector.

Appendix C
Numerical procedure

Our goal here is to provide details on the numerical procedures to compute the contact from eq. (
5
).
We begin by defining the matrix

R_{ab}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\,\left[\phi_{a}(x)^{2}\,\phi_{b}^{\prime}(x)^{2}-\phi_{a}(x)\,\phi_{a}^{\prime}(x)\,\phi_{b}(x)\,\phi_{b}^{\prime}(x)\right]\,,

(172)

truncated to
M
single-particle levels (
a,b=0,\ldots,M{-}1
) and
evaluated by the trapezoidal rule on a uniform grid of

N_{x}=\max(4000,\,6M)
points spanning
[-L,\,L]
with

L=\sqrt{2M}+6
. The wave functions
\phi_{n}(x)
and their
derivatives are generated via the three-term recurrence

\phi_{n+1}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n{+}1}}\;x\,\phi_{n}(x)-\sqrt{\frac{n}{n{+}1}}\;\phi_{n-1}(x)\,,\qquad\phi_{n}^{\prime}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{n}{2}}\;\phi_{n-1}(x)-\sqrt{\frac{n{+}1}{2}}\;\phi_{n+1}(x)\,.

(173)

With the arrays
\phi_{a}(x_{i})
and
\phi_{a}^{\prime}(x_{i})
precomputed,
R_{ab}

is assembled as
R=P-Q
via dense matrix products of cost
\mathcal{O}(M^{2}N_{x})
.

The canonical contact is expressed as a ratio of contour integrals,

\mathcal{C}_{N}=\frac{2}{\pi}\;\mathrm{Re}\left[\frac{\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\theta}}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\theta}-1}W_{k}\,G_{k}\,e^{-iN\theta_{k}}}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\theta}}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\theta}-1}W_{k}\,e^{-iN\theta_{k}}}\right],

(174)

with
\theta_{k}=2\pi k/N_{\theta}
, contour points
z_{k}=r\,e^{i\theta_{k}}
, and

W_{k}=\exp\left[\log\Xi(z_{k})-\log\Xi(z_{0})\right],\qquad G_{k}=\sum_{a,b=0}^{M_{\mathrm{use}}-1}\bar{n}_{a}(z_{k})\,\bar{n}_{b}(z_{k})\,R_{ab}\,.

(175)

Here
\Xi(z)=\prod_{n=0}^{M-1}(1+z\,e^{-\beta\varepsilon_{n}})
,

\bar{n}_{a}(z)=z\,e^{-\beta\varepsilon_{a}}/(1+z\,e^{-\beta\varepsilon_{a}})
,
and the saddle-point radius
r=e^{\beta\mu}
is determined by

\sum_{n}\bar{n}_{n}(r)=N
. The number of contour points

N_{\theta}
is chosen large enough that doubling it changes

\mathcal{C}_{N}
in (
174
) by less than
10^{-5}
in relative
units; in practice
N_{\theta}=256
is sufficient for
N\leq 100

across the entire
\tau\in[0,10]
window.

Crucially, the partition function
\Xi(z)
and the saddle-point condition
use all
M_{\mathrm{full}}=N+14\,\tau N+20
levels needed for
convergence (the coefficient
14
corresponds to retaining levels with
Boltzmann weight as low as
e^{-14}\sim 10^{-6}
at the highest
relevant temperature; the additive offset
20
handles the low-
\tau

band edge), while the spatial integral
G_{k}
uses only

M_{\mathrm{use}}=\min(M_{\mathrm{full}},\,M_{\max})
levels, where

M_{\max}
is the size of the precomputed
R
matrix.
This ensures the entire calculation remains in the canonical
ensemble—no grand-canonical fallback is used.

C.0.1
Spatial truncation at high
\tau

The spatial truncation in
G_{k}
deserves careful comment, because its
behaviour at high
\tau
is more subtle than a naive per-level estimate
suggests. A first-pass bound on the truncation error is the occupation
of the highest retained level at the saddle,
\bar{n}_{M_{\max}}(r)
. At
the worst corner
(N=100,\,\tau=10)
with
M_{\max}=4000
this gives

\bar{n}_{M_{\max}}\sim 10^{-3}
, suggesting a relative deviation of order

10^{-3}
. The actual truncation error at that corner is closer to

15\%
. The discrepancy arises because the spatial matrix elements

R_{ab}
in (
172
) grow as
\sqrt{ab}
for large indices, so
the contribution of pairs
(a,b)
with
a,b\gtrsim M_{\max}
is
enhanced by a factor
\sqrt{ab}
relative to the bare occupation,
and the number of contributing pairs is
\sim(M_{\mathrm{full}}-M_{\max})^{2}
.
The cumulative truncation therefore scales linearly in

1-M_{\max}/M_{\mathrm{full}}
rather than with the per-level occupation.

The empirically clean criterion for
G_{k}
to reproduce the full-level
result to better than
\sim 0.05\%
is

M_{\max}\;\geq\;1.2\,M_{\mathrm{full}}(N,\tau)\;=\;1.2\,(N+14\tau N+20).

(176)

Below this threshold the truncation bias is monotonic and well-modelled
by
\mathcal{C}_{N}^{(M_{\max})}/\mathcal{C}_{N}^{(\infty)}\simeq 1-c_{\tau}(1-M_{\max}/M_{\mathrm{full}})

with
c_{\tau}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
and weakly
\tau
-dependent. Crossing

M_{\max}=M_{\mathrm{full}}
produces the steep transition to the converged
value: at
(N=20,\,\tau=10,\,M_{\mathrm{full}}=2840)
, raising
M_{\max}

from
2000
(
M_{\max}/M_{\mathrm{full}}=0.70
) to
2840
shifts

\mathcal{C}_{N}^{(M_{\max})}
from
978.87
to
979.10
, where it plateaus
(
979.10
at
M_{\max}=3500
). This is the converged value; the
prediction of the scaling law (
12
),
979.27
,
then differs from
it by
0.02\%
, consistent with the expected
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
subleading
corrections to the scaling law.

This
\tau
-dependent saturation is the reason the high-
\tau
ratio plot
behaves differently from the low-
\tau
one even at fixed
M_{\max}
.
At
\tau=0.05
and
N=100
,
M_{\mathrm{full}}=190
, so any

M_{\max}\gtrsim 250
saturates the criterion (
176
) and
the plot collapses cleanly to unity. At
\tau=10
and the same
N
,

M_{\mathrm{full}}=14{,}120
, so
M_{\max}=4000
–
5000
is well below
threshold and the truncation bias dominates the apparent residual. We
therefore restrict the high-
\tau
verification plot to the

(N,\tau)
corner satisfying (

C.2
Padé approximants

C.2.1
Leading coefficient

We write

A_{\mathrm{P}}(\tau)=\frac{a_{0}\left(1+\tilde{\alpha}\,\tau^{2}\right)}{\left(1+\beta\,\tau^{2}\right)^{3/4}}\;\times\;\left[1+\frac{c\,\tau^{2}}{(1+d\,\tau)^{3}}+\frac{e\,\tau^{3}}{(1+d\,\tau)^{4}}\right],

(177)

where
a_{0}=128\sqrt{2}/(45\pi^{3})
.
The base factor (first fraction) is a generalised Padé with fractional
exponent
3/4
in the denominator, which produces the correct

\sqrt{\tau}
growth at large
\tau
.
The multiplicative correction (square bracket) acts as a localised
“bump” that equals unity at both
\tau=0
and
\tau\to\infty
, and
improves accuracy in the crossover region
\tau\sim 1
.

The parameter
\beta
is determined analytically.
At small
\tau
, expanding (
177
) gives

A_{\mathrm{P}}=a_{0}[1+(\tilde{\alpha}-\frac{3}{4}\beta+c)\,\tau^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{3})]
,
so setting
\tilde{\alpha}=a_{2}/a_{0}+\frac{3}{4}\beta-c
enforces the
Sommerfeld result (
39
).
At large
\tau
,

A_{\mathrm{P}}\to a_{0}\,\tilde{\alpha}/\beta^{3/4}\,\sqrt{\tau}
,
so matching (
51
) requires

a_{0}\,\tilde{\alpha}/\beta^{3/4}=1/\pi^{3/2}
.
Eliminating
\tilde{\alpha}
yields a quartic for
t:=\beta^{1/4}
:

\frac{3\,a_{0}}{4}\,t^{4}-\frac{t^{3}}{a_{0}\,\pi^{3/2}}+\frac{a_{2}}{a_{0}}=0\,,\quad\beta=t^{4}\,,

(178)

whose smallest positive root gives
\beta\approx 0.0798
.
The remaining parameters are obtained by minimax fit (minimising the
maximum relative error) to the numerical evaluation
of (
32
):

c=1.4435\,,\qquad e=2.000\,,\qquad d=0.8793\,.

(179)

The resulting maximum relative error of
A_{\mathrm{P}}(\tau)
with
respect to the numerical evaluation of (
32
) is

\max_{\tau\in[0,10]}\left|\frac{A_{\mathrm{P}}(\tau)-A(\tau)}{A(\tau)}\right|\simeq 4.5\%,

(180)

with the worst-case location in the crossover region
\tau\sim 1
.

C.2.2
Subleading coefficient

For
B(\tau)
we use a form that respects both the linear low-
\tau

slope and the
\sqrt{\tau}
high-
\tau
asymptote. Setting

\sigma:=\sqrt{\tau}
, we write

B_{\mathrm{P}}(\tau)=-\frac{\sigma}{\pi^{3/2}}\;\frac{a_{1}\sigma+a_{2}\sigma^{2}+a_{3}\sigma^{3}+a_{4}\sigma^{4}}{1+d_{1}\sigma+d_{2}\sigma^{2}+d_{3}\sigma^{3}+d_{4}\sigma^{4}}\,,

(181)

with two analytic constraints fixing the asymptotic behaviour:

a_{1}=-\pi^{3/2}\,b_{1}=\frac{16\sqrt{2}}{3\pi^{3/2}}\simeq 1.3545\,,\qquad a_{4}=d_{4}\,,

(182)

where the first constraint enforces the Sommerfeld leading slope

B(\tau)\to b_{1}\tau
from (
91
) and the
second enforces
B_{\mathrm{P}}(\tau)\to-\sqrt{\tau}/\pi^{3/2}
at
large
\tau
from (
103
). The
remaining parameters
a_{2},a_{3},d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4}
are determined by
minimax fit to the numerical evaluation of (
67
) on

\tau\in[0.01,10]
:

\displaystyle a_{2}=-0.4116,\quad a_{3}=0.5126,\quad a_{4}=8.6085,

(183)

\displaystyle d_{1}=-0.3860,\quad d_{2}=1.7283,\quad d_{3}=0.2215,\quad d_{4}=8.6085\,.

The resulting maximum relative error of
B_{\mathrm{P}}(\tau)
with
respect to the numerical evaluation of (
67
) is

\max_{\tau\in[0.01,\,10]}\left|\frac{B_{\mathrm{P}}(\tau)-B(\tau)}{B(\tau)}\right|\simeq 0.13\%,

(184)

with the worst-case location at the boundaries of the fitting
interval. The form (
181
) is asymptotically correct in
both regimes by construction: numerically,

B_{\mathrm{P}}(\tau)\to-\sqrt{\tau}/\pi^{3/2}
to within
0.1\%

already by
\tau=10
, and the extrapolation remains accurate
indefinitely (at
\tau=100
,
B_{\mathrm{P}}\simeq-1.798
versus

-\sqrt{100}/\pi^{3/2}\simeq-1.796
). The Padé (
181
) is
therefore a uniformly valid closed-form substitute for the integral
representation (
67
) across the full physical
temperature window.

In analogy with the ratio
\mathcal{R}_{N}
defined in (
68
),
we introduce its Padé counterpart

\mathcal{R}_{N}^{(\mathrm{P})}(\tau):=\frac{\mathcal{C}_{N}(\tau)}{A_{\mathrm{P}}(\tau)\,N^{5/2}+B_{\mathrm{P}}(\tau)\,N^{3/2}},

(185)

in which the universal scaling functions
A(\tau)
and
B(\tau)
are
replaced by their Padé approximants
A_{\mathrm{P}}(\tau)
and

B_{\mathrm{P}}(\tau)
from (
177
) and (
181
).
