Trends in dental caries and related factors among second-grade students in Dongying District, 2018–2022
Authors: Su Shuxia, Gai Zhihui, Qin Jianyong, Zong Min, Zhou Rui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe incidence of dental caries among children in Dongying City is very high. In 2018, the city launched a regional policy that provided free pit and fissure sealants and school-based oral health education. This study aims to assess the five-year impact of a regional public oral health policy.MethodsThis study adopted a census sampling method to include eligible research subjects. Through oral examinations and parent questionnaires, chi-square tests, variance analysis, and trend tests were used to evaluate the effectiveness of the intervention measures for second-grade students.ResultsThe survey results showed that the overall tooth decay rate decreased from 86.4% in 2018 to 84.6% in 2022, and the difference was at the borderline significance level (P = 0.05). Notably, the caries rate and Decayed, Missing due to caries, and Filled Teeth index (DMFT) for permanent teeth demonstrated a significant decline (P < 0.05), whereas those for deciduous teeth showed minimal changes. Although the filling rate for dental caries increased in 2022, it remained relatively low. The key performance indicators all showed significant trends (P < 0.05).ConclusionsThe oral health intervention measures implemented in Dongying City effectively reduced the level of permanent tooth caries among second-grade students. However, the improvement of deciduous tooth caries was not significant, and the filling rate remained low. It is necessary to strengthen the long-term oral health behavior cultivation of both families and schools, enhance the supervision of the intervention projects, and improve the treatment work in key areas to enhance the effect of caries prevention.

Introduction

Dental caries, one of the most prevalent chronic diseases among children globally, significantly impairs their quality of life [1, 2]. The disease causes irreversible damage to dental hard tissues [3], and if left untreated, may lead to premature loss or prolonged retention of deciduous teeth. These outcomes can disrupt the normal eruption and alignment of permanent dentition, adversely affecting occlusal development and maxillofacial growth. Furthermore, dental caries can adversely affect children’s mental health. Pain and compromised chewing function may restrict dietary intake, leading to nutritional deficiencies and hindering overall growth and development [4–7].

Dental caries is highly prevalent among children (3–14 years old) [8]. Second-grade students (7–8 years old) are in the mixed dentition phase, during which deciduous teeth are gradually replaced by permanent teeth. Effective intervention during this critical period is essential for ensuring optimal oral health and overall development [9]. At this age, children’s brushing and dietary habits are still developing; this renders them highly reliant on oral health guidance, the most prominent source of which is parental awareness and behavior [10].

In recent years, the Chinese government has prioritized children’s oral health, implementing a series of policies aimed at reducing the prevalence of dental caries. Dongying city, a key urban center in Shandong Province located within the Yellow River Delta, has a high consumption of sugary foods among its residents, particularly children. This dietary profile contributes to a high-caries-risk environment. Since 2018, Dongying city has actively aligned with national policies by offering free pit and fissure sealing programs for second-grade students and enhancing oral health education initiatives. However, the long-term quantitative impact of these integrated interventions on caries prevalence and related behavioral habits requires systematic assessment.

Therefore, this quantitative study aims to evaluate the longitudinal trends in dental caries prevalence and indices among second-grade students in Dongying from 2018 to 2022. By analyzing annual oral examination data and parent questionnaires, this study seeks to provide evidence for the effectiveness of the current interventions and to identify areas for future improvement in caries prevention strategies.

Methods

Study participants

As part of the pit and fissure sealing program in Dongying city, oral examinations were conducted on second-grade students from 13 primary schools in Dongying District between 2018 and 2022, accompanied by the administration of parent questionnaires. The research subjects each year were the second-year students. This research plan was reviewed and approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Shengli Oilfield Central Hospital (ethical batch number: yxLL202516101). A census sampling method was employed, aiming to include the entire eligible population rather than a calculated sample size. The final sample size was determined by the total number of registered students who met the inclusion criteria and participated each year. Inclusion criteria were: (1) registered as a second-grade student in one of the 13 participating schools during the study years; (2) provided with both an oral examination and a completed parent questionnaire; (3) obtained parental informed consent for participation. Exclusion criteria: (1) students with severe systemic diseases or cognitive/behavioral disorders who are unable to cooperate for oral examination; (2) students who have not undergone a comprehensive oral examination or have not completed the questionnaire survey.

Research methods

The oral examination was conducted in accordance with the “Basic Methods for Oral Health Surveys (Fifth Edition)” of the World Health Organization [11]. Before the survey began, all staff received standardized training and calibration. The training included a theoretical review of diagnostic criteria, practical exercises with dental models, and discussions to minimize diagnostic variability. Each school assigned two experienced dentists (with at least 5 years of experience in pediatric oral care), one of whom was responsible for conducting the examination and the other for verifying the results. Sterilized mouth mirrors and probes were used, and a tooth-by-tooth examination was conducted according to the WHO standards. Dental caries was defined as a significant cavity, softening or pigmentation on the tooth surface, accompanied by sensitivity upon probing; it included mild, moderate, and severe caries, but excluded teeth with surface staining of the enamel.

The survey questionnaire drew on the content of the “Fourth National Oral Health Epidemiological Survey” [12]. It collected information on the social demographic background of children, oral health habits, and dietary behaviors. The questionnaire was distributed to parents through schools. Data were systematically collected and organized. Questionnaires that were not fully completed (with ≥ 3 key pieces of information missing) were excluded.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was conducted via SPSS 20.0. Data from the oral examinations and questionnaires were systematically collected and organized. A unique identifier was used to link each child’s clinical data with their questionnaire. Measurement data (such as average caries index) were compared among different years using one-way analysis of variance; count data (such as caries prevalence rate) were compared among different years using chi-square test; trend analysis was conducted using Cochran-Armitage trend test. The results were considered significant at P < 0.05.

Caries prevalence

The prevalence of dental caries is shown in Fig. 2. The overall prevalence of dental caries among the participants from 2018 to 2022 was 85.2%, showing a downward trend. The prevalence of dental caries in permanent teeth (as shown in Fig. 2) continued to decline every year from 2018 to 2021. According to chi-square test, the caries incidence rate of permanent teeth decreased significantly from 46.8% in 2018 to 30.9% in 2022 (χ²= 100.0, P < 0.001). During the study period, no statistically significant change was observed in the prevalence of dental caries in primary teeth (Fig. 2).

Mean caries index

The trends in the average dental caries index from 2018 to 2022 are shown in Fig. 3. The overall mean index across the five years was 5.04. From 2018 to 2021, the index showed a continuous downward trend (P < 0.05), and in 2022, it slightly increased compared to the previous year. The overall caries incidence rate in 2022 was 0.30 lower than the average value in 2018, and the difference was statistically significant (t = 2.46, P = 0.014) through the one-way analysis of variance. From 2018 to 2021, the average dental caries index of permanent teeth decreased each year, with a small rebound in 2022, but it was still significantly lower than in 2018 (P < 0.05; Fig. 3). The average dental caries index of deciduous teeth did not show statistically significant changes from 2018 to 2022 (P > 0.05; Fig. 3).

Discussion

Our research conducted an in-depth investigation into the overall dental caries situation of second-grade students in Dongying area from 2018 to 2022. The survey results showed that the incidence of permanent tooth caries and overall dental caries decreased significantly over the five years, while the overall trend of caries in deciduous teeth did not change significantly. The overall filling rate of dental caries remained at a relatively low level. This suggests that while preventing dental caries in permanent teeth, we should further understand the causes of dental caries in deciduous teeth and take preventive measures to ensure that the problem of dental caries among children in this area is effectively prevented and alleviated. Furthermore, the five-year trend of the permanent tooth DMFT index shows that the proportion of dental fillings has increased, the proportion of tooth decay has decreased, and the number of missing teeth has remained at a low level. This indicates that the overall oral health problems of second-grade students in Dongying area have improved over the past five years.

Although the global incidence of dental caries in children is declining, it remains a prevalent chronic childhood disease [13, 14].Research has demonstrated that individuals with a history of dental caries face a substantially elevated risk of future caries, with the risk increasing exponentially [15, 16]. A systematic review and meta-analysis study further supported this. The study found that children with early dental caries had a 3.22 times higher risk of developing permanent tooth caries compared to ordinary children [17]. Therefore, preventing dental caries is more cost-effective than treating it [18, 19]. Since 2018, the incidence of dental caries among children in Dongying has remained relatively high. With economic development, both national and local governments have increasingly prioritized addressing dental caries in children. One of the core measures of local government policies for preventing dental caries is free pit and fissure sealing program, which can provide physical protection for pits and cracks on the occlusal surface. Its application targets are the first permanent molars that emerge in children aged 6 to 8. Studies have shown that the first permanent molars are the teeth most prone to tooth decay, and their health condition can affect the healthy development of subsequent teeth. Therefore, preventing initial tooth decay is of vital importance [20, 21]. Evidence shows that during a 2–3 year follow-up period, this method can reduce the incidence of dental caries on normal occlusal surfaces by 76% [22].In 2018, Dongying city introduced a free pit and fissure sealing program for school-age children, mainly for the protection of the first permanent molar of children aged 6–8 years old to prevent the occurrence of dental caries.Medical institutions performed on-site sealant applications in schools and provided oral health education to second-grade students, their parents, and teachers. A downward trend in the overall prevalence of dental caries was observed during the five-year period from 2018 to 2022. This key finding indicated that apart from the government’s implementation of free pit and sulcus sealing, parents’ oral health knowledge and attitudes, children’s dietary habits and oral hygiene behaviors had improved significantly. These positive behavioral and knowledge shifts correspond to a marked decline in both the prevalence of dental caries and the mean caries index among second-grade students in Dongying District. Concurrently, an increase in the dental filling rate was observed within the composition ratio of decayed, missing, and filled teeth, further affirming the effectiveness of the interventions implemented during this period. Notably, the caries prevalence and mean caries index for both permanent and deciduous teeth tended to decrease during this period.

However, a slight increase in the prevalence of caries in permanent teeth in 2022 may be attributed to the COVID-19 pandemic. Takuro Yonezu’s research shows that occlusal caries most commonly occur between 24 and 72 months after the appearance of the first permanent molar, indicating that caries caused by bad habits during the COVID period begin to show up at this time [23]. During the pandemic, home quarantine measures disrupted children’s dietary patterns and oral hygiene behaviors, which is consistent with the findings of Li Zhen [24]. Furthermore, studies have also shown that 62.7% of children increased the number of meals/snacks they had each day during the lockdown [25]. Moreover, the research by Azhar Iqbal also verified this view. During the COVID period, the frequency of meals, snack time, and the consumption of sugary products such as candies, chocolates, milk, and coffee (P < 0.001) all increased significantly [26].Additionally, pandemic-related anxiety likely reduced children’s number of dental clinic visits, contributing to a decline in filling rates for deciduous teeth in 2022 compared with 2021. This is consistent with a study from Australia. During the period from March to September 2020, the number of dental services decreased by 881,454 times [27]. The COVID-19 vaccine altered the rate of saliva secretion, leading to an increase in cases of poor oral health [28]. Additionally, the COVID-19 pandemic had a significant negative impact on providing primary and secondary dental care for children around the world [29]. These adverse factors all directly or indirectly led to the rebound in the incidence of dental caries among children in 2022.

The dental caries status of second-grade children in Dongying District has improved since 2018. Caries prevalence and the mean caries index for permanent teeth declined significantly, the incidence rate of permanent tooth caries decreased from 46.8% to 30.9% over the five years. The reason for this is that pit and fissure sealing program mainly target young permanent teeth [30]. Therefore, this policy directly protects the surface of the permanent molars that are most prone to dental caries and can effectively reduce the incidence of dental caries [31]. During the mixed dentition phase, the upper and lower deciduous incisors and lateral incisors are replaced, leaving the deciduous canines, first deciduous molars, and second deciduous molars in the oral cavity. Notably, deciduous molars are particularly susceptible to caries. Research has found that the occurrence and development of deciduous tooth caries are closely related to the feeding and dietary patterns during infancy and early childhood [32, 33]. Health education in schools may be difficult to effectively reach and change the long-established behavioral patterns within families. Now, nearly one-third of parents hold the misconception that “deciduous teeth will be replaced and do not require treatment,” which contributes to the persistently high caries rate in deciduous teeth among second-grade children. At the same time, it is also related to factors such as preschool children’s dietary habits, limited parental awareness of dental treatment, and children’s fear of dental visits [34]. Furthermore,

In terms of diet, the consumption of sweet foods and sweet drinks reached its lowest point in 2021 and rebounded in 2022. These “hidden high-sugar behaviors” have persisted for a long time, prolonging the exposure time of teeth to sugar and increasing the risk of tooth decay [35]. Although oral hygiene showed a fluctuation of “decreased in 2021 and increased in 2022”, the trend of younger starting age for brushing teeth was significant, which could reduce the risk of caries in permanent teeth. In 2021, the usage rate of parents assisting brushing teeth and fluoride toothpaste decreased, which might indirectly lead to fluctuations in the same period’s caries indicators. The peak of behavioral habits in 2021 coincided with the same period’s caries indicators (such as the slowdown in the improvement of deciduous teeth caries), confirming the interference of external factors (such as the pandemic) on the “behavior-caries association”. In 2022, both rebounded simultaneously, indicating that the recovery of behavior is the key to the rebound of prevention and control effectiveness, and suggesting the need to establish a maintenance mechanism for behavior under external interference. Of the proposed three-tier networks, the measurable impact of the primary prevention tier is most pronounced. The significant decrease in caries prevalence and DMFT index of permanent teeth can be directly attributed to the high coverage of school pit and fissure closure and health education programs implemented at this level. But in contrast, the effects of secondary and tertiary levels are more difficult to quantify. The persistently low filling rate suggests that the referral pathway from primary prevention in schools to filling treatment in community clinics is inefficient. The tertiary level is responsible for complex diagnosis and treatment, which is crucial, but its direct impact on the overall caries rate is not as good as that of primary prevention because it serves a small number of people. Therefore, strengthening the primary tier is the most cost-effective strategy.

The study revealed that the prevalence rates of both permanent and deciduous tooth caries among second-grade students in Dongying District were notably high, whereas the filling rates remained low. The prevalence of caries in deciduous teeth was significantly higher than that reported in developed countries (28% in the USA [36], 43% in the UK [36], 37% in Singapore [36] and the Chinese national average (70.9%) [37], with evidence suggesting a potential link between the prevalence of caries and economic development [38, 39]. In 2022, the caries prevalence in permanent teeth was 30.9%, with a mean caries index of 0.67. This outcome exceeded the targets established in China’s Healthy Oral Action Plan [40] and the Medium and Long-Term Plan for the Prevention and Treatment of Chronic Diseases (2017–2025) [41].

For the “insufficient improvement of deciduous tooth caries” and the “resource optimization to address the disparity in service accessibility”. A “pre-school to school transition management system” is constructed: kindergartens incorporate oral examinations into their routines and offer courses on infant tooth care for parents. In primary schools, on the basis of pit and fissure sealants, additional fluoride application is provided for high-risk students, and oral health records are established for dynamic tracking. A “three-level oral service network” is established: schools set up oral health stations, communities open green channels for student visits, and the district-level coordinates experts to conduct mobile medical services, solving the problem of uneven service coverage.

This study has certain limitations. Firstly, the time range of the research (2018–2022) is determined by a specific policy cycle. This cycle is the first key implementation stage following the release of the “Healthy China 2030” planning outline. Therefore, this study was unable to include data from 2023 to 2025, which to some extent affected the timeliness of the research conclusions in the present context. Secondly, although the sample size of this study is large, the behavioral data collected from parents’ questionnaires may have recall bias or reporting bias. The future research will be extended to 2025 and incorporate post-pandemic data. Use “questionnaires+behavioral observation+dietary logs” for multi-source data to reduce bias and introduce objective indicators such as saliva fluoride content.

Conclusion

The five-year oral health intervention in Dongying City effectively reduced the prevalence of dental caries and the permanent tooth DMFT index among second-grade students, demonstrating the success of this regional policy. However, the stagnation in deciduous tooth caries and the persistently low dental filling rate highlight critical, unresolved challenges.

These findings lead to two specific recommendations for Dongying City and similar regions. First, intervention strategies must be differentiated: the successful model for permanent tooth prevention should be sustained, while new, targeted programs focusing on early childhood habits and parental education are urgently needed to address deciduous tooth caries. Second, It is suggested that the government should enhance prevention education. Incorporate oral health courses into the school curriculum system. Regularly organize educational lectures and utilize multimedia platforms to disseminate oral health knowledge. Optimize resource allocation, expand the coverage of oral health intervention programs in key areas, and improve the efficiency of the utilization of public health resources.

In conclusion, this study provides evidence-based validation for the Dongying policy’s effectiveness in prevention while pinpointing precise areas for future strengthening, specifically concerning deciduous teeth and the treatment continuum.
