The full strong coupling expansion of the cusp anomalous dimension
Zoltan Bajnok a,b , Bercel Boldis a,c , Dennis le Plat a

Abstract
We present the full transseries of the strong-coupling expansion of the cusp anomalous dimension in 𝒩 = 4 {\cal N}=4 super Yang–Mills theory. This quantity admits an exact representation as a ratio of two determinants with particularly simple strong-coupling expansions. Nonperturbative contributions are classified by partitions into distinct non-negative odd integers and obey a universal structure. The corresponding Stokes constants are computed iteratively. The resulting resurgence pattern exhibits fermionic-type behavior.

Introduction

AdS/CFT duality relates superconformal Yang–Mills theories to string
theories on anti–de Sitter backgrounds
[
1
]
.
At weak coupling, the gauge theory admits a perturbative expansion
with finite radius of convergence. At strong coupling, the semiclassical
expansion of string theory yields an asymptotic series that must be
completed by nonperturbative contributions associated with additional
saddle points. Bridging these complementary regimes has relied crucially
on integrability
[
2
]
.

A central observable in establishing this correspondence is the cusp
anomalous dimension
\Gamma_{\mathrm{cusp}}
, see
[
3
]

and references therein. It governs the ultraviolet divergence of lightlike
cusped Wilson loops, the infrared divergence of gluon scattering amplitudes,
and the leading logarithmic growth of twist-two anomalous dimensions
at large spin, which are dual to folded spinning strings. Integrability
leads to an exact integral equation describing
\Gamma_{\mathrm{cusp}}

at arbitrary coupling
[
4
]
. Its notoriously intricate
strong-coupling expansion reveals nonperturbative sectors
[
5
,

,
7
]
,
whose first terms were shown to satisfy resurgence relations
[
8
,

,
10
]
.
It was further observed that
\Gamma_{\mathrm{cusp}}
admits a representation
as
\Gamma_{\mathrm{cusp}}=(1+K)_{11}^{-1}
, where
K
is a semi-infinite
matrix with entries constructed from a symbol and Bessel functions.
The associated determinant generalizes the Tracy–Widom distribution

[
11
,

,

,
14
]

and a one-parameter family of (tilted) deformations appears in various
scattering amplitudes
[

.

Recent progress has uncovered the full nonperturbative structure of
these generalized Tracy–Widom distributions and their matrix extensions

,

,

.
In this Letter we apply these developments to the cusp anomalous dimension
and demonstrate further remarkable simplifications.

2
Transseries for the cusp

The cusp anomalous dimension can be written as a ratio

\Gamma_{\mathrm{cusp}}(g)=\frac{g}{4\pi}\frac{D_{1}(g)}{D_{0}(g)}\quad;\quad g=2\sqrt{\lambda}

where
\lambda=g_{Y\!M}^{2}N
is the ’t Hooft coupling. The quantity

D_{\ell}(g)
is proportional to a determinant of a semi-infinite
matrix whose matrix elements are given by the kernel
(e^{\sqrt{x}/2g}-1)^{-1}

evaluated between appropriately normalized Bessel functions
J_{n}(x)
,
see
[
20
]
for details.

The strong coupling expansion of the observable
D_{\ell}(g)
takes
the form

D_{\ell}(g)=\sum_{\{n_{i}:\mathrm{odd}\}}e^{-n\frac{g}{4}}S^{\{n\}}D^{\{n\}}(g)

(1)

The summation goes over distinct sets of positive odd integers
\{n\}\equiv\{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k}\}

and we use the shorthand
\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}=n
. Thus, at a given
non-perturbative order
n
, multiple contributions appear. The number
of such terms is counted by the fermionic Neveu–Schwartz-type character

\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1+x^{(2j+1)/2})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}x^{\frac{n}{2}}
.
A distinctive feature of the expansion is that it is linear in each
non-perturbative scale
e^{-(2j+1)\frac{g}{4}}
, in close analogy
with the way fermionic modes enter the character.

Each term
D^{\{n\}}(g)
is an asymptotic series in
g^{-1}

D^{\{n\}}(g)=g^{\frac{1}{16}-a^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}D_{j}^{\{n\}}g^{-j},

whose coefficients depend on
\ell
; however, for simplicity we suppress
this dependence in the notation. The perturbative sector of
D_{\ell}(g)

corresponds to
\{n\}=\{\}
. Strikingly, all sectors labelled by
the partitions
\{n\}
share a universal structure, which takes the
form

\displaystyle D_{0}^{\{n\}}

\displaystyle=1\quad;\quad D_{1}^{\{n\}}=-(a^{2}-\ell^{2})I_{2}

\displaystyle D_{2}^{\{n\}}

\displaystyle=(a^{2}-\ell^{2})\left[\frac{1}{2}(a^{2}-\ell^{2}+1)I_{2}^{2}+aI_{3}\right]

\displaystyle D_{3}^{\{n\}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{6}(a^{2}-\ell^{2})(a^{2}-\ell^{2}+1)(a^{2}-\ell^{2}+2)I_{2}^{3}+\dots

The parity of
n_{j}
modulo
4
plays an important role and we
define:
\bar{n}_{j}=(-1)^{j}
for
n_{j}=2j+1
. The parameter
a

depends on the partition through
\bar{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bar{n}_{i}

as
a=\frac{1}{4}-\bar{n}
. Here
\bar{n}
measures the difference
between the number of terms of the two parity types. The moments
I_{j}

also depend on
\{n\}
as

I_{j}=J_{j}+2\cdot 4^{j-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{(j-1)\frac{1+\bar{n}_{i}}{2}}n_{i}^{1-j}

where the perturbative coefficients are

J_{2j+1}=2^{2j}\beta(j)\ ;\quad J_{2j}=(2+2^{2j-1}-2^{4j-2})\zeta(2j-1)

Here
\beta(j)
is the Dirichlet beta function and the term involving
the formally divergent zeta value
\zeta(1)
is understood in the
regularized sense, yielding
J_{2}=-6\log 2
.

The leading
I_{2}^{j}
behaviour suggests that dependence on
I_{2}

can be resummed
[
18
]
. Indeed, we find that

D^{\{n\}}(g)=g^{\frac{1}{16}-\ell^{2}}\bar{g}^{\ell^{2}-a^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\bar{D}_{j}^{\{n\}}\bar{g}^{-j}\quad;\quad\bar{g}=g+I_{2}

where the coefficients
\bar{D}_{j}^{\{n\}}
are obtained from
=D_{j}^{\{n\}}

by setting
I_{2}
to zero:
\bar{D}_{j}^{\{n\}}=D_{j}^{\{n\}}(I_{2}\to 0)
.
The first coefficients are
\bar{D}_{0}^{\{n\}}=1
and
\bar{D}_{1}^{\{n\}}=0
.
Higher order terms can be written compactly as
\bar{D}_{j}^{\{n\}}=\frac{(-1)^{j}(a^{2}-\ell^{2})}{j!}\tilde{D}_{j}^{\{n\}}

with

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{2}^{\{n\}}

\displaystyle=2aI_{3}\quad;\quad\tilde{D}_{3}^{\{n\}}=2(1+5a^{2}-\ell^{2})I_{4}

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{4}^{\{n\}}

\displaystyle=6(2a^{4}+a^{2}\left(9-2\ell^{2}\right)-\ell^{2}+1)I_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+12a\left(7a^{2}-3\ell^{2}+5\right)I_{5}

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{5}^{\{n\}}

\displaystyle=48\left(21a^{4}+7a^{2}\left(5-2\ell^{2}\right)+\ell^{4}-5\ell^{2}+4\right)I_{6}

\displaystyle+40a\left(5a^{4}+a^{2}\left(37-6\ell^{2}\right)+\ell^{4}-13\ell^{2}+18\right)I_{3}I_{4}

The physical origin of this structure is not presently understood;
nevertheless, it provides a useful organizational principle that significantly
simplifies the generation and compactification of the expressions.
We provide the first 20 coefficients in an ancillary
Mathematica

file for
\ell=0,1
. Finally, note that
\bar{g}
itself depends
on the partition
\{n\}
, and therefore it is not useful to reinterpret
it as a new coupling constant.

Stokes constant associated with a partition
\{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\}

can be generated iteratively. Starting from the normalization
S^{\{\}}=1

they are obtained using the recursion

S^{\{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k}\}}=S^{\{n_{2},\dots,n_{k}\}}S_{a}^{\{n_{1}\}}\prod_{i=2}^{k}\left(1-\frac{n_{1}\bar{n}_{i}}{\bar{n}_{1}n_{i}}\right)^{2\bar{n}_{1}\bar{n}_{i}}

where
a=\frac{1}{4}-\bar{n}
and

\displaystyle S_{b}^{\{n_{1}\}}

\displaystyle=e^{i\pi(\ell+1+\bar{n}_{1}b)}\frac{\Gamma(\ell-\bar{n}_{1}b)}{\Gamma(1+\ell+\bar{n}_{1}b)}\left(\frac{n_{1}}{4}\right)^{2b\bar{n}_{1}}\times

\displaystyle\quad\frac{(-1)^{\frac{1}{4}(n_{1}+\bar{n}_{1}+2)}\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n_{1}}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\bar{n}_{1}}{4}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{n_{1}+\bar{n}_{1}}{4}\right)^{2}\Gamma\left(1-\frac{n_{1}}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\bar{n}_{1}}{4}\right)}

In particular, for a single element partition one simply has
S^{\{n_{1}\}}=S_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\{n_{1}\}}
.
Higher order Stokes constans can be generated in several different
ways through the recursion, but all constructions lead to the same
final result.

The strong-coupling expansion of
D^{\{n\}}(g)
leads to factorially
growing coefficients
D_{j}^{\{n\}}
, implying that the transseries
(
1
) is only formal. To assign it a precise meaning,
it must be understood through lateral Borel resummation
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
.
This procedure replaces the asymptotic series with its Borel transform

B^{\{n\}}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}D_{j}^{\{n\}}\frac{s^{j+\frac{1}{16}-a^{2}}}{\Gamma(j+\frac{17}{16}-a^{2})}

which has a finite radius of covergence. Analytic continuation beyond
this domain reveals logarithmic branch-cut singularities on the real
line. The cuts along the positive real line obstruct the naive inverse
Borel transform and one must instead define the lateral Borel resummations

S_{\pm}(D^{\{n\}}(g))=g\int_{0}^{\infty e^{\pm i\epsilon}}e^{-gs}B^{\{n\}}(s)

which differ by an ambiguity originating from the branch cuts. All
these information is encoded in the Stokes autormorphism, which acts
on the transseries and relates the two lateral resummations
S_{+}=S_{-}\circ\mathfrak{S}
.
Its logarithm defines the alien derivatives, which encode the location
of the singularities in the Borel plane as well as the discontinuities
across the cuts. The fact that each non-perturbative scale
e^{-n_{i}\frac{g}{4}}

appears only ones implies the fermionic-type structure

\mathfrak{S}=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1+e^{-(2j+1)\frac{g}{4}}\Delta_{2j+1})

which closely resembles the NS-character formula. The final physical
answer is understood as the
S_{+}
Borel resummation of the transseries
(
1
). If instead the
S_{-}
Borel resummation were
chosen, the transseries would have to be complex conjugated. The action
of the alien derivatives
\Delta_{n_{1}}
on
D^{\{n\}}(g)
can
then be computed as

\displaystyle\Delta_{n_{1}}D^{\{n_{2},\dots,n_{k}\}}(g)

\displaystyle=-2i\bar{n}_{1}\sin\frac{\pi}{4}\ \left|\frac{S^{\{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k}\}}}{S^{\{n_{2},\dots,n_{k}\}}}\right|\times

\displaystyle\quad\quad D^{\{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k}\}}(g)

together with

\Delta_{n_{i}}^{2}=0\quad;\quad\Delta_{n_{i}}\Delta_{n_{j}}=\Delta_{n_{j}}\Delta_{n_{i}}

We tested these relations against the asymptotic behaviour of the
perturbative coefficients.

One can slightly renormalize the expansion function, such that the
transseries (
1
) takes the form

D_{\ell}(g)=\sum_{\{n_{i}:\mathrm{odd}\}}e^{-n\frac{g}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}S^{\{n_{i}\}}\hat{D}^{\{n\}}(g)

This representation allows the introduction of formal parameters
\sigma_{2j+1}

associated with each non-perturbative scale
e^{-(2j+1)\frac{g}{4}}
.
In this way one obtains a multi-parameter transseries

D_{\ell}(g,\{\sigma\})=\sum_{\{n_{i}:\mathrm{odd}\}}\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{n_{i}}e^{-\frac{n_{i}}{4}g}\right\}\hat{D}^{\{n\}}(g)

One can then show that the following bridge equation holds

\Delta_{n_{j}}D_{\ell}(g,\{\sigma\})=-2i\Im m(S^{\{n_{j}\}})\partial_{\sigma_{n_{j}}}D_{\ell}(g,\{\sigma\})

This implies that the Stokes automorphism simply introduces the shift

\sigma_{n_{j}}\to\sigma_{n_{j}}-2i\Im m(S^{\{n_{j}\}})
. The resulting
prescription is not the median resummation
S_{\mathrm{med}}=S_{+}\circ\mathfrak{S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

of the purely perturbative sector
D^{\{\}}(g)
. Instead, at each
non-perturbative order there appears the seed of an additional transseries,
which can be obtained by fixing the transseries parameters as
\sigma_{n_{j}}=\Re e(S^{\{n_{j}\}})=\cos\frac{\pi}{4}|S^{\{n_{j}\}}|
.

3
Conclusions

In this Letter we derived the complete strong-coupling expansion of
the cusp anomalous dimension. It admits the representation
\Gamma_{\mathrm{cusp}}(g)\approx D_{1}(g)/D_{0}(g)
,
where each
D_{\ell}(g)
possesses a remarkably simple transseries
structure. The nonperturbative sectors of
D_{\ell}(g)
are labeled
by partitions into distinct odd integers
\{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\}
.
For every such partition, the corresponding sector
D^{\{n\}}(g)

takes a universal form when expressed in terms of its associated moments

I_{j}
and the Stokes constants can be computed iteratively. This
universality indicates that semiclassical expansions of the string
path integral around the different saddle points contributing to
D_{\ell}(g)

are related in a simple and structured manner. It would be desirable
to understand the underlying string-theoretic origin of this property.

The dressing phase of
{\cal N}=4
SYM theory
[
4
]

is likewise related to matrix elements of the
K
-matrix mentioned
above. Clarifying how the resurgence properties of the dressing phase

[
24
]
connect to the structure uncovered here
would be an interesting direction for future investigation.

Finally, the cusp anomalous dimension governs the large-spin limit
of higher-twist operators, while subleading corrections are controlled
by the
O(6)
sigma model
[
25
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
26
]
.
It would be natural to combine the present results with the recently
identified transseries structure of that model
[
27
,
28
]

in order to obtain a more complete description of the high-spin regime.
