Semi-Streaming Algorithms for Submodular Maximization under Random Arrival Order
Niv Buchbinder, Moran Feldman, Siyue Liu, Sherry Sarkar

Abstract
We study random order semi-streaming algorithms for submodular maximization under a wide range of combinatorial constraint classes, including matroids, matroid p p -parity, p p -exchange systems and p p -systems. For most of these classes of constraints, our results are the first improvement over what is known to be achievable for adversarial order. For matroids, matching and p p -matchoids, previous random order results were known, and we improve over some of these as well. In the case of matroids, our improved results show a separation between adversarial and random order semi-streaming algorithms, and exponentially improve the number of passes necessary for getting 1 − 1 / e − ε 1-1/e-\varepsilon approximation for maximizing a monotone submodular function subject to a matroid constraint. We also prove a new hardness result showing a similar separation for p p -systems. Our results are based on two new technical tools. One tool provides a general way to translate offline algorithms for many classes of constraints into random order semi-streaming algorithms. The other tool is a semi-streaming variant of a recently proposed offline algorithm for matroid constraints. Keywords: submodular maximization, semi-streaming algorithms, random order, matroids, independence systems

Submodular maximization is a central topic in combinatorial optimization, studied extensively over the past five decades since the seminal works of

The goal of the problem is to find a set

that (approximately) maximizes

Thus, much of the literature focuses on structured subclasses of independence systems that do admit good approximation ratios. A well-known example of such a subclass is the class of matroids, which captures a diverse set of scenarios such as linear independence of vectors, forests in graphs and laminar families of bounds. A more general subclass of independence systems is the class of

As mentioned above, submodular maximization subject to classes of independence systems has been studied extensively. At first, these studies concentrated on the standard offline computational model (see the works referenced above). Techniques:

Our main results rely on two new algorithmic components. The first component is a single-pass semi-streaming algorithm with the guarantee stated by the next proposition. Intuitively, this algorithm generates two sets

and

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
f\colon 2^{\mathcal{N}}\rightarrow{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}
be a non-negative submodular function and let

•

Corollary 1.4
.

Assume that

•

such that

Then, by setting

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\max\{f(S_{\delta}),f(A)\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c(p)}{c(p)+\alpha}\cdot f(S_{\delta})+\frac{\alpha}{c(p)+\alpha}\cdot f(A)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c(p)\cdot f(S_{\delta})+f(A^{*})}{c(p)+\alpha}\geq\frac{1-\beta\delta p}{c(p)+\alpha}\cdot f(OPT)\geq\frac{f(OPT)}{c(p)+\alpha+\varepsilon}\kern 5.0pt.\qed

The proof of Proposition
1.3
appears in Section
4
. It builds on algorithmic ideas introduced recently by
[
NVZ25
]
for non-monotone submodular maximization under a matroid constraint, but we substantially simplify and extend them.
At a high level, the first phase of the algorithm scans the initial
\delta n
elements and greedily constructs a feasible set
S_{\delta}\in{\mathcal{I}}
by selecting the most valuable element from each “window” of
\nicefrac{{\delta n}}{{r}}
elements. In its second phase, the algorithm scans the remaining
(1-\delta)n
elements and adds each element
u
to
H
whenever
u
can “replace” some
s\in S_{\delta}
and has a marginal contribution that is strictly larger than the one of
s
at the time
s
entered
S_{\delta}
. With a small probability this process might collect too many elements, which forces the algorithm to “abort” to preserve its space complexity guarantee. However, whenever this algorithm does not abort, it collects enough valuable elements into
H
to guarantee that
S_{\delta}\cup H
contains a good solution. Note that since the decision whether to store in
H
each element of the second phase is done independently for each element, this algorithm is not affected by the order of the
(1-\delta)n
elements in this second phase. Thus, the algorithm works correctly even when they arrive in an adversarial, rather than uniformly random, order.

To get our improved guarantees for monotone submodular maximization under a matroid constraint, we need a second component, which is motivated by the following general idea. The set
S_{\delta}
is computed after observing only
\delta n
elements from the stream, so in general we cannot expect

to be large. Fortunately, however, when
f(S_{\delta})
is indeed small, Proposition
1.3
already implies that the best solution inside
S_{\delta}\cup H
(namely
A^{*}
) is close to optimal. Therefore, the more problematic case from the point of view of Proposition
1.3
is when

happens to be substantial. To handle this case, we take advantage of the fact that
f(S_{\delta})
is substantial, and treat it as a starting point for a process that
boosts
its value further while processing the remaining
(1-\delta)n
elements. Note that typically most of the elements of the optimal solution are among these
(1-\delta)n
elements, and thus, the boosting step indeed encounters valuable elements that can be used to increase the value of the solution. The next proposition formalizes this boosting step (see Section
2
for the definition of the multilinear extension).

Proposition 1.5

(Single-pass random-order semi-streaming with boosting)
.

is an optimal independent subset of

. More generally, given

(the above guarantee corresponds to

Additional result:

Our final result is an inapproximability result. In the offline setting, one can get
p
-approximation for maximizing a linear function subject to a
p
-system constraint via a standard greedy algorithm
[
Jen76
]
, and slightly worse guarantees can be obtained also for submodular functions
[
CCPV11
,
FHK23
,
FNW78
]
. However, the best known semi-streaming algorithm only obtains
O(p^{2}\log p)
-approximation for this problem under an adversarial arrival order
[
HKFK20
]
. Theorem
1.1
above shows that one can improve over that by assuming a uniformly random arrival order, and get an approximation ratio of
O(p)
, like in the offline case. Theorem
1.6
below shows that a similar improvement cannot be obtained for adversarial arrival order, and thus, proves a separation between adversarial and uniformly random arrival orders.

To be more specific, Theorem

shows that any data stream algorithm obtaining an approximation ratio of

for maximizing a linear function subject to a

, which excludes the possibility of a semi-streaming algorithm for the above problem with an approximation ratio of

. Similarly to the hardness result of
[

]
, Theorem

applies to what we call

, i.e., algorithms that store elements in their memory in an explicit form rather than via some encoding or sketching. This natural class of algorithms captures every algorithm that we are aware of for submodular maximization. See Section

.

Under an adversarial arrival order, every

-approximation non-encoding data stream algorithm for maximizing a linear function subject to a

Submodular Functions

A set function
f

{\mathcal{N}}

f\colon 2^{\mathcal{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}

u

S

f(T\mid S)\triangleq f(S\cup T)-f(S)
to denote the marginal contribution of
T
to
S
. For ease of the reading, we often use
S+u
and
S-u
as shorthands for

and

, respectively.

Above we have given one definition of submodularity. Specifically, we stated that a set function
f\colon 2^{\mathcal{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is submodular if
f(u\mid S)\geq f(u\mid T)
for every two sets
S\subseteq T\subseteq{\mathcal{N}}
and element
u\in{\mathcal{N}}\setminus T
. Another well-known equivalent definition of submodularity is that

is submodular if
f(S)+f(T)\geq f(S\cap T)+f(S\cup T)
for every two sets
S,T\subseteq{\mathcal{N}}
. Following is one property of submodular functions that we employ in this paper.

Lemma 2.1

(Lemma 2.2 of
[
FMV11
]
)
.

Let
f\colon 2^{\mathcal{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a submodular function, and let
A
be a subset of
{\mathcal{N}}
. If
A(p)
is a random set that contains every element of
A
with probability

, not necessarily independently, then
{\mathbb{E}}[f(A(p))]\geq(1-p)\cdot f(\varnothing)+p\cdot f(A)

Extensions and their properties

The sets of
2^{{\mathcal{N}}}
can be naturally identified with vertices of the
[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
cube by identifying each set
A\subseteq{\mathcal{N}}
with its characteristic vector (which we denote here by
{\mathbf{1}}_{A}
). For linear functions, there is a natural way to extend any function from
2^{\mathcal{N}}
to the cube
[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
. For submodular functions, the situation is more involved, and several useful extensions have been proposed. We use two such extensions. The first is the Lovász extension, defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

(The Lovász extension)
Given a vector
{\mathbf{x}}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
and a scalar
\lambda\in[0,1]
, let
T_{\lambda}({\mathbf{x}})=\left\{u\in{\mathcal{N}}\mid x_{u}\geq\lambda\right\}
be the set of elements in
{\mathcal{N}}
whose coordinate in
{\mathbf{x}}
is at least
\lambda
. Then,

\hat{f}({\mathbf{x}})=\int_{\lambda=0}^{1}f(T_{\lambda}({\mathbf{x}}))d\lambda\kern 5.0pt.

It is known that the Lovász extension of a submodular function
f
is equivalent to the convex closure of
f
, which yields the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2

(Due to
[
Lov83
]
)
.

Let
f\colon 2^{\mathcal{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a submodular function, and let
\hat{f}
be its Lovász extension. For every vector
{\mathbf{x}}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
, let
D_{\mathbf{x}}
denote an arbitrary distribution over
\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{N}}
such that
\Pr[u\in D_{\mathbf{x}}]=x_{u}
for every
u\in{\mathcal{N}}
(i.e., the marginals of
D_{\mathbf{x}}
agree with
{\mathbf{x}}
). Then,
\hat{f}({\mathbf{x}})\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{A\sim D_{\mathbf{x}}}[f(A)]
.

The second extension is the
multilinear extension
, defined by
[
CCPV11
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
multilinear extension

F\colon[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
of a set function
f\colon 2^{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

F({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{S\subseteq{\mathcal{N}}}\bigg(\prod_{u\in S}x_{u}\prod_{u\in{\mathcal{N}}\setminus S}\mspace{-9mu}(1-x_{u})\cdot f(S)\bigg)\kern 5.0pt.

Notice that the multilinear extension is, as is implied by its name, a multlinear function of the coordinates of the input vector
{\mathbf{x}}
. We explore additional properties of the multilinear extension that we need for our analysis in Section

Oracles

Explicit descriptions of set functions and independence system constraints over a ground set
{\mathcal{N}}
might require space that is exponential in
n=|{\mathcal{N}}|
. However, algorithms processing such functions and constraints are usually only useful for applications when they run in time
{\mathtt{Poly}}(n)
. Accordingly, it is standard in such algorithms to assume that the description of the function or constraint is not part of the input. Instead, the algorithms are given access to these objects through an appropriate oracle.

•

If the algorithm needs access to a set function
f
, then it gets a
value oracle
that given a set
S
returns
f(S)
.

•

If the algorithm needs access to an independence system
({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
, then it gets an
independence oracle
that given a set
S
answers whether
S\in{\mathcal{I}}
.

It is sometimes convenient to assume that the algorithm also has value oracle access to the multilinear extension
F
of
f
, i.e., an oracle that given a vector
{\mathbf{x}}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{N}}
, returns
F({\mathbf{x}})
. As was shown by Cualinescu et al.
[
CCPV11
]
, an approximate version of this oracle can be efficiently implemented by viewing
F({\mathbf{x}})
as the expected value of
f
over sets drawn from an appropriate distribution, and then averaging the value of
f
over multiple samples from this distribution. For simplicity, we ignore in this paper the need to approximate the oracle via sampling in this way, and just assume when necessary that we have value oracle access to
F
.

Single Pass Semi-streaming Algorithm with Boosting

In this section, we prove Proposition

See

In Section

[

Both our algorithm and the algorithm of Ganz et al.
[
GKSS26
]
maintain an integral solution

for a height

\ell

Informally, our algorithm gets as input an initial height
h_{0}\in(0,1]
and a value
p^{\prime}\in(0,1]
.
Each one of the
\ell

and a height

. During the iteration, the algorithm first updates the height to

in a

, and then
samples a random subset of elements from
{\mathcal{N}}
, where the probability of each element to be sampled is

. The algorithm uses the sample to perform at most a single swap replacing a single element

We explore two sets of parameters for our offline algorithm. The first set of parameters results in an offline algorithm that obtains
(\frac{e}{e-1}+\delta)

The second set of parameters is attractive for the data stream model and achieves the bounds of Proposition

Given a non-negative integer

Recall that the relative order according to

6
p
-Systems Hardness

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.6
, which we repeat below for convenience. A non-encoding algorithm is an algorithm that obeys the following properties.

•

If an element
u
is explicitly stored in the memory of the algorithm after the arrival of an element

, then
u
must have also been explicitly stored in the memory of the algorithm before the arrival of
v
.

•

Only elements that are explicitly stored in the memory of the algorithm when it terminates can appear in its output set. Similarly, when the algorithm queries the independence oracle on a set, this set can contain only elements that are explicitly stored in the algorithm’s memory at the time of the query.

See
1.6

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.6
. Section
6.1
describes and analyzes a
p
-system
({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
and a linear function
f
that underlay the proof. Section
6.2
, then, leverages these

6.1
p
-System and Linear Function Construction

In this section, we describe the
p
-system
({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
and linear function
f
that we later use to prove Theorem
1.6
in Section
6.2
. Our construction has four positive integer parameters
k
,
\ell
,
r
and
\alpha\geq 2
(whose values are determined later). For every integer
1\leq i\leq\ell
, let us define two sets

A_{i}=\{a_{i,j}\mid 1\leq j\leq\alpha^{i}\cdot p^{2}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{i}=\{b_{i,j}\mid 1\leq j\leq\alpha^{i}\cdot rp^{2}\}\kern 5.0pt.

The ground set of the
p
-system we construct is simply
{\mathcal{N}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}(A_{i}\cup B_{i})
, and a set
S
is independent in the
p
-system (i.e., belongs to
{\mathcal{I}}
) if for every
1\leq i\leq\ell
it holds that

|S\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|\leq\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\exists_{i<i^{\prime}\leq\ell}|S\cap B_{i^{\prime}}|>\alpha^{i^{\prime}}$}\kern 5.0pt,\\
\alpha^{i}\cdot k&\text{if $\forall_{i<i^{\prime}\leq\ell}|S\cap B_{i^{\prime}}|\leq\alpha^{i^{\prime}}$ and $S\cap B_{i}\neq\varnothing$}\kern 5.0pt,\\
\alpha^{i}\cdot k^{2}&\text{otherwise}\kern 5.0pt.\end{cases}

(16)

The linear function
f\colon 2^{\mathcal{N}}\to{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}
we construct gives a weight of
\alpha^{-i}
for every element of
A_{i}\cup B_{i}
. In other words, for every set
S\subseteq{\mathcal{N}}
,

f(S)=\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\alpha^{-i}\cdot|S\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|\kern 5.0pt.

The following lemma shows that the above constructed
({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
is indeed a
p
-system for an appropriate choice of values for the parameters.

Lemma 6.1
.

If
p\geq k+k^{2}/(\alpha-1)
, then the pair
({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
is a
p
-system. In particular, when
\alpha=k^{2}+1
, this pair is a
p
-system when
p\geq k+1
.

Proof.

Since the right hand side of Inequality (
16
) is a down-monotone function of
S
(i.e.,
g(S)\leq g(S^{\prime})
whenever
S\supseteq S^{\prime}
), the set
{\mathcal{I}}
of independent sets is down-closed. Furthermore, since this right hand side is always non-negative, the empty set is guaranteed to be independent. Thus, to prove the lemma, it suffices to show that
({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
obeys the “ratio of base sizes” property of
p
-systems. In other words, for every subset
{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}
of
{\mathcal{N}}
and an independent set
T\subseteq{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}
, we need to show that every base of
{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}
is of size at least
|T|/p
. Assume towards a contradiction that this is not the case,
i.e.
, that there exists a base
C
of
{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}
whose size is less than
|T|/p
. Since
p>k
, for such a base there must exist an integer
1\leq i\leq\ell
such that
|C\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|<|T\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|/p\leq|T\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|/k
. We assume from now on that
i
is the highest integer of this kind. It is also important to note that the inequality
|C\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|<|T\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|/k
implies that the set
\Delta_{i}\triangleq(T\setminus C)\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})
is not empty. However, since
C
is a base, none of the elements of
\Delta_{i}
can be added to
C
without violating independence.

We now need to consider a few cases. The first case is the case in which there does not exist an integer
i\leq i^{\prime}\leq\ell
such that
|C\cap B_{i^{\prime}}|\geq\alpha^{i^{\prime}}
, and in particular,
|C\cap B_{i}|<\alpha^{i}
. Combined with the observation that
|C\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|<|T\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|/k\leq\alpha^{i}\cdot k
, we get that in this case any single element of
A_{i}\cup B_{i}
can be added to
C
without violating independence. However, this contradicts the fact that
\Delta_{i}
is non-empty.

The second case is the case in which the only integer
i\leq i^{\prime}\leq\ell
such that
|C\cap B_{i^{\prime}}|\geq\alpha^{i^{\prime}}
is
i^{\prime}=i
, which in particular implies that
C\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})
is non-empty. In this case, the inequality
|C\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|<|T\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|/k\leq\alpha^{i}\cdot k
only guarantees that any single element of
A_{i}
can be added to
C
without violating independence, but this is no longer necessarily true for elements of
B_{i}
(because adding them might affect the right hand side of Inequality (
16
) for other
i
values). Since no element of
\Delta_{i}
can be added to
C
, this implies
\Delta_{i}\subseteq B_{i}
. Recalling now that
\Delta_{i}
is a non-empty subset of
T
, we get that
T\cap B_{i}\neq\varnothing
, and plugging this observation into Inequality (
16
) yields that the independent set
T
must obey
|T\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|/k\leq\alpha^{i}\leq|C\cap B_{i}|\leq|C\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|
—the second inequality follows from the definition of the case. However, this contradicts the definition of
i
.

It remains to consider the case in which there exists an integer
i<i^{\prime}\leq\ell
such that
|C\cap B_{i^{\prime}}|\geq\alpha^{i^{\prime}}
; and therefore, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle|C|\geq{}

\displaystyle\varepsilon\cdot|C\cap(A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{i^{\prime}})|+(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{i^{\prime\prime}=i^{\prime}}^{\ell}|C\cap(A_{i^{\prime\prime}}\cup B_{i^{\prime\prime}})|\geq\varepsilon\cdot\alpha^{i^{\prime}}+\frac{1-\varepsilon}{k}\cdot\sum_{i^{\prime\prime}=i^{\prime}}^{\ell}|T\cap(A_{i^{\prime\prime}}\cup B_{i^{\prime\prime}})|

\displaystyle>{}

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon(\alpha-1)}{k^{2}}\cdot\sum_{i^{\prime\prime}=1}^{i^{\prime}-1}\alpha^{i^{\prime\prime}}k^{2}+\frac{1-\varepsilon}{k}\cdot\sum_{i^{\prime\prime}=i^{\prime}}^{\ell}|T\cap(A_{i^{\prime\prime}}\cup B_{i^{\prime\prime}})|

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\min\{\varepsilon(\alpha-1)/k,1-\varepsilon\}}{k}\cdot\sum_{i^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\ell}|T\cap(A_{i^{\prime\prime}}\cup B_{i^{\prime\prime}})|=\frac{\min\{\varepsilon(\alpha-1)/k,1-\varepsilon\}}{k}\cdot|T|\kern 5.0pt,

where the second inequality follows from the definitions of
i
and
i^{\prime}
. Setting now
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{(\alpha-1)/k+1}
, we get

|C|\geq\frac{(\alpha-1)/k}{k[(\alpha-1)/k+1]}\cdot|T|=\frac{|T|}{k+k^{2}/(\alpha-1)}\cdot|T|\geq\frac{|T|}{p}\kern 5.0pt,

which contradicts the definition of
C
, and thus, completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Next, we prove that
{\mathcal{I}}
contains a large value independent set, but no independent set in this system is too large in size.

Lemma 6.2
.

The maximum size
|S|
and value

of an independent set

are
\Theta(k^{2}\alpha^{\ell})
and

Proof.

Consider the set

. One can verify that
S
is independent, and its size is

|S|=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|A_{i}|=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(\alpha^{i}\cdot k^{2})=\alpha k^{2}\cdot\frac{\alpha^{\ell}-1}{\alpha-1}=\Theta(1)\cdot k^{2}(\alpha^{\ell}-1)=\Theta(k^{2}\alpha^{\ell})\kern 5.0pt,

where the third equality holds since
\alpha\geq 2
. Additionally, the value of
S
is

f(S)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|A_{i}|\cdot\alpha^{-i}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(\alpha^{i}\cdot k^{2})\cdot\alpha^{-i}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}k^{2}=\ell\cdot k^{2}\kern 5.0pt.

Consider now an arbitrary independent set

. For every integer

, the size of

must be upper bounded by
\alpha^{i}\cdot k^{2}=|S\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|
due to Inequality (

). Thus, both the size and value of
S^{\prime}
cannot be larger than the size and value of
S
, respectively.
∎

In the next section, we show that using an adversarial arrival order, one can force a data stream algorithm with limited memory to produce an independent set of
({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
that has a small expected intersection with

for every

. The next lemma shows that such an independent set has a low value.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
S
be an independent set of

. Then,

is upper bounded by
k+\ell+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|S\cap A_{i}|\cdot\alpha^{-i}
.

Proof.

Let
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq\ell
be the maximum integer for which
|S\cap B_{i^{\prime}}|>\alpha^{i^{\prime}}
. If there is no such integer, let

. The independence of

. Hence, we can upper bound

by

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|S\cap(A_{i}\cup B_{i})|\cdot\alpha^{-i}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=i^{\prime}}^{\ell}|S\cap A_{i}|\cdot\alpha^{-i}+\sum_{i=i^{\prime}+1}^{\ell}|S\cap B_{i}|\cdot\alpha^{-i}+|S\cap B_{i^{\prime}}|\cdot\alpha^{-i^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|S\cap A_{i}|\cdot\alpha^{-i}+\sum_{i=i^{\prime}+1}^{\ell}\alpha^{i}\cdot\alpha^{-i}+(\alpha^{i^{\prime}}\cdot k)\cdot\alpha^{-i^{\prime}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|S\cap A_{i}|\cdot\alpha^{-i}+(\ell-i^{\prime})+k\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|S\cap A_{i}|\cdot\alpha^{-i}+\ell+k\kern 5.0pt,

where the first inequality is based on the choice of

and the observation that Inequality (
16
) implies that

for every independent set

and integer

.
∎

6.2

In this section, we prove Theorem

({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
and linear function

defined in Section
6.1
. As was mentioned before Lemma
6.3
, to prove Theorem

containing many elements of

Let
ALG

({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
. We assume that the order in which the elements of

Lemma 6.4
.

For every

\Pr[{\mathcal{E}}_{i}]\geq 1-k^{2}M/r

Proof.

Let
H_{i}
be an event fixing an arbitrary order for the elements of

H_{i}
in this proof. In other words, all the expectations, probabilities and values in this proof are implicitly conditions on

.

For every integer
1\leq j\leq|A_{i}\cup B_{i}|

the

to arrive and by

the set of elements explicitly stored in the memory of

immediately after the arrival of

. Assume now that we modify the independence oracle of

. Given this modified oracle, since all the elements of

are now indistinguishable from the point of view of

M_{i,j}
only based on the positions of these elements in the arrival order. In other words, there exists a set
H_{i,j}
containing
|M_{i,j}|
indexes out of
1,2,\dotsc,|A_{i}\cup B_{i}|
(note that
|M_{i,j}|
is deterministic in this setting) such that
M_{i,j}=\{u_{k}\mid k\in H_{i,j}\}
.

Let us consider now the behavior of
ALG
with the original independence oracle. Since the elements of
\bigcup_{i^{\prime}=i+1}^{\ell}(A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{i^{\prime}})
arrive only after the arrival of the elements of
A_{i}\cup B_{i}
, the original independence oracle behaves during the arrival of the elements of
A_{i}\cup B_{i}
just like the modified independence oracle as long as the memory of
ALG
does not explicitly store at least
\alpha^{i}k+1\geq\alpha^{i}+1
elements of
A_{i}
. Thus, as long as this event does not happen up until the arrival of
u_{j}
(including), the set
M_{i,j}
is still given by
\{u_{k}\mid k\in H_{i,j}\}
. Consequently, since
ALG
is a non-encoding algorithm, if its memory ever explicitly stores at least
\alpha^{i}+1
elements of
A_{i}
, then the first time in which this happens must be when some element
u_{j}\in A_{i}
arrives, and the set
\{u_{k}\mid k\in H_{i,j-1}\}
already contains
\alpha^{i}
elements of
A_{i}
and is of size
|H_{i,j-1}|=|M_{i,j-1}|\leq M
. To upper bound the probability that such a thing happens, let us denote the elements of
A_{i}
by
a_{1},a_{2},\dotsc,a_{|A_{i}|}
in an arbitrary (but fixed) order; and for every
1\leq h\leq|A_{i}|
, let
{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}
be the event that the set
H_{i,j-1}
is of size at most
M
and
|\{u_{k}\mid k\in H_{i,j-1}\}\cap A_{i}|=\alpha^{i}
, where
j
is the value obeying
u_{j}=a_{h}
. Given the above discussion,
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
happens whenever none of the events
{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}
happens, and thus,

\Pr[{\mathcal{E}}_{i}]\geq 1-\Pr\left[\bigcup_{h=1}^{|A_{i}|}{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}\right]\geq 1-\sum_{h=1}^{|A_{i}|}\Pr[{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}]\kern 5.0pt,

(17)

where the second inequality follows from union bound.

Fix now some integers
1\leq h\leq|A_{i}|
and
1\leq j\leq|A_{i}\cup B_{i}|
, and let us upper bound the probability
\Pr[{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}\mid a_{h}=u_{j}]
. If
|H_{i,j-1}|\leq M
, then

\Pr[{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}\mid a_{h}=u_{j}]=\Pr[|A_{i}\cap\{u_{k}\mid k\in H_{i,j-1}\}|=\alpha^{i}\mid a_{h}=u_{j}]\\
\leq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[|A_{i}\cap\{u_{k}\mid k\in H_{i,j-1}\}|\mid a_{h}=u_{j}]}{\alpha^{i}}=\frac{\frac{|A_{i}|-1}{|A_{i}\cup B_{i}|-1}\cdot|H_{i,j-1}|}{\alpha^{i}}\leq\frac{(|A_{i}|-1)\cdot M}{\alpha^{i}(|A_{i}\cup B_{i}|-1)}\kern 5.0pt,

where the first inequality follows from Markov’s inequality, and the second equality holds since, given
a_{h}=u_{j}
, every element of
A_{i}
other than
a_{h}
has a chance of
|H_{i,j-1}|/[|A_{i}\cup B_{i}|-1]
to appear in one of the positions of
H_{i,j-1}
. If
|H_{i,j-1}|>M
, then we still get
\Pr[{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}\mid a_{h}=u_{j}]=0\leq\frac{(|A_{i}|-1)\cdot M}{\alpha^{i}(|A_{i}\cup B_{i}|-1)}
by the definition of
{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}
. Thus, by the law of total expectation,

\displaystyle\Pr[{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}]={}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{|A_{i}\cup B_{i}|}\mspace{-9mu}\Pr[a_{h}=u_{j}]\cdot\Pr[{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}\mid a_{h}=u_{j}]=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{|A_{i}\cup B_{i}|}\Pr[{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}\mid a_{h}=u_{j}]}{|A_{i}\cup B_{i}|}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{|A_{i}\cup B_{i}|}\frac{(|A_{i}|-1)\cdot M}{\alpha^{i}(|A_{i}\cup B_{i}|-1)}}{|A_{i}\cup B_{i}|}=\frac{(|A_{i}|-1)\cdot M}{\alpha^{i}\cdot(|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|-1)}=\frac{(|A_{i}|-1)\cdot M}{\alpha^{i}\cdot[(r+1)|A_{i}|-1]}\leq\frac{M}{\alpha^{i}r}\kern 5.0pt.

The lemma now follows by plugging the last bound into Inequality (
17
) and recalling that
|A_{i}|=\alpha^{i}k^{2}
.
∎

Using the last lemma, we can now bound the expected value of the independent set produced by
ALG
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
S
denote the independent set outputted by
ALG
. Then,
{\mathbb{E}}[f(S)]\leq k+2\ell+k^{4}M\ell^{2}/r
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be the intersection of the events
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\}
. By the union bound and Lemma
6.4
, the event
{\mathcal{E}}
happens with probability at least
1-k^{2}M\ell/r
. Given the event
{\mathcal{E}}
, we are guaranteed that
ALG
’s memory never contains more than
\alpha^{i}
elements of
A_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq\ell
, and thus, the set
S
it produces also cannot contain more than this many elements of
A_{i}
(since
ALG
is a non-encoding algorithm). Thus, Lemma
6.3
guarantees that given
{\mathcal{E}}
, we have

f(S)\leq k+\ell+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|S\cap A_{i}|\cdot\alpha^{-i}\leq k+\ell+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha^{i}\cdot\alpha^{-i}=k+2\ell\kern 5.0pt.

Recalling that Lemma
6.2
shows that no independent set (such as
S
) can have a value larger than
\ell\cdot k^{2}
, we also get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[f(S)]={}

\displaystyle\Pr[{\mathcal{E}}]\cdot{\mathbb{E}}[f(S)\mid{\mathcal{E}}]+\Pr[\bar{{\mathcal{E}}}]\cdot{\mathbb{E}}[f(S)\mid\bar{{\mathcal{E}}}]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[f(S)\mid{\mathcal{E}}]+\Pr[\bar{{\mathcal{E}}}]\cdot{\mathbb{E}}[f(S)\mid\bar{{\mathcal{E}}}]

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle(k+2\ell)+(k^{2}M\ell/r)\cdot(\ell\cdot k^{2})=k+2\ell+k^{4}M\ell^{2}/r\kern 5.0pt.\qed

Since
ALG
is an arbitrary deterministic non-encoding data stream algorithm, and the distribution of the arrival order of the elements of
{\mathcal{N}}
is independent of the identity of
ALG
, we get the following corollary by Yao’s principle.

Corollary 6.6
.

For every (possibly randomized) non-encoding data stream algorithm
ALG
using a memory of at most
M
elements, there exists an order over the elements of
{\mathcal{N}}
such that feeding these elements to
ALG
in this order makes
ALG
produce a set of value at most
k+2\ell+k^{4}M\ell^{2}/r
in expectation.

The last component that we need before getting to the proof of Theorem
1.6
itself is the following observation, which relates the size
n
of the ground set
{\mathcal{N}}
to the values of the parameters of our constructed independence system.

Observation 6.7
.

It holds that
n=\Theta(k^{2}\alpha^{\ell}r)
.

Proof.

By the definition of the ground set
{\mathcal{N}}
,

\displaystyle n={}

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{N}}|=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha^{i}k^{2}(1+r)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\alpha k^{2}(1+r)\cdot\frac{\alpha^{\ell}-1}{\alpha-1}=\Theta(1)\cdot k^{2}(1+r)(\alpha^{\ell}-1)=\Theta(k^{2}\alpha^{\ell}r)\kern 5.0pt,

where the penultimate equality holds since
\alpha\geq 2
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Since the guarantee of the theorem is asymptotic, we may assume
p\geq 2
. Thus,
in this proof, we may set the values of the parameters
k
,
\alpha
and
\ell
to
p-1
,
k^{2}+1
and
k
, respectively. Recall that, by Lemma
6.1
, these choices of values for
k
and
\alpha
guarantee that
({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
is a
p
-system.

To prove the first part of the theorem, we need to show that every non-encoding data stream algorithm
ALG
with a memory of
O(n/p^{2p+5})=O(n/p^{2k+7})
achieves an approximation ratio no better than
\Omega(p^{2})
when the elements of
({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
are fed to it in some algorithm specific order.
By Corollary
6.6
, there is such an algorithm specific arrival order for which
ALG
outputs a set whose expected value is only
k+2\ell+k^{4}M\ell^{2}/r=k+2\ell+k^{4}\ell^{2}\cdot O(n/p^{2k+7})/r
. Since Lemma
6.2
shows that the maximum value of an independent set of
({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
is
\ell\cdot k^{2}
, the approximation ratio of
ALG
for this arrival order is no better than

\displaystyle\frac{\ell\cdot k^{2}}{k+2\ell+k^{4}\ell^{2}\cdot O(n/p^{2k+7})/r}={}

\displaystyle\frac{\ell\cdot k^{2}}{k+2\ell+k^{4}\ell^{2}\cdot O(k^{2}\alpha^{\ell}r/p^{2k+7})/r}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{k^{3}}{3k+k^{6}\cdot O(k^{2}p^{2k}r/p^{2k+7})/r}=\frac{k^{2}}{3+O(1)}=\Omega(k^{2})=\Omega(p^{2})\kern 5.0pt,

where the first equality follows from Observation
6.7
, and the second equality follows from our choice of value for
\ell
and the observation that
\alpha=k^{2}+1\leq(k+1)^{2}=p^{2}
.

To prove the second part of the theorem, we need to show that the
p
-system
({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
has the extra properties described in this part, namely, that (i) the largest independent set in it is of size
O(p^{2p})
, and (ii) the size
n
of
{\mathcal{N}}
is larger than the any given function of
p
. Property (i) holds since Lemma
6.2
shows that the maximum size of an independent set of
({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{I}})
is
O(k^{2}\alpha^{\ell})=O(k^{2}p^{2k})=O(p^{2k+2})=O(p^{2p})
. To get Property (ii), we observe that, by Observation
6.7
,
n
can be made arbitrarily large by choosing the parameter
r
to be large enough. Since there is no constraint on how large the parameter
r
can be compared to the other parameters, we can always choose it to be large enough so that
n
is larger than any given function of
p
.
∎
