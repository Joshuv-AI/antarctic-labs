Dirichlet Eigenvalue Approximation on Manifolds with Cylindrical Boundary
Anusha Bhattacharya

Abstract
. We prove that the Dirichlet eigenvalues of the Laplace–Beltrami operator on a compact Riemannian manifold with cylindrical boundary can be approximated by the spectrum of truncated graph Laplacians constructed from ( ε , ρ ) (\varepsilon,\rho) -proximity graphs on the manifold. The approximation is uniform over a class ℳ \mathcal{M} of manifolds, characterized by bounds on Ricci curvature, a lower bound on the injectivity radius, and an upper bound on the diameter. We show that the k k -th eigenvalue of the truncated graph Laplacian lies between k k -th Dirichlet eigenvalues of truncated domains of the manifold. As the parameters ε , ρ \varepsilon,\rho and the ratio ε ρ \frac{\varepsilon}{\rho} tend to zero, these estimates yield convergence of the eigenvalues of the truncated graph Laplacian to the Dirichlet eigenvalues of the Laplace–Beltrami operator.



1.
Introduction

A fundamental problem in spectral geometry is to approximate the spectrum of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on a Riemannian manifold by finite discrete models. Given a finite sampling of points from the manifold, a natural approach is to construct a weighted graph and an associated graph Laplacian on the discrete data. Graph Laplacians are extensively used in unsupervised learning tasks, like dimensionality reduction
[
2
,
10
]
, semi-supervised learning
[
3
,
8
]
and spectral clustering
[
16
,
18
]
. The convergence of learning algorithms in these settings depends on spectral convergence of the graph Laplacian to the Laplace-Beltrami operator.

Existing results on spectral convergence can broadly be divided into probabilistic and deterministic settings. For points sampled according to a known probability measure on the manifold, spectral convergence of graph Laplacian in probability has been studied in
[
4
,
12
]
. For manifolds with boundary, Peoples and Harlim
[
17
]
studied the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem and proved spectral convergence of graph Laplacians with high probability as the number of uniformly sampled points increases.

Several works
[
1
,
11
,
15
]
, have established spectral convergence in the deterministic setting. Burago, Ivanov, and Kurylev
[
7
]
proved spectral convergence of graph Laplacians constructed from
\varepsilon
-nets on compact Riemannian manifolds without boundary under uniform bounds on sectional curvature, injectivity radius, and diameter. Their methods were later extended in joint work with the author
[
14
]
to a broader class of Riemannian manifolds by replacing sectional curvature bounds with bounds on Ricci curvature. Extending these spectral approximation results to manifolds with boundary introduces additional challenges, as neighborhoods of the boundary, although small in volume, can significantly influence the associated energy estimates
[
5
]
. Lu
[
13
]
has derived spectral approximations of the Laplace-Beltrami operator with the Neumann boundary condition using reflected geodesics and the techniques of
[
7
]
.

In this paper, we approximate the Dirichlet spectrum of Laplace-Beltrami operator on compact Riemannian manifolds with cylindrical boundaries using truncated graph Laplacians on suitable discretizations of the manifold. The cylindrical boundary induces a product structure in a neighborhood of the boundary, which allows us to construct discretizations that control energy estimates near the boundary. We can then apply the variational techniques for spectral approximation developed in
[
7
]
.

Definition 3

The truncated graph Laplacian

The following theorem is the main result of the paper. It provides two-sided comparison estimates between eigenvalues of the truncated graph Laplacian and Dirichlet eigenvalues of suitable truncated
domains of the manifold.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
0<\varepsilon<\rho<t<s_{0}
, consider a manifold
M\in\mathcal{M}
. Let
\lambda_{k}(M_{t+\varepsilon})
and
\lambda_{k}(M_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho})
denote the Dirichlet eigenvalues of the Laplace-Beltrami operator restricted to the truncated domains
M_{t+\varepsilon}
and
M_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}
of
M
respectively, and
\lambda_{k}(\Gamma^{t}_{\varepsilon,\rho})
and
\lambda_{k}(M_{t})
be the eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{t}
and
-\Delta_{M}
with the Dirichlet boundary condition. Then, there exist a constant
C_{\mathcal{M}}>0
such that for any
\rho,\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}<\frac{1}{C_{\mathcal{M}}}
,

\left(1-C_{\mathcal{M},k}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}+\rho\right)\right)\lambda_{k}(M_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho})\leq\lambda_{k}(\Gamma^{t}_{\varepsilon,\rho})\leq\left(1+C_{\mathcal{M},k}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}+\rho\right)\right)\lambda_{k}(M_{t+\varepsilon}).

The comparison estimates in the theorem show that eigenvalues of the truncated graph Laplacian are controlled by the Dirichlet eigenvalues of two nearby truncated domains of the manifold. In the proximity graph
\Gamma_{\varepsilon,\rho}^{t}
, the parameter
\varepsilon
controls density of point sampling, while
\rho
determines the scale of local interactions in the graph. As these parameters decrease, the discretization becomes finer and the discrete Dirichlet energy of functions (
2.1
) supported on
X_{t}
increasingly reflects the Dirichlet energy on
M_{t}
. Consequently, eigenvalues of the truncated graph Laplacian converge to Dirichlet eigenvalues of the truncated manifold.

Corollary 1.3
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lambda_{k}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon,\rho}^{t})\to\lambda_{k}(M_{t})\quad\text{as }\varepsilon,\rho\to 0,\ \frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}\to 0.

The truncated domains
M_{t}
are obtained by removing a boundary layer of width
t
from the manifold. As
t\to 0
, these domains exhaust
M
. Hence, the Dirichlet eigenvalues on
M_{t}
converge to the Dirichlet eigenvalues on
M.

Proposition 1.4
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lambda_{k}(M_{t})\to\lambda_{k}(M)\quad\text{as }t\to 0.

Idea of proof

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we consider truncations of the manifold within the cylindrical radius and apply the spectral approximation techniques developed in
[
7
]
. The choice of point sampling near the boundary ensures that the Voronoi cells can be effectively described in the presence of the cylindrical boundary. Moreover, the truncations can be chosen so that the discretization and interpolation maps avoid any interaction with the boundary.

The proof is based on the Min–Max principle.We construct a discretization map on the truncated manifold that allows us to relate the discrete Dirichlet energy on the graph to the energy functional on the manifold. By controlling the Rayleigh quotient on the image under this discretization map of the span of the first
k
eigenfunctions of
-\Delta
, we obtain an upper bound for the
k
-th eigenvalue of the truncated graph Laplacian.

The lower bound for the
k
-th eigenvalue of the truncated graph Laplacian is obtained using an interpolation map that lifts functions from
L^{2}(X_{t})
to
C^{0,1}(M)
. This map acts as an approximate inverse of the discretization map. We establish estimates relating the derivative of the interpolated function to the discrete Dirichlet energy. Restricting the Rayleigh quotient to the image of the interpolation map applied to the span of the first
k
eigenfunctions of the truncated graph Laplacian, and using these interpolation estimates, yields the lower bound.
We then use domain monotonicity and continuity of Dirichlet eigenvalues under monotone sequence of truncations to obtain spectral convergence of the graph Dirichlet eigenvalues to the Dirichlet eigenvalues of the manifold.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the discrete Dirichlet energy associated with the graph discretization and show that it corresponds to the quadratic form of the truncated graph Laplacian
\Delta_{t}
. This connection allows the eigenvalues of
\Delta_{t}
to be studied using variational characterizations analogous to those for the Dirichlet Laplace-Beltrami operator.

For an
(\varepsilon,\rho)
-proximity graph
\Gamma^{t}_{\varepsilon,\rho}
, let
E=E(\Gamma^{t}_{\varepsilon,\rho})
denote the set of directed edges of the graph. On
L^{2}(E)
, we have the following inner product:

\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in E}w(e)\xi(e)\eta(e).

Definition 4
.

Let
u\in L^{2}(X)
. The discrete differential
\delta u:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\delta u(e_{ij}):=u(x_{j})-u(x_{i}).

The corresponding Dirichlet energy is

(2.1)

\|\delta u\|^{2}=\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\sum_{x_{i}\sim x_{j}}\mu_{i}\mu_{j}\,|u(x_{i})-u(x_{j})|^{2}.

To approximate the Dirichlet eigenvalues on
M_{t}
, we consider functions on the graph that are supported in
X_{t}
and extend them by zero outside
X_{t}
. Then the edges whose endpoints both lie outside
X_{t}
do not contribute to the discrete Dirichlet energy. Thus the energy depends only on edges intersecting
X_{t}
, which naturally incorporates the Dirichlet boundary condition.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
u,v\in L^{2}(X_{t})
,

\langle\delta u,\delta v\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}=-\langle\Delta_{\Gamma}u,v\rangle_{L^{2}(X_{t})}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\langle\delta u,\delta u\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x_{i}\sim x_{j}}w_{ij}\bigl(u(x_{j})-u(x_{i})\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x_{i}\sim x_{j}}w_{ij}\bigl(u(x_{i})-u(x_{j})\bigr)u(x_{i})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x_{i}\sim x_{j}}w_{ij}\bigl(u(x_{i})-u(x_{j})\bigr)u(x_{j}).

Since the edges are directed, interchanging the indices
i
and
j
in the second sum and using the symmetry

w_{ij}=w_{ji}
, we obtain

\langle\delta u,\delta u\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}=\sum_{x_{i}}\left(\sum_{x_{j}\sim x_{i}}w_{ij}\bigl(u(x_{i})-u(x_{j})\bigr)\right)u(x_{i}).

Since
u\in L^{2}(X_{t})
,
u(x_{i})=0
, for all
x_{i}\notin X_{t}.
Hence,

\langle\delta u,\delta u\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}=\sum_{x_{i}\in X_{t}}\left(\sum_{x_{j}\sim x_{i}}w_{ij}\bigl(u(x_{i})-u(x_{j})\bigr)\right)u(x_{i}).

Now for
x_{i}\in X_{t}
,

\Delta_{\Gamma}u(x_{i})=\frac{1}{\mu_{i}}\sum_{x_{j}\sim x_{i}}w_{ij}\bigl(u(x_{j})-u(x_{i})\bigr),

which implies

\sum_{x_{j}\sim x_{i}}w_{ij}\bigl(u(x_{i})-u(x_{j})\bigr)=-\mu_{i}\,\Delta_{\Gamma}u(x_{i}).

Substituting this identity yields

\langle\delta u,\delta u\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}=-\sum_{x_{i}}\mu_{i}\,\Delta_{\Gamma}u(x_{i})\,u(x_{i})=-\langle\Delta_{\Gamma}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}(X_{t})}.

Finally, both mappings

(u,v)\mapsto\langle\delta u,\delta v\rangle_{L^{2}(E)},\qquad(u,v)\mapsto\langle\Delta_{\Gamma}u,v\rangle_{L^{2}(X_{t})}

define symmetric bilinear forms on
L^{2}(X_{t})
.
Therefore, the general identity follows from the polarization formula,

\langle\delta u,\delta v\rangle=\frac{1}{4}\bigl(\langle\delta(u+v),\delta(u+v)\rangle-\langle\delta(u-v),\delta(u-v)\rangle\bigr).

∎

2.1.
Min-Max Characterization

The Dirichlet eigenvalues of
\Delta_{t}
satisfy the variational principle

\lambda_{k}(\Gamma^{t}_{\varepsilon,\rho})=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\subset H^{1}_{0}(X_{t})\\
\dim L=k\end{subarray}}\;\max_{u\in L\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\delta u\|^{2}}{\|u\|^{2}}.

3.
Average Dispersion

In this section, we define an average dispersion functional on the truncated manifold
M_{t}
for a suitable scale
r
such that for all
x\in M_{t}
, the balls
B_{r}(x)
do not intersect the boundary of
M
. This restriction is essential for controlling energy estimates of compactly supported functions near the boundary.

Definition 5
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(M_{t})
and
0<r<2\rho<t
. For any measurable set
V\subset M_{t}
, define

E_{r}^{t}(f,V):=\int_{V}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}\,dy\,dx.

We write
E_{r}^{t}(f):=E_{r}^{t}(f,M_{t})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
0<r<2\rho<t
,
there exists a constant
C_{n}
such that for all

f\in L^{2}(M_{t})

E_{r}^{t}(f)\leq C_{n}\nu_{n}r^{n}\,\|f\|_{t}^{2}.

Proof.

Using the inequality

|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}\leq 2\bigl(|f(x)|^{2}+|f(y)|^{2}\bigr),

we obtain

\displaystyle E_{r}^{t}(f)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{M_{t}}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap M_{t}}\bigl(|f(x)|^{2}+|f(y)|^{2}\bigr)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=4\int_{M_{t}}\operatorname{vol}\bigl(B_{r}(x)\cap M_{t}\bigr)|f(x)|^{2}\,dx.

Since
r<2\rho<t
, for
x\in M_{t}
we have
B_{r}(x)\cap\partial M=\phi
. By the volume comparison theorem,

\operatorname{vol}\bigl(B_{r}(x)\cap M_{t}\bigr)\leq\operatorname{vol}\bigl(B_{r}(x))\leq C_{n}\nu_{n}r^{n}\quad\text{for all }x\in M_{t}.

Hence,

E_{r}^{t}(f)\leq C_{n}\nu_{n}r^{n}\,\|f\|_{t}^{2}.

∎

We have the following lemma due to the fact that compactly supported smooth functions are dense in
H^{1}_{0}(M_{t})

Lemma 3.2
.

For
M\in\mathcal{M}
, let
0<r<2\rho<t<\frac{i_{0}}{2}
. For

,

E_{r}^{t}(f)\leq\frac{\nu_{n}r^{n+2}}{n+2}(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r)\int_{M_{t}}|df|^{2}\,dx,

where
\nu_{n}
denotes the volume of the unit ball in

.

Proof.

We follow the arguments in the proof of Lemma 2.3 in
[
14
]
. Since

is compactly supported in

and

, the balls

do not intersect the boundary for any

. Consequently, the Jacobian estimates and the local estimates along the geodesics

,

, used in the proof carry over to our setting.

Hence, for all

4.
Discretization of the manifold

Since the boundary of the manifold is cylindrical, the Voronoi cells corresponding to discretization points near the boundary have the form
W_{t}\times\left[-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]
.
To ensure compatibility with the Dirichlet condition, we define the discretization map only for functions supported in
M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}
. With this choice, the discretized function is assigned the value
0
on the boundary of
X_{t}
.

Definition 6
.

The discretization map
P\colon L^{2}(M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})\to H^{1}_{0}(X_{t},\mu)
is defined by

Pf(x_{i}):=\frac{1}{\mu_{i}}\int_{V_{i}}f(x)\,dx.

We observe that
Pf|_{X\setminus X_{t-\varepsilon}}=0.
For all
t>t^{\prime}
,
\|Pf\|_{t^{\prime}}=\|Pf\|_{t}.

Definition 7
.

The map
P^{*}\colon H^{1}_{0}(X_{t})\to L^{2}(M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})
is defined by

P^{*}u(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}u(x_{i})\,\mathbf{1}_{V_{i}}(x).

Since the boundary is cylindrical, the support of
P^{*}u
is contained in
M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}.
We have the following properties of
P
and
P^{*}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
P:L^{2}(M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})\to L^{2}(X_{t})
and
P^{*}:H^{1}_{0}(X_{t})\to L^{2}(M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})
be defined as above. Then the following hold.

(1)

For all
f\in L^{2}(M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})
,
\|Pf\|_{t}\leq\|f\|_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}.

(2)

For all
u\in H^{1}_{0}(X_{t})
,
\|P^{*}u\|_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}=\|u\|_{t}.

(3)

For all
f\in L^{2}(M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})
and
u\in H^{1}_{0}(X_{t})
,
\langle f,P^{*}u\rangle_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}=\langle Pf,u\rangle_{t}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\varepsilon<r<2\rho<t.
For every
f\in H^{1}_{0}(M_{t+\varepsilon/2})
,

\|f-P^{*}Pf\|_{{t+\varepsilon/2}}^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{4n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r)\varepsilon^{2}\|df\|_{t}^{2}.

Proof.

We have

\|f-P^{*}Pf\|_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{V_{i}}|f(x)-Pf(x_{i})|^{2}\,dx.

For all
x_{i}\in X_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}
,
\operatorname{diam}(V_{i})\leq 2\varepsilon
and due to our choice of points in the cylindrical boundary,
V_{i}\subseteq M_{t}
. Using Lemma
3.3
we have

\int_{V_{i}}|f(x)-Pf(x_{i})|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{c_{n}(r-\varepsilon)^{n}}\,E_{r}(f,V_{i}).

Summing over
i
,

\|f-P^{*}Pf\|_{{t+\varepsilon/2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{c_{n}(r-\varepsilon)^{n}}E_{r}^{t}(f).

The rest of the arguments follow from the proof of Lemma 3.2 in
[
14
]
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
t>\rho+3\varepsilon.
For every
f\in H^{1}_{0}(M_{t+\varepsilon/2})
the following hold:

(1)

|\|Pf\|_{t}-\|f\|_{t+\varepsilon/2}|^{2}\leq\frac{4n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r)\varepsilon^{2}\,\|df\|_{t+\varepsilon/2}^{2}.

(2)

\|\delta(Pf)\|^{2}\leq(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}(\rho+2\varepsilon))\left(1+\frac{2\varepsilon}{\rho}\right)^{n+2}\,\|df\|_{t+\varepsilon/2}
.

Proof.

Since
f\in H^{1}_{0}(M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})
and
P^{*}
is an isometry,

\|Pf\|_{t}=\|Pf\|_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}=\|P^{*}Pf\|_{{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}.

Therefore,

\big|\|Pf\|_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}-\|f\|_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\big|\leq\|P^{*}Pf-f\|_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}

which proves
(1)
using Lemma
4.2
.

Applying Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\big|Pf(x_{j})-Pf(x_{i})\big|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{i}\mu_{j}}\int_{V_{i}}\int_{V_{j}}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}\,dy\,dx.

Hence,

\|\delta(Pf)\|^{2}\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\sum_{x_{i}\sim x_{j}}\int_{V_{i}}\int_{V_{j}}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}\,dy\,dx.

For
x\in V_{i}
, let
U(x):=\bigcup_{x_{j}\sim x_{i}}V_{j}\subseteq B_{\rho+2\varepsilon}(x).

For all
Pf\in H^{1}_{0}(X_{t})
the voronoi cells
V_{i}
corresponding to non-zero entries lie in
M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}.
Since
t>2\rho>\rho+2\varepsilon
,
B_{\rho+2\varepsilon}(x)\cap\partial M=\phi.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\delta(Pf)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\int_{M_{t}}\int_{B_{\rho+2\varepsilon}(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\,E^{t}_{\rho+2\varepsilon}(f).

Applying Lemma
3.2
, since
t>\rho+2\varepsilon
,

E^{t}_{\rho+2\varepsilon}(f)\leq\frac{\nu_{n}}{n+2}(\rho+2\varepsilon)^{n+2}(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}(\rho+2\varepsilon))\,\|df\|_{{t}}^{2}.

Therefore,

\|\delta(Pf)\|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\rho+2\varepsilon}{\rho}\right)^{n+2}(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}(\rho+2\varepsilon))\,\|df\|_{t}^{2}.

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<2\rho<t<s_{0}<\frac{i_{0}}{2}
,
M\in\mathcal{M}
and
\Gamma^{t}_{\varepsilon,\rho}
be an
(\varepsilon,\rho)
-proximity graph on
M
.
Let
\lambda_{k}(M_{t+\varepsilon})
and
\lambda_{k}(\Gamma^{t}_{\varepsilon,\rho})
denote the
k
–th Dirichlet eigenvalues of
-\Delta
on
M_{t+\varepsilon}
and
\Delta_{t}
respectively. There exists positive constants
C_{n}
and
C_{\mathcal{M},k}
such that for any
\rho,\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}<\frac{1}{C_{n}}
,

\lambda_{k}(\Gamma^{t}_{\varepsilon,\rho})\;\leq\;\left(1+C_{\mathcal{M},k}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}+\rho\right)\right)\lambda_{k}(M_{t+\varepsilon}).

Proof.

For a subspace
L\subset H^{1}_{0}(X_{t})
with
\dim L=k
, if

\sup_{0\neq u\in L}\frac{\|\delta u\|^{2}}{\|u\|_{t}^{2}}\leq\left(1+C_{\mathcal{M},k}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}+\rho\right)\right)\lambda_{k}(M_{t+\varepsilon})

then by the min-max principle for
\Delta_{t}
, the conclusion follows.

Let
W\subset H_{0}^{1}(M_{t+\varepsilon/2})
be the
k
-dimensional subspace spanned by the first
k
orthonormal Dirichlet eigenfunctions of
-\Delta
on
M_{t+\varepsilon/2}
.
Then for all
f\in W
, since
M_{t+\varepsilon}\subseteq M_{t+\varepsilon/2}
, applying domain monotonicity on eigenvalues

\|df\|_{t+\varepsilon/2}^{2}\leq\lambda_{k}(M_{t+\varepsilon})\,\|f\|_{t+\varepsilon/2}^{2}.

Let
L:=P(W)\subset H^{1}_{0}(X_{t}).

Using Lemma
4.2
,
P
is injective on
W
for
\rho<\frac{1}{C_{\mathcal{M},k}}
, where the constant is obtained from
[
9
]
which asserts the existence of an upper bound of
\lambda_{k}(M_{t+\varepsilon})
depending only on
\mathcal{M}
and
k
.

Let
u\in L\setminus\{0\}
and choose
f\in W
such that
u=Pf
.
We use Lemma
4.3
to estimate the Rayleigh quotient of

, by following the arguments of the proof of Theorem 3.5 in
[

∎

5.
Smoothing operator

In this section, we present a smoothing operator that maps
L^{2}
functions on
M_{t+\varepsilon/2}
to
C^{0,1}
functions on
M_{t+\varepsilon/2-r}
. The operator spreads the function locally over balls of radius
r
in
M
using a kernel, while avoiding any intersection with the boundary.

Definition 8
.

Let
\psi\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\psi(t):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{n+2}{2\nu_{n}}\,(1-t^{2}),&0\leq t\leq 1,\\[3.00003pt]
0,&t>1,\end{cases}

where
\nu_{n}
denotes the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The normalization constant is chosen so that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(|x|)\,dx=1.

Definition 9
.

Fix
r
such that
0<r<2\rho<t
. For
x\in M
, such that
d(x,\partial M)>r
, we define the kernel as

k_{r}(x,y):=r^{-n}\,\psi\!\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{r}\right).

Due to the choice of
r
, the support of
k_{r}
doesn’t intersect the boundary of
M
. Then,

\mathrm{grad}k_{r}(.,y)(x)=\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n+2}}\exp^{-1}(y),\qquad\forall x\in M_{t}\text{ and }y\in B_{r}(x).

Lemma 5.1
.

The kernel map has the following properties.

(1)

For
x\in M_{r}
,
\big|k_{r}(x,y)\big|\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n}}.

(2)

For
d(x,\partial M)>2r
,
k_{r}(x,y)=k_{r}(y,x).

Definition 10
.

For
0<r<2\rho<t
the integral operator
\Lambda_{r}^{0}\colon L^{2}(M_{t+\varepsilon/2})\to C^{0,1}(M_{t+\varepsilon/2-r})
is defined
by

\Lambda^{0}_{r}f(x):=\int_{M_{t+\varepsilon/2}}k_{r}(x,y)\,f(y)\,dy.

Definition 11
.

For
x\in M
such that
d(x,\partial M)>r
, define
\theta_{r}(x)=\int_{B_{r}(x)}k_{r}(x,y)dy
i.e.,

\theta_{r}(x)=r^{-n}\int_{B_{r}(x)}\psi\!\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{r}\right)\,dy.

Definition 12
.

Let
0<r<2\rho<t
. The normalized smoothing operator
\Lambda_{r}\colon L^{2}(M_{t+\varepsilon/2})\to C^{0,1}(M_{t+\varepsilon/2-r})
is defined as

\Lambda_{r}f(x):=\frac{1}{\theta_{r}(x)}\,\Lambda^{0}_{r}f(x).

Remark 5.2
.

The restriction that
r<2\rho<t
ensures that the smoothing kernel is supported away from the boundary so that all Jacobian and gradient estimates hold exactly as in the case witohut boundary. We state Lemma 4.2 and Lemma 4.3 from
[
14
]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
0<r<2\rho<t<\frac{i_{0}}{2}
. For all
x\in M_{t}
,

(1)

1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r\leq\theta_{r}(x)\leq 1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r,
and

(2)

|\nabla\theta_{r}(x)|\leq c_{\mathcal{M}}.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
0<3r<t<\frac{i_{0}}{2}
. For every
f\in L^{2}(M_{t+\varepsilon/2})
and
r<\frac{1}{c_{\mathcal{M}}}
,

\|\Lambda_{r}f\|^{2}_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-r}\leq\left(\frac{1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r}{1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r}\right)\,\|f\|^{2}_{t+\varepsilon/2}.

Proof.

Let
x\in M_{t+\varepsilon/2-r}.
Since
t+\varepsilon/2-r>r
,
B_{r}(x)\cap\partial M=\phi
and
B_{r}(x)\cap M_{t+\varepsilon/2-r}\subseteq B_{r}(x).
Hence,

\int_{M_{t+\varepsilon/2}}k_{r}(x,y)dy\leq\int_{B_{r}(x)}k_{r}(x,y)dy=\theta_{r}(x).

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle|\Lambda^{0}_{r}f(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M_{t+\varepsilon/2}}k_{r}(x,y)dy\right)\left(\int_{M_{t+\varepsilon/2}}|f(y)|^{2}k_{r}(x,y)\,dy\right)

(5.1)

\displaystyle\leq\theta_{r}(x)\int_{M_{t+\varepsilon/2}}|f(y)|^{2}k_{r}(x,y)\,dy.

Integrating over
x\in M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-r}
and substituting (
5.1
) gives

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{r}f\|_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-r}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-r}}\theta_{r}(x)^{-1}\int_{M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}|f(y)|^{2}k_{r}(x,y)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r)^{-1}\int_{M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}|f(y)|^{2}\int_{M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-r}}k_{r}(x,y)\,dx\,dy.

Since
t>3r
, we have
t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-r>2r
. Using Lemma
5.1
,

\int_{M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-r}}k_{r}(x,y)\,dx\leq\int_{B_{r}(y)}k_{r}(x,y)\,dx=\theta(y)\leq(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r).

The result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
0<3r<t<\frac{i_{0}}{2}
. For every
f\in L^{2}(M_{t+\varepsilon/2})
and
r<\frac{1}{c_{\mathcal{M}}}
,

\|\Lambda_{r}f-f\|_{t+\varepsilon/2-r}^{2}\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n}(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r)}E_{r}^{t-\varepsilon/2-r}(f).

Proof.

Fix
x\in M_{t+\varepsilon/2}
. Since
t>3r
,
B_{r}(x)
doesn’t intersect the boundary of the manifold
M
. Hence, the support of
\theta_{r}(x)
and the kernel
k_{r}
at
x
does not intersect the boundary. We have

\Lambda_{r}f(x)-f(x)=\theta_{r}(x)^{-1}\int_{B_{r}(x)}(f(y)-f(x))k_{r}(x,y)\,dy.

By Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{r}f(x)-f(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\theta_{r}(x)^{-2}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}k_{r}(x,y)dy\right)\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}k_{r}(x,y)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle=\theta_{r}(x)^{-1}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}k_{r}(x,y)\,dy

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n}(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r)}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}

where (
5.2
) follows from Lemma
5.3
and Lemma
5.1
.
∎

We state the bound from Lemma 4.6 of
[
14
]
in our setting for compactly supported functions. Since
B_{r}(x)\cap\partial M=\varnothing
for all
x\in M_{t+\varepsilon/2}
, the estimates in
[
14
]
apply in the present setting.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
0<r<2\rho<i_{0}\text{ and }t>3r
. For every
f\in L^{2}(M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}),

\|d(\Lambda_{r}f)\|^{2}_{t+\varepsilon/2-r}\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n+2}}\frac{(1+2c_{\mathcal{M}}r)^{2}}{(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r)^{3}}E_{r}^{t+\varepsilon/2-r}(f).

6.
Interpolation map

To compare functions on the discretization
X_{t}
with functions on the manifold, we introduce an interpolation map that lifts discrete functions to Lipschitz functions on
M
. The map is obtained by first extending a function on
X_{t}
to the manifold using the operator
P^{*}
and then applying the smoothing operator
\Lambda
. This construction will allow us to relate the discrete Dirichlet energy to the continuous Dirichlet energy on the manifold.

Definition 13
.

The interpolation map
I:H^{1}_{0}(X_{t})\rightarrow C^{0,1}(M_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho})

is defined by

Iu=\Lambda_{\rho-2\varepsilon}P^{*}u.

For
u\in H^{1}_{0}(X_{t})
, the support of
Iu
is contained in

M_{t+\frac{5\varepsilon}{2}-\rho}\subseteq M_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}
. Since
\|Iu\|_{t+\frac{5\varepsilon}{2}-\rho}=\|Iu\|_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}
,
we use the latter notation for simplicity.

From Lemma and the fact that
P^{*}
preserves the norm, we have

\left\lVert Iu\right\rVert_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}\leq\left(\frac{1+c_{\mathcal{M}}(\rho-2\varepsilon)}{1-c_{\mathcal{M}}(\rho-2\varepsilon)}\right)\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{t}.

Lemma 6.1
.

For
0<2\varepsilon<\rho<3\rho<t
,
c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho<\frac{1}{4}
and
\frac{2\epsilon}{\rho}<\frac{1}{n}
, let
u\in H^{1}_{0}(X_{t})
. Then

(1)

|\|Iu\|_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}-\|u\|_{t}|\leq\frac{3\rho^{2}}{1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho}\|\delta u\|
;

(2)

\|d(Iu)\|_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}\leq\left(1-\frac{2\epsilon}{\rho}\right)^{-\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{1+2c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho}{(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|\delta u\|.

Proof.

For
u\in H^{1}_{0}(X_{t})
, using the fact that
P^{*}
preserves the norm and
2\varepsilon<\rho\implies M_{t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\subseteq M_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}
,

|\|Iu\|_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}-\|u\|_{t}|=\big|\left\lVert Iu\right\rVert_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}-\left\lVert P^{*}u\right\rVert_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}\big|\leq\|Iu-P^{*}u\|_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}.

From Lemma
5.5

(6.1)

\|Iu-P^{*}u\|^{2}_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}(\rho-2\epsilon))(\rho-2\epsilon)^{n}}E_{\rho-2\varepsilon}^{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}(P^{*}u).

The Dirichlet energy of
u
when calculated on
M_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}
is

\|\delta u\|^{2}=\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\sum_{x_{i}\in X}\sum_{j:x_{i}\sim x_{j}}\mu_{i}\mu_{j}|u(x_{j})-u(x_{i})|^{2}.

Since,
t>2\rho
, the edges with one endpoint in
\partial M
cannot have another endpoint in
X_{t}
. Hence, these endpoints are not in the support of
u
and they don’t contribute to the Dirichlet energy. For
x\in V_{i}
, let
U(x):=\bigsqcup_{j:x_{j}\sim x_{i}}V_{j}
.
Since
t>2\rho>\varepsilon
and
u\in H^{1}_{0}(M_{t}),
for any
x\in M\setminus M_{\varepsilon}
,
P^{*}u|_{U(x)}\equiv 0.
Hence,

(6.2)

\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert^{2}=\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\int_{M_{\varepsilon}}\int_{U(x)}|P^{*}u(y)-P^{*}u(x)|^{2}\,dy\,dx.

By triangle inequality, for all
y\in B_{\rho-2\varepsilon}(x)
,
d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq d(x,x_{i})+d(x,y)+d(y,x_{j})\leq\rho.
Hence,
B_{\rho-2\varepsilon}(x)\subseteq U(x).
From (
6.2
),

\displaystyle\|\delta u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\int_{M_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{\rho-2\varepsilon}(x)}|P^{*}u(y)-P^{*}u(x)|^{2}\,dy\,dx

(6.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}E^{\varepsilon}_{\rho-2\varepsilon}(P^{*}u).

This implies, from (
6.1
),

\displaystyle\|Iu-P^{*}u\|^{2}_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}(\rho-2\epsilon))(\rho-2\epsilon)^{n}}E_{\rho-2\varepsilon}^{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}(P^{*}u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}(\rho-2\epsilon))(\rho-2\epsilon)^{n}}E_{\rho-2\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}(P^{*}u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\rho^{2}}{1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho}\|\delta u\|^{2}

which proves
(1)
.

From Lemma
5.6
and (
6.3
),

\|d(Iu)\|_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}^{2}\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n+2}}\frac{(1+2c_{\mathcal{M}}r)^{2}}{(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r)^{3}}E_{\rho-2\varepsilon}^{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}(P^{*}u)\leq\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho-2\varepsilon}\right)^{n+2}\frac{(1+2c_{\mathcal{M}}r)^{2}}{(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r)^{3}}\|\delta u\|^{2}.

∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<2\rho<t<s_{0}<\frac{i_{0}}{2}
,
M\in\mathcal{M}
and
\Gamma^{t}_{\varepsilon,\rho}
be an
(\varepsilon,\rho)
-proximity graph on
M
.
Let
\lambda_{k}(M_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho})
and
\lambda_{k}(\Gamma^{t}_{\varepsilon,\rho})
denote the
k
–th Dirichlet eigenvalues of
-\Delta
on
M_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}
and
\Delta_{t}
respectively. There exists positive constants
C_{n}
and
C_{\mathcal{M},k}
such that for any
\rho,\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}<\frac{1}{C_{n}}
,

\lambda_{k}(\Gamma^{t}_{\varepsilon,\rho})\;\geq\;\left(1+C_{\mathcal{M},k}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}+\rho\right)\right)\lambda_{k}(M_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}).

Proof.

The proof follows similar arguments as in Theorem
4.4
.
For a subspace
L\subset H^{1}_{0}(M_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho})
with
\dim L=k
, if

\sup_{0\neq f\in L}\frac{\|df\|^{2}}{\|f\|_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}^{2}}\leq\left(1+C_{\mathcal{M},k}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}+\rho\right)\right)^{-1}\lambda_{k}(\Gamma^{t}_{\varepsilon,\rho})

then by the min-max principle for
-\Delta
, the conclusion follows.

Let
W\subset H_{0}^{1}(X_{t})
be the
k
-dimensional subspace spanned by the first
k
orthonormal Dirichlet eigenfunctions of
-\Delta_{t}
.
Then for all
u\in W
,
\|\delta u\|^{2}\leq\lambda_{k}(\Gamma^{t}_{\varepsilon,\rho})\,\|u\|_{t}^{2}.

Let
L:=I(W)\subset H^{1}_{0}(M_{t+2\varepsilon-\rho}).

Using Lemma
6.1
,
I
is injective on
W
for
\rho<\frac{1}{C_{\mathcal{M},k}}
.

Let
f\in L\setminus\{0\}
and choose
u\in W
such that
f=Iu
.
We then use Lemma
6.1
to estimate the Rayleigh quotient of
f
and the result follows.

∎
