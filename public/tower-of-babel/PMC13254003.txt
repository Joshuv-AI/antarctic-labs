Frequency of Thyroid Cancer in Multinodular Goiter Undergoing Thyroidectomy in Tertiary Care Centre in Pakistan: A Cross‐Sectional Analysis
Authors: Hasan Ahmed, Hira Moosa, Farah Hafiz Yusuf, Aleena Aftab, Rameez Hussain, Afnan W M Jobran, Syed Ali Arsal, Inibehe Ime Okon, Mazhar Iqbal, Muhammad Aniq Amir, Arbaz Zaman, Sakina Abbas, Muhammad Tauseef Alam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionGoiter is a common condition in the northwest regions of Pakistan, with multinodular goiter (MNG) being a frequent presentation of thyroid diseases. Multinodular goiter (MNG) is common worldwide, with variable reported risks of malignancy. Data from South Asia, particularly Pakistan, remains limited and somewhat outdated. This study highlights important expectations regarding suspicion of malignancy and emphasizes the prevalence of different types of malignancies in MNG patients.MethodsA retrospective cross‐sectional study was conducted with data collected from patient records who underwent thyroidectomy in the Department of Surgery from June 2019 to April 2024 in Karachi, Pakistan. A total of 520 patients aged 30–70 years undergoing total thyroidectomy for clinically and radiologically diagnosed MNG were included. Suspicious nodules were identified preoperatively by ultrasound and/or FNAC (Bethesda V–VI). Histopathology confirmed carcinoma. Patients with recurrent goiter, solitary nodules, thyroid crisis, pre‐existing malignancy, or significant comorbidities were excluded. Descriptive statistics, Chi‐square testing, and multivariate logistic regression were applied using SPSS version 25, with p‐values below 0.05 considered statistically significant.ResultsA total of 520 patients with MNG were included, of whom 84 (16%) had thyroid carcinoma. The mean age of carcinoma patients was 49.2 ± 9.4 years, and 76.9% were female. Histological subtypes included papillary carcinoma (64.2%), follicular carcinoma (23.8%), and anaplastic carcinoma (11.9%). In multivariate analysis, smoking (OR 2.41, 95% CI 1.08–5.38, p = 0.03) and urban residence (OR 1.89, 95% CI 1.03–3.47, p = 0.04) were independent predictors of papillary carcinoma.ConclusionThyroid carcinoma was present in 16% of MNG cases, predominantly papillary type. Smoking and urban residence emerged as significant risk factors for papillary thyroid carcinoma. Preoperative ultrasound and FNAC remain critical for risk stratification. Larger multicenter studies with molecular profiling are warranted.Keywords: multinodular goiter, thyroid carcinoma, thyroid nodule, thyroidectomy

Introduction

Multinodular goiter (MNG) affects 4%–7% of the population on neck palpation and up to 30%–50% on ultrasonography [1]. Thyroid tumors account for only 1% of the overall human cancer burden but they represent the most common malignancy of the endocrine system [2].

The incidence of thyroid carcinoma has increased significantly over the past three decades, especially in Asia, Europe, and North America [3, 4]. While improved imaging and widespread use of FNAC partly explain this rise, several studies suggest a true increase in papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC), linked to environmental and lifestyle factors [5, 6].

International data show variable rates of malignancy in MNG, ranging from 7% to 23% [7]. In Pakistan, older reports suggested a lower prevalence (2.9%–6.8%) [8], but recent studies have reported higher rates: Amin et al. 11.6% [9], and Rahman et al. 20% [10]. European multicenter studies report 12% [11], while Middle Eastern data show 10%–18% [12].

Papillary carcinoma accounts for the majority of thyroid cancers globally [13], followed by follicular and, rarely, anaplastic carcinoma [14]. Exposure to ionizing radiation, changing iodine nutrition, and increased detection of small indolent papillary thyroid microcarcinomas through improved imaging and pathological evaluation have been proposed as explanations for the worldwide rise in thyroid carcinoma incidence. The study aimed to determine the frequency of thyroid cancer among patients clinically and radiologically diagnosed with multinodular goiter who underwent thyroidectomy. Existing local studies demonstrate variable findings with limited recent data [15]. Data from this study would help in establishing the local prevalence of thyroid malignancy and addressing the variability of the data. Moreover, effective management plans will be developed with the knowledge gained from this study.

Methods

Ethical Approval

The research has been performed following the Declaration of Helsinki. This study was evaluated and approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Jinnah Sindh Medical College and permission to collect data was authorized by the Senior Registrar. Patient confidentiality and anonymity were maintained throughout the study in accordance with institutional ethical standards. All participants had the right to withdraw from the research at any point, and participation was entirely voluntary.

Study Design

A retrospective cross‐sectional study was conducted with data collected from patient records who underwent thyroidectomy in the Department of Surgery from June 2019 to April 2024 in Karachi, Pakistan. The study aimed to assess the management and outcomes of patients underwent thyroidectomy for suspicious thyroid nodules. Patients, aged 30–70 years, of either gender, were included using non‐probability, consecutive sampling. Exclusion criteria encompassed patients with recurrent goiter, solitary thyroid nodule, head and neck cancer, thyroid crisis, and those with a history of certain chronic illnesses. The work has been reported in line with the STROCSS criteria [16].

Source of Data

The data for this retrospective study were collected from patients presenting with multinodular goiter and suspicious thyroid nodules who underwent thyroidectomy at the Department of Surgery from June 2019 to April 2024 in Jinnah Sindh Medical College. The requirement for individual informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study. Demographic information and medical history were documented. Total thyroidectomy was performed by an experienced surgeon. A pathologist with over 10 years of experience conducted histological analysis of resected specimens. The findings were recorded in a structured proforma.

Participants Assessment

A total of 520 patients with multinodular goiter (MNG) were included and underwent detailed history taking and clinical assessment, focusing on the duration of goiter, presence of compressive symptoms, smoking history, prior radiation exposure, and family history. Physical examination evaluated thyroid size, nodularity, and cervical lymphadenopathy. All patients underwent ultrasound, with nodules assessed for suspicious features such as hypoechogenicity, microcalcifications, irregular margins, and a taller‐than‐wide shape. Based on ultrasound findings, 84 patients were considered suspicious for malignancy and subsequently underwent fine‐needle aspiration cytology (FNAC). Cytological findings were reported according to the Bethesda system, with categories V and VI considered positive.

Element of Data

The study was a retrospective cross‐sectional analysis conducted in the Department of Surgery from June 2019 to April 2024. The sample size was 520 patients, determined using WHO software with a 95% confidence level and a 7% margin of error. Non‐probability consecutive sampling was used. Patients aged 30–70 years who underwent thyroidectomy for suspicious thyroid nodules were included, while those with recurrent goiter, solitary thyroid nodules, head and neck cancer, thyroid crisis, and other significant comorbidities were excluded. Data were collected through informed consent, demographic information, and surgical outcomes, with histological analysis performed by experienced pathologists. Data analysis involved SPSS version 25 with descriptive statistics and stratification by age, gender, and other variables.

Data Collection Procedure

Once approval was granted by Jinnah Sindh Medical College, patients who met the inclusion criteria were included in the study. The data were collected retrospectively using a non‐probability consecutive sampling technique. The collected data were recorded in a structured proforma for analysis.

Data Analysis Procedure

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 25. For continuous variables like age and duration of multinodular goiter, mean ± SD was reported if normally distributed, and median (IQR) if not. Normally distributed continuous variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation. Frequencies and percentages were calculated for categorical variables such as gender, residence status, clinical symptoms, smoking status, and thyroid carcinoma (yes/no). Stratification was performed based on age, gender, residence status, smoking status, and duration of multinodular goiter to examine their effects on the outcome variable. Post‐stratification, Chi‐square, or Fisher's exact tests were applied, with a significance level set at p < 0.05.

Results

A total of 520 patients with MNG were included, out of which 84 (16%) were histologically confirmed to have thyroid carcinoma. The mean age of affected patients was 49.2 ± 9.4 years, with females comprising the majority (n = 60, 76.9%). The mean duration of goiter among these patients was 8.5 ± 2.1 months. Data on residential status, smoking history, and clinical symptoms, including dyspnea in the supine position, dysphagia, palpitations, and hoarseness, were also recorded (Table 1).

Discussion

Thyroid nodules are common worldwide, with clinically palpable nodules reported in 5%–26% of adults and ultrasonography‐detected nodules ranging from 19% to 68% [15, 17]. Most nodules are benign, with an estimated risk of malignancy ranging from 7% to 15%, influenced by factors such as age, sex, radiation exposure, family history, and others [18]. The global incidence of thyroid carcinoma has increased over the past few decades, partly due to the widespread use of high‐resolution ultrasound and other imaging modalities. This may also reflect a genuine rise in papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) [4]. In our study, 16% of all patients with multinodular goiter (MNG) had histologically confirmed thyroid carcinoma, consistent with the upper range of global reports (7%–23%) and slightly higher than older Pakistani data [10]. This suggests both improved detection and potential regional factors contributing to malignancy risk.

Our study population had a female predominance (76.9%), in line with the known higher prevalence of thyroid nodules in women [19]. Although female gender was not an independent predictor of papillary carcinoma in multivariate analysis, hormonal influences, particularly estrogen, may contribute to thyroid tumorigenesis by promoting proliferation in follicular cells [20]. The higher prevalence in urban residents (67.9%) and the significant association between urban residence and papillary carcinoma (OR 1.89, 95% CI 1.03–3.47) likely reflect environmental and lifestyle factors. Urban populations are exposed to higher levels of environmental pollutants, dietary variations, and stress, which may influence thyroid pathology [21]. Additionally, greater access to healthcare and advanced diagnostic facilities in urban settings may lead to higher detection rates of incidental thyroid cancers. Smoking was another independent risk factor (OR 2.41, 95% CI 1.08–5.38), consistent with prior studies linking tobacco exposure to oxidative stress, DNA damage, and carcinogenesis in thyroid tissue [22].

The mean age of patients with carcinoma in our study was 49.2 ± 9.4 years, which is comparable to reports from regional studies [23]. This aligns with the established understanding that thyroid malignancy incidence rises with age, although papillary carcinoma frequently affects middle‐aged adults. The histopathological spectrum observed predominantly papillary carcinoma (64.2%), followed by follicular (23.8%) and anaplastic carcinoma (11.9%) mirrors both local and international data [14]. Papillary carcinoma's predominance may reflect its strong association with radiation exposure, genetic predisposition, and higher rates of incidental detection on imaging [14]. Follicular and anaplastic carcinomas remain less frequent but are associated with more aggressive clinical courses, underscoring the need for vigilant preoperative assessment. Local studies in Pakistan have reported variable distributions of histological subtypes, with papillary carcinoma accounting for a range of 57%–89% of thyroid malignancies, suggesting regional consistency in the predominance of this subtype [8].

Our findings align with several regional and international studies. For example, Islam et al. reported a frequency of 15% thyroid carcinoma in MNG patients in a tertiary hospital in Bangladesh [24]. At the same time, Apostolou et al. observed similar prevalence rates in South Asia [25]. Studies from Saudi Arabia, Ethiopia, and other Middle Eastern countries also support the predominance of papillary carcinoma and similar demographic patterns [26]. Differences in reported prevalence between studies may result from variations in study populations, diagnostic modalities, referral patterns, iodine sufficiency, and environmental exposures. Notably, MNG is often endemic in regions with low dietary iodine, which may influence nodule development and carcinogenic potential [27].

Limitations

Despite these insights, our study has several limitations. It was a single‐center, hospital‐based study, which may introduce referral bias and limit generalizability. The relatively small number of follicular and anaplastic carcinoma cases constrained the statistical power to identify predictors for these subtypes. Data on iodine intake, environmental exposures, and genetic markers were not collected, which prevented an evaluation of these important explanatory factors. Finally, although FNAC and ultrasound were used to identify suspicious nodules preoperatively, interobserver variability and the retrospective component of data collection may have affected diagnostic accuracy. Future multicenter studies incorporating molecular profiling, standardized imaging protocols, and larger cohorts are recommended to enhance the understanding of thyroid carcinoma risk factors and refine preoperative stratification.

Conclusion

Among 520 MNG patients who underwent thyroidectomy over June 2019–April 2024, 84 (16%) had thyroid carcinoma, with papillary carcinoma being the most common subtype. Univariate and multivariate analyses confirmed smoking and urban residence as independent risk factors for papillary carcinoma. Careful preoperative evaluation and larger multicenter studies with multivariate modeling are recommended to refine risk assessment and management strategies in Pakistan.
