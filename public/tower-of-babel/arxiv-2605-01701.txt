Stability and Generalization for Decentralized Markov SGD
Jiahuan Wang, Ziqing Wen, Ping Luo, Dongsheng Li, Tao Sun

Abstract
Stochastic gradient methods are central to large-scale learning, yet their generalization theory typically relies on independent sampling assumptions. In many practical applications, data are generated by Markov chains and learning is performed in a decentralized manner, which introduces significant analytical challenges. In this work, we investigate the stability and generalization of decentralized stochastic gradient descent (SGD) and stochastic gradient descent ascent (SGDA) under Markov chain sampling. Leveraging a stability-based framework, we characterize how Markovian dependence and decentralized communication jointly influence generalization behavior. Our analysis captures the effects of network topology, Markov chain mixing properties, and primal–dual dynamics. We establish non-asymptotic generalization bounds for both algorithms, extending existing results on Markov stochastic gradient methods to decentralized and minimax settings.

Motivated by these gaps, this paper investigates the theoretical foundations of decentralized stochastic gradient descent and descent–ascent algorithms with Markov chain sampling, and aims to provide a rigorous characterization of their generalization behavior. We generalize stability-based generalization theory for Markov chain stochastic gradient methods to decentralized settings, and complement prior studies on decentralized optimization by providing statistically meaningful guarantees. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to systematically study the stability and generalization of decentralized SGD and SGDA under Markovian sampling.

Differences and Technical Challenges

Unlike existing analyses of decentralized SGD under i.i.d. sampling

;

;

;

Compared with centralized Mc-SGD

A key technical challenge is to control the interaction between these two effects without imposing mixing-time or spectral assumptions on the Markov chain.
This is resolved by exploiting aggregation identities that depend only on the update structure, rather than on the specific sampling mechanism.
As a result, i.i.d.-type stability bounds can still be recovered in the decentralized Markovian setting.

Contributions

Our main contributions can be summarized as follows.

Stability analysis of decentralized SGD with Markovian sampling.
We establish on-average stability bounds for decentralized SGD under Markov chain sampling (See Theorem 1).

Preliminaries

We study a decentralized learning setting with
m
computing nodes, where each node stores
n
training samples. The full dataset is denoted by
S=\{S_{1},S_{2},\cdots,S_{m}\}
, and each local dataset
S_{r}=\{Z_{1(1)},\cdots,Z_{k(r)},\cdots,Z_{n(m)}\}
consists of
n
samples drawn from an unknown distribution
\mathcal{D}
.
A learning algorithm
\mathcal{A}
, such as (decentralized) SGD, maps the dataset
S
to a model parameter in a hypothesis space
\mathcal{W}
. The performance of a model
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}
is evaluated through a loss function
f\left(\mathbf{w};Z\right)
, which induces the population risk (expected risk)

\min_{w\in\mathcal{W}}R(\mathbf{w}):=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[f\left(\mathbf{w};Z\right)\right].

Since the data-generating distribution is inaccessible, the algorithm instead minimizes its empirical analogue computed over the observed samples. In the decentralized setting, this empirical objective can be written as

\displaystyle R_{S}(\mathbf{w})=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{r=1}^{m}R_{S_{r}}=\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f\left(\mathbf{w};Z_{k(r)}\right),

(1)

with
R_{S_{r}}
denoting the corresponding local empirical risk at node/machine
r
.

Although
\mathcal{A}(S)
may fit the training data well, good empirical performance does not necessarily translate to good population performance. This motivates the study of the expected gap between the population and empirical risks evaluated at the algorithm output, namely

\epsilon_{\textrm{gen}}:=\mathbb{E}_{S,\mathcal{A}}\left[R(\mathcal{A}(S))-R_{S}(\mathcal{A}(S))\right].

(2)

which we refer to as the generalization error. Beyond generalization, we are also interested in how far the learned model is from the optimal population solution.

Definition 1
.

The excess generalization error of the learned model,
\epsilon_{\textrm{excess}}:=\mathbb{E}_{S,\mathcal{A}}\left[R(\mathcal{A}(S))-R(\mathbf{w}^{*})\right]
, where
\mathbf{w}^{*}
is denote the minimizer of
R(\cdot)
. This term can then be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,\mathcal{A}}\left[R(\mathcal{A}(S))-R(\mathbf{w}^{*})\right]=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{S,\mathcal{A}}\left[R(\mathcal{A}(S))-R_{S}(\mathcal{A}(S))\right]}_{\emph{Generalization\hskip 7.97224ptError}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{S,\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}(\mathcal{A}(S))-R_{S}({\mathbf{w}}_{S}^{*})\right]}_{\epsilon_{\textrm{opt}}:~\emph{Optimization\hskip 7.97224ptError}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{S,\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}({\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*}})-R(\mathbf{w}^{*})\right]}_{\emph{Test\hskip 7.97224ptError}},

where
\mathbf{w}^{\star}
be the minimizer of
R_{S}(\cdot)
.

Remark 1
.

Since
\mathbb{E}\left[R_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}^{*}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[R\left(\mathbf{w}^{*}\right)\right]
and the empirical risk minimizer satisfies
R_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*})\leq R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})
, the last item is guaranteed to be non-positive. Consequently, most theoretical analyses can be reduced to concentrating on the first two terms, the generalization error and the optimization error.

We now turn to decentralized minimax learning problems, which naturally arise in adversarial learning and robust optimization
10
;
25
. In this setting, the objective depends on a pair of variables: a primal variable
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a dual variable
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Given a loss function
f:\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow[0,\infty),
the population-level minimax objective is defined as

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}\max_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}}R(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}),~R(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}):=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[f\left(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v};Z\right)\right].

(3)

As before, the underlying distribution
\mathcal{D}
is unknown, and learning algorithms operate on a finite dataset distributed across
m
workers. The corresponding empirical objective is therefore given by

\displaystyle R_{S}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v})=\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f\left(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v};Z_{k(r)}\right).

(4)

In contrast to standard minimization, minimax learning involves two coupled variables, which makes generalization more nuanced. To streamline notation, we write the (possibly randomized) output of algorithm
\mathcal{A}
as
\mathcal{A}(S)=(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}(S),\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{v}}(S))
. We focus on two notions of performance that will be used throughout the paper
9
;
17
;
47
.

Definition 2

(Weak Primal-Dual (PD) Risk)
.

The weak Primal-Dual population risk
\Delta^{\mathbf{w}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}},\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{v}}\right)
:

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}}\mathbb{E}\left[R\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}({S}),\mathbf{v}\right)\right]-\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}\mathbb{E}\left[R\left(\mathbf{w},\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{v}}({S})\right)\right].

The weak PD empirical risk
\Delta_{\mathrm{emp}}^{\mathbf{w}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}},\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{v}}\right)
:

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}}\mathbb{E}\left[R_{{S}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathcal{S}),\mathbf{v}\right)\right]-\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}\mathbb{E}\left[R_{{S}}\left(\mathbf{w},\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{v}}({S})\right)\right].

The weak PD generalization error of the model:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^{\mathbf{w}}=\Delta^{\mathbf{w}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}},\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\Delta_{\mathrm{emp}}^{\mathbf{w}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}},\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{v}}\right).

Definition 3

(Primal Risk)
.

The primal population and empirical risks of
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})
:

\displaystyle F\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}({S})\right)=\max_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}}R\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathcal{S}),\mathbf{v}\right),

\displaystyle F_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}({S})\right)=\max_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}}R_{{S}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathcal{S}),\mathbf{v}\right).

The primal generalization error is defined as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^{\mathrm{P}}=\mathbb{E}_{S,\mathcal{A}}\left[F\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}({S})\right)-F_{S}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}({S})\right)\right].

The excess primal population risk of the model:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathrm{excess}}^{\mathrm{P}}=\mathbb{E}_{S,\mathcal{A}}\left[F\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}({S})\right)-\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}F(\mathbf{w})\right].

3.1
Decentralized SGD with Markov Sampling

We consider a decentralized optimization framework based on stochastic gradient descent, originally proposed in
(
21
)
, and adopt its projected variant to handle constrained parameter domains. In this setting, a network of
m nodes cooperatively solves a learning problem while maintaining local model copies. Communication between nodes is governed by a weighted graph, through which neighboring nodes exchange information at each iteration. At a high level, each iteration of the algorithm consists of two stages. First, nodes perform a consensus operation by aggregating model information from their neighbors. Subsequently, each node updates its local model using a stochastic gradient computed from a data sample generated by a local Markov chain, followed by a projection step to ensure feasibility. This procedure naturally extends decentralized SGD to scenarios where data are not independently sampled.

Update Order.
Following existing decentralized SGD schemes
21
, the update at each node can be organized according to two distinct patterns. In the Gradient-then-Consensus (GtC) variant, each node first performs a local stochastic gradient step and subsequently mixes the updated parameters with its neighbors:

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}^{t+{1}}{(i)}=\sum_{l=1}^{m}P_{il}\left[\mathbf{w}^{t}{(l)}-\eta_{t}\nabla f(\mathbf{w}^{t}{(l)};Z_{j_{t}(l)})\right].

(5)

In contrast, the Consensus-then-Gradient (CtG) variant reverses this order. Nodes first aggregate information through the gossip matrix and then apply a stochastic gradient update using locally sampled data:

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}^{t+{1}}{(i)}=\sum_{l=1}^{m}P_{il}\mathbf{w}^{t}{(l)}-\eta_{t}\nabla f(\mathbf{w}^{t}{(i)};Z_{j_{t}(i)}).

(6)

From an optimization viewpoint, GtC and CtG exhibit similar convergence behavior, enabling communication and computation to be overlapped in practice. Their difference becomes pronounced in stability and generalization analysis: the CtG update relies solely on local gradients, which magnifies the effects of data heterogeneity and network disagreement. Consequently, establishing stability guarantees for CtG typically requires more refined analysis or stronger structural assumptions
33
;
4
;
46

Markov Chain Sampling.
We now summarize the basic concepts of finite-state, time-homogeneous Markov chains that are relevant to our analysis. These notions formalize the dependence structure induced by Markovian data sampling.

Definition 4
.

A stochastic process
\{X_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
taking values in a finite state space
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
is called a time-homogeneous Markov chain with transition matrix
H\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
if

\Pr\left(X_{k+1}=j\mid X_{0},\ldots,X_{k}=i\right)=\Pr\left(X_{k+1}=j\mid X_{k}=i\right)={H}_{i,j},

for all states
i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and
k\geq 0
.

Let
\pi^{k}
denote the distribution of
X_{k}
, viewed as a row vector.
Then
\pi^{k}=\pi^{k-1}{H}=\pi^{0}{H}^{k}
, where
{H}^{k}
denotes the
k
-step transition matrix. A Markov chain is said to be irreducible if every state can be reached from any other state in a finite number of steps. A state is aperiodic if it does not exhibit deterministic cyclic behavior, and the chain is aperiodic if all states are aperiodic. Under irreducibility and aperiodicity, the chain admits a unique stationary distribution
\pi^{*}
satisfying
\pi^{*}=\pi^{*}{H}
and
\min_{i}\pi_{i}^{*}>0
. Moreover, the transition matrix converges as

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{H}^{k}=\left[\left(\pi^{*}\right)^{\top},\cdots,\left(\pi^{*}\right)^{\top}\right]^{\top}=:\Pi^{*}.

Appendix A
Centralized vs Decentralized

Centralized and decentralized optimization represent two fundamentally different communication paradigms. In centralized SGD, all workers communicate with a central server that aggregates local updates and broadcasts the global model. This design enables fast information aggregation but may suffer from scalability limitations, communication bottlenecks, and single-point failures.
Decentralized SGD removes the central coordinator and relies on peer-to-peer communication over a network. Although information propagation is inherently more gradual and depends on the connectivity of the underlying graph, decentralized methods offer improved scalability, robustness, and fault tolerance, making them well suited for large-scale and distributed environments. The convergence behavior of decentralized methods is therefore closely tied to the spectral properties of the communication graph, with the spectral gap serving as a key measure of information mixing efficiency. In terms of the spectral gap
\gamma=1-\lambda
, larger values correspond to faster mixing and more efficient information propagation. From best to worst (in order of magnitude), the spectral gaps of several common network topologies satisfy:

\displaystyle\mathrm{complete~graphs/expanders/star~graphs}~(\Theta(1))\succ\mathrm{grids}~(\Theta(1/m))\succ\mathrm{rings}~(\Theta(1/m^{2}))

\displaystyle\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|{\mathbf{w}}^{t}-{\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t}\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{4L^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\eta_{s}^{2}+16L^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\eta_{s}\sum_{q=1}^{s-1}\eta_{q}\lambda^{s-q-1}}+\frac{4L}{mn}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\eta_{s+1}

(12)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{T}\right\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t}-\mathbf{w}_{(rk)}^{t}\right\|_{2}}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\left(\sqrt{4L^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\eta_{s}^{2}+16L^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\eta_{s}\sum_{q=1}^{s-1}\eta_{q}\lambda^{s-q-1}}+\frac{4L}{mn}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\eta_{s+1}\right)}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}}.

If

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{T}\right\|_{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\eta\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(2L\eta\sqrt{t}+\frac{4\eta L\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{1-\lambda}}+\frac{4\eta Lt}{mn}\right)}{T\eta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\eta L\sqrt{T}+\frac{4\eta L\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{1-\lambda}}+\frac{4\eta LT}{mn}.

Moreover, we obtain the generlization error in nonsmooth case

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,\mathcal{A}}\left[R(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})-R_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})\right]\leq 2\eta L\sqrt{T}+\frac{4\eta L\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{1-\lambda}}+\frac{4\eta LT}{mn}.

Consider the output for the GtC algorithm,

Theorem 8

(Stability Bound)
.

Suppose that
f(\mathbf{w};Z)
is convex, L-Lipshitz and
\beta
-smooth. If

\eta\leq 2/\beta
, then for DMc-SGD (GtC) with
T
iterations, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathcal{A}(S)-\mathcal{A}(S_{rk})\right\|_{2}\right]\leq\frac{2L}{mn}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{w}}^{t}-{\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t}\right\|_{2}=

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{w}^{t}(i)-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t}(i)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\sum_{l=1}^{m}P_{i,l}\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(l)-\eta_{t}\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(l);Z_{j_{t}(l)}\right)\right)-\sum_{l=1}^{m}P_{i,l}\left({\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t-1}(l)-\eta_{t}\nabla f\left({\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t-1}(l);\tilde{Z}_{j_{t}(l)}\right)\right)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\left\|\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(l)-\eta_{t}\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(l);Z_{j_{t}(l)}\right)\right)-\left({\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t-1}(l)-\eta_{t}\nabla f\left({\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t-1}(l);\tilde{Z}_{j_{t}(l)}\right)\right)\right\|_{2}.

If
j_{t}\neq k
, then

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{w}}^{t}-{\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t}\right\|_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\left\|\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(l)-\eta_{t}\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(l);Z_{j_{t}(l)}\right)\right)-\left({\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t-1}(l)-\eta_{t}\nabla f\left({\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t-1}(l);{Z}_{j_{t}(l)}\right)\right)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(l)-\mathbf{w}_{(rk)}^{t-1}(l)\right\|_{2}=\left\|{\mathbf{w}}^{t-1}-{\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t-1}\right\|_{2}.

j_{t}=k

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{w}}^{t}-{\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t}\right\|_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\left\|\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(l)-\eta_{t}\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(l);Z_{j_{t}(l)}\right)\right)-\left({\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t-1}(l)-\eta_{t}\nabla f\left({\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t-1}(l);\tilde{Z}_{j_{t}(l)}\right)\right)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(l)-\mathbf{w}_{(rk)}^{t-1}(l)\right\|_{2}+\frac{\eta_{t}}{m}\left\|\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(r);Z_{k(r)}\right)-\nabla f\left({\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t-1}(r);\tilde{Z}_{k(r)}\right)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{w}}^{t-1}-{\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t-1}\right\|_{2}+\frac{2\eta_{t}L}{m}.

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{w}}^{t}-{\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|{\mathbf{w}}^{t-1}-{\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{t-1}\right\|_{2}+\frac{2\eta_{t}L}{m}\mathbb{I}_{[j_{t}=k]}.

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{w}}^{T}-{\mathbf{w}}_{(rk)}^{T}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{2L}{m}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{I}_{[j_{t}=k]}.

and
r

where the last inequality uses
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{[j_{t}=k]}=1
.

∎

Appendix E
Optimization Error of DMc-SGD

Theorem 9

(Convex Case)
.

Consider DMc-SGD applied to a convex loss
f(w;Z)
under Assumptions 1–3. Let the algorithm run for T iterations from
\mathbf{w}^{0}=0
with the stepsize
\eta_{t}\leq 2/\beta
. Denote
D_{0}=\left\|\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*}\right\|_{2}
and
D=\left[\sum_{s=1}^{T}\eta_{s}\left(L^{2}+2L^{2}\sum_{q=1}^{s-1}\eta_{q}\lambda^{t-q-1}+2\sup_{Z\in\mathcal{Z}}f(0,Z)\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}+D_{0}
, and define the truncation window

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t}=\min\left\{\max\left\{\lceil\frac{\log\left(2C_{H}Dnt\right)}{\log\left(1/\lambda(H)\right)}\rceil,K_{H}\right\},t\right\},~t\in[T].

Then we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{L^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\left(\sum_{q=t-\mathcal{T}_{t}+1}^{t}\eta_{q}+\sum_{q=t-\mathcal{T}_{t}+1}^{t-1}\eta_{q}\right)}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+4LD\sum_{t=1}^{K_{H}-1}\eta_{t}}{2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2D\beta L\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\sum_{q=1}^{t-1}\eta_{q}\lambda^{t-q-1}+\sum_{t=K_{H}}^{T}\frac{L\eta_{t}}{2t}}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}}+\frac{L^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}^{2}}{2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}}.

Moreover, when Assumption 4 holds. If
\eta_{t}=\eta=1/\sqrt{T\log T}
, the bound simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*})\right]=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\log T}}{\sqrt{T}\log(1/\lambda(H))}+\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)\sqrt{T\log T}}\right).

Proof.

We start from the standard convexity argument for weighted averaging. Since
R_{S}(\cdot)
is convex,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\right)\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{t})-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{t})-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}})\right]+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}})-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*})\right],

(13)

where we introduce a time shift by
\mathcal{T}_{t}=\min\left\{\max\left\{\lceil\frac{\log\left(2C_{H}Dnt\right)}{\log\left(1/\lambda(H)\right)}\rceil,K_{H}\right\},t\right\}
.

Step 1: controlling the drift term

For the first term, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{t})-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}})\right]=

\displaystyle L\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t}-\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right\|_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[\sum_{q=t-\mathcal{T}_{t}+1}^{t}\eta_{q}\left\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w}^{q}(i);Z_{j_{q}(i)}\right)\right\|_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\sum_{q=t-\mathcal{T}_{t}+1}^{t}\eta_{q}.

Step 2: a stationary–deviation decomposition for the Markov term

For the second term, we can estimate that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{j_{t}}\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)\right)\mid\mathbf{w}^{0},\cdots,\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}},Z_{j_{1}(r)},\cdots,Z_{j_{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}(r)}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)\right)\left[H^{\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right]_{j_{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}(r),k}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)\right)\left(\left[H^{\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right]_{j_{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}(r),k}-\frac{1}{n}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)\right)\left(\left[H^{\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right]_{j_{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}(r),k}-\frac{1}{n}\right)\right]+R_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right)-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*}).

Rearranging the above equality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right)-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*})\right]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)\right)\left(\frac{1}{n}-\left[H^{\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right]_{j_{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}(r),k}\right)\right]\right].

Summing over
t
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right)-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)\right)\right]}_{\Im}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)\right)\left(\frac{1}{n}-\left[H^{\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right]_{j_{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}(r),k}\right)\right]\right]}_{\wp}.

Step 3: bounding the “stationary part” via a distance recursion

Estimate the
\Im

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t-1}-\frac{\eta_{t}}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nabla f(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(i);Z_{j_{t}(i)})-\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{2\eta_{t}}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\langle\mathbf{w}^{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*},\nabla f(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(i);Z_{j_{t}(i)})\right\rangle+\frac{\eta_{t}^{2}}{m^{2}}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nabla f(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(i);Z_{j_{t}(i)})\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{2\eta_{t}}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\langle\mathbf{w}^{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*},\nabla f(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(i);Z_{j_{t}(i)})-\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-1};Z_{j_{t}(i)}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{2\eta_{t}}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\langle\mathbf{w}^{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*},\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-1};Z_{j_{t}(i)}\right)\right\rangle+\frac{\eta_{t}^{2}}{m^{2}}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nabla f(\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(i);Z_{j_{t}(i)})\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2D\beta\eta_{t}}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(i)-\mathbf{w}^{t-1}\right\|_{2}-\frac{2\eta_{t}}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-1};Z_{j_{t}(i)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(i)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+{\eta_{t}^{2}}L^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2D\beta\eta_{t}}{\sqrt{m}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t-1}(i)-\mathbf{w}^{t-1}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{2\eta_{t}}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(i)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(i)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\eta_{t}}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(i)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-1};Z_{j_{t}(i)}\right)\right)+{\eta_{t}^{2}}L^{2}

\displaystyle+{2L^{2}\eta_{t}}\sum_{q=t-\mathcal{T}_{t}+1}^{t-1}\eta_{q}+{\eta_{t}^{2}}L^{2}.

t

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(i)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(i)}\right)\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+2D\beta L\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\sum_{q=1}^{t-1}\eta_{q}\lambda^{t-q-1}+L^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\sum_{q=t-\mathcal{T}_{t}+1}^{t-1}\eta_{q}+\frac{L^{2}}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}^{2}.

5

\displaystyle\Bigg|\frac{1}{n}-\left[H^{\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right]_{k,k^{\prime}}\Bigg|\leq\frac{1}{2Dnt}.

Based on the update rule,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq

Recall that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)\right)\left(\frac{1}{n}-\left[H^{\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right]_{j_{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}(r),k}\right)\right]

\displaystyle 2L\left\|\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}-\mathbf{w}^{*}\right\|_{2}\leq 2LD.

Then we get

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{K_{H}-1}\eta_{t}\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)\right)\left(\frac{1}{n}-\left[H^{\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right]_{j_{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}(r),k}\right)\right]\right]\leq 2LD\sum_{t=1}^{K_{H}-1}\eta_{t}.

Combine the above inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left(f\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*};Z_{j_{t}(r)}\right)\right)\left(\frac{1}{n}-\left[H^{\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right]_{j_{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}(r),k}\right)\right]\right]\leq

\displaystyle 2LD\sum_{t=1}^{K_{H}-1}\eta_{t}+\sum_{t=K_{H}}^{T}\frac{L\eta_{t}}{2t}.

Back to the initial term,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}}\right)-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+4D\beta L\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\sum_{q=1}^{t-1}\eta_{q}\lambda^{t-q-1}+L^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\sum_{q=t-\mathcal{T}_{t}+1}^{t-1}\eta_{q}+\frac{L^{2}}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}^{2}+2LD\sum_{t=1}^{K_{H}-1}\eta_{t}+\sum_{t=K_{H}}^{T}\frac{L\eta_{t}}{2t}.

Combine the two term,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\right)\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{t})-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}})\right]+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{t-\mathcal{T}_{t}})-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\sum_{q=t-\mathcal{T}_{t}+1}^{t}\eta_{q}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+2D\beta L\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\sum_{q=1}^{t-1}\eta_{q}\lambda^{t-q-1}

\displaystyle+L^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\sum_{q=t-\mathcal{T}_{t}+1}^{t-1}\eta_{q}+\frac{L^{2}}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}^{2}+2LD\sum_{t=1}^{K_{H}-1}\eta_{t}+\sum_{t=K_{H}}^{T}\frac{L\eta_{t}}{2t}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}^{*})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{L^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\left(\sum_{q=t-\mathcal{T}_{t}+1}^{t}\eta_{q}+\sum_{q=t-\mathcal{T}_{t}+1}^{t-1}\eta_{q}\right)}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+4LD\sum_{t=1}^{K_{H}-1}\eta_{t}}{2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2D\beta L\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\sum_{q=1}^{t-1}\eta_{q}\lambda^{t-q-1}+\sum_{t=K_{H}}^{T}\frac{L\eta_{t}}{2t}}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}}+\frac{L^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}^{2}}{2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}}.

Furthermore, choosing

and noting that

, we can get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[R_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})-R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\right]=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{T}_{t}\eta^{2}+\frac{\eta^{3}T^{3/2}}{(1-\lambda)^{3/2}}+T\eta^{2}+\frac{K_{H}\eta^{2}\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{1-\lambda}}}{T\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\log T}\left(1+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{T}_{t}\eta^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{\eta^{2}\sqrt{T}}{(1-\lambda)^{3/2}}+\frac{K_{H}\eta}{\sqrt{1-\lambda}\sqrt{T}}\right).

(14)

Back to the

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[\max_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}}R_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T},\mathbf{v})-\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}R_{S}(\mathbf{w},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2L(D_{\mathbf{w}}+D_{\mathbf{v}})K_{H}+\sum_{t=K_{H}}^{T}\frac{L}{t^{2}}}{T}+\frac{4(D_{\mathbf{w}}^{2}+D_{\mathbf{v}}^{2})+\frac{16(D_{\mathbf{w}}+D_{\mathbf{v}})\beta L\eta^{2}T}{1-\lambda}}{2T\eta}

\displaystyle+\frac{12L^{2}\eta^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{T}_{t}+{T\eta^{2}}L^{2}}{T\eta}.

Similiar to DMc-SGD case, let
J=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2C_{H}Dn\lambda(H)^{K_{H}}}}
, if
t\leq J
, we can estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{J}\mathcal{T}_{t}\eta^{2}\leq JK_{H}\eta^{2}=\frac{K_{H}}{T\log T\sqrt{2C_{H}Dn\lambda(H)^{K_{H}}}}.

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{J}\mathcal{T}_{t}\eta^{2}+\sum_{t=J+1}^{T}\mathcal{T}_{t}\eta^{2}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{K_{H}}{T\log T\sqrt{2C_{H}n\lambda(H)^{K_{H}}}}+\frac{1}{\log(1/\lambda(H))}\right).

Then we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[\max_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}}R_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T},\mathbf{v})-\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}R_{S}(\mathbf{w},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{K_{H}}{T}+\frac{\eta}{(1-\lambda)}+\frac{1+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{T}_{t}\eta^{2}}{T\eta}+\eta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{K_{H}}{T}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{T\log T}(1-\lambda)}+\frac{\sqrt{\log T}}{\sqrt{T}\log(1/\lambda(H))}+\frac{K_{H}}{T^{3/2}\sqrt{\log T}\sqrt{2C_{H}n\lambda(H)^{K_{H}}}}\right).

If
K_{H}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[\max_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}}R_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T},\mathbf{v})-\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}R_{S}(\mathbf{w},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})\right]=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T\log T}(1-\lambda)}+\frac{\sqrt{\log T}}{\sqrt{T}\log(1/\lambda(H))}\right).

∎

F.5
PD Population Risk

Proof.

When all step sizes are equal, we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathbf{w}}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})-\Delta_{\mathrm{emp}}^{\mathbf{w}}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})\leq\frac{4\sqrt{2}\eta^{2}\beta LT}{1-\lambda}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}\eta LT}{mn}.

Combining with the PD empirical risk (Theorem 16), we obtain that

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathbf{w}}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})=

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathbf{w}}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})-\Delta_{\mathrm{emp}}^{\mathbf{w}}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})+\Delta_{\mathrm{emp}}^{\mathbf{w}}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4\sqrt{2}\eta^{2}\beta LT}{1-\lambda}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}\eta LT}{mn}+\frac{2L(D_{\mathbf{w}}+D_{\mathbf{v}})K_{H}+\sum_{t=K_{H}}^{T}\frac{L}{t^{2}}}{T}+\frac{4(D_{\mathbf{w}}^{2}+D_{\mathbf{v}}^{2})+\frac{16(D_{\mathbf{w}}+D_{\mathbf{v}})\beta L\eta^{2}T}{1-\lambda}}{2T\eta}

\displaystyle+\frac{12L^{2}\eta^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{T}_{t}+{T\eta^{2}}L^{2}}{T\eta}.

If we choose
T=mn
,
\eta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T\log T}}
and
K_{H}=0
, we get

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathbf{w}}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)\log(mn)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{mn\log(mn)}(1-\lambda)}+\frac{\sqrt{\log(mn)}}{\sqrt{mn}\log(1/\lambda(H))}\right).

∎

F.6
Excess Primal Population Risk

Theorem 17

(Excess primal population risk)
.

Suppose that Assumptions 3-6 hold. Assume for all
Z
, the function
(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v})\mapsto f(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v};Z)
is convex-concave. Assume
\mathbf{v}\mapsto R(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v})
is
\rho
-strongly-concave. Let
\left\{\mathbf{w}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\}_{t=1}^{T}
be produced by DMc-SGDA with
\eta_{t}\equiv\eta
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be defined by
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}(S)=\overline{\mathbf{w}}_{T}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{v}}(S)=\overline{\mathbf{v}}_{T}
. If we choose
T=mn,\eta=(T\log(T))^{-1/2}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,\mathcal{A}}\left[F\left(\overline{\mathbf{w}}_{T}\right)\right]-\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}F(\mathbf{w})=\mathcal{O}({1}/{\sqrt{mn\log(mn)}(1-\lambda)}+\sqrt{\log(mn)}/(\sqrt{mn}\log(1/\lambda(P)))).

Proof.

The following decomposition is applied:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})-F(\mathbf{w}^{*,\mathrm{G}})\right]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})-F_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[R_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*,\mathrm{G}},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*,\mathrm{G}},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})-R(\mathbf{w}^{*,\mathrm{G}},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[R(\mathbf{w}^{*,\mathrm{G}},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})-F(\mathbf{w}^{*,\mathrm{G}})\right].

Due to
R(\mathbf{w}^{*,\mathrm{G}},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})\leq F(\mathbf{w}^{*,\mathrm{G}})
, we only need to consider the first three items. For the first term, we can obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})-F_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})\right]\leq 2\sqrt{2}L^{2}(1+\beta/\rho)\left(\frac{2\eta^{2}\beta T}{1-\lambda}+\frac{\eta T}{mn}\right).

The third term, similar to the first, forms a duality and yields a similar result

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[R_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*,\mathrm{G}},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})-R(\mathbf{w}^{*,\mathrm{G}},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})\right]\leq 2\sqrt{2}L^{2}(1+\beta/\rho)\left(\frac{2\eta^{2}\beta T}{1-\lambda}+\frac{\eta T}{mn}\right).

For the second term, it is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[R_{S}(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*,\mathrm{G}},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{T})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2L(D_{\mathbf{w}}+D_{\mathbf{v}})K_{H}+\sum_{t=K_{H}}^{T}\frac{L}{t^{2}}}{T}+\frac{4(D_{\mathbf{w}}^{2}+D_{\mathbf{v}}^{2})+\frac{16(D_{\mathbf{w}}+D_{\mathbf{v}})\beta L\eta^{2}T}{1-\lambda}}{2T\eta}

\displaystyle+\frac{12L^{2}\eta^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{T}_{t}+{T\eta^{2}}L^{2}}{T\eta}.

If we choose
T=mn
,
\eta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T\log T}}
and
K_{H}=0
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F(\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T})-F(\mathbf{w}^{*,\mathrm{G}})\right]=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{(1+\beta/\rho)}{(1-\lambda)\log(mn)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{mn\log(mn)}(1-\lambda)}+\frac{\sqrt{\log(mn)}}{\sqrt{mn}\log(1/\lambda(H))}\right).

∎

Appendix G
Additional discussions

This section provides several clarifications and minor corrections to existing results that are closely related to our analysis. We emphasize that the issues discussed below do not affect the qualitative convergence behavior or the main conclusions of the corresponding works, but addressing them helps ensure technical correctness and consistency.

1. Clarifications on
41

•

Page 16–17.
The inequality stated as “by the convexity of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}
” cannot be directly justified from convexity alone. A valid argument requires a more careful decomposition similar to the technique developed in Section C.2 of this paper, where the bound is derived via an explicit expansion and regrouping of terms rather than a direct convexity claim. Importantly, correcting this step does not change the resulting convergence order.

•

Page 26.
After substituting the stepsize, the term
T\eta/n
does not simplify to
\sqrt{T}/n
. While this algebraic inconsistency affects intermediate expressions, the final convergence rate remains unchanged once the correct substitution is applied.

•

Page 27.
When substituting back into Eq. (B.25), the correct expression should be

\log T/T^{3/2}\eta\log(1/\lambda(P))=2\log n/n^{3/2}\log(1/\lambda(P))

rather than a rate scaling with
\sqrt{n}
.

•

Page 36. The term
\Delta^{\mathbf{w}}
should scale as
\Delta^{\mathbf{w}}=\frac{\sqrt{\log n}}{\sqrt{n}\log(1/\lambda(P))}
.

•

Page 37. The factor
L/\rho
does not influence the term as stated. After proper simplification, the correct convergence rate should again scale as

\frac{\sqrt{\log n}}{\sqrt{n}\log(1/\lambda(P))}
.

2. Clarifications on
47

•

Page 15 (Lemma 1, Case b).
The proof implicitly assumes that “
x
strongly convex +

y
strongly concave” implies strong monotonicity of the joint operator in
(x,y)
. This implication does not generally hold. A correct argument should instead follow the operator-theoretic approach used in
9
;
17
, which combines strong monotonicity with Lipschitz continuity. Due to the absence of a clean non-expansive property, this approach typically requires a more restrictive stepsize condition, leading nonetheless to a comparable convergence rate.

•

Stability notion in the decentralized setting.
The stability analysis in
47
considers perturbations where each worker contains an anomalous sample. This differs from the standard definition of stability in decentralized learning, where a single global sample is perturbed across the distributed dataset
33
;
48
;
1
;
46
. The latter notion is more consistent with classical algorithmic stability and is adopted in the present work.
