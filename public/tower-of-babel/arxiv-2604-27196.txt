Technical Note on Relating Scores of Tilted Distributions
Curtis McDonald

Abstract
Recent results in [ 4 , Lemma 3.3] have shown that for a linear tilt to a reference measure, the scores that would be produced under convolution with a normal variable can be expressed in terms of convolutions of the original density. Here, we extend that result to include constant negative diagonal tilts as well. The relationship follows from relating the denoisers of the two densities, which define the scores via Tweedie formula. A linear tilt results in a location shift to the score operator, while a quadratic tilt results in both a location shift and a time shift. Thus the scores of the tilted density can be understood as the scores of the original convolution process at a different location and noise level. These results are of interest to those in the score based diffusion community, and may lead to better score estimators which take advantage of these tilted score relationships.

Introduction

A core problem that has emerged in modern sampling algorithms has become estimating the score of a convolution of a target density with a normal random variable. Scores produced via convolution with a normal random variable are the natural scores of densities produced under the forward flow Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process, and estimating these scores is a necessary module to implement the reverse score based diffusion model sampling procedure. \displaystyle\tilde{p}

\displaystyle\propto p(x)e^{v^{T}x}\quad\quad~s.t.~\quad\quad\int p(x)e^{v^{T}x}dx<\infty

Then define a new variable under convolution with

\displaystyle W_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}}\tilde{X}+\gamma Z

\displaystyle\tilde{X}\sim\tilde{P},Z\sim N(0,I)

Then

2
Score Relationship

Consider a reference measure
p(x)
. Define a new variable
U
for any
\sigma\in[0,1]

\displaystyle U=\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}X+\sigma Z,Z\sim N(0,I)

(9)

or, equivalently, conditional measure

\displaystyle q(u|x,\sigma)\sim N(\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}x,\sigma^{2}I)

(10)

Then
X
and
U
share the joint density at a fixed
\sigma
,

\displaystyle q_{\sigma}(x,u)=p(x)q(u|x,\sigma)

\displaystyle\propto p(x)e^{-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\|u-\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}x\|^{2}}

(11)

and we have the reverse conditional density which can be written as

\displaystyle q(x|u,\sigma)

\displaystyle\propto p(x)e^{\frac{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}{\sigma^{2}}u^{T}x-\frac{1-\sigma^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\|x\|^{2}}

(12)

this is both a linear tilt and constant negative diagonal tilt to
p(x)
.

Define the marginal density of
U
at a point
\sigma
as
q(u,\sigma)
. There is a well known expression for the score
\nabla\log q(u,\sigma)
under Tweedie formula
[
5
,
3
,
6
,
1

Theorem 1

(Tweedie Formula)
.

\displaystyle\nabla\log q(u,\sigma)

\displaystyle=E_{q}[-\frac{u-\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}x}{\sigma^{2}}|u]=-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}u+\frac{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}{\sigma^{2}}E_{q}[x|u,\sigma]

(13)

An important object is called the denoiser,

Definition 1
.

The denoiser is defined as the reverse conditional expression for
x|u
at a given
\sigma
.

\displaystyle F[u,\sigma]=E_{q}[x|u,\sigma]

(14)

Via Tweedie formula, the score is an affine map of the denoiser. Using the form of the reverse conditional density in (
12
), the denoiser is the expectation of
p(x)
under a specific linear and constant negative diagonal tilt.

\displaystyle F[u,\sigma]

\displaystyle=\int x\frac{p(x)e^{\frac{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}{\sigma^{2}}u^{T}x-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{\sigma}^{2}-1)\|x\|^{2}}}{E_{p}[e^{\frac{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}{\sigma^{2}}u^{T}x-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{\sigma}^{2}-1)\|x\|^{2}}]}dx

(15)

Thus it should be no surprise if
p^{*}
is itself a linear tilt and a constant negative diagonal tilt to
p
its denoiser is the denoiser of
p
under a time and location shift.

Theorem 2
.

Let
p(x)
have denoiser
F[u,\sigma]
. Let
p^{*}(x)
be a linear tilt by a vector
v
to
p
and a constant negative diagonal tilt with scaling
s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle p^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{p(x)e^{v^{T}x-\frac{1}{2}s\|x\|^{2}}}{E_{p}[e^{v^{T}x-\frac{1}{2}s\|x\|^{2}}]}

(16)

Assume
X^{\prime}\sim p^{*}
and for any
\sigma\in[0,1]
define random variable

\displaystyle U^{\prime}=\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}X^{\prime}+\sigma Z,Z\sim N(0,I)

(17)

Denote the denoiser for
p^{*}(x)
as
G[u,\sigma]
. Then the denoiser for
p^{*}
is the denoiser for
p
evaluated at an affine mapping of the inputs. Define the time shift and location shift,

\displaystyle u^{\prime}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\sqrt{(1-\sigma^{2}+s\sigma^{2})(1+s\sigma^{2})}}v+\sqrt{\frac{1-\sigma^{2}}{(1-\sigma^{2}+s\sigma^{2})(1+s\sigma^{2})}}u

(18)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}=\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{1+s\sigma^{2}}}

(19)

Then the denoiser for
p^{*}
is the denoiser for
p
evaluated at these new values

\displaystyle G[u,\sigma]=F[u^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}]

(20)

Proof.

The proof is an algebra procedure in completing the square and collecting like terms. All terms involved are either linear or quadratic and combine under simple rules. See Appendix.
∎

This theorem is a statement that the denoiser operator is closed under constant negative diagonal tilts. The denoiser of a density of the form (
16
) is the same as the denoiser of
p
evaluated at a location shift
u^{\prime}
and time shift
\sigma^{\prime}
.

The scores, being affine maps of the denoiser via Tweedie formula, results in the score of
p^{*}
under convolution being the score of
p
under convolution at a different location and time point, plus an affine map.

Theorem 3
.

Using the definition of
u^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}
in (
18
) and (
19
), the score of the tilt under convolution can be related back to the score of the original density under convolution

\displaystyle\nabla\log q^{*}(u,\sigma)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}(u-\sqrt{\frac{1-\sigma^{2}}{1-(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}}}u^{\prime})+\frac{(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}{\sigma^{2}\sqrt{1-(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}}}\nabla\log q(u^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}).

(21)

Proof.

Using Tweedie formula combined with Theorem
2
, gives the expression for the scores once the denoisers are related. See Appendix.
∎

We refer to the value
u^{\prime}
as a location shift in the score, as we are evaluating the previous score at a different spatial location. We refer to the
\sigma^{\prime}
value as a time shift, as this is the variance that would be produced at a different time point in an OU process.

It may seem that either when
\sigma=0,1
, certain quantities in (
21
) are dividing by zero. However, by rearranging the expression that is not the case, it is a well defined equation for all
\sigma

Corollary 1
.

With linear tilt vector

\displaystyle\nabla\log q^{*}(u,\sigma)

\displaystyle=v\frac{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}{1-\sigma^{2}+s\sigma^{2}}-u(\frac{s}{1-\sigma^{2}+s\sigma^{2}})+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+s\sigma^{2}}}\frac{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}{\sqrt{1+s\sigma^{2}-\sigma^{2}}}\nabla\log q(u^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})

\sigma=0

Proof.

See Appendex

If

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma,u^{\prime}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}v+u,
