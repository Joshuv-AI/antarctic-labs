A chromosome-level reference genome assembly and annotation of Serrodes campana
Authors: Lu Li, Junbao Wen, Liming Jia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Serrodes campana (Lepidoptera: Noctuidae) is an economically important moth distributed in subtropical and tropical Asia, but genomic resources for this species have been lacking. Here, we present a chromosome-level reference genome assembly for S. campana generated using PacBio HiFi long reads, Illumina short reads, Hi-C sequencing, and transcriptomic data from 12 RNA-seq samples. The final assembly spans 627.9 Mb, with a contig N50 of 21.4 Mb and a scaffold N50 of 21.6 Mb, and 99.29% of the assembled sequences were anchored to 31 pseudochromosomes. BUSCO assessment recovered 99.56% complete insect genes, supporting the high completeness of the assembly. We predicted 16,047 protein-coding genes, of which 96.65% were functionally annotated, and identified repetitive elements accounting for 33.79% of the genome. This genome assembly and associated annotation resources provide a foundation for gene annotation, comparative genomics, genome evolution studies, and investigations of host-associated biology in S. campana and related moth species.

Background & Summary

Noctuidae is one of the most diverse and ecologically important families within Lepidoptera and includes numerous species with substantial agricultural and forestry impacts1,2. Many noctuid moths are recognized as pests because of their broad host range, strong feeding capacity, and high reproductive potential2,3. As high-throughput sequencing technologies have advanced, genome-scale resources have become increasingly useful for studying the biology, evolutionary history, and ecological adaptation of noctuid insects4–8. In particular, chromosome-level genome assemblies facilitate comparative genomic analyses and provide a framework for the annotation of genes and gene families associated with host use, chemosensory perception, detoxification, development, and other ecologically relevant traits. However, such assemblies remain available for only a limited number of noctuid species. More broadly, chromosome-scale genome resources reported for Lepidoptera, including Cydia pomonella5, Hermonassa cecilia9, Paracymoriza distinctalis10, and Hestina assimilis11, illustrate the utility of highly contiguous assemblies for describing genome organization and supporting downstream analyses of genome structure and gene repertoire evolution.

Serrodes campana is a noctuid moth distributed in tropical and subtropical regions of Asia and extending to northern Australia, with records mainly from southern and southwestern China12–14. This species is associated with distinct host-use patterns across life stages. Larvae mainly feed on Sapindus mukorossii, consuming young leaves, tender shoots, inflorescences, and young fruits, which can result in defoliation, fruit drop, reduced plant vigor, and yield loss14. Adults also act as fruit-piercing moths and feed on the juices of several economically important fruits12,15. Previous work on S. campana has focused primarily on field occurrence, morphology, life history, interception records, and chemical control12–18, whereas genomic resources for this species have remained unavailable. The absence of a reference genome has limited comparative analyses of gene content and genome structure in this species and constrained efforts to document genes related to host plant use, chemosensory perception, nutrient acquisition, and other life-history-associated functions. Recent advances in long-read sequencing, particularly PacBio HiFi sequencing, have improved the continuity and base-level accuracy of genome assemblies, including those of insect genomes with abundant repetitive elements and substantial heterozygosity. In parallel, transcriptomic data from multiple developmental stages and tissues provide evidence that can improve gene prediction and structural annotation. Here, we describe a chromosome-level genome assembly and annotation dataset for S. campana. By integrating PacBio HiFi sequencing with RNA-seq data from multiple developmental stages and tissues, we generated a highly contiguous genome assembly and an annotated gene set for this species. Repetitive elements and non-coding RNAs were also annotated to document the genomic composition of S. campana. These data provide a resource for comparative genomics and for the identification of genes and gene families that may be relevant to host-associated biology, chemosensory systems, and stage-dependent feeding traits in S. campana and related noctuid moths.

Methods

Sample collection and sequencing

Mature larvae of S. campana were collected from Jianning Economic Development Zone, Jianning County, Fujian Province, China (26.87167 °N, 116.85710 °E), in late July 2025 and reared in the laboratory on fresh host-plant tissues until adult emergence. The emerged adults were allowed to mate and oviposit under laboratory conditions. Species identification was confirmed based on morphological characteristics. One F1 male pupa produced in September 2025 was selected for high-molecular-weight genomic DNA extraction, which was subsequently used for library construction and whole-genome sequencing. Three types of sequencing libraries were constructed, including PacBio HiFi long-read libraries, Illumina short-read libraries, and Hi-C libraries.

For transcriptome sequencing, 12 RNA-seq samples representing major developmental stages and adult tissues were collected from the subsequent laboratory-reared F1 generation derived from these eggs. The samples included eggs (Egg.RNA), one fifth-instar larva (LA.RNA), one female pupa (FP.RNA), one male pupa (MP.RNA), one female adult body (FA.RNA), one male adult body (MA.RNA), two biological replicates of female adult antennae (FA.ANT1 and FA.ANT2), two biological replicates of male adult antennae (MA.ANT1 and MA.ANT2), female terminal abdominal gland tissue (FA.GON), and male terminal abdominal coremata (MA.COR). Egg samples consisted of 20 pooled eggs. Female and male antenna samples each consisted of 10 pooled antennae per replicate. Female terminal abdominal gland tissue consisted of tissues pooled from 10 females, and male coremata consisted of terminal abdominal scent-brush structures pooled from 10 males. Pupal samples were collected at the middle pupal stage, corresponding to day 3 after the prepupal stage. Whole-body samples were used for eggs, larvae, pupae, and adult bodies, whereas antennae and terminal abdominal tissues were dissected from adults using sterilized fine scissors and forceps.

All dissected tissues and developmental-stage materials used for RNA sequencing were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen after collection and stored at −80 °C until RNA extraction. Total RNA was extracted from each sample using TRNzol Universal Reagent (DP424, TIANGEN) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Poly(A) + mRNA was enriched using oligo(dT) magnetic beads and fragmented prior to cDNA synthesis. RNA-seq libraries were prepared through first- and second-strand cDNA synthesis, end repair, A-tailing, adapter ligation, size selection, and PCR amplification, and then sequenced on the DNBSEQ-T7 platform (DNBSEQ-T7RS, BGI) in paired-end 150-bp mode.

PacBio HiFi sequencing was performed on the Sequel II platform. A total of 2.94 million HiFi reads were generated, yielding 61.45 Gb of clean sequence data, corresponding to approximately 98× coverage based on the final genome assembly size of 627.9 Mb. After filtering using the PacBio SMRT Link pipeline, the HiFi reads had an average read length of 20.9 kb and an N50 length of 21.1 kb, with an average quality value of Q37.8. The GC content of the HiFi reads was 36.23%.

Illumina short-read libraries were sequenced in paired-end mode (PE150) on the Illumina platform. Raw reads were quality-filtered using fastp (v0.23.4)19. A total of 107.65 Gb of clean Illumina short-read data were retained, corresponding to approximately 171× coverage based on the final genome assembly size, with no evident adapter contamination or low-quality sequences.

Hi-C libraries were also sequenced on the Illumina platform using paired-end sequencing (PE150). Approximately 8.22 × 10⁸ Hi-C reads were generated. After quality filtering, 122.83 Gb of clean Hi-C data were retained, corresponding to approximately 196× coverage based on the final genome assembly size, providing long-range chromatin interaction information for downstream scaffolding.

Genome assembly and chromosome-level scaffolding

Prior to genome assembly, genome characteristics were estimated using Illumina short-read data based on 21-mer frequency analysis with Jellyfish (v2.1.4)20 and GenomeScope 2.0 (v2.0.1)21. The genome size of S. campana was estimated to be approximately 614.5 Mb, with a heterozygosity rate of 3.29% and repetitive sequences accounting for approximately 28.3% of the genome (Fig. 2A). PacBio HiFi reads were used for de novo genome assembly with hifiasm (v0.19.9-r616)22, which is optimized for high-accuracy long-read data. The initial assembly produced highly contiguous contigs. To improve assembly accuracy, Illumina short reads were aligned to the assembled contigs using BWA (v0.7.18-r1243)23, and long-read alignments were generated using minimap2 (v2.28-r1209)24. Consensus polishing was first performed using Racon (v1.5.0)25 based on long-read alignments, followed by additional polishing with NextPolish2 (v0.2.1)26 using Illumina short-read data.

Hi-C data were used to anchor contigs into chromosome-scale scaffolds and to validate long-range chromatin interaction consistency. Hi-C reads were mapped to the assembled contigs, and chromatin interaction information was used to order and orient contigs into 31 chromosomes using ALLHiC (v0.9.8)27. Genome-wide Hi-C contact maps exhibited strong interaction signals along the diagonal within each pseudochromosome, with clear boundaries between chromosomes, supporting accurate contig ordering and orientation at the chromosome scale (Fig. 2B).

The final genome assembly had a total size of 627.9 Mb, comprising 98 contigs and 95 scaffolds, with a scaffold N50 of 21.6 Mb and a contig N50 of 21.4 Mb (Table 1). The longest scaffold was 35.1 Mb, and only three gaps were detected in the assembly. The overall GC content of the assembled genome was 36.42%. The final assembly size was broadly consistent with the genome size estimated from K-mer analysis. Genome assembly quality assessment

The completeness of the chromosome-level genome assembly was assessed using BUSCO (v5.7.1)28 with the insecta_odb10 dataset. BUSCO analyses were performed on both the assembled genome and the predicted protein-coding gene set. The genome assembly recovered 99.56% complete BUSCOs, including 99.20% single-copy and 0.37% duplicated BUSCOs, whereas 0.22% were fragmented and 0.22% were missing (Table 2). For the predicted protein set, BUSCO analysis identified 98.24% complete BUSCOs, including 97.51% single-copy and 0.73% duplicated BUSCOs. Repeat annotation and non-coding RNA prediction

Repetitive and non-coding components of the genome were annotated prior to protein-coding gene prediction. Repetitive elements in the S. campana genome were identified using a combination of de novo and homology-based approaches. For de novo repeat identification, candidate repetitive sequences were detected using RepLoc (2021-03-12)29, followed by sequence similarity searches with BLAST + (v2.15.0) and clustering with MCL (v22-282) to construct a species-specific repeat library. For homology-based annotation, known repetitive elements were identified by aligning the genome against the Repbase database (v26.11). The de novo library and Repbase-based annotations were subsequently combined and used for genome-wide repeat masking with RepeatMasker (v4.1.8)30 Long terminal repeat (LTR) retrotransposons were identified using LTR_Finder (v1.07)31, and candidate LTR elements were further refined and classified using LTR_retriever (v3.0.1)32.

Repetitive sequences accounted for 33.79% of the assembled genome, with DNA transposons representing the largest category (16.19%), followed by LTR elements (9.09%) and LINEs (5.23%) (Table 3). Gene prediction and structural annotation

Protein-coding genes were predicted using an evidence-based annotation strategy integrating ab initio prediction, transcriptomic evidence, and homologous protein information. RNA-seq reads from the 12 samples described above were aligned to the genome assembly using HISAT2 (v2.2.1)35 to provide transcriptional evidence for gene model construction. Ab initio gene prediction was performed using AUGUSTUS (v3.5.0)36. The different sources of evidence were then integrated using EVidenceModeler (v2.1.0)37 to generate a final, non-redundant gene set.

Transcriptome-supported validation of gene models

Transcriptome data from the 12 RNA-seq samples were used to assess expression support for the predicted gene models. Across the 12 RNA-seq samples, most mapped reads were assigned to exonic regions, with exonic proportions ranging from 81.89% to 91.63%. In contrast, intronic and intergenic proportions ranged from 3.82% to 9.55% and from 3.52% to 8.56%, respectively (Fig. 4). Functional annotation of protein-coding genes

Functional annotation was performed using multiple public databases. Protein sequences were aligned against the NCBI NR and Swiss-Prot databases using DIAMOND (v2.1.9.163)38. Nucleotide-level similarity searches involved in repeat identification were conducted using BLAST + (v2.15.0). Protein domain annotation was performed using HMMER (v3.4)39 against the Pfam database. GO annotations were assigned based on eggNOG (v5.0.2), Pfam2GO, and Gene2GO mappings. Orthology-based annotation was conducted using eggNOG-mapper (v2.1.12), and KEGG pathway annotation was performed using KEGG Mapper (v5.0).

In total, 15,509 genes (96.65%) were annotated in at least one database (Table 5). Data Records

All raw sequencing data generated in this study are available under NCBI BioProject PRJNA141961040 and NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) accession SRP67573341. The SRA dataset includes genomic Illumina short-read data (SRR37461397), Hi-C sequencing data (SRR37461396), PacBio HiFi long-read data (SRR37461395), and RNA-seq datasets (SRR37461387–SRR37461394 and SRR37461398–SRR37461401). The whole genome shotgun project has been deposited at DDBJ/ENA/GenBank under accession JBVVXX00000000042. The version described in this paper is JBVVXX010000000. Genome annotation files, including the GFF3 annotation, CDS, and predicted protein sequences, are available at Zenodo under https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1923023543.

Technical Validation

Assembly quality was evaluated using multiple complementary approaches, including read mapping, k-mer-based consensus quality assessment, BUSCO analysis, Hi-C contact consistency, and transcriptome-supported annotation. PacBio HiFi reads and Illumina short reads were independently aligned to the final assembly to assess mapping consistency and coverage uniformity. A total of 99.59% of PacBio HiFi reads and 98.54% of Illumina short reads were mapped to the assembled genome. In addition, 90.28% of Illumina read pairs were properly mapped, further supporting the accuracy and consistency of the assembly.

K-mer-based consensus quality assessment further supported the base-level accuracy of the assembly. The genome-wide QV was 58.69, corresponding to an estimated base error rate of 1.35 × 10−6. Per-pseudochromosome QV values ranged from 50.46 to 63.87, with estimated base error rates ranging from 4.10 × 10−7 to 8.99 × 10−6, indicating consistently high consensus accuracy across the assembled pseudochromosomes. Genome completeness was assessed using BUSCO (v5.7.1)28 with the insecta_odb10 dataset, recovering 99.56% complete BUSCOs in the genome assembly and 98.24% in the predicted protein-coding gene set. Compared with available chromosome-level genome assemblies of noctuid and other lepidopteran insects, the S. campana assembly shows high continuity and completeness. The assembly has a contig N50 of 21.4 Mb and a scaffold N50 of 21.6 Mb, with 99.56% complete BUSCOs recovered using the insecta_odb10 dataset. These values are comparable to, or higher than, those reported for several representative lepidopteran genome assemblies5,9–11. However, direct comparisons should be interpreted cautiously because sequencing platforms, assembly strategies, polishing procedures, annotation pipelines, and BUSCO datasets may differ among studies. Together with the high read-mapping rates, genome-wide QV of 58.69, and Hi-C anchoring of 99.29% of the assembly to 31 pseudochromosomes, these metrics indicate that the S. campana genome represents a high-quality chromosome-level reference assembly.

Hi-C data were used to assess long-range genome organization and scaffold correctness. The Hi-C contact map showed strong interaction signals along the diagonal within each chromosome-scale scaffold and clear boundaries between scaffolds, supporting accurate contig ordering and orientation. Using Hi-C-based scaffolding, 99.29% of the assembled genome sequence was anchored to 31 pseudochromosomes.

RNA-seq data from 12 samples provided additional support for gene model quality. Across all 12 RNA-seq samples, reads were primarily assigned to exonic regions, with exonic proportions ranging from 81.89% to 91.63%, consistent with the distribution pattern shown in Fig. 4. In addition, 96.7% of the predicted genes showed detectable transcriptional signals in at least one sample.
