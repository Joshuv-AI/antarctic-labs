Developing a core outcome set for intervention studies in post stroke cognitive impairment (COS-PSCI): a protocol
Authors: Sinead Purcell, Katie Robinson, Margaret O’Connor, Jon Salsberg, Ann-Marie Morrissey, Sara Hayes
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPost-stroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) negatively impacts peoples’ quality of life and functional independence. Its complexity poses challenges for both clinical management and intervention research. Despite global guidelines recommending cognitive assessment for all stroke survivors, there is no consensus on what, when or how to measure PSCI. Intervention studies use varied outcome measures, limiting comparability and clinical translation. Developing a core outcome set (COS) for PSCI could address these challenges by standardising outcome measurement, enhancing comparability across studies and facilitating the translation of research into clinical practice. This study aims to develop a COS for intervention research in PSCI.Methods and analysisThis study will follow the Core Outcome Set-STAndards for Development (COS-STAD) framework and will be conducted in six stages. First, a scoping review of intervention studies will identify commonly measured outcomes. Second, a rapid qualitative evidence synthesis will explore outcomes valued by stakeholders in the published literature. Third, qualitative focus groups with stroke survivors, support persons, and healthcare professionals will further identify outcomes of importance. Fourth, findings from the preceding stages will be synthesised to generate a comprehensive list of candidate outcomes. Fifth, a real-time modified eDelphi survey with key stakeholders will prioritise outcomes and identify those meeting predefined consensus criteria for inclusion in a Core Outcome Set for Post-Stroke Cognitive Impairment (COS-PSCI). Finally, international consensus meetings will be held to 1) finalise the COS-PSCI and 2) select appropriate outcome measurement instruments, guided by the COnsensus-based Standards for the selection of health Measurement INstruments (COSMIN). Patient and public involvement (PPI) will be embedded throughout all stages of the study.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval has been obtained from the University of Limerick Education and Health Sciences Research Ethics Committee (2025_03_18_EHS) and the Health Service Executive Mid-West Research Ethics Committee (REC ref: 0296). Findings will be disseminated via peer-reviewed publications, international stroke conferences, social media platforms and public engagement events.RegistrationThis study is registered in the Core Outcome Measures for Effectiveness database and is available at https://www.comet-initiative.org/Studies/Details/3508.

Introduction

Stroke is the second leading cause of disability worldwide, placing growing pressure on individuals, families and health systems.1 Demographic ageing predicts a 27% increase in people with stroke (PwS) across the European Union by 2040 with parallel rises anticipated in healthcare costs and caregiver burden.2 Post-stroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) represents a complex and multidimensional construct that lacks a universal definition.35 PSCI refers to persisting cognitive deficits attributable to stroke. These impairments can range from mild to severe (including post-stroke dementia (PSD)) and may affect various cognitive domains, including attention, processing speed, executive function, memory, language and visuospatial processing. They may or may not impact independence and follow variable recovery trajectories.3 4 6

Up to 60% of stroke survivors experience PSCI in the first year, with a higher rate seen in the acute stroke phase.4 Cognitive impairments may resolve, stabilise or progress over time, with approximately 50% experiencing persistent deficits and up to one third developing dementia, particularly in more severe casess.7 8 These impairments are associated with poorer functional outcomes,9 reduced quality of life,10 heightened risk of depression,11 increased caregiver burden,12 institutionalisation13 and elevated healthcare costs.14

The Stroke Recovery and Rehabilitation Roundtable has identified PSCI as a key research priority.15 Multiple national and international guidelines recommend measuring post-stroke cognitive outcomes.3 5 16 17 However, consensus remains lacking regarding what outcomes to measure and how to measure them in terms of the optimal tools, timing and methodological frameworks for outcome selection and measurement.

PSCI measurement lacks standardisation. Over 300 tools have been used in the literature, with inconsistent thresholds and timing.1820 Even common tools like Montreal Cognitive Assessment vary in cut-offs for PSCI.6 21 22 This variability hampers data synthesis and clinical interpretation. Timing of assessments also varies widely, despite its importance in capturing the diverse trajectories of PSCI, including resolution, stabilisation or progression. Many tools used to assess post-stroke cognition are difficult to access in routine care, applied inconsistently, and can be experienced as stressful or burdensome for patients.2326 Theses limitations have prompted calls for more inclusive measures and functionally meaningful measurements of cognitive outcomes post stroke.16 27 Together, these challenges highlight the need for greater standardisation in how post-stroke cognitive outcomes are selected, measured, and reported.

A core outcome set (COS) is a standardised set of outcomes that should be reported in all trials for a specific condition.28 Despite existing standards for vascular cognitive impairment, no COS exists for PSCI.29 Existing dementia COS do not address PSCI’s unique features, such as diverse cognitive profiles and recovery potential.3032

The Research Outcome Measurement in Aphasia (ROMA) consensus statement has established a core outcome set for post-stroke aphasia treatment research, including language,33 a domain that conceptually overlaps with PSCI. To avoid duplication and promote harmonisation, language will not be included as a core domain in Core Outcome Set for Post-Stroke Cognitive Impairment (COS-PSCI). Instead, ROMA will be explicitly recognised as the reference COS for language outcomes. At consensus stage 6, the ROMA COS will be presented to the expert panel for consideration and potential alignment. In line with Core Outcome Measures in Effectiveness Trials (COMET) standards,28 we will map terminology, domain definitions and measurement principles to ROMA wherever feasible and, where relevant, link COS-PSCI domains and instruments to their ROMA counterparts to support interoperability, reduce redundancy and facilitate future data synthesis.

To address this critical gap for PSCI, we aim to develop the first evidence-based, stakeholder-informed COS for PSCI intervention research (COS-PSCI). COS-PSCI will standardise outcomes, improve comparability and ensure that cognitive outcomes reflect what matters most to stroke survivors, their support persons and clinicians.

Scope

The resulting COS-PSCI will be applicable to people with PSCI, irrespective of the time since diagnosis. It will be suitable for use across all care settings, including acute, rehabilitation and community-based services, ensuring relevance throughout the recovery journey. Critically, the COS-PSCI will be recommended for use in international intervention trials for PSCI. This standardisation will enable reliable assessment of cognitive outcomes and support consistent monitoring of recovery.

The study Steering Committee will provide strategic oversight and expert guidance throughout the research process. Its membership will include clinical specialists, academic researchers, policy makers and people with lived experience, ensuring interdisciplinary input. Public and patient involvement statement

Patient and public involvement (PPI) is embedded throughout, guided by the INVOLVE advisory group guidelines,37 with reporting aligned to the Guidance for Reporting Involvement of Patients and the Public checklist.38 The CO-research Involvement and Engagement in Dementia model39 has been adapted for stroke, including those with aphasia and fatigue. Inclusive strategies such as simplified materials and flexible participation options will be used to support meaningful involvement. A stroke rehabilitation PPI group has already been established, comprising PwS and their support persons.40 Members of this group, with lived experience of PSCI, will contribute to the design, evaluation and dissemination of the study. They will also be invited to join the study’s Steering Committee to ensure that patient perspectives inform the development of the core outcome set. PPI input will help ensure that participation is not overly burdensome, particularly for individuals with cognitive or communication difficulties. Details of PPI involvement will be outlined in each study stage of this protocol.

Stage 1: scoping review

A scoping review will systematically identify outcomes, OMIs and assessment time points in PSCI intervention studies. The review will be conducted in line with the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) methodology41 and reported using Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) guidelines.42 The protocol will be made publicly available in an open-access research repository.

Search methods

We will search five major sources to identify eligible studies: CINAHL (via EBSCOhost), PsycINFO APA (via Ovid), PubMed (via NLM), Scopus and the Cochrane Library (CENTRAL). In addition, we will search the ClinicalTrials.gov registry to identify ongoing, unpublished or recently completed trials that may not yet be indexed elsewhere. Search terms were codeveloped with the research team and iteratively reviewed by a Health Research Methods Librarian to ensure coverage of Peer Review of Electronic Search Strategies (PRESS) domains. The CINAHL search strategy (online supplemental material 2) is provided, and the search methods, including dates, limits, and filters, will be reported in line with PRISMA-ScR guidance.

Study selection

References will be imported to Covidence. title and abstract screening will be performed independently by two reviewers, followed by full-text review. Discrepancies will be resolved by consensus or by a third reviewer. Reasons for exclusion at full-text screening will be recorded and reported in the final review. The selection process will be illustrated in a PRISMA-ScR flowchart.

Data extraction

Two authors will independently extract data on study design, population, intervention, outcomes, outcome measurement instruments and time point using a customised data extraction form.

Analysis and synthesis

Verbatim outcomes will be coded using content analysis44, categorised into outcome constructs and aligned with the COMET taxonomy core areas45 including life impact, physiological/clinical, resource use and adverse events. This will be presented in table format. Frequencies of reported outcomes, OMIs and their time points will be summarised descriptively. Stratification by time since stroke, cognitive severity or intervention type will be conducted where feasible. Following the initial synthesis, a stakeholder consultation step will be conducted, as outlined by JBI.41 This will provide an opportunity to engage with the PPI panel, allowing for valuable input on the findings before finalising the report. Findings will be published and will directly inform the COS-PSCI outcome longlist.

Stage 2: rapid qualitative evidence synthesis

This rapid qualitative evidence synthesis (rQES) will rapidly synthesise qualitative research on the lived experience of PSCI, following Cochrane guidelines,46 adapted for rapid reviews.47 It aims to identify outcomes that matter to individuals with PSCI and their support persons and identify patient-informed language to shape the Delphi survey and dissemination. The protocol will be made publicly available in an open-access research repository.

Search methods

We will search four major sources to identify eligible studies: CINAHL (via EBSCOhost), PsycINFO (via Ovid), Scopus and ProQuest Dissertations & Theses Global, in line with empirical rQES evidence indicating 92% retrieval of relevant qualitative studies.47 48 The search strategy was codeveloped by the research team and peer reviewed by a Health Research Methods Librarian covering all PRESS domains. In accordance with Enhancing Transparency in REporting the synthesis of Qualitative research (ENTREQ), the full CINAHL search strategy is provided (see online supplemental material 3).

Study selection

Search results will be imported to Covidence and screened independently by two reviewers, followed by full-text review. Discrepancies will be resolved by consensus or by a third reviewer. Reasons for exclusion at full-text screening will be recorded and reported in the final review.

Data extraction

Key study characteristics (aim, setting, participants, methodology) and outcome-relevant data will be extracted. A deductive approach will guide the identification of text related to PSCI outcomes reported by PwS and their support persons. Verbatim quotations and author interpretations will be extracted from the results and discussion sections. Data will be recorded in Excel by one reviewer and verified by another.

Sampling strategy

To manage data volume (if indicated) and maintain conceptual clarity, we will (1) assess data richness,49 and (2) to ensure diversity across geographic and healthcare contexts, we will employ purposeful sampling, in line with the Cochrane Effective Practice and Organisation of Care guidance.50

Quality appraisal

Included studies will be appraised using the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme checklist51 to inform confidence in the synthesis, not to exclude studies.

Data analysis

A pragmatic, multimethod approach will be used to analyse qualitative data, combining Best-Fit Framework Synthesis (BFFS)52 and thematic synthesis.53 Using NVivo,54 outcome-related data will first be deductively coded against the core areas of the COMET taxonomy,45 with inductive coding applied for concepts not captured within the existing framework. This approach ensures both structure and flexibility in outcome classification. Thematic synthesis will then be used to identify deeper patterns and priorities, including descriptive and analytical themes, with particular attention to linguistic markers of outcome importance such as frequency and emotional emphasis.53 55 Two reviewers will independently review and interpret outcomes from 50% of the included studies for agreement and one researcher will independently review the remaining studies.

Confidence assessment

GRADE-Confidence in the Evidence from Reviews of Qualitative research56 will be used to assess confidence in findings. Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research tables and evidence profiles will accompany results.57

Reflexivity

Reflexivity will be maintained through journaling, team discussions and PPI engagement to challenge assumptions and incorporate diverse perspectives.58

Reporting

The review will be reported according to ENTREQ guidelines59 and findings will inform subsequent stages of COS-PSCI development.

Stage 3: qualitative descriptive study

Participant recruitment

A purposive sampling strategy will be employed to ensure representation across three stakeholder groups, including (1) community-dwelling PwS experiencing PSCI, (2) support persons providing direct care to PwS and (3) HCPs with ≥2 years’ PSCI experience.

PwS and support persons will be recruited via three main pathways: (1) dissemination of study information in stroke rehabilitation services across the mid-west of Ireland, (2) engagement with gatekeepers in national stroke support organisations and patient advocacy agencies (eg, Irish Heart Foundation) and (3) posts shared on relevant social media platforms. HCPs will be recruited via professional networks (eg, the National Stroke Programme; the Association of Occupational Therapy of Ireland Neurology Advisory Group) and social media outreach.

All participants will receive comprehensive information outlining the study’s aims, procedures and rights, including the right to withdraw. PPI contributors will be involved in codeveloping participant-facing materials, including information leaflets and consent forms, to ensure accessibility and clarity.

For PwS, tailored consent procedures will be followed, incorporating aphasia-friendly and age-appropriate materials inclusive of visual aids, and supported conversations. Informed consent will be guided by the Health Service Executive National Policy for Consent in Health and Social Care Research.60

In line with COS development guidance,34 61 62 we will employ a combined approach using maximum variation sampling within a representativeness framework. Diversity across key characteristics will be ensured using stratified sampling matrices that define stakeholder attributes a priori and minimum recruitment targets. These matrices, presented in online supplemental material 4 (tables A and B), guide recruitment to achieve broad variation. Our strategy draws on prior COS development studies,61 which typically involved 24–36 participants. This structured approach supports transparent planning and monitoring of diversity while capturing a wide range of perspectives essential for robust outcome selection.34 62 63

Data collection

To capture diverse stakeholder perspectives on outcomes relevant to PSCI, we will conduct World Café-style focus groups. This structured, conversational approach facilitates inclusive, iterative dialogue across small-group discussions, encouraging equal participation and collective insight generation.62 64 65

World Cafés will be facilitated by research team members with independent note-takers. To ensure accessible participation for individuals with PSCI, we will provide presession materials such as simplified information, clear agendas and key questions in accessible formats. During sessions, we will consistently use clear, jargon-free language supported by visual aids. Continuous one-to-one facilitator support will be available throughout. Session duration will be limited to 60–90 min, incorporating regular breaks and refreshments and minimising environmental distractions. All table facilitators will be upskilled in PSCI communication techniques. Finally, we will enable flexible contribution methods, allowing participants to express themselves effectively through words, gestures and images, or with clarification from a support person. Where group participation is unsuitable, individual interviews applying these same adaptations will be offered. The research team and PPI panel will codevelop three to four accessible core questions on PSCI experiences, intervention priorities and measurement preferences. Full procedures will be published.

Data analysis

Data from World Café sessions will be analysed by two independent reviewers using thematic analysis66 supported by NVivo 1. Written outputs (eg, table notes) and/or transcribed discussions will be coded line-by-line. Themes will be developed to reflect shared outcome priorities and underlying values expressed by participants. Where appropriate, BFFS52 will be used to map themes to the COMET taxonomy,45 enabling integration with findings from earlier stages. The final list of outcomes will be compared against existing literature to highlight new insights. A second phase of analysis will explore experiences, behaviours and beliefs influencing outcome selection, with a focus on differences between stakeholder perspectives if any.

Reporting

This qualitative phase will be reported following established recommendations for qualitative research methods in COS development, as outlined by Jones et al.67

Stage 4: evidence synthesis

The research team will synthesise outcomes identified through the scoping review, rapid qualitative evidence synthesis and World Cafés to develop a preliminary long list of outcomes relevant to PSCI. This list will be refined by merging duplicates, removing infrequently reported outcomes (eg, those in fewer than 5% of studies) and simplifying language to enhance accessibility for individuals with cognitive or communication impairments.33 68 The refined list will be reviewed by the COS PSCI Steering Group and the PPI panel. Where appropriate, we may also use preranking techniques to help prioritise the most relevant outcomes for inclusion in the consensus process. The final Delphi survey will include no more than 50 outcomes, in line with recommendations on reducing cognitive burden, managing digital fatigue and inclusion and maintaining participant engagement among older adults and individuals with cognitive impairment.69 70 The full process will be transparently documented and reported to ensure rigour and reproducibility.69

Sample size

The target sample size of 80 participants (20 per stakeholder group) balances methodological rigour with practical feasibility. While Delphi studies in COS development often include 50–100 participants,61 71 recent evidence suggests that 60–80 participants with equal stakeholder group representation can replicate 80% of consensus outcomes from larger samples.72 To account for a 20–40% response rate typical of Delphi surveys,73 invitations will be extended to at least 450 potential participants, aiming for proportional representation across stakeholder groups.

Data collection

A real-time Delphi (RTD) survey will reduce burden and attrition.74 RTD allows iterative feedback and score adjustments in one phase,75 76 improving engagement and consensus.7779

Procedure

Participants will provide demographic details and consent electronically. Outcomes derived from stage 4 will be presented in randomised order to mitigate position bias,30 73 accompanied by lay definitions and pictorial prompts.80 HCPs, policy makers and researchers will use the GRADE-recommended 9-point scale (1–3: Not important, 4–6: Important but not critical, 7–9: Critically important),81 while PwS will use a 3-point scale (1: Not particularly important, 2: Important, 3: Very important).30 Real-time feedback will allow score adjustments.

Adaptations for cognitive accessibility and digital inclusion

To maximise inclusivity, the survey integrates evidence-based adaptations tailored to the needs of individuals with cognitive impairment.33 68 82 A simplified 3-point Likert scale reduces complexity and cognitive load, addressing barriers such as decision fatigue, and reflects that outcomes rarely fall into ‘categorically unimportant’.70 80 A secure, mobile-responsive platform supports screen readers, Augmentative and alternative communication tools, and offers real-time feedback limited to aggregated ratings from the participant’s stakeholder group, presented as clear bar charts. Pictorial aids, plain language, high-contrast formatting and optional captioned video walkthroughs enhance accessibility.30 33 Paper-based and researcher-assisted options (in person/phone/video) accommodate limited digital access, while pause-and-resume functionality mitigates fatigue.70 Codeveloped with PPI contributors, the survey will align with best practice recommendations for inclusive core outcome set development among older adults and those with visual, cognitive or communication difficulties.

Survey administration and analysis

The survey will remain open for a minimum of six weeks, extendable to eight, with email reminders at weeks 2, 4 and 6 to optimise response rates.83 73 The survey will include an open question where participants can suggest additional outcomes, which will be reviewed and added if proposed by two or more participants. The PPI panel will review and pilot the survey for face validity and accessibility. Descriptive statistics will identify consensus outcomes.

Reporting

The RTD survey will be reported in line with ACcurate COnsensus Reporting Document consensus reporting guideline.84 Missing data are managed proactively through careful survey design, mandatory responses and automated reminders. Surveys with missing data will be excluded.

Stage 6: hybrid consensus meetings

Agree Core Outcome Set for Post-Stroke Cognitive Impairment

The final stage of the COS-PSCI development process aims to establish consensus on core outcomes through structured hybrid meetings. This requires equitable representation of all stakeholder groups and careful consideration of the logistical challenges associated with international participation.

Sampling and recruitment

A purposive sample of Delphi survey participants will be invited to attend, with four to five representatives selected per stakeholder group (PwS, support persons, HCPs, researchers and policy makers). Consistent with previous COS development61 76 meetings will include 20–25 participants to facilitate meaningful discussion while maintaining manageability.

Sample size and representation

To optimise inclusivity, meetings will be conducted via video conference (Microsoft Teams), with optional in-person attendance for PwS and researcher support. Multiple sessions may be scheduled to accommodate global time zones. Informed consent will be obtained prior to participation.

Data collection procedures

Two facilitators will guide discussions, using the Delphi survey results as a foundation. The facilitators will elicit feedback on the preliminary COS-PSCI and on outcomes with no consensus. Participants will vote electronically on their devices to include (≥70% agreement) or exclude outcomes. Meetings will be limited to 60-90 min, with scheduled breaks to minimise fatigue, particularly for PwS.79 85

Data analysis and reporting

Voting results will be analysed in real time, with outcomes categorised as ‘included’ (≥70% consensus) or ‘excluded’ (<70%). Where appropriate, outcomes may be stratified by severity of cognitive impairment and/or time since stroke. Meeting minutes and anonymised voting records will be maintained for transparency.

Outcome measurement consensus

The final phase of the study will systematically link each agreed outcome to the most appropriate measurement instrument, following the COMET-COSMIN methodology.36 Building on the scoping review, updated literature searches will identify relevant tools. Instruments will be assessed using COSMIN criteria. Tools must meet standards for validity, feasibility and accessibility.36 Instruments may be stratified by time post stroke and cognitive impairment severity.

Where multiple tools meet these criteria, hybrid consensus meetings will be held, involving original COS PSCI stakeholders and supplemented by additional members, if necessary, to determine the most suitable instruments for each outcome. To ensure accessibility, psychometric concepts will be explained using lay language and examples. Voting via devices will be used, with tools accepted if ≥70% of at least three stakeholder groups vote in favour.

Adaptations to consensus meetings for cognitive accessibility and digital inclusion

Hybrid meetings will include adaptations for participants with PSCI, codesigned with the PPI panel. Presession, accessible joining instructions and simplified voting guides will be provided. In-person attendees will benefit from printed materials, quiet spaces and supporter accompaniment. During sessions, two facilitators will jointly manage the hybrid format: one guiding discussion using clear, jargon-free language and visual prompts, while another provides real-time support and monitors engagement. Continuous one-to-one assistance will be offered for voting, interpretation or clarification. Sessions will be limited to 60 min with scheduled breaks to minimise cognitive fatigue. Voting on personal devices ensures anonymity while accommodating varied response speeds.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics

The study will adhere to COS-STAR and COMET Initiative standards to ensure methodological rigour.79 Ethical approval has been obtained from the University of Limerick Education and Health Sciences Research Ethics Committee (2025_03_18_EHS) and the Health Service Executive Mid-West Research Ethics Committee (REC ref: 0296). Informed consent will be obtained and documented prior to engagement in stages 3, 5 and 6 of this study. For stage 3 (qualitative focus groups and interviews), consent will be sought in both verbal and written formats, with researcher support and aphasia-friendly accessible materials provided where needed. In stage 5 (Delphi survey), consent will be obtained electronically through the platform before participants access the survey. In stage 6 (consensus meetings), consent will be documented either verbally or in writing depending on participant preference and mode of participation.

Dissemination

Findings will be shared throughout the study via open-access publications, conference presentations and protocol repositories. Stakeholder engagement will include webinars and briefings developed in collaboration with the Steering Group to foster dialogue among clinicians, researchers and policy makers. Public outreach will use lay summaries, infographics and targeted social media for patient organisations and professional networks. PPI members will coauthor plain-language outputs and help shape dissemination strategies, ensuring findings are accessible and relevant to all audiences.
