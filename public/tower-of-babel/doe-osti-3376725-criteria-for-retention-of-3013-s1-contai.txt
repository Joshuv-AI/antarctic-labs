Criteria for Retention of 3013 S1
Containers Based on Relative Risk and
Expert Judgment
Executive Summary

An evaluation was performed to assess the suitability of thirty-three 3013 containers
proposed for retention. These containers have moisture levels greater than 0.08 wt.% –
the S1 population. The remainder of the S1 population stored at SRS will be down
blended and disposed of by the end of 2028. Based on field surveillance and shelf-life
data available to date as well as informed technical judgment, no container is currently
expected to fail in its 50-year storage period. However, corrosion risk varies across the
S1 population.

Relative risks were evaluated using predicted Consensus Scores and their 95% Upper
Prediction Limits (UPLs). The predicted values are based on a statistical model of
Consensus Score as a function of moisture, chloride, and whether the packaged material
was electrorefining scrap packaged at Hanford. Consensus Score has been shown to be a
useful indicator of corrosion potential, and the UPL captures uncertainty in the model
predictions, providing a conservative indicator of corrosion potential.

Using UPLs to determine relative risks, together with expert review, three containers
were identified as not suitable for retention, and the remainder were determined to be
suitable.

1. Introduction

The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Defense Programs and Nuclear Energy
organizations are pursuing the utilization of plutonium-bearing oxides containing greater
than 80% plutonium. Some of these materials are within a subgroup of Pressure and
Corrosion (P&C) containers that have greater than 0.08 wt% moisture. These containers
are referred to as the S1 population (Kelly, et al., 2016). These materials may no longer
be considered surplus and therefore may not be down blended, requiring evaluation for
safe long-term storage pending future use. The time frame for utilization of the materials
has not been determined and may extend to several decades.

In response to this need, the National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) Office of
Nuclear Nonproliferation (NNP) has requested the Materials Identification and
Surveillance (MIS) working group (MIS-WG) to perform an evaluation of the storage
safety of thirty-three S1 containers containing greater than 80% plutonium for potential

storage durations of up to 50 years. A list of the S1 containers identified for evaluation
has been provided by NNSA and is included in Appendix A. In addition, containers
currently being packaged at Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) with moisture
exceeding the limit of S1 containers will require evaluation to determine their suitability
for retention.

Based on the MIS-WG evaluation, containers may be categorized into one of the
following groups:

 • Containers to be down blended and not retained
 • Containers suitable for retention based on available data
 • Containers requiring additional analysis to justify retention

This report documents the technical basis for identifying the suitability of S1 containers
for retention. The evaluation supports DOE efforts to ensure the safe management of
plutonium-bearing oxides containing greater than 80% plutonium.

2. Methodology

2.1 Consensus Score Used to Assess Relative Risks
Consensus Scores were developed as a tool for prioritizing destructive examinations
(DEs) based on corrosion potential with the goal of prioritizing containers with the
highest corrosion potential (worst-first) (Veirs and Venetz, 2024). The protocol uses
Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) photographs routinely taken as part of DEs.
The images are ranked independently by a subset of experts from the MIS program
Corrosion Working Group (CWG)1 for the presence of coatings on the convenience can
and inner can, and corrosion on the heat tint of the weld region of the inner can lid. The
individual rankings are averaged to give an overall ranking (Venetz, 2025 and 2026).

Consensus Scores are available for 156 containers with DEs and have been shown to be
useful indicators of corrosion potential based on both empirical observations and expert
judgment (Kelly, et al., 2026). Consensus Scores tend to increase with the adsorbed
moisture and the relative humidity (RH) of the packaging glovebox, Best Moisture and
Glove Box RH, respectively. Considerable variability in this trend is observed (Figure 1).
The Best Moisture is a moisture measurement derived from packaging moisture
measurements that have been reviewed and corrected by technical experts (Berg, et al.,
2010). The Glove Box RH was measured at the time of packaging and is only available
for Hanford containers (113 containers in the DE population). Glove Box RH has a
stronger predictive relationship based on both the correlation coefficient (r) and the
coefficient of determination (R²) (Figure 1). For these plots, R² is derived from fitting a

The DOE sites packaging plutonium oxide materials are responsible for ensuring that the
materials are represented by one or more samples in the MIS program (DOE-STD-3013).
Representation was established primarily through process knowledge or by the chemical
composition of the material as determined by prompt gamma analysis. These groups are
called MIS Represented groups (Narlesky et al., 2009). Figure 3 shows boxplots (Tukey,
1977) of Consensus Scores for three categories of MIS Represented materials, where the
box represents the middle 50% of the data (interquartile range), the orange line indicates
the median, and the whiskers show the spread of the data. The three categories of MIS
Represented materials are: Hanford electrorefining material (ER Scrap), Hanford non-ER
Scrap (all other Hanford materials), and non-Hanford non-ER Scrap. All containers with
MIS Represented equal to PyroOx-HN-RF-ERScrap were processed at Hanford.
Therefore, the third category is simply material in all non-Hanford containers, which
includes chloride bearing materials from Rocky Flats Environmental Technology Site
(RFETS), Savannah River Site (SRS), and Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL).

The highest Consensus Scores are associated with Hanford ER Scrap. The elevated
corrosion potential in this category reflects a combination of material characteristics
(likely presence of alkaline earth chlorides) and processing conditions, including
allowance for packaging up to 60% RH, in contrast to other sites where relative humidity
was controlled to much lower levels. Relative humidity at RFETS was controlled by
procedure to 3.5% and to 25% at SRS (Berg et al., 2010).

These observed relationships among Consensus Score, moisture, relative humidity, and
MIS Represented categories inform the selection of variables for predictive modeling.
They also indicate that Consensus Score captures meaningful differences in corrosion
potential (Kelly et al., 2026).

2.2 Predictive Model Formulation
Correlation analyses and evaluation of multiple modeling approaches identified Best
Moisture, Ion Cl, (Ion Cl)², and ER Scrap as the strongest predictors of Consensus Score
for the full population. For the reduced dataset consisting of Hanford containers with
available relative humidity (RH) data, the strongest predictors were Glove Box RH, Ion
Cl, (Ion Cl)², and ER Scrap. Inclusion of Best Moisture in the Hanford model did not
improve predictive performance, indicating that RH captures the variability in Consensus
Score associated with moisture.

Based on coefficient of determination (R²), the best predictive model for the full dataset
is:

Predicted Consensus Score = 𝛽0 + 𝛽1 (Best Moisture) + 𝛽2 (Ion Cl − Ion‾ Cl) + 𝛽3 (Ion Cl
 − Ion‾ Cl)2 + 𝛽4 (ERScrap)

where Ion Cl is measured in percent and centered about its mean (Ion‾ Cl) to reduce
multicollinearity without affecting predictions.

For the reduced dataset, the best predictive model is:

 Predicted Consensus Score = 𝛽0 + 𝛽1 (RH) + 𝛽2 (Ion Cl − Ion‾ Cl) + 𝛽3 (Ion Cl
 − Ion‾ Cl)2 + 𝛽4 (ERScrap)

Figure 4 shows observed versus predicted Consensus Scores for the RH and Best
Moisture models. Both models systematically underestimate Consensus Scores above
approximately 0.6 and explain approximately 50% of the variability in Consensus Score.
This indicates that, while moisture, glove box relative humidity, chloride chemistry, and
material properties are key drivers of corrosion potential, additional factors not
represented in the model—such as internal relative humidity, temperature history,
heterogeneity within ER Scrap materials, and variability in RH exposure post-opening —
also contribute to the observed variability.

Figure 4. The observed versus predicted Consensus Scores for the RH model and the full data
Best Moisture model. Blue X’s represent ERScrap and orange X’s represent non-ERScrap.

2.3 Use of Prompt Gamma Cl as a Surrogate for Ion Cl
Prompt gamma chloride (Cl_PG) measurements (Narlesky et al., 2021) are used as a
surrogate for Ion Cl to evaluate the retention containers. Comparison of Ion Cl and
prompt gamma Cl (Cl_PG) shows strong agreement for values less than 80,000 ppm
(Figure 5).

  Figure 5. Ion Cl versus prompt gamma Cl (Cl_PG) shows strong agreement for Ion Cl values
  less than approximately 80,000 ppm. Greater variability for larger values is expected due
  to inhomogeneity of the material, which affects the magnitude of the prompt gamma signal.

2.4 Relative Risk Metric Definition
Relative risk is evaluated using the predicted Consensus Score and the 95% upper
prediction limit (UPL). The UPL represents a conservative estimate of how high the
Consensus Score could reasonably be for a container, given uncertainty in the model and
natural variability in the data.

The table of the 33 proposed retention containers in Appendix A is ordered by UPL
values from the Best Moisture prediction model using the full dataset. This model is used

for these containers because only two are from Hanford, while the remainder are from
RFETS (R in Item ID) and SRS (S in Item ID), for which relative humidity data are not
available.

CWG meetings were convened to review the results of this analysis along with all
available data for the proposed retention containers. Based on this review, the CWG
experts concluded that, in an abundance of caution, the three containers with the highest
Consensus Scores and UPLs should be down blended rather than retained. The
remaining 30 containers are considered suitable for retention.

4. Conclusions

Consensus Scores have been useful in prioritizing detailed examination for corrosion
evaluation in storage containers. They also provide a useful metric for evaluating
corrosion potential in containers not yet examined. Moisture, glove box relative humidity,
chloride chemistry, and material properties are key drivers of corrosion and are
incorporated into statistical models to predict Consensus Scores. The predicted
Consensus Scores and their 95% upper prediction limits (UPLs) provide metrics for
relative risk ranking of containers.

These relative risk metrics, together with thorough expert review of the proposed
retention containers, support the recommendation to down blend the three containers with
the highest Consensus Scores and UPLs. Although no container is expected to fail over a
50-year storage period based on available data and analysis, these containers are

recommended for down blending as a conservative measure to reduce uncertainty and
provide additional safety margin.
