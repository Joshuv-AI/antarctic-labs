TMEM33 regulates the proliferation and migration of lung adenocarcinoma by promoting the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway
Authors: Muli Wudu, Haijun Lou, Yuyu Ma, Zhenyu Zhang, Sureyan Alimujiang, Yanchao Deng, Xiaoliang Jing, Menggen Meng, Zhipeng Xu, Ayshamgul Hasim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLung adenocarcinoma is a common type of lung cancer with high incidence and mortality rates. TMEM33, a tumor-associated protein, has not been fully elucidated in lung adenocarcinoma. To explore the expression of TMEM33 in lung adenocarcinoma, its impact on tumor progression, and its role in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway. Key genes associated with lung adenocarcinoma were screened using the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) dataset GSE140797 in combination with a weighted correlation network analysis (WGCNA). Gene Ontology (GO) functional enrichment and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses were performed on these key genes. TMEM33 expression in Lung adenocarcinoma patients was validated using data from the TCGA database. The directly interactive molecules were screened through a bioinformatics method. Cellular assays (Western Blot, CCK8, colony formation, scratch assay, Trans well assay) and a nude mouse model were used to investigate the effects of TMEM33 on cell proliferation, migration, and tumor growth. TMEM33 is highly expressed in lung adenocarcinoma tissues (P < 0.05) and associated with poor prognosis. Overexpression of TMEM33 promotes cell proliferation and migration, and activates the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway (P < 0.01). In the nude mouse model, TMEM33 overexpression increases tumor volume (P < 0.001), and PI3K/AKT pathway inhibition suppresses these effects (P < 0.05, P < 0.01). TMEM33 acts as an oncogene in lung adenocarcinoma by activating the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, providing new therapeutic targets.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12935-026-04379-z.Keywords: TMEM33, Lung adenocarcinoma, PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, Cell proliferation, Cell migration

Introduction

Lung cancer, with its high incidence and mortality rates worldwide, poses a severe threat to human health and life. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is the most common form of lung cancer, accounting for about 85% of all lung cancer cases. Lung adenocarcinoma, as one of the main subtypes of lung cancer, has complex molecular mechanisms and poor prognosis, which has always been a hot topic of research. In recent years, with the continuous advancement of molecular biology technology, more and more tumor-related genes have been discovered, playing an important role in the occurrence, development, and metastasis of lung adenocarcinoma.

Transmembrane proteins are critical in non-small cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC), affecting tumor progression, metastasis, and treatment responses. Studies highlight proteins like CD155, DDR1, and EGFR as key regulators of tumor behavior and prognosis [1–3]. CD155 inhibits antitumor immune responses and is linked to poor prognosis in NSCLC patients without genomic alterations[1]. DDR1 promotes NSCLC cell proliferation by enhancing membrane clustering and interacting with pathways like SRC-STAT3 [2]. EGFR inhibitors’ efficacy and drug delivery mechanisms in NSCLC are influenced by transmembrane proteins [2, 3]. Genetic polymorphisms in CLPTM1L and mutations in genes coding for transmembrane proteins like TMEM229A and TSPAN14 are associated with lung cancer risk, metastasis, and poor prognosis, suggesting their potential as biomarkers [4–6].Ultrasound-responsive carriers modulate macrophage polarization, enhancing anti-tumor immunity in NSCLC microenvironments [7]. Glycosylation of transmembrane proteins affects their stability and function, influencing NSCLC cell motility and metastatic potential [8]. Understanding these interactions can guide targeted therapeutic strategies.

TMEM33 is involved in several biological processes critical to cancer pathogenesis. Functional analyses suggest that TMEM33 knockdown in cervical cancer cells leads to decreased cell proliferation and invasion [9]. Moreover, TMEM33 interacts with various tumorigenesis-related genes, indicating its involvement in the molecular pathways that govern cancer cell behavior [10].Beyond its role in proliferation, TMEM33 has been identified as a crucial player in lipid homeostasis regulated by the pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2). The PKM2-TMEM33 axis has been shown to influence cholesterol metabolism in cancer cells, which may suggest a multifaceted role of TMEM33 in supporting tumor growth through metabolic reprogramming [11, 12].Research indicates that TMEM33 functions as a key modulator of the unfolded protein response (UPR), which is pertinent to cancer cell survival under stress conditions. It acts upon critical components of the UPR signaling pathways and affects apoptotic signals, thus influencing cell fate under stress [13].The expression levels of TMEM33 have been negatively correlated with various immune cell types within the tumor microenvironment. This suggests that TMEM33 might contribute to immune evasion, further exacerbating the prognosis for patients with high expression levels in their tumors [9].

This study investigates the expression and function of TMEM33 in lung adenocarcinoma from multiple perspectives, including bioinformatics analysis, cellular experiments, and animal models. We first utilized public databases to analyze the expression differences and prognostic value of TMEM33 in lung adenocarcinoma. Subsequently, we validated these findings in lung adenocarcinoma tissues and cell lines. We then conducted cellular experiments to explore the effects of TMEM33 on cell proliferation and migration. cellular experiments were conducted to explore the impact of TMEM33 on the proliferation and migration of lung adenocarcinoma cells, and to further investigate its role in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway. Finally, a nude mouse model was used to confirm the promoting effect of TMEM33 on tumor growth. The findings of our study are expected to provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms of lung adenocarcinoma and to offer potential targets for the development of new therapeutic strategies.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition

The Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) was utilized, and the GSE140797 dataset was selected. This dataset included 7 lung tissue samples surgically resected from lung adenocarcinoma patients and 7 normal lung tissue samples. The GSE140797 dataset was statistically analyzed and visualized using R software (version 3.6.3). The ggplot2 package was primarily used for visualization. DEGs were identified using the thresholds |log2-fold change (FC)| > 2 and an adjusted P value < 0.01. These DEGs were subsequently subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) annotation and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses. A volcano plot and a heatmap were generated to visualize the results.

Identification of lung adenocarcinoma-associated modules by WGCNA

In this study, Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) was utilized to identify pivotal modules and key genes associated with lung adenocarcinoma. The gene expression profiles were obtained from the GEO database (dataset accession number GSE140797). A soft-thresholding power (β) was determined from a range of 1 to 20 by setting the scale-free topology fit index (R²) to a threshold of > 0.85 to approximate a scale-free network topology.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction and core gene screening

The sequences of the DEGs were imported into the STRING database (version 11.0) to determine the interaction relationships between their encoded proteins. External candidate proteins were excluded during the screening process. A confidence score was set as the reliability indicator for interactions, and all protein‒protein interaction pairs with a confidence score ≥ 0.7 (indicating high confidence) were extracted for further analysis.

Validation of TMEM33 expression using the TCGA database

RNA-seq data from the TCGA-lung adenocarcinoma project, processed using the STAR pipeline, were downloaded and organized from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov). Data in fragments per kilobase of exon model per million mapped fragments (FPKM) format were extracted. The ggplot2 package was used for data visualization, and the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was employed for statistical analysis.

The area under the ROC curve (AUC) was used to validate the diagnostic accuracy of TMEM33 for lung adenocarcinoma

RNA-seq data (in FPKM format) from the TCGA- lung adenocarcinoma project, processed by the STAR pipeline, were downloaded and organized from the TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed on the data using the pROC package, and the results were visualized using the ggplot2 package.

Molecular docking of TMEM33 and PI3K

To predict the potential direct interaction between TMEM33 and PI3K, a rigid molecular docking analysis was performed using the GRAMM platform. First, the canonical amino acid sequences of the target proteins were retrieved from the UniProtKB database using their gene names: TMEM33 (UniProt ID: P57088) and PI3K (UniProt ID: P42399). The three-dimensional structures of both proteins were then submitted to the GRAMM server, and the calculation was initiated using the platform’s default parameters for protein-protein docking. Upon completion, the top 10 resultant conformations, ranked by the docking score, were collected for subsequent analysis. The binding free energy (ΔG) served as the primary evaluation metric (a commonly accepted threshold is ΔG < -4 kcal/mol, with lower values indicating a more stable complex). The optimal docking conformation was further analyzed by examining the interaction interface area, hydrogen bonds, and key amino acid residues. Following the docking procedure, the binding free energy was calculated using PDBePISA, and the complex structures were visualized with PyMOL 3.1. The GRAMM rigid docking generated 10 valid conformations, with the top-ranked conformation exhibiting the most favorable binding characteristics.

Tandem mass tag (TMT)-based quantitative proteomics analysis

To systematically screen for differentially expressed proteins resulting from TMEM33 knockdown and to explore the downstream signaling pathways, we performed a quantitative proteomics analysis using TMT labeling. The 6-plex TMT reagent kit was used to label and quantify the following two sample sets in parallel: the control group (sh-NC, n = 3) and the TMEM33 knockdown group (sh-TMEM33, n = 3). All cellular samples were derived from the H1299 cell line. After protein digestion and peptide labeling, the samples were subjected to liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) analysis. The raw MS data were processed for protein identification and quantification. Significantly differentially expressed proteins were filtered using the criteria of |log2(Fold Change)| > 1.5 and an adjusted P-value (adj. P-value) < 0.05. Subsequently, Gene Ontology (GO) functional annotation (encompassing biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were performed on these proteins to elucidate the potential biological processes and signaling networks regulated by TMEM33. The sample preparation for proteomics, MS detection, and preliminary bioinformatics analysis for this part was conducted by Shanghai Luming Biological Technology Co., Ltd.

Human subjects

Following written informed consent, this study enrolled 10 patients with lung adenocarcinoma who underwent surgical resection at the Department of Thoracic Surgery, The First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University, between July 2023 and December 2024. The cohort had a mean age of 45 ± 12 years and consisted of 6 males and 4 females. The diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma was confirmed in all patients by postoperative histopathological examination. Paired tissue samples were collected from each patient: one set consisted of lung adenocarcinoma tissue obtained from the core region of the tumor lesion (<3 cm from the tumor border), and the other set comprised adjacent normal lung tissue (>3 cm from the tumor border) that was confirmed by pathological evaluation to be free of tumor cell infiltration. All tissue samples were collected within 30 min after resection and were subsequently aliquoted and cryopreserved according to a standardized protocol. This study was conducted in strict accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University (Approval No.: LW2023162).

Cell culture

Lung adenocarcinoma cell lines, including A549, H460, and H1299 (all provided by our laboratory), were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator. These cell lines were used for subsequent experiments to investigate the role of TMEM33 in cell proliferation and migration.

Gene manipulation

Stable overexpression (OE-TMEM33) and knockdown (SH-TMEM33) of TMEM33 were achieved in lung adenocarcinoma cell lines using lentiviral-mediated transfection technology purchased from GenScript (Ji’nan GenScript Biotechnology Co., Ltd.). The transfection efficiency was confirmed by Western Blot analysis, which showed significant changes in TMEM33 expression levels compared to control groups.

Western blot analysis

Protein expression levels were analyzed using Western Blot. Total protein was extracted from cells, and protein concentrations were determined using a Bicinchoninic Acid (BCA) protein assay kit. Proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were incubated with primary antibodies against TMEM33 (A305-597 A-T), PI3K (20584-1-AP), p-PI3K (bs-6417R), AKT (10176-2-AP), p-AKT (80455-1-RR), mTOR (66888-1-IG), p-mTOR (67778-1-IG), and GAPDH (10494-1-AP). The membranes were then incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (SA00001-2) targeting rabbit IgG. Protein bands were detected using an ECL kit and quantified using ImageJ software.

Cell proliferation assays

Cell proliferation was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, catalog number CA1210) and colony formation assays. For the CCK-8 assay, cells were seeded in 96-well plates and treated with the CCK-8 reagent at different time points. Absorbance values at 450 nm were measured using a microplate reader. For the colony formation assay, cells were seeded in 6-well plates and allowed to form colonies over 14 days. Colonies were stained with crystal violet and quantified by counting.

Cell migration assays

Cell migration was evaluated using scratch assays and Transwell assays. In the scratch assay, a scratch was made in the cell monolayer, and the healing of the scratch was monitored over time. For the Transwell assay, cells were seeded in the upper chamber of Transwell inserts coated with Matrigel, and the number of cells that migrated to the lower chamber was quantified after staining with crystal violet.

Animal model

A nude mouse model (BALB/c-Nude, Strain NO. D000521) was used to assess the in vivo effect of TMEM33 on tumor growth. A549 cells with stable overexpression or knockdown of TMEM33 were implanted subcutaneously into nude mice. Tumor growth was monitored by measuring tumor dimensions and calculating tumor volume. At the end of the experiment, tumor tissues were collected for further analysis of protein expression using Western Blot.

Inhibitor treatment

To investigate the role of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway in TMEM33-mediated cell proliferation and migration, we used the PI3K/AKT pathway inhibitor ZSTK474 (SC0411, Selleck Chemicals). Cells were treated with ZSTK474 for 24 h before analysis. The effectiveness of the inhibitor was confirmed by Western Blot analysis, showing reduced phosphorylation levels of PI3K, AKT, and mTOR.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software (version 9.0). Continuous data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Comparisons between two groups were made using a two-sample, two-tailed t-test. Comparisons among three or more groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Bonferroni’s post hoc test for pairwise comparisons if the overall ANOVA was significant. The significance levels were set at P < 0.05, P < 0.01, and P < 0.001. Patient survival analysis was conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method, and differences between groups were assessed with the log-rank test (*P < 0.05). The data shown are derived from at least three independent replicate experiments, with the specific number of replicates indicated in the respective figure legends.

Screening of differentially expressed genes in lung adenocarcinoma and functional analysis of the TMEM33 interaction network

In this study, the GSE140797 dataset (containing 7 cancer tissue samples surgically resected from Lung Adenocarcinoma patients and 7 normal lung tissue samples) was used to perform differential gene expression analysis. The box plot displays the data distribution across all the samples. After data normalization, the median lines of all the samples were aligned at the same level (Supplementary Fig. 1B), indicating good data homogeneity. Principal component analysis (PCA) was subsequently applied to reduce the dimensionality of the high-dimensional data. The results (Supplementary Fig. 1A) clearly revealed significant separation between the cancer and normal tissue samples in the principal component space. On the basis of the screening criteria of |Log2FC| > 2 and adjusted p value (p.adj) < 0.05, a total of 146 significantly upregulated genes and 100 significantly downregulated genes were identified. These DEGs were visualized using a volcano plot (Fig. 1A) and a heatmap (Fig. 1B). Following preprocessing of the GSE140797 dataset, 16,374 genes were retained for subsequent analysis. A soft thresholding power (β) of 16 was selected based on a scale-free topology fit index (R²) of 0.85 to construct a scale-free co-expression network (Fig. 2A, B). Hierarchical clustering was performed, and a heatmap of the topological overlap matrix (TOM) was generated to visualize gene relationships (Fig. 2C). Co-expression modules were identified and subsequently merged using a height cut-off of 0.25, resulting in a final set of 20 distinct modules. Among these, the magenta module demonstrated the highest correlation with lung adenocarcinoma traits (Fig. 2D; R = 0.83, P = 1.5e-5). To pinpoint key genes, a Venn diagram was used to identify the intersection between differentially expressed genes (DEGs) from the GSE140797 dataset and genes within the magenta module, which yielded 14 overlapping co-expressed genes (Fig. 1C). Functional enrichment analysis of the identified DEGs revealed that KEGG pathway enrichment analysis revealed that the DEGs were significantly enriched in PI3K-AKT signaling pathways (Fig. 3B). To further investigate the molecular mechanisms of interactions, the DEGs were mapped to the STRING database to construct a protein‒protein interaction (PPI) network. Analysis of this network revealed that TMEM33 can interact with core oncoproteins such as DMP1, CXCL13, and PI3K (Fig. 3A), suggesting that TMEM33 may play a significant role in the pathogenesis and progression of Lung Adenocarcinoma. Molecular docking analysis revealed a binding energy of -19.5 kcal/mol between TMEM33 and PI3K, indicating spontaneous binding and strong affinity between the two molecules (Fig. 3C).

TMEM33 is highly expressed in lung adenocarcinoma and is associated with poor prognosis

TMEM33 was significantly highly expressed in Lung Adenocarcinoma patients according to the results of the TCGA data analysis (P < 0.001) (Supplementary Fig. 1C). The area under the ROC curve (AUC) for TMEM33 was 0.841, indicating high diagnostic accuracy for Lung Adenocarcinoma (Supplementary Fig. 1D). Clinical data from 512 Lung Adenocarcinoma patients in the TCGA database were used for further validation. Patients were divided into TMEM33 high-expression (n = 270) and low-expression (n = 269) groups on the basis of the median expression value of TMEM33 (> 1.0 defined as high expression; <1.0 defined as low expression). Relationship analysis revealed that TMEM33 expression levels were significantly associated with key clinicopathological features (Supplementary Table 1). To further validate the results from the database analysis, we examined the expression differences of TMEM33 between lung adenocarcinoma tissues and normal lung tissues using qRT-PCR and Western Blot techniques. These experiments confirmed the differential expression observed in the database analysis. We collected six pairs of lung adenocarcinoma and adjacent normal tissue samples and detected the mRNA and protein expression levels of TMEM33 using qRT-PCR and Western Blot techniques. The results showed that the mRNA expression of TMEM33 in lung adenocarcinoma tissues was significantly higher than in adjacent normal tissues (P < 0.001) (Fig. 4A), and the protein expression levels also showed a similar trend (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4B).we detected the protein expression levels of TMEM33 in seven different lung adenocarcinoma cell lines and compared them with those in normal human bronchial epithelial cells (HBE) using Western Blot analysis. Western Blot analysis revealed high expression of TMEM33 in lung adenocarcinoma cell lines (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4C). Through the aforementioned experiments, we concluded that TMEM33 is highly expressed in lung adenocarcinoma and is significantly associated with poor prognosis in patients. This finding provides experimental evidence for TMEM33 as a potential biomarker and therapeutic target for lung adenocarcinoma.

TMEM33 promotes the proliferation of lung adenocarcinoma cells

To investigate the role of TMEM33 in the proliferation of lung adenocarcinoma cells, we first analyzed the baseline expression levels of TMEM33 in three lung adenocarcinoma cell lines (A549, H460, and H1299) using Western Blot. We found that A549 cells exhibited moderate TMEM33 expression, H1299 cells had high expression, and H460 cells had low expression. Based on these findings, we performed stable overexpression (OE-TMEM33) in A549 and H460 cells, and stable knockdown (SH-TMEM33) in A549 and H1299 cells using lentiviral-mediated transfection technology. This approach allowed us to assess the effects of both increased and decreased TMEM33 expression on cell proliferation in different cellular contexts. The transfection efficiency was confirmed by Western Blot, showing that the expression level of TMEM33 was significantly higher in the overexpression group than in the control group (NC-TMEM33), while it was significantly lower in the knockdown group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5A). After confirming the transfection efficiency, we further detected the expression levels of the proliferation marker PCNA (proliferating cell nuclear antigen) using Western Blot (P < 0.01). The results showed that the protein expression of PCNA was significantly higher in the TMEM33 overexpression group compared to the control group, while it was significantly reduced in the TMEM33 knockdown group, indicating that TMEM33 may positively regulate the proliferation of lung adenocarcinoma cells (Fig. 5A). To quantitatively assess the impact of TMEM33 on cell proliferation, we used the CCK8 cell proliferation kit. At different time points after transfection (0 h, 24 h, 48 h, 72 h), we measured the absorbance (OD value) in the cell culture supernatant to reflect cell proliferation. The results showed that the OD value in the TMEM33 overexpression group increased significantly over time, indicating enhanced cell proliferation; in contrast, the OD value in the TMEM33 knockdown group increased slowly, indicating inhibited cell proliferation. Statistical analysis showed that these differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5B). To further verify the effect of TMEM33 on cell proliferation, we conducted a colony formation assay. After transfection, cells were seeded in culture dishes and the formation of colonies was observed at appropriate time points. The results showed that the total area of colonies formed in the TMEM33 overexpression group was significantly greater than that in the control group, while the total area of colonies in the TMEM33 knockdown group was significantly reduced. Statistical analysis of the total colony area further confirmed the promoting effect of TMEM33 on the proliferation of lung adenocarcinoma cells (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5C). Through the aforementioned experiments, we concluded that TMEM33 promotes proliferation in lung adenocarcinoma cells, providing further experimental evidence for TMEM33 as a potential therapeutic target for lung adenocarcinoma.

TMEM33 promotes the migration of lung adenocarcinoma cells

After transfection, we observed changes in cell morphology using a microscope and found that cells overexpressing TMEM33 exhibited a more dispersed and active migratory morphology, while cells with knockdown were relatively concentrated with reduced migratory morphology. We employed a scratch assay to quantitatively assess the impact of TMEM33 on cell migration. After transfection, we manually created a “scratch” in the cell monolayer and recorded the healing of the scratch at different time points (0 h, 24 h). By calculating the closure ratio of the scratch area, we found that the migration ability of cells in the TMEM33 overexpression group was significantly enhanced, with faster scratch healing, while the healing speed was slowed in the TMEM33 knockdown group. These results were quantified using image analysis software and compared with the control group, showing statistically significant differences (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6A). To further verify the effect of TMEM33 on cell migration, we conducted a Transwell assay. After transfection, cells were seeded in the upper chamber of Transwell inserts. After a certain period, we determined the number of cells that had migrated through the Transwell membrane to the lower chamber using crystal violet staining. The results showed that the number of migrating cells in the TMEM33 overexpression group was significantly higher than in the control group, while the migration number in the TMEM33 knockdown group was significantly lower. These results were quantified through microscopic observation and image analysis and were statistically analyzed (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6B). Through the aforementioned experiments, we concluded that TMEM33 promotes migration in lung adenocarcinoma cells, possibly by regulating the expression of proteins related to cell migration. This finding provides further experimental evidence for TMEM33 as a potential therapeutic target for lung adenocarcinoma.

TMEM33 positively regulates the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway

Through high-throughput mass spectrometry analysis, we identified potential interacting proteins of TMEM33 in lung adenocarcinoma cells. The box plot displays the data distribution across all the samples. After data normalization, the median lines of all the samples were aligned at the same level, indicating good data homogeneity (Fig. 7A). PCA was subsequently applied to reduce the dimensionality of the high-dimensional data. The results (Fig. 7B) clearly revealed significant separation between the lung adenocarcinoma cells and normal cell in the principal component space. On the basis of the screening criteria of |Log2FC| > 2 and adjusted p value (p.adj) < 0.05, a total of 53 significantly upregulated genes and 120 significantly downregulated genes were identified. These DEGs were visualized using a volcano plot (Fig. 7C). Functional enrichment analysis of the identified DEGs revealed that at the cellular component (CC) level, the DEGs were primarily enriched in membrane attack complex. At the biological process (BP) level, these genes were involved mainly in regulation of microvillus length and at the molecular function (MF) level, these genes were involved mainly in Ketosteroid monooxygenase activity (Fig. 7D). The results showed that TMEM33 interacts with key proteins in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway (Fig. 7E). This finding suggests that TMEM33 may influence the activity of this signaling pathway either directly or indirectly. To investigate whether TMEM33 affects the biological behavior of lung adenocarcinoma cells through the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, we used Western Blot to detect the expression levels of key proteins in the pathway after transfection, including PI3K, phosphorylated PI3K (p-PI3K), AKT, phosphorylated AKT (p-AKT), mTOR, and phosphorylated mTOR (p-mTOR).The Western Blot results showed that the expression levels of p-PI3K, p-AKT, and p-mTOR were significantly higher in the TMEM33 overexpression group compared to the control group, indicating that TMEM33 overexpression activates the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway. Conversely, in the TMEM33 knockdown group, the expression levels of these phosphorylated proteins were significantly reduced, suggesting that TMEM33 knockdown inhibits the activity of this signaling pathway. Additionally, the total protein expression levels of PI3K, AKT, and mTOR did not show significant differences among the groups, indicating that TMEM33’s effect on the signaling pathway is mediated through the regulation of protein phosphorylation rather than total protein expression levels (Fig. 8A, B).

TMEM33 promotes the proliferation and migration of lung adenocarcinoma cells through the PI3K/AKT pathway

To elucidate the mechanism by which TMEM33 promotes the proliferation and migration of lung adenocarcinoma cells, we first examined the expression of key proteins in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway using Western Blot analysis. We treated A549 and H460 cells overexpressing TMEM33 with the PI3K/AKT pathway inhibitor ZSTK474 (SC0411, Selleck Chemicals) to assess the impact on signaling pathway activation. Western Blot experiments revealed that ZSTK474 treatment significantly reduced the expression levels of p-PI3K, p-AKT, and p-mTOR in cells overexpressing TMEM33 compared to untreated cells (Fig. 9B). The total expression levels of PI3K, AKT, and mTOR remained unchanged, indicating that TMEM33 promotes cell proliferation and migration by specifically enhancing the phosphorylation and activation of these proteins in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway. Next, we evaluated the effect of ZSTK474 treatment on cell proliferation using the colony formation assay. The results showed that in cells overexpressing TMEM33, treatment with ZSTK474 significantly decreased the number of colonies formed compared to untreated cells (P < 0.05) (Fig. 9A). This indicates that inhibition of the PI3K/AKT pathway effectively suppresses the proliferative effect of TMEM33.We also assessed the impact of ZSTK474 treatment on cell migration using scratch and Transwell assays. In the scratch assay, the migration ability of cells overexpressing TMEM33 was significantly reduced after ZSTK474 treatment, with a slower scratch healing speed compared to untreated cells (P < 0.05,) (Fig. 9C). Similarly, in the Transwell assay, the number of migrating cells in the ZSTK474-treated group was significantly lower than in the untreated group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 9D). These results further confirm that the PI3K/AKT pathway is crucial for TMEM33-promoted cell migration.

TMEM33 promotes lung adenocarcinoma tumor growth in a nude mouse model

To further validate the in vivo effects of TMEM33 on the growth of lung adenocarcinoma tumors, we conducted experiments using a nude mouse model. Initially, we subcutaneously implanted A549 cells with stable overexpression of TMEM33 (OE-TMEM33 group), control cells (VC-TMEM33 group), A549 cells with TMEM33 knockdown (SH-TMEM33 group), and control cells (NC-TMEM33 group) into nude mice to establish tumor models. Each group consisted of five nude mice to ensure the reliability of the experimental results. After the implantation of tumor cells, we regularly measured the long and short diameters of the tumors using calipers, calculated the tumor volume, and recorded the body weight of the nude mice. The formula for calculating tumor volume was: Volume = (long diameter × short diameter²) / 2. Meanwhile, we monitored the health status of the nude mice to ensure their welfare during the experiment. At the end of the experiment, we collected tumor tissue samples from the nude mice and used Western Blot technology to detect the expression levels of TMEM33, proteins related to the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, and the proliferation marker PCNA. This helped us understand the in vivo effects of TMEM33 on tumor cell proliferation and signaling pathway activity (Fig. 10C). We plotted the curve of the body weight changes of the nude mice over time (Fig. 10A) and conducted statistical analysis to assess the impact of tumor growth on the overall health status of the nude mice. The results showed that the body weight of nude mice in the OE-TMEM33 group was significantly lower than that in the VC-TMEM33 group (P < 0.0001), and the body weight of nude mice in the NC-TMEM33 group was significantly lower than that in the SH-TMEM33 group (P < 0.0001).Additionally, we recorded the changes in tumor volume over time (Fig. 10B) to evaluate the effects of TMEM33 on tumor growth. The results indicated that the tumor volume in the OE-TMEM33 group was significantly larger than that in the NC-TMEM33 group (P < 0.001), and the tumor volume in the NC-TMEM33 group was significantly larger than that in the SH-TMEM33 group (P < 0.05), further confirming the role of TMEM33 in promoting tumor growth in vivo. Based on the aforementioned experiments, we concluded that TMEM33 significantly promotes the growth of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) tumors in the nude mouse model, which may be related to its activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway in vivo. These findings provide robust in vivo experimental evidence for TMEM33 as a potential therapeutic target for NSCLC.

Discussion

The TMEM family of proteins has garnered attention due to their abnormal expression and functional diversity in various tumors. These proteins play a significant role in the occurrence, development, metastasis, and therapeutic response of tumors. The abnormal expression of TMEM family members is associated with poor prognosis in a variety of cancers, making them potential diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets [14–16].

Members of the TMEM family are often found to be abnormally expressed in cancer cells. For instance, some TMEM proteins are significantly upregulated in tumor tissues compared to normal tissues, while others may be downregulated. This aberrant expression can disrupt normal cellular functions and contribute to the development and progression of cancer. The expression patterns of TMEM proteins can vary significantly across different types of cancer. For example, certain TMEM proteins may be highly expressed in lung adenocarcinoma but not in lung squamous cell carcinoma. This tissue-specific expression makes them potential biomarkers for specific cancer subtypes. Many TMEM proteins are involved in signaling pathways that regulate cell proliferation. For example, TMEM33 has been shown to promote cell proliferation in lung adenocarcinoma by activating the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway [14].TMEM proteins also play crucial roles in cell migration and metastasis. They can influence the cytoskeleton dynamics and cell adhesion properties, facilitating the movement of cancer cells to distant sites. For instance, the overexpression of certain TMEM proteins has been linked to increased metastatic potential in breast cancer [15].The expression levels of TMEM proteins can affect the response of cancer cells to various treatments. Some TMEM proteins are involved in drug resistance mechanisms, making them potential targets for overcoming treatment resistance. For example, studies have shown that targeting specific TMEM proteins can enhance the efficacy of chemotherapy and targeted therapies [16].

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide, with its incidence and mortality rates remaining high. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), as the main subtype of lung cancer, accounts for about 85% of all lung cancer cases [17, 18]. The complex molecular mechanisms and poor prognosis of NSCLC have always been a hot topic of research. Despite certain advancements in targeted therapy and immunotherapy in recent years, the survival rate of NSCLC patients is still not satisfactory, highlighting the need for new therapeutic targets and strategies [19, 20].

Our research results indicate that TMEM33 is highly expressed in lung adenocarcinoma and is associated with poor prognosis. Additionally, the high expression of TMEM33 promotes cell proliferation and migration, which is closely related to its regulatory role in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway. To comprehensively evaluate the impact of TMEM33 on cell proliferation, we employed multiple experimental approaches, including the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay and colony formation assay, and detected the expression levels of proliferation-related proteins using Western Blot. These experiments collectively validated the promoting effect of TMEM33 on cell proliferation from multiple angles. Regarding cell migration, we conducted functional experiments such as scratch and Transwell assays but did not detect changes in the expression levels of migration-related proteins. Western Blot analysis showed that the overexpression of TMEM33 significantly increased the phosphorylation levels of key proteins in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, while the knockdown of TMEM33 inhibited the phosphorylation of these proteins. Furthermore, experiments using the PI3K/AKT pathway inhibitor ZSTK474 demonstrated that TMEM33-induced cell proliferation and migration can be effectively inhibited, further confirming that TMEM33 promotes the progression of lung adenocarcinoma by activating the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway. These findings provide new insights for future therapeutic strategies, suggesting that targeting TMEM33 or its downstream signaling pathways could inhibit tumor growth and dissemination.

The PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway is crucial in cancer biology, promoting tumor growth and survival [21]. Its dysregulation leads to the development of various malignancies, including pancreatic cancer [22]and breast cancer [23], affecting cell proliferation, invasion, and resistance to treatment [24]. Targeting this pathway in cancer therapy shows promising prospects, potentially improving patient outcomes by overcoming resistance mechanisms associated with conventional treatments.

The PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway is frequently dysregulated in lung cancer, leading to enhanced tumor growth and increased resistance to treatment. Studies have shown that PI3K/mTOR inhibitors, such as 5nh, effectively disrupt this pathway and suppress tumor growth in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cell lines [25]. Dual-targeting strategies, like combining PI3K inhibitors with EGFR inhibitors, have also shown potential to enhance therapeutic effects [26]. Components of the tumor microenvironment, including senescent fibroblasts [27] and tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) [28], promote lung cancer progression by influencing the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway. Additionally, long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) such as LINC00941 can enhance tumor cell glycolysis and invasiveness by strengthening this pathway [29]. Novel therapeutic agents, including triazine inhibitors and carbon dot-vesicle conjugates (HACD-TMAV), have demonstrated significant selective cytotoxicity in lung adenocarcinoma cells, highlighting the potential for targeted therapy [30].

Our study confirmed that TMEM33 promotes the proliferation and migration of lung adenocarcinoma cells by activating the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway, further emphasizing its key role in NSCLC. This finding suggests that targeting TMEM33 could be a promising therapeutic strategy for lung cancer. However, while the involvement of the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway in TMEM33-mediated effects is clear, the specific molecular mechanisms and interactions within this pathway require further investigation. While this study elucidates the mechanism by which TMEM33 promotes the proliferation and migration of lung adenocarcinoma through activating the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, it is not without limitations that should be addressed in future work. First, the sample sizes for some key in vitro and in vivo experiments were relatively limited. For instance, certain cell-based functional assays were conducted with three biological replicates (n = 3). Although statistically significant results were obtained with the current sample sizes, employing larger cohorts in future studies would help to further strengthen the robustness and generalizability of the conclusions. Future research should aim to validate and consolidate these findings by expanding the sample sizes.

In summary, this study has revealed the high expression of TMEM33 in lung adenocarcinoma and its role in promoting tumor progression through the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway. Although our research provides important insights into the role of TMEM33 in lung adenocarcinoma, there are some limitations. Firstly, our study primarily focuses on lung adenocarcinoma, and the role of TMEM33 in squamous cell carcinoma of the lung and other lung cancer subtypes remains unclear. Secondly, while we have elucidated the relationship between TMEM33 and the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, other signaling pathways and molecular mechanisms that TMEM33 may be involved in still need further exploration. However, given that ZSTK474 is a broad‑spectrum PI3K inhibitor, we cannot completely exclude the possibility that TMEM33 may also signal through parallel pathways such as MAPK, which represents a limitation of the current study and warrants future investigation. Future research can expand the sample size to delve into the role of TMEM33 in different lung cancer subtypes and investigate its interactions with other signaling pathways. Additionally, conducting clinical trials to validate the potential of TMEM33 as a biomarker and therapeutic target is also an important direction for future research.
