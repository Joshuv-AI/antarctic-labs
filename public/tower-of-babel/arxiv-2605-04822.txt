Stability and Bifurcation Analysis of Fractional Delay Differential Equation with a Delay-dependent Coefficient
Pragati Dutta, Sachin Bhalekar

Abstract
This paper investigates the stability of different regions in the ( k , γ ) (k,\gamma) -plane for a class of fractional delay differential equations given by D α ​ x ​ ( t ) = − γ ​ x ​ ( t ) + g ⁡ ( x ⁡ ( t − τ 1 ) ) − e − γ ​ τ 2 ​ g ​ ( x ⁡ ( t − τ 1 − τ 2 ) ) , 0 < α ≤ 1 , D^{\alpha}x(t)=-\gamma x(t)+g\big(x(t-\tau_{1})\big)-e^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}\,g\big(x(t-\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\big),\qquad 0<\alpha\leq 1, (1) where k = g ′ ​ ( 0 ) k=g^{\prime}(0) . The primary focus is on the stability of the trivial equilibrium of the corresponding linearized system. A detailed stability and bifurcation analysis is carried out for the particular case τ 1 = 0 \tau_{1}=0 and τ 2 ≥ 0 \tau_{2}\geq 0 . Furthermore, a general result is established for the case τ 1 > 0 \tau_{1}>0 , τ 2 ≥ 0 \tau_{2}\geq 0 , which holds for all values of α \alpha and τ 1 \tau_{1} . In addition, illustrative examples are provided in the form of stability diagrams in the ( τ 1 , τ 2 ) (\tau_{1},\tau_{2}) -plane for fixed values of α \alpha , k k , and γ \gamma . These diagrams are generated using appropriate numerical methods to visualize the stability regions and to support the theoretical results.

1
Introduction

In many real-world systems, the present state depends not only on current conditions but also on past behavior. Fractional calculus provides a natural framework to capture such memory effects through derivatives of non-integer order. Unlike classical integer-order derivatives, fractional derivatives are non-local operators and inherently account for system history
34
;
26
. This makes fractional differential equations (FDEs) particularly suitable for modeling phenomena in viscoelasticity, control systems, signal processing, and biological processes
16
;
32
;
29
. Moreover, the flexibility in the order of differentiation allows better agreement with experimental observations
6
;
35
. The qualitative behavior and stability of such systems have been widely studied using Lyapunov-type and related analytical methods
3
.

Another important feature in modeling real systems is the presence of time delays, where the rate of change depends on past states. This leads to delay differential equations (DDEs), which arise naturally in population dynamics, physiology, engineering, and control theory
23
;
18
;
27
. Although similar in form to ordinary differential equations, DDEs are infinite-dimensional and can exhibit oscillations, bifurcations, and chaos even in simple settings
23
;
38
;
36
;
25
. They have been widely applied across scientific and engineering disciplines
30
;
21
;
3
. In this context, discrete and difference equation techniques also play an important role in the analysis of delay systems
4
.

The combination of fractional dynamics with time delays leads to fractional delay differential equations (FDDEs), which provide a more realistic framework for systems exhibiting both memory and delayed responses. Various numerical and analytical methods have been developed for such systems
8
;
15
, and their stability and bifurcation properties have been extensively studied
11
;
7
;
22
;
9
. In particular, practical stability concepts and Razumikhin-type methods have been successfully applied to fractional delay systems
1
;
2
. Moreover, FDDEs are known to exhibit complex dynamics, including chaotic behavior
10
;
14
.

In many applications, systems involve more than one delay, each representing a different mechanism acting over its own time scale
20
;
31
. Such multi-delay models appear in population dynamics and biological systems, for example in describing maturation and feedback processes
13
;
33
;
12
. However, the presence of multiple delays significantly increases analytical complexity. In addition, delay-dependent coefficients further enhance modeling flexibility by allowing the influence of past states to vary explicitly with the delay. Models with such features have been analyzed using geometric and characteristic equation approaches
5
, and delay-dependent stability criteria have been developed for systems with fixed, distributed, and nonlinear delays
24
;
19
.

In our earlier work
17
, we investigated the stability of a nonlinear fractional delay differential equation with two discrete delays and obtained several delay-independent results in the
(k,\gamma)
-plane. However, a complete characterization of the remaining parameter regions and the combined effect of both delays was not fully addressed. Motivated by this, the present paper completes the analysis for the case
\tau_{1}=0
and derives new results for the general two-delay system. We also study the interaction between the delays through stability diagrams in the
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
-plane, providing further insight into the system dynamics.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
presents the necessary preliminaries. Section
3
introduces the model and derives the characteristic equation. Section
4
analyzes the case
\tau_{1}=0
, while Section
5
provides a sufficient condition for instability in the general case. Section
6
presents illustrative examples with stability diagrams in the
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
-plane. Finally, Section
7
concludes the paper.

Definition 2.1

(Riemann–Liouville Fractional Integral)
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(0,b)
and
\mu>0
. The Riemann–Liouville fractional integral of order
\mu
is defined by

I^{\mu}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\mu-1}f(\tau)\,d\tau,\qquad 0<t<b.

Definition 2.2

(Caputo Fractional Derivative)
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(0,b)
,
\mu>0
, and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m-1<\mu\leq m
. The Caputo fractional derivative of order
\mu
is defined by

D^{\mu}f(t)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}f(t),&\text{if }\mu=m,\\[8.0pt]
I^{\,m-\mu}\left(\dfrac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}f(t)\right),&\text{if }m-1<\mu<m,\end{cases}

where
I^{\mu}
is the Riemann–Liouville fractional integral.

Moreover, for
m-1<\mu\leq m
, we have

I^{\mu}\,D^{\mu}f(t)=f(t)-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}t^{k}.

Definition 2.3

(Equilibrium Point)
.

Consider the fractional delay differential equation

D^{\alpha}x(t)=f\big(x(t),x(t-\tau)\big),\qquad 0<\alpha\leq 1,

(*)

where
\tau>0
and
f\in C^{1}(E)
with
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
open.

A constant solution
x(t)=x^{*}
pf (
*
‣
2.3
) is called an equilibrium point, it satisfies

f(x^{*},x^{*})=0.

Definition 2.4

(Initial Value Problem)
.

Consider the delay differential equation together with the initial function

x(t)=\phi(t),\qquad-\tau\leq t\leq 0,

where
\phi:[-\tau,0]\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function.

The corresponding solution is denoted by
x(t,\phi)
, and the norm of
\phi
is defined as

\|\phi\|=\sup_{-\tau\leq t\leq 0}|\phi(t)|.

Definition 2.5

(Stability)
.

An equilibrium point
x^{*}
is said to be stable if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\|\phi-x^{*}\|<\delta\;\Rightarrow\;|x(t,\phi)-x^{*}|<\varepsilon,\quad\text{for all }t\geq 0.

Definition 2.6

(Asymptotic Stability)
.

An equilibrium point
x^{*}
is said to be asymptotically stable if it is stable and there exists
b_{0}>0
such that

\|\phi-x^{*}\|<b_{0}\;\Rightarrow\;\lim_{t\to\infty}x(t,\phi)=x^{*}.

Definition 2.7

(Instability)
.

An equilibrium point that is not stable is called unstable.

Theorem 2.1
.

Consider the scalar FDE with a single discrete delay:

D^{\alpha}x(t)=ax(t)+bx(t-\tau),\quad 0<\alpha\leq 1,\tau\geq 0.

(2)

Then, the zero equilibrium
x_{*}=0
has the following stability behavior:

1.

If
b\in(-\infty,-|a|)
, then the equilibrium is asymptotically stable for
\tau\in[0,\tau_{cr})
and the system undergoes Hopf bifurcation at

\tau_{*}^{\prime}=\frac{\arccos\left(\frac{(a\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})+\sqrt{b^{2}-a^{2}\sin^{2}(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})})\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})-a}{b}\right)}{\left(a\cos\left(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}\right)+\sqrt{b^{2}-a^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}\right)}\right)^{1/\alpha}}.

2.

If
b\in(-a,\infty)
, then the equilibrium is unstable for all
\tau\geq 0
.

3.

If
a<0
and
b\in(a,-a)
, then the equilibrium is asymptotically stable for all
\tau\geq 0
.

Model Formulation and Linearization

We consider the nonlinear fractional delay differential equation of the form

D^{\alpha}x(t)=-\gamma x(t)+g\big(x(t-\tau_{1})\big)-e^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}\,g\big(x(t-\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\big),\qquad 0<\alpha\leq 1,

(3)

where
D^{\alpha}
denotes the Caputo fractional derivative,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
The parameters
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\geq 0
represent discrete delays, while the exponential factor
e^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}
introduces a delay-dependent coefficient.
The integer-order counterpart of (
3
), analyzed in
12

Let
x_{*}
denote an equilibrium point of (
3
). It satisfies

-\gamma x_{*}+g(x_{*})-e^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}\,g(x_{*})=0.

Here, we assume
g
to be a nonlinear function such that
g(0)=0
, it immediately follows that
x_{*}=0
is an equilibrium of (
3
).

Assuming further that
g^{\prime}(0)=k
, we linearize the system in a neighborhood of the equilibrium
x_{*}=0
.

The linearized form of the equation is given by

D^{\alpha}x(t)=-\gamma x(t)+k\,x(t-\tau_{1})-ke^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}\,x(t-\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}).

(4)

The local stability of the nonlinear equation (
3
) in a neighborhood of the equilibrium
x_{*}=0
coincides with that of its linearized equation.
The corresponding characteristic equation associated with Eq. (
4

\lambda^{\alpha}=-\gamma+ke^{-\lambda\tau_{1}}-ke^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}e^{-\lambda(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})}.

(5)

Some stability results for Eq.(
4

17

Theorem 5.1
.

The equilibrium point
x_{*}=0
of Eq. (
3
) i.e

D^{\alpha}x(t)=-\gamma x(t)+g\big(x(t-\tau_{1})\big)-e^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}\,g\big(x(t-\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\big),\qquad 0<\alpha\leq 1

(10)

exhibits the following stability behavior:

Delay-Independent Instability:

(i)

If
0<\gamma<k
, then for

\tau_{2}>-\frac{1}{\gamma}\log\left(\frac{k-\gamma}{k}\right),

the equilibrium is unstable for all
\tau_{1}\geq 0
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
.

(ii)

If
\gamma<0<k
, then for

\tau_{2}<-\frac{1}{\gamma}\log\left(\frac{k-\gamma}{k}\right),

the equilibrium is unstable for all
\tau_{1}\geq 0
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
.

Proof.

Consider the linearized version of fractional delay differential equation (
3
) i.e

D^{\alpha}x(t)=-\gamma x(t)+k\,x(t-\tau_{1})-ke^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}\,x(t-\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}),

(11)

where
0<\alpha\leq 1
. The stability of the equilibrium
x_{*}=0
is determined by the roots of the corresponding characteristic equation i.e

\lambda^{\alpha}=-\gamma+ke^{-\lambda\tau_{1}}-ke^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}e^{-\lambda(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})}.

Equivalently,

\lambda^{\alpha}+\gamma-ke^{-\lambda\tau_{1}}+ke^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}e^{-\lambda(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})}=0.

(12)

Define

\Delta(\lambda):=\lambda^{\alpha}+\gamma-ke^{-\lambda\tau_{1}}+ke^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}e^{-\lambda(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})}.

Evaluating at
\lambda=0
, we get

\Delta(0)=\gamma-k+ke^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}.

The sign of
\Delta(0)
plays a key role in determining the existence of positive real roots.

For real
\lambda>0
, note that

\lambda^{\alpha}\to+\infty\quad\text{as }\lambda\to+\infty,

while the exponential terms remain bounded. Hence,

\Delta(\lambda)\to+\infty\quad\text{as }\lambda\to+\infty.

Thus, if
\Delta(0)<0
, then by continuity there exists
\lambda>0
such that
\Delta(\lambda)=0
. This yields a real positive root, implying instability.

We have

\Delta(0)<0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\gamma-k+ke^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}<0,

which simplifies to

e^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}<\frac{k-\gamma}{k}.

Case (i):
0<\gamma<k
.

Here
\frac{k-\gamma}{k}\in(0,1)
. Taking logarithm, we get,

-\gamma\tau_{2}<\log\left(\frac{k-\gamma}{k}\right).

\Leftrightarrow\tau_{2}>-\frac{1}{\gamma}\log\left(\frac{k-\gamma}{k}\right).

Thus,
\Delta(0)<0
, and hence a positive real root exists. Therefore, the equilibrium is unstable. The condition is independent of
\tau_{1}
, so instability holds for all
\tau_{1}\geq 0
.

Case (ii):
\gamma<0<k
.

Now
\frac{k-\gamma}{k}>1
. Again,

e^{-\gamma\tau_{2}}<\frac{k-\gamma}{k}.

Taking logarithm, we get,

-\gamma\tau_{2}<\log\left(\frac{k-\gamma}{k}\right).

\Leftrightarrow\tau_{2}<-\frac{1}{\gamma}\log\left(\frac{k-\gamma}{k}\right).

Thus,
\Delta(0)<0
, implying existence of a positive real root and hence instability for all
\tau_{1}\geq 0
.

The condition for instability depends only on
\Delta(0)
, which does not involve
\tau_{1}
. Therefore, the instability is delay-independent with respect to
\tau_{1}
.

Hence, in both cases, the equilibrium
x_{*}=0
is unstable for all
\tau_{1}\geq 0
whenever the stated condition on
\tau_{2}
holds.
∎

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we have extended our earlier work
17
on nonlinear fractional delay differential equations with two discrete delays by completing the stability analysis in the remaining regions of the
(k,\gamma)
-plane. In particular, for the case
\tau_{1}=0
, a full characterization of the stability behavior has been obtained, thereby filling the gaps left in the previous study.
For the general case involving two delays, we derived a new stability result that provides a sufficient condition for instability in certain parameter regions.

In addition, we investigated the combined influence of the two delays by constructing stability diagrams in the
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
-plane for selected parameter values.
Overall, the results presented in this work contribute to a more complete understanding of fractional delay systems with multiple delays and delay-dependent coefficients. Future work may consider more general nonlinearities, distributed delays, and higher-dimensional systems.
