1.
Introduction

In 1872, Weierstrass
[
21
]
constructed the pathological function in series form

W(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{n}\cos(2\pi b^{n}x),\quad x\in[0,1],

(1)

where
0<a<1<b,\ ab>1
, and gave the first rigorous proof that
W
is continuous everywhere but nowhere differentiable for some
a
and
b
. This overturned the conventional intuition in classical analysis that “continuity implies almost everywhere differentiability”. In 1916, Hardy
[
6
]
refined the proof of non-differentiability and laid a solid theoretical foundation. In 1937, Besicovitch and Ursell
[
4
]
regarded the graph of
W
as a fractal set from the perspective of geometric measure theory for the first time, and derived bounds for its Hausdorff dimension. In 1977, Mandelbrot
[
13
]
proposed the famous conjecture:

\dim_{H}(G(W))=\dim_{B}(G(W))=2+\frac{\log a}{\log b},

where
\dim_{H},\dim_{B}
are Hausdorff and box dimensions and
G(W)
denotes the graph of
W
over
[0,1]
. This conjecture has become an iconic open problem in fractal geometry.

Figure 1.
The graph of Weierstrass function with
a=0.6,b=4
.

In 1984, Kaplan, Mallet-Paret, and Yorke
[
10
]
employed methods from dynamical systems, viewing the graph of the Weierstrass function as the attractor of an expanding dynamical system, and established the box dimension formula mentioned above. In 1986, Mauldin and Williams
[
12
]
proved that
\dim_{H}(G(W))
is bounded below by
2+{\log a}/{\log b}-O(1/{\log b})
for sufficiently large
b
. Soon later, Przytycki and Urbański
[
14
]
showed that
\dim_{H}(G(W))>1
. In 1992, Ledrappier
[
11
]
used Pesin theory to obtain almost-sure dimension results for the sawtooth-type Weierstrass function. In 1995, Solomyak
[
20
]
provided crucial support for the conjecture using transversality arguments and absolute continuity of Bernoulli convolutions. In 1998, Hunt
[
8
]
introduced an independent and identically distributed random phase sequence
\Theta=\{\theta_{n}\}
and constructed the randomized Weierstrass function

W_{\Theta}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{n}\cos\left(2\pi\left(b^{n}x+\theta_{n}\right)\right).

He proved that with probability one, the Hausdorff dimension of the graph of
W_{\Theta}
equals the conjectured value, i.e.,

\dim_{H}(G(W_{\Theta}))=2+\frac{\log a}{\log b}\qquad\text{a.e.-}\Theta.

In 2003, Heurteaux
[
7
]
studied the Weierstrass-type function with random phases

f_{a,b}^{\phi,\Theta}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{n}\phi(b^{n}x+\theta_{n}),

where
\phi
is a Lipschitz function with period
1
, and the parameters satisfy
b>1
and
1/b<a<1
. He showed that the function satisfies a Hölder condition almost surely, and both the packing dimension and box dimension of its graph equal
2+{\log a}/{\log b}
almost surely. In 2011, Baránski
[
2
]
extended the result to the general deterministic Lipschitz periodic function

f_{a,b}^{\phi}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{n}\phi(b^{n}x),

(2)

and proved that its box dimension satisfies

\dim_{B}(G(f_{a,b}^{\phi}))=2+\frac{\log a}{\log b}.

In 2014, Baránski, Bárány, and Romanowska
[
3
]
showed that Mandelbrot’s conjecture is true for any integer
b>1
and a large set of parameters
a
. In 2018, Shen
[
19
]
completely resolved Mandelbrot’s conjecture using techniques in ergodic theory and dynamical systems, under the conditions that
b>1
is an integer and
1/b<a<1
. In 2021, Ren and Shen
[
15
]
further generalized the above result. They proved that for any integer
b>1
and
1/b<a<1
, and for any real-analytic periodic function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, the function
f_{a,b}^{\phi}
of the form (
2
) is either real-analytic (in which case the graph has dimension
1
) or a fractal function (in which case the Hausdorff dimension of its graph equals
2+{\log a}/{\log b}
).

On the other hand, partial progress has been made on the dimension of graphs of vector-valued Weierstrass functions. It is known that when
b>1
is an integer, the classical Weierstrass function (
1
) is the real part of the lacunary complex power series

w(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{n}z^{b^{n}},\quad z\in\mathbb{C},\ |z|\leq 1

on the unit circle. Salem and Zygmund
[
18
]
and Kahane, Weiss, and Weiss
[
9
]
showed that when
a
is sufficiently close to
1
, the image of
w
on the unit circle becomes a Peano curve. In 2002, Baránski
[
1
]
further proved that the box dimension of the vector-valued Weierstrass function

f_{a,b}(x)=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{n}\cos(2\pi b^{n}x),\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{n}\sin(2\pi b^{n}x)\right)

satisfies

\dim_{B}(G(f_{a,b}))=3+2\frac{\log a}{\log b},

where the graph
G(f_{a,b})
is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In 2023, Ren
[
16
]
extended Baránski’s result by replacing trigonometric functions with a vector-valued Lipschitz function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of period
1
. He proved that if
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\phi(\mathbb{R})
is disconnected, then for any integer
b>1
and for
a
sufficiently close to
1
, the box dimension of the graph of the vector-valued Weierstrass-type function (
2
) is given by

\dim_{B}(G(f_{a,b}^{\phi}))=3+2\frac{\log a}{\log b}.

More recently, Ren and Shen
[
17
]
improved their one-dimensional result into high-dimensional vector-valued Weierstrass functions.

Inspired by the aforementioned results, this paper is devoted to a further investigation of randomized vector-valued Weierstrass functions. Specifically, we focus on verifying the validity of the Mandelbrot conjecture in high-dimensional random contexts, as well as establishing the consistency between the Hausdorff dimension and the box dimension of the graph of such functions.

Let the infinite-dimensional product space be
H=[0,1]^{\infty}
, and let

\Theta=\{\theta_{n}\}\in H
and
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}\in H

be two sequences of random phases, which are mutually independent and uniformly distributed over
[0,1]
. Based on these random phases, we introduce the two-dimensional randomized vector-valued Weierstrass function

f_{\Theta,\Lambda}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
as follows:

f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(t)=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{n}\cos\bigl(2\pi(b^{n}t+\theta_{n})\bigr),\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{n}\sin\bigl(2\pi(b^{n}t+\lambda_{n})\bigr)\right),

(3)

where
0<a<1<b,\ ab>1
. This function serves as a complex extension and randomized generalization of the classical one-dimensional Weierstrass function. Its graph is a typical self-affine fractal set in three-dimensional space, which combines randomness, self-affinity, and nowhere differentiability, making it an ideal object for testing fractal dimension theory.

For the above random fractal function, the main dimension result is stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f_{\Theta,\Lambda}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the random vector-valued Weierstrass function defined in (
3
). If the parameters satisfy

\beta:=-\frac{\log a}{\log b}\in\Bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\Bigr),

then for almost all random phase sequences
\Theta
and
\Lambda
, the Hausdorff dimension of the graph satisfies

\dim_{H}G(f_{\Theta,\Lambda})=\dim_{B}G(f_{\Theta,\Lambda})=3-2\beta.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

By the assumption that
\beta=-\log a/\log b\in(0,1/2)
, we have
a=b^{-\beta}
, so the function in (
3
) can be written as

f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(t)=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b^{-\beta n}\cos 2\pi(b^{n}t+\theta_{n}),\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b^{-\beta n}\sin 2\pi(b^{n}t+\lambda_{n})\right).

Lemma 2.1
.

f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(t)
is
\beta
-Hölder continuous on the interval
[0,1]
.

Proof.

For any
x,y\in[0,1],x\neq y
,

\displaystyle|f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(x)-f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b^{-\beta n}\left|\cos\left(2\pi\left(b^{n}x+\theta_{n}\right)\right)-\cos\left(2\pi\left(b^{n}y+\theta_{n}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b^{-\beta n}\left|\sin\left(2\pi\left(b^{n}x+\lambda_{n}\right)\right)-\sin\left(2\pi\left(b^{n}y+\lambda_{n}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b^{-\beta n}\min\left\{2,2\pi b^{n}|x-y|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}2\pi b^{(1-\beta)n}|x-y|+2\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}2b^{-\beta n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\frac{b^{(1-\beta)m}-1}{b^{1-\beta}-1}|x-y|+4\frac{b^{-\beta m}}{1-b^{-\beta}}

for all
m>0
. Let
m
be the positive integer satisfying
b^{-m}<|x-y|\leq b^{-m+1}
. It follows that

\displaystyle|f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(x)-f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\pi\frac{(b/|x-y|)^{1-\beta}}{b^{1-\beta}-1}|x-y|+4\frac{|x-y|^{\beta}}{1-b^{-\beta}}

(4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4\pi b^{1-\beta}}{b^{1-\beta}-1}+\frac{4}{1-b^{-\beta}}\right)|x-y|^{\beta}.

∎

The following result is straightforward, we provide a proof here for completeness.

Lemma 2.2
.

\dim_{H}G(f_{\Theta,\Lambda})\leq 3-2\beta.

Proof.

Take an integer
m>1
and divide
[0,1]
into
m
subintervals of equal length. Let
J
be one of the subintervals, Hölder continuity implies that

\sup_{x,y\in J}|f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(x)-f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(y)|\leq Cm^{-\beta}

where
C
is the coefficient in (
4
). The graph of the function on
J
can be covered by a cuboid of size

m^{-1}\times Cm^{-\beta}\times Cm^{-\beta}
, and hence by at most
(Cm^{1-\beta}+1)^{2}
cubes of side length
1/m
. Thus the whole graph
G(f_{\Theta,\Lambda})
over
[0,1]
can be covered by at most
m\bigl(Cm^{1-\beta}+1\bigr)^{2}=m^{3-2\beta}\bigl(C+m^{\beta-1}\bigr)^{2}

cubes of side length
1/m
. Therefore

\dim_{H}G(f_{\Theta,\Lambda})\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}G(f_{\Theta,\Lambda})\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{\log\bigl(m^{3-2\beta}(C+m^{\beta-1})^{2}\bigr)}{-\log(1/m)}=3-2\beta

where
\overline{\dim}_{B}
denotes the upper box dimension.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{L}}
be the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
. It can induce a measure
\mu_{\Theta,\Lambda}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
through
f_{\Theta,\Lambda}
in the following way: for any Borel set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, define

\mu_{\Theta,\Lambda}(V)={\mathcal{L}}\bigl(\{t\in[0,1]:(t,f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(t))\in V\}\bigr),

which is supported on the graph of
f_{\Theta,\Lambda}
. For
s>0
, the
s
-energy of
\mu_{\Theta,\Lambda}

[
5
]
is

\displaystyle I_{s}(\mu_{\Theta,\Lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{d\mu_{\Theta,\Lambda}(a)\,d\mu_{\Theta,\Lambda}(b)}{|a-b|^{s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\!\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dxdy}{\bigl((x-y)^{2}+|f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(x)-f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(y)|^{2}\bigr)^{s/2}}.

We will prove that for any
s\in(2,3-2\beta)
, the
s
-energy
I_{s}(\mu_{\Theta,\Lambda})
is finite for almost every pair of phase sequences
(\Theta,\Lambda)\in H\times H
. This implies that the Hausdorff dimension of the graph of
f_{\Theta,\Lambda}
is at least
s
. By letting
s\to 3-2\beta
, we conclude that

\dim_{H}G(f_{\Theta,\Lambda})\geq 3-2\beta

holds for almost every
(\Theta,\Lambda)
. Hence the desired result follows by Lemma
2.2
.

Given an
s\in(2,3-2\beta)
, to show
I_{s}(\mu_{\Theta,\Lambda})<\infty
a.e.-
(\Theta,\Lambda)
, it suffices to prove that the expectation
\mathbb{E}[I_{s}(\mu_{\Theta,\Lambda})]<\infty
. By Fubini-Tonelli theorem, the expectation can be represented as

\mathbb{E}\bigl[I_{s}(\mu_{\Theta,\Lambda})\bigr]=\int_{0}^{1}\!\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{\bigl((x-y)^{2}+|f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(x)-f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(y)|^{2}\bigr)^{s/2}}\right)dxdy.

We claim that there exists
C_{0}>0
such that for
0<|x-y|\leq 1/(2b^{2})
,

\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{\bigl((x-y)^{2}+|f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(x)-f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(y)|^{2}\bigr)^{s/2}}\right)\leq C_{0}|x-y|^{2-s-2\beta}.

Since
2<s<3-2\beta
, it concludes that
\mathbb{E}\bigl[I_{s}(\mu_{\Theta,\Lambda})\bigr]<\infty
.

Fix
x,y\in[0,1]
satisfying
0<|x-y|\leq 1/(2b^{2})
, and define

X=|f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(x)-f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(y)|^{2}.

Regarding
X
as a random variable depending on
(\Theta,\Lambda)
, it can be verified that
X
has a bounded probability density function
h
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{\bigl((x-y)^{2}+|f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(x)-f_{\Theta,\Lambda}(y)|^{2}\bigr)^{s/2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(t)\,dt}{\bigl((x-y)^{2}+t\bigr)^{s/2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h\bigl(|x-y|^{2}\tau\bigr)|x-y|^{2}d\tau}{|x-y|^{s}(1+\tau)^{s/2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl(\sup_{t\geq 0}h(t)\Bigr)\,|x-y|^{2-s}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\tau}{(1+\tau)^{s/2}}.

Hence to prove the claim, it suffices to show that there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
, independent of
x,y
, such that

h(t)\leq C_{1}|x-y|^{-2\beta}.

(5)

By trigonometric identities, we decompose
X
into two parts:

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b^{-\beta n}\bigl[\cos(2\pi(b^{n}x+\theta_{n}))-\cos(2\pi(b^{n}y+\theta_{n}))\bigr]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b^{-\beta n}\bigl[\sin(2\pi(b^{n}x+\lambda_{n}))-\sin(2\pi(b^{n}y+\lambda_{n}))\bigr]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2},

where

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2b^{-\beta n}\sin\Bigl(2\pi b^{n}\frac{x+y}{2}+2\pi\theta_{n}\Bigr)\sin\Bigl(2\pi b^{n}\frac{y-x}{2}\Bigr),

\displaystyle X_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2b^{-\beta n}\sin\Bigl(2\pi b^{n}\frac{x-y}{2}\Bigr)\cos\Bigl(2\pi b^{n}\frac{x+y}{2}+2\pi\lambda_{n}\Bigr).

Let
Z_{1,n}=b_{n}\sin(r_{1,n}+2\pi\theta_{n})
, where

b_{n}=2b^{-\beta n}\sin\Bigl(2\pi b^{n}\frac{y-x}{2}\Bigr),\quad r_{1,n}=\pi b^{n}(x+y).

Then
X_{1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Z_{1,n}
, and
b_{n},r_{1,n}
are deterministic. Since
\theta_{n}\sim\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]
are i.i.d., each
Z_{1,n}
has density function

h_{n}(z)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\pi\sqrt{b_{n}^{2}-z^{2}}},&|z|<|b_{n}|,\\
0,&|z|\geq|b_{n}|.\end{cases}

The density function of
X_{1}
is the infinite convolution
h_{X_{1}}=h_{0}*h_{1}*h_{2}*\cdots
. Since the supremum of a probability density is nonincreasing under convolution, it follows that

\sup_{t}h_{X_{1}}(t)\leq\sup_{t}\bigl(h_{k-2}*h_{k-1}*h_{k}\bigr)(t)

for any fixed
k\geq 2
.

Choose an integer
k
such that
\frac{1}{2b^{k+1}}<|x-y|<\frac{1}{2b^{k}}
. Then

\frac{\pi}{2b^{3}}<\Bigl|2\pi b^{k-2}\cdot\frac{y-x}{2}\Bigr|\leq\Bigl|2\pi b^{k}\cdot\frac{y-x}{2}\Bigr|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}.

So for
n=k-2,k-1,k
,

|b_{n}|>2\sin\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{2b^{3}}\Bigr)b^{-k\beta}>2^{1+\beta}\sin\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{2b^{3}}\Bigr)|x-y|^{\beta}.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{p}
denote the
L^{p}
-norm. For
p=3/2
,

\displaystyle\|h_{n}\|_{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{-|b_{n}|}^{|b_{n}|}\frac{dz}{\bigl(\pi\sqrt{b_{n}^{2}-z^{2}}\bigr)^{3/2}}\right)^{2/3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{-1}\left(\int_{-|b_{n}|}^{|b_{n}|}(b_{n}^{2}-z^{2})^{-3/4}dz\right)^{2/3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{-1}I^{2/3}|b_{n}|^{-1/3},

where
I=\int_{-1}^{1}(1-u^{2})^{-3/4}du<\infty
is a universal constant. Thus for
n=k-2,k-1,k
,

\|h_{n}\|_{\frac{3}{2}}\leq C_{2}|x-y|^{-\beta/3}

where
C_{2}
depends only on
b
. By Young’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\bigl(h_{k-2}*h_{k-1}*h_{k}\bigr)(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|h_{k-2}\|_{\frac{3}{2}}\|h_{k-1}*h_{k}\|_{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|h_{k-2}\|_{\frac{3}{2}}\|h_{k-1}\|_{\frac{3}{2}}\|h_{k}\|_{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{3}|x-y|^{-\beta},

where
C_{3}:=(C_{2})^{3}
. Hence
h_{X_{i}}(t)\leq C_{3}|x-y|^{-\beta}
. The same bound holds for
h_{X_{2}}
.

Finally, we compute the density function of
X=X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}
. For
i=1,2
, the density of
X_{i}^{2}
is

h_{X_{i}^{2}}(t)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{h_{X_{i}}(\sqrt{t})+h_{X_{i}}(-\sqrt{t})}{2\sqrt{t}},&t\geq 0,\\
0,&t<0.\end{cases}

Since
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are independent, the density function of
X
satisfies

\displaystyle h(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigl(h_{X_{1}^{2}}*h_{X_{2}^{2}}\bigr)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{h_{X_{1}}(\sqrt{t-s})+h_{X_{1}}(-\sqrt{t-s})}{2\sqrt{t-s}}\cdot\frac{h_{X_{2}}(\sqrt{s})+h_{X_{2}}(-\sqrt{s})}{2\sqrt{s}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle({C_{3}})^{2}|x-y|^{-2\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{s(t-s)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi({C_{3}})^{2}|x-y|^{-2\beta}.

By letting
C_{1}:=\pi({C_{3}})^{2}
, we complete the proof of (
5
).
∎
