Rare case of gastric submucosal elevation
Authors: Yuexian Li, Xiaozhan Chen, Zhongqiang Ma, Dongxu Wang, Yi Wang
Published: 2026

Quiz

A 52-year-old woman presented with epigastric pain that had persisted for several years. Physical examination revealed no abdominal abnormalities, and routine blood tests (including complete blood count, liver function tests, and tumor markers) yielded normal results. Gastroscopy identified a 1.0-cm “doughnut-shaped” submucosal elevation on the greater curvature of the mid-body of the stomach, with a smooth surface (Fig. 1A). Endoscopic ultrasonography (EUS) miniprobe demonstrated that the lesion originated from the submucosal layer, showing mixed hyperechoic and hypoechoic signals with a tubular hypoechoic structure in the center (Fig. 1B). Histopathological examination of the biopsy specimens revealed fundic gland polyps. Subsequent abdominal computed tomography (CT) revealed a nodular high-density lesion on the inner wall of the stomach, suggesting calcification or ossification (Fig. 1C).

(A) Gastroscopic image showing a “doughnut-shaped” submucosal elevation on the greater curvature of the mid-body of the stomach. (B) Endoscopic ultrasonography miniprobe image revealing mixed hyperechoic and hypoechoic signals in the submucosal layer with a central tubular hypoechoic structure. (C) Abdominal computed tomography scan demonstrating a nodular high-density lesion (arrow) on the gastric wall.

Please answer the following four questions based on the aforementioned information: (1) What is the most likely diagnosis?; (2) Which method should be adopted in this case to achieve a definitive diagnosis and curative treatment?; (3) In the present case, the final pathological diagnosis was a fundic gland polyp with osseous metaplasia. Which of the following statements regarding gastric osseous metaplasia is the most accurate? (4) Why did abdominal CT rather than EUS raise suspicion of osseous metaplasia or calcification in this case?

Answer

Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) was performed for definitive diagnosis and curative treatment. The lesion was completely excised en bloc under endoscopic guidance, and the resection margins were negative (Fig. 2A). Histopathological examination of the ESD specimen revealed a fundic gland polyp with ossification, characterized by mature bone tissue within the submucosal layer (Fig. 2B).

(A) Endoscopic image after complete resection of the lesion by endoscopic submucosal dissection. (B) Histopathological section showing fundic gland polyp tissue with mature bone tissue (arrow) in the submucosal layer (hematoxylin–eosin staining, ×100).

Osseous metaplasia is a rare phenomenon in the gastrointestinal tract, with most reported cases involving colorectal polyps and carcinomas.1 Among gastric lesions, only sporadic case reports have been documented. The literature has reported osseous metaplasia in gastric hyperplastic polyps,2 gastric adenomas,3 and gastric carcinomas4 to date. It is critical to distinguish osseous metaplasia from dystrophic calcification. Osseous metaplasia is an active process involving differentiation of pluripotent stromal cells into osteoblasts, leading to formation of mature bone tissue (including trabeculae and osteocytes), whereas dystrophic calcification is passive calcium deposition in damaged or necrotic tissues without bone cell differentiation or structured bone formation. The presence of mature bone trabeculae in the ESD specimen confirmed the diagnosis of osseous metaplasia.

However, the exact mechanisms underlying bone formation in gastric lesions remain unclear. In this case, the initial biopsy suggested a fundic gland polyp; however, the EUS and CT findings prompted further investigation. We selected ESD over endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) for three key reasons. First, the lesion originated from the submucosal layer and contained suspected ossified components, which are difficult to resect en bloc via EMR. Second, the rare nature of osseous metaplasia requires an intact specimen for definitive pathological confirmation, which ESD uniquely provides. Third, en bloc resection via ESD minimizes the risk of residual lesions and recurrence, a critical consideration for lesions with unclear long-term clinical significance. Consistent with a previous literature report,5 ESD played an important role in this case, as it allowed complete resection of the lesion and pathological confirmation of ossification.

To the best of our knowledge, this case adds to the limited number of reports on fundic gland polyps with osseous metaplasia. ESD is an effective and minimally invasive treatment for submucosal lesions, enabling complete resection and definitive pathological diagnosis. Clinicians should consider this rare histological variant when evaluating gastric submucosal lesions, particularly when imaging findings suggest calcification or high-density changes.
