Knowledge-guided multimodal reasoning framework for intelligent power grid maintenance
Authors: Yi Yang, Jingguo Ren, Zihan Liu, Yalong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPower grid infrastructure maintenance requires reliable integration of heterogeneous data sources including historical fault records, technical documents, and visual inspection imagery. Existing approaches face challenges in ensuring output completeness, consistency verification, and systematic multimodal data integration—critical requirements for high-stakes operational decisions. This paper presents an integrated multimodal reasoning system designed to address these reliability requirements through systematic quality assurance mechanisms. The system architecture comprises three components: (1) a document processing pipeline constructing a structured knowledge base from 10,247 heterogeneous maintenance records (8,198 for training, remaining for validation and testing); (2) a retrieval-augmented reasoning module implementing a Recursive Augmented Thinking (RAT) mechanism with three-dimensional self-evaluation (slot completeness, confidence assessment, logical consistency verification) tailored to power grid fault diagnosis requirements; (3) an attention-enhanced visual detection module for identifying equipment anomalies. Validation on 120 real-world fault cases demonstrates that the RAT mechanism achieves 95% output completeness and 0.82 confidence score through iterative refinement (approximately 98% convergence within 3 iterations), compared to 65% completeness and 0.55 confidence for single-pass generation. Visual detection on 15,648 inspection images achieves 0.952 mAP@.5 with maintained robustness under challenging conditions (87.4% performance retention under occlusion, distance, and adverse weather). Introduction

Power grid infrastructure maintenance presents significant operational challenges due to the critical nature of electrical equipment and the severe consequences of failures. Unplanned outages cost approximately $150 billion annually in the U.S.1, while equipment degradation poses ongoing safety risks2. Maintenance decision-making currently faces three operational challenges: (1) dependence on expert diagnosis that is time-intensive and subject to inter-operator variability3; (2) fragmented data sources—historical fault records, technical specifications, visual inspection imagery—processed through isolated workflows that lack systematic integration; (3) static knowledge systems requiring manual reconfiguration as operational standards evolve, creating potential gaps between documented procedures and current requirements.

Recent advances in natural language processing and computer vision provide technical capabilities for processing heterogeneous industrial data4,5. Large language models (LLMs), combined with retrieval-augmented generation architectures, offer potential solutions for integrating textual and visual information streams6–8.

Motivation for retrieval-augmented language models

Retrieval-augmented LLMs offer four practical capabilities for grid maintenance: (1) natural-language processing of heterogeneous formats (reports, manuals, images) without custom integration pipelines; (2) external-retrieval-based domain adaptation, avoiding full retraining when standards evolve; (3) structured outputs with intermediate reasoning that supports operator verification; (4) instruction-tuning to new equipment/fault patterns with limited labels.

From a broader cyber-physical reliability perspective, maintenance intelligence must be evaluated by data trustworthiness, communication availability, and attack resilience in addition to prediction quality. Recent CPS reliability studies emphasize FDI mitigation and reliability-aware operation under sensing/communication uncertainty9,10; PMU and synchrophasor research highlights placement, cybersecurity, and communication-degradation impacts on DTR/SIPS workflows11–14; survey-level evidence confirms that ICT integration reshapes power-system reliability boundaries15,16. Complementary engineering studies on grid-tied renewable optimization and learning-based forecasting/control further inform our domain-aware design17–19.

However, applying general-purpose LLMs to high-stakes industrial maintenance contexts requires addressing several operational requirements20,21. Generic models lack domain-specific knowledge about electrical equipment specifications, diagnostic protocols, and safety regulations. Power grid maintenance further requires specific capabilities beyond general language understanding: (1) Output completeness verification—ensuring all mandatory decision-support fields (fault cause, affected components, maintenance actions, risk assessment, reference basis) are populated before operator review; (2) Logical consistency checking—validating that generated recommendations align with domain constraints (e.g., correlating gas analysis patterns with appropriate fault mechanisms); (3) Multimodal information integration—incorporating both textual knowledge and visual inspection evidence for comprehensive diagnostics; (4) Traceability and auditability—providing explicit citations to source documents for expert verification.

Existing retrieval-augmented generation approaches provide external knowledge integration but typically employ single-pass generation without systematic quality assessment22,23. While methods such as Self-Refine24 and CRITIC25 introduce iterative improvement, they lack structured evaluation dimensions tailored to domain-specific requirements (e.g., mandatory field completeness, domain rule consistency). Similarly, visual anomaly detection methods26,27 face challenges with small or distant objects in complex backgrounds, and typically operate in isolation without systematic integration with textual diagnostic knowledge.

Weaknesses in Existing Literature

Prior studies reveal three persistent gaps for operational deployment: (i) fragmented treatment of cyber reliability and maintenance reasoning, where communication/data integrity risks are analyzed separately from diagnostic generation quality; (ii) limited output governance, with incomplete or weakly verifiable structured recommendations in high-stakes settings; (iii) weak coupling between text-centric and vision-centric modules, reducing end-to-end robustness in heterogeneous field conditions.

Motivation

These gaps motivate an integrated system architecture that: (1) constructs a structured domain knowledge base from heterogeneous maintenance records; (2) implements systematic quality assurance through multi-dimensional evaluation tailored to power grid operational requirements; (3) integrates textual reasoning and visual detection capabilities for comprehensive fault diagnosis.

Novelty statement

Unlike prior retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems that adopt single-pass or generic self-refinement loops without domain-tailored quality constraints, and unlike prior power-grid AI works that treat textual reasoning and visual inspection as isolated pipelines, this paper introduces three jointly designed innovations that are, to the best of our knowledge, novel: (i) a domain-specific three-dimensional self-evaluation mechanism (mandatory five-slot completeness, weighted confidence, and rule-based logical consistency) that is explicitly aligned with power-grid operational protocols rather than reused from general-purpose RAG critiques such as Self-Refine and CRITIC; (ii) a quality-triggered hierarchical refinement policy (knowledge gap ≻ low confidence ≻ logical inconsistency) that selectively regenerates only deficient slots, achieving 98% convergence within 3 iterations versus uncontrolled iteration budgets used by Self-Refine; (iii) an end-to-end multimodal integration that couples the same structured knowledge base with both the RAT reasoning module and an ACmix-enhanced YOLOv7 inspection module, supporting joint textual–visual diagnosis under cyber-physical operating conditions including communication degradation and partial sensor availability. Related work

Retrieval-augmented generation and quality assurance

Recent advances include RAG22 for knowledge incorporation, CoT23 for reasoning, Self-Refine24 for iterative improvement, and CRITIC25 for external validation. However, these methods lack systematic multi-dimensional quality assessment tailored for domain-specific structured outputs with operational requirements. For power grid maintenance requiring mandatory information fields and domain rule compliance, this study implements three-dimensional evaluation (field completeness, content quality, logical consistency) within a retrieval-augmented architecture, specifically designed for power grid fault diagnosis with task-specific fine-tuning and iterative quality-assured refinement.

Industrial multimodal diagnostics

Data-driven approaches28–30 have shown success with numerical sensor data and single-modality inputs but lack integration of unstructured textual knowledge and interpretability for high-stakes decisions. Object detection methods31–33 face challenges with small objects in complex power grid scenarios. This work addresses these gaps through ACMix attention mechanisms for small object detection and multimodal fusion of textual knowledge with visual inspection data.

Grid-tied renewable optimization and learning-based forecasting/control

Beyond pure language-model literature, our framework is methodologically informed by recent advances in optimization, control, and learning-based forecasting on the power-engineering side. The PV/inverter ratio optimization review17 demonstrates that structured multi-objective evaluation (efficiency, cost, reliability) leads to more deployable grid-tied systems—a principle that motivates our three-dimensional output evaluation (completeness, confidence, consistency) rather than monolithic loss minimization. The robust variable-step perturb-and-observe sliding-mode controller for grid-connected wind systems18 shows how adaptive step-size mechanisms maintain stability under varying operating conditions; this directly inspires the adaptive iterative refinement in our RAT loop, where refinement intensity adapts to the detected deficiency type (knowledge gap, low confidence, or logical inconsistency) rather than applying a uniform regeneration step. The radial basis function neural network short-term load forecasting study for super high voltage grids19 highlights the importance of domain-tailored feature representations for high-voltage grid intelligence; analogously, our knowledge base is constructed using domain-tailored layout, OCR, and NER modules rather than off-the-shelf pipelines. Collectively, these works confirm that domain-aware structuring—rather than generic deep models—is essential for trustworthy power-grid AI, motivating the structured, quality-assured design of our framework.

Positioning within cyber-physical reliability literature

Recent CPS reliability research provides the broader context for our system. Data integrity / FDI:9 mitigates false data injection on DLR systems, and10 models DLR reliability across sensing, communication, and control layers. We operationalize the same data-integrity philosophy by attaching cross-source provenance tags and consistency scores to every knowledge entry, so that RAT inherits explicit data-trust labels. PMU/synchrophasor reliability:11 shows that flexibly-rated PMU placement improves observability and reliability;12 surveys PMU-CPS cybersecurity exposure;13,14 quantify how synchrophasor-based DTR/SIPS reliability degrades under composite cyber stress and communication unavailability. The shared insight—reasoning outputs are only as reliable as the underlying observability and communication channels—motivates two RAT design choices: (i) the consistency dimension flags outputs depending on degraded sensor families, and (ii) iterative refinement re-retrieves alternative evidence when primary channels are unreliable. Survey-level CPS reliability:15,16 demonstrate that ICT integration reshapes reliability boundaries and demands auditable AI–operations interfaces. Prior CPS reliability work has primarily addressed sensing, communication, and protection layers; we extend the same discipline to the decision-support reasoning layer, closing the gap repeatedly identified in the surveys above.

Weaknesses in prior work and motivation

Synthesizing the above, four concrete weaknesses persist: (W1) Fragmented CPS-reliability and reasoning treatment—CPS reliability work10,13,14 models sensing/communication reliability without translating it into reasoning-layer guarantees, while RAG/Self-Refine22,24 optimize text quality without acknowledging upstream cyber conditions. (W2) Weak output governance—existing RAG and CoT systems do not enforce structured field completeness or domain-rule consistency, producing plausible-but-unusable outputs in high-stakes settings. (W3) Loose text–vision coupling—prior power-grid AI21,26,27 treats detection as isolated from textual knowledge. (W4) Domain-agnostic refinement—generic self-refinement24,25 applies uniform critiques regardless of deficiency type. We close (W1)–(W4) by (i) a provenance-tagged knowledge base bridging sensing/communication to reasoning, (ii) explicit five-slot mandatory-field output governance, (iii) shared knowledge between RAT and ACmix detection, and (iv) a domain-aware hierarchical refinement policy that converges in łe 3 iterations on 98% of cases.

Problem motivation and design rationale before method details

Before detailing each module, we summarize the core design rationale that drives the method: Challenge 1—heterogeneous maintenance records have variable quality and irregular layouts, requiring robust knowledge extraction; Challenge 2—single-pass reasoning often produces incomplete or weakly justified outputs, requiring explicit quality-assured iterative refinement; Challenge 3—small, distant, and occluded visual faults require stronger feature modeling than standard detection pipelines. Therefore, the framework is intentionally partitioned into three coordinated stages: structured knowledge construction, RAT-based quality-assured reasoning, and ACmix-enhanced visual detection.

Stage 1: Structured Knowledge Base Construction - Heterogeneous maintenance documents undergo processing through PP-PicoDet (layout analysis), RARE (text recognition), PMRC (named entity extraction), and MASKER (defect classification). The processing pipeline addresses practical challenges including variable document quality (68% scanned PDFs, 72-600 DPI resolution range), irregular layouts (55% multi-column format), and domain-specific terminology under degraded conditions. Processed information populates a dual-indexed knowledge base: (i) vector database supporting semantic similarity retrieval for contextual case matching; (ii) relational database enabling structured queries for specific equipment types or fault patterns. The resulting knowledge base contains 10,247 processed documents yielding 1,083 structured knowledge entries.

Stage 2: Quality-Assured Strategy Generation - The RAT-enhanced reasoning module implements four sequential steps addressing operational quality requirements: (1) Initial retrieval via semantic similarity matching and keyword filtering from the knowledge base; (2) Chain-of-Thought generation using instruction-tuned Baichuan2-13B with retrieved context, producing structured outputs with intermediate reasoning steps; (3) Three-dimensional quality evaluation assessing mandatory field completeness, content quality confidence, and logical consistency against domain rules; (4) Targeted iterative refinement (up to 3 iterations) addressing identified deficiencies through re-retrieval and regeneration. This design ensures outputs meet completeness and consistency requirements before operator review. The 19.4s average inference time (or 8.3s in fast mode) is suitable for maintenance planning scenarios where decision timescales span minutes to hours.

Stage 3: Visual Detection - ACmix-enhanced YOLOv7 processes inspection imagery, employing hybrid attention mechanisms combining convolutional local feature extraction and self-attention global context modeling to address small-scale and distant defect detection challenges. The visual detection module operates independently but can be used in conjunction with the textual reasoning module for comprehensive fault diagnosis when both text-based case information and visual inspection data are available. All three stages access the centralized knowledge base, ensuring consistent application of domain knowledge across the integrated framework.

Construction of the inspection knowledge base for power grid equipment

The knowledge base framework (Fig. 2) addresses three challenges: multi-format documents with varying quality, irregular text layouts, and domain-specific terminology under degraded conditions. The processing flow (preprocessing → layout parsing → OCR → entity extraction) populates the knowledge base consumed by Stages 2 and 3.

Knowledge base construction workflow: the process involves data preprocessing, document layout analysis using PP-PicoDet, text recognition with RARE, and named entity recognition through PMRC. These steps collectively enable the creation of a high-density inspection knowledge base.

PP-PicoDet34 employs CSP-PAN feature fusion with SimOTA assignment for multi-scale detection, enhanced through: (i) adaptive resolution processing for low-resolution scans, (ii) multi-orientation detection for rotated tables, (iii) hierarchical layout parsing for nested structures. This achieves 94.3% layout accuracy.

RARE35 performs robust text recognition via STN (geometric rectification for distortions, curved text, irregular spacing) and SRN (domain-adaptive features, multi-scale attention, noise-robust encoding), achieving 91.7% character-level accuracy versus 78.3% for generic OCR.

PMRC36 extracts defect-related keywords through adaptive prompt templates handling: multi-template ensemble (formal/colloquial/abbreviated notations), synonym expansion (equipment aliases), and context-aware disambiguation. This achieves 88.6% F1-score across 15+ document types versus 71.4% for standard NER.

MASKER37 categorizes defects via contextual masked reconstruction with: hierarchical defect taxonomy, multi-granularity context modeling, and temporal pattern recognition. It achieves 92.1% classification accuracy, handling noisy inputs including incomplete descriptions and informal annotations.

Data trustworthiness and FDI resilience

Beyond extraction accuracy, the knowledge base attaches three integrity attributes to every structured entry, motivated by FDI mitigation9 and CPS reliability modelling10: (i) a source-channel tag (sensor telemetry, lab-test report, expert annotation, regulatory document); (ii) a cross-source consistency score computed against parallel mentions in adjacent documents in the MASKER similarity space, flagging entries whose normalized inter-source disagreement exceeds 0.3 as low-trust; (iii) a temporal validity window restricting use of regulatory entries to their valid date range. These attributes are exposed as retrievable metadata and consumed by the RAT confidence dimension, so that low-trust entries are deprioritized during evidence aggregation. While runtime FDI detection is out of scope, this design provides the structural hooks for downstream integration with online FDI classifiers (e.g.,9)—realizing the principle, repeatedly emphasized in CPS reliability surveys15,16, that reasoning quality cannot exceed upstream sensing/communication trustworthiness.

Knowledge-driven strategy generation for power grid inspection

This study proposes a lightweight power domain-specific pretraining strategy using Baichuan2-13B38 as backbone. Pretraining on large-scale datasets containing equipment failure descriptions, regulatory documents, and standards imparts domain knowledge while maintaining general-purpose robustness. This minimizes training cost and improves generalization on downstream tasks.

Instruction-based fine-tuning is then performed using domain-specific instructions including decision-making schemes and fault diagnostics39. Structuring training instances as question-answer pairs enables the model to generalize across unseen inspection-related tasks while maintaining a lightweight computation footprint.

To address operational requirements for systematic quality assurance in retrieval-augmented generation for power grid maintenance, we implement a Recursive Augmented Thinking (RAT) mechanism (Figure 3).

Pipeline of the recursive augmented thinking (RAT) mechanism: The RAT framework integrates Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompts with retrieval-augmented generation and systematic self-evaluation. It operates in three stages: (1) Task-specific CoT generation for fault cause analysis, troubleshooting recommendations, and disposal plans using external LLMs; (2) Three-dimensional self-evaluation assessing slot completeness (), confidence through weighted aggregation (), and logical consistency via LLM verification and domain knowledge rules; (3) Recursive refinement that iteratively improves outputs based on evaluation feedback until quality requirements are met (approximately 98% convergence within 3 iterations). Retrieved historical cases and external domain knowledge are dynamically injected through LlamaIndex, supported by similarity-based retrieval thresholds and refined with custom templates. This integrated pipeline enables robust, contextually relevant, and quality-assured strategy generation tailored to power grid maintenance tasks.

Design rationale: Power grid fault diagnosis requires outputs satisfying specific completeness and consistency requirements before operator review: all mandatory decision-support fields must be populated (fault cause, affected component, maintenance actions, risk assessment, reference basis), and recommendations must align with domain operational rules (e.g., correlating dissolved gas patterns with appropriate fault mechanisms). Single-pass generation frequently produces incomplete outputs requiring manual completion or outputs containing logical inconsistencies requiring expert correction. RAT addresses these limitations through systematic evaluation and targeted refinement: (i) generates initial strategies using retrieved knowledge and chain-of-thought reasoning; (ii) evaluates outputs across three dimensions using domain-specific criteria (mandatory field presence, content quality indicators, logical consistency with domain rules); (iii) performs targeted re-retrieval and regeneration addressing identified deficiencies; (iv) iterates (maximum 3 cycles) until quality requirements are met or iteration limit reached.

The RAT mechanism builds upon traditional RAG by adding three components: (1) Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting for structured output generation with intermediate reasoning steps, (2) three-dimensional quality evaluation systematically assessing outputs against operational requirements, and (3) targeted iterative refinement addressing identified deficiencies. The implementation employs external LLMs to generate task-specific prompts for fault diagnosis and maintenance strategy formulation using LangChain and LlamaIndex frameworks. CoT outputs and retrieved historical case information are organized in unified message format for the integrated pipeline, combining retrieved domain knowledge with step-by-step reasoning to provide both contextual information and logical analysis supporting maintenance decisions.

Robustness under communication degradation and partial sensing

Synchrophasor/PMU schemes degrade under partial communication availability13,14, and PMU placement/cybersecurity exposure further shapes downstream observability11,12. We make the RAT loop explicitly degradation-aware via three mechanisms: (i) Trust-weighted retrieval—retrieval similarity in Eqs. (1)/(2)/(4) is multiplied by the Stage 1 provenance trust score, deprioritizing evidence from temporarily unreliable channels and enforcing the surveyed principle15,16 that reasoning confidence must inherit sensing/communication reliability. (ii) Channel-aware confidence penalty—when a recommendation primarily depends on a flagged-unavailable sensor family (e.g., synchrophasor-based DTR during a known outage), in Eq. (2) is penalized by 0.2 per affected mandatory slot, deliberately driving confidence below 0.65 and triggering RAT to re-retrieve offline-verifiable alternatives (lab analysis, static specifications). (iii) Consistency-rule fallbacks under partial observability—Dimension 3 includes patterns such as “if PMU/synchrophasor evidence is unavailable, disallow conclusions requiring synchrophasor confirmation and prefer offline-verifiable recommendations”, derived from13,14. Together these mechanisms let the system gracefully degrade—rather than silently mis-recommend—under communication degradation, FDI suspicion, or partial sensor availability.

Datasets and experimental setup

Knowledge base dataset

10,247 PDF documents from five provincial power grid companies (2018-2023): fault reports (3,842), maintenance logs (4,156), inspection records (1,523), technical specifications (726), processed into 1,083 structured knowledge entries covering 14 equipment categories and 127 fault types. Documents split 80/10/10 (8,198/1,025/1,024) with temporal stratification to prevent data leakage.

Strategy generation dataset

120 real-world fault cases (2022-2023) with expert-validated references, collected independently from the knowledge base documents for evaluation purposes. Each case includes: (i) input fault description with available diagnostic data; (ii) expert-annotated ground truth covering the five mandatory slots (fault cause, affected component, maintenance actions, risk level, reference basis) and free-form diagnostic reports for NLP metric evaluation. These cases cover diverse equipment types and fault complexities (35% single-component, 28% multi-equipment, 18% cascading, 19% planning scenarios).

Comparative baseline configuration

All comparison methods receive identical conditions to ensure fair comparison: (a) Retrieval: same top-5 retrieved documents from the knowledge base via semantic similarity search; (b) Prompting: structured output prompts explicitly requesting the five mandatory slots (fault cause, affected component, maintenance actions, risk level, reference basis) with Chain-of-Thought reasoning instructions; (c) Evaluation: assessed using both domain-specific metrics (slot completeness, confidence, consistency) and standard NLP metrics (ROUGE, BERTScore, BLEURT) against expert-validated references. Single-pass baselines lack systematic multi-dimensional evaluation and iterative refinement. Note that Baichuan2-13B was specifically fine-tuned on the five-slot output structure (8,198 training documents), while API-based baselines (GPT-4, Claude, etc.) received only prompting instructions without domain-specific training. The reported improvements reflect the combined effects of domain fine-tuning, structured training, and the RAT mechanism’s iterative quality assurance, with ablation studies (Table 3 Section C) isolating RAT’s independent contribution.

Anomaly detection dataset

15,648 annotated images from 23 substations with 14 fault classes and 47,231 bounding boxes (38% small objects <32 pixels). Data split: 70/15/15 train/val/test.

Experimental configuration

NVIDIA A100 GPUs (40GB). Baichuan2-13B: LoRA fine-tuning (rank=8) for 3 epochs on the 8,198 training documents. RAT: FAISS index with top-k=5, confidence threshold =0.65 (selected via grid search on a development set separate from the 120 test cases). YOLOv7+ACmix: 300 epochs with cosine annealing and multi-scale training on 10,953 training images, with hyperparameters validated on the 2,347-image validation set.

Evaluation metrics

(1) Slot completeness: computed via Equation (1) by checking presence of all five mandatory fields in generated outputs; (2) Confidence scores: self-assessed by the system via Equation (2) during inference, reflecting internal quality estimation (note: these are not calibrated against external ground truth but used for triggering refinement); (3) Logical consistency: verified against 10-pattern rule base derived from domain expert knowledge; (4) Content quality: standard NLP metrics (ROUGE, BERTScore, BLEURT) computed against expert-validated reference texts. For fair comparison, all baselines are evaluated using identical metrics, though only RAT employs self-assessment for iterative refinement.

Clarification on the Ablation rows that look numerically close to full RAT

Reviewer feedback pointed out that several rows in Section C of Table 3 appear numerically similar to Full RAT, which may seem inconsistent with the claim that each evaluation dimension is essential. We explicitly clarify the design reason here, because the visual proximity of numbers can be misleading: the three RAT dimensions are by construction non-redundant, and each ablation only collapses the metric corresponding to the disabled dimension, leaving the other two metrics largely untouched. Specifically, (i) the w/o Slot Completeness Check row degrades primarily on Completeness (, -33 pts), while Confidence (0.82→ 0.79) and Consistency () are nearly unchanged because removing the completeness gate does not break the independent confidence-scoring and consistency-rule pipelines—this is the expected behavior, not an artifact. (ii) The w/o Confidence Analysis row leaves Completeness at 97% and Consistency at 95% because the completeness gate is still actively populating mandatory slots and the rule-based consistency checks still fire; only the confidence dimension is meaningfully removed (shown as “—”). (iii) The w/o Consistency Verification row preserves Completeness (98%) and Confidence (0.81) but collapses Consistency from 96% to 74% (-22 pts), confirming that the consistency dimension is independently responsible for the consistency metric. (iv) Only the w/o Recursive Refinement row degrades all three metrics simultaneously (, 0.82→ 0.61, ), which is consistent with the fact that recursive refinement is the global mechanism that enforces all three evaluation dimensions jointly. In other words, the apparent “baseline-like” values in some ablation rows are not contradictions but are diagnostic of dimension-wise orthogonality: each ablation surgically removes one capability, and the metric tied to that capability is the one that drops sharply, while the other two remain close to Full RAT.

Cross-domain transfer (e.g., chemical or manufacturing) requires re-designing slot structures, re-curating consistency rules, and collecting domain-specific data with expert validation.

System performance within validated scope

Within power-grid maintenance, the system achieves 95% output completeness, 0.82 confidence, and convergence (118/120 cases) within 3 iterations; visual detection reaches 0.847 small-object mAP and retains 87.4% of normal-condition performance under occlusion/distance/lighting/weather. Resource usage is moderate (39.1 GB memory, 19.4 s inference), suitable for non-emergency scenarios. Outputs are traceable via per-slot citations to structured knowledge entries.

Generalizability beyond power grids remains unvalidated; cross-domain deployment requires slot-structure redesign, recalibration of evaluation criteria/rules, domain-specific training data, and expert validation studies.

Cyber-physical reliability implications

The framework should be read as an operational reasoning layer that extends established CPS reliability disciplines to the decision-support layer. (i) Data trustworthiness / FDI resilience: Stage 1’s provenance-aware knowledge base (source-channel tag, cross-source consistency score, temporal validity window) is structurally aligned with9,10; once a sensor stream is flagged compromised, all derived entries are deprioritized in retrieval, preventing the reasoning module from amplifying corrupted upstream data. (ii) Communication-availability awareness: motivated by13,14, the channel-aware confidence penalty and consistency-rule fallbacks (Method) cause the system to re-retrieve offline-verifiable evidence rather than emit a plausible-but-unfounded recommendation when a relied-upon channel is degraded—to our knowledge the first reasoning-layer mechanism propagating communication availability into LLM output gating in this context. (iii) PMU/synchrophasor cybersecurity:11,12 motivate separating PMU- and non-PMU-sourced evidence into trust groups, so that operators can audit afterwards whether a recommendation inherited any PMU-related cybersecurity exposure. (iv) Auditable ICT-integrated operation:15,16 call for auditable AI–operations interfaces; the five-slot mandatory output (cause, component, action, risk, reference) supplies exactly such an audit chain by pairing every recommendation with a citation to a specific structured entry, traceable through the provenance tag back to the originating channel.

Future research directions

Key directions include: (1) Human validation studies comparing system assessments against independent expert judgments to calibrate self-assessment metrics and establish inter-rater reliability, addressing current reliance on internal evaluation criteria; (2) Computational optimization through lightweight evaluation methods and adaptive mechanisms adjusting refinement stringency based on risk factors, reducing the 2.4× overhead for time-sensitive scenarios; (3) Automated knowledge engineering extracting consistency patterns from larger expert-validated corpora to expand coverage beyond the current 10-pattern rule base, addressing rare and novel fault types; (4) Controlled ablation experiments comparing domain-trained baselines with and without RAT to more precisely quantify RAT’s isolated contribution; (5) Long-term deployment studies tracking operator acceptance, intervention rates, and decision quality in operational environments to validate practical utility beyond controlled test conditions.

Practical recommendations

Based on the validated experimental scope, we recommend: (1) deploy RAT full mode for planned maintenance and post-event diagnosis where quality assurance is prioritized; (2) use fast mode for time-critical triage, followed by deferred full-mode verification for final reporting; (3) establish periodic expert review cycles to recalibrate confidence thresholds and update consistency rule patterns; (4) expand knowledge base coverage for rare fault types through structured expert curation; (5) integrate communication-health monitoring to flag reduced trust in upstream sensing/telemetry during strategy generation.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a knowledge-guided multimodal reasoning framework for intelligent power-grid maintenance that integrates a provenance-aware structured knowledge base, the Recursive Augmented Thinking (RAT) reasoning module with three-dimensional quality evaluation, and an ACmix-enhanced YOLOv7 visual detector under a unified cyber-physical reliability perspective. The three-dimensional evaluation (mandatory five-slot completeness, weighted confidence with 0.4/0.3/0.3 aggregation, rule-based logical consistency) and the hierarchical refinement policy distinguish our work from generic single-pass RAG and free-form self-refinement methods.

Quantitative improvements (numerical, vs. baselines)

On 120 expert-validated fault cases, the proposed system achieves the following measurable gains: (i) output completeness 95% vs. 65% for vanilla RAG (+30 pts, +46% relative) and vs. 71% for RAG+CoT (+24 pts, +34% relative); (ii) self-evaluated confidence 0.82 vs. 0.55 for vanilla RAG (+0.27, +49% relative) and vs. 0.65 for Self-Refine (+0.17, +26% relative); (iii) logical consistency 93%+ vs. 70%–82% for the four RAG variants (+11 to +23 pts); (iv) ROUGE-1 F1 0.859, BERTScore F1 0.981, and BLEURT 0.610 vs. the best API baseline (Chat4o-mini: ROUGE-1 F1 0.704, BERTScore F1 0.938, BLEURT 0.554), yielding relative improvements of +22.0%, +4.6%, and +10.1% respectively; (v) approximately 98% convergence rate (118/120 cases) within 3 iterations, with 78.3% of cases converging by iteration 2. On the 2, 347-image visual test set, the ACmix-enhanced YOLOv7 reaches mAP@.5 0.952 vs. 0.921 baseline (+3.1 pts), small-object mAP 0.847 vs. 0.731 (+11.6 pts), and retains 87.4% of normal-condition performance under occlusion/distance/lighting/weather, outperforming YOLOv8 by +4.4 pts and DINO-5scale by +2.0 pts on the averaged challenging-condition mAP.

Quantitative limitations

The reported gains are accompanied by three measurable costs: (i) RAT incurs a 2.4× computation overhead (19.4 s vs. 8.0 s vanilla RAG), partially mitigated by a fast mode of 8.3 s with a graceful confidence drop of 0.82→ 0.74; (ii) the cross-LLM gain of RAT varies between +19% and +25% across five backbones, indicating residual dependence on base-model capability; (iii) 2/120 cases (1.7%) failed to converge within the iteration budget, concentrated in rare fault types outside the current 10-pattern rule base.

Concrete future directions

Three quantitatively scoped directions remain: (a) reducing the 2.4× overhead toward via lightweight evaluators and adaptive iteration budgeting; (b) expanding the consistency rule base from 10 to a target of \ge 50 patterns through automated extraction from larger expert-validated corpora, aiming to push the convergence rate from 98.3% to ; (c) conducting an inter-rater reliability study with \ge 10 domain experts to calibrate the 0.65 confidence threshold against independent human judgment. Together, these results position the proposed framework as a quantitatively validated reasoning layer that extends established cyber-physical reliability disciplines into AI-driven maintenance decision support.
