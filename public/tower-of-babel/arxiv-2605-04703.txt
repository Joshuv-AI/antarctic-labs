Entropy and Distributed Source Coding of Connected Soft Random Geometric Graphs
Oliver Baker and Carl P. Dettmann

Abstract
We consider the distributed compression of Soft Random Geometric Graphs (SRGGs) above the connectivity threshold. We establish the Slepian-Wolf rate region for the SRGG in the setting where there are a finite number of encoders compressing sections of the graph independently. To do so, we prove novel limit theorems and asymptotic equipartition properties for the SRGG and its entropy, which allow us to use random binning techniques for distributed compression.



I
Introduction

In the modern world, devices are more connected than ever. This results in networks becoming larger, increasing the need for research on compression of networks. In particular, in areas of application such as wireless networking, random models of large networks with a spatial embedding have gained popularity
[
8
]
. One popular model for wireless networks is the
Soft Random Geometric Graph
(SRGG). In this model, nodes are connected at random according to a function of their spatial distance
[
12
]
.

Despite their numerous applications, the information theory of these random graphs is not well understood. In recent years there have been a growing number of works that have concentrated on this model or similar ones such as graphons. In
[
10
]
a limit theorem for the entropy of
W
random graphs (which include SRGGs as a special case) was proven. Since then, there have been a number of works attempting to bound or quantify the entropy, such as
[
5
,
2
,
4
]
. Also, the compression of random graphs has gained popularity recently. Models such as sparse Erdős-Rényi graphs
[
3
]
, stochastic block models
[
1
,
14
]
and preferential attachment graphs
[
15
]
have all been considered. Compression of the SRGG (or generalisations) has been studied in
[
6
,
11
,
13
]
.

Crucially, all of these works focus on compression in the centralised setting. That is, we have an encoder that sees the entire graph, and compresses it for a decoder to later decompress. However, in many applications, it is perhaps more realistic to assume that nodes, or blocks of nodes only have access to partial information about the network. In
[
7
]
, the distributed compression of Erdős-Rényi and configuration model graphs is studied, but distributed compression of the SRGG is, to the best of our knowledge, unstudied. In this work, we consider the regime where we have a fixed, but arbitrary number of encoders which have access to different sections of the graph.

Our other source of novelty is the specific regime in which we study the SRGG. In the above works, when the SRGG (or
W
random graph) is made sparse (by which we mean that the expected degree of a node is sublinear in the total number of nodes), it is done by randomly removing a fraction of the edges of a dense ensemble. However, this places less emphasis on the geometry, since the length scale of edges does not scale with the number of nodes. In our work, we study the more commonly considered regime, for example in the work of Penrose
[
12
]
, where the ‘connection range’ of each node is decreasing in the number of nodes. Equivalently, the size of the domain in which we embed the graph is growing as we add more nodes. We make this idea more precise later. To the best of our knowledge, this regime has not yet been studied in an information-theoretic context.

We also note that the SRGG is an example of a
non-standard source
, in that it cannot be written as a string of stationary, ergodic or Markovian symbols. We must therefore employ the ideas of information spectrum theory to study its information theoretic properties. In this work, we contribute the following.

1.

A limit theorem for the entropy rate of the SRGG,

2.

an Asymptotic Equipartition Property (AEP) for the SRGG,

3.

a complete characterisation of the Slepian-Wolf region for the distributed compression of the SRGG.

In Section
II
we formally introduce the model, the information spectrum, and the distributed compression setting. Section
III
presents our main results, and Section
IV
contains proof ideas. The full proofs may be found in the supplemental material. Finally, Section
V
concludes.

II
Model and Problem Formulation

II-A

Soft Random Geometric Graphs

Definition 1

(Soft Random Geometric Graph)
.

Let
Z_{1},...,Z_{n}
be independent, uniformly distributed points in a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with unit volume, a non-empty interior, whose boundary is piecewise smooth, of finite length and has a finite number of corners. The Soft Random Geometric Graph (SRGG)
G_{n}=(V_{n},E_{n})
is the graph with vertex set
V_{n}=[n]:=\{1,2,...,n\}
, and edge set formed by connecting each pair of vertices
i,j\in V_{n}
independently with probability
p_{n}(R_{ij})
, where
R_{ij}:=\|Z_{i}-Z_{j}\|
is the Euclidean distance between nodes
i
and
j
, and
p_{n}:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
is the ‘connection function’. Let
\mathcal{G}_{n}
be the set of all SRGGs on
n
nodes, then
\mathcal{G}_{n}
is called the ensemble of SRGGs.

In this work, we will consider specifically the following form of connection function. Let
\{s(n)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a positive, decreasing sequence of real numbers. We call
s(n)
the
sparsity
of the SRGG. Then, we focus on connection functions
p_{n}(r)=p(r/s(n))
, where
p:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
. One can think of this form of connection function in two equivalent ways. First, we can imagine fixing the domain
\mathcal{K}
, and decreasing the ‘connection range’ of each node, that is, the length scale at which two nodes are likely to be connected is decreasing with
n
. Alternatively, we can imagine instead fixing the connection range, and increasing the size of
\mathcal{K}
with
n
. We note that this is a different regime to the other commonly considered SRGG, where
p_{n}(r)=s(n)p(r)
.

We will require that
1\gg s(n)\gg\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/d}
. This requirement has two interpretations. The first is that this is the regime where the SRGG is connected with high probability as
n\rightarrow\infty

[
12
]
. The second is that the entropy of the labelled graph is asymptotically equivalent to the entropy of the unlabelled graph.

Definition 2

(Entropy of the SRGG)
.

The Shannon Entropy of the SRGG is denoted by
H(\mathcal{G}_{n})
, and is given by

H(\mathcal{G}_{n}):=-\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}}\mathbb{P}\left(G_{n}\right)\log\mathbb{P}\left(G_{n}\right)

(1)

where the logarithm is to base 2, and we take the convention that
0\log 0=0
. Also, if
Z_{n}
is the random vector containing the locations of each node, then the conditional entropy
H(\mathcal{G}_{n}|\mathcal{Z}_{n})=\mathbb{E}[H(\mathcal{G}_{n}|Z_{n})]
is given by

H(\mathcal{G}_{n}|\mathcal{Z}_{n})=\binom{n}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\mathcal{K}}(r)h_{2}(p_{n}(r))dr

(2)

where
f_{\mathcal{K}}
is the probability density of pairwise distances in
\mathcal{K}
, and
h_{2}(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)
is the binary entropy function.

We treat the following as a standing assumption for the remainder of the paper.

Assumption 1
.

The connection function
p_{n}
is given by
p_{n}(r)=p(r/s(n))
, where
p
is a Hölder continuous function, that is, for every
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}^{2}
,

|p(x,y)-p(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|\leq L\|(x,y)-(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\|^{\alpha}

(3)

with
L,\alpha>0
as constants. We assume that, if
d
is the dimension of the space
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2d}h_{2}(p(r))dr<\infty

(4)

and

\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-1}p(r)(1-p(r))\log^{2}\left(\frac{p(r)}{1-p(r)}\right)dr<\infty

(5)

We also define the following integral

h^{*}:=\kappa_{d-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-1}h_{2}(p(r))dr<\infty

(6)

where
\kappa_{d-1}
is the volume of the unit
d-1
ball.

II-B

The Information Spectrum

As briefly mentioned in the introduction, the SRGG is what is known as a ‘non-standard source’. In classical Information Theory, sources are considered to consist of a sequence of i.i.d. symbols, or at the very least, to be stationary and ergodic. However, the SRGG has neither of these properties. Instead, we must consider the graph
G_{n}
in its entirety.
Information spectrum theory
was developed to deal with general sources such as the SRGG. The theory is developed around
spectral entropy rates
. We first define the
limit superior in probability
, and the
limit inferior in probability
. Let
(X_{1},X_{2},...)
be a real-valued sequence of random variables, then

p\mbox{-}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}X_{n}:=\inf\left\{\alpha|\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{n}>\alpha\right)=0\right\}

(7)

p\mbox{-}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}X_{n}:=\sup\left\{\beta|\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{n}<\beta\right)=0\right\}

(8)

If
p\mbox{-}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}X_{n}=p\mbox{-}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}X_{n}=X^{*}
, we say that
p\mbox{-}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}X_{n}=X^{*}
, which is equivalent to
X_{n}\to X^{*}
in probability as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Definition 3

(Spectral Entropy Rates)
.

The spectral sup-entropy of an ensemble of SRGGs
\mathcal{G}_{n}
with sparsity
s(n)
is defined as

\overline{H}(\mathcal{G}_{n})=p\mbox{-}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{d}}\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}\left(G_{n}\right)}

(9)

Similarly, the spectral inf-entropy is defined as

\underline{H}(\mathcal{G}_{n})=p\mbox{-}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{d}}\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}\left(G_{n}\right)}

(10)

These quantities are similar to the spectral entropy rates used in information spectrum theory for general sources
[
9
]
. The difference is in the normalisation. Normalising by the blocklength
\binom{n}{2}
, would give
\overline{H}(\mathcal{G}_{n})=0
. This is not a very interesting result, since the sparsity induced by
s(n)
means the graph has a vanishing density, and so the majority of edges are absent in the
n\to\infty
limit. Our definition may be thought of as an ‘effective’ spectral entropy rate, capturing the leading order behaviour of compression length, rather than simply dividing through by the number of symbols in the source. It is simple to check that these quantities retain their operational meaning after replacing the standard scaling of
1/n
by
1/(\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{d})
.

III
Main Results

We first establish the following limit theorem for the entropy of the SRGG.

Theorem 1

(Limit Theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{n}
be the ensemble of SRGGs with connection function
p_{n}(r)=p(r/s(n))
. Then,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{H(\mathcal{G}_{n})}{\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{d}}=h^{*}

(11)

The next result strengthens the above to an AEP. Instead of dealing with the entropy, we look at the spectral entropy rates. The result shows that the spectral sup-entropy rate is equal to the spectral inf-entropy rate.

Theorem 2

(Asymptotic Equipartition Property)
.

If
G_{n}
is a random SRGG sampled from
\mathcal{G}_{n}
,

p\mbox{-}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{d}}\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}\left(G_{n}\right)}=h^{*}

(12)

In other words,

\overline{H}(\mathcal{G}_{n})=\underline{H}(\mathcal{G}_{n})=h^{*}

(13)

This implies the existence of sets defined for all
\epsilon>0
,

\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\epsilon}:=\left\{G_{n}:\left|\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}\left(G_{n}\right)}-H(\mathcal{G}_{n})\right|\leq\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{d}\epsilon\right\}

(14)

such that

1.

\mathbb{P}\left(G_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\epsilon}\right)\to 1
as
n\rightarrow\infty

2.

For each
G_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\epsilon}
,
2^{-\binom{n}{2}(s(n)^{d}h^{*}+\epsilon)}\leq\mathbb{P}\left(G_{n}\right)\leq 2^{-\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{d}(h^{*}-\epsilon)}

3.

For every
\delta>0
, there exists an
n^{*}
so that for all
n>n^{*}
, we have

(1-\delta)2^{\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{d}(h^{*}-\epsilon)}\leq|\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\epsilon}|\leq 2^{\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{d}(h^{*}+\epsilon)}

(15)

We now introduce the distributed compression scheme for the SRGG. For each node
i\in[n]
, let
N_{i}
be a
\{0,1\}^{(n-1)}
-valued random vector representing the neighbourhood of
i
in
G_{n}
. That is,
N_{i}=(X_{ij})_{j\neq i}
, where
X_{ij}=1
if
i
and
j
share an edge, and is
0
otherwise. The distributed compression problem is formulated as follows. Let
L\in\mathbb{N}
be a constant. We partition the graph
G_{n}
into
L
‘blocks’, given by

B^{(n)}_{l}=\left\{N_{i}:\frac{(l-1)n}{L}<i\leq\frac{ln}{L}\right\}

(16)

for
l=1,...,L
. Denote by
\mathcal{B}_{l}^{(n)}
the set of all realisations of
B^{(n)}_{l}
for each
l
. The distributed compression problem asks whether there exists a sequence of encoders
\{(\phi_{1}^{(n)},...,\phi_{L}^{(n)})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, where
\phi_{l}^{(n)}:\mathcal{B}_{l}^{(n)}\to\mathcal{M}_{l}^{(n)}
, and a sequence of decoders
\{\psi^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
\psi^{(n)}:\prod_{l=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{l}^{(n)}\to\mathcal{G}_{n}
such that

P_{E}^{(n)}:=\mathbb{P}\left(\psi^{(n)}(\phi_{1}^{(n)}(B^{(n)}_{1}),...,\phi_{L}^{(n)}(B^{(n)}_{L}))\neq G_{n}\right)\to 0

(17)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. We say an
L
-tuple of rates
(R_{1},...,R_{L})\in[0,\infty)^{L}
is
achievable
if

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{L}{n(n-1)s(n)^{d}}\log|\mathcal{M}_{l}^{(n)}|\leq R_{l}

(18)

for each
l=1,...,n
, and
P_{E}^{(n)}\to 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. For this case, we have the following result

Theorem 3

(Distributed Compression Rate Region)
.

In the distributed compression scheme as described above, a rate tuple
(R_{1},...,R_{L})
is achievable if and only if

\sum_{l\in\Lambda}R_{l}\geq\frac{|\Lambda|}{L}\frac{h^{*}}{2}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{s\left(\frac{|\Lambda|n}{L}\right)^{d}}{s(n)^{d}}

(19)

for every
\Lambda\subseteq[L]
.

Remark 1
.

In particular, when
\Lambda=[L]
, we require

\sum_{l=1}^{L}R_{l}\geq\frac{h^{*}}{2}.

(20)

The factor of
1/2
comes from the fact that the information about each edge is stored twice overall. To see this, note that the variable
X_{12}
is in both
N_{1}
, and
N_{2}
.

Proof Ideas

IV-A

Theorem
1

The result is a combination of two claims. The first is that
H(\mathcal{G}_{n})=H(\mathcal{G}_{n}|\mathcal{Z}_{n})+O(n\log n)

into

M
is the random vector collecting these labels, which has entropy
H(M)\leq nd\log m

H(\mathcal{G}_{n}|\mathcal{Z}_{n})\leq H(\mathcal{G}_{n})\leq H(\mathcal{G}_{n}|M)+H(M)

(21)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{G}_{n})-H(\mathcal{G}_{n}|\mathcal{Z}_{n})\leq nd\log m+H(\mathcal{G}_{n}|M)-H(\mathcal{G}_{n}|\mathcal{Z}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq nd\log m+\binom{n}{2}(H(X_{12}|M)-H(X_{12}|\mathcal{Z}_{n}))

(22)

Then, letting
m
grow with
n
, the aim is to show that the second term is
o(s(n)^{d})
.
H(X_{12}|M)
is the entropy of an edge existing with respect to an ‘averaged’ version of

, and so using the Hölder continuity of

it can be shown that

H(X_{ij}|M)-H(X_{ij}|\mathcal{Z}_{n})\leq(1+o(1))C\frac{\log(s(n)m)}{s(n)^{\alpha}m^{\alpha}}.

(23)

for some
C>0
. This means that we can choose any
m=m(n)
such that
\log(m)\ll ns(n)^{d}
and
ms(n)^{(d+\alpha)/\alpha}\gg 1
to get

\frac{H(\mathcal{G}_{n})-H(\mathcal{G}_{n}|\mathcal{Z}_{n})}{\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{d}}\leq\frac{nd\log m}{\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{d}}+(1+o(1))C\frac{\log(s(n)m)}{s(n)^{d+\alpha}m^{\alpha}}

which goes to 0 as

. To complete the result, we use Theorem 1 from
[
2
]
. Under the assumption (

), this gives

\frac{H(\mathcal{G}_{n}|\mathcal{Z}_{n})}{\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{d}}\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\to}h^{*}

(25)

Combining with the above argument yields the result. ∎

Theorem

\displaystyle\frac{\text{Var}(\log\mathbb{P}\left(G_{n}|Z_{n}\right))}{\binom{n}{2}^{2}s(n)^{2d}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{Var}(X_{12}\log\mathbb{P}\left(X_{12}|Z_{n}\right)+(1-X_{ij})\log(1-\mathbb{P}(X_{12}|Z_{n})))}{\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{2d}}

Let

s(n)^{-d}\binom{n}{2}^{-1}\left(-\log\mathbb{P}\left(G_{n}|Z_{n}\right)-H(\mathcal{G}_{n}|\mathcal{Z}_{n})\right)\to 0

By Theorem

the first term is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|-\log

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(G_{n}\right)-H(\mathcal{G}_{n}|\mathcal{Z}_{n})|>\binom{n}{2}s(n)^{d}\epsilon)

After establishing the above Lemma

, which we define as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\epsilon}(B^{(n)}_{\Lambda}):=\Bigg\{(B_{\Lambda}^{(n)},B_{\Lambda^{c}}^{(n)}):

\displaystyle\Bigg|\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}\left(B_{\Lambda}^{(n)}|B_{\Lambda^{c}}^{n}\right)}-h^{*}\Bigg|<{\binom{\frac{|\Lambda|n}{L}}{2}s\left(\frac{\Lambda n}{L}\right)^{d}\epsilon}\Bigg\}

(41)

whose existence is guaranteed by the second part of Lemma
2
by taking
S_{n}=\{i:i\in\{\frac{(l-1)n}{L},...,\frac{ln}{L}\text{for some }l\in\Lambda\}\}
.
With this, we can apply the standard random binning argument, which we sketch here. Let
L
be a fixed integer, then we design the codebook as follows for an
L
-tuple of rates
(R_{1},...,R_{L})
. For each
l=1,...,L
, we generate assignments by independently assigning each
b_{l}^{(n)}\in\mathcal{B}_{l}^{(n)}
a uniformly random element, which we call its index, from the codebook

\mathcal{M}_{l}^{(n)}:=\{1,2,3,...,2^{\frac{n}{L}(n-1)s(n)^{d}R_{l}}\}

(42)

we denote the index of
b_{l}^{(n)}
as
\phi_{l}^{(n)}(b_{l}^{(n)})
. Note that more than one realisation
b_{l}^{(n)}
may be assigned the same index. We reveal these assignments to both the encoder, and the decoder. To encode a graph, each encoder
l
sees its block,
b_{l}^{(n)}
. Then, the independent encodings are sent as the tuple
(\phi_{1}^{(n)}(b_{1}^{(n)}),...,\phi_{L}^{(n)}(b_{L}^{(n)}))
. The decoder receives this tuple, and searches for their own tuple
(\hat{b}_{1}^{(n)},...,\hat{b}_{L}^{(n)})
(or equivalently a graph
\hat{G}_{n}
), such that
(\hat{b}_{1}^{(n)},...,\hat{b}_{L}^{(n)})
is unique, typical, and
\phi_{l}^{(n)}(\hat{b}_{l}^{(n)})=\phi_{l}^{(n)}(b_{l}^{(n)})
for every
l
. If they cannot find such a tuple, an error is declared. Therefore, an error occurs if, first, the graph is atypical, or second, there exists an alternative decoding that is typical, and matches the encoding of the target graph. By Theorem
2
the first error probability goes to 0. For
\Lambda\subseteq\{1,...,L\}
, let
B_{\Lambda}^{(n)}=\bigcup_{l\in\Lambda}B_{l}^{(n)}
. Then the probability of the second error event is bounded above by the sum over such
\Lambda
of

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(E_{2}(\Lambda)\right)=\mathbb{P}\Big(\exists

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\neq B_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\text{ s.t. }(N_{\hat{b}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}},N_{B_{\Lambda^{c}}^{(n)}})\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\epsilon}(B_{\Lambda}^{(n)})

\displaystyle\text{ and }\phi_{l}^{(n)}(\hat{b}_{l}^{(n)})=\phi_{l}^{(n)}(B_{l}^{(n)})\forall l\in\Lambda\Big)

Following the standard argument, this can be bounded above by

\mathbb{P}\left(E_{2}(\Lambda)\right)\leq 2^{-\frac{n(n-1)}{L}s(n)^{d}\sum_{l\in S}R_{l}}|\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\epsilon}(B_{\Lambda}^{(n)})|

(43)

\leq 2^{-\frac{n(n-1)}{L}s(n)^{d}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}R_{l}+\binom{v}{2}s(v)^{d}(h^{*}+\epsilon)}

(44)

where
v=|B_{\Lambda}^{(n)}|=|\Lambda|n/L
. From this we can see that a sufficient condition for achievability with high probability as
n\rightarrow\infty
is

\sum_{l\in\Lambda}R_{l}\geq\frac{|\Lambda|}{L}\frac{h^{*}}{2}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{s(|\Lambda|n/L)^{d}}{s(n)^{d}}

(45)

For the converse direction, define
a_{n}(\Lambda)=\frac{|\Lambda|n}{L}(n-1)s\left(\frac{|\Lambda|n}{L}\right)^{d}
. As a consequence of
[
9
, Lemma 7.2.2]
, we have for any
\Lambda\subseteq[L]
,

\displaystyle P_{E}^{(n)}\geq\mathbb{P}\bigg(

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{n}(\Lambda)}\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}\left(B_{\Lambda}^{(n)}|B_{\Lambda^{c}}^{(n)}\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{a_{n}(\Lambda)}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}\log|\mathcal{M}_{l}^{(n)}|+\gamma\bigg)-2^{-a_{n}(\Lambda)\gamma}

(46)

Suppose
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}(\Lambda)}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}\log|\mathcal{M}_{l}^{(n)}|\leq\sum_{l\in\Lambda}R_{l}
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P_{E}^{(n)}\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{a_{n}(\Lambda)}\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}\left(G_{|B_{\Lambda|}}\right)}\geq\sum_{l\in\Lambda}R_{l}+\gamma\Big)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}2\frac{s(n)^{d}(n-1)}{s\left(\frac{|\Lambda|n}{L}\right)^{d}\left(\frac{|\Lambda|n}{L}-1\right)}\left(\sum_{l\in S}R_{l}+\gamma\right)<h^{*}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(47)

which gives that any achievable rate tuple must satisfy

\sum_{l\in\Lambda}R_{l}\geq\frac{|\Lambda|}{L}\frac{h^{*}}{2}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{s(|\Lambda|n/L)^{d}}{s(n)^{d}}

(48)

for the matching converse.
∎

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied the information theoretic properties of the soft random geometric graph in the sparse super-connectivity regime. We gave limit results which characterise the entropy rate of the SRGG, and establish that it is an information-stable source, in that its information content converges to the entropy rate with high probability. Next, we found the Slepian-Wolf rate region for distributed compression of the SRGG where there are a finite number of blocks of nodes. For future research, a tractable distributed compression algorithm would be a practically useful direction. From a theoretical standpoint, it would be interesting to extend our results to the regime where the number of compression units scales with
n
, and compare this region to the finite block size we considered. Is there any benefit in compressing the network using a growing number of encoders? Also, extensions to lossy distributed compression may be of interest. It may be possible to establish Berger-Tung inner and outer bounds for this problem for a wide range of distortion functions.
