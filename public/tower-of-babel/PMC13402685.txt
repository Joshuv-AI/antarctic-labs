Detection and countermeasure integrated framework against false data injection attacks in multi-area coupled power systems
Authors: Songtao Liu, Lei Xi, Zongze Li, Zihao Li, Hongjun Chen, Yanchun Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
False data injection attacks (FDIA) pose severe threat to the secure operation of load frequency control (LFC) systems. The accuracy of state estimation in power systems directly determines the detection precision and countermeasure effectiveness against FDIA. However, the existing state estimation algorithms are constrained by linear assumptions and the static noise models, making them difficult to adapt to the multi-area coupling characteristics of LFC systems. Moreover, the statistical overlap between the state variables and the noise cannot be effectively handled, further limiting estimation accuracy. Thus, an integrated framework for joint detection and countermeasure against FDIA in multi-area coupled power systems is proposed. In the framework, the sigma-point sampling strategy of the unscented kalman filter is employed to efficiently capture the dynamic characteristics of multi-area coupled LFC systems, thereby improving the adaptability of state estimation algorithm. In addition, an adaptive covariance update mechanism is utilized to track the statistical characteristics of noise and states in real time, reducing the impact of their overlap. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves more accurate state estimation compared with various state estimation algorithms. Moreover, under multiple attack scenarios, it provides superior detection accuracy and more effective countermeasures against FDIA.

Introduction

With the widespread deployment of smart grid1 and the increasing digitization of power systems2, cyberattacks3,4 as a significant threat5,6 have emerged to the secure and stable operation of power systems. Among the threats, false data injection attack (FDIA)7 represents one of the most severe cyberattack methods faced by smart grids8,9. The attackers can bypass bad data detection mechanisms, tamper with the measurement data in the systems of load frequency control (LFC)10, mislead the control decisions, and cause the fluctuations of grid frequency or the failures of the system.

However, facing the increasingly complex and large-scale power systems, the existing FDIA detection and defense systems for LFC expose obvious tactical deficiencies: the insufficient sensitivity in complex disturbance environments11, the limited estimation accuracy12, and the lack of dynamic countermeasure mechanisms13. It is difficult to counter adaptive attack strategies from adversaries. Therefore, a high-precision real-time FDIA detection and confrontation framework holds significant scientific importance and engineering application value for safeguarding the cybersecurity of global power systems and ensuring the stable operation of critical infrastructure in the digital era.

In recent years, a growing body of data-driven FDIA detection and recovery research for LFC systems has been conducted. This trend is supported by abundant operational data. Moreover, the capture of system dynamics in these methods are better than that in deterministic models. Deep learning-based detection and countermeasure architectures have been proposed14. Meanwhile, the multi-attack LSTM–autoencoder detectors and the regression-based recovery models for automatic generation control systems, such as governor dead band and generation rate constraint, have been shown nonlinearly to identify the attacks of coordinated FDIA/DoS with high accuracy. A prediction framework based on the neural network have also been introduced, in which the signals of area control error (ACE) are estimated, or the inputs of secure control are generated so that corrupted measurements can be bypassed during the attacks15. In addition, various data-driven attack-recovery schemes have been developed, including deep neural network-based ACE estimators16, adversarial-training autoencoders, and self-attention mechanisms for semi-supervised FDIA detection17. Of course, it also includes BiLSTM-based multi-type FDIA detection for distributed LFC systems in microgrids18, and DL-LSTM/BiGRU-based multi-point FDIA detection and defense schemes for complex LFC systems in which the HVDC links, RESs, and nonlinear characteristics are incorporated19. Through these studies, notable strengths have been demonstrated, such as the capability and adaptability of strong nonlinear modeling to diverse attack patterns and the ability to support simultaneous detection and mitigation.

However, the data-driven approaches also exhibit several inherent limitations that constrain their practical deployment in LFC systems. First, they typically rely on massive volumes of high-quality labeled or semi-labeled data for effective training, which are difficult to obtain in practical power grids, especially for rare and dangerous stealthy attacks. Many deep learning models are sensitive to data-set shift and require frequent retraining to maintain reliability under evolving operating conditions. Furthermore, their computational burden and training cost remain non-negligible; adversarial or GAN-based frameworks in particular may suffer from unstable convergence or expensive optimization, and their interpretability is limited. Consequently, their dependence on data availability and training mechanisms restricts their feasibility in engineering for real-time wide-area LFC applications, although data-driven methods enrich the research landscape of FDIA detection.

Given the above limitations of data-driven approaches, increasing attention has been directed toward model-based FDIA detection and confrontation methods for LFC systems. For example, a dual-mode framework integrating an unknown input observer (UIO) and a robust adaptive observer20 was proposed, which achieved effective detection and counteraction of the attacks by dynamically decoupling the load disturbances and FDIA signals. A combination of sliding mode observer and predictive control strategy was employed21 to resist the cyberattacks through an active compensation mechanism while the transient stability of the system is ensured. Moreover, the method of UIO-based attack detection22 and state and attack observer (SAO) was introduced23, respectively. The former ensured the stability of the frequency and tie-line power via real-time detection, while the latter achieved distributed estimation and mitigation of FDIA signals in multi-area LFC systems. Additionally, the mechanism of real-time closed-loop defense for the LFC systems by reconstructing the sensor signals through a joint input/state estimation algorithm was proposed24. Although the aforementioned methods based on optimized observer architectures can achieve the detection and countermeasure of FDIA, their practical engineering applicability still faces several challenges. The multi-modal observers (such as the dual-framework designed in reference20) require the mechanisms of intricate gain matrix adjustment, which can lead to the difficulties of parameter adjustment as it is applied in the practical power systems. The model-dependent observers, including UIO and SAO, are proposed in the ref. 22 and ref. 23 imposed the strict requirements on the accuracy of dynamic models in the system, and were susceptible to the estimation bias in the presence of noise. Thus, their robustness and applicability in real scenarios were limited.

To this end, the researchers have taken the algorithms that solved the state estimation25 as the starting point for developing the methods of FDIA detection and countermeasure26–35 for the systems of LFC. Within such methodological frameworks, the accuracy of state estimation directly determines the detection precision and countermeasure effectiveness against FDIA26–28. The solution algorithms of state estimation based on the Kalman filter (KF)23–30 achieved the self-adjustment of dynamic parameters through optimal recursive estimation. Thereby, the need for complex gain matrix adjustment mechanisms was avoided. The mainstream algorithms for FDIA detection and mitigation based on the solution of state estimation were obtained. For instance, Kalman filtering was integrated with an evolutionary game model29 in the ref. 30 to construct an attack detection mechanism based on temporal feature analysis. The approach not only enabled real-time detection of FDIA but also leveraged the online estimation capability of Kalman filtering to reconstruct the control signals in the LFC system. Furthermore, the self-healing regulation of system operation was achieved. A collaborative architecture combining a LO with an extended KF was proposed31, and the online estimation accuracy of both the LFC system states and the FDIA signals was significantly improved. Thereby, the quantitative support for precise countermeasures was provided. In addition, a two-stage KF that effectively realized simultaneous decoupling and joint estimation of system operating states and stealthy cyberattacks was introduced32. Meanwhile, a hybrid detection and mitigation scheme based on Kalman filtering and artificial neural networks was proposed33. The efficient detection and counteraction of FDIA in hybrid power systems were gotten. However, the mechanisms of core state prediction are limited by linear assumptions34 and static noise models35. It is difficult to adapt to the multi-area coupling characteristics36 of the LFC systems. Moreover, the statistical overlap between the state variables and the noise cannot be effectively handled, which leads to the limited accuracy of state estimation. The precision detection of FDIA and the effectiveness of countermeasure algorithms was affected ultimately.

In contrast, the unscented Kalman filter (UKF)37 efficiently sampled the dynamic characteristics of multi-area power systems through the sigma point sampling strategy38. It enabled the accurate transmission of the statistical information in the states of the LFC system. Thus, the coupling features between the regions were more precisely described. At the same time, an adaptive covariance update mechanism39 was integrated in this paper to track the noise characteristics in real time, and it dynamically adjusted the covariance matrix parameters. Thus, the limited accuracy on state estimation caused by the statistical overlap between the state variables and the noise was effectively suppressed, which led to the proposal of the adaptive unscented Kalman filter (AUKF) algorithm.

The AUKF is introduced in this paper as the algorithm of state estimation for the LFC systems, and a detection and countermeasure integrated (DCI) framework against FDIA is proposed. The proposed multi-area framework is implemented in a centralized architecture at the regional control center, enabling global state estimation and coordinated countermeasures across different areas. An AUKF-based state estimator integrated with an adaptive covariance update mechanism is constructed in the framework to achieve optimal state estimation. The real-time residuals are computed via the measurement equation, and a threshold detector is designed to perform online analysis of the residual signal. While the residual exceeds a predefined threshold, an FDIA alarm is triggered. Meanwhile, the algorithm of the FDIA countermeasure based on the error signal of state estimation is designed by incorporating the theory of Lyapunov stability40. Unlike observer-based FDIA mitigation methods that require intricate gain tuning, strong model dependence, and are sensitive to noise, and conventional Kalman-based methods constrained by linear assumptions and static noise models and unable to handle multi-area coupling and noise-state overlap, the proposed framework addresses these limitations through nonlinear adaptive estimation. By integrating state estimation, attack detection, and dynamic countermeasure within a unified closed-loop structure, it improves estimation accuracy and robustness in multi-area LFC systems under noisy operating conditions.

The models of IEEE standard two-area LFC41 and 12-bus multi-unit four-area coupled LFC system42 are built to conduct the experiments of FDIA under complex load disturbances and noisy environments. The results verify that the high estimation accuracy and reliable FDIA detection are maintained in the proposed DCI framework. Meanwhile, the countermeasure performance under the noisy and disturbance-rich conditions are increased.

Upon the detection of an attack, the signal estimate of FDIA in real time is calculated in the countermeasure algorithm, shown in Eq. 21, and it is compensated back into the LFC system. As illustrated in Fig. 2a, the output of the actual system and the values of state estimation are in strong agreement under normal operating conditions. It highlights the high accuracy of state estimation in the proposed algorithm. Notably, the system output shows a significant change while a step load disturbance of 0.5 p.u. is introduced to area 1 at the 20 s. Moreover, the algorithm of state estimation continues to accurately track the dynamics of the system, and its precision in state estimation is effectively demonstrated.

In order to verify that the accuracy of state estimation directly affects the effectiveness of the detection and countermeasures of FDIA, it is necessary to compare the accuracy of state estimation in different algorithms. In this paper, the residual characteristics analysis of state estimation is conducted in the algorithms of AUKF, KF, recursive least squares (RLS), sliding mode observer (SMO), Luenberger observer (LO), and H∞ filtering.

It should be noted that the residual is defined based on the posterior estimation error derived from the measurement equation (as shown in Fig. 1 and Eq. 23), rather than the raw measurement noise. Due to the filtering effect of the estimator, the posterior error is significantly attenuated compared with the original noise level. Furthermore, the residual metric is constructed as a quadratic form (i.e., involving the squared error), which further reduces its numerical magnitude. Therefore, the reported residual values are expected to be lower than the measurement noise standard deviation.

The time-varying curves of state estimation residuals under both noise-free and noisy environments (σ = 1 × 10−4 p.u.) in the six algorithms are presented in Fig. 2c, d, and the corresponding maximum residual values are summarized in Fig. 2b. The experimental results show that, under identical disturbance conditions, a maximum residual of 7.096 × 10−6 p.u. in the noise-free case and 8.613 × 10−6 p.u. under the noise is achieved in AUKF. Its residual values are significantly lower than those of the other algorithms. In contrast, the residuals of KF and RLS reach 1.091 × 10−5 p.u. and 1.458 × 10−5 p.u., respectively, in the presence of noise, and exhibit the sustained oscillatory behavior. For the algorithms SMO, LO, and H∞, the maximum residuals in the noise-free environment are 1.003 × 10−5 p.u., 1.002 × 10−5 p.u., and 1.002 × 10−5 p.u., respectively (seen in Fig. 2b). However, under the noisy conditions, their maximum residuals increase significantly to 1.485 × 10−5 p.u., 1.485 × 10−5 p.u., and 1.196 × 10−5 p.u., respectively. In a word, the accuracy of state estimation under the noisy conditions is significantly improved in AUKF compared with the other algorithms.

Specifically, even prior to any cyberattack (0–50 s) under normal operating conditions, the traditional algorithms, such as RLS, LO, and SMO, exhibit high sensitivity to the inherent system noise. Their detection residuals continuously fluctuate and frequently exceed the threshold τ so that in will cause persistent false alarms. The instability is further exacerbated while a normal fluctuation of step load is introduced at the 20th second, thus it causes their metrics to increase drastically. It proves that the baseline estimators are incapable of distinguishing the uncertainties of a normal system from actual malicious injections under a principled statistical framework. In sharp contrast, the detection residual generated by the proposed AUKF remains stably and cleanly below the threshold during both normal noisy operation and load disturbances.

During the attack phase initiated at the 50th second across three attack modes, the limitations of the baseline algorithms are further exposed. The conventional KF and H∞ filters completely fail to detect FDIA. And their detection residuals remain low (well below τ = 15.0863). Furthermore, it will result in continuous missed detections. While RLS, LO, and SMO do cross the threshold to trigger alarms, their residual values suffer from extreme background noise and erratic fluctuations. Moreover, it is difficult to distinguish true attacks from system noise.

Distinctly, the algorithm AUKF consistently maintains its residual strictly above the threshold during the attack intervals. Notably, under the pulse and sinusoidal attacks shown in Fig. 3b, c, AUKF accurately tracks the periodic switching of the attack signals without premature drop-offs. In summary, under the framework of statistically rigorous χ2 detection, AUKF exhibits superior sensitivity to diverse patterns of FDIA, while the exceptional resilience is offered against load disturbances. Finally, a near-zero false alarm and missed detection rate is successfully achieved.

The multi-area LFC system was simulated under strict normal, attack-free operating conditions across three progressively severe measurement noise environments: nominal noise, medium noise, and high noise. For the χ2 detection framework, the target theoretical FAR was governed by a significance level set to α = 0.01. As demonstrated in Table 1, the traditional estimators relying on fixed or nonadaptive covariance structures (i.e., RLS, SMO, and LO) exhibit a catastrophic degradation in reliability as ambient noise increases. Their empirical FARs surge dramatically from 0.32% to an unacceptable 80.86% under severe noise (σ = 5 × 10−2 p.u.). This dramatic increase clearly validates the necessity of adaptive statistical scaling, without which high noise triggers false alarms at nearly every time step. Conversely, the conventional KF and H∞ filters record empirical FARs of exactly 0%. However, zero false alarms is a direct symptom of their severe insensitivity to state anomalies, as previously analyzed. It renders them entirely blind to actual cyberattacks.

Standing apart from the extremes, the proposed algorithm AUKF demonstrates exceptional statistical robustness. It perfectly suppresses the noise interference and maintains an empirical FAR of 0% under both nominal and medium noise conditions. Even under the extraordinarily severe high noise scenario (σ = 5 × 10−2 p.u.), FAR of AUKF experiences only a graceful degradation to 9.01%. Thus, it remains an order of magnitude lower than the 80%+ failure rate of the baseline observers. The resilience is inherently attributed to the adaptive covariance update mechanism of AUKF. Importantly, the detection statistic (λk = εTkS − 1kεk) dynamically self-scales while the χ2-based threshold τ remains a predetermined constant. As ambient noise increases, AUKF proportionally inflates the innovation covariance Sk, which effectively normalizes the increased raw residual εk. Consequently, the detection metric inherently adapts to noise variations, and an optimal trade-off between suppressing false alarms is achieved. Moreover, the high sensitivity is maintained to genuine the threats of FDIA.

Theoretical computational complexity comparison

The derivation of the computational complexity is based on the dominant algebraic operations in each iteration. For the algorithms LO and SMO, the update process primarily involves matrix-vector multiplications (an n × n matrix by an n × 1 vector). Thus, it will result in the complexity of O(n2). Similarly, the standard RLS focuses on the updates of vector-based parameter; moreover, it also falls into the O(n2) category. In contrast, the algorithms KF, H∞, and AUKF must maintain and propagate a state covariance matrix; thus, the matrix-matrix multiplications and inversions is required, which leads to the asymptotic complexity of O(n3).

Notably, the experimental results show that AUKF outperforms KF and RLS in the high-dimensional four-area system. It is attributed to the fact that AUKF utilizes Cholesky decomposition to generate the sigma points while KF relies on explicit matrix inversions (e.g., via LU decomposition), which are computationally intensive and numerically sensitive as the system scale increases. Numerically, Cholesky decomposition is approximately twice as efficient as general inversion methods for positive-definite matrices and offers superior numerical stability. Therefore, the efficiency is gained from avoiding the explicit high-dimensional inversions as the system dimension n increases. Moreover, the efficiency outweighs the cost of sigma-point propagation.

Finally, AUKF is allowed to achieve better practical execution speed in larger test systems.

Discussion

State estimation, as the core of security defense in multi-area interconnected power systems, directly impacts the effectiveness of the detection and the capability of dynamic countermeasures against FDIA. The current methods of FDIA detection generally rely on linear assumptions and static noise models. It is difficult to accurately capture the multi-area coupling characteristics of LFC. Furthermore, the accumulation of estimation bias is likely while the state variables and the noise are overlapped significantly. It leads to the limited accuracy of state estimation. To address the issues, the framework of DCI that combines the algorithm AUKF with adaptive covariance is proposed in this paper. The main contributions are as follows:High-precision state estimation: the multi-area coupling characteristics of LFC are effectively captured by utilizing the sigma point sampling strategy of AUKF. AUKF-based algorithm of FDIA detection and countermeasure

Based on the established mathematical model of the system, the system states of LFC can be further predicted and estimated. The estimated values should closely match the actual measurement signals in the system under normal operating conditions. If a significant deviation occurs, it indicates that the system may be under a cyberattack. Thereby, the effective detection of FDIA is achieved.

Before the specific algorithms are detailed, it is essential to explicitly clarify the implementation architecture of the proposed multi-area framework. The state estimation and closed-loop countermeasure are designed based on a centralized architecture at the regional level. In practical deployments of power systems, the local measurements from phasor measurement units across different sub-areas are transmitted to a central phasor data concentrator located at the area control center. The AUKF-based estimation and the subsequent compensation signals are computed centrally at the dispatch center and then sent back to the local actuators. Given that the computational complexity of AUKF is well within the real-time constraints (as shown in Tables 4 and 5), the centralized multi-area implementation is highly feasible for regional grids. Meanwhile, the advantage of holistic situational awareness is offered without being hindered by distributed consensus delays.

Design of the countermeasure algorithm

Although the detection algorithm of FDIA is proposed in the last Section, its single-stage detection architecture still has inherent limitations in terms of dynamic closed-loop countermeasure capability. Specifically, the dependence on passive alarms from the detection results cannot promptly correct the tampered measurement values while the system of LFC is subjected to persistent or time-varying attacks of FDIA. It leads to the continuous accumulation of frequency deviations and tie-line power imbalances induced by FDIA, which may eventually result in inter-area frequency instability.

To enhance the active countermeasure capability beyond passive FDIA detection, it is necessary to design a compensation mechanism that can suppress the attack effect on real time. The key observation is that FDIA directly influences the measurement residual and consequently appears in the state estimation error generated by the observer of AUKF. Therefore, the error of state estimation provides an effective basis for reconstructing and compensating the unknown FDIA signal.

Motivated by this idea, the countermeasure algorithm is designed using an error-based feedback structure. The error of instantaneous estimation εk reflects the current attack impact, whereas the difference term εk − εk − 1 captures the variation of attack behavior. By linearly combining the two components through weighting coefficients σ1 and σ2, and mapping them through a designed gain matrix Γ, an attack estimation can be generated, which actively cancels the influence of FDIA in the system dynamics. This structure ensures that the closed-loop Lyapunov function decreases over time. Thereby, the stability is improved while the sustained accumulation of frequency deviations and tie-line power imbalances caused by FDIA is suppressed.

Therefore, the error terms can be used to adjust the covariance matrices online. In this way, the filter dynamically adapts to noise variations and prevents the performance degradation caused by fixed covariance assumptions.
