Zeros of Laurent multiple orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle
Rostyslav Kozhan 1, Marcus Vaktnäs 1

Abstract
. We investigate two distinct formulations of Laurent multiple orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle, introduced in [ KVMLOPUC ] and [ KVHP ] respectively. For the first formulation, we prove that all zeros lie strictly within the complex open unit disk for any Angelesco or AT system. For the second formulation, we establish normality of all indices of the form ( 𝒏 ; 𝒏 ) (\bm{n};\bm{n}) , ( 𝒏 + 𝒆 j , 𝒏 ) (\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j};\bm{n}) , and ( 𝒏 , 𝒏 + 𝒆 j ) (\bm{n};\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j}) for any Angelesco or AT system, thereby enabling the full application of the Szegő mapping and Geronimus relations from [ KVHP ] in the multiple orthogonality setting.



1.
Introduction

A fundamental question about orthogonal polynomials is the behaviour of their zeros. For measures supported on the real line (OPRL) they lie on the real line, and for measures supported on the unit circle
\partial\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
(OPUC) they lie inside the unit disc
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
.

The current paper investigates zeros of multiple orthogonal polynomials. They satisfy orthogonality relations with respect to a system of measures
(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})
. For measures supported on the real line (MOPRL), such polynomials
P_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})}
have been studied extensively over the last couple of decades, initially motivated by rational approximation problems with applications in number theory. See
[
Applications
]
for a short introduction with several applications, and the book of Nikishin and Sorokin
[
bookNS
]
for a more thorough introduction.

Unlike the usual orthogonal polynomials,
P_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})}
may not have all zeros real when each measure is supported on the real line. Hence one would like to identify subclasses of systems where this complication does not enter. One example, introduced by Angelesco
[
Angelesco
]
, is when the convex hulls of the supports of the measures are pairwise disjoint. One of the goals of this paper is to identify a similar result for measures supported on the unit circle (MOPUC), leading to all zeros lying inside the unit disc.

1.1.
Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials on the Unit Circle

MOPUC was introduced by Mínguez and Van Assche in
[
MOPUC1
]
, using the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})
be a system of measures supported on
\partial\mathbb{D}
, assumed to all have infinite support, and let
\bm{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
be a multi-index of non-negative integers. The type II multiple orthogonal polynomial
\Phi_{\bm{n}}
is defined by the degree restriction
\deg{\Phi_{\bm{n}}}\leq\left|\bm{n}\right|=n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}
and the orthogonality relations

\int_{\mathbb{\partial\mathbb{D}}}\Phi_{\bm{n}}(z)z^{-p}d\mu_{j}(z)=0,\quad p=0,\ldots,n_{j}-1,\quad j=1,\dots,r.

(1)

We say
\bm{n}
is normal with respect to
\bm{\mu}
if there exists a unique
\Phi_{\bm{n}}
on the form

\Phi_{\bm{n}}(z)=z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}+k_{\left|\bm{n}\right|-1}z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|-1}+\ldots+k_{0}.

(2)

Normality is necessary for almost any study of multiple orthogonality. In MOPRL, this is provided by Angelesco systems and AT systems
[
bookNS
]
, for which a rather simple zero counting approach proves normality. In MOPUC however, only one example of normality was given for the polynomials
\Phi_{\bm{n}}
, in the paper
[
MOPUC2
]
. Numerical simulations also show that
\Phi_{\bm{n}}
may have zeros outside the unit disc when the measures are supported on pairwise disjoint arcs.

1.2.
Laurent Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials on the Unit Circle

Recently, there has been substantial development on MOPUC, through the papers
[
KVMOPUC
,
HueMan
,
KVHP
,
KNik
,
KVMLOPUC
]
. In
[
KVMLOPUC
]
, we identified Angelesco and AT systems on the unit circle (defined in Section
2
), which are the analogue to the Angelesco and AT systems on the real line (see e.g.
[
bookNS
]
). This was done through computations inspired by the proof of
[
Kui
]
. This approach suggested the following alternative approach to MOPUC.

Definition 1.2
.

The type II Laurent multiple orthogonal polynomial
\phi_{\bm{n}}
is defined by
\phi_{\bm{n}}(z)\in\operatorname{span}\{z^{p}\}_{p=-\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}
and

\int\phi_{\bm{n}}(z)z^{-p}d\mu_{j}(z)=0,\quad p=-n_{j}/2,-n_{j}/2+1,\ldots,n_{j}/2-1,\quad j=1,\dots,r.

(3)

We say that a multi-index
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is
\phi
-normal with respect to
\bm{\mu}
if there exists a unique
\phi_{\bm{n}}
on the form

\phi_{\bm{n}}(z)=z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}+\kappa_{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2-1}z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2-1}+\ldots+\kappa_{-\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}z^{-\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}.

(4)

Note that this definition requires a choice of square root function, except the case when each
n_{j}
is even. When
r=1
,
z^{n/2}\phi_{n}(z)
corresponds to the Szegő polynomials
\Phi_{n}(z)
. For
r>2
however, normality in the sense of Definition
1.1
is very different from normality in the sense of Definition
1.2
.

The terminology of
\phi
-normality is used to separate it from Definition
1.1
, but we stress that we currently consider this to be a more useful notion of normality. In particular, the
\phi
-normality of every
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
was established in
[
KVMLOPUC
]
for Angelesco and AT systems on the unit circle, while normality in the sense of Definition
1.1
is a result we do not expect currently. The preliminaries on
\phi
-normality (including Angelesco and AT systems) is discussed further in Section
2
.

The main result of the paper proves that all zeros of
\phi_{\bm{n}}
lie in
\mathbb{D}
for Angelesco and AT systems. The proof goes through some functions
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
, defined in Section
3
, which should be viewed as the analogue to paraorthogonal polynomials
[
JNT
]
. In Section
4
we prove that all zeros of
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
lie on
\partial\mathbb{D}
using similar ideas to
[
KVNikInt
]
. An application of the argument principle then proves our main result (Section
5
).

It recently came to our attention that the functions
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
have a close connection with the trigonometric polynomials studied in
[
Mil
,
TrigMop2
]
. In the case of all
n_{j}
even, they turn into exactly their trigonometric multiple orthogonal polynomials of semi-integer degree. In this paper they define a notion of trigonometric AT system, very closely related to our notion of AT system, and prove a normality condition. Through results of this paper, we will see that they essentially proved
\phi
-normality in AT system for the case when every
n_{j}
is even, 10 years before our paper
[
KVMLOPUC
]
.

1.3.
General Two-Point Hermite–Padé polynomials

In
[
KVHP
]
we took a different approach to MOPUC, through a general Hermite–Padé approximation problem. The Laurent polynomials are given by the following definition.

Definition 1.3
.

For two multi-indices
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, we define the Laurent polynomials
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}
by
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}(z)\in\operatorname{span}\left\{z^{p}\right\}_{p=-\left|\bm{m}\right|}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}
and

\int\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}(w)w^{-p}d\mu_{j}(w)=0,\quad p=-m_{j},\dots,n_{j}-1,\quad j=1,\dots,r.

(5)

The pair of multi-indices
(\bm{n},\bm{m})
is normal if there exists a unique
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}
on the form

\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}(z)=z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}+\ldots+\alpha_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}z^{-\left|\bm{m}\right|}.

(6)

Observe that under the preceding definitions,
\Phi_{\bm{n}}=\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{0}}
and
\phi_{2\bm{n}}=\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{n}}
.
Combined with the results of
[
KVMLOPUC
]
, this implies that all indices
(\bm{n},\bm{n})
are normal for Angelesco and AT systems. A principal result in
[
KVHP
]
establishes the Szegő mapping and the Geronimus relations for multiple orthogonal polynomials, provided that indices of the form
(\bm{n},\bm{n})
,
(\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j},\bm{n})
and
(\bm{n},\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j})
are normal. As the second main result of the current paper, we prove that these specific indices are indeed normal for any Angelesco or AT system, thereby enabling the direct application of the Szegő mapping and Geronimus relations derived in
[
KVHP
]
.
The notion of paraorthogonal polynomials again plays an important part in the proof.

We remark that the Laurent polynomials
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{n}}
,
\Phi_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j},\bm{n}}
are closely related to those studied by Huertas and Mañas in
[
HueMan
]
, where the question of normality was left open.

2.
Normality

In this section we go over the basic preliminaries about normality. For the remainder of the paper we always assume the measures have infinite support. We also fix a branch of the square root function

z^{k/2}=|z|^{k/2}\exp({ik\arg_{[t_{0},t_{0}+2\pi)}}(z)/2),\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},

(7)

for some chosen
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. The choice will depend on the system we work with.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that
z^{k/2}=(z^{1/2})^{k}
, but not necessarily
z^{k/2}=(z^{k})^{1/2}
. Also,
z^{-k/2}=\overline{z^{k/2}}
, but not necessarily
z^{-k/2}=\bar{z}^{k/2}
.

For
f(z)=\sum_{k=-M/2}^{N/2}c_{k}z^{k}
, where
N
and
M
have the same parity, we write
f^{\sharp}
for the function

f^{\sharp}(z)=\overline{f(1/\bar{z})}=\sum_{k=-N/2}^{M/2}\bar{c}_{-k}z^{k}.

(8)

2.1.
\phi
-normality

Note that
\phi_{\bm{n}}^{\sharp}(z)\in\operatorname{span}\{z^{p}\}_{p=-\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}
and

\int\phi_{\bm{n}}^{\sharp}(z)z^{-p}d\mu_{j}(z)=0,\quad p=-n_{j}/2+1,\ldots,n_{j}/2,\quad j=1,\dots,r.

(9)

We then see that
\bm{n}
is
\phi
-normal if and only if there is a unique
\phi_{\bm{n}}^{\sharp}
satisfying (
9
) on the form

\phi^{\sharp}_{\bm{n}}(z)=\bar{\kappa}_{-\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}+\ldots+\bar{\kappa}_{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2-1}z^{-\left|\bm{n}\right|/2+1}+z^{-\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}.

(10)

When
\bm{n}
is
\phi
-normal we always assume the normalizations (
4
) and (
10
).

When solving (
3
)-(
4
) or (
9
)-(
10
) for the coefficients, we get a system of equations with matrix on the form

T_{\bm{n}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}T^{(1)}_{n_{1},|\bm{n}|}\\[3.99994pt]
\hline\cr[0.5pt/2pt]\\[-11.99998pt]
\vdots\\[1.99997pt]
\hline\cr[0.5pt/2pt]\\[-10.00002pt]
T^{(r)}_{n_{r},|\bm{n}|}\end{array}\right),

(11)

where
T^{(j)}_{n_{j},|\bm{n}|}
is the
n_{j}\times|\bm{n}|
matrix given by

T^{(j)}_{n_{j},|\bm{n}|}=\begin{pmatrix}\int z^{-\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}z^{n_{j}/2}d\mu_{j}(z)&\cdots&\int z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2-1}z^{n_{j}/2}d\mu_{j}(z)\\
\int z^{-\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}z^{n_{j}/2-1}d\mu_{j}(z)&\cdots&\int z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2-1}z^{n_{j}/2-1}d\mu_{j}(z)\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\int z^{-\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}z^{-n_{j}/2+1}d\mu_{j}(z)&\cdots&\int z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2-1}z^{-n_{j}/2+1}d\mu_{j}(z)\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(12)

The next result presents some of the conditions that are equivalent to
\phi
-normality of a multi-index
\bm{n}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The following statements are equivalent:

•

There is a unique
\phi_{\bm{n}}
on the form (
4
) satisfying (
3
).

•

There is no
\phi_{\bm{n}}(z)\in\operatorname{span}\{z^{p}\}_{p=-\left|\bm{n}\right|}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|-1}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying (
3
).

•

There is a unique
\phi_{\bm{n}}^{\sharp}
on the form (
10
) satisfying (
9
).

•

There is no
\phi_{\bm{n}}^{\sharp}\in\operatorname{span}\{z^{p}\}_{p=-\left|\bm{n}\right|+1}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying (
9
).

•

\det(T_{\bm{n}})\neq 0
.

Proof.

For each of the first four statements, solving for the coefficients results in a system of equation with matrix equal to
T_{\bm{n}}
.
∎

We remark that
\bm{0}
is always
\phi
-normal, and the above result holds with the convention
\det(T_{\bm{0}})=1
.

2.2.
Angelesco systems on the unit circle

Definition 2.3
.

\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r})
is an Angelesco system on
\partial\mathbb{D}
if there are closed arcs
\Gamma_{j}\subset\mathbb{\partial\mathbb{D}}
such that
\operatorname{supp}\,\mu_{j}\subseteq\Gamma_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,r
, and
\Gamma_{j}
intersects
\Gamma_{k}
at no more than the endpoints of the arcs, whenever
j\neq k
.

We assume that
t_{0}
and the order of
\Gamma_{j}
’s is chosen so that
t_{0}\leq\theta_{1}\leq\dots\leq\theta_{r}\leq t_{0}+2\pi
holds whenever
e^{i\theta_{1}}\in\Gamma_{1},\dots,e^{i\theta_{r}}\in\Gamma_{r}
. We choose the branch of the square root function to be (
7
) with this
t_{0}
.
Let us additionally require that
\mu_{r}
does not have a point mass at
e^{it_{0}}
.

By direct computation of the determinant of the moment matrix
T_{\bm{n}}
we proved the following result.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
KVMLOPUC
]
)
.

If
\bm{\mu}
is an Angelesco system on the unit circle then every
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is
\phi
-normal.

2.3.
AT systems on the Unit Circle

A collection of continuous real-valued functions
\left\{u_{j}(t)\right\}_{j=1}^{n}
on an interval
[a,b]
is a Chebyshev system on
[a,b]
if the determinants

W_{n}(\bm{x}):=\det\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}(x_{1})&u_{1}(x_{2})&\cdots&u_{1}(x_{n})\\
u_{2}(x_{1})&u_{2}(x_{2})&\cdots&u_{2}(x_{n})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
u_{n}(x_{1})&u_{n}(x_{2})&\cdots&u_{n}(x_{n})\\
\end{pmatrix}\neq 0

(13)

for every choice of different points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in[a,b]
. By continuity, the sign of the determinant in (
13
) is always positive or always negative, if we additionally introduce the ordering
x_{1}<x_{2}<\ldots<x_{n}
.

For a function
f(\theta)
we use the notation

\operatorname{Trig}_{m}(f)=\begin{cases}\left\{f(\theta)\cos{\frac{2k-1}{2}\theta},f(\theta)\sin{\frac{2k-1}{2}\theta}\right\}_{k=1}^{m/2},&\mbox{ if }m\mbox{ is even},\\
\left\{1\right\}\cup\left\{f(\theta)\cos{k\theta},f(\theta)\sin{k\theta}\right\}_{k=1}^{(m-1)/2},&\mbox{ if }m\mbox{ is odd}.\end{cases}

(14)

Definition 2.5
.

Assume
d\mu_{j}(e^{i\theta})=w_{j}(\theta)d\mu(e^{i\theta})
,
j=1,\dots,r
, for a measure
\mu
supported on the arc
\Gamma=\{e^{i\theta}:\alpha\leq\theta\leq\beta\}
with
0<\beta-\alpha\leq 2\pi
. Then
\bm{\mu}
is an AT system on
\Gamma
for the index
\bm{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})
if

\operatorname{Trig}_{\bm{n}}(\bm{\mu})=\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}\operatorname{Trig}_{n_{j}}(w_{j})

(15)

is a Chebyshev system on
[\alpha,\beta]
.

For AT system on the unit circle, the choice
t_{0}=\alpha
is natural. Through direct computation of the
\det{T_{\bm{n}}}
we proved the following result.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
KVMLOPUC
]
)
.

If
\bm{\mu}
is an AT system on the unit circle then every
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is
\phi
-normal.

In
[
KNik
]
, the AT property was verified for Nikishin systems on the unit circle, hence providing a large class of systems for which
\phi
-normality holds for a large set of indices.

2.4.
\Phi
-normality

We now discuss the polynomials from Definition
1.3
. We have the following related definition.

Definition 2.7
.

The Laurent polynomials
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}^{*}
are defined by the degree restriction
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}^{*}(z)\in\operatorname{span}\left\{z^{p}\right\}_{p=-\left|\bm{m}\right|}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}
and the orthogonality relations

\int\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}^{*}(w)w^{-p}d\mu_{j}(w)=0,\quad p=-m_{j}+1,\dots,n_{j},\quad j=1,\dots,r.

(16)

For the polynomials
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}^{*}
we want the normalization

\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}^{*}(z)=\beta_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}+\ldots+z^{-\left|\bm{m}\right|}.

(17)

The analogue of Proposition
2.2
for
\Phi
-normality is the following result.

Proposition 2.8
.

Normality of
(\bm{n},\bm{m})
is equivalent to the following statements.

•

There is a unique
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}
on the form (
6
) satisfying (
5
).

•

There is no
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}(z)\in\operatorname{span}\{z^{p}\}_{p=-\left|\bm{m}\right|}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|-1}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying (
5
).

•

There is a unique
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}^{*}
on the form (
17
) satisfying (
16
).

•

There is no
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}^{*}\in\operatorname{span}\{z^{p}\}_{p=-\left|\bm{m}\right|+1}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying (
16
).

Proof.

Similarly to Proposition
2.2
, these items are associated with the same matrix.
∎

We also have the following proposition, relating
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}^{*}
and
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}^{\sharp}
.

Proposition 2.9
.

(\bm{n},\bm{m})
is normal if and only if
(\bm{m},\bm{n})
is normal, and

\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}^{\sharp}=\Phi_{\bm{m},\bm{n}}^{*}.

(18)

Proof.

Simply check the orthogonality relations of
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}^{\sharp}
and
\Phi_{\bm{m},\bm{n}}^{*}
and apply Proposition
2.8
.
∎

3.
Multiple Paraorthogonal Polynomials

The results of the paper will be centered around the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

For any
\phi
-normal index
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and any
\tau\in\mathbb{\partial\mathbb{D}}
, the type II paraorthogonal polynomial
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
is defined by

X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)=z^{1/2}\phi_{\bm{n}}(z)+\tau z^{-1/2}\phi_{\bm{n}}^{\sharp}(z).

(19)

Remark 3.2
.

Note that if
r=1
then

z^{(n+1)/2}X_{n}^{(\tau)}(z)=z\Phi_{n}(z)+\tau\Phi_{n}^{*}(z),

(20)

which is the usual paraorthogonal polynomials
\Phi_{n+1}^{(\tau)}
from the classical theory.

By (
19
), we have
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)\in\operatorname{span}\left\{z^{p}\right\}_{p=-(\left|\bm{n}\right|+1)/2}^{(\left|\bm{n}\right|+1)/2}
and the symmetric orthogonality relations

\int X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)z^{-p}d\mu_{j}(z)=0,\quad p=-(n_{j}-1)/2,\dots,(n_{j}-1)/2.

(21)

We also have

X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)=\tau\overline{X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(1/\bar{z})},

(22)

which we will refer to as the
\tau
-invariance of
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

\bm{n}
is
\phi
-normal if and only if there is no non-trivial
\tau
-invariant
X(z)\in\operatorname{span}\left\{z^{p}\right\}_{p=-(\left|\bm{n}\right|-1)/2}^{(\left|\bm{n}\right|-1)/2}
satisfying (
21
).

Proof.

The matrix of (
21
) for
X(z)\in\operatorname{span}\left\{z^{p}\right\}_{p=-(\left|\bm{n}\right|-1)/2}^{(\left|\bm{n}\right|-1)/2}
is exactly equal to
T_{\bm{n}}
. This is immediate from the orthogonality relations and degree restriction of
\phi=z^{-1/2}X(z)
. We can now build the
\tau
-invariant
X+\tau X^{\sharp}
. If
X+\tau X^{\sharp}\not\equiv 0
we are done. Otherwise, we have
X=-\tau X^{\sharp}
, in which case
iX
is
\tau
-invariant.
∎

As an alternative to Definition
3.1
, we can work with the function

T_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(\theta)=\tfrac{1}{2}\tau^{-1/2}X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(e^{i\theta})=\tfrac{1}{2}\tau^{-1/2}z^{1/2}\phi_{\bm{n}}(z)+\tfrac{1}{2}\tau^{1/2}z^{-1/2}\phi_{\bm{n}}^{\sharp}(z).

(23)

Note that
\tau^{-1/2}X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)
is self-reciprocal since
\tau\in\mathbb{\partial\mathbb{D}}
, so
T_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(\theta)
is a real trigonometric polynomial

T_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(\theta)=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=1/2}^{(|\bm{n}|+1)/2}\left(a_{j}\cos j\theta+b_{j}\sin j\theta\right)&\mbox{if }|\bm{n}|\mbox{ is even},\\
a_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{(|\bm{n}|+1)/2}\left(a_{j}\cos j\theta+b_{j}\sin j\theta\right),&\mbox{if }|\bm{n}|\mbox{ is odd},\end{cases}

(24)

with

a_{(|\bm{n}|+1)/2}=\re(\tau^{1/2}),\quad b_{(|\bm{n}|+1)/2}=\im(\tau^{1/2}).

(25)

The orthogonality conditions (
21
) can be rewritten in the trigonometric form

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+2\pi}T_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(\theta)\cos p\theta\,d\mu_{j}(e^{i\theta})=0,\quad p=-(n_{j}-1)/2,\dots,(n_{j}-1)/2,

(26)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+2\pi}T_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(\theta)\sin p\theta\,d\mu_{j}(e^{i\theta})=0,\quad p=-(n_{j}-1)/2,\dots,(n_{j}-1)/2,

(27)

Such multiple orthogonality for trigonometric polynomials has been studied in
[
Mil
]
, in the case of all even
n_{j}
.

4.
Zeros of Multiple Paraorthogonal Polynomials

In this section we discuss zeros of the polynomials
z^{(\left|\bm{n}\right|+1)/2}X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)
. With a slight abuse of terminology we will refer to them as the zeros of
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
.
Here we write
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)
for any
\tau
-invariant function in
\operatorname{span}\left\{z^{p}\right\}_{p=-(\left|\bm{n}\right|+1)/2}^{(\left|\bm{n}\right|+1)/2}
satisfying (
21
). Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
are analogue to results on the real line from
[
KVNikInt
,
KV1
]
. The first result gives a criterion for no zeros to lie outside the unit circle.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
\bm{n}
is
\phi
-normal for
\bm{\mu}
. Then
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
has all
|\bm{n}|+1
zeros on the unit circle if
\bm{n}
is
\phi
-normal with respect to the systems

d\widehat{\bm{\mu}}(z)=\left|z-z_{0}\right|^{2}d\bm{\mu}(z),\quad 0\neq\left|z_{0}\right|\neq 1.

(28)

Proof.

First,
z^{(\left|\bm{n}\right|+1)/2}X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)
cannot vanish at
0
, since we would then have
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}\in\operatorname{span}\left\{z^{p}\right\}_{p=-(\left|\bm{n}\right|-1)/2}^{(\left|\bm{n}\right|-1)/2}
by
\tau
-invariance. This contradicts
\phi
-normality of
\bm{n}
for
\bm{\mu}
, by Proposition
3.3
.

Now suppose
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z_{0})=0
and
0\neq\left|z_{0}\right|\neq 1
. Then also
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(1/\bar{z}_{0})=0
. Hence we can pull out the factor
z^{-1}(z-z_{0})(z-1/\bar{z}_{0})
from
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
. Note that

\displaystyle z^{-1}(z-z_{0})(z-1/\bar{z}_{0})=-\bar{z}_{0}^{-1}\left|z-z_{0}\right|^{2},\quad z\in\partial\mathbb{D}.

(29)

We write
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)=\left|z-z_{0}\right|^{2}Y(z)
for
z\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, which defines a Laurent polynomial
Y(z)\in\operatorname{span}\left\{z^{p}\right\}_{p=-(\left|\bm{n}\right|-1)/2}^{(\left|\bm{n}\right|-1)/2}
. Then
z^{-1/2}Y(z)
satisfies all the orthogonality relations with respect to
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
, but the degree restriction contradicts
\phi
-normality since
Y(z)\not\equiv 0
.

∎

The next two results concern the behaviour of zeros that lie on the circle.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume
\bm{n}
is
\phi
-normal for
\bm{\mu}
. Then any zero of
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
on
\partial\mathbb{D}
is simple if
\bm{n}
is
\phi
-normal with respect to the systems

d\widehat{\bm{\mu}}(z)=-e^{i\varphi}z^{-1}(z-e^{i\varphi})^{2}d\bm{\mu}(z)=4\sin^{2}\frac{\theta-\varphi}{2}d\bm{\mu}(e^{i\theta}),\quad\varphi\in[0,2\pi).

(30)

Proof.

First note that

-ie^{-i\varphi/2}z^{-1/2}(z-e^{i\varphi})=2\sin\frac{\theta-\varphi}{2}\in\mathbb{R},\quad z\in\partial\mathbb{D}.

(31)

Now,
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
has a zero
e^{i\varphi}
of higher multiplicity if and only if

X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)=e^{i\varphi}z^{-1}(z-e^{i\varphi})^{2}Y(z),\quad Y(z)=\tau\overline{Y(1/\bar{z})}\in\operatorname{span}\left\{z^{p}\right\}_{p=-(\left|\bm{n}\right|-1)/2}^{(\left|\bm{n}\right|-1)/2}.

(32)

The result then follows by Proposition
3.3
applied to
\widehat{\bm{\mu}}
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume
\bm{n}
is
\phi
-normal for
\bm{\mu}
. If
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
has two zeros
e^{i\varphi_{1}}
and
e^{i\varphi_{2}}\neq e^{i\varphi_{1}}
then
\bm{n}
is not
\phi
-normal with respect to

d\widehat{\bm{\mu}}(z)=e^{-\varphi_{1}/2}z^{-1/2}(z-e^{i\varphi_{1}})e^{-\varphi_{2}/2}z^{-1/2}(z-e^{i\varphi_{2}})d\bm{\mu}(z)\\
=4\sin\frac{\theta-\varphi_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{\theta-\varphi_{2}}{2}d\bm{\mu}(e^{i\theta}).

(33)

Proof.

Same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
∎

For Angelesco and AT systems, Theorem
4.1
-
4.3
gather into the following results.

Theorem 4.4
.

If
\bm{\mu}
is an AT system, then all
|\bm{n}|+1
zeros of
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
are simple and lie on the unit circle. At most one zero lies outside of
\operatorname{Int}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

The AT property for
\left|z-z_{0}\right|^{2}d\bm{\mu}
and
\sin^{2}\frac{\theta-\varphi}{2}d\bm{\mu}(e^{i\theta})
clearly holds, so all zeros are simple and unimodular. If
e^{i\varphi_{1}}\neq e^{i\varphi_{2}}
are zeros outside
\operatorname{Int}(\Gamma)
, then
\sin\frac{\theta-\varphi_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{\theta-\varphi_{2}}{2}
keeps the same sign on
\operatorname{Int}(\Gamma)
. Hence Theorem
4.3
applies since
\left|\sin\frac{\theta-\varphi_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{\theta-\varphi_{2}}{2}\right|d\bm{\mu}(e^{i\theta})
is AT with the same multiple orthogonal polynomials as (
33
).
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

If
\bm{\mu}
is an Angelesco system, then all
|\bm{n}|+1
zeros of
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
are simple and lie on the unit circle. At most one zero lies outside
\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}\operatorname{Int}(\Gamma_{j})
.

Proof.

Same as the proof of Theorem
4.4
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

In this paper, we currently focus strictly on the zero location for type II Laurent multiple orthogonal polynomials. The analogous analysis for type I polynomials based on
[
KVNikInt
]
produces similar results. This is the subject of ongoing research by the authors and will be addressed in subsequent work.

Theorem
4.5
can be improved, by the following result, based on the standard zero counting proof for multiple orthogonal polynomials on the real line (see e.g.
[
Ismail
]
).

Theorem 4.7
.

If
\bm{n}
is normal, then
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
has at least
n_{j}
zeros of odd multiplicity in
\operatorname{Int}(\Gamma_{j})
.

Proof.

By (
29
) and (
31
) we have, up to multiplication by a constant, the factorization

X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left|z-z_{j}\right|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|-2k+1}ie^{-i\varphi_{j}/2}z^{-1/2}(z-e^{i\varphi_{j}}),\quad z\in\partial\mathbb{D}.

(34)

Let
e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{\ell}}
be the zeros of
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
in
\operatorname{Int}(\Gamma_{j})
of odd multiplicity. Then we can write
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)=Y(z)L(z)
, where
Y(z)
is real valued and does not change sign on
\Gamma_{j}
, and
L(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}ie^{i\varphi_{j}/2}z^{-1/2}(z-e^{i\varphi_{j}})
.

Assume first that
\ell
and
n_{j}
have different parity, so that if
\ell<n_{j}
then
L(z)\in\operatorname{span}\left\{z^{p}\right\}_{p=-(n_{j}-1)/2}^{(n_{j}-1)/2}
. Since
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)L(z)
does not change sign on
\Gamma_{k}
we have

\int X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)L(z)d\mu_{j}(z)\neq 0.

(35)

By the orthogonality relations (
21
), we must have
\ell\geq n_{j}
.

Now assume that
\ell
and
n_{j}
have the same parity. Then if
\ell<n_{j}
we have
z^{1/2}L(z)\in\operatorname{span}\left\{z^{p}\right\}_{p=-(n_{j}-1)/2}^{(n_{j}-1)/2}
. Note that the imaginary part of
e^{-it_{0}/2}X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)z^{1/2}L(z)
does not change sign on
\Gamma_{j}
. Hence we have

\int X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)z^{1/2}L(z)d\mu_{j}(z)\neq 0.

(36)

We conclude that
\ell\geq n_{j}
.
∎

The factorization (
34
) on trigonometric form is given by

T_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(\theta)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(1+r_{j}^{2}-2r_{j}\cos(\theta-\theta_{j}))\prod_{j=1}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|-2k+1}\sin{\frac{\theta-\varphi_{j}}{2}}.

(37)

The above proof can be translated to the trigonometric point of view without substantial changes. A zero counting proof for AT systems in the case when every
n_{j}
is even can be found in
[
Mil
]
. We also remark that the above zero counting proof is a variation of a result from
[
quasipopuc
]
, which locates zero of quasi-paraorthogonal polynomials.

Remark 4.8
.

Theorem
4.7
verifies the criterion of Proposition
3.3
for Angelesco systems. Hence we have found a second proof of
\phi
-normality. A similar zero counting approach for AT systems, extending the proof of
[
Mil
]
to any
\bm{n}
, gives a second proof of
\phi
-normality of AT systems as well.

5.
Zeros of Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials

Through the results of the previous section we are now ready to prove the main results of the paper.

Theorem 5.1
.

For Angelesco and AT systems, the polynomial
z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}\phi_{\bm{n}}(z)
has
\left|\bm{n}\right|
zeros in
\mathbb{D}
, with each zero counted as many times as its multiplicity.

Proof.

In this proof we work with
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
on the form (
19
). We first show that
P(z)=z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|/2}\phi_{\bm{n}}(z)
has no zeros on
\partial\mathbb{D}
. If we have
\phi_{\bm{n}}(e^{i\varphi})=0
for some angle
\varphi
then we would have
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(e^{i\varphi})=0
for every
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Note that
e^{i\varphi}
cannot be a double zero of
\phi_{\bm{n}}
, since then it is also a double zero of
X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}
, which contradicts Theorem
4.4
-
4.5
. For the polynomial
P^{(\tau)}(z)=z^{(\left|\bm{n}\right|+1)/2}X_{\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)
, we have

\frac{d}{d\theta}P^{(\tau)}(e^{i\theta})\Bigg|_{\theta=\varphi}=e^{i\varphi}\frac{d}{d\theta}P(e^{i\theta})\Bigg|_{\theta=\varphi}+\tau\frac{d}{d\theta}P^{*}(e^{i\theta})\Bigg|_{\theta=\varphi},

(38)

where
P^{*}(z)=z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}\phi_{\bm{n}}^{\sharp}(z)=z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}\overline{P(1/\bar{z})}
. Hence we get a double zero of
P^{(\tau)}
with the choice
\tau=-e^{i(1-\left|\bm{n}\right|)\varphi}\frac{d}{d\theta}P(e^{i\theta})/\frac{d}{d\theta}\overline{P(e^{i\theta})}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, which again contradicts Theorem
4.4
-
4.5
. Hence all zeros of
P
lie in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\partial\mathbb{D}
.

Now consider the Blaschke product

B(z)=\frac{zP(z)}{P^{*}(z)}=z\prod_{j=1}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}\frac{z-z_{j}}{1-\bar{z}_{j}z}.

(39)

On the unit circle we have

B(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\theta}\prod_{j=1}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}\frac{e^{-i\theta}(e^{i\theta}-z_{j})^{2}}{\left|e^{i\theta}-z_{j}\right|^{2}}=e^{i\Psi(\theta)},\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi),

(40)

where
\Psi
is the continuous phase function

\Psi(\theta)=\theta+2\sum_{j=1}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|}\left(-\frac{\theta}{2}+\operatorname{Arg}(e^{i\theta}-z_{j})\right).

(41)

By the argument principle we have

\Psi(\pi-)-\Psi(-\pi)=2\pi(n_{+}+1-n_{-}),

(42)

where
n_{+}
the number of zeros of
P
in
\mathbb{D}
and
n_{-}
is the number of zeros of
P
outside
\mathbb{D}
.

Note that
B(e^{i\theta})=-\tau
when
P^{(\tau)}(e^{i\theta})=0
, in which case
\Psi(\theta)=\operatorname{Arg}(-\tau)+2\pi k
for some integer
k
. We claim that
\Psi
is strictly monotone, so that we would get
\Psi(\pi-)-\Psi(-\pi)=\pm 2\pi(\left|\bm{n}\right|+1)
, as
P^{(\tau)}
has
\left|\bm{n}\right|+1
simple zeros on
\partial\mathbb{D}
. Since
P
has degree
\left|\bm{n}\right|
, this would imply
n_{+}=\left|\bm{n}\right|
and finish the proof.

To see that
\Psi
is monotone, suppose
\Psi^{\prime}(\varphi)=0
for some angle
\varphi
. By differentiating
B(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\Psi(\theta)}
we obtain

\frac{d}{d\theta}(e^{i\theta}P(e^{i\theta}))\Bigg|_{\theta=\varphi}P^{*}(e^{i\varphi})-e^{i\varphi}P(e^{i\varphi})\frac{d}{d\theta}P^{*}(e^{i\theta})\Bigg|_{\theta=\varphi}=0.

(43)

With
\tau=-B(e^{i\varphi})
it follows from (
43
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\theta}P^{(\tau)}(e^{i\theta})

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d\theta}(e^{i\theta}P(e^{i\theta}))\Bigg|_{\theta=\varphi}+\tau\frac{d}{d\theta}P^{*}(e^{i\theta})\Bigg|_{\theta=\varphi}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d\theta}P^{*}(e^{i\theta})\Bigg|_{\theta=\varphi}\left(B(e^{i\varphi})+\tau\right)

\displaystyle=0.

Hence
P^{(\tau)}
has a double zero, which contradicts Theorem
4.4
-
4.5
.
∎

Now we turn to the Hermite–Padé polynomials
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{m}}
of
[
KVHP
]
. The previous result solves the case
\bm{m}=\bm{n}
. Through a modification of the above proof we can now obtain normality in the case
\bm{m}=\bm{n}\pm\bm{e}_{k}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\bm{\mu}
be an Angelesco system or an AT system. Then the multi-indices
(\bm{n},\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j})
and
(\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j},\bm{n})
are normal for each
j=1,\dots,r
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.8
it suffices to show that the polynomial
P(z)=z^{\left|\bm{n}\right|+1}\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j}}(z)
has
2\left|\bm{n}\right|+1
zeros (with each zero counted according to its multiplicity), given the orthogonality relations and degree restrictions of
\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j}}\not\equiv 0
. Note that
\Phi(z)=z^{1/2}\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j}}(z)
satisfies the orthogonality relations

\int\Phi(z)z^{-p}d\mu_{j}(z)=0,\quad p=-n_{j}+1/2,\dots,n_{j}-1/2,\quad j=1,\dots,r.

(44)

Now consider the function

X_{2\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)=z^{1/2}\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j}}(z)+\tau z^{-1/2}\Phi_{\bm{n},\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j}}^{\sharp}(z).

(45)

Observe that
X^{(\tau)}_{2\bm{n}}(z)\in\operatorname{span}\{z^{p}\}_{p=-|\bm{n}|-1/2}^{|\bm{n}|+1/2}
and satisfies (
21
)–(
22
) for index
2\bm{n}
. Note that its highest coefficient is not
1
, unlike the polynomial defined in (
19
).

By Proposition
3.3
, the polynomial
P^{(\tau)}(z)=z^{\left|n\right|+1/2}X_{2\bm{n}}^{(\tau)}(z)
has degree
2\left|\bm{n}\right|+1
. We also have

P^{(\tau)}(z)=P(z)+\tau P^{*}(z),

(46)

where
P^{*}(z)=z^{2\left|\bm{n}\right|+1}\overline{P(1/\bar{z})}
. From here we can repeat the ideas from the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

First, it follows similarly that
P
cannot have zeros on
\partial\mathbb{D}
, and then we can introduce the Blaschke product
B(z)=P(z)/P^{*}(z)
. We have
B(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\Psi(\theta)}
for the continuous phase function

\Psi(\theta)=2\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(-\frac{\theta}{2}+\operatorname{Arg}(e^{i\theta}-z_{j})\right),\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi),

(47)

where
N=\deg{P}\leq 2\left|\bm{n}\right|+1
and
z_{1},\dots,z_{N}
are the zeros of
P
. The argument principle then gives
\Psi(\pi-)-\Psi(-\pi)=2\pi(n_{+}-n_{-})=\pm 2\pi(2\left|\bm{n}\right|+1)
, where the sign depends on whether
\Psi
is increasing or decreasing. Depending on the sign we then have either
2\left|\bm{n}\right|+1
zeros in
\mathbb{D}
or
2\left|\bm{n}\right|+1
zeros in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\bar{\mathbb{D}}
. Either case proves
\deg{P}=2\left|\bm{n}\right|+1
, which completes the proof.
∎
