ke In conversations with other cadres in his unit, operating in QUANG NGAI,
. QUANG NAM and BINH DINH Province, Source learned about a direct air

supply route between HANOI and an airfield somewhere in CAMBODIA; this route
had been in operation since 196. In April 1967, he met an old friend, an
NVA Sergeant, who was on his way to KONTUM and then LAOS to join a truck
convoy to NVN; this convoy was to bring supplies back to SVN. In July 1967,
during his confinement at DA NANG, he overheard another prisoner say that
earlier that same month, right after a battle in QUANG TRI, helicopters
had come to pick up marine prisoners captured by the NVA unit, and taken
them to NVN.

Ze In the conversations with cadres of his unit, Source was told Senior
Captain NGUYEN VAN DY,the Battalion Political Officer, about the air
supply route from HANOI to Cambodia, Source had asked why units in the
northern regions of SVN lacked adequate supplies of weapons while the
NVA/VC troops in the lower parts of SVN were reasonably well equipped.

He was then informed that in 196 an air supply route had been put into
effect connecting a field somewhere in Cambodia with those of NVN. From

the airfield in Cambodia supplies were then trucked to the SVN border for
distribution to NVA/VC troops operating in the southern areas of SVN. He

was also informed that the location of this airfield was kept a secret and
that even the Battalion Political Officer did not have additional information
about this route, the types of aircraft involved or the nationality of

the pilots.

3. In April 1967 Source met Senior Sergeant NGUYEN MANH TUONG in MINH LONG
District, QUANG NG&I Province, This man had infiltrated SVN with Source's
unit, but in 1965 he had been transferred to an Engineering School situated
somewhere along the border of QUANG NAM and KONTUM Provinces. His friend
was now the Adjutant of the engineering school. He told Source that he
was on his way to the border of KONTUM Province and then to LAOS where he
would meet a convoy of trucks heading for NVN. At the engineering school
personnel from Platoon Leaders up to Company Commanders were instructed
on the dismantling of bombs, making of bombs, land mines, torpedos and
water mines. NGUYEN MANH TUONG added that he was going to NVN to bring
back supplies for this school. He was travelling alone at the time, but
once in LAOS he would join up with a truck convoy. When Source asked him
about the danger involved in the journey, his friend told him that the
convoy would be travelling only at night, at high speed, and that he expected
to be in NVN (no definite location given) within ten days. He did not specify
the number of trucks involved but said it was a "large" convoy of "MOLOTOVA"
trucks, each equipped with an "antiaircraft" gun for defense against
enemy aircraft. No comments were made in their conversation about the
condition or size of the road, other than the fact that it ran from LAOS,
swerved West around the demilitarized zone and then East into NVN. His
friend did not say who drove the trucks, or whether the trucks carried
anything in them on the way north. Source knew that high level cadres or
seriously wounded NVA veterans returning to NVN travelled through LAOS
and his friend stated that they used this same road in doing so.

h. During his confinement at DA NANG in July 1967, Source heard one of the 1
other prisoners talk about the battle of "DONG HA" in QUANG TRI Province
staged in July 1967 in which American Marines engaged NVA forces. This
man (name unknown) said that during the battle his uniit (name not mentioned)
had captured many U.S. Marines, and.that NVN helicopters had landed near
the battle scene to pick up these prisoners and take them to NVN. He added
that the NVN Government had sent these helicopters, but he did not say
anything about the direction they came from or departed.
