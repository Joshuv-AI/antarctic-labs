Abstract
. We derive a sharp spectral estimate for a superlinear free boundary problem arising in plasma physics. The semilinear equation is coupled with a constraint, which forces the analysis of a non-local eigenvalue equation. Consequently the corresponding first eigenvalue, say σ 1 \sigma_{1} , is not a standard one and it is shown that it cannot satisfy a general isoperimetric property of Faber-Krahn type. This motivates a careful analysis of the problem on balls in any dimension N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 , where we prove that in fact σ 1 \sigma_{1} is always positive. The implications about the uniqueness problem for the Emden equation are also discussed.



Keywords
: Spectral estimates, Free boundary equations, Emden problems, Uniqueness.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
be a bounded domain of class
C^{3}
and set

p_{{}_{N}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}+\infty,\;N=2\\
\frac{N}{N-2},\;N\geq 3.\end{array}\right.

Motivated by the analysis of Gelfand problems we have recently pursued (
[
3
]
)
the constrained problem,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\psi=[\alpha+{\lambda}\psi]_{+}^{p}\quad\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega\\
\\
\mathop{\text{\LARGE$\int$}}\nolimits\limits_{\Omega}{[\alpha+{\lambda}\psi]_{+}^{p}}=1\\
\\
\psi=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.\qquad{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}

for
1<p<p_{{}_{N}}
,
\lambda\geq 0
and the unknowns
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\psi\in C^{2,r}_{0}(\overline{\Omega}\,)
,

.
The underlying idea of

,

{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}
from

For a fixed

which solves

, we will adopt the following notations,

\mbox{\Large\textrho}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}=[\alpha_{\lambda}+\lambda\psi_{\lambda}]_{+}^{p}\quad\mbox{\rm and }\quad{V_{\lambda}}=[\alpha_{\lambda}+\lambda\psi_{\lambda}]_{+}^{p-1}=\mbox{\Large\textrho}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}^{\frac{1}{q}}.

,

,

,

We follow the traditional convention to assume that the domain has unit volume,
|\Omega|=1
.
In particular
\mathbb{D}_{N}
denotes a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
centered at the origin of unit volume

, whose radius is denoted by

.
For fixed

\Lambda(\Omega,t)=\inf\limits_{w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),w\equiv\!\!\!\!/\;0}\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}}{\left(\int_{\Omega}|w|^{t}\right)^{\frac{2}{t}}}\,,

which is related to the best constant in the Sobolev embedding

via

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{S}(\Omega,t)=\Lambda^{-1/2}(\Omega,t)\quad\mbox{ for }t\in[1,2p_{{}_{N}}).

Definition.

We say that a solution
(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})
of
{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}

[

[

It has been recently proved in

\lambda_{+}(\Omega,p)=\sup\left\{\delta>0\,:\,\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}>0\mbox{ for any solution }(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})\mbox{ of }{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}\mbox{ with }\lambda<\delta\right\}.

Remark.

We will use the fact that
\lambda_{+}(\Omega,p)
is always finite, as readily follows by the variational formulation of solutions of
{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}
.
See also

[
5
, Appendix A]
.

Definition.

Let
(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})
be a solution of
{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}
.
The
energy
of
(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})
is

E_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mbox{\rm$\mbox{\Large\textrho}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}$}\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}.

Let us define

\lambda_{0}(\Omega,p):=\frac{1}{p}\Lambda(\Omega,2p),\quad\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\;N\geq 2,

and

\lambda_{1}(\Omega,p):=\left(\frac{8\pi}{p+1}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2p}}\Lambda^{\frac{p+1}{2p}}(\Omega,p+1),\quad\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}.

The following results about positive solutions have been recently proved in
[
8
,
5
]
.

Theorem A
(
[
8
,
5
]
).
Let
N=2
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
, then
\lambda_{+}(\Omega,p)\geq\lambda_{1}(\Omega,p)

where the equality holds if and only if either
p=1
or
\Omega=\mathbb{D}_{2}
.
Moreover, for any
\lambda<\min\{\lambda_{0}(\Omega,p),\lambda_{1}(\Omega,p)\}
there exists a unique solution to
{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}
,
defining a
real analytic curve of positive solutions which we denote by
\mathcal{G}_{0}(\Omega)
,
such that
\alpha_{0}=1
,
2E_{0}=2E_{0}(\Omega)
is the torsional rigidity of
\Omega
and

\frac{d\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}}{d\lambda}<0,\quad\frac{dE_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}}{d\lambda}>0,\quad\forall(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})\in\mathcal{G}_{0}(\Omega).

We tend to believe that
\lambda_{1}(\Omega,p)>\lambda_{0}(\Omega,p)
, which is always true either for
\Omega=\mathbb{D}_{2}
or for any
p
large enough, see
[
5
]
.
It is not clear whether or not the sharp positivity estimates about
\lambda_{+}(\Omega,p)
obtained in Theorem B can be extended to higher dimensions
N\geq 3
.
For later purposes we also recall the following result in
[
5
]
. Let
u_{0}
be the unique (
[
21
]
) solution of the Emden equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u_{0}=u_{0}^{p}\quad\mbox{in}\;\;B_{1}\\
u_{0}>0\quad\mbox{in}\;\;B_{1}\\
u_{0}=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\;\partial B_{1},\end{array}\right.

and set

I_{p}=\int\limits_{B_{1}}{\displaystyle{u}_{0}^{p}}.

Here
R_{N}
denotes the radius of
\mathbb{D}_{N}
and it has been shown in
[

]
that,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\lambda_{+}(\mathbb{D}_{N},p)={I_{p}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}{R_{N}^{-\frac{N}{p}(1-\frac{p}{p_{{}_{N}}})}}.

Remark that for
N=2
the uniqueness of solutions on balls was first proved in
[
2
]
. This being said, we have,

Theorem B
(
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
).
Let
\Omega=\mathbb{D}_{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
and
p\in(1,p_{{}_{N}})
. Then, for any
\lambda\geq 0
,

{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}
admits a unique solution. Moreover

\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}>0
if and only if
\lambda<\lambda_{+}(\mathbb{D}_{N},p)
,
\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}
is of class
C^{2}
in
(0,+\infty)
and
\frac{d\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}}{d\lambda}<0
for any
\lambda\geq 0
.

As a corollary of Theorem B, we have a new proof of the well known (
[
21
]
) uniqueness of solutions of the Emden problem (
1
), see Section
5
.

It can be shown (see
[
8
]
,
[
3
]
), that the “natural” non-local
eigenvalue problem associated to the linearized equation for
{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}
evaluated at a fixed solution
(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})
takes the form

-\Delta\phi-\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}V_{\lambda}[\phi]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}=\sigma V_{\lambda}[\phi]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},

where, here and in the sequel,
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
denotes an eigenvalue relative to
\phi
,

\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}=\lambda p,

and

<\phi>_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\coloneqq\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}V_{\lambda}\phi}{\int_{\Omega}V_{\lambda}},\qquad[\phi]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\coloneqq\phi\,-<\phi>_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\qquad\forall\,\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega).

For any fixed solution
(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})
, the weighted first eigenvalue is well defined (
[
3
]
), which we will always denote by
\sigma_{1}(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})
.
Let us define

\lambda_{*}(\Omega,p)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\overline{\lambda}>0\,:\,\sigma_{1}(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})>0\mbox{ for any solution }(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})\mbox{ of }{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}\mbox{ with }\lambda<\overline{\lambda}\right\}.

We recently proved the following

Theorem C
(
[
3
]
)
Let
N\geq 2
and
p\in(1,p_{N})
, then
\lambda_{*}(\Omega,p)>\lambda_{0}(\Omega,p)
. Moreover
\lambda_{+}(\Omega,p)\geq\lambda_{0}(\Omega,p)
where the equality holds if and only if
p=1

and for any
\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{*}(\Omega,p))
, there exists a unique solution to
{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}
,
defining a
C^{1}
curve of solutions, denoted by
\mathcal{G}_{*}(\Omega)
, such that
\alpha_{0}=1
,
2E_{0}=2E_{0}(\Omega)
is the torsional rigidity of
\Omega
and

\frac{d\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}}{d\lambda}<0,\quad\frac{dE_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}}{d\lambda}>0,\quad\forall(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})\in\mathcal{G}_{*}(\Omega).

As a corollary of Theorem C we obtained in
[
3
]
a new way to describe the sharp qualitative behavior of the unbounded Rabinowitz continuum (
[
27
]
) of a class of Gelfand problems, including non minimal (
[
16
]
) solutions. Indeed we reduced the problem of the existence of a global strictly monotone (in terms of
E_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}
) parametrization to an estimate about
\lambda_{*}(\Omega,p)
and
\lambda_{+}(\Omega,p)
. Sufficient conditions to come up with this refined description are either

\lambda_{*}(\Omega,p)>\lambda_{+}(\Omega,p),

or

\lambda_{*}(\Omega,p)\geq\lambda_{+}(\Omega,p)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\searrow 0^{+}\mbox{ as }\lambda\nearrow\lambda_{+}(\Omega,p),

see
[
3
]
for further details. Actually we proved in
[
3
]
that

\lambda_{*}(\mathbb{D}_{N},p)\geq\lambda_{+}(\mathbb{D}_{N},p)
and that
\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\searrow 0^{+}
as
\lambda\nearrow\lambda_{+}(\mathbb{D}_{N},p)
. As far as semilinear problems are concerned, spectral estimates of this sort are rather hard to establish,
known results generally relying on isoperimetric properties inherited by the underlying geometric structures, see
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
30
]
and references quoted therein. We come up here with a sharp refinement of the spectral estimate on
\mathbb{D}_{N}
. Indeed we have,

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\in(1,p_{N})
, then

\lambda_{*}(\mathbb{D}_{N},p)=+\infty,

that is, for any
\lambda\geq 0
and for any solution
(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})
of
{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}
on the ball
\mathbb{D}_{N}
the inequality
\sigma_{1}(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})>0
holds true.

We hope this could be useful for later developments, the underlying idea being that to compare the value of
\sigma_{1}(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})
for a fixed
\lambda

on a given domain
\Omega
with that on
\mathbb{D}_{N}
. However there is no chance to rely on general
isoperimetric properties of Faber-Krahn type for
\sigma_{1}
. Indeed, if one could prove that
\lambda_{*}(\Omega,p)=+\infty
, then by Theorem C we could infer the uniqueness of solutions of the Emden problem (
1
) on
\Omega
, see Section
5
for details. Therefore we know for sure that
\lambda_{*}(\Omega,p)<+\infty

for all those domains where uniqueness of the Emden problem fails, see for example
[
18
]
,
[
24
]
and references quoted therein.

This is in striking contrast with the analogue situation arising in the analysis of the first constrained eigenvalue (say again
\sigma_{1}
) for mean field equations (
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
30
]
) where neither the geometry nor the topology of the domain affect the positivity of
\sigma_{1}
.

Actually the proof of Theorem
1.1
heavily relies on the uniqueness and regularity (with respect to
\lambda
) of solutions on
\mathbb{D}_{N}
(see Theorem B) which is not easy to extend to more general situations. This motivates the following

OPEN PROBLEM
Assume
N\geq 2
,
1<p<p_{{}_{N}}
and
\Omega
convex, is it true that
\lambda_{*}(\Omega,p)=+\infty
?

At last, by Propositions
2.7
and
4.1
below, if

is a two dimensional domain, symmetric and convex
with respect to coordinate directions, then we can prove that the curve of unique solutions of

determined in Theorems A and C can be continued to a global (i.e. defined for any

) simple curve without bifurcation points. We conjecture that this curve contains in fact all the solutions of
{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}
, that

and

are monotonic all along the branch, and in particular that
\lambda_{*}(\Omega,p)=+\infty
for these domains.
Interestingly enough, in the same spirit of
[

, this would yield a new description of the global solutions branch of Gelfand problems first pushed forward in
[

]
.

2.
Preliminary results

We will use several well known facts, see
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p\in[1,p_{{}_{N}})
. For any
\overline{\lambda}>0
there exist
\overline{\alpha}=\overline{\alpha}(r,\Omega,\overline{\lambda},p,N)>-\infty
and
C_{1}=C_{1}(r,\Omega,\overline{\lambda},p,N)<+\infty
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\geq\overline{\alpha},\qquad\|\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\|_{C^{2,r}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C_{1}

for any solution
(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})
of
{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}
with
\lambda\in[0,\overline{\lambda}\,]
.

For fixed
\lambda\geq 0
and
p\in(1,p_{{}_{N}})
, let
(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})
be a solution of
{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}
, we recall the following notation from the introduction,

<\phi>_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}=\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}V_{\lambda}\phi}{\int_{\Omega}V_{\lambda}}\quad\mbox{and }\quad[\phi]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}=\phi\,-<\phi>_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\quad\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Let us recall that we are always concerned with classical solutions and
p>1
, whence in particular

(2.2)

\displaystyle V_{\lambda}=[\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}+\lambda\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}]_{+}^{p-1}\in C^{r}(\overline{\Omega}),

for some
r\in(0,1)
. The support of
\mbox{\Large\textrho}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}
is by definition
\overline{\Omega_{+}}
,
whence, if
\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\geq 0
then
\Omega_{+}=\Omega
while if
\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}<0
then, since
\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}
is continuous, we have
\Omega_{+}\Subset\Omega
.

Consider the linear operator
L_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\colon C^{2,r}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\to C^{r}(\overline{\Omega})
defined by

(2.3)

\displaystyle L_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}[\phi]=-\Delta\phi-\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}V_{\lambda}[\phi]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}

with
\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}=\lambda p
.
We say that
\sigma=\sigma(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})\in\mathbb{R}
is an eigenvalue of
L_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}
if the equation

(2.4)

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi=(\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}+\sigma)V_{\lambda}[\phi]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},

admits a non-trivial weak solution
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and the corresponding eigenspace is denoted by
\Eigen(L_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda};\sigma)
.
Note that
L_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}
involves not only nonlocal terms but also a weight which may vanish on a large set. This situation has been
discussed in
[
3
]
after
[
8
]
as we shortly illustrate below.

If we let
G
denote the Green’s function for
-\Delta
with Dirichlet boundary condition
and apply it as a convolution kernel to (
2.4
), then we see that,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\phi=\frac{\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}+\sigma}{\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}}\;G*\left(\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}V_{\lambda}[\phi]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\right).

We consider the operator
T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, to be defined as follows,

(2.6)

\displaystyle T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}(\phi)\coloneqq G*\left(\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}V_{\lambda}[\phi]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\right),

so that (
2.4
) is equivalent to

(2.7)

\displaystyle T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}(\phi)=\mu\phi,\qquad\mbox{ with }\mu=\frac{\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}}{\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}+\sigma}.

In other words,
\phi\in\Eigen(L_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda};\sigma)
iff
\phi\in\Eigen(T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda};\frac{\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}}{\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}+\sigma})
.
Thus it suffices to understand the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of
T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}
, where the advantage is that it is a
linear self-adjoint compact operator on the Hilbert space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
equipped with the inner product

(2.8)

\displaystyle<\xi,\eta>_{H^{1}_{0}}=\int_{\Omega}<\nabla\xi,\;\nabla\eta>,\qquad\forall\xi,\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

see
[
3
]
for details.
As a consequence, the spectrum of
T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}
,
\Spect(T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})
, consists of countably many real nonzero eigenvalues and perhaps also zero.
Each nonzero eigenvalue has finite multiplicity, and the nonzero eigenvalues have zero as the unique accumulation point.
From (
2.4
) it follows that
\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}+\sigma\geq 0
, hence
\Spect(T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
We may thus list the nonzero eigenvalues as follows,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\mu_{3}\geq\cdots>0,\quad\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mu_{j}=0,

and denote the corresponding eigenfunctions by
\phi_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, which satisfy

(2.10)

\displaystyle\delta_{jk}=<\phi_{j},\phi_{k}>_{H^{1}_{0}}=\int_{\Omega}<\nabla\phi_{j},\;\nabla\phi_{k}>,\qquad\forall j,k\geq 1.

We collect the set of nonzero eigenvalues in
\Spect(T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})\setminus\left\{0\right\}
and denote

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}\coloneqq\overline{\oplus_{j\geq 1}\Eigen(T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda};\mu_{j})}=\overline{\Span\left\{\phi_{j}\mid j\geq 1\right\}}

where the closure is taken in the
H^{1}_{0}
norm. This is a closed subspace of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
3
]
)

(i)

0\in\Spect(T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})
iff
\alpha<0
.

(ii)

If
\alpha\geq 0
, then

(2.12)

\displaystyle\Spect(T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})=\left\{\mu_{j}\mid j\geq 1\right\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)=\mathcal{H}_{1}.

(iii)

If
\alpha<0
, then

(2.13)

\displaystyle\Spect(T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})=\left\{0\right\}\cup\left\{\mu_{j}\mid j\geq 1\right\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)=\Eigen(T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda};0)\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1}.

Moreover, the restriction of any function in
\mathcal{H}_{1}
to the set
\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega_{+}}
is harmonic,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\Eigen(T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda};0)=\left\{\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\mid\phi|_{\Omega_{+}}=\,<\phi>_{\lambda}\mbox{a.e. in }\Omega_{+}\right\},

and

\phi\in\Eigen(T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda};0)\Rightarrow{\mbox{\rm$V_{\lambda}$}}[\phi]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}=0\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega.

Remark 2.3
.

In the orthogonal decomposition, we will use

(2.15)

\displaystyle P_{0}\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to\Eigen(T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda};0)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad P_{1}\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{H}_{1}

to denote the corresponding orthogonal projections.
More precisely, for any
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have that

(2.16)

\displaystyle\psi=\psi_{0}+\psi_{1}=P_{0}\psi+P_{1}\psi=P_{0}\psi+\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\beta_{j}\phi_{j}

with the Fourier coefficients

(2.17)

\displaystyle\beta_{j}=<\psi,\phi_{j}>_{H^{1}_{0}}=\int_{\Omega}<\nabla\psi,\;\nabla\phi_{j}>=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}}{\mu_{j}}V_{\lambda}[\phi_{j}]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\psi=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}}{\mu_{j}}V_{\lambda}[\phi_{j}]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}[\psi]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}.

Recall that each
\mu_{j}>0
of
T_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}
corresponds to an eigenvalue
\sigma_{j}
of

3.
Variational Solutions

We discuss a spectral estimate for variational solutions. In fact, solutions of
{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}
can be found (
[
10
]
)
minimizing the free energy

which, takes the form,

(3.1)

\inf\{\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(\rho),\rho\in\mathcal{P}\},\quad\mathcal{P}=\left\{\rho\in L^{1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)\,|\,\int_{\Omega}\rho=1,\;\;\rho\geq 0\;\mbox{a.e. in}\;\Omega\right\}

It has been proved in
[
10
]

see p.422 in
[
10
]

Denoting by

)

which is, putting

{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

be a variational solution of
{\textbf{(}\mathbf{P}\textbf{)}_{\mathbf{\lambda}}}
. Then
\sigma_{1}(\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda},\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})\geq 0

Proof.

Recall that
V_{\lambda}=[\alpha_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}+\lambda\psi_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}]_{+}^{p-1}
.
Let

be any minimizer of the variational problem (
3.1
), we consider variations of the form,

such that

. Then the Taylor formula shows that,

0\leq J_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}(\mbox{\Large\textrho}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}+\varepsilon f)-J_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}(\mbox{\Large\textrho}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\mathcal{D}^{2}J_{\lambda}(\mbox{\Large\textrho}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\lambda})[f,f\,]+\mbox{\rm o}(\varepsilon^{2}),

for any

is not of class

be a first eigenfunction whose eigenvalue is

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathcal{A}(\phi_{1})}{m_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}}=<[\phi_{1}]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}^{2}>_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}-\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}<[\phi_{1}]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}G[V_{\lambda}[\phi_{1}]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}]>_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}=<[\phi_{1}]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}^{2}>_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}-\frac{\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}}{\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}+\sigma_{1}}<[\phi_{1}]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\phi_{1}>_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}

\displaystyle<[\phi_{1}]_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}^{2}>_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}+\sigma_{1}}

5.
On the uniqueness of solutions of the Emden problem

In this section we obtain two results related to the uniqueness of solutions of the Emden equation. The first one is a new proof of the uniqueness on
\mathbb{D}_{N}
which comes as an immediate consequence of Theorem B. The second one is a uniqueness result which follows from some assumption about

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
p\in(1,p_{N})

1
) admits a unique solution.

Proof.

Here
\lambda_{+}=\lambda_{+}(\mathbb{D}_{N},p)
. After a suitable scaling it is enough to prove the uniqueness of solutions of (

replaced by

)

on

for

N
