Endosomal trafficking factor RAB5c acts as a host restriction factor against FAdV-4 propagation through interaction with ORF1B
Authors: Huichao Gao, Keying Liu, Menghui Ma, Qinge Chang, Saimin Zhai, Jianli Li, Xinwei Wang, Xia Yang, Jun Zhao, Xiaozhan Zhang, Zeng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rab small GTPases (RABs), a family of key regulators governing intracellular membrane trafficking and endosomal dynamics, are extensively implicated in diverse physiological and pathological processes. RAB5c, a critical isoform of the RAB5 subfamily that controls early endosome maturation and vesicle transport, also exerts indispensable functions during viral infection. Previous mass spectrometry analysis preliminarily identified RAB5c as a potential interacting partner of the ORF1B protein encoded by fowl adenovirus serotype 4 (FAdV-4). In this study, we further verified the interaction between RAB5c and ORF1B using co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assays, and confocal microscopy revealed that these two proteins were co-localized in the cytoplasm of leghorn male hepatoma (LMH) cells. Furthermore, the expression pattern of RAB5c and its regulatory role during FAdV-4 infection was evaluated. Results demonstrated that both mRNA and protein levels of RAB5c were significantly upregulated upon FAdV-4 infection. Notably, RAB5c acted as a negative regulator of FAdV-4 replication in LMH cells. Viral titers and viral protein synthesis were remarkably reduced in RAB5c-overexpressing cells, but significantly increased in RAB5c-knockdown cells compared to the control ones. Collectively, these results identified RAB5c as a crucial factor that antagonized FAdV-4 replication.

Introduction

Fowl adenoviruses (FAdVs), the main causative pathogens of many clinical diseases in poultry, belong to the genus Aviadenovirus within the family Adenoviridae. Refer to restriction enzyme digest pattern, serum cross-neutralization test, and molecular criteria, FAdVs could be divided into 5 species (A to E) with 12 serotypes (FAdV-1 to 8a and 8b to 11) (Hess, 2000). Thereinto, FAdV-4 received extensive attention since it caused hepatitis-hydropericardium syndrome (HHS) in China (Liu et al., 2016). HHS, also known as “Ankara disease”, is first reported in Ankara, Pakistan, in 1987 (Anjum et al., 1989). It is characterized by a flaccid heart with accumulation of straw-colored fluid in the pericardial sac and necrotic foci in the liver. Since June 2015, HHS has been widely reported across major poultry-raising provinces in China, such as Anhui, Hubei, Shandong, Jiangsu, Henan, and Zhejiang, leading to substantial economic losses (Li et al., 2017).

FAdV-4 is a non-enveloped double-stranded DNA virus with an approximately 43 kb genome. The virion contains 240 hexon proteins, 12 penton bases at the capsid vertices, and two fiber proteins (fiber1 and fiber2) protruding from each penton base. Fiber1 initiates infection through binding to the coxsackievirus and adenovirus receptor (CAR) (Wang et al., 2020b), while fiber2 possesses dominant B-cell epitopes and is capable of eliciting neutralizing antibody responses (Dong et al., 2026). Based on the epitope composition and serum neutralizing properties, hexon protein serves as a key target for molecular epidemiological investigations (Kang et al., 2025). And penton base mediates viral endocytosis by interacting with cellular integrins (Wang et al., 2017). Notably, fiber2 and hexon are closely related to virulence for highly pathogenic FAdV-4 strains (Zhang et al., 2018). Apart from these well-known structural proteins, recent studies highlight key roles of non-structural proteins (Nsps) in viral replication and immune evasion. Gallus anti morte 1 (GAM-1) protein, a well conserved Nsp among FAdVs (Kaján et al., 2012), acts synergistically with ORF22 to promote efficient viral replication in LMH cells. Deletion of GAM-1 alone reduces viral plaque size, while its combined deletion with ORF22 severely impairs viral growth (Liu et al., 2022). Another research highlights the function of 100 K, which interacts with host chaperon protein heat shock cognate 71 kDa protein (HSC70) to facilitate efficient viral replication (Gao et al., 2022). Our previous study finds that ORF1B is the unique Nsp for FAdV-4 (Gao et al., 2023), and it antagonizes type I interferon (IFN-I) expression via inhibiting interferon regulatory factor 7 (IRF7) nuclear translocation (Gao et al., 2025). These results underscore the diverse functions of Nsps in promoting viral replication and modulating host interactions.

Ras-related protein Rab-5c (RAB5c), a small GTPase of the Rab family, acts as a crucial regulator of early endocytic biogenesis, membrane trafficking, and endocytic vesicle fusion (Carney et al., 2005). It cycles between an inactive GDP-bound and an active GTP-bound state. Given its critical role in facilitating endocytosis, a major route for viral entry into host cells, RAB5c has been reported to play multifaceted roles in viral pathogenesis. Studies demonstrate that it promotes the replication of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and adenovirus (ADV) in respiratory epithelial cells by inducing autophagy via binding to Beclin1 (Wang et al., 2024). Research on singapore grouper iridovirus (SGIV) suggests RAB5c is essential for virus endosomal trafficking and simultaneously modulating cellular immune responses (Wang et al., 2020a). These results suggest that RAB5c serves as a key host factor to regulate diverse virus infections.

Our previous research identified 2502 cellular proteins capable of interacting with ORF1B (Wang et al., 2026). Among these candidates, RAB5c stood out prominently due to its key functions in membrane trafficking and endocytic vesicle fusion, processes essential for virus infection. In this study, therefore, we investigated the regulatory role of RAB5c during FAdV-4 infection.

Materials and methods

Cell, viruses, plasmids, and commercial antibodies

Leghorn male hepatocellular (LMH) cells were cultured in DME/F-12 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1×penicillin and streptomycin at 37°C in 5% CO2. FAdV-4 strain CH/HNJZ/2015 (GenBank NO. KU558760) and the chimeric virus rHNJZ-△1966/EGFP, with EGFP gene being inserted into a 1966-bp deletion on the right end of the CH/HNJZ/2015 genome, were preserved in our lab. Recombinant ORF1B expression plasmids, namely pCAGGS-HA-ORF1B and pEGFP-ORF1B, were constructed and maintained in our laboratory. Commercial antibodies included rabbit anti-HA monoclonal antibody (mAb) (TT0050M, Ab-mart), mouse anti-myc mAb (AE010, ABclonal), rabbit anti-GAPDH mAb (A19056, ABclonal), goat anti-rabbit IgG-HRP (AB_2713951, Ab-mart), and goat anti-mouse IgG HRP (AB_2713950, Ab-mart) were used in this study. Home-made rabbit anti-ORF1B polyclonal antibody (pAb) was generated and preserved in our laboratory.

Plasmid construction and antibody generation

Chicken RAB5c (GenBank NO. NM_204525.1) was amplified from cDNA of LMH cells and subsequently inserted into pCMV-myc vector linearized with EcoR I and Xho I enzymes by using homologous recombination. Rabbit anti-RAB5c polyclonal antibody was prepared according to previously established protocols. Briefly, New Zealand White rabbits were intramuscularly immunized in the hind limbs with 200 μg of pCMV-myc-RAB5c plasmid for three rounds, with an interval of two weeks between each immunization (days 1, 14 and 28). One week after the final booster injection, cardiac blood samples were harvested from anesthetized rabbits, and serum was separated and collected for subsequent experiments.

Co-immunoprecipitation and western blot

LMH cells cultured in 6-well plates were co-transfected with the following plasmid combinations at a total amount of 2 μg/well: pCMV-myc-RAB5c+pCAGGS-HA-ORF1B, pCMV-myc-RAB5c+pCAGGS-HA, or pCMV-myc +pCAGGS-HA-ORF1B. At 48 hours post-transfection (hpt), transfected cells were lysed in ice-cold IP lysis buffer (P0013, Beyotime) for 30 min. The cell lysates were then centrifuged to harvest supernatants, which were incubated with anti-HA or anti-Myc antibodies at 4°C overnight. Immunocomplexes were subsequently captured by incubation with Protein G agarose beads (Roche, Switzerland) for 8 h. Following five washes with PBS buffer, the bound proteins were resolved by 12% SDS-PAGE and electrotransferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (GE Healthcare, USA). Membranes were blocked with 5% skimmed milk at 37°C for 1 h, followed by incubation with corresponding primary and secondary antibodies. Finally, immunoreactive protein bands were detected by using chemiluminescence detection reagents (SQ202, Epizyme).

Confocal analysis

LMH cells cultured in confocal culture dish were co-transfected with the following plasmid combinations at a total amount of 2 μg/well: pCMV-myc-RAB5c+pEGFP-ORF1B, pCMV-myc-RAB5c+pEGFP, and pCMV-myc+pEGFP. After 48 h, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde at room temperature for 30 min, followed by being permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X‑100 for 30 min and then blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) in PBS for 1 h. Thereafter, the cells were then sequentially incubated with mouse anti-myc mAb and Multi-rAb® CoraLite® Plus 594-goat anti-mouse recombinant secondary antibody (H+L) (RGAM004, proteintech) at 37°C for 1 h. Finally, cell nuclei were counterstained with 4′,6‑diamidino‑2‑phenylindole (DAPI, Solarbio) for 15 min at room temperature. The subcellular localization of green and red fluorescence was observed using a confocal laser scanning microscope (Zeiss LSM 880, Germany).

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from LMH cells using RNA simple kit (DP419, TIANGEN), and reverse-transcribed into cDNA with HiScript IV RT SuperMix for qPCR with gDNA wiper (R423, Vazyme). The mRNA expression levels of RAB5c and ORF1B were specifically amplified using the primers listed in Table 1, following the manufacturer’s protocol of SupRealQ Ultra Hunter SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Q713, Vazyme). Transcript levels were normalized to the expression of the endogenous reference gene GAPDH. Knockdown of RAB5c via siRNA

Three small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting chicken RAB5c and a non-targeting control siRNA (siNC) were designed and synthesized by Shanghai Sangon Biotech. LMH cells were individually transfected with RAB5c-targeting siRNAs or siNC, respectively. Cells were harvested at 24 hpt for qRT-PCR detection and 48 hpt for western blot analysis to assess the knockdown efficiency.

Growth kinetics of FAdV-4 in LMH cells

LMH cells cultured in 96-well plates were infected with 10-fold serial dilutions of virus and incubate for 48 h at 37°C. Virus titers were then calculated using the Reed-Muench method (Ma et al., 2023).

Statistical analysis

Data were presented as mean values with standard deviations (SD). Statistical differences were analyzed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test using GraphPad Prism 7.0 software. The data was statistically significant with P value less than 0.05.

Results

RAB5c interacts with FAdV-4 ORF1B

To verify that RAB5c served as a binding partner of ORF1B, LMH cells were co-transfected with pCMV-myc-RAB5c and pCAGGS-HA-ORF1B, or their respective empty vectors. Cells lysates were subjected to immunoprecipitation with either anti-HA or anti-myc antibody to pull down the protein complex. Results demonstrated that a clear band with approximately 25 kDa was co-immunoprecipitated with HA-ORF1B (Fig. 1A). Conversely, ORF1B of about 15 kDa was successfully detected, when myc-RAB5c was used as the bait (Fig. 1B). To further validate the interaction between ORF1B and RAB5c, LMH cells were transfected with pEGFP-ORF1B and pCMV-myc-RAB5c either individually or in combination. The cells were then fixed and immunostained with specific antibodies to detect the subcellular distribution of the two proteins. Although myc-RAB5c expressed alone was evenly distributed throughout the cell, it mainly located to the cytoplasm when co-expressed with EGFP-ORF1B (Fig. 1C), exhibiting obvious co‑localization in this compartment. FAdV-4 infection promotes RAB5c expression

To investigate the expression kinetics of RAB5c during FAdV-4 infection, LMH cells were either infected or non-infected with CH/HNJZ/2015, and collected at indicated timepoints for qRT-PCR and western blot analysis. As shown in Fig. 2A, transcriptional level of RAB5c gradually increased over the course of infection, with mRNA levels significantly elevated compared to those in control group at 12, 24, 36, and 48 hours post-infection (hpi). Consistently, RAB5c protein expression was also dramatically increased followed FAdV-4 infection relative to the uninfected group (Fig. 2B). RAB5c inhibited FAdV-4 replication in vitro

To evaluate the regulatory role of RAB5c during FAdV-4 infection, LMH cells transfected with pCMV-myc-RAB5c or pCMV-myc were infected with the chimeric virus rHNJZ-△1966/EGFP at an MOI of 0.01. Green fluorescence was observed and recorded at 12 h intervals. Although the fluorescence intensity gradually increased over the course of infection, cells overexpressing myc‑RAB5c displayed notably weaker fluorescence signals compared with the empty vector‑transfected and non-transfected control groups, particularly at 48 hpi (Fig. 3A). To further verify these findings, LMH cells subjected to the same transfection protocol were infected with CH/HNJZ/2015 at an MOI of 0.01. Cells and supernatants were separately collected to assess virus replication via western blot and 50% tissue culture infective dose (TCID50), respectively. Results showed that the production of ORF1B protein was markedly decreased in RAB5c-overexpressing group compared to both the myc-vector-transfected group and the mock group at all four tested timepoints (Fig. 3B). Moreover, viral titers in myc-RAB5c-transfected group only reached 2.15, 4.6, and 5.65 log10(TCID50/100 μl) at 24, 36, and 48 hpi, respectively, which were substantially lower than the corresponding titers of 3.41, 5.96, and 7.01 log10(TCID50/100 μl) in myc-vector-transfected group (Fig. 3C). Interfering RAB5c promoted FAdV-4 replication in vitro

To validate the above results in a reverse manner, the growth kinetics of FAdV-4 in LMH cells were examined following RAB5c knockdown. LMH cells individually transfected with three siRNAs targeting RAB5c or a negative control siNC were collected at 24 and 48 hpt, qRT-PCR and western blot were performed to evaluate the knockdown efficiency. Data found that only siRAB5c-2 reduced RAB5c expression at mRNA (Fig. 4A) and protein level (Fig. 4B) by approximately 50%, which was therefore selected for subsequent experiments. LMH cells transfected with siRAB5c-2 for 24 h were infected with rHNJZ-△1966/EGFP at an MOI of 0.01. Although green fluorescence intensity gradually increased in all groups, the accumulation rate in RAB5c knockdown group was significantly higher than that in siNC-transfected group and mock group (Fig. 4C). Subsequently, similarly transfected LMH cells were infected with CH/HNJZ/2015 at an MOI of 0.01, and samples harvested at various timepoints were analyzed to assess virus replication. While ORF1B protein accumulated progressively over the course of infection in all three groups, its upregulated expression in RAB5c-knockdown group became increasingly prominent with prolonged infection (Fig. 4D). Consistently, virus titers in siRAB5c-transfected cells reached 6.19 and 8.30 log10(TCID50/100 μl) at 36 and 48 hpi, respectively, which were significantly higher than the corresponding titers of 5.32 and 7.14 log10(TCID50/100 μl) in siNC-transfected group (Fig. 4E). Discussion

As obligate intracellular parasites, viruses rely entirely on host cellular machinery for their replication. Cellular proteins, as key functional mediators, play essential roles in viral infection. Currently, a variety of host factors have been reported to participate in FAdV-4 infection. For instance, heat shock protein 70 (Hsp70) forms a complex with DnaJ heat shock protein 40 family member C 7 (DnaJC7) to indirectly bind viral hexon protein, and mediates hexon degradation through the autophagy pathway to negatively regulate FAdV-4 replication (Cao et al., 2022). Receptor for activated C kinase 1 (RACK1) interacts with hexon protein and targets it for degradation through the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, thereby exerting a significant inhibitory effect on viral replication (Li et al., 2026). BCL-2 associated athanogene 3 (BAG3), another host protein that binds to hexon, is upregulated by hexon via activating the p38 signaling pathway, which in turn induces autophagy to promote viral replication in LMH cells (Li et al., 2023). Additionally, karyopherin alpha 3/4 (KPNA3/4) protein interacts with the N-terminal 1-40 aa domain of fiber2 to effectively assist viral replication, and deletion of this interaction domain results in remarkable attenuation of the virus both in vitro and in vivo (Xie et al., 2021). Similarly, we identified RAB5c as an antiviral host factor against FAdV‑4 replication via interacting with ORF1B protein.

As a small GTPase predominantly localized to the cell membrane, early endosomes, and phagosomes, RAB5c is deeply involved in multiple cellular processes such as clathrin-mediated endocytosis, early endosome fusion, and vesicular trafficking (Redpath et al., 2019; Tu et al., 2022), all of which are essential steps during viral infection. It’s well established that most viruses depending on clathrin-mediated endocytosis are transported to RAB5-positive early endosomes, which serve as a central sorting hub for subsequent intracellular trafficking (Taylor et al., 2011). This conserved pathway has been well documented in diverse viruses. Dengue virus (DEV) is internalized via clathrin-coated pits and subsequently traffics to RAB5-positive early endosomes before fusion (Van der Schaar et al., 2008). Likewise, rabies virus (RABV) colocalizes with RAB5 in neuronal cells during entry, indicating its transit through early endosomal compartments (Ahmad et al., 2017). African swine fever virus (ASFV) also employs clathrin- and dynamin-dependent endocytosis, and its internalized vesicles rapidly recruit RAB5 to form early endosomes during the early stage of infection (Alonso et al., 2012). Therefore, the RAB5-labeled early endosome serves as a common and indispensable intermediate compartment for numerous viruses following clathrin-mediated internalization.

In case of FAdV-4, it initially binds to the D2 domain of coxsackievirus and adenovirus receptor (CAR) on the cell surface via the knob domain of fiber1 protein (Pan et al., 2020), followed by being internalized through cholesterol‑dependent clathrin‑mediated endocytosis in LMH cells (Wang et al., 2023). Whether RAB5c antagonized FAdV-4 replication via regulating endocytosis requires further investigation. Additionally, several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. Since all conclusions were drawn solely from LMH cells, the physiological role of RAB5c in restricting FAdV‑4 infection in vivo remains to be verified in chickens. More importantly, although the interaction between RAB5c and ORF1B was confirmed, the precise binding domains and detailed molecular mechanisms whether RAB5c-mediated inhibition of FAdV-4 replication via utilizing ORF1B remain to be fully elucidated.

Collectively, our findings identify RAB5c functions as a novel negative regulator of FAdV-4 infection. This discovery not only expands the repertoire of known host factors against FAdV-4, but also provides a new perspective for the development of antiviral targets and strategies.
