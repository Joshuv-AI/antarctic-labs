Epidemiological characteristics of severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) in China, 2010–2024: a fifteen-year national surveillance analysis
Authors: Qian Ren, Hui-Jie Qin, Si-Han Li, Shi-Jian Zhou, Jun-Yuan Chen, Di Mu, Yu Li, Qiu-Lan Chen, Yan-Ping Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSevere fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) incidence has risen steadily in China since the disease was first identified in 2009, with both case numbers and the geographic scope of reporting expanding over time. This study analyzed national surveillance data from 2010 to 2024 to characterize the evolving epidemiological patterns of SFTS.MethodsThis retrospective descriptive study included clinically diagnosed and laboratory-confirmed SFTS cases from China Information System for Disease Control and Prevention (CISDCP) (2010–2024). Joinpoint regression was used to analyze incidence trends. Case fatality rate (CFR) differences were assessed by chi-squared or Fisher’s exact tests, and the temporal trend was assessed by linear regression.ResultsDuring 2010–2024, a total of 30 provinces reported 34,055 cases. From 2010 to 2024, the incidence rate of SFTS in China showed a fluctuating upward trend, with an average annual percent change (AAPC) of 43.28% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 43.23%–43.33%;P < 0.01). Most cases were sporadic, with some regional clustering and rapid geographic expansion to previously unaffected areas. The number of reported cases in Anhui Province began to rise rapidly in 2022, making it the province with the highest number of reported cases in China in 2023 and 2024. The average CFR was 4.79%, and the CFR increased with age, reaching 9.15% among those aged 85 years and above.ConclusionsDuring the period from 2010 to 2024, SFTS in China exhibited a marked increase in reported incidence and considerable geographical expansion. The disease is no longer confined to traditional high-risk areas; the rapid rise in cases in province such as Anhui suggests a critical need to re-evaluate the ecological and social factors driving its transmission. Although the overall population CFR showed a declining trend, mortality risk among elderly subgroups still needs attention.

Background

Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is an acute viral infectious disease mainly characterized by fever and thrombocytopenia caused by Dabie bandavirus (SFTS virus) [1]. It is also characterized by obvious bleeding tendencies accompanied by leukopenia and multiple organ dysfunction [2]. Clinical manifestations are often atypical; no licensed vaccine or specific antiviral therapy is currently available, and clinical management mainly relies on supportive care [3, 4]. SFTS was classified as a nationally reportable disease in China in 2010 and was listed by the World Health Organization(WHO) as one of the top 10 priority infectious diseases in the 2017 annual review of diseases prioritized under the Research and Development Blueprint [5, 6]. The WHO released a prioritization of future pathogens of concern on July 30, 2024, in which the priority pathogens of concern for the Western Pacific region include the Dabie bandavirus [7]. This designation underscores the global health importance of SFTS and the need for up-to-date epidemiological data to inform international surveillance efforts. Effective April 1, 2026, SFTS was reclassified as a Class B notifiable infectious disease [8].

Tick bites are the main mode of transmission of the disease. Transmission can also occur through contact with the blood, bloody secretions, excretions, or contaminated materials of infected patients, leading to human-to-human transmission and clusters of cases in household or healthcare settings [9]. People who live and work in mountainous, hilly, or forested areas in endemic regions face a higher risk of infection [10]. Healthcare workers, family members, visitors of patients, and mortuary personnel are at higher risk of infection if they do not adhere to standard protective measures [11].

In addition to China, locally infected cases have been reported in Japan, South Korea, Vietnam, Thailand, Myanmar, Pakistan, and other regions, of which Japan and South Korea have reported the highest number of cases [12–18]. Due to climate change, there has been an expansion of the potential habitat of ticks to wider geographical locations, posing a risk of introducing the exotic tick species that may carry the SFTS virus [19, 20].

Previous national analyses have characterized the epidemiology of SFTS in China for periods through 2021 or 2023 [21–23]. However, no study has examined the complete 15-year surveillance record from SFTS’s initial emergence through 2024, a period that now includes record case and death counts, continued geographic expansion to 30 of 31 mainland provinces. To address this gap, this study analyzed national SFTS surveillance data from 2010 to 2024 to characterize temporal trends, geographic expansion, seasonal patterns, population distribution, time intervals between onset and diagnosis, and case fatality rates (CFR).

Materials and methods

Study design

This was a retrospective descriptive study of national surveillance data.

Data source

SFTS data and annual population data were obtained from the China Information System for Disease Control and Prevention (CISDCP), excluding Hong Kong SAR, Macao SAR, and Taiwan Province [24]. We included cases with onset dates between January 1, 2010, and December 31, 2024. Each case was confirmed by laboratory testing or diagnosed clinically [21, 22, 25]. Case classification followed the diagnosis and treatment scheme for severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (2023 Edition), which had been consistently applied in practice before its official release [26]. The dataset included demographic variables (age, sex, occupation, residence), dates of onset, diagnosis, and death, and case classification.

Statistical analysis

Data were processed, statistically analyzed, and visualized using Microsoft Excel 2016 and R software (version 4.4.2). No observations were excluded from this analysis.

Joinpoint regression was used to analyze temporal trends in annual SFTS incidence rates. A log-linear model was fitted, allowing a maximum of two joinpoints. The optimal number of joinpoints was selected using the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). The annual percent change (APC) for each segment was calculated as (exp(β) − 1) × 100%, and the average annual percent change (AAPC) was computed as the weighted average of segment-specific APCs. The 95% confidence intervals (CI) for APCs and AAPC were derived using the Delta method, with statistical inference under the t-distribution. Analyses were performed using R software version 4.4.2.

The onset-to-diagnosis interval was calculated as the number of days between symptom onset date and diagnosis date, as recorded in CISDCP. Given the right-skewed distribution (except in 2010), the median and interquartile range (IQR; 25th percentile [P25]–75th percentile [P75]) were used as summary measures, as they are robust to extreme values.

Deaths were identified based on CISDCP records for reported SFTS cases. The CFR was calculated as deaths/reported cases × 100%. The 95% CI were estimated using the Wilson score method. Group differences were assessed by chi-squared or Fisher’s exact tests, and the temporal trend was assessed by linear regression.

Results

National temporal trends

From 2010 to 2024, a total of 34,055 SFTS cases were reported across 30 provinces in China. The number of reported cases (6,602 cases) and deaths (306 cases) in 2024 were both the highest levels since the discovery of this disease, with an increase of 30.4% and 60.2% respectively, compared to the reported cases (5061 cases) and deaths (191 cases) in 2023 (Fig. 1).

From 2010 to 2024, the incidence rate of SFTS in China showed a fluctuating upward trend, with an AAPC of 43.28%, and the difference was statistically significant (95% CI: 43.23%–43.33%; P < 0.01). Joinpoint regression identified 2012 as the single inflection point. The incidence rate increased rapidly from 2010 to 2012 (APC = 218.14%, 95% CI: 217.75%–218.52%; P < 0.01), followed by a sustained increase from 2012 to 2024 (APC = 14.14%, 95% CI: 14.09%–14.19%; P < 0.01).

Geographic expansion

The geographic range of reported SFTS cases expanded from 17 counties in 5 provinces in 2010 to 459 counties in 24 provinces in 2024, and exhibited geographically discontinuous emergence of reported cases. By 2024, cases had been reported in 30 of China’s 31 mainland provincial-level administrative divisions, with Ningxia being the sole exception, cumulatively affecting 759 counties nationwide (Fig. 3).

Changes in provincial burden

From 2010 to 2024, cases were mainly concentrated in 7 provinces, including Shandong (9,547 cases), Anhui (7,494 cases), Henan (7,148 cases), Hubei (5,068 cases), Liaoning (1,857 cases), Jiangsu (1,384 cases), and Zhejiang (1,153 cases), with the cumulative number of reported cases accounting for 98.8% of all nationally reported cases. The annual case distribution across these seven provinces exhibited distinct temporal dynamics (Fig. 4). In the early phase (2010–2013), case numbers remained relatively low across all provinces. Shandong and Henan experienced rapid increases beginning in 2014, with Henan reaching its peak during 2015–2016 (976 and 879 cases, respectively). Shandong maintained a relatively high level throughout the period, with a notable surge in 2024 (1,658 cases). The number of reported cases in Anhui Province began to rise rapidly in 2022, making it the province with the highest number of reported cases in China in 2023 and 2024. The number of reported cases in Anhui rose from 544 in 2021 to 841 in 2022, 1,416 in 2023, and reached 1,777 in 2024.

Seasonal patterns

The incidence of SFTS is seasonal, with a gradual increase in the number of cases from March each year, a peak in May-July, and a decreasing trend thereafter; a secondary increase in case counts was observed in October in some provinces. The cumulative number of reported cases in the months of April-October from 2010 to 2024 was 32,963, accounting for 96.8% of the cumulative number of reported cases in the whole year from 2010 to 2024 (Fig. 5). Different provinces exhibited distinct seasonal patterns. Liaoning showed a later peak (July-September), while Shandong peaked during May-August. A secondary increase in case counts was observed in October in Anhui, Hubei, and Jiangsu provinces.

Population distribution

Cases were reported in all age groups, mainly in people aged 40–84 years (94.3%), mainly farmers (83.5%), slightly more females than males, and the male-to-female ratio was 0.89:1. The CFR increased with age (Fig. 6). Notably, 37 healthcare workers (0.1%) were among reported cases.

Time intervals between onset and diagnosis

With the exception of 2010 (skewness − 0.12), the distribution of onset-to-diagnosis intervals was right-skewed in all years (skewness range: 2.34–13.97), supporting the use of median rather than mean. The overall median onset-to-diagnosis interval over the 15-year period was 7 days (Interquartile Range [IQR]: 4–11). Annual median intervals and IQRs are shown in Fig. 7. A declining trend was observed from 2010 to 2012, followed by an increase in 2014. From 2015 to 2024, the interval had decreased again, reaching a median of 5 days.

CFR analysis

From 2010 to 2024, a total of 1,632 deaths were recorded among 34,055 reported cases, yielding an overall CFR of 4.79%. The national CFR declined significantly from 12.68% in 2010 to 4.63% in 2024 (linear trend, P = 0.004; Fig. 8). The CFR increased with age, from 0.29% in the 30–34 age group to 9.15% in those aged ≥ 85 years (P < 0.001). Males had a higher CFR than females (5.41% vs. 4.24%, P < 0.001). Laboratory-confirmed cases showed a higher CFR than clinically diagnosed cases (5.55% vs. 1.95%, P < 0.001). Substantial provincial variation was observed (P < 0.001), with CFRs among seven provinces with the highest case counts ranging from 2.25% (Henan) to 11.97% (Zhejiang).

Discussion

Compared with previous national analyses covering earlier periods, the present study extends the surveillance period through 2024, capturing the record-high numbers of cases and deaths reported in that year, the emergence of Anhui Province as the leading contributor to the national burden, and the continued geographic expansion.

This study analyzes 15 years of SFTS surveillance data and reveals that SFTS in China is characterized not only by a fluctuating upward trend in reported incidence but also by significant geographical expansion. From 2010 to 2024, a total of 34,055 SFTS cases were reported across 30 provinces. The number of affected counties expanded from 17 in 2010 to 459 in 2024, with a cumulative total of 759 counties ever reporting cases. Both the case count and death count in 2024 reached record highs, with the number of deaths increasing by 60.2% compared to 2023, signaling a worsening disease burden. We note that the observed increase in reported incidence may partly reflect improvements in surveillance sensitivity, diagnostic capacity, and clinician awareness over the 15-year period, particularly during the early years of the study. It is worth noting that this expansion did not spread uniformly but showed characteristics of geographically discontinuous emergence of reported cases. We hypothesize that the geographically discontinuous emergence of reported cases may be driven not only by ecological factors such as climate change, which expands suitable tick habitats, but also by migratory birds and socioeconomic activities such as agricultural trade, which may introduce ticks or infected animal hosts [19, 27, 28]. In 2024, the proportion of laboratory-confirmed cases reached its highest level at 95.3%, reflecting the enhancement of laboratory diagnostic capacity.

Joinpoint regression analysis revealed one inflection point in the incidence of SFTS in China in 2012, dividing the study period into two distinct phases. The rapid increase from 2010 to 2012 (APC = 218.14%) likely reflects, at least in part, the scale-up of surveillance and diagnostic capacity after SFTS was classified as a notifiable disease, rather than a true biological increase in transmission [21]. The period 2012–2024 was a sustained growth phase, with an APC of 14.14%. The increase in cases may reflect the spread of the epidemic, improvements in diagnosis, and the initial effectiveness of comprehensive prevention and control measures implemented in traditional core epidemic areas such as Henan Province [29]. We caution that the AAPC of 43.28% for the entire 2010–2024 period is substantially influenced by the extreme APC during 2010–2012 (218.14%), which reflects improved surveillance sensitivity rather than genuine epidemic growth. The APC for the sustained growth phase from 2012 to 2024 (14.14%) provides a more accurate characterization of the epidemic’s trajectory during the mature surveillance period.

National cases were mainly concentrated in some prefectural cities and counties (districts) in seven provinces, including Shandong, Anhui, Henan, Hubei, Liaoning, Jiangsu, and Zhejiang, which together accounted for over 98.8% of all reported cases. This geographic distribution closely aligns with that reported by Huang et al. for the overlapping period of 2018–2021, supporting the consistency of national surveillance data across analyses [23]. Data from this study show that Shandong had the highest cumulative cases (9,547), followed by Anhui (7,494) and Henan (7,148). The number of cases in Anhui Province increased rapidly starting in 2022, making it the province with the highest number of reported cases in China in 2023 and 2024. This is a significant finding with scientific and early-warning implications. Possible reasons include: climate change leading to environmental conditions in Anhui more favorable for tick vectors and animal host reproduction and virus transmission, or enhanced surveillance and increased awareness among healthcare workers leading to improved case detection [19, 21, 28, 30]. These hypotheses remain to be tested with independent environmental and surveillance quality data. This phenomenon suggests that SFTS risk mapping needs to be dynamically updated, and the allocation of prevention and control resources should not rely solely on historical data but should incorporate meteorological and environmental data to establish an intelligent early warning system.

This study reaffirms the distinct seasonality and population clustering of SFTS. The number of cases gradually increased from March, reached a peak from May to July, and then showed a downward trend. Some provinces had a secondary increase in case counts in October. Consistent with previous research findings [31]. The high-incidence period from April to October represents a critical window for prevention and control. Notably, seasonal patterns varied across provinces. For instance, Liaoning exhibited a later peak (July-September) while Shandong was concentrated in May-August, a secondary increase in case counts was observed in October in Anhui, Hubei, and Jiangsu provinces, suggesting that each province should tailor its prevention and control window accordingly. The population distribution accurately identifies high-risk groups: middle-aged and elderly farmers (40–84 years, 94.3% of cases) engaged in outdoor work with high exposure opportunities [21]. This indicates that future interventions must be highly time-specific and target-specific. For example, concentrated health education and distribution of protective products to farmers in key areas should be conducted before the start of the epidemic season. Notably, 37 healthcare workers (0.1%) were among reported cases. Although the surveillance data do not distinguish occupational from community-acquired infections, the known risk of nosocomial SFTS transmission warrants strict adherence to standard precautions in healthcare settings.

This study revealed a fluctuating but overall decreasing trend in the time interval between symptom onset and diagnosis of SFTS cases in China from 2010 to 2024, with the median interval shortening to 5 days by 2024. The high value in 2010 may reflect retrospective case ascertainment or incomplete early surveillance rather than true diagnostic delay. The temporary increase observed in 2014 may be attributable to the insufficient diagnostic experience at the primary care level of the newly affected counties. This result is consistent with the findings of previous studies that use only laboratory-confirmed cases [32]. The annual median intervals during 2011–2016 were comparable to those reported by Sun et al. (2018), who analyzed laboratory-confirmed cases over the same period (7 vs. 7, 6 vs. 6, 6 vs. 6, 13 vs. 27, 9 vs. 10, and 8 vs. 12 days, respectively). The concordance in trends, particularly the 2014 peak observed in both studies, supports the robustness of our findings, while the slightly lower medians in our study during 2014–2016 likely reflect the inclusion of both clinically diagnosed and laboratory-confirmed cases [32].

The declining trend in the national average CFR reflects significant improvements in early diagnosis, clinical management capabilities, public awareness of seeking medical care, or the increase in the detection of mild cases in China. There are significant differences in reported CFR among different regions, which may be related to the level of diagnosis and treatment of cases in different regions, as well as the improvement in case follow-up systems [33, 34]. The overall CFR observed in our study (4.79%) is substantially lower than those reported in Japan (19.42%) and South Korea (18.54%) [35]. The age of death was concentrated in middle-aged and elderly people, with the CFR climbing to 9.15% in the ≥ 85 years age group. Given the markedly higher CFR among older age groups, the continued aging of China’s rural population is likely to increase the overall societal burden of SFTS [36]. This is consistent with previous research findings and may be related to lower immune function, higher infection rate, and higher incidence of complications in elderly people [1, 37]. Therefore, specific treatment strategies for elderly patients should be developed to reduce the risk of severe illness and death.

This study is based on passive surveillance data, which may be subject to underreporting, misreporting, and reporting delays. As diagnostic capacity and clinical awareness improved over the study period, mild or atypical cases may have been more readily identified in later years, potentially contributing to the observed increase in incidence. CFR comparisons across provinces and age groups should be interpreted with caution, as differences in case ascertainment, testing practices, and healthcare access may influence observed estimates. The lack of individual exposure history, underlying medical conditions, treatment information, and clinical outcome data limits deeper investigation into transmission chains and prognostic risk factors. Future studies should integrate epidemiological, vector surveillance, and clinical data to address these deeper questions.

Based on these findings, resource allocation should shift from a historical burden approach to a risk prediction approach. Targeted interventions for high-risk groups, such as middle-aged and elderly farmers, should be implemented and evaluated in key areas. Given the risk of nosocomial transmission, occupational protection training for healthcare workers should also be strengthened. A One Health approach should be promoted, fostering multidisciplinary collaboration among public health, clinical medicine, and environmental science to systematically study the circulation of the virus among animal hosts, vectors, and the environment, thereby controlling the outbreak at its source.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the epidemic of SFTS in China has entered a new phase characterized by rapid geographical expansion. By 2024, cases had been reported in 30 of China’s 31 mainland provincial-level divisions, with both case counts and deaths reaching record highs, and Anhui Province becoming the top-ranking province for two consecutive years. Up to now, there is still no licensed vaccine or approved specific antiviral therapy for SFTS [1, 31, 38]. Future research directions include the development of intelligent early warning systems integrating climate and environmental data, which may help predict further geographic spread. Future efforts should focus on developing effective interventions targeting high-risk populations.
