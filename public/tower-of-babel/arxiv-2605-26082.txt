Quantitative Einstein relation for reversible diffusions in a random environment
Ahmed Bou-Rabee, Ruizhe Xu

Abstract
. The Einstein relation describes the response of a diffusing particle to a small constant external force. It states that, as the force tends to zero, the ratio of the limiting velocity to the force magnitude converges to the diffusivity matrix of the unforced particle, evaluated in the force direction. Gantert et al. 2012 proved this identity for reversible diffusions in random environments. We prove a quantitative version, with an explicit quenched algebraic rate.



1.
Introduction

A particle diffusing in a disordered medium, subjected to a small constant force, eventually drifts at an asymptotic velocity. To leading order, this velocity is a linear function of the force; the proportionality matrix is called the
mobility
. The
Einstein relation
predicts that the mobility equals the diffusivity matrix of the unforced particle. The relation admits several mathematical formulations, depending on the order in which the small-force and long-time limits are taken (see Section
1.5
for further discussion). We work with the formulation of
Gantert et al. 2012
for reversible diffusions in a random environment, and we prove a quantitative relation, showing that the mobility error is bounded by an explicit power of the force magnitude (Theorem
1.2
).

1.1.
Setting

Let
\mathbf{a}
be a uniformly elliptic, Lipschitz random matrix field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
V
be a bounded, Lipschitz random potential. Both are stationary with finite range of dependence; precise hypotheses are stated in Assumption
1.1
below. Let
X
be the reversible Markov diffusion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with generator

\mathcal{L}f=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{2V}\nabla\cdot\bigl(e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}\nabla f\bigr)\,.

Under Assumption
1.1
,
X
satisfies the invariance principle of
De Masi et al. 1989
(see also
Kipnis and Varadhan 1986
;
Papanicolaou and Varadhan 1982
;
Kozlov 1985
): for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e. environment,

\varepsilon X(\varepsilon^{-2}t)\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\downarrow 0]{}W_{t}\quad\text{in law},\qquad\mathbb{E}[W_{t}\otimes W_{t}]=\Sigma_{X}t\,,

for a deterministic effective diffusivity
\Sigma_{X}
.

Applying a constant force of magnitude
\lambda\in(0,1]
in the direction
e_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0)
adds a drift term to the generator:

(1.1)

\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}f\coloneqq\mathcal{L}f+\lambda\mathbf{a}e_{1}\cdot\nabla f\,.

The resulting biased diffusion
X^{\lambda}
, started at the origin, satisfies a quenched law of large numbers
[
Shen 2003
;
Gantert et al. 2012
]
: for almost every environment,

\frac{X^{\lambda}(t)}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}\ell_{X}(\lambda)\quad\text{almost surely}\,,

for a deterministic effective velocity
\ell_{X}(\lambda)
. The choice of direction
e_{1}
is without loss of generality. The Einstein relation predicts

(1.2)

\lim_{\lambda\downarrow 0}\frac{\ell_{X}(\lambda)}{\lambda}=\Sigma_{X}e_{1}\,.

Gantert et al. 2012
established (
1.2
) as a qualitative limit.

1.2.
Main result

Throughout, we assume:

Assumption 1.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q})
be a probability space, let
\Lambda\geq 1
, and let
r_{0}\geq 1
be an integer. Let
(\mathbf{a},V)
be a measurable random field on
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\mathbf{a}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\mathrm{sym},+}
and
V\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that
(\mathbf{a},V)
satisfies, for every
\omega\in\Omega
:

(S1)

(
Uniform ellipticity.
)
\Lambda^{-1}|\xi|^{2}\leq\xi\cdot\mathbf{a}(x)\xi\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}
for every
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(S2)

(
Bounded potential.
)
|V(x)|\leq\Lambda
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(S3)

(
Lipschitz regularity.
) Both
\mathbf{a}
and
V
are
\Lambda
-Lipschitz on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\mathbf{a}
measured in the operator norm.

In addition, the law of
(\mathbf{a},V)
under
\mathbb{Q}
satisfies:

(S4)

(
Stationarity.
) For every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
(\mathbf{a}(\cdot+z),V(\cdot+z))
and
(\mathbf{a},V)
have the same joint law.

(S5)

(
Finite range of dependence.
) For every pair of Borel sets
U_{1},U_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\mathrm{dist}(U_{1},U_{2})>r_{0}
, the
\sigma
-algebras of
(\mathbf{a},V)|_{U_{1}}
and
(\mathbf{a},V)|_{U_{2}}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-independent.

All deterministic constants will depend only on
(d,\Lambda,r_{0})
unless explicitly specified.

Theorem 1.2
.

Under Assumption
1.1
with
d\geq 2
,
\mathbb{Q}
-almost surely,

(1.3)

\Bigl|\frac{\ell_{X}(\lambda)}{\lambda}-\Sigma_{X}e_{1}\Bigr|\leq\lambda^{\beta^{*}}\qquad\text{for every }0<\lambda\leq\lambda_{0}(\omega)\,,

where
\beta^{*}=\beta^{*}(d)\in(0,1/4)
is a deterministic exponent and
\lambda_{0}\colon\Omega\to(0,1]
is a random scale satisfying the tail bound

(1.4)

\mathbb{Q}\bigl(\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\bigr)\leq C\exp\bigl(-c(\log(1/\lambda))^{\sigma}\bigr)\qquad\text{for every }\lambda\in(0,1]\,,

with
C=C(d,\Lambda,r_{0})<\infty
,
c=c(d,\Lambda,r_{0})>0
, and an exponent
\sigma=\sigma(d,\Lambda,r_{0})\in(0,1)
.

The exponent
\beta^{*}
is not expected to be optimal: we conjecture the sharp rate is
\lambda
in
d\geq 3
and
\lambda\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)}
in
d=2
; see Subsection
1.4
for further discussion. The one-dimensional setting has a separate literature with sharper, often explicit, results; see
Lam and Depauw 2016
and
Faggionato and Salvi 2019
for reversible one-dimensional random walks, and
Faggionato et al. 2019
and
Gantert et al. 2019
for related one-dimensional models.

1.3.
Proof strategy

The starting point is a balance of scales. Under a force of magnitude
\lambda
, the ballistic displacement
\lambda t
and the diffusive fluctuation
\sqrt{t}
are comparable precisely when
t\asymp\lambda^{-2}
, that is, on spatial scales of order
\lambda^{-1}
. This scale is called the
critical length scale
and is the scale at which the proof operates.

Our strategy is as follows. First, a time change absorbs the potential into the divergence-form coefficient, which makes the tilted operator amenable to homogenization. Second, on a cube of side slightly larger than
\lambda^{-1}
, we solve a resolvent equation whose solution, via the Feynman–Kac formula, encodes the mobility error as an exponentially weighted average of the path. Third, we expand this average into four contributions — short-time, long-time, exit, and homogenization — and estimate each separately. The short-time and exit terms are controlled directly. The long-time term is controlled by a law of large numbers uniform in
\lambda
(Section
5
), and the homogenization term by a quantitative estimate for the tilted resolvent (Section
4
). Optimizing the free parameters in the resulting bound yields the algebraic rate of Theorem
1.2
.

1.3.1.
Time-change reduction to divergence form

Multiplying
\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}
by
e^{-2V}
moves the potential into the divergence-form coefficient, leaving a stationary coefficient field
e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}
and an explicit first-order tilted drift:

e^{-2V}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}f=\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}\nabla f)+\lambda e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}e_{1}\cdot\nabla f\,.

The diffusion
Y^{\lambda}
generated by
e^{-2V}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}
is a time change of
X^{\lambda}
. Set

A^{\lambda}(s)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{s}e^{-2V(Y^{\lambda}(u))}du\,.

The quantity
A^{\lambda}(s)
is the elapsed
X^{\lambda}
-clock at
Y^{\lambda}
-time
s
. The two processes are related pathwise by

(1.5)

X^{\lambda}(t)=Y^{\lambda}\circ(A^{\lambda})^{-1}(t)\,.

Let
\ell(\lambda)\coloneqq\lim_{t\to\infty}Y^{\lambda}(t)/t
and
\eta(\lambda)\coloneqq\lim_{t\to\infty}A^{\lambda}(t)/t
be the asymptotic velocity and clock rate of
Y^{\lambda}
. Let
\bar{\mathbf{a}}
be the homogenized matrix of
-\nabla\cdot(e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}\nabla)
, the analogue of
\Sigma_{X}
for
Y^{0}
. Combining (
1.5
) with these limits gives

(1.6)

\ell_{X}(\lambda)=\eta(\lambda)^{-1}\ell(\lambda),\qquad\Sigma_{X}=\mathbb{E}[e^{-2V(0)}]^{-1}\bar{\mathbf{a}}\,,

so Theorem
1.2
reduces to two intermediate estimates:

(1.7)

\Bigl|\frac{\ell(\lambda)}{\lambda}-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}\Bigr|\leq C\lambda^{\beta^{*}}

and

|\eta(\lambda)-\mathbb{E}[e^{-2V(0)}]|\leq C\lambda^{\beta^{*}}\,.

1.3.2.
Feynman–Kac decomposition at the critical scale

We describe the mobility case (
1.7
); the estimate of the other error term is similar. Choose an integer
m
at the critical length scale, so that
3^{m}=\rho/\lambda
, with
\rho\geq 1
a free parameter.

Fix a buffer
h\geq 0
. On the triadic cube
\square_{m+h}
of side
3^{m+h}
, let
u_{m}\colon\square_{m+h}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
solve the velocity resolvent equation

(1.8)

(\lambda/3^{m})u_{m}-e^{-2V}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}u_{m}=\mathbf{b}\quad\text{in }\square_{m+h},\qquad u_{m}=3^{m}\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}\quad\text{on }\partial\square_{m+h}\,,

where
\mathbf{b}\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(e^{-2V}\mathbf{a})+\lambda e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}e_{1}
is the Itô drift of
Y^{\lambda}
. Replacing
e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}
by
\bar{\mathbf{a}}
in (
1.8
) gives the homogenized tilted equation. Since
\bar{\mathbf{a}}
is constant, the drift term
\lambda\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}\cdot\nabla\bar{u}
and the divergence
\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(\bar{\mathbf{a}}\nabla\bar{u})
both vanish for constant
\bar{u}
, so
\bar{u}\equiv 3^{m}\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}
solves

(\lambda/3^{m})\bar{u}=\lambda\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}\qquad\text{in }\square_{m+h},\qquad\bar{u}=3^{m}\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}\quad\text{on }\partial\square_{m+h}\,.

The deviation
3^{-m}u_{m}(0)-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}
measures the homogenization error at scale
3^{m}
.

Let
\tau_{m,h}\coloneqq\inf\{t\geq 0:Y^{\lambda}(t)\notin\square_{m+h}\}
be the exit time. The Feynman–Kac formula for
Y^{\lambda}
started at the origin gives

(1.9)

u_{m}(0)=\frac{\lambda}{3^{m}}E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}\biggl[\int_{0}^{\tau_{m,h}}e^{-(\lambda/3^{m})t}Y^{\lambda}(t)dt\biggr]+E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}\Bigl[e^{-(\lambda/3^{m})\tau_{m,h}}\bigl(Y^{\lambda}(\tau_{m,h})+3^{m}\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}\bigr)\Bigr]\,.

At
3^{m}\sim\lambda^{-1}
, the integral concentrates on the diffusive timescale
t\sim\lambda^{-2}
, and substituting the law-of-large-numbers approximation
Y^{\lambda}(t)\approx\ell(\lambda)t
gives
u_{m}(0)/3^{m}\approx\ell(\lambda)/\lambda
. The mobility error then decomposes as

(1.10)

\frac{\ell(\lambda)}{\lambda}-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}=\underbrace{\Bigl(\frac{\ell(\lambda)}{\lambda}-\frac{u_{m}(0)}{3^{m}}\Bigr)}_{\text{time-average error}}+\underbrace{\Bigl(\frac{u_{m}(0)}{3^{m}}-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}\Bigr)}_{\text{homogenization error}}\,.

1.3.3.
The four error contributions

The time-average error in (
1.10
) splits into three terms: a short-time term where the law-of-large-numbers approximation
Y^{\lambda}(t)\approx\ell(\lambda)t
fails; a long-time term controlled by the uniform-in-
\lambda
quenched LLN of Corollary
5.13
; and a boundary term of order
e^{-c3^{h}}
controlled by an exit-time bound (Proposition
4.12
). The homogenization error in (
1.10
) is the fourth, controlled by a resolvent homogenization estimate (Proposition
4.8
). With
\rho\coloneqq\lambda 3^{m}\geq 1
, these combine to give

\Bigl|\frac{\ell(\lambda)}{\lambda}-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}\Bigr|\leq\underbrace{C\rho^{-2}\lambda^{-2\varepsilon}}_{\text{short-time}}+\underbrace{C\rho^{-1/2+\theta}}_{\text{LLN rate}}+\underbrace{Ce^{-c3^{h}}}_{\text{boundary}}+\underbrace{C\rho^{1/2}3^{(d/2+1-\beta_{h})h-\delta m}}_{\text{homogenization}}\,,

where
\beta_{h}>0
is a homogenization exponent supplied by
Armstrong and Kuusi 2022
(Proposition
3.1
),
\delta>0
is the exponent of our homogenization result, Proposition
4.8
below, and
\theta,\varepsilon>0
are small parameters. Optimizing the choice of parameters then gives an algebraic rate.

The uniform-in-
\lambda
LLN required by this argument (Corollary
5.13
) is established by a conditional renewal identity at regeneration times (Lemma
5.2
), using the regeneration structure of
Shen 2003
and the
\lambda
-dependent adaptation in
Gantert et al. 2012
.

1.4.
Heuristic for the conjectural rate

We conjecture the sharp dimension-dependent upper bound

(1.11)

\Bigl|\frac{\ell_{X}(\lambda)}{\lambda}-\Sigma_{X}e_{1}\Bigr|\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda,&d\geq 3\,,\\[2.84526pt]
\lambda\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)},&d=2\,.\end{cases}

By (
1.6
), it suffices to bound the velocity response
\ell(\lambda)/\lambda-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}
and the clock error
\eta(\lambda)-\mathbb{E}[e^{-2V(0)}]
at the same rates. We sketch the heuristic for the velocity. The decomposition (
1.10
) splits the error into a time-average contribution and a homogenization contribution; we conjecture an optimal rate for each.

Refining the Feynman–Kac decomposition (
1.9
), we decompose the expected displacement into a ballistic part
\ell(\lambda)t
and a remainder
R^{\omega}_{\lambda}(t)
:

E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[Y^{\lambda}(t)]=\ell(\lambda)t+R^{\omega}_{\lambda}(t)\,.

Substituting this into (
1.9
), the ballistic part contributes
3^{m}\ell(\lambda)/\lambda
to
u_{m}(0)
, while the boundary term gives an exit-tail
O(e^{-c3^{h}})
. Dividing by
3^{m}
yields

\frac{u_{m}(0)}{3^{m}}\approx\frac{\ell(\lambda)}{\lambda}+\frac{\bar{R}^{\omega}_{\lambda}}{3^{m}}+O(e^{-c3^{h}})\,,

where

\bar{R}^{\omega}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\frac{\lambda}{3^{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\lambda/3^{m})t}R^{\omega}_{\lambda}(t)dt

is the time-average of
R^{\omega}_{\lambda}(t)
against an exponential kernel of mean
3^{m}/\lambda
. At the critical length scale
3^{m}\sim\lambda^{-1}
, this mean is the diffusive timescale
\lambda^{-2}
.

At
\lambda=0
, the homogenization corrector
\chi

[
Kozlov 1985
]
— the sublinear vector field with
\chi(0)=0
and
\nabla\cdot(e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}\nabla(x_{i}+\chi_{i}))=0
for each
i
— makes
Y^{0}+\chi(Y^{0})
a martingale, so

E_{0}^{0,\omega}[Y^{0}(t)]=-E_{0}^{0,\omega}[\chi(Y^{0}(t))]\,.

Heuristically, at time
t\sim 3^{2m}
the heat kernel for
Y^{0}
averages mostly over
\square_{m}
, suggesting that
|R^{\omega}_{0}(t)|
should be controlled by the corrector’s amplitude on
\square_{m}
:

A_{m}\coloneqq\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\|\chi_{i}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{m})},\qquad A_{m}\lesssim\begin{cases}1,&d\geq 3\,,\\[2.84526pt]
\sqrt{m},&d=2\,,\end{cases}

where the inequality on the right follows from
Armstrong et al. 2019
. This gives the heuristic estimate
|R^{\omega}_{0}(t)|\lesssim A_{m}
.

We conjecture that this corrector control persists for the biased process at the critical length scale
3^{m}\asymp\lambda^{-1}
:

(1.12)

\bar{R}^{\omega}_{\lambda}=O(A_{m})\quad\text{uniformly as }\lambda\to 0\,.

The existing argument, which relies on regeneration alone, gives
\bar{R}^{\omega}_{\lambda}=O(\lambda^{-1})
: blocks have duration
\asymp\lambda^{-2}
and displacement
\asymp\lambda^{-1}
, and the exponential kernel of mean
\lambda^{-2}
averages over
O(1)
blocks. The conjecture (
1.12
) requires sharper cancellation, analogous to the corrector mechanism of the symmetric case above. Granting (
1.12
), the remainder term
\bar{R}^{\omega}_{\lambda}/3^{m}
in the schematic expansion has size
\lesssim A_{m}/3^{m}
. Combined with the exit-tail, this gives the conjectural time-average rate

\Bigl|\frac{\ell(\lambda)}{\lambda}-\frac{u_{m}(0)}{3^{m}}\Bigr|\lesssim\frac{A_{m}}{3^{m}}+e^{-c3^{h}}\asymp\lambda A_{m}\,,

equal to
\lambda
in
d\geq 3
and
\lambda\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)}
in
d=2
.

We also expect the homogenization error to be controlled by the interior slope of the corrector across
\square_{m}
: an oscillation of size
A_{m}
across a cube of side
3^{m}
produces a slope
A_{m}/3^{m}
, so

(1.13)

\Bigl|\frac{u_{m}(0)}{3^{m}}-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}\Bigr|\lesssim\frac{A_{m}}{3^{m}}\,.

Adding the two contributions gives (
1.11
).

Making this heuristic rigorous requires resolving two problems, one for each contribution.

For the time-average contribution, the bound
|R^{\omega}_{0}(t)|\lesssim A_{m}
comes from the martingale
Y^{0}+\chi
, which exists because
\chi
corrects the self-adjoint generator
\mathcal{L}^{0}
; the biased generator
\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}
has no analogous corrector with quantitative control of its amplitude. The steady-state approach gives the leading small-
\lambda
behavior of the weak steady state
\nu_{\lambda}
: for centered observables
f
,

\nu_{\lambda}(f)=\lambda\bar{\Gamma}(f)+o(\lambda),\qquad\bar{\Gamma}(f)=-2\int\chi fd\mathbb{Q}\,.

This is proved in
Mathieu and Piatnitski 2018
when a stationary corrector exists, for instance under finite range in
d\geq 3
;
Gantert et al. 2017
prove the analogous expansion on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in
d\geq 3
. The conjecture (
1.12
) is different: it is a finite-time statement at the critical timescale
t\sim\lambda^{-2}
. Even a quantitative second-order bound such as
|\nu_{\lambda}(b)-\lambda\bar{\Gamma}(b)|\leq C\lambda^{2}A_{m}
for the velocity observable
b
would only control the infinite-time velocity component. One would also need a critical-scale relaxation estimate from the initial environment to
\nu_{\lambda}
. In
d\geq 3
, this second-order input is a
C^{1,1}
-type regularity statement for
\nu_{\lambda}
at
\lambda=0
; in
d=2
, the same formal argument predicts the additional
\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)}
loss.

For the homogenization contribution, our proof uses the sharp homogenization estimates of
Armstrong and Kuusi 2022
but does not recover the conjectural rate
A_{m}/3^{m}
of (
1.13
). Two passages introduce slack. First, the resolvent equation carries a first-order drift and a spectral parameter that the corrector equation does not, and these inflate the energy bound. Second, we need the value at a single point, while the energy estimate gives an
L^{2}
average over the full cube. To extract a pointwise bound, we apply a deterministic interior
L^{\infty}
estimate, which requires the elliptic operator to be uniformly elliptic. The tilted operator
-\nabla\cdot(e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\mathbf{a}\nabla)
has ellipticity constants that depend on
e^{2\lambda x_{1}}
, which ranges over
e^{\pm\lambda 3^{m+h}}
on the full cube
\square_{m+h}
and is unbounded as
m\to\infty
. We therefore restrict to a concentric subcube
\square_{m_{1}}
small enough that
e^{2\lambda x_{1}}
is bounded by an absolute constant. The corresponding volume ratio is
3^{d(m-m_{1}+h)/2}
: the loss from
m-m_{1}
is absorbed by the homogenization exponent, while the
h
-dependent loss is controlled in the final parameter choice.

1.5.
Background

The Einstein relation is a prototype of
fluctuation–dissipation
: the principle that the equilibrium fluctuations of a system determine its response to a small external perturbation. Here the fluctuation is the diffusive spreading of the unforced particle, and the response is the drift induced by the force. In its classical form the relation reads

D=\mu\,k_{B}T\,,

where
D
is the diffusion constant,
\mu
the mobility,
T
the temperature, and
k_{B}
the Boltzmann constant.

For a test particle in a stationary random environment, the Einstein relation has been approached in three ways, differing in how the small-force and long-time limits are ordered.
Lebowitz and Rost 1994
couple the two limits, scaling the force together with a diffusive rescaling of space and time; this sidesteps the need to interpret the mobility as a genuine derivative, at the price of a weaker conclusion. The remaining two approaches both send time to infinity at a fixed force, but extract different objects from that limit.
Mathieu and Piatnitski 2018
and
Gantert et al. 2017
encode it in an invariant measure — a
steady state
— for the environment viewed from the biased particle, and recover the Einstein relation from the small-force expansion of that measure. We follow the third route, due to
Gantert et al. 2012
: for each fixed
\lambda
the long-time limit yields the velocity
\ell_{X}(\lambda)
, and the small-force limit of
\ell_{X}(\lambda)/\lambda
is taken afterward.

A parallel theory for tagged particles in interacting particle systems was initiated by
Lebowitz and Spohn 1982
, with a general framework given in
Ferrari et al. 1985
.

The general principle of fluctuation–dissipation, expressing transport coefficients as equilibrium time correlations, was developed in
Nyquist 1928
;
Onsager 1931a
;
Onsager 1931b
;
Callen and Welton 1951
; a rigorous Markov-process formulation appears in
Dembo and Deuschel 2010
. The invariant-measure approach described above embeds the Einstein relation in this framework through the weak steady state
\nu_{\lambda}
of the environment seen from the biased particle, with early constructions in related settings due to
Komorowski and Krupa 2004
. In our continuous reversible setting,
Mathieu and Piatnitski 2018
construct
\nu_{\lambda}
, prove the Lipschitz bound
|\nu_{\lambda}(f)|\leq C\lambda\|f\|_{H^{-1}_{\infty}}
, and identify
\partial_{\lambda}\nu_{\lambda}|_{\lambda=0}
in terms of the corrector. On
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in
d\geq 3
,
Gantert et al. 2017
obtain the analogous first-order expansion of
\nu_{\lambda}
, building on the variance-decay estimates of
Mourrat 2011
and
de Buyer and Mourrat 2015
. A different perturbative expansion is obtained by
Campos and Ramírez 2017
in the low-disorder regime on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where the small parameter is the disorder rather than the bias
\lambda
.

The Einstein relation has been studied in many related discrete models. On
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in
d\geq 3
, qualitative versions are known for nearest-neighbor walks with two-valued random jump rates
[
Komorowski and Olla 2005b
]
, for balanced i.i.d. walks under a Kalikow-type ballisticity condition and for Sznitman-ballistic i.i.d. walks
[
Guo 2016
]
, and for tagged tracers in time-mixing environments
[
Komorowski and Olla 2005a
]
. One-dimensional models admit separate techniques: reversible walks in ergodic conductances
[
Lam and Depauw 2016
]
, random walks in random environment
[
Faggionato and Salvi 2019
]
, biased walks on a one-dimensional percolation model
[
Gantert et al. 2019
]
, Mott variable-range hopping
[
Faggionato et al. 2019
]
, and the complex (frequency-dependent) mobility matrix for the random conductance model
[
Faggionato and Salvi 2025
]
.

On trees, the relation has been established for biased walks on Galton–Watson trees with deep traps
[
Ben Arous et al. 2013
]
. For interacting particle systems,
Loulakis 2002
proved the tagged-particle Einstein relation under a constant external force for symmetric simple exclusion in
d\geq 3
, building on
Kipnis and Varadhan 1986
; in one dimension under weak asymmetry, the driven tracer was treated earlier by
Landim et al. 1998
.

Reversibility of the process is an essential hypothesis: for mean-zero asymmetric simple exclusion in
d\geq 3
,
Loulakis 2005
showed that the mobility need not coincide with the self-diffusion matrix.

Theorem
1.2
gives, to our knowledge, the first quantitative rate in
\lambda
for the Einstein relation under a constant external force, in dimensions
d\geq 2
. The rate is quenched: it holds for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e. environment, on a random window
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}(\omega)]
whose threshold has the tail (
1.4
). The physics conjecture (
1.11
) is quenched in the same sense.

Unlike previous work, our proof relies on the sharp quantitative homogenization estimates of
Armstrong and Kuusi 2022
;
Armstrong and Kuusi 2025a
;
Armstrong and Kuusi 2025b
. The renewal estimates of Section
5

2.1.1.
Triadic cubes and volume-normalized integrals

For every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, the triadic cube of side length

centered at the origin is

\square_{m}\coloneqq\Bigl(-\tfrac{3^{m}}{2},\tfrac{3^{m}}{2}\Bigr)^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

For a bounded domain
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we use volume-normalized integrals and
L^{p}
norms,

(f)_{U}\coloneqq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{U}f(x)dx=\frac{1}{|U|}\int_{U}f(x)dx,\qquad\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(U)}\coloneqq\Bigl(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{U}|f(x)|^{p}dx\Bigr)^{1/p}\,.

2.1.2.
Volume-normalized Sobolev norms

For
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, the volume-normalized Sobolev seminorm is

(2.1)

[f]_{\underline{W}^{s,p}(U)}\coloneqq\Bigl(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{U}\int_{U}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}dydx\Bigr)^{1/p}\,.

Here only the outer integral is volume-normalized.
The full norm is

\|f\|_{\underline{W}^{s,p}(U)}\coloneqq\Bigl(|U|^{-sp/d}\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(U)}^{p}+[f]_{\underline{W}^{s,p}(U)}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}\,.

For
s\in(0,1/2]
and
p\in(1,\infty)
with Hölder conjugate
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
, define the volume-normalized negative dual norm by

(2.2)

\|f\|_{\hat{\phantom{W}}\mathchoice{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\underline{W}^{-s,p^{\prime}}(U)}\coloneqq\sup\Bigl\{\langle f,g\rangle_{U}:g\in\underline{W}^{s,p}(U),\ \|g\|_{\underline{W}^{s,p}(U)}\leq 1\Bigr\}\,,

where
\langle f,g\rangle_{U}
denotes the volume-normalized dual pairing, equal to
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{U}fg
when
f
is a function.
Set
\underline{H}^{s}\coloneqq\underline{W}^{s,2}
and
\hat{\phantom{H}}\mathchoice{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\underline{H}^{-s}\coloneqq\hat{\phantom{W}}\mathchoice{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\underline{W}^{-s,2}
. For
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued functions, norms are taken componentwise: if
f\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then

\|f\|^{2}_{\underline{H}^{s}(U)}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|f_{i}\|^{2}_{\underline{H}^{s}(U)},\qquad\|f\|^{2}_{\hat{\phantom{H}}\mathchoice{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\underline{H}^{-s}(U)}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|f_{i}\|^{2}_{\hat{\phantom{H}}\mathchoice{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\underline{H}^{-s}(U)}\,.

For matrix fields
F\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, we use the Frobenius component convention:

\|F\|^{2}_{\underline{H}^{s}(U)}\coloneqq\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\|F_{ij}\|^{2}_{\underline{H}^{s}(U)}\,,

and analogously for
\|F\|_{\hat{\phantom{H}}\mathchoice{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\underline{H}^{-s}(U)}
.

For
f
defined on
\square_{M}
, set

\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{x})\coloneqq f(3^{M}\widetilde{x})\qquad(\widetilde{x}\in\square_{0})\,.

The same definition applies componentwise to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
- and
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
-valued
f
. Since
|\square_{0}|=1
, the volume-normalized norms on
\square_{0}
coincide with the ordinary unnormalized ones, and we drop the underline in the identities that follow. We write
H^{s}(\square_{0})\coloneqq W^{s,2}(\square_{0})
and
\hat{H}^{-s}(\square_{0})\coloneqq(H^{s}(\square_{0}))^{*}
. A direct computation from (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) gives, for
s\in(0,1)
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{M})}

\displaystyle=\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{2}(\square_{0})}\,,

\displaystyle[f]_{\underline{H}^{s}(\square_{M})}

\displaystyle=3^{-sM}[\widetilde{f}]_{H^{s}(\square_{0})}\,,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\underline{H}^{s}(\square_{M})}

\displaystyle=3^{-sM}\|\widetilde{f}\|_{H^{s}(\square_{0})}\,.

Additionally, for
s\in(0,1/2]
,

(2.4)

\|f\|_{\hat{\phantom{H}}\mathchoice{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\underline{H}^{-s}(\square_{M})}=3^{sM}\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\hat{H}^{-s}(\square_{0})}\,.

The gradient scales as
\|\nabla\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{2}(\square_{0})}=3^{M}\|\nabla f\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{M})}
.

2.2.
The reversible diffusion and its tilt

This subsection gives the SDE form of the tilted diffusion and the probabilistic notation. After the time change in Subsection
2.3
, the notation is simplified.

The tilted generator (
1.1
) admits the Itô decomposition

\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}f=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\mathbf{a}_{ij}\partial_{ij}f+\mathbf{b}^{\lambda}\cdot\nabla f\,,

with diffusion coefficient
\mathbf{a}
and Itô drift

\mathbf{b}^{\lambda}\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{a}\nabla V+\lambda\mathbf{a}e_{1},\qquad(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{a})_{j}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}\mathbf{a}_{ij}\,,

where
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{a}
and
\nabla V
are taken in the a.e. sense. The corresponding SDE is

dX^{\lambda}(t)=\mathbf{b}^{\lambda}(X^{\lambda}(t))dt+\mathbf{a}^{1/2}(X^{\lambda}(t))dW(t),\qquad X^{\lambda}(0)=x\,,

where
\mathbf{a}^{1/2}
is the symmetric square root of
\mathbf{a}
. The Lipschitz assumptions give a unique-in-law solution. We write
P^{\lambda,\omega}_{x},E^{\lambda,\omega}_{x}
for the quenched law and expectation and
\mathbb{E}^{\lambda}_{x}\coloneqq\int E^{\lambda,\omega}_{x}d\mathbb{Q}(\omega)
for the annealed expectation.

2.3.
The time change

The potential
V
enters (
1.1
) both as a coefficient and through the equilibrium measure
e^{-2V}dx
of the unperturbed operator. A time change absorbs the potential into the divergence-form coefficient, leaving it visible only through the clock. Write the generator as

\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}f=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{2V}\nabla\cdot(e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}\nabla f)+\lambda\mathbf{a}e_{1}\cdot\nabla f\,.

Multiplying by
e^{-2V}
removes the prefactor in front of the divergence:

(2.5)

\displaystyle e^{-2V}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}f

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}\nabla f)+\lambda e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}e_{1}\cdot\nabla f

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-2\lambda x_{1}}\nabla\cdot\bigl(e^{2\lambda x_{1}}e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}\nabla f\bigr)\,,

Thus
e^{-2V}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}
is in divergence form. Its coefficient matrix is
e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}
. The operator is symmetric in
L^{2}(e^{2\lambda x_{1}}dx)
.

The original generator and the divergence-form generator (
2.5
) correspond to the same diffusion with different time parametrizations. We record this time change in both directions. Let
t(s)
be the inverse of the strictly increasing map

r\mapsto\int_{0}^{r}e^{2V(X^{\lambda}(u))}du\,.

Then
dt/ds=e^{-2V(X^{\lambda}(t(s)))}
, and the time-changed process

Y^{\lambda}(s)\coloneqq X^{\lambda}(t(s))

has generator
e^{-2V}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}
.

In the other direction, the additive clock of
Y^{\lambda}
is

A^{\lambda}(s)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{s}e^{-2V(Y^{\lambda}(u))}du\,.

This clock equals
t(s)
, and inverting gives

(2.6)

X^{\lambda}(t)=Y^{\lambda}\bigl((A^{\lambda})^{-1}(t)\bigr)\qquad\text{(pathwise)}\,.

For
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\chi_{p}
be the corrector for the field
e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}
in direction
p
, characterized by
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\nabla\chi_{p}]=0
and

-\nabla\cdot\bigl(e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}(p+\nabla\chi_{p})\bigr)=0\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}

in the weak sense. The homogenized matrix of
-\nabla\cdot(e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}\nabla)
is the matrix
\bar{\mathbf{a}}
defined by

(2.7)

p\cdot\bar{\mathbf{a}}q\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\Bigl[(p+\nabla\chi_{p}(0))\cdot e^{-2V(0)}\mathbf{a}(0)(q+\nabla\chi_{q}(0))\Bigr]\qquad(p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{d})\,.

The existence of these correctors and the deterministic matrix
\bar{\mathbf{a}}
follows from
Kozlov 1985
. Set

(2.8)

\ell(\lambda)\coloneqq\lim_{s\to\infty}s^{-1}Y^{\lambda}(s),\qquad\eta(\lambda)\coloneqq\lim_{s\to\infty}s^{-1}A^{\lambda}(s)\,,

the asymptotic velocity of the time-changed diffusion and its asymptotic clock rate; the existence of both limits is proved in Section
5
. The time-change identities are

(2.9)

\ell_{X}(\lambda)=\eta(\lambda)^{-1}\ell(\lambda),\qquad\Sigma_{X}=\mathbb{E}[e^{-2V(0)}]^{-1}\bar{\mathbf{a}}\,.

The first identity follows from (
2.6
); the second is the identity
\Sigma_{Y}=\gamma\Sigma
near the end of the proof of
Gantert et al. 2012
, applied with
\gamma=\mathbb{E}[e^{-2V(0)}]
and
\Sigma_{Y}=\bar{\mathbf{a}}
.

reduce, after subtracting the finite-volume correctors, to bounds on the difference

3.1.
Finite-volume correctors and quantitative estimates

For every
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
w_{p,M}=w(\cdot,\square_{M},p)\in p\cdot x+H^{1}_{0}(\square_{M})
be the Dirichlet corrector on
\square_{M}
in the sense of
Armstrong and Kuusi 2025a
, that is, the unique weak solution of

(3.1)

-\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla w_{p,M})=0\quad\text{in }\square_{M}\,,\qquad w_{p,M}=p\cdot x\quad\text{on }\partial\square_{M}\,.

When the slope
p
is a unit basis vector
e_{j}
, we abbreviate
w_{j,M}\coloneqq w_{e_{j},M}
.

The next proposition collects the two homogenization estimates used in Section
4
: a coarse-graining bound for solutions with a divergence-form right-hand side, and the finite-volume Dirichlet corrector estimate. Both hold beyond a random scale
\mathcal{X}_{\beta_{h}}
with a stretched-exponential tail.

Proposition 3.1

(Quantitative homogenization estimates)
.

Fix any
\beta_{h}\in(0,1/8]
. There exist deterministic constants
C=C(d,\Lambda,r_{0},\beta_{h})<\infty
and
c=c(d,\Lambda,r_{0},\beta_{h})>0
, a deterministic exponent
\sigma=\sigma(d,\Lambda,r_{0},\beta_{h})\in(0,1)
, and a random scale
\mathcal{X}_{\beta_{h}}\colon\Omega\to[0,\infty)
with the following properties. First, the random scale has a stretched-exponential tail:

(3.2)

\mathbb{Q}\bigl(\mathcal{X}_{\beta_{h}}\geq M\bigr)\leq C\exp\bigl(-cM^{\sigma}\bigr)\qquad\text{for every }M\geq 0\,.

Second, on a
\mathbb{Q}
-full-probability event, the following estimates hold for every integer
M\geq\mathcal{X}_{\beta_{h}}(\omega)
.

•

Let
k\in\{1,d\}
, let
u\in H^{1}(\square_{M};\mathbb{R}^{k})
, and let
f\in H^{1/6}(\square_{M};\mathbb{R}^{k}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy
-\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla u)=\nabla\cdot f
componentwise in
\square_{M}
. Then

(3.3)

3^{-M/6}\|(\mathbf{a}-\bar{\mathbf{a}})\nabla u\|_{\hat{\phantom{H}}\mathchoice{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\underline{H}^{-1/6}(\square_{M})}\leq C3^{-\beta_{h}M}\bigl(\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{M})}+3^{M/6}[f]_{\underline{H}^{1/6}(\square_{M})}\bigr)\,.

•

For every
j
with
1\leq j\leq d
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle 3^{-M}\|w_{j,M}-x_{j}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{M})}+3^{-M/6}\|\nabla w_{j,M}-e_{j}\|_{\hat{\phantom{H}}\mathchoice{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\underline{H}^{-1/6}(\square_{M})}

\displaystyle+3^{-M/6}\|\mathbf{a}\nabla w_{j,M}-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{j}\|_{\hat{\phantom{H}}\mathchoice{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\underline{H}^{-1/6}(\square_{M})}\leq C3^{-\beta_{h}M}\,.

Proof.

The finite range of dependence in Assumption
1.1
implies the concentration-for-sums condition of
Armstrong and Kuusi 2022
. The coarse-graining estimate with a right-hand side, namely (
3.3
), is supplied by
Armstrong et al. 2026
. For (
3.4
), we use the finite-volume Dirichlet corrector estimate of
Armstrong and Kuusi 2025a
: their display (5.54) is the definition of
w(\cdot,\square_{m},e)
used in (
3.1
), and displays (5.61)–(5.62), with
s=1/6
and
a_{1}=\bar{\mathbf{a}}
, reduce the three bounds in (
3.4
) to their large-scale finite-volume error estimate. In the uniformly elliptic finite-range setting,
Armstrong and Kuusi 2025a
imply that this error is at most
C3^{-\beta_{h}M}
above a random scale with a stretched-exponential tail, after taking

no larger than the deterministic exponent supplied there. Finally, we choose

as the maximum of the scales required for (

) and (

).
∎

Throughout the rest of the paper we will enlarge

by deterministic constants depending only on

to absorb deterministic threshold conditions arising in later proofs.

The remaining bookkeeping lemma converts a stretched-exponential scale tail, together with a polynomial tail for a small-

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\sigma\in(0,1]
. Let
T\colon\Omega\to[0,\infty)
and
\lambda_{0}\colon\Omega\to(0,1]
be random variables. Suppose there exist deterministic
C_{T}<\infty
and
c_{T}>0
such that

(3.5)

\mathbb{Q}(T\geq M)\leq C_{T}\exp(-c_{T}M^{\sigma})\qquad\text{for every }M\geq 0\,,

and that for every
p>0
there exists a deterministic
C_{p}<\infty
such that

(3.6)

\mathbb{Q}(\lambda_{0}\leq 2^{-k})\leq C_{p}2^{-pk}\qquad\text{for every }k\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Let
\lambda_{*}(\omega)=3^{-(1-\alpha)T(\omega)}\wedge\lambda_{0}(\omega)\wedge 1
. Then there exist deterministic
C_{*}<\infty
and
c_{*}>0
, depending only on
\alpha
,
\sigma
,
C_{T}
,
c_{T}
, and the family
(C_{p})
, such that

(3.7)

\mathbb{Q}(\lambda_{*}\leq\lambda)\leq C_{*}\exp\bigl(-c_{*}(\log(1/\lambda))^{\sigma}\bigr)\qquad\text{for every }\lambda\in(0,1]\,.

Proof.

The event
\{\lambda_{*}\leq\lambda\}
is contained in
\{T\geq\log_{3}(1/\lambda)/(1-\alpha)\}\cup\{\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda\}
. By (
3.5
), the first event has
\mathbb{Q}
-probability at most
C_{T}\exp(-c_{T}((1-\alpha)\log 3)^{-\sigma}(\log(1/\lambda))^{\sigma})
. For the second, dyadic discretization
2^{-k-1}<\lambda\leq 2^{-k}
and (
3.6
) give
\mathbb{Q}(\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda)\leq 2^{p}C_{p}\lambda^{p}
for every

; since

and
\lambda^{p}\leq\exp(-p(\log(1/\lambda))^{\sigma})
for

, choosing

and applying the union bound gives (

. For

, the trivial bound

is dominated by

by the factor
\exp(c_{*})
.
∎

4.
Tilted resolvent estimates

This section proves the analytic estimates used in the proof of
Theorem
1.2
. Recall from Section
2
that

\ell(\lambda)
and
\eta(\lambda)
denote the asymptotic velocity and clock
rate of the time-changed diffusion, and that the time-change identities reduce
the mobility error
\ell_{X}(\lambda)/\lambda-\Sigma_{X}e_{1}
to estimates on

\ell(\lambda)/\lambda-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}
and

\eta(\lambda)-\mathbb{E}[e^{-2V(0)}]
.

As explained in the introduction, the velocity error decomposes into a
time-average contribution and a homogenization contribution. The time-average
contribution is controlled by the renewal estimates of Section
5
.
The homogenization contribution is the deviation

3^{-m}u_{m}(0)-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}
, where
u_{m}
solves a resolvent equation on a cube

\square_{m+h}
at the critical length scale
3^{m}\sim\lambda^{-1}
, with
h

controlling the distance from the origin to the boundary. In a homogeneous
medium with constant coefficient
\bar{\mathbf{a}}
, the resolvent has the constant
solution
3^{m}\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}
, so
3^{-m}u_{m}(0)-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}
measures how far the
heterogeneous resolvent departs from its homogenized value.

The additional difficulty, compared to the homogenization estimates of
Section
3
, is the first-order drift

\lambda(\mathbf{a}e_{1})\cdot\nabla u
in the resolvent equation. The estimates of
Section
3
apply to solutions of the symmetric
operator
-\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla u)=0
. The tilted resolvent instead solves an
equation of the form

-\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla u)+\lambda(\mathbf{a}e_{1})\cdot\nabla u=\cdots
, and the drift
term is not perturbative at the critical scale. To see why, test the equation
against its solution: in the symmetric case, this produces an energy identity
that controls
|\nabla u|_{L^{2}}
. The drift adds an integral

\lambda\int u\,(\mathbf{a}e_{1})\cdot\nabla u
, which at
\lambda\sim 3^{-m}
carries
no small prefactor. We handle it by subtracting the finite-volume Dirichlet
correctors of Section
3
: the correctors absorb
the leading-order part of this integral, and the residual is small enough to
close the energy estimate (Lemma
4.4
). This gives a global

\underline{L}^{2}
bound on the homogenization error. A pointwise bound at the
origin then follows by restricting to a concentric subcube on which the
exponential weight
e^{2\lambda x_{1}}
is bounded, so that a deterministic
interior
L^{\infty}
estimate applies. The velocity bound is
Proposition
4.8
. The clock resolvent, which captures the homogenization contribution to the clock error
\eta(\lambda)-\mathbb{E}[e^{-2V(0)}]
, is treated by the same two-step argument, with the corrector bound replaced by cancellations in the negative-Sobolev norm of
e^{-2V}-\mathbb{E}[e^{-2V(0)}]
; the result is Proposition
4.10
.

The exit-time bound (Proposition
4.12
) does not use homogenization; it transfers a crude
Aronson heat-kernel estimate from the symmetric diffusion to the tilted one via
Girsanov’s theorem. A more refined exit-time bound is accessible via homogenization theory, but we do not pursue this.

Subsection
4.1
sets up the equations and the
Feynman–Kac formula. Subsection
4.2
records the analytic tools.
Subsections
4.3
and
4.4
prove the
velocity and clock estimates, and Subsection
4.5
proves the
exit-time bound.

4.1.
Setup

Following the convention of Subsection
2.4
,
\mathbf{a}
denotes the renamed field
e^{-2V}\mathbf{a}
and
X^{\lambda}
denotes the time-changed diffusion
Y^{\lambda}
. The tilted generator is

\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}f(x)\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-2\lambda x_{1}}\nabla\cdot(e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\mathbf{a}(x)\nabla f)\,.

Lemma 4.2

Proof.

Fix

into triadic subcubes

with centers ranging over

test functions and combined with the fractional Poincaré inequality on cubes

\rho\leq 3^{\delta m/2}
and
h\leq\zeta_{H}m
, the exponents in
m
of the (I) and (II.b) prefactors are bounded above by
\tfrac{7\delta}{24}+\max(0,\tfrac{1}{6}-\beta_{h})\zeta_{H}-\beta_{h}
and
\tfrac{19\delta}{24}-\beta_{h}+(\tfrac{7}{6}+\max(0,\tfrac{1}{6}-\beta_{h})-\tfrac{1}{6})\zeta_{H}
, both strictly negative by the choice of
\zeta_{H}
. For
m\geq\mathcal{X}_{\beta_{h}}(\omega)
enlarged by a deterministic constant depending only on
(d,\Lambda,r_{0},\beta_{h})
, the corresponding
3^{[\cdots]m}
factors are at most
\tfrac{1}{64}
, so

\mathrm{(I)}+\mathrm{(II.b)}\leq\tfrac{1}{32}\mathsf{E}_{z}\,.

For (II.a), Young’s inequality with
(12/11,12)
gives
N_{z}^{11/6}E_{z}^{1/6}\leq\mathsf{E}_{z}
. The prefactor exponent of
m
is at most that of (II.b) (since
13/12<19/12
), so this exponent is also strictly negative, and the same enlargement of
\mathcal{X}_{\beta_{h}}
gives
\mathrm{(II.a)}\leq\tfrac{1}{64}\mathsf{E}_{z}
.

For (II.c), the degree of
(N_{z},E_{z})
is
5/6+1/6=1
. The elementary inequality
N_{z}^{5/6}E_{z}^{1/6}\leq N_{z}+E_{z}\leq\sqrt{2\mathsf{E}_{z}}
and Young’s inequality,

a\sqrt{2\mathsf{E}_{z}}\leq\tfrac{1}{64}\mathsf{E}_{z}+32a^{2},

give

\mathrm{(II.c)}\leq\tfrac{1}{64}\mathsf{E}_{z}+C^{2}\rho^{19/6}3^{-m/3+(1/3-2\beta_{h})M+4h-2m}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}\,.

Substituting
\rho\leq 3^{\delta m/2}
and
h\leq\zeta_{H}m
, the residual factors as

C\rho^{2}3^{2h-2m}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}\cdot 3^{[7\delta/12-2\beta_{h}+(2+\max(0,1/3-2\beta_{h}))\zeta_{H}]m}\,,

with bracketed exponent strictly negative by the choice of
\zeta_{H}
. The same enlargement of
\mathcal{X}_{\beta_{h}}
makes the
3^{[\cdots]m}
factor
\leq 1
, so the residual is bounded by
C\rho^{2}3^{2h-2m}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}
.

Adding the three contributions to the
\mathsf{E}_{z}
coefficient (
\tfrac{1}{32}+\tfrac{1}{64}+\tfrac{1}{64}=\tfrac{1}{16}
),

|A_{z}|\leq\tfrac{1}{16}\mathsf{E}_{z}+C\rho^{2}3^{2h-2m}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}\,,

which is (
4.19
).
∎

We next record the interior
L^{2}
–
L^{\infty}
estimate.

Lemma 4.5

(Interior
L^{2}
–
L^{\infty}
estimate, Theorem 4.1 in
Han and Lin 2011
)
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{a}:\square_{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be uniformly elliptic with constants
(\Lambda^{-1},\Lambda)
, let
c\in L^{\infty}(\square_{M})
with
c\geq 0
and
\|c\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\Lambda
, and let
f\in L^{\infty}(\square_{M})
. If
u\in H^{1}(\square_{M})
is a weak solution of
cu-\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla u)=f
in
\square_{M}
, then there exists
C=C(d,\Lambda)<\infty
such that

\sup_{x\in\square_{M-1}}|u(x)|\leq C\bigl(\|u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{M})}+3^{2M}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\square_{M})}\bigr)\,.

4.3.
Velocity resolvent

We first prove an energy estimate for the velocity resolvent error and then obtain the pointwise estimate by passing to a subcube on which the exponential tilt is uniformly bounded.

Proposition 4.6

(Global
\underline{L}^{2}
bound for the velocity resolvent)
.

There exists
C<\infty
such that, for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega
and every
(m,h,\rho)
in the admissible regime (
4.17
), the homogenization error
U_{m}
defined by (
4.7
) satisfies

(4.29)

\|U_{m}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{m+h})}\leq C\rho^{1/2}3^{h}3^{-\beta_{h}(m+h)}\,.

Proof.

Throughout,
C
denotes a deterministic constant depending only on
(d,\Lambda,r_{0},\beta_{h})
, which may change from line to line. The full-probability event is the intersection of the events from Proposition
3.1
and Lemma
4.4
. Set
M\coloneqq m+h
.

Step 1.
For
1\leq i\leq d
, set
z_{i}\coloneqq U_{m,i}-3^{-m}(w_{i,M}-x_{i})
and
z\coloneqq(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})
. Since
U_{m}\in H^{1}_{0}(\square_{M};\mathbb{R}^{d})
from (
4.7
) and
w_{i,M}-x_{i}\in H^{1}_{0}(\square_{M})
,
z\in H^{1}_{0}(\square_{M};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
w_{i,M}
is
\mathbf{a}
-harmonic,
\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla(w_{i,M}-x_{i}))=-\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}e_{i})
in
\square_{M}
. Substituting into (
4.9
) and using
\nabla\cdot(\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{i})=0
yields

(4.30)

\rho 3^{-2m}z_{i}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla z_{i})-\lambda(\mathbf{a}e_{1})\cdot\nabla z_{i}=T_{i},\qquad T_{i}\coloneqq\lambda 3^{-m}e_{1}\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla w_{i,M}-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{i})-\rho 3^{-3m}(w_{i,M}-x_{i})\,,

componentwise in
\square_{M}
. Multiplying (
4.30
) by
2
puts it in the form (
4.18
) of Lemma
4.4
.

Set

N_{z}=\rho^{1/2}3^{-m}\|z\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{M})},\qquad E_{z}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\square_{M}}\nabla z_{i}\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla z_{i},\qquad\mathsf{E}_{z}=N_{z}^{2}+E_{z}^{2}\,,

and

A_{z}\coloneqq\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\square_{M}}z_{i}\bigl((\mathbf{a}-\bar{\mathbf{a}})e_{1}\bigr)\cdot\nabla z_{i}\,.

Step 2.
We derive the energy identity (
4.31
). Test (
4.30
) against
z_{i}
, integrate
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\square_{M}}
, sum over
i
, and use
z_{i}\in H^{1}_{0}(\square_{M})
to integrate by parts:
-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\square_{M}}z_{i}\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla z_{i})=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\square_{M}}\nabla z_{i}\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla z_{i}
. With the notation above,

N_{z}^{2}+E_{z}^{2}-\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\square_{M}}z_{i}(\mathbf{a}e_{1})\cdot\nabla z_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\square_{M}}z_{i}T_{i}\,.

Split
\mathbf{a}=(\mathbf{a}-\bar{\mathbf{a}})+\bar{\mathbf{a}}
in the drift integral. The
\bar{\mathbf{a}}
-piece vanishes by integration by parts:

\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int z_{i}(\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1})\cdot\nabla z_{i}=\frac{\lambda}{2}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int(\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1})\cdot\nabla|z|^{2}=0.

Here
|z|^{2}\in W^{1,1}_{0}(\square_{M})
since
z\in H^{1}_{0}(\square_{M};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\nabla|z|^{2}=2\sum_{i=1}^{d}z_{i}\nabla z_{i}\in L^{1}
, while
\nabla\cdot(\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1})=0
. Therefore

(4.31)

N_{z}^{2}+E_{z}^{2}=A_{z}+R,\qquad R\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\square_{M}}z_{i}T_{i}\,.

Step 3.
We bound
|R|
. Decompose
R=R_{\mathrm{flux}}+R_{w}
where

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{flux}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lambda 3^{-m}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\square_{M}}z_{i}\bigl(e_{1}\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla w_{i,M}-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{i})\bigr),

\displaystyle R_{w}

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\rho 3^{-3m}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\square_{M}}z_{i}(w_{i,M}-x_{i})\,.

For
R_{\mathrm{flux}}
, the duality
\underline{H}^{1/6}
–
\hat{\phantom{H}}\mathchoice{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\underline{H}^{-1/6}
together with
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|\mathbf{a}\nabla w_{i,M}-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{i}\|^{2}_{\hat{\phantom{H}}\mathchoice{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\underline{H}^{-1/6}}\leq C3^{M/3}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}
from (
3.4
) and the interpolation (
4.27
) give, with
\|z\|_{\underline{L}^{2}}=\rho^{-1/2}3^{m}N_{z}
and
\|\nabla z\|_{\underline{L}^{2}}\leq\sqrt{2\Lambda}E_{z}
,

\displaystyle|R_{\mathrm{flux}}|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda 3^{-m}\sqrt{d}3^{M/6}3^{-\beta_{h}M}\cdot C(\rho^{-1/2}3^{m}N_{z})^{5/6}(\sqrt{2\Lambda}E_{z})^{1/6}

\displaystyle\leq C\rho^{7/12}3^{-m+h/6}3^{-\beta_{h}M}N_{z}^{5/6}E_{z}^{1/6}\,.

The inequalities
r^{a}s^{b}\leq r+s\leq\sqrt{2(r^{2}+s^{2})}
for
a+b=1
and Young’s inequality
r\sqrt{\mathsf{E}_{z}}\leq\tfrac{1}{32}\mathsf{E}_{z}+8r^{2}
then give

|R_{\mathrm{flux}}|\leq\tfrac{1}{32}\mathsf{E}_{z}+C\rho^{7/6}3^{-2m+h/3}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}\leq\tfrac{1}{32}\mathsf{E}_{z}+C\rho^{2}3^{2h-2m}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}\,,

using
\rho^{7/6}\leq\rho^{2}
(since
\rho\geq 1
) and
3^{h/3}\leq 3^{2h}
. For
R_{w}
, Cauchy–Schwarz with
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|w_{i,M}-x_{i}\|^{2}_{\underline{L}^{2}}\leq C3^{2M}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}
from (
3.4
) gives

|R_{w}|\leq\rho 3^{-3m}\rho^{-1/2}3^{m}N_{z}\cdot\sqrt{d}3^{M}3^{-\beta_{h}M}=\sqrt{d}\rho^{1/2}3^{h-m}3^{-\beta_{h}M}N_{z}\leq\tfrac{1}{32}N_{z}^{2}+C\rho 3^{2h-2m}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}\,.

Adding the two bounds,

(4.32)

|R|\leq\tfrac{1}{16}\mathsf{E}_{z}+C\rho^{2}3^{2h-2m}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}\,.

Step 4.
We close the energy estimate via Lemma
4.4
. The PDE (
4.18
) required by that lemma is verified via Step 1; applying it on the regime
\rho\in[1,3^{\delta m/2}]
, with
h\leq\zeta_{H}m
and
m\geq\mathcal{X}_{\beta_{h}}(\omega)
, gives

(4.33)

|A_{z}|\leq\tfrac{1}{16}\mathsf{E}_{z}+C\rho^{2}3^{2h-2m}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}\,.

Combining (
4.31
), (
4.32
), and (
4.33
),

\mathsf{E}_{z}=A_{z}+R\leq|A_{z}|+|R|\leq\tfrac{1}{8}\mathsf{E}_{z}+C\rho^{2}3^{2h-2m}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}\,,

whence
\mathsf{E}_{z}\leq\tfrac{8}{7}C\rho^{2}3^{2h-2m}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}
.

Step 5.
We conclude. The bound
\mathsf{E}_{z}\leq C\rho^{2}3^{2h-2m}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}
gives
\|z\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{M})}\leq C\rho^{1/2}3^{h}3^{-\beta_{h}M}
. Since
U_{m,i}=z_{i}+3^{-m}(w_{i,M}-x_{i})
and
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|3^{-m}(w_{i,M}-x_{i})\|^{2}_{\underline{L}^{2}}\leq C3^{2h}3^{-2\beta_{h}M}
by (
3.4
),

\|U_{m}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{M})}\leq C\rho^{1/2}3^{h}3^{-\beta_{h}M}\,.

Since
M=m+h
, this gives (
4.29
) after enlarging
C
.
∎

The global
\underline{L}^{2}
estimate of Proposition
4.6
holds on the full cube
\square_{m+h}
, but the passage to a pointwise bound via the interior estimate of Lemma
4.5
cannot be carried out there: the constant in Lemma
4.5
depends on the ellipticity ratio of the divergence-form matrix
e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\mathbf{a}
, and on
\square_{m+h}
the weight
e^{2\lambda x_{1}}
ranges over
e^{\pm\lambda 3^{m+h}}=e^{\pm\rho 3^{h}}
, which grows with
\rho
and is unbounded in the admissible regime. The next lemma resolves this by restricting to a concentric subcube
\square_{m_{1}}
, smaller than
\square_{m}
by a factor
3^{-\delta_{0}m}
, on which
|2\lambda x_{1}|\leq 3
and the tilt weight is pinned in
[e^{-3},e^{3}]
.

On
\square_{m_{1}}
the matrix
e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\mathbf{a}
is then uniformly elliptic with constants depending only on
(d,\Lambda)
, and Lemma
4.5
applies with a constant independent of
\rho
and
h
. Passing from the global
\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{m+h})
estimate to a pointwise bound on the smaller cube
\square_{m_{1}}
costs the volume ratio
3^{d(m-m_{1}+h)/2}
. The part coming from
m-m_{1}
is absorbed by the homogenization gain through the choice of
\delta
. The
h
-dependent part is the price of keeping the origin separated from the boundary, and is controlled later by choosing
h
much smaller than
m
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
, let
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq 1/(1-\delta_{0})
, and let
\rho\geq 1

satisfy
\rho\,3^{-\delta_{0}m}\leq 1
. With
\lambda=\rho 3^{-m}
and

m_{1}\coloneqq\lfloor(1-\delta_{0})m\rfloor
,

m-m_{1}\in[\delta_{0}m,\delta_{0}m+1),\qquad 1\leq m_{1}\leq m\,,

and on
\square_{m_{1}}
,

|2\lambda x_{1}|\leq 3,\qquad e^{-3}\leq e^{\pm 2\lambda x_{1}}\leq e^{3}\,.

Proof.

From
m_{1}=\lfloor(1-\delta_{0})m\rfloor\in\bigl((1-\delta_{0})m-1,(1-\delta_{0})m\bigr]
we
get
m-m_{1}\in[\delta_{0}m,\delta_{0}m+1)\subseteq[\delta_{0}m-1,\delta_{0}m+1]
. The
bound
m_{1}\leq m
holds since
\delta_{0}>0
, and
m_{1}\geq 1
holds since

(1-\delta_{0})m\geq 1
by the hypothesis
m\geq 1/(1-\delta_{0})
. On
\square_{m_{1}}
,

|x_{1}|\leq 3^{m_{1}}/2
, so

|2\lambda x_{1}|\leq\lambda\,3^{m_{1}}=\rho\,3^{-(m-m_{1})}\leq\rho\,3^{-\delta_{0}m+1}\leq 3\,,

using
m-m_{1}\geq\delta_{0}m-1
and the hypothesis
\rho\,3^{-\delta_{0}m}\leq 1
. The
weight bound
e^{-3}\leq e^{\pm 2\lambda x_{1}}\leq e^{3}
follows.
∎

Combining the previous two lemmas gives the pointwise velocity resolvent bound.

Proposition 4.8

(Localized
L^{\infty}
bound for the velocity resolvent)
.

There exists
C<\infty
such that, for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega
and every
(m,h,\rho)
in the admissible regime (
4.17
), the velocity resolvent
u_{m}
satisfies

(4.34)

\bigl|3^{-m}u_{m}(0)-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}\bigr|=|U_{m}(0)|\leq C\rho^{1/2}3^{(d/2+1-\beta_{h})h-\delta m}\,.

Proof.

Throughout this proof,
m_{1}\coloneqq\lfloor(1-\delta)m\rfloor
, where
\delta=2\beta_{h}/(d+4)
as in (
4.16
). We may assume
m\geq 1/(1-\delta)
. The hypothesis
\rho\leq 3^{\delta m/2}
gives
\rho 3^{-\delta m}\leq 3^{-\delta m/2}\leq 1
, which is the input required by Lemma
4.7
. Applied with
\delta_{0}=\delta
, that lemma gives, for
m\geq\mathcal{X}_{\beta_{h}}(\omega)
,
\square_{m_{1}}\subseteq\square_{m+h}
and

(4.35)

e^{-3}\leq e^{\pm 2\lambda x_{1}}\leq e^{3}\qquad\text{on }\square_{m_{1}}\,.

Step 1.
We construct a local corrector
\psi
on
\square_{m_{1}}
. The weighted operator
-\nabla\cdot(e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\mathbf{a}\nabla\cdot)
on
H^{1}_{0}(\square_{m_{1}})
is uniformly elliptic with weight
e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\in[e^{-2\lambda 3^{m_{1}}/2},e^{2\lambda 3^{m_{1}}/2}]
on
\square_{m_{1}}
. Consider the boundary value problem

(4.36)

-\frac{1}{2}e^{-2\lambda x_{1}}\nabla\cdot(e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\mathbf{a}\nabla\psi_{j})=\frac{1}{2}e^{-2\lambda x_{1}}\nabla\cdot(e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\mathbf{a}e_{j})\quad\text{in }\square_{m_{1}},\quad\psi_{j}=0\text{ on }\partial\square_{m_{1}}\,.

Then
\psi+x
satisfies
-\nabla\cdot(e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\mathbf{a}\nabla(\psi+x))=0
in
\square_{m_{1}}
and
\psi+x=x
on
\partial\square_{m_{1}}
. Applied componentwise, the maximum principle for the elliptic operator
-\nabla\cdot(e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\mathbf{a}\nabla\cdot)
gives

\|\psi_{j}+x_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\square_{m_{1}})}\leq\sup_{x\in\partial\square_{m_{1}}}|x_{j}|\leq\frac{1}{2}3^{m_{1}}\qquad\text{for every }1\leq j\leq d\,.

Therefore,

(4.37)

\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\square_{m_{1}})}\leq\|\psi+x\|_{L^{\infty}(\square_{m_{1}})}+\|x\|_{L^{\infty}(\square_{m_{1}})}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{d}3^{m_{1}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{d}3^{m_{1}}\leq\sqrt{d}3^{m_{1}}\,.

Step 2.
We derive the equation satisfied by
r_{m}\coloneqq U_{m}-3^{-m}\psi
on
\square_{m_{1}}
. Combining (
4.8
) with (
4.36
),

\rho 3^{-2m}r_{m}-\frac{1}{2}e^{-2\lambda x_{1}}\nabla\cdot\bigl(e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\mathbf{a}\nabla r_{m}\bigr)=-\rho 3^{-2m}\bigl(\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}+3^{-m}\psi\bigr)\quad\text{in }\square_{m_{1}}\,.

Multiplying both sides by
2e^{2\lambda x_{1}}
produces the divergence form

c(x)r_{m}-\nabla\cdot\bigl(e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\mathbf{a}\nabla r_{m}\bigr)=f(x)\quad\text{in }\square_{m_{1}}\,,

where, by (
4.35
),

c(x)\coloneqq 2\rho 3^{-2m}e^{2\lambda x_{1}},\qquad\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(\square_{m_{1}})}\leq 2e^{3}\rho 3^{-2m}\,,

f(x)\coloneqq-2\rho 3^{-2m}e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\bigl(\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}+3^{-m}\psi\bigr),\qquad\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\square_{m_{1}})}\leq C\rho 3^{-2m}\,.

Since
\rho 3^{-\delta m}\leq 1
and
\delta<1
,
\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(\square_{m_{1}})}\leq 2e^{3}3^{-(2-\delta)m}\leq 1\leq e^{3}\Lambda
for
m
large enough. The matrix
e^{2\lambda x_{1}}\mathbf{a}
is uniformly elliptic on
\square_{m_{1}}
with constants
(e^{-3}\Lambda^{-1},e^{3}\Lambda)
, and Lemma
4.5
applies with
e^{3}\Lambda
in place of
\Lambda
.

Step 3.
We bound the
L^{2}
norm of
r_{m}
. By (
4.37
),
\|3^{-m}\psi\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{m_{1}})}\leq\sqrt{d}3^{-(m-m_{1})}\leq 3\sqrt{d}\cdot 3^{-\delta m}
. From
\square_{m_{1}}\subseteq\square_{m+h}
,
|\square_{m+h}|/|\square_{m_{1}}|=3^{d(m-m_{1}+h)}
, and Proposition
4.6
,

\|U_{m}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{m_{1}})}\leq 3^{d(m-m_{1}+h)/2}\|U_{m}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{m+h})}\leq C\rho^{1/2}3^{d(m-m_{1}+h)/2+h-\beta_{h}(m+h)}\,.

Using
m-m_{1}\leq\delta m+1
and
\delta=2\beta_{h}/(d+4)
,

\tfrac{d}{2}(m-m_{1})-\beta_{h}m\leq\tfrac{d\beta_{h}m}{d+4}-\beta_{h}m=-\tfrac{4\beta_{h}m}{d+4}=-2\delta m\leq-\delta m\,,

up to an additive constant absorbed into
C
. The coefficient
d/2+1-\beta_{h}
is positive since
\beta_{h}<1
. Hence

\|U_{m}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{m_{1}})}\leq C\rho^{1/2}3^{(d/2+1-\beta_{h})h-\delta m}\,.

By the triangle inequality and
3^{-m}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\square_{m_{1}})}\leq 3\sqrt{d}\cdot 3^{-\delta m}
,

(4.38)

\|r_{m}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{m_{1}})}\leq\|U_{m}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\square_{m_{1}})}+3\sqrt{d}\cdot 3^{-\delta m}\leq C\rho^{1/2}3^{(d/2+1-\beta_{h})h-\delta m}\,,

using
\rho\geq 1
and
h\geq 0
to absorb the additive

4.5.
Exit-time estimate

The last input for the Feynman–Kac decomposition is an exit estimate. We first derive a maximal displacement bound for the symmetric diffusion from Aronson’s heat-kernel estimate, and then extend it to the tilted diffusion via Girsanov’s theorem on a time interval
[0,T_{0}]
with
T_{0}\asymp\rho^{-1}3^{2m+h}
.

Lemma 4.11

(Displacement tail for the symmetric diffusion)
.

Fix an environment
\omega
. Let
Z_{t}
be a Feller diffusion with generator
\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla)
, where
\mathbf{a}
is symmetric, uniformly elliptic, and Lipschitz with constants
(\Lambda^{-1},\Lambda)
. There exist constants
c,C>0
depending only on
(d,\Lambda)
such that, for every
R>0
and
T>0
and every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(4.50)

P_{x}^{0,\omega}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq T}|Z_{s}-x|\geq R\right)\leq C\exp\left(-c\frac{R^{2}}{T}\right)\,.

Proof.

Apply the fundamental-solution estimate of
Aronson 1967
to the equation
\partial_{t}v=\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla v)
. Since the coefficient matrix
\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}
has ellipticity constants depending only on
\Lambda
, the transition density
p^{\omega}(t,x,y)
of
Z
with respect to Lebesgue measure satisfies

(4.51)

p^{\omega}(t,x,y)\leq Ct^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{Ct}\right),\qquad t>0\,,

with
C<\infty
depending only on
d
and the ellipticity ratio
\Lambda
(the factor
\tfrac{1}{2}
in the generator only changes
C
). In particular, integrating (
4.51
) over
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(x)
gives

(4.52)

P_{x}^{0,\omega}(|Z_{t}-x|\geq r)\leq C\exp\left(-c\frac{r^{2}}{t}\right),\qquad r,t>0\,.

Let
\tau_{R}\coloneqq\inf\{s\geq 0:\ |Z_{s}-x|\geq R\}
. There exists
\vartheta_{0}=\vartheta_{0}(d,\Lambda)>0
such that for
T>\vartheta_{0}R^{2}
the conclusion (
4.50
) is trivial after enlarging
C
by
\exp(c/\vartheta_{0})
, so we may assume
T\leq\vartheta_{0}R^{2}
. For every
y
with
|y-x|=R
and every
z\in B_{R/2}(x)
,
|z-y|\geq R/2
. Applying (
4.51
) and integrating over
B_{R/2}(x)
, for every
0<u\leq T\leq\vartheta_{0}R^{2}
,

P_{y}^{0,\omega}(|Z_{u}-x|<R/2)\leq C\left(\frac{R^{2}}{u}\right)^{d/2}\exp\left(-c\frac{R^{2}}{u}\right)\leq C\exp(-c/\vartheta_{0})\,,

using
r^{d/2}e^{-cr/2}\leq C(d,c)
for
r>0
, applied with
r=R^{2}/u
, in the last step. Choose
\vartheta_{0}
so that
C\exp(-c/\vartheta_{0})\leq 1/2
. By continuity of paths and the strong Markov property at
\tau_{R}
,

P_{x}^{0,\omega}(|Z_{T}-x|\geq R/2)\geq\frac{1}{2}P_{x}^{0,\omega}(\tau_{R}\leq T)\,.

Combining this with (
4.52
) applied with
r=R/2
yields (
4.50
).
∎

We now transfer this displacement bound to the tilted diffusion.

Proposition 4.12

(Laplace transform of the exit time)
.

Let
\mathbf{a}
be a symmetric uniformly elliptic Lipschitz coefficient field with constants
(\Lambda^{-1},\Lambda)
, and let
\tau_{m,h}
be the exit time defined in (
4.5
). For every
\kappa\in(0,1]
there exist
c_{\kappa},C_{\kappa}>0
depending only on
(\kappa,d,\Lambda)
such that, for every
\rho\geq 1
and
m,h\in\mathbb{N}
with
\lambda=\rho 3^{-m}
,

E^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}\bigl[\exp(-\kappa\rho 3^{-2m}\tau_{m,h})\bigr]\leq C_{\kappa}\exp(-c_{\kappa}3^{h})\,.

Proof.

Pick a small
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(d,\Lambda)>0
to be fixed below, and set
T_{0}\coloneqq(\varepsilon_{0}/2)3^{2m+h}/\rho
. With this choice
\kappa\rho 3^{-2m}T_{0}=(\kappa\varepsilon_{0}/2)3^{h}
. Let
\mathsf{C}\coloneqq C(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the canonical path space with coordinate process
Z_{s}(\xi)\coloneqq\xi(s)
and canonical filtration. Let
P^{0,\omega}_{0}
denote the law on
\mathsf{C}
of the symmetric reversible diffusion with generator
\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla)
, and
P^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}
the law of the tilted diffusion with generator
\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla)+\lambda\mathbf{a}e_{1}\cdot\nabla
. Set

\tau\coloneqq\inf\{t\geq 0:\ Z_{t}\notin\square_{m+h}\}\,,

a stopping time for the canonical filtration;
\tau
has the same distribution as
\tau_{m,h}
under
P^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}
. Under
P^{0,\omega}_{0}
, the canonical martingale
M_{s}\coloneqq Z_{s}-\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{a}(Z_{u})du
is a continuous local martingale with quadratic variation
\langle M^{i},M^{j}\rangle_{s}=\int_{0}^{s}\mathbf{a}_{ij}(Z_{u})du
. Since exit from
\square_{m+h}
before time
T_{0}
implies
\sup_{0\leq s\leq T_{0}}|Z_{s}|\geq 3^{m+h}/2
, Lemma
4.11
applied with
R=3^{m+h}/2
gives

(4.53)

P^{0,\omega}_{0}(\tau\leq T_{0})\leq C\exp\left(-c\frac{(3^{m+h}/2)^{2}}{T_{0}}\right)\leq C\exp\left(-\frac{c}{2\varepsilon_{0}}\rho 3^{h}\right)\,.

By Girsanov’s theorem on canonical path space,
P^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}
is absolutely continuous on
\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}
with respect to
P^{0,\omega}_{0}
, with density

D_{T_{0}}=\exp\left(\lambda\int_{0}^{T_{0}}e_{1}\cdot dM_{s}-\tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}e_{1}\cdot\mathbf{a}(Z_{s})e_{1}ds\right)\,.

Set
N_{t}\coloneqq\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e_{1}\cdot dM_{s}
, so that
D_{T_{0}}=\exp(N_{T_{0}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle N\rangle_{T_{0}})
. Since
e_{1}\cdot\mathbf{a}e_{1}\leq\Lambda
, the quadratic variation
\langle N\rangle_{T_{0}}=\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}e_{1}\cdot\mathbf{a}(Z_{s})e_{1}ds
is bounded deterministically by
\Lambda\lambda^{2}T_{0}
. Moreover,

D_{T_{0}}^{2}=\exp(2N_{T_{0}}-2\langle N\rangle_{T_{0}})\exp(\langle N\rangle_{T_{0}})\,.

The first factor is a nonnegative
P^{0,\omega}_{0}
-local martingale and therefore a supermartingale of expectation at most
1
. The second factor is bounded by
\exp(\Lambda\lambda^{2}T_{0})
, so
E^{0,\omega}_{0}[D_{T_{0}}^{2}]\leq\exp(\Lambda\lambda^{2}T_{0})=\exp(C\varepsilon_{0}\rho 3^{h})
. By Cauchy–Schwarz and (
4.53
),

P^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}(\tau\leq T_{0})\leq E^{0,\omega}_{0}[D_{T_{0}}^{2}]^{1/2}P^{0,\omega}_{0}(\tau\leq T_{0})^{1/2}\leq C\exp\left(\tfrac{C\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\rho 3^{h}-\tfrac{c}{4\varepsilon_{0}}\rho 3^{h}\right)\,.

Choose
\varepsilon_{0}
so small that
c/(4\varepsilon_{0})-C\varepsilon_{0}/2\geq c^{\prime}>0
; then
P^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}(\tau\leq T_{0})\leq C\exp(-c^{\prime}\rho 3^{h})
. Consequently,

E^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}[e^{-\kappa\rho 3^{-2m}\tau_{m,h}}]\leq P^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}(\tau\leq T_{0})+e^{-\kappa\rho 3^{-2m}T_{0}}\leq C_{\kappa}e^{-c_{\kappa}3^{h}}\,.

∎

5.
Renewal estimates

This section proves a quenched law of large numbers for the time-changed diffusion
X^{\lambda}
that is uniform in the tilt parameter on a random window
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}(\omega)]
. The main result is Corollary
5.13
, which controls both the displacement error
E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|X^{\lambda}(t)-\ell(\lambda)t|
and the clock error
\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|A^{\lambda}(t)-\eta(\lambda)t|
uniformly on that window.

The argument builds on the regeneration construction of
Shen 2003
, in the
\lambda
-dependent form of
Gantert et al. 2012
. For a fixed
\lambda
, the construction cuts the path of
X^{\lambda}
at random times
0=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\cdots
into blocks whose displacement increments
\Delta X_{k}^{\lambda}
are i.i.d. and whose centered versions
\Delta X_{k}^{\lambda}-\ell(\lambda)\Delta\tau_{k}
have mean zero. Each block has duration of order
\lambda^{-2}
and displacement of order
\lambda^{-1}
. A law of large numbers at fixed
\lambda
follows from the i.i.d. structure; this is
Gantert et al. 2012
. The block moment bounds, the exponential tail of
\tau_{1}
, and the linear velocity bound
|\ell(\lambda)|\leq C\lambda
are also from
Gantert et al. 2012
and are recorded below in Lemmas
5.4
–
5.6
.

The new ingredient is uniformity in
\lambda
. The annealed moment bound is already uniform: an interval
[0,t]
contains about
\lambda^{2}t
blocks, each contributing a centered displacement of size
\lambda^{-1}
, so the sum fluctuates on scale
\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}t}=\sqrt{t}
. Rosenthal’s inequality and the Doob maximal inequality make this cancellation precise (Proposition
5.8
). The upgrade to a quenched bound uniform in
\lambda
then proceeds by discretization. On a dyadic grid in
\lambda
, Markov’s inequality turns the annealed bound into a quenched bound at each grid point that fails with rapidly summable probability; Borel–Cantelli produces a random index beyond which all grid points are good. A Girsanov second-moment comparison (Lemma
5.10
) transfers the bound from each grid point to nearby values of
\lambda
, filling the gaps.

The clock
A^{\lambda}
requires an additional step. Its block increments
\Delta A_{k}^{\lambda}=\int_{\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k+1}}e^{-2V(X^{\lambda}(s))}\,ds
depend on the environment along the path, so the existing renewal theorems of
Shen 2003
and
Gantert et al. 2012
, which record only the path, do not directly apply. A renewal identity at a general regeneration time that includes the environment (Lemma
5.2
, proved in Section
7
) shows that the clock blocks are stationary and
2
-dependent, after which the annealed clock bound (Proposition
5.9
) then follows by the same Rosenthal argument.

Subsection
5.2
records the i.i.d. and
2
-dependent block structure from
Shen 2003
and
Gantert et al. 2012
; Subsection
5.3
derives the annealed bounds from these inputs; and Subsection
5.4
carries out the upgrade to a uniform window.

5.1.
Setup

The regeneration times
0=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\cdots
are constructed following
Gantert et al. 2012
, which adapts the method of
Shen 2003
to a
\lambda
-dependent spatial scale. The construction requires auxiliary randomness: we enlarge the probability space to carry the diffusion path
X^{\lambda}
together with i.i.d. Bernoulli marks
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
. We write
\widehat{P}^{\lambda,\omega}_{x}
,
\widehat{E}^{\lambda,\omega}_{x}
for the quenched law and expectation on this enlarged space, and
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\lambda}_{x}\coloneqq\int\widehat{P}_{x}^{\lambda,\omega}\,d\mathbb{Q}(\omega)
,
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}^{\lambda}_{x}
for the annealed versions. The
X
-marginal of
\widehat{P}_{x}^{\lambda,\omega}
equals
P_{x}^{\lambda,\omega}
, so for functionals of the path alone,
\widehat{E}_{x}^{\lambda,\omega}
and
E_{x}^{\lambda,\omega}
agree.

The construction depends on a deterministic integer
l=l(d,\Lambda,r_{0})\in\mathbb{N}
and the induced spatial scale
R(\lambda)\coloneqq l/\lambda
. We summarize the procedure of
Gantert et al. 2012
. Write

M(t)\coloneqq\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}e_{1}\cdot(X^{\lambda}(s)-X^{\lambda}(0))

for the running forward maximum, and let
\lceil\cdot\rceil_{\lambda}
denote rounding up to the next multiple of
\lambda^{-2}
, with
\lceil\infty\rceil_{\lambda}=\infty
. The successive ladder times
\lceil V_{k}\rceil_{\lambda}
are the rounded times at which
e_{1}\cdot(X^{\lambda}-X^{\lambda}(0))
first exceeds
M(\lceil V_{k-1}\rceil_{\lambda})+R(\lambda)
. At each ladder time, the Bernoulli variable attached to that lattice time is checked; if it equals
1
, the candidate proceeds to the bridge step. The successful ladder time is followed by one additional time step of duration
\lambda^{-2}
, giving a candidate time
S
; during this bridge step the path is confined to a ball of radius
6R(\lambda)
by
Gantert et al. 2012
. At
S
the no-backtracking condition is tested: with

T_{-R(\lambda)}\coloneqq\inf\{t\geq 0:e_{1}\cdot(X^{\lambda}(t)-X^{\lambda}(0))\leq-R(\lambda)\},\qquad D\coloneqq\lceil T_{-R(\lambda)}\rceil_{\lambda}\,,

the event
\{D=\infty\}
is that the path never drops
R(\lambda)
below its initial
e_{1}
-level. If
D\circ\theta_{S}=\infty
, then
\tau_{1}\coloneqq S
; if
D\circ\theta_{S}<\infty
, the ladder search resumes from the backtracking time
S+D\circ\theta_{S}
. Subsequent regeneration times satisfy
\tau_{k+1}=\tau_{k}+\tau_{1}\circ\theta_{\tau_{k}}
. The integer
l
is fixed large enough that

(5.1)

2l>r_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}(D=\infty)\geq c_{0}>0\quad\text{uniformly in }\lambda\in(0,1]\,;

the first condition gives finite-range decoupling at distance
2R(\lambda)
, while the second ensures that each regeneration attempt succeeds with probability bounded below uniformly in
\lambda
.

By the construction
Gantert et al. 2012
, all
\tau_{k}
lie in
\lambda^{-2}\mathbb{N}
, and by
Gantert et al. 2012
,
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}(\tau_{k}<\infty)=1
for every
k\geq 1
. The
k
-th block is the stretch of path on
[\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}]
; we record its increments

\Delta\tau_{k}\coloneqq\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k},\qquad\Delta X_{k}^{\lambda}\coloneqq X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k+1})-X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k}),\qquad\Delta A_{k}^{\lambda}\coloneqq\int_{\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k+1}}e^{-2V(X^{\lambda}(s))}\,ds\,,

and the truncated block

(5.2)

\mathcal{Z}_{k}\coloneqq\bigl((X^{\lambda}((\tau_{k}+t)\wedge(\tau_{k+1}-\lambda^{-2}))-X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k}))_{t\geq 0},\ \Delta X_{k}^{\lambda},\ \Delta\tau_{k}\bigr)\,,

which discards the final segment of duration
\lambda^{-2}
from each block.

First,
\lambda^{2}\Delta\tau_{k}
is a positive integer for every
k\geq 0
:

(5.3)

\lambda^{2}\Delta\tau_{k}\in\{1,2,\ldots\}\qquad\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}\text{-a.s.}

Indeed, the regeneration times lie in
\lambda^{-2}\mathbb{N}
and increase strictly.
Moreover, the paragraph following
Gantert et al. 2012
gives

\tau_{1}\geq 2\lambda^{-2}
, and the recursion

\tau_{k+1}=\tau_{k}+\tau_{1}\circ\theta_{\tau_{k}}
gives

\tau_{k}\geq 2k\lambda^{-2}
, so the deterministic slice

\{\tau_{k}=n\lambda^{-2}\}
is empty unless
n\geq 2k
. Second, within each
block, the path during the last interval of duration
\lambda^{-2}
remains in a bounded
neighborhood of its starting point:

(5.4)

\sup_{\tau_{k+1}-\lambda^{-2}\leq s\leq\tau_{k+1}}|X^{\lambda}(s)-X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k+1}-\lambda^{-2})|\leq 11R(\lambda)=\frac{11l}{\lambda}\,,

which follows from
Gantert et al. 2012
.

5.2.
Block structure

For
k\geq 1
the displacement increments are i.i.d. under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
(Lemma
5.1
), while the clock–duration pairs
(\Delta A_{k}^{\lambda},\Delta\tau_{k})
are stationary and
2
-dependent (Lemma
5.3
).

Lemma 5.1

(Regeneration blocks and velocity)
.

The blocks
\{\mathcal{Z}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
are independent under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
, and
\{\mathcal{Z}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are i.i.d. with common law equal to that of
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
conditioned on
\{D=\infty\}
. The limit
\ell(\lambda)
in (
2.8
) exists
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
-almost surely and equals

\ell(\lambda)=\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\Delta X_{1}^{\lambda}]}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\Delta\tau_{1}]}\,.

Proof.

The a.s. finiteness hypothesis in
Gantert et al. 2012
is supplied by
Gantert et al. 2012
. Theorem 5.6 gives exactly the independence and common conditional law of the truncated path blocks
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
in (
5.2
). The renewal formula for the velocity is
Gantert et al. 2012
applied to the same time-changed process.
∎

For a Borel set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, write
\mathcal{H}_{S}
for the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
(\mathbf{a},e^{-2V})|_{S}
. When
S
depends on a random variable
Y
, the notation
\mathcal{H}_{\{z:e_{1}\cdot z\leq e_{1}\cdot Y-4R\}}
denotes the
\sigma
-algebra generated by bounded variables
F(Y,\omega)
such that
F(y,\cdot)
is
\mathcal{H}_{\{z:e_{1}\cdot z\leq e_{1}\cdot y-4R\}}
-measurable for every
y
.

Lemma 5.2

(Conditional renewal at regeneration times)
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
R=R(\lambda)
. Define

\mathsf{L}_{k}\coloneqq\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:e_{1}\cdot y\leq e_{1}\cdot X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k})-4R\}\,,\qquad\mathsf{R}_{+}\coloneqq\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:e_{1}\cdot y\geq-2R\}\,.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{k}
denote the
\sigma
-algebra generated by:

(a)

the time
\tau_{k}
, the diffusion path
(X^{\lambda}(s))_{0\leq s\leq\tau_{k}-\lambda^{-2}}
, and the endpoint
X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k})
;

(b)

the auxiliary Bernoulli variables
(Y_{n})_{0\leq n<\lambda^{2}(\tau_{k}-\lambda^{-2})}
;

(c)

the environment
(\mathbf{a},e^{-2V})|_{\mathsf{L}_{k}}
.

Let the shifted future data be

\mathcal{Y}_{k}\coloneqq\bigl((X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k}+t)-X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k}))_{t\geq 0},\ (Y_{\lambda^{2}\tau_{k}+n})_{n\geq 0},\ T_{X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k})}(\mathbf{a},e^{-2V})\bigr|_{\mathsf{R}_{+}}\bigr)\,,

where
T_{x}(\mathbf{a},e^{-2V})(z)\coloneqq(\mathbf{a},e^{-2V})(x+z)
, and let
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
be given by the same formula with
k=0
and
\tau_{0}=0
. Then for every bounded measurable function
\Phi
of such triples and every bounded
\mathcal{G}_{k}
-measurable random variable
H
,

(5.5)

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{k})H]=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\mid D=\infty]\,\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[H]\,.

In particular,
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
is independent of
\mathcal{G}_{k}
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
, and the law of
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
equals the law of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\,\cdot\mid D=\infty]
.
Moreover, writing
M_{i}\coloneqq(\Delta A_{i}^{\lambda},\Delta\tau_{i})
for the clock-duration block:

(i)

for every integer
i
with
1\leq i\leq k-2
, the block
M_{i}
is
\mathcal{G}_{k}
-measurable;

(ii)

there is a measurable map
\Psi
, defined on the common state space of the shifted future data, such that

(M_{k},M_{k+1},\ldots)=\Psi(\mathcal{Y}_{k})

holds
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
-almost surely.

In item (a), the path is recorded only up to
\tau_{k}-\lambda^{-2}
, not up to
\tau_{k}
; the endpoint
X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k})
is included separately because it determines the boundary of
\mathsf{L}_{k}
and the spatial shift in
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
. The proof first treats functions of the shifted future data that split into a path-mark factor and an environment factor, and then extends to the bounded measurable
\Phi
stated here by a monotone-class argument. The proof of Lemma
5.2
is given in Section
7
.

The displacement increments
\Delta X_{k}^{\lambda}
are i.i.d. for
k\geq 1
by Lemma
5.1
. The clock-and-duration increments
(\Delta A_{k}^{\lambda},\Delta\tau_{k})
are not i.i.d., because the post-
\tau_{k}
clock contribution depends on
e^{-2V}
on a half-space whose
\sigma
-algebra is independent of the past only at distance
2R
.

Lemma 5.3

(Clock blocks are stationary and 2-dependent)
.

Let
M_{k}\coloneqq(\Delta A_{k}^{\lambda},\Delta\tau_{k})
for
k\geq 1
. The sequence
\{M_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is strictly stationary and
2
-dependent under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
: for every
r\geq 1
the law of
(M_{j},\ldots,M_{j+r-1})
is independent of
j
, and for every
I,J\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots\}
with
\mathrm{dist}(I,J)\geq 3
,

(5.6)

\sigma(M_{i}:i\in I)\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma(M_{j}:j\in J)\quad\text{are independent}\,.

Proof.

Throughout,
\mathcal{G}_{k}
,
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
, and the measurable map
\Psi
are as in Lemma
5.2
.

Step 1.
We prove independence of past and future at separation
3
. Fix
j\geq 3
. By Lemma
5.2
(i), applied with
k=j-1
, every block
M_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq j-3
is
\mathcal{G}_{j-1}
-measurable. By Lemma
5.2
(ii), applied with
k=j-1
,

(M_{j-1},M_{j},M_{j+1},\ldots)=\Psi(\mathcal{Y}_{j-1})

almost surely. Therefore
(M_{j},M_{j+1},\ldots)
is, up to a null set, a measurable function of
\mathcal{Y}_{j-1}
.

For bounded measurable
F
and
G
, define
\Phi_{G}
on the state space of the shifted future data by applying
G
to the sequence obtained from
\Psi
after deleting its first coordinate. Then

G(M_{j},M_{j+1},\ldots)=\Phi_{G}(\mathcal{Y}_{j-1})

almost surely, and
F(M_{1},\ldots,M_{j-3})
is
\mathcal{G}_{j-1}
-measurable. Applying (
5.5
) at
k=j-1
with
H=F(M_{1},\ldots,M_{j-3})
and
\Phi=\Phi_{G}
, and then applying the same identity with
H\equiv 1
, gives

(5.7)

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[F(M_{1},\ldots,M_{j-3})G(M_{j},M_{j+1},\ldots)]=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[F(M_{1},\ldots,M_{j-3})]\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[G(M_{j},M_{j+1},\ldots)]\,,

so
\sigma(M_{i}:i\leq j-3)\perp\sigma(M_{j},M_{j+1},\dots)
for every
j\geq 3
.

Step 2.
We deduce (
5.6
) from (
5.7
), first for finite sets. Let
I,J\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots\}
be finite and disjoint with
\mathrm{dist}(I,J)\geq 3
. Partition
I\cup J
into maximal runs
C_{1},\ldots,C_{q}
of indices in
I\cup J
where consecutive indices differ by at most
2
. By definition, two consecutive runs are separated by a gap of at least
3
.

No run contains both an
I
-index and a
J
-index. Indeed, otherwise traversing the run from first index to last would produce a transition from an
I
-index
i_{l}
to a
J
-index
i_{l+1}
, or conversely, with
|i_{l+1}-i_{l}|\leq 2
, contradicting
\mathrm{dist}(I,J)\geq 3
. Hence each run is contained in either
I
or
J
.

Iterating (
5.7
) at the boundaries between runs shows that the run
\sigma
-fields
\sigma(M_{i}:i\in C_{r})
are mutually independent. Therefore
\sigma(M_{i}:i\in I)
and
\sigma(M_{j}:j\in J)
are products of disjoint subcollections of these mutually independent run
\sigma
-fields, and hence are independent.

For general separated sets, let
\mathcal{C}_{I}
be the cylinder
\pi
-system generated by events
\{(\Delta A_{i}^{\lambda},\Delta\tau_{i})\in B_{i}:i\in I_{0}\}
with finite
I_{0}\subseteq I
, and define
\mathcal{C}_{J}
similarly. The finite-set result gives independence on
\mathcal{C}_{I}\times\mathcal{C}_{J}
, and Dynkin’s
\pi
-
\lambda
theorem extends this to
\sigma(M_{i}:i\in I)
and
\sigma(M_{j}:j\in J)
.

Step 3.
We prove stationarity. Fix
j\geq 1
and
r\geq 1
. Let
\Psi_{r}
be the first
r
coordinates of the sequence-valued map
\Psi
. By Lemma
5.2
(ii),

\Psi_{r}(\mathcal{Y}_{j})=(M_{j},\ldots,M_{j+r-1})

almost surely. For every bounded measurable
g
on
(\mathbb{R}\times[\lambda^{-2},\infty))^{r}
, Lemma
5.2
at
k=j
with
H\equiv 1
and
\Phi=g\circ\Psi_{r}
gives

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\bigl[g(M_{j},\ldots,M_{j+r-1})\bigr]=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\bigl[g(\Psi_{r}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}))\mid D=\infty\bigr]\,.

The right-hand side does not depend on
j
, so
\{M_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is stationary.
∎

5.3.
Annealed moment bounds

Lemma 5.4

(Maximal moment bound,
[
Gantert et al. 2012
, Lemma 4.5]
)
.

For every
p\geq 1
, there exists a finite constant
C=C(p,d,\Lambda)>0
such that for every environment
\omega
and every
\lambda\in(0,1]
,

(5.8)

E^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}\big[\max_{0\leq s\leq t}|X^{\lambda}(s)|^{p}\big]\leq C(\lambda t)^{p}\qquad\text{for every }t\geq\lambda^{-2}\,.

The first regeneration time has quenched exponential tails on the diffusive timescale
\lambda^{-2}
. After annealing, the uniform lower bound on
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}(D=\infty)
implies the same tail for every later block.

Lemma 5.5

(Regeneration-time tails)
.

There exist constants
C=C(d,\Lambda,r_{0})>0
and
c=c(d,\Lambda,r_{0})>0
such that for every
\lambda\in(0,1]
and
t>0
,

(5.9)

\sup_{\omega}\widehat{P}^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}\bigl(\tau_{1}\geq\lambda^{-2}t\bigr)\leq Ce^{-ct}\,,

and for every
k\geq 1
,

(5.10)

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\lambda}_{0}\bigl(\Delta\tau_{k}\geq\lambda^{-2}t\bigr)\leq Ce^{-ct}\,.

Proof.

The first estimate is the quenched bound proved in
Gantert et al. 2012
. For
k\geq 1
, Lemma
5.1
gives the law of
\Delta\tau_{k}
as the law of
\tau_{1}
conditioned on
\{D=\infty\}
. Together with (
5.1
),

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\lambda}_{0}\bigl(\Delta\tau_{k}\geq\lambda^{-2}t\bigr)\leq c_{0}^{-1}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\lambda}_{0}\bigl(\tau_{1}\geq\lambda^{-2}t\bigr)\leq Ce^{-ct}\,,

after increasing
C
.
∎

Lemma 5.6

(Linear bound on the regeneration velocity,
[
Gantert et al. 2012
, (4.25)]
)
.

There exists
C=C(d,\Lambda,r_{0})<\infty
such that for every
\lambda\in(0,1]
,

(5.11)

|\ell(\lambda)|\leq C\lambda\,.

Lemma 5.7

(Regeneration block moment bounds)
.

Let

R_{k}\coloneqq\max_{0\leq s\leq\Delta\tau_{k}}|X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k}+s)-X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k})|\,.

For every integer
r\geq 1
, there exists
C=C(r,d,\Lambda,r_{0})<\infty
such that, for every
\lambda\in(0,1]
and every
k\geq 0
,

(5.12)

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[(\Delta\tau_{k})^{r}]\leq C\lambda^{-2r},\qquad\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[R_{k}^{r}]\leq C\lambda^{-r}\,.

Consequently,

(5.13)

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|\Delta X_{k}^{\lambda}|^{r}\leq C\lambda^{-r}\qquad\text{for }k\geq 0\,,

and, for

W_{k}=\Delta X_{k}^{\lambda}-\ell(\lambda)\Delta\tau_{k}\,,

(5.14)

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|W_{k}|^{r}\leq C\lambda^{-r}\qquad\text{for }k\geq 0\,.

Proof.

Step 1.
We first treat
k=0
. Integration by parts and the quenched tail (
5.9
) give

\sup_{\omega}\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[\tau_{1}^{r}]=\sup_{\omega}r\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{r-1}\widehat{P}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}(\tau_{1}\geq u)du\leq r\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{r-1}\sup_{\omega}\widehat{P}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}(\tau_{1}\geq u)du\leq C\lambda^{-2r}\,.

For
R_{0}
, decompose on the events
\{\tau_{1}\in[\lambda^{-2}n,\lambda^{-2}(n+1))\}
for
n\geq 0
. Cauchy–Schwarz, Lemma
5.4
with
p=2r
at
t=\lambda^{-2}(n+1)\geq\lambda^{-2}
, and (
5.9
) give

\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[R_{0}^{r}]\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\bigl(\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[\max_{s\leq\lambda^{-2}(n+1)}|X^{\lambda}(s)|^{2r}]\bigr)^{1/2}\widehat{P}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}(\tau_{1}\geq\lambda^{-2}n)^{1/2}\,.

The right-hand side is at most

C\sum_{n\geq 0}\lambda^{-r}(n+1)^{r}e^{-cn/2}\leq C\lambda^{-r}

uniformly in
\omega
. Integrating over
\mathbb{Q}
proves (
5.12
) for
k=0
.

Step 2.
We next treat
k\geq 1
. By Lemma
5.1
, the blocks
\{\mathcal{Z}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are i.i.d. with the law of
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
conditioned on
\{D=\infty\}
. Since
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}(D=\infty)\geq c_{0}>0
, every nonnegative functional
F
of
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
satisfies

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[F\mid D=\infty]\leq c_{0}^{-1}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[F]\,.

Applying this to
F=(\Delta\tau_{0})^{r}
gives the first bound in (
5.12
) for every
k\geq 1
.

Step 3.
We control the range over a block. Decompose
R_{k}=R_{k}^{\rm trunc}\vee R_{k}^{\rm tail}
, where
R_{k}^{\rm trunc}
runs over
s\leq\Delta\tau_{k}-\lambda^{-2}
and
R_{k}^{\rm tail}
over
s\in[\Delta\tau_{k}-\lambda^{-2},\Delta\tau_{k}]
. The truncated max is a functional of
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
alone, so Step 2 gives
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[(R_{k}^{\rm trunc})^{r}]\leq C\lambda^{-r}
for
k\geq 1
. On the final segment, (
5.4
) and the triangle inequality give

R_{k}^{\rm tail}\leq R_{k}^{\rm trunc}+\frac{11l}{\lambda}.

Hence
R_{k}\leq R_{k}^{\rm trunc}+11l/\lambda
, and the second bound in (
5.12
) follows.

Step 4.
It remains to bound
\Delta X_{k}^{\lambda}
and
W_{k}
. The bound (
5.13
) follows from
|\Delta X_{k}^{\lambda}|\leq R_{k}
, and (
5.14
) follows from (
5.11
), (
5.12
), and (
5.13
).
∎

Proposition 5.8

(Annealed polynomial moment bound for the displacement)
.

For every integer
M\geq 2
, there exists
C=C(M,d,\Lambda,r_{0})<\infty
such that for every
\lambda\in(0,1]
and every
t\geq\lambda^{-2}
,

(5.15)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\widehat{E}^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}\big|X^{\lambda}(t)-\ell(\lambda)t\big|^{M}\leq Ct^{M/2}\,.

Proof.

Let

\mathbf{n}(t)\coloneqq\max\{n\geq 0:\tau_{n}\leq t\}\,.

By the triangle inequality applied pointwise,

|X^{\lambda}(t)-\ell(\lambda)t|^{M}\leq 3^{M-1}\bigl(|X^{\lambda}(t)-X^{\lambda}(\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)})|^{M}+|X^{\lambda}(\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)})-\ell(\lambda)\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)}|^{M}+|\ell(\lambda)|^{M}|t-\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)}|^{M}\bigr)\,,

so taking
\widehat{E}^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}
and then
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}
,

(5.16)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\widehat{E}^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}\big|X^{\lambda}(t)-\ell(\lambda)t\big|^{M}\leq 3^{M-1}\bigl(J_{1}(t)+J_{2}(t)+J_{3}(t)\bigr)\,,

with

\displaystyle J_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\widehat{\mathbb{E}}^{\lambda}_{0}\big|X^{\lambda}(t)-X^{\lambda}(\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)})\big|^{M}\,,

\displaystyle J_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\widehat{\mathbb{E}}^{\lambda}_{0}\big|X^{\lambda}(\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)})-\ell(\lambda)\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)}\big|^{M}\,,

\displaystyle J_{3}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq|\ell(\lambda)|^{M}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}^{\lambda}_{0}\big|t-\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)}\big|^{M}\,.

Step 1.
We estimate
J_{1}
and
J_{3}
by replacing the random index
\mathbf{n}(t)
with the deterministic bound
N_{t}+1
. Let
R_{k}
and
W_{k}
be as in Lemma
5.7
.

The block moment bounds
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[R_{k}^{M}]\leq C\lambda^{-M}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[(\Delta\tau_{k})^{M}]\leq C\lambda^{-2M}
from (
5.12
) are used below uniformly in
k\geq 0
. Let
N_{t}\coloneqq\lfloor\lambda^{2}t\rfloor
. The initial block is left out of this counting argument and will be included in the maxima below. The integer-valuedness of
\lambda^{2}\Delta\tau_{k}
in (
5.3
) gives
\Delta\tau_{k}\geq\lambda^{-2}
for
k\geq 1
, so

(5.17)

(\mathbf{n}(t)-1)_{+}\lambda^{-2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{(\mathbf{n}(t)-1)_{+}}\Delta\tau_{k}\leq\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)}\leq t,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\mathbf{n}(t)\leq N_{t}+1\,.

Since
\mathbf{n}(t)\leq N_{t}+1
by (
5.17
), while
t-\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)}\leq\Delta\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)}
and
|X^{\lambda}(t)-X^{\lambda}(\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)})|\leq R_{\mathbf{n}(t)}
,

|X^{\lambda}(t)-X^{\lambda}(\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)})|\leq\max_{0\leq k\leq N_{t}+1}R_{k},\qquad|t-\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)}|\leq\max_{0\leq k\leq N_{t}+1}\Delta\tau_{k}\,.

The bound
\max_{0\leq k\leq N_{t}+1}a_{k}^{M}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{N_{t}+1}a_{k}^{M}
for nonnegative
a_{k}
and (
5.12
) with
r=M
give

J_{1}(t)=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}^{\lambda}_{0}\big|X^{\lambda}(t)-X^{\lambda}(\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)})\big|^{M}\leq\widehat{\mathbb{E}}^{\lambda}_{0}\Big[\max_{0\leq k\leq N_{t}+1}R_{k}^{M}\Big]\leq\sum_{k=0}^{N_{t}+1}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}^{\lambda}_{0}[R_{k}^{M}]\leq C(N_{t}+2)\lambda^{-M}\,.

Similarly, using
|\ell(\lambda)|\leq C\lambda
(Lemma
5.6
) and (
5.12
) with
r=M
,

J_{3}(t)\leq C\lambda^{M}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{t}+1}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}^{\lambda}_{0}[(\Delta\tau_{k})^{M}]\leq C(N_{t}+2)\lambda^{M}\cdot\lambda^{-2M}=C(N_{t}+2)\lambda^{-M}\,.

Since
t\geq\lambda^{-2}
, we have
(N_{t}+2)\leq C\lambda^{2}t
, and therefore

(5.18)

J_{1}(t)\leq C\lambda^{2-M}t\leq Ct^{M/2}\,,

(5.19)

J_{3}(t)\leq C\lambda^{2-M}t\leq Ct^{M/2}\,,

using
t\geq\lambda^{-2}
, which implies
\lambda^{-1}\leq\sqrt{t}
and hence
\lambda^{2-M}t=\lambda^{-(M-2)}t\leq t^{M/2}
.

Step 2.
We estimate
J_{2}
. We have

X^{\lambda}(\tau_{n})-\ell(\lambda)\tau_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}W_{k},\qquad J_{2}(t)=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}^{\lambda}_{0}\Big|\sum_{k=0}^{\mathbf{n}(t)-1}W_{k}\Big|^{M}\,.

By Lemma
5.1
,
\{W_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are i.i.d. and centered under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
, while
W_{0}
is independent of
\{W_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
. The deterministic bound (
5.17
) of Step 1 gives
\mathbf{n}(t)-1\leq N_{t}=\lfloor\lambda^{2}t\rfloor
. Set

S_{m}\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{m}W_{k},\qquad m\geq 1,\qquad S_{0}\coloneqq 0\,.

Then

\Big|\sum_{k=0}^{\mathbf{n}(t)-1}W_{k}\Big|^{M}\leq 2^{M-1}\Big(|W_{0}|^{M}+\max_{1\leq m\leq N_{t}}|S_{m}|^{M}\Big)\,,

and hence, by (
5.14
) with
r=M
,

(5.20)

J_{2}(t)\leq C\lambda^{-M}+C\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\Big[\max_{1\leq m\leq N_{t}}|S_{m}|^{M}\Big]\,.

Now the sequence
\{S_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}
is an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued martingale under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
, so
\{|S_{m}|\}_{m\geq 0}
is a nonnegative submartingale. By Doob’s
L^{M}
maximal inequality,

(5.21)

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\Big[\max_{0\leq m\leq N_{t}}|S_{m}|^{M}\Big]\leq\Big(\frac{M}{M-1}\Big)^{M}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|S_{N_{t}}|^{M}\,.

Write
S_{N_{t}}=(S_{N_{t}}^{(1)},\dots,S_{N_{t}}^{(d)})
.
Since

|x|^{M}\leq d^{M/2-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|^{M},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,

it is enough to estimate each coordinate. For each
i
, the random variables
\{W_{k}^{(i)}\}_{k\geq 1}
are i.i.d. mean-zero real-valued random variables under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
. By Rosenthal’s inequality and (
5.14
) with
r=2
and
r=M
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|S_{N_{t}}^{(i)}|^{M}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(N_{t}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|W_{1}^{(i)}|^{M}+\big(N_{t}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|W_{1}^{(i)}|^{2}\big)^{M/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(N_{t}\lambda^{-M}+N_{t}^{M/2}\lambda^{-M}\right)\leq CN_{t}^{M/2}\lambda^{-M}\,,

where in the last step we used
M\geq 2
. Summing over
1\leq i\leq d
yields

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|S_{N_{t}}|^{M}\leq CN_{t}^{M/2}\lambda^{-M}\,.

Together with (
5.21
),

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\Big[\max_{0\leq m\leq N_{t}}|S_{m}|^{M}\Big]\leq CN_{t}^{M/2}\lambda^{-M}\,.

Since
N_{t}\leq\lambda^{2}t
,

N_{t}^{M/2}\lambda^{-M}\leq t^{M/2}\,.

Substituting this into (
5.20
), we obtain

(5.22)

J_{2}(t)\leq C\big(t^{M/2}+\lambda^{-M}\big)\,.

Combining (
5.16
), (
5.18
), (
5.19
), and (
5.22
),

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\widehat{E}^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}\big|X^{\lambda}(t)-\ell(\lambda)t\big|^{M}\leq C\big(t^{M/2}+\lambda^{-M}\big)\leq Ct^{M/2}\,,

where the last inequality uses
t\geq\lambda^{-2}
.
∎

Proposition
5.9
is the clock analogue of Proposition
5.8
. It differs in two ways: the bound carries an extra factor
\lambda^{-M}
, and, because the clock blocks are only
2
-dependent, the partial sums are handled by splitting into three i.i.d. residue classes rather than by a single application of Rosenthal’s inequality.

Proposition 5.9

(Annealed renewal for the additive clock)
.

For every
\lambda\in(0,1]
, the limit
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}A^{\lambda}(t)
exists
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
-almost surely and in
L^{1}(\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda})
and is the deterministic number

(5.23)

\eta(\lambda)=\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\left[\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}e^{-2V(X^{\lambda}(s))}ds\right]}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\Delta\tau_{1}]}\,.

Moreover,

(5.24)

\Lambda^{-1}\leq\eta(\lambda)\leq\Lambda\,.

For every integer
M\geq 2
, there exists a finite constant
C=C(M,d,\Lambda,r_{0})>0
such that for all
\lambda\in(0,1]
and all
t\geq\lambda^{-2}
,

(5.25)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\widehat{E}^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}\big|A^{\lambda}(t)-\eta(\lambda)t\big|^{M}\leq C\lambda^{-M}t^{M/2}\,.

Proof.

Let

\bar{\eta}(\lambda)\coloneqq\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\Delta A_{1}^{\lambda}]}{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\Delta\tau_{1}]}\,.

Since
\Lambda^{-1}\Delta\tau_{1}\leq\Delta A_{1}^{\lambda}\leq\Lambda\Delta\tau_{1}
, we have
\Lambda^{-1}\leq\bar{\eta}(\lambda)\leq\Lambda
. Define

Z_{k}\coloneqq\Delta A_{k}^{\lambda}-\bar{\eta}(\lambda)\Delta\tau_{k}\,.

By Lemma
5.3
, the pairs
(\Delta A_{k}^{\lambda},\Delta\tau_{k})
for
k\geq 1
are stationary and
2
-dependent under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
. By stationarity and the definition of
\bar{\eta}(\lambda)
,

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}Z_{k}=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\Delta A_{1}^{\lambda}]-\bar{\eta}(\lambda)\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\Delta\tau_{1}]=0\qquad\text{for every }k\geq 1\,,

so
\{Z_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is stationary,
2
-dependent, and centered.
Since
e^{-2V}\leq\Lambda
and
\bar{\eta}(\lambda)\leq\Lambda
,

|\Delta A_{k}^{\lambda}|+|Z_{k}|\leq C\Delta\tau_{k}\,.

Using Lemma
5.7
, for every integer
r\geq 1
,

(5.26)

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|\Delta A_{k}^{\lambda}|^{r}+\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|Z_{k}|^{r}\leq C\lambda^{-2r}\qquad\text{for }k\geq 0\,.

Let
\mathbf{n}(t)\coloneqq\max\{n\geq 0:\tau_{n}\leq t\}
and
N_{t}\coloneqq\lfloor\lambda^{2}t\rfloor
. As in (
5.17
),
\mathbf{n}(t)-1\leq N_{t}
. Decompose pointwise

A^{\lambda}(t)-\bar{\eta}(\lambda)t=B_{t}+\mathrm{Rem}_{t},\qquad B_{t}\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{\mathbf{n}(t)-1}Z_{k},\quad\mathrm{Rem}_{t}\coloneqq A^{\lambda}(t)-A^{\lambda}(\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)})-\bar{\eta}(\lambda)(t-\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)})\,,

so
|A^{\lambda}(t)-\bar{\eta}(\lambda)t|^{M}\leq 2^{M-1}(|B_{t}|^{M}+|\mathrm{Rem}_{t}|^{M})
pointwise. The remainder satisfies
|\mathrm{Rem}_{t}|\leq C\Delta\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)}\leq C\max_{0\leq k\leq N_{t}+1}\Delta\tau_{k}
, so

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|\mathrm{Rem}_{t}|^{M}\leq C\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\max_{0\leq k\leq N_{t}+1}(\Delta\tau_{k})^{M}\leq C(N_{t}+2)\lambda^{-2M}\leq C\lambda^{-M}t^{M/2}

using
t\geq\lambda^{-2}
.

For the block sum, set
S_{n}\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{n}Z_{k}
for
n\geq 1
and
S_{0}\coloneqq 0
, so
B_{t}=Z_{0}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathbf{n}(t)\geq 1\}}+S_{(\mathbf{n}(t)-1)\vee 0}
and
|B_{t}|^{M}\leq 2^{M-1}(|Z_{0}|^{M}+\max_{0\leq n\leq N_{t}}|S_{n}|^{M})
pointwise. Hence

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|B_{t}|^{M}\leq C\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|Z_{0}|^{M}+C\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\max_{0\leq n\leq N_{t}}|S_{n}|^{M}\,.

The initial block contributes

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|Z_{0}|^{M}\leq C\lambda^{-2M}\leq C\lambda^{-M}t^{M/2}\,,

because
t\geq\lambda^{-2}
. For the centered
2
-dependent block sum, split into three residue classes modulo
3
:

S_{n}=\sum_{r\in\{0,1,2\}}S_{n}^{(r)},\qquad S_{n}^{(r)}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n\\
k\equiv r\pmod{3}\end{subarray}}Z_{k}\,.

Within each residue class, indices are at distance at least
3
. Lemma
5.3
and stationarity therefore make each residue-class subsequence i.i.d. and centered. Pointwise,

\max_{1\leq n\leq N_{t}}|S_{n}|^{M}\leq C\sum_{r\in\{0,1,2\}}\max_{0\leq m\leq N_{t}+1}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq m\\
k\equiv r\pmod{3}\end{subarray}}Z_{k}\right|^{M}\,.

Doob’s
L^{M}
maximal inequality and Rosenthal’s inequality applied to each reindexed i.i.d. centered subsequence, using (
5.26
), give

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\max_{0\leq m\leq N_{t}+1}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq m\\
k\equiv r\pmod{3}\end{subarray}}Z_{k}\right|^{M}\leq C\left(N_{t}\lambda^{-2M}+N_{t}^{M/2}\lambda^{-2M}\right)\leq CN_{t}^{M/2}\lambda^{-2M}\,.

Therefore

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\max_{1\leq n\leq N_{t}}|S_{n}|^{M}\leq CN_{t}^{M/2}\lambda^{-2M}\,.

Since
N_{t}\leq\lambda^{2}t
,
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|B_{t}|^{M}\leq C\lambda^{-M}t^{M/2}
. Combining with the remainder bound gives

(5.27)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\widehat{E}^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}\big|A^{\lambda}(t)-\bar{\eta}(\lambda)t\big|^{M}\leq C\lambda^{-M}t^{M/2}\,.

With
M=2
, this gives

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|t^{-1}A^{\lambda}(t)-\bar{\eta}(\lambda)|\leq C\lambda^{-1}t^{-1/2}\to 0\qquad\text{as }t\to\infty\,.

Thus the
L^{1}
limit defining
\eta(\lambda)
exists and equals

\bar{\eta}(\lambda)
, which proves (
5.23
) and
(
5.24
). Replacing
\bar{\eta}(\lambda)
by

\eta(\lambda)
in (
5.27
) yields
(
5.25
). It remains to prove almost-sure
convergence.

For almost-sure convergence, again split
S_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}Z_{k}
by
residues modulo
3
. On each residue class, Lemma
5.3

and stationarity give an i.i.d. centered sequence with finite variance by
(
5.26
). Applying the strong law to each
residue class gives
n^{-1}S_{n}\to 0

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
-almost surely.

For the remainder, fix an integer
m\geq 1
. Applying
(
5.10
) with
u=\lambda^{2}k/m
gives

\sum_{k\geq 1}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}(\Delta\tau_{k}\geq k/m)\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}Ce^{-c\lambda^{2}k/m}<\infty\,.

Borel–Cantelli gives
\Delta\tau_{k}<k/m
eventually for each fixed
m
. Intersecting over integers
m\geq 1
yields
\Delta\tau_{k}/k\to 0
almost surely. Since
\mathbf{n}(t)\to\infty
and
\mathbf{n}(t)\leq\lambda^{2}t+2
by (
5.17
), for all sufficiently large
t
this gives

\frac{|\mathrm{Rem}_{t}|}{t}\leq C\frac{\Delta\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)}}{t}=C\frac{\Delta\tau_{\mathbf{n}(t)}}{\mathbf{n}(t)}\frac{\mathbf{n}(t)}{t}\to 0

almost surely. The strong law also gives

\frac{S_{(\mathbf{n}(t)-1)\vee 0}}{t}=\frac{S_{(\mathbf{n}(t)-1)\vee 0}}{(\mathbf{n}(t)-1)\vee 1}\frac{(\mathbf{n}(t)-1)\vee 1}{t}\longrightarrow 0,

using
\mathbf{n}(t)\leq\lambda^{2}t+2
. Together with
Z_{0}/t\to 0
, the decomposition
A^{\lambda}(t)-\bar{\eta}(\lambda)t=Z_{0}\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathbf{n}(t)\geq 1\}}+S_{(\mathbf{n}(t)-1)\vee 0}+\mathrm{Rem}_{t}
gives
t^{-1}A^{\lambda}(t)\to\bar{\eta}(\lambda)

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
-almost surely.
∎

5.4.
Uniform quenched renewal control

The annealed bounds of Subsection
5.3
hold with constants uniform in
\lambda
. We upgrade them to a quenched bound uniform on a random window
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}(\omega)]
, by a Borel–Cantelli argument over a dyadic grid with Girsanov transfer between nearby tilts.

Lemma 5.10

(Girsanov second-moment bound)
.

There exists
C=C(d,\Lambda)<\infty
such that, for every environment

\omega
, every
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in(0,1]
, every
t>0
, and every
nonnegative, possibly
\omega
-dependent, path functional
F
measurable
with respect to
\sigma(X(s):0\leq s\leq t)
,

(5.28)

E_{0}^{\lambda^{\prime},\omega}[F]\leq\exp\bigl(C(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)^{2}t/2\bigr)\bigl(E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[F^{2}]\bigr)^{1/2}\,.

If in addition
|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|^{2}t\leq 1
, then for every
square-integrable, possibly
\omega
-dependent, path functional
G

measurable with respect to
\sigma(X(s):0\leq s\leq t)
,

(5.29)

\bigl|E_{0}^{\lambda^{\prime},\omega}[G]-E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[G]\bigr|\leq C|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|\sqrt{t}\bigl(E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[G^{2}]\bigr)^{1/2}\,.

The same estimates hold with the expectations
\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}
whenever
F
and
G
depend only on the diffusion path. For every integer
q\geq 1
, the second estimate also holds for
\mathbb{R}^{q}
-valued
G
, with a constant depending additionally on
q
and with
|G|^{2}
in place of
G^{2}
.

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{b}_{\mu}(x)\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{a}(x)+\mu\mathbf{a}(x)e_{1}
. Under
P_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}
, the SDE (
4.1
) has drift
\mathbf{b}_{\lambda}
and diffusion coefficient
\mathbf{a}^{1/2}
. Define

M_{t}\coloneqq(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{a}^{1/2}(X^{\lambda}(s))e_{1}\cdot dW_{s},\qquad R_{t}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\coloneqq\exp\bigl(M_{t}-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle M\rangle_{t}\bigr)\,.

The quadratic variation
\langle M\rangle_{t}=(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e_{1}\cdot\mathbf{a}(X^{\lambda}(s))e_{1}ds\leq\Lambda(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})^{2}t
is bounded deterministically. This gives the exponential integrability needed for the exponential martingale, so
R_{t}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
is a true
P_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}
-martingale with
E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[R_{t}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}]=1
. Girsanov’s theorem changes the drift from
\mathbf{b}_{\lambda}
to
\mathbf{b}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
, and uniqueness in law identifies
R_{t}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
as the Radon–Nikodym derivative of
P_{0}^{\lambda^{\prime},\omega}|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}
with respect to
P_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}
. Moreover,

(R^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}_{t})^{2}=\exp(2M_{t}-2\langle M\rangle_{t})\exp(\langle M\rangle_{t})\,.

The first factor is a nonnegative
P_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}
-local martingale and therefore a supermartingale of expectation at most
1
. The second factor is bounded by
\exp(\Lambda(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})^{2}t)
, so

E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}\bigl[(R_{t}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}})^{2}\bigr]\leq\exp(C(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})^{2}t)\,.

Cauchy–Schwarz on
E_{0}^{\lambda^{\prime},\omega}[F]=E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[R_{t}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}F]
gives (
5.28
). If
|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|^{2}t\leq 1
, then
E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[(R_{t}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}-1)^{2}]=E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[(R_{t}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}})^{2}]-1\leq e^{C|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|^{2}t}-1\leq C|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|^{2}t
, and Cauchy–Schwarz on
|E_{0}^{\lambda^{\prime},\omega}[G]-E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[G]|=|E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[(R_{t}^{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}-1)G]|
gives (
5.29
).
∎

The annealed renewal estimates now give moment bounds for the quenched
L^{2}
errors.

Lemma 5.11

(Quenched second-moment renewal bound)
.

Fix an integer
M\geq 2
. There is a constant
C=C(M,d,\Lambda,r_{0})
such that, for every
\lambda\in(0,1]
and every
t\geq\lambda^{-2}
,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\Bigl[\bigl(E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|X^{\lambda}(t)-\ell(\lambda)t|^{2}\bigr)^{M/2}\Bigr]\leq Ct^{M/2}\,,

and

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\Bigl[\bigl(\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|A^{\lambda}(t)-\eta(\lambda)t|^{2}\bigr)^{M/2}\Bigr]\leq C\lambda^{-M}t^{M/2}\,.

Proof.

Jensen’s inequality gives, for every environment,

\bigl(E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|Z|^{2}\bigr)^{M/2}\leq E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|Z|^{M}

for any path functional
Z
. Apply this with
Z=X^{\lambda}(t)-\ell(\lambda)t
, average over
\mathbb{Q}
, and use Proposition
5.8
. The clock estimate is identical, with
\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}
and Proposition
5.9
.
∎

To pass from dyadic grids of tilts to all tilts, we need a deterministic modulus of continuity for the limiting velocity and clock rate.

Lemma 5.12

(Hölder continuity of
\ell
and
\eta
in
\lambda
)
.

Let
I_{k}=(2^{-k-1},2^{-k}]
and
L_{k}=2^{-k}
. There exists
C=C(d,\Lambda,r_{0})<\infty
such that for every
k\geq 1
and every
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in I_{k}
,

(5.30)

|\ell(\lambda)-\ell(\lambda^{\prime})|\leq C\bigl(L_{k}|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|\bigr)^{1/2}\,,

and

(5.31)

|\eta(\lambda)-\eta(\lambda^{\prime})|\leq C\bigl(|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|/L_{k}\bigr)^{1/2}\,.

Proof.

Set
\Delta\coloneqq|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|
. If
\Delta=0
, then both left sides are zero; assume
\Delta>0
. If
\Delta\geq L_{k}/4
, then by Lemma
5.6

|\ell(\lambda)-\ell(\lambda^{\prime})|\leq C(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})\leq CL_{k}\leq C(L_{k}\Delta)^{1/2}
, and
|\eta(\lambda)-\eta(\lambda^{\prime})|\leq 2\Lambda\leq C(\Delta/L_{k})^{1/2}
. Assume
\Delta<L_{k}/4
and set
T\coloneqq(L_{k}\Delta)^{-1}
. Since
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\geq L_{k}/2
,
T\geq 4L_{k}^{-2}\geq\lambda^{-2}\vee(\lambda^{\prime})^{-2}
and
\Delta^{2}T=\Delta/L_{k}<1/4
.

Step 1.
We prove (
5.30
). Jensen’s inequality applied to
X^{\lambda}(T)-\ell(\lambda)T
, combined with the case
M=2
of Lemma
5.11
, gives

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\bigl[(E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|X^{\lambda}(T)-\ell(\lambda)T|)^{2}\bigr]\leq CT\,.

The
L^{2}
-to-
L^{1}
bound then gives

\displaystyle\Bigl|T^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{0}^{\lambda}[X^{\lambda}(T)]-\ell(\lambda)\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq T^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{0}^{\lambda}|X^{\lambda}(T)-\ell(\lambda)T|

\displaystyle\leq T^{-1}\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\bigl[(E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|X^{\lambda}(T)-\ell(\lambda)T|)^{2}\bigr]\bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{-1/2}\,,

and the same with
\lambda^{\prime}
. Hence

(5.32)

|\ell(\lambda)-\ell(\lambda^{\prime})|\leq 2CT^{-1/2}+T^{-1}\bigl|\mathbb{E}_{0}^{\lambda}[X^{\lambda}(T)]-\mathbb{E}_{0}^{\lambda^{\prime}}[X^{\lambda^{\prime}}(T)]\bigr|\,.

Applying (
5.29
) with the canonical functional
G(\gamma)=\gamma(T)
gives, for every
\omega
,

|E_{0}^{\lambda^{\prime},\omega}[X^{\lambda^{\prime}}(T)]-E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[X^{\lambda}(T)]|\leq C\Delta\sqrt{T}(E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|X^{\lambda}(T)|^{2})^{1/2}\,.

Lemma
5.4
with
p=2
gives
E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|X^{\lambda}(T)|^{2}\leq C(\lambda T)^{2}\leq C(L_{k}T)^{2}
. Averaging over
\mathbb{Q}
and inserting into (
5.32
), we obtain

|\ell(\lambda)-\ell(\lambda^{\prime})|\leq 2CT^{-1/2}+CL_{k}\Delta T^{1/2}=C(L_{k}\Delta)^{1/2}\,,

using
T^{-1/2}=(L_{k}\Delta)^{1/2}=L_{k}\Delta T^{1/2}
.

Step 2.
We prove (
5.31
). Jensen’s inequality applied to
A^{\lambda}(T)-\eta(\lambda)T
, combined with the case
M=2
of Lemma
5.11
, gives

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\bigl[(\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|A^{\lambda}(T)-\eta(\lambda)T|)^{2}\bigr]\leq C\lambda^{-2}T\,.

Hence by Cauchy–Schwarz,

\displaystyle\Bigl|T^{-1}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[A^{\lambda}(T)]-\eta(\lambda)\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq T^{-1}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}|A^{\lambda}(T)-\eta(\lambda)T|

\displaystyle\leq T^{-1}\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\bigl[(\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|A^{\lambda}(T)-\eta(\lambda)T|)^{2}\bigr]\bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{-1}T^{-1/2}\leq CL_{k}^{-1}T^{-1/2}=C(\Delta/L_{k})^{1/2}\,,

and the same with
\lambda^{\prime}
. Applying (
5.29
) with the canonical functional
G(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2V(\gamma_{s})}ds
and the deterministic bound
0\leq G\leq\Lambda T
gives

\bigl|\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda^{\prime},\omega}[A^{\lambda^{\prime}}(T)]-\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}[A^{\lambda}(T)]\bigr|\leq C\Delta\sqrt{T}\cdot T=C\Delta T^{3/2}\,.

Dividing by
T
gives
C\Delta T^{1/2}=C(\Delta/L_{k})^{1/2}
. Combining yields (
5.31
).
∎

The annealed bounds and the Hölder modulus combine via Markov’s inequality and a Borel–Cantelli argument over a dyadic grid in
\lambda
to produce a single quenched bound, uniform in
\lambda
on a random window.

Corollary 5.13

(Quenched renewal estimate uniform in
\lambda
)
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1/4)
and
\varepsilon>0
. There exist a
\mathbb{Q}
-full-probability event
\Omega_{\theta,\varepsilon}
and a deterministic constant
C=C(\theta,\varepsilon,d,\Lambda,r_{0})<\infty
such that, for every
\omega\in\Omega_{\theta,\varepsilon}
, there exists a finite random scale
\lambda_{0}(\omega)>0
with the property that for every
0<\lambda\leq\lambda_{0}(\omega)
and every
t\geq\lambda^{-2-\varepsilon}
,

(5.33)

E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|X^{\lambda}(t)-\ell(\lambda)t|\leq C\sqrt{t}(\lambda^{2}t)^{\theta}\,,

and

(5.34)

\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|A^{\lambda}(t)-\eta(\lambda)t|\leq C\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{t}(\lambda^{2}t)^{\theta}\,.

Moreover,
\lambda_{0}
has polynomial tails of every order: for each
p>0
there is
C_{p}=C_{p}(p,\theta,\varepsilon,d,\Lambda,r_{0})<\infty
such that

(5.35)

\mathbb{Q}\bigl(\lambda_{0}\leq 2^{-k}\bigr)\leq C_{p}2^{-pk}\qquad\text{for every integer }k\geq 0\,.

Proof.

Define the quenched
L^{2}
functionals

\mathcal{W}_{n}^{X}(\lambda,\omega)\coloneqq\bigl(E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|X^{\lambda}(n)-\ell(\lambda)n|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2},\qquad\mathcal{W}_{n}^{A}(\lambda,\omega)\coloneqq\bigl(\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|A^{\lambda}(n)-\eta(\lambda)n|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}\,.

For
k\geq 1
, set
L_{k}\coloneqq 2^{-k}
,
I_{k}\coloneqq(2^{-k-1},2^{-k}]
,
N_{k}\coloneqq\lceil L_{k}^{-2-\varepsilon}\rceil
, and choose a
r_{k,n}
-net
\mathcal{N}_{k,n}\subseteq I_{k}
with mesh

(5.36)

r_{k,n}\coloneqq c_{\rm net}L_{k}^{4\theta-1}n^{2\theta-1}

for
n\geq N_{k}
, where
c_{\rm net}\in(0,1]
is deterministic and depends on
(\theta,\varepsilon,d,\Lambda,r_{0})
. Since
2\theta-1<0
and
n\geq N_{k}
, choosing
c_{\rm net}
small enough gives

r_{k,n}\leq Cc_{\rm net}L_{k}^{1+\varepsilon(1-2\theta)}\leq L_{k}\,.

Thus the net may be chosen so that
|\mathcal{N}_{k,n}|\leq CL_{k}/r_{k,n}\leq CL_{k}^{2-4\theta}n^{1-2\theta}
.

Step 1.
Choose an integer

M_{0}\geq 2
with
M_{0}\theta>2/\varepsilon+2
.

Lemma
5.11
requires the renewal time

n\geq(\lambda^{\prime})^{-2}
. Since
\lambda^{\prime}\geq L_{k}/2
on
I_{k}
, we have

(\lambda^{\prime})^{-2}\leq 4L_{k}^{-2}\leq N_{k}
as soon as

L_{k}^{-\varepsilon}\geq 4
, i.e.,
k\geq k_{*}(\varepsilon)\coloneqq\lceil 2/\varepsilon\rceil
. For
k\geq k_{*}
, every
n\geq N_{k}
satisfies

n\geq(\lambda^{\prime})^{-2}
for every
\lambda^{\prime}\in I_{k}
.

By Markov’s inequality and Lemma
5.11
, for
every
k\geq k_{*}
, every
\lambda^{\prime}\in I_{k}
, and every
n\geq N_{k}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\bigl(\mathcal{W}_{n}^{X}(\lambda^{\prime},\cdot)>\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{n}((\lambda^{\prime})^{2}n)^{\theta}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq C((\lambda^{\prime})^{2}n)^{-M_{0}\theta}\,,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\bigl(\mathcal{W}_{n}^{A}(\lambda^{\prime},\cdot)>\tfrac{1}{2}(\lambda^{\prime})^{-1}\sqrt{n}((\lambda^{\prime})^{2}n)^{\theta}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq C((\lambda^{\prime})^{2}n)^{-M_{0}\theta}\,.

Let
B_{k,n}
be the event that some
\xi\in\mathcal{N}_{k,n}
violates
either bound. Since
L_{k}/2<\xi\leq L_{k}
for
\xi\in I_{k}
, the union bound gives, for

k\geq k_{*}
,

\mathbb{Q}(B_{k,n})\leq CL_{k}^{2-4\theta-2M_{0}\theta}n^{1-2\theta-M_{0}\theta}\,.

The choice
M_{0}\theta>2/\varepsilon+2
makes the exponent of
n

strictly less than
-1-2/\varepsilon
, hence summable. Summing in

n\geq N_{k}
and using
N_{k}=\lceil L_{k}^{-2-\varepsilon}\rceil
, the integral
test gives

\sum_{n\geq N_{k}}\mathbb{Q}(B_{k,n})\leq CL_{k}^{-2+\varepsilon(M_{0}\theta+2\theta-2)}
. The exponent of
L_{k}
is
positive, so with
E_{k}\coloneqq\bigcup_{n\geq N_{k}}B_{k,n}
,

\sum_{k\geq k_{*}}\mathbb{Q}(E_{k})<\infty
. Borel–Cantelli yields a

\mathbb{Q}
-full-probability event
\Omega_{\theta,\varepsilon}
on which only
finitely many
E_{k}
occur.

Step 2.
Fix
\omega\in\Omega_{\theta,\varepsilon}
. For the
prefactor
\exp(C\Delta^{2}n/2)
in (
5.28
) to remain
bounded, the mesh must satisfy
\Delta^{2}n\leq 1
. With
\Delta\leq r_{k,n}
from
(
5.36
) and
n\geq N_{k}=\lceil L_{k}^{-2-\varepsilon}\rceil
, and using

4\theta-1<0
together with
n\geq N_{k}
,

\Delta^{2}n\leq r_{k,n}^{2}n=c_{\rm net}^{2}L_{k}^{8\theta-2}n^{4\theta-1}\leq c_{\rm net}^{2}L_{k}^{8\theta-2}N_{k}^{4\theta-1}\leq c_{\rm net}^{2}L_{k}^{\varepsilon(1-4\theta)}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
, since
\theta<1/4
. Decrease
c_{\rm net}
, if
necessary, so that the constants multiplying
c_{\rm net}^{1/2}
in the
displacement and clock estimates are at most one, and then choose an
integer
k_{1}=k_{1}(\theta,\varepsilon,d,\Lambda,r_{0})<\infty
with

c_{\rm net}^{2}L_{k}^{\varepsilon(1-4\theta)}\leq 1
for every
k\geq k_{1}
.

Define

(5.37)

k_{0}(\omega)\coloneqq\max\Bigl\{k_{1},1+\sup\{j\geq k_{*}:E_{j}(\omega)\text{ occurs}\}\Bigr\}\,,

with the convention
\sup\emptyset\coloneqq k_{*}-1
, so that

k_{0}(\omega)<\infty
on
\Omega_{\theta,\varepsilon}
. Then for every

k\geq k_{0}(\omega)
,
E_{k}(\omega)
does not occur, hence
B_{k,n}^{c}
for
every
n\geq N_{k}
. For every
\lambda\in I_{k}
with
k\geq k_{0}(\omega)
and
every integer
n\geq N_{k}
, choose
\xi\in\mathcal{N}_{k,n}
with

\Delta\coloneqq|\lambda-\xi|\leq r_{k,n}
, so that
\Delta^{2}n\leq 1
.
Lemma
5.10
(
5.28
) then gives, for
every
\omega
,

E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|X^{\lambda}(n)-\ell(\xi)n|\leq e^{C\Delta^{2}n/2}\bigl(E_{0}^{\xi,\omega}|X^{\xi}(n)-\ell(\xi)n|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}\leq C\mathcal{W}_{n}^{X}(\xi,\omega)\,.

The triangle inequality and Lemma
5.12
(
5.30
) yield

E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|X^{\lambda}(n)-\ell(\lambda)n|\leq C\mathcal{W}_{n}^{X}(\xi,\omega)+n|\ell(\lambda)-\ell(\xi)|\leq C\sqrt{n}(L_{k}^{2}n)^{\theta}+Cn(L_{k}\Delta)^{1/2}\,.

The second term satisfies
Cn(L_{k}\Delta)^{1/2}\leq Cn(L_{k}r_{k,n})^{1/2}=Cc_{\rm net}^{1/2}\sqrt{n}(L_{k}^{2}n)^{\theta}
(using
\Delta\leq r_{k,n}
), which is absorbed into the first term after fixing
c_{\rm net}
small. Since
L_{k}/2<\lambda\leq L_{k}
,
(L_{k}^{2}n)^{\theta}\leq C(\lambda^{2}n)^{\theta}
, giving

(5.38)

E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|X^{\lambda}(n)-\ell(\lambda)n|\leq C\sqrt{n}(\lambda^{2}n)^{\theta}\qquad\text{for every integer }n\geq N_{k}\,.

For the clock, Lemma
5.10
(
5.28
) applied to the path functional
\gamma\mapsto\left|\int_{0}^{n}e^{-2V(\gamma_{s})}ds-\eta(\xi)n\right|
gives

\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|A^{\lambda}(n)-\eta(\xi)n|\leq e^{C\Delta^{2}n/2}\bigl(\widehat{E}_{0}^{\xi,\omega}|A^{\xi}(n)-\eta(\xi)n|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}\leq C\mathcal{W}_{n}^{A}(\xi,\omega)\,.

From (
5.31
) and
\Delta\leq r_{k,n}
,

n|\eta(\lambda)-\eta(\xi)|\leq Cn(\Delta/L_{k})^{1/2}\leq Cc_{\rm net}^{1/2}L_{k}^{-1}\sqrt{n}(L_{k}^{2}n)^{\theta}\,,

again absorbed by choosing
c_{\rm net}
small. Combining with
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{A}(\xi,\omega)\leq CL_{k}^{-1}\sqrt{n}(L_{k}^{2}n)^{\theta}
,

(5.39)

\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|A^{\lambda}(n)-\eta(\lambda)n|\leq C\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{n}(\lambda^{2}n)^{\theta}\qquad\text{for every integer }n\geq N_{k}\,.

Step 3.
For arbitrary
t\geq\lambda^{-2-\varepsilon}
, set
n\coloneqq\lceil t\rceil
. Then
n-t\leq 1
and
n\geq N_{k}
. By boundedness of the drift and the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality applied on
[t,n]
,
E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|X^{\lambda}(t)-X^{\lambda}(n)|\leq C
. Together with
|\ell(\lambda)|\leq C\lambda\leq C
from Lemma
5.6
and (
5.38
), this gives
E_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}|X^{\lambda}(t)-\ell(\lambda)t|\leq C\sqrt{n}(\lambda^{2}n)^{\theta}+C
. Since
\sqrt{t}(\lambda^{2}t)^{\theta}\geq 1
for
t\geq\lambda^{-2}
, the right-hand side is at most
C\sqrt{t}(\lambda^{2}t)^{\theta}
, proving (
5.33
).

For the clock,
0\leq e^{-2V}\leq\Lambda
and (
5.24
) give
|A^{\lambda}(t)-A^{\lambda}(n)|+|\eta(\lambda)||t-n|\leq C
; combining this with (
5.39
) proves (
5.34
).

Setting
\lambda_{0}(\omega)\coloneqq 2^{-k_{0}(\omega)}
on
\Omega_{\theta,\varepsilon}
, and
\lambda_{0}(\omega)\coloneqq 1
on
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\theta,\varepsilon}
, defines
\lambda_{0}
as a random variable on all of
\Omega
.

Step 4.
We prove the tail bound for
\lambda_{0}
. The thresholds in
B_{k,n}
and
E_{k}
do not depend on the moment exponent, so we may repeat Step 1 with a larger exponent. Fix
p>0
and choose an integer
M_{p}\geq 2
with
-2+\varepsilon(M_{p}\theta+2\theta-2)\geq p
. Using
M_{p}
in Markov’s inequality and summing over
n\geq N_{k}
gives, for every
k\geq k_{*}
,

\mathbb{Q}(E_{k})\leq\sum_{n\geq N_{k}}\mathbb{Q}(B_{k,n})\leq C_{p}L_{k}^{-2+\varepsilon(M_{p}\theta+2\theta-2)}\leq C_{p}2^{-pk}\,,

with
C_{p}
depending on
(p,\theta,\varepsilon,d,\Lambda,r_{0})
. From the canonical definition (
5.37
),
\{k_{0}(\omega)\geq k\}\subseteq\{k\leq k_{1}\vee k_{*}\}\cup\bigcup_{j\geq\max\{k-1,k_{*}\}}E_{j}
. For
k\leq k_{1}\vee k_{*}
the inclusion is trivially absorbed by enlarging
C_{p}
; for
k>k_{1}\vee k_{*}
the first term is empty, so

\mathbb{Q}\bigl(\lambda_{0}\leq 2^{-k}\bigr)=\mathbb{Q}\bigl(k_{0}(\omega)\geq k\bigr)\leq\sum_{j\geq k-1}\mathbb{Q}(E_{j})\leq C_{p}2^{-pk}\,,

which is (
5.35
) and completes the proof.
∎

6.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

This section proves Theorem
1.2
. By the time change of
Subsection
2.3
, the mobility error

\ell_{X}(\lambda)/\lambda-\Sigma_{X}e_{1}
is controlled, through the identities
(
2.9
), by two errors of the time-changed
diffusion: the velocity error
\ell(\lambda)/\lambda-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}
and the clock
error
\eta(\lambda)-\mathbb{E}[e^{-2V(0)}]
. We prove algebraic rates in

\lambda
for these two quantities.

The Feynman–Kac formula connects the resolvent solution
u_{m}(0)
from
Section
4
to the path of
X^{\lambda}
: it represents

3^{-m}u_{m}(0)
as an exponentially weighted time-average of the rescaled
displacement of the particle, plus an exponentially damped boundary term. On the diffusive time scale

t\sim\lambda^{-2}
, the law of large numbers thus gives

3^{-m}u_{m}(0)\approx\ell(\lambda)/\lambda
. The velocity error

\ell(\lambda)/\lambda-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}
therefore splits into the difference

\ell(\lambda)/\lambda-3^{-m}u_{m}(0)
, which measures the law-of-large-numbers
error inside the Feynman–Kac average, and the deviation

3^{-m}u_{m}(0)-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}
, which is the homogenization error estimated in
Section
4
.

The first difference decomposes further into three terms: a short-time term
where the law-of-large-numbers approximation has not yet taken effect; a
long-time term controlled by the uniform quenched law of large numbers of
Section
5
; and a boundary term controlled by the exit estimate
of Proposition
4.12
. Together with the homogenization term, this gives
four contributions to the velocity error. The clock error is treated by the
same decomposition applied to the clock resolvent
q_{m}
from
(
4.4
). Lemmas
6.1

and
6.2
make the four-term bounds explicit, and
Theorem
1.2
follows by optimizing the choices of parameters in each of these estimates.

We first record the scaled Feynman–Kac identity used below. Define

v_{\lambda}(r)\coloneqq\frac{\lambda}{r}X^{\lambda}\Bigl(\frac{r}{\lambda^{2}}\Bigr),\qquad r>0\,.

Set
v_{\lambda}(0)\coloneqq 0
.
The Feynman–Kac formula (
4.6
) gives, for
m,h\in\mathbb{N}
with
\lambda=\rho 3^{-m}
,

(6.1)

3^{-m}u_{m}(0)=E^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}\int_{0}^{\rho 3^{-2m}\tau_{m,h}}\xi e^{-\xi}v_{\lambda}(\rho\xi)d\xi+E^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}\left[e^{-\rho 3^{-2m}\tau_{m,h}}\left(3^{-m}X^{\lambda}(\tau_{m,h})+\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}\right)\right]\,.

For an exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
, a parameter
\zeta>0
, and each
\lambda\in(0,1]
, write

m(\lambda)\coloneqq\Bigl\lceil\frac{-\log_{3}\lambda}{1-\alpha}\Bigr\rceil,\qquad\rho(\lambda)\coloneqq\lambda 3^{m(\lambda)},\qquad h(\lambda)\coloneqq\lfloor\zeta m(\lambda)\rfloor\,,

so that

\displaystyle 3^{\alpha m(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq\rho(\lambda)\leq 3^{1-\alpha}3^{\alpha m(\lambda)}\,,

(6.2)

\displaystyle\rho(\lambda)

\displaystyle\asymp\lambda^{-\alpha/(1-\alpha)}\,,

\displaystyle 3^{-m(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\asymp\lambda^{1/(1-\alpha)}\,.

Let
\beta_{h}
and
\delta=2\beta_{h}/(d+4)
be as in Section
3
, and let
\zeta_{H}
be the constant fixed in Section
4
.

The next lemma decomposes the time-changed velocity error into the four terms described above.

Lemma 6.1

(Velocity decomposition)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\delta/2)
, let
\zeta\in(0,\zeta_{H}]
, let
\theta\in(0,1/4)
, and let
\varepsilon>0
satisfy
\varepsilon(1-\alpha)<\alpha
.
There exist deterministic constants
C,C^{\prime}<\infty
and

(6.3)

\mathbb{Q}\bigl(\lambda_{*}^{H}\leq\lambda\bigr)\leq C^{\prime}\exp\bigl(-c(\log(1/\lambda))^{\sigma}\bigr)\qquad\text{for every }\lambda\in(0,1]\,.

The constants
C,C^{\prime},c,\sigma
depend only on the displayed parameters and on
(d,\Lambda,r_{0},\beta_{h})
. For
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega
and every

,

(6.4)

\displaystyle\Bigl|\frac{\ell(\lambda)}{\lambda}-\bar{\mathbf{a}}e_{1}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq C\rho(\lambda)^{-2}\lambda^{-2\varepsilon}+C\rho(\lambda)^{-1/2+\theta}+Ce^{-c3^{h(\lambda)}}

\displaystyle+C\rho(\lambda)^{1/2}3^{(d/2+1-\beta_{h})h(\lambda)-\delta m(\lambda)}\,.

Proof.

We work on the intersection of the
\mathbb{Q}
-full-probability events of Proposition
4.8
and of Corollary
5.13
applied with parameters

,

,

.
By (

) and

, the deterministic scale conditions required by Proposition

, including

, hold for all sufficiently large

.

Step 1.
The boundary data

). From (

),
\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi e^{-\xi}d\xi=1

\Bigl|\frac{\ell(\lambda)}{\lambda}-3^{-m}u_{m}(0)\Bigr|\leq\underbrace{E^{\lambda,\omega}_{0}\int_{0}^{\rho 3^{-2m}\tau_{m,h}}\xi e^{-\xi}\Bigl|v_{\lambda}(\rho\xi)-\frac{\ell(\lambda)}{\lambda}\Bigr|d\xi}_{(a)}\\

\lambda^{-\varepsilon}/\rho\leq 3^{1-\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha/(1-\alpha)-\varepsilon}\to 0

Step 3.
For

(b)+(c)\leq Ce^{-c3^{h}}\,.

Step 5.
Combining (

)–(

) with Proposition

) with deterministic

The condition
\lambda\leq\lambda_{*}^{H}(\omega)

7.
Renewal identity at regeneration times

The goal of this section is to prove Lemma
5.2
. The clock estimates in Section
5
require a renewal identity at a general regeneration time that includes the environment. The available results do not directly give this:
Shen 2003
;
Shen 2004
prove the first-regeneration decomposition and the half-space measurability that underlie the environment factorization, but only at
\tau_{1}
;
Gantert et al. 2012
adapts the construction to a
\lambda
-dependent scale and establishes the path renewal structure for all
\tau_{k}

\Delta A_{k}^{\lambda}=\int_{\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k+1}}e^{-2V(X^{\lambda}(s))}\,ds

depends on the environment after
\tau_{k}

The terminal bridge ending at
r\Delta
is controlled by

\sup_{r\Delta-\Delta\leq s\leq r\Delta}e_{1}\cdot X^{\lambda}(s)\leq e_{1}\cdot X^{\lambda}(r\Delta-\Delta)+11R\leq e_{1}\cdot X^{\lambda}(q\Delta-\Delta)+R.

The shifted
k=1
pre-segment bound inside the next block gives

\sup_{r\Delta\leq s\leq q\Delta-\Delta}e_{1}\cdot X^{\lambda}(s)\leq e_{1}\cdot X^{\lambda}(q\Delta-\Delta)+R.

If the bound holds up to
r\Delta-\Delta
, the inequality above and the previous bridge estimate make it hold up to
q\Delta-\Delta
. Iterating over the finitely many completed blocks in the sub-slice proves (
7.3
).

For (
7.4
), every
y\in B^{X^{\lambda}(n\Delta-\Delta)}=B_{R}(X^{\lambda}(n\Delta-\Delta)+9Re_{1})
satisfies

e_{1}\cdot y\geq e_{1}\cdot X^{\lambda}(n\Delta-\Delta)+8R.

Combining this with (
7.3
) yields (
7.4
).

On
\{Y_{n-1}=1\}
, the endpoint condition
X^{\lambda}(n\Delta)\in B^{X^{\lambda}(n\Delta-\Delta)}
holds, so (
7.5
) follows by taking
y=X^{\lambda}(n\Delta)
in (
7.4
).
∎

Lemma 7.3

(Renewal identity on a deterministic slice)
.

Fix
k\geq 1
and
n\geq 2k
. Let
H_{\rm past}
be a bounded random variable measurable with respect to

\sigma(X^{\lambda}_{s}:0\leq s\leq n\Delta-\Delta)\vee\sigma(Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{n-2}),

let
G
be a Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded measurable with
F(y,\cdot)

\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{L}(y\cdot e_{1}-4R)}
-measurable for each
y
. Define

(7.7)

H=\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{k}=n\Delta\}}H_{\rm past}\mathbf{1}_{\{X^{\lambda}(n\Delta)\in G\}}F(X^{\lambda}(n\Delta),\omega),\qquad n\geq 2k\,,

and let
\Phi
be a bounded function of the shifted future data that has the following form: for bounded measurable
f
of the shifted path and marks and bounded measurable
g
of the shifted environment restricted to
\mathsf{R}_{+}
,

\Phi(\gamma,\beta,\omega_{+})=f(\gamma,\beta)g(\omega_{+})\,.

Then

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{k})H]=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\mid D=\infty]\,\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[H]\,.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma_{k,n}
contains only finite-time past data, the terminal mark, bridge endpoint, and no-backtracking event can be averaged as in the proof of
Shen 2003
, around the endpoint integration leading to display (2.21).

On the slice
\{\tau_{k}=n\Delta\}
, the trace of
\mathcal{G}_{k}
is generated by the path on
[0,n\Delta-\Delta]
, the marks
Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{n-2}
, the endpoint
X^{\lambda}(n\Delta)
, and the environment in
\mathsf{L}(e_{1}\cdot X^{\lambda}(n\Delta)-4R)
. As
H_{\rm past}
,
G
, and
F
range over their generating classes, the variables
H
in (
7.7
) generate this trace, with
F(y,\cdot)
supplying the environment generators.

Step 1.
Slice formula. With this form of
\Phi
,

\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{k})=f\bigl(X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k}+\cdot)-X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k}),(Y_{\lambda^{2}\tau_{k}+j})_{j\geq 0}\bigr)g\bigl(T_{X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k})}(\mathbf{a},e^{-2V})|_{\mathsf{R}_{+}}\bigr)\,,

and
H
as in (
7.7
). The decomposition (
7.2
) from Lemma
7.2
restricts the integration to the slice

\{\tau_{k}=n\Delta\}=\Gamma_{k,n}\cap\{Y_{n-1}=1\}\cap\{D\circ\theta_{n\Delta}=\infty\}\,.

By Lemma
7.1
(c), the mark
\{Y_{n-1}=1\}
contributes
p_{*}
, the endpoint is averaged uniformly over
B^{X^{\lambda}(n\Delta-\Delta)}
, and the shifted future path and marks restart from
y
. Thus

(7.8)

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\bigl[\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{k})H\bigr]=p_{*}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\Gamma_{k,n}}H_{\rm past}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B^{X^{\lambda}(n\Delta-\Delta)}}\mathbf{1}_{\{y\in G\}}F(y,\omega)\widehat{E}_{y}^{\lambda,\omega}\bigl[\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{0});D=\infty\bigr]dy\right]\,.

To factor the expectation using finite-range independence, first take the quenched expectation over the path and marks before
n\Delta
. For fixed
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
define

A^{\rm pre}_{k,n}(y,\omega)\coloneqq\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,\omega}\!\left[\mathbf{1}_{\Gamma_{k,n}}H_{\rm past}\mathbf{1}_{\{y\in B^{X^{\lambda}(n\Delta-\Delta)}\}}\right],

and set

A^{\rm past}_{k,n}(y,\omega)\coloneqq\mathbf{1}_{\{y\in G\}}F(y,\omega)A^{\rm pre}_{k,n}(y,\omega),\qquad A^{\rm future}_{\Phi}(y,\omega)\coloneqq\widehat{E}_{y}^{\lambda,\omega}\bigl[\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{0});D=\infty\bigr].

The factors
A^{\rm past}_{k,n}
and
A^{\rm future}_{\Phi}
are jointly Borel measurable in
(y,\omega)
. When
H_{\rm past}
is an indicator function, measurability of the past factor follows from the finite-time construction of
\Gamma_{k,n}
and the measurable coupled kernels; bounded
H_{\rm past}
then follow by the monotone-class theorem. The future factor is measurable because
\{D=\infty\}
is a monotone limit of finite-time events.
By Fubini’s theorem, (
7.8
) is equivalently

(7.9)

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\bigl[\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{k})H\bigr]=\frac{p_{*}}{|B_{R}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\left[A^{\rm past}_{k,n}(y,\cdot)A^{\rm future}_{\Phi}(y,\cdot)\right]\,dy.

Step 2.
Half-space factorization. The event
\Gamma_{k,n}\cap\{y\in B^{X^{\lambda}(n\Delta-\Delta)}\}
appearing in
A^{\rm pre}_{k,n}(y,\omega)
is supported, by (
7.4
), on

\left\{\sup_{0\leq s\leq n\Delta-\Delta}e_{1}\cdot X^{\lambda}(s)\leq y\cdot e_{1}-7R\right\}.

Lemma
7.1
(e) therefore makes
A^{\rm pre}_{k,n}(y,\cdot)

\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{L}(y\cdot e_{1}-4R)}
-measurable, first for indicator
H_{\rm past}
and then, by monotone class, for bounded
H_{\rm past}
. Multiplying by
F(y,\cdot)
preserves this measurability, so
A^{\rm past}_{k,n}(y,\cdot)
is also
\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{L}(y\cdot e_{1}-4R)}
-measurable. The future factor
A^{\rm future}_{\Phi}(y,\cdot)
is
\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{R}(y\cdot e_{1}-2R)}
-measurable by the future half-space statement in the same lemma.

Since the half-spaces
\mathsf{L}(y\cdot e_{1}-4R)
and
\mathsf{R}(y\cdot e_{1}-2R)
are separated by distance
2R\geq 2l>r_{0}
, Assumption
1.1
gives, for every fixed
y
,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\left[A^{\rm past}_{k,n}(y,\cdot)A^{\rm future}_{\Phi}(y,\cdot)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\left[A^{\rm past}_{k,n}(y,\cdot)\right]\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\left[A^{\rm future}_{\Phi}(y,\cdot)\right].

Step 3.
Conclusion on the slice. Lemma
7.1
(f) gives

\widehat{E}_{y}^{\lambda,\omega}\bigl[\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{0});D=\infty\bigr]=\widehat{E}_{0}^{\lambda,T_{y}(\mathbf{a},e^{-2V})}\bigl[\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{0});D=\infty\bigr]

for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Combined with the spatial stationarity of the law of
(\mathbf{a},e^{-2V})
under
\mathbb{Q}
, this makes the
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-mean of
A^{\rm future}_{\Phi}(y,\cdot)
independent of
y
and equal to
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{0});D=\infty]
. Applying (
7.9
) with
\Phi\equiv 1
gives

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[H]=\frac{p_{*}}{|B_{R}|}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}(D=\infty)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\left[A^{\rm past}_{k,n}(y,\cdot)\right]\,dy.

Since
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}(D=\infty)>0
by (
5.1
), the formula for
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[H]
above and (
7.9
) give

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}\bigl[\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{k})H\bigr]=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[\Phi(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\mid D=\infty]\cdot\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}^{\lambda}[H]\,.

∎

To extend the identity proved on each slice
\{\tau_{k}=n\Delta\}
to the clock-duration blocks
M_{i}
, past blocks must be
\mathcal{G}_{k}
-measurable and future blocks must be determined by
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
.

Lemma 7.4

(Past and future measurability of clock blocks)
.

For every
k\geq 1
, the following hold.

(i)

If
1\leq i\leq k-2
, then
M_{i}
is
\mathcal{G}_{k}
-measurable.

(ii)

There is a measurable map
\Psi
on the common state space of the shifted future data such that

(M_{k},M_{k+1},\ldots)=\Psi(\mathcal{Y}_{k})

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
-almost surely.

Proof.

Step 1.
We prove (i). Fix an integer
i
with
1\leq i\leq k-2
and work on the slice
\{\tau_{k}=n\Delta\}
with
n\geq 2k
.

Unfold the recursive construction of
\Gamma_{k,n}
in Lemma
7.2
, starting from the terminal slice
\tau_{k}=n\Delta
and continuing down to
\tau_{1}
. This gives a finite disjoint family of finite-time cells
E_{\mathbf{n}}
indexed by tuples

\mathbf{n}=(n_{0},\ldots,n_{k}),\qquad n_{0}=0,\quad n_{k}=n,\quad n_{j}-n_{j-1}\geq 2\quad(1\leq j\leq k)\,.

After intersecting with
\{Y_{n-1}=1\}\cap\{D\circ\theta_{n\Delta}=\infty\}
, these cells partition
\{\tau_{k}=n\Delta\}
up to null sets, and on each cell
\tau_{j}=n_{j}\Delta
for every
0\leq j\leq k
. Every intermediate no-backtracking condition has already been replaced by a finite-time condition before
n\Delta-\Delta
, so each
E_{\mathbf{n}}
is measurable with respect to

\sigma(X^{\lambda}_{s}:0\leq s\leq n\Delta-\Delta)\vee\sigma(Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{n-2}).

By (
5.3
),
\tau_{k-1}\leq\tau_{k}-\Delta=n\Delta-\Delta
on the slice. Since
i+1\leq k-1
, also
\tau_{i+1}\leq n\Delta-\Delta
, so
\Delta\tau_{i}
is determined on each cell
E_{\mathbf{n}}
. For the clock increment, (
7.5
) places the path on
[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]
inside
\mathsf{L}_{k}
. Hence

\Delta A_{i}^{\lambda}=\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}e^{-2V(X^{\lambda}(s))}\,ds

is a measurable function of the path on
[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]
and the restricted field
e^{-2V}|_{\mathsf{L}_{k}}
, that is, of the data in items (a) and (c) of Lemma
5.2
. Since
\tau_{k}
is recorded in
\mathcal{G}_{k}
, the slices are
\mathcal{G}_{k}
-measurable; assembling these slicewise representatives over the countably many slices gives a
\mathcal{G}_{k}
-measurable representative of
M_{i}
. This proves (i).

Step 2.
We prove (ii). We work on the
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}^{\lambda}
-full event
\{\tau_{j}<\infty\text{ for every }j\geq 1\}
. On the slice
\{\tau_{k}=n\Delta\}
, the factor
\{D\circ\theta_{n\Delta}=\infty\}
in (
7.2
) gives

e_{1}\cdot(X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k}+s)-X^{\lambda}(\tau_{k}))\geq-R\qquad\text{for every }s\geq 0\,.

Thus the shifted post-
\tau_{k}
path lies in
\mathsf{R}_{+}
. Every future clock integral uses only the restricted environment in
\mathsf{R}_{+}
, and the future bridge balls also stay in
\mathsf{R}_{+}
: their starts have shifted
e_{1}
-coordinate at least
-R
, while Lemma
7.1
(d) centers each radius-
6R
bridge ball another
5R
forward.

Let
\mathfrak{Y}
be the common state space of triples

(\gamma,(\beta_{n})_{n\geq 0},\omega_{+})\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\times\Omega_{\mathsf{R}_{+}},

with its product Borel
\sigma
-field, where
\Omega_{\mathsf{R}_{+}}
is the restriction space for the field on
\mathsf{R}_{+}
. On a triple in
\mathfrak{Y}
, run the deterministic regeneration recursion using
\gamma
and
\beta
. The hitting times, rounded hitting times, candidate times, and Bernoulli tests are Borel functionals of
(\gamma,\beta)
.

Let
s_{0}=0<s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots
be the resulting regeneration times when they are all finite. Since the fields are uniformly Lipschitz, the evaluation map
(\omega_{+},z)\mapsto e^{-2V_{+}}(z)
is Borel. On the Borel event that all
s_{j}
are finite and
\gamma([s_{j},s_{j+1}])\subseteq\mathsf{R}_{+}
for every
j\geq 0
, set

\Psi_{j}(\gamma,\beta,\omega_{+})\coloneqq\left(\int_{s_{j}}^{s_{j+1}}e^{-2V_{+}(\gamma(u))}\,du,\ s_{j+1}-s_{j}\right),\qquad j\geq 0,

where
e^{-2V_{+}}
denotes the clock integrand in
\omega_{+}
. Off this event, assign a fixed deterministic default sequence. The map
(\gamma,\omega_{+},u)\mapsto e^{-2V_{+}(\gamma(u))}
is jointly Borel, so the time integrals are measurable by Fubini’s theorem applied to the jointly Borel integrand, after truncating on
\{s_{j+1}\leq m\}
and letting
m\uparrow\infty
. This gives a measurable map

\Psi\colon\mathfrak{Y}\to([0,\infty)\times[\Delta,\infty))^{\mathbb{N}}.

On the actual shifted future data
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
, no-backtracking keeps the shifted path in
\mathsf{R}_{+}
for all future times. The path–mark recursion read from
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
is, by translation covariance, the regeneration recursion for the blocks after
\tau_{k}
. Each future clock integral uses only the displayed environment restriction, so
(M_{k},M_{k+1},\ldots)=\Psi(\mathcal{Y}_{k})
almost surely. This proves (ii).
∎

Combining these lemmas via a monotone-class argument gives Lemma

.
