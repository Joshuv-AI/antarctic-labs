The mitochondrial genome of Helina trivittata (Diptera: Muscidae) and its phylogenetic analysis
Authors: Hao Liu, Yuli Zhang, Jinglu Sun, Yuanrun Zheng, Shengjuan Zhao, Wenliang Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe genus Helina Robineau-Desvoidy, 1830 is one of the most species-rich genera in Muscidae, yet mitochondrial genomic resources remain scarce. Here, we present the first complete mitochondrial genome of Helina trivittata (Zetterstedt, 1860). The mitogenome is 18,959 bp long and contains the typical set of 37 mitochondrial genes, with an overall AT content of 82.01%. Phylogenetic analyses support the monophyly of Phaoniinae and recover Helina as the sister group of Phaonia. This study provides a useful genomic resource for future evolutionary and systematic investigations of Muscidae.Keywords: Mitochondrial genome, Muscidae, Helina, Helina trivittata

Introduction

The family Muscidae is one of the most diverse groups within Diptera, comprising approximately 180 genera and over 5,200 described species worldwide (de Carvalho et al. 2005). Among them, the genus Helina exhibits remarkable species diversity, with 683 known species recorded worldwide (Li et al. 2025). Historically, the taxonomic status of Helina has been a subject of debate, and it was once considered a junior synonym of Phaonia (Li et al. 2023). To date, mitochondrial genetic data for this genus remain relatively limited; only two mitochondrial genomic sequences have been documented within this genus. The species Helina trivittata (Zetterstedt, 1860) has been recorded from many regions across China. This study presents the first complete mitochondrial genome of H. trivittata. This achievement is of great significance for clarifying the phylogenetic position of the genus Helina.

Materials and methods

Sample collection and sequencing

The specimens of Helina trivittata were collected on July 22, 2021, in the Tianchi Town, Arxan City, Xing’an League, Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, China (X407 Road) (47° 21′N, 120° 9′E). As the collected samples were insects, no ethical approval or relevant permits were required for this collection. The genitalia preparation procedure involved excising the terminal portion of the abdomen and immersing it in a cold, saturated NaOH solution for four hours to soften the tissues. Subsequently, the specimen was thoroughly rinsed with water, neutralized, and subjected to detailed dissection and examination. After observation in glycerin, the specimen was transferred to fresh glycerin, housed in a micro-vial, and affixed beneath the main specimen mount. This specimens were identified by Hao Liu. H. trivittata can be differentiated from other species based on the following morphological characteristics: vein R4 + 5 with setulae ventrally at base, scutellum soft-haired ventrally at tip, meron setulose below spiracle, 3 postsutural dorsocentrals, short prealar, katepisternals 2 + 2, and plumose arista. The specimens are currently preserved in 100% absolute ethanol and archived at Henan University of Science and Technology, Henan, China (www. haust. edu. cn, contact person: Liu Hao; e-mail: 240320191193@stu. haust. edu. cn) under the voucher number Helina 201800009.

Genome assembly and annotation

Adult specimens of H. trivittata were photographed using a Sony α7R IV digital camera mounted on a stereomicroscope (Sony, Tokyo, Japan), and images were stacked using HELICON FOCUS v8.1.0 (Helicon Soft, Kharkiv, Ukraine) (Figure 1).

The specimen map of helina trivittata. This species reference image was taken by Liu.

Total genomic DNA was extracted from adult specimens and sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform by Qingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). Raw reads were filtered using fastp to remove low-quality reads and adapter sequences, yielding 13.22 Gb of clean data.

The mitochondrial genome was de novo assembled using SPAdes v3.14.1 with k-mer values of 21, 45, 65, 85, and 105 (Bankevich et al. 2012). The assembly graph was visualized using Bandage (Wick et al. 2015). The assembled mitochondrial genome was annotated using MITOS, and the annotation results were manually corrected using the mitochondrial genome of Phaonia falleni (GenBank accession number OZ310644) as a reference. Finally, a circular mitochondrial genome map was generated using Organellar Genome DRAW (OGDRAW) v1.2 (Greiner et al. 2019).

Phylogenetic analysis

Sequence alignment and trimming of the 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs) and 2 ribosomal RNA genes of H. trivittata, along with 27 additional sequences retrieved from the GenBank database, were conducted using MAFFT v7.526 software with the L-INS-i (Rozewicki et al. 2019). These retrieved sequences included four Dacinae species, four Phytomyzinae species, four Exoristinae species, two Muscinae species, four Reinwardtiinae species, two Azeliinae species and five Phaoniinae species. Subsequently, the phylogenetic tree was constructed employing the maximum likelihood (ML) method implemented in IQ-TREE software (Minh et al. 2020). The optimal nucleotide substitution model was selected using the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), and GTR+F + R4 was identified as the best-fit model. This model was subsequently applied to the dataset for maximum-likelihood phylogenetic reconstruction. Branch support was assessed using the ultrafast bootstrap approximation (UFBoot) with 4,000 replicates and the SH-aLRT test with 1,000 replicates. Furthermore, a Bayesian inference (BI) analysis was performed using MrBayes v3.2.7. Two independent Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) runs were executed for 2,000,000 generations, with sampling every 1,000 generations, and the initial 25% of samples were discarded as burn-in.

Results

The assembled mitochondrial genome was supported by high sequencing coverage across the entire genome (Figure S1), with an average sequencing depth of 702.98×, a maximum depth of 7998×, and a minimum depth of 154×.

The complete mitochondrial genome of H. trivittata (Figure 2) is 18,959 bp in length, with a strong AT bias (82.01%). It comprises the standard set of 37 mitochondrial genes and a complete control region. Both gene content and gene order are highly conserved and conform to the typical mitochondrial genome organization observed in other Muscidae species.

Genes located on the inner side of the circle are transcribed in the counterclockwise direction, while those on the outer side are transcribed clockwise. Genes are color-coded according to their functional categories. The inner gray histogram represents the GC content across the genome, with the Central gray line indicating the 50% threshold.

Among the 13 PCGs of H. trivittata, standard ATN start codons are predominant. Specifically, six genes (COX2, ATP6, COX3, ND4, ND4L, and CYTB) utilize ATG, while five genes (ND2, ATP8, ND3, ND5, and ND6) start with ATT. The ND1 gene initiates with TTG, and the start codon of COX1 remains unconventional (TCG). Regarding termination, eight PCGs terminate with complete stop codons (TAA for ND2, ATP8, ATP6, COX3, ND3, ND4L, ND6, and ND1; TAG is not observed). Incomplete stop codons (T) are assigned to the remaining five genes (COX1, COX2, ND5, ND4, and CYTB). The rrnL and rrnS genes are 1,326 bp and 784 bp in length, respectively. Gene overlaps are present, notably a 7-bp overlap between ATP8 and ATP6, and a 1-bp overlap between ND4 and ND4L (Table S1).

The mitochondrial genome of H. trivittata was compared with the two previously reported Helina mitogenomes, H. impuncta and H. latitarsis. All three species shared the typical mitochondrial gene complement of 37 genes and exhibited identical gene arrangements. The overall nucleotide composition showed a pronounced AT bias in all species. Genome size varied slightly among the three taxa, whereas gene content and organization remained highly conserved.

Both maximum likelihood (ML) and Bayesian inference (BI) analyses recovered identical topologies with strong support values at all major nodes (Figure 3). The monophyly of Muscidae was strongly supported (BS = 100, PP = 1.00). The recovered phylogenetic relationships were as follows: Dacinae + (Phytomyzinae + (Exoristinae + ((Azeliinae + Muscinae) + (Reinwardtiinae + Phaoniinae)))).

The monophyly of Phaoniinae was strongly supported (BS = 100, PP = 1.00). Helina trivittata was stably placed within the Phaoniinae clade. Within the subfamily, the monophyly of Helina was not recovered. Phylogenetic analysis showed that H. trivittata formed a sister group relationship with species of Phaonia. The recovered topology was: H. trivittata + ((Phaonia angelicae + Phaonia falleni) + (Phaonia tuguriorum + (Helina latitarsis + Helina impuncta))).

Discussion and conclusion

In this study, we report the first complete mitochondrial genome of Helina trivittata, providing valuable mitochondrial genomic data for a genus with limited genomic resources. The genome exhibits typical characteristics of Dipteran mitochondrial genomes, including a strong AT bias, conserved gene arrangement, and the presence of incomplete stop codons in several protein-coding genes. The COI gene appears to utilize an atypical start codon (TCG), a feature that has also been reported in other Dipteran species.

Both maximum likelihood (ML) and Bayesian inference (BI) analyses recovered congruent topologies with strong nodal support. The phylogenetic trees were largely consistent with the traditional taxonomic classification of Muscomorpha. Helina trivittata was robustly placed within the monophyletic Muscoidea, and the recovered relationships, Dacinae + (Phytomyzinae + (Exoristinae + ((Azeliinae + Muscinae) + (Reinwardtiinae + Phaoniinae)))), provide additional molecular evidence supporting previous morphology-based hypotheses. The resulting topology strongly supports the monophyly of Phaoniinae. Within this clade, H. trivittata was recovered as the sister taxon to the genus Phaonia with high support values in both ML and BI analyses. This relationship further clarifies the phylogenetic position of Helina within Muscidae and provides genomic evidence for its close affinity with Phaonia.

This work provides useful data for exploring evolutionary relationships within Phaoniinae and supplements taxon sampling for mitochondrial phylogenetic research in Diptera. While these findings contribute to the field, future studies should expand taxon sampling by including more species, which will allow more robust comparative analyses and contribute to a better understanding of the evolutionary history of Helina. Future investigations incorporating nuclear genomic data alongside morphological analyses may help further clarify the phylogeny and taxonomy of this diverse fly lineage.
