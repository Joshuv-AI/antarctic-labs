From finite to infinite length modules over tame hereditary algebras
Lidia Angeleri Hügel, Andrew Hubery, Henning Krause

Abstract
. A self-contained introduction to infinite dimensional representations over a tame hereditary algebra is provided, assuming a basic knowledge of the category of finite dimensional representations. This includes a complete description of all pure-injective modules. Of particular interest are the torsionfree divisible modules, which are precisely the direct sums of copies of the unique generic module.



1.
Introduction

Beyond finite representation type, a systematic study of
infinite dimensional representations over finite dimensional algebras was
initiated by Claus Michael Ringel in his seminal ‘Rome notes’
published in 1979
[
27
]
. We quote from its introduction:

Why is it of interest to consider infinite dimensional
representations? We believe that the only reason for the usual
restriction in dealing with finite dimensional algebras to consider
only modules of finite length, is the fact that modules of finite
length are easier to handle.

One of the main achievements in Ringel’s work is to single out a
particular module for a tame hereditary algebra; it is the unique
indecomposable representation which is of infinite length but of
finite endolength. Following Crawley-Boevey, such modules are now
called ‘generic’
[
4
]
. Let us see how Ringel introduces this
module in his work:

A typical example would be the Kronecker module

(k[X],k[X],\text{id},\cdot X)
. Of course, in dealing with
finitely generated modules over a general ring, the injective
envelopes of the finitely generated modules are important. They are
usually no longer finitely generated, but are for certain types of
rings, for example noetherian rings, well behaved. In a suitable
subcategory, the injective envelope of the Kronecker module
(k[X],k|X],\text{id},\cdot X)
is just the Kronecker module
Q=(k(X),k(X),\text{id},\cdot X)
,
with
k(X)
being the field of rational functions in one variable. We
will see that this Kronecker module plays a dominant role, it will
be characterized as the unique indecomposable ‘torsionfree
divisible’ module. Thus, there always will be certain important
infinite dimensional representations, and we will see that the
investigation of these modules also gives some new insight into the
behaviour of the modules of finite length.

Almost 50 years later, some further techniques have been developed,
and the purpose of this note is to give from this perspective a rather
self-contained introduction to infinite dimensional representations
over a tame hereditary algebra. We assume a basic knowledge about
the category of finite dimensional representations and concentrate on
techniques that help to understand the class of pure-injective
modules. A full classification of these modules is possible when the
algebra is tame hereditary, and this class includes the torsionfree
divisible modules.

Most of the results about modules over tame hereditary
algebras which are presented here are due to Ringel
[
27
]
. In the last section we
include some further material and give appropriate references. In
particular, we provide a complete description of all pure-injective
modules; while the result is known, our proof seems to be new. For unexplained concepts
and general facts about modules of infinite length we refer to

[
5
,
15
,
19
,
16
,
25
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
k
be a field, and
\Lambda
a finite dimensional
k
-algebra
which is tame hereditary and connected. We study the category

\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
of all right
\Lambda
-modules, and denote by
\operatorname{mod}\Lambda

the full subcategory of finite dimensional
\Lambda
-modules. For the
definition of a tame hereditary algebra and the structure of

\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
we refer to
[
6
]
.

The vector space dual
D=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(-,k)
yields a duality between modules
over
\Lambda
and its opposite
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
.

For an additive category
\mathcal{C}
and full additive subcategories
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
we write

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{1}\vee\mathcal{C}_{2}

provided
\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}=\{0\}
and every object
C\in\mathcal{C}
admits a
decomposition
C=C_{1}\oplus C_{2}
with
C_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
.

Given a full additive subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
we set

\bar{\mathcal{C}}\coloneqq\{X\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\mid X=\operatorname*{colim}_{i\in I}C_{i}\text{ with all }C_{i}\in\mathcal{C}\},

so the completion of
\mathcal{C}
under filtered colimits. Also, let

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}\coloneqq\{X\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\mid\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(C,X)=0=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(C,X)\text{ for all }C\in\mathcal{C}\}

be the right perpendicular category.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
be a torsion pair for
\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
. Then

(\bar{\mathcal{T}},\bar{\mathcal{F}})
is a torsion pair for
\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
, and

\bar{\mathcal{F}}=\{Y\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\mid\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(X,Y)=0\text{ for all
}X\in\mathcal{T}\}
.

Proof.

For
X=\operatorname*{colim}_{i}X_{i}
in
\bar{\mathcal{T}}
and
Y=\operatorname*{colim}_{j}Y_{j}
in
\bar{\mathcal{F}}
we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(X,Y)=\lim\nolimits_{i}\operatorname*{colim}\nolimits_{j}\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(X_{i},Y_{j})=0.

For an arbitrary module
X=\operatorname*{colim}_{i\in I}X_{i}
, written as filtered colimit of finite dimensional modules, we have the canonical exact sequences

0\to\mathfrak{t}(X_{i})\to X_{i}\to\mathfrak{f}(X_{i})\to 0\qquad(i\in I)

with
\mathfrak{t}(X_{i})\in\mathcal{T}
and
\mathfrak{f}(X_{i})\in\mathcal{F}
. Taking their filtered
colimit provides an exact sequence with end terms in
\bar{\mathcal{T}}
and

\bar{\mathcal{F}}
, respectively. The last assertion is clear since

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(-,Y)
takes filtered colimits to limits.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is a split torsion pair for
\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
. Then
any exact sequence
0\to X^{\prime}\to X\to X^{\prime\prime}\to 0
with

X^{\prime}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}
and
X^{\prime\prime}\in\bar{\mathcal{F}}
is pure exact. Moreover such a
sequence splits whenever there are only finitely many indecomposables
either in
\mathcal{T}
or in
\mathcal{F}
; see Corollary
3.7
.

Our starting point is the decomposition

\operatorname{mod}\Lambda=\mathcal{P}\vee\mathcal{R}\vee\mathcal{I}

where
\mathcal{P}
,
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{I}
are the full subcategories of
preprojective
,
regular
, and
preinjective
modules, respectively. Note that
(\mathcal{R}\vee\mathcal{I},\mathcal{P})
is a split torsion pair for
\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
, as is
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{P}\vee\mathcal{R})
. It follows that
(\overline{\mathcal{R}\vee\mathcal{I}},\bar{\mathcal{P}})
is a torsion pair for
\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
, and so every module
X
admits a pure exact sequence

0\to\mathfrak{t}(X)\to X\to\mathfrak{f}(X)\to 0.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a
\Lambda
-module. Then we say:

(1)

X
is
preinjective
if it is in
\bar{\mathcal{I}}

(2)

X
is
torsion
if it is in
\overline{\mathcal{R}\vee\mathcal{I}}

(3)

X
is
torsionfree
if it is in
\bar{\mathcal{P}}

As every finite dimensional preinjective module is a quotient of a regular module, we see that
X
is torsionfree if and only if
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{R},X)=0
. We further define

(4)

X
is
divisible
if
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(X,\mathcal{R})=0

(5)

X
is
regular
if
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(X,\mathcal{P})=0=\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{I},X)

A module
X
is divisible if and only if
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{R},X)=0
. This
follows from the Auslander–Reiten formula.

Preinjective modules

As usual we define the bimodule
\tau^{-}\Lambda\coloneqq\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(D\Lambda,\Lambda)
, where we consider the extensions as right
\Lambda
-modules. We obtain the adjoint pair of functors
\tau^{-}\coloneqq-\otimes_{\Lambda}\tau^{-}\Lambda
and
\tau\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\tau^{-}\Lambda,-)
on
\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
, extending the usual Auslander–Reiten translate on
\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
. A finite dimensional indecomposable module
X
is then preprojective if and only if
\tau^{n}X=0
for some
n>0
, and preinjective if and only if
\tau^{-n}X=0
for some
n>0
.

As
\Lambda
is tame, one can furthermore define the
defect

\partial(X)
of a finite dimensional module
X
. This is an additive function taking integer values such that, if
X
is indecomposable, then
X
is preprojective if and only if
\partial(X)<0
, and preinjective if and only if
\partial(X)>0
. We further assume that
\partial
is normalised, so has range
\mathbb{Z}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Every module
X
admits a maximal preinjective submodule
\mathfrak{i}(X)
,
which is furthermore a pure submodule and a direct sum of
indecomposable direct summands of
X
lying in
\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

We follow
[
27
, §3]
. Using the split torsion pair

(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{P}\vee\mathcal{R})
on
\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
we see that every module
X
admits
a maximal preinjective submodule
\mathfrak{i}(X)
, which is furthermore a
pure submodule of
X
. Explicitly, we proceed inductively as
follows. For
n\geq 0
let

\mathcal{I}_{n}=(\operatorname{Ker}\tau^{-n})\cap\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
. This is a torsion class in

\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
and we write
\mathfrak{i}_{n}(X)
for the maximal submodule of
X

in
\bar{\mathcal{I}}_{n}=\operatorname{Ker}\tau^{-n}
, which is a direct summand of

X
. Here one uses that
\mathcal{I}_{n}
contains up to isomorphism only finitely many
indecomposables, and therefore
\bar{\mathcal{I}}_{n}
consists of the
direct sums of copies of the indecomposables in
\mathcal{I}_{n}
; cf. Corollary
3.7
. We obtain a
chain of split monomorphisms

\mathfrak{i}_{0}(X)\to\mathfrak{i}_{1}(X)\to\mathfrak{i}_{2}(X)\to\cdots
such that

\mathfrak{i}(X)=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathfrak{i}_{n}(X)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(\mathfrak{i}_{n+1}/\mathfrak{i}_{n})(X)

where
(\mathfrak{i}_{n+1}/\mathfrak{i}_{n})(X)
is a
direct sum of copies of the finitely many indecomposables in

\mathcal{I}_{n+1}-\mathcal{I}_{n}
.
∎

3.
Purity and endofiniteness

In this section we collect some of the concepts that are used to study
modules of infinite length. Of particular interest are
pure-injective modules, with important examples being the endofinite
modules. Most of this material is independent from
the fact that
\Lambda
is tame hereditary.

Recall that an exact sequence
0\to X\to Y\to Z\to 0
in
\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
is

pure exact
if it stays exact after applying
-\otimes_{\Lambda}M

for any
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
-module
M
. An equivalent condition is that the
sequence is a filtered colimit of split exact sequences. In this case

X\to Y
is called
pure monomorphism
, and
X
is

pure-injective
if every pure monomorphism
X\to Y
splits. The
module
X
is
\Sigma
-pure injective
if every direct sum of copies
of
X
is pure-injective, and
X
is
endofinite
if it is of finite
length as an
\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(X)
-module.

Coherent functors

Recall that an additive functor
F\colon\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\to\mathrm{Ab}
into the
category of abelian groups is
coherent
if it admits a presentation

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(C_{1},-)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(C_{0},-)\to F\to 0

with
C_{0},C_{1}\in\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
. We note that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(C,-)
and
-\otimes_{\Lambda}C^{\prime}
are coherent for all
C\in\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
and
C^{\prime}\in\operatorname{mod}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
.

The following lemma collects some basic properties which will be used freely.

Lemma 3.1
.

The coherent functors form a full abelian subcategory of the
category of all additive functors
\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\to\mathrm{Ab}
.
Moreover, for
F
coherent the following hold.

(1)

The functor
F
preserves filtered colimits and products.

(2)

The functor
F
maps pure exact sequences to exact sequences.

(3)

The assignment

\operatorname{Coker}\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\phi,-)=F\longmapsto F^{\vee}\coloneqq\operatorname{Ker}(\phi\otimes_{\Lambda}-)

induces a contravariant equivalence between the coherent functors for

\Lambda
and those for
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
. Moreover, there is a natural
isomorphism
F\cong(F^{\vee})^{\vee}
.

(4)

For
X\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
and
Y\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
there are natural isomorphisms

F(D^{2}X)\cong D^{2}F(X)\qquad\text{and}\qquad DF^{\vee}(Y)\cong F(DY).

(5)

The functor
F
is simple if and only
F=\operatorname{Coker}\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\phi,-)
for some left almost split morphism
\phi
in
\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
.

(6)

A
\Lambda
-module
X
has no finite length indecomposable direct summands if and only if
S(X)=0
for each simple coherent functor
S
.

Proof.

It is clear that coherent functors are closed under taking
cokernels. Using the fact that
\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
has cokernels, one
shows that coherent functors are also closed under taking
kernels. This yields the first assertion. Notice that the category
of coherent functors has enough projective objects. This follows
from the fact that the representable functors are projective by
Yoneda’s Lemma.

(1) This is clear when
F=\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(C,-)
, and the general case follows since taking cokernels commutes with filtered colimits and products.

(2) Every pure exact sequence is a filtered colimit of split exact sequences.

(3) We write
F
as the cokernel of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\phi,-)
for some

\phi\colon C_{0}\to C_{1}
. Then
F^{\vee}
is coherent because it is the
kernel of a morphism between coherent functors. Given a coherent
functor
G
for
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
we claim that there is a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}(F,G^{\vee})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(G,F^{\vee}).

This is clear when
F=\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(C,-)
and the general case follows
using the exactness of
0\to F^{\vee}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(C_{0},-)^{\vee}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(C_{1},-)^{\vee}
. The unit
F\to(F^{\vee})^{\vee}
of the adjunction is an isomorphism. Again, this is
clear when
F
is representable and the general case follows by exactness.

(4) The adjunction

D(\phi\otimes_{\Lambda}Y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\phi,DY)

for

Y\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
gives the isomorphism
DF^{\vee}(Y)\cong F(DY)
.
Applying this isomorphism twice and using the isomorphism

F\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}(F^{\vee})^{\vee}
yields, for
X\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
, the isomorphism

D^{2}F(X)\cong F(D^{2}X).

(5) See
[
2
, II.2]
. This uses the existence of left almost split morphisms for modules over a finite dimensional
k
-algebra.

(6) follows from (5).
∎

Definable subcategories

A full subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
is
definable
if there is a family of coherent functors
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
such that

\mathcal{C}=\{X\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\mid F_{i}(X)=0\text{ for all }i\in I\}.

It follows immediately from Lemma
3.1
(4) that
\mathcal{C}
is then closed under taking double duals. Also, every
X\in\mathcal{C}
admits a pure embedding
X\to D^{2}X
where
D^{2}X\in\mathcal{C}
is pure-injective. In particular,
\mathcal{C}
is closed under pure-injective envelopes.

Example 3.2
.

(1) Let
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
be a torsion pair for
\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
. Then

\bar{\mathcal{F}}=\{X\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\mid\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{T},X)=0\}

is definable. Specific examples are the torsionfree modules
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
and the modules in
\overline{\mathcal{P}\vee\mathcal{R}}
.

(2) For
C\in\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
there is a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(-,C)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(-,D^{2}C)\cong D(-\otimes_{\Lambda}DC)

given by adjunction, and therefore

\{X\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\mid\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(X,C)=0\}
is definable. Choosing
for
C
the modules in
\mathcal{R}
or in
\mathcal{P}
, we deduce that
the divisible modules and regular modules both form definable
subcategories.

(3) Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
. Then
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
is definable.

(4) The
\Lambda
-modules having no finite length indecomposable
direct summands form a definable subcategory, see
Lemma
3.1
(6).

Subgroups of finite definition

Let
X
be a
\Lambda
-module. Given a module
C
and an element
c\in C
we have the evaluation map

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(C,X)\to X,\qquad\phi\mapsto\phi(c).

If
C\in\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
then we say that the image
X_{C,c}
is a
subgroup of finite definition
of
X
. Note that
c
induces a morphism
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(C,-)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\Lambda,-)
, whose image
F_{C,c}
is a coherent subfunctor of the forgetful functor, and
F_{C,c}(X)=X_{C,c}
.

Equivalently, every subgroup of finite definition arises as the kernel of a map

X\to X\otimes_{\Lambda}C,\qquad x\mapsto x\otimes c

for some module
C\in\operatorname{mod}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
and element
c\in C
, and is
therefore given by the kernel of the morphism
-\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda\to-\otimes_{\Lambda}C
.

The subgroups of finite definition of
X
are all
\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(X)
-submodules and form a modular lattice
\mathbf{L}(X)
. Moreover, taking a subspace
U\rightarrowtail X
to the kernel of
DX\twoheadrightarrow DU
induces a lattice isomorphism

\mathbf{L}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathbf{L}(DX).

Let
\operatorname{Add}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Ab})
denote the category of additive
functors
\operatorname{mod}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Ab}
. The assignment

X\longmapsto T_{X}\coloneqq X\otimes_{\Lambda}-

provides a useful embedding of
\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
into this functor category.
In fact,
X
is pure-injective if and
only if the object
T_{X}
is injective, and every injective object
E

in this functor category is isomorphic to
T_{Y}
where
Y=E(\Lambda)
.
Moreover, for every coherent
F
there is a natural isomorphism

F(X)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(F^{\vee},T_{X}).

Chain conditions on subgroups of finite definition provide useful characterisations of pure-injective and endofinite modules.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
. Then
X
is
\Sigma
-pure injective if and only if it has the descending chain condition (dcc) on subgroups of finite definition. It is endofinite if and only if it has both acc and dcc on subgroups of finite definition.

Proof.

See
[
13
]
,
[
37
]
,
[
4
, Theorem 3.1 and
Proposition 4.1]
. We sketch the proof, but omit one
implication that is less relevant.

First observe that if
X
has dcc on subgroups of finite definition,
then so too does
X^{(I)}
for any set
I
, so it is enough to show
that
X
is pure-injective.

Consider a short exact sequence of additive functors

0\to F\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\Lambda,-)\to G\to 0
on

\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
. This we can write as the filtered colimit of a
sequence of coherent functors

0\to F_{i}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\Lambda,-)\to G_{i}\to 0
. For

T_{X}=X\otimes_{\Lambda}-
the functor
\operatorname{Hom}(-,T_{X})
is exact on
sequences of coherent functors, and

\operatorname{Hom}(F_{i},T_{X})=F_{i}^{\vee}(X)
. Thus we obtain the filtered
system of short exact sequences

0\to G_{i}^{\vee}(X)\to X\to F_{i}^{\vee}(X)\to 0.

Note that
G_{i}^{\vee}(X)
is a subgroup of finite definition, so the dcc condition for
X
implies that these sequences satisfy the Mittag-Leffler condition, and hence remain exact after taking limits. Thus

0\to\operatorname{Hom}(G,T_{X})\to\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\Lambda,-),T_{X})\to\operatorname{Hom}(F,T_{X})\to 0

is exact, and hence
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(G,T_{X})=0
.

Now let
X\rightarrowtail Y
be a pure-injective envelope. This yields
an exact sequence
0\to T_{X}\to T_{Y}\to T_{Y/X}\to 0
. If
Y/X\neq 0
,
then there is a non-zero morphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\Lambda,-)\to T_{Y/X}
. Denoting its image by
G
, the
functor
T_{X}\oplus G
identifies with a subfunctor of
T_{Y}
, which
contradicts the minimality of
X\rightarrowtail Y
. Thus
X
is
pure-injective.

Next, given
x\in X
, we obtain a morphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\Lambda,-)\to T_{X}
, say with image
G_{x}
. Note that the
induced morphism
\operatorname{Hom}(T_{X},T_{X})\to\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\Lambda,-),T_{X})

identifies with the map
\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(X)\to X
,

\phi\mapsto\phi(x)
. Thus, using that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(G_{x},T_{X})=0
, we see
that
\operatorname{Hom}(G_{x},T_{X})
identifies with the cyclic

\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(X)
-submodule of
X
generated by
x
. Moreover, writing

G_{x}
as a colimit of coherent functors
G_{x,i}
as above, we see
that
\operatorname{Hom}(G_{x},T_{X})=\lim\operatorname{Hom}(G_{x,i},T_{X})
is an intersection of
subgroups of finite definition. Thus our assumption on
X
implies
that each cyclic
\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(X)
-submodule of
X
is of finite
definition.

If moreover
X
has acc on subgroups of finite definition, then every

\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(X)
-submodule has finite definition, and thus
X

is endofinite.

Conversely, an endofinite module
X
satisfies acc and dcc on
subgroups of finite definition since each subgroup of finite
definition is an
\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(X)
-submodule.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Given a pure submodule
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
, the lattice
\mathbf{L}(X^{\prime})

identifies with a sublattice of
\mathbf{L}(X)
. This follows from the
fact that
F(X^{\prime})=X^{\prime}\cap F(X)
for all coherent subfunctors
F
of
the forgetful functor. In particular,
\mathbf{L}(X^{\prime})
inherits any chain
condition from
\mathbf{L}(X)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be an indecomposable pure-injective module. Then
\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(X)
is local. If moreover
X
is endofinite, then
\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(X)
has nilpotent radical, and this vanishes whenever
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(X,X)=0
.

Proof.

Set
E\coloneqq\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(X)
. The functor

X\mapsto T_{X}
from
\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
to

\operatorname{Add}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Ab})
is fully faithful and sends
(indecomposable) pure-injective modules to (indecomposable)
injective functors. Thus
E
is isomorphic to

\operatorname{End}(T_{X})
, which is local when
X
is indecomposable pure-injective.

Now suppose that
X
is indecomposable endofinite. Then
E
has
nilpotent radical by Nakayama’s Lemma. Assume further that
X
has no
self-extensions. Given a non-injective
f\in E
, we can
factor it via its image
I
as
f=ip
. Set
C\coloneqq\operatorname{Coker}f
and

\xi\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(C,I)
the corresponding extension. As
\Lambda

is hereditary and
X
has no self-extensions, there are surjections

\operatorname{Hom}(I,X)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(C,X)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(C,I)
. In
particular, there exists
j\colon I\to X
such that
\xi=pj\xi
. Now

jp\in E
is again non-injective, so nilpotent, and hence
\xi=0
. As
X
is indecomposable,
I=0
. An analogous argument holds if
f
is not surjective.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Every
\Sigma
-pure injective
\Lambda
-module admits an essentially unique
decomposition
X=\bigoplus_{i}X_{i}
into indecomposable modules with local
endomorphism rings.

Proof.

The argument is a straightforward application of Zorn’s Lemma. First
observe that there is an indecomposable direct summand when

X\neq 0
; see Lemma
6.7
. Now choose a maximal pure submodule of
X

which is of the form
\bigoplus_{i}X_{i}
with all
X_{i}

indecomposable; it is a direct summand by
Remark
3.4
, and it equals
X
by our first
observation.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
X
be an endofinite
\Lambda
-module. Then all direct sums of
indecomposable direct summands of
X
form a definable
subcategory. When
X
is of finite length this equals
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
where

\mathcal{X}\subseteq\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
is the additive closure of
X
.

Proof.

All modules in the definable subcategory generated by
X
satisfy
acc and dcc on subgroups of finite definition; so they decompose
into indecomposable modules by Corollary
3.6
, and
all the indecomposables are direct summands of
X
by
Remark
6.9
. When
X\in\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
let
\mathcal{X}

denote its additive closure. We have already seen that
\bar{\mathcal{X}}

contains the definable subcategory generated by
X
. On the other
hand, any definable subcategory is closed under filtered colimits,
so the one generated by
X
contains
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
.
∎

4.
Torsion regular and divisible modules

In this section we look at torsion regular and divisible

\Lambda
-modules. In particular we discuss when these modules are
pure-injective. As a consequence, we see that there is a unique
generic
\Lambda
-module up to isomorphism.

Torsion regular modules

The category
\mathcal{R}
is an abelian subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
which is
furthermore
uniserial
, so each indecomposable object admits a
unique composition series in
\mathcal{R}
. Moreover, the ext-quiver of
\mathcal{R}
is
a disjoint union of (infinitely many) oriented cycles. Writing

\mathbb{X}
for the indexing set of the cycles in the ext-quiver of

\mathcal{R}
, we have that

\mathcal{R}=\coprod_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{R}_{x}

meaning that
every module in
\mathcal{R}
is a direct sum
M=\bigoplus_{x}M_{x}
of modules

M_{x}\in\mathcal{R}_{x}
such that
M_{x}=0
for almost all
x
and

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(M_{x},M_{y})=0
for all
x\neq y
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The category of torsion regular
modules equals the completion
\bar{\mathcal{R}}
.

Proof.

It is clear that all modules in
\bar{\mathcal{R}}
are torsion
regular. Conversely, let
M
be torsion regular. As
M
is torsion
the quotient
M/\mathfrak{i}(M)
belongs to
\bar{\mathcal{R}}
. On the other hand,

\mathfrak{i}(M)=0
by Proposition
2.4
since
M
is regular.
∎

The category
\bar{\mathcal{R}}
is a Grothendieck category since
\mathcal{R}
is abelian,
and we remark that
\bar{\mathcal{R}}
is a proper subcategory
of all regular modules. In fact, as we will see, the generic module is
regular but not in
\bar{\mathcal{R}}
.

For each simple object
S\in\mathcal{R}
there is a chain of monomorphisms

S=S_{1}\rightarrowtail S_{2}\rightarrowtail S_{3}\rightarrowtail\cdots

in
\mathcal{R}
such that
S_{n}
is indecomposable and of length

n
in
\mathcal{R}
. The
Prüfer module
with regular socle
S
is by
definition

\bar{S}:=\operatorname*{colim}_{n\geq 1}S_{n}.

An application of Baer’s
criterion shows that
\bar{S}
is an injective object in
\bar{\mathcal{R}}
, so
an injective envelope of
S
, and therefore
\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(\bar{S})
is
local.

Lemma 4.2
.

Every non-zero torsion module admits an indecomposable direct
summand, which is either of finite length or a Prüfer module.

Proof.

Let
M\neq 0
be torsion. If
\mathfrak{i}(M)\neq 0
, then
\mathfrak{i}(M)
admits
a finite length indecomposable direct summand by
Proposition
2.4
, which is also a summand of
M
since

\mathfrak{i}(M)
is a pure submodule of
M
. Otherwise,
\mathfrak{i}(M)=0
and

M
lies in
\bar{\mathcal{R}}
. Then the assertion follows from the fact that

\mathcal{R}
is uniserial; see
[
19
, Theorem 13.1.28.]
.
∎

For each simple object
S\in\mathcal{R}
there exists
n\geq 1
such that

\tau^{n}S\cong S
. Then the cokernel of the inclusion

S_{n}\rightarrowtail S_{in}
is isomorphic to
S_{(i-1)n}
for all

i\geq 1
. The family of epimorphisms

\phi_{i}\colon S_{in}\twoheadrightarrow S_{(i-1)n}
induces an
epimorphism
\phi\colon\bar{S}\twoheadrightarrow\bar{S}
such that

\phi=\bigcup_{i\geq 1}\phi_{i}
and
\operatorname{Ker}\phi^{i}=S_{in}
for all

i\geq 1
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Each Prüfer module is artinian over its endomorphism ring, and
hence
\Sigma
-pure injective over
\Lambda
. Moreover, the
injective objects in
\bar{\mathcal{R}}
are precisely the coproducts of
Prüfer modules.

Proof.

Consider
\bar{S}
as a module over the noetherian local ring

k[t]_{(t)}
via the homomorphism
k[t]\to\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(\bar{S})
that
sends
t
to
\phi
. As such
\bar{S}
is an essential extension of

S_{n}=\operatorname{Ker}\phi
. The latter is finite dimensional, so
\bar{S}
embeds into
a finite direct sum of the injective envelope of
k
, which is
artinian by
[
21
, Proposition 3]
. Thus
\bar{S}
is artinian
over
\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(\bar{S})
, and therefore
\Sigma
-pure injective by
Proposition
3.3
.

The category
\bar{\mathcal{R}}
is a
locally noetherian Grothendieck category
, so
it has a generating set of noetherian objects. Therefore the
injective objects are precisely the coproducts of the indecomposable
injective objects, which are the injective envelopes of the simple
objects
[
10
, IV.2]
.
∎

The following result summarises the structure of the torsion regular
modules, which follows from the fact that
\mathcal{R}
is a uniserial
category, and its decomposition into connected components.

Proposition 4.4
.

Every torsion regular module admits a unique decomposition

M=\bigoplus_{x\in\mathbb{X}}M_{x}
where
M_{x}\in\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{x}
. Moreover,
there are pure exact sequences
0\to L_{x}\to M_{x}\to N_{x}\to 0
where

L_{x}
is a direct sum of modules in
{\mathcal{R}_{x}}
and
N_{x}
is a direct
sum of Prüfer modules in
\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{x}
.

Proof.

Given
M\in\bar{\mathcal{R}}
, we have
M=\bigoplus_{x}M_{x}
, where

M_{x}\in\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{x}
is the colimit over all submodules
U\subseteq M

with
U\in\mathcal{R}_{x}
. Now fix
x\in\mathbb{X}
and let
M\in\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{x}
. For

n\geq 0
let
\mathcal{R}_{x,n}
denote the subcategory of objects in
\mathcal{R}_{x}
of
Loewy length at most
n
; it has up to isomorphism only finitely many indecomposable
objects. Let
M_{n}
be the maximal pure submodule of
M
which is of
Loewy length at most
n
; it is a direct sum of indecomposables
in
\mathcal{R}_{x,n}
and therefore a direct summand. We obtain a chain of
split monomorphisms
M_{0}\to M_{1}\to M_{2}\to\cdots
and set

L\coloneqq\bigcup_{n\geq 0}M_{n}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}M_{n+1}/M_{n}

where
M_{n+1}/M_{n}
is a direct sum of copies of the finitely many
indecomposables in
\mathcal{R}_{x,n+1}-\mathcal{R}_{x,n}
. This yields a pure exact
sequence
0\to L\to M\to N\to 0
in
\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{x}
. Recall from
Lemma
4.2
that every non-zero module in

\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{x}
must have a direct summand which is either in
{\mathcal{R}_{x}}
or a
Prüfer module. By construction
N
has no direct summand in
{\mathcal{R}_{x}}
;
thus
N
is a direct sum of Prüfer modules. Here, one uses that
every object has a maximal injective summand since
\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{x}
is
locally noetherian.
∎

We note every torsion module is the direct sum of a preinjective
module and a torsion regular module; see
Proposition
6.1
. On the other hand, if
X
is
regular, then both
\mathfrak{t}(X)
and
\mathfrak{f}(X)
are regular, so

\mathfrak{t}(X)\in\bar{\mathcal{R}}
.

Torsionfree and divisible modules

For a
\Lambda
-module
X
and a
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
-module
Y
we have a natural isomorphisms

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(X,DY)\cong D(X\otimes_{\Lambda}Y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}}(Y,DX).

This implies:

\displaystyle X\text{ divisible}\qquad

\displaystyle\iff\qquad DX\text{ torsionfree}

\displaystyle X\text{ torsionfree}\qquad

\displaystyle\iff\qquad DX\text{ divisible}.

The first equivalence is clear. As
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(R,-)
is coherent for
all
R\in\mathcal{R}
, the module
X
is torsionfree if and only if
D^{2}X
is
torsionfree by Lemma
3.1
. Then the isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(R,D^{2}X)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}}(DX,DR)
yields the
second equivalence.

We observe that the subcategory of torsionfree divisible modules
identifies with
\mathcal{R}^{\perp}
. Also, every torsionfree divisible module is
regular, and has no non-zero finite dimensional direct summands.

The following is a key result for our discussion.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
P
be a projective
\Lambda
-module of defect
-1
and
X
a regular
\Lambda
-module.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{f}(X)
is non-zero, then
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(P,\mathfrak{f}(X))\neq 0
, and
every non-zero map
P\to\mathfrak{f}(X)
is injective with regular cokernel.

(2)

There is a submodule
P_{X}\subseteq X
such that
P_{X}
is a direct sum of copies of
P
and
X/P_{X}
is torsion regular.

Proof.

(1) The subcategory of
\Lambda
-modules
M
satisfying
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(P,M)=0
identifies with the module category of a proper factor algebra of
\Lambda
, which is therefore necessarily of finite representation type. Thus if
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(P,\mathfrak{f}(X))=0
, then
\mathfrak{f}(X)
is a direct sum of finite dimensional preprojective modules, contradicting that
X
is regular. It follows that there exists a non-zero morphism
\phi\colon P\to\mathfrak{f}(X)
.

Next
\partial(\operatorname{Im}\phi)<0
as
\mathfrak{f}(X)
is torsionfree, so
\operatorname{Ker}\phi=0
. Also,
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{f}(X)/P,\mathcal{P})=0
as
X
is regular. Finally, if we have
P\subset U\subset\mathfrak{f}(X)
with
U/P\in\mathcal{I}
, then
\partial(U)=\partial(U/P)-1\geq 0
, contradicting that
\mathfrak{f}(X)
is torsionfree. Thus
\mathfrak{f}(X)/P
is regular.

(2) Let
\mathcal{U}
denote the set of all submodules
U\subseteq X
that are direct sums of copies of
P
, and such that
X/U
is regular. Note that both direct sums of copies of
P
and regular modules are closed under taking filtered colimits. Thus every chain in
\mathcal{U}
has an upper bound, and so
\mathcal{U}
has a maximal element
P_{X}
by Zorn’s Lemma.

We claim that
X/P_{X}
is torsion. If not, then by (1) there is an
inclusion
P\rightarrowtail\mathfrak{f}(X/P_{X})
with regular cokernel
C
. We
lift this to an inclusion
P\rightarrowtail X
and obtain an exact
sequence
0\to\mathfrak{t}(X/P_{X})\to X/(P+P_{X})\to C\to 0
. Both end terms are regular. Thus
X/(P+P_{X})
is also regular, contradicting the maximality of
P_{X}
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
X,Y
be
\Lambda
-modules such that
X
is regular and
Y
is divisible. Then
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(X,Y)=0
. In particular, every divisible module in
\bar{\mathcal{R}}
is a direct sum of Prüfer modules.

Proof.

Let
P
be projective of defect
-1
. By Lemma
4.5
there
is a short exact sequence
0\to P_{X}\to X\to X^{\prime}\to 0
such that

P_{X}
is a direct sum of copies of
P
and
X^{\prime}
is torsion regular. Now

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(P_{X},Y)
vanishes as
P
is projective.

We are reduced to considering the case when
X
is torsion regular. By
Lemma
4.3

X
embeds into a direct sum of Prüfer
modules, so it is enough to prove the result when
X=\bar{S}
is itself
a Prüfer module. We have
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(\bar{S},Y)=0
, since

\bar{S}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}S_{n}
and
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(S_{n}/S_{n-1},Y)=0
for all

n\geq 1
; cf.
[
12
, Lemma 6.2]
.

Finally, we see that every divisible
Y\in\bar{\mathcal{R}}
is an injective object, so a direct
sum of Prüfer modules by Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Every divisible
\Lambda
-module is
\Sigma
-pure injective.

Proof.

Let
X
be divisible. Since the divisible modules form a definable
subcategory, there is a pure exact sequence

\xi\colon 0\to X\to Y\to Z\to 0
where
Y
is pure-injective and
all terms are divisible (take
Y=D^{2}X
). We claim that this
sequence splits. Recall from Proposition
2.4
that

\mathfrak{i}(Z)
is a direct sum of finite length modules and therefore
pure-projective. Hence the pullback along the embedding

\mathfrak{i}(Z)\rightarrowtail Z
splits. Thus
\xi
is the pullback of an
exact sequence in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(Z/\mathfrak{i}(Z),X)
, which vansihes by
Lemma
4.6
as
Z/\mathfrak{i}(Z)
is
regular. We conclude that
\xi
splits and

X
is pure-injective. Given that every direct sum of copies of
X

is divisible, it follows that
X
is
\Sigma
-pure injective.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

If
X,Y
are
\Lambda
-modules such that
\mathfrak{i}(X)=0
and
Y
is
divisible, then
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(X,Y)=0
.

Proof.

Let
\xi\colon 0\to Y\to E\to X\to 0
be an exact sequence. The
pullback along the embedding
X^{\prime}\rightarrowtail X
of a finite
length submodule splits, because in this case
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}\vee\mathcal{R}
, and
every module from
\mathcal{P}
embeds into a module from
\mathcal{R}
. It follows
that
\xi
is pure exact, and therefore
\xi
splits since
Y
is
pure-injective by Proposition
4.7
.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Every torsionfree divisible
\Lambda
-module is endofinite, and all
indecomposable torsionfree divisible
\Lambda
-modules are isomorphic.

Proof.

Let
X
be a torsionfree divisible
\Lambda
-module. We apply
Proposition
3.3
and need to show that

X
satisfies both chain conditions on
subgroups of finite definition. The module
X

is
\Sigma
-pure injective by
Proposition
4.7
. The dual
DX
is also torsionfree divisible over

\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
. Thus
X
satisfies both
chain conditions on subgroups of finite definition and is therefore
endofinite.

Let
X
and
Y
be indecomposable torsionfree divisible

\Lambda
-modules modules. Take a projective module
P
of defect
-1
.
Then Lemma
4.5
gives monos
P\rightarrowtail X
and

P\rightarrowtail Y
with regular cokernels. Their pushout yields monos

X\rightarrowtail E
and
Y\rightarrowtail E
, which split by
Lemma
4.6
. This gives maps

\phi\colon X\to Y
and
\psi\colon Y\to X
such that
\psi\phi
is
the identity on
P
and therefore an isomorphism since
the endomorphism ring of
X
is a division ring by
Lemma
3.5
. The same holds for
\phi\psi
and we
conclude that
X\cong Y
.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
X
be a torsionfree
\Lambda
-module. Then
X
is divisible if and
only if it is
\Sigma
-pure injective without any finite length
indecomposable direct summand.

Proof.

We have already seen that a torsionfree divisible module is
endofinite (Theorem
4.9
) and that it has no finite length
indecomposable direct summands.

For the converse assume that
X
is
\Sigma
-pure injective and has no
finite length indecomposable direct summand. First of all, observe
that both properties are preserved under taking the double dual. This
follows from Proposition
3.3
and
Example
3.2
(4).

We need to show that
DX
is torsionfree. If not, then
\mathfrak{t}(DX)

admits an indecomposable direct summand
U
, which is either of finite
length, or a Prüfer module; see Lemma
4.2
. In
both cases
U
is also a direct summand of
DX
since

\mathfrak{t}(DX)\subseteq DX
is a pure submodule. Thus
DU
is a direct
summand of
D^{2}X
and this leads to a contradiction. For,
U
cannot
be of finite length, since
D^{2}X
has no finite length indecomposable
direct summands, and
U
cannot be a Prüfer module, since duals of
Prüfer modules are not
\Sigma
-pure injective.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

An indecomposable endofinite
\Lambda
-module of infinite length is
torsionfree divisible, and therefore unique up to isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
X
be an indecomposable endofinite
\Lambda
-module of infinite
length. The torsion part
\mathfrak{t}(X)
is a pure submodule of
X
, so

\mathfrak{t}(X)
is endofinite and therefore a direct summand of
X
; cf. Remark
3.4
. The only indecomposable torsion modules
that are not of finite length are the Prüfer modules, which are
not endofinite. So
X
is torsionfree. By
Proposition
4.10
it is also divisible. Now the
uniqueness follows from Theorem
4.9
.
∎

5.
The generic module

A module is called
generic
provided it is indecomposable,
endofinite, and of infinite length. From Corollary
4.11
we
know that such a module over
\Lambda
must be torsionfree divisible.

Two constructions

We offer two constructions of torsionfree divisible modules.

Fix an indecomposable projective
\Lambda
-module
P
of defect
-1
.
For each simple object
S\in\mathcal{R}
and
r\geq 1
there is a minimal
universal coextension

0\to P\to E_{r}\to S_{r}^{n}\to 0,

so with

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(S_{r},S_{r}^{n})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(S_{r},P)
, where
n
equals
the dimension of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(S,P)
over
\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(S)
. These form a
filtered system, so their colimit is an exact sequence

\xi_{S}\colon 0\to P\to E\to\bar{S}^{n}\to 0.

Note that each

E_{r}
is preprojective, so
E
is torsionfree. The family of these
sequences
\xi_{S}
, where
S
runs through a representative set of
simple objects in
\mathcal{R}
, determines an exact sequence

\xi\colon 0\to P\to Q\to\bigoplus\nolimits_{S}\bar{S}^{n}\to 0.

The following is a consequence of the construction of this sequence.

Lemma 5.1
.

The
\Lambda
-module
Q
is generic.

Proof.

See
[
27
, 5.2]
. For each regular simple
S
the exact
sequence
\xi_{S}
determines an isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(S,\bar{S}^{n})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(S,P)
. The same then
holds for
\xi
. Now, as
P
is torsionfree and

\bigoplus_{S}\bar{S}^{n}
is divisible,
Q
must be torsionfree
divisible.

Let
Q=Q^{\prime}\oplus Q^{\prime\prime}
be a decomposition and
Q^{\prime}\neq 0
. Both summands are torsionfree divisible, so the composite

P\to Q\to Q^{\prime}
is a mono with regular cokernel
C
by
Lemma
4.5
. We obtain an exact sequence

0\to Q^{\prime\prime}\to\bigoplus\nolimits_{S}\bar{S}^{n}\to C\to 0
which splits
by Lemma
4.6
. As
Q^{\prime\prime}
is torsionfree
divisible, it must be zero. Hence
Q
is indecomposable.
∎

For the second construction we begin with the observation that any
pure submodule of a
\Sigma
-pure injective module is again

\Sigma
-pure injective and therefore a direct summand; cf. Remark
3.4
. In particular, this applies to products
of Prüfer modules. More precisely, there is a lattice isomorphism

\mathbf{L}(\bar{S}^{I})\cong\mathbf{L}(\bar{S})
, so
\bar{S}^{I}
is again

\Sigma
-pure injective by Lemma
4.3
and
Proposition
3.3
.

Lemma 5.2
.

The module
\mathfrak{f}(\bar{S}^{I})
is torsionfree divisible, and it is zero if and only if
I
is finite.

Proof.

For
R\in\mathcal{R}
we have

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(R,\bar{S}^{I})\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(R,\bar{S})^{I}=0
, so

\bar{S}^{I}
is divisible. It follows that
\mathfrak{f}(\bar{S}^{I})
is
torsionfree divisible. If
I
is infinite, then there is an inclusion

\bar{S}^{\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\bar{S}^{I}
, and any
element
(x_{n})
with
x_{n}\in S_{n}-S_{n-1}
lies in
\bar{S}^{I}
,
but not in its torsion part
\mathfrak{t}(\bar{S}^{I})
. Therefore

\mathfrak{f}(\bar{S}^{I})
is not zero.
∎

The category of torsionfree divisible modules

The category of torsionfree divisible modules consists of semisimple
objects, and the generic module is the unique simple object in this category.

Theorem 5.3
.

There is up to isomorphism a unique generic
\Lambda
-module
Q
. Its
endomorphism ring
\Gamma=\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda}(Q)
is a division ring and the
assignment
X\mapsto X\otimes_{\Gamma}Q
yields an equivalence

\operatorname{Mod}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{R}^{\perp}.

Proof.

From Lemma
5.1
or
Lemma
5.2
we know that there exists
an indecomposable torsionfree divisible
\Lambda
-module, which is
generic by Theorem
4.9
, and its endomorphism ring is
a division ring by Lemma
3.5
. The uniqueness
follows from Corollary
4.11
. The modules in
\mathcal{R}^{\perp}

are precisely the direct sums of copies of
Q
, again by
Theorem
4.9
, because any
\Sigma
-pure injective module
decomposes into indecomposables by
Corollary
3.6
. Thus a quasi-inverse for

-\otimes_{\Gamma}Q
is given by
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(Q,-)
.
∎

There is a more general approach to the existence of
Q
. In fact,
every infinite product of copies of a Prüfer module contains
Q
as
a direct summand. This can be deduced from a general result: if

M
is an indecomposable module which admits a surjective, locally
nilpotent endomorphism with kernel of finite length, then the
countable product
M^{\mathbb{N}}
has a direct summand which is a
generic module
[
18
,
29
]
. A construction and examples of
modules
M
with these properties are given in
[
33
]
.

A further point of view is provided by work of Cohn and Schofield

[
35
]
. There is a ring epimorphism
\lambda\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda_{\mathcal{R}}
,
called the
universal localisation
of
\Lambda
at
\mathcal{R}
, which
inverts the projective resolutions of the simple regular modules and
is universal with this property. It is easy to see that
\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda_{\mathcal{R}}

is equivalent to the category
\mathcal{R}^{\perp}
of torsionfree divisible
modules. Moreover, it is shown in
[
3
]
that
\Lambda_{\mathcal{R}}
is a simple
artinian ring whose unique simple module is isomorphic to
Q
. Note
that
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{\Lambda}(\Lambda_{\mathcal{R}},\Lambda_{\mathcal{R}})=0
, so the ring epimorphism
\lambda

is
pseudo-flat
. Since
\Lambda
is hereditary also the higher
Tor-groups vanish, that is,
\lambda
is a
homological ring
epimorphism
.

In fact, this is a special instance of a more general
phenomenon. Recall that a module is called a
brick
if its
endomorphism ring is a division ring, and it is called a
stone

if it is an endofinite brick without self extensions.

Proposition 5.4

(
[
28
]
)
.

For any finite dimensional algebra
A
, there is a
one-to-one-correspondence between isomorphism classes of endofinite
bricks over
A
and equivalence classes of ring epimorphisms from

A
to simple artinian rings, which restricts to a bijection between
stones and pseudoflat ring epimorphisms.

Here two ring epimorphisms
\lambda_{i}\colon A\to B_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are
said to be
equivalent
if there is a ring isomorphism
\mu
such
that
\lambda_{1}=\mu\lambda_{2}
.

Proof.

To every endofinite brick
M
over
A
one can associate a ring
epimorphism
\lambda\colon A\to B
by
taking
B\coloneqq\operatorname{End}_{D}(M)
where
D=\operatorname{End}_{A}(M)
, and by defining

\lambda(a)
to be left multiplication by the element
a
on

M
.

Conversely, if
\lambda\colon A\to B
is a ring epimorphism and
B

simple artinian, then for some
d\geq 1
,
B
is the full
d\times d
matrix ring over
some division ring
D
, and there
is a unique simple
B
-module
M
up to isomorphism, which is
isomorphic to
D^{d}
as a
D
-module. By regarding
M
as an

A
-module via
\lambda
we obtain an endofinite brick in
\operatorname{Mod}{A}
.

This yields the stated one-to-one-correspondence. That stones
correspond to pseudo-flat ring epimorphisms under this bijection
follows from
[
35
, Theorem 4.8]
, because
M
is a stone if and
only if the image of
\operatorname{Mod}B
in
\operatorname{Mod}A
via restriction of
scalars is closed under extensions.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

(1) For hereditary algebras there is a further
one-to-one-correspondence between stones and indivisible Schur
roots. We refer to
[
5
, 3.3]
for details.

(2) There are many large bricks over
\Lambda
. An infinite semibrick
(i.e. a collection of Hom-orthogonal bricks) consisting of infinite

[

, 6.9]
. This is used to embed the module category of a
wild hereditary algebra as a full exact abelian subcategory into

\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
. When
\Lambda
is the Kronecker algebra a more direct

[

]
. It
leads to a full exact embedding into

of the (wild)

3
-Kronecker quiver.

6.
Global results

The results in the previous sections yield some insight into the
global structure of the category of
\Lambda
-modules. In particular, we
are able to complete the classification of the pure-injective modules.
Some of the results in this section hold true more generally for
canonical algebras, as shown in
[
26
]
.

We start out with an immediate consequence of
Proposition
4.7
.

Proposition 6.1
.

For every
\Lambda
-module
M
the inclusion
\mathfrak{i}(M)\to M
splits.

Proof.

The canonical sequence
0\to\mathfrak{i}(M)\to M\to M/\mathfrak{i}(M)\to 0
is
pure exact. The preinjective module
\mathfrak{i}(M)
is clearly divisible
and therefore pure-injective. Thus the sequence is split exact.
∎

A trisection

Recall that two classes of modules
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda

form a
cotorsion pair

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
if

\mathcal{X}=\{X\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\mid\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(X,Y)=0\text{ for all
}Y\in\mathcal{Y}\}

and

\mathcal{Y}=\{Y\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\mid\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(X,Y)=0\text{ for all }X\in\mathcal{X}\}.

The pair is
complete
if for all
M\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
there exist short exact
sequences

0\to M\xrightarrow{f}Y^{M}\to X^{M}\to 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0\to Y_{M}\to X_{M}\xrightarrow{g}M\to 0

such that
X_{M},X^{M}\in\mathcal{X}
and
Y_{M},Y^{M}\in\mathcal{Y}
. In
[
7
]
it is proven that every set of
modules
\mathcal{X}_{0}

generates
a complete cotorsion pair

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
where

\mathcal{Y}=\{Y\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\mid\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(X,Y)=0\text{ for all }X\in\mathcal{X}_{0}\}.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{X}
is closed under filtered colimits, then one knows
from
[
8
]
that
f
and
g
can be chosen such that
f
is left
minimal and
g
is right
minimal. In other words,
f
is a
\mathcal{Y}
-envelope,
g
is a
\mathcal{X}
-cover,
and these approximation sequences are uniquely determined up to
isomorphism.

Let us consider the following subcategories.

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{X\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\mid\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{I},X)=0\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{X\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\mid\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(X,\mathcal{R})=0\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{X\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\mid\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{D},X)=0\}

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{D}

Then we have
\mathcal{C}=\overline{\mathcal{P}\vee\mathcal{R}}
and the objects in
\mathcal{D}
are
precisely the divisible modules. The modules in
\mathcal{E}
are called

reduced
. The notation reflects the fact that the category of
divisible modules fits into a cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
and a torsion
pair
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})
, as we now explain.

Proposition 6.2
.

The pair
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
is a complete cotorsion pair that is generated by the
class
\mathcal{R}
of finite dimensional regular modules. Moreover, there
are the following approximation sequences.

(1)

For each
M\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a short exact sequence

0\to M\xrightarrow{f^{\omega}}D^{M}\to C^{M}\to 0

such that

f^{\omega}
is a
\mathcal{D}
-envelope and
D^{M},C^{M}\in\omega
.

(2)

For each
M\in\mathcal{D}
there exists a short exact sequence

0\to D_{M}\to C_{M}\xrightarrow{g_{\omega}}M\to 0

such that

g_{\omega}

is a
\mathcal{C}
-cover and
C_{M},D_{M}\in\omega
.

Proof.

By definition
\mathcal{X}_{0}=\mathcal{R}
generates a complete cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})

with
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{D}
. We need to show that
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{C}
. The inclusion

\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
follows from the Auslander–Reiten formula,
and the reverse inclusion is Corollary
4.8
.

The existence of the minimal approximations in (1) and (2) is now a consequence of the fact that

\mathcal{C}
is closed under filtered colimits.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

The pair
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})
is a split torsion pair.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
. Thus
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})
is a
torsion pair which splits as
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
is a cotorsion pair.
∎

We obtain the following structural result for
\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Every
\Lambda
-module can be decomposed as

M=E\oplus Q^{(\alpha)}\oplus R\oplus I

where
E
is reduced,
R

a direct sum of Prüfer modules,
I
a direct sum of
indecomposable preinjective modules, and
\alpha
a cardinal.

Moreover, the category
\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
admits the following trisection:

The direction of non-zero morphisms is from left to
right and any morphism from
\mathcal{E}
to
\bar{\mathcal{I}}
factors through

\omega
.

Proof.

Using that
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})
is a split torsion pair we decompose

M=M^{\prime}\oplus E
with
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
and
E\in\mathcal{E}
. According to
Proposition
6.1
, we can further write

M^{\prime}=I\oplus W
with
I\in\bar{\mathcal{I}}
and
W\in\omega
. Next consider
the pure exact sequence
0\to\mathfrak{t}(W)\to W\to\mathfrak{f}(W)\to 0
, which
splits since divisible modules are
\Sigma
-pure injective by
Proposition
4.7
. Therefore

\mathfrak{t}(W)\in\bar{\mathcal{R}}
is divisible and
\mathfrak{f}(W)
is torsionfree
divisible. It follows from Lemma
4.6
and
Theorem
4.9
that
\mathfrak{t}(W)
is a direct sum of
Prüfer modules, and
\mathfrak{f}(W)
is a direct sum of copies of the
generic module
Q
. Thus
W
is of the required form.

For the trisection, observe that every reduced module admits a

\mathcal{D}
-envelope lying in
\omega
. This yields the stated
factorisation property.
∎

Notice that the splitting results shown above under the assumption
that
\Lambda
has tame representation type is in fact characteristic for
the tame case, as is the existence of a torsionfree generic module.

Theorem 6.5
.

The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

The algebra
\Lambda
is of tame representation type.

(2)

The torsion pair
(\bar{\mathcal{I}},\overline{\mathcal{P}\vee\mathcal{R}})
is a split torsion pair.

(3)

Every divisible module is pure-injective.

(4)

The pair
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
is a cotorsion pair.

(5)

There is a torsionfree generic
\Lambda
-module.

Proof.

The equivalence of the first three conditions is proven in
[
27
, 3.7
and 3.9]
and
[
20
, Theorem 4.6]
. (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(4)
is
[
1
, Theorem 18]
, and (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(5) is shown in

[
34
]
.∎

The classification of all pure-injectives

First of all, we need to introduce the dual counterpart of the
Prüfer modules. For every simple regular module
S\in\mathcal{R}
we denote
by
\hat{S}
the
adic module
constructed as the inverse limit
of the chain of irreducible epimorphisms ending in
S
.
Note that
\hat{S}=D\bar{T}
for
T=DS
since
D(\operatorname*{colim}_{n\geq 1}T_{n})=\lim_{n\geq 1}DT_{n}
.
This shows in particular that

\hat{S}
is torsionfree, reduced, and regular; it is indecomposable
as shown e.g. in
[
5
, p. 49]
.

We are ready to provide the complete description of
the pure-injective
\Lambda
-modules.

Theorem 6.6
.

The following is, up to isomorphism, a complete list of indecomposable
pure-injective
\Lambda
-modules.

(1)

The indecomposable modules of finite length (preprojective,
regular, and preinjective).

(2)

For each simple regular
S
the Prüfer module
\bar{S}

and the adic module
\hat{S}
.

(3)

The unique torsionfree divisible module
Q
.

Moreover, each pure-injective
\Lambda
-module
X
is a pure-injective
envelope of a direct sum
\bigoplus_{i\in I}X_{i}
of indecomposable
pure-injective modules, and the
X_{i}
are uniquely determined, up to
isomorphism, by
X
.

The proof uses a refined version of the duality
D=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(-,k)

between right and left
\Lambda
-modules that is known as
elementary
duality
. We formulate this for an arbitrary
k
-algebra
A
. A
pure-injective
A
-module
M
is called
simply reflexive
if
there is a coherent functor
F
such that
F(M)\neq 0
but
F^{\prime}(M)=0

or
(F/F^{\prime})(M)=0
for every coherent subfunctor
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
. In
this case we call
(M,F)
a
simple pair
.

Lemma 6.7
.

Every simply reflexive pure-injective module has
an indecomposable direct summand.
A non-zero pure-injective module satisfying acc or dcc on
subgroups of finite definition is simply reflexive.

Proof.

We use the functor category
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\operatorname{Add}(\operatorname{mod}A^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Ab})
. Let

M
be a pure-injective
A
-module and
F
coherent such that
(M,F)
a simple pair. Choose
a non-zero element in
F(M)
which corresponds to a morphism

\phi\colon F^{\vee}\to T_{M}
. An injective envelope

\operatorname{Im}\phi\rightarrowtail T_{N}
in
\mathcal{A}
provides an indecomposable
direct summand
N
of
M
since
F^{\vee}
is a simple object in the
localisation of
\mathcal{A}
with respect to
{}^{\perp}{T_{M}}
and any injective
envelope of a simple object is indecomposable; see
[
10
, Chap. III,
Proposition 6]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}
be the localisation
with respect to a Serre subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
. An object

\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}
if and only if
X\not\in\mathcal{C}
and for
every subobject

X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
or
X/X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}

Now suppose that

Proposition 6.8

(
[

,

Let

be a simply reflexive indecomposable pure-injective

with the following property: for each coherent functor

F

\operatorname{Mod}A^{\mathrm{op}}
we have

F(M^{\vee})=0\quad\iff\quad F^{\vee}(M)=0.

The module
M^{\vee}
is

M\cong(M^{\vee})^{\vee}
is a direct summand of
DM
.

Proof.

Choose
F
coherent such that
(M,F)
is a simple pair. Then

(DM,F^{\vee})
is a simple pair, and Lemma
6.7

yields the desired simply reflexive indecomposable summand
M^{\vee}

of

. More precisely, let
\operatorname{Coh}(A)
denote the category of

Note that

\mathcal{C}_{M^{\vee}}\supseteq\mathcal{C}_{DM}=(\mathcal{C}_{M})^{\vee}

. Now let

is

\operatorname{Coh}(A^{\mathrm{op}})/(\mathcal{C}_{M})^{\vee}

Remark 6.9
.

The proof of Lemma

(M,F)
the indecomposable direct summand

Remark 6.11

The pure-injective divisible modules are completely described by
