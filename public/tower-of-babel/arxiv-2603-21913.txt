Collision-Free Velocity Scheduling for Multi-Agent Systems on Predefined Routes via Inexact-Projection ADMM
Seungyeop Lee, Jong-Han Kim

Abstract
In structured multi-agent transportation systems, agents often must follow predefined routes, making spatial rerouting undesirable or impossible. This paper addresses route-constrained multi-agent coordination by optimizing waypoint passage times while preserving each agent’s assigned waypoint order and nominal route assignment. A differentiable surrogate trajectory model maps waypoint timings to smooth position profiles and captures first-order tracking lag, enabling pairwise safety to be encoded through distance-based penalties evaluated on a dense temporal grid spanning the mission horizon. The resulting nonlinear and nonconvex velocity-scheduling problem is solved using an inexact-projection Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) algorithm that combines structured timing updates with gradient-based collision-correction steps and avoids explicit integer sequencing variables. Numerical experiments on random-crossing, bottleneck, and graph-based network scenarios show that the proposed method computes feasible and time-efficient schedules across a range of congestion levels and yields shorter mission completion times than a representative hierarchical baseline in the tested bottleneck cases.



I
INTRODUCTION

Safe coordination is a fundamental requirement in multi-agent transportation systems such as urban air mobility (UAM), automated warehouse logistics, and corridor-based robotic operations. In many practical settings, agents are constrained to predefined routes due to infrastructure or operational requirements. Under such conditions, collision avoidance cannot rely on spatial rerouting and must instead be achieved by coordinating the timing of motion along the routes.

Existing approaches to multi-agent collision avoidance can be broadly categorized into reactive local methods and planning-based global methods. Reactive schemes, including reciprocal collision avoidance, safety-critical control, and model predictive control, provide fast responses with low computational overhead

. However, their reliance on local information often leads to conservatism or deadlocks in dense environments. Global coordination methods, such as priority-based planning, conflict-based search, sequential convex programming, and mixed-integer formulations, leverage global information to produce more consistent solutions
[
11
,
17
,
1
,
13
,
3
,
14
]
. Nevertheless, these approaches primarily resolve conflicts through spatial path modification, which is incompatible with route-constrained systems.

Temporal scheduling offers a natural alternative by adjusting agent velocities while preserving fixed paths. A representative framework determines passing orders at conflict regions via mixed-integer programming and subsequently generates dynamically feasible trajectories through convex optimization
[
20
]
. While effective for moderate problem sizes, such hierarchical methods suffer from poor scalability due to the combinatorial growth of discrete sequencing variables, and may exhibit numerical sensitivity in dense scenarios.

To alleviate these limitations, recent approaches attempt to avoid explicit sequencing. For instance,
[
16
]
formulates collision avoidance through projection onto nonconvex constraints at intersection points. Although this reduces combinatorial complexity, its event-based formulation is limited in handling shallow-angle crossings or prolonged proximity between agents.

Motivated by these challenges, this paper proposes a distance-based velocity scheduling framework for multi-agent systems on predefined routes. Waypoint passage times are treated as optimization variables, and a differentiable surrogate trajectory model maps these timings to continuous position profiles while capturing first-order tracking dynamics. Based on this representation, pairwise safety is enforced through distance-based penalties evaluated over the mission horizon. The resulting nonlinear and nonconvex problem is solved using an inexact-projection Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) framework that combines structured timing updates with gradient-based collision correction, thereby avoiding explicit integer sequencing variables.

The main contributions of this paper are threefold. First, we formulate route-constrained multi-agent coordination as a waypoint-time optimization problem with distance-based safety constraints defined over the mission horizon. Second, we develop a differentiable surrogate trajectory model and an inexact-projection ADMM solver tailored to the resulting nonconvex structure. Third, we validate the proposed approach in random-crossing, bottleneck, and graph-based network scenarios, and compare it with a representative hierarchical temporal-scheduling baseline
[
20
]
. The results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves feasible and time-efficient coordination across a range of congestion conditions without requiring explicit priority assignment.

\left\{1,2,\dots,N\right\}

II-B

Assumptions

We consider a system consisting of
K
agents.
Agent
i
is assigned a sequence of
N_{i}
waypoints

\{\mathbf{p}^{(i)}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N_{i}}
together with the corresponding waypoint passage times

\mathbf{t}^{(i)}:=[t^{(i)}_{1},\dots,t^{(i)}_{N_{i}}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{i}}
.

The agent motion is modeled using a segment-wise constant-velocity assumption.
Specifically, the agent moves along the straight-line segment connecting

\mathbf{p}^{(i)}_{n}
and
\mathbf{p}^{(i)}_{n+1}
with constant velocity.
Let the segment duration and length be defined as

\Delta t^{(i)}_{n}:=t^{(i)}_{n+1}-t^{(i)}_{n}
and

d^{(i)}_{n}:=\|\mathbf{p}^{(i)}_{n+1}-\mathbf{p}^{(i)}_{n}\|_{2}
, respectively.

The velocity along segment
n
is therefore given by

\mathbf{v}^{(i)}_{n}(\mathbf{t}^{(i)})=\frac{\mathbf{p}^{(i)}_{n+1}-\mathbf{p}^{(i)}_{n}}{\Delta t^{(i)}_{n}},\qquad n=1,\dots,N_{i}-1.

(1)

illustrates the waypoint-based path representation.
To facilitate stable gradient-based collision handling, we later introduce a smooth trajectory approximation while preserving the same timing variables.

II-C

Problem Formulation

The decision variables are the waypoint passage times

\{\mathbf{t}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{K}
.
For agents with prescribed terminal times, define

\mathcal{K}_{f}\subseteq\{1,\dots,K\}
and let

\mathcal{K}_{u}:=\{1,\dots,K\}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{f}
denote the remaining agents.
Let
\mathcal{P}:=\{(i,j)\mid 1\leq i<j\leq K\}
be the set of unordered agent pairs.

The objective is to minimize the sum of completion time of agents without fixed arrival constraints. This promotes early task completion while allowing agents with prescribed terminal times to satisfy their deadlines.

The optimization is subject to the following constraints:
(i) fixed departure times,
(ii) optional fixed arrival times,
(iii) speed bounds expressed as linear constraints on segment durations, and
(iv) continuous-time collision avoidance throughout the mission horizon.

The mission time horizon is defined as

\mathcal{T}:=\Big[\min_{i}t^{(i)}_{1},\;\max_{i}t^{(i)}_{N_{i}}\Big].

(2)

The resulting optimization problem is formulated as

\displaystyle\minimize_{\{\mathbf{t}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{K}}\;\;

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}_{u}}t^{(i)}_{N_{i}}

(3)

\displaystyle\subjectto

\displaystyle t^{(i)}_{1}=t^{(i)}_{s},\quad\forall i\in[K],

\displaystyle t^{(i)}_{N_{i}}=t^{(i)}_{f},\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{K}_{f},

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{d}^{(i)}}{v_{\max}}\leq\Delta\mathbf{t}^{(i)}\leq\frac{\mathbf{d}^{(i)}}{v_{\min}},\quad\forall i\in[K],

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbf{p}^{(i)}(\mathbf{t}^{(i)},\tau)-\mathbf{p}^{(j)}(\mathbf{t}^{(j)},\tau)\big\|_{2}\geq d_{\rm safe},

\displaystyle\forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{P},\ \forall\tau\in\mathcal{T}.

where
\mathbf{d}^{(i)}:=[d^{(i)}_{1},\dots,d^{(i)}_{N_{i}-1}]^{T}
and

\Delta\mathbf{t}^{(i)}:=[\Delta t^{(i)}_{1},\dots,\Delta t^{(i)}_{N_{i}-1}]^{T}
. The
\mathbf{p}^{(i)}(\mathbf{t}^{(i)},\tau)
denotes the position of agent
i
at time
\tau
when the agent traverses its waypoint sequence according to the waypoint passage times
\mathbf{t}^{(i)}
.

II-D

Piecewise Trajectory Approximation

The segment-wise constant-velocity model produces discontinuous switching at waypoint times, which makes the inter-agent distance function non-smooth and sensitive to tracking latency.
To obtain a differentiable mapping from waypoint times to continuous-time positions, we replace the hard segment activation with smooth sigmoid transitions.

Specifically, the velocity of agent
i
at time
\tau
is approximated as

\mathbf{\tilde{v}}^{(i)}(\mathbf{t}^{(i)},\tau)=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{i}-1}\mathbf{v}^{(i)}_{n}(\mathbf{t}^{(i)})\Big(\sigma\!\big(\beta(\tau-\hat{t}^{(i)}_{n})\big)-\sigma\!\big(\beta(\tau-\hat{t}^{(i)}_{n+1})\big)\Big),

(4)

where
\sigma(x)=1/(1+e^{-x})
,
\beta>0
controls the transition sharpness, and
\hat{t}^{(i)}_{n}:=t^{(i)}_{n}+b
introduces a time bias
b
to account for tracking delay.

Integrating (
4
) yields a closed-form position model.
Let
\zeta(x):=\log(1+e^{x})
denote the softplus function. The position of agent
i
is then given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{\tilde{p}}^{(i)}(\mathbf{t}^{(i)},\tau)=\mathbf{p}^{(i)}_{1}+\sum_{n=1}^{N_{i}-1}\frac{\mathbf{v}^{(i)}_{n}(\mathbf{t}^{(i)})}{\beta}\Big(\zeta\!\big(\beta(\tau-\hat{t}^{(i)}_{n})\big)

(5)

\displaystyle-\zeta\!\big(\beta(\tau-\hat{t}^{(i)}_{n+1})\big).\Big),

which satisfies
\mathbf{\tilde{p}}^{(i)}(\mathbf{t}^{(i)},t^{(i)}_{1})=\mathbf{p}^{(i)}_{1}
and is differentiable with respect to both
\tau
and
\mathbf{t}^{(i)}
. For sufficiently large
\beta
, the approximation closely matches the original piecewise trajectory while remaining differentiable.

In our implementation, the parameters
(\beta,b)
are selected by matching a critically damped second-order velocity-loop model with bandwidth
\omega

b\approx 1.678/\omega
and
\beta\approx 1.47\,\omega

III
INEXACT-PROJECTION ADMM SOLVER

Problem (
3
) is convex except for the collision-avoidance constraints, which introduce nonconvexity through inter-agent distance requirements.
To efficiently handle this structure, we apply the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM), which separates the timing and speed constraints from collision handling.
The former admits closed-form updates, while the latter is treated through an inexact projection step
[
9
,
19
]
.

III-A

ADMM Standard Form

To cast the problem into a standard ADMM form, we define a stacked decision vector

\mathbf{t}:=[\mathbf{t}^{(1)};\dots;\mathbf{t}^{(K)}]\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

that contains all waypoint passage times, where

N=\sum_{i=1}^{K}N_{i}
.
To decouple the constraints, auxiliary variables
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{z}
are introduced to represent segment durations and a copy of the timing variables used for collision evaluation, respectively.

The temporal dynamics are represented by a stacked forward-difference operator
\mathbf{D}
such that

\mathbf{D}\mathbf{t}=\mathbf{x}=[\Delta\mathbf{t}^{(1)};\dots;\Delta\mathbf{t}^{(K)}].

A boundary selector matrix
\mathbf{E}
enforces all fixed-time constraints, including initial departure times and prescribed arrival times, through

\mathbf{E}\mathbf{t}=\mathbf{e}=[t^{(1)}_{s},\dots,t^{(K)}_{s},\{t^{(i)}_{f}\}_{i\in\mathcal{K}_{f}}]^{T}.

The mission objective is represented by a selector vector
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

\mathbf{q}^{T}\mathbf{t}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}_{u}}t^{(i)}_{N_{i}},

where entries of
\mathbf{q}
corresponding to terminal-time indices of agents in
\mathcal{K}_{u}
are set to one and the remaining entries are zero. The physical constraints are expressed through the feasible sets for velocity limits and inter-agent safety:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathbf{x}:\mathbf{d}/v_{\max}\leq\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{d}/v_{\min}\},

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathbf{z}:\|\mathbf{\tilde{p}}^{(i)}(\mathbf{z}^{(i)},\tau)-\mathbf{\tilde{p}}^{(j)}(\mathbf{z}^{(j)},\tau)\|_{2}\geq d_{\rm safe},

\displaystyle\forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{P},\forall\tau\in\mathcal{T}.\}

Using indicator functions
I_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{x})
and
I_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{z})
, the optimization problem can be written in the following consensus ADMM form:

\displaystyle\minimize_{\mathbf{t,x,z}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}^{T}\mathbf{t}+I_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{x})+I_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{z})

(7)

\displaystyle\subjectto

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\mathbf{t}=\mathbf{e},\quad\mathbf{D}\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0},\quad\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{0}.

III-B

ADMM Updates

To solve the reformulated problem, we construct the scaled augmented Lagrangian with penalty parameter
\rho>0
and dual variables
\mathbf{u}_{E}
,
\mathbf{u}_{x}
, and
\mathbf{u}_{z}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z},\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{q}^{T}\mathbf{t}+I_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{x})+I_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{z})+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\mathbf{E}\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{e}+\mathbf{u}_{E}\|_{2}^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u}_{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{u}_{z}\|_{2}^{2}.

The ADMM iterations minimize (
8
) with respect to each primal variable sequentially.

\mathbf{t}
-update

The
\mathbf{t}
-update solves the quadratic problem

\displaystyle\mathbf{t}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{\mathbf{t}}\Big\{\mathbf{q}^{T}\mathbf{t}+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\mathbf{E}\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{e}+\mathbf{u}_{E}^{k}\|_{2}^{2}

(9)

\displaystyle+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{x}^{k}+\mathbf{u}_{x}^{k}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{z}^{k}+\mathbf{u}_{z}^{k}\|_{2}^{2}\Big\}.

Applying the first-order optimality condition yields the sparse linear system

\displaystyle(\mathbf{E}^{T}\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{D}^{T}\mathbf{D}+\mathbf{I})\mathbf{t}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}^{T}(\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{u}_{E}^{k})+\mathbf{D}^{T}(\mathbf{x}^{k}-\mathbf{u}_{x}^{k})

(10)

\displaystyle+(\mathbf{z}^{k}-\mathbf{u}_{z}^{k})-\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbf{q}.

Since the coefficient matrix is constant, it can be factorized once prior to the iterations for efficient updates.

\mathbf{x}
-update

The
\mathbf{x}
-update corresponds to the proximal operator of the indicator function
I_{\mathcal{V}}
. This reduces to a projection of the updated temporal differences onto the convex set
\mathcal{V}
:

\mathbf{x}^{k+1}=\Pi_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{t}^{k+1}+\mathbf{u}_{x}^{k}),

(11)

where
\Pi_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{x})=\min(\mathbf{d}/v_{\min},\;\max(\mathbf{d}/v_{\max},\mathbf{x}))
is applied element-wise.

\mathbf{z}
-update

Exact projection onto the nonconvex collision-free set
\mathcal{C}
is computationally intractable. Instead, we perform an inexact update using a normalized penalty function
f(\mathbf{z})
that measures safety violations over a dense temporal grid
\mathcal{T}_{\rm d}=\{\tau_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}
with uniform spacing
\delta\tau
over the sampled mission horizon:

f(\mathbf{z})=\frac{\delta\tau}{N_{p}T_{s}}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\rm d}}\max\Big(1-\|\mathbf{\tilde{p}}^{(i)}(\mathbf{z}^{(i)},\tau)\\[-4.0pt]
-\mathbf{\tilde{p}}^{(j)}(\mathbf{z}^{(j)},\tau)\|_{2}/d_{\rm safe},0\Big),

(12)

where
N_{p}
is the number of agent pairs and
T_{s}
denotes the horizon length used for penalty evaluation.

Starting from the proximal candidate

\hat{\mathbf{z}}^{k}:=\mathbf{t}^{k+1}+\mathbf{u}_{z}^{k},

the update is computed using a Polyak-type gradient step with adaptive momentum:

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{k}\mathbf{v}^{k}-\frac{f(\hat{\mathbf{z}}^{k})}{\|\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{z}}^{k})\|_{2}^{2}+\epsilon}\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{z}}^{k}),

(13)

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathbf{z}}^{k}+\mathbf{v}^{k+1},

which is computed via
N_{\rm ref}
refinement steps, and
\epsilon>0
is a small numerical small regularization constant introduced to prevent division by zero.
We use the active correction parameter:

\alpha^{k}=\begin{cases}\alpha_{\mathrm{active}},&\text{if }|f(\hat{\mathbf{z}}^{k})-f(\hat{\mathbf{z}}^{k-m_{\mathrm{stag}}})|<\varepsilon_{\Delta f},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

so that momentum is activated when the decrease in the penalty term becomes negligible, allowing the iteration to escape numerical stagnation. Here, the progress test is evaluated over a downsampled interval of
m_{\mathrm{stag}}
iterations, rather than between consecutive iterates, to reduce sensitivity to small oscillations in the penalty value
[
21
]
.

Finally, the dual variables are updated to enforce consensus across the linear constraints:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{E}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{u}_{E}^{k}+\mathbf{E}\mathbf{t}^{k+1}-\mathbf{e},

(14)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{x}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{u}_{x}^{k}+\mathbf{D}\mathbf{t}^{k+1}-\mathbf{x}^{k+1},

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{z}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{u}_{z}^{k}+\mathbf{t}^{k+1}-\mathbf{z}^{k+1}.

The algorithm is terminated when both the primal residual and the safety violation are sufficiently small,
i.e.
,

\|\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{pri}}^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pri}},\qquad f(\mathbf{z}^{k})\leq\varepsilon_{f}.

Random Crossing

IV-D

Graph-Based Network Scenario

To evaluate the proposed method in a more realistic network setting, we consider a graph-based trajectory environment representative of systems such as Urban Air Mobility (UAM) corridors or logistics networks. In these systems, agents travel through predefined nodes and edges rather than an open workspace.

The network is generated as follows:

•

Node generation:
Nodes are uniformly distributed so that the average spacing between neighboring nodes is approximately
d_{\text{node}}
.

•

Edge construction:
Edges are created using Delaunay triangulation, which is commonly used to generate efficient network structures
[
15
]
.

•

Route selection:
For each agent, a start node is randomly selected and a goal node is chosen from the opposite side of the network. The shortest path is obtained using Dijkstra’s algorithm. To reduce frequent head-on conflicts that may lead to infeasible situations, all edges are assumed to be one-way.

V
CONCLUSION

This paper presented a velocity-scheduling framework for route-constrained multi-agent coordination. By parameterizing each agent motion with waypoint passage times, the collision-avoidance problem was cast as a nonlinear optimization problem that adjusts timing along predefined routes rather than spatially rerouting the assigned waypoint sequence.

The key technical ingredient is a differentiable surrogate trajectory model that maps waypoint timings to smooth position profiles and enables distance-based safety penalties to be evaluated over the mission horizon. The resulting nonconvex problem was solved using an inexact-projection ADMM algorithm that combines structured timing updates with gradient-based collision-correction steps while avoiding explicit integer sequencing variables. Numerical results on random-crossing, bottleneck, and graph-based network scenarios showed that the proposed method computes feasible and time-efficient schedules across a range of congestion conditions. In the tested bottleneck cases, it also achieved shorter mission completion times than a representative hierarchical MIP–SOCP baseline.

Future work will focus on strengthening the theoretical characterization of the surrogate model and temporal discretization, improving robustness under higher-fidelity vehicle dynamics, and extending the framework to larger networked systems with uncertainty and online replanning.
