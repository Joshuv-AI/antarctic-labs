Decoupling for AD-regular sets on the parabola Thanks: The first author is partially supported by the NSF grant DMS-2349828
Ciprian Demeter, Yuqiu Fu

Abstract
. We improve the decoupling exponent for functions with spectrum inside AD-regular collections of arcs on the parabola. We achieve this by incorporating recent Szemerédi–Trotter-type estimates into the bootstrapping argument from [ 3 ] . As an application, our results complement, and in some cases improve earlier results [ 4 ] for arithmetic Cantor sets.



1.
Introduction

Let us start with a definition.

Definition 1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

For
R\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{I}_{R}
be a collection of pairwise disjoint intervals of length
R^{-1}
inside
[-1,1]
, and let
C_{AD}\geq 1
. We call
\mathcal{I}_{R}
an
(\alpha,R,C_{AD})
-AD regular collection if for each interval
J
of length
2/R\leq|J|\leq 4
centered at some
x\in\cup_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{R}}I
we have

(1)

C_{AD}^{-1}(|J|R)^{\alpha}\leq\#\{I\in\mathcal{I}_{R}:\;I\subset J\}\leq C_{AD}(|J|R)^{\alpha}.

When
\mathcal{I}_{R}
is the whole partition of
[-1,1]
, we may take
\alpha=1
and
C_{AD}\sim 1
. Examples for
\alpha<1
are included in Section
2
. Note that

\#(\mathcal{I}_{R})\sim_{C_{AD}}R^{\alpha}.

Consider the truncated parabola

\mathbb{P}=\{(x,x^{2}):\;x\in[-1,1]\}.

We write
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}
for its
\delta
-neighborhood. For each collection
\mathcal{I}
of intervals, we consider the corresponding subset of this neighborhood

\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\mathcal{I})=\{(x,x^{2}+t):\;x\in\cup_{I\in\mathcal{I}}I,\;|t|\leq\delta\}.

We denote by
\Theta(\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{-1/2}})
the collection of rectangle-like, essentially flat regions
\theta
with dimensions
\delta^{1/2}\times\delta
, that cover
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{-1/2}})
. We use notation

\widehat{F_{\theta}}=\widehat{F}1_{\theta}.

Theorem 1
.

Assume
\alpha\in(0,1)
. For each
6<p<\infty
there is
c_{p,\alpha}>0
such that for each
(\alpha,R^{1/2},C_{AD})
-AD regular collection
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{R^{1/2}}
and each
F:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
with spectrum inside
\mathcal{N}_{1/R}(\mathcal{I})
we have

(2)

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim_{\epsilon,C_{AD},p,\alpha}R^{\epsilon}(\#\Theta(\mathcal{I}))^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}-c_{p,\alpha}}(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal{I})}\|F_{\theta}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})})^{1/2}.

Implicit constants will typically depend on
p
and
\alpha
, but we will rarely record that. For
R
-dependent quantities
A_{R},B_{R}
we will use
A_{R}\lessapprox B_{R}
to denote the fact that the inequality

A_{R}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}B_{R}
holds true for arbitrary
\epsilon>0
. For example,

(O(\log R))^{O((\log\log R)^{O(1)})}\lessapprox 1.

The dependence on
C_{AD}
in (
2
) may be tracked to be of the form
(C_{AD})^{O(\log\log R)^{O(1)}}
. This is indeed
\lessapprox_{C_{AD}}1.

The exponent
c_{p,\alpha}
we get is easily computable, though it looks less appealing.
There is an interesting question about what the best possible value of
c_{p,\alpha}
should be. Taking
F=F_{\theta}
shows that

c_{p,\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}.

Constructive interference for
F
equal to the exponential sum with one frequency for each
I
shows that

c_{p,\alpha}\leq\frac{3(1-\alpha)}{p\alpha}.

This upper bound is applicable to arbitrary collections
\mathcal{I}
, it does not take advantage of regularity. In Section
2
we show that within the AD-regular framework, the following stronger constraint arises from energy considerations

(3)

c_{p,\alpha}\leq\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{p\alpha}.

It seems tempting to conjecture that the optimal
c_{p,\alpha}
is

(4)

c_{p,\alpha}=\min(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p},\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{p\alpha}).

This is equivalent to upgrading (
2
) to square root cancellation

(5)

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim_{\epsilon,C_{AD}}R^{\epsilon}(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal{I})}\|F_{\theta}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})})^{1/2}

for

p=2+\frac{4}{\alpha}.

This is only known to be true when
\alpha=1
. The inequality

(6)

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lessapprox(\#\Theta(\mathcal{I}))^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}}(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal{I})}\|F_{\theta}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})})^{1/2}

was proved in
[
3
]
for
p\geq 6
, for arbitrary collections
\mathcal{I}
of pairwise disjoint
R^{-1/2}
-intervals. In the absence of any regularity assumption, and apart from
R^{\epsilon}
-losses, the exponent
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}
is sharp for certain collections of (any number of) intervals. For example, this is the case whenever the centers of the intervals form an arithmetic progression, as can be seen from constructive interference, by testing (
6
) with exponential sums. Testing the same example in Theorem
1
shows that at most

{M}\lessapprox_{C_{AD}}(\frac{1}{d})^{\frac{3}{3+pc_{p,\alpha}}}

many of the intervals
I\in\mathcal{I}
can lie inside an arithmetic progression with step
d
, for each
(\alpha,R^{1/2},C_{AD})
-regular collection
\mathcal{I}
. However, it is very easy to see the bound that follows from (
1
), namely

M\lesssim C_{AD}^{2}(\frac{1}{d})^{\alpha}.

Due to (
3
) this latter bound is better than the previous one, since
\frac{3}{3+pc_{p,\alpha}}>\alpha
. Thus, not surprisingly, even a decoupling with a sharp
c_{p,\alpha}
would not have interesting consequences for the additive structure of AD-regular sets on
\mathbb{R}
. Instead, our decoupling implies certain lack of additive structure of AD-regular sets on the parabola. See Remark
2
. There are a growing number of papers addressing this phenomenon. We refer the reader to Question 2.13 in
[
3
]
, as well as
[
10
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
and the references therein.

To prove Theorem
1
we use the original bootstrapping argument in
[
3
]
, that combines
L^{2}
orthogonality with bilinear
(p,\frac{p}{2})
decoupling. The latter is a reformulation of the bilinear restriction inequality. The reason for using the half exponent
p/2
comes from the role of the
(4,2)
decoupling in the bilinear setting, and interpolation with the other endpoint
(\infty,\infty)
.

Our new critical input here is an improved bilinear
(p,\frac{p}{2})
decoupling.

Let
J_{1},J_{2}\subset[-1,1]
be intervals separated by
\sim 1
. For
i\in\{1,2\}
, let
\mathcal{I}_{i}
be
(\alpha,R^{1/2},C_{AD})
-AD regular collections of intervals inside
J_{i}
.

Theorem 2

(Improved bilinear
(p,\frac{p}{2})
decoupling)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and let
4<p<\infty
.
Assume
F_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
has spectrum inside
\mathcal{N}_{1/R}(\mathcal{I}_{i})
. There is

\Gamma_{p,\alpha}<\alpha(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{2}{p}

such that

(7)

\|(F_{1}F_{2})^{1/2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim_{\epsilon,C_{AD}}R^{\Gamma_{p,\alpha}+\epsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{2}(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal{I}_{i})}\|F_{i,\theta}\|^{2}_{L^{p/2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})})^{1/4}.

See (
86
) for an explicit relation between the exponents in Theorems
1
and
2
.

Inequality (
7
) is known to hold with exponent
\alpha(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{2}{p}
in place of
\Gamma_{p,\alpha}
for arbitrary collections
\mathcal{I}_{i}
of
M=R^{\alpha/2}
many intervals, without any regularity assumption. This follows by interpolating
p=4
with
p=\infty
, see Section
6
. One sharp example comes by considering
M
overlapping wave packets for each
i
, in consecutive directions. Constructive interference can be enforced on a
\sqrt{R}/M
-square.
The better exponent
\Gamma_{p,\alpha}
in (
7
) is a consequence of AD-regularity, as consecutive directions are now forbidden.

In particular,
\Gamma_{4,\alpha}=-\frac{1}{2}
for each
\alpha\in(0,1]
. It is an interesting question as to what the sharp exponent
\Gamma_{p,\alpha}
should be for
p>4
. The most severe lower bound we found is

(8)

\Gamma_{p,\alpha}\geq\begin{cases}-\frac{2}{p},\;p\leq\frac{4}{\alpha}\\
\frac{\alpha}{4}-\frac{3}{p},\;p>\frac{4}{\alpha}\end{cases}.

We do not exclude the possibility that more intricate constructions could improve this lower bound. (
8
) follows from constructive interference as follows. Assume each
F_{i,\theta}
is a single, non-oscillatory wave packet

F_{i,\theta}\approx 1_{T_{\theta}},

with
T_{\theta}
a
\sqrt{R}\times R
rectangle centered at the origin. Let
M\lesssim R^{1/2}
. Due to regularity, we can only have
M^{\alpha}
out of
M
consecutive directions contributing to each
F_{i}
. In the saturated case,
|F_{i}|\sim M^{\alpha}
on a
\sqrt{R}/M\times R/{M^{2}}
rectangle
R_{i}
centered at the origin. Due to transversality,
|R_{1}\cap R_{2}|\sim R/M^{2}
, and thus (
7
) implies that

M^{\alpha}(R/M^{2})^{1/p}\lesssim R^{\Gamma_{p,\alpha}}M^{\alpha/2}(R^{3/2})^{2/p}.

To get (
8
) we choose
M=1
when
p\leq 4/\alpha
and
M=\sqrt{R}
when
p>4/\alpha
.

It would be particularly interesting to know whether
\Gamma_{p,\alpha}=-\frac{2}{p}
for some
p>4
. If true, this would entail square root cancellation. Indeed, the square function version of this inequality holds true, see (
71
). It would also imply (
5
), as explained in Remark
5
.

Remark 1
.

It is not clear whether the half exponent pair
(p,p/2)
is as fundamental when
\alpha<1
, as it is in the case
\alpha=1
. Other
(p,q)
decouplings might be more relevant to consider in order to improve the bootstrapping mechanism when
\alpha<1
. See Remark
4
.

Theorem
2
is a consequence of Theorem
3
and interpolation, see Section
6
.

Theorem 3

(Improved bilinear
(8,4)
decoupling)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Assume
F_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
has spectrum inside
\mathcal{N}_{1/R}(\mathcal{I}_{i})
. There is

\Gamma_{8,\alpha}<\frac{\alpha}{8}-\frac{1}{4}

such that

(9)

\|(F_{1}F_{2})^{1/2}\|_{L^{8}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim_{\epsilon,C_{AD}}R^{\Gamma_{8,\alpha}+\epsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{2}(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal{I}_{i})}\|F_{i,\theta}\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})})^{1/4}.

The proof will reveal that we may take

\Gamma_{8,\alpha}+\frac{1}{4}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{10},&\displaystyle\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{4(3-\alpha)},&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}<\alpha\leq\frac{2}{3},\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{-3\alpha^{2}+13\alpha-2}{32(3-\alpha)},&\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}\leq\alpha<1.\end{cases}

There is nothing special about
p=8
, other than potentially being the critical exponent for
\Gamma_{p,\alpha}
when
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, assuming (
8
) is sharp. We chose to work with this concrete exponent in order to simplify the computations. We could instead have worked out our argument for generic
p
, with possibly a different outcome than the one we get by interpolation. See also Remark
3
on the sharpness of our approach.

At the heart of our argument for Theorem
3
when
\alpha\leq 1/2
lies the Szemerédi–Trotter-type bound in
[
7
]
, for rectangles (tubes) satisfying certain non-concentration assumptions. That result was proved (and is only true in that form) in the case when
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
. The sharp analogous result for
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
was proved more recently in
[
6
]
.

The inspiration for our approach to Theorem
3
comes from an argument used by Guth to reprove decoupling for the parabola. Its details are presented in Section 10.4 of
[
8
]
.
It relies on the bilinear Kakeya inequality. One of the difficult points in our argument is to find the right way to incorporate the stronger Szemerédi–Trotter-type estimate. It seems possible that a variant of this argument could be used to prove Theorem
1
directly, without using Theorem
2
.

The proof of Theorem
3
in Sections
4
and
5
takes advantage of all the scales. Strictly speaking, it does not use induction on scales, it relies merely on a direct argument based on efficiently iterating inequalities derived in Section
3
. On the other hand, induction on scales is used in Section
6
as in
[
3
]
, to prove Theorem
1
based on Theorem
2
.

2.
An application to Cantor sets

Let us now describe one of the motivations behind our Theorem
1
, following the work
[
4
]
on generalized arithmetic Cantor sets. Given
n\geq k\geq 2
and an alphabet
\mathbb{D}=\{d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{k}\}\subset\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\},
we write

(10)

C=\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\}}=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}n^{-j}:a_{j}\in\mathbb{D}\right\}.

This is a self-similar set with Hausdorff dimension

\dim(C)=\alpha=\frac{\log k}{\log n}.

For a given
C=\mathcal{C}_{n}^{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}},
and
i\geq 1
we write

C_{i}=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{j}n^{-j}:a_{j}\in\mathbb{D}\right\}

and let
\mathcal{I}_{i}
be the
collection of
k^{i}
many pairwise disjoint intervals
I
of the form

(11)

[\;\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{j}n^{-j},\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{j}n^{-j}+n^{-i}\;],\;a_{j}\in\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\}.

The collection
\mathcal{I}_{i}
is
(\alpha,n^{i},C_{AD})
-AD regular, with

C_{AD}\sim k.
Indeed, the upper bound in (
1
) is always satisfied with
C_{AD}\sim k^{1-\alpha}
, as the worst case scenario is a fully packed interval of length
k/n^{i}
. The lower bound in (
1
) is always satisfied with
C_{AD}\sim k
, as the worst case scenario is having an interval of length
\frac{1}{2}n^{-i+1}
containing only one point in
C_{i}
.

We let
D_{p}(C_{i})
denote the best constant such that

(12)

\|\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}F_{I}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq D_{p}(C_{i})(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}\|F_{I}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})})^{1/2}

holds for every
F_{I}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{supp}\hat{F}_{I}\subset I.
An interpolation argument between
L^{2}
and
L^{\infty}
shows that

(13)

D_{p}(C_{i})\lesssim k^{i(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})},\;2\leq p<\infty,

with an implicit constant independent of
k
,
i
,
n
and the alphabet. This inequality is sometimes far from being sharp. Indeed, due to self-similarity we see that (for some
A_{p}\geq 1
)

D_{p}(C_{i+j})\leq A_{p}D_{p}(C_{i})D_{p}(C_{j}),

in particular

D_{p}(C_{i})\leq(A_{p}D_{p}(C_{1}))^{i-1}.

Fix a large enough constant
B_{p}
,
p>2
.
Choose a
\Lambda(p)
alphabet
\mathbb{D}
with implicit constant at most
B_{p}
,

D_{p}(C_{1})\leq B_{p},

for
p
satisfying
n^{2/p}\sim k
. See
[
2
]
for the existence of such
\mathbb{D}
.
In this case

D_{p}(C_{i})\leq(A_{p}B_{p})^{i}.

However,
k,n
may be chosen so that
k^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}
is as large as we wish, much larger than
A_{p}B_{p}
.

There are also instances when (
13
) is essentially sharp. We present two types of examples. The first one relies on constructive interference, and becomes increasingly more efficient when
\alpha
is close to 1. We test the inequality (
12
) with

f(x)=\phi(\frac{x}{n^{i}})\sum_{f\in C_{i}}e(fx)

where
\phi
is a smooth approximation of
1_{[-1,1]}
with spectrum inside
[-1,1]
. Since

|f(x)|\gtrsim k^{i},\;\;|x|\ll 1

we find that for each
p\geq 2

D_{p}(C_{i})\gtrsim\frac{k^{i(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}}{(\frac{n}{k})^{\frac{i}{p}}}.

This lower bound is close to the upper bound (
13
) when
(n/k)^{1/p}\sim 1
.

For the second example we use an even
p
, and the fact that

D_{p}(C_{i})\gtrsim\frac{(E_{p}(C_{i}))^{1/p}}{(\#C_{i})^{1/2}},

where the
p
-energy is defined as

E_{p}(C_{i})=\#\{(f_{1},\ldots,f_{p})\in(C_{i})^{p}:\;f_{1}+\ldots+f_{p/2}=f_{p/2+1}+\ldots+f_{p}\}.

This follows by noting that if
\widehat{\phi^{p}}(0)=1
and
\widehat{\phi}\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\|\phi(\frac{x}{n^{i}})\sum_{f\in C_{i}}e(fx)\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=n^{i}\sum_{f_{1},\ldots,f_{p}\in C_{i}}\widehat{\phi^{p}}({n^{i}}(f_{1}+\ldots+f_{p/2}-f_{p/2+1}-\ldots-f_{p}))

\displaystyle\geq n^{i}E_{p}(C_{i}).

If we choose the alphabet to be an arithmetic progression of length
k
, then an easy exercise shows that, for some appropriate
K_{p}

E_{p}(C_{i})\geq(\frac{k^{p-1}}{K_{p}})^{i}.

This shows that for each
i

(14)

D_{p}(C_{i})\gtrsim(\frac{k^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}}{K_{p}^{1/p}})^{i}.

This lower bound is close to the upper bound (
13
) when, say,
\log k>K_{p}^{1/p}
.

Now consider the partition
\Omega_{i}
of the
n^{-2i}
-neighborhood
\mathcal{N}_{n^{-2i}}(\mathcal{I}_{i})

into sets
\theta_{I}
, where
\theta_{I}=\mathcal{N}_{n^{-2i}}\cap(I\times\mathbb{R})
for
I\in\mathcal{I}_{i}.
We note that each
\theta_{I}
is roughly a
n^{-i}\times n^{-2i}
rectangle, and there are
k^{i}
many
\theta_{I}.

We let
\textup{Dec}_{p}(C_{i})
denote the best constant such that

(15)

\|\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}F_{\theta_{I}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\textup{Dec}_{p}(C_{i})(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}\|F_{\theta_{I}}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})})^{1/2}

holds for every
F_{\theta_{I}}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{supp}(\hat{F}_{\theta_{I}})\subset\theta_{I}.

[
4
]
proved the following relationship between
D_{p}(C_{i})
and
\textup{Dec}_{p}(C_{i}).
We caution that
N(1)
and
\dim(C)
in
[
4
]
are denoted here by
k
and
\alpha
, respectively.

Theorem 4
.

Let
p\geq 2
. Let
C
be an arithmetic Cantor set defined as in (
10
), and let
C_{i}
,
D_{p}(C_{i}),

\textup{Dec}_{p}(C_{i})
be defined as in the previous paragraphs.
Assume that

(16)

D_{p}(C_{i})\lesssim k^{\kappa_{p}i}

for some
\kappa_{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
and each
i
. Then

(17)

\textup{Dec}_{3p}(C_{i})\lesssim_{\epsilon,k,\alpha}k^{(\kappa_{p}+\epsilon)i}

for each
i
and
\epsilon>0
.

We note that
\lesssim_{\epsilon,k,\alpha}
is the same as
\lesssim_{\epsilon,k,n}
.

At its core, the proof
[
3
]
of the decoupling for the parabola (
\alpha=1
) relies on a bootstrapping argument that combines bilinear Kakeya with
L^{2}
orthogonality. The derivation of (
17
) can be seen from the same argument, with (
16
) used in place of
L^{2}
orthogonality. See Remark
6
.

Inequality (
17
) becomes powerful when (
16
) is known to hold
with
\kappa_{p}<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
, as the latter represents an improvement over
L^{2}
orthogonality.

Theorem
4
gives (via (
13
)) the universal estimate

(18)

\textup{Dec}_{p}(C_{i})\lesssim_{\epsilon,k,\alpha}(\#C_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}+\epsilon},\;p\geq 6

for arbitrary
C_{i}
. However, in reality this bound does not depend on self-similarity or AD-regularity. It simply follows by interpolating the
L^{6}
decoupling for the parabola

[
3
]
with the trivial
L^{\infty}
bound, that only relies on the cardinality of
C_{i}
.

We note that when
\log k>K_{p}^{1/p}
, the lower bound (
14
)
shows that (
16
)
does not hold for any
\kappa_{p}<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}-\log_{k}(K_{p}^{1/p})
, when
C_{i}
as in the second example from above. When applied to these sets, Theorem
4
delivers an upper bound no better than

\displaystyle\textup{Dec}_{3p}(C_{i})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\epsilon,k,\alpha}k^{(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}-\log_{k}(K_{p}^{1/p})+\epsilon)i}

\displaystyle\sim_{\epsilon,k,\alpha,p}k^{(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon)i},\;p\geq 2.

This is the same as the generic upper bound (
18
).

As a consequence of our Theorem
1
, we improve on (
18
) in all cases. We also improve on (
17
) whenever

\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}-c_{p,\alpha}<\kappa_{p/3}.

Theorem 5
.

Let
C
be an arithmetic Cantor set defined as in (
10
). For each
6<p<\infty
, let
c_{p,\alpha}>0
be the exponent in Theorem
1
. Then for each
i\geq 1

(19)

\textup{Dec}_{p}(C_{i})\lesssim_{\epsilon,k,\alpha}k^{i(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}-c_{p,\alpha}+\epsilon)}.

Proof.

Write
R=n^{2i}
. The collection
\mathcal{I}_{i}
in (
11
) is
(\alpha,R^{1/2},C_{AD})
-AD regular, with

C_{AD}\sim k.

We apply Theorem
1
to find

\textup{Dec}_{p}(C_{i})\lesssim_{\epsilon,k,\alpha}R^{\epsilon}k^{i(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}-c_{p,\alpha})}.

Since
R^{\epsilon}=k^{i2\epsilon/\alpha}
, we are done.

Remark 2
.

Consider the lattice points on the parabola given by

S_{i}=\{(\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}a_{j}n^{j},(\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}a_{j}n^{j})^{2}):\;a_{j}\in\mathbb{D}\}.

An application of Cauchy–Schwarz shows that for each even
p\geq 2

E_{p}(S_{i})\gtrsim\frac{(\#S_{i})^{p}}{\#(S_{i}+\ldots+S_{i})},

where the sum in the denominator is
p/2
-fold.

Assume now the alphabet
\mathbb{D}=\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\}
is an arithmetic progression, so
C_{i}
is a generalized arithmetic progression of dimension
i
. Since

S_{i}+\ldots+S_{i}\subset\{\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}b_{j}n^{j}:\;b_{j}\in\mathbb{D}+\ldots+\mathbb{D}\}\times\{0,1,\ldots,pn^{2i}/2\},

and since

(20)

\#(\mathbb{D}+\ldots+\mathbb{D})\sim_{p}\#\mathbb{D},

we find that

\#(S_{i}+\ldots+S_{i})\lesssim_{p}(O_{p}(1)k)^{i}n^{2i},

and thus

E_{p}(S_{i})\gtrsim_{p}\frac{k^{i(p-1)}}{(O_{p}(1)n)^{2i}}.

The standard argument of testing (
15
) with exponential sums
shows that for each even
p\geq 2

(21)

E_{p}(S_{i})\lesssim\textup{Dec}_{p}(C_{i})^{p}(\#S_{i})^{p/2}.

These together force the lower bound

\textup{Dec}_{p}(C_{i})\gtrsim\left(\frac{k^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}}{O_{p}(1)n^{2/p}}\right)^{i}=\left(\frac{k^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{p\alpha}}}{O_{p}(1)}\right)^{i}.

Comparing this with (
19
) when
p\geq 6
, we find that

c_{p,\alpha}\leq\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{p\alpha}.

Theorem
1
together with (
21
) shows that for each even
p\geq 8

E_{p}(S_{i})\lesssim_{\epsilon,k}n^{i\epsilon}(\#S_{i})^{p-3-pc_{p,\alpha}}.

If (
4
) is indeed true, this would imply diagonal behavior

E_{p}(S_{i})\lesssim_{\epsilon,k}n^{i\epsilon}(\#S_{i})^{p/2}

for the critical exponent
p=2+\frac{4}{\alpha}
. This in turn implies that if the
p/2
-fold sumset
S_{i}+\ldots+S_{i}
has minimal expansion (
20
) in the
x
-direction, it should have essentially maximal expansion in the
y
-direction, filling in a significant fraction of
\{0,1,\ldots,pn^{2i}/2\}
.

Remark 3

(Limit of the method)
.

The exponent

For

a_{\Delta,i}^{p}
we invoke first Minkowski’s inequality then Proposition
3
to get

(79)

\sum_{\Delta}\|F_{i}\|_{p,R^{\frac{1}{2}},\Delta}^{p}\lesssim\|F_{i}\|_{p,R^{\frac{1}{2}},B_{R}}^{p}\lesssim\textup{Dec}_{p,\alpha}(R^{1/2})^{p}\|F_{i}\|_{p,R,B_{R}}^{p}.

We next show how to sum the factors
b_{\Delta}^{p}
.
Note that by
L^{2}
orthogonality followed by Hölder’s inequality, for each
\Delta

(80)

\|F_{i}\|_{2,R^{\frac{1}{2}},\Delta}\lesssim\|F_{i}\|_{2,R,\Delta}\lesssim R^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\|F_{i}\|_{p,R,\Delta}.

Due to the uncertainty principle, each

is essentially constant on each
\Delta
. Thus, in particular we can write

\sum_{\Delta\subset B_{R}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|F_{i}\|_{p,R,\Delta}^{\frac{p}{2}}\lesssim\sum_{\Delta\subset B_{R}}\|\prod_{i=1}^{2}(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{i,0}}|F_{i,\theta}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|_{L^{p}({\Delta})}^{p}

(81)

\lesssim\|\prod_{i=1}^{2}(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{i,0}}|F_{i,\theta}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|_{L^{p}({B_{R}})}^{p}.

Now (
80
), (
81
) and (

(82)

\sum_{\Delta}b_{\Delta}^{p}\lesssim R^{-2}R^{\frac{p}{2}-1}(\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|F_{i}\|_{\frac{p}{2},R,B_{R}})^{1/2}.

To end the argument simply invoke the estimates (
77
), (
78
), (
79
) and (

).

∎

Write

A_{\beta}(R)=R^{\psi(\beta)},

and

\textup{Dec}_{p,\alpha}(R)=R^{\gamma_{p,\alpha}}.

Proposition 6
.

Assume
p>6
. Then

(83)

\gamma_{p,\alpha}\leq\frac{\psi(1)(1-2\xi)+2\eta}{1-\xi}.

Proof.

Proposition

\psi(\xi^{s+1})\leq\frac{1}{2}\psi(\xi^{s})+\frac{\gamma_{p,\alpha}}{2}(1-\xi^{s+1})+\eta\xi^{s}.

Iterating (

\psi(\xi^{s})\leq\frac{1}{2^{s}}\psi(1)+\gamma_{p,\alpha}(1-2^{-s})+2(\frac{\eta}{\xi}-\frac{\gamma_{p,\alpha}}{2})\frac{2^{-s}-\xi^{s}}{\xi^{-1}-2}.

When combined with (

) for
\beta=\xi^{s}
(

) implies

\textup{BilDec}_{p,\alpha}(R)\lesssim R^{\frac{2\xi^{s}}{p}}R^{\frac{1}{2^{s}}\psi(1)+\gamma_{p,\alpha}(1-2^{-s})+2(\frac{\eta}{\xi}-\frac{\gamma_{p,\alpha}}{2})\frac{2^{-s}-\xi^{s}}{\xi^{-1}-2}}.

Using Proposition
4
, this further leads to

\gamma_{p,\alpha}\leq\frac{2\xi^{s}}{p}+\frac{1}{2^{s}}\psi(1)+\gamma_{p,\alpha}(1-2^{-s})+2(\frac{\eta}{\xi}-\frac{\gamma_{p,\alpha}}{2})\frac{2^{-s}-\xi^{s}}{\xi^{-1}-2},

\gamma_{p,\alpha}\frac{1-\xi-\xi(2\xi)^{s}}{1-2\xi}\leq\frac{2(2\xi)^{s}}{p}+\psi(1)+\frac{2\eta}{1-2\xi}(1-(2\xi)^{s}).

Recalling (

∎

Remark 4

The bootstrapping mechanism we used relies critically on the fact that

R\mapsto R^{1/2}
. The value of
\xi
in (

and 2 in connection with the exponent

orthogonality is enforced, as in

was explained in Remark
