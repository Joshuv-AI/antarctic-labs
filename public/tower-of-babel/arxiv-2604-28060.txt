Turán-Type Extremal Results for Distance- k k Graphs
Zhen He, Nika Salia, Casey Tompkins, Xiutao Zhu

Abstract
We study Turán-type extremal problems for distance graphs, motivated by work of Csikvári, Bollobás, Tyomkyn, and Uzzell. We determine the maximum number of vertex pairs at distance three in an n n -vertex graph with no triangle formed by these pairs, resolving the first case of a conjecture of Tyomkyn and Uzzell. We also determine the maximum number of vertex pairs at distance two in an n n -vertex graph with no triangle formed by these pairs and give a complete characterization of the extremal graphs, settling another problem of Tyomkyn and Uzzell.

Introduction

Turán’s theorem determines the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex graph containing no clique of a fixed size. In this paper we study the corresponding problem in which edges are replaced by pairs of vertices at a prescribed distance. Given a graph
G
and an integer
k\geq 1
, the
k
-distance graph
G_{k}
is the graph on
V(G)
in which two vertices are adjacent if and only if they are at distance exactly
k
in
G
. Thus
|E(G_{k})|
is the number of unordered pairs of vertices at distance
k
in
G
.

This problem is related to earlier work on walks and paths in trees. Csikvári
[
2
]
proved that among
n
-vertex trees the star and the path maximize and minimize, respectively, the number of closed walks of length
k
. Bollobás and Tyomkyn
[
1
]
later gave another proof and extended the result to all walks of length
k
. They also considered the problem of maximizing the number of paths of length
k
in an
n
-vertex tree, showing that the extremal examples belong to a family of trees they called
t
-brooms. Since in a tree every path of length
k
is determined by its endpoints, this is equivalent to maximizing the number of pairs of vertices at distance
k
.

Tyomkyn and Uzzell
[
6
]
initiated the corresponding problem for general graphs. More generally, for a fixed graph
H
, define

\operatorname{ex}_{k}(n,H):=\max\{|E(G_{k})|:\ |V(G)|=n\text{ and }G_{k}\text{ is }H\text{-free}\}.

The graphs
G
for which
|E(G_{k})|=\operatorname{ex}_{k}(n,H)
are referred to as extremal graphs.
They proved that when
k=2
, the star is an extremal graph. For
k\geq 3
and
n
sufficiently large, they conjectured that the maximum is attained by a
t
-broom for an appropriate choice of
t
. For even
k\geq 4
, a
t
-broom consists of a central vertex joined to one endpoint of each of
t
paths on
(k-2)/2
vertices, with an arbitrary number of leaves attached to the other endpoint of each path. For odd
k\geq 3
, a
t
-broom consists of a clique of size
t
, where each clique vertex is joined to one endpoint of a path on
(k-3)/2
vertices, again with leaves attached to the other endpoint of each path.

We consider here the problem of maximizing
|E(G_{k})|
under a bound on the clique number of the distance graph
G_{k}
. A particularly natural case is the triangle-free distance-two problem. Tyomkyn and Uzzell
[
6
]
constructed an
n
-vertex graph
G
such that
G_{2}
is triangle-free and

|E(G_{2})|=\left\lfloor\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+1,

and asked whether this value is the best possible. We positively answer this question and determine all extremal graphs.

Theorem 1
.

For all
n\geq 5
,

{\rm ex}_{2}(n,K_{3})=\left\lfloor{\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}}\right\rfloor+1.

The extremal graphs are the complements of graphs obtained from a Turán graph
T(n-1,2)
with bipartition
(A,B)

by fixing a vertex
a_{1}\in A
and a non-trivial partition
B=B^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime\prime}
,
removing the edges between
a_{1}
and
B^{\prime}
, and introducing a new vertex
a_{2}

with the neighbourhood
\{a_{1}\}\cup B^{\prime}
.

Going beyond distance
2
-graphs, Tyomkyn and Uzzell
[
6
]
considered the problem of maximizing
|E(G_{k})|
when
G_{k}
contains no clique of size
t+1
.

Conjecture 2

(Tyomkyn–Uzzell
[
6
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
t\geq 2
. There is a function
h(k,t)
such that if
n\geq h(k,t)
, then the maximum of
|E(G_{k})|
over
n
-vertex graphs
G
with
\omega(G_{k})\leq t
is attained when
G_{k}
is isomorphic to the
k
-distance graph of a
p
-broom.

As a step in proving this conjecture, they gave the following sharp result in the case of
t=2
for
k
and
n
sufficiently large.

Theorem 3

(Tyomkyn–Uzzell
[
6
]
)
.

For
k
sufficiently large and
n
sufficiently large as a function of
k
, every
n
-vertex graph
G
such that
G_{k}
is triangle-free satisfies

|E(G_{k})|\leq\frac{(n-k+1)^{2}}{4}.

Moreover, if equality holds, then
G_{k}
is isomorphic to
D_{k}
, where
D
is a double broom.

They made the following conjecture, implying that the conclusion of Theorem
3
holds for
k=3
and
n\geq 9
.

Conjecture 4

(Tyomkyn–Uzzell
[
6
]
)
.

For
k\geq 3
and
n\geq k+1
except when
k=3
and
n=8
, every
n
-vertex graph
G
such that
G_{k}
is triangle-free satisfies

|E(G_{k})|\leq\frac{(n-k+1)^{2}}{4}.

Moreover, if equality holds, then
G_{k}
is isomorphic to
D_{k}
, where
D
is a balanced double broom.

Li, Ma, Shi and Yue
[
5
]
showed that Conjecture
4
for
k=3
holds if one includes the additional assumption that the graph contains a vertex whose neighborhood is covered by at most two cliques.

We prove that the upper bound in Conjecture
4
holds for
k=3
without additional assumptions. We further exhibit a broader class of extremal graphs, showing that the proposed characterization in Conjecture
4
is incomplete. In particular, we construct a graph
H
whose
3
-distance graph attains the bound, but for which
H_{3}
is not isomorphic to
D_{3}
for all
n\geq 9
.

Theorem 5
.

For
n\geq 18
,

{\rm ex}_{3}(n,K_{3})=\left\lfloor{\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}}\right\rfloor.

The proofs of Theorems
1
and
5
are given in Section
2
.

Proofs of the main results

We will make use of the following result about the maximum size of a triangle-free non-bipartite graph, which appeared in the paper of Erdős
[
3
]
as a lemma, where it was attributed to Erdős and Gallai, and independently to Andrásfai. In fact, we will make use of a stronger version appearing in a paper by Kang and Pikhurko
[
4
]
, which also characterizes the equality cases.

Theorem 6

(Erdős–Gallai, Andrásfai
[
3
]
, Kang–Pikhurko
[
4
]
)
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex triangle-free graph that is not bipartite with
n\geq 5
. Then

|E(G)|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+1.

Equality holds if and only if
G
is constructed from a Turán graph
T(n-1,2)
with bipartition
(A,B)

by fixing a vertex
a_{1}\in A
and a non-trivial partition
B=B^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime\prime}
,
removing the edges between
a_{1}
and
B^{\prime}
, and introducing a new vertex
a_{2}

with the neighbourhood
\{a_{1}\}\cup B^{\prime}
.

We now give the proofs of Theorems
1
and
5
.

2.1
Triangle-Free Distance-Two Graphs

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph such that
G_{2}
is
K_{3}
-free and

|E(G_{2})|\geq\left\lfloor{\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}}\right\rfloor+1.

Let
v\in V(G)
be a vertex of maximum degree in
G_{2}
, and denote
d_{G_{2}}(v)=\Delta
.
Since
|E(G_{2})|\leq\frac{n\Delta}{2}
, we have

\Delta\geq\frac{n-1}{2}.

Set
N=N_{G_{2}}(v)
, and note that
G_{2}
is triangle-free, so the induced subgraph
G_{2}[N]
is independent.
We distinguish two cases:
G_{2}
is bipartite, and
G_{2}
is non-bipartite.

Case 1.
G_{2}
is bipartite with bipartition
(A,B)
.

The set
N
lies entirely in one bipartition class of
G_{2}
, say
A
.
If
|A|>|N|=\Delta
, then
|B|\leq n-\Delta-1
and

|E(G_{2})|\leq\Delta|B|\leq\Delta(n-\Delta-1)\leq\Big\lfloor\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\Big\rfloor,

a contradiction.

If
A=N
, then every vertex
u\in N
is at distance two from
v
in
G
.
Therefore, for each such
u
there exists a vertex

x_{u}\in V(G)\setminus(\{v\}\cup N)
with
ux_{u}\in E(G)
.
In particular,
u
and
x_{u}
are not adjacent in
G_{2}
.
It follows that
d_{G_{2}}(u)\leq n-1-\Delta.

Summing over
A
, we obtain

|E(G_{2})|\leq|A|(n-1-\Delta)\leq\Big(\frac{n-1}{2}\Big)^{2},

a contradiction.

Case 2.
G_{2}
is not bipartite.

By Theorem
6
,

|E(G_{2})|\leq\Big\lfloor\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\Big\rfloor+1,

which yields the desired bound.
Moreover, by Theorem
6
, we have a well-defined structure of
G_{2}
.
The graph
G_{2}
has the following structure.
Start with the Turán graph
T(n-1,2)
with bipartition
(A,B)
.
Fix a vertex
a_{1}\in A
and choose a non-trivial partition

B=B^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime\prime}
.
Remove all edges between
a_{1}
and
B^{\prime}
, and add a new vertex
a_{2}

whose neighbourhood is exactly
\{a_{1}\}\cup B^{\prime}
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the complement of
G_{2}
.
The resulting graph
G^{\prime}
has diameter
2
, and therefore its distance-two graph is precisely
G_{2}
.
It remains to show that no proper subgraph of
G^{\prime}
has the same
distance-two graph.

Suppose that
G^{\prime\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
satisfies
(G^{\prime\prime})_{2}=G_{2}
.
Since
G_{2}
contains all edges between
A\setminus\{a_{1}\}
and

B^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime\prime}
, it follows that in
G^{\prime\prime}
every vertex of

A\setminus\{a_{1}\}
must be adjacent to both
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
,
and every vertex of
B^{\prime}
(respectively
B^{\prime\prime}
) must be adjacent to

a_{1}
(respectively
a_{2}
).
Furthermore, since the subgraphs of
G_{2}
induced on

A
,
B^{\prime}
, and
B^{\prime\prime}
are empty, the corresponding induced subgraphs in
G^{\prime\prime}
must be complete.
Thus all edges inside
A
, inside
B^{\prime}
, and inside
B^{\prime\prime}

are present in
G^{\prime\prime}
.

Finally, because
a_{1}
and the vertices of
B^{\prime\prime}
are adjacent in
G_{2}
,
there must exist at least one edge between
B^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime\prime}
in
G^{\prime\prime}
.
However, since
G_{2}
contains no edges inside
B^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime\prime}
,
this forces the induced subgraph on
B^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime\prime}
in
G^{\prime\prime}

to be complete.
Consequently, all edges of
G^{\prime}
are present in
G^{\prime\prime}
,
and hence
G^{\prime\prime}=G^{\prime}
. This completes the proof.
∎

2.2
Triangle-Free Distance-Three Graphs

Proof of Theorem
5
.

For the lower bound, consider the following construction. Take a star with
\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor
or
\left\lfloor{(n+1)/2}\right\rfloor

\left\lfloor{\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4}}\right\rfloor

, but are not all isomorphic.
Indeed, consider two extremal constructions in the case when

In the first case, the
3
-distance graph is a complete balanced bipartite graph on

In the second case, it is obtained from a complete balanced bipartite graph on

{\rm ex}_{3}(n,K_{3})\geq\left\lfloor{\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4}}\right\rfloor

First, assume there is a vertex

Let

.

(1)

.
