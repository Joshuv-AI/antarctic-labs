Introduction

Bilinear systems arise naturally in the modeling of many physical and engineering
processes, in which the control acts multiplicatively on the state.

This course is devoted to the analysis and design of feedback laws for the
stabilization of bilinear control systems evolving in infinite-dimensional
spaces. The first part of the course focuses on weak and strong stabilization
of bilinear systems in a Hilbert space setting. In this first part, the Hilbertian framework is
adopted for pedagogical reasons, as it allows the main ideas underlying
stabilization strategies to be presented in a clear and intuitive manner.
The existence of weakly stabilizing feedback laws is investigated under an
approximate observability assumption. The course subsequently addresses strong
and polynomial stabilization. Sufficient conditions ensuring strong stability
are presented, based on appropriate observability estimates, together with
feedback strategies leading to polynomial decay rates of the system
trajectories.

A central part of the course is devoted to the exponential stabilization of
bilinear systems, which will be considered in the context of Banach spaces. Particular attention is paid to the
additional difficulties arising in this framework due to the lack of a
Hilbertian structure, which implies that the differentiability of the state
norm is no longer guaranteed ; an essential property for the derivation of
energy estimates.

Finally, the course introduces the concept of finite-time stabilization of bilinear systems. This part highlights recent results and open problems related to finite-time convergence and situates this type of stabilization within the broader framework of nonlinear and infinite-dimensional control theory.

Part I

This first part of the course focuses on weak and strong stabilization

Necessary Conditions for Stability

Consider the initial value problem

\frac{d}{dt}z(t)=Az(t)+F(t,z(t)),\ z(0)=z_{0},

(1)

where

\star

H
is a Hilbert space,

\star

A
generates a
C_{0}-
semigroup
S(t)
on
H
,

\star

F:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times H\rightarrow H
is a given function such that
F(t,0)=0,\;\forall t\geq 0
(so that
0
is an equilibrium point for (
1
)).

\bullet
Recall that (
1
) is weakly (resp. strongly) stable at the origin if there is a unique solution
z
such that

\star

0
is lyapunov stable (i.e., every trajectory starting inside the
\alpha-
ball remains inside
the
\varepsilon-
ball for all future time),

\star
the solution tends to
0
, as
t\to+\infty
, weakly (resp. strongly).

The purpose of this part is to establish the necessary conditions for weak and strong stabilization.

Define the following sets :

\mathcal{F}=\{y\in H/F(t,S(t)y)=0,\forall t\geq 0\},

I_{w}=\{y\in H/S(t)y\rightharpoonup 0,\;\mbox{as}\;t\to+\infty\}

I_{s}=\{y\in H/S(t)y\rightarrow 0,\;\mbox{as}\;t\to+\infty\}.

Notice that

\star

The semigroup
S(t)
is weakly (resp. strongly) stable iff
I_{w}=H
(resp.
I_{s}=H
).

\star

If the initial state belongs to
\mathcal{F}
, then (in case of uniqueness) the solution of (
1
) coincides with that of its linear part.

\star

For the class of controlled systems considered next, the set
\mathcal{F}
will be the set of unobservable initial states.

The following result provides necessary conditions for the stability of system (
1
).

Proposition 2.1
.

A necessary condition for system (
1
) to be weakly (resp. strongly) stable is that
\mathcal{F}\subset I_{w}
(resp.
\mathcal{F}\subset I_{s}
).

Proof.

Assume that system (
1
) is weakly stable, and let
y\in H
be such that

F(t,S(t)y)=0
for all
t\geq 0
. Then
z(t)=S(t)y
solves the system (
1
) with initial state
z(0)=y
. Hence,

S(t)y=z(t)\rightharpoonup 0\quad\text{as }t\to+\infty.

The case of strong stability follows from similar arguments.

3.1

Necessary condition for weak stabilization

The conventional feedback control for the weak stabilization of bilinear systems is typically a quadratic function of the state :

v=-\langle z(t),Bz(t)\rangle.

This type of control was considered by Ball and Slemrod (1979) to establish weak stabilization of bilinear/affine-control systems in the context of a Hilbert space.

We will start with the following control law :

v:=v_{0}=-\langle z(t),Bz(t)\rangle,

which makes the energy
E(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|z(t)\|^{2}

decreasing.

In the sequel, we apply Proposition
2.1
to system (
BLS
) closed with
v_{0}
. To this end, let us introduce the following set :

\mathcal{B}=\{\,y\in H\mid BS(t)y=0,\;\forall t\geq 0\,\},

and, for a feedback
v(t)=V(z(t))
with
V:H\to H
, we set

\mathcal{V}=\{\,y\in H\mid V(S(t)y)=0,\;\forall t\geq 0\,\}.

The following result provides necessary conditions for weak stabilization.

Corollary 3.1
.

(i) If the system (
BLS
) is weakly stabilizable, then we have
\mathcal{B}\subset I_{w}
.

(ii) A necessary condition for a feedback
v(t)=V(z(t))
to be a weakly stabilizing control for the system (
BLS
) is
\mathcal{V}\subset I_{w}
.

Proof.

Let
v=V(z)
be a stabilizing feedback, and let us take
F(t,y)=V(y)By
for all
t\geq 0
,
y\in H
.

Then, in both cases (i)-(ii), the result follows from Proposition
2.1
and the fact that
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{F}
.

As an application to the control
v_{0}
, we have the following
corollary :

Corollary 3.2
.

A necessary condition for the control
v_{0}
to be a
weakly stabilizing one for
the system (
BLS
) is
G\subset I_{w},
where

G=\{y\in H/\langle BS(t)y,S(t)y\rangle=0,\forall t\geq 0\}.

In other words, for all
y\in H
, we have the implication :

\langle BS(t)y,S(t)y\rangle=0,\forall t\geq 0\Rightarrow S(t)y\rightharpoonup 0,\;\mbox{as}\;t\to+\infty.

3.2

Sufficient conditions for weak stabilization

Based on the properties of the orbits and the
\omega-
limit sets, a weak stabilization result was proved in [Ball and Slemrod, 1979], using the conventional control
v_{0}
under the assumption

\langle BS(t)y,S(t)y\rangle=0,\;\forall t\geq 0\Longrightarrow y=0\cdot

(2)

The proof relies on properties of the orbits and the
\omega-
limit sets. Here, we give a direct proof.

The result concerning weak stability of (
BLS
) is as
follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

(Ball and Slemrod, 1979).

Suppose that

(i)
A
generates a semigroup of contractions
S(t)
,

(ii)
B\in\mathcal{L}(H)
is a compact operator such that (
2
) holds.

Then the control
v_{0}
weakly stabilizes (
BLS
).

Proof.

Since the closed-loop operator

F_{0}:y\mapsto\langle y,By\rangle By

is locally Lipschitz, there exists a unique local mild solution, which is a classical one for
y_{0}\in D(A)
. In this case we have, by dissipativity of
A
:

\frac{d}{dt}\|y(t)\|^{2}\leq-2|\langle y,By\rangle|^{2},

from which, we can deduce by density that for all
y_{0}\in H,

\int_{0}^{t}|\langle y(s),By(s)\rangle|^{2}ds\leq\frac{\|z_{0}\|}{2}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\|z(t)\|\leq\|z_{0}\|.

In particular, the solution is global.

Step 1
: Let us prove the following estimate :

\big(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\langle BS(s)z(t),S(s)z(t)\rangle|ds\big)^{2}=O\big(\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+T}\displaystyle|\langle z(s),Bz(s)\rangle|^{2}ds\big),\;t\to+\infty\cdot

The mild solution is given by the variation of constants formula (VCF)

z(t)=S(t)z_{0}-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\displaystyle\langle z(s),Bz(s)\rangle S(t-s)Bz(s)ds\cdot

Then using that
\|z(t)\|\leq\|z_{0}\|,\;\forall t\geq 0,
we get, for any given
T>0

\|z(t)-S(t)z_{0}\|\leq\|B\|\|z_{0}\|\bigg(T\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle|\langle z(s),Bz(s)\rangle|^{2}ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},\ \forall t\in[0,T].

Using the fact that
S(t)
is a semigroup of contractions and that
\|z(t)\|\leq\|z_{0}\|,\;\forall t\geq 0,
we
obtain the inequality

|\langle BS(s)z_{0},S(s)z_{0}\rangle|\leq 2\|B\|\|z(s)-S(s)z_{0}\|\|z_{0}\|+|\langle Bz(s),z(s)\rangle|.

Then, it follows from the two above inequalities that

|\langle BS(s)z_{0},S(s)z_{0}\rangle|\leq 2\|B\|^{2}\|z_{0}\|^{2}\bigg(T\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle|\langle z(s),Bz(s)\rangle|^{2}ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+|\langle Bz(s),z(s)\rangle|.

Replacing
z_{0}
by
z(t)
and using the superposition property of the solution, we get

|\langle BS(s)z(t),S(s)z(t)\rangle|\leq 2\|B\|^{2}\|z(t)\|^{2}\big(T\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle|\langle z(t+\tau),Bz(t+\tau)\rangle|^{2}d\tau\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

+|\langle Bz(s+t),z(s+t)\rangle|,\ \forall t,s\geq 0.

Integrating this last inequality over the interval
[0,T]
and using
the semigroup property of the solution
z(t)
, it comes

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\langle BS(s)z(t),S(s)z(t)\rangle|ds\leq

(2T^{\frac{3}{2}}\|B\|^{2}\|z(t)\|^{2}+T^{\frac{1}{2}})\big(\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+T}\displaystyle|\langle z(s),Bz(s)\rangle|^{2}ds\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot

Finally using again the fact that
\|z(t)\|\leq\|z_{0}\|,\;\forall t\geq 0,
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\langle BS(s)z(t),S(s)z(t)\rangle|ds\leq\big(2T^{\frac{3}{2}}\|B\|^{2}\|z_{0}\|^{2}+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\big(\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+T}\displaystyle|\langle z(s),Bz(s)\rangle|^{2}ds\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot

This gives the estimate

\big(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\langle BS(s)z(t),S(s)z(t)\rangle|ds\big)^{2}=O\big(\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+T}\displaystyle|\langle z(s),Bz(s)\rangle|^{2}ds\big),\;t\to+\infty\cdot

Step 2
: Weak convergence to the equilibrium.

Let
\varphi\in H
. We shall show that
\langle z(t),\varphi\rangle\to 0
as
t\to+\infty
. Let
(t_{n})\geq 0
be a sequence of real numbers such that
t_{n}\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
.

Since
\|z(t_{n})\|\leq\|z_{0}\|
for all
n\geq 1
, the sequence
x_{n}:=\langle z(t_{n}),\varphi\rangle
is bounded, so we can extract a convergent subsequence.

Let
x_{\gamma(n)}
be an arbitrary convergent subsequence of
x_{n}
. Since
z(t_{\gamma(n)})
is bounded in the Hilbert space
H
, we can extract an other subsequence, still denoted by
z(t_{\gamma(n)})
, such that

z(t_{\gamma(n)})\rightharpoonup\phi\in H\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty.

Using the compactness of
B
, we deduce via the dominated convergence theorem that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\langle BS(s)z(t_{\gamma(n)}),S(s)z(t_{\gamma(n)})\rangle|ds\to\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\langle BS(s)\phi,S(s)\phi\rangle|ds,n\to+\infty.

Moreover, it follows from the estimate

\big(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\langle BS(s)z(t),S(s)z(t)\rangle|ds\big)^{2}=O\big(\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+T}\displaystyle|\langle z(s),Bz(s)\rangle|^{2}ds\big),\;t\to+\infty

that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\langle BS(s)z(t_{\gamma(n)}),S(s)z(t_{\gamma(n)})\rangle|ds\to 0,n\to+\infty.

Hence

\langle BS(t)\phi,S(t)\phi\rangle=0,\quad\forall t\geq 0,

which, by virtue of (
2
), implies
\phi=0
. Hence,
x_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
, and consequently
\langle z(t),\varphi\rangle\to 0
as
t\to+\infty
. In other words,

z(t)\rightharpoonup 0\quad\text{as }t\to+\infty.

From the proof of the above theorem, one can see that weak stability still holds if the compactness of the control operator
B
is replaced by that of the semigroup
S(t)
. In the latter case, it also follows from the (VCF) that the stability is, in fact, strong (see also [Bounit and Hammouri, 1999], [Berrahmoune et al., 2001] and [Berrahmoune, 2010]).

We have the following similar result regarding strong stability :

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that (i)
A
generates a compact contractive semigroup
S(t)
and that
(ii)
B\in\mathcal{L}(H)
satisfies (
2
).

Then the control
v_{0}
strongly stabilizes (
BLS
).

Proof

From the same arguments as in the above theorem, we can see that, by using the compactness of
S(t)
in place of that of
B
, the solution
z(t)
of the system (
BLS
), closed with
v_{0}
, tends weakly to
0
as
t\to+\infty
.

Moreover, for any given
T>0
, the variation of constants formula implies

z(t+T)=S(T)z(t)+\int_{t}^{t+T}v_{0}(s)\,S(t+T-s)Bz(s)\,ds.

By again using the compactness of
S(t)
, we deduce that
S(T)z(t)\rightarrow 0,
as
t\to+\infty
.

Moreover, we have

\|\int_{t}^{t+T}v_{0}(s)\,S(t+T-s)Bz(s)\,ds\|\leq\|B\|\>\sqrt{T}\>\|z_{0}\|\bigg(\int_{t}^{t+T}|\langle z(s),Bz(s)\rangle|^{2}\,ds\bigg)^{1/2}\to 0\;\mbox{as}\;t\to+\infty.

This achieves the proof.

Remarks 3.5
.

1.

From the proofs, it is not clear whether the (necessary) condition
G\subset I_{w}

is also sufficient for weak stabilization in general.

2.

Notice that, in the case of a self-adjoint operator
B
, we have
G=\mathcal{B}
.
However, if for instance,
B
is skew-adjoint, then
G=\{0\}
, which is not necessarily the case for
\mathcal{B}
.

3.

Observing that
G
is invariant under
S(t)
, we see that if
G^{\perp}
is invariant under
S^{*}(t)
and both
G
and
G^{\perp}
are invariant under
B,
then one can use the orthogonal decomposition

H=G\oplus G^{\perp}.

Indeed, under the (necessary) condition
G\subset I_{w},
the initial system induces a weakly stable dynamics on
G
, while on
G^{\perp}
, the necessary and sufficient conditions for weak stabilization coincide.

3.3

Application to the undamped wave equation

Let
\Omega=(0,1)
and
Q=\Omega\times(0,+\infty)
, and consider the wave equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}z}{\partial t^{2}}(x,t)=\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}z}{\partial x^{2}}(x,t)+v(t)z(x,t),&\text{in }Q,\\
\\
z(0,t)=z(1,t)=0,&\text{on }t\in(0,+\infty).\end{array}\right.

(3)

Let
A_{1}=\partial_{xx}
with
D(A_{1})=H_{0}^{1}(0,1)\cap H^{2}(0,1).
The space
V=L^{2}(\Omega)
possesses an orthonormal basis
(\varphi_{j}=\sqrt{2}\sin(j\pi x))_{j\geq 1}
of eigenfunctions of the operator
-A_{1}
with DBC, associated with eigenvalues
(\lambda_{j}=(j\pi)^{2})_{j\geq 1}
.

The system (
3
) can be written in the abstract form (BLS) by setting

H=V_{1}\times V,\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
I&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\,\mbox{and}\,A=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
A_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}.

For
z_{1}=(y_{1},w_{1})
and
z_{2}=(y_{2},w_{2})
in
H
, we define

\langle z_{1},z_{2}\rangle=\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle_{H_{0}^{1}(0,1)}+\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)},

so that
H
is a separable Hilbert space.

Moreover, the operator
A
generates the semigroup
S(t)
defined, for all
\psi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\begin{pmatrix}a_{n}\\
b_{n}\end{pmatrix}\varphi_{n}
, by

S(t)\psi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\begin{pmatrix}a_{n}\cos(n\pi t)+\frac{b_{n}}{n\pi}\sin(n\pi t)\\
\\
-n\pi a_{n}\sin(n\pi t)+b_{n}\cos(n\pi t)\end{pmatrix}\varphi_{n}.

Then

\langle BS(t)\psi,S(t)\psi\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\pi\Big[\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{n}^{2}-\frac{b_{n}^{2}}{(n\pi)^{2}}\Big)\sin(2n\pi t)-\frac{a_{n}b_{n}}{n\pi}\cos(2n\pi t)\Big].

Hence,

\langle S(t)\psi,BS(t)\psi\rangle=0,\ \forall t\geq 0\quad\Rightarrow\quad a_{n}=b_{n}=0,\;\;\forall n\geq 1,

\Rightarrow\psi=0.

Thus condition (
2
) holds.

We conclude that the feedback control

v_{0}(t)=-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}z(x,t)\frac{\partial z}{\partial t}(x,t)\,dx

ensures the weak stabilization of system (
3
).

Remarks 3.6
.

1.

The authors in [Ball and Slemrod, 1979] raised the question of whether strong convergence holds for the stabilized state of the bilinear undamped wave equation.

2.

Strong stabilization of the undamped bilinear systems has been first proved in [Muller, 1989].
More precisely, the author showed that the stability of the bilinear undamped wave equation, with the quadratic control
v_{0}
,
is indeed strong. However, no a priori estimates for the rate of energy decay are available. It turns out that,
for every solution, the energy converges to zero as
t\to\infty
, but the decay may be arbitrarily slow.

3.

Under a strong observation assumption, the control
v_{0}
yields the estimate (see [Berrahmoune, 1999] and [Ouzahra, 2008])

\|z(t)\|=O\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big).

This estimate is optimal, as can be seen by taking
A=0
and
B=I
.

4.

Next, we provide a class of controls, which includes the quadratic one, that enhances the degree of stabilization and allows the achievement of any prescribed polynomial decay rate for the system state.

4.2

Sufficient conditions for strong stabilization

\bullet
To construct a stabilizing control
v(t)
for system (BLS), a natural approach is to formally compute the time derivative of the energy

E(t):=\frac{1}{2}\|z(t)\|^{2},

yielding :

\frac{dE(t)}{dt}\leq{\cal R}e\left(v(t)\langle Bz(t),z(t)\rangle\right).

\bullet
To ensure that the energy is non-increasing, one may consider feedback controls of the form
v(t)=f(z(t))
, where
f
satisfies the following energy dissipation inequality :

{\cal R}e\left(f(z(t))\langle Bz(t),z(t)\rangle\right)\leq 0,\quad\forall t\geq 0.

\bullet
As a class of feedback controls that satisfy this inequality, we consider the following family of controls :

v_{r}(t)=-\frac{\langle z(t),Bz(t)\rangle}{\|z(t)\|^{r}}\mathbf{1}_{\big(z(t)\neq 0\big)},\quad r\in\mathbb{R}.

(7)

In the following, we investigate the strong stabilization of system (BLS) using the control (
7
).

With the control (
7
), the system (BLS) becomes

\frac{dz(t)}{dt}=Az(t)+F_{r}(z(t)),\ z(0)=z_{0},

(8)

where
F_{r}(y)=-\displaystyle\frac{\langle y,By\rangle}{\|y\|^{r}}By,
if
y\neq 0
and
F_{r}(0)=0.

The following result provides conditions for the existence and uniqueness of a solution to the closed-loop system (
8
).

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
A
generate a semigroup of contractions
S(t)
and let
B\in\mathcal{L}(H)
. Then for all
r\in(-\infty,2]
, the system
(
8
) possesses a unique global mild solution.

Proof
.

Let us show that the map
F_{r}
is locally Lipschitz
from
H
to
H
. Let
z,y\in H
such that

0<\|y\|\leq\|z\|\leq R
.
Then we have

\frac{\langle Bz,z\rangle}{\|z\|^{r}}Bz-\frac{\langle By,y\rangle}{\|y\|^{r}}By=\frac{\langle Bz,z\rangle Bz-\langle By,y\rangle By}{\|z\|^{r}}-\frac{(\|z\|^{r}-\|y\|^{r})\langle By,y\rangle By}{\|z\|^{r}\|y\|^{r}}\cdot

From the relation

Lemma 4.7
.

(see [Ammari & Tucsnak, 2000]).

Let
(s_{k})
be a sequence of positive real numbers satisfying

s_{k+1}\leq s_{k}-Cs_{k+1}^{\alpha+2},\;\forall k\geq 0,

where
C>0
and
\alpha>-1
are constants.

Then there exists a positive constant
M
(depending on
\alpha
and
C
) such that

s_{k}\leq\frac{M}{(k+1)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}},\;\forall k\geq 0.

(15)

Proof of the lemma.

Define the sequence

f_{k}=\frac{M}{(k+1)^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}},

where
M>0
will be chosen later.

We will prove that

s_{k}\leq f_{k},\;k\geq 0.

Using a first-order expansion for large
k
, we can see that

f_{k}-f_{k+1}=M\Big((k+1)^{-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}-(k+2)^{-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}\Big).

\sim\frac{M}{1+\alpha}k^{-1}(k+2)^{-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}

Hence,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\Big((f_{k}-f_{k+1})k(k+2)^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}\Big)=\frac{M}{1+\alpha}.

Thus there exists
k_{0}>0
such that

f_{k}-f_{k+1}\leq\frac{2M}{(1+\alpha)k(k+2)^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}},\qquad k\geq k_{0}.

We have

\frac{1}{k(k+2)^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}}\leq\frac{2}{(k+2)}\frac{1}{(k+2)^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}}=\frac{2}{(k+2)^{\frac{2+\alpha}{1+\alpha}}},\qquad k\geq 2.

Then, observing that

f_{k+1}^{2+\alpha}=\frac{M^{2+\alpha}}{(k+2)^{\frac{2+\alpha}{1+\alpha}}},

we can see that

f_{k}-f_{k+1}\leq\frac{4}{(1+\alpha)M^{1+\alpha}}f_{k+1}^{2+\alpha},\qquad k\geq k_{1}\;(k_{1}:=k_{0}+2).

Now, choose
M
large enough such that

\frac{4}{(1+\alpha)M^{1+\alpha}}<C\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;f_{k_{1}}\geq s_{1}.

Then

f_{k}-f_{k+1}\leq Cf_{k+1}^{2+\alpha},\qquad k\geq k_{1}.

We prove

s_{k}\leq f_{k},\qquad k\geq k_{1}.

\star
For
k=k_{1}
, the property follows directly from the choice of
M
.

Let
k\geq k_{1}
, and assume that
s_{k}\leq f_{k}.

\star
By the induction hypothesis and the estimates above, we obtain

s_{k+1}+Cs_{k+1}^{2+\alpha}\leq s_{k}\leq f_{k}\leq f_{k+1}+Cf_{k+1}^{2+\alpha}.

Since the function

\phi(x)=x+Cx^{2+\alpha}

is increasing on

Thus the result holds for

By induction,

s_{k}\leq f_{k}=\frac{M}{(k+1)^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}},\qquad k\geq k_{1},

which give the estimate (
15

This also covers the finite number of indices

s_{k}\leq s_{1}\leq f_{k_{1}}\leq f_{k},\;k=1,..,k_{1}.

Introduction

Motivation
:

Many modeling problems naturally arise in non-reflexive state spaces (see e.g. [Rhandi, 1998], [Rhandi and Schnaubelt, 1999], [Fattorini, 1974], [Fattorini, 2001] and [Vieru, 2005]). For instance,

—

The appropriate state space for age-dependent population dynamics with spatial diffusion is

—

For the heat equation, spaces of continuous functions, such as

Constraints may also naturally be expressed in non-reflexive settings.

\phi(\cdot)\in J(\zeta(\cdot))
if
\phi(t)\in J(\zeta(t)),\,\forall t\geq 0.

7.2

Necessary conditions

Let us prove the following lemma :

Lemma 7.1
.

If the assumption
({\cal A}_{2})
holds, then the closed-loop operator
z\mapsto F(z)=\frac{\langle Bz,J(z)\rangle}{\|z\|^{2}}Bz
is globally Lipschitz.

Proof.

Let
y,z\in X-(0)
such that
\|z\|\leq\|y\|.
We have

F(y)-F(z)=\frac{\langle By,J(y)\rangle}{\|y\|^{2}}By-\frac{\langle Bz,J(z)\rangle}{\|z\|^{2}}Bz

=\frac{\|z\|^{2}\langle By,J(y)\rangle By-\|y\|^{2}\langle Bz,J(z)\rangle Bz}{\|y\|^{2}\|z\|^{2}}

=\frac{\|z\|^{2}\big(\langle By,J(y)\rangle By-\langle Bz,J(z)\rangle Bz\big)+\big(\|z\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2}\big)\langle Bz,J(z)\rangle Bz}{\|y\|^{2}\|z\|^{2}}

=\frac{\langle By,J(y)\rangle By-\langle Bz,J(z)\rangle Bz}{\|y\|^{2}}+\frac{\big(\|z\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2}\big)\langle Bz,J(z)\rangle Bz}{\|y\|^{2}\|z\|^{2}}

Then, using assumption
({\cal A}_{2}),
we get

\|F(y)-F(z)\|\leq\frac{|\langle By,J(y)\rangle-\langle Bz,J(z)\rangle|\|By\|}{\|y\|^{2}}+\frac{|\langle Bz,J(z)\rangle|\|By-Bz\|}{\|y\|^{2}}+

\frac{|\|z\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2}||\langle Bz,J(z)\rangle|\|Bz\|}{\|y\|^{2}\|z\|^{2}}

\leq\frac{\|B\|{\cal K}\big(\|y\|,\|z\|\big)\|y-z\|}{\|y\|}+\frac{\|B\|^{2}\|z\|^{2}\|y-z\|}{\|y\|^{2}}+

\frac{\big(\|y\|+\|z\|\big)|\|z-y\||\|B\|^{2}\|z\|}{\|y\|^{2}}

Then, using
\|z\|\leq\|y\|
and, once again, assumption
({\cal A}_{2})
, we derive

\|F(y)-F(z)\|\leq C\|B\|\|y-z\|+\|B\|^{2}\|y-z\|+2\|B\|^{2}\|z-y\|

=\big(C\|B\|+3\|B\|^{2}\big)\|z-y\|

Thus
F
is
L-
Lipschitz with
L=C\|B\|+3\|B\|^{2}.

Next we give necessary conditions for exponential stabilization. Let us consider the feedback law :

u(t)=-\lambda\displaystyle\frac{\langle B(z(t)),J(z(t))\rangle}{\|z(t)\|^{2}},\;\mbox{if}\;z(t)\neq 0\;\ \mbox{and}\;\;u(t)=0\;\mbox{if}\;z(t)=0,

(18)

where
\lambda>0
is the control gain.

Theorem 7.2
.

—

Let assumption
({\cal A}_{2})
hold and suppose that the semigroup
S(t)
is of
isometries
.

—

If the control (
18
) uniformly exponentially stabilizes (BLS) for some
\lambda>0
, then the observation condition
({\cal A}_{3})
holds.

Proof :

It is clear that, without loss of generality, we may assume
\lambda=1
in this proof.

Assume that the system (BLS) closed with
u(t)
is exponentially stable :

\|z(t)\|\leq Me^{-\sigma t}\|z_{0}\|,\forall t\geq 0,\;z_{0}\in X.

We know from the above lemma, that assumption
({\cal A}_{2})
implies that the function

z\mapsto F(z)=\frac{\langle Bz,J(z)\rangle}{\|z\|^{2}}Bz

is globally Lipschitz (with a Lipschitz constant
L
).

Step 1.
We have

\|F(z(t))\|

\leq\|F(S(t)z_{0})\|+\|F(z(t))-F(S(t)z_{0})\|

\leq\|F(S(t)z_{0})\|+L\|z(t)-S(t)z_{0}\|

\leq\|F(S(t)z_{0})\|+L\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|\langle Bz(s),J(z(s))\rangle|}{\|z(s)\|^{2}}\|Bz(s)\|ds

\leq\|F(S(t)z_{0})\|+L\int_{0}^{t}\|F(z(s))\|ds

Then, Gronwall’s inequality (see e.g. [Corduneanu, 2008]) yields

\|F(z(t))\|\leq\|F(S(t)z_{0})\|+L\int_{0}^{t}\|F(S(s)z_{0})\|e^{L(t-s)}ds

\leq\|F(S(t)z_{0})\|+Le^{Lt}\int_{0}^{t}\|F(S(s)z_{0})\|ds

Integrating over
[0,T]
with
T>0,
this gives

\int_{0}^{T}\|F(z(s))\|ds\leq\big(1+TLe^{LT)}\big)\int_{0}^{T}\|F(S(t))z_{0}\|ds

Let
z_{0}\in D(A)
. Based on the variation of constants formula (VCF),
one may show that the function
t\mapsto\|z(t)\|^{2}
is
Lipschitz continuous (recall that the norm
square
\|\cdot\|^{2}
is not necessarily differentiable), which will show that
\|z(t)\|^{2}
is differentiable for almost every
t
in
(0,+\infty).

For this end, we will show that
t\mapsto\|z(t)\|
is Lipschitz on any interval
[0,T],\ T>0.

For all
h,t\in[0,T]
we have

z(t+h)-z(t)=S(t+h)z_{0}-S(t)z_{0}+\int_{0}^{h}S(t+h-s)F(z(s))ds

+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)(F(z(s+h))-F(z(s)))ds\cdot

We know that
\|S(t+h)z_{0}-S(t)z_{0}\|\leq h\|Az_{0}\|.
Then using assumptions
(\mathcal{A}_{1})-(\mathcal{A}_{2}),
we deduce that

\|z(t+h)-z(t)\|\leq h\|Az_{0}\|+hC_{T}\|z_{0}\|+L\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s+h)-z(s)\|ds,\;(C_{T}>0)

which by Gronwall’s inequality gives

\|z(t+h)-z(t)\|\leq h\>\big(\|Az_{0}\|+C_{T}\|z_{0}\|\big)e^{LT},\;\;\forall(t,h)\in[0,T]^{2}.

Then taking the graph norm :
\|z\|_{\mathcal{D}(A)}=\big(\|Az\|^{2}+\|z\|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\;z\in\mathcal{D}(A),
we get
the following estimate :

\|z(t+h)-z(t)\|\leq M_{0}h\|z_{0}\|_{\mathcal{D}(A)},\;\;\forall(t,h)\in[0,t_{1}]^{2},

where
M_{0}=\big(1+C_{T}\big)e^{LT}
.
It follows that
\|z(t)\|
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to
t
on any interval
[0,T]
. Hence,
\|z(t)\|
is differentiable almost everywhere and the following equality holds for every
t>0
(see
[
37
,
21
,
59
]
) :

C(z):=\frac{d}{dt}\|z(t)\|^{2}+2\frac{|\langle Bz(t),J(z(t))\rangle|^{2}}{\|z(t)\|^{2}}=2\langle Az(t),J(z(t))\rangle

We have

\sup_{\phi\in J(z(.))}C(z)=\sup_{\phi\in J(z(.))}2\langle Az(t),J(z(t))\rangle=0

Since
z\mapsto\langle Bz,J(z)\rangle
is single-valued, so is the function
t\mapsto\langle Az(t),J(z(t))\rangle
.
Hence, since
A
is conservative, it follows that

\frac{d}{dt}\|z(t)\|^{2}+2\frac{|\langle Bz(t),J(z(t))\rangle|^{2}}{\|z(t)\|^{2}}=0

Then we have

\frac{d}{dt}\|z(t)\|^{2}=-2\frac{|\langle Bz(t),J(z(t))\rangle|^{2}}{\|z(t)\|^{2}}

\Rightarrow 2\int_{0}^{T}\frac{|\langle Bz(t),J(z(t))\rangle|^{2}}{\|z(t)\|^{2}}dt=\|z_{0}\|^{2}-\|z(T)\|^{2}

Then we have

2\int_{0}^{T}\frac{|\langle Bz(t),J(z(t))\rangle|^{2}}{\|z(t)\|^{2}}dt\geq\big(1-M^{2}e^{-2\sigma T}\big)\|z_{0}\|^{2}

It follows that (recall that
\|F(z)\|=\frac{|\langle Bz,J(z)\rangle|}{\|z\|^{2}}\|Bz\|
)

2\int_{0}^{T}\|F(z(t))\|dt\geq\big(1-M^{2}e^{-2\sigma T}\big)\|z_{0}\|

and hence there exist
T,\alpha>0
such that

\int_{0}^{T}\|F(S(t))z_{0}\|ds\geq\alpha\|z_{0}\|,\;\forall z_{0}\in X

The semigroup being of isometries, we have
\|S(t)z_{0}\|=\|z_{0}\|,\;\forall t\geq 0.
Then

\|F(S(t))z_{0}\|=\frac{|\langle BS(t)z_{0},J(S(t)z_{0})\rangle|}{\|S(t)z_{0}\|^{2}}\|BS(t)z_{0}\|

\leq\|B\|\frac{|\langle BS(t)z_{0},J(S(t)z_{0})\rangle|}{\|S(t)z_{0}\|}

=\|B\|\frac{|\langle BS(t)z_{0},J(S(t)z_{0})\rangle|}{\|z_{0}\|}

We conclude that

\int_{0}^{T}|\langle BS(t)z_{0},J(S(t)z_{0})\rangle|dt\geq\alpha\|z_{0}\|^{2}

which extends by density to all
z_{0}\in X.

7.3

Sufficient conditions for exponential stabilization : The case where
B_{J}
is single-valued

Let us consider the feedback law

u(t)=-\lambda\displaystyle\frac{\langle B(z(t)),J(z(t))\rangle}{\|z(t)\|^{2}},\;\mbox{if}\;z(t)\neq 0\;\ \mbox{and}\;\;u(t)=0\;\mbox{if}\;z(t)=0,

where
\lambda>0
is the control gain.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let assumptions

({\cal A}_{1})\;\&\;({\cal A}_{2})\;\&\;({\cal A}_{3})
hold.

Then, for
\lambda>0
small enough, the control (
18
) ensures the uniform exponential stabilization of system (BLS).

Proof :

According to the above lemma, we have that under assumption
(\mathcal{A}_{2})
, the closed-loop operator generated by the control (
18
) is globally Lipschitz.

Consequently, there exists a unique global mild solution
z(t)
for the system in closed-loop, which is given for any
z_{0}\in X
and for all
t\geq 0
by the variation of constants formula (VCF) :

z(t)=S(t)z_{0}-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\langle Bz(s),J(z(s))\rangle}{\|z(s)\|^{2}}S(t-s)Bz(s)ds\cdot

Observing that
z_{0}=0
implies
z(t)=0
for all
t\geq 0,

we can assume in the sequel that
z(t)
does not vanish.

Moreover, using an argument of density as in the theorem of necessity, one can also show that, for
y_{0}\in X
. Indeed, similarly to the case
\lambda=1,
we can show that
\|z(t)\|
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to
t
on any interval
[0,T],T>0
. Hence,
\|z(t)\|
is differentiable almost everywhere and the following equality holds (see
[
37
,
21
,
59
]
) :

\frac{d}{dt}\|z(t)\|^{2}=2\mathcal{R}e\big(\left<\dot{z}(t),\phi\right>\big),\,a.e.\;t>0,\;\forall\phi\in J(z(t)),

(19)

According to assumption
(\mathcal{A}_{3})
, the mapping
t\mapsto\mathcal{R}e\big(\langle F(z(t)),\phi(t)\rangle\big)

is continuous for all
\phi(\cdot)\in J(z(\cdot)).
Thus we can integrate (
19
) to get (since
A
is dissipative)

\|z(t)\|^{2}-\|z(s)\|^{2}\leq-2\lambda\int_{s}^{t}\big(\langle F(z(\tau)),\phi(\tau)\rangle\big)d\tau,\;\forall 0\leq s\leq t,

for all
\phi(\cdot)\in J(z(\cdot)),
which we may also write as follows.

\|z(t)\|^{2}-\|z(s)\|^{2}\leq-2\lambda\int_{s}^{t}\big(\langle F(z(\tau)),J(z(\tau))\rangle\big)d\tau,\;0\leq s\leq t.

(20)

Moreover, it follows from assumption
(\mathcal{A}_{3}),
together with the density of
\mathcal{D}(A)
in
X
, that the inequality (
20
) extends to all
z_{0}\in X
.

\|z(t)\|^{2}-\|z(s)\|^{2}\leq-2\lambda\int_{s}^{t}\frac{|\langle Bz(\tau),J(z(\tau))\rangle|^{2}}{\|z(\tau)\|^{2}}d\tau,\;0\leq s\leq t.

(21)

Based on the (VCF), we deduce via Gronwall’s inequality

\|z(t)\|\leq\|z_{0}\|e^{\lambda K^{2}t},\;\forall t\geq 0\;(K:=\|B\|).

Now, from the variation of constants formula (VCF), we derive that for all
t\geq T,
we have

\|z(t)\|\leq\|S(T)z_{0}\|+\lambda K^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|ds

\leq\|S(T)z_{0}\|+\lambda K^{2}\|z_{0}\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda K^{2}s}ds

\leq\|S(T)z_{0}\|+\|z_{0}\|\big(e^{\lambda K^{2}t}-1\big)ds,

which gives for
t=T

\|z(T)\|\leq\|S(T)z_{0}\|+K_{\lambda}\|z_{0}\|.

K_{\lambda}\sim\lambda K^{2}T=o(1),
as
\lambda\to 0^{+}.

Thus for all
j\geq 0,
we have

\|z((j+1)T)\|\leq\|S(T)z(jT)\|^{2}+K_{\lambda}\|z(jT)\|\cdot

(22)

We have used the following estimations :

\|S(t)z_{0}\|\leq\|z_{0}\|\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\|z(t)\|\leq\|z_{0}\|

which imply that

\mathcal{K}(\|S(t)z_{0}\|,\|z(t)\|)\leq\mathcal{K}(\|z_{0}\|,\|z_{0}\|)\leq C\|z_{0}\|,

where
C
is the constant defined in assumption
(\mathcal{A}_{2})
.

It follows from assumption
(\mathcal{A}_{2})
that for all
t\in[0,T],

|\left<BS(t)z_{0},J(S(t)z_{0})\right>-\left<Bz(t),J(z(t))\right>|\leq C\|z_{0}\|\;\|z(t)-S(t)z_{0}\|,

and hence

|\left<BS(t)z_{0},J(S(t)z_{0})\right>|\leq\left<Bz(t),J(z(t))\right>|+C\|z_{0}\|\;\|z(t)-S(t)z_{0}\|

\leq\frac{|\langle Bz(t),J(z(t))\rangle|}{\|z(t)\|}\|z_{0}\|+C\lambda\|B\|\|z_{0}\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\frac{|\langle Bz(t),J(z(t))\rangle|}{\|z(t)\|}dt.

Integrating the last inequality and using again that
\|z(t)\|
is non-increasing, we get via H
\ddot{o}
lder’s inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\left<BS(t)z_{0},J(S(t)z_{0})\right>|dt\leq C_{\lambda}\|z_{0}\|\big(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\frac{|\langle Bz(t),J(z(t))\rangle|^{2}}{\|z(t)\|^{2}}dt\big)^{1/2}

where
C_{\lambda}=\big(1+C\|B\|\lambda\big)\sqrt{T}\leq C_{1}
for
\lambda\in(0,1).

In the sequel, we suppose that
\lambda\in(0,1).

Replacing
z_{0}
by
z(jT),j\geq 0
in the last inequality and using the observation estimate, we deduce that

C_{\lambda}^{2}\|z(jT)\|^{2}\displaystyle\int_{jT}^{(j+1)T}\frac{|\langle f(z(t),u_{0}),J(z(t))\rangle|^{2}}{\|z(t)\|^{2}}dt\geq\delta^{2}\|S(T)z(jT)\|^{4},\;\forall j\geq 0\cdot

This together with

\|z(t)\|^{2}-\|z(s)\|^{2}\leq-2\lambda\int_{s}^{t}\frac{|\langle Bz(\tau),J(z(\tau))\rangle|^{2}}{\|z(\tau)\|^{2}}d\tau,\;0\leq s\leq t,

gives

\|z(jT)\|^{4}-\|z((j+1)T)\|^{2}\|z(jT)\|^{2}\geq\frac{2\lambda\delta^{2}}{C_{1}^{2}}\|S(T)z(jT)\|^{4}\cdot

Hence using the following estimate (see inequality (
22
)) :

\|z((j+1)T)\|\leq\|S(T)z(jT)\|^{2}+K_{\lambda}\|z(jT)\|,

and the fact that

\|z((j+1)T)\|\leq\|z(jT)\|,

it comes

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\|z(jT)\|^{4}-\|z((j+1)T)\|^{4}&\geq&\frac{2\lambda\delta^{2}}{C_{1}^{2}}\|S(T)z(jT)\|^{4}\\
&\geq&\frac{\lambda\delta^{2}}{4C_{1}^{2}}\bigg(\|z((j+1)T)\|^{4}-K_{\lambda}\|z(jT)\|^{4}\bigg)\end{array}

Hence

\|z((j+1)T)\|^{4}\leq\zeta\|z(jT)\|^{4},

where
\zeta:=\frac{1+\frac{\lambda\delta^{2}}{4C_{1}^{2}}K_{\lambda}}{1+\frac{\lambda\delta^{2}}{4C_{1}^{2}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\in(0,1)}
for
\lambda>0
sufficiently small (recall that
K_{\lambda}=o(1),
as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
).

Thus

\|z(jT)\|\leq\zeta^{\frac{j}{4}}\|z_{0}\|,\;j\geq 0,

gives (recall that
\|z(t)\|
is decreasing)

\|z(t)\|\leq\|z(jT)\|,\;j=E(t/T).

This gives the estimate

\|z(t)\|\leq Me^{-\sigma t}\|z_{0}\|,\;t\geq 0

with
\sigma=\frac{-\ln\zeta}{4T}
and
M=\zeta^{-1/4}.

Remarks 7.4
.

Note that the above results still are valid in the context of complex Banach space using the conjugate of the above normalized feedback control.

7.4

Sufficient conditions for exponential stabilization : The case where
B_{J}
is not single-valued

In case where
B_{J}
is not single-valued, one may replace assumption
({\cal A}_{2})
by the following one :

({\cal A}_{2})^{\prime}

There exists a linear bounded operator
{\cal B}:X\to X
such that

{\cal B}_{J}
satisfies
({\cal A}_{2})
and

in the sense that

\langle By,J(y)\rangle\geq\langle{\cal B}y,J(y)\rangle,\qquad\forall y\in X.

In other words,

\langle By,y^{*}\rangle\geq\langle{\cal B}y,y^{*}\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\forall y\in X,\ \forall y^{*}\in J(y).

B_{J}
is single-valued.

Using the same arguments as above, we obtain the following result :

Theorem 7.5
.

Assume that
({\cal A}_{1})
,
({\cal A}_{2})^{\prime}
, and
({\cal A}_{3})
hold,
with
B
replaced by
{\cal B}
.

Consider the control law

u=-\lambda\frac{\langle{\cal B}z(t),J(z(t))\rangle}{\|z(t)\|^{2}},\qquad\text{if }z(t)\neq 0,

and

where
\lambda>0
is the control gain.

Then, for

small enough, the control

ensures the

Idea of the proof :

Following the same method as for the lemma, we can see that the new closed operator

Moreover, the estimate (
21
) becomes

\|z(t)\|^{2}-\|z(s)\|^{2}\leq-2\lambda\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\langle{\cal B}z(\tau),J(z(\tau))\rangle\;\langle Bz(\tau),J(z(\tau))\rangle}{\|z(\tau)\|^{2}}d\tau,\;0\leq s\leq t.

\leq-2\lambda\int_{s}^{t}\frac{|\langle{\cal B}z(\tau),J(z(\tau))\rangle|^{2}}{\|z(\tau)\|^{2}}d\tau,\;0\leq s\leq t.

It follows from assumption

where

is the constant defined in assumption

Then we proceed as in the above theorem.

Remarks 7.6
.

1.

Note that in assumptions
({\cal A}_{2})
and

, one can replace

\langle x,y\rangle_{P}:=\langle Px,y\rangle,

Exemple 7.7
.

Let
\Omega
denote a bounded open subset of

with smooth boundary

, and let

Consider the following wave equation :

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}z(x,t)}{\partial t^{2}}=\Delta z(x,t)+v(t)a(x)\frac{\partial z(x,t)}{\partial t},&\text{in }Q,\\
\\

This system can be written in the abstract form of equation (BLS) by setting

H=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega),\quad\langle(y_{1},z_{1}),(y_{2},z_{2})\rangle=\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}+\langle z_{1},z_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

We deduce that the feedback control

.

, under a weaker observability condition (see Ammari and Ouzahra, 2021).

Exemple 7.9
.

Let
\Omega=\left(0,+\infty\right)
and let us consider the following system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y_{t}(\cdot,t)=-y_{x}(\cdot,t)+u(t)h(x)y(\cdot,t),&\mbox{in}\;\Omega\times(0,+\infty)\\
y(0,t)=0,&\mbox{in}\,(0,+\infty)\\
y(\cdot,0)=y_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega),&\mbox{in}\;\Omega\end{array}\right.

(24)

Here,
X=L^{1}(\Omega)
is the state space, the parameter
u(t)
represents the control,
and the corresponding solution
z(t):=y(\cdot,t)\in X
is the state.

Moreover, we assume that
h\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and we define the control operator by

By=h(x)\,y,\quad\forall u\in U:=\mathbb{R},\ \forall y\in X.

The duality map is a multivalued function and for all
y\in X
, we have

J(y)=\{\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega):\;\xi(x)\in\,\,\mbox{sign}(y(x))\cdot\|y\|\},

where the sign function is defined by sign
(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&s>0\\
I,&s=0\\
-1,&s<0\end{array}\right.\;\;
with

.

The unbounded operator

A=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\;\mbox{with domain}\;D(A)=\left\{y\in W^{1,1}\left(\Omega\right);\;y\left(0\right)=0\right\}

generates an isometric semigroup

on

which is defined for all

by

S(t)y(\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y(\xi-t),&\mbox{if}\;\xi-t\geq 0\\
\\
0,&\mbox{else.}\end{array}\right.

Hence, a necessary condition for uniform exponential stabilization with the control (
18
) is

Introduction

The concept of stability (introduced by A. M. Lyapunov in 1892) is one of the central notions in control theory and describes the system’s response to small perturbations of the initial conditions. The notion of asymptotic stability in control theory implies convergence of the system trajectories to an equilibrium state over an
infinite horizon
. In many applications, it is desirable that the system trajectories converge to the equilibrium state in
finite time
, rather than only as
t\to+\infty
.

The notion of finite-time stability was first introduced in the finite-dimensional setting by Roxin, (1966). Earlier contributions appeared in the Russian literature between 1951 and 1954 (see, e.g., Erugin (1951), Kamenkov (1953), and Lebedev (1954)). This concept has since been extensively developed by many researchers in both finite- and infinite-dimensional settings :

\star
Finite-stability (without controls) : [Bhat, S. P.,
\&
Bernstein, D. S. (2000), (2005)], [Dorato, P. (2006)], [Moulay, E., & Perruquetti, (2006)]…

\star
Finite-time stabilisation of abstract Eqs : [Polyakov et al. (2018)], [Amaliki, Y., & Ouzahra, M. (2025)], [Jammazi et al. (2023)]…

\star
FTS of PDEs : [Coron, J. M.,
\&
Nguyen, H. M. (2017)], [Barbu, V. (2018)], [Espitia et al. (2019)], [Zhang, C. 2019], [Ouzahra, M. (2021)]…

Considered system and definitions

Let us consider the system :

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(\text{BLS})}\hskip 28.45274pt\dot{x}(t)=Ax(t)+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}u(t)Bx(t)},\quad x(0)=x_{0},

—

Let us recall the notion of stabilization in finite time.

Definition.

\bullet
A control system (

(FTS) at the origin (
0

\rightsquigarrow
there exists a feedback control such that the system in closed-loop admits a unique solution such that the corresponding closed-loop system satisfies the following properties :

\rightsquigarrow

(0)

\rightsquigarrow

\bullet

Remarks 9.1
.

\star
If the closed-loop system possesses the property of backward uniqueness in time, then finite-time stabilization (FTS) cannot occur.

\star
In particular, if the system is reversible in time, then there exists no control

\star

9.3

Necessary conditions for FTS/FxTS/PrTS

Theorem 9.2
.

\bullet

If
the system
(BLS)
is FTS,
then

\forall\xi\in H,\quad BS(t)\xi=0,\quad\forall t\geq 0\Rightarrow\;\exists t_{1}=t_{1}(\xi)>0\;/\;S(t_{1})\xi=0.

\bullet

If
the system
(BLS)
is FxTS,
then
there exists

\forall\xi\in H,\quad BS(t)\xi=0,\quad\forall t\in[0,t_{1}]\Rightarrow\;\;S(t_{1})\xi=0.

\bullet

If
the system
(BLS)
is PrTS,
then
for every
\tau>0,
we have

\forall\xi\in H,\quad BS(t)\xi=0,\quad\forall t\in[0,\tau]\Rightarrow\xi=0.

Proof.

We will treat the case of
FTS
, as the other cases can be done similarly.

Assume that the system
(BLS)
is globally finite-time stable, and let
x_{0}\in H
be such that
BS(t)x_{0}=0
for all

.

Then the unique solution of system (BLS) with initial condition

is given by

x(t)=S(t)x_{0},\;t\geq 0,

independently of the choice of the feedback control
u(x)
.

Moreover, because the system is globally finite-time stable, there exists a finite
time
t_{1}=t_{1}(x_{0})\geq 0

S(t)x_{0}=x(t)=0,\;\forall\,t\geq t_{1}.

This concludes the proof.

Remarks 9.3
.

If
S(t)
is one to one (which is the case for instance if

is self-adjoint), then

\star

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(OBS)}\hskip 28.45274pt\forall\xi\in H,\quad BS(t)\xi=0,\quad\forall t\geq 0\Rightarrow\xi=0.

\star
the necessary condition of FxTS is equivalent to

\forall\xi\in H,\quad BS(t)\xi=0,\quad\forall t\in[0,t_{1}]\Rightarrow\xi=0.

Building on the general necessary condition presented earlier, the following corollary establishes a particular case of the above necessary condition.

Corollary 9.4

Assume that the system (BLS) is globally finite-time stable.

If

is invariant under

then the semigroup
S(t)
induces on the subspace

a nilpotent semigroup

Proof.

If the system (BLS) is finite-time stable, then there exists a feedback control

Let

\star
In the finite-dimensional state-space case, the same conclusion follows from arguments specific to finite-dimensional spaces. However, in this setting, the property holds only when

Theorem 9.6
.

Assume that the assumptions
(\mathcal{A})
and
(\mathcal{B})
hold, and that

\ker(B)^{\perp}
is invariant under the semigroup

Then the system

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}u(x)=-\langle Bx,x\rangle^{-\mu}},

Furthermore, if

is absorbent, in a finite time

u=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,\;0\leq t\leq\tau\\
\\
-\langle Bx,x\rangle^{-\mu}{\bf 1}_{(x\neq 0)},\;t>\tau\end{array}\right.

Proof.

I- Well-posedness.
First, observe that the space
F=(\ker(B))^{\perp}
is a Hilbert space as it is a closed subspace of
H
.

The system in closed loop induces a subsystem on
(\ker(B))^{\perp}
can be written as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dfrac{d}{dt}x(t)+\mathcal{A}x(t)=0,\quad t>0,\\
x(0)=x_{0}\in(\ker(B))^{\perp},\end{array}\right.

(26)

where the operator

\mathcal{A}:D(A)\cap(\ker(B))^{\perp}\to(\ker(B))^{\perp}

is defined by

\mathcal{A}z=-Az+\Theta(z),

and
\Theta
is defined for all

by

\Theta(z)=\begin{cases}\langle Bz,z\rangle^{-\mu}Bz,&z\neq 0,\\
0,&z=0.\end{cases}

To establish the well-posedness of system
(
26
)
,
we use an approach based on maximal monotone operators.

Since
S(t)
is a contraction semigroup, the operator
A
with

is maximal monotone.
It therefore suffices to prove that
\Theta

Consider the functional

\varphi(z)=\frac{\langle Bz,z\rangle^{1-\mu}}{2(1-\mu)},\qquad\forall z\in(\ker(B))^{\perp}.

Clearly,
\varphi
is convex and continuous on
(\ker(B))^{\perp}
.

Writing

we see that
\varphi
is Fréchet differentiable for every

\varphi^{\prime}(z)=h^{\prime}(\psi(z))\psi^{\prime}(z)=\langle Bz,z\rangle^{-\mu}Bz.

For

, we verify that

\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\varphi(h)}{\|h\|}=0.

We conclude that
\varphi
is differentiable on all of
(\ker(B))^{\perp}

and
\Theta=\varphi^{\prime}
. Hence (see Brezis, 1973), the operator
\Theta
is maximal monotone.

Therefore, (see Brezis, 1973), the operator
\mathcal{A}
generates a nonlinear semigroup and the closed-loop system

26

admits a unique weak solution

.

That is, there exists a sequence

, and the system
(

II-FTS

\star
For almost every

and

\frac{d}{dt}x^{n}(t)=Ax^{n}(t)-\langle Bx^{n}(t),x^{n}(t)\rangle^{-\mu}Bx^{n}(t)\mathbf{1}_{\{x^{n}(t)\neq 0\}}.

\star
To establish the finite-time stability of (
26

V(x)=\|x\|^{2}.

Its derivative along the trajectories of (
26

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|x^{n}(t)\|^{2}=\langle Ax^{n}(t),x^{n}(t)\rangle-\langle Bx^{n}(t),x^{n}(t)\rangle^{1-\mu}.

Using the dissipativity of

we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|x^{n}(t)\|^{2}\leq-\beta^{1-\mu}\|x^{n}(t)\|^{2(1-\mu)}\quad\text{for a.e. }t\geq 0.

Integrating this differential equation over the maximal interval

,

, on which

does not vanish,
we see that

and that (we can also apply the comparison principle)

\\
\|x^{n}(t)\|=0,&t\geq\dfrac{\|x_{0}^{n}\|^{2\mu}}{2\mu\beta^{1-\mu}}.\end{cases}

Letting

, we deduce that

\begin{cases}\|x(t)\|^{2\mu}\leq\|x_{0}\|^{2\mu}-2\mu\beta^{1-\mu}t,&t\leq\dfrac{\|x_{0}\|^{2\mu}}{2\mu\beta^{1-\mu}},\\

giving rise to finite time extinction of the solution.

Hence we have the FTS with the following estimation of the settling time :

T_{*}(x_{0})\leq\dfrac{\|x_{0}\|^{2\mu}}{2\mu\beta^{1-\mu}}.

Finally, the GFTS follows by applying the first point to the new initial state
X_{0}=S(\tau)x_{0}\in\ker(B)^{\perp}.

(see [Parsegov et al., 2012])

9.8

FTS : Without invariance assumption

In the sequel, we consider the Lyapunov function candidate :

V(x)=\langle Bx,x\rangle.

\star
We will give a FTS result on
\ker(B)^{c}\supset\ker(B)^{\perp}-(0)
for the system (BLS).

\star
The (generalized) Lyapunov function
V=V(x)=\langle Bx,x\rangle
is
positive but not necessarily defined
on the whole space, so establishing FTS may require
additional (observability) assumptions
.

We formally compute, along the trajectories of the system,

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\dot{V}=2\langle Ax,Bx\rangle+2u(t)\langle Bx,Bx\rangle}.

To ensure that
V
satisfies a differential inequality of the form
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\dot{V}\leq-mV^{\alpha}},
for some
m>0,0<\alpha<1
, we design a control
u=u_{1}+u_{2}
, where the controls
u_{1}
and
u_{2},
have the following roles :

\star

u_{1}
: will be chosen to ’cancel’ the term
\langle Ax,Bx\rangle
.

\star

u_{2}
: will be selected such that
\dot{V}(x)\leq-mV(x)^{\alpha}.

\bullet
Let us consider the assumption

({\cal AB})\;\;\;{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\langle Ax,x\rangle_{B}\leq\alpha\|x\|^{2}_{B},\;\forall x\in D(A)},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{B}=\langle B\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\|\cdot\|_{B}=\langle B\cdot,\cdot\rangle^{1/2}
.

This assumption means that the operator
A
is quasi-dissipative with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{B}
.

\star
We can replace the above assumption by the existence of a function
f:H\to\mathds{R}
such that

\frac{\langle Ax,x\rangle_{B}}{\|x\|^{2}_{B}}\leq f(x),\;\forall x\in D(A)-\ker(B),

with
F:=f\;B
being a Lipschitz function.

Theorem 9.12
.

Let assumptions
({\cal A})-({\cal B})-({\cal AB})
hold, and assume that
for all
\xi\in H,
we have

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(OBS)\;\;\;BS(t)\xi=0,\;\;\forall t\geq 0\;\;\Rightarrow\xi=0\cdot}

Then the control law

u(x)=-\alpha-\langle Bx,x\rangle^{-\mu}{\bf 1}_{(Bx\neq 0)},\;(0<\mu<1/2)

stabilizes the system
(LS)
in finite time for any
x_{0}
s.t
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}Bx_{0}\neq 0}
(in particular those of
(\ker B)^{\perp}
).

Furthermore, the settling time is such that

T_{*}\leq\frac{\langle Bx_{0},x_{0}\rangle^{\mu}}{\beta\mu}.

Proof :

The closed-loop system is

\dot{x}=Ax-\alpha Bx-\langle Bx,x\rangle^{-\mu}Bx.

By the same argument as above, it possesses a unique global solution
x
, and the function

V(t)=\langle Bx(t),x(t)\rangle

satisfies the Ineq :

\dot{V}\leq-2\beta V^{1-\mu/2},

which is valid in a non trivial interval as
Bx_{0}\neq 0.

It follows that

\langle Bx(t),x(t)\rangle=0,\quad\forall t\geq T_{*}:=\frac{\langle Bx_{0},x_{0}\rangle^{\mu/2}}{2\beta\mu}.

Hence,

\dot{x}=Ax,\quad\forall t\geq T_{*},

which implies

x(t)=S(t-T_{*})x(T_{*}),\quad\forall t\geq T_{*}.

Therefore,

BS(t-T_{*})x(T_{*})=0,\quad\forall t\geq T_{*},

and consequently

BS(t)x(T_{*})=0,\quad\forall t\geq 0.

The observation condition (OBS) then yields
x(T_{*})=0
,
which implies the finite-time stabilization (FTS).

Concluding Remarks

\bullet
While the
additive
FTS control is continuous near the equilibrium, the
bilinear
FTS control is not. However, this discontinuity is compensated by the fact that the control enters the system multiplicatively (through the state).

\bullet
The blow-up phenomenon observed in bilinear control cannot, in general, be avoided, as we can see in the context of the first (elementary) example discussed above, where a positive exponent in the feedback law cannot guarantee the FTS.

\bullet
Blow-up can be avoided in certain situations of FTS where backward uniqueness fails to hold. For example, one can consider the case of nilpotent semigroup
S(t)
, in which situation one can take
u=0
regardless of the choice of
B
(or
B=0
regardless of the choice of
u
).

Some problems that still are open

\bullet
FTS of the heat equation with local control :

For the linear version,

some partial results can be obtained if the global existence of the solution is assumed [Amaliki and Ouzahra, 2026].

\bullet
The second approach to FTS seems to be more suitable for the degenerate case (work in progress) :
