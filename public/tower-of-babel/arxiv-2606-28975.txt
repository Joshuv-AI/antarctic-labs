Cohomological beta function
Oleksandr Gamayun, Maxim Gritskov, Andrey Losev

Abstract
. We propose a cohomological approach to computing the conformal anomaly. Using the example of J ​ J ¯ J\bar{J} -deformations of two-dimensional conformal field theories, we reproduce the well-known Cardy formula for the leading contribution to the perturbative beta function as the coefficient of the cocycle that realizes the obstruction to deforming the Virasoro module structure on the state space. In addition to offering a novel conceptual perspective on the conformal anomaly, the proposed approach is anticipated to provide an efficient tool for computing higher-order coefficients of perturbative beta functions.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

A natural approach to studying deformations of various algebraic structures relies on associating them with a cochain complex. The deformation theory of complexes is well established: infinitesimal deformations correspond to the first cohomology of the adjoint complex, while obstructions manifest as multilinear operations from the first cohomology to the second. These operations endow the cohomology of the adjoint complex with the structure of an
L_{\infty}
-
algebra
.

In this work, to a two-dimensional conformal field theory we associate the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of two Virasoro algebras acting on the space of states. Deformations of the conformal theory triggered by a marginal observable are mapped to certain classes in the first Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology. In quantum field theory, it is well known that the obstructions to deforming a conformal theory are encoded in the coefficients of the
beta function
. Thus, the beta function can be interpreted in a purely algebraic manner as a set of obstructions to deforming the representation of the conformal algebra. Remarkably, it turns out that constructing the cohomological conformal anomaly requires no local data of quantum field theory, such as correlation functions or ultraviolet cutoffs.

We implement this program for
J\bar{J}
-
deformations
of a two-dimensional conformal field theories and demonstrate that the first obstruction to deforming a chiral Virasoro module indeed coincides with the well-known
Cardy formula
for the quadratic contribution to the beta function.

1.2.
Deformation theory of Virasoro modules

Consider a
d=2
conformal field theory with the state space
\mathcal{H}
. That forms a module over the Virasoro algebra. This structure is defined by the action of the generators
L_{m}
and the central element
C
on
\mathcal{H}
, satisfying the commutation relations:

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\delta_{m+n,0}\cdot\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}\cdot C\,.

Under a first-order marginal deformation CFT remains conformal, hence it induces a
deformation of the Virasoro algebra representation
on
\mathcal{H}
. Such deformations and their obstructions are described using methods of homological algebra.

Definition 1
.

The Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the algebra
\mathfrak{Vir}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{H}
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
[
3
]

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{CE}^{\bullet}_{Q}(\mathfrak{Vir},\mathcal{H})=\mathrm{S}^{\bullet}\mathfrak{Vir}^{*}[-1]\otimes\mathcal{H}\,,

equipped with the differential
Q
given by the formula:

(2)

\displaystyle Q=\gamma\,C+\sum_{m}c^{\,m}L_{m}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m,n}(m-n)c^{\,m}c^{\,n}\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{\,m+n}}-

\displaystyle-\sum_{m}\frac{(m^{3}-m)}{24}c^{\,m}c^{\,-m}\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}\,.

Here,
c^{\,m}
are the odd ghost basis elements corresponding to
L_{m}
, while
\gamma
is the ghost variable dual to the central charge
C
.

Definition 2
.

Along with the complex (
1
), we will introduce the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex with values in the adjoint representation
\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{H})
, whose differential is defined as
\{Q,\cdot\}
.

An infinitesimal deformation of the
\mathfrak{Vir}
-module structure on
\mathcal{H}
is encoded in a deformation of the differential
Q
:

(3)

\displaystyle Q\rightarrow Q+g\,\delta^{(1)}Q\,,

where
g
is the deformation parameter. Here,
\delta^{(1)}Q
takes the following form:

(4)

\delta^{(1)}Q=\sum_{m}c^{\,m}\delta^{(1)}L_{m}\,,

where
\delta^{(1)}L_{m}
are endomorphisms of the vector space
\mathcal{H}
. The nilpotency condition for the infinitesimaly deformed differential reads:

(5)

\{Q,\delta^{(1)}Q\}=0\,.

This condition means that
\delta^{(1)}Q
is a
\{Q,\cdot\}
-
cocycle
. It may occur, however, that the first-order deformed representation is
intertwined
with the original one, rendering the deformation trivial. This corresponds to the situation where the cocycle
\delta^{(1)}Q
is
\{Q,\cdot\}
-
exact
:

(6)

\delta^{(1)}Q=\{Q,S\}\,.

Here,
S
is a
0
-cochain in the complex (
1
), or simply an element of
\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{H})
. Thus, the infinitesimal deformations of the
\mathfrak{Vir}
-module structure on the space
\mathcal{H}
are given by the first cohomology of the differential
\{Q,\cdot\}
.

It is useful to rewrite these equations in components. The condition that
\delta^{(1)}Q
is a cocycle is equivalent to an infinite system of equations for
\delta^{(1)}L_{m}
:

(7)

[L_{m},\delta^{(1)}L_{n}]+[\delta^{(1)}L_{m},L_{n}]-(m-n)\,\delta^{(1)}L_{m+n}=0\,.

The exactness condition for the cocycle reads as follows:

(8)

\displaystyle\delta^{(1)}L_{m}=[L_{m},S]\,.

Now that the basic notation has been established, we can formulate our first key statement: marginal observables of a two-dimensional CFT correspond to certain classes in the
first cohomology
of the operator
\{Q,\cdot\}
. The converse, generally speaking, does not hold, as there exist examples of non-marginal deformations of CFT, such as the dilaton deformation
[
4
]
. In this paper, we discuss a particular case of marginal deformations, namely, so-called
current-current
deformations of a CFT
[
5
,
7
]
. The general cocycles associated with these deformations are considered in detail in
Section
2.1
. The most illustrative example is discussed in
Section
2.2
.

In quantum field theory, a marginal deformations are generally obstructed by
conformal anomaly
. While the theory remains conformal at first order in perturbation theory, at second order, quantum effects give rise to a non-vanishing quadratic term of the
beta function

[
2
]
. At the level of the Virasoro algebra representation, this comes from the second cohomological obstruction to deforming the
\mathfrak{Vir}
-module structure on
\mathcal{H}
.

Namely, consider the extension of the marginal deformation to higher orders of perturbation theory:

(9)

Q\rightarrow Q+g\,\delta^{(1)}Q+g^{2}\,\delta^{(2)}Q+g^{3}\,\delta^{(3)}Q+...

In particular, the second-order correction
\delta^{(2)}Q
satisfies the
Maurer-Cartan
equation:

(10)

\{Q,\delta^{(2)}Q\}+\frac{1}{2}\{\delta^{(1)}Q,\delta^{(1)}Q\}=0\,.

The obstruction to the existence of
\delta^{(2)}Q
is given by the cohomology class of the
2
-cocycle
\{\delta^{(1)}Q,\delta^{(1)}Q\}
in
H_{\{Q,\cdot\}}^{2}(\mathfrak{Vir},\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{H}))
. In
Section
2.3
, we explicitly show that for current-current deformations, this cohomology class is proportional to the
Cardy formula

[
2
]
, which determines the quadratic contribution to the beta function in conformal perturbation theory.

2.
Beta function as cohomological obstruction

2.1.
General
J\bar{J}
-cocycles

A special case of marginal CFT deformations is provided by the so-called
current-current
deformations
[
5
,
7
]
.

Definition 3
.

Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with an invariant bilinear form
\eta
, the associated current algebra
\mathfrak{Cur}_{\mathfrak{g},\eta}
is defined as follows. Consider a basis
e_{\alpha}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
f_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}
denote the structure constants of
\mathfrak{g}
in this basis, and let
\eta_{\alpha\beta}
be the matrix elements of the form
\eta
. Then the generators
J_{\alpha(m)}
of the algebra
\mathfrak{Cur}_{\mathfrak{g},\eta}
satisfy the following commutation relations:

(11)

[J_{\alpha(m)},J_{\beta(n)}]=f_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\,J_{\gamma(m+n)}+\eta_{\alpha\beta}\cdot m\cdot\delta_{m+n,0}\,.

Remark
.

The underlying Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is embedded into
\mathfrak{Cur}_{\mathfrak{g},\eta}
as the zero-mode subalgebra spanned by
J_{\alpha(0)}
. If this zero-mode algebra is simple, the invariant form
\eta
can be chosen proportional to be the Killing form. In this case,
\mathrm{Cur}_{\mathfrak{g},\eta}
coincides with the standard affine Kac-Moody algebra
[
8
]
.

Suppose that the state space
\mathcal{H}
, in addition to being a representation space for the Virasoro algebra, also carries a representation of two copies of the current algebra
\mathfrak{Cur}_{\mathfrak{g},\eta}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{Cur}}_{\bar{\mathfrak{g}},\bar{\eta}}
. In general, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
need not coincide with its anti-chiral counterpart
\bar{\mathfrak{g}}
. The relations between the current and Virasoro algebra generators take the following form:

(12)

[L_{m},J_{\alpha(n)}]=-n\,J_{\alpha(m+n)}\,,\quad[L_{m},\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(n)}]=0\,.

Furthermore, we assume that for any state
\ket{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

(13)

J_{\alpha(m)}\ket{\phi}=\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m)}\ket{\phi}=0

for sufficiently large positive
m
.

Remark
.

It is crucial to emphasize that we do not assume the conformal algebra generators to be constructed from
J_{\alpha(m)}
and
\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m)}
, as is the case in the Sugawara construction
[
11
]
. Instead, we merely assume that the CFT under consideration contains the chiral and anti-chiral current algebras.

Early analyses of the deformation of the CFT stress-energy tensor under marginal perturbations were carried out in the context of conformal and string field theory
[
1
,
10
]
. Motivated by their arguments, we arrive at the definition of cocycles governing the deformation of the
\mathfrak{Vir}
-module structure on the space of states
\mathcal{H}
corresponding to a current-current deformation of the CFT, as formulated below.

Definition 4
.

We define the
J\bar{J}
-cocycles as

(14)

\delta^{(1)}Q_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}=\sum_{m}c^{\,m}J_{\alpha(m)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}\,.

Using relations (
12
), it is straightforward to show that it is indeed a
\{Q,\cdot\}
-cocycle. Thus, this cocycle defines an infinitesimal deformation of the differential
Q
:

(15)

Q\rightarrow Q+g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}\delta^{(1)}Q_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}\,.

Here,
g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}
denote the deformation parameters. In terms of the deformations of the Virasoro algebra generators, this reads:

(16)

L_{m}\rightarrow L_{m}+g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}J_{\alpha(m)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}\,.

Remark
.

Strictly speaking, the cocycle considered in
[
1
,
10
]
has the form

(17)

\delta^{(1)}Q_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}=\sum_{m}c^{\,m}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}J_{\alpha(m+k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(k)}\right)\,.

This expression should be understood as an element of the CE complex with values in
\overline{\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{H})}
, where the bar denotes the completion with respect to the weak topology. However, one can show that within the complex
\mathrm{CE}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{Vir},\overline{\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{H})})
, the cocycles (
14
) and (
17
) are equivalent.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose that the space
\mathcal{H}
is equipped with a nondegenerate inner product
\braket{\cdot,\cdot}:\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, and, furthermore,
L_{0}=L_{0}^{\dagger}
is a diagonalizable operator. Then
\delta^{(1)}Q_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}
is not an exact cocycle.

Proof
.

We prove this by contradiction. According to (
8
), exactness of the cocycle
\delta^{(1)}Q_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}
would imply the existence of an operator
S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}
such that

(18)

J_{\alpha(m)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}=[L_{m},S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}]\,.

Suppose there exists an operator
S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}
such that
J_{\alpha(0)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}=[L_{0},S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}]
. Consider
\ket{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}
such that
L_{0}\ket{\phi}=h_{\phi}\ket{\phi}
. Then

\displaystyle\bra{\phi}J_{\alpha(0)}^{\,\dagger}\bar{J}^{\,\dagger}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}J_{\alpha(0)}\ket{\phi}=\bra{\phi}J_{\alpha(0)}^{\,\dagger}\bar{J}^{\,\dagger}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}[L_{0},S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}]\ket{\phi}=

(19)

\displaystyle=\bra{\phi}J_{\alpha(0)}^{\,\dagger}\bar{J}^{\,\dagger}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}L_{0}S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}\ket{\phi}-h_{\phi}\cdot\bra{\phi}J_{\alpha(0)}^{\,\dagger}\bar{J}^{\,\dagger}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}\ket{\phi}.

Using that
L_{0}=L_{0}^{\dagger}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bra{\phi}J_{\alpha(0)}^{\,\dagger}\bar{J}^{\,\dagger}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}L_{0}S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}\ket{\phi}-h_{\phi}\cdot\bra{\phi}J_{\alpha(0)}^{\dagger}\bar{J}^{\dagger}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}\ket{\phi}=

(20)

\displaystyle=h_{\phi}\cdot(\bra{\phi}J_{\alpha(0)}^{\,\dagger}\bar{J}^{\,\dagger}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}\ket{\phi}-\bra{\phi}J_{\alpha(0)}^{\,\dagger}\bar{J}^{\,\dagger}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}\ket{\phi})=0\,.

Since the inner product was assumed to be non-degenerate, it follows that
J_{\alpha(0)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}
acts as zero on the state space
\mathcal{H}
. This is a contradiction.

2.2.
Example: free boson

Let us consider a key example: a free boson whose target space is a circle of radius
R
. In this case, the algebras of chiral and anti-chiral currents are abelian:

(21)

\displaystyle[J_{(m)},J_{(n)}]=[\bar{J}_{(m)},\bar{J}_{(n)}]=m\cdot\delta_{m,-n}\,.

The corresponding representation of the conformal algebra is given by

(22)

\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{V}_{m,n}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{m,n}\,.

The space
\mathcal{V}_{m,n}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{m,n}
is generated by the action of the creation operators
J_{(-k)}
and
\bar{J}_{(-k)}
(
k>0
) on the highest-weight vector
|m,n\rangle
. The zero modes, in turn, act as follows:

(23)

J_{(0)}\ket{m,n}=\left(\frac{m}{R}+\frac{nR}{2}\right)\ket{m,n}\,,\quad\bar{J}_{(0)}\ket{m,n}=\left(\frac{m}{R}-\frac{nR}{2}\right)\ket{m,n}\,.

The chiral
\mathfrak{Vir}
-generators are given by the following expressions
[
6
,
11
]
:

(24)

L_{0}=\frac{1}{2}J_{(0)}^{\,2}+\sum_{n>0}J_{(-n)}J_{(n)}\,,\quad L_{m}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}J_{(-n)}J_{(n+m)}\,.

Consider a deformation induced by an infinitesimal dilatation of the radius:

(25)

R\rightarrow(1-g)\cdot R\,.

Note that, since
R
appears only in the zero-mode expressions (
23
), this rescaling is realized by the following transformation:

J_{(0)}\rightarrow J_{(0)}+g\cdot\bar{J}_{(0)}\,,\quad\bar{J}_{(0)}\rightarrow\bar{J}_{(0)}+g\cdot J_{(0)}\,.

It is worth emphasizing a striking feature of this formula: the correction to the chiral current modes explicitly involves the modes of the anti-chiral currents. Such a deformation of the zero modes induces a deformation of the Virasoro algebra generators, coinciding exactly with the deformation (
16
):

(26)

L_{m}\rightarrow L_{m}+g\cdot J_{(m)}\bar{J}_{(0)}\,.

Clearly, this deformation is non-trivial. Furthermore, in the case at hand, it is free of cohomological obstructions at higher orders and can be unobstructed further to all orders of perturbation theory. Indeed, the radius scaling induces the following transformation of
J_{(0)}
to all orders in
g
:

(27)

J_{(0)}\rightarrow J_{(0)}+g\cdot\bar{J}_{(0)}+\sum_{k\geq 2}\left(\frac{J_{(0)}+\bar{J}_{(0)}}{2}\right)\cdot g^{k}

ubstituting
J_{(0)}
into the Sugawara construction (
24
), we obtain corrections to the chiral Virasoro generators
L_{m}
to all orders in
g
.

Remark
.

It is natural to ask what such a deformation corresponds to in the case of a free boson with a non-compact target space. Due to the presence of a position zero mode
q
satisfying

(28)

[q,J_{(m)}]=[q,\bar{J}_{(m)}]=\delta_{m,0}\,,

the cocycle
J_{(m)}\bar{J}_{(0)}
becomes
exact
, and the corresponding deformation is trivial. This does not contradict the general arguments presented in the proof of
Proposition 1
, as the free boson theory with target space
\mathbb{R}
involves
non-normalizable
highest-weight vectors
[
6
]
.

2.3.
First obstruction and Cardy formula

In this section, we compute the general form of the cocycle whose class constitutes a cohomological obstruction to the
J\bar{J}
-deformation. We will show that this class encodes the second order coefficient of the perturbative
beta-function

[
2
]
.

Thus, we consider a deformation of the
\mathfrak{Vir}
-module along the direction

(29)

\delta^{(1)}Q=g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}\delta^{(1)}Q_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}},,

where
\delta^{(1)}Q_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}
is given by (
14
) and
g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}
denote the deformation parameters. In contrast to the notation introduced in
Section 1.2
, here the deformation parameters are absorbed into the definition of
\delta^{(1)}Q
. Then, according to the equation (
10
), the cohomological obstruction to the deformation along
\delta^{(1)}Q
is given by the class
\{\delta^{(1)}Q,\delta^{(1)}Q\}
. Since this class is
independent
of the choice of a representative for the cohomology class of
\delta^{(1)}Q
, we propose to pass from the deformation defined by (
14
) to an
equivalent
one, determined by an equivalent cocycle.

Proposition 2
.

The cocycle (
14
) is cohomologically equivalent to

(30)

\delta^{(1)}\tilde{Q}_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}=\sum_{m}c^{\,m}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}J_{\alpha(m-k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(k)}\right)\,.

Proof
.

The exactness of the cocycle
\delta^{(1)}Q_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}-\delta^{(1)}\tilde{Q}_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}
means that there exists an operator
S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}
such that

(31)

\sum_{k\neq 0}J_{\alpha(m-k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(k)}=[L_{m},S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}]\,.

And such an operator indeed exists:

(32)

S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}=\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{J_{\alpha(-k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(k)}}{k}\,.

Remark
.

Since
J_{\alpha(m)}\ket{\phi}=\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m)}\ket{\phi}=0
, the cocycle (
30
) is well-defined.

Let us rewrite equation (
10
) for the second-order correction to
Q
in components. Let us introduce the notation:

(33)

\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m}=g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}\sum_{k}J_{\alpha(m-k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(k)}\,.

The cocycle representing the potential obstruction is given by

(34)

\frac{1}{2}\{\delta^{(1)}\tilde{Q},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{Q}\}=\sum_{m<n}c^{\,m}c^{\,n}\cdot[\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{n}]\,.

Then the equation (
10
) for
\delta^{(2)}\tilde{Q}
in components takes the form:

(35)

\displaystyle[L_{m},\delta^{(2)}\tilde{L}_{n}]+[\delta^{(2)}\tilde{L}_{m},L_{n}]-(m-n)\,\delta^{(2)}\tilde{L}_{m+n}-

\displaystyle\ -\,\delta_{m+n,0}\cdot\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}\cdot\delta^{(2)}\tilde{C}+[\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{n}]=0\,.

Our goal now is to evaluate the commutator
[\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{n}]
.

Proposition 3
.

The commutator
[\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{n}]
is given by:

(36)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}f_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\cdot(m-n)\cdot\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}J_{\gamma(m+n-k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k)}-

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\geq 0}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot l\cdot(J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m+l)}-J_{\beta(m-l)}J_{\alpha(n+l)})-

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\leq-1}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot l\cdot(J_{\alpha(m+l)}J_{\beta(n-l)}-J_{\alpha(n+l)}J_{\beta(m-l)})+

\displaystyle+\frac{(m-n)}{2}\cdot\sum_{l\geq 0}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m+n-l)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}+

\displaystyle+\frac{(m-n)}{2}\cdot\sum_{l\leq-1}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m+n-l)}\,.

Proof
.

Let us introduce an approximating sequence:

(37)

\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}=\sum_{k=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}J_{\alpha(m-k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(k)}\,.

Then, it is clear that for any vector
\ket{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

(38)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}([\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\Lambda)}]-[\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{n},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}])\ket{\phi}=

\displaystyle=[\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{n}]\ket{\phi}

for a sufficiently large positive
\Lambda
. Then, we obtain

(39)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}f_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}J_{\gamma(m+n-k-l)}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k+l)}J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m-k)}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m-k)}\cdot k\cdot\delta_{k+l,0}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}\cdot(m-k)\cdot\delta_{m+n-k-l,0}\,.

The first term is symmetric under the exchange of
m
and
n
and therefore does not contribute into the commutator. Subtracting from this expression its counterpart with
m
and
n
interchanged, we obtain:

(40)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}([\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\Lambda)}]-[\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{n},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}])=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k+l)}(J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m-k)}-J_{\beta(m-l)}J_{\alpha(n-k)})+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot k\cdot\delta_{k+l,0}(J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m-k)}-J_{\beta(m-l)}J_{\alpha(n-k)})+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}\cdot(m-n)\cdot\delta_{m+n-k-l,0}\,.

Next, we commute the current algebra generators within these sums to compute the limit as
\Lambda\rightarrow\infty
. Now, let us turn to the second term:

(41)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot k\cdot\delta_{k+l,0}(J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m-k)}-J_{\beta(m-l)}J_{\alpha(n-k)})=

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot l\cdot(J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m+l)}-J_{\beta(m-l)}J_{\alpha(n+l)})=

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=0}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot l\cdot(J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m+l)}-J_{\beta(m-l)}J_{\alpha(n+l)})-

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{-1}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot l\cdot(J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m+l)}-J_{\beta(m-l)}J_{\alpha(n+l)})\,.

In the second sum, we commute the generators
J_{\alpha(m+l)}
and
J_{\alpha(n+l)}
to the far left:

(42)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot k\cdot\delta_{k+l,0}(J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m-k)}-J_{\beta(m-l)}J_{\alpha(n-k)})=

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=0}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot l\cdot(J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m+l)}-J_{\beta(m-l)}J_{\alpha(n+l)})-

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{-1}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot l\cdot(J_{\alpha(m+l)}J_{\beta(n-l)}-J_{\alpha(n+l)}J_{\beta(m-l)})-

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{-1}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\cdot l\cdot\left((n-l)-(m-l)\right)\cdot\delta_{m+n,0}\,.

Due to the last term growing indefinitely as
\Lambda\rightarrow\infty
, this contribution does not have a limit on its own. However, as we will shortly see, it cancels out with a similar term originating from the third sum of (
40
). Now, we treat the third term in (
40
) in the same manner:

(43)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}\cdot(m-n)\cdot\delta_{m+n-k-l,0}=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m+n-l)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}\cdot(m-n)=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=0}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m+n-l)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}\cdot(m-n)+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{-1}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m+n-l)}\cdot(m-n)+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{-1}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot(m+n-l)\cdot\delta_{m+n,0}\cdot(m-n)\,.

The last terms in (
42
) and (
43
) exactly cancel each other, and we obtain

(44)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}([\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\Lambda)}]-[\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{n},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m}^{(\Lambda)}])=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k+l)}(J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m-k)}-J_{\beta(m-l)}J_{\alpha(n-k)})-

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=0}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot l\cdot(J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m+l)}-J_{\beta(m-l)}J_{\alpha(n+l)})-

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{-1}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot l\cdot(J_{\alpha(m+l)}J_{\beta(n-l)}-J_{\alpha(n+l)}J_{\beta(m-l)})+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=0}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m+n-l)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}\cdot(m-n)+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{-1}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m+n-l)}\cdot(m-n)\,.

Next, we turn to the first sum in (
40
), which is the antisymmetric part of the following expression:

(45)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k+l)}J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m-k)}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=n+1}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k+l)}J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m-k)}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{n}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k+l)}J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m-k)}\,.

Now, in the second sum, we commute
J_{\beta(n-l)}
and
J_{\alpha(m-k)}
:

(46)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k+l)}J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m-k)}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=n+1}^{\Lambda}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k+l)}J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m-k)}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{n}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k+l)}J_{\alpha(m-k)}J_{\beta(n-l)}-

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{n}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}f_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k+l)}J_{\gamma(m+n-k-l)}\,.

The limit of the first two terms as
\Lambda\rightarrow\infty
exists and is symmetric under the exchange of
m
and
n
, and therefore does not contribute. Then

(47)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=-\Lambda}^{n}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}f_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k+l)}J_{\gamma(m+n-k-l)}=

\displaystyle=(\Lambda-n)\cdot\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}f_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k)}J_{\gamma(m+n-k)}\,.

After subtracting the counterpart with
m
and
n
interchanged,
\Lambda
cancels out.

Putting everything together, we obtain:

(48)

\displaystyle[\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{n}]=\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}f_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\cdot(m-n)\cdot\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}J_{\gamma(m+n-k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(k)}-

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\geq 0}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot l\cdot(J_{\beta(n-l)}J_{\alpha(m+l)}-J_{\beta(m-l)}J_{\alpha(n+l)})-

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\leq-1}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot l\cdot(J_{\alpha(m+l)}J_{\beta(n-l)}-J_{\alpha(n+l)}J_{\beta(m-l)})+

\displaystyle+\frac{(m-n)}{2}\cdot\sum_{l\geq 0}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m+n-l)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}+

\displaystyle+\frac{(m-n)}{2}\cdot\sum_{l\leq-1}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m+n-l)}\,.

Proposition 4
.

In formula (
48
), all terms except the first are cohomologically trivial. That means that they can be eliminated from the equation

(49)

\displaystyle+[\delta^{(2)}\tilde{L}_{m},L_{n}]-(m-n)\,\delta^{(2)}\tilde{L}_{m+n}-

\displaystyle\ -\,\delta_{m+n,0}\cdot\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}\cdot\delta^{(2)}\tilde{C}+[\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{m},\delta^{(1)}\tilde{L}_{n}]=0

by a shift of
\delta^{(2)}\tilde{L}_{m}
.

Proof
.

Indeed, a direct calculation shows that substituting

(50)

\displaystyle\delta^{(2)}\tilde{L}_{m}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\geq 0}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot J_{\beta(-l)}J_{\alpha(m+l)}-

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\leq-1}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\cdot J_{\alpha(m+l)}J_{\beta(-l)}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{l\geq 0}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m-l)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{l\leq-1}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\bar{J}_{\bar{\beta}(l)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(m-l)}

eliminates the contributions of the last four terms of the cocycle (
48
).

Proposition 5
.

Consider the 2-cocycle

(51)

\sum_{m<n}c^{\,m}c^{\,n}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(m-n)J_{\alpha(m+n-k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(k)}\right)\,.

It is cohomologically equivalent to the cocycle

(52)

\sum_{m<n}c^{\,m}c^{\,n}\cdot(m-n)\cdot J_{\alpha(m+n)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}\,.

Proof
.

A direct calculation shows that the difference between cocycles (
51
) and (
52
) is equal to
\{Q,S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}\}
, where the 1-cochain
S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}
is given by

(53)

S_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}=\sum_{m}c^{\,m}\left(\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{m}{k}\cdot J_{\alpha(m-k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(k)}\right)\,.

We now turn to a discussion of our results. We have shown that the cocycle corresponding to the obstruction to the
J\bar{J}
-deformation is given by

(54)

\beta_{2}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}f_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\,\bar{\gamma}}\cdot\sum_{m<n}c^{\,m}c^{\,n}\cdot(m-n)\cdot J_{\gamma(m+n)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\gamma}(0)}\,.

Strikingly, this expression is proportional to a remarkable structure that coincides with
Cardy formula
for the leading contribution to the beta function:

(55)

\beta_{2}^{\gamma\bar{\gamma}}(g)=\frac{1}{2}\,g^{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}g^{\beta\bar{\beta}}f_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\bar{f}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}^{\bar{\gamma}}\,.

From a field-theoretic point of view, it is well known that a non-vanishing beta function
\beta_{2}^{\gamma\bar{\gamma}}(g)
constitutes an obstruction to preserving conformal symmetry of the deformed theory at second order in perturbation theory. Therefore, its appearance on the r.h.s. of equation (
35
) hints that this equation does not have a solution. Indeed, the following proposition holds.

Proposition 6
.

The cocycle

(56)

\sum_{m<n}c^{\,m}c^{\,n}\cdot(m-n)\cdot J_{\alpha(m+n)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}\,

is not exact.

Proof
.

Consider the operator
b_{0}=\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{\,0}}
of degree
-1
on
\mathrm{CE}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{Vir},\mathcal{H})
. Then

(57)

\{Q,b_{0}\}=L_{0}+\sum_{k}k\cdot c^{\,k}\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{\,k}}\,.

Let us denote the right-hand side by
d
. Then, we notice that

(58)

\{Q,d\}=\{d,b_{0}\}=0\,,

which implies that the complex
\mathrm{CE}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{Vir},\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{H}))
splits into a direct sum of
\mathrm{ker}^{\bullet}(\{d,\cdot\})
and an acyclic component (since
\{Q,\cdot\}
is inverted there by means of
\{b_{0},\cdot\}
). The cocycles (
14
) and (
56
) lie in the component
\mathrm{ker}^{\bullet}(\{d,\cdot\})
.

Now, we notice that within the subcomplex
\mathrm{ker}^{\bullet}(\{d,\cdot\})
, the following relation holds:

(59)

\{Q,\{b_{0},\cdot\}\}=\{b_{0},\{Q,\cdot\}\}\,.

Thus,
\{b_{0},\cdot\}
induces a well-defined map on the cohomology of
\{Q,\cdot\}
, as it maps exact cochains to exact ones and closed cochains to closed ones. Now, suppose for a moment that the cocycle (
56
) were exact. This would imply that its image under
\{b_{0},\cdot\}
must also be exact. On the other hand

(60)

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m,n}\{b_{0},c^{\,m}c^{\,n}\cdot(m-n)\}\cdot J_{\alpha(m+n)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}=-\sum_{k}c^{k}\cdot k\cdot J_{\alpha(k)}\bar{J}_{\bar{\alpha}(0)}\,.

This cocycle, in turn, is precisely the Chodos-Thorn cocycle
[
4
]
. It is non-trivial because, for instance, if the indices
\alpha
correspond to a commutative subalgebra, this deformation is not obstructed and shifts the central charge at second order.

Remark
.

It is also worth noting that the terms containing
\eta_{\alpha\beta}
and
\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}
turn out to be exact and do not contribute to the cohomology class of the obstruction. Much like their vanishing contributions to the OPE structure constants in the marginal sector, they consequently fail to affect the leading order of the quantum-field-theoretic beta function.

3.
Conclusions and Discussion

In this paper, we have established a rigorous algebraic framework for
J\bar{J}
-deformations of two-dimensional conformal field theories by explicitly constructing the deformed chiral
\mathfrak{Vir}
-module structure. Furthermore, we demonstrated that the field-theoretic beta function at the leading order is naturally recovered from the Chevalley-Eilenberg cocycle governing the deformation obstructions.

Generally speaking, conformal symmetry in a two-dimensional quantum field theory manifests as the symmetry of
two copies
of the Virasoro algebra: the chiral
\mathfrak{Vir}
and the antichiral
\overline{\mathfrak{Vir}}
. Consequently, along with the chiral differential
Q
, there is also an antichiral component
\bar{Q}
, and these differentials
commute
with one another. A marginal deformation of the theory leads to a consistent joint deformation of both
Q
and
\bar{Q}
. The requirement that the deformed differentials remain commuting is non-trivial; however, based on preliminary calculations, we expect that no new structures will arise from this constraint. A detailed analysis of this coupled deformation and the full non-chiral setting will be presented in a forthcoming paper.

A natural next step is the construction of higher-order coefficients of the beta function as
higher cohomological obstructions
to the deformation of the
\mathfrak{Vir}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{Vir}}
action on the space of states
\mathcal{H}
. To achieve this, one needs to explicitly construct a
homotopy

h
for the operator
\{Q+\bar{Q},\cdot\}
or, equivalently, an explicit decomposition of the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex into its cohomology and an acyclic subcomplex. If we view the obstructions as maps

(61)

\mu_{n}:(H^{1}_{\{Q,\cdot\}})^{\otimes n}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\{Q,\cdot\}}\,,

the higher obstructions are constructed iteratively by means of the homotopy operator. Namely, the second obstruction is simply given by
\mu_{2}=\{\cdot,\cdot\}
, and the matrix element of this map evaluated for the cocycles
\delta^{(1)}Q_{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}
has been computed in
Section 2.1
. Then, for example, the third obstruction is given by the formula

(62)

\mu_{3}=\{\cdot,h(\{\cdot,\cdot\})\}\,.

Based on preliminary calculations, we expect that such a homotopy can be chosen in a way that allows the cohomological subcomplex to contain a marginal subcomplex spanned by the cocycles (
14
) and (
56
).

In the case of
J\bar{J}
-deformations, we expect this approach to provide a rigorous proof of certain physical conjectures regarding beta functions. For instance, when either
\eta_{\alpha\beta}
or
\bar{\eta}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}
vanishes, the beta function is known to be polynomial, terminating at the third order. From the viewpoint of deformation theory, this would correspond to the vanishing of all higher obstructions. This is the case, for example, in supersymmetric theories.

Finally, we anticipate a deep relationship between the cocycles (
14
) and (
56
), reminiscent of the structures arising in BRST-symmetric theories
[
9
]
. Namely, we expect that there exists a canonical one-to-one correspondence between marginal 1-cocycles and 2-cocycles. Such a connection would allow one to interpret the
L_{\infty}
-
identities
as quadratic relations on the coefficients of the beta function.
