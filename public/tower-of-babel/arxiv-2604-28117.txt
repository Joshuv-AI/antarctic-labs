Strong Mpemba Effect Through a Reentrant Phase Transition
Kristian Blom, Doron Benyamin, Uwe Thiele, Oren Raz, Aljaž Godec

Abstract
We investigate temperature quenches across the reentrant phase transition of the antiferromagnetic Ising model in a magnetic field and show that the strong direct and inverse Mpemba effects arise when quenches terminate in the paramagnetic phase. These anomalous relaxation phenomena originate from the selective excitation of the slowest relaxation mode, which in the paramagnetic phase is purely staggered. Consequently, quenches starting from the paramagnetic phase have zero overlap with the slow mode and exhibit a strong (inverse) Mpemba effect. Quenches from the antiferromagnetic phase excite the staggered mode and display a slow-relaxation tail. By varying the lattice coordination number we show that the strong Mpemba effect disappears in the absence of reentrance. Our results provide the first demonstration of the strong (inverse) Mpemba effect in the antiferromagnetic Ising model based on the pair-approximation, and establish a link between anomalous relaxation and the equilibrium phase behavior.

Lattice setup and dynamics.
—We consider the Ising model on a Bethe lattice with coordination number

a_{\rm slow}(T_{\rm i},T_{\rm f})=s_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i})
. This selective excitation of the staggered
relaxation mode is the origin of both the Mpemba and inverse Mpemba
effects. As evidenced by the
qualitatively different
relaxation trajectories in the insets of Fig.
2
a,b, the state with nonzero staggered magnetization relaxes with a finite projection onto the slow staggered mode. As a result, one trajectory exhibits the slow exponential decay associated with

\lambda_{\rm slow}
, while the other follows the faster asymptotic
decay shown by the dashed lines in Fig.
2
b. Hence, although the former may initially lie
closer to equilibrium, it is eventually overtaken by the state that
does not excite the slow mode, producing a crossing in

D(t)
. To see that this is a genuinely far-from-equilibrium effect,
note that a spontaneous fluctuation producing antiferromagnetic
order in the paramagnetic phase has vanishing probability, such
that Onsager’s regression hypothesis fails.

Conclusion.
—We have shown that reentrant phase transitions
provide a natural mechanism for anomalous relaxation in interacting
spin systems. Using the BG description of Glauber dynamics, we
demonstrated that the Mpemba and inverse Mpemba effects arise from the
selective excitation of the staggered relaxation mode when quenches
terminate in the paramagnetic phase. The reentrant topology of the
phase diagram plays a crucial role, as it enables quenches between two
paramagnetic states with qualitatively different projections onto the
slowest mode. Several questions remain open.
In particular, it would
be interesting to understand more deepely the fluctuations around the
average order parameters. Another important direction is the construction of a
spatially extended dynamical field theory for the antiferromagnetic
Ising model. While for ferromagnetic systems with single spin-flip
dynamics such descriptions lead to Cahn–Hilliard-type
equations
Blom et al. 2026
;
Penrose 1991
;
Ducastelle 1996
;
Blom et al. 2023
, no analogous framework
is currently available that consistently incorporates the staggered
magnetization as an independent slow field. Establishing such a theory
would be a key step toward understanding spatially resolved relaxation
and pattern formation in antiferromagnetic systems.

Appendix A: Proof of the Lyapunov property.
—Using the chain rule, the time derivative of the BG free energy given by Eq. (
8
) is (omitting the explicit arguments of
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x};T)
)

\frac{{\rm d}\mathcal{F}}{{\rm d}t}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial q}\frac{{\rm d}q}{{\rm d}t}+\sum_{\mu}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial m^{\mu}}\frac{{\rm d}m^{\mu}}{{\rm d}t}.

(A1)

Substituting Eq. (
7
) gives

\frac{{\rm d}\mathcal{F}}{{\rm d}t}=2\sum_{\mu}\sum_{l=0}^{z}\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial m^{\mu}}+\alpha_{l}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial q}\right](w_{l}^{\mu-}-w_{l}^{\mu+}),

(A2)

which can be rewritten
using the local detailed-balance relation (
10
) as

\frac{{\rm d}\mathcal{F}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{1}{2\beta}\sum_{\mu}\sum_{l=0}^{z}\bigl(w_{l}^{\mu-}-w_{l}^{\mu+}\bigr)\ln\biggl(\frac{w_{l}^{\mu-}}{w_{l}^{\mu+}}\biggr)\leq 0,

(A3)

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
\beta\geq 0
,
w_{l}^{\mu\pm}\geq 0
, and for any
x,y>0
one has

(x-y)\ln{(x/y)}\geq 0
. Hence
\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x};T)
given by Eq. (
8
) is a Lyapunov function of the BG dynamics (
7
).

Appendix B: Monotonicity of the excess free energy.
—Here we show that the initial excess free energy

\Delta\mathcal{F}(T_{\rm i},T_{\rm f})\equiv\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i});T_{\rm f})-\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm f});T_{\rm f})

(B1)

increases monotonically with
|T_{\rm i}-T_{\rm f}|
. By construction,
\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm f})
minimizes
\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x};T_{\rm f})
, so that
\Delta\mathcal{F}(T_{\rm i},T_{\rm f})\geq 0
with equality only for
T_{\rm i}=T_{\rm f}
. To determine how
\Delta\mathcal{F}(T_{\rm i},T_{\rm f})
varies with the initial temperature, we differentiate Eq. (
B1
) with respect to
T_{\rm i}
at fixed
T_{\rm f}
. Since only the first term depends on
T_{\rm i}
, the chain rule gives

\partial_{T_{\rm i}}\Delta\mathcal{F}(T_{\rm i},T_{\rm f})=\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i});T_{\rm f})^{\rm T}\frac{{\rm d}\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i})}{{\rm d}T_{\rm i}},

(B2)

where
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\equiv(\partial_{m},\partial_{s},\partial_{q})^{\rm T}
with
\partial_{x}\equiv\partial/\partial x
, and we use the shorthand notation

\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i});T_{\rm f})\equiv\left.\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x};T_{\rm f})\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i})}.

(B3)

Using the decomposition
\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x};T)=\mathcal{U}(\boldsymbol{x})-T\mathcal{S}_{\rm BG}(\boldsymbol{x})
where
\mathcal{U}(\boldsymbol{x})
is the internal energy,
together with the equilibrium condition
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i});T_{\rm i})=0
,
we can rewrite the gradient in Eq. (
B2
) as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i});T_{\rm f})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}[\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i});T_{\rm f})-\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i});T_{\rm i})]

\displaystyle=(T_{\rm i}-T_{\rm f})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{S}_{\rm BG}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i})).

(B4)

Substituting this into Eq. (
B2
) yields

\partial_{T_{\rm i}}\Delta\mathcal{F}(T_{\rm i},T_{\rm f})=(T_{\rm i}{-}T_{\rm f})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{S}_{\rm BG}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i}))^{\rm T}\frac{{\rm d}\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i})}{{\rm d}T_{\rm i}}.

(B5)

To proceed further, we differentiate the equilibrium condition

\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T);T)=0

with respect to
T
. This gives

\mathbf{H}(T)\frac{{\rm d}\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T)}{{\rm d}T}+\partial_{T}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T);T)=0,

(B6)

where
\mathbf{H}(T)
is the Hessian of the free energy evaluated at equilibrium,

\mathbf{H}_{ij}(T)=\left.\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x};T)\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T)},\ i,j\in\{m,s,q\}.

(B7)

Since
\partial_{T}\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x};T)=-\mathcal{S}_{\rm BG}(\boldsymbol{x})
, it follows that

\partial_{T}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T);T)=-\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{S}_{\rm BG}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T)).

(B8)

Hence, since
\mathbf{H}(T)
is invertible at (stable) equilibrium

\frac{{\rm d}\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T)}{{\rm d}T}=\mathbf{H}^{-1}(T)\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{S}_{\rm BG}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T)).

(B9)

Substituting Eq. (
B9
) into Eq. (
B5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{T_{\rm i}}\Delta\mathcal{F}(T_{\rm i},T_{\rm f})=(T_{\rm i}-T_{\rm f})\times

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{S}_{\rm BG}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i}))^{\rm T}\mathbf{H}^{-1}(T_{\rm i})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{S}_{\rm BG}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm i})).

(B10)

Along a stable equilibrium branch, the Hessian
\mathbf{H}(T_{\rm i})
is positive definite, so the quadratic form on the right-hand side of Eq. (
B10
) is nonnegative. Moreover, the equilibrium order parameters
\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T)
remain continuous across the phase boundary (see Fig.
1
), and since
\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x};T)
is continuous in both
\boldsymbol{x}
and
T
, the excess free energy
\Delta\mathcal{F}(T_{\rm i},T_{\rm f})
is continuous in
T_{\rm i}
. Thus, although
\partial_{T_{\rm i}}\Delta\mathcal{F}
may be nonanalytic at the phase boundary,
\Delta\mathcal{F}
itself cannot jump. Combined with Eq. (
B10
), this implies that
\Delta\mathcal{F}(T_{\rm i},T_{\rm f})
is minimized at
T_{\rm i}=T_{\rm f}
and increases monotonically with
|T_{\rm i}-T_{\rm f}|
over the full temperature range considered.

Appendix C: Linearized dynamics.—
Linearizing Eq. (
7

\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm f})=(m_{\rm eq},s_{\rm eq},q_{\rm eq})
and using Eq. (
10

\frac{{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t}}{{\rm d}t}=\mathbf{M}\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t}=-\beta_{\rm f}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{H}\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t},

(C1)

where
\beta_{\rm f}=1/k_{\rm B}T_{\rm f}

\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{H}(T_{\rm f})
denotes the Hessian of the free energy evaluated at the post-quench temperature [see Eq. (
B7

\mathbf{L}(T)=\sum_{\mu,l}w^{\mu}_{l,{\rm eq}}(T)\mathbf{c}_{l}^{\mu}(\mathbf{c}_{l}^{\mu})^{\rm T},

Appendix D: Spectrum in the paramagnetic phase.—
Here and in the following, we use the shorthand notation

\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm eq}\equiv\left.\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{x};T_{\rm f})\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm f})},\ i,j\in\{m,s,q\},

(D1)

where
T_{\rm f}
is assumed to be chosen in the paramagnetic phase with

w_{l,{\rm eq}}^{a}=w_{l,{\rm eq}}^{b}

0&S_{0}&0\\
2S_{1}&0&4S_{2}\end{pmatrix},

(D2)

(D3)

{\rm det}(\mathbf{L})=4S_{0}(S_{0}S_{2}-S_{1}^{2})

\mathbf{L}

\partial_{q}\partial_{s}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm eq}=0

\beta_{\rm f}\mathbf{H}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial^{2}_{m}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm eq}&0&\partial_{m}\partial_{q}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm eq}\\

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\left(\!1,0,\frac{\lambda_{\mp}{+}2S_{1}\partial_{m}\partial_{q}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm eq}{+}4S_{2}\partial_{q}^{2}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm eq}}{S_{0}\partial_{m}\partial_{q}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm eq}{+}2S_{1}\partial_{q}^{2}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm eq}}\!\right)^{\rm T},

\!\!\!\lambda_{\pm}{=}{-}2\left(\!\frac{S_{0}}{4}\partial_{m}^{2}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm eq}{+}S_{1}\partial_{m}\partial_{q}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm eq}{+}S_{2}\partial_{q}^{2}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\rm eq}\!\right)\!{\pm}\sqrt{\Delta},

Appendix S1
Derivation of the dynamical equations

In this section we derive the dynamical equations used in the main text. We start from microscopic single-spin-flip Glauber dynamics and then apply the Bethe-Guggenheim (BG) approximation to obtain a closed set of equations for the macroscopic variables
\boldsymbol{x}=(m,s,q)
. Let
P(\boldsymbol{\sigma};t)
denote the probability of finding the system in the spin configuration

\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\{\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{N}\}
at time
t
. This probability evolves according to the master equation

\frac{{\rm d}P(\boldsymbol{\sigma};t)}{{\rm d}t}=\sum_{i}[w(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma})P(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i};t)-w(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i})P(\boldsymbol{\sigma};t)],

(S1)

where

\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i}=\{\sigma_{1},...,-\sigma_{i},...,\sigma_{N}\}

is the configuration obtained from
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
by flipping the spin at site
i
. The transition rates satisfy detailed balance with respect to the Boltzmann distribution associated with
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
. Assuming nearest-neighbor interactions and spatial isotropy, this gives
Glauber 1963

w(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i})=\left[1-\tanh{\left(\beta\Delta\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i})/2\right)}\right]/2\tau,

(S2)

where
\tau
is an intrinsic timescale for a spin-flip attempt,
\beta\equiv 1/k_{\rm B}T
is the inverse thermal energy, and
\Delta\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i})
denotes the change in energy when spin
i
is flipped in the configuration
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
. Using the Ising Hamiltonian, this energy change can be written as

\Delta\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i})\equiv\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i})-\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})=2\sigma_{i}\left(J\sum\nolimits_{\langle ij\rangle}\sigma_{j}+H\right).

(S3)

From the master equation (
S1
) one directly obtains evolution equations for the first two spin moments (see also Eqs. (28) and (29) in
Glauber 1963
):

\displaystyle\tau\frac{{\rm d}\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle(t)}{{\rm d}t}+\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle(t)

\displaystyle=\langle\sigma_{i}\tanh{(\beta\Delta\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i})/2)}\rangle(t),,

(S4)

\displaystyle\tau\frac{{\rm d}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle(t)}{{\rm d}t}+2\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle(t)

\displaystyle=\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}[\tanh{(\beta\Delta\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i})/2)}+\tanh{(\beta\Delta\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{j})/2)}]\rangle(t).

(S5)

Here, the averaging brackets denote averaging w.r.t.
P(\boldsymbol{\sigma};t)
, i.e.,

\langle f\rangle(t)\equiv\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}P(\boldsymbol{\sigma};t)f(\boldsymbol{\sigma}),

(S6)

for any function
f(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
.
Equations (
S4
) and (
S5
) are exact, but they are

not
yet closed because their right-hand sides still involve
higher-order correlations. They provide the starting point for the
derivation of the macroscopic dynamics of
\boldsymbol{x}=(m,s,q)
.

We now derive closed equations for the order parameters within the BG approximation. As in the main text, we partition the lattice into two interpenetrating sublattices
\Lambda^{a}
and
\Lambda^{b}
. Summing Eq. (
S4
) over all spins on a given sublattice yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tau\frac{{\rm d}m^{a}(t)}{{\rm d}t}+m^{a}(t)&=\frac{1}{|\Lambda^{a}|}\sum_{i\in\Lambda^{a}}\langle\sigma_{i}\tanh{(\beta\Delta\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i})/2)}\rangle(t),\\
\tau\frac{{\rm d}m^{b}(t)}{{\rm d}t}+m^{b}(t)&=\frac{1}{|\Lambda^{b}|}\sum_{i\in\Lambda^{b}}\langle\sigma_{i}\tanh{(\beta\Delta\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i})/2)}\rangle(t).\end{split}

(S7)

The task is therefore to evaluate the averages on the right-hand side. A useful simplification follows from Eq. (
S3
). Since
\tanh(\sigma_{i}x)=\sigma_{i}\tanh(x)
for
\sigma_{i}=\pm 1
, we obtain

\langle\sigma_{i}\tanh{(\beta\Delta\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i})/2)}\rangle{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{dH}}}{{=}}}\langle\tanh{(\beta[J\sum\nolimits_{\langle ij\rangle}\sigma_{j}+H])}\rangle.

(S8)

Thus, the right-hand side depends only on the local environment of site
i
, i.e., on the number of up spins among its
z
nearest neighbors. Because the sum
\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\sigma_{j}
can only take the discrete values
-z,-z+2,\dots,z-2,z
, we write

\beta J\sum\nolimits_{\langle ij\rangle}\sigma_{j}+\beta H=(2l-z)\beta J+\beta H,

(S9)

where
l\in\{0,...,z\}
denotes the number of neighboring up spins. The expectation value in Eq. (
S8
) can therefore be decomposed into contributions from the different values of
l
. This gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{|\Lambda^{a}|}\sum_{i\in\Lambda^{a}}\langle\sigma_{i}\tanh{(\beta\Delta\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i})/2)}\rangle&=\sum_{l=0}^{z}\mathcal{P}^{a}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\tanh{([2l-z]\beta J+\beta H)},\\
\frac{1}{|\Lambda^{b}|}\sum_{i\in\Lambda^{b}}\langle\sigma_{i}\tanh{(\beta\Delta\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{i})/2)}\rangle&=\sum_{l=0}^{z}\mathcal{P}^{b}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\tanh{([2l-z]\beta J+\beta H)},\end{split}

(S10)

where
\mathcal{P}^{a}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})
and

\mathcal{P}^{b}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})
denote the probabilities that a
spin on sublattice
a
or
b
has exactly
l
neighboring up spins in
a macrostate characterized by
\boldsymbol{x}=(m,s,q)
. We now
evaluate these probabilities within the BG approximation.

For definiteness, consider
\mathcal{P}^{a}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})
. We first split it according to whether the central spin is up or down:

\mathcal{P}^{a}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})=\mathcal{P}^{a+}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})+\mathcal{P}^{a-}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x}).

(S11)

To obtain explicit expressions, we introduce the numbers of nearest-neighbor pairs of the various types:

\displaystyle N^{ab}_{\pm\pm}

\displaystyle={\rm total\ number\ of\ (\pm,\pm)\ n.n.\ pairs},

\displaystyle N^{ab}_{\pm\mp}

\displaystyle={\rm total\ number\ of\ (\pm,\mp)\ n.n.\ pairs}.

These quantities are extensive, i.e., they scale with the total system size
N
. The BG approximation now assumes that nearest-neighbor pairs are randomly distributed throughout the lattice, subject only to the fixed macroscopic values of the relevant densities. Under this assumption,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{P}^{a+}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})&=\frac{N^{a}_{+}}{N/2}\times\binom{N^{ab}_{++}}{l}\binom{N^{ab}_{+-}}{z-l}/\binom{N^{ab}_{++}+N^{ab}_{+-}}{z},\\
\mathcal{P}^{a-}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})&=\frac{N^{a}_{-}}{N/2}\times\binom{N^{ab}_{-+}}{l}\binom{N^{ab}_{--}}{z-l}/\binom{N^{ab}_{-+}+N^{ab}_{--}}{z},\end{split}

(S12)

where we used
|\Lambda^{a}|=|\Lambda^{b}|=N/2
, and
N^{a}_{\pm}
denotes the total number of up/down spins on sublattice
\Lambda^{a}
, given by

N^{a}_{\pm}=N^{ab}_{\pm+}+N^{ab}_{\pm-}.

(S13)

To rewrite everything in terms of the order parameters, we next express the pair numbers through
(m^{a},m^{b},q)
. By definition,

q\equiv\frac{2}{zN}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle=\frac{2}{zN}(N^{ab}_{++}+N^{ab}_{--}-N^{ab}_{+-}-N^{ab}_{-+}),

(S14)

where the factor of
2/zN
on the right-hand side represents the total number of pairs on a lattice with coordination number
z
, i.e.

zN/2=N^{ab}_{++}+N^{ab}_{--}+N^{ab}_{+-}+N^{ab}_{-+}.

(S15)

Likewise, the magnetizations on the two sublattices can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}m^{a}&=\frac{2}{zN}\left(N^{ab}_{++}+N^{ab}_{+-}-N^{ab}_{-+}-N^{ab}_{--}\right),\\
m^{b}&=\frac{2}{zN}\left(N^{ab}_{++}+N^{ab}_{-+}-N^{ab}_{+-}-N^{ab}_{--}\right).\end{split}

(S16)

Together, Eqs. (
S14
)–(
S16
) form four linear equations for the four unknowns
\{N^{ab}_{++},N^{ab}_{+-},N^{ab}_{-+},N^{ab}_{--}\}
. Solving them gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}N^{ab}_{\pm\pm}&=(zN/8)(1\pm m^{a}\pm m^{b}+q),\\
N^{ab}_{\pm\mp}&=(zN/8)(1\pm m^{a}\mp m^{b}-q).\end{split}

(S17)

Substituting Eqs. (
S17
) into Eq. (
S12
) gives
\mathcal{P}^{a\pm}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})
in closed form in terms of
(m^{a},m^{b},q)
, and therefore also in terms of
\boldsymbol{x}=(m,s,q)
through the relations

\displaystyle\begin{split}m&\equiv(m^{a}+m^{b})/2,\\
s&\equiv(m^{a}-m^{b})/2.\end{split}

(S18)

The final simplification is obtained in the thermodynamic limit

N\to\infty
, where we use the following asymptotic result for the binomial coefficient

\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\binom{Nx}{l}=\frac{(Nx)^{l}}{l!}+\mathcal{O}(N^{l-1}),\ l\in\mathbb{N}.

(S19)

This in turn yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{P}^{a+}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})&\simeq\binom{z}{l}\frac{(1+m^{a})^{1-z}}{2^{z+1}}\frac{(1+m^{a}+m^{b}+q)^{l}}{(1+m^{a}-m^{b}-q)^{l-z}},\\
\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{P}^{a-}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})&\simeq\binom{z}{l}\frac{(1-m^{a})^{1-z}}{2^{z+1}}\frac{(1-m^{a}+m^{b}-q)^{l}}{(1-m^{a}-m^{b}+q)^{l-z}}.\end{split}

(S20)

The corresponding expressions for
\mathcal{P}^{b\pm}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})
follow by exchanging
a\leftrightarrow b
. Since
q
appears explicitly in Eqs. (
S20
), we also need an equation for its time evolution. This can be obtained from Eq. (
S5
) in the same way as above. The resulting BG dynamics is the closed system of three coupled autonomous ordinary differential equations

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tau\frac{{\rm d}m^{a}(t)}{{\rm d}t}+m^{a}(t)&=\sum_{l=0}^{z}\mathcal{P}^{a}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\tanh{([2l-z]\beta J+\beta H)},\\
\tau\frac{{\rm d}m^{b}(t)}{{\rm d}t}+m^{b}(t)&=\sum_{l=0}^{z}\mathcal{P}^{b}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\tanh{([2l-z]\beta J+\beta H)},\\
\tau\frac{{\rm d}q(t)}{{\rm d}t}+2q(t)&=\sum_{l=0}^{z}\alpha_{l}[\mathcal{P}^{a}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})+\mathcal{P}^{b}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})]\tanh{([2l-z]\beta J+\beta H)},\end{split}

(S21)

where
\alpha_{l}\equiv 2l/z-1
. To recover the compact form used in the main text, it is convenient to introduce the coarse-grained transition rates

w_{l}^{\mu\pm}(\boldsymbol{x};T)\equiv\mathcal{P}^{\mu\pm}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})[1\mp\tanh([2l-z]\tilde{J}+\tilde{H})]/2\tau,\qquad\mu\in\{a,b\},

(S22)

with
\tilde{J}=\beta J
and
\tilde{H}=\beta H
. The probabilities
\mathcal{P}^{\mu\pm}_{l}
satisfy the sum rules

\sum_{l=0}^{z}[\mathcal{P}^{\mu+}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})-\mathcal{P}^{\mu-}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})]=m^{\mu},\qquad\sum_{l=0}^{z}\alpha_{l}[\mathcal{P}^{\mu+}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})-\mathcal{P}^{\mu-}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})]=q.

(S23)

Using these identities, the BG equations can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tau\frac{{\rm d}m^{\mu}(t)}{{\rm d}t}&=2\sum_{l=0}^{z}\left[w_{l}^{\mu-}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t};T)-w_{l}^{\mu+}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t};T)\right],\\
\tau\frac{{\rm d}q(t)}{{\rm d}t}&=2\sum_{\mu}\sum_{l=0}^{z}\alpha_{l}\left[w_{l}^{\mu-}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t};T)-w_{l}^{\mu+}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t};T)\right],\end{split}

(S24)

which is equivalent to Eq. (7) in the main manuscript.

Appendix S2
Derivation of the free energy

Having derived the probabilities

\mathcal{P}^{a\pm}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})
and

\mathcal{P}^{b\pm}_{l}(\boldsymbol{x})
, we can now use them to
construct the BG free energy. Since the previous subsection already
expressed these probabilities in terms of the macroscopic order
parameters, we here only summarize the ingredients needed for the free-energy functional. The quantities
\mathcal{P}^{\mu\pm}_{l}
are joint probabilities: they give the probability that a central spin on sublattice
\mu\in\{a,b\}
has orientation
\sigma=\pm 1
and exactly
l
neighboring up spins. By construction, they satisfy the normalization condition

\sum_{l=0}^{z}[\mathcal{P}_{l}^{\mu+}(\boldsymbol{x})+\mathcal{P}_{l}^{\mu-}(\boldsymbol{x})]=1.

(S25)

Summing over
l
gives the corresponding single-site probabilities,

P_{\pm}^{\mu}=\sum_{l=0}^{z}\mathcal{P}_{l}^{\mu\pm}(\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{1\pm m^{\mu}}{2}.

(S26)

The nearest-neighbor pair probabilities are obtained from the average number of up and down neighbors surrounding a central spin. For instance,

P_{++}^{ab}(\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{l=0}^{z}l\,\mathcal{P}_{l}^{a+}(\boldsymbol{x}),\qquad P_{+-}^{ab}(\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{l=0}^{z}(z-l)\,\mathcal{P}_{l}^{a+}(\boldsymbol{x}),

(S27)

and analogous relations hold for
P_{--}^{ab}
and
P_{-+}^{ab}
. Evaluating these sums using Eq. (
S20
) gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}P^{ab}_{\pm\pm}(\boldsymbol{x})&=(1\pm m^{a}\pm m^{b}+q)/4,\\
P^{ab}_{\pm\mp}(\boldsymbol{x})&=(1\pm m^{a}\mp m^{b}-q)/4.\end{split}

(S28)

The BG entropy follows from the cluster-variational construction

Kikuchi 1951
. In this approximation, the entropy is built
from nearest-neighbor pair marginals and single-site marginals. The
contribution from all nearest-neighbor pairs is counted once, however,
this overcounts the site contribution: each site belongs to
z
different nearest-neighbor pairs, whereas it should contribute only once to the total entropy. One therefore has to subtract the excess
(z-1)
site contributions. This gives

S_{\rm BG}(\boldsymbol{x})=S_{\rm pair}(\boldsymbol{x})-(z-1)S_{\rm site}(\boldsymbol{x}),

(S29)

where

\displaystyle S_{\rm pair}(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=-\frac{zN}{2}\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}=\pm}P^{ab}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\ln(P^{ab}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}),

(S30)

\displaystyle S_{\rm site}(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=-\frac{N}{2}\sum_{\mu}\sum_{\sigma=\pm}P^{\mu}_{\sigma}\ln(P^{\mu}_{\sigma}).

(S31)

Here the prefactor
zN/2
is the total number of nearest-neighbor pairs in a lattice with coordination number
z
. Substituting the site and pair probabilities above, and subsequently dividing by
N
, yields the entropy expression given by Eq. (10) in the main text. To obtain the BG free energy, we combine this entropy with the average energy. Using the definitions of the order parameters, one finds

\langle\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\rangle=-\frac{J}{2}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle-H\sum_{i}\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle=N\left[-zJq/2-Hm\right].

(S32)

The BG free energy per spin therefore reads

\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x};T)=\frac{1}{N}\left[\langle\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\rangle-\beta^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{\rm BG}\right],

(S33)

which coincides with Eq. (8) in the main manuscript.

Appendix S3
Concavity of the phase boundary and conditions for reentrance

In this section, we determine for which values of the coordination number
z
the phase diagram becomes reentrant. Having derived the free energy and the critical magnetic field
H_{c}(T)
, we analyze the shape of the phase boundary by studying its second derivative:

\frac{d^{2}H_{c}(T)}{dT^{2}}=-\frac{J^{2}(z-2)(z-1)z\operatorname{csch}^{4}\left(J/T\right)\sqrt{\operatorname{tanh}\left(J/T\right)+z-1}}{2T^{3}\left(\operatorname{coth}\left(J/T\right)+z-1\right)^{3/2}\left((z-1)\operatorname{coth}\left(J/T\right)+1\right)^{2}},

(S34)

where, for simplicity, we have set
k_{\rm B}=1
. We now examine the sign of this expression in the antiferromagnetic case,
J<0
. Since the coordination number satisfies
z>2
, all factors in the numerator are positive. In particular, the square-root factor is well defined because
\tanh(J/T)>-1
, so

\tanh\left(J/T\right)+z-1>z-2>0.

(S35)

To determine the sign of the denominator, recall that the critical magnetic field is defined only for temperatures in the range

0<T<T_{c}(H=0)\equiv-\frac{2J}{\ln\left(\frac{z}{z-2}\right)}.

(S36)

Within this interval, all factors in the denominator are also positive. In particular, we must show that

\operatorname{coth}\left(J/T\right)+z-1>0.

(S37)

This inequality can be rewritten as
J/T>\operatorname{arccoth}(1-z)
. Using

\operatorname{arccoth}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1+1/x}{1-1/x}\right)
,
we obtain

\frac{J}{T}>-\frac{1}{2}\ln{\left(\frac{z}{z-2}\right)}.

(S38)

This is equivalent to Eq. (
S36
). Hence the denominator is positive throughout the physically relevant temperature range. Therefore, for the antiferromagnetic case with
J<0
and
z>2
, we find

\frac{d^{2}H_{c}(T)}{dT^{2}}<0.

(S39)

Thus,
H_{c}(T)
is concave throughout its full domain of definition, and therefore its derivative
dH_{c}/dT
is a strictly decreasing function of temperature. This immediately constrains the possible shape of the phase boundary. If the low-temperature slope is negative, then
dH_{c}/dT<0
for all
T
, and the phase boundary is monotonic. By contrast, if the low-temperature slope is positive, then concavity alone implies that the slope must decrease with temperature. Since
H_{c}(T)
must vanish at
T=T_{c}(H=0)
, a positive initial slope is incompatible with monotonic growth over the full interval
0<T<T_{c}(H=0)
. The derivative must therefore cross zero at a finite temperature, which means that
H_{c}(T)
develops a maximum and is non-monotonic. Evaluating the low-temperature behavior of
H_{c}(T)
shows that its slope is positive if and only if
z>z^{*}
. We therefore conclude that the phase boundary is non-monotonic, and hence reentrant, for
z>z^{*}
, while it is monotonic for
z<z^{*}
. This is consistent with the behavior discussed in the main manuscript.

Appendix S4
Linear stability analysis around equilibrium

In this section we analyze the linear stability of Eq. (
S24
). We define the currents as

J_{l}^{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x};T)\equiv w_{l}^{\mu-}(\boldsymbol{x};T)-w_{l}^{\mu+}(\boldsymbol{x};T).

(S40)

Let
\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}=\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}(T)
be the equilibrium state of Eq. (
S24
) at temperature
T
. By definition,

w_{l}^{\mu+}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq};T)=w_{l}^{\mu-}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq};T)\equiv w_{l,{\rm eq}}^{\mu},

(S41)

for all
\mu\in\{a,b\}
and
l\in\{0,\dots,z\}
. We now consider small perturbations
\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t}\equiv\boldsymbol{x}_{t}-\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}
such that
|\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t}|\ll 1
. To linearize the currents around equilibrium, we employ the local detailed-balance relation

\ln\biggl(\frac{w_{l}^{\mu+}(\boldsymbol{x};T)}{w_{l}^{\mu-}(\boldsymbol{x};T)}\biggr)=4[\partial_{m^{\mu}}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{x};T)+\alpha_{l}\partial_{q}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{x};T)],

(S42)

where
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{x};T)=\beta\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{x};T)
with
\beta\equiv 1/k_{\rm B}T
, and
\partial_{x}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{x};T)\equiv\partial\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{x};T)/\partial x
. Expanding both sides of Eq. (
S42
) to linear order in
\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t}
yields

\ln\biggl(\frac{w_{l}^{\mu+}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}+\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t};T)}{w_{l}^{\mu-}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}+\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t};T)}\biggr)\simeq\frac{1}{w^{\mu}_{l,{\rm eq}}}\boldsymbol{\nabla}\left.[w_{l}^{\mu+}(\boldsymbol{x};T)-w_{l}^{\mu-}(\boldsymbol{x};T)]\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t}=\frac{-1}{w^{\mu}_{l,{\rm eq}}}\left.\boldsymbol{\nabla}J_{l}^{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x};T)\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t},

(S43)

4[\partial_{m^{\mu}}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}+\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t};T)+\alpha_{l}\partial_{q}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}+\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t};T)]\simeq 4\boldsymbol{\nabla}\left.[\partial_{m^{\mu}}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{x};T)+\alpha_{l}\partial_{q}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{x};T)]\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t},

(S44)

where

\boldsymbol{\nabla}\equiv\left(\partial_{m},\partial_{s},\partial_{q}\right)^{\rm T}.

(S45)

Combining these expressions, we obtain the linearized currents in terms of the second derivatives of the free energy:

J_{l}^{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}+\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t};T)\simeq\left.\boldsymbol{\nabla}J_{l}^{\mu}(\boldsymbol{x};T)\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t}=-4w^{\mu}_{l,{\rm eq}}\boldsymbol{\nabla}\left.[\partial_{m^{\mu}}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{x};T)+\alpha_{l}\partial_{q}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{x};T)]\right|_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\rm eq}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta x}_{t}.

(S46)

Inserting this expression into Eq. (
S24
) and transforming to the
(m,s,q)
basis, we obtain the closed linear system reported as Eq. (C1) in the main manuscript.
