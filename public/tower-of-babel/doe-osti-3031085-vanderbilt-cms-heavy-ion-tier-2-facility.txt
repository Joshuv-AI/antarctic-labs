.
1. Abstract summarizing the general topics addressed during the award. This should be a
minimum of one paragraph and written in terms understandable by an educated
layperson.

The CMS experiment at the LHC at CERN in Geneva, Switzerland spends a portion of its
running time colliding heavy ions. These heavy ion collisions are a key probe of a state of matter
known as a Quark Gluon Plasma, which is a state of matter found in the very early universe 10s
of microseconds after the big bang. These collision data are recorded by the CMS experiment
and then undergo several stages of refinement to extract physics properties of the collisions
from the raw data recorded by the various detector subsystems in CMS. In addition, copies of
these datasets are stored on tape archival storage for data preservation. This award funds the
deployment, maintenance, and operation of a computing facility at Vanderbilt University which
processes, stores, and analyzes these collision data.

used, and findings. Include, if applicable, facts, figures, analyses, and assumptions used

This award supports the Vanderbilt CMS Tier-2 facility, which is the only permanent disk copy of
CMS Heavy-Ion data. During this award, the LHC has had four periods of Lead-Lead (PbPb)
collisions, a period of Proton-Lead (pPb) collisions, as well as several calibration and light-ion
collision periods (Including Oxygen-Oxygen, Proton-Oxygen, Xenon-Xenon, and Neon-Neon).
These data are processed and stored at the Vanderbilt CMS Tier-2 facility where they are made
available to CMS physicists for both physics research as well as development tasks (such as
algorithm development and detector performance measurements). A goal of the facility is to
receive and make available the data at the lowest latency possible, enabling physicists to
analyze the recorded data as quickly as feasible. This award, along with federal and
Vanderbilt-internal funding have expanded Wide-Area Network connectivity from 10 to
400Gigabits per-second to both decrease the time-to-insight for researchers, but also to support
the ever-increasing data rates from the CMS detector.

The Vanderbilt CMS Tier-2 facility is tightly integrated into the Worldwide LHC Computing Grid
(WLCG) along with CMS’ other computing resources. The scale and technologies of the WLCG
as well as computing more are continually evolving, and the Vanderbilt CMS Tier-2 facility has
kept pace with these changes by both increasing compute/storage capacity as well as updating
and deploying the software infrastructure at the facility. Vanderbilt technical and computing staff
have additional roles within the greater CMS Offline and Computing computing group as well as

the national U.S. CMS Software and Computing group. These additional roles are beneficial to
the Vanderbilt CMS Tier-2 facility as Vanderbilt staff are able to influence and direct the technical
evolution of the CMS Offline and Computing group. The CMS experiment recently published the
Conceptual Design Report (CDR) for the evolution of the computing model to meet the needs of
High Luminosity LHC (https://cds.cern.ch/record/2957472/files/LHCC-G-186.pdf) In addition,
U.S. CMS charged a Facilities Evolution Task Force with identifying how U.S. facilities will
evolve during the next decade. Vanderbilt staff contributed to the CDR and led the Task Force,
helping shape the CMS computing model for the future.

3. Paragraph indicating the number of presentations made and venues. Indicate
presentations given at national labs, industry, universities, national and international
professional meetings.

During the award period, Vanderbilt CMS Tier-2 personnel focused primarily on computing
operations and storage infrastructure supporting CMS heavy-ion data processing and analysis.
While no dedicated presentations on Tier-2 operations were delivered, the computing resources
supported physics analyses whose results were presented at major national and international
conferences, including Quark Matter, Hard Probes, Initial Stages, the American Physical Society
Division of Particles and Fields meetings, and numerous other conferences and workshops
worldwide. The CMS conference management system has recorded 1,095 CMS Heavy-Ion
presentations from the time period 2015-2025. As the only permanent on-disk copy of the
Heavy-Ion data recorded from CMS, the Tier-2 center played a critical enabling role in the
dissemination of CMS heavy-ion scientific results.
