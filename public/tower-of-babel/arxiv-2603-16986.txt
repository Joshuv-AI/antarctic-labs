On the structure of the Gram matrix for Gabor systems generated by B-splines.
Martin Buck, Christina Frederick, Kasso A. Okoudjou, Alexander Stangl

Abstract
We consider the Gabor system 𝒢 ⁡ ( g , a ​ ℤ × b ​ ℤ ) \mathcal{G}(g,a\mathbb{Z}\times b\mathbb{Z}) generated by a continuous, compactly supported function g g over the time-frequency lattice generated by the parameters a a and b b . We show that, under an appropriate ordering of the Gabor elements, certain submatrices of the Gram matrix of 𝒢 ⁡ ( g , a ​ ℤ × b ​ ℤ ) \mathcal{G}(g,a\mathbb{Z}\times b\mathbb{Z}) exhibit a block-Toeplitz structure. This structural property enables us to derive spectral results for finite sub-blocks of the Gram matrix by appealing to the spectral theory of Toeplitz matrices. In particular, we apply our results to the Gram matrix of Gabor systems generated by the N N th-order B-spline.

Introduction

1.1
Background on the frame set problem

Gabor analysis originated from two distinct perspectives: the operator-theoretic framework of J. von Neumann
44
in 1932 and the communication-theoretic approach of D. Gabor
20
in 1946. Both authors independently investigated the completeness of the system

\mathcal{G}(g,\mathbb{Z}^{2})=\{g_{(n,k)}(\cdot)=g(\cdot-n)e^{2\pi ik\cdot}:(n,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\},

(1)

utilizing the Gaussian
g(x)=e^{-\pi x^{2}}
for its optimal localization in the time-frequency plane. This dual legacy highlights the fundamental nature of the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
in tiling phase space (see
34
for a detailed history).

More generally, the
Gabor system
generated by a non-zero window function

g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
along a (countable) discrete set of time-frequency shifts

\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\mathcal{G}(g,\Gamma)=\{g_{\gamma}:=g(\cdot-\gamma_{1})e^{2\pi i\gamma_{2}\cdot}:\,\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\Gamma\},

(2)

is said to be a frame provided that there exist constants
0<\alpha\leq\beta<\infty
such that for each
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

\alpha\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}\leq\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left\lvert\left\langle f,g_{\gamma}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}\leq\beta\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}.

(3)

It follows that there exists an associated set of functions

\left\{h_{\gamma}\right\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
which is also a Gabor frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for which the following reconstruction formulas hold for each
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
:

f=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left\langle f,h_{\gamma}\right\rangle g_{\gamma}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left\langle f,g_{\gamma}\right\rangle h_{\gamma}.

(4)

When
\Gamma=a\mathbb{Z}\times b\mathbb{Z}
is a separable lattice, it can be shown that the set of functions
\left\{h_{\gamma}\right\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is a Gabor frame generated by a window function
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and called a
dual window
to
g
. In this case,
\left\{h_{\gamma}\right\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is a
dual Gabor frame
to
\left\{g_{\gamma}\right\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}

The full understanding of all window functions
g
, and all positive parameters
a,b
such that
\mathcal{G}(g,a\mathbb{Z}\times b\mathbb{Z})
is a Gabor frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, is still incomplete. Nonetheless, the last few decades of research have resulted in an impressive body of work on Gabor systems. For example, there exists a Nyquist-type condition dating back to the work of Daubechies
16
, in which it is proved that when
ab>1
is rational then
\mathcal{G}(g,a\mathbb{Z}\times b\mathbb{Z})
is incomplete, for the irrational case we refer to
4
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{G}(g,a\mathbb{Z}\times b\mathbb{Z})
is a Gabor frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
then
ab\leq 1
. The critical case
ab=1
corresponds to the case when it is possible for
\mathcal{G}(g,a\mathbb{Z}\times b\mathbb{Z})
to be an orthonormal or a Riesz basis. In such a case, the window function is necessarily poorly localized in the time-frequency plane as dictated by the Balian-Low Theorem

We refer to

1.2
Contributions

This paper aims to investigate the frameset of continuous, compactly supported functions. To this end, we initiate an analysis of the Gram matrix of the corresponding Gabor systems.
We build upon the structural investigations introduced in
9
, and provide a spectral and structural analysis of the Gram matrices of Gabor systems generated by a class of continuous compactly supported windows, with a specific focus on B-splines of order
N
. In particular, we identify a unique structural hierarchy within these Gram matrices; we demonstrate in Theorem
3.3
that certain a finite sub-Gramian is a block-Toeplitz matrix where each constituent block admits a Hadamard factorization into a real-valued Toeplitz matrix and a rank-one Hankel matrix. Secondly, we derive an explicit analytic formulation for the Laurent symbols
t^{[\ell]}(x)
associated with these blocks, proving in Theorem
4.4
that they are governed by periodic sums of
\text{sinc}^{2N}
kernels. Using this formulation, we establish an
O(\ell^{-N})
spectral decay rate for the off-diagonal blocks, which provides the first formal mathematical justification for the block-diagonal dominance observed in the numerical analysis of spline-based Gabor systems. Finally, we leverage the theory of asymptotically equivalent circulant matrices to provide a pathway for estimating spectral bounds, specifically demonstrating how rational lattice parameters
a\in\mathbb{Q}
induce the decay of the lower frame bounds in truncated systems.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
sets the notations use throughout the paper. Section
3
establishes the internal block structure of the Gram matrix and provides the proof for the Toeplitz-dot-Hankel factorization. Section
4
presents the spectral analysis of the block operators
\mathscr{G}^{[\ell]}
via their associated Laurent symbols. We also introduce a sequence of asymptotically equivalent circulant matrices used for finite-section eigenvalue estimation. In addition, we discuss the implications of these spectral results on the frame set problem, specifically regarding the behavior of frame bounds on rational and irrational lattices. Finally, Section
5
summarizes our findings and proposes future directions.

Notation and preliminaries

Given a discrete collection of functions

\left\{g_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we define the following operators:

•

The
synthesis operator

\mathscr{T}
is a mapping from

\mathbb{C}^{\Lambda}
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
defined as

\mathscr{T}\left(c_{\lambda}\right)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}c_{\lambda}g_{\lambda}.

(8)

•

Its (formal) adjoint is the
analysis operator

\mathscr{T}^{*}
is given by

\mathscr{T}^{*}f=\left(\left\langle f,g_{\lambda}\right\rangle\right)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}.

(9)

•

The
frame operator

\mathscr{S}
defined on

L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is the composition of the synthesis and
analysis operators and given by
\mathscr{T}\mathscr{T}^{*}

\mathscr{S}f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\langle f,g_{\lambda}\right\rangle g_{\lambda}.

(10)

•

The
Gram operator

\mathscr{G}
is defined on

\mathbb{C}^{\Lambda}
as the composition of the analysis and
synthesis operators
\mathscr{T}^{*}\mathscr{T}
and given by

\mathscr{G}\left(c_{\lambda}\right)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}=\left(\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda}c_{\lambda^{\prime}}\left\langle g_{\lambda^{\prime}},g_{\lambda}\right\rangle\right)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}.

(11)

The matrix corresponding to
\mathscr{G}
is
referred to as the
Gram matrix
.

The frame operator and the Gram operator are related in that the two
share a spectrum, with the possible exception of zero, which may be
present in the spectrum of the Gram operator but not that of the frame
operator. For this reason, while the frame operator may be more readily
amenable to a finite sections method the presence of zero in the
spectrum of the Gram operator means that the spectra of the finite sections of the Gram
operator do not necessarily converge to the non-zero portion of the spectrum of the Gram operator (see
14
).
This manifests as
pollution
filling the spectral gap between
zero and
\left\lVert S^{-1}\right\rVert^{-1}
when taking finite sections of
\mathscr{G}
.

In this section we show first that the enumeration of
\Lambda_{n}

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

and define the shifted set:

\widetilde{\Lambda_{n}}:=\Lambda_{n}+(as,bt),

Then, for any

\langle\tilde{g}_{j,k},\tilde{g}_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\rangle=e^{2\pi ibas(j-j^{\prime})}\langle g_{j,k},g_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\rangle.

The shifting factor

is unchanged. Furthermore, the spectra of
G_{n}
and

Proof.

The proof is a simple change-of-variables

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{g}_{j,k},\tilde{g}_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x-ka-sa)e^{2\pi i(jb+tb)x}\cdot g(x-{k^{\prime}}a-sa)e^{-2\pi i({j^{\prime}}b+tb)x}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(u-ka)e^{2\pi ijb(u+sa)}\cdot g(u-{k^{\prime}}a)e^{-2\pi i{j^{\prime}}b(u+sa)}\,du

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi ibsa(j-j^{\prime})}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(u-ka)g(u-{k^{\prime}}a)e^{2\pi i(jb-{j^{\prime}}b)u}\,du

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi ibsa(j-j^{\prime})}\langle g_{j,k},g_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\rangle.

Setting

\displaystyle(G_{n}^{[\ell]})_{k,k^{\prime}}

(18)

∎

which govern the finite and infinite matrices

and

through the similarity relations proved in Section

where

(resp.

4.1
Classical results

We summarize classical results on Toeplitz matrices and refer to
23
;
32
;
48
;
7
for comprehensive treatments.

Let
\mathbf{S}^{1}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
denote the complex unit circle. The
Wiener algebra

W
consists of functions
f:\mathbf{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{C}
whose Fourier series converge absolutely:

f(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{f}_{n}z^{n},\quad\|f\|_{W}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}|\hat{f}_{n}|<\infty.

The Fourier coefficients
\hat{f}_{n}
are given by

\hat{f}_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{i\theta})e^{-in\theta}\,d\theta.

To each
f\in W
corresponds an infinite Toeplitz matrix
T(f)
defined by

T(f)=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{f}_{0}&\hat{f}_{-1}&\hat{f}_{-2}&\cdots\\
\hat{f}_{1}&\hat{f}_{0}&\hat{f}_{-1}&\cdots\\
\hat{f}_{2}&\hat{f}_{1}&\hat{f}_{0}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{bmatrix}.

The function
f
, called the
symbol
of the Toeplitz operator
T(f)
, defines a bounded linear operator on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})
for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
, with operator norm satisfying
\|T(f)\|_{\ell^{p}\to\ell^{p}}\leq\|f\|_{W}

8
.

When the Fourier series of
f
has only finitely many non-zero coefficients, the associated Toeplitz matrix
T(f)
is a symmetric, banded bi-infinite matrix. In this case,
f
is called a
Laurent polynomial
, and has the form

f(z)=\sum_{j=-r}^{s}\hat{f}_{j}z^{j},\quad z\in\mathbf{S}^{1},

for some integers
r,s\geq 0
and real coefficients
\hat{f}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
.

In this case,
T(f)
has no eigenvalues, and its spectrum is the interval
[\min f,\max f]

(
7
, Theorem 1.4)
.

Theorem 4.1

(
32
)
.

Let
f
be a real Laurent polynomial and let
T(f)
be the associated Toeplitz operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then

\operatorname{sp}(T(f))=\left[\min_{z\in\mathbf{S}^{1}}f(z),\,\max_{z\in\mathbf{S}^{1}}f(z)\right].

Moreover, if
f
is nonconstant, then
T(f)
has empty point spectrum (i.e., no eigenvalues).

Theorem 4.2

(Lemma 4.1 in
23
and Corollary 2.18 in
7
)
.

Let
T_{n}
be the
n\times n
Hermitian Toeplitz matrix associated with the real-valued, continuous symbol
t(x)
defined on
\mathbf{S}^{1}
. Then the eigenvalues of
T_{n}
lie in the interval

[\inf_{x}t(x),\sup_{x}t(x)],

and satisfy the limits

\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(T_{n})=\inf_{x}t(x),\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}(T_{n})=\sup_{x}t(x).

The following Szegö-type limit theorem can be applied.

Theorem 4.3

(Theorem 4.1,
23
)
.

Let
T_{n}
be a banded Toeplitz matrix with eigenvalues
\lambda_{m}(T_{n})
and real valued symbol
t
. Then for any function
F(x)
continuous on
[\inf_{x}t(x),\sup_{x}t(x)]
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}F(\lambda_{m}(T_{n}))=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(t(\omega))d\omega.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
N\geq 2
. The Laurent polynomials
t^{[\ell]}(x)
generated by the
N
th order
B-spline
g=s_{N}
have the form

t^{[\ell]}(x)=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\sinc^{N}\!\left(\frac{r-(x-ab\ell/2)}{a}\right)\sinc^{N}\!\left(\frac{r-(x+ab\ell/2)}{a}\right)

(19)

Proof.

Applying the change of variables
y=x-\tfrac{ak}{2}
, so
x=y+\tfrac{ak}{2}
to (
18
), we obtain

t^{[\ell]}_{k}=e^{\pi iab\ell k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(y)\,g(y+ak)\,e^{2\pi ib\ell y}\,dy.

Define
f_{\ell}(y)=g(y)\,e^{2\pi ib\ell y}
.
Then

t^{[\ell]}_{k}=e^{\pi iab\ell k}\,(f_{\ell}*g)(ak),\qquad(f_{\ell}*g)(z)=\int g(y)\,g(y+z)\,e^{2\pi ib\ell y}\,dy.

Hence the Laurent polynomial has the form

t^{[\ell]}(x)=\sum_{|k|\leq\lfloor\frac{N}{a}\rfloor}e^{\pi iab\ell k}\,(f_{\ell}*g)(ak)\,e^{2\pi ikx},

and applying the Poisson summation formula gives

t^{[\ell]}(x)=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{(f_{\ell}*g)}\!\left(\frac{r-(x-ab\ell/2)}{a}\right)=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{g}\!\left(\tfrac{r-(x-ab\ell/2)}{a}\right)\widehat{g}\!\left(\tfrac{r-(x-ab\ell/2)}{a}-b\ell\right).

The Fourier transform of
g=s_{N}
has the form

\widehat{g}(\xi)=\sinc^{N}(\xi)=\left(\frac{\sin(\pi\xi)}{\pi\xi}\right)^{N}.

Hence the representation of
t^{[\ell]}(x)
is given by
(
19
).

∎

Corollary 4.4.1
.

Suppose that
a=\tfrac{1}{p}
with
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\geq 2
, and
\ell\geq 0
and let

T_{\infty}^{[\ell]}
denote the Toeplitz operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with symbol

t^{[\ell]}(x)
. Then

\Spec\big(T_{\infty}^{[\ell]}\big)\subset[0,p].

Moreover, when
\ell=0
we have

\Spec\big(T_{\infty}^{[0]}\big)=[0,p].

Proof.

By Theorem
4.4
with
\ell=0
and
a=1/p
, we have

t^{[0]}(x)=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\sinc^{2N}\!\left(\frac{r-x}{a}\right)=p\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\sinc^{2N}\big(p(r-x)\big).

At integer points
x\in\mathbb{Z}
, only the term
r=x
contributes, so
t^{[0]}(x)=p
.

On the other hand, if
x=k/p
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus p\mathbb{Z}
, then

p(r-x)=pr-k
is a nonzero integer for every
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, and hence
each
\sinc
factor vanishes. Thus
t^{[0]}(k/p)=0
for

k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus p\mathbb{Z}
.
Since
t^{[0]}
is continuous and nonnegative, it follows that by Theorem
4.1

\Spec(T_{\infty}^{[0]})=\left[\inf_{x}t^{[0]}(x),\sup_{x}t^{[0]}(x)\right]=[0,p].

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
|\ell|>0
and let
t^{[\ell]}(x)
be the Laurent polynomial corresponding to the
N
th-order B-spline. Then, there exists a
C>0
such that

|\sup(t^{[\ell]}(x))-\inf(t^{[\ell]}(x))|\leq C{\ell}^{-N}.

Proof.

Suppose that
|\ell|>\tfrac{4}{ab}
and let
x\in[-1/2,1/2]
. From (
19
) we have:

\displaystyle|t^{[\ell]}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{a^{2N-1}}{\pi^{2N}}\tfrac{1}{|x-ab\ell/2|^{N}|x+ab\ell/2|^{N}}+\tfrac{a^{2N-1}}{\pi^{2N}}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{|r-(x-ab\ell/2)|^{N}|r-(x+ab\ell/2)|^{N}}+\tfrac{a^{2N-1}}{\pi^{2N}}\sum_{r=-\infty}^{-1}\tfrac{1}{|r-(x-ab\ell/2)|^{N}|r-(x+ab\ell/2)|^{N}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{a^{2N-1}}{\pi^{2N}}\tfrac{1}{|x-ab\ell/2|^{N}|x+ab\ell/2|^{N}}+\tfrac{a^{2N-1}}{\pi^{2N}}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{|r-(x-ab\ell/2)|^{N}|r-(x+ab\ell/2)|^{N}}+\tfrac{a^{2N-1}}{\pi^{2N}}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{|r+(x-ab\ell/2)|^{N}|r+(x+ab\ell/2)|^{N}}.

We assume that
\ell>\tfrac{4}{ab}
and the case
\ell<-\tfrac{4}{ab}
is treated similarly.

It follows that

\sup_{x\in[-1/2,1/2]}\tfrac{1}{|x-ab\ell/2|^{N}|x+ab\ell/2|^{N}}\leq 4^{3N}(ab)^{-2N}\ \ell^{-2N}.

Let
L_{0}=\lfloor\tfrac{ab\ell+1}{2}\rfloor
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{|r-(x-ab\ell/2)|^{N}|r-(x+ab\ell/2)|^{N}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{L_{0}-1}\tfrac{1}{|r-(x-ab\ell/2)|^{N}|r-(x+ab\ell/2)|^{N}}+\sum_{r=L_{0}}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{|r-(x-ab\ell/2)|^{N}|r-(x+ab\ell/2)|^{N}}.

By writing
\tfrac{ab\ell+1}{2}=L_{0}+\theta

for some
\theta\in(0,1)
we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{r=L_{0}}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{|r-(x-ab\ell/2)|^{N}|r-(x+ab\ell/2)|^{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r=L_{0}}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{|r+\tfrac{ab\ell-1}{2}|^{N}}\tfrac{1}{|r-\tfrac{ab\ell+1}{2}|^{N}}

\displaystyle\leq(L_{0}+\tfrac{ab\ell-1}{2})^{-N}\sum_{r=L_{0}}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{|r-\tfrac{ab\ell+1}{2}|^{N}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{N}(ab)^{-N}\ell^{-N}\sum_{r=L_{0}}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{|r-L_{0}-\theta|^{N}}

\displaystyle=2^{N}(ab)^{-N}\ell^{-N}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{|r-\theta|^{N}}\leq_{N}\ell^{-N}.

Next, for each
r\in\{1,2,\ldots,L_{0}-1\}
we have that

\sup_{x\in[-1/2,1/2]}\tfrac{1}{|r-(x-ab\ell/2)|^{N}}\leq\tfrac{2^{N}}{(1+ab\ell)^{N}}\leq 2^{N}(ab)^{-N}\ell^{-N}.

Moreover,

\sup_{x\in[-1/2,1/2]}\sum_{r=1}^{L_{0}-1}\tfrac{1}{|r-(x+ab\ell/2)|^{N}}=\sup_{x\in[-1/2,1/2]}\sum_{r=1}^{L_{0}-1}\tfrac{1}{|r-x-L_{0}-\theta+1/2|^{N}}=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\sum_{r=1}^{L_{0}-1}\tfrac{1}{|r-L_{0}+x-\theta|^{N}}<\infty

because
r-L_{0}-\theta\leq-1-\theta<-1
.

We can estimate
\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{|r+(x-ab\ell/2)|^{N}|r+(x+ab\ell/2)|^{N}}
similarly to conclude that

|t^{[\ell]}(x)|\leq a^{2N-1}\pi^{-2N}(4^{3N}(ab)^{-2N}\ell^{-2N}+C_{1}2^{N+1}(ab)^{-N}\ell^{-N}+C_{2}2^{N+1}(ab)^{-N}\ell^{-N})\leq C_{N}\ell^{-N}

for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}
, and
C_{N}
.

∎

4.3
Spectral analysis of the finite Gram matrix
G_{n}

Given Corollary
3.3.2
, the infinite block
\mathscr{G}^{[\ell]}
within
\mathscr{G}
has the same singular values as the real-valued, banded Toeplitz matrix
T_{\infty}^{[\ell]}
whose entries are given by (
18
).

Since
G_{n}^{[0]}
is a principal sub-matrix of
G_{n}
, Cauchy’s interlacing theorem states that their eigenvalues interlace, leading to bounds on the minimum and maximum eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(G_{n})
and
\lambda_{n^{2}}(G_{n})
of
{G}_{n}
:

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(G_{n})\leq\lambda_{k}(G_{n}^{[0]})\leq\lambda_{k+n^{2}-n}(G_{n}),\qquad k=1,\ldots n.

In particular,
\lambda_{1}(G_{n})\leq\lambda_{1}(G_{n}^{[0]})
and
\lambda_{n}(G_{n}^{[0]})\leq\lambda_{n^{2}}(G_{n})
. Figure
4
demonstrates this for
n=15
.

Consequently, we have the following result.

Corollary 4.5.1
.

For
n\geq 1
, there exists an
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\forall n\geq K
, we have the upper and lower estimates:

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(G_{n})\leq\inf_{x}t^{[0]}(x)\leq\lambda_{1}(G_{n}^{[0]})

(20)

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}(G_{n}^{[0]})\leq\sup_{x}t^{[0]}(x)\leq\lambda_{n}(G_{n}).

(21)

Proof.

We will only establish the first set of inequalities as the second one is proved in a similar manner.

We note that
\inf_{x}t^{[0]}(x)\leq\lambda_{1}(T_{n}^{[0]})
follows from Corollary
4.2
. Next, by the interlacing relation, we know that
\lambda_{1}(G_{n})\leq\lambda_{1}(G_{n}^{[0]})
. It follows that

\limsup_{n}\lambda_{1}(G_{n})\leq\limsup_{n}\lambda_{1}(G_{n}^{[0]})=\lim_{n}\lambda_{1}(G_{n}^{[0]})=\inf_{x}t^{[0]}(x).

Thus, there exists an integer
K\geq 1
such that for all
n\geq K
, we have

\lambda_{1}(G_{n})\leq\inf_{x}t^{[0]}(x).

∎

4.4
Asymptotically Equivalent Circulant Matrices

The exact computation of the spectrum of a Toeplitz matrix
G_{n}
, especially for large
n
, is generally intractable. However, Theorem
3.3
shows that the spectral behavior of
G_{n}
is governed by the singular values of the Toeplitz blocks
T_{n}^{[\ell]}
. To analyze these efficiently, we introduce a family of
circulant matrices

C_{n}^{[\ell]}
that approximate the Toeplitz matrices
T_{n}^{[\ell]}
in a precise asymptotic sense.

4.4.1
Background on Asymptotic Equivalence

Let
T_{n}
be a banded Toeplitz matrix of bandwidth
m
, defined by a symbol
t(x)=\sum_{k=-m}^{m}t_{k}e^{2\pi ikx}
. Define the corresponding circulant matrix
C_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
by wrapping the Toeplitz coefficients as:

c_{k}=\begin{cases}t_{-k},&0\leq k\leq m,\\
t_{n-k},&n-m\leq k\leq n-1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This defines the first row of
C_{n}
, from which the rest is filled by circular shifts.

Lemma 4.6

(Lemma 4.2 in
23
)
.

The matrices
T_{n}
and
C_{n}
are asymptotically equivalent, denoted by
T_{n}\sim C_{n}
i.e.,

1.

Both sequences are uniformly bounded in operator norm:
\|T_{n}\|_{2},\|C_{n}\|_{2}\leq M
for some constant
M
independent of
n
,

2.

The difference vanishes in the normalized Hilbert–Schmidt norm:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|(T_{n})_{i,j}-(C_{n})_{i,j}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}=0.

When
T_{n}\sim C_{n}
holds for Hermitian matrices, their eigenvalues are said to be
asymptotically equally distributed
, meaning that for any integer
s\geq 1
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\left(\lambda^{s}_{m}(A_{n})-\lambda^{s}_{m}(C_{n})\right)=0.

For general theory, see
(
23
, Chapter 2)
. In special cases, such as banded Toeplitz matrices, stronger pointwise convergence of eigenvalues is known
48
.

Circulant matrices admit explicit spectral decompositions. Specifically:

Theorem 4.7

(Theorem 5.15,
7
)
.

Let
C_{n}
be the circulant matrix constructed from a trigonometric polynomial
t
. Then its eigenvalues are given (up to permutation) by uniformly spaced samples of
t
:

\operatorname{spec}(C_{n})=\left\{t\left(\tfrac{p}{n}\right):p=0,\dots,n-1\right\}.

4.4.2
Application to Gram Matrices for
\mathcal{G}(s_{N},\Lambda_{n})

Let
T_{n}^{[\ell]}
denote the Toeplitz blocks with symbol
t^{\ell}(x)
generated by the second order B-spline, and define
C_{n}^{[\ell]}
to be the circulant matrix associated with the same symbol.

Therefore, letting
\{\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}\}
be the ordered eigenvalues of
C_{n}^{[\ell]}
, i.e.,

\psi_{m}=t^{[\ell]}\left(\tfrac{p_{m}}{n}\right)\quad\text{for }p_{m}\in\{0,\dots,n-1\},

we have that for any
s\geq 1
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\left(\lambda^{s}_{m}(T_{n}^{[\ell]})-\psi^{s}_{m}\right)=0.

This approximation enables efficient estimation of spectral bounds for the finite Gram matrix
G_{n}
. The asymptotic behavior of the difference in eigenvalues between

5
Conclusions

We conclude by connecting the results of this paper to the frame set problem for the B-spline.

We recall that if
\mathscr{G}
is the Gram operator for a frame with frame bounds
\alpha,\beta
, then

\{0\}\subseteq\sigma(\mathscr{G})\subseteq\{0\}\cup[\alpha,\beta].

Let
\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}
denote its Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse.
Estimates on the operator norm of the pseudo-inverse
\|\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}\|
are also valuable as its reciprocal is the lower frame bound
\alpha=\|\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}\|^{-1}
, see
1
.

Furthermore, the following result can be applied to obtain frame bounds.

Lemma 5.1

(Lemmas 4 and 5 in
1
)
.

Let
\Phi
be a linearly independent frame with frame bounds
\alpha
and
\beta
.

1.

The truncated Gram matrix
G_{n}
is invertible with
\|G^{-1}_{n}\|^{-1}=A_{n}
and
\|G_{n}\|=B_{n}
, where

and
B_{n}
are the frame bounds of the truncated frame
\Phi_{n}
.

2.

The sequences
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\{B_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
are monotonically nonincreasing and monotonically nondecreasing, respectively.

3.

B_{n}\leq\beta
for all
n
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{n}=\beta
.

4.

\liminf_{n}A_{n}>0
iff
\Phi
is a Riesz basis.

In this paper, we have provided a detailed analysis of the structural properties of the Gram matrix
\mathscr{G}
associated with Gabor systems generated by B-splines of order
N
. By exploiting the compact support of
s_{N}
, we established a structural hierarchy that allows for a rigorous treatment of the properties of the frame operator’s finite sections.

Our primary structural result, Theorem
3.3
, demonstrates that the blocks of the Gram matrix
G_{n}^{[\ell]}
admit a Hadamard factorization
G_{n}^{[\ell]}=T_{n}^{[\ell]}\circ H_{n}^{[\ell]}
. This decomposition isolates the modulation-induced phase into a rank-one Hankel matrix
H_{n}^{[\ell]}
, while the singular values are dictated by a real-valued, symmetric, and banded Toeplitz matrix
T_{n}^{[\ell]}
. This result connects Gabor analysis and classical Toeplitz theory, enabling the use of Szegő-type limit theorems.
