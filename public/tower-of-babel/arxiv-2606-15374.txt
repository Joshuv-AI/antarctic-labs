Counting Algebraic Integers of Bounded Height in Cyclotomic Fields
Phillip Harris, John Yin

Abstract
In this note, we fix a height bound B B , and give a bound on the number of algebraic integers and units of absolute height at most B B in cyclotomic extensions ℚ ⁡ [ ζ q ] {\mathbb{Q}}[\zeta_{q}] , where q q is a prime power. The bound is asymptotic in q q .



1
Introduction

Let
K
be a number field and let
L
be a finite extension. We define the exponential Weil height relative to
K
of an element of
[\alpha_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\dots\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\alpha_{n}]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{K}(L)
by

H([\alpha_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\dots\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\alpha_{n}])=\left(\prod_{\nu\in\text{ places of }L}\max(|\alpha_{0}|_{\nu},\dots,|\alpha_{n}|_{\nu})\right)^{1/[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]},

where
L
can be taken to be any number field containing the
\alpha_{i}
. We also define the logarithmic Weil height relative to
K
to be

h([\alpha_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\dots\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\alpha_{n}])=\log(H([\alpha_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\dots\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\alpha_{n}])).

Much of the literature on counting points of bounded height derives its motivation from the Batyrev-Manin conjecture, which fixes a base field
K
, and predicts the number of
K
points of bounded height
B
, as
B
grows. In this note, we are curious about fixing the height bound, and letting the field vary instead.

Previous work in this aspect includes
[
4
]
. Here, Dubickas showed the following. Let
N(d,B)
be the number of
\alpha\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
of degree at most
d
, with log Weil height at most
B
. Then, for each fixed
B>0
,
\log N(d,B)\sim Bd^{2}
as
d\to\infty
.

We choose to study the prime power cyclotomic fields
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q}),q=p^{r}
as
r\to\infty
. Our main theorem concerns units in the ring of integers. Define

U(n,B)=\#\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{n}]^{\times}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h(\alpha)<B\},

and

U_{\cyc}(n,B)=\#\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{n}]^{\times}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h(\alpha)<B,\alpha\text{ is a cyclotomic unit}\}.

Then, we will show

Theorem 1
.

Let
q=p^{r}
be an odd prime power. For all
\epsilon>0
and
B>0
, the cyclotomic units in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})
satisfy

U_{\cyc}(q,B)\ll q^{3}\exp\left(C_{\epsilon}B^{1/3}q^{5/6+\epsilon}\right).

Moreover, the full unit group of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})
satisfies

U(q,B)\ll q^{3}\exp\left(C_{\epsilon}(h_{q}^{+}B)^{1/3}q^{5/6+\epsilon}\right),

where
h_{q}^{+}
is the class number of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})^{+}
.

While we do not have much control over
h^{+}_{q}
, numerical evidence suggests it is small. In particular, using Cohen-Lenstra heuristics, Buhler, Pomerance, and Robertson conjecture that
h_{p^{r}}^{+}=h_{p^{r+1}}^{+}
for all but finitely many pairs
(p,r)

[
1
]
. Thus we conjecturally have a subexponential bound for fixed
p
as
r\to\infty
.

We also give an exponential upper bound for algebraic integers.

Theorem 2
.

Let
q=p^{r}
be an odd prime power, let
D=\phi(q)
, and set

I(q,B)=\#\{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{q}]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h(\alpha)\leq B\}.

Then

\log I(q,B)\ll D(B+1).

Finally, we observe that both the above quantities have a polynomial lower bound. We also note that
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
is a natural function field analogue for
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})
, and in this case, the height is just the degree in
t
, giving
\#\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h(\alpha)\leq B\}=q^{B+1}
. We might speculate that the true quantity of
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{q}]
-points is roughly polynomial in
q
.

Proof

The main geometric input is the following lattice point counting lemma of Davenport
[
2
]
. Let
{\mathcal{P}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
be a compact body. For each
0\leq j\leq D
, set

V_{j}({\mathcal{P}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I\right\rvert}=j\end{subarray}}\Vol_{j}(\pi_{I}({\mathcal{P}})),

where
\pi_{I}
is the projection to
\mathbb{R}^{I}
and we normalize
V_{0}({\mathcal{P}})=1
. If every line parallel to a coordinate axis meets
{\mathcal{P}}
in at most
h
intervals, and the same is true for every coordinate projection
\pi_{I}({\mathcal{P}})
, then

\left|\#(\mathbb{Z}^{D}\cap{\mathcal{P}})-\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{P}})\right|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{D-1}h^{D-j}V_{j}({\mathcal{P}}).

We also record the standard volume formula for zonotopes (the projection of a parallepiped, equivalently, a Minkowski sum of line segments). Let
M
be a
d\times n
matrix with columns
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, defining a zonotope

Z=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}v_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0\leq t_{i}\leq 1\right\}.

Write
[M]_{I,J}
for the determinant of the submatrix of
M
with rows
I
and columns
J
. Then

\Vol_{d}(Z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I\right\rvert}=d\end{subarray}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert[M]_{[d],I}\right\rvert}.

We will also use Jacobi’s complementary minor identity: if
A
is an invertible
D\times D
matrix and
I,J\subset[D]
satisfy
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J\right\rvert}
, then writing
I^{c}=[D]\setminus I
and
J^{c}=[D]\setminus J
one has

[A]_{I,J}=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in I}i+\sum_{j\in J}j}\det(A)[A^{-1}]_{J^{c},I^{c}}.

2.1
Units

Let
q=p^{r}
with
p
an odd prime. Let
E_{q}\subset\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{q}]^{\times}
be the group of units. Let
V_{q}\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})^{\times}
be the multiplicative subgroup generated by
\zeta_{q}
and
1-\zeta_{q}^{a}
for all
a\neq 0\pmod{q}
. Then
C_{q}=E_{q}\cap V_{q}
is called the group of
cyclotomic units
of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})
.
Let
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})^{+}=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q}+\zeta_{q}^{-1})
be the real subfield and
C^{+}_{q}\subset E^{+}_{q}
the corresponding real subgroups. The cyclotomic units have finite index in the full unit group; unlike the full unit group, they enjoy a simple explicit basis
[
5
, Theorem 8.2]
:

Proposition 1
.

For
1<a<q/2
,
(a,q)=1
, define

\xi_{a}=\zeta_{q}^{(1-a)/2}\frac{1-\zeta_{q}^{a}}{1-\zeta_{q}}.

The factor
\zeta_{q}^{(1-a)/2}
is chosen so that
\xi_{a}\in\mathbb{R}
. The
\xi_{a}
and
-1
form a basis for
C^{+}_{q}
and we have

\left[E_{q}^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C_{q}^{+}\right]=h_{q}^{+}

where
h_{q}^{+}
is the class number of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})^{+}
.

For any unit
u\in E_{q}^{+}
, we have
u^{h_{q}^{+}}\in C_{q}^{+}
and
h(u^{h_{q}^{+}})=h_{q}^{+}h(u)
. Thus, we may count real units of height at most
B
by counting real cyclotomic units of height at most
h_{q}^{+}B
. Also, the units of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})
are those of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})^{+}
times a root of unity, so passing back to
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})
we pick up a factor of
q
. Thus, to prove Theorem
1
, it suffices to prove the following:

Proposition 2
.

For all
\epsilon>0
, we have

U^{+}_{\cyc}(q,B)\ll q^{2}\exp(C_{\epsilon}B^{1/3}q^{5/6+\epsilon}).

Proof.

Let
u
be a real cyclotomic unit and write
u=\prod_{i}\xi_{i}^{c_{i}}
. For
1\leq j<q/2
with
(j,q)=1
, let
\sigma_{j}
be the element of the Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})^{+}
sending
\zeta_{q}+\zeta_{q}^{-1}
to
\zeta_{q}^{j}+\zeta_{q}^{-j}
. Set
D=[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]-1=\phi(q)/2-1
. We write the Weil height

h(u)=\frac{1}{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{\nu}\max(0,\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert}_{\nu}).

Since
u
is a unit, the non-archimedean valuations are trivial. The archimedean valuations are the absolute values of the Galois conjugates
\sigma_{j}(u)
. Thus, the height simplifies to

h(u)=\frac{1}{D+1}\sum_{j}\max(0,\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\sigma_{j}(u)\right\rvert}).

Furthermore, using
h(u)=h(u^{-1})
, we have

\displaystyle h(u)=\frac{h(u)+h(u^{-1})}{2}=\frac{1}{2(D+1)}\sum_{j}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\sigma_{j}(u)\right\rvert}\right\rvert}.

Form a
D\times D
matrix
A
with entries

A_{ij}=\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\sigma_{j}(\xi_{i})\right\rvert}

for
1<i,j<q/2
with
(i,q)=(j,q)=1
. Then

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Ac\right\rVert}_{L^{1}}=\sum_{j\neq 1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\sigma_{j}(u)\right\rvert}\right\rvert},

and since
\sum_{j}\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\sigma_{j}(u)\right\rvert}=0
we have
2(D+1)h(u)\leq 2\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Ac\right\rVert}_{L^{1}}
, so

\frac{1}{2(D+1)}\leq\frac{h(u)}{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Ac\right\rVert}_{L^{1}}}\leq\frac{1}{D+1}.

Define

\displaystyle N(q,T)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\{c\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Ac\right\rVert}_{L^{1}}<T\}.

Then
U^{+}_{\cyc}(q,B)
is between
N(q,B(D+1))
and
N(q,2B(D+1))
.

Now we can explicitly compute the action of
A
. For a nontrivial even Dirichlet character
\chi
modulo
q
, define a vector
(v_{\chi})_{i}=\chi(i)
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\chi
be a nontrivial even Dirichlet character modulo
q
, with conductor
f
, induced by
\chi_{f}
. Then

Av_{\chi}=\lambda_{\chi}v_{\overline{\chi}}

where

\lambda_{\chi}=-\frac{1}{2}\tau(\chi_{f})L(1,\overline{\chi_{f}}).

In particular, for every
\epsilon>0
,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda_{\chi}\right\rvert}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{f}\,\mathinner{\!\left\lvert L(1,\overline{\chi_{f}})\right\rvert}\geq C_{\epsilon}\sqrt{p}\,q^{-\epsilon}.

Proof.

First we record the following identity:

\displaystyle\sum_{(a,q)=1}\chi(a)\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\zeta_{q}^{a}\right\rvert}=-\tau(\chi_{f})L(1,\overline{\chi_{f}}).

To derive this, write
q=p^{r}
and
f=p^{s}
. We have

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq p^{r}\\
a\equiv b\,(p^{s})\end{subarray}}(1-\zeta_{p^{r}}^{a})=1-\zeta_{p^{s}}^{b},

so

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq p^{r}\\
(a,p)=1\end{subarray}}\chi(a)\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\zeta_{p^{r}}^{a}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq b\leq p^{s}\\
(b,p)=1\end{subarray}}\chi_{f}(b)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq p^{r}\\
a\equiv b\,(p^{s})\end{subarray}}\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\zeta_{p^{r}}^{a}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq b\leq p^{s}\\
(b,p)=1\end{subarray}}\chi_{f}(b)\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\zeta_{p^{s}}^{b}\right\rvert}.

By
[
5
, Theorem 4.9]
,

\displaystyle L(1,\overline{\chi_{f}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\tau(\overline{\chi_{f}})}{f}\sum_{(b,f)=1}\chi_{f}(b)\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\zeta_{f}^{b}\right\rvert},

and since
\chi_{f}
is even,
\tau(\chi_{f})\tau(\overline{\chi_{f}})=\chi(-1)f=f
and the identity follows.
Now we compute
(Av_{\chi})_{j}
. Using evenness of
\chi
we have

\displaystyle(Av_{\chi})_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<i<q/2\\
(i,q)=1\end{subarray}}\chi(i)\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{1-\zeta_{q}^{ij}}{1-\zeta_{q}^{j}}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq q\\
(i,q)=1\end{subarray}}\chi(i)\left[\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\zeta_{q}^{ij}\right\rvert}-\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\zeta_{q}^{j}\right\rvert}\right].

where we also extend the range of the summation from
1<i<q
to
1\leq i\leq q
since the summand vanishes for
i=1,q-1
. Since
\chi
is nontrivial, the second term vanishes. Changing variables
k=ij
in the first term yields

\displaystyle(Av_{\chi})_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\overline{\chi(j)}\sum_{(k,q)=1}\chi(k)\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\zeta_{q}^{k}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\overline{\chi(j)}\tau(\chi_{f})L(1,\overline{\chi_{f}}),

Thus
Av_{\chi}=\lambda_{\chi}v_{\overline{\chi}}
. Taking absolute values and using
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\tau(\chi_{f})\right\rvert}=\sqrt{f}
gives

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda_{\chi}\right\rvert}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{f}\,\mathinner{\!\left\lvert L(1,\overline{\chi_{f}})\right\rvert}.

Finally, by Siegel’s lower bound for Dirichlet
L
-functions
[
3
, Ch. 21]
,
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert L(1,\chi_{f})\right\rvert}\geq C_{\epsilon}f^{-\epsilon}
, so

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda_{\chi}\right\rvert}\geq C_{\epsilon}\sqrt{p}\,q^{-\epsilon}.

∎

Set

N=D+1=\phi(q)/2,\qquad G=(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}/\{\pm 1\}.

For
a\in G\setminus\{1\}
, let
e_{a}
be the basis vector with a
1
at coordinate
a
and
0
elsewhere. Then

e_{a}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\chi\neq 1}\left(\overline{\chi(a)}-1\right)v_{\chi}.

The
v_{\chi}
are not quite orthogonal, but we have

\langle v_{\chi},v_{\psi}\rangle=\sum_{b\in G\setminus\{1\}}\chi(b)\overline{\psi(b)}=N\delta_{\chi,\psi}-1.

Therefore, for any coefficients
c_{\chi}
,

\left\|\sum_{\chi\neq 1}c_{\chi}v_{\chi}\right\|_{2}^{2}=N\sum_{\chi\neq 1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert c_{\chi}\right\rvert}^{2}-\left|\sum_{\chi\neq 1}c_{\chi}\right|^{2}\leq N\sum_{\chi\neq 1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert c_{\chi}\right\rvert}^{2}.

Using
Av_{\chi}=\lambda_{\chi}v_{\overline{\chi}}
and relabeling
\chi
by
\overline{\chi}
, we get

A^{-1}e_{a}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\chi\neq 1}\frac{\chi(a)-1}{\lambda_{\chi}}v_{\chi}.

Hence

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A^{-1}e_{a}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\chi\neq 1}\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\chi(a)-1\right\rvert}^{2}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda_{\chi}\right\rvert}^{2}}.

If
\chi
has conductor
f_{\chi}=p^{s}
, then

\frac{1}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda_{\chi}\right\rvert}^{2}}\ll_{\epsilon}q^{2\epsilon}\frac{1}{p^{s}},

so after grouping characters by conductor we get

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A^{-1}e_{a}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}^{2}\ll_{\epsilon}\frac{q^{2\epsilon}}{N}\sum_{s=1}^{r}\frac{1}{p^{s}}\sum_{f_{\chi}=p^{s}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\chi(a)-1\right\rvert}^{2}\ll\frac{q^{2\epsilon}}{N}\sum_{s=1}^{r}\frac{N_{s}}{p^{s}},

where
N_{s}
is the number of nontrivial even characters with conductor
p^{s}
. We have

N_{1}=\frac{p-3}{2},\qquad N_{s}=\frac{\phi(p^{s})-\phi(p^{s-1})}{2}=\frac{p^{s-2}(p-1)^{2}}{2}\quad(s\geq 2).

Therefore
N_{s}/p^{s}\ll 1
, and since
r\ll\log q
, after renaming
\epsilon
we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A^{-1}e_{a}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}\ll_{\epsilon}q^{-1/2+\epsilon}.

Let
\rho=2B(D+1)
, and let
{\mathcal{B}}_{\rho}
be the
L^{1}
-ball of radius
\rho
in
\mathbb{R}^{D}
. We rewrite the problem as

\displaystyle N(q,2B(D+1))=\#(\mathbb{Z}^{D}\cap A^{-1}{\mathcal{B}}_{\rho}).

Now
A^{-1}{\mathcal{B}}_{\rho}
is a convex polytope spanned by the vertices
\pm\rho A^{-1}e_{a}
. The vertices have norm at most

R=C_{\epsilon}B(D+1)q^{-1/2+\epsilon}.

Now we are ready to apply the Davenport lemma. Let
{\mathcal{P}}=A^{-1}{\mathcal{B}}_{\rho}
. For a projection indexed by
I
, write
d=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I\right\rvert}
. Each projection
\pi_{I}({\mathcal{P}})
is also a convex polytope, spanned by at most
2D
vertices, and having circumradius at most
R
. To bound the volume, note that we can cover
\pi_{I}({\mathcal{P}})
with
\binom{2D}{d+1}
simplices, defining each simplex by choosing a subset of
d+1
vertices. The volume of a
d
-simplex with circumradius
R
is maximized when the simplex is regular. The volume of a regular
d
-simplex is

\displaystyle R^{d}\frac{(d+1)^{(d+1)/2}}{d^{d/2}d!}\ll\frac{R^{d}}{(d-1)!}.

Summing over all projections and all covering simplices gives

\displaystyle\#(\mathbb{Z}^{D}\cap{\mathcal{P}})

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{d=1}^{D}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I\right\rvert}=d\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Vol}(\pi_{I}({\mathcal{P}}))

\displaystyle\ll 1+\sum_{d=1}^{D}\binom{D}{d}\binom{2D}{d+1}\frac{R^{d}}{(d-1)!}.

Using

\binom{n}{k}\leq\left(\frac{en}{k}\right)^{k},\quad n!\geq\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}

we bound the log of the summand as follows.

\displaystyle\log\binom{D}{d}\binom{2D}{d+1}\frac{R^{d}}{(d-1)!}

\displaystyle\leq\log D+d\log D+d\log 2D+d\log R-3d\log d+3d

\displaystyle=\log D+d\log 2D^{2}R-3d\log d+3d.

Differentiating and solving
\log(2D^{2}R)-3\log d=0
, we see the maximum is achieved at
d^{*}=\min(D,(2D^{2}R)^{1/3})
. If
R\geq D
, then
d^{*}=D
and the log of the sum is

\displaystyle\leq 2\log D+D\log 2D^{2}R-3D\log D+3D

\displaystyle\leq 2\log D+D\log(2R/D)+3D.

Plugging in
R=C_{\epsilon}BDq^{-1/2+\epsilon}
we have

\displaystyle N(q,2B(D+1))

\displaystyle\ll D^{2}\left(C_{\epsilon}Bq^{-1/2+\epsilon}\right)^{D}

\displaystyle\ll q^{2}\left(C_{\epsilon}Bq^{-1/2+\epsilon}\right)^{\phi(q)/2-1}.

This is the expected asymptotic in the regime where the field is fixed and
B
grows. On the other hand, unconditionally we have
d^{*}\leq(2D^{2}R)^{1/3}
. The log of the sum is then

\displaystyle\leq 2\log D+d^{*}\log 2D^{2}R-3d^{*}\log d^{*}+3d^{*}

\displaystyle\leq 2\log D+3(2D^{2}R)^{1/3}.

This gives

\displaystyle N(q,2B(D+1))

\displaystyle\ll D^{2}\exp\left(C_{\epsilon}B^{1/3}Dq^{-1/6+\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\ll q^{2}\exp\left(C_{\epsilon}B^{1/3}q^{5/6+\epsilon}\right).

∎

We can also establish a lower bound for the number of cyclotomic units of bounded height by showing that the generators
\xi_{a}
have height bounded by an absolute constant. Indeed,

Proposition 4
.

For sufficiently large
B
,
\log U_{\cyc}(q,B)\gg_{B}\log q
.

Proof.

Recall that the height of
\xi_{a}
is given by

h(\xi_{a})=\frac{1}{2(D+1)}\sum_{(j,q)=1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{1-\zeta_{q}^{aj}}{1-\zeta_{q}^{j}}\right\rvert}\right\rvert}.

By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle h(\xi_{a})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{(j,q)=1}\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\zeta_{q}^{aj}\right\rvert}\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\zeta_{q}^{j}\right\rvert}\right\rvert}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\phi(q)}\sum_{(j,q)=1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\zeta_{q}^{j}\right\rvert}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\log\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\zeta_{q}^{j}\right\rvert}\right\rvert}.

Using the identity
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\zeta_{q}^{j}\right\rvert}=2\sin(\pi j/q)
, we see that this is a Riemann sum for the integral

\int_{0}^{1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\log(2\sin(\pi x))\right\rvert}\,dx,

and it is easy to check that Riemann sums for this integral converge. Thus
h(\xi_{a})\leq C
for some absolute constant
C>0
.

Now if
u=\prod_{a}\xi_{a}^{c_{a}}
with
\sum_{a}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert c_{a}\right\rvert}\leq\lfloor B/C\rfloor
, we have
h(u)<B
. The number of such
c_{a}
is given by the formula

\sum_{k=0}^{\min(D,\lfloor B/C\rfloor)}2^{k}\binom{D}{k}\binom{\lfloor B/C\rfloor}{k}.

In particular, for
q
sufficiently large in terms of
B
, taking the
k=\lfloor B/C\rfloor
term gives
\gg_{B}q^{\lfloor B/C\rfloor}
. We obtain our theorem after taking logs.

∎

2.2
Integers

We now turn to counting integers. As with units, a polynomial lower bound is easy to see. To construct an integer of height
<B
, we may take any

x=\sum_{i=0}^{D-1}c_{i}\zeta_{q}^{i}

with
\sum_{i}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert c_{i}\right\rvert}<e^{B}
, then each conjugate has absolute value
<e^{B}
. If
n=\lceil e^{B}\rceil-1
, then like before there are

\sum_{k=0}^{\min(D,n)}2^{k}\binom{D}{k}\binom{n}{k}\gg_{B}q^{n}

such choices of coefficients for
q
sufficiently large in terms of
B
. We now prove the upper bound for integers.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Write
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})
and
D=\varphi(q)
. Choose representatives
(\sigma_{i})_{1\leq i\leq D/2}
for the Galois group up to conjugation, and define the real Minkowski embedding

\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{D},\qquad\Phi(\alpha)=\left(\operatorname{Re}\sigma_{1}(\alpha),\operatorname{Im}\sigma_{1}(\alpha),\operatorname{Re}\sigma_{2}(\alpha),\operatorname{Im}\sigma_{2}(\alpha),\dots\right).

If
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{q}]
, then only the archimedean places contribute to the Weil height, hence

h(\alpha)=\frac{1}{D}\sum_{\tau\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}}\log^{+}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\tau(\alpha)\right\rvert}=\frac{1}{D}\sum_{i=1}^{D/2}\log^{+}\left((\operatorname{Re}\sigma_{i}(\alpha))^{2}+(\operatorname{Im}\sigma_{i}(\alpha))^{2}\right).

Define

R_{1}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{D}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\sum_{i=1}^{D/2}\log^{+}\left(x_{2i-1}^{2}+x_{2i}^{2}\right)\leq DB\right\},

Then

I(q,B)=\#(\Phi(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{q}])\cap R_{1}).

Now enlarge
R_{1}
by replacing the disk at each complex place by a square:

R_{2}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{D}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\sum_{i=1}^{D/2}\log^{+}\max\bigl(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{2i-1}\right\rvert}^{2},\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{2i}\right\rvert}^{2}\bigr)\leq DB\right\}.

This clearly contains
R_{1}
, so

I(q,B)\leq\#(\Phi(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{q}])\cap R_{2}).

Set

L=\left\lceil\frac{DB}{\log 2}\right\rceil+\frac{D}{2},

and

\mathcal{K}=\left\{k=(k_{i})_{1\leq i\leq D/2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{D/2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\sum_{i=1}^{D/2}k_{i}=L\right\}.

For each
k\in\mathcal{K}
, we define a box

Q(k)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{D}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{2i-1}\right\rvert},\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{2i}\right\rvert}\leq 2^{k_{i}/2}\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq D/2\right\}.

All these boxes have the same real volume

\Vol(Q(k))=\prod_{i=1}^{D/2}\left(2^{1+k_{i}/2}\right)^{2}=2^{L+D}.

Now let
x\in R_{2}
and define

\kappa_{i}=\max\left(0,\left\lceil\log_{2}\max\bigl(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{2i-1}\right\rvert}^{2},\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{2i}\right\rvert}^{2}\bigr)\right\rceil\right).

Then
\sum_{i}\kappa_{i}\leq L
. If we choose
k\in\mathcal{K}
with
k_{i}\geq\kappa_{i}
for all
i
, then
x\in Q(k)
. Thus the family of boxes
Q(k)
covers
R_{2}
.
Taking a maximum over all boxes we have

I(q,B)\ll\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{K}\right\rvert}\max_{k}\#(\Phi(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{q}])\cap Q(k)).

By stars and bars, the number of such
k
is

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{K}\right\rvert}=\binom{L+D/2-1}{D/2-1}\leq\exp(C_{1}D(B+1)).

Write

Q(k)=\prod_{t=1}^{D}[-\ell_{t}/2,\ell_{t}/2]\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}.

For the two real coordinates attached to a fixed
\sigma_{i}
, the corresponding side lengths are both equal to
2^{1+k_{i}/2}
.

Now let
M
be the
D\times D
matrix defined by

\displaystyle M_{2j-1,i}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}(\sigma_{j}\zeta_{q}^{i-1})

\displaystyle M_{2j,i}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Im}(\sigma_{j}\zeta_{q}^{i-1})

for
1\leq i\leq D
and
1\leq j\leq D/2
. Then
\Phi(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{q}])=M\mathbb{Z}^{D}
, so

\#(\Phi(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{q}])\cap Q)=\#(\mathbb{Z}^{D}\cap M^{-1}Q).

Applying Davenport’s lemma gives

\#(\mathbb{Z}^{D}\cap M^{-1}Q)\leq 1+\sum_{\emptyset\neq I\subset[D]}h^{D-\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I\right\rvert}}\Vol(\pi_{I}(M^{-1}Q)),

and since
M^{-1}Q
is convex, every line meets
\pi_{I}(M^{-1}Q)
in at most one interval, so we may set
h=1
.Fix
j
and sum over all
I\subset[D]
with
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I\right\rvert}=j
. Since
Q
is a box, each
\pi_{I}(M^{-1}Q)
is a zonotope, and the zonotope volume formula gives

\Vol(\pi_{I}(M^{-1}Q))\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J\right\rvert}=j\end{subarray}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert[M^{-1}]_{I,J}\right\rvert}\prod_{t\in J}\ell_{t}.

Hence by Cauchy-Schwarz,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I\right\rvert}=j\end{subarray}}\Vol(\pi_{I}(M^{-1}Q))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J\right\rvert}=j\end{subarray}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert[M^{-1}]_{I,J}\right\rvert}\prod_{t\in J}\ell_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J\right\rvert}=j\end{subarray}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert[M^{-1}]_{I,J}\right\rvert}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J\right\rvert}=j\end{subarray}}\prod_{t\in J}\ell_{t}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

For
0\leq j\leq D
, define

E_{j}(Q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J\right\rvert}=j\end{subarray}}\prod_{t\in J}\ell_{t}.

The second factor is

\binom{D}{j}^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J\right\rvert}=j\end{subarray}}\prod_{t\in J}\ell_{t}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\binom{D}{j}^{1/2}E_{j}(Q),

since
\sqrt{\sum x_{i}^{2}}\leq\sum x_{i}
.
Also,

\sum_{j=0}^{D}E_{j}(Q)=\prod_{t=1}^{D}(1+\ell_{t})\ll\prod_{i=1}^{D/2}(1+C2^{k_{i}/2})^{2}\ll C_{2}^{D}2^{\sum_{i}k_{i}}\leq\exp(C_{3}D(B+1)).

For the first factor, Jacobi’s complementary minor identity gives

[M^{-1}]_{I,J}=\pm\frac{[M]_{J^{c},I^{c}}}{\det(M)}.

Therefore

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J\right\rvert}=j\end{subarray}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert[M^{-1}]_{I,J}\right\rvert}^{2}=\frac{1}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\det(M)\right\rvert}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I^{\prime},J^{\prime}\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I^{\prime}\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J^{\prime}\right\rvert}=D-j\end{subarray}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert[M]_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}}\right\rvert}^{2}.

By Cauchy-Binet, for each fixed
I^{\prime}\subset[D]
with
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I^{\prime}\right\rvert}=D-j
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J^{\prime}\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J^{\prime}\right\rvert}=D-j\end{subarray}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert[M]_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}}\right\rvert}^{2}=[MM^{T}]_{I^{\prime},I^{\prime}}.

Therefore

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I^{\prime},J^{\prime}\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I^{\prime}\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J^{\prime}\right\rvert}=D-j\end{subarray}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert[M]_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}}\right\rvert}^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I^{\prime}\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I^{\prime}\right\rvert}=D-j\end{subarray}}[MM^{T}]_{I^{\prime},I^{\prime}}.

Each row of
M
has Euclidean norm at most
\sqrt{D}
, so every diagonal entry of
MM^{T}
is at most
D
. Hence Hadamard’s inequality gives

[MM^{T}]_{I^{\prime},I^{\prime}}\leq D^{D-j},

and therefore

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I^{\prime},J^{\prime}\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I^{\prime}\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J^{\prime}\right\rvert}=D-j\end{subarray}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert[M]_{I^{\prime},J^{\prime}}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\binom{D}{j}D^{D-j}.

Thus

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[D]\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert I\right\rvert}=j\end{subarray}}\Vol(\pi_{I}(M^{-1}Q))\leq\binom{D}{j}\frac{D^{(D-j)/2}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\det(M)\right\rvert}}E_{j}(Q).

Now use the cyclotomic discriminant formula

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\disc(K)\right\rvert}=\frac{q^{D}}{p^{D/(p-1)}},

so

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\det(M)\right\rvert}=2^{-D/2}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\disc(K)\right\rvert}^{1/2}=2^{-D/2}q^{D/2}p^{-D/(2(p-1))}.

Since
D=q(1-1/p)
, we obtain

\frac{D^{D/2}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\det(M)\right\rvert}}=\left(\sqrt{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{p}}\,p^{1/(2(p-1))}\right)^{D}\leq C_{4}^{D},

and therefore

\frac{D^{(D-j)/2}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\det(M)\right\rvert}}\leq\frac{D^{D/2}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\det(M)\right\rvert}}\leq C_{4}^{D}.

It follows that

\#(\mathbb{Z}^{D}\cap M^{-1}Q)\leq 1+C_{4}^{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}\binom{D}{j}E_{j}(Q)\leq C_{5}^{D}\sum_{j=0}^{D}E_{j}(Q)\leq\exp\left(C_{6}D(B+1)\right).

Finally, summing over all dyadic boxes gives

\log I(q,B)\ll D(B+1).

∎
