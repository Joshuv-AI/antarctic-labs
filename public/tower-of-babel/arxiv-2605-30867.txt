. 1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{n}=\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
denote the finite cyclic group equipped with the normalized counting measure
\mu_{n}
. The Poisson-like semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
applied to
f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}\chi_{k}(x)\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})
is given by

(1.1)

(P_{t}f)(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{-t\psi_{n}(k)}a_{k}\chi_{k}(x),

where
\psi_{n}(k)=\min(k,n-k)
is the word-length function on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, and
\chi_{k}(x)=e^{\frac{2\pi ikx}{n}}\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
. The hypercontractivity problem is to determine the optimal time
t_{p,q}
with
1<p\leq q<\infty
such that

\lVert P_{t}f\rVert_{q}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{p}\qquad\text{for all }t\geq t_{p,q}.

A standard series expansion of
\lVert P_{t}(1+\epsilon f)\rVert_{q}
and
\lVert 1+\epsilon f\rVert_{p}
at
\epsilon=0
shows the universal lower bound

t_{p,q}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\log\big(\tfrac{q-1}{p-1}\big).

The main result of this article is that this bound is sharp for every
n\geq 4
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
n\geq 4
, we have

\lVert P_{t}f\rVert_{q}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{p}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad t\geq\frac{1}{2}\log(\frac{q-1}{p-1})

for
1<p\leq q<\infty
.

Before the present work, the known results were confined to a few special cases. For the case
n=2
, the optimal time
t_{p,q}=\tfrac{1}{2}\log\big(\tfrac{q-1}{p-1}\big)
follows from the classical two-point inequality. That inequality was first proved by Bonami
[
6
]
, and later rediscovered by Nelson
[
19
]
, Gross
[
14
]
, and Beckner
[
3
]
. The case
n=3
is drastically different: using Wolff’s reduction
[
23
, Corollary 3.1]
, it was established in
[
17
,
20
]
that the optimal time
t_{2,q}
for
P_{t}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
is

t_{2,q}=\frac{1}{2}\log\!\Bigg(\frac{\frac{2}{3}\big(\frac{1}{3}\big)^{\frac{2}{q}-1}-\frac{1}{3}\big(\frac{2}{3}\big)^{\frac{2}{q}-1}}{\big(\frac{2}{3}\big)^{\frac{2}{q}}-\big(\frac{1}{3}\big)^{\frac{2}{q}}}\Bigg),

whereas the optimal time for general
p,q
was determined recently by Cao, Fan, Han, Qiu, and Wang
[
7
]
. For
n\geq 4
, a few isolated cases were studied:

•

n=4
:
t_{p,q}=\tfrac{1}{2}\log\big(\tfrac{q-1}{p-1}\big)
by Beckner, Janson, and Jerison
[
5
]
in 1983. They also conjectured that this optimal time holds for all
n\neq 3
.

•

n=5
:
t_{2,q}=\tfrac{1}{2}\log(q-1)
for
q\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{+}
by Andersson
[
1
]
in 2002.

•

n\geq 6
:
t_{2,q}=\tfrac{1}{2}\log(q-1)
for
q\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{+}
for even
n
and the same holds for odd
n
whenever
n\geq q
by Junge, Palazuelos, Parcet, and Perrin
[
16
]
in 2017.

•

n\in\{2^{k},3\cdot 2^{k}\}
with
k\geq 1
:
t_{p,q}=\tfrac{1}{2}\log\big(\tfrac{q-1}{p-1}\big)
by our work
[
24
]
in 2025.

Hypercontractivity has also been extensively studied for semigroups on manifolds. Weissler
[
22
]
proved optimal hypercontractivity for the heat and Poisson semigroups on the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, while Rothaus
[
21
]
independently treated the heat case. For related results on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, see
[

,
15
,
4
,

.

It is known that the word-length function
\psi_{n}
is conditionally negative; see, e.g.
[
16
]
. In this setting, a standard route to hypercontractivity proceeds through Log–Sobolev inequalities (LSI), following Gross’s celebrated work
[
14
]
:
\lVert P_{t}f\rVert_{q}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{p}
holds whenever
t\geq\frac{C}{4}\log\big(\frac{q-1}{p-1}\big)
if and only if the corresponding LSI holds with constant
C
. Thus it is enough to establish the following
n
–LSI with the optimal constant
2
for all
n\geq 4
. The case
n=4
follows from
[
5
]
together with Gross’s extrapolation technique
[
14
]
, while
n=3
is rather different; see
[
2
,
11
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
n\geq 4
, we have the following LSI with the optimal constant
2
:

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}f^{2}\log f^{2}\differential\mu_{n}-\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\log\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq 2\langle f,A_{\psi_{n}}f\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})},\qquad f\in L_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n}),

where
A_{\psi_{n}}
is the generator of the semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
.

As a consequence, we recover Weissler’s hypercontractive estimate and LSI for the Poisson semigroup on the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. The Poisson semigroup
(\mathcal{P}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
applied to
f(\theta)=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{m}e^{im\theta}\in L_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\frac{\differential\theta}{2\pi})
is given by

(\mathcal{P}_{t}f)(\theta)=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{m}e^{-t\lvert m\rvert}e^{im\theta}.

Corollary 1.3

(
[
22
]
)
.

We have

\lVert\mathcal{P}_{t}f\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad t\geq\frac{1}{2}\log(\frac{q-1}{p-1})

for
1<p\leq q<\infty
. Equivalently, for every
f\geq 0
almost everywhere with Fourier expansion
f(\theta)=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{m}e^{im\theta}
, we have

\int f^{2}\log f\leq\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\lvert m\rvert\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2}+\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{2}\log\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}

provided the right-hand side is finite.

It is worth noting that Theorem
1.2
also yields the sharp LSI for the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Earlier exact results were known for all even
n
and for odd
n\leq 21
; see
[

,
9
,
8
,

]
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
K
be the Markov kernel of the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, given by

K(x,y)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},&y=x\pm 1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

with indices taken modulo
n
. Then, for every
n\geq 4
, we have the following LSI with the optimal constant
\frac{2}{1-\cos(\frac{2\pi}{n})}
:

(1.2)

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}f^{2}\log f^{2}\differential\mu_{n}-\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\log\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{1-\cos(\frac{2\pi}{n})}\langle f,(I-K)f\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})},\qquad f\in L_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n}).

The main novelty of this work is a reduction that converts an optimization problem involving
logarithmic terms
into the analysis of explicit
low-degree polynomials
. More precisely, we show that the
n
–LSI follows from the nonnegativity of an explicit polynomial of degree at most
3
on the region
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}:0<\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}^{2}<n\}
. We further observe that this polynomial admits a simple representation on the Fourier side, which enables suitable estimates. Building on this observation, we carry out a substantial technical analysis of the resulting polynomial inequalities, based on coefficient decompositions with careful use of arithmetic–geometric mean arguments.

The article is organized as follows. For completeness, Section
2
reviews the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) approach to the
n
–LSI from
[
24
]
. In Section
3
, we reduce the
n
–LSI to a polynomial inequality

Q_{n,2}(x)+Q_{n,4}(x)-Q_{n,3}(x)\geq 0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor},

where each
Q_{n,d}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
. Section
4
proves this inequality in the odd case, with
n=5
treated separately, and Section
5
deals with the even case.

2.
Preliminary results and definitions

Let
F_{n}
denote the
n\times n
discrete Fourier transform (DFT) matrix

F_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&\cdots&1\\
1&\omega&\omega^{2}&\cdots&\omega^{n-1}\\
1&\omega^{2}&\omega^{4}&\cdots&\omega^{2(n-1)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\omega^{n-1}&\omega^{2(n-1)}&\cdots&\omega^{(n-1)(n-1)}\end{pmatrix},

where
\omega=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n}}
and
(F_{n})_{j,k}=\omega^{jk}
for
0\leq j,k\leq n-1
. It is well known that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}F_{n}
is unitary (see, e.g.,
[
10
, Section 2.5]
). For a column vector
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we write its DFT as

\hat{x}=(\hat{x}_{0},\ldots,\hat{x}_{n-1})^{\mathrm{T}}=F_{n}x,\qquad\hat{x}_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x_{j}\omega^{jk},\quad 0\leq k\leq n-1.

Given a non-zero vector
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
, define the entropy functional on the vector
\lambda
by

\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda]\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{2}\log(\lambda_k^2)-\frac{\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}^{2}}{n}\log(\frac{\norm{\lambda}_2^2}{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{2}\log(\frac{n\lambda_k^2}{\norm{\lambda}_2^2}),

with the convention
0\log 0=0
. Set

\operatorname{diag}(\psi_{n})\coloneqq\operatorname{diag}\big(\psi_{n}(0),\psi_{n}(1),\ldots,\psi_{n}(n-1)\big)\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\psi_{n}(k)=\min(k,n-k)
is the word-length function on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Let

\Psi(n)=\frac{1}{n}F_{n}\operatorname{diag}(\psi_{n})F_{n}^{-1}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

By identifying
f
with its value vector
(f(0),\dots,f(n-1))^{\mathrm{T}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, an easy consequence of the unitarity of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}F_{n}
is that the
n
–LSI

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}f^{2}\log f^{2}\differential\mu_{n}-\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\log\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq 2\langle f,A_{\psi_{n}}f\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})},\qquad f\in L_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n}),

is equivalent to

(2.1)

\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda]\leq 2\langle\lambda,\Psi(n)\lambda\rangle,\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.

Moreover,
\Psi(n)
is a real symmetric matrix (see, e.g.,
[
24
, Lemma 2.1]
).

Since both
\langle\lambda,\Psi(n)\lambda\rangle
and
\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda]
are homogeneous of degree
2
, it suffices to study the functional
g(\lambda)\coloneqq 2\langle\lambda,\Psi(n)\lambda\rangle-\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda]
on the nonnegative part of the unit sphere

\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\coloneqq\Big\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}:\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{2}=1\Big\}.

Accordingly, we consider the optimization problem

\min_{\lambda\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}2\langle\lambda,\Psi(n)\lambda\rangle-\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda],

and analyze the associated Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) system. In particular, to prove the nonnegativity of
2\langle\lambda,\Psi(n)\lambda\rangle-\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda]
, it suffices to show that a modification of the corresponding KKT system has no solution
[
24
, Lemma 2.3]
. We record this criterion from
[
24
]
for later use.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
24
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Let
Q\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be a symmetric matrix and set

g(\lambda)=2\langle\lambda,Q\lambda\rangle-\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda].

If the system

(2.2)

\begin{cases}4Q\lambda-\dfrac{4}{n}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{0}\log(\lambda_0)\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{n-1}\log(\lambda_{n-1})\end{pmatrix}-\nu=0,\\
0<\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}^{2}<n,\\
\lambda_{j}\geq 0,\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-1,\\
\lambda_{j}\nu_{j}=0,\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-1,\\
\nu_{j}\geq 0,\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-1,\end{cases}

has no solution, then
g\geq 0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
.

3.
Reduction to a polynomial inequality

The key novelty of our work is to reduce the existence of solutions to (
2.2
) to an explicit polynomial inequality of degree at most three, since the original system (
2.2
) is typically intractable.

Let
S_{n}
be the
n\times n
cyclic shift matrix, so that

(S_{n}\lambda)_{i}=\lambda_{i+1},

where all indices are taken modulo
n
. Define the forward difference operator on the
n
-cycle by

\nabla\lambda\coloneqq(I-S_{n})\lambda,\qquad(\nabla\lambda)_{i}=\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1},

and the discrete Laplacian
L=\nabla^{*}\nabla
by

L\lambda\coloneqq(I-S_{n}^{\mathrm{T}})(I-S_{n})\lambda=(2I-S_{n}-S_{n}^{\mathrm{T}})\lambda,\qquad(L\lambda)_{i}=2\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i-1}.

For
\lambda=(\lambda_{0},\dots,\lambda_{n-1})^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, write

\lambda^{2}\coloneqq(\lambda_{0}^{2},\dots,\lambda_{n-1}^{2})^{\mathrm{T}}.

We define the functional
K_{n}:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(3.1)

K_{n}(\lambda)\coloneqq 4\langle\lambda,L\Psi(n)\lambda\rangle-\frac{2}{n}\langle\lambda^{2},L\lambda\rangle.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
satisfying
0<\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}^{2}<n
, one has

(3.2)

K_{n}(\lambda)\geq 0.

Then the system (
2.2
) admits no solution. Consequently, the
n
–LSI (
2.1
) holds.

Proof.

Suppose, for contradiction, that
(\lambda^{*},\nu^{*})
is a solution of (
2.2
). We first show that
\lambda^{*}
cannot be a constant vector. Indeed, if
\lambda^{*}=c(1,\dots,1)=c\mathbf{1}
, then
0<\lVert\lambda^{*}\rVert_{2}^{2}=nc^{2}<n
implies
c\in(0,1)
. Since every coordinate of
\lambda^{*}
is strictly positive, the condition
\lambda_{j}^{*}\nu_{j}^{*}=0
and
\nu^{*}_{j}\geq 0
for all
j
gives
\nu^{*}=0
. On the other hand,
\mathbf{1}
is an eigenvector of
\Psi(n)
with eigenvalue
0
, so the first equation in (
2.2
) becomes

-\frac{4}{n}c\log c\,\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{0},

which is impossible because
c\log c\neq 0
for
0<c<1
.

Next we claim that

(3.3)

\frac{a\log a-b\log b}{a-b}<\frac{a+b}{2},\qquad a,b\geq 0,\quad a\neq b,

with the convention
0\log 0=0
. If
a,b>0
, then the Hermite–Hadamard inequality

\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\differential x<f(\frac{a+b}{2})

applied to the strictly concave function
x\mapsto 1+\log x
on
[\min(a,b),\max(a,b)]
gives

\frac{a\log a-b\log b}{a-b}<1+\log\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)\leq\frac{a+b}{2}.

If
b=0
, then

\frac{a\log a-b\log b}{a-b}=\log a<\frac{a}{2}=\frac{a+b}{2},

so (
3.3
) holds in all cases.

For each
i
, define

T_{n,i}(\lambda)\coloneqq\bigl(4(\Psi(n)\lambda)_{i}-4(\Psi(n)\lambda)_{i+1}\bigr)(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1})-\frac{2}{n}(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{i+1})(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1})^{2}.

If
\lambda_{i}^{*}\neq\lambda_{i+1}^{*}
, then by the first equation in
(
2.2
) and (
3.3
), we have

\frac{4(\Psi(n)\lambda^{*})_{i}-4(\Psi(n)\lambda^{*})_{i+1}-(\nu_{i}^{*}-\nu_{i+1}^{*})}{\lambda_{i}^{*}-\lambda_{i+1}^{*}}=\frac{4}{n}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{*}\log\lambda_{i}^{*}-\lambda_{i+1}^{*}\log\lambda_{i+1}^{*}}{\lambda_{i}^{*}-\lambda_{i+1}^{*}}<\frac{2}{n}(\lambda_{i}^{*}+\lambda_{i+1}^{*}).

Multiplying both sides by
(\lambda_{i}^{*}-\lambda_{i+1}^{*})^{2}>0
, we obtain

T_{n,i}(\lambda^{*})<(\nu_{i}^{*}-\nu_{i+1}^{*})(\lambda_{i}^{*}-\lambda_{i+1}^{*}).

Moreover, since
\lambda_{j}^{*},\nu_{j}^{*}\geq 0
and
\lambda_{j}^{*}\nu_{j}^{*}=0
for all
j
, we have

(\nu_{i}^{*}-\nu_{i+1}^{*})(\lambda_{i}^{*}-\lambda_{i+1}^{*})=-\nu_{i}^{*}\lambda_{i+1}^{*}-\nu_{i+1}^{*}\lambda_{i}^{*}\leq 0.

Hence
T_{n,i}(\lambda^{*})<0
whenever
\lambda_{i}^{*}\neq\lambda_{i+1}^{*}
. If instead
\lambda_{i}^{*}=\lambda_{i+1}^{*}
, then clearly

T_{n,i}(\lambda^{*})=0.

Since
\lambda^{*}
is not constant, there exists at least one index
i
such that
\lambda_{i}^{*}\neq\lambda_{i+1}^{*}
, and therefore

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}T_{n,i}(\lambda^{*})<0.

On the other hand,

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}T_{n,i}(\lambda)=4\langle\nabla\lambda,\nabla(\Psi(n)\lambda)\rangle-\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{i+1})(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1})^{2}.

Since
L=\nabla^{*}\nabla
, the first term is

4\langle\nabla\lambda,\nabla(\Psi(n)\lambda)\rangle=4\langle\lambda,L\Psi(n)\lambda\rangle.

Also,

\nabla(\lambda^{2})=\bigl((\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1})(\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{1}),\dots,(\lambda_{n-1}+\lambda_{0})(\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda_{0})\bigr)^{\mathrm{T}},

and hence

\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{i+1})(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1})^{2}=\frac{2}{n}\langle\nabla(\lambda^{2}),\nabla\lambda\rangle=\frac{2}{n}\langle\lambda^{2},L\lambda\rangle.

Therefore, we have

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}T_{n,i}(\lambda)=K_{n}(\lambda).

Applying this identity to
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
yields

K_{n}(\lambda^{*})<0,

which contradicts the assumption (
3.2
). Hence (
2.2
) has no solution. The final assertion now follows from Lemma
2.1
.
∎

The next step is to estimate the cubic term
\langle\lambda^{2},L\lambda\rangle
on the Fourier side. To simplify the notation, we first introduce the relevant index sets and combinatorial coefficients. Let
n\geq 4
, and for a triple
(a,b,c)\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}^{3}
define

w_{n,(a,b,c)}=\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n}).

If
n=2m+1
, define

\begin{split}&E_{2m+1}^{(1)}=\{(a,b,c):a+b=c,1\leq a\leq b\leq c\leq m\},\\
&E_{2m+1}^{(2)}=\{(a,b,c):a+b+c=2m+1,1\leq a\leq b\leq c\leq m\},\end{split}

and set

E_{2m+1}:=E_{2m+1}^{(1)}\cup E_{2m+1}^{(2)}.

If
n=2m
, define

\begin{split}&E_{2m}^{(1)}=\{(a,b,c):a+b=c,1\leq a\leq b\leq c\leq m-1\},\\
&E_{2m}^{(2)}=\{(a,b,c):a+b+c=2m,1\leq a\leq b\leq c\leq m-1\},\\
&E_{2m}^{(3)}=\{(a,b,m):a+b=m,1\leq a\leq b\leq m-1\},\end{split}

and set

E_{2m}:=E_{2m}^{(1)}\cup E_{2m}^{(2)}\cup E_{2m}^{(3)}.

Here and below,
E_{n}
denotes
E_{2m+1}
when
n=2m+1
, and
E_{2m}
when
n=2m
. The following lemma is a simple combinatorial observation, and we state it without proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
n\geq 4
, define

\mathcal{E}_{n}:=\{(r,s,t)\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}^{3}:r+s+t=0\bmod n\},

and

\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{n}:=\left\{(a,b,c)\in\{1,\dots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}^{3}:a+b+c=n,\,\text{or }\,a+b=c,\,\text{or }\,a+c=b,\,\text{or }\,b+c=a\right\}.

Define the map
\pi:\mathcal{E}_{n}\to\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
by

\pi(r,s,t)\coloneqq(\min(r,n-r),\min(s,n-s),\min(t,n-t)).

Then
\pi(\mathcal{E}_{n})=\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
, and
\pi
is two-to-one. More precisely,

\pi^{-1}(a,b,c)=\begin{cases}\{(a,b,c),(n-a,n-b,n-c)\},&a+b+c=n,\\
\{(a,b,n-c),(n-a,n-b,c)\},&a+b=c,\\
\{(a,n-b,c),(n-a,b,n-c)\},&a+c=b,\\
\{(n-a,b,c),(a,n-b,n-c)\},&b+c=a,\\
\end{cases}

For every
(a,b,c)\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
, its nondecreasing rearrangement is the unique element of
E_{n}
obtained by permuting
(a,b,c)
. Let
N_{n,(a,b,c)}
be the number of distinct ordered triples obtained by permuting
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}
. Then

N_{2m+1,(a,b,c)}=\begin{cases}1,&(a,b,c)\in E_{2m+1}^{(2)}\text{ and }a=b=c,\\
3,&(a,b,c)\in E_{2m+1}^{(2)}\text{ and exactly two of }a,b,c\text{ are equal},\\
6,&(a,b,c)\in E_{2m+1}^{(2)}\text{ and }a,b,c\text{ are distinct},\\
3,&(a,b,c)\in E_{2m+1}^{(1)}\text{ and }a=b,\\
6,&(a,b,c)\in E_{2m+1}^{(1)}\text{ and }a<b,\end{cases}

and

N_{2m,(a,b,c)}=\begin{cases}1,&(a,b,c)\in E_{2m}^{(2)}\text{ and }a=b=c,\\
3,&(a,b,c)\in E_{2m}^{(2)}\text{ and exactly two of }a,b,c\text{ are equal},\\
6,&(a,b,c)\in E_{2m}^{(2)}\text{ and }a,b,c\text{ are distinct},\\
3,&(a,b,c)\in E_{2m}^{(1)}\cup E_{2m}^{(3)}\text{ and }a=b,\\
6,&(a,b,c)\in E_{2m}^{(1)}\cup E_{2m}^{(3)}\text{ and }a<b,\end{cases}

For
n\geq 4
and a triple
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}
, define the weight
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}=\frac{N_{n,(a,b,c)}}{3}
for the triple
(a,b,c)
.
For
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
, set

Q_{n,3}(x)=\frac{1}{n^{3}}\sum_{(a,b,c)\in E_{n}}\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}w_{n,(a,b,c)}x_{a}x_{b}x_{c}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 4
, and write
n=2m
or
n=2m+1
according to parity. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
, let
\hat{\lambda}=F_{n}\lambda
and
\tilde{\lambda}=(\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{1}\rvert,\dots,\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
. If
n=2m+1
, then we have

\langle\lambda^{2},L\lambda\rangle\leq\frac{16}{n^{2}}\hat{\lambda}_{0}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+8nQ_{n,3}(\tilde{\lambda}).

If
n=2m
, then we have

\langle\lambda^{2},L\lambda\rangle\leq\frac{16}{n^{2}}\hat{\lambda}_{0}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}\right)+8nQ_{n,3}(\tilde{\lambda}).

Proof.

Define

\mu\coloneqq\lambda-\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\mathbf{1}.

By
L\mathbf{1}=0
, we have
L\lambda=L\mu
. Therefore,

\langle\lambda^{2},L\lambda\rangle=\langle(\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\mathbf{1}+\mu)^{2},L\mu\rangle=\frac{2\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\langle\mu,L\mu\rangle+\langle\mu^{2},L\mu\rangle.

Since
\hat{\mu}_{0}=0
,
\hat{\mu}_{k}=\hat{\lambda}_{k}=\overline{\hat{\lambda}_{n-k}}
for
k\neq 0
and

F_{n}LF_{n}^{-1}=F_{n}(2I-S_{n}-S_{n}^{-1})F_{n}^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}\left(0,4\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{n}),4\sin^{2}(\frac{2\pi}{n}),\dots,4\sin^{2}(\frac{(n-1)\pi}{n})\right),

Parseval’s identity yields

\langle\mu,L\mu\rangle=\frac{4}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2},

and therefore

(3.4)

\frac{2\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\langle\mu,L\mu\rangle=\frac{8}{n^{2}}\hat{\lambda}_{0}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}.

If
n=2m+1
, by
\hat{\lambda}_{k}=\overline{\hat{\lambda}_{n-k}}
for real
\lambda
and
\hat{\mu}_{k}=\hat{\lambda}_{k}
for
k\neq 0
, we get

(3.5)

\frac{2\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\langle\mu,L\mu\rangle=\frac{16}{n^{2}}\hat{\lambda}_{0}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})|\hat{\lambda}_{k}|^{2},

If
n=2m
, similarly

(3.6)

\frac{2\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\langle\mu,L\mu\rangle=\frac{16}{n^{2}}\hat{\lambda}_{0}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})|\hat{\lambda}_{k}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\hat{\lambda}_{m}|^{2}\right).

It remains to estimate the cubic term
\langle\mu^{2},L\mu\rangle
. Since

\mu_{j}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega^{-jk}\hat{\mu}_{k},\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-1,

we have

(\mu^{2})_{j}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega^{-jk}\hat{\mu}_{k})(\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\omega^{-j\ell}\hat{\mu}_{\ell}),\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-1,

and hence

\left(\widehat{\mu^{2}}\right)_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega^{jk}(\mu^{2})_{k}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,\ell=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega^{jk}\omega^{-ki}\omega^{-k\ell}\hat{\mu}_{i}\hat{\mu}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,\ell=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega^{k(j-i-\ell)}\hat{\mu}_{i}\hat{\mu}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,\ell=0\\
j=i+\ell\bmod n\end{subarray}}^{n-1}\hat{\mu}_{i}\hat{\mu}_{\ell}.

Applying Parseval’s identity again, we obtain

\begin{split}\langle\mu^{2},L\mu\rangle=&\frac{1}{n}\langle\widehat{\mu^{2}},\widehat{L\mu}\rangle=\frac{4}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\overline{\hat{\mu}_{k}}\left(\widehat{\mu^{2}}\right)_{k}=\frac{4}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\hat{\mu}_{n-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,j=0\\
\ell+j=k\bmod n\end{subarray}}^{n-1}\hat{\mu}_{\ell}\hat{\mu}_{j}\\
=&\frac{4}{n^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,j,k=0\\
\ell+j=k\bmod n\end{subarray}}^{n-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\hat{\mu}_{n-k}\hat{\mu}_{\ell}\hat{\mu}_{j}=\frac{4}{n^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,j,k=0\\
\ell+j+k=0\bmod n\end{subarray}}^{n-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\hat{\mu}_{\ell}\hat{\mu}_{j}\hat{\mu}_{k}.\end{split}

Since the product
\hat{\mu}_{\ell}\hat{\mu}_{j}\hat{\mu}_{k}
is symmetric in
(\ell,j,k)
, symmetrization gives

\langle\mu^{2},L\mu\rangle=\frac{4}{3n^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k,\ell=0\\
j+k+\ell=0\bmod n\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{n,(j,k,\ell)}\hat{\mu}_{j}\hat{\mu}_{k}\hat{\mu}_{\ell}.

Recalling
\hat{\mu}_{0}=0
and
\hat{\mu}_{k}=\hat{\lambda}_{k}=\overline{\hat{\lambda}_{n-k}}
for
k\neq 0
, we obtain

(3.7)

\langle\mu^{2},L\mu\rangle=\frac{4}{3n^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k,\ell=1\\
j+k+\ell=0\bmod n\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{n,(j,k,\ell)}\hat{\mu}_{j}\hat{\mu}_{k}\hat{\mu}_{\ell}\leq\frac{4}{3n^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k,\ell=1\\
j+k+\ell=0\bmod n\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{n,(j,k,\ell)}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{j}\rvert\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{\ell}\rvert

By Lemma
3.2
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{4}{3n^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k,\ell=1\\
j+k+\ell=0\bmod n\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{n,(j,k,\ell)}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{j}\rvert\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{\ell}\rvert&=\frac{8}{3n^{2}}\sum_{(a,b,c)\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{n}}w_{n,(a,b,c)}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{a}\rvert\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{b}\rvert\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{c}\rvert\\
&=\frac{8}{n^{2}}\sum_{(a,b,c)\in E_{n}}\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}w_{n,(a,b,c)}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{a}\rvert\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{b}\rvert\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{c}\rvert.\end{split}

Therefore, we have

(3.8)

\begin{split}\langle\mu^{2},L\mu\rangle\leq\frac{8}{n^{2}}\sum_{(a,b,c)\in E_{n}}\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}w_{n,(a,b,c)}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{a}\rvert\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{b}\rvert\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{c}\rvert=8nQ_{n,3}(\tilde{\lambda}).\\
\end{split}

Combining (
3.5
) with (
3.8
) in the odd case, and (
3.6
) with (
3.8
) in the even case, we obtain the desired estimate of
\langle\lambda^{2},L\lambda\rangle
in both cases.
∎

We now derive a lower bound of
K_{n}(\lambda)
based on the analysis on the frequency side. For
n=2m+1
and
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
, define

\begin{split}&Q_{2m+1,2}(x)=\frac{2}{(2m+1)^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}(k-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{2m+1})x_{k}^{2},\\
&Q_{2m+1,4}(x)=\frac{2}{(2m+1)^{4}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}x_{k}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{2m+1})x_{k}^{2}\right).\end{split}

For
n=2m
and
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
, define

\begin{split}&Q_{2m,2}(x)=\frac{2}{(2m)^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{2m})x_{k}^{2}+\frac{m-1}{(2m)^{2}}x_{m}^{2},\\
&Q_{2m,4}(x)=\frac{2}{(2m)^{4}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}x_{k}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}x_{m}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{2m})x_{k}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}x_{m}^{2}\right).\end{split}

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
n\geq 4
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
satisfy
0<\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}^{2}<n
. Set

\tilde{\lambda}=(\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{1}\rvert,\dots,\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\rvert)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.

Then

K_{n}(\lambda)\geq 16(Q_{n,2}(\tilde{\lambda})+Q_{n,4}(\tilde{\lambda})-Q_{n,3}(\tilde{\lambda})).

Consequently, if

Q_{n,2}(x)+Q_{n,4}(x)\geq Q_{n,3}(x)\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor},

then
K_{n}(\lambda)\geq 0
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
with
0<\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}^{2}<n
.

Proof.

Since the DFT matrix diagonalizes both
L
and
\Psi(n)
:

F_{n}\Psi(n)F_{n}^{-1}=\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{diag}(\psi_{n}),\qquad F_{n}LF_{n}^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}\left(0,4\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{n}),4\sin^{2}(\frac{2\pi}{n}),\dots,4\sin^{2}(\frac{(n-1)\pi}{n})\right),

Parseval’s identity gives

(3.9)

\langle\lambda,L\Psi(n)\lambda\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\langle\hat{\lambda},F_{n}LF_{n}^{-1}F_{n}\Psi(n)F_{n}^{-1}\hat{\lambda}\rangle=\frac{4}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\psi_{n}(k)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}.

We treat the odd and even cases separately.

Case 1:
n=2m+1
.
By
\psi_{n}(k)=\min(k,n-k)
and
\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{n-k}\rvert=\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert
for
1\leq k\leq m
, (
3.9
) becomes

\langle\lambda,L\Psi(n)\lambda\rangle=\frac{8}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}k\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}.

By Lemma
3.3
, we obtain

(3.10)

\begin{split}K_{n}(\lambda)&\geq\frac{32}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}k\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}-\frac{2}{n}\left(\frac{16}{n^{2}}\hat{\lambda}_{0}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+8nQ_{n,3}(\tilde{\lambda})\right)\\
&=\frac{32}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(k-\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\right)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}-16Q_{n,3}(\tilde{\lambda}).\end{split}

Next, Parseval’s identity and
\hat{\lambda}_{k}=\overline{\hat{\lambda}_{n-k}}
give

\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}^{2}}{n^{2}}+\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}=\frac{\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}^{2}}{n}<1.

Since
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and
\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}^{2}<n
, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have
\hat{\lambda}_{0}<n
. It follows that

1-\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}=\frac{1-\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}^{2}}{n^{2}}}{1+\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}}>\frac{\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}}{1+\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}}>\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}}{n^{2}}.

Therefore, we have

\begin{split}\frac{32}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(k-\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\right)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}=&\frac{32}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(k-1+1-\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\right)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}\\
\geq&\frac{32}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}(k-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{32}{n^{4}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}\\
=&16Q_{n,2}(\tilde{\lambda})+16Q_{n,4}(\tilde{\lambda}).\end{split}

Combining this with (
3.10
), we conclude that

K_{n}(\lambda)\geq 16Q_{n,2}(\tilde{\lambda})+16Q_{n,4}(\tilde{\lambda})-16Q_{n,3}(\tilde{\lambda}),

which proves the theorem for odd
n=2m+1
.

Case 2:
n=2m
.
In this case, the middle Fourier coefficient
\hat{\lambda}_{m}
occurs only once in (
3.9
). By
\psi_{n}(k)=\min(k,n-k)
and
\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{n-k}\rvert=\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert
for
1\leq k\leq m
, we have

\begin{split}\langle\lambda,L\Psi(n)\lambda\rangle=\frac{8}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}k\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{2}{n}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}.\end{split}

By Lemma
3.3
, we obtain

(3.11)

\begin{split}K_{n}(\lambda)\geq&\frac{32}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}k\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{8}{n}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}-\frac{2}{n}\left(\frac{16}{n^{2}}\hat{\lambda}_{0}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}\right)+8nQ_{n,3}(\tilde{\lambda})\right)\\
=&\frac{32}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\left(k-\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\right)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\left(\frac{8}{n}-\frac{16\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n^{3}}\right)\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}-16Q_{n,3}(\tilde{\lambda}).\end{split}

Next, Parseval’s identity and the reality of
\lambda
give

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\hat{\lambda}_{0}^{2}+\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}=\frac{\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}^{2}}{n}<1.

Since
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and
\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}^{2}<n
, we have
\hat{\lambda}_{0}<n
. It follows that

1-\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}=\frac{1-\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}^{2}}{n^{2}}}{1+\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}}>\frac{\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}}{1+\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}}>\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}\right).

Using this estimate, we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{32}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\left(k-\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\right)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}=\frac{32}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\left(k-1+1-\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\right)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}\\
&\qquad\geq\frac{32}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{32}{n^{4}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2},\end{split}

and

\left(\frac{8}{n}-\frac{16\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n^{3}}\right)\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}=\frac{16}{n^{2}}\left(m-1+1-\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\right)\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}>\frac{16}{n^{2}}(m-1)\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}+\frac{16}{n^{4}}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}\right).

Adding the last two inequalities gives

\begin{split}&\frac{32}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\left(k-\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n}\right)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\left(\frac{8}{n}-\frac{16\hat{\lambda}_{0}}{n^{3}}\right)\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}\\
&\qquad\geq\frac{32}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{32}{n^{4}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}\\
&\qquad\quad+\frac{16}{n^{2}}(m-1)\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}+\frac{16}{n^{4}}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert\hat{\lambda}_{m}\rvert^{2}\right)\\
&\qquad=16Q_{n,2}(\tilde{\lambda})+16Q_{n,4}(\tilde{\lambda}).\end{split}

Combining this with (
3.11
), we conclude that

K_{n}(\lambda)\geq 16Q_{n,2}(\tilde{\lambda})+16Q_{n,4}(\tilde{\lambda})-16Q_{n,3}(\tilde{\lambda}),

which proves the theorem for even
n=2m
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Let
P_{t}^{(n)}
denote the Poisson-like semigroup on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
in (
1.1
), and let
\mathcal{P}_{t}
denote the Poisson semigroup on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
A standard series expansion of
\lVert\mathcal{P}_{t}(1+\epsilon\sin\theta)\rVert_{q}
and
\lVert 1+\epsilon\sin\theta\rVert_{p}
at
\epsilon=0
shows that the condition

t_{p,q}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\log\big(\tfrac{q-1}{p-1}\big)

is necessary. Thus it remains to prove that this condition is sufficient.

Fix
t\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\log\big(\tfrac{q-1}{p-1}\big)
. By the density of trigonometric polynomials in
L_{p}
and
L_{p}
-contractivity of
\mathcal{P}_{t}
, it is sufficient to treat the case where
f
is a trigonometric polynomial. Write
f
as

f(\theta)=\sum_{\lvert m\rvert\leq M}a_{m}e^{im\theta}.

Choose
n>2M
. Let
\omega=e^{2\pi i/n}
, and define
f_{n}:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
by

f_{n}(j)=f(2\pi j/n)=\sum_{\lvert m\rvert\leq M}a_{m}\omega^{mj}=\sum_{m=0}^{M}a_{m}\omega^{mj}+\sum_{m=1}^{M}a_{-m}\omega^{(n-m)j},\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-1.

Since
n>2M
, for every
1\leq m\leq M
, we have

\psi_{n}(m)=\psi_{n}(n-m)=m.

Therefore

\displaystyle(P_{t}^{(n)}f_{n})(j)=\sum_{m=0}^{M}e^{-tm}a_{m}\omega^{mj}+\sum_{m=1}^{M}e^{-tm}a_{-m}\omega^{(n-m)j}=\sum_{\lvert m\rvert\leq M}e^{-t\lvert m\rvert}a_{m}\omega^{mj}=(\mathcal{P}_{t}f)(2\pi j/n).

By Theorem
1.1
, we have

\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left\lvert\mathcal{P}_{t}f(2\pi j/n)\right\rvert^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\lvert f(2\pi j/n)\rvert^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Letting
n\to\infty
, the Riemann sums on both sides converge and thus

\lVert\mathcal{P}_{t}f\rVert_{L_{q}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{L_{p}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}

for every trigonometric polynomial
f
. Finally, the LSI follows from Gross’s theorem
[
14
]
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Let
\frac{1}{\alpha}
denote the log-Sobolev constant of the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. By
[
9
, Theorem 1]
, we have
2\alpha\leq\lambda
, where
\lambda=1-\cos(\frac{2\pi}{n})
is the spectral gap. So any admissible constant in (
1.2
) must be at least

\frac{1}{\alpha}\geq\frac{2}{1-\cos(\frac{2\pi}{n})}.

Therefore it suffices to prove (
1.2
).

Let
f\in L_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})
, and write

f=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j}\chi_{j},\qquad\chi_{j}(x)=e^{\frac{2\pi ijx}{n}}.

Since

(A_{\psi_{n}}\chi_{j})(x)=\psi_{n}(j)\chi_{j}(x),\qquad(K\chi_{j})(x)=\frac{1}{2}(\chi_{j}(x+1)+\chi_{j}(x-1))=\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)\chi_{j}(x),

we have

\langle f,A_{\psi_{n}}f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\psi_{n}(j)\lvert a_{j}\rvert^{2}

and

\langle f,(I-K)f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(1-\cos(\frac{2\pi j}{n})\right)\lvert a_{j}\rvert^{2}.

Therefore it remains to show that

(3.12)

\psi_{n}(j)\leq\frac{1-\cos(\frac{2\pi j}{n})}{1-\cos(\frac{2\pi}{n})},\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-1.

Since
\psi_{n}(j)=\min(j,n-j)\leq\frac{n}{2}
and
\cos(\frac{2\pi j}{n})=\cos(\frac{2\pi(n-j)}{n})
, it suffices to prove

j\left(1-\cos(\frac{2\pi}{n})\right)\leq 1-\cos(\frac{2\pi j}{n}),\qquad 0\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor.

Define

\varphi(x)=1-\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi x}{n}\right),\qquad 0\leq x\leq\frac{n}{2}.

For
a,b\geq 0
with
a+b\leq\frac{n}{2}
, we have

\varphi(a+b)-\varphi(a)-\varphi(b)=4\sin(\frac{\pi a}{n})\sin(\frac{\pi b}{n})\cos(\frac{\pi(a+b)}{n})\geq 0.

Hence
\varphi
is superadditive on
[0,\frac{n}{2}]
. By induction,

1-\cos(\frac{2\pi j}{n})=\varphi(j)\geq j\,\varphi(1)=j\left(1-\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)\right),\qquad 0\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor.

This proves (
3.12
). Applying Theorem
1.2
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}f^{2}\log f^{2}\,d\mu_{n}-\|f\|_{2}^{2}\log\|f\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\langle f,A_{\psi_{n}}f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})}\leq\frac{2}{1-\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)}\langle f,(I-K)f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})}.

Thus inequality (
1.2
) holds, and the constant is optimal.
∎

4.
Proof of Lemma
3.5
for
\mathbb{Z}_{2m+1}
with
2m+1\geq 5

We first prove Lemma
3.5
in the special case
n=5
by direct computation. The general odd case
n=2m+1\geq 7
is considerably more involved.

Proof of Lemma
3.5
in the case
n=5
.

A direct computation gives

Q_{5,2}(x)=\frac{2}{5^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{2}(k-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{5})x_{k}^{2}=\frac{5+\sqrt{5}}{100}x_{2}^{2},

Q_{5,4}(x)=\frac{2\sum_{k=1}^{2}x_{k}^{2}}{5^{4}}\sum_{k=1}^{2}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{5})x_{k}^{2}=\frac{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}{2500}((5-\sqrt{5})x_{1}^{2}+(5+\sqrt{5})x_{2}^{2}),

and

Q_{5,3}(x)=\frac{1}{5^{3}}\left(w_{5,(1,2,2)}x_{1}x_{2}x_{2}+w_{5,(1,1,2)}x_{1}x_{1}x_{2}\right)=\frac{1}{1000}((15+\sqrt{5})x_{1}x_{2}^{2}+(15-\sqrt{5})x_{1}^{2}x_{2}).

Expanding the difference yields

\begin{split}&Q_{5,2}(x)+Q_{5,4}(x)-Q_{5,3}(x)\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{2500}\left((5-\sqrt{5})x_{1}^{4}+10x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}+(5+\sqrt{5})x_{2}^{4}+25(5+\sqrt{5})x_{2}^{2}-\frac{5}{2}(15-\sqrt{5})x_{1}^{2}x_{2}-\frac{5}{2}(15+\sqrt{5})x_{1}x_{2}^{2}\right).\end{split}

Now complete the square in the bracket:

\begin{split}&(5-\sqrt{5})x_{1}^{4}+10x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}+(5+\sqrt{5})x_{2}^{4}+25(5+\sqrt{5})x_{2}^{2}-\frac{5}{2}(15-\sqrt{5})x_{1}^{2}x_{2}-\frac{5}{2}(15+\sqrt{5})x_{1}x_{2}^{2}\\
&\qquad=(5-\sqrt{5})\left(x_{1}^{2}-\frac{5}{8}(7+\sqrt{5})x_{2}\right)^{2}+10\left(x_{1}-\frac{15+\sqrt{5}}{8}\right)^{2}x_{2}^{2}+(\sqrt{5}+5)x_{2}^{4}+\frac{25}{16}(7+9\sqrt{5})x_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

It follows that

Q_{5,2}(x)+Q_{5,4}(x)-Q_{5,3}(x)\geq 0.

This proves Lemma
3.5
for
n=5
.
∎

We now turn to the case
n=2m+1\geq 7
. For a triple
(a,b,c)
, set

A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}\coloneqq\frac{w_{n,(a,b,c)}^{2}}{4\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\right)},\qquad A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}\coloneqq\frac{w_{n,(a,b,c)}^{2}}{4\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})\right)}.

Hereafter, for a triple
(a,b,c)
, the superscripts indicate whether the second or third entry of the triple is being singled out. For example, if
(a,b,c)=(2,3,4)
, then
A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}=A_{n,(2,3,4)}^{(c)}=A_{n,(2,3,4)}^{(4)}
, while
A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}=A_{n,(2,3,4)}^{(b)}=A_{n,(2,3,4)}^{(3)}
.

The following proposition gives a sufficient criterion for the estimate

Q_{n,2}(x)+Q_{n,4}(x)-Q_{n,3}(x)\geq 0

for the odd case
n=2m+1\geq 7
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
n=2m+1\geq 7
. Suppose that for every
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}
, one can choose nonnegative numbers
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}
and
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}
such that

\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}=\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}+\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}.

For
1\leq u\leq v\leq m
, define

L_{uv}\coloneqq\sum_{(u,v,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(u,v,c)}^{(c)}+\sum_{(u,b,v)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(u,b,v)}^{(b)},

and for
1\leq j\leq m
, define

R_{j}\coloneqq\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)}A_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)}+\sum_{(a,j,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,j,c)}^{(b)}A_{n,(a,j,c)}^{(b)}.

Assume moreover that

(1)

L_{uv}\leq 2
for all
1\leq u<v\leq m
;

(2)

L_{uu}\leq 1
for all
1\leq u\leq m
;

(3)

R_{j}\leq 2(j-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})
for all
1\leq j\leq m
.

Then

Q_{n,2}(x)+Q_{n,4}(x)-Q_{n,3}(x)\geq 0,\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}.

Proof.

We have

n^{3}Q_{n,3}(x)=\sum_{(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\bigl(\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}+\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}\bigr)w_{n,(a,b,c)}x_{a}x_{b}x_{c}.

Fix
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}
. By the inequality
yz\leq\frac{y^{2}}{n}+\frac{nz^{2}}{4}
, we obtain

(4.1)

\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}w_{n,(a,b,c)}x_{a}x_{b}x_{c}\leq\frac{1}{n}\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\right)x_{a}^{2}x_{b}^{2}+n\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}x_{c}^{2},

and similarly,

(4.2)

\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}w_{n,(a,b,c)}x_{a}x_{b}x_{c}\leq\frac{1}{n}\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})\right)x_{a}^{2}x_{c}^{2}+n\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}x_{b}^{2}.

Fix
(a,b)=(u,v)
and sum the first term on the right-hand side of (
4.1
) over the index
c
. Also fix
(a,c)=(u,v)
and sum the first term on the right-hand side of (
4.2
) over the index
b
. This gives

\begin{split}&\sum_{(u,v,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{n}\theta_{n,(u,v,c)}^{(c)}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}+\sum_{(u,b,v)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{n}\theta_{n,(u,b,v)}^{(b)}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{n}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}\left(\sum_{(u,v,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(u,v,c)}^{(c)}+\sum_{(u,b,v)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(u,b,v)}^{(b)}\right)\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{n}L_{uv}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}.\end{split}

Similarly, fixing
c=j
and summing the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.1
) over
(a,b)
, and fixing
b=j
and summing the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.2
) over
(a,c)
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}n\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)}A_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)}x_{j}^{2}+\sum_{(a,j,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}n\theta_{n,(a,j,c)}^{(b)}A_{n,(a,j,c)}^{(b)}x_{j}^{2}\\
&\qquad=nx_{j}^{2}\left(\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)}A_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)}+\sum_{(a,j,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,j,c)}^{(b)}A_{n,(a,j,c)}^{(b)}\right)\\
&\qquad=nx_{j}^{2}R_{j}.\end{split}

Therefore, after collecting terms by the pair
(u,v)
with
u\leq v
and by the index
j
, we obtain

n^{3}Q_{n,3}(x)\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leq u\leq v\leq m}L_{uv}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}+n\sum_{j=1}^{m}R_{j}x_{j}^{2}.

By assumptions (1) and (2),

\begin{split}&\sum_{1\leq u\leq v\leq m}L_{uv}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}\\
&\qquad=\sum_{u=1}^{m}2L_{uu}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})x_{u}^{4}+\sum_{1\leq u<v\leq m}L_{uv}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}\\
&\qquad\leq\sum_{u=1}^{m}2\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})x_{u}^{4}+\sum_{1\leq u<v\leq m}2\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}\\
&\qquad=\sum_{u=1}^{m}\sum_{v=1}^{m}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}\\
&\qquad=2\sum_{v=1}^{m}x_{v}^{2}\sum_{u=1}^{m}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})x_{u}^{2}\\
&\qquad=n^{4}Q_{n,4}(x).\end{split}

By assumption (3), we also have

\sum_{j=1}^{m}R_{j}x_{j}^{2}\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{m}(j-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})x_{j}^{2}=n^{2}Q_{n,2}(x).

Hence, we have,

n^{3}Q_{n,3}(x)\leq n^{3}Q_{n,4}(x)+n^{3}Q_{n,2}(x).

This proves the proposition.
∎

We first derive an upper bound for
A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}
that depends only on the third entry
c
.

Lemma 4.2

(Upper bounds for
A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}
)
.

For
n\geq 4
, let
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}
. Then

A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}\leq\begin{cases}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})}{4}\right),&(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)},\\
\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})}{2}\right),&(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(2)}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}
, we have either
a+b=c
or
a+b+c=n
. In both cases,

\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})=\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(a+b)}{n}).

Hence

\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})-\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})=\pm 2\sin(\frac{\pi a}{n})\sin(\frac{\pi b}{n})\cos\left(\frac{\pi c}{n}\right),

and therefore

\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})-\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})\right)^{2}=4\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n}).

It follows that

\begin{split}&\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})\right)^{2}\\
&\qquad=\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})-\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})\right)^{2}+4\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\right)\\
&\qquad=4\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})+4\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\right)\\
&\qquad=4\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\right)\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})+\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})\frac{\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})}{\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})}\right).\end{split}

Consequently,

(4.3)

A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}=\frac{(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n}))^{2}}{4\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\right)}=\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})+\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})\frac{\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})}{\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})}.

Using
\frac{xy}{x+y}\leq\frac{x+y}{4}
for
x,y>0
, we obtain

(4.4)

A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}\leq\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})}{4}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\right).

If
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}
, then
a+b=c\leq m
. Hence
\cos(\frac{\pi c}{n})\geq 0
, and so

\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})-\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})=-2\sin(\frac{\pi a}{n})\sin(\frac{\pi b}{n})\cos(\frac{\pi(a+b)}{n})\leq 0.

Thus
\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\leq\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})
. Substituting this into (
4.4
) gives

A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}\leq\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})}{4}\right),

which proves the
E_{n}^{(1)}
-case.

If
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(2)}
, then
1\leq a\leq b\leq c\leq m
. Since
\sin x
is
increasing on
[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, we have
\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})\leq\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n}),\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\leq\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})
, and hence
\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\leq 2\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})
. Substituting this into (
4.4
) gives

A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}\leq\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi c}{n})}{2}\right),

which proves the
E_{n}^{(2)}
-case.
∎

The next lemma counts the weights
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}
with fixed third entry
c=j
. It will be used later to estimate
R_{j}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
n\geq 4
, define for
1\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor

M_{j}^{(1)}\coloneqq\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(1)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)},\qquad M_{j}^{(2)}\coloneqq\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}.

For
n=2m
even, define

M_{m}^{(3)}\coloneqq\sum_{(a,b,m)\in E_{n}^{(3)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,m)}.

Then

M_{j}^{(1)}=j-1,\qquad M_{j}^{(2)}=\begin{cases}0,&3j<n,\\
\frac{1}{3},&3j=n,\\
3j-n,&3j>n,\end{cases}

and, in the even case,

M_{m}^{(3)}=m-1.

Proof.

We first compute
M_{j}^{(1)}
. If
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}
with
c=j
, then
a+b=j
and
1\leq a\leq b\leq j
, so the admissible triples are exactly

(a,b,j)=(a,j-a,j),\qquad 1\leq a\leq j-a.

If
j
is odd, there are
\frac{j-1}{2}
such triples, all with
a<b
, hence
\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}=2
. Therefore

M_{j}^{(1)}=2\cdot\frac{j-1}{2}=j-1.

If
j
is even, there are
\frac{j}{2}-1
triples with
a<b
, each with
\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}=2
, and one triple
(\frac{j}{2},\frac{j}{2},j)
with
\theta_{n,(\frac{j}{2},\frac{j}{2},j)}=1
. Hence

M_{j}^{(1)}=2\left(\frac{j}{2}-1\right)+1=j-1.

In the even case, the computation of
M_{m}^{(3)}
is identical, because the triples in
E_{n}^{(3)}
are precisely
(a,m-a,m)
with
1\leq a\leq m-a
. Thus

M_{m}^{(3)}=m-1.

Next we compute
M_{j}^{(2)}
. If
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(2)}
and
c=j
, then

1\leq a\leq b\leq j,\qquad a+b+j=n.

We consider three cases.

If
3j<n
, then
a,b,c\leq j
, so

a+b+c\leq 3j<n,

which is impossible. Hence
M_{j}^{(2)}=0
.

If
3j=n
, then necessarily
(a,b,c)=(j,j,j)
, and therefore

M_{j}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{3}.

Finally, suppose that
3j>n
. Then every admissible triple can be written as

(a,b,c)=(n-2j+t,j-t,j),

where the condition
a\leq b\leq j
is equivalent to

0\leq t\leq\left\lfloor\frac{3j-n}{2}\right\rfloor.

If
3j-n
is odd, then the first triple
(n-2j,j,j)
has weight
\theta_{n,(n-2j,j,j)}=1
, and the remaining
\lfloor\frac{3j-n}{2}\rfloor=\frac{3j-n-1}{2}
triples have weight
\theta_{n,(n-2j+t,j-t,j)}=2
. Therefore

M_{j}^{(2)}=1+2\cdot\frac{3j-n-1}{2}=3j-n.

If
3j-n
is even, then the first triple
(n-2j,j,j)
and the last triple
\left(\frac{n-j}{2},\frac{n-j}{2},j\right)
have weight
1
, while the remaining
\lfloor\frac{3j-n}{2}\rfloor-1=\frac{3j-n}{2}-1
triples have weight
\theta_{n,(n-2j+t,j-t,j)}=2
. Therefore

M_{j}^{(2)}=1+2\left(\frac{3j-n}{2}-1\right)+1=3j-n.

This completes the proof.
∎

As a second step in verifying the bound on
R_{j}
, define

B_{j}\coloneqq\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}A_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)},

for
1\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
. Since
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}\leq\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}
, the next lemma gives an upper bound for the term in
R_{j}
related to the superscript
c
.

Lemma 4.4

(Upper bound for
B_{j}
)
.

For
n\geq 4
, we have

B_{j}\leq 2(j-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n}),\qquad 1\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
, we have

(4.5)

\begin{split}B_{j}=&\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}A_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)}\\
\leq&\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})\left[\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})}{4}\right)\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(1)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}+\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})}{2}\right)\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}\right]\\
=&\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})\left[\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})}{4}\right)M_{j}^{(1)}+\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})}{2}\right)M_{j}^{(2)}\right].\end{split}

If
3j<n
, then
M_{j}^{(2)}=0
, so (
4.5
) gives

B_{j}\leq(j-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})\Bigl(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})}{4}\Bigr)\leq 2(j-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n}).

If
3j=n\geq 4
, then
\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})=\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi}{3})=\frac{1}{4}
,
j=\frac{n}{3}\geq 2
, and
M_{j}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{3}
. Hence

B_{j}\leq\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})\left[(j-1)\left(1+\frac{1}{16}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\left(1+\frac{1}{8}\right)\right]\leq 2(j-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n}).

Finally, suppose that
3j>n
. Then
M_{j}^{(1)}=j-1
and
M_{j}^{(2)}=3j-n
, so by (
4.5
) it suffices to prove

(j-1)\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})}{4}\right)+(3j-n)\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})}{2}\right)\leq 2(j-1).

This is equivalent to

(7j-2n-1)\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})\leq 4(n-2j-1).

Since
7j-2n-1=j+2(3j-n)-1>j-1\geq 0
, this is in turn equivalent to

\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})\leq\frac{4(n-2j-1)}{7j-2n-1}.

Write
j=\lfloor n/2\rfloor-t
. Because
j\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor-1
and
3j>n\geq 2\lfloor n/2\rfloor
, we have
1\leq t<\frac{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{3}
. If
n=2m+1
, then by
2t+1\leq 3t
for
t\geq 1
, the bound
t<\frac{m}{3}
, and the numerical fact
9\pi^{2}<128
, we have

\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})=\sin^{2}\left(\frac{(2t+1)\pi}{4m+2}\right)\leq\left(\frac{(2t+1)\pi}{4m+2}\right)^{2}<\frac{9\pi^{2}t^{2}}{16m^{2}}<\frac{8t}{3m}<\frac{8t}{3m-7t-3}=\frac{4(n-2j-1)}{7j-2n-1}.

If
n=2m
, then by
t<\frac{m}{3}
and
\pi^{2}<16
, we have

\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})=\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi t}{2m}\right)\leq\left(\frac{\pi t}{2m}\right)^{2}<\frac{4t}{3m}\leq\frac{8t-4}{3m-7t-1}=\frac{4(n-2j-1)}{7j-2n-1}.

Thus, in all cases,

B_{j}\leq 2(j-1)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi j}{n}\right),\qquad 1\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Now we can conclude Lemma
3.5
in the odd case
n=2m+1\geq 7
from the following theorem.

Theorem 4.5

(Explicit choice of
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}
and
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}
for odd
n\geq 7
)
.

For every odd
n=2m+1\geq 7
, let

(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})=(2,m-1,m).

Define

\Delta_{n}\coloneqq B_{m}-2(m-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n}),\qquad\delta_{n}\coloneqq\frac{\Delta_{n}}{A_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}^{(c)}}.

Choose

\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}=\begin{cases}\delta_{n},&(a,b,c)=(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*}),\\
0,&(a,b,c)\neq(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*}),\end{cases}\qquad\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}=\begin{cases}\theta_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}-\delta_{n},&(a,b,c)=(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*}),\\
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)},&(a,b,c)\neq(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*}).\end{cases}

Then
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)},\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}\geq 0
for all
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}
. Moreover, for this choice of nonnegative numbers, the hypotheses of Proposition
4.1
are satisfied. Consequently,

Q_{n,2}(x)+Q_{n,4}(x)-Q_{n,3}(x)\geq 0,\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}.

Proof.

We first estimate
\Delta_{n}
. Recall that

B_{j}=\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}A_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)},\qquad M_{j}^{(1)}=\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(1)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)},\qquad M_{j}^{(2)}=\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}.

Since we are in the odd case
n=2m+1\geq 7
, Lemma
4.3
gives

M_{m}^{(1)}=M_{m}^{(2)}=m-1.

Hence

\Delta_{n}=B_{m}-(M_{m}^{(1)}+M_{m}^{(2)})\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n})=\sum_{(a,b,m)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,m)}\left(A_{n,(a,b,m)}^{(c)}-\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n})\right).

By (
4.3
),

(4.6)

A_{n,(a,b,m)}^{(c)}-\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n})=\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n})\frac{\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})}{\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})}>0.

Therefore,

\Delta_{n}>0.

Moreover, since
\frac{xy}{x+y}\leq\frac{1}{2}
for
0<x,y<1
, we have

A_{n,(a,b,m)}^{(c)}-\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n})\leq\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n})}{2}.

Consequently,

0<\Delta_{n}\leq\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n})}{2}\sum_{(a,b,m)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,m)}=\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n})}{2}(M_{m}^{(1)}+M_{m}^{(2)})=(m-1)\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n}).

Since
n=2m+1
,

\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n})=\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{2n})\leq\left(\frac{\pi}{2n}\right)^{2}.

Also, the function
m\mapsto\frac{m-1}{(2m+1)^{2}}
is decreasing for
m\geq 3
. Hence

\Delta_{n}\leq(m-1)\left(\frac{\pi}{2n}\right)^{2}\leq 2\left(\frac{\pi}{14}\right)^{2}<\frac{1}{4}.

To prove that
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}
and
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}
are nonnegative, it suffices to show that

0\leq\delta_{n}\leq\theta_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}.

From the definition of
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}
, we have
\theta_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}=\theta_{n,(2,m-1,m)}\in\{1,2\}
. By (
4.6
), we have

A_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}^{(c)}>\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n})\geq\sin^{2}(\frac{3\pi}{7})>\frac{1}{2}.

Together with
0<\Delta_{n}<\frac{1}{4}
, this implies

0<\delta_{n}=\frac{\Delta_{n}}{A_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}^{(c)}}<1\leq\theta_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}.

Hence
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)},\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}\geq 0
for all
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}
.

We next verify the bound on
R_{j}
. Since only the weight
\theta_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}
for the triple
(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})=(2,m-1,m)
is modified, we have

\begin{split}R_{j}&=\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)}A_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)}+\sum_{(a,j,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,j,c)}^{(b)}A_{n,(a,j,c)}^{(b)}\\
&=B_{j}-\delta_{n}A_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}^{(c)}\mathbf{1}_{\{j=m\}}+\delta_{n}A_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}^{(b)}\mathbf{1}_{\{j=m-1\}},\qquad 1\leq j\leq m.\end{split}

Case
j=m
.

By the definitions of
\delta_{n}
and
\Delta_{n}
, we have

R_{m}=B_{m}-\delta_{n}A_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}^{(c)}=B_{m}-\Delta_{n}=2(m-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n}).

Case
1\leq j\leq m-2
.

By Lemma
4.4
, we have

R_{j}=B_{j}\leq 2(j-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n}).

Case
j=m-1
.

First,

\frac{A_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}^{(b)}}{A_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}^{(c)}}=\frac{A_{n,(2,m-1,m)}^{(b)}}{A_{n,(2,m-1,m)}^{(c)}}=\frac{\sin^{2}(\frac{2\pi}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})}{\sin^{2}(\frac{2\pi}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n})}<1.

Therefore,

R_{m-1}=B_{m-1}+\delta_{n}A_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}^{(b)}=B_{m-1}+\frac{\Delta_{n}}{A_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}^{(c)}}A_{n,(a_{*},b_{*},c_{*})}^{(b)}<B_{m-1}+\Delta_{n}.

Thus it is enough to prove that

B_{m-1}+\Delta_{n}\leq 2(m-2)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n}).

By (
4.5
), we get

B_{m-1}\leq\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})\left[(m-2)\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})}{4}\right)+M_{m-1}^{(2)}\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})}{2}\right)\right].

Hence

\begin{split}&2(m-2)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})-B_{m-1}\\
&\qquad\geq\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})\left[2(m-2)-(m-2)\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})}{4}\right)-M_{m-1}^{(2)}\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})}{2}\right)\right]\\
&\qquad=\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})\left[m-2-M_{m-1}^{(2)}-\frac{2M_{m-1}^{(2)}+m-2}{4}\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})\right].\\
\end{split}

Since

\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})=\sin^{2}(\frac{3\pi}{2n})\leq\left(\frac{3\pi}{2n}\right)^{2},\quad 2M_{m-1}^{(2)}+m-2>0,\quad\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})\geq\sin^{2}(\frac{2\pi}{7}),

we obtain

2(m-2)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})-B_{m-1}>\sin^{2}(\frac{2\pi}{7})\left[m-2-M_{m-1}^{(2)}-\frac{2M_{m-1}^{(2)}+m-2}{4}\left(\frac{3\pi}{2n}\right)^{2}\right].

If
n=7
, then
M_{m-1}^{(2)}=0
, so

2(m-2)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})-B_{m-1}>\left(1-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{784}\right)\sin^{2}(\frac{2\pi}{7})>0.54>\frac{1}{4}>\Delta_{n}.

If
n=9
, then
M_{m-1}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{3}
, so

2(m-2)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})-B_{m-1}>\left(\frac{5}{3}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{54}\right)\sin^{2}(\frac{2\pi}{7})>0.90>\frac{1}{4}>\Delta_{n}.

If
n=2m+1\geq 11
, then
M_{m-1}^{(2)}=m-4
, and therefore

2(m-2)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n}\right)-B_{m-1}>\sin^{2}(\frac{2\pi}{7})\left(2-\frac{9\pi^{2}(3m-10)}{16(2m+1)^{2}}\right).

Since for
m\geq 5
,

\frac{3m-10}{(2m+1)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4},

it follows that

2(m-2)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n}\right)-B_{m-1}>\left(2-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{64}\right)\sin^{2}(\frac{2\pi}{7})>0.37>\frac{1}{4}>\Delta_{n}.

Thus, in every case,

2(m-2)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n})-B_{m-1}>\Delta_{n},

and hence

R_{m-1}<B_{m-1}+\Delta_{n}\leq 2(m-2)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-1)}{n}).

Therefore,

R_{j}\leq 2(j-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n}),\qquad 1\leq j\leq m.

It remains to verify the bounds on
L_{uv}
. For each fixed pair
(u,v)
with
1\leq u\leq v\leq m
, there is at most one triple
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}
with
(a,b)=(u,v)
:

•

In
E_{n}^{(1)}
, it must be
(u,v,u+v)
, provided
u+v\leq m
;

•

In
E_{n}^{(2)}
, it must be
(u,v,n-u-v)
, provided
v\leq n-u-v\leq m
;

These two possibilities are mutually exclusive. Define

L_{uv}^{(0)}\coloneqq\sum_{(u,v,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(u,v,c)}.

Then
L_{uv}^{(0)}
is either
0
or the weight
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}
for a single triple
(a,b,c)
, and hence

\begin{cases}L_{uv}^{(0)}\leq 2\qquad\text{ if }u<v,\\
L_{uu}^{(0)}\leq 1.\end{cases}

Note that

L_{uv}=\sum_{(u,v,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(u,v,c)}^{(c)}+\sum_{(u,b,v)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(u,b,v)}^{(b)}=\begin{cases}L_{uv}^{(0)}-\delta_{n},&(u,v)=(2,m-1),\\
\delta_{n},&(u,v)=(2,m),\\
L_{uv}^{(0)},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Since
0<\delta_{n}<1
, we conclude that

\begin{cases}L_{uv}\leq 2\qquad\text{ if }u<v,\\
L_{uu}\leq 1.\end{cases}

Thus all hypotheses of Proposition
4.1
are satisfied, and the conclusion follows.
∎

5.
Proof of Lemma
3.5
for
\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
with
2m\geq 4

We now treat the case
n=2m\geq 4
, following the same strategy as in Section
4
. Unlike the odd case, one may take the trivial decomposition
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}=\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}
and
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}=0
. We therefore work directly with the original coefficients in
Q_{n,3}(x)
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
n=2m\geq 4
. For
1\leq u\leq v\leq m-1
, define

L_{uv}\coloneqq\sum_{(u,v,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}\cup E_{n}^{(3)}}\theta_{n,(u,v,c)},

and for
1\leq j\leq m
, define

R_{j}\coloneqq\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}\cup E_{n}^{(3)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}A_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)}.

Assume that

(1)

L_{uv}\leq 2
for all
1\leq u<v\leq m-1
;

(2)

L_{uu}\leq 1
for all
1\leq u\leq m-1
;

(3)

R_{j}\leq 2(j-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})
for all
1\leq j\leq m-1
;

(4)

R_{m}\leq m-1
.

Then

Q_{n,2}(x)+Q_{n,4}(x)-Q_{n,3}(x)\geq 0,\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}.

Proof.

We have

n^{3}Q_{n,3}(x)=\sum_{(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}\cup E_{n}^{(3)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}w_{n,(a,b,c)}x_{a}x_{b}x_{c}.

Fix
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}\cup E_{n}^{(3)}
. By the inequality
yz\leq\frac{y^{2}}{n}+\frac{nz^{2}}{4}
, we obtain

(5.1)

\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}w_{n,(a,b,c)}x_{a}x_{b}x_{c}\leq\frac{1}{n}\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\right)x_{a}^{2}x_{b}^{2}+n\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}x_{c}^{2}.

Fixing
(a,b)=(u,v)
and summing the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.1
) over the third index
c
, we obtain

\sum_{(u,v,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}\cup E_{n}^{(3)}}\frac{1}{n}\theta_{n,(u,v,c)}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}L_{uv}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}.

Similarly, fixing
c=j
and summing the second term on the right-hand side of (
5.1
) over the first two indices
(a,b)
, we obtain

\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}\cup E_{n}^{(3)}}n\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}A_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)}x_{c}^{2}=nR_{j}x_{j}^{2}.

Therefore,

n^{3}Q_{n,3}(x)\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leq u\leq v\leq m-1}L_{uv}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}+n\sum_{j=1}^{m}R_{j}x_{j}^{2}.

By assumptions (1) and (2),

\begin{split}&\sum_{1\leq u\leq v\leq m-1}L_{uv}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}\\
&\qquad=\sum_{u=1}^{m-1}2L_{uu}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})x_{u}^{4}+\sum_{1\leq u<v\leq m-1}L_{uv}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}\\
&\qquad\leq\sum_{u=1}^{m-1}2\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})x_{u}^{4}+\sum_{1\leq u<v\leq m-1}2\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}\\
&\qquad=\sum_{u=1}^{m-1}\sum_{v=1}^{m-1}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}\\
&\qquad=2\left(\sum_{v=1}^{m-1}x_{v}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{u=1}^{m-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})x_{u}^{2}\right).\end{split}

On the other hand,

\displaystyle n^{4}Q_{n,4}(x)

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}x_{k}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}x_{m}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})x_{k}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}x_{m}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}x_{k}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})x_{k}^{2}\right)+x_{m}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}x_{k}^{2}+x_{m}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi k}{n})x_{k}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}x_{m}^{4}.

Hence

\sum_{1\leq u\leq v\leq m-1}L_{uv}\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi u}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi v}{n})\right)x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}\leq n^{4}Q_{n,4}(x).

By assumptions (3) and (4), we have

\sum_{j=1}^{m}R_{j}x_{j}^{2}\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(j-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n})x_{j}^{2}+(m-1)x_{m}^{2}=n^{2}Q_{n,2}(x).

Therefore, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
,

n^{3}Q_{n,3}(x)\leq n^{3}Q_{n,4}(x)+n^{3}Q_{n,2}(x).

This proves the proposition.
∎

Now we can conclude Lemma
3.5
in the even case
n=2m\geq 4
from the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2

(Trivial choice of
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(b)}
and
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}
for even
n\geq 4
)
.

For every even
n=2m\geq 4
, the hypotheses of Proposition
5.1
are satisfied. Consequently,

Q_{n,2}(x)+Q_{n,4}(x)-Q_{n,3}(x)\geq 0,\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}.

Proof.

We verify the hypotheses of Proposition
5.1
.

Case
j=m
.

If
(a,b,m)\in E_{n}^{(3)}
, then
a+b=m
. Since
n=2m
, we have

\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})=\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{2m})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi(m-a)}{2m})=1.

Hence

A_{n,(a,b,c)}^{(c)}=\frac{(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{n}))^{2}}{4\left(\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi a}{n})+\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi b}{n})\right)}=\frac{(1+1)^{2}}{4}=1.

Since triples in
E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}
satisfy
c\leq m-1
, only triples in
E_{n}^{(3)}
contribute to
R_{m}
. Therefore, by Lemma
4.3
,

R_{m}=\sum_{(a,b,m)\in E_{n}^{(3)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,m)}A_{n,(a,b,m)}^{(c)}=\sum_{(a,b,m)\in E_{n}^{(3)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,m)}=M_{m}^{(3)}=m-1.

Case
1\leq j\leq m-1
.

Since every triple in
E_{n}^{(3)}
has
c=m
, we have

R_{j}=\sum_{(a,b,j)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}}\theta_{n,(a,b,j)}A_{n,(a,b,j)}^{(c)}=B_{j}.

By Lemma
4.4
, it follows that

R_{j}=B_{j}\leq 2(j-1)\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{n}),\qquad 1\leq j\leq m-1.

It remains to verify the bounds on
L_{uv}
. For each fixed pair
(u,v)
with
1\leq u\leq v\leq m-1
, there is at most one triple
(a,b,c)\in E_{n}^{(1)}\cup E_{n}^{(2)}\cup E_{n}^{(3)}
with
(a,b)=(u,v)
:

•

In
E_{n}^{(1)}
, it must be
(u,v,u+v)
, provided
u+v\leq m-1
;

•

In
E_{n}^{(3)}
, it must be
(u,v,m)
, provided
u+v=m
;

•

In
E_{n}^{(2)}
, it must be
(u,v,n-u-v)
, provided
v\leq n-u-v\leq m-1
.

These possibilities are mutually exclusive. Hence
L_{uv}
is either
0
or the weight
\theta_{n,(a,b,c)}
for a single triple
(a,b,c)
. Therefore,

\begin{cases}L_{uv}\leq 2\qquad\text{ if }u<v,\\
L_{uu}\leq 1.\end{cases}

Thus all hypotheses of Proposition
5.1
are satisfied, and the conclusion follows.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Although the direct proof above settles the even case, one can also recover it from previously known results in
[
24
]
and the odd case. Every even integer
n\geq 4
can be written as
n=n_{0}\cdot 2^{k}
with
k\geq 1
and
n_{0}
odd. The cases
n_{0}=1
and
n_{0}=3
, that is,
n\in\{2^{k},3\cdot 2^{k}\}
, were proved in
[
24
]
. For odd
n_{0}\geq 5
, the odd case established in Section
4
, together with
[
24
, Theorem 4.2]
, yields the corresponding even cases
n=n_{0}\cdot 2^{k}
. Thus all even integers
n\geq 4
can also be covered by this indirect argument.
