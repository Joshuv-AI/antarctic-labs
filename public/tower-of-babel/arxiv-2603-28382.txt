Anick Resolution for Lawvere Theories from Algebraic Discrete Morse Theory
Mirai Ikebuchi

Abstract
Inspired by Brown’s collapsing method (or discrete Morse theory) to obtain a free resolution of ℤ {\mathbb{Z}} over the monoid ring ℤ ​ M {\mathbb{Z}}M , we apply algebraic discrete Morse theory to compute the homology groups of Lawvere theories, which is defined as Tor of a certain module. We reinterpret known partial free resolutions arising from complete term rewriting systems in terms of collapsing of the normalized bar resolution. This perspective yields homological inequalities that bound the number of equational axioms in presentations and recovers classical results, such as lower bounds for group axiomatizations. Our main contribution is to extend these resolutions to higher dimensions.

Introduction

In this paper, we apply algebraic discrete Morse theory to compute the (co)homology groups of Lawvere theories.
Before stating our results, we explain the background and motivation of this work.

For a monoid (or a group)

\bigoplus_{R}{\mathbb{Z}}M\to\bigoplus_{\Gamma}{\mathbb{Z}}M\to{\mathbb{Z}}M\to{\mathbb{Z}}\to 0.

This implies the inequality

\#R-\#\Gamma\geq s(\Tor_{2}^{{\mathbb{Z}}M}({\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Z}}))-\rank(\Tor_{1}^{{\mathbb{Z}}M}({\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Z}}))

(1)

where

Note that

In

If

Brown observed that if

]

From this viewpoint, we can call (

We consider an analogue of this story for Lawvere theories.
A (single-sorted) Lawvere theory is a small category with finite products such that every object is a finite power of a distinguished object.
The notion of Lawvere theories gives an abstract framework for universal algebra

14
]

The goal of this paper is to extend the resolution (

Lemma 2.9
.

For sorts

,

.

\displaystyle\phi:H/{\sim_{\Pi}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathrm{Term}_{\Sigma}^{X_{1}}(V)\times\dots\times\mathrm{Term}_{\Sigma}^{X_{m}}(V)/{\sim_{\alpha}},

\displaystyle[\vec{\omega}]_{\sim_{\Pi}}

\displaystyle\mapsto[(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})]_{\sim_{\alpha}}\quad(\vec{\omega}=(\Gamma\mid_{\emptyset}(t_{1},\dots,t_{m}))).

Moreover, for any

is a permutation iff

is an

Proof.

We first show that

Let

.

by definition.

We construct an inverse

of

Given terms

of sorts

respectively, let

Let

Ringoids, modules, and algebraic discrete Morse theory

In this section, we review the basic definitions and facts on ringoids and modules over ringoids which are developed in
[
16
]
.
Then, we introduce algebraic discrete Morse theory for chain complexes of modules over ringoids.

3.1
Preliminaries on ringoids and modules

A
ringoid
is a small
\mathbf{Ab}
-enriched category.
A
two-sided ideal
of a ringoid
{\cal R}
is a subobject of the Hom functor
{\cal R}(-,-)=\mathrm{Hom}_{{\cal R}}(-,-):{\cal R}^{\mathrm{op}}\times{\cal R}\to\mathbf{Ab}
in the category of additive functors from
{\cal R}^{\mathrm{op}}\times{\cal R}
to
\mathbf{Ab}
.
For a set
U\subset\mathrm{Mor}({\cal R})
, we write
(U)
for the two-sided ideal

(U)(X,Y)=\ab\{\sum_{r_{1},r_{2}}r_{1}ur_{2}~\middle|~u\in U\cap{\cal R}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime}),r_{1}\in{\cal R}(j^{\prime},j),r_{2}\in{\cal R}(i,i^{\prime})\}

where the sum runs over finite combinations of
r_{1},r_{2}
.
For a ringoid
{\cal R}
and a two-sided ideal
I
of
{\cal R}
, the
quotient ringoid
is the ringoid
{\cal R}/I
such that
\mathrm{Ob}({\cal R}/I)=\mathrm{Ob}({\cal R})
and
({{\cal R}/I})(i,j)={\cal R}(i,j)/I(i,j)
for any
i,j\in\mathrm{Ob}({\cal R})
.

For a quiver (that is, a directed multigraph)
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
, the
ringoid freely generated
by
Q
is the ringoid
{\mathbb{Z}}Q
such that

\mathrm{Ob}({\mathbb{Z}}Q)=Q_{0}
, and for any
v_{1},v_{2}\in Q_{0}
,
{{\mathbb{Z}}Q}(v_{1},v_{2})
is the free abelian group generated by the set of paths from
v_{1}
to
v_{2}
in
Q
.
Also, for a set
U\subset\mathrm{Mor}({\mathbb{Z}}Q)
, the
ringoid presented
by
(Q,U)
is the quotient ringoid
{\mathbb{Z}}Q/(U)
.

A
(left)
{\cal R}
-module
for a ringoid
{\cal R}
is an additive functor
{\cal R}\to\mathbf{Ab}
.
A
right
{\cal R}
-module
is an additive functor
{\cal R}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Ab}
.
We often just say
{\cal R}
-module for a left
{\cal R}
-module in this paper.
An
{\cal R}
-linear map between (either left or right)
{\cal R}
-modules
M,N
is a natural transformation
M\to N
.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
16
]

The category of (either left or right)
{\cal R}
-modules is an abelian category with enough projectives.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a ringoid and let
{\cal X}=({\cal X}_{i})_{i\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{R})}
be a
family of sets. The
free left
\mathcal{R}
–module on
{\cal X}
is the
left
{\cal R}
-module

defined by

F({\cal X})(i)\;=\;\bigoplus_{j\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{R})}\;\bigoplus_{x\in{\cal X}_{j}}\mathcal{R}(i,j)\,{x},

the direct sum of copies of
\mathcal{R}(i,j)
indexed by generators

x\in{\cal X}_{j}
. A morphism of left
{\cal R}
-modules
F({\cal X})\to M
is determined uniquely by
the images of the elements
{x}\in F({\cal X})(j)
for
x\in{\cal X}_{j}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any left
{\cal R}
-module
M
, there is an exact sequence of
{\cal R}
-modules

F({\cal Y})\xrightarrow{\;\phi\;}F({\cal X})\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0

where:

•

{\cal X}=({\cal X}_{i})
is a family of sets of
generators

x\in{\cal X}_{i}

for
M(i)
;

•

{\cal Y}=({\cal Y}_{i})
is a family of sets of
relations
, where each

r\in{\cal Y}_{i}
is a finite
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of elements of
the form
u\cdot x
with
u\in\mathcal{R}(i,j)
,
x\in{\cal X}_{j}
;

•

the map
\phi
encodes these relations, and
M
is, up to isomorphism,
the unique module generated by the
{\cal X}_{i}
subject to the relations
{\cal Y}_{i}
.

Concretely, one often writes a presentation of a left
\mathcal{R}
-module
M

by listing:

•

the generators
x\in{\cal X}_{j}
, each labelled by the object
j
at which it
lives;

•

the relations, which are formal
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combinations

\sum_{k}u_{k}\cdot x_{k}=0

with
u_{k}\in\mathcal{R}(i,j_{k})
and
x_{k}\in{\cal X}_{j_{k}}
.

Definition 3.4
.

The
tensor product
of a right
\mathcal{R}
-module
M
and a left

\mathcal{R}
-module

is the coend

M\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}N\;:=\;\int^{i\in\mathcal{R}}M(i)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N(i),

that is, the cokernel of the pair of maps

\bigoplus_{r\colon i\to j}M(j)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N(i)\rightrightarrows\bigoplus_{i\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{R})}M(i)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N(i),

where the two arrows are given on a generator

M(r)(m)\otimes n\qquad\text{and}\qquad m\otimes N(r)(n),

for each morphism

in
\mathcal{R}
.

4.2

In this subsection, we define normalized bar resolution by applying algebraic discrete Morse theory to the unnormalized bar resolution defined in the previous subsection.

We define an equivalence relation
