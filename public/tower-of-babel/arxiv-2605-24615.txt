Discrete Laplacian Structure and Kernel Reduction of the Gap Equation in d = 4 ​ k + 3 d=4k+3 Gross–Neveu Model at Imaginary Chemical Potential
Evangelos G. Filothodoros

Abstract




\eplabstract

We observe a remarkable mathematical structure in the gap equations of the large-
N
Gross–Neveu model at imaginary chemical potential in odd spacetime dimensions
d=4k+3
. We show they can be written as the sum of two parts: one defined by higher-order discrete Laplacian patterns and a cut-off dependent part given by truncated asymptotic expansion of a hypergeometric function. We argue that this picture corresponds to a deeper relationship between thermal field theories in these odd
d
and exactly-solvable one-dimensional quantum problems. We find that the thermal mass at specific imaginary chemical potential values is fixed where internal energy balances entropic states of thermal modes which is physically equivalent with OPE inversion formula techniques where thermal mass values arise from transcendental sets of equations.

Introduction

The
U(N)
Gross-Neveu model in odd dimensions exhibits remarkable analytic properties at finite temperature. The phase structure of the model in
d
Euclidean dimensions at imaginary chemical potential
\mu=-i\alpha
and finite temperature has been studied extensively in thermal field theory and statistical transmutation
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4

but also it has been used at its regularised lattice version in order to achieve dynamical mass generation
[

.At large

\sigma
(with
z=e^{-\beta\sigma}e^{-i\beta\alpha}
) exhibits interesting regularities in odd dimensions
d=3,7,11,15,\dots
, i.e.,
d=4k+3

We begin with the calculation of the two distinct parts of the fermion condensate gap equation. One vacuum part forms a truncated asymptotic expansion of a hypergeometric function (generating a hierarchy of UV-divergent terms with decreasing powers of cut-off
\Lambda
) and one thermal part governed by higher-order discrete Laplacian contributions. The coefficients of the thermal part are interestingly the same as those of the
(2k)
-th order finite difference operators—three-point stencils for
d=7

d=11

d=15

We further observe a type of dimensional reduction: the
d
-dimensional thermal part of the thermal field theory seems equivalent to a one-dimensional quantum mechanical problem with Hamiltonian of non-interacting fermions. This correspondence arises from the thermal part of a
1d
quantum system of two twisted fermionic harmonic oscillators. The main purpose of our results is not to solve the gap equation but rather to reveal its hidden discrete and low–dimensional origin.

2.1
The thermal part

\displaystyle I_{\text{therm}}=-C_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\,p^{4k+1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p^{2}+\sigma^{2}}}\Big(\frac{1}{e^{\beta\sqrt{p^{2}+\sigma^{2}}+i\beta\alpha}+1}+

Changing variables:

\displaystyle p^{4k+1}dp=(E^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2k}\cdot\sqrt{E^{2}-\sigma^{2}}\cdot\frac{E}{\sqrt{E^{2}-\sigma^{2}}}dE=

\displaystyle(E^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2k}EdE

(13)

So:

\frac{1}{E_{p}}p^{4k+1}dp=(E^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2k}dE

(14)

Thus:

I_{\text{therm}}=-C_{d}\int_{\sigma}^{\infty}dE\,(E^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2k}\left(\frac{1}{e^{\beta E+i\beta\alpha}+1}+\frac{1}{e^{\beta E-i\beta\alpha}+1}\right)

(15)

(E^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2k}=\sum_{j=0}^{2k}\binom{2k}{j}E^{4k-2j}(-\sigma^{2})^{j}

\displaystyle I_{\text{therm}}=-C_{d}\sum_{j=0}^{2k}\binom{2k}{j}(-\sigma^{2})^{j}

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma}^{\infty}dE\,E^{4k-2j}\Big(\frac{1}{e^{\beta E+i\beta\alpha}+1}+\frac{1}{e^{\beta E-i\beta\alpha}+1}\Big)

(17)

The binomial coefficients

2k

Discrete Laplacian Structure - Dimensional ladder

In
d=4k+3
(
k=0,1,2,\dots
) the thermal part of the gap equation takes the form

\displaystyle I_{\text{therm}}^{(d)}(\sigma)=C_{d}\int_{\sigma}^{\infty}\bigl(E^{2}-\sigma^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{d-3}{2}}f_{\text{therm}}(E)dE,

(30)

\displaystyle d^{\prime}\equiv 2d-3=8k+3,

(31)

so that
\frac{d^{\prime}-3}{2}=4k

can be obtained by applying a discrete higher
-

Let

-

2k

\epsilon

[

9
]

\displaystyle\Delta_{\epsilon}^{2k}F(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{2k}(-1)^{j}\binom{2k}{j}F\!\bigl(x+(k-j)\epsilon\bigr).

(32)

Acting on
I_{\text{therm}}^{(d^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\Delta_{\epsilon}^{2k}I_{\text{therm}}^{(d^{\prime})}(x)=C_{d^{\prime}}\sum_{j=0}^{2k}(-1)^{j}\binom{2k}{j}

\displaystyle\int_{\sqrt{x+(k-j)\epsilon}}^{\infty}\bigl[E^{2}-(x+(k-j)\epsilon)\bigr]^{4k}f(E)\,dE.

(33)

Write each integral as
\int_{\sqrt{x}}^{\infty}
minus (or plus) a boundary piece over the interval

[\sqrt{x},\sqrt{x+(k-j)\epsilon}]
(or
[\sqrt{x+(k-j)\epsilon},\sqrt{x}]
).
For the part with common lower limit
\sqrt{x}

E

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2k}(-1)^{j}\binom{2k}{j}\bigl[E^{2}-(x+(k-j)\epsilon)\bigr]^{4k}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{2k}\frac{(4k)!}{(2k)!}\,\bigl(E^{2}-x\bigr)^{2k}+O(\epsilon^{2k+2}),

(34)

This happens because the left
-
hand side is the
2k
-
th Laplacian operator of the degree
-
4k

P_{4k}(u)=(E^{2}-u)^{4k}
, and

\Delta_{\epsilon}^{2k}P_{4k}(x)=\epsilon^{2k}P_{4k}^{(2k)}(x)+O(\epsilon^{2k+2})
.
Since the boundary contributions are integrals over intervals with width
O(\epsilon)
,
the integrand vanishes as
(E^{2}-x)^{4k}\sim O(\epsilon^{4k})

so the total boundary contribution is

\frac{\textbf{Boundary}}{\textbf{Bulk}}\rightarrow\epsilon^{2k+1}

\displaystyle+O(\epsilon^{2k+2}).

(36)

But the right

\epsilon^{2k}\frac{C_{d^{\prime}}}{C_{d}}\,I_{\text{therm}}^{(d)}(\sqrt{x})

Thus, up to higher orders in
\epsilon

\displaystyle\boxed{\;\Delta_{\epsilon}^{2k}\!\left[\frac{C_{d}}{C_{d^{\prime}}\epsilon^{2k}}\frac{(2k)!}{(4k)!}I_{\text{therm}}^{(d^{\prime})}(\sqrt{x})\right]=I_{\text{therm}}^{(d)}(\sqrt{x})\;}.

5.1
The fermionic model in
d=3

For dimension
d=3

[
3
]
(corresponding to
k=0
or
d=4k+3
),
the general formula for the thermal part simplifies to

I_{\text{therm}}^{(3)}(\sigma)=-C_{3}\int_{\sigma}^{\infty}f(E)\,dE,

(66)

The constant
C_{3}=1/(2\pi)
.
The integral can be evaluated by setting
x=\beta E
,
dx=\beta dE
:

\displaystyle I_{\text{therm}}^{(3)}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi\beta}\int_{\beta\sigma}^{\infty}\Bigl(\frac{1}{e^{x+i\beta\alpha}+1}+\frac{1}{e^{x-i\beta\alpha}+1}\Bigr)\,dx.

(67)

Using the identity

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{e^{x+c}+1}=\ln\!\bigl(1+e^{-a-c}\bigr)
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\text{therm}}^{(3)}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi\beta}\Bigl[\ln\!\bigl(1+e^{-\beta\sigma-i\beta\alpha}\bigr)+\ln\!\bigl(1+e^{-\beta\sigma+i\beta\alpha}\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi\beta}\,\ln\!\Bigl[1+2e^{-\beta\sigma}\cos(\beta\alpha)+e^{-2\beta\sigma}\Bigr].

(68)

Introducing the complex variable
z=e^{-\beta\sigma-i\beta\alpha}
(so that

\bar{z}=e^{-\beta\sigma+i\beta\alpha}
) the result takes the form

\displaystyle\boxed{\;I_{\text{therm}}^{(3)}(\sigma)=-\frac{1}{2\pi\beta}\bigl[\ln(1+z)+\ln(1+\bar{z})\bigr]\;}.

(69)

5.2
Free energy for 1D twisted harmonic oscillators with fermionic statistics

We consider a quantum system of 1D twisted harmonic oscillators with fermionic statistics. Each pair of fermions has two species
\alpha=1,2
with operators
f_{n,\alpha}
,
f_{n,\alpha}^{\dagger}
satisfying
[
17
]

\displaystyle\{f_{n,\alpha},f_{m,\beta}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{nm}\delta_{\alpha\beta},\quad\{f_{n,\alpha},f_{m,\beta}\}=0.

(70)

The
U(1)
charge operator is defined as

\displaystyle Q=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\big(N_{n,1}-N_{n,2}\big),\qquad N_{n,\alpha}=f_{n,\alpha}^{\dagger}f_{n,\alpha}.

(71)

We compute the twisted partition function

Z=\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H+i\beta\alpha Q},

(72)

where
i\alpha
is the chemical potential conjugate to
Q
.

For non-interacting fermions (
k=0
) with mass
m
, the Hamiltonian is

H_{0}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2}mf_{n,\alpha}^{\dagger}f_{n,\alpha}.

Since the system is translationally invariant, we Fourier transform:

f_{n,\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{q}e^{iqn}\tilde{f}_{q,\alpha},\quad q=\frac{2\pi\ell}{N},\ \ell=0,\dots,N-1.

Then

H_{0}=\sum_{q}m\big(\tilde{f}_{q,1}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{q,1}+\tilde{f}_{q,2}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{q,2}\big).

The charge in momentum space is

Q=\sum_{q}\big(\tilde{f}_{q,1}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{q,1}-\tilde{f}_{q,2}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{q,2}\big).

Thus the exponent becomes

-\beta H_{0}+i\beta\alpha Q=\sum_{q}\big[-\beta(m-i\alpha)\tilde{f}_{q,1}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{q,1}-\beta(m+i\alpha)\tilde{f}_{q,2}^{\dagger}\tilde{f}_{q,2}\big].

(73)

For each fermionic mode, the trace over the two-dimensional Hilbert space (
n_{q}=0,1
) gives

\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta EN_{q}}=1+e^{-\beta E}.

(74)

Therefore,

Z=\prod_{q}\big[1+e^{-\beta(m-i\alpha)}\big]\big[1+e^{-\beta(m+i\alpha)}\big].

(75)

The free energy
F=-\beta^{-1}\ln Z
is

F=-\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{q}\Big[\ln\!\big(1+e^{-\beta(m-i\alpha)}\big)+\ln\!\big(1+e^{-\beta(m+i\alpha)}\big)\Big].

(76)

In the thermodynamic limit
N\to\infty
,
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q}\to\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dq
, yielding

\displaystyle\frac{F}{N}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi\beta}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Big[\ln\!\big(1+e^{-\beta(m-i\alpha)}\big)+\ln\!\big(1+e^{-\beta(m+i\alpha)}\big)\Big]dq

\displaystyle I_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\beta}\Big[\ln\!\big(1+e^{-\beta(m-i\alpha)}\big)+\ln\!\big(1+e^{-\beta(m+i\alpha)}\big)\Big]=

\displaystyle 2\pi I_{\text{therm}}^{(3)}(\sigma)

(77)

Now, it is easy to show that the thermal part
I_{0}
of the
1d
fermionic system generates the higher dimensional theory thermal part through repeated integration as a building block for higher dimensional thermal parts. In order to do this we define an operator
\mathcal{I}
that acts on a function
\mathcal{F(\sigma)}
as

\mathcal{I}^{n}[\mathcal{F(\sigma)}]

(78)

So if one want to extract all thermal kernels he has to continue integration.
Using the integration identity

\mathcal{I}^{n}[F_{0}](\sigma)=\frac{1}{2^{n}n!}\int_{\sigma}^{\infty}(E^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{n}f(E)\,dE

(79)

with
n=2k
or since
\frac{d-3}{2}=2k
for
d=4k+3
, we obtain

\mathcal{I}^{2k}[I_{0}](\sigma)=\frac{1}{2^{2k}(2k)!}\int_{\sigma}^{\infty}(E^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2k}f_{\text{therm}}(E)\,dE.

(80)

Therefore,

\boxed{I_{\text{therm}}^{(4k+3)}(\sigma)=C_{4k+3}\,2^{2k}(2k)!\,\mathcal{I}^{2k}[I_{0}](\sigma)=\mathcal{A}\mathcal{I}^{2k}[I_{0}](\sigma)}

(81)

and in the same way for
n=4k
or since
\frac{d^{\prime}-3}{2}=4k
for
d^{\prime}=8k+3
we take

\boxed{I_{\text{therm}}^{(8k+3)}(\sigma)=C_{8k+3}\,2^{4k}(4k)!\,\mathcal{I}^{4k}[I_{0}](\sigma)=\mathcal{B}\mathcal{I}^{4k}[I_{0}](\sigma)}.

(82)

where
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are constants depending on
k
index.

6
Conclusion

We have uncovered a deep mathematical structure in the gap equations of odd-dimensional Gross–Neveu models: the finite part follows higher-order discrete Laplacian patterns, while the cutoff-dependent part forms a truncated asymptotic expansion of a hypergeometric function. This structure appears only in
d=4k+3
dimensions and suggests these theories are secretly equivalent to
1d
quantum mechanical problems with exact solvability properties. The discovery opens several directions including generalization to other models, connection to integrability, implications for the phase structure of thermal field theories in odd dimensions but also for thermal bootstrap methods and inversion formulas where transcendental mass-fixing conditions appear.
