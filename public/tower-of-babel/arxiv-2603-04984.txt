L 2 \mathrm{L}^{2} –convergence of the time-splitting scheme for nonlinear Dirac equation in 1+1 dimensions
Ningning Li, Yongqian Zhang, Qin Zhao

Abstract
. We study the time-splitting scheme for approximating solutions to the Cauchy problem of the nonlinear Dirac equation in 1+1 dimensions. Under the assumption that the initial data for the scheme are convergent in L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) \mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}) , we prove that the approximate solutions constructed by the corresponding time-splitting scheme are strongly convergent in C ⁡ ( [ 0 , ∞ ) , L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) ) \mathrm{C}([0,\infty);\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})) to the global strong solution of the nonlinear Dirac equation. To achieve this, we first establish the pointwise estimates for time-splitting solutions. Based on these estimates, a modified Glimm-type functional is carefully designed to show that it is uniformly bounded in time, which yields L 2 \mathrm{L}^{2} stability estimates for the scheme. Furthermore, we prove that the set of time-splitting solutions is precompact in C ⁡ ( [ 0 , T ] , L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) ) \mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})) for any T > 0 T>0 . Finally, we show that the limit of any subsequence of the time-splitting solutions is the unique strong solution to the Cauchy problem of the nonlinear Dirac equation.

We are concerned with the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-convergence of a time-splitting scheme for nonlinear Dirac equation in 1+1 dimensions, involving Thirring type
[
44
]
and Gross-Neveu type
[
20
]

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}+u_{x}=imv+i\alpha u|v|^{2}+i2\beta(\overline{u}v+u\overline{v})v,\\
v_{t}-v_{x}=imu+i\alpha v|u|^{2}+i2\beta(\overline{u}v+u\overline{v})u,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

with initial data

\left\{\begin{aligned} u|_{t=0}=u_{0}(x),\\
v|_{t=0}=v_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

where
(u,v)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a complex vector,
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the variables
t
and

\alpha

\alpha u|v|^{2}

The well-posedness of NLDE Cauchy problem (

1.1.
Time splitting schemes and main result

We now introduce the time-splitting scheme to construct approximate solutions to the NLDE Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
The key idea is to split the system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) into two subproblems. The first subproblem consists of linear transport equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}u=0,\\
&\partial_{t}v-\partial_{x}v=0,\\
&(u,v)|_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0}),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

while the second subproblem corresponds to solving the nonlinear differential equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}=imv+i\alpha u|v|^{2}+i2\beta(\overline{u}v+u\overline{v})v,\\
&v_{t}=imu+i\alpha v|u|^{2}+i2\beta(\overline{u}v+u\overline{v})u,\\
&(u,v)|_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0}).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

Let
S^{1}_{t}
and
S^{2}_{t}
be the semigroup in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
generated by (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), respectively, that is,
S^{1}_{t}(u_{0},v_{0})
and
S^{2}_{t}(u_{0},v_{0})
is the strong solutions to (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) with the initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, respectively.

Then the construction of approximate solutions is based on the solutions of (
1.3
)–(
1.4
) with suitable initial data, namely, the semigroups
S^{1}_{t}
and
S^{2}_{t}
.
First, given a spatial mesh size
\tau:=\Delta x>0
, which will serve as a parameter, the spatial grid points are
x_{j}=j\tau
for
j=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots
. Then we choose the time step size equal to the spatial one, i.e.
\Delta t=\Delta x:=\tau>0
, and define the time steps

t_{n}=n\tau
for
n=0,1,2,\ldots
. Thus, we build the grid of mesh-points

(x_{j},t_{n})
with
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Next, let
\tau>0
. At the initial time
t=0
, the approximate solution
\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)
is defined by

\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)(x,0)=\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)(x_{j},0),\quad x\in[j\tau,(j+1)\tau),

(1.5)

which is a piecewise constant approximation of the initial data
\big(u_{0},v_{0}\big)
in the sense that

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\big(\|u^{(\tau)}(x,0)-u_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|v^{(\tau)}(x,0)-v_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big)=0.

(1.6)

Here
\big(u_{j}^{0},v_{j}^{0}\big)=\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)(x_{j},0)
.

Then, on the domain
\{(x,t):x\in\mathbb{R},t\geq 0\}
, the approximate solution
\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)
to the NLDE Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), constructed via the time-splitting scheme, is defined by

\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)(x,t)=S^{1}_{t-n\tau}\Big(S^{2}_{\tau}S^{1}_{\tau}\Big)^{n}\left(\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)(\cdot,0)\right),\quad t\in[n\tau,(n+1)\tau),\,\,n=0,1,2,\ldots.

(1.7)

More precisely, we present a detailed description of the time-splitting scheme in an inductive manner.
Assume that the approximate solution
\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)
has been defined on
\{(x,t):-\infty<x<\infty,0\leq t\leq n\tau\}
for some
n\geq 0
, with
\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{\tau}\big)(\cdot,n\tau)\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then we determine
\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)
on the strip
\{(x,t):-\infty<x<\infty,n\tau<t\leq(n+1)\tau\}
by the following two steps,
\mathbf{Step(n,1)}
and
\mathbf{Step(n,2)}
.

\mathbf{Step(n,1)}:
for
\{(x,t):-\infty<x<\infty,n\tau<t<(n+1)\tau,\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, the approximate solution
\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)
is defined as

\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)(x,t):=\left(u^{(\tau)}_{n,1},v^{(\tau)}_{n,1}\right)(x,t)=S^{1}_{t-n\tau}\left(\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)(\cdot,n\tau)\right),

which satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{n,1}^{(\tau)}+\partial_{x}u_{n,1}^{(\tau)}=0,\\
\partial_{t}v_{n,1}^{(\tau)}-\partial_{x}v_{n,1}^{(\tau)}=0,\end{cases}

(1.8)

The initial data of the system (
1.8
) at the time step
t_{n}=n\tau
is taken as

\big(u_{n,1}^{(\tau)},v_{n,1}^{(\tau)}\big)(x,t)\Big|_{t=n\tau}=\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)(x,n\tau).

(1.9)

\mathbf{Step(n,2)}:
to define the approximate solution
\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)
at the time
t=(n+1)\tau
, we solve the following Cauchy problem for the system of nonlinear differential equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\mathrm{d}u^{(\tau)}_{n,2}}{\mathrm{d}s}=imv^{(\tau)}_{n,2}+i\alpha u^{(\tau)}_{n,2}|v^{(\tau)}_{n,2}|^{2}+i2\beta\Big(\overline{u^{(\tau)}_{n,2}}v^{(\tau)}_{n,2}+u^{(\tau)}_{n,2}\overline{v^{(\tau)}_{n,2}}\Big)v^{(\tau)}_{n,2},\\
\frac{\mathrm{d}v^{(\tau)}_{n,2}}{\mathrm{d}s}=imu^{(\tau)}_{n,2}+i\alpha v^{(\tau)}_{n,2}|u^{(\tau)}_{n,2}|^{2}+i2\beta\Big(\overline{u^{(\tau)}_{n,2}}v^{(\tau)}_{n,2}+u^{(\tau)}_{n,2}\overline{v^{(\tau)}_{n,2}}\Big)u^{(\tau)}_{n,2},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.10)

for
(x,s)\in(-\infty,\infty)\times[0,\tau]
, with the initial condition

\big(u^{(\tau)}_{n,2},v^{(\tau)}_{n,2}\big)(x,s=0)=\lim_{t\rightarrow(n+1)\tau-}\big(u^{(\tau)}(x,t),v^{(\tau)}(x,t)\big)\\
:=\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)(x,(n+1)\tau-).

(1.11)

We remark that, as shown in Section
2
,
the approximate solution
\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)

can be defined globally, and we define the approximate solution
at time
t=(n+1)\tau
by

\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)(x,t=(n+1)\tau):=\big(u^{(\tau)}_{n,2},v^{(\tau)}_{n,2}\big)(x,s=\tau)=S^{2}_{\tau}\left(\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)(x,(n+1)\tau-)\right).

In this way, we inductively complete the definition of our time-splitting scheme.

We call these solutions
\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)

time-splitting solutions
of the NLDE Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
).
Here and in the sequel, let

\displaystyle\big(u_{j}^{n},v_{j}^{n}\big)=

\displaystyle\big(u^{(\tau)}(j\tau,n\tau),v^{(\tau)}(j\tau,n\tau)\big),

\displaystyle\big(u_{j}^{n+1-},v_{j}^{n+1-}\big)=

\displaystyle\big(u^{(\tau)}(j\tau,(n+1)\tau-),v^{(\tau)}(j\tau,(n+1)\tau-)\big),

and let

\big(u_{j}^{n,2}(s),v_{j}^{n,2}(s)\big)=\big(u_{n,2}^{(\tau)}(j\tau,s),v_{n,2}^{(\tau)}(j\tau,s)\big)

for
j=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots,n=0,1,2,\ldots
, and
s\in[0,\tau]
.
Then, from the construction, we conclude that

\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)(x,n\tau)=\big(u^{(\tau)}(j\tau,n\tau),v^{(\tau)}(j\tau,n\tau)\big)=\big(u_{j}^{n},v_{j}^{n}\big)\qquad\text{ for }x\in[j\tau,(j+1)\tau).

Now, we state our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and suppose that

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0+}\big(\|u^{(\tau)}(x,0)-u_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|v^{(\tau)}(x,0)-v_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big)=0.

Let
(u,v)
be the unique strong solution to the NLDE Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), and let
\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)
be the time-splitting solution
to (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with initial data
(u^{(\tau)}(x,0),v^{(\tau)}(x,0))
, defined by the scheme (
1.7
) for
\tau>0
. Then, for any
T>0
,

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0+}\Big(\|u^{(\tau)}-u\|_{\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|v^{(\tau)}-v\|_{\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\Big)=0.

The definition of strong solutions for the NLDE Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) can be stated as follows, in the same manner as in
[
39
]
, while the corresponding definition for linear hyperbolic equations can be found in
[
31
,
39
]
.

Definition 1.1

(Strong solutions)
.

A pair of functions
(u,v)\in\mathrm{C}([0,\infty);\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
is called a strong solution of the NLDE Cauchy problem
(
1.1
)–(
1.2
) on
\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)
if there exists a sequence of smooth solutions
(u_{\mathrm{k}},v_{\mathrm{k}})
such that

\lim_{\mathrm{k}\rightarrow\infty}\big(\|u_{\mathrm{k}}(x,0)-u_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|v_{\mathrm{k}}(x,0)-v_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big)=0,

and for any
T>0
,

\lim_{\mathrm{k}\rightarrow\infty}\big(\|u_{\mathrm{k}}-u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])}+\|v_{\mathrm{k}}-v\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])}\big)=0.

In particular,
(u,v)(\cdot,0)=(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

1.2.
Main difficulties and key ideas of the proof

There are two main difficulties to study the strong convergence in
\mathrm{L}^{2}
of time-splitting solutions to the NLDE Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
).
First, unlike previous implicit quantum lattice Boltzmann schemes, the time-splitting scheme decomposes the full problem into a linear transport equation with potential and a nonlinear subproblem, each of which can be solved exactly in time.
As a result, it is necessary to exploit the structure of the linear part to identify quantities that control the growth of the nonlinear terms.
Thus, the Bony-type functional and the Glimm-type functional previously used to establish compactness of solutions in the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
norm, introduced in
[
34
]
, could not be applied directly.
The second difficulty lies in proving the uniqueness of the limit of convergent subsequence of time-splitting solutions.
Notice that for smooth solutions the nonlinear effects propagate uniformly along characteristic directions, for time-splitting solutions the nonlinear interactions are concentrated at discrete time steps. Therefore, the fundamental challenge arises from comparing classical solutions and time-splitting solutions whose nonlinear growth mechanisms are inherently different.

To overcome these difficulties, we first observe that the time-splitting solutions conserve the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
norm for each subproblem.
Using the discrete characteristic triangle
\Delta(j_{1},n_{1};n_{0})
(see Fig.
2.1
), we establish a priori bounds for the time-splitting solutions to the NLDE Cauchy problem
(
1.1
)–(
1.2
).
We then introduce a modified Glimm-type functional
\mathcal{F}
defined on discrete characteristic triangles.
By choosing a suitable weight, we show that
\mathcal{F}
uniformly bounded in time direction, thereby obtaining local
\mathrm{L}^{2}
stability bounds for the difference of two time-splitting solutions. A crucial ingredient in its proof is the introduction of a new Bony-type functional
\mathcal{Q}
into
\mathcal{F}
, which acts as a potential to control the growth induced by the nonlinear terms.
As a consequence, by decomposing the strip
\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]
into two subdomains, we obtain global
\mathrm{L}^{2}
stability estimates of time-splitting solutions for any
T>0
.
Finally, we establish uniform estimates in both space and time, ensuring that the set of time-splitting solutions forms a precompact subset of
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
.

As for the uniqueness of the limit of convergent subsequence of time-splitting solutions, we first consider the difference between a smooth solution and a time-splitting solution in a discrete characteristic triangle
\Delta(j+1,n+1;n)
. Between two consecutive time levels, we employ the method of characteristics to establish uniform estimates for the difference between the smooth solution and the solution of the subproblem (
1.3
). At the temporal grid points, we derive pointwise estimates for the difference between the smooth solution and the subproblem solution (
1.4
). Based on these estimates, we introduce a new Glimm-type functional
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
. Moreover, uniform
boundedness of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
is proved.
Consequently, the limit of any convergent subsequence converges strongly in
\mathrm{L}^{2}
to the unique strong solution of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) as the mesh size tends to zero. It is worth pointing out that our error bounds and convergence result are valid globally in time, while the rigorous error estimates established in
[
4
,
6
]
are only local in time. All these theoretical results are collected in Theorem
1.1
. Finally, we remark that the Glimm-type functional has been used in the study of hyperbolic conservation laws (see, for instance, Bressan
[
12
]
, Dafermos
[
15
]
, and Glimm
[
19
]
), while the Bony-type functional has been used in the study of discrete Boltzmann equations (see, for instance, Bony
[
10
]
and Ha–Tzavaras
[
21
]
).

1.3.
Organization of the paper

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we establish pointwise estimates for time-splitting solutions. Based on these estimates, we introduce a Glimm-type functional
\mathcal{F}
and prove that an appropriate weight can be chosen so that
\mathcal{F}
uniformly bounded, which yields the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
stability estimates for time-splitting solutions in a characteristic triangle. Then, in Section
3
, we extend these local
\mathrm{L}^{2}
stability estimates to the global strip
\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]
by decomposing it into finitely many characteristic triangular domains. As a result, we obtain global
\mathrm{L}^{2}
stability estimates and prove the compactness of the set of time-splitting solutions in
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}
.
Finally, in Section
4
, we show that the limit of any convergent subsequence of time-splitting solutions is the unique strong solution to the NLDE Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), which implies the uniqueness of the limiting solution.

2.
A priori estimates

In this section, we first establish estimates for time-splitting solutions along characteristic directions. We then introduce a discrete characteristic triangle
\Delta(j_{1},n_{1};n_{0})
(see Fig.
2.1
) to derive pointwise bounds. Moreover, by constructing a nonlinear Glimm-type functional
\mathcal{F}
in
\Delta(j_{1},n_{1};n_{0})
and
proving that the functional is uniformly bounded with respect to the time step, we obtain local
\mathrm{L}^{2}
stability estimates for time-splitting solutions constructed by (
1.7
).

Since
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, it follows from (
1.5
) and
(
1.6
) that there exist constants
\bar{\tau}\in(0,1)
and

such that, for any

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}\big(|u_{j}^{0}|^{2}+|v_{j}^{0}|^{2}\big)\cdot\tau\leq c_{0}.

This estimate provides a uniform

2.1.
Estimates on the time-splitting solutions along characteristic directions

Given the piecewise constant initial data
\big(u_{j}^{0},v_{j}^{0}\big)
, we first establish several properties of the solutions to the linear subproblem (
1.8
) and the nonlinear subproblem (
1.10
), which are collected in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
j=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots,n=0,1,2,\ldots
and
\tau>0
, the time-splitting solution
\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)
can be defined globally. Moreover, the following properties hold:

(i)

Any solution
\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)
of the linear subproblem (
1.8
)–(
1.9
) satisfies

u_{j+1}^{n+1-}=u_{j}^{n},\ \ v_{j-1}^{n+1-}=v_{j}^{n}.

(2.2)

(ii)

Any solution
\big(u^{(\tau)}_{n,2},v^{(\tau)}_{n,2}\big)
of the nonlinear subproblem (
1.10
)–(
1.11
) satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}|u_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}}{\mathrm{d}s}\leq m\big(|u_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}+|v_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}\big)+4|\beta||u_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}\cdot|v_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}|v_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}}{\mathrm{d}s}\leq m\big(|u_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}+|v_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}\big)+4|\beta||u_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}\cdot|v_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2},

(2.4)

and

\frac{\mathrm{d}|u_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}}{\mathrm{d}s}+\frac{\mathrm{d}|v_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}}{\mathrm{d}s}=0,

(2.5)

where
s\in[0,\tau]
.

Proof.

The proof is carried out by induction on
n
. Suppose that
\big(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)}\big)
has been defined for
0\leq t\leq n\tau
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Then we consider the case
t\in(n\tau,(n+1)\tau]
.

For
x\in[j\tau,(j+1)\tau)
and
n\tau\leq t\leq(n+1)\tau
, the linear transport equations (
1.8
) with the constant initial data
\big(u_{j}^{n},v_{j}^{n}\big)
can be solved by the method of characteristics, namely,

u^{(\tau)}(x,t_{n})=u^{(\tau)}(x+t-t_{n},t),\quad v^{(\tau)}(x,t_{n})=v^{(\tau)}(x-t+t_{n},t),

which also gives (
2.2
).

Then we turn to the proof of the property (ii). For any
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we denote by
\mathrm{Re}\,{z}
the real part of
z
. With this notation, multiplying the first equation in (
1.10
) by
\overline{u_{j}^{n,2}(s)}
and the second one by
\overline{v_{j}^{n,2}(s)}
, we obtain

\frac{\mathrm{d}|u_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}}{\mathrm{d}s}=2m\mathrm{Re}\left\{iv_{j}^{n,2}(s)\overline{u_{j}^{n,2}(s)}\right\}+4\beta\mathrm{Re}\left\{i\big(\overline{u_{j}^{n,2}(s)}\big)^{2}\big(v_{j}^{n,2}(s)\big)^{2}\right\}

(2.6)

and

\frac{\mathrm{d}|v_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}}{\mathrm{d}s}=2m\mathrm{Re}\left\{iu_{j}^{n,2}(s)\overline{v_{j}^{n,2}(s)}\right\}+4\beta\mathrm{Re}\left\{i\big(u_{j}^{n,2}(s)\big)^{2}\big(\overline{v_{j}^{n,2}(s)}\big)^{2}\right\}.

(2.7)

Since
m\geq 0

\displaystyle\int_{(j_{1}-n_{1}-[\frac{t}{\tau}])\tau}^{(j_{1}+n_{1}-\left[\frac{t}{\tau}\right])\tau}\left(\left|U^{(\tau)}\left(x-\left(t-\left[\frac{t}{\tau}\right]\tau\right),\left[\frac{t}{\tau}\right]\tau\right)\right|^{2}+\left|V^{(\tau)}\left(x+\left(t-\left[\frac{t}{\tau}\right]\tau\right),\left[\frac{t}{\tau}\right]\tau\right)\right|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left(\left[\frac{t}{\tau}\right]\tau;\Delta\right)\leq\mathrm{e}^{C(T)}\mathcal{F}(n_{0};\Delta),

where the last inequality is a consequence of (
3.2
). This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we establish the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
stability estimates for time-splitting solutions in the following two cases, depending on whether
(x,t)\in\mathscr{E}(\Theta,T)
or

(x,t)\in(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])\cap\Lambda(0,\Theta+4T;0)
.

Case 1. In the subdomain
\mathscr{E}(\Theta,T)
.

For
(x,t)\in\mathscr{E}(\Theta,T)
, the following lemma provides the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
stability estimate for time-splitting solutions to (
1.1
)–(
1.2
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
T>0
, there exists a constant
\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{E}}>0
, such that, for all
|\mu|\leq\frac{\Theta}{4}
and
\tau\in(0,\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{E}})
,

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq\Theta+t}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-u^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-v^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{C(T)}\int_{|x|\geq\Theta}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-u^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-v^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x,

where the positive constant
C(T)
is as in Proposition
2.1
, and
\Theta>0
is given by (
3.1
).

Proof.

Recall that
\bar{\tau}\in(0,1)
is given by (
2.1
) and that
\Theta>0
is given by (
3.1
). By (
1.6
), we can choose a
\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{E}}\in(0,\bar{\tau}]
small enough such that
\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{E}}<\frac{\Theta}{36}
and for any
\tau\in(0,\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{E}})
,

\|u^{(\tau)}(x,0)-u_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(|x|\geq\Theta/2)}^{2}+\|v^{(\tau)}(x,0)-v_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(|x|\geq\Theta/2)}^{2}\leq\frac{\delta}{4}.

(3.3)

Then, using (
3.1
) and (
3.3
), we have

\|u^{(\tau)}(x,0)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(|x|\geq\Theta/2)}^{2}+\|v^{(\tau)}(x,0)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(|x|\geq\Theta/2)}^{2}\leq\delta,

(3.4)

which together with
|\mu|\leq\frac{\Theta}{4}
yields

\|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(|x|\geq[\frac{\Theta}{\tau}]\tau)}^{2}+\|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(|x|\geq[\frac{\Theta}{\tau}]\tau)}^{2}\leq\delta,

(3.5)

where
\delta>0
is the constant of Proposition
2.1
, and
[\frac{\Theta}{\tau}]
denotes the greatest integer in
\frac{\Theta}{\tau}
.

Therefore, combining (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and Proposition
3.1
, we find that for
|\mu|\leq\frac{\Theta}{4}
and
\tau\in(0,\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{E}})
,

\displaystyle\int_{[\frac{\Theta}{\tau}]\tau+t}^{[\frac{\Theta}{\tau}]\tau+2n_{1}\tau-t}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-u^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-v^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{C(T)}\int_{[\frac{\Theta}{\tau}]\tau}^{[\frac{\Theta}{\tau}]\tau+2n_{1}\tau}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-u^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-v^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x

and

\displaystyle\int^{-[\frac{\Theta}{\tau}]\tau-t}_{-[\frac{\Theta}{\tau}]\tau-2n_{1}\tau+t}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-u^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-v^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{C(T)}\int^{-[\frac{\Theta}{\tau}]\tau}_{-[\frac{\Theta}{\tau}]\tau-2n_{1}\tau}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-u^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-v^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x.

Consequently, by letting
n_{1}\to\infty
, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Case 2. In the subdomain
(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])\cap\Lambda(0,\Theta+4T;0)
.

In the following lemma, we establish the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
stability estimates for time-splitting solutions on the domain
(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])\cap\Lambda(0,\Theta+4T;0)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
T>0
, there exists a constant
\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\Lambda}>0
, such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,
|\mu|<\frac{\Theta}{4}
, and
\tau\in(0,\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\Lambda})
,

\displaystyle\int_{-\Theta-4T+t}^{\Theta+4T-t}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-u^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-v^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{C(T)}\int_{-\Theta-4T}^{\Theta+4T}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-u^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-v^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x,

where the positive constant
C(T)
is as in Proposition
2.1
, and
\Theta>0
is given by (
3.1
).

Proof.

We will use Proposition
3.1
to get the desired result. First, for any interval
(x_{1},x_{2})\subset(-\infty,\infty)
at time
t=n\tau\in[0,T]
with
n=0,1,2,\ldots
, we consider two cases:

(x_{1},x_{2})\subset(-\infty,-\Theta-n\tau)\cup(\Theta+n\tau,+\infty)\quad\text{and}\quad(x_{1},x_{2})\subset(-\Theta-4T+n\tau,\Theta+4T-n\tau).

For the former case, it follows from (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and Lemma
2.2
that

\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\big(|u^{(\tau)}|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}|^{2}\big)(x,n\tau)\mathrm{d}x\leq\delta\>\>\text{ and }\>\>\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\big(|u^{(\tau)}|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}|^{2}\big)(x+\mu,n\tau)\mathrm{d}x\leq\delta

(3.7)

for
(x_{1},x_{2})\subset(-\infty,-\Theta-n\tau)\cup(\Theta+n\tau,+\infty)
and for
|\mu|<\frac{\Theta}{4}
.

For the latter case, from (
2.14
) and (
2.15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x,n\tau)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x,n\tau)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\Big(|u^{(\tau)}(x-n\tau,0)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+n\tau,0)|^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}x+2m_{1}c_{0}c_{2}(x_{2}-x_{1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\Big(\int_{x_{1}-n\tau}^{x_{2}-n\tau}|u^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{x_{1}+n\tau}^{x_{2}+n\tau}|v^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)+2m_{1}c_{0}c_{2}(x_{2}-x_{1}).

We now estimate the right-hand side of (
3.8
). Since
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists a constant
\sigma\in(0,\Theta/8)
such that for any
x_{2}-x_{1}\in(0,4\sigma]
,

c_{2}\Big(\|u_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}((x_{1},x_{2}))}^{2}+\|v_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}((x_{1},x_{2}))}^{2}\Big)\leq\frac{\delta}{8},\qquad 2m_{1}c_{0}c_{2}(x_{2}-x_{1})\leq\frac{\delta}{2}.

(3.9)

Then, by (
1.6
), we choose a
\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\Lambda}\in(0,\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{E}})
small enough such that for any
x_{2}-x_{1}\in(0,4\sigma]
,

c_{2}\Big(\|u^{(\tau)}(x,0)-u_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}((x_{1},x_{2}))}^{2}+\|v^{(\tau)}(x,0)-v_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}((x_{1},x_{2}))}^{2}\Big)\leq\frac{\delta}{8}.

(3.10)

Combining (
3.10
) and (
3.9
), we deduce that if
\tau\in(0,\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\Lambda})
, then for any interval
[x_{1},x_{2}]\subset[-2\Theta-4T,2\Theta+4T]
with
x_{2}-x_{1}\in(0,4\sigma]
,

c_{2}\Big(\|u^{(\tau)}(x,0)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}((x_{1},x_{2}))}^{2}+\|v^{(\tau)}(x,0)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}((x_{1},x_{2}))}^{2}\Big)\leq\frac{\delta}{2}.

(3.11)

Consequently, by substituting (
3.9
) and (
3.11
) into
(
3.8
) and using (
3.7
), we conclude that there exists a constant
\sigma>0
such that, if
\tau\in(0,\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\Lambda})
and
n=0,1,\ldots,[\frac{T}{\tau}]+1
, then for any interval
[x_{1},x_{2}]\subset[-\Theta-4T,\Theta+4T]
with
x_{2}-x_{1}\in(0,4\sigma]
,

\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\big(|u^{(\tau)}|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}|^{2}\big)(x,n\tau)\mathrm{d}x\leq\delta\>\>\text{ and }\>\>\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\big(|u^{(\tau)}|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}|^{2}\big)(x+\mu,n\tau)\mathrm{d}x\leq\delta.

(3.12)

Next, without loss of generality, we assume that

\Theta+4T=N\cdot(4\sigma)
with
N
being a positive integer. Choose the grid points
P_{j,n}=(j\sigma,n\sigma)
with
j=0,\pm 1,\ldots,\pm 4N
and
n=0,1,\ldots,[\frac{T}{\sigma}]+1
,
in the domain
\Delta(0,\Theta+4T;0)
, and we then consider the
L^{2}
stability of time-splitting solutions on a characteristic triangle
\Delta((j+2)\sigma,(n+2)\sigma;n\sigma)
.

For any
t>0
, we let

\tilde{C}_{2}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{(C_{3}+\kappa C_{4})\left[\frac{t}{\sigma}\right]\sigma+\kappa C_{4}\sum_{p=0}^{\left[\frac{t}{\sigma}\right]-1}(\mathfrak{q}(p;\Delta)\sigma^{2}+\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}(p;\Delta)\sigma^{2})}.

Then using (
3.7
), (
3.12
) and Proposition
3.1
, we find that for any
-4N\leq j\leq 4N-4
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,2\sigma]}\int_{j\sigma+t}^{(j+4)\sigma-t}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-u^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-v^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{2}(2\sigma)\int_{j\sigma}^{(j+4)\sigma}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-u^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-v^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x.

Hence, for
t\in[0,\sigma]
, (
3.6
) follows by summing the above inequalities over all
j\in[-4N,4N-4]
.

Arguing by induction, we assume that for
t\in[0,n\sigma]
,

\displaystyle\int_{j\sigma}^{(j+4)\sigma}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,n\sigma)-u^{(\tau)}(x,n\sigma)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,n\sigma)-v^{(\tau)}(x,n\sigma)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{2}(n\sigma)\int_{j\sigma-n\sigma}^{(j+4)\sigma+n\sigma}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-u^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-v^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x.

Then, we proceed to prove (
3.6
) for
t\in[0,(n+1)\sigma]
. Note that
\Lambda(0,\Theta+4T;0)\cap(\mathbb{R}\times[0,(n+1)\sigma])
is a subset of

\left(\Lambda(0,\Theta+4T;0)\cap(\mathbb{R}\times[0,n\sigma])\right)\cup\left(\cup_{-4N+n\leq j\leq 4N-n-4}\Lambda((j+2)\sigma,(n+2)\sigma;n\sigma)\right).

For any characteristic triangle

\Delta((j+2)\sigma,(n+2)\sigma;n\sigma)

with
-4N+n\leq j\leq 4N-n-4
, it follows from
(
3.7
), (
3.12
),
(
3.13
), and Proposition
3.1

that

\displaystyle\int_{j\sigma+t}^{(j+4)\sigma-t}\Big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-u^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-v^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{(C_{3}+\kappa C_{4})\left(\left[\frac{t}{\sigma}\right]-n\right)\sigma+\kappa C_{4}\sum_{p=n}^{\left[\frac{t}{\sigma}\right]-1}\big(\mathfrak{q}(p;\Delta)\sigma^{2}+\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}(p;\Delta)\sigma^{2}\big)}

\displaystyle\cdot\int_{j\sigma}^{(j+4)\sigma}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,n\sigma)-u^{(\tau)}(x,n\sigma)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,n\sigma)-v^{(\tau)}(x,n\sigma)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{2}((n+1)\sigma)\int_{j\sigma-n\sigma}^{(j+4)\sigma+n\sigma}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-u^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,0)-v^{(\tau)}(x,0)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x.

Finally, summing the above inequalities over
j=-4N+n,\ldots,4N-n-4
and recalling the definition
\displaystyle C(T)=(C_{3}+\kappa C_{4})(T+1)+2\kappa C_{4}c(T)
given in the proof of Proposition
2.1
, we obtain (
3.6
). This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Compactness of the family of time-splitting solutions

In this subsection, we prove the compactness of the family of time-splitting solutions. For any
T>0
, we define

S_{T}:=\Big\{(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)})\big|_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]}\;:\;\tau\in(0,\tau_{*})\Big\}

(3.14)

where
\tau_{*}=\min\{\bar{\tau},\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{E}},\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\Lambda}\}
, with
\bar{\tau}
,
\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{E}}
, and

\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle\Lambda}
defined in (
2.1
),
Lemma
3.1
, and Lemma
3.2
.

It follows from Lemmas
3.1
–
3.2
that, to prove the compactness of
S_{T}
, it suffices to establish a uniform estimate on
(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)})
along the characteristics, uniformly in both
x
and
t
. This is provided by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
T>0
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist constants
\delta_{1}>0
and
C_{1}(T)>0
, such that for any
(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)})\in S_{T}
, if
\tau\in(0,\delta_{1})
and
h\in(0,\delta_{1})
, then

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|u^{(\tau)}(x+h,t_{0}+h)-u^{(\tau)}(x,t_{0})\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x<C_{1}(T)(\varepsilon+\tau)

and

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|v^{(\tau)}(x-h,t_{0}+h)-v^{(\tau)}(x,t_{0})\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x<C_{1}(T)(\varepsilon+\tau).

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1. Estimates on
(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)})
at the grid points
(x,t_{0})=(j\tau,n_{0}\tau)
with
h=(n_{1}-n_{0})\tau
.

The first equation in (
1.10
) yields

\Big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{j}^{n,2}(s)}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathrm{d}s\Big|\leq m\int_{0}^{\tau}|v_{j}^{n,2}(s)|\mathrm{d}s+(|\alpha|+4|\beta|)\int_{0}^{\tau}|u_{j}^{n,2}(s)||v_{j}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s,

which, together with (
2.2
), (
2.13
), and (
2.16
), implies

\displaystyle|u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j-1}^{n}|\leq

\displaystyle\left[m+(|\alpha|+4|\beta|)\mathrm{e}^{2|\beta|c_{0}}\Big(|u_{j-1}^{n}|^{2}|v_{j+1}^{n}|^{2}+m_{1}(|u_{j-1}^{n}|^{2}+|v_{j+1}^{n}|^{2})^{2}\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\cdot\Big(|v_{j+1}^{n}|^{2}+m_{1}(|u_{j-1}^{n}|^{2}+|v_{j+1}^{n}|^{2})\tau+c_{1}|u_{j-1}^{n}|^{2}|v_{j+1}^{n}|^{2}\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau.

Summing the above inequalities over all
n=n_{0},\ldots,n_{1}
yields

\displaystyle|u_{j}^{n_{1}+1}-u_{j-1}^{n_{1}}|+|u_{j-1}^{n_{1}}-u_{j-2}^{n_{1}-1}|+\cdots+|u_{j-p}^{n_{1}-p+1}-u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|+\cdots|u_{j-n_{1}+n_{0}}^{n_{0}+1}-u_{j-n_{1}+n_{0}-1}^{n_{0}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[m+(|\alpha|+4|\beta|)\mathrm{e}^{2|\beta|c_{0}}\Big(\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}+m_{1}\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}(|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}+|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2})^{2}\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\tau

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}+m_{1}\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}(|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}+|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2})\tau+c_{1}\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then, setting
C_{0}=(|\alpha|+4|\beta|)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{4|\beta|c_{0}}
and using Young’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}|u_{j}^{n_{1}+1}-u_{j-n_{1}+n_{0}-1}^{n_{0}}|^{2}\tau

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}\left[m^{2}\tau^{2}+C_{0}\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\tau^{2}+2m_{1}C_{0}\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}\big(|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}+|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\big)^{2}\tau^{3}\right]

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\tau+m_{1}\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}(|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}+|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2})\tau^{2}+c_{1}\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\tau\cdot|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\tau\right).

We next estimate the right-hand side of (
3.15
) one by one.
By Lemma
2.4
, we have

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\tau^{2}=\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}|u_{j}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}|v_{j}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\tau^{2}\leq c(T).

Combining (
2.9
) and (
2.14
)–(
2.15
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}

\displaystyle|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\tau\leq\sum_{j=j-1-n_{1}+n_{0}}^{j-1+n_{1}-n_{0}}\big(|u_{j}^{n_{0}}|^{2}+|v_{j}^{n_{0}}|^{2}\big)\tau

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=j-1-n_{1}+n_{0}}^{j-1+n_{1}-n_{0}}c_{2}\Big(\big(|u_{j-n_{0}}^{0}|^{2}+|v_{j+n_{0}}^{0}|^{2}\big)+2m_{1}c_{0}\Big)\tau

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\int_{(j-1-n_{1}+n_{0})\tau}^{(j-1+n_{1}-n_{0})\tau}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x-n_{0}\tau,0)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+n_{0}\tau,0)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x+2m_{1}c_{0}c_{2}(n_{1}-n_{0})\tau.

Moreover, in view of (
2.19
), it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}\left(|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}+|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\right)\tau^{2}\;

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}\left(|u_{j}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}+|v_{j}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\right)\tau^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}\left(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}(|u_{j}^{0}|^{2}+|v_{j}^{0}|^{2})\tau\right)\tau\leq 2c_{0}(n_{1}-n_{0})\tau.

For the remaining terms in (
3.15
), we observe that

\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}\big(|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}+|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\big)^{2}\tau^{3}=\sum_{p=0}^{n_{1}-n_{0}}\big(|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}+|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\big)\tau^{2}\cdot\big(|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}+|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\big)\tau.

Again by (
2.19
) and (
2.1
), we deduce that for all
p=0,1,2,\ldots,n_{1}-n_{0}
,

\big(|u_{j-p-1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}+|v_{j-p+1}^{n_{1}-p}|^{2}\big)\tau\leq c_{0}.

Since
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, it follows from (
1.6
) that there is
\delta_{1}=\delta_{1}(\varepsilon)>0
such that, for all
h=(n_{1}-n_{0})\tau<\delta_{1}
,

\int_{(j-n_{1}+n_{0}-1)\tau}^{(j+n_{1}-n_{0}+1)\tau}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x-n_{0}\tau,0)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+n_{0}\tau,0)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x\leq\varepsilon

and

(n_{1}-n_{0})\tau<\varepsilon.

Finally, by combining the above estimates together with (
3.15
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}|u_{j}^{n_{1}+1}-u_{j-n_{1}+n_{0}-1}^{n_{0}}|^{2}\tau\leq C_{1}(T)(\varepsilon+\tau),

(3.16)

where
C_{1}(T)=2(m^{2}+C_{0}c(T)+4m_{1}C_{0}c_{0}^{2})(c_{2}(1+2m_{1}c_{0})+2m_{1}c_{0}+c_{1}c_{2}(1+2m_{1}c_{0})c_{0})
.

Similarly, using the second equation in (
1.10
), we derive that

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}|v_{j}^{n_{1}+1}-v_{j+n_{1}-n_{0}+1}^{n_{0}}|^{2}\tau\leq C_{1}(T)(\varepsilon+\tau).

(3.17)

Step 2. Estimates on
(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)})
for
(x,t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]
and sufficiently small
h\in\mathbb{R}
.

By the construction of the time-splitting scheme, it follows from (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|u^{(\tau)}(x+h,t_{0}+h)-u^{(\tau)}(x,t_{0})\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|u^{(\tau)}\left(x+h-\big(t_{0}+h-\left[\frac{t_{0}+h}{\tau}\right]\tau\big),\left[\frac{t_{0}+h}{\tau}\right]\tau\right)-u^{(\tau)}\left(x-\big(t_{0}-\left[\frac{t_{0}}{\tau}\right]\tau\big),\left[\frac{t_{0}}{\tau}\right]\tau\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{j\tau}^{(j+1)\tau}\left|u^{(\tau)}\left(x,\left[\frac{t_{0}+h}{\tau}\right]\tau\right)-u^{(\tau)}\left(x-\left[\frac{t_{0}+h}{\tau}\right]\tau+\left[\frac{t_{0}}{\tau}\right]\tau,\left[\frac{t_{0}}{\tau}\right]\tau\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left|u_{j}^{\left[\frac{t_{0}+h}{\tau}\right]}-u_{j-\left[\frac{t_{0}+h}{\tau}\right]+\left[\frac{t_{0}}{\tau}\right]}^{\left[\frac{t_{0}}{\tau}\right]}\right|^{2}\tau\leq C_{1}(T)(\varepsilon+\tau),

where the last inequality is a consequence
of (
3.16
).

The remaining estimate in Lemma
3.3
for
v^{(\tau)}
is obtained in the same way as that for
u^{(\tau)}
, by using (
3.17
) in place of (
3.16
). This completes the proof.
∎

In view of Lemmas
3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
, the compactness of the set
S_{T}
of time-splitting solutions follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then for any
T>0
, the set of time-splitting solutions

S_{T}=\{(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)})|_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]}:\tau\in(0,\tau_{*})\}

defined by (
3.14
) is a precompact subset of
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
\delta_{*}>0
such that, for any
\mu\in(0,\delta_{*})
, we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(|u^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-u^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}+|v^{(\tau)}(x+\mu,t)-v^{(\tau)}(x,t)|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x\leq\varepsilon.

From this estimate, together with (

), (

), Lemma

, and
[

, we deduce that the set

is precompact in

. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Uniqueness of the limit of time-splitting solutions for the NLDE

In this section, we prove the main theorem of the paper. By Lemma
3.4
, we extract a convergent subsequence from the family of time-splitting solutions. For any initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the NLDE Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) admits a unique global strong solution
(\hat{u},\hat{v})\in\mathrm{C}([0,\infty);\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
(see
[
46
, Theorem 1.1]
). We then show that, as the mesh size tends to zero, this subsequence converges strongly in

\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to the unique strong solution of
(
1.1
)–(
1.2
), and that the limit is independent of the choice of the mesh size.

To begin with, let
(\hat{u},\hat{v})\in\mathrm{C}([0,\infty);\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
be the strong solution of the NLDE Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) given by Definition
1.1
. We choose a sequence of smooth functions

(\hat{u}_{\mathrm{k}},\hat{v}_{\mathrm{k}})(x,0)\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\mathrm{k}=1,2,\ldots

such that

\lim_{\mathrm{k}\to\infty}\Big(\|\hat{u}_{\mathrm{k}}(x,0)-u_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\hat{v}_{\mathrm{k}}(x,0)-v_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\Big)=0.

(4.1)

For any
T>0
, the theorem in
[
46
, Theorem 1.1]
ensures the existence of a sequence of smooth solutions

(\hat{u}_{\mathrm{k}},\hat{v}_{\mathrm{k}})\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])

to (
1.1
) with initial data
(\hat{u}_{\mathrm{k}}(x,0),\hat{v}_{\mathrm{k}}(x,0))
, such that

\lim_{\mathrm{k}\to\infty}\Big(\|\hat{u}_{\mathrm{k}}-\hat{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])}+\|\hat{v}_{\mathrm{k}}-\hat{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])}\Big)=0.

(4.2)

Next, let
\{\tau_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}\subset(0,\tau_{*})\subset(0,1)
be a sequence such that
\tau_{l}\to 0
as
l\to\infty
.
For each
\mathrm{k}
, we define a sequence of piecewise constant functions

(\hat{u}^{(\tau_{l})}_{\mathrm{k}},\hat{v}^{(\tau_{l})}_{\mathrm{k}})(x,n\tau_{l})=(\hat{u}_{\mathrm{k}},\hat{v}_{\mathrm{k}})(j\tau_{l},n\tau_{l}),\quad x\in[j\tau_{l},(j+1)\tau_{l}),

which approximate
(\hat{u}_{\mathrm{k}},\hat{v}_{\mathrm{k}})
as
l\to\infty
.
Then, as
l\to\infty
, it holds that

\|\hat{u}^{(\tau_{l})}_{\mathrm{k}}-\hat{u}_{\mathrm{k}}\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{R})}+\|\hat{v}^{(\tau_{l})}_{\mathrm{k}}-\hat{v}_{\mathrm{k}}\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{R})}\to 0.

(4.3)

On the other hand, for each mesh size
\tau_{l}
,
l=1,2,3,\ldots
, we obtain a set of time-splitting solutions, denoted by
\{(u^{(\tau_{l})},v^{(\tau_{l})})\}
.

Thus, it suffices to show that, for any convergent subsequence of the time-splitting solutions (still denoted by
\{(u^{(\tau_{l})},v^{(\tau_{l})})\}
), we have, as
l\to\infty
and
\mathrm{k}\to 0
,

\|\hat{u}^{(\tau_{l})}_{\mathrm{k}}-u^{(\tau_{l})}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])}+\|\hat{v}^{(\tau_{l})}_{\mathrm{k}}-v^{(\tau_{l})}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])}\to 0,

(4.4)

where
(u,v)
denotes the unique strong solution of the NLDE Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

For simplicity, we drop the subscript
l
and
\mathrm{k}
whenever no confusion arises from the context, namely,
(\hat{u}^{(\tau)},\hat{u}^{(\tau)})
denote the piecewise approximations of the smooth solutions

\{(\hat{u}_{\mathrm{k}},\hat{v}_{\mathrm{k}})\}_{\mathrm{k}=1}^{\infty}
, while
(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)})
are the time-splitting solutions. Moreover, we set

\mathcal{N}_{1}(u,v)=\alpha u|v|^{2}+2\beta(\overline{u}v+u\overline{v})v,\quad\mathcal{N}_{2}(u,v)=\alpha v|u|^{2}+2\beta(\overline{u}v+u\overline{v})u.

Now, we consider the difference between the piecewise approximation
(\hat{u}^{(\tau)},\hat{v}^{(\tau)})
and the time-splitting solution
(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)})
on the discrete characteristic triangle
\Delta(j+1,n+1;n)
for
j=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots,n=0,1,2,\ldots
with
0\leq n\leq[T/\tau]+1
.

By the method of characteristics, we deduce from (
1.1
) that, for any
s\in[0,\tau]
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\hat{u}^{(\tau)}(j\tau+s,n\tau+s)=[im\hat{v}^{(\tau)}+i\mathcal{N}_{1}(\hat{u}^{(\tau)},\hat{v}^{(\tau)})](j\tau+s,n\tau+s),\\
&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\hat{v}^{(\tau)}((j+2)\tau-s,n\tau+s)=[im\hat{u}^{(\tau)}+i\mathcal{N}_{2}(\hat{u}^{(\tau)},\hat{v}^{(\tau)})]((j+2)\tau-s,n\tau+s).\end{aligned}\right.

(4.5)

Meanwhile, the time-splitting solution
(u^{(\tau)},v^{(\tau)})
satisfies, for all
s\in[0,\tau)
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}{u}^{(\tau)}(j\tau+s,n\tau+s)=0,\\
&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}{v}^{(\tau)}((j+2)\tau-s,n\tau+s)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.6)

Moreover, it satisfies for all
s\in[0,\tau)
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)=imv_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)+i\mathcal{N}_{1}(u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s),v_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)),\\
&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}v_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)=imu_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)+i\mathcal{N}_{2}(u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s),v_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)),\\
&(u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s),v_{j+1}^{n,2}(s))\big|_{s=0}=(u_{j+1}^{n+1-},v_{j+1}^{n+1-}).\end{aligned}\right.

(4.7)

Recall that

(u_{j+1}^{n+1},v_{j+1}^{n+1})=(u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s),v_{j+1}^{n,2}(s))\big|_{s=\tau},\qquad(u_{j+1}^{n+1-},v_{j+1}^{n+1-})=(u_{j}^{n},v_{j+2}^{n}).

We denote the difference between the two solutions by

(\tilde{U}^{(\tau)},\tilde{V}^{(\tau)}):=(\hat{u}^{(\tau)}-u^{(\tau)},\hat{v}^{(\tau)}-u^{(\tau)}).

For simplicity of notation, let

\hat{u}_{j}^{n}(s):=\hat{u}^{(\tau)}(j\tau+s,n\tau+s),\qquad\hat{v}_{j+2}^{n}(s):=\hat{v}^{(\tau)}((j+2)\tau-s,n\tau+s),

and let

\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}(s):=\hat{u}_{j}^{n}(s)-u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s),\qquad\mathcal{V}_{j+2}^{n}(s):=\hat{v}_{j+2}^{n}(s)-v_{j+1}^{n,2}(s).

With these notations, we find that for
s\in[0,\tau)

\tilde{U}^{(\tau)}(j\tau+s,n\tau+s)=\hat{u}_{j}^{n}(s)-u_{j}^{n},\quad\tilde{V}^{(\tau)}((j+2)\tau-s,n\tau+s)=\hat{v}_{j+2}^{n}(s)-v_{j+2}^{n}.

In particular, for
s=0

\tilde{U}^{(\tau)}(j\tau,n\tau)=\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}(0),\quad\tilde{V}^{(\tau)}(j\tau,n\tau)=\mathcal{V}_{j}^{n}(0),

while for
s=\tau
,

\tilde{U}^{(\tau)}((j+1)\tau,(n+1)\tau)=\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}(\tau)=\mathcal{U}_{j+1}^{n+1}(0),\quad\tilde{V}^{(\tau)}((j+1)\tau,(n+1)\tau)=\mathcal{V}_{j+2}^{n}(\tau)=\mathcal{V}_{j+1}^{n+1}(0).

Since
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
(\hat{u}^{(\tau)},\hat{v}^{(\tau)})\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])
, it follows from (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) that there exist constants
\mathcal{M}_{0}
and
\mathcal{M}
, depending only on
T
,
c_{0}
, and the system (
1.1
), such that

\mathcal{M}_{0}=\mathcal{M}_{0}(T)=\max_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]}(|\hat{u}|+|\hat{v}|+1)

and

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(T)=\max_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]}(|\hat{u}_{t}|+|\hat{u}_{x}|+|\hat{v}_{t}|+|\hat{v}_{x}|+1),

see Lemma 2.2 in
[
46
]
for the proof.

Then between two consecutive time steps, we obtain the following estimates on the difference
(\tilde{U}^{(\tau)}(j\tau+s,n\tau+s),\tilde{V}^{(\tau)}(j\tau+s,n\tau+s))
for
s\in[0,\tau)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a constant
\hat{C}_{0}>0
, depending only on the system (
1.1
), such that for all

\displaystyle|\tilde{U}^{(\tau)}(j\tau+s,n\tau+s)|^{2}+|\tilde{V}^{(\tau)}((j+2)\tau-s,n\tau+s)|^{2}\leq\mathrm{e}^{2\tau}\left(|\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}(0)|^{2}+|\mathcal{V}_{j+2}^{n}(0)|^{2}+\hat{C}_{0}\mathcal{M}_{0}^{6}\cdot\tau\right).

Proof.

By (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we deduce

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}|\tilde{U}^{(\tau)}(j\tau+s,n\tau+s)|^{2}=2\mathrm{Re}\left\{[im\hat{v}^{(\tau)}\overline{\tilde{U}^{(\tau)}}+i\mathcal{N}_{1}(\hat{u}^{(\tau)},\hat{v}^{(\tau)})\overline{\tilde{U}^{(\tau)}}](j\tau+s,n\tau+s)\right\},\\
&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}|\tilde{V}^{(\tau)}((j+2)\tau-s,n\tau+s)|^{2}=2\mathrm{Re}\left\{[im\hat{u}^{(\tau)}\overline{\tilde{V}^{(\tau)}}+i\mathcal{N}_{2}(\hat{u}^{(\tau)},\hat{v}^{(\tau)})\overline{\tilde{V}^{(\tau)}}]((j+2)\tau-s,n\tau+s)\right\}.\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle|\tilde{U}^{(\tau)}(j\tau+s,n\tau+s)|^{2}+|\tilde{V}^{(\tau)}((j+2)\tau-s,n\tau+s)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{2s}\left(|\tilde{U}^{(\tau)}(j\tau,n\tau)|^{2}+|\tilde{V}^{(\tau)}((j+2)\tau,n\tau)|^{2}+2(m+|\alpha|+4|\beta|)^{2}\mathcal{M}_{0}^{6}\cdot s\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{2\tau}\left(|\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}(0)|^{2}+|\mathcal{V}_{j+2}^{n}(0)|^{2}+2(m+|\alpha|+4|\beta|)^{2}\mathcal{M}_{0}^{6}\cdot\tau\right),

∎

Next, to estimate the difference
(\tilde{U}^{(\tau)},\tilde{V}^{(\tau)})((j+1)\tau,(n+1)\tau)=(\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}(\tau),\mathcal{V}_{j+2}^{n}(\tau))
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}^{n}(s):=|\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}(s)|^{2}+|\mathcal{V}_{j+2}^{n}(s)|^{2}

(4.8)

and

\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{j}^{n}(s):=|\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}(s)|^{2}|v_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}+|\mathcal{V}_{j+2}^{n}(s)|^{2}|u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}.

(4.9)

Then for a pair of integers
(j,n)

, there exists a constant
\hat{C}_{1}>0
, depending only on
\mathcal{M}_{0}
and the system (

), such that for all

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}|\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}(s)|^{2}}{\mathrm{d}s}\leq\hat{C}_{1}\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}^{n}(s)+\hat{C}_{1}\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{j}^{n}(s)+\hat{C}_{1}(|\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}(s)|^{2}+|u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}+1)\mathcal{M}^{2}\tau^{2},

(4.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}|\mathcal{V}_{j+2}^{n}(s)|^{2}}{\mathrm{d}s}\leq\hat{C}_{1}\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}^{n}(s)+\hat{C}_{1}\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{j}^{n}(s)+\hat{C}_{1}(|\mathcal{V}_{j+2}^{n}(s)|^{2}+|v_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}+1)\mathcal{M}^{2}\tau^{2},

(4.11)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}^{n}(s)}{\mathrm{d}s}\leq 2\hat{C}_{1}\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{j}^{n}(s)+\hat{C}_{1}(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j}^{n}(s)+|u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)|+|v_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)|^{2}+2)\mathcal{M}^{2}\tau^{2}.

(4.12)

Proof.

By combining the equations in (
4.5
) and (
4.7
), we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}(s)=im\left(\hat{v}_{j,+}^{n}(s)-v_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)\right)+i\left(\mathcal{N}_{1}(\hat{u}_{j}^{n}(s),\hat{v}_{j,+}^{n}(s))-\mathcal{N}_{1}(u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s),v_{j+1}^{n,2}(s))\right),\\
&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathcal{V}_{j+2}^{n}(s)=im\left(\hat{u}_{j+2,-}^{n}-u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)\right)+i\left(\mathcal{N}_{2}(\hat{u}_{j+2,-}^{n}(s),\hat{v}_{j+2}^{n}(s))-\mathcal{N}_{2}(u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s),v_{j+1}^{n,2}(s))\right),\end{aligned}\right.

(4.13)

\displaystyle+2\mathrm{Re}\left\{i\left(\mathcal{N}_{1}(\hat{u}_{j}^{n}(s),\hat{v}_{j,+}^{n}(s))-\mathcal{N}_{1}(u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s),v_{j+1}^{n,2}(s))\right)\cdot\overline{\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}}(s)\right\},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(|\alpha|+4|\beta|)|\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}(s)|^{2}\cdot\mathcal{M}_{0}^{2}+2(|\alpha|+4|\beta|)|u_{j+1}^{n,2}(s)|\cdot\Big(|\mathcal{V}_{j+2}^{n}(s)|+2\tau\cdot\mathcal{M}(T)|\Big)\cdot\mathcal{M}_{0}\cdot|\mathcal{U}_{j}^{n}(s)|

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\|\hat{u}(\cdot,t)-u_{\flat}(\cdot,t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\hat{v}(\cdot,t)-v_{\flat}(\cdot,t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\lim\limits_{\mathrm{k}\to\infty}\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\|\hat{u}_{\mathrm{k}}^{(\tau_{l})}(\cdot,t)-u^{(\tau_{l})}(\cdot,t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\lim\limits_{\mathrm{k}\to\infty}\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\|\hat{v}_{\mathrm{k}}^{(\tau_{l})}(\cdot,t)-v^{(\tau_{l})}(\cdot,t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hat{C}(T)\left(\lim\limits_{\mathrm{k}\to\infty}\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\|\hat{u}_{\mathrm{k}}^{(\tau_{l})}(x,0)-u^{(\tau_{l})}(x,0)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\lim\limits_{\mathrm{k}\to\infty}\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\|\hat{v}_{\mathrm{k}}^{(\tau_{l})}(x,0)-v^{(\tau_{l})}(x,0)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hat{C}(T)\left(\lim\limits_{\mathrm{k}\to\infty}\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\|\hat{u}_{\mathrm{k}}^{(\tau_{l})}(x,0)-u_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\|u_{0}(x)-u^{(\tau_{l})}(x,0)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)

\displaystyle+\hat{C}(T)\left(\lim\limits_{\mathrm{k}\to\infty}\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\|\hat{v}_{\mathrm{k}}^{(\tau_{l})}(x,0)-v_{0}(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\|v_{0}(x)-v^{(\tau_{l})}(x,0)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,
