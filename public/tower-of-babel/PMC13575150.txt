Identification and validation of BLK and OSBPL10 as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers for nasopharyngeal carcinoma through machine learning algorithms
Authors: Luying Zhang, Guohui Dong, Huilin Chen, Mengxia Deng, Yun Pan, Bo Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDespite substantial advances in radiotherapy and chemotherapy for nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC), a subset of patients still develops metastasis or recurrence following initial treatment. Additionally, the atypical early symptoms of NPC often lead to clinical misdiagnosis or missed diagnosis, resulting in late diagnosis and unfavorable prognosis. Thus, novel diagnostic biomarkers are urgently required. By integrating five GEO datasets and applying four machine learning models, namely LASSO, SVM-RFE, XGBOOST, and mRMR, this study identified two key NPC-related genes, BLK and OSBPL10. Bioinformatic analyses revealed that both genes are significantly downregulated in NPC, and this downregulation pattern was further validated in the GSE61218 dataset. Notably, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves confirmed their high diagnostic efficacy for NPC. BLK and OSBPL10 are involved in pathways such as B-cell receptor signaling and lipid metabolic regulation, respectively, and are closely associated with the infiltration of various immune cells. Immunohistochemical staining validation further confirmed that the protein expression levels of BLK and OSBPL10 are downregulated in NPC tissues compared with those in benign lesions, and their low expression is strongly associated with the poor prognosis of patients. In summary, these findings indicated that BLK and OSBPL10 may serve as candidate biomarkers for NPC diagnosis and prognosis, although further validation in independent cohorts is warranted.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-59045-1.Keywords: Nasopharyngeal carcinoma, Machine learning, BLK, OSBPL10, Diagnostic biomarker, PrognosisSubject terms: Biomarkers, Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Oncology

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a malignant tumor originating from the epithelial lining of the nasopharynx and is characterized by pronounced geographic clustering and ethnic susceptibility, with a high incidence in southern China and Southeast Asia1,2. Histologically, NPC is predominantly squamous cell carcinoma, accounting for more than 95% of cases. According to the 2017 WHO classification, NPC is categorized into keratinizing, non-keratinizing, and basaloid squamous cell carcinoma, with non-keratinizing squamous cell carcinoma further subdivided into differentiated and undifferentiated types3. More than 95% of NPC cases are poorly differentiated or undifferentiated carcinomas4, which exhibit high malignancy, rapid growth, and a strong propensity for lymphatic or hematogenous metastasis. A hallmark of NPC is the presence of poorly differentiated malignant epithelial cells within a complex microenvironment characterized by extensive lymphocytic infiltration5, which creates new challenges for the treatment and investigation of NPC.

Artificial intelligence (AI) has been applied in oncological research6, and machine learning (ML), as a core implementation of AI, can effectively address many analytical challenges associated with gene expression profiling in cancer studies. In recent years, various machine learning methods have been refined and upgraded for feature selection, among which LASSO, SVM-RFE, XGBOOST, and mRMR are commonly used approaches for gene screening. At present, ML is increasingly applied in NPC diagnosis and prognostic evaluation, and continuously improved algorithms and systems have enhanced processing efficiency and diagnostic accuracy, demonstrating great potential for providing more precise medical services to NPC patients7,8. Wang et al.9 employed an integrated machine learning strategy to identify the NPC-related biomarker DTL and investigated the critical role of immune cell infiltration in NPC, but did not further experimentally validate the expression pattern or function of this biomarker. Wan et al.10 designed three SVM-based prognostic models for 97 patients with locally advanced NPC by integrating tissue molecular biomarkers covering six tumorigenesis-related signaling pathways with Epstein-Barr virus-related biomarkers, and validated the models through a five-year follow-up. Despite this, the models lacked applicability for prognostic assessment in early-stage NPC patients, limiting their extension to the entire disease course. Therefore, additional NPC candidate biomarkers with diagnostic and prognostic value still need to be identified to meet the demands of individualized clinical treatment.

B-lymphoid tyrosine kinase (BLK) is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase involved in B-lymphocyte development, differentiation, and signal transduction11. BLK is expressed in approximately half of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma cases, and its high expression is associated with worse survival outcomes12. Oxysterol-binding protein-like 10 (OSBPL10) is a member of the oxysterol-binding protein (OSBP/ORP) family that participates in intracellular lipid transport, and it is upregulated in pancreatic cancer and associated with poor prognosis13. However, the expression patterns, diagnostic value, and prognostic significance of BLK and OSBPL10 in NPC remain unclear.

This study aimed to identify diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers for NPC by integrating five distinct GEO datasets, jointly screening key genes using four machine learning algorithms, namely LASSO, SVM-RFE, XGBOOST, and mRMR, followed by validation of the identified candidates in an independent external dataset. The functions of BLK and OSBPL10 were further elucidated through differential expression analysis, GO/KEGG enrichment analysis, and immune infiltration analysis, and we additionally conducted immunohistochemical validation of their protein expression and Kaplan-Meier analysis to evaluate their prognostic value.

Materials and methods

Data sources and preprocessing

Six NPC-related gene expression datasets (GSE12452, GSE13597, GSE40290, GSE53819, GSE68799, and GSE61218) were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database14 by searching the keyword “nasopharyngeal carcinoma”. Based on the probe annotation files, probe IDs were converted into gene symbols, genes with > 50% missing values and samples with > 50% missing values were removed, and missing values were imputed using the impute.knn function in the R package impute with the default number of neighbors15. Subsequently, log2 transformation stabilizes variance, row-wise Z-score normalization centers each gene’s expression, column-wise Z-score normalization adjusts for sample-specific differences, finally, combined row and column Z-score normalization was applied to harmonize scales further before merging. These steps applied to eliminate differences in data scale and systematic bias among samples. After normalization, GSE12452, GSE13597, GSE40290, GSE53819, GSE68799 were merged, and batch effects in the combined dataset were corrected using the ComBat function in the R package SVA based on an empirical Bayes algorithm16 After the above preprocessing steps, the final merged dataset comprised 141 NPC tumor samples, 43 normal tissue samples, and 3,803 genes. GSE61218 was used as an independent validation dataset, including 10 NPC tumor samples and 6 normal samples.

Differential gene screening

Limma (linear models for microarray data) is a differential expression screening method based on generalized linear models17, and the R package limma (version 3.4.6) was used to identify genes differentially expressed between NPC samples and normal nasopharyngeal tissues. The expression profile data were log2-transformed, and multivariate linear regression was performed using the lmFit function. The eBayes function was subsequently applied to moderate the standard errors to a common value via an empirical Bayes approach, thereby calculating moderated t-statistics, moderated F-statistics, and log-odds of differential expression to determine the statistical significance for each gene. Genes with |log₂ fold change (FC)| > 1 and an adjusted P value < 0.05 were defined as the final differentially expressed genes (DEGs). The analysis results were visualized using volcano plots and heatmaps.

GO and KEGG functional enrichment analysis

The DAVID database18 was used to perform GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses. GO analysis characterized the functional attributes of genes from three aspects: biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF). KEGG pathway analysis further identified significantly enriched signaling pathways, thereby predicting potential functions of genes. Bubble plots and circular plots were generated using R software for visualization.

LASSO model

LASSO (least absolute shrinkage and selection operator) is a feature selection method proposed by Tibshirani in 199619, which constructs a penalty function to shrink some regression coefficients to zero, thereby yielding a parsimonious model and enabling biased estimation for data with multicollinearity. Variables were determined by identifying the λ value that minimized classification error. After data preprocessing, 70% of samples from the merged dataset were selected as the training set and 30% as the validation set, and a binomial LASSO model was constructed using the scikit-learn package in Python. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted to screen candidate genes for NPC.

SVM-RFE model

SVM-RFE (support vector machine–recursive feature elimination) was proposed by Guyon20 and has demonstrated good performance in feature selection. It is a support vector machine–based machine learning method that identifies optimal variables by iteratively eliminating features generated by the SVM. After data preprocessing, 75% of samples from the merged dataset were selected as the training set and 25% as the validation set, and an SVM-RFE model was constructed using the scikit-learn package in Python to screen candidate genes for NPC.

XGBOOST model

XGBOOST (extreme gradient boosting) is a machine learning method proposed by Chen Tianqi in 201421 and is one of the most effective supervised learning algorithms, exhibiting significant advantages in both speed and performance compared with other algorithms. After data preprocessing, 75% of samples from the merged dataset were selected as the training set and 25% as the validation set, and an XGBOOST model constructed using the scikit-learn package in Python was applied to screen candidate genes for NPC.

mRMR model

mRMR (Max-Relevance and Min-Redundancy) is a classic filter-based feature selection algorithm proposed by Peng Hanchuan22, and it is frequently used to analyze tumor gene expression profiles. After data preprocessing, 75% of samples from the merged dataset were selected as the training set and 25% as the validation set, and an mRMR model constructed using the scikit-learn package in Python was applied to screen candidate genes for NPC.

Construction of a neural network model to validate diagnostic efficacy

A diagnostic model was constructed using a multilayer perceptron (MLP) classifier23, with the classification variable being NPC tumor versus benign nasopharyngeal tissue and input features comprising the expression levels of OSBPL10 and BLK. The model parameters were set as follows: the activation function (activation) was ReLU, the hidden layer sizes were set to (20, 10), and the maximum number of iterations was 20. The analysis included 184 samples from the merged dataset, with 20% (36 samples) randomly selected as the test set using stratified sampling, and the remaining 148 samples used as the training set, along with 20-fold cross-validation to optimize model stability. Model construction and statistical analyses were performed using R software version 3.6.3 and Python 3.7. To interpret the output of the MLP and quantify the contributions of BLK and OSBPL10, we conducted SHapley Additive exPlans (SHAP) analysis using the SHAP package in Python. The mean absolute SHAP value for each gene was calculated to represent its average impact on the model’s output magnitude.

Independent dataset validation

Independent validation was performed using gene expression data from 10 cases of NPC and 6 cases of benign nasopharyngeal in the GSE61218 dataset, ROC curve analysis was conducted using the R package pROC to calculate the area under the curve (AUC), and the ci function in pROC was used to estimate the AUC and 95% confidence interval. Differential expression boxplots for the validation data were generated using GraphPad Prism, and intergroup differences were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Immune infiltration analysis

The immune infiltration landscape of NPC was analyzed using the CIBERSORT algorithm24. The normalized gene expression data from the merged discovery cohort were uploaded to the TIMER 2.0 platform25, and the relative abundances of 22 immune cell types were estimated for each sample. Differences in immune cell infiltration between NPC and benign tissues were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank‑sum tests for each of the 22 cell types. To account for multiple comparisons, the Benjamini‑Hochberg (BH) procedure was applied to control the false discovery rate (FDR). An FDR‑adjusted P‑value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Immune cell infiltration data for individual samples and groups were visualized using the ggplot2 package. Spearman rank correlation analysis was performed to assess the relationships among 22 immune cell types. The Timer 2.0 database25 was also used to verify whether the copy number and expression levels of BLK and OSBPL10 correlate with the infiltration of six major immune cells, including B cells, CD8⁺ T cells, CD4⁺ T cells, macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells in the tumor immune microenvironment of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma.

Construction of the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network

The STRING database26 was used to input BLK and OSBPL10, with the species set to Homo sapiens, to analyze the PPI network of BLK and OSBPL10. The top 10 proteins most closely associated with BLK and OSBPL10 were identified, and the hub scores of the interacting proteins were calculated using the cytoHubba plugin in Cytoscape27. To validate the potential functional relevance of the identified protein-protein interaction networks, we analyzed the expression correlations between BLK or OSBPL10 and their top 10 interacting proteins using the TCGA-HNSC dataset GEPIA2 database (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/).

Single-cell analysis

Single-cell dataset GSE103322 of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (h5 format) and annotation files were downloaded from the TISCH database28. The original dataset comprised 18 samples with a total of 5,902 cells. The single-cell data were processed and analyzed using the R packages MAESTRO and Seurat, and t-SNE was applied for cell clustering and subgroup identification. The expression levels of BLK and OSBPL10 across different cell clusters were then visualized. Two independent NPC single‑cell RNA‑sequencing datasets (GSE150430 and GSE162025) were also retrieved from the TISCH2 database (http://tisch.comp‑genomics.org/). The gene expression levels for BLK and OSBPL10 across major cell types (including CD4⁺ T cells, CD8⁺ T cells, regulatory T cells, NK cells, B cells, plasma cells, dendritic cells, monocytes/macrophages, and malignant cells) were extracted. Heatmaps were generated using the TISCH2 online visualization tool to illustrate the expression distribution of BLK and OSBPL10 in the NPC microenvironment.

Cancer stem cell‑related features analysis

The mRNA expression‑based stemness index (mRNAsi)29 is a stemness index calculated based on mRNA expression profiles, utilizing the one-class logistic regression model (OCLR) algorithm to quantify the similarity between tumor cells and stem-like states. Using BLK and OSBPL10 expression levels from the TCGA‑HNSC dataset, patients were stratified into high‑ and low‑expression groups using the median expression as one cutoff, and the top 25% vs. bottom 25% of expression levels as another cutoff. Differences in mRNAsi score between groups were assessed using Wilcoxon rank‑sum tests. Spearman correlation analysis was performed to evaluate the linear relationship between gene expression and mRNAsi score. In addition, single‑sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was performed using the CAMOIP platform (https://www.camoip.net/). Two Reactome stemness‑related gene sets were analyzed, including the gene set for “POU5F1 (OCT4), SOX2 and NANOG activates genes related to proliferation”, and the gene set for “POU5F1 (OCT4), SOX2 and NANOG repress genes related to differentiation”. The ssGSEA enrichment scores were compared between high and low expression groups of OSBPL10 using Wilcoxon rank‑sum tests.

Hematoxylin and eosin staining

Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissues of 68 cases of pathologically confirmed NPC (Supplementary Table 1) and 13 cases of nasopharyngeal benign lesions (Supplementary Table 2) were obtained from the Department of Pathology, First Affiliated Hospital of Dali University. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Dali University (Approve No. DFY20260112001), and conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, as well as relevant national and institutional research guidelines and regulations. Tissue sections of 4 μm thickness were prepared, deparaffinized, and rehydrated through graded alcohols. Sections were first stained with hematoxylin for several minutes to render nuclei blue, followed by differentiation in acid alcohol to remove non-nuclear staining and rinsing under running water to enhance nuclear color. Subsequently, sections were counterstained with eosin for several seconds to minutes to impart a pink color to the cytoplasm and collagen. Finally, sections were dehydrated through graded alcohols, cleared in xylene, and mounted with neutral resin, allowing clear visualization of nuclei (blue) and cytoplasm/extracellular matrix (pink) under a microscope.

Immunohistochemistry

Deparaffinization, antigen retrieval, and endogenous peroxidase inhibition were performed on 4 μm-thick paraffin sections. Sections were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with anti-BLK (rabbit polyclonal, Proteintech, Cat No.10510-1-AP, dilution 1:200) or anti-OSBPL10 (rabbit polyclonal, Affinity Biosciences, Cat No. DF13209, dilution 1:100) antibodies, followed by 30 min labeling at room temperature using a goat anti-rabbit IgG polymer. Signals were visualized using DAB chromogen, followed by counterstaining with hematoxylin. Whole slides were scanned into digital whole‑slide images. For each slide, five non‑overlapping high‑power fields at 400× magnification and measuring 1024 × 1024 pixels each were systematically selected from uniformly stained regions, avoiding necrotic or hemorrhagic areas. Image Pro Plus was then used to measure the integrated optical density (IOD) in five fields per slide, and the mean IOD value was calculated.

Survival analysis

Follow-up was conducted for patients, and survival information was collected for 40 cases of NPC, with survival time defined as the interval from diagnosis to death or the last follow-up. NPC cases were divided into high and low expression groups based on the median cutoff values for BLK and OSBPL10 expression. Overall survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan‑Meier method, and differences between groups were compared using the two‑sided log‑rank test. Hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using the Cox proportional hazards model, with the low‑expression group serving as the reference.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis used in this study was conducted using SPSS version 25.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA), R software (version 3.6.3), and Python (3.7). Key genes were screened using Limma differential expression analysis and four machine learning algorithms (LASSO, SVM-RFE, XGBOOST, mRMR), and their diagnostic performance was evaluated using ROC curves. Gene functions were explored via GO and KEGG enrichment analysis and immune infiltration assessment, and t-test and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis were used to validate differences in protein expression levels and their association with prognosis.

Results

Identification of differentially expressed genes between nasopharyngeal carcinoma and benign lesions

From the GEO database, we analyzed five NPC gene expression datasets, including GSE12452, GSE13597, GSE40290, GSE53819, and GSE68799 (Fig. 1A). After batch effect removal and normalization, 3,803 common genes were identified across the five datasets (Fig. 1B). In the discovery cohort (merged GSE12452, GSE13597, GSE40290, GSE53819, GSE68799), differential expression analysis identified 181 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between NPC and benign nasopharyngeal tissues, including 87 upregulated genes and 94 downregulated genes (Fig. 1C). The top five upregulated genes included ESM1, TNFSF15, EXT1, ATP2C1, and PHF14, while the top five downregulated genes included CD72, BLK, DGKD, STAG3, and ABHD6 (Fig. 1C). Heatmap analysis showed that the top 50 DEGs exhibited markedly distinct expression patterns between NPC and normal tissues (Fig. 1D).

Functional enrichment analysis of DEGs in NPC

The DEGs identified between NPC and benign nasopharyngeal tissues were imported into the DAVID database for GO and KEGG analyses. BP analysis indicated that DEGs were mainly involved in signal receptor binding, molecular function regulation, receptor-ligand activity, and cytokine activity (Fig. 2A). CC analysis showed that the products encoded by DEGs were primarily localized to the extracellular region, endomembrane system, plasma membrane, extracellular matrix, and cell surface (Fig. 2B). MF analysis revealed that DEGs were mainly associated with system development, regulation of response to stimulus, response to external stimulus, secretion, biological adhesion, cell adhesion, and cell activation (Fig. 2C). KEGG enrichment analysis demonstrated that DEGs were significantly associated with cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, amoebiasis, fluid shear stress and atherosclerosis, small cell lung cancer, and NF-κB signaling pathway (Fig. 2D).

Determination of crucial candidate genes by four machine learning models

To accurately identify key genes in NPC, four machine learning algorithms LASSO, SVM-RFE, XGBOOST, and mRMR were applied for integrated analysis across five datasets. The LASSO model was used to plot coefficient trajectories of individual variables (Fig. 3A) and perform cross-validation analysis (Fig. 3B). Genes and model scores were then selected at λ = 0.05 (Fig. 3C), resulting in the identification of 20 candidate NPC genes, including BLK, GNMT, PTPRN2, FMO5, and OSBPL10 (Table 1). The cross-validation plot of the SVM-RFE model demonstrated strong predictive performance (Fig. 3D), leading to the selection of 31 candidate NPC genes, including BCL6, BLK, CA2, CA9, and OSBPL10 (Table 1). The cross-validation plot of the XGBOOST model indicated relatively stable performance (Fig. 3E), and five genes BLK, CENPF, KCTD3, OSBPL10, and VNN1 were ultimately identified as NPC candidate genes (Table 1). The mRMR model was applied, and its cross-validation plot demonstrated highly significant feature selection performance (Fig. 3F), resulting in the identification of 20 candidate NPC genes, including BLK, CRISP2, CD72, OSBPL10, and DGKD (Table 1). A Venn diagram was constructed to integrate candidate genes identified by the four machine learning methods, ultimately revealing BLK and OSBPL10 as the overlapping genes in the discovery cohort (Fig. 3G).

Evaluation and validation of the diagnostic value of BLK and OSBPL10 for NPC by neural network model

To evaluate the discriminatory ability of BLK and OSBPL10 between NPC and benign nasopharyngeal lesions, a neural network model was constructed using these two genes as features. ROC curve analysis showed that the model achieved an AUC of 0.909 in the test set, indicating good diagnostic discrimination (Fig. 4A). Decision curve analysis (DCA) demonstrated that within the threshold probability range of 0.5–0.8, the model yielded a significantly higher net benefit than the strategies of treating all patients or treating none, indicating strong clinical utility (Fig. 4B). Further SHAP analysis clarified the critical contributions of BLK and OSBPL10 to the classification outcomes of the model (Fig. 4C). GSE61218 was subsequently used as an independent validation dataset, showing that the mRNA expression levels of BLK and OSBPL10 were significantly higher in normal nasopharyngeal tissues than in NPC tissues (Fig. 4D, E). ROC curves based on this dataset revealed an AUC of 0.950 for BLK (Fig. 4F) and 0.967 for OSBPL10 (Fig. 4G), indicating excellent diagnostic performance of BLK and OSBPL10 expression levels for NPC.

BLK and OSBPL10 are related to immunocyte infiltration levels in NPC

Absolute immune infiltration scores were obtained using the CIBERSORT algorithm, reflecting the absolute abundance of 22 immune cell types in each sample, which were normalized to a total infiltration of 100% to calculate the relative proportion of each immune cell type. Immune infiltration distribution patterns revealed that, 18 types of immune cells showed significantly different infiltration levels between NPC and benign nasopharyngeal tissues (Fig. 5A, Supplementary Table 3). Compared with benign tissues, NPC tissues showed significantly lower infiltration of naïve B cells, memory B cells, activated CD4⁺ memory T cells, follicular helper T cells, monocytes, M2 macrophages, resting myeloid dendritic cells, and resting mast cells (Fig. 5A, Supplementary Table 3). In contrast, NPC tissues exhibited significantly higher infiltration of regulatory T cells, γδ T cells, resting and activated NK cells, M0 and M1 macrophages, activated myeloid dendritic cells, activated mast cells, eosinophils, and neutrophils (Fig. 5A, Supplementary Table 3). The heatmap also showed that the overall proportions of the 22 immune cell types differed between the two groups (Fig. 5B). Correlation analysis demonstrated distinct associations among the 22 immune cell types in NPC, with follicular helper T cells showing a strong positive correlation with resting NK cells, while naïve B cells exhibited a strong negative correlation with regulatory T cells (Fig. 5C).

Further investigation of the effects of different copy number states of BLK and OSBPL10 on immune infiltration showed that arm-level deletion of BLK was associated with infiltration levels of B cells, CD8+ T cells, CD4+ T cells, macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells, whereas deep deletion of BLK was only associated with B-cell infiltration (Fig. 6A). Arm-level deletion of OSBPL10 was associated with infiltration levels of B cells, CD8+ T cells, CD4+ T cells, and dendritic cells, while deep deletion of OSBPL10 was correlated with infiltration of B cells and neutrophils (Fig. 6B). In addition, validation using the TIMER2.0 database demonstrated that BLK expression levels were positively correlated with B cells, CD8+ T cells, CD4+ T cells, macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma. OSBPL10 expression levels were positively correlated with CD4+ T cells, macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells (Fig. 6C).

PPI networks and functional enrichment analysis of BLK and OSBPL10

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) analysis of BLK and OSBPL10 was performed using the STRING database. The top 10 proteins most closely interacting with BLK were BLNK, CD79A, CD79B, CBL, PLCG2, EGFR, PAX5, STAT3, PXN and ELF2 (Fig. 7A). The top 10 proteins most closely interacting with OSBPL10 were VAPA, PLEK, ZNF860, BBS1, VAPB, LRRC58, RENBP, DAB1, GBAS and TTC14 (Fig. 7B). Most interacting proteins exhibit a significant positive correlation with BLK or OSBPL10 (Supplementary Fig. 1). GO and KEGG analyses of BLK, OSBPL10, and their interacting proteins revealed that BLK and its interactors were mainly involved in the B-cell receptor signaling pathway, transmembrane receptor protein tyrosine kinase signaling pathway, and cell receptor signaling pathways (Fig. 7C). OSBPL10 and its interacting proteins were mainly involved in biological processes such as sterol transport, phospholipid transport, dendrite development, hippocampal development, and endoplasmic reticulum-to-Golgi vesicle-mediated transport, as well as pathways related to cholesterol metabolism, amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism, herpes simplex virus type 1 infection, and metabolism (Fig. 7D).

Cell lineage specific distribution of BLK and OSBPL10 in the tumor microenvironment

To elucidate the expression levels of BLK and OSBPL10 across different cell types in the tumor microenvironment, we performed an in-depth analysis using the single-cell dataset of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (GSE103322). Cell clustering was conducted using the t-SNE algorithm, resulting in 11 distinct cell types (Fig. 8A). BLK was predominantly expressed in CD4+ conventional T cells and plasma cell clusters (Fig. 8B). OSBPL10 was mainly expressed in endothelial cells, plasma cells, fibroblasts, and myofibroblasts (Fig. 8C). To further determine the expression of BLK and OSBPL10 in the NPC tumor microenvironment, we analyzed two NPC single‑cell datasets (GSE150430 and GSE162025) from the TISCH2 database. As shown in Fig. 8D and E, both BLK and OSBPL10 exhibited low expression levels in malignant cell type in these NPC datasets, consistent with the downregulation observed in bulk tissue analyses. In GSE150430, BLK showed relatively higher expression in B cells, CD8⁺ T cells and NK cells, whereas OSBPL10 was weakly detected in B cells and plasma cells. In GSE162025, the expression of both genes was nearly absent in malignant cells.

Association of BLK and OSBPL10 expression with stemness features in cancer

Given that stemness is closely related to the malignant progression of tumors, it is one of the core factors driving tumor heterogeneity, recurrence, drug resistance, and treatment failure30,31. Therefore, we conducted a cancer stemness index analysis in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma. No significant difference in mRNAsi score was observed between BLK high and low expression groups, regardless of whether the median cutoff or the top/bottom 25% cutoffs were used (Supplementary Figs. 2A, B). In contrast, OSBPL10 low‑expression groups exhibited significantly higher mRNAsi scores compared with high‑expression groups under both stratification strategies (Supplementary Figs. 2C, D). Spearman correlation analysis also revealed a significant negative correlation between OSBPL10 expression and mRNAsi (Supplementary Figs. 2E), indicating that lower OSBPL10 expression is associated with higher stemness. In addition, ssGSEA was performed to evaluate two signaling pathways critical for stem cell pluripotency. Low OSBPL10 expression was associated with significantly higher ssGSEA scores for both Reactome pathways, including proliferation activation and differentiation repression that are mediated by OCT4/SOX2/NANOG (Supplementary Figs. 2F), which indicates enhanced activity of the stem cell transcription factor network.

The protein expression and prognostic value of BLK and OSBPL10 in NPC

To clarify the expression characteristics and clinical significance of BLK and OSBPL10 in clinical tissue samples, NPC tissues and benign nasopharyngeal tissues were collected for H&E and immunohistochemical staining. Under low magnification, HE staining revealed that NPC tumor cells formed large, synchronous clusters with irregular nest-like or cord-like arrangements and marked cellular pleomorphism. At high magnification, tumor cell nuclei were markedly enlarged with diverse morphologies and pronounced atypia, and multiple mitotic figures were observed (Fig. 9A). Immunohistochemical staining showed that the protein expression levels of BLK and OSBPL10 in NPC were significantly lower than those in benign nasopharyngeal lesion tissues (Fig. 9B, C). Among benign lesions, strong BLK staining was observed in all 13 cases (100%) and strong OSBPL10 staining in 12 cases (92.3%). In contrast, in 68 cases of NPC tissues, low or negative expression was detected in 56 cases (82.35%) for BLK and in 52 cases (76.47%) for OSBPL10. The mean protein expression levels of BLK and OSBPL10 were 6.97‑fold and 6.23‑fold higher in benign lesions than in NPC tissues, respectively (Fig. 9B, C). To evaluate the prognostic significance of BLK and OSBPL10, 40 patients with follow‑up information were divided into high‑expression and low‑expression groups according to the median expression level of each gene. The median follow‑up time was 43.2 months (ranging from 7.5 to 94.5 months). The BLK low‑expression group experienced 7 deaths, whereas the BLK high‑expression group experienced 3 deaths. The OSBPL10 low‑expression group had 8 deaths, while the OSBPL10 high‑expression group had 2 deaths. Kaplan–Meier analysis showed that patients with low expression of BLK had worse overall survival compared (HR = 0.146, 95% CI: 0.036–0.600, P = 0.0073). Similarly, patients with low OSBPL10 expression exhibited significantly shorter overall survival than those with high OSBPL10 expression (HR = 0.232, 95% CI: 0.062–0.862, P = 0.029).

Discussion

In this study, by integrating public databases and machine learning approaches, we systematically identified key biomarkers closely associated with prognosis in NPC. Our findings indicate that BLK and OSBPL10 may serve as potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers for NPC. To our knowledge, the role of these genes in NPC has not been described previously. We additionally reveal the potential mechanisms by which they contribute to disease progression through regulation of the tumor immune microenvironment and cellular lipid metabolism, respectively.

Currently, the integrated application of multiple machine learning algorithms to identify biomarkers has become a key strategy for discovering reliable biomarkers. Compared with traditional single statistical methods, the combined use of algorithms based on different principles including LASSO, SVM-RFE, XGBOOST, and mRMR, in conjunction with cross-validation strategies, can effectively reduce the bias of any single algorithm32, thereby significantly improving the reliability and biological relevance of the results33. Our methodological framework is consistent with the concept of ensemble learning advocated in current precision medicine research34, and through algorithmic synergy, it provides a more powerful tool for mining biomarkers with clinical translational potential from high-throughput data.

The two genes identified in this study, BLK and OSBPL10, were validated for their diagnostic performance using an independent dataset, in which ROC curve analysis yielded AUC values exceeding 0.9 for both genes. These results indicate that BLK and OSBPL10 have high diagnostic accuracy in distinguishing NPC from normal nasopharyngeal tissues, supporting their potential as diagnostic biomarkers for NPC.

At present, many prognostic models for NPC primarily focus on patients with locally advanced disease, as this group accounts for a large proportion of cases and is characterized by intensive treatment regimens, substantial treatment-related toxicities, and the highest risk of distant metastasis35, thereby creating an urgent clinical demand for precise prognostic biomarker. However, this focus inevitably introduces limitations, prognostic models developed exclusively from locally advanced patient cohorts tend to incorporate predictive variables that are strongly associated with the tumor biology of this specific stage, and when directly applied to early-stage patients, their predictive performance often declines markedly or even fails36. Therefore, there is a critical need to identify prognostic biomarkers applicable to early-stage disease or spanning the entire disease course. In this study, BLK and OSBPL10 were identified based on five independent datasets encompassing patients across all clinical stages from stage I to stage IV, demonstrating considerable biological significance. These findings indicate that BLK and OSBPL10 possess prognostic value for early-stage as well as full-course NPC patients.

The role of BLK in the initiation and progression of malignancies remains controversial. Cui et al.12 reported that BLK is expressed in approximately 39.2% of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma cases, and BLK positivity serves as an independent risk factor for poor prognosis in patients treated with the R-CHOP regimen. Petersen DL et al.37 found ectopic BLK expression in malignant T cells from the majority of patients with cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, suggesting that BLK promotes malignant T-cell proliferation. In contrast, Xu et al.38 demonstrated that low BLK expression is associated with reduced survival in patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), while BLK overexpression suppresses NSCLC tumor growth and markedly inhibits oncogene expression within downstream regulatory networks. Zhang et al.39 showed that BCR-ABL down-regulates BLK through c-myc in chronic myeloid leukemia, and that BLK functions as a tumor suppressor in leukemia stem cells but does not affect normal hematopoietic stem cells or hematopoiesis. Our study likewise indicates that BLK is downregulated in NPC and associated with poor prognosis, supporting its potential tumor-suppressive function in NPC.

As a key initiating kinase in B-cell receptor signaling, BLK promotes B-cell activation and development40. BLK is also essential for the development of murine T cells and IL-17-producing γδ T cells, and is expressed in lymphoid progenitors where it regulates thymocyte numbers during this process41. Through immune infiltration analysis, we found that BLK expression was positively correlated with the infiltration levels of multiple immune cell types, particularly B cells and CD8+T cells. Single-cell analysis further demonstrated that BLK is most abundantly expressed in CD4+T cells and plasma cells, suggesting that the downregulation of BLK may directly impair the function of immune surveillance. We speculate that decreased expression of BLK may inhibit the infiltration and activation of CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells, impairing their ability to kill tumor cells. Furthermore, reduced BLK expression may impair the proper activation of the B-cell receptor signaling pathway, leading to suppressed B-cell function, decreased antibody production, and diminished antigen-presenting capacity. Consequently, this disruption of antitumor immune responses may facilitate tumor immune evasion, thereby closely linking BLK to the tumor immune microenvironment. Currently, immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting PD-1/PD-L1 have demonstrated significant therapeutic efficacy in recurrent or metastatic NPC42. As a regulator of immune signaling, BLK expression levels are therefore likely to be associated with responsiveness to immunotherapy, suggesting that BLK may serve as a potential predictive biomarker for immunotherapeutic outcomes, which warrants further investigation.

OSBPL10 is a member of the oxysterol-binding protein family, which is widely involved in intracellular lipid transport, metabolism, and signal transduction43. A previous study has demonstrated that OSBPL10 is upregulated in pancreatic cancer, in which it promotes the proliferation and metastasis of pancreatic cancer cells by mediating the expression of cellular nucleic acid-binding protein (CNBP)13. Genomic analyses have identified OSBPL10 mutations as driver events in breast cancer44. However, Tian et al.45 reported downregulated OSBPL10 expression in hepatocellular carcinoma. Additionally, the level of promoter methylation exhibited a significant negative correlation with OSBPL10 mRNA expression in pan-cancer46. Our results similarly confirmed low OSBPL10 expression in NPC, but the association between its expression and promoter methylation remains to be further explored. Tumor lipid metabolism reprogramming has emerged as a novel hallmark of cancer47. Cancer cells remodel lipid metabolism to meet the demands of rapid proliferation, membrane biosynthesis, and signaling molecule production48. Consistently, our KEGG analysis revealed significant enrichment in pathways such as cholesterol metabolism, strongly suggesting that OSBPL10 primarily participates in lipid metabolic regulation in NPC. Therefore, we speculate that low OSBPL10 expression may disrupt intracellular cholesterol and lipid homeostasis, leading to abnormal lipid droplet accumulation or dysregulated lipid signaling, thereby promoting tumor cell proliferation, invasion, and therapeutic resistance.Notably, dysregulated lipid metabolism may further modulate the immune microenvironment. Previous studies have shown that within the tumor microenvironment, lipid metabolic reprogramming leads to the abnormal accumulation of lipids and their metabolites, which not only provides sustained energy to tumor cells but also impairs the antitumor functions of immune cells, thereby facilitating tumor immune evasion49. Particularly in NPC, fatty acid metabolism and related lipid metabolic pathways have been confirmed to play central roles in tumor progression and immune regulation, with their reprogramming being closely associated with immune cell dysfunction50. In this context, lipid metabolic disorders caused by OSBPL10 downregulation may synergize with BLK downregulation mediated immunosuppression to jointly promote NPC progression. These findings also suggest several directions for future research. The development of BLK agonists in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors may be explored to relieve immunosuppression and restore tumor immune surveillance. In addition, the application of lipid-modulating agents may reverse the lipid metabolic imbalance induced by OSBPL10 downregulation, thereby inhibiting NPC progression.

Although our bioinformatics analysis suggests that BLK and OSBPL10 have immune related effects in NPC, the direct protein interactions between these molecules and immune related proteins have not been experimentally validated. Future research could use co-immunoprecipitation combined with mass spectrometry assays to identify immune related binding partners of BLK and OSBPL10 in NPC cell lines and clinical specimens. These studies will provide mechanistic insights into how these genes regulate the tumor immune microenvironment and may reveal potential targets for combination immunotherapy.

This study employed multiple machine learning models to jointly screen NPC-related genes, which enhances the reliability of the results. However, several limitations remain in this study. Although we applied ComBat to correct batch effects, sample processing and platform differences between GEO datasets, as well as residual technical differences, still exist. Furthermore, the diagnostic performance of BLK and OSBPL10 was validated in a single, small external GEO dataset containing only 16 samples. While the AUC values were high, the small sample size may result in wide confidence intervals. In addition, the clinical heterogeneity of patients, such as EBV status and treatment history, cannot be fully controlled. It is still necessary to validate in a queue of prospective collection and unified processing in the future. The prognostic analysis is based on a relatively small cohort of 40 nasopharyngeal carcinoma patients from a single institution, which may limit the statistical power and generalizability of the survival findings. Although we observed a statistically significant association between BLK/OSBPL10 expression and overall survival, the limited sample size may decrease the accuracy of hazard ratio estimation and increase the risk of overfitting. Therefore, it is crucial to validate the prognostic value of BLK and OSBPL10 using standardized treatment regimens and longer follow-up times in larger multicenter cohorts in the future. The biological functions of BLK and OSBPL10 in NPC progression have not been validated through in vitro cell experiments or in vivo animal models, and the potential direct or indirect interactions between BLK and OSBPL10 remain unclarified. Additionally, this study only validated the mRNA and protein expression levels of BLK and OSBPL10 in tissue samples, without investigating their expression in non-invasive samples such as blood, which limits their potential clinical translation as non-invasive diagnostic tools. Future studies with larger multi-center cohorts, functional experiments, and exploration of non-invasive detection approaches are required to confirm the clinical value of BLK and OSBPL10.

Conclusion

The expression of BLK and OSBPL10 are significantly downregulated in NPC and are closely associated with poor prognosis, suggesting that they may serve as candidate biomarkers for NPC diagnosis and prognosis, although further validation in independent cohorts is warranted.
