Hierarchy of entropy production and thermodynamic trade-off relations in non-Markovian systems
Ken Funo, Tan Van Vu, Keiji Saito

Abstract
Non-Markovian dynamics arise when a system is coupled to a bath with finite correlation time, giving rise to memory effects that allow the bath to temporarily store and return excitations. However, how memory modifies irreversibility and whether it can be exploited to improve thermodynamic performance is not well established. We address this question by employing a Markovian embedding of generalized Langevin dynamics, in which bath memory is encoded in auxiliary modes and irreversible dissipation in a residual Markovian bath. Here we show that the entropy production defined for the original non-Markovian system upper bounds that of the embedded system, thereby establishing a hierarchy of entropy production under Markovian embedding. Leveraging this hierarchy, we derive non-Markovian extensions of the thermodynamic uncertainty relation, speed limit, and power-efficiency trade-off. For underdamed generalized Langevin systems, sufficiently structured baths allow finite heat currents at vanishingly small entropy production, whereas Carnot efficiency at finite power remains unattainable for ordinary spectral densities. In the overdamped regime, memory effects can simultaneously reduce entropy production and current fluctuations, thereby enhancing the precision-to-dissipation ratio. We further discuss the extension of the hierarchy to generic bath models and the quantum regime. These results provide a quantitative framework for exploiting memory as a thermodynamic resource and for designing energy-efficient protocols in structured environments.

Results

Gaussian bath model.—

We start by assuming a Gaussian bath model or the Zwanzig model

Hierarchy of entropy production.—

Now, let us assume that the Markovian embedded model also satisfies the detailed balance condition, in which the steady-state of Eq. (
3

\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{t}}\propto\exp(-\beta H_{SA}^{\rm tot})

joint
system
SA

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm emb}:=\dot{S}_{SA}-\beta\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}\geq 0,

(4)

where
S_{SA}
is the Shannon entropy of the joint system and the heat flux from the residual Markovian bath is defined as
\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}:=\int d\bm{z}_{S}d\bm{z}_{A}H_{SA}^{\rm tot}\partial_{t}f^{SA}_{t}

We emphasized that the entropy production for the Markovian embedded model
\Sigma_{\rm emb}:=\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm emb}

\Sigma_{\rm emb}
depends on specific choices of Markovian embedding,

A natural question is therefore how these two definitions of the entropy productions are related to each other.

Our first main result is to show the hierarchy of entropy production under Markovian embedding, i.e., the non-Markovian entropy production
\Sigma
is

than or equal to that of the Markovian embedded model
\Sigma_{\rm emb}
(see also Fig.

\displaystyle\Sigma\geq\Sigma_{\rm emb}.

(5)

This hierarchy is obtained from the decomposition of
\Sigma
into two non-negative contributions as

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+\Sigma_{\rm mem},

(6)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\rm mem}

\displaystyle:=D(f^{SA}_{\tau}||f^{S}_{\tau}\pi^{A|S})\geq 0,

(7)

where we use the initial condition
f^{SA}_{0}=f^{S}_{0}\pi^{A|S}
to derive this result (see Methods).
Here,

is the relative entropy, and equality of (

f^{SA}_{\tau}=f^{S}_{\tau}\pi^{A|S}

Recalling that the Markovian embedding technique effectively separates the bath into the memoryless part (the residual Markovian bath) and the memory part (the auxiliary modes), Eq. (
6

\Sigma_{\rm emb}
describes dissipation into the residual Markovian bath, while
\Sigma_{\rm mem}
captures system-auxiliary modes correlation and deviations of the auxiliary modes from conditional equilibrium.
Therefore, the entropy production

From the first main result (

Entropic bound.—

Let us introduce the bath-induced current
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{O}}(t)
, which is part of the change in the observable
\mathcal{O}_{t}(X,P)
generated by the bath. As a special case, by choosing
\mathcal{O}_{t}=H^{\lambda_{t}}_{S}
, the bath-induced current reproduces the heat current:
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{O}}=\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}
.
Our second main result is a non-Markovian entropic bound:

\displaystyle\Bigl(\int^{\tau}_{0}dt|\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{O}}(t)|\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Theta\Sigma,

(8)

where

\displaystyle\Theta:=\frac{\mathcal{S}}{\beta}\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\int dXdP(\partial_{P}\mathcal{O}_{t})^{2}f_{t}^{S}(X,P)

(9)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k}\frac{c_{k}^{2}}{m_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}}\Bigl(\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{k}}{\gamma_{k}^{x}}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta_{k}}{\gamma_{k}^{p}}\Bigr),

(10)

where
c_{k}
,
m_{k}
,
\omega_{k}
,
\theta_{k}
,
\gamma_{k}^{x}
, and
\gamma_{k}^{p}
are the parameters of the spectral density (
19
).
This bound (
8
) shows a thermodynamic trade-off relation between the bath-induced current and entropy production in the non-Markovian regime.
Here,
\mathcal{S}
is lower-bounded by the effective magnitude of the memory kernel,
\int_{0}^{\infty}dsK(s)
, and the bound is saturated for the overdamped Drude–Lorentz spectral density (
25
).
The bound is controlled by
\Theta
, which is determined by the bath-side property
\mathcal{S}/\beta
and the system-side observable
\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\langle(\partial_{P}\mathcal{O}_{t})^{2}\rangle
, which makes it possible to give physical statements on the behavior of thermodynamic quantities based on (
8
) as follows.

Case 1: finite
\Theta
.
It follows from Eq. (
8
) that whenever the prefactor
\Theta
is finite,
\Sigma\rightarrow 0
implies that time-integrated
absolute
value of the current
\int^{\tau}_{0}dt|\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{O}}(t)|
also vanishes. Note that this property is the key to show the unattainability of Carnot efficiency with finite power in heat engines, as we discuss later. To investigate the condition in which
\Theta
is finite, we use the inequality
\mathcal{S}\leq K(0)/\gamma_{\rm min}
, where
\gamma_{\rm min}=\min_{k}\{\gamma_{k}^{x},\gamma_{k}^{p}\}
. Therefore,
\Theta
is finite when
K(0)/\gamma_{\rm min}
and
\langle(\partial_{P}\mathcal{O}_{t})^{2}\rangle
are finite. Note that
1/\gamma_{\rm min}
and
K(0)
are finite when the spectral density does not have sharp peaks and decays sufficiently fast at large frequencies, respectively.

Case 2: divergent
\Theta
.
Conversely, when
\Theta
diverges, the inequality allows finite currents with vanishing entropy production
50
. As a concrete example, let us take
\gamma_{k}^{x}=\sin\theta_{k}=O(\epsilon)
, such that the bound diverges:
\Theta=O(\epsilon^{-1})
. Then, the scaling of
\Sigma=O(\epsilon)
and

is allowed by the trade-off relation (

and other parameters to be

When

and the scaling of

Power-efficiency trade-off relation.—

Let us apply the entropic bound to a heat engine setup in which the system is in contact at hot and cold baths with inverse temperatures
\beta_{H}
and
\beta_{C}
, respectively.
When the engine cycle is stationary, i.e.,
f_{0}^{S}=f_{\tau}^{S}
, and satisfies the condition under which it operates as a heat engine, i.e.,
W_{\rm ext}\geq 0,Q^{H}_{\rm sys}\geq 0,Q^{C}_{\rm sys}\leq 0
, the power and efficiency of the heat engine are defined as
\mathcal{P}:=W_{\rm ext}/\tau
and
\eta:=W_{\rm ext}/Q^{H}_{\rm sys}
. Here
W_{\rm ext}:=-W=Q^{H}_{\rm sys}+Q^{C}_{\rm sys}
is the extractable work and
\tau
is the time-duration of one engine cycle. The efficiency is bounded from above by the Carnot efficiency
\eta_{\rm Car}:=1-\beta_{H}/\beta_{C}
as
\eta\leq\eta_{\rm Car}
. Then, the entropic bound for heat currents with multiple baths and time-dependent system-bath couplings (see Methods Eq. (
98
)) is reformulated as a trade-off between the power and efficiency.
Note that our setup allows time-dependent couplings, which is crucial for incorporating the work cost of switching on/off the coupling to hot and cold baths during the heat engine cycle
33
.

We now present our third main result, the power-efficiency trade-off relation for non-Markovian heat engines:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\leq\beta_{C}\bar{\Theta}\eta(\eta_{\rm Car}-\eta),

(11)

where
\bar{\Theta}:=\Theta/\tau
with
\Theta
defined in Eq. (
36
) captures the non-Markovian bath property and controls the prefactor of the bound.
When
\bar{\Theta}
is finite, the bound (
11
) implies
\mathcal{P}\rightarrow 0
when
\eta\rightarrow\eta_{\rm Car}
, i.e., Carnot efficiency at finite power is not possible.
In the Markovian limit, Eqs. (
8
) and (
11
) reproduce the results obtained in Ref.
46
(see Methods for details).

Application to overdamped systems.—

We next consider the overdamped limit, in which thermodynamic speed limits and TURs are most naturally formulated. The reduced dynamics of the system is described by the overdamped generalized Langevin equation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}\frac{dX}{dt}=\frac{\xi_{t}}{\mu}-\partial_{X}V_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}(X)-\int_{0}^{t}dsK^{\rm od}(t-s)\dot{X}(s)+\eta_{t}^{\rm od},

Generalization of the hierarchy to generic bath models and quantum regime.—

The trade-off relations above were derived for generalized Langevin systems. However, the hierarchy relation itself extends more broadly for generic bath models in both the classical and quantum regimes.
In the quantum regime, non-Markovian open-system modeling is often essential if the timescale of the bath is comparable or even longer than that of the system dynamics, such as in solid-state quantum devices
35
, complex molecular systems (e.g., quantum biology
28
and chemical physics
53
), and photonic systems
30
.
Although the framework of strong-coupling thermodynamics in the quantum regime is not well established
51
, we discuss a natural extension of our results using the definitions based on the Hamiltonian of mean force
43
.
We consider Markovian embedding based on the Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad (GKSL) master equation
5

\partial_{t}\rho^{SA}_{t}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{SA}^{\rm tot},\rho^{SA}_{t}]+\mathcal{D}_{t}[\rho^{SA}_{t}]
. The Hamiltonian reads
H_{SA}^{\rm tot}=H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}+H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}}+H_{A}
, where
g_{t}
is a time-dependent coupling, and the steady state is given by the thermal state of the joint system
\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}}\propto\exp(-\beta H_{SA}^{\rm tot})
.
We show in the Methods that

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+\Sigma_{\rm mem},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\rm mem}

\displaystyle:=D(\rho^{SA}_{\tau}||\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{\tau},g_{\tau}})-D(\rho^{S}_{\tau}||\pi^{S}_{\lambda_{\tau},g_{\tau}})\geq 0,

(17)

where
\pi^{S}_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}}:=\text{Tr}_{A}[\pi_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}}]
and we assume the initial condition (
31
). Note that this initial condition is satisfied for physically relevant scenarios such as (i) the product state
\rho^{SA}_{0}=\rho^{S}_{0}\otimes\pi^{A}
with the interaction switched off at
t=0
:
H_{\rm int}^{g_{0}}=0
and (ii) the thermal state of the joint system
\rho^{SA}_{0}=\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{0},g_{0}}
. We further note that the initial condition (
31
) reproduces the assumption of a conditional thermal state
f^{SA}_{0}=f^{S}_{0}\pi^{A|S}_{g_{0}}
in the classical regime, and Eq. (
17
) generalizes Eq. (
6
) to the case of generic baths and time-dependent coupling
g_{t}
.

Conclusion and discussion

We have established a hierarchical relationship between entropy productions under Markovian embedding, showing that the entropy production for the original non-Markovian system is larger than that of the embedded system (
5
), and their difference gives a non-negative memory contribution (
6
). We further extend this structure to generic bath models and the quantum regime (
17
).
Applying this framework to generalized Langevin systems, we derive the entropic bound (
8
) and power-efficiency trade-off relation for non-Markovian heat engines (
11
); in both cases, the prefactor of the bound is controlled by bath spectral properties.
We identify conditions under which this prefactor is finite, proving the unattainability of Carnot efficiency at finite power for non-Markovian heat engines. We further identify conditions under which the prefactor diverges, allowing finite heat currents with vanishing entropy production in genuinely non-Markovian regimes.
Extending the hierarchy in the overdamped regime (
13
), we derive thermodynamic speed limits (
14
) and TURs (
16
) for non-Markovian systems. In particular, we show that memory effects can reduce both the entropy production and current fluctuation, thereby improving the precision-to-dissipation ratio
\mathcal{Q}
.

Notably, bipartite overdamped Langevin systems describing a standard Maxwell’s demon setup constitute a natural Markovian embedding of the overdamped generalized Langevin equation. In this interpretation, the auxiliary modes play the role of both Maxwell’s demon and bath memory. Further elucidation of the bath memory effect from the viewpoint of information thermodynamics
37
is a promising direction for future work.
We note that in Ref.
12
, it was shown that the embedded entropy production can be related to the non-Markovian one in terms of work and information exchange between the system and the bath under suitable assumptions.
Another compelling open question is whether the derived thermodynamic trade-off relations extend to the quantum regime, where the interplay of coherence and entanglement with bath memory remains largely unexplored
9
;
40
.

Methods

Zwanzig model.—

The Hamiltonian of the Zwanzig model is given by

\displaystyle H^{\rm tot}_{SB}=H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}+\sum_{k}\Bigl[\frac{(p_{k}^{\rm Z})^{2}}{2m^{\rm Z}_{k}}+\frac{m_{k}^{\rm Z}(\omega_{k}^{\rm Z})^{2}}{2}\Bigl(x_{k}^{\rm Z}-\frac{c^{\rm Z}_{k}X}{m_{k}^{\rm Z}(\omega_{k}^{\rm Z})^{2}}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigl].

(18)

The spectral density for Eq. (
18
) is given by
J(\omega)=\pi\sum_{k}[(c_{k}^{\rm Z})^{2}/2m^{\rm Z}_{k}(\omega^{\rm Z}_{k})^{2}]\delta(\omega-\omega^{\rm Z}_{k})
. Note that the definition (
2
) is consistent with the strong-coupling stochastic thermodynamics framework
43
;
19
;
51
, as the Hamiltonian of mean force
H_{S}^{*}
reduces to the system Hamiltonian itself for the Zwanzig model (
18
).

Markovian embedding.—

We now assume the following parameterization of the spectral density

\displaystyle J(\omega)=\omega\sum_{k}\frac{c_{k}^{2}}{2m_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}}\Bigg[

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{k}+\delta_{k}\cos 2\theta_{k}(1-\omega/\Omega_{k})}{\Gamma_{k}^{2}+(\Omega_{k}-\omega)^{2}}+\frac{\Gamma_{k}+\delta_{k}\cos 2\theta_{k}(1+\omega/\Omega_{k})}{\Gamma_{k}^{2}+(\Omega_{k}+\omega)^{2}}\Bigg],

(19)

where its corresponding memory kernel reads

\displaystyle K(t)=\sum_{k}\frac{c_{k}^{2}e^{-\Gamma_{k}|t|}}{m_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}}\Bigl[\cos(\Omega_{k}t)+\frac{\delta_{k}\cos 2\theta_{k}}{\Omega_{k}}\sin(\Omega_{k}|t|)\Bigr],

(20)

with
\Omega_{k}:=\sqrt{\omega_{k}^{2}-\delta_{k}^{2}}
. To ensure
J(\omega)\geq 0
for
\omega\geq 0
, we impose

\gamma_{k}^{p}:=\Gamma_{k}+\delta_{k}\geq 0
and
\gamma_{k}^{x}:=\Gamma_{k}-\delta_{k}\geq 0
.
Note that Eq. (
19
) allows us to represent a wide variety of spectral densities, including the Drude-Lorentz spectral density, underdamped Brownian oscillator spectral density, and super-Ohmic spectral density (see the Supplementary material for details). See also Ref.
54
which shows the convergence analysis for discrete-mode approximations (e.g., using a sum of Drude-Lorentz spectral densities).

In the Supplementary material, we show that the following Markovian embedded model gives the generalized Langevin equation with the spectral density (
19
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{t}^{SA}

\displaystyle=\{H^{\rm tot}_{SA},f_{t}^{SA}\}+\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}^{p}\partial_{p_{k}}\Bigl(\delta p_{k}+\frac{m_{k}}{\beta}\partial_{p_{k}}\Bigr)f_{t}^{SA}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}^{x}\partial_{x_{k}}\Bigl(\delta x_{k}+\frac{1}{\beta m_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}}\partial_{x_{k}}\Bigr)f_{t}^{SA},

(21)

with the initial distribution

\displaystyle f_{0}^{SA}=f^{S}_{0}\pi^{A|S},\ \pi^{A|S}:=\frac{e^{-\beta(H_{\rm int}+H_{A})}}{\int d\bm{x}d\bm{p}e^{-\beta(H_{\rm int}+H_{A})}},

(22)

where
\pi^{A|S}
is the conditional thermal distribution of the auxiliary modes.
Here, the auxiliary Hamiltonian including the coupling reads

\displaystyle H_{\rm int}+H_{A}=\sum_{k}\Bigl(\frac{\delta p_{k}^{2}}{2m_{k}}+\frac{m_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}}{2}\delta x_{k}^{2}\Bigr),

(23)

where

\displaystyle\delta x_{k}:=x_{k}-\frac{c_{k}\cos\theta_{k}}{m_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}}X,\ \delta p_{k}:=p_{k}-\frac{c_{k}\sin\theta_{k}}{\omega_{k}}X

(24)

are the shifted position and momentum of the auxiliary modes due to the coupling to the system. The steady-state of Eq. (
21
) is given by
\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{t}}\propto\exp(-\beta H_{SA}^{\rm tot})
, and the reduced thermal equilibrium state of the system
\pi_{\lambda_{t}}^{S}:=\int d\bm{x}d\bm{p}\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{t}}
is given by the thermal distribution using the bare system Hamiltonian:
\pi_{\lambda_{t}}^{S}\propto\exp(-\beta H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}})
, for this Gaussian bath model (
23
). Due to this property, the general strong-coupling definition of
\Sigma
given in Refs.
43
;
19
;
51
reduces to the expression given in Eq. (
2
).
Note that Eq. (
21
) is the most general quadratic form of noninteracting damped auxiliary modes. Further generalization of Eq. (
21
) to the interacting case allows the representation of a wider class of spectral densities, which is left for future study.

By taking
\omega_{k}\rightarrow 0
with fixed
\kappa_{k}:=m_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}
and
\mu_{k}\kappa_{k}:=\gamma_{k}^{x}=\gamma_{k}^{p}
, the spectral density (
19
) reduces to the overdamped Drude-Lorentz spectral density

\displaystyle J^{\rm od}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=\omega\sum_{k}\frac{\mu_{k}c_{k}^{2}}{\omega^{2}+\mu_{k}^{2}\kappa_{k}^{2}},K^{\rm od}(t):=\sum_{k}\frac{c_{k}^{2}}{\kappa_{k}}e^{-\mu_{k}\kappa_{k}|t|},

(25)

and the auxiliary modes in Eq. (
21
) reduce to overdamped particles. Therefore, it is natural to use Eq. (
25
) when the system is described by overdamped dynamics (
12
).

Derivation of the hierarchy of entropy production.—

Here, we derive Eq. (
17
). Note that this derivation also shows Eq. (
6
) as a special case.
In the strong coupling regime, the internal energy
\langle\mathcal{E}_{S}\rangle
and entropy rate
\dot{S}_{S}^{*}
of the system are modified by the interaction as
\langle\mathcal{E}_{S}\rangle:=\langle\partial_{\beta}(\beta H^{*}_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}})\rangle
and
\dot{S}_{S}^{*}:=\dot{S}_{S}+\beta^{2}\frac{d}{dt}\langle\partial_{\beta}H^{*}_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}}\rangle

43
;
19
;
51
, where

\displaystyle H^{*}_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}}

\displaystyle:=-\beta^{-1}\ln\Bigl(\text{Tr}_{A}[e^{-\beta H_{\rm tot}^{SA}}/Z_{A}]\Bigr)

(26)

is the Hamiltonian of mean force and
Z_{A}:=\text{Tr}_{A}[e^{-\beta H_{A}}]
.
The heat current is then defined via the first law of thermodynamics as
\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}:=\frac{d}{dt}\langle\mathcal{E}_{S}\rangle-\dot{W}
, where the work rate is defined as
\dot{W}:=\langle\dot{H}_{SA}^{\rm tot}\rangle
. The entropy production is defined as
\Sigma:=\int^{\tau}_{0}dt(\dot{S}^{*}_{S}-\beta\dot{Q}_{\rm sys})
.

To show Eq. (
17
), we use the relation
\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}=\frac{d}{dt}\langle H_{SA}^{\rm tot}\rangle-\dot{W}
and obtain

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}=\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}+\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{d}{dt}\langle\ln\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}}-\ln\pi^{S}_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}}\rangle+\frac{d}{dt}\langle\beta\partial_{\beta}H_{S}^{*}\rangle,

(27)

and the entropy production reads

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+\mathcal{C}_{\tau}-\mathcal{C}_{0},

(28)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{t}

\displaystyle:=D(\rho^{SA}_{t}||\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}})-D(\rho^{S}_{t}||\pi^{S}_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}})\geq 0.

(29)

The condition
\mathcal{C}_{t}=0
is satisfied if and only if
\rho^{SA}_{t}=\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{t}}^{\text{Tr}_{A}}(\rho^{S}_{t})
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\pi_{t}}^{\text{Tr}_{A}}(\rho^{S}_{t}):=\varphi_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\tilde{\varphi}_{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{S}_{t}\tilde{\varphi}_{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1_{A}\Bigr)\varphi_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}

(30)

with
\varphi_{t}:=\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}}
and
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}:=\pi^{S}_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}}
is the Petz recovery map
38
under partial trace. Therefore, if the initial state is given by

\displaystyle\rho^{SA}_{0}=\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{0}}^{\text{Tr}_{A}}(\rho^{S}_{0}),

(31)

the last term in Eq. (
28
) vanishes, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}_{0}=0
, and we obtain Eq. (
17
).
Note that the initial state (
31
) contains two important cases: (i) the product initial state
\rho^{S}_{0}\otimes\pi^{A}
and (ii) the thermal state
\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{0},g_{0}}
. By choosing the interaction Hamiltonian to vanish at
t=0
, i.e.,
H^{g_{0}}_{\rm int}=0
, we have
\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{0}}^{\text{Tr}_{A}}(\rho^{S}_{0})=\rho^{S}_{0}\otimes\pi^{A}
. On the other hand, by choosing
\rho^{S}_{0}=\pi^{S}_{\lambda_{0},g_{0}}
, we have
\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{0}}^{\text{Tr}_{A}}(\rho^{S}_{0})=\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{0},g_{0}}
. If we impose the initial product state
\rho^{S}_{0}\otimes\pi^{A}
with a nonvanishing interaction Hamiltonian, we can still show the hierarchy relation
\Sigma\geq\Sigma_{\rm emb}
, but with an alternative definition of the entropy production, where the mesoscopic-leads case was obtained in Ref.
27
(see Supplementary material for details).

Let us comment on the classical case. If all operators commute with each other, Eq. (
31
) recovers the conditional thermal state for the auxiliary modes, as
\mathcal{R}_{\pi_{0}}^{\text{Tr}_{A}}(f^{S}_{0})=f^{S}_{0}\pi^{A|S}_{g_{0}}
holds by noting that
\pi^{A|S}_{g_{0}}=\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{0},g_{0}}/\pi^{S}_{\lambda_{0},g_{0}}
, and Eq. (
17
) recovers the relation

\displaystyle\Sigma=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+\Sigma_{\rm mem},\quad\Sigma_{\rm mem}=D(f^{SA}_{\tau}||f^{S}_{\tau}\pi^{A|S}_{g_{\tau}}).

(32)

By further assuming a constant coupling (
g_{t}=g
), Eq. (
32
) reproduces Eq. (
6
). We also note that the entropy production reads
\Sigma=-\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\int d\bm{z}_{S}\partial_{t}f^{S}_{t}\ln(f^{S}_{t}/\pi^{S}_{\lambda_{t},g})
, i.e., it depends only on the probability distribution
f^{S}_{t}
and the thermal state
\pi^{S}_{\lambda_{t},g}
of the reduced system.

Details of the entropic bound and multiple bath case.—

Let us show details of the entropic bound. First, we formally integrate out the auxiliary degrees of freedom from Eq. (
21
) and write down the time-evolution equation of the reduced system dynamics as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f^{S}_{t}=\{H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}},f_{t}^{S}\}-\partial_{P}(F^{\rm NM}_{t}f_{t}^{S}),

(33)

where
F^{\rm NM}_{t}:=\int d\bm{x}d\bm{p}\partial_{X}H_{\rm int}f_{t}^{A|S}(\bm{x},\bm{p}|X,P)
is the effective force from the non-Markovian bath and
f_{t}^{A|S}:=f^{SA}_{t}/f^{S}_{t}
. Then, the bath-induced current is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{O}}(t):=-\int dXdP\mathcal{O}_{t}(X,P)\partial_{P}(F^{\rm NM}_{t}f_{t}^{S}).

(34)

When we consider multiple heat baths and time-dependent couplings
g_{t}^{a}
, the entropic bound (
8
) for the heat current reads

\displaystyle\Bigl(\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\sum_{a}|\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}|\Bigr)^{2}\leq\Theta\Sigma,

(35)

and

\displaystyle\Theta

\displaystyle:=\sum_{a}\frac{\mathcal{S}_{a}}{\beta_{a}}\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\int dXdP\Bigl(\frac{g_{t}^{a}P}{M}+\dot{g}_{t}^{a}X\Bigr)^{2}f_{t}^{S}(X,P),

(36)

where
\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}
,
\beta_{a}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{a}
are the heat current, inverse temperature, and
\mathcal{S}
defined in Eq. (
10
) for the
a
-th bath (see Supplementary material for derivation).

Markovian limit.—

Let us now discuss the Markovian limit of Eqs. (

) and (

) by taking the Ohmic spectral density, in which Eq. (

) reproduces the underdamped Langevin equation with friction coefficient

\delta_{k}=0

Appendix B
Generalized Langevin equation for the Markovian embedded model

Here, we give details of the Markovian embedding in the case of multiple heat baths with time-dependent couplings

and show that the Markovian embedded model yields the generalized Langevin equation.

We begin with the total Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{SA}^{\rm tot}=H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}+\sum_{a}(H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}+H_{A_{a}}),

(48)

where

\displaystyle H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}

\displaystyle:=-g_{t}^{a}X\sum_{k}c_{k,a}\Bigl(\cos\theta_{k,a}x_{k,a}+\frac{\sin\theta_{k,a}p_{k,a}}{m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}}\Bigr)+(g_{t}^{a})^{2}\sum_{k}\frac{c_{k,a}^{2}}{2m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}}X^{2}

-m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}&-\gamma_{k,a}^{p}\end{pmatrix}.

(61)

We now introduce the row vector

\displaystyle r_{k,a}^{\mathsf{T}}:=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_{k,a}&\dfrac{\sin\theta_{k,a}}{m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}}\end{pmatrix},

(62)

so that Eq. (
56
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{dP}{dt}

\displaystyle=-V^{\prime}(X)+\sum_{k,a}g_{t}^{a}c_{k,a}\,r_{k,a}^{\mathsf{T}}\delta z_{k,a}(t).

(63)

Substituting the formal solution (
60
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{dP}{dt}

\displaystyle=-V^{\prime}(X)+\sum_{k,a}g_{t}^{a}c_{k,a}\,r_{k,a}^{\mathsf{T}}e^{M_{k,a}t}\delta z_{k,a}(0)+\sum_{k,a}g_{t}^{a}c_{k,a}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,r_{k,a}^{\mathsf{T}}e^{M_{k,a}(t-s)}(\eta_{k,a}(s)-u_{k,a}\dot{Y}_{a}(s)).

(64)

We now introduce the noise and the memory kernel as

\displaystyle\eta_{a}(t)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k}c_{k,a}\,r_{k,a}^{\mathsf{T}}e^{M_{k,a}t}\delta z_{k,a}(0)+\sum_{k}c_{k,a}\int_{0}^{t}du\,r_{k,a}^{\mathsf{T}}e^{M_{k,a}(t-u)}\eta_{k,a}(u),

(65)

\displaystyle K_{a}(t-s)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k}c_{k,a}\,r_{k,a}^{\mathsf{T}}e^{M_{k,a}|t-s|}u_{k,a},

(66)

and rewrite Eq. (
64
) in the form of the generalized Langevin equation with time-dependent coupling:

\displaystyle\frac{dX}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{P}{M}

\displaystyle\frac{dP}{dt}

\displaystyle=-V^{\prime}(X)-\sum_{a}g_{t}^{a}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,K_{a}(t-s)\Bigl[\dot{g}_{s}^{a}X(s)+g_{s}^{a}\dot{X}(s)\Bigr]+\eta(t),

(67)

where
\eta(t):=\sum_{a}g_{t}^{a}\,\eta_{a}(t)
.

To evaluate the explicit form of the memory kernel, we use

\displaystyle e^{M_{k,a}|t|}=e^{-\Gamma_{k,a}|t|}\left[\cos(\Omega_{k,a}t)\,I+\frac{\sin(\Omega_{k,a}|t|)}{\Omega_{k,a}}\,\bigl(M_{k,a}+\Gamma_{k,a}I\bigr)\right],

(68)

with

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k,a}:=\frac{\gamma_{k,a}^{x}+\gamma_{k,a}^{p}}{2},\qquad\delta_{k,a}:=\frac{\gamma_{k,a}^{p}-\gamma_{k,a}^{x}}{2},\qquad\Omega_{k,a}:=\sqrt{\omega_{k,a}^{2}-\delta_{k,a}^{2}}.

(69)

A direct calculation gives

\displaystyle K_{a}(t)=\sum_{k}\frac{c_{k,a}^{2}}{m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}}e^{-\Gamma_{k,a}|t|}\left[\cos(\Omega_{k,a}t)+\frac{\delta_{k,a}\cos(2\theta_{k,a})}{\Omega_{k,a}}\sin(\Omega_{k,a}|t|)\right].

(70)

Therefore, the memory kernel
K(t)
and the spectral density
J(\omega)
of the Markovian embedded model (
52
) for the single bath case are given by Eqs. (
39
) and (
38
), respectively.

Using the initial condition Eq. (
54
), the noise satisfies
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\eta(t)\right]=0
and the fluctuation-dissipation relation

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\eta(t)\eta(s)\right]=\sum_{a}\frac{g_{t}^{a}g_{s}^{a}}{\beta_{a}}\,K_{a}(|t-s|).

(71)

B.1
Derivation of the fluctuation-dissipation relation

We now show Eq. (
71
). First, the ensemble average of the initial values of the auxiliary modes
\delta z_{k,a}(0)
with respect to the conditional thermal state (
55
) reads
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta z_{k,a}(0)\right]=0

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta z_{k,a}(0)\delta z_{l,b}(0)^{\mathsf{T}}\right]=\delta_{ab}\delta_{kl}\,C_{k,a},

(72)

where

\displaystyle C_{k,a}=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{1}{\beta_{a}m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}}&0\\[8.0pt]
0&\dfrac{m_{k,a}}{\beta_{a}}\end{pmatrix}.

(73)

Now, let us first assume that
t\geq s
. We first define

\displaystyle\delta y_{k,a}(t):=e^{M_{k,a}t}\delta z_{k,a}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}du\,e^{M_{k,a}(t-u)}\eta_{k,a}(u).

(74)

Using the representation

\displaystyle\delta y_{k,a}(t)=e^{M_{k,a}(t-s)}\delta y_{k,a}(s)+\int_{s}^{t}du\,e^{M_{k,a}(t-u)}\eta_{k,a}(u),

(75)

and using the fact that the second term is independent of
\delta y_{k,a}(s)
and has zero mean,
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta y_{k,a}(t)\delta y_{k,a}(s)^{\mathsf{T}}\right]=e^{M_{k,a}(t-s)}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta y_{k,a}(s)\delta y_{k,a}(s)^{\mathsf{T}}\right].

(76)

We now define the equal-time covariance matrix

\displaystyle\Xi_{k,a}(t):=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta y_{k,a}(t)\delta y_{k,a}(t)^{\mathsf{T}}\right],

(77)

and show in the following that

\displaystyle\Xi_{k,a}(t)=C_{k,a}.

(78)

By taking the derivative of
\Xi_{k,a}(t)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Xi_{k,a}(t)=M_{k,a}\Xi_{k,a}(t)+\Xi_{k,a}(t)M_{k,a}^{\mathsf{T}}+2Q_{k,a},

(79)

where

\displaystyle 2Q_{k,a}:=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{2\gamma_{k,a}^{x}}{\beta_{a}m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}}&0\\
0&\dfrac{2\gamma_{k,a}^{p}m_{k,a}}{\beta_{a}}\end{pmatrix}.

(80)

We now note that the matrix
C_{k,a}
in Eq. (
73
) satisfies the equation

\displaystyle M_{k,a}C_{k,a}+C_{k,a}M_{k,a}^{\mathsf{T}}+2Q_{k,a}=0.

(81)

Because the initial condition satisfies
\Xi_{k,a}(0)=C_{k,a}
using Eq. (
72
), we find that
\Xi_{k,a}(t)=C_{k,a}
for
t\geq 0
by combining Eqs. (
79
) and (
81
). Therefore, Eq. (
78
) is obtained.

By combining Eqs. (
65
), (
76
), and (
77
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\eta_{a}(t)\eta_{a}(s)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}c_{k,a}^{2}\,r_{k,a}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta y_{k,a}(t)\delta y_{k,a}(s)^{\mathsf{T}}\right]r_{k,a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}c_{k,a}^{2}\,r_{k,a}^{\mathsf{T}}e^{M_{k,a}(t-s)}C_{k,a}r_{k,a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta_{a}}\sum_{k}\frac{c_{k,a}^{2}}{m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}}e^{-\Gamma_{k,a}(t-s)}\left[\cos\!\bigl(\Omega_{k,a}(t-s)\bigr)+\frac{\delta_{k,a}\cos 2\theta_{k,a}}{\Omega_{k,a}}\sin\!\bigl(\Omega_{k,a}(t-s)\bigr)\right].

(82)

The case of
t\leq s
is almost identical, and we finally obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\eta_{a}(t)\eta_{a}(s)\right]=\frac{1}{\beta_{a}}K_{a}(|t-s|).

(83)

We finally note that
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\eta_{a}(t)\eta_{b}(s)\right]=0
for
a\neq b
because the noises are independent for different baths, which completes the derivation of Eq. (
71
).

Appendix C
Details of the entropic bound and multi-bath heat-current bound

In this section, we give details of the derivation of the entropic bound on bath-induced currents, where we generalize the setup to the case of multiple baths and time-dependent couplings as discussed in Sec.
B
.

C.1
Work and heat current

We first give the expression of work in the case of time-dependent couplings
g_{t}^{a}
. The work rate is defined as the expectation value of the time-derivative of the total Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle\dot{W}=\langle\dot{H}_{SA}^{\rm tot}\rangle=\langle\dot{\lambda_{t}}\partial_{\lambda_{t}}H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}\rangle+\sum_{a}\langle\dot{g}_{t}^{a}\partial_{g_{t}^{a}}H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}\rangle.

(84)

Using Eqs. (
56
) and (
67
), the term
\partial_{g_{t}^{a}}H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}
reads

\displaystyle\partial_{g_{t}^{a}}H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}(t)

\displaystyle=-X(t)\sum_{k}c_{k,a}\Bigl(\cos\theta_{k,a}\delta x_{k,a}(t)+\frac{\sin\theta_{k,a}\delta p_{k,a}(t)}{m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=-X(t)\Bigl[\eta_{a}(t)-\int^{t}_{0}dsK_{a}(t-s)\Bigl(g_{s}^{a}\frac{P(s)}{M}+\dot{g}_{s}^{a}X(s)\Bigr)\Bigr].

(85)

Therefore, the work rate is expressed as

\displaystyle\dot{W}=\langle\dot{\lambda}_{t}\partial_{\lambda_{t}}H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}\rangle-\sum_{a}\dot{g}_{t}^{a}\Bigl\langle X(t)\Bigl[\eta_{a}(t)-\int^{t}_{0}dsK_{a}(t-s)\Bigl(g_{s}^{a}\frac{P(s)}{M}+\dot{g}_{s}^{a}X(s)\Bigr)\Bigr]\Bigr\rangle.

(86)

Next, we obtain the explicit form of the heat current. For multiple heat baths, we decompose the system heat current
\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}
into contributions from each bath labeled by
a
, which we denote as
\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}
.
We start from the definition of
\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}
and use the generalized Langevin equation (
67
) to obtain

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}

\displaystyle:=\frac{d}{dt}\langle H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}\rangle-\dot{W}

\displaystyle=\langle\dot{\lambda}_{t}\partial_{\lambda_{t}}H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}\rangle+\langle V^{\prime}\frac{dX}{dt}\rangle+\langle\frac{P}{M}\frac{dP}{dt}\rangle-\dot{W}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\Bigl\langle\Bigl(g^{a}_{t}\frac{P(t)}{M}+\dot{g}^{a}_{t}X(t)\Bigr)\Bigl[\eta_{a}(t)-\int^{t}_{0}dsK_{a}(t-s)\Bigl(g^{a}_{s}\frac{P(s)}{M}+\dot{g}^{a}_{s}X(s)\Bigr)\Bigr]\Bigr\rangle.

(87)

From this expression, we decompose the heat into contributions from the bath labeled by
a
as
\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}=\sum_{a}\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}
, where
\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}
is defined as

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\langle\Bigl(g^{a}_{t}\frac{P(t)}{M}+\dot{g}^{a}_{t}X(t)\Bigr)\Bigl[\eta_{a}(t)-\int^{t}_{0}dsK_{a}(t-s)\Bigl(g^{a}_{s}\frac{P(s)}{M}+\dot{g}^{a}_{s}X(s)\Bigr)\Bigr]\Bigr\rangle.

(88)

Note that the expressions of work and heat [Eqs. (
86
), (
87
) and (
88
) depend only on the quantities that appear in generalized Langevin equation and is thus independent of the specific details of the Markovian embedding. For the single bath case with time-dependent coupling, the expression Eq. (
88
) has been derived in Ref.
2
.

Next, let us express Eq. (
88
) using the Markovian embedded model (
52
).
We use Eqs. (
56
) and (
67
) to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k}c_{k,a}\Bigl(\cos\theta_{k,a}\delta x_{k,a}(t)+\frac{\sin\theta_{k,a}\delta p_{k,a}(t)}{m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}}\Bigr)=\eta_{a}(t)-\int^{t}_{0}dsK_{a}(t-s)\Bigl(g^{a}_{s}\frac{P(s)}{M}+\dot{g}^{a}_{s}X(s)\Bigr).

(89)

Substituting this expression to Eq. (
88
) gives

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}

\displaystyle=\int dXdPd\bm{x}d\bm{p}\Bigl(\frac{g_{t}^{a}P}{M}+\dot{g}_{t}^{a}X\Bigr)\sum_{k}c_{k,a}\Bigl(\cos\theta_{k,a}\delta x_{k,a}+\frac{\sin\theta_{k,a}\delta p_{k,a}}{m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}}\Bigr)f_{t}^{SA}(X,P,\bm{x},\bm{p})

\displaystyle=\int dXdPd\bm{x}d\bm{p}\Bigl(\{H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}},H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}\}-\dot{g}_{t}^{a}\partial_{g_{t}^{a}}H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}\Bigr)f_{t}^{SA}(X,P,\bm{x},\bm{p}).

(90)

C.2
Hierarchy of the entropy production

We now derive the hierarchy of the entropy production between
\Sigma_{\rm emb}
and
\Sigma
.
For the multi-bath setup, the entropy production is defined as

\displaystyle\Sigma:=\Delta S_{S}-\sum_{a}\beta_{a}\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}\geq 0,

(91)

whereas the Markovian entropy production for the embedded model reads

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\rm emb}:=\Delta S_{SA}-\sum_{a}\beta_{a}\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}^{a},

(92)

and the heat from the residual Markovian bath is defined as

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}^{a}

\displaystyle:=\int dXdPd\bm{x}d\bm{p}H^{\rm tot}_{SA}\mathcal{D}_{a}f_{t}^{SA}

\displaystyle=-\int dXdPd\bm{x}d\bm{p}\sum_{k}\Bigl[\gamma_{k,a}^{x}m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}\delta x_{k,a}\Bigl(\delta x_{k,a}+\frac{1}{\beta_{a}m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}}\partial_{x_{k,a}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\gamma_{k,a}^{p}\frac{\delta p_{k,a}}{m_{k,a}}\Bigl(\delta p_{k,a}+\frac{m_{k,a}}{\beta_{a}}\partial_{p_{k,a}}\Bigr)\Bigr]f_{t}^{SA}.

(93)

We first derive the relation between
\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}
and
\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}^{a}
as follows. We first use the relation
\{H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}},H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}\}=\{H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}},H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}+H_{A_{a}}\}=-\{H_{\rm tot}^{SA},H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}+H_{A_{a}}\}
and express the system heat current as

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}

\displaystyle=-\int dXdPd\bm{x}d\bm{p}(H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}+H_{A_{a}})\{H_{SA}^{\rm tot},f_{t}^{SA}\}-\langle\dot{g}_{t}^{a}\partial_{g_{t}^{a}}H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}\rangle.

(94)

Next, we use
\int dXdPd\bm{x}d\bm{p}H^{\lambda_{t}}_{S}\mathcal{D}_{a}f_{t}^{SA}=0
and obtain

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}

\displaystyle=\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}^{a}-\int dXdPd\bm{x}d\bm{p}(H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}+H_{A_{a}})\Bigl(\{H_{SA}^{\rm tot},f_{t}^{SA}\}+\mathcal{D}_{a}f_{t}^{SA}\Bigr)-\langle\dot{g}_{t}^{a}\partial_{g_{t}^{a}}H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}^{a}-\frac{d}{dt}\langle H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}+H_{A_{a}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}^{a}+\beta_{a}^{-1}\frac{d}{dt}\langle\ln\pi^{A_{a}|S}_{g_{t}^{a}}\rangle.

(95)

Using the above expression (
95
), we find that

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=\Delta S_{S}-\sum_{a}\beta_{a}\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}^{a}-\sum_{a}\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\frac{d}{dt}\langle\ln\pi^{A_{a}|S}_{g_{t}^{a}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+\Delta S_{S}-\Delta S_{SA}+\int dXdPd\bm{x}d\bm{p}\Bigl(f_{0}^{SA}\ln\pi_{\bm{g}_{0}}^{A|S}-f_{\tau}^{SA}\ln\pi_{\bm{g}_{\tau}}^{A|S}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+D(f_{\tau}^{SA}||f^{S}_{\tau}\pi^{A|S}_{\bm{g}_{\tau}})-D(f_{0}^{SA}||f^{S}_{0}\pi^{A|S}_{\bm{g}_{0}}),

(96)

where the last term vanishes for the initial condition given by Eq. (
54
), i.e.,
D(f_{0}^{SA}||f^{S}_{0}\pi^{A|S}_{\bm{g}_{0}})=0
. Therefore, the entropy production is decomposed as

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+D(f_{\tau}^{SA}||f^{S}_{\tau}\pi^{A|S}_{\bm{g}_{\tau}})\geq\Sigma_{\rm emb},

(97)

which completes the explicit derivation of the hierarchy relation between
\Sigma
and
\Sigma_{\rm emb}
for the Gaussian bath model with multiple baths and time-dependent couplings.

C.3
Thermodynamic trade-off relation for multiple baths

In this subsection, we derive the trade-off relation between the heat current and the entropy production presented in the main text:

\displaystyle\Bigl(\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\sum_{a}|\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}|\Bigr)^{2}\leq\Theta\Sigma.

(98)

We first show that the entropy production rate
\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm emb}
satisfies the property

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm emb}

\displaystyle=\dot{S}_{SA}-\sum_{a}\beta_{a}\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}^{a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,a}\beta_{a}\gamma_{k,a}^{x}m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}\int dXdPd\bm{x}d\bm{p}\Bigl[\delta x_{k,a}+\frac{1}{\beta_{a}m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}}\partial_{x_{k,a}}\ln f_{t}(\bm{x},\bm{p}|X,P)\Bigr]^{2}f_{t}(\bm{x},\bm{p}|X,P)f_{t}^{S}(X,P)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k,a}\frac{\beta_{a}\gamma_{k,a}^{p}}{m_{k,a}}\int dXdPd\bm{x}d\bm{p}\Bigl[\delta p_{k,a}+\frac{m_{k,a}}{\beta_{a}}\partial_{p_{k,a}}\ln f_{t}(\bm{x},\bm{p}|X,P)\Bigr]^{2}f_{t}(\bm{x},\bm{p}|X,P)f_{t}^{S}(X,P)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k,a}\int dXdP\Bigl(\beta_{a}\gamma_{k,a}^{x}m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}[v_{k,a}^{x}(X,P)]^{2}+\frac{\beta_{a}\gamma_{k,a}^{p}}{m_{k,a}}[v_{k,a}^{p}(X,P)]^{2}\Bigr)f_{t}^{S}(X,P),

(99)

where we define

\displaystyle v_{k,a}^{x}(X,P)

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{x}d\bm{p}\delta x_{k,a}f_{t}(\bm{x},\bm{p}|X,P),

(100)

\displaystyle v_{k,a}^{p}(X,P)

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{x}d\bm{p}\delta p_{k,a}f_{t}(\bm{x},\bm{p}|X,P).

(101)

Next, using the expression for
\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}
(
90
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{a}|\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\Bigl|\int dXdP\Bigl(\frac{g_{t}^{a}P}{M}+\dot{g}_{t}^{a}X\Bigr)\sum_{k}c_{k,a}\Bigl(\cos\theta_{k,a}v_{k,a}^{x}(X,P)+\frac{\sin\theta_{k,a}v_{k,a}^{p}(X,P)}{m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}}\Bigr)f_{t}^{S}(X,P)\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a}\sqrt{\dot{\Theta}_{a}(t)\sum_{k}\int dXdP\Bigl(\beta_{a}\gamma_{k,a}^{x}m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}[v_{k,a}^{x}(X,P)]^{2}+\frac{\beta_{a}\gamma_{k,a}^{p}}{m_{k,a}}[v_{k,a}^{p}(X,P)]^{2}\Bigr)f_{t}^{S}(X,P)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{a}\dot{\Theta}_{a}(t)\sum_{k,a}\int dXdP\Bigl(\beta_{a}\gamma_{k,a}^{x}m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}[v_{k,a}^{x}(X,P)]^{2}+\frac{\beta_{a}\gamma_{k,a}^{p}}{m_{k,a}}[v_{k,a}^{p}(X,P)]^{2}\Bigr)f_{t}^{S}(X,P)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{a}\dot{\Theta}_{a}(t)\dot{\sigma}_{\rm emb}},

(102)

where we use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Eq. (
99
). We also define

\displaystyle\dot{\Theta}_{a}(t):=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{a}}{\beta_{a}}\int dXdP\Bigl(\frac{g_{t}^{a}P}{M}+\dot{g}_{t}^{a}X\Bigr)^{2}f_{t}^{S}(X,P),

(103)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{a}:=\sum_{k}\frac{c_{k,a}^{2}}{m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}}\Bigl(\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{k,a}}{\gamma_{k,a}^{x}}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta_{k,a}}{\gamma_{k,a}^{p}}\Bigr).

(104)

From Eq. (
102
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\sum_{a}|\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}|

\displaystyle\leq\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\sqrt{\sum_{a}\dot{\Theta}_{a}(t)\dot{\sigma}_{\rm emb}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\Theta\Sigma_{\rm emb}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\Theta\Sigma},

(105)

where the second inequality is obtained by applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for time integral, and the final inequality is obtained by using Eq. (
97
). Here, we define

\displaystyle\Theta

\displaystyle:=\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\sum_{a}\dot{\Theta}_{a}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\frac{\mathcal{S}_{a}}{\beta_{a}}\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\int dXdP\Bigl(\frac{g_{t}^{a}P}{M}+\dot{g}_{t}^{a}X\Bigr)^{2}f_{t}^{S}(X,P).

(106)

Finally, by taking the square of both sides of Eq. (
105
), we obtain Eq. (
98
).

C.4
Entropic bound

Next, we derive an entropic bound presented in the main text:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}dt\,\sum_{a}\bigl|\mathcal{J}_{t}^{a}[\mathcal{O}]\bigr|\right)^{2}\leq\Sigma\sum_{a}\frac{\mathcal{S}_{a}}{\beta_{a}}\int_{0}^{\tau}dt\int dX\,dP\,\bigl[\partial_{P}\mathcal{O}_{t}(X,P)\bigr]^{2}f_{t}^{S}(X,P).

(107)

Here, the bound is slightly generalized to the case of multiple baths.

We start by writing the formal time-evolution equation of the system as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f^{S}_{t}=\{H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}},f^{S}_{t}\}-\partial_{P}\Bigl(\sum_{a}F^{{\rm NM},a}_{t}f_{t}^{S}\Bigr),

(108)

where the memory force is defined as

\displaystyle F^{{\rm NM},a}_{t}(X,P):=\sum_{k}c_{k,a}\Bigl(\cos\theta_{k,a}v_{k,a}^{x}(X,P)+\frac{\sin\theta_{k,a}v_{k,a}^{p}(X,P)}{m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}}\Bigr).

(109)

Then, we define the bath-induced current from
a
-th bath is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{t}^{a}[\mathcal{O}]:=-\int dX\,dP\,O_{t}(X,P)\,\partial_{P}\Bigl(F^{{\rm NM},a}_{t}(X,P)f_{t}^{S}(X,P)\Bigr).

(110)

Following a derivation similar to that of Eq. (
98
), we obtain Eq. (
107
).

C.5
Bath heat current and trade-off relation

We now discuss yet another definition of heat based on the energy change of the bath Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm bath}^{a}

\displaystyle=-\Bigl\langle g_{t}^{a}X(t)\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl[\eta_{a}(t)-\int^{t}_{0}dsK_{a}(t-s)\Bigl(\frac{g_{s}^{a}P(s)}{M}+\dot{g}_{s}^{a}X(s)\Bigr)+g_{t}^{a}X(t)K_{a}(0)\Bigr]\Bigr\rangle,

(111)

which differs by a change in the interaction Hamiltonian compared with
\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}
:

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}=\dot{Q}_{\rm bath}^{a}-\frac{d}{dt}\langle H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}\rangle.

(112)

For the Markovian embedded model,
\dot{Q}_{\rm bath}^{a}
is defined as

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm bath}^{a}

\displaystyle:=\int dXdPd\bm{x}d\bm{p}H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}^{a}}\Bigl(\{H_{A_{a}},f_{t}^{SA}\}+\mathcal{D}_{a}f_{t}^{SA}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\int dXdPd\bm{x}d\bm{p}f_{t}^{SA}(X,P,\bm{x},\bm{p})

\displaystyle\times\sum_{k}g_{t}^{a}c_{k,a}X\Bigl[\Bigl(\gamma_{k,a}^{x}\cos\theta_{k,a}+\omega_{k,a}\sin\theta_{k,a}\Bigr)\delta x_{k,a}+\Bigl(\gamma_{k,a}^{p}\sin\theta_{k,a}-\omega_{k,a}\cos\theta_{k,a}\Bigr)\frac{\delta p_{k,a}}{m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}}\Bigr].

(113)

The derivation of the trade-off relation for
\dot{Q}_{\rm bath}^{a}
is almost identical to that of
\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}^{a}
discussed in Sec.
C.3
. We start from the expression (
113
) and obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{a}|\dot{Q}_{\rm bath}^{a}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\Bigl|\int dXdPg_{t}^{a}X\sum_{k}\Bigl[\Bigl(\gamma_{k,a}^{x}\cos\theta_{k,a}+\omega_{k,a}\sin\theta_{k,a}\Bigr)v_{k,a}^{x}(X,P)

\displaystyle+\Bigl(\gamma_{k,a}^{p}\sin\theta_{k,a}-\omega_{k,a}\cos\theta_{k,a}\Bigr)\frac{v_{k,a}^{p}(X,P)}{m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}}\Bigr]f_{t}^{S}(X,P)\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a}\sqrt{\dot{\tilde{\Theta}}_{a}(t)\sum_{k}\int dXdP\Bigl(\beta_{a}\gamma_{k,a}^{x}m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}[v_{k,a}^{x}(X,P)]^{2}+\frac{\beta_{a}\gamma_{k,a}^{p}}{m_{k,a}}[v_{k,a}^{p}(X,P)]^{2}\Bigr)f_{t}^{S}(X,P)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{a}\dot{\tilde{\Theta}}_{a}(t)\sum_{k,a}\int dXdP\Bigl(\beta_{a}\gamma_{k,a}^{x}m_{k,a}\omega_{k,a}^{2}[v_{k,a}^{x}(X,P)]^{2}+\frac{\beta_{a}\gamma_{k,a}^{p}}{m_{k,a}}[v_{k,a}^{p}(X,P)]^{2}\Bigr)f_{t}^{S}(X,P)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{a}\dot{\tilde{\Theta}}_{a}(t)\dot{\sigma}_{\rm emb}},

(114)

where

\displaystyle\dot{\tilde{\Theta}}_{a}(t):=\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{a}}{\beta_{a}}\int dXdP(g_{t}^{a}X)^{2}f_{t}^{S}(X,P),

(115)

and

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{a}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k}\frac{c_{k,a}^{2}}{m_{k,a}}\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{1}{\gamma_{k,a}^{x}}+\frac{\gamma_{k,a}^{p}}{\omega_{k,a}^{2}}\Bigr)\sin^{2}\theta_{k,a}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{\gamma_{k,a}^{p}}+\frac{\gamma_{k,a}^{x}}{\omega_{k,a}^{2}}\Bigr)\cos^{2}\theta_{k,a}\Bigr).

(116)

By further using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the time integral and Eq. (
97
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Bigl(\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\sum_{a}|\dot{Q}_{\rm bath}^{a}|\Bigr)^{2}\leq\tilde{\Theta}\Sigma,

(117)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\Theta}

\displaystyle:=\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\sum_{a}\dot{\tilde{\Theta}}_{a}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{a}}{\beta_{a}}\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\int dXdP(g_{t}^{a}X)^{2}f_{t}^{S}(X,P).

(118)

C.6
Derivation of power-efficiency trade-off relation

Finally, we derive the power-efficiency trade-off relation presented in the main text:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\leq\beta_{C}\bar{\Theta}\eta(\eta_{\rm Car}-\eta).

(119)

From Eq. (
98
) and
|Q_{\rm sys}^{a}|\leq\int^{\tau}_{0}dt|\dot{Q}^{a}_{\rm sys}|
, we obtain

\displaystyle(Q^{H}_{\rm sys})^{2}\leq(|Q^{H}_{\rm sys}|+|Q^{C}_{\rm sys}|)^{2}\leq\Theta\Sigma.

(120)

Combining with the relation

\displaystyle\Sigma=-\beta_{H}Q^{H}_{\rm sys}-\beta_{C}Q^{C}_{\rm sys}=\beta_{C}Q^{H}_{\rm sys}(\eta_{\rm Car}-\eta),

(121)

multiplying
W_{\rm ext}
on both sides of Eq. (
120
), we obtain

\displaystyle W_{\rm ext}\leq\Theta\beta_{C}(\eta_{\rm Car}-\eta)\frac{W_{\rm ext}}{Q_{\rm sys}^{H}}.

(122)

We further use
\mathcal{P}=W_{\rm ext}/\tau
,
\bar{\Theta}=\Theta/\tau
, and
\eta=W_{\rm ext}/Q_{\rm sys}^{H}
, and obtain Eq. (
119
).

Appendix D
Overdamped regime

In this section, we consider the overdamped generalized Langevin dynamics (
124
) and show the hierarchy relation of the entropy production. Because Eq. (
124
) is obtained by taking the small-mass limit of the generalized Langevin equation, the relation (
97
) is still valid. In the following, we explicitly show this relation. Moreover, we derive an extended hierarchy relation:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}_{\rm tp}\leq\tilde{\Sigma}_{S}\leq\Sigma_{\rm emb}\leq\Sigma=\Sigma_{\rm M}+\Sigma_{\rm NM}\leq\Sigma_{\rm M},

(123)

where
\tilde{\Sigma}_{S}
is the partial entropy production of the system defined in Eq. (
144
). Finally, we give details of the thermodynamic trade-off relations presented in the main text.

D.1
Overdamped generalized Langevin equation

In the following, we consider taking the small mass limit of the generalized Langevin equation and obtain the overdamped generalized Langevin equation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}\dot{X}(t)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{X}V_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}-\int^{t}_{0}dsK^{\rm od}(t-s)\dot{X}(s)+\eta^{\rm od}(t)+\frac{1}{\mu}\xi(t),

(124)

where
\mu
is the mobility,
\xi(t)
and
\eta^{\rm od}(t)
are uncorrelated Gaussian white noise and Gaussian colored noise satisfying

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi(t)]=\mathbb{E}[\eta^{\rm od}(t)]=0,\quad\mathbb{E}[\xi(t)\xi(s)]=\frac{2\mu}{\beta}\delta(t-s),\quad\mathbb{E}[\eta^{\rm od}(t)\eta^{\rm od}(s)]=\frac{1}{\beta}K^{\rm od}(|t-s|),

(125)

and
K^{\rm od}(t)
is a memory kernel defined in Eq. (
45
) by assuming the overdamped Drude-Lorentz spectral density (
44
).

For simplicity, let us start from the underdamped generalized Langevin equation with a single heat bath and constant coupling
g_{t}=1
:

\displaystyle\frac{dX}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{P}{M}

\displaystyle\frac{dP}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{X}V_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}-\int^{t}_{0}dsK(t-s)\frac{P(s)}{M}+\eta(t).

(126)

We first take the following form of the memory kernel
K(t)
and the noise
\eta(t)
:

\displaystyle K(t-s)

\displaystyle=K^{\rm od}(t-s)+\frac{2}{\mu}\delta(t-s),

\displaystyle\eta(t)

\displaystyle=\eta^{\rm od}(t)+\frac{1}{\mu}\xi(t).

(127)

Then, the generalized Langevin equation (
126
) takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{dP}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{X}V_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}-\frac{1}{\mu}\dot{X}(t)-\int^{t}_{0}dsK^{\rm od}(t-s)\dot{X}(s)+\eta^{\rm od}(t)+\frac{1}{\mu}\xi(t).

(128)

We now formally take the small-mass limit
M\rightarrow 0
to obtain
34

\displaystyle 0=-\partial_{X}V_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}-\frac{1}{\mu}\dot{X}(t)-\int^{t}_{0}dsK^{\rm od}(t-s)\dot{X}(s)+\eta^{\rm od}(t)+\frac{1}{\mu}\xi(t),

(129)

which gives Eq. (
124
).

Next, we consider a different route to obtain Eq. (
124
) via Markovian embedding.
We start from the generalized Langevin equation with underdamped Brownian oscillator spectral density.
The Langevin equation of the joint system reads

\displaystyle\frac{dX}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{P}{M},

\displaystyle\frac{dP}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{X}V_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}+\sum_{k}c_{k}\left(x_{k}-\frac{c_{k}}{\kappa_{k}}X\right)-\frac{1}{\mu}\dot{X}+\frac{1}{\mu}\xi,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\frac{dx_{k}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\kappa_{k}\left(x_{k}-\frac{c_{k}}{\kappa_{k}}X\right)+\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\xi_{k},

(130)

where
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{k}(t)\xi_{l}(s)]=(2\mu_{k}/\beta)\delta(t-s)\delta_{k,l}
.

We now take the small-mass limit of Eq. (
130
) and obtain coupled overdamped Langevin dynamics for
(X,\bm{x})
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}\frac{dX}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{X}V_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}+\sum_{k}c_{k}\left(x_{k}-\frac{c_{k}}{\kappa_{k}}X\right)+\frac{1}{\mu}\xi,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\frac{dx_{k}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\kappa_{k}\left(x_{k}-\frac{c_{k}}{\kappa_{k}}X\right)+\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\xi_{k}.

(131)

We formally solve Eq. (
131
) and obtain

\displaystyle x_{k}(t)-\frac{c_{k}}{\kappa_{k}}X(t)=e^{-\mu_{k}\kappa_{k}t}\Bigl(x_{k}(0)-\frac{c_{k}}{\kappa_{k}}X(0)\Bigr)-\int^{t}_{0}dse^{-\mu_{k}\kappa_{k}(t-s)}\Bigl(\frac{c_{k}}{\kappa_{k}}\dot{X}(s)+\xi_{k}(s)\Bigr),

(132)

which gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k}c_{k}\Bigl(x_{k}(t)-\frac{c_{k}}{\kappa_{k}}X(t)\Bigr)=-\int^{t}_{0}dsK^{\rm od}(t-s)\dot{X}(s)+\eta^{\rm od}(t),

(133)

where

\displaystyle\eta^{\rm od}(t):=\sum_{k}c_{k}e^{-\mu_{k}\kappa_{k}t}\Bigl(x_{k}(0)-\frac{c_{k}}{\kappa_{k}}X(0)\Bigr)+\int^{t}_{0}ds\sum_{k}c_{k}e^{-\mu_{k}\kappa_{k}(t-s)}\xi_{k}(s).

(134)

We assume the initial state of the form

\displaystyle f^{SA}_{0}(X,\bm{x})=f_{0}^{S}(X)\pi^{A|S}(\bm{x}|X),

(135)

where
\pi^{A|S}(\bm{x}|X)
is the auxiliary thermal state conditioned on
X
, given by

\displaystyle\pi^{A|S}(\bm{x}|X):=\frac{e^{-\beta(H_{\rm int}+V_{A})}}{Z_{A}},

(136)

with
Z_{A}=\int d\bm{x}\exp(-\beta V_{A})
and the potentials read

\displaystyle H_{\rm int}

\displaystyle:=-\sum_{k}c_{k}x_{k}X+\sum_{k}\frac{c_{k}^{2}}{2\kappa_{k}}X^{2},\quad V_{A}:=\sum_{k}\frac{\kappa_{k}}{2}x_{k}^{2}.

(137)

Note that under the initial condition (
135
), the noise
\eta^{\rm od}(t)
(
134
) satisfies the property given by Eq. (
125
).

Finally, substituting Eq. (
133
) to Eq. (
131
) yields the overdamped generalized Langevin equation (
124
).

D.2
Fokker-Planck equation and entropy production for embedded system

From the Langevin equation (
131
) for the joint system
SA
, the Fokker-Planck equation for the Markovian embedded model reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f^{SA}_{t}(X,\bm{x})=-\partial_{X}(\nu_{X}f_{t}^{SA})-\sum_{k}\partial_{x_{k}}(\nu_{x_{k}}f_{t}^{SA}),

(138)

where the mean local velocities are

\displaystyle\nu_{X}=\mu(-\partial_{X}H_{SA}^{\rm tot}-\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_{X}\ln f_{t}^{SA}),\quad\nu_{x_{k}}=\mu_{k}(-\partial_{x_{k}}H_{SA}^{\rm tot}-\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_{x_{k}}\ln f_{t}^{SA}).

(139)

Here,

\displaystyle H^{\rm tot}_{SA}

\displaystyle=V_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}+H_{\rm int}+V_{A}

\displaystyle=V_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}(X)+\sum_{k}\frac{\kappa_{k}}{2}\Bigl(x_{k}-\frac{c_{k}}{\kappa_{k}}X\Bigr)^{2}

(140)

is the potential of the joint system.
The steady state of Eq. (
138
) is given by

\displaystyle\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{t}}(X,\bm{x})=\frac{e^{-\beta H_{SA}^{\rm tot}(X,\bm{x})}}{Z}.

(141)

For the joint system
SA
, the standard Markovian entropy production rate is defined as

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm emb}

\displaystyle:=\dot{S}_{SA}-\beta\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{\mu}\int dXd\bm{x}\nu_{X}^{2}f_{t}^{SA}+\sum_{k}\frac{\beta}{\mu_{k}}\int dXd\bm{x}\nu_{x_{k}}^{2}f_{t}^{SA}

\displaystyle=\dot{\tilde{\Sigma}}_{S}+\dot{\tilde{\Sigma}}_{A}\geq 0,

(142)

where the heat is defined as

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}

\displaystyle:=\int dXd\bm{x}H_{SA}^{\rm tot}\partial_{t}f_{t}^{SA}.

(143)

and
\dot{S}_{SA}
is the Shannon entropy change of the joint system
SA
. We also introduce the partial entropy production of the subsystems (see, for example Ref.
32
):

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}_{S}

\displaystyle:=\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\frac{\beta}{\mu}\int dXd\bm{x}\nu_{X}^{2}f_{t}^{SA}\geq 0,

(144)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}_{A}

\displaystyle:=\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\sum_{k}\frac{\beta}{\mu_{k}}\int dXd\bm{x}\nu_{x_{k}}^{2}f_{t}^{SA}\geq 0.

(145)

From Eq. (
142
) and the nonnegativity of the partial entropy productions, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}_{S}\leq\Sigma_{\rm emb},

(146)

which shows the first inequality of Eq. (
123
).

D.3
Hierarchy of entropy production

D.3.1
\Sigma_{\rm emb}\leq\Sigma

Now, we define the system heat flux as the change of the system energy (note that we take constant coupling
g_{t}=1
for simplicity):

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}

\displaystyle:=\int dXd\bm{x}\ V_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}(X)\partial_{t}f^{SA}_{t},

(147)

and define the non-Markovian entropy production as

\displaystyle\Sigma=\Delta S_{S}-\beta\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}.

(148)

By comparing Eqs. (
143
) and (
147
), the relation between
\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}
and
\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}
reads

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}

\displaystyle=\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}-\int dXd\bm{x}(H_{\rm int}^{SA}+V_{A})\partial_{t}f_{t}^{SA}

\displaystyle=\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}+\frac{1}{\beta}\int dXd\bm{x}(\ln\pi^{A|S})\partial_{t}f_{t}^{SA}.

(149)

Then, we find that

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=\Delta S_{S}-\beta\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}-\int dXd\bm{x}f_{\tau}^{SA}\ln\pi^{A|S}+\int dXd\bm{x}f_{0}^{SA}\ln\pi^{A|S}

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+D(f_{\tau}^{SA}||f_{\tau}^{S}\pi^{A|S})-D(f_{0}^{SA}||f_{0}^{S}\pi^{A|S})

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+D(f_{\tau}^{SA}||f_{\tau}^{S}\pi^{A|S})

\displaystyle\geq\Sigma_{\rm emb},

(150)

where we use the initial condition (
135
) and obtain the third line. This expression (
150
) shows the second inequality of Eq. (
123
).

D.3.2
\Sigma_{\rm M}
and
\Sigma_{\rm NM}

We further decompose
\Sigma
into two contributions. First, let us write down the continuity equation for the marginal distribution of the system
f_{t}^{S}(X)
by integrating out auxiliary degrees of freedom from Eq. (
138
) as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{t}^{S}(X)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{X}J_{t}^{S}(X),

(151)

with current

\displaystyle J_{t}^{S}(X):=\mu\Bigl(-V_{\lambda_{t}}^{\prime}(X)-\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_{X}\ln f_{t}^{S}(X)+F^{\rm NM}_{t}(X)\Bigr)f_{t}^{S}(X),

(152)

where

\displaystyle F^{\rm NM}_{t}(X)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k}c_{k}v_{k}^{A}(X),

(153)

is the thermodynamic force from the non-Markovian bath, with

\displaystyle v^{A}_{k}(X)

\displaystyle:=\int d\bm{x}\delta x_{k}f_{t}(\bm{x}|X),\quad\delta x_{k}:=x_{k}-\frac{c_{k}}{\kappa_{k}}X,

(154)

and
f_{t}(\bm{x}|X)=f_{t}^{SA}(X,\bm{x})/f_{t}^{S}(X)
. We also introduce

\displaystyle\nu_{t}(X):=-V_{\lambda_{t}}^{\prime}(X)-\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_{X}\ln f_{t}^{S}(X).

(155)

Then, we decompose the entropy production into

\displaystyle\Sigma=\mu\beta\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\int dX\nu_{t}(X)[\nu_{t}(X)+F_{t}^{\rm NM}(X)]f_{t}^{S}(X)=\Sigma_{\rm M}+\Sigma_{\rm NM},

(156)

with

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\rm M}

\displaystyle:=\mu\beta\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\int dXa_{t}^{2}(X)f_{t}^{S}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{\mu}\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\int dX\Bigl(-V_{\lambda_{t}}^{\prime}(X)-\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_{X}\ln f_{t}^{S}(X)\Bigr)^{2}f_{t}^{S}(X)

(157)

is the usual expression of the entropy production for Markovian overdamped systems, and

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\rm NM}:=\mu\beta\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\int dX\nu_{t}(X)F_{t}^{\rm NM}(X)f_{t}^{S}(X)

(158)

quantifies the non-Markovian correction.

In what follows, we show the hierarchy relation

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\rm NM}\leq 0,\quad\Sigma_{\rm M}\geq\Sigma.

(159)

To show Eq. (
159
), we first use the relation

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}_{S}

\displaystyle=\mu\beta\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\int dXd\bm{x}\Bigl[\nu_{t}(X)-\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_{X}\ln\frac{f_{t}(\bm{x}|X)}{\pi^{A|S}(\bm{x}|X)}\Bigr]^{2}f_{t}(\bm{x}|X)f_{t}^{S}(X)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm M}+2\Sigma_{\rm NM}+I_{X},

(160)

where

\displaystyle I_{X}:=\frac{\mu}{\beta}\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\int dXd\bm{x}\Bigl[\partial_{X}\ln\frac{f_{t}(\bm{x}|X)}{\pi^{A|S}(\bm{x}|X)}\Bigr]^{2}f_{t}^{SA}(X,\bm{x})\geq 0,

(161)

and we use the relation

\displaystyle\int d\bm{x}f_{t}(\bm{x}|X)\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_{X}\ln\frac{f_{t}(\bm{x}|X)}{\pi^{A|S}(\bm{x}|X)}=-F_{t}^{\rm NM}(X).

(162)

Finally, we combine with Eq. (
150
) and obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+D(f_{\tau}^{SA}||f^{S}_{\tau}\pi^{A|S})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\Sigma}_{S}+\tilde{\Sigma}_{A}+D(f_{\tau}^{SA}||f^{S}_{\tau}\pi^{A|S})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\Sigma}_{A}+D(f_{\tau}^{SA}||f^{S}_{\tau}\pi^{A|S})+\Sigma+\Sigma_{\rm NM}+I_{X},

(163)

which leads to

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\rm NM}=-\tilde{\Sigma}_{A}-D(f_{\tau}^{SA}||f^{S}_{\tau}\pi^{A|S})-I_{X}\leq 0.

(164)

D.3.3
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\rm tp}

We introduce a transport cost associated with the continuity equation (
152
) as

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}_{\rm tp}:=\frac{\beta}{\mu}\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\int dX\frac{[J_{t}^{S}(X)]^{2}}{f_{t}^{S}(X)}\geq 0.

(165)

This quantity is related to the entropy production as

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}_{\rm tp}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\Sigma}_{S}+\tilde{I}_{X}-I_{X}\leq\tilde{\Sigma}_{S},

(166)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{X}

\displaystyle:=\mu\beta\int_{0}^{\tau}dt\int dX\,F_{t}^{\rm NM}(X)^{2}f_{t}^{S}(X),

(167)

which satisfies
I_{X}\geq\tilde{I}_{X}
.

We also obtain a relation

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}_{\rm tp}=\Sigma+\Sigma_{\rm NM}+\tilde{I}_{X}\leq\Sigma,

(168)

where

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\rm NM}+\tilde{I}_{X}=-\tilde{\Sigma}_{A}-D(f_{\tau}^{SA}||f^{S}_{\tau}\pi^{A|S})-(I_{X}-\tilde{I}_{X})\leq 0.

(169)

D.4
Thermodynamic trade-off relations in the overdamped regime

D.4.1
Thermodynamic speed limit

We now derive the thermodynamic speed limit for overdamped generalized Langevin systems described by Eq. (
124
):

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{W}(f^{S}_{0},f^{S}_{\tau})^{2}}{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{\beta}\Sigma.

(170)

To show Eq. (
170
), we use the continuity equation (
152
) and note that the Wasserstein distance is bounded from above as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{W}(f^{S}_{0},f^{S}_{\tau})^{2}}{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{\beta}\tilde{\Sigma}_{\rm tp}\leq\frac{\mu}{\beta}\tilde{\Sigma}_{S}.

(171)

Note that Eq. (
171
) was derived in the context of bipartite overdamped Langevin systems in Ref.
32
(see also Ref.
20
that shows the achievability of this bound).
We further combine Eq. (
171
) with the hierarchy of the entropy production (
123
) and obtain Eq. (
170
).

D.4.2
TUR

Let us discuss details of the TUR shown in the main text:

\displaystyle 2\frac{\left[\langle\mathcal{J}_{\tau}^{S}\rangle+\Delta\langle\mathcal{J}_{\tau}^{S}\rangle\right]^{2}}{\text{Var}[\mathcal{J_{\tau}^{S}}]}\leq\Sigma.

(172)

Here, the explicit form of the current
\mathcal{J}_{\tau}^{S}
that we consider reads:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\tau}^{S}=\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}dtf[X(t),\lambda_{vt}]\circ\dot{X}(t),

(173)

where
f
is an arbitrary function and
\circ
denotes the Stratonovich product. By further using the overdamped generalized Langevin equation (
124
), the current is expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\tau}^{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}dtf[X(t),\lambda_{vt}]\circ\xi_{t}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}dtf[X(t),\lambda_{vt}]\Bigl(-\mu\partial_{X}V_{S}^{\lambda_{vt}}(X(t))-\int^{t}_{0}dsK^{\rm od}(t-s)\dot{X}(s)+\eta^{\rm od}_{t}\Bigr).

(174)

From this expression, the current
\mathcal{J}^{S}_{\tau}
can be obtained by solving Eq. (
124
) and is independent of the specific form of the Markovian embedding.

We now express Eq. (
174
) using the Markovian embedding, which reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\tau}^{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}dtf[X(t),\lambda_{vt}]\circ\xi_{t}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}dtf[X(t),\lambda_{vt}]\Bigl[-\mu\partial_{X}V_{S}^{\lambda_{vt}}(X(t))+\mu\sum_{k}c_{k}\Bigl(x_{k}(t)-\frac{c_{k}}{\kappa_{k}}X(t)\Bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\Bigl(f[X(t),\lambda_{vt}]\circ\xi_{t}-\mu f[X(t),\lambda_{vt}]\partial_{X}H^{\rm tot}_{SA}(X,\bm{x})\Bigr).

(175)

Therefore, using Eq. (
175
) for Markovian embedded overdamped system allows direct application of Ref.
25
and obtain

\displaystyle 2\frac{\left[\langle\mathcal{J}_{\tau}^{S}\rangle+\Delta\langle\mathcal{J}_{\tau}^{S}\rangle\right]^{2}}{\text{Var}[\mathcal{J_{\tau}^{S}}]}\leq\Sigma_{\rm emb}.

(176)

By further applying the hierarchy of the entropy production (
123
), we obtain the non-Markovian TUR (
172
). Note that all the quantities appearing in Eq. (
172
) are independent of the specific choice of embedding.

D.4.3
Entropic bound

We consider a current of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{t}^{\rm od}[\mathcal{O}]:=-\int dX\,\mathcal{O}_{t}(X)\,\partial_{X}J_{t}^{S}(X).

(177)

By using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the entropic bound is obtained as:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}dt\,\bigl|\mathcal{J}_{t}^{\rm od}[O]\bigr|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{\beta}\,\tilde{\Sigma}_{\rm tp}\int_{0}^{\tau}dt\int dX\,\bigl[\partial_{X}O_{t}(X)\bigr]^{2}f_{t}^{S}(X)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{\beta}\,\Sigma\int_{0}^{\tau}dt\int dX\,\bigl[\partial_{X}O_{t}(X)\bigr]^{2}f_{t}^{S}(X).

(178)

Appendix E
Hierarchy of entropy production for general bath case in the classical regime

As for consistency, we give explicit derivation of the hierarchy of entropy production for general bath case in the classical regime. Note that this derivation is essentially the classical version of the hierarchy relation in the quantum regime presented in the main text and in the Methods.

We now show the hierarchy relation

\displaystyle\Sigma=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+D(f^{SA}_{\tau}||f^{S}_{\tau}\pi^{A|S})

(179)

for the general bath case, where the total Hamiltonian given by

\displaystyle H^{\rm tot}_{SA}(\bm{z}_{S},\bm{z}_{A})=H^{S}_{\lambda_{t}}(\bm{z}_{S})+H_{\rm int}(\bm{z}_{S},\bm{z}_{A})+H_{A}(\bm{z}_{A}).

(180)

Here, we only let
H^{S}_{\lambda_{t}}
to depend on time via a control parameter
\lambda_{t}
.

Let us start from the definition of the heat current

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}:=\frac{d}{dt}\langle\mathcal{E}_{S}\rangle-\dot{W},

(181)

where the internal energy reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{S}

\displaystyle:=H_{S}^{*}+\beta\partial_{\beta}H_{S}^{*}

(182)

and the Hamiltonian of mean force is given as

\displaystyle H_{S}^{*}

\displaystyle:=H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}-\beta^{-1}\ln\Bigl[\int d\bm{z}_{A}e^{-\beta(H_{\rm int}+H_{A})}/Z_{A}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=H_{SA}^{\rm tot}+\beta^{-1}\ln\pi^{A|S}+\beta^{-1}\ln Z_{A},

(183)

with the conditional thermal state of the auxiliary modes

\displaystyle\pi^{A|S}:=\frac{e^{-\beta(H_{\rm int}+H_{A})}}{\int d\bm{z}_{A}e^{-\beta(H_{\rm int}+H_{A})}}.

(184)

Now, using the relation

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}=\frac{d}{dt}\langle H_{SA}^{\rm tot}\rangle-\langle\dot{H}_{SA}^{\rm tot}\rangle=\frac{d}{dt}\langle H_{SA}^{\rm tot}\rangle-\dot{W},

(185)

we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}

\displaystyle=\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}-\frac{d}{dt}\langle H^{\rm tot}_{SA}\rangle+\frac{d}{dt}\langle H^{*}_{S}\rangle+\frac{d}{dt}\langle\beta\partial_{\beta}H_{S}^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}+\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{d}{dt}\langle\ln\pi^{A|S}\rangle+\frac{d}{dt}\langle\beta\partial_{\beta}H_{S}^{*}\rangle,

(186)

by noting that
\ln Z_{A}
is time-independent.

Using the definitions of the entropy production

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle:=\Delta S^{*}_{S}-\beta\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\dot{Q}_{\rm sys},

(187)

\displaystyle\Delta S^{*}_{S}

\displaystyle:=\Delta S_{S}+\beta^{2}\Delta\langle\partial_{\beta}H_{S}^{*}\rangle,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\rm emb}

\displaystyle:=\Delta S_{SA}-\beta\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\dot{Q}_{\rm emb},

(188)

We find that

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=\Delta S^{*}_{S}-\beta\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}-\Delta\langle\ln\pi^{A|S}\rangle-\beta^{2}\Delta\langle\partial_{\beta}H_{S}^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+\Delta S_{S}-\Delta S_{SA}-\Delta\langle\ln\pi^{A|S}\rangle

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+D(f^{SA}_{\tau}||f^{S}_{\tau}\pi^{A|S})-D(f_{0}^{SA}||f^{S}_{0}\pi^{A|S}),

(189)

where the last term vanishes under the initial condition
f_{0}^{SA}=f_{0}^{S}\pi^{A|S}
, i.e.,
D(f_{0}^{SA}||f^{S}_{0}\pi^{A|S})=0
, which completes the derivation of Eq. (
179
).

Let us now introduce the reduced thermal state of the system
\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}
as

\displaystyle\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}(\bm{z}_{S}):=\int d\bm{z}_{A}\frac{e^{-\beta H_{SA}^{\rm tot}}}{Z_{SA}},

(190)

and express thermodynamic quantities using
\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}
.

First, we express
\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}
using
\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}
. Note that using the relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{S}

\displaystyle=H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}-\partial_{\beta}\ln\Bigl[\int d\bm{z}_{A}e^{-\beta(H_{\rm int}+H_{A})}/Z_{A}\Bigr],

(191)

the heat current is expressed as

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{z}_{S}d\bm{z}_{A}\mathcal{E}_{S}\partial_{t}f^{SA}_{t}+\langle\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{S}\rangle-\dot{W}

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{z}_{S}d\bm{z}_{A}\mathcal{E}_{S}\partial_{t}f^{SA}_{t}.

(192)

We further use the relation

\displaystyle H_{S}^{*}

\displaystyle=-\beta^{-1}\ln\Bigl[\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}Z_{SA}/Z_{A}\Bigr],

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{S}

\displaystyle:=-\partial_{\beta}\ln\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}-\partial_{\beta}\ln\frac{Z_{SA}}{Z_{A}},

(193)

and obtain

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm sys}

\displaystyle=-\int d\bm{z}_{S}d\bm{z}_{A}\Bigl(\partial_{\beta}\ln\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}+\partial_{\beta}\ln\frac{Z_{SA}}{Z_{A}}\Bigr)\partial_{t}f^{SA}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\int d\bm{z}_{S}\partial_{\beta}\ln\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}(\bm{z}_{S})\partial_{t}f^{S}_{t}(\bm{z}_{S}).

(194)

Next, let us express
\dot{S}^{*}_{S}
using
\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}
. We first note that
\partial_{\beta}H_{S}^{*}
does not depend on
\lambda_{t}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\dot{S}^{*}_{S}

\displaystyle=\dot{S}_{S}+\beta^{2}\int d\bm{z}_{S}d\bm{z}_{A}\partial_{\beta}H_{S}^{*}\partial_{t}f_{t}^{SA}

\displaystyle=\dot{S}_{S}-\beta^{2}\int d\bm{z}_{S}d\bm{z}_{A}\partial_{\beta}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}+\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\frac{Z_{SA}}{Z_{A}}\Bigr)\partial_{t}f_{t}^{SA}

\displaystyle=\dot{S}_{S}-\beta^{2}\int d\bm{z}_{S}\partial_{\beta}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}(\bm{z}_{S})\Bigr)\partial_{t}f_{t}^{S}(\bm{z}_{S}).

(195)

Combining the expressions (
194
) and (
195
), we find that

\displaystyle\Sigma=-\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\int d\bm{z}_{S}\partial_{t}f^{S}_{t}(\bm{z}_{S})\ln\frac{f_{t}^{S}(\bm{z}_{S})}{\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}(\bm{z}_{S})},

(196)

and the entropy production Eq. (
196
) depends only on the marginal probability distribution
f^{S}_{t}(\bm{z}_{S})
and the reduced thermal state of the system
\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}(\bm{z}_{S})
. Therefore, as long as the reduced system dynamics of
f^{S}_{t}
and the thermal state
\pi^{*}_{\lambda_{t}}
reproduce those of the original non-Markovian dynamics, the entropy production Eq. (
187
) does not depend on the specific choice of Markovian embedding.

Appendix F
Alternative definition of entropy production for product initial states

In this section, we consider alternative definition of the entropy production for product initial state with non-vanishing interaction Hamiltonian for the quantum case and derive the hierarchy of entropy production.

F.1
Unitary system-bath model

The total Hamiltonian of the system-bath model reads

\displaystyle H^{\rm tot}_{SB}=H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}+H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}}+H_{B},

(197)

and the time-evolution is described by the von Neumann equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{SB}_{t}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H^{\rm tot}_{SB},\rho^{SB}_{t}].

(198)

We assume that the initial state is given by the product state of the form

\displaystyle\rho^{SB}_{0}=\rho^{S}_{0}\otimes\pi^{B},

(199)

where
\pi^{B}:=\exp(-\beta H_{B})/Z_{B}
with
Z_{B}:=\text{Tr}[\exp(-\beta H_{B})]
. The state at time
t=\tau
is given by
\rho^{SB}_{\tau}=U_{SB}\rho^{SB}_{0}U_{SB}^{\dagger}
, where
U_{SB}=T\exp(-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int^{\tau}_{0}dtH_{SB}^{\rm tot})
is the unitary time-evolution operator from
t=0
to
t=\tau
.

We define bath heat current as the change in the bath energy:

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm bath}

\displaystyle=-\frac{d}{dt}\langle H_{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\text{Tr}[H_{B}[H_{SB}^{\rm tot},\rho^{SB}_{t}]]

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}[(H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}+H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}})\partial_{t}\rho_{t}^{SB}].

(200)

Then, the entropy production is defined as
14

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=\Delta S_{S}-\beta Q_{\rm bath}

\displaystyle=D(\rho^{SB}_{\tau}||\rho^{S}_{\tau}\otimes\pi^{B})\geq 0,

(201)

where
Q_{\rm bath}:=\int^{\tau}_{0}dt\dot{Q}_{\rm bath}
and
\Delta S_{S}
is the von Neumann entropy change of the system.

F.2
Markovian embedded model

The total Hamiltonian of the Markovian embedded model reads

\displaystyle H_{SA}^{\rm tot}=H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}+H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}}+H_{A},

(202)

and the initial state is given by

\displaystyle\rho^{SA}_{0}=\rho^{S}_{0}\otimes\pi^{A}.

(203)

The time-evolution equation is assumed to take the Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad (GKSL) form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{SA}_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{t}[\rho^{SA}_{t}]=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{SA}^{\rm tot},\rho^{SA}_{t}]+\mathcal{D}_{t}[\rho^{SA}_{t}],

(204)

and we assume that the steady-state is given by the thermal state
\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}}:=\exp(-\beta H_{SA}^{\rm tot})/Z_{SA}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}_{t}[\pi^{SA}_{\lambda_{t},g_{t}}]=0
.
Then, the Markovian entropy production defined for the embedded model reads

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}_{\rm emb}=\dot{S}_{SA}-\beta\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}\geq 0,

(205)

where

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}=\text{Tr}[H_{SA}^{\rm tot}\partial_{t}\rho^{SA}_{t}].

(206)

We now define the bath heat current for the Markovian embedded model as

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm bath}=\text{Tr}[(H_{S}^{\lambda_{t}}+H_{\rm int}^{g_{t}})\partial_{t}\rho^{SA}_{t}].

(207)

It should be noted that whether Eq. (
207
) reproduces the bath heat current defined for the original non-Markovian dynamics (
200
) requires careful analysis because of the dependence on the interaction Hamiltonian.

Using this expression (
207
), we obtain the relation between
\dot{Q}_{\rm bath}
and
\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}
:

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\rm bath}=\dot{Q}_{\rm emb}-\text{Tr}[H_{A}\partial_{t}\rho^{SA}_{t}].

(208)

We now use Eq. (
208
) and relate
\Sigma
and
\Sigma_{\rm emb}
as

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=\Delta S_{S}-\beta Q_{\rm bath}

\displaystyle=\Delta S_{S}-\beta Q_{\rm emb}-\int_{0}^{\tau}dt\text{Tr}[\partial_{t}\rho^{SA}_{t}\ln\pi^{A}]

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+\Delta S_{S}-\Delta S_{SA}-\text{Tr}[(\rho^{SA}_{\tau}-\rho^{SA}_{0})\ln\pi^{A}]

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+D(\rho^{SA}_{\tau}||\rho^{S}_{\tau}\otimes\pi^{A})-D(\rho^{SA}_{0}||\rho^{S}_{0}\otimes\pi^{A}),

(209)

where the last term vanishes using the initial condition (
203
), i.e.,
D(\rho^{SA}_{0}||\rho^{S}_{0}\otimes\pi^{A})=0
. Hence, we finally show

\displaystyle\Sigma=\Sigma_{\rm emb}+D(\rho^{SA}_{\tau}||\rho^{S}_{\tau}\otimes\pi^{A})\geq\Sigma_{\rm emb}.

(210)

Note that Eq. (
210
) also applies to classical case with initial factorized states, i.e.,
f_{0}^{SA}=f_{0}^{S}\pi^{A}
.
