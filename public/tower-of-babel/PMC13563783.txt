Predictive value of peripheral lymphocyte subsets in relation to opportunistic infections in people living with HIV/AIDS
Authors: Rui Yuan, Wenjia Hu, Qianhui Chen, Ke Zhuang, Fangzhou Jiao, Xien Gui, Yong Xiong, Rongrong Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOpportunistic infections (OIs) remain a major clinical challenge in people living with HIV/AIDS (PLWHA), and CD4⁺ T-cell counts alone may not fully reflect immune dysfunction. This study aimed to establish predictive cut-off values for lymphocyte subsets to improve the assessment of OIs risk in PLWHA.MethodsPeripheral blood samples were collected to assess lymphocyte subsets using flow cytometry between August 17, 2021, and April 19, 2023. Correlation analysis was performed to identify lymphocyte subset indicators strongly associated with CD4+ T-cell counts. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was then performed to determine cut-off values for predicting OIs. Finally, the performance of cut-off values was tested in hospitalized PLWHA.ResultsEven when CD4 cell counts were above 200 cells/µl or close to normal, patients with abnormal immunological phenotypes had a higher prevalence of OIs. Other lymphocyte subsets have a high positive correlation with CD4+ T-cell counts, with the exception of CD3+CD8+/CD3+, CD3+HLA-DR+/CD3+, and CD3+CD8+HLA-DR+/CD8+, which are strongly negatively correlated. The correlation coefficients of CD4+CD28+ cells were particularly close to 1.0. Furthermore, CD4+CD28+ T lymphocytes demonstrated an AUC of 0.819 with a cut-off of 129 cells/µl, second only to CD4⁺ T cells in terms of AUC. Furthermore, the AUCs for CD3+CD4+CD45RA+, CD3+CD4+CD45RO+, CD3+CD4+CD25+CD127low, CD45RA+CD3+CD4+CD25+CD127low, and CD45RO+CD3+CD4+CD25+CD127low T cells were all > 0.800, with sensitivities exceeding 90%.ConclusionIn addition to lymphocyte counts, abnormal lymphocyte phenotype and immune activation status are strongly associated with OIs. The proposed cut-off values for selected lymphocyte subsets may help identify hospitalized PLWHA at increased risk of OIs.Keywords: Lymphocyte subsets, PLWHA, Cut-off scores, Opportunistic infections, CD4+T lymphocytes

Introduction

Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (AIDS), caused by the Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), is an immunosuppressive disease that predisposes individuals to life-threatening opportunistic infections (OIs) and malignancies [1]. Globally, approximately 75% of people living with HIV/AIDS (PLWHA) are aware of their HIV-positive status, while in China this proportion is nearly 70%, reflecting gaps in HIV diagnosis coverage. An estimated 690,000 AIDS-related deaths occurred worldwide in 2019, and 1.7 million new HIV infections were reported in the same year [2]. In China, 962,809 cumulative AIDS cases and 316,477 AIDS-related deaths had been recorded by the end of 2019 [3]. Despite decreases in the prevalence of AIDS-defining OIs following the introduction of effective combination antiretroviral therapy (ART), OIs remain major causes of hospitalization and mortality among PLWHA in the United States. Global research also suggests that OIs significantly contribute to disease burden and AIDS-related mortality [4–6], highlighting the ongoing challenges in HIV prevention and control. Consequently, OIs associated with advanced immunosuppression have become a major focus of clinical diagnosis and management.

Given the challenges in obtaining timely etiological diagnoses and the frequent late presentation of PLWHA, prompt identification and treatment of OIs are crucial to prevent rapid clinical deterioration and improve survival. It has been reported that CD4+ T-lymphocytes are a crucial marker for assessing the health of AIDS patients and forecasting the progression of the disease [7]. However, it has been demonstrated that OIs are still occurring in patients who started ART and had relatively high or even normal CD4+ T cell levels in earlier cohorts [8, 9]. In addition to absolute CD4⁺ T-cell counts, alterations in T-cell differentiation status (naïve and memory subsets), costimulatory molecule expression (such as CD28), immune activation, and regulatory T-cell compartments may provide a more comprehensive assessment of immune competence in PLWHA and its association with OIs.

Chronic immune activation is a hallmark consequence of HIV infection. Even among individuals receiving sustained ART with undetectable plasma HIV RNA, residual immune activation may persist and has been associated with increased comorbidity and mortality. It has been well established that chronic immune activation in PLWHA is associated with a range of non-AIDS-related complications, including liver steatosis and cirrhosis, atherothrombotic disease, osteoporosis, and cognitive impairment. However, the relationship between chronic immune activation and AIDS-defining OIs remains poorly understood [10]. In this study, we aimed to investigate the association between immune activation-related immunological phenotypes and the occurrence of OIs in PLWHA. Although CD4⁺ T-cell count and HIV viral load remain central to the clinical monitoring of HIV infection, they may not fully reflect OI risk in patients with preserved CD4⁺ T-cell counts but aberrant lymphocyte phenotypes and persistent immune activation. Furthermore, the impact of immune activation levels on HIV-related opportunity infections is still being studied. In addition to immune activation, alterations in regulatory T-cell (Treg) subsets have also been implicated in HIV-associated immune dysregulation. In this study, it was discovered that HIV-related OIs might still arise in patients with CD4+ T-cell count higher than 200 cells/µl or even normal, and those with abnormal T cell immune phenotype and abnormal immunological activation were more likely to occur. This study examined immunological markers associated with an increased risk of OIs, based on T-cell related immune phenotypes and markers of immune activation. These findings may help inform the clinical assessment of immunological risk in PLWHA.

Materials and procedures

Patients

Two hundred and forty five patients were screened out as study subjects among all hospitalized PLWHA at Zhongnan Hospital of Wuhan University. Their demographic information and clinical data were collected. Two hundred patients were males and the other 45 patients were females. The prevalence of OIs was 63.7%, affecting 156 individuals. The study cohort comprised all PLWHA who were studied for lymphocyte subsets, detection and had well-documented records of OIs. A CD4+ T-cell count above 500 cells/mm³ is recognized in the medical literature as a benchmark for a healthy immune phenotype [11]. The inclusion criteria were: (1) confirmed HIV infection; (2) available lymphocyte phenotyping; (3) had complete clinical and laboratory data and maintained good compliance. The exclusion criteria were: (1) persons under the age of 18 or over the age of 70; (2) woman who is pregnant; (3) had other diseases that induce immune insufficiency besides HIV (such as aplastic anemia, leukemia, multiple myeloma, diabetes mellitus, nephrotic syndrome, Hepatic and renal failure, systemic lupus erythematosu, etc.); (4) had a malignant tumor or other underlying illness; (5) using immunosuppressant concurrently. Out of the 245 patients ultimately enrolled in our study, 156 patients (63.60%) presented with concurrent OIs, while 89 (36.30%) did not. Diagnostic criteria for OIs

OIs were diagnosed according to applicable clinical guidelines and routine hospital practice [12]. Diagnostic classification incorporated clinical manifestations, imaging findings, microbiological or pathological evidence when available, and attending physician assessment. The common OIs identified in this cohort included bacterial, viral or pneumocystis pneumonia, mycobacterium tuberculosis infection, bacillus marneffei, candidiasis, viral hepatitis, cryptococcus neoformans infection, and other parasitosis [13].

Sample collection and preparation

Blood samples were collected within one week of individuals being admitted to the hospital for HIV-related OIs or other HIV-related issues. With an EDTA-K2 anticoagulant vacuum tube, 3–5 ml of peripheral venous blood was obtained. The whole blood was reversed and mixed frequently to avoid blood clotting, and the test was completed within 24 h.

Flow cytometry analysis and antibody preparation

A BD FACSCanto™ flow cytometer was used in this study. Ten antibodies were divided into two panels for multicolor flow cytometry. Panel 1 included CD3 (APC-H7), CD4 (PerCP), CD45 (V500), CD28 (PE), HLA-DR (APC) and CD8 (PE-Cy7). Panel 2 included CD3 (APC-H7), CD4 (PerCP), CD45 (V500), CD25 (PE), CD127 (APC), CD45RO (BV421), and CD45RA (FITC). All antibodies were purchased from BD Biosciences. Note that CD28 and CD25 were both conjugated to PE, and HLA-DR and CD127 were both conjugated to APC, but they were assigned to different panels to avoid spectral overlap. For CD4⁺ T-cell enumeration, lymphocytes were identified and gated on CD3⁺ T cells, after which CD3⁺CD4⁺ T cells were defined in the CD3 versus CD4 dot plot (Fig. 1d).

Preparation and detection of whole blood specimens

Thirty and 35 µl of antibody were taken from the two flow-through tubes, respectively. We added 100 µl of whole blood, mixed the samples thoroughly, and incubated them for 15 min at room temperature (20–25 °C) in the dark. Next, we added 2 ml of FACS hemolysin, mixed the samples completely, and incubated them for an additional 15 min at room temperature (20–25 °C). We then centrifuged the samples at 1300 rpm for 5 min and carefully discarded the supernatant. After adding 2 ml of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), we mixed the samples well and centrifuged them again at 1300 rpm for 5 min before removing the supernatant. Finally, we added 200 µl of PBS and mixed thoroughly. A total of 1,000 cells were acquired for analysis using Multiset software.

Flow cytometry gating strategy

The detailed gating strategy is illustrated in Fig. 1 and Fig. 2. Briefly, lymphocytes were first identified based on forward- and side-scatter characteristics and CD45 expression, followed by gating of CD3⁺T cells. In tube 1, CD4⁺and CD8⁺T-cell subsets, as well as activated T cells (HLA-DR+) and CD28-expressing subsets, were subsequently defined. In tube 2, naïve (CD45RA+) and memory (CD45RO⁺) CD4⁺T-cell subsets, together with regulatory T cells (CD3⁺CD4⁺CD25⁺CD127low) and their corresponding fractions, were identified.

Definition

Abnormal lymphocyte phenotype

An abnormal lymphocyte phenotype was defined as any deviation in the frequency (%) or absolute count of prespecified T-cell subsets beyond their established reference intervals, as determined by standardized multiparameter flow cytometry. The immunophenotyping panel included CD3⁺CD4⁺ (Th) and CD3⁺CD8⁺ (Ts) T-cell populations; CD28-expressing T-cell subsets; CD45RA-defined naïve and CD45RO-defined memory CD4⁺ T-cell compartments; HLA-DR–expressing activated T cells; and regulatory T-cell (Treg) subsets identified as CD3⁺CD4⁺CD25⁺CD127low cells, including their CD45RA⁺ naïve and CD45RO⁺ memory fractions. CD3⁺CD4⁺CD28⁺ cells were gated within total CD4⁺ T cells and analyzed as a costimulatory subset rather than a differentiation-defined lineage, as CD28 expression may be retained in both CD45RA⁺ naïve and CD45RO⁺ memory CD4⁺ T cells. The reference intervals applied in this study were: CD3⁺CD4⁺CD28⁺/Th, 84.11–100.00%; CD3⁺CD4⁺CD28⁺/Ts, 48.04–77.14%; CD3⁺HLA-DR⁺ T cells, 9.04–25.62%; CD3⁺CD8⁺HLA-DR⁺/Ts, 20.73–60.23%; CD3⁺CD4⁺CD45RA⁺/Th, 27.29–57.07%; CD3⁺CD4⁺CD45RO⁺/Th, 44.44–68.94%; CD3⁺CD4⁺CD25⁺CD127low, 1.83–6.49%; CD45RA⁺CD3⁺CD4⁺CD25⁺CD127low cell, 1.44–3.26%; and CD45RO⁺CD3⁺CD4⁺CD25⁺CD127low cell, 0.27–4.55%. Values below the lower limit or above the upper limit of the corresponding reference interval were classified as abnormal.

CD45RA+CD3+CD4+CD25+CD127low cells

CD45RA⁺CD3⁺CD4⁺CD25⁺CD127low cells were defined in this study as peripheral quiescent regulatory T cells (Tregs). This phenotypically distinct subset is characterized by the surface expression of CD45RA on CD3⁺CD4⁺ T cells, together with high CD25 and low CD127 expression, a commonly accepted phenotypic signature used to identify human regulatory T cells in peripheral blood [14, 15].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS statistical software (version 25.0, Armonk, New York). All skewed variables were expressed through median (interquartile range). All nonskewed variables were expressed with means and standard deviations (SD). X2 analysis was conducted to examine the categorical variables. Box-and-scatter plots were generated in R (version 4.5.1) using ggplot2. Comparisons between the OIs and Non-OIs groups were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Spearman’s correlations were used to evaluate relationships between lymphocytes subsets and CD4+ T lymphocytes. The lymphocyte subset indicators with strong associations with CD4+ T-cell counts were subsequently entered into exploratory receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis to evaluate their performance. Optimal cut-off values were identified using the Youden index. For data results, Graphpad Prism 8.0 Statistical analysis was used and differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Results

Characteristics of the study population

A total of 245 hospitalized PLWHA were included in this study. Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of the cohort, including sex distribution, ART status, OIs, and CD4⁺ T-cell counts, are summarized in Table 1. Among these participants, 156 (63.6%) had documented OIs, whereas 89 (36.4%) did not have OIs, forming the basis for subsequent comparative analyses.

OIs occurrence in PLWHA with normal CD4+ T-lymphocytes and abnormal lymphocyte phenotypes

In this study, 36 PLWHA had CD4⁺ T-cell counts within the normal reference range (550–1440 cells/µl). Despite CD4⁺ T-cell recovery, a subset of these patients exhibited abnormal T-cell immunophenotypes and increased immune activation, as evidenced by elevated frequencies of CD3⁺HLA-DR⁺ and CD3⁺CD8⁺HLA-DR⁺ T cells (Table 2; Fig. 3). Despite the limited sample size, patients with elevated CD3⁺HLA-DR⁺ or CD3⁺CD8⁺HLA-DR⁺ counts showed a significantly increased risk of OIs (P < 0.05) compared with PLWHA who had normal CD4⁺ T-cell counts and no evidence of abnormal immune activation. Although the difference was not statistically significant, PLWHA with abnormal lymphocyte phenotypes tended to have a higher frequency of OIs than those with normal CD4⁺ T-cell counts and no evidence of immune activation.

OIs occurrence in PLWHA with CD4 counts at 200–500 cells/µl and abnormal lymphocyte phenotypes

In this study, a CD4+ T-lymphocyte count at 200–500 cells/µl was seen in 66 PLWHA. OIs occurred more frequently in patients with abnormal lymphocyte phenotypes (Table 3; Fig. 3). No significant differences were observed in CD4⁺CD28⁺ T-cell absolute counts or in the proportion of CD3⁺HLA-DR⁺ cells among CD3⁺ T cells (P ≥ 0.05). By contrast, PLWHA with OIs exhibited reduced absolute counts of both naïve and memory CD4⁺ T-cell counts, whereas their proportions showed opposite trends, with a decrease in naïve CD4⁺ T cells (CD3⁺CD4⁺CD45RA⁺) and an increase in memory CD4⁺ T cells (CD3⁺CD4⁺CD45RO⁺). Regulatory T-cell subsets were also significantly reduced, as reflected by lower frequencies and absolute counts of CD3⁺CD4⁺CD25⁺CD127low cells and CD45RO⁺CD3⁺CD4⁺CD25⁺CD127low cells. In parallel, significantly increased levels of activated T cells were observed, particularly CD3⁺HLA-DR⁺ and CD3⁺CD8⁺HLA-DR⁺ T cells (all P < 0.05). Overall, compared with patients without OIs, those with OIs showed a pattern characterized by reduced naïve, memory and regulatory T-cell subsets together with increased activated T-cell subsets.

OIs in patients with CD4 at 200–500 cells/µl and abnormal immunological phenotype

OIs occurrence in PLWHA with CD4 counts less then 200 cells/µl and abnormal lymphocyte phenotypes

As shown in Table 4; Figs. 3 and 143 PLWHA had CD4⁺ T-cell counts below 200 cells/µl. In this advanced stage of immunodeficiency, OIs were highly prevalent, affecting approximately 80% of patients. Compared with patients without OIs, those with OIs showed significantly altered lymphocyte phenotypes, including reduced proportions of CD28-expressing T cells (CD4⁺CD28⁺/CD4⁺ and CD8⁺CD28⁺/CD8⁺), a decreased proportion of naïve CD4⁺ T cells (CD3⁺CD4⁺CD45RA⁺/CD4⁺), and an increased proportion of memory CD4⁺ T cells (CD3⁺CD4⁺CD45RO⁺/CD4⁺). In contrast, immune activation was significantly increased in the OIs group compared with the non-OIs group, as indicated by elevated absolute counts of CD3⁺HLA-DR⁺ and CD3⁺CD8⁺HLA-DR⁺ T cells. Regulatory T-cell alterations were also observed, with reduced proportions of total Tregs (CD3⁺CD4⁺CD25⁺CD127low) and reduced counts and proportions of memory Tregs (CD45RO⁺CD3⁺CD4⁺CD25⁺CD127low) (all P < 0.05). Taken together, the OIs group showed lower levels of co-stimulatory and regulatory T-cell subsets, a decreased proportion of naïve CD4⁺ T cells, an increased proportion of memory CD4⁺ T cells, and higher levels of activated T-cell subsets than the non-OIs group.

Correlation between lymphocyte subsets and CD4+ T-lymphocytes

We next evaluated the correlation between each of the indices of lymphocyte subsets, which are not routinely used in clinical practice, and CD4+ T lymphocytes that are commonly monitored in PLWHA. As shown in Figs. 4, 5 and 6, our findings demonstrated that amongst lymphocyte subsets with a correlation coefficient ≥ 0.5, most were positively correlated with CD4+ T lymphocytes counts, with an exception of CD3+CD8+/CD3+(%), CD3+HLA-DR+/CD3(%) and CD3+CD8+HLA-DR+/CD8(%), which were negatively correlated. The correlation coefficients were 0.876 and 0.889 in CD4+/CD8+ ratio (Fig. 4d) and CD3+CD4+/CD3+ ratio (Fig. 4c), 0.787 and 0.786 in the number of lymphocyte counts and CD3+ T lymphocytes, respectively (Figs. 4a, b), as well as 0.614 and 0.523 in the number of CD19+ B lymphocytes and CD16+CD56+ NK lymphocytes (Figs. 4e, f). In contrast to CD8+CD28+ T cells (r = 0.696, P < 0.001, Fig. 5b) and CD8+CD28+/CD8+ ratio (r = 0.535, P < 0.001, Fig. 5c), the correlation index in the number of CD4+CD28+ T cells is relatively high (r = 0.990 P < 0.001, Fig. 5a). Similarly, both naïve and memory CD4⁺ T-cell subsets were significantly positively correlated with CD4+ cells, with an extremely high correlation index in the number of CD3+CD4+CD45RA+ cells (r = 0.928, P < 0.001) and CD3+CD4+CD45RO+ cells (r = 0.982, P < 0.001), which were shown in Fig. 5f and g. In contrast, activated T-cell subsets were inversely correlated with CD4 cells, merely with relatively low correlation indexes (Figs. 5d, e). Notably, all regulatory lymphocytes were positively correlated with CD4 cells, and the correlation indexes in the number of CD3+CD4+CD25+CD127low cells, CD45RA +CD3+CD4+CD25+CD127low cells and CD45RO+CD3+CD4 +CD25+CD127low cells were greater than 0.90 (Fig. 6a, c,e), whereas those for their proportions were 0.715, 0.747, 0.631, respectively (Fig. 6b, d,f).

Lymphocyte subsets cut-off values for OIs warning

Subsequently, we performed ROC analysis on lymphocyte subset indicators that were strongly correlated with CD4⁺ T-cell counts to determine the optimal cut-off values for predicting HIV-associated OIs. The area under the ROC curve (AUC), the optimal cut-off values, sensitivity, and specificity of each indicator are shown in Fig. 7. As expected, the absolute counts of CD3⁺CD4⁺ T cells showed the highest performance in diagnosing HIV-associated OIs (AUC = 0.820, 95%CI[0.766–0.874], P < 0.001). In contrast to these conventional lymphocytes (Fig. 7a, b), CD4⁺CD28⁺ T-cell absolute counts also showed a high AUC of 0.819 (95%CI[0.764–0.874], P < 0.001), with a cut-off of 129 cells/µl, a sensitivity of 71.6%, and a specificity of 87.6% (Fig. 7c), which is higher than the activate T-cell subsets (Fig. 7d). Furthermore, the CD3+CD4+CD45RA+, CD3+CD4+CD45RO+, CD3+CD4+CD25+CD127low, CD45RA+CD3+CD4+CD25+CD127low, and CD45RO+CD3+CD4+CD25+CD127low T lymphocytes all had AUCs greater than 0.800, indicating good diagnostic performance (Fig. 7e, f).

Validation of lymphocyte subsets cut-off values in predicting OIs

One hundred and fifty-six of the 245 PLWHA included in this study were infected with OIs. As shown in Table 5, the cut-off values identified above were used to categorize the lymphocyte subset indicators to further evaluate their clinical performance. After stratification on these cut-off values, the incidence of OIs differed significantly between the two groups (P < 0.05), with relatively high sensitivity and specificity. In particular, lymphocyte counts, CD4⁺CD28⁺ T-cell counts, and CD3⁺CD4⁺CD45RO⁺ T-cell counts showed sensitivities of ≥ 90%, second only to CD4⁺ T-cell counts. The predictive accuracy of lymphocyte counts < 835 cells/µl for OIs was 93.7% (89/95), which was slightly higher than that of CD3⁺CD4⁺ T-cell absolute counts ≤ 138 cells/µl (90.8%, 109/120). Moreover, 122 patients had CD4+CD28+ T cells ≤ 129 cells/µl, among whom the consistency rate was 91.0% (111/122). Similarly, the accuracy of CD3+CD4+CD45RO+ T cells and CD3+CD4+CD45RA+ T cells to predict OIs were 90.6% (106/117) and 86.5% (115/133), respectively.

Discussion

HIV infection causes progressive immune dysfunction and increases susceptibility to OIs and malignancies, with OIs remaining a leading cause of death in PLWHA [16]. The majority of PLWHA are diagnosed with HIV infection at hospital admission or during the initial clinical evaluation for OIs, resulting in delayed diagnosis and delayed treatment initiation. Therefore, prevention and management of OIs remain a major public health priority. However, routine surveillance data in China remain limited, and further evidence is needed to strengthen prevention and control efforts.

Absolute count of CD4⁺ T cells is inversely related to viral load [17], and a low CD4+ T-cell count is a major risk factor to OIs in PLWHA [18]. The detection of absolute CD4+ T-cell count is a standard metric for assessing the immunological state of PLWHA. Patients whose CD4+ T-cell counts below 200 cells/µl are given the highest priority by clinicians since it is well-established that they are more exposed to a variety of OIs. However, some patients with substantial CD4⁺ T-cell recovery can still develop OIs and experience some clinical adverse effects [19]. In this study, multiple individuals exhibited abnormal immune activation and lymphocyte phenotypes even when their CD4 counts were above 200 cells/µl or close to normal levels. These findings imply that, in addition to CD4+ T-cell counts, more comprehensive immunological markers are required to accurately assess immune status in PLWHA [20].

Previous studies in PLWHA have suggested that, beyond low CD4⁺ T-cell counts, persistent immune dysregulation reflected by lymphocyte-subset indices, particularly a low CD4/CD8 ratio and abnormal CD8⁺ T-cell recovery trajectories, may be associated with adverse clinical outcomes and increased mortality risk [21]. Yet few studies have systematically evaluated the relationship between multiple lymphocyte subsets and clinical OIs. In the present study, we evaluated the occurrence of OIs in PLWHA with CD4⁺ T-cell counts > 200 cells/µl and within the normal range.We found that OIs were associated with aberrant immune activation and T-cell dysfunction, even in patients with relatively preserved CD4⁺ T-cell counts. These findings indicate that comprehensive immunophenotyping may serve as a valuable complement to CD4⁺ T-cell monitoring in the early identification of OI risk.

Patients with CD4 counts < 200 cells/µl are at increased risk of developing OIs including tuberculosis, pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PJP) and cryptococcosis [22]. The risk of acquiring various types of OIs is determined by the level of CD4 cell count, which has been deemed the best predictor of OI risk and type so far [23]. Additionally, Evernden C and Chen Y-H et al. [24, 25]explicitly stated that having CD4+ T lymphocytes < 200 cells/µl increases the likelihood of developing PJP in HIV-positive or immunosuppressive individuals. Furthermore, Tang G et al. [21] discovered that CD3+ and CD8+ T-cell counts were associated with PJP risk, and CD3+ T-cell counts showed a higher AUC than CD4+ T-cell count, consistent with another study [26]. These findings highlight the importance of integrating multiple immunological indicators when assessing OIs risk in PLWHA.

Consistent with these findings, our research showed that when CD4 cell counts were above 200 cells/µl or near normal levels, patients with aberrant immune activation or lymphocyte dysfunction remained at increased risk of OIs. This finding suggests that lymphocyte subset alterations may provide complementary information for OI risk stratification beyond absolute CD4⁺ T-cell counts. Furthermore, correlation analysis demonstrated that most lymphocyte subsets were strongly associated with CD4⁺ T-cell counts, supporting their potential clinical relevance. Notably, CD4⁺CD28⁺ T cells and several regulatory T-cell subsets showed very high correlation coefficients, indicating that these markers may reflect overall immune competence.

To further evaluate the clinical utility of these indicators, we performed ROC analysis to determine optimal cut-off values for predicting OIs. In addition to CD3⁺CD4⁺ T-cell absolute counts, CD4⁺CD28⁺ T-cell counts demonstrated comparable diagnostic performance, with a high AUC and balanced sensitivity and specificity. Several other lymphocyte subsets, including naïve and memory CD4⁺ T-cell subsets and regulatory T cells, also showed good diagnostic performance (AUC > 0.800). These findings suggest that multiple lymphocyte subset indicators may serve as complementary biomarkers for predicting OIs in PLWHA.

To further validate the association between the cut-off values of these lymphocytes and HIV-related OIs, we categorized the above lymphocytes into two groups based on their cut-off values. Our results showed a marked difference in the incidence of OIs between those two groups, with a high sensitivity. And we also found that the Lymphocyte counts < 835 cells/µl, CD3+CD4+ T-cell counts ≤ 138 cells/µl, CD4+CD28+ T cells ≤ 129 cells/µl, CD3+CD4+CD45RO+ Tcells ≤ 31 cells/µl, CD3+CD4+CD45RA+ Tcells ≤ 103 cells/µl, CD3+CD4+CD25+CD127low ≤ 3 CD45RA+CD3+CD4+CD25+CD127low ≤ 1 cells/µl, CD45RO+CD3+CD4+CD25+CD127low AUCs ≤ 3 cells/µl all demonstrated sensitivities greater than 90%. Herein, with CD4+CD28+ T cells as an example, one hundred and eleven out of one hundred and twenty-two(111/122) PLWHA diagnosed as OIs will truly be infected.

Thus, if these lymphocyte subsets are used as predictive indicators, the proportion of individuals correctly classified as having OIs approaches 90%, indicating that these markers have good diagnostic performance in PLWHA. However, without external validation, these findings should be considered preliminary, and their diagnostic performance should be interpreted with caution.

Notably, these lymphocyte subset parameters showed relatively low specificity, generally around 60%, indicating a considerable risk of false-positive results when used to assess OIs. The absolute CD4⁺ T-cell count was a significant predictor of HIV-related infections, which is consistent with previous research [27]. Nevertheless, the cut-off value for CD4⁺ T-cell counts in our investigation differed slightly from prior results, which may be attributed to patient heterogeneity [28, 29]and the broad spectrum of OIs.

Chronic immune activation is a hallmark of HIV infection and is characterized by persistent activation of both CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells [30], commonly reflected by increased HLA-DR expression. Sustained cellular activation has been associated with immune dysfunction, cellular senescence, and accelerated immunological aging, contributing to the development of organ-related comorbidities [31].

Importantly, even in the context of effective ART and sustained viral suppression, HLA-DR expression often fails to normalize to levels observed in HIV-negative individuals [32], indicating persistent immune activation and chronic inflammation [33]. In the present study, immune activation was evaluated using HLA-DR expression on T-cell subsets. We found that elevated levels of HLA-DR–expressing T cells were associated with an increased risk of OIs, even among PLWHA with restored CD4⁺ T-cell counts. These findings underscore the clinical relevance of immune activation beyond CD4⁺ T-cell recovery alone.

CD28 is a crucial co-stimulatory molecule expressed on T cells, playing an essential role in T-cell activation, proliferation, survival, and differentiation [34]. The activation of CD28 can enhance the expression of cytokine genes like IL-2, which plays a critical role in regulating T-cell survival, proliferation, and differentiation [35]. It should be noted that CD4⁺CD28⁺ T cells do not represent a distinct differentiation stage, but rather comprise both naïve (CD45RA⁺) and central memory (CD45RO⁺) CD4 T cells that retain CD28 expression. Therefore, in this study, CD4⁺CD28⁺ T cells were analyzed as a functional marker of preserved co-stimulatory capacity, while differentiation-defined subsets were assessed separately.

Reduced CD28 expression in PLWHA is associated with immune decline, telomere shortening, and T cell exhaustion, akin to aging-related senescence [36]. Furthermore, the loss of CD28 expression partially reflects the infection burden of latent viruses such as CMV and EBV, leading to disease worsening [37]. ART reduce the percentage of T cells that lack CD28 expression, although CD28(-) T cell levels persist after ART [38], suggesting that ART may not fully restore CD28 expression levels. PLWHA may remain susceptible to OIs despite ART if CD28 expression remains low. As HIV progresses, CD28 expression on CD4+ and CD8+ T cells declines, indicating worsening cellular immune dysfunction [39]. Previous studies have shown that the absolute counts of CD4⁺CD28⁺ T cells are associated with the immune status and disease progression of HIV-infected individuals [36]. A decrease in these cells may indicate an increased risk of infection, highlighting CD28 expression as a potential biomarker for OIs in PLWHA. In our study, we found that CD4⁺CD28⁺ T-cell counts were strongly correlated with CD4⁺ T cells and showed potential for identifying HIV-associated OIs with a sensitivity of 91%, demonstrating higher predictive performance than CD8⁺CD28⁺ T cells. Overall, our findings highlight the critical role of CD28 in HIV disease progression and its potential value in assessing immune status and predicting infection risks in PLWHA. Further large-scale studies are necessary to confirm its prognostic value in HIV-associated complications.

CD4+ T lymphocytes comprise distinct naïve and memory subsets with different functional properties and surface markers [40]. The baseline percentage of naïve CD4+ T lymphocytes is predictive of short-term immune reconstitution [41]and may also serve as a reliable predictor of long-term immune reconstitution [42]. Pre-ART depletion of naïve CD4⁺ T cells can result in impaired immune reconstitution and a poor prognosis. Veronica Bordoni [43] discovered that RO+CD4+ T cells are positively associated with favorable immunological parameters and negatively associated with markers of disease progression after HIV infection. Greater depletion of naïve CD4⁺ T cells has been associated with increased mortality risk in advanced HIV infection [44]. Studies by Timothy W Schacker et al. [45] indicate that a higher percentage of naïve CD4+ T cells before ART in patients with CD4+ T-cell counts of 200–500 cells/µl is associated with a lower risk of immune reconstitution failure, OIs and all-cause mortality. Furthermore, the proportion of naïve CD4⁺ T cells is a better predictor of CD4⁺ T-cell recovery compared with baseline CD4⁺ T-cell counts or HIV RNA levels [45]. These finding may help explain why, despite higher CD4⁺ T-cell counts, some patients still exhibit poor immune recovery and may help identify individuals more likely to achieve effective immune reconstitution after delayed ART initiation.

Depletion of CD4⁺ T cells is associated with increased activation of CD8⁺ T cells and an expansion of memory CD8⁺ T cells, which are linked to HIV disease progression and mortality [46]. Protective immunity is strengthened by memory T lymphocytes, which react to antigen re-exposure more rapidly and effectively than naïve cells. The proportion of memory CD8⁺ T cells in untreated HIV patients are inversely correlated with viral load and T cell activation, indicating that higher levels of these cells are associated with a more favorable prognosis [47]. Although the exact mechanisms by which memory CD8+ T cells modulate immune activation remain unclear, they may involve the maintenance of antigen-specific memory T-cell precursors or preservation of intestinal barrier integrity [47]. Premature loss of memory CD4+ T cells is associated with a higher risk of disease progression. Following HIV infection, expansion of this population correlates with tthe burden of infected CD4⁺ T cells and may contribute to the establishment of viral reservoirs.

Our research shows that when CD4+ T lymphocytes are abnormal, both the ratio and absolute count of naïve and memory T lymphocytes decrease markedly, which raises the risk of OIs. Furthermore, both naïve and memory T lymphocytes are positively correlated with CD4+ T-cell counts and demonstrate promising clinical performance in predicting HIV-associated OIs and immune reconstitution. In conclusion, we propose that naïve and memory CD4⁺ T-cell subsets may serve as useful biomarkers for predicting and monitoring immune status, thereby facilitating improved clinical management.

CD4+ regulatory T cells (Tregs) maintain self-tolerance and immune homeostasis by suppressing autoreactive T cells and limiting excessive immune activation [48]. In HIV infection, Tregs play a dual role, as they help control inflammation and viral spread, while potentially impairing viral clearance and promoting viral reservoir formation. Ana Horta et al. [49] reported a significant association between the proportion of Tregs and HIV disease progression, with PLWHA exhibiting a higher proportion of Tregs than healthy controls. These findings suggest that an increased Treg proportion is associated with impaired CD4⁺ T-cell reconstitution. Additionally, previous studies have indicated that Tregs may contribute to the persistence of chronic viral infections [50–52]. Reducing the percentage of Tregs in advanced HIV infection has been proposed as a potential strategy to decrease the risk of CMV-related end-stage illnesses and other OIs [52].

HIV disease progression has been linked to increased Treg frequencies in lymphoid tissues [53], whereas heightened immune activation is linked to reduced circulating Tregs [54]. This implies that in individuals initiating HAART, Tregs may suppress HIV-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell responses, potentially leading to poorer clinical outcomes [55, 56]. Furthermore, CD4⁺CD25⁺ Tregs have been shown to inhibit tumor-specific CD4⁺ T-cell responses in melanoma patients [57], while CD25+ T lymphocytes depletion enhances virus-specific CD4⁺ and/or CD8⁺ T-cell responses. In line with previous findings, increased Treg frequency in HPV-positive invasive tumors has been associated with reduced efficacy of anti-tumor immune therapies.

Our research, excluding immunocompromised individuals, shows a robust relationship between CD4+ T cells and regulatory T cells in PLWHA receiving effective ART. Abnormal CD4+ T cells are associated with lower Treg levels despite normal ART, suggesting a persistent risk of immune activation–related OIs. This highlights the need for continuous monitoring and timely clinical intervention in PLWHA, regardless of CD4⁺ T-cell counts.

Collectively, comprehensive assessment of lymphocyte subsets is essential for accurately evaluating immune status in PLWHA, as a single subset alone may not adequately reflect overall immune function. Therefore, regular and comprehensive monitoring of lymphocyte subsets is warranted in PLWHA to track disease progression, evaluate the effectiveness of ART, anticipate potential complications, and guide individualized clinical management.

The purpose of this study was to provide a preliminary evaluation of the clinical significance of lymphocyte subsets in identifying OIs. Further exploration are warranted to elucidate the underlying mechanisms of regulatory lymphocyte alterations across different stages of HIV infection. Notably, the lymphocyte subset abnormalities predictive of OIs in our study—specifically, decreased CD4+CD28+ T cell counts (AUC = 0.819) and increased levels of activated CD3+HLA-DR+ T cells (P < 0.001)—likely reflect not only immune deficiency but also the in vivo effects of chronic antigenic stimulation driven by OIs. This notion is mechanistically grounded in established research on similar contexts, such as immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome(IRIS) [58] and chronic co-infections like Cytomegalovirus(CMV) [59], in which persistent antigenic stimulation drives T-cell exhaustion and dysregulated differentiation. Thus, early detection of these alterations may facilitate timely identification of OIs and help mitigate pathogenic immune activation, thereby preserving residual immune function and improving long-term clinical outcomes.

Overall, this study had several noteworthy findings. Firstly, the results in this study proved that even when CD4+ T-cell counts were above 200 cells/µl or close to normal, patients with abnormal immune activation or lymphocyte dysfunction exhibited a greater prevalence of OIs. Then, this study provides evidence that both aberrant immune activation and lymphocyte dysfunction indicators are linked to CD4+ T-cell counts. Furthermore, we proposed cut-off values for selected immune activation and lymphocyte dysfunction indicators to estimate the risk of OIs, which may be useful in guiding clinical risk assessment and management of HIV-associated opportunistic infections. However, clinical evaluation and etiological diagnosis remain the gold standard for OI identification, with immunological markers serving as complementary tools for supportive assessment and early risk stratification.

Despite potential clinical implications, several limitations should be acknowledged. Firstly, as a single-center retrospective study, certain selection bias is inevitable. Secondly, the limited sample size and clinical heterogeneity of OIs limited our ability to examine the associations between specific lymphocyte subsets and individual OIs. Furthermore, potential confounders including ART status, HIV viral load, and duration of HIV infection, were not fully controlled, which may have influenced the observed associations and warrants cautious interpretation of the results. Thirdly, the cut-off values identified in this study require further validation in larger, multicenter cohorts of PLWHA.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that peripheral lymphocyte subset profiling may more accurately reflect overall immune status in PLWHA beyond conventional CD4+ T-cell monitoring. Even when CD4+T lymphocytes are above 200 cells/µl or within the normal reference range, patients with immune dysfunction or aberrant immune activation have a higher likelihood of OIs. Several lymphocyte subsets indicators closely associated to CD4+ T-cell counts showed potential to identify patients of OIs. These findings remain preliminary, however, and require validation in prospective multicenter studies to confirm their clinical applicability.
