The triality of the twisted discrete trace formula for PGSO ⁡ ( 8 ) \operatorname{PGSO}(8)
Tuoping Du, Zhifeng Peng*, Haoyang Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish the triality twisted trace formula for PGSO ⁡ ( 8 ) \operatorname{PGSO}(8) , including its discrete part, and obtain a coarse classification of its automorphic representations by combining the properties of triality. By comparing the standard trace formula for G 2 G_{2} with the triality twisted trace formula for PGSO ⁡ ( 8 ) \operatorname{PGSO}(8) , we derive a corresponding coarse classification for automorphic representations of G 2 G_{2} . Specifically, we construct the triality-twisted elliptic endoscopic data for PGSO ⁡ ( 8 ) \operatorname{PGSO}(8) and the elliptic endoscopic data for G 2 G_{2} . Based on these constructions and the general framework of trace formulas, we establish the relevant trace formulas. Utilizing the triality property of PGSO ⁡ ( 8 ) \operatorname{PGSO}(8) , we obtain a coarse classification of its automorphic representations, which in turn yields a coarse classification for those of G 2 G_{2} .

1.
Introduction

The trace formula is a fundamental tool in the study of automorphic representations of connected reductive groups. Arthur
[
3
]
developed the standard trace formula and applied it to classify automorphic representations of classical groups. His approach is based on the theory of endoscopy in both the standard and twisted case, comparing the trace formula for classical groups with the twisted trace formula for general linear groups. This method, however, relies on the assumption that the twisted trace formula for general linear groups is stabilized. In 2014, Moeglin and Waldspurger
[
22
,
23
]
established the stabilization of the twisted trace formula for reductive groups. Subsequently, in 2015, Mok
[
15
]
obtained analogous results for quasi-split unitary groups using Arthur’s techniques.

In this work, we apply the triality twisted trace formula for
\mathrm{PGSO}(8)
to investigate the classification of automorphic representations of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
, by comparing the trace formula for
\mathrm{G}_{2}
with the triality twisted trace formula for
\mathrm{PGSO}(8)
.

The Lie algebra of type
D_{4}
exhibits richer symmetry than generic type
D_{n}
Lie algebras. In Lie theory, the outer automorphism group
\mathrm{Out}(D_{4})
is isomorphic to the symmetric group
S_{3}
, whereas for
n\neq 4
,

\mathrm{Out}(D_{n})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

Hence, the Lie algebra of type
D_{4}
admits an outer automorphism
\theta
of order three, known as
triality
. At the group level, the outer automorphism group of
\operatorname{SO}(8)
is
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, where the nontrivial outer automorphism is realized by an element in
\mathrm{O}(8)\setminus\operatorname{SO}(8)
. Consequently, the triality automorphism
\theta
of
\mathfrak{so}(8)
does not lift to
\operatorname{SO}(8)
, but does lift to the simply connected group
\operatorname{Spin}(8)
and to the adjoint group
\mathrm{PGSO}(8)
. The center of
\operatorname{Spin}(8)
is the finite group scheme
\mu_{2}\times\mu_{2}
, which contains three nontrivial subgroups of order two. The triality automorphism permutes these three subgroups while preserving the center, and therefore descends to an automorphism of the adjoint group
\mathrm{PGSO}(8)
.

The complex group
\operatorname{Spin}(8)
, which is the dual group of
\mathrm{PGSO}(8)
, admits three non-isomorphic
8
-dimensional irreducible orthogonal representations: the standard representation

\rho:\operatorname{Spin}(8)\to\operatorname{SO}(8),

and two half-spin representations

\rho^{+}:\operatorname{Spin}(8)\to\operatorname{GL}({\textstyle\bigwedge}^{+}W),\quad\rho^{-}:\operatorname{Spin}(8)\to\operatorname{GL}({\textstyle\bigwedge}^{-}W),

which are cyclically permuted by the triality automorphism. Then they define an embedding

i=(\rho,\rho^{+},\rho^{-}):\operatorname{Spin}(8)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{SO}(8)^{3}.

This embedding is equivariant with respect to an action of
S_{3}
on
\operatorname{Spin}(8)
, which permutes the isomorphism classes of the three representations. In fact, this action is induced by the permutation action on
\operatorname{SO}(8)^{3}
. Correspondingly, there exist three non-conjugate homomorphisms

\lambda_{i}:\operatorname{SO}(8)\to\mathrm{PGSO}(8),\quad i=1,2,3,

which are also cyclically permuted by the triality automorphism. The existence of these three distinct maps
\lambda_{i}
is a direct consequence of the triality symmetry.

If
F
is a local field, there are three distinct homomorphisms

\lambda_{i}:\operatorname{SO}(8)(F)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{SO}(8)(F)/\mu_{2}(F)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{PGSO}(8)(F)

with finite cokernel isomorphic to

, such that the images of any two

generate
\mathrm{PGSO}(8)(F)

yields complementary information about the representation theory.

The exceptional group

appears as the stabilizer of the triality action on
\mathrm{PGSO}(8)
. This motivates our study of the triality twisted trace formula for
\mathrm{PGSO}(8)
, whose twisted elliptic endoscopic groups include
G_{2}
.

2.
Triality

2.1.
Octonion algebra.

Let
F
be a field and
\mathbb{O}
an octonion algebra over
F
. Then
\mathbb{O}
is an
8
-dimensional non-associative and non-commutative composition algebra with multiplicative identity, equipped with an involution
x\mapsto\bar{x}
such that the quadratic form
N(x)=x\cdot\bar{x}
satisfies

N(x\cdot y)=N(x)\cdot N(y).

Let
b_{N}(x,y)=N(x+y)-N(x)-N(y)
be the associated non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form, and define the linear trace map by
\operatorname{tr}(x)=x+\bar{x}
. The octonion algebra may be realized via the Zorn model

\mathbb{O}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&v\\
w^{\ast}&b\end{pmatrix}:a,b\in F,\;v\in V,\;w^{\ast}\in V^{\ast}\right\},

where
V
is a
3
-dimensional
F
-vector space and
V^{\ast}
is its dual. The isomorphism

\textstyle\bigwedge^{2}V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}V^{\ast},\qquad x\wedge y\longmapsto\bigl(v\mapsto x\wedge y\wedge v\bigr),

where
x,y,v\in V
and
x\wedge y\wedge v\in\bigwedge^{3}V\cong F
, together with the analogous identification

\textstyle\bigwedge^{2}V^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}V,

endows
\mathbb{O}
with the multiplication

\begin{pmatrix}a&v\\
w^{\ast}&b\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}c&x\\
y^{\ast}&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}ac+\langle y^{\ast},v\rangle&ax+dv+w^{\ast}\wedge y^{\ast}\\
cw^{\ast}+by^{\ast}+v\wedge x&bd+\langle w^{\ast},x\rangle\end{pmatrix}.

The algebra
\mathbb{O}
is equipped with an anti-involution

\overline{\begin{pmatrix}a&v\\
w^{\ast}&b\end{pmatrix}}=\begin{pmatrix}b&-v\\
-w^{\ast}&a\end{pmatrix},

a norm form
N:\mathbb{O}\to F
given by

N\!\begin{pmatrix}a&v\\
w^{\ast}&b\end{pmatrix}=ab-\langle w^{\ast},v\rangle,

and a linear trace
\operatorname{tr}:\mathbb{O}\to F
defined by

\operatorname{tr}\!\begin{pmatrix}a&v\\
w^{\ast}&b\end{pmatrix}=a+b.

Although the multiplication in
\mathbb{O}
is not associative, the trilinear form
\operatorname{tr}(xyz)
is well-defined and satisfies

\operatorname{tr}(xyz)=\operatorname{tr}(yzx)=\operatorname{tr}(zxy),

owing to the cyclic symmetry and the trace identity

\operatorname{tr}(xy)=\operatorname{tr}(yx),

for all
x,y,z\in\mathbb{O}
. We denote by
\mathbb{O}_{0}
the
7
-dimensional subspace of trace-zero elements in
\mathbb{O}
.

The automorphism group of
\mathbb{O}
over
F
is isomorphic to the exceptional group
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)
. It is a split simple linear algebraic group of rank
2
that is both simply connected and adjoint. Its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{2}=\operatorname{Lie}(\mathrm{G}_{2})
is identified with
\operatorname{Der}(\mathbb{O})
, the derivation algebra of
\mathbb{O}
, and is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{O}))
. Over
\mathbb{R}
, there are two real forms of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
: the non-compact split form described above, and a compact form associated with a division algebra
[
16
]
.

We record the dimensions

\dim_{F}(\mathbb{O})=8,\qquad\dim_{F}(\mathrm{G}_{2})=14.

Root system

2.3.
Clifford algebra,

Let
V
be a vector space over a field
F

Definition 2.3.1
.

A
Clifford algebra

C(V,q)
is an
F
-algebra equipped with an
F
-linear map
J:V\to C(V,q)
satisfying the following:

•

J(v)^{2}=q(v)\cdot 1
for all
v\in V

•

(Universal Property) For any
F
-algebra
A
and any
F
-linear map
\phi:V\to A
such that
\phi(v)^{2}=q(v)\cdot 1_{A}
for all
v\in V
, there exists a unique
F
-algebra homomorphism
\psi:C(V,q)\to A
with
\phi=\psi\circ J
.

The Clifford algebra
C(V,q)
exists for any quadratic form
q:V\to F
and can be constructed as the quotient of the tensor algebra

T(V)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}V^{\otimes k}

by the two-sided ideal generated by all elements of the form
v\otimes v-q(v)\cdot 1
for
v\in V
.

Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
be an orthogonal basis of
V
with respect to the bilinear form associated to
q
, and set
\alpha_{i}=q(e_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,n
. If
\phi:V\to A
is an
F
-linear map into an
F
-algebra
A
, then both the expression

\phi(v)\phi(w)+\phi(w)\phi(v)

and the associated bilinear form
b_{q}(v,w)=q(v+w)-q(v)-q(w)
are symmetric and bilinear. Consequently,

\phi(v)\phi(w)+\phi(w)\phi(v)=b_{q}(v,w)\cdot 1_{A}

holds for all
v,w\in V
if and only if it holds for all pairs of basis vectors
e_{i},e_{j}
. This yields the following description.

Proposition 2.3.2
.

The Clifford algebra
C(V,q)
is the algebra generated by
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
subject to the relations:

\displaystyle e_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}\quad\text{for all }i,

\displaystyle e_{i}e_{j}+e_{j}e_{i}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for all }i\neq j.

If
A
is an
F
-algebra of dimension
2^{n}
as a vector space, with elements
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
satisfying
u_{i}^{2}=\alpha_{i}
and
u_{i}u_{j}+u_{j}u_{i}=0

i\neq j

F
-algebra homomorphism

sending

is an isomorphism.

When
q(v)=0

, we have
C(V,q)\cong\bigwedge^{\bullet}V
, where
J:V\hookrightarrow\bigwedge^{\bullet}V
is the natural embedding. The exterior algebra
\bigwedge^{\bullet}V
is defined as the direct sum

\bigwedge\nolimits^{\bullet}V=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\bigwedge\nolimits^{k}V,

equipped with the structure of an associative, graded-commutative

Let
\bigwedge^{+}V
and
\bigwedge^{-}V
denote the even and odd parts of

\bigwedge\nolimits^{+}V=\bigoplus_{k}\bigwedge\nolimits^{2k}V,\qquad\bigwedge\nolimits^{-}V=\bigoplus_{k}\bigwedge\nolimits^{2k+1}V.

Now assume
F=\mathbb{C}
and let
q
be the non-degenerate quadratic form
q(x)=-(x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n}^{2})
on

. Set

and choose totally isotropic subspaces

of dimension

. Explicit bases for
W
and

are given by

where

\overline{x}=\iota(x)^{t}
and
\iota:\bigwedge^{\bullet}W\to\bigwedge^{\bullet}W
is the unique
\mathbb{C}
-linear map extending
w\mapsto w
on
W
. The two pairings are related by

\overline{\mathcal{N}}(x,y)=\mathcal{N}(\iota(x),y).

When
m=4
, both
\mathcal{N}
and
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
are non-degenerate and symmetric.

2.4.
Spin groups

In this subsection, let
F
be either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, and let
V
denote
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{C}^{n}
equipped with the quadratic form
q(x)=-\|x\|^{2}
.

Definition 2.4.1
.

The
pin group

\operatorname{Pin}_{n}
is the set of elements
v\in C(V,q)
satisfying:

•

v
is homogeneous (either even or odd in the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-grading);

•

v\overline{v}=1
;

•

for all
u\in V
, the conjugate
vu\overline{v}
lies in
V
.

The
spin group

\operatorname{Spin}_{n}
is the subgroup of even elements in
\operatorname{Pin}_{n}
.

Here, the conjugation
v\mapsto\overline{v}
is the anti-automorphism of
\bigwedge^{\bullet}V
defined by

\overline{v}=\iota(v)^{t}=\iota(v^{t}).

Thus
\operatorname{Pin}_{n}
and
\operatorname{Spin}_{n}
are subgroups of the group of units in
C(V,q)
.

For each
x\in\operatorname{Pin}_{n}
, define a linear map
\rho(x):V\to V
by

\rho(x)v=\iota(x)v\overline{x},\qquad v\in V.

Then
\rho(x)
is orthogonal, since for any
v\in V
,

\displaystyle\|\rho(x)v\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-q(\rho(x)v)=-(\rho(x)v)^{2}

\displaystyle=-(\iota(x)v\overline{x})^{2}=-\iota(x)v\overline{x}\cdot\iota(x)v\overline{x}

\displaystyle=-\iota(x)v(\overline{x}\iota(x))v\overline{x}=-\iota(x)v(x\overline{x})^{-1}v\overline{x}

\displaystyle=-\iota(x)v^{2}\overline{x}=q(v)\iota(x)\overline{x}=\|v\|^{2}.

This defines a homomorphism
\rho:\operatorname{Pin}_{n}\to\mathrm{O}_{n}
, which is surjective with kernel
\{\pm 1\}
. In particular,
\operatorname{Spin}_{n}
is an index-
2
subgroup of
\operatorname{Pin}_{n}
, and the restriction
\rho:\operatorname{Spin}_{n}\to\mathrm{SO}_{n}
is also surjective with kernel
\{\pm 1\}
.

The group
\operatorname{Spin}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is contained in the group of units of
C^{+}(\mathbb{C}^{n},q)
. Hence every
C^{+}(\mathbb{C}^{n},q)
-module induces a representation of
\operatorname{Spin}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, and likewise of
\operatorname{Spin}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. The spinor space
\bigwedge^{\bullet}W
carries a natural
C^{+}(\mathbb{C}^{n},q)
-module structure. When
n
is even, it decomposes as the direct sum of two half-spin representations
\bigwedge^{+}W
and
\bigwedge^{-}W
, both of which are irreducible. Denote these representations by

\rho^{+}:\operatorname{Spin}_{n}\to\operatorname{End}({\textstyle\bigwedge^{+}}W),\qquad\rho^{-}:\operatorname{Spin}_{n}\to\operatorname{End}({\textstyle\bigwedge^{-}}W).

Since
\operatorname{Spin}_{n}
is contained in the group of units of

C^{+}(\mathbb{C}^{n},q)\cong\operatorname{End}({\textstyle\bigwedge^{+}}W)\times\operatorname{End}({\textstyle\bigwedge^{-}}W),

which is isomorphic to
\operatorname{GL}(\bigwedge^{+}W)\times\operatorname{GL}(\bigwedge^{-}W)
, it follows that
\rho^{+}
and
\rho^{-}
factor through
\operatorname{SL}(\bigwedge^{+}W)
and
\operatorname{SL}(\bigwedge^{-}W)
, respectively. For
n\geq 3
, the commutator subgroup of
\operatorname{Spin}_{n}
is
\operatorname{Spin}_{n}
itself.

In particular, the two half-spin representations of
\operatorname{Spin}(8)
yield an embedding

\operatorname{Spin}_{8}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{SO}(8,\mathbb{C})\times\operatorname{SO}(8,\mathbb{C}),

as the bilinear forms
\mathcal{N}
and
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
are invariant under the action of
\operatorname{Spin}_{8}
.

2.5.
Triality

We take the base field
F=\mathbb{C}
and write
\operatorname{Spin}(n)
and
\operatorname{SO}(n)
for
\operatorname{Spin}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
\operatorname{SO}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, respectively. The two half-spin modules
\bigwedge^{+}W
and
\bigwedge^{-}W
are denoted by
S^{+}
and
S^{-}
.

Consider three non-degenerate quadratic spaces
(V_{1},q_{1})
,
(V_{2},q_{2})
, and
(V_{3},q_{3})
. A bilinear map
f:V_{1}\times V_{2}\to V_{3}
is called
orthogonal
if

q_{3}(f(v_{1},v_{2}))=q_{1}(v_{1})q_{2}(v_{2})

for all
v_{1}\in V_{1}
and
v_{2}\in V_{2}
. A linear map
f_{v}:V_{1}\to V_{2}
is called
isometric
if
q_{2}(f_{v}(v_{1}))=q_{1}(v_{1})
for all
v_{1}\in V_{1}
.

A trilinear form
\mathcal{T}:V_{1}\times V_{2}\times V_{3}\to\mathbb{C}
induces a bilinear map
t_{3}:V_{1}\times V_{2}\to V_{3}
defined by

q_{3}(t_{3}(v_{1},v_{2}),v_{3})=\mathcal{T}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}).

This gives an isomorphism between the space of trilinear forms on
V_{1}\times V_{2}\times V_{3}
and the space of bilinear maps from
V_{1}\times V_{2}
to
V_{3}
. Similarly, define
t_{3}^{\prime}:V_{2}\times V_{1}\to V_{3}
by

q_{3}(t_{3}^{\prime}(v_{2},v_{1}),v_{3})=\mathcal{T}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}).

Since
q_{3}
is non-degenerate, we have
t_{3}(v_{1},v_{2})=t_{3}^{\prime}(v_{2},v_{1})
. In the same way, define bilinear maps

\displaystyle t_{1},t_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:V_{2}\times V_{3}\to V_{1},

\displaystyle t_{2},t_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:V_{1}\times V_{3}\to V_{2},

by the relations

q_{1}(t_{1}(v_{2},v_{3}),v_{1})=\mathcal{T}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})=q_{1}(t_{1}^{\prime}(v_{3},v_{2}),v_{1}),

q_{2}(t_{2}(v_{1},v_{3}),v_{2})=\mathcal{T}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})=q_{2}(t_{2}^{\prime}(v_{3},v_{1}),v_{2}).

Then
t_{1}(v_{2},v_{3})=t_{1}^{\prime}(v_{3},v_{2})
and
t_{2}(v_{1},v_{3})=t_{2}^{\prime}(v_{3},v_{1})
.

Definition 2.5.1
.

A
triality
is a quadruple
(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},\mathcal{T})
, where
(V_{1},q_{1})
,
(V_{2},q_{2})
,
(V_{3},q_{3})
are non-degenerate quadratic spaces of positive dimension, and
\mathcal{T}:V_{1}\times V_{2}\times V_{3}\to\mathbb{C}
is a trilinear form such that each of the induced bilinear maps
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}
is orthogonal.

Remark 2.5.2
.

The orthogonality of each
t_{i}
(
i=1,2,3
) forces
\dim V_{1}=\dim V_{2}=\dim V_{3}
. In fact, it suffices that only one of the
t_{i}
be orthogonal. Trialities are exceedingly rare; the
dimension of a triality
is the common dimension of the
V_{i}
.

Theorem 2.5.3

(Hurwitz)
.

A triality can exist only in dimensions
1
,
2
,
4
, and
8
.

Proof.

See
[
2
, Theorem 10.8]
.
∎

Consider
V_{1}=\mathbb{C}^{8}
,
V_{2}=S^{+}
,
V_{3}=S^{-}
, where the quadratic forms are given by
q(x)=-(x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{8}^{2})
for
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{8})\in\mathbb{C}^{8}
, and
\mathcal{N}
restricted to
S^{+}
and
S^{-}
, respectively. Define the bilinear map
t_{3}:\mathbb{C}^{8}\times S^{+}\to S^{-}
via the Clifford module structure

t_{3}(v,x)=v\cdot x.

Note that
v\cdot x\neq x\cdot v
in general, but we may define an orthogonal bilinear map
t_{3}^{\prime}(x,v)=v\cdot x=t_{3}(v,x)
. By
[
2
, Lemma 9.21]
, we have

\mathcal{N}(v\cdot x)=q(v)\mathcal{N}(x)\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathbb{C}^{8},\ x\in S^{+},

so
t_{3}
is orthogonal. Define the trilinear form
\mathcal{T}:\mathbb{C}^{8}\times S^{+}\times S^{-}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\mathcal{T}(v,x,y)=\mathcal{N}(t_{3}(v,x),y).

This induces two additional bilinear maps

\displaystyle t_{1}

\displaystyle:S^{+}\times S^{-}\to\mathbb{C}^{8},

\displaystyle t_{2}

\displaystyle:\mathbb{C}^{8}\times S^{-}\to S^{+},

satisfying

q(v,t_{1}(x,y))=\mathcal{T}(v,x,y)=\mathcal{N}(t_{2}(v,y),x),

for all
v\in\mathbb{C}^{8}
,
x\in S^{+}
,
y\in S^{-}
. We also set
t_{1}(x,y)=t_{1}^{\prime}(y,x)
and
t_{2}(v,y)=t_{2}^{\prime}(y,v)
.

We claim that
(\mathbb{C}^{8},S^{+},S^{-},\mathcal{T})
is a triality. From
[
2
, Lemma 9.21]
, we have
\mathcal{N}(v\cdot x,y)=\mathcal{N}(x,v\cdot y)
, so by non-degeneracy of
\mathcal{N}
,

t_{2}(v,y)=v\cdot y,

which is orthogonal. To show
t_{1}
is orthogonal, suppose
\mathcal{N}(x)\neq 0
and define

f_{x}:S^{-}\to\mathbb{C}^{8},\quad f_{x}(y)=t_{1}(x,y),\qquad g_{x}:\mathbb{C}^{8}\to S^{-},\quad g_{x}(v)=v\cdot x.

Then
\mathcal{N}(g_{x}(v))=q(v)\mathcal{N}(x)
for all
v\in\mathbb{C}^{8}
. For any
v,v_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{8}
,

\displaystyle q(f_{x}g_{x}(v),v_{1})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}(g_{x}(v),g_{x}(v_{1}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\mathcal{N}((v+v_{1})\cdot x)-\mathcal{N}(v\cdot x)-\mathcal{N}(v_{1}\cdot x)\bigr)

\displaystyle=q(v,v_{1})\mathcal{N}(x).

Hence
f_{x}\circ g_{x}=\mathcal{N}(x)\cdot\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb{C}^{8}}
. Since
\dim\mathbb{C}^{8}=\dim S^{-}
and
\mathcal{N}(x)\neq 0
, it follows that
g_{x}\circ f_{x}=\mathcal{N}(x)\cdot\operatorname{Id}_{S^{-}}
. Therefore,

q(t_{1}(x,y))=q(f_{x}(y),f_{x}(y))=\mathcal{N}(g_{x}f_{x}(y),y)=\mathcal{N}(x)\mathcal{N}(y),

so
t_{1}
is orthogonal. Thus
(\mathbb{C}^{8},S^{+},S^{-},\mathcal{T})
is a triality.

For simplicity, we write
xy=t_{1}(x,y)=t_{1}^{\prime}(y,x)
for
x\in S^{+}
,
y\in S^{-}
.

Lemma 2.5.4
.

Let
(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},\mathcal{T})
be a triality. For any permutation
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
, and for all
v_{i},v_{i}^{\prime}\in V_{i}
,
v_{k}\in V_{k}
, we have:

(1)

\displaystyle v_{i}(v_{i}^{\prime}v_{k})+v_{i}^{\prime}(v_{i}v_{k})

\displaystyle=2q_{i}(v_{i},v_{i}^{\prime})v_{k},

(2)

\displaystyle v_{i}(v_{i}v_{k})

\displaystyle=q_{i}(v_{i})v_{k}.

Proof.

The two identities are equivalent via polarization. It suffices to prove (2). Consider the maps

v_{i}\cdot(-):V_{j}\to V_{k},\quad v_{i}\cdot(-):V_{k}\to V_{j},

defined by
v_{i}\cdot v_{j}=v_{i}v_{j}
and
v_{i}\cdot v_{k}=v_{i}v_{k}
. Since
q_{j}(v_{i}v_{j})=q_{i}(v_{i})q_{j}(v_{j})
for all
v_{j}\in V_{j}
, we have for any
v_{k},v_{k}^{\prime}\in V_{k}
:

\displaystyle q_{k}(v_{i}(v_{i}v_{k}),v_{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(v_{i},v_{i}v_{k},v_{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=q_{j}(v_{i}v_{k},v_{i}v_{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=q_{i}(v_{i})q_{k}(v_{k},v_{k}^{\prime}).

By non-degeneracy of
q_{k}
, it follows that
v_{i}(v_{i}v_{k})=q_{i}(v_{i})v_{k}
.
∎

Definition 2.5.5
.

Let
\tau=(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},\mathcal{T})
be a triality. A
triality automorphism
of
\tau
is a triple
(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})
, where each
A_{i}
is an orthogonal automorphism of
V_{i}
, such that

\mathcal{T}(A_{1}(v_{1}),A_{2}(v_{2}),A_{3}(v_{3}))=\mathcal{T}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})

for all
v_{1}\in V_{1}
,
v_{2}\in V_{2}
,
v_{3}\in V_{3}
.

The set of all such triality automorphisms, with group structure given by component-wise composition, is called the
triality automorphism group
of
\tau
, denoted
\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(\tau)
. Thus
\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(\tau)
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{O}(V_{1},q_{1})\times\mathrm{O}(V_{2},q_{2})\times\mathrm{O}(V_{3},q_{3})
.

Proposition 2.5.6
.

Let
\tau=(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},\mathcal{T})
be a triality, and
(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})
a triple of orthogonal automorphisms of
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}
respectively. Then
(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})
is a triality automorphism if and only if

t_{3}(A_{1}(v_{1}),A_{2}(v_{2}))=A_{3}(t_{3}(v_{1},v_{2}))

for all
v_{1}\in V_{1}
,
v_{2}\in V_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
\tau
is a triality, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})

\displaystyle=q_{3}(t_{3}(v_{1},v_{2}),v_{3})=q_{3}(A_{3}(t_{3}(v_{1},v_{2})),A_{3}(v_{3})),

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(A_{1}(v_{1}),A_{2}(v_{2}),A_{3}(v_{3}))

\displaystyle=q_{3}(t_{3}(A_{1}(v_{1}),A_{2}(v_{2})),A_{3}(v_{3})).

By non-degeneracy of
q_{3}
, the equality

q_{3}(A_{3}(t_{3}(v_{1},v_{2})),A_{3}(v_{3}))=q_{3}(t_{3}(A_{1}(v_{1}),A_{2}(v_{2})),A_{3}(v_{3}))

holds for all
v_{3}\in V_{3}
if and only if

t_{3}(A_{1}(v_{1}),A_{2}(v_{2}))=A_{3}(t_{3}(v_{1},v_{2})).

∎

The center of
\operatorname{Spin}(8)
is
C(\operatorname{Spin}(8))=\{\pm 1,\pm\eta\}
, where
\eta=e_{1}\cdots e_{8}\in\operatorname{Spin}(8)
. Since
e_{i}
anti-commutes with
e_{j}
for
i\neq j
but commutes with itself, we have
\eta e_{i}=(-1)^{7}e_{i}\eta=-e_{i}\eta
; hence
\eta v=-v\eta
for all
v\in\mathbb{C}^{8}
. As every element of
\operatorname{Spin}(8)
is a product of an even number of unit vectors in
\operatorname{Pin}(8)
, it follows that
\eta
lies in the center of
\operatorname{Spin}(8)
.

The complex group
\operatorname{Spin}(8)
admits three non-isomorphic
8
-dimensional irreducible orthogonal representations:

•

the standard representation
\rho:\operatorname{Spin}(8)\to\operatorname{SO}(8)
;

•

the two half-spin representations
\rho^{+}:\operatorname{Spin}(8)\to\operatorname{GL}(S^{+})
and
\rho^{-}:\operatorname{Spin}(8)\to\operatorname{GL}(S^{-})
, which factor through
\operatorname{SO}(S^{+},\mathcal{N})
and
\operatorname{SO}(S^{-},\mathcal{N})
, respectively.

These satisfy

\displaystyle\rho|_{C(\operatorname{Spin}(8))}

\displaystyle=1;

\displaystyle\rho^{+}(1)=\rho^{+}(\eta)

\displaystyle=1,\quad\rho^{+}(-1)=\rho^{+}(-\eta)=-1;

\displaystyle\rho^{-}(1)=\rho^{-}(-\eta)

\displaystyle=1,\quad\rho^{-}(-1)=\rho^{-}(\eta)=-1.

Proposition 2.5.7
.

The map

(\rho,\rho^{+},\rho^{-}):\operatorname{Spin}(8)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{SO}(8)\times\operatorname{SO}(S^{+},\mathcal{N})\times\operatorname{SO}(S^{-},\mathcal{N})

is an embedding whose image is the triality automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(\mathbb{C}^{8},S^{+},S^{-},\mathcal{T})
.

Proof.

For
a\in\operatorname{Spin}(8)
, define

\displaystyle\rho(a)v

\displaystyle=av\overline{a},\quad v\in\mathbb{C}^{8},

\displaystyle\rho^{+}(a)x

\displaystyle=ax,\quad x\in S^{+},

\displaystyle\rho^{-}(a)y

\displaystyle=ay,\quad y\in S^{-},

where the products are taken in the Clifford algebra. Then

t_{3}(\rho(a)v,\rho^{+}(a)x)=(av\overline{a})(ax)=a(vx)=\rho^{-}(a)(t_{3}(v,x)).

Hence
(\rho(a),\rho^{+}(a),\rho^{-}(a))
is a triality automorphism.

To show surjectivity, note that the standard representation
\rho:\operatorname{Spin}(8)\to\operatorname{SO}(8)
is surjective with kernel
\{\pm 1\}
. Let
\sigma:\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(\mathbb{C}^{8},S^{+},S^{-},\mathcal{T})\to\operatorname{SO}(8)
be the projection onto the first factor. We show
\ker\sigma
has order
2
.

Let
(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(\tau)
. By functoriality,
A_{1}
extends uniquely to an automorphism of
C^{+}(\mathbb{C}^{8},q)
. The identities

A_{1}(v)A_{2}(x)=A_{3}(vx),\qquad A_{1}(v)A_{3}(y)=A_{2}(vy)

imply that
A_{2}\oplus A_{3}:S^{+}\oplus S^{-}\to S^{+}\oplus S^{-}
is an isomorphism of
C(\mathbb{C}^{8},q)
-modules. Hence
A_{1}\in\operatorname{SO}(8)
.

Now consider
(\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb{C}^{8}},A_{2},A_{3})\in\ker\sigma
. Then
A_{3}(vx)=vA_{2}(x)
for all
v\in\mathbb{C}^{8}
,
x\in S^{+}
, so
A_{2}\oplus A_{3}
is a
C(\mathbb{C}^{8},q)
-module endomorphism of the simple module
\bigwedge^{\bullet}W=S^{+}\oplus S^{-}
. By Schur’s lemma,
A_{2}\oplus A_{3}
is a scalar map. Since
A_{2},A_{3}
are orthogonal, we must have
A_{2}=A_{3}=\pm 1
. Therefore
\ker\sigma\cong\{\pm 1\}
, and the embedding is an isomorphism.
∎

3.1.
Triality for Octonion Algebras

Let
V_{1}=V_{2}=V_{3}=\mathbb{O}
be
8
-dimensional vector spaces, and define the trilinear form
\mathcal{T}(x,y,z)=\operatorname{tr}(xyz)
. Then
(\mathbb{O},\mathbb{O},\mathbb{O},\operatorname{tr})
forms a triality.

Since
\operatorname{tr}(xy)=\operatorname{tr}(yx)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{O}
, we have the cyclic symmetry

\operatorname{tr}(xyz)=\operatorname{tr}(yzx)=\operatorname{tr}(zxy),\qquad\forall x,y,z\in\mathbb{O}.

Let
b_{N}(x,y)=N(x+y)-N(x)-N(y)
be the polar form of
N
, and note that
\overline{xy}=\bar{y}\bar{x}
. Then

\operatorname{tr}(xy)=b_{N}(x,t(y)),\quad\text{where }t(y)=\bar{y}.

Define
t_{1}(y,z)=\overline{yz}
. Then

\operatorname{tr}(xyz)=b_{N}(x,t_{1}(y,z)).

Define quadratic forms

q_{1}(y,z)=b_{N}(\bar{y},\bar{z}),\qquad q_{2}(x,y)=b_{N}(x,y),\qquad q_{3}(x,y)=\tfrac{1}{2}b_{N}(x,y).

Using the composition property
N(xy)=N(x)N(y)
, one verifies

q_{1}(t_{1}(y,z))=q_{2}(y)q_{3}(z).

Similarly, define

t_{2}(x,y)=\bar{x}y,\qquad t_{3}(x,y)=2\bar{x}y,

and check

q_{2}(t_{2}(x,z))=q_{1}(x)q_{3}(z),\qquad q_{3}(t_{3}(x,y))=q_{1}(x)q_{2}(y).

Thus
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}
are orthogonal, and
(\mathbb{O},\mathbb{O},\mathbb{O},\operatorname{tr})
is a triality.

Since
\operatorname{Spin}(8)\cong\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(\tau)
, we have

\operatorname{Spin}(8)\cong\{(A,B,C)\in\operatorname{SO}(\mathbb{O})^{3}:\operatorname{tr}(A(x)B(y)C(z))=\operatorname{tr}(xyz),\ \forall x,y,z\in\mathbb{O}\}.

An
S_{3}
-action on

x_{1}=1\in\mathbb{O}

\sigma_{1}(A,B,C)=(\widehat{A},\widehat{C},\widehat{B}),\qquad\sigma_{2}(A,B,C)=(\widehat{C},\widehat{B},\widehat{A}),

lift the transpositions in
S_{3}
, and
\theta=\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}
satisfies
\theta(A,B,C)=(B,C,A)
with
\theta^{3}=1
.

For

5.
Trace formula of
\operatorname{G}_{2}

Let
F
be a number field with adele ring
\mathbb{A}
, and let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group over
F
. Then
G(\mathbb{A})
is a locally compact topological group containing
G(F)
as a discrete subgroup. Let
\mathcal{H}(G)
denote the Hecke algebra of smooth, compactly supported, complex-valued functions on
G(\mathbb{A})
that are
K
-finite for some maximal compact subgroup
K\subset G(\mathbb{A})
.

The regular representation
R
of
G(\mathbb{A})
on
L^{2}(G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb{A}))
is defined by

(R(y)\phi)(x)=\phi(xy),\qquad x,y\in G(\mathbb{A}),\ \phi\in L^{2}(G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb{A})).

For
f\in\mathcal{H}(G)
, the convolution operator
R(f)=\int_{G(\mathbb{A})}f(y)R(y)\,dy
has kernel

K(x,y)=\sum_{\gamma\in G(F)}f(x^{-1}\gamma y).

This kernel admits two natural decompositions

K(x,y)=\sum_{\mathfrak{o}\in\mathcal{O}}K_{\mathfrak{o}}(x,y)=\sum_{\chi\in\Pi}K_{\chi}(x,y),

where
\mathcal{O}
is the set of conjugacy classes in
G(F)
, and
\Pi
is a set of spectral data.

To handle divergence issues, Arthur introduced truncated kernels
K_{\mathfrak{o}}^{T}(x,f)
and
K_{\chi}^{T}(x,f)
depending on a parameter
T
in a positive Weyl chamber. This yields the identity

(5.0.1)

\sum_{\mathfrak{o}\in\mathcal{O}}K_{\mathfrak{o}}^{T}(x,f)=\sum_{\chi\in\Pi}K_{\chi}^{T}(x,f).

Integrating both sides gives the weighted trace formula

(5.0.2)

J(f)=\sum_{\mathfrak{o}\in\mathcal{O}}J_{\mathfrak{o}}(f)=\sum_{\chi\in\Pi}J_{\chi}(f).

Arthur
[
9
, Theorem 8.1]
defined the weighted orbital distributions
J_{M}(\gamma,f)
for Levi subgroups
M
and
\gamma\in M(F)
, leading to the expansion

(5.0.3)

J_{\mathfrak{o}}(f)=\sum_{M\in\mathcal{L}}|W_{0}^{M}|\,|W_{0}^{G}|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\in(M(F)\cap\mathfrak{o})_{M,S}}a^{M}(S,\gamma)\,J_{M}(\gamma,f),

where
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(M_{0})
is the set of Levi subgroups containing a fixed minimal Levi
M_{0}
, and
W(M)=\operatorname{Norm}_{G}(A_{M})/M
denotes the relative Weyl group.

To obtain invariant distributions, define inductively

I_{M}(\gamma,f)=J_{M}(\gamma,f)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{L}(M)\\
L\neq G\end{subarray}}\hat{I}_{M}^{L}(\gamma,\phi_{L}(f)),

where
\phi_{L}
transfers functions from
G(F_{S})
to
L(F_{S})
. Then the invariant geometric expansion becomes

I(f)=\sum_{M\in\mathcal{L}}|W_{0}^{M}|\,|W_{0}^{G}|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\in(M(F))_{M,S}}a^{M}(S,\gamma)\,I_{M}(\gamma,f).

The spectral side admits a parallel decomposition

I(f)=\sum_{t\geq 0}I_{t}(f)=\sum_{t\geq 0}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{L}}|W_{0}^{M}|\,|W_{0}^{G}|^{-1}\int_{\Pi(M,t)}a^{M}(\pi)\,I_{M}(\pi,f)\,d\pi,

where
I_{t}(f)
sums over classes
\chi
with
\|v_{\pi,I}\|=t
for
v_{\pi}
the infinitesimal character, and
v_{\pi,I}
is the imaginary part of
v_{\pi}
.

To stabilize the trace formula, define

S(f)=I(f)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{ell}}(G)\\
G^{\prime}\neq G\end{subarray}}\iota(G,G^{\prime})\,\hat{S}^{G^{\prime}}(f^{\prime}),

where
f\mapsto f^{\prime}
is the global transfer map, and

\iota(G,G^{\prime})=k(G,G^{\prime})\,|\overline{Z}(\widehat{G}^{\prime})^{\Gamma}|^{-1}\,|\operatorname{Out}_{G}(G^{\prime})|^{-1},

with

k(G,G^{\prime})=|\ker^{1}(F,Z(\widehat{G}^{0}))|^{-1}\,|\ker^{1}(F,Z(\widehat{G}^{\prime}))|.

Arthur
[
8
]
proved, under the weighted fundamental lemma (established by Chaudouard–Laumon
[
18
,
19
]
), that
S(f)
is stable and admits a decomposition

S(f)=\sum_{M\in\mathcal{L}}|W_{0}^{M}|\,|W_{0}^{G}|^{-1}\sum_{\delta\in\Delta(M,V)}b^{M}(\delta)\,S_{M}(\delta,f),

where
\Delta(M,V)
denotes stable conjugacy classes in
M(F_{V})
.

The discrete part of the invariant trace formula
[
4
,
5
]
expands as

\displaystyle I_{\text{disc},t}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{M\in\mathcal{L}}|W_{0}^{M}|\,|W_{0}^{G}|^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{w\in W(M)_{\text{reg}}}|\det(w-1)_{\mathfrak{a}^{G}_{M}}|^{-1}\,\operatorname{tr}\bigl(M_{P,t}(w,\chi)\,\mathcal{I}_{P,t}(\chi,f)\bigr),

where
W(M)_{\text{reg}}=\{w\in W(M):\det(w-1)_{\mathfrak{a}^{G}_{M}}\neq 0\}
, and
M_{P,t}(w,\chi)
is the global intertwining operator

M_{P}(w,\chi_{\lambda})=\ell(w)\circ M_{P^{\prime}|P}(\chi_{\lambda}),\qquad P^{\prime}=w^{-1}P,

defined meromorphically in
\lambda\in(\mathfrak{a}^{G}_{M})^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
, and unitary on
i(\mathfrak{a}^{G}_{M})^{*}
.

The stabilized discrete trace is

S_{\text{disc},t}(f)=I_{\text{disc},t}(f)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{ell}}(G)\\
G^{\prime}\neq G\end{subarray}}\iota(G,G^{\prime})\,\hat{S}^{\prime}_{\text{disc},t}(f^{\prime}),

and we obtain the full stabilization

I_{\text{disc},t}(f)=\sum_{G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{ell}}(G)}\iota(G,G^{\prime})\,\hat{S}^{\prime}_{\text{disc},t}(f^{\prime}).

Theorem 5.0.1
.

For
G=G_{2}
and
f\in\mathcal{H}(G)
, the discrete invariant distribution expands as

\displaystyle I_{\text{disc},t}(f)=

\displaystyle\;\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\mathcal{I}_{G,t}(1,f)\bigr)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{w\in W(\operatorname{GL}(2)_{s})_{\text{reg}}}|\det(w-1)_{\mathbb{R}}|^{-1}\,\operatorname{tr}\bigl(M_{P,t}(w,1)\,\mathcal{I}_{P,t}(1,f)\bigr)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{w\in W(\operatorname{GL}(2)_{l})_{\text{reg}}}|\det(w-1)_{\mathbb{R}}|^{-1}\,\operatorname{tr}\bigl(M_{P^{\prime},t}(w,1)\,\mathcal{I}_{P^{\prime},t}(1,f)\bigr)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{12}\sum_{w\in W(T)_{\text{reg}}}|\det(w-1)_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|^{-1}\,\operatorname{tr}\bigl(M_{B,t}(w,\chi)\,\mathcal{I}_{B,t}(1,f)\bigr),

where
\operatorname{GL}(2)_{s}
and
\operatorname{GL}(2)_{l}
denote the Levi subgroups corresponding to the short and long roots, respectively.

Theorem 5.0.2
.

For
G=G_{2}
and
f\in\mathcal{H}(G)
, the discrete invariant distribution stabilizes as

I_{\text{disc},t}(f)=S^{G_{2}}_{\text{disc},t}(f)+\iota(G,\operatorname{SO}(4))\,\widehat{S}^{\operatorname{SO}(4)}_{\text{disc},t}(f^{\prime})+\iota(G,\operatorname{PGL}(3))\,\widehat{S}^{\operatorname{PGL}(3)}_{\text{disc},t}(f^{\prime\prime}),

and the full invariant trace formula becomes

I(f)=\sum_{G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{ell}}(G)}\iota(G,G^{\prime})\,\widehat{S}^{\prime}(f^{\prime}),

where
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ell}}(G)=\{G_{2},\operatorname{SO}(4),\operatorname{PGL}(3)\}
, and
f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}
are the Langlands–Kottwitz–Shelstad transfers of
f
.

6.
The Triality Twisted Trace Formula for
\operatorname{PGSO}(8)

Let
\widetilde{G}=(G,\theta,\omega)
be a triple over
F
, where
G
is connected reductive,
\theta
is a semisimple automorphism, and
\omega
is a character on
G(\mathbb{A})
trivial on
G(F)
. We write
\widetilde{G}=G\rtimes\theta
for the associated
G
-bitorsor.

The Hecke module
\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{G})
consists of smooth, compactly supported,
K
-finite functions on
\widetilde{G}
. The conjugation actions are

\displaystyle x^{-1}(y\rtimes\theta)x

\displaystyle=x^{-1}y\theta(x)\rtimes\theta,\quad x,y\in G,

\displaystyle(x\rtimes\theta)^{-1}y(x\rtimes\theta)

\displaystyle=\theta^{-1}(x^{-1}yx),\quad x,y\in G.

Define:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\widetilde{G}}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{a}_{G}^{\theta}=\{H\in\mathfrak{a}_{G}:\operatorname{Ad}(\theta)H=H\},

\displaystyle A_{\widetilde{G}}

\displaystyle=(A_{G}^{\theta})^{0},

\displaystyle Z(\widetilde{G}(\mathbb{A}))

\displaystyle=Z(G(\mathbb{A}))^{\theta}.

Let
\chi
be a character on
(\ker\omega\cap Z(\widetilde{G}(\mathbb{A})))/(Z(\widetilde{G}(F))\cap\ker\omega)
. The equivariant Hecke module
\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{G},\chi)
consists of functions satisfying

f(xz)=f(x)\chi(z)^{-1},\quad z\in\ker\omega\cap Z(\widetilde{G}(\mathbb{A})).

For
M\in\mathcal{L}(M_{0})
, define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{G,\theta}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{a}_{G}/\{X-\operatorname{Ad}(\theta)X:X\in\mathfrak{a}_{G}\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}^{\widetilde{G}}_{M}

\displaystyle=\ker(\mathfrak{a}_{M}\to\mathfrak{a}_{\widetilde{G}}),

\displaystyle W(M)

\displaystyle=W^{\widetilde{G}}(M)=\operatorname{Norm}(A_{M},\widetilde{G})/M,

\displaystyle W_{\text{reg}}(M)

\displaystyle=\{w\in W(M):\det(w-1)_{\mathfrak{a}^{\widetilde{G}}_{M}}\neq 0\}.

For
P=N_{P}M\in\mathcal{P}(M)
, let
\mathcal{H}_{P}
be the space of left
N_{P}(\mathbb{A})
-invariant functions
\phi
on
G(\mathbb{A})
with
\phi(mk)\in L^{2}_{\text{disc}}(M(F)\backslash M(\mathbb{A}),\chi)\otimes L^{2}(K)
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{P,t}(\chi)
be the invariant subspace under
M_{P}(w,\chi_{\lambda})
.

Define
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{P,t}(\chi)
as functions
\phi
on
\widetilde{G}(\mathbb{A})
such that
\phi(xy)\in\mathcal{H}_{P,t}(\chi)
for all
y\in\widetilde{G}(\mathbb{A})
. The induced representation is

(\mathcal{I}_{P}^{G}(\chi,y)\phi)(x)=\phi(xy)\omega(\theta^{-1}xy),\quad\phi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{P,t}(\chi).

For
f\in\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{G},\chi)
, define

\mathcal{I}_{P,t}(\chi,f)=\int_{\widetilde{G}(\mathbb{A})/(Z(\widetilde{G}(\mathbb{A}))\cap\ker\omega)}f(y)\,\mathcal{I}^{G}_{P,t}(x,y)\,dy.

The intertwining operator
M_{P,t}(w,\chi_{\lambda})
is the meromorphic composition

\mathcal{H}_{P,t}(\chi)\xrightarrow{M_{P^{\prime}|P}(\chi_{\lambda})}\mathcal{H}_{P^{\prime},t}(\chi)\xrightarrow{\ell(w)}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{P,t}(\chi),

where
(\ell(w)\phi)(x)=\phi(w^{-1}x\theta^{-1})
. At
\lambda=0
, denote this by
M_{P,t}(w,\chi)
.

For
M=G
, the operator
I_{G}(\chi,f)\circ M_{G}(\theta,\chi)
acts on
L^{2}(G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb{A}),\chi)
with kernel

K(x,y)=\sum_{\gamma\in\widetilde{G}}f(x^{-1}\gamma y)\omega(y),\quad x,y\in G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb{A}).

The twisted trace formula is concerned with the study of this kernel. In our case,
\omega(y)
is trivial.

The twisted invariant trace formula
I(f)
and its discrete part
I_{\text{disc},t}(f)
can be established as in
[
24
]
. The stabilization takes the form

(6.0.1)

I(f)=\sum_{G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{ell}}(\widetilde{G})}\iota(\widetilde{G},G^{\prime})\,\widehat{S}^{\prime}(f^{\prime}),

and

(6.0.2)

I_{\text{disc},t}(f)=\sum_{G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{ell}}(\widetilde{G})}\iota(\widetilde{G},G^{\prime})\,\widehat{S}^{\prime}_{\text{disc},t}(f^{\prime}),

with coefficients

\iota(\widetilde{G},G^{\prime})=|\pi_{0}(\kappa_{\widetilde{G}})|^{-1}\,k(\widetilde{G},G^{\prime})\,|\overline{Z}(\widehat{G}^{\prime})^{\Gamma}|^{-1}\,|\operatorname{Out}_{\widetilde{G}}(G^{\prime})|^{-1},

where
\kappa_{\widetilde{G}}=Z(\widehat{\widetilde{G}}^{\Gamma})\cap(Z(\widehat{G}^{0})^{\Gamma})^{0}
.

Assume that the twisted weighted fundamental lemma holds. Moeglin and Waldspurger
[
22
,
23
]
established the two identities (
6.0.1
) and (
6.0.2
); we thus obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6.0.1
.

Let
\widetilde{G}=\mathrm{PGSO}(8)\rtimes\theta
with
\theta^{3}=1
, and let
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(G,\chi)
. Then the triality twisted trace formula is given by

I(f)=\widehat{S}^{G_{2}}(f^{\prime})+\tfrac{1}{4}\widehat{S}^{\operatorname{SO}(4)}(f^{\prime}_{4})+\tfrac{1}{3}\widehat{S}^{\operatorname{SL}_{3}}(f^{\prime}_{3}),

and its discrete part satisfies

(6.0.3)

I_{\text{disc},t}(f)=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{G},t}(f)\bigr)+\tfrac{1}{6}\sum_{w\in W_{\text{reg}}(M)}|\det(w-1)_{\mathfrak{a}^{\widetilde{G}}_{M}}|^{-1}\,\operatorname{tr}\bigl(M_{P,t}(w,1)\,\mathcal{I}_{P,t}(1,f)\bigr)

and

\displaystyle I_{\text{disc},t}(f)

\displaystyle=\widehat{S}^{G_{2}}_{\mathrm{disc},t}(f^{\prime})+\tfrac{1}{4}\widehat{S}^{\operatorname{SO}(4)}_{\mathrm{disc},t}(f^{\prime}_{4})+\tfrac{1}{3}\widehat{S}^{\operatorname{SL}_{3}}_{\mathrm{disc},t}(f^{\prime}_{3}),

where
M=\operatorname{GL}(2)
, and
f^{\prime},f^{\prime}_{4},f^{\prime}_{3}
denote the Langlands–Kottwitz–Shelstad transfers of
f
.

Remark 6.0.2
.

The stable distributions
\widehat{S}^{\operatorname{SL}_{3}}_{\text{disc},t}(f^{\prime}_{3})
and
\widehat{S}^{\operatorname{SO}(4)}_{\text{disc},t}(f^{\prime}_{4})
are well-understood, while
\widehat{S}^{G_{2}}_{\text{disc},t}(f^{\prime})
requires further study to obtain explicit formulas.

7.
Expansion of discrete invariant distribution

Assume that
F
is a number field. A hypothetical global Langlands group
L_{F}

[
25
, §2]
is thought to be a locally compact extension

1\longrightarrow K_{F}\longrightarrow L_{F}\longrightarrow W_{F}\longrightarrow 1

of the Weil group by a compact connected group
K_{F}
. However, its existence is a very deep problem, and Arthur
[
3
]
constructed an approximation
L_{F}^{*}
of the global Langlands group, which depends on the endoscopic classification of automorphic representations of classical groups.

7.1.
A-parameters

An Arthur parameter (A-parameter) of
\operatorname{GL}(8)
is an equivalence class of unitary
8
-dimensional representations

\psi:L_{F}^{*}\times\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}(8,\mathbb{C}).

We denote by
\Psi(\operatorname{GL}(8))
the set of A-parameters of
\operatorname{GL}(8)
. To any
\psi\in\Psi(\operatorname{GL}(8))
, we attach an
8
-dimensional representation

\phi_{\psi}(u)=\psi\bigg(u,\begin{pmatrix}|u|^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
0&|u|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}\bigg),\qquad u\in L_{F}^{*},

of
L_{F}^{*}
. This corresponds to the unique irreducible quotient
\pi_{\psi}
established by Moeglin and Waldspurger
[
21
]
. We then have a bijection from
\Psi(\operatorname{GL}(8))
to
\mathcal{A}(\operatorname{GL}(8))
mapping
\psi
to
\pi_{\psi}
. Here
\mathcal{A}(\operatorname{GL}(8))
is the subset of irreducible unitary representations
\pi
of
\operatorname{GL}(8,\mathbb{A})
whose restrictions to
\operatorname{GL}(8,\mathbb{A})^{1}
are irreducible constituents of the space
L^{2}(\operatorname{GL}(8,F)\backslash\operatorname{GL}(8,\mathbb{A})^{1})
, where

\operatorname{GL}(8,\mathbb{A})^{1}=\{x\in\operatorname{GL}(8,\mathbb{A}):|\chi(x)|=1,\ \chi\in X(\operatorname{GL}(8))_{F}\}

is the closed subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(8,\mathbb{A})
and
X(\operatorname{GL}(8))_{F}
is the additive group of characters of
\operatorname{GL}(8)
defined over
F
.

An automorphic representation
\pi
of
\operatorname{GL}(8)
is a weakly continuous, irreducible representation of
\operatorname{GL}(8)
. It can be written as a restricted tensor product

\pi=\bigotimes_{v}^{\prime}\pi_{v}

of irreducible representations of the local groups
\operatorname{GL}(F_{v})
, almost all of which are unramified
[
13
]
. When
\pi_{v}
is unramified, the local Langlands correspondence is well known: there is a bijection between the set of unramified representations of
\operatorname{GL}(8,F_{v})
and the set of semisimple
\operatorname{GL}(8,\mathbb{C})
-orbits in
\operatorname{GL}(8,\mathbb{C})\times W_{F_{v}}
that project to the Frobenius generator of
W_{F_{v}}/I_{F_{v}}
, where
I_{F_{v}}
is the inertia subgroup of the local Weil group
W_{F_{v}}
.

The automorphic representation
\pi
of
\operatorname{GL}(8)
gives rise to a family of semisimple conjugacy classes

c^{S}(\pi)=\{c_{v}(\pi):v\notin S\}

in
\operatorname{GL}(8,\mathbb{C})
, where
S
is some finite set of valuations of
F
outside of which
\operatorname{GL}(8)
is unramified. We define
c^{S}(\pi)
and
(c^{\prime})^{S^{\prime}}(\pi)
to be equivalent if
c_{v}(\pi)=c^{\prime}_{v}(\pi)
for almost all
v
. Denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{aut}}(\operatorname{GL}(8))
the set of equivalence classes of
c^{S}(\pi)
. This yields a map
\pi\mapsto c(\pi)
from the set of automorphic representations of
\operatorname{GL}(8)
onto
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{aut}}(\operatorname{GL}(8))
.

The outer automorphism
\theta:x\mapsto x^{\vee}
of
\operatorname{GL}(8)
acts on
\Psi(\operatorname{GL}(8))
, transforming a parameter
\psi
to its contragredient
\psi^{\vee}
. We denote the subset

\widetilde{\Psi}(\operatorname{GL}(8)\rtimes\theta)=\{\psi\in\Psi(\operatorname{GL}(8)):\psi^{\vee}=\psi\}

of self-dual parameters in
\Psi(\operatorname{GL}(8))
.

We denote by
\Psi(\operatorname{SO}(8))
the set of conjugacy classes of
L
-homomorphisms

\psi^{\prime}:L_{F}^{*}\times\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow\operatorname{SO}(8,\mathbb{C})\times W_{F}.

For any
\psi^{\prime}\in\Psi(\operatorname{SO}(8))
, there exists an A-parameter
\psi\in\widetilde{\Psi}(\operatorname{GL}(8))
such that

\xi\circ\psi^{\prime}=\psi,

where
\xi
is the embedding of
\operatorname{SO}(8,\mathbb{C})\times W_{F}
into
\operatorname{GL}(8,\mathbb{C})\times W_{F}
that is part of the twisted endoscopic datum. Since
\psi
and
\xi
are to be regarded as
\operatorname{GL}(8,\mathbb{C})
-conjugacy classes of homomorphisms, there are two
\operatorname{SO}(8,\mathbb{C})
-orbits of homomorphisms
\psi^{\prime}
in the class of
\psi
. We write
\widetilde{\Psi}(\operatorname{SO}(8))
for the set of
\mathrm{O}(8,\mathbb{C})/\operatorname{SO}(8,\mathbb{C})
-orbits
\psi^{\prime}
in
\Psi(\operatorname{SO}(8))
. Arthur
[
3
]
constructed the global Langlands correspondence for discrete series of
\operatorname{SO}(8)
(modulo
\mathrm{O}(8)/\operatorname{SO}(8)
).

We write
\Psi(\operatorname{PGSO}(8))
for the set of conjugacy classes of
L
-homomorphisms

\psi:L_{F}^{*}\times\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spin}(8,\mathbb{C})\times W_{F}.

Given
\psi\in\Psi(\operatorname{PGSO}(8))
, we have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{F}^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spin}(8,\mathbb{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SO}(8,\mathbb{C})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}

Thus
\rho\circ\psi=\psi^{\prime}
, yielding an A-parameter
\psi^{\prime}\in\Psi(\operatorname{SO}(8))
. The kernel of
\rho
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Given an A-parameter
\psi^{\prime}
, a natural question is whether there exists a lift
\psi
such that
\rho\circ\psi=\psi^{\prime}
.

In this paper, we consider the
triality A-parameter

(\psi^{\prime}_{1},\psi^{\prime}_{2},\psi^{\prime}_{3})
, where
\psi^{\prime}_{i}\in\Psi(\operatorname{SO}(8))
. The composition

L_{F}^{*}\xrightarrow{\psi}\operatorname{Spin}(8,\mathbb{C})\xrightarrow{(\rho,\rho\circ\theta,\rho\circ\theta^{2})}\operatorname{SO}(8,\mathbb{C})^{3}

equals
(\psi^{\prime}_{1},\psi^{\prime}_{2},\psi^{\prime}_{3})
. The automorphism
\theta
induces an outer automorphism of
\operatorname{SO}(8,\mathbb{C})^{3}
such that

\theta(\rho,\rho\circ\theta,\rho\circ\theta^{2})=(\theta(\rho),\theta^{2}(\rho),\rho)=(\rho\circ\theta,\rho\circ\theta^{2},\rho).

Hence

(\rho,\rho\circ\theta,\rho\circ\theta^{2})(\theta(\psi))=\theta(\rho,\rho\circ\theta,\rho\circ\theta^{2})(\psi).

The symmetric group
S_{3}
acts naturally on
\operatorname{SO}(8,\mathbb{C})^{3}
; there exists
\sigma\in S_{3}
such that

\sigma(\rho,\rho\circ\theta,\rho\circ\theta^{2})=(\rho\circ\theta^{2},\rho,\rho\circ\theta).

Set
\tau=\sigma\theta^{*}
; then

\tau(\rho,\rho\circ\theta,\rho\circ\theta^{2})=(\rho,\rho\circ\theta,\rho\circ\theta^{2}),

so
\tau(\psi^{\prime}_{1},\psi^{\prime}_{2},\psi^{\prime}_{3})=(\psi^{\prime}_{1},\psi^{\prime}_{2},\psi^{\prime}_{3})
and
\tau^{3}=1
. The triality A-parameter
(\psi^{\prime}_{1},\psi^{\prime}_{2},\psi^{\prime}_{3})
is therefore
\tau
-stable. We denote by
\Psi^{\tau}(\operatorname{PGSO}(8))
the set of conjugacy classes of triality A-parameters.

We write
\widetilde{\Psi}(\operatorname{PGSO}(8))
for the set of
\theta
-stable A-parameters in
\Psi(\operatorname{PGSO}(8))
. For
\psi\in\Psi(\operatorname{PGSO}(8))
, we have an irreducible decomposition

\psi=\ell_{1}\psi_{1}\oplus\ell_{2}\psi_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus\ell_{k}\psi_{k}

for inequivalent irreducible representations
\psi_{i}
of
L_{F}^{*}\times\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
of degree
n_{i}
, with multiplicities
\ell_{i}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\ell_{i}n_{i}=8.

The representation
\psi
is
\theta
-stable if and only if there exists a cyclic permutation
\sigma
of order
3
on the indices such that for all
i
,

\theta(\psi_{i})=\psi_{\sigma(i)},\qquad\theta^{2}(\psi_{i})=\psi_{\sigma^{2}(i)},

and
\ell_{i}=\ell_{\sigma(i)}=\ell_{\sigma^{2}(i)}
. We say that
\psi
is
square integrable
if
\ell_{i}=1
for all
i
, and
elliptic
if
\ell_{i}\leq 2
for all
i
.

Assume
\psi
is square integrable and
\theta
-stable. Then there are two types of components
\psi_{i}
:
\theta
-stable and unstable. If
\psi_{i}
is an irreducible
\theta
-stable representation, we can write

\theta(\psi_{i})((w,y))=\theta(\psi_{i}((w,y)))=x_{i}\psi_{i}((w,y))x_{i}^{-1},\qquad(w,y)\in L_{F}^{*}\times\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C}),

for a fixed element
x_{i}\in\operatorname{SO}(n_{i},\mathbb{C})
. Applying
\theta
twice yields

\theta^{2}(\psi_{i}((w,y)))=\theta(x_{i})\theta(\psi_{i}((w,y)))\theta(x_{i})^{-1}

and

\psi_{i}((w,y))=\theta^{2}(x_{i})\theta(x_{i})x_{i}\psi_{i}((w,y))x_{i}^{-1}\theta(x_{i})^{-1}\theta^{2}(x_{i})^{-1}.

Since
\psi_{i}
is irreducible, the product
\theta^{2}(x_{i})\theta(x_{i})x_{i}
is a scalar matrix. If
x_{i}=I_{n_{i}}
, then
\theta(\psi_{i})=\psi_{i}
and
\operatorname{Im}\psi_{i}\subseteq G_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. As
G_{2}\subset\operatorname{SO}(7,\mathbb{C})
, we have
n_{i}\leq 7
. In particular, if
\psi
is a
\theta
-stable,
7
-dimensional irreducible representation of
L_{F}^{*}\times\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, then
\operatorname{Im}\psi\subseteq G_{2}
.

If
x_{i}=\omega I_{n_{i}}
, where
\omega
is a primitive cube root of unity, then
\operatorname{Im}(\psi_{i})\subseteq\operatorname{Ad}(\operatorname{PGL}(3,\mathbb{C}))
. If
\psi
is a
\theta
-stable,
8
-dimensional irreducible representation of
L_{F}^{*}\times\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, then
\operatorname{Im}\psi\subseteq\operatorname{Ad}(\operatorname{PGL}(3,\mathbb{C}))
.

If
\psi_{i}
is not
\theta
-stable, it will appear in the decomposition of
\xi^{\prime}_{4}\circ\psi^{\prime}
for some
\psi^{\prime}\in\Psi(\operatorname{SO}(4))
. Alternatively,
\psi_{i}\in\Psi(\operatorname{GL}(2))
and there exist A-parameters
\psi_{j}
and
\psi_{m}
, with
j,m\neq i
, satisfying

\psi_{j}=\theta(\psi_{i}),\qquad\psi_{m}=\theta(\psi_{j}).

If
\psi^{\prime}\in\Psi(\operatorname{SO}(4))
is irreducible, then
\psi=\xi_{4}^{\prime}\circ\psi^{\prime}\cong 2\psi^{\prime}
is an elliptic A-parameter but not square integrable. Similarly, if
\psi=\xi_{4}^{\prime}\circ\psi^{\prime}
, then
\psi
is not square integrable.

Suppose
n_{i}\leq 2
and
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
are distinct irreducible
2
-dimensional representations such that
\theta(\psi_{1})\neq\psi_{1}
, while
\theta(\psi_{2})
is self-dual reducible of dimension
2
and
\theta(\psi_{2})=\psi_{2}
. Then

\psi=\psi_{1}\oplus\theta(\psi_{1})\oplus\theta^{2}(\psi_{1})\oplus\psi_{2}

satisfies
\operatorname{Im}\psi\subseteq\xi_{2}(\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{C}))
, and
\psi
is not an elliptic A-parameter.

9.0.1
asserts that
I^{G_{2}}_{\mathrm{disc},t,c}(f)
vanishes. We have the decomposition

\displaystyle I^{G_{2}}_{\mathrm{disc},t,c}(f)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\mathcal{I}_{G_{2},t,c}(1,f)\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{w\in W(\operatorname{GL}(2)_{s})_{\mathrm{reg}}}|\det(w-1)_{\mathbb{R}}|^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(M_{P,t,c}(w,1)\mathcal{I}_{P,t,c}(1,f)\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{w\in W(\operatorname{GL}(2)_{l})_{\mathrm{reg}}}|\det(w-1)_{\mathbb{R}}|^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(M_{P^{\prime},t,c}(w,1)\mathcal{I}_{P^{\prime},t,c}(1,f)\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{12}\sum_{w\in W(T)_{\mathrm{reg}}}|\det(w-1)_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(M_{B,t,c}(w,\chi)\mathcal{I}_{B,t,c}(1,f)\bigr).

Let
M
denote a proper Levi subgroup of
G_{2}
, i.e.,
M=\operatorname{GL}(2)_{s}
,
\operatorname{GL}(2)_{l}
, or
T
. The operator
I^{G_{2}}_{P,t,c}(1_{M},f)
is induced from the
(t,c)
-component of the automorphic discrete spectrum of
M
. By Arthur’s work
[
3
,
\lx@sectionsign
1]
, this operator vanishes, and hence so does the corresponding summand above.

Therefore we obtain

I^{G_{2}}_{\mathrm{disc},t,c}(f)=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\mathcal{I}_{G_{2},t,c}(1_{G_{2}},f)\bigr)=0.

Since

\mathcal{I}_{G_{2},t,c}(f)=\mathcal{I}_{G_{2},t,c}(1_{G_{2}},f)

is the operator on
L^{2}_{\mathrm{disc},t,c}(G_{2}(F)\backslash G_{2}(\mathbb{A}))
given by right convolution by
f
, its trace equals the sum

\sum_{\pi}m(\pi)\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\pi(f)\bigr),\qquad\|\mu_{\pi,I}\|=t,\;c(\pi)=c,

over irreducible subrepresentations of this space. If all multiplicities
m(\pi)
were positive, this sum could not vanish for every
f\in\mathcal{H}(G_{2})
. Hence
m(\pi)=0
for each
\pi
, and the sum is over an empty set. Consequently,

L^{2}_{\mathrm{disc},t,c}\bigl(G_{2}(F)\backslash G_{2}(\mathbb{A})\bigr)=0.

∎

Remark 9.0.4
.

We have used the
A
-parameters of
\operatorname{PGSO}(8)
to classify the automorphic representations of
G_{2}
. For some
\psi\in\Psi^{\mathcal{O}}(\operatorname{PGSO}(8))
, the space
L^{2}_{\mathrm{disc},\psi}(G_{2}(F)\backslash G_{2}(\mathbb{A}))
may vanish. It remains to determine which
A
-parameters
\psi
contribute to the discrete spectrum of
G_{2}
and to give an explicit stable multiplicity formula. In a subsequent paper, we will study the stable distribution
S^{G_{2}}_{\mathrm{disc},\psi}(f)
in detail.
