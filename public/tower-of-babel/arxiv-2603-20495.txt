Commutators in Central Products of Cayley-Dickson Loops
Adam Chapman, Ilan Levin

Abstract
. This paper studies the triviality of commutators in central products of Cayley-Dickson loops. Two immediate outcomes of this study are (1) the construction of a sequence of non-commutative loops in which the chance of a random commutator to be trivial approaches 1, and (2) an easy proof for why if two central products of n n -fold Cayley-Dickson loops are isomorphic for n ⩾ 3 n\geqslant 3 , then the loops in the first product are term-wise isomorphic to the loops in the second product.

1.
Cayley-Dickson Algebras

Given a field
F
of
\operatorname{char}(F)\neq 2
and
\gamma\in F^{\times}
, a Cayley-Dickson doubling
B=A\{\gamma\}
of an algebra
A
over
F
with involution
\sigma
is defined to be
A\oplus A\ell
where multiplication is given by
(q+r\ell)(s+t\ell)=qs+\gamma\sigma(t)r+(tq+r\sigma(s))\ell
.
The involution extends from
A
to
B
by
\sigma(q+r\ell)=\sigma(q)-r\ell
.
We define the
n
-fold Cayley-Dickson algebra
(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})_{F}
recursively to be
B=A[\gamma_{n}]
where
A=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n-1})_{F}
, and the initial 0-fold algebra is
F
with the trivial involution.
It is known that

•

when
n=1
,
(\alpha)_{F}
are quadratic étale algebras, i.e., either
F\times F
or quadratic field extensions
K
of
F
, with the involution being the nontrivial automorphism of the Galois group of
K/F
;

•

when
n=2
,
(\alpha,\beta)_{F}
are quaternion algebras over
F
, with the involution being the symplectic involution;

•

when
n=3
,
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)_{F}
are octonion algebras;

•

when
n=4
,
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)_{F}
are sedenion algebras, etc.

These algebras, especially for
n\geqslant 3
, are important for various reasons (see for example
[
5
]
).
Tensor products of these algebras, especially for
n=2
(see
[
7
]
), but also for higher
n
(for instance in
[
1
]
) are also objects of interest in the active research of mathematics.
In
[
8
]
, isomorphisms between tensor products of Cayley-Dickson algebras were studied. The norm form of
(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})_{F}
given by
x\mapsto x\sigma(x)
is the
n
-fold Pfister form
\langle\!\langle\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}\rangle\!\rangle
(see
[
9
]
).

2.
Cayley-Dickson Loops

For the construction of the loops of concern in this paper, first note that the recursive construction of
(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})_{F}
defines a set
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n}
of generators of this algebra over
F
.
Fixing a subgroup
Z
of
F^{\times}
including
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}
, we define the
n
-fold Cayley-Dickson loop
(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})_{Z}
to be the multiplicative subloop of
(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})_{F}
generated by
Z
and
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n}
. Note that it always contains
-1
. For example, if
Z=\{\pm 1\}
and any field of characteristic
0
, then
(-1,-1)_{Z}
is the famous quaternion group
Q_{8}
, and
(-1,-1,-1)_{Z}
is the octonion loop
O_{16}
(see
[
4
]
).
We define the central product
L_{1}\ast L_{2}
of two loops with center
Z
in the same manner as in groups (see
[
3
, Chapter 2, Section 5]
), i.e.,
L_{1}\times L_{2}
modulo
\{(x,x^{-1})|x\in Z\}
.
Note that the central product
L_{1}\ast\dots\ast L_{m}
of
m
Cayley-Dickson loops
L_{i}=(\gamma_{i,1},\dots,\gamma_{i,n})_{Z}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, is also the multiplicative subloop of the corresponding tensor product
A_{1}\otimes_{F}\dots\otimes_{F}A_{m}
over
F
of the Cayley-Dickson algebras
A_{i}=(\gamma_{i,1},\dots,\gamma_{i,n})_{F}
generated by the generators of these algebras and
Z
. All the loops mentioned above are di-associative (that is, every two elements generate an associative subgroup), so they are ”close” to being groups in some sense.

In this paper, we study the commutators and centralizers of central products of Cayley-Dickson loops.
This study has at least two immediate implications:

(1)

We show that the chance of a random commutator in
L_{1}\ast L_{2}
approach 1 as
n
approaches
\infty
when
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are
n
-fold Cayley-Dickson loops over
Z
of
|Z|<\infty
.

(2)

We show that if
D_{1},\dots,D_{m},E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
are
n
-fold Cayley-Dickson loops over
Z
such that
n\geqslant 3
and
D_{1}\ast\dots\ast D_{m}\cong E_{1}\ast\dots\ast E_{m}
, then there exists
\sigma\in S_{m}
such that
D_{k}\cong E_{\sigma(k)}
for all
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.

Note that (1) is related to a classical question about groups, where “the commuting probability” or “commutativity degree”, which means the chance of a random commutator to be trivial in a finite non-abelian group, is bounded from above by
\frac{5}{8}
. For loops, intuitively, there should not be an upper bound (strictly smaller than 1). See
[
6
]
for a similar discussion about the associativity degree of Moufang loops.
Item (2) is related to questions about automorphisms of Cayley-Dickson loops, as were studied in
[
4
]
and
[
2
]
, and serves as a loop analogue (at least for
n\geqslant 3
) of
[
8
]
. We also study the associativity degree of the
n
-fold Cayley-Dickson Loops
(-1,\dots,-1)_{Z}
when
Z
is finite.

3.
Commutants and Commutators

Given a loop
L
and
x\in L
, define the commutant as
C_{L}(x):=\{y\in L|xy=yx\}
. The commutator
[x,y]
of
x,y\in L
is the unique element such that
(xy)=[x,y](yx)
.

Let
D_{1},\dots,D_{m}
be
n
-fold Cayley-Dickson loops over
Z
. Note that each
D_{i}
possesses a surjective homomorphism from
D_{i}
to
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{n}
sending the
k
-th generator of
D_{i}
to
e_{k}
for all
k=1,\dots,n
, and the kernel is clearly
Z
. Hence,
D_{i}/Z\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{n}
. For
A=D_{1}\ast\dots\ast D_{m}
, we obtain in a similar way an isomorphism
A/Z\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{mn}
.
The finiteness of
A/Z
as a set enables us to carry out the following calculations.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
D_{1},\dots,D_{m}
be
n
-fold Cayley-Dickson loops over
Z
and let
x\in D_{k}\setminus Z
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. Then,
C_{A}(x)/Z
is of cardinality
2^{(m-1)n+1}=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}2^{mn}=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\cdot|A/Z|
.

Proof.

This readily follows from the fact that
C_{D_{k}}(x)/Z
is of dimension
1
,
D_{k}/Z
is of dimension
n
and the definition of the central product.
∎

We say that an element
y\in A
is of rank
k
if:

(1)

y=x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}
;

(2)

x_{i_{j}}\in D_{i_{j}}\setminus Z
for all
j=1,\dots,k
and;

(3)

i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{k}
.

And denote it by
\operatorname{rank}_{A}(y)=k
. If
y\in Z
, then
\operatorname{rank}_{A}(y):=0
. Note that this is well defined, as we can omit brackets and change the order of the
x
’s as we wish, because all associators and commutators vanish.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
D_{1},\dots,D_{m}
be
n
-fold Cayley-Dickson loops over
Z
,
y\in A
and
\operatorname{rank}_{A}(y)=k
.
Then
\frac{|C_{A}(y)/Z|}{2^{mn}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(2p-1)^{k}
, where
p=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}
.

Proof.

Set
b_{k}=\frac{1}{2^{mn}}|C_{A}(y)/Z|
.
Let us prove by induction on
k
. The base case is covered by the previous lemma. Assume it is true for
k-1
. Write
y=x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}
.
Let
\tilde{a}\in A/Z
. Note that
[y,\tilde{a}]=[x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{k-1}},\tilde{a}]\cdot[x_{i_{k}},\tilde{a}]
, so
\tilde{a}\in C_{A}(y)
if and only if
[x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{k-1}},\tilde{a}]=[x_{i_{k}},\tilde{a}]=1
or
[x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{k-1}},\tilde{a}]=[x_{i_{k}},\tilde{a}]=-1
. The probability of the former is
pb_{k-1}
and the probability of the latter is
(1-p)(1-b_{k-1})
. So
b_{k}=pb_{k-1}+(1-p)(1-b_{k-1})=(1-p)+(2p-1)b_{k-1}
, and by plugging the induction assumption
b_{k-1}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(2p-1)^{k-1}
we are done.
∎

When
|Z|<\infty
, one can see that

(
\clubsuit
)

\#\{x\in A{\,:\ \,}\operatorname{rank}_{A}(x)=k\}=|Z|\cdot\binom{m}{k}(2^{n}-1)^{k}.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
x,y\sim\operatorname{Unif}(L)
where
L=D_{1}\ast D_{2}
is the central product of two
n
-fold Cayley-Dickson loops over
Z
with
|Z|<\infty
. Then
\mathbb{P}_{c}(L)=\mathbb{P}(xy=yx)=1-6\cdot\frac{1}{2^{n}}+22\cdot\frac{1}{4^{n}}-24\cdot\frac{1}{8^{n}}+8\cdot\frac{1}{16^{n}}
.

Proof.

Note that
|L|=|Z|\cdot 2^{2n}
, and
xy=yx
in
L
iff
[x][y]=[y][x]
in
L/Z
. Recall that
p=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}
. By Theorem
3.2
and (

♣

‣
3
),

\mathbb{P}(xy=yx)=\sum_{i=0}^{2}\mathbb{P}(xy=yx|\operatorname{rank}_{A}(x)=i)\cdot\mathbb{P}(\operatorname{rank}_{A}(x)=i)=

=\frac{|Z|}{|A|}(1+p\cdot 2\cdot(2^{n}-1)+(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(2p-1)^{2})\cdot(2^{n}-1)^{2})=

=1-6\cdot\frac{1}{2^{n}}+22\cdot\frac{1}{4^{n}}-24\cdot\frac{1}{8^{n}}+8\cdot\frac{1}{16^{n}}

∎

Corollary 3.4
.

There exist a sequence of loops
\{\mathcal{K}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that the commutativity degree approaches 1, i.e.,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{c}(\mathcal{K}_{n})=1
.

Proof.

Take
\mathcal{K}_{n}
to be as in the previous corollary and
n\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

This demonstrates how versatile loops are, even rather natural ones, compared to groups in terms of their commutativity degree.

Remark 3.5
.

If we consider
D_{1},\dots,D_{m}
,
n
-fold Cayley-Dickson loops over
Z
,
A_{m}=D_{1}\ast\cdots\ast D_{m}
, fix
n
and take
m\rightarrow\infty
, then we get
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{c}(A_{m})=\frac{1}{2}
, just as it is in the associative case, where we take
n=2
.

We now turn our concern to understanding automorphisms of these objects, when
n\geqslant 3
.

Theorem 3.6
.

If
D_{1},\dots,D_{m},E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
are
n
-fold Cayley-Dickson loops over
Z
with
n\geqslant 3
and
D_{1}\ast\dots\ast D_{m}\cong E_{1}\ast\dots\ast E_{m}
, then there exists
\sigma\in S_{m}
for which
D_{j}\cong E_{\sigma(j)}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be the automorphism. First of all, notice that the sequence
c_{k}=|b_{k}-\frac{1}{2}|=\frac{1}{2}|2p-1|^{k}
is strictly decreasing, since
p=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}
and
n\geqslant 3
. Hence,
\{b_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
from Theorem
3.2
has no repetitions. Thus,
\varphi
preserves the rank. Let
x\in D_{j}
,
\operatorname{rank}_{D}(x)=1
, for some
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. Then
\varphi(x)\in E_{k}
, for some
k
, and it is of rank
1
. The only elements anti-commuting with
x
are
D_{j}\setminus Z\langle x\rangle
, and the only elements anti-commuting with
\varphi(x)
are
E_{k}\setminus Z\langle\varphi(x)\rangle
, so
\varphi(D_{j}\setminus Z\langle x\rangle)=E_{k}\setminus Z\langle\varphi(x)\rangle
, and altogether we get that
\varphi(D_{j})=E_{k}
. So there exists
\sigma\in S_{m}
for which
D_{j}{\,\cong\,}E_{\sigma(j)}
.
∎

4.
Associativity Degree

In
[
6
]
it was shown that the associativity degree of a finite non-associative Moufang loop is bounded from above by
\frac{43}{64}
.
Here we study the associativity degree of the
n
-fold Cayley Dickson loops
L_{n}=(-1,\dots,-1)_{Z}
, for
|Z|<\infty
. It is important to note that the order of an
n
-fold Cayley-Dickson loop is
2^{n}|Z|
.
In this context, we define the associativity degree, denoted by
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
, to be the chance that for a random triple of generators
(x,y,z)
chosen from
L_{n}\times L_{n}\times L_{n}
with uniform distribution,
\langle x,y,z\rangle
is a group. This coincides with the definition in
[
6
]
for Moufang loops, where this is equivalent to saying that
(xy)z=x(yz)
because of Moufang’s theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

The associativity degree of
L_{n}
is
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}(L_{n})=\frac{7\cdot 4^{n}-14\cdot 2^{n}+8}{8^{n}}
.

Proof.

It amounts to counting the number of triples generating a subgroup.
If
a\in Z
, then
b
and
c
can be any elements. We have
2^{2n}\cdot|Z|
such triples.
If
a\not\in Z
(and there are
2^{n}\cdot|Z|-|Z|
such elements), we consider two cases:
b\in\langle a,Z\rangle
and
b\not\in\langle a,Z\rangle
. Note that
|\langle a,Z\rangle|=2|Z|
.
If
b\in\langle a,Z\rangle
, then
c
can be any element, and there are
|Z|(2^{n}-1)\cdot 2|Z|\cdot 2^{n}|Z|
such triples.
If
b\not\in\langle a,Z\rangle
, then
c
has to belong to
\langle a,b,Z\rangle
, and there are
|Z|(2^{n}-1)\cdot|Z|(2^{n}-2)\cdot 4|Z|
such triples. After summing everything and deividing by
|L_{n}|^{3}
, we finally get that
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{A}}(L_{n})=\frac{7\cdot 4^{n}-14\cdot 2^{n}+8}{8^{n}}
.
∎

For example, when
n=2
,
\frac{7\cdot 4^{2}-14\cdot 2^{2}+8}{8^{2}}=\frac{64}{64}=1
, as expected,
and when
n=3
,
\frac{7\cdot 4^{3}-14\cdot 2^{3}+8}{8^{3}}=\frac{344}{512}=\frac{43}{64}
, as was already found in
[
6
]
.
