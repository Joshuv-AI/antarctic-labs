Interpreting the apparent benefit of thoracic radiotherapy in metastatic NSCLC: patient selection and analytical issues remain important—a comment on Xue et al
Authors: Lili Qin, Shasha Wang, Haifeng Qin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThoracic radiotherapy (TRT) has been proposed as a potentially beneficial adjunct to first-line targeted therapy or chemoimmunotherapy in metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). This commentary discusses the retrospective study by Xue et al. and highlights several methodological issues that may influence interpretation of the reported survival benefit. Although the observed improvement in overall survival is clinically encouraging, imbalances in metastatic burden, inconsistent incorporation of key prognostic variables, and limited maturity of survival data may introduce residual confounding. Additional concerns include the causal interpretation of multivariable models, overlapping definitions of pulmonary toxicity, time-dependent adverse events, and unadjusted subgroup analyses. Overall, TRT may benefit carefully selected patients, but prospective studies and robust real-world analyses using causal-inference methods are needed to define optimal candidates and ensure safe implementation.Keywords: Thoracic radiotherapy, Metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, Chemoimmunotherapy, Real-world evidence

Introduction

We read with great interest the retrospective cohort study by Xue et al., which explored the addition of thoracic radiotherapy (TRT) to first‑line targeted therapy or chemoimmunotherapy in metastatic non‑small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) (Xue et al. 2026). The authors are to be commended for addressing a clinically relevant question and for providing detailed subgroup analyses that may inform treatment personalisation. Their finding that TRT prolonged overall survival (OS) in both targeted therapy and chemoimmunotherapy groups is encouraging and warrants further investigation. Nevertheless, we would like to raise several methodological concerns that may affect the interpretation and generalisability of their results.

First, baseline comparability between the TRT and non-TRT groups remains a major concern. Although many demographic variables were balanced, clinically important prognostic factors differed significantly, including the number of metastatic sites, lung metastasis, and bone metastasis. In particular, the TRT group had a higher proportion of patients with a single metastatic site, suggesting a lower disease burden and a greater likelihood of oligometastatic disease. Notably, the Methods specified ECOG 0–1 for TRT eligibility, yet Table 1 reports 43.45% of TRT patients with ECOG ≥ 2. These characteristics are themselves strongly associated with longer survival and may partly explain the superior progression-free survival and overall survival observed in the TRT group. However, metastatic burden and metastatic site were not consistently incorporated into the multivariable survival models. Therefore, the observed benefit may reflect favourable patient selection rather than the independent effect of TRT.

Second, the multivariable modelling strategy may not fully support the causal interpretation of TRT benefit. The key clinical question is whether TRT independently improves outcomes after adequate adjustment for disease burden, molecular subtype, systemic therapy regimen, PD-L1 expression, and other relevant prognostic factors. However, the presented Cox models mainly evaluate prognostic factors within the overall cohort or TRT subgroup, rather than constructing a treatment-effect model with TRT as the central exposure variable. In addition, the overall survival models did not identify stable independent prognostic factors, which suggests limited explanatory power and possible residual confounding. More rigorous approaches, such as propensity score matching, inverse probability weighting, or landmark analysis, would strengthen the interpretation (Sun et al. 2026; Lien et al. 2024).

Third, the maturity of the overall survival data should be interpreted cautiously. The median follow-up was 24 months, whereas the reported median overall survival for the entire cohort was 60 months, and the median overall survival in several TRT subgroups was not reached. Under these circumstances, the tail of the Kaplan–Meier curve may depend on a relatively small number of patients at risk. Without detailed confidence intervals, censoring patterns, and sensitivity analyses, the magnitude of overall survival benefit may be unstable. This is particularly relevant in the chemoimmunotherapy cohort, where TRT improved overall survival but not progression-free survival (Tomiyoshi et al. 2024; Smith 2025; Xue et al. 2026).

Fourth, the safety and exploratory subgroup analyses require clearer methodological handling. Pulmonary toxicity was described using overlapping terms such as pneumonia, pneumonitis, radiation pneumonitis, and immune-related pneumonitis, but the diagnostic criteria and attribution process were not clearly differentiated. This is important because infection, radiation injury, immune-related toxicity, and tumour-related radiographic changes may have different clinical implications. Moreover, treatment-emergent adverse events such as pneumonitis or lymphopenia are time-dependent events, yet they appear to have been analysed as fixed covariates in Cox models. Multiple subgroup analyses across different TKIs and immune checkpoint inhibitors were also performed without adjustment for multiplicity, increasing the possibility of chance findings.

In summary, Xue et al. provide valuable real-world data suggesting that TRT may be beneficial for selected patients with metastatic NSCLC receiving modern systemic therapy. However, baseline imbalance, incomplete adjustment for metastatic burden, limited maturity of overall survival data, and methodological challenges in toxicity and subgroup analyses indicate that the findings should be considered exploratory. Future prospective studies or carefully designed real-world analyses using robust causal-inference methods are needed to clarify which patients truly benefit from TRT and how this strategy can be implemented safely.
