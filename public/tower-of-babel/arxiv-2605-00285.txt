The logarithmic leaf complex and foliated d-semistability
Maurício Corrêa, Pablo Perrella, Sebastián Velazquez

Abstract
. We study holomorphic foliations on normal crossings varieties arising as semistable degenerations. We do so by we exploring the notion of foliated d d -semistability using the language of logarithmic structures in the sense of Fontaine–Illusie. First, we identify both local and global obstructions to d d -semistability. In order to analyze the existence of smoothings, we develop a logarithmic deformation theory of foliations, and show that the corresponding moduli functor admits a versal hull.

1.
Introduction

The main goal of this paper is to introduce the notions of
d
-semistability and log smoothness in the context of foliated pairs and their moduli.

In light of recent advances in the moduli theory of foliations
[
23
]
, it has become increasingly clear that a satisfactory understanding of foliations on varieties with normal crossings and their deformation theory is essential, even if one was primarily interested in foliations on smooth varieties, since these objects arise naturally at the boundary of moduli problems. Moreover, given a family of foliated pairs
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{F})\to\Delta
whose general fiber is smooth, the Semistable Reduction Theorem produces, after a finite base
\Delta^{\prime}\to\Delta
, a birational model
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathscr{X}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}\times_{\Delta}\Delta^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{\prime}}

with smooth total space, reduced normal crossings central fiber, and agreeing with the pulled-back family away from the central fiber. Endowing this new model with the pullback foliation, one is naturally led to study the induced foliated structure on the central fiber and its
smoothing

(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\mathscr{F}})\to\Delta
. In this way, the problem of understanding families whose generic fiber is a smooth variety naturally leads to the study of “smoothings” of foliations on normal crossing varieties.
This motivates the following:

Question:
which foliations on varieties with normal crossings admit smoothings?

In the non-foliated setting, this question was first addressed in
[
8
]
, where the notion of
d
-semistability was introduced. It is not difficult to see that a variety admitting a smoothing as above is necessarily
d
-semistable; however, determining when the converse holds has led to a substantial body of interesting mathematics. These ideas were later further developed through the lens of logarithmic geometry (see
[
13
,
12
,
11
]
), which has proven to be particularly well-suited to this problem.

Loosely speaking, logarithmic geometric
can be viewed as an enriched version of algebraic geometry in which one equips a ringed space
\underline{X}
with a sheaf of monoids
\mathscr{M}_{X}
and a morphism

\alpha:\mathscr{M}_{X}\to\mathscr{O}_{X},

where the latter is regarded as a sheaf of monoids under multiplication. When no confusion arises, we will use the notation
\underline{X}
for the underlying space and reserve the symbol
X
for the logarithmic space
(\underline{X},\mathscr{M}_{X})
. This notion was first introduced in
[
12
]
, and gives rise to a geometric category closely parallel to that of classical algebraic geometry.
In this setting, there is a notion of log smoothness which behaves, in many respects, analogously to the usual notion of smoothness, allowing one to extend a wide range of results and geometric intuition to more singular spaces.
In the case of a variety with normal crossings, the existence of certain log smooth morphisms
X\to(\ast,\mathbb{N})
to the standard log point (see Example
2.6
) is equivalent to
\underline{X}
being
d
-semistable, thereby illustrating the convenience of this approach.

To a morphism of logarithmic spaces
X\to S
one can associate a relative tangent sheaf
T_{X/S}
, endowed with a Lie bracket and a forgetful morphism of sheaves of Lie algebras
T_{X/S}\to T_{\underline{X}/\underline{S}}
.
In the case of a semistable morphism
X\to(\ast,\mathbb{N})
, the forgetful map is an injection and hence it makes sense to say that a foliation
\mathscr{F}
on
\underline{X}
is
d
-semistable if
T_{\mathscr{F}}\subseteq T_{X/(\ast,\mathbb{N})}\subseteq T_{\underline{X}}
.
It is important to point out that, while varieties with normal crossings are automatically “locally semistable”
[
12
, Prop. 11.3]
, this is no longer the case once a foliation is added. Indeed, we show that local semistability imposes strong compatibility conditions on the foliations defined on the irreducible components.

The paper is organized as follows. We begin by recalling the notions from logarithmic geometry that will be needed in what follows.

Section 3 is devoted to the analysis of
d
-semistability. On the one hand, we exhibit different instances of local
d
-semistability and establish a compatibility relation for the Camacho–Sad indices of the induced foliations on the irreducible components.
With this in place, we turn to the study of global
d
-semistability. We show that the obstruction to global
d
-semistability lies in the cohomology group

H^{1}\big(\underline{X},\mathscr{O}^{\ast}_{\underline{X}/\mathscr{F}}/G_{\mathscr{F}}\big),

where
\mathscr{O}^{\ast}_{\underline{X}/\mathscr{F}}
is the sheaf of invertible first integrals of
\mathscr{F}
and
G_{\mathscr{F}}
the kernel of the morphism
\mathscr{O}^{\ast}_{\underline{X}/\mathscr{F}}\to\mathscr{O}_{D}^{\ast}
. Among other consequences, this shows that if the double locus is a leaf of
\mathscr{F}
, then the obstruction to
d
-semistability is of topological nature.

In Section 4 we develop a log-smooth deformation theory for singular log foliations. Inspired by the works of
[
11
,
9
]
, we show that the deformation theory of foliations on log smooth varieties is governed by a logarithmic analogue of the leaf complex and prove that versal hulls exist, provided that
\underline{X}
is proper.

Combining the results of Sections 3 and 4, we construct families of foliations that admit smoothings and lay the groundwork for a systematic study of this problem.

Preliminaries

Conventions

Along the way all monoids are assumed to be commutative and will have a neutral element. We will denote by
P^{\mathrm{gp}}
the Grothendieck group of
P
. We write
\mathbb{N}
for additive monoid of non-negative integer.

Definition 2.1
.

A monoid
P
is said to be

(1)

finitely generated
if there is a surjective homomorphism
\mathbb{N}^{k}\rightarrow P
for some
k\geqslant 1
,

(2)

integral
if the natural homomorphism
P\rightarrow P^{\mathrm{gp}}
is injective,

(3)

saturated
if
P
is integral and for each
x\in P^{\mathrm{gp}}
such that

for some integer
k\geqslant 1
, then

.

In order to simplify verbose statements, from now on all monoids will be finitely generated, integral and saturated.

On the other hand, all our spaces will be complex analytic and the sheaves on them will be considered with respect to their analytic topology.

2.1.
Logarithmic structures

We will begin by introducing the general notions on logarithmic geometry necessary for our purposes. For more complete expositions, the reader is referred to
[

,

,

.

Definition 2.2
.

A
pre-log structure
on a complex analytic space
\underline{X}
is a sheaf of monoids
\mathscr{M}_{X}
on
\underline{X}
and a multiplicative morphism of sheaves
\alpha:\mathscr{M}_{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\underline{X}}
. If the restriction
\alpha^{-1}(\mathscr{O}^{\ast}_{\underline{X}})\rightarrow\mathscr{O}^{\ast}_{\underline{X}}
is an isomorphism, we will say that
\mathscr{M}_{X}
is a
log structure
. The pair
(\underline{X},\mathscr{M}_{X})
will be called a
log space
, and will be denoted by
X
to distinguish it from its underlying space
\underline{X}
. We shall use the notation
\mathscr{O}_{X}
instead of
\mathscr{O}_{\underline{X}}
for simplicity.

Definition 2.3
.

A
morphism of log spaces

f:X\rightarrow Y
consists of a morphism between the underlying complex analytic spaces
f:\underline{X}\rightarrow\underline{Y}
together with a morphism of sheaves
f^{\flat}:f^{-1}\mathscr{M}_{Y}\rightarrow\mathscr{M}_{X}
making the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{-1}\mathscr{M}_{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{M}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{-1}\mathscr{O}_{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{O}_{X}}

commute. Two log structures
\mathscr{M}_{X}
and
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{X}
on
\underline{X}
are
equivalent
if there exists an isomorphism of log spaces
(\underline{X},\mathscr{M}_{X})\to(\underline{X},\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{X})
, i.e. an isomorphism of log spaces witch is the identity on
\underline{X}
.

Example 2.4
.

Complex analytic spaces
\underline{X}
can be naturally be viewed a log spaces. In fact, the inclusion
\mathscr{M}_{X}:=\mathscr{O}_{\underline{X}}^{\ast}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X}
is a log structure.

Example 2.5
.

Given a hypersurface
D
on a complex manifold
\underline{X}
, the subsheaf of monoids

given by those local functions

that do not vanish at any point of

is a log structure. We will denote the corresponding log space by
(X,D)
.

Given a pre-log structure
\alpha:\mathscr{M}_{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{X}
we can construct its
associated log structure

\mathscr{M}_{X}^{a}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{X}
as the log structure obtained as the push-out diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha^{-1}(\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\ast})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{M}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\ast}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{M}^{a}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{O}_{X}.}

This procedure allow us to construct new logarithmic structures in the following way. Given a monoid
P
, a homomorphism
P\rightarrow\Gamma(\underline{X},\mathscr{O}_{\underline{X}})
induces a pre-log structure
P_{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\underline{X}}
where
P_{X}
is a constant sheaf. Therefore we obtain an associated log structure
(P_{X})^{a}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{X}
as before.

Example 2.6
.

Given a monoid
Q
, the homomorphism
\alpha:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by
\alpha(x)=0
for each
1\neq x\in Q
, and
\alpha(1)=1
produces a logarithmic structure on the point

that will be denoted by
(\ast,Q)
. The
standard log point
is the log space
(\ast,\mathbb{N})
. As a matter of fact, any log structure on

is equivalent to one of this form
[

]
.

Example 2.7
.

This will be the leading log structure of this work. The homomorphism
\mathbb{N}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}/(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})
defined on the canonical basis of

by
e_{i}\mapsto\overline{x}_{i}/u_{i}
for some units

defines a log space

whose underlying space is the simple normal crossing germ
\underline{X}=\Spec\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}/(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})
.

Example 2.8
.

Given a commutative analytic ring
R
and a monoid
P
, there is a canonical homomorphism
P\rightarrow R[P]
to the monoid algebra
R[P]
that produces a log structure on
\underline{X}=\Spec R[P]
.

Example 2.9
.

Every toric variety
\underline{X}_{\Sigma}
associated to a fan
\Sigma\subseteq N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
carries a natural logarithmic structure
\mathscr{M}_{X_{\Sigma}}
. Indeed, this variety admits an open cover by affine toric varieties of the form
U_{\sigma}=\Spec\mathbb{C}[M\cap\sigma^{\vee}]
, where
\sigma\in\Sigma
and
M=\Hom(N,\mathbb{Z})
is the dual lattice. The logarithmic structures on each

, induced by the monoid algebras as in the previous example, glue to define
\mathscr{M}_{X_{\Sigma}}
.

Definition 2.10
.

A
chart
of a log space
X
is a morphism
P\rightarrow\Gamma(X,\mathscr{M}_{X})
with

a monoid such that the associated map
(P_{X})^{a}\rightarrow\mathscr{M}_{X}

All the examples of log spaces presented so far are fine log spaces. Consider for instance the log space
(X,D)
of induced by a divisor as in Example

. Locally on
\underline{X}
, the hypersurface

can be described as the zero locus of some prime elements

. The morphism
\mathbb{N}^{k}\rightarrow\Gamma(U,\mathscr{O}_{X})
induced by those functions produce the desired chart on
\mathscr{M}_{(X,D)}|_{U}
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
f:\underline{X}\rightarrow\underline{Y}
be a morphism of complex analytic spaces. Given a log structure
\mathscr{M}_{Y}
on
\underline{Y}
, we the define the
pullback

f^{\ast}\mathscr{M}_{Y}
as the log structure on
\underline{X}
associated to the pre-log structure
f^{-1}\mathscr{M}_{Y}\rightarrow f^{-1}\mathscr{O}_{Y}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{X}
. A morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
of log spaces is

if
f^{\flat}:f^{\ast}\mathscr{M}_{Y}\rightarrow\mathscr{M}_{X}

2.2.
Semistable logarithmic structures

In his seminal paper
[
8
]
, Friedman studied what properties inherit a semistable degeneration of smooth varieties. He recognized that the special fiber should satisfy certain topological and analytical constraints. Hence he introduced the notion of
d-semistablity
and proved that d-semistable K3 surfaces are semistable degenerations. With a deformation theoretical approach, Kawamata and Namikawa extended this result to d-semistable Calabi-Yau varieties
[
13
]
. They recognized that
d
-semistable varieties are, in terms of logarithmic geometry, those having a special type of logarithmic structure (see Theorem
2.13

below).

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\underline{X}
be a variety with normal crossings. A logarithmic structure
\mathscr{M}_{X}
on
\underline{X}
is of
semistable type
if there exists a morphism of log spaces
f:(\underline{X},\mathscr{M}_{X})\rightarrow(\ast,\mathbb{N})
such that locally on
X
we can find charts
\mathbb{N}^{r}\rightarrow\Gamma(U,\mathscr{M}_{X})
making the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Gamma(\ast,\mathscr{M}_{(\ast,\mathbb{N})})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{N}^{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Gamma(U,\mathscr{M}_{X})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\flat}}

commute, with the left vertical arrow being the diagonal map. Such morphism
f
will be called a
semistable map
. We will say that
\underline{X}
is
d-semistable
if it admits a logarithmic structure of semistable type.

From the definition itself, determining whether a given normal crossings variety admits a semistable logarithmic structure appears to be a subtle problem. Before moving on, we present a practical criterion to construct such structures in terms of the module
T^{1}_{\underline{X}}=\Ext^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\underline{X}},\mathscr{O}_{\underline{X}})
.

Let
\underline{X}=\Spec\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}/(x_{1}\dots x_{r})
be a normal
crossing germ with singular locus
D
. Denoting
X_{i}=V(x_{i})\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
, we have that the sheaves
I_{X_{i}}|_{D}
are locally free on
D
and generated by
\overline{x}_{i}
. Hence the sheaf

I_{X_{1}}|_{D}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{X_{r}}|_{D}

is also locally free of rank one and generated by
\overline{x}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\overline{x}_{r}
. By a straightforward computation
[
8
, Prop. 2.3]
, there exists a canonical isomorphism

T^{1}_{\underline{X}}\big|_{D}\simeq\big(I_{X_{1}}|_{D}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{X_{r}}|_{D}\big)^{\vee}.

Theorem 2.13

(
[
13
, Prop. 1.1]
)
.

A normal crossing variety
\underline{X}
with singular locus
D
is d-semistable if and only if
T_{\underline{X}}^{1}\big|_{D}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{D}
.

We briefly recall the proof, as we will need it in the next section. Assume first that
\underline{X}
admits a semistable log structure
\mathscr{M}_{X}
and take a semistable map
f:X\to(\ast,\mathbb{N})
. Let
\mathbb{N}^{r}\to\Gamma(U,\mathscr{M}_{X})
be a local chart as in Definition
2.12
, and denote
x_{i}=\alpha(e_{i})
. We claim that the local sections
\overline{x}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\overline{x}_{r}
constructed on each
U
glue to a nowhere-vanishing global section of
T^{1}_{\underline{X}}
. Indeed, a different choice of chart
\mathbb{N}^{r}\to\mathscr{M}_{X}
will induce some element
\overline{x}_{1}^{\prime}\otimes\cdots\otimes\overline{x}_{r}^{\prime}
such that
x_{i}=u_{i}x_{i}^{\prime}
and
\prod_{i=1}^{r}u_{i}=1
(by the good definition of the map
f^{b}
above), showing that the constructed sections of
T^{1}_{\underline{X}}
coincide on the overlaps. This proves the first implication. Reading the argument backwards we see that an isomorphism
(T^{1}_{X}\big|_{D})^{\vee}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{D}
induces a logarithmic structure of semistable type.

Example 2.14

(
[
8
]
)
.

Consider the “tetrahedron”
\underline{X}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}
obtained as the union of 4 planes
X_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}
in general position. This variety is no d-semistable because
T_{\underline{X}}^{1}=I_{X_{1}}|_{D}\otimes I_{X_{2}}|_{D}\otimes I_{X_{3}}|_{D}\otimes I_{X_{4}}|_{D}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{D}(4)
. However, the blow up of
\underline{X}
along 4 general points at each of the 6 irreducible components of
D
does admits such structure.

2.3.
Log derivations and differential forms

Definition 2.15
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a morphism of log spaces. The sheaf
T_{X/S}
of
relative log derivations
is the sheaf of germs of pairs
\theta=(v,\delta)
where
v:\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{X}
is a
f^{-1}\mathscr{O}_{S}
-linear derivation and
\delta:\mathscr{M}_{X}\to\mathscr{O}_{X}
is such that

(1)

\delta(ab)=\delta(a)+\delta(b)
,

(2)

\alpha(a)\delta(a)=v(\alpha(a))
,

(3)

\delta\circ f^{\flat}=0
.

In the logarithmic setting, the sheaf
T_{X/S}
in endowed with the relative Lie bracket that takes two log derivations
\theta_{1}=(v_{1},\delta_{1})
and
\theta_{2}=(v_{2},\delta_{2})
and gives

\big[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\big]:=\big([v_{1},v_{2}],v_{1}\delta_{2}-v_{2}\delta_{1}\big),

where
[v_{1},v_{2}]
is the relative Lie bracket of vector fields on
\underline{X}/\underline{S}

[
14
, Prop. 1.1.2]
.

Definition 2.16
.

The sheaf of log differentials relative to a morphism
f:X\rightarrow S
is the quotient

\Omega^{1}_{X/S}=\big(\Omega^{1}_{\underline{X}/\underline{S}}\oplus(\mathscr{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathscr{M}_{X}^{gp})\big)/K,

where
K
is the
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module generated by the expressions
(d\alpha(a),0)-(0,\alpha(a)\otimes a)
for
a\in\mathscr{M}_{X}
and
(0,1\otimes\varphi(b))
for
b\in f^{-1}\mathscr{M}_{S}
.

If the base
S
is the trivial log point then we will remove part of the subscript out of
T_{X/S}
and
\Omega^{1}_{X/S}
, and write instead
T_{X}
and
\Omega^{1}_{X}
respectively since those are the usual tangent and cotangent sheaves of a space.

Example 2.17
.

Consider the log space
(X,D)
associated to a normal crossing divisor as in Example
2.5
. The sheaf of logarithmic differentials of
(X,D)
is
\Omega^{1}_{(X,D)}=\Omega^{1}_{\underline{X}}(\log D)
, famously introduced by Deligne
[
5
]
. Similarly,
T_{(X,D)}=T_{\underline{X}}(-\log D)
is the sheaf of derivations

such that
v(I_{D})\subseteq I_{D}
.

Example 2.18
.

Suppose
X_{\Sigma}
is the log space associated to a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial toric variety without torus factors. Then we have isomorphisms

\Omega^{1}_{X_{\Sigma}}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{X_{\Sigma}}\otimes N\quad\text{and}\quad T_{X_{\Sigma}}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{X_{\Sigma}}\otimes M,

where
M
and
N
are the character and 1-parameter subgroup lattices of
X_{\Sigma}
respectively.

Example 2.19
.

Let
\underline{X}=\Spec\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}/(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})
be endowed with log structure
\mathscr{M}_{X}
defined by
\mathbb{N}^{r}\to\mathscr{O}_{X}
sending
e_{i}\mapsto\overline{x}_{i}/u_{i}
as in Example
2.7
. If we write
\hat{x}_{i}=x_{1}\cdots\hat{x}_{i}\cdots x_{n}
, then the usual (non-logarithmic) module of Kähler differentials is

\Omega^{1}_{\underline{X}}=\mathscr{O}_{\underline{X}}dx_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\underline{X}}dx_{n}/(\widehat{x}_{1}dx_{1}+\cdots+\widehat{x}_{r}dx_{r})\\

and therefore the tangent module is generated by the following derivations

\displaystyle T_{\underline{X}}=\big\langle x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}},\dots,x_{r}\partial_{x_{r}},\partial_{x_{r+1}},\dots,\partial_{x_{n}}\big\rangle\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{\underline{X}}\partial_{x_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\underline{X}}\partial_{x_{n}}.

On the other hand, we can also compute differentials and derivations relative to the morphism
\underline{X}\rightarrow(\ast,\mathbb{N})
associated to the diagonal map
\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r}
. We denote by
u=u_{1}\cdots u_{r}
the product of the former units. Therefore

\displaystyle\Omega^{1}_{X/(\ast,\mathbb{N})}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathscr{O}_{X}\frac{dx_{i}}{x_{i}}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=r+1}^{n}\mathscr{O}_{X}dx_{i}\Big/\Big(\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{dx_{r}}{x_{r}}-\frac{du}{u}\Big),

\displaystyle T_{X/(\ast,\mathbb{N})}

\displaystyle=\bigg\{v=\sum^{r}_{i=1}b_{i}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}+\sum^{n}_{i=r+1}a_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}\in T_{\underline{X}}:v(u)-\bigg(\sum^{r}_{i=1}b_{i}\bigg)u=0\bigg\}.

Remarkably, the logarithmic (co)tangent sheaves computed so far are locally free, even for log spaces whose underlying spaces are quite singular. This is an incarnation of smoothness in the logarithmic context. In fact, this one of the main reasons to work with log spaces: they provide a broader class of smooth objects.

Definition 2.20
.

A morphism of log spaces
T\rightarrow T^{\prime}
is an

if it is strict and the underlying morphism

. The

such that

.

Definition 2.21
.

A morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
between fine log spaces is

if the underlying map
f:\underline{X}\rightarrow\underline{Y}
is locally of finite presentation and it satisfies the following

: given a diagram of solid arrows
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

where the left vertical arrow is an infinitesimal thickening, locally there exist a dotted arrow making the previous triangles commute.

3.
Log foliations and
d
-semistability

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X\rightarrow(\ast,Q)
be a log smooth morphism from a fine log space
X
into the log point associated to some monoid
Q
. A
log (singular) foliation

\mathscr{F}
of codimension
q
on
X/(\ast,Q)
consists of a short exact sequence of coherent sheaves

0\rightarrow T_{\mathscr{F}}\rightarrow T_{X/(\ast,Q)}\rightarrow N_{\mathscr{F}}\rightarrow 0

such that
N_{\mathscr{F}}
is torsion-less of rank
q
and
[T_{\mathscr{F}},T_{\mathscr{F}}]\subseteq T_{\mathscr{F}}
. We will call
T_{\mathscr{F}}
and
N_{\mathscr{F}}
respectively the
tangent
and
normal sheaf
of the foliation
\mathscr{F}
.

Recall that a coherent sheaf
N
is
torsion-less
if the natural map
N\rightarrow N^{\vee\vee}
is a monomorphism. This is a replacement of the usual torsion free hypothesis in the singular setting. This allow us to equivalently represent a log foliation by an exact sequence of coherent sheaves

0\rightarrow I_{\mathscr{F}}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{X/(\ast,Q)}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{\mathscr{F}}\rightarrow 0

where
\Omega^{1}_{\mathscr{F}}

Example 3.2

Example 3.3

Consider a toric variety

To produce a wide range of examples, we explain below how to glue usual foliations on smooth varieties to obtain foliations on normal crossings singular spaces.

3.1.
Pushout foliations

We now move on to viewing foliations on varieties with normal crossings through the lens of the notions explained in the previous sections.
We will begin by briefly discussing the gluing process for foliations.

The gluing of schemes along subspaces corresponds to the notion of pushout. Although arbitrary pushouts may not exist in the same category, we do have an existence theorem that applies to our setting. By
[
7
, Theorem 5.4 and 7.1]
, given a family of smooth varieties
X_{i}
of dimension

with simple normal crossing divisors
D_{i}
and a family of isomorphisms

\varphi_{ij}:D_{ij}\subseteq D_{i}\to D_{ji}\subseteq D_{j}

\varphi_{ji}=\varphi_{ij}^{-1}
and the cocycle condition on the triple intersections, we can define the pushout scheme
\coprod_{D}X_{i}
, which is the variety corresponding to the gluing of the

along this data and will have normal crossings depending on the combinatorics of
\{\varphi_{ij}\}
. When no confusion arises, we will write
\underline{X}
instead of
\coprod_{D}X_{i}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

This follows straightforward from the computations made in Example

∎

Let

Lemma 3.5

Let

(1)

(2)

Proof.

respectively.

For the second assertion, notice that the hypotheses imply that

with

be a local

-invariant,

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a germ of semistable foliation on
\underline{X}=V(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
, and denote by
\mathscr{F}_{i}
the restriction of
\mathscr{F}
to each irreducible component
X_{i}=V(x_{i})

D_{i}=D\cap X_{i}
are
\mathscr{F}_{i}
-invariant, then their Camacho-Sad indexes satisfy the relations

CS(\mathscr{F}_{i},D_{ij},0)+CS(\mathscr{F}_{j},D_{ij},0)=r-2.

Proof.

By assumption, there exists a local semistable structure
(X,\mathscr{F})\to(\ast,\mathbb{N})
. Since
D
is a leaf of
\mathscr{F}
, there is a surjection

R:\bigwedge^{n-1}T_{\mathscr{F}}\to i_{*}\bigwedge^{n-1}T_{D}.

Let
v\in\bigwedge^{n-1}T_{\mathscr{F}}
be any element such that
R(v)
is nonzero along every component of
D
. The contraction of a top log-form
\Omega\in\Omega^{n}_{X/(\ast,\mathbb{N})}
yields a section
\omega\in\Omega^{1}_{X/(\ast,\mathbb{N})}(U)
for some
U
containing all generic points of
D
and such that
\omega(T_{\mathscr{F}})=0
.

Writing
\omega=a_{1}dx_{1}/x_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}dx_{r}/x_{r}+\eta
, the restriction of the foliation
\mathscr{F}
to
X_{i}
is induced by

\omega_{i}=(a_{1}-a_{i})\widehat{x}_{1i}dx_{1}+\cdots+(a_{r}-a_{i})\widehat{x}_{ri}dx_{r}+\widehat{x}_{i}\eta.

Consider the algebraic leaf
D_{ij}=V(x_{i},x_{j})\subseteq X_{i}
. Then the Camacho-Sad index of
\mathscr{F}_{i}
along
D_{ij}
is

\displaystyle CS(\mathscr{F}_{i},D_{ij})

\displaystyle=\Res\bigg(\frac{1}{(a_{j}-a_{i})\widehat{x}_{ij}}\Big(\sum_{k\neq i,j}(a_{k}-a_{i})\widehat{x}_{ijk}dx_{k}+\widehat{x}_{ij}\eta\Big)\Big|_{D_{ij}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\Res\bigg(\frac{1}{(a_{j}-a_{i})}\Big(\sum_{k\neq i,j}\frac{(a_{k}-a_{i})}{x_{k}}dx_{k}+\eta\Big)\Big|_{D_{ij}}\bigg)

In particular, comparing these residues along different components we get

\displaystyle CS(\mathscr{F}_{i},D_{ij})+CS(\mathscr{F}_{j},D_{ij})

\displaystyle=\Res\bigg(\frac{1}{(a_{j}-a_{i})}\Big(\sum_{k\neq i,j}\frac{(a_{k}-a_{i})+(a_{j}-a_{k})}{x_{k}}dx_{k}\Big)\Big|_{D_{ij}}\bigg)

3.3.
Criterion for foliated semistability

For a general complex space
\underline{X}
with cotangent complex

the coherent sheaf on
\underline{X}
defined by

T_{\underline{X}}^{i}=\Ext^{i}(L_{\underline{X}},\mathscr{O}_{\underline{X}}).

It is well-known that the sheaf of graded modules
T_{\underline{X}}^{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{i\geqslant 1}T_{\underline{X}}^{i}
is endowed with a structure of graded Lie algebra
[
20
]
. Coming back to our case of interest, let
\underline{X}
be a normal crossing variety. Then
T_{\underline{X}}^{i}=0
for
i\geqslant 2
because
\underline{X}
is a local complete intersection. As a consequence, the graded Lie algebra structure on
T_{\underline{X}}^{\bullet}=T_{\underline{X}}^{0}\oplus T_{\underline{X}}^{1}
is quite simple: besides the usual Lie concentrated in degree zero, it is completely determined by the bracket
[-,-]:T_{\underline{X}}^{0}\otimes T_{\underline{X}}^{1}\rightarrow T_{\underline{X}}^{1}
.

Locally on
\underline{X}
, this operation becomes very explicit
[
8
, Section 4]
. By the computations in
[
22
, Section 3.1]
, given a local embedding
\underline{X}=V(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
, we get an identification

where
\hat{x}_{i}=x_{1}\cdots\hat{x}_{i}\cdots x_{r}
. Then the bracket between the class of a function
g\in T_{\underline{X}}^{1}
and a vector field
v=\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{i}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}+\sum_{i>r}a_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}\in T_{\underline{X}}^{0}
is

(1)

[v,g]=v(g)-\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{i}\bigg)g.

It is clearly
\mathscr{O}_{\underline{X}}
-linear in the first variable and thus defines a
connection

\nabla
on
T^{1}_{\underline{X}}
.

.

\mathscr{F}
is a foliation on a normal crossing variety
\underline{X}

\mathscr{F}

It follows from Equation (

) and Example
2.19
.
∎

,
\overline{f}_{i}
,
\beta_{ji}
and
g_{i}
be the elements defined above. Then

g_{j}-g_{i}=[\theta_{ij},f_{j}]+f_{i}\beta_{ij},

where

is the inclusion defining
\mathscr{F}

Proof.

By pulling back the gluing data of
(\overline{X},\overline{\mathscr{F}})
and using Lemma

we get a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{\mathscr{F}_{A^{\prime}}}|_{U_{ij}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{\mathscr{F}_{A^{\prime}}}|_{U_{ij}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{X_{A^{\prime}}/{(A^{\prime},Q)}}|_{U_{ij}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{X_{A^{\prime}}/{(A^{\prime},Q)}}|_{U_{ij}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{j}+g_{j}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1+\beta_{ij}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{i}+g_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1+[\theta_{ij},-]}

Its commutativity of the diagram is equivalent to the relation

which together with the fact that

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a subalgebra of a Lie algebra
\mathscr{G}
and
0\to J\to A^{\prime}\to A\to 0
be a small extension. If
\mathscr{H}_{A}\subseteq\mathscr{G}_{A}
is a deformation of
\mathscr{H}\subseteq\mathscr{G}
and
\mathscr{G}_{A^{\prime}}
is an extension of
\mathscr{G}_{A}
over
A^{\prime}
, then the obstruction to extend
\mathscr{H}_{A}
to some
\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathscr{G}_{A^{\prime}}
lies in the group
H^{2}(\mathscr{H},\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{H})\otimes J
, where
H^{2}
stands for the second Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the
\mathscr{H}
-module
\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{H}
.

Proof.

Let us first observe that since
\mathscr{H}
is locally free, so is
\mathscr{H}_{A}
. This means that we can locally extend the morphism
of
\mathscr{O}_{X_{A}}
-modules
f_{A}:\mathscr{H}_{A}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{G}_{A}
to some
\mathscr{O}_{X_{A^{\prime}}}
-linear arrow
f_{A^{\prime}}:\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{G}_{A^{\prime}}
.
In the same manner, we can also extend the Lie bracket

[\,,]_{A}:\bigwedge^{2}\mathscr{H}_{A}\to\mathscr{H}_{A}

to some
\mathscr{O}_{X_{A^{\prime}}}
-linear

\{\,,\}_{A^{\prime}}:\bigwedge^{2}\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}\to\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}.

If we were too lucky, these extensions would satisfy the equation

f_{A^{\prime}}\{x,y\}=[f_{A^{\prime}}(x),f_{A^{\prime}}(y)]_{A^{\prime}},

providing an extension of
\mathscr{H}_{A}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{G}_{A}
to
A^{\prime}
.
If this was not the case, we could aim to modify them to force this equality. We will now see that the obstruction to do so lies in the claimed group.

Let us consider the map
b:\bigwedge^{2}\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}\to J\otimes_{A^{\prime}}(\mathscr{G}_{A^{\prime}}/\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}})\simeq J\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{H}

defined as

b(x,y)=\overline{[f_{A^{\prime}}(x),f_{A^{\prime}}(y)]_{A^{\prime}}-f_{A^{\prime}}(\{x,y\}_{A^{\prime}})}.

Since
m_{A^{\prime}}\cdot J=0
,
b
factors through some
\overline{b}:\bigwedge^{2}\mathscr{H}\to J\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{H}
:

there are natural isomorphisms

\Hom_{A^{\prime}}\bigg(\bigwedge^{k}\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}},J\otimes_{A^{\prime}}\mathscr{G}_{A^{\prime}}/\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}\bigg)\simeq\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}\bigg(\bigwedge^{k}\mathscr{H},J\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{H}\bigg).

If
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in\mathscr{H}
then

\displaystyle\delta\overline{b}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})

\displaystyle=\overline{\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}(-1)^{i+1}[f(v_{i}),\overline{b}(\widehat{v}_{i})]+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\overline{b}([v_{i},v_{j}],v_{k})}

\displaystyle=\overline{[f(v_{1}),\overline{b}(v_{2},v_{3})]}-\overline{[f(v_{2}),\overline{b}(v_{1},v_{3})]}+\overline{[f(v_{3}),\overline{b}(v_{1},v_{2})]}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\overline{b}([v_{1},v_{2}],v_{3})+\overline{b}([v_{1},v_{3}],v_{2})-\overline{b}([v_{2},v_{3}],v_{1}).

In order to compute this, we can pick liftings
v^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}
of
v_{i}\in\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{m}_{A^{\prime}}\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}
, so that

\displaystyle\big[f(v_{i}),\overline{b}(v_{j},v_{k})\big]

\displaystyle=\overline{\big[f_{A^{\prime}}(v^{\prime}_{i}),[f_{A^{\prime}}(v^{\prime}_{j}),f_{A^{\prime}}(v_{k})]_{A^{\prime}}-f_{A^{\prime}}(\{v^{\prime}_{j},v^{\prime}_{k}\}_{A^{\prime}})\big]}_{A^{\prime}}.

Putting the last two expressions together and using the fact that
[\,,]_{A^{\prime}}
satisfies de Jacobi identity gives
\delta\overline{b}=0
.

If we had chosen a different lifting for the bracket, say
\{\,,\}^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}}=\{\,,\}_{A^{\prime}}+\eta
for some
\eta:\bigwedge^{2}\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}\to J\otimes_{A^{\prime}}\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}
, then

\displaystyle[f_{A^{\prime}}(x),f_{A^{\prime}}(y)]_{A^{\prime}}-f_{A^{\prime}}\{x,y\}

\displaystyle=[f_{A^{\prime}}(x),f_{A^{\prime}}(y)]_{A^{\prime}}-f_{A^{\prime}}(\{x,y\}^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=[f_{A^{\prime}}(x),f_{A^{\prime}}(y)]_{A^{\prime}}-f_{A^{\prime}}(\{x,y\}_{A^{\prime}})-f_{A^{\prime}}(\eta(x,y))

\displaystyle=b(x,y)-f_{A^{\prime}}(\eta(x,y)),

which indeed coincides with
b
after passing to the quotient
\mathscr{G}_{A^{\prime}}/\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}
.

On the other hand, a different choice of
f_{A^{\prime}}
changes
\overline{b}
by a Chevalley–Eilenberg boundary: for a morphism of the form
f^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}}=f_{A^{\prime}}+h
, with
h:\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}\to J\otimes\mathscr{G}_{A^{\prime}}
, we get

\displaystyle b^{\prime}(x,y)

\displaystyle=[f^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}}(x),f^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}}(y)]_{A^{\prime}}-f^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}}(\{x,y\}_{A^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=[f_{A^{\prime}}(x)+h(x),\,f_{A^{\prime}}(y)+h(y)]_{A^{\prime}}-f_{A^{\prime}}(\{x,y\}_{A^{\prime}})-h(\{x,y\}_{A^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=b(x,y)+[f_{A^{\prime}}(x),h(y)]_{A^{\prime}}-[f_{A^{\prime}}(y),h(x)]_{A^{\prime}}-h(\{x,y\}_{A^{\prime}}).

On
J\otimes\mathscr{G}_{A^{\prime}}/\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}\simeq J\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{H})
, this becomes

\bar{b}^{\prime}(x,y)=\bar{b}(x,y)+\overline{[f(x),h(y)]}-\overline{[f(y),h(x)]}-\overline{h([x,y])},

because
\{x,y\}_{A^{\prime}}
reduces to
[x,y]\in\mathscr{H}
.
Observe that the right-hand side is

\bar{b}^{\prime}(x,y)=\bar{b}(x,y)+(\delta\bar{h})(x,y)
,
where
\bar{h}:\mathscr{H}\to J\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{H})
denotes the class of
h
. This shows that the obstruction is a well-defined class in

H^{2}(\mathscr{H},\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{H})\otimes J.

Moreover, this obstruction is zero precisely when
\overline{b}=\delta\overline{h}
for some
\overline{h}:\mathscr{H}\to J\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{H})
. In this case, choosing an
\mathscr{O}_{X_{A^{\prime}}}
-lifting

h:\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}\to J\otimes_{A^{\prime}}\mathscr{G}_{A^{\prime}},

we see by the argument above that
f^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}}=f_{A^{\prime}}-h

is a
\mathscr{O}_{X_{A^{\prime}}}
-linear morphism that satisfies

[f^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}}(x),f^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}}(y)]_{A^{\prime}}\in f^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}}(\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}})

for every
x,y
, hence defining an extension of
\mathscr{H}_{A^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathscr{G}_{A^{\prime}}
of
\mathscr{H}_{A}\subseteq\mathscr{G}_{A}
.
∎

, we see that
Def^{ls}_{(X,F)}
is less (hence equally) obstructed than the functor of log smooth deformations of
X\to(\ast,Q)
. In particular, if
X

admits a formal deformation then so does
(X,\mathscr{F})
.

On the other hand, it is important to point out that that hypothesis on
T_{\mathscr{F}}
being locally free was indeed used in the construction of the obstructions. A priori, there is no guarantee that even the sheaf
T_{\mathscr{F}_{A}}
itself extends to
X_{A}^{\prime}
. These extra obstructions should appear at the level of
\sExt^{1}(T_{\mathscr{F}},N_{\mathscr{F}})
. The leaf complex does not seem to perceive these subtleties, and therefore we do not expect it to provide an obstruction space in full generality.

We end the analysis of the log-smooth deformations of
(X,\mathscr{F})\to(\ast,Q)
by showing the existence of versal hulls in the sense of
[
21
]
(i.e., a formal ring
R\in\widehat{\mathscr{A}}_{Q}
and a smooth morphism
h_{R}\to Def^{ls}_{(X,\mathscr{F})}
inducing an isomorphism at the level of tangent spaces).

Theorem 4.15
.

If
\underline{X}
is proper, then functor
Def^{ls}_{(X,\mathscr{F})}
admits a versal hull.

Proof.

We will do this by showing that
Def^{ls}_{(X,\mathscr{F})}
satisfies conditions the hypotheses of Schlessinger’s representability theorem
[
21
, Theorem 2.11]
. We will begin by noting that the groups
\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\mathscr{L}^{\bullet}(\mathscr{F}))
are finite-dimensional since they can be computed through a spectral sequence whose second page consists of the cohomology groups of the (coherent) sheaves appearing in the leaf complex.

In order to show that condition
H_{1}
(resp.
H_{2}
) holds, we need to check that if
(X_{A^{\prime}},\mathscr{F}_{A^{\prime}})\to(A^{\prime},Q)
and
(X_{A^{\prime\prime}},\mathscr{F}_{A^{\prime\prime}})\to(A^{\prime\prime},Q)
(resp. take
A^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^{2})
)
are smooth liftings of
(X_{A},\mathscr{F}_{A})\to(A,Q)
, then there exists a common (resp. unique) lifting over
(B=A^{\prime}\times_{A}A^{\prime\prime},Q)
. Since by
[
11
, Theorem 8.7]
the corresponding non-foliated functor admits a hull, we know that these conditions hold for the log-smooth maps only.

Let
X_{B}\to(B,Q)
be a (resp. the unique) lifting. We are left to analyze the existence of a lifting
\mathscr{F}_{B}
. To do this, observe that by construction the coherent sheaf
T_{X_{B}/(B,Q)}
restricts to the corresponding relative tangent sheaves over
A^{\prime}
and
A^{\prime\prime}
. By
[
18
, Theoréme 1]
, the functor assigning to each
f:S\to B
the set of subsheaves of
f^{*}T_{X_{B}/(B,Q)}
on
f^{*}X_{B}
with flat cokernel and restricting to
T_{\mathscr{F}}
on the central fiber is representable, and hence conditions
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
holds in this context, finishing the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.16
.

Let
X
be a proper variety with normal crossings and
(X,\mathscr{F})\to(\ast,\mathbb{N})
be a log smooth morphism, with
T_{\underline{\mathscr{F}}}
locally free. If there exists a formal deformation
(\widehat{X},\widehat{\mathscr{F}})\to(\Spec(\mathbb{C}[[t]]),\mathbb{N})
then
(X,\mathscr{F})
admits a smoothing. In particular, if
(\underline{X},\underline{\mathscr{F}})
is
d
-semistable and
\mathbb{H}^{2}(X,\mathscr{L}^{\bullet}(\mathscr{F}))=0
for the corresponding logarithmic structure, then it admits a smoothing.

Proof.

Let us denote
\mathscr{X}\to K
the Kuranishi space of
X
, and
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{H})\to D
the universal subsheaf of
T_{\mathscr{X}/B}
mentioned above.
Since the formal deformation is log smooth, for every
n>0
we have inclusions

T_{\mathscr{F}_{n}}\subseteq T_{X_{n}/(\Spec(\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^{n})),\mathbb{N})}\subseteq T_{X_{n}/\Spec(\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^{n}))}.

As a consequence, if we forget the logarithmic structures then the data of
\widehat{\underline{X}}
and
\widehat{\underline{\mathscr{F}}}
yields a morphism
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[[t]])\to D
. By Artin’s Approximation Theorem
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
, there exists a convergent family
(X_{\Delta},\mathscr{F}_{\Delta})\to\Delta
coinciding with the formal family at first order, i.e. a smoothing of
(\underline{X},\underline{\mathscr{F}})
.
The second claim is straightforward from Theorem
4.12
.
∎

Example 4.17
.

Let
\underline{X}=X_{1}\sqcup_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}X_{2}
be the gluing of two
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundles
X_{i}\to C_{i}
along a fiber
D
, where
C
is a smooth curve.
Let
\mathscr{F}_{1}
and
\mathscr{F}_{2}
the foliations induced by the rulings of
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
respectively. The pushout foliation
\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{F}_{1}\sqcup_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathscr{F}_{2}
is d-semistable thanks to Proposition
3.15
. Let us denote
X
the corresponding logarithmic variety, smooth of the log point. Observe that Lemma
3.5
implies that
T_{\mathscr{F}}
coincides with the line bundle
T_{\mathscr{F}_{1}}\times_{D}T_{\mathscr{F}_{2}}
.

Gluing the curves
C_{i}
along the distinguished points we get a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\pi:X\rightarrow C=C_{1}\sqcup_{p}C_{2}
inducing the foliation
\mathscr{F}
, with log-normal sheaf
\pi^{\ast}T_{C/(\ast,\mathbb{N})}
.
Let us analyze when
H^{1}\big(X,\sHom(T_{\mathscr{F}},T_{X/(\ast,\mathbb{N})}/T_{\mathscr{F}})\big)=0
. By Remark
4.14
this will imply that the obstruction to extending a log smooth deformation of
(X,\mathscr{F})
maps injectively to the obstruction to only deforming
X
, and hence
\mathscr{F}
will deform along any formal deformation of
X
. Since
X
admits a smoothing
[
17
, Chapter V]
, then by Corollary
4.16
this will guarantee the existence of a smoothing of
(\underline{X},\underline{\mathscr{F}})
.

Observe that by adjunction

H^{1}\big(X,\sHom(T_{\mathscr{F}},T_{X/(\ast,\mathbb{N})}/T_{\mathscr{F}})\big)=H^{1}\big(C,\sHom(\pi_{\ast}T_{X/C},T_{C/(\ast,\mathbb{N})})\big),

where in the last equality we used that
R\pi_{\ast}\mathscr{O}_{X}=\mathscr{O}_{C}
. Since
C
is Cohen-Macaulay, the sheaf
\sHom(\pi_{\ast}T_{X/C},T_{C/(\ast,\mathbb{N})})
is locally free and
\Omega^{1}_{C/(\ast,\mathbb{N})}
is a dualizing sheaf for
C

[
6
, Proposition 7.70]
. With this in mind, Serre-Grothendieck duality implies that

H^{1}\big(C,\sHom(\pi_{\ast}T_{X/C},T_{C/(\ast,\mathbb{N})})\big)=H^{0}\big(C,\pi_{\ast}T_{X/C}\otimes(\Omega^{1}_{C/(\ast,\mathbb{N})})^{\otimes 2}\big)^{\vee}.

A global section of this bundle corresponds to global sections of its restrictions to the components
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
that coincide along
D
. These new sheaves are
\pi_{*}T_{X_{i}/C_{i}}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{C_{i}}(\log\,p)^{\otimes 2}
, and it may be the case that they do not admit global sections.

If, for instance, the fibrations are Hirzebruch surfaces
X_{i}=\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(n))\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, from the Euler exact sequence of a projective bundle follows that

\pi_{\ast}T_{X_{i}/\mathbb{P}^{1}}\simeq\sEnd(E)/\mathscr{O}_{C_{i}}\operatorname{id}_{E}=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(n)\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-n).

But then

\pi_{*}T_{X_{i}/C_{i}}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{C}(\log\,p)^{\otimes 2}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-2)\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(n-2)\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-n-2),

showing that the desired cohomology group vanishes whenever
n=0,1
, i.e., when
X_{i}
is either
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
or
Bl_{p}\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Based on its non-foliated counterparts, we believe that the notions developed in this work are indeed necessary to approach existence of smoothings for normal crossings foliated pairs. However, in order to give a full answer to this issue one should restrict the analysis to a special family, for instance log Calabi-Yau foliations by means of the
T^{1}
-lifting technique
[
13
]
. Hopefully, this will be the subject of future work.
