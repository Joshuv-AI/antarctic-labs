Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

Calion Technologies, Inc. project team investigated various embodiments of the ionocaloric
cycle. Ionocaloric technology provides a new platform for all heating and cooling that is
zero-GWP, non-toxic, non-hazardous, and non-flammable. It is well-poised to replace vapor
compression technology which uses high-GWP refrigerants which are on track to account for
25% of our global warming budget. Developing the ionocaloric technology as a drop-in
replacement for vapor compression was the primary research thrust under this CRADA.

Summary of Research Results:

This project developed a performance map of an ionocaloric heat pump/chiller efficiency,
power density, and temperature lift. The largest engineering and scientific risks are associated
with the separation step, and various experiments were conducted to characterize the
thermophysical properties of the working fluid and the transport properties necessary for the
separation step. In parallel, the heat exchangers and recuperators were investigated to de-risk
system cost and reliability concerns associated with the transport of solid/liquid mixtures.
Success was determined by the feasibility of a system design aimed at staying under $200 / kW
of heat while maintaining an efficiency of at least 40% of Carnot with an overall system level
footprint of at least 100 W/L. This was rigorously evaluated in various controlled environments
to test the system’s ability to reach performance targets in practice and at cost, while de-risking
reliability concerns through cycling and stress-testing at upper and lower bound design
conditions. This involved rapid, iterative design and development of each sub-component, along
with proper design of experimental test beds to extract real world performance data.
