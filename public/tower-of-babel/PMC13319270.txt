Response to “Insomnia subtype heterogeneity in comorbid insomnia and sleep apnea: a missing piece in mortality risk assessment”
Authors: Sahrai Saeed, Ragnhild Stokke Lundetræ, Andrea Romarheim, Ingvild West Saxvig, Sverre Lehmann, Bjørn Bjorvatn
Published: 2026

We thank Dr Minzhi Yang and colleagues [1] for their interest in our recently published work [2]. In our study, insomnia symptoms were assessed using the validated Bergen Insomnia Scale (BIS), which is directly aligned with core insomnia criteria (difficulty initiating and maintaining sleep, early awakenings, daytime impairment, and duration/frequency) [3]. This makes it useful for identifying insomnia disorder rather than simply “poor sleep”. Because the BIS is diagnostic-criterion-oriented and brief, it performs well in large population surveys and in clinical screening. However, the BIS is a screening tool and does not constitute a full clinical assessment; it should therefore be supplemented by a clinical interview or broader questionnaires in clinical practice [4]. We agree that insomnia is a heterogeneous condition, and that re-stratifying COMISA patients according to predominant insomnia complaints (problems with sleep onset, sleep maintenance, and/or early morning awakening) and examining their associations with cardiovascular disease and mortality would be clinically interesting and important. In our dataset, however, insomnia was treated as a single entity in line with the International Classification of Sleep Disorders (ICSD-3-TR), and their clinical phenotypes were not assessed. We will in future studies consider investigating whether problems with sleep onset, sleep maintenance and/or early morning awakening differentially affect cardiovascular morbidity and mortality.
