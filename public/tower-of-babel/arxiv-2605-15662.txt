Global dynamics of a supercritical wave equation in a large data regime
Shijie Dong, Zoe Wyatt, Jingya Zhao

Abstract
. We prove the existence of global solutions to the nonlinear wave equation in ℝ 1 + 3 \mathbb{R}^{1+3} Φ t ​ t − Δ ​ Φ ± Φ ​ | Φ | p − 1 = 0 \Phi_{tt}-\Delta\Phi\pm\Phi|\Phi|^{p-1}=0 in the energy-supercritical regime p > 5 p>5 , for a class of large initial data. Our initial data can be decomposed into two pieces, one which is dispersed in the sense of having large L 2 L^{2} norm, while the other piece takes a localised short-pulse form. Consequently, we can obtain global existence for a class of initial data which is large in every homogeneous Sobolev norm H ˙ x s \dot{H}^{s}_{x} with s ≥ 0 s\geq 0 .



1.
Introduction

We consider the semilinear wave equation in
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}

(1.1a)

\Phi_{tt}-\Delta\Phi\pm\Phi|\Phi|^{p-1}=0,\qquad p>5,

with real-valued solutions.
The equation is called focusing or defocusing according to whether the sign of the nonlinearity is negative or positive.
The initial conditions are prescribed at
t_{0}=1
by

(1.1b)

\displaystyle(\Phi,\partial_{t}\Phi)(t_{0})=(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1}).

Our goal in the present article is to prove global existence for (
1.1
) for a class of data which is large in every homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}_{x}
with
s\geq 0
. This is best interpreted through the scaling symmetry

(1.2)

\Phi(t,x)\mapsto\lambda^{2\over p-1}\Phi(\lambda t,\lambda x),

which preserves the space of solutions to (
1.1
). In three space dimensions, the homogeneous Sobolev norm
\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}\times\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}-1}
, where
s_{c}=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{2}{p-1}
, is called
critical
since it is invariant under the rescaling (
1.2
).
Consequently, the equation (
1.1
) is called energy-supercritical since the
solution cannot be controlled in the energy norm as
1<s_{c}<\frac{3}{2}
. Indeed all conservation laws and monotonicity formula have scaling below the critical regularity and so cannot be used in this supercritical setting. Moreover, equation (
1.1
) can be regarded as a model problem relevant to the Navier-Stokes equations in the sense that the nonlinearities in both equations are supercritical compared with their conserved quantity.

The class of initial data that we consider in the current article can be decomposed as

\displaystyle(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1})=(u_{0},u_{1})+(w_{0},w_{1})\,.

Our intuition is that the first piece
(u_{0},u_{1})
is ‘dispersed’ in the sense of having large
L^{2}
norm. An example of such data is achieved by supposing that

\displaystyle(u_{0},u_{1})\sim(\epsilon^{1\over 2}g_{0}(\epsilon x),\epsilon^{{3\over 2}}g_{1}(\epsilon x)),

where
g_{0},g_{1}
are smooth functions decaying sufficiently fast at infinity. This ensures that the norms
\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
are large for any
s\in[0,1]
. Our next assumption is that the second piece
(w_{0},w_{1})
is localised short-pulse data of the form

(1.3)

\begin{split}(w_{0},w_{1})&=\big(\epsilon^{1\over 2}h_{0}(\tfrac{1-r}{\epsilon},\omega),\epsilon^{-{1\over 2}}h_{1}(\tfrac{1-r}{\epsilon},\omega)\big),\qquad w_{1}+\partial_{r}w_{0}\sim\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{1\over 2}),\end{split}

where
h_{0}(\rho,\omega),h_{1}(\rho,\omega)
are smooth functions compactly supported away from the origin with respect to their first argument
\rho
.
Such data is inspired by Christodoulou’s characteristic initial data in his work on the formation of black holes
[
4
]
and shock formation for the irrotational relativistic Euler equations
[
3
]
. The choice (
1.3
) ensures that the norms
\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
are large with
s\geq 1
.

We can now state a rough version of our main result. The precise statement is given in Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a class of initial data that are large in any
\dot{H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
s\geq 0
, the energy supercritical nonlinear wave equation (
1.1
) admits a classical global solution.

1.1.
Previous work

The energy subcritical and energy critical versions of equation (
1.1
) (when
p\leq 5
in 3D) have been extensively studied in the dispersive PDE community. Situations of global regularity or finite time blow-up can occur depending on whether the nonlinearity is focusing or defocusing.
We refer to
[
30
,
34
]
and references within for further information on the large body of work in this direction.

Our focus, however, in the present article is on the energy supercritical setting, and so we now review progress in this direction. On the one hand, global smooth solutions are known for small data by John and Lindblad–Sogge in
[
10
,
18
]
, and global weak solutions (including for large data) are constructed by Segal in
[
26
]
.
The works by Kenig–Merle and Killip–Visan in
[
12
,
11
]
, analyse the nature of blow-up in the sense that a solution to (
1.1
) can only blow-up
in finite time, or be global but fail to scatter, if its critical Sobolev norm diverges. See also a related blow-up result for a supercritical nonlinear wave system with a particular defocusing smooth potential by Tao in
[
29
]
.
In terms of large data results for the energy supercritical problem, much less is known. In both
[
13
,
2
]
, equation (
1.1
) is studied under radial symmetry. In the first work
[
13
]
by Krieger and Schlag, the authors show global existence and restricted stability for a class of solutions with infinite critical Sobolev norm. In the latter work
[
2
]
by Beceanu and Soffer, the authors establish global existence for a class of outgoing radial data with infinite critical Sobolev norm.

In the present article, we take the alternative perspective of using “short pulse data” as introduced by Christodoulou in
[
3
,
4
]
. The “pulse” terminology is because the data has large amplitude relative to its support. Generally speaking in the short pulse method, smallness restrictions are still imposed on the data along directions tangential to the outgoing light cone

{t-r=\text{constant}}
, however certain orthogonal directions involving
\underline{L}=\partial_{t}-\partial_{r}
are allowed to be large. Similar to the product of “good” and “bad” derivatives in the null condition, the short pulse profile is assumed to be large in certain components in such a way that in the nonlinear interactions, large
components are always coupled with small components so that the terms are overall controllable.

Motivated by this milestone method of Christodoulou, many works have since established global existence for nonlinear wave equations subject to short pulse initial data. We start with results in 3D, where results showing global existence given short pulse data have been given for a nonlinear wave equation satisfying the null condition by Miao–Pei–Yu in
[
25
]
; see also
[
6
]
by Ding–Lu–Yin. Complementary to these global regularity results, we mention also the shock formation result by Miao and Yu in
[
24
]
. Note also several global existence results for
3D nonlinear hyperbolic equations with large data that is not of short pulse form in
[
33
,
21
,
20
,
8
]
.
Results showing global existence for 2D nonlinear wave equations given short pulse data have been established for the relativistic membrane equations by Wang and Wei in
[
31
]
, the isentropic and irrotational Euler equations for Chaplygin fluids by Ding–Xin–Yin
[
7
]
, and a null form nonlinearity by the first author and Ding–Xu in
[
5
]
.

In the case where
\Phi
is allowed to be complex valued, Shao–Wei–Zhang
[
27
]
have shown that the defocusing equation (
1.1
) admits finite time blow-up solutions arising from
smooth initial data. This extended ideas from the breakthrough
[
23
]
, which proved the existence of blow-up solutions for the energy-supercritical defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger, to the setting of the defocusing equation (
1.1
).

Finally, we recall some existence results for energy-supercritical wave-type equations with large data.
In
[
15
]
, Li developed an original non-local energy bootstrap strategy and established large data global well-posedness of hedgehog solutions for the Skyrme model which is an energy-supercritical problem. More recently, Wang in
[
32
]
proved global existence for the supercritical Vlasov-Maxwell system with cylindrical large data.

1.2.
Main result and the proof

The precise version of our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p>5
and consider the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) with initial data at
t=t_{0}
of the form

\displaystyle(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1})=(u_{0},u_{1})+(w_{0},w_{1})

where

\displaystyle w_{0}(r,\omega)=\epsilon^{1\over 2}h_{0}(\tfrac{1-r}{\epsilon},\omega),\quad w_{1}(r,\omega)=\epsilon^{-{1\over 2}}h_{1}(\tfrac{1-r}{\epsilon},\omega)\,.

Then, there exists an
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
and for all functions
u_{0}(x),u_{1}(x)
satisfying for
k=0,1,2
:

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|\leq 6-|J|,\,|J|=k}

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle^{|I|}\nabla^{I}\nabla^{J}u_{0}\|

(1.4)

\displaystyle+\sum_{|I|\leq 5-\max\{|J|-1,0\},\,|J|=k}\|\langle x\rangle^{|I|+\max\{1-|J|,0\}}\nabla^{I}\nabla^{\max\{|J|-1,0\}}u_{1}\|\leq\epsilon^{k-1}\,,

and all functions
h_{0}(x),h_{1}(x)
supported in
\{1\leq r\leq 2\}
satisfying

(1.5a)

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|\leq 6}|\nabla^{I}h_{0}(x)|+\sum_{|J|\leq 5}|\nabla^{J}h_{1}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq 1,

(1.5b)

\displaystyle|w_{1}+\partial_{r}w_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{1\over 2},

there exists a classical global solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) satisfying

(1.6)

\displaystyle|\Phi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{-1}\langle t+r\rangle^{-1}\langle t-r\rangle^{-1/2}.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that in Theorem
1.2
we do not need to assume that
L\Phi|_{t=t_{0}}
is small, where
L
is the null derivative that is tangential to the forward Minkowski lightcone. We do however need to assume in (
1.5b
) that the
L
derivative of the short pulse component
(w_{0},w_{1})
is small.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that the critical Lebesgue norm for (
1.1
) is
\Phi_{0}\in L^{p_{c}}
with
p_{c}=3(p-1)/2
. While our data are small in this norm, and certain smallness conditions arise naturally in the short-pulse method, our data are
large at the level of energy and its Sobolev norms cannot all be made uniformly small using the scaling invariance of the equation.

Outline and novel ideas in the proof

The starting point in our proof of Theorem
1.2
is to analyse the PDE (
1.1a
) through two auxiliary PDEs which arise from the decomposed nature of our initial data.

Part I: Global existence for dispersed data.

We first prove the global existence of large data solutions to the auxiliary PDE

(1.7)

\begin{split}\psi_{tt}-\Delta\psi&=\pm|\psi|^{p-1}\psi,\\
(\psi,\partial_{t}\psi)(t_{0})&=(u_{0},u_{1}),\end{split}

with dispersed initial data in the sense of (
1.4
). We use a standard continuity argument relying on the natural and conformal
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
-based energy functionals:

\mathcal{E}(t,\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\|\partial_{t}\psi\|^{2}+\|\partial_{\vec{x}}\psi\|^{2}\big),\quad\mathcal{E}_{con}(t,\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\|L_{0}\psi\|^{2}+\sum_{a}\|L_{a}\psi\|^{2}+\|\Omega\psi\|^{2}+\|\psi\|^{2}\big)\,.

In the above
L_{a}
denotes the Lorentz boosts,
\Omega
the rotations and
L_{0}
denotes the conformal Killing vector field
L_{0}
, also called the scaling vector field. We use
\Gamma
to denote the full Lorentz group of Minkowski Killing vector fields together with
L_{0}
. Precise notation is given in Section
2
.

Due to the initial data assumptions in (
1.4
), at lowest order the solution behaves as
\|\psi\|\sim\epsilon^{-1}
, while higher derivatives of the data lead to increasing smallness:
\|\partial\psi\|\sim 1,\,\|\partial^{\geq 2}\psi\|\sim\epsilon
. This poses challenges to controlling the nonlinearity
|\psi|^{p-1}\psi
where no derivatives appear. For this reason, we use a bootstrap which involves both the natural and conformal energies.
For our pointwise estimates (at lowest order), a naive application of the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality to estimate
|\psi|
requires control on
\mathcal{E}_{con}(t,\Gamma^{\leq 2}\psi)
, and all together produces decay like
|\psi|\sim\epsilon^{-1}t^{-1}
. However by using the fundamental theorem of calculus, and the decay estimate derived from
\mathcal{E}(t,\Gamma^{\leq 2}\psi)
, we interpolate our decay estimates together to gain either a smallness coefficient
|\psi|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}
or a time decay factor
|\psi|\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}
. Together with the Hardy inequality, this is sufficient to close the bootstrap assumption provided
\delta\ll p-5
.

Part II: Global existence for short-pulse data.

We next treat the second auxiliary PDE

(1.8)

\begin{split}\phi_{tt}-\Delta\phi&=\pm\Big(|\psi+\phi|^{p-1}\phi+(|\psi+\phi|^{p-1}-|\psi|^{p-1})\psi\Big)=:\pm\,\mathcal{N},\\
(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi)(t_{0})&=(w_{0},w_{1}),\end{split}

with short-pulse initial data in the sense of (
1.5
). Due to the initial data assumptions, the hierarchy is now reversed in the sense that higher derivatives generate largeness:

\|\phi\|\lesssim\epsilon,\quad\|\partial\phi\|\lesssim 1,\quad\|\partial^{2}\phi\|\lesssim\epsilon^{-1}.

To be more precise, smallness is present in certain directions (in particular the angular derivatives
\Omega\phi
and the null derivative
L\phi
), but not for full derivatives. For instance as in
[
7
,
5
]
, but unlike some other short-pulse works such as
[
25
]
, we only need to assume control of one
L
derivative of
w
in (
1.3
).

As before, one of the main issues is that we do not have smallness on
\phi
using the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality since the estimate yields only
|\phi|\lesssim\|\Gamma^{\leq 2}\phi\|\lesssim\epsilon^{-1}
. This again causes problems to control the nonlinearity.
To address this, we decompose spacetime into a short-time slab of size
O(\epsilon)
and two global regions (an exterior
\mathcal{D}^{\text{ex}}
and interior
\mathcal{D}^{\text{in}}
region) separated by a boundary null hypersurface
t-r=4\epsilon
denoted by
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
. See Figure
1
for the precise decomposition. The standard approach is to exploit the different dispersion mechanisms in these three different regions.

(i) Local in time existence for short-pulse data.

We first establish local existence on the time interval
[t_{0}=1,t_{1}=t_{0}+2\epsilon]
. We make bootstrap assumptions for natural and conformal energies which depend on the number of coordinate derivatives
\partial
being taken:

\mathcal{E}(t,\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)^{1/2}\sim\epsilon^{-|I|}\,,\quad\mathcal{E}_{con}(t,\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)^{1/2}\sim\epsilon^{1-|I|}\,,\qquad|I|+|J|\leq 5\,.

In contrast to the standard Klainerman–Sobolev inequality (see (
2.3
)) which fails to yield any smallness when estimating
\phi
, the smallness of the time interval in the local-in-time slab enables us to derive an improved local Sobolev inequality of the form
|\phi|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial\Omega^{\leq 2}\phi\|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
This refined inequality converts geometric smallness coming from the size of the support, into analytic smallness in the
L^{\infty}
norm.

Turning next to the nonlinearity appearing in (
1.8
), we note that

\displaystyle\big||\psi+\phi|^{p-1}-|\psi|^{p-1}\big|\lesssim|\psi|^{p-2}|\phi|+|\phi|^{p-1}.

A crucial observation is that at least one factor of
\phi
is always present in the terms on the RHS. This ensures that the nonlinear terms inherit the compact support of
\phi
. This key property facilitates our subsequent estimates by confining the influence of the nonlinear interactions to a bounded spacetime region, thereby avoiding unbounded contributions from the large derivatives discussed earlier.
For higher-order estimates on the nonlinearity, numerous terms arise from the Leibniz rule. For example, when taking one derivative we have

\displaystyle|\Gamma\mathcal{N}|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\phi|^{p-1}|\Gamma\phi|+|\phi|^{p-1}|\Gamma\psi|+|\psi|^{p-1}|\Gamma\phi|+|\phi||\psi|^{p-2}|\Gamma\psi|\,.

Some care is needed here when
\Gamma=\Omega
. While
\Omega\phi
is not large, the angular derivative exhibits unfavorable behavior on the dispersed part since
\|\Omega\psi\|\lesssim\epsilon^{-1}
.
We counteract this large growth via the identity
\Omega\psi\sim r\partial\psi\sim\partial\psi
, where in the last step we used that
r\sim 1
in the local-in-time region.
By combining the pointwise smallness of
\phi
, the compact support, and analysing the differentiated nonlinearities, we are able to control all such terms and close the bootstrap for the local-in-time evolution.

Finally, at the boundary of the local region
t=t_{1}
we identify additional hidden smallness by expressing the wave equation (
1.8
) in a null frame and integrating in the
u
- or
v
-direction. Specifically, we find

|L^{\leq 2}\phi|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
holds for values of
r
which propagate into the exterior region. While this is one derivative more than we assumed in (
1.5b
), this procedure relies on the fundamental theorem of calculus and the
\epsilon
-size support of the solution. Thus we have lost regularity here (i.e. we cannot estimate
L^{2}\Gamma^{\geq 1}\phi
) due to this
L^{\infty}-L^{\infty}
argument. Similarly, we obtain an estimate on
|\underline{L}^{\leq 2}\phi|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
for values of
r
which propagate into the interior region. This explicit quantitative smallness then yields suitable initial data for the global analysis.

(ii) Global existence in the exterior region.
We next prove global existence in the exterior region
\mathcal{D}^{\text{ex}}
for the PDE (
1.8
) with initial data posed at
t=t_{1}
. Due to the compact support of short-pulse data, the exterior region consists of the domain
\mathcal{D}^{\text{ex}}=\{(t,x):t\geq t_{1},\ t-5\epsilon\leq r\leq t-\epsilon\}
which we foliate using constant
t-
time slices
\Sigma^{ex}_{t}
. We rely on natural energies to control the solution and the results from
(i)
inform our bootstrap which again depends on the number of coordinate derivatives
\partial
:

\|\partial\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma^{ex}_{t})}\lesssim\epsilon^{-|I|},\qquad|I|+|J|\leq 5\,.

For brevity here, we do not state also the bootstrap on the boundary
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
.
We can gain an additional smallness factor on the field in
L^{2}
by using a standard Hardy inequality involving a weight depending on the distance from the lightcone

\|(t-r)^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma^{ex}_{t})}\lesssim\|\partial_{r}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma^{ex}_{t})}\,.

Since
t-r\sim\epsilon
in the exterior region, we have
\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma^{ex}_{t})}\lesssim\epsilon\|\partial_{r}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma^{ex}_{t})}\lesssim\epsilon
.
Notably, nonlinear terms without derivatives can be directly controlled by this inequality.
For the
L^{\infty}
norm of
\phi
, we follow the same strategy as in the local case, obtaining from a standard Sobolev inequality the estimate
|\phi|\lesssim r^{-1}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Turning next to the nonlinear term
\mathcal{N}
, similar problematic terms as in
(i)
occur.
For instance, using the estimates for the dispersed component, we obtain

\|\Omega^{J}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma^{ex}_{t})}\lesssim\epsilon^{-1},\qquad|\Omega^{J}\psi|\lesssim\sum_{|J_{1}|=|J|-1}|r\partial\Omega^{J_{1}}\psi|.

Unlike
(i)
where we used
r\sim 1
, we inevitably incur a loss here – either a loss in
\epsilon
or a loss in the decay rate.
Therefore, we must carefully balance these two types of losses to close the exterior bootstrap.

Finally, as input for the interior analysis, we derive additional estimates including conformal energies and boundary contributions arising from the interface
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
with the exterior region, together with refined
L^{\infty}
estimates.

(iii) Global existence in the interior region.

Finally we prove global existence in the interior region
\mathcal{D}^{in}=\{(t,x):t\geq t_{1},\ 0\leq r\leq t-4\epsilon\}
which is significantly more challenging. We employ the hyperboloidal vector field approach using a hierarchy of energy estimates along hyperboloidal hypersurfaces
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
. In particular, we make use of both the standard hyperboloidal energy
\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\varphi)
and a conformal energy
\mathcal{E}_{\text{con}}^{in}(\tau,\varphi)
.
A key difficulty lies in obtaining suitable pointwise control without losing too many derivatives. In the region
r\geq t/8
, the classical Klainerman–Sobolev inequality reads (see the precise estimate (
6.8
))

|\phi|\lesssim\tau^{-1}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|(\tau/t)\Omega^{\leq 2}\phi\|^{1/2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\|(\tau/t)\Omega^{\leq 2}Z\phi\|^{1/2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}+\text{endpoint data}.

Note the last factor requires three derivatives on
\phi
.
However, to control
\|(\tau/t)\Omega^{2}Z\phi\|

one is forced to commute the equation twice, which produces nonlinear terms of the form
|\phi|^{p-2}\Omega\phi\,\Omega\phi
. Controlling such terms requires
L^{\infty}
bounds for
\Omega\phi
, and hence higher-order Sobolev norms such as
\|(\tau/t)Z\Omega^{3}\phi\|
. This in turn necessitates bootstrap assumptions at the level of three derivatives. As a consequence, certain energy terms cannot be closed; for instance, we cannot control a boundary term of the form

\int_{B_{4\epsilon}}|L_{a}\Omega^{3}\phi|\,dx,\qquad\text{where}\ \ |L_{a}\Omega^{3}\phi|\lesssim r^{-1}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{r}\Omega^{\leq 2}L_{a}\Omega^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t}^{ex})},

without invoking estimates for up to six derivatives, which is not compatible with the range
p>5
.

To overcome this difficulty, we introduce a different strategy by combining
L^{\infty}
bounds with
L^{q}
estimates. Our crucial observation is that in the region
r\geq t/8
, we can prove an
L^{q}-L^{2}
estimate that requires only two derivatives on the right hand side (see (
6.9
) for the precise statement),

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau}\cap\{r\geq t/8\})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{-1}r^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{q}}\big\|(\tau/t)\Omega^{\leq 1}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|(\tau/t)Z\Omega^{\leq 1}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\text{endpoint data},\quad q>6.

The condition
q>6
is used to rewrite an integral involving
L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
into one involving
L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})
, which is required after we invoke the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
. In turn, the condition
q>6
is used in our proof to get enough decay when applying the above estimate.

Our
L^{q}
estimate allows us to lower the regularity level in the bootstrap assumptions: instead of controlling three derivatives, it suffices to work at essentially one derivative above the energy level. By contrast other works for instance
[
7
,
25
]
require far more derivatives than we do here.
In particular, we impose bootstrap bounds of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\Omega^{\leq 1}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,\Omega^{\leq 1}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\partial\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,L_{a}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,L_{a}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 1.

In this bootstrap it is also crucial that we can reduce the number of derivatives in order to preserve smallness in
\epsilon
. Indeed as already seen in
(i, ii)
above, higher-order derivatives tend to lose any smallness coming from the initial data. For example, refined pointwise estimates at time
t_{1}
do not yield any
\epsilon
-gain at the level of three derivatives, e.g.

\|\partial\Omega^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}\lesssim O(1),

which shows that no smallness is available at this level. In contrast, the use of
L^{q}
estimates allows us to avoid such losses.
With these ingredients, we are able to close the bootstrap argument in the interior region. The proof relies on a delicate combination of the hyperboloidal energy estimates, the mixed
L^{\infty}
and
L^{q}
control, and the previously established results in the local and exterior regions, which together yield global existence in
\mathcal{D}^{in}
.

1.3.
Organization

In Section
2
we present some preliminary notation and standard results from functional analysis. In Section
3
we prove global existence for the decoupled problem (
1.7
) with dispersed data. In Section
4
we turn to the second decoupled problem (
1.8
) and prove local in time existence, followed by global existence in the exterior and interior regions in Sections
5
and
6
respectively. Finally in Section

we upgrade our global solution to a global classical one.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Our problem is posed on the Minkowski spacetime
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
equipped with the metric
\eta=\text{diag}(-1,1,1,1)
. We denote by
\Box=-\partial_{t}\partial_{t}+\Delta
the d’Alembert operator. We use Roman letters to denote spatial indices
a,b,\cdots\in\{1,2,3\}
, and Greek letters to denote spacetime indices
\alpha,\beta,\cdots\in\{0,1,2,3\}
. The indices are raised or lowered by the metric
\eta
, and the Einstein summation convention for repeated upper and lower indices is adopted. For a spacetime point
(t,x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})
,
x^{0}
denotes the time variable
t
, and
r
denotes the spatial radius
\sqrt{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}+(x^{3})^{2}}
.

We rely on the standard Minkowski vector fields: the translations
\{\partial_{\alpha}=\partial_{x^{\alpha}}\}
, the rotations
\{\Omega_{ab}=x_{a}\partial_{b}-x_{b}\partial_{a}:a<b\}
, the Lorentz boosts
\{L_{a}=t\partial_{a}+x_{a}\partial_{t}\}
and the scaling vector field
L_{0}=t\partial_{t}+x^{a}\partial_{a}
. We collect the vector fields as

\Gamma=(\Gamma_{1},\cdots,\Gamma_{11})=(\partial,\Omega,L_{1},L_{2},L_{3},L_{0}),

where we use the shorthand notation
\partial=(\partial_{t},\nabla)=(\partial_{t},\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3})
and
\Omega=(\Omega_{12},\Omega_{13},\Omega_{23})
. We sometimes denote
Z=\{\partial_{t},L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}\}.

It is important to express these vector fields with respect to the natural null frame defined on Minkowski.
First, we denote the null coordinates
v=t+r,u=t-r
, which leads to

\displaystyle L=\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}=2\partial_{v},\qquad\underline{L}=\partial_{t}-\partial_{r}=2\partial_{u},

where
\partial_{r}=\omega^{a}\partial_{a}
with
\omega_{a}=x_{a}/r
.
We also introduce a notion of ‘good’ derivatives

G_{a}=\omega_{a}\partial_{t}+\partial_{a},\quad a=1,2,3,

and set
G=(G_{1},G_{2},G_{3})
.
These definitions lead to the following identities amongst the vector fields.

(2.1)

\displaystyle L=\omega^{a}G_{a}=\frac{L_{0}+\omega^{a}L_{a}}{t+r},\quad\partial_{t}=\frac{1}{2}(L+\underline{L}),\quad\partial_{a}=\frac{\omega_{a}}{2}(L-\underline{L})-\frac{\omega^{b}}{r}\Omega_{ab},

\displaystyle G_{a}=\frac{\omega_{a}}{t+r}L_{0}+\frac{1}{t}L_{a}-\frac{\omega_{a}x^{b}}{t(t+r)}L_{b}=\omega_{a}L-\frac{\omega^{b}}{r}\Omega_{ab},

\displaystyle L_{a}=\frac{\omega_{a}}{2}((t+r)L-(t-r)\underline{L})-\frac{t}{r}\omega^{b}\Omega_{ab}.

We use the standard notation

|\Gamma f|=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{11}|\Gamma_{k}f|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and for any multi-index
I=(I_{1},\cdots,I_{11})\in\mathbb{N}^{11}
, we write
\Gamma^{I}=\Gamma^{I_{1}}_{1}\cdots\Gamma^{I_{11}}_{11}
and
|I|=\sum_{k=1}^{11}I_{k}
. We occasionally use the shorthand notation
\Gamma^{\leq k}
to denote a sum of
\Gamma^{I}
with
|I|\leq k
.
Finally, we use the Japanese bracket
\langle b\rangle=(1+|b|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
for a scalar or vector
b
, and denote by
\|\cdot\|
the
L^{2}
norm. We write
A\lesssim B
to indicate
A\leq C_{0}B
with
C_{0}
a universal constant. We write
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

Finally, we will need to write numerous summations involving terms such as
|I_{1}|+\ldots+|I_{5}|
. Since this becomes cumbersome to read, we introduce the following simplified (and slightly abused) notation

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I_{1}|+\cdots+|I_{k}|=|I|,\\
\text{Another condition}\end{subarray}}=\sum_{I_{1\to k}=I}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Another}\\
\text{ condition}\end{subarray}}.

Furthermore, we occasionally write
\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}
as
\Gamma^{K}
when it is not necessary to emphasise the value of
I
, and associate
K=(I,J)
with
|K|=|I|+|J|
.

Lemma 2.1

The following commutation identities hold

\displaystyle=0,\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq a<b\leq 3.

(

For any smooth function

Let
\varphi

is large. Then

(2.3)

|\varphi(t,x)|\lesssim\langle t+r\rangle^{-1}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\|\Gamma^{I}\varphi(t,x)\|.

[
16
]

Suppose that

Lemma 2.7

.

Let

be a nonnegative function that satisfies the integral inequality

f(t)\leq c+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(a(s)f(s)+b(s)f^{\frac{1}{2}}(s))\,ds,

where

f(t)\leq\Big(c^{\frac{1}{2}}\exp\Big(\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}a(s)\,ds\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}b(s)\exp\Big(\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}a(\tilde{s})\,d\tilde{s}\Big)\,ds\Big)^{2}.

3.
Global existence for dispersed data

3.1.
Global existence and decay

We first consider the decoupled problem

(3.1)

\begin{split}-\Box\psi=\pm|\psi|^{p-1}\psi,\\
(\psi,\partial_{t}\psi)(t_{0})=(u_{0},u_{1}),\end{split}

where the initial data will be
dispersed
in the sense that the
L^{2}
norm is large, i.e.
\|u_{0}\|\sim\epsilon^{-1}
, while higher derivatives of the data lead to increasing smallness, e.g.
\|\partial u_{0}\|\sim 1,\,\|\partial^{2}u_{0}\|\sim\epsilon
.
Our goal in this section is to prove the following global existence result.

Theorem 3.1
.

The Cauchy problem (
3.1
) with initial data satisfying

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|\leq 6-|J|,\,|J|=k}

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle^{|I|}\nabla^{I}\nabla^{J}u_{0}\|

(3.3)

\displaystyle+\sum_{|I|\leq 5-\max\{|J|-1,0\},\,|J|=k}\|\langle x\rangle^{|I|+\max\{1-|J|,0\}}\nabla^{I}\nabla^{\max\{|J|-1,0\}}u_{1}\|\leq\epsilon^{k-1},\quad k=0,1,2\,,

(i.e. dispersed in the sense of (
1.4
))
admits a global solution.

Based on the boundedness and smallness assumptions in (
3.2
), we make the following bootstrap assumptions on
[t_{0},T)
:

(3.4a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{con}^{1\over 2}(t,\Gamma^{I}\psi)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\epsilon^{-1},\qquad

\displaystyle|I|\leq 5,

(3.4b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{1\over 2}(t,\Gamma^{I}\psi)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1},\qquad

\displaystyle|I|\leq 5,

(3.4c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{1\over 2}(t,\partial\Gamma^{I}\psi)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\epsilon,\qquad

\displaystyle|I|\leq 4.

In the above,
C_{1}\gg 1
is to be determined,
\epsilon\ll 1
is the size of the data such that
C_{1}<\epsilon^{-1}
, and

T:=\sup\{s\in(t_{0},+\infty):\eqref{eq:BA-disp}\text{ hold}\}.

In our next proposition we prove pointwise estimates using the bootstrap assumptions. We point out the key estimate for
|\Gamma^{\leq 2}\psi|
in (
3.5
): a naive application of the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality (
2.3
) combined with (
3.4a
) would suggest
|\Gamma^{\leq 4}\psi|\sim\epsilon^{-1}
. However, we gain a smallness coefficient of
\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-}
by working at lower order and applying an interpolation argument.

Lemma 3.2

(Preliminary estimates)
.

.

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{1}\langle t+r\rangle^{-{1\over 2}+\delta},\qquad

\displaystyle|I|\leq 3.

(3.7)

\displaystyle|\partial\partial\Gamma^{I}\psi|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{1}\epsilon\langle t+r\rangle^{-1}\langle t-r\rangle^{-{1\over 2}},\qquad

Lemma 3.3

(Bounds on the nonlinearities)
.

Under the assumptions (
3.4

(3.11)

\displaystyle\big\|\langle t+r\rangle\Gamma^{I}(|\psi|^{p-1}\psi)\big\|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{1}^{p}\epsilon^{-1+\delta}\langle t\rangle^{-1-\delta},\qquad

\displaystyle|I|\leq 5,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\big\|\Gamma^{I}(|\psi|^{p-1}\psi)\big\|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{1}^{p}\epsilon^{\delta}\langle t\rangle^{-1-\delta},\qquad

\displaystyle|I|\leq 5,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\big\|\partial\Gamma^{I}(|\psi|^{p-1}\psi)\big\|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{1}^{p}\epsilon^{1+\delta}\langle t\rangle^{-1-\delta},\qquad

We first prove (
3.12

Our starting point is taking (
3.14

3.5

\displaystyle\|\langle t+r\rangle\Gamma^{I}\big(|\psi|^{p-1}\psi\big)\|

4.1.
Local in time existence

Our goal now is to prove the following:

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small such that (
4.1
) subject to the data (
4.2
) admits a local solution on
[t_{0},t_{1}]
, where
t_{1}=1+2\epsilon
.

Due to the properties of the support of the short pulse data (
4.2
), we have that
\|\phi(t_{0})\|\sim\epsilon
and
\|\partial_{t}\phi(t_{0})\|\sim 1
. Motivated by this, we make the following bootstrap assumptions on
[t_{0},t_{1}]
:

(4.3a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t,\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\epsilon^{-|I|},\quad\quad|I|+|J|\leq 5,

(4.3b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{con}(t,\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\epsilon^{1-|I|},\quad\quad|I|+|J|\leq 5.

In the above,
C_{2}>C_{1}\gg 1
is to be determined, and
\epsilon
is the size of the initial data.

We first prove a Sobolev inequality which, thanks to the smallness in the support of the function, yields a key smallness factor of
\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Lemma 4.2

(Local Sobolev inequality)
.

Suppose that, for any fixed
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
,
f(t,x)
is supported in
2-2\epsilon-t\leq r\leq t-\epsilon
. Then we have

(4.4)

|f(t,x)|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|J|\leq 2}\|\partial_{r}\Omega^{J}f\|.

Proof.

First, recall the Sobolev embedding theorem on a unit sphere

(4.5)

|f(t,x)|\lesssim\sum_{|J|\leq 2}\|\Omega^{J}f(t,r\omega)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})},

where
(t,x)=(t,r\omega)
with
r=|x|
and
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
. From the fundamental theorem of calculus, integrating
\partial_{r}f
from
r
to
t-\epsilon
with
\omega
fixed, we obtain

\displaystyle|f(t,rw)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{r}^{t-\epsilon}|\partial_{\rho}f(t,\rho\omega)|\,{\rm{d}}\rho=\int_{r}^{t-\epsilon}|\partial_{\rho}f|\rho^{-1}\rho\,{\rm{d}}\rho\lesssim r^{-1}\int_{r}^{t-\epsilon}|\partial_{\rho}f|\,\rho\,{\rm{d}}\rho

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-1}\left(\int_{r}^{t-\epsilon}1\,{\rm{d}}\rho\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{r}^{t-\epsilon}|\partial_{\rho}f|^{2}\rho^{2}\,{\rm{d}}\rho\right)^{1/2}\lesssim r^{-1}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{r}^{t-\epsilon}\sum_{|J|\leq 2}\|\Omega^{J}\partial_{\rho}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}\rho^{2}\,d\rho\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-1}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|J|\leq 2}\|\partial_{r}\Omega^{J}f\|,

where in the penultimate step we used (
4.5
) as well as
r\sim 1
.
∎

Proposition 4.3

(Energy and pointwise estimates)
.

Under the assumptions (
4.3
), the following estimates hold for
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
: the energy bounds

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|\partial\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{-|I|},

\displaystyle|I|+|J|\leq 5,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|L_{0}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|+\sum_{a=1}^{3}\|L_{a}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|+\|\Omega\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|+\|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{1-|I|},

\displaystyle|I|+|J|\leq 5,

as well as the pointwise bounds

(4.8)

\displaystyle|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-|I|},

\displaystyle|I|+|J|\leq 3,

(4.9)

\displaystyle|L_{0}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi|+\sum_{a=1}^{3}|L_{a}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-|I|},

\displaystyle|I|+|J|\leq 2.

Proof.

The
L^{2}
estimates (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) easily follow from the bootstrap assumptions (
4.3
).
To prove (
4.8
), we combine the above Sobolev inequality (
4.4
), the commutator estimates (
2.2
), and the natural energy bound (
4.6
), to obtain

|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|J_{1}|\leq 2}\|\partial_{r}\Omega^{J_{1}}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|J_{1}|\leq 2}\|\partial_{r}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J_{1}}\Omega^{J}\phi\|\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-|I|},\qquad|I|+|J|\leq 3\,.

Similarly, combining Lemma
4.2
with the
L^{2}
estimates (
4.7
), we get

\displaystyle|L_{\alpha}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi|\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-|I|},\quad\alpha=0,1,2,3,\quad|I|+|J|\leq 2.

∎

Remark 4.4

(Differentiated nonlinearities and notation)
.

In the next lemma and following subsections, we need to distribute vector fields
\Gamma\in\{\partial,\Omega\}
across our nonlinear term

\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)=|\psi+\phi|^{p-1}(\psi+\phi)-|\psi|^{p-1}\psi\,.

When there are no vector fields acting, we can simply use the elementary inequality

(4.10)

|\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)|\lesssim|\phi|^{p}+|\psi|^{p-1}|\phi|\,.

For the differentiated nonlinearity, numerous terms arise from the Leibniz rule. For example

\displaystyle|\Gamma\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\phi|^{p-1}|\Gamma\phi|+|\phi|^{p-1}|\Gamma\psi|+|\psi|^{p-1}|\Gamma\phi|+|\phi||\psi|^{p-2}|\Gamma\psi|\,,

\displaystyle|\Gamma^{2}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\phi|^{p-1}|\Gamma^{2}\phi|+|\phi|^{p-1}|\Gamma^{2}\psi|+|\psi|^{p-1}|\Gamma^{2}\phi|+|\phi||\psi|^{p-2}|\Gamma^{2}\psi|+|\phi|^{p-2}|\Gamma\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle+|\phi|^{p-2}|\Gamma\phi||\Gamma\psi|+|\phi|^{p-2}|\Gamma\psi|^{2}+|\psi|^{p-2}|\Gamma\phi|^{2}+|\psi|^{p-2}|\Gamma\phi|\Gamma\psi|+|\phi||\psi|^{p-3}|\Gamma\psi|^{2}\,.

We will typically extract from each nonlinear term a factor of
|\star|^{p-5}
, for
\star\in\{\phi,\psi\}
. So, looking at two example terms in
\Gamma^{2}\mathcal{N}
above, we may write

|\phi|^{p-1}|\Gamma^{2}\phi|=|\phi|^{p-5}\cdot|\Gamma^{2}\phi||\phi|^{4},\quad|\phi||\psi|^{p-3}|\Gamma\psi|^{2}=|\psi|^{p-5}\cdot|\phi||\Gamma\psi|^{2}|\psi|^{2}.

More generally, there can then be at most 5 factors that remain to be controlled (i.e. the terms that appear after the
\cdot
above). We can then estimate such terms by the following products

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|+|J|=2}|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1\to 5}=|I|\\
{J_{1\to 5}}=|J|\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{0\leq m\leq 5}|\phi|^{p-5}\prod_{k=1}^{m}|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi|\prod_{l=m+1}^{5}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{1\leq m\leq 5}|\psi|^{p-5}\prod_{k=1}^{m}|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi|\prod_{l=m+1}^{5}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|\Big).

Note that the product
\prod
is defined to be 1 if the top index is strictly smaller than the bottom index.
These products introduce many new terms that do not actually appear in our nonlinearities. However this does not pose a problem, as key to our analysis is keeping track of the number of factors of
\phi,\psi
and keeping track of where the derivatives fall, e.g. terms like
\partial\phi
lead to factors of
\epsilon^{-1}
while
\Omega\phi
do not.

Lemma 4.5

(Local-in-time bounds on the nonlinearities)
.

Under the assumptions (
4.3
), the following estimate holds for
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
:

\displaystyle\|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|+\|\langle t+r\rangle\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|\lesssim C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}-(p-1)\delta-|I|},

\displaystyle\quad|I|+|J|\leq 5\,.

Proof.

Since on the small interval on
[t_{0},t_{1}]
the weight
\langle t+r\rangle
is comparable to a positive constant, we only need to study

\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)=\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\big(|\psi+\phi|^{p-1}(\psi+\phi)-|\psi|^{p-1}\psi\big)\,.

We apply Leibniz rule
and separately study two cases depending on the total number of derivatives
|K|=|I|+|J|
. The first case, when
|I|+|J|=0
, can be directly estimated by taking
\psi
only in
L^{\infty}
. Using Proposition
4.3
and the pointwise estimates on
\psi
given in (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|\lesssim\||\phi|^{p}\|+\||\psi|^{p-1}\phi\|\lesssim\|\phi\|\|\phi\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}}+\|\phi\|\|\psi\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p+1}{2}-(p-1)\delta}.

The second case, where
1\leq|I|+|J|\leq 5
, is more involved. Following the discussion in Remark
4.4
, we express the nonlinear terms into two pieces distinguished by an overall factor of
|\phi|^{p-5}
or
|\psi|^{p-5}
:

\displaystyle\|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1\to 5}=I\\
J_{1\to 5}=J\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{0\leq m\leq 5}\Big\||\phi|^{p-5}\prod_{k=1}^{m}|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi|\prod_{l=m+1}^{5}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|\Big\|

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq m\leq 5}\Big\||\psi|^{p-5}\prod_{k=1}^{m}|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi|\prod_{l=m+1}^{5}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|\Big\|\Big)=:\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2}.

Estimate for
\mathcal{R}_{1}
.
We first break
\mathcal{R}_{1}
into 3 pieces based on terms with just products of
\Gamma\phi
, those terms with mixed products of
\Gamma\phi
and
\Gamma\psi
(which are the ‘worst’ terms in the sense of
\epsilon
growth), and those terms just with products of
\Gamma\psi
. Each term will be estimated by placing the higher-order derivative in
L^{2}
and controlling all remaining factors in
L^{\infty}
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1\to 5}=I\\
J_{1\to 5}=J\end{subarray}}\Big(\Big\||\phi|^{p-5}\prod_{k=1}^{5}|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi|\Big\|+\sum_{1\leq m\leq 4}\Big\||\phi|^{p-5}\prod_{k=1}^{m}|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi|\prod_{l=m+1}^{5}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|\Big\|

\displaystyle+\Big\||\phi|^{p-5}\prod_{l=1}^{5}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|\Big\|\Big)=:\mathcal{R}_{1}^{1}+\mathcal{R}_{1}^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{1}^{3}.

For
\mathcal{R}_{1}^{1}
, which involves only
\phi
factors, we insert the estimates from Proposition
4.3
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}^{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1\to 5}=I\\
J_{1\to 5}=J\end{subarray}}\sum_{|K_{1}|\geq|K_{2}|,\ldots,|K_{5}|}\|\partial^{I_{1}}\Omega^{J_{1}}\phi\|\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-5}\prod_{k=2}^{5}\|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p+1}{2}-|I|}.

For the term
\mathcal{R}_{1}^{2}
, we also need to use the
L^{\infty}
estimates on
\psi
from (
3.5
). We find

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1\to 5}=I\\
J_{1\to 5}=J\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|K_{2}|,\ldots,|K_{5}|\leq 2\\
1\leq m\leq 4\end{subarray}}\|\partial^{I_{1}}\Omega^{J_{1}}\phi\|\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-5}\prod_{k=2}^{m}\|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\prod_{l=m+1}^{5}\|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|K_{1}|,\ldots,|K_{4}|\leq 2\\
|K_{5}^{\prime}|=|K_{5}|-1\geq 2\\
1\leq m\leq 4\end{subarray}}\|\partial\partial^{I^{\prime}_{5}}\Omega^{J^{\prime}_{5}}\psi\|\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-5}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\prod_{k=m+1}^{4}\|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}-3\delta-|I|},

where we used the facts that
\langle r\rangle\sim 1
and

(4.11)

|\Omega^{J}\psi|\lesssim\sum_{|J^{\prime}|=|J|-1}|\langle r\rangle\partial\Omega^{J^{\prime}}\psi|.

For
\mathcal{R}_{1}^{3}
, all derivatives act on
\psi
and none on
\phi
. Consequently, using (
4.11
), (
3.5
) and Proposition
4.3
we derive

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1\to 5}=I\\
J_{1\to 5}=J\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|K_{1}^{\prime}|=|K_{1}|-1\\
|K_{1}|\geq|K_{2}|,\ldots,|K_{5}|\end{subarray}}\|\partial\partial^{I^{\prime}_{1}}\Omega^{J^{\prime}_{1}}\psi\|\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-5}\prod_{k=2}^{5}\|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}-4\delta}.

Estimate for
\mathcal{R}_{2}
.
We similarly break
\mathcal{R}_{2}
into two pieces – one which just involves products of
\Gamma\phi
, and the other with mixed products of
\Gamma\phi
and
\Gamma\psi
(these latter terms being the worse to estimate).

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2}\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1\to 5}=I\\
J_{1\to 5}=J\end{subarray}}\Big(\Big\||\psi|^{p-5}\prod_{k=1}^{5}|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi|\Big\|+\sum_{1\leq m\leq 4}\Big\||\psi|^{p-5}\prod_{k=1}^{m}|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi|\prod_{l=m+1}^{5}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|\Big\|\Big)=:\mathcal{R}_{2}^{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2}^{2}.

For
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{1}
, where no derivatives appear on the
\psi
factor, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2}^{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1\to 5}=I\\
J_{1\to 5}=J\end{subarray}}\sum_{|K_{1}|\geq|K_{2}|,\ldots,|K_{5}|}\|\partial^{I_{1}}\Omega^{J_{1}}\phi\|\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-5}\prod_{k=2}^{5}\|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p+1}{2}-(p-5)\delta-|I|}.

For the term
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{2}
, we have mixed products of
\Gamma\phi
and
\Gamma\psi
. We obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1\to 5}=I\\
J_{1\to 5}=J\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|K_{2}|,\ldots,|K_{5}|\leq 2\\
1\leq m\leq 4\end{subarray}}\|\partial^{I_{1}}\Omega^{J_{1}}\phi\|\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-5}\prod_{k=2}^{m}\|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\prod_{l=m+1}^{5}\|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|K_{1}|,\ldots,|K_{4}|\leq 2\\
|K_{5}^{\prime}|=|K_{5}|-1\geq 2\\
1\leq m\leq 4\end{subarray}}\|\partial\partial^{I^{\prime}_{5}}\Omega^{J^{\prime}_{5}}\psi\|\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-5}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\prod_{l=m+1}^{4}\|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p+1}{2}-(p-1)\delta-|I|}+C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}-(p-2)\delta-|I|}\lesssim C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}-(p-2)\delta-|I|}.

Combining the above estimates, we conclude that for
|I|+|J|\leq 5
,

\|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|\lesssim C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}-(p-1)\delta-|I|}.

∎

We now conclude the proof of Proposition
4.1
by closing the bootstrap assumptions on
[t_{0},t_{1}]
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

By considering the commuted equation

-\Box\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi=\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)=\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\big(|\psi+\phi|^{p-1}(\psi+\phi)-|\psi|^{p-1}\psi\big),

we apply the energy estimate (
2.8
) and the nonlinear bounds from Lemma
4.5
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t,\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t_{0},\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{-|I|}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}-(p-1)\delta-|I|}\,ds\lesssim\epsilon^{-|I|}+C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p+1}{2}-(p-1)\delta-|I|}.

Similarly, employing the conformal energy (
2.9
) and the nonlinear bounds from Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{con}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t,\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{con}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t_{0},\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\langle s+r\rangle\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{1-|I|}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}-(p-1)\delta-|I|}\,ds\lesssim\epsilon^{1-|I|}+C_{2}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p+1}{2}-(p-1)\delta-|I|}.

These estimates strictly improve the bootstrap assumptions (
4.3
) by taking
C_{2}^{p-1}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}-(p-1)\delta}\ll 1
. Therefore, the bootstrap argument closes, and the solution
\phi
exists on
[t_{0},t_{1}].

∎

Before proceeding to the analysis of the exterior and interior regions, we record the following refined
L^{\infty}
estimates at time
t_{1}
. The proof involves writing the wave equation with respect to the null frame and integrating in
u
or
v
directions (for (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) respectively) using the fundamental theorem of calculus and the fact that the solution is supported on an interval of size
\epsilon
. We remark also a key smallness gain of
|L^{2}\phi|\sim\epsilon^{1/2}
in (

) (compared to
\epsilon^{-1/2}
from (

)) which will be used later on in the boundary flux estimates.

Lemma 4.6

(Refined pointwise estimates at
t_{1}
)
.

The solution
\phi
from Proposition
4.1
obeys the following bounds for
L^{\leq 2}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi
:

(4.12)

\begin{split}|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi(t_{1},x)|&\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-|I|},\quad r\in[1-4\epsilon,1+\epsilon],\quad|I|+|J|\leq 3,\\
|L\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi(t_{1},x)|&\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-|I|},\quad r\in[1-4\epsilon,1+\epsilon],\quad|I|+|J|\leq 2,\\
|L^{2}\phi(t_{1},x)|&\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\quad\ r\in[1-3\epsilon,1+\epsilon],\end{split}

the following estimates for
\underline{L}\underline{L}^{\leq 1}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi
:

(4.13)

\begin{split}|\underline{L}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi(t_{1},x)|&\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}-|I|},\quad r\in[1-4\epsilon,1],\quad|I|+|J|\leq 1,\\
|\underline{L}^{2}\phi(t_{1},x)|&\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\quad\ r\in[1-4\epsilon,1],\end{split}

and the following estimates for
\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi
:

(4.14)

\begin{split}|\Omega^{J}\phi(t_{1},x)|&\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\qquad\ \ r\in[1-4\epsilon,1+\epsilon],\quad|J|\leq 3,\\
|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi(t_{1},x)|&\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}-|I|},\quad\quad\ r\in[1-4\epsilon,1],\quad|I|\geq 1,\quad|I|+|J|\leq 2.\end{split}

Proof.

For this region, we introduce the notation
D_{R},D_{L}
shown in Figure
1
and defined by

D_{R}=\{(t,x):t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{1},\;2-\epsilon-t\leq r\leq t-\epsilon\},\quad D_{L}=\{(t,x):t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{1},\;2-2\epsilon-t\leq r\leq t-2\epsilon\}\,.

\bullet
We start by proving (
4.12
). Notice that
L=(t+r)^{-1}(L_{0}+\omega^{a}L_{a})
. Therefore, the first two estimates follow directly from the
L^{\infty}
bounds in Proposition
4.3
. Moreover, when
t_{1}=1+2\epsilon
, the support of
\phi
is contained in the region
r\in[1-4\epsilon,\,1+\epsilon]
, so that
r\sim 1
on this time slice.
We next turn to the pointwise bound for
L^{2}\phi
in (
4.12
). First, we rewrite the equation for
\phi
in the form

(4.15)

\underline{L}L\phi=\frac{1}{r}L\phi-\frac{1}{r}\underline{L}\phi+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi\pm\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi),

where
\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
denotes the Laplace–Beltrami operator on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
Acting the vector field
L
on both sides of (
4.15
), we have

\displaystyle\underline{L}L^{2}\phi

\displaystyle=L\left(\frac{1}{r}L\phi-\frac{1}{r}\underline{L}\phi+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi\pm\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}L^{2}\phi-\frac{1}{r}L\underline{L}\phi+\frac{1}{r^{2}}L\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi-\frac{1}{r^{2}}L\phi+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\underline{L}\phi-\frac{2}{r^{3}}\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi\pm L\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi).

Using the pointwise estimates on
\psi
in (
3.5
)–(
3.6
), and the pointwise bounds on
\phi
from (
4.8
)–(
4.9
), together with the fact that
r\sim 1
in the region under consideration, we deduce that

|\underline{L}L^{2}\phi|\lesssim|L^{2}\phi|+C_{2}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus along the
u
-direction, we obtain

|L^{2}\phi(t,x)|\leq\int_{\epsilon}^{5\epsilon}|\partial_{u}L^{2}\phi|\,du\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\epsilon}^{5\epsilon}|\underline{L}L^{2}\phi|\,du\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\epsilon}^{5\epsilon}|L^{2}\phi|\,du,

for any
(t,x)\in D_{R}
.
By Gronwall inequality, it follows that

|L^{2}\phi(t,x)|\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\mbox{for}\quad(t,x)\in D_{R}.

\bullet
We now prove (
4.13
) by studying the commuted equation

-\Box\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi=\pm\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi),\quad|I|+|J|\leq 1.

Rewriting the wave operator in terms of the null frame, we obtain

\displaystyle L\underline{L}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}L\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi-\frac{1}{r}\underline{L}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\pm\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi).

Using the estimates on
\psi
established in (
3.5
), and on
\phi
in (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we deduce

|L\underline{L}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi|\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-|I|}+|\underline{L}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi|.

Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus along the
v
-direction, we obtain

\displaystyle|\underline{L}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2-2\epsilon}^{2+2\epsilon}|\partial_{v}\underline{L}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi|\,dv\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{2-2\epsilon}^{2+2\epsilon}|L\underline{L}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi|\,dv\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}-|I|}+\int_{2-2\epsilon}^{2+2\epsilon}|\underline{L}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi|\,dv,

for any
(t,x)\in D_{L}
.
Combining the above estimate with Gronwall inequality, we have

|\underline{L}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi(t,x)|\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}-|I|},\quad\mbox{for}\quad(t,x)\in D_{L}.

Next, acting
\underline{L}
on both sides of the equation (
4.15
), we have

\displaystyle L\underline{L}^{2}\phi

\displaystyle=\underline{L}\left(\frac{1}{r}L\phi-\frac{1}{r}\underline{L}\phi+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi\pm\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\underline{L}L\phi-\frac{1}{r}\underline{L}^{2}\phi+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\underline{L}\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi+\frac{1}{r^{2}}L\phi-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\underline{L}\phi+\frac{2}{r^{3}}\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi\pm\underline{L}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi).

Using again the previously established bounds, we find

|L\underline{L}^{2}\phi|\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}+|\underline{L}^{2}\phi|.

Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus along the
v
-direction, we apply Gronwall inequality to conclude that

|\underline{L}^{2}\phi(t,x)|\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\mbox{for}\quad(t,x)\in D_{L}.

\bullet
We now prove (
4.14
). The first inequality, where
|I|=0
, simply follows from (
4.12
). For the second inequality, when
|I|\geq 1
and
|I|+|J|\leq 2
, we write

\displaystyle|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi|\lesssim\sum_{|I_{1}|=|I|-1}|\partial\partial^{I_{1}}\Omega^{J}\phi|\lesssim\sum_{|I_{1}|=|I|-1}\left(|L\partial^{I_{1}}\Omega^{J}\phi|+|\underline{L}\partial^{I_{1}}\Omega^{J}\phi|+\left|\frac{1}{r}\Omega\partial^{I_{1}}\Omega^{J}\phi\right|\right).

Using the bounds established in (
4.12
) and (
4.13
), we deduce

|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi(t_{1},x)|\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}-|I|},\quad\mbox{for}\quad r\in[1-4\epsilon,1].

∎

5.
Global existence for short-pulse data in the exterior

In this section, we establish the following global existence of the solution in the exterior region:

Proposition 5.1
.

There exists
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small such that (
4.1
) admits a global in time solution in
\mathcal{D}^{ex}=\{(t,x):t\geq t_{1},t-5\epsilon\leq r\leq t-\epsilon\}.

5.1.
Notation and auxiliary estimates

We begin by introducing some notation and auxiliary estimates.
In the exterior region, for any
\epsilon\leq\tilde{u}\leq 5\epsilon
, we define

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t,\tilde{u}}^{ex}

\displaystyle=\{(s,x)\in\mathcal{D}^{ex}:s=t,s-\tilde{u}\leq|x|\leq s-\epsilon\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}

\displaystyle=\{(s,x):t_{1}\leq s\leq t,s-\tilde{u}\leq|x|\leq s-\epsilon\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{u}}

\displaystyle=\{(s,x)\in\mathcal{D}^{ex}:s-|x|=\tilde{u}\}.

The natural energy and the conformal energy are defined as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{ex}(\tilde{u},t,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t,\tilde{u}}^{ex}}\big(|\partial_{t}\varphi|^{2}+\sum_{a}|\partial_{a}\varphi|^{2}\big)\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{ex}_{con}(\tilde{u},t,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t,\tilde{u}}^{ex}}\big(|L_{0}\varphi+2\varphi|^{2}+|\Omega\varphi|^{2}+\sum_{a}|L_{a}\varphi|^{2}\big)\,dx.

It is also useful to define the following energies with the various boundary terms that will arise in the energy estimates:

\displaystyle E^{ex}(\tilde{u},t,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}^{ex}(\tilde{u},t,\varphi)+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{u}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}}|G\varphi|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle E_{con}^{ex}(\tilde{u},t,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}^{ex}_{con}(\tilde{u},t,\varphi)+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{u}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}}|(t+r)L\varphi+2\varphi|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{u}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}}\tilde{u}^{2}\big|\frac{\Omega\varphi}{r}\big|^{2}\,dx.

Lemma 5.2

(Exterior energy estimates)
.

Let
\varphi
be a solution to the wave equation vanishing on
\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}
. Then, the following natural energy estimate holds

(5.1)

\mathcal{E}^{ex}(\tilde{u},t,\varphi)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{u}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}}|G\varphi|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{ex}(\tilde{u},t_{1},\varphi)^{1/2}+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\|\Box\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma^{ex}_{s,\tilde{u}})}\,ds,

as well as the conformal energy estimate

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{ex}_{con}(\tilde{u},t,\varphi)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{u}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}}|(t+r)L\varphi+2\varphi|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{u}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}}\tilde{u}^{2}\big|\frac{\Omega\varphi}{r}\big|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{ex}_{con}(\tilde{u},t_{1},\varphi)^{1/2}+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\|(s+r)\Box\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma^{ex}_{s,\tilde{u}})}\,ds.

Proof.

\bullet
We prove (
5.1
) by multiplying the wave equation by
\partial_{t}\varphi
and integrating over the region
\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}
. This yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t,\tilde{u}}^{ex}}|\partial\varphi|^{2}\,dx+\sum_{a}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{u}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}}|G_{a}\varphi|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1},\tilde{u}}^{ex}}|\partial\varphi|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}}2(-\Box\varphi)\partial_{t}\varphi\,dxds,

and the conclusion follows by applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to the spacetime integral and then Gronwall inequality.

\bullet
We next prove (
5.2
). Multiplying the wave operator by
(r^{2}+t^{2})\partial_{t}\varphi+2rt\partial_{r}\varphi+2t\varphi
, we get

\displaystyle-\Box\varphi\,\cdot

\displaystyle\left((r^{2}+t^{2})\partial_{t}\varphi+2rt\partial_{r}\varphi+2t\varphi\right)=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\left((r^{2}+t^{2})|\partial\varphi|^{2}+4tr\partial_{r}\varphi\partial_{t}\varphi+4t\varphi\partial_{t}\varphi+4\varphi^{2}+4r\varphi\partial_{r}\varphi\right)

\displaystyle-\partial^{a}\left((r^{2}+t^{2})\partial_{a}\varphi\partial_{t}\varphi+tx_{a}(|\partial_{t}\varphi|^{2}-\partial^{b}\varphi\partial_{b}\varphi)+2tr\partial_{r}\varphi\partial_{a}\varphi+2t\varphi\partial_{a}\varphi+2x_{a}\varphi\partial_{t}\varphi\right).

Integrating the above identity over the region
\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t,\tilde{u}}^{ex}}\big(|L_{0}\varphi+2\varphi|^{2}+|\Omega\varphi|^{2}+\sum_{a}|L_{a}\varphi|^{2}\big)\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{u}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}}|(t+r)L\varphi+2\varphi|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{u}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}}\tilde{u}^{2}\big|\frac{\Omega\varphi}{r}\big|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1},\tilde{u}}^{ex}}\big(|L_{0}\varphi+2\varphi|^{2}+|\Omega\varphi|^{2}+\sum_{a}|L_{a}\varphi|^{2}\big)\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{D}^{ex}_{t,\tilde{u}}}2(-\Box\varphi)[s(L_{0}\varphi+2\varphi)+x^{a}L_{a}\varphi]\,dxds.

The conclusion follows by again applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the spacetime integral and then Gronwall inequality.
∎

The next result, due to Lindblad and Rodnianski, is a type of spacetime Hardy inequality where the weight depends on the distance from the light cone
t=r
.

Lemma 5.3

(Hardy inequality
[
17
,
19
]
)
.

Assume that
\varphi
vanishes on
\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}
. Then in the exterior region
\mathcal{D}^{ex}
, for any
\tilde{u}\in[\epsilon,5\epsilon]
, we have the following Hardy inequality

(5.3)

\left\|(t-r)^{-1}\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,\tilde{u}}^{ex})}\lesssim\|\partial_{r}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,\tilde{u}}^{ex})}.

Proof.

Let
t
be fixed and
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Define
\chi(r)=r\varphi(t,r\omega)
so that
\partial_{r}\chi=\varphi+r\partial_{r}\varphi
. Since
\varphi
vanishes on
\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}
, we have
\chi(t-\epsilon)=0
. We compute

\displaystyle\int_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}\left|\frac{\chi}{t-r}\right|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}\chi^{2}\,d\left(\frac{1}{t-r}\right)=\frac{\chi^{2}}{t-r}\Big|_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}-2\int_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}\frac{\chi}{t-r}\partial_{r}\chi\,dr

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\chi^{2}(t-\tilde{u})}{\tilde{u}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}\left|\frac{\chi}{t-r}\right|^{2}\,dr+2\int_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}|\partial_{r}\chi|^{2}\,dr,

where Young’s inequality was used in the last step. Since
\tilde{u}\in[\epsilon,5\epsilon]
, we obtain

\int_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}\left|\frac{\chi}{t-r}\right|^{2}\,dr\leq 4\int_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}|\partial_{r}\chi|^{2}\,dr.

On the other hand, we observe that

|\partial_{r}\varphi|^{2}r^{2}=\Big|\partial_{r}\chi-\frac{\chi}{r}\Big|^{2}=|\partial_{r}\chi|^{2}-\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{\chi^{2}}{r}\Big),

which implies

\int_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}|\partial_{r}\chi|^{2}\,dr=\frac{\chi^{2}}{r}\Big|_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}+\int_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}|\partial_{r}\varphi|^{2}r^{2}\,dr\leq\int_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}|\partial_{r}\varphi|^{2}r^{2}\,dr.

Combining the above inequalities, we conclude that

\int_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}\Big|\frac{\varphi}{t-r}\Big|^{2}r^{2}\,dr\leq 4\int_{t-\tilde{u}}^{t-\epsilon}|\partial_{r}\varphi|^{2}r^{2}\,dr.

Integrating over the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
yields the desired estimate (
5.3
).
∎

We next record a Sobolev estimate in
\mathcal{D}^{ex}
, which can be shown adapting the proof of Lemma
4.2
to the exterior region. Note the key factor of the smallness parameter
\epsilon^{1/2}
that appears. Additionally, since we are in the exterior region, we also now gain decay in
r
compared to Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 5.4
.

For any point
(t,x)\in\mathcal{D}^{ex}
, the following pointwise decay estimate holds:

(5.4)

|f(t,x)|\lesssim r^{-1}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{r}\Omega^{\leq 2}f\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}.

5.2.
Bootstrap and global existence argument

We now initiate the bootstrap argument by making the following assumptions in the exterior region:

(5.5)

E^{ex}(5\epsilon,t,\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{3}\epsilon^{-|I|},\quad|I|+|J|\leq 5,\quad\mbox{for}\ \ t\in[t_{1},T^{*}).

Here,
C_{3}>C_{2}\gg 1
is a bootstrap constant to be chosen later, and
\epsilon
denotes the size of the initial data. We define

T^{*}:=\sup\{s\in(t_{1},+\infty):~\eqref{est:boots_ex}\ \mbox{holds on}\ [t_{1},s]\}.

Before improving these assumptions, we prove several immediate consequences.

Proposition 5.5
.

Under the bootstrap assumptions (
5.5
), the following hold on
[t_{1},T^{*})
:

(5.6)

\displaystyle\|\partial\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}+\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{5\epsilon}}|G\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{-|I|},\quad

\displaystyle|I|+|J|\leq 5,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{1-|I|},\ \quad

\displaystyle|I|+|J|\leq 5,

(5.8)

\displaystyle|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-|I|}r^{-1},\quad\ \quad\quad

\displaystyle|I|+|J|\leq 3.

Proof.

The estimate (
5.6
) follows directly from the bootstrap bounds (
5.5
).
For (
5.7
), we apply the Hardy inequality in Lemma
5.3
to
\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi
and obtain

\|(t-r)^{-1}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}\lesssim\|\partial_{r}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}.

Since
\epsilon\leq t-r\leq 5\epsilon
on the slice
\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex}
, we have

\|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}\lesssim\epsilon\|(t-r)^{-1}\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{1-|I|},

where we used (
5.6
) in the last step.
Finally, the pointwise estimate (
5.8
) follows from combining (
5.6
) with the Sobolev estimate (
5.4
).
∎

Lemma 5.6

(Exterior bounds on the nonlinearities)
.

Under the bootstrap assumptions (
5.5
),

(5.9)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}}\lesssim C_{3}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p-5}{2}-|I|}t^{-p+4+4\delta}+C_{3}^{p}\epsilon^{1-\delta-|I|}t^{-\frac{p}{2}+\frac{3}{2}+(p-3)\delta},\quad|I|+|J|\leq 5.

Proof.

\bullet
We first prove the bound when
|I|+|J|=0
. From the elementary inequality (
4.10
), we take
\psi
in
L^{\infty}
and apply Proposition
5.5
and both the pointwise estimates in (
3.5
) to find

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}^{p-1}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}^{p-1}\lesssim C_{3}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}-\delta}t^{-\frac{p}{2}+1+(p-2)\delta}.

\bullet
We next consider the case when
1\leq|I|+|J|\leq 5
. As in the local analysis and following Remark
4.4
, applying Leibniz rule yields

\displaystyle\|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1\to 5}=I\\
J_{1\to 5}=J\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{0\leq m\leq 5}\Big\||\phi|^{p-5}\prod_{k=1}^{m}|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi|\prod_{l=m+1}^{5}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq m\leq 5}\Big\||\psi|^{p-5}\prod_{k=1}^{m}|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi|\prod_{l=m+1}^{5}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}\Big).

Each term is estimated analogously to the local case by placing the highest-order derivative in
L^{2}
and controlling all remaining factors in
L^{\infty}
, using the estimates on
\phi
in Proposition
5.5
, the energy bound on
\psi
from (
3.4b
), and the pointwise bounds on
\psi
from (
3.5
). In the exterior region, the pointwise estimates of
\psi
and
\phi
yield the additional time decay.
Consequently, for
1\leq|I|+|J|\leq 5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,5\epsilon}^{ex})}\lesssim C_{3}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p-5}{2}-|I|}t^{-p+4+4\delta}+C_{3}^{p}\epsilon^{1-\delta-|I|}t^{-\frac{p}{2}+\frac{3}{2}+(p-3)\delta}.

∎

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

We are now in a position to close the bootstrap assumptions (
5.5
). First, by applying the
L^{2}
estimates in Proposition
4.3
, we have

\mathcal{E}^{ex}(5\epsilon,t_{1},\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|\partial\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\|_{{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1},5\epsilon}^{ex})}}\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{-|I|}.

Then by the energy estimate (
5.1
) and Lemma
5.6
, we deduce that

(5.10)

\displaystyle E^{ex}(5\epsilon,t,\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{ex}(5\epsilon,t_{1},\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\|\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{{L^{2}(\Sigma_{s,5\epsilon}^{ex})}}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{-|I|}+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}C_{3}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p-5}{2}-|I|}s^{-p+4+4\delta}\,ds+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}C_{3}^{p}\epsilon^{1-\delta-|I|}s^{-\frac{p}{2}+\frac{3}{2}+(p-3)\delta}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{-|I|}+C_{3}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p-5}{2}-|I|}+C_{3}^{p}\epsilon^{1-\delta-|I|}.

This estimate strictly improves the bootstrap bounds in (

) provided that

. Consequently, the bootstrap argument closes, and the solution exists globally in the exterior region.
Similar to (

), we also have the following energy bound

(5.11)

\displaystyle E^{ex}(4\epsilon,t,\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{-|I|},

which we will use in the next section to control certain boundary flux term.
∎

5.3.
Boundary flux estimates

In order to prove global existence in the interior region, we need to control certain flux terms along the null boundary
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
, shown in Figure
1
, that we will obtain from the exterior estimates. Since in the interior region we need to use both the natural and conformal energy in our bootstrap argument (unlike the exterior region where we only required the natural energy), these boundary flux terms will involve both natural and conformal energy components. For this reason, in the following proposition we derive several conformal energy estimates in the exterior region.
The last estimate in (
5.14
), involving the natural energy, will actually be used later in Section
7
when we establish regularity for our global solution.

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose that

(5.12)

C_{3}^{p-1}\epsilon^{(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}<1,\quad C_{3}^{p-1}\epsilon^{1-\delta}<1,\quad 0<\delta<\tfrac{p-5}{2(p+3)}.

Then in the exterior region
\mathcal{D}^{ex}
, for any
\tilde{u}\in[2\epsilon,5\epsilon],
we have the following conformal energy estimates

(5.13)

\begin{split}E_{con}^{ex}(\tilde{u},t,\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{1-|I|},\qquad|I|+|J|\leq 3,\\
E_{con}^{ex}(\tilde{u},t,\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{1-|I|},\qquad|I|+|J|=4,\quad|I|\geq 1,\\
E_{con}^{ex}(\tilde{u},t,\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{1-|I|},\qquad|I|+|J|=5,\quad|I|\geq 2,\end{split}

as well as the following natural and conformal energy estimates for
L_{\alpha}\phi

(5.14)

\begin{split}E_{con}^{ex}(\tilde{u},t,L_{\alpha}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon,\qquad\alpha=0,\cdots,3,\\
E_{con}^{ex}(\tilde{u},t,L_{a}\partial^{I}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{-|I|}+C_{3},\qquad a=1,2,3,\ |I|=1,2,\\
E^{ex}(\tilde{u},t,L_{a}\partial^{I}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{-|I|}+C_{3},\qquad\ a=1,2,3,\ |I|=1,2.\end{split}

Proof.

\bullet
We first prove (
5.13
) by considering the conformal energy estimates on
\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi
and dividing into cases depending on the number of vector fields.

Case 1.
|I|+|J|\leq 3
.
Note that in this case we can always estimate
\psi
in
L^{\infty}
using (
3.5
) and we can arrange for the term which remains in
L^{2}
to be
\phi
. We break up the nonlinear terms as follows

\displaystyle\|(t+r)

\displaystyle\partial^{I}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,\tilde{u}}^{ex})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1\to 3}=I\\
J_{1\to 3}=J\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{0\leq m\leq 3}\Big\|t|\phi|^{p-3}\prod_{k=1}^{m}|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi|\prod_{l=m+1}^{3}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,\tilde{u}}^{ex})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq m\leq 3}\Big\|t|\psi|^{p-3}\prod_{k=1}^{m}|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi|\prod_{l=m+1}^{3}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,\tilde{u}}^{ex})}+\Big\|t\phi|\psi|^{p-4}\prod_{l=1}^{3}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,\tilde{u}}^{ex})}\Big)

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{A}_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{2}+\mathcal{A}_{3}.

Estimate for
\mathcal{A}_{1}
.
The terms in
\mathcal{A}_{1}
involve at least a factor of
|\phi|^{p-3}
. We break these up depending on whether they contain at least one
\Gamma\phi
factor, or solely involve
\Gamma\psi
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1\to 3}=I\\
J_{1\to 3}=J\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{1\leq m\leq 3}\Big\|t|\phi|^{p-3}\prod_{k=1}^{m}|\partial^{I_{k}}\Omega^{J_{k}}\phi|\prod_{l=m+1}^{3}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,\tilde{u}}^{ex})}+\Big\|t|\phi|^{p-3}\prod_{l=1}^{3}|\partial^{I_{l}}\Omega^{J_{l}}\psi|\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t,\tilde{u}}^{ex})}\Big)

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{A}_{1}^{1}+\mathcal{A}_{1}^{2}.

Using Proposition
5.5
and (
3.5
) on the terms in

). Then acting
L
on both sides of (
5.18
), we obtain

\displaystyle\underline{L}L^{2}\phi

\displaystyle=L\left(\frac{1}{r}L\phi-\frac{1}{r}\underline{L}\phi+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi\pm\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}L^{2}\phi-\frac{1}{r}L\underline{L}\phi+\frac{1}{r^{2}}L\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi-\frac{1}{r^{2}}L\phi+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\underline{L}\phi-\frac{2}{r^{3}}\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi\pm L(\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)).

We compute, for
(t,x)\in\mathcal{D}^{ex}
,

|\underline{L}(rL^{2}\phi)|\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t+r)^{-2}.

Employing the fundamental theorem of calculus along the
u
-direction, we obtain

\displaystyle|rL^{2}\phi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &|rL^{2}\phi|_{\{t=t_{1},\ 1-3\epsilon\leq r\leq 1+\epsilon\}}\\
&|rL^{2}\phi|_{\{t-r=\epsilon\}}\end{aligned}\right.+\int_{\epsilon}^{5\epsilon}|\partial_{u}(rL^{2}\phi)|\,du

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-2}+\int_{\epsilon}^{5\epsilon}|\underline{L}(rL^{2}\phi)|\,du\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}(t+r)^{-2},

where we used Lemma
4.6
and the fact that
L^{2}\phi
vanishes on
t-r=\epsilon
. Finally, in the exterior region since
t-r\in[\epsilon,5\epsilon]
then
r\sim t\sim\langle t+r\rangle
, and thus

|L^{2}\phi|\lesssim r^{-1}\,|rL^{2}\phi|\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle t+r\rangle^{-3}.

∎

6.
Interior global existence for short-pulse data

In this section, we establish the following:

Proposition 6.1

(Global in time existence in the interior region)
.

There exists
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small such that (
4.1
) admits a global in time solution in the interior region

\mathcal{D}^{in}=\{(t,x):t\geq t_{1}=1+2\epsilon,\ 0\leq r\leq t-4\epsilon\}.

A key point is that the boundary flux control on
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
obtained in the exterior analysis (see Proposition
5.7
and Lemma
5.9
) provides the necessary boundary input for the interior energy inequalities. We use the hyperboloidal method and close the bootstrap by deriving refined bounds. Combining the interior and exterior arguments yields a global solution in
\mathcal{D}^{ex}\cup\mathcal{D}^{in}
for all
t\geq t_{1}
.

Before presenting the proof, we introduce the geometric setup and several auxiliary definitions that are used throughout this section.

6.1.
Hyperboloidal setting and basic estimates

In this subsection, we introduce the hyperboloidal foliation of the interior region
and collect several analytic tools that will be used in the proof of global existence. For
\tau\geq\tau_{0}:=\sqrt{3(1+4\epsilon)}
, we define (see also Figure
1
)

\mathcal{H}_{\tau}:=\{(t,x)\in\mathcal{D}^{in}:(t+1)^{2}-r^{2}=\tau^{2}\}.

We also decompose the hyperboloid into the two regions

(6.1)

\mathcal{H}_{\tau}=\big(\mathcal{H}_{\tau}\cap\{r\leq t/4\}\big)\cup\big(\mathcal{H}_{\tau}\cap\{r\geq t/8\}\big)=:{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}}\cup{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}}.

On each hyperboloid
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
, the time coordinate satisfies
\tau\leq t+1\leq\tau^{2}
.
Associated with this hyperboloidal foliation, we introduce the tangential vector fields

\displaystyle\not{\partial}_{\tau}=\frac{\tau}{t+1}\partial_{t},\quad\not{\partial}_{a}=\frac{x_{a}}{t+1}\partial_{t}+\partial_{a}=-\frac{x_{a}}{t(t+1)}\partial_{t}+\frac{L_{a}}{t},

and define the radial tangential derivative by
\not{\partial}_{r}=\omega^{a}\not{\partial}_{a}
.

For
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
, we define the
L^{2}
norm of a function
f
on the hyperboloid
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
by

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}:=\int_{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}}|f(t,x)|^{2}\,dx.

Using the parametrization
(t,x)=\big(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|y|^{2}}-1,\,y\big),

this norm can be equivalently written as

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}=\int_{r_{*}(\tau)\leq|y|\leq r^{*}(\tau)}|f(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|y|^{2}}-1,y)|^{2}\,dy,

where the integration region is given by

(6.2)

r_{*}(\tau)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{(2+2\epsilon)^{2}-\tau^{2}},&\tau_{0}\leq\tau\leq 2+2\epsilon,\\[4.0pt]
0,&\tau\geq 2+2\epsilon,\end{cases}\qquad r^{*}(\tau)=\dfrac{\tau^{2}-(1+4\epsilon)^{2}}{2(1+4\epsilon)},\quad\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

Lemma 6.2

(Hardy inequality)
.

In the interior region
\mathcal{D}^{in}
, we have the following Hardy inequality

(6.3)

\displaystyle\|r^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim r_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}-1,r_{*}\omega)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+(r^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f(r^{*}+4\epsilon,r^{*}\omega)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|\not{\partial}_{r}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})},

where
r_{*}
and
r^{*}
are defined in (
6.2
).

Proof.

Let
(t,x)=(t,r\omega)\in\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
with
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
. Then
f(t,x)=f(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|x|^{2}}-1,x)
. By the definition, we derive

(6.4)

\|r^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}=\int_{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}}\frac{f^{2}}{r^{2}}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{r_{*}}^{r^{*}}f^{2}(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|x|^{2}}-1,x)\,drd\omega,

where
r_{*}
and
r^{*}
are given in (
6.2
).
Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we derive

\displaystyle\int_{r_{*}}^{r^{*}}f^{2}(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|x|^{2}}-1,x)\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{r_{*}}^{r^{*}}\partial_{r}(rf^{2})(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|x|^{2}}-1,x)\,dr-2\int_{r_{*}}^{r^{*}}rf\partial_{r}f(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+|x|^{2}}-1,x)\,dr

\displaystyle\leq(rf^{2})\Big|_{r_{*}}^{r^{*}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{r_{*}}^{r^{*}}f^{2}\,dr+2\int_{r_{*}}^{r^{*}}|\not{\partial}_{r}f|^{2}r^{2}\,dr.

Integrating over the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and using (
6.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|r^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim r_{*}\|f(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}-1,r_{*}\omega)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+r^{*}\|f(r^{*}+4\epsilon,r^{*}\omega)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}+\|\not{\partial}_{r}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}.

∎

Definition 6.3
.

The natural and conformal energy functionals on constant hyperboloidal time slices read

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\not{\partial}_{\tau}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a}\|\not{\partial}_{a}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{con}}^{in}(\tau,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\tau\not{\partial}_{\tau}\varphi+2x^{a}\not{\partial}_{a}\varphi+2\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a}\|\tau\not{\partial}_{a}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}.

Lemma 6.4

(Hyperboloidal energy inequality
[
14
,
9
,
22
]
)
.

Suppose that
\varphi
is the solution to the wave equation, then we have the natural energy estimate

(6.5)

\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\varphi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|\partial\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{in}}|G\varphi|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\Box\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau},

and the conformal energy estimate

(6.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{con}}^{in}(\tau,\varphi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{in}}\Big[(t+r+1)L\varphi+2\varphi\Big]^{2}+\Big|\frac{1}{r}\Omega\varphi\Big|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\tilde{\tau}\Box\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau}.

Proof.

\bullet
We first prove (
6.5
).
First, multiplying the equation by
\partial_{t}\varphi
, and integrating over the region
\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{in}=\{(t,x)\in\mathcal{D}^{in}:(t+1)^{2}-|x|^{2}\leq\tau^{2}\}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\partial\varphi|^{2}+\frac{x^{a}}{t+1}\partial_{a}\varphi\partial_{t}\varphi\right)\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in}}|\partial\varphi|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{in}}|G\varphi|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{in}}\frac{\tilde{\tau}}{t+1}(-\Box\varphi)\partial_{t}\varphi\,dxd\tilde{\tau}.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}}\big(\frac{1}{2}|\partial\varphi|^{2}+\frac{x^{a}}{t+1}\partial_{a}\varphi\partial_{t}\varphi\big)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}\varphi+\frac{x^{a}}{t+1}\partial_{a}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a}\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\partial_{a}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\frac{\Omega\varphi}{t+1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\not{\partial}_{\tau}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a}\|\not{\partial}_{a}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}.

Then we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\varphi)\lesssim\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in}}|\partial\varphi|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{in}}|G\varphi|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\Box\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tilde{\tau},\varphi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,d\tilde{\tau}.

An application of Gronwall inequality (
2.7
) yields the desired result.

\bullet
We next turn to (
6.6
). Define a multiplier as
\mathcal{M}=(r^{2}+(t+1)^{2})\partial_{t}+2r(t+1)\partial_{r}
. Multiplying the equation by
\mathcal{M}\varphi+2(t+1)\varphi
, we obtain

\displaystyle-\Box\varphi\big(\mathcal{M}\varphi+2(t+1)\varphi\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\Big[(r^{2}+(t+1)^{2})|\partial\varphi|^{2}+4r(t+1)\partial_{r}\varphi\partial_{t}\varphi+4(t+1)\varphi\partial_{t}\varphi+4\varphi^{2}+4r\varphi\partial_{r}\varphi\Big]

\displaystyle-\partial^{a}\Big[(r^{2}+(t+1)^{2})\partial_{a}\varphi\partial_{t}\varphi+(t+1)x_{a}(|\partial_{t}\varphi|^{2}-\sum_{b}|\partial_{b}\varphi|^{2})

\displaystyle+2r(t+1)\partial_{r}\varphi\partial_{a}\varphi+2(t+1)\varphi\partial_{a}\varphi+2x_{a}\varphi\partial_{t}\varphi\Big].

Integrating over the region
\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{in}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{con}^{in}(\tau,\varphi)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in}}\big(|\partial\varphi|^{2}+|\varphi|^{2}\big)\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}[(r+t+1)L\varphi+2\varphi]^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}\big|\frac{1}{r}\Omega\varphi|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{in}}\big|\frac{\tilde{\tau}}{t+1}(\Box\varphi)(M\varphi+2(t+1)\varphi)\big|\,dxd\tilde{\tau},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{con}^{in}(\tau,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\tau\not{\partial}_{\tau}\varphi+2x^{a}\not{\partial}_{a}\varphi+2\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a}\|\tau\not{\partial}_{a}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big\|\frac{\mathcal{M}\varphi}{t+1}+2\varphi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a}\|\tau\not{\partial}_{a}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{2}.

Using Gronwall inequality we are done.
∎

Next, we introduce the Klainerman-Sobolev inequalities on truncated hyperboloids. For simplicity, we denote
Z=\{\partial_{t},L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}\}.
While the proof is straightforward, a key part of our analysis relies on the novel
L^{q}
estimate, for
q>6
, given in (
6.9
). Note that in this estimate we only need to take
\Omega^{\leq 1}
as a vector field derivative, instead of
\Omega^{\leq 2}
, on the right hand side.

Lemma 6.5

(Klainerman-Sobolev inequalities on truncated hyperboloids)
.

(i)

If
(t,x)\in{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}}
, then

(6.7)

|\varphi(t,x)|\lesssim\tau^{-1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\epsilon^{(|I|-\frac{3}{2})\lambda}\left\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}Z^{I}\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})},

where
\lambda
is an arbitrary nonnegative constant.

(ii)

If
(t,x)\in{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}}
, then

(6.8)

\displaystyle|\varphi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\varphi(t^{*},x^{*})|+\tau^{-1}|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|J_{1}|\leq 2,\ |J_{2}|\leq 2\\
|I|=1\end{subarray}}\left\|\frac{\tau}{t}\Omega^{J_{1}}\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\frac{\tau}{t}\Omega^{J_{2}}Z^{I}\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
(t^{*},x^{*})
denotes the intersection point of
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
and
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
.

(iii)

Let
q>6
. If
(t_{0},x_{0})\in{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}}
, then

(6.9)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau}\cap\{r_{0}\leq|x|\leq r^{*}\})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(r^{*})^{\frac{3}{q}}\sum_{|J|\leq 1}\|\Omega^{J}\varphi(t^{*},r^{*}\omega)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle+\tau^{-1}r_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|J|\leq 1,\ |I|=1\end{subarray}}\left\|\frac{\tau}{t}\Omega^{J}\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\frac{\tau}{t}Z^{I}\Omega^{J}\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
(t^{*},x^{*})
denotes the intersection point of
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
and
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
.

Proof.

\bullet
We first prove (
6.7
). Fix
(t_{0},x_{0})\in\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
with
|x_{0}|\leq t_{0}/4
. For
|z|\leq 1
, set

x(z)=x_{0}+t_{0}\epsilon^{\lambda}z,\qquad t(z)=\sqrt{|x(z)|^{2}+\tau^{2}}-1,\qquad g(z)=\varphi(t(z),x(z)).

By Sobolev embedding on the unit ball
H^{2}(B_{1})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(B_{1})
,

|g(0)|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\int_{|z|\leq 1}|\partial_{z}^{I}g(z)|^{2}\,dz.

Since
\frac{\partial t}{\partial x_{a}}=\frac{x_{a}}{t+1}
, we have the chain rule identity

(6.10)

\partial_{z_{a}}=t_{0}\epsilon^{\lambda}\Big(\partial_{x_{a}}+\frac{x_{a}}{t+1}\partial_{t}\Big)=t_{0}\epsilon^{\lambda}\not{\partial}_{a}.

Then

\displaystyle|g(0)|^{2}=|\varphi(t_{0},x_{0})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\int_{|z|\leq 1}|\partial_{z}^{I}\varphi(t(z),x(z))|^{2}\,dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{|x-x_{0}|\leq t_{0}\epsilon^{\lambda}}|\varphi(t,x)|^{2}(t_{0}\epsilon^{\lambda})^{-3}\,dx+\int_{|x-x_{0}|\leq t_{0}\epsilon^{\lambda}}\sum_{a}|\not{\partial}_{a}\varphi(t,x)|^{2}(t_{0}\epsilon^{\lambda})^{-1}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{|x-x_{0}|\leq t_{0}\epsilon^{\lambda}}\sum_{a,b}|\not{\partial}_{a}\not{\partial}_{b}\varphi(t,x)|^{2}(t_{0}\epsilon^{\lambda})\,dx.

Using (
6.10
), we deduce

\displaystyle|\not{\partial}_{a}\varphi|\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\sum_{|I|=1}|Z^{I}\varphi|,\qquad|\not{\partial}_{a}\not{\partial}_{b}\varphi|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{2}}\sum_{1\leq|I|\leq 2}|Z^{I}\varphi|,

which implies

\displaystyle|\varphi(t_{0},x_{0})|\lesssim\tau^{-1}t_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\epsilon^{(|I|-\frac{3}{2})\lambda}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}Z^{I}\varphi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

\bullet
We next prove (
6.8
).
Let
(t,x)\in\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
with
|x|\geq t/8
. By the fundamental theorem of calculus,

(6.11)

\varphi^{2}(t,x)=\varphi^{2}(t^{*},x^{*})-\int_{r}^{r^{*}}\partial_{\rho}(\varphi^{2}(t,\rho\omega))\,d\rho,

where
(t^{*},x^{*})
denotes the intersection point of
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
and
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
, satisfying

(6.12)

t^{*}=|x^{*}|+4\epsilon,\qquad|x^{*}|=\frac{\tau^{2}-(1+4\epsilon)^{2}}{2(1+4\epsilon)}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}(\varphi^{2}(t,\rho\omega))

\displaystyle=2\varphi\partial_{\rho}\varphi(t(\rho),\rho\omega),\qquad t(\rho)=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\tau^{2}}-1,

\displaystyle|\partial_{\rho}\varphi(t(\rho),\rho\omega)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{a}|\not{\partial}_{a}\varphi|\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\sum_{|I|=1}|Z^{I}\varphi|,\quad Z=\{\partial_{t},L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}\}.

Applying the above estimates to (
6.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\varphi(t,x)|^{2}\leq|\varphi(t^{*},x^{*})|^{2}+2\sum_{|I|=1}\int_{r}^{r^{*}}\frac{1}{t}|\varphi||Z^{I}\varphi|\,d\rho,

together with the Sobolev embedding on the unit sphere,
H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
,

|\varphi(t,r\omega)|\lesssim\sum_{|J|\leq 2}\|\Omega^{J}\varphi(t,r\omega)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})},

giving

\displaystyle|\varphi(t,x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\varphi(t^{*},x^{*})|^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|J_{1}|\leq 2,\ |J_{2}|\leq 2\\
|I|=1\end{subarray}}\int_{r}^{r^{*}}\frac{1}{t}\|\Omega^{J_{1}}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\Omega^{J_{2}}Z^{I}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\,d\rho

\displaystyle\lesssim|\varphi(t^{*},x^{*})|^{2}+\tau^{-2}r^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|J_{1}|\leq 2,\ |J_{2}|\leq 2\\
|I|=1\end{subarray}}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t}\Omega^{J_{1}}\varphi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t}\Omega^{J_{2}}Z^{I}\varphi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

\bullet
Lastly we prove (
6.9
).
Let
q>6
and assume
(t_{0},x_{0})\in\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
,
x_{0}=|x_{0}|\omega
and
|x_{0}|\geq t_{0}/8,
where
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Fix
\omega
,
(t^{*},x^{*})
is the intersection point of
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
and
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
, which satisfies (
6.12
).
For a fixed
\rho\in[r_{0},r^{*}]
we can apply the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
to obtain

\|\varphi(t(\rho),\rho\omega)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim_{q}\sum_{|J|\leq 1}\|\Omega^{J}\varphi(t(\rho),\rho\omega)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})},\quad t(\rho)=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\tau^{2}}-1.

Repeating the argument of (
6.8
) for
|J|\leq 1
yields

\displaystyle\|\Omega^{J}\varphi(t(\rho),\rho\omega)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Omega^{J}\varphi(t^{*},r^{*}\omega)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\tau^{-2}\rho^{-1}\sum_{|I|=1}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t}\Omega^{J}\varphi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t}Z^{I}\Omega^{J}\varphi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

Summing over
|J|\leq 1
gives

\displaystyle\|\varphi(t(\rho),\rho\omega)\|^{2}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|J|\leq 1}\|\Omega^{J}\varphi(t^{*},r^{*}\omega)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\tau^{-2}\rho^{-1}\sum_{|I|=1,\ |J|\leq 1}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t}\Omega^{J}\varphi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t}Z^{I}\Omega^{J}\varphi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

Integrating in
\rho
we have

\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau}\cap\{r_{0}\leq r\leq r^{*}\})}^{q}=\int_{r_{0}}^{r^{*}}\|\varphi(t(\rho),\rho\omega)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{q}\,\rho^{2}\,d\rho.

Thus, combining with the above estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau}\cap\{r_{0}\leq r\leq r^{*}\})}^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q}\sum_{|J|\leq 1}\|\Omega^{J}\varphi(t^{*},r^{*}\omega)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{q}\int_{r_{0}}^{r^{*}}\rho^{2}\,d\rho

\displaystyle+\tau^{-q}\sum_{|I|=1,\ |J|\leq 1}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t}\Omega^{J}\varphi\Big\|^{\frac{q}{2}}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t}Z^{I}\Omega^{J}\varphi\Big\|^{\frac{q}{2}}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\int_{r_{0}}^{r^{*}}\rho^{-\frac{q}{2}+2}\,d\rho.

Since
q>6
, we have
-\frac{q}{2}+2<-1
, hence together with
r_{0}\leq r^{*}
,

\int_{r_{0}}^{r^{*}}\rho^{2}\,d\rho\lesssim(r^{*})^{3},\qquad\int_{r_{0}}^{r^{*}}\rho^{-\frac{q}{2}+2}\,d\rho\lesssim r_{0}^{-\frac{q}{2}+3}.

Taking the
q
-th root gives (
6.9
).
∎

6.2.
Bootstrap assumptions

In order to prove Proposition
6.1
, we introduce the following bootstrap assumptions for
\phi
. For a sufficiently large constant
C_{4}>C_{3}\gg 1
to be determined later, we impose the following assumptions on
[\tau_{0},\tau^{*})
:

(6.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\Omega\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,\Omega\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\partial\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{a}\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,L_{a}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{a}\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,L_{a}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}.

In the above,
\epsilon
is the size of the initial data, and we define

\tau^{*}=\sup\{\tau\in(\tau_{0},\infty):\eqref{est:Boot_in}\ holds\ on\ [\tau_{0},\tau)\}.

We first write down some immediate
L^{2}
estimates based on our bootstrap assumptions.

Proposition 6.6

(Preliminary interior energy estimates)
.

Under the assumptions (
6.13
), for
\tau\in[\tau_{0},\tau^{*})
, the following bounds hold

(6.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|\leq 1}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\partial\Omega^{J}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},

(6.15)

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\partial\partial\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}})}+\sum_{a=1}^{3}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\partial L_{a}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{4},

(6.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|\leq 1}\sum_{a=1}^{3}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}L_{a}\Omega^{J}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}})}+\sum_{|J|\leq 2}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega^{J}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},

(6.17)

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{3}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}L_{a}\partial\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}})}+\sum_{a,b}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}L_{a}L_{b}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{4}.

Proof.

Estimates (
6.14
) and (
6.15
) follow directly from the interior bootstrap assumptions (
6.13
) and the definition of the natural energy on hyperboloids. Then, the first term in (
6.17
) follows from Lemma
2.2
, and (
6.14
)–(
6.15
).

Next, by applying the Hardy inequality (
6.3
) and noting that
r\leq t+1
on
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\frac{\tau}{r}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau r_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\phi(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+r_{*}^{2}}-1,r_{*}\omega)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\tau(r^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\phi(r^{*}+4\epsilon,r^{*}\omega)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|\tau\not{\partial}_{r}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})},

where
r_{*},r^{*}
are defined in (
6.2
) and
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
Using (
6.13
), Propositions
4.3
and
5.5
, we get

\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}})}\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using the identities

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{t+1}L_{a}=\frac{t}{t+1}\tau\not{\partial}_{a}+\frac{x_{a}}{t+1}\not{\partial}_{\tau},\qquad\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega_{ab}=\frac{x_{a}}{t+1}\tau\not{\partial}_{b}-\frac{x_{b}}{t+1}\tau\not{\partial}_{a},

together with the energy bounds in (
6.13
), we obtain (
6.16
) and the second term in (
6.17
).

∎

Lemma 6.7
.

Under the bootstrap assumptions (

), for any

, we have

(6.18)

On the other hand, for any

,

Proof.

\bullet

). Let

. Applying estimate (
6.7
) in Lemma

, we get

\displaystyle|\phi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{-1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\epsilon^{(|I|-\frac{3}{2})\lambda}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}Z^{I}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim\tau^{-1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigl(C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2}\lambda}+C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda}+C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda}\bigr),

.

\bullet
Next we prove (

and

. Then

. Using estimate (

\displaystyle\|\phi(t,x)

\displaystyle\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau}\cap\{r\geq t/8\})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(r^{*})^{\frac{3}{q}}\sum_{|J|\leq 1}\|\Omega^{J}\phi(t^{*},r^{*}\omega)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\tau^{-1}r^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{q}}\sum_{|J|\leq 1,\ |I|=1}\left\|\frac{\tau}{t}\Omega^{J}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\frac{\tau}{t}Z^{I}\Omega^{J}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{-1}(r^{*})^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{q}}+C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{-1}r^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{q}}\lesssim C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{-1}r^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{q}},

where we used Proposition
6.6
and (

).
∎

6.3.
Combining the boundary flux and interior estimates

In this subsection, we close the interior bootstrap assumptions (
6.13
) on
\phi
up to the maximal hyperboloidal time
\tau^{*}
. The argument relies on the hyperboloidal energy estimates in the interior region, whose right-hand side contains three types of contributions: initial terms on
\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in}
, boundary flux terms on
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
and spacetime integrals of the nonlinear source. Accordingly, we first derive uniform
L^{2}
bounds for the boundary terms on
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
, and then establish
L^{2}
bounds on
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
for the nonlinear difference

\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)=|\psi+\phi|^{p-1}(\psi+\phi)-|\psi|^{p-1}\psi.

Combining these with the interior energy estimates yields improved bounds, which closes the bootstrap for
\tau\in[\tau_{0},\tau^{*})
once
\epsilon
is sufficiently small.

We start with the boundary terms. Since the interior energy identities pick up flux contributions involving good derivatives, rotations, and the modified conformal combinations, we record the following uniform bounds.

Lemma 6.8

(Boundary estimates)
.

The following boundary
L^{2}
estimates: for good derivatives we have:

(6.20)

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|\leq 1}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|G\Omega^{J}\phi|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|G\partial\phi|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}+\sum_{a}\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|GL_{a}\phi|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{1/2}.

For rotations, we have

(6.21)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq|J|\leq 2}\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}\Big|\frac{1}{r}\Omega^{J}\phi\Big|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}+\sum_{a}\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}\Big|\frac{1}{r}\Omega L_{a}\phi\Big|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{1/2}.

The following modified conformal boundary terms obey:

(6.22)

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|\leq 1}\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}[(r+t+1)L\Omega^{J}\phi+2\Omega^{J}\phi]^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}+\sum_{a}\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}[(r+t+1)LL_{a}\phi+2L_{a}\phi]^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{1/2}.

Proof.

Since we work on the boundary
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
where
r=t-4\epsilon\geq t_{1}-4\epsilon=1-2\epsilon
, we note that
t=r+4\epsilon\sim r
.

\bullet
We first prove (
6.20
) and (
6.21
). We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1:
The first step is to derive pointwise estimates on
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
. Note the identities

(6.23)

\displaystyle G_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{a}}{t+r}L_{0}+\frac{1}{t}L_{a}-\frac{\omega_{a}x^{b}}{t(t+r)}L_{b}=\omega_{a}L-\frac{\omega^{b}}{r}\Omega_{ab},\quad\partial_{t}=\frac{1}{2}(L+\underline{L}),

\displaystyle\partial_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{a}}{2}(L-\underline{L})-\frac{\omega^{b}}{r}\Omega_{ab},\quad L_{a}=\frac{\omega_{a}}{2}((t+r)L-(t-r)\underline{L})-\frac{t}{r}\omega^{b}\Omega_{ab}.

Hence, for
|J|\leq 1
, using
t\sim r
on
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
and Proposition
5.8
, we obtain

(6.24)

|G\Omega^{J}\phi|\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\Big(|L_{0}\Omega^{J}\phi|+\sum_{a}|L_{a}\Omega^{J}\phi|\Big)\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-2}.

Next, using the standard decomposition and estimates in Proposition
5.8
and Lemma
5.9
, we infer that

\displaystyle|G\partial\phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim|L^{2}\phi|+|L\underline{L}\phi|+\frac{1}{r}|L\Omega\phi|+\frac{1}{r}|L\phi|+\frac{1}{r}\sum_{1\leq|J|\leq 2}|\Omega^{J}\phi|+\frac{1}{r}|\underline{L}\Omega\phi|+\frac{1}{r}|\underline{L}\phi|\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-2},

and similarly

\displaystyle|GL_{a}\phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim(t+r)|L^{2}\phi|+|L\phi|+(t-r)|L\underline{L}\phi|+\frac{t}{r}|L\Omega\phi|+\frac{t-r}{r^{2}}|\Omega\phi|+\frac{1}{r}|L_{a}\Omega\phi|+\frac{1}{r}|L_{a}\phi|\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-2}.

Moreover, for
1\leq|J|\leq 2
, it follows from (
5.8
) that

\Big|\frac{1}{r}\Omega^{J}\phi\Big|\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-2}.

Finally, we use (
2.2
) and Proposition
5.8
to conclude

(6.25)

\Big|\frac{1}{r}\Omega L_{a}\phi\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\big(|L_{b}\Omega\phi|+|L_{b}\phi|\big)\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-2}.

Step 2:
Our second step is to consider
L^{2}
-integrals over
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
.
Using
dx\sim r^{2}\,dr\,d\omega
on
\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}
and the bound

|Q|\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-2}
(where
Q
stands for any of the quantities in
(
6.24
)–(
6.25
)), we compute

(6.26)

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|Q|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{r\geq 1-2\epsilon}(C_{3}^{2}\epsilon\,r^{-4})\,r^{2}\,dr\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Applying (
6.26
) to
Q\in\{G\Omega^{\leq 1}\phi,G\partial\phi,GL_{a}\phi\}
, we obtain (
6.20
). Applying (
6.26
) to

Q\in\{\frac{1}{r}\Omega\Omega^{\leq 1}\phi,\frac{1}{r}\Omega L_{a}\phi\}
gives (
6.21
).

\bullet
We finally turn to (
6.22
). First, using (
6.23
), we have

\displaystyle|LL_{a}\phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim(t+r)|L^{2}\phi|+|L\phi|+(t-r)|L\underline{L}\phi|+\frac{t}{r}|L\Omega\phi|+\frac{t-r}{r^{2}}|\Omega\phi|\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-2}.

Proposition
5.8
gives us

|L\Omega^{J}\phi|\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-2},\qquad|J|\leq 1.

Applying (
6.26
) to
Q\in\{LL_{a}\phi,L\Omega^{\leq 1}\phi\}
we obtain

(6.27)

\sum_{a}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|LL_{a}\phi|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{|J|\leq 1}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|L\Omega^{J}\phi|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence, for
|J|\leq 1
, applying (
6.27
) and estimates in (
5.14
) with
\tilde{u}=4\epsilon\in[2\epsilon,5\epsilon]
, we get

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}[(r+t+1)L\Omega^{J}\phi+2\Omega^{J}\phi]^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}[(r+t)L\Omega^{J}\phi+2\Omega^{J}\phi]^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|L\Omega^{J}\phi|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon+C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{1-2\epsilon}^{\infty}r^{-2}\,dr\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Similarly, for each
a
we apply the same splitting:

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}[(r+t+1)LL_{a}\phi+2L_{a}\phi]^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}[(r+t)LL_{a}\phi+2L_{a}\phi]^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|LL_{a}\phi|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

Next, we estimate the nonlinear source term. The goal is to control
\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)
and its derivatives in
L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})
, both with and without the hyperboloidal weight
\tau
, in a form that is integrable in
\tau
.

Lemma 6.9
.

Under the bootstrap assumptions (
6.13
), the following
L^{2}
estimates hold

(6.28)

\begin{split}\|\partial^{\leq 1}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}+\|\Omega\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}+\|L_{a}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{p+3}{4}+\frac{p+3}{2}\delta},\end{split}

and the following weighted
L^{2}
estimates hold

(6.29)

\begin{split}\|\tau\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}+\|\tau\Omega\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}+\|\tau L_{a}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{p-1}{4}+\frac{p+3}{2}\delta}.\end{split}

Proof.

Recall that for any
\tilde{\Gamma}\in\{\Omega,\partial,L_{a}\}

(6.30)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\phi|^{p}+|\phi||\psi|^{p-1},

(6.31)

\displaystyle|\tilde{\Gamma}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\phi|^{p-1}|\tilde{\Gamma}\phi|+|\phi|^{p-1}|\tilde{\Gamma}\psi|+|\psi|^{p-1}|\tilde{\Gamma}\phi|+|\phi||\psi|^{p-2}|\tilde{\Gamma}\psi|.

We now decompose the
L^{2}
integrals into two terms coming from an interior region
{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}}=H_{\tau}\cap\{r\leq t/4\}
and a ‘far’ or exterior region
{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}}=\mathcal{H}_{\tau}\cap\{r\geq t/8\}
.

\bullet
We first prove (
6.28
).
Using (
6.30
), we obtain

(6.32)

\|\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim\||\phi|^{p}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}+\|\phi|\psi|^{p-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}=:\mathcal{A}_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{2}.

Interior contribution for
\mathcal{A}_{1}
.

On
\{r\leq t/4\}
we use the weighted
L^{2}
control of
\phi
together with the
L^{\infty}
decay of
\phi
,
obtaining

(6.33)

\displaystyle\||\phi|^{p}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\Big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\phi|^{p-1}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p-1}{8}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}(p-1)}.

Here we used (
6.16
) and (
6.18
) for the
L^{\infty}
bound.

Exterior contribution for
\mathcal{A}_{1}
.

On
\{r\geq t/8\}
we apply Hölder inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\||\phi|^{p}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{2}=\||\phi|^{2p}\|_{L^{1}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\Big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\phi|^{2p-1}\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}.

Since
\tau\leq t+1\leq\tau^{2}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
, the second factor can be bounded by

\Big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\phi|^{2p-1}\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim\tau\|\phi\|_{L^{4p-2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{2p-1}.

We then apply the
L^{q}
estimate (
6.19
) from Lemma
6.7
to obtain

\|\phi\|_{L^{4p-2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{3}{4p-2}}\,.

All together, this implies

(6.34)

\||\phi|^{p}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim(C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}})^{p}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+2}.

Estimate of
\mathcal{A}_{2}
.

From (
3.5
), we derive

(6.35)

\displaystyle|\psi|\lesssim C_{1}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta},\qquad|\psi|\lesssim C_{1}\langle t+r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}.

Using the Hölder inequality again, we find

\mathcal{A}_{2}\lesssim\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\Big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\psi|^{p-1}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

Since
\tau\leq t+1\leq\tau^{2}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
, using (
6.35
), the second factor can be bounded by

\Big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\psi|^{p-1}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{1}^{p-1}\epsilon^{(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{p+3}{4}+\frac{p+3}{2}\delta},

which implies

(6.36)

\mathcal{A}_{2}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{p+3}{4}+\frac{p+3}{2}\delta}.

Combining (
6.32
), (
6.33
), (
6.34
) and (
6.36
) gives the first bound in (
6.28
).

Next, we illustrate the estimate for
\tilde{\Gamma}=\Omega
; the cases
\tilde{\Gamma}=\partial
and
\tilde{\Gamma}=L_{a}
are similar.
From (
6.31
), we decompose

\displaystyle\|\Omega\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}+\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}+\||\psi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}+\|\phi|\psi|^{p-2}\Omega\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

The last two terms are estimated in the same way as
\mathcal{A}_{2}
using
L^{\infty}
decay for
\psi
or
\Omega\psi
and
L^{2}
estimates for
\phi
or
\Omega\phi
.
For the first term
\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}}
, we again split it into two parts. On
\{r\leq t/4\}
, combining (
6.16
) and (
6.18
), we deduce

\displaystyle\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}\lesssim\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\Big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\phi|^{p-1}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p-1}{8}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}(p-1)}.

On
\{r\geq t/8\}
, the Hölder inequality implies

\displaystyle\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}

\displaystyle=\||\phi|^{2p-2}|\Omega\phi|^{2}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim\Big\||\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\frac{t^{2}}{\tau^{2}}|\phi|^{2p-2}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
\tau\leq t+1\leq\tau^{2}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
, the second factor can be bounded by

(6.37)

\left\|\frac{t^{2}}{\tau^{2}}|\phi|^{2p-2}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim\tau^{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{4p-4}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{2p-2}\lesssim C_{4}^{2p-2}\epsilon^{p-1}\tau^{-3p+\frac{11}{2}},

where we used (
6.19
) in the last inequality.
For the first factor, applying Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, we derive

(6.38)

\displaystyle\Big\||\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi\Big\|_{L^{4}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi\Big\|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{\frac{3}{4}}.

Combining (
6.37
) and (
6.38
) and using Proposition
6.6
, we obtain

\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}-\frac{3}{8}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{11}{4}}.

For the second term
\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\psi\|_{L^{2}}
, we bound it by

\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\psi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}+\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\psi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}.

On
\{r\leq t/4\}
, using (
6.16
), (
6.18
), and (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\psi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\left\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\phi|^{p-2}\Omega\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{1-\delta+\frac{p-2}{8}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+3}.

On
\{r\geq t/8\}
, the Hölder inequality yields

\displaystyle\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\psi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim\left\||\phi|^{p-1}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\|\Omega\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

From (
6.19
), we derive

\left\||\phi|^{p-1}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}=\|\phi\|^{p-1}_{L^{2p-2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p-1}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+3}.

Thus, combining it with (

), we get

\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\psi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}-\delta}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+3}.

Therefore, we conclude that

\|\Omega\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{p+3}{4}+\frac{p+3}{2}\delta}.

The bounds for
\partial\mathcal{N}
and

follow in the same way by replacing
\Omega
with
\partial
or
L_{a}
. This completes the proof of (
6.28
).

\bullet
We now turn to (
6.29
).
Multiplying (
6.30
) and (
6.31
) by
\tau
, we repeat the above argument.
For the first bound in (
6.29
), using (
6.30
), we get

\|\tau\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim\|\tau|\phi|^{p}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}+\|\tau\phi|\psi|^{p-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}=:\mathcal{A}_{3}+\mathcal{A}_{4}.

Estimate of
\mathcal{A}_{3}
.
Splitting
\mathcal{A}_{3}
into two parts

\mathcal{A}_{3}\lesssim\|\tau|\phi|^{p}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}+\|\tau|\phi|^{p}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}.

On
\{r\leq t/4\}
, applying (
6.16
) and (
6.18
), we get

(6.39)

\displaystyle\|\tau|\phi|^{p}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}\left\|(t+1)|\phi|^{p-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p-1}{8}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{5}{2}}.

On
\{r\geq t/8\}
, using the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\tau|\phi|^{p}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}=\left\|\tau^{2}|\phi|^{2p}\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\left\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}}))}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\tau(t+1)|\phi|^{2p-1}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the second factor, combining with (
6.19
), we see

\left\|\tau(t+1)|\phi|^{2p-1}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim\tau^{3}\|\phi\|^{2p-1}_{L^{4p-2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{2p-1}\epsilon^{p-\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{-3p+6},

where we used the fact that
\tau\leq t+1\leq\tau^{2}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
.
Together with (
6.16
), we conclude

(6.40)

\|\tau|\phi|^{p}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+3}.

Estimate of
\mathcal{A}_{4}
.
Employing (
6.16
) and (
3.5
), we have

(6.41)

\mathcal{A}_{4}\lesssim\left\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\left\|(t+1)|\psi|^{p-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{p-1}{4}+\frac{p+3}{2}\delta}.

Combining (
6.39
), (
6.40
), and (
6.41
) gives the first bound in (
6.29
).

Next, we illustrate the estimate for
\tilde{\Gamma}=\Omega
; the case
\tilde{\Gamma}=L_{a}
is similar.
From (
6.31
), we decompose

(6.42)

\displaystyle\|\tau\Omega\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tau|\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}+\|\tau|\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle+\|\tau|\psi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}+\|\tau\phi|\psi|^{p-2}\Omega\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

The last two terms are estimated in the same way as
\mathcal{A}_{4}
, so we omit the details.
For the first term in (
6.42
), we split it into two parts

\|\tau|\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim\|\tau|\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}+\|\tau|\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}.

On
\{r\leq t/4\}
, using the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\tau|\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\left\|(t+1)|\phi|^{p-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p-1}{8}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{5}{2}},

where we used (
6.16
) and (
6.18
).
On
\{r\geq t/8\}
, using the Hölder inequality again, we deduce

\displaystyle\|\tau|\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}=\left\|\tau^{2}|\Omega\phi|^{2}|\phi|^{2p-2}\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\left\||\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi|^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|t^{2}|\phi|^{2p-2}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the first factor, applying Lemmas
2.4
-
2.5
and Proposition
6.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\||\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi|^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi\right\|_{L^{4}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim\left\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{1\over 4}\left\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi\right\|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{3\over 4}\lesssim C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
\tau\leq t+1\leq\tau^{2}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
, we apply (
6.19
) to the second factor

\left\|t^{2}|\phi|^{2p-2}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\tau^{2}\|\phi\|^{p-1}_{L^{4p-4}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p-1}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{17}{4}}.

Combining the above estimates, we conclude that

\|\tau|\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{17}{4}}.

For the second term in (
6.42
), we decompose it into two parts

\displaystyle\|\tau|\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\left\|(t+1)|\phi|^{p-2}\Omega\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}+\left\||\phi|^{p-1}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\|\tau\Omega\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}=:\mathcal{A}_{5}+\mathcal{A}_{6}.

Employing (
6.16
), (
6.18
) and (
3.12
), we obtain

\mathcal{A}_{5}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{1-\delta+\frac{p-2}{8}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+4}.

Using (
6.19
), we have

\mathcal{A}_{6}\lesssim\|\phi\|^{p-1}_{L^{2p-2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\|\tau\Omega\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}-\delta}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+4},

where we used (
3.12
) in the last inequality.
The above estimates yield

\|\tau|\phi|^{p-1}\Omega\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{1-\delta+\frac{p-2}{8}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+4}.

Therefore, we conclude that

\|\tau\Omega\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{p-1}{4}+\frac{p+3}{2}\delta}.

The bound for
L_{a}\mathcal{N}
follows in the same way by replacing
\Omega
with
L_{a}
. This proves (
6.29
).
∎

With the boundary estimates and nonlinear estimates at hand, we can now apply the interior natural energy and conformal energy inequalities to
\phi
and its derivatives. This will improve the bootstrap assumptions made in (
6.13
) and thus close our global existence proof in the interior region.

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

Step 1: Initial data at
t=t_{1}
.

By Proposition
4.3
, we have the
L^{2}
bounds on the initial slice

\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in}
:

(6.43)

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|\leq 1}\|\Omega^{J}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\sum_{a}\|L_{a}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\sum_{|J|\leq 1}\|\partial\Omega^{J}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon,

\displaystyle\|\partial\partial\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\sum_{a}\|\partial L_{a}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}.

Step 2: Natural energy for
\Omega^{J}\phi
(
|J|\leq 1
).

Applying
\Omega^{J}
to the wave equation for
\phi
and using the natural energy estimate
(
6.5
), we obtain, for
|J|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial\Omega^{J}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|G\Omega^{J}\phi|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau}.

Employing (
6.43
), (
6.20
) and Lemma
6.9
, we obtain

(6.44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon+C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}\tilde{\tau}^{-\frac{p+3}{4}+\frac{p+3}{2}\delta}\,d\tilde{\tau}\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}.

Step 3: Natural energy for
\partial\phi
and
L_{a}\phi
.

Next, we apply the natural energy estimate (
6.5
) to the differentiated equations. For
\partial\phi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\partial\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial\partial\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|G\partial\phi|^{2}\,dx\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\partial\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau}.

Combining (
6.43
), (
6.20
) and Lemma
6.9
, we conclude

(6.45)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\partial\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}+C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}\tilde{\tau}^{-\frac{p+3}{4}+\frac{p+3}{2}\delta}\,d\tilde{\tau}\lesssim C_{3}+C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}.

Similarly, applying (
6.5
) to
L_{a}\phi

(a=1,2,3)
, we obtain

(6.46)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,L_{a}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial L_{a}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|GL_{a}\phi|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|L_{a}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{3}+C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}},

where we used (
6.43
), Lemma
6.8
and Lemma
6.9
.

Step 4: Conformal energy for
\Omega^{J}\phi
(
|J|\leq 1
).

Using the conformal energy estimate (
6.6
) in Lemma
6.4
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial\Omega^{J}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\|\Omega^{J}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}\Big[(t+r+1)L\Omega^{J}\phi+2\Omega^{J}\phi\Big]^{2}+\Big|\frac{1}{r}\Omega\Omega^{J}\phi\Big|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\tilde{\tau}\Omega^{J}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau}.

Using (
6.43
) and Lemmas
6.8
-
6.9
, we have

(6.47)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon+C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}\tilde{\tau}^{-\frac{p-1}{4}+\frac{p+3}{2}\delta}\,d\tilde{\tau}\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}.

Step 5: Conformal energy for
L_{a}\phi

(a=1,2,3)
.
Similarly, applying the conformal energy estimate (
6.6
) to
L_{a}\phi
yields

(6.48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,L_{a}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial L_{a}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\|L_{a}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}

\displaystyle+\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}\big[(t+r+1)LL_{a}\phi+2L_{a}\phi\big]^{2}+\Big|\frac{1}{r}\Omega L_{a}\phi\Big|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\tilde{\tau}\,L_{a}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{2}+C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}\tilde{\tau}^{-\frac{p-1}{4}+\frac{p+3}{2}\delta}\,d\tilde{\tau}\lesssim C_{3}+C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}.

Step 6: Closing the bootstrap argument.

Combining (
6.44
), (
6.45
), (
6.46
), (
6.47
) and (
6.48
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|\leq 1}\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{|J|\leq 1}\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,\Omega^{J}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\partial\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{a}\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,L_{a}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{a}\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,L_{a}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{3}+C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}.

Choosing
C_{4}
large enough so that
C_{3}\ll C_{4}
and taking
\epsilon
sufficiently small so that

C_{4}^{p-1}\epsilon^{(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)\frac{p-5}{2}}\ll 1

yields improved bounds with the factor
\frac{1}{2}C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
(and
\frac{1}{2}C_{4}
) compared to (
6.13
). This closes the bootstrap and completes the proof.

∎

7.
Global existence for classical solutions

In this section, we upgrade the global solution constructed in the previous sections to a global
classical solution. Recall that
\phi
is already globally defined on

\mathcal{D}^{ex}\cup\mathcal{D}^{in}
for
t\geq t_{1}
.
In Sections
7.1
,
7.2
,
7.3
we will (respectively) derive pointwise control of
\phi
,
\partial\phi
and

\partial^{2}\phi
in
\mathcal{D}^{in}

, we can then conclude that

is a global classical solution to the equation (

) for

.

7.1.
Pointwise bound for
\phi

In the previous section we have already obtained pointwise bound for
\phi
in the interior part

\{r\leq t/4\}
of
\mathcal{D}^{in}
(see (
6.18
)). In this subsection we derive the remaining pointwise control on

\{r\geq t/8\}
by first establishing hyperboloidal energy bounds for
\Omega^{2}\phi
and then applying
the Sobolev inequality.

Lemma 7.1

(Energy bounds for
\Omega^{2}\phi
)
.

For all
\tau\in[\tau_{0},+\infty)
,

(7.1)

\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\Omega^{2}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,\Omega^{2}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}.

Proof.

Commuting
\Omega^{2}
with (
4.1
) gives
-\Box\Omega^{2}\phi=\pm\Omega^{2}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)
.
By a direct differentiation of
\mathcal{N}
we obtain the pointwise bound

(7.2)

\displaystyle|\Omega^{2}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[|\phi|^{p-1}|\Omega^{2}\phi|+|\phi|^{p-2}\big(|\Omega\phi|^{2}+|\Omega\phi||\Omega\psi|\big)+|\psi|^{p-2}|\Omega\phi|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+\Big[|\phi|^{p-1}|\Omega^{2}\psi|+|\phi|^{p-2}|\Omega\psi|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+\Big[|\psi|^{p-1}|\Omega^{2}\phi|+|\phi||\psi|^{p-2}|\Omega^{2}\psi|+|\psi|^{p-2}|\Omega\phi||\Omega\psi|+|\phi||\psi|^{p-3}|\Omega\psi|^{2}\Big]=:\mathcal{G}_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{2}+\mathcal{G}_{3}.

\bullet

We start by applying the interior natural energy inequality (
6.5
) to the commuted equation:

(7.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\Omega^{2}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\partial\Omega^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|G\Omega^{2}\phi|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\Omega^{2}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau}.

We now estimate each of these terms in turn. Firstly, the term involving initial data can be controlled by Proposition
4.3
as

\|\partial\Omega^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}\lesssim C_{2}.

Next the boundary flux term can be controlled using (
5.11
), to get

\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|G\Omega^{2}\phi|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim C_{3}.

We next turn to the spacetime intergral term in (
7.3
) which we will analyse using (
7.2
). We make three groupings of the terms appearing in (
7.2
) based on how they are estimated.
We will also use repeatedly the decomposition
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}={\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}}\cup{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}}
,
the interior pointwise bound (
6.18
), the
L^{q}
bounds on
\{r\geq t/8\}
from
Lemma
6.7
(in particular (
6.19
)), and the following decay estimates for
\psi
,

(7.4)

|\Omega^{\leq 2}\psi|\lesssim C_{1}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta},\qquad|\Omega^{\leq 2}\psi|\lesssim C_{1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta},\qquad|\Omega^{\leq 2}\psi|\lesssim C_{1}\epsilon^{-1}\langle t+r\rangle^{-1}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Group
\mathcal{G}_{1}
:
We start with the term
|\phi|^{p-1}\Omega^{2}\phi
as the remainder in this group can be treated in the same way. Using (
6.1
), Hölder, and Lemma
2.4
, we find

\displaystyle\big\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega^{2}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega^{2}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\phi|^{p-1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}

\displaystyle+\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega^{2}\phi\big\|_{L^{6}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\phi|^{p-1}\big\|_{L^{3}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}.

By (
6.18
) and
\tau\leq t+1\leq\tau^{2}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
we have

\big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\phi|^{p-1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p-1}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{8}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{3}{2}},

and the
L^{q}
bound on
\{r\geq t/8\}
gives

\big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\phi|^{p-1}\big\|_{L^{3}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p-1}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{7}{2}}.

Applying Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega^{2}\phi\big\|_{L^{6}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\lesssim\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega^{2}\phi\big\|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}.

Combining the above bounds, we conclude

\big\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega^{2}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p-1}{8}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{7}{2}}+C_{4}^{p-1}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{7}{2}}\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\Omega^{2}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Similarly, we decompose

\displaystyle\big\||\psi|^{p-2}\Omega\phi\Omega\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi\big\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\big\|\frac{(t+1)^{2}}{\tau^{2}}|\psi|^{p-2}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

Employing Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi\big\|^{\frac{1}{4}}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Omega\phi\big\|^{\frac{3}{4}}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The pointwise bounds in (
7.4
) yield

\big\|\frac{(t+1)^{2}}{\tau^{2}}|\psi|^{p-2}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{1}^{p-2}\epsilon^{-1}\tau^{-\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+(p-3)\delta},

where we used that fact
\tau\leq t+1\leq\tau^{2}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
.
Using the above estimates, we derive

\displaystyle\big\||\psi|^{p-2}\Omega\phi\Omega\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\tau^{-\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+(p-3)\delta}.

The remaining terms in
\mathcal{G}_{1}
are treated in the same way, using the same decomposition (
6.1
), together with Lemmas
2.4
–
2.5
, (
6.18
), (
6.19
) and (

).

Group
\mathcal{G}_{2}
:
We start with the term
|\phi|^{p-1}\Omega^{2}\psi
. Using the decomposition (
6.1
), we have

\displaystyle\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega^{2}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\phi|^{p-2}\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}\big\|\Omega^{2}\psi\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle+\||\phi|^{p-1}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\|\Omega^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}=:\mathcal{A}_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{2}.

To estimate
\mathcal{A}_{1}
, we apply (
6.18
) and
\tau\leq t+1\leq\tau^{2}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\tau}
to find

\big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\phi|^{p-2}\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p-2}\epsilon^{\frac{p-2}{8}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+3}.

This implies

\mathcal{A}_{1}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p+6}{8}-\delta}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+3}.

Next to estimate
\mathcal{A}_{2}

(

In a similar way, we get

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\phi|^{p-3}\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{i}})}\big\|\Omega\psi\big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle+\||\phi|^{p-2}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{H}_{\tau}^{f}})}\|\Omega\psi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p}\epsilon^{\frac{p+5}{8}-\delta}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{9}{2}}.

Group
\mathcal{G}_{3}
:
Lastly, we consider

Using (

) and
\tau\leq t+1\leq\tau^{2}

The other terms in

Collecting the above estimates yields

(7.5)

\|\Omega^{2}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\ \lesssim C_{4}^{p-1}\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{7}{2}}\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\Omega^{2}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{4}^{p}\tau^{-\frac{p}{2}+1+(p-2)\delta}.

Inserting (

) into the energy inequality (

).

\bullet
We conclude by turning to the conformal estimate, which is obtained in a similar manner. Applying (

) to the commuted equation gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,\Omega^{2}\phi)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial\Omega^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\|\Omega^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}\Big[(t+r+1)L\Omega^{2}\phi+2\Omega^{2}\phi\Big]^{2}+\Big|\frac{1}{r}\Omega^{3}\phi\Big|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\tilde{\tau}\,\Omega^{2}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau}.

The initial and boundary terms are controlled exactly as above, using Propositions
4.3
,
5.7
and Lemma
5.9
. Finally the spacetime integral is estimated in the same way as (

), with an additional factor

\tilde{\tau}
, which remains integrable.
∎

Proposition 7.3

\bullet
We start with the natural energy bound.
Applying the energy inequality (
6.5
) to the commuted equation, we get

Next, using Proposition
5.8

\psi

(7.10)

\displaystyle|\psi|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta},

\displaystyle|\psi|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{1}\epsilon^{-1}\langle t+r\rangle^{-1}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(7.11)

Group
\mathcal{G}_{1}
:

For

6.6

Employing Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\big\|_{L^{6}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\big\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

Applying Proposition
6.6
and Lemma
7.2
, we get

\big\||\phi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{3+\frac{(p+1)(p-3)}{2}}\epsilon^{\frac{p+5}{8}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{13}{2}}.

Similarly, for
|\phi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|

\displaystyle|\phi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\big\|\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\big\|\frac{(t+1)^{2}}{\tau^{2}}|\phi|^{p-3}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})},

together with Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, (
3.5
), giving

\big\||\phi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{3+\frac{(p+1)(p-3)}{2}}\epsilon^{\frac{p+2}{8}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{7}{2}}.

The remaining terms in
\mathcal{G}_{4}
are treated in the same way.

Inserting all the above estimates into the energy inequality (
7.12
), we obtain the desired bound.

\bullet
Next we turn to the conformal estimate, which proceeds in a very similar manner. Applying (
6.6
) to the equation (
4.1
) commuted with
\partial\Omega^{2}
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}

\displaystyle(\tau,\partial\Omega^{2}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|\partial^{2}\Omega^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\|\partial\Omega^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}\Big[(t+r+1)L\partial\Omega^{2}\phi+2\partial\Omega^{2}\phi\Big]^{2}+\Big|\frac{1}{r}\Omega\partial\Omega^{2}\phi\Big|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\tilde{\tau}\,\partial\Omega^{2}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau}.

The initial and boundary terms are controlled exactly as above, using
Propositions
4.3
,
5.7
and (
5.11
). The spacetime term is estimated in the same way, except that the conformal energy inequality introduces an additional factor
\tilde{\tau}
in the source term. Consequently, the integral is finite if and only if
p>\frac{17}{3}
.
∎

Lemma 7.5

(Pointwise bound for
\partial\phi
)
.

For
(t,x)\in\mathcal{D}^{in}
, we have

(7.13)

\displaystyle|\partial\phi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{4}^{\frac{p^{2}+1}{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{11}{8}}\tau^{-1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\text{ for }r\leq t/4,

\displaystyle|\partial\phi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{4}^{\frac{p^{2}+p}{2}}\epsilon^{-1}\tau^{\frac{13-3p}{4}}r^{-\frac{1}{2}},&p<\frac{17}{3},\\
C_{4}^{\frac{p^{2}+p}{2}}\epsilon^{-1}\tau^{-1}(\log\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-\frac{1}{2}},&p=\frac{17}{3},\\
C_{4}^{\frac{p^{2}+p}{2}}\epsilon^{-1}\tau^{-1}r^{-\frac{1}{2}},&p>\frac{17}{3},\end{array}\right.\qquad\text{ for }r\geq t/8.

Proof.

Recall that
Z=\{\partial_{t},L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}\}.

On
\{r\leq t/4\}
, using (
6.7
) with

, we have

\displaystyle|\partial\phi(t,x)|\lesssim\tau^{-1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\epsilon^{(|I|-\frac{3}{2})\lambda}\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}Z^{I}\partial\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

Applying Propositions
6.6
and
7.3
, we get

|\partial\phi(t,x)|\lesssim C_{4}^{\frac{p^{2}+1}{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{11}{8}}\tau^{-1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

On
\{r\geq t/8\}
, using (
6.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\partial\phi(t,x)|\lesssim|\partial\phi(t,x)|_{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}\cap\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|J_{1}|\leq 2,|J_{2}|\leq 2\\
|I|=1\end{subarray}}\tau^{-1}|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\frac{\tau}{t}\Omega^{J_{1}}\partial\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\frac{\tau}{t}\Omega^{J_{2}}Z^{I}\partial\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{\frac{1}{2}},

together with (
5.8
), Propositions
6.6
,
7.3
,
7.4
, giving the desired result.
∎

Proposition 7.6

(
L^{2}
bounds for higher derivatives III)
.

For all
\tau\in[\tau_{0},+\infty)
, the following bounds hold:

(7.14)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\partial^{3}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{E}_{con}^{in}(\tau,\partial^{3}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{-3}+C_{4}^{p^{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{17}{8}},\\
\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,L_{a}\partial^{2}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{E}_{con}^{in}(\tau,L_{a}\partial^{2}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{-3}+C_{4}^{p^{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{17}{8}}.\end{split}

Commuting
\Gamma^{I}=\partial^{3}\ (\mbox{or}\ L_{a}\partial^{2})
with (
4.1
) and applying the interior natural energy inequality (
6.5
) produces

\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\Gamma^{I}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\;\|\partial\Gamma^{I}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|G\Gamma^{I}\phi|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\Gamma^{I}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,\Gamma^{I}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial\Gamma^{I}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\|\Gamma^{I}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}\Big[(t+r+1)L\Gamma^{I}\phi+2\Gamma^{I}\phi\Big]^{2}+\Big|\frac{1}{r}\Omega\Gamma^{I}\phi\Big|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\tilde{\tau}\,\Gamma^{I}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau}.

In both inequalities, the initial and boundary terms are controlled exactly as above, using Propositions
4.3
,
5.7
and (
5.11
).
The estimate for
\partial^{3}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)
is proved in the same way as for
\partial\Omega^{2}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)

in Proposition
7.4
, with the two
\Omega
–derivatives replaced by two
\partial
–derivatives.
The only difference is that, in the product estimates for
\partial^{3}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)
, we may place one factor

\partial\phi
in
L^{\infty}
(see (
7.13
)), and estimate the remaining
factors in
L^{2}
. The bound for
L_{a}\partial^{2}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)
is estimated similarly.
∎

Proposition 7.7

(
L^{2}
bounds for higher derivatives IV)
.

For all
\tau\in[\tau_{0},+\infty)
, the following bounds hold:

(7.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{4}^{\frac{7}{4}p^{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{41}{8}}+C_{4}^{p^{2}}\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}-p},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{con}^{in}(\tau,\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{4}^{\frac{7}{4}p^{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{41}{8}}+C_{4}^{p^{2}}\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}-p},&p>\frac{17}{3},\\
C_{4}^{\frac{7}{4}p^{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{41}{8}}\log\tau,&p=\frac{17}{3},\\
C_{4}^{\frac{7}{4}p^{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{41}{8}}\tau^{\frac{17-3p}{2}},&p<\frac{17}{3}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

\bullet
We start with proving the natural energy estimate. Commuting
\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}
with (
4.1
) and applying the interior natural energy inequality (
6.5
) to this commuted equation gives

(7.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}(\tau,\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\partial\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|G\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau}.

We first estimate the initial data term using Proposition
4.3
to find

\|\partial\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}\lesssim C_{2}\epsilon^{-2}.

Next, the boundary flux term is controlled using the exterior bound (
5.11
), giving

\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}|G\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim C_{3}\epsilon^{-2}.

Finally we turn to the spacetime term.
We estimate
\|\Gamma^{I}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}
, where
\Gamma^{I}=\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}
, by splitting it into two groups according to the number of differentiated
\phi
factors, namely factors of the form
\Gamma^{I_{k}}\phi
with
|I_{k}|\geq 1
:

\mathcal{G}_{1}
contains at most one such factor, whereas
\mathcal{G}_{2}
contains at least two.

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}

\displaystyle:\{|\phi|^{p-1}\Gamma^{I}\phi,\ |\phi|^{p-1}\Gamma^{I}\psi,\ |\psi|^{p-1}\Gamma^{I}\phi,\ \phi|\psi|^{p-2}\Gamma^{I}\psi,\ |\phi|^{p-2}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi,\ |\phi|^{p-2}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi,\

\displaystyle|\psi|^{p-2}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi,\ \phi|\psi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi,\ |\phi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi,\ |\phi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi,\

\displaystyle|\psi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi,\ \phi|\psi|^{p-4}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi,\ |\phi|^{p-4}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{4}}\psi,\

\displaystyle|\phi|^{p-4}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{4}}\psi,\ |\psi|^{p-4}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{4}}\psi,\ \phi|\psi|^{p-5}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{4}}\psi\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}

\displaystyle:\{|\phi|^{p-2}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi,\ |\psi|^{p-2}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi,\ |\phi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\phi,\ |\psi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\phi,\ |\phi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi,\

\displaystyle|\psi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi,\ |\phi|^{p-4}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{4}}\phi,\ |\phi|^{p-4}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{4}}\psi,\ |\phi|^{p-4}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{4}}\psi,\

\displaystyle|\psi|^{p-4}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{4}}\phi,\ |\psi|^{p-4}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{4}}\psi,\ |\psi|^{p-4}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\psi\Gamma^{I_{4}}\psi\},

where in each term
\sum_{k}{I_{k}}=I
and
|I_{k}|\geq 1
.
Throughout we use the
L^{2}
bounds in Propositions
6.6
,
7.3
, Lemma
7.1
, the interior pointwise bounds in Lemmas
7.2
,
7.5
and the decay estimates in Lemma

.

Estimates for group
\mathcal{G}_{1}
.
Start by considering
|\phi|^{p-1}\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi
. Using Proposition
7.4
and Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\big\||\phi|^{p-1}\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

For

, Lemma
7.1

and (

) give

\displaystyle\big\||\psi|^{p-1}\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{4}^{p^{2}+p-1}\epsilon^{-2}\tau^{-\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+(p-1)\delta}.

Next consider
|\phi|^{p-2}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi
. Using the Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\||\phi|^{p-2}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\big\|\Gamma^{I_{2}}\psi\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\big\|\frac{t+1}{\tau}|\phi|^{p-2}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

6.6

3.2

The remaining terms in

Estimates for group
\mathcal{G}_{2}
.

\displaystyle\big\||\phi|^{p-2}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\big\|\frac{(t+1)^{2}}{\tau^{2}}|\phi|^{p-2}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

Employing Lemmas
2.4
and

, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\big\|^{\frac{1}{4}}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\big\|^{\frac{3}{4}}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})},

Similarly, we bound

\displaystyle\big\||\psi|^{p-2}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

Employing Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\big\|_{L^{6}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\big\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

6.6

\big\||\phi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p^{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{5}{4}}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{13}{2}}.

\big\||\psi|^{p-3}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{3}}\phi\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\lesssim C_{4}^{p^{2}}\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}-p}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}p+\frac{13}{2}}.

The remaining terms in the second group are treated in a similar way.

Finally we turn to the conformal estimate, which is obtained in a similar manner. Applying the conformal energy inequality (

) to the commuted equation gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{in}_{con}(\tau,\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}+\|\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t_{1}}\cap\mathcal{D}^{in})}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}\Big[(t+r+1)L\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi+2\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi\Big]^{2}+\Big|\frac{1}{r}\Omega\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\phi\Big|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\|\tilde{\tau}\,\Omega^{2}\partial^{2}\mathcal{N}(\psi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\tau}})}\,d\tilde{\tau}.

The initial and boundary terms are controlled exactly as above, using

4.3
and

. The spacetime term is estimated in the same way, except that the conformal energy inequality introduces an additional factor

∎

Lemma 7.8

(Pointwise bound for
\partial^{2}\phi
)
.

For
(t,x)\in\mathcal{D}^{in}
, we have

(7.17)

\displaystyle|\partial^{2}\phi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{4}^{p^{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{5}{2}}\tau^{-1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad r\leq t/4,

\displaystyle|\partial^{2}\phi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} C_{4}^{\frac{11}{8}p^{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{57}{16}}\tau^{\frac{-3p+13}{4}}r^{-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad p<17/3,\\
C_{4}^{\frac{11}{8}p^{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{57}{16}}\tau^{-1}(\log\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad p=17/3,\\
(C_{4}^{\frac{11}{8}p^{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{57}{16}}+C_{4}^{p^{2}}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{p}{2}})\tau^{-1}r^{-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad p>17/3,\end{aligned}\qquad r\geq t/8.\right.

\bullet
We first consider the case when

. From (

) it follows that

\displaystyle|\partial^{2}\phi(t,x)|\lesssim\tau^{-1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\epsilon^{(|I|-\frac{3}{2})\lambda}\Big\|\frac{\tau}{t+1}Z^{I}\partial^{2}\phi\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}.

Choosing
\lambda=1
and applying Propositions
6.6
,
7.3
and
7.6
, we get the desired estimate

\bullet
Next we turn to the case

. Using (
6.8
), we have

\displaystyle|\partial^{2}\phi(t,x)|\lesssim|\partial^{2}\phi(t,x)|_{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}\cap\mathcal{B}_{4\epsilon}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|J_{1}|\leq 2,|J_{2}|\leq 2\\
|I|=1\end{subarray}}\tau^{-1}|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\frac{\tau}{t}\Omega^{J_{1}}\partial^{2}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\frac{\tau}{t}\Omega^{J_{2}}Z^{I}\partial^{2}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Utilising the bounds from Propositions
6.6
,
7.3
,
7.4
,
7.6
,
7.7
, gives the desired estimates.
∎
