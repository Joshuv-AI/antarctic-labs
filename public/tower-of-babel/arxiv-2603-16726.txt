Maximal regularity for time-fractional Schrödinger equations and application to nonlinear equations
S. E. Chorfi, F. Et-tahri, L. Maniar, M. Yamamoto

Abstract
. We study the maximal regularity problem for abstract time-fractional Schrödinger equations ∂ t α ( u − u 0 ) − i ​ A ​ u = f \partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-u_{0})-\mathrm{i}Au=f , with a fractional derivative ∂ t α \partial_{t}^{\alpha} of order α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) . We assume that A A is a self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent on a Hilbert space H H . First, we prove the maximal L 2 L^{2} -regularity by leveraging properties of Mittag-Leffler functions with an imaginary argument. Compared to existing results for the subdiffusion equations, our proof avoids using the complete monotonicity of Mittag-Leffler functions, which seems difficult to prove within the setting of an imaginary argument. Then, we prove the maximal L p L^{p} -regularity for p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) using the operator-valued version of Mikhlin’s multiplier theorem. Finally, we apply the maximal regularity results to prove the local well-posedness of quasilinear and semilinear time-fractional Schrödinger equations.

1.
Introduction and motivation

The time-fractional Schrödinger equation has attracted significant interest because of its capacity to capture anomalous diffusion and memory effects in quantum systems. Applications include quantum mechanics, optics and photonics, condensed matter physics, and plasma physics; see, for instance,
[
27
]
,
[
35
]
,
[
48
]
, and
[
49
]
, along with the references therein. The incorporation of fractional time dynamics breaks the usual unitarity and time-reversal symmetry, allowing the probability density to either dissipate or accumulate over time.

More precisely, we consider the time-fractional Schrödinger equation

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-u_{0})-\mathrm{i}Au=f,\qquad t\in(0,T),

(1.1)

where
T>0
and
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
is a fractional derivative of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
(see Section
2.2
).

Inspired by time-fractional diffusion equations, M. Naber
[
38
]
initiated the study of the time-fractional Schrödinger equation by giving a variant of (
1.1
) with a factor
\mathrm{i}^{-\alpha}
instead of
\mathrm{i}
. On the other hand, B.N. Achar et al.
[
3
]
obtained a special case of (
1.1
) via the Feynman path integral method, allowing to recover the classical Schrödinger equation as
\alpha\to 1^{-}
. Moreover, H. Emamirad and A. Rougirel
[
24
]
have proposed a time-fractional equation that preserves the classical quantum mechanical properties.

Maximal regularity for parabolic evolution equations means that
\partial_{t}u
and
Au
enjoy the same regularity as
f
, and this property has attracted considerable attention as a powerful tool for studying the well-posedness of nonlinear and non-autonomous equations via linearization and fixed-point arguments; see, for instance,
[
42
]
and the references therein.

Maximal regularity for time-fractional evolution equations like (
1.1
) means that
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-u_{0})
and
Au
enjoy the same regularity as
f
. It has recently garnered an increasing interest in studying regularity properties of models with anomalous or memory effects. It is well known that classical parabolic equations enjoy maximal regularity. It would therefore be natural to expect this property to be inherited by fractional parabolic equations. In this paper, we prove a surprising result on the maximal regularity of the time-fractional Schrödinger equation, which is not satisfied by the classical Schrödinger equation.

As pioneering works for parabolic equations, we can refer to P. E. Sobolevskii
[
46
]
and O. A. Ladyzhenskaya et al.
[
34
]
. Then, G. Da Prato and P. Grisvard
[
17
]
reformulated the problem using analytic semigroups, leading to two main approaches: the first one relies on singular integral operators; see, e.g., L. De Simon
[
18
]
, and M. Hieber and J. Prüss
[
29
]
for Gaussian kernel estimates. The second approach is the operator-sum method, which yields interesting results via interpolation spaces
[
17
]
. We refer to the works of G. Dore and A. Venni, e.g.,
[
22
]
and
[
21
]
. We also refer to H. Amann
[

, P. C. Kunstmann and L. W. Weis

, R. Denk et al.
[

, but the list is far from being exhaustive. As a recent reference, we mention W. Arendt and M. Sauter
[
9

for generalized boundary conditions in time.

As first results for time-fractional equations, we can refer to the monograph by J. Prüss
[
41

, where the author studied more general evolutionary integral equations. However, it is not clear whether one can obtain sharp regularity results for time-fractional equations. In
[

In

The article is organized as follows. In Section

Notations

In what follows, we consider a Hilbert space
(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, with the corresponding norm
\|\cdot\|:=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
. We assume throughout that
A:D(A)\subset H\rightarrow H
is a self-adjoint operator, negative-definite, and with compact resolvent. We denote by
\sigma(-A)=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
the spectrum of
-A
and by
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset D(A)
an associated orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. Therefore, the operator
A
satisfies

\langle Au,u\rangle<0\qquad\forall u\in D(A)\setminus\{0\},

and the spectrum
\sigma(-A)=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is such that

0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}\leq\cdots\to\infty.

We will denote by
\mathcal{B}(H)
the space of bounded linear operators on
H
equipped with the operator norm. Then,
A^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}(H)
and
\|A^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}
. Therefore, we can endow
D(A)
with the equivalent norm

\|u\|_{D(A)}:=\|Au\|,\qquad u\in D(A).

For any
z\in\rho(A)
(the resolvent set), we denote by
R(z,A)=(zI_{H}-A)^{-1}
the resolvent operator of
A
, where
I_{H}
is the identity operator on
H
.

For any
p>1
, we denote by
q
its conjugate exponent defined by
\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{1}{q}=1
. We will also use the weak Lebesgue spaces
L^{p,\infty}(0,T;V),

1\leq p<\infty,
where
(V,\|\cdot\|_{V})
is a Hilbert space (see, e.g.,
[
47
, Section 1.18.6]
). The
L^{p,\infty}(0,T;V)
quasi-norm of a measurable function
v:(0,T)\to V
is given by

\|v\|_{L^{p,\infty}(0,T;V)}:=\sup_{\lambda>0}\,\lambda\Big|\Big\{t\in(0,T)\colon\|v(t)\|_{V}>\lambda\Big\}\Big|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This functional fails to satisfy the triangle inequality and therefore does not
define a norm. However, for any
v\in L^{p}(0,T;V)
, one has

\|v\|_{L^{p,\infty}(0,T;V)}\leq\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T;V)},

which implies the continuous embedding
L^{p}(0,T;V)\subset L^{p,\infty}(0,T;V)
. Moreover, the spaces
L^{p,\infty}(0,T;V)
are complete, and for
p>1
they are Banach spaces.

For
\theta\in(0,1)
and
1\leq p\leq\infty,
we denote by
(X,Y)_{\theta,p}
the real interpolation space for an interpolation couple
(X,Y)
of Banach spaces; see, e.g.,
[
47
, Section 1.5]
. We write
X\hookrightarrow Y
for a continuous embedding. Finally, we denote by
\overline{B}_{X}(r)
the closed ball of radius
r>0
centered at the origin in the space
X
.

Mittag-Leffler functions

We recall the two-parametric Mittag-Leffler function defined by

\displaystyle E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha n+\beta)}\qquad\forall z\in\mathbb{C},

where
\alpha>0
,

. We refer to
[
37
]
and the monographs
[

39
]

We also recall the asymptotic expansions of the Mittag-Leffler function near

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary. Let
\mu

Consequently, we obtain:

and

, such that

\displaystyle\left|E_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\mathrm{i}t\right)\right|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{1+|t|}\leq C_{0}\qquad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

2.2.
Fractional differentiation

Let
\alpha>0
and
p>1
. We define the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral operator by

J^{\alpha}v(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}v(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad v\in L^{p}(0,T;H).

(2.4)

Then, one can prove that the operator
J^{\alpha}:L^{p}(0,T;H)\rightarrow J^{\alpha}L^{p}(0,T;H)\subset L^{p}(0,T;H)
is one-to-one and
onto. We denote by
J^{-\alpha}:=(J^{\alpha})^{-1}
its inverse operator. Then, we define the functional space

W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H):=J^{\alpha}L^{p}(0,T;H),

(2.5)

with the corresponding norm
\|v\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)}:=\|J^{-\alpha}v\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}
.
We can check that
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)
is a Banach space with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)}
. Moreover, for the case
p=2,
we set

H_{\alpha}(0,T;H):=W_{\alpha,2}(0,T;H).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha>\frac{1}{p}
. Then the continuous embedding

W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)\hookrightarrow\{u\in C([0,T];H)\colon u(0)=0\}

holds.

Proof.

The proof is a modification of
[
50
, Theorem 2.1.4]
concerning the case
p=2
.

Let
u\in L^{p}(0,T;H)
and
0\leq t_{0}<t\leq T
. We have

\displaystyle\Gamma(\alpha)\left(J^{\alpha}u(t)-J^{\alpha}u(t_{0})\right)=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}u(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\left(\left(t_{0}-s\right)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\right)u(s)\mathrm{d}s=:I_{1}-I_{2}.

Since
\alpha>\frac{1}{p}
,
q(\alpha-1)>-1
. Then, we estimate
I_{1}
by Hölder’s inequality as follows

\displaystyle\left\|I_{1}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\|u(s)\|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(t-s)^{q(\alpha-1)}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|u(s)\|^{p}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\left(t-t_{0}\right)^{q(\alpha-1)+1}}{q(\alpha-1)+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}.

(2.6)

Next, we estimate
I_{2}
. By
\alpha>\frac{1}{p}
, we choose a small
\varepsilon>0
so that
\alpha>\frac{1}{p}+\varepsilon
. Since
0<1-\alpha<1
, we have
\left|a^{1-\alpha}-b^{1-\alpha}\right|\leq|a-b|^{1-\alpha}
for all
a,b\geq 0
. Then

\left|(t-s)^{1-\alpha}-\left(t_{0}-s\right)^{1-\alpha}\right|\leq\left|t-t_{0}\right|^{1-\alpha}.

Therefore, using
t-s\geq t-t_{0}
and
t-s\geq t_{0}-s
for
0<s<t_{0}<t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|I_{2}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\left|\left(t_{0}-s\right)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\right|\|u(s)\|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\frac{\left|(t-s)^{1-\alpha}-\left(t_{0}-s\right)^{1-\alpha}\right|}{(t-s)^{1-\alpha}\left(t_{0}-s\right)^{1-\alpha}}\|u(s)\|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\frac{\left(t-t_{0}\right)^{1-\alpha}}{(t-s)^{1-\alpha-\varepsilon}(t-s)^{\varepsilon}\left(t_{0}-s\right)^{1-\alpha}}\|u(s)\|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(t-t_{0}\right)^{1-\alpha}}{\left(t-t_{0}\right)^{1-\alpha-\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\left(t_{0}-s\right)^{\alpha-1-\varepsilon}\|u(s)\|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\left(t-t_{0}\right)^{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\left(t_{0}-s\right)^{q(\alpha-1-\varepsilon)}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\|u(s)\|^{p}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(t-t_{0}\right)^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{t_{0}^{q(\alpha-1-\varepsilon)+1}}{q(\alpha-1-\varepsilon)+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)},

where
q(\alpha-1-\varepsilon)+1>0
because
\alpha>\frac{1}{p}+\varepsilon
. Hence,

\left\|\Gamma(\alpha)\left(J^{\alpha}u(t)-J^{\alpha}u\left(t_{0}\right)\right)\right\|\leq C\left(\left(t-t_{0}\right)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}+\left(t-t_{0}\right)^{\varepsilon}T^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}-\varepsilon}\right)\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)},

This implies that
J^{\alpha}u\in C([0,T];H)
.
Moreover, by (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Gamma(\alpha)J^{\alpha}u(t)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{t^{q(\alpha-1)+1}}{q(\alpha-1)+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}\leq Ct^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}.

Therefore,
\lim\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\left\|J^{\alpha}u(t)\right\|=0
. This completes the proof.

3.
Notion of weak solution

First, to prove the uniqueness of solutions, we need the following coercivity result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
v\in H_{\alpha}(0,T;\mathbb{C})
. Then

\mathrm{Re}\int_{0}^{T}\overline{v}(t)\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\frac{T^{-\alpha}}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)},

where
\overline{v}
denotes the conjugate function of
v
.

Proof.

The proof is a consequence of the analogue for real functions. Indeed, let
v=u+\mathrm{i}w
with real-valued
u,w\in H_{\alpha}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
. Linearity gives
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v=\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u+\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}w
and
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u,\partial_{t}^{\alpha}w
are real-valued. Hence

\overline{v}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v=u\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u+w\partial_{t}^{\alpha}w+\mathrm{i}\left(u\partial_{t}^{\alpha}w-w\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\right).

Taking the real part,

\mathrm{Re}\int_{0}^{T}\overline{v}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\left(u\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u+w\partial_{t}^{\alpha}w\right)\mathrm{d}t.

By the coercivity of real-valued functions (see
[
32
, Theorem 3.1 (ii)]
), we have

\int_{0}^{T}\phi(t)\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\phi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\frac{T^{-\alpha}}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}

for
\phi=u
and
\phi=w
. Then

\mathrm{Re}\int_{0}^{T}\overline{v}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\frac{T^{-\alpha}}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\right)=\frac{T^{-\alpha}}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}.

∎

Let
0<\alpha<1
and consider the fractional Schrödinger equation

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u-\mathrm{i}Au=f,\qquad t\in(0,T).

(3.1)

For
t\in[0,T]
, we decompose the solution

u(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}(t)\,\varphi_{n},

and set
f_{n}(t):=\langle f(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle
.

Taking the inner product of (
4.1
) with
\varphi_{n}
and using the symmetry of

, we formally obtain

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u_{n}(t)+\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n}u_{n}(t)=f_{n}(t),\qquad t\in(0,T).

(3.2)

We prove the uniqueness of a solution to (
3.2
) in
H_{\alpha}(0,T;\mathbb{C})
as follows. Let
v\in H_{\alpha}(0,T;\mathbb{C})
such that
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v(t)+\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n}v(t)=0
for all
t\in(0,T)
. Multiplying by
\overline{v},
integrating on

and taking the real part, Lemma

yields

0=\mathrm{Re}\int_{0}^{T}\overline{v}(t)\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\frac{T^{-\alpha}}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

Hence,

in

.

Therefore, we see that the solution to (

Therefore, we obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|AK(s)x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2}|k_{n}(s)|^{2}|\langle x,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}^{2}s^{-2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}s^{2\alpha}}{1+\lambda_{n}^{2}s^{2\alpha}}|\langle x,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}^{2}s^{-2}\|x\|^{2}.

(iii) Since
K(s)
and
A
are diagonal, then they commute on
D(A)
.
∎

The next lemma provides a representation of the formal solution (
3.3
) as a convolution with an operator-valued kernel.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(0,T;H)
for some
p\geq 1
. Then the formal solution (
3.3
) can be represented as follows

u(t)=\int_{0}^{t}K(t-s)f(s)~\mathrm{d}s.

(3.8)

Proof.

First, using (
2.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle|k_{n}(\tau)|\leq C_{0}\tau^{\alpha-1}\qquad\text{for all }\tau>0.

(3.9)

We now justify interchanging the sum and the integral. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in(0,T)
. Put

S_{N}(t):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\int_{0}^{t}f_{n}(s)k_{n}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\varphi_{n}=\int_{0}^{t}g_{N}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
f_{n}(s):=\langle f(s),\varphi_{n}\rangle
and
g_{N}(s):=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{n}(s)k_{n}(t-s)\varphi_{n}
.
Using (
3.9
), for a.e.
s\in(0,t)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|g_{N}(s)\|

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|f_{n}(s)|^{2}|k_{n}(t-s)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\|f(s)\|.

Since
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
f\in L^{p}(0,T;H)
, by the properties of convolution, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\|f(s)\|~\mathrm{d}s<\infty\qquad\mbox{for a.e.}\;t\in(0,T).

Hence, the dominated convergence theorem for Bochner integrals implies

\displaystyle u(t)=

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}g_{N}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(s)k_{n}(t-s)\,\varphi_{n}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K(t-s)f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

∎

Hereafter, we set

u(g)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}K(t-s)g(s)\mathrm{d}s,\qquad g\in L^{p}(0,T;H),\quad 0<t<T.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T;D(A))
. Then
u(g)\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)\cap L^{p}(0,T;D(A))
.

Proof.

For
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we set

u_{\varepsilon}(g)(t)=\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}K(t-s)g(s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad 0<t<T.

Therefore, by Riemann-sum approximation and Lemma
3.3
-(iii), we can prove
u_{\varepsilon}(g)(t)\in D(A)
and
Au_{\varepsilon}(g)(t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}K(t-s)Ag(s)\mathrm{d}s
. Therefore, we have
u_{\varepsilon}(g)(t)\to u(g)(t)
and
Au_{\varepsilon}(g)(t)\to\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K(t-s)Ag(s)\mathrm{d}s
as
\varepsilon\to 0
in
H
. Since
A
is closed, we obtain
u(g)(t)\in D(A)
and

Au(g)(t)=A\int_{0}^{t}K(t-s)g(s)\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}K(t-s)Ag(s)\mathrm{d}s.

Then, by Lemma
3.3
-(i),

\displaystyle\|Au(g)(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|K(t-s)\|_{\mathcal{B}(H)}\|Ag(s)\|\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\|Ag(s)\|\,\mathrm{d}s.

Young’s convolution inequality yields

\displaystyle\|u(g)\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|t^{\alpha-1}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\|g\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\frac{T^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\|g\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))}<\infty.

(3.10)

Thus
u(g)\in L^{p}(0,T;D(A))
.

Using the Laplace transform, we can prove that

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(g)=\mathrm{i}Au(g)+g.

Therefore,
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(g)\in L^{p}(0,T;H)
, and hence,
u(g)=J^{\alpha}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(g))\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume
f\in L^{p}(0,T;H).
Then there exists a unique weak solution to
(
3.1
)
given by (
3.3
).

Proof.

First, we prove the uniqueness. Let
u\in L^{p}(0,T;H)
such that

\,_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}\langle u,\,((\partial_{t}^{\alpha})^{\prime}+\mathrm{i}A)\psi\rangle_{L^{q}(0,T;H)}=0

(3.11)

for all
\psi\in\Psi
. We need to prove that
u=0
. We write
u(t)
as

u(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}(t)\,\varphi_{n},\qquad u_{n}\in L^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{C}),

where
u_{n}(t)=\langle u(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle
. For a fixed
n\geq 1
, we take arbitrary function
\eta\in C^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{C})
such that
\eta(T)=0
, and we set
\psi(t)=\eta(t)\varphi_{n}\in\Psi
. Then, by (
3.11
), we obtain

0=\int_{0}^{T}u_{n}(t)\big((\partial_{t}^{\alpha})^{\prime}\overline{\eta}(t)+\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n}\overline{\eta}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

It remains to prove that

\Phi_{n}:=\left\{(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})^{\prime}\overline{\eta}+\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n}\overline{\eta}:\ \overline{\eta}\in C^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{C}),\ \overline{\eta}(T)=0\right\}

is dense in

. Next, we prove that
C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{C})\subset\Phi_{n}
. For

, we solve the fractional ordinary equation

(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})^{\prime}\overline{\eta}+\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n}\overline{\eta}=g.

(3.12)

By
[
52
, Lemma 3.1]
, we have
(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})^{\prime}\overline{\eta}
is the right-sided Riemann–Liouville derivative for
\tau_{T}\overline{\eta}\in D(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})
. Then, the solution to (
3.12
) is given by

\overline{\eta}(t):=\int_{t}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}\big(-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n}(s-t)^{\alpha}\big)\,g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore,
\Phi_{n}
is dense in
L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{C})
and
u_{n}=0
for all
n\geq 1
. Thus

.

Next, we prove that the formal solution (
3.3
) is a weak solution to
(
3.1
)
.

By Lemma
3.4
, the formal solution (
3.3
) can be written as

u(t)=\int_{0}^{t}K(t-s)f(s)~\mathrm{d}s.

Then

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|K(t-s)\|_{\mathcal{B}(H)}\|f(s)\|\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\|f(s)\|\,\mathrm{d}s.

By Young’s convolution inequality, we infer

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|t^{\alpha-1}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\frac{T^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}<\infty.

Then
u\in L^{p}(0,T;H)
.

Now, we choose a sequence
f_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T;D(A))
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f_{n}\to f
in

. By Lemma

, we have
u(f_{n})\in D(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})\cap L^{p}(0,T;D(A))
. Then we obtain

\left\langle u(f_{n}),\left(\left(\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\right)^{\prime}+\mathrm{i}A\right)\psi\right\rangle=\left\langle f_{n},\psi\right\rangle\quad\text{ for all }\psi\in\Psi\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.13)

On the other hand, one has

u(f_{n})\to u(f)\quad\text{ in }L^{p}(0,T;H)\quad\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Letting

in (

), we obtain

\left\langle u(f),\left(\left(\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\right)^{\prime}+\mathrm{i}A\right)\psi\right\rangle=\left\langle f,\psi\right\rangle\quad\text{ for all }\psi\in\Psi.

Therefore,
u
is the unique weak solution to
(
3.1
)
.
∎

Regarding the continuity of the weak solution on
[0,T]
with values in
H
, we establish the following result.

Proposition 3.7
.

Assume
f\in L^{p}(0,T;H)
for
p>\frac{1}{\alpha}
. Then the weak solution to (
3.1
) satisfies
u\in C([0,T];H)
, and there exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,p)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C([0,T];H)}\leq CT^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}.

(3.14)

Proof.

Recall from Lemma
3.3
-(i) that we have

\|K(s)\|_{\mathcal{B}(H)}\leq C_{0}s^{\alpha-1}\qquad\text{for all }s>0.

Since
\alpha p>1
, then
(\alpha-1)q>-1
and

\displaystyle\|K\|_{L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{B}(H))}\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\left(\frac{T^{(\alpha-1)q+1}}{(\alpha-1)q+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CT^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}.

where
C=C(\alpha,p)>0
.
For
t\in[0,T]
, using (
3.8
) and Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|K(s)\|_{\mathcal{B}(H)}\|f(s)\|\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\|K\|_{L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{B}(H))}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}.

(3.15)

Thus
u(t)
is well-defined for all
t\in[0,T]
.
To prove the right continuity at
t\in[0,T)
, let
h\geq 0
be small so
t+h\in[0,T]
(left continuity can be proved similarly). By a change of variables, we can write

\displaystyle u(t+h)-u(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big(\tau_{h}K(s)-K(s)\big)f(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{h}K(s)\,\tau_{h}f(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Taking the
H
-norm and using Hölder’s inequality yields

\|u(t+h)-u(t)\|\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}\Big(\|\tau_{h}K-K\|_{L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{B}(H))}+\|K\|_{L^{q}(0,h;\mathcal{B}(H))}\Big).

The second term tends to
0
as
h\to 0
by the integrability of
K\in L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{B}(H))
.

Next, we prove that the first term tends to
0
as
h\to 0
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since continuous compactly supported functions are dense in
L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{B}(H))
, choose
\Psi\in C_{c}((0,T);\mathcal{B}(H))
with

\|K-\Psi\|_{L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{B}(H))}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

By uniform continuity of
\Psi
on its compact support, we can choose
|h|
small enough so that

\|\tau_{h}\Psi-\Psi\|_{L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{B}(H))}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Now, for such
h
, the triangle inequality gives

\displaystyle\|\tau_{h}K-K\|_{L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{B}(H))}\leq

\displaystyle\|\tau_{h}K-\tau_{h}\Psi\|_{L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{B}(H))}+\|\tau_{h}\Psi-\Psi\|_{L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{B}(H))}

\displaystyle+\|\Psi-K\|_{L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{B}(H))}.

Each of the terms is bounded by
\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
. This proves the desired result.
Finally,
u\in C([0,T];H)
, and the estimate (
3.14
) follows from (
3.15
).
∎

4.
Maximal regularity

In this section, we investigate the maximal regularity for the following fractional Schrödinger equation

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u-\mathrm{i}Au=f,\qquad t\in(0,T).

(4.1)

Definition 4.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. We say that the equation
(
4.1
)
satisfies the maximal
L^{p}
-regularity, if for all
f\in L^{p}(0,T;H)
the unique weak solution
u
belongs to
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)
. That is, the solution to the equation
(
4.1
)
belongs to
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)\cap L^{p}(0,T;D(A))
, where
D(A)
is endowed with the graph norm.

In this case, by the Closed Graph Theorem, there is
C>0
such that

\|u\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)}+\|Au\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}.

(4.2)

4.1.
Maximal
L^{2}
-regularity

We start with the
L^{2}
-case, where we prove the maximal
L^{2}
-regularity in an elementary manner.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume
f\in L^{2}(0,T;H).
Then (
4.1
) satisfies the maximal
L^{2}
-regularity. In particular,
u\in L^{2}(0,T;D(A))
and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|u\|_{H_{\alpha}(0,T;H)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;D(A))}\leq C\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}.

Proof.

For
t\in[0,T]
, write

u(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}(t)\,\varphi_{n},\qquad u_{n}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}f_{n}(s)\,(t-s)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}\big(-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n}(t-s)^{\alpha}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(4.3)

where
f_{n}(s):=\langle f(s),\varphi_{n}\rangle
.

We can check that

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u_{n}(t)+\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n}u_{n}(t)=f_{n}(t).

(4.4)

Using Young’s convolution inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u_{n}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\left|f_{n}(t)\right|^{2}dt+C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left|f_{n}(t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left|\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha}\right)\right|\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2}.

(4.5)

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By the change of variable
s=\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}t
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha-1}\left|E_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha}\right)\right|\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}T}s^{\alpha-1}\left|E_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(-\mathrm{i}s^{\alpha}\right)\right|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\alpha-1}\left|E_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(-\mathrm{i}s^{\alpha}\right)\right|\mathrm{d}s=:I(\alpha).

Note that the last integral is convergent, i.e.,
I(\alpha)<\infty
. Indeed, near
0
, by (
2.3
), we have
\left|E_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(-\mathrm{i}s^{\alpha}\right)\right|\leq C
for all
s>0
, and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{\alpha}
. On the other hand, near
\infty
, the asymptotic expansion (
2.1
) at order
N=2
yields
E_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(-\mathrm{i}s^{\alpha}\right)=\mathcal{O}(s^{-2\alpha})
as
s\to\infty
. Then
s^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(-\mathrm{i}s^{\alpha}\right)=\mathcal{O}(s^{-\alpha-1})
and
\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{\alpha}
.

Therefore,

\|\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u_{n}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\left|f_{n}(t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t.

(4.6)

Hence,

\displaystyle\left\|\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u_{n}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq C\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}|f_{n}(t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t=C\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}.

Moreover, by (
4.4
) and (
4.6
), we infer

\|\lambda_{n}u_{n}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\left|f_{n}(t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t.

Then, similarly, we obtain

\left\|Au\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}.

∎

Remark 1
.

The proof of Theorem
4.2
breaks down when
\alpha=1
because the constant
I(\alpha)
becomes infinite.

Remark 2
.

The analog of Theorem
4.2
has been proven in
[
43
, Theorem 2.2 (i)]
for fractional diffusion equations. The proof relies on the complete monotonicity of the function
E_{\alpha,1}(-s^{\alpha})
for
s>0
when
0<\alpha<1
; see, e.g.,
[
40
]
and also
[
44
]
. In particular, this implies that

for

. In our case, the complete monotonicity of the function

, for

[

for more details. Thus, we needed to modify the proof without using the complete monotonicity.

4.2.
Maximal
L^{p}
-regularity

Concerning maximal
L^{p}
-regularity, we first prove the following partial result.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume
f\in L^{p}(0,T;D(A)).
Then (
4.1
) satisfies the maximal
L^{p}
-regularity. In particular,
u\in L^{p}(0,T;D(A))
and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))}\leq C\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))}.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{p}(0,T;D(A))
be arbitrary. Choose a sequence of functions
f_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T;D(A))

converging to
f
in
L^{p}(0,T;D(A))
, and denote the unique weak solution
associated with
f_{n}
by
u_{n}
. By Lemma
3.5
, we have
u_{n}\in L^{p}(0,T;D(A))
.
By the estimate (
3.10
), the functions
\{Au_{n}\}
form a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}(0,T;H)
and therefore converges to a
limit
v
in
L^{p}(0,T;H)
. Using the definition of weak solutions, we can prove that
\{u_{n}\}
converges
weakly to
u
in
L^{p}(0,T;H)
, where
u
is the weak solution of
(
4.1
) associated with
f
. Hence,

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{weakly in }L^{p}(0,T;H),\qquad Au_{n}\to v\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p}(0,T;H).

Then

(u_{n},Au_{n})\rightharpoonup(u,v)\quad\text{weakly in }L^{p}(0,T;H\times H).

On the other hand, since
\{(u_{n},Au_{n})\}\subset L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{G}(A))
, where

\mathcal{G}(A)\subset H\times H
denotes the graph of
A
, and since
A
is closed,
the graph
\mathcal{G}(A)
is a closed subset of
H\times H
.
It follows that the set
L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{G}(A))

is a closed subspace of
L^{p}(0,T;H\times H)
(due to Rellich–Kondrachov).
In particular, being a closed linear subspace in a Banach space,

L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{G}(A))
is weakly closed in
L^{p}(0,T;H\times H)
(see Theorem 3.7 in
[
12
]
). Consequently

(u,v)\in L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{G}(A))
.
Then
u\in L^{p}(0,T;D(A))
and
Au=v
in
L^{p}(0,T;H)
. Consequently

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{weakly in }L^{p}(0,T;D(A)).

Then,
\{\|u_{n}\|\}
is bounded in
L^{p}(0,T;D(A))
and (see Proposition 3.5 in
[
12
]
)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf\|u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))}.

Using (
3.10
), it follows

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf\,(C_{0}\frac{T^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))})

\displaystyle=C_{0}\frac{T^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))}.

∎

To prove the maximal
L^{p}
-regularity for
1<p<\infty
, we use the following theorem; see
[
45
, Corollary 16]
. Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R};H)
denote the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing smooth
H
-valued functions.

Theorem 4.4

(Mikhlin’s multiplier theorem)
.

Assume that for a function
m\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{*};\mathcal{B}(H))
the sets

\{m(s):s\in\mathbb{R}^{*}\}\text{ and }\left\{s\,m^{\prime}(s):s\in\mathbb{R}^{*}\right\}

are bounded in
\mathcal{B}(H)
. Then the Fourier multiplier operator

\mathcal{T}_{m}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m(\cdot)\widehat{f}(\cdot)),\quad f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R};H),\quad

extends to a bounded operator
\mathcal{T}_{m}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R};H)
for
1<p<\infty
. Here,
\widehat{f}
denotes the Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};H)
defined by

\widehat{f}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\mathrm{i}st}\,f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
0<\alpha<1,

1<p<\infty
, and assume that
f\in L^{p}(0,T;H).
Then (
4.1
) satisfies the maximal
L^{p}
-regularity. In particular,
u\in L^{p}(0,T;D(A))
and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|u\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))}\leq C\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}.

Proof.

Note that, for
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T;D(A))
,

Au(g)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}AK(t-s)g(s)\mathrm{d}s

is well-defined (see Lemma
3.5
). Now, for
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R};H)
,
Au(g)(t)
is defined as a singular integral of convolution type, since the kernel
AK(s)
behaves like
s^{-1}
; see Lemma
3.3
-(ii).

The Fourier transform is given by

\widehat{Au(g)}(s)=\widehat{AK(t)}(s)\widehat{g}(s),\qquad g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R};H),\quad s\in\mathbb{R}.

We have

AK(t)\,\varphi_{n}=-\lambda_{n}k_{n}(t)\,\varphi_{n}.

Then

\displaystyle\widehat{AK(t)}(s)\varphi_{n}

\displaystyle=-\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\mathrm{i}st}\,\lambda_{n}k_{n}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\varphi_{n}

\displaystyle=-\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mathrm{i}st}\,\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\varphi_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{(\mathrm{i}s)^{\alpha}+\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n}}\varphi_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}\lambda_{n}}{-\mathrm{i}(\mathrm{i}s)^{\alpha}+\lambda_{n}}\,\varphi_{n},\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}^{*}.

Here,
s^{\alpha}
for
s<0
is defined by taking the principal branch. Thus

\widehat{AK(t)}(s)=-\mathrm{i}AR(-\mathrm{i}(\mathrm{i}s)^{\alpha},A)=-(\mathrm{i}s)^{\alpha}R(-\mathrm{i}(\mathrm{i}s)^{\alpha},A)+\mathrm{i}I_{H}=:m(s),\;s\in\mathbb{R}^{*}.

Then

s\,m^{\prime}(s)=-\alpha(\mathrm{i}s)^{\alpha}R(-\mathrm{i}(\mathrm{i}s)^{\alpha},A)-\alpha\mathrm{i}[(\mathrm{i}s)^{\alpha}R(-\mathrm{i}(\mathrm{i}s)^{\alpha},A)]^{2}.

Since
A
is self-adjoint and negative, it generates a bounded analytic semigroup on
H
. Therefore, for
\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
there is
M>0
such that

\Sigma_{\theta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\colon|\arg(z)|<\theta\}\subset\rho(A)

and

\|R(z,A)\|_{\mathcal{B}(H)}\leq\frac{M}{|z|},\qquad z\in\Sigma_{\theta}.

(4.7)

For
z=-\mathrm{i}(\mathrm{i}s)^{\alpha}
, we have

\arg(z)=\begin{cases}-\frac{\pi}{2}(1-\alpha)\qquad\mbox{ if }s>0,\\
-\frac{\pi}{2}(1+\alpha)\qquad\mbox{ if }s<0.\end{cases}

For all
0<\alpha<1,
we take
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}(\frac{1+\alpha}{2}+1)\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
so that
z\in\Sigma_{\theta}
for all
s\neq 0
.
Therefore, the resolvent estimate (
4.7
) implies that
m(s)
and
s\,m^{\prime}(s)
are bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{*}
with values in
\mathcal{B}(H)
. Then, Theorem
4.4
implies that the operator
g\mapsto Au(g)
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{p}(0,T;H)
denoted by the same notation. Hence, there is a constant
C>0
such that

\|Au\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}\leq C\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)},

which yields the maximal
L^{p}
-regularity.
∎

5.
Application to nonlinear Schrödinger equations

In this section, we apply the maximal regularity results of the previous section to study the well-posedness of some quasilinear and semilinear Schrödinger equations. We refer, for example, to
[
36
]
for quasilinear reaction-diffusion systems with an integer derivative.

To simplify the notation, for fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
p>\frac{1}{\alpha},
we set

\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T):=\left\{u\in C([0,T];H)\cap L^{p}(0,T;D(A))\colon u-u(0)\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)\right\}

(5.1)

for the maximal regularity space. By Proposition
2.3
, the space
\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T),
endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}:=\|u-u(0)\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))}+\|u(0)\|,\qquad u\in\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T),

is a Banach space.

5.1.
Quasilinear equations

We consider the quasilinear fractional Schrödinger equation

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u(t)-u_{0})-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{A}(u(t))u(t)=0,\qquad t\in(0,T),

(5.2)

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
p>\frac{1}{\alpha}
.

Next, we choose
0<\varepsilon<\alpha-\frac{1}{p}
and introduce the following assumption for the quasilinear term:

(H
ε
)

\mathcal{A}(0)=A
and
\mathcal{A}\colon\mathcal{X}_{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}\to\mathcal{B}(D(A),H)
is Lipschitz continuous on bounded subsets of
\mathcal{X}_{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}
.

Then, we prove the following key lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

The continuous embedding

\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)\hookrightarrow C\left([0,T];\mathcal{X}_{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}\right)

holds, i.e., there is a constant
C_{\mathrm{em}}>0
, depending on
\alpha,p,\varepsilon,T
, such that for every

u\in\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)
, we have

\|u\|_{C([0,T];\mathcal{X}_{\alpha-\varepsilon,p})}\leq C_{\mathrm{em}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}.

(5.3)

Proof.

First, following
[
51
, Theorem 2.1 (i)]
, we can prove that the embedding

W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)\hookrightarrow W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T;H)

(5.4)

is continuous, where
W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T;H)
is the Sobolev-Slobodeckii space (see Adams
[
4
]
). Then, by
(\alpha-\varepsilon)p>1
and extension by zero to
(0,\infty),

[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
yields

W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T;H)\cap L^{p}(0,T;D(A))\hookrightarrow C\left([0,T];\mathcal{X}_{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}\right)

continuously. Let
u\in\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)
and
v:=u-u(0)\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)
. We have
v\in W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T;H)
and
u(0)\in W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T;H).
Then
u\in W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T;H)\cap L^{p}(0,T;D(A)).
Therefore,
u\in C\left([0,T];\mathcal{X}_{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}\right)
and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C\left([0,T];\mathcal{X}_{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u\|_{W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T;H)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u-u_{0}\|_{W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T;H)}+T^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u_{0}\|+\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;D(A))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}.

This yields the desired result.
∎

Now, we state the well-posedness of (
5.2
) for arbitrary
T
and small initial data.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
T>0
be fixed, and assume the assumption
(H
ε
)
. Then there exists
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
such that for all
u_{0}\in\overline{B}_{\mathcal{X}_{\alpha,p}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})
,
the equation (
5.2
) has a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T).

Proof.

We define the map
\mathcal{L}(f,u_{0})=u,
where
u
is the solution to (
1.1
). By the maximal regularity (Theorem
4.8
), we have

\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{B}\left(L^{p}(0,T;H)\times\mathcal{X}_{\alpha,p},\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)\right).

We define

\Psi(u)(t):=\mathrm{i}(\mathcal{A}(u(t))-A)u(t)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\Phi(u):=\mathcal{L}(\Psi(u),u_{0}).

Then
u
solves (
5.2
) if and only if
\Phi(u)=u
. Therefore, we will apply the Banach contraction mapping theorem. We set

B_{r}:=\overline{B}_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}(r),

where
r>0
is sufficiently small to be fixed later. First, we prove that
\Phi(B_{r})\subset B_{r}
. For
v\in B_{r},
by (
5.3
) and
\mathcal{A}(0)=A
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}(v(t))-A\|_{\mathcal{B}(D(A),H)}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\mathcal{A}}\|v(t)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\mathcal{A}}C_{\mathrm{em}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\mathcal{A}}C_{\mathrm{em}}r

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\|\mathcal{L}\|}

for small
r
. Then

\displaystyle\|\Phi(v)\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}\leq\|\mathcal{L}\|\left(\|\Psi(v)\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}+\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\alpha,p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{L}\|\left(\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mathcal{A}(v(t))-A\|_{\mathcal{B}(D(A),H)}\|v\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}+\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\alpha,p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{L}\|\left(\frac{1}{4\|\mathcal{L}\|}r+\frac{1}{4\|\mathcal{L}\|}r\right)=\frac{r}{2}<r,

if we choose
\varepsilon^{\prime}:=\dfrac{r}{4\|\mathcal{L}\|}
. Now, we prove that
\Phi
is a contraction on
B_{r}
in a similar way. For
v_{1},v_{2}\in B_{r},
we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi(v_{2})\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}\leq\|\mathcal{L}\|\|\Psi(v_{1})-\Psi(v_{2})\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{L}\|\left(\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mathcal{A}(v_{1}(t))-A\|_{\mathcal{B}(D(A),H)}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 36.98866pt\left.+\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mathcal{A}(v_{1}(t))-\mathcal{A}(v_{2}(t))\|_{\mathcal{B}(D(A),H)}\|v_{2}\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{L}\|\left(\frac{1}{4\|\mathcal{L}\|}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}+L_{\mathcal{A}}C_{\mathrm{em}}r\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}.

By Banach’s contraction mapping theorem, there is a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T).

∎

Remark 6
.

In the case
p=2
and
\alpha>\frac{1}{2},
we can choose
\varepsilon=0
in the assumption
(H
ε
)
. Indeed, it has been proven in
[
50

that the continuous embedding

H_{\alpha}(0,T;H)\hookrightarrow H^{\alpha}(0,T;H)

holds, which is stronger than the embedding (

). However, in the case
p\neq 2,
we do not
know if we have a continuous embedding

W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)\hookrightarrow W^{\alpha,p}(0,T;H).

We now present an example for which Theorem
5.2
applies in a semilinear setting.

Example.

We consider
H=L^{2}(\Omega)
as a base space, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\;(d\leq 3)
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary. We consider the following time-fractional Schrödinger equation with nonlinear diffusivity and reaction terms

\begin{cases}\mathrm{i}~\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\big(u-u_{0}\big)+\nabla\cdot\big(a(u)\nabla u\big)=f(u),&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
u=0,&\text{in }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(5.5)

where
\nabla\cdot
denotes the divergence operator. Here, we assume that:

•

the diffusivity coefficient satisfies

a\in W^{2,\infty}_{\mbox{loc}}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad a(0)>0;

(5.6)

•

the reaction term satisfies

f\in W^{2,\infty}_{\mbox{loc}}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad f(0)=0.

(5.7)

A classical example of a reaction term is the cubic nonlinearity, which corresponds to a bistable reaction:
f(u)=u-|u|^{2}u.

In this case,
\mathcal{A}(u)(v)=\nabla\cdot(a(u)\nabla v)-g(u)v
, where

g(\xi)=\begin{cases}\frac{f(\xi)}{\xi},\quad&\mbox{if}\quad\xi\neq 0,\\
f^{\prime}(0),\quad&\mbox{if}\quad\xi=0,\end{cases}

and
A=\mathcal{A}(0)=a(0)\Delta-g(0),
with domain
D(A)=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
We further assume
g(0)=f^{\prime}(0)\geq 0
so that
\sigma(-A)\subset(0,\infty)
. Note that
g\in W^{1,\infty}_{\mbox{loc}}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
with Lipschitz constant
L_{g}=\frac{L_{f^{\prime}}}{2}
due to
g(\xi)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}(s\xi)\mathrm{d}s,\;\xi\in\mathbb{C}
.

We can also consider the equation in non-divergence form:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{i}~\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\big(u-u_{0}\big)+a(u)\Delta u=f(u),&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
u=0,&\text{in }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

for which it is sufficient to assume that
a\in W^{1,\infty}_{\mbox{loc}}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R})
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
T>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
be fixed, and assume (

)-(

We will prove that the quasilinear operator

\textbf{(H${}_{\varepsilon}$)}.
For this purpose, we first prove the continuous embedding

\mathcal{X}_{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,4}(\Omega)

for

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(z)y-\mathcal{A}(w)y=

B
.
∎

5.2.
Semilinear equations

We consider the following semilinear fractional Schrödinger equation

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u(t)-u_{0})-\mathrm{i}Au(t)=F(u(t)),\qquad t\in(0,T),

(5.10)

where we consider the following assumption:

(H)

F:\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)\to L^{p}(0,T;H)
satisfies the following continuity property: for any
\epsilon>0
, there
exists a constant
C=C(\epsilon,p)>0
such that

\|F(v)-F(w)\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}\leq\epsilon\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}+C\|v-w\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}

(5.11)

for all
v,w\in\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T).

A sufficient condition under which this hypothesis holds is given by the following (see
[
8
, Lemme 8.2]
):

F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T),L^{p}(0,T;H))\;\mbox{is compact}.

(5.12)

Another simple assumption implying
(H)
is the following (see
[
1
, Example 4.1]
):

F:D(A)\to H\quad\text{is globally Lipschitz}.

Remark 7
.

Although we follow
[
1
]
in this part, we address the following issues:

•

In
[
1
, p. 14]
, the maximal regularity space
MR(\alpha,p,\tau)
depends on a fixed initial datum
u_{0}
(see also
[
54
, p. 8]
and
[
2
, p. 528]
).

•

The trace space
Tr^{p}_{\alpha}
of initial data is not well defined (see
[
1
, p. 14]
). The natural space for
\alpha>\frac{1}{p}
is given by
\mathcal{X}_{\alpha,p}
defined in (
4.9
).

•

The associated quantity
\|\cdot\|_{MR(\alpha,p,\tau)}
in
[
1
, p. 14]
is not a norm when
\alpha<\frac{1}{p}
and
u_{0}\neq 0
.

Therefore, the results in
[
1
]
hold only for
u_{0}=0
. This is not the case for our maximal regularity space
\mathcal{MR}_{\alpha,p}(T)
defined in (
5.1
).

Next, we state the well-posedness of (
5.10
) for small times.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
p>\frac{1}{\alpha}
and assume the assumption
(H)
. Then, there exists
T_{1}>0
such that, for all
u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}_{\alpha,p}
and all
T\in(0,T_{1})
, the equation (
5.10
) has a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T).

Remark 8
.

In the above theorem, we only consider small times, in contrast to
[
1
, Theorem 4.2]
, whose proof omits the initial datum in the final estimate on p. 20.

To prove Theorem
5.4
, we need some preliminary estimates. For
\alpha>0
, we set

k_{\alpha}(t)=\begin{cases}t^{\alpha-1},\quad&t>0,\\
0,\quad&t\leq 0,\end{cases}

and we denote by
k_{\alpha}^{*n}
the
n
-fold convolution of
k_{\alpha}
with itself, that is,

k_{\alpha}^{*n}:=\underbrace{k_{\alpha}*k_{\alpha}*\cdots*k_{\alpha}}_{n\ \text{times}}.

Then, for all
t>0
, the following explicit formula holds

k_{\alpha}^{*n}(t)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)^{n}}{\Gamma(n\alpha)}\,t^{n\alpha-1}.

(5.13)

We prove the following key lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

For all
u\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|^{p}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T^{\frac{\alpha p}{q}}}{\alpha^{\frac{p}{q}}\Gamma(\alpha)^{p}}\int^{T}_{0}k_{\alpha}(T-r)\int_{0}^{r}\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(s)\|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}s\;\mathrm{d}r.

(5.14)

Proof.

Let
u\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)
. From (
2.7
), we have

u=J^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u,\qquad\mbox{ in}\quad L^{p}(0,T;H).

Then, for a.e.
t\in(0,T),
and by Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|

\displaystyle=\|J^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\int^{t}_{0}k_{\alpha}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}k_{\alpha}(t-s)\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(s)\|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}}{\alpha^{\frac{1}{q}}\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\int^{t}_{0}k_{\alpha}(t-s)\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(s)\|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then integrating over
(0,T)
and applying Tonelli’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|^{p}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T^{\frac{\alpha p}{q}}}{\alpha^{\frac{p}{q}}\Gamma(\alpha)^{p}}\int_{0}^{T}\int^{t}_{0}k_{\alpha}(t-s)\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(s)\|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{T^{\frac{\alpha p}{q}}}{\alpha^{\frac{p}{q}}\Gamma(\alpha)^{p}}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{s}^{T}k_{\alpha}(t-s)\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(s)\|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{T^{\frac{\alpha p}{q}}}{\alpha^{\frac{p}{q}+1}\Gamma(\alpha)^{p}}\int^{T}_{0}k_{\alpha+1}(T-s)\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(s)\|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{T^{\frac{\alpha p}{q}}}{\alpha^{\frac{p}{q}}\Gamma(\alpha)^{p}}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{s}^{T}k_{\alpha}(T-r)\mathrm{d}r\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(s)\|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{T^{\frac{\alpha p}{q}}}{\alpha^{\frac{p}{q}}\Gamma(\alpha)^{p}}\int^{T}_{0}k_{\alpha}(T-r)\int_{0}^{r}\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(s)\|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}s\;\mathrm{d}r.

This yields the desired estimate.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

We define the linear operator
\mathcal{L}:L^{p}(0,T;H)\times\mathcal{X}_{\alpha,p}\to\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)
by
\mathcal{L}(f,u_{0})=u,
where
u
is the solution to (
1.1
) associated with
u_{0}
and
f
. By the maximal regularity (Theorem
4.8
), we have

\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{B}\left(L^{p}(0,T;H)\times\mathcal{X}_{\alpha,p},\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)\right).

We fix
u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}_{\alpha,p}
and define
\Phi:\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)\to\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)
by

\Phi(v):=\mathcal{L}(F(v),u_{0}).

Note that, for all
v\in\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)
, we have
F(v)\in L^{p}(0,T;H)
, so
\Phi
is well defined. Then
u
solves (
5.10
) if and only if
\Phi(u)=u
. Therefore, we will apply the Banach contraction mapping theorem for iterates of
\Phi
.

Let
v,w\in\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)
. Then,

\displaystyle\|\Phi(v)-\Phi(w)\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}=\|\mathcal{L}(F(v)-F(w),0)\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{L}\|\|F(v)-F(w)\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\|\mathcal{L}\|\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}+C\|\mathcal{L}\|\|v-w\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\|\mathcal{L}\|\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\mathcal{L}\|\left(\|(v-w)-(v(0)-w(0))\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}+\|v(0)-w(0)\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon\|\mathcal{L}\|\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\mathcal{L}\|\left(\|(v-w)-(v(0)-w(0))\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H)}+T^{\frac{1}{p}}\|v(0)-w(0)\|\right).

Using the estimate (
5.14
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Phi(v)-\Phi(w)\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}\leq(\epsilon+CT^{\frac{1}{p}})\|\mathcal{L}\|\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}

\displaystyle+C\|\mathcal{L}\|\frac{T^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}}{\alpha^{\frac{1}{q}}\Gamma(\alpha)^{p}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}k_{\alpha}(T-r)\int_{0}^{r}\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}((v-w)-(v(0)-w(0)))(s)\|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}s\;\mathrm{d}r\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq(\epsilon+CT^{\frac{1}{p}})\|\mathcal{L}\|\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}+C\|\mathcal{L}\|\frac{T^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}}{\alpha^{\frac{1}{q}}\Gamma(\alpha)^{p}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}k_{\alpha}(T-r)\|v-w\|^{p}_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}(r)}\;\mathrm{d}r\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}+\gamma\left(\int^{T}_{0}k_{\alpha}(T-r)\|v-w\|^{p}_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}(r)}\;\mathrm{d}r\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where we choose
\epsilon=\frac{1}{4\|\mathcal{L}\|}
,
0<T\leq\frac{1}{(4C\|\mathcal{L}\|)^{p}}
and set
\gamma:=C\|\mathcal{L}\|\frac{T^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}}{\alpha^{\frac{1}{q}}\Gamma(\alpha)^{p}}
.

Thus, by induction, for every
n\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{n}(v)-\Phi^{n}(w)\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{n-j}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}k_{\alpha}^{(*j)}(T-r)\,\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{MR}(r)}^{p}\,dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\leq\left[\frac{1}{2^{n}}+\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{n-j}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}k_{\alpha}^{(*j)}(T-r)\,\,dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right]\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}.

Using the formula (
5.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{n}(v)-\Phi^{n}(w)\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left[1+\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{n}2^{j}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)^{j}}{\Gamma(j\alpha+1)}T^{j\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right]\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T)}.

The convergence of the series
\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{j}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)^{j}}{\Gamma(j\alpha+1)}T^{j\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
follows from Stirling’s formula and the Cauchy root test. Then, for
n
sufficiently large,

\Phi^{n}
is a contraction. By Banach’s contraction mapping theorem, there is a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,p}(T).

∎

W_{\alpha,p}(0,T;H)\hookrightarrow W_{\beta,p}(0,T;H),\qquad 0<\beta<\alpha.

Example.
Let

,

and

. The following equation is locally well-posed in the sense of Theorem

:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\big(u-u_{0}\big)-\mathrm{i}\Delta u+\xi\partial_{t}^{\beta}(u-u_{0})=f(u),&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\

6.
Conclusion and final comments

In this work, we have investigated the maximal regularity problem for abstract time-fractional Schrödinger equations governed by a self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent on a Hilbert space, where the fractional derivative is of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
. We first established maximal
L^{2}
-regularity by exploiting refined estimates for Mittag–Leffler functions with the imaginary argument. In contrast to the classical analysis of subdiffusion equations, our approach does not rely on the complete monotonicity of Mittag–Leffler functions, a property that appears difficult to handle in the presence of the imaginary argument. We then extended these results to maximal
L^{p}
-regularity for any
p\in(1,\infty)
by applying the operator-valued Mikhlin multiplier theorem.

We stress that, in contrast to the time-fractional case considered here, the classical Schrödinger equation does not enjoy maximal regularity (see Remark
3
), which highlights a fundamental difference at this level between the classical and fractional models.

To illustrate the applicability of our maximal regularity results, we have proven the well-posedness for some quasilinear and semilinear Schrödinger equations with time-fractional derivatives. Furthermore, our results may serve as a basis for establishing well-posedness of non-autonomous time-fractional Schrödinger equations in the spirit of the recent paper
[
2
]
.

As a perspective for future work, we aim to extend the analysis of time-fractional Schrödinger equations to Banach space settings, with particular emphasis on well-posedness, maximal regularity, controllability and inverse problems, in a broader and more general framework; see, for instance, the recent works
[
10
,
16
]
.
