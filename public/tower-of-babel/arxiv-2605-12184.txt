Thomas Jackson, Bruno Nachtergaele, Amanda Young

Abstract
. We prove that the ground state of the AKLT models on the hexagonal lattice and the Lieb lattice satisfy the local topological quantum order (LTQO) condition. This will be a consequence of proving that the finite volume ground states are indistinguishable from a unique infinite volume ground state. Concretely, we identify a sequence of increasing and absorbing finite volumes for which any finite volume ground state expectation is well approximated by the infinite volume state with error decaying at a uniform exponential rate in the distance between the support of the observable and boundary of the finite volume. As a corollary to the LTQO property, we obtain that the spectral gap above the ground state in these models is stable under general small perturbations of sufficient decay. We prove these results by a detailed analysis of the polymer representation of the ground states state derived by Kennedy, Lieb and Tasaki (1988) with the necessary modifications required for proving the strong form of ground state indistinguishability needed for LTQO.

Introduction and Main Results

1.1.
Introduction

The mathematical study of gapped ground states of quantum spin systems has yielded a broad variety of fascinating results over the past two decades. We speak about a gapped ground state phase when there is a positive gap in the spectrum of the Hamiltonian of the infinite system that is stable under a broad class of gently perturbed Hamiltonians. These gapped ground state phases, which in their simplest form are described by a unique ground state without ordinary long-range order, may exhibit so-called topological orders. Characterizing and mathematically classifying the possible toplogical orders has been a very active and fruitful area of research. For a selection of mathematical results of this type see
[
35
,
13
,
38
,
39
,
10
,
26
,
25
,
24
,
9
]
.

While the spectral gap question is typically rather trivial for concrete commuting Hamiltonians, even for small perturbations of classical models the question whether a model is gapped or gapless is, in general, undecidable
[
14
]
. For non-commuting interactions, even frustration-free ones, the question has been addressed in only a few examples in two or more dimensions: PVBS models
[
5
,
7
,
6
]
, AKLT models on decorated lattices
[
1
,
41
,
33
]
, and the AKLT model on the hexagonal lattice
[
30
,
41
]
.

Given the existence of a positive gap, the next question to address is its stability under gentle perturbations of the Hamiltonian. The goal is to show that the gap remains open after the perturbation of the model by a broad class of interactions as long they are sufficiently small. This typically involves proving that the perturbation is relatively (form) bounded with respect to the unperturbed one after a suitable spectrum-preserving transformation. Strategies of varying generality to achieve such bounds have been developed. For example, the first spectral gap stability result for an AKLT model relied on a cluster expansion
[
45
]
, as do some later results
[
16
,
8
]
. In
[
21
,
17
,
18
]
the authors introduce and apply a transformation they call Lie-Schwinger block diagonalization to prove relative boundedness. The most general results have been obtained using the Bravyi-Hastings-Michalakis (BHM) strategy
[
11
,
12
,
34
,
36
]
. The BHM strategy is sufficiently general to apply to AKLT models including the two-dimensional AKLT models we consider in this work. The crucial property one needs to apply this approach is called Local Topological Quantum Order (LTQO) of the ground states which can alternatively be stated as an indistinguishability property of finite-volume ground states (see below). In this paper we prove this property for the AKLT models on the hexagonal lattice and on the Lieb lattice.

Apart from being an essential ingredient in proofs of spectral gap stability, LTQO is a structural property relevant for characterizing the quantum code provided by the ground state of Kitaev’s quantum double and the Levin-Wen string-net models
[
28
,
31
,
15
]
and a closely related condition is employed in the construction of the boundary algebra describing topological order
[
24
]
.

AKLT models, which were introduced by Affleck, Kennedy, Lieb, and Tasaki in
[
2
,
3
]
, are frustration-free models with
SU(2)
invariant nearest neighbor interactions. For their general definition see Section
1.2
.
For one-dimensional AKLT models (defined on
{\mathbb{Z}}
or a more general quasi-one-dimensional lattice), generally speaking, the ground states, the spectral gap, and spectral gap stability, can be analyzed systematically. For example, if such a model has a finite number of infinite-volume gapped ground states, these states satisfy LTQO and the gap above those ground states is stable
[
36
, Appendix B]
.

The main objective of this paper is to prove the LTQO property for the hexagonal and Lieb lattices. The AKLT model on the hexagonal lattice has a spin 3/2 degree of freedom at each site and a nearest neighbor interaction given by the orthogonal projection on the total spin 3 states for each pair of nearest neighbor spin. The Lieb lattice is a decorated version of the regular square lattice. It can be viewed as a square lattice with one vertex added at each of the edges (see Figure
1
). The corresponding AKLT model has a spin 2 at each degree 4 site and a spin 1 at each site of degree 2. The interaction is nearest neighbor and given by the spin 3 projection for each nearest neighbor pair of the Lieb lattice.

In
[
3
]
it was conjectured that the AKLT models in regular higher-dimensional lattices would have a unique ground state in the thermodynamic limit and a spectral gap if the degree of the vertices is at most 4. This includes the hexagonal and square lattices. For higher degree lattices one expects gapless ground states and Néel order. This dependence on the degree is also supported by results for the AKLT model on trees
[
19
,
22
]
. AKLT models on so-called decorated lattices are also of interest. For example, their ground state can serve as a resource for universal quantum computation
[
43
,
44
]
. In this paper we study the models on the hexagonal lattice and the square lattice with regular decoration consisting of
m
vertices inserted at each edge. We prove LTQO for all these models except for the square lattice, for which we have to assume
m\geq 1
. The undecorated square lattice is expected to have a unique gapped ground state as suggested by the exponential decay of the spin-spin correlation function proved in
[
27
]
. We note that there exist several families
of
SU(N)
-invariant generalizations of the AKLT models. A particular family introduced in
[
3
]
involves alternating conjugate symmetric representations (with the spin dimension determined by the degree of the lattice site). This is well-defined for bipartite graphs, and has a similar cluster expansion as the ones used in this paper. LTQO results of the present paper can be generalized to those models
[
23
]
.

We now briefly summarize prior results about the ground state gap and the LTQO property of these and some related models.

LTQO is an essential property for all existing spectral gap stability results that are applicable to the AKLT models, including the models on the hexagonal and Lieb lattices. The existence of a uniform gap for the hexagonal lattice was originally conjectured in
[
3
]
, and there is strong evidence to support this claim. Kennedy, Lieb, and Tasaki proved that the frustration-free ground state
\omega
has exponential decay of correlations
[
27
]
, a property that generically occurs together with a non-vanishing gap in the spectrum above the ground state. AKLT models on decorated hexagonal lattices have also been shown to be uniformly gapped
[
1
,
33
]
, and convincing corroboration of a uniform gap for the (undecorated) hexagonal lattice model was independently obtained by combining analytical methods with sophisticated numerical techniques in two almost simultaneously appearing papers,
[
30
,
41
]
. The results in
[
40
]
also indicate a gap for the AKLT model on the Lieb lattice.

LTQO and, as a consequence, spectral gap stability has previously been shown in
[
33
]
for the AKLT on the decorated hexagonal lattice with decoration number five or more. Here we show LTQO for the AKLT model on the hexagonal lattice with any number of decoration, including the standard, undecorated lattice. We also show LTQO for the AKLT model on the decorated square lattice with decoration number
m\geq 1
, which includes the Lieb lattice. Our proofs rely very much on the approach taken in
[
33
]
and the polymer representation of
[
27
]
. The main difference consists of the way we use the convergence criteria of Kotecký and Preiss
[
29
]
and Ueltschi
[
42
]
. Since the arguments in
[
40
]
show the AKLT model on the Lieb lattice to be gapped, we also obtain that the gap is stable. Furthermore, we use the cluster expansion techniques to show exponential decay of the two-point correlation for the same class of models.

For completeness we now state a spectral gap stability result which, due to the LTQO property we prove, follows from known results. Gap stability results utilizing LTQO fall into two different categories depending on the type of spectral gap the model exhibits. A model is said to be uniformly gapped if there exists an increasing and absorbing sequence of finite volumes for which the spectral gaps of the associated local Hamiltonians are all uniformly bounded from below by the same nonzero constant. Alternatively, one can consider the spectral gap question for a ground state of the infinite volume system
[
37
]
. Frustration-free models (which includes AKLT models) that exhibit LTQO necessarily have a unique infinite volume frustration-free ground state.
The state is then called a gapped ground state if the GNS Hamiltonian has a gap in its spectrum above the ground state. When the lattice for the model has no boundary, the GNS gap is also referred to as the bulk gap. As such, the existence of the bulk gap immediately follows if an associated sequence of finite volume models has a uniform gap. However, in certain cases, the bulk gap can be positive even if the finite-volume gaps of the limiting sequence tends to zero. The LTQO condition one verifies for stability is the same regardless of the type of gap stability one wishes to prove.

For the AKLT model on the hexagonal and Lieb lattices the stability of the uniform gap follows from Corollary
1.2
given that the model is uniformly gapped across the sequence
\Lambda_{N}
. A similar statement for the bulk gap can be obtained by making small adjustments to the statement of
[
33
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Section

The case that

2.
Description of the Ground States

2.1.
Ground States and Expectations of AKLT Models

As in
[
27
]
, we use the Weyl representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}(2)
acting on homogeneous polynomials of two variables to give a description of the ground state space of the AKLT Hamiltonian. As we will consider several values of spin, we introduce the Hilbert space representing a particle of general spin
s\in{\mathbb{N}}/2
. Namely,

{\mathcal{H}}^{(s)}=\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{2s}\lambda_{k}u^{k}v^{2s-k}:\lambda_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}

where
u=\cos(\theta/2)e^{i\phi/2}
and
v=\sin(\theta/2)e^{-i\phi/2}
are functions of
0\leq\phi<2\pi,

0\leq\theta<\pi
, and the inner product for all
\Psi,\,\Phi\in{\mathcal{H}}^{(s)}
is

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Psi,\Phi\right\rangle=\int d\Omega\overline{\Psi(\theta,\phi)}\Phi(\theta,\phi),\qquad d\Omega=\frac{1}{4\pi}\sin(\theta)d\phi d\theta\,.

Here, we note that
d\Omega
is the measure associated with the spherical variable

(2.2)

\Omega=(\sin(\theta)\cos(\phi),\sin(\theta)\sin(\phi),\cos(\theta))

and
{\mathcal{H}}^{(s)}\subseteq L^{2}(d\Omega)
for all
s\in{\mathbb{N}}/2
, the latter of which is independent of
s
. The spin-
s
matrices in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}^{(s)})
given in terms of the Weyl representation are

(2.3)

S^{3}=\frac{1}{2}\left(v\partial_{v}-u\partial_{u}\right),\qquad S^{-}=u\partial_{v},\qquad S^{+}=v\partial_{u}\,.

To described the class AKLT models considered in this work in the context of the Weyl representation, let
\Gamma=({\mathcal{V}}_{\Gamma},{\mathcal{E}}_{\Gamma})
be any simple connected graph. The local Hilbert space for any
x\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\Gamma}
is given by
{\mathcal{H}}_{x}:={\mathcal{H}}^{(s_{x})}
where
s_{x}=\deg_{\Gamma}(x)/2
. Let
u_{x},\,v_{x},\,\theta_{x},\,\phi_{x},

\Omega_{x}
and
d\Omega_{x}
denote the variables associated to
x
. Then, for any finite subgraph
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma
, the inner product on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda}=\bigotimes_{x\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda}}{\mathcal{H}}_{x}
is

(2.4)

\langle\Phi,\Psi\rangle=\int d\Omega^{\Lambda}\overline{\Phi(\theta,\phi)}\Psi(\theta,\phi)\qquad\forall\Phi,\Psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda}

where
d\Omega^{\Lambda}
is the product measure associated with
\{d\Omega_{x}:x\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda}\}
, and
\Phi(\theta,\phi)
and
\Psi(\theta,\phi)
are functions of the variables
\theta=(\theta_{x})_{x\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda}}
and
\phi=(\phi_{x})_{x\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda}}
. As described in Section
1.2
, the AKLT interaction
P_{e}
any edge
e=(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Gamma}
, is the orthogonal projection onto the subspace of maximal total spin
s_{x}+s_{y}
. Considering (
2.3
), this is the orthogonal projection onto the subspace

(2.5)

{\rm span}\left\{(u_{x}\partial_{v_{x}}+u_{y}\partial_{v_{y}})^{k}v_{x}^{2s_{x}}v_{y}^{2s_{y}}:0\leq k\leq s_{x}+s_{y}\right\}\cong{\mathcal{H}}^{(s_{x}+s_{y})}\,.

The ground state space of the AKLT model for any finite subgraph
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma
is then given by the following result.

Theorem 2.1

(Ground State Space
[
27
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma=({\mathcal{V}}_{\Gamma},\,{\mathcal{E}}_{\Gamma})
be any simple connected graph. For any finite subgraph
\Lambda=({\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda},{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda})\subseteq\Gamma
, the ground state space of
H_{\Lambda}=\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}}P_{e}
is

(2.6)

{\mathcal{G}}_{\Lambda}=\left\{\Psi(f)=f\cdot\prod_{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}}(u_{x}v_{y}-v_{y}u_{x}):f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\partial\Lambda}^{\rm gss}\right\}

where

(2.7)

{\mathcal{H}}_{\partial\Lambda}^{\mathrm{gss}}=\bigotimes_{x\in\partial\Lambda}{\mathcal{H}}^{(d_{x}^{\prime}/2)},\qquad d_{x}^{\prime}=\deg_{\Gamma}(x)-\deg_{\Lambda}(x)

In particular, this holds for any finite subgraph of the
m
-decorated square or hexagonal lattice, with
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.

This result follows from first proving that for any edge
e=(x,y)\in\Gamma
,

\mathrm{ker}P_{e}=\left\{(v_{x}u_{y}-u_{x}v_{y})g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{x}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{y}:g\in{\mathcal{H}}^{(s_{x}-1/2)}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{(s_{y}-1/2)}\right\}.

Since
{\mathcal{H}}_{x}={\mathcal{H}}^{(s_{x})}
is the set of all homogeneous polynomials of degree
s_{x}=\deg_{\Gamma}(x)/2
in two variables, it follows that every interaction
P_{e}
,
e=(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}
, is simultaneously minimized by any vector
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda}
that has a singlet
u_{x}v_{y}-v_{x}u_{y}
across each edge. Since the polynomials over
\mathbb{C}
form a unique factorization domain, this produces (
2.6
). For complete details, see
[
27
]
.

It was shown
[
4
]
that for any finite subgraph,
\Lambda
, of a simple connected graph,
\Gamma
, and any
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}
, there exists a polynomial
A({\bf\Omega})
in the variables
(u_{x},v_{x},\overline{u_{x}},\overline{v_{x}})_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(A)}
, called the
symbol of
A
, such that the matrix element of
A
for any vectors
\Psi,\Phi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda}
is

(2.8)

\left\langle\Psi,A\Phi\right\rangle=\int d\Omega^{\Lambda}\overline{\Psi(\theta,\phi)}\Phi(\theta,\phi)A({\bf\Omega})\,.

This is a consequence of using that
\{\partial_{u}^{k}\partial_{v}^{l}u^{k+j}v^{l-j}:k,l\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\,-k\leq j\leq l\}
is a spanning set of
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}^{(s)})
for any
s
, and then applying the identity

\left\langle\Phi,\partial_{u}^{k}\partial_{v}^{l}u^{k+j}v^{l-j}\Psi\right\rangle=C_{k,l}\left\langle u^{k}v^{l}\Phi,u^{k+j}v^{l-j}\Psi\right\rangle,\quad C_{k,l}=\frac{(2s+l+k+1)!}{(2s+1)!}\,.

See
[
27
,
33
]
for more details. We note that the symbol
A({\bf\Omega})
is not necessarily unique, but that our calculations will not depend on the particular choice.

An immediate consequence of (
2.8
) and Theorem
2.1
, is that the expectation of an observable
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}
in any ground state
\Psi(f)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\Lambda}
is given by

(2.9)

\langle\Psi(f),A\Psi(f)\rangle=\int d\Omega^{\Lambda}\prod_{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}}|u_{x}v_{y}-v_{x}u_{y}|^{2}|f|^{2}A({\bf\Omega})=\int d\rho_{\Lambda}|f|^{2}A({\bf\Omega})

where one replaces
|u_{x}v_{y}-v_{x}u_{y}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1-\Omega_{x}\cdot\Omega_{y})
and defines

(2.10)

d\rho_{\Lambda}=\rho_{\Lambda}d\Omega^{\Lambda},\qquad\rho_{\Lambda}=2^{-|{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}|}\prod_{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}}(1-\Omega_{x}\cdot\Omega_{y})\,.

Theorem
2.1
establishes that any two AKLT ground states on a finite volume
\Lambda
differ only by the choice of boundary polynomial. In the case that there is a unique limiting infinite volume ground state one understands intuitively that, for any observable
A
supported sufficiently far from the boundary of
\Lambda
, the expectation in any finite volume state on
\Lambda
should be roughly that of the infinite volume state. Theorem
1.1
makes explicit upper bounds on how different these expectations can be from one another, and is a consequence of analyzing two different maps, called the
bulk-boundary map
and
bulk state
.

2.2.
The Bulk-Boundary Map and Bulk State for an Increasing and Absorbing Sequence of Volumes

The goal of this section is to introduce these maps in a relatively general setting, which will in particular, be applicable both the hexagonal and square lattices as well as any decorated version of these lattices.

For the remainder of this section, we assume that
\Gamma=({\mathcal{V}}_{\Gamma},{\mathcal{E}}_{\Gamma})
is any infinite, simple connected graph, and that
\Lambda=({\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda},{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda})
and
\Lambda^{\prime}=({\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda^{\prime}},{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda^{\prime}})
are two finite subgraphs of
\Gamma
for which

(i)

\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\mathring{\Lambda}
.

(ii)

|\partial\Lambda\cap\{x,y\}|\leq 1
for any edge
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}
.

Above,
\mathring{\Lambda}
denotes the interior of
\Lambda
. The first condition guarantees that the boundaries of
\Lambda^{\prime}
and
\Lambda
are disjoint, and the second condition guarantees that only one vertex across any edge can belong to the boundary,
\partial\Lambda
. We note that, in particular, these properties are satisfied if one takes
\Lambda=\Lambda_{N}^{(m)}
and
\Lambda^{\prime}=\mathring{\Lambda}_{K}^{(m)}
for any
K<N
, where these are the
m
-decorated sequences defined in (
1.3
)-(
1.4
), see also (
1.14
) and the text that follows.

Lemma
2.2
below, which is a generalization of
[
33
, Lemma 4.2]
, shows that for any
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
as above, the ground state expectation for any
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
in any state
\Psi(f)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\Lambda}
can be written in terms of
|f|^{2}
and
the bulk-boundary map

\omega_{\Lambda}
defined by

(2.11)

\displaystyle\omega_{\Lambda}(A;\Omega^{\partial\Lambda})

\displaystyle:=Z_{\Lambda}(A;\Omega^{\partial\Lambda})/Z_{\Lambda}(\Omega^{\partial\Lambda})

\displaystyle Z_{\Lambda}(A;\Omega^{\partial\Lambda})

\displaystyle:=\int d\Omega^{\mathring{\Lambda}}\rho_{\Lambda}A(\Omega),\quad Z_{\Lambda}(\Omega^{\partial\Lambda})=Z_{\Lambda}({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}};\Omega^{\partial\Lambda})

We note that condition (i) guarantees that
\omega_{\Lambda}(A,;\Omega^{\partial\Lambda})
is a polynomial in the boundary variables
\Omega^{\partial\Lambda}=(\Omega_{x}:x\in\partial\Lambda)
. In particular, this is a continuous function of these variables.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is an infinite, simple connected graph and
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
are any two finite volumes satisfying properties (i)-(ii) above. For any
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
and
\Psi(f)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\Lambda}
, one has

(2.12)

\langle\Psi(f),A\Psi(f)\rangle=\int d\rho_{\Lambda}|f|^{2}\omega_{\Lambda}(A;\Omega^{\partial\Lambda})\,.

Proof.

This is a consequence of recognizing that since
\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\mathring{\Lambda}
,

\langle\Psi(f),A\Psi(f)\rangle=\int d\Omega^{\Lambda}|f|^{2}\rho_{\Lambda}A(\Omega)=\int d\Omega^{\partial\Lambda}|f|^{2}\int d\Omega^{\mathring{\Lambda}}\rho_{\Lambda}\frac{\int d\Omega^{\mathring{\Lambda}}\rho_{\Lambda}A(\Omega)}{\int d\Omega^{\mathring{\Lambda}}\rho_{\Lambda}}

and verifying that
Z_{\Lambda}(\Omega^{\partial\Lambda})=\int d\Omega^{\mathring{\Lambda}}\rho_{\Lambda}>0
for all values of the boundary variables
(\Omega_{x}:x\in\partial\Lambda)
. To prove the latter,
x\in\partial\mathring{\Lambda}
if and only if there exists
y\in\partial\Lambda
such that
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}
. As a consequence, for any fixed choice of the boundary variables
\Omega^{\partial\Lambda}=(\Omega_{x}:x\in\partial)
,

Z_{N}(\Omega^{\partial\Lambda})=\|\Psi(g_{\Omega^{\partial\Lambda}})\|^{2}\quad\text{where}\quad g_{\Omega^{\partial\Lambda}}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}:\\
x\in\partial\Lambda\end{subarray}}(u_{x}v_{y}-u_{y}v_{x})\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\partial\mathring{\Lambda}}^{\mathrm{gss}}\,.

By condition (ii),
g_{\Omega^{\partial\Lambda}}
is a nonzero polynomial for any choice of
\Omega^{\partial\Lambda}
. Therefore, by Theorem
2.1
,
\Psi(g_{\Omega^{\partial\Lambda_{N}}})\neq 0
as desired.
∎

The definition for the
bulk state

\omega_{\Lambda}^{\rm bulk}:{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}\to{\mathbb{C}}
comes from averaging
Z_{\Lambda}(A;\Omega^{\partial\Lambda})
over the boundary variables. Since all ground states
\Psi(f)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\Lambda}
only differ by the choice of boundary polynomial, in the case that one expects there to be a unique infinite volume ground state,
\omega_{\Lambda}^{\rm bulk}(A)
should produce a good approximation of
\left\langle\Psi(f),A\Psi(f)\right\rangle
for any normalized
\Psi(f)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\Lambda}
and observable
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
when
d(\Lambda^{\prime},\partial\Lambda)>>1
. Concretely, for any
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\omega_{\Lambda}^{\rm bulk}(A)

\displaystyle:=Z_{\Lambda}^{\rm bulk}(A)/Z_{\Lambda}^{\rm bulk}

\displaystyle Z^{\mathrm{bulk}}_{\Lambda}(A)

\displaystyle:=\int d\rho_{\Lambda}A(\Omega)=\int d\Omega^{\partial\Lambda}Z_{\Lambda}(A;\Omega^{\partial\Lambda}),\quad Z^{\mathrm{bulk}}_{\Lambda}=Z^{\mathrm{bulk}}_{\Lambda}(\mathbbm{1})\,.

While it is not a priori clear whether
\omega_{\Lambda}^{\rm bulk}
is a state on
{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda}
, it is a ground state of
H_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
. This can be seen from applying Theorem
2.1
where one replaces the infinite graph
\Gamma
with
\tilde{\Gamma}=\Lambda
. In this case
\omega_{\Lambda}^{\rm bulk}
is the unique ground state on
\tilde{\Gamma}
. By frustration-freeness, this is also a ground state of
H_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
, and by property (i), this Hamiltonian is unchanged from replacing
\Gamma
with
\tilde{\Gamma}
.

In the case that
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
with
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
satisfying properties (i)-(ii) we also define
\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda
to be the minimal subgraph whose edge set is
{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}\setminus{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
. This is motivated by partitioning the product in (
2.10
) based on which edges are contained in
\Lambda^{\prime}
. Since
A(\Omega)
only depends on the variables

, we can write the integrals in (

) and (
2.13
) as

Z_{\Lambda}(A;\Omega^{\partial\Lambda})=\int d\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime}}A({\bf\Omega})\Phi_{\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}}({\bf\Omega}),\qquad Z_{\Lambda}^{{\rm bulk}}(A)=\int d\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime}}A({\bf\Omega})\Phi_{\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}}^{{\rm bulk}}({\bf\Omega})

where

(2.14)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}}({\bf\Omega})

\displaystyle=2^{-|{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}}|}\int d\Omega^{(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime})\setminus\partial(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime})}\prod_{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}}}(1-\Omega_{x}\cdot\Omega_{y})

(2.15)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}}^{{\rm bulk}}({\bf\Omega})

\displaystyle=2^{-|{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}}|}\int d\Omega^{(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime})\setminus\partial\Lambda^{\prime}}\prod_{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}}}(1-\Omega_{x}\cdot\Omega_{y})\,.

Here, we note that property (i) guarantees that
\partial(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime})=\partial\Lambda\cup\partial\Lambda^{\prime}
. Above, the integral in (
2.14
) is taken with respect to the product measure
\Omega_{x}
associated to the interior sites

of
\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}
, and (
2.15
) is taken with respect to the product measure associated to the sites of
\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}
that are not boundary sites of
\Lambda^{\prime}
.

1.1

We are now in a position to outline the proof of Theorem
1.1
. To this end, given the
m
-decorated sequence
\Lambda_{N}^{(m)}
as in (
1.3
), in the case that
\Gamma={\mathbb{H}}
, or (
1.4
), in the case that
\Gamma={\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, we define for any
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Lambda_{N}^{(m)}}
,

(2.16)

\omega_{N}^{(m)}(A;\Omega^{\partial\Lambda_{N}})=\omega_{\Lambda_{N}^{(m)}}(A;\Omega^{\partial\Lambda_{N}}),\qquad\omega_{N}^{\mathrm{bulk},(m)}(A)=\omega_{\Lambda_{N}^{(m)}}^{\mathrm{bulk}}(A)

and analogously label
Z_{N}^{(m)}(A;\Omega^{\partial\Lambda_{N}})
,
Z_{N}^{(m)}(\Omega^{\partial\Lambda_{N}})
,
Z^{\mathrm{bulk},(m)}_{N}(A)
and
Z^{\mathrm{bulk},(m)}_{N}
the pieces defining
\omega_{N}^{(m)}
and
\omega_{N}^{\mathrm{bulk},(m)}
, respectively. Given the previous observations, any weak-
*
limit of
\omega_{N}^{{\rm bulk},(m)}
is a frustration-free ground state of the AKLT model on
\Gamma^{(m)}
. If
\omega_{N}^{{\rm bulk},(m)}(A)
is Cauchy for all
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}^{\rm loc}
, then there is only one such weak-
*
limit and

\lim_{N\to\infty}\omega_{N}^{{\rm bulk},(m)}(A)=\omega^{(m)}(A)\qquad\forall\,A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma^{(m)}}^{\rm loc}\,.

We will prove that this is the case for the specific AKLT models covered in Theorem
1.1
, and it is with respect to this infinite volume ground state that Theorem
1.1
is stated. It follows immediately from the bound in (
1.5
) that this must be the only weak-* limit of any sequence of IAS finite volume ground states, and hence the unique infinite-volume, frustration-free ground state.

Then the strategy for proving Theorem
1.1
is as follows. For any normalized
\Psi(f)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\Lambda_{N}^{(m)}}
and fixed observable
A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathring{\Lambda}_{K}^{(m)}}
, we apply Lemma
2.2
and bound

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\Psi(f),A\Psi(f)\right\rangle-\omega^{(m)}(A)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\Psi(f),A\Psi(f)\right\rangle-\omega_{N}^{{\rm bulk},(m)}(A)\right|+\left|\omega_{N}^{{\rm bulk},(m)}(A)-\omega^{(m)}(A)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int d\rho_{\Lambda_{N}^{(m)}}|f|^{2}[\omega_{N}^{(m)}(A;\Omega^{\partial\Lambda_{N}})-\omega^{\mathrm{bulk},(m)}_{N}(A)]\right|

\displaystyle\;+\left|\omega_{N}^{{\rm bulk},(m)}(A)-\omega^{(m)}(A)\right|

(2.17)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega^{\partial\Lambda_{N}}}\left|\omega_{N}^{(m)}(A;\Omega^{\partial\Lambda_{N}})-\omega^{\mathrm{bulk},(m)}_{N}(A)\right|

Recalling that

is the

defined as in (

and (

(2.19)

\displaystyle\Phi_{N,K}^{(m)}({\bf\Omega})

\displaystyle=2^{-|{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda_{N,K}^{(m)}}|}\int d\Omega^{\Lambda_{N,K}^{(m)}\setminus\partial\Lambda_{N,K}}\prod_{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda_{N,K}^{(m)}}}(1-\Omega_{x}\cdot\Omega_{y})

(2.20)

\displaystyle\Phi_{N,K}^{{\rm bulk},(m)}({\bf\Omega})

\displaystyle=2^{-|{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda_{N,K}^{(m)}}|}\int d\Omega^{\Lambda_{N,K}^{(m)}\setminus\partial\mathring{\Lambda}_{K}}\prod_{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda_{N,K}^{(m)}}}(1-\Omega_{x}\cdot\Omega_{y})\,.

The desired bounds on (

We make two comments about this approach. First, any sequence of ground states could have been used in place of

in (

We conclude this section by proving a result that produces bounds on (

(3.14)

\Phi_{N,K}^{(0)}({\bf\Omega})=2^{-|{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda_{N,K}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{hard core}\\

. Since

Thus, in order to prove the desired bounds on (

in Section

4.
The Kotecký-Preiss-Ueltschi Condition for Cluster Expansion Convergence

Theorem
1.1
will be a consequence of estimating the logarithm differences from Lemma
2.3
that arise from (
3.7
) using the expansion in Lemma
3.1
as well as their decorated square lattice model counterparts. These logarithms can be written explicitly as cluster expansions so long as a summability criterion on the polymer sets is satisfied. In this section, we review the convergence criterion due to Ueltschi
[
42
]
which generalizes the convergence criterion for polymers with a hard core interaction from Kotecký and Preiss
[
29
]
to polymer sets with a soft core interaction, and we refer to this more general condition as the Kotecký-Preiss-Ueltschi (KPU) criterion. As will be shown in Section
7
, the ground states for the AKLT model on the decorated square lattice have a soft core polymer representation, so this more general setting is needed. In addition, we also state a consequence of the convergence criterion, Corollary
4.2
below, that will be vital in our proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be an nonempty, finite set of polymers, and
W:{\mathcal{P}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a weight function on
{\mathcal{P}}
. Assume that
{\mathcal{P}}
is endowed with a polymer interaction
\delta:{\mathcal{P}}\times{\mathcal{P}}\to[0,1]
, meaning that

\delta(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=\delta(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma),\qquad\delta(\gamma,\gamma)=0\qquad\forall\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}.

The interaction is
hard core
if
\delta
only takes values in
\{0,1\}
, and
soft core
otherwise. The
partition function
associated to
{\mathcal{P}},W
and
\delta
is defined by

(4.1)

\Xi({\mathcal{P}},W,\delta)=\sum_{\vec{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}}W(\vec{\gamma})\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\delta(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}),\qquad W(\vec{\gamma})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}W(\gamma_{i})\,.

To connect this to the polymer representation for the AKLT model on the hexagonal lattice from Section
3.2
, fix
N>K\geq 1
and recall the weight function
W_{m}
from (
3.6
) and polymer interaction
\delta
from (
3.9
). Then,
\Phi_{N,K}^{(m)}
and
Z_{N}^{(m)}
can be written as

\Phi_{N,K}^{(m)}=2^{-(m+1)|{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda_{N,K}}|}\Xi({\mathcal{P}}_{N,K},W_{m},\delta),\qquad Z_{N}^{(m)}=2^{-(m+1)|{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda_{N}}|}\Xi({\mathcal{P}}_{N,0},W_{m},\delta)

and analogously for
\Phi_{N,K}^{\rm bulk,(m)}
and
Z_{N}^{\rm bulk,(m)}
using the bulk polymer sets. Here, we note that
W_{m}(\gamma)
is actually a continuous function of the boundary variables
(v_{x},u_{x}:x\in\partial\Lambda_{N,K})
. However, in Section
6
we will apply the cluster expansion pointwise, and in Section
5.2
we use the uniform bound
|W_{m}(\gamma)|\leq 3^{-(m+1)|\gamma|+1}
to verify that the convergence criterion from Theorem
4.1
below holds for all choices of the boundary variables.

For any set of polymers,
{\mathcal{P}}
, with polymer interaction,
\delta
, a nonempty subset
C=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}\subset{\mathcal{P}}
is called a
cluster
if it cannot be partitioned into two nonempty sets
C_{1},\,C_{2}
such that

(4.2)

\zeta(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=0\quad\forall\gamma_{1}\in C_{1},\,\gamma_{2}\in C_{2}\qquad\text{where}\qquad\zeta(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}):=\delta(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})-1\,.

Said differently,
C
is a cluster if the graph
G=(\{1,\ldots,n\},E)
with
E=\{(i,j):\zeta(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j})\neq 0\}
is connected. The set of all clusters is denoted
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{P}})
and, for any
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}
and
C\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{P}})
, we write
\gamma\nmid C
if
C\cup\{\gamma\}
is a cluster.

For the AKLT models considered here, every polymer can be naturally mapped to a connected subgraph of the lattice. In the hexagonal model case,
\zeta(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=0
if and only if the subgraphs associated to the polymers are disjoint. In the square model case,
\zeta(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=0
if and only if the subgraphs associated to the polymers are disjoint except for, possibly, on
\partial\Lambda_{N,K}
. In either case, a set
C=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}
being a cluster requires that the graph
G=(\cup_{i}{\mathcal{V}}_{\gamma_{i}},\cup_{i}{\mathcal{E}}_{\gamma_{i}})
is connected, and this condition is an equivalence in the hexagonal model case. We note that the notation for intersecting polymers introduced in (
3.8
) for the hexagonal AKLT model is consistent with the cluster notation above since
\gamma\nmid C
if and only if there exists
\gamma^{\prime}\in C
such that
\gamma\nmid\gamma^{\prime}
.

When satisfied, a cluster expansion criterion guarantees that the formal sum for
\log\Xi({\mathcal{P}},W)
given below, called the
cluster expansion
, is absolutely convergent and, thus, a well-defined equality of functions. Specifically,

(4.3)

\log\Xi({\mathcal{P}},W)=\sum_{C\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{P}})}W^{T}(C),\qquad W^{T}(C)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n})\in{\mathcal{P}}^{n}:\\
C=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}\end{subarray}}\varphi(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}W(\gamma_{i})\,

where,
\varphi
is the Ursell function defined by

\varphi(\gamma_{1})=1,\qquad\varphi(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G=(V_{n},E):\\
G\;\text{connected}\end{subarray}}\prod_{(i,j)\in E}\zeta(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}),\quad n\geq 2

and
V_{n}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We state the Kotechký-Preiss-Ueltschi cluster expansion convergence criterion, and point the interested reader to
[
20
, Chapter 5]
for a derivation of (
4.3
) and a simplified proof of its absolute convergence when the KPU criterion holds for a discrete polymer model.

Theorem 4.1

(KPU Criterion for Cluster Expansion Convergence
[
29
,
42
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a nonempty finite set of polymers with weight function
W
and polymer interaction
\delta:{\mathcal{P}}\to[0,1]
. Suppose there exists functions
a,b:{\mathcal{P}}\to[0,\infty)
such that

(4.4)

\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}}e^{a(\gamma^{\prime})+b(\gamma^{\prime})}|W(\gamma^{\prime})|\cdot|\zeta(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})|\leq a(\gamma),\qquad\forall\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}

where
\zeta=\delta-1
. Then the triple sum in (
4.3
) is absolutely convergent. Moreover, for any
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}
,

(4.5)

\sum_{C\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{P}}),C\nmid\gamma}|W^{T}(C)|e^{b(C)}\leq a(\gamma)\quad\text{where}\quad b(C)=\sum_{\gamma\in C}b(\gamma)\,.

The convergence of the cluster expansion would also hold if
b(\gamma)=0
for all
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}
. However, the following corollary will be particularly useful for proving Theorem
1.1
given that a nonzero
b:{\mathcal{P}}\to[0,\infty)
can be established. This is a slight modification of the bound proved in
[
29
]
.

Corollary 4.2

(
[
29
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be any nonempty, finite set of polymers with weight function
W
and polymer interaction
\delta:{\mathcal{P}}\to[0,1]
, and suppose that
a,b:{\mathcal{P}}\to[0,\infty)
are such that Theorem
4.1
holds. Then, for any nonempty
A\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}
and
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}
,

(4.6)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{P}}):\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,\,C\nmid\gamma\end{subarray}}|W^{T}(C)|\leq e^{-b(\gamma,A)}a(\gamma)

where
b(\gamma,A)=\inf\{b(C):C\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{P}}),\,C\nmid\gamma,\,C\cap A\neq\emptyset\}
.

Proof.

Since
b
is nonnegative, the desired bound follows from Theorem
4.1
as

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{P}}):\\
C\cap A\neq\emptyset,\,C\nmid\gamma\end{subarray}}|W^{T}(C)|e^{b(\gamma,A)}\leq\sum_{C\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{P}}),\,C\nmid\gamma}|W^{T}(C)|e^{b(C)}\leq a(\gamma)\,.

∎

We verify (
4.4
) in Section
5
for the (un)decorated hexagonal AKLT model. The case of the AKLT model on the decorated square lattice will be the content of Section
7
.

5.
Convergence of the Cluster Expansion for the Hexagonal Model

We once again turn to considering the AKLT model on the hexagonal lattice. Using the weight function (
3.6
) and polymer interaction (
3.9
), the first step in proving Theorem

for this model is to show that the cluster expansion (

{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}
or
{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}^{\rm bulk}
with appropriate choices of

4.4
) for
{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}
. Key to proving Theorem

is that the functions used to check this criterion are independent of the chosen

and

. To this end, define

by

(5.1)

0.56,&l=4\\
0.66,&l=5\\
0.70,&l=6\\
0.15l,&l\geq 7\\
\end{cases}

Above, the domain of

reflects the fact that
|\gamma|\geq 3
for any

Theorem 5.1

(Cluster Expansion Convergence for
{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}
)
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. Fix
N
and

such that
N-K\geq 53
and

or

, and consider

and

as defined in (

), (
3.6
) and (
3.9
). Then, for any
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}

(5.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}\end{subarray}}|W_{m}(\gamma^{\prime})|\cdot|\zeta(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})|\cdot e^{a_{m}(|\gamma^{\prime}|)+\epsilon(m+1)|\gamma^{\prime}|}\leq a_{m}(|\gamma|)\,

where
a_{m}(l)=(m+1)a(l)
and
\epsilon=0.0086
.

The approach we use to prove Theorem
5.1
is the same as the one from
[
27
]
that verified (
5.2
) held for
{\mathcal{P}}_{N,0}
with
\epsilon=0
. As such, we prove Theorem
5.1
in Section
5.2
for
K\geq 25
as this can be adapted with minor (but tedious) changes to also hold for
K=0
with
\epsilon=0.0086
. We specifically work with
{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}
in order to establish the dependence of (
1.10
) on
|\gamma^{K,(m)}|
. Since
{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}\not\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}_{N,0}

[
27

cannot be directly invoked to verify Theorem

, and certain arguments used in that work need to be modified so that they hold for the more general case.

The strategy for proving Theorem
5.1
is as follows. From (
3.6
), it is clear that for any
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}
,

|W_{m}(\gamma)|\leq 3^{-(m+1)|\gamma|+1}\qquad\forall m\geq 0.

Since
\zeta(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=1
if
\gamma\nmid\gamma^{\prime}
and
\zeta(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=0
otherwise, we restrict the sum in (
5.2
) to
\gamma\nmid\gamma^{\prime}
and drop the
\zeta
term. Then, it follows that for any
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}
,

(5.3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}\\
\gamma^{\prime}\nmid\gamma\end{subarray}}|W_{m}(\gamma^{\prime})|\cdot e^{a_{m}(|\gamma^{\prime}|)+\epsilon(m+1)|\gamma^{\prime}|}\leq\sum_{l=3}^{\infty}w_{m}(l)|{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}^{\gamma,l}|

(5.4)

w_{m}(l):=3\left(\frac{e^{a(l)+\epsilon l}}{3^{l}}\right)^{m+1}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}^{\gamma,l}:=\{\gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}:\gamma\nmid\gamma^{\prime},\;|\gamma^{\prime}|=l\}\,.

Since
w_{m}(l)/(m+1)
is decreasing in
m
, the result for all

follows from verifying

(5.5)

\sum_{l=3}^{\infty}w_{0}(l)|{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}^{\gamma,l}|\leq a(|\gamma|).

To simplify notation moving forward, we suppress the subscript above and write
w(l)=w_{0}(l)
.

One only needs to produce sufficiently tight upper bound on

to verify (
5.2
) holds. This will be achieved by considering loops and walks separately, so for any
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}
and

, let

(5.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{N,K}^{\gamma,l}

\displaystyle=\{\gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{N,K}:\gamma\nmid\gamma^{\prime},\;|\gamma^{\prime}|=l\}

(5.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{N,K}^{\gamma,l}

\displaystyle=\{\gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{N,K}:\gamma\nmid\gamma^{\prime},\;|\gamma^{\prime}|=l\}

denote the set of all walks (resp. loops) of length
l
from
{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}
that intersect
\gamma
. For convenience later on, we also denote the set of all walks (resp. loops) of length
l
by

(5.8)

\mathcal{W}_{N,K}^{l}=\{\gamma\in{\mathcal{W}}_{N,K}:|\gamma|=l\},\quad\mathcal{L}_{N,K}^{l}=\{\gamma\in{\mathcal{L}}_{N,K}:|\gamma|=l\}.

Given these definitions, one can write

(5.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=3}^{\infty}w(l)|{\mathcal{P}}_{N,K}^{\gamma,l}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq l\leq 20\\
l\;\text{even}\end{subarray}}w(l)|{\mathcal{W}}_{N,K}^{\gamma,l}|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq l\leq 20\\
l\;\text{odd}\end{subarray}}w(l)|{\mathcal{W}}_{N,K}^{\gamma,l}|+\sum_{l=6,10}w(l)|{\mathcal{L}}_{N,K}^{\gamma,l}|

\displaystyle+\sum_{12\leq l\leq 28}w(l)|{\mathcal{L}}_{N,K}^{\gamma,l}|+\sum_{l\geq 30}w(l)|{\mathcal{L}}_{N,K}^{\gamma,l}|+\sum_{l\geq 21}w(l)|{\mathcal{W}}_{N,K}^{\gamma,l}|

Appendix A
Exponential Decay of Correlations

As already discussed in the paper by Kotecký and Preiss
[
29
]
, in a suitable context, convergent cluster expansions can be used to prove exponential decay of two- and multi-point correlation functions. To prove exponential decay of correlations in the ground states of the AKLT models on the hexagonal and Lieb lattices with the cluster expansion we are using in this paper, it turns out that the convergence criterion needs to be slightly adjusted. To prove LTQO for an observable
A
supported in
\Lambda_{K}
, the basic geometry of finite volumes to study was given by the sets
\Lambda_{N,K}
, for sufficiently large
N
. To study correlation functions we will need to consider finite volumes with holes to fit the supports of each of the observables involved. This modifies the combinatorics required to check the convergence criterion for the cluster expansion. Since all of this is slightly non-standard, we provide a proof of exponential decay here.

We explicitly analyse the two-point correlations of observables that are translates of
A
and
B
, supported in balls of radius
M/2
, for
M
even, in the dual lattice, meaning there exist points
x_{A},x_{B}
in the dual lattice and balls

\Lambda_{A}:=\Lambda_{M/2,x_{A}},\quad\Lambda_{B}:=\Lambda_{M/2,x_{B}}

such that
\operatorname{supp}(A)\subset\Lambda_{A}
and
\operatorname{supp}(B)\subset\Lambda_{B}
. We use the lattice metric to define the distance between these balls:

d(\Lambda_{A},\Lambda_{B}):=\inf\{d(a,b)\>:\>a\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda_{A}},\;b\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\Lambda_{B}}\}.

Multi-point correlation functions can be estimated by iterating the two-point estimate.

It will be useful to have a notation for the smallest loops enclosing
\Lambda_{A}
and
\Lambda_{B}
, namely
\gamma^{A}:=\partial\mathring{\Lambda}_{A},
and
\gamma^{B}:=\partial\mathring{\Lambda}_{B}
.

Theorem A.1
.

be observables supported in balls of radius

,

and
