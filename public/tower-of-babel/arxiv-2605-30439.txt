Equivariant irrationality of very general symmetric Verra fourfolds
Aideen Fay

Abstract
. We prove that a very general complex symmetric Verra fourfold is not ℤ / 2 \mathbb{Z}/2 -birational to ℙ 4 \mathbb{P}^{4} , using the theory of atoms introduced by Katzarkov–Kontsevich–Pantev–Yu ( 14 ) in the equivariant setting as in Cavenaghi–Katzarkov–Kontsevich ( 6 ) .



1.
Introduction

In their seminal work, Katzarkov–Kontsevich–Pantev–Yu
(
14
)
introduce the
theory of atoms
, which combines Hodge theory and Gromov–Witten invariants to define birationally invariant properties of smooth complex projective varieties. This is underpinned by Iritani’s blowup formula
(
13
, Theorem 1.1)
, which dictates the additive decomposition of quantum
D
-modules under blowups along smooth centres. This approach has already settled long-standing conjectures on the irrationality of certain Fano varieties—most recently for cubic
14
;
10
and Gushel–Mukai
4
fourfolds.

Progress to date leaves the Verra fourfold as the last of the classical Fano fourfolds of K3 type with index
>1

5
, whose rationality remains unknown. Realized as a double cover

\pi\colon X\to\mathbf{P}^{2}\times\mathbf{P}^{2}

branched along a smooth divisor
B
of bidegree
(2,2)
, a Verra fourfold
X
naturally admits two quadric fibrations over
\mathbf{P}^{2}
. The discriminant locus of each fibration is a plane sextic curve whose double cover, when smooth, is a
degree-two K3 surface
(
15
)
. This explicit K3 geometry presents a unique challenge to the birational invariants introduced above, which remain inconclusive here
3
.

If we shift perspective, we notice that the branch locus
B
of a
symmetric
Verra remains invariant under swapping of the
\mathbf{P}^{2}\times\mathbf{P}^{2}
factors. In this
case the factor-swap lifts to an automorphism
\epsilon:X\longrightarrow X
, and hence defines an action of the group
G=\mathbb{Z}/2=\langle\epsilon\rangle
on the cohomology of
X
.

This observation motivates us to adopt the
G
-equivariant obstructions introduced by Cavenaghi–Katzarkov–Kontsevich
(
6
)
to prove
\mathbb{Z}/2
-irrationality of the very general symmetric Verra fourfold.

Definition 1.1

(
\mathbb{Z}/2
-rationality)
.

Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
with an involution

\iota:Y\to Y
. The pair
(Y,\iota)
is
\mathbb{Z}/2
-rational
if there is a
birational map
\varphi:Y\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}
and a regular involution

\sigma:\mathbb{P}^{n}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
\sigma=\varphi\circ\iota\circ\varphi^{-1}
on the Zariski dense open subset where
\varphi^{-1}
is defined. Otherwise
(Y,\iota)
is
\mathbb{Z}/2
-irrational
.

Theorem

6.4
.

Let
X
be a very general symmetric Verra fourfold, and let
\epsilon
be its factor-swap involution. Then
(X,\epsilon)
is
\mathbb{Z}/2
-irrational. Equivalently, for every regular involution
\iota\in\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{4})
, the pair

(X,\epsilon)
is not
\mathbb{Z}/2
-birational to
(\mathbb{P}^{4},\iota)
.

Remark 1.2
.

The same argument applies if
\mathbb{P}^{4}
is replaced by any smooth projective
fourfold
P
with a regular
\mathbb{Z}/2
-action and no
cohomology classes of Hochschild degree 2.
For instance, one may take
P=\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
However, the argument does not apply to an arbitrary smooth rational fourfold. Thus the
involution
\iota
in the statement must be regular and not just rational.

In pursuit of this, we verify the
G
-equivariance of Iritani’s blowup decomposition (
Theorem

5.2
), and introduce an evaluated
G
-equivariant version of these obstructions, following Guéré’s work in the non-equivariant setting
(
10
)
. Specifically, we define in this paper what very general
means for symmetric Verra fourfolds. Indeed, the
natural condition of being Hodge-general is actually
empty (see
Corollary

3.5
), but a more refined condition
that we call
\mathbb{Z}/2
-Hodge generality is not (see

Proposition

4.4
). We refer to
Section

4
for the precise definitions.

2.
Evaluated Hodge invariants

We recall the notation used throughout the paper. We always work with smooth projective varieties over
\mathbb{C}
. The notation and definitions follow Guéré’s evaluated formulation
(
10
)
of the Hodge-theoretic invariants introduced in
(
14
)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Y
be a smooth projective complex variety. A class

\gamma\in H^{2p}(Y,\mathbb{Q})

is a
rational Hodge class
if its complexification lies in
H^{p,p}(Y)
. We write

H^{*}(Y,\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{Hdg}}:=\bigoplus_{p}(H^{2p}(Y,\mathbb{Q})\cap H^{p,p}(Y)).

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, set

H^{(k)}(Y):=\bigoplus_{p-q=k}H^{p,q}(Y)\subset H^{*}(Y,\mathbb{C}).

We call
H^{(k)}(Y)
the Hochschild-degree-
k
part of the Hodge decomposition.

Let
\operatorname{NE}(Y)\subset H_{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
be the monoid of effective curve classes in
Y
. For
d\in\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(Y)
, we associate the formal Novikov variable
Q^{d}\in\mathbb{Q}[\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(Y)]
which records the curve class of stable maps
contributing to a Gromov–Witten invariant. It is assigned the grading
\deg Q^{d}=2\,c_{1}(Y)\cdot d
.

Fix a homogeneous basis
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=0}^{m}
of the rational Hodge classes

H^{*}(Y,\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{Hdg}}
. Let
\{t^{i}\}_{i=0}^{m}
be formal variables, graded by
\deg t^{i}=2-\deg\alpha_{i}
. A Hodge
parameter is then
\tau=\sum_{i}t^{i}\alpha_{i}
. We use the completed Novikov–Hodge ring

\widehat{R}(Y,K):=K[q^{\prime\pm 1}]\llbracket\mathrm{NE}(Y)\rrbracket\llbracket t^{0},\dots,t^{m}\rrbracket,

where
K
is a number field,
q^{\prime}
is a formal Laurent variable coming from Iritani’s blowup formula, and the completion is graded; see

(
13
, Section 2.2)
. The subring
R(Y,K)\subset\widehat{R}(Y,K)
consists of series with no non-zero powers of
q^{\prime}
.

The big quantum product is denoted by
\star_{\tau}
. Let

\operatorname{Eu}_{\tau}
be the Euler vector field defined as

\operatorname{Eu}_{\tau}:=c_{1}(Y)+\sum_{i=0}^{m}\left(1-\frac{\deg\alpha_{i}}{2}\right)t^{i}\alpha_{i}.

We then define the operator
\kappa_{\tau}
by

\kappa_{\tau}:=\operatorname{Eu}_{\tau}\star_{\tau}(-).

At
\tau=0
, this specializes to the small quantum multiplication operator

\kappa_{0}=c_{1}(Y)\star_{0}(-).

The operator
\kappa_{\tau}
preserves rational Hodge classes and Hochschild
degree
(
10
, Proposition 14)
.

An evaluation, in the sense of
(
10
, Definition 20)
,
is a continuous degree-preserving homomorphism

\operatorname{ev}:\widehat{R}(Y,K)\longrightarrow S_{K}

where
S_{K}=F_{K}[b^{\pm 1}]
, and
F_{K}
is the Levi–Civita field over
K
.

Applying
\operatorname{ev}
to the coefficients of

\kappa_{\tau}
gives an
S_{K}
–linear endomorphism of
H^{*}(Y,K)\otimes_{K}S_{K}
.
Passing, if necessary, to a graded field extension
L\supseteq S_{K}
splitting
the characteristic polynomial of
\operatorname{ev}(\kappa_{\tau})
, we regard the evaluated
operator
\operatorname{ev}(\kappa_{\tau})
as an endomorphism of
H^{*}(Y,K)\otimes_{K}L
.

Definition 2.2

(Evaluated generalized eigenspaces and Hochschild rank)
.

Let
\lambda\in L
. The evaluated generalized eigenspace is

E^{Y}_{\operatorname{ev},\lambda}:=\ker\bigl(\operatorname{ev}(\kappa_{\tau})-\lambda\bigr)^{N},\qquad N\gg 0,

with Hodge-class part

\mathcal{H}^{Y}_{\operatorname{ev},\lambda}:=E^{Y}_{\operatorname{ev},\lambda}\cap\bigl(H^{*}(Y,\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{Hdg}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}L\bigr).

We set

\rho^{Y}_{\operatorname{ev},\lambda}:=\operatorname{rk}_{L}\mathcal{H}^{Y}_{\operatorname{ev},\lambda},

and define the Hochschild-degree-two rank

\nu^{Y}_{\operatorname{ev},\lambda}:=\operatorname{rk}_{L\otimes\mathbb{C}}\Bigl((E^{Y}_{\operatorname{ev},\lambda}\otimes\mathbb{C})\cap\bigl(H^{(2)}(Y)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}(L\otimes\mathbb{C})\bigr)\Bigr).

Now suppose a finite group
G
acts algebraically on
Y
. An evaluation is

G
-invariant
if

\operatorname{ev}(Q^{g_{*}d})=\operatorname{ev}(Q^{d})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{ev}(g^{*}\tau)=\operatorname{ev}(\tau)

for every
d\in\operatorname{NE}(Y)
and every
g\in G
. For small quantum cohomology all
Hodge variables are evaluated at zero, so the second condition is automatic.

Definition 2.3

(Invariant rational Hodge rank)
.

When
E^{Y}_{\operatorname{ev},\lambda}
is
G
-stable, define

\rho^{G,Y}_{\operatorname{ev},\lambda}:=\operatorname{rk}_{L}\bigl(\mathcal{H}^{Y}_{\operatorname{ev},\lambda}\bigr)^{G}.

Thus
\rho^{G}
is the rank of the
G
-fixed rational Hodge classes in the
evaluated generalized eigenspace.

Remark 2.4
.

For any
G
-invariant evaluation map
\operatorname{ev}
, the endomorphism
ev(\kappa_{\tau})
is
automatically
G
-equivariant. This is because the
virtual fundamental cycle used in the definition of
Gromov–Witten invariants is
G
-invariant.
Indeed, this follows from the
G
-action on the moduli
spaces of stable maps to
Y
and the
G
-equivariance
of the perfect obstruction theory defining the virtual
fundamental cycle.

3.
Symmetric Verra Fourfolds

Following
8
, we realize the Verra fourfold
X
as a hypersurface in the smooth toric fivefold
\mathcal{F}:=\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{2}\times\mathbf{P}^{2}}(1,1))
. The total space
\mathcal{F}
is given by the Cox-coordinate quotient

\mathcal{F}=\bigl((\mathbb{C}^{3}\setminus\{0\})\times(\mathbb{C}^{3}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{C}\bigr)/(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}

with coordinates
(x_{i},y_{i},w)
and weight matrix

(1)

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc|c}x_{0}&x_{1}&x_{2}&y_{0}&y_{1}&y_{2}&w&\\
\hline\cr 1&1&1&0&0&0&1&L\\
0&0&0&1&1&1&1&M\end{array}

For
f_{2,2}\in H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{2}\times\mathbf{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}(2,2))
, the equation

(2)

w^{2}=f_{2,2}(x,y)

defines a hypersurface

X\in|\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}(2,2)|
, which is smooth for general
f_{2,2}
. Let

\varpi:\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}

be the bundle projection, and set

\pi:=\varpi|_{X}:X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}.

Then
\pi
is the double cover branched along

B=\{f_{2,2}=0\}\in|\mathcal{O}(2,2)|.

Composing
\pi
with the two projections from

\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
gives two maps

p_{1}:X\to\mathbb{P}^{2},\qquad p_{2}:X\to\mathbb{P}^{2},

which are the two quadric surface fibrations on
X
.

Let
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(2,2))^{+}
be the
+1
-eigenspace for the action induced by
the factor-swap
(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)
on

H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}(2,2))
. The corresponding symmetric linear subsystem is

|\mathcal{O}(2,2)|^{+}:=\mathbb{P}\bigl(H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}(2,2))^{+}\bigr).

If
f_{2,2}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(2,2))^{+}
, then the factor-swap lifts to a regular
automorphism

\epsilon:X\longrightarrow X,\qquad(x,y,w)\longmapsto(y,x,w).

This linear system has no base points. Hence, Bertini’s theorem
(
11
, Corollary III.10.9)
gives a nonempty open locus of
smooth symmetric branch divisors. After trivializing
\mathcal{O}(1,1)
, Equation (
2
) is locally defined as
u^{2}=f_{2,2}(x,y)
. By the Jacobian criterion,
X
is singular exactly over the singular locus of
B
. Therefore, in characteristic zero,
X
is smooth if and only if
B
is smooth. Consequently, we restrict our attention to the open symmetric locus where
B
is smooth.

Remark 3.1
.

In contrast, the discriminant sextics
\Delta_{i}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
, which define the singular loci of the quadric surface fibrations
p_{i}
, can acquire ordinary double points even when
X
remains smooth; see
(
2
, Proposition 1.2)
.

3.1.
Ambient cohomology

Let
h_{1},h_{2}
be the hyperplane classes on the two factors of

\mathbf{P}^{2}\times\mathbf{P}^{2}
, and set
H_{1}:=\pi^{*}h_{1}=c_{1}(L)|_{X}
and
H_{2}:=\pi^{*}h_{2}=c_{1}(M)|_{X}
. By adjunction,
X
is a Fano fourfold of index two with

-K_{X}=2(H_{1}+H_{2}).

By the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem, we have

H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}H_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}H_{2}.

Consider now the ambient part
A\subset H^{*}(X)
, defined as the image of
pullback from the base

A:=\operatorname{Im}\!\left(\pi^{*}:H^{*}(\mathbf{P}^{2}\times\mathbf{P}^{2},\mathbb{Q})\to H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q})\right).

Because the composition
\pi_{*}\circ\pi^{*}=2\cdot\mathrm{id}
, the pullback is injective. Thus the relations
h_{1}^{3}=h_{2}^{3}=0
pull back directly to
H_{1}^{3}=H_{2}^{3}=0
on
X
, giving the presentation

A=\mathbb{Q}[H_{1},H_{2}]/(H_{1}^{3},H_{2}^{3}).

The only top intersection needed below is

\int_{X}H_{1}^{2}H_{2}^{2}=\int_{X}\pi^{*}(h_{1}^{2}h_{2}^{2})=2\int_{\mathbf{P}^{2}\times\mathbf{P}^{2}}h_{1}^{2}h_{2}^{2}=2.

Let
d_{i}\in NE(X)
be the class of a line of
H_{i}
-degree one. Thus
H_{i}\cdot d_{j}=\delta_{ij}
. Equivalently,
d_{1}
is represented by a line in a general fibre of
p_{2}
, and

d_{2}
is represented by a line in a general fibre of
p_{1}
. Therefore, for
H:=H_{1}+H_{2}
, we have

H\cdot d_{i}=1,\qquad c_{1}(X)\cdot d_{i}=2.

3.2.
Hodge numbers

The Hodge numbers of the Verra fourfold were computed by
15
, with the corrected value of
h^{2,2}
later provided by
9
. The associated Hodge diamond is:

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}&&&&1&&&&\\
&&&0&&0&&&\\
&&0&&2&&0&&\\
&0&&0&&0&&0&\\
0&&1&&22&&1&&0\\
&0&&0&&0&&0&\\
&&0&&2&&0&&\\
&&&0&&0&&&\\
&&&&1&&&&\end{array}

The middle cohomology of
X
satisfies
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})=24
with
h^{3,1}=1
, while all odd cohomology groups vanish. Let the 3-dimensional ambient middle cohomology be

A_{\mathrm{mid}}:=A\cap H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})=\langle H_{1}^{2},\;H_{1}H_{2},\;H_{2}^{2}\rangle.

We define the primitive cohomology
V(X)
as the orthogonal complement of
A_{\mathrm{mid}}
with respect to the Poincaré pairing

H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})=A_{\mathrm{mid}}\oplus V(X),\qquad\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}V(X)=21.

Remark 3.2

(On special loci)
.

While
V(X)
and the transcendental cohomology coincide generically, the space
H^{2,2}(X)
can acquire additional algebraic classes on special loci, which causes the transcendental cohomology to become a proper subspace of
V(X)
. This indeed happens for symmetric Verra fourfolds.

The complexified primitive cohomology decomposes as

V(X)_{\mathbb{C}}=H^{3,1}(X)\oplus V^{2,2}(X)\oplus H^{1,3}(X),

where

V^{2,2}(X):=V(X)_{\mathbb{C}}\cap H^{2,2}(X).

The dimensions are

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{3,1}(X)=1,\qquad\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V^{2,2}(X)=19,\qquad\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{1,3}(X)=1.

The involution acts on ambient classes by

\epsilon^{*}H_{1}=H_{2},\qquad\epsilon^{*}H_{2}=H_{1}.

Thus

A=A^{+}\oplus A^{-}.

In middle degree, the invariant
A^{+}_{mid}
and anti-invariant
A^{-}_{mid}
parts have the respective bases

A_{\mathrm{mid}}^{+}=\langle H_{1}^{2}+H_{2}^{2},\;H_{1}H_{2}\rangle,\qquad A_{\mathrm{mid}}^{-}=\langle H_{1}^{2}-H_{2}^{2}\rangle.

In particular,

\dim A_{\mathrm{mid}}^{+}=2,\qquad\dim A_{\mathrm{mid}}^{-}=1.

Since
\epsilon^{*}
preserves the Poincaré pairing and preserves

A_{\mathrm{mid}}
, it also preserves
V(X)=A_{\mathrm{mid}}^{\perp}
. We write the induced eigenspace decomposition as

V(X)=V^{+}(X)\oplus V^{-}(X).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a symmetric Verra fourfold with the involution
\epsilon
. Then

\dim H^{4}(X)^{+}=16,\qquad\dim H^{4}(X)^{-}=8.

Consequently,

\dim V^{+}(X)=14,\qquad\dim V^{-}(X)=7.

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
be the diagonal. Because the lifted involution
\epsilon
fixes the fibre coordinate
w
, its fixed locus
X^{\epsilon}
is the double cover of
\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}
branched along
C_{4}:=B\cap\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}
. The curve
C_{4}
is a plane quartic, smooth by

Proposition

A.1
. Hence
g(C_{4})=3
, and therefore
\chi(X^{\epsilon})=2\chi(\mathbb{P}^{2})-\chi(C_{4})=10
.

By the topological Lefschetz fixed-point theorem,

\sum_{i=0}^{8}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\epsilon^{*}\big|H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})\bigr)=\chi(X^{\epsilon})=10.

The odd cohomology of
X
vanishes. The involution
\epsilon^{*}
acts trivially on
H^{0}(X)
and
H^{8}(X)
. It swaps the generators of
H^{2}(X)
and
H^{6}(X)
which gives a trace of zero in degrees
2
and
6
. The Lefschetz formula then reduces to

1+\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\epsilon^{*}\bigm|H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})\bigr)+1=10,

which implies
\operatorname{Tr}(\epsilon^{*}\mid H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q}))=8
.

Since the total dimension is
\dim H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})=24
, the trace and dimension constraints yield the eigenspace dimensions
\dim H^{4}(X)^{+}=16
and
\dim H^{4}(X)^{-}=8
. Finally, subtracting
\dim A_{\mathrm{mid}}^{+}=2
and
\dim A_{\mathrm{mid}}^{-}=1
gives the stated dimensions of
V^{+}(X)
and
V^{-}(X)
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

The involution
\epsilon^{*}
acts trivially on
H^{3,1}(X)
.

Proof.

A generator of
H^{3,1}(X)
is represented by the residue of a local toric 5-form on
\mathcal{F}

\operatorname{Res}_{X}\left(\frac{dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}\wedge dy_{1}\wedge dy_{2}\wedge dw}{(w^{2}-f(x,y))^{2}}\right).

The involution leaves the denominator invariant by the symmetry of
Eq.

2
. The numerator is also fixed by anti-commutativity of the wedge product—an even number of permutations of the differentials yields
(-1)^{2\cdot 2}=+1
. Hence, the residue generator is fixed.
∎

Corollary 3.5

(Anti-invariant Hodge classes)
.

The eigenspace
V^{-}(X)
is
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Moreover,

H^{3,1}(X)\oplus H^{1,3}(X)\subset V^{+}(X)_{\mathbb{C}},

and hence

V^{-}(X)_{\mathbb{C}}\subset H^{2,2}(X).

Thus every class in
V^{-}(X)
is a rational Hodge class.

Proof.

Since
\epsilon^{*}
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and preserves
V(X)
, the
eigenspaces

V^{\pm}(X)=\ker(\epsilon^{*}\mp 1)\cap V(X)

are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. By
Lemma

3.4
,
H^{3,1}(X)\subset V^{+}(X)_{\mathbb{C}}
. Since
\epsilon
is algebraic, it commutes with complex conjugation, so
H^{1,3}(X)\subset V^{+}(X)_{\mathbb{C}}
. The only Hodge types in
V(X)
are
(3,1)
,
(2,2)
, and
(1,3)
. Therefore
V^{-}(X)_{\mathbb{C}}
has only type
(2,2)
, so that the sub-Hodge structure
V^{-}(X)
contains only rational Hodge classes.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Ordinary Hodge-generality is the condition that there
are no Hodge classes in the primitive cohomology
V(X)
.
Therefore, this condition always fails for symmetric Verra
fourfolds, due at least to the 7 independent Hodge
classes in the anti-invariant part.
This motivates our equivariant definition of Hodge
generality.

Definition 3.7

(Equivariant Hodge-generality)
.

A symmetric Verra fourfold
X
is called
\mathbb{Z}/2
-Hodge-general if

V^{+}(X)\cap H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{Hdg}}=0.

Equivalently,
V^{+}(X)_{\mathbb{C}}\cap H^{2,2}(X)
contains no nonzero rational Hodge classes.

4.
Very general symmetric Verra fourfolds

In this section we use a Noether-Lefschetz argument to prove that a very general symmetric Verra fourfold is
\mathbb{Z}/2
-Hodge-general.

Proposition 4.1

(Dimension of the symmetric Verra moduli space)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sym}}
be
the moduli space of smooth symmetric Verra fourfolds. Then
\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sym}}=12
.

Proof.

Let
V:=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(2))
. Then
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}(2,2))^{+}\cong\operatorname{Sym}^{2}V
.
Since
\dim V=6
, the symmetric branch divisors form an open subset of
\mathbb{P}(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}V)\cong\mathbb{P}^{20}
.
Quotienting by the diagonal action of
\operatorname{PGL}_{3}
yields
\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sym}}=12
.
∎

Proposition 4.2

(Dimension of the invariant period domain)
.

Let
D^{+}
be the period domain of the invariant primitive variation
V^{+}(X)
. Then
\dim D^{+}=12
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda^{+}
be a reference lattice for
V^{+}(X)
equipped with the Poincaré pairing. By
Lemmas

3.3
and
3.4
,
V^{+}(X)
has
Hodge numbers

h^{3,1}=1,\qquad h^{2,2}=12,\qquad h^{1,3}=1.

The corresponding invariant period domain is

D^{+}:=\Bigl\{[\omega]\in\mathbb{P}(\Lambda^{+}_{\mathbb{C}})\ \Bigm|\ (\omega,\omega)=0,\;(\omega,\overline{\omega})>0\Bigr\}.

Here
[\omega]
records the line
H^{3,1}
. Since

\dim\Lambda^{+}_{\mathbb{C}}=14
, the ambient projective space is

\mathbb{P}(\Lambda^{+}_{\mathbb{C}})\cong\mathbb{P}^{13}
. The condition
(\omega,\omega)=0
cuts out a quadric hypersurface of complex dimension
12
. The Hodge–Riemann positivity condition is an open real condition on this quadric, so it does not change the complex dimension.
Therefore
\dim D^{+}=12
.

∎

Let
\Gamma^{+}
denote the monodromy group acting on
\Lambda^{+}
.

Proposition 4.3

(Period map dominance)
.

The invariant period map

\Phi^{+}:\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sym}}\longrightarrow\Gamma^{+}\backslash D^{+}

is dominant. Equivalently, its differential has full rank
12
at a general point.
In particular,
\Phi^{+}
is locally open at a general point.

Proof.

At a smooth point
[X]
, Griffiths transversality implies the differential of the period map takes the form

d\Phi^{+}_{[X]}\colon T_{[X]}\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sym}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(H^{3,1}(V^{+}(X)),H^{2,2}(V^{+}(X))\bigr).

Since
h^{3,1}(V^{+}(X))=1
, the target is naturally identified with
H^{2,2}(V^{+}(X))
, which has dimension
12
.

By
Proposition

B.1
, there exists a smooth symmetric branch
polynomial
f_{0}
for which this differential has rank
12
. Because maximal
rank is an open condition,
d\Phi^{+}
has rank
12
at a general point of

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sym}}
. Finally, since

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sym}}=\dim D^{+}=12

(
Propositions

4.1
and
4.2
), the differential is invertible at a general
point. Hence
\Phi^{+}
has open image near such a point, and therefore

\Phi^{+}
is dominant.
∎

Proposition 4.4

(Very general
\mathbb{Z}/2
-Hodge-generality)
.

A very general symmetric Verra fourfold is
\mathbb{Z}/2
-Hodge-general.

Proof.

By
Proposition

4.3
, the invariant period map
\Phi^{+}
is locally open at a general point. In a small neighborhood in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sym}}
, we identify the invariant rational cohomology of the fibers with a fixed reference lattice
\Lambda^{+}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. A nonzero class

is of Hodge type
(2,2)
if and only if it is orthogonal to the
(3,1)
-period. This condition cuts out a proper hyperplane section

D^{+}_{\gamma}:=\bigl\{[\omega]\in D^{+}\bigm|(\gamma,\omega)=0\bigr\}\subsetneq D^{+}.

Because
\Phi^{+}
is locally open, its image is not contained in

. Thus, the Noether–Lefschetz locus

is a proper analytic subvariety of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sym}}
.

Taking the union over all nonzero classes in the countable lattice
\Lambda^{+}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, the locus of fourfolds that acquire an invariant rational Hodge class is a countable union of proper analytic subvarieties. By definition, a very general

avoids this union. Hence

Thus

is
\mathbb{Z}/2
-Hodge-general.
∎

Corollary 4.5

(Hodge classes on the very general symmetric locus)
.

For a very general symmetric Verra fourfold

,

In particular, the only non-ambient primitive rational Hodge classes are the

Proof.

By
Corollary

3.5
,
V^{-}(X)
consists of rational Hodge

,

contains no

∎

5.
The equivariant fourfold obstruction

In this section we verify the equivariance of Iritani’s blowup decomposition
and we state the equivariant fourfold irrationality obstruction used in the proof of
Theorem
6.4
. Here,
G
will denote a finite group
acting on a smooth projective variety
Y
, that we will call a
G
-variety.

Lemma 5.1

(
G
-stability of evaluated eigenspaces)
.

Let
Y
be a smooth projective
G
-variety and
\operatorname{ev}
a
G
-invariant evaluation.
Then
\operatorname{ev}(\kappa_{\tau})
commutes with the
G
-action. In particular every
evaluated generalized eigenspace
E^{Y}_{\operatorname{ev},\lambda}
is
G
-stable.

Proof.

By functoriality of genus-zero Gromov–Witten invariants under automorphisms,

g^{*}(a\star_{\tau}b)=g^{*}a\star_{g^{*}\tau}g^{*}b
for every
g\in G
. Given that

g^{*}c_{1}(Y)=c_{1}(Y)
and
g^{*}
respects the cohomology degree, we have
g^{*}\mathrm{Eu}_{\tau}=\mathrm{Eu}_{g^{*}\tau}
. Hence

g^{*}\circ\kappa_{\tau}=\kappa_{g^{*}\tau}\circ g^{*}
. Applying
\operatorname{ev}
and using its

G
-invariance, which gives
\operatorname{ev}(\kappa_{g^{*}\tau})=\operatorname{ev}(\kappa_{\tau})
, we obtain

g^{*}\circ\operatorname{ev}(\kappa_{\tau})=\operatorname{ev}(\kappa_{\tau})\circ g^{*}
. Thus
g
preserves each
generalized eigenspace
E^{Y}_{\operatorname{ev},\lambda}
.
∎

Theorem 5.2

(

-equivariant Iritani blowup decomposition)
.

Let

-variety, let

-invariant centre of codimension

Iritani’s proof relies on a geometric construction. Let

\mathbb{C}^{*}

The continuous Fourier transforms is introduced directly after

in

(Evaluated

.

6.
Verra Small Quantum Cohomology

In this section we provide the small quantum multiplication matrix restricted to the ambient part
for the Verra fourfold. Rather than computing it directly using mirror theorems, as in
14
, or enumerative geometry, as in
4
;
3
, we take a short cut, deriving it via the quantum differential operator. The full method and computation are provided in Appendix
Quantum Differential Equation—Small Quantum Cohomology Computation
.

Let
H:=H_{1}+H_{2}
, and let
d_{1},d_{2}\in NE(X)
be the bidegree-one line
classes defined in
Section

3.1
. The factor-swap involution exchanges
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, and therefore exchanges

d_{1}
and
d_{2}
. Write

Q_{1}:=Q^{d_{1}},\qquad Q_{2}:=Q^{d_{2}}.

Since
c_{1}(X)\cdot d_{i}=2
, the Novikov variables
Q_{i}
have degree
4
. We restrict to the
G
-fixed Novikov diagonal

Q_{1}=Q_{2}=q.

This is the natural locus in this equivariant setting as every
G
-invariant evaluation must identify
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
.

We now choose the specific evaluations for
X
. Let

F:=\mathbb{Q}((a^{\mathbb{Q}})),\qquad S^{*}:=F[b^{\pm 1}],\qquad\deg b=1.

For
0\neq\zeta\in F
with
|\zeta|<1
, define the diagonal small
evaluation

\operatorname{ev}_{\zeta}(Q_{1})=\operatorname{ev}_{\zeta}(Q_{2})=\zeta b^{4},\qquad\operatorname{ev}_{\zeta}(t^{i})=0\quad\text{for all Hodge variables }t^{i}.

This is degree-preserving and
G
-invariant, since
\deg q=4

and the exchanged variables
Q_{1},Q_{2}
are sent to the same element.

As discussed in
Section

3
, the involution decomposes the ambient cohomology
A
as
A=A^{+}\oplus A^{-}
. It is closed under quantum multiplication by
H

(
12
, Corollary 2.5)
. Hence, on the diagonal we obtain an endomorphism

m_{H}(q):=H\star_{q}(-)|_{A}.

Since
H
is invariant under the factor-swap,
m_{H}(q)
preserves
A^{+}
and

A^{-}
. We write
M_{H}(q)
as the matrix of
m_{H}(q)
in the ordered basis for this decomposition.

We compute
M_{H}(q)
using the quantum period
G_{X}(t)
of
X
, which is a
generating function for one-point genus-zero descendant Gromov–Witten
invariants along the anticanonical direction. We refer the reader to
(
7
)
for a full discussion of quantum periods.

For the Verra fourfold deformation family, the quantum period was computed by
Coates–Galkin–Kasprzyk–Strangeway
(
8
)
as

(3)

G_{X}(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=\ell}^{\infty}\frac{(2m)!}{(\ell!)^{3}m!((m-\ell)!)^{3}}t^{2m}.

Only even powers of
t
occur. Since the primitive curve classes on the
anticanonical diagonal have anticanonical degree
2
, we set
q=t^{2}
, and write the same period in the variable
q
as

G_{X}(q)=1+4q+15q^{2}+\frac{280}{9}q^{3}+\frac{6055}{144}q^{4}+\cdots.

The small quantum connection for multiplication by
H
gives the first-order
system

Dy=M_{H}(q)y,\qquad D=q\frac{d}{dq}.

The small
J
-function is a solution of this quantum connection, and the
quantum period

is the scalar component selected by the unit class; see

(
12
, Section 2)
. Applying the cyclic-vector algorithm to this same
first-order system eliminates the other components and gives a scalar differential
operator

L_{X}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}p_{k}(q)\,D^{k},

where

is the order of the operator and the
p_{k}(q)
are polynomials.
Therefore the same scalar component
G_{X}(q)
is annihilated by
L_{X}

(4)

L_{X}G_{X}(q)=0,\qquad G_{X}(0)=1.

Since
1\in A^{+}
and
m_{H}(q)
preserves
A^{+}
, the cyclic subspace generated
by the unit class lies entirely in
A^{+}
. Hence the scalar operator
L_{X}
obtained
from the cyclic-vector algorithm depends only on
M_{+}(q)
. We can therefore
match coefficients against the known expansion of
G_{X}(q)
to obtain equations
for the Gromov–Witten invariants appearing in

. The anti-invariant block

is computed separately in
Quantum Differential Equation—Small Quantum Cohomology Computation
. The outcome is the following.

Proposition 6.1

(Ambient quantum cohomology)
.

On the diagonal
Q_{1}=Q_{2}=q

of the ambient operator

decomposes as

with respect to

In the ordered basis

\mathcal{B}_{+}=\bigl(1,\,H_{1}+H_{2},\,H_{1}^{2}+H_{2}^{2},\,H_{1}H_{2},\,H_{1}^{2}H_{2}+H_{1}H_{2}^{2},\,H_{1}^{2}H_{2}^{2}\bigr)

of

of

The matrices act on column vectors in the displayed ordered bases.

Proposition 6.2

(Characteristic polynomial of the ambient quantum operator)
.

Let

Then the characteristic polynomial of

on the ambient cohomology
along the anticanonical diagonal is

Consequently, for every diagonal evaluation

This is the characteristic polynomial of the block diagonal matrix

from Proposition

Let

be a splitting field for the characteristic polynomial of

(Invariant rank of the zero eigenspace)
.

For every diagonal small evaluation
\operatorname{ev}_{\zeta}
defined above,

If

is
\mathbb{Z}/2
-Hodge-general, then

Proof.

By
(

, small quantum multiplication by

vanishes on the primitive cohomology of a Verra fourfold.

∎

Theorem 6.4

(Equivariant irrationality)
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-Hodge-general symmetric Verra fourfold with factor-swap involution

. Then
(X,\epsilon)
is not

The evaluations
\operatorname{ev}_{\zeta}
, with

and

, form an infinite family of

Appendix A
Smoothness of the diagonal quartic

Proposition A.1
.

Let
B=\{f_{2,2}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}

be the smooth
(2,2)
-divisor and let
\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}

be the diagonal. Then

C_{4}:=B\cap\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}

is a smooth plane quartic.

Proof.

Suppose
p\in C_{4}
were singular. Then

d(f_{2,2}|_{\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}})_{p}=0.

By symmetry of
f_{2,2}
, at the point
(p,p)\in\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}
we have

df_{2,2}|_{(p,p)}=(\alpha,\alpha)\in T_{p}^{\vee}\mathbb{P}^{2}\oplus T_{p}^{\vee}\mathbb{P}^{2}.

Restricting to the diagonal gives

d(f_{2,2}|_{\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}})_{p}=2\alpha.

Since we work over
\mathbb{C}
, this implies
\alpha=0
. Hence

df_{2,2}|_{(p,p)}=0.

As
p\in C_{4}
, we also have
f_{2,2}(p,p)=0
. Therefore
B
would be singular
at
(p,p)
, which contradicts the smoothness of
B
. Thus
C_{4}
is smooth.
∎
