Introduction

Zadeh1 defined a fuzzy set (FS)’s membership grade as the real number “ℓ (x)” in the interval [0, 1]. Zadeh’s Fuzzy Set Theory includes a membership function with a range of . Dubois et al.2 extended the FS notion to include fuzzy numbers. A subset of the real numbers with the highest membership entries that are near the mean value is commonly referred to as a fuzzy number. In 2011, Zadeh3 introduced a new type of fuzzy number called a Z-number. The Z-number consists of two components, namely the restriction and reliability components. The first component represents the restriction (fuzzy constraint) describing the possible values of a variable, while the second component reflects the reliability or certainty associated with that restriction. For simplicity, both components are often modeled as trapezoidal fuzzy numbers3. For example, the statement “The internet connection in an e-learning system is very fast, very reliable” can be interpreted as a Z-statement in the context of IT-based education systems. Here, the first component (very fast) represents the perceived performance level of the internet speed affecting learning activities, while the second component (very reliable) indicates the confidence level of this assessment based on user experience or system monitoring. In another practical situation, a teacher might say that the learning management system is efficient and trustworthy with a high level of certainty, with efficiency being the effect that the IT system has on the teaching effectiveness, and certainty reflecting the confidence in the evaluation. The application of Z-numbers in such IT-driven educational environments highlights their strength in capturing both performance-related (DM) uncertainty and the reliability of expert judgments, making them highly suitable for advanced DM in e-learning system impact analysis. IFSs, introduced by Atanassov, are an extension of classical fuzzy theory where, besides the membership and hesitation degree, a non-membership degree is also introduced in order to be able to describe in a more realistic way uncertain human reasoning in incomplete information situations14. Several researchers have extended this base to integrate reliability in the decision process and to improve further the uncertainty modeling by using IFS logic combined with Z-numbers. This hybridization is especially helpful in cases where expert opinions are not only uncertain, but the extent of the uncertainty is also unknown. The proposed framework is compared with traditional IF aggregation methods such as IFWA and IFWG, as well as existing -based DM approaches discussed in15,36,37. Unlike conventional aggregation models, the proposed operators simultaneously consider membership, non-membership, reliability grades, and fairness behavior under group DM environments.

Moreover, traditional IF aggregation methods mainly focus on uncertainty representation without incorporating reliability information, whereas the proposed fairly AOs integrate both uncertainty and reliability aspects through the structure. Besides, the fairness mechanism will offset the extreme expert assessments of the dominance effect and give balanced aggregation result in complex e-learning evaluation environment. A benchmark analysis of the effectiveness of the proposed fairly aggregation framework is performed by comparing the framework with the current DM frameworks. The proposed model is evaluated with traditional aggregation models, the already existing models and ranking methods based on GRA. Discussion and summary

Discussion

This study proposed an advanced DM framework that can overcome the uncertainty, indeterminacy, and inconsistency in a complex real world system. The proposed approach combines fermatean neutrosophic structures and algebraic AOs, that can be more expressive and flexible than the classical fuzzy, IF and standard neutrosophic approaches to model uncertain information. This is one of the assets of the proposed model that it can deal with membership, indeterminacy, and non-membership information at the same time in a higher-order environment. This enables decision makers to retain the subtlety of the experts’ opinion, which can not be sufficiently described with conventional numerical or probabilistic models. The fermatean structure allows for a wider range of uncertainty representation while maintaining logical consistency, especially in enhancing the expressive capacity of the system. The aggregation mechanism designed in this research guarantees that the multiple expert evaluations or multiple criteria inputs are aggregated in a mathematically consistent way. Under conditions that are appropriate, the algebraic operators have essential properties preserving monotonicity, boundedness and idempotency, which are important in maintaining stability in decision making processes. Moreover, the proposed framework guarantees that the combined result is in the admissible neutrosophic space, so that there are no logical contradictions. Theoretically, the model promotes the development of neutrosophic decision theory by developing a structured hybrid aggregation system.Theoretically, the model could help to promote the development of neutrosophic decision theory by developing a structured hybrid aggregation system. This is a system that brings together the theoretical uncertainty modelling and practical decision applications. It is also a general structure that can be contracted into several well-known models as instances; therefore it is very adaptable and extensible. The proposed approach is practical and can be applied to many problems such as the risk assessment, medical diagnosis, engineering design, artificial intelligence systems and MCDM problems, etc. Uncertainty is a natural by-product of many of these environments, and can also be caused by incomplete information, subjective interpretations or the behaviour of a dynamic system. The proposed framework offers a strong instrument to resolve such challenges successfully. The model has some drawbacks, however, even with its benefits. As the number of criteria and decision experts rises the computational complexity may also change, impacting scalability in large-scale systems. Furthermore, the parameters used to configure the AOs can affect the results, suggesting a requirement for fine-tuning or tuning mechanisms that can adapt the parameters based on the data. Interpretability is another crucial factor to take into account. The model has a mathematically rich structure for the handling of uncertainty but the complexity of the neutrosophic representations could be a challenge for non-expert users. So, future research would be directed on the visualization techniques or simplified decision interfaces to improve the interpretability.

Summary

This paper presented a comprehensive and rigorous DM model using combination of algebraic AOs with the fermatean neutrosophic sets. The proposed model overcomes the major drawbacks of the current fuzzy and neutrosophic methods, which are limited in flexibility and expressive power by reducing information to a simple binary form. The results show the proposed framework is theoretically valid and applicable. It lays a solid base for solving decision making issues in ambiguously defined situations with incomplete information and various expert opinions. It would be interesting for future research to extend the proposed framework to dynamic and time-dependent decision systems, to combine the proposed framework with other models for uncertainty, e.g., spherical or picture FSs, and to utilize machine learning algorithms in order to adaptively optimize the uncertainty parameters. Additionally, real-world case studies in fields such as healthcare, transportation, and intelligent systems would further validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach.

In conclusion, the proposed model contributes significantly to the field of uncertainty modeling and MCDM by offering a unified, flexible, and mathematically consistent framework capable of handling complex decision environments.
