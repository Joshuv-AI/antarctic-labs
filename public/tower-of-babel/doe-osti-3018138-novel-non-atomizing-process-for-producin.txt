 ABSTRACT

Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) worked with Metal Powder Works (MPW) to demonstrate
feasibility of MPW proprietary DirectPowder™ process to fabricate powder from DuAlumin-3D (ORNL
developed high temperature additively manufactured - AM - aluminum) alloy ingots. Adoption of
DirectPowder™ process powder has significant potential to reduce cost, energy expenditure, eliminate
CO2 emissions, and provide a higher quality product.

 NOVEL NON-ATOMIZING PROCESS FOR PRODUCING POWDER FOR ADDITIVE
 MANUFACTURING

This phase 1 technical collaboration project (MDF-TC-2022-235) began in July 2022 and concluded in
July 2024. The collaboration partner Metal Powder Works is a small business. The project evaluated
feasibility of extruded DuAlumin-3D turned into powder through the DirectPowder™ process.

1.1 BACKGROUND

 The metal Additive Manufacturing (AM) industry is accustomed to spherical powders because
atomization is the primary method of making feedstock and as such there is a perception that non-
spherical powders are undesirable. MPW has developed a method of manufacturing metal powder
through a computer controlled mechanical process called DirectPowder™. In this process, small particles
of metals (or polymers) are created through processing of an ingot, rod, or bar stock. Due to the
numerical control of the process, the size and morphology of the powder can be controlled. Features such
as the size, shape and distribution of particles can be controlled to create extremely consistent powder
feedstocks. There are several economic benefits to the production of powder in this manner. One is that
more consistent feedstock material can be produced via this method than what is typically produced via
the gas or plasma atomization processes. In conventional gas atomization-based processes, the process
has a tendency to produce a bell-shaped distribution of powder particles. In most alloys for AM, this
includes the production of a significant number of fines and coarse particles that are not used in the
process. This can limit the overall yield up to 35% of the starting ingot volume for a particular size
distribution, significantly driving up the cost of the final powder. For the DirectPowder™ process, the
output can achieve 100% conversion to the desired size. Additional benefits of the process are that it is
continuous and scalable. Because it is continuous and highly efficient, it enables the desired production
quantity to be made simply by running the process for a fixed length of time. Additional material can be
made by running longer. Users are not limited to individual batches as is traditionally done with gas
atomization. This can be beneficial when very large articles are being built, or when multiple items of the
same design are being produced.

 The DirectPowder™ process could also be an extremely valuable tool for the development and
production of new alloys for AM. However, it is not clear if the powder produced via this process will
give equivalent performance to existing powder production technologies. ORNL has developed unique
alloys for Al (e.g. DuAlumin-3D for high temperature applications) that can be costly to produce in small
batch form due to the atomization process. This project will produce powder from DuAlumin-3D alloy.
Additional aim was to characterize these powders and determine if any necessary post processing
methods are required in order to achieve equivalent performance in a laser powder bed system.

1.2 TECHNICAL RESULTS

Phase 1 of this project attempted to manufacture powders from DuAlumin-3D alloy using
DirectPowder™ process. Billets of DuAlumin-3D alloy were produced by a casting process, which were
used as a feedstock for the DirectPowder™ process. As shown in Figure 1, as-cast microstructure of this
alloy comprises a high fraction (~27 vol.%) of various types of Ce- and Ni-rich intermetallic particles.
These intermetallic particles are relatively coarse. An initial effort was made to turn these cast billets into
powders using DirectPowder™ process. However, this process did not produce high-quality powders due
to the high fraction of coarse intermetallic particles in the microstructure of the alloy (Figure 1). Such
coarse intermetallic particles are intrinsic to the casting process of this type of alloys as the cooling rate
during solidification of the cast ingot is very low.

 Figure 1. Micrographs, acquired using scanning electron microscope (SEM), showing the typical as-cast microstructures of
DuAlumin-3D alloy. Microstructures in Area A and B point to the heterogeneity in the size and distribution of these intermetallic
 particles across the microstructure.

It was hypothesized that refinement of these intermetallic particles by any means may lead to strain
hardening in the material and a successful powder-manufacturing process using DirectPowder™
technology. Therefore, the initial plan was modified to add extrusion process between casting and
powder-manufacturing processes. This extrusion process is expected to refine intermetallic particles, thus
enabling a more successful DirectPowder™ processing of DuAlumin-3D alloy. Direct extrusion process
was carried out at elevated temperature on cast billets preheated to 500 °C using 1050 psi pressure in the
press. This extrusion process turned cast billets with the diameter of 1.73 inches into extruded rods with
0.75-inch diameter.

Typical microstructures of extruded DuAlumin-3D alloy are shown in Figure 2. As compared to cast
microstructures (Figure 1), extruded microstructures (Figure 2) show significantly refined intermetallic
particles. This refinement occurs due to particle fracture and realignment during deformation associated
with extrusion.

 Figure 2. SEM micrographs, acquired at different magnifications, showing typical microstructures of extruded DuAlumin-3D
 alloy. Extrusion direction is indicated.

Extrusion at elevated temperature also led to dynamic recrystallization, as evidenced by the presence of
the fine grains sized within 5-12 µm range (Figure 3a,d). The low grain orientation spread (GOS) and
grain reference orientation distribution (GROD) angle values measured by electron backscatter diffraction
(EBSD) is consistent with the dynamic recrystallization of new grains during extrusion. Another
microstructural feature supporting the occurrence of dynamic recrystallization is the absence of both as-
deformed grains elongated along extrusion direction and any texture attributable to as-deformed grains.

  Figure 3. SEM electron backscatter diffraction maps showing (a,d) the grain structure and orientations relative to extrusion
 direction (ED) as well as (b,e) the average misorientation angle distribution within a grain and (c,f) a gradient in the
 misorientation angle distribution within a grain on surfaces (a-c) parallel and (d-f) perpendicular to ED.

Given a significant refinement of both intermetallic particles and α-Al grains, tensile tests were conducted for both
cast and extruded specimens from DuAlumin-3D alloy, as summarized in Figure 4. Cast alloy has a
combination of low strength and ductility, consistent with a high fraction of coarse intermetallic particles.
Extrusion led to a significant improvement of both strength and ductility, which underscores the extensive
level of intermetallic refinement. These results prove the feasibility of introducing a hot-extrusion
process to refining the intermetallic particles to the level required for a more efficient powder-
manufacturing process with a DirectPowder™ technology.

 Figure 4. Tensile stress-strain curves for DuAlumin-3D alloy in both as-cast and extruded states.

Then, extruded rods were shipped to Metal Powder Works produce powders using DirectPowder™
technology. The straightness of the rods was not within the specifications for making powder from them.
Further studies are required to recast new billets and extrude them to a larger diameter and machine them
to the right dimensions before manufacturing powders at quantities sufficient for the subsequent laser
powder-bed fusion additive manufacturing process. The outcome from Phase 1 study points to the
feasibility of making powders from extruded DuAlumin-3D. The inputs from Phase 1 will enable us
manufacture extrudates with appropriate geometry and quantity and use earlier-optimized process
parameters within DirectPowder™ process to turn the extrudates into powders. A potential continuation
of this project in a future effort will enable to explore the effect of DirectPowder™ process powder
characteristics on the density and additive processability of DuAlumin-3D alloy as well as on the
feasibility of achieving comparable or even superior fatigue resistance for AM DuAlumin-3D alloy by
changing the powder source from the traditional gas-atomization process to a new DirectPowder™
process.

1.3 IMPACTS

Adoption of DirectPowder™ process powder has significant potential to reduce cost, energy expenditure,
associated with powder production for use within additive manufacturing processes.

1.4 SUBJECT INVENTIONS

NA

1.5 CONCLUSIONS

The goal of this project was to study the feasibility of reducing the manufacturing cost for an AM
DuAlumin-3D alloy by changing the powder source from gas-atomization process to a new
DirectPowder™ process developed by MPW. While attempts were made to make powder for the
DuAlumin-3D alloy with the DirectPowder™ process, these were not successful. The alloy in cast form

was too soft and in the extruded form was not straight enough for being compatible with the
DirectPowder™ process. Since the extruded rods displayed desired strain hardening, there is good
chance of success in a follow-on effort to apply extruded rods that are machined to be compatible with the
DirectPowder™ process.

 2. METAL POWDER WORKS BACKGROUND

Metal Powder Works was founded in 2017 by John E. Barnes and Christopher Aldridge, veterans of the
aerospace and defense markets with profound knowledge of additive manufacturing and powder
production. Their experience in buying powders and using them various AM processes generated a desire
to rethink about powder production in a revolutionary new way. The result is the patent pending
DirectPowder™ process which produces narrow size distributions which are controllable in a wide
variety of alloys.
