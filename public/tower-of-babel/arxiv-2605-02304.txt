On higher order regionally proximal relations and topological characteristic factors for group actions
Axel Álvarez

Abstract
. We study several aspects of higher-order regionally proximal relations for group actions. First, we develop an algebraic approach to study higher-order regionally proximal relations. To this end, we introduce a new topology on a subgroup of the universal minimal system, which can be seen as a higher-order analogue of the classical τ \uptau -topology. Using this topology, we obtain an algebraic characterization of the relation ℝ ​ ℙ [ d ] \mathbb{RP}^{[d]} for abelian actions. Then, we study higher-order regionally proximal relations via recurrence sets, extending results of Huang, Shao, and Ye for ℤ \mathbb{Z} -actions to more general group actions under suitable assumptions. We then study topological characteristic factors and prove, modulo almost one-to-one factors, that the maximal factor of order d − 1 d-1 is the topological characteristic factor of order d d for cubic configurations for arbitrary group actions, and for arithmetic progressions for finitely generated abelian group actions. As a consequence, we show that ℝ ​ ℙ [ d ] \mathbb{RP}^{[d]} and 𝔸 ​ ℙ [ d ] \mathbb{AP}^{[d]} coincide on minimal points for finitely generated abelian group actions, and we apply this to obtain results on independence along arithmetic progressions.

1.
Introduction

A topological dynamical system is a pair
(X,T)
, where
X
is a compact Hausdorff space and
T
is a discrete group acting on
X
. In recent years, the class of nilsystems and their inverse limits has been a central object of study in topological dynamics. These systems relate to many deep dynamical properties, such as being characteristic for multiple ergodic averages, and have important applications in number theory and additive combinatorics (see, for example,
[
41
, Chapter 1]
and the references therein). In the topological category, a particularly useful and important aspect of nilsystems is that they can be characterized by closed invariant relations, namely, the regionally proximal relations.
In the context of
\mathbb{Z}
-actions, the regionally proximal relations were introduced by Host, Kra, and Maass in
[
42
]
. In this context, they proved that these relations (more precisely, the property of these relations being trivial) are exactly what allow us to precisely characterize nilsystems and their inverse limits, and they may be viewed as higher-order analogues of the classical regionally proximal relation
\mathbb{RP}
. They showed, among other things, that in a distal minimal system,
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
is an equivalence relation. Soon after, Shao and Ye in
[
64
]
extended these results by proving that
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
is an equivalence relation in any minimal system for abelian actions and that the quotient by it corresponds to the maximal pronilsystem factor. In
[
30
]
, Glasner, Gutman, and Ye gave a natural generalization of the definition of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
for general group actions and introduced the nilpotent regionally proximal relation, denoted by
\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}
.

A central problem in this theory is to give characterizations of the regionally proximal relations. Having a precise description of these relations is very useful, as their description can be used to obtain applications to saturation, structure and recurrence theorems. In the case where
d=1
, this has long been a classical topic in topological dynamics (see for instance
[
66
,
5
,
55
,
65
,
7
]
). More recently, extensive work has been done in the context of the higher-order versions
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
(see for instance
[
1
,
64
,
59
,
42
,
43
,
61
,
62
]
). In this paper, we focus on two types of characterizations: an algebraic one and one based on recurrence properties. We then derive several consequences.

1.1.
Characterization through algebraic properties

In
[
1
]
, the authors obtained a characterization of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
in algebraic terms for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions. However, that proof relies essentially on the characterization of systems of order
d
via the enveloping semigroup proved in
[
16
,
61
]
, namely, that a minimal system is of order
d
if and only if its enveloping semigroup is a
d
-step nilpotent group. As up to now, this theorem is only known for
\mathbb{Z}
actions, the argument does not extend to more general settings. This leads naturally to the question of whether one can obtain an algebraic characterization of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
without using the enveloping-semigroup characterization of systems of order
d
. In this paper, we develop a framework for the study of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
for abelian actions using the so-called algebraic theory, or Galois theory, of minimal systems proposed by Ellis and developed by several authors
[
21
,
19
,
28
,
20
,
27
,
24
,
67
]
. A central tool in this theory is the
\uptau
-topology, introduced by Furstenberg in
[
24
]
, which has proven useful in the study of regionally proximal relations (see, for instance,
[
1
,
64
,
6
,
3
,
22
,
68
,
21
]
). However, when dealing with higher-order regionally proximal relations, the
\uptau
-topology seems to be insufficient to describe the relations
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
, as it fails to capture the behavior of so-called face transformations. To overcome this, we introduce a new topology, denoted
\uptau^{[d]}
, inspired by the
\uptau
-topology. This new topology is tailored to capture the behavior of face transformations and shares many important properties with the
\uptau
-topology. As a consequence, it provides a natural algebraic framework for the study of higher-order proximal relations and allows us to extend the algebraic machinery of Ellis and Furstenberg to the setting of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
. Using the framework developed in this paper, we give an algebraic characterization of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
for abelian actions, providing an alternative algebraic characterization to the one given in
[
1
, Theorem 5.10]
for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions. We refer to
Section

3.1
and
Section

2.7
for the precise definitions of the algebraic objects.

Theorem
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal system with
T
being an abelian group. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)=\{(x,vhx):x\in X,h\in H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(G),v\in J(M)\}.

This theorem shows that the relation
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
can be described in terms of the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology. Consequently, the structure of systems of order
d
may also be studied through this topology.

We expect that this algebraic characterization holds for systems satisfying the Bronstein condition, that is, the set of minimal points is dense in the product space. However, we are currently unable to prove this result due to technical difficulties. More specific comments on these issues are provided at the end of
Section

3.2
.

We also conjecture the following characterization.

Conjecture
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))=\{(x,vhgx):x\in X,h\in H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(G),g\in D,v\in J(M)\},

where
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))
denotes the smallest closed invariant equivalence relation containing
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
.

It is known that this conjecture is true for
d=1
. This follows from the results in
[
4
,
68
]
. We show that this conjecture holds whenever it holds for distal systems. We also obtain some consequences, assuming that the conjecture is true. In particular, we give a necessary and sufficient condition for
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
to be an equivalence relation.

We believe this new topology might be useful in studying other problems related to the
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
relation and systems of order
d
.

1.2.
Characterization through recurrence properties

In
[
45
]
, the authors show several characterizations of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions using recurrence sets. We extend their arguments to general group actions under certain conditions. In particular, we obtain a characterization of
\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}
for finitely generated group actions, as well as necessary conditions for a pair to belong to
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
in the case of finitely generated abelian group actions (
Theorem

4.11
). To obtain these results, we also study multiple correlation sequences for actions of finitely generated abelian groups. The study of multiple correlations has been a central topic in ergodic theory since Furstenberg’s proof of Szemerédi’s theorem. In
[
8
]
, linear correlation sequences were shown to admit a decomposition into a nilsequence and a null sequence (see
Section

4.1
for precise definitions). Since then, decompositions of multiple correlation sequences have been extensively studied in recent years (see for instance
[
52
,
51
,
53
,
18
,
23
,
49
]
). In particular, we derive the following decomposition for a certain class of multiple correlation sequences.

Theorem
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be an ergodic measure preserving system, with
T
being a finitely generated abelian group, let
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. The sequence
c_{f}(t)
is the sum of a null-sequence and a
d
-step nilsequence, where

\displaystyle c_{f}(t)=\int f(x)\cdot f(tx)\cdot\ldots\cdot f(t^{d}x)\,d\mu(x).

1.3.
Topological characteristic factors and applications

In the study of multiple ergodic averages, characteristic factors play a crucial role. The notion of characteristic factors was first introduced in a paper by Furstenberg and Weiss
[
26
]
, and its relevance was solidified in the groundbreaking work of Host and Kra
[
40
]
. A counterpart of the notion of characteristic factors in a topological dynamical system was first studied by Glasner
[
27
]
. Later, Cai and Shao defined the notion of characteristic factors along cubes
[
11
]
.

In
Section

5.2
, we show that the system of order
d-1
is the topological characteristic factor along cubes of order
d
, modulo almost one-to-one factors, for any group action, thereby extending and providing a new proof of the results in
[
1
,
11
]
. Specifically, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal metric system and
d\geq 2
be an integer. Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(X_{d-1},T)
be the factor to the maximal factor of order
d-1
. Then, there is a commutative diagram of factors of minimal systems

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X_{d-1}}}

such that

is the topological characteristic factor along cubes of order

of

, where

In
[
31
]
, the authors significantly advanced Glasner’s earlier work on topological characteristic factors by showing that, for

-actions, for any

, the maximal factor of order

of a minimal system is a topological characteristic factor along arithmetic progressions of order

, modulo almost one-to-one factors. This result was later refined in
[

]
, where it was shown that, for any

, the factor of order

is a topological characteristic factor of order

, for any

, again modulo an almost one-to-one factor. The breakthrough result of

has inspired extensive research on topological characteristic factors and their applications in recent years (see for instance

,

,

,

,

Using the connection between
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
and recurrence sets, we extend the main results of

from

2.
Background

2.1.
Dynamical systems

2.1.1.
Topological dynamical systems

A topological dynamical system (or just a system) is a pair
(X,T)
, where
X
is a compact Hausdorff space and
T
is a discrete group acting as a group of homeomorphisms of the space
X
. When
X
is a metric space, we say that
(X,T)
is a
metric system
. If
(X_{i},T)_{i\in I}
is a family of systems, the action of
T
on the product space
\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}
is defined coordinatewise:
t(x_{i})_{i\in I}=(tx_{i})_{i\in I}
for each
t\in T
and
(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}
.

The system is
transitive
if there is a point
x\in X
such that its orbit
Tx:=\{tx:t\in T\}
is dense in
X
. The system is
minimal
if the orbit of any point is dense in
X
. A pair of points
(x,y)
in
X\times X
is
proximal
if there is a net
(t_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
T
and a point
z\in X
such that
\lim t_{\lambda}x=\lim t_{\lambda}y=z
and
(x,y)
is a
distal
pair if it is not proximal. A point
x\in X
is a
distal point
if it is proximal only to itself. The set of proximal pairs is denoted by
\mathbb{P}(X)
, and called the
proximal relation
.

An important class of topological dynamical systems is the class of equicontinuous systems. A system
(X,T)
is
equicontinuous
if the collection of maps defined by the group
T
is an equicontinuous family. These systems can be characterized by the
regionally proximal relation
. A pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
is said to be regionally proximal if there are nets
(x_{i})_{i\in I},(y_{i})_{i\in I}
in
X
and
(t_{i})_{i\in I}
in
T
with
x_{i}\to x
,
y_{i}\to y
and
(t_{i}x_{i},t_{i}y_{i})\to(z,z)
, for some
z\in X
. The set of regionally proximal pairs is denoted by
\mathbb{RP}(X)
and is the
regionally proximal relation
.

A continuous onto map
\pi\colon(X,T)\to(Y,T)
between systems is a
factor
(or an
extension
) if
t\pi(x)=\pi(tx)
for every
x\in X
and
t\in T
. We say that the factor is proximal (distal) if, for every
y\in Y
, every pair of points in
\pi^{-1}(y)
is proximal (distal). We say that a factor
\pi
between metric systems is
almost one-to-one
if there exists a dense
G_{\delta}
set
X_{0}\subseteq X
such that
\pi^{-1}(\pi(x))=\{x\}
for any
x\in X_{0}
. If a factor
\pi\colon(X,T)\to(Y,T)
between minimal systems is an almost one-to-one factor, then it is also a proximal factor (
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
).

One can relativize the notion of equicontinuity. A factor map
\pi\colon(X,T)\to(Y,T)
is called equicontinuous if for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{U}_{X}
, there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{U}_{X}
such that if
(x,y)\in R_{\pi}
and
x,y\in\beta
then
(tx,ty)\in\alpha
for all
t\in T
. Here,
R_{\pi}=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:\pi(x)=\pi(y)\}
and
\mathcal{U}_{X}
denotes the uniform structure on
X
(see
[
2
, Appendix II]
for basic properties of uniform spaces). This class of factors can be characterized by a relativized regionally proximal relation. Let
R
be a closed invariant equivalence relation in
X
and
\pi:X\to X/R
the quotient map, we define

\displaystyle Q(R)=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathcal{U}_{X}}\overline{T(R\cap\alpha)}.

When
R=R_{\pi}
, we call
Q(R_{\pi})
the
\pi
-regionally proximal relation
.
Q(R_{\pi})
is trivial if and only if
\pi
is equicontinuous.

A particular example of an equicontinuous factor is a group factor. We say that
\pi\colon(X,T)\to(Y,T)
is a
group factor with group
K
whenever the following conditions are fulfilled:

(i)

K
is a compact Hausdorff topological group

(ii)

There is a continuous mapping
(x,k)\mapsto xk:X\times K\to X
such that

(a)

\forall x\in X,\forall k_{1},k_{2}\in K
:
x(k_{1}k_{2})=(xk_{1})k_{2}
,
xe_{k}=x
.

(b)

\forall t\in T,\forall k\in K,\forall x\in X
:
t(xk)=(tx)k
.

(iii)

\forall x\in X
:
\pi^{-1}(\pi(x))=xK
.

2.1.2.
Measure preserving systems

A
measure preserving system
is a quadruple
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
, where
T
is a topological group and
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu)
is a Lebesgue probability space such that
\mu
is
T
-invariant, that is,
\mu(tA)=\mu(A)
for every
A\in{\mathcal{X}}
and every
t\in T
. We write
\mathcal{I}(T)
for the
\sigma
-algebra
\{A\in{\mathcal{X}}:tA=A\text{ for all }t\in T\}
of invariant sets. The system is called
ergodic
if every
T
-invariant set has measure either
0
or
1
.

For a measure preserving system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
, a
measurable factor
is used with two meanings: it is a
T
-invariant sub-
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathcal{Y}}
of
{\mathcal{X}}
or a system
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,T)
and a measurable map
\pi:X\to Y
such that
\pi\mu=\nu
and
t\circ\pi=\pi\circ t
for every
t\in T
. These two definitions coincide under the identification of the
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathcal{Y}}
of
Y
with the invariant sub-
\sigma
-algebra
\pi^{-1}({\mathcal{Y}})
of
{\mathcal{X}}
. In this case, we say that
Y
is a measurable factor of
X
. If
f
is an integrable function on
X
, we denote by
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(f\mid{\mathcal{Y}})
the conditional expectation of
f
on the measurable factor
{\mathcal{Y}}
. We write
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(f\mid Y)
for the function on
Y
defined by
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(f\mid{\mathcal{Y}})=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(f\mid Y)\circ\pi
. This conditional expectation is characterized by

\displaystyle\int_{X}f\cdot g\circ\pi\,d\mu=\int_{Y}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(f\mid Y)\cdot g\,d\nu

for all
g\in L^{\infty}(\nu)
.

2.2.
Host-Kra cube groups associated with a group

Let
d\geq 1
be an integer, and write
[d]=\{1,2,\dots,d\}
. We view an element of
\{0,1\}^{d}
, the Euclidean cube, either as a sequence
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{d})
of 0’s and 1’s; or as a subset of
[d]
. A subset
\epsilon
corresponds to the sequence
(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{d})\in\{0,1\}^{d}
such that
i\in\epsilon
if and only if
\epsilon_{i}=1
for
i\in[d]
. For example,
\overrightarrow{0}=(0,\dots,0)\in\{0,1\}^{d}
is the same as
\emptyset\subset[d]
and
\overrightarrow{1}=(1,\dots,1)
is the same as
[d]
.

If
X
is a set, we denote
X^{2^{d}}
by
X^{[d]}
and we write a point
{\bm{x}}\in X^{[d]}
as
{\bm{x}}=(x_{\epsilon}:\epsilon\subseteq[d])
. For example, for
d=2
we have
{\bm{x}}=(x_{\emptyset},x_{\{1\}},x_{\{2\}},x_{\{1,2\}})
. Sometimes, it is convenient to view an element of
X^{[d]}
as a map from
\{0,1\}^{d}
to
X
. We can isolate the first coordinate, writing
X^{[d]}_{*}=X^{2^{d-1}}
and writing a point
{\bm{x}}\in X^{[d]}
as
{\bm{x}}=(x_{\emptyset},{\bm{x}}_{*})
, where
{\bm{x}}_{*}=(x_{\epsilon}:\epsilon\neq\emptyset)\in X^{[d]}_{*}
. For a point
x\in X
we let
x^{[d]}\in X^{[d]}
and
x^{[d]}_{*}\in X^{[d]}_{*}
be the diagonal points all of whose coordinates are
x
.

Let
0\leq\ell\leq d
be an integer. An
\ell
-dimensional face
of
\{0,1\}^{d}
, or equivalently a
face of codimension
d-\ell
of
\{0,1\}^{d}
, is a subset of
\{0,1\}^{d}
obtained by fixing the values of
d-\ell
coordinates. We write dim(
\alpha
) and codim(
\alpha
) for the dimension and codimension of a face
\alpha
. A
facet
of
\{0,1\}^{d}
is defined to be a face of codimension
1
. A face
\alpha
is an
upper face
if
\overrightarrow{1}\in\alpha
.

Let
L
be a group and
d\geq 1
be an integer. If
\alpha
is a face of
\{0,1\}^{d}
, for
g\in L
we define
g^{(\alpha)}\in L^{[d]}
by

\displaystyle g^{(\alpha)}(\epsilon)=\left\{\begin{matrix}g&\text{if }\epsilon\in\alpha\\
e&\text{otherwise}.\end{matrix}\right.

where
e
is the identity element of
L
.

We call the subgroup of
L^{[d]}
generated by all
g^{(\alpha)}
, where
g\in L
and
\alpha
is a facet of
\{0,1\}^{d}
, the
Host-Kra cube group
(of order
d
) associated with
L
and denote it by
\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(L)
. We call the subgroup of
L^{[d]}
generated by all
g^{(\alpha)}
, where
g\in L
and
\alpha
is an upper facet of
\{0,1\}^{d}
, the
face cube group
and denote it by
\mathcal{F}^{[d]}(L)
. When there is no ambiguity, we omit the group
L
from the notation.

The Host-Kra and face cube groups originally appeared in
[
40
, Section 5]
and coincide with the parallelepiped groups and face groups respectively of
[
42
, Definition 3.1]
introduced for abelian actions. See also
[
37
, Appendix E]
for treatment of Host-Kra cube groups in nilpotent Lie groups.

2.3.
Nilsystems and Host-Kra seminorms

We refer to
[
41

Let

be a
d
-step nilpotent Lie group and
\Gamma
a discrete cocompact subgroup of

is called a
d
-step nilmanifold
. Observe that

acts naturally on

by left translation. If

is a topological group and

is a continuous homomorphism, the induced action
(X,T)
is called a
nilsystem of order
d
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be a measure preserving system. We define, by induction, a probability measure
\mu^{[d]}
on
X^{[d]}
that is invariant under

. Set
\mu^{[0]}=\mu
. For

\mathcal{I}^{[d]}

\sigma
-algebra of

. We define
\mu^{[d+1]}
to be the relatively independent square of
\mu^{[d]}
over
\mathcal{I}^{[d]}
.

For a bounded function

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d}\coloneqq\left(\int_{X^{[d]}}\prod_{j=0}^{2^{d}-1}f(x_{j})\,d\mu^{[d]}({\bm{x}})\right)^{1/2^{d}}.

These seminorms define factors of
{\mathcal{X}}
. More precisely, the sub-
\sigma
-algebra

of
{\mathcal{X}}
is characterized by

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(f\mid{\mathcal{Z}}_{d-1}(X))=0\,\text{ if and only if }\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{d}=0.

We denote by

the factor of

associated to
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}(X)
. When there is no ambiguity, we write
Z_{d}
and
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
instead of
Z_{d}(X)
and
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}(X)
.

In
[
40
]
, a structure theorem for systems satisfying

was proved for

-actions. Later, in
[

]
, this result was extended to

-actions. Recently, it was extended to actions of finitely generated nilpotent groups in
[

Theorem 2.1

(
[

, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let

be an ergodic measure preserving system with

being a finitely generated nilpotent group. Then, for each positive integer

Let
(X,T)
be a system. The Host-Kra cube group of

\displaystyle(t{\bm{x}})(\epsilon)=t_{\epsilon}{\bm{x}}_{\epsilon}

Let

\displaystyle\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)=\overline{\{tx^{[d]}:t\in\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T),x\in X\}}.

\displaystyle\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)=\overline{\{tx_{0}^{[d]}:t\in\mathcal{HK}^{[d]}(T)\}}=\overline{\{tx^{[d]}:t\in\mathcal{F}^{[d]}(T),x\in X\}}.

\displaystyle\bm{Q}_{x}^{[d]}(X)

\displaystyle=\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)\cap(\{x\}\times X^{2^{d}-1})

(2)

(3)

2.5.
Higher order regionally proximal relations

2.5.1.
Regionally proximal relation of order
d

Let
(X,T)
be a system and
d\geq 1
be an integer. A pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
is said to be
regionally proximal of order
d
if there are nets
f_{i}\in{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}
,
x_{i},y_{i}\in X
, and
a_{*}\in X^{[d]}_{*}
such that
(f_{i}x^{[d]}_{i},f_{i}y_{i}^{[d]})\to(x,a_{*},y,a_{*})
. The set of regionally proximal pairs of order
d
is denoted by
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X,T)
and is called the
regionally proximal relation of order
d
. When there is no ambiguity, we write
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
instead of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X,T)
. Note that
\mathbb{RP}^{[1]}(X)
is the classical regionally proximal relation.

The relation
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
is a closed and invariant relation. Moreover, (see
[
30
, Lemma A.5]
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^{[d+1]}(X)\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^{[1]}(X).

2.5.2.
Nilpotent regionally proximal relation of order
d

Let
(X,T)
be a system and
d\geq 1
be an integer. A pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
is said to be
nilpotent regionally proximal of order
d
if
(x,y^{[d+1]}_{*})\in\bm{Q}^{[d+1]}(X)
. The set of nilpotent regionally proximal pairs of order
d
is denoted by
\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X,T)
and is called the
nilpotent regionally proximal relation of order
d
. When there is no ambiguity, we write
\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X)
instead of
\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X,T)
. We say that
(X,T)
is a
system of order
d
if
\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X)
coincides with the diagonal relation.

The relation
\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X)
is a closed and invariant relation. Moreover, (see
[
30
, Lemma A.5]
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathbb{NRP}^{[d+1]}(X)\subseteq\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathbb{NRP}^{[1]}(X).

The following theorem shows some properties of the nilpotent regionally proximal relation of order
d
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
30
, Corollary 4.2, Theorems 3.8 and 6.1, and Proposition 8.9]
)
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be a factor between minimal systems and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then:

(1)

(x,y)\in\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X)
if and only if
(x,y^{[d+1]}_{*})\in\overline{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d+1]}x^{[d+1]}}
.

(2)

\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X)
is an equivalence relation.

(3)

\pi\times\pi(\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X))=\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(Y)
.

(4)

\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)\subseteq\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X)
.

It is worth mentioning that
\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}
is not necessarily equal to
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
(see
[
30
, Remark 8.10]
). Nevertheless, for abelian actions, these two relations coincide (
[
64
, Theorem 3.2]
).

Under certain conditions, systems of order
d
have a structure theorem, analogous to the ergodic structure theorem.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
39
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal metric system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Suppose that
T
has a dense subgroup generated by a compact set. If
(X,T)
is a system of order
d
then
(X,T)
is isomorphic to an inverse limit of
d
-step nilsystems.

The assumption that the system is metric in
Theorem

2.4
can be removed for countable actions, by the following result.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
33
, Proposition 4.1]
)
.

Let
(X,T)
be a system. If

is countable, then
(X,T)
is an inverse limit of metric systems.

2.6.
Enveloping semigroups

The
enveloping semigroup
(or
Ellis semigroup
)
E(X,T)
of a system
(X,T)
is defined as the closure of
T
in
X^{X}
endowed with the product topology. For an enveloping semigroup
E(X,T)
, the maps
E(X,T)\to E(X,T)
,
p\mapsto pq
and
p\mapsto tp
are continuous for all
q\in E(X,T)
and
t\in T
.

Ellis introduced this notion, and it has proved to be a useful tool in studying dynamical systems. The algebraic properties of
E(X)
can be converted into the dynamical properties of
(X,T)
and the reverse is also true. To exemplify this, we recall the following theorem.

Theorem 2.6

(see
[
2
, Chapters 3,4 and 5]
)
.

Let
(X,T)
be a system. Then,

(1)

(X,T)
is distal if and only if
E(X,T)
is a group.

(2)

(X,T)
is equicontinuous if and only if
E(X,T)
is a group of continuous transformations.

(3)

If
T
is an abelian group,
(X,T)
is equicontinuous if and only if
E(X,T)
is an abelian group.

2.7.
The universal system

We refer to
[
14
, Sections IV, V and VI]
for the material discussed in this section. It is known that the semigroup
\beta T
, the Stone–Čech compactification of the discrete group
T
, is the universal point transitive system. That is, for every transitive system
(X,T)
and a point
x\in X
with dense orbit, there exists an extension of systems
(\beta T,T)\to(X,T)
which sends
e
, identity element of
T
, onto
x
. Therefore, by universality, there exists a unique extension
\Phi_{X}:(\beta T,T)\to(E(X,T),T)
, which is also a semigroup homomorphism, and we can interpret the
\beta T
action on
X
via this homomorphism.

The semigroup
\beta T
admits many minimal left ideals, which coincide with the minimal subsystems. All these ideals are isomorphic to each other both as compact right topological semigroups and as minimal systems. We will fix a minimal left ideal
M
of
\beta T
. The universality of
\beta T
implies that
(M,T)
is the universal minimal system. For a semigroup, the element
v
with
v^{2}=v
is called an
idempotent
. By the Ellis Namakura theorem, the set
J(M)
of idempotents in
M
is nonempty. Moreover,
vM
is a group with identity
v
, where
v\in J(M)
. We fix one such idempotent
u\in J(M)
.

If
(X,T)
is minimal, then
X=Mx
for every
x\in X
. A necessary and sufficient condition for
x
to be minimal is that
ux=x
for some
u\in J(M)
. A minimal system
(X,T)
is distal if and only if
X=vX
for every
v\in J(M)
. So, a minimal system
(X,T)
is distal iff
\Phi_{X}(M)=\Phi_{X}(vM)=E(X,T)
for every
v\in J(M)
. Also, we can characterize the proximal points: a pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
is proximal if and only if there exists a minimal left ideal
I
of
\beta T
such that
y=vx
for some
v\in J(I)
.

Let
2^{X}
be the collection of nonempty closed subsets of
X
endowed with the Hausdorff topology. The action of
T
on
2^{X}
is given by

\displaystyle tA=\{ta:a\in A\}

for each
t\in T
and
A\in 2^{X}
. This action induces another action of
\beta T
on
2^{X}
, and we denote this action by the circle operation:
p\circ_{T}A
, where
p\in\beta T
and
A\subset X
, to distinguish it from the subset
pA
. For
p\in\beta T
and
A\subseteq X
, we define
p\circ_{T}A=p\circ_{T}\overline{A}
and
p\circ_{T}\emptyset=\emptyset
, where
\overline{A}
denotes the closure of
A
in the usual topology of
X
. It holds that

\displaystyle p\circ_{T}A=\{x\in X:\exists(t_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq T,\exists(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq A\text{ with }t_{\lambda}\to p,t_{\lambda}x_{\lambda}\to x\}.

for all
p\in\beta T
and
A\in 2^{X}
, where
t_{\lambda}\to p
denotes the convergence of
(t_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
to
p
in the usual topology of
\beta T
. We use this notation for convergence in the usual topology throughout the paper. The circle operation has the following properties.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
p\in M
and
v,w\in J(M)
. Then

(1)

vp\circ_{T}wA=v\circ_{T}pA
.

(2)

p\circ_{T}wA=p\circ_{T}vA
.

(3)

p(w\circ_{T}A)=p(v\circ_{T}A)
.

(4)

(w\circ_{T}A)\cap wA=w(w\circ_{T}A)
.

The circle operation also behaves well with factor maps.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be a factor between systems,
p,q\in M
,
v,w\in J(M)
,
A\subseteq X
and
y\in Y
. Then

(1)

\pi(p\circ_{T}A)=p\circ_{T}\pi(A)
.

(2)

p\circ_{T}\pi^{-1}(y)\subseteq\pi^{-1}(py)
with equality if
\pi
is open.

(3)

p\circ_{T}v\pi^{-1}(qy)=pq\circ_{T}v\pi^{-1}(wy)
.

The set
G=uM
is a subgroup of
M
with identity
u
. The set
\{vM:v\in J(M)\}
is a partition of
M
and each
p\in M
has a unique representation
p=vg
, where
v\in J(M)
and
g\in G
. We sometimes write
p^{-1}
for
vg^{-1}
. The group
G
plays a central role in the algebraic theory of minimal systems. It carries a
T_{1}
compact topology, called by Ellis the
\uptau
-topology
, which is weaker than the relative topology induced on
G
as a subset of
M
. This topology was first introduced by Furstenberg in
[
24
]
, and developed by Ellis, Glasner and Shapiro in
[
21
]
. For any subset
A\subseteq G
, the
\uptau
-topology is determined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau}(A)=u(u\circ_{T}A)=(u\circ_{T}A)\cap G.

Let
(g_{i})_{i\in I}
be a net in
G
and
g\in G
. The convergence of
(g_{i})_{i\in I}
to
g
with respect to the
\uptau
-topology is denoted by
g_{i}\xrightarrow{\uptau}g
.

For a
\uptau
-closed subgroup
F
of
G
the
derived group

H(F)
is given by:

\displaystyle H(F)=\bigcap\{\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau}(V):V\text{ a }\uptau\text{-open neighborhood of }u\text{ in }F\}.

The group
H(F)
is a
\uptau
-closed normal subgroup and is the smallest
\uptau
-closed subgroup
H
of
F
such that
F/H
is a compact Hausdorff topological group (for the quotient topology induced by the
\uptau
-topology).

For a point
x_{0}\in uX
, the

of the pointed system
(X,x_{0})
is the
\uptau
-closed subgroup

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{G}}(X,x_{0})=\{g\in G:gx_{0}=x_{0}\}.

When there is no ambiguity, we omit the fixed point
x_{0}
and denote the Ellis group of
X
by
{\mathfrak{G}}(X)
. Whenever
x_{0}
is used, we will refer to the element with respect to which the group
{\mathfrak{G}}(X)
is defined. For
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
a factor of minimal systems we have
{\mathfrak{G}}(X)\subseteq{\mathfrak{G}}(Y)
, where the group
{\mathfrak{G}}(Y)
is defined by the point
\pi(x_{0})
. The Ellis group is very useful when studying factors. Below, there are a few applications of this concept.

Theorem 2.9

(see
[
2
, Chapters 10 and 14]
)
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be a factor between minimal systems. Then,

(1)

\pi
is proximal if and only if
{\mathfrak{G}}(X)={\mathfrak{G}}(Y)
.

(2)

\pi
is distal if and only if
\pi^{-1}(py_{0})=p{\mathfrak{G}}(Y)x_{0}
, for all

.

(3)

\pi

\pi

.

(4)

\pi

\pi

.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be a factor of minimal systems. We say that

is

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y^{\prime}}}

3.
The
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology and
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}

3.1.
The
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology

In this section, we define the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology using the circle operation and study its properties. This topology will be crucial for our study of the regionally proximal relation of order
d
for abelian actions.

To define the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology, we first need the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1

(
[
30
, Lemma 4.7]
)
.

Let
v\in J(M)
. Then
v^{[d]}_{*}
is a minimal idempotent of
\beta{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
.

We define the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology in a system
(X,T)
, for
d\geq 1
, by introducing a closure operator. Recall that throughout this paper,
u
denotes the idempotent of
M
fixed in
Section

2.7
to define the group
G
. Given a subset
A\subseteq uX
, we define
\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)
as the set of all
x\in uX
such that
x^{[d]}_{*}\in u^{[d]}_{*}(u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A))
. Note that, by the definition of the Vietoris topology,
x\in\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)
if and only if
x\in uX
and, for any net
(f_{i})_{i}
in
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
with
f_{i}\to u^{[d]}_{*}
, there exists a net
(a_{i})_{i}
in
A
such that
f_{i}a_{i*}^{[d]}\to x^{[d]}_{*}
. Note that, by definition of the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology we have the following inclusions

\displaystyle\uptau=\uptau^{[1]}\subseteq\uptau^{[2]}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\uptau^{[d]}\subseteq\uptau^{[d+1]}\subseteq\cdots.

The following result shows that the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology is indeed a topology.

Proposition 3.2
.

\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(\cdot)
is a closure operator.

Proof.

It is clear that
A\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)
and
\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(F)
for any
A,F\subseteq uX
with
A\subseteq F
. By definition of
\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(\cdot)
, we have
\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A))\subseteq u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A)
. Therefore,
u^{[d]}_{*}(u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)))\subseteq u^{[d]}_{*}(u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A))=u^{[d]}_{*}(u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A))
. Thus, we obtain that
\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A))=\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

The
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology on
uX
is compact and
T1
.

Proof.

Note that
u\overline{A}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)
for all
A\subseteq uX
. Let
(A_{i})_{i}
be a family of
\uptau^{[d]}
-closed subsets of
uX
with the finite intersection property. Therefore,
(\overline{A_{i}})_{i}
has the finite intersection property in
X
. Since
X
is a compact set, there exists
x\in\bigcap_{i}\overline{A_{i}}
. It follows from
u\overline{A}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)
, for any
A\subseteq uX
, that
ux\in\bigcap_{i}\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A_{i})=\bigcap_{i}A_{i}
. Hence, the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology is compact. Note that, by definition of the circle operation,
\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(\{x\})=\{x\}
for all
x\in uX
, and consequently it is
T1
.
∎

Note that the definition of the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology depends on the choice of the fixed idempotent
u
. The following result shows that this choice does not affect the properties of the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology.

Lemma 3.4
.

The map
x\mapsto vx
defines a
\uptau^{[d]}
-isomorphism from
uX
onto
vX
, where
v\in J(M)
.

Proof.

Let
A
be a
\uptau^{[d]}
-closed subset of
uX
. By
Proposition

2.7
, we have

\displaystyle v^{[d]}_{*}(v^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A))

\displaystyle=v^{[d]}_{*}(u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A))

\displaystyle=v^{[d]}_{*}u^{[d]}_{*}(u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A)).

Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(vA)=v\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)=vA.

Thus,
vA
is
\uptau^{[d]}
-closed in
vX
, and hence we conclude that the map is a
\uptau^{[d]}
-isomorphism of
uX
onto
vX
.
∎

Let
(g_{i})_{i\in I}
be a net in
G
and
g\in G
. The convergence of
(g_{i})_{i\in I}
to
g
with respect to the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology is denoted by
g_{i}\xrightarrow{\uptau^{[d]}}g
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(x_{i})_{i}
be a net in
uX
with
x_{i}\to x
, for some
x\in X
. Then
x_{i}\xrightarrow{\uptau^{[d]}}ux
.

Proof.

Let
A_{j}=\{x_{i}:i\geq j\}
. Since
x_{i}\to x
, we have that
x\in\overline{A_{j}}
for every

, and consequently
ux\in u\overline{A_{j}}
. It follows from

that
ux\in\bigcap_{j}\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A_{j})
. Since we can apply the same reasoning to any subnet of

, we conclude that
x_{i}\xrightarrow{\uptau^{[d]}}ux
.
∎

It is known that the inversion and left and right multiplication are
\uptau^{[1]}
-continuous. However, this continuity does not generally hold for higher orders. We will show instead that these operations are
\uptau^{[d]}
-continuous at points satisfying a suitable condition. We will first provide a basis for this topology. For this, we first recall some basic properties of the basis for the topology of the Stone-Čech compactification
\beta T
.

Since the group
T
is discrete, for every subset
U\subseteq T
, the closure
\overline{U}
(taken in
\beta T
) is both open and closed in
\beta T
, and
\overline{U}\cap T=U
. Moreover the family

\displaystyle\{\overline{U}:U\subseteq T\}

form a basis for the topology of
\beta T
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(X,T)
be a system and

. A basis for the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology at

is given by the sets

\displaystyle(U,V)_{d}=\bigcup_{f\in V}\bigcap_{\epsilon\neq\emptyset}f_{\epsilon}^{-1}U\cap uX

where
V\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
such that
u^{[d]}_{*}\in\overline{V}
and

is a neighborhood of

in

.

Proof.

Let

be a
\uptau^{[d]}
-open neighborhood of

in
uX
, and set

, so

is a
\uptau^{[d]}
-closed subset of

. Since

, where

is an open neighborhood of

in

. Now, consider the family
(U_{i})_{i}
of open neighborhoods of

contained in

. For each

, define the open set

, and set

such that
u^{[d]}_{*}\in\overline{V_{i}}\cap M^{[d]}\subseteq O_{i*}^{[d]}\cap\beta{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
.

Suppose that
(U_{i},V_{i})_{d}\cap C\neq\emptyset
for all
i
, and consider
x_{i}\in(U_{i},V_{i})_{d}\cap C
. Then there exists
f_{i}\in V_{i}
such that
f_{i}x_{i*}^{[d]}\in U_{i*}^{[d]}
. By the definitions of

and

, it follows that
f_{i}x_{i*}^{[d]}\to x^{[d]}_{*}
and
f_{i}x^{[d]}_{*}\to x^{[d]}_{*}
. Without loss of generality, by passing to a subnet if necessary, we may assume that
f_{i}\to f
. Therefore, we have
fx^{[d]}_{*}=x^{[d]}_{*}
, which in particular implies that
u^{[d]}_{*}f^{-1}x^{[d]}_{*}=x^{[d]}_{*}
. Thus, we obtain that

\displaystyle x^{[d]}_{*}=\lim f_{i}x_{i*}^{[d]}=\lim f_{i}u^{[d]}_{*}x_{i*}^{[d]}\in\lim f_{i}u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(C)=fu^{[d]}_{*}\circ\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(C),

which implies that

\displaystyle x^{[d]}_{*}=u^{[d]}_{*}f^{-1}x^{[d]}_{*}=u^{[d]}_{*}f^{-1}u^{[d]}_{*}x^{[d]}_{*}\in u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}(C).

Therefore, we obtain that
x\in\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(C)=C
, which is a contradiction. Hence, there exists

such that
(U_{i},V_{i})_{d}\cap C=\emptyset
, and we conclude that
(U_{i},V_{i})_{d}\subseteq W
.

Conversely, let
(U,V)_{d}
such that
u^{[d]}_{*}\in\overline{V}
and
U
is a neighborhood of
x
in
X
, and define
C=uX\setminus(U,V)_{d}
. In particular, if

then, for every

, we have that

. Now, let

. Then there exist a net
(f_{i})_{i}
in
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
and a net
(x_{i})_{i}
in
C
such that
z^{[d]}_{*}=\lim f_{i}x_{i*}^{[d]}
and
f_{i}\to u^{[d]}_{*}
. Eventually,
f_{i}\in\overline{V}
which is a neighborhood of
u^{[d]}_{*}
, and then
f_{i}\in V
. Therefore, we have that
f_{i}x_{i*}^{[d]}\notin U_{*}^{[d]}
, and consequently

. Thus, we deduce that

\displaystyle x\in U\cap uX\subseteq uX\setminus\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(C)=\mathrm{int}_{\uptau^{[d]}}((U,V)_{d}).

Hence, we conclude that
(U,V)_{d}
is a neighborhood of

.
∎

In particular, for

, using the fact that the family
{\overline{U}:U\subseteq T}
is a basis for

, we obtain the following basis for the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology on

.

Corollary 3.7
.

A basis for the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology at
g\in G
is given by the sets

\displaystyle(U,V)_{d}=\bigcup_{f\in V}\bigcap_{\epsilon\neq\emptyset}f_{\epsilon}^{-1}\overline{U}\cap G

where

such that

and

such that

.

First, we prove that the left multiplication is a
\uptau^{[d]}
-homeomorphism.

Proposition 3.8
.

Left multiplication in

is a
\uptau^{[d]}
-homeomorphism.

Proof.

Let
g\in G
and let

be a
\uptau^{[d]}
-closed subset of

. Let

, so we have

\displaystyle h^{[d]}_{*}\in u^{[d]}_{*}(u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(Ag))=u^{[d]}_{*}(u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A))g^{[d]}_{*}.

Therefore, we get that

, and consequently
\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(Ag)=Ag
. That is, the left multiplication is a
\uptau^{[d]}
-homeomorphism.
∎

Now, we show that the right multiplication by an element under a certain condition is continuous with respect to the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
g\in G
be such that
g^{[d]}_{*}\in\overline{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}u^{[d]}_{*}}
. Then

is
\uptau^{[d]}
-continuous.

Proof.

Let

be a
\uptau^{[d]}
-closed subset of

. Since

, we have

\displaystyle u^{[d]}_{*}(u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}g^{[d]}_{*}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A))

\displaystyle=u^{[d]}_{*}(g^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A))

\displaystyle=g^{[d]}_{*}(g^{-1})^{[d]}_{*}(g^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A))

\displaystyle=g^{[d]}_{*}((g^{-1})^{[d]}_{*}\circ(g^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A)))

\displaystyle=g^{[d]}_{*}(u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A)).

Therefore, it follows that
\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(gA)=g\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)=gA

∎

Note that for

-homeomorphism. Nevertheless, for

, we are unable to prove that the right multiplication is a

is the best we can expect.

Now, we can prove the inversion is

-continuous at points that satisfy the same condition as in

3.9
. Before, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

For
U\subseteq T
,

and

. If
g^{[d]}_{*}\in\bigcup_{\textbf{g}\in U^{[d]}_{*}}\textbf{g}^{-1}\overline{V}\cap G^{[d]}_{*}

Let

. Therefore, there is a
{\bm{g}}\in U^{[d]}_{*}
such that
{\bm{g}}g^{[d]}_{*}\in U^{[d]}_{*}\in\overline{V}

. Since

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
g\in G
be such that
g^{[d]}_{*}\in\overline{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}u^{[d]}_{*}}
. Then the map

of

onto itself is
\uptau^{[d]}
-continuous at

.

Proof.

First we show that the inversion is continuous at

. Let

and

be such that

and
u^{[d]}_{*}\in\overline{V}
, and define

. Let

, in particular we have

. Then there exist a net

in
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
and a net

in

. Therefore, eventually

, we have

\displaystyle u^{[d]}_{*}\in\overline{V}\cap\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(G)\subseteq\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(G\setminus\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(C))=\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(\mathrm{int}_{\uptau^{[d]}}((U,V)_{d}^{-1})).

.

Now, let

and let

be a net in

, we have

∎

The following theorem gives some topological properties of factor maps with respect to the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be a factor between minimal systems, and set
\pi_{u}=\pi|_{uX}
. Then,

(i)

\pi_{u}
is
\uptau^{[d]}
-continuous.

(ii)

\pi_{u}
is a
\uptau^{[d]}
-homeomorphism if and only if
\pi
is a proximal factor.

Proof.

(i)

Let
A\subseteq uX
and

. In particular, we have
x^{[d]}_{*}\in u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A)
. By
Proposition

2.8
, it follows that

\displaystyle(\pi_{u}(x))^{[d]}_{*}\in u^{[d]}_{*}(u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(\pi_{u}(A))).

Thus, we obtain that
\pi_{u}(x)\in\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(\pi_{u}(A))
, and hence
\pi_{u}
is
\uptau^{[d]}
-continuous.

(ii)

Note that
\pi
is a proximal factor if and only if
\pi_{u}
is bijective, so it suffices to prove the non-trivial implication. Suppose that
\pi
is a proximal factor, it is enough to show that
\pi_{u}
is closed.

Let
A\subseteq uX
and
y\in\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(\pi_{u}(A))
. Therefore, there exist nets
(f_{i})_{i}
in
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
and
(a_{i})_{i}
in
A
such that
f_{i}\to u^{[d]}_{*}
and
f_{i}\pi(a_{i})_{*}^{[d]}\to y^{[d]}_{*}
. Consider
x\in uX
such that

, and we may assume that there exists
\mathbb{a}\in u^{[d]}_{*}X^{[d]}_{*}
such that
f_{i}a_{i*}^{[d]}\to\mathbb{a}
. Since
\pi(\mathbb{a})=y^{[d]}_{*}
and

is a proximal factor, it follows that
u^{[d]}_{*}\mathbb{a}=x^{[d]}_{*}
. Thus, we have that

\displaystyle x^{[d]}_{*}=u^{[d]}_{*}\mathbb{a}\in u^{[d]}_{*}(u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A)).

Hence, we obtain that
y\in\pi_{u}(\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A))
, and we conclude that
\pi_{u}
is
\uptau^{[d]}
-closed.

∎

Let
F
be a
\uptau^{[d]}
-closed subgroup of
G
with
{\mathfrak{G}}(X)\subseteq F
. Let
{\mathcal{N}}_{\uptau^{[d]}}^{F}(X)
denote the open neighborhood filter at

of

for the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology. We define

\displaystyle H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(Fx_{0})=\bigcap\{\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(V):V\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\uptau^{[d]}}^{F}(X)\}.

Note that
x\in H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(Fx_{0})
if and only if there exists a net
(x_{i})_{i}
in
Fx_{0}
such that
x_{i}\xrightarrow{\uptau^{[d]}}x
and
x_{i}\xrightarrow{\uptau^{[d]}}x_{0}
. This object will be essential in giving an algebraic characterization of the relation
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
F
be a
\uptau^{[d]}
-closed subgroup of
G
and let
g\in F
. Then
g\in H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(F)
if and only if there exist
(f_{i})_{i}
a net in
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}
and nets
(g_{i})_{i},(g_{i}^{\prime})_{i}
in
F
such that
f_{i}\to(e,u^{[d]}_{*})
and
(f_{i}g_{i}^{[d]},f_{i}{g_{i}^{\prime}}^{[d]})\to(r,g^{[d]}_{*},r,u^{[d]}_{*})
for some

.

Proof.

Suppose that there exist
(f_{i})_{i}
a net in
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}
and nets
(g_{i})_{i},(g_{i}^{\prime})_{i}
in
F
such that
f_{i}\to(e,u^{[d]}_{*})
and
(f_{i}g_{i}^{[d]},f_{i}{g_{i}^{\prime}}^{[d]})\to(r,g^{[d]}_{*},r,u^{[d]}_{*})

.
By

, it follows that
g_{i}\xrightarrow{\uptau^{[d]}}ur

. Let

, and we conclude that

Conversely, suppose that

. Then there exists a net
(g_{i})_{i}
in
F
such that

and

. Let

in

and

A direct consequence of

is the following.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let

. Then
(g,u)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(M)
, and consequently

In particular, every

satisfies the hypothesis of

3.9
and

. Using this, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
F
be a
\uptau^{[d]}
-closed subgroup of

. Then
H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(F)
is a
\uptau^{[d]}
-closed subgroup of

.

Proof.

First, we show that
H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(F)V\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(V)
for each

. Let

and

. Since

, there exists a net
(g_{i})_{i\in I}
in

such that

. Therefore, we have
g_{i}v\xrightarrow{\uptau^{[d]}}v
. Since

is a
\uptau^{[d]}
-open set, it follows that

eventually. Hence, it follows from
g_{i}v\xrightarrow{\uptau^{[d]}}gv

.

Now, we prove that
H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(F)\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(V)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(V)

. Let

and

, and let

be a net in

such that

. By

, we have

\displaystyle H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(F)\bigcap_{V\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\uptau^{[d]}}^{F}(M)}\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(V)\subseteq\bigcap_{V\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\uptau^{[d]}}^{F}(M)}H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(F)\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(V)\subseteq\bigcap_{V\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\uptau^{[d]}}^{F}(M)}\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(V).

Hence, we conclude that

is a semigroup. Let

, so

is also a semigroup, and by the Ellis Namakura theorem, it contains an idempotent. Since

and that

is a group, we have

. It follows that

is invertible, and consequently

Furthermore, the

-topology also has the Ellis Trick.

Lemma 3.16

Let

is
F
-minimal.

(ii)

if

is open subset of

, then
\mathrm{int}_{\uptau^{[d]}}\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(V\cap wF)\neq\emptyset
.

(i)

\overline{F}
is an

-invariant closed subset of

, so there exists a minimal
F
-invariant subset

. Let

and consider

and

such that
p=wg
. Since
p\in\overline{F}
, there exists a net
(f_{i})_{i}
in
F
such that
f_{i}\to p
. Therefore, by
Lemma

3.5
, we have
f_{i}\xrightarrow{\uptau^{[d]}}g
. Since
F
is
\uptau^{[d]}
-closed, it follows that
g\in F
. Now, because
K
is
F
-invariant, we obtain that
w=pg^{-1}\in KF\subseteq K
. Thus, by the minimality of
K
, we conclude that
\overline{wF}=K
.

(ii)

By the
F
-minimality of
\overline{wF}
we have
\overline{wF}\subseteq VF
. Since
\overline{wF}
is compact, it follows that there exist
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
in
F
such that
\overline{wF}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}Vf_{i}
. In particular, we have
wF\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}(V\cap wF)f_{i}
, and consequently
wF\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(V\cap wF)f_{i}
. Thus, at least one of the sets
\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(V\cap wF)f_{i}
must have nonempty
\uptau^{[d]}
-interior. Hence, we conclude that
\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(V\cap wF)
has nonempty
\uptau^{[d]}
-interior.

∎

We now introduce a new topology, inspired by the approach used by Furstenberg in his definition of the
\uptau
-topology. We will later show that this topology coincides with the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology.

Let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Let
\Sigma_{d}
denote the set of all continuous pseudometrics on
X^{[d]}_{*}
. For every
\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}
define
F_{\sigma}^{[d]}:X^{[d]}_{*}\times X^{[d]}_{*}\to\mathbb{R}
and
F_{\sigma,d}:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle F_{\sigma}^{[d]}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}})

\displaystyle=\inf\{\sigma(f{\bm{x}},f{\bm{y}}):f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}\}

\displaystyle F_{\sigma,d}(x,y)

\displaystyle=F_{\sigma}^{[d]}(x^{[d]}_{*},y^{[d]}_{*}).

Note that
F_{\sigma}^{[d]}
is symmetric, invariant under
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
and upper semicontinuous, so
F_{\sigma,d}
is symmetric and upper semicontinuous.

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}})\in X^{[d]}_{*}\times X^{[d]}_{*}
,
\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}
and
\bm{p}\in\beta{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
. Then
F_{\sigma}^{[d]}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}})\leq F_{\sigma}^{[d]}(\bm{p}{\bm{x}},\bm{p}{\bm{y}})
with equality if
({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}})
is a
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
-minimal point.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
F_{\sigma}^{[d]}
is an upper semicontinuous function, the set

\displaystyle\{\bm{q}\in\beta{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}:F_{\sigma}^{[d]}(\bm{q}{\bm{x}},\bm{q}{\bm{y}})<F_{\sigma}^{[d]}(\bm{p}{\bm{x}},\bm{p}{\bm{y}})+\varepsilon\}

is an open set. Therefore, since
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
is dense in
\beta{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
, there exists
f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
such that

\displaystyle F_{\sigma}^{[d]}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}})=F_{\sigma}^{[d]}(f{\bm{x}},f{\bm{y}})<F_{\sigma}^{[d]}(\bm{p}{\bm{x}},\bm{p}{\bm{y}})+\varepsilon.

Thus, it follows that
F_{\sigma}^{[d]}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}})\leq F_{\sigma}^{[d]}(\bm{p}{\bm{x}},\bm{p}{\bm{y}})
.

Now, if
({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}})
is a
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
-minimal point. Then there exists
\bm{q}\in\beta{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
such that
(\bm{q}\bm{p}{\bm{x}},\bm{q}\bm{p}{\bm{y}})=({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}})
, hence we conclude that
F_{\sigma}^{[d]}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}})=F_{\sigma}^{[d]}(\bm{p}{\bm{x}},\bm{p}{\bm{y}})
.
∎

Now define for every
x\in X
,
\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}
and
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle U_{d}(x,\sigma,\varepsilon)=\{y\in X:F_{\sigma,d}(x,y)<\varepsilon\}.

When there is no ambiguity, we write
U(x,\sigma,\varepsilon)
instead of
U_{d}(x,\sigma,\varepsilon)
. We will show that these sets form a basis for a topology. To prove this, we first need the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.18

(see
[
10
, Chapter IX]
)
.

Given a uniformity
\mathcal{U}
on a set
X
, there is a family of pseudometrics on
X
such that the uniformity defined by this family is identical to
\mathcal{U}
.

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}
,
\varepsilon>0
, and let
(x,y)\in uX\times uX
be such that
y\in U(x,\sigma,\varepsilon)
. Then there are
\rho\in\Sigma_{d}
and
\delta>0
such that
U(y,\rho,\delta)\subseteq U(x,\sigma,\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

Since
(x^{[d]}_{*},y^{[d]}_{*})
is a
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
-minimal point, it follows that
Z=\overline{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}(x^{[d]}_{*},y^{[d]}_{*})}
is a
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
-minimal set. Let
c>0
be such that
F_{\sigma,d}(x,y)<c<\varepsilon
and let
V=\{\bm{z}\in Z:\sigma(\bm{z})<c\}
. Since
F_{\sigma,d}(x,y)<c
, we obtain that
V\neq\emptyset
. Therefore, by minimality, there exist
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
such that
Z=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}^{-1}V
.

Let
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Since
f_{i}
and
\sigma
are uniformity continuous, it follows that there exists
\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{U}_{X^{[d]}_{*}}
such that
\sigma(f_{i}{\bm{x}}_{1},f_{i}{\bm{x}}_{2})<\varepsilon-c
whenever
({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{x}}_{2})\in\alpha_{i}
. By
Lemma

3.18
, there exist
\rho_{i}\in\Sigma_{d}
and
\delta_{i}>0
such that
({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{x}}_{2})\in\alpha_{i}
whenever
\rho_{i}({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{x}}_{2})<\delta_{i}
.

Now, define
\rho=\max_{i=1,\dots,n}\rho_{i}
and
\delta=\min_{i=1,\dots,n}\delta_{i}
. Let
z\in U(y,\rho,\delta)
, then there exists
f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
such that
\rho(fy^{[d]}_{*},fz^{[d]}_{*})<\delta
. Therefore, we have
\sigma(f_{i}fy^{[d]}_{*},f_{i}fz^{[d]}_{*})<\varepsilon-c
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Since
f(x^{[d]}_{*},y^{[d]}_{*})\in Z
, there exists
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
f_{i}f(x^{[d]}_{*},y^{[d]}_{*})\in V
. Thus, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sigma(f_{i}fx^{[d]}_{*},f_{i}fz^{[d]}_{*})\leq\sigma(f_{i}fx^{[d]}_{*},f_{i}fy^{[d]}_{*})+\sigma(f_{i}fy^{[d]}_{*},f_{i}fz^{[d]}_{*})<c+\varepsilon-c=\varepsilon

Hence, we get that
F_{\sigma,d}(x,z)<\varepsilon
, and we conclude that
U(y,\rho,\delta)\subseteq U(x,\sigma,\varepsilon)
.
∎

Theorem 3.20
.

The family

\displaystyle\{U(x,\sigma,\varepsilon):x\in uX,\sigma\in\Sigma_{d},\varepsilon>0\}

forms a basis for a topology on
uX
, which we call the
\mathscr{F}^{[d]}
-topology.

Proof.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in uX
,
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma_{d}
and
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
be such that
U(x_{1},\sigma_{1},\varepsilon_{1})\cap U(x_{2},\sigma_{2},\varepsilon_{2})\neq\emptyset
. Let
y\in U(x_{1},\sigma_{1},\varepsilon_{1})\cap U(x_{2},\sigma_{2},\varepsilon_{2})
, and define
\sigma=\max\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\}
and
\varepsilon=\min\{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\}
. Note that

\displaystyle U(y,\sigma,\varepsilon)\subseteq U(x_{1},\sigma_{1},\varepsilon_{1})\cap U(x_{2},\sigma_{2},\varepsilon_{2}),

completing the proof.
∎

Now, to prove that the
\mathscr{F}^{[d]}
-topology coincides with the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.21
.

If a net
({\bm{x}}_{i})_{i\in I}
in
u^{[d]}_{*}X^{[d]}_{*}
and a point
{\bm{x}}
in
u^{[d]}_{*}X^{[d]}_{*}
satisfy that
F^{[d]}_{\sigma}({\bm{x}}_{i},{\bm{x}})\to 0
for every
\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}
then there exist a subnet
({\bm{x}}_{j})_{j\in J}
of
({\bm{x}}_{i})_{i\in I}
and a net
(f_{j})_{j\in J}
in
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
such that
\sigma(f_{j}{\bm{x}}_{j},f_{j}{\bm{x}})\to 0
for every
\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}
.

Proof.

Let
J=\Sigma_{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times I
, note that
J
is a directed set, where
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is endowed with the inverse order (that
\epsilon_{1}\succ\epsilon_{2}
if
\epsilon_{1}\leq\epsilon_{2}
). For each
j=(\sigma,\epsilon,i)\in J
, choose
\phi(j)\geq i
in
I
such that
F^{[d]}_{\sigma}({\bm{x}}_{\phi(j)},{\bm{x}})<\epsilon
. Then there exists
f_{j}\in{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
such that
\sigma(f_{j}{\bm{x}}_{\phi(j)},f_{j}{\bm{x}})<\epsilon
. Let
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{d}
and
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, for all
i\in I
we have

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}(f_{j}{\bm{x}}_{\phi(j)},f_{j}{\bm{x}})\leq\sigma(f_{j}{\bm{x}}_{\phi(j)},f_{j}{\bm{x}})<\epsilon<\epsilon^{\prime}

for every
j=(\sigma,\epsilon,i)\succ(\sigma^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},i)
. This proves the desired result.
∎

Remark 3.22
.

Note that, directly from the definition of the
\mathscr{F}^{[d]}
-topology, if
(x_{i})_{i}
is a net in
uX
and
(f_{i})_{i}
is a net in
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
, and there exists
x\in uX
such that
\sigma(f_{i}x_{i*}^{[d]},f_{i}x^{[d]}_{*})\to 0
for every
\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}
then the net
(x_{i})_{i}
converges to

with respect to the
\mathscr{F}^{[d]}
-topology.

Theorem 3.23
.

The
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology coincides with the
\mathscr{F}^{[d]}
-topology on
uX
.

Proof.

Let
A\subseteq uX
and let
x\in\mathrm{cl}_{\mathscr{F}^{[d]}}(A)
, where
\mathrm{cl}_{\mathscr{F}^{[d]}}
denotes the closure operator with respect to the
\mathscr{F}^{[d]}
-topology. Then, there exists a net
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
in
A
convergent to
x\in uX
with respect to the
\mathscr{F}^{[d]}
-topology. By
Lemma

3.21
, there exist a subnet
(x_{j})_{j\in J}
of
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
and a net
(f_{j})_{j\in J}
in
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
such that
\sigma(f_{j}x_{j*}^{[d]},f_{j}x^{[d]}_{*})\to 0
for every
\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}
.

Passing to a subnet if necessary, assume that
f_{j}x_{j*}^{[d]}\to{\bm{x}}
and
f_{j}\to f
for some
{\bm{x}}\in X^{[d]}_{*}
and
f\in\beta{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
. Therefore, we have
\sigma({\bm{x}},fx^{[d]}_{*})=0
for every
\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}
, and hence
{\bm{x}}=fx^{[d]}_{*}
. In particular, we get that
x^{[d]}_{*}=(u^{[d]}_{*}fu^{[d]}_{*})^{-1}{\bm{x}}
. Moreover, since
{\bm{x}}\in fu^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A_{k})
for every
k\in J
, where
A_{k}=\{x_{j}:j\geq k\}
, it follows that

\displaystyle x^{[d]}_{*}\in(u^{[d]}_{*}fu^{[d]}_{*})^{-1}(fu^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A_{k}))\subseteq(u^{[d]}_{*}fu^{[d]}_{*})^{-1}fu^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A_{k})=u^{[d]}_{*}\circ_{{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}}\Delta^{[d]}_{*}(A_{k}).

Hence, after passing to a subnet if necessary, we obtain
x_{j}\xrightarrow{\uptau^{[d]}}x
, which implies that
x\in\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)
. Therefore, we conclude that
\mathrm{cl}_{\mathscr{F}^{[d]}}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)
.

Conversely, let
x\in\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)
. Then there exist a net
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
in
A
and a net
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
in
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
such that
f_{i}\to u^{[d]}_{*}
and
f_{i}x_{i*}^{[d]}\to x^{[d]}_{*}
. Therefore, we have
\sigma(f_{i}x_{i*}^{[d]},f_{i}x^{[d]}_{*})\to 0
for every
\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}
, and thus
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
converge to
x
with respect to the
\mathscr{F}^{[d]}
-topology. Hence, we conclude that
\operatorname{cl}_{\uptau^{[d]}}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{cl}_{\mathscr{F}^{[d]}}(A)
.
∎

3.2.
An algebraic characterization of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}

In this subsection we show an algebraic characterization of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
, for abelian actions, using the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology. Throughout, we assume that in every system
(X,T)
, the acting groups
T
is an abelian group.

Theorem 3.24
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)=\{(x,vgx):x\in X,v\in J(M),g\in H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(G)\}.

Proof.

By
Proposition

3.14
and
Theorem

2.3
, it is enough to prove that

\displaystyle\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)\subseteq\{(x,vgx):x\in X,v\in J(M),g\in H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(G)\}.

We first consider the case
X=M
. Let
(g,u)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(M)
with
g\in G
. Then there exists
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
a net in
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}
and
(g_{i})_{i\in I}
a net in
M
such that

\displaystyle(f_{i}g_{i}^{[d]},f_{i}u^{[d]})\to(g,u^{[d]}_{*},u,u^{[d]}_{*}).

Since
G
is dense in
M
, we may assume that
(g_{i})_{i\in I}\subseteq G
. By
Lemma

3.5
, we have
g_{i}\xrightarrow{\uptau^{[d]}}g
. Let
\sigma
be a pseudometric on
M^{[d]}_{*}
. Since
F_{\sigma}^{[d]}
is upper semicontinuous and
{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}_{*}
-invariant, it follows that

\displaystyle\limsup F_{\sigma,d}(g_{i},u)=\limsup F_{\sigma}^{[d]}(f_{i*}g_{i*}^{[d]},f_{i*}u^{[d]}_{*})\leq F^{[d]}_{\sigma}(u^{[d]}_{*},u^{[d]}_{*})=0.

Thus, we obtain that
g_{i}\xrightarrow{\uptau^{[d]}}u
, and hence
g\in H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(G)
.

Now, let
(p,q)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(M)
, note that
(uq^{-1}p,u)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(M)
since
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(M)
is invariant. Therefore, we have
up\in uqH_{\uptau^{[d]}}(G)
, and we conclude that
p=vqg
for some
v\in J(M)
and
g\in H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(G)
.

For the general case, it follows from the fact that
\pi\times\pi(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(M))=\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
, where
\pi:M\to X
is a factor map.
∎

A direct consequence is the following algebraic characterization of systems of order
d
.

Corollary 3.25
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then,
(X,T)
is a system of order
d
if and only if
(X,T)
is a distal system and
H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(G)\subseteq{\mathfrak{G}}(X)
. Moreover,
{\mathfrak{G}}(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))=H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(G){\mathfrak{G}}(X)
.

Using the algebraic characterization, we give an alternative proof of the following theorem without using the gluing property of the dynamical cube in the distal case.

Theorem 3.26
.

Let
d\geq 2
be an integer and let
(X,T)
be a system of order
d
. Then the following is a sequence of group factors:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(X,T)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d-1]}(X),T)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(X/\mathbb{RP}^{[1]}(X),T)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\{\cdot\}}}

Proof.

We first show that
\pi_{d-1}:X\to X/\mathbb{RP}^{[d-1]}(X)
is an equicontinuous factor. Let
(x,y)\in Q(\mathbb{RP}^{[d-1]}(X))
, and let
\alpha\in\mathcal{U}_{X}
. Consider
\beta\in\mathcal{U}_{X}
such that
\beta\subseteq\alpha
and
\beta^{2}\subseteq\alpha
. Since
(x,y)\in Q(\mathbb{RP}^{[d-1]}(X))
, there exist
(x_{1},y_{1})\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d-1]}(X)
and
t\in T
such that
(x,x_{1}),(y,y_{1}),(tx_{1},ty_{1})\in\beta
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{U}_{X}
, with
\gamma\subseteq\beta
, be such that
(a,b)\in\gamma
implies
(ta,tb)\in\alpha
. Since
(x_{1},y_{1})\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d-1]}(X)
, there exist
x_{2},y_{2}\in X
and
f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{[d-1]}(X)
such that
(x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2}),(f_{\epsilon}x_{2},f_{\epsilon}y_{2})\in\gamma
for each
\epsilon\neq\emptyset
. Therefore, we have
(x,x_{2}),(y,y_{2})\in\beta^{2}
and
(tf_{\epsilon}x_{2},tf_{\epsilon}y_{2})\in\alpha
for each
\epsilon\neq\emptyset
. Define
f^{\prime}=(f,t^{[d-1]}f)\in{\mathcal{F}}^{[d]}
. Then
(x,y_{2}),(y,y_{2}),(f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}x_{2},f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}y_{2})\in\alpha
for each
\varepsilon\neq\emptyset
, and hence
(x,y)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)=\Delta
. Thus, we conclude that
\pi_{d-1}
is equicontinuous.

Note that, by
Theorem

3.24
, we have

\displaystyle H_{\uptau^{[d-1]}}(G)=\{g\in G:(u,g)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d-1]}(M)\}.

In particular, we deduce that
H_{\uptau^{[d-1]}}(G)
is a normal subgroup. Furthermore, since
H_{\uptau^{[d-1]}}(G)
is a normal subgroup and
X
is a distal system, by
Theorem

2.9
and
Corollary

3.25
, it follows that
\pi_{d-1}
is a group factor.
∎

We expect that
Theorem

3.24
also holds for systems satisfying the Bronstein condition. This would imply that
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
is an equivalence relation for such systems, thereby providing a positive answer to a question of Gutman, Glasner, and Ye
[
30
, Question 10.5]
. The main difficulties in proving this using the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology, rather than the
\uptau
-topology, are the following. First, right multiplication and inversion are not homeomorphisms with respect to the
\uptau^{[d]}
-topology. Second, when

, one has
(\pi^{-1}(x_{0}))^{[d]}_{*}={\mathfrak{G}}(X)^{[d]}_{*}
instead of
\Delta^{[d]}_{*}({\mathfrak{G}}(X))
, where

is the natural factor map, this creates a difficulty, since the definition of the

-topology requires elements of the form

.

3.3.
A conjecture on
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]})
and its consequences

In this subsection, we state a conjecture that gives an algebraic description of the smallest closed invariant equivalence relation containing
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
for any group action, not necessarily abelian. We also show some consequences, assuming that the conjecture is true.

Let
R
be a relation on a system
(X,T)
, and
\mathcal{A}(R)
denote the smallest closed, invariant equivalence relation containing
R
.

Every system
(X,T)
possesses a maximal distal factor. Moreover, there exists a closed, invariant equivalence relation
S_{dis}(X)
such that the quotient
X/S_{dis}(X)
is the maximal distal factor of
X
,
S_{dis}(X)
is called the
distal structure relation
of
X
. Similarly, for every system
(X,T)
and
d\geq 1
, there exists a closed, invariant equivalence relation
S_{d}(X)
such that the quotient
X/S_{d}(X)
is the maximal factor of order
d
of
X
. Moreover, it is straightforward to see that
S_{d}(X)=\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))
, and consequently
M/\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(M))
is the maximal minimal system of order
d
.

Let
M_{dis}
be the maximal distal factor of
M
and let
D={\mathfrak{G}}(M_{dis})
. The group
D
is a
\uptau
-closed normal subgroup of
G
and is generated by
\{g\in G:(p,gp)\in\overline{P(M)}\text{ for each }p\in M\}
. Moreover,
D=\{g\in G:(p,gp)\in S_{dis}(M)\}
. For more details on this group, see
[
4
]
.

Conjecture 3.27
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))=\{(x,vhgx):x\in X,h\in H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(G),g\in D,v\in J(M)\}.

The conjecture is known to be true in the case
d=1
. This follows from the results in
[
4
,
68
]
.

To prove
3.27
, it is enough to prove it in the distal case. More precisely, if
(X,T)
is a distal minimal system, then it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))=\{(x,vhgx):x\in X,h\in H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(G),v\in J(M)\}.

Theorem 3.28
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Assume that
3.27
holds for distal systems. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))=\{(x,vhgx):x\in X,h\in H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(G),g\in D,v\in J(M)\}.

Proof.

Let
(x,y)\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))
. Since
X_{dis}/\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X_{dis}))
and
X/\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))
are isomorphic, it follows that
(u\pi(x),u\pi(y))\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X_{dis}))
, where
\pi:X\to X_{dis}
is the quotient map. Since
3.27
is true for
X_{dis}
, there exists
h\in H_{\uptau^{[d]}}(G)
such that
u\pi(y)=uh\pi(x)
. Since

, there exists

such that

. Now, let

be such that

\displaystyle y=vuy=vuhrx=vhrx.

Now, let

,

,
g\in D
and
v\in J(M)
. By

, it follows that

(x,vdhx)\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X))

∎

We now show some consequences, assuming that

In this case,

Theorem 3.29
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)

, there exists

,

and

we have

∎

Another consequence is the following characterization of

(X,T)

\displaystyle RS=\{(x,z):(x,y)\in R,(y,z)\in S\text{ for some }y\}

Recurrence sets and
\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}

4.1.
Correlation for finitely generated abelian actions

In this subsection, we assume that in every system
(X,T)
, the acting group
T
is a finitely generated abelian group.

Let
H
be a countable topological group, written with multiplicative notation. A
Følner sequence
in
H
is a sequence
\Phi=(\Phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
of nonempty finite subsets of
H
such that for every
h\in H
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|h\Phi_{N}\Delta\Phi_{N}|}{|\Phi_{N}|}=0.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
be an integer and let
(X=L/\Gamma,T)
be a
d
-step nilsystem. Let
\phi
be a continuous function on
X
and
b\in X
. The sequence
\{\phi(tb)\}_{t\in T}
is called a
basic
d
-step nilsequence
. A
d
-step nilsequence
is a uniform limit of basic
d
-step nilsequences.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\{a_{g}\}_{g\in H}
be a bounded sequence indexed by elements of a countable amenable group
H
. We say that
(a_{g})_{g\in H}
is a
null-sequence
if it tends to zero in uniform density, that is,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{g\in H}|a_{g}|=0

for any Følner
\Phi
of
H
.

In this subsection, we show that for a finitely generated abelian group
T
, the sequence
(c(t))_{t\in T}
is the sum of a null-sequence and a
d
-step nilsequence, where

\displaystyle c(t)=\int f(x)\cdot f(tx)\cdot\ldots\cdot f(t^{d}x)\,d\mu(x).

We follow the approach of
[
8
,
52
]
.

In
[
52
]
, Leibman studied multiparameter polynomial multiple correlations for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-actions. The results of that paper also hold for actions of finitely generated abelian groups. In particular, we will use the following decomposition result for nilsystems.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
52
, Theorem 4.3]
)
.

Let
(X,T)
be a
d
-step nilsystem, let
Y
be a subnilmanifold of
X
and let
f\in C(X)
. Then the sequence
c(t)=\int_{tY}fd\mu_{tY}
,
t\in T
, can be decomposed into a sum of a null-sequence and a
d
-step basic nilsequence.

We will also use the following result.

Lemma 4.4

(
[
9
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

Let
(a_{g})_{g\in H}
be a bounded family of elements of a Hilbert space indexed by elements of a countable amenable group
H
and let
\Phi
be a Følner sequence in
H
. If

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|^{2}}\left(\limsup_{M\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{M}|}\sum_{g\in\Phi_{M}}\sum_{h,k\in\Phi_{N}}\langle a_{hg},a_{kg}\rangle\right)=0

Then

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left\|\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{g\in\Phi_{N}}a_{g}\right\|^{2}=0

Using this version of the van der Corput lemma, we obtain the following result, which is well known for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions (
[
40
, Theorem 12.1]
).

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be an ergodic measure preserving system, let
d\geq 1
be an integer and let
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
be bounded functions on
X
. If at least one of these functions has zero conditional expectation on
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}
then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{t\in\Phi_{N}}f_{0}\cdot f_{1}\circ t\cdot f_{2}\circ t^{2}\cdots f_{d}\circ t^{d}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0

for any Følner
\Phi
of
T
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction. For
d=1
, assume, without loss of generality, that
\lvert\!|\!|f_{1}|\!|\!\rvert_{1}=0
, by the ergodic theorem, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{t\in\Phi_{N}}f_{0}\cdot f_{1}\circ t\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\left\|\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{t\in\Phi_{N}}f_{1}\circ t\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\to\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\lvert\!|\!|f_{1}|\!|\!\rvert_{1}=0

Now, let
d\geq 1
and assume that the statement is true for
d
. Let
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{d+1}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Set

\displaystyle u_{t}=\prod_{j=0}^{d+1}f_{j}\circ t^{j}.

By
Lemma

4.4
, it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\limsup_{M\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{M}|}\sum_{t\in\Phi_{M}}\langle u_{ht},u_{kt}\rangle=0

for every
k,h\in T
.

Let
k,h\in T
,

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{M}|}\sum_{t\in\Phi_{M}}\langle u_{ht},u_{kt}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{M}|}\sum_{t\in\Phi_{M}}\int\prod_{j=0}^{d+1}f_{j}(h^{j}t^{j}x)\cdot f_{j}(k^{j}t^{j}x)\,d\mu(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int\left(f_{0}(hx)\cdot f_{0}(kx)\right)\cdot\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{M}|}\sum_{t\in\Phi_{M}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d+1}(f_{j}\circ h^{j}\cdot f_{j}\circ k^{j})(t^{j}x)\right)\,d\mu(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{0}\circ h\cdot f_{0}\circ k\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\cdot\left\|\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{M}|}\sum_{t\in\Phi_{M}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d+1}(f_{j}\circ h^{j}\cdot f_{j}\circ k^{j})\circ t^{j}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=\|f_{0}\circ h\cdot f_{0}\circ k\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\cdot\left\|\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{M}|}\sum_{t\in\Phi_{M}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d+1}(f_{j}\circ h^{j}\cdot f_{j}\circ k^{j})\circ t^{j-1}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

and by the inductive assumption,

\displaystyle\left\|\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{M}|}\sum_{t\in\Phi_{M}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d+1}(f_{j}\circ h^{j}\cdot f_{j}\circ k^{j})\circ t^{j-1}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\to 0,

completing the proof.
∎

From this theorem we obtain the following result.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be an ergodic measure preserving system, let
d\geq 1
be an integer and let
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
be bounded functions on
X
. If at least one of these functions has zero conditional expectation on
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d-1}
then

\displaystyle\int f_{0}(x)f_{1}(tx)f_{2}(t^{2}x)\cdots f_{d}(t^{d}x)\,d\mu(x)

converges to zero in uniform density.

Proof.

Let
\Phi
be a Følner sequence of
T
and let
\mu\times\mu=\int_{Z}\mu_{s}\,dm(s)
be the ergodic decomposition
\mu\times\mu
under
T
, where
Z
is the Kronecker factor and
m
is the Haar measure. Note that for
m
-almost every
s\in Z
, one of the functions
f_{0}\otimes f_{0},f_{1}\otimes f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\otimes f_{d}
has zero conditional expectation on
{\mathcal{Z}}_{d}(X\times X,\mu_{s},T)
, and therefore, by
Theorem

4.5
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{t\in\Phi_{N}}\int f_{0}(x)f_{0}(x^{\prime})f_{1}(tx)f_{1}(tx^{\prime})\dots f_{d}(t^{d}x)f_{d}(t^{d}x^{\prime})\,d\mu_{s}(x,x^{\prime})=0.

Integrating with respect to
s
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{t\in\Phi_{N}}\left(\int f_{0}(x)f_{1}(tx)\dots f_{d}(t^{d}x)\,d\mu(x)\right)^{2}=0.

∎

Finally, we can prove the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be an ergodic measure preserving system, let
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. The sequence
(c(t))_{t\in T}
is the sum of a null-sequence and a
d
-step nilsequence, where

\displaystyle c_{f}(t)=\int f(x)\cdot f(tx)\cdot\ldots\cdot f(t^{d}x)\,d\mu(x).

Proof.

We follow the steps of the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 1.9]
.

Let
\widetilde{f}=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(f|{\mathcal{Z}}_{d})
. By
Corollary

4.6
, the sequence
(c_{f}(t)-c_{\widetilde{f}}(t))_{t\in T}
converges to zero in uniform density. Therefore, it suffices to prove the theorem for the function
\widetilde{f}
, so we can assume that
f
is measurable with respect to
Z_{d}(X)
. By
Theorem

2.1
,
Z_{d}(X)
is the inverse limit of a sequence of ergodic
d
-step nilsystems. Thus, there exists a factor
X^{\prime}
of
Z_{d}(X)
such that

\displaystyle\|f-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(f|{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime})\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\leq 1/((d+1)r),

where
r\geq 1
is an integer. Consequently, we have
|c_{f}(t)-c_{f^{\prime}}(t)|\leq 1/r
, where
t\in T
and
f^{\prime}=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(f\mid{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime})
. Applying
Theorem

4.3
to the nilmanifold
X^{\prime d+1}
, the diagonal subnilmanifold
Y=\{(x,\dots,x):x\in X^{\prime}\}
and the function
F(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=f^{\prime}(x_{0})\cdot f^{\prime}(x_{1})\cdot f^{\prime}(x_{2})\cdots f^{\prime}(x_{d})
, we have that
c_{f^{\prime}}
can be decomposed as a sum of a
d
-step nilsequence and a null sequence. Therefore,

\displaystyle c_{f}(t)=a_{r}(t)+b_{r}(t)+c_{r}(t)

where
|a_{r}|\leq 1/r
,
b_{r}
is a null sequence and
c_{r}
is a
d
-step basic nilsequence. From
[
8
, Lemma 1.11]
, it follows that
(c_{r})_{r}
converges uniformly to some sequence
(c(t))_{t\in T}
, and we conclude the desired result.
∎

4.2.
\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}
and recurrence sets

In this subsection, following the approach of
[
45
]
, we study
\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}
via recurrence sets.

We first give the definitions of the recurrence sets that we will use.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
d\geq 1
be an integer and
T
be a topological group.

(1)

We say that
S\subseteq T
is a set of
d
-recurrence
if for every measure preserving system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
and for every
A\in{\mathcal{X}}
with
\mu(A)>0
, there exists
t\in S
such that
\mu(A\cap tA\cap\dots\cap t^{d}A)>0
.

(2)

We say that
S\subseteq T
is a set of
d
-topological recurrence
if for every minimal system
(X,T)
and for every nonempty open subset
U
of
X
, there exists
t\in S
such that
U\cap tU\cap\dots\cap t^{d}U\neq\emptyset
.

(3)

We say that
S\subseteq T
is a Nil
d
Bohr
0
-set, if there are a
d
-step nilsystem
(X,T)
,
x\in X
and a neighborhood of
x
such that
N(x,U)\subseteq S
, where
N(x,U)=\{t\in T:tx\in U\}
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}_{\operatorname{Poi}_{d}}
(resp.
{\mathscr{F}}_{\operatorname{Bir}_{d}}
,
{\mathscr{F}}_{d}
) be the family consisting of all sets of
d
-recurrence (resp. sets of
d
-topological recurrence, the sets of Nil
d
Bohr
0
-set).

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal system, with
T
being a finitely generated group, and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then
(x,y)\in\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X)
if and only if
N(x,U)\in{\mathscr{F}}_{d}^{*}
for each neighborhood
U
of
y
, that is that
N(x,U)\cap A\neq\emptyset
for each Nil
d
Bohr
0
-set
A
.

Proof.

First suppose that
N(x,U)\in{\mathscr{F}}_{d}^{*}
for each neighborhood
U
of
y
. Let
(X_{d},T)
be the maximal factor of order
d
of
X
and let
\pi:X\to X_{d}
be the factor map. It follows from
Theorem

2.4
that
X_{d}
is isomorphic to an inverse limit of nilsystems of order
d
. Thus, since
N(x,U)\in{\mathscr{F}}_{d}^{*}
, it follows that
N(x,U)\cap N(\pi(x),V)\neq\emptyset
for any neighborhood
V
of
\pi(x)
. Therefore, there exists a net
(t_{i})_{i\in I}
in
T
such that
t_{i}(x,\pi(x))\to(y,\pi(x))
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\pi(y)=\pi(\lim_{i}t_{i}x)=\lim_{i}t_{i}\pi(x)=\pi(x),

and hence
(x,y)\in\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X)
.

Now, suppose that
(x,y)\in\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X)
and
U
is a neighborhood of
y
. Let
(Z,T)
be a
d
-step nilsystem, let
\widetilde{z}\in Z
and let
V
be a neighborhood of
\widetilde{z}
. Define

\displaystyle W=\prod_{z\in Z}Z.

Note that
(W,T)
is a distal system since
(Z,T)
is distal. Choose
\omega^{*}\in W
such that
\omega^{*}(z)=z
for all
z\in Z
and set
Z_{\infty}=\overline{T\omega^{*}}
. Observe that
(Z_{\infty},T)
is a minimal distal system. In particular, for any
\omega\in Z_{\infty}
, there exists
g\in G
such that
\omega(z)=g\omega^{*}(z)=gz
, and consequently
\omega(g^{-1}\widetilde{z})=\widetilde{z}
. Therefore, there exists
z_{\omega}\in Z
such that
\omega(z_{\omega})=\widetilde{z}
.

Let
(Y,T)
be a minimal subsystem of
(X\times Z_{\infty},T)
and let
\pi_{X}:Y\to X
be the natural coordinate projection. In particular,
\pi_{X}
is a factor map between minimal systems. Since
(x,y)\in\mathbb{NRP}^{[d]}(X)
, by

S_{j}
-minimal point. Let
{\bm{x}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in N_{d}(X)
and let
y\in\pi^{-1}(\pi(x_{j}))
. By
Theorem

5.4
, there exists a net
({\bm{x}}_{i})_{i\in I}
in
N_{d}(X)
of
S_{j}
-minimal points
{\bm{x}}_{i}=({\bm{x}}_{i,1},\dots,{\bm{x}}_{i,d})\to{\bm{x}}
. By the definition of

, we have
{\bm{x}}_{i,j}=x_{j}
. Since

is an open map, it follows that

\displaystyle\pi^{-1}(\pi({\bm{x}}_{i,j}))\to\pi^{-1}(\pi(x_{j})).

Therefore, there exists a net

in
\pi^{-1}(\pi({\bm{x}}_{i,j}))
with

. Since
{\bm{x}}_{i}
is a

-minimal point and
\pi({\bm{x}}_{i,j})=\pi(y_{i})
, we have that

\displaystyle({\bm{x}}_{i,1},{\bm{x}}_{i,2},\dots,{\bm{x}}_{i,j-1},y_{i},{\bm{x}}_{i,j+1},\dots,{\bm{x}}_{i,d})\in N_{d}(X).

Since

is closed, we obtain

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{j-1},y,x_{j+1},\dots,x_{d})\in N_{d}(X).

Hence, we conclude that
(\pi^{(d)})^{-1}(\pi^{(d)}({\bm{x}}))\subseteq N_{d}(X)
.
∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime}}}

, there is a dense

for each

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X_{d-1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X_{d-1}^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Applications

First, we note an application of

. In

, we proved that the maximal distal factor of a dynamical cube is the dynamical cube of the maximal distal factor. The main theorem used in that proof is
[

, Proposition 5.16]
. By

, the same result holds for any group action. We omit the proof, since it is exactly the same.

Theorem 5.22
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then,
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X_{dis})
is the maximal distal factor of
\bm{Q}^{[d]}(X)
, where
X_{dis}
is the maximal distal factor of

.

We now give some applications of the topological characteristic factor along arithmetic progressions. Throughout, we assume that in every system
(X,T)
, the acting group
T
is a finitely generated abelian group. First, we need the following definition, which was studied in
[
32
]
for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions.

Definition 5.23
.

Let
(X,T)
be a system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. We say that
(x,y)\in X\times X
is a
regionally proximal pair of order
d
along arithmetic progressions
if there are nets
(t_{i})_{i\in I}\subseteq T
,
((x_{i},y_{i}))_{i\in I}\subseteq X\times X
and
a_{*}\in X^{d}
such that

\displaystyle(x_{i},t_{i}x_{i},t_{i}^{2}x_{i},\dots,t_{i}^{d}x_{i},y_{i},t_{i}y_{i},t_{i}^{2}y_{i}\dots,t_{i}^{d}y_{i})\to(x,a_{*},y,a_{*}).

The set of all such pairs is denoted by
\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}(X)
and is called the
regionally proximal relation of order
d
along arithmetic progressions
.

We next give a characterization of the minimal points of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
.

Theorem 5.24
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. If
(x,y)\in X\times X
is a minimal point, then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

(x,y)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
.

(2)

\{x,y\}^{d+2}\subseteq N_{d+2}(X)
.

(3)

(x,y)\in\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}(X)
.

Proof.

Clearly
(2)\Rightarrow(3)\Rightarrow(1)
. Thus, it suffices to show that
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. By
Proposition

2.5
,
[
45
, Proposition A.2.3]
and
[
54
, Lemma 3.6]
, we may assume that
(X,T)
is a metric system.

By
Lemma

5.19
, there exists a dense
G_{\delta}
set
X_{0}
of
X
such that
(\pi^{(d+2)})^{-1}(\pi^{(d+2)}(z^{(d+2)}))\subseteq N_{d+2}(X)
for every
z\in X_{0}
, where
\pi:X\to X_{d}
is the factor map onto the maximal factor of order
d
. Let
x^{\prime}\in X_{0}
and let
p\in M
be such that
x=px^{\prime}
. Since
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
is invariant, we have
(x^{\prime},p^{-1}y)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\{x^{\prime},p^{-1}y\}^{d+2}\subseteq(\pi^{(d+2)})^{-1}(\pi^{(d+2)}({x^{\prime}}^{(d+2)}))\subseteq N_{d+2}(X).

Since
N_{d+2}(X)
is
T
-invariant, it follows that
\{x,y\}^{d+2}\subseteq N_{d+2}(X)
.
∎

Note that using the same proof it is possible to obtain the following result.

Corollary 5.25
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Suppose that the factor map from
X
to its maximal factor of order
d
is open, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

(x,y)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
.

(ii)

\{x,y\}^{d+2}\subseteq N_{d+2}(X)
.

(iii)

(x,y)\in\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}(X)
.

In particular
Corollary

5.25
proves that
\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}(X)=\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
for distal systems.

One consequence of
Theorem

5.24
is that
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
is the smallest closed invariant equivalence relation containing
\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}
. To prove this, we need the following lemma, which was proved for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions, but the same proof works for finitely generated abelian actions.

Lemma 5.26

(
[
32
, Theorem 3.8]
)
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,T)
be a proximal factor between minimal systems and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then
R_{\pi}\subseteq\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}(X)
.

Theorem 5.27
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Then
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
is the smallest closed invariant equivalence relation containing
\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}(X))
denote the smallest closed invariant equivalence relation containing
\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}
. Clearly
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}(X))\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
.

Let
(x,y)\in\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}(X)
. By
Theorem

5.24
, it follows that
(y,vx)\in\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}(X)
, where
v
is a minimal idempotent in
M
such that
vy=y
. Furthermore, by
Theorem

5.24
and
Lemma

5.26
, we deduce that
\mathbb{P}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}(X))
. In particular,
(x,vx)\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}(X))
. Since
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}(X))
is an equivalence relation, we conclude that
(x,y)\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}(X))
.
∎

Now, we use the characterization of the minimal points of
\mathbb{RP}^{[d]}
to study independence.

Definition 5.28
.

Let
(X,T)
be a system. Given a tuple
\mathscr{A}=(U_{0},U_{1},\dots,U_{k})
of subsets of
X
, we say that
F\subseteq T
is an independence set for
\mathscr{A}
if for any non-empty finite set
J\subseteq T
and any
s=(s(j):j\in J)\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}^{J}
we have

\displaystyle\bigcap_{j\in J}jU_{s(j)}\neq\emptyset.

We shall denote the collection of all independence sets for
\mathscr{A}
by
\operatorname{Ind}(U_{0},U_{1},\dots,U_{k})
.

Definition 5.29
.

Let
(X,T)
be a system and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. A pair
(x_{1},x_{2})\in X\times X
is called an
\operatorname{Ind}_{ap}
-pair of order
d
if for every pair of neighborhoods
U_{1},U_{2}
of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
respectively, there exists some
t\in T\setminus\{e\}
, where
e
is the identity of
T
, such that
\{t,t^{2},\dots,t^{d}\}\in\operatorname{Ind}(U_{1},U_{2})
.

A pair
(x_{1},x_{2})\in X\times X
is called an
\operatorname{Ind}_{ap}
-pair if for every integer
d\geq 1
,
(x_{1},x_{2})
is an
\operatorname{Ind}_{ap}
-pair of order
d
.

Denote by
\operatorname{Ind}_{ap}(X)
the set of all
\operatorname{Ind}_{ap}
-pairs of order
d
. Note that
\operatorname{Ind}_{ap}(X)\subseteq\mathbb{AP}^{[d]}(X)
, for every
d\geq 1
being an integer.

The following characterization of
\operatorname{Ind}_{ap}(X)
was proved for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions, but its proof also works for abelian group actions.

Lemma 5.30

(
[
12
, Lemma 7.6]
)
.

Let
(X,T)
be a system and
(x_{1},x_{2})\in X\times X\setminus\Delta(X)
. Then
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\operatorname{Ind}_{ap}(X)
if and only if for every integer
d\geq 1
,
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}^{d}\subseteq N_{d}(X)
.

By
Theorem

5.24
and
Lemma

5.30
, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 5.31
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal system. If
(x_{1},x_{2})\in X\times X
is a minimal point, then
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{RP}^{[\infty]}(X)
if and only if
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\operatorname{Ind}_{ap}(X)
.

By
Proposition

5.31
, one can prove that every minimal system without nontrivial
\operatorname{Ind}_{ap}
-pairs is a proximal extension of its maximal factor of order
\infty
. The following theorem gives a refinement of this result.

Theorem 5.32
.

Let
(X,T)
be a minimal metric system without nontrivial
\operatorname{Ind}_{ap}
-pairs. Then
(X,T)
is an almost one-to-one extension of its maximal factor of order
\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:X\to X_{\infty}
be the factor map onto its maximal factor of order
\infty
. By
Lemma

5.18
, there exists a dense
G_{\delta}
set
X_{0}
of
X
such that, for every integer
d\geq 1
and every
x\in X_{0}
,
(\pi^{(d)})^{-1}(\pi^{(d)}(x^{(d)}))\subseteq N_{d}(X)
.

Let
x\in X_{0}
and
y\in\pi^{-1}(x)
. Then

\displaystyle\{x,y\}^{d}\subseteq(\pi^{(d)})^{-1}(\pi^{(d)}(x^{(d)}))\subseteq N_{d}(X)

for every integer
d\geq 1
.

It follows from
Lemma

5.30
that
x=y
, and hence
\pi
is almost one-to-one.
∎
