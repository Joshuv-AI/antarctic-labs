A semantic-aided abnormal broadcast content analysis method
Authors: Qi Zhang, Fengming Zhou, Yuzhi Zhang, Huan Wang, Jianxin Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abnormal broadcast activities are becoming more and more rampant, since it is easy and cheap to achieve a radio broadcasting station. These abnormal broadcasts disseminate illegal information, which can have serious negative impacts on society. However, detecting and tracing these abnormal broadcast contents can be challenging as they are often scattered and difficult to identify. In this article, we propose a semantic-aided analysis method for broadcast content (SAAM), which is based on Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) to construct a model for detecting and analyzing abnormal broadcasts. The LDA model intelligently captures the latent semantic topics implied in the broadcasting content, enabling the establishment of criteria for identifying anomaly and facilitating to investigate. The analysis method further exploits the Apriori algorithm to explore the associations between the content of abnormal broadcast and relevant organizations based on semantic topics from LDA. This allows for detailed analysis of abnormal broadcasts as well as tracing relevant organizations involved in such activities. In the experiment, the abnormal broadcast data is collected to verify the accuracy of SAAM in detecting and tracing abnormal broadcast. The experiment results show that, the accuracy rate of SAAM in detecting abnormal broadcasts is more than 0.91 and the tracing-accuracy is more than 0.8.

Introduction

With the advancement of national information construction, broadcast remains one of the most popular methods for multimedia communication, providing convenience to people’s lives while also providing a means for the spread of abnormal information1. The abnormal broadcast refers to an unauthorized broadcast signal that does not comply with national regulations. It is not authorized by the State Administration of Radio, Film and Television (SARFT), radio management agencies, or other management departments. It often broadcasts abnormal content and operates on the same frequency as authorized broadcast by suppressing the authorized broadcast2. At present, the radio transmitting equipment is gradually small, lightweight and its components are easy to assemble. Thus, radio is widely used by illegal organizations to spread fraud, vulgarity, pornography, cults or other abnormal broadcast content, and even installed in the border areas to preach sermons, reactionary, violent content, and so on. The content spread by abnormal broadcasts infringes on the rights and interests of the public, endangers national security, and harms the social environment3. Furthermore, abnormal broadcast transmission equipment has high-power and electromagnetic radiation, which seriously disrupts the order of electromagnetic waves, interferes with normal broadcast signals, and poses a threat to public safety.

Abnormal broadcasts often display characteristics of concealment and trick. Illegitimate broadcast organizations often occupy legally authorized frequency bands to avoid detection by regulatory authorities. Moreover, in order to confuse the public, these organizations frequently update the text of abnormal content and mix it with regular programming, such as interactive communication, which results in the scattered and diversity of abnormal broadcast content. This makes it challenging to accurately define the features of abnormal content, further complicating efforts to identify abnormal broadcasts. Especially, the abnormal broadcast content released by illegal organizations lacks structured data directly related to the publishers, making it difficult to associate the corresponding organizations.

The initial approach for identifying abnormal broadcasts primarily relies on manual monitoring and public complaints, which necessitates a significant amount of manpower and equipment. However, the effectiveness of these methods in identification is unsatisfactory. Currently, the common method involves analyzing and evaluating the frequency or audio characteristics of collected abnormal broadcast bands5, as described in references3,6–12. Since abnormal broadcasts fundamentally deviate from national regulations, it is more effective to identify abnormalities based on their content itself. Nowadays, keyword matching methods are employed in abnormal content analysis by creating rules with a word list to recognize abnormal information. To determine whether the tested text contains any abnormal content, they are accomplished by the manually screening keywords and matching with the tested text, such as the solutions in references13–17. Although this method is simple and easy to implement, it requires extensive human involvement in screening abnormal keywords. Additionally, rules obtained in this way are subjective and rely heavily on expert experience. And the rules are inadequate to solve the problem of word polysemy and context. That may lead to more false positives. Furthermore, machine learning techniques are frequently utilized for identifying abnormal content18–27. Through algorithmic model training, the machine learning approach effectively mines rules for identifying and classifying content. The machine learning domain is not reliant on manual selection of keywords and can efficiently accomplish anomaly detection of content. However, some analysis processes of the machine learning resemble black boxes resulting in limited interpretability of anomalies. Therefore, it is difficult to the further fine-grained analysis of the detected abnormal broadcast. For instance, we want to discover the association between the abnormal broadcasts and the relevant organization to trace their accountability organization.

In fact, the abnormal broadcasts consist of diverse keywords and possess a central idea, despite their complex and scattered content. Therefore, by extracting the text’s topic, we can capture the main clues running throughout the entire broadcast to determine its abnormality. On the other hand, there are few characteristics indicating the accountability organization in abnormal broadcast content. However, these broadcasts released by specific organizations generally revolve around particular topics. For instance, when radio is utilized to promote an informal medicine to the public, constant insertions of small talk regarding the medicine’s efficacy and target audience occur. Therefore, through analyzing the correlation between semantic topics and accountability organizations, we can establish attribution criteria for abnormal broadcasts and related entities. Motivated by it, this article aims to develop a system that aggregates existing case of abnormal broadcasts for detecting abnormal broadcast as well as tracing the relevant organizations. We introduce a semantic aided analysis method for abnormal broadcasts (SAAM).

The basic idea behind SAAM is that we employ sematic analysis to mine the hidden broadcast topic and the keywords related to the topic. LDA is an unsupervised machine learning algorithm that is able to extract text topics and obtain keywords related to the topic. At present, LDA has achieved success in the identification of malicious emails and social media, system logs, network traffic, and other anomalies detection28. For example, Jian Yang29 uses LDA algorithm to capture potential attack intent and reconstruct APT scenarios to achieve detection of multi-stage threats over a long period of time. Similarly, LDA is able to carry out topic mining for SAAM. The obtained keywords with high probability related to the topic can serve as the objective rules for anomaly detection. After the anomaly detection, an association analysis is required to further trace abnormal broadcasts. Apriori algorithm is widely applicable and easy to generate interpretable association rules30. It has shown excellent performance in fraud detection31, network traffic analysis32, and other fields by effectively mining the association relationships between items33. Therefore, we further invoke Apriori algorithm to extract the associations between the abnormal broadcasts and the accountability organization. Finally, the abnormal broadcast is discovered and the hidden organization is exposed.

Related work

The traditional methods for identifying abnormal broadcasts primarily involve manual mobile patrol listening or relying on public complaints, which necessitate significant human and material resources, exhibit low efficiency, and are challenging to implement34. Currently, the commonly used method is to detect abnormal broadcasts based on broadcast audio features or frequency band features. One of the techniques utilizing audio feature recognition technology typically involves retrieving segments containing broadcast audio content and comparing the acoustic features of keywords to identify abnormal broadcasts. For instance, this may entail extracting speech acoustic matrix information and employing neural networks for recognition35. However, abnormal broadcasts often come with a substantial amount of background noise that significantly affects the comparison of acoustic features. Furthermore, the method for detecting anomaly broadcasts has evolved into identifying them through their frequency characteristics, such as using machine learning methods to study the spectrum information. Lin Qiuhai7 and colleagues introduced the Bayesian discriminative method, which extracts six signal features that effectively identify legal music broadcasts, legal news broadcasts, and illegal Frequency Modulation (FM) broadcasts. Chaowei Wang8 used standard Euclidean distance to extract the feature signals and then used Support Vector Machine (SVM) classifier based on clustered samples to classify the broadcast signals. Jitong Ma9 and colleagues used Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) identification network to identify abnormal broadcasts, which can be more effective in identifying abnormal broadcasts in terms of recognition accuracy, and through the special gate structure inside the LSTM framework, the identification features such as the channel state information and the Radio Frequency (RF) identification features are effectively extracted. Zhu Ye11 developed a method to detect abnormal broadcasts by reporting the characteristics of the zero-point signal spectrum at consecutive integer points. Park Dae-kyeong et al.12 proposed an unsupervised learning-based threat detection system using radio frequency signal characteristic data. Huan Wang et al.3 extract time–frequency features of radio signals and comprise an autoencoder and a Generative Adversarial Network to detect anomalous radio. The mentioned methods leverage broadcast signals for extracting and classifying features and has a good effect on identifying abnormal broadcasts signals36,37.

The aforementioned approach aims to assess the signal characteristics of broadcasts for anomaly detection, but it is more informative to distinguish anomalies based on the content of the broadcasts. Existing methods for analyzing abnormal content can be broadly categorized into two types. One kind of them employs a keyword matching technique, where abnormal keywords are manually identified in advance and compiled into a specialized lexicon. This lexicon is then utilized to compare and determine whether the tested text contains any abnormal information. For example, Dimopoulos V13 converts character comparison into state transfer to match abnormal content strings through a finite state machine; Xian et al.14 propose an improved exhaustive automaton for sensitive word recognition in order to improve the problem of low matching efficiency; Deng Yigui et al.15 propose a decision tree-based information filtering algorithm which takes into account factors other than sensitive words; Manber et al.16 proposed a WM algorithm that constructs three tables of shift, hash, and prefix by introducing a block of characters for a large jump of bad characters. Liao Qinting et al.17 proposed an improved and efficient BMHS pattern matching algorithm for fast localization of sensitive information in text content. The involvement of manual intervention is inevitable in such methods. Usually, if the recognition rules are too subjective, the accuracy of abnormal content recognition will be affected.

Another kind is to use machine learning for abnormal content detection. Lu Xiangyan18 proposed a sensitive information detection method using SVM algorithm training, which is useful for identifying unwanted information on the Internet. Tong Ying19 proposed an early warning algorithm based on deep neural network for highly sensitive information on the network. To eliminate the need for human expert intervention in feature engineering, Trivikram Muralidharan20 proposes a detection framework to detect malicious content in all email segments using deep integrated learning analysis. Zhao21 used pre-trained language model to analyzed text content to identify fake news. Che22and Birunda23 respectively prompt learning and random forest models to detect fake news. Zouak25 leveraged BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) model reveals the sematic content of spam email. Das24, Choi26, and Olivo27 respectively used LSTM and deep convolutional neural networks, SVM and autoencoder, ensemble classifier to detect spam.

Machine learning overcomes the drawbacks of traditional keyword matching methods and enables the analysis of abnormal content without relying on manual keyword selection. BERT and sBERT have the capabilities for nuanced language analysis, and they are pre-trained model. Decision Tree, Support Vector Machine (SVM), neural network and Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) are also commonly used for classification of natural language processing. Decision Tree, SVM and neural network are supervised learning methods, and they require manual labeled data for training29. LDA is an unsupervised learning topic modeling technique and does not require pre-training. Some of these machine learning algorithms often suffer from the “black box” dilemma, a lack of transparency that hinders their applicability in security contexts38. And it is difficult for them to achieve further trace. To address the issues, this paper proposes a novel approach that integrates LDA semantic fusion of the broadcast text for both anomaly detection and attribution.

Methods

This paper aims to extract the implied theme of the broadcast’s content, in order to detect its anomaly as well as mining the accountability organization. The overview of semantic-aided analysis method for abnormal broadcasts as shown in Fig. 1. The proposed method includes the following stages: preprocessing of broadcast data, identification of abnormal broadcast content, organization associating and integration of alarm information alerts.

Abnormal content is disseminated in various forms by abnormal broadcast propagators. Such contents are centered around specific topics and there are certain correlations exist between the abnormal broadcasting topics and their organizations. Based on this fact, this paper firstly automatically generates rules from historical abnormal broadcast recordings by sematic and association analysis. Then, the new broadcast is analyzed semantically and to be matched with the generated rules to achieve anomaly analysis. According to Fig. 1 the rule generation and new broadcast anomaly analysis basic operation process are explained as follow.

Fig. 1The architecture of SAAM.

The architecture of SAAM.

Firstly, the multi-source historical anomaly broadcast content is integrated and converted into text.

Then LDA algorithm is utilized to extract the topics of these broadcast text to automatically generate the detection rules from these historical broadcasts. After that, based on the topic classification from LDA the association algorithm Apriori is used to deduce the association criteria between abnormal broadcasting topics and their accountability organizations.

The pending broadcasts are also transformed into text. LDA analysis to get the topic of each broadcast text and the keywords corresponding to the topic. The topic keywords are matched with the pre-determined detection rules to detection conclusion. Finally, the detected abnormal broadcasts’ topic is matched with the organization association criteria in order to achieve the accountability institution tracing.

Data preprocessing

Firstly, the raw broadcast audio will be processed into text documents by speech recognition. The text documents will be further processed, including the removal of punctuation and special characters. Finally, the text is divided into word units to facilitate more accurate expression of the meaning of the text. Through the processing of speech recognition, data cleaning, and Chinese word segmentation in the data preprocessing module, the broadcast audio is transformed into easier-to-process word sequences for semantic analysis.

Semantic analysis model for broadcasts

After preprocessing, this paper uses semantic analysis to mine the hidden topics in the broadcast so as to realize the identification and trace of the abnormal broadcast. The LDA algorithm is currently a popular model for mining potential topics of text in the field of natural language processing and has been widely used in the fields of text mining and information retrieval34. In our method, the identification of abnormal broadcasts is based on the main idea of its content which is regarded as the feature of various types of abnormal broadcasts. Through LDA semantic analysis, the potential idea of each broadcast can be given, while the essence of the topic can be given by the probability distribution of high-probability words in the broadcast. The LDA semantic analysis can assist us in understanding the potential idea of each broadcast so that the abnormal broadcasts can be captured more accurately.

Fig. 2LDA model for broadcast document.

LDA model for broadcast document.

LDA model is a multilayer Bayesian topic model based on the bag-of-words approach, which extracts topic information using unsupervised learning techniques39. There are three levels to the LDA representation: document, topic and word. Meanwhile the LDA considers a document is produced by multiple topics with varying probabilities, and each word is generated by multiple topics with different probabilities. Both the document-topic distribution and the topic-word distribution follow the Dirichlet distribution40.

Experiment

Our experiment consists of three phases. The first stage is the broadcast dataset collection from the field in each city. In the second stage, the detection rules and tracing rules are mined. Finally, in the third stage, the performance of proposed method for abnormal broadcast analysis is presented after comparative experiment.

Datasets

As far as we know, there is no dataset about abnormal broadcast content. The dataset in this experiment is collected from several cities (Chengdu, Guangyuan, Luoyang and Mianyang) in China and all the collected radio broadcast are in the 87 ~ 108 MHz band. The historical dataset and test dataset are generated from the above collected radio broadcast. The historical dataset is used to emulate a prior abnormal broadcast. The test dataset is used to validate the performance of the proposed SAAM in detecting and tracing abnormal broadcast. Their abnormal contents are fake drugs and hospitals advertisements about eight categories: treatment of stretch marks, medicines for hypertension, medicines for eye diseases, traditional Chinese herb medicines, hospital for andrology, Nanshan Andrology Research Institute, Qiyang medicinal herbs, and ion medicines. The above advertisements are published by 8 accountability organizations respectively. We collate the above data, and convert them into text. Then we annotate their accountability organizations.

In total, the historical dataset has 327 broadcast documents, which contains eight categories of abnormal radio broadcast, as shown in Table 1. The content of abnormal broadcasts is highly repetitive day by day and the rush hour for abnormal broadcasts is from 7 p.m. to the late at night every day. There are a few normal broadcasts during this period. we emulate the broadcast data during a rush hour in a day to be the test dataset. The test dataset consists of 448 test broadcast texts, including 244 abnormal broadcast texts from the above 8 categories of topics and 204 normal texts. Comparative experiment

This section shows the performance improvement of our proposed detection method by comparing it with three benchmark approaches: Deterministic Finite Automaton (DFA) algorithm43, the Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) algorithm44 and Sentence-BERT (SBERT)45.

DFA is a commonly-used keyword matching method. It identifies anomalies by matching the segmented broadcast data with a preset sensitive lexicon, and determines whether the broadcast is abnormal based on the identification result. The CNN algorithm is commonly used in text categorization, by transforming text data into word embedding representation which is an input to the convolutional layer for feature extraction and classification. Jarrah MAM44 used CNN algorithm to detect hate speech in tweets, and the CNN algorithm is used to identify abnormal broadcasts in this section. SBERT is a model based on the Transformer for state-of-the art sentence embeddings in tasks including large-scale semantic similarity comparison, clustering and information retrieval via semantic search46.

This section uses DFA, CNN, SBERT and SAAM to detect abnormal broadcasts in the test dataset Group V listed in Table 2. In the DFA experiments, the sensitive lexicon contains 2084 anomalies downloaded from the online public dataset47, which covers most of the anomalies currently in use.

In the SAAM experiment, the detection rules are taken from Sect. 4.2.1. In the CNN experiment, after labelling the abnormal or normal broadcasts and preprocessing them, and the models are trained. And the CNN detect the abnormal broadcasts among them after the tested broadcast data are fed into the model. In the SBERT experiment, the historical dataset with 327 anomalies is fed to SBERT model after preprocessing. SBERT generates abnormal semantic vector as the abnormal rule. The tested data are embedded into vector by SBERT to calculate similarity with the rule.

In this section, for the sake of analysis and comparison, we count the detection results of SAAM and the three benchmark approaches. The detection results statistics are shown in Table 10.

Conclusions

In this paper, we developed a new semantic-aided content analysis system SAAM for the detection problem of the abnormal broadcasts. SAAM considered the semantic context to carry out a semantic correlation analysis approach for broadcasts content to detect abnormal broadcasts and trace back to its organization. The semantic analysis relying on LDA to self-discover the topics and the key words related to anomaly, thus generating the abnormal identification rules from the historical broadcast text intelligently. Then, the topics from LDA was employed to assist Apriori algorithm to capture latent associations rules with accountability organizations for the abnormal broadcast text. When detecting the broadcast, SAAM performed LDA analysis on test broadcasts, then detected anomalies and traced them according to the above rules. An experiment has been conducted to demonstrate the achievable performance of our proposed method (SAAM). By comparing it with the subjective rule method (DFA) and machine learning methods (CNN, SBERT), we illustrated the improved performance of the proposed SAAM in both identifying the anomalies as well as tracing each accountability organization.
