Theory of quantum decoherence in macroscopic topological insulators
Xian-Peng Zhang, Yan-Qing Feng, Wanxiang Feng, Yugui Yao

Abstract
Quantum decoherence–the loss of quantum coherence due to interactions with an environment–plays a central role in quantum transport, and controlling this ubiquitous yet inevitable phenomenon is essential for practical quantum technologies. Despite its importance, the microscopic mechanisms of decoherence in infinite-size topological insulators remain poorly understood. Here, we develop a comprehensive theory that quantitatively investigates how quantum decoherence shapes the quantum spin Hall effect in macroscopic topological insulators, and reveal that decoherence-induced corrections scale quadratically with impurity density. Besides, we uncover a previously unidentified mechanism of the extrinsic spin Hall effect: a second-order skew-scattering process intrinsically tied to quantum decoherence–fundamentally distinct from, yet substantially stronger than, the conventional third-order skew-scattering mechanism. Furthermore, we predict a new scaling law in which the decoherence-induced spin Hall conductivity scales quadratically with the longitudinal conductivity, providing a clear experimental signature of decoherence effects. Our results establish the essential role of decoherence in quantum transport of topological insulators and reveal that macroscopic topological insulators offer a promising platform for next-generation spintronic applications.

Conclusion–
We have developed a microscopic theory of quantum transport driven by decoherence in macroscopic topological insulators, where edge contributions are absent and the density of states vanishes at the Fermi energy. Within this framework, we quantitatively establish how quantum decoherence governs the QSHE. We further identify a previously unrecognized mechanism for the extrinsic SHE, arising from a second-order skew-scattering process intrinsically linked to decoherence-fundamentally distinct from, and parametrically stronger than, the conventional third-order mechanism. Our results highlight quantum decoherence not merely as a limiting factor, but as a fundamental driver of transport. Controlling decoherence is therefore essential, both for realizing robust quantum spin Hall transport in next-generation spintronic devices and for advancing fault-tolerant quantum computation based on topologically protected states.

D.2
Ordinary collision integral

First, we recover the standard Fermi Golden rule, based on the intraband collision integral,
\mathcal{J}^{\eta\eta}_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\delta\varrho)
. To this end, we exclude the interband correlation of the density matrix by assuming the following diagonal approximation

\displaystyle\varrho_{\boldsymbol{k}s}^{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}\simeq\delta_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}\varrho_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta_{1}}.

(178)

Then, the intraband collision term [i.e.,
\eta_{1}=\eta
and
\eta_{2}=\eta
terms of Eq. (
D
)] reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\eta\eta}_{\boldsymbol{k}s}(\delta\varrho)

\displaystyle=\pi\frac{n_{\text{i}}U^{2}}{\hbar V}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}}\left\{\delta(\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}\eta}-\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}})\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}\eta,\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}}^{s}\delta\varrho_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}s\eta^{\prime}}\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\eta}^{s}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\delta(\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}\eta})\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}\eta,\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}}^{s}\delta\varrho_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}s\eta^{\prime}}\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\eta}^{s}\right.

(179)

\displaystyle\left.-\delta(\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}\eta}-\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}})\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}\eta,\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}}^{s}\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\eta}^{s}\delta\varrho_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\delta(\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}\eta})\delta\varrho_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}\eta,\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}}^{s}\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\eta}^{s}\right\},

i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\eta\eta}_{\boldsymbol{k}s}(\varrho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}}W_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})(\delta\varrho_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}s\eta^{\prime}}-\delta\varrho_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}).

(180)

Within the diagonal approximation (

\displaystyle W_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})=2\pi n_{\text{i}}\frac{U^{2}}{\hbar}|\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}\eta,\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}}^{s}|^{2}\delta(\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}\eta}-\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}}).

(181)

D.1
)]. Then, the intraband collision term (

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\eta\eta}_{\boldsymbol{k}s,\text{ra}}(\delta\varrho)

\displaystyle=2\pi\frac{n_{\text{i}}U^{2}}{\hbar}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\cos\Theta_{k}\cos\Theta_{k^{\prime}}+\sin\Theta_{k}\sin\Theta_{k^{\prime}}\cos\theta_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{k}}\right]\delta(\epsilon_{k\eta}-\epsilon_{k^{\prime}\eta})(\delta\varrho_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}s\eta}-\delta\varrho_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}),

(183)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\eta\eta}_{\boldsymbol{k}s,\text{er}}(\delta\varrho)

\displaystyle=2\pi\frac{n_{\text{i}}U^{2}}{\hbar}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}\left[1-\cos\Theta_{k}\cos\Theta_{k^{\prime}}-\sin\Theta_{k}\sin\Theta_{k^{\prime}}\cos\theta_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{k}}\right]\delta(\epsilon_{k\eta}-\epsilon_{k^{\prime}\bar{\eta}})(\delta\varrho_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}s\bar{\eta}}-\delta\varrho_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}).

describes the contribution from the interband scattering events. Hereafter, we omit the interband ones because the interband scattering event is energy unfavorable. As a result, we attain the intraband collision integral as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\eta\eta}_{\boldsymbol{k}s}(\delta\varrho)

\displaystyle\simeq 2\pi\frac{n_{\text{i}}U^{2}}{\hbar}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\cos^{2}\Theta_{k}+\sin^{2}\Theta_{k}\cos\theta_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{k}}\right]

with

\displaystyle+\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{n_{\text{i}}\nu_{\eta}(\epsilon_{k\eta})U^{2}}{\hbar}\sin^{2}\Theta_{k}\delta\varrho_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}.

(197)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau^{0}_{k\eta}}=2\pi\frac{n_{i}\nu_{\eta}(\epsilon_{k\eta})U^{2}}{\hbar}.
