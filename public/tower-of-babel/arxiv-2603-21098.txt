Some remarks on the horizon in the dust cloud collapse
Koushiki, Włodzimierz Piechocki, Grzegorz Plewa

Abstract
We examine the existence of an apparent horizon in the collapse of an isolated dust cloud using expansion functions. Our results indicate that in the region of spacetime far away from the gravitational singularity, the considered system has a horizon, in which case the singularity is covered. Using this method may have limited applicability in the neighbourhood of the gravitational singularity, where quantum effects are expected to be essential.

III
Matching conditions

To understand the dynamics, the collapsing LTB cloud has to be stitched to an external space-time.
And these two space-times, stitched at a boundary, then describes the collapsing inhomogeneous dust cloud
surrounded by another static space-time: the Schwarzschild space-time (due to Birkhoff’s theorem).
To do this, we use the Israel-Darmois matching conditions
Poisson
. These conditions require the first fundamental
form (induced metric) and the second fundamental form (extrinsic curvature) to be equal at the hypersurface separating
two spaces.

For the matching we use the LTB metric inside the most external shell, while
the exterior geometry is given by the Schwarzschild metric.

IV
Conclusions

During the collapse, the amount of matter within the outermost shell
r_{b}
is constant, since
M
is constant as shown by Eqs. (
50
) and (
54
),
and reads

F(r_{b})=2M\,.

(57)

The existence of the trapped region and the apparent horizon may occur if we have, owing to Eq. (
21
), the following

R(t,r)\leq F(r)\,.

(58)

Suppose at the beginning of the star’s collapse, we have

R(t,r)>F(r)

(59)

so that there is no apparent horizon. During the evolution to the gravitational singularity (see App.
B
),
the radius
R
of the collapsing star decreases. This is why Eq. (
59
) turns into Eq. (
58
).
Therefore, the considered gravitational system becomes a black hole with the trapped region covered by the apparent horizon.

The above reasoning is justified in the region of a collapsing star far away from the vicinity of a gravitational singularity,
where general relativity is well defined. The situation may be different in the neighbourhood of the singularity, as the full
applicability of general relativity is expected to be limited due to quantum effects. There, using the expansion functions to
derive (
21
) and (
22
) may be questionable. This may concern situations in which
F(r_{b})
is so small that
satisfying (
22
) requires entering the Planck scale.

The results obtained a long time ago
Joshi1
;
Joshi3
;
Singh
;
Joshi:2001xi
on dust cloud collapse suggest the possibility of naked
singularities. The corresponding analyses, however, are based on the geometry of spacetime in the neighbourhood of the
gravitational singularity. Possible quantum contributions have been ignored.

Further examination is needed that accounts for quantum effects. This issue will be considered elsewhere.

Appendix A
Solution to classical dynamics

Inserting the metric (
1
) into the Einstein equations for dust, one finds the following three independent equations:

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{R}^{2}}{R^{2}}+\frac{2\dot{R}\dot{R}^{\prime}}{R\,R^{\prime}}=\rho,

(60)

\displaystyle\frac{R^{\prime}}{R}\left(\dot{R}^{2}+2R\ddot{R}\right)=0,

(61)

\displaystyle\frac{R}{R^{\prime}}\left(\dot{R}\dot{R}^{\prime}+R^{\prime}\ddot{R}+R\ddot{R}^{\prime}\right)=0,

(62)

where
R=R(t,r)
,
\rho=\rho(t,r)
.
Eqs. (
61
)–(
62
) can be used to eliminate
\ddot{R}
and
\ddot{R}^{\prime}
derivatives

\displaystyle\ddot{R}=-\frac{\dot{R}^{2}}{2R},

(63)

\displaystyle\ddot{R}^{\prime}=\frac{R^{\prime}\dot{R}^{2}}{2R^{2}}-\frac{\dot{R}\dot{R}^{\prime}}{R}.

(64)

It can be verified that Eqs. (
61
)–(
62
) are satisfied by
R(t,r)
that reads

R(t,r)=\left(r^{\frac{3}{2}}-\frac{3}{2}t\sqrt{F(r)}\right)^{2/3}.

(65)

On the other hand, from (
60
) one gets the relation between energy density and
F(r)

\rho(t,r)=\frac{4\sqrt{F(r)}F^{\prime}(r)}{\left(3t\sqrt{F(r)}-2r^{3/2}\right)\left(tF^{\prime}(r)-2\sqrt{rF(r)}\right)}.

(66)

Hence, the solution to the Einstein equation gives
R
and
\rho
to be a functions of the mass
F(r)
.

Appendix B
Curvature invariants

Curvature invariants are used to measure the geometric curvature of spacetime.
Most common are the Ricci and Riemann invariants.
From the metric (
1
) one finds that the Ricci scalar reads

The Kretschmann scalar is found to be

(68)
