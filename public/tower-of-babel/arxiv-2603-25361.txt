A sharp quantitative stability result near infinitely concentrated minimisers
Melanie Rupflin and Sebastian Woodward

Abstract
. We consider the question of quantitative stability of minimisers for a well-known variational problem for which the infimum of the energy is not achieved in the classical sense, namely for the Dirichlet energy of degree 1 1 maps from closed surfaces ( Σ , g Σ ) (\Sigma,g_{\Sigma}) of positive genus into the unit sphere S 2 ⊂ ℝ 3 S^{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3} . For this variational problem it is natural to view configurations which consist of a constant map from the given domain and an infinitely concentrated rotation as generalised minimisers and to hence ask whether the distance of almost minimisers v : Σ → S 2 v:\Sigma\to S^{2} to this set of infinitely concentrated minimisers can be controlled in terms of the energy defect δ v = E ⁡ ( v ) − inf E = E ⁡ ( v ) − 4 ​ π \delta_{v}=E(v)-\inf E=E(v)-4\pi . In this paper we develop a new dynamic approach that allows us to change the topology of the domain in a well controlled manner and to deform almost minimising maps from surfaces of general genus into harmonic maps from the sphere in a way that yields sharp quantitative estimates on all key features that characterise the distance to the set of infinitely concentrated minimisers, i.e. the scale of concentration, the H 1 H^{1} -distance to the nearest bubble on the concentration region and the H 1 H^{1} -distance to the nearest constant away from the concentration point.



1.
Introduction and main results

Let
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
be a closed orientable surface. We consider the Dirichlet energy

(1.1)

E(v)=E(v,g_{\Sigma}):=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{g_{\Sigma}}v|_{g_{\Sigma}}^{2}\text{d}v_{g_{\Sigma}}

of maps
v:(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})\to S^{2}
.
We recall that
E
is invariant under conformal changes of the domain metric and isometries on the target. In the following, we can hence restrict our attention to metrics
g_{\Sigma}
with curvature
K_{g_{\Sigma}}\equiv 1,0,-1
for surfaces of genus
\gamma=0,1
and
\geq 2
respectively, additionally normalised by
\text{Area}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})=1
if
\gamma=1
, and maps with non-negative degree.

We also recall that the Dirichlet energy of maps from surfaces into arbitrary targets
N

is always bounded from below by the
area (counted with multiplicity), with equality if and only if the map is (weakly) conformal, i.e. if and only if
v^{*}g_{N}=\rho^{2}g_{\Sigma}
for a function
\rho\geq 0
.

In particular, for maps
v:(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})\to S^{2}
of a given degree
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
we have

E(v)\geq 4\pi k

with equality if and only if
v
is conformal.
Furthermore, we always have

(1.2)

\inf\{E(v,g_{\Sigma}):\quad v:\Sigma\to S^{2}\text{ with }\deg(v)=k\}=4\pi k.

Indeed, writing for short
I=I(g_{\Sigma}):=\inj(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
and denoting by

(1.3)

\pi:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to S^{2},\quad\pi(x)=\big(\tfrac{2x}{1+|x|^{2}},\tfrac{1-|x|^{2}}{1+|x|^{2}}\big)\text{ and }\pi_{\mu}(\cdot)=\pi(\mu\cdot),\quad\mu>0

the (rescaled) inverse stereographic projection, we can e.g. obtain a sequence of degree
k
maps
v_{n}:\Sigma\to S^{2}
with
E(v_{n})\to 4\pi k
by
working in local conformal coordinates
(x_{1},x_{2})
on a ball
B_{I}(a)
,
a\in\Sigma
,
taking highly scaled copies
\pi_{\mu_{n}}(z^{k})
,
\mu_{n}\to\infty
, of the degree
k
harmonic map
{\mathbb{C}}\ni z\mapsto\pi(z^{k})\in S^{2}

and cutting them off to a constant on
B_{I}(a)\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}I}(a)
. Here, and in the following, we identify
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
{\mathbb{C}}
whenever convenient.

Conversely, the question of whether the minimum is achieved, i.e. whether the set of (classical) minimisers

(1.4)

{\mathcal{M}}_{k}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma}):=\{v:(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})\to(S^{2},g_{S^{2}})\text{ conformal with }\deg(v)=k\}

is non-empty, depends on both the domain
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
and the prescribed degree
k
.

If
\Sigma
is a sphere, then this set can be identified with
the set of meromorphic functions from
\hat{}{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}
to itself by working in stereographic coordinates on both the domain and the target, so
\dim({\mathcal{M}}_{k}(S^{2}))=4k+2>0
for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

Conversely,
the Riemann-Hurwitz formula excludes the existence of degree
1
meromorphic functions from any surface
\Sigma
of positive genus to
S^{2}
so for all such domains the set of classical minimisers
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
is empty and

(1.5)

\delta_{v}:=E(v)-4\pi>0\text{ for every degree }1\text{ map }v:(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})\to S^{2}.

At the same time, we can view configurations which are given by a constant map
\omega_{0}:\Sigma\to S^{2}
away from a single point
a\in\Sigma
and there described by an infinitely concentrated copy of a rotation
{\mathcal{R}}\in SO(3)
as generalised, infinitely concentrated minimisers of the above problem.

It is hence natural to ask whether degree
1
maps
v:\Sigma\to S^{2}
which almost minimise the energy must always be close to such a singular minimising configuration.

It is easy
to answer this question positively at a qualitative level, i.e. to show that
sequences of maps
v_{n}
whose energy defect

\delta_{v_{n}}=E(v_{n})-4\pi
tends to zero subconverge (in a suitably defined
H^{1}
sense) to such a singular minimiser.

In the present paper we study the more challenging question of
quantitative stability
, i.e. whether (and how) the distance of a (classically defined) almost minimiser to the set of such infinitely concentrated minimising configurations can be controlled in terms of the energy defect.

We note that the question of quantitative stability of minimisers for well known variational problems has attracted very significant attention in recent years, including in the context of the Isoperimetric problem, the Brunn Minkowski inequality, the Yamabe problem, optimal Sobolev inequalities, spectral problems and conformal immersions, see e.g.
[

,

,

,

,

,

At the same time, there are few instances of quantitative stability results that apply in situations where the set of minimisers is genuinely non-compact (also modulo symmetries) or where the question of stability of minimisers is either false, or ill-posed, in the classical sense.

A distinct feature of the problem under consideration, and a major challenge in the analysis, is that we want to compare objects of different topological type, namely a (classically defined) map
v
from the given surface
\Sigma
of positive genus with a minimising configuration that consists of a constant map from
\Sigma
and an infinitely concentrated bubble.

Before formulating any result, we hence first need to introduce a suitable notion of distance that captures the key features of the problem, i.e. that describes

•

the distance of
v
to the closest constant away from the concentration point;

•

the distance of
v
to the closest (rescaled) rotation on the bubble region;

•

the scale of concentration.

Formally, we can view a singular minimiser as a combination of a constant map
\omega_{0}\equiv p\in S^{2}
on
\Sigma\setminus\{a\}
and an infinitely concentrated version
{\mathcal{R}}\cdot\pi_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\to\infty
, of the map
{\mathcal{R}}\cdot\pi:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to S^{2}
that represents a given rotation
{\mathcal{R}}\in SO(3)
in stereographic coordinates. For the latter, it is natural to equip
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with the metric
g_{\pi,\lambda}:=\pi_{\lambda}^{*}g_{S^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}|^{2}dx^{2}
that represents the standard metric of
S^{2}
in the appropriately scaled stereographic coordinates.

To measure the distance of a given
v:\Sigma\to S^{2}
from such a singular configuration, it is hence natural to consider

(i)

the
\dot{H}^{1}(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(a),g_{\Sigma})
-norm,
R>0
, of
v-p
and, as the behaviour of small regions does not contribute significantly to the
L^{2}
norm, the full
L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
norm of
v-p
.

(ii)

the deviation from
\infty
of the scale
\lambda
for which the
H^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota},g_{\pi,\lambda})
norm of
v_{a}-{\mathcal{R}}\pi_{\lambda}
is
minimal and the size of this norm.

Here, and in the following, we denote by
v_{a}
the function that represents
v
in standard conformal coordinates
x=F_{a}(p)
around
a\in\Sigma
, i.e. coordinates which are chosen so that they provide an isometry from
B_{I}(a)
,
I=\inj(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
, to the disc
\mathbb{D}_{\iota}=\mathbb{D}_{\iota}(0)
in
({\mathbb{R}}^{2},dx^{2})
for
\iota=I
(if
\gamma(\Sigma)=1
) or in the Poincaré hyperbolic disc
(\mathbb{D}_{1},\frac{1}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}dx^{2})
for
\iota=\tanh(I)
(if
\gamma(\Sigma)\geq 2
).
More generally, we denote by
B_{R}(a)
geodesic balls in the given constant curvature domain
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
, and use the shorthands
r_{\Sigma}(R)
for the radius of the corresponding disc, i.e. set
r_{\Sigma}(R)=R
if
\gamma=1
and
r_{\Sigma}(R)=\tanh(R)
if
\gamma\geq 2
, and
R_{\Sigma}(\cdot)
for the inverse of this function
r_{\Sigma}(\cdot)
.

In this paper, we prove the following quantitative stability result
that provides sharp bounds on all of the above key quantities that characterise the distance of a map to such infinitely concentrated minimisers.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
be
any closed oriented surface with positive genus. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that the following holds true.

For any degree
1
map
v\in H^{1}(\Sigma,S^{2})
, there exists a rotation
{\mathcal{R}}\in SO(3)
, a point
a\in\Sigma
, a value
p\in S^{2}
and a scale
\lambda>1

such that the following holds.

(i)

The map
v
is essentially given by the constant map
\omega_{0}\equiv p
on
\Sigma\setminus\{a\}
in the sense that

(1.6)

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(a),g_{\Sigma})}\leq CR^{-1}\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{ for every }R\in(0,\inj(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})),

and

(1.7)

\|v-p\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|\log\delta_{v}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(ii)

The function
v_{a}:=v\circ F_{a}^{-1}
which describes
v
in standard conformal coordinates around
a

is essentially given by
{\mathcal{R}}\pi_{\lambda}
in the sense that

(1.8)

\|v_{a}-{\mathcal{R}}\pi_{\lambda}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota},g_{\pi,\lambda})}\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}},

g_{\pi,\lambda}=\pi_{\lambda}^{*}g_{S^{2}}
,
and the scale
\lambda
satisfies

(1.9)

\lambda^{-1}\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We remark that the exponents in all of these estimates are sharp.
This is trivially true for the exponent
\frac{1}{2}
in (
1.6
)
and (
1.8
)
as this is the maximal exponent for which a quantitative stability estimate of the form
\text{dist}(u,{\mathcal{M}})\lesssim\delta_{u}^{\alpha}
can hold for any smooth variational problem.
Similarly,
as cutting
\pi_{\lambda}
off to
\pi(\infty)=(0,0,-1)
on a fixed size annulus gives a map
v
with
\delta_{v}\sim\lambda^{-2}
for which

(1.10)

\|v-\fint_{\Sigma}v\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}\gtrsim\|(\pi_{\lambda}^{1},\pi_{\lambda}^{2})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{r})}\sim\lambda^{-1}(\log\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}},

we see that also the estimates (
1.7
) and (
1.9
)
are sharp.

Remark 1.2
.

We note that the constant
C
in the above theorem must be allowed to depend not only on the topology of
\Sigma
, but also on the metric
g_{\Sigma}
(or some relevant geometric quantity associated to
g_{\Sigma}
like a lower bound on
\inj(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
that excludes a degeneration in moduli space). To see this one can e.g. consider degree
1
maps
v_{j}
from degenerating hyperbolic surfaces
(\Sigma,g_{j})
with
E(v_{j},g_{j})\to 4\pi
whose restriction to a disconnecting degenerating hyperbolic collar
(\mathcal{C}(\sigma_{j}),g_{j})\simeq([-X(\ell_{j}),X(\ell_{j})]\times S^{1},\rho_{\ell_{j}}^{2}(s)(ds^{2}+d\theta^{2}))
,
\ell_{j}=L_{g_{j}}(\sigma_{j})\to 0
,
converge to the harmonic map
(s,\theta)\mapsto\pi(e^{s+i\theta})
from the infinite cylinder
{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}
.

Similarly, we cannot expect that the above statements hold for
p={\mathcal{R}}\pi(\infty)
as we can e.g. construct maps
v_{\lambda}:(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})\to S^{2}

which are given by
\pi_{\lambda}
on a ball with radius
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
around some
b
, coincide with a constant
p\neq\pi(\infty)
outsides of a fixed ball
B_{r}(b)
and whose energy defect
\delta_{v_{\lambda}}\sim(\log\lambda)^{-1}
tends to zero as
\lambda\to\infty
.

We note that in the case where
\Sigma
is a sphere, the question of quantitative stability of degree
1

minimisers is significantly different, and far less delicate, than for domains of positive genus. Indeed, if
\Sigma=S^{2}
then the minimum is not only achieved, but the set of minimisers indeed agrees with the symmetry group
\textit{M\"{o}b}(S^{2})
of Möbius transforms. In practice this means that the difficulty posed by concentration of energy can be avoided by working in the correct gauge, compare
[
33
]
, and quantitative stability of minimisers holds in the classical sense. Indeed, Bernand-Mantel, Muratov and Simon
[
1
]
first established that

(1.11)

\text{dist}_{\dot{H}^{1}(S^{2},g)}(v,\text{M\"{o}b}(S^{2}))\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}

holds true
for every degree
1
map
v:S^{2}\to S^{2}
and a universal constant
C
, alternative proofs of this result
were subsequently given by Hirsch and Zemas in
[
15
]
using elliptic regularisation techniques and by Topping in
[
33
]
using the harmonic map flow, and extensions to higher dimensions were recently proven by Guerra, Lamy and Zemas in
[
13
,
14
]
.
We note that a quantitative understanding of the Dirichlet energy of maps into
S^{2}
is not only of interest in geometric analysis, but also in applications where the Dirichlet energy (also called exchange energy) forms a key term e.g. in models from material science, see e.g.
[
5
,
22
]
and the references therein.

We also note that the quantitative stability of minimisers for maps
v:S^{2}\to S^{2}
of arbitrary degree
k\geq 2
was addressed by the first author in
[
28
]
. In this case the set of (classical) minimisers
{\mathcal{M}}_{k}(S^{2})
is given by the degree
k
rational functions, so non-empty, but stability of minimisers fails in the classical sense even at the qualitative level, i.e. there exist sequences of maps
v_{n}
of any given degree
k\geq 2
with
E(v_{n})\to 4\pi k
for which
\text{dist}_{H^{1}}(v_{n},{\mathcal{M}}_{k}(S^{2}))\nrightarrow 0
. However, if one expands the class of comparison objects to additionally include collections of rational functions of total degree
k
which are concentrated on essentially disjoint subsets then not only qualitative, but also quantitative stability of minimisers can be recovered. Indeed, the results of
[
28
]
provide sharp estimates
of the form
\text{dist}\leq C\delta_{u}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|\log\delta_{u}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}
on the distance of any given degree
k
map to the nearest collection of such rational maps, and provide additional (not necessarily sharp) control on the relative scales and location of the set on which the maps that make up this collection are concentrating, see Theorems 1.1 and Theorems 1.4 of
[
28
]
for detail. As the arguments in
[
28
]
exploit the symmetry group
\textit{M\"{o}b}(S^{2})
of the domain
\Sigma=S^{2}
and the fact that all components of the limiting configuration can be parametrised over this
\Sigma
they are not applicable in the present setting of maps from surfaces of positive genus. We hence need to develop a new and more flexible approach that provides a well controlled way to deform a map from a given surface into a limiting map that is defined on a domain of different topology.

Overview of the key steps in the proof of our main result:

A key aspect of our proof is that we will simultaneously deform the map and the domain.
To achieve the latter we will construct families of metrics that deform suitably chosen representatives of the given conformal class
[g_{\Sigma}]
of metrics on the domain surface of positive genus into a punctured sphere in a well controlled way that in particular allows us to keep the metric essentially fixed on the bubble region.

To this end we first consider metrics
\bar{g}_{\mu,b}
,
b\in\Sigma
,
\mu\geq\mu_{*}=\mu_{*}(g_{\Sigma})
, which are conformal to
g_{\Sigma}
but weighted in a way that
they scale the ball
B_{R_{\Sigma}(\mu^{-1})}(b)
up to a hemisphere, are so that a fixed size ball
B_{R_{2}}(b)
in the original surface becomes isometric to
S^{2}\setminus B^{g_{S^{2}}}_{s}(\pi(\infty))
for some
s\sim\mu^{-1}
and so that
g_{\Sigma}
gets scaled down by a constant
c_{\mu}\sim\mu^{-2}
outside of a slightly larger ball
B_{R_{0}}(b)
. In particular, in the limit
\mu\to\infty
these weighted metrics turn
\Sigma
into a punctured sphere.

Instead of directly working with these metrics
\bar{g}_{\mu,b}
, we then construct a second family of metrics
g_{\mu,b}
which are isometric to
\bar{g}_{\mu,b}
but chosen in a way that the curves
\mu\mapsto g_{\mu,b}
provides a far more efficient way of deforming
(\Sigma,g_{\mu,b})
into a punctured sphere than what would be possible by just conformal deformations.
To be more precise, given any
b\in\Sigma

we will construct diffeomorphisms
T_{\mu,b}:\Sigma\to\Sigma
,
\mu\geq\mu_{*}
, so that the resulting metrics

g_{\mu,b}=T_{\mu,b}^{*}\bar{g}_{\mu,b}
satisfy

\|\partial_{\mu}g_{\mu,b}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\mu,b})}\sim\mu^{-2}
, allowing us to deform a given such metric
g_{\mu_{0},b}
into a singular metric
g_{\infty,b}
by moving only a distance of

(1.12)

\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\infty}\|\partial_{\mu}g_{\mu,b}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\mu,b})}\sim\mu_{0}^{-1}.

We will also be able to ensure that these metrics are essentially ordered in the sense that the inequality
g_{\mu,b}\geq cg_{\tilde{\mu},b}
holds for all
\tilde{\mu}\geq\mu
and a universal constant
c>0
(later chosen as
c=e^{-3})
.

Given a map
v
with small energy defect
we will then first choose
b\in\Sigma
and
\mu_{0}\gg 1

so that

(1.13)

E(v,B_{R_{\Sigma}(\mu_{0}^{-1})}(b))=\sup_{\tilde{b}\in\Sigma}E(v,B_{R_{\Sigma}(\mu_{0}^{-1})}(\tilde{b}))=2\pi.

We then devise a way that allows us to deform the initial pair
(U_{0}:=v,G_{0}:=\bar{g}_{\mu_{0},b})
of map and metric
into a limit
(U_{\infty},G_{\infty})
which consists of

•

a singular metric
G_{\infty}
which is supported on a subset
\Omega_{\infty}
of
\Sigma
that corresponds to a ball
B_{\hat{R}_{\infty}}(b)
with
\hat{R}_{\infty}\in(R_{2},R_{0})
in the original surface
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
, agrees with
G_{0}
on a fixed set
B_{R_{2}}(b)
and is so that
(\Omega_{\infty},G_{\infty})
is isometric to the punctured sphere
S^{2}\setminus\{\pi(\infty)\}
,

•

a harmonic map
U_{\infty}:(\Omega_{\infty},G_{\infty})\to S^{2}
.

Crucially, we will be able to carry out this deformation through a curve of maps
U(t)
and metrics
G(t)=(T_{\mu_{0},b})_{*}g_{\mu(t),b}
for which

(1.14)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\partial_{t}U\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,G(t))}+\|\partial_{t}G\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,G(t))}dt\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This will yield both the desired bound (
1.9
) on the bubble scale, compare (
1.12
), as well as a weighted
L^{2}
estimate for the map component of the form

\|v-U_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty},G_{\infty})}\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}

from which we will then be able to derive all other claims made in our main result.

To obtain this curve
(U(t),G(t))
we first
pull-back both the given map
U_{0}=v
and the given weighted metric
G_{0}=\bar{g}_{\mu_{0},b}
by the corresponding (fixed) diffeomorphism
T_{\mu_{0},b}:\Sigma\to\Sigma
to obtain a new pair
(u_{0},g_{0})=T_{\mu_{0},b}^{*}(U_{0},G_{0})=(v\circ T_{\mu_{0},b},g_{\mu_{0},b})

that represents the given configuration in new coordinates, but is now so that
g_{0}
is an element of our specially constructed family of metrics
{\mathcal{G}}_{b}:=\{g_{\mu,b},\,\mu\geq\mu_{*}\}
.

We then deform
(u_{0},g_{0})
using a new geometric flow that evolves both the map and the metric, similarly to
[
25
]
. Namely we consider the negative
L^{2}
-gradient flow of the Dirichlet energy
\mathcal{E}_{b}=E|_{H^{1}(\Sigma,S^{2})\times{\mathcal{G}}_{b}}
, considered as a function of both an (arbitrary)
H^{1}
map
u:\Sigma\to S^{2}
and a metric
g
that is constraint to lie in this
1
-parameter family of metrics
{\mathcal{G}}_{b}
for the fixed point
b
chosen in (
1.13
). Formally, this flow is given by

(1.15)

\partial_{t}u=-\nabla_{u}^{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}E(u,g),\qquad\partial_{t}g=-P^{T_{g}{\mathcal{G}}_{b}}(\nabla_{g}^{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}E(u,g)),

where
P^{T_{g}{\mathcal{G}}_{b}}
denotes the
L^{2}(\Sigma,g)
orthogonal projection from the space of
(0,2)
-tensors to
T_{g}{\mathcal{G}}_{b}
.

We recall that the negative
L^{2}
-gradient of the Dirichlet energy with respect to the map component is given by the tension field, so here where we map into
S^{2}
by

-\nabla_{u}^{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}E(u,g)=\tau_{g}(u)=\Delta_{g}u+|du|_{g}^{2}u.

We also recall that the negative
L^{2}
-gradient of the Dirichlet energy with respect to general variations of the metric component is given in terms of the energy stress-tensor, namely

(1.16)

-\nabla_{g}^{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}E(u,g)=\tfrac{1}{2}k(u,g)\text{ for }k(u,g):=u^{*}g_{S^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{2}|du|_{g}^{2}g.

Our flow hence evolves a given initial map
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Sigma,S^{2})
and a given initial metric
g_{0}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{b}
by

(1.17)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\tau_{g}(u),\qquad\partial_{t}g=\tfrac{1}{2}P^{T_{g}\mathcal{G}_{b}}\left(k(u,g)\right).

A crucial role in the analysis of this flow
is played by a Łojasiewicz estimate of the form

(1.18)

|E(u,g)-4\pi|\leq C\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{b}(u,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}^{2},

which
provides uniform bounds on the energy defect in terms of the norm of the gradient

\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{b}(u,g)=\big(\nabla_{u}^{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}E(u,g)\,,\,P^{T_{g}\mathcal{G}_{b}}\nabla_{g}^{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}E(u,g)\big)
of the restricted energy functional
{\mathcal{E}}_{b}=E|_{H^{1}(\Sigma,S^{2})\times{\mathcal{G}}_{b}}
.

We prove two versions of
this and related Łojasiewicz estimates that might be of independent interest
in Section
3
, one in the more typical form of a result that applies in a suitable neighbourhood of the set of critical points (which in our case are however infinitely concentrated), see Theorem
3.8
, and a version for maps with small energy defect that is crucially used in the proof of our main result, see
Theorem
3.5
. As explained in Section
3
, this second version can be obtained from the first version based on a careful analysis of almost critical points of
{\mathcal{E}}_{b}
, which in particular uses that control on
P^{T_{g}\mathcal{G}_{b}}\nabla^{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}_{g}E
excludes the formation of non-trivial necks in the image even when the geometry of the domain degenerates, compare Remark
3.11
. Theorem
3.8
in turn will be proven in
Sections
6
and
7

based on the approach developed by Malchiodi, Sharp and the first author in
[
21
]
.

For maps with small energy defect this key Łojasiewicz estimate will allow us to a priori exclude the formation of singularities of the flow that are caused by the bubbling off of harmonic spheres, hence allowing us to continue the flow smoothly until it achieves its goal of changing the topology of the domain from a surface of positive genus to a punctured sphere.
Equally importantly, the Łojasiewicz estimate (
1.18
) yields a priori bounds on the distance travelled by solutions of (
1.17
), and hence by
(U,G)(t)=(T_{\mu_{0},b})_{*}(u,g)(t)
,
in the precise form (
1.14
) that we require to derive the sharp quantitative stability estimates stated in our main result.

Definition and basic properties of metrics

To define these metrics

3.

Before we state our main Łojasiewicz estimates, we collect some basic properties of maps which will be used throughout this and the following sections.

To begin with, we recall that harmonic maps

, where
J
is chosen as the identity if
\deg(\omega)=1
, respectively a reflection if
\deg(\omega)=-1
.
We also remark that
x_{0}
and
\lambda>0
can be uniquely characterised by the condition that

(3.1)

E(\omega,\mathbb{D}_{\lambda^{-1}}(x_{0}))=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{D}_{\lambda^{-1}}}E(\omega,\mathbb{D}_{\lambda^{-1}}(y))=2\pi

a fact which can be easily checked, e.g. by using the explicit expression for

|\nabla\pi(x)|=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{1+|x|^{2}}
to see that

\partial_{\eta}|_{\eta=0}E(\pi,\mathbb{D}_{r}((1+\eta)x))<0
for any
r>0
and
x\neq 0
.

We will also repeatedly use the following remark on the behaviour of maps with bounded tension, which is an immediate consequence of the
compactness theory of almost harmonic maps developed in
[
6
,
18
,
23
,
30
]
.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be any closed surface and let
v_{n}\in H^{2}(M,S^{2})
be any sequence of maps with

\sup_{n}E(v_{n})<8\pi\text{ and }\sup_{n}\|\tau_{g}(v_{n})\|_{L^{2}(M,g)}<\infty.

Then, after passing to a subsequence, one of the following statements holds.

•

v_{n}
converges weakly in
H^{2}_{\text{loc}}(M,g)
, and hence in particular strongly in
H^{1}_{\text{loc}}\cap L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}
, to a limit
v_{\infty}
.

•

v_{n}
converges weakly in
H^{2}_{\text{loc}}(M\setminus\{b\},g)
to a limit
v_{\infty}
away from a point
b\in\Sigma
at which a single degree
\pm 1
bubble forms; that is, there exist
\lambda_{n}\to\infty
,
x_{n}\to 0
,
{\mathcal{R}}\in SO(3)
and
J
as above so that the maps
v_{n,b}
which represent
v_{n}
in the usual conformal coordinate chart
F_{b}:B_{I}(b)\to\mathbb{D}_{\iota}
are so that

v_{n,b}(x)-({\mathcal{R}}\pi(\lambda_{n}J(x-x_{n}))-{\mathcal{R}}\pi(\infty))-v_{\infty,b}\to 0\text{ strongly in }H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota}).

If
\|\tau_{g}(v_{n})\|_{L^{2}(M,g)}\to 0
then the above statements hold with strong, rather than weak,
H^{2}
convergence and the limiting map
v_{\infty}
will be harmonic.

Remark 3.2
.

Since the Riemann-Hurtwiz theorem precludes the existence of a degree
1
map from
\Sigma
to
S^{2}
with energy
4\pi
, it follows from
[
26
, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.3]
that there exists
\delta_{*}=\delta_{*}(g_{\Sigma})>0
such
E(u)\geq 4\pi+\delta_{*}
holds for all degree
1
harmonic maps
u:\Sigma\to S^{2}
.

These remarks in particular ensure that for degree
1
maps
u_{n}:\Sigma\to S^{2}
whose energy defect tends to zero and whose tension remains bounded, all of the energy
will concentrate near a single point.
Indeed, this statement remains valid also for maps with unbounded tension, namely we have

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
R\in(0,I)
and all
c>0
, there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that

(3.2)

\sup_{b\in\Sigma}E(v,B_{R}(b))\geq 4\pi-c,

for all degree
1
maps
v\in H^{1}(\Sigma,S^{2})
with
\delta_{v}\leq\delta_{1}
.

For the proof of this lemma we recall that the energy decays according to
\tfrac{d}{dt}E(u)=-\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}^{2}

along solutions
u(t)
of the classical harmonic map flow
\partial_{t}u=\tau_{g_{\Sigma}}(u)
on the
fixed
surface
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
, and that local energy estimates of the form

(3.3)

\displaystyle E(u(t_{2}),B_{\frac{R}{2}}(b))

\displaystyle\leq E(u(t_{1}),B_{R}(b))+CR^{-1}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}dt

\displaystyle\leq E(u(t_{1}),B_{R}(b))+CR^{-1}(E(t_{1})-E(t_{2}))^{\frac{1}{2}}(t_{2}-t_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}

are valid for a constant
C
that only depends on an upper bound
\bar{E}
on the initial energy.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

As observed above, we can choose
\tilde{\delta}_{1}>0
so that the stronger bound of

(3.4)

\sup_{b\in\Sigma}E(u,B_{R/2}(b),g_{\Sigma})\geq 4\pi-\tfrac{c}{2},

holds
for all degree
1
maps
u
with

\delta_{u}\leq\tilde{\delta}_{1}
which additionally satisfy

\|\tau_{g_{\Sigma}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}\leq 1
.
We now set

\delta_{1}:=\min(\tilde{\delta}_{1},\frac{cR}{2C},\pi,\delta_{*})
,
\delta_{*}>0
as in Remark
3.2
and
C
so that (
3.3
) holds for
\bar{E}=5\pi
.

Given
v
as in the lemma, we now let
u(t)
be the corresponding solution of the harmonic map flow and let
T_{max}\in(0,\infty]
be the maximal time until which the flow exists and remains smooth.
Since
v
is not homotopic to a harmonic map, we know that
u(t)
must become singular as
t\uparrow T_{\max}
, irrespective of whether or not
T_{\max}
is finite. Therefore, (
3.4
) will hold eventually, allowing us to consider the
first time
T_{1}\in[0,T_{\max})
for which it is valid.

If
T_{1}=0
then the claim trivially holds. Otherwise, we can use that
the definition of
\tilde{\delta}_{1}
and the fact that
\delta_{u(t)}\leq\delta_{v}\leq\delta_{1}\leq\tilde{\delta}_{1}
ensure that

\|\tau_{g_{\Sigma}}(u(t))\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}>1
at all times where
(
3.4
) is violated, so in particular on all of
[0,T_{1})
. This allows us to bound

T_{1}\leq\int_{0}^{T_{1}}\|\tau_{g_{\Sigma}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}^{2}dt=E(v)-E(u(T_{1}))=\delta_{v}-\delta_{u(T_{1})}\leq\delta_{1},

which, when inserted into (
3.3
),
allows us to bound

\displaystyle 4\pi-\tfrac{c}{2}\leq\sup_{b\in\Sigma}E(u(T_{1}),B_{R/2}(b))

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{b\in\Sigma}E(v,B_{R}(b))+\tfrac{C}{R}\delta_{1}\leq\sup_{b\in\Sigma}E(u,B_{R}(b))+\tfrac{c}{2},

and hence deduce the claimed estimate (
3.2
).
∎

Remark 3.4
.

We can in particular apply this lemma for
c=2\pi
to deduce that for any degree
1
map
v
with small energy defect there exist
\mu_{0}\geq 4\mu_{*}
and
b\in\Sigma
so that (

) is satisfied.

While we will use this specific choice of parameters later to associate to

an initial metric
G_{0}=\bar{g}_{\mu_{0},b}
for our flow, for the analysis of (degree
1
) maps
\bar{u}
with small energy defect it suffices to ensure that we are always working with metrics
\bar{g}_{\mu,b}
which are compatible with the energy distribution of
\bar{u}
in the sense of (

), this weaker condition (
3.5
) will remain valid along our flow, hence allowing us to control the evolution of the flow using the following key Łojasiewicz estimate.

Theorem 3.5
.

For every closed orientable surface
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
of positive genus, there exist constants
\delta_{1}>0
,
\varepsilon_{2}>0
and
C
so that the following holds true.

Let
\bar{u}\in H^{1}(\Sigma,S^{2})
be any degree
1
map with
\delta_{\bar{u}}\leq\delta_{1}
and let
\mu\geq\mu_{*}
and
b\in\Sigma
be so that

(3.5)

E(\bar{u},B_{R_{\Sigma}(\frac{1+\varepsilon_{2}}{\mu})}(b))\geq 2\pi-\varepsilon_{2}\text{ and }\max_{d_{g_{\Sigma}}(\tilde{b},b)\leq R_{\Sigma}(\mu^{-1})}E(\bar{u},B_{R_{\Sigma}(\frac{1-\varepsilon_{2}}{\mu})}(\tilde{b}))\leq 2\pi+\varepsilon_{2}.

Then, setting
u:=T_{\mu,b}^{*}\bar{u}
, we can bound

(3.6)

|E(\bar{u})-4\pi|\leq C\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{b}(u,g_{\mu,b})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\mu,b})}^{2}

and

(3.7)

\mu^{-1}\leq C\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{b}(u,g_{\mu,b})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\mu,b})},

and there exist
\lambda\sim\mu
,
a\in\Sigma
and
{\mathcal{R}}\in SO(3)
so that
\bar{u}_{a}:=\bar{u}\circ F_{a}^{-1}
satisfies

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}_{a}-{\mathcal{R}}\pi_{\lambda}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota},\pi_{\lambda}^{*}g_{S^{2}})}+\|\bar{u}-{\mathcal{R}}\pi(\infty)\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma\setminus B_{I}(a),c_{\lambda}^{2}g_{\Sigma})}\leq C\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{b}(u,g_{\mu,b})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\mu,b})}.

Here
T_{\mu,b},\bar{g}_{\mu,b}
and
g_{\mu,b}=T_{\mu.b}^{*}\bar{g}_{\mu,b}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{b}
are the diffeomorphisms and metrics constructed in Section
2
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{b}
denotes the restriction of the Dirichlet energy onto

. The norm appearing on the right hand side of the above inequalities can hence be equivalently computed as

(3.9)

\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{b}(u,g_{\mu,b})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\mu,b})}^{2}=\|\tau_{\bar{g}_{\mu,b}}(\bar{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,\bar{g}_{\mu,b})}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\left\|\bar{P}_{\bar{g}_{\mu,b}}(k(\bar{u},\bar{g}_{\mu,b}))\right\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,\bar{g}_{\mu,b})}^{2},

\bar{P}_{\bar{g}_{\mu,b}}
the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto the tangent space of
(T_{\mu,b}^{-1})^{*}\mathcal{G}_{b}
at
\bar{g}_{\mu,b}=(T_{\mu,b}^{-1})^{*}g_{\mu,b}
.

At times it will be useful to translate the condition (
3.5
) into a condition on the energy of the map
\bar{u}_{b}
on suitable discs, and for this it is useful
to note

Remark 3.6
.

For
\tilde{b}
close to
b
, the set
F_{b}(B_{R}(\tilde{b}))
, which represents

,
R\leq I-d_{b,\tilde{b}}

,
is well approximated by
\mathbb{D}_{r}(F_{b}(\tilde{b}))
,

, in the sense that

\mathbb{D}_{(1-\varepsilon)r}(F_{b}(\tilde{b}))\subset F_{b}(B_{R}(\tilde{b}))\subset\mathbb{D}_{(1+\varepsilon)r}(F_{b}(\tilde{b}))\text{ holds for some }\varepsilon\sim d_{b,\tilde{b}}^{2}+d_{b,\tilde{b}}R,

,
compare (

).

In the specific situation of the theorem, the number

scales like

which, for maps with sufficiently small energy defect, will in particular be less than
\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{2}
, compare Lemma

. We hence obtain

Remark 3.7
.

Theorem
3.5
is applicable for all maps
\bar{u}
with sufficiently small energy defect for which

E(\bar{u}_{b},\mathbb{D}_{(1+\varepsilon_{2})\mu^{-1}})\geq 2\pi-\varepsilon_{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sup_{|y|\leq\mu^{-1}}E(\bar{u}_{b},\mathbb{D}_{(1-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{2})\mu^{-1}}(y))\leq 2\pi+\varepsilon_{2}.

Remark 3.9
.

We note that a Łojasiewicz estimate of the form

(3.14)

|E(\bar{u})-4\pi|\leq C\|\tau_{g_{\Sigma}}(\bar{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}^{2}\left(1+|\log\|\tau_{g_{\Sigma}}(\bar{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}|\right),

that involves the tension with respect to the standard metric was obtained by the first author in
[

and that Łojasiewicz estimates for maps from

[

,

]
and Waldron
[

While (

\|\tau_{\bar{\rho}_{\mu,b}^{2}g_{\Sigma}}(\bar{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,\bar{\rho}_{\mu,b}^{2}g_{\Sigma})}=\|\bar{\rho}_{\mu,b}^{-1}\tau_{g_{\Sigma}}(\bar{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}

As the above Łojasiewicz estimates are all trivially true if

Lemma 3.10

Let
\bar{u}_{n}\in H^{1}(\Sigma,S^{2})
,
\deg(\bar{u}_{n})=1
, and
\bar{g}_{n}=\bar{g}_{\mu_{n},b_{n}}

\tau_{n}:=\|\tau_{\bar{g}_{n}}(\bar{u}_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,\bar{g}_{n})}\to 0,\qquad\mathcal{P}_{n}:=\left\|\bar{P}_{\bar{g}_{n}}(k(\bar{u}_{n},{\bar{g}_{n}}))\right\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,{\bar{g}_{n}})}\to 0\text{ and }\delta_{\bar{u}_{n}}\to 0

{\mathcal{R}}_{n}\in SO(3)

(3.18)

Remark 3.11

Proof of Lemma

As it suffices to

\displaystyle 0\text{ for every }\Lambda<\infty,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathop{{\mathrm{osc}}}\limits_{\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(b_{n})}\bar{u}_{n}

\displaystyle 0\text{ for every }R>0,

\displaystyle\lesssim E(v_{n},I_{n}(e\Lambda,ce^{-1})\times S^{1})e^{-\text{dist}(s,\partial I_{n}(e\Lambda,e^{-1}c))}+\int_{I_{n}(e\Lambda,ce^{-1})\times S^{1}}|\tau_{g_{\text{cyl}}}(v_{n})|^{2}e^{-|s-q|}d\theta dq

\displaystyle=o_{\Lambda,n}e^{-\text{dist}(s,\partial I_{n}(\Lambda,c))}+\int_{I_{n}(e\Lambda,ce^{-1})\times S^{1}}|\tau_{\rho^{2}{g_{\text{cyl}}}}(v_{n})|^{2}\rho^{2}(q)e^{-|s-q|}dv_{\rho^{2}g_{\text{cyl}}}

\displaystyle=o_{\Lambda,n}e^{-\text{dist}(s,\partial I_{n}(\Lambda,c))}+\tau_{n}e^{-|s|}

for all
s\in I_{n}(\Lambda,c)
where the last step follows as
\rho(q)\sim e^{-|q|}
.

This not only implies that

\sup_{I_{n}(\Lambda,c)}\Theta_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}=o_{\Lambda,n}
and hence that the first claim in (
3.25
) holds, but also that

\int_{I_{n}(\Lambda,c)}\Theta_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds=o_{\Lambda,n}
and hence that

(3.27)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{n}(\Lambda,c)\times S^{1}}|\partial_{s}v_{n}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I_{n}(\Lambda,c)}\big(\int_{S^{1}}|\partial_{s}v_{n}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\int_{I_{n}(\Lambda,c)}(\alpha_{n}+\Theta_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim o_{\Lambda,n}+\int_{I_{n}(\Lambda,c)}|\alpha_{n}|^{\frac{1}{2}}

for
\alpha_{n}(s):=\int_{\{s\}\times S^{1}}|\partial_{s}v_{n}|^{2}-|\partial_{\theta}v_{n}|^{2}d\theta
. It thus remains to show that also
\int_{I_{n}(\Lambda,c)}|\alpha_{n}(s)|^{\frac{1}{2}}ds=o_{\Lambda,n}
.

We recall that the anti-holomorphic derivative of the function
\phi=|v_{s}|^{2}-|v_{\theta}|^{2}-2iv_{s}\cdot v_{\theta}
, that describes the
Hopf-differential
\phi dz^{2}
, is given by

(\partial_{s}+i\partial_{\theta})\phi=2(v_{ss}+v_{\theta\theta})(v_{s}-iv_{\theta})=2\tau_{g_{\text{cyl}}}(v)(v_{s}-iv_{\theta})
. As
\int_{S^{1}}\partial_{\theta}\phi=0
we can thus write

\partial_{s}\alpha_{n}(s)=\int_{\{s\}\times S^{1}}\text{Re}((\partial_{s}+i\partial_{\theta})\phi_{n})d\theta=2\int_{S^{1}}\tau_{{g_{\text{cyl}}}}(v_{n})\cdot\partial_{s}v_{n}d\theta
and estimate

(3.28)

\displaystyle|\alpha_{n}(s_{1})-\alpha_{n}(s_{2})|

\displaystyle\lesssim E(v_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tau_{{g_{\text{cyl}}}}(v_{n})\|_{L^{2}([s_{1},s_{2}]\times S^{1})}\lesssim e^{-s_{1}}\tau_{n}\text{ for all }s_{1}<s_{2}\leq X_{\mu_{n}}=\log(r_{1}\mu_{n}).

In particular,
|\alpha_{n}(s)-\alpha_{n}(X_{\mu_{n}})|\lesssim e^{-(X_{\mu_{n}}-4)}\tau_{n}\lesssim\mu_{n}^{-1}\tau_{n}
on
\text{supp}(\psi^{\prime}(\cdot-X_{\mu_{n}}))\subset[X_{\mu_{n}}-4,X_{\mu_{n}}]
for
\psi
as in (
2.8
).
We now crucially use that the assumption that
{\mathcal{P}}_{n}\to 0
implies that

\mu_{n}\left|\int\psi^{\prime}(s-X_{\mu_{n}})\alpha_{n}(s)ds\right|\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{n}\to 0,

see Corollary
2.5
and Remark
2.3
.
As
\psi^{\prime}\geq 0
and

\int\psi^{\prime}(s)ds=1
we can hence bound

\mu_{n}|\alpha_{n}(X_{\mu_{n}})|\leq\mu_{n}\left|\int\psi^{\prime}(s-X_{\mu_{n}})\cdot[\alpha_{n}(X_{\mu_{n}})-\alpha_{n}(s)]ds\right|+C{\mathcal{P}}_{n}\lesssim\tau_{n}+{\mathcal{P}}_{n}.

Inserted into
(
3.28
) this gives

|\alpha_{n}(s)|\lesssim(e^{-s}+\mu_{n}^{-1})o_{n}

where
o_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. As
|I_{n}(\Lambda,c)|\lesssim\log\mu_{n}
we hence obtain that indeed
\int_{I_{n}(\Lambda,c)}|\alpha_{n}(s)|^{\frac{1}{2}}ds\leq(1+\mu_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log\mu_{n})o_{n}=o_{n}
for every
\Lambda
and
c
, thus completing the proof.
∎

4.
Analysis of our new gradient flow

Since
{\mathcal{G}}_{b}
is 1-dimensional, we can view (
1.17
) as a system that consists of a semi-linear parabolic PDE coupled with a first-order ODE with locally Lipschitz right hand side. Short time existence of solutions for initial data
(u_{0},g_{0})\in C^{2,\alpha}(\Sigma,S^{2})\times{\mathcal{G}}_{b}

can hence be obtained by a standard iteration argument, see e.g.
[
24
, Appendix A]
.
The definition of the flow (
1.17
) ensures that the energy decays according to

(4.1)

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{dt}E(u,g)=-\|\partial_{t}(u,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}^{2}=-\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{b}(u,g_{\mu,b})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\mu,b})}^{2}

along smooth solutions
(u(t),g(t)=g_{\mu(t),b})
while the evolution of localised energies

E_{\phi}(u,g):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{2}|du|_{g}^{2}dv_{g},

obtained using
time independent
cut-off functions
can be controlled by the following standard estimates.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma,[0,1])
and any smooth solution
(u,g)
of (
1.17
)
we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left|\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{d}{dt}$}}E_{\phi}(u,g)+\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}dv_{g}\right|\leq C\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\text{supp}(\phi),g)}+C\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma,g)}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)},

for a constant
C
that only depends on an upper bound on the energy
E_{\phi}(u,g)
at the corresponding time.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Since
\phi
is time-independent and

\partial_{t}u=\tau_{g}(u)=P_{u}(\Delta_{g}u)
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{\varepsilon}E_{\phi}(u(\cdot+\varepsilon),g)+\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}dv_{g}|

\displaystyle=|\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{t}u\langle du,d(\phi^{2})\rangle_{g}dv_{g}|\leq CE_{\phi}(u,g)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma,g)}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)},

while the fact that
\|\phi^{2}k(u,g)\|_{L^{1}(\Sigma,g)}\leq CE_{\phi}(u,g)
allows us to bound

\displaystyle|\partial_{\varepsilon}E_{\phi}(u,g(\cdot+\varepsilon))|

\displaystyle=\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{2}$}}|\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{2}\langle\partial_{t}g,k(u,g)\rangle_{g}dv_{g}|\leq CE_{\phi}(u,g)\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\text{supp}(\phi),g)}.

Combined, this yields (
4.2
).
∎

We also remark that (
2.17
) and (
2.13
) yield a priori bounds of

\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma,g)}\lesssim\mu^{2}
and hence of

|\partial_{t}\mu|\lesssim\mu^{3}

and note that any
C^{k}
norm of
\partial_{t}g
remains controlled for as long as
\mu
remains bounded.

Up to minor modifications, we can hence argue exactly as in the original paper
[
30
]
of Struwe on the classical harmonic map flow to deduce that for any
(u_{0},g_{0})\in H^{1}(\Sigma,S^{2})\times{\mathcal{G}}_{b}
there exists a solution
(u,g)
of the
flow (
1.17
), which exists and is smooth (away from
t=0
) on a maximal time interval
[0,T_{\max})
where
T_{\max}
can only be finite if either

•

\mu
leaves the admissible range of parameters, i.e. is so that
\mu(t)\to\infty
or
\mu(t)\to\mu_{*}
as
t\uparrow T_{\max}
, or

•

\mu(t)
converges to some number
\mu(T_{\max})\in(\mu_{*},\infty)
as
t\uparrow T_{\max}
, but the map becomes singular due to the formation of at least one bubble.

In the following we will focus on the analysis of solutions of (
1.17
) with small energy defect for which the initial metric is chosen so that condition (
1.13
), which we recall in (
4.3
) below, is satisfied, compare also Remark
3.4
.

A key feature of our flow is that this condition remains essentially preserved along the flow. This not only prevents the formation of singularities for the map component, but also allows us to apply the Łojasiewicz estimates obtained in Section
3
to control the total
L^{2}
distance travelled by both the map and metric component. To be more precise, we show

Proposition 4.2
.

For every closed orientable surface
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
with positive genus there exist constants
\delta_{2}>0
and
C
such that
the following holds true. Let

be any degree
1
map with
\delta_{v}\leq\delta_{2}
and let
\mu_{0}\geq\mu_{*}
and

be so that

(4.3)

E(v,B_{R_{\Sigma}(\mu_{0}^{-1})}(b))=\sup_{p\in\Sigma}E(v,B_{R_{\Sigma}(\mu_{0}^{-1})}(p))=2\pi.

Then the maximal solution
(u(t),g(t)=g_{\mu(t),b})
,

, of (
1.17

(i)

3.5
are satisfied

3.8

(ii)

The total distance travelled by the flow
is a priori bounded by

\int_{0}^{T_{\max}}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}+\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}dt\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(iii)

We have
\mu(t)\to\infty
as

irrespective of whether

is finite or infinite and we have a uniform lower bound of
\mu(t)\geq c\delta_{v}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for all

.

Remark 4.3
.

We note that the last statement in particular ensures that

(4.5)

\mu_{0}^{-1}\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{ for }\mu_{0}\text{ as in \eqref{def:flow-scale}}.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

We show that the proposition holds for a sufficiently small

and

As

We also recall that (

while

and

compare Remark

.
This choice of

by

\displaystyle-2\tfrac{d}{dt}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}(-\tfrac{d}{dt}E(u,g))=\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{t}(u,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}^{2}=\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{b}(u,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}\cdot\|\partial_{t}(u,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}\|\partial_{t}(u,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}

\displaystyle|E_{\phi}((u,g)(t_{2}))-E_{\phi}((u,g)(t_{1}))|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\phi_{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{2}})}\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\delta_{v}\leq C(1+\|\nabla\phi_{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{2}})})\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}

for every

.

Given any

\mu_{\infty}=\infty

We first note that (

Hence

∎

indeed converges smoothly locally to
U(T_{\max})
on
(\Omega_{\infty},G_{\infty})
and that (
5.5
) is valid also at
t=T_{\max}
.

We next explain why our construction ensures that the degree of
U(T_{\max})
is still
1
.
For this we can use that
(\Omega_{\infty}\setminus\{b\},G_{\infty})
is conformal to the infinite cylinder
C_{\infty}={\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}
via a diffeomorphism
F_{\infty}^{\text{cyl}}
which maps (punctured) balls
B_{R}(b)\setminus\{b\}
to cylinders
(-\infty,s_{R})\times S^{1}
.
We now note that given any map
w:C_{\infty}\to S^{2}
with finite energy and any
s^{*}\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
\int_{S^{1}}|\partial_{\theta}w(s^{*},\theta)|^{2}d\theta<\varepsilon\ll 1
, we can cut
w(s^{*},\theta)
off to a constant on unit cylinders and project onto
S^{2}
to obtain maps
w^{\pm}:C_{\infty}\to S^{2}
with
w^{\pm}\equiv w
for
\pm(s-s^{*})\geq 0
and
w^{\pm}
constant for
\pm(s-s^{*})\leq-1
so that

\deg(w^{+})+\deg(w^{-})=\deg(w)\text{ and }E(w^{+})+E(w^{-})=E(w)+o(1),

where
o(1)
can be made arbitrarily small by reducing
\varepsilon
, compare
[
28
, Section
3
]
.
The smooth local convergence of the maps
w(t):=U(t)\circ(F_{\infty}^{\text{cyl}})^{-1}
to
w(T_{\max}):=U(T_{\max})\circ(F_{\infty}^{\text{cyl}})^{-1}
means that we
can carry out this construction with a suitably chosen fixed
s^{*}>s_{R_{2}}
for all
t\leq T_{\max}
that are sufficiently close to
T_{\max}
. The resulting maps
U^{\pm}(t)=w^{\pm}(t)\circ(F_{\infty}^{\text{cyl}})
are so that
E(U^{+}(t))\leq E(u(t),\Sigma\setminus B_{R_{2}}(b))<3\pi
, ensuring that
\deg(U^{+}(t))=0
, and so that
U^{-}(t)\to U^{-}(T_{\max})
smoothly on
\Sigma
. We thus conclude that

\deg(U(T_{\max}))=\deg(U^{-}(T_{\max}))=\lim_{t\uparrow T_{\max}}\deg(U^{-}(t))=\lim_{t\uparrow T_{\max}}\deg(U(t))=1
.

We note that (
5.3
)
ensures that
U(T_{\max})
is harmonic if
T_{\max}=\infty
. If
T_{\max}<\infty
we instead use
U(T_{\max})
as initial data for the standard harmonic map flow on the sphere, i.e. extend our flow past
T_{\max}
as

\partial_{t}U(t)=\tau_{G_{\infty}}(U(t)),\qquad G(t)\equiv G_{\infty}\text{ on }\Omega_{\infty}\times[T_{\max},\infty).

Instead of using the Łojasiewicz estimate (
3.6
) from Theorem
3.5
, we can now use the
Łojasiewicz estimate for maps from
S^{2}
to itself obtained by Topping in
[
31
]
which allows us to bound

(5.6)

E(U(t),G_{\infty})-4\pi\leq C\|\tau_{G_{\infty}}(U(t))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty},G_{\infty})}^{2}

for as long as the solution
U(t)
,
t>T_{\max}
, remains smooth.
After reducing
\delta_{0}
if necessary, we can hence repeat the argument used in the proof of Proposition
4.2
to deduce that the solution remains smooth and
the estimates

(5.7)

E(U_{b}(t),\mathbb{D}_{\mu_{0}^{-1}(1+2\varepsilon_{2})})\geq 2\pi-2\varepsilon_{2}\text{ and }\max_{|y|\leq\mu_{0}^{-1}}E(U_{b}(t),\mathbb{D}_{\mu_{0}^{-1}(1-2\varepsilon_{2})}(y))\leq 2\pi+2\varepsilon_{2}

remain valid on all of
[T_{\max},\infty)
. Hence, as
t\to\infty
, the maps
U(t)
converge smoothly to a degree
1
harmonic map
U(\infty)
which satisfies
(
5.7
) and, thanks to
(
5.6
) and
(
4.8
),

\|U(T_{\max})-U(\infty)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty},G_{\infty})}\leq\int_{T_{\max}}^{\infty}\|\partial_{t}U\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty},G_{\infty})}dt\leq C(E(U(T_{\max}),G_{\infty})-4\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Irrespective of whether
T_{\max}
is finite or infinite, we can thus conclude that
U_{\infty}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}U(t)
is a harmonic map from the punctured sphere
(\Omega_{\infty},G_{\infty})
to
S^{2}
which satisfies (
5.7
) and

(5.8)

\|v-U_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty},G_{\infty})}\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We now note that
(
2.12
) (applied for
\mu_{1}=\mu_{0}
) ensures that

G_{\infty}\equiv G(0)\equiv\bar{g}_{\mu_{0},b}=F_{b}^{*}g_{\pi,\mu_{0}}\text{ on }B_{R_{2}}(b),

meaning that we can map

(\Omega_{\infty},G_{\infty})
isometrically to
({\mathbb{R}}^{2},g_{\pi,\mu_{0}})
using a diffeomorphism that is given by the identity in the usual conformal coordinates on
B_{R_{2}}(b)\subset\Omega_{\infty}
. The standard description of degree
1
harmonic maps from
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
to
S^{2}
hence allows us to write
U_{\infty}
in the usual coordinates on
B_{R_{2}}(b)
as

(5.9)

U_{\infty,b}:=U_{\infty}\circ F_{b}^{-1}={\mathcal{R}}\pi(\lambda(x-x_{0}))\text{ for some }x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\lambda>0\text{ and }{\mathcal{R}}\in SO(3)

and thus deduce that

(5.10)

\|v_{b}-{\mathcal{R}}\pi(\lambda(x-x_{0}))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{2}},g_{\pi,\mu_{0}})}\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since the energy estimate (
5.7
) also applies for
U_{\infty,b}

we immediately see that

(5.11)

|x_{0}|\lesssim\mu_{0}^{-1}\text{ and }\lambda\sim\mu_{0}\text{ and hence in particular }|x_{0}|+\lambda^{-1}\lesssim\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}},

compare Remark
4.3
.

We now prove that the claims of the theorem holds for this choice of
\lambda
,
{\mathcal{R}}
and
a:=F_{b}^{-1}(x_{0})
, noting that it suffices to carry out this argument
in the case where
{\mathcal{R}}=\text{Id}
(as we can otherwise replace

by
{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}v
) and that
(
1.9
) already follows from (
5.11
).

We begin by proving that
v_{a}:=v\circ F_{a}^{-1}

satisfies

(5.12)

\displaystyle\|v_{a}-\pi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota},g_{\pi,\lambda})}\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

To see this we note that since
(
5.11
) implies that
d_{g_{\Sigma}}(a,b)\lesssim\lambda^{-1}
is small,
the transition map between the conformal coordinate charts at
a
and

is essentially given by a translation, namely so that

F_{b,a}:=F_{a}\circ F_{b}^{-1}=Id-x_{0}+\text{err}_{b,a}
for an error term that
satisfies

|\text{err}_{b,a}(x)|\lesssim d_{g_{\Sigma}}^{2}(a,b)|x|+d_{g_{\Sigma}}(a,b)|x|^{2}\leq\lambda^{-1}|x|
, see (
7.4
).
As
|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}(x)|\sim|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}(\tilde{x})|
for all
x,\tilde{x}
with
|x-\tilde{x}|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}
, so in particular for points
\tilde{x}
on the segment connecting
x-x_{0}
and
F_{b,a}(x)
, we can hence bound

|\pi_{\lambda}(F_{b,a}(x))-\pi_{\lambda}(x-x_{0})|\lesssim|\text{err}_{b,a}(x)||\nabla\pi_{\lambda}(x)|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}|x||\nabla\pi_{\lambda}(x)|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}.

As (
5.11
) ensures that

\bar{g}_{\mu_{0},b}\sim\bar{g}_{\lambda,a}
and that
B_{R_{\Sigma}(\frac{1}{2}r_{2})}(a)\subset B_{R_{\Sigma}(r_{2})}(b)
we can combine this bound with (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\|v_{a}-\pi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{\frac{1}{2}r_{2}},g_{\pi,\lambda})}

\displaystyle=\|v-\pi_{\lambda}\circ F_{a}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{\Sigma}(\frac{1}{2}r_{2})}(a),\bar{g}_{\lambda,a})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v-\pi_{\lambda}\circ F_{a}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{\Sigma}(r_{2})}(b),\bar{g}_{\mu_{0},b})}=\|v_{b}-\pi_{\lambda}\circ F_{b,a}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{2}},g_{\pi,\mu_{0}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{b}-\pi(\lambda(x-x_{0}))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{2}},g_{\pi,\mu_{0}})}+\lambda^{-1}\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
\text{Area}_{g_{\pi,\lambda}}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\frac{1}{2}r_{2}})\lesssim\lambda^{-2}\lesssim\delta_{v}
, compare (
2.4
) and (
5.11
), and since we can trivially bound

this yields the
estimate (
5.12
) claimed above, i.e. the

version of the

-estimate (

) claimed in the theorem.

To carry out the proof of the remaining claims we now show that
the estimate

\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla v-\nabla\bar{z}|^{2}dv_{g_{\Sigma}}\leq C\delta_{v}

holds true for the

that was constructed by the first author in

]
, and whose precise definition is recalled in Section

.
This degree

map

is essentially given by

in the usual coordinates on

in the sense that

\|\bar{z}_{a}-\pi_{\lambda}\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota})}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad|\Delta\bar{z}_{a}-\Delta\pi_{\lambda}|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}|+\lambda^{-2},

and so that

and

\|\Delta_{g_{\Sigma}}\bar{z}\|_{C^{0}(\Sigma\setminus B_{I}(a))}\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
, compare
[
26
, Section 3]
, in particular estimate (3.15).
Here we continue to use
the convention that quantities on
\Sigma
are computed using
g_{\Sigma}
, while quantities on discs are computed using

unless indicated otherwise.

Similar to
[
33
]
and
[
28
]
we now estimate

(5.15)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla v-\nabla\bar{z}|^{2}=\int_{\Sigma}\nabla(v-\bar{z})\nabla(v+\bar{z}-2\bar{z})=2\int_{\Sigma}\Delta\bar{z}\cdot(v-\bar{z})+2(E(v)-E(\bar{z}))

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\iota}}\Delta\bar{z}_{a}\cdot(v_{a}-\bar{z}_{a})+4\|\Delta\bar{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma\setminus B_{I}(a))}+2\delta_{v}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\iota}}\Delta\pi_{\lambda}\cdot(v_{a}-\bar{z}_{a})+C\lambda^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\iota}}|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}||v_{a}-\bar{z}_{a}|+C\lambda^{-2}+2\delta_{v}

\displaystyle\leq-2\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\iota}}|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}|^{2}\pi_{\lambda}\cdot(v_{a}-\bar{z}_{a})+C\|v_{a}-\bar{z}_{a}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota},g_{\pi,\lambda})}^{2}+C\lambda^{-2}+2\delta_{v}.

Since
v_{a}
,
\bar{z}_{a}
and
\pi_{\lambda}
all map into
S^{2}
we have
pointwise estimates of

|P_{\pi_{\lambda}}^{\perp}(v_{a}-\pi_{\lambda})|\lesssim|v_{a}-\pi_{\lambda}|^{2}
and
|P_{\pi_{\lambda}}^{\perp}(\bar{z}_{a}-\pi_{\lambda})|\lesssim|\bar{z}_{a}-\pi_{\lambda}|^{2}\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
for the projections of these vectors onto the corresponding normal space
T^{\perp}_{\pi_{\lambda}}S^{2}=\text{span}(\pi_{\lambda})
.
Combined with (
5.14
), (
5.15
)
and the already established bounds (
5.12
)
and
\lambda^{-1}\lesssim\delta_{v}
this yields that

I\lesssim\|v_{a}-\pi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota},g_{\pi,\lambda})}^{2}+\lambda^{-2}+\delta_{v}\lesssim\delta_{v}
as claimed.

From (
5.13
), (
5.14
) and the conformal invariance of the energy we immediately deduce that

\|v_{a}-\pi_{\lambda}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota},g_{\pi,\lambda})}=\|\nabla(v_{a}-\pi_{\lambda})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota})}\lesssim\|\nabla(v_{a}-\bar{z}_{a})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota})}+\|\nabla(\bar{z}_{a}-\pi_{\lambda})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota})}\lesssim\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}},

thus completing the proof of (
1.8
), and obtain (
1.6
) as

(5.16)

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(a),g_{\Sigma})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(v-\bar{z})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(a),g_{\Sigma})}+\|\nabla\bar{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(a),g_{\Sigma})}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\lambda^{-1}+\|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r_{\Sigma}(R)})}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C(1+r_{\Sigma}(R)^{-1})\lambda^{-1}\lesssim R^{-1}\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\text{for all $R\in(0,I)$. }

To establish (
1.7
) we
first note that

\big|\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\iota}}[\pi_{\lambda}(x)-\pi(\infty)]\rho_{\Sigma}^{2}dx\big|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\iota}}(1+\lambda^{2}|x|^{2})^{-1}dx\lesssim\lambda^{-2}\log\lambda\leq\lambda^{-1},

as
\rho_{\Sigma}(x)=\rho_{\Sigma}(|x|)\sim 1
,

\pi^{1,2}(\infty)=0=\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}}\pi_{\lambda}^{1,2}
for every
r\in(0,\iota)
and
|\pi_{\lambda}^{3}(x)-\pi(\infty)|\lesssim(1+\lambda^{2}|x|^{2})^{-1}
.
As
\bar{z}_{a}=\pi_{\lambda}+O(\lambda^{-1})
on
\mathbb{D}_{\iota}
and
\bar{z}=\pi(\infty)+O(\lambda^{-1})
on
\Sigma\setminus B_{I}(a)
this ensures that

m_{\bar{z}}:=\fint_{\Sigma}\bar{z}dv_{g_{\Sigma}}=\pi(\infty)+O(\lambda^{-1})
.
Setting
m_{v}:=\fint_{\Sigma}vdv_{g_{\Sigma}}
, we can thus combine (
5.13
) with the Poincaré inequality on the fixed surface
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})

to bound

\displaystyle\|v-m_{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}

\displaystyle\leq\|v-\bar{z}-(m_{v}-m_{\bar{z}})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}+\|\bar{z}-m_{\bar{z}}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla(v-\bar{z})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}+\|\bar{z}-\pi(\infty)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}+\lambda^{-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\pi_{\lambda}-\pi(\infty)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{\iota},\rho_{\Sigma}^{2}dx^{2})}+\lambda^{-1}\lesssim\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda^{-1}(1+(\log\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}})\lesssim\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\log\delta_{v}|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

As
|v|=1
on
\Sigma
this also ensures that

|1-|m_{v}||\leq(\text{Area}_{g_{\Sigma}}(\Sigma))^{-1}\|v-m_{v}\|_{L^{1}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}\lesssim\delta_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\log\delta_{v}|^{\frac{1}{2}}

and hence that (
1.7
) holds for
p:=\frac{m_{v}}{|m_{v}|}
.

This completes the proof of our main theorem, up to the proof of the Łojasiewicz estimates from Theorem

which is carried out in the remaining part of this paper.

6.
Proof of Theorem
3.8

In this section we prove the Łojasiewicz estimates stated in Theorem
3.8
, based on some technical estimates whose proof will be given in the subsequent
Section
7
.
In the current and the next section we will use the
convention that all statements are to be understood to hold true provided the numbers
\lambda_{0}>1
and
\varepsilon_{1}>0
that appear in the theorem are chosen sufficiently large, respectively small, and will furthermore use the shorthand
o(1)
to denote quantities that can be made smaller than any given positive constant by choosing
\varepsilon_{1}
(and later also
\varepsilon_{0}
) small enough and
\lambda_{0}
large enough.

The key idea of the proof is to use dimension reduction techniques and carry out a precise analysis of the energy and its variation on a suitably chosen finite dimensional set of models following the approach of
[
21
]
, where in the present setting the models are given by suitably chosen pairs of maps and metrics.
For this we want to work with a

6
parameter family

(6.1)

{\mathcal{Z}}:=\{{\mathcal{R}}\bar{z}_{\lambda,a},\quad{\mathcal{R}}\in SO(3),\,a\in\Sigma,\,\lambda\geq 2\lambda_{0}\}

of maps which are so that

(6.2)

\bar{z}_{\lambda,a}\approx\pi_{\lambda}\circ F_{a}\text{ on }B_{I}(a)\text{ and }\bar{z}_{\lambda,a}\approx\pi(\infty)\text{ on }\Sigma\setminus B_{I}(a).

We note that maps
\bar{z}_{\lambda,a}:\Sigma\to S^{2}
for which (
6.2
) holds up to carefully chosen modifications of order
\lambda^{-1}
in
C^{1}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
were constructed by the first author in
[
26
]
in a way that yields the crucial bounds

(6.3)

|E(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a})-4\pi|\lesssim\lambda^{-2},\qquad-\tfrac{d}{d\lambda}E(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a})\sim\lambda^{-3}

and

(6.4)

|dE(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a})(w)|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}(\log\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma,\bar{g}_{\lambda,a})}\text{ for all }w\in\Gamma^{H^{1}}(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a}^{*}TS^{2}).

Another key feature of
{\mathcal{Z}}
concerns the behaviour of the second variation of the energy

(6.5)

d^{2}E(u)(v,w):=\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}\tfrac{d}{d\delta}E(\pi_{S^{2}}(u+\varepsilon v+\delta w))=\int_{\Sigma}\nabla v\cdot\nabla w-|\nabla u|^{2}v\cdot w\,dv_{g_{\Sigma}},v,w\in\Gamma^{H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}(u^{*}TS^{2}).

To make this precise, we note that we are working in a setting where the bubbles
\omega:S^{2}\to S^{2}
which we glue onto constant maps are minimisers, rather than more general critical points as considered in
[
26
,
27
]
, and that the kernel of

coincides with the tangent space to the set of these bubbles, i.e. degree

Möbius maps. Thus the construction of the maps
\bar{z}_{\lambda,a}
in

]
ensures that
the restriction of

to the

-orthogonal complement

of

in
\Gamma^{H^{1}}(\bar{z}^{*}TS^{2})

is uniformly positive definite.

Remark 6.1
.

While we will only ever evaluate

for

which are tangent to

at

, it will at times be useful to extend the definition of

to general vector fields, and we will do this by extending

by zero in normal directions, i.e. setting

.
We note that this allows us to write

for general

and hence to compute

\tfrac{d}{dt}(dE(u_{t})(f_{t}))=d^{2}E(u_{t})(\partial_{t}u_{t},f_{t})+dE(u_{t})(\partial_{t}f_{t}),

for any
family of maps

and corresponding tangent fields
f_{t}\in\Gamma^{H^{1}}(u_{t}^{*}TS^{2})
. We note that for vector fields

which are obtained
by projecting a fixed

the second term

cannot be expected to vanish unless
f
happens to tangential to
T_{u_{t}}S^{2}
at the corresponding time

.

In addition to these maps
\bar{z}_{\lambda,a}
we will use
the weighted metrics
{\bar{g}_{\mu,b}}

defined in Section
2
.
While we will not impose that the parameters
(\lambda,a)
of the map and

of the metric coincide, we restrict our attention to pairs of parameter which are compatible in the sense that

(6.7)

\lambda^{-1}\mu\in[1-\varepsilon_{0},1+\varepsilon_{0}]\text{ and }d_{a,b}:=d_{\Sigma}(a,b)\leq\varepsilon_{0}\lambda^{-1},

for a small number
\varepsilon_{0}>0
for which we use the convention described at the start of the section. We note that
this compatibility condition and the construction of the maps

in particular ensure that

|\nabla_{g_{\Sigma}}\bar{z}_{\lambda,a}|\lesssim\bar{\rho}_{\lambda,a}\text{ and }\bar{\rho}_{\lambda,a}\sim\bar{\rho}_{\mu,b}.

As in the rest of the paper it will be crucial to view the energy as a function of both map and metric and to exploit the flexibility that is provided by working either with pairs
(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a},\bar{g}_{\mu,b})
for which the metric is conformal to

or their pull-backs

(z_{\mu,b}^{\lambda,a},g_{\mu,b})=T_{\mu,b}^{*}(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a},\bar{g}_{\mu,b})
,
T_{\mu,b}
as in Section

, as convenient.

We note that (
6.4
) already provides a bound on
dE(z_{\mu,b}^{\lambda,a},g_{\mu,b})(f,0)=dE(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a},\bar{g}_{\mu,b})(\bar{f},0)=dE(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a})(\bar{f})
and will furthermore show the following estimate for the second variation

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
a,b,\mu,\lambda
be parameters which are compatible as described in (
6.7
). Then we can bound

(6.9)

|d^{2}E(z,g)(\partial_{\varepsilon}(z_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon}),(f,0))|\leq C\big[\lambda^{-3}(\log\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}}|\partial_{\varepsilon}\lambda|+\lambda^{-1}|\partial_{\varepsilon}a|+\lambda^{-3}|\partial_{\varepsilon}\mu_{\varepsilon}|\big],

for any variation

(z_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})=T_{\mu_{\varepsilon},b}^{*}(\bar{z}_{\lambda_{\varepsilon},a_{\varepsilon}},\bar{g}_{\mu_{\varepsilon},b})

and any

with
\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma,g)}=1
.

We note that
d^{2}E(z,g)(\partial_{\varepsilon}(z_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon}),(f,0)):=\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}\tfrac{d}{d\delta}E(\pi_{S^{2}}(z_{\varepsilon}+\delta f),g_{\varepsilon})=\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}[dE(z_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})(f,0)]

as

at

As the metrics
\bar{g}_{\mu,b}

only depends
on

and

. The dependence on

|\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}E(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}|\partial_{\varepsilon}a|,

Variations for which

\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}E(z_{\varepsilon},g)\sim\lambda^{-3}.

While such variations of

always result in a change of

, we can ensure that the change of the pulled-back maps and metrics is far smaller as described in the following lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let

and

(7.5)

|\nabla_{a}\text{err}_{b,a}(x)|+|x||\nabla_{x}\nabla_{a}\text{err}_{b,a}(x)|\lesssim|x|^{2}+d_{a,b}|x|.

The construction of these maps ensures that

(7.6)

\bar{z}_{\lambda,a}=\hat{z}_{\lambda,a}\circ F_{a}\text{ on }B_{R_{\Sigma}(\frac{1}{2}r_{0})}(a)\text{ for }\hat{z}_{\lambda,a}:=\pi_{S^{2}}(\hat{v}_{\lambda,a})=\pi_{S^{2}}(\pi_{\lambda}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{2}{\la}$}}\widehat{j_{a}}(x))

as well as that for any fixed
R>0

(7.7)

\displaystyle\|\bar{z}_{\lambda,a}-\pi(\infty)\|_{C^{1}(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(a))}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\varepsilon}\bar{z}_{\lambda_{\varepsilon},a_{\varepsilon}}\|_{C^{1}(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(a))}\lesssim\lambda^{-2}|\partial_{\varepsilon}\lambda|+\lambda^{-1}|\partial_{\varepsilon}a|,

(7.8)

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{g_{\Sigma}}\bar{v}_{\lambda,a}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(a))}\lesssim\lambda^{-2}

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\varepsilon}\Delta_{g_{\Sigma}}\bar{v}_{\lambda_{\varepsilon},a_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(a))}\lesssim\lambda^{-3}|\partial_{\varepsilon}\lambda|+\lambda^{-2}|\partial_{\varepsilon}a|.

These estimates in particular ensure that

(7.9)

\|\tau(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(a))}\lesssim\lambda^{-2}\text{ and }\|\partial_{\varepsilon}\tau(\bar{z}_{\lambda_{\varepsilon},a_{\varepsilon}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(a))}\lesssim\lambda^{-3}|\partial_{\varepsilon}\lambda|+\lambda^{-2}|\partial_{\varepsilon}a|.

Writing

\tau(\hat{z}_{\lambda,a})=P_{\hat{z}_{\lambda,a}}(\Delta\pi_{S^{2}}(\hat{v}_{\lambda,a}))=P_{\hat{z}_{\lambda,a}}(d^{2}\pi_{S^{2}}(\hat{v}_{\lambda,a})(\nabla\hat{v}_{\lambda,a},\nabla\hat{v}_{\lambda,a})+d\pi_{S^{2}}(\hat{v}_{\lambda,a})(\Delta\hat{v}_{\lambda,a}))

and using that
\widehat{j_{a}}

is a harmonic function which vanishes at
x=0
one can also see that

(7.10)

\displaystyle|\tau(\hat{z}_{\lambda,a})(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-2}+\lambda^{-1}|x||\nabla\pi_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}+\lambda^{-1}|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}(x)|(1+\lambda|x|)^{-1}\sim\lambda^{-2}+(1+\lambda|x|)^{-3},

compare also

[
26
, Estimate (3.49)]
as well as that

(7.11)

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\varepsilon}\tau(\hat{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle|\partial_{\varepsilon}\hat{v}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}}|(|\nabla\hat{v}_{\lambda,a}|^{2}+|\Delta\hat{v}_{\lambda,a}|)+|\nabla\partial_{\varepsilon}\hat{v}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}}||\nabla\hat{v}_{\lambda,a}|\lesssim\;\lambda^{-1}(|x||\nabla\pi_{\lambda}|^{2}+|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}|)\lesssim\lambda^{-1}|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}|

whenever
|\partial_{\varepsilon}a|\lesssim 1
.
In particular

(7.12)

\|\tau(\hat{z}_{\lambda,a})(1+\lambda|x|)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}})}+\|\partial_{\varepsilon}\tau(\hat{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}|^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}})}\lesssim\lambda^{-1},

noting that the second term is simply the expression for
\|\partial_{\varepsilon}\tau_{\bar{g}_{\lambda,a}}(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}}\circ F_{a_{\varepsilon},a})\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{1}}(a),\bar{g}_{\lambda,a})}
in local coordinates.

7.2.
Variations of
E(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a})
with respect to
a

Here we briefly explain how the above estimates imply that
\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}E(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
whenever

|\partial_{\varepsilon}a|=1
, in order to establish the estimate
(
6.10
) used in the previous section.
To see this we fix a function

\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{\frac{1}{2}r_{0}},[0,1])
with
\psi\equiv 1
on
\mathbb{D}_{\frac{1}{3}r_{0}}
and use the cut-off functions
\psi^{a_{\varepsilon}}:=\psi\circ F_{a_{\varepsilon}}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{R_{\Sigma}(\frac{1}{2}r_{0})}(a_{\varepsilon}),[0,1])
to
split

E(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})=\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{2}$}}\int_{\Sigma}(1-\psi^{a_{\varepsilon}})|\nabla_{g_{\Sigma}}\bar{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}}|^{2}dv_{g_{\Sigma}}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{2}$}}\int_{\Sigma}\psi^{a_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla_{g_{\Sigma}}\bar{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}}|^{2}dv_{g_{\Sigma}}=:I_{1}(\varepsilon)+I_{2}(\varepsilon).

Since
1-\psi^{a_{\varepsilon}}
vanishes on a fixed ball
B_{R}(a)
(for
\varepsilon
small) we immediately get from (
7.7
) that

|\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}I_{1}|\leq C\lambda^{-2}.

We now use that
\bar{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}}=\hat{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}}\circ F_{a_{\varepsilon}}
on the support of
\psi^{a_{\varepsilon}}=\psi\circ F_{a_{\varepsilon}}
, compare (
7.6
), and the conformal invariance of
E

to write

I_{2}(\varepsilon)=\tfrac{1}{2}\int\psi|\nabla\hat{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}}|^{2}dx
. As
\partial_{\varepsilon}\hat{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}}\in T_{\hat{z}_{\lambda,a}}S^{2}

we hence get that

(7.13)

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int\psi\nabla\partial_{\varepsilon}\hat{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}}\nabla\hat{z}_{\lambda,a}dx=-\int\nabla\psi\partial_{\varepsilon}\hat{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}}\nabla\hat{z}_{\lambda,a}dx-\int\psi\partial_{\varepsilon}\hat{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}}\tau(\hat{z}_{\lambda,a})dx.

As
\widehat{j_{a}}
is smooth with
\widehat{j_{a_{\varepsilon}}}(0)=0
we can bound

|\partial_{\varepsilon}\hat{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}}(x)|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}|\partial_{\varepsilon}\widehat{j_{a_{\varepsilon}}}(x)|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}|x|\leq\lambda^{-2}(1+\lambda|x|)
. Combined with
(
7.12
)
and the fact that

|\nabla\hat{z}_{\lambda,a}|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}
on

\text{supp}(\nabla\psi)

this allows us to estimate

(7.14)

\displaystyle|\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}I_{2}(\varepsilon)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-2}+\lambda^{-2}\|(1+\lambda|x|)|\tau(\hat{z}_{\lambda,a})|\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}})}\lesssim\lambda^{-2},

thus completing the proof of the claimed bound
\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}E(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
that we need to establish (
6.10
).

7.3.
Proof of Lemma
6.3

Here we establish
Lemma
6.3
, i.e.
that
\|\partial_{\varepsilon}z_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\Sigma,g)}+\|\partial_{\varepsilon}z_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
for

z_{\varepsilon}=\bar{z}_{\lambda_{\varepsilon},a_{\varepsilon}}\circ T_{\mu_{\varepsilon},b}
determined by parameters
\mu_{\varepsilon}
,
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
and
a_{\varepsilon}
satisfying (
6.12
) and
g=g_{\mu,b}
.
As the parameters are assumed to be compatible, compare (
6.7
), we know that
\Sigma_{0}:=\Sigma\setminus B_{R_{\Sigma}(r_{1})}(b)
is disjoint from a fixed sized ball
B_{R}(a)
around
a

and that

B_{R_{\Sigma}(r_{1})}(b)
is contained in the ball
B_{R_{\Sigma}(\frac{1}{2}r_{0})}(a_{\varepsilon})
where (
7.6
) is applicable.

On
\Sigma_{0}
we have
T_{\mu,b}\equiv\id
,
so the maps and metrics are simply given by

z_{\varepsilon}=\bar{z}_{\lambda_{\varepsilon},a_{\varepsilon}}
and
g={\bar{g}_{\mu,b}}
and the claimed bounds trivially hold as
(
7.7
) ensures that

\|\partial_{\varepsilon}z_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\Sigma_{0},g)}+\|\partial_{\varepsilon}z_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{0})}\lesssim\|\partial_{\varepsilon}\bar{z}_{\lambda_{\varepsilon},a_{\varepsilon}}\|_{C^{1}(\Sigma_{0})}\lesssim\lambda^{-2}|\partial_{\varepsilon}\lambda|+\lambda^{-1}|\partial_{\varepsilon}a|\lesssim\lambda^{-2},

since
|\partial_{\varepsilon}\lambda|\sim 1
and
|\partial_{\varepsilon}a|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
, compare (
6.14
).

To establish the claims on

B_{R_{\Sigma}(r_{1})}(b)
where
g_{\mu,b}=(T_{\mu}\circ F_{b})^{*}g_{\pi,\mu}

we can work
in the (
\varepsilon
independent) coordinates
x=T_{\mu}(F_{b}(p))
in which
z_{\varepsilon}=\bar{z}_{\lambda_{\varepsilon},a_{\varepsilon}}\circ T_{\mu_{\varepsilon},b}=\pi_{S^{2}}\circ\hat{v}_{\lambda_{\varepsilon},a_{\varepsilon}}\circ F_{a_{\varepsilon}}\circ F_{b}^{-1}\circ T_{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\circ F_{b}

is given by

(7.15)

\tilde{z}_{\varepsilon}(x):=z_{\varepsilon}\circ F_{b}^{-1}\circ T_{\mu}^{-1}=\pi_{S^{2}}(\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}+\tilde{j}_{\varepsilon})\text{ for }\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}=\pi_{\lambda_{\varepsilon}}\circ F_{b,a_{\varepsilon}}\circ T_{\mu,\mu_{\varepsilon}},\;\tilde{j}_{\varepsilon}:=\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{2}{\la_\eps}$}}\widehat{j_{a_{\varepsilon}}}\circ F_{b,a_{\varepsilon}}\circ T_{\mu,\mu_{\varepsilon}},

compare (
7.1
) and (
7.2
).
The claimed bound on

\|\partial_{\varepsilon}z_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}
thus follows if we prove that
\|\partial_{\varepsilon}\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{\varepsilon}\tilde{j}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
,
while this and the fact that

|\nabla(\tilde{w}+\tilde{j})|\lesssim|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}\circ F_{b,a}|+\lambda^{-1}\lesssim|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}|
, compare (
6.8
), then
reduce the proof of the required
H^{1}
bound to showing that
\|\nabla\partial_{\varepsilon}\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}})}+\|\nabla\partial_{\varepsilon}\tilde{j}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}})}\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
.

As

\|\partial_{\varepsilon}\widehat{j_{a_{\varepsilon}}}\|_{C^{1}}+|\partial_{\varepsilon}{F_{b,a_{\varepsilon}}}|\lesssim|\partial_{\varepsilon}a_{\varepsilon}|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
, and as (
2.7
) ensures that

\partial_{\varepsilon}T_{\mu,\mu_{\varepsilon}}
is of order
O(\lambda^{-1})
in
H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
, the required bounds on
\partial_{\varepsilon}\tilde{j}_{\varepsilon}
immediately follow.

It hence remains to analyse
\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}=\pi\circ F_{\varepsilon}
,

F_{\varepsilon}(x):=\lambda_{\varepsilon}F_{b,a_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\mu,\mu_{\varepsilon}}(x)).

As the
transition maps of
T_{\mu}
and
S_{\mu}=\mu T_{\mu}
are related by

T_{\mu,\tilde{\mu}}(x)=\tfrac{1}{\tilde{\mu}}S_{\mu,\tilde{\mu}}(\mu x)

we can write

F_{\varepsilon}(x):=\lambda_{\varepsilon}F_{b,a_{\varepsilon}}(\frac{1}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}S_{\mu,\mu_{\varepsilon}}(\mu x))

and hence use

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}=\tfrac{\lambda}{\mu}dF_{b,a}(x)\partial_{\varepsilon}S_{\mu,\mu_{\varepsilon}}(\mu x)+\partial_{\varepsilon}[\lambda_{\varepsilon}F_{b,a_{\varepsilon}}(\tfrac{\mu x}{\mu_{\varepsilon}})]=:f_{1}+f_{2}

to split
\partial_{\varepsilon}\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}=d\pi(\lambda F_{b,a}(x))f_{1}+d\pi(\lambda F_{b,a}(x))f_{2}
.
We note that

(7.16)

|(d\pi)(\lambda F_{b,a}(x)|\lesssim(1+\lambda|x|)^{-2}\text{ and }|\nabla((d\pi)(\lambda F_{b,a}(x)))|\lesssim\lambda(1+\lambda|x|)^{-3}

since
|(d\pi)(y)|\sim(1+|y|)^{-2}
,
|(d^{2}\pi)(y)|\lesssim(1+|y|)^{-3}
and since (
6.7
) guarantees
1+|\lambda F_{b,a}(x)|\sim 1+\lambda|x|
.

Our choice of variation and Remark
7.1
allow us to write

\displaystyle f_{2}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\varepsilon}[\tfrac{\lambda_{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\mu x-\lambda_{\varepsilon}F_{b}(a_{\varepsilon})+\lambda_{\varepsilon}\text{err}_{b,a_{\varepsilon}}(\tfrac{\mu x}{\mu_{\varepsilon}})]=\partial_{\varepsilon}[\lambda_{\varepsilon}\text{err}_{b,a_{\varepsilon}}(\tfrac{\mu x}{\mu_{\varepsilon}})]

\displaystyle=\partial_{\varepsilon}\lambda_{\varepsilon}\text{err}_{b,a}(x)+\lambda\nabla_{a}\text{err}_{b,a}(x)\partial_{\varepsilon}a-\lambda\mu^{-1}\partial_{\varepsilon}\mu\nabla_{x}\text{err}_{b,a}(x)x

and hence use (
7.4
), (
7.5
) and
|\partial_{\varepsilon}a_{\varepsilon}|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
to bound

\displaystyle|f_{2}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim(d_{a,b}+\lambda^{-1})(|x|^{2}+d_{a,b}|x|)\lesssim\lambda^{-2}|x|(1+\lambda|x|)

\displaystyle|\nabla f_{2}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\nabla_{x}\text{err}_{b,a}(x)|+\lambda|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{a}\text{err}_{b,a}(x)||\partial_{\varepsilon}a|+|\nabla_{x}^{2}\text{err}_{b,a}(x)||x|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}(1+\lambda|x|).

Combined with (
7.16
)
this immediately gives the required
H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
bounds for
d\pi(\lambda F_{b,a})f_{2}
.

To obtain the analogue bounds on
d\pi(\lambda F_{b,a})f_{1}
it now suffices to recall from (
2.7
) that

(7.17)

\partial_{\varepsilon}S_{\mu,\mu+\varepsilon}(\mu x)=\partial_{\varepsilon}((\mu+\varepsilon)T_{\mu,\mu+\varepsilon}(x))=x-\phi_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x)x=\psi(\log r_{1}^{-1}|x|)x

is supported on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r_{2}}
where both
|(d\pi)(\lambda F_{b,a}(x)|
and
|\nabla((d\pi)(\lambda F_{b,a}(x)))|

are of order
O(\lambda^{-2})
.

7.4.
Bounds on the second variation

We finally establish Lemma
6.2
, i.e. show that (
6.9
) holds for any
f\in\Gamma^{H^{1}}(z^{*}TS^{2})
with

\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma,g)}=1
and any variation

(z_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})=T_{\mu_{\varepsilon},b}^{*}(\bar{z}_{\varepsilon}=\bar{z}_{\lambda_{\varepsilon},a_{\varepsilon}},\bar{g}_{\varepsilon}=\bar{g}_{\mu_{\varepsilon},b})
.
As all
\bar{g}_{\varepsilon}
are conformal to
g_{\Sigma}
we can write

\displaystyle d^{2}E(z,g)(\partial_{\varepsilon}(z_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon}),(f,0))=\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}dE(z_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})(f,0)=\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}[dE(\bar{z}_{\varepsilon},\bar{g}_{\varepsilon})(f\circ T_{\mu_{\varepsilon},b}^{-1},0)]=\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}\big[dE(\bar{z}_{\varepsilon})(\bar{f}\circ{T}_{\mu_{\varepsilon},\mu,b})\big]

for

T_{\mu_{\varepsilon},\mu,b}:=T_{\mu,b}\circ T_{\mu_{\varepsilon},b}^{-1}
and

\bar{f}:=f\circ T_{\mu,b}^{-1}
and use that

\|\bar{f}\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma,\bar{g}_{\lambda,a})}\sim\|\bar{f}\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma,\bar{g}_{\mu,b})}=1
, compare (
6.8
).

As an estimate of

|\partial_{\lambda}\big[dE(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a})(\bar{f})\big]|\lesssim\lambda^{-3}(\log\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}}
was already established in Lemma 3.11 of
[
26
]
it hence suffices to prove that

(7.18)

\displaystyle|\partial_{\varepsilon}\big[dE(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})(\bar{f})\big]|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}\text{ for }|\partial_{\varepsilon}a_{\varepsilon}|=1

(7.19)

\displaystyle|dE(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a})(\partial_{\varepsilon}\bar{f}_{\varepsilon})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda^{-3}\text{ for }\bar{f}_{\varepsilon}:=\bar{f}\circ T_{\mu+\varepsilon,\mu,b}

To prove (
7.19
) we
use that
\partial_{\varepsilon}T_{\mu+\varepsilon,\mu,b}
, and hence

\partial_{\varepsilon}\bar{f}_{\varepsilon}
, is
supported on

B_{R_{\Sigma}(r_{1})}(b)\subset B_{R_{\Sigma}(\frac{1}{2}r_{0})}(a)
and work in the coordinates
x=F_{a}(p)
in which

\bar{z}
is given by
\hat{z}_{\lambda,a}
from (
7.1
),
while
\bar{f}_{\varepsilon}
is represented by

\bar{f}_{a}\circ\hat{T}_{\varepsilon}
for
\hat{T}_{\varepsilon}=F_{a,b}^{-1}\circ T_{\mu+\varepsilon,\mu}\circ F_{a,b}
.
As

\partial_{\varepsilon}T_{\mu+\varepsilon,\mu}=-\partial_{\varepsilon}T_{\mu,\mu+\varepsilon}
is described by (
2.7
) we have

(7.20)

|\partial_{\varepsilon}\hat{T}_{\varepsilon}(x)|\lesssim|\partial_{\varepsilon}T_{\mu,\mu+\varepsilon}(F_{a,b}(x))|\lesssim\mu^{-1}|F_{a,b}(x)|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}(|x|+d_{a,b})\lesssim\lambda^{-2}(1+\lambda|x|)

allowing us to use (
7.12
) to bound

(7.21)

|dE(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a})(\partial_{\varepsilon}\bar{f}_{\varepsilon})|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}}\tau(\hat{z}_{\lambda,a})\nabla\bar{f}_{a}\partial_{\varepsilon}\hat{T}_{\varepsilon}dx\right|\leq\lambda^{-2}\|\nabla\bar{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}\|\tau(\hat{z}_{\lambda,a})(1+\lambda|x|)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}})}\lesssim\lambda^{-3}.

To prove
(
7.18
) we argue as in Section
7.2
, using cut-off functions

\psi^{a_{\varepsilon}}:=\psi\circ F_{a_{\varepsilon}}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{R_{\Sigma}(\frac{1}{2}r_{0})}(a_{\varepsilon}),[0,1])
with
\psi\equiv 1
on
\mathbb{D}_{\frac{1}{3}r_{0}}

to split
dE(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})(\bar{f})=\int_{\Sigma}\tau_{g_{\Sigma}}(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})\bar{f}dv_{g_{\Sigma}}
as

(7.22)

\displaystyle dE(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})(\bar{f})=\int_{\Sigma}(1-\psi^{a_{\varepsilon}})\tau_{g_{\Sigma}}(\bar{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})\cdot\bar{f}dv_{g_{\Sigma}}+\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\iota}}\psi\tau({\hat{z}}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})\cdot\bar{f}_{a_{\varepsilon}}dx=:I_{1}(\varepsilon)+I_{2}(\varepsilon)

where
\bar{f}_{a_{\varepsilon}}=\bar{f}_{a}\circ F_{a,a_{\varepsilon}}
is the function that represents
\bar{f}
in the coordinates
x=F_{a_{\varepsilon}}(p)
.
Estimate
(
7.9
) immediately implies that

|\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}I_{1}|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}|\partial_{\varepsilon}a_{\varepsilon}|=\lambda^{-2}
while

|\partial_{\varepsilon}F_{a,a_{\varepsilon}}|\lesssim 1
and (
7.12
) allow us to bound

(7.23)

\displaystyle|\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}I_{2}(\varepsilon)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}}|\tau(\hat{z}_{\lambda,a})||\nabla\bar{f}_{a}|dx+\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}}|\partial_{\varepsilon}\tau(\hat{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})||\bar{f}_{a}|dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tau(\hat{z}_{\lambda,a})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}})}+\|\partial_{\varepsilon}\tau(\hat{z}_{\lambda,a_{\varepsilon}})|\nabla\pi_{\lambda}|^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}})}\|\bar{f}_{a}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}},g_{\pi,\lambda})}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}.

This completes the proof of (
7.18
) and hence the proof of the lemma.
