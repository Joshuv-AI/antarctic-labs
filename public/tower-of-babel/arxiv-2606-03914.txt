Quantum Erasure Imaging: Complementary Modalities from Delayed-Choice Erasure
Sean D. Huver, Sanjaya Lohani

Abstract
Quantum Erasure Imaging (QEI) turns delayed-choice erasure into a practical imaging protocol. Entangled photon pairs encode two classical modalities, absorption T ⁡ ( x , y ) T(x,y) and a phase-sensitive cosine quadrature of ϕ ⁡ ( x , y ) \phi(x,y) , reconstructed from a single run of time-tagged coincidences by retrospective sorting on a remote ancilla. Measuring the ancilla in H/V yields T T via which-path information; D/A yields interference visibility ∝ 2 ​ T T + 1 ​ cos ⁡ ϕ \propto\frac{2\sqrt{T}}{T+1}\cos\phi ; and a rotated orthonormal analyzer continuously trades between them. We derive balanced two-port estimators whose denominators are analyzer independent (completeness / no signaling), together with Fisher information (FI) and Cramér–Rao bounds (CRBs) that establish an equivalence to time division under labeled randomization. The advantages of QEI are operational: single-run acquisition, perfect co-registration, and remote / delayed mode choice. We illustrate the protocol with Monte-Carlo simulations and open source our code.



I
Introduction

QEI turns delayed-choice quantum erasure into an imaging protocol
Wheeler 1978
;
Scully and Drühl 1982
;
Kim et al. 2000
;
Walborn et al. 2002
: from a single labeled acquisition of time-tagged coincidences, a remote measurement on an entangled ancilla retrospectively sorts the event stream into complementary imaging channels. The which-path channel reconstructs absorption
T(x,y)

Bagan et al. 2016
, while the erasure channel reconstructs a co-registered phase-sensitive visibility proportional to
\cos\phi(x,y)
. Rotating the ancilla analyzer continuously tunes the relative weight of which-path information and interference contrast.

The protocol does not circumvent complementarity: the analyzer angle sets predictability and visibility in accordance with the standard bound
Englert 1996
;
Jacques et al. 2008
(saturating it near
T\simeq 1
). The complete protocol delivers single-run acquisition of co-registered complementary observables with the object arm unchanged and basis choices that can be randomized or delayed within the same time-tagged dataset.

Quantum‑eraser experiments, including delayed‑choice variants, have long established the role of context‑dependent interference

;

;

;

;

;

;

;

;
Kaiser et al. 2012
, and QEI draws directly on this foundation by repurposing specifically the underlying mechanism for image formation with explicit

;
Erkmen and Shapiro 2010
;
Bennink et al. 2002
;

;

;

Prior demonstrations of two‑photon imaging quantum erasers and random delayed‑choice implementations have likewise shown wave–particle behavior in imaging geometries

;
Wang et al. 2019
, and QEI extends this trajectory by integrating these ideas into a unified, operationally efficient imaging protocol.

II
Theoretical Framework

We prepare polarization-entangled pairs via type-II spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC)
Kwiat et al. 1995
:

\ket{\Psi_{0}}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\!\left(\ket{H}_{A}\ket{H}_{B}+\ket{V}_{A}\ket{V}_{B}\right).

(1)

PBS1 maps
\ket{H}_{A}\!\to
Arm 1 (sample),
\ket{V}_{A}\!\to
Arm 2 (reference), creating path–polarization entanglement:

\ket{\Psi_{1}}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\!\left(\ket{\mathrm{H}}_{Arm1_{A}}\ket{H}_{B}+\ket{\mathrm{V}}_{Arm2_{A}}\ket{V}_{B}\right).

(2)

The sample imparts transmission
T(x,y)
and phase
\phi(x,y)
in Arm 1. A half-wave plate in Arm 2 rotates
\ket{V}\!\to\!\ket{H}
so both paths share polarization at recombination. Note that for absorbing samples (
T<1
), this unnormalized expression implicitly conditions on the photon not being lost to vacuum:

\ket{\Psi_{2}}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\!\left(\sqrt{T}e^{i\phi}\ket{\mathrm{H}}_{Arm1_{A}}\ket{H}_{B}+\ket{\mathrm{H}}_{Arm2_{A}}\ket{V}_{B}\right).

(3)

BS2 (50/50) maps

\displaystyle\ket{\mathrm{H}}_{Arm1_{A}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\!\left(\ket{D1}_{A}+\ket{D2}_{A}\right),

(4)

\displaystyle\ket{\mathrm{H}}_{Arm2_{A}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\!\left(\ket{D1}_{A}-\ket{D2}_{A}\right),

(5)

yielding

\begin{split}\ket{\Psi_{3}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\Big[&\ket{D1}_{A}(\sqrt{T}e^{i\phi}\ket{H}_{B}+\ket{V}_{B})\\
&+\ket{D2}_{A}(\sqrt{T}e^{i\phi}\ket{H}_{B}-\ket{V}_{B})\Big].\end{split}

(6)

R(D1,H)\propto T,\quad R(D1,V)\propto 1\quad(\text{and similarly for D2}),

(7)

\widehat{T}(x,y)=\frac{R(D1,H)+R(D2,H)}{R(D1,V)+R(D2,V)}.

(8)

II.2
Intermediate Analyzer

We can also introduce an orthonormal rotation to continuously tune which-path information versus interference. We rotate B’s polarization in the following orthonormal basis:

\displaystyle\ket{e_{1}(\theta)}

\displaystyle=\cos\theta\,\ket{H}+\sin\theta\,\ket{V},

(12a)

\displaystyle\ket{e_{2}(\theta)}

\displaystyle=-\sin\theta\,\ket{H}+\cos\theta\,\ket{V}.

(12b)

Projecting Eq. (
6
) onto
\{\ket{e_{1,2}}\}_{B}
, the D1 intensities are

\displaystyle R(D1,e_{1})

\displaystyle\propto T\cos^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta+2\sqrt{T}\sin\theta\cos\theta\cos\phi,

(13)

\displaystyle R(D1,e_{2})

\displaystyle\propto T\sin^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\theta-2\sqrt{T}\sin\theta\cos\theta\cos\phi,

(14)

with the interference term reversing sign at D2. Summing over
\ket{e_{1,2}}
on a given port gives
T+1
(up to normalization), independent of
\theta
, as required by completeness/no-signaling:

\displaystyle R(D1,e_{1})+R(D1,e_{2})

\displaystyle\propto T+1,

(15a)

\displaystyle R(D2,e_{1})+R(D2,e_{2})

\displaystyle\propto T+1.

(15b)

Using both ports, the normalized differential (generalizing Eq. (
11
)) is

V_{\theta}(x,y)=\frac{2\sqrt{T(x,y)}\,\sin(2\theta)}{T(x,y)+1}\,\cos\phi(x,y),

(16)

which reduces to Eq. (
11
) at
\theta=\pi/4
and vanishes at
\theta=0,\pi/2
. Notably, for the two-port estimator the denominator is
\theta
-independent.

III
Estimator Statistics and Cramér–Rao Bounds

Let
N
be the expected coincidences per pixel in one run; a fraction
\alpha
is measured in H/V (WPI) and
\beta=1-\alpha
in D/A or a rotated basis.

III.1
Absorption (
T
) from WPI

Pooling both ports gives two outcomes
\{H,V\}
with probabilities
p_{H}=T/(T+1)
,
p_{V}=1/(T+1)
. With
S_{T}=\alpha N
WPI-tagged coincidences,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\!\left[\widehat{T}\right]

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{T(1+T)^{2}}{S_{T}},\qquad\mathrm{SNR}_{T}\simeq\frac{\sqrt{S_{T}\,T}}{1+T}.

(17)

This saturates the CRB
Helstrom 1969
for WPI-tagged events.

Phase (
\phi

Using both ports with
S_{\phi}=\beta N
erasure-tagged coincidences, the per-event FI for
\phi

\mathcal{I}_{\phi}^{(D/A)}=\frac{4T\sin^{2}\phi}{(T+1)^{2}-4T\cos^{2}\phi}\!,

(18)

and the CRB is
\mathrm{Var}[\widehat{\phi}]\geq 1/(S_{\phi}\,\mathcal{I}_{\phi}^{(D/A)})
. At
T=1

\mathcal{I}_{\phi}^{(D/A)}=1
for all
\phi

III.3
Phase from the Rotated Basis

For the orthonormal analyzer Eq. (
12
), the two-port per-event FI can be written compactly by defining

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=T\cos^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=T\sin^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\theta,

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{T}\sin\theta\cos\theta\cos\phi,

as

\mathcal{I}_{\phi}^{(\theta)}=\frac{4T\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta\,\sin^{2}\phi}{T+1}\left[\frac{A}{A^{2}-\Delta^{2}}+\frac{B}{B^{2}-\Delta^{2}}\right]\!,

(19)

which reduces at
T=1
to

\mathcal{I}_{\phi}^{(\theta)}\big|_{T=1}=\frac{\sin^{2}(2\theta)\,\sin^{2}\phi}{1-\sin^{2}(2\theta)\,\cos^{2}\phi}\,,

(20)

maximized at
\theta=\pi/4
.

IV
Information-Theoretic Analysis

For small parameter changes
\delta\boldsymbol{\vartheta}
with
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}=(T,\phi)^{\!\top}
, local distinguishability is set by
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\approx\!\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta\boldsymbol{\vartheta}^{\!\top}\mathcal{J}\,\delta\boldsymbol{\vartheta}
, where the total FIM is

\mathcal{J}=S_{T}\,\mathcal{I}^{(\mathrm{WPI})}+S_{\phi}\,\mathcal{I}^{(\mathrm{erase/rot})},

(21)

with
\mathcal{I}^{(\mathrm{WPI})}=\mathrm{diag}\!\big(\tfrac{1}{T(1+T)^{2}},\,0\big)
and
\mathcal{I}^{(\mathrm{erase/rot})}
from Eqs. (
18
) or (
19
).

IV.1
Equivalence to Time-Division

Proposition 1 (FIM equivalence under labeled randomization).
Consider QEI where each coincidence is recorded along with the ancilla basis/outcome, and the random choice over bases
\{\theta_{k}\}
is independent of
(T,\phi)
. Assume equal detection efficiencies across B outcomes and identical object-arm configuration, negligible drift and accidentals (or appropriately corrected). Then the per-coincidence FIM of QEI equals the convex combination of the per-coincidence FIMs of time-division experiments that run each basis
\theta_{k}
for fraction
w_{k}
:

\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{QEI}}=\sum_{k}w_{k}\,\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k}).

The observed outcomes include the label
(\theta_{k},\text{outcome})
, so the log-likelihood decomposes as
\sum_{i}\log p(y_{i}|\theta_{k_{i}};\boldsymbol{\vartheta})
. Taking expectations under the product measure with fixed
\{w_{k}\}
gives
\mathbb{E}[-\partial_{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}^{2}\log\mathcal{L}]=\sum_{k}w_{k}\,\mathbb{E}[-\partial_{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}^{2}\log p(\cdot|\theta_{k})]
, yielding the stated equality.

IV.2
Operational Advantage

By operational, we mean advantages in experimental workflow and implementation, rather than enhancements to per-photon information: (i) single-run acquisition of both modalities without touching the object arm; (ii) perfect co-registration since both reconstructions derive from the same time-tagged acquisition stream, partitioned into basis-labeled subsets; (iii) remote/delayed basis choice (and continuous tuning) via the ancilla measurement, reducing drift/alignment risk relative to time-division.

V
Discussion and Conclusion

QEI is useful in two ways: it helps make the physics intuitive, and it gives a workable imaging protocol. Conceptually, the orthonormal analyzer sweep [Eqs. (
12
)–(
16
)] turns the abstract complementarity trade-off into a pixel-resolved morphing of images while respecting completeness/no-signaling [Eq. (
15
)]. Technically, the explicit estimators and FI/CRBs quantify performance and clarify that QEI’s advantages are operational: one run, co-registration, and a remote, even delayed, modality choice.

We presented a theory of QEI with: (i) an orthonormal intermediate analyzer enabling a continuous trade-off, (ii) closed-form dual-modality estimators and visibilities [Eqs. (
8
), (
11
), (
16
)], (iii) singles/no-signaling checks, and (iv) FI/CRBs [Eqs. (
18
)–(
20
)] and a precise statement of information-theoretic equivalence to time-division. These results sharpen QEI as both a clean visualization of complementarity and a convenient imaging protocol.
