Activation of conventional and plasmacytoid dendritic cells and enhancement of anti-viral immune responses by Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus OLL1073R-1 and Streptococcus thermophilus OLS3059

Hiroki Kono, Toshihiro Sashihara

ABSTRACT
Dendritic cells are classified into conventional dendritic cells (cDCs) and plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs), both of which play critical roles in anti-viral immune responses. Previous studies have reported that dairy consumption of yogurt fermented with Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus OLL1073R-1 and Streptococcus thermophilus OLS3059 had the effect of inhibiting viral infections. Recent studies have shown that the activation of both cDCs and pDCs and the decrease of respiratory symptoms were induced by the consumption of this yogurt. However, the components responsible for these effects and the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Since several reports have shown that cDCs and pDCs were activated by lactic acid bacteria, we hypothesized L. bulgaricus OLL1073R-1 and S. thermophilus OLS3059 induced anti-viral immune responses by activating cDCs and pDCs. Here we demonstrated that both L. bulgaricus OLL1073R-1 and S. thermophilus OLS3059 activate cDCs and pDCs in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells. We also showed that both cDCs and pDCs were necessary for type I interferon production, whereas cDCs, but not pDCs, were essential for enhancing effector memory CD4(+) T cell responses to viral antigens when stimulated by L. bulgaricus OLL1073R-1. These findings indicate that anti-viral immune responses induced by consumption of this yogurt are mediated by both L. bulgaricus OLL1073R-1 and S. thermophilus OLS3059 through activation of cDCs and pDCs.

Introduction Dendritic cells (DCs) are the central regulators of the immune system, bridging innate and adaptive immunity by recognizing pathogens through various receptors and inducing antigen-specific responses and immune memory [ 1 ]. Human DCs differentiate from CD34(+) hematopoietic progenitor cells into two major subsets: conventional dendritic cells (cDCs) and plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs), which migrate into blood and peripheral tissues [ 2 , 3 ]. cDCs are predominant DC subset and are responsible for activating T cells by antigen presentation or cytokine production [ 3 , 4 ]. In contrast, pDCs are characterized by producing large amounts of type I interferons (IFNs) in response to pathogens [ 3 , 5 ]. DCs have been reported to play an important role in the control of infectious diseases as well as in the pathogenesis of various immune diseases including autoimmune diseases, allergy, cancer [ 6 , 7 ]. cDCs recognize viruses, bacteria and fungus through a variety of pattern recognition receptors such as Toll-like receptors (TLRs) [ 8 ]. pDCs highly express TLR7 and TLR9 which are known as intracellular receptors and recognize viral single-stranded RNA and CpG rich DNA [ 8 ]. Several reports have shown that experimental depletion of cDCs leads to increase pathogen burden and reduce survival rate during infection of viruses or bacteria [ 9 – 12 ]. Similarly, depletion of pDCs has been shown to impair antigen-specific antibody production during viral infections [ 10 ]. In humans, both cDCs and pDCs have been also reported to be related to induction of immune responses and clearance of pathogens during infections [ 13 , 14 ]. Collectively, these findings suggest that the activation of cDCs and pDCs plays a critical role in suppressing infection of various viruses or bacteria. Several reports have shown that the consumption of probiotic drink containing lactic acid bacteria decrease the frequency of catching respiratory infectious diseases [ 15 – 17 ]. Our studies have shown that immunomodulatory effects such as activation of natural killer cells and improvement of respiratory symptoms were induced by the consumption of the yogurt fermented with Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus OLL1073R-1 (hereafter referred as OLL1073R-1) and Streptococcus thermophilus OLS3059 (hereafter referred as OLS3059) [ 18 – 22 ]. We have also reported by using influenza infection mouse model that intake of this yogurt reduced viral load and improved survival rate [ 23 ]. We have shown in human study that influenza-reactive salivary immunoglobulin A (IgA) and serum IgG levels after influenza vaccination were increased by the consumption of this yogurt [ 24 ]. These findings collectively indicate that suppression of viral infection is mediated by the consumption of yogurt fermented with OLL1073R-1 and OLS3059. It has been reported that cDCs and pDCs are activated by several kinds of lactic acid bacteria in vitro experiments [ 25 – 28 ]. Our recent human studies have shown that both activation of cDCs and pDCs and improvement of respiratory symptoms are induced by daily consumption of the yogurt [ 20 , 21 ]. Based on these observations, we hypothesized that OLL1073R-1 and OLS3059 contained in the yogurt activated cDCs and pDCs and thereby enhanced anti-viral immune responses. In the present study, we used human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) to clarify whether cDCs and pDCs were activated by OLL1073R-1 and OLS3059 and determine how such activation contributes to anti-viral immune responses. Through this approach, we aimed to understand whether and how lactic acid bacteria induce anti-viral immune responses by acting on cDCs and pDCs. RNA-seq analysis reveals immune pathways enhanced by OLL1073R-1 in cDCs and pDCs It remained unclear whether activation of cDCs and pDCs by OLL1073R-1 contributes to anti-viral immune responses. We used RNA samples of cDCs and pDCs stimulated by OLL1073R-1 for 6 h, as gene expression changes need to be captured at an earlier time point than phenotypic outcomes. Pathways associated with CD40 signaling were ranked higher in both cDCs and pDCs, which indicated that activation of cDCs and pDCs were also confirmed by RNA-seq data. In addition, IL-1 and non-apoptotic CD95 signaling pathways, which have been reported to enhance CD40 expression, were also highly ranked [ 33 , 34 ]. Toll-like receptor (TLR) signaling pathways and downstream NF-κB signaling pathways were enriched in both DC subsets, suggesting that recognition of OLL1073R-1 by TLRs might be related to the activation of cDCs and pDCs. Some pathways were characteristically enhanced either in cDCs or pDCs. Pathways related to CD4(+) T cell activation and IL-12 production were selectively enhanced in cDCs ( Fig 3C ). Since it is widely known that IL-12 contributes to activation of CD4(+) T cells [ 35 ], it is suggested that cDCs activated by OLL1073R-1 promote CD4(+) T cell responses through IL-12 production. In contrast, pathways related to IFN-α/β signaling were selectively enhanced in pDCs ( Fig 3D ). In the context of viral infection, type Ι IFNs are critical for anti-viral responses at early phase of infection, whereas CD4(+) T cells contribute to antigen specific responses and immunological memory at later phase of infection [ 36 ]. On the other hand, isolated DC cultures do not fully reflect interaction between immune cells such as cooperative production of type Ι IFNs by cDCs and pDCs upon stimulation with lactic acid bacteria, which has been previously reported [ 37 ]. Therefore, we examined how both DC subsets contributed to anti-viral responses on stimulation with OLL1073R-1 in PBMC. To clarify the coordinate effects of cDCs and pDCs on the type I IFN response, PBMCs, cDCs-depleted PBMCs and pDCs-depleted PBMCs were stimulated with OLL1073R-1 for 24 h, and type Ι IFN production was assessed. IFN-α2a and IFN-β levels in supernatants were significantly increased in intact PBMCs but not in cDCs-depleted PBMCs and pDCs-depleted PBMCs by OLL1073R-1 stimulation ( Fig 4B , C ). These findings indicated that both cDCs and pDCs are necessary for type Ι IFN production in PBMCs, despite pDCs being the producer of type Ι IFNs in isolated DC culture. We further investigated the role of these cells in anti-viral responses of CD4(+) T cells upon stimulation of OLL1073R-1. We focused on CD4(+) T EM which was reported to activate during viral infections such as influenza and play a role in suppressing infections [ 38 ]. PBMCs, cDCs-depleted PBMCs and pDCs-depleted PBMCs were stimulated with OLL1073R-1 in the presence of influenza antigen. CD4(+) T EM in PBMCs were analyzed by flow cytometry using gating strategies shown in S5 Fig . The ratio of IFN-γ expressing cells in CD4(+) T EM was significantly increased in PBMCs and pDCs-depleted PBMCs but not in cDCs-depleted PBMCs following OLL1073R-1 stimulation ( Fig 4D ). Consistently, IL-12 levels in supernatants were also significantly elevated in PBMCs and pDCs-depleted PBMCs but not in cDCs-depleted PBMCs ( Fig 4E ). These results indicate that cDCs, but not pDCs, are essential for enhancement of anti-viral CD4(+) T cell responses induced by OLL1073R-1 through IL-12 production. Discussion In the present study, we demonstrated that OLL1073R-1 and OLS3059 activated both cDCs and pDCs in PBMCs. Using isolated DC population, we also showed OLL1073R-1 directly act on and activate cDCs and pDCs. In addition, the experiments using cDCs or pDCs-depleted PBMCs cleared that both cDCs and pDCs were necessary for type Ι IFN production, whereas cDCs, but not pDCs, were essential for the enhancement of anti-viral responses of CD4(+) T cells with the stimulation of OLL1073R-1. These findings indicated by which mechanism lactic acid bacteria enhance anti-viral immunity through activating two different DC subsets. Our previous report has shown that the ratio of CD86 expressing cDCs and pDCs in PBMCs was increased by the consumption of yogurt fermented with OLL1073R-1 and OLS3059 [ 20 ]. Another human study has demonstrated that intake of this yogurt increased the ratio of CD86 expressing cDCs and the change rate of CD40 expressing pDCs when stimulated by viral antigen [ 21 ]. Since our results showed the ability of OLL1073R-1 and OLS3059 to increase the ratio of CD86 and CD40 expressing cDCs and pDCs, it is suggested that both strains contained in this yogurt activate two DC subsets. In addition, both human studies have shown that DC activation was associated with the reduction of respiratory symptoms. In this study, we demonstrated the enhancement of anti-viral immune responses with the stimulation of OLL1073R-1 through activating cDCs and pDCs, which might lead to improvement of these symptoms. Type Ι IFNs are critical mediators of anti-viral innate immune response, particularly during viral infection at early phase [ 36 , 40 ]. pDCs are recognized as primary sources of type Ι IFNs and are characterized by producing large amounts of type Ι IFNs [ 40 ]. In the present study, isolated pDCs, but not cDCs, produced type Ι IFNs following direct stimulation with OLL1073R-1. However, depletion experiments revealed that removal of either cDCs or pDCs from PBMC cultures impaired type I IFN production in response to OLL1073R-1. One of the reasons for this apparent contradiction is that intercellular communication between DC subsets is critical for production of type Ι IFNs in a multicellular immune environment. Previous study has shown that type Ι IFN production of pDCs stimulated with L. lactis strain was elevated when co-cultured with cDCs [ 37 ]. Another study has shown that L. paragasseri strain activated both cDCs and pDCs and induced type Ι IFN production in PBMCs [ 41 ]. Since type Ι IFNs have their character of positive feedback [ 42 ], it might be possible that type Ι IFNs produced by pDCs stimulated with lactic acid bacteria makes cDCs produce type Ι IFNs in turn. In the current study, the number of pDCs in PBMCs was much smaller than the number of isolated pDCs we used because the ratio of pDCs in PBMCs was less than 1%. Therefore, it is suggested that type Ι IFN production was not increased by individual activity of small number of pDCs but by cooperative interaction between cDCs and pDCs in PBMCs. Our results indicate that both cDCs and pDCs are required for type Ι IFN production in response to OLL1073R-1 in vivo environment where various immune cells are present. Our study showed that cDCs, but not pDCs, were necessary for enhancement of CD4(+) T EM activation by OLL1073R-1 when stimulated by influenza antigen. This is consistent with previous report which showed cDCs-depletion, but not pDCs-depletion, decreased IFN-γ production and body weight during influenza infection [ 10 ]. We have shown in previous study that the consumption of yogurt fermented with OLL1073R-1 and OLS3059 enhanced immune response to influenza and suppress influenza infection, which is suggested that activation of cDCs might contribute to these effects [ 23 , 24 ]. In contrast to cDCs, pDCs-depletion have been reported to make no significant influence on survival during other viral infections such as local infection of herpes simplex virus (HSV) −2 and lymphocytic choriomeningitis mammarenavirus (LCMV) infection, as well as influenza infection [ 43 , 44 ]. These findings indicate that enhancement of OLL1073R-1-mediated anti-viral immune responses through activating cDCs might be particularly crucial for suppressing some infectious diseases. Previous reports have shown that some lactic acid bacteria activate cDCs and pDCs through several kinds of TLRs [ 25 , 26 , 40 ]. In the present study, mRNA-seq analysis revealed that TLR signaling and downstream NF-κB signaling were enriched in both cDCs and pDCs with the stimulation of OLL1073R-1. Our results further showed that both cDCs and pDCs intake OLL1073R-1; similar uptake was also observed with OLS3059 ( S7 Fig ). These findings suggest that not only TLRs expressing on cell surface, but also endosomal TLRs may be involved in activation of DCs by lactic acid bacteria. Indeed, it has been shown that L. acidophilus and L. rhamnosus strains activate cDCs via TLR2 which expresses on cell surface [ 25 , 26 ]. In the same study, phagocytosis was also required for cDCs activation [ 26 , 45 ]. Another study has shown that L. lactis strain activates pDCs via TLR9 which is an endosomal receptor [ 40 ]. However, in our experiment, there was no significant change in TLR 2, 4, 7 and 9 gene expressions which were included in the TLR signaling pathway identified by mRNA-seq when analyzed by qPCR ( S8 Fig ). The result of mRNA-seq also showed that only downstream genes of TLR signaling pathway upregulated by OLL1073R-1, such as NF-κB, AP-1, IRF7 ( S9 Fig ). Further researches such as detecting TLR gene expressions at earlier time point are required for identifying which kind of TLRs are related to activation of cDCs and pDCs by OLL1073R-1. Our study showed that the ability of OLL1073R-1 to activate cDCs was higher than that of other L. bulgaricus strains, while OLS3059 had similar ability compared to other S. thermophilus strains ( S10 Fig ). The reason why L. bulgaricus strains and S. thermophilus strains have distinct trend for activating cDCs might be that there are differences in cell surface components such as peptidoglycan, lipoteichoic acid and exopolysaccharide (EPS) among lactic acid bacteria [ 46 ]. It has been reported that OLL1073R-1 is a Lactobacillus strain producing large amount of EPS with TLR agonist activity [ 18 ]. These findings suggest that OLL1073R-1 efficiently activate cDCs through enhancing TLR signaling by EPS on the surface of bacteria. In contrast, the ability of OLL1073R-1 and OLS3059 to activate pDCs was each as well as that of other L. bulgaricus and S. thermophilus strains ( S10 Fig ). A previous study reported that a specific Lactococcus strain, but not Lactobacillus strain was taken up by mouse bone marrow-derived pDCs and induced their activation [ 37 ]. In contrast, another study demonstrated that pDCs isolated from human PBMCs were activated by a Lactobacillus strain following bacteria uptake [ 28 ]. Consistent with these findings, our results also indicate that the ability of lactic acid bacteria to activate human pDCs through phagocytosis is not restricted to a single species or strain. The discrepancy among these studies may be attributable to differences in the species origin of the immune cells examined. This study has several limitations. All experiments were conducted in vitro , and thus may not fully reflect the complex immune interactions occurring in vivo . However, our previous studies have shown that cDCs and pDCs were activated by the consumption of yogurt fermented with OLL1073R-1 and OLS3059 [ 20 , 21 ], which suggests that our in vitro results are physiologically possible. It is plausible that OLL1073R-1 and OLS3059, upon reaching the intestinal mucosa, activate resident cDCs and pDCs, thereby enhancing type I IFN production and anti-viral T cell responses in peripheral or lymphoid tissues. Conclusion In conclusion, we demonstrate that L . bulgaricus OLL1073R-1 and S. thermophilus OLS3059 activate cDCs and pDCs. We further showed that L . bulgaricus OLL1073R-1 enhance anti-viral immune responses by activating cDCs and pDCs. Our findings provide insight into the mechanism how probiotic lactic acid bacteria enhance innate and acquired anti-viral immunity through activating DC subsets.
