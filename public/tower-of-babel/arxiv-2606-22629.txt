Counting Triangles of Graphs via Randomized Trace Estimation with Incomplete Matrix-Vector Products
Soumyadip Ghosh, Lior Horesh, Vasileios Kalantzis, Yingdong Lu, Tomasz Nowicki, Shashanka Ubaru

Abstract
Counting triangles in graphs is a fundamental operation in network analysis, underpinning metrics such as clustering coefficients and serving as a signal for community detection, link prediction, and anomaly detection. The standard approach computes the trace of the cube of the adjacency matrix, but explicitly forming 𝐀 3 \mathbf{A}^{3} is infeasible for large graphs. Hutchinson’s randomized trace estimator offers an efficient alternative by approximating Tr ⁡ ( 𝐀 3 ) \operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3}) through stochastic averaging of quadratic forms, requiring only matrix–vector products with 𝐀 \mathbf{A} . However, in distributed and heterogeneous computing environments, observing all entries of these products can be costly due to communication overhead and straggler effects. To address this, we propose a new variant of Hutchinson’s estimator that operates under partial observation constraints, where both the number and identities of observed entries are random. We provide theoretical guarantees on unbiasedness, variance bounds, and sample complexity, and demonstrate through experiments on synthetic and real-world graphs that our method achieves accurate triangle count estimates while reducing synchronization costs. This work highlights the adaptability of randomized algorithms to modern computational architectures and opens avenues for efficient motif counting in large-scale network analytics.

Introduction

Counting the number of triangles in graphs is an important computational kernel in
network analysis
[
24
]
. Graph triangles represent the smallest nontrivial higher-order motif, and captures notions such as
transitivity

and
triadic closure
that underlie widely used statistics such as clustering coefficients. Moreover, high triangle density often correlates with strong community structure and cohesive subgraph formation, and the triangle count
serves as a signal for link prediction and recommendation in data collections
represented through graphs. Applications of triangle counting include anomaly
and fraud detection, capturing social reinforcement and the formation of trust
networks, identifying protein–protein interaction modules and functional motifs
in metabolic and transcriptional networks, link prediction, recommendation, and

[

Let

\Delta({\cal G})=\frac{1}{6}\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3})

\mathbf{A}

{\cal G}
, forming
and storing the matrix power
\mathbf{A}^{3}
explicitly becomes prohibitively
expensive. In practice, most network analytic applications require only a
crude approximation of
\Delta({\cal G})

In particular, Hutchinson’s randomized trace estimator

\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

More generally, randomized trace estimators

The computational cost of Hutchinson’s trace estimator is dominated by the computational

\mathbf{A}

phenomenon becomes more prevalent in distributed computing environments with non-dedicated resources
where one or more processors can be much slower than its or their peers. Therefore, it is important to
consider and analyze an asynchronous

33

,

2
A new estimator with inexact matrix-vector products

In this section we present Hutchinson’s trace estimator for triangle counting with partial
(asynchronous) matrix-vector products. First, we briefly describe the classical
approach. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and consider
m
independent realizations
\bm{x}_{1},\ldots\bm{x}_{n}
, of the Rademacher vector
where each entry of
\bm{x}_{i}
is
\pm 1
independently with equal probability (Rademacher variables). Then,
Hutchinson’s trace estimator,
K_{n}
, provides an unbiased estimation of
\Delta({\cal G})
,

\displaystyle K_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\bf x}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{3}{\bf x}_{k}.

Let now
T\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
be an integer random variable, and define the random matrix

\mathbf{I}_{T}=\sum_{i\in{\cal T}}{\bf e}_{i}{\bf e}_{i}^{\top},

for some random set
{\cal T}\subseteq[N]
of cardinality
T
, where
{\bf e}_{i}
denotes the
i
-th column of the
N\times N
identity matrix. Throughout the rest of this paper
we will call
\mathbf{I}_{T}
a
row-selection matrix
. Furthermore, we make the following assumption on the random set
{\cal T}
.

Assumption 1
.

For each
i,j\in[N]
, and
i\neq j
,
\mbox{\sf P}[i\in{\cal T}]=\mbox{\sf P}[j\in{\cal T}]
, and it is denoted as
p_{1}
. Furthermore,
\mbox{\sf P}[i,j\in{\cal T}]
is independent of the selection of
i
and
j
, and is denoted as
p_{2}
.

Consider

\displaystyle L_{n}=\frac{N^{3}}{nT^{3}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\bf x}_{k}^{\top}(\mathbf{I}_{T_{k,3}}\mathbf{A})(\mathbf{I}_{T_{k,2}}\mathbf{A})(\mathbf{I}_{T_{k,1}}\mathbf{A}{\bf x}_{k}),

where
T_{k,i},i=1,2,3
are three independent samples of
\mathbf{I}_{T}
.
This formulation enables non-blocking progress across processing units, allowing late or straggling updates to contribute stochastically without enforcing global synchronization.
When coupled with randomized estimators such as the Hutchinson trace estimator, asynchronous evaluation of
{\bf x}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{3}{\bf x}
yields unbiased estimates of
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3})
with controllable variance while significantly improving hardware utilization in bandwidth- and latency-limited environments.
The resulting algorithmic framework integrates naturally with modern distributed linear algebra systems and serves as a scalable primitive for higher-order graph statistics, spectral moment estimation, and triangle counting in massive networks.

2.1
Unbiasedness of the Estimation

The independence between the Rademacher vector and the row-selection matrices
I_{T_{i}},i=1,2,3
and among the row-selection matrices themselves leads to the easy conclusion that the estimator
L_{n}
is unbiased.

Lemma 1
.

Under Assumption
1
,
L_{n}
is an unbiased estimator, i.e.
{\bf\sf E}[L_{n}]=\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3})
.

Let
{\bf\sf E}_{T}
denote the expectation operator taken with respect to the row selection matrices, then

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[L_{n}]=

\displaystyle\frac{N^{3}}{nT^{3}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\bf\sf E}[{\bf x}^{\top}(\mathbf{I}_{T_{k,3}}\mathbf{A})(\mathbf{I}_{T_{k,2}}\mathbf{A})(\mathbf{I}_{T_{k,1}}\mathbf{A}{\bf x})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N^{3}}{nT^{3}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\bf\sf E}[{\bf x}^{\top}{\bf\sf E}_{T}[(\mathbf{I}_{T_{k,3}}\mathbf{A})(\mathbf{I}_{T_{k,2}}\mathbf{A})(\mathbf{I}_{T_{k,1}}\mathbf{A})]{\bf x})]

\displaystyle={\bf\sf E}[{\bf x}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{3}{\bf x}]=\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3}).

Thus confirms the desired unbiasedness.
∎

3
Analysis of the algorithm

In this section, we will provide probabilistic analysis on the estimator
L_{n}
, thus quantify the effectiveness of the Monte Carlo
algorithm.

3.1
Technical Lemmata of Randomized Linear Algebra

First, we collect some technical lemmas that will be utilized heavily in
the subsequent analysis.

Lemma 2
.

For any
N\times N
matrix
\mathbf{Z}
and
N
-dimensional Rademacher
vector
{\bf x}
, we have

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[({\bf x}{\bf x}^{\top})\mathbf{Z}({\bf x}{\bf x}^{\top})]=\mathbf{Z}+\mathbf{Z}^{\top}+\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{Z})\mathbf{I}-2\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{Z}).

(1)

with
\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{Z})
denotes the diagonal matrix whose
(i,i)
-th entry is
Z_{ii}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,N
.

For any
i,j=1,\ldots,N
, we have, for
\mathbf{Z}=(Z_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{N}
,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[({\bf x}{\bf x}^{\top})\mathbf{Z}({\bf x}{\bf x}^{\top})]_{ij}={\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}[{\bf x}]_{i}[{\bf x}]_{k}Z_{k\ell}[{\bf x}]_{\ell}[{\bf x}]_{j}\right].

Therefore, in the diagonal case, that is
i=j
, we have,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[({\bf x}{\bf x}^{\top})\mathbf{Z}({\bf x}{\bf x}^{\top})]_{ii}={\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{k,\ell}[{\bf x}]_{i}[{\bf x}]_{k}Z_{k\ell}[{\bf x}]_{\ell}[{\bf x}]_{i}\right]={\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{k,\ell}[{\bf x}]_{k}Z_{k\ell}[{\bf x}]_{\ell}\right]=\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{Z}).

The second equality is due to the fact that
[{\bf x}]_{i}^{2}=1
always holds.
In the case of
i\neq j
,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[({\bf x}{\bf x}^{\top})\mathbf{Z}({\bf x}{\bf x}^{\top})]_{ij}=

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{k,\ell}[{\bf x}]_{i}[{\bf x}]_{k}Z_{k\ell}[{\bf x}]_{\ell}[{\bf x}]_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[[{\bf x}]_{i}^{2}Z_{ij}[{\bf x}]_{j}^{2}+[{\bf x}]_{i}[{\bf x}]_{j}Z_{ji}[{\bf x}]_{i}[{\bf x}]_{j}]=Z_{ij}+Z_{ji}.

It is easy to check that the above two cases match the right hand side of (
1
).
∎

with
\mathbf{\Lambda}

see, e.g.

Lemma 3
.

Under assumption
1
, for any
N\times N
matrix
\mathbf{Z}
, we have,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{I}_{T}]=

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}p_{2}+\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{Z})(p_{1}-p_{2}).

The lemma follows from the following direct calculation,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{I}_{T}]=

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{i\in{\cal T}}\sum_{j\in{\cal T}}{\bf e}_{i}{\bf e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}{\bf e}_{j}{\bf e}_{j}^{\top}\right]={\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{i\in{\cal T}}\sum_{j\in{\cal T}}Z_{ij}{\bf e}_{i}{\bf e}_{j}^{\top}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}p_{2}+\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{Z})(p_{1}-p_{2}).

∎

Lemma 4
.

Under Assumption
1
, for any
N\times N
matrices
\mathbf{U}
,
\mathbf{V}
and
\mathbf{W}
, we have

{\bf\sf E}[\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{U}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{W})]_{ij}=p_{2}U_{ij}(\mathbf{VW})_{jj}-(p_{2}-p_{1})U_{ij}V_{ji}W_{ij},

or, in matrix form,

{\bf\sf E}[\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{U}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{W})]=p_{2}\mathbf{U}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{VW})+(p_{1}-p_{2})\mathbf{U}\odot\mathbf{V}^{\top}\odot\mathbf{W},

where
\odot
denotes the Hadamard (element-wise) product.

To compute
{\bf\sf E}[\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{U}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{W})]
, for
i,j=1,2,\ldots,N
, we have,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{U}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{W})]_{ij}={\bf\sf E}[(\mathbf{I}_{T})_{ii}U_{ij}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{V})_{jj}]={\bf\sf E}[(\mathbf{I}_{T})_{ii}U_{ij}(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{W})_{jj}].

Expand the last term, we can further write,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{k}(\mathbf{I}_{T})_{ii}U_{ij}V_{jk}(\mathbf{I}_{T})_{kk}W_{kj}\right]=

\displaystyle p_{2}\sum_{k}U_{ij}V_{jk}W_{kj}-(p_{2}-p_{1})U_{ij}V_{ji}W_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}U_{ij}(\mathbf{UV})_{jj}-(p_{2}-p_{1})U_{ij}V_{ji}W_{ij}.

∎

3.2
Second Moment Calculation

3.2.1
A general Lemma

Lemma 5
.

Under assumption
1
, for any
N\times N
matrix
\mathbf{B}
, we have,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}({\bf x}^{\top}\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB}{\bf x}{\bf x}^{\top}\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB}{\bf x})]=

\displaystyle p_{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{ABAB})+p_{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{AB})^{2}+p_{1}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{ABB}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top})

\displaystyle-2p_{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{AB})\mathbf{AB}).

(2)

First, write,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}({\bf x}^{\top}\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB}{\bf x}{\bf x}^{\top}\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB}{\bf x})]={\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB}{\bf x}{\bf x}^{\top}\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB}{\bf x}{\bf x}^{\top})]

By Lemma
2
, we can take the expectation with respect to the Rademacher vector
{\bf x}
, and obtain the following decomposition

\displaystyle\underbrace{{\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB}\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB})]}_{I}+\underbrace{{\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{ABB}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{I}_{T})]}_{II}+\underbrace{{\bf\sf E}^{2}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB})]}_{III}-2\underbrace{{\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB}))]}_{IV}.

Now, let us deal with each term separately. For the first term, we can write

\displaystyle I=\mbox{\sf Tr}({\bf\sf E}[\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB}\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB}])=p_{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{ABAB})+(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{AB})\mathbf{AB}).

Similarly, for the second term we have

\displaystyle II=

\displaystyle\mbox{\sf Tr}({\bf\sf E}[\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{ABB}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{I}_{T}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{AB}\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top})+(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{AB}\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{AB}\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top})+(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{AB}\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{1}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{AB}\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top}).

Finally, the third and fourth terms can be expanded as

\displaystyle III=

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}^{2}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}[I_{T}]_{ii}A_{ik}B_{ki}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}[I_{T}]_{jj}A_{j\ell}B_{\ell j}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}A_{ik}B_{ki}A_{j\ell}B_{\ell j}\right]+(p_{1}-p_{2})\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}A_{ik}B_{ki}A_{i\ell}B_{\ell i}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}[\mathbf{AB}]_{ii}[\mathbf{AB}]_{jj}\right]+(p_{1}-p_{2})\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}[\mathbf{AB}]_{ii}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{AB})^{2}+(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{AB})\mathbf{AB}),

and

\displaystyle IV=

\displaystyle\mbox{\sf Tr}({\bf\sf E}[\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{I}_{T}\mathbf{AB})])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{AB})\mathbf{AB})+(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{AB}\odot\mathbf{AB})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{1}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{AB})\mathbf{AB}),

respectively. Combining all of the above terms together, results to (
2
).
∎

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle p_{2}^{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{6})+p_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{3}))+p_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{3}))

\displaystyle+(p_{1}-p_{2})^{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2}\odot\mathbf{A})\mathbf{A})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}^{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{6})+2p_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{3}))+(p_{1}-p_{2})^{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}((\mathbf{A\odot A}^{2})^{2}),

where (1) is the result of applying Lemma
3
and (2) is the result of applying Lemma
3
to the first term and Lemma
4
to the second term. To see that last equality, notice that since
A
is symmetric,

\displaystyle\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2}\odot\mathbf{A})\mathbf{A})=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}A_{ij}[\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2}\odot\mathbf{A}]_{jk}A_{ki}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k}[\mathbf{A}^{2}]_{jk}[\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2}\odot\mathbf{A}]_{jk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k}[\mathbf{A\odot A}^{2}]_{jk}[\mathbf{A\odot A}^{2}]_{kj}.

The second term:

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}^{2}(\mathbf{AB})]={\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{AI}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{AI}_{T_{3}}\mathbf{A})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{AI}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{AI}_{T_{3}}\mathbf{A})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{i,j,k}A_{ij}[I_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[I_{T_{3}}]_{kk}A_{ki}\sum_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}A_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}[I_{T_{2}}]_{j^{\prime}j^{\prime}}A_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}[I_{T_{3}}]_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime}}A_{k^{\prime}i^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}^{2}\sum_{i,j,k}A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}\sum_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}A_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}A_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}A_{k^{\prime}i^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+p_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})\sum_{i,j,k}A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}\sum_{i^{\prime},k^{\prime}}A_{i^{\prime}j}A_{jk^{\prime}}A_{k^{\prime}i^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+p_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})\sum_{i,j,k}A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}\sum_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}A_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}A_{j^{\prime}k}A_{ki^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+(p_{1}-p_{2})^{2}\sum_{i,j,k}A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}\sum_{i^{\prime}}A_{i^{\prime}j}A_{jk}A_{ki^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}^{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}^{2}(\mathbf{A}^{3})+2p_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{3}))+(p_{1}-p_{2})^{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}((\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2})^{2}).

To see why the last equation holds, notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,i^{\prime},k^{\prime}}A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}A_{i^{\prime}j}A_{jk^{\prime}}A_{k^{\prime}i^{\prime}}=\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime}}A_{ij}[A^{2}]_{ji}A_{i^{\prime}j}[A^{2}]_{ji^{\prime}}=\sum_{j}[A^{3}]_{jj}[A^{3}]_{jj},

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,i^{\prime}}A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}A_{i^{\prime}j}A_{jk}A_{ki^{\prime}}=\sum_{j,k}[A^{2}]_{jk}A_{jk}[A^{2}]_{jk}A_{jk}=\sum_{j,k}(\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2})_{jk}(\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2})_{kj}.

The third term:

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{AB}\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}I_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A}{\bf\sf E}_{3}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}\mathbf{A}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}]\mathbf{A}\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A}])

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle p_{2}{\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A}^{4}\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A}])+(p_{1}-p_{2}){\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{2})\mathbf{A}\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A}])

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle p^{2}_{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{6})+p_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{4})\mathbf{A}^{2})+p_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{2})\mathbf{A}^{4}])

\displaystyle+(p_{1}-p_{2})^{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{4}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{2}_{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{6})+(p^{2}_{1}-p^{2}_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{4})\mathbf{A}^{2}),

where, once again, (1) and (2) result by applying Lemma
3
.

The fourth term:

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{AB})\mathbf{AB})]={\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{AI}_{T_{2}}A{\bf\sf E}_{3}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}A\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{AI}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{AI}_{T_{3}}\mathbf{A})])]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}{\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{AI}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A}^{2}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{AI}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A}^{2}))]+(p_{1}-p_{2}){\bf\sf E}[\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{AI}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{A}\odot(\mathbf{AI}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A})^{\top}\odot\mathbf{A})])]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}^{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{3}))+p_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{A}^{2}\odot\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2}))

\displaystyle+(p_{1}-p_{2}){\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{i,j,k}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{A}\odot(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A})^{\top}\odot\mathbf{A}]_{ki}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}^{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{3}))+2p_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}((\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2})^{2})+(p_{1}-p_{2})^{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}((\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A})^{3}).

To see the above, notice that

\displaystyle\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{A}^{2}\odot\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}[\mathbf{A}^{2}\odot\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2}]_{ji}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}[\mathbf{A}^{2}]_{ij}A_{ij}[\mathbf{A}^{2}]_{ii}

\displaystyle=\mbox{\sf Tr}((\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2})^{2}),

while, at the same time,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{i,j,k}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{A}\odot(\mathbf{AI}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A})^{\top}\odot\mathbf{A}]_{ki}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{i,j,k}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}A_{ki}[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}\mathbf{A}]_{ik}A_{ki}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{i,j,k,\ell}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}A_{ki}A_{i\ell}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{\ell\ell}A_{\ell k}A_{ki}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}\sum_{i,j,k,\ell}A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}A_{i\ell}A_{\ell k}A_{ki}+(p_{1}-p_{2})\sum_{i,j,k}A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}\sum_{i,k}[\mathbf{A}^{2}]_{ik}A_{ki}[\mathbf{A}^{2}]_{ik}A_{ki}+(p_{1}-p_{2})\sum_{i,j,k}A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}((\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2})^{2})+(p_{1}-p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}((\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A})^{3}).

Put these four terms together, we obtain the following expression for the second moment,

Proposition 1
.

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[L_{n}^{2}]=

\displaystyle(p_{1}+p_{2})p_{2}^{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{6})+p_{1}(p_{1}^{2}-p_{2}^{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{2}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{4}))

\displaystyle+[4p_{2}^{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})-2p_{2}^{3}]\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3}\mbox{\sf Diag}(\mathbf{A}^{3}))

\displaystyle+p^{3}_{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}^{2}(\mathbf{A}^{3})+2p_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})(p_{1}-3p_{2})\mbox{\sf Tr}((\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2})^{2})

\displaystyle-2p_{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})^{2}\mbox{\sf Tr}((\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A})^{3}).

(4)

Remark 2
.

Recall that
p_{2}
is roughly
p_{1}^{2}
, so the dominant term in (
4
) is the second one, which is of the order of
p_{1}^{3}
. For the variance calculation, the square of the average needs to be deducted, but that terms will be of

order, not making much difference.

3.3
Sample Complexity

Next, we develop sample complexity results similar to that in
[
18
]
with the techniques on concentration inequalities developed in
[
43
]
.

Proposition 2
.

Under the Assumption
1
, there exists a
n_{0}
, such that when
n\geq n_{0}
, the asynchronous estimator
L_{n}
is an
(\epsilon,\delta)
-estimator of
\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3})
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mbox{\sf P}\left[|L_{n}-\mbox{\sf Tr}(\mathbf{A}^{3})|\leq\epsilon\right]\geq 1-\delta,

(5)

with
n_{0}=Cp_{1}^{6}\epsilon^{-2}\log(\delta^{-1})
for a
C>0
.

Lemma 3.2 in
[
43
]
states that whenever a mean-zero sub-Gaussian random variable
X
satisfies

\sup\limits_{p\geq 1}\left(p^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\bf\sf E}|X|^{p})^{1/p}\right)<K
for certain
K>0
, then for
A>0
any
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|a|\leq A
and
\lambda<1/(4eK^{2}A)
, we have

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}(\exp(\lambda aX^{2})\leq 1+\lambda a{\bf\sf E}[X^{2}]+16\lambda^{2}a^{2}K^{4}.

(6)

This lemma allows us to utilize the second moment calculation in the large deviations calculations. More specifically, for any
t>0
, the probability that the estimator will have an error more than
t

\displaystyle\mbox{\sf P}\left[\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}(\mathbf{I}_{T_{1}})_{ii}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}]_{kk}A_{ki}[\mathbf{x}]_{i}^{2}-p_{1}^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N}[\mathbf{A}^{3}]_{ii}\bigg|>t\right],

can be bounded via the Chebyshev’s inequality by

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{1}}]_{ii}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}]_{kk}A_{ki}[\mathbf{x}]_{i}^{2}-\lambda p_{1}^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N}[\mathbf{A}^{3}]_{ii}\right)\right]e^{-\lambda t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{1}}]_{ii}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}]_{kk}A_{ki}[\mathbf{x}]_{i}^{2}\right)\right]\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\exp(\lambda p_{1}^{3}[\mathbf{A}^{3}]_{ii})\right)^{-1}e^{-\lambda t}

for any
\lambda>0
. Thus, we would like to calculate the following moment generating function,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{1}}]_{ii}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}]_{kk}A_{ki}[\mathbf{x}]_{i}^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}_{T}\left[{\bf\sf E}_{x}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{1}}]_{ii}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{A}_{T_{3}}]_{kk}A_{ki}[\mathbf{x}]_{i}^{2}\right)\right]\right]

Denote

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}:=\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{1}}]_{ii}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}]_{kk}A_{ki},

Therefore,
{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i}]=p_{1}^{3}A_{ii}
. Furthermore, when
\lambda<1/(4eN^{3})
, we have,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}_{T}\left[{\bf\sf E}_{x}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Lambda_{i}[\mathbf{x}]_{i}^{2}\right)\right]\right]

\displaystyle={\bf\sf E}_{T}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}_{x}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\Lambda_{i}[\mathbf{x}]_{i}^{2}\right)\right]\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\bf\sf E}_{T}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1+\lambda\Lambda_{i}+16\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}_{i})\right]

where the inequality follows from (
6
) with
K=1
due to the Rademacher assumption. Because the entries of the incidence matrix
\mathbf{A}
are binary, we also
have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}\leq\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{1}}]_{ii}A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}=[\mathbf{I}_{T_{1}}]_{ii}[\mathbf{A}^{3}]_{ii}.

(7)

Hence,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}_{T}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1+\lambda\Lambda_{i}+16\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}_{i}K^{4})\right]=

\displaystyle 1+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i}]+16\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]

\displaystyle+\sum_{k,m}(16)^{m}\lambda^{k+2m}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\Lambda_{i_{k}}\Lambda_{j_{1}}\cdots\Lambda_{j_{m}}].

For each term in the last summation, we apply the inequality (
7
), and obtain,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\Lambda_{i_{k}}[\mathbf{\Lambda}^{2}]_{j_{1}}\cdots[\mathbf{\Lambda}^{2}]_{j_{m}}]\leq p_{1}^{k+m}[\mathbf{A}^{3}]_{i_{1},i_{1}}\cdots[\mathbf{A}^{3}]_{i_{k},i_{k}}[\mathbf{A}^{6}]_{j_{1},j_{1}}\cdots[\mathbf{A}^{6}]_{j_{1},j_{1}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{k,m}(16K^{4})^{m}\lambda^{k+2m}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\Lambda_{i_{k}}\Lambda_{j_{1}}\cdots\Lambda_{j_{m}}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1+p_{1}\lambda\mathbf{A}^{3}_{ii}+16p_{1}\lambda^{2}[\mathbf{A}^{6}]_{ii})-\left(1+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i}]\right)=:\Omega(\lambda).

Note that each term in
\Omega(\lambda)
contains
\lambda^{p}
with
p\geq 2
, and there exists an
\Omega^{*}>0
such that
\Omega(\lambda)\leq\lambda^{2}\Omega^{*}
for all
\lambda<1/(4eN^{3})
. This leads to

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}_{T}\left[{\bf\sf E}_{x}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Lambda_{i}x_{i}^{2}\right)\right]\right]\leq

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}_{T}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{N}\{1+\lambda\Lambda_{i}+16\lambda^{2}[(I_{T_{1}})_{ii}[\mathbf{A}^{3}]_{ii}]^{2}\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i}]+16\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]+\Omega(\lambda).

From the basic inequality
1+x\leq e^{x}
, we have,

\displaystyle 1+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i}]+16\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]+\Omega\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left[\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i}]+16\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]+\Omega\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\exp\left(\lambda{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i}]+16\lambda^{2}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]+\frac{\Omega(\lambda)}{N}\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{1}}]_{ii}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{A}_{T_{3}}]_{kk}A_{ki}[\mathbf{x}]_{i}^{2}-\lambda p_{1}^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N}[\mathbf{A}^{3}]_{ii}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\exp\left(\lambda{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i}]+16\lambda^{2}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]+\frac{\Omega}{N}\right)\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\exp(\lambda p_{1}^{3}[\mathbf{A}^{3}]_{ii})\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(16\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]+\Omega(\lambda)\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle\mbox{\sf P}\left[\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{1}}]_{ii}A_{ij}[I_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}]_{kk}A_{ki}[\mathbf{x}]_{i}^{2}-p_{1}^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N}[\mathbf{A}^{3}]_{ii}\bigg|>t\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\lambda t+16\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]+\Omega(\lambda)\right).

\lambda
can then be optimized to get a better bound. But for the ease of exposition, we take
\lambda^{*}=\arg\min_{t}\{-\lambda t+16\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]\lambda^{2}\Omega^{*}\}=\frac{t}{32\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]+2\Omega^{*}}
, and

\displaystyle\mbox{\sf P}\left[\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{1}}]_{ii}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}]_{kk}A_{ki}[\mathbf{x}]_{i}^{2}-p_{1}^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N}[\mathbf{A}^{3}]_{ii}\bigg|>t\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{64\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]+4\Omega^{*}}\right).

(8)

For any
i=1,2,\ldots,N
,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]=

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}=1}^{N}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{1}}]_{ii}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}]_{kk}A_{ki}A_{ij^{\prime}}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{j^{\prime}j^{\prime}}A_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}]_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime}}A_{k^{\prime}i}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{1}{\bf\sf E}\left[\sum_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}=1}^{N}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}]_{kk}A_{ki}A_{ij^{\prime}}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{j^{\prime}j^{\prime}}A_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}})_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime}}A_{k^{\prime}i}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{1}(p_{2}^{2}([\mathbf{A}^{3}]_{ii})2-2p_{1}(p2-p1)[\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A}^{2}]_{ii}+(p_{2}-p_{1})^{2}(\mathbf{A}\odot\mathbf{A})^{3}]_{ii}.

Similar arguments worked for the off diagonal term. More specifically, for the offdiagonal terms, we need to estimate,

\displaystyle{\bf\sf E}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}(I_{T_{1}})_{ii}A_{ij}(I_{T_{2}})_{jj}A_{jk}(I_{T_{3}})_{kk}A_{k\ell}[\mathbf{x}]_{i}[\mathbf{x}]_{\ell}\right)\right]

Apply the same argument, we have,

\displaystyle\mbox{\sf P}\left[\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{1}}]_{ii}A_{ij}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{2}}]_{jj}A_{jk}[\mathbf{I}_{T_{3}}]_{kk}A_{k\ell}[\mathbf{x}]_{i}[\mathbf{x}]_{\ell}\bigg|>t\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{64\sum_{i,j=1,i\neq j}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{j}]+4{\tilde{\Omega}}^{*}}\right),

(9)

with

\displaystyle{\tilde{\Omega}}^{*}:=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1+p_{1}\lambda[\mathbf{A}^{3}]_{ij}+16p_{1}\lambda^{2}[\mathbf{A}^{6}]_{ij})-\left(1+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{j}]\right),

and
{\tilde{\Omega}}^{*}
is defined as
\inf\{M:{\tilde{\Omega}}\leq\lambda^{2}M,\lambda<1/(4eN^{3})\}
, which is finite.
Inequalities (
8
) and (
9
) allow us to estimate
L_{n}
. More precisely, given
(\epsilon,\delta)
, following the same arguments in
[
17
]
,

\displaystyle\mbox{\sf P}\left[|L_{n}-\Delta(\mathbf{A})|>\frac{t}{np_{1}^{3}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{64\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]+4\Omega^{*}}\right)

\displaystyle+\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{64\lambda^{2}\sum_{i,j=1,i\neq j}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{j}]+4{\tilde{\Omega}}^{*}}\right).

Therefore, we need to pick
t=p_{1}^{3}\epsilon
, and
n
such that,

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{n^{2}p_{1}^{6}\epsilon^{2}}{64\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda^{2}_{i}]+4\Omega^{*}}\right)\leq\delta/2,

and

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{n^{2}p_{1}^{6}\epsilon^{2}}{64\lambda^{2}\sum_{i,j=1,i\neq j}^{N}{\bf\sf E}[\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{j}]+4{\tilde{\Omega}}^{*}}\right)\leq\delta/2

Thus,
n_{0}=Cp_{1}^{6}\epsilon^{-2}\log(\delta^{-1})
for a fixed
C>0
suffices.
∎

For each sampling fraction

\widehat{\Delta}_{f,n}({\cal G})\,\pm\,1.96\,\dfrac{s_{f}}{\sqrt{n}}.

The resulting interval quantifies the statistical precision of the estimator, i.e.,
narrower intervals indicate that additional sampling yields diminishing returns,
while wider intervals reflect greater variability due to aggressive masking or
insufficient sampling.

Conclusion

In this paper, we considered the problem of estimating the number of triangles in large-scale graphs under constraints that limit full observation of matrix–vector products with the adjacency matrix. By extending Hutchinson’s randomized trace estimator, we introduced a novel variant tailored for scenarios where only partial and randomly selected entries of each product are available. Our theoretical analysis established unbiasedness, second-moment bounds, and sample-complexity guarantees for the proposed estimator, demonstrating its robustness even under asynchronous and incomplete observation regimes. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world graphs confirmed that the method achieves competitive accuracy while significantly reducing communication and synchronization costs in distributed environments. These findings highlight the potential of randomized algorithms to adapt to modern computational architectures, where data movement and heterogeneity increasingly dominate performance considerations.

will focus on several directions. First, we aim to explore adaptive sampling strategies that dynamically adjust the number and location of observed entries based on variance estimates or graph structure, potentially improving efficiency and accuracy. Second, we plan to investigate extensions to streaming and dynamic graphs, where triangle counts must be updated incrementally as the graph evolves. Finally, a promising direction is to combine our partial-observation estimator with Hutch++, leveraging its ability to capture dominant spectral components before applying stochastic estimation to the residual subspace. Such integration could significantly reduce variance and improve convergence, especially for graphs with strong spectral decay.
