Isolated Palmar Hyperpigmentation: Rare Presentation of B12 Deficiency
Authors: Mukti Nath Sankhi, Rajiv Sitaula, Samir Samadarshi, Rohit Vashisht, Santosh Kumar Singh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTVitamin B12 deficiency has a myriad of presentations. Skin hyperpigmentation, especially at the knuckles, is common. However, in rare instances, a patient may present with isolated palmar hyperpigmentation instead of the usual features, such as knuckle hyperpigmentation, smooth tongue, symptoms related to anemia, and neurologic features.

Clinical Question

Should isolated palmar hyperpigmentation as presenting complaint raise suspicion of vitamin B12 deficiency?

Case

A 45‐year‐old male, strict vegetarian, presented with complaints of gradual darkening of both palms over 4 months. There was no history of dyspnea on exertion, easy fatiguability, paresthesia, diarrhea, weight loss or any gastrointestinal symptoms. He denied any history of drug intake or exposure to chemicals or any topical application. On examination, there was a diffuse hyperpigmentation over both palms accentuated on the thenar and hypothenar areas as well as palmar creases (Figure 1). There was no similar hyperpigmentation over knuckles or any other parts of the body. He had pallor; however, other relevant general and systemic examination revealed normal findings. On evaluation he had pancytopenia (Hemoglobin‐ 8.4 g/dL), Total Leucocytes count‐ 3.1 × 109/L, Platelet count: 132 × 109/L, Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV): 100 fl, Serum vitamin B12: 88.95 pg/mL (Reference: 211–946 pg/mL), Serum Folate: 19 ng/mL (Reference: 3–20 ng/mL). He was managed with intramuscular methylcobalamine with resolution of pancytopenia and improvement of hyperpigmentation.

Hyperpigmentation of both palms, with accentuated pigmentation at the thenar eminences as well as palmar creases in patient with vitamin B12 deficiency.

Answer and Discussion

Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is an important water‐soluble vitamin required for DNA synthesis, hematopoiesis and neurologic integrity [1]. It is not synthesized in the body hence needs to be supplemented from outside. Deficiency can arise from various causes like poor dietary intake, malabsorption or autoimmune disease and has myriad of presentation including hematologic, neurologic or mucocutaneous manifestations [2]. Among the cutaneous manifestations, hyperpigmentation is recognized. However, it is infrequently documented and often misinterpreted as a manifestation of endocrine or metabolic disorder. Common sites of hyperpigmentation in B12 deficiency includes the knuckles, flexures, and oral mucosa whereas isolated palmar involvement is rare. Palmar hyperpigmentation may be early or only presentation of vitamin B12 deficiency [3]. Timely recognition of such unusual manifestation is of great significance as early intervention can prevent severe hematological changes and often irreversible neurological damage.

Conclusion

Isolated palmar hyperpigmentation is not a common presenting complaint in cases of vitamin B12 deficiency. Although palmar hyperpigmentation has various other etiologies, in an appropriate clinical background, the physician must suspect B12 deficiency and look for other parameters associated with this disorder. It is helpful for prompt diagnosis and treatment to avoid often irreversible neurological complications. This case also underscores the significance of skin as a window to systemic disease, reminding clinicians that careful observation of cutaneous changes can lead to timely and accurate diagnosis.
