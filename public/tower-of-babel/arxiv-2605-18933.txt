Introduction

1.1
Problem Statement

Recent work on extremely low-bit large language models—notably BitNet b1.58
(
11
)
—demonstrates that pre-norm Transformer architectures
(
19
)
can operate with weights constrained to the ternary set
\{-1,0,+1\}
while retaining most of their linguistic capability. This is remarkable: ternary quantization discards all magnitude information, preserving only the sign pattern and sparsity structure. Why should this suffice?

Empirically, the importance of weight signs over magnitudes has been documented since the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis era.
22

12

15

in LayerNorm-based models (Pythia): angular perturbations dominate language-modeling damage (up to
42.9\times

why
sign carries disproportionate functional information in pre-norm (RMSNorm) architectures has been lacking.

2.2
RMSNorm

RMSNorm
(
21
)
simplifies LayerNorm
(
1
)
by omitting the re-centering operation. For
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}

\operatorname{RMSNorm}(y)=\frac{\sqrt{m}}{\|y\|}\,y=\sqrt{m}\cdot\hat{y}

(1)

This maps all nonzero vectors to the sphere of radius
\sqrt{m}

Architectural role.

In a pre-norm Transformer, the block structure is
x_{l+1}=x_{l}+\text{FFN}(\operatorname{RMSNorm}(x_{l}))

input

\hat{y}=\operatorname{RMSNorm}(W_{2}\,\operatorname{ReLU}(W_{1}\hat{x}))

\hat{x}

\operatorname{RMSNorm}
represents the pre-norm of the
next
layer applied to the current layer’s FFN output. This captures the signal path between consecutive normalizations in a pre-norm architecture.

2.3
Sign Quantization

Given a weight matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, we analyze the row-wise sign quantization:

W_{T}=\operatorname{diag}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\cdot\operatorname{sign}(W),\quad s_{i}=\|w_{i}\|_{1}/n

(2)

where
\operatorname{sign}
is applied entry-wise and
s_{i}
is the mean absolute value of row
i

W_{T}
takes values in
\{-s_{i},+s_{i}\}
—a

(two-level) quantization per row, not a genuine ternary
\{-1,0,+1\}

Relationship to published schemes.

BitNet
(
17
)
uses a
per-tensor
(single scalar) variant:
W_{T}=\beta\cdot\operatorname{sign}(W)

\beta=\frac{1}{mn}\|W\|_{1}
, equivalent to setting
s_{i}=\beta
for all rows. Our per-row formulation (
2
) is a slight generalization; since
s_{i}\to\mathbb{E}[|w_{ij}|]
a.s. for each row as
n\to\infty
(by SLLN), the per-row and per-tensor schemes become asymptotically equivalent under assumption (A1’), and Theorem
4

\cos^{2}\to 2/\pi
holds for both.
1
1

1

The convergence rate may differ: per-row has
\operatorname{Var}(s_{i})=O(1/n)

m

\beta

\operatorname{Var}(\beta)=O(1/(mn))

2.5
Perturbation Types

Definition 1

(Perturbation type)
.

A perturbation
\Delta W
applied to a pre-norm layer is called
sign-flip-type
if it reverses the sign of a subset of entries (
\operatorname{sign}(W^{\prime}_{ij})\neq\operatorname{sign}(W_{ij})
for perturbed entries), and
magnitude-type
(sign-preserving) if
\operatorname{sign}(W+\Delta W)=\operatorname{sign}(W)
entry-wise.

Functional characterization
(consequence of Theorem 3, under the constant-
\delta
model (A4) specifically).
Under assumptions (A1)–(A6), as
n\to\infty
with
p
fixed:

•

magnitude-type (constant-
\delta
):

\mathcal{R}(\Delta y)\xrightarrow{P}2/\pi+O(\sqrt{p})

•

sign-flip-type:

\mathcal{R}(\Delta y)\xrightarrow{P}p-4p^{3/2}/(3\pi)+O(p^{5/2})

The asymptotic separation of these radial fractions (
2/\pi
vs.
O(p)
) is the geometric source of the asymmetry quantified in Theorem 3. For general magnitude-type perturbations (e.g., true ternary quantization error), the radial fraction differs; see Section 8.3.

3
Sign Captures Directional Energy

This section establishes that sign patterns dominate the directional content of weight vectors, while magnitude with random signs carries vanishing directional information.

3.1
The Bussgang Constant

Fact 1

(Sign directional coverage;
3
)
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with i.i.d. symmetric components satisfying
\mathbb{E}[|w_{1}|],\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{2}]<\infty
,
\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{2}]>0
, and
\Pr(w_{j}=0)=0
. Then:

\cos^{2}(w,\operatorname{sign}(w))\xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}\frac{(\mathbb{E}[|w_{1}|])^{2}}{\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{2}]}\quad(n\to\infty)

(3)

For Gaussian components
w_{j}\sim N(0,\sigma^{2})
, the limit is the
Bussgang constant
:

\cos^{2}(w,\operatorname{sign}(w))\xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}\frac{2}{\pi}\approx 0.6366

(4)

Proof.

Write
\cos(w,\operatorname{sign}(w))=w^{\top}\operatorname{sign}(w)/(\|w\|_{2}\cdot\|\operatorname{sign}(w)\|_{2})
. The numerator equals
\|w\|_{1}
and
\|\operatorname{sign}(w)\|_{2}=\sqrt{n}
a.s., so
\cos(w,\operatorname{sign}(w))=\|w\|_{1}/(\sqrt{n}\cdot\|w\|_{2})
. By the SLLN,
\|w\|_{1}/n\to\mathbb{E}[|w_{1}|]
and
\|w\|_{2}^{2}/n\to\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{2}]
a.s. The continuous mapping theorem (applied to
g(a,b)=a/\sqrt{b}
, continuous at
b>0
) yields:

\cos(w,\operatorname{sign}(w))\xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}\frac{\mathbb{E}[|w_{1}|]}{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{2}]}}

Squaring gives the general result. For the Gaussian case,
\mathbb{E}[|w_{1}|]=\sigma\sqrt{2/\pi}
and
\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{2}]=\sigma^{2}
, yielding
\cos^{2}\to 2/\pi
.
∎

Convergence rate.

Under the additional assumption
\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{4}]<\infty
, the Delta method applied to the bivariate CLT for
(\|w\|_{1}/n,\,\|w\|_{2}^{2}/n)
gives
\cos^{2}-2/\pi=O_{P}(1/\sqrt{n})
.

3.2
Magnitude with Random Signs is Uninformative

Proposition 2
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with i.i.d. symmetric components,
0<\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{2}]<\infty
,
\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{8}]<\infty
. Let
s\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
be an independent Rademacher vector. Then:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\cos^{2}(w,\;s\odot|w|)\right]=\frac{\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{4}]}{n\cdot(\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{2}])^{2}}\cdot(1+o(1))\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0

(5)

For Gaussian components:
\mathbb{E}[\cos^{2}]=3/(n+2)
(exact for all
n\geq 1
).

Proof.

Step 1.
Using
w_{j}|w_{j}|=\operatorname{sign}(w_{j})w_{j}^{2}
and
\|s\odot|w|\|=\|w\|
:

\cos(w,s\odot|w|)=\frac{\sum_{j}c_{j}w_{j}^{2}}{\sum_{j}w_{j}^{2}}

where
c_{j}:=s_{j}\cdot\operatorname{sign}(w_{j})
are i.i.d. Rademacher, independent of
(|w_{1}|,\ldots,|w_{n}|)
.

Step 2.
Conditioning on
|w|
and using
\mathbb{E}[c_{j}c_{k}]=\delta_{jk}
:

\mathbb{E}[\cos^{2}\mid|w|]=\frac{\sum_{j}w_{j}^{4}}{(\sum_{j}w_{j}^{2})^{2}}

Step 3.
By SLLN,
\sum_{j}w_{j}^{4}/n\to\mu_{4}
and
\sum_{j}w_{j}^{2}/n\to\mu_{2}
a.s. A good-event/bad-event argument (defining
G_{n}=\{S_{n}\geq n\mu_{2}/2\}
, using uniform integrability on
G_{n}
and Chernoff bound
\Pr(G_{n}^{c})\leq e^{-cn}
on
G_{n}^{c}
) upgrades a.s. convergence to
L^{1}
, giving
\mathbb{E}[\cos^{2}]=\mu_{4}/(n\mu_{2}^{2})\cdot(1+o(1))
. (The uniform integrability step requires
\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{8}]<\infty
; full derivation in Appendix C.)

Step 4 (Gaussian exact).
For
w_{j}\sim N(0,\sigma^{2})
, the normalized squares
(w_{1}^{2},\ldots,w_{n}^{2})/\sum_{k}w_{k}^{2}\sim\operatorname{Dir}(1/2,\ldots,1/2)
by the Gamma-Dirichlet theorem. Using
\mathbb{E}[u_{j}^{2}]=3/(n(n+2))
for
\operatorname{Dir}(1/2,\ldots,1/2)
:
\mathbb{E}[\cos^{2}]=n\cdot 3/(n(n+2))=3/(n+2)
.
∎

3.3
Corollary: Sign is the Dominant Directional Component

Corollary 1

(Sign dominates direction)
.

For i.i.d. symmetric weight vectors
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{8}]<\infty
(inheriting the moment condition from Proposition
2
):

•

The sign pattern
\operatorname{sign}(w)
captures
2/\pi\approx 63.7\%
of directional energy (Fact
1
);

•

Magnitude with random signs provides only
O(1/n)\to 0
directional information (Proposition
2
).

The two are complementary: sign determines the “directional region,” while magnitude provides fine-grained refinement within that region. This decomposition motivates why sign-flip perturbations are far more damaging to network outputs than magnitude perturbations of equal norm.

RMSNorm Geometry

We now analyze how RMSNorm interacts with perturbations. Theorem
1
quantifies scale absorption; Theorem
2

4.1
Per-Row Scaling Bound

Theorem 1

(Per-Row Scaling Bound)
.

Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
,
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
with
d_{i}\geq 0
and
Dy\neq 0
. Define the energy-weighted probability
p_{i}=y_{i}^{2}/\|y\|^{2}
and corresponding mean and variance:

\bar{d}_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}d_{i},\qquad\operatorname{Var}_{p}(d)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}(d_{i}-\bar{d}_{p})^{2}

Then:

\frac{\|\operatorname{RMSNorm}(Dy)-\operatorname{RMSNorm}(y)\|}{\|\operatorname{RMSNorm}(y)\|}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{p}(d)}}{\bar{d}_{p}}=\operatorname{CV}_{p}(d)

(6)

When
D=\alpha I
(uniform scaling),
\operatorname{Var}_{p}(d)=0
and the error vanishes exactly.

Proof.

Step 1.
Set
y^{\prime}=Dy
. The difference vector has components
\Delta_{i}=\operatorname{RMSNorm}(y)_{i}\cdot(d_{i}/r-1)
where
r=\|Dy\|/\|y\|
.

Step 2.
The squared relative error becomes
\|\Delta\|^{2}/\|\operatorname{RMSNorm}(y)\|^{2}=\sum_{i}p_{i}(d_{i}/r-1)^{2}
, which simplifies to
2(1-\bar{d}_{p}/r)
using
r^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{p}[d^{2}]=\bar{d}_{p}^{2}+\operatorname{Var}_{p}(d)
.

Step 3.
Let
\theta:=\angle(\operatorname{RMSNorm}(Dy),\operatorname{RMSNorm}(y))
. The geometric identity
\cos\theta=\bar{d}_{p}/r
gives the exact expression as the spherical chord-length formula
2(1-\cos\theta)
.

Step 4.
Applying
1-1/\sqrt{1+x}\leq x/2
for
x=\operatorname{Var}_{p}(d)/\bar{d}_{p}^{2}\geq 0
:

2\left(1-\frac{\bar{d}_{p}}{r}\right)\leq\frac{\operatorname{Var}_{p}(d)}{\bar{d}_{p}^{2}}=\operatorname{CV}_{p}(d)^{2}

Taking square roots completes the proof.
∎

Tightness.

The bound is extremely tight for small CV: at
\operatorname{CV}=0.1
, the exact value is
0.9926\%
vs. the bound
1.0\%
(slack ratio
1.0075
).

4.2
Fréchet Derivative: The Transverse Projector

Theorem 2

(RMSNorm Transverse Projection)
.

Let
f(y)=\operatorname{RMSNorm}(y)=\sqrt{m}\cdot y/\|y\|
. The Fréchet derivative at
y\neq 0
is:

Df(y)=\frac{\sqrt{m}}{\|y\|}\,P_{y^{\perp}}

(7)

where
P_{y^{\perp}}=I_{m}-\hat{y}\hat{y}^{\top}
is the orthogonal projector onto the subspace perpendicular to
y
.

Proof.

Computing
\partial f_{i}/\partial y_{j}=(\sqrt{m}/\|y\|)(\delta_{ij}-y_{i}y_{j}/\|y\|^{2})
, the Jacobian matrix is
(\sqrt{m}/\|y\|)(I-\hat{y}\hat{y}^{\top})=(\sqrt{m}/\|y\|)P_{y^{\perp}}
. Since
f
is
C^{\infty}
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
, the Jacobian equals the Fréchet derivative.
∎

Corollary 2

(Perturbation Decomposition)
.

For a perturbation
\Delta y
:

f(y+\Delta y)-f(y)=\frac{\sqrt{m}}{\|y\|}\,P_{y^{\perp}}\Delta y+O\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{m}\|\Delta y\|^{2}}{\|y\|^{2}}\right)

(8)

The radial component
(\hat{y}^{\top}\Delta y)\hat{y}
is eliminated at first order; the transverse component
P_{y^{\perp}}\Delta y
passes through completely. This is the mechanism by which RMSNorm acts as a
radial filter
: it absorbs perturbations aligned with the output direction and preserves those orthogonal to it.

Corollary 3

(Second-Order Remainder)
.

For
\|\Delta y\|\leq\eta\|y\|
with
0<\eta<1
:

\|f(y+\Delta y)-f(y)-Df(y)\Delta y\|\leq\frac{3\sqrt{m}}{(1-\eta)^{2}\|y\|^{2}}\|\Delta y\|^{2}

(9)

The relative remainder
\|R_{2}\|/\|f(y)\|\leq 3\eta^{2}/(1-\eta)^{2}
is dimension-independent. For
\eta=0.05
:
\leq 0.8\%
; for
\eta=0.10
:
\leq 3.7\%
.

Proof.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus,
R_{2}=\int_{0}^{1}[Df(y+t\Delta y)-Df(y)]\Delta y\,dt
. Differentiating the Jacobian
Df(y)=(\sqrt{m}/\|y\|)P_{y^{\perp}}
gives the Hessian bilinear form:

D^{2}f(y)[h,h]=-\frac{\sqrt{m}}{\|y\|^{2}}\bigl[2(\hat{y}\cdot h)\,P_{y^{\perp}}h+\|P_{y^{\perp}}h\|^{2}\,\hat{y}\bigr]

The two bracketed terms are orthogonal (
P_{y^{\perp}}h\perp\hat{y}
), so by the triangle inequality
\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}\leq|a|+|b|
:

\|D^{2}f(y)[h,h]\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{m}}{\|y\|^{2}}\bigl(2|\hat{y}\cdot h|\,\|P_{y^{\perp}}h\|+\|P_{y^{\perp}}h\|^{2}\bigr)\leq\frac{3\sqrt{m}\|h\|^{2}}{\|y\|^{2}}

where the last step uses
|\hat{y}\cdot h|\leq\|h\|
and
\|P_{y^{\perp}}h\|\leq\|h\|
. For any
z=y+t\Delta y
on the segment,
\|z\|\geq(1-\eta)\|y\|
, so
\|D^{2}f(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 3\sqrt{m}/((1-\eta)^{2}\|y\|^{2})
. By the mean-value inequality on the derivative:

\|Df(y+t\Delta y)-Df(y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq t\|\Delta y\|\cdot\sup_{z}\|D^{2}f(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\frac{3\sqrt{m}\,t\|\Delta y\|}{(1-\eta)^{2}\|y\|^{2}}

Substituting into the integral representation and bounding
\sup_{t\in[0,1]}t\leq 1
yields the stated bound. (The constant
3
is not tight; using the integral form
\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\,dt=1/2
and the AM-GM refinement
|\hat{y}\cdot h|\,\|P_{y^{\perp}}h\|\leq\|h\|^{2}/2
yields a sharper constant of
1
. We retain
3
for a self-contained single-step argument.)
∎

5
Single-Layer Equivalence and Its Insufficiency

5.1
Energy Equivalence

Proposition 1

(Single-Layer Energy Equivalence)
.

Let
\Delta W^{(1)},\Delta W^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
be given matrices (deterministic, or any fixed realization of random matrices) with
\|\Delta W^{(1)}\|_{F}=\|\Delta W^{(2)}\|_{F}
. For
\hat{x}\sim\operatorname{Unif}(S^{n-1})
:

\mathbb{E}_{\hat{x}}\!\left[\|\Delta W^{(1)}\hat{x}\|^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\hat{x}}\!\left[\|\Delta W^{(2)}\hat{x}\|^{2}\right]=\frac{\|\Delta W^{(1)}\|_{F}^{2}}{n}

(10)

(The expectation is over
\hat{x}
only;
\Delta W^{(k)}
are held fixed.)

Proof.

For any
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
\hat{x}\sim\operatorname{Unif}(S^{n-1})
:
\mathbb{E}[\|M\hat{x}\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\hat{x}^{\top}M^{\top}M\hat{x}]=\operatorname{tr}(M^{\top}M)/n=\|M\|_{F}^{2}/n
, using the spherical identity
\mathbb{E}[\hat{x}\hat{x}^{\top}]=I_{n}/n
. Equal Frobenius norms imply equal output energies.
∎

5.2
Why Single-Layer Cannot Explain the Asymmetry

Despite equal total energy, the two perturbation types already exhibit different
directional structure
relative to the unperturbed output
y=W\hat{x}
:

•

Sign perturbation
:
\mathbb{E}[\Delta y^{\mathrm{sign}}\mid W,\hat{x}]=-2p\cdot y
(conditional mean is pure radial), giving radial fraction
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{sign}}\approx p
.

•

Magnitude perturbation
(additive independent model):
\operatorname{Cov}(\Delta y^{\mathrm{mag}})=(4p/n)\,I_{m}
(isotropic, with variance
\sigma_{\delta}^{2}=\delta^{2}=4p/n
matching the sign-flip Frobenius norm), giving radial fraction
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{mag}}=1/m
.

Under this additive model, a single-layer RMSNorm predicts (as
m\to\infty
, so
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{mag}}=1/m\to 0
):

\frac{\mathbb{E}[\|P_{y^{\perp}}\Delta y^{\mathrm{mag}}\|^{2}]}{\mathbb{E}[\|P_{y^{\perp}}\Delta y^{\mathrm{sign}}\|^{2}]}\to\frac{1}{1-p}>1

(11)

This predicts magnitude perturbations are
more
damaging than sign perturbations after RMSNorm—opposite to experimental observation. The resolution requires: (i) adopting the
sign-preserving
magnitude model (assumption (A4), where
\Delta W^{\mathrm{mag}}=\operatorname{sign}(W)\odot\delta
depends on
W
through
\operatorname{sign}(W)
, creating Bussgang correlation
\rho=\sqrt{2/\pi}
between
\Delta z
and
z
) rather than the additive independent model considered above; and (ii) introducing ReLU to create hidden-space geometric asymmetry. Both ingredients are provided by Theorem 3 (Section 6). The additive independent model of this subsection is solely a motivating counterexample; all subsequent analysis uses the sign-preserving model (A4).

Core Result: The
\pi/(\pi-2)
Asymmetry

6.1
Model Setup

Consider a simplified two-layer pre-norm block:

\hat{x}=x/\|x\|\in S^{n-1},\quad z=W_{1}\hat{x},\quad a=\operatorname{ReLU}(z),\quad y=W_{2}a,\quad\hat{y}=\operatorname{RMSNorm}(y)

(12)

with
W_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
(i.i.d.
N(0,1/n)
),
W_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times m}
(i.i.d.
N(0,1/m)
), independent of each other.

Perturbation models

(matched expected Frobenius norm
\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta W_{1}\|_{F}^{2}]=4pm
; for sign-flips this is an expectation with variance
O(pm)
that concentrates for large
mn

•

Sign-flip
: Each entry of
W_{1}
independently flips sign with probability
p
.

•

Magnitude (sign-preserving)
:
W_{1}^{\prime}=W_{1}+\operatorname{sign}(W_{1})\odot\delta
, with
\delta^{2}=4p/n
.

6.2
Theorem Statement

Theorem 3

(ReLU-Induced Differential Suppression)
.

Under assumptions
(A1)–(A6)
:

(Asymptotic limit)
For each fixed
p\in(0,1/2)
, as
n,m,d_{\mathrm{out}}\to\infty
with
m/n\to\gamma\in(0,\infty)
:

c(p):=\frac{\|P_{y^{\perp}}\Delta y^{\mathrm{sign}}\|^{2}}{\|P_{y^{\perp}}\Delta y^{\mathrm{mag}}\|^{2}}\xrightarrow{P}\frac{1-\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{sign}}(p)}{1-\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{mag}}(p)}\cdot R_{A}(p)

(13)

where
R_{A}(p)=\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta y^{\mathrm{sign}}\|^{2}]/\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta y^{\mathrm{mag}}\|^{2}]
is the pre-RMSNorm energy ratio, and
\mathcal{R}
denotes the radial fraction.

(Small-
p
behavior)
Taking
p\to 0
in the resulting limit:

\boxed{c(p)=\frac{\pi}{\pi-2}+O(\sqrt{p})\quad\text{as }p\to 0,\qquad\frac{\pi}{\pi-2}\approx 2.75}

(14)

The leading constant
\pi/(\pi-2)
is the theorem’s primary rigorous claim. The
O(\sqrt{p})
remainder is bounded but its coefficient is not explicitly determined; a structural decomposition is given in Remark
1
.

Remark 1

(Structural decomposition)
.

The
O(\sqrt{p})
remainder in Theorem
3
admits a
factored decomposition
with partially identified coefficients:

c(p)=\frac{\pi}{\pi-2}\,(1-p)\!\left(1-\frac{4\sqrt{p}}{3\pi}\right)\!\bigl(1+\varepsilon(p)\bigr)

where each factor has a clear provenance:
(1-p)
from the radial fraction ratio (Part B),
(1-4\sqrt{p}/(3\pi))
from the energy ratio
R_{A}(p)
(Part A; the coefficient
4/(3\pi)
is exact from Steps A4–A6, with an
O(\sqrt{p})
multiplicative remainder from Step A8), and
(1+\varepsilon(p))
from Part B’s boundary corrections. Both the factor
(1-4\sqrt{p}/(3\pi))
and
\varepsilon(p)
contribute at
O(\sqrt{p})
, so the factored form is
not
a traditional asymptotic expansion with separated orders; it is a structural decomposition in which the coefficient
4/(3\pi)
is exact while
\varepsilon(p)
’s coefficient remains bounded but undetermined (
|\varepsilon(p)|\leq C\sqrt{p}
for some
C>0
; empirically
C\lesssim 0.2
from Table 6). Note that the factored form retains an explicit
O(p)
term via
(1-p)
that the additive bound
O(\sqrt{p})
does not isolate; the two forms are compatible as upper-bound statements but serve different goals (numerical comparison vs. asymptotic order). This factored form is used for the “theory” column in Tables 2 and 6.

Remark 2

(Limit ordering and finite-
n
interpretation)
.

Theorem
3
involves a
double limit
: first
n\to\infty
(for each fixed
p
), then
p\to 0
for the leading-order expansion. The bivariate Gaussian approximation (Step A3 in Appendix A) has Berry–Esseen error
O(\|\hat{x}\|_{\infty})=O(1/\sqrt{n})
under delocalization. Consequently:

•

Convergence mode
: The left-hand side of (13) is a sample ratio (a single draw of
\hat{x},W_{1},W_{2}
). The proof computes
\mathbb{E}[\|\cdot\|^{2}]/\mathbb{E}[\|\cdot\|^{2}]
(ratio of expectations over
\hat{x}
). Since both
\|\Delta y^{\mathrm{sign}}\|^{2}
and
\|\Delta y^{\mathrm{mag}}\|^{2}
are averages of
d_{\mathrm{out}}
i.i.d. projections (conditioned on
(W_{1},\hat{x})
; the randomness comes from
W_{2}
’s rows), the SLLN gives concentration of each around its conditional mean as
d_{\mathrm{out}}\to\infty
. The denominator concentrates around a positive constant (since
\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta y^{\mathrm{mag}}\|^{2}]>0
), so by Slutsky’s theorem the ratio converges in probability to the ratio of expectations.

•

The primary claim
\lim_{p\to 0}c(p)=\pi/(\pi-2)
is rigorous: it is the
n\to\infty
limit for any fixed small
p
, followed by
p\to 0
in the resulting deterministic function. The leading constant
\pi/(\pi-2)
relies on exact population moment identities (

•

The
\sqrt{p}
correction in the factored expansion (Remark
1
) requires the CLT’s

error to be negligible relative to
\sqrt{p}
, i.e.,
p\gg 1/n
—easily satisfied at all experimental values (

,
n=2048
:
1/n\approx 5\times 10^{-4}
).

•

In experimental tables (fixed

and residual finite-

6.3
Proof Sketch

The proof decomposes into two parts: (A) computing the total energy ratio
R_{A}(p)
before RMSNorm, and (B) computing the radial fractions that determine how much energy survives RMSNorm’s transverse projection.

Part A: Energy ratio before RMSNorm.

The key computation is the expected squared activation change
\mathbb{E}[(\Delta a_{i})^{2}]
for each neuron
i
. For sign-flip perturbations, there are two contributions:

1.

Gate-flip energy

\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}
: When the sign of
z_{i}
changes (neuron switches on/off). Using the bivariate Gaussian CDF with correlation
\rho=1-2p
:

\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}=\frac{8p^{3/2}}{3\pi}+O(p^{5/2})

(15)

2.

Smooth energy

\beta_{\mathrm{smooth}}
: When the neuron remains in the same activation state. Via the Price theorem / ReLU product formula
\mathbb{E}[\max(X,0)\max(X^{\prime},0)]=(2\pi)^{-1}[\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}+\rho(\pi/2+\arcsin\rho)]
:

\beta_{\mathrm{smooth}}=2p-\frac{8p^{3/2}}{\pi}+O(p^{5/2})

(16)

For magnitude perturbations,
\mathbb{E}[(\Delta a_{i}^{\mathrm{mag}})^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{z_{i}>0}(\Delta z_{i})^{2}]=(1/2)\cdot\delta^{2}=2p/n
(half-space symmetry:
\Pr(z_{i}>0)=1/2
for symmetric
z_{i}
;
\delta^{2}=4p/n
by (A4)). This gives:

R_{A}(p)=\left(1-\frac{4\sqrt{p}}{3\pi}\right)\!\bigl(1+O(\sqrt{p})\bigr)

(17)

where the leading factor has an exact coefficient from Steps A4–A6, and the multiplicative remainder
O(\sqrt{p})
arises from boundary gate-flip corrections in Step A7.

Part B: Radial fraction analysis.

Using SLLN concentration and the random projection angle-preservation lemma (
W_{2}
preserves angles as
d_{\mathrm{out}}\to\infty
):

•

Sign perturbation
: The reflection symmetry identity
\mathbb{E}[\Delta a_{i}\cdot a_{i}]=-\mathbb{E}[(\Delta a_{i})^{2}]/2
gives
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{sign}}\to p-4p^{3/2}/(3\pi)+O(p^{5/2})
.

•

Magnitude perturbation
: By Lemma
A.1
(Bussgang-ReLU correlation; Appendix A.2), the Bussgang correlation
\operatorname{Corr}(\Delta z_{i},z_{i})=\sqrt{2/\pi}
propagates through ReLU to give
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{mag}}\to 2/\pi+O(\sqrt{p})
.

Combining via
\|P_{y^{\perp}}\Delta y\|^{2}=(1-\mathcal{R})\|\Delta y\|^{2}
:

c(p)=\frac{\|P_{y^{\perp}}\Delta y^{\mathrm{sign}}\|^{2}}{\|P_{y^{\perp}}\Delta y^{\mathrm{mag}}\|^{2}}=\frac{1-\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{sign}}}{1-\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{mag}}}\cdot\underbrace{\frac{\|\Delta y^{\mathrm{sign}}\|^{2}}{\|\Delta y^{\mathrm{mag}}\|^{2}}}_{R_{A}(p)}\to\frac{1-p}{1-2/\pi}\cdot R_{A}(p)=\frac{\pi(1-p)}{\pi-2}\cdot R_{A}(p).

The complete proof is given in Appendix A.

6.4
Intuition: “Sign Structure + ReLU Create, RMSNorm Exposes”

The factor
\pi/(\pi-2)
arises from two complementary mechanisms:

1.

Sign-preserving structure provides Bussgang alignment; ReLU propagates it
: The correlation

between

and

is a pre-existing property of the weight geometry—it is

1
).

2.

RMSNorm exposes the asymmetry

2

(1-2/\pi)\approx 36.3\%
of magnitude perturbation energy but
(1-p)\approx 99\%
of sign perturbation energy (for small

.

7.1

7.2

The gap from the single-layer baseline
2.75\times
to the larger sign sensitivity observed in real models is not multi-layer compounding but rather
violation of assumption (A2)
—the delocalization condition—by outlier features.
Note
: The single-layer outlier leverage ratio
R(\alpha,n)\approx n\alpha^{2}
is rigorously established (Appendix
E
, Theorem
6
), and experimentally validated: at linear response (
p\leq 0.5\%
; Exp E), count-matched NLL leverage stabilizes at
{\sim}10\times
, matching
n\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{emp}}[\alpha^{2}]\approx 7
–
10
; Exps F–G further confirm the
\alpha^{2}
scaling (Spearman
\rho=0.955
and
0.927
). The column-group energy theory (Appendix
F
) bounds
R_{\mathrm{col}}\leq 19
for
\gamma\leq 0.5
.
Log-loss resolution
: The all-column
\Delta
PPL ratio (
1{,}265\times
median) appeared to exceed this by
{\sim}67\times
, but since
\mathrm{PPL}=\exp(\mathrm{NLL})
is exponentially nonlinear, the correct comparison uses log-loss: the all-column NLL ratio is only
5.0\times
(median, IQR
[4.8,5.4]
), which falls
within

R_{\mathrm{col}}\leq 19
.
Caveat
:
R_{\mathrm{col}}
is a post-ReLU
energy
ratio, while NLL is a downstream loss metric; their proportionality is a heuristic alignment (under small perturbations
\Delta\mathrm{NLL}\approx\frac{1}{2}(\Delta y)^{\top}H\,(\Delta y)
, which reduces to energy scaling when the loss Hessian
H
is approximately isotropic in the perturbation subspace). A rigorous energy-to-NLL mapping is an open problem (O1c). The
251\times
compression from PPL to NLL ratio accounts for the bulk of the apparent gap. Multi-layer propagation (O1b) remains open but is no longer needed to explain the all-column observation.

Important distinction
: Theorem
3
’s
\pi/(\pi-2)
quantifies the ratio of
sign-flip vs. magnitude
perturbation damage (cross-type comparison, equal Frobenius norm). The Exp D measurement in Section 9.5 quantifies the ratio of
outlier-column vs. non-outlier-column
sign-flip damage (within-type comparison, same perturbation type applied to different columns). These are complementary observations: Exp D demonstrates that
absolute
sign sensitivity is dominated by outlier features (explaining why real models show extreme sign sensitivity overall), while Theorem
3
explains the

Mechanism.

Consider a sign-flip restricted to a single column
k
of
W
(i.e.,
\Delta W_{:,k}\neq 0
,
\Delta W_{:,j}=0
for
j\neq k
). Since
\Delta y=\Delta W\hat{x}=\Delta W_{:,k}\hat{x}_{k}
(a scalar times an
m

\|\Delta y\|^{2}=\|\Delta W_{:,k}\|^{2}\cdot\hat{x}_{k}^{2}

For the general case of i.i.d. Bernoulli(
p
) sign-flips across all entries:

\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta y\|^{2}]=4p\sum_{k}\hat{x}_{k}^{2}\|W_{:,k}\|^{2}

revealing that each column
k

\hat{x}_{k}^{2}
. Under delocalization (

), all columns contribute equally. But real transformer hidden states contain
outlier features

(
5
)
: a small fraction of dimensions with
\hat{x}_{k}^{2}=O(1)
rather than
O(1/n)
. Sign-flips on these columns have leverage
R\approx n\alpha^{2}
times that of average columns (Theorem
6
).

Magnitude perturbations on outlier columns are similarly amplified in total energy, but their geometric alignment—which under delocalization is characterized by the Bussgang constant
\sqrt{2/\pi}
(Lemma
A.1
)—is conjectured to channel the amplification predominantly into the radial direction (absorbed by RMSNorm). Since Lemma
A.1
relies on assumption (A2), this radial-channeling argument is heuristic for outlier columns (see Section 10.3, limitation 4). Sign perturbations lack this radial shelter regardless, so outlier features may selectively amplify the sign/magnitude asymmetry beyond
2.75\times
—but this conjecture has not been formally verified (O1 in Section
11.2
).

8
Application: Ternary Quantization Error

8.1
Theorem Statement

Theorem 4

(Ternary Quantization Angular Error)
.

Under assumptions
(A1’)–(A4’)
, as
m,n\to\infty
with
m/n=O(1)
:

\cos^{2}\!\angle(W\hat{x},\;W_{T}\hat{x})\xrightarrow{P}\frac{2}{\pi}

(18)

Equivalently, the angular error satisfies
\sin^{2}\!\angle\to 1-2/\pi\approx 0.363
. After RMSNorm:

\frac{\|\operatorname{RMSNorm}(W_{T}\hat{x})-\operatorname{RMSNorm}(W\hat{x})\|^{2}}{\|\operatorname{RMSNorm}(W\hat{x})\|^{2}}\xrightarrow{P}2\!\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\right)\approx 0.404

(19)

8.2
Proof

Step 1: Row-wise Bussgang analysis.
Using
\mathbb{E}_{\hat{x}}[\hat{x}_{j}\hat{x}_{k}]=\delta_{jk}/n
, the row-level correlation between
y_{i}=w_{i}^{\top}\hat{x}
and
y_{T,i}=s_{i}\operatorname{sign}(w_{i})^{\top}\hat{x}
is:

\operatorname{Corr}(y_{i},y_{T,i}\mid W)=\frac{\|w_{i}\|_{1}}{\sqrt{n}\|w_{i}\|}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}

(20)

by Fact
1
.

Step 2: Vector-level concentration
(sketch; full proof in Appendix B). Three quadratic forms
\hat{x}^{\top}M_{k}\hat{x}
(for
M_{k}\in\{W^{\top}W,\,W_{T}^{\top}W_{T},\,W^{\top}W_{T}\}
) must concentrate around their means
\operatorname{tr}(M_{k})/n
. By Lévy’s spherical concentration inequality with Lipschitz constant
L=2\|M_{k}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O(1)
(via the operator norm bound
\|W\|_{\mathrm{op}}\to 1+\sqrt{\gamma}
; see Appendix B), each deviates by
O(n^{-1/2+\epsilon})
. The row-scale averages
\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}s_{i}^{2}\to 2/(\pi n)
follow from a triangular-array Chebyshev argument (variance
O(1/(mn^{2}))
).

Step 3: Combining.
The three concentrated quadratic forms yield:

\cos^{2}\!\angle(y,y_{T})\xrightarrow{P}\frac{\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}s_{i}^{2}}{\|W\|_{F}^{2}/(mn)}=\frac{2/(\pi n)}{1/n}=\frac{2}{\pi}

(Here
\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}s_{i}^{2}\to 2/(\pi n)
is the per-row average of
s_{i}^{2}
, and
\|W\|_{F}^{2}/(mn)\to 1/n
is the per-entry variance; the factor
m
cancels between numerator and denominator.)
By Step 1,
\mathbb{E}_{\hat{x}}[y_{i}\cdot y_{T,i}\mid W]=s_{i}^{2}>0
for each row, so
\mathbb{E}_{\hat{x}}[y\cdot y_{T}\mid W]=\sum_{i}s_{i}^{2}\to 2m/(\pi n)
which is
\Theta(1)
when
m/n=\gamma
. The Lévy concentration gives fluctuations
O(1/\sqrt{n})
around this mean (Appendix
B
). Since the mean is
\Theta(1)
and bounded away from zero while fluctuations are
O(1/\sqrt{n})
, Lévy concentration gives
\Pr(y\cdot y_{T}<0)\leq 2\exp(-\Omega(n))
, so
y\cdot y_{T}>0
with probability
1-o(1)
. The positive root
\cos\theta\to\sqrt{2/\pi}
therefore applies. The spherical chord-length formula gives the RMSNorm error:
2(1-\sqrt{2/\pi})\approx 0.404
. ∎

8.3
A Mechanism for Ternary Quantization Tolerance

The explanatory chain connecting Theorem
4
to ternary quantization tolerance:

1.

Theorem
4
: Ternary quantization error
\Delta W=W_{T}-W
satisfies
\Delta w_{ij}=\operatorname{sign}(w_{ij})(s_{i}-|w_{ij}|)
—a
sign-preserving
perturbation. The quantized weight
w_{ij}+\Delta w_{ij}=s_{i}\operatorname{sign}(w_{ij})
preserves the sign of the original weight, so no weight signs are flipped.

2.

Theorem
3
: For the constant-
\delta
sign-preserving model (
\Delta w_{ij}=\operatorname{sign}(w_{ij})\delta
), the Bussgang-ReLU mechanism (Lemma
A.1
) channels
2/\pi
of the post-ReLU perturbation energy into the radial direction, yielding
(\pi-2)/\pi\approx 36\%
transverse energy relative to sign-flip perturbations.

3.

Structural analogy and caveat
: Ternary quantization error has element-wise varying magnitudes
s_{i}-|w_{ij}|
(not the constant
\delta
of Theorem
3
’s model), and the sign-preserving property means
\operatorname{sign}(w_{ij}+\Delta w_{ij})=\operatorname{sign}(w_{ij})
(the
quantized
weight preserves sign), not that
\Delta w_{ij}
is proportional to
\operatorname{sign}(w_{ij})
—indeed when
|w_{ij}|>s_{i}
, the perturbation
\Delta w_{ij}
opposes
\operatorname{sign}(w_{ij})
. Consequently, the pre-ReLU Bussgang correlation has
opposite sign
:
\operatorname{Corr}(z_{i},\Delta z_{i})\to-\sqrt{1-2/\pi}\approx-0.60
for ternary error, versus
+\sqrt{2/\pi}\approx+0.80
for Theorem
3
’s constant-
\delta
model.

Derivation of
\operatorname{Corr}(z_{i},\Delta z_{i})\to-\sqrt{1-2/\pi}
: The ternary perturbation is
\Delta w_{ij}=\operatorname{sign}(w_{ij})(s_{i}-|w_{ij}|)
. With
\hat{x}
independent of
W
and
\mathbb{E}[\hat{x}_{j}\hat{x}_{k}]=\delta_{jk}/n
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}(z_{i},\Delta z_{i})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_{j}\mathbb{E}[w_{ij}\cdot\operatorname{sign}(w_{ij})(s_{i}-|w_{ij}|)]=\tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_{j}\mathbb{E}[|w_{ij}|(s_{i}-|w_{ij}|)].

By i.i.d. symmetry, each term equals
\mathbb{E}[|w_{i1}|\cdot s_{i}]-\mathbb{E}[w_{i1}^{2}]
. Since
s_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k}|w_{ik}|
, we get
\mathbb{E}[|w_{i1}|s_{i}]=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}[w_{i1}^{2}]+\frac{n-1}{n}(\mathbb{E}|w_{i1}|)^{2}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\frac{2(n-1)}{\pi n^{2}}
. Subtracting
\mathbb{E}[w_{i1}^{2}]=1/n
gives
\operatorname{Cov}(z_{i},\Delta z_{i})\to(2/\pi-1)/n
as
n\to\infty
. Similarly,
\operatorname{Var}(\Delta z_{i})\to(1-2/\pi)/n
(from
\operatorname{Var}(|w_{ij}|-s_{i})\to(1-2/\pi)/n
). With
\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})=1/n
:

\operatorname{Corr}(z_{i},\Delta z_{i})=\frac{(2/\pi-1)/n}{\sqrt{(1/n)\cdot(1-2/\pi)/n}}=\frac{2/\pi-1}{\sqrt{1-2/\pi}}=-\sqrt{1-2/\pi}.

Since the radial fraction depends on
\operatorname{Corr}^{2}
, this gives a
pre-ReLU
radial fraction
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ternary}}_{\mathrm{pre}}\to 1-2/\pi\approx 0.363
versus
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{mag}}_{\mathrm{pre}}\to 2/\pi\approx 0.637
. (These sum to 1 by the variance decomposition of
|w_{ij}|
: writing
|w_{ij}|=\mathbb{E}|w_{ij}|+(|w_{ij}|-\mathbb{E}|w_{ij}|)
decomposes the absolute value into its mean—proportional to the constant-
\delta
direction
\operatorname{sign}(w_{ij})\cdot\delta
—and a centered residual—proportional to the ternary direction
\operatorname{sign}(w_{ij})(s_{i}-|w_{ij}|)
. Since
w_{ij}=\operatorname{sign}(w_{ij})\cdot|w_{ij}|
, the identity
\mathbb{E}[w^{2}]=(\mathbb{E}|w|)^{2}+\operatorname{Var}(|w|)
gives
(\mathbb{E}|w|)^{2}/\mathbb{E}[w^{2}]+\operatorname{Var}(|w|)/\mathbb{E}[w^{2}]=2/\pi+(1-2/\pi)=1
. After projection through
\hat{x}
(independent of
W
), the squared correlations of
z_{i}
with each perturbation direction inherit this partition.)
Important
: these are pre-ReLU radial fractions; the post-ReLU values are conditional on gate-preservation (i.e.,
z_{i}+\Delta z_{i}
keeping the same sign as
z_{i}
). For Theorem
3
’s constant-
\delta
model,
|\Delta z_{i}|=O(\delta)
while
f_{z_{i}}(0)=\sqrt{n/(2\pi)}
, giving gate-flip probability
O(\sqrt{n}\delta)=O(\sqrt{p})
(small for
p\to 0
); the pre- and post-ReLU fractions therefore agree to leading order. For ternary quantization, however, the perturbation
|\Delta z_{i}|=O(1/\sqrt{n})
—comparable to
|z_{i}|
itself—so the gate-flip probability is
O(1)
, and the pre-ReLU radial fraction does
not
directly transfer to the post-ReLU regime. Ternary quantization error is thus
less
radial than the idealized constant-
\delta
model at the pre-ReLU level, though still more radial than sign-flip perturbations (
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{sign}}\approx p
). The qualitative ordering
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{sign}}_{\mathrm{pre}}<\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ternary}}_{\mathrm{pre}}<\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{mag(const.\text{-}\delta)}}_{\mathrm{pre}}
holds at the pre-ReLU level; the post-ReLU radial fraction for ternary error is computed in Section
8.4
and Appendix
G
.

8.4
Post-ReLU Ternary Radial Fraction

The pre-ReLU analysis above leaves open whether the
O(1)
gate-flip rate (
{\sim}20.6\%
) significantly alters the radial fraction. We resolve this by computing the post-ReLU radial fraction directly (full derivation in Appendix
G
).

Setup.

The pre-activations
(z_{i},z_{i}^{Q})
are asymptotically bivariate Gaussian (Lindeberg–Feller CLT under delocalization) with:

\sigma_{1}=1/\sqrt{n},\quad\sigma_{2}=\sqrt{2/(\pi n)}\approx 0.798\,\sigma_{1},\quad\rho=\sqrt{2/\pi}.

Note
\sigma_{2}<\sigma_{1}
: the quantized model produces systematically smaller pre-activations.

Gate-flip rate.

By the Van Vleck arcsine law,
P_{\mathrm{flip}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\arccos(\rho)=\phi/\pi\approx 20.6\%
, where
\phi=\arccos(\sqrt{2/\pi})\approx 0.647\,\mathrm{rad}
.

Post-ReLU energy.

Using the Price–Cho–Saul formula
\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{ReLU}(X)\operatorname{ReLU}(Y)]=\frac{\sigma_{X}\sigma_{Y}}{2\pi}(\sin\theta+(\pi-\theta)\cos\theta)
for bivariate normals with
\theta=\arccos(\rho)
, define the auxiliary constant:

S=\frac{\cos\phi\,(\sin\phi+(\pi-\phi)\cos\phi)}{2\pi}\approx 0.3293.

The per-neuron error energy is
\mathbb{E}[\delta a_{i}^{2}]=\frac{1}{n}(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{\pi}-2S)\approx 0.1597/n
.

Radial fraction.

The radial projection is
\langle\delta a,\hat{a}\rangle\to(S-\tfrac{1}{2})\sqrt{2}\approx-0.241
(negative: ternary quantization contracts the activation vector). The post-ReLU radial fraction is:

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ternary}}_{\mathrm{post}}=\frac{2(S-\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}}{\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{\pi}-2S}\approx 0.365.

(21)

Near-equality with pre-ReLU.

The pre-ReLU value
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ternary}}_{\mathrm{pre}}=1-2/\pi\approx 0.363
is
exact
(Bussgang orthogonality:
\mathbb{E}[\delta z\cdot z^{Q}]=0
). The post-ReLU value
0.365
is the asymptotic closed form (
n\to\infty
). These differ by only
{\sim}0.4\%
relative. Despite the 20.6% gate-flip rate, ReLU is approximately “transparent” to the radial/transverse decomposition for ternary error—a non-trivial structural property absent from the small-
\delta
perturbative analysis of Theorem
3
.

Equal-norm comparison.

Matching Frobenius norms between ternary error (
\|\Delta W\|^{2}=n(1-2/\pi)
) and sign-flip perturbation requires
p=(1-2/\pi)/4\approx 0.091
. At this
p
, sign-flips produce
1.43\times
more transverse energy than ternary error, consistent with
c(0.091)\approx 2.18
from Theorem
3
.

8.5
Scope and Caveats

In the same angular metric, the pre-RMSNorm angular deviation is
1-\cos\theta=1-\sqrt{2/\pi}\approx 0.202
, and the post-RMSNorm chord-length error is
\|\hat{u}_{1}-\hat{u}_{2}\|^{2}=2(1-\cos\theta)\approx 0.404
—simply twice the angular deviation (the standard relationship between angle and chord on a unit sphere).
Single-layer RMSNorm does not differentially suppress quantization error
: the radial fraction of ternary quantization error is
\mathcal{R}\approx 0.365
both pre- and post-ReLU (Section
8.4
), i.e., only
{\sim}36.5\%
radial and
{\sim}63.5\%
transverse. Since the majority of the perturbation energy is already transverse, RMSNorm’s radial filtering absorbs only a minority (
36.5\%
) of the error, while
63.5\%
passes through as functionally relevant output distortion.

The true suppression mechanism requires the
multi-component pipeline
: the sign-preserving weight structure provides Bussgang correlation between perturbation and activation; ReLU propagates this into the hidden space, creating the differential treatment of sign vs. magnitude perturbations; and RMSNorm then exposes this differential. The quantization tolerance is a property of the
architecture
(sign structure + ReLU + RMSNorm together), not of RMSNorm alone.

10.3
Limitations

1.

Single-layer theory
: The
\pi/(\pi-2)
result applies to a two-layer toy model, not the full transformer stack.

2.

Gaussian assumption
: Real trained weights are not i.i.d. Gaussian; the cross-model
\cos^{2}
deviations from
2/\pi
reported in Section 9.2 reflect this.

3.

ReLU only
: Theorem
3
is proved for ReLU, which provides the clean half-space partition enabling closed-form integration. Extension to gated activations (SwiGLU/GeGLU, used in modern LLMs) requires a bivariate-Gaussian-conditional-on-gate analysis not attempted here (though Exp B shows qualitatively similar
C_{L}
behavior with SiLU).

4.

Outlier theory gap
: The single-layer outlier leverage ratio (
R\approx n\alpha^{2}
, Theorem
6
) predicts per-entry
R\in[20,162]
for extreme outliers (
\alpha\in[0.1,0.3]
,
{\sim}16\%
of outlier dimensions); the aggregate
n\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{emp}}[\alpha^{2}]\approx 7
–
10
accounts for the full
\alpha
distribution. At linear response (
p\leq 0.5\%
; Exp E), count-matched NLL leverage stabilizes at
{\sim}10\times
, matching this prediction; the
37.6\times
at
p=5\%
reflects an empirically observed
{\sim}4\times
multi-flip interaction inflation (no closed-form derivation is available for the interaction terms). The all-column
\Delta
PPL ratio (
1{,}265\times
) appeared to exceed the energy prediction (
R_{\mathrm{col}}\leq 19
) by
{\sim}67\times
, but log-loss analysis shows this gap is an artifact of PPL nonlinearity: the all-column NLL ratio is only
5.0\times
(Table
8
), well within
R_{\mathrm{col}}\leq 19
. Multi-layer propagation remains open but is no longer needed to explain the observation. The
\alpha^{2}
scaling is additionally confirmed by noise-floor sweep (Exp E, Table
9
), activation-level perturbation energy (Exp F, Spearman
\rho=0.955
), and column-flip
\Delta
NLL measurements (Exp G, Spearman
\rho=0.927
).

5.

Multi-layer propagation
: How
\mathcal{R}
evolves across depth is an open problem.

6.

Position dependence
: Theorem
3
applies to weights whose output passes through a downstream ReLU (the
W_{1}
position). Quantization of weights
after
a nonlinearity (e.g., FFN down-projection in a real transformer) acts on non-Gaussian inputs, where the Bussgang relation (Lemma
A.1
) no longer holds exactly. The mechanism may still apply approximately but is not covered by our analysis.

11
Conclusion

11.1
Summary

We have provided a geometric explanation for why pre-norm transformers tolerate ternary weight quantization:

1.

Sign patterns capture
2/\pi\approx 63.7\%
of weight directional energy (Fact
1
), while magnitude with random signs is uninformative (Proposition
2
).

2.

RMSNorm acts as a radial filter whose Fréchet derivative is a transverse projector (Theorem
2
), absorbing perturbations aligned with the output direction.

3.

In a two-layer ReLU + RMSNorm model, sign-flip perturbations produce
\pi/(\pi-2)\approx 2.75
times more transverse energy than magnitude perturbations (Theorem
3
). The mechanism: the sign-preserving weight structure provides Bussgang alignment; ReLU propagates it into an asymmetry between sign-flip and magnitude perturbations’ radial fractions (
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{sign}}\approx p
vs.
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{mag}}\approx 2/\pi
); RMSNorm exposes this differential.

4.

Ternary quantization error is a sign-preserving perturbation with angular alignment
\cos^{2}\to 2/\pi
(Theorem
4
). Its pre-ReLU radial fraction (
1-2/\pi\approx 0.363
, exact via Bussgang orthogonality) places it between sign-flips (
\mathcal{R}\approx p
) and the idealized constant-
\delta
model (
\mathcal{R}=2/\pi
). Despite a 20.6% gate-flip rate, the post-ReLU radial fraction (
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ternary}}_{\mathrm{post}}\approx 0.365
; Section
8.4
, Appendix
G
) agrees with the pre-ReLU value to within
0.4\%
: ReLU is approximately transparent to the radial/transverse decomposition for ternary error.

5.

The gap from the single-layer
2.75\times
baseline to the substantial sign sensitivity observed in real models comes from outlier features violating delocalization (Section 7.2), not from multi-layer compounding (which is not experimentally supported). The per-entry leverage
R\approx n\alpha^{2}
(Appendix
E
) and column-group leverage
R_{\mathrm{col}}\leq 19
for
\gamma\leq 0.5
(Appendix
F
) are rigorously derived. At linear response (
p\leq 0.5\%
; Exp E), count-matched NLL leverage stabilizes at
{\sim}10\times
, matching
n\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{emp}}[\alpha^{2}]\approx 7
–
10
; Exps F–G further confirm the
\alpha^{2}
scaling (Spearman
\rho=0.955
and
0.927
, respectively).

Appendix A
Complete Proof of Theorem 3

A.1
Part A: Energy Ratio Before RMSNorm

Goal
: Show
R_{A}(p):=\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta y^{\mathrm{sign}}\|^{2}]/\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta y^{\mathrm{mag}}\|^{2}]=1-4\sqrt{p}/(3\pi)+O(\sqrt{p})
.

Step A1: Output energy via
W_{2}
.

Since
W_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times m}
has i.i.d.
N(0,1/m)
entries independent of
(W_{1},\hat{x})
:

\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta y\|^{2}\mid\Delta a]=\frac{d_{\mathrm{out}}}{m}\|\Delta a\|^{2}

Taking expectations:
\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta y\|^{2}]=d_{\mathrm{out}}\cdot\mathbb{E}[(\Delta a_{i})^{2}]
(by i.i.d. structure of rows of
W_{1}
).

Step A2: Activation change decomposition.

For neuron
i
with pre-activation
z_{i}=w_{i}^{\top}\hat{x}
(original) and
z_{i}^{\prime}=w_{i}^{\prime\top}\hat{x}
(perturbed):

\Delta a_{i}=\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i}^{\prime})-\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})

Decompose into gate-flip (sign of
z_{i}
changes) and smooth (both remain active) contributions:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(\Delta a_{i})^{2}]

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[z_{i}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{z_{i}>0,\,z_{i}^{\prime}<0}]+\mathbb{E}[z_{i}^{\prime 2}\mathbf{1}_{z_{i}<0,\,z_{i}^{\prime}>0}]}_{2\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[(z_{i}^{\prime}-z_{i})^{2}\mathbf{1}_{z_{i}>0,\,z_{i}^{\prime}>0}]}_{\beta_{\mathrm{smooth}}}

(22)

(Note: on
\{z_{i}<0,z_{i}^{\prime}<0\}
both ReLU outputs are zero, contributing nothing to
\mathbb{E}[(\Delta a_{i})^{2}]
.)

Step A3: Rescaling to unit variance.

For
w_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with i.i.d.
N(0,1/n)
entries and
\hat{x}\in S^{n-1}
, we have

\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})\;=\;\hat{x}^{\top}\mathbb{E}[w_{i}w_{i}^{\top}]\hat{x}\;=\;\frac{\|\hat{x}\|^{2}}{n}\;=\;\frac{1}{n}.

Define the rescaled variables
X:=\sqrt{n}\,z_{i}
and
X^{\prime}:=\sqrt{n}\,z^{\prime}_{i}
. Under assumption (A1), both marginals
z_{i}
and
z^{\prime}_{i}
are exactly Gaussian (
z_{i}
is a linear combination of independent Gaussians;
z^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{j}S_{ij}W_{1,ij}\hat{x}_{j}
with
S_{ij}W_{1,ij}\sim N(0,1/n)
marginally). The correlation is exact:

\rho\;=\;\operatorname{Corr}(z_{i},z^{\prime}_{i})\;=\;\mathbb{E}[S_{ij}]\;=\;1-2p,

where
S_{ij}\in\{-1,+1\}
denotes the sign-flip indicator with
\Pr(S_{ij}=-1)=p
. However, the
joint
distribution
(z_{i},z^{\prime}_{i})
is not exactly bivariate Gaussian for finite
n
: since each summand pair
(W_{ij}\hat{x}_{j},\,S_{ij}W_{ij}\hat{x}_{j})
is supported on two lines (depending on
S_{ij}
), exact joint normality holds only in the
n\to\infty
limit by the multivariate Lindeberg-Feller CLT (via Cramér-Wold device applied to all linear combinations of the bivariate sum) under delocalization (A2). The Lindeberg condition is verified by
\max_{j}\hat{x}_{j}^{2}=\|\hat{x}\|_{\infty}^{2}=o(1)
, ensuring that the bivariate Gaussian approximation error vanishes in the asymptotic limit and does not affect the leading-order constant
\pi/(\pi-2)
. Concretely: the Berry–Esseen-type error for the bivariate CLT is
O(\|\hat{x}\|_{\infty})=O(1/\sqrt{n})
(the delocalization rate). Since Theorem
3
takes
n\to\infty
for
fixed

p
(see the statement: “for each fixed
p
, as
n,m,d_{\mathrm{out}}\to\infty
”), this CLT error
O(1/\sqrt{n})
vanishes before the small-
p
expansion is applied, and thus does not contaminate the
p^{3/2}
coefficients computed below.
3
3

3

Strictly, the uniformity of CLT convergence in
p
(i.e.,
\sup_{p\in(\epsilon,1/2)}|\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}(p,n)-\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}(p,\infty)|\to 0
for each
\epsilon>0
) is an implicit assumption here; as
p\to 0
the gate-flip event degenerates, and Berry–Esseen-type bounds may depend on
p
through the conditional density at the boundary. The limit interchange is rigorously justified for any fixed
p>0
(Remark
2
); near-zero behavior is assumed by continuity of
\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}
in
p
.
We proceed under this asymptotic bivariate normal model with correlation
\rho
.

Step A4: Computing
\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}
.

In the asymptotic limit where
(X,X^{\prime})
converges to standard bivariate normal with correlation
\rho
(Step A3), we compute the gate-flip contribution:

\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}\;:=\;\mathbb{E}\!\left[X^{2}\,\mathbf{1}_{X>0,\,X^{\prime}<0}\right]

By direct integration over the quarter-plane
\{X>0,X^{\prime}<0\}
:

\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}\;=\;\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{a}{1+a^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\arctan(a),\qquad a\;=\;\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}.

With
\rho=1-2p
, we have
\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}=2\sqrt{p(1-p)}
and
a=\rho/\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\approx 1/(2\sqrt{p})
for small
p
. Expanding to next order (the
p^{3/2}
coefficients are needed to verify the final constant):

\displaystyle a/(1+a^{2})

\displaystyle=\rho\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}=2\sqrt{p}-5p^{3/2}+O(p^{5/2}),

\displaystyle\arctan(a)

\displaystyle=\pi/2-\arctan(1/a)=\pi/2-2\sqrt{p}-p^{3/2}/3+O(p^{5/2}).

Substituting: the constant terms cancel (
1/4-\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}=1/4-1/4=0
), the
\sqrt{p}
terms cancel, and collecting
p^{3/2}
coefficients gives
5/(2\pi)+1/(6\pi)=16/(6\pi)=8/(3\pi)
:

\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}\;=\;\frac{8p^{3/2}}{3\pi}+O(p^{5/2}).

(23)

Step A5: Computing
\beta_{\mathrm{smooth}}
.

Using the ReLU product formula (a classical result obtained from Price’s theorem
[
13
]
by integrating
\partial\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{ReLU}(X)\operatorname{ReLU}(X^{\prime})]/\partial\rho=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{X>0}\mathbf{1}_{X^{\prime}>0}]
):

\mathbb{E}[\max(X,0)\max(X^{\prime},0)]\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}+\rho\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\arcsin\rho\right)\right].

The smooth-event energy (on
\{X>0,X^{\prime}>0\}
, the region where both neurons remain active) is

\beta_{\mathrm{smooth}}\;:=\;\mathbb{E}[(X^{\prime}-X)^{2}\,\mathbf{1}_{X>0,\,X^{\prime}>0}]\;=\;\mathbb{E}[X^{2}\mathbf{1}_{X>0,\,X^{\prime}>0}]+\mathbb{E}[X^{\prime 2}\mathbf{1}_{X>0,\,X^{\prime}>0}]-2\mathbb{E}[XX^{\prime}\mathbf{1}_{X>0,X^{\prime}>0}].

By exchangeability of
(X,X^{\prime})
under joint Gaussian with equal marginals, the first two terms are equal. Using
\mathbb{E}[X^{2}\mathbf{1}_{X>0,\,X^{\prime}>0}]=\mathbb{E}[X^{2}\mathbf{1}_{X>0}]-\mathbb{E}[X^{2}\mathbf{1}_{X>0,\,X^{\prime}<0}]=1/2-\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}
and the ReLU-product formula
\mathbb{E}[XX^{\prime}\mathbf{1}_{X>0,X^{\prime}>0}]=\mathbb{E}[\max(X,0)\max(X^{\prime},0)]
, substituting
\rho=1-2p
and expanding:

\beta_{\mathrm{smooth}}\;=\;2p-\frac{8p^{3/2}}{\pi}+O(p^{5/2}).

(24)

Step A6: Total sign-flip energy.

Since the calculations of
\beta_{\mathrm{flip}},\beta_{\mathrm{smooth}}
were done in the asymptotic bivariate normal model (rescaled unit-variance scale), the sign-flip activation energy is (asymptotically as
n\to\infty
):

\mathbb{E}[(\Delta a_{i}^{\mathrm{sign}})^{2}]\;=\;\frac{1}{n}\left(2\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}+\beta_{\mathrm{smooth}}\right)\;=\;\frac{1}{n}\cdot 2p\!\left(1-\frac{4\sqrt{p}}{3\pi}\right)+O(p^{5/2}/n),

where the factor
1/n
accounts for
\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})=1/n
(Step A3), and

\frac{16p^{3/2}}{3\pi}-\frac{8p^{3/2}}{\pi}\;=\;\frac{16-24}{3\pi}\,p^{3/2}\;=\;-\frac{8p^{3/2}}{3\pi},

yielding the displayed form.

Step A7: Magnitude energy (gate-state decomposition).

For the sign-preserving model
\Delta z_{i}^{\mathrm{mag}}=\delta\sum_{j}\operatorname{sign}(W_{1,ij})\hat{x}_{j}
with
\delta^{2}=4p/n
:

\operatorname{Var}(\Delta z_{i}^{\mathrm{mag}})\;=\;\delta^{2}\sum_{j}\hat{x}_{j}^{2}\cdot\operatorname{Var}(\operatorname{sign}(W_{1,ij}))\;=\;\delta^{2}\;=\;\frac{4p}{n}.

After ReLU, decompose into four gate-state events for the perturbed activation

•

Smooth
(gate stays on):
z_{i}>0

\Delta a_{i}^{\mathrm{mag}}=\Delta z_{i}^{\mathrm{mag}}

f_{z_{i}}(0)\cdot O(\delta)=\sqrt{n/(2\pi)}\cdot O(\sqrt{p/n})=O(\sqrt{p})

\tfrac{1}{2}-O(\sqrt{p})
, contributing
(\tfrac{1}{2}-O(\sqrt{p}))\cdot\delta^{2}=2p/n-O(p^{3/2}/n)
.

•

Gate stays off
:
z_{i}<0
and
z_{i}+\Delta z_{i}^{\mathrm{mag}}<0
. Contribution:
\Delta a_{i}^{\mathrm{mag}}=0
.

•

Gate-on flip
(
\text{off}\to\text{on}
):
z_{i}<0<z_{i}+\Delta z_{i}^{\mathrm{mag}}
. Probability
=O(\sqrt{p})
. On this event

, so
(\Delta a_{i})^{2}=O(p/n)
. Contribution:
O(\sqrt{p})\cdot O(p/n)=O(p^{3/2}/n)
.

•

Gate-off flip
(
\text{on}\to\text{off}
):
z_{i}>0
and
z_{i}+\Delta z_{i}^{\mathrm{mag}}<0
. Then
a_{i}=z_{i}
,
a_{i}^{\prime}=0
, so
\Delta a_{i}=-z_{i}
with
|z_{i}|\leq O(\delta)
. Probability
=O(\sqrt{p})
by the same boundary argument. Contribution:
O(\sqrt{p})\cdot O(\delta^{2})=O(p^{3/2}/n)
.

Combining smooth + both gate-flip events:

\mathbb{E}[(\Delta a_{i}^{\mathrm{mag}})^{2}]\;=\;\frac{2p}{n}+O(p^{3/2}/n).

Step A8: Energy ratio.

Both energies are at the
1/n
scale. The denominator carries an

boundary correction from Step A7, so dividing:

R_{A}(p)\;:=\;\frac{\mathbb{E}[(\Delta a_{i}^{\mathrm{sign}})^{2}]}{\mathbb{E}[(\Delta a_{i}^{\mathrm{mag}})^{2}]}\;=\;\frac{(2p/n)\bigl(1-4\sqrt{p}/(3\pi)\bigr)}{(2p/n)(1+O(\sqrt{p}))}\;=\;1+O(\sqrt{p}).\qquad\square

Note on the structure of

Part B: Radial Fraction Analysis

Goal
: Show

Step B1: Setup.

By Theorem

\mathcal{R}=(\Delta y\cdot\hat{y})^{2}/\|\Delta y\|^{2}=\cos^{2}(\Delta y,y)

Step B2: Angle-preservation lemma.

For fixed

and

with i.i.d.
N(0,1/m)

W_{2}u
and

\cos^{2}(W_{2}u,W_{2}v)\xrightarrow{P}\cos^{2}(u,v)\quad(d\to\infty)

Application
: In our setting,

and

are functions of
(W_{1},\hat{x})
. Since

is independent of
(W_{1},\hat{x})
by assumption (A5), we condition on

Step B3: Sign radial fraction.

We need

. Three ingredients:

(i)
Reflection symmetry identity
: Since
(z_{i},z_{i}^{\prime})\overset{d}{=}(-z_{i},-z_{i}^{\prime})

(using equal marginals) gives:

\mathbb{E}[\Delta a_{i}\cdot a_{i}]=\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i}^{\prime})\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})]-\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})^{2}]=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[(\Delta a_{i})^{2}]

(ii)
Activation second moment

\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})=1/n
. By symmetry:

\mathbb{E}[a_{i}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[z_{i}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{z_{i}>0}]=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})=\frac{1}{2n}

(iii)
Perturbation second moment

\mathbb{E}[(\Delta a_{i})^{2}]=\beta_{\mathrm{total}}/n

\beta_{\mathrm{total}}:=2\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}+\beta_{\mathrm{smooth}}=2p(1-4\sqrt{p}/(3\pi))+O(p^{5/2})
denotes the unit-variance-scale total energy.

Combining via SLLN concentration and the random projection lemma (B2) (all

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{sign}}=\cos^{2}(\Delta a,a)=\frac{(\mathbb{E}[\Delta a_{i}\cdot a_{i}])^{2}}{\mathbb{E}[(\Delta a_{i})^{2}]\cdot\mathbb{E}[a_{i}^{2}]}=\frac{(\beta_{\mathrm{total}}/(2n))^{2}}{(\beta_{\mathrm{total}}/n)\cdot(1/(2n))}=\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{total}}}{2}=p-\frac{4p^{3/2}}{3\pi}+O(p^{5/2})

Note on terminology
: Throughout this appendix, “
\operatorname{Corr}(X,Y)
” denotes the
uncentered

and

is the uncentered version.

Lemma A.1

(Bussgang-ReLU correlation)
.

Under assumptions
(A1)–(A2)
, let
z_{i}=W_{1,i}\hat{x}
,
\Delta z_{i}^{\mathrm{mag}}=\delta\cdot\operatorname{sign}(W_{1,i})\cdot\hat{x}
with
\delta^{2}=4p/n
,
a_{i}=\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})
, and
\Delta a_{i}=\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i}+\Delta z_{i})-\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})
. Then as
n\to\infty
:

\operatorname{Corr}(\Delta a_{i},a_{i})\to\sqrt{2/\pi}+O(\sqrt{p}).

The
O(\sqrt{p})
residual arises from gate-flip boundary events (
\Pr(0<z_{i}<|\Delta z_{i}|)=O(\sqrt{p})
) that contribute
O(\sqrt{p})
relative corrections to
\mathbb{E}[(\Delta A)^{2}]
. For finite
n
, the boundary term in
\mathbb{E}[A\,\Delta A]
is
O(\delta^{2}\sqrt{p})=O(p^{3/2}/n)
(proof part (2)), which relative to the main term
\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Cov}(z_{i},\Delta z_{i})=\delta/(2\sqrt{n})\cdot\sqrt{2/\pi}=O(\sqrt{p}/n)
gives an
O(p)
relative correction; combined with the
O(\sqrt{p})
correction from
\mathbb{E}[(\Delta A)^{2}]
, the dominant residual is
O(\sqrt{p})
.

Proof.

(1) Joint asymptotic normality.

Write
z_{i}=\sum_{j}W_{j}\hat{x}_{j}
and
\Delta z_{i}=\delta\sum_{j}\operatorname{sign}(W_{j})\hat{x}_{j}
, sums of independent terms across
j
. The bivariate moments are:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{j}\hat{x}_{j}^{2}\cdot\operatorname{Var}(W_{j})\;=\;\frac{1}{n},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(\Delta z_{i})

\displaystyle\;=\;\delta^{2}\sum_{j}\hat{x}_{j}^{2}\cdot\operatorname{Var}(\operatorname{sign}(W_{j}))\;=\;\delta^{2},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}(z_{i},\Delta z_{i})

\displaystyle\;=\;\delta\sum_{j}\hat{x}_{j}^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}[W_{j}\operatorname{sign}(W_{j})]\;=\;\delta\cdot\mathbb{E}[|W_{1}|]\;=\;\delta\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi n}},

where the last line uses
\mathbb{E}[W_{j}\operatorname{sign}(W_{j})]=\mathbb{E}[|W_{j}|]=\sqrt{2/(\pi n)}
for
W_{j}\sim N(0,1/n)
. Thus

\operatorname{Corr}(z_{i},\Delta z_{i})\;=\;\frac{\delta\sqrt{2/(\pi n)}}{\sqrt{(1/n)\cdot\delta^{2}}}\;=\;\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}\quad\text{(population second-moment identity, valid for all }n\text{)}.

Note: this is an identity for the ratio of population moments (first- and second-order); the
joint distribution
of
(z_{i},\Delta z_{i})
is not bivariate Gaussian at finite
n
. Under delocalization
\|\hat{x}\|_{\infty}\to 0
, each summand contributes
o(\sigma_{n})
in probability with
\sigma_{n}:=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})}=1/\sqrt{n}
, so the multivariate Lindeberg-Feller condition is satisfied (via Cramér-Wold). Define rescaled variables
Z:=z_{i}/\sigma_{n}=\sqrt{n}\,z_{i}
and
\Delta Z:=\Delta z_{i}/\delta
(each unit-variance). Then
(Z,\Delta Z)\xrightarrow{d}N(0,\Sigma)
as
n\to\infty
, where
\Sigma
has off-diagonal correlation
\sqrt{2/\pi}
.

(2) Post-ReLU correlation.

Let
A:=\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})
and
\Delta A:=\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i}+\Delta z_{i})-\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})
. We compute
\mathbb{E}[A\cdot\Delta A]
by decomposing on the smooth event
\mathcal{E}_{s}:=\{z_{i}>0,\,z_{i}+\Delta z_{i}>0\}
and its complement.

Smooth contribution.
On
\mathcal{E}_{s}
,
A=z_{i}
and
\Delta A=\Delta z_{i}
, so

\mathbb{E}[A\Delta A\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{s}}]\;=\;\mathbb{E}[z_{i}\Delta z_{i}\,\mathbf{1}_{z_{i}>0}]-\mathbb{E}[z_{i}\Delta z_{i}\,\mathbf{1}_{z_{i}>0,\,z_{i}+\Delta z_{i}<0}].

The first term: by the standard identity for jointly Gaussian variables (applied in the
n\to\infty
limit where Part (1) establishes
(z_{i},\Delta z_{i})\xrightarrow{d}N(0,\Sigma)
; by the bivariate CLT and uniform integrability (Part 3),
\mathbb{E}[z_{i}\Delta z_{i}\mathbf{1}_{z_{i}>0}]
converges to the Gaussian-limit value with error
O(\|\hat{x}\|_{\infty})=o(1)
, absorbed into the
O(\sqrt{p})
remainder),

\mathbb{E}[z_{i}\Delta z_{i}\,\mathbf{1}_{z_{i}>0}]\;=\;\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(z_{i},\Delta z_{i})}{\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})}\cdot\mathbb{E}[z_{i}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{z_{i}>0}]\;=\;\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(z_{i},\Delta z_{i})}{\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})}\cdot\frac{\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})}{2}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Cov}(z_{i},\Delta z_{i}).

The second (boundary) term is bounded by
E[|z_{i}|\cdot|\Delta z_{i}|\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}}]
where
\mathcal{B}:=\{0<z_{i}<-\Delta z_{i},\Delta z_{i}<0\}
. Since
\Pr(\mathcal{B})=f_{z_{i}}(0)\cdot O(\delta)=O(\sqrt{p})
(the density factor
\sqrt{n/(2\pi)}
combines with
\delta=2\sqrt{p/n}
) and on
\mathcal{B}
we have
|z_{i}|\leq|\Delta z_{i}|\leq O(\delta)
, the boundary term is
O(\delta^{2}\sqrt{p})=O(p^{3/2}/n)
.

Gate-flip contribution.
On
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{c}\cap\{z_{i}>0\}
(gate turns off),
\Delta A=-z_{i}
. The contribution is
-\mathbb{E}[z_{i}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{z_{i}>0,z_{i}+\Delta z_{i}<0}]
, bounded by
\sigma_{n}^{2}\cdot\Pr(\mathcal{B})=O(p^{3/2}/n)
. On
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{c}\cap\{z_{i}<0\}
(gate turns on),
A=0
so contribution vanishes.

Combining:
\mathbb{E}[A\Delta A]=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Cov}(z_{i},\Delta z_{i})+O(p^{3/2}/n)=\frac{\delta}{2\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{2/\pi}+O(p^{3/2}/n)
.

Correlation.
Since
z_{i}
is unperturbed (independent of
p
),
\mathbb{E}[A^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[z_{i}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{z_{i}>0}]=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})=\frac{1}{2n}
exactly. For
\mathbb{E}[(\Delta A)^{2}]=\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}(1+O(\sqrt{p}))
(the
O(\sqrt{p})
accounts for gate-flip boundary events of probability

\operatorname{Corr}(\Delta A,A)\;=\;\frac{\mathbb{E}[A\Delta A]}{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[A^{2}]\mathbb{E}[(\Delta A)^{2}]}}\;=\;\frac{\frac{\delta}{2\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{2/\pi}}{\frac{\delta}{2\sqrt{n}}\,(1+O(\sqrt{p}))^{1/2}}\;=\;\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}+O(\sqrt{p}).

(3) Uniform integrability.

Since ReLU is 1-Lipschitz,
|A|\leq|z_{i}|
and
|\Delta A|\leq|\Delta z_{i}|
. For any
\epsilon>0
, by Cauchy–Schwarz:
\mathbb{E}[|A_{n}\Delta A_{n}|^{1+\epsilon}]\leq\mathbb{E}[|z_{i}|^{2(1+\epsilon)}]^{1/2}\,\mathbb{E}[|\Delta z_{i}|^{2(1+\epsilon)}]^{1/2}
, and both factors are uniformly bounded in

\mathbb{E}[|X|^{k}]\leq(C\sigma)^{k}k^{k/2}
with
\sigma^{2}=\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})=1/n
. Hence
\{A_{n}\Delta A_{n}\}
and
\{A_{n}^{2}\}
are uniformly integrable, and moment convergence follows from the bivariate CLT.
∎

\displaystyle c(p)

nT_{n}/S_{n}^{2}\leq nT_{n}/(n\mu_{2}/2)^{2}=4T_{n}/(n\mu_{2}^{2})
. Since
T_{n}/n\to\mu_{4}
a.s., it remains to justify
L^{1}
convergence. The sequence
\{4T_{n}/(n\mu_{2}^{2})\}
is uniformly integrable:
\mathbb{E}[(T_{n}/n)^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j}w_{j}^{4})^{2}]=\mu_{4}^{2}+\text{Var}(w_{1}^{4})/n\leq\mu_{4}^{2}+\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{8}]/n<\infty
uniformly (using
\mathbb{E}[w_{1}^{8}]<\infty
). By the Vitali convergence theorem (a.s. convergence + UI
\Rightarrow

L^{1}
convergence):

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{nT_{n}}{S_{n}^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{G_{n}}\right]\to\frac{\mu_{4}}{\mu_{2}^{2}}

C.2
Chernoff Bound on Bad Event

On
G_{n}^{c}
: By
T_{n}\leq S_{n}^{2}
(since
\sum a_{j}^{2}\leq(\sum a_{j})^{2}
for
a_{j}\geq 0
):
nT_{n}/S_{n}^{2}\leq n
.

The probability: for i.i.d. non-negative
w_{j}^{2}
with mean
\mu_{2}
, by Chernoff/Hoeffding:

\Pr(S_{n}/n<\mu_{2}/2)\leq\exp(-cn)

for some
c>0
depending on the distribution of
w_{1}^{2}
.

Therefore:
\mathbb{E}[nT_{n}/S_{n}^{2}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{G_{n}^{c}}]\leq n\cdot e^{-cn}\to 0
exponentially fast.

Combining
:
n\mathbb{E}[T_{n}/S_{n}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[nT_{n}/S_{n}^{2}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{G_{n}}]+\mathbb{E}[nT_{n}/S_{n}^{2}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{G_{n}^{c}}]\to\mu_{4}/\mu_{2}^{2}+0
. ∎

Appendix E
Outlier Amplification Theory

When Assumption (A2) (delocalization) is violated—i.e., an input dimension
j^{*}
carries macroscopic energy
|\hat{x}_{j^{*}}|=\alpha=\Theta(1)
—the gate-flip mechanism amplifies sign perturbations far beyond the
\pi/(\pi-2)
baseline. This appendix derives closed-form leverage formulas for a single outlier weight flip.

E.1
Setup

Consider the single-layer model of Section 5 with
W_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, entries i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,1/n)
(Assumption A1). The input
\hat{x}\in S^{n-1}
has one outlier dimension:

|\hat{x}_{j^{*}}|=\alpha=\Theta(1),\qquad|\hat{x}_{j}|=O(1/\sqrt{n})\text{ for }j\neq j^{*}.

We flip one weight:
W_{1,ij^{*}}\to-W_{1,ij^{*}}
, producing perturbed pre-activation

z_{i}^{\prime}=z_{i}-2W_{1,ij^{*}}\hat{x}_{j^{*}},\qquad\Delta z_{i}=-2W_{1,ij^{*}}\hat{x}_{j^{*}}.

E.2
Correlation Structure

Proposition 2
.

Under the single-entry flip model,
\operatorname{Corr}(z_{i},z_{i}^{\prime})=1-2\alpha^{2}
.

Proof.

Decompose
z_{i}=A+B
where
A=W_{1,ij^{*}}\hat{x}_{j^{*}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\alpha^{2}/n)
and
B=\sum_{j\neq j^{*}}W_{1,ij}\hat{x}_{j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,(1-\alpha^{2})/n)
, with
A\perp B
. After the flip,
z_{i}^{\prime}=-A+B
. Then:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Var}(z_{i}^{\prime})=\tfrac{1}{n},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}(z_{i},z_{i}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Cov}(A+B,\,-A+B)=-\operatorname{Var}(A)+\operatorname{Var}(B)=\tfrac{1-2\alpha^{2}}{n}.

Hence
\rho=(1-2\alpha^{2})/n\div(1/n)=1-2\alpha^{2}
.
∎

Remark 3
.

Since
A
and
B
are jointly Gaussian (both linear in i.i.d. Gaussian weights), the pair
(z_{i},z_{i}^{\prime})
is
exactly
bivariate normal. This is not an asymptotic statement—it holds for all finite
n
under (A1).

E.3
Gate-Flip Probability

Theorem 5

(Outlier gate-flip probability)
.

For a single outlier weight flip with
|\hat{x}_{j^{*}}|=\alpha
:

P_{\mathrm{flip}}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arccos(1-2\alpha^{2})=\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin(\alpha).

Proof.

By Proposition
2
and the preceding remark,
(z_{i},z_{i}^{\prime})
is bivariate normal with correlation
\rho=1-2\alpha^{2}
. The Van Vleck arcsine law gives
\Pr(\operatorname{sign}(z_{i})\neq\operatorname{sign}(z_{i}^{\prime}))=\frac{1}{\pi}\arccos\rho
. The identity
\arccos(1-2\alpha^{2})=2\arcsin\alpha
follows from
\cos(2\theta)=1-2\sin^{2}\theta
with
\theta=\arcsin\alpha
.
∎

Asymptotics.

•

\alpha\to 0
:
P_{\mathrm{flip}}\approx 2\alpha/\pi
. In the delocalized limit
\alpha=1/\sqrt{n}
(single entry), this gives
P_{\mathrm{flip}}\sim 2/(\pi\sqrt{n})\to 0
, recovering the single-entry baseline; the
O(\sqrt{p})
gate-flip rate of Theorem
3
arises from aggregating
\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)
flips across all
n
entries (Step A7).

•

\alpha=1/\sqrt{2}
:
\rho=0
,
P_{\mathrm{flip}}=1/2
(complete decorrelation).

•

\alpha\to 1
:
P_{\mathrm{flip}}\to 1
(deterministic gate flip).

E.4
Gate-Flip Energy

Proposition 3

(Per-neuron gate-flip energy)
.

The gate-flip energy contribution per neuron (physical scale) is:

\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}^{\mathrm{outlier}}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2\pi n}\bigl[\arccos(1-2\alpha^{2})-(1-2\alpha^{2})\cdot 2\alpha\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}\,\bigr].

Proof.

For standardized bivariate normal
(X,Y)
with correlation
\rho
, the Plackett integral (cf. Step A4 in Appendix
A
) gives:

\mathbb{E}[X^{2}\mathbf{1}_{X>0,\,Y<0}]=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigl[\arcsin\rho+\rho\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\,\bigr].

Substituting
\rho=1-2\alpha^{2}
:

\displaystyle\arcsin(1-2\alpha^{2})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\pi}{2}-\arccos(1-2\alpha^{2}),

\displaystyle\rho\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}

\displaystyle=(1-2\alpha^{2})\cdot 2\alpha\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}.

Hence:

\mathbb{E}[X^{2}\mathbf{1}_{X>0,Y<0}]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigl[\arccos(1-2\alpha^{2})-(1-2\alpha^{2})\cdot 2\alpha\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}\,\bigr].

Scaling by
\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})=1/n
yields the physical-scale result.
∎

Small-
\alpha
expansion.

Using
\arccos(1-2\alpha^{2})=2\arcsin\alpha\approx 2\alpha+\alpha^{3}/3
and
(1-2\alpha^{2})\cdot 2\alpha\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}\approx 2\alpha-5\alpha^{3}
:

\beta_{\mathrm{flip}}^{\mathrm{outlier}}\approx\frac{1}{2\pi n}\cdot\frac{16\alpha^{3}}{3}=\frac{8\alpha^{3}}{3\pi n}.

Setting
p=\alpha^{2}
formally recovers the Step A4 result
8p^{3/2}/(3\pi)
on unit-variance scale.

E.5
Per-Flip Leverage Ratio

Theorem 6

(Outlier leverage ratio)
.

Define
R(\alpha,n)=E_{\mathrm{outlier}}/E_{\mathrm{non\text{-}outlier}}
as the ratio of post-ReLU energy change from one outlier flip to one non-outlier flip. Then:

R(\alpha,n)=\frac{f(1-2\alpha^{2})}{f(1-2/n)},

where
f(\rho)=1-\rho+\frac{1}{\pi}\bigl(\rho\arccos\rho-\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\bigr)
is the post-ReLU energy function for bivariate normal with correlation
\rho
.

Proof.

For standard bivariate normal
(X,Y)
with correlation
\rho
, the ReLU energy difference is:

f(\rho)\;=\;\mathbb{E}[(\mathrm{ReLU}(X)-\mathrm{ReLU}(Y))^{2}]\;=\;1-\frac{1}{\pi}\bigl(\sin\theta+(\pi-\theta)\cos\theta\bigr),

where
\theta=\arccos\rho
, which simplifies to the stated form. Since both
z_{i}
and
z_{i}^{\prime}
have variance
1/n
, the physical energy is
(1/n)\cdot f(\rho)
. The ratio cancels the
1/n
factor. For a non-outlier entry with
|\hat{x}_{j}|^{2}=1/n
, the correlation is
\rho_{\mathrm{non}}=1-2/n
.
∎

Leading-order expansion.

Expanding
f(1-2\alpha^{2})
for small
\alpha
:

f(1-2\alpha^{2})=2\alpha^{2}\Bigl(1-\frac{4\alpha}{3\pi}+O(\alpha^{2})\Bigr).

For the non-outlier denominator,
f(1-2/n)=\frac{2}{n}\bigl(1-\frac{4}{3\pi\sqrt{n}}+O(1/n)\bigr)
. In the regime
\alpha\gg 1/\sqrt{n}
(i.e., the outlier amplitude significantly exceeds the isotropic baseline
\alpha_{0}=1/\sqrt{n}
), the
O(1/\sqrt{n})
denominator correction is negligible relative to the
O(\alpha)
numerator correction, giving:

\boxed{\;R(\alpha,n)\;=\;n\alpha^{2}\Bigl(1-\frac{4\alpha}{3\pi}\Bigr)+O(n\alpha^{4})+O(\alpha^{2}/\!\sqrt{n}\,).\;}

(28)

At leading order,
R\approx n\alpha^{2}
. The correction
-4\alpha/(3\pi)
arises from the nonlinear ReLU gate-flip interaction (the pre-ReLU energy ratio is exactly
n\alpha^{2}
with no correction). For
\alpha
near
\alpha_{c}=1/\sqrt{n}
(the crossover where
R\approx 1
), the
O(\alpha^{2}/\sqrt{n})
term contributes
{\sim}1\%
at
n=2048
.

Consistency with Theorem
3
.

Main text Step A6 gives total post-ReLU energy
E(p)=2p(1-4\sqrt{p}/(3\pi)+O(p))
on unit-variance scale. Setting
p=\alpha^{2}
:
E_{\mathrm{outlier}}=2\alpha^{2}(1-4\alpha/(3\pi))
, matching
f(1-2\alpha^{2})
.

Appendix F
Two-Population Outlier Model

This appendix extends the single-entry leverage theory of Appendix
E
to a
population-level
model, deriving column-group leverage formulas relevant to Exp D (Section
9.5
).

F.1
Setup

Partition the
n
input dimensions into
k
outlier and
n-k
non-outlier dimensions:

•

Outlier
:
|\hat{x}_{j}|=\alpha
for
j\in\mathcal{O}
,
|\mathcal{O}|=k

•

Non-outlier
:
|\hat{x}_{j}|=\beta
for
j\notin\mathcal{O}

with unit-norm constraint
k\alpha^{2}+(n-k)\beta^{2}=1
.

Define the key parameters:

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=k/n\quad\text{(outlier column fraction)},

(29)

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=k\alpha^{2}\quad\text{(outlier energy fraction)}.

(30)

For Exp D:
\eta=0.05
(top 5% columns).

F.2
Per-Flip Leverage Under Uniform Flips

From Theorem
6
, flipping a single entry
W_{1,ij}
in column
j
produces post-ReLU energy proportional to
\hat{x}_{j}^{2}
. Define the damage fraction from outlier flips under uniform
p
-flips:

F_{\mathrm{outlier}}=\gamma.

The fraction of total damage from outlier columns
equals
the outlier energy fraction, regardless of the detailed activation structure. Per-flip, however, outlier columns have amplified impact: each outlier flip has leverage
n\alpha^{2}=\gamma/\eta
relative to a delocalized entry.

F.3
Column-Group Leverage

Exp D does not perform uniform
p
-flips; it compares flipping
all
outlier-column weights versus
all
non-outlier-column weights. This is a collective perturbation whose analysis requires the bivariate-Gaussian post-ReLU energy function.

Setup.

For neuron
i
, decompose the pre-activation as
z_{i}=A_{i}+B_{i}
where:

\displaystyle A_{i}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{O}}W_{1,ij}\hat{x}_{j}\sim N(0,\gamma/n),

(31)

\displaystyle B_{i}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum_{j\notin\mathcal{O}}W_{1,ij}\hat{x}_{j}\sim N(0,(1{-}\gamma)/n),

(32)

with
A_{i}\perp B_{i}
(independence by disjoint index sets).

Case A (outlier flip):

Flip all
k
outlier-column weights, negating
A_{i}
. The perturbed pre-activation is
z_{i}^{\prime}=-A_{i}+B_{i}
. The correlation between
z_{i}=A_{i}+B_{i}
and
z_{i}^{\prime}=-A_{i}+B_{i}
is:

\rho_{A}=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(B_{i})-\operatorname{Var}(A_{i})}{\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})}=\frac{(1{-}\gamma)-\gamma}{1}=1-2\gamma.

Case B (non-outlier flip):

Flip all
(n{-}k)
non-outlier-column weights, negating
B_{i}
:

\rho_{B}=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(A_{i})-\operatorname{Var}(B_{i})}{\operatorname{Var}(z_{i})}=2\gamma-1.

Note
\rho_{B}=-\rho_{A}
: the two perturbations are “complementary” in correlation space.

Post-ReLU energy function.

For bivariate Gaussian
(X,Y)
with equal variances
\sigma^{2}
and correlation
\rho
:

f(\rho):=\frac{\mathbb{E}[\|\operatorname{ReLU}(X)-\operatorname{ReLU}(Y)\|^{2}]}{\sigma^{2}}=1-\rho+\frac{1}{\pi}\!\left(\rho\arccos\rho-\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\right).

(33)

This follows from the Price–Cho–Saul formula. Key values:

•

f(1)=0
(perfect correlation: no perturbation)

•

f(0)=1-1/\pi\approx 0.682

•

f(-1)=2
(anti-correlation: maximum perturbation)

Column-group leverage ratio.

The per-flip leverage (accounting for flip-count asymmetry) is:

R_{\mathrm{col}}=\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}\cdot\frac{f(1-2\gamma)}{f(2\gamma-1)}.

(34)

Appendix G
Post-ReLU Ternary Error Derivation

This appendix provides the complete derivation of the post-ReLU radial fraction
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ternary}}_{\mathrm{post}}\approx 0.365
stated in Section
8.4
.

G.1
Pre-Activation Statistics

Let
z_{i}=\sum_{j}W_{1,ij}\hat{x}_{j}
(original) and
z_{i}^{Q}=s_{i}\sum_{j}\operatorname{sign}(W_{1,ij})\hat{x}_{j}
(quantized), where
s_{i}=\|w_{i}\|_{1}/n\to\sqrt{2/(\pi n)}
by SLLN.

Marginal distributions.

z_{i}\sim N(0,\sigma_{1}^{2}),\quad\sigma_{1}^{2}=1/n;\qquad z_{i}^{Q}\text{ has variance }\sigma_{2}^{2}=s_{i}^{2}=2/(\pi n).

Note
\sigma_{2}=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\sigma_{1}\approx 0.798\,\sigma_{1}
: the quantized model produces smaller pre-activations.

Joint distribution.

By the multivariate Lindeberg–Feller CLT under delocalization (A2):

(z_{i},z_{i}^{Q})\xrightarrow{d}N\!\left(0,\begin{pmatrix}1/n&2/(\pi n)\\
2/(\pi n)&2/(\pi n)\end{pmatrix}\right).

The covariance
\operatorname{Cov}(z_{i},z_{i}^{Q})=s_{i}\cdot\mathbb{E}[|W_{1,ij}|]=2/(\pi n)
, giving:

\rho=\frac{2/(\pi n)}{\sqrt{(1/n)\cdot 2/(\pi n)}}=\sqrt{2/\pi}\approx 0.798.

(35)

G.2
Post-ReLU Energy via Price–Cho–Saul

Define
\phi=\arccos(\sqrt{2/\pi})\approx 0.647\,\mathrm{rad}
.

Cross term.

For bivariate normals with unequal variances:

\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})\,\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i}^{Q})]=\frac{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}{2\pi}\!\left(\sin\phi+(\pi-\phi)\cos\phi\right)=\frac{S}{n},

where the auxiliary constant is:

S=\frac{\cos\phi\,(\sin\phi+(\pi-\phi)\cos\phi)}{2\pi}\approx 0.3293.

(36)

Per-neuron error energy.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\delta a_{i}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i}^{Q})^{2}]-2\,\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})\,\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i}^{Q})]

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}}{2}-\frac{2S}{n}=\frac{1}{n}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\pi}-2S\right)\approx\frac{0.1597}{n}.

(37)

G.3
Radial Component

Per-neuron radial contribution.

\mathbb{E}[a_{i}\,\delta a_{i}]=\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})\,\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i}^{Q})]-\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})^{2}]=\frac{S-\tfrac{1}{2}}{n}\approx\frac{-0.1707}{n}.

The negative sign reflects radial
contraction
:
\sigma_{2}<\sigma_{1}
.

Radial projection.

By SLLN,
\|a\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{ReLU}(z_{i})^{2}\to m\cdot\sigma_{1}^{2}/2=m/(2n)
, so
\|a\|\to\sqrt{m/(2n)}
. Thus:

\langle\delta a,\hat{a}\rangle=\frac{\sum_{i}a_{i}\,\delta a_{i}}{\|a\|}\to\frac{m\cdot(S-\tfrac{1}{2})/n}{\sqrt{m/(2n)}}=(S-\tfrac{1}{2})\sqrt{2m/n}.

Although the intermediate quantities depend on
m/n
, the ratio
m/n
cancels in the final radial fraction (Section
G.4
).

G.4
Assembly

The post-ReLU radial fraction:

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ternary}}_{\mathrm{post}}=\frac{\langle\delta a,\hat{a}\rangle^{2}}{\|\delta a\|^{2}}=\frac{2(m/n)(S-\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}}{(m/n)(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{\pi}-2S)}=\frac{2(S-\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}}{\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{\pi}-2S}\approx\frac{0.0583}{0.1597}\approx 0.365.

(38)

The factor
m/n
cancels between numerator and denominator, making the radial fraction independent of the aspect ratio
m/n
.

Compared to the pre-ReLU value
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ternary}}_{\mathrm{pre}}=1-2/\pi\approx 0.363
(exact by Bussgang orthogonality), the difference is
{\sim}0.4\%
relative. The pre-ReLU exactness follows from
\mathbb{E}[\delta z\cdot z^{Q}]=0
: the quantization error is
L^{2}
-orthogonal to the quantized output, a structural consequence of choosing
s_{i}=\mathbb{E}[|W_{ij}|]
as the per-row scale.

G.5
Gate-Flip Analysis

By the Van Vleck arcsine law:

P_{\mathrm{flip}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\arccos\!\left(\sqrt{2/\pi}\right)=\frac{\phi}{\pi}\approx 20.6\%.

This is scale-invariant (depends only on
\rho
, not on
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
individually). Despite this substantial gate-flip rate, the post-ReLU radial fraction remains within
0.4\%
of its pre-ReLU value, indicating that the gate-preserved and gate-flipped contributions to the radial fraction approximately cancel.

Structural conjecture.

Gate-preserved neurons (
{\sim}79.4\%
) contribute a “smooth” radial fraction
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{smooth}}>1-2/\pi
(the perturbation is a magnitude rescaling), while gate-flipped neurons (
{\sim}20.6\%
) contribute
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{flip}}<1-2/\pi
(flips inject purely transverse energy). The weighted average
0.794\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{smooth}}+0.206\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{flip}}\approx 0.365
produces the observed near-equality. Verifying this decomposition requires computing conditional expectations over the four quadrants of
(z_{i},z_{i}^{Q})
and is left as an open question.
