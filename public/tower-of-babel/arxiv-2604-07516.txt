Lifting banal representations of classical groups
Johannes Droschl

Abstract
Let G \mathrm{G} be a symplectic or a split orthogonal group over a local non-archimedean field F \mathrm{F} . A prime ℓ \ell is called banal with respect to G \mathrm{G} if it does not divide the cardinality of the k k -points of G \mathrm{G} , where k k is the residue field of F \mathrm{F} . In this paper we show that for every banal prime ℓ \ell , any smooth irreducible 𝔽 ¯ ℓ {\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}} -representation of G ⁡ ( F ) \mathrm{G}(\mathrm{F}) admits a lift to ℚ ¯ ℓ {\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}} . We also state similar results for more general classical groups of symplectic, orthogonal or unitary type. As an application we prove Howe-duality in the strongly banal case for symplectic-orthogonal or unitary dual pairs.

Introduction

Let
\mathrm{F}
be a local non-archimedean field of characteristic different from
2
with ring of integers
\mathrm{O}
and residue field
k
of cardinality
q
. Let
\ell
be a prime not dividing
q
and
\mathrm{G}
a reductive group over
\mathrm{F}
which we assume in this introduction to be split. The study of representations of
\mathrm{G}(\mathrm{F})
over various fields of coefficients lies at the heart of the automorphic side of the Local Langlands program. Depending on the chosen field and the group
\mathrm{G}
, several classification results have been achieved. For example, if
\mathrm{G}
is a general linear group, the work of Bernstein and Zelevinsky
[
5
]
settles the case of representations with complex coefficients, and the work of Vignéras, see for example
[
30
]
, and Mínguez and Sécherre,
cf
.
[
23
]
, the case of
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}
-coefficients. In the case of classical groups, the theory of Arthur
[
1
]
provides us with a good understanding of the tempered representations with complex coefficients. However, unlike in the case of the general linear group, the behavior of representations of classical groups with coefficients in fields of non-zero characteristic remains largely a mystery.

Taking inspiration from finite groups, the
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}
-representation theory should be reminiscent of the
\mathbb{C}
-representation theory, as long as
\ell
is large in comparison to the size of
\mathrm{G}
. Such
\ell
are called
\emph{banal}
. Over a finite group
W

\ell\nmid\lvert W\lvert
. In this case the
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}
-representations of
W
behave exactly as their complex counterparts. In particular, one can realize every irreducible representation in a family over
{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}
,
i.e.
there exists a
{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}[W]
-stable lattice in any irreducible
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}
-representation, and tensoring this lattice with
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}
yields an irreducible representation. Moreover, any irreducible representation over
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}
can be constructed in this way.

The main goal of this note is to establish a similar result for symplectic, orthogonal, or unitary groups over non-archimedean local fields. We will assume in this introduction that all groups are split, however we will treat in the paper also the non-split case. Note that for general linear groups and their inner forms this has been achieved in
[
25
]
, however our methods are quite different and in fact can also be adapted to give a new proof in the general linear case. To be more precise, we call a smooth irreducible
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}
-representation
\tilde{\pi}
of
\mathrm{G}(\mathrm{F})
a
lift
of an irreducible
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}
-representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{G}(\mathrm{F})
if there exists a
{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}
-lattice
\mathfrak{o}
inside
\tilde{\pi}
, in the sense of
[
30
]
, such that
\mathfrak{o}\otimes_{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}\cong\pi
.

We assume that
G
is unramified over
\mathrm{O}
. In this case we can take
[
10
, Lemma 5.22]
as a definition of a banal prime.

Definition 1
.

A prime

is called banal with respect to
\mathrm{G}
if it does not divide
\lvert\mathrm{G}(k)\rvert
.

The main result of the paper is the following, see

6.1

Theorem 1

Let

be a symplectic or split orthogonal group and

-representation

Moreover, we will also explain how a similar result can be achieved for general linear groups and more general classical groups of symplectic, orthogonal, or unitary type.

The starting point of our explorations will be the following theorem.

(

)
.

From here our work builds on the following four pillars, from which in turn the main result follows easily. The main idea is to construct recursively and explicitly all irreducible representations, both over

Intertwining operators

The theory of intertwining operators as developed in

Assume that the following are satisfied.

Derivatives

We recall the theory of derivatives for classical groups, as their counterpart for general linear groups is slightly less involved. Originally due to
[

Let

In the case of the general linear group, the corresponding theorem is already enough to prove

Representations of Arthur type

We use the following results, which stem from the Arthur classification of tempered representations of

Lemma 3

(
[

, §5.3]
)

Let

Progenerators

To understand representations of the above form, we use
the work of
[
19
]
and
[
28
]

be the intersection of the kernels of all unramified characters of

be a summand of the restriction of

It is possible to understand

We then use the description of

As an application of the above tools we have the following proposition.

Proposition 1

Assume

are two integral irreducible smooth

In the last section, we give a proof of the modular Howe-duality conjecture in the strongly banal case for symplectic-orthogonal and unitary dual pairs. The modular local theta correspondence has been studied and developed in great detail in
[

Thanks to the work of
[
29

it will thus suffice to prove that the reduction mod
\ell

Using the above results, we can achieve this without much effort and hence complete the last missing step in the proof of the modular theta correspondence in the strongly banal case as laid out in
[

]

Section

and

in a compatible way.
For

, we denote by

the unramified character of

obtain the composition of
\nu
with the evaluation map

.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\tilde{\pi}\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(M)
,
\mathfrak{o}
be an integral structure of
\tilde{\pi}
, and write
\pi\coloneqq\mathfrak{o}\otimes_{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}
. Assume that the following is satisfied.

1.

\dim_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi),\mathrm{Ind}_{\overline{P}}^{G}(\pi))=1
.

2.

\Lambda(\tilde{\pi},P,\overline{P})=\Lambda(\pi,P,\overline{P})=0
.

Then

J_{P,\overline{P}}(\tilde{\pi})(\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\mathfrak{o}))\subseteq\mathrm{Ind}_{\overline{P}}^{G}\mathfrak{o}).

Moreover, if for all
1\neq a\in{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}^{\times}
with
a=1\mod\ell
, we have that

\Lambda(\tilde{\pi}\otimes\nu_{\alpha},P,\overline{P})=\alpha(\tilde{\pi}\otimes\nu_{\alpha},P)=0,

then

J_{P,\overline{P}}(\tilde{\pi})(\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\mathfrak{o}))\otimes_{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}=\mathrm{Im}(J_{P,\overline{P}}(\pi)).

Proof.

We start by proving the first inclusion. Indeed, from the Bauer-Nesbitt principle of Vigneras, it follows that there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that the map
I\coloneqq\ell^{k}J_{P,\overline{P}}(\tilde{\pi})
satisfies the inclusion, and we fix a minimal such

. We want to show that

. We thus can reduce

mod
\ell
to obtain a non-zero map

, which implies that up to a scalar

equals to
J_{P,\overline{P}}(\pi)
. Note that by
Lemma

3.2
the latter does not vanish for some

with support in
P\overline{P}
. Similarly, by

3.2
, it follows that the same statement holds for

. Now if

, it would follow that

vanishes on all

with support in

, a contradiction. Thus

and the claim follows.

For the second claim we use the rationality of intertwining operators. It suffices to prove that for

with

,
J_{P,\overline{P}}(\tilde{\pi})(f)=0\mod\ell
implies
J_{P,\overline{P}}(\tilde{\pi})(f)=0
.
Let

be a sufficiently small open compact subgroup of

fixing

.
We therefore find a basis
\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\}
of
\mathrm{Ind}_{P\cap K}^{K}(\tilde{\pi})^{K^{\prime}}
and
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\}
of
\mathrm{Ind}_{\overline{P}\cap K}^{K}(\tilde{\pi})^{K^{\prime}}

such that there exists a
k\times k
matrix
P=(P_{i,j})
with entries in
R[T,T^{-1}]
such that

\mathrm{res}_{K}(J_{P,\overline{P}}(\tilde{\pi}\otimes\nu_{a})(\mathrm{res}_{K}^{-1}(f_{j})))=\sum_{i}P_{i,j}(a)f_{j}.

We can assume without loss of generality that the
h_{i}
’s and
f_{i}
’s have values in
\mathfrak{o}
and hence for every
{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}
-valued unramified character
\nu_{a}
we have that
P_{i,j}(\nu_{a})\in{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}
.
We let

\nu\colon\mathfrak{X}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}[M]\rightarrow{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}[T,T^{-1}]

be the ring map used in the definition of intertwining operators.
By assumption, the matrix
\nu(P)(1)\mod\ell
has non-maximal rank and hence
\det(\nu(P)(1))=0\mod\ell
.
Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}
be the zeros of
\det(\nu(P))
over
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}
. Since
\det(\nu(P)(1))\in{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}
and equals to
0
mod
\ell
, at least one of the

must be integral and reduce to

.
Therefore, there exists an integral character
\nu_{\alpha_{i}}
, which lifts the trivial character over
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}
to
{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}
, such that
\det(P(\nu_{\alpha_{i}}))=0
. But then this implies that
J_{P,\overline{P}}(\tilde{\pi}\otimes\nu_{\alpha_{i}})
is not an
isomorphism, which in turn implies via
Lemma

3.1
and the assumption of the lemma that
\alpha_{i}=1
.
Hence
J_{P,\overline{P}}(\tilde{\pi})(f)=0
as desired.
∎

If we assume that

is a maximal standard subgroup of

, the character
\nu
can be taken to send
(a,b)\in M
to
T^{-\mathrm{val}_{\mathrm{F}}(\det(a))}
. In this case, the condition of the lemma is easy to verify in the following case.
Assume that both
\tilde{\pi}
and
\pi
are irreducible,
\pi
appears in
r_{\overline{P}}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi))
with multiplicity

, and recall that

is banal. Let
a\in{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}
with

and consider the twist

. By

, it follows that

for all

Progenerators

We assume throughout this section that
G
is a classical group and
\ell
is a banal prime with respect to
G
.
Let
P_{\alpha}\subseteq G,\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})
, and
\pi=\rho_{1}\otimes\ldots\rho_{k}\otimes\sigma\in\mathrm{Irr}_{R,c}(M_{\alpha})
where all
\rho_{i}
are
\mathfrak{c}
-self-dual representations of
G_{\alpha_{i}}
and
\sigma
is a cuspidal representation of a classical group.
We let
\Pi\coloneqq\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{X}_{R}[M]\otimes\psi_{un})
.
Let
\mathrm{Rep}_{R,\pi,M}(G)
be the block of
\mathrm{Rep}_{R}(G)
associated to the cuspidal representation
\pi
of
M
.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
28
, 1.6]
)
.

The representation
\Pi
is projective and faithful in
\mathrm{Rep}_{R,\pi,M}(G)
.
Hence
\mathrm{Rep}_{R,\pi,M}(G)
is equivalent to the category of
\mathrm{End}(\Pi)
-modules.

We note that the theorem is stated in
[
28
, 1.6]
only for representations of characteristic
0
. However, the result requires only two ingredients: Firstly, that every cuspidal representation is projective, which is satisfied since
\ell
is banal, and secondly, the second adjointness theorem, which has been established in
[
8
, Corollary 1.3]
in the desired generality.

As a corollary we obtain that any endomorphism of
\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi)
lifts to an endomorphism of
\Pi
. The endomorphism algebra of
\Pi
was described in
[
19
]
. To do this we need to recall some of the notation of
[
19
]
, which he only employs for split symplectic or orthogonal classical groups, but the results we need readily generalizes to our context.
Firstly, let
\mathscr{O}=\{\pi\otimes\chi:\chi\in\mathfrak{X}_{R}[M]\}
and let
W_{\alpha}(\mathscr{O})
be subgroup of
W_{\alpha}
that stabilizes
\mathscr{O}
. For each
w\in W_{\alpha}(\mathscr{O})
one has that
\pi^{w}\cong\pi
, where we denote by
w
the twist by the element
w
. One can then fix canonical isomorphism
\rho_{w}\colon\mathrm{Ind}_{wPw^{-1}}(\pi)\rightarrow\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi)
for all
w\in W_{\alpha}(\mathscr{O})
, which are defined as composition of normalized intertwining operators, see
[
19
, 2.4, 2.5]
.
Using the rational intertwining operators of
[
7
]
and the above
\rho_{w}
, one can then finally describe for each
w\in W_{\mathscr{O}}
an element

A_{w}(\pi)\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{X}_{R}[M]\otimes\psi_{un}),\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi\otimes_{R}\mathcal{K}\otimes\psi_{un})).

After multiplying by a suitable element in
\mathcal{K}
, see
[
19
, 4.6]
, on obtains elements
J_{w}(\pi)
which are defined as compositions of suitable morphisms
A_{w^{\prime}}(\pi)
. We then have elements

J_{w}(\pi)\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{X}_{R}[M]\otimes\psi_{un}),\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi\otimes_{R}\mathcal{K}\otimes\psi_{un}))

for all
w\in W_{\alpha}(\mathscr{O})
.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
19
, Theorem 4.9]
)
.

There is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{K}
-vector spaces

\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{X}_{R}[M]\otimes\psi_{un}),\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi\otimes_{R}\mathcal{K}\otimes\psi_{un}))\cong\bigoplus_{w\in W_{\alpha}(\pi)}\mathcal{K}\cdot J_{w}(\pi).

Let us remark quickly that although
[
19
]
proves the theorem only for complex representations, the proof goes through
muta mutandis
in our banal setting, since both the Geometric Lemma and the construction of the intertwining operators work in exactly the same fashion.

We return now to
\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi))
and define for each element
w\in W_{\alpha}(\mathscr{O})
an intertwining operator
J^{\prime}_{w}(\pi)\colon\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi)\rightarrow\mathrm{Ind}_{wPw^{-1}}^{G}(\pi)\rightarrow\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi)
, where the second map is the isomorphism coming from conjugation by
w
.
As a corollary from
Theorem

4.2
we obtain the following.

Corollary 4.1
.

The set
\{J_{w}(\pi)^{\prime}:w\in W_{\alpha}(\mathscr{O})\}
is an
R
-generating set of
\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi))
.

Proof.

Indeed, by
Theorem

4.1
any map in
\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi))
can be lifted to a map in

\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{X}_{R}[M]\otimes\psi_{un}),\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{X}_{R}[M]\otimes\psi_{un})).

By the explicit description of the latter space in
Theorem

4.2
,
[
19
, Theorem 5.10]
and
[
7
, Proposition 7.8]
the claim follows.
∎

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
P=P_{\alpha}\subseteq G,\,\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
, be a parabolic subgroup of a classical group.
Let
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}\in\mathrm{Irr}_{R,c}(G_{\alpha_{i}})
be
\mathfrak{c}
-self-dual and pairwise non-isomorphic. Let
\sigma\in\mathrm{Irr}_{R,c}(G^{\prime})
, where
G^{\prime}
is the classical component of
M_{\alpha}
. Let

be the number of
\rho_{i}
such that
\rho_{i}\otimes\sigma
is of type II.
Then for all

, we have that

\Pi_{r}\coloneqq\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\pi),\,\pi\coloneqq\overbrace{\rho_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\rho_{1}}^{r_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\overbrace{\rho_{k}\otimes\ldots\otimes\rho_{k}}^{r_{k}}\otimes\sigma

is semi-simple of length
2^{d}
and multiplicity-free.

In particular, if
R={\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}
and we have a lift
\tilde{\pi}
of
\pi
to
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}
, any irreducible summand of
\Pi_{r}
lifts uniquely to a summand of
\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\tilde{\pi})
.

Proof.

Over
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}
the result follows from
[
1
]
, see also
[
4
, §4]
.
Over
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}
, we use the case of
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}
as an input.
Firstly, by
Lemma

3.4
we can assume without loss of generality that the
\rho_{i}
such that
\rho_{i}\otimes\sigma
is of type II are exactly those with
1\leq i\leq d
.
We start by noting that by
Corollary

4.1
,
Lemma

3.5
,
3.1
, and
Lemma

3.3
we have that
\dim_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}\mathrm{End}(\Pi_{r})\leq 2^{d}
. Indeed, denote for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
by
s_{i}
the simple element of
W_{\alpha}
such that
s_{i}
acts on the Levi factor

M_{\alpha}=G_{\alpha_{1}}\times\ldots\times G_{\alpha_{1}}\times G_{\alpha_{2}}\times\ldots\times G_{\alpha_{i}}\times\ldots

trivial everywhere except on the
r_{i}
-th factor of
G_{\alpha_{i}}
, where it acts via
g\mapsto{}^{t}g^{-1}
.

We then have nontrivial morphisms

J_{w}(\pi)^{\prime}\in\mathrm{End}(\Pi_{r}),\,w=s_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}}\ldots s_{d}^{\epsilon_{d}},\,\epsilon_{i}\in\{0,1\}.

By
Corollary

4.1
,
Lemma

3.5
,
3.1
,
Lemma

3.3
, and
[
7
, Proposition 7.8]
, these morphisms are a spanning set of

.
Thus, it follows that
\dim_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}(\Pi_{r})\leq 2^{d}
. Furthermore, if we denote for
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}(G)
by
d_{\pi}
the multiplicity of
\pi
in the socle of
\Pi_{r}
, and hence also by the MVW-involution in the cosocle of
\Pi_{r}
. Thus, we obtain

\sum_{\pi}d_{\pi}^{2}\leq 2^{d}

by considering maps of the form
\Pi_{r}\rightarrow\pi\rightarrow\Pi_{r}
. Note that if
\Pi_{r}
were not semi-simple, then the identity would not be within the span of these morphisms and hence the equality would be strict.

But now we can choose a lift
\tilde{\pi}
of

thanks to
Theorem

2.3
and use the fact that we already proved that
\tilde{\pi}
is semi-simple of the desired length. In particular, we know that the socle of

has length at least

. From this it quickly follows that the socle is multiplicity free and

\Pi_{r}
is semi-simple and multiplicity free of length
2^{d}
.
∎

d_{\rho}(\pi)

If

In this section we show the promised lifting properties. Throughout this section we assume that
\ell
is a banal prime.
We start with the case of the general linear group, which was already proven in
[

]
, however our methods allow us to give a new proof.

Corollary 6.1

(
[
25
, Theorem 6.1]
)
.

Let
\tilde{\pi}\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G_{n})
be an irreducible representation with an integral banal cuspidal support. Then

is irreducible.
If
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}(G_{n})
, then
\pi
admits a lift to
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}
.

We argue by induction on

, the base case being
Theorem

2.3
. Let
{\tilde{\rho}}
be an cuspidal representation in such that

. If

, the case follows from the fact that
\rho\times\ldots\times\rho
is irreducible,
cf.

[
25
]
. Otherwise, we know that
\mathcal{D}_{\rho}(\tilde{\pi})
and

{\tilde{\rho}}
- respectively

-reduced. Indeed, the first observation follows by definition and the second one follows from the first via the fact that Jacquet-functor commutes with reduction mod

up to semi-simplificaition and the fact that cuspidal support is banal. The first part of the theorem follows now from the induction hypothesis,
Lemma

3.7
and
Lemma

3.4
.
Indeed, according to the comment after
Lemma

3.7

one just needs to check that
\alpha((({\tilde{\rho}})^{d_{\tilde{\rho}}}\otimes\nu_{a})\otimes\mathcal{D}_{\rho}(\tilde{\pi}))=0
for

. By the assumption on the cuspidal support and

3.4
the claim follows.
The last part follows from
Lemma

2.7
.
∎

.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
\tilde{\pi}\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G)

Let

is banal.

We finish with the following proposition.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}}(G)
and
\ell
banal with respect to
G
.
If there exists an essentially discrete series lift to
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}
, then any lift to
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}
is an essentially discrete series.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\pi}_{1}
and
\tilde{\pi}_{2}
be two lifts of
\pi
, with
\tilde{\pi}_{1}
an essentially discrete series. We want to show that
\tilde{\pi}
is an essentially discrete series.

If
G
is a general linear group, essentially discrete series representations are classified in
[
5
]
. Namely, let
{\tilde{\rho}}
be a cuspidal representation, and
a\leq b\in\mathbb{Z}
. Set then
\mathrm{L}([a,b]_{\tilde{\rho}})
to be the unique irreducible subrepresentation of
{\tilde{\rho}}\otimes\lvert\det\rvert^{b}\times\ldots\times{\tilde{\rho}}\otimes\lvert\det\rvert^{a}
. Than any essentially discrete series representation is of this form, in particular we assume that
\tilde{\pi}_{1}=\mathrm{L}([a,b]_{\tilde{\rho}})
is of this form.

Since
\ell
is banal, it follows that
\pi
has to be the unique irreducible subrepresentation of
\rho\otimes\lvert\det\rvert^{b}\times\ldots\times\rho\otimes\lvert\det\rvert^{a}
,
\rho=r_{\ell}([{\tilde{\rho}}])
, which we also denote by
\mathrm{L}([a,b]_{\rho})
, see for example
[
23
]
. Moreover,
\mathrm{L}([a,b]_{\tilde{\rho}})
is a lift of
\mathrm{L}([a,b]_{\tilde{\rho}})
. We now prove by induction on the rank of
G
that
\tilde{\pi}_{2}
is of the form
\mathrm{L}([a,b]_{{\tilde{\rho}}^{\prime}})
, where
{\tilde{\rho}}^{\prime}
is a lift of
\rho
.
Indeed, if
\tilde{\pi}_{2}
is any other lift of
\pi
, then there exists
{\tilde{\rho}}^{\prime\prime}
and
\tilde{\pi}^{\prime}
irreducible such that
\tilde{\pi}_{2}\hookrightarrow{\tilde{\rho}}^{\prime\prime}\times\pi^{\prime}
. It follows quickly that
r_{\ell}([{\tilde{\rho}}^{\prime\prime}])=\rho\otimes\lvert\det\rvert^{b}
and
r_{\ell}([\pi^{\prime}])=\mathrm{L}([a,b-1]_{\rho})
. By the induction hypothesis,
\pi^{\prime}\cong\mathrm{L}([a,b-1]_{{\tilde{\rho}}^{\prime}})
for some lift
{\tilde{\rho}}^{\prime}
of
\rho
. Now
\tilde{\pi}_{2}\ncong{\tilde{\rho}}^{\prime\prime}\times\pi^{\prime}
, since otherwise we get via the Geometric Lemma a contradiction to
[
23
, Lemma 7.13]
. Thus it follows by
[
23
, Theorem 7.23]
that
{\tilde{\rho}}^{\prime\prime}\cong{\tilde{\rho}}^{\prime}\otimes\lvert\det\rvert^{\epsilon},\,\epsilon\in\{a-1,b\}
. Again by
[
23
, Lemma 7.13]
and since
\ell
is banal,
\epsilon=b
and hence the claim follows from
[
23
, Proposition 7.16]
.

Next, we come to the case of classical groups. Note that in the general linear case we proved that every lift had banal cuspidal support and we will show the same in the classical case. We argue by induction on the rank of
G
. Let
P_{\alpha}
be a maximal parabolic subgroup and
\Pi
an irreducible constituent of
r_{\alpha}(\tilde{\pi}_{2})
. Then by the above observation we have that
\Pi\cong\mathrm{L}([a,b]_{{\tilde{\rho}}_{2}})\otimes\tilde{\sigma}_{2},
where
{\tilde{\rho}}_{2}
is a cuspidal representation. Let
\tilde{\chi}_{2}
be the central character of
{\tilde{\rho}}_{2}
. We then want to show that
\frac{a+b}{2}+\mathrm{Re}(\tilde{\chi}_{2})>0
. By contradiction, we assume the contrary and it suffices to show the claim where
\Pi
is a quotient of the Jacquet-module. Then by Frobenius reciprocity we have that

\tilde{\pi}_{2}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}([a,b]_{{\tilde{\rho}}_{2}})\rtimes\tilde{\sigma}_{2}.

We set
\sigma\coloneqq r_{\ell}([\tilde{\sigma}_{2}])
.
Arguing as in
[
13
, 3.2]
, we also note that
\sigma
is uniquely determined by
\pi
as the irreducible representation such that

\pi\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}([a,b]_{r_{\ell}([{\tilde{\rho}}_{2}])}))\rtimes\sigma

We can also find
{\tilde{\rho}}_{1},a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}
such that

\mathrm{L}([a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]_{{\tilde{\rho}}_{1}})\rtimes\tilde{\sigma}_{1}
appears as a quotient of
r_{\alpha}(\tilde{\pi}_{1})
, and
\mathrm{L}([a,b]_{{\tilde{\rho}}_{2}})
and
\mathrm{L}([a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]_{{\tilde{\rho}}_{1}})
reduce to the same representation mod
\ell
. By Casselman’s criterion
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}
is an essentially discrete series representation, and by the induction hypothesis it has banal cuspidal support, hence reduces mod
\ell
to an irreducible representation, which by Frobenius reciprocity and the above is isomorphic to
\sigma
.
We will now quickly argue that we cannot have an isomorphism

\tilde{\pi}_{2}\cong\mathrm{L}([a,b]_{{\tilde{\rho}}_{2}})\rtimes\tilde{\sigma}_{2}.

Indeed, it would imply that there exists isomorphism

\tilde{\pi}_{1}\cong\mathrm{L}([a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]_{{\tilde{\rho}}_{1}})\rtimes\tilde{\sigma}_{1},

which would contradict via the Geometric Lemma the discreteness of
\tilde{\pi}_{1}
.

By the induction hypothesis we know that the cuspidal support of
\tilde{\sigma}_{2}
is banal.
Thus
{{\tilde{\rho}}_{2}}\otimes\lvert\det\rvert^{k}
is
\mathfrak{c}
-self-dual for some
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. Indeed, if
k\notin\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, we can apply
Lemma

3.6
, and obtain the above excluded isomorphism.
Next, we will argue that
\frac{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}}{2}+\mathrm{Re}(\tilde{\chi}_{1})=\frac{a+b}{2}+\mathrm{Re}(\tilde{\chi}_{2})
, which will finish the proof. To see this, we not that since
\ell
is banal, we would have otherwise that there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\frac{a+b}{2}+\mathrm{Re}(\tilde{\chi}_{2})=\frac{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}}{2}+\mathrm{Re}(\tilde{\chi}_{1})+\frac{k}{2}\geq d_{W,m(b-a+1)}
and hence we can apply again
Lemma

3.6
, and obtain the excluded isomorphism.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

Note that the above Lemma is no longer true if one replaces
discrete series
with
tempered
.
For example, let
\rho\otimes\sigma
be a cuspidal
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}
-representation of
M_{\alpha}
of the Levi-factor of a maximal parabolic subgroup of a classical group such that
\rho\rtimes\sigma
is irreducible. Then any lift
{\tilde{\rho}}\rtimes\tilde{\sigma}
with
{\tilde{\rho}}

\mathfrak{c}
-self-dual is also tempered, however the lifts

o(q)\lvert k

7
Applications to the modular theta correspondence

In this section we give a straightforward application of the above results to the modular theta correspondence. In the classical setting over
\mathbb{C}
the precise behavior of Theta lifts is at this point well understood, and we refer the reader to
[
18
]
for an excellent exposition to these topics for example. In the modular setting in type II
[
24
]
gives a satisfactory answer in the banal case, which was extended in
[
14
]
to any
\ell
which does not divide
q
. Note that as soon as one leaves the banal setting, classical Howe duality breaks down and several new phenomena appear. However, in the case of dual pairs of type I, not even the banal case has been treated, due to some difficulties involving the composition series of degenerate principal series. We are now able to overcome these issues and prove a modular Howe-duality in the (strongly) banal case in this section.

We recall the setup of the (modular) Theta correspondence as presented in
[
29
]
. Throughout this section we assume that
\mathrm{F}
has characteristic
0
.
Let
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
,
W
be an
\epsilon
-hermitian
\mathrm{E}
-vector space of dimension
n
and
V
an
-\epsilon
-hermitian
\mathrm{E}
-vector space of dimension
m
. If
\mathrm{E}=\mathrm{F}
we also assume that both
n
and
m
are even, and set
\epsilon_{0}
to
\epsilon
if
\mathrm{E}=\mathrm{F}
and to
0
otherwise. The parity condition on
n
and
m
exists only to avoid metaplectic covers, since we do not prove the analogous results for them.
We also assume without loss of generality that
n\leq m+\epsilon_{0}
.
We denote their symmetry groups with
G(W)
and
G(V)
. Moreover, let
\mathbb{W}=W\otimes V
be equipped with its natural symplectic form. Then
G(W)\times G(V)
is a type I dual pair inside
G(\mathbb{W})
. We fix an
{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}
-valued smooth additive character
\psi\colon\mathrm{F}\rightarrow{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}

\ell
by abuse of notation with the same letter.

We denote by

the Weil representation of

R

Denote by

We also set

I(s)\coloneqq\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{n}}^{G(W+W^{-})}(\chi_{V}\lvert\det\lvert^{s}),

Lemma 7.1

We have
\Theta_{V,W+W^{-},\psi,R}(\chi_{W})\hookrightarrow I(s_{m,n})

Proposition 7.1
.

The representation
\Theta_{V,W+W^{-},\psi,{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}}(\chi_{W})

Proof.
