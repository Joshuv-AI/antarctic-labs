IMU Propagation as Preintegration
Jianzhu Huai 1

Abstract
IMU preintegration is widely used in factor-graph-based visual–inertial, lidar–inertial, and radar–inertial state estimation, yet it is often treated as a specialized implementation separate from conventional IMU propagation. This note shows that IMU preintegration and propagation are equivalent realizations of the same underlying computation. We present a convention-agnostic view in which the preintegrated measurement, bias Jacobians, and covariance can be obtained by wrapping an existing IMU propagation routine, while a preintegration module can conversely recover state-transition matrices and propagated covariances. This perspective simplifies the reuse of existing propagation code, supports translation across different error-state definitions, and provides practical consistency checks for preintegration implementations. Experiments with random IMU sequences demonstrate close agreement between an RK4-based propagation implementation and GTSAM’s tangent and manifold preintegration modules in the recovered Jacobians, covariances, and transition matrices.

IMU Propagation, IMU Preintegration, Multisensor Fusion

1
Introduction

IMU preintegration
[
1
]
has become a standard component in factor-graph-based state estimation, including visual–inertial
[
5
]
, lidar–inertial
[
8
]
, and radar–inertial
[
4
]
odometry and mapping systems. Compared with direct IMU propagation, preintegration offers a convenient way to summarize many high-rate IMU measurements between two key states into a single factor that can be reused during iterative optimization. This makes it especially attractive in graph-based estimation, where the same IMU interval may be evaluated many times.

Despite its popularity, IMU preintegration is often perceived as requiring a dedicated implementation that is separate from conventional IMU propagation. In practice, however, many codebases already contain a reliable propagation module, often tied to a particular state or error-state definition. This raises two practical questions. First, does adopting IMU preintegration require reimplementing the IMU model from scratch? Second, how can one validate that a preintegration implementation, especially its bias Jacobians and covariance, is correct?

Classic works on IMU preintegration
[
9
,
3
]
provide elegant derivations. However, these formulations are usually presented as standalone constructions under specific perturbation conventions, which can obscure their relation to the more familiar IMU propagation used in filtering and navigation code. As a result, porting preintegration across different error-state definitions, or reusing an existing propagation implementation, may appear more complicated than it actually is.

This note shows that IMU preintegration and IMU propagation can be viewed as two equivalent realizations of the same underlying computation. We first describe both in a way that is not tied to a particular perturbation convention. We then show that the preintegrated measurement, its Jacobian with respect to the initial IMU bias, and its covariance can all be obtained by wrapping an existing IMU propagation routine. Conversely, a preintegration module can be used to recover state-transition matrices and propagated covariances. This view also clarifies how to adapt preintegration across different error-state definitions without re-deriving bias Jacobians and residual covariances from scratch.

We validate the analysis by converting an RK4-based IMU propagation implementation to and from the GTSAM preintegration modules. In experiments with random IMU sequences, the recovered Jacobians, covariances, and transition matrices closely match those produced by GTSAM’s tangent and manifold preintegration. These results suggest that a robust propagation implementation can serve both as a simple path to preintegration and as a practical reference for validating preintegration code.

2
Background

2.1
Propagation

The system state
\mathbf{x}
typically includes the IMU position
\mathbf{p}_{s}
, velocity
\mathbf{v}_{s}
, and orientation
\mathbf{R}_{ws}
, as well as the gyroscope and accelerometer biases
\mathbf{b}=[\mathbf{b}_{g},\mathbf{b}_{a}]
. In some formulations, the gravity vector
\mathbf{g}
is also included in the state. A perturbation, or error state, is defined by

\mathbf{x}=\widehat{\mathbf{x}}\boxplus^{p}\delta\mathbf{x}^{p},

(1)

where the perturbation may be applied on either the left or the right of the estimate
\widehat{\mathbf{x}}
. The superscript
p
indicates that
\boxplus
is defined for propagation, and will be omitted when no ambiguity arises.

Given 6-axis IMU measurements
\mathbf{z}_{k}=[\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k}^{m},\mathbf{a}_{k}^{m}]
at times
t_{k}
,
k\in[s,e]
, IMU propagation can be summarized as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Prop}:[\mathbf{x}_{s},\ \mathbf{I},\ \Sigma_{s},\ \mathbf{z}_{s:e}]

\displaystyle\rightarrow[\mathbf{x}_{e|s},\ \Phi(t_{e},t_{s}),\ \Sigma_{e|s}],

(2)

\displaystyle\Phi(t_{e},t_{s})

\displaystyle\doteq\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{e|s}\doteq\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{e|s}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{s}},

(3)

where
\mathbf{x}_{e|s}
is the predicted state at time
t_{e}
,
\Phi(t_{e},t_{s})
is the state-transition matrix from
t_{s}
to
t_{e}
, and
\Sigma_{s}
and
\Sigma_{e|s}
are the covariances of the error states
\delta\mathbf{x}_{s}
and
\delta\mathbf{x}_{e|s}
at
t_{s}
and
t_{e}
, respectively. For clarity, we use the subscripts
s
and
e
to denote the start and end times.

The continuous-time system model with control input
\mathbf{u}
and Gaussian noise
\mathbf{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{Q}\delta(t))
is

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{n}),

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\delta\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\,\delta\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{G}(t)\,\mathbf{n}.

(5)

After discretization over
dt=t_{k}-t_{k-1}
, with discrete noise
\mathbf{w}_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{Q}/dt)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{k}

\displaystyle=g(\mathbf{x}_{k-1},\mathbf{u}_{k},\mathbf{w}_{k}),

(6)

\displaystyle\delta\mathbf{x}_{k}

\displaystyle=\Phi(t_{k},t_{k-1})\,\delta\mathbf{x}_{k-1}+\mathbf{B}_{k}\,\mathbf{w}_{k},

(7)

where

\Phi(t_{k},t_{k-1})=\exp\!\big(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\,dt\big)\approx\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{F}dt+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{F}^{2}dt^{2}.

The full transition matrix is

\Phi(t_{e},t_{s})=\Phi(t_{e},t_{e-1})\cdots\Phi(t_{s+1},t_{s}).

State propagation integrates (
4
), for example by using a fourth-order Runge–Kutta (RK4) method. The covariance is propagated as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k}

\displaystyle=\Phi(t_{k},t_{k-1})\,\Sigma_{k-1}\,\Phi^{\top}(t_{k},t_{k-1})

(8)

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\Phi(t_{k},\tau)\,\mathbf{G}(\tau)\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{G}^{\top}(\tau)\,\Phi^{\top}(t_{k},\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle\approx\Phi(t_{k},t_{k-1})\,\Sigma_{k-1}\,\Phi^{\top}(t_{k},t_{k-1})

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}\Big[\Phi(t_{k},t_{k-1})\,\mathbf{G}(t_{k-1})\,\mathbf{Q}\,\mathbf{G}^{\top}(t_{k-1})\,\Phi^{\top}(t_{k},t_{k-1})

\displaystyle+\mathbf{G}(t_{k})\,\mathbf{Q}\,\mathbf{G}^{\top}(t_{k})\Big].

2.2
Preintegration

The preintegrated inertial measurement, defined independently of the initial navigation state, is

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{R}

\displaystyle\doteq\Delta\mathbf{R}_{se}\doteq\prod_{k=s+1}^{e}\mathrm{Exp}\!\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\boldsymbol{\omega}(t)\,dt\right),

(9)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{p}

\displaystyle\doteq\Delta\mathbf{p}^{s}_{e}\doteq\int_{t_{s}}^{t_{e}}\int_{t_{s}}^{\tau}\mathbf{R}^{s}_{t}\,\mathbf{a}(t)\,dt\,d\tau,

(10)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle\doteq\Delta\mathbf{v}^{s}_{e}\doteq\int_{t_{s}}^{t_{e}}\mathbf{R}^{s}_{t}\,\mathbf{a}(t)\,dt,

(11)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{b}

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{s}}^{t_{e}}n_{b}dt,

(12)

\displaystyle\Delta t

\displaystyle=t_{e}-t_{s}.

(13)

Here,
\mathbf{a}
and
\boldsymbol{\omega}
denote the true IMU specific force and angular velocity, free of IMU imperfections:

\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{a}_{m}-\mathbf{b}_{a}-\mathbf{n}_{a},\qquad\boldsymbol{\omega}=\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}-\mathbf{b}_{g}-\mathbf{n}_{g}.

The operator
\mathrm{Exp}(\cdot)
maps a rotation vector to a rotation matrix via

\mathrm{Exp}(\boldsymbol{\phi})=\exp([\boldsymbol{\phi}]_{\times})
, and

\mathrm{Log}(\cdot)
denotes its inverse.

We also define the error state
\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}
of the preintegrated increment
\Delta\mathbf{x}
by

\Delta\mathbf{x}=\Delta\widehat{\mathbf{x}}\boxplus^{g}\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{15},

(14)

where the superscript
g
indicates that
\boxplus
is defined for preintegration and is omitted when clear from context.

The preintegration operation can be summarized as

\mathrm{Preint}:\ [\mathbf{b}_{s},\mathbf{z}_{s:e},\mathbf{q}]\ \rightarrow\ [\Delta\mathbf{x}_{se},\ \mathbf{J}_{b_{s}},\ \Sigma_{\Delta\mathbf{x}}],

(15)

where

\mathbf{J}_{b_{s}}=\frac{\partial\,\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}}{\partial\,\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}}\in\mathbb{R}^{15\times 6},\qquad\Sigma_{\Delta\mathbf{x}}=\mathrm{cov}(\delta\mathbf{x}_{se})\in\mathbb{R}^{15\times 15},

(16)

and
\mathbf{q}
denotes the IMU noise parameters,
e.g
.
, measurement noise densities and bias random-walk densities. Let
\mathbf{J}_{b_{s}}^{n}
denote the first nine rows of
\mathbf{J}_{b_{s}}
. The last six rows, corresponding to the propagated biases, are trivially zero since the bias change over
\Delta t
is unrelated to the bias error at
t_{s}
. The covariance
\Sigma_{\Delta\mathbf{x}}
captures the uncertainty accumulated over the interval
[t_{s},t_{e}]
due to gyroscope and accelerometer noise.

Using these increments, the predicted IMU state is obtained with
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{s},\Delta\mathbf{x}_{se}(\mathbf{b}_{s}))
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{w,e|s}

\displaystyle\doteq\mathbf{R}_{ws}\,\Delta\mathbf{R},

(17)

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{e|s}

\displaystyle\doteq\mathbf{p}_{s}+\mathbf{v}_{s}\,\Delta t+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{g}\,\Delta t^{2}+\mathbf{R}_{ws}\,\Delta\mathbf{p},

(18)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{e|s}

\displaystyle\doteq\mathbf{v}_{s}+\mathbf{R}_{ws}\,\Delta\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{g}\,\Delta t,

(19)

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{e|s}

\displaystyle\doteq\mathbf{b}_{s}+\Delta\mathbf{b}_{se},

(20)

where
\mathbf{g}
is the gravity in the world frame.
These equations form the bridge between preintegration and propagation.

3
Preintegration from and to Propagation

3.1
Preintegration by Propagation

Suppose that an IMU propagation module is already available and we would like to reuse it to implement the preintegrated measurement.
First,
\mathbf{J}_{b_{s}}
can be obtained as

\mathbf{J}_{b_{s}}=\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{g}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}^{g}}=\left(\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{e|s}^{p}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{g}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{e|s}^{p}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}^{p}}\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}^{p}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}^{g}},

(21)

where
\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{e|s}^{p}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{g}}
follows from the definitions of
\delta\mathbf{x}_{e|s}^{p}
,
\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{g}
, and the prediction equations (
17
)–(
20
), and

\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}^{p}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}^{g}}
follows from the bias perturbation definitions.

Second, the covariance of the preintegrated measurement is obtained by propagation starting with zero covariance:

\Sigma_{\Delta\mathbf{x}}=\left(\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{e|s}^{p}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{g}}\right)^{-1}\mathrm{cov}(\delta\mathbf{x}_{e|s}^{p})\left(\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{e|s}^{p}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{g}}\right)^{-T}.

(22)

These relations show that IMU preintegration can be implemented directly on top of IMU propagation.
The propagation error state
\delta\mathbf{x}_{n,e|s}
need not coincide with the one used by the optimizer. More broadly, improvements in preintegration methods can also benefit propagation; for example, the closed-form preintegration of
[
2
]
can be used to improve the integration of (
4
) in propagation.

3.2
Propagation by Preintegration

The reverse direction is also possible: state propagation can be realized using preintegration.
First, the state value propagation follows directly from
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{s}^{p},\Delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{g}(\mathbf{b}_{s}^{p}))
(
17
)–(
20
).
Second, to obtain the transition matrix, we
take differentials of
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{s}^{p},\Delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{g}(\mathbf{b}_{s}^{p}))
yielding

\begin{split}\delta\mathbf{x}_{e|s}^{p}&=\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{e|s}\delta\mathbf{x}_{s}^{p}+\mathbf{G}_{e|s}\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{g},\\
\mathbf{G}_{e|s}&=\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{f}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{g}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{G}_{e|s}^{nn}&\mathbf{0}_{9\times 6}\\
\mathbf{0}_{6\times 9}&\frac{\partial\mathbf{f}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}^{g}}\end{bmatrix},\\
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{e|s}&=\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{f}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{s}^{p}}+\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{G}_{e|s}\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{g}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}^{g}}\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}^{g}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}^{p}}\end{bmatrix}\\
&=\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{f}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{s}^{p}}+\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{G}_{e|s}^{nn}\mathbf{J}_{b_{s}}^{n}\mathbf{P}_{b}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}_{6\times 6}\end{bmatrix},\end{split}

(23)

where

\mathbf{P}_{b}=\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}^{g}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}^{p}},\quad\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{g}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}^{g}}=\mathbf{J}_{b_{s}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{J}_{b_{s}}^{n}\\
\mathbf{0}_{6\times 6}\end{bmatrix}.

Third, with the above derivatives
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{e|s}
and
\mathbf{G}_{e|s}
, the preint measurement covariance
\Sigma_{\Delta\mathbf{x}}
and the covariance of the initial state
\Sigma_{s}
, the propagated covariance is obtained by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{e|s}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{e|s}\Sigma_{s}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{e|s}^{\top}+\mathbf{G}_{e|s}\Sigma_{\Delta\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{G}_{e|s}^{\top}.

(24)

3.3
Preintegration Factors in Optimization

The preint measurement’s primary purpose is to build a residual in nonlinear optimization.
A typical residual as realized by
PreintegrationBase::computeError
in
GTSAM
, is defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Log}\left(\mathbf{R}_{we}^{\top}\mathbf{R}_{w,e|s}\right),

(25)

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{p}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{R}_{we}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{p}_{e|s}-\mathbf{p}_{e}\big),

(26)

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{v}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{R}_{we}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{v}_{e|s}-\mathbf{v}_{e}\big),

(27)

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{b}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{b}_{e|s}-\mathbf{b}_{e}.

(28)

Alternative residual definitions are also possible.
An often desirable synergy between the residual and preint perturbation is that the residual Jacobian relative to the perturbation is close to the identity matrix (see (
56
) for example).

To build a factor in graph-based optimization, we need the residual Jacobians and covariance.
First, we observe that the Jacobians of the residuals with respect to position, velocity, and orientation are independent of the specific preintegration formulas in (
9
)–(
13
).
Therefore, changing the error-state definition only requires re-deriving these Jacobians from the residual definitions,
e.g
.
(
25
)–(
28
).
Second, the Jacobians of the residuals with respect to the initial bias can be obtained through preintegration:

\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}}\frac{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{s}}.

(29)

The covariance of the residual is obtained by the chain rule:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{r}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}}\ \mathrm{cov}(\delta\mathbf{x}_{se})\ \left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}}\right)^{\top}.

(30)

If the residual perturbation is defined such that

\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}}\approx\mathbf{I}
,
then the residual covariance is approximately the preintegrated measurement covariance.

To avoid recomputing preintegration whenever the bias estimate changes during optimization, the residuals are often linearized at a nominal bias
\widehat{\mathbf{b}}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{r}_{\theta}(\widehat{\mathbf{b}})+\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}_{\theta}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}_{g}}\,\delta\mathbf{b}_{g},

(31)

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{p}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{r}_{p}(\widehat{\mathbf{b}})+\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}_{p}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}}\,\delta\mathbf{b},

(32)

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{v}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{r}_{v}(\widehat{\mathbf{b}})+\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}_{v}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{b}}\,\delta\mathbf{b}.

(33)

3.4
Implementation

The above derivations are generic with respect to the error-state definitions of

\delta\mathbf{x}_{s}
,
\delta\mathbf{x}_{e}
,
\delta\mathbf{x}_{e|s}
, and
\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}
.
In the following validation of the equivalence between preintegration and propagation, we use the left error states
[
6
]
for

\delta\mathbf{x}_{s}^{p}
,
\delta\mathbf{x}_{e}^{p}
, and

The left error state is defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{p}}+\delta\mathbf{p},\quad\mathbf{R}=\mathrm{Exp}(\delta\boldsymbol{\theta})\,\widehat{\mathbf{R}},

(34)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{v}}+\delta\mathbf{v},\quad\mathbf{b}_{g}=\widehat{\mathbf{b}}_{g}+\delta\mathbf{b}_{g},

(35)

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{a}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{b}}_{a}+\delta\mathbf{b}_{a}.

(36)

The right error state as realized in

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{R}}\mathrm{Exp}(\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}),\quad\mathbf{p}=\widehat{\mathbf{p}}+\mathbf{R}\delta\mathbf{p},

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{v}}+\mathbf{R}\delta\mathbf{v},\quad\mathbf{b}_{a}=\widehat{\mathbf{b}}_{a}+\delta\mathbf{b}_{a},

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{g}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{b}}_{g}+\delta\mathbf{b}_{g}.

The covariance of tangent preintegration also follows this perturbation definition.
As a result of (

)-(

\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{j}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{J}_{r}^{-1}(\mathbf{r}_{\theta})&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{M}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\\

In contrast, the covariance of manifold preintegration adopts a right perturbation
\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{c}

\begin{split}\Delta\mathbf{R}_{se}&=\Delta\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{se}\,\mathrm{Exp}(\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}^{c}_{se}),\\

\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{c}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{J}_{r}^{-1}(\mathbf{r}_{\theta})&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\\

\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&-\mathbf{I}_{3}\end{bmatrix},

(47)

The preint residual in

is defined differently from that in

]
. Forster

define the residual as

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Log}\left(\mathbf{R}_{w,e|s}^{\top}\mathbf{R}_{we}\right),

(48)

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{p}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{R}_{ws}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{p}_{e}-\mathbf{p}_{e|s}\big),

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{R}_{se}

\displaystyle=\Delta\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{se}\mathrm{Exp}(-\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}^{f}_{se}),

(52)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{p}^{s}_{e}

\displaystyle=\Delta\widehat{\mathbf{p}}^{s}_{e}-\delta\mathbf{p}^{f}_{se},

(53)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{v}^{s}_{e}

\displaystyle=\Delta\widehat{\mathbf{v}}^{s}_{e}-\delta\mathbf{v}^{f}_{se},

(54)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{b}_{se}

\displaystyle=-\delta\mathbf{b}.

(55)

The above residual and preint perturbation lead to a residual Jacobian close to the identity matrix,

\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\delta\mathbf{x}_{se}^{f}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{J}_{l}^{-1}(\mathbf{r}_{\theta})&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\\

\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{I}_{3}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{I}_{3}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{I}_{3}\end{bmatrix},

(56)

that is, Forster’s preint measurement covariance is nearly identical to the residual covariance.

IMU Propagation in Detail.

Details of IMU propagation can be found in
[
7
]
. For the simple case without Earth rotation or gravity deflection, which is typical for consumer-grade IMUs, the continuous-time state model is

\frac{d}{dt}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{p}\\
\mathbf{R}\\
\mathbf{v}\\
\mathbf{b}_{g}\\
\mathbf{b}_{a}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{v}\\

|r-e|,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
