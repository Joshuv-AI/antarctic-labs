Vutrisiran in transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy: a structured review of the HELIOS-B trial and its secondary analyses
Authors: Ahsanullah Niazai, Bakht Muhammad Zurmati, Syed Tayyab Shah, Gulmadin Hikmat, Hafiz Umar Sohaib, Hafiza Hooria Zainab, Mohammad Jawad Khan Faridzai, Mah Rukh Nisar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTransthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) is a progressive infiltrative cardiomyopathy caused by myocardial deposition of misfolded transthyretin, leading to heart failure, conduction abnormalities, and functional decline. Recent therapeutic advances have shifted management toward disease-modifying strategies that target transthyretin production and stability. Vutrisiran, a subcutaneously administered small interfering RNA therapy, suppresses hepatic transthyretin synthesis and has emerged as a potential treatment option for ATTR-CM.MethodsThis structured narrative review was prospectively registered in PROSPERO (CRD420261307493) and aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of vutrisiran in patients with ATTR-CM. A systematic literature search was conducted on 12 February 2026 across PubMed, ScienceDirect, Google Scholar, PubMed Central, and ClinicalTrials.gov. Study selection and screening were performed independently by two reviewers in accordance with PRISMA guidelines, with disagreements resolved by consensus. The review included one primary randomized controlled trial and associated secondary and post hoc analyses. Risk of bias was assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool.ResultsVutrisiran reduced all-cause mortality (HR 0.65–0.72), cardiovascular events (HR 0.67–0.72), and recurrent events (RR 0.68–0.73) over a median follow-up of ~ 33 months (up to ~ 36 months). It improved functional capacity (6MWD + 26.5–32.1 m; KCCQ-OS + 5.8–8.7) and NYHA class stability/improvement (66–68% vs. 56–61%). Cardiac remodeling improved with reductions in LV mass (− 10.6 g/m²), LV wall thickness (− 0.4 mm), and improvements in LVEF (+ 1.6–2%) and GLS (+ 0.7–1.2%). Biomarkers were reduced (NT-proBNP and troponin I ratio 0.68). Benefits were greater in earlier disease stages and included fewer HF events (HR 0.73) and reduced days lost to death/hospitalization (− 32 to − 64). Safety remained favorable with low discontinuation (3.5%) and no new organ toxicity signals.ConclusionVutrisiran represents a disease-modifying therapy for transthyretin amyloidosis with cardiomyopathy, demonstrating consistent biochemical and clinical benefits alongside a generally favorable safety profile. These findings support its emerging role within the therapeutic landscape and underscore the potential of transthyretin-silencing strategies to improve patient outcomes. Nevertheless, further long-term follow-up and additional trials and real-world studies are needed to better define the durability of benefit and its optimal positioning within evolving treatment algorithms.

Introduction

Transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) is a progressive infiltrative heart disease caused by the misfolding and extracellular deposition of transthyretin protein within the myocardium, leading to restrictive cardiomyopathy, heart failure, conduction abnormalities, and functional decline [1]. Both hereditary and wild-type forms contribute substantially to morbidity and mortality, particularly in older adults [2]. Until recently, management relied largely on supportive care and conventional heart failure therapies, which often show limited efficacy and poor tolerability in this population [3, 4]. Advances in disease-modifying therapies have shifted treatment strategies toward targeting transthyretin production and stability [5].

The introduction of transthyretin stabilizers marked the first major therapeutic advance, with tafamidis shown to reduce mortality and cardiovascular hospitalizations by preventing transthyretin tetramer dissociation [6]. More recently, additional stabilizing agents such as acoramidis have demonstrated clinical benefit by enhancing transthyretin stability and slowing disease progression (Philip Gillmore et al., 2024) [7]. Gene-silencing therapies represent a further evolution in treatment by directly reducing transthyretin production [8]. Patisiran, another RNA interference therapy, showed improvement in functional capacity and quality-of-life outcomes in patients with ATTR-CM, supporting the concept of transthyretin suppression as a disease-modifying strategy (Scott Solomon et al., 2023) [9]. Vutrisiran builds on this therapeutic class with a subcutaneous formulation designed for sustained transthyretin reduction, offering a targeted approach to limiting amyloid deposition and cardiac dysfunction [10]. Vutrisiran is a small interfering RNA (siRNA) therapeutic that suppresses hepatic transthyretin synthesis, thereby reducing the circulating precursor responsible for amyloid formation [11]. Clinical evaluation of vutrisiran in ATTR-CM has been established through the HELIOS-B trial, which demonstrated meaningful reductions in transthyretin levels and improvements in cardiovascular outcomes [12–14].

Despite growing therapeutic options, evidence regarding vutrisiran in ATTR-CM remains distributed across primary trial reports, secondary analyses, and emerging clinical data [15–19]. Although the HELIOS-B trial and its multiple secondary analyses have individually demonstrated promising efficacy and safety outcomes, the evidence remains dispersed across several publications focusing on different clinical endpoints, subgroup analyses, and exploratory outcomes. To date, there is no single comprehensive synthesis integrating findings from the primary HELIOS-B trial together with its subsequent secondary reports. A consolidated analysis is therefore needed to systematically evaluate the overall consistency and magnitude of vutrisiran’s clinical benefits, assess safety outcomes across publications, and provide a clearer understanding of its therapeutic role in transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM). Such an approach would help clinicians and researchers interpret the expanding body of evidence more cohesively, identify remaining knowledge gaps, and support evidence-based incorporation of transthyretin-silencing therapies into contemporary cardiac amyloidosis management.

Method

This structured narrative review was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines, ensuring a transparent and standardized approach to literature identification, screening, data extraction, and synthesis. Adhering to PRISMA 2020 allowed us to systematically report the search strategy, study selection process, and critical appraisal, thereby enhancing the reproducibility and reliability of our findings [20].

Prospero registration

This systematic review has been registered in PROSPERO with the registration number CRD420261307493. The review aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of vutrisiran in transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM).

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria

Studies were considered eligible if they include patients with confirmed transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM), including both hereditary and wild type forms. Diagnosis was established through accepted clinical methods such as imaging (e.g., echocardiography, cardiac MRI, or nuclear scintigraphy), biopsy, or genetic testing. The intervention of interest is vutrisiran, a small interfering RNA (siRNA) therapy targeting transthyretin. Studies that compared vutrisiran with placebo, standard care, or other transthyretin-targeted therapies were included. Eligible studies must report at least one of the primary outcomes: all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, or heart failure–related hospitalization. Secondary outcomes considered include changes in NT-proBNP levels, echocardiographic parameters (such as left ventricular wall thickness, diastolic function, or global longitudinal strain), functional capacity measured by the 6-minute walk test, quality of life scores, and adverse events or serious adverse events. All study designs including randomized controlled trials (RCTs), non-randomized clinical trials, prospective and retrospective cohort studies, and post-hoc analyses of clinical trials were considered. Only studies published in English were included. No restriction was applied for the year of publication.

Exclusion criteria

Studies were excluded if they focus on patients without cardiac involvement of transthyretin amyloidosis, preclinical or animal studies, or pediatric populations. Additionally, case reports, narrative reviews, editorials, and studies not reporting relevant clinical outcomes were excluded. Studies that do not provide sufficient information on intervention or comparator details, or those lacking relevant follow-up data for primary or secondary outcomes, were also excluded.

Search strategy

A literature search was conducted on 12 February 2026 across PubMed, Science Direct, Google Scholar, PubMed Central, and ClinicalTrials.gov to identify studies on vutrisiran in transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM). Literature search strategy

Data collection and screening

A comprehensive systematic search was conducted in accordance with PRISMA reporting guidelines across multiple electronic databases, including PubMed, Google Scholar, ScienceDirect, PubMed Central (PMC), and ClinicalTrials.gov. Two reviewers independently performed the literature search, study screening, and eligibility assessment, with disagreements resolved through discussion and consensus. During the systematic literature search, no additional primary clinical trials evaluating vutrisiran in ATTR-CM were identified apart from the HELIOS-B trial; therefore, the review included the primary HELIOS-B study along with its available post hoc and secondary analyses to comprehensively evaluate the existing evidence.

Risk of bias assessment

Risk of bias for the HELIOS-B trial was assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) tool for randomized controlled trials. Overall, the study was judged to have a low risk of bias across most domains. The trial employed a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design with centralized allocation procedures, minimizing the risk of bias arising from the randomization process. Blinding of participants, investigators, and outcome assessors was maintained throughout the study, reducing the likelihood of performance and detection bias. Outcome data were largely complete, with appropriate handling of missing data using prespecified statistical methods, suggesting a low risk of attrition bias. Prespecified outcomes were reported consistently with the study protocol, indicating a low risk of selective reporting. Although, as with many large multicenter trials, minor concerns may arise regarding potential heterogeneity in background therapy and exploratory post-hoc analyses, these did not materially affect the primary outcome assessment. Therefore, the overall risk of bias for HELIOS-B was considered low, supporting the internal validity and reliability of its findings.

Results

A comprehensive literature search was conducted across four major databases, PubMed, PubMed Central (PMC), Google Scholar, and ScienceDirect, as well as the clinical trial registry ClinicalTrials.gov. The search initially identified 149 records. After screening titles, abstracts, and full texts, and excluding non-scientific sources such as news reports, documentaries, and registry summaries, a total of ten reports were included consisting of one primary clinical trial and its secondary and post-hoc analyses. Patient characteristics (HELIOS-B trial)

The HELIOS-B trial enrolled patients with confirmed transthyretin amyloidosis with cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM), including both wild-type and variant disease. The study population largely reflected the typical epidemiologic profile of ATTR-CM, consisting predominantly of older adults with a strong male predominance. Most participants had symptomatic heart failure at baseline, with the majority classified as New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class II or III. A substantial proportion of patients were receiving background disease-modifying therapy, particularly tafamidis, reflecting contemporary real-world management patterns. Baseline cardiac functional capacity and biomarker levels indicated clinically significant disease burden, with reduced exercise tolerance and elevated cardiac stress markers. Overall, the enrolled cohort represented a moderately symptomatic ATTR-CM population with established structural and functional cardiac involvement, making it appropriate for evaluating disease-modifying therapeutic effects shown in Table 2.

Study characteristics of HELIOS-B reports

A total of ten articles from the HELIOS-B clinical trial program were included. These comprised the one primary randomized controlled trial, and nine reports including prespecified clinical outcome analyses, secondary mechanistic and imaging studies, patient-reported outcome analyses, and long-term extension data. Collectively, these studies provide comprehensive evidence on mortality, cardiovascular events, cardiac remodeling, functional capacity, quality of life, and durability of treatment response. The randomized phase of HELIOS-B included a comparable follow-up duration of approximately 30–36 months for both the vutrisiran and placebo groups, with additional 12-month data available from open-label extension analyses. Cardiac outcomes of HELIOS-B reports

In the HELIOS-B trial, vutrisiran demonstrated reductions in mortality and cardiovascular morbidity, preservation of functional capacity and quality of life, stabilization of biomarkers, and attenuation of adverse cardiac remodeling. Subgroup and post-hoc analyses, highlighted clinically meaningful benefits across diverse endpoints and patient populations. Clinically significant improvements were observed in hard cardiovascular endpoints including mortality and heart failure hospitalization, supported by favorable trends in functional capacity and quality-of-life measures. Additionally, imaging and biomarker analyses demonstrated consistent patterns of cardiac remodeling, suggesting a disease-modifying effect, although several exploratory analyses were not powered for definitive statistical inference. Discussion

Transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) is a progressive infiltrative disorder characterized by myocardial deposition of misfolded transthyretin fibrils, leading to restrictive cardiomyopathy, heart failure progression, and premature mortality. Therapeutic strategies targeting hepatic transthyretin production aim to modify disease progression at a mechanistic level. Vutrisiran, a subcutaneously administered RNA interference therapeutic, reduces circulating transthyretin levels and represents an important disease-modifying intervention in ATTR-CM. The clinical development framework and therapeutic rationale for vutrisiran in cardiomyopathy were established in the HELIOS-B trial design [25].

Cardiac efficacy across clinical endpoints

Across multiple reports from the HELIOS-B trial, vutrisiran demonstrates a coherent and clinically meaningful cardiovascular benefit that extends beyond isolated endpoints. The most compelling evidence relates to hard outcomes, where treatment was associated with significant reductions in all-cause mortality and major cardiovascular events compared with placebo, supporting its role as a disease-modifying therapy in transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (Witteles 2025) [22]. This survival advantage is reinforced by additional analyses demonstrating consistent reductions in cardiovascular mortality and composite cardiovascular outcomes across prespecified definitions, further strengthening the robustness of these findings (Witteles 2026) [26]. Collectively, these results suggest that sustained transthyretin suppression translates into meaningful improvements in disease trajectory.

Beyond mortality and major events, vutrisiran also appears to meaningfully reduce the burden of heart failure–related morbidity. Treated patients experienced fewer cumulative days lost due to hospitalization or death, reflecting improved clinical stability and a reduction in recurrent decompensation events that are a hallmark of ATTR-CM progression (Yilmaz 2025) [24]. This reduction in hospitalization burden is particularly relevant given its strong association with both reduced quality of life and increased healthcare utilization in this population.

Improvements are also observed in functional and patient-centered outcomes, providing an important dimension to its clinical impact. Vutrisiran treatment was associated with preservation of exercise capacity and attenuation of functional decline, alongside favorable changes in patient-reported health status measures, suggesting that clinical benefits extend into daily functioning and symptom burden (Sheikh 2025; Siddiqi 2025) [27, 28]. These findings are complemented by biomarker analyses demonstrating reductions in markers of myocardial stress and injury, which provide biological support for reduced cardiac disease activity (Maurer 2025) [19]. Imaging-based assessments further suggest favorable changes in cardiac structure and function, raising the possibility that vutrisiran may influence underlying disease progression rather than solely modifying symptoms (Schwaerzer 2025) [29].

Importantly, the observed therapeutic effects appear broadly consistent across clinically relevant subgroups, including patients with varying baseline heart failure severity and those with significant comorbidities, such as advanced chronic kidney disease (Maurer 2025; Sheikh 2025) [15, 27]. This consistency supports the generalizability of benefit across the heterogeneous ATTR-CM population.

Taken together, the HELIOS-B evidence positions vutrisiran as a disease-modifying therapy with multidimensional benefits spanning survival, cardiovascular morbidity, functional capacity, quality of life, and biomarkers of cardiac injury. The convergence of these effects suggests that transthyretin suppression may alter the clinical course of ATTR-CM in a sustained and clinically meaningful manner, particularly when initiated earlier in disease progression.

Cardiac biomarker analysis in HELIOS-B

Cardiac biomarker analyses in HELIOS-B focused primarily on NT-proBNP and high-sensitivity troponin as markers of myocardial stress and injury in ATTR-CM. Vutrisiran was associated with a more favorable biomarker trajectory compared with placebo, demonstrated by attenuation of NT-proBNP rise and relative stabilization of troponin levels. These findings were consistent across key clinical subgroups, supporting robustness of the observed effect [6, 19].

In the broader context, Albulushi et al. highlighted that NT-proBNP and cardiac troponins are the most validated biomarkers in cardiac amyloidosis, providing strong diagnostic and prognostic utility [6]. NT-proBNP reflects myocardial wall stress, while troponins indicate ongoing myocardial injury, together enabling risk stratification and disease monitoring [30].

Background use of tafamidis in HELIOS-B

Tafamidis, a transthyretin (TTR) stabilizer and current standard-of-care therapy for transthyretin amyloidosis with cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) [6], was permitted as background therapy in the HELIOS-B trial. As a result, a proportion of patients in both the vutrisiran and placebo groups were receiving tafamidis at baseline [21]. This reflects contemporary real-world clinical practice, where combination or sequential use of TTR-targeted therapies may occur. Importantly, subgroup analyses were conducted to evaluate whether the efficacy of vutrisiran differed according to baseline tafamidis use. Safety and adverse events

Safety was favorable across HELIOS-B and it’s pooled analyses. In the pivotal trial, Fontana, 2025 (NEJM) reported adverse event rates were similar between arms (99% vs. 98%), with serious adverse events slightly lower in the vutrisiran group (62% vs. 67%) [21]. Pooled safety analyses by Witteles, 2025 (JACC Advances) confirmed tolerability: TEAEs occurred in 88% of patients, severe AEs in 36.9%, and discontinuations in only 3.5% [22]. Injection-site reactions were infrequent and mild, and no ocular, hepatic, or renal safety concerns were identified [22]. Other system-specific safety outcomes also showed no clinically significant differences between groups. Peripheral edema occurred at similar rates, and renal safety remained stable, with comparable proportions of patients experiencing clinically relevant declines in kidney function, including among those with chronic kidney disease [22, 26]. Hepatic safety was likewise reassuring, with rare and reversible transaminase elevations occurring at similar frequencies in both treatment arms [22].

Safety was consistent across subgroups (NYHA, NAC stage, tafamidis use) and long-term follow-up, reinforcing robustness of the tolerability profile [15, 17–19, 21, 23]. Open-label extension results by Garcia-Pavia, 2025 (EHJ Suppl) further confirmed long-term safety, with no new signals emerging at 12 months [11].

Comparison of vutrisiran with other disease-modifying therapies in ATTR-CM

Transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) is managed using transthyretin stabilizers and gene-silencing therapies with distinct mechanisms and evidence bases. Tafamidis is a selective TTR stabilizer that binds to the thyroxine-binding sites of the TTR tetramer, preventing dissociation into amyloidogenic monomers [31], and has demonstrated reductions in mortality and cardiovascular hospitalizations in the ATTR-ACT trial, remaining the current first-line therapy for ATTR-CM (Maurer et al., 2018) [6]. In contrast, patisiran and vutrisiran are small interfering RNA (siRNA) therapies that reduce hepatic transthyretin production through RNA interference–mediated degradation of TTR messenger RNA. Patisiran has shown exploratory cardiac benefit in the APOLLO-B trial (Maurer et al., 2023) [32], while vutrisiran has demonstrated significant reductions in mortality and cardiovascular events in the HELIOS-B trial, including in patients receiving background tafamidis [21]. A recent systematic review and network meta-analysis further highlights variability in treatment effects across tafamidis, patisiran, and vutrisiran, while underscoring the absence of head-to-head comparisons [33]. Overall, tafamidis remains the standard first-line therapy, whereas RNA interference–based agents such as patisiran and vutrisiran are emerging disease-modifying options with increasing evidence of benefit, and their optimal sequencing or combination within clinical practice remains to be defined.

Recent evidence highlights the clinical utility of cascade genetic screening in hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis (ATTRv-CM). Evaluation of the 2021 ESC recommendations demonstrated that systematic family screening facilitates the identification of asymptomatic pathogenic variant carriers as well as individuals with early or subclinical cardiac involvement, supporting earlier diagnosis and structured clinical follow-up (Müller et al., 2024) [34]. In addition, a large multicenter cohort study showed that cascade screening enables detection of affected relatives at earlier disease stages compared with index cases and provides prognostic stratification based on stage at diagnosis (Cappelli et al., 2026) [35]. In the context of emerging disease-modifying therapies, these findings underscore the potential clinical relevance of identifying patients earlier in the disease course, allowing timely initiation of established monitoring and treatment strategies as clinically indicated.

Future directions, strengths, and limitations

The strength of this review lies in its position as the first to bring together all published HELIOS-B analyses into a single synthesis, integrating efficacy, mechanistic, biomarker, patient-centered, and safety outcomes. By including subgroup and post-hoc analyses, the review adds important layers of detail: stratified findings by NYHA class, NAC stage, and NT-proBNP tertiles provide clinical granularity; mechanistic studies of echocardiographic and biomarker changes deepen biological understanding; and patient-centered endpoints such as days lost and outpatient heart failure events highlight real-world relevance. Although exploratory in nature, these analyses enrich the evidence base by illustrating consistent and clinically meaningful benefits across diverse outcomes and patient groups.

Future research should aim to extend the evidence base for vutrisiran in transthyretin amyloidosis with cardiomyopathy. Dedicated studies in patients with advanced disease (NYHA class IV, NAC stage 3 with renal dysfunction) are needed to establish generalizability beyond the current trial population. Head-to-head comparisons with tafamidis, as well as evaluations of combination therapy, could clarify optimal sequencing or synergy between treatments. Longer-term follow-up beyond 42 months will be important to assess durability of benefit and long-term safety.

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the available evidence. This systematic review is primarily based on a single phase 3 randomized controlled trial (HELIOS-B), along with multiple secondary and post-hoc analyses, which limits the availability of independent confirmatory evidence and precludes robust quantitative synthesis across trials. Additionally, most subgroup and exploratory analyses are hypothesis-generating and may be affected by limited statistical power and multiplicity. Follow-up duration, although sufficient to demonstrate clinically meaningful benefit, may not fully capture long-term disease modification or survival trajectories beyond the trial period. Additionally, real-world effectiveness in broader clinical populations remains to be fully characterized. Variability in baseline disease severity, comorbid burden, and background therapy may also influence treatment response and complicate cross-study comparisons.

Conclusion

This review suggests that vutrisiran demonstrates clinically meaningful benefits in patients with transthyretin amyloidosis with cardiomyopathy, with consistent effects observed across mortality, cardiovascular events, functional capacity, and biomarker outcomes in the HELIOS-B trial and supporting analyses. The therapy appears to maintain a favorable safety profile across clinically relevant subgroups, including those receiving background tafamidis. However, the current evidence base is primarily derived from a single phase 3 trial with supporting secondary and post-hoc analyses, and therefore remains limited in scope for definitive long-term or comparative conclusions. Further independent studies and real-world data are needed to better define its long-term effectiveness, optimal positioning, and potential role in combination or sequential transthyretin-directed therapy strategies.
