Explicit formulas for the Casimir eigenvalues of S ​ L ​ ( n , ℤ ) SL(n,\mathbb{Z}) -Maass forms
Vishal Muthuvel

Abstract
. Maass forms for S ​ L ​ ( n , ℤ ) SL(n,\mathbb{Z}) are defined to be eigenfunctions of the Casimir operators 𝒟 m , n \mathcal{D}_{m,n} of orders 1 ≤ m ≤ n 1\leq m\leq n for G ​ L ​ ( n , ℝ ) GL(n,\mathbb{R}) . For any 1 ≤ m ≤ n 1\leq m\leq n and Maass form ϕ \phi for S ​ L ​ ( n , ℤ ) SL(n,\mathbb{Z}) , we provide a formula for the eigenvalue of 𝒟 m , n \mathcal{D}_{m,n} associated with ϕ \phi in terms of the Langlands parameters of ϕ \phi . In the case m = 2 m=2 , we recover the formula for the Laplace eigenvalue of a Maass form due to Terras, the Casimir differential operator of order 2 2 being the Laplacian. Our proof takes a graph-theoretic approach, relating the action of every elementary differential operator of order m m for G ​ L ​ ( n , ℝ ) GL(n,\mathbb{R}) to the partitions of a directed, edge-ordered, weighted graph with m m edges and at most m m vertices.



1.
Introduction

Let
n\geq 2
. Consider the Lie group
G=GL(n,\mathbb{R})
with maximal compact subgroup
K=O(n,\mathbb{R})
and center
Z(G)\cong\mathbb{R}^{\times}
. We study automorphic forms for
\Gamma_{n}=SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
. These are elements of

\mathcal{L}^{2}\left(\Gamma_{n}\backslash G/(K\cdot Z(G))\right),

i.e., functions on the generalized upper half-plane

\mathfrak{h}^{n}\ :=\ G/(K\cdot Z(G))

that are invariant by
\Gamma_{n}
on the left and square-integrable under the
G
-invariant measure on
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
. In particular, these are joint eigenfunctions of the
G
-invariant differential operators acting on
\mathcal{L}^{2}\left(\Gamma_{n}\backslash\mathfrak{h}^{n}\right)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{R})
. This is the Lie algebra associated to
G
, consisting of all
n\times n
matrices with coefficients in
\mathbb{R}
. Casimir operators for
G
are generators for the center of the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}

Z(U(\mathfrak{g})),

which contains precisely the
G
-invariant differential operators acting on
\mathcal{L}^{2}\left(\Gamma_{n}\backslash\mathfrak{h}^{n}\right)
(see Section
2
). For each
1\leq m\leq n
, there is a Casimir operator of order
m
for
G
; the Casimir operator of order
2
is notably the Laplacian.

The actions of the Casimir and Hecke operators on
\mathcal{L}^{2}\left(\Gamma_{n}\backslash\mathfrak{h}^{n}\right)
commute and simultaneously diagonalize the space of automorphic forms for
\Gamma_{n}
. Selberg
[
Sel56
]
obtained the decomposition of the joint spectrum of these operators when
n=2
using his famous trace formula. Arthur and Langlands
[
Art79
,
Lan76
]
generalized Selberg’s spectral decomposition to all
n
, showing that the joint spectrum decomposes into a continuous, a cuspidal, and a residual part. The continuous spectrum is uncountable and comes from Eisenstein series associated to the parabolic subgroups of
G
, the residual spectrum comes from the residues of these Eisenstein series, and the cuspidal spectrum is countable and comes from Maass forms for
\Gamma_{n}
. In this paper, we consider the actions of the Casimir operators on Maass forms for
\Gamma_{n}
.

By the standard adelic lifting, Maass forms for
\Gamma_{n}
correspond to cuspidal automorphic representations

\pi\ =\ \bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}

of
GL(n,\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, where the product is over places
v
that are either finite primes or
\infty
. Each of these places is described by certain parameters. The infinite place, in particular, is described by a list of
n
complex numbers

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

(1.1)

called the Langlands parameters, which satisfy
\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\alpha_{\ell}=0
. The
\alpha_{\ell}
familiarly appear in the Gamma factors of the completed
L
-function associated to
\pi
.

We provide explicit formulas for the eigenvalues of Casimir operators associated with Maass forms for
\Gamma_{n}
, in terms of the Langlands parameters of the Maass forms. Our formulas can be readily generalized to minimal parabolic (Borel) Eisenstein series as well.

Since the Casimir operators for

have been so difficult to derive with current numerical methods, their eigenvalues have proved even harder to compute. While the Harish-Chandra isomorphism
[

identifies the Casimir operators for

Explicit formulas of this type, by specifying relations on Langlands parameters, offer a fresh perspective on important spectral questions. For example, Miller

3.
Graph theory

3.1.
Background

Let
G=G_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})}
be a graph on
\leq m
vertices
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\}
with
m
edges
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{m}\}
, where
e_{j}=[i_{j},i_{j+1})
is a directed edge from
i_{j}
to
i_{j+1}
with weight

w_{j}\ =\ \begin{cases}-t_{j}&\text{if }i_{j}\neq i_{j+1},\\
-\frac{t_{j}}{1+t_{j}}&\text{if }i_{j}=i_{j+1},\end{cases}

(3.1)

for all
1\leq j\leq m
. The graph is allowed to have multi-edges: if
e_{j}
and
e_{j^{\prime}}
start and end at the same points (i.e.,
i_{j}=i_{j^{\prime}}
and
i_{j+1}=i_{j^{\prime}+1}
) for some

, we still consider
e_{j}
and
e_{j^{\prime}}
as different edges. The edges are also ordered: if
j\leq k
, then we cannot traverse
e_{j}
if we previously traversed
e_{k}
; in other words, paths on
G
(see Definition
3.5
) necessarily traverse its edges in increasing order.

While the following definitions apply for general directed, edge-ordered, weighted graphs, we content ourselves with stating them in the specificity of
G
.

Definition 3.1

(vertex set)
.

Let
S=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\}
be a non-empty subset of
\{1,\dots,m\}
. We call

\nu(S)\ =\ \left\{i_{j_{1}},\ i_{j_{1}+1},\ i_{j_{2}},\ i_{j_{2}+1},\ \dots,\ i_{j_{k}},\ i_{j_{k}+1}\right\}

(3.2)

the vertex set of
S
, and denote the first and second minimum elements of
\nu(S)
by
v_{1}(S)
and
v_{2}(S)
, respectively. In the case that
|\nu(S)|=1
, we adopt the convention
v_{2}(S)=0
.

Definition 3.2

(indegree, outdegree)
.

Consider any
v\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\}
, and
S=\subset\{1,\dots,m\}
. The indegree (outdegree) of

in

is the number of edges that go enter (leave)

in
S
:

\displaystyle\text{in}_{v}(S)\

\displaystyle:=\ \#\{j\in S\ |\ i_{j+1}=v\},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\text{out}_{v}(S)\

\displaystyle:=\ \#\{j\in S\ |\ i_{j}=v\},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\Delta_{v}(S)\

\displaystyle:=\ \text{in}_{v}(S)-\text{out}_{v}(S).

(3.5)

Definition 3.3

(path, cycle)
.

Let

be a non-empty subset of

. We call

to

if

defines a path from

to

on

\displaystyle E_{i_{j_{1}},i_{j_{1}+1}}E_{i_{j_{2}},i_{j_{2}+1}}\cdots E_{i_{j_{k}},i_{j_{k}+1}}\ =\ E_{v,w}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\iff\

\displaystyle v\ =\ i_{j_{\ell}},\ \ i_{j_{\ell}+1}\ =\ i_{j_{\ell+1}}\ \text{ for all }\ 1\leq\ell\leq k-1,\ \text{ and }\ i_{j_{k}+1}\ =\ w,

(3.7)

where

is the
n\times n
elementary matrix with

in position
(i,j)
and
0
everywhere else. In the case

, we also refer to

as a cycle around

Definition 3.4

(proper cycle)
.

We call

Definition 3.5

(weight of a path)

Let

be a path in

is the product of the weights of the edges in

(3.8)

When we expand the product

(3.9)

\displaystyle A\ =\ \sum_{S\subset\{1,\dots,m\}}\left(\frac{\prod\limits_{j\in S}\ \ \ -t_{j}}{\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in S\\

where the elementary matrix product is taken along the ascending order of the elements of

\text{is a path}\\

3.2.
Combinatorial Lemmas for Differentiation

We are interested in calculating mixed partial derivatives of multivariable functions. While this is an exercise in calculus, we approach it combinatorially in keeping with our graph-theoretic approach. The following lemma offers a combinatorial perspective on the generalized product rule for mixed partial derivatives.

Lemma 3.6
.

Consider any integer
k\geq 1
. For each
1\leq i\leq k
, let
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}
be a complex-valued function in
m
real variables
t_{1},\dots,t_{m}
that is at least
m
times differentiable at the origin
\mathbf{0}
. Then the product of these
k
functions
\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}
is also a complex-valued function in
t_{1},\dots,t_{m}
that is at least
m
times differentiable at
\mathbf{0}
. Furthermore, for any
S\subset\{1,\dots,m\}
, the mixed partial derivative of
\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}
at
\mathbf{0}
with respect to
t_{j}
(
j\in S
) is

\frac{\partial^{|S|}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}\right)}{\prod_{j\in S}\partial t_{j}}(\mathbf{0})\ =\ \sum_{\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}=S}\ \prod_{i=1}^{k}\ \frac{\partial^{|S_{i}|}f_{i}}{\prod_{j\in S_{i}}\partial t_{j}}(\mathbf{0}),

(3.13)

where the sum is over ordered partitions of
S
.

That is, we can express the mixed partial derivative of a
k
-fold product of functions with respect to “the variables in
S
” by “distributing” these variables across the
k
functions and considering their mixed partial derivatives individually.

Proof.

We prove this lemma via (finite) induction on
|S|
. The base case
|S|=0
is satisfied in that we only need to consider the trivial partition
\emptyset=\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}\emptyset
.

For the inductive case, we assume that the lemma holds when
0\leq|S|=m^{\prime}<m
. Take any
S\subset\{1,\dots,m\}
such that
|S|=m^{\prime}+1
, and any
j^{\prime}\in S
. We know that the lemma holds for
S\backslash\{j^{\prime}\}
, so

\frac{\partial^{|S|-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}\right)}{\prod_{j\in S\backslash\{j^{\prime}\}}\partial t_{j}}(\mathbf{0})\ =\ \sum_{\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}=S\backslash\{j^{\prime}\}}\ \prod_{i=1}^{k}\ \frac{\partial^{|S_{i}|}f_{i}}{\prod_{j\in S_{i}}\partial t_{j}}(\mathbf{0}).

(3.14)

Differentiating both sides with respect to
t_{j^{\prime}}
and applying the familiar product rule for single-variable derivatives, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{|S|}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}\right)}{\prod_{j\in S}\partial t_{j}}(\mathbf{0})\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}=S\backslash\{j^{\prime}\}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{j^{\prime}}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\ \frac{\partial^{|S_{i}|}f_{i}}{\prod_{j\in S_{i}}\partial t_{j}}\right)(\mathbf{0})

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}=S\backslash\{j^{\prime}\}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\partial^{|S_{i}|+1}f_{i}}{\prod\limits_{j\in S_{i}\cup\{j^{\prime}\}}\partial t_{j}}\prod_{i^{\prime}\neq i}\frac{\partial^{|S_{i^{\prime}}|}f_{i^{\prime}}}{\prod_{j\in S_{i^{\prime}}}\partial t_{j}}\right)(\mathbf{0})

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}=S}\ \prod_{i=1}^{k}\ \frac{\partial^{|S_{i}|}f_{i}}{\prod_{j\in S_{i}}\partial t_{j}}(\mathbf{0}).

(3.15)

To generate the
k
-partitions of
S
, we can generate the
k
-partitions of
S\backslash\{j^{\prime}\}
; and for each partition
\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}=S\backslash\{j^{\prime}\}
, we can select any
S_{i}
and append
j^{\prime}
to it. This recursive procedure guides the last equality above. This completes the induction.
∎

In this vein, we also develop a combinatorial outlook on the generalized power rule for mixed partial derivatives.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
, and let
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}
be a complex-valued function in
m
real variables
t_{1},\dots,t_{m}
that is at least
m
times differentiable in a neighborhood of the origin
\mathbf{0}
. Then the power function

is also a complex-valued function in
t_{1},\dots,t_{m}
that is at least
m
times differentiable in some neighborhood of
\mathbf{0}
. Furthermore, for any non-empty subset
S\subset\{1,\dots,m\}
, the mixed partial derivative of
f^{\beta}
at the origin with respect to
t_{j}
(
j\in S

\frac{\partial^{|S|}(f^{\beta})}{\prod_{j\in S}\partial t_{j}}(\mathbf{0})\ =\ \sum_{k=1}^{|S|}\ (\beta)_{k}\cdot f^{\beta-k}(\mathbf{0})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}=S,\\
S_{i}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\ \prod_{i=1}^{k}\ \frac{\partial^{|S_{i}|}f}{\prod_{j\in S_{i}}\partial t_{j}}(\mathbf{0}),

(3.16)

where the sum is over unordered partitions of
S
, and

(3.19)

\displaystyle+\

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{|S|}(f^{\beta})}{\prod_{j\in S}\partial t_{j}}(\mathbf{0})

(3.21)

\displaystyle+\

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{|S|-1}\ (\beta)_{k}\cdot f^{\beta-k}(\mathbf{0})\sum_{\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}=S}\ \prod_{i=1}^{k}\ \frac{\partial^{|S_{i}|}f}{\prod_{j\in S_{i}}\partial t_{j}}(\mathbf{0}).

(3.22)

The above equality can be justified using the same recursive argument for
