Super T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} deformation and the RNS non-critical superstring
Marcelo Rezende Barbosa

Abstract
In this paper we review the super T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} deformation of 𝒩 = ( 1 , 1 ) \mathcal{N}=(1,1) theories in the superspace formulation, alongside its interpretation in the context of noncritical string theory. By combining the superspace approach with concepts from the study of super-Riemann surfaces, we demonstrate that super T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} deformations in superspace can be naturally interpreted as a noncritical RNS superstring theory. We also propose a possible interpretation of the super T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} deformations as 2D supergravity in the superspace through some field redefinitions.



1
Introduction

Deformations have garnered significant attention within the modern theoretical physics community in recent years. Through the study of deformations, we can explore several aspects of quantum field theories and forge connections between fundamentally different theories.

One deformation that has been extensively studied is the so-called
T\bar{T}
deformation. We can highlight several aspects of this theory that make it of high interest. The
T\bar{T}
deformation is defined in a 2D field theory through the operator:

\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}}=-\text{det}[T^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}]=\frac{1}{2}(T^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}-T^{\mu}_{\;\;\mu}T^{\nu}_{\;\;\nu}).

(1)

One of the primary reasons this deformation is so attractive to theoretical physicists is that it is exactly solvable; meaning that if we know the spectrum of the seed theory, we can derive the spectrum of the deformed theory through the formula
[
12
,
45
,
37
]
:

E(R,\lambda)=\frac{R}{2\lambda}\Bigg(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4\lambda}{R}E_{(0)}+\frac{4\lambda^{2}}{R^{2}}J_{(0)}^{2}}\Bigg),

(2)

where
E_{(0)}
, and
J_{(0)}
are respectively, the energy and momentum in the seed theory,
R
, the radius of the cylinder we put the theoy, and
\lambda
, the coupling of the deformation.

It is important to notice that
E(R,\lambda)=E(\lambda/R)
, so the functional dependence in coupling is fully determined if we now how it changes with the size of the space.

Other reasons why the study of this deformation is compelling include its connections to other theories, such as 2D topological gravity
[
11
,
22
,
38
]
, the Nambu-Goto string theory
[
37
]
, and non-critical string theories
[
46
,
9
,
4
]
. In this paper, we generalize the relation between the
T\bar{T}
deformation and string theories to the supersymmetric case.

From the spectrum, it was recognized that the deformation of a scalar theory yields exactly the spectrum expected for a string theory in the Nambu-Goto description in the static gauge
[
37
]
. In
[
46
]
, the authors reverse engineered deformed CFTs by employing the Stückelberg field method to restore conformal symmetry after the deformation. They demonstrated that this procedure yields a Polyakov string theory, in which the spacetime coordinates
X^{0,1}
are identified with the Stückelberg fields.

In
[
9
]
, the opposite path was studied. Starting with a CFT coupled to a Polyakov action for
X^{0,1}
, it was shown that after integrating out the extra fields, the theory is equivalent on-shell to the
T\bar{T}
deformation of the original CFT. In that paper, they considered two cases: one where the CFT plus the Polyakov action has a vanishing central charge, making it possible to quantize, and another where it has a non-vanishing central charge. In the latter case, it is necessary to couple the theory to 2D gravity on the worldsheet and modify the energy-momentum tensor in a subtle way.

In both cases, only NS-NS fields were turned on, in agreement with
[
5
]
, which established the equivalence between integrable deformations and string theories in NS-NS background fields.

In this context, supersymmetry is introduced, and in
[
4
]
it was shown that the
T\bar{T}
deformation can be lifted to a description in superspace, thus making it compatible with supersymmetry.

Extensive literature explores the relationship connecting
T\bar{T}
deformations, supersymmetry, and string theory; notable examples include
[
19
,
15
,
14
]
.

In this paper, we bring together the two concepts presented above: the string theory realization of the
T\bar{T}
deformation and supersymmetry. The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review the basics of the string construction of the
T\bar{T}
deformation for both critical and non-critical cases. In Section 3, we comment on the lifting of the deformation to the
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
2D superspace, where we briefly review necessary concepts, such as the description of the energy-momentum tensor in the unconstrained formulation of supergravity and the SUSY algebra. In Section 4, we generalize the string construction to the supersymmetric case, using the RNS (or spinning) string in superspace to describe super
T\bar{T}
deformations. We discuss both critical and non-critical cases, demonstrating that we indeed recover the expected spectrum.

2
Review of the Bosonic Approach

In this section, we briefly review the relation between the
T\bar{T}
deformation and the Polyakov action. We follow closely
[
9
]
. We refer to
[
9
,
44
]
for a more detailed exposition.

We start with a CFT described by some action
S_{CFT}
. We then define the total action as
S_{CFT}
plus the action of a 2D spacetime string theory in light-cone coordinates, i.e.

S=S_{CFT}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}z\partial v\bar{\partial}u,

(3)

where
v=x^{1}-x^{0}
and
u=x^{1}+x^{0}
. In order to be quantizable, this CFT should have central charge
c_{CFT}=24
. These fields decompose into holomorphic and anti-holomorphic parts on-shell, i.e.

v(z,\bar{z})=v(\bar{z})+\tilde{v}(z),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\;\;u(z,\bar{z})=u(z)+\tilde{u}(\bar{z}).

(4)

The space coordinate has the boundary condition

x^{1}(e^{2\pi i}z)=x^{1}(z)+2\pi R.

(5)

The Virasoro constraints are

\displaystyle-\partial u\,\partial v+T_{\mathrm{CFT}}=0,

(6)

\displaystyle-\bar{\partial}u\,\bar{\partial}v+\bar{T}_{\mathrm{CFT}}=0,

and we can use an on-shell gauge fixing,

\partial u=\bar{\partial}v=1.

(7)

It is important to note that this is a different gauge choice from the one that is usually adopted in the string theory literature. In the standard approach, one typically fixes the variable
v
(or
u
) completely, rather than only its holomorphic (or anti-holomorphic) part.

We can now insert (
7
) into (
6
) and substitute back into the action to obtain the
T\bar{T}
deformation.

If the theory is a CFT with
C_{CFT}\neq 24
, we need to change the Virasoro constraint in order to obtain an algebra with vanishing total central charge. This amounts to modifying the energy-momentum tensor of the 2D string theory as

\displaystyle T=-\partial u\,\partial v-\kappa\,\partial^{2}\log\partial u,

(8)

\displaystyle\bar{T}=-\bar{\partial}u\,\bar{\partial}v-\kappa\,\bar{\partial}^{2}\log\bar{\partial}v.

It is not hard to check that (
8
) is a well-defined energy-momentum tensor. We skip this computation here; it can be found in
[
9
]
, and a very similar computation will be carried out for the supersymmetric case below.

With this new energy-momentum tensor, the central charge of the 2D string theory is
c=2-24\kappa
, and imposing the vanishing of the total central charge gives

\kappa=\frac{c_{CFT}}{24}-1.

(9)

We can again impose the gauge-fixing condition to obtain the correct deformation.

We can now obtain the spectrum of the theory. To do so, we use the Virasoro constraints to derive the level-matching and mass-shell conditions,

\displaystyle L_{0}^{\text{total}}+\bar{L}_{0}^{\text{total}}-2=0,

(10)

\displaystyle L_{0}^{\text{total}}-\bar{L}_{0}^{\text{total}}=0.

We also notice that we can rewrite the action as

S_{X}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}z\left(G_{ab}+B_{ab}\right)\partial x^{a}\bar{\partial}x^{b},

(11)

for
G_{ab}=\eta_{ab}
and a constant
B
-field
B_{ab}=\varepsilon_{ab}
.

We also need the mode expansion for
x^{a}
,

\partial x^{a}=-\frac{i}{2}\frac{p_{L}^{a}}{z}-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n\neq 0}\alpha_{n}^{a}z^{-n-1},\quad\bar{\partial}x^{a}=-\frac{i}{2}\frac{p_{R}^{a}}{\bar{z}}-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n\neq 0}\bar{\alpha}_{n}^{a}\bar{z}^{-n-1},

(12)

where
p_{L,R}^{a}=\left(p^{a}\pm\left(G^{ab}\pm B^{ab}\right)w_{b}\right)
,
p^{a}
are spacetime momenta, and
w_{a}
are winding zero modes.

We parameterize
p^{a}
as

p_{L}^{0}=p_{R}^{0}=\mathcal{E}+BR,\quad p_{L}^{1}=\frac{J}{R}+R,\quad p_{R}^{1}=\frac{J}{R}-R,

(13)

where
\mathcal{E}
is the spacetime energy and
J
the spacetime angular momentum. Plugging these back into the level-matching and mass-shell conditions, respectively, we get

\displaystyle\Delta_{R}-\Delta_{L}=\frac{1}{4}p_{1L}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}p_{1R}^{2}=J,

(14)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}p_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}p_{1L}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}p_{1R}^{2}+\Delta_{L}+\Delta_{R}-2\kappa-2=0,

where
\Delta_{L,R}
are the left- and right-scaling dimensions in the CFT. Substituting (
9
) and the parameterizations for
p^{a}
, and writing the energy of the CFT as
E=\frac{1}{R}(\Delta_{L}+\Delta_{R}-\frac{c_{CFT}}{12})
, we solve for the spacetime energy
\mathcal{E}
,

\mathcal{E}=R\Bigg(-1+\sqrt{1+\frac{2E}{R}+\frac{J^{2}}{R^{4}}}\Bigg).

(15)

We can recognize the spectrum of a
T\bar{T}
-deformed theory when we identify the inverse of the radius as the coupling.

A comment is in order here. The energy-momentum tensor (
8
) can be derived from the action
[
9
]
:

S=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}z\bar{\partial}u\partial v-\frac{\kappa}{4}R^{(2)}\,\,\text{ln}(\partial u\bar{\partial}v),

(16)

where
R^{(2)}
is the 2D curvature scalar on the worldsheet. This action and the energy-momentum tensor are intimately related to 2D gravity, as discussed in
[
9
,
47
,
16
,
10
]
.

We will generalize these arguments for the RNS string using the superspace formulation. Some normalizations will differ from those used in this section, but the calculations and conclusions are the same.

3
SUSY and
T\bar{T}

In this section we review some basics of 2D supergravity in
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
superspace. An incomplete list of references that treat superspace supergravity is
[
23
,
21
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
28
,
29
,
7
]
.

At the end of this section, we review the uplift of the
T\bar{T}
deformation to the
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
superspace, as carried out in
[
4
]
.

We start by defining the coordinates in this superspace,
z^{M}=(z,\bar{z},\theta,\bar{\theta})
, where the first two coordinates are bosonic and the other two are fermionic.
We use unconstrained prepotentials to work with supergravity; that is, we start from a description that solves the supergravity constraints. With that stated, the SUSY operators are

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\theta\partial,\;\;\;\;\;\mathcal{Q}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}-\theta\partial,

(17)

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{D}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\theta}}+\bar{\theta}\bar{\partial},\;\;\;\;\;\bar{\mathcal{Q}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\theta}}-\bar{\theta}\bar{\partial}

and their algebra is

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{2}=\partial,\;\;\;\;\;\mathcal{Q}^{2}=-\partial,

(18)

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{2}=\bar{\partial},\;\;\;\;\;\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{2}=-\bar{\partial}.

All other anticommutators vanish (equivalently, one may rewrite the above relations using anticommutators and appropriate factors of two).

We have three independent unconstrained prepotentials, which we denote by
H_{-}^{\;z}(z^{M})
,
H_{+}^{\;\bar{z}}(z^{M})
, and
S(z^{M})
. In this notation,
S(z^{M})
is the super-Weyl compensator, and in what follows it decouples from the theory because we work with CFTs.

The coupling between matter and supergravity, in terms of the prepotentials, is

S=\int d^{2}zd^{2}\theta\bigg(H_{-}^{\;z}\mathcal{T}+H_{+}^{\;\bar{z}}\bar{\mathcal{T}}\bigg).

(19)

Under a superdiffeomorphism, the variations are

\delta H_{-}^{\;z}=-\bar{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{K}^{z},\;\;\;\;\;\delta H_{+}^{\;\bar{z}}=-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{K}^{\bar{z}},

(20)

and, using integration by parts and demanding invariance of the action, we obtain the conservation equations

\bar{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{T}=0,\;\;\;\;\;\mathcal{D}\bar{\mathcal{T}}=0,

(21)

modulo matter equations of motion.

Focusing on the holomorphic sector and choosing holomorphic coordinates, the super energy-momentum tensor (here denoted by
\mathcal{T}
) has the component decomposition

\mathcal{T}(z,\theta)=G(z)+\theta T(z),

(22)

where
G(z)
and
T(z)
are the worldsheet supercurrent and energy-momentum tensor, respectively.

From the above decomposition, it follows straightforwardly that

\mathcal{D}\mathcal{T}=T(z).

(23)

Using the superconservation equations (
21
), it is also straightforward to derive the usual conservation for
T
, i.e.

\bar{\partial}T=0.

(24)

The same statements hold for the anti-holomorphic part.

All in all, we can define the supersymmetric
T\bar{T}
operator as

\mathcal{O}_{sTT}=\bar{\mathcal{T}}\mathcal{T},

(25)

and, working out the SUSY algebra, we can show that it defines an invariant supersymmetric deformation. First, we need to notice that

T\bar{T}=\mathcal{D}\bar{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{O}_{sTT}|_{\theta=\bar{\theta}=0}=\int d^{2}\theta\,\mathcal{O}_{sTT}.

(26)

With the above equation, we can lift the
T\bar{T}
deformation to a deformation in
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
2D superspace. Then, using standard arguments in superspace calculus, it follows that the superdeformation defined by
\mathcal{O}_{sTT}
is indeed supersymmetric.

4
The RNS Superstring

4.1
The critical case

In this section, we connect both the Callebaut et al. formulation using Polyakov strings and the more recently discovered supersymmetric
T\bar{T}
deformation reviewed in the previous section.
The starting point is the following theory:

S[\Phi,X]=S_{CFT}[\Phi]+\frac{1}{2}\int d\mathbf{\mu}\bar{\mathcal{D}}X_{a}\mathcal{D}X^{a},

(27)

Here,
\Phi
represents the fields in the CFT defined by the action
S_{CFT}[\Phi]
, and
X_{a=0,1}
are superscalar fields in a free superscalar theory with target space signature
1+1
. In the above formula, the measure is the measure on the super-Riemann surface with supervielbein determinant
E
:
d\mathbf{\mu}=d^{2}z\,d^{2}\theta\,E
.

In the following, we define
\mathcal{T}
as the super-stress tensor of the pair of super-scalars,
\mathcal{T}_{CFT}
as the stress tensor for the SCFT, and their conjugates
\bar{\mathcal{T}},\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{CFT}
, respectively.

In what follows, we work with the holomorphic part only, but everything carries over to the anti-holomorphic one.

The super Virasoro constraint that follows from the action (
27
) is

\mathcal{T}+\mathcal{T}_{CFT}=0,

(28)

and the same holds for the anti-holomorphic currents.

We now define the boundary conditions that will be important in the next steps. In analogy with (
5
), we impose that after a
2\pi
rotation in the spatial direction,

X^{1}(\text{e}^{2\pi i}z,\text{e}^{-2\pi i}\bar{z},-\theta,-\bar{\theta})=X^{1}(z,\bar{z},\theta,\bar{\theta})+2\pi R.

(29)

From the above equation it follows that the bosonic part of
X^{1}
behaves as in the bosonic case, while the fermionic parts have NS–NS boundary conditions. This is in agreement with
[
5
]
, which states that the deformations are equivalent to a string in the NS–NS sector.

This conclusion is also important for computing the spectrum, since it depends on the normal-ordering constant, which differs between the sectors of the RNS string.

For what is to come, it is convenient to go to light-cone coordinates:

X^{1}(z,\theta)=\frac{U(z,\theta)+V(z,\theta)}{\sqrt{2}};\;\;\;X^{0}(z,\theta)=\frac{U(z,\theta)-V(z,\theta)}{\sqrt{2}}.

(30)

Here the notation
(z,\theta)
really means
(z,\bar{z},\theta,\bar{\theta})
.

The action for the pair of scalar fields can be expressed as

S_{X}=\int d\mu\,\bar{\mathcal{D}}U\mathcal{D}V.

(31)

In these coordinates, the super Virasoro constraints read

\mathcal{T}_{CFT}-\frac{1}{2}[\mathcal{D}U\partial V+\mathcal{D}V\partial U]=0.

(32)

Following Callebaut et al.’s approach for the bosonic case, we now fix the gauge by imposing
\mathcal{D}U=\theta p
, where
p
is a constant.
This gauge fixing implies a “light-cone” type gauge
\partial U=p
. In this setup, we can write

\mathcal{D}V=\frac{1}{p}\mathcal{T}_{CFT}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}V.

(33)

Similarly, we can treat the anti-holomorphic equations with the gauge fixing
\bar{\mathcal{D}}V=\bar{\theta}k
, where
k
is a constant, to get

\bar{\mathcal{D}}U=\frac{1}{k}\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{CFT}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\theta}}U.

(34)

We can now substitute (
33
) and (
34
) back into the action (
31
) to get

S[\Phi]=S_{CFT}[\Phi]+\frac{1}{kp}\int d^{2}zd^{2}\theta\,\,\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{CFT}\,\mathcal{T}_{CFT}+\cdots,

(35)

where the
\cdots
are terms that vanish because of (anti-)holomorphicity and Grassmann integral properties. More precisely, the
\cdots
are terms proportional to the equations of motion, which vanish on-shell.

Action (
35
) is the action for a
T\bar{T}
-deformed
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
SCFT, as we saw in previous sections.

4.2
Quantization

To quantize the above theory, we should ensure that the total central charge vanishes. If
c_{CFT}
is the central charge of the seed theory, the total central charge is

c=c_{CFT}+\frac{3}{2}(2)-15,

(36)

where the
2
comes from the two super-scalars, and the
-15
comes from the super-ghosts.

The above result means that, given a super-CFT with central charge
12
, we can interpret its
T\bar{T}
deformation as a 2D string theory whose internal CFT is given by the seed theory. For a theory of 8 free super-scalars, the
T\bar{T}
deformation is exactly the 10D string theory, as observed in
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
.

4.2.1
The Non-Critical Case

As done in
[
9
]
, in the non-critical case we need to modify the stress-energy tensor (the super stress-energy tensor in our case). It turns out that the generalization of the term used in
[
9
]
to the supersymmetric case is highly non-trivial. The naive guess would be

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}=-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{D}U\partial V+\mathcal{D}V\partial U)-\kappa\mathcal{D}\partial\text{ln}[\partial U],

(37)

but, because
\partial
is not so well behaved under superconformal transformations (see appendix
B
for a review), this is incorrect. Indeed, as we show below, this deformation is only the first term of the correct deformation.

In the supersymmetric case, the modification that accomplishes this is given by a quasi superprojective connection
1
1

1

This is also how it works in the bosonic case, but there we did not need to summon such fancy names
:

(38)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{T}}=-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{D}U\partial V+\mathcal{D}V\partial U)-\kappa\mathcal{D}\partial\text{ln}[\mathcal{D}\Psi_{U}],

\displaystyle\bar{\tilde{\mathcal{T}}}=-\frac{1}{2}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}U\bar{\partial}V+\bar{\mathcal{D}}V\bar{\partial}U)-\kappa\bar{\mathcal{D}}\bar{\partial}\text{ln}[\bar{\mathcal{D}}\bar{\Psi}_{V}],

where
\Psi_{U}=\frac{\mathcal{D}U}{\sqrt{\partial U}}
, and the anti-holomorphic part is obtained by replacing
\Psi_{U}
with
\bar{\Psi}_{V}=\frac{\bar{\mathcal{D}}V}{\sqrt{\bar{\partial}V}}
.

To justify the above super stress-energy tensor, we can study the finite transformation of modified stress-energy tensors, i.e.
\tilde{T}=T-\kappa\Phi
, for some composite operator
\Phi
. To do so, we start with the infinitesimal transformation (here we use the bosonic theory, which can be generalized straightforwardly to the supersymmetric one):

\delta\tilde{T}(z)=\oint_{w}\epsilon(w)\tilde{T}(w)\tilde{T}(z),

(39)

where we assume radial ordering and use the normalization
\oint_{w}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint dw
.
From this, we can derive

\delta\tilde{T}=-\frac{1}{12}c\partial^{3}\epsilon-2T\partial\epsilon-\epsilon\partial T+a[u;z,z^{\prime}]+\kappa\delta^{\prime}\Phi.

(40)

In (
40
),
a[u;z,z^{\prime}]
is a field-(
u
)-dependent infinitesimal anomaly, and
\delta^{\prime}\Phi
is the variation generated by the original stress-energy tensor
T
. These quantities are given by

(41)

\displaystyle a[u;z,z^{\prime}]\equiv-\kappa\oint_{w}\epsilon(w)\Phi(w)T(z),

\displaystyle\kappa\delta^{\prime}\Phi\equiv-\kappa\oint_{w}\epsilon(w)T(w)\Phi(z).

We assume (as in the case of interest) that the OPE
\Phi\Phi
has only regular terms.

In order for
\tilde{T}
to be an allowed stress-energy tensor, the variation (
40
) should take the form

\delta\tilde{T}=-\frac{1}{12}\tilde{c}\partial^{3}\epsilon-2\tilde{T}\partial\epsilon-\epsilon\partial\tilde{T},

(42)

which implies

a[u;z,z^{\prime}]+\kappa\delta^{\prime}\Phi=-\frac{1}{12}c^{\prime}\partial^{3}\epsilon+2(\kappa\Phi)\partial\epsilon+\epsilon\partial(\kappa\Phi),

(43)

where
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}(\kappa)
and
\tilde{c}=c+c^{\prime}
. Following
[
9
]
closely, we say that
a[u;z,z^{\prime}]
can be separated into a field-dependent anomaly and a central-charge anomaly term, and that
\delta^{\prime}\Phi
transforms almost as a stress-energy tensor, differing from it by a field-dependent anomaly. The field-dependent anomalies of both contributions must cancel, and the central-charge anomalies must add up to give
c^{\prime}
.

Now, integrating the variation of
\Phi
(with appropriate normalization) gives

\Phi^{\prime}{dz^{\prime}}^{2}=\Phi dz^{2}+\frac{1}{12}\{f(z),z\}dz^{2}-\mathcal{A}[u],

(44)

where
\mathcal{A}
is the integrated anomaly, properly normalized. This is the point we need in order to motivate the form of (
38
).

When
\mathcal{A}=0
,
\Phi
is a well-known quantity, called a projective affine connection (see appendix
B
and
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
31
]
), and is given by

\Phi(u)=\partial\gamma(u)-\frac{1}{2}[\gamma(u)]^{2},\,\,\,\,\gamma(u)=\partial\text{ln}(\partial u).

(45)

In the above equation,
\gamma
is an affine connection.

We can recognize from the above equation that the first term is indeed the modification used in
[
9
]
.

We can generalize the transformations and objects mentioned above to
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
superspace. The super affine connection is
\Gamma(U)=\mathcal{D}\text{ln}(\mathcal{D}\Psi_{U})
, where
\mathcal{D}U=\Psi_{U}\mathcal{D}\Psi_{U}
. As commented in appendix
A
, this can be solved for
\Psi_{U}
, giving
\Psi_{U}=\frac{\mathcal{D}U}{\sqrt{\partial U}}
. The super projective connection is

\Phi(U)=-[\partial\Gamma(U)+\Gamma(U)\mathcal{D}\Gamma(U)],

(46)

and again we can recognize the first term as the proposed modification for the supersymmetric case.

This modification indeed reproduces a new allowed super stress-energy tensor, as can be checked below.

We can write (
38
) in terms of derivatives of
U
only:

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}=\mathcal{T}-\kappa\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[\mathcal{D}\partial\text{ln}(\partial U)+\mathcal{D}\partial\bigg[\frac{\mathcal{D}U\mathcal{D}\partial U}{(\partial U)^{2}}\bigg]\Bigg].

(47)

We can now compute the OPEs for this new stress-energy tensor. Using the notation
Z_{i}=(z_{i},\theta_{i})
,
Z_{ij}=z_{i}-z_{j}-\theta_{i}\theta_{j}
, and
\theta_{ij}=\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}
, we get

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}(Z_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}(Z_{2})\sim\frac{\frac{1}{4}\hat{\tilde{c}}}{Z_{12}^{3}}+\frac{3}{2}\frac{\theta_{12}}{Z_{12}^{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}(Z_{2})+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{Z_{12}}\mathcal{D}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}(Z_{2})+\frac{\theta_{12}}{Z_{12}}\partial\tilde{\mathcal{T}}(Z_{2}),

(48)

where
\tilde{c}=\frac{3}{2}(2)-12\kappa=3-12\kappa
. If we now impose the vanishing of the total central charge (original CFT + 2D string + ghosts), we get

0=c_{CFT}+(3-12\kappa)-15\implies\kappa=\frac{c_{CFT}}{12}-1.

(49)

We can similarly work out the anti-holomorphic part.

To end this section, we compute the useful OPEs whose main result is (
48
). We focus on the holomorphic part only, since the anti-holomorphic part follows in the same way.

The starting point is the OPE between the fields
U
and
V
:

U(Z_{1})V(Z_{2})=V(Z_{1})U(Z_{2})\sim-\text{ln}Z_{12}.

(50)

The next step is to compute the OPE between
V
and

\mathcal{D}\Psi_{U}=\sqrt{\partial U}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}U\mathcal{D}\partial U\frac{1}{(\partial U)^{\frac{3}{2}}},

(51)

using the OPE above.

Then, using the chain rule and performing the calculations, we get

\displaystyle V(Z_{1})\mathcal{D}\Psi_{U}(Z_{2})\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2Z_{12}}(\partial U(Z_{2}))^{-1/2}+\frac{\theta_{12}}{2Z_{12}^{2}}(\mathcal{D}U(Z_{2}))(\partial U(Z_{2}))^{-3/2}

(52)

\displaystyle-\frac{\theta_{12}}{2Z_{12}}(\partial U(Z_{2}))^{-3/2}\,\partial\mathcal{D}U(Z_{2})-\frac{3}{4Z_{12}}(\mathcal{D}U(Z_{2}))(\partial U(Z_{2}))^{-5/2}\,\partial\mathcal{D}U(Z_{2}).

Using the above OPE, we can carefully apply
\mathcal{D}
and
\partial
in the first variable,
Z_{1}
, to obtain

\mathcal{T}(Z_{1})\mathcal{D}\Psi_{U}(Z_{2})\sim\frac{1}{2}\frac{\theta_{12}}{Z_{12}^{2}}\mathcal{D}\Psi_{U}(Z_{2})+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{Z_{12}}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{D}\Psi_{U}(Z_{2}))+\frac{\theta_{12}}{Z_{12}}\partial(\mathcal{D}\Psi_{U}(Z_{2})).

(53)

From the above OPE, we see that
\mathcal{D}\Psi_{U}
is a superprimary of conformal dimension
h=1/2
.

To simplify notation in the next steps, we set
J=\mathcal{D}\Psi_{U}
. Using the above OPE and the chain rule, we obtain

\mathcal{T}(Z_{1})\text{ln}[J(Z_{2})]\sim\frac{1}{2}\frac{\theta_{12}}{Z_{12}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{Z_{12}}\mathcal{D}(\text{ln}[J(Z_{2})])+\frac{\theta_{12}}{Z_{12}}\partial(\text{ln}[J(Z_{2})]).

(54)

Now we apply
\mathcal{D}_{2}\partial_{2}
to the above OPE, being careful with Grassmann properties of
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{D}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(Z_{1})\mathcal{D}_{2}\partial_{2}\ln J(Z_{2})\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{12}^{3}}+\frac{\theta_{12}}{Z_{12}^{3}}\mathcal{D}_{2}\ln J(Z_{2})+\frac{1}{2Z_{12}^{2}}\partial_{2}\ln J(Z_{2})

(55)

\displaystyle+\frac{3\theta_{12}}{2Z_{12}^{2}}\mathcal{D}_{2}\partial_{2}\ln J(Z_{2})+\frac{1}{2Z_{12}}\partial_{2}^{2}\ln J(Z_{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{\theta_{12}}{Z_{12}}\mathcal{D}_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}\ln J(Z_{2}).

Finally, to derive (
48
), we need to compute
(\mathcal{D}\partial\text{ln}J(Z_{1}))\mathcal{T}(Z_{2})
. The simplest way to perform this calculation is to swap
1\leftrightarrow 2
in (
55
), anticommute the operators (paying attention to Grassmann properties), and expand the functions of
Z_{1}
appearing on the right-hand side. This gives

(\mathcal{D}\partial\ln J(Z_{1}))\mathcal{T}(Z_{2})\sim\frac{1}{Z_{12}^{3}}-\frac{\theta_{12}}{Z_{12}^{3}}\mathcal{D}\ln J(Z_{2})-\frac{1}{2Z_{12}^{2}}\partial\ln J(Z_{2}).

(56)

Adding (
56
) and (
55
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{2}{Z_{12}^{3}}+\frac{3\theta_{12}}{2Z_{12}^{2}}\mathcal{D}_{2}\partial_{2}\ln J(Z_{2})+\frac{1}{2Z_{12}}\mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathcal{D}_{2}\partial_{2}\ln J(Z_{2}))

(57)

\displaystyle+\frac{\theta_{12}}{Z_{12}}\partial_{2}(\mathcal{D}_{2}\partial_{2}\ln J(Z_{2})).

Adding this to the OPE of
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{T}
yields (
48
).

5
Spectrum

To compute the spectrum, we follow again
[
9
]
. The (super)Virasoro constraint is

\mathcal{T}_{CFT}-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{D}U\partial V+\mathcal{D}V\partial U)+\kappa\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[\mathcal{D}\partial\text{ln}(\partial U)+\mathcal{D}\partial\bigg[\frac{\mathcal{D}U\mathcal{D}\partial U}{(\partial U)^{2}}\bigg]\Bigg]=0.

(58)

We can proceed exactly as described in the review section, but being careful with some subtleties that appear in the computation.

The first subtlety is that, for the NS sector of the RNS string, we have
L_{0}^{\text{total}}+\bar{L}_{0}^{\text{total}}-1=0
, instead of
L_{0}^{\text{total}}+\bar{L}_{0}^{\text{total}}-2=0
. As the “2” in the bosonic case is crucial for the correct spectrum, the “1” in the supersymmetric case is crucial as well.

The second subtlety appears when we try to naively use the on-shell conditions of the previous paragraph to compute the spectrum. At first sight, it seems that we have
T\bar{T}
deformations plus extra contributions coming from the fermions. This can be seen by separating the components of the new super stress-energy tensor:

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}=\tilde{G}(z)+\theta\tilde{T}(z),

(59)

which gives

\displaystyle\tilde{T}(z)=T(z)-\frac{\kappa}{2}\Bigg\{\partial^{2}\ln(\partial u)+(\partial u)^{-2}\Bigg[2\left(\partial^{2}\ln(\partial u)-2(\partial\ln(\partial u))^{2}\right)(\partial\psi_{u})\psi_{u}

(60)

\displaystyle+4\partial\ln(\partial u)(\partial^{2}\psi_{u})\psi_{u}-(\partial^{3}\psi_{u})\psi_{u}-(\partial^{2}\psi_{u})(\partial\psi_{u})\Bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(z)=G(z)-\frac{\kappa}{2}(\partial u)^{-1}\left[2\partial^{2}\psi_{u}-3\partial\ln(\partial u)\partial\psi_{u}-\left(\partial^{2}\ln(\partial u)-(\partial\ln(\partial u))^{2}\right)\psi_{u}\right].

We see from (
60
) that there are extra terms, absent in the bosonic case, which are related to the fermionic fields.

We can define a grading based on the fermions, where
\psi_{u}
has fermion number
1
and
\psi_{v}
has fermion number
-1
.

When we impose
T^{\text{tot}}(z)\phi\sim 0
, where
\phi
is a vertex operator,
T(z)
contains terms with no fermions and terms proportional to
\psi_{u}\psi_{v}
; together with the
\partial^{2}\text{ln}(\partial u)
contribution, these have fermion-number grading
0
. The extra terms have fermion-number grading
2
, so when acting on physical states their contribution vanishes separately.

Then, proceeding with the computation of the spectrum as in the bosonic case, we have

L_{0}^{\text{total}}+\bar{L}_{0}^{\text{total}}-1=-\frac{1}{2}p_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}p_{1L}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}p_{1R}^{2}+\Delta_{L}+\Delta_{R}-\kappa-1=0,

(61)

and using (
49
) we obtain

-2\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}}{2}+\frac{BR}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{J^{2}}{2R^{2}}+\frac{R^{2}}{2}+RE=0.

(62)

Solving for
\mathcal{E}
, we recover the correct
T\bar{T}
spectrum:

\mathcal{E}=R\Bigg(-1+\sqrt{1+\frac{2E}{R}+\frac{J^{2}}{R^{4}}}\Bigg).

(63)

A clearer derivation of the spectrum may be possible within an alternative Superstring formulation.

6
Supergravity and Modified action

Last but not least, we can try to understand the action that gives rise to (
38
). The action whose stress-energy tensor is (
38
) is

S=\int d\mu\,\bigg(\bar{\mathcal{D}}U\mathcal{D}V-\kappa^{\prime}\mathcal{R}\,\,\text{ln}(\mathcal{D}\Psi_{U}\bar{\mathcal{D}}\bar{\Psi}_{V})\bigg),

(64)

where
\mathcal{R}
is the supercurvature scalar.

That said, we can interpret the term with
\varrho(U,V)=\text{ln}(\mathcal{D}\Psi_{U}\bar{\mathcal{D}}\bar{\Psi}_{V})
as a super-dilaton-type coupling. Furthermore, defining
\Omega=\Psi_{U}\bar{\Psi}_{V}
, we can rewrite the modified action as the supergravity action:

S=\int d\mu\,\bigg(\Omega\text{e}^{\varrho}-\kappa^{\prime}\mathcal{R}\,\,\varrho\bigg).

(65)

The above action seems to be related to 2D supergravity in superspace, and it opens discussion for a possible interpretation of the super
T\bar{T}
deformation as being equivalent to a coupling to supergravity.

7
Conclusion and Next Steps

As shown in the main text, the super
T\bar{T}
deformation of a CFT can be recast as a non-critical spinning string theory (or RNS string theory) in a two-dimensional spacetime upon fixing the light-cone gauge. Our construction generalizes the method presented in
[
9
]
to the supersymmetric framework of
[
4
]
. In particular, it provides an RNS realization of the Green–Schwarz argument developed in
[
4
]
, where the deformation of eight superscalars was shown to be equivalent to a superstring theory. As in the bosonic case, our approach is more general, since it can, in principle, be applied to any supersymmetric field theory formulated in two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
superspace.

In this work, we have focused on extending the relation between the
T\bar{T}
deformation and Polyakov string theory (or its RNS counterpart in the supersymmetric case). However, our analysis does not include a covariant quantization of the resulting theory, as was carried out initially in
[
9
]
and subsequently studied in greater detail in
[
44
]
.

Another important question raised by our construction concerns the connection between the super
T\bar{T}
deformation and (topological) supergravity in superspace. Such a relation may emerge through suitable field redefinitions of the target-space fields.

Two additional directions for future investigation can be identified, one closely related to the previous discussion and the other motivated by holography. First, given the possibility of performing a covariant quantization in the RNS formalism, it is natural to ask whether super
T\bar{T}
deformations admit a pure-spinor formulation.

The second direction concerns holography. Can this interpretation of the
T\bar{T}
deformation provide new insight into a top-down approach to the holographic duality of deformed CFTs?

Appendix B
Review of Superprojective Affine Connections

This appendix provides a brief review of projective and affine connections, starting with the standard bosonic theory on Riemann surfaces and subsequently generalizing to the supersymmetric (SUSY) framework on super Riemann surfaces (SRS). The focus is on their transformation properties and the fundamental relationships connecting them.

B.1
The Bosonic Case

B.1.1
Conformal Transformations and the Schwarzian Derivative

On a Riemann surface equipped with local holomorphic coordinates
(z,\bar{z})
, a change of local coordinates to another system
(z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})
is given by a conformal (biholomorphic) transformation:

z^{\prime}=z^{\prime}(z),\quad\bar{z}^{\prime}=\bar{z}^{\prime}(\bar{z})

(68)

For any such smooth conformal change of coordinates, the
Schwarzian derivative

S(z^{\prime};z)
of the transformation is defined as:

S(z^{\prime};z)\equiv\partial^{2}\ln(\partial z^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial\ln(\partial z^{\prime})\right)^{2}

(69)

where
\partial\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
. The Schwarzian derivative vanishes if and only if the mapping
z\to z^{\prime}(z)
is a projective (Möbius) transformation.

B.1.2
Projective and Affine Connections

A
projective connection
(or Schwarzian connection) is a collection of locally holomorphic functions
\{R_{zz}(z)\}
that do not transform strictly as tensors, but instead pick up an inhomogeneous term proportional to the Schwarzian derivative under conformal mappings:

R_{z^{\prime}z^{\prime}}(z^{\prime})=(\partial z^{\prime})^{-2}\left[R_{zz}(z)-S(z^{\prime};z)\right]

(70)

Similarly, an
affine connection
is a collection of locally holomorphic functions
\{\gamma_{z}(z)\}
that transform with an inhomogeneous logarithmic derivative under coordinate changes:

\gamma_{z^{\prime}}(z^{\prime})=(\partial z^{\prime})^{-1}\left[\gamma_{z}(z)-\partial\ln(\partial z^{\prime})\right]

(71)

B.1.3
Relation Between Affine and Projective Connections

Given an affine connection
\gamma_{z}
, one can always construct a projective connection
R_{zz}
. The relationship between the two is expressed through a Riccati-type equation (often referred to as a Miura transformation in the context of integrable systems):

R_{zz}=\partial_{z}\gamma_{z}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{z}^{2}

(72)

This relation ensures that the resulting quantity
R_{zz}
satisfies the correct transformation law involving the Schwarzian derivative.

B.2
The Supersymmetric Case

B.2.1
Superconformal Transformations and the Super Schwarzian

In the supersymmetric setting, a super Riemann surface (SRS) is locally parametrized by superconformal coordinates
(z,\bar{z},\theta,\bar{\theta})
, where
\theta
and
\bar{\theta}
are odd Grassmann variables. A superconformal change of coordinates
(z,\theta)\to(z^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
is a smooth mapping satisfying the
superconformal condition
:

\mathcal{D}z^{\prime}=\theta^{\prime}\mathcal{D}\theta^{\prime}

(73)

where
\mathcal{D}
is the superderivative defined by
\mathcal{D}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
.

To describe the transformation laws, it is convenient to define an even superfield
w
via the relation
e^{-w}\equiv\mathcal{D}\theta^{\prime}
. The
super Schwarzian derivative

\mathcal{S}(z^{\prime},\theta^{\prime};z,\theta)
associated with the superconformal map is then defined by:

\mathcal{S}(z^{\prime},\theta^{\prime};z,\theta)\equiv-\left[\partial\mathcal{D}w+(\partial w)(\mathcal{D}w)\right]

(74)

The super Schwarzian derivative properly generalizes its bosonic counterpart, vanishing identically for superprojective transformations.

B.2.2
Super Projective and Super Affine Connections

A
super projective connection
is a collection of odd, locally superanalytic superfields
\{\mathcal{R}_{z\theta}(z,\theta)\}
that transform under superconformal mappings as:

\mathcal{R}_{z^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}}(z^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})=e^{3w}\left[\mathcal{R}_{z\theta}(z,\theta)-\mathcal{S}(z^{\prime},\theta^{\prime};z,\theta)\right]

(75)

By analogy, a
super affine connection
is a collection of odd superfields
\{\Gamma_{\theta}(z,\theta)\}
defining a covariant derivative
\nabla\equiv\mathcal{D}+p\Gamma_{\theta}
. It transforms under superconformal coordinate changes according to:

\Gamma_{\theta^{\prime}}(z^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})=e^{w}\left[\Gamma_{\theta}(z,\theta)-\mathcal{D}w\right]

(76)

B.2.3
Relation Between Super Affine and Super Projective Connections

Just as in the bosonic theory, a super projective connection
\mathcal{R}_{z\theta}
can be constructed from a super affine connection
\Gamma_{\theta}
. This relationship is governed by the super Miura transformation:

\mathcal{R}_{z\theta}\equiv-\left[\partial\Gamma_{\theta}+\Gamma_{\theta}(\mathcal{D}\Gamma_{\theta})\right]

(77)

This fundamental identity guarantees that the combination on the right-hand side perfectly absorbs the inhomogeneous shift of
\Gamma_{\theta}
to produce the correct super Schwarzian transformation law required for
\mathcal{R}_{z\theta}
.
