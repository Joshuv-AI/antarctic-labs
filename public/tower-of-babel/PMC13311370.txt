Increased Expression of miR‐326 Mediates Chemosensitivity in Pediatric Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia Through ABCA2 and YY1 Downregulation: An Observational and Experimental Study
Authors: Narges Aberuyi, Soheila Rahgozar, Melika Katebi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimsMultidrug resistance (MDR) is a major determinant of relapse‐associated mortality in pediatric acute lymphoblastic leukemia (pALL). Although miR‐326 downregulation has been associated with poor prognosis, its role in chemoresistance remains undefined. This study investigated the mechanistic contribution of miR‐326 to MDR in pALL.MethodsmiR‐326 expression was quantified by RT‐qPCR in 58 fresh bone marrow samples and 14 paired diagnosis–relapse slides from pALL patients. A multidrug‐resistant CD10−CD34− B‐cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B‐ALL) cell line was transfected with miR‐326 mimic, and drug sensitivity was assessed by MTT assay. Bioinformatic analyses identified ABCA2 and YY1 as potential targets of miR‐326, which were validated by RT‐qPCR, Western blotting, and dual‐luciferase reporter assays.ResultsLow miR‐326 expression was associated with relapse and reduced 4‐year disease‐free survival. Restoration of miR‐326 expression enhanced chemosensitivity and downregulated ABCA2 and YY1 at both mRNA and protein levels. Reporter assays confirmed direct targeting of both genes.ConclusionmiR‐326 attenuates MDR in pALL through direct suppression of ABCA2 and YY1, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target for overcoming chemoresistance in pediatric B‐ALL.

Introduction

Pediatric acute lymphoblastic leukemia (pALL) is the most common malignancy in children, accounting for approximately 26% of cancers in individuals under 15 years of age [1]. Despite significant advances in treatment outcomes, multidrug resistance (MDR) and relapse remain major challenges in the management of pALL [2]. Dysregulation of microRNAs (miRNAs) [3] and overexpression of ATP‐binding cassette (ABC) transporters [4, 5] are recognized as key mechanisms underlying MDR in pALL. Additionally, Yin Yang‐1 (YY1), a transcription factor with dual roles as both activator and repressor [6], regulates the expression of multiple genes, including ABCB1, and plays a pivotal role in MDR pathways [7, 8, 9].

miRNAs are small non‐coding RNAs, typically almost 22 nucleotides in length, that regulate gene expression and have been implicated in chemoresistance in cancer. Their regulatory roles extend to ABC transporters, transcription factors, and various signaling molecules [4]. Among these, miR‐326 has emerged as a tumor suppressor that modulates the expression of genes involved in cancer progression [10, 11]. Reduced expression of miR‐326 has been reported in association with MDR in both solid tumors and leukemia [12, 13, 14]. Our previous studies have highlighted the diagnostic and prognostic value of miR‐326 in pALL [10, 15, 16]; however, its causal role in drug resistance has not yet been fully elucidated [4, 17].

The present study aimed to evaluate the impact of extended chemotherapy on miR‐326 expression and to elucidate its mechanistic role in chemoresistance. Based on our prior findings showing that miR‐326 expression profiles derived from archived bone marrow smears are comparable to those obtained from fresh‐frozen samples in ALL patients [18], archived bone marrow slides were used for miRNA extraction and analysis. We further assessed the effect of exogenous miR‐326 on the chemosensitivity of the drug‐resistant RN95 B‐cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B‐ALL) cell line using 3‐[4,5‐dimethylthiazol‐2‐yl]−2,5‐diphenyl tetrazolium bromide (MTT) assays. Furthermore, potential targets of miR‐326 were identified in B‐ALL through an integrated approach combining bioinformatic predictions, dual‐luciferase reporter assays, real‐time quantitative PCR (RT‐qPCR), and Western blot analyses.

Additionally, 14 archived bone marrow slides were analyzed for paired diagnosis‐relapse comparisons.

Inclusion criteria comprised a confirmed diagnosis of precursor B‐ALL in patients aged 1–14 years, established by flow cytometry, along with > 30% blasts in the bone marrow aspirate. Exclusion criteria included a diagnosis of Burkitt lymphoma, presence of the Philadelphia chromosome translocation t(9;22)(q34;q11), and inability to complete the 4‐year follow‐up due to non‐attendance. Control samples consisted of bone marrow aspirates from age‐matched non‐cancer individuals.

Written informed consent was obtained from the parents of all participants. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the University of Isfahan (approval number: IR.UI.REC.1397.164, issued on 2018.1.11). All patients were treated according to the AIEOP‐BFM ALL 2000 study protocol [19].

Additionally, archived bone marrow slides from 14 B‐ and T‐ALL patients, obtained at diagnosis and at relapse, were examined. miR‐326 was isolated using the High Pure miRNA isolation Kit (Roche, Switzerland) according to the manufacturer's instructions, and its expression levels were compared between diagnosis and relapse samples.

The precursor B‐ALL CD10− CD34− RN95 cell line was established in our laboratory from an 8‐year‐old patient at relapse, as previously described [20]. Given the previously reported poor prognostic significance of CD10/CD34 expression in B‐ALL [20], this multidrug‐resistant cell line (RN95) was used to evaluate the role of miR‐326 in drug resistance.

HEK‐293 cells were obtained from the Cell Bank of Pasteur Institute (Tehran, Iran). RN95 and HEK‐293 cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 and DMEM media (Gibco, Milan, Italy), respectively, supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco, Milan, Italy), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin (Bioidea, Tehran, Iran), and maintained at 37°C and 5% CO2. All cell lines tested negative for mycoplasma contamination.

RNA Extraction, cDNA Synthesis, and RT‐qPCR Analysis

Mononuclear cells were isolated using Lymphoprep density gradient centrifugation (Axis Shailed Diagnostics Ltd., Oslo, Norway) according to the manufacturer's protocol. Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, California, CA), and cDNA was synthesized using the miR‐CURY LNA Universal RT microRNA PCR kit (Qiagen, Denmark).

Reverse transcription was performed at 42°C for 60 min, followed by enzyme inactivation at 95°C for 5 min. miR‐326 expression levels were quantified by RT‐qPCR using ExiLENT SYBR Green Master Mix and miR‐326‐specific LNA primers (Qiagen, Denmark) on a Chromo4 system (Bio‐Rad, USA). RNU6 small nucleolar RNA was used as an internal reference.

All samples were analyzed in duplicate. No‐reverse transcription (no‐RT) and no‐template control (NTC) reactions were included to confirm assay specificity. Relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method and expressed as fold change relative to controls.

Transfection of RN95 Cells With Exogenous miR‐326

During the exponential growth phase, RN95 cells were transfected with 250 nM miR‐326 mimic and scrambled miRNA control (Exiqon, Denmark) using an electroporation system (Eppendorf Multiporator, Germany). Electroporation conditions were optimized at 250 V, 40 ms pulse width, and a single pulse.

Mock control cells were subjected to the same conditions without any oligonucleotides. After electroporation, cells were seeded in six‐well plates (2 × 106 cells/well) and incubated in 4 mL RPMI 1640 supplemented with 10% FBS at 37°C and 5% CO2. Cells were harvested 48 h post‐transfection for downstream analyses. The same transfection procedure was applied to HEK‐293 cells (see Section 2.7).

Drug Cytotoxicity Assay

The cytotoxic effects of selected chemotherapeutic agents on miR‐326–transfected RN95 cells were assessed using an MTT assay. Twenty‐four hours after transfection, cells were seeded in quintuplicate at a density of 1.5 × 105 cells per well.

Cells were treated for 72 h with methotrexate (88 nM; Gibco, Milan, Italy), doxorubicin (0.67 µM; Pfizer, New York, USA), and dexamethasone (1.65 mM; Caspian Tamin, Rasht, Iran). Drug concentrations were based on previously determined IC50 values for RN95 cells [20]. Cytarabine (100 mM; Ebewe, Unterach am Attersee, Austria) was used based on prior optimization, as RN95 cells are resistant to increasing cytarabine concentrations [20].

Cell viability was quantified using the MTT assay, and data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism version 7.04.

Bioinformatic Analysis

Potential miR‐326 target genes were predicted using RNAhybrid (http://bibiserv.cebitec.uni-bielefeld.de/rnahybrid), miRDB (http://mirdb.org), miRTar (https://tools4mirs.org/software/target_functional_analysis/mirtar/), TargetScan 7.1 (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_71/), microRNA.org (http://www.microrna.org ), and miRWalk 2.0 (zmf.umm.uni‐heidelberg.de/apps/zmf/mirwalk2/). The UCSC Genome Browser (https://genome.ucsc.edu) was used to identify potential YY1 binding sites within the ABCA2 promoter.

Western Blot Analysis

Total protein was extracted using lysis buffer, and protein concentration was determined by the Bradford assay (Bio‐Rad). Equal amounts of protein (30 µg) were separated by 7.5% SDS‐PAGE and transferred onto nitrocellulose membranes (Amersham Biosciences).

Membranes were blocked with 5% BSA (w/v) in TBST for 1 h and incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against ABCA2 (rabbit polyclonal anti‐ABCA2; 1:750, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), YY1 (rabbit polyclonal anti‐YY1; 1:750, Abcam, USA), or β‐actin (mouse anti‐β‐actin; 1:250, Abcam, USA). After incubation with HRP‐conjugated secondary antibodies (anti‐rabbit; 1:4000, Sigma, USA, or anti‐mouse; 1:1000, Sigma, USA, respectively), protein bands were visualized using ECL solution (Bio‐Rad, Hercules, CA) and X‐ray film 48 h after electroporation.

Dual‐Luciferase Reporter Assay

Fragments of the 3′‐UTR regions of ABCA2 and YY1 containing predicted miR‐326 binding sites were amplified by PCR from DNA template using designed primers listed in Table S1. The fragments were then cloned into the NotI and XhoI restriction sites of a psiCHECK2 vector (Promega, US). The mutant 3′‐UTR fragments at miR‐326 binding sites were generated using Site‐Directed Mutagenesis by Splicing by Overlap Extension PCR. All constructs were confirmed by sequencing and co‐transfected into HEK‐293 cells with mimic or scrambled miRNA using standard calcium phosphate transfection. HEK‐293 cells cultured in 96‐well plates were co‐transfected with 100 ng of the reporter constructs and 30 nM miR‐326 mimic or scrambled miRNA, following the manufacturer's protocol for the Dual‐Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA). 48 h post‐transfection, Renilla and firefly luciferase activities were quantified using a luminometer. Firefly luciferase activity was used for normalization.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 23.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism version 7.04 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). The data represent two independent experiments conducted in duplicate or quintuplicate. Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test and Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests. Disease‐free survival (DFS) was estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and compared using the log‐rank (Mantel–Cox) test. Associations between miR‐326 expression and clinicopathological characteristics were assessed using Pearson's Chi‐square test. The optimal cut‐off value (0.055) for miR‐326 expression was determined by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis using Cutoff Finder software and consistently applied across analyses. Paired comparisons were analyzed using a paired t‐test. All statistical tests were two‐sided unless otherwise specified. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression was not performed because the limited number of events would have increased the risk of overfitting and unreliable hazard ratio estimates in this exploratory study. For cell‐based assays, one‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's post hoc test was used to analyze percent viability, relative gene expression, and luciferase activity in RN95 cells transfected with miR‐326 or control constructs. Protein band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software. All data are expressed as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM), and statistical significance was defined as p ≤ 0.05.

Results

Effect of Chemotherapy on miR‐326 Expression: Overall and Paired Analyses

All patients included in this study were treated according to the AIEOP‐BFM ALL 2000 protocol. Clinical outcomes were evaluated in 37 newly diagnosed pediatric ALL patients (Table 1). During the 4‐year follow‐up period, 7 patients experienced relapse (defined as “Cohort relapse”; Table 1). An additional 14 relapsed patients referred from external centers were excluded from survival analyses due to incomplete follow‐up data, but were included in the expression analyses (designated as “Referred relapse”; Table 1).

ROC curve analysis identified an optimal cut‐off value of 0.055 for miR‐326 expression. Low miR‐326 expression (miR‐326−) was associated with significantly reduced 4‐year DFS (log‐rank test, p = 0.009; Table 1, Figure 1). Notably, extended chemotherapy did not appear to overcome the adverse prognostic impact associated with persistently low miR‐326 expression.

Kaplan–Meier curves for 4‐year disease‐free survival (DFS) in pALL stratified by miR‐326 expression. Survival analysis of 44 newly diagnosed and relapsed ALL cases demonstrated significantly improved 4‐year DFS in patients with high miR‐326 expression (miR‐326+) compared with those with low expression (miR‐326−) (log‐rank, p = 0.009). Patients were stratified using an optimal miR‐326 cut‐off value of 0.055, determined by ROC curve analysis.

To further assess changes in miR‐326 expression over time, paired analysis was performed on bone marrow samples from 14 patients with matched diagnosis‐relapse specimens (clinical characteristics shown in Table S2). Data normality was confirmed using the Shapiro–Wilk test, followed by a two‐sided paired t‐test. RT‐qPCR analysis revealed a significant decrease in miR‐326 expression at relapse compared with diagnosis (Figure 2), indicating that chemotherapy did not restore miRNA levels.

Restoration of Chemosensitivity by Exogenous miR‐326 in B‐ALL Cells

To investigate the role of miR‐326 in modulating chemotherapy response, RN95 B‐ALL cells were transfected with miR‐326 mimic or scrambled miRNA control, followed by treatment with chemotherapeutic agents. Successful transfection was confirmed by RT‐qPCR, which demonstrated a significant increase in miR‐326 expression in miR‐326 mimic‐transfected cells compared with untreated cells, scrambled miRNA controls, and mock control cells (Figure 3A). Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay. miR‐326 overexpression significantly reduced cell survival following treatment with methotrexate, dexamethasone, doxorubicin, and cytarabine compared with scrambled miRNA controls (37.539 ± 3.612% vs. 55.535 ± 4.490%, p = 0.040; 33.208 ± 1.660% vs. 48.686 ± 2.208%, p = 0.002; 43.205 ± 2.428% vs. 59.979 ± 3.637%, p = 0.010; and 71.638 ± 0.095% vs. 86.156 ± 1.086%, p = 0.006; respectively) (mean ± SEM; n = 3). Statistical significance was determined using a one‐way ANOVA following confirmation of normal data distribution by the Shapiro–Wilk test (Figure 3B). These findings indicate that miR‐326 enhances chemosensitivity in drug‐resistant B‐ALL cells.

Discussion

ALL is the most prevalent type of leukemia in pediatric patients, accounting for approximately 70% of cases [21]. Despite substantial advances in chemotherapy protocols for pALL, the emergence of MDR and subsequent relapse remains a major clinical challenge and a leading cause of mortality in children [7, 22]. Although miR‐326 has previously been identified as a prognostic biomarker for chemoresistance, its causal role in drug resistance has not yet been fully elucidated [10, 14, 16, 23, 24]. In this context, the present study aimed to investigate the impact of miR‐326 on patient survival and to assess the reciprocal effect of chemotherapy on miR‐326 expression levels.

Survival analysis of 44 ALL cases, together with 14 relapsed cases referred from external centers and 14 paired diagnosis–relapse samples using RT‐qPCR and Western blotting, consistently demonstrated reduced miR‐326 expression at relapse, indicating persistent downregulation despite chemotherapy. This reduction was significantly associated with poorer 4‐year DFS, underscoring the potential need for adjunctive therapeutic approaches to restore miR‐326 expression and overcome chemoresistance.

To explore this therapeutic potential, we evaluated the effect of exogenous miR‐326 overexpression in the multidrug‐resistant RN95 B‐ALL cell line. Our results showed enhanced chemosensitivity to methotrexate, dexamethasone, doxorubicin, and cytarabine following miR‐326 restoration. These findings, supported by MTT assay data, align with prior studies in solid tumors illustrating that miR‐326 increases sensitivity to chemotherapeutic agents such as doxorubicin [13], adriamycin [14], cisplatin [25], and curcumin [26]. This effect is mediated through the regulation of ABC transporters and transcription factors, including ABCC1 [13, 14] and Sp1 [25], respectively. Although preliminary, these findings provide valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms of chemoresistance in pALL and suggest a potential therapeutic strategy.

Building on our previous investigations [10, 27, 28], literature evidence, and in silico analyses, ABCA2 and YY1 were selected as candidate targets of miR‐326. Both are strongly implicated in MDR and oncogenic signaling in hematological malignancies [6, 10, 27, 28, 29, 30], supporting their biological relevance. Predicted miR‐326 binding sites within the 3′‐UTR of ABCA2 and YY1 were identified using TargetScan and microRNA.org and subsequently validated using dual‐luciferase reporter assays and Western blot analysis. Furthermore, non‐parametric binomial test analysis of 26 randomly selected ALL samples demonstrated a significant inverse association between miR‐326 expression and protein levels of ABCA2 and YY1 (Table 3).

ABCA2, a member of the ABC transporter family, plays a crucial role in MDR pALL [27, 28, 31], while YY1 functions as a transcription factor with both activating and repressive capabilities [6]. Overexpression of YY1 has been associated with angiogenesis, metastasis, and poor prognosis across various cancers [6, 32, 33, 34]. Our findings suggest a regulatory network in pALL involving miR‐326, ABC transporters (ABCA2, ABCC1, and ABCB1), and transcription factors (YY1 and Sp1), which may contribute to the modulation of MDR (Figure 6). The interaction between YY1 and Sp1 has been previously documented [35, 36, 37, 38], and further studies are underway to explore the potential regulatory interplay of miR‐326 with the YY1– wt1 axis.

Proposed mechanism by which miR‐326 modulates multidrug resistance in pALL. miR‐326 targets ABCA2, ABCC1, YY1, and Sp1, thereby influencing the MDR network. YY1 and Sp1 transcription factors may regulate the expression of downstream targets, including ABCA2 and ABCB1. miR‐326 is also likely to impact MDR through direct regulation of the ABCA2 and ABCC1 transporters. Overexpression of these drug efflux transporters promotes the extrusion of chemotherapeutic agents from leukemic cells.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. Although the cohort size was sufficient to detect statistically significant associations and reinforce our prior observations on the prognostic value of miR‐326, it remains limited for definitive survival and validation analyses. The use of a single B‐ALL cell line (RN95) may not fully capture disease heterogeneity, highlighting the need for validation in additional models, including patient‐derived xenografts and primary leukemic cells. Furthermore, while ABCA2 and YY1 were identified as direct targets, in vivo studies will be essential to confirm their causal role in chemoresistance. Broader evaluation of additional chemotherapeutic agents, particularly those associated with non‐coding RNA regulation [16, 23, 24], as well as investigation of upstream pathways regulating miR‐326 under treatment stress, will be critical to translating miR‐326 from a biomarker into a therapeutic target.
