Factorizing quarkonium production matrix elements using effective field theory
Marston Copeland, Ivan Vitev

Abstract
We use effective field theory to factorize production matrix elements that appear in quarkonium cross sections in NRQCD. By applying a Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation we show that the soft and ultrasoft sectors of NRQCD can be decoupled from the heavy quark and antiquark fields in a hybrid vNRQCD/pNRQCD Lagrangian at leading order in the velocity power counting. This enables us to separate quarkonium production matrix elements in terms of matrix elements of color-singlet composite fields, which we can write as the wavefunction at the origin, and state independent vacuum correlators of chromo-electric and chromo-magnetic gluon fields. This approach verifies powerful connections between the LDMEs of different S-wave vector quarkonium states, originally derived using pNRQCD. Additionally, we find new operator contributions for the color-octet P-wave mechanism, which satisfy a similar set of relationships. Finally, this approach allows us to factorize the production matrix elements that appear in the transverse momentum dependent factorization framework, known as TMD soft transition functions, in terms of state independent gluon correlators. This work restores some universality for TMD production operators and dramatically improves the predictive power of NRQCD in the TMD framework.

Since the discovery of the

Their relatively simple internal structure makes them ideal candidates for studying bound state formation under strong interactions. In particular, quarkonia are non-relativistic bound states composed of heavy quarks with large masses and slow relative motion within the system. This means that quarkonium bound state formation is a process that contains many different energy scales - the heavy quark masses (

One of the first attempts to exploit this separation of scales came from the EFT known as non-relativistic QCD (NRQCD)

The vNRQCD approach differs in that it simultaneously keeps both the soft and ultrasoft degrees of freedom in the low-energy theory, but separates gluon fields whose components scale homogeneously as soft or ultrasoft into distinct fields via a mode expansion. These two formalisms, while different in their approach, have thus far been shown to be equivalent for all calculations

Both of these improved versions of NRQCD were developed to systematically compute corrections to the potential between heavy quarks. Manohar:1999xd

Background

In this section we review the fundamental assumptions of quarkonium production in NRQCD. We also review the vNRQCD formalism and the soft and ultrasoft scales within the effective theory.

2.1
NRQCD

NRQCD is an EFT developed to study non-relativistic bound states of heavy quarks in QCD. The power-counting parameter of NRQCD is the small relative velocity of the

2.2
vNRQCD

In the ordinary theory of quarkonium production, scales below
2m
are not carefully considered. This means that the LDMEs contain physics in both the soft (
mv
) and ultrasoft (
mv^{2}
) sectors of the theory. In large part, this is because both of these scales are non-perturbative, and hence so are the low-energy operators. This makes it is difficult to make any analytical statements about the LDMEs. However, as we will see, a careful delineation of the scales in an effective field theory enables powerful factorization theorems to be derived within the production matrix elements. The delineation of these scales can be handled either using the pNRQCD framework
Brambilla:1999xf
or the vNRQCD framework
Luke:1999kz
. Since the vNRQCD framework explicitly maintains the quark and antiquark degrees of freedom in the Lagrangian, it is much easier to connect this EFT to prior quarkonium production work completed in NRQCD. Additionally, vNRQCD has the advantage of simultaneously keeping the soft and ultrasoft scales in the theory, which allows it to be easily paired with SCET
Bauer:2000ew
;
Bauer:2000yr
to derive the factorization theorems for quarkonium cross sections. For these reasons, we find it important to understand how the soft and ultrasoft degrees of freedom can be separated in the context of the vNRQCD framework.

In the theory of vNRQCD, gluons are separated into different “modes”, which scale as the momentum they carry. The two gluons that appear in the theory are those that can satisfy the onshell condition,
p^{2}=0
. These are the soft gluons, which scale as

A_{\bf p}^{\mu}\sim(m\text{v},m{\bf v}),

(4)

and the ultrasoft gluons,

A_{us}^{\mu}\sim(m\text{v}^{2},m{\bf v}^{2}).

(5)

Quarks have potential momentum scaling

p_{c}\sim(m\text{v}^{2},m{\bf v}),

(6)

so as to satisfy a non-relativistic disperion relation. Gluons with potential momentum scaling are off shell since gluons are necessarily relativistic. Therefore, they are integrated out of the theory. vNRQCD uses a label formalism, where soft momentum is phased out via a field redefinition,

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\sum_{\bf p}e^{-i{\bf p}\cdot{\bf x}}\psi_{\bf p}(x),

(7)

\displaystyle A^{\mu}(x)=\sum_{\bf q}e^{-iq\cdot x}A_{q}^{\mu}(x),

\bm{\mathcal{P}}^{i}\psi_{{\bf p}}={\bf p}^{i}\psi_{\bf p}\,,\qquad\mathcal{P}^{\mu}A_{q}^{\nu}=q^{\mu}A_{q}^{\nu}\;.

2.3
A comment on quarkonium production operators

When matching quarkonium production cross sections onto NRQCD, the production matrix elements of the effective theory are written as local quark-antiquark operators, as in eq. (
3
).
The locality of the production matrix element can be understood by the matching procedure, where full QCD amplitudes describing the production of a
Q\bar{Q}
pair are expanded order-by-order in
v
so hard propagators carrying momentum at the scale
2m
(or larger) are shrunk to a point and quark/antiquark spinors are also expanded, which can be matched onto operators that have been multipole expanded. Importantly, this means that the fields in the production operators have zero radial separation. While this result is universal, the steps to show that this is true are subtly different in the collinear factorization vs. TMD factorization approaches. For clarity, we will review the matching procedures in both approaches.

2.3.1
Collinear factorization

In the collinear factorization framework the produced quarkonium necessarily carries hard momentum at or above the scale
M_{V}\sim 2m

(P^{+}_{V},P^{-}_{V},{\bf P}_{V,T})\gtrsim(m,m,m).

(11)

In general, any quarkonium production process will require at least one heavy quark and antiquark of the same flavor to be produced. Therefore, the partonic matrix element describing the production can be written in the form,

\bra{0}T\{\overline{\Psi}_{i}(x){\cal O}_{ij}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}\Psi_{j}(y)\}\ket{Q\bar{Q}(n)+X},

(12)

where we’ve used the capital psi,
\Psi
, to denote the relativistic heavy quark field. Here,
{\cal O}_{ij}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}
is a placeholder for the intermediate Dirac structures, fields, and propagators that connect
\overline{\Psi}(x)
and
\Psi(y)
. The Lorentz indices come from intermediate gluon and photon vertices and propagators, and the spin indices
i,j
come from Dirac structures that appear in the amplitudes at each order in the NRQCD power-counting. We have suppressed color indices for clarity.

The NRQCD framework requires us to match the quark and antiquark fields onto their NRQCD counterparts by integrating out the large components of the momentum. In general, the full QCD field
\Psi
will have both quark and antiquark components, but because this particular matrix element in the example is describing the production of a
Q\bar{Q}
pair, it is sufficient to replace

\displaystyle\Psi(x)\to\sum_{v}e^{-imv\cdot x}\psi(x),

(13)

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}(x)\to\sum_{v}e^{-imv\cdot x}\chi^{\dagger}(x),

at leading order, where
v
is the direction of motion for the
Q\bar{Q}
pair. Now, since the quarkonium carries hard momentum in the collinear factorization framework,
P\sim m
, each component of the coordinates
x
and
y
must scale as
1/m
. Therefore, the NRQCD fields in eq. (
18
) should be multipole expanded

\displaystyle\psi(x)=e^{x\cdot\partial}\psi(0)e^{-x\cdot\partial}\approx\psi(0)+{\cal O}(\text{v}),

(14)

\displaystyle\chi^{\dagger}(x)=e^{x\cdot\partial}\chi^{\dagger}(0)e^{-x\cdot\partial}\approx\chi^{\dagger}(0)+{\cal O}(\text{v}),

where the partial derivative acts on any residual momentum of the field that scales as
m\text{v}
, so that the combination
x\cdot\partial\sim m\text{v}/m=\text{v}
.

Therefore, after expanding the connecting operator in powers of
v
, the quarkonium production operators in the collinear factorization framework reduce to an object with heavy
Q\bar{Q}
fields located at the origin,

C(Q,M)\chi^{\dagger}(0){\cal K}\cdot\Gamma\psi(0).

(15)

These fields have zero radial separation.

2.3.2
TMD factorization

In TMD factorization, we can work in a frame where the quarkonium’s momentum scales as

P_{V}^{\mu}=(P^{+}_{V},P^{-}_{V},{\bf P}_{V,T})\sim(m,m,m\text{v}).

(16)

As in collinear factorization, the production of a
Q\bar{Q}
can be described in the full theory via a partonic matrix element,

\bra{0}T\{\overline{\Psi}_{i}(x){\cal O}_{ij}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}(x-y)\Psi_{j}(y)\}\ket{Q\bar{Q}(n)+X}.

(17)

where the operator,
{\cal O}(x-y)
, will generically be some combination of propagators and vertices that connect the two quark fields in the amplitude.

Again, we can match the quark and antiquark fields onto their NRQCD counterparts,

\displaystyle\Psi(x)\to\sum_{v}e^{-imv\cdot x}\psi(x),

(18)

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}(x)\to\sum_{v}e^{-imv\cdot x}\chi^{\dagger}(x).

After matching onto the NRQCD fields, due to the distinct momentum scaling in the TMD framework (given by eq. (
16
)), only the plus and minus components of the quark field’s positions can be multipole expanded away,

\displaystyle\psi(x)=e^{(x^{+}\partial^{-}+x^{-}\partial^{+})}\psi(x_{T})e^{-(x^{+}\partial^{-}+x^{-}\partial^{+})}\approx\psi(x_{T})+{\cal O}(\text{v}),

(19)

\displaystyle\chi^{\dagger}(y)=e^{(x^{+}\partial^{-}+x^{-}\partial^{+})}\chi^{\dagger}(y_{T})e^{-(x^{+}\partial^{-}+x^{-}\partial^{+})}\approx\chi^{\dagger}(y_{T})+{\cal O}(\text{v}).

The hard partonic matching coefficient for matching onto the effective theory operators will come from expanding the amplitude generated from the matrix element in eq. (
17
) in the limit
m\gg m\text{v},m\text{v}^{2}
. According to eq. (
16
),
p_{T}\sim m\text{v}
, so the connecting operator should be expanded so that it only contains hard momentum,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{ij}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle(x-y)=\int\frac{d^{4}p_{H}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\tilde{{\cal O}}_{ij}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}(p_{H})e^{ip\cdot(x-y)}

(20)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int\frac{dp_{H}^{+}dp^{-}_{H}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{{\cal O}}_{ij}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}(p^{+}_{H},p_{H}^{-})e^{ip^{+}(x-y)^{-}+ip^{-}(x-y)^{+}}\delta^{(2)}(x_{T}-y_{T})+{\cal O}\bigg(\frac{p_{T}^{2}}{m^{2}}\bigg).

Therefore, we see that this momentum expansion forces
y_{T}\to x_{T}
in the effective TMD production operator. Therefore we can write the final NRQCD operator in the TMD factorization framework as

C^{\prime}(Q,M)\chi^{\dagger}(x_{T}){\cal K}\cdot\Gamma\psi(x_{T}).

(21)

While this operator is slightly different than the production operator in the collinear factorization framework, (the matching coefficient is also distinct), it too has zero radial separation.

3
Hubbard-Stratonovich transformations in vNRQCD

In this section, we review a powerful technique introduced by Fleming and Mehen in ref.
Fleming:2005pd
that allows one to match the vNRQCD framework onto the pNRQCD Lagrangian at leading order. We discuss how the soft and ultrasoft scales can be separated within this formalism.

3.1
pNRQCD from vNRQCD

vNRQCD and pNRQCD are two distinct EFTs for bound states of heavy quarks that describe the low-energy dynamics far below the scale
2m
. While supposedly describing the same physics, they are written in terms of completely different degrees of freedom -
vNRQCD
contains soft and ultrasoft gluons and maintains the explicit quark and antiquark degrees of freedom, while pNRQCD integrates out the soft scale so that only composite color-singlet (
S_{\bf r}
) or color-octet (
O_{\bf r}
) fields and the ultrasoft gluons remain. These composite fields, which are bosons, describe the dynamics of the quark/antiquark bound state, not the individual particles themselves. Since these EFTs supposedly describe the same physics, it is natural to ask: how can they be related?

Ref.
Fleming:2005pd
showed that the answer to this question is via a Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation. This useful technique, which is often used in quantum many-body calculations, rewrites any four-fermion interaction in a Lagrangian in terms of auxiliary bosonic degrees of freedom. In general, this transformation takes the form

The intermediate Lagrangian in eq. (
25
) serves as a useful tool for understanding the dynamics of boundstate formation. Since both the composite fields (which are closely related to the physical quarkonium states) and the quark and antiquark degrees of freedom are explicit, this formulation is a good springboard for studying the full range of physics and scales involved in quarkonium production - which begins with the production of a heavy
Q\bar{Q}
pair and ends with a fully hadronized quarkonium particle in the final state. In particular, this technique will be useful for studying how scales can be separated from each other at each step in the production process.

3.2
Decoupling the ultrasoft gluons

In the composite Lagrangian of eq. (
25
), there are many different field “modes” at play. There are interactions between the ultrasoft gluons and the potential scaling quark/antiquarks, and there are also interactions with soft gluons and the quark/antiquarks. Moreover, there are interactions with the composite fields, which carry only ultrasoft momentum, with the quark and antiquark sector. These many interactions make it difficult to disentangle the effects of different scales on quarkonium production.

For example, in the composite field formulation presented in the previous section, there is an interaction between the ultrasoft
A^{0}
gluon and the quark and antiquark fields which is the same order as the kinetic term in the power counting. Therefore, this interaction does not experience a power suppression and can appear at all orders in
\alpha_{s}(m\text{v}^{2})
. Fortunately, this interaction can be easily decoupled from the Lagrangian via a BPS field redefinition on the fields
Fleming:2005pd
. This redefinition is given by

\psi_{\bf p}(x)\to U_{v}(x)\psi^{\prime}_{\bf p}(x),~~~~~~~~~U_{v}(x)={\cal P}\exp\bigg(-ig\int_{-\infty}^{0}dsA_{0}(x+s)\bigg).

(27)

Under the field redefinition, the leading pieces of the ultrasoft Lagrangian reduce to

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{(0),us}_{c}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bf p}\psi^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}_{\bf p}\bigg(i\partial_{0}-\frac{{\bfcal P}^{2}}{2m}\bigg)\psi^{\prime}_{\bf p}+\sum_{\bf p}\chi^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}_{\bf p}\bigg(i\partial_{0}+\frac{{\bfcal P}^{2}}{2m}\bigg)\chi^{\prime}_{\bf p}

(28)

\displaystyle+\int d^{3}{\bf r}V^{(1)}(r)\bigg(S_{\bf r}^{\dagger}S_{\bf r}-S^{\dagger}_{\bf r}\sum_{\bf p}e^{i{\bf p}\cdot{\bf r}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{c}}}\chi^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}_{-{\bf p}}\psi_{\bf p}-S_{\bf r}\sum_{\bf q}e^{-i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{c}}}\psi^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}_{\bf q}\chi^{\prime}_{-{\bf q}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\int d^{3}{\bf r}V^{(1)}(r)\bigg(\tilde{O}_{\bf r}^{\dagger,a}\tilde{O}^{a}_{\bf r}-\tilde{O}^{\dagger,b}_{\bf r}\sum_{\bf p}e^{i{\bf p}\cdot{\bf r}}\sqrt{2}\chi^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}_{-{\bf p}}T^{b}\psi_{\bf p}-\tilde{O}^{b}_{\bf r}\sum_{\bf q}e^{-i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}\sqrt{2}\psi^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}_{\bf q}T^{b}\chi^{\prime}_{-{\bf q}}\bigg)\,.

where
\tilde{O}^{b}={\cal U}_{v}^{ba}O^{a}
absorbs the adjoint Wilson line defined by
{\cal U}_{v}^{ba}T^{b}=U^{\dagger}_{v}T^{a}U_{v}
. Importantly, this field redefinition also modifies the power corrections to the Lagrangian as well. At NLP, the ultrasoft interactions are modified to
Fleming:2005pd

{\cal L}^{(1),us}=\frac{1}{2m}\psi_{\bf p}^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}\bigg(2{\bf p}\cdot{\bfcal E}_{us}+{\bfcal E}_{us}^{2}\bigg)\psi_{\bf p}^{\prime}+\frac{c_{F}}{2m}\psi_{\bf p}^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}\bm{\sigma}\cdot{\bfcal B}_{us}\psi^{\prime}_{\bf p}+(\psi\to\chi),

(29)

where the gauge invariant chromo-magnetic and chromo-electric fields are defined as

\displaystyle{\bfcal B}^{k}_{us}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}U_{v}^{\dagger}\epsilon^{ijk}G^{ij}_{us}U_{v},

(30)

\displaystyle{\bfcal E}_{us}^{i}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{g}U_{v}^{\dagger}({\bm{\nabla}}^{i}-g{\bf A}_{us}^{i})U_{v},

with
{\bf E}_{us}=iv\cdot\partial{\bfcal E}_{us}
. After making these field redefinitions, integrating out the quark and antiquark fields yields the interaction Lagrangian for the composite fields

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{c}^{(1)}=

\displaystyle i\frac{g}{\sqrt{2N_{c}}}\int d^{3}{\bf r}\bigg[-\frac{2}{m}[\nabla_{\bf r}\tilde{O}^{a}_{\bf r}]S_{\bf r}\cdot{\bfcal E}^{a}_{us}+(V^{(1)}(r)-V^{(8)}(r))\tilde{O}^{a}_{\bf r}S_{\bf r}{\bf r}\cdot{\bfcal E}_{us}^{a}\bigg].

(31)

After some simplification, this can be written in the form of the classic subleading pNRQCD Lagrangian
Brambilla:1999xf
;
Fleming:2005pd

{\cal L}_{p}=g\sqrt{\frac{2}{N_{c}}}\int d^{3}{\bf r}{\rm Tr}[O^{\dagger}{\bf r}\cdot{\bf E}_{us}]S_{\bf r}+h.c.+...

(32)

Notice that this field redefinition will cause Wilson lines in the soft sector as well. If one wishes to disentangle these effects, the redefinition

A_{\bf q}^{\mu}\to U_{v}^{\dagger}A_{\bf q}^{{}^{\prime}\mu}U_{v}

(33)

will leave the soft Lagrangian unchanged:

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{s}=

\displaystyle-g^{2}\sum_{q,q^{\prime},{\bf p},{\bf p}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}U_{\mu\nu}({\bf p},{\bf p}^{\prime},q,q^{\prime})\psi^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}_{{\bf p}^{\prime}}[A_{q^{\prime}}^{{}^{\prime}\mu},A_{q}^{{}^{\prime}\nu}]\psi^{\prime}_{\bf p}+(\psi\leftrightarrow\chi,\,T\leftrightarrow\bar{T})

(34)

\displaystyle-g^{2}\sum_{q,q^{\prime},{\bf p},{\bf p}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}W_{\mu\nu}({\bf p},{\bf p}^{\prime},q,q^{\prime})\psi^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}_{{\bf p}^{\prime}}\{A_{q^{\prime}}^{{}^{\prime}\mu},A_{q}^{{}^{\prime}\nu}\}\psi^{\prime}_{\bf p}+(\psi\leftrightarrow\chi,\,T\leftrightarrow\bar{T}).

It is important to note that, the field redefinitions in eq. (
27
) and (
33
) remove the leading order
A_{0}
interaction from the lowest order terms in the Lagrangian, but this interaction is not removed from the theory. Instead, it is moved into sub-leading terms in the Lagrangian and the currents involved in the matching. The utility of this, however, is that it allows the ultrasoft Hilbert space to be separated at leading order from the potential and soft sectors,

\ket{X_{Q/\bar{Q}},X_{s},X_{us}}=\ket{X_{Q/\bar{Q}},X_{s}}\otimes\ket{X_{us}}.

(35)

This is a necessary step to factorize the ultrasoft sector from the other pieces of the Lagrangian. In the section that follows, we will not be concerned with factorizing the ultrasofts, but instead, we will want to factorize the soft sector from the quarks and antiquarks, which is a considerably more challenging task.

3.3
A soft sector in pNRQCD?

The soft sector of vNRQCD contains an interaction between two soft-gluons and the quarks (or antiquarks) that also scales as
\text{v}^{5}
in the power-counting. This interaction forms a “seagull vertex” and does not experience any power suppression with respect to the kinetic term. This means it can appear at all orders in perturbation theory without any penalty. Why doesn’t this interaction appear in the pNRQCD framework?

When matching the vNRQCD framework onto pNRQCD using the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation, ref.
Fleming:2005pd
showed that the seagull interaction can indeed keep intermediate quark (or antiquark) near it’s mass shell, but it will change the three momentum of the heavy quark so that the quark/antiquark pair no longer have equal and opposite momentum. Therefore, the authors argue that the seagull vertex should not couple to the composite field and form a bound state. This is illustrated in the left panel of fig.
3
.

One exception exists when the seagull interaction transfers a net zero soft momentum to the quark or antiquark, i.e.
{\bf q}={\bf q}^{\prime}
. However, in this case the matching coefficient is divergent since the coefficient for the seagull vertex is proportional to
1/({\bf q}-{\bf q}^{\prime})^{2}
. This momentum region is identically removed from the theory as a zero-bin subtraction. Therefore, the authors of ref.
Fleming:2005pd
reason that a composite field coupling soft gluons cannot exist in the pNRQCD Lagrangian.

The true story is actually more subtle than this. Consider the case where two soft gluons also scatter off the antiquark and induce the same momentum change as on the quark. This is illustrated in the right panel of fig.

{\bf q}-{\bf q}^{\prime}
. Therefore, while one insertion of the seagull soft gluon vertex (left side of fig.

\alpha_{s}(mv)^{2}

\alpha_{s}(mv)^{2}\text{v}^{3}\sim\text{v}^{5}
and is not power suppressed with respect to the leading order diagrams in fig.
2
. This implies that soft-gluon interactions can appear at leading power in the pNRQCD framework. This is a stark contrast to the typical ideology of pNRQCD where the soft scale is completely integrated out from the theory. These interactions prevent the soft sector and composite fields from being completely decoupled.

For quarkonium production, however, there is a way out of this predicament. As discussed in sec.
2.3
, heavy
Q\bar{Q}
pairs produced at the scale
2m
are matched onto local quark-antiquark operators due to the multipole expansion. These operators are not radially separated, which is a property that can be exploited. Consider, the production matrix element which describes the transition of a local
{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}

Q\bar{Q}
pair to a vector quarkonium state. In vNRQCD, such a matrix element is given by,

\sum_{\bf q}\bra{0}\chi^{\dagger}_{-{\bf q}}\sigma^{i}\psi_{\bf q}\ket{V+X},

(36)

where other final states,
X
, will be summed over in the matrix element squared. Evaluating the matrix element in time-ordered perturbation theory with two insertions of the seagull vertex and a coupling to the color-singlet composite field yields diagrams such as fig.
4
. Using the gauge-invariant formulation of the soft interaction to simplify the representation of the gluons
Rothstein:2018dzq
, this diagram is given by the expression

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{s}(mv)^{2}}{4\sqrt{N_{c}}}\bra{0}\sigma^{i}\bigg[i\int d^{3}{\bf r}\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{1}{E+\bar{q}_{us}^{0}+p^{0}-\frac{({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}+i\epsilon}\frac{1}{E-\bar{q}_{us}^{0}-p^{0}-\frac{({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}+i\epsilon}

(37)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{E+p^{0}-\frac{{\bf p}^{2}}{2m}+i\epsilon}\frac{1}{E-p^{0}-\frac{{\bf p}^{2}}{2m}+i\epsilon}\bigg[-if^{abc}T^{c}\frac{2q^{0}}{\bar{{\bf q}}^{2}}{\bfcal E}^{a}_{\bf q}\cdot{\bfcal E}^{b}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\times\bigg[-if^{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}\bar{T}^{c_{1}}\frac{2q^{0}}{\bar{{\bf q}}^{2}}{\bfcal E}^{a^{\prime}}_{-{\bf q}}\cdot{\bfcal E}^{b^{\prime}}_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}\bigg]V^{(1)}(r)e^{i({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})\cdot{\bf r}}S_{\bf r}^{\dagger}\bigg]\ket{V+X},

where
\bar{q}=q-q^{\prime}
and

(38)

is the gauge invariant soft gluon building block in vNRQCD

.

We can integrate out the quark loops from the diagram to determine the effective coupling to the composite field. Here, there are two
p^{0}

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{s}(mv)^{2}}{4\sqrt{N_{c}}}\bra{0}\sigma^{i}i\int d^{3}{\bf r}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bf p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg[\frac{1}{E_{Q\bar{Q}}-\frac{({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{m}}\frac{1}{E-\bar{q}^{0}_{us}-\frac{({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}-\frac{{\bf p}^{2}}{2m}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{\bar{q}^{0}_{us}+\frac{({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}-\frac{{\bf p}^{2}}{2m}}+\frac{1}{E_{Q\bar{Q}}-\frac{{\bf p}^{2}}{m}}\frac{1}{E_{Q\bar{Q}}+\bar{q}_{us}^{0}-\frac{{\bf p}^{2}}{2m}-\frac{({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}}\frac{1}{-\bar{q}_{us}^{0}+\frac{{\bf p}^{2}}{2m}-\frac{({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\times\bigg[-if^{abc}T^{c}\frac{2q^{0}}{\bar{{\bf q}}^{2}}{\bfcal E}^{a}_{\bf q}\cdot{\bfcal E}^{b}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\bigg]\bigg[-if^{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}\bar{T}^{c_{1}}\frac{2q^{0}}{\bar{{\bf q}}^{2}}{\bfcal E}^{a^{\prime}}_{-{\bf q}}\cdot{\bfcal E}^{b^{\prime}}_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}\bigg]V^{(1)}(r)e^{i({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})\cdot{\bf r}}S_{\bf r}^{\dagger}\bigg]\ket{V+X}.

Here, we define
E_{Q}=E_{\bar{Q}}=E_{Q\bar{Q}}/2

({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}e^{i({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})\cdot{\bf r}}=-\nabla^{2}e^{i({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})\cdot{\bf r}}
and

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{s}(mv)^{2}}{4\sqrt{N_{c}}}\bra{0}\sigma^{i}i\int d^{3}{\bf r}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bf p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg[\frac{1}{E_{Q\bar{Q}}+\frac{\nabla^{2}}{m}}\frac{1}{E_{Q\bar{Q}}-\bar{q}^{0}_{us}+\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m}-\frac{(-i\nabla-{\bf q})^{2}}{2m}}\frac{1}{\bar{q}^{0}_{us}-\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m}+\frac{(-i\nabla-\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}}

(40)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{E_{Q\bar{Q}}-\frac{(-i\nabla-\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{m}}\frac{1}{E_{Q\bar{Q}}+\bar{q}_{us}^{0}-\frac{(-i\nabla-\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}+\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m}}\frac{1}{-\bar{q}_{us}^{0}+\frac{(-i\nabla-\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}+\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\times\bigg[-if^{abc}T^{c}\frac{2q^{0}}{\bar{{\bf q}}^{2}}{\bfcal E}^{a}_{\bf q}\cdot{\bfcal E}^{b}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\bigg]\bigg[-if^{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}\bar{T}^{c_{1}}\frac{2q^{0}}{\bar{{\bf q}}^{2}}{\bfcal E}^{a^{\prime}}_{-{\bf q}}\cdot{\bfcal E}^{b^{\prime}}_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}\bigg]V^{(1)}(r)e^{i({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})\cdot{\bf r}}S_{\bf r}^{\dagger}\bigg]\ket{V+X}.

Since this expression is now independent of
{\bf p}
, we can evaluate the
{\bf p}
integral, which sets

\displaystyle\lim_{{\bf a}\to 0}\frac{\alpha_{s}(mv)^{2}}{4\sqrt{N_{c}}}\bra{0}\sigma^{i}i\int d^{3}{\bf r}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bf p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg[\frac{1}{E_{Q\bar{Q}}+\frac{\nabla^{2}}{m}}\frac{1}{E_{Q\bar{Q}}-\bar{q}^{0}_{us}+\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m}-\frac{(-i\nabla-{\bf q})^{2}}{2m}}\frac{1}{\bar{q}^{0}_{us}-\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m}+\frac{(-i\nabla-\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}}

(41)

\bigg(E_{Q\bar{Q}}+\frac{\nabla^{2}}{m}\bigg)=V^{(1)}(r)

gives

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 0}\frac{\alpha_{s}(mv)^{2}}{4\sqrt{N_{c}}}\sum_{X_{s},X_{us}}\bra{0}\sigma^{i}i\int d^{3}{\bf r}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bf p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg[\frac{1}{V^{(1)}(r)}\frac{1}{V^{(1)}(r)-\bar{q}^{0}_{us}-\frac{-2i\nabla\cdot{\bf q}+{\bf q}^{2}}{2m}}\frac{1}{\bar{q}^{0}_{us}-\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m}+\frac{(-i\nabla-\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}}

(43)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{V^{(1)}(r)-\frac{-2i\nabla\cdot\bar{{\bf q}}+\bar{{\bf q}}^{2}}{m}}\frac{1}{V^{(1)}(r)+\bar{q}_{us}^{0}-\frac{-2i\nabla\cdot{\bf q}+\bar{{\bf q}}^{2}}{2m}}\frac{1}{-\bar{q}_{us}^{0}+\frac{(-i\nabla-\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}+\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\times\bigg[-if^{abc}T^{c}\frac{2q^{0}}{\bar{{\bf q}}^{2}}{\bfcal E}^{a}_{\bf q}\cdot{\bfcal E}^{b}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\bigg]\bigg[-if^{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}\bar{T}^{c_{1}}\frac{2q^{0}}{\bar{{\bf q}}^{2}}{\bfcal E}^{a^{\prime}}_{-{\bf q}}\cdot{\bfcal E}^{b^{\prime}}_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}\bigg]V^{(1)}(r)\delta^{(3)}({\bf r}-{\bf a})e^{i\bar{{\bf q}}\cdot{\bf r}}S_{\bf r}^{\dagger}\bigg]\ket{V+X}.

Evaluating the limit
{\bf a}\to 0
forces eq. (
43
) to 0. This is because the potential is singular at
{\bf r}=0
and we have more factors of
V(r)
in the denominator than the numerator. Therefore, we reason that all composite field interactions with soft external fields during production vanish due to the
{\bf r}=0
condition. This fact should be true at all orders in perturbation theory. So we conclude that the seagull vertex contributions do not play a role in production matrix elements. This allows the soft sector to be decoupled from the composite fields when
{\bf r}=0.

In general, for
r\neq 0
, we find that such an insertion can exist and cannot be decoupled from the composite fields. Therefore, if it can be shown that the radius deviates from zero for production, then it is not valid to neglect these seagull diagrams. For the purposes of this work, however, we rely on the
{\bf r}=0
condition and will separate the soft sector from the composite fields to “factorize” production matrix elements in the following sections.

Lastly, we comment that BPS field re-definition techniques, which sum interactions to all orders and transform away the resulting Wilson-line structures to decouple different sectors in the Lagrangian, are not applicable here because non-relativistic quark and antiquark propagators don’t eikonalize. Therefore the resulting transformation is not unitary. A thorough discussion of this interaction can be found in Appendix
A
.

Within NRQCD, quarkonium production matrix elements describe the probability for a
Q\bar{Q}
pair (with quantum numbers denoted by
n
) to transition to a final state quarkonium. In vNRQCD, however, the leading production operators are more explicit. When matching onto vNRQCD, soft gluon radiation can occur at the scale
m\text{v}
to knock the quark and antiquark far off shell
Copeland:2025vop
;
Fleming:2019pzj
;
Echevarria:2024idp
;
Echevarria:2019ynx
. Not only can this radiation generate Wilson lines, but at sub-leading power this radiation changes the quantum numbers of the heavy
Q\bar{Q}
pair. The radiation that ultimately places the pair in a color singlet generates the leading contributions for the color-octet mechanisms generated at the hard scale. Diagrammatically, this is represented by fig.
5
. The process of transitioning from a color-octet configuration to a color-singlet is encoded by vNRQCD operators where the soft fields are explicitly written with the quark and antiquark fields.

(44)

S_{v}(x)={\cal P}\exp\bigg(-ig\int_{-\infty}^{0}dsv\cdot A_{\bf q}(x+s)\bigg).

(46)

The gauge-invariant chromo-electric field is defined in eq.(

\displaystyle{\bfcal B}^{k}_{q}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}S_{v}^{\dagger}\epsilon^{ijk}G^{ij}S_{v}.

(47)

{\cal K}\cdot\Gamma

({\cal K}\cdot\Gamma)\in\{\Gamma^{a}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}},\Gamma^{\ell,a}_{{}^{3}S_{1}^{[8]}},\Gamma^{\ell j,a}_{{}^{3}P_{J}^{[8]}},...\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{a}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}}=T^{a},

(49)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\ell j,a}_{{}^{3}P_{J}^{[8]}}=\sigma^{\ell}{\bf q}^{j}T^{a}.

To find the leading contribution, we must project out the
~{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}
configuration of each operator, since this matches the quantum numbers for S-wave vector quarkonium states. Procedurally, one should substitute the vertices of eq. (
49

{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}

For an initial

\displaystyle\psi^{\dagger}_{\bf p_{Q}}P_{{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}}\big[{\cal O}_{3+4}(\Gamma^{a}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}})\big]\chi_{\bf p_{\bar{Q}}}=\frac{-g}{2N_{c}m}\psi^{\dagger}_{\bf p_{Q}}{\cal S}_{v}^{ab}\bigg[\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}c_{F}{\bfcal B}^{b}\cdot\bm{\sigma}\bigg]\chi_{\bf p_{\bar{Q}}}\,,

(50)

where

indicates that we are projecting out the color-singlet

S^{\dagger}_{v}T^{a}S_{v}={\cal S}_{v}^{ab}T^{b}.

(51)

Note we’ve included a factor of

Manohar:1999xd

In ref.

, it was conjectured that these vNRQCD operators could be related to the chromo-magnetic and chromo-electric correlators that appear from matching the LDMEs onto the pNRQCD framework
Brambilla:2022ayc
, but the exact connection was not obvious. This is because the soft scale cannot be factorized from the quark and antiquarks in the pure vNRQCD framework. In the following sections we will show that, because the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation of sec.
3
allows the soft and ultrasoft scales in the Lagrangian to be separated during production, the transition operators of eqs. (

First, consider the
{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}
color singlet LDME,
\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[1]})}
, which does not need to be matched onto an intermediate transition operator. For an S-wave vector quarkonia state,
\ket{V}
, this matrix element in vNRQCD is defined as

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[1]})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bf q}\sum_{X_{s}}\bra{0}\chi^{\dagger}_{-{\bf q}}\sigma^{i}\psi_{\bf q}\ket{V,X_{s}}\bra{V,X_{s}}\psi^{\dagger}_{\bf q}\sigma^{i}\chi_{-{\bf q}}\ket{0}.

(54)

We can match this operator onto the color-singlet composite fields by taking a time-ordered product of the color-singlet + quark/antiquark interaction from eq. (
25
). This is shown in fig.
6
.
Using the Feynman rules in fig.
1
, we find that the LDME reduces to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{X_{s}}\bra{0}\sigma^{i}\bigg[\int d^{3}{\bf r}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bf q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}S_{\bf r}^{\dagger}\frac{V^{(1)}(r)}{E_{Q\bar{Q}}-\frac{\nabla_{\bf r}^{2}}{m}}e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}\bigg]\ket{V,X_{s}}

(55)

\displaystyle\times\bra{V,X_{s}}\bigg[\int d^{3}{\bf r}^{\prime}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bf q}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}S_{{\bf r}}\frac{V^{(1)}(r^{\prime})}{E_{Q\bar{Q}}-\frac{\nabla_{{\bf r}^{\prime}}^{2}}{m}}e^{i{\bf q}^{\prime}\cdot{\bf r}^{\prime}}\bigg]\sigma^{i}\ket{0}.

where again
E_{Q}=E_{\bar{Q}}=E_{Q\bar{Q}}/2
. Now using the equations of motion for the composite field to set
V^{(1)}(r)=E_{Q\bar{Q}}-\nabla^{2}_{\bf r}/m
,
\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[1]})}
reduces to

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[1]})}=

\displaystyle N_{c}\sum_{X_{s}}\bra{0}[\sigma^{i}S_{0}^{\dagger}(x)]\ket{V,X_{s}}\bra{V,X_{s}}[S_{0}(x)\sigma^{i}]\ket{0},

(56)

where
S_{0}(x)
is the composite color singlet field evaluated at
r=0
and the Pauli matrices act on on the composite fields. Once quarks and antiquarks are completely integrated out of the theory (which happens as we go from eq. (
25
) to eq. (
26
)),
S_{0}
cannot interact with soft fields and is decoupled from the soft sector in the effective Lagrangian, for reasons described in sec.
3.3
. Therefore, we can “factorize” the Hilbert space accordingly,

\ket{V,X_{s}}=\ket{V}\otimes\ket{X_{s}},

(57)

so that the LDME becomes

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[1]})}=

\displaystyle N_{c}\sum_{X_{s}}\braket{0|X_{s}}\braket{X_{s}|0}\otimes\sum_{X_{us}}\bra{0}[\sigma^{i}S_{0}^{\dagger}(x)]\ket{V,X_{us}}\bra{V,X_{us}}[S_{0}(x)\sigma^{i}]\ket{0}\,

(58)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{c}\bra{0}[\sigma^{i}S_{0}^{\dagger}(x)]\ket{V}\bra{V}[S_{0}(x)\sigma^{i}]\ket{0}\,.

This result is rather simple since the soft matrix element evaluates to one. However the techniques used here will be very useful for studying the color-octet LDMEs in the following sections.

The remaining matrix elements are related to the vector quarkonium’s radial wave function at the origin since the composite field is evaluated at
r=0

Bodwin:1994jh
,

\bra{0}[\sigma^{i}S_{0}^{\dagger}(x)]\ket{V}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi}}R_{V}^{(0)}(0).

(59)

Thus, eq. (
58
) reproduces the classic NRQCD prediction
Bodwin:1994jh
;
Brambilla:2022rjd
,

\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[1]})}=\frac{3N_{c}}{2\pi}|R_{V}^{(0)}(0)|^{2},

(60)

where the factor of 3 comes from summing over polarizations.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4N_{c}M^{2}}\bra{0}\bigg[{\cal S}_{v}^{\dagger,ag}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}\bigg(d^{gbc}g^{2}{\bfcal E}^{b}\cdot{\bfcal E}^{c}\bigg)\bigg][{\bm{\sigma}}^{j}S^{\dagger}_{0}(x)]\ket{V,X_{s},X_{us}}

(67)

\displaystyle\times\bra{V,X_{s}}[S_{0}(x){\bm{\sigma}}^{j}]\bigg[{\cal S}_{v}^{ag^{\prime}}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}\bigg(d^{g^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}g^{2}{\bfcal E}^{b^{\prime}}\cdot{\bfcal E}^{c^{\prime}}\bigg)\bigg]\ket{0}.

After separating the Hilbert spaces, we find

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[8]})}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4N_{c}M^{2}}\bra{0}[{\bm{\sigma}}^{j}S^{\dagger}_{0}(x)]\ket{V}\bra{V}[S_{0}(x){\bm{\sigma}^{j}}]\ket{0}

(68)

\displaystyle\times\bra{0}\bigg[{\cal S}_{v}^{ag}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}\bigg(d^{gbc}g^{2}{\bfcal E}^{b}\cdot{\bfcal E}^{c}\bigg)\bigg]^{2}\ket{0}.

Defining the double electric field correlator,

\braket{{\bfcal{EE}}}=\bra{0}\Bigg|\frac{1}{2}{\cal S}_{v}^{ag}\bigg[\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}d^{gbc}g^{2}{\bfcal E}^{b}\cdot{\bfcal E}^{c}\bigg]\Bigg|^{2}(x)\ket{0},

(69)

we can write this in the compact form,

\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[8]})}=\frac{1}{N_{c}M^{2}}\braket{{\bfcal{EE}}}\frac{3|R_{V}(0)|^{2}}{2\pi}(1+{\cal O}(\text{v}^{2}))

(70)

which agrees with the pNRQCD result
Brambilla:2022ayc
.

4.4
P-wave matrix elements

To factorize the matrix elements for quark-antiquark pairs produced in P-wave configurations we will need to be slightly more careful. In traditional NRQCD, the
\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}P_{J}^{[8]})}
contributions are determined by matching onto operators of the form

\psi^{\dagger}{\bf q}^{i}\sigma^{j}T^{a}\chi,

(71)

where
{\bf q}
is the relative three-momentum between the
Q\bar{Q}
when they are produced at the scale
\mu\sim 2m
, which has soft scaling
{\bf q}\sim m\text{v}
. Often, this operator is written with a covariant derivative instead

\psi^{\dagger}\frac{i}{2}\overleftrightarrow{{\bf D}}^{i}\sigma^{j}T^{a}\chi.

(72)

When matching onto vNRQCD, the
Q\bar{Q}
radiates soft gluons, which changes the relative three momentum of the
Q\bar{Q}
, so that the initial
{\bf q}
can be decomposed into two pieces

{\bf q}=\sum{\bf p}_{s,i}+{\bf q}^{\prime},

(73)

where
{\bf p}_{s,i}
is the three momentum of each soft gluon, and
{\bf q}^{\prime}
is the relative momentum of the final
Q\bar{Q^{\prime}}.
Therefore, the NRQCD P-wave operator can be matched onto the following object in vNRQCD,

\psi^{\dagger}{\bf q}^{i}\sigma^{j}T^{a}\chi=\sum_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}[\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}S_{v}^{\dagger}(\frac{1}{2}\overleftrightarrow{{\bfcal P}}^{i}-g{\bf A}_{k}^{i})\sigma^{j}T^{a}S_{v}\chi_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}],

(74)

where
\overleftrightarrow{{\bfcal P}}={\bfcal P}-{\bfcal P}^{\dagger}
. Note that this operator has three contributions,

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\bigg(\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}\overleftrightarrow{{\bfcal P}}^{i}\sigma^{j}\chi_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}[{\cal S}_{v}^{ab}T^{b}]+\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}[S_{v}^{\dagger}({\bfcal P}^{i}-g{\bf A}_{k}^{i})S_{v}][{\cal S}_{v}^{ab}T^{b}]\sigma^{j}\chi_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}

(75)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}[{\bfcal P}^{i}{\cal S}_{v}^{ab}T^{b}]\sigma^{j}\chi_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}\bigg),

where we have used
S_{v}^{\dagger}S=1
and the brackets indicate which fields the projector can act on. The first term is proportional to the relative momentum of the
Q\bar{Q}
after soft radiation. This term is still in a genuine P-wave state after matching onto vNRQCD and is similar to the operators written down in ref.
Fleming:2019pzj
, which were used to study P-wave quarkonium decays in the TMD formalism.

The last two operators project out the momentum from the soft Wilson lines. Importantly, we can identify the second term as being proportional to the chromo-electric field using eq. (
38
)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}[S_{v}^{\dagger}({\bfcal P}^{i}-g{\bf A}_{k}^{i})S_{v}][{\cal S}_{v}^{ab}T^{b}]\sigma^{j}\chi_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}=\sum_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}(g{\bfcal E}_{k}^{i})[{\cal S}_{v}^{ab}T^{b}]\sigma^{j}\chi_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}.

(76)

This contribution is leading in the
v
power-counting with respect to the other contributions in eq. (
75
) because it can place the state directly in a
{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}
. Projecting out this component gives the P-wave transition operator,

\sum_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}P_{{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}}\bigg[[g{\bfcal E}_{k}^{i}]{\cal S}_{v}^{ab}T^{b}\sigma^{j}\bigg]\chi_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2N_{c}}\sum_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\big[{\cal S}_{v}^{ab}g{\bfcal E}_{k}^{i,b}\big]\sigma^{j}\chi_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}.

(77)

Hence, no further transitions are necessary and it will provide the leading, at least in theory, contribution to S-wave vector quarkonium production. The direct relation of the P-wave operator to a chromo-electric field operator was also observed by ref.
Brambilla:2022ayc
when matching onto pNRQCD.

Note, however, that the first term in eq. (
75
) is the only true P-wave operator at the soft scale. Therefore, there is potentially some ambiguity in the matching, and it could be the case that this contribution is the correct operator to match the
\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}P_{J}^{[8]})}
onto. To contribute to S-wave vector quarkonium production, this operator will need additional chromo-electric field transitions to be placed in a
{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}
state. We find the final contribution for the “genuine P-wave operator” to be

\displaystyle\frac{g}{2m}\sum_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}P_{{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}}\big[\{S_{v}^{\dagger}{\bf q}^{{}^{\prime}j}\sigma^{i}T^{a}S_{v},\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}{\bf q}^{\prime}\cdot{\bfcal E}\}\big]\chi_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}

(78)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\frac{g}{2N_{c}m}\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\sigma^{i}{\bf q}^{{}^{\prime}j}{\bf q}^{{}^{\prime}k}\bigg[{\cal S}_{v}^{ab}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}{\bfcal E}^{k,b}\bigg]\chi_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}.

where the derivation is given in appendix
C
.

5
Discussion and applications

The purpose of this work was to show that the soft scale can be factorized from the other sectors in quarkonium production matrix elements in the vNRQCD framework which keeps soft degrees of freedom explicit Using the pNRQCD formulation, ref.
Brambilla:2022ayc
showed that important relations exist between the NRQCD production matrix elements for different S-wave quarkonium states. Here we discuss these relations in the context of the vNRQCD approach and highlight key differences in our findings.

5.1
Universality of the S-wave vector quarkonium LDMEs

First, we note that the chromo-electric and chromo-magnetic field correlators in the precious section are completely universal. That is, they are independent of state and are universally defined, regardless of the S-wave vector quarkonium state being measured. Therefore, taking ratios of the LDMEs between two different vector quarkonium states,
V
and
V^{\prime}
produces powerful constraints on the color-octet matrix elements,

\displaystyle\braket{O^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[8]})}(\mu)=\frac{m_{Q^{\prime}}^{2}}{m_{Q}^{2}}\frac{|R_{V}(0)|^{2}}{|R_{V^{\prime}}(0)|^{2}}\braket{O^{V^{\prime}}(^{3}S_{1}^{[8]})}(\mu),

(87)

\displaystyle\braket{O^{V}(^{3}P_{J}^{[8]})}=\frac{\big|R_{V}(0)\big|^{2}}{\big|R_{V^{\prime}}(0)\big|^{2}}\braket{O^{V^{\prime}}(^{3}P_{J}^{[8]})},

\displaystyle\braket{O^{V}(^{1}S_{0}^{[8]})}=\frac{m_{Q^{\prime}}^{2}}{m_{Q}^{2}}\frac{c_{F}^{2}(m_{Q},\mu)}{c_{F}^{2}(m_{Q^{\prime}},\mu)}\frac{|R_{V}(0)|^{2}}{|R_{V^{\prime}}(0)|^{2}}\braket{O^{V^{\prime}}(^{1}S_{0}^{[8]})},

where the running of the matrix elements was derived in ref.
Brambilla:2022ayc
. These ratios agree exactly with the relationships first derived using pNRQCD
Brambilla:2022ayc
;
Brambilla:2022rjd
. Importantly, if one considers instead the “genuine P-wave contribution” from eq. (
85
), then the analogous relation between the matrix elements of different states would be given by

\braket{O^{V}(^{3}P_{J}^{[8]})}^{(1)}=\bigg(\frac{m_{Q}\braket{\text{v}^{2}}_{V}}{m_{Q^{\prime}}\braket{\text{v}^{2}}_{V^{\prime}}}\bigg)^{2}\frac{\big|R_{V}(0)\big|^{2}}{\big|R_{V^{\prime}}(0)\big|^{2}}\braket{O^{V^{\prime}}(^{3}P_{J}^{[8]})}^{(1)}.

(88)

The connections between LDMEs in eqs. (
87
) and (
88
) are quite remarkable. In essence, they reduce the number of non-perturbative parameters for S-wave vector quarkonium production from 12 to 3. These results have already been exploited to some degree. Using matrix elements determined from a global analysis of
J/\psi
production data in order to determine
\Upsilon(3S)
data
Brambilla:2024iqg
, where good agreement with the data is found. In future studies, these relations can be used to more precisely constrain the LDMEs of all S-wave vector quarkonium states as the number of free parameters are greatly reduced and the number of available measurements grows. Such an analysis should further significantly reduce the uncertainties on the quarkonium production matrix elements, such as those extracted via global analysis in table
1
.

5.2
Velocity scaling of the LDMEs

Since soft gluons and soft momenta scale homogeneously as
m\text{v}
, we can power count the matrix elements and vacuum correlators to estimate the importance of each production mechanism.
First, we can start with
\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[1]})}
since it is leading in the power counting and is known to scale as

\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[1]})}\sim\text{v}^{3}.

(89)

All factors of the wave function at the origin also scale as

|R_{V^{\prime}}(0)|^{2}\sim\text{v}^{3}.

(90)

Now, for the color-octet production matrix elements, we need to estimate the size of the chromo-magnetic, chromo-electric, and double chromo-electric vacuum correlators. In the vNRQCD power counting,

\displaystyle\braket{{\bfcal B}}

\displaystyle\sim\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})\text{v}^{2},

(91)

\displaystyle\braket{{\bfcal{EE}}}

\displaystyle\sim\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})^{2}\text{v}^{2},

\displaystyle\braket{{{\bfcal E}}}

\displaystyle\sim\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})\text{v}^{2}.

Using the defining assumption that
\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})\sim\text{v}
in Coulombic systems, we find that the
\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{1}S_{0}^{[8]})}
and
\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[8]})}
LDMEs scale as
\text{v}^{6}
and
\text{v}^{7}
, respectively,

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{1}S_{0}^{[8]})}\propto\braket{{\bfcal B}}|R_{V}(0)|^{2}\sim\text{v}^{6},

(92)

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[8]})}\propto\braket{{{\bfcal{EE}}}}|R_{V}(0)|^{2}\sim\text{v}^{7}

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}P_{J}^{[8]})}\propto\braket{{{\bfcal E}}}|R_{V}(0)|^{2}\sim\text{v}^{6}

\big|\overline{\nabla^{2}R}_{V}^{(0)}(0)\big|^{2}\sim\braket{\text{v}}_{V}^{4}|R_{V}(0)|^{2}\sim\text{v}^{7}.

(93)

5.3
Ultrasoft transitions in the matrix elements

\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[8]})}

For the
\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}S_{1}^{[8]})}

Q\bar{Q}

{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}

\frac{1}{2m}\tilde{\psi}_{\bf p}^{\dagger}\tilde{{\bfcal E}}_{us}^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{\bf p}^{\prime}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{us}^{4}}{N_{c}}\lim_{{\bf a}\to 0}\sum_{X_{s}}\bra{0}\sigma^{i}\bigg[S_{\bf r}^{\dagger}\frac{1}{V^{(1)}(r)+\bar{p}^{0}}{\bfcal E}_{us}^{2}\bigg]_{{\bf r}={\bf a}}\ket{V,X_{s}}

\displaystyle\times\bra{V,X_{s}}\bigg[{\bfcal E}_{us}^{2}\frac{1}{V^{(1)}(r^{\prime})+\bar{p}^{0}}S_{{\bf r}^{\prime}}\bigg]_{{\bf r}^{\prime}={\bf a}}\sigma^{i}\ket{0},

where we have evaluated the integral using the same techniques and employed the equation of motion for the color singlet field,

\bigg(E_{Q\bar{Q}}-\frac{\nabla_{\bf r}^{2}}{m}\bigg)S_{\bf r}=V^{(1)}(r)S_{\bf r}.

(96)

As we saw in sec.
3.3
when considering a soft seagull vertex on the quark loop, evaluating the limit
{\bf a}\to 0
forces eq. (
95
) to 0 because the potential is singular at
{\bf r}=0
. This is a direct consequence of the LDMEs being defined as local operators with no radial separation, as we discussed in sec.
2.3
. This same sequence occurs for all other ultrasoft transitions because the quarks and antiquark fields will always stay near their mass shell and propagate before the interaction. The zero-radial separation from the production operator will always kill these diagrams. Therefore, we argue that ultrasoft transitions can be neglected. Higher order terms in the multipole expansion of eq. (
14
) will allow for operators with non-zero radial separation, but these contributions will be power-suppressed.

Formally, the matrix element in eq. (
95
) is non-perturbative and its evaluation should not be taken too seriously. It is possible that non-perturbative effects will generate new scales in the matrix element and shift the radial separation to a non-zero value. Hence it may still be relevant. However, even still, ultrasoft transitions should be sub-leading because they occur at later time-scales than the soft transitions considered throughout this paper. That is, ultrasoft transitions will occur at time-scales
\tau_{us}\sim 1/m\text{v}^{2}
while the soft transitions occur at times
\tau_{s}\sim 1/m\text{v}
. If the assumption that the soft and ultrasoft scales are widely separated is correct,
m\text{v}\gg m\text{v}^{2}
, then
\tau_{s}\ll\tau_{us}
so the time-ordering of the matrix elements will guarantee that the soft transition will occur first. These transitions will place the state in a
{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}
configuration. Further ultra-soft transitions will be unnecessary and power-suppressed.

5.4
Applications to the TMD framework

In the TMD framework, the production matrix operators have been identified to be not the LDMEs, but TMD Shape Functions (TMDShFs)
Echevarria:2024idp
and TMD Soft Transition Functions (TMDSTFs)
Copeland:2025vop
. TMDSTFs were identified to be leading in the vNRQCD power-counting. Therefore, we will apply the factorization techniques developed in this paper to the
{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}
TMDSTF of ref.
Copeland:2025vop
to address quarkonium production in SIDIS in the TMD framework. At leading order, the gauge invariant TMDSTF is given by

\displaystyle T^{V}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}}({\bf b}_{T};\mu,\eta)=

(97)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M^{2}N_{c}^{2}\sqrt{S({\bf b}_{T})}}\sumint_{X_{s}}\bra{0}\big[{\cal S}_{v}^{\dagger,bc}{\cal S}_{n}^{\dagger ba}\chi^{\dagger}_{\bf p_{\bar{Q}}}\bigg[\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}gc_{F}{\bm{\sigma}\cdot{\bfcal B}}_{s}^{c}\bigg]\psi_{\bf p_{Q}}\big]({\bf b}_{T})\ket{V,X_{s}}

\displaystyle\times\bra{V,X_{s}}\big[{\cal S}_{n}^{b^{\prime}a}{\cal S}_{v}^{b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}\psi^{\dagger}_{\bf p_{Q}}\bigg[\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}gc_{F}{\bm{\sigma}\cdot{\bfcal B}}_{s}^{c^{\prime}}\bigg]\psi_{\bf p_{Q}}](0)\ket{0}.

Note that there is a difference from ref.
Copeland:2025vop
by a factor of 4 in the overall normalization, which we have absorbed into definition of the chromo-magnetic gluon field. Importantly, the TMDSTF is process dependent because it contains soft radiation from the initial state in the form of
n
-directional Wilson lines,
{\cal S}_{n}
. This is a consequence of the cross section factorization occurring for
Q\sim M_{V}
, since in this regime the soft-collinear effective theory
Bauer:2000yr
soft scale is parametrically similar to the vNRQCD soft scale
{\bf q}_{T}\sim m\text{v}
. It is also because the matrix element is defined in vNRQCD where the soft scale interacts with the potential sector of the quarks and antiquarks. Therefore, they cannot be disentangled from each other.

Invoking the composite field techniques of sec.
3.3
, we can integrate out the quarks/antiquarks and re-write the matrix element as

\displaystyle T^{V}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}}({\bf b}_{T};\mu,\eta)=

(98)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M^{2}N_{c}\sqrt{S({\bf b}_{T})}}\sumint_{X_{s}}\bra{0}\bigg[{\cal S}_{v}^{\dagger,bc}{\cal S}_{n}^{\dagger ba}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}gc_{F}{\bm{\sigma}\cdot{\bfcal B}}_{s}^{c}\bigg]^{i}({\bf b}_{T})\big[\sigma^{i}S^{\dagger}_{0}({\bf b}_{T})\big]\ket{V,X_{s}}

\displaystyle\times\bra{V,X_{s}}\bigg[{\cal S}_{n}^{b^{\prime}a}{\cal S}_{v}^{b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}gc_{F}{\bm{\sigma}\cdot{\bfcal B}}_{s}^{c^{\prime}}\bigg]^{j}(0)\big[\sigma^{j}S_{0}(0)\big]\ket{0}.

Once the quark and antiquark fields are integrated out, we know the composite field and soft Hilbert spaces factorize as in eq. (
57
), so the TMDSTF reduces to

\displaystyle T^{V}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}}({\bf b}_{T};\mu,\eta)=

(99)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M^{2}N_{c}\sqrt{S({\bf b}_{T})}}\bra{0}\bigg[{\cal S}_{v}^{\dagger,bc}{\cal S}_{n}^{\dagger ba}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}gc_{F}{\bm{\sigma}\cdot{\bfcal B}}_{s}^{c}\bigg]^{i}({\bf b}_{T})\bigg[{\cal S}_{n}^{b^{\prime}a}{\cal S}_{v}^{b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}gc_{F}{\bm{\sigma}\cdot{\bfcal B}}_{s}^{c^{\prime}}\bigg]^{j}(0)\ket{0}

\displaystyle\times\bra{0}\big[\sigma^{i}S^{\dagger}_{0}({\bf b}_{T})\big]\ket{V}\bra{V}\big[\sigma^{j}S_{0}(0)\big]\ket{0}.

Since the composite field only carries ultrasoft momentum we can multi-pole expand away it’s dependence on
{\bf b}_{T}
,

S_{0}({\bf b}_{T})\approx S_{0}(0)+{\cal O}({\bf b}_{T}m\text{v}^{2}).

(100)

After averaging over spins and summing over polarizations, we can write the TMDSTF in a simplified form,

T^{V}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}}({\bf b}_{T};\mu,\eta)=\frac{1}{3N_{c}M^{2}}\braket{{{\bfcal B}_{n}}({\bf b}_{T};\mu,\eta)}\frac{3|R_{V}(0)|^{2}}{2\pi},

(101)

where

\displaystyle\braket{{{\bfcal B}_{n}}({\bf b}_{T};\mu,\eta)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{S({\bf b}_{T})}}{{\rm Tr}_{c}}\bra{0}\bigg[{\cal S}_{v}^{\dagger,bc}{\cal S}_{n}^{\dagger ba}g\frac{c_{F}}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}{{\bfcal B}}_{s}^{c}\bigg]^{i}({\bf b}_{T})\bigg[\bigg[{\cal S}_{n}^{b^{\prime}a}{\cal S}_{v}^{b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}g\frac{c_{F}}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}{{\bfcal B}}_{s}^{a}\bigg]^{i}(0)\ket{0}

(102)

is the process-dependent chromo-magnetic vacuum correlator.

The factorized expression in eq. (
101
) has two distinct advantages over the initial definition in eq. (
97
). First, all of the
{\bf b}_{T}
dependence is encoded in the vacuum matrix element
\braket{{{\bfcal B}_{n}}({\bf b}_{T};\mu,\eta)}
, which, while still non-peturbative, can in principle be computed using lattice QCD. Second, the vacuum correlator
\braket{{{\bfcal B}_{n}}({\bf b}_{T};\mu,\eta)}
is
universal
. This implies that the following relationship for the TMDSTFs of different quarkonium states

T^{V}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}}({\bf b}_{T};\mu,\eta)=\frac{m_{Q^{\prime}}^{2}}{m_{Q}^{2}}\frac{c_{F}^{2}(m_{Q},\mu)}{c_{F}^{2}(m_{Q^{\prime}},\mu)}\frac{|R_{V}(0)|^{2}}{|R_{V^{\prime}}(0)|^{2}}T^{V^{\prime}}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}}({\bf b}_{T};\mu,\eta).

(103)

where we have evolved the TMDSTFs for
V
and
V^{\prime}
to the same scale. This is remarkably useful because, while the TMDSTFs are not process-independent, the relationship in eq. (
103
) implies a
state-independence
for quarkonium production in SIDIS at small
{\bf q}_{T}
. Consequently, the TMDSTFs for different quarkonium states can be related to each other, dramatically improving the predictive power of the TMD framework. For example, if one measured
\Upsilon(1S)
production at the EIC, then in principle the TMDSTFs for
J/\psi
produced at the EIC will be constrained. Therefore the only free parameter in a TMD factorization of the
e+p\to J/\psi+X
cross section, would be the gluon TMDs - thereby enabling a clean extraction.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigated the similarities and differences between vNRQCD and pNRQCD with regard to the factorization of LDMEs in quarkonium production.
Our analysis verified
Brambilla:2022ayc
and derived powerful new factorization theorems for quarkonium production matrix elements in NRQCD. To accomplish this, we used a Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation on the vNRQCD Lagrangian and argued that the soft sector decouples during production. We discussed how the color-octet LDMEs can be matched onto “transition operators” which mediate non-perturbative the color-octet to color-singlet transition via soft gluon radiation in the vNRQCD framework. We then employed the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation to factorize each of the color-octet LDMEs in terms of the wave function at the origin and a vacuum correlator of either chromo-electric or chromo-magnetic fields. Our analysis shows that these results permit equivalence relations between the color-octet LDMEs for different S-wave vector quarkonium LDMEs, first derived in ref.
Brambilla:2022ayc
. Additionally, we find novel operator contributions to the color-octet P-wave mechanism and argue that these may be relevant for explaining discrepancies between data and NRQCD predictions at small
p_{T}
. We further power-counted the fields in the vacuum correlators and found that the LDMEs obey a slightly different scaling than what is expected in traditional NRQCD. We discussed potential ultrasoft power corrections to the results derived here and provide arguments as to why these may be negligible. Finally, we apply these techniques to the recently derived TMDSTFs, which are the dominant operators for quarkonium production in SIDIS in the TMD framework
Copeland:2025vop
. The factorized results for the TMDSTFs are perhaps even more critical than those derived for the LDMEs, because the TMDSTFs are process dependent and hence have a limited predictive ability on their own. However, using the techniques in this paper, we find a new state-independence property for the TMDSTFs in SIDIS, increasing the ability to constrain these non-perturbative production matrix elements for quarkonium production in the TMD framework.

Moving forward these results can be used to constrain quarkonium production LDMEs in future analyses. Similar to what was done in ref.
Brambilla:2024iqg
, a global analysis of all S-wave vector quarkonium production data should be performed to constrain these universal matrix elements and the chromo-electric and chromo-magnetic field correlators. In this analysis, it will be useful to include
1/p_{T}^{2}
power corrections for hadroproduction in the collinear factorization framework, which occur in the form of double parton fragmentation functions
Fleming:2013qu
;
Lee:2021oqr
;
Kang:2014tta
;
Kang:2011mg
. Additionally, it may be necessary to explore additional power corrections in the
v
expansion for the quarkonium production operators since it is known that these can be large for charmonium
Bodwin:1994jh
;
Braaten:2002fi
.

At small
p_{T}
, there are many open questions about quarkonium production in the TMD framework. The
{}^{3}S_{1}^{[8]}
and
{}^{3}P_{J}^{[8]}
sub-leading color-octet TMDSTFs
Copeland:2025vop
still need to be derived for SIDIS and their TMD evolution kernels need to be computed. Moreover, the correct TMDSTFs and TMD factorization theorems should be derived to study quarkonium production at small
p_{T}
in other processes, such as
e^{+}e^{-}
. The results from this paper will help to determine the appropriate non-perturbative production operators for the TMD formalism, which will be a critical effort if the gluon TMD PDFs in the proton are to be properly constrained.

Lastly, a number of theoretical models have been proposed to address the modification of quarkonium cross sections in hot and cold QCD matter, see
Brambilla:2010cs
;
Andronic:2015wma
;
Rothkopf:2019ipj
;
Boer:2024ylx
;
Arleo:2025oos
and references therein for discussion in the context of current and future measurements.
At the fundamental level, interactions with a nuclear medium have been incorporated in the NRQCD Lagrangian via off-shell Glauber and Coulomb gluon exchanges
Makris:2019kap
;
Makris:2019ttx
and the leading and subleading corrections to vacuum dynamics were obtained for different momentum scaling of the medium quasi-particles. Phenomenological applications that take as input the dissociation of quarkonia based on broadening of the
Q\bar{Q}
state in matter due to collisional interactions have shown promise in describing the hierarchy of nuclear attenuation of ground and excited
J/\psi
and
\Upsilon
states at intermediate and high transverse momenta/energies
Sharma:2012dy
;
Aronson:2017ymv
;
Boer:2024ylx
.
This work opens new avenues to extend the first principles treatment of quarkonium production in reactions with nuclei to small transverse momenta and to address transitions between states based on microscopic dynamics.

All of these directions should be explored in future work.

Appendix A
Re-summing soft interactions in the vNRQCD Lagrangian

A.1
The
W_{AA}
transformation

In section
3.3
, we discuss a potential soft interaction with the composite fields, and argue that it vanishes for quarkonium production. However, this interaction could in principle contribute for processes with
r\neq 0
and soft final states. In this appendix, we discuss this interaction further.

At leading order, the interaction of a quark field with two soft gluons generates the amplitude in the non-relativistic limit,

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})\frac{1}{p^{0}+\bar{q}_{us}-\frac{({\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}}\frac{i}{2}f^{abc}T^{c}U^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}({\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime})\epsilon^{\mu,a}({\bf q})\epsilon^{\nu,b}({\bf q}^{\prime})\sqrt{2m}\xi

(104)

\displaystyle\to\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})\sum_{{\bf p},{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}\frac{i}{2}f^{abc}T^{c}U^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}({\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime})A_{\bf q}^{\mu,a}A_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}^{\nu,b}\frac{1}{i\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{0}}-\frac{\overleftrightarrow{{\bfcal P}}^{2}}{2m}}\psi_{\bf p},

where
\bar{{\bf q}}=({\bf q}-{\bf q}^{\prime})
and the leading order coefficients,
U^{(0),ij}
, can be found in ref.
Manohar:1999xd
. Here, we define
\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{0}}=i\overrightarrow{\partial}_{0}-i\overleftarrow{\partial_{0}}
and
\overleftrightarrow{\bfcal P}={\bfcal P}-{\bfcal P}^{\dagger}
. In the second line we have replaced the polarization vectors and spinors with the gluon and quark fields respectively, since this operator reproduces the interaction in the first line of eq. (
104
). Even though this contribution appears at
{\cal O}(\alpha_{s})
, the contribution multiplying the quark scales as
\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})/\text{v}\sim 1
overall and hence is not suppressed. This means we can repeat the interaction an arbitrary number of times without penalty. Since gluons are bosons, we can sum over all permutations of the two gluon vertex and average over the number of insertions. This generates the all-orders structure

\sum_{k}\sum_{\rm perm.}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})}{2}\bigg)^{k}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{{\bf q}_{j},{\bf q}_{j}^{\prime}}A_{{\bf q}_{j}}^{\mu_{j},a_{j}}A_{{\bf q}^{\prime}_{j}}^{\nu_{j},b_{j}}\frac{if^{a_{j}b_{j}c_{j}}T^{c_{j}}U^{(0)}_{\mu_{j}\nu_{j}}({\bf q}_{j},{\bf q}^{\prime}_{j})}{i\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{0}}-\frac{\overleftrightarrow{{\bfcal P}}^{2}}{2m}}\bigg)\psi_{\bf p}.

(105)

Define the combination that appears,

W_{AA}=\sum_{k}\sum_{\rm perm.}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})}{2}\bigg)^{k}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{{\bf q}_{j},{\bf q}_{j}^{\prime}}A_{{\bf q}_{j}}^{\mu_{j},a_{j}}A_{{\bf q}^{\prime}_{j}}^{\nu_{j},b_{j}}\frac{if^{a_{j}b_{j}c_{j}}T^{c_{j}}U^{(0)}_{\mu_{j}\nu_{j}}({\bf q}_{j},{\bf q}^{\prime}_{j})}{i\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{0}}-\frac{\overleftrightarrow{{\bfcal P}}^{2}}{2m}}\bigg),

(106)

as the operator encoding the all orders insertion of the double soft vertex. The
W_{AA}
structure exponentiates

\displaystyle W_{AA}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\rm perm.}\exp\Bigg[\sum_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}\frac{i\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})}{2}f^{abc}T^{c}U^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}({\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime})A_{{\bf q}}^{\mu,a}A_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}^{\nu,b}\frac{1}{i\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{0}}-\frac{(\bfcal{P}-\bfcal{P}^{\dagger})^{2}}{2m}}\Bigg]

(107)

and satisfies the properties

\displaystyle i{\partial}_{\mu}W_{AA}{\cal O}=\frac{\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})}{2}\sum_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle A_{\bf q}^{\mu,a}A_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}^{\nu,b}\frac{if^{abc}T^{c}U^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}({\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime})}{i\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{0}}-\frac{\overleftrightarrow{{\bfcal P}}^{2}}{2m}}W_{AA}i\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{\mu}}{\cal O},

(108)

\displaystyle f({\cal P}^{\mu})W_{AA}{\cal O}=\frac{\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})}{2}\sum_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle A_{\bf q}^{\mu,a}A_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}^{\nu,b}\frac{if^{abc}T^{c}U^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}({\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime})}{i\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{0}}-\frac{\overleftrightarrow{{\bfcal P}}^{2}}{2m}}W_{AA}f(\overleftrightarrow{{\cal P}}^{\mu}){\cal O},

where
f({\cal P}^{\mu})
is some generic function of the projector. See the following section for more details regarding these properties. Likewise, for the antiquark field we that notice that an interaction with the double gluon vertex gives the amplitude

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})\frac{1}{-p^{0}+\bar{q}^{0}_{us}-\frac{(-{\bf p}+\bar{{\bf q}})^{2}}{2m}}\frac{i}{2}f^{abc}T^{c}U^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}({\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime})\epsilon^{\mu,a}({\bf q})\epsilon^{\nu,b}({\bf q}^{\prime})\sqrt{2m}\eta

(109)

\displaystyle\equiv\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})\sum_{{\bf p},{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}\frac{i}{2}f^{abc}T^{c}U^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}({\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime})A_{\bf q}^{\mu,a}A_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}^{\nu,b}\frac{1}{i\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{0}}-\frac{(\bfcal{P}-\bfcal{P}^{\dagger})^{2}}{2m}}\chi_{-{\bf p}}.

So, after inserting an arbitrary number of these interactions and summing over all permutations, we see the antiquark also gets a factor of
W_{AA}
.

Importantly, we find that the structure
W_{AA}
is not a unitary operator (see app.
A.2
). That is,

W_{AA}^{\dagger}W_{AA}\neq 1.

(110)

This is in stark contrast to Wilson lines, which are necessarily unitary operators. The non-unitarity of
W_{AA}
occurs because soft-interactions change the label momentum of the quark fields and the non-relativistic quark propagators do not eikonalize.
This means that we cannot field redefine this interaction away. In other words, BPS-type field redefinitions, as in sec.
3
, do not work here.

To show what we mean, consider the following field redefinition

\psi_{\bf p}\to W_{AA}\tilde{\psi}_{\bf p},~~~~~~~~~\chi_{-{\bf p}}\to W_{AA}\tilde{\chi}_{-{\bf p}}.

(111)

Under this transformation, the kinetic term in the vNRQCD Lagrangian becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{\bf p}\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\bf p}W_{AA}^{\dagger}\bigg(i\partial_{0}-\frac{\bfcal{P}^{2}}{2m}\bigg)W_{AA}\tilde{\psi}_{\bf p}.

(112)

Using the properties in eq. (
108
), we can combine this with the leading order double soft gluon interaction and that the combination reduces to

\displaystyle\sum_{\bf p}\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\bf p}W_{AA}^{\dagger}\bigg(i\partial_{0}-\frac{\bfcal{P}^{2}}{2m}-\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})\sum_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}\frac{i}{2}f^{abc}T^{c}U^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}({\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime})A_{\bf q}^{\mu,a}A_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}^{\nu,b}\bigg)W_{AA}\tilde{\psi}_{\bf p}

(113)

\displaystyle=

In this section we illustrate why the

\displaystyle(W_{AA})^{\dagger}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\rm perm.}\exp\Bigg[\sum_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}\frac{i\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})}{2}f^{abc}T^{c}U^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}({\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime})\frac{1}{-i\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{0}}-\frac{(\bfcal{P}^{\dagger}-\bfcal{P})^{2}}{2m}}A_{{\bf q}}^{\mu,a}A_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}^{\nu,b}\bigg].

\displaystyle W_{AA}\tilde{\psi}_{\bf p}

(118)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rm perm.}\exp\Bigg[\sum_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}\frac{i\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})}{2}f^{abc}T^{c}U^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}({\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime})A_{{\bf q}}^{\mu,a}A_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}^{\nu,b}\frac{1}{\bar{q}_{us}^{0}-\frac{2\bar{{\bf q}}\cdot{\bf p}+{\bf q}^{2}}{2m}}\Bigg]\tilde{\psi}_{\bf p},

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\bf p}W_{AA}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rm perm.}\tilde{\psi}_{\bf p}^{\dagger}\exp\Bigg[\sum_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}\frac{-i\alpha_{s}(m\text{v})}{2}f^{abc}T^{c}U^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}({\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime})A_{{\bf q}}^{\mu,a}A_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}^{\nu,b}\frac{1}{-\bar{q}_{us}^{0}-\frac{-2\bar{{\bf q}}\cdot{\bf p}+{\bf q}^{2}}{2m}}\bigg],

E-{\bf p}^{2}/(2m)=0

\displaystyle\sum_{\bf p}\big[\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\bf p}W_{AA}^{\dagger}\big]\big[W_{AA}\tilde{\psi}_{\bf p}\big]\neq\sum_{\bf p}\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\bf p}\tilde{\psi}_{\bf p},

Interestingly, the non-unitarity can be traced to the fact that the propagator in the argument of
W_{AA}
does not eikonalize. To see what we mean, compare with a normal time-like Wilson line,

W_{v}=\sum_{\rm perm}\exp\bigg[\sum_{\bf q}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\cal P}}A_{\bf q}\bigg]

(120)

Notice, under a dagger, the factor in the argument of the exponential becomes,

\bigg(\sum_{q}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\cal P}}A^{0}_{q}\bigg)^{\dagger}=\sum_{q}\frac{1}{q^{0}}A_{-q}^{0}=\sum_{q}\frac{1}{-q^{0}}A_{q}^{0}=\sum_{q}A^{0}_{q}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\cal P}^{\dagger}}

(121)

In this case, we say that the propagator from the off-shell quark has “eikonalized”, and under a dagger, it is allowed to pick up a minus sign. This means that the time-like Wilson line satisfies

W_{v}W_{v}^{\dagger}=1.

(122)

Appendix C
Other soft contributions to P-waves

In sec.
4.4
we saw that the
\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}P_{J}^{[8]})}
matched onto multiple operators in vNRQCD, but only one contributed to S-wave vector quarkonium production at leading order in
v
. The other operators were suppressed because they required further transitions to be put in a
{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}
configuration. The operator

\sum_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}{\bfcal P}^{i}\sigma^{j}T^{b}\chi_{-{\bf q}^{\prime}}S_{v}^{ab}

(123)

is particularly interesting because this is the only contribution in eq. (
75
) that is proportional to the relative momentum of the
Q\bar{Q}
. Therefore, it can be interpreted as a genuine P-wave state at the scale
\mu\sim m\text{v}
and will play a greater role in P-wave quarkonium production. As an exploratory effort, in this section we derive the transitions necessary for this operator to contribute to S-wave vector quarkonium production, which is the main topic of this paper. This effort will explicitly show that its contributions are formally subleading with respect to the dominant operator identified in sec.
4.4
, but also that there is a new relation between matrix elements.

C.1
Tom’s RPI technique for P-waves

Since eq. (
123
) is in a true P-wave state, by conventional wisdom, it must flip to a
{}^{3}S_{1}^{[8]}
via a chromo-electric dipole emission. In ref.
Fleming:2019pzj
, it was shown that gauge invariant chromo-electric dipole transitions can quickly be derived using reparameterization invariance. This is because these operators contain information about the small relative momentum of the quark/antiquark pair which can be related to the initial velocity of the heavy quarks via a reparameterization of the form,

v^{\mu}\to v_{\pm}^{\mu}=v^{\mu}\pm\frac{q^{\mu}}{2m}+{\cal O}\bigg(\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\bigg).

(124)

Under this transformation, we can define new fields in vNRQCD that satisfy

\displaystyle\not{v}_{\pm}\psi_{{\bf q},\pm}=

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf q},\pm},

(125)

\displaystyle\not{v}_{\pm}\chi_{-{\bf q},\pm}=

\displaystyle-\chi_{-{\bf q},\pm},

\displaystyle v_{\pm}\cdot DS_{v,\pm}=

\displaystyle 0.

The P-wave matching to these new fields generates an operator with the same form as

\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q},+}S_{v,+}^{\dagger}{\bf q}\cdot\Gamma S_{v,-}\chi_{-{\bf q},-}.

(126)

By solving for the new fields in terms of the old ones
Fleming:2019pzj

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf q},\pm}=\psi_{\bf q}\pm\frac{\not{q}}{4m}\psi_{\bf q},

(127)

\displaystyle\chi_{-{\bf q},\pm}=\chi_{-{\bf q}}\mp\frac{\not{q}}{4m}\chi_{-{\bf q}},

\displaystyle S_{v,+}=S_{v}\mp\frac{g}{2m}S_{v}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}{\bf q}\cdot{\bfcal E},

we can derive the subleading operators in the original theory that are related to eq. (
72
) by RPI

\displaystyle\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q},+}S_{v,+}^{\dagger}{\bf q}\cdot\Gamma S_{v,-}\chi_{-{\bf q},-}=

\displaystyle\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}}S_{v}^{\dagger}{\bf q}\cdot\Gamma S_{v}\chi_{-{\bf q}}-\frac{{\bf q}^{i}}{4m}\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}}S_{v}^{\dagger}\{{\bm{\gamma}}\cdot{\bf q},\Gamma\}S_{v}\chi_{-{\bf q}}

(128)

\displaystyle+\frac{g}{2m}\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}}\{S_{v}^{\dagger}{\bf q}\cdot\Gamma S_{v},\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}{\bf q}\cdot{\bfcal E}\}\chi_{-{\bf q}}.

The first term is just the leading order operator while the last two are subleading in the power-counting. While both of the subleading operators in eq. (
128
) can flip the P-wave into an S-wave, only the last operator can bring the state to a color singlet. Therefore, we identify the last term in eq. (
128
) as the dominant transition operator. We can easily recognize this contribution as a gauge invariant chromo-electric dipole insertion on the leading P-wave operator, as we expected.

For a generic
{}^{3}P_{J}^{[8]}
configuration, we can replace the vertex structure with

{\bf q}\cdot\Gamma\to\sigma^{i}{\bf q}^{j}T^{a},

(129)

where contracting the cartesian indices with
\delta^{ij}
, antisymmetric, and symmetric traceless tensors will give the vertices for the
{}^{3}P_{0}^{[8]}
,
{}^{3}P_{1}^{[8]}
, and
{}^{3}P_{2}^{[8]}
configurations, respectively. From here, we derive the leading transition operator in vNRQCD by projecting out the
{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}
component in eq. (
128
)

\frac{g}{2m}\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}}P_{{}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}}\big[\{S_{v}^{\dagger}{\bf q}^{j}\sigma^{i}T^{a}S_{v},\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}{\bf q}\cdot{\bfcal E}\}\big]\chi_{-{\bf q}}=\frac{g}{2N_{c}m}\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}}\sigma^{i}{\bf q}^{j}{\bf q}^{k}\bigg[{\cal S}_{v}^{ab}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}{\bfcal E}^{k,b}\bigg]\chi_{-{\bf q}}.

(130)

We begin by considering the vacuum matrix element squared of the operator derived in the previous section. This is given by

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}P_{J}^{[8]})}^{(1)}=\frac{g^{2}}{4N_{c}^{2}m^{2}}{\cal T}^{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}_{J}\sum_{X}

(131)

and the
{\cal T}^{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}

80

Inserting a color singlet composite field interaction and integrating out the quark loops, we find the subleading operator reduces to

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}P_{J}^{[8]})}^{(1)}=\frac{g^{2}}{4N_{c}m^{2}}{\cal T}^{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}_{J}\sum_{X}

\displaystyle\bra{0}\bigg[{\cal S}_{v}^{\dagger,ab}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}{\bfcal E}^{k,b}\bigg]\big[\sigma^{i}{\bf q}^{j}{\bf q}^{k}S^{\dagger}_{0}(x)\big]\ket{V,X}

(132)

\displaystyle\times\bra{V,X}\big[S_{0}(x)\sigma^{i^{\prime}}{\bf q}^{j^{\prime}}{\bf q}^{k^{\prime}}\big]\bigg[{\cal S}_{v}^{ab^{\prime}}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}{\bfcal E}^{k^{\prime},b^{\prime}}\bigg]\ket{0}.

Factorizing the Hilbert space,

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}P_{J}^{[8]})}^{(1)}=\frac{g^{2}}{36N_{c}m^{2}}{\cal T}^{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}_{J}

\displaystyle\bra{0}\big[\sigma^{i}{\bf q}^{2}S^{\dagger}_{0}(x)\big]\ket{V}\bra{V}\big[S_{0}(x)\sigma^{i^{\prime}}{\bf q}^{2}\big]\ket{0}

(133)

\displaystyle\times\bra{0}\bigg[{\cal S}_{v}^{ab}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}{\bfcal E}^{j,b}\bigg]\bigg[{\cal S}_{v}^{ab^{\prime}}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}{\bfcal E}^{j^{\prime},b^{\prime}}\bigg]\ket{0},

where we have averaged over the Cartesian indices in the first line. These matrix elements are not related to the wave function at the origin, but instead can be identified as
Bodwin:1994jh

\bra{0}{\bf q}^{2}[\sigma^{i}S_{0}^{\dagger}(x)]\ket{V}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi}}\overline{\nabla^{2}R}_{V}^{(0)}(0),

(134)

where the overline indicates that the wave function has been renormalized to subtract off linear divergences as
r\to 0

134

\overline{\nabla^{2}R}_{V}^{(0)}(0)\approx m^{2}\braket{\text{v}^{2}}_{V}R_{V}(0)+{\cal O}(\text{v}^{2}).,

(135)

where we defined the parameter
\braket{\text{v}^{2}}_{V}
which is related to the binding energy,

m\braket{\text{v}^{2}}_{V}=(M_{V}-2m),

(136)

to make the
\text{v}^{2}
suppression of the operator apparent.

Therefore, after averaging over spins and summing over polarizations, we can write the factorized P-wave matrix element as,

\braket{\mathcal{O}^{V}(^{3}P_{J}^{[8]})}^{(1)}=\frac{(m\braket{\text{v}^{2}}_{V})^{2}}{108N_{c}}(2J+1)\frac{3\big|R_{V}(0)\big|^{2}}{2\pi}\braket{{\bfcal E}}^{(1)},

(137)

with the definition

\braket{{\bfcal E}}^{(1)}=\bra{0}\bigg[{\cal S}_{v}^{ab}\frac{1}{v\cdot{\mathcal{P}}}{\bfcal E}^{j,b}\bigg]^{2}\ket{0}.

(138)

4.4

C.3
{}^{3}S_{1}^{[8]}
and
{}^{3}P_{J}^{[8]}

One may wonder if the leading contribution in eq. (

\displaystyle=g^{2}d^{g^{\prime}bc^{\prime}}d^{gbc}\int\frac{d^{D}{\bf q}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\frac{1}{2E_{q}}\frac{1}{{\bf q}^{2}}(\delta^{ij}-\frac{{\bf q}^{i}{\bf q}^{j}}{E_{q}^{2}})\bra{0}\big|{\cal S}_{v}^{ag}g{\bfcal E}^{c}\big|^{2}(x)\ket{0}

\braket{{\bfcal{EE}}}

\frac{d}{d\log\mu}\braket{{\bfcal{EE}}}=\frac{4\alpha_{s}}{3\pi}B_{F}\bra{0}\big|{\cal S}_{v}^{ac}g{\bfcal E}^{c}\big|^{2}(x)\ket{0}.

(140)
