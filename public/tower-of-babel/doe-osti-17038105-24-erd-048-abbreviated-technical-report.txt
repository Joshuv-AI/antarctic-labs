 Improved Symmetry of Ablator Capsules for Robust Inertial Confinement Fusion
 Ignition
 Sean Hayes
                                          24-ERD-048

Project Overview
This LDRD investigated the basic mechanism of symmetry control of ablators coated on
spherical substrates. Wall thickness non-uniformity (Mode 1, M1) defects are one of the
primary sources of degradation of NIF ICF implosions. Strategies to reduce wall thickness non-
uniformities have focused on increased agitation of the substrates, however, increasing
agitation often leads to a drastic increase in defects in capsule walls. Efforts focused on
understanding the mechanisms at which asymmetry develops and scales as well as strategies to
mitigate defects formed from enhanced agitation to reduce asymmetry.
Through careful experimentation on stationary shells as well as development of motion tracking
software and video analysis, scaling laws for M1 were validated. Additionally, new agitation
metrics were derived from videos of the agitation process captured in-situ. These metrics were
correlated to defect rates through a model of agitation derived from the stationary shell work.
Finally, a new methodology for screening agitation schemes was developed on larger capsules
needed for future ICF capabilities, reducing screening time by a factor of 10.

Mission Impact
Work from this LDRD is likely to significantly increase the pace of development for HDC
capsules and potentially other ablator types. Understanding the trade-space between defect
types allows for design and batch yield optimization. Additionally, the scaling work provides
relevant data for current generation capsule quality for NIFs future enhanced yield capability,
where larger radius and thicker capsules will be required to take advantage of higher laser
energy. The ready-to-deploy GUI for motion tracking developed under this LDRD allows for
non-expert users to rapidly analyze agitation metrics with the potential for real-time in-situ
feedback. The addition of AI/ML to the manufacturing process through object detection and
tracking also enables future smart manufacturing capabilities, potentially reducing batch error
rate and identifying process deviations earlier in the production process.
