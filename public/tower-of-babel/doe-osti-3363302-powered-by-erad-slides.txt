Planning Gap and
Resilience Challenge

 Utilities Operate in a Complex Planning Environment

• More than 3,000 distribution utilities with different constraints
• Aging infrastructure and new load growth
• Multiple hazards affecting the same system
• High-cost, long-life investment decisions.

 The challenge is making consistent
 decisions across this complexity.

 Why This Conversation Matters Now

 • Extreme events are increasing in frequency and severity.
 • Distribution systems drive the majority of outages.
 • Utilities are investing at an unprecedented scale.
 • Planning decisions now carry long-term cost and risk implications.

 Utilities are making billion-dollar decisions under deep uncertainty.

 Where Current Planning Breaks Down

Today’s Reality
• Hazard studies are siloed (e.g., flood, wildfire, wind).
• Results are hard to compare across
  interventions.
• Planning is often deterministic or scenario limited.

What Planners Actually Need
• Multihazard comparison
• Probabilistic risk understanding
• Direct link to investment decisions.

We don’t have a modeling problem. We have a decision translation problem.

 What Actually Drives Resilience Value

Pole Upgrades
• Low cost and frequently selected
• Provide incremental resilience
 benefits.
Undergrounding
• Higher capital cost
• High benefit in severe scenarios.
Substations
• Critical points of failure
• Highest impact when protected.

 Resilience planning requires targeted, data-driven investments across
 assets and scenarios.

 studies.
