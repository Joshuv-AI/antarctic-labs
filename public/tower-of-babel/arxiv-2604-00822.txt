Introduction

For an elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
with complex multiplication, Deuring proved that the number of primes
p<X
at which
E
has a supersingular reduction is asymptotically equal to

\frac{1}{2}\frac{X}{\log X}.

For an elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
without complex multiplication, Lang and Trotter conjectured that the number of such primes is asymptotically equal to

\frac{c\sqrt{X}}{\log X},

where
c>0
is a constant depending only on
E
. It is still an open question, however, Elkies
[
9
]
proved a weaker result, namely that there are infinitely many supersingular primes. Furthermore, an average estimation related to the Lang–Trotter conjecture was established by Fouvry and Murty
[
10
]
. They calculated

\displaystyle\frac{1}{AB}\sum_{\lvert a\rvert\leq A}\sum_{\lvert b\rvert\leq B}\#\{p<X\mid E_{a,b}\textrm{ has a supersingular reduction at }p\}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{4\pi}{3}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X},

for
A,B>X^{1+\epsilon}
, where
E_{a,b}
is an elliptic curve defined by an equation
y^{2}=x^{2}+ax+b
and
a,b
run through the integers. They also showed that the above average estimation holds for
A,B>X^{1/2+\epsilon}
and
AB>X^{3/2+\epsilon}
. This result is called
the Lang–Trotter conjecture on average
. One of the key
points
in their proof is that the number of supersingular elliptic curves
E_{a,b}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
(
p\neq 2,3
) with
0\leq a,b<p
can be written as

\frac{p}{2}\cdot\left(h(-4p)+h(-p)\right)+O(p),

where
h(-p)
and
h(-4p)
are the class numbers of primitive quadratic forms with discriminants
-p
and
-4p
, respectively. We note that
E_{a,b}
has a supersingular reduction at
p
if and only if the trace of the Frobenius morphism of
E_{a,b}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
, denoted by
a_{p}(E_{a,b})
, is zero and that the result by Fouvry and Murty is the case of
a_{p}(E_{a,b})=0
. It was extended to the case
a_{p}(E_{a,b})=r
for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}
by David and Pappalardi
[
6
]
and the improved result was given by Baier
[
4
]
.

In this paper, we extend the Lang–Trotter conjecture on average to curves of genus 2. Let
K
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p\neq 2
. Curves of genus
2
are
classified into 7 types
with respect to their reduced automorphism groups (cf. Igusa
[
15
, § 8]
, also see
[
12
, 1.2]
). Here, the
reduced automorphism group

{\rm RA}(C)
of hyperelliptic curve
C
is the quotient of the automorphism group of
C
by the central subgroup generated by the hyperelliptic involution.

In our previous work
[
3
]
, we obtained the Lang–Trotter conjecture on average for the family of genus-
2
curves

y^{2}=x(x-1)(x+1)(x-{\lambda})\left(x-\frac{1}{\lambda}\right),

whose reduced automorphism groups contain Klein-
4
group
.
In the present paper, we focus on another type of them, the genus-
2
curves defined by the following equation with parameter
\lambda\in K
:

C_{\lambda}:y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-{\lambda})\left(x-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\right)\left(x-\frac{1}{1-\lambda}\right).

These genus-
2
curves are characterized as having the reduced automorphism group
{\rm RA}(C_{\lambda})
containing symmetric group
S_{3}
. For almost all
\lambda
, it is known that
{\rm RA}(C_{\lambda})
is isomorphic to
S_{3}
.
Let
p
be a prime such that
p\geq 5
. We consider whether
C_{\lambda}
has a superspecial reduction at
p
.
Here, a curve
C
over a field
K
is
superspecial
if its Jacobian variety is isomorphic to a product of supersingular elliptic curves over
\overline{K}
, the algebraic closure.
Our aim is to compute the average of

{\phi}_{\lambda}(X):=\#\{p<X\mid C_{\lambda}\text{ has a superspecial reduction at }p\}.

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
. To this end, we firstly determine the value

{\psi}_{p}:=\#\{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\mid C_{\lambda}\text{ is superspecial}\}

for each prime
p
.
The number of isomorphism classes of superspecial genus-
2
curves was computed for each of the 7 types by
Ibukiyama, Katsura and Oort
[
12
]
. In addition, the number of isomorphism classes of superspecial genus-
2
curves that have models over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
was determined by Ibukiyama and Katsura
[
13
]
in terms of the class numbers and the type numbers.
Moreover, Katsura and Oort
[
17
]
obtained the number of
(a,b)\in{\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}^{2}

such that the genus-
2
curve
C_{a,b}:y^{2}=(x^{2}-1)(x^{2}-a)(x^{2}-b)
is superspecial.
However, neither the number of
(a,b)\in{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{2}
such that the genus-2 curve
C_{a,b}
is superspecial has not been argued, nor the number of
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial. The first theorem determines
\psi_{p}
in terms of the class numbers
h(-p)
and
h(-3p)
.

Theorem A
.

Let
p\geq 5
be a prime. Then we have

{\psi}_{p}=\frac{3}{2}h(-3p)
if
p\bmod 4=1
,

{\psi}_{p}=0
if
p\bmod 12=7
,

{\psi}_{p}=3h(-p)
if
p\bmod 12=11
.

To prove Theorem
A
, we use the result of Ibukiyama, Katsura and Oort
[
12
, §1.2]
that
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial if and only if two elliptic curves

E_{{\Lambda}^{-}(\lambda)}:y^{2}=x(x-1)\left(x-(1-\lambda)(\lambda-\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-\lambda+1})^{2}\right)

and

E_{{\Lambda}^{+}(\lambda)}:y^{2}=x(x-1)\left(x-(1-\lambda)(\lambda+\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-\lambda+1})^{2}\right)

are supersingular.
Thus, it suffices to count the number of
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that two associated elliptic curves
E_{\Lambda^{-}(\lambda)}
and
E_{{\Lambda}^{+}(\lambda)}
are supersingular.
In fact, if either of the elliptic curves is supersingular, then both are supersingular (cf.
[
12
, Proposition 1.10]
).
To count such
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, a new and more elaborate argument was needed, as outlined below, because the defining equation contains radicals and differs from those treated in previous works (e.g.
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
10
]
) on elliptic curves.

We firstly show that the
j
-invariants of supersingular elliptic curves
E_{\Lambda^{-}(\lambda)}
and
E_{{\Lambda}^{+}(\lambda)}
are the roots of
P_{3p}(X)\bmod p
if
p\equiv 1\bmod 4
and are the roots of
P_{p}(X)\bmod p
if
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
and that conversely any root of
P_{3p}(X)\bmod p
for
p\equiv 1\bmod 4
and any root of
P_{p}(X)\bmod p
for
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
is equal to the
j
-invariant of a supersingular elliptic curve
E_{\Lambda^{\varepsilon}(\lambda)}
with some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
(see Section
6
). Here
P_{3p}(X)
and
P_{p}(X)
are the Hilbert class polynomials of level
3p
and
p
respectively (see §
2.1
). An important fact is that the degrees of the polynomials
P_{3p}(X)
and
P_{p}(X)
are
h(-3p)
and
h(-p)
respectively. We require an elaborate argument, since the endomorphism rings of the elliptic curves are far from obvious.
We also use the factorization of
P_{3p}(X)
and
P_{p}(X)
mod
p
to
determine the value
\psi_{p}
. The factorization of
P_{p}(X)\bmod p
for
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
was determined by Elkies
[
9
]
or Kaneko
[
18
, Appendix]
but that of
P_{3p}(X)\bmod p
for
p\equiv 1\bmod 4
has not been obtained, so we give a careful observation concerning
P_{3p}(X)\bmod p
for
p\equiv 1\bmod 4
in Section
7
.

Using the value
\psi_{p}
, we compute the average of
\phi_{\lambda}(X)
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
|\lambda|
is less than a positive number
N>X^{1+\epsilon}
.

Theorem B
.

Let
X,\epsilon
be positive numbers and
N
a positive number that satisfies
N>X^{1+\epsilon}
. Then, we have

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\lvert\lambda\rvert\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)\sim\frac{(6+4\sqrt{3})\pi}{9}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}

as
X\rightarrow\infty
.

We use the idea of Fouvry and Murty, and this theorem is followed by a similar argument to our previous work
[
3
, §5]
.

Furthermore, we also calculate the average of
\phi_{\lambda}(X)
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
0pt{(\lambda)}<N
. Here, for
\lambda=b/a
with coprime numbers
a,b
, the height of
\lambda
, denoted by
0pt{(\lambda)}
, is max
\{|a|,|b|\}
.

Theorem C
.

Let
X,\epsilon
be positive numbers and
N
a positive number that satisfies
N>X^{1+\epsilon}
. Then,

\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q},\ 0pt(\lambda)\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)\sim\frac{4(3+2\sqrt{3})}{3\pi}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}

as
X\rightarrow\infty
.

This result immediately follows from Theorem
B
and our previous work
[
3
, §6]
.

Our paper is organized into 10 sections. In Section
2
we recall some properties of elliptic curves with complex multiplication in addition to the Hilbert class polynomials and the modular polynomials and related results by Gross-Zagier. We also review genus-

curves. These are crucial in the proof of Theorem

, we construct isogenies between the two elliptic curves

, we conversely observe elliptic curves over

with a certain endomorphism ring. In Section

we discuss the relationship between the

-invariants of the supersingular elliptic curves

and the Hilbert class polynomials. In Section

we determine the factorization of

for

and Theorem

Preliminaries

2.1
The Hilbert class polynomials and modular polynomials

In this section, we recall the Hilbert class polynomials and modular polynomials.

Let

has a good reduction at

Lemma 2.1

(Deuring:
[
7
]
)
.

Let

be an odd prime of good reduction for

has supersinular redction at

if and only if there exists some
D\equiv 0\textrm{ or }3\mod 4
such that

divides the numerator of
P_{D}(j(E))
and
\left(\frac{-D}{p}\right)=-1
or the highest power of
p
dividing

Deuring also proved that complex multiplication in characteristic

Let

\alpha

lying above

is isomorphic to

, the reduction of

at
\mathfrak{B}

Proof.

S. Lang:
[
20

Proof of Theorem
B

We prove Theorem
B
in
the introduction
. We use the idea by Fouvry and Murty
[
10
, Theorem 4]
.

Let
X
,
\epsilon
and
N
be as in Theorem B. Theorem
A
enables us to write the summations
\sum_{|\lambda|\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)
with the class numbers as follows.

\displaystyle\sum_{|\lambda|\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\lambda|\leq N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
C_{\lambda}\text{ is superspecial}\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{p<X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\lambda|\leq N\\
C_{\lambda}\text{ is superspecial}\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p<X}\left(\frac{2N}{p}+O(1)\right){\psi}_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 1\bmod 4\end{subarray}}\frac{2N}{p}\cdot\frac{3}{2}h(-3p)+\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 11\bmod 12\end{subarray}}\frac{2N}{p}\cdot 3h(-p)+O\left(X^{\frac{3}{2}}\right).

We note that the sum of
h(-p)
and
h(-3p)
over primes less than
X
is
O(X^{3/2})
.
(cf. Mertens
[
21
]
and Siegel
[
22
]
).
Recall the Dirichlet class number formula

h(d)=\frac{\sqrt{|d|}}{\pi}L(1,{\chi}_{d}),

where
\chi_{d}
is the character defined by the Kronecker symbol
(-d\ |\ \cdot)
.
Then the sum (
) is

\displaystyle\frac{2N}{\pi}\left(\frac{3}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 1\bmod 4\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{p}}L(1,{\chi}_{-3p})+\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 11\bmod 12\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\frac{3}{\sqrt{p}}L(1,{\chi}_{-p})\right)+o\left(\frac{N\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right).

(14)

From Polya’s inequality (cf. Apostol
[
2
, Theorem 8.21]
) and Abel’s identity (cf. Apostol
[
2
, Theorem 4.2]
), for any parameter
U>1
, we have

\left|\sum_{U<n}\frac{{\chi}_{-p}(n)}{n}\right|<\frac{3\sqrt{p}\log p}{U}

and the same inequality for
-3p
.
We choose
U=X^{3/4}
. Then (
14
) is as follow
s
.

\displaystyle\frac{6N}{\pi}\left\{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 1\bmod 4\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sum_{n\leq U}\frac{{\chi}_{-3p}(n)}{n}+\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 11\bmod 12\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sum_{n\leq U}\frac{{\chi}_{-p}(n)}{n}\right\}+o\left(\frac{N\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right).

(15)

We recall that
{\chi}_{-3p}
and
{\chi}_{-p}
are expressed by the Legendre symbols:
if
p\equiv 1\bmod 4
, then

{\chi}_{-3p}=\left(\frac{n}{3}\right)\left(\frac{n}{p}\right),

if
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
, then

{\chi}_{-p}=\left(\frac{n}{p}\right).

Substituting these
in
(
15
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{3\sqrt{3}N}{\pi}S(X,U)+\frac{6N}{\pi}T(X,U)+o\left(\frac{N\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right),

(16)

where

\displaystyle S(X,U)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq U}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 1\bmod 4\end{subarray}}\frac{\left(\frac{n}{3}\right)\left(\frac{n}{p}\right)}{\sqrt{p}},

\displaystyle T(X,U)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq U}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 11\bmod 12\end{subarray}}\frac{\left(\frac{n}{p}\right)}{\sqrt{p}}.

We calculate these sums using the idea of Fouvry and Murty
[
10
, Theorem 4]
.

We first calculate
T(X,U)
. If we sum over only
n\leq U
such that
n
is a perfect square or
n/3
is a perfect square, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq U\\
n=d^{2}\text{ for some }d\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 11\bmod 12\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq U\\
n=3l^{2}\text{ for some }l\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{3l^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 11\bmod 12\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}.

we note that
3\in{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{*}}^{2}
if
p\equiv 11\bmod 12
.
We know that the inner sum of each term is equal to

\frac{2}{\varphi(12)}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}+o\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right),

where
\varphi
is Euler’s totient function.
Thus, we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq U\\
n=d^{2}\text{ for some }d\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 11\bmod 12\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}=\frac{{\pi}^{2}}{12}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}+o\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right)

and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq U\\
n=3l^{2}\text{ for some }l\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{3l^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 11\bmod 12\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}=\frac{{\pi}^{2}}{36}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}+o\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right).

Following a similar approach to
Fouvry and Murty
[
10
, p. 92]
and using Lemma
2.11
, the sum of the remaining terms is ultimately negligible compared to
\sqrt{X}/\log X
.
Hence we obtain

\displaystyle T(X,U)=\frac{{\pi}^{2}}{9}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}+o\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right).

(17)

Second, we calculate
S(X,U)
. If
3
divides
n
, then
{\chi}_{-3p}(n)
is
0
so we only treat
n
such that
3\nmid n
. If we sum over only such
n\leq U
that is also perfect square, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq U\\
n=d^{2}\text{ for some }d\in\mathbb{N},\ 3\nmid d\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 1\bmod 4\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq U\\
n=d^{2}\text{ for some }d\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d^{2}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq U\\
n=(3d)^{2}\text{ for some }d\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(3d)^{2}}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 1\bmod 4\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}.

We know that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 1\bmod 4\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}=\frac{2}{\varphi(4)}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}+o\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right).

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq U\\
n=d^{2},\ \exists d\in\mathbb{N},\ 3\nmid d\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
p\equiv 1\bmod 4\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}=\frac{{4\pi}^{2}}{27}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}+o\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right).

Following a similar approach to
Fouvry and Murty
[
10
, p.92]
, the sum of the remaining terms is ultimately negligible compared to
\sqrt{X}/\log X
. Thus we find that

\displaystyle S(X,U)=\frac{4{\pi}^{2}}{27}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}+o\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right).

(18)

Substituting the equations (
17
), (
18
) for (
16
) yields

\sum_{|\lambda|\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)=\frac{(6+4\sqrt{3})\pi N}{9}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}+o\left(\frac{N\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right).

Hence we obtain

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{|\lambda|\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)=\frac{(6+4\sqrt{3})\pi}{9}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}+o\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right).

10
Proof of Theorem
C

In this section, we prove Theorem
C
. From our previous work
[
3
, §6]
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{ht}(\lambda)\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p<X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{ht}(\lambda)\leq N\\
C_{\lambda}\text{ is superspecial}\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p<X}\left(\frac{12}{{\pi}^{2}}\frac{N^{2}}{p}+o(N\log N)+O\left(\frac{N^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)\right){\psi}_{p}.

Thus, from the result of Theorem B we find that

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q},\ 0pt(\lambda)\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)=\frac{4(3+2\sqrt{3})}{3\pi}\frac{N^{2}\sqrt{X}}{\log X}+o\left(\frac{N^{2}\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right).

Hence we obtain

\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q},\ 0pt(\lambda)\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)=\frac{4(3+2\sqrt{3})}{3\pi}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}+o\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right).
