Monodromy Representations of Mixed Braid Groups
Chitrabhanu Chaudhuri, Saswati Mukherjee

Abstract
. We explicitly describe unitary representations of mixed braid groups on the cohomology of Abelian branched covers of ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{CP}^{1} . We show that the image of the representation is generated by complex reflections and relate it to the multivariate Burau representation.



1.
Introduction

The action of the braid group on the cohomology of covering spaces of the punctured
plane is a classical subject. Note that a tuple
p=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
n

distinct points determines a compact Riemann surface
X_{p}
with affine part

\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid y^{d}=(x-p_{1})(x-p_{2})\cdots(x-p_{n})\}.

The map
X_{p}\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}
extending
(x,y)\mapsto x
is a Galois (branched) covering
with cyclic Galois group
\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
. Now if we vary
p
over the unordered
configuration space

\conf_{n}(\mathbb{C})=\frac{\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid z_{i}\neq z_{j}\}}{S_{n}}

we have a (smooth) fiber bundle
\pi:\mathfrak{X}\to\conf_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with fibers
\pi^{-1}(p)=X_{p}
.
Let
\mathbb{C}_{\mathfrak{X}}
be the (locally) constant sheaf on
\mathfrak{X}
.
Taking the first higher direct image sheaf:

\mathcal{V}=R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}_{\mathfrak{X}}

we obtain a local system
\mathcal{V}
over
\conf_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with fibers

\mathcal{V}_{p}=H^{1}(X_{p},\mathbb{C}).

Consequently we have a monodromy representation of the Braid group

\mathcal{B}_{n}=\pi_{1}(\conf_{n}(\mathbb{C}))\to\mathrm{GL}(H^{1}(X_{p},\mathbb{C})).

In
[
14
]
, McMullen gives a complete account of this representation, and
the geometric structures on moduli space, that arise via this branched-cover
construction.

This paper generalises this construction from cyclic covers to Abelian covers. Let

\Lambda=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})
be a partition of
n
. Consider the compact Riemann
surface
X_{p}
which completes

\{(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\mid y_{j}^{d_{j}}=(x-p_{h_{j-1}+1})\cdots(x-p_{h_{j}})\}\qquad\text{where}\qquad h_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}n_{i}.

Now the map
X_{p}\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}
,
(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\mapsto x
is a Galois cover with
abelian Galois group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/d_{1}\mathbb{Z}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}/d_{m}\mathbb{Z}
.
The Galois group is generated by

T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}\qquad\text{where}\qquad T_{j}(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})=(x,y_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{d_{j}}y_{j},\ldots,y_{m}).

Consider the variation of the point
p=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
within the configuration
space
\conf_{n,\Lambda}(\mathbb{C})
. This space is defined as the quotient:

\conf_{n,\Lambda}(\mathbb{C})=\frac{\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid z_{i}\neq z_{j}\}}{S_{n,\Lambda}}.

Here,
S_{n,\Lambda}
is the subgroup of the symmetric group
S_{n}
containing all
permutations that preserve the partition
\Lambda
. This results in a
fiber bundle
\pi:\mathfrak{X}\to\conf_{n,\Lambda}(\mathbb{C})
, where each fiber
is given by
X_{p}
. In this way we get a monodromy representation

(1.1)

\theta:\pi_{1}(\conf_{n,\Lambda}(\mathbb{C}))\to\mathrm{GL}(H^{1}(X_{p},\mathbb{C})).

The fundamental group
\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}:=\pi_{1}(\conf_{n,\Lambda}(\mathbb{C}))
is called the mixed
braid group or coloured braid group for the partition
\Lambda
.

Let us fix
p\in\conf_{n,\Lambda}(\mathbb{C})
and write
X:=X_{p}
. The representation
\theta

preserves the hermitian intersection form

\left\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right\rangle=\int_{X}\xi_{1}\wedge\overline{\xi}_{2},\qquad\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in H^{1}(X)

and commutes with the Galois group
\mathbb{Z}/d_{1}\mathbb{Z}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}/d_{m}\mathbb{Z}
.
Let us decompose
H^{1}(X)
into
T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}
eigenspaces

H^{1}(X)=\bigoplus_{\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m})}\Big(H^{1}(X)_{\rho}:=\cap_{j}\ker(T_{j}-\rho_{j}\id)\Big)

where the direct sum is over
\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots\rho_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
,
with
\rho_{j}^{d_{j}}=1
. The representation
\theta
preserves each eigenspace

H^{1}(X)_{\rho}
. Restricting the intersection form to
H^{1}(X)_{\rho}

we thus get a unitary representation

\theta_{\rho}:\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}\to\U(H^{1}(X)_{\rho})\cong\U(r,s)

where
(r,s)
is the signature of the hermitian form on
H^{1}(X)_{\rho}
.
Here
\U(r,s)
is the pseudo-unitary group of signature
(r,s)
.

Description of results

In (
3.2
) we compute the signature using the Chevalley-Weil formula
to find that when
\rho=(\zeta_{d_{1}}^{-k_{1}},\ldots,\zeta_{d_{j}}^{-k_{m}})

and
0<k_{j}<d_{j}
for each
j
,

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\left\lceil\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{n_{j}k_{j}}{d_{j}}-1\right\rceil,

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=\left\lceil n-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{n_{j}k_{j}}{d_{j}}-1\right\rceil

and in this case,
\dim H^{1}(X)_{\rho}=n-1
when

\rho_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\rho_{m}^{n_{m}}\neq 1
,
otherwise
n-2
(here
\zeta_{d_{j}}=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/d_{j}}
).

The crux of the paper is finding an explicit spanning set
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-1}
for

H^{1}(X)_{\rho}
in which the intersection form is given by

\displaystyle\left\langle\omega_{i},\omega_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}(\rho_{j}+1)(\rho_{j}-1)^{-1}

\displaystyle h_{j-1}<i<h_{j},

\displaystyle\left\langle\omega_{h_{j}},\omega_{h_{j}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}(\rho_{j}\rho_{j+1}-1)(\rho_{j}-1)^{-1}(\rho_{j+1}-1)^{-1}

\displaystyle 1\leq j<m,

\displaystyle\left\langle\omega_{i-1},\omega_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}(-\rho_{j})(1-\rho_{j})^{-1}

\displaystyle h_{j-1}<i\leq h_{j}.

The spanning set is obtained by taking weighted sums of Poincaré duals of lifts of
certain curves in
\mathbb{C}-\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\}
(see Figure
3

and definitions (
3.3
)).

The central result of the paper is Theorem
5.2
where we describe
the representation relative to the spanning set above utilising the intersection form.
This description holds for any
\rho
satisfying
\rho_{j}\neq 1
and

\rho_{j}\rho_{k}\neq 1
for any
j,k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
These conditions account for the generic case for large
d_{j}
; indeed, if the
d_{j}

are pairwise co-prime, the second condition holds by default. Furthermore,
in Proposition
5.4
, we demonstrate that
\theta_{\rho}
is irreducible
under these assumptions
.

The representation
\theta_{\rho}
is not faithful, in fact in Proposition

5.1
, we show that if
\tau=(\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n-1})^{n}
,
that is the generator of the center of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, (where
\sigma_{i}
are the standard
generators of the braid group), then
\tau^{f}
is always in the kernel of

\theta_{\rho}
where
f=\ord(n_{1}T_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}T_{m})
.

We show that the representation
\theta_{\rho}
is isomorphic to the
dual of the reduced multivariate Burau representation
evaluated at
\overline{\rho}
(see Proposition
5.5
). When
\Lambda=(n)
the
representation
\theta_{\rho}
is the reduced Burau representation for the braid group

\mathcal{B}_{n}
evaluated at
\overline{\rho}
(see Theorem 5.5 of
[
14
]
). At the other extreme
for
\Lambda=(1,\ldots,1)
we get the dual of reduced Gassner representation
evaluated at
\overline{\rho}
. Thus our family of representations interpolate between the
reduced Burau and the reduced Gassner representations.

Finally we observe that

\theta_{\rho}(\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda})
is infinite whenever for some
j
,
n_{j}\geq 3
,

\rho_{j}
is a primitive
d_{j}
-th root of unity and

(n_{j},d_{j})\not\in\{(3,3),(3,4),(3,6),(3,10),(4,4),(4,6),(5,6),(6,6)\}.

Connections with related literature

It has been shown by Murakami
[
16
]
, Morton
[
15
]
, and Gabrovšek-Horvat

[
8
]
that the multivariate Burau representation can be used to calculate the
multivariate Alexander polynomial of links. This establishes a direct connection
between the representations investigated in this paper and knot theory,
suggesting significant potential for future research into these connections.

The topological framework of this paper is deeply tied to the classical Deligne-Mostow
theory of hyper-geometric monodromy
[
5
]
. In Section 12, Deligne-Mostow study
monodromy representation of cyclic covers of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
of the form

y^{d}=(x-a_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(x-a_{n})^{k_{n}}

primarily to classify which rational weights
(k_{1}/d,\ldots,k_{n}/d)
yield
discrete arithmetic or non-arithmetic lattices in the complex hyperbolic isometry
group
\mathrm{PU}(1,N)
. This is related to the image of
\theta_{\rho}
when
when
\rho=(\zeta_{d}^{k_{1}},\ldots,\zeta_{d}^{k_{m}})
and

\lceil n-\sum_{i}k_{i}/d-1\rceil=1
.

The images of the Burau and Gassner representations were studied
extensively by Venkataramana in
[
18
]
and
[
17
]
, where he demonstrated
that these images are arithmetic in a wide range of cases.

In a related direction, Looijenga
[
10
]
investigated Abelian covers of surfaces of
genus at least 2. He demonstrated that the image of the representation of the liftable
mapping class group on the cohomology of the cover is an arithmetic group in all but a
few cases in genus 2.

Future directions

It will be very interesting to investigate the image and kernel of
\theta_{\rho}
for
different partitions
\Lambda
and different roots of unity
\rho
.
The following question is open but notoriously difficult:

Question 1.1
.

What is the kernel of
\theta_{\rho}
? What is the image

\theta_{\rho}(\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda})
in
\U(r,s)
?

An exhaustive account seems out of reach right now but small cases
can be analysed to start with. We plan to take this up in a subsequent paper.

In a different direction it will be worthwhile to explore the structures on the
moduli space
M_{0,n}
of genus zero Riemann surfaces with
n
marked points that
are induced by
\theta_{\rho}
. This was explored in the cyclic case by McMullen in
sections 7 and 8 of
[
14
]
.

Finally in the future we would like to generalise our work to the most general
Abelian covers

\Big\{(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\mid y_{j}^{d_{j}}=(x-p_{h_{j-1}+1})^{a_{h_{j-1}+1}}\cdots(x-p_{h_{j}})^{a_{h_{j}}}\Big\}.

2.
Topological Construction of the Monodromy

Mixed braid groups

Let
\mathcal{B}_{n}
be Artin’s braid group on
n
strands defined as,

\mathcal{B}_{n}:=\Pi_{1}((\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\Delta)/S_{n})

where
\Delta=\{(z_{1},...,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid z_{i}=z_{j}\text{ for some }i\neq j\}
is the big diagonal and
S_{n}
is the symmetric group of degree
n
.

The braid group admits the standard presentation

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{n}=\langle\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n-1}\mid

\displaystyle\ \sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\quad\forall\ 1\leq i<j-1<n-1,

\displaystyle\ \sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\quad 1\leq i<n-1\rangle

where the generators are the braids:

There is a natural surjective homomorphism

\Phi_{n}:\mathcal{B}_{n}\to S_{n},\qquad\Phi_{n}(\sigma_{i})=(i,i+1)

which maps a braid to the permutation it induces on its end points.

Let
\Lambda
be a partition of the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
and denote by

S_{n,\Lambda}
the subgroup of
S_{n}
consisting of all permutations
that preserve
\Lambda
. The
mixed braid group

\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
corresponding
to
\Lambda
is defined as

\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}:=\Phi_{n}^{-1}(S_{n,\Lambda}).

It turns out that

\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}=\Pi_{1}((\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\Delta)/S_{n,\Lambda}).

Since
\Lambda=\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m}\}
is a partition of

\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we may, after
relabelling the elements, assume that each block consists of consecutive integers.
That is,

\Lambda_{i}=\{h_{i-1}+1,\ldots,h_{i}\},

for some integers
0=h_{0}<h_{1}<\ldots<h_{m}=n
. For convenience of notation
we shall denote the partition as a tuple
\Lambda=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})
where

n_{j}=h_{j}-h_{j-1}
.

Generators and relations

Manfredini
[
13
, Theorem 4]
gives a presentation of
\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
. In that
presentation the generators are

\sigma_{i}\quad 1\leq i<n,\ i\neq h_{j},\ 1\leq j<m,\qquad\text{and}\qquad A_{j,k}\quad 1\leq j<k\leq m,

where
\sigma_{i}
are the standard generators of the braid group and

A_{j,k}=(\sigma_{h_{k-1}}\sigma_{h_{k-1}-1}\cdots\sigma_{h_{j}+1})\,\sigma_{h_{j}}^{2}\,(\sigma_{h_{k-1}}\sigma_{h_{k-1}-1}\cdots\sigma_{h_{j}+1})^{-1}.

Hence
A_{j,k}
only moves the last element of
\Lambda_{j}
and first element of

\Lambda_{k}
.

We do not list the relations in general, however, when
m=2
the presentation
simplifies significantly:

Generators
(m=2):

\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{h_{1}-1},\,A_{1,2}=\sigma_{h_{1}}^{2},\,\sigma_{h_{1}+1},\ldots,\sigma_{n-1}

Relations
(m=2):

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle i<j-1,

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}A_{1,2}

\displaystyle=A_{1,2}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle i\not\in\{h_{1}-1,h_{1}+1\},

\displaystyle\sigma_{h_{1}-1}A_{1,2}\sigma_{h_{1}-1}A_{1,2}

\displaystyle=A_{1,2}\sigma_{h_{1}-1}A_{1,2}\sigma_{h_{1}-1}

\displaystyle\sigma_{h_{1}+1}A_{1,2}\sigma_{h_{1}+1}A_{1,2}

\displaystyle=A_{1,2}\sigma_{h_{1}+1}A_{1,2}\sigma_{h_{1}+1}

\displaystyle(\sigma_{h_{1}-1}A_{1,2}\sigma_{h_{1}-1}^{-1})(\sigma_{h_{1}+1}A_{1,2}\sigma_{h_{1}+1}^{-1})

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{h_{1}+1}A_{1,2}\sigma_{h_{1}+1}^{-1})(\sigma_{h_{1}-1}A_{1,2}\sigma_{h_{1}-1}^{-1}).

Relation to Mapping class groups

For distinct points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
there is an isomorphism

(2.1)

\mathcal{B}_{n}\cong\mcg_{c}(\mathbb{C},\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\})

where
\mcg_{c}(\mathbb{C},\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\})
is the group of isotopy classes of compactly
supported homeomorphisms of
\mathbb{C}
that fix the set
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\}
. This
isomorphism takes the generator
\sigma_{i}
to the half twist of a disk containing

p_{i},p_{i+1}
as in Figure
1
(see
[
9
, Section 1.6.2]
).

Figure 1.
Generators of
\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
seen as mapping classes.

Under this isomorphism the generators
A_{j,k}
of
\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
can be realised
as the mapping class of the full twist in a disk containing

p_{h_{j}},p_{h_{k-1}+1}
with
p_{h_{j}}
as the center. We shall use the
identification
(
2.1
)
throughout this paper.

Abelian Covers of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}

Let
\Gamma
be the finite Abelian group

\Gamma:=\mathbb{Z}/d_{1}\mathbb{Z}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}/d_{m}\mathbb{Z}

with standard generators
T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}
.
Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
be
n
distinct points. Note that
the fundamental group of
\mathbb{C}
punctured at
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
is the free group

\Pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}-\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\})=\langle c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\rangle

where
c_{i}
is a small counter-clockwise loop encircling
p_{i}
. We consider
a surjective homomorphism

\Psi:\Pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}-\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\})\to\Gamma,\quad\text{where}\quad\Psi(c_{i})=T_{j}\text{ for some }j.

There is a regular cover of
\mathbb{C}-\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\}
with Deck group
\Gamma

associated to this homomorphism.
By Riemann existence theorem, this cover extends to a branched Galois cover

\pi:X\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\aut_{\mathbb{CP}^{1}}(X)\cong\Gamma.

This cover is ramified over
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
and infinity.

After relabelling the branch points we may assume there are integers

h_{0}=0<h_{1}<\ldots<h_{m}=n
such that
\Psi(c_{i})=T_{j}
if

h_{j-1}<i\leq h_{j}
. Note that
X
can be realised as the compact Riemann
surface that completes the affine curve

\{(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\mid y_{j}^{d_{j}}=(x-p_{h_{j-1}+1})\cdots(x-p_{h_{j}}),\quad 1\leq j\leq m\}

and
\pi(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})=x
. As before if
n_{j}=h_{j}-h_{j-1}
, then

\bullet

\pi^{-1}(p_{i})
consists of
|\Gamma|/d_{j}
points, each with the
ramification index
d_{j}
, if
h_{j-1}<i\leq h_{j}
.

\bullet

\pi^{-1}(\infty)
consists of
e
points with ramification index

f=\ord(n_{1}T_{1}+\ldots+n_{m}T_{m})

where
e=|\Gamma|/f
.

Then applying Riemann Hurwitz formula we obtain:

\displaystyle g(X)\quad=\quad\frac{1}{2}\left(|\Gamma|(n-1)-|\Gamma|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{n_{j}}{d_{j}}-e\right)+1.

Representation of mixed braid group on
H^{1}(X)

For
b\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
, notice that

(2.2)

\Psi(b_{*}c_{i})=\Psi(c_{i})\iff b\in\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}.

Hence every element of
\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
lifts to
X
. It also
follows from (
2.2
) that for
b\in\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
and any lift

\widetilde{b}:X\to X
we have

\widetilde{b}\,\gamma=\gamma\,\widetilde{b}\qquad\text{for all}\qquad\gamma\in\Gamma.

Since
b
fixes a neighbourhood
N
of
\infty
in
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
point wise, there is a
unique lift
\widetilde{b}
which fixes
\pi^{-1}(N)
in
X
. This gives rise to
a homomorphism

(2.3)

\lift:\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}\to\mcg(X)^{\Gamma},\qquad b\mapsto\widetilde{b},

where
\mcg(X)^{\Gamma}
is the centraliser of
\aut_{\mathbb{CP}^{1}}(X)\cong\Gamma
in

\mcg(X)
.

The mapping class group
\mcg(X)
acts on
H^{1}(X):=H^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
by

b\cdot\xi=\left(\widetilde{b}^{-1}\right)^{*}\xi,\qquad\text{where}\qquad b\in\mcg(X)\text{ and }\xi\in H^{1}(X).

Thus the homomorphism (
2.3
) induces a representation of
\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
on

H^{1}(X)
. By a standard argument involving lifts of isotopies this
is the same as the monodromy representation
\theta
defined in

(
1.1
)
.

Let
\mu(d_{j})
be the set of
d_{j}
-th roots of unity and

\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m})\in\mu(d_{1})\times\ldots\times\mu(d_{m})
.
For a
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
module
V
we define the
\rho
eigenspace of
\Gamma
in

V
to be the subspace

V_{\rho}:=\{v\in V\mid T_{j}v=\rho_{j}v\ ,\quad 1\leq j\leq m\}.

Then
\mcg(X)^{\Gamma}
preserves the eigenspaces
H^{1}(X)_{\rho}
hence so does

\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
.

The standard hermitian form on
H^{1}(X)
given by

(2.4)

\left\langle\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\right\rangle=\int_{X}\omega_{1}\wedge\overline{\omega}_{2},\qquad\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in H^{1}(X)

is invariant under the
\mcg(X)
action. Thus if
(r,s)
is the signature of

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
on
H^{1}(X)_{\rho}
the representation of
\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
on

H^{1}(X)_{\rho}
yields a homomorphism

\theta_{\rho}:\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}\to\U(r,s).

3.
Eigenspace Decomposition of the Cohomology

In this section, we investigate the
\Gamma
-eigenspaces
H^{1}(X)_{\rho}
.
For any orientable surface
S
we have an isomorphism
\eta:H_{1}(S)\to H^{1}_{c}(S)

induced by Poincaré duality, (see
[
2
, Chapter 1, §5]
and

[
7
, §III.1.1]
), such that for any
x\in H_{1}(S)
and any
\omega\in H^{1}(S)

\int_{x}\omega=\int_{S}\omega\wedge\eta(x).

Moreover, if
f:S\to S
is a diffeomorphism then

\eta(f_{*}x)=(f^{-1})^{*}\eta(x).

When
x
is represented by a simple closed curve, see
[
7
, §II.3.3]
for an
explicit description of
\eta(x)
. Thus the isomorphism

\eta:H_{1}(X)\to H^{1}(X)

is in fact, an isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
modules, when
\Gamma
acts on
H_{1}(X)

via the push-forward

\gamma\cdot\xi=\gamma_{*}\xi\qquad\text{for}\qquad\gamma\in\Gamma,\xi\in H_{1}(X),

while the action on
H^{1}(X)
is given by the pull-back of the inverse:

\gamma\cdot\xi=(\gamma^{-1})^{*}\xi\qquad\text{for}\qquad\gamma\in\Gamma,\xi\in H^{1}(X).

Consequently we may work with
H_{1}(X)
or
H^{1}(X)
interchangeably as far as the

\Gamma
action is concerned.

Dimension

Consider the CW structure on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
with
0
cells:
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}

and
\infty
;
1
cells:
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
where
E_{i}
has end points
\infty

and
p_{i}
;
2
cells:
F
with obvious attaching map.
This induces the following CW structure on
X
:

•

0
cells:

–

\tilde{p}_{i}^{\,\gamma}
, with

\gamma\in\Gamma_{j}:=\dfrac{\Gamma}{(T_{j})}

for
h_{j-1}<i\leq h_{j}
,

–

\widetilde{\infty}^{\,\gamma}
for

\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty}:=\dfrac{\Gamma}{\Big(n_{1}T_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}T_{m}\Big)}
.

•

1
cells:
\widetilde{E}_{i}^{\gamma}
for
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

•

2
cells:
\widetilde{F}^{\gamma}
for
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

The deck group
\Gamma
acts on
X
by permuting cells, so the cellular
chain complex

0\to\mathcal{C}_{2}(X)\to\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)\to\mathcal{C}_{0}(X)\rightarrow 0

is also a chain complex of

Theorem 4.4
.

The elements
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-1}
span
H^{1}(X)_{\rho}
for any

\rho\in\mu^{\prime}(d_{1})\times\ldots\times\mu^{\prime}(d_{m})
. If

\rho_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\rho_{m}^{n_{m}}\neq 1
they are linearly
independent, otherwise they satisfy the following linear relation

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\ \sum_{i=h_{j-1}+1}^{h_{j}}\left(1-\rho_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\rho_{j-1}^{n_{j-1}}\,\rho_{j}^{\,i-h_{j-1}}\right)\omega_{i}=0.

Moreover for
1\leq j
and
j+1<k\leq m

\phi_{j,k}=\omega_{h_{j}}+\omega_{h_{j}+1}+\ldots+\omega_{h_{k-1}}.

Proof.

Let
M(P,\rho)
denote the matrix

M(P,\rho):=\Big(\left\langle\omega_{i},\omega_{j}\right\rangle,1\leq i,j\leq n-1\Big).

Since the only non-zero entries of
M
occur on the diagonal and the
adjacent off-diagonals, its determinant can be computed directly, giving

\det M(P,\rho)=(\sqrt{-1})^{n-1}\left(\frac{1-\rho_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\rho_{m}^{n_{m}}}{(1-\rho_{1})^{n_{1}}\cdots(1-\rho_{m})^{n_{m}}}\right).

Thus when
\rho_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\rho_{m}^{n_{m}}\neq 1
the
matrix
M
is non-singular and hence
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-1}

are linearly independent. Since in this case
\dim H^{1}(X)_{\rho}=n-1
,
this collection forms a basis.

When
\rho_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\rho_{m}^{n_{m}}=1
, the matrix

M
is singular. In fact in this case

\dim H^{1}(X)_{\rho}=n-2.

Let
M^{\prime}
be the
(n-2)\times(n-2)
minor of
M
obtained by deleting
its last row and last column. A similar reasoning as above
shows that in this case
M^{\prime}
is invertible and therefore,

\mathrm{rank}(M)=n-2
. Consequently the elements

\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-1}
span
H^{1}(X)_{\rho}
. However,
now they must satisfy a linear relation. To verify the relation
we consider the element

R=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\ \sum_{i=h_{j-1}+1}^{h_{j}}\left(1-\rho_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\rho_{j-1}^{n_{j-1}}\,\rho_{j}^{\,i-h_{j-1}}\right)\omega_{i}.

A direct computation shows that
\left\langle R,\omega_{i}\right\rangle=0
for all

i=1,\ldots,n-1
, and therefore
R=0
.

Finally, defining

\psi=\sum_{i=h_{j}}^{h_{k-1}}\omega_{i}

it is straight forward to verify
\left\langle\phi_{j,k}-\psi,\omega_{i}\right\rangle=0

for all
i=1,\ldots,n-1
.
∎

5.

Recall from (

given by
\gamma\mapsto\widetilde{\gamma}
induces the representation of
\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
on

H^{1}(X)_{\rho}
by
\gamma\cdot\xi=(\widetilde{\gamma}^{-1})^{*}\xi

In this section we shall describe this representation for

\rho\in\mu^{\prime}(d_{1})\times\cdots\times\mu^{\prime}(d_{m})

this case because if
\rho_{j}=1
for some index
j
then

H^{1}(X)_{\rho}\cong H^{1}(X/(T_{j}))_{\rho^{\prime}}\qquad\text{where}\qquad\rho^{\prime}=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\rho_{j}},\ldots,\rho_{m})

as
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma/(T_{j})]
-modules. Our arguments allow us to compute the representation
when
\rho_{j}\rho_{k}\neq 1
for any

since we need the self
intersections
\left\langle\omega_{i},\omega_{i}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle\phi_{j,k},\phi_{j,k}\right\rangle

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\tau=(\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n-1})^{n}
, then

\widetilde{\tau}=T_{1}^{-n_{1}}\cdots T_{m}^{-n_{m}}
in
\mcg(X)
and

\ker\Big(\lift:\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}\to\mcg(X)^{\Gamma}\Big)=\left(\,\tau^{f}\,\right)

Proof.

There is the capping homomorphism

\Cap:\mcg_{c}(\mathbb{C},P)\to\mcg(\mathbb{C},P)

whose kernel is the cyclic subgroup generated by
\tau
(see

[
6

\mcg(X)^{\Gamma}\to\mcg(\mathbb{C},P)
since the cover
\pi:X\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}
has the
Birman-Hilden property (
[
11

). The composition

\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}\xrightarrow{\lift}\mcg(X)^{\Gamma}\to\mcg(\mathbb{C},P)

is just
\Cap
restricted to
\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
. Hence
\ker(\lift)\subset\ker(\Cap)=(\tau)
.

Since
\tau
is in the kernel of
\Cap
the lift
\widetilde{\tau}
is homotopic
to a deck transformation. Moreover,
\widetilde{\tau}
fixes all the pre-images of
\infty
.
Thus
\widetilde{\tau}\simeq S^{l}
for some

, where

is a generator of the stabiliser
subgroup (of the deck group) for any pre-image of infinity.

Choose

such that
|p_{i}|<a
for all

. The mapping class

\tau
is represented by the Dehn twist supported on the annulus

a\leq|z|\leq a+1
:

\tau(z)=\begin{cases}\exp\big(2\pi\sqrt{-1}(|z|-a)\big)\,z,&a\leq|z|\leq a+1,\\
z,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Consider the homotopy
H_{t}
from
H_{0}=\tau
to
H_{1}=\id

defined by:

H_{t}(z)=\begin{cases}z&|z|<a,\\
\exp\big(2\pi\sqrt{-1}(1-t)(|z|-a)\big)\,z,&a\leq|z|\leq a+1,\\
\exp\big(2\pi\sqrt{-1}(1-t)\big)\,z,&|z|>a+1.\end{cases}

Note that this homotopy is not compactly supported.
Let
\widetilde{H}_{t}
be the unique lift of this homotopy such that

\widetilde{H}_{0}=\widetilde{\tau}
. Then we must have
\widetilde{H}_{1}=S^{l}
.

On a small disk
B
centred at
\infty
the homotopy
H_{t}
is given by

H_{t}(w)=\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}(t-1))\,w
in the local coordinate
w=1/z
. Let

\widetilde{\infty}_{0}
be a pre-image of
\infty
in
X
and
\widetilde{B}_{0}
be the component of

\pi^{-1}B
which contains
\widetilde{\infty}_{0}
. In suitable coordinates, the map

\pi:\widetilde{B}_{0}\to B
is a branched covering of the form
\pi(w)=w^{f}
.
It follows that the restriction of the lift
\widetilde{H}_{t}
to
\widetilde{B}_{0}
is

\widetilde{H}_{t}(w)=\zeta_{f}^{k}\,\exp\big(2\pi\sqrt{-1}(t-1)/f)\,w\qquad\text{for some}\qquad k\in\{0,\ldots,f-1\}.

Since
\widetilde{H}_{0}=\widetilde{\tau}
is identity on
\widetilde{B}_{0}
we see that
k=1
.
Evaluating at
t=1

S^{l}(w)=\widetilde{H}_{1}(w)=\zeta_{f}\,w\qquad\text{on}\qquad\widetilde{B}_{0}.

Now let us find out how
S
acts on
\widetilde{B}_{0}
. Note that
X
is the completion of

\{(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\mid y_{j}^{d_{j}}=(x-p_{h_{j-1}+1})\cdots(x-p_{h_{j}})\}.

The function
x
has a pole of order
f
at
\widetilde{\infty}_{0}
and

have poles
of order

. It can be seen that
f,fn_{1}/d_{1},\ldots,fn_{m}/d_{m}
are
co-prime, hence there are integers
\mu,\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{m}
such that

\mu f+\nu_{1}\left(\frac{fn_{1}}{d_{1}}\right)+\ldots+\nu_{m}\left(\frac{fn_{m}}{d_{m}}\right)=1.

Thus

is a local coordinate at

\widetilde{\infty}_{0}
. In this coordinate

S(w)=\zeta_{d_{1}}^{-n_{1}\nu_{1}}\cdots\zeta_{d_{m}}^{-n_{m}\nu_{m}}\,w=\zeta_{f}^{-1}w.

Thus it follows that
\widetilde{\tau}\simeq S^{-1}

Let

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\rho\in\mu^{\prime}(d_{1})\times\cdots,\mu^{\prime}(d_{m})
such that

\rho_{j}\neq\overline{\rho}_{k}
for any

In that case the action of the generators of the mixed braid group

\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
on
\xi\in H^{1}(X)_{\rho}
is given by complex
reflections as follows

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\xi-(\rho_{j}+1)\frac{\left\langle\xi,\omega_{i}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\omega_{i},\omega_{i}\right\rangle}\,\omega_{i}

\displaystyle h_{j-1}<i<h_{j},

\displaystyle A_{j,k}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\xi+\big(\rho_{j}\rho_{k}-1\big)\frac{\left\langle\xi,\phi_{j,k}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\phi_{j,k},\phi_{j,k}\right\rangle}\phi_{j,k}

\displaystyle 1\leq j<k\leq m,

where we define
\phi_{j,j+1}=\omega_{h_{j}}

Proof.

First note that
\sigma_{i}
is supported on the disk
\mathcal{D}_{i}
.
The eigenspace
H^{1}_{c}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i})_{\rho}
is one-dimensional and spanned by
\omega_{i}
.
Suppose
h_{j-1}<i<h_{j}
, then it follows from
[
14
, Theorem 4.4]
that

\sigma_{i}\cdot\omega_{i}=-\rho_{j}\omega_{i}.

We have a
\sigma_{i}
equivariant exact sequence

0\to H^{1}_{c}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i})_{\rho}\to H^{1}(X)_{\rho}\to H^{1}(X-\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i})_{\rho}\to 0.

Since
\sigma_{i}
acts trivially on
H^{1}(X-\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i})_{\rho}
the eigenvalues of

\sigma_{i}
on
H^{1}(X)_{\rho}
are
-\rho_{j}
and
1
. By our assumption

\rho_{j}\neq-1
and hence

\left\langle\omega_{i},\omega_{i}\right\rangle=2\im\rho_{j}\neq 0
.
Thus we have an orthogonal decomposition

H^{1}(X)_{\rho}=H^{1}_{c}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i})_{\rho}\oplus H^{1}_{c}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i})_{\rho}^{\perp}

with respect to the inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
. Thus

H^{1}_{c}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i})_{\rho}^{\perp}
must be the

-eigenspace of
\sigma_{i}
.

Now turning to
A_{j,k}
we note that it is supported on the disk
\mathcal{E}_{j,k}
.
As in proof of Proposition
3.1
we can identify
\mathcal{E}_{j,k}

with the open disk of radius
2
in
\mathbb{C}
, in which case we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j,k}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\cong\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid y^{d_{k}}=x^{d_{j}}-1,\quad|x^{d_{j}}|<2\}.

Under these identifications the branched covering

\pi:\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j,k}^{\gamma}\to\mathcal{E}_{j,k}
is given by

\pi(x,y)=x^{d_{j}}

and the deck transformations are
T_{j}(x,y)=(\zeta_{d_{j}}x,y)
and

T_{k}(x,y)=(x,\zeta_{d_{k}}y)
.
The mapping class
A_{j,k}
on
\mathcal{E}_{j,k}
is represented by the homeomorphism
(see Figure
1
)

A_{j,k}(z)=\begin{cases}\exp\big(2\pi\sqrt{-1}|z|\big)\,z&|z|\leq 1,\\
\exp\big(2\pi\sqrt{-1}(2-|z|)\big)\,z&1\leq|z|\leq 2.\end{cases}

Let
B:=\{x\in\mathbb{C}\mid|x|<2^{1/d_{j}}\}
, then
\pi
is the composition

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{j,k}^{\gamma}\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}}B\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime\prime}}\mathcal{E}_{j,k}

where
\pi^{\prime}(x,y)=x

\pi^{\prime\prime}(x)=x^{d_{j}}

A_{j,k}
to

B
under
\pi^{\prime\prime}

Again since

The rest of the argument is similar to the previous case.

The Multivariate Burau representation

The multivariate Burau representation

\widetilde{\beta}:\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}\left(\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{m}^{\pm 1}]\right)

arises from the action of
\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
on the homology, (with coefficients in

\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{m}^{\pm 1}]
), of the infinite Abelian cover of the
punctured plane
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\}
, obtained from the
homomorphism

\Pi_{1}(\mathbb{C}-\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\})\to\mathbb{Z}^{m},\qquad c_{i}\mapsto(0,\ldots,0,\underbrace{1}_{j},0,\ldots,0\}\text{ for }h_{j-1}<i\leq h_{j}.

This is a type of Magnus representation (see
[
1
, Chapter 3]
).
Explicitly, the action of the generators is given by the matrices

\widetilde{\beta}(\sigma_{i})=\begin{pmatrix}\id_{i-1}&0&0\\
0&\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1-t_{j}&1\\
t_{j}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)&0\\
0&0&\id_{n-i-1}\end{pmatrix}\qquad h_{j-1}<i<h_{j}

and for
1\leq j<k\leq m

\widetilde{\beta}(A_{j,k})=\id_{n}\ +\ \widetilde{R}_{j,k}\,\widetilde{S}_{j,k}

where the column vector
\widetilde{R}_{j,k}
and row vector
\widetilde{S}_{j,k}
are defined as:

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{j,k}

\displaystyle=(1-t_{k})e_{h_{j}}+(t_{j}-1)e_{h_{k-1}+1}

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{j,k}

\displaystyle=-t_{j}e_{h_{j}}^{T}\ +\ \sum_{r=j+1}^{k-1}\sum_{i=h_{r-1}+1}^{h_{r}}(1-t_{r})e_{i}^{T}\ +\ e_{h_{k-1}+1}^{T}.

These are calculated using formula 3-19 of
[
1
]
.
(Here as usual
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
are the columns of the identity matrix.
We have transposed the matrices from
[
1
]
to account for the difference in
convention; while
[
1
]
uses a right action with row vectors, we
have used the more standard left action with column vectors.)

The representation
\widetilde{\beta}
is reducible. Specifically, the submodule
spanned by

\sum_{j=1}^{m}(1-t_{j(i)})e_{i},\qquad\text{where}\qquad j(i)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\text{ such that }h_{j(i)-1}<i\leq h_{j(i)}

is invariant and carries the trivial representation. By passing to the quotient,
we obtain the reduced multivariate Burau representation:

\beta:\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}\left(\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{m}^{\pm 1}]\right).

For any choice of roots of unity
\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m})
, let

\mathrm{ev}_{\rho}:\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{m}^{\pm 1}]\to\mathbb{C}
be the
evaluation map
t_{i}\mapsto\rho_{i}
. The composition of
\beta
with
\mathrm{ev}_{\rho}
yields
a complex representation

\beta_{\rho}:\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{C})

referred to as the reduced multivariate Burau representation evaluated at
\rho
.

Recall the dual representation
\beta_{\rho}^{*}
is defined as

\beta_{\rho}^{*}(b)=\beta_{\rho}(b^{-1})^{T},\qquad b\in\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}.

Proposition 5.5
.

The representation
\theta_{\rho}
is isomorphic to the dual
\beta_{\overline{\rho}}^{*}
of the
reduced multivariate Burau representation evaluated at
\overline{\rho}
, when

\rho_{j}\rho_{k}\neq 1
for any
j,k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and

\prod_{j}\rho_{j}^{n_{j}}\neq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\beta}_{\rho}=\mathrm{ev}_{\rho}\circ\widetilde{\beta}
, then we shall show that

\widetilde{\beta}_{\overline{\rho}}^{*}\cong\theta_{\rho}\oplus\mathbf{1}\qquad\text{where}\qquad\mathbf{1}\text{ is the trivial representation.}

Let
v_{i}=-\overline{\rho}_{j(i)}e_{i}+e_{i+1}
, then

•

for
i\neq h_{j}
the matrix
\beta_{\overline{\rho}}(\sigma_{i})^{T}
is a complex reflection with

\beta_{\overline{\rho}}(\sigma_{i})^{T}v_{i}=\overline{\rho}_{j(i)}v_{i}
and

•

\beta_{\overline{\rho}}(A_{j,j+1})^{T}
is a complex reflection with

\beta_{\overline{\rho}}(A_{j,j+1})^{T}v_{h_{j}}=\overline{\rho}_{j}\overline{\rho}_{j+1}v_{h_{j}}
.

Moreover, let
w=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1-\rho_{j(i)})e_{i}
then

\widetilde{\beta}_{\overline{\rho}}(\sigma_{i})^{T}w=\widetilde{\beta}_{\overline{\rho}}(A_{j,k})^{T}w=w,\qquad\text{for all }1\leq j<k\leq n\text{ and }i\neq h_{j}.

Thus we work with the basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1},w
.
Let
B
be the change of basis matrix

B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-\overline{\rho}_{j(1)}&0&0&&\cdots&0&1-\overline{\rho}_{j(1)}\\
1&-\overline{\rho}_{j(2)}&0&&\cdots&0&1-\overline{\rho}_{j(2)}\\
0&1&\ddots&\\
0&0&\ddots\\
\vdots&\vdots&&&&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&&&\cdots&-\overline{\rho}_{j(n-1)}&1-\overline{\rho}_{j(n-1)}\\
0&0&&&\cdots&1&1-\overline{\rho}_{j(n)}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\quad\in\quad\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

Then, a direct computation shows

B^{-1}\left(\widetilde{\beta}_{\overline{\rho}}(\sigma_{i})^{T}\right)^{-1}B=\begin{pmatrix}\theta_{\rho}(\sigma_{i})&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B^{-1}\left(\widetilde{\beta}_{\overline{\rho}}(A_{j,k})^{T}\right)^{-1}B=\begin{pmatrix}\theta_{\rho}(A_{j,k})&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\widetilde{\beta}_{\rho}\cong\beta_{\rho}\oplus\mathbf{1}
the result follows.
∎

Image of the representation

The image
\theta_{\rho}(\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda})
is contained in

\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}\big(\mathbb{Z}[\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}]\big)
. It will be a very
interesting problem to classify the partitions
\Lambda
and the roots of unity

\rho
for which the image is an arithmetic subgroup.

If
P=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})
and
n_{i}>1
for some
i
, we have

\mathcal{B}_{n_{i}}\subset\mathcal{B}_{n,\Lambda}
. Let

\rho_{i}=\zeta_{d_{i}}^{-k_{i}},\qquad r_{i}=\lceil n_{i}(k_{i}/d_{i})-1\rceil\qquad\text{and}\qquad s_{i}=\lceil n(1-k_{i}/d_{i})-1\rceil

then
\theta_{\rho}

\mathcal{B}_{n_{i}}\to\U(r_{i},s_{i})\subset U(r,s)

\mathcal{B}_{n_{i}}

given in
[
14

14

We suspect this to be an infinite group, in which case the question of discreteness
