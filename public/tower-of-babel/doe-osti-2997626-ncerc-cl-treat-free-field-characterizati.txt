1 Introduction
This report summarizes the analysis and preliminary MCNP reaction rate modeling of free field
characterization measurements performed in the Transient Reaction Test Facility (TREAT) [1] at
Idaho National Laboratory (INL) between 12/18/2023 and 2/22/2024. A series of seven irradiations
were performed using the Big-BUSTER (Broad Use Specimen Transient Experiment Rig) [1] core
configuration. Foil sets including 19.5% LEU-Zr alloy wire, S, Au, Fe, Ni, Co, Ti, and Zr were
deployed in each irradiation. Identical foil sets were supplied to Los Alamos National Laboratory
(LANL), Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL), and INL. All foils were supplied by
INL. Additional details on the experiment can be found in [2].
The foil sets were shipped to the National Criticality Experiments Research Center Counting Labo-
ratory (NCERC-CL) branch at LANL (NCERC-CL NISC) and were received on 3/25/2024. Addi-
tional details on receipt and counting are reported in [3]. All reactor dosimetry measurements were
performed in adherence to the ASTM standards applicable to reactor dosimetry.
Measured reaction rates from Au, Co, Fe, Ni, and Ti are reported. Select reaction rate ratios and
simulated reaction rates and ratios are discussed. Reaction rates for all measured reaction products
were modeled with MCNP 6.3.1 [4] and a TREAT input deck supplied by Edward Lum. Neu-
tron emission estimates calculated using the measured and simulated reaction rates show excellent
agreement between the three fast-threshold n-p reactions with the percent differences being < 9%
between the three reactions. The capture reactions had poorer, but still reasonable, agreement with
< 20% percent differences between the neutron emission estimates of the three capture reactions.
The percent difference between the fast-threshold and capture reactions is ∼ 100% for each reaction.
This supports the TREAT model is not accurately modeling the neutron spectrum and additional
measurements are required to characterize TREAT and match measurements to simulation.

4 Reaction rate measurements and analysis
All counting and analysis methods were performed in adherence to ASTM E720-23 [5] and the
ASTM standards referenced in E720-23.

4.1 Reaction product counting
The Au, Co, Fe, Ni, and Ti foils were counted on two high-purity germanium detectors (HPGes)
for validation. One was a Canberra 100% efficiency GX10023 (detector ID Leo). The second was
an Ortec PopTop 77% efficient coaxial HPGe (detector ID Starburst). All foils were counted 6 cm
from the front face of the detectors. An Ortec DSPEC 502A multi-channel analyzer (MCA) was
used with Starburst, and an Ortec DigiDart 32k channel MCA was used with Leo. All reaction
rates and ratios presented in this report were measured using Starburst.
The detector efficiencies were measured using two multi-nuclide sources each with 13 gamma lines
between 59 keV and 1836 keV. Multiple measurements were performed with each source. Agreement
between the two sources was < 2% over the energy range of the foil reaction product gammas.
Efficiencies were calculated and fit using the InterSpec software package [6]. The efficiency fits for
Starburst between 400 keV and 1500 keV are shown in Figure 2. Multi-nuclide source ID SN2457-
12 was measured 3 times on the same day, where the source was removed, replaced, and then
remeasured in the 6 cm sample holder slot to test reproducibility. The vertical dashed line marks
the 412 keV Au-198 gamma energy where the spread between the different calibration measurements
was the largest for the energy range applicable to the measured reaction products in the foils. The
maximum to minimum bounds at 412 keV for the two different sources and four measurements is
1.2%.

5 Results

5.1 Measured reaction rates and ratios
The measured reaction rates are tabulated in Table 7 in Appendix A. Uncertainty is only reported for
the specific activity (eq. (7)). This uncertainty is representative for all reported quantities because
it is dominated by (1) the efficiency calibration and (2) measurement systematics. 58 Fe(n,γ)59 Fe
is an exception to this due to a large uncertainty contribution from the 58 Fe natural abundance
(∼4% uncertainty compared to ∼0% uncertainty for the other target nuclides). Reaction rate ratios
with 58 Fe(n,γ)59 Fe (Table 8), 59 Co(n,γ)60 Co (Table 9), and 197 Au(n,γ)198 Au are also tabulated in
Tables 8 to 10 in Appendix A.
Figure 3 shows the reaction rates in units of product atom per target atom. The reaction rates
follow the expected trend of the total energy released in each burst reported in Table 1. Figure 4
shows the reaction rate ratios for all products with (a) Co-60 in the denominator and (b) with Fe-59
in the denominator. Figure 5 shows a zoomed-in view of Figure 4b. The reaction rate ratios for
a given numerator and denominator are in agreement within uncertainty, supporting that neither
the reactor neutron spectrum nor the cross sections are being impacted outside of the measurement
uncertainty by the temperature differences between the bursts. All reaction rate ratios for a given
nuclide agree within 2-σ for all bursts.

5.3 Neutron emission estimates
The combination of measured and simulated data can be used to estimate the total neutron fluence
which can give insight into potential sources of bias between simulation and measurement. The
reaction rates are related to the source term by

 Rmeas = SRsim (8)

where Rmeas is the measured reaction rate in product atom per target atom, Rsim is the simulated
reaction rate, and S is the number of neutrons emitted in the burst. Table 6 shows the inputs
for eq. (8) and the neutron emission estimate. The F5 tally output, as defined by the FM tally
multiplier shown in the previous section, must be multiplied by 1E-24 to relate it to the measured
reaction rate and neutron emission as shown in eq. (8). The percent difference columns show the
fast threshold reactions are in excellent agreement with each other. The capture reactions are also
in general agreement with 58 Fe(n,γ)59 Fe as a slight outlier. The mean neutron emission estimates

6 Discussion
TREAT is a graphite moderated, thermal reactor. Unknowns associated with the graphite com-
position, form, radiation damage, and thermal scatter laws can lead to large deviations between
measurements and simulation. The reported reaction rates provide some insight into these un-
knowns, but more measurements would be required for any conclusive statements. There is clear
disagreement between the measurements and the simulations in the ratio of the fast to thermal
reactions. As stated above, this could be explained by issues with modeling the graphite modera-
tor. More measurements, including more capture reactions and cadmium covered capture reactions,
would be required to make any conclusive statements.
Another point of interest in the comparison of the thermal capture reaction rate results is the
difference between the cross sections. Figure 7 shows the measured reaction product cross sections.
The 197 Au(n,γ)198 Au cross section is much larger in the thermal regime than 59 Co(n,γ)60 Co, which
has a larger cross section at lower energy than 58 Fe(n,γ)59 Fe. 58 Fe(n,γ)59 Fe has significantly higher
sensitivity to intermediate neutron energies. It also has the poorest agreement with simulation with
the neutron emission estimate being 20 - 25% higher than the other capture reactions. The data
reported here is not enough for any conclusive statements, but additional measurements of TREAT
are required to account for the discrepancies between measurements and simulations.

6.1 Notes for future TREAT measurements
Lessons learned and items that should be noted for future TREAT irradiations are listed below:
 • Burnout of 58 Ni(n,p)58 Co (and 58mCo) in thermal fluxes > 3 × 1012 n·cm−2 ·s−1 is a noted
 issue in ASTM E264-19 and should be addressed.
 • Mn diffusion from Fe at temps > 700◦ C is noted in ASTM E263-18. Fe should not be used if
 temperatures approach 700◦ C.
 • 58 Fe(n,γ)59 Fe is not recommended by ASTM E720 for adjustments due to “large uncertainties
 or because of conflicts with other reactions during spectrum adjustment procedures.” While it
 is not recommend for use, it is a valuable data point for comparison to other capture reactions.

Los Alamos National Laboratory Page 12

 • ASTM E526-22 notes that 46 Sc depletion should be investigated for the 46 Ti(n,p)46 Sc reaction
 in high thermal-neutron fluence rates.
 • The Co-Ag method from ASTM E481-23 should be considered for measuring thermal neutron
 fluence if temperatures exceed the Cd melting point.

7 Conclusions
Measured and simulated reaction rates and reaction rate ratios from the 2024 TREAT Big BUSTER
free field characterization experiments were reported. Neutron emission estimates calculated using
the measured and simulated reaction rates show excellent agreement between the fast-threshold n-p
reactions with the percent differences being < 9% for the three reactions measured. The capture
reactions had poorer, but still reasonable, agreement with < 20% percent differences between the
neutron emission estimates of the three capture reactions. The percent difference between the fast-
threshold and capture reactions is ∼ 100% for each reaction. The reported results support that the
MCNP model does not appropriately capture the neutron energy spectrum. Additional measure-
ments of TREAT are needed to understand discrepancies between the measured and simulated data
and to improve the models.
