1.
Introduction

The spaces now called de Branges–Rovnyak spaces
were introduced by de Branges and Rovnyak
in the appendix of
[
5
]

and further studied in
[
6
]
.
They were later popularized by the book of Sarason
[
18
]
.
It is now known that these spaces have a variety of connections
with other topics in complex analysis and operator theory,
and they have attracted the interest of many authors.

When working with de Branges–Rovnyak spaces,
it is very helpful
to have a formula for the norm (and, via polarization,
for the inner product) of functions in these spaces,
expressed in terms of their Taylor coefficients.
There does indeed exist such a formula, namely,

(1)

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}\text{~``=''~}\sum_{m\geq 0}|\widehat{f}(m)|^{2}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}.

Here
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
is the de Branges–Rovnyak space
associated to a non-extreme point
b
of the unit ball of
H^{\infty}
,
and
\phi=b/a
, where
a
is the Pythagorean mate of
b

(all these terms will be explained in §
2
below).
Also
\widehat{f}(n),\widehat{\phi}(n)
are the
n
-th
coefficients of the Taylor expansions around
0
of
f,\phi
respectively.

What is noteworthy about the formula (
1
)
is that it is only sometimes
true—hence the inverted commas around the equals sign.
It
is
always true if
f
is a polynomial.
Indeed, the special case when
f(z)=z^{k}
, namely

\|z^{k}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}=1+\sum_{n=0}^{k}|\widehat{\phi}(n)|^{2},

is well known and used in many places in the literature.
More generally, equality holds in (
1
)
if
f
is holomorphic in a neighbourhood of the closed unit disk;
this is implicit in
[
20
]
, and proved explicitly in

[
3
]
. As polynomials are dense
in
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
, one might be tempted to infer that same formula
holds for all
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
, but in fact this turns out to be false
in general.
Counterexamples can be found, for example, in
[
8
]

and
[
16
]
, and there are more in this article.

In view of the potential applications,
it is of great interest to widen the scope of validity
of the formula to include as many functions
f
as possible.
This is the goal of the present paper.
Here is a summary of our main results:

•

If
\phi
belongs to the Hardy space
H^{2}
,
then equality holds in (
1
)
for all
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
(Theorem
3.1
).
On the other hand, this is no longer necessarily the
case if merely
\phi\in\cap_{p<2}H^{p}

(Corollary
6.2
).

•

If
\phi
belongs to
H^{p}
for some
p\in(0,2]
, then
equality holds in (
1
) for all
f\in H^{2}

for which the right-hand side of (
1
) is finite
and such that, in addition,
\sum_{m\geq 0}m^{2/p-1}|\widehat{f}(m)|^{2}<\infty

(Theorem
4.1
).

•

If
\phi
is rational
then equality holds in (
1
) for all
f\in H^{2}

such that the right-hand side of (
1
) is finite.
If, in addition,
all the poles of
\phi
on the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}
are simple,
then equality holds in (
1
) for all
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
.
On the other hand, if
\phi
has a multiple pole on
{\mathbb{T}}
,
then (
1
) breaks down for at least one
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)

(Theorem
5.1
).

•

There is a limit form of the formula (
1
)
that is valid for all all Pythagorean pairs
(b,a)
and all
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
(Theorem
7.1
).

2.
Background on de Branges–Rovnyak spaces

In this section we review de Branges–Rovnyak spaces and some related notions
that will be needed in the sequel. The treatment is deliberately brief.
The interested reader may find much more information in the books
of Sarason
[
18
]
and Fricain–Mashreghi
[
10
]
.

2.1.
De Branges–Rovnyak spaces

Let
b\in H^{\infty}
with
\|b\|_{H^{\infty}}\leq 1
.
Then the map
(z,w)\mapsto(1-\overline{b(w)}b(z))/(1-\overline{w}z)
is
positive-definite on
{\mathbb{D}}\times{\mathbb{D}}
, so it is the reproducing kernel
of a unique Hilbert function space on
{\mathbb{D}}
. This space is
called the
de Branges–Rovnyak space
associated to
b
,
and is denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
. It is always contractively contained
in the Hardy space
H^{2}
.

The theory of de Branges–Rovnyak spaces is governed by a fundamental
dichotomy, namely whether or not
b
is an extreme point of the unit ball of
H^{\infty}
.
For example, if
b
is non-extreme, then
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
contains the polynomials and they are dense
in
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
. On the other hand, if
b
is extreme, then the polynomials in
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
only span a
finite-dimensional subspace. In this article, we shall be concerned exclusively with the case where
b

is non-extreme.

2.2.
Pythagorean pairs

We say that
(b,a)
is a
Pythagorean pair
if

•

b,a\in H^{\infty}
;

•

|b|^{2}+|a|^{2}=1
a.e. on
{\mathbb{T}}
;

•

a
is outer and
a(0)>0
.

The function
a
is completely determined by
b
, and is
called its
Pythagorean mate
.
A function
b

occurs as the first element of a Pythagorean pair
iff it is a non-extreme point of the unit ball of
H^{\infty}
.
Thus the map
(b,a)\mapsto b
is a bijection between the set of
Pythagorean pairs and the set of non-extreme points of the unit
ball of
H^{\infty}
.

Given a Pythagorean pair
(b,a)
, we write

T_{\overline{b}}
and
T_{\overline{a}}
for the bounded Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}

with symbols
\overline{b}
and
\overline{a}
respectively.
The following characterization of
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
will be fundamental in what follows.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
18
, §IV-1]
)
.

Let
(b,a)
be a Pythagorean pair, and let
f\in H^{2}
.
Then
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
iff there exists
g\in H^{2}
such that
T_{\overline{b}}f=T_{\overline{a}}g
.
In this case,
g
is uniquely determined, and we have

(2)

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

2.3.
The Smirnov class

The Smirnov class
N^{+}
consists of those holomorphic functions

\phi
on
{\mathbb{D}}
that can be represented as

\phi=\psi/\chi
, where
\psi,\chi\in H^{\infty}
and
\chi
is outer.
It is an algebra containing all the Hardy spaces
H^{p}~(0<p\leq\infty)
.
For more about
N^{+}
and
H^{p}
we refer to
Duren’s book
[
7
]
.

Evidently, if
(b,a)
is a Pythagorean pair, then
b/a\in N^{+}
.
Conversely, every
\phi\in N^{+}
has a unique representation

\phi=b/a
, where
(b,a)
is a Pythagorean pair
(see e.g.
[
20
, Proposition 3.1]
).
Thus the map
(b,a)\mapsto b/a
is a bijection
between the set of Pythagorean pairs and the Smirnov class.

The correspondence between
(b,a)
and
\phi
comes into play
when considering formulas for
\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}
expressed in terms of the
Taylor coefficients of
f
.
The following result
is implicit in the work of Sarason
[
20
]

and was formally established in
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(b,a)
be a Pythagorean pair and let
\phi:=b/a
.
If
f
is holomorphic on an open neighbourhood of
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
, then
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
and

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}|\widehat{f}(m)|^{2}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}.

As explained in the introduction, the goal of this article is to extend
Theorem
2.2
to a wider range of functions
f
.

3.
The case when
\phi\in H^{2}

The de Branges–Rovnyak spaces
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
for which
\phi\in H^{2}

form a well-known subclass. Indeed, from
[
17
, Theorem 1]
, we have

\phi\in H^{2}\iff\frac{1}{1-|b|^{2}}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})\iff\sup_{n\geq 0}\|z^{n}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}<\infty\iff H^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{H}}(b).

A recent result in
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
shows that we can replace
H^{\infty}

in this last statement by any one of the spaces BMOA, VMOA (holomorphic functions of bounded/vanishing mean oscillation), or

A({\mathbb{D}})
(the disk algebra).
The condition
\phi\in H^{2}
also characterizes the de Branges–Rovnyak spaces that admit
a reverse Carleson measure
[
1
, Corollary 4.4]
.

Our goal in this section is to establish the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(b,a)
be a Pythagorean pair such that
\phi:=b/a\in H^{2}
,
and let
f\in H^{2}
.

(i)

For each
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we have

\sum_{n\geq 0}|\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)|<\infty.

(ii)

We have
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
if and only if

(3)

\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}<\infty,

in which case

(4)

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}|\widehat{f}(m)|^{2}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}.

Proof.

(i)
For each
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}|\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)|

\displaystyle\leq(\sum_{n\geq 0}|\widehat{\phi}(n)|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{n\geq 0}|\widehat{f}(n)|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\|\phi\|_{H^{2}}\|f\|_{H^{2}}<\infty.

This proves (i). For future use, we note also that
\overline{\phi}f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
and that

\widehat{\overline{\phi}f}(m)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\quad(m\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

(ii)
Suppose first that (
3
) holds.
Set

g(z):=\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl(\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr)z^{m}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

Then
g\in H^{2}
,
and so
(\overline{\phi}f-g)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
.
Also
\widehat{g}(m)=\widehat{\overline{\phi}f}(m)

for all
m\geq 0
, so the non-negative Fourier coefficients
of
(\overline{\phi}f-g)
all vanish.
Therefore there exists
h\in H^{1}_{0}
such that

\overline{\phi}f-g=\overline{h}\quad\text{a.e. on~}{\mathbb{T}}.

Multiplying up by
\overline{a}
, we get

\overline{b}f-\overline{a}g=\overline{ah}\quad\text{a.e. on~}{\mathbb{T}}.

Then
ah\in H^{1}_{0}\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})=H^{2}_{0}
, so
P_{+}(\overline{ah})=0
, where
P_{+}:L^{2}\to H^{2}
is the canonical projection.
We deduce that
T_{\overline{b}}(f)=T_{\overline{a}}(g)
.
By Theorem
2.1
, it follows that
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
and that

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}|\widehat{f}(m)|^{2}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2},

which establishes (
4
).

Conversely, suppose that
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
.
By Theorem
2.1
, there exists
g\in H^{2}

such that
T_{\overline{b}}(f)=T_{\overline{a}}(g)
. This means that there exists
h\in H^{2}_{0}
such that

\overline{b}f-\overline{a}g=\overline{h}\quad\text{a.e. on~}{\mathbb{T}}.

Dividing through by
\overline{a}
, we get

\overline{\phi}f-g=\overline{h/a}\quad\text{a.e. on~}{\mathbb{T}}.

Now
h\in H^{2}
and
a
is a bounded outer function,
so
h/a\in N^{+}
. Also, since
\phi,f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
,
we have

(\overline{\phi}f-g)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
, and thus
h/a\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
.
By the Smirnov maximum principle
(see e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 2.11]
),
it follows that
h/a\in H^{1}
. Further,
since
h(0)=0
, we in fact have
h/a\in H^{1}_{0}
.
Therefore the non-negative Fourier coefficients of

(\overline{\phi}f-g)
all vanish, and so

\widehat{g}(m)=\widehat{\overline{\phi}f}(m)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\quad(m\geq 0).

As
g\in H^{2}
, we deduce that (
3
) holds.
∎

4.
The case when
\phi\in H^{p}

Let
(b,a)
be a Pythagorean pair and let
\phi:=b/a
.
If
0<p\leq\infty
, then

\phi\in H^{p}\iff(1-|b|^{2})^{-1/2}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}).

Our goal in this section is to broaden the scope
of Theorem
3.1
to cover the case when
\phi\in H^{p}

where
0<p\leq 2
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
p\in(0,2]
, and
let
(b,a)
be a Pythagorean pair such that
\phi:=b/a\in H^{p}
.
Let
f
be a holomorphic function on
{\mathbb{D}}
whose Taylor coefficients satisfy

(5)

\sum_{m\geq 0}m^{2/p-1}|\widehat{f}(m)|^{2}<\infty.

(i)

For all
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we have

\sum_{n\geq 0}\Bigl|\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|<\infty.

(ii)

If

(6)

\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}<\infty,

then
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
and

(7)

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}|\widehat{f}(m)|^{2}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}.

Remark
.

Theorem
4.1
is less satisfactory than
Theorem
3.1
since it requires the extra hypothesis
(
5
) and, even with this hypothesis, it does not assert that the condition (
6
) holds for all
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
.
On the other hand, as we shall see later,
we cannot simply replace the assumption

\phi\in H^{2}
in Theorem
3.1
by
\phi\in H^{p}
or even by

\phi\in\cap_{p<2}H^{p}
(see Corollary
6.2
below).

The proof of Theorem
4.1
makes use of
distributions on the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}
,
so, before embarking upon the proof, we review
some basic facts about these distributions that will be needed
in what follows.

A
distribution
on
{\mathbb{T}}
is a linear functional
u:C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})\to{\mathbb{C}}

such that, for some constants
C,k
,

(8)

|\langle u,\psi\rangle|\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|\psi^{(j)}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\quad(\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})).

A sequence
(u_{j})
of distributions
converges in distribution
to
u
if

\langle u_{j},\psi\rangle\to\langle u,\psi\rangle

for each
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
.
The
Fourier coefficients
of a distribution
u
on
{\mathbb{T}}
are defined by

\widehat{u}(n):=\langle u,e^{-in\theta}\rangle\quad(n\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

If
u
satisfies (
8
), then
\widehat{u}(n)=O(|n|^{k})

as
|n|\to\infty
. A distribution
u
can be recovered from its
Fourier coefficients via the formula

\langle u,\psi\rangle=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{u}(n)\widehat{\psi}(n)\quad(\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})).

Let
\phi\in H^{p}
, where
0<p\leq\infty
. Then
\phi
can be identified
with a distribution via the formula

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle:=\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\phi(re^{i\theta})\psi(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta\quad(\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})).

The non-negative Fourier coefficients of
\phi
then coincide with its Taylor coefficients (so there is no ambiguity in the notation
\widehat{\phi}(n)
). The negative Fourier coefficients are all zero.

For each
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the
Sobolev space

W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}})

consists of those distributions
u
on
{\mathbb{T}}
such that

\|u\|_{W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}^{2}:=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(1+n^{2})^{s}|\widehat{u}(n)|^{2}<\infty.

Hardy spaces and Sobolev spaces are linked by
the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p\in(0,2]
and let
s=1/p-1/2
.
Then
H^{p}\subset W^{-s,2}({\mathbb{T}})
,
and there exists a constant
C_{p}
such that

(9)

\|\phi\|_{W^{-s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq C_{p}\|\phi\|_{H^{p}}\quad(\phi\in H^{p}).

Proof.

For
p\in[1,2]
, this is a consequence of
[
11
, Theorem 1]
.
For
p\in(0,1)
it follows from
[
7
, Theorem 6.6]
.
∎

If
u,v\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
, then
uv\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
and its Fourier coefficients are given by

\widehat{uv}(m)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{u}(n)\widehat{v}(m-n)\quad(m\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

If
u\in W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}})
and
v\in W^{-s,2}({\mathbb{T}})

for some
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then we can reverse-engineer this
last formula to define the product
uv
as a distribution on
{\mathbb{T}}
.
The details are elaborated in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let
u\in W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}})
and
v\in W^{-s,2}({\mathbb{T}})
.

(i)

For each
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

(10)

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\widehat{u}(n)\widehat{v}(m-n)|\leq(1+m^{2})^{s/2}\|u\|_{W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}\|v\|_{W^{-s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}.

(ii)

There exists a unique distribution
uv
on
{\mathbb{T}}
such that

(11)

\widehat{uv}(m)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{u}(n)\widehat{v}(m-n)\quad(m\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

(iii)

There exists a constant
C_{s}
such that, for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
,

(12)

|\langle uv,\psi\rangle|\leq C_{s}\|u\|_{W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}\|v\|_{W^{-s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}\sum_{0\leq j\leq s+2}\|\psi^{(j)}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}.

Proof.

By Cauchy–Schwarz, for each
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\widehat{u}(n)\widehat{v}(m-n)|

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(1+n^{2})^{s}|\widehat{u}(n)|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(1+n^{2})^{-s}|\widehat{v}(m-n)|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{(1+n^{2})^{s/2}}{(1+(m-n)^{2})^{s/2}}\|u\|_{W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}\|v\|_{W^{-s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}

\displaystyle\leq(1+m^{2})^{s/2}\|u\|_{W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}\|v\|_{W^{-s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}.

This establishes (i).
We shall prove (ii) and (iii) together.
Define a linear functional
uv:C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

\langle uv,\psi\rangle:=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Bigl(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{u}(n)\widehat{v}(m-n)\Bigr)\widehat{\psi}(m)\quad(\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})).

Then, for each
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
, we have

\displaystyle|\langle uv,\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Bigl|\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{u}(n)\widehat{v}(m-n)\Bigr||\widehat{\psi}(m)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(1+m^{2})^{s/2}\|u\|_{W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}\|v\|_{W^{-s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}|\widehat{\psi}(m)|

and, if
k
is the smallest integer such that
k>s+1/2
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(1+m^{2})^{s/2}|\widehat{\psi}(m)|

\displaystyle\leq|\widehat{\psi}(0)|+\Bigl(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{(1+m^{2})^{s}}{m^{2k}}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}|m^{2k}\widehat{\psi}(m)|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}+\Bigl(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{(1+m^{2})^{s}}{m^{2k}}\Bigr)^{1/2}\|\psi^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}.

Combining these observations leads quickly to the estimate (
12
).
It also shows that
uv
is a distribution on
{\mathbb{T}}
. Clearly
the Fourier coefficients of
uv
are given by (
11
), and they determine
uv
uniquely.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

(i) Let
s:=1/p-1/2
.
By Lemma
4.2
,
\phi\in W^{-s,2}({\mathbb{T}})
,
and by the hypothesis (
6
),
we have
f\in W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}})
. Hence, by Lemma
4.3
(i),
for each
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we have

\sum_{n\geq 0}|\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)|\leq(1+m^{2})^{s/2}\|\phi\|_{W^{-s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}\|f\|_{W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}<\infty.

This proves (i). We also note for future use that
\overline{\phi}f
is a distribution
on
{\mathbb{T}}
satisfying

\widehat{\overline{\phi}f}(m)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\quad(m\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

(ii) The argument here is broadly similar to that in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, but more care is required because
some of the terms being manipulated
are distributions rather than functions.

Suppose that the condition (
6
) holds.
Set

g(z):=\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl(\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr)z^{m}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

Then
g\in H^{2}
and
\widehat{g}(m)=\widehat{\overline{\phi}f}(m)

for all
m\geq 0
, so
(\overline{\phi}f-g)
is a distribution on
{\mathbb{T}}

whose non-negative Fourier coefficients all vanish.
Let
r\in(0,1)
and let
a_{r}
be the
r
-dilation of
a
.
Since
a_{r}
is holomorphic on a neighbourhood of
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
, we can multiply by
\overline{a}_{r}

to obtain a distribution
\overline{a}_{r}(\overline{\phi}f-g)
that still has
the property that its non-negative Fourier coefficients vanish.
We claim that
\overline{a}_{r}(\overline{\phi}f-g)
converges in
distribution to
(\overline{b}f-\overline{a}g)
as
r\to 1
.
If so, then the non-negative Fourier coefficients of

(\overline{b}f-\overline{a}g)
all vanish. Since

(\overline{b}f-\overline{a}g)\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
, it follows that

P_{+}(\overline{b}f-\overline{a}g)=0
, where
P_{+}:L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})\to H^{2}

is the canonical projection. We deduce that
T_{\overline{b}}(f)=T_{\overline{a}}(g)
.
By Theorem
2.1
, it follows that
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
and that

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}|\widehat{f}(m)|^{2}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2},

which establishes (
7
).

It remains to justify the claim that

\overline{a}_{r}(\overline{\phi}f-g)
converges in
distribution to
(\overline{b}f-\overline{a}g)
as
r\to 1
.
Since
g\in H^{2}
and
a\in H^{\infty}
, the
dominated convergence theorem implies
that
\|\overline{a}_{r}g-\overline{a}g\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}\to 0
,
so in particular
\overline{a}_{r}g
converges
in distribution to
\overline{a}g
.
The proof that
\overline{a}_{r}(\overline{\phi}f)
converges
in distribution to
\overline{b}f
is more delicate.
The first step is to show that

\overline{a}_{r}(\overline{\phi}f)=(\overline{a_{r}\phi})f
.
For this, it suffices to check that the two sides have the
same Fourier coefficients, and a calculation shows that
this boils down to showing that a certain double series commutes.
Since
a_{r}
is holomorphic on a neighbourhood of
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
,
its Fourier coefficients decay exponentially, which implies that
the double series in question converges absolutely, and hence it does commute.
So indeed

\overline{a}_{r}(\overline{\phi}f)=(\overline{a_{r}\phi})f

for each
r<1
. Next, by the dominated convergence theorem,
we have
\|(a_{r}-a)\phi\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\to 0
as
r\to 1
.
As
a,a_{r}\in H^{\infty}
,
\phi\in H^{p}
and
a\phi=b
,
this implies that
\|a_{r}\phi-b\|_{H^{p}}\to 0
.
From (
9
) it follows that

\|\overline{a_{r}\phi}-\overline{b}\|_{W^{-s,2}({\mathbb{T}})}\to 0
.
By (
12
), this in turn implies that

\langle(\overline{a_{r}\phi}-\overline{b})f,\psi\rangle\to 0

for each
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
, in other words,
that
(\overline{a_{r}\phi})f

converges in distribution to
\overline{b}f
.
This completes the justification of the claim
and with it, the proof of the
theorem.
∎

5.
The case when
\phi
is rational

The de Branges–Rovnyak spaces
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
with
rational
b
have been studied by a number of authors.
References include
[
4
,
9
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.
In particular, there are interesting connections with Dirichlet-type spaces,
which were first noticed by Sarason
[
19
]
.

Let
(b,a)
a Pythagorean pair and let
\phi=b/a
. Then

b\text{~is rational~}\iff(b,a)\text{~are both rational~}\iff\phi\text{~is rational}.

In this case, the zeros of
a
on
{\mathbb{T}}
are exactly the poles of

\phi
on
{\mathbb{T}}
, with the same multiplicities.
Listing them (with multiplicity) as
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}
,
we have

(13)

f\in H(b)\iff f(z)=g(z)\prod_{j=1}^{k}(z-\lambda_{j})+p(z),

where
g\in H^{2}
and
p
is a polynomial of degree at most
k-1

(see
[
4
, Lemma 4.3]
).

Our goal in this section is to establish the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(b,a)
be a rational Pythagorean pair, and let
\phi:=b/a
.

(i)

If
f\in H^{2}
and

(14)

\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}<\infty,

then
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
and

(15)

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}|\widehat{f}(m)|^{2}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}.

(ii)

If all the poles of
\phi
on
{\mathbb{T}}
are simple, and if

f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
, then (
14
) holds.

(iii)

If
\phi
has a multiple pole on
{\mathbb{T}}
,
then there exist
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
and
m\geq 0
such that the series

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)

diverges, and consequently (
14
) and (
15
) fail to hold
for this
f
.

Remark
.

It is implicit in (
14
)
that each individual series

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)

converges (not necessarily absolutely).

For the proof of Theorem
5.1
,
we use the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\rho(z)
be a rational function with poles outside
{\mathbb{D}}
.

(i)

If there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
\widehat{\rho}(j)=O(j^{\alpha})
as
j\to\infty
,
then all the poles of
\rho
on
{\mathbb{T}}
are simple.

(ii)

Conversely, if all the poles of
\rho
on
{\mathbb{T}}
are simple, then

\sup_{j}|\widehat{\rho}(j)|<\infty
.

Proof.

(i) Suppose that
|\widehat{\rho}(j)|\leq C(1+j)^{\alpha}
for all
j\geq 0
.
Applying Hölder’s inequality with
p=1/\alpha
and
q=1/(1-\alpha)
, we see that, for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
,

\displaystyle|\rho(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}C(1+j)^{\alpha}|z|^{j}\leq C\Bigl(\sum_{j\geq 0}(1+j)|z|^{j}\Bigr)^{\alpha}\Bigr(\sum_{j\geq}|z|^{j}\Bigr)^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle=C(1-|z|)^{-2\alpha}(1-|z|)^{-(1-\alpha)}=C(1-|z|)^{-1-\alpha}.

Evidently, this estimate implies that all the poles of
\rho
on
{\mathbb{T}}
are simple.

(ii) Suppose that all the poles of
\rho
on
{\mathbb{T}}

are simple.
Expanding
\rho(z)
in partial fractions, we can write it
as a polynomial plus a linear combination of fractions of the form

1/(z-\lambda)~(|\lambda|=1)
and
1/(z-\mu)^{k}~(|\mu|>1,k\geq 1)
,
each of which has a Taylor expansion with bounded coefficients.
Hence the Taylor coefficients of
\rho(z)
are bounded.
∎

Proof Theorem
5.1
.

We begin with some preliminary remarks that apply to all
parts of the proof.
As
\phi
is rational, we may write it as
\phi:=p/q
,
where
p,q
are polynomials with no common zeros.
By a well-known result of Fejér,
there exists a polynomial
r
with all its zeros
outside
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
such that

|p|^{2}+|q|^{2}=|r|^{2}
on
{\mathbb{T}}
.
Then
(p/r,\,q/r)
is a Pythagorean pair such that

(p/r)/(q/r)=\phi
, so, by uniqueness of
(b,a)
, we must have

p/r=b
and
q/r=a
.
The poles of
\phi
on
{\mathbb{T}}
are exactly the zeros
of
q
on
{\mathbb{T}}
, which in turn are exactly the zeros of
a
on
{\mathbb{T}}
.

(i) Suppose that
f\in H^{2}
and that (
14
) holds.
Set

g(z):=\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl(\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr)z^{m}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

Then
g\in H^{2}
. Also, for each
m\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\deg q}\overline{\widehat{q}(j)}\widehat{g}(j+m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\deg q}\overline{\widehat{q}(j)}\Bigl(\sum_{n\geq j}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n-j)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{j=0}^{\min\{n,\deg q\}}\overline{\widehat{q}(j)}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n-j)}\widehat{f}(m+n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\deg p}\overline{\widehat{p}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n).

Hence
T_{\overline{q}}g=T_{\overline{p}}f
,
in other words,
\overline{p}f-\overline{q}g=\overline{h}
on
{\mathbb{T}}
,
where
h\in H^{2}_{0}
.
Dividing through by
\overline{r}
, we obtain
\overline{b}f-\overline{a}g=\overline{h/r}
on
{\mathbb{T}}
, where
h/r\in H^{2}_{0}
.
This implies that
T_{\overline{b}}f=T_{\overline{a}}g
.
Using Theorem
2.1
, we deduce that
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)

and that

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}|\widehat{f}(m)|^{2}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2},

which establishes (
4
).

(ii) Suppose now that all the poles of
\phi
on
{\mathbb{T}}

are simple, or equivalently, that all the zeros of
q
on
{\mathbb{T}}
are simple.
Let
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
.
By Theorem
2.1
, there exists
g\in H^{2}

such that
T_{\overline{b}}f=T_{\overline{a}}g
,
in other words,
\overline{b}f-\overline{a}g=\overline{h}
on
{\mathbb{T}}
,
where
h\in H_{0}^{2}
. Multiplying both sides by
\overline{r}
,
we obtain
\overline{p}f-\overline{q}g=\overline{rh}

on
{\mathbb{T}}
, where
rh\in H^{2}_{0}
. It follows that

T_{\overline{p}}f=T_{\overline{q}}f
.
In terms of coefficients, this says that

(16)

\sum_{n=0}^{\deg p}\overline{\widehat{p}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)=\sum_{n=0}^{\deg q}\overline{\widehat{q}(n)}\widehat{g}(m+n)\quad(m\geq 0).

By Lemma
5.2
(ii), we can write

1/q(z)=\sum_{j\geq 0}c_{j}z^{j}
for
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
,
where
\sup_{j}|c_{j}|<\infty
.
Rewriting (
16
) as

\sum_{n=j}^{j+\deg p}\overline{\widehat{p}(n-j)}\widehat{f}(m+n)=\sum_{n=j}^{j+\deg q}\overline{\widehat{q}(n-j)}\widehat{g}(m+n)\quad(j,m\geq 0),

multiplying both sides by
\overline{c}_{j}
and summing
from
j=0
to
J
, we obtain

\sum_{j=0}^{J}\overline{c}_{j}\sum_{n=j}^{j+\deg p}\overline{\widehat{p}(n-j)}\widehat{f}(m+n)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\overline{c}_{j}\sum_{n=j}^{j+\deg q}\overline{\widehat{q}(n-j)}\widehat{g}(m+n)\quad(m,J\geq 0).

After rearrangement, this becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{J+\deg p}\sum_{j=\max\{0,n-\deg p\}}^{\min\{J,n\}}\overline{c}_{j}\overline{\widehat{p}(n-j)}\widehat{f}(m+n)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{n=0}^{J+\deg q}\sum_{j=\max\{0,n-\deg q\}}^{\min\{J,n\}}\overline{c}_{j}\overline{\widehat{q}(n-j)}\widehat{g}(m+n)\quad(m,J\geq 0).

Now, since
(1/q(z))p(z)=\phi(z)
, the coefficients
c_{j}
satisfy

\sum_{j=\max\{0,n-\deg p\}}^{n}c_{j}\widehat{p}(n-j)=\widehat{\phi}(n)\quad(n\geq 0).

Therefore, for all
m,J\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{J+\deg p}\sum_{j=\max\{0,n-\deg p\}}^{\min\{J,n\}}\overline{c}_{j}\overline{\widehat{p}(n-j)}\widehat{f}(m+n)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{n=0}^{J}\sum_{j=\max\{0,n-\deg p\}}^{n}\overline{c}_{j}\overline{\widehat{p}(n-j)}\widehat{f}(m+n)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{n=J+1}^{J+\deg p}\sum_{j=\max\{0,n-\deg p\}}^{J}\overline{c}_{j}\overline{\widehat{p}(n-j)}\widehat{f}(m+n)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{n=0}^{J}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)+B_{J,m},

where

|B_{J,m}|\leq(\deg p)^{2}(\sup_{j}|c_{j}|)(\sup_{k}|\widehat{p}(k)|)(\sup_{n\geq J}|\widehat{f}(n)|)=o(1)\quad(J\to\infty).

Likewise, since
(1/q(z))q(z)=1
, the coefficients
c_{j}
satisfy

\sum_{j=\max\{0,n-\deg q\}}^{n}c_{j}\widehat{q}(n-j)=\begin{cases}1,&n=0,\\
0,&n>0.\end{cases}

Therefore, for each
m,J\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{J+\deg q}\sum_{j=\max\{0,n-\deg q\}}^{\min\{J,n\}}\overline{c}_{j}\overline{\widehat{q}(n-j)}\widehat{g}(m+n)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{n=0}^{J}\sum_{j=\max\{0,n-\deg q\}}^{n}\overline{c}_{j}\overline{\widehat{q}(n-j)}\widehat{g}(m+n)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{n=J+1}^{J+\deg q}\sum_{j=\max\{0,n-\deg q\}}^{J}\overline{c}_{j}\overline{\widehat{q}(n-j)}\widehat{g}(m+n)

\displaystyle\quad=\widehat{g}(m)+A_{J,m},

where

|A_{J,m}|\leq(\deg q)^{2}(\sup_{j}|c_{j}|)(\sup_{k}|\widehat{q}(k)|)(\sup_{n\geq J}|\widehat{g}(n)|)=o(1)\quad(J\to\infty).

Combining these observations, we deduce that

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)=\widehat{g}(m)\quad(m\geq 0).

Thus

\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}|\widehat{g}(m)|^{2}=\|g\|_{H^{2}}^{2}<\infty,

and so (
14
) holds.

(iii) Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}

be the poles of
\phi
on
{\mathbb{T}}
,
listed according to multiplicity.
Fix an integer
K>k
and
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, and define

g(z):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{z^{Kn}}{n^{\alpha}}\quad\text{and}\quad f(z):=g(z)\prod_{j=1}^{k}(1-\overline{\lambda}_{j}z).

Then
g\in H^{2}
, so
we can apply the criterion (
13
)
to deduce that
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
.

Suppose that the series

(17)

\sum_{n\geq m}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)

converges for each
m\geq 0
. Then certainly

\lim_{n\to\infty}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n-m)}\widehat{f}(n)=0

for each
m\geq 0
.
Now, because of the gaps in the series defining
g
, we have

\widehat{f}(Kn)=\widehat{g}(Kn)=1/n^{\alpha}\quad(n\geq 1).

It follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(Kn-m)}/n^{\alpha}=0\quad(m\geq 0).

This implies that
\widehat{\phi}(n)=O(n^{\alpha})
as
n\to\infty
.
By Lemma
5.2
(i),
it follows that all the poles of
\phi
on
{\mathbb{T}}

are simple.
Thus, if
\phi
has a multiple pole on
{\mathbb{T}}
,
then the series (
17
) diverges
for at least one value of
m\geq 0
.
∎

6.
Counterexamples

Our goal in this section is to establish the following theorem,
which is a rich source of counterexamples.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\phi_{0}\in N^{+}
be a function that is real on the real axis
and satisfies
\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}\phi_{0}(r)=\infty
.
Let
B
be an infinite Blaschke product whose zeros
(t_{n})_{n\geq 1}
lie in
(0,1)

and satisfy

(18)

\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{1-t_{n+1}}{1-t_{n}}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{n\geq 1}\frac{1-t_{n+1}}{1-t_{n}}<1/2.

Set
\phi(z):=B(z)^{2}\phi_{0}(z)/(1-z)^{1/2}
, and let
(b,a)
be the Pythagorean
pair with
\phi=b/a
. Then there exists

f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
such that

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(n)

diverges.

Before proving Theorem
6.1
,
we present two applications.
The first of these is an example
showing that Theorem
3.1
is sharp, in the sense
that one cannot replace
the hypothesis
\phi\in H^{2}
by
\phi\in\cap_{p<2}H^{p}
.

Corollary 6.2
.

There exist a Pythagorean pair
(b,a)
and

such that

\phi:=b/a\in\cap_{p<2}H^{p}
,
yet
\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(n)
diverges.

Proof.

Let
(b,a)
be the Pythagorean pair corresponding to
the function

\phi(z):=-B(z)^{2}\log(1-z)/(1-z)^{1/2}
,
where
B
is a Blaschke product whose zeros

(t_{n})_{n\geq 1}
lie in
(0,1)
and
satisfy (
18
).
By Theorem
6.1
, applied with
\phi_{0}(z):=-\log(1-z)
,
there is an
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
such that the series
\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(n)
diverges.
Also, we clearly have
\phi\in\cap_{p<2}H^{p}
.
∎

The second application of Theorem
6.1

is an example showing that part (ii) of Theorem

may hold for certain

\phi\notin H^{2}
(even for
\phi\notin H^{1}
),
but that it may then be unstable
under multiplication by Blaschke products.

Corollary 6.3
.

There exist a Pythagorean pair
(b,a)

and a Blaschke product

with the following properties:

•

\phi:=b/a\in N^{+}\setminus H^{1}
;

•

) holds for all

) for

.

Let
\phi(z):=1/(1-z)
and let
(b,a)
be the Pythagorean pair
such that
\phi=b/a
.
Clearly
\phi\in N^{+}\setminus H^{1}
.
By Theorem
5.1
,
the formula (
4
)
holds for
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
.

Let
B
be an infinite Blaschke product whose zeros

).
Then
(B^{2}b,a)

applied with

We now turn to
the proof of Theorem

.
This is strongly influenced by the arguments in
[
8
]
. We need three lemmas,
the first of which is a special case of
[
8
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
B
be a Blaschke product whose zeros
(t_{n})_{n\geq 1}

lie in
(0,1)
and satisfy (
18
).
Then

\inf_{n\geq 1}|B(t_{n}^{2})|>0.

The second lemma is a standard property of coanalytic
Toeplitz operators.
We write
k_{w}(z):=1/(1-\overline{w}z)
,
the reproducing kernel of
H^{2}
at
w\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\psi\in H^{\infty}
and let
w\in{\mathbb{D}}
.
Then

T_{\overline{\psi}}k_{w}=\overline{\psi(w)}k_{w}.

Proof.

For all
f\in H^{2}
, we have

\langle f,T_{\overline{\psi}}k_{w}\rangle=\langle\psi f,k_{w}\rangle=\psi(w)f(w)=\psi(w)\langle f,k_{w}\rangle=\langle f,\overline{\psi(w)}k_{w}\rangle.\qed

The third lemma is a calculation.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
(b,a)
be a Pythagorean pair, let
f\in H^{2}
, and let
r\in(0,1)
.
Then
T_{\overline{b}}(f_{r})=T_{\overline{a}}(g)
, where
g\in H^{2}

and

g(0)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(n)r^{n}.

Proof.

We shall use of the following simple observation
about
r
-dilations: if
u\in H^{\infty}
and
v\in H^{2}
, and
P_{+}:L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})\to H^{2}

is the canonical projection, then

(19)

P_{+}(\overline{u}v_{r})=(P_{+}(\overline{u_{r}}v))_{r}\quad(0<r<1).

Clearly we have
\overline{\phi_{r}}f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
.
Define
g:=(P_{+}(\overline{\phi_{r}}f))_{r}
. Then
g\in H^{2}
.
Also, using the observation (
19
) above,
we have

\displaystyle T_{\overline{a}}g

\displaystyle=P_{+}\bigl(\overline{a}(P_{+}(\overline{\phi_{r}}f))_{r}\bigr)=\bigl(P_{+}\bigl(\overline{a_{r}}(P_{+}(\overline{\phi_{r}}f))\bigr)\bigr)_{r}=\bigl(P_{+}\bigl(\overline{a_{r}}\overline{\phi_{r}}f)\bigr)_{r}

\displaystyle=\bigl(P_{+}\bigl(\overline{b_{r}}f)\bigr)_{r}=P_{+}(\overline{b}f_{r})=T_{\overline{b}}(f_{r}).

Finally, we have

g(0)=(P_{+}(\overline{\phi_{r}}f))_{r}(0)=(\overline{\phi_{r}}f)(0)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)r^{n}}\widehat{f}(n).\qed

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Since
\phi_{0}(r)\to\infty
as
r\to 1^{-}
,
there exists
n_{0}\geq 1
such that

\phi_{0}(r)\geq 0
for all
r\geq t_{n_{0}}^{2}
.
Also
\phi_{0}(t_{n}^{2})\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
,
so there exists a non-negative sequence

(c_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
such that

(20)

\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}\phi_{0}(t_{n}^{2})=\infty.

Set

f:=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}k_{t_{n}}.

We claim that
f
is well-defined and that it belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
,
but that the series
\sum_{m\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(m)}\widehat{f}(m)
diverges.

First we show that
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
.
By Lemma
6.5
,
we have
T_{\overline{b}}k_{w}=\overline{b(w)}k_{w}

for all
w\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Also,
for all
n
, we have
B(t_{n})=0
, and so

b(t_{n})=a(t_{n})\phi(t_{n})=a(t_{n})B(t_{n})^{2}\phi_{0}(t_{n})/(1-t_{n})^{1/2}=0.

Therefore
T_{\overline{b}}k_{t_{n}}=0
.
Applying (
2
) with
f=k_{t_{n}}
and
g=0
, we deduce that

\|k_{t_{n}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\|k_{t_{n}}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{1-t_{n}^{2}}.

Thus

\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}\|k_{t_{n}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}<\infty.

Therefore the series defining
f
converges absolutely in
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
.

Now we show that
\sum_{m\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(m)}\widehat{f}(m)
diverges.
Let
r\in(0,1)
. Then

f_{r}=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}k_{rt_{n}},

where the series converges absolutely in the norm of
H^{2}
.
Using Lemma
6.5
once again, we have

\displaystyle T_{\overline{b}}f_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}T_{\overline{b}}k_{rt_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}\overline{b(rt_{n})}k_{rt_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}\overline{\phi(rt_{n})a(rt_{n})}k_{rt_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}\overline{\phi(rt_{n})}T_{\overline{a}}k_{rt_{n}}

\displaystyle=T_{\overline{a}}g,

where

g=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}\overline{\phi(rt_{n})}k_{rt_{n}},

the series converging in
H^{2}
.
By Theorem
2.1
,
g
is the unique function in
H^{2}

such that
T_{\overline{b}}(f_{r})=T_{\overline{a}}g
,
and by Lemma
6.6

g(0)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(m)}\widehat{f}(m)r^{m}.

We deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(m)}\widehat{f}(m)r^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}\overline{\phi(rt_{n})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}B(rt_{n})^{2}\phi_{0}(rt_{n})(1-rt_{n})^{-1/2}.

If, further,
r\geq t_{n_{0}}
, then all the terms in this last series are
positive, and so, for each
n\geq n_{0}
,

\sum_{m\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(m)}\widehat{f}(m)r^{m}\geq c_{n}(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}B(rt_{n})^{2}\phi_{0}(rt_{n})(1-rt_{n})^{-1/2}.

In particular, taking
r=t_{n}
, we obtain

\sum_{m\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(m)}\widehat{f}(m)(t_{n})^{m}\geq c_{n}B(t_{n}^{2})^{2}\phi_{0}(t_{n}^{2})\quad(n\geq n_{0}).

By Lemma
6.4
,
\inf_{n\geq n_{0}}B(t_{n})^{2}>0
,
and from (
20
) we have
\sup_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}\phi_{0}(t_{n}^{2})=\infty
.
Hence

\sup_{n\geq n_{0}}\sum_{m\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(m)}\widehat{f}(m)(t_{n})^{m}=\infty.

By Abel’s theorem, it follows
that
\sum_{m\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(m)}\widehat{f}(m)

diverges, as claimed.
∎

7.
A limit formula

The main result of this section is a limit form of the formula (
1
).
Though it is more complicated than (
1
),
it has the virtue that it holds for all Pythagorean pairs

(b,a)
and all
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(b,a)
be a Pythagorean pair.
For
\epsilon>0
, let
a_{\epsilon}
be the outer function
such that
|a_{\epsilon}|=\max\{|a|,\epsilon\}

a.e. on
{\mathbb{T}}
and
a_{\epsilon}(0)>0
. Set
\phi_{\epsilon}:=b/a_{\epsilon}
.

(i)

If
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
, then

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}|\widehat{f}(m)|^{2}+\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}_{\epsilon}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}.

(ii)

If
f\in H^{2}
but
f\notin{\mathcal{H}}(b)
, then

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}_{\epsilon}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}=\infty.

For the proof, we use a lemma on coanalytic
Toeplitz operators.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
(\psi_{\ell})
be a sequence in
H^{\infty}
.
The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\|T_{\overline{\psi}_{\ell}}h\|_{H^{2}}\to 0
for all
h\in H^{2}
;

(ii)

\sup_{\ell}\|\psi_{\ell}\|_{H^{\infty}}<\infty
and
\psi_{\ell}\to 0
pointwise on
{\mathbb{D}}
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Suppose that
\|T_{\overline{\psi}_{\ell}}h\|_{H^{2}}\to 0

for each
h\in H^{2}
.

We first show that
\sup_{\ell}\|\psi_{\ell}\|_{H^{\infty}}<\infty
.
As
\sup_{\ell}\|T_{\overline{\psi}_{\ell}}h\|_{H^{2}}<\infty
for each
h\in H^{2}
,
the Banach–Steinhaus theorem implies that

\sup_{\ell}\|T_{\overline{\psi}_{\ell}}\|<\infty
.
According to the Brown–Halmos theorem
[
2
, p.95]
,
we have
\|T_{\overline{\psi}_{\ell}}\|=\|\psi_{\ell}\|_{H^{\infty}}
for each
\ell
.
Hence
\sup_{\ell}\|\psi_{\ell}\|_{H^{\infty}}<\infty
, as claimed,

Now we show that
\psi_{\ell}\to 0
pointwise in
{\mathbb{D}}
.
Since
\sup_{\ell}\|\psi_{\ell}\|_{H^{\infty}}<\infty
,
the sequence
(\psi_{\ell})
forms a normal family on
{\mathbb{D}}
.
Therefore it suffices to prove that
\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}(k)\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
for each
k\geq 0
.
Let
k\geq 0
and set
f_{k}(z):=z^{k}
.
Then, for each
\ell
, we have

\|T_{\overline{\psi}_{\ell}}f_{k}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}}(n)\widehat{f}_{k}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}|\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}(j)|^{2}\geq|\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}(k)|^{2}.

Since
\|T_{\overline{\psi}_{\ell}}f_{k}\|_{H^{2}}\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
,
we get
\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}(k)\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
,
as desired.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Suppose that
\sup_{\ell}\|\psi_{\ell}\|_{H^{\infty}}<\infty

and
\psi_{\ell}\to 0
pointwise on
{\mathbb{D}}
.
Since
\sup_{\ell}\|\psi_{\ell}\|_{H^{\infty}}<\infty
,
the sequence
(\psi_{\ell})
forms a normal family on
{\mathbb{D}}
,
and since
\psi_{\ell}\to 0
pointwise on
{\mathbb{D}}
, the
convergence is in fact locally uniform on
{\mathbb{D}}
.
Therefore
\psi_{\ell}^{(j)}(0)\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
for each
j
,
and so
\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}(j)\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
.
Writing
f_{k}(z):=z^{k}
, it follows that

\|T_{\overline{\psi}_{\ell}}f_{k}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}|\widehat{\psi}_{\ell}(j)|^{2}\to 0\quad(\ell\to\infty).

Re-using the hypothesis that
\sup_{\ell}\|\psi_{\ell}\|_{H^{\infty}}<\infty
,
we have
\sup_{\ell}\|T_{\overline{\psi}_{\ell}}\|<\infty
.
As the set
\{f_{k}:k\geq 0\}
spans a dense subspace of
H^{2}
,
it follows that
\|T_{\overline{\psi}_{\ell}}h\|_{H^{2}}\to 0

for all
h\in H^{2}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

(i) Let
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
. By Theorem
2.1
,
there exists a unique
g\in H^{2}
such that

T_{\overline{b}}f=T_{\overline{a}}g
, and then

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{2}}^{2}
.
Also, for each
\epsilon>0
, we have

\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}_{\epsilon}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}=\|T_{\overline{\phi_{\epsilon}}}f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

Thus, to prove (i), it suffices to show that

T_{\overline{\phi}_{\epsilon}}f\to g
in
H^{2}
as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Let
(\epsilon_{j})
be any positive sequence such that
\epsilon_{j}\to 0
. For each
j
, set
\psi_{j}:=a/a_{\epsilon_{j}}
.
Then
\|\psi_{j}\|_{H^{\infty}}\leq 1
for all
j
and
\psi_{j}\to 1

pointwise on
{\mathbb{D}}
. By Lemma
7.2
,
applied to the sequence
(\psi_{j}-1)
, we have
\|T_{\overline{\psi}_{j}}h-h\|_{H^{2}}\to 0
for each
h\in H^{2}
. In particular

\|T_{\overline{\psi}_{j}}g-g\|_{H^{2}}\to 0
. On the other hand,
since
\psi_{j}=a/a_{\epsilon_{j}}
and
T_{\overline{a}}g=T_{\overline{b}}f
, we have

T_{\overline{\psi}_{j}}g=T_{1/\overline{a}_{\epsilon_{j}}}T_{\overline{a}}g=T_{1/\overline{a}_{\epsilon_{j}}}T_{\overline{b}}f=T_{\overline{\phi}_{\epsilon_{j}}}f.

Thus
\|T_{\overline{\phi}_{\epsilon_{j}}}f-g\|_{H^{2}}\to 0
.
As
(\epsilon_{j})
is an arbitrary positive sequence tending to zero,
we conclude that
\|T_{\overline{\phi_{\epsilon}}}f-g\|_{H^{2}}\to 0

as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, as desired.

(ii) Let
f\in H^{2}
, and suppose that

(21)

\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}_{\epsilon}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}<\infty.

We shall show that
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
.
The inequality (
21
) amounts to saying that we have

\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\|T_{\overline{\phi}_{\epsilon}}f\|_{H^{2}}<\infty
.
Therefore there exists a positive sequence
(\epsilon_{j})
tending to zero
such that
\sup_{j}\|T_{\overline{\phi}_{\epsilon_{j}}}f\|_{H^{2}}<\infty
.
As bounded subsets of
H^{2}
are weakly relatively compact,
we may replace
(\epsilon_{j})
by a subsequence and suppose that

T_{\overline{\phi}_{\epsilon_{j}}}f
converges weakly
to some
g\in H^{2}
. It follows that

(22)

T_{\overline{a}}T_{\overline{\phi}_{\epsilon_{j}}}f\to T_{\overline{a}}g\quad\text{weakly}.

Set
\psi_{j}:=a/a_{\epsilon_{j}}
.
As in the proof of part (i), we have

\|T_{\overline{\psi}_{j}}h-h\|_{H^{2}}\to 0
for each
h\in H^{2}
.
In particular,

(23)

T_{\overline{\psi}_{j}}T_{\overline{b}}f\to T_{\overline{b}}f\quad\text{in norm}.

Also, since
b\psi_{j}=a\phi_{\epsilon_{j}}
for all
j
, we have

(24)

T_{\overline{\psi}_{j}}T_{\overline{b}}f=T_{\overline{a}}T_{\overline{\phi}_{\epsilon_{j}}}f.

Combining (
22
), (
23
) and (
24
),
we deduce that
T_{\overline{b}}f=T_{\overline{a}}g
.
Consequently, by Theorem
2.1
, we have
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

8.
An open problem

We conclude this article with a brief discussion of
the following problem, which remains unsolved.

Problem 8.1
.

Let
(b,a)
be a Pythagorean pair and let
\phi=b/a
.
Is it true that, if
f\in H^{2}
and

(25)

\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}<\infty,

then
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
and

(26)

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}|\widehat{f}(m)|^{2}+\sum_{m\geq 0}\Bigl|\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)\Bigr|^{2}?

We have seen that answer is affirmative if
\phi\in H^{2}

(Theorem
3.1
) or if
\phi
is rational (Theorem
5.1
), but for general
\phi\in N^{+}
,
the question remains open. We suspect that the answer
is negative, but at present there is no counterexample.
(The counterexamples in this article, and
those of which we are aware elsewhere in
the literature, treat only the converse question,
namely whether (
25
)
holds for all
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
.)

The following proposition suggests a possible route towards
a counterexample of the type that we seek.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
(b,a)
be a Pythagorean pair and let
\phi=b/a
.
Suppose, in addition, that
b

is an outer function (or, equivalently, that
\phi
is outer). If there exists
f\in H^{2}\setminus\{0\}

such that

(27)

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{\widehat{\phi}(n)}\widehat{f}(m+n)=0\quad(m\geq 0),

then the answer to Problem
8.1
is negative
for this pair
(b,a)
.

Proof.

Suppose, if possible, that the answer to
Problem
8.1
is affirmative
for
(b,a)
.
Then the condition (
27
) implies that

f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)

and
\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}
.
By Theorem
2.1
, since
f\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
,
there exists
g\in H^{2}
such that

T_{\overline{b}}f=T_{\overline{a}}g
and

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}=\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{2}}^{2}
.
Comparing the two expressions for
\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}(b)}^{2}
,
we see that
\|g\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=0
, so
g=0
, and hence

T_{\overline{b}}f=T_{\overline{a}}0=0
.
This in turn implies that
f\in(bH^{2})^{\perp}
.
By assumption,
b
is outer, so
bH^{2}
is dense in
H^{2}

and
(bH^{2})^{\perp}=\{0\}
. Hence, finally,
f=0
,
contrary to assumption. This contradiction establishes
the proposition.
∎
