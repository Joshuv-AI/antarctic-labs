Computational analysis and automated classification of eating disorder discourse on Twitter’s EDTWT community
Authors: Jace Li, Chuduo Zhang, Anam Nawaz Khan, Hollie Raynor, Xueping Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTwitter’s “EDTWT” community constitutes a prominent online space for eating disorder (ED) discourse, yet large-scale computational characterization remains limited.ObjectiveTo characterize EDTWT behavioral patterns, emotional dynamics, thematic structure, and develop automated content classification methods through computational analysis of a three-year dataset.MethodsWe analyzed 48,341 tweets from 18,587 users (January 2022–February 2025). Analyses included engagement patterns, temporal dynamics, clinical keyword prevalence, multi-method sentiment analysis (TextBlob, VADER, clinical affect lexicons), topic modeling (LDA, NMF), and ensemble-based multi-label classification.ResultsUser engagement followed a highly skewed distribution in which a small number of highly active users generated the majority of content (62.1% of users posted only once, while the top 10% produced 48.9% of all content), suggesting that a concentrated subset of users may warrant particular clinical attention. Temporal patterns showed a 7.5-fold difference in posting volume between Friday night peaks and Tuesday morning troughs, with consistent nocturnal peaks between 21:00 and 23:00. Clinical keywords appeared in 26.6% of tweets (body image 14.4%, restrictive eating 6.7%, recovery 4.3%). Sentiment was slightly positive (M=0.060, SD=0.282) with moderate subjectivity (M=0.298). Topic modeling revealed ten themes including calorie tracking (13.2%), Spanish (9.7%) and Polish (8.2%) subcommunities, and recovery discourse (9.4%). An ensemble of automated classifiers trained to categorize eating disorder content achieved strong performance (macro F1=0.753, κ =0.703, where higher values indicate better classification accuracy), outperforming a biomedical-domain language model by 21.4% on the least frequently occurring content categories.ConclusionsEDTWT exhibits complex heterogeneity with coexisting pro-ED content, recovery discourse, and culturally-specific subcommunities. The concentration of activity among a small number of highly active users enables efficient identification of individuals who may be at elevated risk and in need of targeted support. Automated classification enables scalable content monitoring for digital mental health surveillance and evidence-based platform moderation.

Introduction

Eating disorders (EDs) affect approximately 8.4% of women and 2.2% of men globally, with substantial morbidity, mortality, and healthcare burden [1, 2]. Social media platforms have altered ED expression and community formation, enabling large-scale peer interaction among affected individuals [3]. Twitter’s “EDTWT” community (hashtag #edtwt and keyword “edtwt”) represents the contemporary evolution of earlier pro-anorexia (pro-ana) and pro-bulimia (pro-mia) communities that migrated from dedicated websites to mainstream social media [4, 5], now embedded within algorithm-driven architecture potentially exposing vulnerable individuals through recommendation systems.

Evolution of online ED communities

Early pro-anorexia websites simultaneously normalized eating pathology while providing social support for isolated individuals [6, 7]. Exposure to pro-ED content was consistently associated with increased body dissatisfaction, greater symptom engagement, and reduced quality of life [4, 8]. Communication patterns included “thinspiration” (thinspo) imagery, restrictive eating guides, and competitive weight loss discourse framing starvation as achievement [4].

As these communities evolved, distinctions between “thinspiration” and “fitspiration” (fitspo) content became increasingly blurred. Both genres reinforce appearance-focused ideals and weight loss preoccupation despite fitspo’s health promotion veneer [9–11]. Alongside this convergence, online pro-recovery communities emerged, conceptualizing recovery as multifaceted processes encompassing physical, psychological, and social dimensions [12, 13]. The coexistence of pro-ED and pro-recovery discourse within shared hashtag spaces creates complex informational environments that are difficult to characterize through manual review alone.

Computational approaches to ED social media research

Computational approaches have made it possible to analyze mental health discourse at a scale that would be impossible through manual review alone. Natural language processing refers broadly to methods that enable computers to read and interpret human text; researchers have applied these methods to eating disorder social media content to detect disorder-related material [14], estimate symptom severity [15], track linguistic changes as a disorder progresses [16], and characterize community structure [17]. Building on these foundations, sentiment analysis tools such as VADER [18], TextBlob [19], and LIWC [20] measure the emotional tone of text by applying linguistic rules or dictionaries to assign polarity scores; combining multiple tools provides a more robust characterization of emotional expression than any single method alone. Complementing sentiment analysis, topic modeling algorithms such as LDA [21] and NMF [22] discover recurring themes across large text collections without requiring researchers to define those themes in advance, making them well suited to the heterogeneous nature of eating disorder communities where multiple distinct discourse streams coexist within a shared online space.

More recently, transformer-based language models [23] such as BERT, RoBERTa, BioBERT, and DeBERTa have substantially advanced automated text classification by leveraging pre-training on large quantities of text to capture rich linguistic context, and they have demonstrated strong performance on clinical text classification tasks [24–27]. Multi-label classification extends this capability to posts that express multiple themes simultaneously, which is common in mental health discourse [28]. A persistent challenge, however, is class imbalance: when certain content types are rare in a dataset, standard models tend to overlook them, which is especially consequential in clinical contexts where rare presentations such as recovery-oriented content may be the most important to identify [29]. Techniques including focal loss, targeted data augmentation, and ensemble methods show promise in addressing this problem [30], yet their application to eating disorder social media content classification remains underexplored.

Research gap and study objectives

Comprehensive large-scale analyses of EDTWT remain limited. Prior studies have examined eating disorder discourse on social media using smaller samples, non-Twitter platforms, or single-method approaches. Sowles et al. conducted a content analysis of a pro-eating disorder community on Reddit using four weeks of discussion threads (N=125 comments) [31], identifying expressions of disordered eating behaviors and peer support, but the small sample and single-method design precluded broader inference about community dynamics. Park et al. applied structural topic modeling to 11,484 posts from the r/EatingDisorders subreddit, discovering themes related to emotional support and informational exchange [32], yet the study employed a single analytical method and did not examine temporal patterns, sentiment, or classification. Similarly, Baskal et al. [33] compared eating disorder content across Reddit and Tumblr using a multilingual qualitative categorization approach, finding that platform context shaped the nature of content, but their dataset comprised fewer than 3,100 posts in total and lacked computational classification or temporal analysis. More recently, a qualitative analysis of 175 EDTWT tweets identified two primary themes, feeding behaviors and body image, demonstrating normalization of disordered eating and moralization of food and weight [34], but the small sample size precluded population-level inference. Across these studies, temporal dynamics, emotional characteristics, and thematic heterogeneity have not been systematically characterized at scale, and automated classification systems enabling scalable content monitoring are lacking.

This study addresses these gaps through multi-method computational analysis of 48,341 tweets spanning three years. Objectives are: (1) characterize distributional properties and temporal patterns; (2) quantify clinical ED keyword prevalence; (3) assess emotional tone using multiple sentiment analysis frameworks; (4) discover latent thematic structure through topic modeling; and (5) develop and evaluate ensemble-based multi-label classification for automated ED content categorization (Fig. 1). We hypothesized power-law user engagement distributions, diurnal posting rhythms, pervasive clinical terminology, complex emotional valence patterns, and thematically diverse content reflecting both pro-ED and recovery-oriented discourse. We further hypothesized that ensemble approaches combining multiple transformer architectures with imbalance mitigation strategies would outperform single-model baselines including domain-specific BioBERT on minority class detection.

This study analyzes publicly available tweets to characterize community-level discourse patterns rather than diagnose individual users. Methods

Data collection and ethics

We retrieved publicly available tweets through Twitter API v2 between January 2, 2022 and February 9, 2025. Search queries targeted tweets containing hashtag #edtwt or keyword “edtwt” (case-insensitive). The final dataset comprised 48,341 tweets from 18,587 unique users. Retweets were included to capture community amplification; quote tweets were treated as original content. No personally identifiable information beyond public usernames was retained. The institutional review board determined this study exempt as it involved secondary analysis of publicly available, non-identifiable data [35].

Qualitative analysis and theme development

We conducted targeted qualitative analysis of a stratified subsample (n=175, 0.36% of corpus) to: (1) provide interpretive context for topic modeling and sentiment analysis, and (2) inform multi-label classification category definitions through inductive thematic identification [36]. Sampling criteria: English-language content, interpretable text without exclusive media reliance, minimum 5 words after preprocessing, and temporal distribution across the three-year period. Tweets referencing explicit self-harm were excluded [37].

Iterative coding process

The analysis employed a three-cycle coding process [38]. First, tweets were cleaned (spelling corrections, abbreviation expansion, non-substantive element removal). In vivo coding preserved participants’ language, with codes generated through line-by-line analysis. Second, codes were organized using color-coded annotation: positive descriptors (green), negative descriptors (red), opposing concepts (matching colors). Four categories emerged: Health, Food, Control, and Body Image. Third, the four categories were abstracted into two themes through constant comparison [39]. Two independent coders achieved 92% agreement (Cohen’s κ =0.89).

Emergent themes

Two themes emerged, aligning with DSM-5 criteria [40]. Theme 1: Feeding Behaviors encompassed food procurement, consumption, or avoidance [41]. Food Morality assigned moral value to foods based on perceived healthiness (“good” foods: salads, vegetables; “bad” foods: burgers, candy). Representative tweets: “You’re only as skinny as your last meal” and “Eating junky makes you chunky.” Self-Control emphasized discipline and willpower: “If not now, then when?” and “if you are not able to control yourself, none of this will be able to help.” Theme 2: Body Image encompassed self-perception [42]. Weight Morality assigned moral value to body weight (thinness equated with virtue; higher weight stigmatized). Exercise was framed as compensatory behavior: “anytime they exceeded 500 calories, they would expunge those calories via exercise purging.” These qualitative themes informed clinical keyword dictionary construction, topic modeling interpretation, and classification category definitions. The qualitative subsample served conceptual grounding and category validation rather than population inference [43].

Computational text analysis

Dataset characterization

Data quality assessment included missing value analysis, duplicate detection (0.3% removed), timestamp validation, and text length profiling. Temporal analysis employed time series decomposition. User behavior analysis calculated posting frequency distributions, engagement concentration via Gini coefficient and Lorenz curves, and power-law relationships through log-log regression. Content characteristics included tweet length statistics, hashtag usage, mention patterns, URL prevalence, and numeric content detection.

Clinical keyword analysis

We employed a manually curated dictionary informed by DSM-5 criteria [40] and previous research [44, 45]. Eight categories: restrictive behaviors (71 terms), compensatory behaviors (43 terms), body image (89 terms), food morality (52 terms), calorie obsession (34 terms), control and discipline (28 terms), negative affect (67 terms), and recovery language (45 terms). Keyword prevalence was calculated as percentage of tweets containing at least one term per category.

Sentiment analysis

Multi-method sentiment analysis combined: TextBlob polarity and subjectivity scoring [19], VADER [18], and clinical affect lexicons from LIWC2015 [20]. TextBlob computed polarity scores (-1 to +1) and subjectivity scores (0 to 1). VADER generated compound scores (-1 to +1), classifying tweets as positive (>0.05), negative (<−0.05), or neutral. Clinical affect lexicons: positive affect (87 terms), negative affect (134 terms), body dissatisfaction (56 terms). Temporal dynamics examined daily average polarity, subjectivity, and affect scores. Statistical comparisons employed Welch’s t-tests and chi-square tests.

Topic modeling

Unsupervised topic modeling employed LDA [21] and NMF [22]. Text preprocessing: lowercase conversion, URL and mention removal, punctuation removal, stopword elimination (NLTK plus social media terms), term filtering (5+ documents, <50% prevalence). TF-IDF weighted document-term matrices: 5,000 terms. LDA used scikit-learn 1.7.2 with Dirichlet priors α =0.1 and β =0.01, optimized through variational Bayes. Models trained with k=5 to k=15 topics; k=10 selected. NMF used Frobenius norm minimization, k=5 to k=12 topics; k=8 selected. Both: 1000 iterations, convergence tolerance 0.0001. Topic interpretation: top 15 words per topic, prevalence calculation, qualitative labeling by two coders (92% agreement, Cohen’s κ =0.89). Coherence quantified using NPMI.

All analyses used Python 3.11 with pandas 2.3.3, numpy 2.3.4, matplotlib 3.10.7, seaborn 0.13.2, and nltk 3.9.2. Statistical significance: α = 0.05.

Multi-label classification framework

To ensure theoretical validity and methodological feasibility, this study adopted a content classification framework comprising six categories derived from prior literature [46–53]. These categories included body morality, dietary morality, self-control, exercise, support for recovery, and neutrality, and were used to categorize social media remarks related to eating disorders.

Text preprocessing and annotation

Tweets converted to lowercase; line breaks removed. Non-textual elements (URLs, mentions, hashtags, emojis, special characters) stripped. Tweets without meaningful content excluded. English-language only. Duplicates removed. Hybrid annotation combined LLM-assisted labeling with expert verification. LLM assigned preliminary categories using structured guide for six labels: Weight Morality, Food Morality, Self-Control, Exercise, Pro-Recovery, Neutral (Fig. 2). Results

Dataset characteristics and user engagement

The dataset comprised 48,341 tweets from 18,587 unique users (January 2, 2022 to February 9, 2025; 1,135 days). User posting frequency exhibited strong power-law characteristics (Gini=0.71, Fig. 4): 62.1% (n=11,543) posted exactly one tweet, while top 10% (n=1,859) contributed 48.9% of content (n=23,639) and top 1% (n=186) accounted for 18.9% (n=9,137). Most prolific user: 1,247 tweets (>1 tweet/day over three years). Mean user contribution: 2.6 tweets (median=1, SD=11.3, range: 1-1,247).

Tweet characteristics: mean length 19.3 words (median=16, SD=14.2), mean characters 121.7 (median=101, SD=86.4). Numeric content: 35.9% (n=17,366). Hashtag prevalence: 78.3% (mean=3.2 per tweet). User mentions: 31.2%.

High-frequency users shared detailed tracking (e.g., “284 cals today”), body checking updates (e.g., “down 0.8 lbs”), and motivational mantras (e.g., “nothing tastes as good as skinny feels”). Single-post users posted introductions (e.g., “hi edtwt! looking for moots”) or exploratory questions (e.g., “is this community supportive or triggering?”).Fig. 4User Engagement Patterns in EDTWT. a Posting Frequency. b Cumulative Contribution. c Lorenz Curve. d Log-log Plot Confirming Power-law

Daily posting volume: 8-335 tweets (mean=79.1, SD=42.6). Weekly patterns: Friday peak (mean=118.3) and Tuesday trough (mean=63.7), representing 85.7% increase. Monthly aggregation: winter elevation (December-February, mean=87.3 tweets/day) versus summer (June-August, mean=74.2).

Discussion

Key findings and qualitative-computational integration

The two qualitative themes—Feeding Behaviors and Body Image, mapped systematically onto computational results. Feeding Behaviors manifested in Food Morality classification (F1=0.700), Self-Control classification (F1=0.776), and keyword co-occurrence between control and restrictive eating terminology (r=0.31, p<0.001). Body Image manifested in body image keywords’ highest prevalence (14.4%), Weight Morality classification (F1=0.850), and Exercise classification (F1=0.720). Qualitative analysis explained paradoxical positive sentiment (TextBlob M=0.060): achievement emotions around weight loss goals and control satisfaction, not well-being. High Social Connection topic prevalence (14.7%) reflected community infrastructure function beyond symptom expression.

Recovery-focused topics (LDA 9.4%, NMF 7.9%) alongside pro-symptom content indicate parallel discourse streams. Recovery content shares vocabulary with pro-ED content but inverts semantic valence, explaining classification difficulty (Pro-Recovery F1=0.714 vs. Weight Morality F1=0.850). Robust Spanish (9.7−12.4%) and Polish (8.2−9.8%) community detection reveals international reach with high topic coherence (NPMI: Spanish=0.56, Polish=0.52), indicating tightly-knit language-based subcommunities.

Slightly positive aggregate sentiment (TextBlob M=0.060, VADER M=0.118) reflects achievement-oriented framing of weight loss and social connection value, not psychological well-being. Recovery tweets exhibited highest sentiment variance (SD=0.41 vs. corpus SD=0.28, F=2.14, p<0.001), confirming emotional ambivalence. Moderate subjectivity (M=0.298) indicates multiple functions: informational exchange, practical advice sharing, social networking. High diagnostic keyword prevalence (26.6%) confirms specialized ED discourse space. Strong co-occurrence patterns (body image × calorie obsession r=0.31, body image × restrictive eating r=0.28) support dimensional psychopathology models [54] emphasizing interconnected cognitive-behavioral systems.

Clinical and public health implications

Power-law distribution (Gini = 0.71) indicates extreme content concentration: the top 10% of users generated 48.9% of content, while the top 1% posted >1 tweet/day on average over three years. High-frequency users’ content featured rigid rule-setting and detailed tracking, suggesting ED centrality to identity. Single-post users (62.1%) posted exploratory content, suggesting early-stage engagement amenable to intervention. Among users who actively engage with EDTWT, posting frequency and content patterns may offer partial signals of illness severity and chronicity; prior research has shown that frequent social media posting is associated with elevated mental health difficulties [55]. However, this relationship is subject to important selection bias: individuals with severe symptoms may avoid public posting due to stigma, privacy concerns, or acute illness, meaning that high-frequency engagement likely reflects a specific subpopulation of help-seeking or identity-invested users rather than the full severity spectrum.

Pronounced temporal patterns (7.5-fold Friday night to Tuesday morning differential) indicate behavioral synchronization. Late evening tweets (21:00-23:00) showed higher negative affect density (M=0.041) versus afternoon posts (M=0.028, t=3.47, p<0.001), consistent with clinical observations of heightened evening ED symptoms including post-binge guilt and body-checking behaviors [56]. Several complementary mechanisms may explain this nocturnal activity peak. First, from a clinical perspective, individuals with EDs frequently report that distress, urges to restrict, and post-binge guilt intensify during evening hours when daily structure dissolves and social oversight diminishes [56]. Second, nighttime social isolation—when offline social networks are unavailable—may drive vulnerable individuals toward online communities as an accessible source of validation and connection; research has demonstrated that loneliness and anxiety are significant mediating factors linking eveningness to problematic social media use [57]. Third, the online disinhibition effect [58] suggests that reduced social cues and perceived anonymity during nighttime hours lower individuals’ threshold for self-disclosure, facilitating more candid expression of distressing thoughts and behaviors that might be suppressed during daytime interactions. Platform interventions should target delivery of recovery content to peak vulnerability periods (21:00-23:00). Real-time monitoring systems leveraging the ensemble classification framework developed here could detect posting pattern deviations signaling acute deterioration or crisis. Concretely, such a system could operate through three complementary mechanisms. First, at the content level, the trained classifier could continuously flag high-risk posts for expedited human review by clinical staff or platform safety teams. Second, at the temporal level, our finding that negative affect density peaks sharply during late evening hours (21:00-23:00) provides actionable scheduling information: monitoring intensity and recovery resource delivery could be algorithmically amplified during these identified vulnerability windows rather than deployed uniformly. Third, at the user level, longitudinal aggregation of per-post classification profiles could generate individual risk trajectories, enabling detection of escalation patterns before crisis onset. Precedent for this approach exists in adjacent literature: real-time NLP-based monitoring systems have demonstrated feasibility for suicide risk detection on Twitter [59], and multimodal social media analysis has been proposed as a first-pass clinical filter that routes flagged content to human reviewers rather than replacing clinical judgment [60]. Critically, such systems carry significant ethical responsibilities: deployment must prioritize supportive outreach over punitive surveillance, and robust human oversight must be maintained at decision points involving individual users, given the inherent limitations of automated classifiers in high-stakes clinical contexts. Multilingual community presence complicates temporal analysis due to timezone variation requiring geographic stratification. Recovery language (4.3%) suggests harm-reduction potential through amplification of pro-recovery voices and connection to evidence-based resources. Low compensatory behavior prevalence (1.3%) may reflect content moderation, community norms against explicit behavior encouragement, or coded language not captured by keyword dictionaries. Food Morality served as mediating framework connecting body dissatisfaction to restrictive behaviors, suggesting this cognitive pattern as potential intervention target.

Methodological contributions

The ensemble classification framework achieved a macro F1 score of 0.753 and a Cohen’s kappa of 0.703, demonstrating that automated categorization of eating disorder content is feasible at scale and could address critical gaps in digital mental health surveillance where manual review of tens of thousands of posts would be prohibitively resource-intensive. Comparison with traditional machine learning baselines provides important context: logistic regression and linear SVM, which represent text as simple word frequency counts without capturing meaning or context, achieved macro F1 scores of 0.513 and 0.463 respectively, with SVM failing entirely on the Pro-Recovery category. The 31.9-−38.5% performance gap over these baselines empirically justifies the additional computational complexity of transformer-based models for this task. Notably, the ensemble also outperformed BioBERT, a model specifically pre-trained on biomedical literature, by 21.4% on the Pro-Recovery category. This finding suggests that informal social media language, which differs substantially from clinical text in vocabulary, grammar, and tone, is better captured by general-domain language representations combined with targeted data augmentation than by biomedical domain specialization alone. The Pro-Recovery category presented a particular classification challenge because recovery-oriented content shares much of the same vocabulary as pro-eating-disorder content, differing primarily in the absence of food and weight morality framing rather than the presence of distinct positive vocabulary; targeted augmentation informed by qualitative category definitions was essential to achieving acceptable performance on this clinically important class.

The strong minority class performance achieved through augmentation and focal loss, with Pro-Recovery reaching F1=0.714 and Exercise reaching F1=0.720, carries broader implications for mental health natural language processing. Rare content categories are often the most clinically significant, yet standard machine learning approaches systematically underperform on them without explicit mitigation strategies. These results demonstrate that with appropriate techniques, automated systems can detect clinically important rare presentations with sufficient reliability to support, though not replace, human clinical judgment.

The mixed-methods design of this study, combining qualitative thematic analysis with large-scale computational modeling, produced insights that neither approach could have generated independently. Qualitative analysis provided the interpretive grounding necessary to define classification categories with clinical validity; computational analysis then enabled population-level inference, temporal dynamics detection, and the discovery of minority patterns across a dataset too large for manual review. The integration of both approaches also revealed emergent findings: qualitative coding identified Food Morality and Weight Morality as conceptually parallel constructs, and computational analysis confirmed that while they co-occur in 8.7% of posts, they are statistically independent, supporting their treatment as distinct categories. The resulting classification framework has practical clinical applications, including real-time flagging of high-risk content for human review, temporal tracking of category prevalence to inform intervention timing, and user-level risk profiling based on aggregated posting histories. Given the robust multilingual subcommunities identified in this dataset, language-specific adaptations of these tools will be necessary to support culturally tailored interventions.

Platform moderation and harm reduction

Content moderation requires nuanced approaches beyond keyword-based removal. Shared vocabulary across pro-ED, recovery, educational, and harm-reduction content necessitates ML classifiers for differential treatment. Social networking function (14.7% topic prevalence) indicates community connection value independent of ED content; complete platform disruption through broad removal may drive users to less moderated platforms eliminating intervention access. Harm-reduction frameworks, which aim to preserve peer connection while reducing exposure to dangerous content, have been proposed as a more nuanced alternative to broad content removal in online ED communities. Lerman et al. demonstrated that emotional support remains a core function of ED communities even on heavily moderated platforms, and explicitly recommend softer moderation strategies—such as warning labels and community-based reporting systems—that preserve peer support while minimizing harm, rather than blunt content removals that may silence users seeking empathy and validation [55]. Furthermore, evidence from other online communities suggests that aggressive deplatforming may displace users toward less moderated platforms, potentially increasing rather than reducing harmful content exposure [61]. While direct empirical evaluation of harm-reduction approaches within ED-specific platforms remains limited, these findings collectively support the rationale for nuanced moderation strategies over wholesale community removal. Temporal targeting of moderation to high-activity periods could enable detection of viral spread of particularly dangerous content, enabling rapid response before widespread exposure. Automated classification systems carry inherent dual-use risks; deployment for punitive surveillance or involuntary intervention rather than supportive outreach could harm vulnerable individuals and erode trust in peer support spaces.

Limitations

Dataset captures only public tweets using explicit edtwt hashtag/keyword, excluding private accounts and individuals discussing EDs without community-specific terminology, likely overrepresenting core members. Cross-sectional design precludes causal inference regarding EDTWT participation effects on individual trajectories; longitudinal analysis required to assess symptom progression, recovery, and relapse prediction. Keyword dictionaries may miss coded language, evolving slang, and non-English terms beyond topic modeling detection. Sentiment analysis and topic modeling have context insensitivity limitations (sarcasm, negation) addressed through multi-method triangulation but residual measurement error remains. During the study period (2022–2025), Twitter was rebranded as X; we retain the original platform name for consistency with data collection and community terminology (#edtwt).

Qualitative subsample size (n=175, 0.36%) limits generalizability of thematic prevalence estimates; purpose was conceptual grounding and category validation rather than population inference [43]. Single-coder qualitative analysis introduces interpretation bias, though independent validation (92% agreement, κ =0.89) provides reliability evidence. Temporal sentiment stability (slope=−0.00002, p=0.34) suggests equilibrium despite membership turnover, though episodic negative spikes warrant investigation to identify triggering events creating intervention opportunities. Longitudinal panel designs tracking individual users over time represent the most critical next step, enabling assessment of EDTWT participation effects on symptom progression, recovery trajectories, and differential outcomes by content exposure type.

Conclusions

This computational analysis of 48,341 EDTWT tweets spanning three years provides comprehensive characterization of online eating disorder discourse with direct clinical implications. Three principal findings emerge. First, extreme engagement concentration (Gini=0.71, top 10% generating 48.9% of content) enables efficient risk stratification: high-frequency users exhibit severe, chronic presentations requiring intensive intervention, while 62.1% single-post users represent early-stage engagement amenable to prevention. Second, pronounced temporal synchronization (7.5-fold Friday evening to Tuesday morning differential) reveals strategic timing opportunities for delivering recovery content during peak vulnerability periods (21:00-23:00) when nocturnal posting coincides with heightened negative affect and body-checking behaviors. Third, thematic heterogeneity,coexistence of pro-symptom content (body image 14.4%, restrictive eating 6.7%) with recovery discourse (4.3%) and multilingual subcommunities (Spanish 9.7%, Polish 8.2%) demonstrates functional and cultural complexity requiring nuanced moderation approaches that preserve peer support while limiting harmful content. The ensemble classification framework (macro F1=0.753, κ =0.703) outperformed traditional baselines (LR F1=0.513, SVM F1=0.463) by 31.9-−38.5% and domain-specific BioBERT by 21.4% on minority classes, demonstrating feasibility of automated content categorization at scale. Mixed-methods integration validated complementary strengths: qualitative analysis provided interpretive depth while computational analysis enabled population-level inference and temporal dynamics detection. Findings enable targeted clinical risk stratification, culturally-adapted harm reduction approaches, and scalable content monitoring for evidence-based platform moderation policies addressing vulnerable online ED populations.
