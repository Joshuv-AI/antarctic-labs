Genome-wide identification and functional analysis of the BES1-like (VfBES1) gene family in Vernicia fordii reveals its role in floral development
Authors: Chong Ge, Jing Gao, Lin Zhang, Jie Cao, Xiang Li, Junjie Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundVernicia fordii Hemsl (also known as Tung tree), an significant commercial oil-producing tree species, is a monoecious and diclinous species with male and female flowers on the same inflorescence; however, the molecular mechanisms governing its floral sex determination remain elusive, particularly the genetic basis underlying the skewed female-to-male flower ratio and the evolutionary dynamics of sex-related gene families, which severely restrict targeted breeding for yield enhancement. In the model plant Arabidopsis, the BRI1 EMS SUPPRESSOR 1 (BES1) transcription factor family plays a crucial role in Brassinosteroid (BR) signaling and reproductive development. However, its function remains largely unexplored in woody perennials.ResultsIn this study, we introduce the genome-wide identification and functional characterization of the BES1-like (VfBES1) gene family in the Tung tree for the first time. Integrative multi-omics approaches reveal seven VfBES1 genes that are clustered into three phylogenetically distinct clades, each characterized by clade-specific motifs and structural simplicity. Segmental duplication events (VfBES1-1/VfBES1-5 and VfBES1-4/VfBES1-7) and promoter cis-element enrichment (hormone-responsive and abiotic stress-related motifs) highlight evolutionary innovation and functional diversification. Spatiotemporal expression profiling reveals VfBES1 genes’ tissue- and stage-specific roles. VfBES1-1 predominantly expresses in female flowers and fruits, suggesting its possible roles in late-stage sex maintenance or ovule and fruit development. VfBES1-2 and VfBES1-6 exhibit male flower-specific and early floral developmental activation, respectively. Nuclear-localized VfBES1-6 displays co-expression with VfMYB35-1 gene, which is a regulator of male structure degeneration.ConclusionsFindings in this study shed light on the regulatory roles of VfBES1 genes in the floral development of the Tung tree, providing a reference for its precision breeding to enhance flowering synchrony and seed productivity. This study also provides a comparative framework for understanding the functional diversity of BES1-like genes in non-model woody plants.Keywords: Vernicia fordii, BES1-like gene family, Floral development, Gene duplication, Multi-omics analysis.

Main conclusion

This study identifies seven BES1-like (VfBES1) genes in Vernicia fordii and reveals their species-specific regulatory roles in floral development, providing genetic targets for enhancing reproductive efficiency through precision breeding.

Introduction

The Tung tree (Vernicia fordii Hemsl.), a perennial woody plant of the Euphorbiaceae family, is one of the world’s most significant oilseed crops. Its oil, rich in triacylglycerol, is extensively utilized for biodiesel production, industrial paints, and pharmaceutical products [1–3]. However, the Tung tree’s reproductive efficiency is relatively low, a result of insufficient understanding of the molecular regulatory network governing reproductive development. Recent studies on plant genomics have revealed that species-specific gene family expansion and functional diversification play crucial roles in regulating reproductive traits in non-model woody perennials [4, 5].

The BES1 gene family, a subgroup of the transcription factor family, was first identified for its crucial role in regulating genes mediated by Brassinosteroids (BR) [6]. This family consists of six highly homologous members: BRI1 EMS SUPPRESSOR 1 (BES1), BRASSINAZOLE RESISTANT 1 (BZR1), BES1/BZR1 Homolog 1 (BEH1), BES1/BZR1 Homolog 2 (BEH2), BES1/BZR1 Homolog 3 (BEH3), and BES1/BZR1 Homolog 4 (BEH4) [7]. These transcription factors regulate diverse growth and developmental processes, such as cell elongation, vascular differentiation, floral initiation, stamen fertility, and stress responses [8]. Notably, BES1 genes also function downstream in the EMS1-TPD1-SERK receptor kinase pathway, which is essential for tapetum formation and pollen development [9]. In Arabidopsis, this pathway ensures timely tapetal programmed cell death and pollen maturation. BES1 members further modulate the expression of key sporophytic factors such as DYSFUNCTIONAL TAPETUM1 (DYT1) and MALE STERILITY1 (MS1), which are critical for anther and pollen development [10–12]. Although the BES1 family has been studied in Arabidopsis and several crops [13–17], its role in woody perennials—particularly in floral sex determination—remains poorly understood. Given the importance of BR signaling in reproductive development, we hypothesize that BES1-like genes in Tung tree may influence its sexually dimorphic flower development, contributing to its skewed sex ratio and reproductive efficiency.

This study represents the genome-wide identification and functional characterization of the VfBES1 gene family in the Tung tree for the first time. We clarified the phylogenetic divergence, gene duplication history, and promoter cis-element architectures of VfBES1 paralogs by using integrative multi-omics approaches. Time-resolved transcriptome profiling at key floral developmental stages revealed stage-specific regulation of VfBES1 expression during floral transition, organogenesis, and gametophyte development. These findings deepen our understanding of evolutionary innovation and regulatory specialization of BES1 homologs in woody plants and provide usable genetic targets for precise breeding to improve the flowering synchrony, inflorescence architecture, and seed productivity of the Tung tree. This work provides a comparative framework for reproductive development studies in non-model woody perennials.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

The Tung trees used in this study were cultivated in the experimental area of Central South University of Forestry and Technology, located in Qingping Town (28.2°N, 112.9°E), Yongshun County, Hunan Province.

Identification and classification of VfBES1 Genes

The Tung tree genome, obtained from the NCBI (BioProject: PRJNA503685), was analyzed to identify VfBES1genes. A Hidden Markov Model (HMM) profile corresponding to the BES1 domain (PF05687) was retrieved from the Pfam database [18]. Using TBtools, the genome was scanned with an e-value threshold of < 1.0 × 10− 10 to ensure stringent identification of candidate genes [19]. Putative VfBES1 sequences were further validated for domain integrity using SMART, Pfam, and Conserved Domain Database (CDD) analyses. Subcellular localization predictions were performed via BUSCA, while physicochemical properties (molecular weight, isoelectric point, grand average of hydropathicity) were computed using ExPASy ProtParam [20].

Phylogenomic reconstruction, conserved motif analysis, and gene structure analysis

The ClustalW was used for multiple alignments of VfBES1and the phylogenetic tree was constructed using MEGA7.0 software based on the neighbor-joining method (NJ) [21]. The phylogenetic tree was further curated with iTOL online software (https:/itolembl.de/). When analyzing the conserved motifs of VfBES1, the maximum number of motifs was set to 10, and the Multiple Expectation Maximization for Motif (MEME) was used to evaluate the conserved motifs of VfBES1 with default parameters. Finally, TBtools software was utilized to visualize the exon-intron structures and conserved motifs of VfBES1, using gene structure data from the Tung tree genome annotation file (gff3 format) and motif information from an XML file.

Chromosomal localization and collinearity analysis of VFBES1 genes

The chromosomal localization information was included in the GFF3 annotation files, and all VfBES1 genes were mapped and visualized by TBtools. Gene duplication includes whole genome duplication, segmental duplication, and out-of-linkage duplication using the Multiple Collinearity Scanning toolkit MCScanX in TBtools to assess VfBES1 duplication events.

Expression pattern analysis of VfBES1 in different tissues

Samples were collected from mature Tung trees grown under natural conditions in Changsha, Hunan Province, China (28.2°N, 112.9°E) during June - July 2023. Root, stem, leaf, female flower, male flower, and fruit samples were collected from the mature Tung tree. The RNA was extracted separately using Trizol reagent, then reverse transcribed into cDNA. The VfEF1a gene was utilized as an internal control for analyzing the expression levels of VfBESs genes in these samples via qRT-PCR (primer sequences are provided in Table 1). All experiments were conducted in triplicate, and the relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Different letters above bars indicate differences at P < 0.05 by one-way ANOVA with LSD test. Data are presented as mean values ± SD. Origin 2022 software was employed for graph illustration.

Expression patterns of VfBES1 at different developmental stages of female and male flowers

To comprehensively elucidate the expression patterns of VfBES1 across various developmental stages of female and male flowers, we conducted an in-depth analysis of RNA-seq data derived from four distinct periods of floral development. These periods encompassed 30 days before flowering (DBF, corresponding to stages C1 and X1 for female and male flowers, respectively), 20 DBF (C2 and X2), 10 DBF (C3 and X3), and 0 days before flowering (DAF, C4 and X4). The data sources and analytical methodologies employed in this study were based on the research conducted by Liu et al. [3]. Transcript abundance (FPKM values) was quantified using the HISAT2-StringTie pipeline.

For sample collection, flowers were randomly harvested at 5-day intervals from March to April 2017. Male and female flowers at stage 2 (30 DBF, C1 and X1, as depicted in Fig. 1C and I), stage 4 (20 DBF, C2 and X2, shown in Fig. 1D and J), stage 6 (10 DBF, C3 and X3, illustrated in Fig. 1K and L), and DAF (C4 and X4) were separately gathered from the same plant. These samples were subsequently subjected to transcriptome sequencing to facilitate the analysis of VfBES1 expression patterns.

Subcellular localization analysis of VfBES1-6

VfMYB35-1 plays a pivotal role in the early degradation of male structures within female flowers [3]. Consequently, this research conducted an in-depth investigation into the possible interactions between VfMYB35-1 and VfBES1-6. Utilizing the BUSCA software for analysis revealed that the VfBES1-6 protein is primarily located in the cell nucleus. To ascertain the precise subcellular localization of this gene, a fusion expression vector, VfBES1-6-pCAMBIA1300-GFP, was crafted and subsequently introduced into the protoplasts of the Tung tree. The preparation of Tung tree protoplasts involves chopping and washing callus for pretreatment, followed by cell wall digestion using an enzyme solution containing cellulase and macerozyme under vacuum and shaking conditions, with final purification achieved through steps of filtration, centrifugation, and buffer replacement. The localization of VfBES1-6 was then visualized using a confocal microscope (Leica, Wetzlar, Germany). Furthermore, we conducted real-time fluorescent quantitative analysis on the successfully transformed protoplasts.

Results

Identification and physicochemical property analysis of the tung tree BES1 genes

Through the analysis of the whole genome data of the Tung tree, seven candidate genes containing the BES1 domain were identified in the Tung tree. All seven members passed the verification of SMART, NCBI-CDD, and PFAM websites. They were named VfBES1-1 to VfBES1-7 based on their physical location order on the Tung tree chromosome (Table 2). The physicochemical property analysis results of the VfBES1 protein showed that the sequence length ranged from 780 to 34,359 bp, the molecular weight distribution ranged from 24643.38 to 79044.11 Da, and the isoelectric point varied between 5.44 and 9.10. Subcellular localization prediction indicated that this gene family is located in the nucleus.

Phylogenetic, conserved motif analysis and gene structure analysis of VfBES1 gene family

The phylogenetic tree was constructed using the 7 selected Tung tree VfBES1 protein sequences. The results showed that the VfBES1 genes in Tung tree can be divided into three branches. Conserved motif analysis results showed that the number of conserved motifs in the VfBES1 gene family of the Tung tree ranged from 2 to 8, among which Motif1 and Motif2 appeared in all genes, indicating that these two motifs are highly conserved. In addition, Motif3 appeared in 6 VfBES1 genes. VfBES1 proteins within the same branch have similar motif compositions, for example, the most closely related VfBES1-2 and VfBES1-3 both contain Motif1, Motif2, Motif3, Motif6, Motif7, Motif8, and Motif9, while VfBES1-1, VfBES1-4, VfBES1-5, and VfBES1-7 all contain Motif1, Motif2, Motif3, Motif4, Motif5, and Motif10 (Fig. 1). Gene structure analysis results showed that VfBES1 genes contain a maximum of 2 introns, among which the proteins of clade1 all lack introns, indicating that the VfBES1 genes of clade1 have simpler gene structure characteristics. Gene structure analysis results showed that VfBES1 genes contain a maximum of one intron, and six genes lack intron, only the VfBES1-6 gene has a simpler gene structure.

Cis-acting elements in the promoter of the VfBES1 genes

To understand the pathways in which the VfBES1 genes is involved in signal transduction in the Tung tree, the cis-acting elements of the 2000 bp promoter region upstream of the ATG of seven VfBES1 genes were analyzed through the PlantCARE website. The results showed that the promoter region of the VfBES1 gene contains a series of important growth and abiotic stress response elements, such as auxin, gibberellin, light reaction, and low temperature response elements (Fig. 2). These predicted results indicate that the VfBES1 family may be involved in responses to hormone signal transduction and environmental stress.

Chromosomal location analysis of the VfBES1 Genes

The seven VfBES1 genes are unevenly distributed across the 6th chromosomes of the Tung tree. Specifically, chromosome 7 carries 2 VfBES1 genes, while chromosomes 1, 6, 9, 10, and 11 each have one VfBES1 gene (Fig. 3). There was no VfBES1 gene found on the other chromosomes.

Analysis of gene duplication relationships and collinearity in VfBES1 genes

To further analyze the evolutionary relationship of the Tung tree genes, a genomic intra-duplication event analysis was conducted. The results showed that there are 2476 collinear genes among the Tung tree genes, of which two pairs of collinear gene pairs exist between VfBES1 genes: VfBES1-1/VfBES1-5 and VfBES1-4/VfBES1-7, indicating that gene duplication events have occurred in the VfBES1 gene family (Fig. 4).

The expression patterns of VfEBS1 across various tissues

The expression patterns of VfEBS1 genes exhibited marked variations across diverse tissues. Specifically, VfEBS1-1 was predominantly expressed in the female flower and fruit, displaying a significant difference in expression level between male and female flowers (Fig. 5A). This underscores its potential role in sex determination. VfEBS1-2 was predominantly expressed in the fruit, indicating a possible association with fruit development (Fig. 5B). Conversely, VfEBS1-3, VfEBS1-4, VfEBS1-5, VfEBS1-6 and VfEBS1-7 were primarily expressed in the root, suggesting these genes may be linked to the growth stability and stress resistance of the Tung tree (Fig. 5C, D, E, F, G).

Expression patterns of VfBES1 in different flowering phases

To elucidate the potential functions of VfBES1 in flower development, the expression patterns of VfBES1 in different developmental stages of female and male flowers in the Tung tree were explored through transcriptome data analysis. We screened 7 differentially expressed VfBES1 genes from the transcriptome database, and the expression characteristics of these genes revealed their possible roles in the process of flower development. Notably, VfBES1-1 exhibited an upward expression trend starting from stage C2 (20 days before flowering), which precedes morphological sex fixation, though the difference reached statistical significance primarily at C4 relative to C1. The results showed that VfBES1-1 and VfBES1-7 maintained high expression throughout the development of female and male flowers, and in addition, these three genes, VfBES1-3, VfBES1-4, and VfBES1-5, had similar expression patterns, suggesting that these genes may play a vital role in the entire development process of flowers in Tung tree, and may be involved in regulating the core process of flower development (Fig. 6A, C, D, E, G). Notably, VfBES1-2 expressed higher levels in the X1-X3 stage of male flower development than in the female flower (Fig. 6B). This discovery suggests that VfBES1-2 may play a more important role in the specific stages of male flower development and may be involved in regulating the unique processes or traits of male flower development. Whereas VfBES1-6 showed high expression levels in the early stages of both female and male flower development, indicating that this gene may play a key role in the initiation phase of flower development and may be involved in regulating the formation of flower buds or the early establishment of flower morphology.

VfEBS1-6 protein localization in the nucleus

VfBES1-6 demonstrated pronounced expression characteristics during the initial stages (C1 and X1) of floral development in both sexes, subsequently diminishing rapidly in other developmental stages. Notably, this expression pattern closely aligns with that of VfMYB35-1, as documented previously [3], underscoring the pivotal roles VfBES1-6 might serve during specific intervals of floral development. This transient co-expression rather than sex-specific expression suggests that both may be regulated by the same upstream signal. To elucidate its function further, subcellular localization experiments were conducted, revealing that the VfBES1-6 protein is exclusively localized within the nucleus (Fig. 7A). To investigate potential interactions between VfBES1-6 and VfMYB35-1, the former was overexpressed in protoplasts via transformation assays. This resulted in a marked augmentation of VfBES1-6 expression in OE-VfBES1-6 relative to empty vector (EV), concomitant with a noticeable alteration in VfMYB35-1 expression (Fig. 7B, C). This observation intimates a potential regulatory interaction between VfBES1-6 and VfMYB35-1. However, neither yeast one-hybrid nor two-hybrid assays confirmed any direct interaction between the two proteins. Consequently, this suggests that the regulatory mechanism governing the expression of VfBES1-6 and VfMYB35-1 during floral development is likely intricate, potentially entailing indirect interactions or the involvement of additional regulatory elements. Notably, their temporal co-activation—rather than sex-specific expression—may imply a shared upstream regulator.

Discussion

The genome-wide analysis of the VfBES1 gene family in the Tung tree has yielded crucial insights into the species-specific regulatory mechanisms that underpin floral development in woody perennials. In this study, we identified seven VfBES1 genes, uncovering phylogenetic divergence, gene duplication history, and cis-regulatory architectures. These findings collectively highlight the evolutionary dynamism of this transcription factor family. Furthermore, the expression dynamics of VfBES1 across different floral developmental stages and tissues underscore their specialized roles in reproductive processes. These insights provide important foundations for improving Tung tree breeding strategies.

The phylogenetic analysis grouped VfBES1 into three clades. This result followed the conserved motif compositions and structural simplicity of the clade-specific genes. Clade-specific motifs (Motif1 and Motif2 were universally retained across all paralogs) might contribute to functional conservation in BR signaling, as demonstrated in Arabidopsis thaliana and Brassica napus BES1 homologs [7, 13, 14], whereas clade-specific motifs (Motif4-10) may promote functional diversification. This divergence was similar to that in Arabidopsis thaliana and Brassica napus, where BES1 homologs acquired distinct regulatory roles through motif shuffling and neofunctionalization after duplication [7, 14]. The occurrence of tandem and segmental duplication (VfBES1-1/VfBES1-5 and VfBES1-4/VfBES1-7) indicated that recurrent duplication followed by subfunctionalization led to adaptive evolution of VfBES1 towards ecological or developmental constraints, which has also been reported in other woody perennials [5].

Analysis of promoter cis-elements demonstrated an enrichment of hormone-responsive (auxin and gibberellin) and stress-related elements in VfBES1. This suggests their potential role in facilitating cross-talk between BR signaling and environmental adaptation. Notably, this is congruent with research conducted on Zea mays and Gossypium, where BES1 homologs were found to integrate developmental cues with responses to abiotic stress [15, 16]. The nuclear localization of VfBES1-6, which is consistent with its function as a transcription factor, further bolsters its regulatory capacity in target gene activation during floral transitions.

Analysis of spatiotemporal expression revealed tissue- and stage-specific functions of VfBES1. Preferential expression of VfBES1-1 in female flowers and fruits indicated its possible roles in sex determination and ovule development, similar to BES1-mediated stamen fertility in Arabidopsis thaliana [9]. Root-specific expression of VfBES1-3 to VfBES1-7 suggested their participation in root growth and stress tolerance, which extended the role of BES1 beyond reproductive development. Notably, VfBES1-6 showed the highest expression during early floral development (C1/X1), which was quite similar to the temporal expression pattern of VfMYB35-1, a male structure degeneration regulator [3]. Although no direct protein interaction was observed, their temporal co-expression suggests possible coregulation by shared upstream factors or participation in related developmental process, which might be achieved by unknown upstream factors or chromatin modifiers. Similarly, in Arabidopsis thaliana, BES1 proteins regulate developmental programs via combinatorial interactions with MYB and bHLH transcription factors [11].

Although these findings are significant, there are still some limitations. The lack of a direct interaction between VfBES1-6 and VfMYB35-1 in yeast assays indicates that their coordination might rely on intermediary proteins or epigenetic mechanisms. This repression appears to operate as a negative-feedback circuit that precisely calibrates feminizing-signal strength, rather than representing a direct synergistic activation. Future studies should employ Chromatin Immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) to identify direct targets of VfBES1-6 and VfMYB35-1, and use CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing in transgenic tung trees to functionally validate their roles in floral development and sex determination.

In conclusion, this study unravels the evolutionary history and regulatory diversification of VfBES1 and thus defines them as key regulators of reproductive development in the Tung tree. Our integrative analyses on phylogenomic, expression, and cis-regulatory data establish a basis for the strategic exploitation of VfBES1 in molecular breeding for improved synchronization of flowering and seed production, and also allow comparative studies on BES1-mediated signaling in non-model woody species.
