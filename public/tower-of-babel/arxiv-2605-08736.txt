II
Tower of local constraints

The reciprocal symmetry
f_{s}(z)=f_{s}(1/z)
is equivalent to the statement that

h(u)\;\equiv\;f_{s}(e^{u})

(2)

is an even function of
u
. All odd-order derivatives of an even function vanish at the origin:

h^{(2k+1)}(0)\;=\;0,\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots.

(3)

Each of these conditions is a separate local constraint on the multiplicity distribution at
n=\langle n\rangle
.

II.1
Stirling-number expansion

The chain rule gives
d/du=z\,d/dz
. The standard identity

\left(z\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{n}\;=\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}S(n,k)\,z^{k}\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}

(4)

where
S(n,k)
are Stirling numbers of the second kind, yields

h^{(n)}(0)\;=\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}S(n,k)\,f_{s}^{(k)}(1).

(5)

With
g(z)\equiv e^{z}\langle n\rangle P(z\langle n\rangle)
so that
f_{s}=g-1
, one has
f_{s}^{(k)}(1)=g^{(k)}(1)
for
k\geq 1
. The Leibniz rule applied to
g(z)=e^{z}q(z)
with
q(z)=\langle n\rangle P(z\langle n\rangle)
and
q^{(j)}(1)=\langle n\rangle^{j+1}P^{(j)}(\langle n\rangle)
gives

g^{(k)}(1)\;=\;e\,\langle n\rangle\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\langle n\rangle^{j}P^{(j)}(\langle n\rangle).

(6)

We define the dimensionless quantities

\xi_{j}\;\equiv\;\frac{\langle n\rangle^{j}P^{(j)}(\langle n\rangle)}{P(\langle n\rangle)},\qquad\xi_{0}=1,

(7)

and

A_{k}\;\equiv\;\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\xi_{j}\;=\;\frac{g^{(k)}(1)}{e\,\langle n\rangle\,P(\langle n\rangle)}.

(8)

The constraint (
3
) for odd
n=2k+1
then takes the form

\sum_{m=1}^{2k+1}S(2k+1,m)\,A_{m}\;=\;0.

(9)

II.2
Explicit constraints

In this subsection we work out the first three members of the tower in turn. We compute the constraint at order
\bm{k=0}
(recovering the previously established result), then the new constraint at order
\bm{k=1}
(the main analytical contribution of this section), then the constraint at order
\bm{k=2}
to expose the general pattern.

\bm{k=0}
.

Since
S(1,1)=1
,

A_{1}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;1+\xi_{1}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;P^{\prime}(\langle n\rangle)=-\frac{P(\langle n\rangle)}{\langle n\rangle}.

(10)

This recovers the result of
Ouchen:2026
.

\bm{k=1}
.

With
S(3,1)=1
,
S(3,2)=3
,
S(3,3)=1
and
A_{1}=0
already imposed,

A_{3}+3A_{2}=0.

(11)

Substituting the explicit forms,

4+9\xi_{1}+6\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}\;=\;0.

(12)

Using
\xi_{1}=-1
, this reduces to

\boxed{\;\xi_{3}+6\,\xi_{2}\;=\;5\;}

(13)

or, in terms of
P
and its derivatives at
n=\langle n\rangle
,

\boxed{\;\langle n\rangle^{3}P^{\prime\prime\prime}(\langle n\rangle)+6\,\langle n\rangle^{2}P^{\prime\prime}(\langle n\rangle)\;=\;5\,P(\langle n\rangle).\;}

(14)

\bm{k=2}
.

The
S(5,m)
coefficients are
(1,15,25,10,1)
. After using the lower-order constraints, one obtains

\xi_{5}+15\,\xi_{4}\;=\;290\,\xi_{2}-276,

(15)

which involves
\xi_{2}
,
\xi_{4}
and
\xi_{5}
together. The

k

\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\ldots,\xi_{2k+1}

II.3
Dimensionless ratios

For experimental tests, it is convenient to define dimensionless ratios that equal unity if the symmetry holds. The
k=0

Ouchen:2026

\rho_{0}\;\equiv\;-\langle n\rangle\,\frac{P^{\prime}(\langle n\rangle)}{P(\langle n\rangle)},\qquad\rho_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm sym.}}{{=}}1.

(16)

The

\rho_{1}\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{5}\!\left[\frac{\langle n\rangle^{3}P^{\prime\prime\prime}(\langle n\rangle)}{P(\langle n\rangle)}+6\,\frac{\langle n\rangle^{2}P^{\prime\prime}(\langle n\rangle)}{P(\langle n\rangle)}\right],\qquad\rho_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm sym.}}{{=}}1.

A test of the symmetry beyond the leading derivative reduces to an experimental determination of

II.4
Methodological remark

The Gaussian-in-

IV
Multiplicative noise on Mueller’s Markov branching process

We now examine whether the reciprocal symmetry of
f_{s}
can be derived from a small modification of the color-dipole cascade. In the model of Mueller
Mueller:1994
the multiplicity distribution is geometric,

P_{n}^{\rm Mueller}=e^{-\alpha Y}(1-e^{-\alpha Y})^{n-1},

(22)

with mean
\langle n\rangle=e^{\alpha Y}
. In the KNO limit
\langle n\rangle\to\infty
this gives
\langle n\rangle P_{n}\to e^{-z}
, hence
f_{s}\equiv 0
.

We consider an event-by-event fluctuation of the cascade rate,
\alpha\to\alpha(1+\sigma\nu)
with
\nu
a unit-variance random variable,
\langle\nu\rangle=0
and (for definiteness in the leading-order expansion below) all odd moments
\langle\nu^{2k+1}\rangle=0
for
k\geq 1
, as is the case for any symmetric noise distribution including a Gaussian. The event-mean is
\langle n\rangle_{\rm event}=\langle n\rangle_{0}\,e^{\eta}
with
\langle n\rangle_{0}\equiv e^{\alpha Y}
the baseline (noise-free) cascade mean and
\eta\equiv\sigma\nu\,\ln\langle n\rangle_{0}
a noise variable with
\langle\eta\rangle=0
and
\langle\eta^{2}\rangle\equiv\sigma_{\eta}^{2}
. The ensemble mean that enters the experimental KNO variable
z=n/\langle n\rangle
is

\langle n\rangle\;=\;\langle\langle n\rangle_{\rm event}\rangle\;=\;\langle n\rangle_{0}\,\langle e^{\eta}\rangle\;=\;\langle n\rangle_{0}\!\left(1+\frac{\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\sigma_{\eta}^{4}),

(23)

which differs from
\langle n\rangle_{0}
at order
\sigma_{\eta}^{2}
and must be tracked when rescaling the model prediction to experimental coordinates. Averaging the geometric distribution at fixed event,

\langle n\rangle_{0}\,P_{n}^{\rm event}\;=\;e^{-\eta}\exp(-z_{0}e^{-\eta}),\qquad z_{0}\equiv n/\langle n\rangle_{0},

(24)

over the noise distribution and expanding to
\mathcal{O}(\sigma_{\eta}^{2})
,

\langle\,\langle n\rangle_{0}P_{n}\,\rangle\;=\;e^{-z_{0}}\!\left[1+\frac{\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}{2}(z_{0}^{2}-3z_{0}+1)\right]+\mathcal{O}(\sigma_{\eta}^{4}).

(25)

Eq. (
25
) is the answer in
z_{0}=n/\langle n\rangle_{0}
. To convert to the experimental variable
z=n/\langle n\rangle
, use (
23
) so
z_{0}=z(1+\sigma_{\eta}^{2}/2)
, and multiply by the Jacobian
dz_{0}/dz=1+\sigma_{\eta}^{2}/2
to preserve normalisation. Expanding to
\mathcal{O}(\sigma_{\eta}^{2})
,

\langle n\rangle P_{n}\;=\;e^{-z}\!\left[1+\frac{\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}{2}(z^{2}-4z+2)\right]+\mathcal{O}(\sigma_{\eta}^{4}),

(26)

so that

f_{s}(z)\bigg|_{\rm mult.\,noise}\simeq\frac{\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}{2}\,(z^{2}-4z+2).

(27)

The polynomial
z^{2}-4z+2
satisfies
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-z}(z^{2}-4z+2)\,dz=0
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}z\,e^{-z}(z^{2}-4z+2)\,dz=0
, ensuring the normalisation
\int\psi dz=1
and the constraint
\langle z\rangle=1
that hold by construction in experimental data. Its roots,
z=2\pm\sqrt{2}\approx 0.59,\,3.41
, are not reciprocals of each other (their product is
2
). It is therefore not invariant under
z\to 1/z
: the value at
z=2
is
-2
, the value at
z=1/2
is
1/4
. A multiplicative log-symmetric noise on the cascade rate does not reproduce
f_{s}(z)=f_{s}(1/z)
at leading order.

Figure

compares the multiplicative-noise prediction
f_{s}\propto z^{2}-4z+2
with the ATLAS 13 TeV data. The polynomial has its minimum at
z=2
, whereas the data dip lies at
z\simeq 1
; once normalised to the same dip depth, the model curve is too broad and misplaced. The data
f_{s}(z)
(filled circles) and
f_{s}(1/z)
(open squares) track each other across the full window, while the multiplicative-noise polynomial does not exhibit this property.

The same conclusion holds for two-component geometric mixtures and for the negative binomial distribution. For a two-component mixture
P_{n}=w_{1}G(n;\langle n_{1}\rangle)+w_{2}G(n;\langle n_{2}\rangle)
with
G(n;\bar{m})
the geometric distribution of mean
\bar{m}
, the resulting
f_{s}
in the KNO limit takes the form
f_{s}(z)=-1+\sum_{i}w_{i}r_{i}e^{(1-r_{i})z}
with
r_{i}=\langle n\rangle/\langle n_{i}\rangle
, which is a sum of exponentials in
z
and is not invariant under
z\to 1/z
except in the degenerate case
r_{1}=r_{2}=1
, in which
f_{s}\equiv 0
. For the negative binomial of parameter
k
, the KNO-limit form
\langle n\rangle P_{n}=(k^{k}/\Gamma(k))z^{k-1}e^{-kz}
gives
f_{s}(z)=-1+(k^{k}/\Gamma(k))z^{k-1}e^{-(k-1)z}
, which equals
f_{s}(1/z)
only at
k=1
(the geometric limit,
f_{s}\equiv 0
).

The reciprocal symmetry observed in the data therefore constrains the cascade dynamics beyond rate fluctuations and beyond the standard mixture and negative-binomial extensions. The tower of local constraints (
9
) can be used as a filter for candidate cascade extensions: any model whose KNO-scaled multiplicity satisfies the reciprocal symmetry must, in particular, satisfy
\xi_{3}+6\xi_{2}=5
at
n=\langle n\rangle
. The AGK extension of Mueller’s chain
Ouchen:2025AGK
provides a concrete example. At leading order in
1/\langle n\rangle
, the AGK distribution reduces to
\langle n\rangle P_{n}\to e^{-z}[1+2/\langle n\rangle]
with
f_{s}\simeq 2/\langle n\rangle
independent of
z
, which trivially satisfies
f_{s}(z)=f_{s}(1/z)
. At subleading order in
1/\langle n\rangle
, the symmetry is broken: the AGK distribution exhibits the intersection points
z=1/2
and
z=2
but not the full reciprocal symmetry, and these intersection points carry the genuine
z
-dependence of
f_{s}
at this order
Ouchen:2025AGK
. The role of
z=2
as a fixed point of approximate KNO restoration in the ATLAS data has been examined in
Ouchen:2025MEM
. The non-trivial
z
-dependence of
f_{s}
, which carries the information about the symmetry, requires the inclusion of dipole merging (Pomeron loops) or analogous higher-order contributions. Dipole evolution including recombinations and transitions to the vacuum has been studied recently in
Kutak:2025
;
Kutak:2025-2
; whether such schemes generate the reciprocal symmetry of
f_{s}
is an open question that the local constraints (
14
) can be used to address quantitatively.

V
Discrete symmetry and conserved structures

The following discussion is offered mainly as a motivation for the question why the reciprocal map appears in the present context, not as a derivation; we do not establish a continuous symmetry of an underlying action.

Noether’s theorem associates a conserved current with each continuous one-parameter symmetry of an action. The reciprocal symmetry
z\to 1/z
is discrete (
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
): there is no continuous parameter, and Noether’s theorem does not apply directly. Discrete symmetries instead generate selection rules. In the present context, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
action
u\to-u
on the variable
u=\ln z
splits any function
h(u)
into even and odd parts,
h=h_{\rm even}+h_{\rm odd}
, and the symmetry sets
h_{\rm odd}\equiv 0
. This is the structural origin of the tower (
3
): each odd derivative of
h
at the fixed point
u=0
projects onto
h_{\rm odd}
and must therefore vanish.

A natural question is whether the discrete reciprocal symmetry is the remnant of a continuous symmetry of the underlying QCD dynamics. The map
z\to(az+b)/(cz+d)
is a Möbius (fractional linear) transformation, and the inversion
z\to 1/z
is one of its discrete elements. Möbius invariance is a known feature of the BFKL kernel in leading-logarithmic accuracy, where the kernel acts on transverse impact-parameter coordinates
Lipatov:1986
;
Lipatov:1993
. A direct connection between the BFKL Möbius group and the multiplicity ratio
z=n/\langle n\rangle
has not been established. This connection deserves further investigation.

VI
Entanglement entropy

The Shannon entropy of the multiplicity distribution is

S=-\sum_{n}P_{n}\ln P_{n},

(28)

which coincides with the von Neumann entanglement entropy of the partonic density matrix when the latter is diagonal in the relevant Fock basis and the hadronic multiplicity faithfully tracks the partonic one
Kharzeev:2017
; in this section we use “entanglement entropy” in this established proxy sense. The relation
S\simeq\ln\langle n\rangle+1
, valid at leading order for the geometric cascade, has been compared to the H1 measurement
H1:2021
and extended to BFKL-evolved unintegrated gluon distributions in
Hentschinski:2022
;
Hentschinski:Straka:2022
, to diffractive DIS in
Hentschinski:2023
, and to QCD evolution more generally in
Hentschinski:2024
; cascade-based entropies including dipole recombinations have been studied in
Kutak:2025
;
Kutak:2025-2
. A complementary line of work probes spin-spin Bell-type entanglement of quark–antiquark pairs in diffractive heavy-quark production
Fucilla:2025kit
, in exclusive deep inelastic scattering via Generalized Parton Distributions
Hatta:Schoenleber:2025
, and in inclusive heavy-quark electroproduction
Fucilla:Hatta:Xiao:2026
, probing distinct facets of the entanglement structure of small-
x
QCD. In the continuum approximation
\sum_{n}\to\int dn
, with
P_{n}=\langle n\rangle^{-1}\psi(z)
,

S\;\simeq\;\ln\langle n\rangle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\psi(z)\ln\psi(z)\,dz.

(29)

VI.1
Cancellation of the linear term

Writing
\psi(z)=e^{-z}\,(1+f_{s}(z))
on the support
\mathcal{S}=[z_{\min},z_{\max}]
of
\psi
(with
z_{\min}=n_{\min}/\langle n\rangle
set by the lowest measured multiplicity and
z_{\max}
by the largest), and expanding
\ln\psi=-z+\ln(1+f_{s})=-z+f_{s}-f_{s}^{2}/2+\mathcal{O}(f_{s}^{3})
,

\displaystyle\psi\ln\psi

\displaystyle=-z\,e^{-z}(1+f_{s})+e^{-z}(1+f_{s})\!\left(f_{s}-\frac{f_{s}^{2}}{2}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=-z\,e^{-z}(1+f_{s})+e^{-z}\!\left(f_{s}+\frac{f_{s}^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{O}(f_{s}^{3})\right).

(30)

Two integral identities follow from the basic properties of
\psi
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}}\!\!\psi(z)\,dz

\displaystyle=1\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\int_{\mathcal{S}}\!\!e^{-z}f_{s}(z)\,dz=1-I_{0},

(31)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}}\!\!z\psi(z)\,dz

\displaystyle=1\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\int_{\mathcal{S}}\!\!z\,e^{-z}f_{s}(z)\,dz=1-I_{1},

(32)

where

I_{0}\equiv\!\int_{\mathcal{S}}\!e^{-z}dz,\qquad I_{1}\equiv\!\int_{\mathcal{S}}\!z\,e^{-z}dz.

(33)

Both identities reduce to the cancellation
\int e^{-z}f_{s}=\int ze^{-z}f_{s}=0
in the idealised limit
z_{\min}\to 0
,
z_{\max}\to\infty
, in which
I_{0},I_{1}\to 1
. Combining them with (
30
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}}\psi\ln\psi\,dz

\displaystyle=-I_{1}-(1-I_{1})+(1-I_{0})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\!\int_{\mathcal{S}}e^{-z}f_{s}^{2}dz+\mathcal{O}(f_{s}^{3})

\displaystyle=-I_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\!\int_{\mathcal{S}}e^{-z}f_{s}^{2}dz+\mathcal{O}(f_{s}^{3}),

(34)

where the constant term and the two linear-in-
f_{s}
contributions combine to give
-I_{0}
. Inserting into (
29
),

\boxed{\;S\;=\;\ln\langle n\rangle+I_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\!\int_{\mathcal{S}}\!e^{-z}f_{s}^{2}(z)\,dz+\mathcal{O}(f_{s}^{3}).\;}

(35)

In the idealised continuum limit,
I_{0}\to\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-z}dz=1
and (
35
) reduces to

S\;=\;\ln\langle n\rangle+1-\frac{1}{2}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!e^{-z}f_{s}^{2}(z)\,dz+\mathcal{O}(f_{s}^{3}),

(36)

which is the simpler form quoted in the abstract.

Three observations follow.
(i)
The leading correction to the geometric-cascade entropy
\ln\langle n\rangle+I_{0}
is negative-definite, so any deviation of
\langle n\rangle P_{n}
from
e^{-z}
on the support reduces
S
.
(ii)
The correction is quadratic in
f_{s}
, not linear, which explains why
S\simeq\ln\langle n\rangle+1

Kharzeev:2017
;
Ouchen:2026
approximates the data well even though
|f_{s}|
reaches
\sim 0.2
–
0.4
in the central window.
(iii)
The cancellation of the linear term follows from normalisation and
\langle z\rangle=1
alone, independently of the reciprocal symmetry; the formula (
35
) is therefore complementary to, and independent of, the local relations of Sec.
II
.

VII
Conclusion

We have developed three complementary lines of analysis of the reciprocal symmetry
f_{s}(z)=f_{s}(1/z)
identified in
Ouchen:2026
: a tower of local algebraic constraints, a model-discrimination test against multiplicative-noise extensions of Mueller’s cascade, and a model-independent expression for the entanglement entropy.

The reformulation
h(u)=f_{s}(e^{u})
with
u=\ln z
makes the symmetry an evenness condition on
h
and yields an infinite tower of local algebraic constraints on
P(n)
at
n=\langle n\rangle
. The first member (
k=0
) is the relation
P^{\prime}(\langle n\rangle)=-P(\langle n\rangle)/\langle n\rangle
of
Ouchen:2026
; the second (
k=1
) is the new relation
\langle n\rangle^{3}P^{\prime\prime\prime}(\langle n\rangle)+6\langle n\rangle^{2}P^{\prime\prime}(\langle n\rangle)=5P(\langle n\rangle)
, equivalent to the unconditional residual
\delta_{3}=1-2\rho_{0}+\rho_{1}=0
. The
k=0
test is automatically passed by any distribution with an extremum near
z=1
and is therefore weak evidence for the symmetry, but
\delta_{3}=0
is a non-trivial constraint that does not follow from the existence of an extremum. Local polynomial fits to ATLAS data give
\delta_{3}=-0.02\pm 0.11
at
13
TeV (
W=6
, fully consistent with zero),
\delta_{3}=-0.32\pm 0.18
at
7
TeV, and an inconclusive
\delta_{3}=-0.50\pm 0.82
at
8
TeV; the
7
TeV value should be read with caution given the comparable systematic spread across polynomial degrees and fit windows. A
\chi^{2}
test of the global symmetry across
1/3<z<3
is consistent with the symmetry at
7
and
8
TeV but rejects it decisively at
13
TeV, where the better experimental precision exposes a residual asymmetry away from
z=1
. The overall picture is therefore that the reciprocal symmetry holds at the leading two non-trivial orders near
z=1
but is not exact globally, and is best understood as an approximate symmetry whose precision improves towards the central scaling point.

A multiplicative log-symmetric noise on the cascade rate of Mueller’s Markov branching process produces
f_{s}\propto z^{2}-4z+2
, which is not invariant under
z\to 1/z
. The same negative result holds for two-component geometric mixtures and for the negative binomial distribution. The dynamical origin of the reciprocal symmetry therefore lies beyond simple extensions of the geometric cascade, and the tower of local constraints provides a quantitative filter for candidate models.

The entanglement entropy formula
S=\ln\langle n\rangle+I_{0}-\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{S}}e^{-z}f_{s}^{2}dz+\mathcal{O}(f_{s}^{3})
, with
I_{0}=\int_{\mathcal{S}}e^{-z}dz
and
\mathcal{S}
the support of the measured distribution, holds at leading order in the KNO-violating term and is a consequence of normalisation and
\langle z\rangle=1
, independent of the symmetry. In the continuum limit
\mathcal{S}\to(0,\infty)
,
I_{0}\to 1
and the simpler form quoted in the abstract is recovered. Numerical evaluation on ATLAS data agrees with the direct entropy at the
10^{-3}
level.

Several questions remain open. A cascade-level derivation of the reciprocal symmetry, perhaps along the lines of dipole evolution with merging
Kutak:2025
;
Kutak:2025-2
or the AGK extension with Pomeron loops
Ouchen:2025AGK
, is needed to connect the symmetry to the underlying QCD dynamics. The possible link between the discrete
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
acting on
z
and the Möbius (SL(2)) invariance of the BFKL kernel is suggestive but not established. Tests of
\rho_{k}
in DIS
H1:2021
,
e^{+}e^{-}
,
pA
and
AA
data would clarify whether the symmetry is universal or specific to high-energy
pp
collisions.
