Development and validation of measurement instruments for Ayurveda research: A systematic review protocol
Authors: M.S. Anagha, V.P. Ribin, N.K. Arun, T. Anju Aravind, Shalini Rai, Anand B. More
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The growing global recognition of Ayurveda, an ancient holistic system of medicine, highlights the urgent need for well-developed and validated measurement instruments to assess its core constructs and clinical concepts used in diagnosis and treatment. Although several psychometric and clinical tools have been designed to quantify these traditionally qualitative aspects, their evolution has often been marked by methodological inconsistencies and inadequate psychometric validation, limiting their acceptance and practical application in both research and clinical domains. Addressing this gap, the present systematic review aims to comprehensively identify, map, and characterize the existing measurement instruments developed or adapted for Ayurvedic constructs and concepts. It further seeks to delineate the methodological challenges encountered during their development and validation, and to suggest future directions for improving their psychometric rigor, clinical relevance, and scientific robustness to enhance their use in evidence-based Ayurvedic research. A systematic and exhaustive search will be carried out across multiple databases including PubMed, MEDLINE, Scopus, EBSCO, Cochrane Library, AYUSH Research Portal, DHARA, Shodhganga, and Google Scholar, using a combination of controlled vocabulary (such as MeSH terms) and free-text keywords integrated with Boolean operators. In addition, reference lists of selected studies and grey literature sources will be screened to ensure comprehensive coverage. Two independent reviewers will perform study screening, data extraction, and quality assessment following standardized procedures. This protocol has been registered with PROSPERO (registration number: CRD420251102044). Since this is a review protocol, definitive results are not yet available. However, the forthcoming review is expected to present an extensive overview of the current landscape of Ayurvedic measurement instruments, elucidate existing methodological and psychometric gaps, and propose strategies for their refinement. These outcomes will be instrumental in reinforcing the scientific foundation of Ayurveda, promoting methodological rigor, and fostering its integration into global healthcare systems.

Introduction

The assessment of person-centred constructs is essential in Ayurvedic healthcare, where clinical decision-making relies heavily on an individualized diagnostic framework [1]. Constructs such as Prakriti (constitutional phenotype) [2], Agni (digestive/metabolic function) [3], and psychosomatic typologies [4] play an integral role in evaluating health status, disease predisposition, and therapeutic response. As Ayurveda increasingly interfaces with contemporary health sciences and evidence-based models, the need to rigorously define and operationalize these constructs through validated measurement instruments has become critical [5]. Over the past two decades, a number of instruments have been developed to quantify key Ayurvedic domains, particularly in the areas of constitution typing [6], mental attributes [7] and quality-of-life assessment [8]. However, the development and validation processes of these tools exhibit considerable variability in conceptual grounding, methodological execution, and psychometric reporting. Most instruments are developed in academic or clinical contexts with limited documentation of standardized procedures for item generation, content validation, construct testing, and reliability estimation [9]. Moreover, few studies employ established frameworks such as the COSMIN (COnsensus-based Standards for the selection of health Measurement INstruments) [10] checklist for evaluating measurement properties, raising concerns about reproducibility and generalizability. The Ayurvedic diagnostics which is based on culturally specific and experience-based clinical reasoning adds further complexity to psychometric evaluation. While the subjective and holistic dimensions of Ayurvedic constructs are central to its practice, translating them into standardized, quantifiable formats poses inherent methodological challenges. These include defining latent constructs with conceptual clarity, adapting tools for diverse linguistic and cultural populations, and validating instruments in the absence of universally accepted gold standards [5,11]. This systematic review will critically evaluate the processes used in the development and validation of measurement instruments in Ayurveda. It will assess their psychometric properties, methodological robustness, and adherence to international validation standards. By identifying current practices, methodological gaps, and context-specific challenges, this review aims to inform the future development of scientifically grounded and culturally relevant tools for use in Ayurvedic research and practice.

Condition or domain being studied

This review focuses on the development and validation of measurement instruments grounded in Ayurvedic principles. It evaluates tools used to assess diagnostic, prognostic, and conceptual constructs within Ayurvedic clinical and research contexts, with emphasis on methodological rigor and psychometric quality.

Ayurvedic constructs

Ayurvedic constructs refer to entities that are theoretically grounded in classical Ayurvedic literature and used for assessment, diagnosis, prognosis, or therapeutic evaluation within Ayurvedic practice. These include constitutional phenotypes (Prakriti, Deha Prakriti, Manasa Prakriti), pathophysiological parameters (Dosha assessment, Agni, Ojas, Ama, Srotas, Dhatu, Mala), prognostic indicators (Vikruti, Bala, disease severity scales), psychobehavioural attributes (Sattva-Rajas-Tamas, Manasika Bhavas), Ayurveda-specific quality of life measures, and therapeutic response outcomes, which represent the main domains relevant for evaluation of Ayurvedic instruments.

Approaches to development and validation of instruments in Ayurveda

The review considers both qualitative and quantitative methods in developing and validating Ayurvedic measurement instruments. Qualitative processes, including item generation, expert and patient input and cognitive testing will be systematically extracted and evaluated. Content validity, involving both qualitative assessment and quantitative indices (e.g., CVI, CVR), will also be evaluated. Quantitative properties such as structural validity, reliability, and construct validity will be assessed for methodological rigor. All components will be evaluated using the COSMIN Risk of Bias checklist. (All coded variables, their definitions, and operational criteria are provided in Supplementary Table 1)

Context

This review will include studies conducted in any setting relevant to the development, cultural adaptation, or psychometric validation of Ayurvedic measurement instruments. This encompasses hospitals, Ayurveda research institutions, academic settings, clinical setups, and community environments, irrespective of geographical location. The main criterion is that the instrument must be based on Ayurvedic constructs and is not defined by the population (healthy or diseased; paediatric to geriatric).

Main outcomes

Psychometric properties of Ayurvedic measurement instruments, including reported validity (content, construct, criterion) and reliability (internal consistency, test-retest, inter-rater), along with the methodological approaches used for their evaluation.

Additional outcomes

To identify and synthesize methodological limitations, inconsistencies in reporting, and context-specific challenges encountered in the development and psychometric validation of Ayurvedic measurement instruments.

Search strategy

A comprehensive literature search will be conducted to identify studies reporting on the development, validation, or psychometric evaluation of measurement instruments used within the context of Ayurveda. The search strategy will be developed with adherence to PRISMA-S and guidelines. The following electronic databases will be systematically searched from inception to July 31,2025: MEDLINE (via PubMed and Ovid), Scopus, Web of Science, CINAHL (via EBSCOhost), PsycINFO (via EBSCOhost), Cochrane Library, AYUSH Research Portal, and the Digital Helpline for Ayurveda Research Articles (DHARA).

Grey literature will be searched through Google Scholar, Shodhganga (for theses and dissertations), and institutional repositories. To maintain the methodological rigor, theses and dissertations will be included only when they provide original data not published elsewhere or possess more methodological detail than related journal articles. To minimize duplication and ensure integrity of data all potentially overlapping records (e.g., thesis–journal article pairs) will be cross-checked by comparing authorship, affiliations, instruments, and psychometric findings. Peer-reviewed journal articles will be treated as the primary data source. When overlap is suspected but cannot be confirmed, the corresponding authors will be contacted. All decisions regarding duplicate handling will be documented as well as reported in the PRISMA flow diagram.

Reference lists of included studies will be manually screened, backward and forward citation tracking will identify additional studies, and searches will be updated before final analysis. The search strategy will combine controlled vocabulary (e.g., MeSH terms) and free-text terms structured around three core concepts: (1) Ayurveda, (2) measurement instruments, and (3) development and psychometric validation. Boolean operators (AND, OR), truncation symbols, and proximity operators (where supported) will be used to optimize the sensitivity and specificity of the search. An illustrative strategy for PubMed includes the following terms: Ayurveda [MeSH] OR Ayurvedic OR "Indian traditional medicine"; "Surveys and Questionnaires"[MeSH] OR Psychometrics [MeSH] OR questionnaire OR scale OR instrument OR tool OR "outcome measure"; and "Validation Studies as Topic"[MeSH] OR validat* OR reliab* OR "internal consistency" OR "construct validity" OR develop* OR "measurement properties" OR responsiveness OR "item generation" OR "item selection". These tconcepts will be combined using AND to identify relevant literature.All search results will be imported into reference management software (e.g., Zotero) for de-duplication and organized for screening using systematic review software.

Review process

This review includes screening of all retrieved records in two stages: title and abstract screening and full-text review, using predefined eligibility criteria. In the initial phase, titles and abstracts will be screened independently and in duplicate using Rayyan software, with reviewers blinded to each other's decisions. This review will engage six team members. Two will manage the literature search and reference organization. Independent duplicate screening will be performed by Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2, while data extraction will be handled by Reviewer 3 and Reviewer 4. Discrepancies at any stage will be resolved by discussion, with Reviewer 5 or Reviewer 6 serving as adjudicators. All members will collaboratively contribute to data synthesis and manuscript writing, ensuring accuracy and transparency. Articles deemed potentially relevant by either reviewer will proceed to full-text review, where eligibility will be reassessed against the inclusion criteria. During full-text screening, a minimum reporting criteria using a standardized checklist will be used. Studies lacking at least basic content-validity information (e.g., item-generation details or CVI data) will be excluded directly. Authors will be contacted only for studies that meet the minimum content-validity threshold but require clarification or additional information (4-week response window, with a reminder at 2 weeks). All exclusions and reasons will be documented in the review. Studies published in English will be included. This criterion applies to the publication itself, not the original language or naming of the measurement instrument, provided its development and validation details are accessible in English. During the full-text review, potential duplicates will be assessed. Overlapping publications will be grouped, with the peer-reviewed article designated as the primary source. Any unique supplementary information from related sources will be clearly attributed.

Software for screening

Rayyan software will be used to manage the screening process. The platform allows for blinded, independent screening and tagging of studies (e.g., inclusion, exclusion, or further review), and facilitates transparent documentation of decisions. A PRISMA 2020 flow diagram will be used to report the study selection process in the final review.

Data extraction

Key information which is to be extracted will include study design and methodology; participant demographics; detailed instrument characteristics such as constructs measured, qualitative development processes (item generation, stakeholder involvement, content validity, cognitive testing, expert/patient input, item revisions), quantitative psychometric properties (validity, reliability), applied validation frameworks (COSMIN), statistical methods and risk of bias assessments.

The data extraction form will identify the type of publication (e.g., journal article, thesis, dissertation), any related publications reporting overlapping data, and ensure author confirmations when there is a need to verify any potential duplication.

Extraction and evaluation of qualitative development processes

Qualitative methods in instrument development will be systematically extracted and evaluated as core components of content validity following COSMIN principles.

For item generation, information will be extracted on theoretical basis (classical texts, contemporary literature, clinical experience), stakeholder involvement (patients, professionals, experts), methods used (interviews, focus groups, Delphi panels, concept elicitation), sample characteristics, item pool development procedures, and documentation quality.

For content validity, data will be extracted on relevance, comprehensiveness, and comprehensibility assessments, expert panel composition, quantitative metrics (I-CVI, S-CVI); patient/public involvement; cultural and linguistic considerations; and item modification procedures. All qualitative steps will be evaluated using the COSMIN PROM Development and Content Validity checklists, with quality ratings assigned using the ‘worst score counts’ principle.

Handling missing data

Corresponding authors will be contacted to obtain incomplete or unclear data. Persistent gaps will be transparently reported and considered in the data synthesis.

Tools for data management

Extraction and management will utilize Cochrane RevMan to maintain systematic, auditable records. Reference and screening management will be supported by Zotero and Rayyan software, respectively.

Risk of bias (quality) assessment

The methodological quality of included studies will be assessed by two independent reviewers using the COSMIN Risk of Bias checklist for systematic reviews of Patient-Reported Outcome Measures (PROMs). This tool is specifically designed for evaluating the development and psychometric properties of health measurement instruments. Disagreements will be resolved by discussion or a third reviewer. Two independent reviewers will assess methodological quality using the COSMIN Risk of Bias checklist for PROMs [12]. The checklist evaluates key measurement properties such as PROM development, content validity, structural validity, internal consistency, cross-cultural validity, reliability, measurement error, criterion validity, construct validity, and responsiveness. Each property will be rated using the relevant COSMIN standards box on the four-point scale (very good, adequate, doubtful, inadequate), with overall scores determined by the ‘worst-score-counts’ rule. Disagreements will be resolved by discussion or a third reviewer. All ratings will be recorded in standardized extraction forms and summarized in tables.

Strategy for data synthesis

A narrative descriptive synthesis will be employed to summarize the characteristics of included studies, instrument development methodologies, and reported psychometric properties. Data on methodological quality, derived from the COSMIN Risk of Bias assessment, will be integrated into the synthesis to contextualize the findings. Heterogeneity among instruments, populations, and methodologies will be discussed qualitatively, highlighting patterns, gaps, and areas for future research in Ayurvedic instrument development. Quantitative pooling of effect sizes and reporting of traditional measures of effect are not applicable, as this review primarily employs a narrative synthesis.

Narrative synthesis

A comprehensive narrative descriptive synthesis will serve as the primary method for presenting the findings. This will involve a structured summary and critical discussion of instrument development methodologies, reported psychometric properties, and methodological quality (informed by the COSMIN Risk of Bias assessment). The synthesis will aim to map the current landscape, identify methodological gaps, explore challenges, and highlight future directions in Ayurvedic instrument development and validation. Heterogeneity will be discussed qualitatively.

Tools for synthesis

Search results will be managed using a reference management software (e.g., Zotero). Data extraction will employ a standardized, pre-piloted form (e.g., in Microsoft Excel). For descriptive summarization of extracted data, standard spreadsheet software or basic statistical functions may be utilized. No specialized meta-analysis software will be used, as a quantitative synthesis (meta-analysis) is not planned.

Sensitivity analysis

A formal quantitative sensitivity analysis, as defined for meta-analyses, is not applicable to this systematic review, as it primarily employs a narrative descriptive synthesis and does not involve quantitative pooling of effect sizes. However, to ensure the robustness of our findings, we will qualitatively assess the impact of methodological quality (as determined by the COSMIN Risk of Bias assessment) on the reported psychometric properties and identified methodological patterns. Discussions will highlight whether conclusions regarding the current landscape and methodological hurdles differ significantly when considering studies of varying methodological rigor.

Language restrictions and potential bias

The review being limited to only English language publications may introduce a bias as other instruments in regional languages will be excluded. This is due to the resource limitations for translation and need of the review to accurately represent in terms of psychometric terminology. To address this, databases with English abstracts such as DHARA and will be searched, authors of relevant studies will be contacted for English material, document the studies not in English and discuss the language bias in the final review. This may underrepresent the instruments developed in vernacular languages. The findings will be interpreted accordingly.

Results

This being a systematic review protocol, no definitive results are available at this stage. However, this review is expected to identify and critically analyse studies reporting on the development and validation of measurement instruments specifically designed for Ayurvedic constructs. Anticipated findings include: an overview of instrument types and constructs, synthesis of development methodologies, assessment of reported psychometric properties and methodological quality via COSMIN, and identification of key methodological hurdles and gaps. These results will inform future rigorous instrument development and enhance evidence-based assessment in Ayurveda.

Discussion

This systematic review protocol addresses the critical need for validated measurement instruments within Ayurveda. As the global interest in this ancient medical system grows, robust tools are essential for characterizing foundational constructs such as Prakriti and Agni, which underpin Ayurvedic diagnosis and treatment. Despite existing efforts, the development of these instruments has been hindered by methodological inconsistencies and insufficient psychometric validation.

This review aims to comprehensively map current Ayurvedic measurement instruments, identify methodological challenges in their development, and assess the quality of their reported psychometric properties. It will critically examine the applicability of established validation frameworks, such as COSMIN, within the Ayurvedic context, and highlight discrepancies with international guidelines.

Through a systematic search across major biomedical and Ayurvedic databases, this review will synthesize evidence on the development, validation, and psychometric characteristics of these instruments. The findings will contribute to understanding current practices, identifying key methodological gaps, and proposing future directions to enhance the scientific rigor and clinical utility of Ayurvedic measurement tools. Ultimately, this work will strengthen the evidence base for Ayurvedic research and facilitate its integration into broader healthcare systems.

Limitations

This systematic review acknowledges potential limitations including anticipated heterogeneity across Ayurvedic constructs, instrument types, and validation methodologies, which may constrain direct comparability. Variations in study design for instrument development and inconsistent reporting quality in primary studies could hinder comprehensive assessment and synthesis. Furthermore, evolving psychometric standards over time may impact data consistency and may require careful contextualization. Restricting the review to English-language publications introduces language bias, which may exclude the instruments developed in other regional Indian languages. Although an attempt will be made to remove this bias through database search and contacting the author, the review will be mainly representing the English-accessible literature. Future reviews should adopt multilingual search as well as translation strategies to address this gap.

Conclusion

Robust measurement instruments are vital for advancing evidence-based Ayurveda. This systematic review protocol outlines a comprehensive approach to map the current landscape of Ayurvedic instrument development, critically analyse methodological hurdles, and propose essential future directions. By synthesizing existing evidence and identifying key gaps, this review aims to significantly enhance the methodological rigor and psychometric quality of tools used in Ayurvedic research and practice. Ultimately, its findings will strengthen the scientific credibility and global applicability of Ayurvedic healthcare.
