A lightweight hybrid framework for real-time data refinement in resource-constrained underwater and underground wireless sensor networks
Authors: Samia Allaoua Chelloug, Sajal Nazir, Rahim Khan, Muazzam A. Khan Khattak, Fahad Alturise, Salem Alkhalaf
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
With the help of harsh propagation environments, reliable data acquisition in UWSNs/UGWSNs may suffer from packet loss a few times, extra communication latency, energy scarcity, and retransmission overhead. These elements lead to two common problems for the data quality: missing values attributed to packets lost or nodes going down and duplicate readings introduced by retransmissions/synchronization problems. Current data filtering techniques either solve these issues separately or adopt computationally complex models, which are not appropriate for resource-constrained UWSN/UGWSN scenarios. To address this, we have introduced a computationally simple real-time hybrid data refinement framework that leverages a Kalman filter (KF) for imputation of missing entities and relies on a Sliding Window that is based on Manhattan Distance (SWMD) approach for detection of repetitive entries. The proposed scheme along with existing approaches have been implemented and experimentally validated in the OMNeT++ simulation environment for UWSN-realistic conditions—where, in addition to node energy spanning percentage losses and random delays, 20–40% of packets are lost—the framework jointly considers the two types of anomalies without needing any training data or cloud offloading. Evaluated on 1200 diverse sensor nodes over 20,000 recordings, the proposed method reaches a Mean Absolute Deviation (MAE) of 1.20 and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 1.75 for missing value estimation; it has filtered out 20.5% redundant packets, and enhances the Packet Delivery Ratio (PDR) to up to 88% than existing methodologies. More importantly, the network lifetime achieved was up to 122s (over two-times longer than state-of-the-art baselines methodologies), while the average end-to-end delay is maintained within 11.1 ms. By supporting high-quality, both accurate and complete, as well as energy-efficient data streams, this framework enables robust real-time analytics for long-term monitoring in environments preventing break-in accessibility, such as deep-sea observatories, subterranean infrastructure, and mining systems.

Introduction

Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) are proven to be a crucial infrastructure for environmental monitoring, industrial automation, emergency response, and structure management. One of the most difficult—and scientifically rewarding—environment in which sensor nodes can be deployed are underwater wireless sensor networks (UWSNs) and underground wireless sensor networks (UGWSNs). These extreme-environment networks provide continuous coverage for domains in which humans cannot penetrate: deep-ocean ecosystems for climate study and biodiversity monitoring1, subsea oil and gas pipelines for integrity check2, mining tunnels for safety monitoring3, and buried utilities for smart city resilience4. Despite their captivating nature, the unique characteristics of UWSNs and UGWSNs confronting harsh physical limitations as well as operational constraints fundamentally threaten data authenticity. In particular, the acoustic (in water) and magnetic induction or seismic (in soil) communication channels are severely affected by large propagation delays (~seconds in deep water), multipath effects, shadowing, extremely high packet losses rates that frequently exceed 30–50% in practical applications5,6. These channel imperfections, coupled with the energy-constrained characteristics of battery-driven sensor nodes result in two interrelated, but often misunderstood data anomalies: (i) missing values caused by packets that are lost irreversibly or due to node failures, and (ii) duplicate readings produced by protocol-level retransmissions, clock drift,s or overlapping sensing regions7. Having a combination of null and duplicate data is not just an inconvenience—it is actively sabotaging the fidelity of your downstream analytics. For instance in underwater chemical plume tracking, missing salinity or temperature points may cause incorrect trajectory inference, while failing to detect the duplicates can artificially inflate concentration estimates, leading to false pollution alerts8. Likewise, in tunnel structural health monitoring systems, redundant strain gauge data can obscure the onset of cracks, whereas voids in vibrating data can delay decisions about critical maintenance9. Common practices of data cleaning—like mean imputation, linear interpolation, or pairwise Euclidean-distance based deduplication—are inappropriate in these settings. Such methods either neglect temporal characteristics (mean/median imputation)10, assume stationary (ARIMA), or have high computational cost (KNN and deep learning)11,12. Furthermore, the vast majority of current methods tackle missing and duplicate data individually: see13 where empirical results show that their causes (e.g. duplicates triggered by retransmission vis-à-vis gaps caused by losses) are closely related in lossy UWSN channels. These challenges have been recently mitigated by new developments; however, there are still important areas that remain uncovered. For example, an energy-efficient data aggregation was introduced, which claims to has suppresses redundant transmissions in static WSNs, but does not address the reconstruction of lost data15. Likewise, Altowaijri’s Grid-Hashing based Efficient Data Aggregation16, which achieves strong deduplication accuracy for cloud-assisted IoT via Merkle hash trees, requires centralized processing and consistent connection—requirements not met in subterranean or deep-sea settings. On the imputation aspect, Zhang et al.17 showed GANs can generate realistic missing values for IoT stream, but it does need a lot of offline training and GPU-level resources, which is impractical for microcontroller-class sensor nodes. Even simple Bayesian approaches such as KF, which has been applied in the area of navigation and tracking for a long time18, have only recently been investigated for UWSN data recovery (e.g., see Li et al. 8 used KF for reconstructing temperature gaps in shallow-water networks, without addressing duplicate suppression.

Important, however, is that none of these consider a unified, real-time solution for detecting both types of the data anomalies under the stringent energy, latency and computational constraints encountered in UWSNs/UGWSNs. This is not an inconsequential omission: as Decorte, et al.19 very recently noted in an extensive benchmark of 14 imputation approaches against environmental IoT datasets “the most accurate imputation model does not matter if it cannot live on the edge or fails when subjected to simultaneous data corruption modes.” Likewise, the Scientific Reports editorial board has pleaded for “lightweight, physics-informed data refinement tools that preserve scientific validity in resource-constrained sensing environments.

To fill this gap, we propose a new hybrid data refinement framework that integrates the classical Kalman Filter (KF) for real-time missing data reconstruction and a recent Sliding Window-based Manhattan Distance (SWMD) approach for duplicate elimination. Related work

Data quality challenges in extreme-environment sensor networks

Underground and underwater sensor networks as well as underwater wireless-sensor-network operation, deal with the most unfavorable communication environments in the context of wireless engineering. In UWSNs, acoustic propagation is a dominant force, but comes with relatively low bandwidth (typically 30% packet loss rates even in shallow deployments24. The UGWSN is also based on magnetic induction or seismic waves, which have high attenuation in soil or rock, leading to extremely short communication range (longest< 10 m) and bursty, asymmetric links25. With non-rechargeable battery-powered operation and no maintenance access as well as physical limitations, it’s a perfect storm for data corruption. Important, two types of anomalies are prevalent: (1) missing values, resulted from non-recoverable packet loss, node sleep cycles or hardware failures; and (2) duplicate readings, caused by protocol retransmissions, clock drift or overlapping sensing footprints26. In contrast to terrestrial IoT, where cloud processing can be used for cleanup offloading, in UGSWN/UGWSN the nodes need to address these challenges on-device and in real-time while preserving energy.

Missing data imputation: from statistics to deep learning

A broad range of imputation methods has been investigated for IoT sensor streams. Classical statistical techniques– like mean/median imputation or linear interpolation are computationally lightweight but rely on making unrealistic assumptions: they forget about temporal dependence and work under the assumption that data is missing completely at random (MCAR)27. ARIMA models and other time-series analyses can capture the temporal correlations, but craves for stationary data, and hand-tuning (p,d,q) parameters which are unrealistic in the dynamic oceanic or subterranean domains with nonstationary drift or sudden change in sensor readings.

To address these limitations, machine learning techniques have been developed. K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) imputation uses spatial correlation among neighbouring nodes28 which however does not scale well with network size (O(n²) distance computations) and assumes dense deployment—far from true in sparse deep sea arrays. Random Forests and support vector regression provides the ability to produce a non-linear model, but requires high memory capacity and big amounts of training data28. Lately, deep learning models such as autoencoders29 and GANs have shown a high imputation accuracy by capturing complex data manifolds. However, as Decorte et al.28 empirically demonstrated in 12 environmental datasets, these models are also orders of magnitude more energy-hungry (10–100×) than statistical baselines and fundamentally break down under >20

On the contrary, Kalman Filter (KF) finds a very interesting balance. By iteratively fusing a process model and with sensor observation, KF yields real-time optimal (when Gaussian distributed) state estimates with little space requirement. Li et al. 20 adopted KF to denoise missing temperature and pressure data in a shallow-water UWSN, with 28% lower RMSE than that of ARIMA. However, they only considered imputation and not duplicate data — a critical concomitant anomaly in lossy channels where retransmissions yield both gaps (lost originals) and duplicates (successful second transmissions).

Duplicate detection and deduplication mechanisms

Duplicate suppression in WSNs has relied on metadata (e.g., sequence numbers, timestamps) or content hashing. However, it is impossible to keep per-packet metadata at memory-limited nodes (e.g., <64 KB RAM), and clock synchronization is notoriously challenging underwater because of depth-dependent sound speed30. Content-Based methods therefore, emerge as a synchronization-free option.

Basic deduplication solutions based on Euclidean distance have also been investigated in generic IoT scenarios. The pairwise comparison of the method is quadratic in data volume, and it involves expensive square-root operations, which are prohibitive to energyconstrained nodes31. Yu et al.32 used Manhattan distance (L1 norm) and fuzzy hashing to enhance the efficiency and save 40% CPU cycles on ARM Cortex-M4. Their method was proven to work in stable communication smart grids but not UWSNs with high loss.

More sophisticated schemes leverage network-level redundancy. Benson et al.33 introduced EE-FDAS, a fuzzy logic-based aggregation scheme that suppresses duplicate transmissions at the MAC layer. By comparing sensed values with neighbors before forwarding, EE-FDAS reduced energy consumption and extended network lifetime. Yet, it assumes spatial correlation and dense topology—conditions violated in sparse or mobile UWSNs. Moreover, like most deduplication works, it treats only duplicates, ignoring missing data.

Altowaijri’s GH-EDA34 employs a grid-hashing Merkle tree for cloud-assisted deduplication, achieving high accuracy with low recomputation. However, it requires centralized processing and stable uplinks—unavailable in deep-sea or mining scenarios where surface connectivity is intermittent or absent. Chen et al.35 modeled multi-sensor correlations as a graph to detect redundant data, but their method demands O(n²) memory to store adjacency matrices, rendering it impractical for networks with >100 nodes.

Yet only a very small part of works pertains to both anomalies simultaneously even if the underlying root causes (e.g., retransmission-induced duplicates plus loss-induced gaps) are inherently coupled in error-prone channels36. Guo et al. identified ,,double-sampling” as a problem in delay-tolerant UWSNs, and presented a time-windowed consistency check), but their approach assumed clock synchronization and left values unspecified. There already exist solutions based on cloud resources (no autonomous UWSN), with idealized network conditions such as perfect synchronization or that focus on small engineering improvements without addressing the by-product of data anomalies. We directly address this gap in our work through a unified, physics-informed, as well as an open-source framework that works fully at the edge—ensuring sustainable and future-proof scientific monitoring in some of Earth’s most challenging environments.

Methodology

This part introduces the over-all method procedure of our hybrid data refinement framework for UWSNs/UGWSNs. The framework has put emphasis on three key principles, (1) on-node feasibility – all operations can be run with resource limited sensor hardware; (2) real-time processing – data should not be buffered or rely on an external cloud to operate, (3) anomaly co-handling – addressing missing and duplicate values within a unified model. The approach includes four inter-related parts like (i) system architecture, (ii) the simulation of dataset under UWSN-authentic conditions, (iii) the Sliding Window-based Manhattan Distance (SWMD) algorithm to detect duplicates and, (iv) the Kalman Filter (KF) for missing data filling. All modules are developed in OMNeT++ 6.1 and verified based on a temperature-like scalar dataset containing 20,000 sampling points over 200 non-identical sensor nodes.

System architecture

The proposed system follows the star-topology, which is often an implementation scenario adopted in real UWSNs when a direct node-to-sink connection is preferred to avoid Node-to-Sink multi-hop energy dissipation cost and routing overhead, especially for mobile or sparse scenarios37. As shown in Fig. 2, the network is composed of two main parts: Sensor nodes: 200 different types of IoT devices, integrated with a temperature sensor, low-power microcontroller (ARM Cortex-M4 based) and an acoustic modem (OMNeT++ gates emulation). Each node is self-contained and provides one scalar measurement every second during a 10,000-second simulation window. Packets are packaged as DataPacket messages including: (a) the sender’s senderID (1–200), (b) a monotonically increasing sequenceNumber per node, (c) a new measurement with floating point accuracy (value, temperature in °C), and (d) a timestamp (timestamp) indicating the simulation time at which this data was sent into the network in seconds. Importantly, all processing –validation of sequences, discarding duplicates and imputation of missing values–occurs on the fusion center to mimic edge-computing approaches where light weight process is carried out on the network edge not in cloud. Kalman filter for missing data imputation

The Kalman filter (KF) provides optimal recursive estimation for linear Gaussian systems—ideal for slowly varying UWSN phenomena. Our KF implementation operates in two modes:Update mode: when a valid (non-duplicate) packet arrives, KF fuses the observation with the prior estimate.Prediction mode: when sequence gaps are detected, KF extrapolates missing values using system dynamics alone.State model: we assume temperature evolves as:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x_t = x_{t-1} + w_t, \quad w_t \sim \mathcal {N}(0, Q)$$\end{document}where Q = 0.1 models process noise (small drift expected in deep sea).

Conclusion and future work

A novel hybrid data mining system, which is real-time and well-suited for subterranean or undersea urgent threshold of wireless sensor network in this paper. By a hybrid approach of KF in processing for making up loss and SWMD in operation for deleting duplicate packets, our solution combines these two most typical problems to a single feed stream worked at the feeding point. Verified through extensive simulations on a realistic UWSN, with packet loss rate between 20-40%, delay variations, and 200 heterogeneous nodes, the method has a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 1.20; and eliminates over 20.5% redundant packets. PDR up to 88% and NETWORK LIFETIME over 122 s long—Venabling for more than twice the latest potentials—are achieved while keeping the end-to-end delay less than 11.1 ms in length. Notably, the scheme is performs entirely on-node. And it does not need to be trained or have data, and it is serverless (or offloading) to run this. It also represents the temporal information necessary in remote monitoring systems, such as deep-sea observatories, subsea pipelines, and mines.

The proposed approach results show that methodological simplicity, provided it is physically realistic and respects the domain constraints. It works better than the sophisticated learning-based methods in extreme environment perception. This is in accordance with the recent appreciation of the fact that edge intelligence for scientific IoT should give more importance to the notions of robustness, energy efficiency, and reproducibility than to model complexity.

Future work

From this study, several interesting outlines emerge:Embracing multivariate sensor data streams: while the focus of this paper is on the single-valued data (e.g., degrees), all future incarnations have instructed us to customize the frame work so as to include multivariate UWSN streamlets such as provided from pressure and salinity besides vector Kalman filters terminal and multivariate distance metrics in a holistic fashion that involves off-line profiling of environmental characteristic features.Adaptive sampling with SWMD: leveraging SWMD in duty-cycling protocols may lead to ‘adaptive sampling’: low transmission rates when the environment is stable (i.e., when the SWMD distance is small), and increased transmissions before and after an anomaly (i.e., if the distance high), potentially further extending battery life.Deployment and validation in real-world setting: we plan to deploy this framework on commercial underwater platforms (e.g., Teledyne Benthos modems connected to RPi-class edge nodes) in both coastal and ocean floor testbeds, where the performance of our protocol will be tested under realistic oceanographic conditions — including bio-fouling, current-induced drift of node positions and tidal noise.Non-linearity: for highly turbulent environments such as hydrothermal vents, etc., we would search for simplistic non-linear filters, etc. (such as EKF or UKF) but keeping the SWMD deduplication core.Distributed implementation: although our network relies on a fusion center for the implemented version, we would like to investigate a fully distributed version where neighbouring nodes work together and enhance the data using gossip protocols— which is more robust in case of single-failure points as experienced in sparse or mobile UWSN.
