 Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) to conduct a SAF study in
 New England
 • Purpose: identify the feedstocks and technology pathways
 to produce SAF in New England and assess distribution and
 blending of Jet A in the region
 • Sections:
 ◦ Resource Assessment: quantify regional feedstocks in

 ◦ Fuel Pathway Assessment (techno-economic analysis):
 estimate regional SAF production volumes and
 minimum selling price

 Key Takeaways
• The resource assessment found that New England has ample feedstock to produce
 SAF and other biofuels and bioproducts
• Municipal solid waste, woody biomass, and food waste were the most abundant
 resources
• A techno-economic analysis was conducted on total potential New England biofuel
 production with a theoretical maximum of 850 million gallons of gasoline equivalent
 per year
• SAF produced outside of New England is expected to be pre-blended with Jet A in
 other markets due to infrastructure size and flexibility
• SAF produced within New England requires more consideration with space
 constraints and fuel receipt modes
• Many New England terminal owners expressed a willingness to work with future
 SAF producers to enable blending and delivery to airports within the region

Techno-Economic Analysis
(TEA)

 Methodology
• NLR develops and maintains TEA models that describe the the process and
 production of conceptual conversion pathways for biofuels and bioproducts
• Data were generated with Aspen Plus process simulation using a set of
  conversion parameters, material and energy balances, and flow rates used
  to size and cost process equipment and operating costs (see report for detailed assumptions)
• Minimum Fuel Selling Price (MFSP) is based on discounted cash flow rate of
 return required to obtain a net present value of zero for a 10% internal rate of
 return
• TEA outputs carry a degree of uncertainty with assumptions made on capital
 and variable costs
• The TEA outputs of production volume and MFSP are best used to compare
 different pathways and feedstocks against one another
                                                                            National Laboratory of the Rockies | 26

 TEA Information
• Westfield-Barnes Regional Airport and Worcester Regional Airport were the
 primary locations for the TEA representing airports near Boston and western
 Massachusetts
 ◦ The TEA included HEFA pathway for fats/oils/greases and anaerobic digestion pathway
 of food waste and sludge
• Portland International Jetport was selected for the gasification pathway of
 woody biomass and select municipal solid waste due to Maine’s robust wood
 industry
• MFSP is shown both with and without feedstock costs to allow for comparison of
 conversion costs

  identified as the ideal

Jet A & SAF
Blending
• Jet A meets ASTM D1655
• SAF meets ASTM D7566
• SAF and Jet A are stored
  in two separate tanks and
  blended at the desired
  ratio into a third tank
• The blended fuel is tested
  to ASTM D1655 standards
  and upon successful
  testing is approved for
  use in aircraft

 SAF Produced Outside of
 New England
• NLR met with all New England transportation fuel terminal owners to discuss
 options for SAF delivery, storage, and blending
• SAF produced outside of New England is expected to be delivered pre-blended
 due to existing production volumes, terminal space constraints in New England,
 and infrastructure availability
• Two primary options for delivery of SAF/Jet A blends to New England:
 ◦ Blend at a Gulf Coast terminal, ship on Colonial Pipeline to New York Harbor and
 delivered to New England terminals by barge/tanker
 ◦ Blend at a New York Harbor terminal as they are larger and offer more storage and
 fuel receipt flexibility

                                                                            National Laboratory of the Rockies | 42

 SAF Produced in New
 England
• SAF produced locally requires more consideration and planning due to
 infrastructure constraints
• A preferred blending terminal could lower costs for locally produced SAF storage
 and blending if:
 ◦ Located near a SAF production facility
 ◦ Currently stores jet fuel (existing dedicated infrastructure reduces costs)
 ◦ Existing infrastructure to receive fuel by truck and offload by both truck and marine
• Other terminals not currently storing jet stated that if there is a business case,
 they could potentially store and blend SAF
• Terminal owners expressed a willingness to work with local SAF producers to
 enable the local market

 Conclusions
• The resource assessment found that MSW and food waste are the most
  abundant resources within 50- and 100-mile radii, reflecting the airports’
  locations in densely populated urban areas.
• The TEA determined the ability to produce SAF in meaningful quantities near
 New England Airports with MSW providing the highest volume drawing
 feedstock from a 100-mile radii
• The logistics assessment determined that SAF produced outside of New England
 will be delivered pre-blended from New York Harbor or the Gulf Coast. This is
 due to current low SAF volumes and the larger and less space constrained
 terminals in other areas
• New England SAF producers should work with terminals early in the
 development to determine where to blend their fuel for delivery to airports
