Four intuitionistic modal connectives
Philippe Balbiani Çiğdem Gencer

Abstract
We introduce the syntax and the semantics of intuitionistic modal logics based on a connective ◊ \lozenge à la Přenosil, its dual connective □ \square , a connective ⧫ \blacklozenge à la Wijesekera and its dual connective ■ \blacksquare . We analyze the modal definability of some elementary classes of frames. We study the complete axiomatizability of the sets of valid formulas determined by these classes of frames. We prove the decidability of the minimal intuitionistic modal logic determined by the class of all frames.

Conclusion

Much remains to be done.

Inference rules
(\mathbf{R}8^{+})
and
(\mathbf{R}8^{-})
have ben used in the proofs of Proposition
59
and
60
.
However, we do not know of any formula whose formal proof in the IMLs considered in this article requires the use of these inference rules.
Therefore, a natural question is to determine if one can replace inference rules
(\mathbf{R}8^{+})
and
(\mathbf{R}8^{-})
by finitely many signed formulas considered as additional axioms without affecting the meaning of the definition of IMLs.

We have seen in Proposition
70
and
71
that
\mathtt{Log}({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{ref}})
and
\mathtt{Log}({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{ser}})
are finitely axiomatizable.
Therefore, a natural question is to determine whether
\mathtt{Log}({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{sym}})
and
\mathtt{Log}({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{tra}})
are finitely axiomatizable too.
In this respect, it might be helpful to understand how far is
\mathbf{L}_{\min}{\odot}\{(+,p{\rightharpoonup}{\square}{\lozenge}p),(-,p{\leftharpoondown}{\lozenge}{\square}p))\}
from
\mathtt{Log}({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{sym}})
and how far is
\mathbf{L}_{\min}{\odot}\{(+,{\square}p{\rightharpoonup}{\square}{\square}p),(-,{\lozenge}p{\leftharpoondown}{\lozenge}{\lozenge}p))\}
from
\mathtt{Log}({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{tra}})
.

We have seen in Proposition
72
that
\mathtt{Log}({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{f}})
and
\mathtt{Log}({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{d}})
are finitely axiomatizable.
Therefore, a natural question is to determine whether
\mathtt{Log}({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{b}})
and
\mathtt{Log}({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{u}})
— as well as
\mathtt{Log}({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{fb}})
,
\mathtt{Log}({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{fd}})
, etc — are finitely axiomatizable too.
Notice that
by Proposition
24
,
34
,
52
,
55
,
56
,
65
and
68
, we already know that
\mathtt{Log}({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{fd}}){=}\mathbf{L}_{\min}{\odot}\{(+,{\square}(p{\rightharpoonup}
q){\rightharpoonup}({\lozenge}p{\rightharpoonup}{\blacklozenge}q)),(-,{\lozenge}(p{\leftharpoondown}q){\leftharpoondown}({\square}p{\leftharpoondown}{\blacksquare}q))\}
.

We have seen in Proposition
76
that the membership problem in
\mathbf{L}_{\min}
is decidable.
Therefore, a natural question is to determine the complexity class to which this problem belongs.
In this respect, it might be helpful to use an embedding of
\mathbf{L}_{\min}
into
\mathbf{S}4_{\mathbf{t}}{\otimes}\mathbf{K}
— the fusion of tense
\mathbf{S}4
and
\mathbf{K}
— in the spirit of the embedding of IMLs into modal logics containing
\mathbf{S}4{\otimes}\mathbf{K}
— the fusion of
\mathbf{S}4
and
\mathbf{K}
— proposed by Wolter and Zakharyaschev
[
39
]
.

With a few exceptions such as
[
21
,
35
]
, the technique of the filtration has not been so much adapted to IMLs, probably because it does not easily work with conditions such as forward confluence, backward confluence, downward confluence and upward confluence.
Therefore, a natural question is to understand how filtration-like arguments can be used in order to determine whether the membership problems in the IMLs considered in this article are decidable.
