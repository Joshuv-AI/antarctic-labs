On vacua and bounded masses in the general 2HDM
José M. Camacho, Carlos Miró, Miguel Nebot, Tomás Tobarra

Abstract
Two Higgs doublets models with a scalar potential that breaks electroweak symmetry spontaneously can have either one or two local minima. While potentials with one minimum can have a decoupling regime where all the new scalars are heavy, we show that, for potentials with two local minima, the masses of all the scalars are bounded if the dimensionless quartic couplings obey perturbativity constraints.

In some cases, not necessarily multi-Higgs scenarios, scalar sectors where an imposed symmetry yields a number of quadratic parameters in the potential not larger than the number of stationarity conditions, the quadratic parameters can be expressed in terms of bounded quartic parameters and vacuum expectation values, giving as a result that all the new scalars have bounded masses. One illustrative case is the 2HDM where the potential is CP invariant and the vacuum breaks CP spontaneously: the 3 real quadratic parameters in the potential are reexpressed in terms of dimensionless quartic parameters and vevs, and then perturbativity constraints on the quartic parameters force
\mathcal{O}(v_{\rm EW})

;
31

This motivates the present work: since the general 2HDM can have either one or two possible vacua
19
, is something similar at work? 2HDMs, or at least some of them, can have a decoupling regime for the new scalars
14
;
13

The surprising answer is that in the case of a potential with two minima, the masses of all the scalars are bounded, they cannot be much larger than the electroweak scale, if perturbativity constraints are imposed on the quartic couplings.

II
The general 2HDM

The most general scalar potential for two
SU(2)_{L}
doublets with hypercharge
1/2
,
\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}
and
\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}
, is

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}_{2}(\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})+\mathcal{V}_{4}(\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}),

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{2}(\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})

\displaystyle=\mu^{2}_{1}\,\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}+\mu^{2}_{2}\,\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}+\mu^{2}_{12}\,\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}+\mu^{2\,\ast}_{12}\,\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{4}(\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}(\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}})^{2}+\lambda_{2}(\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})^{2}+\lambda_{3}(\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}})(\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})+\lambda_{4}(\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})(\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}})

\displaystyle+\lambda_{5}(\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})^{2}+\lambda_{5}^{\ast}(\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}})^{2}

\displaystyle+(\lambda_{6}\,\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}+\lambda_{6}^{\ast}\,\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}})(\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}})+(\lambda_{7}\,\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}+\lambda_{7}^{\ast}\,\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}})(\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})\,,

where
\mu^{2}_{1}
,
\mu^{2}_{2}
,
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
,
\lambda_{3}
,
\lambda_{4}
are necessarily real, while
\mu^{2}_{12}
,
\lambda_{5}
,
\lambda_{6}
,
\lambda_{7}
are, in general, complex. Spontaneous symmetry breaking occurs for a minimum of the function

\mathrm{V}(\langle\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle,\langle\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle)\equiv\mathcal{V}(\langle\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle,\langle\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle)\,,

(2)

with
\langle\Phi_{a}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle\neq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Electroweak spontaneous symmetry breaking is assumed for vevs

\langle\Phi_{a}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle=\frac{v_{a}e^{i\theta_{a}}}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},\qquad v_{a},\theta_{a}\in\mathbb{R}\,,\quad v_{a}\geq 0\,,

(3)

and the fields are expanded as

\Phi_{a}^{\phantom{\dagger}}=\frac{e^{i\theta_{a}}}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{2}\mathrm{C}^{+}_{a}\\
v_{a}+\mathrm{R}_{a}+i\,\mathrm{I}_{a}\end{pmatrix}.

(4)

Only the phase difference
\theta\equiv\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}
(mod
2\pi
) is physical and can produce spontaneous CP violation. We use
v\equiv\sqrt{v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}}
; for adequate electroweak symmetry breaking, one sets
v=v_{\rm EW}
. The 3 stationarity conditions, necessarily fulfilled at a minimum of the potential, are

\displaystyle\partial_{v_{1}}\mathrm{V}

\displaystyle=\mu^{2}_{1}v_{1}+\text{Re}\left(\mu^{2}_{12}e^{i\theta}\right)v_{2}+\lambda_{1}v_{1}^{3}+\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})v_{1}v_{2}^{2}

(5)

\displaystyle+\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{5}e^{i2\theta}\right)v_{1}v_{2}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{6}e^{i\theta}\right)v_{1}^{2}v_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{7}e^{i\theta}\right)v_{2}^{3}=0\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{v_{2}}\mathrm{V}

\displaystyle=\mu^{2}_{2}v_{2}+\text{Re}\left(\mu^{2}_{12}e^{i\theta}\right)v_{1}+\lambda_{2}v_{2}^{3}+\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})v_{1}^{2}v_{2}

(6)

\displaystyle+\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{5}e^{i2\theta}\right)v_{1}^{2}v_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{6}e^{i\theta}\right)v_{1}^{3}+\frac{3}{2}\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{7}e^{i\theta}\right)v_{1}v_{2}^{2}=0\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\mathrm{V}

\displaystyle=-\text{Im}\left(\mu^{2}_{12}e^{i\theta}\right)v_{1}v_{2}-\text{Im}\left(\lambda_{5}e^{i2\theta}\right)v_{1}^{2}v_{2}^{2}-\text{Im}\left((\lambda_{6}v_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{7}v_{2}^{2})e^{i\theta}\right)\frac{v_{1}v_{2}}{2}=0\,.

(7)

Notice that, with 4 quadratic parameters and 3 stationarity conditions, one cannot
engineer
a potential with the chosen vevs and all quadratic parameters fixed in terms of the vevs and other parameters: one typically chooses the vacuum configuration
\{v_{1},v_{2}e^{i\theta}\}
, and fixes
\mu^{2}_{1}
,
\mu^{2}_{2}
and
\text{Re}\left(\mu^{2}_{12}\right)
through
eqs.

5
,
6
and
7
, while
\text{Im}\left(\mu^{2}_{12}\right)
is free.

The mass terms for the charged
{\vec{\mathbf{C}}}^{\dagger}=(\mathrm{C}^{+}_{1},\mathrm{C}^{+}_{2})
and neutral fields
{\vec{\mathbf{N}}}^{T}=(\mathrm{R}_{1},\mathrm{R}_{2},\mathrm{I}_{1},\mathrm{I}_{2})
are

-\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Mass}}={\vec{\mathbf{C}}}^{\dagger}\,{\rm M_{\pm}^{2}}\,{\vec{\mathbf{C}}}+\frac{1}{2}{\vec{\mathbf{N}}}^{T}\,{\rm M_{0}^{2}}\,{\vec{\mathbf{N}}}\,,

{\rm M_{\pm}^{2}}

2\times 2

{\rm M_{0}^{2}}
is the
4\times 4

{\rm M_{\pm}^{2}}
and
{\rm M_{0}^{2}}
have null eigenvalues corresponding to the would-be Goldstone bosons, with eigenvectors

\displaystyle\vec{c}_{G}=(v_{1},v_{2})^{T}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle M_{\pm}^{2}}\,\vec{c}_{G}=\left(\partial_{v_{1}}\mathrm{V}+\frac{i}{v_{1}}\partial_{\theta}\mathrm{V},\ \partial_{v_{2}}\mathrm{V}-\frac{i}{v_{2}}\partial_{\theta}\mathrm{V}\right)^{T}=(0,0)^{T}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle\vec{n}_{G}=(0,0,v_{1},v_{2})^{T}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle M_{0}^{2}}\,\vec{n}_{G}=\left(\frac{1}{v_{1}}\partial_{\theta}\mathrm{V},\ -\frac{1}{v_{2}}\partial_{\theta}\mathrm{V},\ \partial_{v_{1}}\mathrm{V},\ \partial_{v_{2}}\mathrm{V}\right)^{T}=(0,0,0,0)^{T}\,.

{\rm M_{\pm}^{2}}
is diagonalized with a
2\times 2
unitary matrix
U
, which gives the would-be Goldstone bosons
G^{\pm}
and the physical charged scalar
\mathrm{H}^{\pm}

(\mathrm{C}^{-}_{1},\mathrm{C}^{-}_{2})^{T}=U\,(G^{-},\mathrm{H}^{-})^{T}\,,\qquad U^{\dagger}\,{\rm M_{\pm}^{2}}\,U=\text{Diag}(0,M_{\mathrm{H}^{\pm}}^{2})\,.

(10)

{\rm M_{0}^{2}}
is diagonalized with a
4\times 4
real orthogonal matrix
O
, which gives the would-be Goldstone boson
G^{0}
and the physical neutral scalars. We assume that the lightest neutral scalar is “the Higgs”
h
(this is just a convenient choice). We have

(\mathrm{R}_{1},\mathrm{R}_{2},\mathrm{I}_{1},\mathrm{I}_{2})^{T}=O\,(G^{0},h,\mathrm{H}_{1}^{0},\mathrm{H}_{2}^{0})^{T}\,,\qquad O^{T}\,{\rm M_{0}^{2}}\,O=\text{Diag}(0,m_{h}^{2},M_{\mathrm{H}_{1}^{0}}^{2},M_{\mathrm{H}_{2}^{0}}^{2})\,,

(11)

with
m_{h}\leq M_{\mathrm{H}_{1}^{0}}\leq M_{\mathrm{H}_{2}^{0}}
.

The diagonalization of
{\rm M_{\pm}^{2}}
is straightforwardly achieved by rotating to “the Higgs basis”
3
with

U=\frac{1}{v}\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}&-v_{2}\\
v_{2}&v_{1}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(12)

From the diagonalization, or computing directly
\text{Tr}\left({\rm M_{\pm}^{2}}\right)
, one obtains the mass of the charged scalar

M_{\mathrm{H}^{\pm}}^{2}=\mu^{2}_{1}+\mu^{2}_{2}+\lambda_{1}v_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}v_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{3}v^{2}+\text{Re}\left((\lambda_{6}+\lambda_{7})e^{i\theta}\right)v_{1}v_{2}\,,

(13)

expressed for simplicity in terms of
\mu^{2}_{1}+\mu^{2}_{2}
as the free quadratic parameter left; one can equivalently express
M_{\mathrm{H}^{\pm}}^{2}
, for example, in terms of
\text{Im}\left(\mu^{2}_{12}\right)
:

M_{\mathrm{H}^{\pm}}^{2}=\frac{v^{2}}{2v_{1}v_{2}\sin\theta}\text{Im}\left(2\mu^{2}_{12}+(2\lambda_{5}-\lambda_{4})e^{i\theta}v_{1}v_{2}+\lambda_{6}v_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{7}v_{2}^{2}\right)\,.

(14)

For
{\rm M_{0}^{2}}
, the calculation of the eigenvalues is not straightforward, but it is sufficient for now to notice that, from
\text{Tr}\left({\rm M_{0}^{2}}\right)
,

m_{h}^{2}+M_{\mathrm{H}_{1}^{0}}^{2}+M_{\mathrm{H}_{2}^{0}}^{2}=2(\mu^{2}_{1}+\mu^{2}_{2})+4(\lambda_{1}v_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}v_{2}^{2})+(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})v^{2}+4\text{Re}\left((\lambda_{6}+\lambda_{7})e^{i\theta}\right)v_{1}v_{2}\,.

(15)

From
eqs.

13
and
15
, it is clear that giving masses to the new scalars much larger than the electroweak scale
v_{\rm EW}
, requires
\mu^{2}_{1}+\mu^{2}_{2}\gg v_{\rm EW}^{2}
. The sole use of the stationarity conditions in
eqs.

5
,
6
and
7
does not appear to bar that possibility, and thus a decoupling regime for (all) the new scalars is possible.
A crucial comment is in order. We have mentioned
the
vacuum
\{v_{1},v_{2}e^{i\theta}\}
and have discussed accordingly the stationarity conditions, but it might happen that the scalar potential has not one but
two
local symmetry breaking minima, as analyzed in depth in
19
(see also
1
;
2
;
18
;
12
). If that is the case, then a second solution
\{v_{1}^{\prime},v_{2}^{\prime}e^{i\theta^{\prime}}\}
of
eqs.

5
,
6
and
7
exists. Then, the possibility of having a decoupling regime might be jeopardized: since we would have a number of conditions to be fulfilled in excess of the number of quadratic parameters, the latter can be expressed as products of quartic couplings and vevs, and thus bounded by perturbativity requirements.

III
Numerical exploration

We now address a numerical analysis of the central question of this work: the goal is to explore the general 2HDM
model space
focusing, for each model, on: (i) does the potential have one or two spontaneous symmetry breaking minima?, and (ii) in each case what is the spectrum of the new scalars? These are the steps followed in the process.

1.

We generate random values of all quartic parameters and check whether the quartic part of the potential is bounded from below. This is achieved by minimizing and/or randomly probing that, for a fixed value
v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}\neq 0
,
\mathrm{V}_{4}(v_{1},v_{2},\theta)\equiv\mathcal{V}_{4}(\langle\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle,\langle\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle)>0
. If the set of generated
\lambda
’s does not fulfill the condition, it is rejected, and the process is restarted.

2.

We generate random values of the quadratic parameters;
\mathrm{V}(v_{1},v_{2},\theta)
is now completely defined, and we proceed repeatedly (
e.g.
30 times) to minimize it numerically, with different random starting values of
\{v_{1},v_{2},\theta\}
.

3.

The resulting list of minima is filtered, eliminating repeated minima (and potentials without spontaneous symmetry breaking). Specifically we compute the real vectors
\vec{B}=(\langle\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle^{\dagger}\langle\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle,\langle\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle^{\dagger}\langle\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle,\frac{1}{2}\langle\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle^{\dagger}\langle\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle^{\dagger}\langle\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle,\frac{i}{2}\langle\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle^{\dagger}\langle\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle-\frac{i}{2}\langle\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle^{\dagger}\langle\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle)
for two candidate minima,
\vec{B}_{\rm Min\,1}
,
\vec{B}_{\rm Min\,2}
, and
\vec{\Delta}=\vec{B}_{\rm Min\,1}-\vec{B}_{\rm Min\,2}
: if
\vec{\Delta}\cdot\vec{\Delta}<
(Tolerance)
2
, both candidate minima are “the same”, and one is eliminated. We set, for example, Tolerance
=10^{-5}
. Repeating this comparison, we are finally left with either one or two minima.

4.

In case the potential has two minima, we choose the deepest one as the vacuum.

5.

We now proceed with parameter redefinitions to obtain appropriate electroweak symmetry breaking vevs. For a vacuum with
\langle\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle=\frac{v_{1,0}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
,
\langle\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle=\frac{v_{2,0}e^{i\theta_{0}}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, all quadratic parameters are rescaled as

\mu^{2}_{a},\mu^{2}_{ab}\ \mapsto\ \frac{v_{\rm EW}^{2}}{v_{0}^{2}}\,\mu^{2}_{a},\mu^{2}_{ab}\,,\qquad v_{0}^{2}\equiv v_{1,0}^{2}+v_{2,0}^{2}\,.

(16)

The vacuum of the resulting potential is now
\langle\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle=\frac{v_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
,
\langle\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\rangle=\frac{v_{2}e^{i\theta_{0}}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, with
v_{1}=v_{\rm EW}\frac{v_{1,0}}{v_{0}}
and
v_{2}=v_{\rm EW}\frac{v_{2,0}}{v_{0}}
: then
v=v_{\rm EW}
by construction.

6.

We compute the mass matrices and their eigenvalues. As a check, there should be one null eigenvalue — within numerical precision — in each
{\rm M_{\pm}^{2}}
and
{\rm M_{0}^{2}}
corresponding to the would-be Goldstone bosons, and the remaining eigenvalues should be positive. With
m_{0}^{2}
the smallest non-vanishing eigenvalue of
{\rm M_{0}^{2}}
, we now rescale all parameters

\mu^{2}_{a},\mu^{2}_{ab},\lambda_{x}\ \mapsto\ \frac{m_{h}^{2}}{m_{0}^{2}}\,\mu^{2}_{a},\mu^{2}_{ab},\lambda_{x}\,,

(17)

with
m_{h}=125.1
GeV the Higgs mass. The vacuum is unchanged and now the lightest neutral scalar has mass
m_{h}
(as already mentioned, this is in fact just a choice, but very appropriate in order to address the question of potentially heavy new scalars).

At this stage, we have a potential which is bounded from below, gives electroweak spontaneous symmetry breaking as desired, and has the lightest neutral scalar with the Higgs mass.

7.

We now check that perturbative unitarity conditions, as discussed in
11
, are fulfilled; if not, that potential is rejected and the process is restarted.

8.

As additional checks, (i) two different parametrizations for the minimization of
\mathrm{V}(v_{1},v_{2},\theta)
are separately used, in terms of
\{v_{1},v_{2},\theta\}
and in terms of
\{v,\frac{v_{2}}{v_{1}},\theta\}
, and, also separately, (ii) a random unitary rotation of the
\{\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\}
basis is performed before minimization (with all parameters in the potential accordingly transformed). The results of all three analyses are fully consistent.

IV
Discussion

The central result to analyze, as illustrated with the numerical exploration of the previous section, is the fact that the masses of all the new scalars are necessarily bounded — when perturbativity considerations constrain the quartic parameters — if the scalar potential has 2 local minima. Starting with the chosen vacuum, given by
\{v_{1},v_{2}e^{i\theta}\}
, the stationarity conditions in
eqs.

5
,
6
and
7
already fix 3 of the 4 quadratic parameters. The remaining freedom in the quadratic parameters, encoded in the combination
\mu^{2}_{1}+\mu^{2}_{2}
or, equivalently,
\text{Im}\left(\mu^{2}_{12}\right)
, could allow for a regime with masses of the new scalars much larger than
v_{\rm EW}
. If there is a second minimum given by
\{v_{1}^{\prime},v_{2}^{\prime}e^{i\theta^{\prime}}\}
, the stationarity conditions are also satisfied with
v_{1}\mapsto v_{1}^{\prime}
,
v_{2}\mapsto v_{2}^{\prime}
,
\theta\mapsto\theta^{\prime}
. Focusing on
eq.

7
, we thus have

\displaystyle-\text{Im}\left(\mu^{2}_{12}e^{i\theta}\right)v_{1}v_{2}-\text{Im}\left(\lambda_{5}e^{i2\theta}\right)v_{1}^{2}v_{2}^{2}-\text{Im}\left((\lambda_{6}v_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{7}v_{2}^{2})e^{i\theta}\right)\frac{v_{1}v_{2}}{2}=0\,,

(20)

\displaystyle-\text{Im}\left(\mu^{2}_{12}e^{i\theta^{\prime}}\right)v_{1}^{\prime}v_{2}^{\prime}-\text{Im}\left(\lambda_{5}e^{i2\theta^{\prime}}\right)v_{1}^{\prime\,2}v_{2}^{\prime\,2}-\text{Im}\left((\lambda_{6}v_{1}^{\prime\,2}+\lambda_{7}v_{2}^{\prime\,2})e^{i\theta^{\prime}}\right)\frac{v_{1}^{\prime}v_{2}^{\prime}}{2}=0\,.

We assume
v_{1}v_{2}\neq 0
,
v_{1}^{\prime}v_{2}^{\prime}\neq 0
, and solve for
\text{Re}\left(\mu^{2}_{12}\right)
and
\text{Im}\left(\mu^{2}_{12}\right)
:

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left(\mu^{2}_{12}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sin(\theta^{\prime}-\theta)}\left([E]\cos\theta^{\prime}-[E^{\prime}]\cos\theta\right)\,,

(21)

\displaystyle\text{Im}\left(\mu^{2}_{12}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sin(\theta^{\prime}-\theta)}\left([E^{\prime}]\sin\theta-[E]\sin\theta^{\prime}\right)\,,

(22)

where

\displaystyle[E]

\displaystyle=\text{Im}\left(\lambda_{5}e^{i2\theta}\right)v_{1}v_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\text{Im}\left((\lambda_{6}v_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{7}v_{2}^{2})e^{i\theta}\right)\,,

(23)

\displaystyle[E^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=\text{Im}\left(\lambda_{5}e^{i2\theta^{\prime}}\right)v_{1}^{\prime}v_{2}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\text{Im}\left((\lambda_{6}v_{1}^{\prime\,2}+\lambda_{7}v_{2}^{\prime\,2})e^{i\theta^{\prime}}\right)\,.

(24)

It is then clear that now
all
quadratic parameters are bounded by perturbativity requirements on the
\lambda
’s, and thus the whole spectrum is bounded, as
fig.

2
illustrates transparently.

One caveat worth commenting on is the following: in
eqs.

5
,
6
and
7
, the quadratic parameters appear multiplied by vevs and thus strong hierarchies in these vevs,
v_{1}\gg v_{2}
or
v_{1}\ll v_{2}
, might apparently invalidate the argument leading to boundedness of all quadratic parameters. This is, nevertheless, not the case: this potential concerns is bypassed by the fact that, working in a generic basis
\{\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\}
, one can always rotate to a different basis where such hierarchies are absent. Then, with quadratic parameters bounded in such a basis, no change of basis can make them arbitrarily large. The assumption
v_{1}v_{2}\neq 0
and
v_{1}^{\prime}v_{2}^{\prime}\neq 0
after
eq.

20
is justified on these same grounds.

The second point of interest concerns the result in
fig.

4
, obtained in the basis where the hermitian matrix of quadratic parameters is diagonal,
eq.

18
. Notice, incidentally, that spontaneous symmetry breaking requires that either
\mu^{2}_{\rm d1}
, or
\mu^{2}_{\rm d2}
, or both
\mu^{2}_{\rm d1}
and
\mu^{2}_{\rm d2}
are negative. If both are negative, with
\mu^{2}_{1}+\mu^{2}_{2}<0
in
eqs.

13
and
15
, then the whole spectrum is necessarily bounded (this might happen for a potential with either one or with two minima). Considering that specific basis leads to a conundrum: the number of quadratic parameters is reduced to 2, while there are 3 stationarity conditions. How can we have mass spectra that are not bounded, since it might appear that all quadratic parameters can be expressed as quartic parameters times vevs? — the spectrum of masses does not depend, of course, on the basis. The point is subtle: in that special basis, the stationarity conditions read

\displaystyle\partial_{v_{1}}\mathrm{V}=

\displaystyle\ \mu^{2}_{\rm d1}v_{1}+\lambda_{1}v_{1}^{3}+\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})v_{1}v_{2}^{2}

(25)

\displaystyle+\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{5}e^{i2\theta}\right)v_{1}v_{2}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{6}e^{i\theta}\right)v_{1}^{2}v_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{7}e^{i\theta}\right)v_{2}^{3}=0\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{v_{2}}\mathrm{V}=

\displaystyle\ \mu^{2}_{\rm d2}v_{2}+\lambda_{2}v_{2}^{3}+\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})v_{1}^{2}v_{2}

(26)

\displaystyle+\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{5}e^{i2\theta}\right)v_{1}^{2}v_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{6}e^{i\theta}\right)v_{1}^{3}+\frac{3}{2}\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{7}e^{i\theta}\right)v_{1}v_{2}^{2}=0\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\mathrm{V}=

\displaystyle-\text{Im}\left(\lambda_{5}e^{i2\theta}\right)v_{1}^{2}v_{2}^{2}-\text{Im}\left((\lambda_{6}v_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{7}v_{2}^{2})e^{i\theta}\right)\frac{v_{1}v_{2}}{2}=0\,.

(27)

The first aspect to notice is that the last equation,
\partial_{\theta}\mathrm{V}=0
, does not involve
\mu^{2}_{\rm d1}
and
\mu^{2}_{\rm d2}
. There are just 2 equations for the 2 quadratic parameters and one can straightforwardly obtain

\mu^{2}_{\rm d1}+\mu^{2}_{\rm d2}=-\lambda_{1}v_{1}^{2}-\lambda_{2}v_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}+2\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{5}e^{i2\theta}\right)\right)v^{2}\\
-\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{6}e^{i\theta}\right)\frac{v_{1}}{2v_{2}}(3v_{2}^{2}+v_{1}^{2})-\text{Re}\left(\lambda_{7}e^{i\theta}\right)\frac{v_{2}}{2v_{1}}(3v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2})\,.

(28)

It is then clear that
\mu^{2}_{\rm d1}+\mu^{2}_{\rm d2}\gg v_{\rm EW}^{2}
is still feasible provided there is a strong hierarchy in the vevs. This is exactly the behaviour that
fig.

4
shows for
M_{\mathrm{H}^{\pm}}>1
TeV. One can then interpret the distinction between one and two minima through
v_{2}/v_{1}
for
M_{\mathrm{H}^{\pm}}<1
TeV as a continuation of the just-one-minimum case with milder vev hierarchies and
\mu^{2}_{\rm d1}+\mu^{2}_{\rm d2}\sim v_{\rm EW}^{2}
.
Some final comments are in order. Typical usage of 2HDMs involves setting
v=v_{\rm EW}
, choosing parameters such as
\tan\beta=v_{2}/v_{1}
, masses and mixings, and then enforcing through the stationarity conditions and the mass matrices what the corresponding potential should be. The results of this work might be helpful to dismiss or not the concerns that the existence of a second minimum of the potential can trigger, such as, for example, if the considered vacuum is the lowest one, or the cosmological consequences of having domains with different electroweak symmetry breaking properties. If one is considering a 2HDM with new scalars heavier than 1 TeV, there is no concern in that respect. For new scalars lighter than 1 TeV, if
v_{2}/v_{1}
(in the basis of diagonal quadratic terms) is not within the range
[10^{-2};10^{2}]
, there is again no concern in that respect, the potential has no additional vacuum. By contrast, for
v_{2}/v_{1}\in[10^{-2};10^{2}]
the scalar potential can have a second minimum, and it might not be safely ignored. This regime with light scalars might give signals within the discovery reach of the LHC, and could be of immediate interest. To address such phenomenological implications, the Yukawa couplings to fermions cannot be ignored: they are thus beyond the scope of this work, which focuses on the scalar sector on its own.

Conclusions

We have shown how a simple, global property of the scalar potential in general 2HDMs, namely the existence of two local minima, can have a clear and somehow unexpected consequence: the whole scalar spectrum is bounded, with all masses below 1 TeV, owing to perturbativity constraints. In addition, we have explored the possibility of distinguishing, to some extent, the presence of two minima through the ratio of vevs in a specific basis.
