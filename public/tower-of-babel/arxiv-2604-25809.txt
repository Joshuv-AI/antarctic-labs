Instruction-Evidence Contrastive Dual-Stream Decoding for Grounded Vision-Language Reasoning
Yashwant Pravinrao Bangde, Debaditya Roy

Abstract
Vision–Language Models (VLMs) exhibit strong performance in instruction following and open-ended vision-language reasoning, yet they frequently generate fluent outputs that are weakly grounded in visual evidence. Prior works have shown that instruction prompting further worsens this issue by amplifying language priors, especially when the visual signal is uncertain or ambiguous. To address this challenge, we propose a decoding framework that explicitly balances linguistic informativeness and visual faithfulness during generation. Our method, Instruction-Evidence Contrastive Dual-Stream Decoding (IECD 2 ), maintains two parallel probability distribution of tokens at each decoding step: an instruction-driven stream that promotes expressive and informative responses, and an evidence-driven stream that enforces strict grounding in the image. These two streams are adaptively fused using a symmetric KL-based contrastive gate, which suppresses tokens favored by language priors but unsupported by visual evidence, while preserving them when both distributions agree. We evaluate IECD 2 on multiple datasets spanning various generative vision-language reasoning tasks such as captioning and visual question answering on multiple datasets such as, POPE, MME, VQAv2, AMBER, MS-COCO, and LlaVA-Bench. IECD 2 demonstrates consistent improvements in task accuracy and reasoning performance, alongside a substantial reduction in hallucination across all evaluation metrics compared to state-of-the-art decoding approaches.

Introduction

Figure 1:
Overview of the Instruction–Evidence Contrastive Dual-Stream (IECD
2
) framework

To enable unified multimodal interpretation and generation, Vision-Language Models (VLMs) integrate large language models (LLMs) with pretrained visual encoders. Despite their impressive capabilities, VLMs remain prone to hallucination, producing fluent and plausible descriptions or answers that are not supported by the image. Prior work attributes this behavior to dataset bias and the dominance of language priors when visual evidence is weak or ambiguous (
27
;
29
;
28
). To tackle this, Contrastive Decoding (CD) (
10
) offers a training-free alternative by explicitly contrasting token distributions under different contexts, suppressing tokens driven by spurious or biased language priors while retaining those supported by reliable visual evidence. In this work, building on the notion of
instruction disturbance
introduced in Instruction Contrastive Decoding (ICD) (
25
), which appends role-based prefixes to modulate multimodal alignment uncertainty in VLMs, we further examine how variations in instruction prompt influence the internal token distributions and contribute to visually inconsistent generation.

Empirically, instruction-aligned prompts induce a distribution that favors informativeness and coverage, whereas prompts that explicitly constrain the model to rely only on visible evidence strongly suppress hallucination but often lead to overly conservative and less descriptive outputs (
16
). This reveals a fundamental trade-off between linguistic richness and visual faithfulness: prompt formulations do not merely control surface style, but actively shift the probability mass toward either language-prior-dominated or evidence-grounded decoding regimes.

Motivated by this observation, we propose that instruction-following and evidence-grounding prompts produce complementary but contradictory token distributions. Rather than committing to either mode, we preserve both distributions and reconcile them dynamically during generation. As illustrated in Fig.
1
, this leads to our
Instruction–Evidence Contrastive Dual-Stream Decoding
(IECD
2
), which maintains parallel instruction and evidence streams—capturing language priors and strict visual grounding, respectively—and adaptively fuses them at each decoding step through a divergence-aware contrastive gate. By explicitly contrasting linguistic priors against visual grounding, IECD
2
suppresses hallucination when the two disagree, while retaining fluency and descriptive power when they align. Our contributions are as follows:

1.

We propose IECD
2
, a dual-stream decoding framework that explicitly maintains parallel instruction-driven and evidence-grounded token distributions throughout generation, enabling decoding to jointly model linguistic priors and visual grounding.

2.

We introduce a divergence-based contrastive gating mechanism that resolves conflicts between instruction and evidence streams. By measuring bidirectional disagreement via symmetric KL divergence, the gate selectively suppresses hallucination-prone tokens while preserving informative language content.

3.

We conduct extensive experiments on vision-language reasoning tasks such as captioning and VQA and IECD
2
consistently reduces hallucination and improves grounding accuracy while maintaining descriptive coverage.

Related Work

Hallucination in VLMs refers to the generation of fluent but visually unsupported content, including nonexistent objects, incorrect attributes, or fabricated relations (
15
;
1
). Earlier work in NLP characterized hallucination as a factual inconsistency with source inputs (
18
), while in vision-language settings, object hallucination has emerged as a central concern (
19
). Hallucinations in VLMs are categorized into three types:
category
,
attribute
, and
relation
(
1
). However, most existing methods are designed to improve overall visual grounding and factual consistency by mitigating multiple hallucination types simultaneously.

Category Hallucination.

Category-level hallucination arises when a model predicts the presence of an object class that is not supported by the image, often driven by strong language priors and dataset frequency bias. Inference-time correction methods aim to mitigate this effect without retraining. Post-hoc verification frameworks such as Woodpecker (
26
) revise generated outputs using auxiliary detectors or external consistency checks; however, such pipelines depend on additional models and handcrafted decision rules, introducing error propagation and limiting scalability to open-ended generation. Contrastive decoding (
10
) offers a training-free alternative by explicitly contrasting token distributions induced by
clean
and
disturbed
inputs, where the disturbance refers to perturbations that weaken visual grounding (e.g., corrupted images or misleading instructions). Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) (
9
) suppresses category hallucination by contrasting predictions from original and visually perturbed images, thereby down-weighting object tokens that remain confident under visual corruption and are thus likely language-prior driven, while Instruction Contrastive Decoding (ICD) (
25
) exploits disturbance in the instruction space to expose category-level hallucinations amplified by prompt-induced language bias. Multi-Frequency Contrastive Decoding (MFCD) (
14
) introduces frequency-domain disturbances by selectively removing high- and low-frequency components from images to isolate hallucination-prone distributions. Object-aligned visual contrastive decoding (
2
) masks salient object regions to produce semantically meaningful counterfactual inputs that enhance visual grounding and suppress language-driven object predictions.

Attribute Hallucination.

Attribute hallucination occurs when a model assigns incorrect properties (e.g., color, size, state) to visually present objects. VACoDe (
8
) addresses this by selecting among multiple image augmentations to maximize contrast, encouraging the model to discount attribute predictions that are unstable under visual variation. Re-Balancing Contrastive Decoding (RBD) (
12
) further targets attribute errors by correcting imbalanced cross-modal attention, reweighting textual and visual contributions to prevent over-dominance of language priors in attribute realization. From an internal representation perspective, IFCD (
22
) contrasts disturbed hidden states to suppress spurious attribute logits, while VaLiD (
23
) introduces uncertainty-aware layer-wise fusion to counteract attribute distortions caused by noisy visual encoding.

Relation Hallucination.

Relation hallucination refers to incorrect spatial or semantic relationships between objects (e.g., left-right, holding, riding), which often emerge from long-range dependency errors and misaligned multimodal reasoning. Token-level and adaptive strategies such as CATCH (
7
) dynamically gate contrastive strength at each decoding step, allowing the model to suppress relation tokens when visual support is weak. Octopus (
21
) further observes that hallucination types vary across decoding stages and proposes a multi-strategy contrastive framework that adaptively switches among disturbance sources to correct relation-level inconsistencies. Extending contrastive decoding to multi-source reasoning, Multi-Path Information Contrastive Decoding (MPI-CD) (
20
) introduces a tri-branch framework that contrasts predictions from original, salient-region, and non-salient-region inputs to enhance multimodal reasoning and reduce hallucinations arising from incorrect relational associations.

3
Methodology

In this section, we introduce Instruction-Evidence Contrastive Dual-Stream Decoding (IECD
2
), a training-free decoding framework for mitigating object hallucinations in VLMs. IECD
2
maintains two token-probability streams during generation: an instruction-guided stream and a visually grounded evidence stream. These streams are fused using a divergence-aware gating mechanism that suppresses instruction-only predictions when they diverge from visual evidence to reduce overdependence on language prior and improve grounded generation. Section
3.1
formalizes the dual-stream formulation, while Section
3.2
presents the divergence-aware fusion mechanism.

3.1
Dual-Stream Token Distributions

Prior work has shown that hallucinations in VLMs are often amplified by instructional prompting, which encourages models to prioritize response fluency and task completion over strict visual grounding.
To explicitly separate these two behavioral tendencies, IECD
2
maintains
two parallel decoding streams
throughout generation, both instantiated from the same underlying VLM
but conditioned via different prompt contexts, shown in Figure
1
.
Formally, at each decoding step
t
given history
y_{<t}
,
we construct two token probability distributions, Instruction stream and Evidence stream, over the shared vocabulary space.

Instruction stream

models the behavior of a standard instruction-following VLM.
This stream encourages descriptive, coherent and stylistically appropriate responses:

p_{t}^{(I)}(v)=p_{\theta_{I}}\left(v\mid y_{<t},S\right),

(1)

where
S
denotes the natural-language instruction prompt.
Since typical instructions tend to reward fluency, helpfulness,
and semantic plausibility,
this branch implicitly preserves linguistic prior knowledge.
However, the same property also means that

p_{t}^{(I)}
may assign high probability to visually unsupported objects,
thereby acting as a potential source of hallucination.

Evidence stream.

To counterbalance the instruction stream, we introduce a second branch that
explicitly constrains generation to rely on visual evidence:

p_{t}^{(E)}(v)=p_{\theta_{E}}\left(v\mid y_{<t},x_{\text{evidence}}\right),

(2)

where
x_{\text{evidence}}
is a prompt variant instructing the model
to answer strictly based on observable image content.
Unlike the instruction stream, this branch suppresses unsupported inference
by penalizing uncertain or speculative continuations.
As a consequence,

p_{t}^{(E)}
tends to be conservative, meaning that it assigns high probability only to tokens that are strongly supported by visible evidence and avoids committing to uncertain or weakly grounded entities, but visually faithful.
Notably, no retraining or parameter modification is required and the two token distributions arise due to different conditioning prompts as shown in Figure
1
.
Consequently, IECD
2
is fully model-agnostic and applicable to any instruction-tuned VLMs.

, with their image caption. IECD
2
retains the visual grounding of evidence stream. Image from AMBER (
24
) dataset.

3.2
Contrastive Gating via Stream Divergence

Robust multimodal generation requires both linguistic fluency and visual grounding
10
. IECD
2
achieves this by combining the instruction and evidence token distributions through a gated geometric fusion mechanism building on geometric-mean fusion in
25
.

Formally, the decoding distribution at step
t
is defined as

p_{t}^{(\mathrm{IECD^{2}})}(v)\propto\left(p_{t}^{(I)}(v)\right)^{g_{t}}\left(p_{t}^{(E)}(v)\right)^{1-g_{t}},

(3)

where
g_{t}\in[0,1]
controls the relative contribution of the instruction and evidence streams. In log-space,

\log p_{t}^{(\mathrm{IECD^{2}})}(v)=g_{t}\log p_{t}^{(I)}(v)+(1-g_{t})\log p_{t}^{(E)}(v)-\log Z_{t}.

(4)

To adaptively determine
g_{t}
, we measure the disagreement between the two streams using the symmetric KL divergence (
6
):

D_{t}=\mathrm{KL}\!\left(p_{t}^{(I)}\,\middle\|\,p_{t}^{(E)}\right)+\mathrm{KL}\!\left(p_{t}^{(E)}\,\middle\|\,p_{t}^{(I)}\right).

(5)

Now, the divergence is assigned to a gating coefficient via

g_{t}=\frac{\exp(\eta D_{t})}{1+\exp(\eta D_{t})},

(6)

where
\eta<0
is a contrast-temperature parameter controlling the sensitivity of the gate.

Behavior.

The gating mechanism exhibits the following behavior:

•

Small divergence (
D_{t}\approx 0
):

The two streams agree closely, indicating that the instruction-driven continuation is already consistent with visible content.
In this case,
g_{t}
remains moderate, allowing the instruction stream to retain stronger influence, thereby preserving linguistic fluency and descriptive richness.

•

Large divergence (
D_{t}\gg 0
):

The instruction stream may diverge from the evidence stream when the model relies excessively on language priors, such as predicting commonly expected objects that are not actually present in the image. In such situations,
g_{t}
decreases sharply toward 0, reducing the contribution of the instruction stream and encouraging the decoder to rely more on visually grounded evidence for token generation.

Evidence Stream alone is not fully reliable because, while it is strictly grounded in visible cues and avoids hallucination, it is overly conservative and tends to under-emphasize higher-level semantic concepts such as actions and interactions. As illustrated in the Fig.
2
, it assigns low probability to activities like playing or running despite their plausibility, since it focuses on local visual attributes rather than integrating broader contextual and relational information. Consequently, its predictions can be incomplete or overly literal, motivating the need to combine it with the instruction stream in IECD
2
to achieve both visual accuracy and semantic completeness.

Unlike static weighting strategies (
10
;
9
;
25
), contrastive gating enables IECD
2
to operate adaptively.
IECD
2
preserves descriptive expressiveness when the model is visually consistent while actively constraining object speculation only when hallucination risk is detected.

4
Experiments

4.1
Experimental Settings

Benchmarks.

We evaluate IECD
2
across five widely-used VLM datasets.
POPE
(
11
) benchmark isolates object hallucination by asking binary object-existence queries under adversarial, popular, and random sampling conditions.
MME
(
4
) benchmark measures multimodal perception and cognition, including existence recognition, attribute reasoning, counting, OCR, landmark identification, translation and commonsense inference. General visual question answering ability is assessed on the
VQAv2
(
5
) validation split, which consists of open-ended answer type questions. Hallucination in caption generation is evaluated on Caption Hallucination Assessment with Image Relevance (CHAIR) (
19
) on the
MSCOCO
validation set (
13
). Following prior work, we randomly sample 500 images from the validation split and generate captions for evaluation and we perform this experiment 5 times and report the average performance. Finally, we use the
AMBER
(
24
) benchmark to evaluate grounding robustness on a generative captioning task and a discriminative verification task. Together, these datasets jointly assess hallucination suppression, visual grounding reliability, and task-level vision-language reasoning capability.
LLaVA-Bench
(
17
) comprises of 24 images accompanied by 60 queries that encompass a variety of contexts, including indoor and outdoor scenes, memes, paintings, and sketches. This dataset is designed to evaluate the ability of VLMs to address more complex tasks and their adaptability to novel domains. We perform a qualitative analysis of this dataset to illustrate the effectiveness of our proposed IECD
2
.

Evaluation Metrics.

For POPE, we report standard binary-classification metrics: Accuracy and F1 score. For MME, we follow the official leaderboard protocol and report normalized aggregate score (Accuracy+). For AMBER, we evaluate the generative task using CHAIR, together with Coverage, Hal, and Cog to quantify grounding reliability, and compute Accuracy and F1 for the discriminative verification task. For VQAv2, we report the standard VQA accuracy, while hallucination robustness is further assessed via CHAIR metrics, CHAIR
i
and CHAIR
s
, computed on MS-COCO val2014 captions. On POPE and MME, we measure grounding reliability under binary verification and perception tasks. On VQAv2, MS-COCO captioning, and AMBER, instruction-aligned VLMs are known to be more prone to visually unsupported generation (
16
).

VLMs used.

We evaluate IECD
2
on two representative vision–language models, LLaVA-1.5-7B (
17
) and InstructBLIP (
3
) (Vicuna-7B backbone) that are also used by other decoding approaches. These VLMs differ in visual encoders, multimodal alignment, and instruction-tuning strategies, providing a diverse testbed for evaluating hallucination mitigation. For all VLMs across datasets, IECD
2
is applied only at decoding time without parameter updates or retraining, enabling a fair and model-agnostic evaluation.

Implementation Details.

For each model, we compare its default decoding with IECD
2
using instruction and evidence prompts from Section
3
, setting instruction temperature
T_{I}=1.0
, evidence temperature
T_{E}=0.9
, and
\eta=-3.0
, with symmetric KL divergence for contrastive gating. The instruction and evidence temperatures control the sharpness of the token distributions generated by each stream. A lower temperature produces a sharper distribution, assigning higher probability to a few top-ranked tokens and making the stream more confident in its predictions. In contrast, a higher temperature produces a flatter distribution, distributing probability more evenly across multiple tokens and reducing prediction confidence. Decoding lengths are limited to 3–16 tokens for VQA and 20–64 tokens for captioning, and all experiments are conducted on an NVIDIA L40 48GB GPU.

Ablation Study on the MME Benchmark

We analyze the behavior of IECD
2
on a benchmark that consists of diverse perception and cognition tasks covering multiple types of hallucinations, to study how the instruction and evidence streams contribute to visual understanding. As shown in Table
3
, on the perception tasks, IECD
2
achieves strong performance on core object-level categories, including Existence (190.0 for both LLaVA-1.5 and InstructBLIP), Counting (165.0 and 55.0), and Color recognition (160.0 and 115.0). also shows stable behavior, achieving 112.86 and 77.50 on commonsense and code reasoning for LLaVA-1.5, and 104.29 and 65.00 for InstructBLIP, respectively, further demonstrating that the interaction between instruction and evidence is essential for robust performance on hallucination-sensitive evaluation settings.

We evaluate IECD
2
on three representative discriminative benchmarks, namely POPE (MSCOCO), MME, and AMBER Discriminative Task, which collectively assess visual grounding reliability and object-attribute reasoning under diverse bias conditions.

POPE (MSCOCO).

POPE explicitly probes object-level hallucination under Random, Popular, and Adversarial sampling regimes. As shown in Table
5
, IECD
2
yields clear improvements over standard decoding for both LLaVA-1.5 and InstructBLIP. Averaged across the three settings, IECD
2
improves Accuracy by up to
4.9%
and
5.1%
, with corresponding F1 gains of
5.0%
and
4.8%
, respectively. Moreover, while strong baselines such as ICD, VCD, VaLiD, IFCD, CATCH, and Octopus exhibit notable performance drops when moving from Random to Adversarial splits, IECD
2
shows a smaller degradation margin (approximately
4-6%
), indicating improved robustness against frequency-induced bias and adversarial object confusions.

AMBER Discriminative Task.

The AMBER Discriminative Task measures discriminative visual grounding. As reported in Table
5
, IECD
2
consistently improves grounding accuracy across different VLMs. On LLaVA-1.5, IECD
2
achieves an Accuracy/F1 of 77.8/82.6, corresponding to relative gains of +12.4% and +14.7% over direct decoding (69.2/72.0). Similarly, on InstructBLIP, IECD
2
attains 76.6/82.1, improving upon direct decoding (68.2/74.6) by +12.3% in Accuracy and +10.1% in F1. These results indicate that contrastive dual-stream decoding substantially enhances the consistency of visual-linguistic decisions, reducing mismatches between predicted answers and image evidence.

VQAv2.

VQAv2 measures general visual question answering capability under diverse linguistic and visual conditions. As shown in Table
6

Inference Time.

IECD
2
exhibits distinct runtime behavior across tasks and caching settings. Without KV cache, it requires 2.378 seconds per query for generative tasks and 0.129 seconds for discriminative tasks. The average number of generated tokens per query is 38.31. With KV cache enabled, the generative inference time decreases substantially to 1.144 seconds, whereas the discriminative setting shows a moderate increase to 0.167 seconds. At the token level, KV caching reduces the per-token cost for generative queries (from 0.066s to 0.032s), while introducing a slight overhead for discriminative queries (from 0.065s to 0.080s). This trend reflects the effectiveness of caching in autoregressive generation, where repeated reuse of past key-value states improves efficiency, in contrast to discriminative settings where shorter sequences limit reuse and introduce cache management overhead. 5
Conclusion

We presented Instruction–Evidence Contrastive Dual-Stream Decoding (IECD
2
), a decoding-time framework that improves the reliability of vision–language model generation by explicitly contrasting instruction-driven and evidence-grounded token distributions. By maintaining two parallel probability streams and fusing them through a symmetric KL–based adaptive gate, IECD
2
dynamically suppresses visually unsupported continuations while preserving linguistic fluency when the two distributions are consistent. This formulation enables the model to balance language priors and visual grounding at each decoding step, rather than committing to a single prompting regime. Extensive experiments across both generative and discriminative vision–language reasoning tasks show that IECD
2
consistently reduces hallucination and improves grounding accuracy, demonstrating that contrastive, divergence-aware fusion at inference time is an effective and general mechanism for enhancing the robustness of modern VLM decoding.
