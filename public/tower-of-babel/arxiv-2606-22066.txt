1
Introduction

A positive function
f
on
(0,\infty)
is hyperbolically completely monotone (HCM) if for every
u>0
, the product

v\longmapsto f(uv)f(u/v)

(4)

is completely monotone as a function of the hyperbolic variable

w=v+v^{-1}.

(5)

Namely, for each fixed
u>0
, the resulting function
g_{u}
of
w
has derivatives of all orders on
(2,\infty)
and

(-1)^{n}g_{u}^{(n)}(w)\geq 0\qquad(n\geq 0,\ w>2).

(6)

The class was developed systematically by Bondesson in connection with Thorin’s generalized gamma convolutions (GGCs) and infinite divisibility; see
Thorin 1977
;
Bondesson 1981
;
Bondesson 1992
. We use only the density-side definition above, but the motivation is the standard hierarchy: an HCM density yields a GGC law, and GGC laws are infinitely divisible
Bondesson 1992
. Gamma densities are elementary HCM examples. Indeed, if

\gamma_{a,b}(x)=\frac{b^{a}}{\Gamma(a)}x^{a-1}e^{-bx},\qquad a,b>0,

(7)

then

\gamma_{a,b}(uv)\gamma_{a,b}(u/v)=C_{u}e^{-bu(v+v^{-1})},

(8)

which is completely monotone in
w=v+v^{-1}
.

The noncentral chi-square density is a natural deformation of this central gamma case. For degrees of freedom
\mu>0
and noncentrality
\lambda>0
, the density is

\chi_{\mu,\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-(x+\lambda)/2}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\mu/4-1/2}I_{\mu/2-1}(\sqrt{\lambda x}),\qquad x>0,

(9)

with the usual limiting interpretation at
\lambda=0
, where the central density is
\gamma_{\mu/2,1/2}
. The same family is tied to the generalized Marcum-
Q
function and to standard applications of the noncentral chi-square distribution; see, for example,
Baricz et al. 2021
. Equivalently, it is a Poisson mixture of central chi-square densities:

\chi_{\mu,\lambda}(x)=e^{-\lambda/2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda/2)^{m}}{m!}\,\chi_{\mu+2m,0}(x).

(10)

Since each central chi-square density is a gamma density and hence HCM, this representation gives a natural test case for whether fixed-rate Poisson mixing over the gamma shape preserves HCM.

This test case is also a concrete open range problem in the recent literature on infinitely divisible modified Bessel distributions. The noncentral chi-square law is already known to be infinitely divisible for all positive degrees of freedom by Ismail and Kelker
(
Ismail and Kelker 1979
, Theorem 1.6)
; it also belongs to Bondesson’s larger class of generalized convolutions of mixtures of exponential distributions
(
Bondesson 1981
, p. 43)
, as recalled in
(
Baricz et al. 2026
, Section 4.2)
. Because HCM densities give GGC laws, and because neighboring modified-Bessel laws such as the
K
-distribution fall on the positive side of the HCM/GGC theory
(
Baricz et al. 2026
, Section 2.5)
, Baricz, Prabhu K, Singh and Vijesh asked for the optimal range of parameters for which the noncentral chi-square density is HCM. Their Theorem 25 establishes the first two necessary HCM signs: for
\mu>1
, the hyperbolic product is decreasing when
\lambda\leq\mu
, and convex when
2\lambda\leq\mu
. It therefore leaves open the full alternating derivative chain required for HCM
(
Baricz et al. 2026
, Section 4.2 and Theorem 25)
. Product-closure results such as
Bondesson 2015
explain why such positive examples are natural in the broader GGC/HCM closure story.

The main point of this paper is that the HCM range collapses to the central gamma line. Any positive noncentrality destroys HCM. More generally, every positive
{}_{0}F_{1}
-gamma deformation of a gamma density fails to be HCM. This is an HCM statement only; it makes no claim about the GGC range.

The mechanism is also the main conceptual contribution. The failure is not detected by inspecting the density directly. Instead, HCM imposes infinitely many alternating derivative signs on the hyperbolic product. The leading small-
u
forms of these signs are governed by complete Bell polynomials. Their signed generating function is a Bessel function in disguise, and a positive zero of
J_{a-1}
forces one of the required signs to fail.

2
Main results

We begin with the general
{}_{0}F_{1}
-gamma deformation. For
a,b>0
and
\theta\geq 0
, define

p_{a,b,\theta}(x)=\frac{b^{a}e^{-\theta}}{\Gamma(a)}x^{a-1}e^{-bx}\,{}_{0}F_{1}(;a;b\theta x),\qquad x>0.

(11)

Here

{}_{0}F_{1}(;a;z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{m}}{m!(a)_{m}},\qquad(a)_{m}=\frac{\Gamma(a+m)}{\Gamma(a)}.

(12)

The parameter
\theta
is the deformation parameter. When
\theta=0
, (
11
) is the gamma density
\gamma_{a,b}
. When
\theta>0
, (
11
) is a positive
{}_{0}F_{1}
-tilt of
\gamma_{a,b}
. The first structural observation is that (
11
) is not an artificial density.

Proposition 1

(Poisson-gamma representation)
.

For
a,b>0
and
\theta\geq 0
,
p_{a,b,\theta}
is a probability density on
(0,\infty)
, and

p_{a,b,\theta}(x)=e^{-\theta}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{m}}{m!}\,\gamma_{a+m,b}(x),

(13)

where

\gamma_{\alpha,b}(x)=\frac{b^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-bx}.

(14)

Equivalently, if
N\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\theta)
, then
p_{a,b,\theta}
is the density of a gamma law with random shape
a+N
and fixed rate
b
.

Proof.

Using the defining series of
{}_{0}F_{1}
,

p_{a,b,\theta}(x)=\frac{b^{a}e^{-\theta}}{\Gamma(a)}x^{a-1}e^{-bx}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(b\theta x)^{m}}{m!(a)_{m}}.

(15)

Since
(a)_{m}=\Gamma(a+m)/\Gamma(a)
, this becomes

p_{a,b,\theta}(x)=e^{-\theta}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{m}}{m!}\frac{b^{a+m}}{\Gamma(a+m)}x^{a+m-1}e^{-bx},

(16)

which is (
13
). Each summand is a gamma density, and the weights form a Poisson probability mass function. Hence
p_{a,b,\theta}
is a probability density.
∎

The main theorem is the following sharp classification.

Theorem 1

(Bessel-zero obstruction for
{}_{0}F_{1}
-gamma deformations)
.

Let
a,b>0
and
\theta\geq 0
. Then

p_{a,b,\theta}\in\mathrm{HCM}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\theta=0.

(17)

Equivalently, every positive
{}_{0}F_{1}
-gamma deformation of a gamma density fails to be hyperbolically completely monotone.

The proof is given in Section
5
, after the small-
u
Bell obstruction and Bessel-zero lemmas.

Corollary 1

(Noncentral chi-square HCM range)
.

Let
\mu>0
and
\lambda\geq 0
. The noncentral chi-square density
\chi_{\mu,\lambda}
is hyperbolically completely monotone if and only if

\lambda=0.

(18)

Thus the HCM range is exactly the central gamma line.

Proof.

If
\lambda=0
, then
\chi_{\mu,0}=\gamma_{\mu/2,1/2}
, which is HCM by the gamma case in Theorem
1
. Suppose now that
\lambda>0
. Put

a=\frac{\mu}{2},\qquad b=\frac{1}{2},\qquad\theta=\frac{\lambda}{2}.

(19)

Using the standard modified-Bessel representation of the noncentral chi-square density
Johnson et al. 1994
and the standard series identity for
I_{\nu}

(
NIST DLMF 2026
, Eq. 10.25.2)
,

I_{a-1}(z)=\frac{(z/2)^{a-1}}{\Gamma(a)}{}_{0}F_{1}\!\left(;a;\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right),

(20)

one obtains

\chi_{\mu,\lambda}(x)=\frac{2^{-a}e^{-\lambda/2}}{\Gamma(a)}x^{a-1}e^{-x/2}{}_{0}F_{1}\!\left(;a;\frac{\lambda x}{4}\right)=p_{a,1/2,\lambda/2}(x).

(21)

By Theorem
1
, this density is not HCM for
\lambda>0
. Together with the central case, this proves the claim.
∎

The same argument gives a more structural probabilistic consequence.

Corollary 2

(Poisson shape-mixtures of gamma densities need not be HCM)
.

The class of HCM densities is not closed under Poisson mixing over the gamma shape parameter, even when the rate is fixed. More precisely, for every
a,b,\theta>0
,

p_{a,b,\theta}(x)=e^{-\theta}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{m}}{m!}\,\gamma_{a+m,b}(x)

(22)

is a Poisson mixture of HCM gamma densities, but
p_{a,b,\theta}\notin\mathrm{HCM}
.

Proof.

Each gamma density
\gamma_{a+m,b}
is HCM. The identity is Proposition
1
. Since
\theta>0
, Theorem
1
gives
p_{a,b,\theta}\notin\mathrm{HCM}
.
∎

3
Hyperbolic complete monotonicity and the small-
u
test

We recall the two sign conventions used below.

Definition 1

(Completely monotone functions)
.

A
C^{\infty}
function
\phi
on an interval is completely monotone if

(-1)^{n}\phi^{(n)}(x)\geq 0

(23)

for every
n\geq 0
and every
x
in the interval.

Definition 2

(Hyperbolically completely monotone functions)
.

A positive function
f
on
(0,\infty)
is hyperbolically completely monotone (HCM) if for every
u>0
, the function

v\mapsto f(uv)f(u/v)

(24)

is completely monotone as a function of

w=v+v^{-1}.

(25)

Equivalently, for every
u>0
, the function

H_{u}(w)=f(uv(w))f(u/v(w)),\qquad v(w)=\frac{w+\sqrt{w^{2}-4}}{2},\qquad w>2,

(26)

satisfies

(-1)^{n}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\,\mathrm{d}w^{n}}H_{u}(w)\geq 0

(27)

for all
n\geq 0
and all
w>2
.

All complete-monotonicity assertions in the hyperbolic variable below are understood on the interval
w\in(2,\infty)
. For the density
p_{a,b,\theta}
, the power
x^{a-1}
disappears from the hyperbolic product:

(uv)^{a-1}(u/v)^{a-1}=u^{2a-2}.

(28)

Thus only the exponential factor and the
{}_{0}F_{1}
factor can affect the HCM signs.

Let

F_{a}(z)={}_{0}F_{1}(;a;z).

(29)

Since
F_{a}(0)=1
, its logarithm is analytic near
0
. Write

\log F_{a}(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}q_{m}z^{m}

(30)

in a sufficiently small disk around the origin. In particular,

q_{1}=\frac{1}{a}.

(31)

For
m\geq 0
, define the hyperbolic polynomials

P_{m}(w)=v^{m}+v^{-m},\qquad w=v+v^{-1}.

(32)

Then
P_{0}(w)=2
,
P_{1}(w)=w
, and

P_{m+1}(w)=wP_{m}(w)-P_{m-1}(w).

(33)

Hence
P_{m}
is a polynomial of degree
m
with leading coefficient
1
.

We shall use the complete exponential Bell polynomials
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, defined by

\exp\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}x_{m}\frac{z^{m}}{m!}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

(34)

with
\mathcal{B}_{0}=1
.

Proposition 2

(Leading Bell obstruction)
.

Let
a,b,\theta>0
, and let
p=p_{a,b,\theta}
. Define

H_{u}(w)=p(uv(w))p(u/v(w)),\qquad w>2.

(35)

Let

d_{1}=b\left(\frac{\theta}{a}-1\right),\qquad d_{m}=m!q_{m}(b\theta)^{m}\quad(m\geq 2),

(36)

where the
q_{m}
’s are defined by (
30
). Then, for each fixed
n\geq 1
,

\frac{1}{H_{u}(w)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\,\mathrm{d}w^{n}}H_{u}(w)=u^{n}\mathcal{B}_{n}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})+O(u^{n+1})\qquad(u\downarrow 0),

(37)

locally uniformly for
w
in compact subintervals of
(2,\infty)
.

Consequently, if
p_{a,b,\theta}
is HCM, then

(-1)^{n}\mathcal{B}_{n}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\geq 0\qquad(n\geq 1).

(38)

Proof.

Fix a compact interval
K\subset(2,\infty)
and an integer
n\geq 1
. On
K
, the functions
v(w)
and
v(w)^{-1}
are bounded. Since
F_{a}(0)=1
, choose
\rho>0
such that
F_{a}
is zero-free on
|z|<\rho
, and choose the branch of
\log F_{a}
there with value
0
at the origin. For all sufficiently small
u>0
, the two arguments
b\theta uv(w)
and
b\theta u/v(w)
lie in a compact subdisk of
|z|<\rho
, uniformly for
w\in K
. Hence the Taylor series for
\log F_{a}
, differentiated after composition with
v(w)
and
v(w)^{-1}
up to order
n
, converges uniformly on
K
. In particular, all remainders below are locally uniform in
w
.

For
w>2
, let
v=v(w)
. Up to factors independent of
w
,

H_{u}(w)=e^{-bu(v+v^{-1})}F_{a}(b\theta uv)F_{a}(b\theta u/v).

(39)

Taking logarithms and using (
30
), for sufficiently small
u
we have, uniformly on
K
,

\log H_{u}(w)=C_{u}-buw+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}q_{m}(b\theta u)^{m}P_{m}(w),

(40)

where
C_{u}
is independent of
w
.

The analyticity just noted also controls the differentiated tail. To see this explicitly, put

R_{K}=\sup_{w\in K}\max\{v(w),v(w)^{-1}\}.

(41)

For each
0\leq r\leq n
, differentiating
P_{m}(w)=v(w)^{m}+v(w)^{-m}

r
times gives

\sup_{w\in K}|P_{m}^{(r)}(w)|\leq C_{K,r}m^{r}R_{K}^{m}\qquad(m\geq 1).

(42)

Choose
u_{0}>0
so that
|b\theta|u_{0}R_{K}
lies inside the disk of convergence of
\log F_{a}
. Then, uniformly for
0<u<u_{0}
and
w\in K
, the
r
-th derivative of the tail

\sum_{m\geq r+1}q_{m}(b\theta u)^{m}P_{m}(w)

(43)

is bounded by

C_{K,r}\sum_{m\geq r+1}|q_{m}|\,m^{r}(|b\theta|u)^{m}R_{K}^{m}=O(u^{r+1}).

(44)

Thus the contribution of the terms
m\geq r+1
to the
r
-th
w
-derivative is
O(u^{r+1})
, locally uniformly on
K
.

Since
P_{m}
has degree
m
and leading coefficient
1
,

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{r}}{\,\mathrm{d}w^{r}}P_{m}(w)=0\quad(m<r),

(45)

and

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{r}}{\,\mathrm{d}w^{r}}P_{r}(w)=r!.

(46)

Thus, for
r=1
,

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}w}\log H_{u}(w)=\left(q_{1}b\theta-b\right)u+O(u^{2})=b\left(\frac{\theta}{a}-1\right)u+O(u^{2})=d_{1}u+O(u^{2}),

(47)

and for
2\leq r\leq n
,

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{r}}{\,\mathrm{d}w^{r}}\log H_{u}(w)=r!q_{r}(b\theta)^{r}u^{r}+O(u^{r+1})=d_{r}u^{r}+O(u^{r+1}).

(48)

The standard exponential derivative formula gives

\frac{H_{u}^{(n)}(w)}{H_{u}(w)}=\mathcal{B}_{n}\left((\log H_{u})^{\prime}(w),(\log H_{u})^{\prime\prime}(w),\dots,(\log H_{u})^{(n)}(w)\right).

(49)

Substituting the previous asymptotics into the weighted homogeneity of the complete Bell polynomials gives (
37
). Explicitly, every monomial in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
has weighted degree
n
, where the variable in position
r
has weight
r
; replacing
(\log H_{u})^{(r)}
by
d_{r}u^{r}+O(u^{r+1})
therefore gives the leading term
u^{n}\mathcal{B}_{n}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
and an
O(u^{n+1})
remainder, locally uniformly on
K
.

If
p
is HCM, then
(-1)^{n}H_{u}^{(n)}(w)\geq 0
for every
u>0
,
w>2
, and
n\geq 1
. Since
H_{u}(w)>0
,
(-1)^{n}H_{u}^{(n)}(w)/H_{u}(w)\geq 0
. Dividing (
37
) by
u^{n}>0
and then letting
u\downarrow 0
, with any fixed
w>2
, gives (
38
).
∎

4
The Bessel-zero obstruction

The Bell-polynomial sequence in Proposition
2
has an explicit generating function.

Lemma 1

(Signed Bell generating function)
.

With
d_{m}
as in Proposition
2
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}_{n}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=e^{-bz}F_{a}(b\theta z).

(50)

Equivalently,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\mathcal{B}_{n}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{bt}F_{a}(-b\theta t).

(51)

Moreover, equivalently as an ordinary Taylor expansion,

e^{bt}F_{a}(-b\theta t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{b^{n}}{(a)_{n}}L_{n}^{(a-1)}(\theta)t^{n},

(52)

where
L_{n}^{(a-1)}
denotes the generalized Laguerre polynomial, with normalization

L_{n}^{(a-1)}(\theta)=\frac{(a)_{n}}{n!}\,{}_{1}F_{1}(-n;a;\theta).

(53)

Consequently,

(-1)^{n}\mathcal{B}_{n}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})=\frac{n!b^{n}}{(a)_{n}}L_{n}^{(a-1)}(\theta).

(54)

Proof.

By the defining identity (
34
), as a formal power series and hence as an analytic identity near
z=0
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}_{n}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\exp\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}d_{m}\frac{z^{m}}{m!}\right).

(55)

Using the definitions of
d_{m}
,

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}d_{m}\frac{z^{m}}{m!}=-bz+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}q_{m}(b\theta z)^{m}=-bz+\log F_{a}(b\theta z).

(56)

Therefore the Taylor coefficients at the origin of the left-hand side are those of the entire function
e^{-bz}F_{a}(b\theta z)
. Since
e^{-bz}F_{a}(b\theta z)
is entire, the series in (
50
) has infinite radius of convergence and the identity holds for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Replacing
z
by
-t
gives (
51
).

For (
52
), expand directly, using the standard normalization of generalized Laguerre polynomials
(
NIST DLMF 2026
, Eq. 18.5.12)
:

e^{bt}F_{a}(-b\theta t)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(bt)^{r}}{r!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-b\theta t)^{k}}{k!(a)_{k}}.

(57)

The coefficient of
t^{n}
is

b^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-\theta)^{k}}{(n-k)!k!(a)_{k}}=\frac{b^{n}}{n!}\,{}_{1}F_{1}(-n;a;\theta).

(58)

Using

L_{n}^{(a-1)}(\theta)=\frac{(a)_{n}}{n!}\,{}_{1}F_{1}(-n;a;\theta),

(59)

we obtain (
52
).
∎

The obstruction is now a one-line consequence of a classical Bessel identity.

Lemma 2

(Positive zero of the signed generator)
.

Let
a,b,\theta>0
. Then the entire function

t\mapsto e^{bt}{}_{0}F_{1}(;a;-b\theta t)

(60)

has a positive real zero.

Proof.

The classical identity
(
NIST DLMF 2026
, Eq. 10.16.9)

{}_{0}F_{1}\!\left(;a;-\frac{x^{2}}{4}\right)=\Gamma(a)\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{1-a}J_{a-1}(x)

(61)

holds for
a>0
. The prefactor
\Gamma(a)(x/2)^{1-a}
is nonzero for
x>0
. Since
a-1>-1
is real, the Bessel function
J_{a-1}
has positive real zeros
(
NIST DLMF 2026
, Section 10.21(i))
. Let
j_{a-1,1}>0
denote its first positive zero. Set

t_{0}=\frac{j_{a-1,1}^{2}}{4b\theta}>0.

(62)

Then

{}_{0}F_{1}(;a;-b\theta t_{0})={}_{0}F_{1}\!\left(;a;-\frac{j_{a-1,1}^{2}}{4}\right)=0.

(63)

Multiplication by
e^{bt_{0}}>0
does not change the zero.
∎

5
Proof of Theorem
1

First suppose
\theta=0
. Then

p_{a,b,0}(x)=\gamma_{a,b}(x)=\frac{b^{a}}{\Gamma(a)}x^{a-1}e^{-bx}.

(64)

For
u>0
,

\gamma_{a,b}(uv)\gamma_{a,b}(u/v)=C_{u}e^{-bu(v+v^{-1})}=C_{u}e^{-buw}.

(65)

Therefore

(-1)^{n}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\,\mathrm{d}w^{n}}\left(C_{u}e^{-buw}\right)=C_{u}(bu)^{n}e^{-buw}\geq 0,

(66)

so
p_{a,b,0}\in\mathrm{HCM}
.

Now suppose
\theta>0
, and assume for contradiction that
p_{a,b,\theta}\in\mathrm{HCM}
. Proposition
2
gives

(-1)^{n}\mathcal{B}_{n}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\geq 0\qquad(n\geq 1).

(67)

Since
\mathcal{B}_{0}=1
, all Taylor coefficients of

e^{bt}F_{a}(-b\theta t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\mathcal{B}_{n}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

(68)

are nonnegative, and the constant coefficient is
1
. By Lemma
1
, this is an entire Taylor expansion, so it may be evaluated at every
t>0
. Hence this entire function must be strictly positive for every
t>0
. Indeed, at every
t>0
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\mathcal{B}_{n}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\geq 1.

(69)

This contradicts Lemma
2
, which gives a positive zero. Therefore
p_{a,b,\theta}\notin\mathrm{HCM}
whenever
\theta>0
. The proof is complete.

\square

Remark 1

(Laguerre-polynomial form of the obstruction)
.

By (
54
), the HCM necessary signs would imply

L_{n}^{(a-1)}(\theta)\geq 0\qquad(n\geq 0).

(70)

The Bessel-zero argument proves that for every
a>0
and every
\theta>0
, at least one index
n\geq 1
satisfies

L_{n}^{(a-1)}(\theta)<0.

(71)

For such an
n
, Proposition
2
gives an actual violation of the
n
-th HCM derivative sign for all sufficiently small
u>0
.

Remark 2

(Why the result is not a mere convexity counterexample)
.

The obstruction is stronger than a low-order failure such as non-log-convexity or non-monotonicity. The first few HCM signs may hold for some parameter ranges. The proof shows that no matter how many early signs survive, the infinite HCM sign sequence cannot survive positive deformation, because the signed Bell generator has a positive Bessel zero.

6
Final remarks

The theorem gives the sharp HCM answer to the noncentral chi-square range problem raised in
(
Baricz et al. 2026
, Section 4.2)
: the central gamma line is the whole HCM range, and every positive noncentrality destroys HCM. It also gives a concrete closure failure: fixed-rate Poisson mixing over the shape parameter can take gamma, hence HCM, densities outside the HCM class. This negative result is deliberately limited to density-side HCM. It does not contradict the known infinite divisibility of the noncentral chi-square law, and it does not decide whether any positive-noncentrality subfamily belongs to the generalized-gamma-convolution class.
