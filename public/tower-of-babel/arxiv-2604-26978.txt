Positive mass theorem for initial data sets with arbitrary ends
Tin-Yau Tsang

Abstract
. We showed a positive energy theorem for asymptotically flat initial data sets with the concept of spectral PSC by [ 34 , 6 , 7 ] and the Jang equation [ 47 , 23 , 35 ] . Then, we proved a quantitative shielding theorem concerning the causal property of the energy-momentum vector of an asymptotically hyperbolic manifold. As a result, we established the positive mass theorem for complete asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds satisfying the dominant energy condition. As corollaries, we also obtained corresponding results for manifolds with asymptotically locally hyperbolic ends with a certain symmetry.



1.
Introduction

For general relativity, it is a fascinating question to seek global invariants to indicate energy for a spacelike slice. Arnowitt, Deser and Misner defined the ADM mass in
[
4
]
. The positive mass theorem proved by Schoen and Yau
[
45
]
showed that asymptotically flat manifolds with non-negative scalar curvature must have non-negative ADM mass. Furthermore, they showed that Euclidean space is characterised by vanishing ADM mass. Witten (
[
52
]
, cf.
[
43
]
) gave a proof on spin manifolds.

For another standard model arising from Einstein’s theory with negative cosmological constant, the hyperbolic space, Min-Oo
[
41
]
under certain strong assumptions has shown a scalar curvature rigidity theorem, and this is furthered by Andersson and Dahl
[
1
]
. Wang found a geometric invariant vector for energy-momentum
[
51
]
under a strong asymptotics which we referred to as Wang’s asymptotics. It is then generalised by Chruściel and Herzlich
[
19
]
. Corresponding to the positive mass theorem aforementioned, they
[
51
,
19
]
showed that the energy momentum vector for a spin asymptotically hyperbolic manifold with scalar curvature bounded from below by
-n(n-1)
must be future-pointing timelike or vanishes. Moreover, the hyperbolic space is the only such with the vanishing energy-momentum vector.

There are then different approaches for manifolds not necessarily spin. While assuming the sign of the mass aspect function, this was shown by Andersson, Cai and Galloway
[
2
]
by patching with the hyperbolic space. Chruściel, Galloway, Nguyen and Paetz
[
17
]
ruled out the past-pointing timelike case by approximating the metric by those with constant mass aspect function and Chruściel and Delay
[
16
]
eliminated the spacelike case by the Maskit gluing. The Jang equation
[
47
]
approach is adopted by Sakovich
[
44
]
and Lundberg
[
40
]
. On the other hand, a more general rigidity result is established by Huang, Jang and Martin
[
32
]
.

Motivated by the Liouville Theorem
[
48
]
, it is conjectured (
[
48
,
49
]
) that for a complete manifold with non-negative scalar curvature and an asymptotically flat end
\mathcal{E}
, the ADM mass of
\mathcal{E}
is non-negative. It is first proved by Lesourd, Unger and Yau in
[
39
]
with the assumption that
\mathcal{E}
is asymptotically Schwarzschild. This is generalised by Zhu
[
53
]
, Chen, Liu, Shi and Zhu
[
13
]
and Lee, Lesourd and Unger
[
36
]
. While for spin manifolds, this was done by Bartnik and Chruściel
[
5
]
and Cecchini and Ziedler
[
10
]
.

A natural question is whether the causal property of the energy momentum vector behaves similarly of an asymptotically hyperbolic end of a complete manifold with scalar curvature bounded from below by
-n(n-1)
. Chai and Wan
[
11
]
gave an affirmative answer for spin manifolds.

In this paper, we are going to establish without the spin assumption the positive mass theorem for asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds with arbitrary ends. We first proved a quantitative shielding theorem by considering the Maskit gluing (
[
16
]
), patching with the hyperbolic space (
[
2
]
) and the positive energy theorem for asymptotically flat initial data sets (Theorem
1.3
, c.f.
[
47
,
23
]
, the spacetime positive mass theorem with boundary
[
26
,
37
]
) in particular its rigidity statement (
[
34
]
).

(Quantitative shielding theorem, cf.
[
36
]
Theorem 1.1)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
,

, be an asymptotically hyperbolic manifold of Hölder type
({\alpha},\delta)
where
{\alpha}\in(0,1)
and
\delta>\frac{n}{2}
. Let
U_{0}
,
U_{1}
, and
U_{2}
be neighborhoods of an asymptotically hyperbolic end
\mathcal{E}
such that
\overline{U_{2}}\subset U_{1}

D_{0}=\mathrm{dist}_{g}(\partial U_{0},U_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad D_{1}=\mathrm{dist}_{g}(U_{2},\partial U_{1}).

If the following hold:

(1)

has no points of incompleteness in
U_{0}

(2)

R_{g}\geq-n(n-1)

2.
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
(\overline{X},d)
be its completion. For example,
\overline{X}
can be constructed by taking appropriate equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences. A point in
\overline{X}\setminus X
is called a
point of incompleteness
for
X
. A set
S\subset X
is said to be
complete
if its closure in
X
remains closed under the inclusion
X\to\overline{X}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
M^{n}
be a noncompact manifold with a distinguished end
\mathcal{E}
. We say that
(M,g)
possesses a
structure of infinity
along
\mathcal{E}
if
\mathcal{E}
possesses no points of incompleteness and there exists a diffeomorphism

(2.1)

\Phi:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{0}}

for some
r_{0}>0

and the coordinate norm
r:=|x|
diverges as we go out along the end. The set
\mathring{M}=M\setminus\mathcal{E}
is called the
core
. Note that in our definition, the core is not assumed to be compact and
(M,g)
is not assumed to be complete. We will often identify
\mathcal{E}
with the set
\{|x|\geq r_{0}\}
. Under
\Phi
, we can express

g_{\mathbb{H}}=dr^{2}+\sinh^{2}(r)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}

the hyperbolic metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{0}}\cong(r_{0},\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, which we extend arbitrarily to a complete metric on all of
M
and denote by
\overline{g}
. We interchangeably denote
(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{0}},g_{\mathbb{H}})
by
(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{0}},g_{\mathbb{H}})
when the context is clear.

We also allow for
M
to have a boundary, but of course require
\partial M
to not intersect
\mathcal{E}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
,
\mathcal{E}
, and
\Phi
be as in the previous definition. Let
N
be a closed subset of
M
which contains
\mathcal{E}
and such that
N\setminus\mathcal{E}
is compact.
For
u:N\to\mathbb{R}
, we say that

u\in C_{\delta}^{k,\alpha}(N)
for a non-negative integer
k
, and
0<\alpha\leq 1
if

\left\|u\right\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{\delta}(N)}:=\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(N\setminus\mathcal{E})}+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{0}}}e^{\delta r(x)}\left\|\Phi_{*}u\right\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(B_{1}(x))}<\infty.

Here is an equivalent definition of the

C^{k,\alpha}_{\delta}(\mathcal{E})
norm.

\left\|u\right\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{\delta}(\mathcal{E})}=\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}\sup_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{0}}}|e^{\delta r}\nabla^{j}(\Phi_{*}u)|+\|e^{\delta r}\nabla^{k}(\Phi_{*}u)\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{0}})}.

One can naturally extend the definition to tensor bundles.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a noncompact smooth Riemannian manifold possessing a structure of infinity
\Phi
along
\mathcal{E}
. Let
{\alpha}\in(0,1]
and
\delta>\frac{n}{2}
.
We say that
(M,g)
is an asymptotically hyperbolic
manifold of Hölder type
({\alpha},\delta)
,
if there exists a diffeomorphism

\Phi:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{0}},

such that
h:=\Phi_{*}g-g_{\mathbb{H}}\in C^{2,{\alpha}}_{\delta}(\mathcal{E})
and

(2.2)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{0}}}|R_{g}+n(n-1)|\cosh r\,d\mu_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}<\infty.

Furthermore,
g
has
Wang’s asymptotics
if

\Phi_{*}g=dr^{2}+\sinh^{2}(r)g_{r}

where

g_{r}=g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}+me^{-nr}+O^{k,\alpha}\bigl(e^{-(n+1)r}\bigr)\,,

is an
r
-dependent family of symmetric 2-tensors on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
m\in C^{k,{\alpha}}
is a symmetric 2-tensor on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Note that
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}m
is called the mass aspect function.

Let

\mathcal{N}:=\{V\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\mid\nabla^{\mathbb{H}}\nabla^{\mathbb{H}}V=Vg_{\mathbb{H}}\}
.
This is a vector space of the static potentials of the hyperbolic space with a basis consisting of the functions

V_{(0)}=\cosh r,\,V_{(1)}=x^{1}\sinh r,\dots,\,V_{(n)}=x^{n}\sinh r,

where the functions
x^{1},\dots,x^{n}
are the coordinate functions
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
restricted to
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

The linear functional
H^{g}_{\Phi}
on
\mathcal{N}
defined by (
[
51
,
19
]
)

H_{\Phi}^{g}(V)=\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{r}}\left(V(div_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h-dtr_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h)+(tr_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}h)dV-h(\nabla^{\mathbb{H}}V,\cdot)\right)(\nu)\,d\mu_{g_{\mathbb{H}}}

is called the
mass functional
of
(M,g)
with respect to
\Phi
, where
\nu
is the unit normal vector pointing to the infinity of
\mathcal{E}
with respect to
g_{\mathbb{H}}
. The energy momentum vector
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E},g)=(m_{0},m_{1},...,m_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is defined by
m_{{\beta}}:=H^{g}_{\Phi}(V_{({\beta})})
for
{\beta}=0,1,...,n
.

Correspondingly, one can define asymptotically locally hyperbolic ends with different backgrounds and a scalar mass with different static potentials, see
[
31
]
Section 2 for details.

A 3-tuple
(M,g,k)
, where
g
is a Riemannian metric and
k
is a symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor, is called an initial data set. Define the conjugate momentum tensor by
\pi=k-(tr_{g}k)g
. Under constraint equations, we can define the mass density
\mu
and the current density
J
by

\mu=\frac{1}{2}(R_{g}+(tr_{g}k)^{2}-|k|_{g}^{2})

and

J=div_{g}(k-(tr_{g}k)g)=div_{g}\pi.

(M,g,k)
is said to satisfy the dominant energy condition if

\mu\geq|J|_{g}.

An asymptotically hyperbolic manifold
(M,g)
can be regarded as an initial data set
(M,g,-g)
. In this case, the dominant energy condition is translated into

R_{g}\geq-n(n-1).

On the other hand, for asymptotically flat initial data sets (
[
24
]
Section 1,
[
37
]
Section 2), one can define the ADM energy-momentum vector
(E,P)

E=\frac{1}{2(n-1)|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{|x|=r}(g_{ij,i}-g_{ii,j})\nu^{j},

P_{i}:=\frac{1}{(n-1)|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{|x|=r}\pi_{ij}\nu^{j},\,\ \,\ \,\ i=1,2,...,n,

where the outward unit normal
\nu
and surface integral are with respect to the Euclidean metric.

For a smooth closed hypersurface
S\subset M
, we say
S
is a weakly outer trapped surface if on
S
, the outer null expansion

\theta_{+}=H+tr_{S}k\leq 0,

and a marginally outer trapped surface (
MOTS
) if

\theta_{+}=0;

correspondingly,
a weakly inner trapped surface if the inner null expansion

\theta_{-}=H-tr_{S}k\leq 0,

and a marginally inner trapped surface (
MITS
) if

\theta_{-}=0,

where
H
is computed with respect to the normal pointing to the infinity of the designated end
\mathcal{E}
. A surface is weakly trapped if it is either weakly outer trapped or weakly inner trapped.

Remark 2.1
.

In this note, the mean curvature is computed in the convention that
\mathbb{S}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
has positive mean curvature with respect to the outward normal.

The positive mass theorem (
[
24
]
) states that for an asymptotically flat initial data set satisfying the dominant energy condition,
E\geq|P|
. While for initial data sets with weakly trapped boundary, it is studied in
[
26
]
and
[
37
]
. Moreover, if
E=0
, then
(M,g,k)
is topologically
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and can be isometrically embedded into Minkowski space (
[
47
,
23
,
37
]
). These results were stated up to dimension 7. And we will show corresponding statements in higher dimensions in Section
4
.

3.
Preparation of initial data sets with negative mass

In this section, we are going to construct an initial data set which has a Euclidean end as motivated by
[
16
]
.

Let
n\geq 4
. Let
(M,g)
be an asymptotically hyperbolic manifold with
R_{g}\geq-n(n-1)
. By Theorem
1.5
, we can assume that it is with Wang’s asymptotics. Assume on the contrary, its energy momentum vector
\mathfrak{m}=(m_{0},m_{1},...,m_{n})
is neither causal future-pointing nor vanishing. Here we follow the strategy of
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
.

3.1.
Construction of a metric with past-pointing timelike energy momentum vector

If the energy momentum vector is spacelike, we can choose a coordinate such that

(3.1)

\begin{split}\mathfrak{m}=(m_{0},m_{1},0,...,0),\end{split}

where
m_{1}\geq|m_{0}|
by the assumption of its causal property and
m_{0}<0
.

Then, observe that Theorem 3.3 in
[
15
]
is actually “local”, i.e. concerning only the conformal boundary. Thus for all
\varepsilon>0
, it can be applied to
(M,g)
to construct a metric
g_{\varepsilon}
on
M
such that
g_{\varepsilon}|_{U_{\varepsilon}}=g_{\mathbb{H}}
and
g_{\varepsilon}|_{U_{2\varepsilon}}=g
, with
R_{g_{\varepsilon}}\geq-n(n-1)
on
M
. Here,
U_{a}
denotes a coordinate half ball in the upper half space model centred on the conformal boundary with radius
a
with respect to the compactified metric. Moreover, for
{\beta}=0,1,...,n
,
m_{\varepsilon,{\beta}}-m_{{\beta}}=o(\varepsilon^{\frac{n-4}{2}})
when
n\geq 5
while
o(\varepsilon^{2})
when
n=4
. (See
[
16
]
Section 2.)

Then, as described in
[
16
]
Section 2, we consider 2 copies of
(M,g_{\varepsilon})
above, one can further impose a conformal transformation to obtain a half hyperbolic space on each of them. Then, we can glue the non-trivial parts along the boundary of the half hyperbolic space. We denote the resulted manifold by
(\hat{M},\hat{g})
which is asymptotically hyperbolic with
R_{\hat{g}}\geq-n(n-1)
.

One can follow the computation in
[
16
]
Section 3 P.10 (3.8) to carry out another conformal transformation. We therefore obtain a timelike past-pointing energy-momentum vector denoted by
\hat{\mathfrak{m}}
if we choose
\gamma=\gamma(\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon})>0
and
\varepsilon
aforementioned, both sufficiently small, where

(3.2)

\begin{split}\hat{\mathfrak{m}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{1-\cos^{2}\gamma}}\left(m_{\varepsilon,0}-\cos\gamma\,m_{\varepsilon,1},\vec{0}\right).\end{split}

Note that the Wang’s asymptotics, if disturbed, can always be restored using Theorem
1.5
.

3.2.
Perturbation to a metric with an exactly hyperbolic end

By the previous subsection, it is sufficient to deal with the case where the energy-momentum vector is past-pointing timelike. Note that if
\hat{\mathfrak{m}}
is past-pointing timelike, one can apply a coordinate change as mentioned in
[
17
]
so that

(3.3)

\hat{\mathfrak{m}}=(-\sqrt{\hat{m}_{0}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{m}_{i}^{2}},0,...,0).

Then, one can apply
[
17
]
Corollary 1.4 and choice of coordinates in
[
17
]
Section 3 which only considers a tubular neighbourhood of the conformal boundary to perturb
\hat{g}
to
\tilde{g}
such that
