Peripapillary Retinal Nerve Fibre and Ganglion Cell Layer Changes Following Intravitreal Aflibercept 8 mg Therapy in Neovascular Age-Related Macular Degeneration: A Longitudinal OCT Study
Authors: Giulio Volpe, Matteo Bonanata, Arianna Peyla, Ludovico Ruscitti, Veronika Eggarter, Martina Pache, Arianna Paris, Moreno Menghini, Gabriela Grimaldi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionIntravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) therapy is the standard of care for neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) and other retinal vascular diseases. However, transient post-injection intraocular pressure elevations have raised concerns regarding potential cumulative effects on inner retinal structures, including the peripapillary retinal nerve fibre layer (pRNFL) and macular ganglion cell–inner plexiform layer (mGCIP). As longitudinal evidence on the neuro-retinal structural safety of higher-dose formulations such as aflibercept 8 mg remains limited, this study evaluated 12-month changes in pRNFL and mGCIP thickness following intravitreal aflibercept 8 mg.MethodsThis single-centre, observational longitudinal cohort study included patients with nAMD treated with intravitreal aflibercept 8 mg or aflibercept 2 mg. Patients receiving aflibercept 8 mg were prospectively enrolled, while patients treated with 2 mg aflibercept were retrospectively identified. Spectral-domain optical coherence tomography was used to assess pRNFL and mGCIP thickness at baseline and at 3, 6 and 12 months. Longitudinal within-eye changes relative to baseline were analysed, alongside inter-eye comparisons with fellow untreated eyes and between-treatment group comparisons at 12 months.ResultsThirty-eight eyes from 35 patients were included. In eyes treated with aflibercept 8 mg, mGCIP thickness remained stable throughout follow-up, with no significant changes at 3, 6 or 12 months compared with baseline. At 12 months, mGCIP thickness was comparable between aflibercept 8 mg-treated eyes and fellow untreated eyes, as well as between aflibercept 8 mg and aflibercept 2 mg-treated eyes. Similarly, pRNFL thickness showed no significant global or sectoral changes over 12 months and did not differ between treatment groups.ConclusionOver 12 months of follow-up, intravitreal aflibercept 8 mg was not associated with significant thinning of pRNFL or mGCIP thickness, supporting its neuro-retinal structural safety in routine clinical practice.Keywords: Aflibercept 8 mg, Anti-VEGF therapy, Neovascular age-related macular degeneration, Optical coherence tomography, Peripapillary retinal nerve fibre layer, Ganglion cell–inner plexiform layer, Intravitreal injections, Intraocular pressure

Introduction

Intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) therapy represents the standard of care for neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD), diabetic macular oedema (DMO), and cystoid macular oedema secondary to retinal vein occlusion. Despite their clinical effectiveness, intravitreal anti-VEGF injections are well recognised to be associated with an immediate and transient elevation in intraocular pressure (IOP) as a procedural side effect. Transient post-injection IOP increases have been consistently reported following the administration of several intravitreal agents, including pegaptanib [1], ranibizumab [2], bevacizumab [3], aflibercept [4] and faricimab [5].

Based on the hypothesis that repeated acute IOP elevations may affect optic nerve health [6], subsequent studies have explored the potential impact of repeated intravitreal anti-VEGF treatment, and the associated transient IOP spikes, on inner retinal structures such as the retinal nerve fibre layer (RNFL) and macular ganglion cell and inner plexiform layer (mGCIP) [7, 8]. Clinical investigations assessing RNFL and mGCIP thickness following anti-VEGF therapy have yielded heterogeneous results, with most studies reporting stable RNFL measurements or only modest reductions, while thinning of the mGCIP has been observed in selected cohorts [9–11]. Accordingly, meta-analyses and narrative reviews have highlighted substantial variability in study design, analytical approaches, and outcomes, with no consistent overall effect of anti-VEGF therapy on RNFL thickness identified [7, 8, 11–13].

More recently, transient post-injection IOP elevations have also been reported with higher-dose formulations such as aflibercept 8 mg [5]. Aflibercept 8 mg is a higher-concentration formulation of aflibercept 2 mg with enhanced molecular stability and is administered in a larger injection volume of 0.07 mL [14]. Despite its increasing clinical use, evidence regarding the longitudinal effects of aflibercept 8 mg on peripapillary RNFL (pRNFL) and mGCIP thickness remains limited. Importantly, the evaluation of higher-dose anti-VEGF therapy extends beyond IOP-related considerations, as VEGF has been recognised as a neurotrophic and neuroprotective factor for retinal neurons, raising theoretical concerns regarding sustained VEGF inhibition and potential neuro-retinal effects [14–18].

Accordingly, the present study was designed to help address this gap in knowledge by prospectively evaluating longitudinal changes in pRNFL and mGCIP thickness over a 12-month period in eyes undergoing repeated intravitreal treatment with aflibercept 8 mg.

Methods

Study Design

This was a single-centre, observational longitudinal cohort study conducted at the Ophthalmology Department of Ospedale Regionale of Lugano, Switzerland. The study included both prospectively and retrospectively enrolled cohorts according to treatment exposure. Assessments were performed at predefined timepoints, including baseline (T0) and follow-up visits at 3 months (T1), 6 months (T2) and 12 months (T3) [19].

Participants

Consecutive patients diagnosed with nAMD requiring intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy were eligible for inclusion. Diagnosis of nAMD was established by retina specialists using spectral-domain optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT) and optical coherence tomography angiography. Fluorescein angiography and indocyanine green angiography were used at the discretion of the treating physician. Patients treated with aflibercept 8 mg who attended the medical retina clinic between February 2024 and December 2024 were prospectively enrolled, while patients treated with aflibercept 2 mg during the same clinical period were retrospectively identified from institutional medical records. Treatment allocation was determined by routine clinical practice and not by study protocol. Patients were classified into two exposure groups according to the administered agent: aflibercept 2 mg (group 1) and aflibercept 8 mg (group 2).

Eligibility Criteria

Exclusion criteria included poor quality of imaging, a diagnosis of glaucoma, ocular hypertension, optic neuropathy, prior vitrectomy surgery, high myopia or hyperopia, the presence of intraretinal fluid at baseline, and refusal or inability to provide informed consent.

Clinical and Imaging Assessments

At baseline, all participants underwent a comprehensive ophthalmological examination, including best-corrected visual acuity assessment, air-puff tonometry, slit-lamp examination, fundus examination with indirect ophthalmoscopy, and SD-OCT, in accordance with standard clinical practice in the retinal therapy unit. The same imaging protocol was applied at all follow-up visits.

IVT Technique

Intravitreal injections were performed under clean-room conditions according to the standard clinical protocol of our institution, as previously described [5].

Optical Coherence Tomography

All OCT imaging was acquired using Spectralis SD-OCT devices (Heidelberg Engineering, Heidelberg, Germany). The pRNFL was measured using an 11.9° or 3.5-mm-diameter peripapillary ring scan. We analysed both the global thickness and the different sectors. The mGCIP thickness was determined from a 3-mm-diameter annulus around the fovea, excluding the central 1-mm cylinder, with measurements performed across retinal sectors. Subsequent scans were acquired in follow-up mode to ensure accurate realignment and longitudinal comparability. OCT scans were segmented using the Heidelberg Eye Explorer (HEYEX) software (Heidelberg Engineering, Heidelberg, Germany; version 1.12.1.0), and any segmentation errors identified were manually corrected to ensure accuracy. All pRNFL OCT images met the OSCAR-IB criteria [20] and have been presented in compliance with the recommendations of Apostel V.2.0 [21].

Statistical Analyses

We conducted a comprehensive statistical analysis of the data using appropriate non-parametric tests, selected on the basis of the distributional characteristics and sample structure. Specifically, the paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test was applied to compare measurements within the same subjects, while the Mann–Whitney U test for independent samples was used to assess differences between two independent groups. All analyses were performed using the online statistical software Statistics Kingdom. Statistical significance was defined as a p value < 0.05.

Ethical Considerations

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Canton Ticino (CE 4556; approval number 2024-00481). All research procedures and data management were conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Written informed consent for participation and data use was obtained from all prospectively enrolled participants before enrolment. Written informed consent had also previously been obtained from retrospectively identified patients.

Discussion

Repeated intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy has raised long-standing concerns regarding potential neuro-retinal damage, particularly affecting the RNFL and the mGCIP. Several observational and longitudinal studies in nAMD have reported progressive thinning of the macular ganglion cell layer or mGCIP over time, whereas RNFL findings have remained inconsistent, ranging from stability to modest global or sectoral thinning. These discrepancies are likely influenced by heterogeneity in study design, disease activity, imaging protocols and analytical approaches [9–11]. In particular, many prior investigations relied on cross-sectional analyses, fellow-eye comparisons or retrospective cohorts, often with limited control for intraretinal fluid status and non-standardised baseline imaging, thereby constraining causal inference regarding treatment-related neuro-retinal change [11–13].

In contrast to previous reports [9, 10], the present study was not limited to fellow-eye comparisons but incorporated longitudinal within-eye analyses relative to baseline and excluded eyes with intraretinal fluid, thereby reducing potential segmentation artefacts and disease-related confounding. In addition, we evaluated the mGCIP in its entirety rather than the ganglion cell layer alone [10, 12, 13] and assessed peripapillary RNFL thickness instead of macular RNFL measurements, which have been used in several earlier studies [9–12, 22]. These methodological choices may enhance sensitivity for detecting subtle longitudinal structural changes, as mGCIP measurements sample a larger volume of inner retinal tissue and have demonstrated robust performance for change detection over time, while peripapillary RNFL thickness remains a widely adopted and clinically relevant marker of optic nerve integrity in longitudinal assessments [23, 24]. Importantly, the present study addresses an unmet need by providing the first prospective longitudinal evaluation of pRNFL and mGCIP thickness following repeated intravitreal treatment with aflibercept 8 mg, using standardised OCT acquisition and complementary analytical approaches to more rigorously assess neuro-retinal safety.

To our knowledge, no previous study has prospectively assessed neuro-retinal structural outcomes over a 12-month period specifically in eyes treated with this higher-dose formulation. Over the study period, we found no evidence of significant longitudinal thinning of either pRNFL or mGCIP thickness. These findings suggest that, despite the higher concentration and injection volume, aflibercept 8 mg does not appear to adversely affect inner retinal structural integrity over 1 year of follow-up. This observation is clinically relevant not only in the context of transient post-injection IOP elevations but also in light of the recognised neurotrophic and neuroprotective role of VEGF in retinal neurons, which has raised theoretical concerns regarding sustained VEGF inhibition.

The strengths of this study include its longitudinal design, well-defined eligibility criteria, standardised imaging protocol and high-quality OCT segmentation. Moreover, these data contribute postmarketing phase IV evidence that complements and extends safety insights derived from phase III clinical trials, offering additional reassurance in a real-world clinical setting. In keeping with this real-world design, treatment allocation was determined by routine clinical practice rather than by study protocol. While this approach may introduce selection bias, it reflects typical clinical decision-making and may increase the generalisability of our findings. Similarly, the lower number of injections observed in the aflibercept 8 mg group may reflect treatment interval extension in routine clinical practice and should be considered when interpreting between-group comparisons. Several additional limitations should also be acknowledged. The sample size was modest, which may limit the ability to detect very small structural changes. The 12-month follow-up period, while adequate to capture early and intermediate effects, may not reflect very long-term neuro-retinal changes associated with prolonged anti-VEGF exposure. In addition, although within-eye longitudinal analyses reduce interindividual variability, disease-related factors intrinsic to nAMD may still influence retinal layer measurements independently of treatment effects. A substantial proportion of baseline IOP data was missing in the retrospectively collected aflibercept 2 mg group, which may have limited the comparability of baseline measurements and contributed to the observed difference between groups. However, we excluded patients with glaucoma and ocular hypertension to minimise potential confounding factors that could affect retinal measurements. Finally, while pRNFL and mGCIP thickness are clinically relevant OCT-derived structural biomarkers, they may not capture functional alterations detectable with complementary modalities such as visual field testing, microperimetry or electrophysiological assessment. These findings are specific to aflibercept 2 mg and 8 mg and should not be extrapolated to other anti-VEGF agents or dosing regimens without further investigation.

Conclusions

Repeated intravitreal treatment with aflibercept 8 mg was not associated with significant longitudinal changes in pRNFL or mGCIP thickness over 12 months in patients with nAMD. Structural OCT measurements remained stable over time and were comparable to both fellow untreated eyes and eyes treated with aflibercept 2 mg, despite the higher concentration and injection volume of the 8 mg formulation. These findings provide preliminary real-world evidence supporting the OCT-based neuro-retinal structural safety of aflibercept 8 mg in routine clinical practice. Larger studies with longer follow-up and complementary functional assessments are warranted to further clarify the long-term neuro-retinal effects of sustained high-dose anti-VEGF therapy.
