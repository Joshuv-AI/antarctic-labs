Safety and HBsAg reduction with a lentiviral vaccine J-51 in chronic hepatitis B: An investigator-initiated pilot trial
Authors: Jieyu Song, Qiran Zhang, Yumeng Zhang, Jingjing Shen, Rongrong Wei, Kun Wang, Chunyan Shen, Wenjuan Wang, Mingmei Chu, Fang Zhou, Xiao Liang, Linshu Zhang, Jingyan Huang, Chao Qiu, Hong Wu, Wenhong Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis first-in-human, investigator-initiated trial evaluated the novel non-integrating lentiviral-vector-based therapeutic vaccine J-51 for chronic hepatitis B. The open-label pilot study enrolled seven HBeAg-negative patients with HBsAg <5,000 IU/mL. Participants received varying doses (5.0 × 107 TU–2.0 × 109 TU) in sequential cohorts to assess safety and preliminary efficacy. J-51 demonstrated a favorable safety profile across all doses, with no serious adverse events reported; all adverse events were mild and self-limiting. Preliminary efficacy in the per-protocol set (n = 6) showed that three patients (50%) achieved a >0.5 log10 IU/mL HBsAg reduction from baseline. Notably, one patient in the 1.0 × 109 TU cohort achieved HBsAg seroclearance by week 52. Responders were characterized by low baseline HBsAg levels (<120 IU/mL). These results indicate that J-51 is well tolerated and exhibits potential early biological activity, supporting a subsequent clinical trial with a larger sample size, randomized controlled design, and longer follow-up to properly evaluate its therapeutical potential.Keywords: chronic hepatitis B, therapeutic vaccine, lentiviral vector, immunotherapy, first-in-human trial

Introduction

Chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection remains a major global health burden, affecting more than 250 million people and resulting in approximately 1.1 million annual deaths from complications such as cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).1 The primary goal of clinical management is to reduce long-term morbidity and mortality by preventing disease progression.2 However, these clinical outcomes develop over decades, rendering them impractical as short-term trial endpoints. Growing evidence indicates that HBsAg loss is strongly associated with sustained remission and a reduced risk of HCC.3,4,5 Reflecting this, current guidelines endorse functional cure—defined as HBsAg loss with undetectable HBV DNA—as a key treatment objective.2,6

First-line therapies, including nucleos(t)ide analogues (NAs) and pegylated interferon-α (PEG-IFN-α), have markedly improved HBV management, yet both exhibit limited efficacy in achieving HBsAg loss. NAs potently suppress viral replication but rarely lead to HBsAg seroclearance due to their inability to eliminate covalently closed circular DNA (cccDNA) or suppress integrated HBV DNA-originated HBsAg production.7,8 In contrast, PEG-IFN-α exerts immunomodulatory effects and can induce higher rates of HBsAg loss in a finite course, but its utility is constrained by suboptimal response rates and significant side effects.9,10

These limitations have spurred the development of novel agents with diverse mechanisms of action. Chronic HBV infection is characterized by immune tolerance, featuring weakened T cell responses.11 Therapeutic vaccines have thus emerged as a promising strategy to achieve functional cure by restoring virus-specific T cell immunity.12,13 While some viral vectors induce robust immunity, pre-existing immunity in populations often limits their clinical applicability.14 J-51 is built upon a replication-incompetent, non-pathogenic lentiviral vector platform,15,16 which was originally developed by the Pasteur Institute and engineered by Jinwei Biotechnology to encode the HBV genotype C large S antigen.17 This self-inactivating vector efficiently transduces antigen-presenting cells without genomic integration, enabling prolonged transient antigen expression. Through major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class I presentation, J-51 activates potent HBV-specific CD8+ T cell responses, facilitating viral clearance and HBsAg reduction.17 Key advantages include its capacity to transduce non-dividing professional antigen-presenting cells, such as dendritic cells, owing to the lentiviral vector’s intrinsic property to cross the intact nuclear membrane, thereby supporting the induction of robust and durable cellular immunity.15,16 This first-in-human study evaluated the safety, tolerability, and preliminary efficacy of J-51 at various dose levels in patients with chronic hepatitis B (CHB).

Results

Participants

As of September 2025, a total of seven subjects were enrolled in this two-part clinical study. The cohort consisted of three participants in part 1 (dose and regimen exploration) and 4 participants in part 2 (dose-escalation safety and efficacy validation). In part 1, serum HBV DNA was below the lower limit of quantification in all participants; in part 2, this was the case for 50% of participants (Table S1). All subjects completed the planned 24-week follow-up period. Notably, all three participants in part 1 completed the full 24-week follow-up after receiving the booster vaccination (Figure 1). After the completion of planned visits, all patients entered an extended long-term follow-up phase to monitor delayed adverse events and long-term efficacy. The safety set (SS) included all seven enrolled patients, while the Per-protocol set (PPS) comprised six patients, as one participant from part 2 was excluded due to non-compliance with the inclusion criteria.

Safety and tolerability

In part 1, which focused on exploratory assessment of safety, dosing, and regimen optimization, no serious adverse events (SAEs) were reported among the three participants. Two of the three patients (66.7%) experienced AEs, all of which were mild and showed no dose-dependent relationship (Table S2). Importantly, no AEs were reported during the initial low-dose J-51 vaccination phase or the subsequent 24-week follow-up period (Figure 1). These participants subsequently received a high-dose booster regimen (Figure 1). Reported adverse events included one case each of upper respiratory tract infection, vaginitis, and bilateral lower limb weakness. All AEs resolved without sequelae and were assessed as either unrelated or possibly unrelated to J-51 treatment, indicating that the high-dose booster regimen also demonstrated a favorable safety profile. The favorable safety profile observed in this low-dose exploratory phase supported the decision to proceed with dose escalation in part 2.

In part 2, which involved dose escalation for further safety and efficacy exploration, the favorable safety profile observed in part 1 was maintained across higher dose groups. No SAEs were reported among the four participants. The most common AE was injection site pain (six instances), which was mild, self-limiting, and resolved spontaneously (Table S2). Other AEs included dizziness (one instance, related), arrhythmia (two instances, unrelated), pharyngitis (one instance, unrelated), right ear numbness (one instance, unrelated), and hyperlipidemia (one instance, related). All AEs were mild and exhibited no dose-dependent trend, further supporting the favorable safety profile of J-51 and enabling its continued evaluation in broader clinical cohorts.

No participants discontinued treatment due to tolerability issues. No clinically significant abnormalities were observed in laboratory parameters, electrocardiogram (ECG) findings, or vital signs. Notably, even the highest dose group (2.0 × 109 TU) demonstrated favorable tolerability, supporting its selection as a candidate dose for subsequent expanded phase I safety trials.

Efficacy

In part 1, one of three patients (33.3%) achieved a serum HBsAg response, defined as a maximum reduction greater than 0.5 log10 IU/mL. The maximum HBsAg declines observed were 0.31, 1.25, and 0.35 log10 IU/mL, respectively (Figure 2). In part 2, a more pronounced trend was observed. Two of the three patients (66.7%) exhibited a serum HBsAg response, with maximum reductions of 3.64 and 0.54 log10 IU/mL, respectively (Figure 3). Notably, the patient receiving the 1 × 109 TU dose achieved HBsAg seroclearance (HBsAg <0.05 IU/mL) by week 52 (Figure 3).

All responders exhibited low baseline HBsAg levels (<120 IU/mL) and did not experience ALT flares (defined as ALT ≥5× ULN) (Figures 2 and 3). Hepatitis B core-related antigen (HBcrAg) levels remained consistently low across treatment groups, with only minor fluctuations throughout the study (Figures 2 and 3). Regarding HBV DNA, baseline levels in all patients except patient 7 (group E) were near or below the lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) and showed minimal variation during treatment (Figures 2 and 3). At week 6, when HBsAg levels reached nadir, patient 7 initiated antiviral therapy with Tenofovir alafenamide (TAF), which led to rapid decline in HBV DNA to undetectable levels, followed by a transient rebound and subsequent overall downward trend in HBsAg (Figure 3). Furthermore, HBsAg reduction was more pronounced in the higher-dose groups—specifically, the 1.0 × 108 TU group in part 1 and the 1 × 109 TU group in part 2.

These efficacy results suggest that patients with low baseline HBsAg levels are more likely to respond to J-51 treatment, identifying this subgroup as a target population for further investigation. Although spontaneous HBsAg seroclearance can occur in patients with low HBsAg levels, various studies indicate that the annual clearance rate remains relatively low.18,19 In this study, half of the participants showed HBsAg reduction, and one case achieved HBsAg seroclearance, both occurring within a short time frame after vaccination. This encouraging antiviral activity observed in these higher dose cohorts, combined with the favorable safety profile, supports advancing the high-dose regimen into expanded trials.

Discussion

This first-in-human, exploratory trial provides an initial clinical evaluation of the safety, tolerability, and preliminary biological activity of J-51, a non-integrating lentiviral-vector-based therapeutic vaccine, in patients with CHB. The results demonstrate that J-51 was well tolerated across all tested dose levels, with no serious adverse events or treatment discontinuations.

In terms of biological activity, serum HBsAg responses were noted in three of six evaluable patients (50%). One patient (patient 6), who received 1 × 109 TU, achieved HBsAg seroclearance (0.01 IU/mL) by week 52. It is important to note, however, that all responding patients had low baseline HBsAg levels (<120 IU/mL), and spontaneous HBsAg clearance is known to occur at a low but non-negligible annual rate in such populations (approximately 3.83% per year for HBsAg <100 IU/mL).18 In the context of a preliminary case series, these findings highlight a favorable safety profile and suggest early biological activity, as serum HBsAg responses were observed in three of six evaluable patients. A subsequent phase I/II trial, featuring a larger sample size, a randomized controlled design, and longer follow-up, is planned to properly evaluate the candidate’s potential. The present pilot study is considered to provide a useful foundation for that next step.

Consistent with the preclinical mechanism of action, immune profiling of patients 1 and 2 in the previous experimental study demonstrated J-51 can induce robust HBV-specific multifunctional, cytotoxic CD8+ T cells.17 Although detailed immunological monitoring was not performed for all patients, a vaccine-induced T cell response was observed prior to the HBsAg decline. This ability to break HBsAg-specific immune tolerance fundamentally distinguishes J-51 from modalities that only transiently modulate immunity or suppress viral replication.20,21,22

From a clinical perspective, J-51 required only two injections (1 week apart) to induce sustained HBsAg reduction through the end of observation, contrasting with the 48-week dosing and significant side effects of PEG-IFN-α.9 The favorable safety profile also aligns with the theoretical advantage of non-integrating lentiviral vectors over adenoviral or mRNA platforms, which may be limited by pre-existing immunity or inflammatory reactions.23,24,25

Interestingly, patient 6 achieved HBsAg clearance in the absence of ALT elevation. This may be explained by the patient’s low baseline levels of HBsAg and HBV DNA, coupled with normal ALT, indicating a low level of viral antigen burden and no apparent liver injury. As such, even if cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) were activated, the resulting cytolytic events may have been too limited to drive a marked ALT elevation in serum. Of note, slight ALT fluctuations were also observed in other patients (e.g., patients 1, 2, and 3), albeit below the upper limit of normal and of modest magnitude. The association of HBsAg decline with low HBcrAg further suggests reduced cccDNA activity, supporting the potential of J-51 to target both integrated and episomal antigen sources.7,26,27,28

Unlike RNAi therapies, which often lead to post-treatment HBsAg rebound,29 J-51 facilitated a consistent decline in HBsAg during extended follow-up without observed rebound. Although one patient (patient 7) experienced a transient HBsAg increase upon NA therapy initiation, the overall trend supports the potential of J-51 for sustained antigen suppression, likely reflecting the restoration of durable T cell immunity against HBsAg.

Several limitations of this study are acknowledged, including the small sample size (n = 7), lack of a control group, and the absence of detailed immunological assessments. Additional evaluations, such as anti-HBs antibody development and HBV genotyping, would further help characterize the virological and immunological outcomes.

In summary, J-51 demonstrated a favorable safety profile and presented the potential of inducing sustained HBsAg reduction, including one seroclearance, in this small cohort of patients with CHB. These observations support the planned phase I and phase II trials to further confirm safety and efficacy, optimize treatment strategies, and elucidate the immune mechanisms underlying treatment-induced response.

Materials and methods

Study design

This single-arm, open-label, two-part, investigator-initiated pilot trial (ChiCTR2200062625) was designed to evaluate the safety and preliminary efficacy of J-51—a novel non-integrating lentiviral-vector-based therapeutic vaccine—in patients with chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection (Figure 1).

Part 1: Exploratory evaluation of ultra-low doses and booster regimen

Part 1 constituted an initial dose-exploration phase designed to assess the safety, tolerability, and preliminary immunogenicity of J-51 at ultra-low dose levels, with an integrated evaluation of a booster strategy. Two sequential cohorts were enrolled: group A was administered 5.0 × 107 TU on days 1 and 8, followed by a booster dose of 5.0 × 108 TU no earlier than week 24; group B received 1.0 × 108 TU on days 1 and 8, followed by the same booster regimen. The safety data collected from the initial two-dose series (prior to booster administration) in part 1 were used to determine the appropriate dose levels for subsequent evaluation in part 2.

Part 2: Dose escalation at higher fixed doses

Part 2 involved a dose-escalation phase to further evaluate the safety profile and antiviral activity of J-51 administered at higher fixed doses, without a booster component. Three sequential cohorts were evaluated: group C received 5.0 × 108 TU on days 1 and 8; group D received 1.0 × 109 TU on days 1 and 8; and group E received 2.0 × 109 TU on days 1 and 8. A 14-day safety observation period was implemented following the last dose in each cohort, after which a formal safety review was conducted jointly by the principal investigator and the sponsor’s medical lead. Enrollment into the next higher dose cohort was permitted only if no pre-specified suspension or termination criteria were met. Should any such criteria be satisfied, the investigator and sponsor would jointly determine whether to implement a temporary pause or permanent discontinuation of the trial.

After a screening period, J-51 was administered via intramuscular injection. Following injection, patients entered a follow-up phase for safety monitoring and efficacy evaluation until week 24. Based on interim data, the follow-up period was extended to acquire longer-term safety and response durability data. Criteria for treatment discontinuation are detailed in the Supplemental Materials & methods.

The study protocol and informed consent documents were reviewed and approved by the institutional review boards of Changzhi People’s Hospital and the National Health Commission of China. The study was conducted in accordance with the International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice (ICH-GCP) guidelines, applicable local regulations, and the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided written informed consent before undergoing any study-related procedures.

Patient population

Key eligibility criteria included adults aged 18–65 years with chronic HBV infection (HBsAg and/or HBV DNA positive for more than 6 months before screening), HBeAg-negative status, persistently normal ALT levels, no evidence of cirrhosis, and HBsAg level <5,000 IU/mL at screening. Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in the Supplemental Materials & methods.

Endpoints

The objectives were evaluating the safety and efficacy of J-51. Safety assessments included AEs, clinical laboratory tests, vital signs, ECGs, and physical examinations. The primary safety endpoint was SAE, the secondary safety endpoint was other AEs and abnormal clinical laboratory test result with clinical significance. The primary efficacy endpoint was the HBsAg response, defined as achieving a >0.5 log10 IU/mL HBsAg reduction from baseline at any time point during follow-up. The secondary efficacy endpoint was the maximum reduction in serum HBsAg from baseline.

Assessments

In part 1, patients underwent intensive safety and immunogenicity monitoring after first injection (days 4, 8, and 14; weeks 4, 6, 8, 12, 18, and 24) and post-booster (days 8 and 14; weeks 4, 8, 12, 18, and 24). In part 2, assessments were conducted on days 8 and 14 and weeks 4, 6, 8, 12, 18, and 24 post-injection. An extended follow-up could be initiated based on the 24-week safety and efficacy profile. Detailed virological assessment methods are provided in the supplemental materials.

AEs were graded according to the World Health Organization (WHO) Toxicity Grading Scale and monitored continuously throughout the study. Safety evaluations included ECGs, vital signs, injection-site reactions, and comprehensive laboratory panels (hematology, clinical chemistry, urinalysis, and coagulation parameters). These assessments were performed at screening, during each dosing visit, and throughout the follow-up period.

Statistical analysis

The SS included all patients who received at least one dose of J-51. Efficacy analyses were performed using the per-protocol set (PPS), which included all randomized patients who complied with the study protocol and excluded those with major protocol deviations. Analyses were conducted according to the assigned treatment groups. Safety data were summarized using descriptive statistics. Efficacy endpoints were evaluated using both descriptive and correlation-based methods. Statistical analyses were conducted with the use of SAS software, v.9.4.
