A characterization of the sphere in terms of the stereographic projection Thanks: Dedicated to Peter Gruber.
Efrén Morales-Amaya

Abstract
. Let K K be a convex body in the 3-dimensional Euclidian space 𝔼 3 \mathbb{E}^{3} and let N , S ∈ bd K N,S\in\operatorname*{bd}K , N ≠ S N\not=S . Suppose that the support plane Π S \Pi_{S} of K K at S S is unique. For every point x ∈ bd K \ { N } x\in\operatorname*{bd}K\backslash\{N\} we define the stereographic projection Ψ : bd K \ { N } → Π S \Psi:\operatorname*{bd}K\backslash\{N\}\rightarrow\Pi_{S} of x x onto Π S \Pi_{S} as the point y := L ⁡ ( N , x ) ∩ Π S y:=L(N,x)\cap\Pi_{S} . It is a well known property of the sphere 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} in 𝔼 3 \mathbb{E}^{3} that the stereographic projection maps circles onto circles (see [ 6 ] pag. 248). In this work we investigate what geometric elements determines that this property is fulfilled. Here we demonstrate that the following two properties of a convex body K ⊂ 𝔼 3 K\subset\mathbb{E}^{3} in terms of the stereographic projection characterize the sphere in 𝔼 3 \mathbb{E}^{3} : (1) The cones defined by the sections of K K and the point N N are axially symmetric (that is, they are invariant under a rotation by an angle of π \pi ). (2) given a section K Γ K_{\Gamma} of K K , the rotation that leaves the cone defined by K Γ K_{\Gamma} and N N invariant is such that it maps K Γ K_{\Gamma} into a homothetic figure to Ψ ⁡ ( K Γ ) \Psi(K_{\Gamma}) by a homothety with center of homothety at N N . An important element in the proof of the main theorem of this work is a characterization of the circle based on a geometric property, which will be called the stereographic property. It is worth highlighting that the stereographic projection defined on the sphere maps circles onto circles is intimately linked to the conditions (1) and (2) and the stereographic property of the circle.

Theorem 6

Let

and the line

Claim.
The convex body

Proof
. Let
x\in\operatorname*{bd}K
. Let

of the

\square

homothety, with center of homothety at
N
, which sends
R^{\Gamma}(\Gamma)
into
\Pi_{S}

sends
R^{\Gamma}(S_{\Gamma})
into
\Psi(S_{\Gamma})
. Therefore
\Psi(S_{\Gamma})
is a circle.
\square

Proof of Theorem

We take a system of coordinates

Lemma 1

Lemma 2
.

The body
K
is a body of revolution with axis the line

.

Proof.

In order to prove that

. Let

, let

. We denote by
\Gamma

. By virtue of the hypothesis, there exists a rotation

) holds. By Lemma

,

Lemma 3
.

Let
\Delta
be a plane containing the line

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be a plane containing the line

. In order to prove that

6
, thus we are going to prove that
\Delta\cap K

perpendicular to
\Delta
, let

\Gamma

be a plane containing

and parallel to

. We denote by
B
the bisectriz of the angle defined by the lines

and by

the reflections of

with respect to

. We are going to prove that the line segment

.

By virtue of the hypothesis, there exists a rotation
