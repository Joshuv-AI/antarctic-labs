A Short Note on a Variant of the Squint Algorithm
Haipeng Luo

Abstract
This short note describes a simple variant of the Squint algorithm of 3 for the classic expert problem. Via an equally simple modification of their proof, we prove that this variant ensures a regret bound that resembles the one shown in a recent work by 2 for a variant of the NormalHedge algorithm [ 1 ] .



1
Expert Problem and Squint

In the classic expert problem, a learner interacts with an adversary for
T
rounds.
In each round
t=1,\ldots,T
,
the learner decides a distribution
p_{t}\in\Delta_{N}
over
N
experts,
and the adversary decides the loss
\ell_{t}\in[0,1]
.
The learner then suffers loss
\left\langle{p_{t},\ell_{t}}\right\rangle
and observes
\ell_{t}
.
For any
\epsilon\in[1/N,1)
, the
\epsilon
-quantile regret of the learner is defined as

\text{\rm Reg}_{\epsilon}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\langle{p_{t},\ell_{t}}\right\rangle-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_{t,i_{\epsilon}}

where
i_{\epsilon}
is the
\lfloor\epsilon N\rfloor
-th best expert (according to their cumulative loss over
T
rounds).
When
\epsilon=1/N
,
\text{\rm Reg}_{\epsilon}
reduces to the standard external regret that compares the total loss of the learner to that of the best expert in hindsight.

Define the instantaneous regret vector
r_{t}
as
\left\langle{p_{t},\ell_{t}}\right\rangle\mathbf{1}-\ell_{t}
(where
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the all-one vector) and the cumulative regret vector as
R_{t}=\sum_{s=1}^{t}r_{t}
.
Further define the Squint potential as

\Phi(R,V)=\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{e^{\eta R-\eta^{2}V}-1}{\eta}d\eta

for
R\in\mathbb{R}
and
V\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, which satisfies the following property.

Lemma 1
.

For any
x\in[-1,1],R\in\mathbb{R}
and
V\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we have

\Phi(R+x,V+x^{2})\leq\Phi(R,V)+\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial R}(R,V)\cdot x.

Proof.

By definition, one has

\displaystyle\Phi(R+x,V+x^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{e^{\eta R-\eta^{2}V}e^{\eta x-\eta^{2}x^{2}}-1}{\eta}d\eta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{e^{\eta R-\eta^{2}V}(1+\eta x)-1}{\eta}d\eta=\Phi(R,V)+\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial R}(R,V)\cdot x,

where we use the inequality
e^{y-y^{2}}\leq 1+y
for any
y\geq-\tfrac{1}{2}
.

Squint Algorithm and Guarantee

The original Squint algorithm
(
3
)
predicts
p_{t}\in\Delta_{N}
at time
t
such that

p_{t,i}\propto\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial R}(R_{t-1,i},V_{t-1,i}),

(original Squint)

where
V_{t,i}=\sum_{s=1}^{t}v_{t,i}
and
v_{t,i}=r_{t,i}^{2}
.
The following lemma shows that the sum of the potential of the algorithm never increases, which is the key for the analysis.

Lemma 2

(Lemma 1 of
3
)
.

The Squint algorithm ensures

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi(R_{T,i},V_{T,i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi(R_{T-1,i},V_{T-1,i})\leq\cdots\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi(R_{0,i},V_{0,i})=0

Proof.

For any
t
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Phi(R_{t,i},V_{t,i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Phi(R_{t-1,i}+r_{t,i},V_{t-1,i}+r_{t,i}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Phi(R_{t-1,i},V_{t-1,i})+\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial R}(R_{t-1,i},V_{t-1,i})\cdot r_{t,i}

(Lemma
1
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Phi(R_{t-1,i},V_{t-1,i}),

where the last equality is because
\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{t,i}r_{t,i}=0
and
p_{t,i}\propto\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial R}(R_{t-1,i},V_{t-1,i})
.

Solely based on the fact
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi(R_{T,i},V_{T,i})\leq 0
,

3
then prove the following regret bound for Squint, which holds simultaneously for all
\epsilon
:

\text{\rm Reg}_{\epsilon}\leq\sqrt{2V_{T,i_{\epsilon}}}\left(1+\sqrt{2\ln\!\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}+\ln(T+1)}{\epsilon}\right)}\right)+5\ln\!\left(1+\frac{1+2\ln(T+1)}{\epsilon}\right).

2
A Variant of Squint

Now, consider the following variant of Squint:
at time
t
, predicts
p_{t}\in\Delta_{N}
such that

p_{t,i}\propto\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial R}(R_{t-1,i},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}V_{t-1}}),

(Squint varaint)

where
V_{t}=\sum_{s=1}^{t}v_{t}
,
v_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}q_{t,i}r_{t,i}^{2}
, and

q_{t,i}\propto-\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial V}(R_{t,i},V_{t})=\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial R^{2}}(R_{t,i},V_{t})=\int_{0}^{1/2}\eta e^{\eta R_{t,i}-\eta^{2}V_{t}}d\eta.

Although the definition of
v_{t}
is recursive (since it depends on
q_{t}
, which itself depends on
v_{t}
), one can still find
v_{t}
efficiently via a simple line search.
Specifically,
note that
v_{t}
is the root of the function

f(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial R^{2}}(R_{t,i},V_{t-1}+v)\left(v-r_{t,i}^{2}\right).

The facts that
f(v)
is continuous,
f(0)\leq 0
, and
f(1)\geq 0
imply that a root of
f
must exist because
0
and
1
and that it can be found via binary search.

Analysis

Via a simple modification of the proof of Lemma
2
,
one can show that the sum of the potential of this Squint variant also never increases.

Lemma 3
.

The Squint variant ensures

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi(R_{T,i},V_{T})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi(R_{T-1,i},V_{T-1})\leq\cdots\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi(R_{0,i},V_{0})=0

Proof.

For any
t
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Phi(R_{t,i},V_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Phi(R_{t,i},V_{t-1}+r_{t,i}^{2})+\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial V}(R_{t,i},V_{t})(v_{t}-r_{t,i}^{2})

(convexity of
\Phi
in
V
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Phi(R_{t,i},V_{t-1}+r_{t,i}^{2})

(definition of
v_{t}
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Phi(R_{t-1,i}+r_{t,i},V_{t-1}+r_{t,i}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Phi(R_{t-1,i},V_{t-1})+\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial R}(R_{t-1,i},V_{t-1})\cdot r_{t,i}

(Lemma
1
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Phi(R_{t-1,i},V_{t-1}),

where the last equality is again because
\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{t,i}r_{t,i}=0
and
p_{t,i}\propto\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial R}(R_{t-1,i},V_{t-1})
.

Therefore, repeating the exact same arguments of
3
, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

The
\epsilon
-quantile regret of the Squint variant satisfies:

\text{\rm Reg}_{\epsilon}\leq\sqrt{2V_{T}}\left(1+\sqrt{2\ln\!\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}+\ln(T+1)}{\epsilon}\right)}\right)+5\ln\!\left(1+\frac{1+2\ln(T+1)}{\epsilon}\right).

simultaneously for all
\epsilon
.

The only difference of this bound compared to that of the original Squint is the replacement of
V_{T,i_{\epsilon}}
with
V_{T}
.
The two bounds are incomparable in general, but the one for the new Squint variant resembles the bound from a recent work of
2
for a different algorithm.
Specifically, they analyze a variant of the NormalHedge algorithm of
1
and prove a similar bound with a different definition of the potential function.

As a final remark, we note that using the same idea as
4
, one can scale the update rule by any prior distribution
q\in\Delta_{N}
(that is,
p_{t,i}\propto q_{i}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial R}(R_{t-1,i},{V_{t-1}})
),
and convert the adaptive quantile bound of Theorem
4
into a regret bound against any distribution
u\in\Delta_{N}
, replacing the dependency of
\ln(1/\epsilon)
with
\text{KL}(u,q)
.
