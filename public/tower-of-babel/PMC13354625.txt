ANXA3 activates HIF-1α/VEGF Signaling Via the PI3K/AKT/mTOR Pathway to Promote Osteosarcoma Stem Cell-Like Phenotype
Authors: Shengtao Wang, Yan Sun, Wei Peng, Yuhan Sui, Shujuan Xu, Yufan Bai, Zhaoxu Li, Wenzhi Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAberrant activation of HIF‑1α contributes to the maintenance of CSC phenotypes in various malignancies. Our previous work revealed that ANXA3 is up‑regulated in OS cells. Here, we further investigated how ANXA3 regulates CSC‑like properties of OS cells through the HIF‑1α/VEGF signaling pathway.MethodsLentiviral vectors were used to modulate ANXA3 expression in OS cell lines. The expression of stemness‑associated genes was examined by qPCR and Western blotting, and sphere‑formation assays were performed to evaluate CSC self‑renewal capacity. Migration, invasion, and clonogenic survival under cisplatin treatment were assessed using Transwell and colony‑formation assays. An orthotopic intratibial implantation model in NOD-SCID mice was established to evaluate the effects of ANXA3 on tumor growth, metastasis, and CSC‑related features. Potential signaling mechanisms were analyzed by Western blotting and ELISA, and pathway‑specific inhibitors were applied to dissect the PI3K/Akt/mTOR–HIF‑1α/VEGF cascade.ResultsANXA3 expression was markedly elevated in osteosarcoma spheres compared with parental cells. Introduction

Osteosarcoma (OS) is the most common primary malignant bone tumor in adolescents and young adults [1]. Despite advances in multimodal therapy—particularly surgical resection combined with multidrug chemotherapy—the long-term prognosis for OS patients remains unsatisfactory [2–3]. High rates of recurrence and metastasis persist after surgery, and survival benefits for patients with advanced disease are limited [4–5]. A major obstacle is the incomplete understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying OS initiation and progression, especially those driving chemoresistance, relapse, and distant dissemination. Identifying and characterizing key regulatory molecules involved in OS pathogenesis is therefore critical for developing novel targeted strategies to improve clinical outcomes.

Cancer stem cells (CSCs) are a subpopulation of tumor cells with the capacity for self-renewal and multilineage differentiation [6–7]. CSCs are thought to be central to tumor initiation, sustained growth, therapy resistance, recurrence, and metastasis [8]. In OS, cells with stem-like properties—often referred to as osteosarcoma stem cells (OSCs)—have been identified [9–11]. These cells display enhanced sphere-forming ability, increased migratory and invasive potential, and intrinsic resistance to conventional chemotherapeutic agents [12]. Dissecting the molecular mechanisms that regulate OSC maintenance and targeting these pathways may provide new therapeutic opportunities to overcome current treatment limitations.

Annexin A3 (ANXA3), a calcium-dependent phospholipid-binding protein, has been implicated in the regulation of proliferation, migration, and angiogenesis in various solid tumors [13–15]. In our previous studies, ANXA3 was found to be significantly upregulated in OS cell lines (HOS and U2OS), and ANXA3 knockdown promoted apoptosis in these cells [16]. However, whether ANXA3 contributes to OS cell survival through the regulation of CSC-like properties remains unclear. Prior studies have highlighted the central role of hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) and its downstream effector vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) as a core signaling axis linking the hypoxic tumor microenvironment, stemness maintenance, and angiogenesis [17–18]. HIF-1α not only drives VEGF-mediated neovascularization but also activates multiple stemness-related transcriptional programs that enhance self-renewal and therapeutic resistance [19]. The PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway is a well-established upstream regulator of HIF-1α, stabilizing HIF-1α at both translational and metabolic levels to amplify its biological effects [20].

In this study, we hypothesized that ANXA3 acts as a critical regulator of CSC properties in OS by promoting tumor progression through the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling axis. Our in vitro and in vivo data demonstrate that ANXA3 upregulates HIF-1α and VEGF expression and promotes the expression of stemness-related genes in a PI3K/Akt/mTOR-dependent manner, thereby playing an essential role in maintaining CSC-like phenotypes in osteosarcoma.

Materials and Methods

Cell lines and Animals

Osteosarcoma cell line HOS (RRID: CVCL_0312), U-2OS (RRID: CVCL_0042) were kindly provided by Wuhan Pricella Biotechnology Co., Ltd. HOS were cultured in McCoy’s 5 A medium supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% P/S; U-2OS were cultured in MEM with 10% FBS and 1%P/S. All human cell lines have been authenticated using STR profiling within the last three years (Pricella Biotechnology). All in vitro experiments were performed with at least three independent biological replicates. Male 4- to 6-week-old non-obese diabetic severe combined immunodeficiency (NOD-SCID) mice were obtained from Liaoning Changsheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd (RRID: IMSR_CRL:39). All model mice were maintained under specific pathogen-free conditions. All animal experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee of Nanxishan Hospital of Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region (NXSYY-202402) and were conducted in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals of the National Institutes of Health. Clinical trial number is not applicable. After acclimatization, mice were randomly assigned to the indicated experimental groups prior to tumor cell inoculation or treatment. Tumor growth and endpoint-related data were recorded using predefined criteria. Histological evaluation was performed independently according to the same criteria for all groups. For in vivo experiments, the sample size for each group is indicated in the corresponding method section and figure legends, and the animal sample sizes were determined based on our prior experience with similar tumor models, and ethical considerations to minimize animal use while maintaining sufficient sensitivity to detect robust differences in tumor growth and metastatic burden.

Sphere-Formation Assays

Serum-free medium for sphere culture consists of McCoy’s 5 A/MEM, with 1% P/S, 20 ng/ml human recombinant epidermal growth factor, 20 ng/ml human recombinant basic fibroblast growth factor, 1% non-essential amino acids, 1% GlutaMax, 2% B27 supplement (Invitrogen, USA), and 1% methylcellulose (Sigma, USA). Osteosarcoma cell lines were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 2000 cells per well for culture. When the diameter of the spheres reached 100 μm, the sphere formation efficiency was calculated, and the spheres were collected for further use.

RNA Isolation and qPCR

RNA was extracted using TRIzol method (Takara, Japan), quantified by NanoDrop, and reverse-transcribed into cDNA at a concentration of 2000 ng using the Reverse Transcriptase M-MLV (Takara, Japan). cDNA was quantified using SYBR Green Master Mix (Yeasen, Shanghai, China). β-actin was used as the reference gene for all data analysis.ΔCq method (Cqtarget–Cqcontrol) was applied to quantify the relative expression of target genes. The primer sequences were as follows: ANXA3-F: GAG AGG TCA AAT GCA CAG CG; ANXA3-R: CTG GTG GAG TCA CTA GGG C; EpCAM-F: TGA TCC TGA CTG CGA TGA GAG; EpCAM-R: CTT GTC TGT TCT TCT GAC CCC; Nanog-F: TTT GTG GGC CTG AAG AAA ACT; Nanog-R: AGG GCT GTC CTG AAT AAG CAG; Oct-1-F: ACC CAC CTT AGC TTG TCG TC; Oct-1-R: GCT CTC TGT TCA CAG TCG CC; SOX2-F: GCC GAG TGG AAA CTT TTG TCG; SOX2-R: GGC AGC GTG TAC TTA TCC TTC T; β-actin-F: TGG AAT CCT GTG GCA TCC ATG AAA C; β-actin-R: TAA AAC GCA GCT CAG TAA CAG TCC.

Protein extraction and Western blot

Proteins were extracted in lysis buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) was mixed with PMSF(Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Protein concentration was quantified using a BCA kit༈Beyotime, Shanghai, China༉. Proteins (10–20 µg) mixed with sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) loading buffer were loaded onto 10% SDS-PAGE gels and then wet-transferred to PVDF membranes. Non-specific binding on the membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk in TBST (Tris-buffered saline with 0.1% Tween-20) at room temperature for 2 h. Incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies: Rabbit anti ANXA3 (1:1000, ab127924, Abcam), Rabbit anti Nanog (1:1000, ab214549, Abcam), Rabbit anti Oct1 (1:1000, ab178869, Abcam), Rabbit anti SOX2(1:1000, ab92494, Abcam), Rabbit anti HIF1α(1:1000, abs111338, Absin), Rabbit anti VEGF(1:1000, ab32152, Abcam), Rabbit anti PI3K(1:1000, 82796-4-RR, Proteintech), Rabbit anti Akt(1:1000, 10176-2-AP, Proteintech), Rabbit anti p-Akt(1:1000, 80455-1-RR, Proteintech), Rabbit anti mTOR(1:1000, 28273-1-AP, Proteintech), Rabbit anti p-mTOR(1:1000, 80596-1-RR, Proteintech), Mouse anti β-actin (1:1000, KTD101, Abbkine). Subsequently incubated with goat anti-rabbit IgG (1:1000, Zsbio, ZB2301). Afterwards, the immunoblots were exposed to ECL chemiluminescent imaging system, with β-actin serving as the loading control. Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software (NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA). The gray value of each target protein band was normalized to that of β-actin, and the relative protein expression level was calculated accordingly.

Flow cytometric analysis

Target cells in the logarithmic growth phase were harvested, digested into a single-cell suspension with 0.25% trypsin, centrifuged, then resuspended in PBS containing 2% FBS, and the cell density was adjusted to 1 × 10⁶ cells/mL. Fluorescently labeled specific antibodies were added separately: FITC-anti-CD44 (11-0441-82, eBioscience), PE-anti-CD133 (12-1338-42, eBioscience), and APC-anti-CD117 (17-1171-82, eBioscience, ), with 5 µL of each antibody added to every 100 µL of the cell suspension. Meanwhile, isotype control tubes were set up (corresponding fluorescently labeled IgG isotype control antibodies were added), and the mixture was incubated at 4℃ in the dark for 30 min. After incubation, the cells were washed twice with PBS to remove unbound antibodies, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Sigma, USA) for 15 min, and subsequently detected using a BD FACSCanto II flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, USA). Data were analyzed using FlowJo 10.0 software.

Cell viability assay

The sensitivity of osteosarcoma cell lines to chemotherapeutic agents was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay (Dojindo Laboratories, Japan). Briefly, cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 1 × 10³ cells per well and treated with either cisplatin or doxorubicin (MCE Corporation, USA). Following the manufacturer’s protocol, the CCK-8 reagent was then added to each well. All experiments were conducted in triplicate.

Cell transfection and inhibitor treatment

siRNAs targeting ANXA3, HIF-1α, and PI3K, as well as the corresponding negative control siRNA, were purchased from Shanghai GeneChem Co., Ltd. The ANXA3 and HIF-1α overexpression plasmid was purchased from GenePharma. Cells were transiently transfected with the indicated siRNAs or plasmids using Lipofectamine 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. 11668027) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The knockdown or overexpression efficiency was verified by Western blotting before subsequent functional assays.

For PI3K inhibition experiments, cells were treated with the PI3K inhibitor LY294002 (Abcam, ab120243) at 10 µM for 24 h before subsequent analyses. For PI3K activation rescue experiments, ANXA3-knockdown cells were treated with the PI3K activator 740Y-P (MedChemExpress, MCE, Cat. No. HY-P0175) at 10 µM for 24 h. For HIF-1α rescue experiments, ANXA3-knockdown cells were transfected with the HIF-1α overexpression plasmid. In combined-intervention experiments, 740Y-P-mediated PI3K activation was combined with HIF-1α knockdown, or HIF-1α overexpression was combined with LY294002-mediated PI3K inhibition, to evaluate the hierarchical relationship between PI3K activation and HIF-1α signaling. Pathway activation or inhibition was confirmed by Western blotting for PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway-related proteins before functional assays.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

To evaluate HIF-1α-mediated transcriptional activity, an HRE-driven dual-luciferase reporter assay was performed. The HRE-Luc luciferase reporter plasmid was used as the firefly luciferase reporter construct (Yeasen Biotechnology, Shanghai, China; 11593ES03). Cells were seeded into 24-well plates and transfected when they reached 60%–80% confluence. For each well, 300 ng of HRE-Luc reporter plasmid and 30 ng of Renilla luciferase internal control plasmid were co-transfected, together with the indicated target gene expression plasmid, empty vector, or corresponding negative control plasmid according to the experimental grouping. The total amount of transfected DNA was equalized among groups by supplementing with the corresponding empty vector. Transfection was performed using Lipofectamine™ 3000 Transfection Reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA; L3000001) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, plasmid DNA and transfection reagent were separately diluted in serum-free medium, mixed to allow formation of transfection complexes at room temperature, and then added to the cells. After 6 h of transfection, the medium was replaced with complete culture medium. At 24 h after transfection, cells were subjected to the indicated treatments according to the experimental design. After 24 h the culture medium was removed, and cells were lysed using the lysis buffer provided in the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Gene Assay Kit (Yeasen Biotechnology, Shanghai, China; 11402ES60). The cell lysates were then transferred to a 96-well plate for luminescence detection. Firefly luciferase substrate reagent was first added according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and firefly luciferase activity was measured. Subsequently, Renilla luciferase substrate was added, and Renilla luciferase activity was detected. HIF-1α-mediated transcriptional activity was calculated as the ratio of firefly luciferase activity to Renilla luciferase activity in the same sample to correct for differences in transfection efficiency and sample handling. The Firefly/Renilla ratio of each experimental group was then normalized to that of the corresponding control group. All experiments were independently repeated three times, with three technical replicates in each group.

Evaluation of self-renewal capacity and differentiation capacity

In our study, self-renewal capacity refers to the potential of sphere cells for continuous sphere formation, and differentiation capacity refers to their ability to transform into mature phenotypes. Both capacities were evaluated via serial sphere formation assays in medium supplemented with 10% FBS, with the expression of stem cell-related genes determined by RT-PCR. Epithelial cell adhesion molecule (EpCAM) was used as a reference gene reflecting cancer stem cell characteristics due to its universal expression pattern in CSCs.

Limiting dilution assays

A limiting dilution assay was performed to compare the in vivo tumorigenic capacity of different osteosarcoma cell populations. Male NOD-SCID mice aged 4–6 weeks were randomly assigned to groups (n = 5 per group) and maintained under specific pathogen-free conditions. After sorting and counting, cells were suspended in a 1:1 mixture of serum-free DMEM and Matrigel (BD Biosciences, USA) and serially diluted to 1 × 10⁶, 1 × 10⁵, and 1 × 10⁴ cells per inoculum. Cells at each dilution were injected into recipient mice, and tumor formation was monitored over a defined observation period. Tumor formation was considered positive when tumor volume reached ≥ 100 mm³. At the experimental endpoint, tumor incidence and tumor weight were recorded.

Colony formation assays

When the diameter of tumor spheres reached 100 μm, they were collected by low-speed centrifugation, dissociated with trypsin-EDTA (Invitrogen, USA), and mechanically disrupted using a pipette. The resulting cells were gently centrifuged to remove trypsin. Single cells were seeded in DMEM medium containing 10% FBS, in 6-well plates at a seeding density of 2000 gene-knockdown cells per well. siNC cells were seeded at the same density as the control group to evaluate colony-forming ability. After two weeks, the cells were stained with crystal violet (Sigma, USA), and the number of colonies (> 70 cells) was counted under a microscope to assess colony-forming ability.

Migration and invasion

To evaluate the migration and invasion abilities of ANXA3 knockdown or overexpressing cells, 500 µL of complete medium was added to the lower chamber of the transwell. ANXA3-knockdown and over-expressing cells were resuspended in serum-free medium and added to the upper chamber of the transwell. For invasion assays, the upper chamber was pre-coated with Matrigel, whereas no Matrigel coating was used for migration assays. The transwell was incubated in an incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ for 24 h. After discarding the medium in the chamber, the cells were fixed with paraformaldehyde, stained with crystal violet, and the samples were mounted using neutral balsam. Migrated or invaded cells were quantified using ImageJ software (NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA). For each membrane, cells were counted in at least three randomly selected microscopic fields, and the mean cell number was used for statistical analysis. All migration and invasion assays were performed at least three independent biological replicates.

Tumor formation assay

Tumorigenicity was defined as the ability of a certain number of cells to form tumor nodules in immunodeficient mice at specific time intervals. Male NOD-SCID mice aged 4 to 6 weeks were randomly divided into groups (5 mice per group) and housed in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) environment. After quantifying the cell number, the cells were suspended in a mixture of serum-free DMEM and Matrigel (BD Biosciences, USA), and then orthotopically injected into the tibial medullary cavity. The tumorigenicity assay was terminated four weeks after injection, and mice with no obvious tumor nodules at the injection site were considered negative. The size, weight, and incidence of tumors were recorded. A linear tool was used to measure tumor width (W) and length (L), and tumor volume was calculated using the formula: Volume [mm³] = L [mm] × W² [mm²] ÷ 2. All measurements were performed independently in triplicate, with results expressed as mean±standard deviation (SD).

Assessment of Lung Metastatic Nodules

To observe the effect of ANXA3 inhibition or overexpression on osteosarcoma lung metastasis in vivo, the constructed plasmids were transfected into osteosarcoma cell lines. Forty-eight hours after transfection, the cells were orthotopic intratibial injected into NOD-SCID mice. Mice were sacrificed 4 weeks after cell injection, and lungs were harvested from the same tumor-bearing mice and examined for spontaneous pulmonary metastatic nodules.

Immunohistochemistry and HE Stain

Tibias and femurs with primary tumors, as well as lung tissues containing metastatic tumors, were harvested. All tissues were formalin-fixed and then embedded in paraffin. After deparaffinization of the tumor tissues, antigen retrieval was performed using citrate buffer heated to 100 °C. When the buffer temperature dropped to 95 °C, the samples were added, and then the samples were allowed to cool to 55 °C in the buffer. Non-specific antigens were blocked with PBS containing 10% normal goat serum at room temperature for 30 min. Primary antibodies were added: Rabbit anti ANXA3 (1:1000, ab127924, Abcam), Rabbit anti Nanog (1:1000, ab214549, Abcam), Rabbit anti SOX2(1:1000, ab92494, Abcam), Rabbit anti CD133 (1:1000, ab222782, Abcam). Then the samples were incubated overnight in a humid chamber at 4 °C. After washing the samples with PBS, secondary antibodies were added for incubation. The antibodies were developed with peroxidase/DAB solution (Beyotime, Shanghai, China), followed by counterstaining with hematoxylin. Meanwhile, Hematoxylin and Eosin (HE) staining was performed on lung tissue sections with metastatic tumors, and the histological changes of the sections were observed under a light microscope. Immunohistochemical staining was quantified using ImageJ software (NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA). For each sample, positive staining was evaluated in at least three randomly selected microscopic fields, and the mean integrated optical density or positive staining area was calculated for statistical analysis.

ELISA

The secretion of VEGF was quantified using a human VEGF ELISA kit (YEASEN, Cat.97053ES96) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, U2OS cells under the indicated treatments were cultured for the specified time, and the supernatants were collected and centrifuged to remove cell debris. The clarified supernatants were then added to ELISA plates pre-coated with anti-VEGF antibody, followed by incubation with the detection antibody and enzyme conjugate. After substrate development, the absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader, and VEGF concentrations were calculated based on the standard curve.

Statistics

All experiments were performed with independent biological replicates. Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism software. Comparisons between two groups were performed using an unpaired Student’s t-test. Comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for multiple comparisons. Linear regression analysis was used where appropriate. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Our previous studies indicated that ANXA3 expression was significantly higher in osteosarcoma cell lines than in osteoblasts. Here, we found that osteosarcoma-derived spheroid cells exhibited higher ANXA3 expression compared to parental cells (Fig. 1A, ***P < 0.001). We then knocked down ANXA3 in the HOS cell line, which expresses relatively high levels of ANXA3, and overexpressed ANXA3 in the U2OS cell line, which expresses relatively low levels (Supplementary Fig. 1, **P < 0.01). Dynamic changes in ANXA3 mRNA expression were assessed over three serial passages; EpCAM (a common CSC marker) expression served as an internal control for CSC traits. The results showed that ANXA3 mRNA expression was significantly upregulated in sphere cells of both HOS and U2OS lines and decreased after differentiation induced by 10% FBS (Fig. 1D). Using a limiting dilution assay, target cells were inoculated into mice. Compared with control cells, ANXA3 knockdown significantly reduced the reduced the relative in vivo tumorigenic potential of osteosarcoma cells, as reflected by lower tumor formation rates and reduced tumor burden (Fig. 1E, *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01). The proportion of tumor-free mice was significantly higher in the knockdown group, and this difference became more pronounced over time. In summary, these data suggest that ANXA3 promotes self-renewal and in vivo tumor tumorigenic potential in osteosarcoma cells.

ANXA3 is essential for maintaining stem-like properties in osteosarcoma

qPCR results indicated that ANXA3 knockdown significantly reduced the mRNA expression levels of stemness genes, such as Nanog, OCT-1, and SOX2 (Fig. 2A, *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001), whereas ANXA3 overexpression had the opposite effect (Fig. 2B, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001). Western blot analysis further confirmed these findings (Fig. 2C). Concurrently, ANXA3 knockdown also significantly reduced the proportion of CD44+CD133+CD117+ cancer stem cells (Fig. 2D, ***P < 0.001). Notably, following ANXA3 knockdown in HOS cells, the mRNA expression levels of the stemness genes Nanog, OCT-1, and SOX2 decreased in a time-dependent manner (Fig. 2E, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001). These data demonstrate that ANXA3 is crucial for maintaining the stemness-related molecular phenotype in osteosarcoma.

ANXA3 influences the proliferation, migration, and chemoresistance of osteosarcoma cells

We then first evaluated the impact of ANXA3 on the migration and invasion of osteosarcoma cells in the absence of chemotherapy. ANXA3 knockdown significantly inhibited the migration and invasion capabilities of HOS cells (without chemotherapy), while its overexpression promoted these abilities (Fig. 3A-B, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001). Then, colony formation assays showed that ANXA3 knockdown significantly reduced the number of colonies formed and increased the sensitivity of osteosarcoma cells to Cisplatin and Doxorubicin, as indicated by lower IC50 values (Fig. 3C-D, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001). Conversely, ANXA3 overexpression significantly increased the number of colonies and decreased cellular sensitivity to these chemotherapeutic agents (Fig. 3E-F). These results suggest that ANXA3 drives the migratory and invasive capacities of tumor cells and influences their sensitivity to chemotherapy drugs.

ANXA3 promotes osteosarcoma metastasis in vivo

To elucidate the role of ANXA3 in the in vivo progression of osteosarcoma, we established osteosarcoma cell models with either ANXA3 knockdown or overexpression. These modified osteosarcoma cells were orthotopically implanted into the tibial medullary cavity of NOD-SCID mice to establish an intratibial osteosarcoma model, combined with a lung metastasis model, to comprehensively investigate its functions in tumor growth, stemness, and metastatic capacity.

In the orthotopic intratibial tumor model, tumor volume and weight were significantly reduced in the ANXA3 knockdown group compared to the control group (Fig. 4A, ***P < 0.001). Dynamic monitoring of tumor volume revealed that the growth rate in the ANXA3 knockdown group was significantly slower than that in the siNC group from day 9 to day 27 post-inoculation (Fig. 4B, ***P < 0.001). Conversely, ANXA3 overexpression significantly promoted tumor growth, as evidenced by markedly increased tumor volume, weight, and growth rate compared to the control group (Fig. 4C-D, ***P < 0.001). To further investigate the regulatory role of ANXA3 on cancer stem cell properties, we assessed the protein expression levels of ANXA3 and the cancer stem cell markers CD133, Nanog, and SOX2 in primary orthotopic tumor tissues via immunohistochemistry. The results showed that ANXA3 knockdown significantly downregulated ANXA3 protein expression in tumor tissues, concurrently suppressing the expression of CD133, Nanog, and SOX2 (Fig. 5A-B, ***P < 0.001). In contrast, ANXA3 overexpression significantly enhanced the expression of these proteins compared to the control group (Fig. 5C-D, ***P < 0.001).

ANXA3 promotes the stem-like phenotype in osteosarcoma cells by upregulating HIF-1α/VEGF

In solid tumors such as osteosarcoma, the hypoxic tumor microenvironment rapidly activates the transcription factor HIF-1α, which promotes tumor angiogenesis by directly transcriptionally upregulating Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF). Recent studies have further indicated that sustained high activation of the HIF-1α/VEGF axis is closely associated with cancer stem cell expansion and enhanced DNA damage repair capacity [21]. Based on this, we hypothesized that HIF-1α/VEGF might be the key downstream effector axis through which ANXA3 regulates the stem-like phenotype in osteosarcoma.

To test this hypothesis, we established osteosarcoma cell models with ANXA3 overexpression (oeANXA3) alone or in combination with HIF-1α knockdown (oeANXA3 + siHIF-1α). Western blot analysis confirmed (Fig. 7A-B) that compared to the NC group, the oeANXA3 group exhibited significantly increased protein levels of ANXA3, the cancer stem cell markers Nanog, SOX2, and Oct-1, as well as HIF-1α and VEGF (**P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001). Importantly, concomitant HIF-1α knockdown in ANXA3-overexpressing cells significantly reversed the upregulation of all these markers except ANXA3 itself (#P < 0.05, ##P < 0.01, ###P < 0.001). ELISA results further demonstrated (Fig. 7C) that VEGF secretion was significantly higher in the oeANXA3 group compared to the NC group (***P < 0.001), and this increase was markedly attenuated by HIF-1α knockdown (###P < 0.001). Furthermore, the tumor sphere formation assay (Fig. 7D) revealed a significant increase in the number of spheres in the oeANXA3 group (***P < 0.001), an effect that was significantly abolished by HIF-1α knockdown (###P < 0.001). Finally, chemosensitivity tests (Fig. 7E-F) showed that the IC₅₀ values for Cisplatin and Doxorubicin in the oeANXA3 group increased to 28.21 µM and 26.41 µM, respectively. In contrast, HIF-1α knockdown significantly reduced the IC₅₀ values to 14.94 µM and 13.49 µM, indicating that silencing HIF-1α reversed the ANXA3-mediated chemoresistant phenotype.

In summary, ANXA3 likely drives the acquisition of a stem-like phenotype and enhances chemoresistance in osteosarcoma cells by upregulating the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling axis.

ANXA3 upregulates HIF-1α expression via the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway and mediates the stem-like phenotype in osteosarcoma

Previous studies have established that PI3K directly upregulates HIF-1α expression by enhancing its transcription and stabilizing its protein level, and serves as a pivotal driver in maintaining and inducing the stem-like phenotype of tumor cells [22–23]. We therefore hypothesized that ANXA3 might upregulate HIF-1α by activating PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling, thereby mediating the stem-like phenotype in osteosarcoma cells. To test this hypothesis, we established oeANXA3 and siPI3K cell models. Western blot analysis revealed that, compared to the control group, the oeANXA3 group exhibited significantly increased phosphorylation levels of PI3K, Akt, and mTOR, alongside markedly upregulated protein expression of HIF-1α and VEGF (Fig. 8A-F, *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001). However, in the oeANXA3 + siPI3K combination group, the expression of these phospho-proteins and downstream targets was significantly suppressed, although ANXA3 protein levels remained high (#P < 0.05, ###P < 0.001). ELISA further confirmed that PI3K knockdown significantly reduced VEGF secretion (***P < 0.001); this reduction was effectively reversed in the oeANXA3 + siPI3K group ($$$P < 0.001).

To determine whether ANXA3 regulates HIF-1α transcriptional activity through PI3K signaling, we performed a dual-luciferase reporter assay in cells subjected to ANXA3 overexpression, HIF-1α silencing, and PI3K inhibition. The results showed that ANXA3 overexpression significantly enhanced HIF-1α transcriptional activity, whereas HIF-1α silencing markedly reduced it. Importantly, inhibition of PI3K significantly attenuated the ANXA3-induced increase in HIF-1α transcriptional activity, indicating that ANXA3 promotes HIF-1α transcriptional activation in a PI3K-dependent manner (Fig. 8H, ***P < 0.001). Consistent with the luciferase results, ANXA3 overexpression significantly increased the expression of Nanog, Sox2, and Oct-1, whereas HIF-1α silencing markedly abrogated these effects. Similarly, PI3K inhibition also significantly weakened the promotive effect of ANXA3 on the expression of these stemness-related genes (Fig. 8I-K, *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001). These findings further support that ANXA3 enhances osteosarcoma stem-like properties, at least in part, through PI3K-dependent activation of HIF-1α signaling.

To further elucidate the functional role of the ANXA3–PI3K/Akt/mTOR–HIF-1α/VEGF axis in maintaining the stem-like phenotype of osteosarcoma, we performed rescue experiments by pharmacologically activating PI3K signaling and overexpressing HIF-1α in ANXA3-knockdown osteosarcoma cells. Western blot results (Fig. 9A) showed that, in the context of ANXA3 knockdown, either 740Y-P-mediated PI3K activation or overexpression of HIF-1α partially restored the suppressed expression of the stemness markers Nanog, SOX2, and Oct-1 (*P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001). Concurrently, these interventions partially reversed the inhibitory effects of siANXA3 on tumor sphere formation, colony formation, migration, and invasion capabilities (Fig. 9B-F, ***P < 0.001). Conversely, the rescue effects conferred by 740Y-P-mediated PI3K activation were markedly attenuated when HIF-1α was simultaneously knocked down, while the rescue effects induced by HIF-1α overexpression were weakened by PI3K inhibition with LY294002 (##P < 0.01, ###P < 0.001; $$P < 0.01, $$$P < 0.001).Collectively, these results demonstrate that ANXA3 maintains osteosarcoma stem-like properties and invasive capacity, at least in part, through the PI3K/Akt/mTOR–HIF-1α/VEGF signaling axis.

Discussion

This study systematically elucidates the critical role of ANXA3 in the formation and maintenance of CSC-like traits in OS at cellular, molecular, and in vivo levels. Our results support that elevated expression of ANXA3 is closely associated with sustaining the stem-like phenotype of osteosarcoma stem cells (OSCs). Mechanistically, ANXA3 upregulates HIF-1α and VEGF expression in a PI3K/Akt/mTOR-dependent manner, thereby promoting OS cell survival and chemoresistance.

The annexin A (ANXA) family comprises highly conserved calcium-dependent phospholipid-binding proteins that are widely involved in membrane dynamics, cytoskeletal interactions, and cellular differentiation [24]. Dysregulation of ANXA family proteins has been implicated in the progression of various malignancies [25]. Notably, ANXA3 exhibits context-dependent roles in cancer, acting either as an oncogene or a tumor suppressor depending on the cancer type. In hepatocellular carcinoma, lung cancer, and ovarian cancer, ANXA3 functions as an oncogene by enhancing cell proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and angiogenesis [26]. Conversely, in prostate cancer, downregulation of ANXA3 has been linked to disease progression [27–28]. Although studies specifically addressing ANXA3 in sarcomas remain limited, these findings from other malignancies support a broader role for ANXA3 in tumor progression and aggressive biological behavior. Our previous studies have shown that ANXA3 is significantly upregulated in OS cell lines; however, its functional role in OS tumor biology has not been comprehensively explored. In this study, we found that ANXA3 expression was markedly elevated in tumor-sphere cultures compared to parental OS cells, and that overexpression of ANXA3 enhanced tumorsphere formation. Furthermore, limiting dilution assays revealed that ANXA3 knockdown in HOS cells significantly reduced in vivo tumorigenic potential and tumor burden in vivo. These findings suggest a strong association between ANXA3 expression and CSC-like characteristics in OS.

CSCs represent a subpopulation of tumor cells endowed with stem-like properties, including enhanced self-renewal capacity and the ability to differentiate into diverse non-stem cancer cell types [29], which are widely regarded as a key driver of tumor heterogeneity. A better understanding of CSC biology and strategies to selectively target them is critical for the development of more effective anticancer therapies. Previous studies have shown that ANXA3 is highly expressed in CD133⁺ liver cancer stem cell subsets, and both endogenous and secreted forms of ANXA3 promote the self-renewal ability of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) stem cells [30]. However, whether dysregulated ANXA3 expression contributes to the acquisition or maintenance of CSC-like traits in OS remained unclear.

In this study, we observed a positive correlation between ANXA3 expression and multiple stemness-related markers, including Nanog, Sox2, and Oct-1, in HOS and U2OS OS cells, with expression levels showing a time-dependent increase. ANXA3 knockdown reduced the proportion of CSC-like subpopulations (CD44⁺CD133⁺CD117⁺) and significantly impaired tumorsphere formation and self-renewal capacity, further supporting the critical role of ANXA3 in maintaining CSC traits in OS. Beyond tumor initiation and growth, CSCs are also known to possess enhanced migratory and invasive abilities and are considered major contributors to metastatic dissemination [31]. Additionally, due to their relatively quiescent state and elevated DNA repair capacity, CSCs are thought to be intrinsically resistant to conventional chemo- and radiotherapies [32]. Functionally, we found that ANXA3 overexpression promoted OS cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and colony formation. In orthotopic and metastatic models, ANXA3 knockdown markedly reduced lung nodule formation and histological damage. Moreover, previous studies in other tumor types have suggested that ANXA3 may contribute to resistance to chemotherapeutic agents such as cisplatin and doxorubicin, suggesting a potentially conserved role for ANXA3 in promoting chemoresistance. Collectively, these findings link ANXA3 to multiple malignant phenotypes—including stemness, invasion, metastasis, and drug resistance—and support its further investigation as a potential therapeutic target and biomarker in OS.

The hypoxic microenvironment is a hallmark of solid tumors and plays a crucial role in promoting stem-like properties by stabilizing HIF-1α [33]. HIF-1α can directly or indirectly upregulate a variety of embryonic stem cell markers, such as OCT4, SOX2, and NANOG, thereby enabling cancer cells to acquire and maintain a stem-like phenotype. Among HIF-1α’s downstream targets, VEGF is one of the most pivotal. The HIF-1α/VEGF signaling axis promotes tumor angiogenesis, ensuring oxygen and nutrient supply to support micro-tumor growth [34]. Notably, CSCs exploit this vascular network to facilitate invasion and metastasis [35]. Although elevated ANXA3 expression has been associated with HIF-1α accumulation in certain cancers, such as lung cancer, its direct impact on HIF-1α signaling and downstream functions remained uncharacterized. Here, gain-of-function experiments revealed that ANXA3 overexpression upregulated HIF-1α and VEGF expression in OS cells, accompanied by increased expression of stemness-related genes and enhanced cell migration and chemoresistance. Further mechanistic investigations suggested that ANXA3-mediated regulation of the HIF-1α/VEGF axis may be closely linked to the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway, which is known to govern critical cellular processes, including proliferation, survival, and metabolism [36]. Under normoxic conditions, PI3K/Akt activation stimulates mTOR signaling, and the mTORC1 complex enhances HIF-1α mRNA translation via phosphorylation of downstream effectors such as 4EBP1 and S6K1 [37]. Moreover, even in the presence of sufficient oxygen, the activated PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway can prevent HIF-1α ubiquitination and degradation, promoting its stabilization and accumulation [38]. Our data provide compelling evidence that ANXA3 functions upstream of this axis. ANXA3 knockdown suppressed the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway, leading to downregulation of HIF-1α and VEGF expression. Crucially, simultaneous inhibition of PI3K and HIF-1α reversed the expression of stemness genes and suppressed OS cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. These findings demonstrate, for the first time, the essential role of the ANXA3/PI3K/HIF-1α axis in maintaining osteosarcoma stemness and highlight the central regulatory role of this signaling cascade.

This study presents several novel insights into OS biology. First, we identify ANXA3 as a critical regulator of CSC-like traits in OS and establish its causal involvement in tumor proliferation, migration, drug resistance, and metastasis. Second, we propose a novel mechanistic model in which ANXA3 engages the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway to activate the HIF-1α/VEGF cascade, thereby promoting the maintenance of CSC properties and contributing to OS progression. This mechanistic axis not only expands our understanding of OS stemness regulation but also provides a theoretical foundation for developing multi-targeted therapies against ANXA3 and its downstream effectors, particularly HIF-1α. At the same time, the translational application of ANXA3 as a therapeutic target may face several challenges, including the lack of direct molecular interaction data, the absence of clinically validated targeting strategies, and the potential heterogeneity of ANXA3 expression across osteosarcoma samples. In addition, given that ANXA3 expression in our study was associated with stemness-related marker expression, invasion, metastasis, and chemoresistance, ANXA3 may also have potential as a prognostic biomarker in OS. However, this possibility should be validated in larger patient cohorts with well-annotated clinical follow-up data.

Also, several limitations of the present study warrant consideration. Firstly, we did not investigate the clinical expression pattern and prognostic relevance of ANXA3 in OS patient samples. Secondly, although our functional data support a role for ANXA3 in modulating the PI3K/Akt/mTOR–HIF-1α/VEGF axis, direct evidence of molecular interaction remains insufficient. For example, it remains unclear whether ANXA3 stabilizes HIF-1α by preventing its ubiquitin-mediated degradation, or whether ANXA3 forms protein complexes with PI3K to orchestrate downstream HIF-1α/VEGF signaling.

In summary, future studies should expand the sample size and clinical scope to evaluate the correlation between ANXA3 expression levels and patient outcomes. We also aim to assess the tumorigenic potential of ANXA3high CSC populations and employ co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) and mass spectrometry-based proteomic analyses to define the physical and functional interactions between ANXA3 and components of the PI3K–HIF-1α signaling cascade. These efforts may pave the way for more precise and effective therapeutic interventions targeting the ANXA3-driven oncogenic network in OS.
