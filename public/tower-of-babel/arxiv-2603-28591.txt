Universal Approximation Constraints of Narrow ResNets: The Tunnel Effect
Christian Kuehn, Sara-Viola Kuntz & Tobias Wöhrer

Abstract
We analyze the universal approximation constraints of narrow Residual Neural Networks (ResNets) both theoretically and numerically. For deep neural networks without input space augmentation, a central constraint is the inability to represent critical points of the input-output map. We prove that this has global consequences for target function approximations and show that the manifestation of this defect is typically a shift of the critical point to infinity, which we call the “tunnel effect” in the context of classification tasks. While ResNets offer greater expressivity than standard multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), their capability strongly depends on the signal ratio between the skip and residual channels. We establish quantitative approximation bounds for both the residual-dominant (close to MLP) and skip-dominant (close to neural ODE) regimes. These estimates depend explicitly on the channel ratio and uniform network weight bounds. Low-dimensional examples further provide a detailed analysis of the different ResNet regimes and how architecture-target incompatibility influences the approximation error.



Keywords: neural ODEs, deep learning, universal approximation, ResNets

Introduction

Universal approximation theory of neural networks has initially focused on shallow networks of arbitrary width
[
5
,
14
]
. However, the success of deep neural networks has shifted interest toward architectures characterized by bounded width and significant depth
[
25
]
. As a result, research in recent years focused on determining the expressive power of these practical implementations where layer capacity is limited.

Figure 1
:
The “tunnel effect” in non-augmented MLPs.

It is established that both feedforward neural networks and neural ODEs are unable to approximate certain functions well for narrow architectures where the natural input space is not augmented
[
20
,
6
]
. Mathematically, a central issue is the inability of these models’ input-output maps to express critical (or stationary) points. This topological constraint does not just have local consequences but limits global approximation and generalization capabilities. The classification of the two-dimensional circle toy dataset illustrates this limitation: a trained MLP with two neurons per layer and an arbitrary number of layers generates a “tunnel” in the prediction level sets which leads to unavoidable misclassifications, see Figure
1
. Since the model structure cannot express a critical point, it must shift the critical point to infinity which creates the barrier. Remarkably, the same observation holds for the structurally very different neural ODEs, where the input-output map is realized through continuous trajectories
[
6
,
4
]
.

The structure of Residual Neural Networks (ResNets)
[
12
]
can be understood as “in-between” classical feedforward neural networks (such as MLPs) and neural ODEs, and is able to resolve these topological constraints
[
23
]
. The idea of ResNets is to split each layer into an identity channel that simply advances the layer input unchanged and a residual channel that is a nonlinear transformation containing the trainable parameters. The layer update is formulated as
h_{l}=h_{l-1}+f(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
, such that the network learns the difference between the layer input and the layer output rather than the full transformation. This approach enables the training of very deep architectures as it provides stable gradient computations
[
9
,
35
]
. Beyond these remarkable optimization benefits, it is striking that the architecture also improves the expressivity of the input-output map and is able to embed critical points without additional input augmentation. However, we observe that this ability depends rather strongly on the choice of parameter initialization. Let us again consider the circle toy dataset. Training a ResNet of width two, moderate depth, and standard weight initialization for sigmoidal activations, the “tunnel effect” of Figure
1
typically does not appear. By contrast, when the parameters are rescaled closer to
0
at initialization, the “tunnel” appears after training.

This work proves that the “tunnel effect” appears whenever ResNets are unable to embed critical points but try to approximate target functions that contain such points. It further provides explicit estimates on the embedding restrictions of ResNets in the model parameter regimes close to MLPs and neural ODEs. This implies that ResNets that roughly maintain a balance between the identity channel and the transformation channel have advantageous expressivity in practical settings. We formulate these results by parameterizing the ratio between the identity and residual channel of each ResNet layer.

ResNet Embedding Capabilities Depending on Channel Ratio

Let us outline the embedding results in more detail by assuming ResNets of constant input width and network parameters
\theta
(weights and biases) that are restricted to a fixed parameter regime,
\theta\in\Theta
, where
\Theta
fulfills explicit (and reasonable in implementations) upper and lower bounds. In this context, we reformulate the ResNet layer iteration with a
skip parameter

\varepsilon>0
and a
residual parameter

\delta>0
as

h_{l}=\varepsilon h_{l-1}+\delta f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l}),\quad l\in\{1,\ldots,L\},

(1.1)

where the canonical choice of the residual function is given by

f_{l}(\theta_{l},h)=\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma(W_{l}h+b_{l})+\tilde{b}_{l},

(1.2)

with weight matrices
W_{l},\widetilde{W}_{l}
, bias vectors
\tilde{b}_{l},b_{l}
and a sigmoidal activation function
\sigma
.
Our goal is to show how the ResNets’ ability to express critical points depends on the ratio
\alpha:=\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}>0
.

Let us start with pointing out that we can always normalize (
1.1
) to
\alpha=1
by choosing
\widetilde{W}_{l}=\hat{W}_{l}\varepsilon/\delta
. But then, we naturally also have to rescale the considered model parameter regime
\Theta
when comparing embedding capabilities. Here we take the perspective of fixing
\Theta
and analyzing the expressivity for different regimes of
\alpha>0
.

•

0<\alpha\ll 1
: In the case that
\alpha
is sufficiently small, in relation to the Lipschitz constant
K_{f}
of the residual transformation
f_{l}
, we show that ResNets are unable to express critical points. This is true independently of the number of ResNet layers
L
. We also show that this specifically holds for ResNets that are Euler discretizations of continuous neural ODEs (where
\varepsilon=1
), as long as the step size
\alpha=1/L
is sufficiently small, which implies that the number of layers
L
is sufficiently large. This links the discrete ResNet expressivity to that of continuous neural ODEs and we provide explicit estimates depending on
\alpha
and the
\Theta
-bounds, see Figure
2
.

•

\alpha\gg 1
:
In the case that the ratio
\alpha
is sufficiently large in relation to the lower Lipschitz constant
k_{f}
, we show that the ability of ResNets to express critical points also breaks down. When setting
\varepsilon=0
in (
1.1
), the skip connection vanishes and we recover a standard MLP. For such MLPs without input space augmentation, it is known that critical points cannot be embedded, see Figure
2
. We extend these results to ResNets with
\alpha
sufficiently large and give explicit bounds dependent on the upper and lower bounds of the parameter space
\Theta
.

For canonical residual functions (
1.2
) with sigmoidal activation functions the constant
K_{f}
is determined by a uniform upper bound on the weights and biases, while
k_{f}
additionally relies on a uniform lower bound on the singular values of the matrix product
\widetilde{W}_{l}W_{l}
.
We reiterate that the expressivity results remain relevant for “standard” ResNets, where
\varepsilon=\delta=1
. In this case, the restrictions are expressed through the bounds of the parameter regime
\Theta
, necessary to obtain suitable Lipschitz constants
K_{f}
and
k_{f}
.

Our theoretical results do not focus on the trainability of ResNet through gradient-based algorithms, but rather on the fundamental limitations on expressivity depending on the parameter regimes. Techniques such as batch normalization are training-oriented modifications that employ related ideas. Their objective, however, is to ensure the reachability of optimal parameters assuming a well-posed setting. ResNets with rescaled channels were originally also investigated from such a trainability perspective
[
12
]
. To link the implications of our theoretical results to the parameter training of ResNet models, we provide numerical examples for the different parameter regimes.

Related Work

The work
[
23
]
proves universal approximation for ResNets with one-neuron hidden layer and ReLU activation. According to our definitions below, this corresponds to a non-augmented ResNet architecture of input width and hence is in agreement with our results. While the approximation scheme is constructive, the model depth and model parameters do not represent typically trained ResNets. In comparison, we assume bounded and smooth activation functions, and prove weight dependent estimates.

[
26
]
proves that if the weights of a ResNet are initialized from a (Lipschitz) continuous training parameter function, then the parameters maintain this property throughout a gradient descent optimization, provided the weights maintain a uniform upper bound through rescaling (weight clipping). Our results characterize the expressivity of different parameter regimes of ResNets which improves interpretability. While we observe the parameter regimes remain throughout training, the corresponding implicit regularization proofs are an open problem.

In
[
11
]
the expressivity of ReLU ResNets is investigated. The paper considers ResNets simplified to a linear structure and shows that if the spectral norm of each
A_{l}
is small,
x\mapsto Ax
cannot express non-degenerate critical points. We prove results of similar kind for general nonlinear ResNets.

The works
[
38
,
25
]
investigate the expressivity of ResNets but always assume augmented input spaces.

Structure of the Paper

In Section
2
, we introduce the considered ResNet architectures, derive their input gradients, and examine the global topological restrictions caused by the absence of critical points. In Section
3
, we link ResNets with small channel ratios to continuous neural ODEs, establishing explicit approximation errors and proving they share the same inability to embed critical points. In Section
4
, we analyze large channel ratios, showing that ResNets behave like perturbed feed-forward neural networks and similarly fail to express critical points. Finally, in Section
5
, we present numerical experiments on low-dimensional datasets to illustrate the theoretical constraints and visualize the resulting “tunnel effect”.

2
Critical Points in Residual Neural Networks

We begin in Section
2.1
to recall the universal approximation and universal embedding properties of continuous input–output mappings relevant to our setting. We show that, for such mappings, the inability to represent a critical point in a single output coordinate implies that the universal approximation property cannot hold.

Section
2.2
introduces the Residual Neural Network (ResNet) architectures considered in this work, whose layers depend on a skip parameter
\varepsilon
and a residual parameter
\delta
. Depending on the input and hidden dimensions, we distinguish between non-augmented and augmented architectures.

Section
2.3
states the specific assumptions on ResNets that are necessary for our analysis in the remaining part of this work.

In Section
2.4
, we derive the ResNet input gradient. In the non-augmented case, we identify conditions under which the gradient never vanishes. In particular, we prove that if the ratio
\alpha\coloneqq\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}
is sufficiently small or sufficiently large, the network admits no critical points.

Finally, Section
2.5
examines the topological consequences of the absence of critical points. For small
\alpha
, the networks can be interpreted as discretizations of neural ODEs, whereas for large
\alpha
, they act as perturbations of classical feed-forward neural networks (FNNs). In both regimes, we derive bounds on the distance to the respective limiting models and discuss the resulting topological restrictions.

2.1
Universal Approximation and Critical Points

Our goal in this work is to study the expressivity of ResNets depending on the specific architectural choices. For that purpose, we specialize the general definitions of universal approximation and universal embedding from
[
21
]
to the Euclidean setting, focusing on the space
C^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, of
k
-times continuously differentiable functions
f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}}
. We use the max-norm
\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{\infty}\coloneqq\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}\left\lvert y_{i}\right\rvert
for vectors
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and denote the induced sup-norm of
f\in C^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
on a set
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{X}
by

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{D}}\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\left\lVert f(x)\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n_{\textup{out}}\}}\left\lvert f_{i}(x)\right\rvert.

Definition 2.1

(Universal Approximation
[
21
]
)
.

A neural network family
\mathcal{N}=\{\Phi_{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}
with
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open and parameters
\theta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
has the universal approximation property with respect to the space
\left(C^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}}),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\right)
,
k\geq 0
, if for every
\varepsilon>0
, every compact subset
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{X}
and every function
\Psi\in C^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
, there exists
\theta\in\Theta
, such that
\left\lVert\Phi_{\theta}-\Psi\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}<\varepsilon
.

As we study in the upcoming sections the input-output map of different neural network architectures directly, it is useful to additionally introduce the stronger concept of universal embedding, which requires an exact representation of the target function.

Definition 2.2

(Universal Embedding
[
21
]
)
.

A neural network family
\mathcal{N}=\{\Phi_{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}
with
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open and parameters
\theta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
has the universal embedding property with respect to the space
C^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
k\geq 0
, if for every function
\Psi\in C^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
, there exists
\theta\in\Theta
, such that
\Phi_{\theta}(x)=\Psi(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

An important property characterizing the dynamics of the input-output map of neural networks
\Phi\in C^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
with input space
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open and output space
\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}}
, is the existence of critical points, i.e., zeros of the network input gradient
\nabla_{x}\Phi
. It is crucial not to confuse the input gradient
\nabla_{x}\Phi
with the parameter gradient
\nabla_{\theta}\Phi
, where
\theta\in\Theta
denotes all parameters of the considered neural network. The parameter gradient
\nabla_{\theta}\Phi
is needed for backpropagation algorithms in the training process of neural networks.

It is instructive to study the existence of critical points in the input-output map of different neural network architectures, as this has direct implications for their embedding and approximation capabilities
[
21
]
. The following theorem implies that neural network architectures in which at least one component map has no critical point cannot have the universal approximation property.

Theorem 2.3

(Maps without Critical Points are not Universal Approximators)
.

Consider a set of functions
\mathcal{S}\subset C^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, wherein for every map
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}
, there exists a component
i\in\{1,\ldots,n_{\textup{out}}\}
, such that
\nabla_{x}\Phi_{i}(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
Then the set
\mathcal{S}
cannot have the universal approximation property with respect to the space
\left(C^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}}),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\right)
for every
k\geq 0
.

Proof.

The statement follows directly by generalizing
[
21
, Theorem 2.6]
from scalar maps to multiple output components: by the respective theorem, there exists a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{X}
with non-empty interior
\text{int}(\mathcal{K})
, such that the sup-norm between the quadratic function

\Psi_{z}:\mathcal{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad\Psi_{z}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\textup{in}}}(x_{j}-z_{j})^{2}\quad\text{with}\quad z\in\text{int}(\mathcal{K})

(2.1)

and any scalar map without any critical point cannot be made arbitrarily small. Consequently, the quadratic function
\Psi_{z}
also cannot be approximated with arbitrary precision by any of the considered component maps
\Phi_{i}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\nabla_{x}\Phi_{i}(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
, i.e.,
\left\lVert\Phi_{i}-\Psi_{z}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}\geq\mu
for some
\mu>0
. Hence, every map
\Psi\in C^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
with
i
-th component map
\Psi_{i}|_{\mathcal{K}}=\Psi_{z}
as defined in (
2.1
) cannot be approximated with arbitrary precision by any map
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}
on
\mathcal{K}
, where the
i
-th component fulfills the given assumptions.
∎

As Theorem
2.3
considers maps in which single output components cannot have any critical points, we can, without loss of generality, restrict our upcoming analysis to scalar neural network architectures with
n_{\textup{out}}=1
.
In the recent work
[
21
]
, the expressivity of multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) and neural ODEs is studied by characterizing the existence and regularity of critical points. One of the main results shows that non-augmented MLPs and non-augmented neural ODEs cannot have critical points and hence lack the universal approximation property (see also Section
3.3
and Section
4.3
). In the augmented case, the universal approximation property of MLPs and neural ODEs is well established in the literature
[
6
,
14
,
15
,
18
,
29
,
37
]
.
In the following sections, we first introduce ResNet architectures and then study their expressivity using Theorem
2.3
.

2.2
Residual Neural Networks

Residual neural networks (ResNets) are feedforward neural networks structured in layers
h_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
,
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
, consisting of
n_{\textup{hid}}
nodes each. Given an initial layer
h_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
, the hidden layers are iteratively updated by

h_{l}=\varepsilon h_{l-1}+\delta f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l}),\qquad l\in\{1,\ldots,L\},

(2.2)

with a (typically nonlinear)
residual function

f_{l}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
and
hidden parameters

\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}
, where
\Theta_{l}
denotes the set of parameters of layer
l
and
\Theta=\Theta_{1}\times\ldots\times\Theta_{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
the total parameter space of the ResNet. The layer update rule (
2.2
) includes two terms: a skip connection scaled by the
skip parameter

\varepsilon>0
, and a residual term weighted by the
residual parameter

\delta>0
.

In contrast to classical feed-forward neural networks, corresponding to the case
\varepsilon=0
and
\delta>0
in (
2.2
) (cf. Section
4
), the ResNet update rule contains the linear term
\varepsilon h_{l-1}
. It is called a skip- or shortcut connection (cf.
[
12
]
), as it allows the layer input to bypass the transformation of the residual function, see Figure
3
3(a)
for a visualization. The residual function
f_{l}
can be an arbitrary map, but typical choices include

f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})\coloneqq\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma_{l}(W_{l}h_{l-1}+b_{l})+\tilde{b}_{l}=\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma_{l}(a_{l})+\tilde{b}_{l}

(2.3)

for
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
with parameters
\theta_{l}\coloneqq(W_{l},\widetilde{W}_{l},b_{l},\tilde{b}_{l})
consisting of weight matrices
W_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}\times n_{\textup{hid}}}
,
\widetilde{W}_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}\times m_{l}}
, and biases
b_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}}
,
\tilde{b}_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
. The map
\sigma_{l}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called an
activation function
, applied component-wise to the
pre-activated states

a_{l}\coloneqq W_{l}h_{l-1}+b_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}}
, as visualized in Figure
3
3(b)
. By a slight abuse of notation, we also write
\sigma_{l}
for the component-wise extension
\sigma_{l}:\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}}
as in (
2.3
). The intended meaning will be clear from the argument. Typical choices for the activation function
\sigma_{l}
include
\tanh
,
\operatorname{sigmoid}(y)=(1+e^{-y})^{-1}
or
\operatorname{ReLU}(y)=\max\{0,y\}
. The hidden dimension
n_{\textup{hid}}
is constant across the layers of the ResNet, whereas the dimension
m_{l}
of the pre-activated state
a_{l}
, which also controls the number of parameters used in layer
h_{l}
, can vary.

To be flexible in the input and output dimensions of ResNets, two additional transformations are applied before the layer
h_{0}
and after the layer
h_{L}
, resulting in the input-output map

\Phi:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}},\;\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}},\qquad\Phi(x)=\tilde{\lambda}(h_{L}(\lambda(x))),

(2.4)

with
input transformation

\lambda:\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
,
output transformation

\tilde{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}}
and composite map
h_{L}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
, which maps the
transformed input

h_{0}=\lambda(x)
to the last hidden layer
h_{L}
via the iterative update rule (
2.2
). For the ResNet architecture (
2.4
), we call
x\in\mathcal{X}
the
input
,
h_{0}=\lambda(x)
the
transformed input
,
h_{1},\ldots,h_{L}
the
hidden layers
, and
\Phi(x)
the
output
of the neural network. Often, the transformations
\lambda
and
\tilde{\lambda}
are chosen to be affine linear, but nonlinear functions are also possible. For example, for classification tasks with
n_{\textup{out}}=1
, the output is often normalized to a probability, i.e.,
\Phi(x)\in[0,1]
.

l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
: residual functions
f_{l}(\cdot,\theta_{l})\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
for each fixed
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}
.

In the case of
f_{l}
of the form (
2.3
), the condition
f_{l}(\cdot,\theta_{l})\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
is equivalent to
\sigma_{l}\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
for
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
. We call the corresponding ResNets
canonical
and denote the subset of
canonical ResNet architectures
by
\textup{RN}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})\subset\textup{RN}_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})\subset C^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
.

The regularity of ResNets
\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
k\geq 0
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, follows directly from the regularity of the residual functions
f_{l}
or the activation functions
\sigma_{l}
, respectively.

Remark 2.5
.

Throughout this work, we assume strictly positive parameters
\varepsilon
and
\delta
to simplify the notation. This choice is based on typical ResNets (where
\varepsilon=\delta=1
) and their interpretation as neural ODE discretizations (where
\varepsilon=1
,
\delta>0
). As most of the upcoming results only depend on the magnitude of the ratio
\alpha\coloneqq\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}
, they also extend to the case
\alpha<0
, by considering the absolute value
\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert
in the respective bounds.

Depending on the input dimension
n_{\textup{in}}
and the hidden dimension
n_{\textup{hid}}
, we distinguish between non-augmented and augmented ResNet architectures.

Definition 2.6

(ResNet Classification)
.

The class of ResNets
\textup{RN}_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
k\geq 0
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, is subdivided as follows:

•

Non-augmented ResNet

\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\textup{N}}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
: it holds
n_{\textup{in}}\geq n_{\textup{hid}}
.

•

Augmented ResNet

\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\textup{A}}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
: it holds
n_{\textup{in}}<n_{\textup{hid}}
.

For canonical ResNets
\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
, we analogously denote non-augmented architectures by
\textup{RN}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma,\textup{N}}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
and augmented architectures by
\textup{RN}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma,\textup{A}}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
.

The concept of non-augmented and augmented ResNets is visualized in Figure

2.4
and Section
2.5

2.3
. Before we calculate the ResNet input gradient in Section

2.3
Assumptions on Non-Augmented Architectures

In this section, we state the assumptions used for the analysis of non-augmented ResNet architectures in Section
2.4
and Section
2.5
. These conditions also appear in the study of ResNets related to neural ODEs (Section
3
) and to feed-forward neural networks (Section
4
). The assumptions are introduced below for reference; each of the upcoming theorems explicitly states which of the assumptions are required.

Assumptions on the Activation Functions

Canonical ResNets
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{k}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
k\geq 0
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, with residual functions as defined in (
2.3
), depend on the component-wise applied activation functions
\sigma_{l}\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
,
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
. The same dependence applies to multilayer perceptrons, which are introduced and analyzed in Section
4
. In the following, we state three assumptions, all of which are satisfied by standard sigmoidal nonlinearities such as
\tanh
and
\operatorname{sigmoid}(y)=(1+e^{-y})^{-1}
, but exclude activation functions of ReLU-type. In the literature, ReLU activation functions are often excluded if smoothness properties such as the continuity of the derivative is needed (cf.
[
26
]
).

Assumption (A1)

(Lipschitz Continuous Activation)
.

The activation functions
\sigma_{l}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
are uniformly globally Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
K_{\sigma}>0
. In particular, if
\sigma_{l}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, Lipschitz continuity is equivalent to
\left\lVert\sigma_{l}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathbb{R}}\leq K_{\sigma}
for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
.

Assumption (A2)

(Bounded Activation)
.

The activation functions
\sigma_{l}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
are uniformly bounded, i.e., there exists a constant
S>0
such that
\left\lVert\sigma_{l}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathbb{R}}\leq S
for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
.

Assumption (A3)

(Strictly Monotone Activation)
.

The activation functions
\sigma_{l}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
are strictly monotone for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
. In particular, if
\sigma_{l}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, strict monotonicity is equivalent to
\left\lvert\sigma_{l}^{\prime}(y)\right\rvert>0
for every
y\in\mathbb{R}
and all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
.

Remark 2.7
.

The three assumptions on the activation functions are standard in the analytical study of deep neural networks. The global Lipschitz continuity of Assumption
(A1)
ensures stability of the input-output mapping. By the mean value theorem, the upper bound of
\left\lVert\sigma_{l}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathbb{R}}
defines a Lipschitz constant of the activation function. The boundedness of the activation function in Assumption
(A2)
is relevant when estimating the distance between ResNets and neural ODEs or ResNets and MLPs. The monotonicity Assumption
(A3)
guarantees non-degeneracy of the layer-wise Jacobians and is essential in our analysis of critical points.

Assumptions on the Parameters

In the following, we formulate two assumptions on the parameters appearing in the considered neural networks. For a parameter tuple
\theta=(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})
consisting of matrices or vectors
A_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{a_{j}\times b_{j}}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
we consider the standard Euclidean norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{2}
, the max-norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}
and their induced matrix norms defined by

\left\lVert A_{j}\right\rVert_{q}\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{b_{j}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\left\lVert A_{j}x\right\rVert_{q}}{\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{q}},\qquad q\in\{2,\infty\}.

To ensure that the upcoming uniform parameter bound applies to each component of the tuple
\theta
, we define the norm of
\theta
as the maximum of the individual norms, i.e.,
\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{q}\coloneqq\max_{j\in\{1,\dots,k\}}\left\lVert A_{j}\right\rVert_{q}
. Since different architectures involve different parameters, their notation and dimensions are specified in the respective theorems in which they are used.

Assumption (B1)

(Bounded Parameters)
.

For a set of parameters
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
, all parameters are uniformly bounded in the max-norm or the Euclidean norm, i.e., there exist constants
\omega_{\infty}\geq 0
or
\omega_{2}\geq 0
, such that it holds
\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\omega_{\infty}
or
\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{2}\leq\omega_{2}
respectively for all
\theta\in\Theta
.

Remark 2.8
.

•

Since all norms on finite-dimensional spaces are equivalent, uniform boundedness in any norm is sufficient for Assumption
(B1)
.

•

For a fixed neural network with a finite number of parameters
\theta
, the uniform upper bound is trivially satisfied.

•

The assumption on bounded parameters, especially weight matrices, is standard in the analysis and implementation of deep neural networks. It helps prevent exploding gradients during training and ensures reasonable global Lipschitz constants of the considered networks
[
28
,
32
,
27
]
. In practice, many learning algorithms enforce these bounds implicitly or explicitly through regularization techniques, such as weight decay
[
8
]
or spectral regularization
[
36
]
.

Assumption (B2)

(Full Rank Weight Matrices)
.

For a set of parameters
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
, all weight matrices
W\in\mathbb{R}^{a\times b}
contained in
\Theta
have full rank, i.e.,
\textup{rank}(W)=\min\{a,b\}
.

Remark 2.9
.

The full-rank assumption is generic, as by
[
21
, Lemma 3.6]
, the subset of singular weight matrices has Lebesgue measure zero. This assumption is especially relevant to prove the non-existence of critical points in certain non-augmented neural network architectures.

Assumptions on the Input and Output Transformations

We make the following assumption on the input and output transformations
\lambda
and
\tilde{\lambda}
of the considered non-augmented network architectures with
n_{\textup{in}}\geq n_{\textup{hid}}
.

Assumption (C1)

(Non-Singular Input and Output Transformation)
.

The input and output transformations
\lambda\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
and
\tilde{\lambda}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
with
n_{\textup{in}}\geq n_{\textup{hid}}
fulfill the following:

•

The Jacobian matrix
\partial_{x}\lambda(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}\times n_{\textup{in}}}
has full rank
n_{\textup{hid}}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
.

•

The Jacobian matrix
\partial_{y}{\tilde{\lambda}}(y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}\times n_{\textup{hid}}}
has full rank
\min\{n_{\textup{hid}},n_{\textup{out}}\}
for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
.

Remark 2.10
.

Assumption
(C1)
is satisfied if the input and output transformations
\lambda
and
\tilde{\lambda}
have the typical form of the residual function as in (
2.3
), such that

\lambda(x)=\widetilde{W}_{0}\sigma_{0}(W_{0}x+b_{0})+\tilde{b}_{0},\qquad\tilde{\lambda}(y)=\widetilde{W}_{L+1}\sigma_{L+1}(W_{L+1}y+b_{L+1})+\tilde{b}_{L+1},

(2.5)

with a component-wise applied activation function
\sigma
that satisfies Assumption
(A3)
and weight matrices that fulfill Assumption
(B2)
, cf.
[
21
, Lemma C.1]
.

2.4
Existence of Critical Points

To understand the existence of critical points in ResNet architectures, we derive in the following the input gradient of ResNets
\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open. Due to Theorem
2.3
, we can without loss of generality restrict our analysis to single output components, or equivalently, scalar neural networks. The upcoming proposition applies to both non-augmented and augmented ResNets.

Proposition 2.11

(ResNet Input Gradient)
.

The input gradient of a scalar ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
at
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, with iterative update rule (
2.2
) is given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\Phi(x)

\displaystyle=\Bigl[\partial_{h_{L}}\tilde{\lambda}(h_{L})\cdot(\varepsilon\cdot\textup{Id}_{n_{\textup{hid}}}+\delta\cdot\partial_{h_{L-1}}f_{L}(h_{L-1},\theta_{L}))\cdots\Bigr.

(2.6)

\displaystyle\cdots\Bigl.(\varepsilon\cdot\textup{Id}_{n_{\textup{hid}}}+\delta\cdot\partial_{h_{0}}f_{1}(h_{0},\theta_{1}))\cdot\partial_{x}\lambda(x)\Bigr]^{\top}\quad\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}

with Jacobian matrix
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
of the residual function
f_{l}
with respect to the layer
h_{l-1}
.

For canonical ResNets
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
with the explicit residual function in (
2.3
), the Jacobian matrix
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
of the ResNet input gradient is given by

\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})=\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})W_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}\times n_{\textup{hid}}},\qquad l\in\{1,\ldots,L\},

with diagonal matrix
\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})\coloneqq\textup{diag}(\sigma_{l}^{\prime}([a_{l}]_{1}),\ldots,\sigma_{l}^{\prime}([a_{l}]_{m_{l}}))
, where
a_{l}\coloneqq W_{l}h_{l-1}+b_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}}
.

Proof.

By the multi-dimensional chain rule applied to ResNets as defined in (
2.4
), it holds that

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\Phi(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial h_{L}}\cdot\frac{\partial h_{L}}{\partial h_{L-1}}\cdots\frac{\partial h_{1}}{\partial h_{0}}\cdot\frac{\partial h_{0}}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=\partial_{h_{L}}\tilde{\lambda}(h_{L})\cdot(\varepsilon\cdot\textup{Id}_{n_{\textup{hid}}}+\delta\cdot\partial_{h_{L-1}}f_{L}(h_{L-1},\theta_{L}))\cdots

\displaystyle\hskip 46.94704pt\cdots(\varepsilon\cdot\textup{Id}_{n_{\textup{hid}}}+\delta\cdot\partial_{h_{0}}f_{1}(h_{0},\theta_{1}))\cdot\partial_{x}\lambda(x)\quad\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times n_{\textup{in}}}.

The first result follows by taking the transpose, as
\nabla_{x}\Phi(x)=[\partial_{x}\Phi(x)]^{\top}
.

For canonical ResNets
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{k}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
with the explicit residual function in (
2.3
), the Jacobian matrix
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
with respect to the layer
h_{l-1}
is given by

\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})=\partial_{h_{l-1}}\left(\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma_{l}(W_{l}h_{l-1}+b_{l})+\tilde{b}_{l}\right)=\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})W_{l}\quad\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}\times n_{\textup{hid}}},

for
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
, with diagonal matrix
\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})\coloneqq\textup{diag}(\sigma_{l}^{\prime}([a_{l}]_{1}),\ldots,\sigma_{l}^{\prime}([a_{l}]_{m_{l}}))
, as the activation function is applied component-wise to the pre-activated state
a_{l}\coloneqq W_{l}h_{l-1}+b_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}}
.
∎

To apply Theorem
2.3
to residual architectures, we formulate the criterion for non-vanishing gradients via the rank of the layer-wise Jacobians. The following lemma shows that the rank
solely depends on the ratio

\alpha\coloneqq\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}
of the residual parameter
\delta
and the skip parameter
\varepsilon
and not on their individual size.

Lemma 2.12

(Layer-Wise Jacobians)
.

Given
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
, parameters
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, and a residual function
f_{l}(\cdot,\theta_{l})\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
with
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}
, the layer-wise Jacobian at
h_{l-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}

D_{l}\coloneqq\varepsilon\cdot\textup{Id}_{n_{\textup{hid}}}+\delta\cdot\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}\times n_{\textup{hid}}}

has full rank if and only if
-\frac{1}{\alpha}=-\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}
is not an eigenvalue of the Jacobian matrix
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
.

Proof.

The statement follows directly from the definition of the eigenvalues of
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
after rescaling the matrix
D_{l}
by
\frac{1}{\delta}
.
∎

In the case of non-augmented ResNets
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, Lemma
2.12
allows us to formulate conditions on the transformations
\lambda
,
\tilde{\lambda}
and the parameters
\varepsilon
and
\delta
, under which the gradient
\nabla_{x}\Phi(x)
never vanishes for any
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

Proposition 2.13

(Non-Augmented ResNets without Critical Points)
.

Let
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open,
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, be a scalar non-augmented ResNet, which fulfills:

•

-\frac{1}{\alpha}=-\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}
is not an eigenvalue of the Jacobian matrix
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
,
h_{l-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
and parameters
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}
.

•

The input and output transformations
\lambda
and
\tilde{\lambda}
fulfill Assumption
(C1)
.

Then
\Phi
cannot have any critical points, i.e.,
\nabla_{x}\Phi(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.12
, all layer-wise Jacobians
D_{l}\coloneqq\varepsilon\cdot\textup{Id}_{n_{\textup{hid}}}+\delta\cdot\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
have full rank for all
h_{l-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
and
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}
. As the ResNet
\Phi
is scalar and non-augmented, it holds
n_{\textup{in}}\geq n_{\textup{hid}}\geq n_{\textup{out}}=1
, such that the dimensions in the matrix product (
2.6
) are monotonically decreasing. Together with Assumption
(C1)
, it follows that the gradient
\nabla_{x}\Phi(x)
always has full rank
1
, uniformly in
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
∎

Depending on the ratio
\alpha\coloneqq\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}
between the skip parameter
\varepsilon
and the residual parameter
\delta
, we analyze in Section
3.3
and Section
4.3
when the first assumption of Proposition
2.13
is fulfilled. We identify two parameter regimes,
0<\alpha\ll 1
and
\alpha\gg 1
, for which non-augmented ResNets cannot have any critical points, as visualized in Figure
2
. In these parameter regimes, ResNets lack the universal approximation property as implied by Theorem
2.3
. Consequently, we extend the results of
[
21
]
, regarding the non-existence of critical points in non-augmented neural ODEs and non-augmented MLPs, to ResNets.

In intermediate parameter regimes of
\alpha
, non-augmented ResNets can have the universal approximation property, as demonstrated in
[
22
]
for the standard case
\varepsilon=\delta=1
. In contrast, for augmented ResNet architectures, no equivalent statement to Proposition
2.13
about the non-existence of critical points exists. Instead, it follows analogously to
[
21
, Theorem 3.18]
that due to dimension augmentation, critical points can exist in augmented architectures even when all other assumptions of Proposition
2.13
are fulfilled.

While the previous results establish the absence of critical points in specific regimes, we now turn to a global topological perspective. In the following, we quantify the expressivity of non-augmented ResNets by measuring their distance to function classes that are already known to lack universal approximation.

2.5
Global Topological Restrictions

In Section
2.4
, we discussed that non-augmented ResNets cannot have critical points in the parameter regimes
0<\alpha\ll 1
and
\alpha\gg 1
. Complementing that direct analysis, we characterize the expressivity of ResNets by comparing them to reference networks
\overline{\Phi}
without critical points, such as non-augmented neural ODEs (cf. Theorem
3.9
) and non-augmented FNNs (cf. Theorem
4.16
).
In Section
3.2
, we show that the distance between ResNets with
\varepsilon=1
and neural ODEs scales linearly in the residual parameter
\delta
. Similarly, in Section
4.2
, we establish that for fixed
\delta>0
and small
\varepsilon>0
, the distance between ResNets and FNNs scales asymptotically linearly in the skip parameter
\varepsilon
.

We divide the upcoming analysis into two steps. First, we establish the topological restrictions of a continuously differentiable map
\overline{\Phi}
lacking critical points. Second, we introduce a uniform distance bound to show that these topological restrictions extend to any continuous neural network
\Phi
that closely approximates
\overline{\Phi}
.

2.5.1
Topological Restrictions of Maps Without Critical Points

We first discuss the topological implications for a reference network
\overline{\Phi}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, under the assumption that
\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. To study how this property restricts the expressivity of the network, we introduce level sets, sub-level sets, and super-level sets.

Definition 2.14

(Level Sets)
.

Given
f\in C^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
, we define the following sets for
c\in\mathbb{R}
:

•

Level set
S_{c}(f)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{X}\mid f(x)=c\}
,

•

Sub-level set
S_{c}^{\leq}(f)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{X}\mid f(x)\leq c\}
, strict sub-level set
S_{c}^{<}(f)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{X}\mid f(x)<c\}
,

•

Super-level set
S_{c}^{\geq}(f)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{X}\mid f(x)\geq c\}
, strict super-level set
S_{c}^{>}(f)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{X}\mid f(x)>c\}
.

The absence of critical points has direct implications on the compactness of the closed sub- and super-level sets. Although the following lemma relates to classical Morse theory, we prove it directly using elementary calculus.

Lemma 2.15

(Non-Compactness of Sub- and Super-Level Sets)
.

Let
\overline{\Phi}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
,
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, with
\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Then for every
c\in(\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\overline{\Phi}(x),\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\overline{\Phi}(x))
, the sub- and super-level sets
S_{c}^{\leq}(\overline{\Phi})
and
S_{c}^{\geq}(\overline{\Phi})
are non-compact in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
.

Proof.

As
\overline{\Phi}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
has no critical points, it is non-constant around each
x\in\mathcal{X}
, such that the interval
(\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\overline{\Phi}(x),\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\overline{\Phi}(x))
is non-empty. Hence, for each
c\in(\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\overline{\Phi}(x),\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\overline{\Phi}(x))
, the strict sub- and super-level sets
S_{c}^{<}(\overline{\Phi})
and
S_{c}^{>}(\overline{\Phi})
are non-empty.
Assume by contradiction that the sub-level set
S_{c}^{\leq}(\overline{\Phi})
is compact in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
. By the extreme value theorem (cf.
[
31
]
), the continuous function
\overline{\Phi}
attains its minimum on
S_{c}^{\leq}(\overline{\Phi})
at some point
x_{\min}
. Since the strict sub-level set
S_{c}^{<}(\overline{\Phi})
is non-empty, there exists some
y
with
\overline{\Phi}(y)<c
, which implies the minimum must satisfy
\overline{\Phi}(x_{\min})\leq\overline{\Phi}(y)<c
.
As
\overline{\Phi}
is continuous and
\mathcal{X}
is open, the strict sub-level set
S_{c}^{<}(\overline{\Phi})
is an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
and is contained in the interior
\text{int}(S_{c}^{\leq}(\overline{\Phi}))
. Hence, the minimum is attained in the interior
x_{\min}\in\text{int}(S_{c}^{\leq}(\overline{\Phi}))
. A necessary condition for the existence of a local minimum
x_{\text{min}}
in the interior of a domain for a continuously differentiable function is that
\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x_{\text{min}})=0

[
7
]
. This contradicts the assumption
\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
, thus,
S_{c}^{\leq}(\overline{\Phi})
cannot be compact in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
.

The statement for the super-level set
S_{c}^{\geq}(\overline{\Phi})
follows analogously by replacing the considered minimum with the maximum
x_{\max}
attained in the interior
\text{int}(S_{c}^{\geq}(\overline{\Phi}))
.
∎

Lemma
2.15
has direct implications on classification tasks, where decision boundaries are defined as level sets. In the following, we state the classical binary classification problem in an abstract form (cf.
[
2
]
). Throughout this section, we consider a compact domain
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
as our reference frame, since practical classification datasets are bounded.

Definition 2.16

(Binary Classification Problem)
.

For a compact set
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
and
c_{0},c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
with
c_{0}<c_{1}
consider a given dataset

A_{c_{0},c_{1}}\coloneqq\left\{(x_{i},y_{i})_{i=1}^{N_{\textup{data}}}\mid x_{i}\in\mathcal{K},y_{i}\in\{c_{0},c_{1}\}\right\}.

A function
\Phi\in C^{0}(\mathcal{K},\mathbb{R})
successfully classifies
A_{c_{0},c_{1}}
if there exists
c^{\ast}\in(c_{0},c_{1})
, such that

\displaystyle A_{c_{0}}\coloneqq\left\{(x_{i})_{i=1}^{N_{\textup{data}}}\mid(x_{i},c_{0})\in A_{c_{0},c_{1}}\right\}\subset S_{c^{\ast}}^{<}(\Phi),

\displaystyle A_{c_{1}}\coloneqq\left\{(x_{i})_{i=1}^{N_{\textup{data}}}\mid(x_{i},c_{1})\in A_{c_{0},c_{1}}\right\}\subset S_{c^{\ast}}^{>}(\Phi).

The level set
S_{c^{\ast}}(\Phi)
is called the decision boundary of
\Phi
.

For a given dataset, the goal of a classification problem is to classify correctly as many data points as possible. As we study in this section the topological restrictions induced by neural networks without critical points independently of a specific dataset, we focus on the topology of level sets, especially the decision boundary. For a reference map
\overline{\Phi}
without critical points, the topology of its decision boundary is strictly constrained. We separate the one-dimensional case from the higher-dimensional case
n_{\textup{in}}\geq 2
, as the boundary of a compact set
\mathcal{K}
is disconnected in one dimension but can be connected in higher dimensions, leading to different topological restrictions on the decision boundary.

One-Dimensional Case

In the one-dimensional case
n_{\textup{in}}=1
, Lemma
2.15
is equivalent to the fact that all one-dimensional maps without critical points are strictly monotone. Naturally, it follows that strictly monotone maps cannot satisfactorily approximate non-monotone target functions. This fact can also be formulated from the perspective of binary classifications. For a strictly increasing map
\overline{\Phi}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
and any
c^{\ast}\in\overline{\Phi}(\mathcal{X})
, there exists a unique
x_{c^{\ast}}\in\mathcal{X}
with
\overline{\Phi}(x_{c^{\ast}})=c^{\ast}
, and the strict level sets take the form

S_{c^{\ast}}^{<}(\overline{\Phi})=(-\infty,x_{c^{\ast}})\cap\mathcal{X},\qquad S_{c^{\ast}}(\overline{\Phi})=\{x_{c^{\ast}}\},\qquad S_{c^{\ast}}^{>}(\overline{\Phi})=(x_{c^{\ast}},\infty)\cap\mathcal{X}.

(2.7)

This implies that for such functions
\overline{\Phi}
, any dataset whose classes
A_{c_{0}}
and
A_{c_{1}}
cannot be separated by two disjoint intervals, cannot be successfully classified. Therefore,
\overline{\Phi}
can only separate data that is split into two disjoint intervals.

Higher-Dimensional Case

If the boundary
\partial\mathcal{K}
is connected, Lemma
2.15
has direct implications for nested datasets, such as the two-dimensional circle dataset, where one class is entirely surrounded by another. To perfectly classify such a dataset, the decision boundary
S_{c^{\ast}}(\overline{\Phi})
would need to form a closed curve (or a hypersphere for
n_{\textup{in}}>2
), contained entirely within the interior of the compact domain
\mathcal{K}
. However, this would create a strictly bounded, and therefore compact, sub- or super-level set
S_{c^{\ast}}^{\leq}(\overline{\Phi})
or
S_{c^{\ast}}^{\geq}(\overline{\Phi})
, which directly contradicts Lemma
2.15
.

As we show in the upcoming Theorem
2.18
, this implies that any decision boundary attempting to separate a nested dataset is mathematically forced to intersect the domain boundary
\partial\mathcal{K}
. When minimizing the empirical classification error, the natural geometric compromise is to form a “tunnel”, a continuous, narrow region of the inner class extending to the boundary
\partial\mathcal{K}
. As illustrated in the introduction in Figure
1
, this topology effectively shifts the required critical point outside of the observation domain
\mathcal{K}
. We will visualize the higher-dimensional “tunnel effect” in the following section, where we demonstrate that these topological limitations transfer to any network
\Phi
in close proximity to
\overline{\Phi}
.

2.5.2
Extension to Networks in Close Proximity

In the upcoming analysis, we study the topological restrictions implied by a small distance in the sup-norm between a function
\Phi\in C^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, and a second reference map
\overline{\Phi}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
without critical points, i.e.,
\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. We assume that there exists
\mu>0
, such that on a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{X}
it holds that

\left\lVert\Phi-\overline{\Phi}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}\leq\mu.

(2.8)

The reference map
\overline{\Phi}
may be a neural ODE, an MLP, or a ResNet in the restricted parameter regimes
0<\alpha\ll 1
or
\alpha\gg 1
identified in Section
2.4
.
The estimate (
2.8
) implies that the map
\Phi
is trapped in a
\mu
-tube around
\overline{\Phi}
. As we will show, this forces the topological limitations of
\overline{\Phi}
discussed in Section
2.5.1
to carry over to
\Phi
. Again, we treat the one-dimensional and higher-dimensional cases separately, as the boundary
\partial\mathcal{K}
of a compact set is disconnected in one dimension, but can be connected in higher dimensions.

One-Dimensional Case

While every one-dimensional map
\overline{\Phi}
without critical points is strictly monotone, the
\mu
-close map
\Phi
is allowed to oscillate and possess critical points. However, as the distance between
\Phi
and
\overline{\Phi}
is bounded, the level sets of
\Phi
have topological restrictions, too.

Theorem 2.17

(Topological Restrictions for
n_{\textup{in}}=1
)
.

Consider
\Phi\in C^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
with
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}
open, and
\overline{\Phi}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
with
\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Let
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{K}=[k_{0},k_{1}]\subset\mathcal{X}
be a compact interval and
a=\overline{\Phi}(k_{0})
,
b=\overline{\Phi}(k_{1})
. If

\left\lVert\Phi-\overline{\Phi}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}\leq\mu<\frac{\left\lvert b-a\right\rvert}{2},

then for any
c\in(\min(a,b)+\mu,\max(a,b)-\mu)
, none of the sub- and super-level sets
S_{c}^{<}(\Phi)
and
S_{c}^{>}(\Phi)
can be entirely contained within the interior
\textup{int}(\mathcal{K})=(k_{0},k_{1})
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\overline{\Phi}
is strictly monotonically increasing, such that
a<b
. The bound for the distance between
\Phi
and
\overline{\Phi}
implies that

\Phi(k_{0})\leq\overline{\Phi}(k_{0})+\mu=a+\mu,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Phi(k_{1})\geq\overline{\Phi}(k_{1})-\mu=b-\mu.

Since
\mu<\frac{\left\lvert b-a\right\rvert}{2}
, the interval
(a+\mu,b-\mu)
is non-empty, such that for any
c\in(a+\mu,b-\mu)
it follows
\Phi(k_{0})<c<\Phi(k_{1})
. Consequently, any sub-level set
S_{c}^{<}(\Phi)
must contain
k_{0}
and any super-level set
S_{c}^{>}(\Phi)
must contain
k_{1}
, such that the statement follows. By symmetry, the same argumentation holds for monotonically decreasing maps.
∎

Theorem
2.17
implies that
\Phi
cannot correctly classify any dataset

where the boundary points

c_{1}

Higher-Dimensional Case

In the higher-dimensional case
n_{\textup{in}}\geq 2
, the boundary
\partial\mathcal{K}
of a compact set
\mathcal{K}
can be connected, which fundamentally changes the topological restrictions compared to the one-dimensional case. The following theorem proves that the decision boundary
S_{c^{\ast}}(\Phi)
of a map
\Phi
that is
\mu
-close to a reference map
\overline{\Phi}
without critical points cannot be entirely contained within the interior of
\mathcal{K}
, but instead must intersect the boundary
\partial\mathcal{K}
.

Theorem 2.18

(Topological Restrictions for
n_{\textup{in}}\geq 2
)
.

Consider
\Phi\in C^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
with
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open,
n_{\textup{in}}\geq 2
, and
\overline{\Phi}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
with
\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Let
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{X}
be a compact set with a connected boundary
\partial\mathcal{K}
, and let
[a,b]\subset\overline{\Phi}(\mathcal{K})
be a non-empty interval. If

\left\lVert\Phi-\overline{\Phi}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}\leq\mu<\frac{b-a}{2},

then for all
c\in(a+\mu,b-\mu)
, the level set
S_{c}(\Phi)
intersects the boundary
\partial\mathcal{K}
, i.e.,
S_{c}(\Phi)\cap\partial\mathcal{K}\neq\varnothing
.

Proof.

Let
0<\nu<\frac{b-a}{2}-\mu
be arbitrary.
Since
[a,b]\subset\overline{\Phi}(\mathcal{K})
, the sub-level set
\mathcal{S}\coloneqq S_{a+\nu}^{\leq}(\overline{\Phi})
has a non-empty intersection with the domain
\mathcal{K}
. With the assumptions on
\overline{\Phi}
and
\Phi
and
a+\nu\in(\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\overline{\Phi}(x),\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\overline{\Phi}(x))
, it follows from Lemma
2.15
that
\mathcal{S}
is non-compact in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
. Furthermore, by the continuity of
\overline{\Phi}
, the set
\mathcal{S}=\overline{\Phi}^{-1}((-\infty,a+\nu])
is closed in
\mathcal{X}
.

We first show that
\mathcal{S}
must intersect the boundary of
\mathcal{K}
, as visualized in Figure
6
. Assume by contradiction that
\mathcal{S}\cap\partial\mathcal{K}=\varnothing
. Since there exists
x^{\ast}\in\mathcal{K}
with
\overline{\Phi}(x^{\ast})=a
, the intersection
\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{K}
is non-empty. If this intersection does not intersect the boundary
\partial\mathcal{K}
, then
\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{K}
is entirely contained in the interior
\textup{int}(\mathcal{K})
. Because
\mathcal{S}
is closed and
\mathcal{K}
is compact, their intersection
\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{K}
is compact, too. The continuous function
\overline{\Phi}
must then attain its global minimum on the compact set
\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{K}
at some point
x_{\min}
. As
\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{K}\subset\textup{int}(\mathcal{K})
,
x_{\min}
would be an interior point of
\mathcal{K}
.
A necessary condition for the existence of the minimum
x_{\min}
of the continuously differentiable function
\overline{\Phi}:\textup{int}(\mathcal{K})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is that
\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x_{\min})=0
, as
\textup{int}(\mathcal{K})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
is an open set
[
7
]
. This contradicts the assumption that
\overline{\Phi}
has no critical points, thus it follows that
\mathcal{S}\cap\partial\mathcal{K}\neq\varnothing
.
By an analogous argument applied to the super-level set
S_{b-\nu}^{\geq}(\overline{\Phi})
, we conclude that:

S_{a+\nu}^{\leq}(\overline{\Phi})\cap\partial\mathcal{K}\neq\varnothing\qquad\text{and}\qquad S_{b-\nu}^{\geq}(\overline{\Phi})\cap\partial\mathcal{K}\neq\varnothing.

(2.9)

The uniform estimate
\left\lVert\Phi-\overline{\Phi}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}\leq\mu
implies the following set inclusions:

S_{a+\nu}^{\leq}(\overline{\Phi})\subset S_{a+\nu+\mu}^{\leq}(\Phi)\qquad\text{and}\qquad S_{b-\nu}^{\geq}(\overline{\Phi})\subset S_{b-\nu-\mu}^{\geq}(\Phi).

(2.10)

Combining the properties (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) yields:

S_{a+\nu+\mu}^{\leq}(\Phi)\cap\partial\mathcal{K}\neq\varnothing\qquad\text{and}\qquad S_{b-\nu-\mu}^{\geq}(\Phi)\cap\partial\mathcal{K}\neq\varnothing.

(2.11)

The setting is visualized in Figure
6
. The intersections (
2.11
) imply the existence of a point
x_{a}\in\partial\mathcal{K}
with
\Phi(x_{a})\leq a+\nu+\mu
and a point
x_{b}\in\partial\mathcal{K}
with
\Phi(x_{b})\geq b-\nu-\mu
. Since
\Phi
is continuous and the boundary
\partial\mathcal{K}
is a connected subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
, the image
\Phi(\partial\mathcal{K})
is an interval in
\mathbb{R}
by the intermediate value property
[
30
, Corollary 22.3]
.
Given the assumption on
\nu
, we have
a+\nu+\mu<b-\nu-\mu
, which implies:

[a+\nu+\mu,b-\nu-\mu]\subset[\Phi(x_{a}),\Phi(x_{b})]\subset\Phi(\partial\mathcal{K}).

Since
\nu>0
was arbitrary, it follows that for every
c\in(a+\mu,b-\mu)
, there exists some
x_{c}\in\partial\mathcal{K}
such that
\Phi(x_{c})=c
. Therefore, the level set
S_{c}(\Phi)
necessarily intersects the boundary
\partial\mathcal{K}
.
∎

In Section

3.1
Neural ODEs

In this section, we introduce neural ODEs, which can be interpreted as the infinite-depth limit of ResNets. In analogy to ResNets, we include two additional transformations before and after the initial value problem, and we distinguish between non-augmented and augmented architectures.
We consider neural ODEs based on the solution
h:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
of an initial value problem

\frac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t}=f(h(t),\theta(t)),\qquad h(0)=a\in\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},

(3.1)

with a non-autonomous vector field
f:\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
, parameter function
\theta\in\Theta_{\text{NODE}}\subset C^{0}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{p})
, and a non-empty set of possible initial conditions
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
. When required, the solution with initial condition
h(0)=a
is denoted by
h_{a}(t)
to emphasize the dependence on the initial condition.

As with ResNets, we allow flexibility regarding the input and output dimensions of neural ODEs. We define
neural ODEs
as the input-output maps given by

\overline{\Phi}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}},\qquad\overline{\Phi}(x)=\tilde{\lambda}(h_{\lambda(x)}(T)),

(3.2)

with
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, input transformation
\lambda:\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
, and output transformation
\tilde{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}}
. To more easily distinguish between neural ODE and ResNet architectures, we denote them in this section as
\overline{\Phi}
and
\Phi
, respectively. As with ResNets,
\lambda
and
\tilde{\lambda}
are often chosen to be affine linear, but they can also be nonlinear maps. The architecture (
3.2
) depends on the time-
T
map
h_{\lambda(x)}(T)
of the initial value problem (
3.1
). We assume that for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
, the solution of (
3.1
) exists on the entire time interval
[0,T]
. In the upcoming definition, we denote by
C^{i,j}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})
the space of functions
f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}
that are
i
-times continuously differentiable in the first variable and
j
-times continuously differentiable in the second variable.

Definition 3.1

(Neural ODE)
.

For
k\geq 1
, we denote by
\textup{NODE}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
the set of all neural ODE architectures
\overline{\Phi}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}}
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, defined by (
3.2
) with

•

input transformation
\lambda\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
,

•

output transformation
\tilde{\lambda}\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,

•

parameter function
\theta\in\Theta_{\text{NODE}}\subset C^{0}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{p})
,

•

vector field
f\in C^{k,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
with a set of possible initial conditions
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
, such that there exists a unique solution of (
3.1
) on
[0,T]
for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
.

The set of possible initial conditions of (
3.1
) is defined by
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\lambda(\mathcal{X})
, as the input
x\in\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
of the neural ODE is mapped under the transformation
\lambda
to
a=\lambda(x)
. The regularity of neural ODEs
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{NODE}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
follows directly from the regularity of the vector field
f
, as explained in
[
21
, Lemma 4.3]
. We restrict Definition
3.1
to the case
k\geq 1
to guarantee uniqueness of solution curves of (
3.1
) and hence well-posedness of the neural ODEs.

Remark 3.2
.

The regularity assumptions on the vector field
f
and the parameter function
\theta
can be weakened by considering, for example, the Carathéodory conditions (cf.
[
10
]
), as discussed in
[
20
]
. In this way, the parameter function
\theta
does not need to be continuous, allowing typical choices such as piecewise constant parameter functions (cf.
[
4
]
).

The following classification of non-augmented and augmented neural ODEs is independent of the choice of the vector field
f
and applies to all neural ODEs
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{NODE}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
. The concept of non-augmented and augmented neural ODEs is illustrated in Figure
8
.

Definition 3.3

(Neural ODE Classification)
.

The class of neural ODEs denoted by
\textup{NODE}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
k\geq 1
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, is subdivided as follows:

•

Non-augmented neural ODE

\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{NODE}^{k}_{\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})

n_{\textup{in}}\geq n_{\textup{hid}}
.

\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{NODE}^{k}_{\textup{A}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})

3.2
Relationship to ResNets

The following proposition demonstrates that, given our precise definitions, ResNets (cf. Definition
2.4
) and neural ODEs (cf. Definition
3.1
) can be understood as discrete and continuous counterparts of the same underlying dynamics. This is based on the well-established observation that ResNets can be interpreted as the explicit Euler discretization of neural ODEs
[
35
]
.

Proposition 3.4

(Relationship between ResNets and Neural ODEs)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
be open,
k\geq 1
.

(a)

Given
L\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
and
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{NODE}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
defined over
[0,T]
, the explicit Euler discretization of
\overline{\Phi}
with step size
\delta\coloneqq\frac{T}{L}
results in a ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{k}_{1,\delta}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
with
L
hidden layers.

(b)

Let
\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
be a ResNet with
L
hidden layers. If the parameter dimensions are constant, the layer functions are identical, i.e.,
p_{l}=p
and
f_{l}(\cdot,\theta_{l})\coloneqq f_{\textup{RN}}(\cdot,\theta_{l})
for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
, and
f_{\textup{RN}}\in C^{k,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
, then there exists a neural ODE
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{NODE}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
defined over
[0,L\delta]
such that
\Phi
is its explicit Euler discretization.

The proof of this proposition relies on standard arguments and mainly consists of translating the notation between ResNets and discretized neural ODEs. It is therefore proven in Appendix
A.1
and included for completeness. The following remark argues that the assumptions of Proposition
3.4
(b)
are not restrictive in typical settings.

Remark 3.5
.

The assumption
p_{l}=p
that the layer parameters are of constant dimension for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
can be established for any given
\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
by setting
p=\max\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{L}\}
and adding zero components to every strictly smaller
p_{l}
.

Furthermore, the assumption
f_{l}(\cdot,\theta_{l})\coloneqq f_{\textup{RN}}(\cdot,\theta_{l})
for
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
is fulfilled if the residual function
f_{l}
is of the typical form (
2.3
) with
\sigma_{l}=\sigma
for every layer. The dependence on the layer
l
is then only encoded in the parameters
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}
.

Finally, the additional assumption
f_{\textup{RN}}\in C^{k,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
in part
(b)
requires joint continuity of the residual function in both the hidden state
h
and the parameters
\theta
. This is not restrictive in practice: for canonical ResNets
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{k}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
with activation function
\sigma\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, the residual function (
2.3
) is a composition of matrix-vector products and continuous nonlinearities, and hence automatically satisfies
f_{\textup{RN}}\in C^{k,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
.

In the following, we explicitly calculate the approximation error between the ResNet and neural ODE architectures considered, thereby adapting
[
33
, Proposition 1]
to the more general neural ODE architecture of Definition
3.1
. The following theorem shows that the global error of the input-output map between neural ODEs and their explicit Euler discretization depends linearly on the step size or residual parameter
\delta
. Unless stated otherwise, we always consider Lipschitz constants with respect to the max-norm.

Theorem 3.6

(Approximation Error between ResNets and Neural ODEs)
.

Consider a neural ODE
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{NODE}^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, based on the initial value problem

\frac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t}=f(h(t),\theta(t)),\qquad h(0)=a\in\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},

(3.3)

on the time interval
t\in[0,T]
with the following properties:

•

The vector field
f\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
is continuously differentiable.

•

The set of possible parameter functions fulfills
\Theta_{\textup{NODE}}\subset C^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{p})
.

•

The output transformation
\tilde{\lambda}
has global Lipschitz constant
K_{\tilde{\lambda}}
.

•

The vector field
f
has global Lipschitz constant
K_{\theta}
with respect to the first variable
h
, and the solution
h:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
has a bounded second derivative
M_{\theta}\coloneqq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert h^{\prime\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}
uniformly for all
\theta\in\Theta_{\textup{NODE}}
.

Fix
L\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, then the ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{1,\delta}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
obtained by Euler’s explicit method with step size
\delta\coloneqq\frac{T}{L}
and the same transformations
\lambda
,
\tilde{\lambda}
, has the iterative update rule

h_{l}=h_{l-1}+\delta\cdot f(h_{l-1},\theta_{l}),\qquad\theta_{l}\coloneqq\theta((l-1)\delta),\qquad l\in\{1,\ldots,L\},

with
h_{0}=h(0)
. The global approximation error between the neural ODE and the corresponding ResNet is bounded by

\left\lVert\Phi-\overline{\Phi}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\leq K_{\tilde{\lambda}}\cdot\frac{M_{\theta}\delta}{2K_{\theta}}\cdot\left(e^{K_{\theta}T}-1\right).

(3.4)

Proof.

By the regularity assumptions
f\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
and
\theta\in\Theta_{\textup{NODE}}\subset C^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{p})
, the solution
h:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
is twice continuously differentiable. As
\Phi
and
\overline{\Phi}
are based on the same input transformation
\lambda
, it holds
h_{0}=h(0)
for every input
x\in\mathcal{X}
. According to
[
1
, Theorem 6.3]
, the error between the solution of (
3.3
) and the discrete solution
\{h_{0},\ldots,h_{L}\}
obtained by Euler’s explicit method satisfies

\max_{l\in\{0,\ldots,L\}}\left\lVert h(t_{l})-h_{l}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\frac{M_{\theta}\delta}{2K_{\theta}}\cdot\left(e^{K_{\theta}T}-1\right)

with constants
K_{\theta}
,
M_{\theta}
and step size
\delta=\frac{T}{L}
, uniformly in
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Consequently, it follows for the approximation error between the ResNet and the neural ODE that

\left\lVert\Phi-\overline{\Phi}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\big\lVert\tilde{\lambda}(h_{L})-\tilde{\lambda}(h(T))\big\lVert_{\infty}\leq K_{\tilde{\lambda}}\cdot\frac{M_{\theta}\delta}{2K_{\theta}}\cdot\max_{l\in\{0,\ldots,L\}}\left\lVert h(t_{l})-h_{l}\right\rVert_{\infty}

using the Lipschitz continuity of the output transformation
\tilde{\lambda}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

While we focus on the explicit Euler discretization due to its direct structural relation to standard ResNets, higher-order numerical schemes such as Runge-Kutta methods can be used to obtain tighter error bounds. In the discrete setting, these higher-order methods correspond to multi-step ResNet architectures
[
9
,
24
]
.

We now specialize Theorem
3.6
to canonical ResNets with the typical residual function (
2.3
). The proof consists of explicitly computing the constants
M_{\theta}
and
K_{\theta}
and is given in Appendix
B.1
.

Corollary 3.8

(Approximation Error between canonical ResNets and Neural ODEs)
.

Consider a neural ODE
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{NODE}^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, based on the initial value problem

\frac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t}=\widetilde{W}\sigma(Wh(t)+b)+\tilde{b},\qquad h(0)=h_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},

with parameters
(W,\widetilde{W},b,\tilde{b})\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{m\times n_{\textup{hid}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}\times m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
on the time interval
t\in[0,T]
with the following properties:

•

The activation function
\sigma\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
fulfills Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
, i.e., there exist constants
S_{\sigma},K_{\sigma}>0
with
\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathbb{R}}\leq S_{\sigma}
and
\left\lVert\sigma^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathbb{R}}\leq K_{\sigma}
.

•

The output transformation
\tilde{\lambda}
has global Lipschitz constant
K_{\tilde{\lambda}}
.

•

The weight matrices
W
,
\widetilde{W}
, and the biases
\tilde{b}
fulfill Assumption
(B1)
with respect to the max-norm, i.e., there exist constants
\omega_{\infty},\widetilde{\omega}_{\infty},\tilde{\beta}_{\infty}\geq 0
, such that
\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\omega_{\infty}
,
\big\lVert\widetilde{W}\big\lVert_{\infty}\leq\widetilde{\omega}_{\infty}
and
\big\lVert\tilde{b}\big\lVert_{\infty}\leq\tilde{\beta}_{\infty}
for all
(W,\widetilde{W},b,\tilde{b})\in\Theta
.

Then, with the notation from Theorem
3.6
it follows that

\left\lVert\Phi-\overline{\Phi}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\leq K_{\tilde{\lambda}}\cdot\frac{\left(\widetilde{\omega}_{\infty}S_{\sigma}+\tilde{\beta}_{\infty}\right)\cdot\delta}{2\omega_{\infty}}\cdot\left(e^{K_{\sigma}\widetilde{\omega}_{\infty}\omega_{\infty}T}-1\right).

(3.5)

The preceding theorem and corollary show that, in the limit
\delta\rightarrow 0
, neural ODEs and the corresponding ResNets can be viewed as continuous and discrete realizations of the same underlying dynamics. In the setting of Theorem
3.6
, this correspondence holds for ResNets with skip parameter
\varepsilon=1
. More generally, ResNets with arbitrary skip parameter
\varepsilon\neq 1
are also related to neural ODEs, as discussed in Proposition
3.4
(b)
. In this general case, taking the limit
\delta\rightarrow 0
for fixed
\varepsilon>0
means
\alpha\coloneqq\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0
, as also discussed in Sections
2.4
and
2.5
.

3.3
Existence of Critical Points

In this section, we characterize the existence of critical points in neural ODEs and ResNets in the parameter regime
0<\alpha\ll 1
. For that purpose, we first extend the result of
[
21
]
that non-augmented neural ODEs cannot have any critical points to our setting of general input and output transformations.

Theorem 3.9

(No Critical Points in Non-Augmented Neural ODEs)
.

Let
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{NODE}^{1}_{\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, be a scalar non-augmented neural ODE and let the input and output transformations
\lambda
,
\tilde{\lambda}
fulfill Assumption
(C1)
. Then
\overline{\Phi}
cannot have any critical points, i.e.,
\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

As
\overline{\Phi}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
, we can calculate the network input gradient with the multi-dimensional chain rule, which yields

\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x)=\left(\partial_{x}\lambda(x)\right)^{\top}\left(\partial_{a}h_{a}(T)\right)^{\top}\left(\partial_{h_{a}(T)}{\tilde{\lambda}}(h_{a}(T))\right)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}},

(3.6)

where
\partial_{x}\lambda(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}\times n_{\textup{in}}}
,
\partial_{a}h_{a}(T)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}\times n_{\textup{hid}}}
, and
\partial_{h_{a}(T)}{\tilde{\lambda}}(h_{a}(T))\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times n_{\textup{hid}}}
are Jacobian matrices.
By
[
21
, Proposition 4.10]
, the Jacobian matrix
\partial_{a}h_{a}(T)
of the time-
T
map
h_{a}(T)
with respect to the initial condition
a
always has full rank
n_{\textup{hid}}
. By Assumption
(C1)
, also the Jacobian matrices
\partial_{x}\lambda(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}\times n_{\textup{in}}}
and
\partial_{h_{a}(T)}{\tilde{\lambda}}(h_{a}(T))\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times n_{\textup{hid}}}
have both full rank.

As
\overline{\Phi}
is scalar and non-augmented, it holds that
n_{\textup{in}}\geq n_{\textup{hid}}\geq n_{\textup{out}}=1
, such that the dimensions in the matrix product (
3.6
) are monotonically decreasing. It follows that
\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x)
always has full rank
1
(i.e., it is non-zero), uniformly in
x\in\mathcal{X}
, cf.
[
21
, Lemma C.1]
.
∎

In the following theorem, we show for non-augmented ResNets
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, that the property that no critical points exist, persists for
\alpha\coloneqq\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}
sufficiently small. In the case of canonical ResNets we provide an explicit upper bound on the ratio
\alpha
, below which
\Phi
cannot have any critical points.

Theorem 3.10

(No Critical Points in Non-Augmented ResNets with
0<\alpha\ll 1
)
.

Consider a scalar non-augmented ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, with the following properties:

•

The input and output transformations
\lambda
and
\tilde{\lambda}
fulfill Assumption
(C1)
.

•

All residual functions
f_{l}(\cdot,\theta_{l})\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
are globally Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
K_{f}
(w.r.t. the max-norm), uniformly for all
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}
and
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
.

Then, if
\alpha=\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{K_{f}}
,
\Phi
cannot have any critical points, i.e.,
\nabla_{x}\Phi(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. If the ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
is canonical, the assumption on the residual functions
f_{l}
can be replaced by the following:

•

The component-wise applied activation functions
\sigma_{l}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
fulfill Assumption
(A1)
, i.e., there exists a constant
K_{\sigma}>0
with
\left\lVert\sigma^{\prime}_{l}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathbb{R}}\leq K_{\sigma}
for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
.

•

The largest singular value of the matrix product
W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}
is uniformly bounded from above, i.e., there exists a constant
\nu_{\max}>0
such that
\sigma_{\max}(W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l})\leq\nu_{\max}
for all
\theta_{l}=(W_{l},\widetilde{W}_{l},b_{l},\tilde{b}_{l})\in\Theta_{l}
and
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
.

Then, if
\alpha<\frac{1}{\nu_{\max}\cdot K_{\sigma}}
,
\Phi
cannot have any critical points.

Proof.

We prove the theorem by applying Proposition
2.13
, which requires showing that for
\alpha<\frac{1}{K_{f}}
, it holds that
-\frac{1}{\alpha}
is not an eigenvalue of the Jacobian matrix
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
,
h_{l-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
, and
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}
. For the eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
, we can estimate

\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n_{\textup{hid}}\}}\left\lvert\lambda_{i}(\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l}))\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq K_{f}<\frac{1}{\alpha},

where we used the fact that the maximal absolute value of the eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
is bounded above by every induced matrix norm. By the mean value theorem, the global Lipschitz constant
K_{f}
is an upper bound for the norm of the Jacobian matrix
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
. Consequently,
-\frac{1}{\alpha}
cannot be an eigenvalue of
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
for every
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
, such that the statement follows from Proposition
2.13
.

In the case
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
, we have
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})=\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})W_{l}
with
a_{l}\coloneqq W_{l}h_{l-1}+b_{l}
, which is the explicit form of the Jacobian matrix determined in Proposition
2.11
. As for two matrices
A\in\mathbb{R}^{a\times b}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{b\times a}
, the matrix products
AB\in\mathbb{R}^{a\times a}
and
BA\in\mathbb{R}^{b\times b}
have the same non-zero eigenvalues (cf.
[
13
]
), we can estimate

\displaystyle\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n_{\textup{hid}}\}}\left\lvert\lambda_{i}(\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})W_{l})\right\rvert=\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n_{\textup{hid}}\}}\left\lvert\lambda_{i}(W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l}))\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;\big\lVert W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})\big\lVert_{2}\leq\big\lVert W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}\big\lVert_{2}\cdot\left\lVert\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})\right\rVert_{2}\leq\nu_{\max}\cdot K_{\sigma}<\frac{1}{\alpha}

for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Here, we used that the maximum absolute eigenvalue is bounded by the sub-multiplicative Euclidean norm, that
\big\lVert W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}\big\lVert_{2}=\sigma_{\max}(W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l})
and that
\left\lVert\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert\sigma_{l}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathbb{R}}
, as
\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})
is a diagonal matrix. Since
-\frac{1}{\alpha}
cannot be an eigenvalue of
\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})W_{l}
for any
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
, the statement follows from Proposition
2.13
.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

Under Assumption
(B1)
, that all weight matrices are uniformly bounded, a uniform upper bound for the largest singular value of the matrix products
W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}
can always be found.

4
Case
\alpha\gg 1
: Close to Feed-Forward Neural Networks

In Section
4.1
, we define general feed-forward neural networks (FNNs) and their canonical form, given by multilayer perceptrons (MLPs). In Section
4.2
, we link ResNets to FNNs by taking the parameter limit
\alpha\coloneqq\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\infty
, corresponding to a ResNet with dominating residual term. Additionally, we calculate the approximation error between FNNs and ResNets for large residual terms (large
\alpha
). Finally, in Section
4.3
we show that (in analogy to non-augmented MLPs) critical points cannot exist in non-augmented ResNets for
\alpha
sufficiently large.

4.1
Feed-Forward Neural Networks

Classical feed-forward neural networks, such as MLPs, are structured in consecutive layers. In the following, we introduce the general FNN architecture we study, which, unlike ResNets, has no skip connection. We introduce FNNs with
L+2
layers to align with the notation introduced for ResNets.
General FNNs are structured in layers
h_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{l}}
defined by the iterative update rule

h_{l}=\delta f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l}),\qquad l\in\{0,\ldots,L+1\},

(4.1)

with input
h_{-1}\coloneqq x\in\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
, a (typically nonlinear)
layer map

f_{l}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{l-1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{l}}
, and
parameters

\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}
, where
\Theta_{l}
denotes the set of parameters of layer
l
. As in the definition of ResNets, we include the
scaling parameter

\delta>0
. In the case of MLPs, the layer map has the explicit form

f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})\coloneqq\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma_{l}(W_{l}h_{l-1}+b_{l})+\tilde{b}_{l}=\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma_{l}(a_{l})+\tilde{b}_{l}

(4.2)

where
\widetilde{W}_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{l}\times m_{l}}
,
W_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}\times n_{l-1}}
are weight matrices,
b_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}}
,
\tilde{b}_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{l}}
are biases,
h_{-1}\coloneqq x
is the input data and
\sigma_{l}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a component-wise applied activation function. As for ResNets, we write
\sigma_{l}
for the component-wise extension
\sigma_{l}:\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}}
, where
m_{l}
is the dimension of the pre-activated states
a_{l}\coloneqq W_{l}h_{l-1}+b_{l}
,
l\in\{0,\ldots,L+1\}
. The layer map in (
4.2
) agrees with the residual functions of canonical ResNets defined in (
2.3
) up to the layer-dependent dimension
n_{l}
. We denote the FNN input-output map by

\overline{\Phi}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}},\;\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}},\qquad\overline{\Phi}(x)=h_{L+1}(x)

(4.3)

with
n_{\textup{out}}\coloneqq n_{L+1}
. To more easily distinguish general feed-forward architectures (including MLPs) from ResNet architectures, we denote them in this section by
\overline{\Phi}
and
\Phi
, respectively. In contrast to ResNets, where the update rule (
2.2
) requires constant layer width, the layer widths of FNNs can change. As the input and output dimensions
n_{\textup{in}}\coloneqq n_{-1}
and
n_{\textup{out}}\coloneqq n_{L+1}
are for FNNs not required to be equal, we do not include two additional transformations
\lambda
,
\tilde{\lambda}
to the architecture as for ResNets in (
2.4
).

Definition 4.1

(Feed-Forward Neural Network and Multilayer Perceptron)
.

For
k\geq 0
and
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, the set
\textup{FNN}_{\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})\subset C^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
\delta>0
, denotes all FNNs
\overline{\Phi}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}}
as defined in (
4.3
) with layer map
f_{l}(\cdot,\theta_{l})\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{l-1}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{l}})
for each fixed
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}
,
l\in\{0,\ldots,L+1\}
.

In the canonical case, the set
\textup{MLP}_{\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})\subset C^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
\delta>0
, denotes all MLPs
\overline{\Phi}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}}
with layer map as defined in (
4.2
) satisfying
\sigma_{l}\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
for
l\in\{0,\ldots,L+1\}
.

In Section
4.2
, we discuss how FNNs are related to ResNets with
\varepsilon=0
, and how MLPs connect to canonical ResNets with
\varepsilon=0
.

Depending on the dimensions
n_{l}
of the layers
h_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{l}}
and the dimensions
m_{l}
of the pre-activated states
a_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}}
, we define non-augmented FNNs and MLPs as follows.

Definition 4.2

(Non-Augmented FNNs and MLPs)
.

The classes
\textup{FNN}_{\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
and
\textup{MLP}_{\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
k\geq 0
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, are subdivided as follows:

•

Non-Augmented FNN

\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{FNN}^{k}_{\delta,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
: it holds
n_{l-1}\geq n_{l}
for
l\in\{0,\ldots,L+1\}
.

•

Non-Augmented MLP

\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{MLP}^{k}_{\delta,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
: it holds
n_{l-1}\geq m_{l}\geq n_{l}
for
l\in\{0,\ldots,L+1\}
.

.

Remark 4.3
.

The definition of a non-augmented FNN or MLP is more restrictive than that of a non-augmented ResNet: for MLPs, the dimensions of the input
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
, all layers
h_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{l}}
, and pre-activated states
a_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}}
have to be monotonically decreasing. In the case of ResNets, it is only necessary that the input dimension
n_{\textup{in}}
is larger than or equal to the hidden dimension
n_{\textup{hid}}
; the dimension
m_{l}
of the pre-activated states is not relevant in our context.

Remark 4.4
.

Unlike ResNets, FNNs admit variable layer widths
n_{l}
. Architectures that violate the monotonicity requirements of Definition
4.2
can be further classified as
augmented
,
bottleneck
, or
degenerate
, as discussed in
[
21
]
. Since our focus is on non-augmented architectures, we omit formal definitions of the alternative structures.

While FNNs allow for arbitrary layer maps, we now focus on the algebraic structure of MLPs.

Remark 4.5
.

The general update rule (
4.2
) includes as special cases the following two typical MLP update rules: if
m_{l}=n_{l}
,
\widetilde{W}_{l}=\textup{Id}_{n_{l}}
and
\tilde{b}_{l}=0
, MLPs with an “outer nonlinearity” are obtained, i.e.,
h_{l}=\delta\sigma_{l}(W_{l}h_{l-1}+b_{l})
, and if
m_{l}=n_{l-1}
,
W_{l}=\textup{Id}_{n_{l-1}}
and
b_{l}=0
for
l\in\{0,\ldots,L+1\}
, the update rule simplifies to MLPs with an “inner nonlinearity”, i.e.,
h_{l}=\delta(\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma_{l}(h_{l-1})+\tilde{b}_{l})
.

Remark 4.6
.

The general update rule (
4.2
) includes two affine transformations that can always be reduced to a single one per layer without changing the input-output map: the transformation
\sigma_{l}(a_{l})\mapsto a_{l+1}
amounts to

a_{l+1}=W_{l+1}h_{l}+b_{l+1}=W_{l+1}\left(\delta\left(\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma_{l}(a_{l})+\tilde{b}_{l}\right)\right)+b_{l+1}=\delta W_{l+1}\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma_{l}(a_{l})+\delta W_{l+1}\tilde{b}_{l}+b_{l+1},

(4.4)

which is a single affine linear transformation of the activated state
\sigma_{l}(a_{l})
. Nevertheless, we choose the update rule (
4.2
), as it allows us to express the structure of
\overline{\Phi}
with only full-rank weight matrices, see Theorem
4.7
.

Given an MLP, the following theorem allows us to always suppose that Assumption
(B2)
holds, i.e., that all weight matrices have full rank.

Theorem 4.7

(MLP Normal Form
[
21
]
)
.

Let
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{MLP}_{\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
k\geq 0
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, be an MLP, where the weight matrices
W_{0}
and
\widetilde{W}_{L+1}
have full rank. Then
\overline{\Phi}
is equivalent to an MLP
\widetilde{\Phi}\in\textup{MLP}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
with only full rank weight matrices, i.e.,
\overline{\Phi}(x)=\widetilde{\Phi}(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
\widetilde{\Phi}
is called the
normal form
of
\overline{\Phi}
.

Proof.

The result is a special case of
[
21
, Theorem 3.10]
under the additional assumption that
W_{0}
and
\widetilde{W}_{L+1}
have full rank.
∎

The assumption that
W_{0}
and
\widetilde{W}_{L+1}
have full rank is analogous to Assumption
(C1)
that the Jacobian matrices of the input and output transformation have full rank. The proof of
[
21
, Theorem 3.10]
replaces the matrix product
W_{l+1}\widetilde{W}_{l}
in (
4.4
) by a product of full rank matrices of possibly smaller dimensions. For ResNet architectures, it is not possible to define a normal form in the same way.

Remark 4.8
.

It is important to note that the classes (non-augmented, augmented, or bottleneck) of an MLP
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{MLP}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
and its normal form
\widetilde{\Phi}
(cf. Theorem
4.7
) are not necessarily the same. A non-augmented or augmented MLP with singular weight matrices can be equivalent to an MLP in normal form with a bottleneck. The MLP results of Section
4.3
are stated for MLPs in normal form, where all weight matrices have full rank and hence fulfill Assumption
(B2)
.

4.2
Relationship to ResNets

In the following proposition, we show that given a ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{0,\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
with skip parameter
\varepsilon=0
, there exists an FNN
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{FNN}_{\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
, that has the same input-output map as
\Phi
. Conversely, the statement also holds if the FNN’s hidden layers have a constant width. In the previous section, we introduced FNNs with
L+2
layers to maintain the analogy with ResNets, which have, in addition to the
L
hidden layers, one input and one output transformation.

Proposition 4.9

(Relationship between ResNets and FNNs)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open,
k\geq 0
and
\delta>0
.

(a)

For every ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{0,\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
, there exists an FNN
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{FNN}_{\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
with the same input-output map as
\Phi
, i.e.,
\Phi(x)=\overline{\Phi}(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

(b)

For every FNN
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{FNN}_{\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
, where all hidden layers
h_{l}
have the same dimension
n_{l}=n_{\textup{hid}}
for
l\in\{0,1,\ldots,L\}
, there exists a ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{0,\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
with the same input-output map, i.e.,
\Phi(x)=\overline{\Phi}(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

If the input and output transformations
\lambda
,
\tilde{\lambda}
have the typical form (
2.5
), the statements holds as a special case for canonical ResNets
\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{0,\delta,\sigma}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
and MLPs
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{MLP}_{\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
.

The proof of this proposition only relies on the translation of the notation between ResNets and FNNs. It is therefore proven in Appendix
A.2
and included for completeness. Proposition
4.9
shows that the ResNet and FNN dynamics coincide in the case
\varepsilon=0
, which relates to the limit
\alpha\coloneqq\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\infty
, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
for fixed
\delta>0
, as also discussed in Section
2.4
and Section
2.5
.

In the following, we aim to quantify the distance between FNNs and ResNets with a small skip parameter
0<\varepsilon<1
. For this purpose, we first calculate the explicit input-output map of ResNets. Although Definition
2.4
assumes
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, the following lemma includes the limiting cases
\varepsilon=0
and
\delta=0
.

Lemma 4.10

(ResNet Input-Output Map)
.

For a ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{0}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, with
\varepsilon,\delta\geq 0
, for the
l
-th layer it holds that

h_{l}=\varepsilon^{l}\lambda(x)+\delta\sum_{j=1}^{l}\varepsilon^{l-j}f_{j}(h_{j-1},\theta_{j}),\qquad l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}.

(4.5)

Proof.

The statement follows by induction on the update rule (
2.2
). For
l=1
, it holds

h_{1}=\varepsilon h_{0}+\delta f_{1}(h_{0},\theta_{1})=\varepsilon\lambda(x)+\delta f_{1}(\lambda(x),\theta_{1}),

which matches (
4.5
). For
l\in\{2,\ldots,L\}
, assume that formula (
4.5
) holds for
l-1
, then we conclude for layer
l
:

\displaystyle h_{l}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon h_{l-1}+\delta f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\left(\varepsilon^{l-1}\lambda(x)+\delta\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}\varepsilon^{l-1-j}f_{j}(h_{j-1},\theta_{j})\right)+\delta f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{l}\lambda(x)+\delta\sum_{j=1}^{l}\varepsilon^{l-j}f_{j}(h_{j-1},\theta_{j}).\qed

Remark 4.11
.

Lemma
4.10
reveals a fundamental asymmetry in how the parameters
\varepsilon
and
\delta
influence the ResNet input-output map. The residual parameter
\delta
acts as a uniform global multiplier for every residual function
f_{l}
. In contrast, the skip parameter
\varepsilon
induces a depth-dependent exponential scaling: the contribution of the
l
-th hidden layer is scaled by
\varepsilon^{L-l}
, and the input
\lambda(x)
is scaled by
\varepsilon^{L}
.
Consequently, for
0\leq\varepsilon<1
, the contribution from earlier layers decays exponentially as it propagates toward the output.
This phenomenon is illustrated for low-dimensional examples in Section
5.1.3
.

The following theorem shows that the global error of the input-output map between ResNets and FNNs scales linearly in the skip parameter
\varepsilon
asymptotically for
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and fixed
\delta>0
and
L>0
.

Theorem 4.12

(Approximation Error between ResNets and FNNs)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
and
\delta>0
and consider a ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{0}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, with update rule

h_{l}=\varepsilon h_{l-1}+\delta f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l}),\qquad l\in\{1,\ldots,L\},

parameters
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}
, input transformation
\lambda:\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
and output transformation
\tilde{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}}
.
Furthermore, consider the corresponding FNN
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{FNN}^{0}_{\delta}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
with update rule

h_{l}=\delta f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l}),\qquad l\in\{1,\ldots,L\},

and
h_{0}=\lambda(x)
,
h_{L+1}=\tilde{\lambda}(h_{L})
. Assume that:

•

The residual functions
f_{l}
are globally bounded, i.e., there exists a constant
S_{f}>0
, such that
\left\lVert f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq S_{f}
for all
h_{l-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{l-1}}
,
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}
and
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
.

•

The residual functions
f_{l}
are globally Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
K_{f}
for all
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}
and
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
.

•

The input transformation
\lambda
is globally bounded by the constant
S_{\lambda}>0
, i.e.,
\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\leq S_{\lambda}
.

•

The output transformation
\tilde{\lambda}
is globally Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
K_{\tilde{\lambda}}
.

Then it holds for the global approximation error between the ResNet
\Phi
and the FNN
\overline{\Phi}
that

\left\lVert\Phi-\overline{\Phi}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}\leq\varepsilon\cdot K_{\tilde{\lambda}}\left((\delta K_{f})^{L-1}S_{\lambda}+(S_{\lambda}+\delta S_{f})\sum_{j=0}^{L-2}(\delta K_{f})^{j}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})

(4.6)

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{X}
be arbitrary. To estimate
\lVert\Phi-\overline{\Phi}\lVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}
, we first bound the distance between the hidden states of the ResNet, denoted by
h_{l}^{\textup{RN}}
, and the hidden states of the FNN, denoted by
h_{l}^{\textup{FNN}}
for
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
. The pointwise error at layer
l
is defined to be
\Delta_{l}\coloneqq\left\lVert h_{l}^{\textup{RN}}-h_{l}^{\textup{FNN}}\right\rVert_{\infty}
.
For the first hidden layer it holds

\Delta_{1}=\left\lVert h_{1}^{\textup{RN}}-h_{1}^{\textup{FNN}}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert\varepsilon\lambda(x)+\delta f_{1}(\lambda(x),\theta_{1})-\delta f_{1}(\lambda(x),\theta_{1})\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert\varepsilon\lambda(x)\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon S_{\lambda}.

To determine
\Delta_{l}
for
l\in\{2,\ldots,L\}
, we first estimate with Lemma
4.10
:

\displaystyle\left\lVert h_{l}^{\textup{RN}}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\varepsilon^{l}\lambda(x)+\delta\sum_{j=1}^{l}\varepsilon^{l-j}f_{j}(h_{j-1}^{\textup{RN}},\theta_{j})\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon^{l}\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert_{\infty}+\delta\sum_{j=1}^{l}\varepsilon^{l-j}\left\lVert f_{j}(h_{j-1}^{\textup{RN}},\theta_{j})\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{l}S_{\lambda}+\delta S_{f}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\varepsilon^{j}\leq S_{\lambda}+\frac{\delta S_{f}}{1-\varepsilon}\eqqcolon H,

where we used the bound of the geometric series for
0<\varepsilon<1
. It follows for
l\in\{2,\ldots,L\}
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{l}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\varepsilon h_{l-1}^{\textup{RN}}+\delta f_{l}(h_{l-1}^{\textup{RN}},\theta_{l})-\delta f_{l}(h_{l-1}^{\textup{FNN}},\theta_{l})\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\left\lVert h_{l-1}^{\textup{RN}}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\delta\left\lVert f_{l}(h_{l-1}^{\textup{RN}},\theta_{l})-f_{l}(h_{l-1}^{\textup{FNN}},\theta_{l})\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon H+\delta K_{f}\Delta_{l-1},

where we used the Lipschitz continuity of the residual functions
f_{l}
. As the bound of
\Delta_{l}
depends linearly on
\Delta_{l-1}
, we can estimate

\Delta_{L}\leq\varepsilon H+\delta K_{f}\Delta_{L-1}\leq\ldots\leq(\delta K_{f})^{L-1}\Delta_{1}+\varepsilon H\sum_{j=0}^{L-2}(\delta K_{f})^{j}.

Finally, we use the Lipschitz continuity of the output transformation
\tilde{\lambda}
to obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi-\overline{\Phi}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\left\lVert\tilde{\lambda}(h_{L}^{\textup{RN}})-\tilde{\lambda}(h_{L}^{\textup{FNN}})\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq K_{\tilde{\lambda}}\Delta_{L}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\cdot K_{\tilde{\lambda}}\left((\delta K_{f})^{L-1}S_{\lambda}+\left(S_{\lambda}+\frac{\delta S_{f}}{1-\varepsilon}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{L-2}(\delta K_{f})^{j}\right),

(4.7)

such that the statement follows after expanding
\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}
in
\varepsilon
.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

The asymptotic bound in Theorem
4.12
is sharp to leading order in
0<\varepsilon<1
. An explicit, non-asymptotic upper bound valid for all
0<\varepsilon<1
is given by equation (
4.7
). Furthermore, the asymptotic bound indicates a regular perturbation problem
[
19
]
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, which is reasonable considering the Lipschitz assumptions on the nonlinear terms. Yet, even if these Lipschitz assumptions would be dropped, one could attempt to find an asymptotic bound using techniques for singularly perturbed iterated maps
[
17
]
. This would be technically an extremely challenging extension.

Remark 4.14
.

The global approximation error (
4.6
) depends on the product
\delta K_{f}
and the depth
L
. We observe that for
\delta K_{f}<1
, the error remains bounded, even for very deep networks with
L\gg 1
, as the sum
\sum_{j=0}^{L-2}(\delta K_{f})^{j}
converges. In this case, the ResNet is an approximation of the FNN with stable approximation error with respect to the depth. In the case
\delta K_{f}>1
, the global approximation error grows exponentially with depth
L
. This highlights that while the approximation is
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
for any fixed
L
, the error grows as the network becomes deeper unless the residual functions are sufficiently contractive.

We now consider Theorem
4.12
for canonical ResNets with the typical residual function (
2.3
). The proof consists of explicitly computing the constants
S_{f}
,
K_{f}
,
S_{\lambda}
and

Corollary 4.15

(Approximation Error between Canonical ResNets and MLPs)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
and
\delta>0
and consider a canonical ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{0}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
,

open, with update rule

as defined in (
2.3

Let the input and output transformations have the typical form (

(W_{l},\widetilde{W}_{l},b_{l},\tilde{b}_{l})\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}\times m_{l}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}\times n_{\textup{hid}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
for

Furthermore, consider the corresponding

and
h_{0}=\lambda(x)
,

. Assume that:

•

The activation functions

fulfill Assumption

\sigma_{l}
,

•

The weight matrices

4.3
Existence of Critical Points

In this section, we characterize the existence of critical points in FNNs and ResNets in the parameter regime
\alpha\gg 1
. For that purpose, we first extend a result of
[
21
]
, that non-augmented MLPs in normal form cannot have any critical points, to our setting of general layer maps.

Theorem 4.16

(No Critical Points in Non-Augmented FNNs)
.

Let
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{FNN}^{1}_{\delta,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, be a scalar non-augmented FNN with the following properties:

•

All Jacobian matrices
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{l}\times n_{l-1}}
have full rank
n_{l}
for all
h_{l-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{l-1}}
,
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}
and
l\in\{0,\ldots,L+1\}
.

Then
\overline{\Phi}
cannot have any critical points, i.e.,
\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

In the case that the feed-forward neural network is a non-augmented MLP
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{MLP}^{1}_{\delta,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
with
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, the assumption can be replaced by the following conditions:

•

The component-wise applied activation functions
\sigma_{l}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
,
l\in\{0,\ldots,L+1\}
fulfill Assumption
(A3)
, i.e., they are strictly monotone and it holds
\lvert\sigma_{l}^{\prime}(y)\rvert>0
for every
y\in\mathbb{R}
and all
l\in\{0,\ldots,L+1\}
.

•

All weight matrices
(W_{0},\widetilde{W}_{0},\ldots,W_{L+1},\widetilde{W}_{L+1})\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
, have full rank, cf. Assumption
(B2)
.

Proof.

For the first part of the statement, we calculate the FNN input gradient, given by

\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x)=\Bigl[\delta\cdot\partial_{h_{L}}f_{L+1}(h_{L},\theta_{L+1})\cdots\delta\cdot\partial_{h_{0}}f_{1}(h_{0},\theta_{1})\cdot\delta\cdot\partial_{x}f_{0}(x,\theta_{0})\Bigr]^{\top}\quad\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}.

(4.8)

As the FNN
\overline{\Phi}
is scalar and non-augmented, it holds
n_{\textup{in}}=n_{-1}\geq\ldots\geq n_{\textup{out}}=n_{L+1}=1
, such that the dimensions in the matrix product (
4.8
) are monotonically decreasing. As all Jacobian matrices have full rank, the gradient
\nabla_{x}\overline{\Phi}(x)
always has full rank
1
, uniformly in
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

In the case of non-augmented MLPs, the theorem follows directly from
[
21
, Theorem 3.16]
: the definition of MLPs in
[
21
]
only differs by the number of layers and the standing assumption on the strict monotonicity of the activation functions, which we assume here additionally.
∎

In the following theorem, we show for non-augmented ResNets
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, that the absence of critical points persists for
\alpha\coloneqq\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}
sufficiently large. In the case of canonical ResNets, we provide an explicit lower bound on the ratio
\alpha
above which
\Phi
cannot have any critical points.

Theorem 4.17

(No Critical Points in Non-Augmented ResNets with
\alpha\gg 1
)
.

Consider a scalar non-augmented ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
open, with the following properties:

•

The input and output transformations
\lambda
and
\tilde{\lambda}
fulfill Assumption
(C1)
.

•

Every residual function
f_{l}(\cdot,\theta_{l})\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
fulfills the lower Lipschitz condition

\left\lVert f_{l}(y_{1},\theta_{l})-f_{l}(y_{2},\theta_{l})\right\rVert_{2}\geq k_{f}\left\lVert y_{1}-y_{2}\right\rVert_{2}

for some
k_{f}>0
for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
,
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
and all
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}
.

Then, if
\alpha\coloneqq\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}>\frac{1}{k_{f}}
,
\Phi
cannot have any critical points, i.e.,
\nabla_{x}\Phi(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. If the ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
is canonical, the assumption on the residual functions
f_{l}
can be replaced by the following:

•

The activation functions
\sigma_{l}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
fulfill Assumption
(A3)
, i.e., they are strictly monotone and it holds
\left\lvert\sigma_{l}^{\prime}(y)\right\rvert>0
for every
y\in\mathbb{R}
and all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
. Additionally we assume that there exists a constant
k_{\sigma}>0
such that
\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}|\sigma_{l}^{\prime}([a_{l}]_{i})|\geq k_{\sigma}
with
a_{l}\coloneqq W_{l}h_{l-1}+b_{l}
and
h_{0}=x
for all
\theta_{l}=(W_{l},\widetilde{W}_{l},b_{l},\tilde{b}_{l})\in\Theta_{l}
with
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,m_{l}\}
.

•

The smallest singular value of the matrix products
W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}
is uniformly bounded from below, i.e., there exists a constant
\nu_{\min}>0
such that
\sigma_{\min}(W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l})\geq\nu_{\min}
for all
\theta_{l}=(W_{l},\widetilde{W}_{l},b_{l},\tilde{b}_{l})\in\Theta_{l}
and
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
.

Then, if
\alpha>\frac{1}{\nu_{\min}\cdot k_{\sigma}}
,
\Phi
cannot have any critical points.

Proof.

We aim to prove the theorem by applying Proposition
2.13
, which requires showing that for
\alpha>\frac{1}{k_{f}}
, it holds that
-\frac{1}{\alpha}
is not an eigenvalue of the Jacobian matrix
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
,
h_{l-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
, and
\theta_{l}\in\Theta_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p_{l}}
. By the assumed lower Lipschitz condition on the residual functions
f_{l}
, it holds for
\mu>0
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
:

\frac{\left\lVert f_{l}(h_{l-1}+\mu\cdot v,\theta_{l})-f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lvert\mu\right\rvert}\geq\frac{k_{f}\cdot\left\lVert\mu\cdot v\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lvert\mu\right\rvert}=k_{f}\cdot\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{2}.

(4.9)

As
f_{l}(\cdot,\theta_{l})\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
, we can take the limit
\mu\rightarrow 0
in (
4.9
), which yields

\left\lVert\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})\cdot v\right\rVert_{2}\geq k_{f}\cdot\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{2}.

(4.10)

By the min-max characterization of singular values (cf.
[
13
]
), it holds for a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{a\times b}
that

\sigma_{\min}(A)=\min_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{b}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\left\lVert Av\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{2}}.

(4.11)

Hence, we can conclude for the smallest singular value of the Jacobian matrix
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
:

\sigma_{\min}(\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l}))=\min_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},v\neq 0}\frac{\left\lVert\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})\cdot v\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{2}}\geq k_{f}.

As the smallest singular value is a lower bound for the absolute value of all eigenvalues (cf.
[
13
]
), it holds

\min_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n_{\textup{hid}}\}}|\lambda_{i}(\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l}))|\geq\sigma_{\min}(\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l}))\geq k_{f}>\frac{1}{\alpha}.

Consequently,
-\frac{1}{\alpha}
cannot be an eigenvalue of
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
for any
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
, such that the first statement follows from Proposition
2.13
.

In the case
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma,\textup{N}}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
, we have
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})=\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})W_{l}
with
a_{l}\coloneqq W_{l}h_{l-1}+b_{l}
, which is the explicit form of the Jacobian matrix determined in Proposition
2.11
. As for two matrices
A\in\mathbb{R}^{a\times b}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{b\times a}
, the matrix products
AB\in\mathbb{R}^{a\times a}
and
BA\in\mathbb{R}^{b\times b}
have the same non-zero eigenvalues (cf.
[
13
]
), it holds

\min_{i:\lambda_{i}\neq 0}|\lambda_{i}(\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})W_{l})|=\min_{i:\lambda_{i}\neq 0}|\lambda_{i}(W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l}))|.

Let
\lambda^{*}
be an arbitrary non-zero eigenvalue of the Jacobian
\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})W_{l}
, so it is also a non-zero eigenvalue of the matrix product
W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})
. Let
v^{*}\neq 0
be the corresponding eigenvector such that

W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})v^{*}=\lambda^{*}v^{*}.

Taking the Euclidean norm on both sides yields

\left\lvert\lambda^{*}\right\rvert\cdot\left\lVert v^{*}\right\rVert_{2}=\big\lVert W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})v^{*}\big\lVert_{2}\geq\sigma_{\min}(W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l})\left\lVert\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})v^{*}\right\rVert_{2}\geq\nu_{\min}\cdot k_{\sigma}\cdot\left\lVert v^{*}\right\rVert_{2},

where we used the minimal singular value characterization from (
4.11
), the given assumptions on
\sigma_{\min}(W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l})
, the fact that
\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})
is a diagonal matrix and the assumed lower bound on the activation functions. Dividing by
\left\lVert v^{*}\right\rVert_{2}>0
results in

\left\lvert\lambda^{*}\right\rvert\geq\nu_{\min}\cdot k_{\sigma}>\frac{1}{\alpha}.

This shows that any non-zero eigenvalue of the Jacobian matrix
\widetilde{W}_{l}\sigma^{\prime}_{l}(a_{l})W_{l}
has an absolute value strictly greater than
\frac{1}{\alpha}
. Since
-\frac{1}{\alpha}\neq 0
, potential zero eigenvalues do not play a role in the relevant analysis. Consequently,
-\frac{1}{\alpha}
cannot be an eigenvalue of
\partial_{h_{l-1}}f_{l}(h_{l-1},\theta_{l})
for any
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
, such that the second statement follows from Proposition
2.13
.
∎

Remark 4.18
.

The assumption for canonical ResNets of Theorem
4.17
that there exists a constant
k_{\sigma}>0
with
\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}|\sigma_{l}^{\prime}([a_{l}]_{i})|\geq k_{\sigma}
with
a_{l}\coloneqq W_{l}h_{l-1}+b_{l}
and
h_{0}=x
for all
l\in\{0,\ldots,L\}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,m_{l}\}
is standard in applications. Indeed, it is satisfied for typical activation functions such as
\tanh
or sigmoidal functions whenever the input domain
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
and all parameters
\theta\in\Theta=\Theta_{1}\times\cdots\times\Theta_{L}
are bounded (cf. Assumption
(B1)
). If the domain
\mathcal{X}
is unbounded, no such
k_{\sigma}>0
exists, since
|\sigma_{l}^{\prime}(y)|\to 0
as
|y|\to\infty
for these activation functions.

Remark 4.19
.

Assumption
(B2)
guarantees that for a given parameter regime
\Theta
all weight matrices contained in
\Theta
have full rank and hence strictly positive singular values. However, this does not prevent the infimum of all singular values of
\Theta
from being zero. Theorem
4.17
requires the stronger
\Theta
-uniform lower bound for all matrix products
W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}
.

The assumption in Theorem
4.17
that the smallest singular value of
W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}
is uniformly bounded from below imposes an architectural constraint: Because
W_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}\times n_{\textup{hid}}}
and
\widetilde{W}_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}\times m_{l}}
, their product is an
m_{l}\times m_{l}
matrix. By the properties of matrix rank, it holds
\operatorname{rank}(W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l})\leq\min(m_{l},n_{\textup{hid}})
. For
\sigma_{\min}(W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l})
to be strictly positive, the matrix must have full rank
m_{l}
, which implies
n_{\textup{hid}}\geq m_{l}
for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
.

This restriction aligns with the definition of non-augmented architectures: In classical MLPs, non-augmented networks are restricted to monotonically decreasing or constant layer dimensions (cf. Definition
4.2
). Similarly, requiring
n_{\textup{hid}}\geq m_{l}
ensures that no intermediate augmentations of pre-activated states
a_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{l}}
exist. The case
m_{l}>n_{\textup{hid}}
is mathematically prohibited for
\alpha\gg 1
because the resulting rank-deficiency of
W_{l}\widetilde{W}_{l}
would introduce zero eigenvalues, which are close to the critical eigenvalue
-\frac{1}{\alpha}
. It is worth noting, however, that the dimension constraint is an artifact of the usage of uniform singular value bounds.

Before analyzing this one-dimensional setting in detail, let us state the implementations’

For the remaining cases, let us calculate the input derivative of (

\Phi^{\prime}(x)=\widetilde{W}_{2}\delta\Big[\frac{1}{\alpha}+\widetilde{W}_{1}\tanh^{\prime}(W_{1}x+b_{1})W_{1}\Big],\quad x\in(-1,1),

(5.2)

and assume

\Phi^{\prime}(x)\neq 0\iff\alpha\neq\frac{1}{-\widetilde{W}_{1}W_{1}\tanh^{\prime}(W_{1}x+b_{1})}.

(5.3)

For the following case distinction, denote the model parameter set of the ResNets considered as

\Theta:=\{(\widetilde{W}_{2},\widetilde{W}_{1},W_{1},b_{1},\tilde{b}_{1},\tilde{b}_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{6}\mid\widetilde{W}_{2}\neq 0,\widetilde{W}_{1}\neq 0,W_{1}\neq 0\}.

(5.4)

•

Case
0<\alpha\ll 1
:
In this case, Theorem
3.10
yields that no critical point exists for
\Phi
provided
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{|\widetilde{W}_{1}W_{1}|}
where

5.1.2
Multi-Layer ResNets

We now extend the analysis from the one-layer case to networks of arbitrary depth

Consider
\Phi\in\textup{RN}^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma,\textup{N}}((-1,1),\mathbb{R})
with
\varepsilon,\delta>0
.
To simplify the model (as often done in applications), we only look at layers with “outer” nonlinearities

h_{l}=\varepsilon h_{l-1}+\delta\tanh(W_{l}h_{l-1}+b_{l})=\varepsilon h_{l-1}+\delta\sigma(a_{l}),

(5.12)

where

,

with

for all

from (

), this corresponds to taking

and
\tilde{b}_{l}=0
for all layers

in (

). As in the one-layer example, we consider an identity input transformation and an affine output transformation, such that the input-output map is

\Phi(x)=\widetilde{W}_{L+1}h_{L}(x)+\tilde{b},\quad\widetilde{W}_{L+1},\tilde{b}_{L+1}\in\mathbb{R},\,x\in(-1,1).

(5.13)

The criterion that an input

is not a critical point naturally extends from the one-layer case of (

) to

)

\Phi^{\prime}(x)\neq 0\iff\alpha\neq\frac{1}{-W_{l}\tanh^{\prime}(W_{l}h_{l-1}(x)+b_{l})}\quad\text{for all }l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}.

(5.14)

Due to

for all

In fact, the remaining parameter regime can embed critical points. We see this by generalizing the explicit solution of (

) from the one-layer case with

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Phi
be a non-augmented ResNet of type (
5.12
)–(
5.13
) with
\varepsilon,\delta>0
,
L\geq 1
. Then an arbitrary input
x\in(-1,1)
is a critical point if and only if there exists an
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
such that the parameters
\alpha=\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}>0,W_{l}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},b_{l}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

W_{l}\leq-\frac{1}{\alpha},\quad b_{l}=\pm(\tanh^{\prime})^{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{\alpha W_{l}}\right)-W_{l}h_{l-1}(x).

(5.15)

Proof.

The function
\tanh^{\prime}(y)=\cosh^{-2}(y)
is an even function and bijective on
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,+\infty)\to(0,1]
and
(-\infty,0]\to(0,1]
. Then, with the notation
a_{l}(x)=W_{l}h_{l}(x)+b_{l}
, and assuming
W_{l}\leq\frac{-1}{\alpha}
, we get from directly differentiating (
5.12
) (cf. Lemma
2.12
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h_{l}(x)}{\partial h_{l-1}(x)}=\varepsilon+\delta W_{l}\tanh^{\prime}(a_{l}(x))=0

(5.16)

\displaystyle\iff a_{l}(x)=(\tanh^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}})^{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{\alpha W_{l}}\right)\quad\text{or}\quad a_{l}(x)=-(\tanh^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}})^{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{\alpha W_{l}}\right)

\displaystyle\iff h_{l-1}(x)=\frac{1}{W_{l}}\left(\pm(\tanh^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}})^{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{\alpha W_{l}}\right)-b_{l}\right),

(5.17)

where
\pm
is to be interpreted as positive
or
negative sign.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

•

Lemma
5.1
confirms that for
\alpha\approx 1
, critical points can be embedded with reasonable bounds on the magnitude of the model parameters.

•

The crucial restriction in Lemma
5.1
is the necessity of a negative layer parameter, namely
W_{l}\leq\frac{-1}{\alpha}<0
. The following implementations will show that the trained model approximations strongly depend on the sign of the different layer weights at initialization. The reason is that in our setting, the SGD iterations rarely change the weight signs during training. This relates to the influence of zero
input
gradients on the vanishing
parameter
gradient problem. These observations raise further questions regarding the implicit regularization of SGD in this context, which remain beyond the scope of this work.

•

Equation (
5.17
) also shows that, as
\tanh^{\prime}
is an even function, non-degenerate zeros of the layer-wise derivative appear in pairs, when considering
h_{l-1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
a_{l}\neq 0
. This can have the unwanted effect that the number of generated critical points “multiplies” from layer to layer (cf. Figure
12
).

Let us summarize the additional observations regarding ResNet expressivity that we gathered from the two-layer case study:

–

Which layer in the model hierarchy generates a critical point matters.
Whether a critical point is generated in the first or second layer can lead to qualitatively very different outcomes in the input-output map
\Phi
. Specifically, due to the (anti-)symmetry of the activation function
\sigma=\tanh
, critical points present in the first layer can be “multiplied” by the second layer, specifically for a dominant skip parameter
\varepsilon
.

–

Not only
\alpha
but also the values of
\varepsilon
and
\delta
matter.
The channel ratio
\alpha=\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}
governs the structural capacity to embed critical points, but the absolute scales of
\varepsilon
and
\delta
are equally relevant for the model’s expressivity (cf. Remark
4.11
). A small
\delta
forces
\Phi
toward near piecewise linear behavior (regardless of
\alpha
). Large values of
\varepsilon
(regardless of
\alpha
) can lead to catastrophic approximation.

–

Initialization matters.
In our one-dimensional setting the parameter training keeps the sign of the weights from initialization unchanged. This leads to SGD convergence to sign-based local minima. There are various design choices that can circumvent this problem, such as increasing the parameter dimension
m_{l}
in (
2.3
). Nonetheless, it shows that the parameter regime at initialization strongly influences the trained final outcome due to implicit regularization effects of SGD based algorithms
[
26
]
.

Parameter Training:
1400 training points are drawn from the

The trained models are all based on the class

and output dimension

6
Conclusion

In this work, we investigated the universal approximation limitations of non-augmented (or narrow) ResNets, focusing on their ability to express critical points. By parameterizing the layer update rule with a skip parameter and a residual parameter, we introduced the channel ratio
\alpha
. This framing established a unified mathematical perspective on narrow ResNet architectures, and allowed us to formally bridge the gap between continuous neural ordinary differential equations and standard feed-forward neural networks. The inability to express critical points in the input-output map fundamentally restricts a network’s global approximation capabilities. We proved that the lacking expressivity forces the network to shift required critical points outside the observation domain, which leads to a “tunnel effect”. In classification tasks with nested data, this means the decision boundary always has to intersect with the domain boundary which leads to undesired misclassifications.

We systematically categorized the embedding capabilities of narrow ResNets across three distinct regimes of the channel ratio
\alpha
:

•

The neural ODE regime (
0<\alpha\ll 1
): When the residual channel is small relative to the skip connection, ResNets act similar to continuous neural ODEs. We provided explicit upper bounds on
\alpha
below which the network strictly inherits the topological limitations of non-augmented neural ODEs, and hence is unable to embed critical points.

•

The MLP regime (
\alpha\gg 1
): When the residual channel dominates, the architecture reduces to a perturbed standard feed-forward neural network. We established lower bounds on
\alpha
above which the ResNet is equally incapable of expressing critical points, mirroring the constraints of standard narrow feed-forward networks.

•

The intermediate regime (
\alpha\approx 1
): In this balanced regime, typical of standard ResNets, the embedding of critical points is generally possible. The numerical examples demonstrated that the ability to successfully embed a critical point remains sensitive to the precise channel ratio and magnitude of the initialized layer weights.

While non-augmented ResNets can theoretically resolve the topological restrictions of their MLP and neural ODE counterparts, this structural advantage is fragile. Because of implicit regularization effects of SGD, models initialized in sub-optimal parameter regions may converge to solutions that exhibit the tunnel effect despite having the theoretical capacity to avoid it.

A.2
Proof of Proposition
4.9

Proof.

To prove part
(a)
, given a ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{0,\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
with the notation introduced in Section
2.2
, we construct an equivalent FNN
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{FNN}_{\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
as follows. We denote the layers and layer maps of the FNN with a dash to distinguish them from those of the ResNet.

•

As the ResNet
\Phi
consists of an input transformation
\lambda
, hidden layers
h_{0},\ldots,h_{L}
, and an output transformation
\tilde{\lambda}
, we consider an FNN with
L+2
layers as introduced in Section
4.1
. The input of the FNN is
\bar{h}_{-1}=x\in\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}}
, the hidden layers are
\bar{h}_{0},\ldots,\bar{h}_{L}
with
\bar{h}_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}}
, and the output is
\bar{h}_{L+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}}
, such that the FNN is a map
\overline{\Phi}:x=\bar{h}_{-1}\mapsto\bar{h}_{L+1}
.

•

The FNN layer dimensions are chosen to be
\bar{n}_{l}=n_{\textup{hid}}
for all
l\in\{0,\ldots,L\}
.

•

For the hidden layers
\bar{h}_{1},\ldots,\bar{h}_{L}
, the layer maps and parameters are chosen to agree with those of the ResNet, i.e.,
\bar{f}_{l}(\cdot,\bar{\theta}_{l})\coloneqq f_{l}(\cdot,\theta_{l})
and
\bar{\theta}_{l}\coloneqq\theta_{l}
for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
.

•

The layer map of the first FNN layer
\bar{h}_{0}
is chosen as
\bar{f}_{0}(x,\bar{\theta}_{0})\coloneqq\frac{1}{\delta}\lambda(x)
, such that
\bar{h}_{0}=\delta\bar{f}_{0}(x,\bar{\theta}_{0})=\lambda(x)
.

•

The layer map of the output layer
\bar{h}_{L+1}
is chosen as
\bar{f}_{L+1}(y,\bar{\theta}_{L+1})\coloneqq\frac{1}{\delta}\tilde{\lambda}(y)
, such that
\bar{h}_{L+1}=\delta\bar{f}_{L+1}(\bar{h}_{L},\bar{\theta}_{L+1})=\tilde{\lambda}(\bar{h}_{L})
.

Since
\lambda\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
m
\tilde{\lambda}\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
and
\delta>0
, the constructed layer maps
\bar{f}_{0}
and
\bar{f}_{L+1}
inherit the required regularity, i.e.,
\bar{f}_{0}(\cdot,\bar{\theta}_{0})\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{in}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}})
and
\bar{f}_{L+1}(\cdot,\bar{\theta}_{L+1})\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{hid}}},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
.

By construction it holds
\bar{h}_{-1}=x
,
\bar{h}_{0}=\lambda(x)
,
\bar{h}_{l}=h_{l}
for all
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
, and
\bar{h}_{L+1}=\tilde{\lambda}(h_{L})
. Hence the input-output maps of the ResNet
\Phi
and the FNN
\overline{\Phi}
agree:

\Phi(x)=\tilde{\lambda}(h_{L}(\lambda(x)))=\bar{h}_{L+1}(x)=\overline{\Phi}(x)\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathcal{X},

and part
(a)
follows.

For part
(b)
, the construction above can be reversed: given an FNN
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{FNN}_{\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
whose hidden layers all have the same dimension
\bar{n}_{l}=n_{\textup{hid}}
for
l\in\{0,\ldots,L\}
, we define a ResNet
\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{0,\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
by setting the input transformation
\lambda(x)\coloneqq\delta\bar{f}_{0}(x,\bar{\theta}_{0})
, the residual functions
f_{l}(\cdot,\theta_{l})\coloneqq\bar{f}_{l}(\cdot,\bar{\theta}_{l})
for
l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
, and the output transformation
\tilde{\lambda}(y)\coloneqq\delta\bar{f}_{L+1}(y,\bar{\theta}_{L+1})
. By the same argumentation as above, the resulting ResNet
\Phi
has the same input-output map as
\overline{\Phi}
, and part
(b)
follows.

In particular, if the input and output transformations
\lambda
,
\tilde{\lambda}
have the typical form (
2.5
), the statements hold for canonical ResNets
\Phi\in\textup{RN}_{0,\delta,\sigma}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
and MLPs
\overline{\Phi}\in\textup{MLP}_{\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textup{out}}})
as a special case, since the layer maps of canonical ResNets and MLPs are of the form (
2.3
) and hence fulfill the regularity requirements of Definition
4.1
.
∎

B.1
Proof of Corollary
3.8

Proof.

To apply Theorem
3.6
to the given neural ODE, we calculate the constants
M_{\theta}
and
K_{\theta}
. The Lipschitz constant of the vector field of the neural ODE with respect to the variable
h
is given by
K_{\theta}\coloneqq K_{\sigma}\widetilde{\omega}_{\infty}\omega_{\infty}
, as

\displaystyle\big\lVert\widetilde{W}

\displaystyle\sigma(Wh_{1}(t)+b)+\tilde{b}-(\widetilde{W}\sigma(Wh_{2}(t)+b)+\tilde{b})\big\lVert_{\infty}\leq\;\widetilde{\omega}_{\infty}\cdot\big\lVert\sigma(Wh_{1}(t)+b)-\sigma(Wh_{2}(t)+b)\big\lVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;\widetilde{\omega}_{\infty}K_{\sigma}\cdot\left\lVert Wh_{1}(t)+b-(Wh_{2}(t)+b)\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\;\widetilde{\omega}_{\infty}K_{\sigma}\omega_{\infty}\cdot\left\lVert h_{1}(t)-h_{2}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty},
