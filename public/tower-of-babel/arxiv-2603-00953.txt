Global solutions of the 2D inhomogeneous incompressible viscoelastic system Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author: Yong Wang, wangyongxmu@163.com
Chengfei Ai, Yong Wang ∗, Yunshun Wu

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the global existence of strong solutions for the inhomogeneous incompressible viscoelastic system with only velocity dissipation on ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} . Due to the criticality of the time-weight, the methods for the corresponding problem on ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} cannot be directly applied to the two-dimensional case. To overcome the main difficulties, we first transform the original system into a suitable dissipative system by introducing an effective tensor. Then we develop a new fractional time-weighted energy framework, combined with elegant commutator and bilinear estimates, to prove the global existence of strong solutions without the help of the common “div-curl” structure on the viscoelastic system.

1.
Introduction

Viscoelastic fluids, due to their rich rheological phenomena and a wide range of applications in industry and engineering (e.g., shampoo, ketchup, drilling mud, natural asphalt, paints, biofluids, polymer solutions, etc.), have received a lot of attention in recent years. As a specific type of non-Newtonian fluids, viscoelastic fluids are such materials that behave like both (viscous) liquids and (elastic) solids, which possess many physical properties that are different from Newtonian fluids, such as elastic instability and elastic turbulence at low Reynolds numbers (see e.g.,
[
16
,
55
]
). In this paper, we are devoted to the analysis of the inhomogeneous incompressible viscoelastic system (
1.1
), which is derived by using an energetic variational approach as done in
[
1
]
even though it has been directly proposed in
[
26
]
.

Precisely, we study the Cauchy problem of the two-dimensional (2D) inhomogeneous incompressible viscoelastic system:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\tilde{\rho}_{t}+u\cdot\nabla\tilde{\rho}=0,\\
\tilde{\rho}u_{t}+\tilde{\rho}u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=\mu\Delta u+c^{2}\diver(\tilde{\rho}\mathbb{F}\mathbb{F}^{\top}),\\
\mathbb{F}_{t}+u\cdot\nabla\mathbb{F}=\nabla u\mathbb{F},\\
\diver u=0,\qquad\qquad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\end{cases}

which is subject to the initial data

(1.2)

(\tilde{\rho},u,\mathbb{F})(x,t)\mid_{t=0}=(\tilde{\rho}_{0}(x),u_{0}(x),\mathbb{F}_{0}(x))\to(1,0,\mathbb{I})\quad\mbox{as}\quad x\to\infty.

Here
\tilde{\rho}>0
is the fluid density,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the velocity field,
\mathbb{F}\in\mathbb{M}^{2\times 2}
(the set of
2\times 2
matrices with positive determinants) is the deformation gradient of fluids,
p
is the pressure (the Lagrange multiplier), and
\mathbb{I}
is the identity matrix. The constants
\mu>0
and
c>0
represent the shear viscosity and the speed of elastic wave propagation, respectively. The superscript
\top
represents the transpose.

\tilde{\rho}\equiv 1

1.1

\mathbb{F}

\diver\mathbb{E}^{\top}=0

(1.3)

\nabla_{k}\mathbb{E}^{ij}-\nabla_{j}\mathbb{E}^{ik}=\mathbb{E}^{lj}\nabla_{l}\mathbb{E}^{ik}-\mathbb{E}^{lk}\nabla_{l}\mathbb{E}^{ij}.

Combining the “div” structure with the “curl” structure, Lei et al.

After that, Fang, Zhang and Zi

Until now, it is unknown that the global well-posedness in Sobolev settings for the inhomogeneous incompressible viscoelastic system (
1.1
) without using the “div-curl” structure in the 2D whole space. Due to the criticality of the time-weight, the processing method of the 3D system in
[

[

established the global well-posedness of the 2D homogeneous incompressible Oldroyd-B model in a periodic domain, whose results also hold for the homogeneous incompressible case of (

[

[

cannot be applied to the 2D whole space due to the failure of Poincaré’s inequality. Through some carefully designed fractional time-weighted energy framework, Chen and Zhu
[
5

proved the global existence of small solutions in the Sobolev setting for the homogeneous incompressible viscoelastic system in the 2D whole space. However, it is not enough to prove the global well-posedness of solutions for the inhomogeneous incompressible viscoelastic system (
1.1
) by the methods in
[
47
,
5
]
. The main difficulties are as follows: since the density in the system (
1.1
) is not constant at all, the velocity equation in (
1.1
) is a parabolic equation with the variable coefficient, and thus the pressure term
\nabla p
cannot be directly eliminated through the Helmholtz projection operator. At the same time, it is very tricky to capture the damping mechanism of density
\tilde{\rho}
and deformation
\mathbb{F}
. In order to overcome these difficulties mentioned above, we need to develop a more delicate fractional time-weighted energy framework as done in this paper.

The novelty of this article mainly lies in the following several aspects. Firstly, how can we deal with
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}-s_{0}}}^{1-\theta}\|\nabla u\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}^{\theta}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}
in (
2.3
) so as to close the assistant energy
\mathcal{E}_{a}(t)
? Due to the unboundedness of the integral
\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+t)^{-1}dt=\infty
, it is usually very difficult to obtain the dissipation estimate of
\|\nabla u\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}-s_{0}}}^{1-\theta}\|\nabla u\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}^{\theta}
. For this purpose, by virtue of a more refined selection of weighted indices
s_{0}
and
s_{1}
as stated in the following

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{7}-1}{3}<s_{1}<1,\quad\max\Big\{s_{1},\frac{3s_{1}^{2}-2s_{1}+2}{3s_{1}}\Big\}<s_{0}<\min\{1+s_{1},2s_{1}\}

such that
\frac{(1+2s_{1}-s_{0})}{2-\theta}>1
, from which it can be derived
\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+t)^{-\frac{(1+2s_{1}-s_{0})}{2-\theta}}dt<\infty
, then we can establish the estimate of (
2.3
). Similarly, we can also obtain estimates of (
2.3
) and (
2.5
).

Secondly, when estimating the basic energy
\mathcal{E}_{0}(t)
, the main difficulty is to deal with the pressure term
\frac{1}{\rho+1}\nabla p
in the first equation of system (
2.4
). Our idea is to transform the pressure
p
into
\tilde{p}
by
\frac{1}{\rho+1}\nabla p:=\nabla\tilde{p}
, so that we can overcome the pressure term
\nabla p
which cannot be directly eliminated through the Helmholtz projection operator.

Lastly, to get the estimate of the fractional time-weighted energy
\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)
as stated in Lemma
2.4
, several new and essential difficulties appear in the later analysis since the density is not constant. In particular, it is hard to estimate the terms
J_{5}
in (
2.5
) and
J_{10}
in (
2.71
). To control these two terms
J_{5}
and
J_{10}
effectively, we usually need the dissipation estimation of
\|g(\rho)\diver G\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}-s_{0}}}\|\nabla u\|_{\dot{H}^{1+s_{1}-s_{0}}}
, which seems impossible to achieve by the previous method in
[
5
]
. Fortunately, by defining the appropriate assistant energy
\mathcal{E}_{a}(t)
(which includes
\|\rho(t^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}-s_{0}}}
), we can prove that
\|\rho(t^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}-s_{0}}}
is bounded in (
2.3
). Moreover, we derive a new bilinear estimate in Lemma
A.5
, which is an extension of Proposition 2.4 in
[
5
]

\mathcal{E}_{a}(t)
and Lemma
A.5

J_{5}
and
J_{10}

2.5
) and (
2.5

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that the initial data
(\tilde{\rho}_{0},u_{0},\mathbb{F}_{0})
with

\displaystyle\frac{3s_{1}^{2}-2s_{1}+2}{3s_{1}}<s_{0}<2s_{1},\quad\frac{\sqrt{7}-1}{3}\approx 0.5486<s_{1}<1.

Then the Cauchy problem (

2.
Energy Framework

In this section, we first make appropriate transformations to system (
1.1
). Then we give various energies and useful result that play important roles in the subsequent sections.

2.1.
Reformulation

Since the specific values of the positive coefficients
\mu>0,c>0
in (
1.1
) are not essential in the present article, so we take
\mu=c=1
and define
\rho:=\tilde{\rho}-1
in the rest of this paper. Next, in order to effectively analyze (
1.1
), we provide the definition of the effective tensor
G
as follows

(2.1)

G:=\tilde{\rho}\mathbb{F}\mathbb{F}^{\top}-\mathbb{I}.

And we have

(2.2)

(\tilde{\rho}\mathbb{F}\mathbb{F}^{\top})_{t}+(u\cdot\nabla\tilde{\rho})\mathbb{F}\mathbb{F}^{\top}+\tilde{\rho}(u\cdot\nabla\mathbb{F})\mathbb{F}^{\top}+\tilde{\rho}\mathbb{F}(u\cdot\nabla\mathbb{F}^{\top})=\tilde{\rho}(\nabla u\mathbb{F})\mathbb{F}^{\top}+\tilde{\rho}\mathbb{F}\mathbb{F}^{\top}(\nabla u)^{\top}.

From (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we can derive the evolution for effective tensor
G

(2.3)

G_{t}+u\cdot\nabla G+Q(\nabla u,G)=2D(u),

where
Q(\nabla u,G)=-\nabla uG-G(\nabla u)^{\top}
and
D(u)=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\top})
.

Combining (
1.1
) with (
2.3
), the following new system is established

(2.4)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u+u\cdot\nabla u+(1-\frac{1}{\rho+1})\Delta u+\frac{1}{\rho+1}\nabla p+(1-\frac{1}{\rho+1})\diver G=\diver G,\\
G_{t}+u\cdot\nabla G+Q(\nabla u,G)=2D(u),\\
\diver u=0,\qquad\qquad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}.\end{cases}

2.2.
Various Refined Energies

Based on the analysis above, for any
t>0
, we can state the following various refined energies for the new system (
2.4
). We first define the following basic energy:

(2.5)

\mathcal{E}_{0}(t):=\sup_{0\leq t^{\prime}\leq t}(\|u(t^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{-s_{1}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}}^{2}+\|G(t^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{-s_{1}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}}^{2})+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\nabla u(t^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{-s_{1}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathbb{P}\diver G(t^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{-s_{1}}\cap\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)\,dt^{\prime},

where
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{I}+\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\diver
is the Helmholtz projection operator (cf.
[
57
]
).

Next, the fractional time-weighted energy given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{0\leq t^{\prime}\leq t}(1+t^{\prime})^{1+2s_{1}-s_{0}}(\|u(t^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{1+s_{1}-s_{0}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathbb{P}\diver G(t^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}-s_{0}}\cap\dot{H}^{1}}^{2})

(2.6)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\int_{0}^{t}(1+t^{\prime})^{1+2s_{1}-s_{0}}(\|\nabla u(t^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{1+s_{1}-s_{0}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathbb{P}\diver G(t^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{1+s_{1}-s_{0}}\cap\dot{H}^{1}}^{2})\,dt^{\prime},

where

are arbitrary given positive constants satisfying

.

Moreover, to obtain the uniform bound of

, we also need the following assistant energy

\mathcal{E}_{a}(t):=\sup_{0\leq t^{\prime}\leq t}\left(\|\rho(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{s_{1}-s_{0}}}^{2}+\|(\mathbb{F}-\mathbb{I})(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\right).

Finally, the total energy

is defined as follows

(2.8)

\mathcal{E}_{total}(t):=\mathcal{E}_{0}(t)+\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{E}_{a}(t).

Next, we also recall the following useful result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
[\mathbb{F}_{ij}]_{2\times 2},u
be a some smooth tensor and two dimensional vector, respectively. Then it holds that

\mathbb{P}\diver(u\cdot\nabla\mathbb{F})=\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla\mathbb{P}\diver\mathbb{F})+\mathbb{P}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\mathbb{F})-\mathbb{P}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta^{-1}\diver\diver\mathbb{F}),

where the

\nabla u\cdot\nabla\mathbb{F}
and
\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta^{-1}\diver\diver\mathbb{F}
are written as

\displaystyle[\nabla u\cdot\nabla\mathbb{F}]_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\partial_{j}u\cdot\nabla\mathbb{F}_{ij};

\displaystyle[\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta^{-1}\diver\diver\mathbb{F}]_{i}=\partial_{i}u\cdot\nabla\Delta^{-1}\diver\diver\mathbb{F}.

Proof.

See Proposition 3.1 in
[
64
]
.

2.3.
Estimates of Assistant Energy

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
\mathcal{E}_{a}(t)
is defined as in (

\mathcal{E}_{a}(t)\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{a}(0)+\mathcal{E}_{a}(t)\mathcal{E}_{total}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)+\mathcal{E}_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\mathcal{E}_{total}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)

Proof.

Taking
L^{2}-
inner product of the first equation in (
1.1
) with
\rho
, using Lemma
A.1
, Hölder and Young’s inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}-s_{0}}}^{1-\theta}\|\nabla u\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}^{\theta}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}},

where
\theta=\frac{s_{0}-s_{1}}{2+s_{0}-s_{1}}
. Integrating (
2.3
) with respect to
t^{\prime}
over
(0,t)
, we get

\displaystyle\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
