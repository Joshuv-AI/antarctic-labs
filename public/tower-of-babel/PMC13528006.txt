Carbapenemase-producing Enterobacterales infections: a real-world cohort study from a secondary hospital in Shanghai, China
Authors: Wen Zhu, Yi Liang, Feifei Dai, Chao Weng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCarbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales (CRE) have become a major global public health concern. The production and spread of carbapenemases have rendered carbapenemase-producing Enterobacterales (CPE) a severe clinical threat. Epidemiological data regarding CPE infections remain scarce in secondary hospitals. This study aims to characterize carbapenemase phenotypes and antimicrobial resistance profiles of CRE isolates from a secondary hospital, and explore factors associated with patient clinical outcome.MethodsA cohort study was conducted on clinical CRE isolates collected from January 2023 to September 2024. Carbapenemases were identified by immunochromatography. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression models were conducted to identify factors associated with clinical failure of CPE-infected patients.ResultsOf 131 strains from enrolled CRE infected patients, the detection rate of carbapenemases was 100% via immunochromatography. Phenotypes distribution revealed 98 (74.8%) Klebsiella pneumoniae carbapenemase (KPC), 25 (19.1%) metallo-β-lactamases (MBL) and 8 (6.1%) double carbapenemases-producer (DCP), with distinct antimicrobial resistance features across groups. Elevated aCCI (aOR 1.4, 95% CI 1.1–1.8, p = 0.007), mechanical ventilation (aOR 7.8, 95% CI 2.2–28.1, p = 0.002) and hospital-onset (aOR 6.7, 95% CI 2.0–23.0, p = 0.002) were independent factors associated with clinical failure.ConclusionThe secondary hospitals face intense carbapenemase prevalence pressure and complex resistance profiles, particularly among DCP strains. Patients with more comorbidities and hospital-acquired infection may develop poor clinical outcomes. Introduction

Enterobacterales represent a large family of aerobic Gram-negative pathogens prevalent in healthcare settings and frequently cause severe nosocomial infections, including pneumonia, urinary tract infections, bloodstream infections, and others. Owing to their strong adaptability and frequent antimicrobial exposure, Enterobacterales are highly prone to acquiring multidrug resistance (MDR), posing substantial challenges to clinical anti-infective therapy [1]. Over the past three decades, antimicrobial resistance among clinical Enterobacterales isolates has increased rapidly worldwide [2].

Carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales (CRE) have evolved into one of the most critical public health threats globally. CRE-associated infections are characterized by limited therapeutic alternatives, poor clinical responses, and extremely high mortality rates [3, 4]. The predominant clinical CRE pathogens include carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (CRKP), carbapenem-resistant Escherichia coli (CREC), and carbapenem-resistant Enterobacter cloacae (CRECL). The primary mechanism underlying carbapenem resistance is the production of carbapenemases, which hydrolyze and inactivate nearly all β-lactam antibiotics. According to the Ambler classification, clinically prevalent carbapenemases are categorized into three major groups: class A serine carbapenemases (predominantly Klebsiella pneumoniae carbapenemase, KPC), class B metallo-β-lactamases (MBLs, including NDM, IMP, and VIM types), and class D oxacillinases (OXA-type). Notably, these subtypes exhibit distinct antimicrobial resistance profiles [5]. According to the Expert Consensus on the Diagnosis, Treatment, and Prevention of Carbapenem-Resistant Enterobacteriaceae Infections in China [6], currently available therapeutic options mainly include novel β-lactamase inhibitor combinations such as ceftazidime/avibactam, polymyxins, tigecycline, and aminoglycosides, which are often used alone or in combination.

The World Health Organization (WHO) has listed antimicrobial resistance as one of the top ten global public health threats and prioritized carbapenemase-producing Enterobacterales (CPE) as critical pathogens urgently requiring new therapeutic strategies and optimized infection control measures [7, 8]. Rapid and accurate phenotypic identification of carbapenemase subtypes is essential for guiding individualized antimicrobial therapy, enabling early clinical intervention, and curbing nosocomial transmission and horizontal dissemination of drug resistance genes [9]. Currently, the majority of domestic and international CPE epidemiological data originate from tertiary hospitals, which primarily enroll critically ill patients. These studies have described the basic epidemiology, resistance patterns, and risk factors of CPE infections in high-level medical centers [10, 11]. Previous Chinese epidemiological studies have documented the predominance of KPC in CRKP isolates and significant phenotypic resistance differences between KPC and MBL producers [10, 12].

Nevertheless, epidemiological evidence regarding CPE infections in secondary hospitals remains insufficient. As an essential component of China’s hierarchical medical system, secondary-care institutions primarily provide general medical treatment, chronic disease management, and tertiary hospital transitional rehabilitation care. Patients admitted to these settings are characterized by advanced age, multiple chronic comorbidities, frequent cross-transfers between medical and long-term care facilities, high antibiotic exposure rates, and longer average hospital stays, resembling the functional characteristics of long-term acute care hospitals (LTACHs) [13].These unique population features create a distinct epidemiological environmental for CPE colonization and transmission, with a higher potential for silent nosocomial spread compared with tertiary hospital settings [14, 15]. However, restricted by laboratory testing resources, routine carbapenemase phenotypic detection is rarely implemented in most secondary hospitals in China, resulting in a severe lack of systematic, prospective CPE surveillance data from this key medical setting.

Against this background, this study conducted an observational cohort study in a typical secondary-care hospital in Shanghai to fill these research gaps. In contrast to previously published Chinese datasets dominated by critically ill tertiary hospital cohorts, our cohort enrolled inpatients with relatively lower acute severity but a high burden of chronic diseases and frequent hospital readmissions, representing the real-world CPE epidemic status in urban secondary medical institutions. This study aimed to systematically investigate the molecular epidemiological distribution of carbapenemase subtypes, antimicrobial resistance profiles, and clinical characteristics of CPE-infected patients, and to further explore factors associated with clinical failure. The findings may provide a basis for tailored infection prevention and control measures and clinical treatment strategies.

Materials and methods

Study setting

This was an observational cohort study conducted at a secondary hospital (686 beds) in Shanghai, China, from January 2023 to September 2024. Clinical isolates of CRE were prospectively collected, retaining only the first isolate per patient. Corresponding clinical and demographic data were collected from the Hospital Information System (HIS) and Laboratory Information System (LIS). Data included gender, age, hospitalization history, surgical interventions, comorbidities, age-adjusted Charlson Comorbidity Index (aCCI), laboratory results, invasive devices, and antimicrobial therapy. Inclusion criteria: incident CRE infection identified for the first time during the study period, and clinical diagnosis of CRE infection as defined in the diagnostic criteria. Exclusion criteria: (1) Hospital stay < 3 calendar days; (2) Bacterial culture positive without clinical signs of infection or suspected contaminated specimens; (3) Hospice patients without active anti-infective therapy; (4) Patients with incomplete or unavailable medical records.

Patients were followed up until hospital discharge, with antibiotic regimens, clinical outcomes, and hospitalization costs recorded. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethical Review Committee of Shidong Hospital. The ethics committee confirmed minimal risk for this observational study and granted a waiver of written informed consent.

Definitions

CRE were defined as isolates resistant to at least one carbapenem agent, including imipenem, meropenem and ertapenem. CRE infection was diagnosed when CRE was recovered from valid clinical specimens, accompanied by at least one of the following clinical evidences of active infection: (1) Typical signs and symptoms of infection; (2) Elevated inflammatory biomarkers; (3) Imaging evidence of the infected site consistent with infection at the corresponding site. Isolates with positive CRE culture results but without any above-mentioned infectious manifestations were defined as CRE colonization, and such colonized patients were excluded. Given that respiratory specimens accounted for the majority of our samples and respiratory colonization is prevalent among elderly and mechanically ventilated patients, we strictly implemented the above criteria to differentiate true infection from asymptomatic colonization for all respiratory isolates.

Hospital-onset infection was determined based on the 3-midnight rule stipulated in the Chinese Diagnostic Criteria for Nosocomial Infections. Patients whose first CRE-positive specimen was obtained more than 3 calendar days after hospital admission were categorized as having hospital-onset infection. Community-onset infection was defined as cases with the first CRE-positive specimen collected within 3 calendar days after admission.

Based on medical record review, clinical outcomes were classified into two groups. A positive outcome referred to cure or clinical improvement at discharge, while clinical failure indicated persistent or aggravated infectious manifestations or death.

Appropriate antimicrobial therapy was defined as either of the two scenarios: (1) Targeted therapy was adjusted to effective antibiotics within 48 h after microbiological results were obtained; (2) The initial empirical regimen was active against the pathogen and continued throughout the treatment course. A regimen was deemed appropriate if at least one selected antibiotic showed in vitro activity against the isolated pathogen.

Bacterial identification and antimicrobial susceptibility testing

Clinical specimens were processed for bacterial culture following standard laboratory protocols. Bacterial species were identified using the VITEK MS matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS, bioMérieux, Germany).

Antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) was performed by the VITEK 2 Compact automated system (bioMérieux) with GN 335 susceptibility cards. For antibiotics not included in the VITEK 2 test panel, the Kirby-Bauer disk diffusion method (Oxoid Ltd., Basingstoke, UK) was applied as a supplementary assay. We determined the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) via automated testing and measured inhibition zone diameters for disk diffusion assays. Susceptibility results were interpreted in accordance with the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) M100 guidelines. Two exceptions were applied for specific agents: tigecycline results were interpreted based on U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) criteria, while polymyxin B results were referenced to the European Committee on Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (EUCAST) guidelines. All polymyxin-resistant isolates were further confirmed using the broth microdilution method.

Escherichia coli ATCC 25,922 was used as the quality control strain throughout testing. All microbiological analyses and susceptibility reports were reviewed and released under the laboratory’s standard double-check system.

Detection of carbapenemases

The NG-test CARBA 5 Carbapenemase Assay Kit (colloidal gold immunochromatography, FOSUN DIAGNOSTICS) was used for in vitro carbapenemase typing of purified CRE isolates. Briefly, bacterial colonies were collected from MacConkey agar plates using a 1-µL inoculation loop and mixed with five drops of extraction buffer. The prepared suspension was then loaded onto the CARBA-5 test cassette. Results were interpreted after a 15-minute incubation at room temperature. A valid and positive result was defined as the appearance of a red control line (C line) plus one or more red bands in the specific reaction regions corresponding to NDM, KPC, OXA, VIM and IMP, indicating the presence of one or more carbapenemase types in the isolate. All test results were independently reviewed and confirmed by two laboratory technicians. Invalid test results or inconsistent interpretations between the two reviewers would lead to retesting with brand-new assay kits.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS26.0. Continuous variables were presented as median (interquartile range, IQR). The Mann-Whitney U test was used for two-group comparisons, and the Kruskal-Wallis H test was applied for comparisons among three independent groups. Categorical variables were summarized as counts and percentages. The Chi-square test or two-tailed Fisher’s exact test was adopted for two-group comparisons. Multiple rate comparisons were made by the Bonferroni correction.

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify factors associated with clinical failure. Results were reported as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographics and clinical characteristics of patients with CPE

During the study period, 51,960 patients were hospitalized. A total of 2,245 non-duplicate Enterobacterales isolates were collected, among which 258 were CRE (11.5% of all Enterobacterales). After excluding 89 colonizing isolates, 169 cases represented clinically infections, resulting in a CRE infection incidence of 3.25 per 1,000 admissions. Following sequential exclusions based on our inclusion criteria, 131 infected patients were enrolled, and carbapenemase detection was exclusively performed on these isolates. All three groups maintained high susceptibility to polymyxin B, with very low resistance rates (< 5%). Notably, while KPC and MBL producers showed minimal resistance to tigecycline (levels comparable to polymyxin B), the DCP group displayed distinct resistance characteristics: all 8 DCP strains were 100% resistant to tigecycline. Factors associated with clinical failure in CPE inpatients

Among CPE patients, clinical failure occurred in 23.7% (31/131). Univariable analysis identified several factors associated with clinical failure, including elevated aCCI, ICU admission, mechanical ventilation, central venous catheter, urinary catheterization, and hospital-onset infection. The distribution of outcomes also varied significantly by infection site. Notably, recurrent hospitalization within one year was less frequent in the clinical failure group compared to the positive outcome group. The overall proportion of patients receiving appropriate antimicrobial therapy was 42.7% (56/131), with no significant difference observed between the positive and clinical failure groups. Total hospital length of stay, total hospitalization costs, and antimicrobial expenditures were significantly higher in the clinical failure group compared to those with positive outcomes (p < 0.05, Table 4).

Given the limited number of clinical failure events (n = 31), we selected core covariates to minimize the risk of model overfitting. Markers of intensive supportive care (e.g., ICU admission, central venous catheter) and comorbidities reflecting underlying health status (e.g., cerebrovascular disease) were excluded from the final multivariable model. These factors represent intermediates along the causal pathway of severe illness and may introduce collinearity, potentially masking key study associations.

The finalized regression model included aCCI, mechanical ventilation, hospital-onset and infection site. After multivariate adjustment, higher aCCI (aOR 1.4, 95% CI 1.1–1.8), mechanical ventilation (aOR 7.8, 95% CI 2.2–28.1) and hospital-onset infection (aOR 6.7, 95% CI 2.0–23.0) were independently associated with clinical failure. Compared to urinary tract infection, respiratory tract infection (aOR 4.6, 95% CI 1.1–19.9) and bloodstream infection (aOR 8.3, 95% CI 1.5–46.8) were associated with a higher likelihood of clinical failure (Table 5).

Discussion

Carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales have emerged as a major global public health threat. Their extensive drug resistance leaves limited treatment options and leads to poor clinical outcomes [3]. The production of carbapenemases mediated by plasmids is the dominant resistance mechanism of CRE. This mechanism enables the horizontal transfer of resistance genes and accelerates the nosocomial spread of resistant strains [16]. Shanghai is a populous megacity with a sound hierarchical medical system, and numerous secondary general hospitals undertake healthcare services for local residents. These facilities differ from tertiary hospitals in terms of patient profiles, disease spectrum, medical resources and drug accessibility [15]. To date, systematic research on the epidemiological features, resistance profiles and clinical outcomes of CPE infections in secondary hospitals remains scarce. The present study collected real-world data on CPE epidemiology, antimicrobial resistance characteristics and prognostic factors in a secondary hospital in Shanghai city. The findings can provide evidence for regional infection prevention and control as well as individualized anti-infective treatment.

In this study, the overall CRE detection rate accounted for 11.5% of all non-duplicate Enterobacterales isolates. This proportion was higher than the surveillance data of secondary hospitals released by the China Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System (CARSS) in 2025 [17], indicating strong antimicrobial selection pressure and elevated drug resistance risks in economically developed regions of eastern China. The detection rate was lower than that reported in a European multicenter study [18], which can be explained by the fact that the European research only enrolled critically ill patients in intensive care units. The secondary hospitals in this study share similar operational characteristics with long-term acute care hospitals [13]. Our cohort exhibited distinct clinical epidemiological characteristics compared with previous domestic and overseas studies. The enrolled patients had a median age of 72 years, which was higher than the average age of 62 years reported in national CRE studies [3]. Most patients had multiple chronic comorbidities, with a median aCCI score of 6. More than half of the participants had a history of recurrent hospitalization within one year, which reflects frequent medical exposure among this elderly population.

Consistent with recent domestic surveillance results [12, 19, 20], Klebsiella pneumoniae was the predominant CPE strain in our cohort, and KPC was the main carbapenemase detected. Escherichia coli and Enterobacter cloacae primarily carried metallo-β-lactamases including NDM and IMP. A key finding was the 100% detection rate of carbapenemases via immunochromatography. Although molecular sequencing remains the gold standard, the high sensitivity and specificity of the assay verified by the expert consensus [6], combined with the absence of non-carriage in our isolates, underscore the intense transmission pressure within this healthcare network.

Interestingly, recurrent hospitalization within one year was more frequent in the positive outcome group than in the clinical failure group. This phenomenon may be attributed to survival bias. Patients who tolerate repeated hospitalizations generally have better baseline physical conditions. Frequent contact with healthcare settings increases their risk of CPE acquisition [15], and these patients may further transmit resistant strains to more vulnerable inpatients who are prone to adverse clinical outcomes. Despite the older age of our cohort, the overall clinical failure rate was lower than that reported in tertiary hospital-based CRE studies [19, 21], which may be attributed to the relatively milder disease severity of patients admitted to secondary hospitals and the high overall medical care level in Shanghai city, which also supports the existence of survival bias in this population.

Significant differences in antimicrobial susceptibility were observed among strains carrying different carbapenemases. Isolates producing single KPC and double-carbapenemase showed higher resistance to aminoglycosides, tetracyclines and cotrimoxazole compared with single MBL producers. The overall polymyxin resistance rate in this cohort was 3.1%, which is consistent with the range of 2.5% to 4.1% from recent CHINET multicenter surveillance data [22, 23]. Although polymyxins are recommended for the treatment of CRE infections, nephrotoxicity and neurotoxicity of polymyxins require full consideration in clinical anti-infective therapy, especially among elderly patients with impaired renal function [24]. Tigecycline exhibited excellent in vitro activity against single KPC and MBL producing strains, whereas DCP strains presented 100% tigecycline resistance, indicating the extreme drug resistance phenotype of redundant enzyme-producing strains and a severe clinical therapeutic dilemma [25]. Despite the small sample size of patients infected with DCP, the clinical significance of such resistant strains cannot be ignored.

Ceftazidime/avibactam displayed distinct susceptibility patterns against different enzyme subtypes. All KPC-producing isolates were susceptible to this agent, while MBL and DCP strains had high resistance rates ranging from 75% to 92%. Ceftazidime/avibactam is recommended as a first-line therapy for KPC-CRE infections in domestic and international guidelines [6, 26]. It should be noted that ceftazidime-avibactam was first approved for marketing in China by the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) in 2019, however, its formal inclusion into the formulary of our secondary hospital was delayed until February 2024. Due to its initial exclusion from the national reimbursement list, the actual clinical utilization rate remained low. This limited exposure likely accounts for the observed 100% susceptibility to ceftazidime-avibactam among the KPC-producing isolates in our cohort. Furthermore, previous multicenter studies have reported that approximately 6% of KPC-producing strains exhibit resistance to ceftazidime-avibactam [27, 28]. This result can be explained by the fact that most of the KPC variants that confer resistance to ceftazidime/avibactam show susceptibility to carbapenems and negativity to immunochromatographic tests and therefore they escape surveillance [29].

For MBL-producing CRE, combination therapy with ceftazidime/avibactam plus aztreonam was currently the preferred therapeutic strategy in CRE treatment guidelines (2021 edition) [6, 30, 31]. Polymyxins demonstrate limited efficacy, high toxicity, and a propensity to induce MCR-mediated resistance, underscoring the urgent need for novel agents [32, 33]. New antimicrobial agents including aztreonam/avibactam and cefiderocol have potent in vitro activity against metallo-β-lactamase producing strains [34]. Aztreonam/avibactam was approved for clinical use in China in 2025, and cefiderocol is expected to be launched in 2026. The 2026 Chinese expert consensus has recommended the two agents as the preferred regimen for MBL infections [35]. However, new drugs are usually added to the formularies of secondary hospitals much later than tertiary hospitals due to budget constraints and medical insurance policies. The limited accessibility of novel agents leaves clinicians with fewer treatment options when managing extensively drug-resistant strains. Although tigecycline is widely used in clinical practice and has good in vitro susceptibility against most CRE isolates, relevant expert consensuses indicate that this drug achieves low concentrations in blood and cerebrospinal fluid. Its therapeutic effect is limited for CRE-associated bloodstream infections and central nervous system infections.

It is important to note that the overall rate of appropriate antimicrobial therapy in this study was 42.7%, which is lower than the data from international studies [36]. This finding suggests that clinical antimicrobial management in secondary hospitals still needs further improvement. It is essential to accelerate the introduction of new antimicrobial agents to grassroots medical institutions. Meanwhile, continuous training for clinical pharmacists should be strengthened to improve their professional competency and clinical influence. Clinicians need to select antimicrobial agents rationally in accordance with relevant guidelines and local epidemiological characteristics.

Multivariate regression analysis demonstrated that elevated aCCI scores, hospital-onset, mechanical ventilation, respiratory tract infection and bloodstream infection were independently associated with clinical failure. Higher aCCI scores reflect heavier comorbidity burden and weakened immune function, which increase the risk of adverse prognosis. The association with hospital-onset infection is particularly complex, it likely reflects a confluence of unmeasured confounders, including greater underlying frailty, prolonged exposure to invasive devices prior to infection, and the overall intensity of healthcare utilization, rather than acting as a direct causative agent. In line with previous studies, respiratory and bloodstream infections were correlated with higher clinical failure risks compared with urinary tract infections, which reminds clinicians to implement intensive intervention for severe invasive CPE infections [11, 25]. Patients with clinical failure also had longer hospital stays and higher costs for medical care and antimicrobial treatment, which further reflects the clinical and economic burden brought by CPE infections among high-risk populations. Implementing bundled infection prevention and control (IPC) measures within healthcare facilities is essential.

This study has several limitations. First, the single-center design and modest sample size limit generalizability of the findings and preclude further stratified analyses. Second, constrained by laboratory resources and funding, carbapenemase detection relied solely on immunochromatographic assays, without molecular confirmation of resistance genes. Despite these shortcomings, this study was conducted after the COVID-19 pandemic, and all enzyme detection work was performed prospectively with standardized protocols, ensuring the authenticity and stability of real-world clinical data.

In conclusion, secondary hospitals in Shanghai face substantial CPE transmission pressure. Continuous resistance surveillance and epidemiological analysis are of paramount importance in these settings. Optimizing CRE therapy requires integrating infection sites, comorbidities, susceptibility data, and carbapenemase phenotypes to guide rational prescribing and the judicious use of existing antimicrobials, while advocating for the expedited adoption of novel agents at the secondary healthcare Institutions. Concurrently, a multidisciplinary IPC strategy incorporating active screening, and discharge alerts for CRE carriers is essential to disrupt the nosocomial-to-community transmission chain.
