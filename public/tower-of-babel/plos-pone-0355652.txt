Patient overlap, cohort-relative scaling, and biological context in cross-cohort evaluation of a mitochondrial proteostasis-associated score in triple-negative breast cancer

Xiaoyuan Weng, Yanqi Tang, Caiji Wang, Zhaofeng Huang, Xiyin Huang, Jinsheng Wang

ABSTRACT
Background: Public transcriptomic cohort evaluation can be distorted by patient reuse, dependence among study sources, and cohort-relative normalization. Objective: To evaluate the association between a fixed 15-gene cohort-relative mitochondrial proteostasis score and source-study-defined pathological complete response (pCR) across four accession-defined cohorts from three principal study sources. Methods: The fixed score comprised 15 genes with equal positive weights, a specified expected direction, and a platform-coverage rule. After patient-level deduplication and duplicate-aliquot collapse, 285 patients with strict triple-negative breast cancer, including 104 with pCR, were analyzed. Cohort-specific odds ratios (ORs) per one-standard-deviation score increase were pooled by restricted maximum-likelihood random-effects meta-analysis with modified Hartung-Knapp inference. Sensitivity analyses summarized three study sources, used Paule-Mandel heterogeneity estimation, and applied one fixed single-sample RankScore implementation. Results: The primary four-cohort estimate was OR 0.958 (95% CI, 0.639–1.437; P = 0.760; tau^2 = 0). The three-source REML estimate was OR 0.971 (95% CI, 0.492–1.914; P = 0.868; tau^2 = 0.0201). The RankScore sensitivity was imprecise (OR 0.613 per 0.10 RankScore; 95% CI, 0.148–2.542; P = 0.354). The overlap assessment identified 57 strict-TNBC patients duplicated between GSE20194 and GSE25055; treating them as an additional accession effect increased nominal n from 285 to 342 without adding independent participants. Contextual NeoTRIP, single-cell, and I-SPY2 analyses were included to characterize biological and treatment context. Conclusions: Across four accession-defined cohorts from three principal study sources, the evaluated score implementations did not show a reproducible association with pCR. Source-level estimates remained imprecise. Patient overlap, reference dependence, and limited independent study sources are key considerations in cross-cohort biomarker evaluation.

Introduction Pathological complete response (pCR) after neoadjuvant systemic therapy is associated with improved long-term outcomes in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), but a molecular correlate observed in one dataset is not automatically a clinically useful or broadly generalizable predictor [ 1 , 2 ]. A candidate biomarker should be evaluated across independent patients and study sources, and its interpretation should account for platform, treatment, specimen-processing, and broad biological programs such as proliferation and tissue composition. Public expression repositories make multi-cohort evaluation possible, but accession numbers are administrative identifiers rather than evidence of participant independence. Reuse of the same patients across accessions can double-count observations, place the same individuals in nominally separate cohorts, and narrow uncertainty without adding biological information. A second challenge is that cohort-specific standardization can make a transcriptomic score reproducibly calculable while leaving it dependent on the reference distribution used for scoring. MitoCarta3.0 provides manually curated mitochondrial pathway annotations, including chaperone and protein-quality-control functions [ 3 ]. Chemotherapy-surviving TNBC models have shown adaptive increases in mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation and mitochondrial fusion [ 4 ]; chaperones and protein-quality-control systems maintain mitochondrial proteome function under stress, providing a biological rationale to examine mitochondrial proteostasis in relation to treatment response. We used the MitoCarta3.0 Chaperones pathway to define an equally weighted 15-gene candidate score. We evaluated whether the fixed 15-gene cohort-relative score was associated with pCR across non-overlapping TNBC cohorts and examined the consistency and interpretation of that association across study sources. The analysis included patient-level overlap assessment, cross-cohort meta-analysis, a single-sample scoring sensitivity, and longitudinal and single-cell analyses of biological context. Materials and methods Study design, estimand, and reporting This retrospective, multi-cohort study used publicly accessible, de-identified transcriptomic and clinical data. The Medical Ethics Committee of Quanzhou Medical College determined that this secondary analysis of publicly available, de-identified data did not require ethical approval; no reference number was issued. No additional informed consent was required. Ethics approvals and consent procedures for the original data collection are described in the corresponding source publications and repository records. Reporting followed REMARK and STROBE principles [ 5 , 6 ]. The primary estimand was the association between the fixed 15-gene cohort-relative score and source-study-defined pCR. We estimated cohort-specific ORs for pCR per one-standard-deviation increase in the primary cohort-relative score and summarized these associations across four accession-defined cohorts. Because GSE25055 and GSE25065 arose from the same Hatzis/MD Anderson parent study series, we also summarized the evidence at the level of three principal study sources. The study evaluates cross-cohort reproducibility of an association; clinical deployment and treatment-effect prediction were outside the estimand. The primary clinical analysis included pretreatment tumors with strict TNBC annotation and a binary pCR versus residual-disease outcome. The retained accession-defined cohorts were GSE25055 and GSE25065 from the Hatzis taxane-anthracycline series [ 7 ], GSE32646 [ 8 ], and GSE163882 [ 9 ]. Treatment and pathologic-response definitions were retained as specified by the source studies rather than retrospectively harmonized. GSE32646 defined pCR as no residual invasive cancer in both breast and axilla. GSE163882 used the pathology-report definition from each contributing institution. For GSE25055/GSE25065, the source binary pCR label was retained; breast/node/DCIS details not separately specified in the accessible main report were not inferred. Patient-overlap and duplicate-aliquot assessment Before modeling, patient identifiers were harmonized using deterministic source-ID rules. In the GSE20194/GSE25055 audit, matching was based on patient identifiers; receptor annotation, pCR concordance, and expression similarity were secondary checks rather than matching criteria. Direct screening of public GSE20194 metadata for ER-negative, PR-negative, HER2-negative tumors with a non-missing binary pCR/residual-disease outcome identified 71 records. Three documented R1 repeat records (M233R1, M375R1, and M402R1) were collapsed to their underlying patients, leaving 68 unique patients. Fifty-eight patient identifiers were present in GSE25055; 57 satisfied strict-TNBC criteria in both accessions, while M180 had concordant pCR but discordant PR annotation and was not included in the strict-to-strict duplicate set. Ten GSE20194 patients had no matching GSE25055 identifier. Because participant reuse was extensive, GSE20194 was excluded as an independent primary cohort; the unmatched residual subset was not retained as a post hoc cohort defined by overlap status. In GSE163882, BA00095 and BA00096 mapped to patient S10-7089 [ 9 ]. Their expression-derived composite values were collapsed at patient level before final cohort re-standardization. All clinical analyses used one row per patient. To illustrate the effect of accession-level duplicate weighting, the 57 confirmed strict-to-strict duplicated patients were analyzed as a duplicate-only GSE20194 subset and that accession effect was naively added to the four primary cohort effects. This counterfactual was descriptive only and did not add independent participants. Pathway definition and score construction The primary score was anchored to the 16-gene MitoCarta3.0 Chaperones pathway [ 3 ]. The minimum 80% common-platform coverage rule was applied across GPL96, GPL570, and the I-SPY2 expression platform. Fifteen genes were reliably measurable across those three platforms: DNAJA3, FKBP10, HSPD1, HSPE1, MAIP1, NRDC, PHB1, PHB2, TIMM10, TIMM10B, TIMM13, TIMM8A, TIMM8B, TIMM9, and TRAP1. PHB was mapped to PHB1; the aliases C2orf47 to MAIP1 and NRD1 to NRDC were used where required. DNLZ was excluded because GPL96 lacked a reliable probe. GSE163882 was incorporated after confirming that all 15 target genes were present in its processed RNA-seq matrix. The 15-gene set, equal positive weights, expected direction (higher score associated with lower pCR probability), platform-coverage rule, and no-cutpoint rule defined the primary score specification used in this study. Project records describe this specification as fixed before the present score-specific pCR analysis, after related pCR analyses using a different mitochondrial gene set in overlapping public cohorts. This was a retrospective specification, not a public preregistration or a fully outcome-blinded development process. The analytical history and subsequent analysis changes are documented in S1 File . In the microarray cohorts, probes were mapped to unambiguous current gene symbols and multiple retained probes for a gene were summarized by their within-sample median. For GSE163882, TPM values were transformed as log2(TPM + 1), and rows with the same gene symbol after alias harmonization were summarized by their within-sample maximum. Each of the 15 target genes was z-standardized using all samples in that accession’s public processed expression matrix (222 profiles for GSE163882). For GSE163882, aliquot-level composites for BA00095 and BA00096 were averaged for patient S10-7089 before the final composite was re-standardized across the 89 retained patients. Formally, for target gene g and sample i in accession a, z_gia=(x_gia-mean_ga)/SD_ga; the equal-weight composite was m_ia=(1/15) sum_g z_gia; and the reported analysis score was S_ic=(m_ia-mean_c[m])/SD_c[m] within analysis cohort c after restriction to retained patients and patient-level aliquot handling. Changing the accession reference changes mean_ga/SD_ga, and changing the analysis cohort changes mean_c[m]/SD_c[m]; the same biological sample can therefore receive a different numerical score under a different reference distribution. The primary score is cohort-relative rather than a fixed single-sample clinical assay. Primary clinical models and meta-analysis Cohort-specific logistic regression estimated the OR for pCR per one within-cohort SD increase in the primary score. The raw-score model was primary. Exploratory models added a composite proliferation score and then proliferation, ESTIMATE immune score, and ESTIMATE stromal score [ 14 , 15 ]. A proliferation-only model provided directional context. A post hoc within-cohort residual score was obtained by regressing the primary score on proliferation, immune, and stromal scores separately within each cohort and then z-standardizing the residual. Because nuisance coefficients and residual scale were estimated in the same cohort used for the outcome model, this residual analysis was treated as biological decomposition rather than external validation. Cohort-specific log-ORs were pooled using random-effects meta-analysis with restricted maximum-likelihood (REML) estimation of between-study variance and modified Hartung-Knapp (mKH) inference [ 16 , 17 ]. The tau^2 = 0 boundary was compared with the numerical REML optimum. With only three to four analysis units, I^2 was treated as descriptive. As an estimator sensitivity, tau^2 was also estimated with the Paule-Mandel method while retaining the same mKH inference. The primary meta-analysis used the four accession-defined cohorts. In a source-structure sensitivity analysis, GSE25055 and GSE25065 retained separate accession intercepts and their accession-specific score scaling but contributed one common score slope for the Hatzis/MD Anderson source. That source effect was pooled with GSE32646 and GSE163882 to give three principal study-source effects. No model-specific attrition occurred in the retained score, proliferation, immune, stromal, residual, leave-one-cohort-out (LOCO), or rank-score analyses: GSE25055 contributed 112 patients/37 pCR events, GSE25065 58/20, GSE32646 26/10, and GSE163882 89/37, for a total of 285/104. Fixed single-sample rank-score sensitivity As an implementation sensitivity, we used one outcome-independent single-sample rank score with the same 15 genes and positive directions. The implementation used a 12,151-gene background universe measurable across GPL96, GPL570, and the GSE163882 RNA-seq matrix, together with defined alias and multi-probe handling. Within each sample, genes were ranked across this fixed universe and RankScore = [mean rank of the 15 target genes – (15 + 1)/2]/ (G-15), with G = 12,151. Ranks are ascending within sample across the fixed universe, and tied values receive their average rank. The primary effect unit was an OR for pCR per 0.10-unit increase in RankScore; ORs per one within-cohort SD were reported secondarily for scale comparison. The same 285 patients were analyzed. A single RankScore definition was applied uniformly across the four primary cohorts. This sensitivity reduces dependence on cross-patient gene-level z-standardization while retaining the upstream processed expression matrices. Proliferation, immune, and tissue-composition analyses Proliferation was represented by MKI67 , Hallmark E2F targets, Hallmark G2M checkpoint, and a standardized composite [ 14 ]. ESTIMATE immune and stromal scores were used as expression-derived tissue-composition proxies [ 15 ]. CIBERSORTx LM22 relative fractions were estimated with 1,000 permutations using platform-appropriate preprocessing [ 18 ], and MCP-counter supplied a broad-lineage analysis [ 19 ]. Spearman correlations and cell-fraction-adjusted logistic models were meta-analyzed across cohorts. Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction [ 20 ] was applied within the prespecified cell-type families. These analyses provided exploratory biological context. Leave-one-cohort-out and leave-one-source-out nuisance-relation diagnostics For four-fold leave-one-cohort-out (LOCO) residualization, nuisance slopes relating the primary score to standardized proliferation, immune, and stromal variables were estimated in the other cohorts with training-cohort intercepts. The held-out residual was expressed in units of the training-set residual SD, and a residual-only pCR model was then fitted in the held-out cohort. This residual-only model estimates whether the nuisance relationship learned in other cohorts transports to the held-out cohort; it estimates a different parameter from the direct multivariable model that retains the raw score and background covariates. For each held-out cohort we additionally recorded the training residual SD, the held-out residual SD expressed in training-SD units, and Pearson correlations between the held-out residual and proliferation, immune, and stromal variables. A source-level diagnostic held GSE25055 and GSE25065 out together; when both accessions were evaluated together, the pCR model used accession-specific intercepts and one shared residual slope. Source-level effects were pooled across the three principal sources using the same REML+mKH procedure. NeoTRIP paired longitudinal analysis The NeoTRIP analysis used 150 patients with paired baseline and D1C2 profiles from GSE319641 [ 10 , 11 ]. The raw 15-gene score was calculated from the provider-supplied linear-scale matrix: each target gene was z-standardized across all 401 supplied profiles using population SD, the 15 z scores were averaged, and the composite was again standardized across all 401 profiles. Within-patient raw-score changes were tested with paired t and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. The E2F, G2M, ESTIMATE, proliferation-composite, residual, and joint-change calculations used the same processed inputs. These decompositions were interpreted as exploratory context. Single-cell source analysis The analysis used the 10-TNBC subset of GSE176078 [ 12 ], represented by 42,512 cells in the processed single-cell data. Source-provided patient identifiers (`orig.ident`) and cell-type annotations were retained; `Cancer Epithelial` was used as the malignant epithelial compartment. Counts were aggregated by patient and major cell type, converted to counts per 10,000, transformed with log1p, and averaged across the 15 target genes. Patient-level pseudobulk was the inferential unit. For the descriptive cell-type display, patient-cell-type groups with at least 20 cells were retained and pseudobulk scores were centered within patient across eligible major cell types. For the paired malignant-versus-noncancer comparison, a patient required one `Cancer Epithelial` row and at least one non-cancer row with positive pooled counts. Eight of 10 TNBC patients met this rule; CID3946 and CID44041 lacked a `Cancer Epithelial` row. All malignant epithelial counts and all non-cancer counts were pooled separately within each eligible patient before the same counts-per-10,000, log1p, and 15-gene averaging steps. Paired scores were compared with the exact two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test. A cycling-state sensitivity was evaluated separately; the main comparison was malignant epithelium versus pooled non-cancer cells. Statistical software and reproducibility Core analyses were performed in Python 3.13.5 using pandas 2.2.3, NumPy 2.3.5, SciPy 1.17.0, and statsmodels 0.14.6; figures were generated with matplotlib 3.10.8 and Pillow 12.2.0. Discussion Across four accession-defined strict-TNBC cohorts from three principal study sources, the primary 15-gene cohort-relative score did not show a reproducible association with pCR. The primary four-cohort estimate was close to the null, the three-source estimate was similarly centered but less precise, and the fixed single-sample RankScore analysis was also imprecise. Together, the two fixed implementations did not identify a stable cross-cohort association. The overlap analysis shows why patient provenance matters in public-cohort biomarker studies. Fifty-seven strict-TNBC patients were represented in both GSE20194 and GSE25055. Treating those patients as an additional accession effect increased nominal n from 285 to 342 without adding independent participants. The pooled point estimate changed little here, but duplicate weighting can alter precision and effect estimates. Cross-cohort analyses should establish participant independence from source identifiers and provenance rather than accession labels alone. The score is reference-dependent. Each target gene was standardized against all samples in its accession-level processed matrix before the 15-gene composite was formed, and the composite was then re-standardized within the final analysis cohort. The implementation therefore measures position relative to study-specific expression distributions rather than a fixed single-sample scale. The fixed RankScore sensitivity reduced one aspect of cross-patient reference dependence. On a common one-within-cohort-SD scale, the primary-score estimate was OR 0.958 and the RankScore estimate was OR 0.868; both confidence intervals crossed 1. The two fixed implementations therefore produced similarly imprecise source-level inference. Direct adjustment and residual-only LOCO answer different questions. The LOCO residuals were scaled to training-set residual SDs, and nuisance relationships learned outside the held-out cohort did not remain orthogonal in every cohort. The LOCO analysis showed that the empirical relationship between the score and broad background programs varied across study contexts. The biological-context analyses add localization and covariate information. The score correlated with proliferation across the four primary cohorts, while immune and stromal relationships varied. Single-cell pseudobulk showed relative malignant-epithelial enrichment in eight paired patients. In NeoTRIP, the bulk-tissue score decreased early during therapy, while E2F/G2M/ESTIMATE-derived analyses provided proliferation and tissue-composition context. These results characterize the biological context of the score rather than a tumor-cell-intrinsic mitochondrial mechanism. Precision is limited by the small number of independent study sources. GSE25055 and GSE25065 are separate accessions from the same Hatzis/MD Anderson parent series, leaving three principal sources. The primary-score three-source confidence interval was approximately 0.49–1.91, and the fixed RankScore source-level interval was wider. With so few units, I^2 and tau^2 estimates are themselves imprecise; the effect estimates and confidence intervals are more informative than heterogeneity point estimates alone. I-SPY2 provides contextual information from a different receptor-defined, multi-arm treatment setting. The patient-level analysis included the adjusted and interaction models. Its HR-negative/HER2-negative eligibility and treatment structure differ from the strict-TNBC primary cohorts, so I-SPY2 was not included in the primary meta-analysis. Treatment-effect prediction requires a prespecified treatment-selection framework and reproducible score-by-treatment interaction evidence. Future validation should use independent study populations, a fixed single-sample scoring implementation or external reference distribution, prospectively specified preprocessing and analysis rules, and more independent study sources. Treatment-selection studies should prespecify score-by-treatment interaction analyses. Prior examination of related gene sets and pCR outcomes in overlapping cohorts leaves potential analytical flexibility despite the fixed specification of the present score. The study remains limited by its retrospective design, heterogeneity among public cohorts in treatment, specimen type, pathology definitions, expression platform, and available covariates, and only three principal study sources. pCR followed the source-study definitions. The primary analysis contains 285 patients, including one cohort with 26 patients and 10 pCR events. ESTIMATE and digital cytometry are expression-derived proxies rather than direct histology, and RankScore still depends on upstream processed expression matrices. NeoTRIP lacked linked patient-level clinical outcomes in the available data used for this analysis. The single-cell localization analysis included 10 tumors, with eight informative paired compartments, and used source-provided cell-type labels. These features limit clinical deployment, treatment-selection, and causal-mechanism inference. Conclusions Across four accession-defined cohorts from three principal study sources, the evaluated cohort-relative 15-gene score implementations did not show a reproducible association with source-study-defined pCR. Source-level estimates remained imprecise. Robust cross-cohort biomarker evaluation requires patient-level deduplication, explicit study-source accounting, transparent reference distributions, reproducible score construction, and clear separation of association, biological decomposition, and treatment-effect prediction.
