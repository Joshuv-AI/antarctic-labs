Residual quantum correlations and non-Markovian noise Preprint: SB/F/498-25
Hermann L. Albrecht, David M. Bellorin

Abstract
Wu et al. introduced residual quantum correlations (RQC) in 2015 and defined them in terms of two complementary bases. Given a measure for classical correlations, its optimization defines a local basis. Relative to this local basis, one defines a new one that is mutually unbiased to the first one. In the latter, the corresponding measure for quantum correlations is calculated. Local available quantum correlations (LAQC) define a measure for maximal RQC and were introduced by Mundarain and Ladrón de Guevara. In previous articles, we derived an analytical exact solution for this measure for 2-qubit X states. Using those results and deriving an expression for the RQC measure introduced by Wu et al., we analyze their behavior for two non-Markovian quantum dephasing channels: Random Telegraph (RT) and Modified Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (MOU) noises. We derive general conditions for sudden death and revival of RQC in X states and illustrate these results with three families of bipartite qubit states: Werner states, Maximally Nonlocal Mixed States (MNMS), and Maximally Entangled Mixed States (MEMS).



I
Introduction

The interaction of a system with its environment is unavoidable in real-world scenarios where we intend to implement quantum information tasks
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
;
Nakahara and Ohmi 2008
;
Vedral 2006
. Said interaction mediates the loss of coherence and is detrimental to quantum correlation, the core resource in quantum information science and technology. Therefore, the study and analysis of quantum open systems has become an intense field of research
Aolita et al. 2015
;
Schlosshauer 2019
.

The study of such systems oftentimes relies on the use of a master equation
Schlosshauer 2007
;
Campaioli et al. 2024
dderived from the Liouville-von Neumann equation for the system-environment state. When the Markov and Born approximations apply, these master equations are known as the Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan equation
Gorini et al. 1976
, which can be further simplified into a Lindbladian form
Lindblad 1976
. Although often called the Lindblad equation, it is commonly referred to as the Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan-Linblad (GKSL) equation
Manzano 2020
.

The quantum dynamical semigroup underlying the Lindblad equation allows us to express this time evolution as a map analogous to a superposition of transformations with a finite set of operators that obey a closure relation
Havel 2003
;
Nakazato et al. 2006
;
Andersson et al. 2007
. Such operators are labeled as the Kraus operators
Kraus 1971
;
Kraus 1983
of the quantum channel.

On the other hand, analyzing dynamics where the Markovian approximation is no longer valid broadens the type of physical processes that can be studied. Although many publications have studied such dynamics via a master equation
Gardiner and Zoller 2004
, in some cases, we can analyze non-Markovian quantum decoherence with an operator-based approach, analogous to the Kraus formalism. In particular, some proposals for non-Markovian quantum noise rely on the extension of the Kraus operators for Markovian quantum channels, such as the dephasing (phase damping) or phase-flipping channels
Daffer et al. 2004
;
Kumar et al. 2018
;
Thapliyal et al. 2017
;
Naikoo et al. 2019
;
Mishra et al. 2022
;
Sabale et al. 2024
;
Aiache et al. 2025
.

The memory kernel present in these non-Markovian master equations generally allows one to model colored noise
Gardiner and Zoller 2004
;
Benedetti et al. 2013
;
Benedetti et al. 2014
. Among these type of noises, several authors have analyzed Random Telegraph Noise
Daffer et al. 2004
(RTN) and the Modified Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Noise
Uhlenbeck and Ornstein 1930
;
Maller et al. 2009
(MOUN).

Studying the dynamics of multipartite open systems can become a challenging task for many-partite ones, where the complexity can increase dramatically with the number of subsystems involved. Nevertheless, there have been results that point to a universal behavior for the entanglement dynamics in the large-size limit of many-particle systems
Tiersch et al. 2013
. Practical applications in quantum information science and technology require the theoretical understanding of such large systems.

Despite this, a route often taken in this endeavor is to understand much simpler systems. In this sense, the simplest multipartite quantum system is a bipartite qubit system, and the so-called X states
Yu and Eberly 2007

Since quantum correlations are the main resource in quantum information, computation, and communication, it is essential that we understand how different types of quantum noises, whether Markovian or not, affect them. Although a large number of studies have focused on analyzing the dynamics of entanglement

;
Tiersch et al. 2009
, the introduction of quantum discord
Ollivier and Zurek 2001
;
Henderson and Vedral 2001
and other quantum correlations

has expanded the focus on the approach to quantum information resources and features. For instance, Naikoo et al.

studied the time evolution of Quantum Fisher Information

;
Braunstein et al. 1996

A rather mostly unexplored type of quantum correlations is residual ones

;
Wu et al. 2015

;
Bengtsson 2007
;
Durt et al. 2010

Mundarain and Ladrón de Guevara 2015

It should be noted that due to its definition, where the MUBs are defined in relation to a local basis that minimizes the measure for classical correlations and another where the measure for quantum correlations is maximized, LAQC can be regarded as Maximal RQC. In recent years, we have been able to derive exact results for its quantifier for Bell Diagonal States

;
Bellorin and Albrecht Q. 2021
as well as for 2-qubit X states
Albrecht Q. et al. 2022
;
Bellorin et al. 2022

;

In this work, we analyze the behavior of RQC under non-Markovian phase flipping decoherence, particularly in the cases of RTN

and MOUN

;
Kumar et al. 2018
. Given the way these phase-flipping channels affect them, we obtain general results for 2-qubit X states. We further illustrate them by studying three different state families, namely Werner states

;

(MEMS). Contrary to our previous results for Markovian decoherence

;
Bellorin et al. 2022

II
Brief recount of residual quantum correlations

In
Wu et al. 2015
, Wu et al. focused their analysis on quantum correlations in complementary bases, oftentimes labeled as residual quantum correlations (RQC). Although their analysis was aimed at discussing discord-like RQC, in a final appendix, they defined a symmetric correlation vector, which leads to the definition of a measure for classical correlations and the related RQC quantifier.

Given a computational basis
\quantity{\ket{i\,j}}
, the probability that the resulting state after a projective measurement is
\ket{i,j}
is given by
p_{ij}=\Tr\quantity(\Pi_i\otimes\Pi_j\,\rho^{AB})
. In this context, the classical mutual information, defined as

I\quantity(p_{ij})=H(p_{i}^{A})+H(p_{j}^{B})-H(p_{ij}),

(6)

where
H(p_{ij})=-\sum_{ij}p_{ij}\log_{2}p_{ij}
is the Shannon entropy
Shannon 1948
and
p_{i}^{I}=\sum_{j}p_{ij}
, for
I=A,B
,
characterizes the results of local measurements. Wu et al. defines the symmetric measure of classical correlations
C^{s}
as the maximal classical mutual information over all possible projective local measurements,

C^{s}\quantity(\rho^{AB})=\max_{\quantity{\Pi^{A}\otimes\Pi^{B}}}I\quantity(p_{ij}).

(7)

Since the above given measure defines a set of computational bases, there is also a corresponding set of complementary bases so that they are MUB. Such bases can be defined by considering transformations by the associated complex Hadamard matrix
Bengtsson 2007
;
Durt et al. 2010
,

\mathbbm{H}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,}}\matrixquantity[\lx@physics@matrix 1 & 1 \\
\vb{e}^{i\Phi} & -\vb{e}^{i\Phi}\endlx@physics@matrix]\;\;\in\;U(2),

(8)

where
0\leq\Phi\leq 2\pi
. Finally, Wu et al. defined their symmetric measure of maximal quantum correlation as the maximal classical mutual information over all possible projective local measurements on bases that are mutually unbiased with the ones defining the classical measure. That is,

Q^{s}\quantity(\rho^{AB})=\max_{\quantity{\tilde\Pi^{A}\otimes\tilde\Pi^{B}}}\quantity[\max_{\qty{\Pi^{A}\otimes\Pi^{B}}} I\qty(p'_{ij})],

(9)

where
\tilde{\Pi}^{I}
are the projectors to the complementary basis. The maximization over the projectors defined by Eq. (
7
) is only necessary if there is no unique basis maximizing the classical measure.

Inspired by these results, Mundarain and Ladrón de Guevara further explored RQC, introducing what they labeled as local available quantum correlations (LAQC)
Mundarain and Ladrón de Guevara 2015
. Contrary to the measure defined in Eq. (
7
), Mundarain et al. were interested in defining their measures in relation to the least classically correlated basis. Therefore, they define the classical correlations quantifier as the result of minimizing the quantum mutual information of a post-measurement state without readout over all possible von Neumann measurements. That is,

\mathcal{C}\quantity(\rho^{AB})=\min_{\quantity{\Pi^{A}\otimes\Pi^{B}}}I\quantity(p_{ij}).

(10)

The projectors involved in the above minimization define what is deemed as the optimal computational basis. The original state
\rho^{AB}
is rewritten in this basis so that the measure for LAQC is defined as the maximal quantum mutual information of a post-measurement state without readout over all possible von Neumann measurements using bases that are complementary to the optimal one. That is,

\mathcal{L}\quantity(\rho^{AB})=\max_{\quantity{\tilde\Pi^{A}\otimes\tilde\Pi^{B}}}I\quantity(p'_{ij}).

(11)

II.1
RQC of 2-qubit X states

As previously stated, 2-qubit X states are widely used in the literature. Therefore, obtaining analytical expressions for the measures of different quantum correlations for this particular family of states has been a goal within the quantum information community. For many quantum correlations beyond entanglement, such as quantum discord
Ollivier and Zurek 2001
;
Henderson and Vedral 2001
, an exact analytical expression is only possible
Girolami and Adesso 2011
;
Huang 2013
for a limited set of states, such as Werner and Bell-Diagonal states
Luo 2008
. For general X states, we have so far approximate analytical expressions
Huang 2013
;
Quesada et al. 2012
;
Liao et al. 2015
. For LAQC, on the other hand, we were able to compute an exact analytical result for general 2-qubit states in 2022
Albrecht Q. et al. 2022
;
Bellorin et al. 2022
.

Since the Pauli matrices, along with the identity, form a basis for the qubit density matrix space, 2-qubit states can be written in the Fano form
Fano 1983
as

\rho^{AB}=\frac{1}{4}\,\sum_{\mu,\nu=0}^{3}T_{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu}\otimes\sigma_{\nu},

(12)

where
T_{\mu\nu}=\Tr\quantity[\qty(\sigma_\mu\otimes\sigma_\nu)\rho^{AB}]
and
\sigma_{0}=\mathbbm{1}
. For X states (
2
), the five non-zero real parameters are
T_{30},T_{03},T_{11},T_{22}
, and
T_{33}
, which are related to the previous ones in (
2
) by

\displaystyle T_{30}

\displaystyle=a+b-c-d,

(13a)

\displaystyle T_{03}

\displaystyle=a-b+c-d,

(13b)

\displaystyle T_{11}

\displaystyle=2(s+r),

(13c)

\displaystyle T_{22}

\displaystyle=2(s-r),

(13d)

\displaystyle T_{33}

\displaystyle=a-b-c+d.

(13e)

Given the following expressions,

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}

\displaystyle=1+T_{i0}+T_{0i}+T_{ii},

(14a)

\displaystyle\beta_{i}

\displaystyle=1+T_{i0}-T_{0i}-T_{ii},

(14b)

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}

\displaystyle=1-T_{i0}+T_{0i}-T_{ii},

(14c)

\displaystyle\delta_{i}

\displaystyle=1-T_{i0}-T_{0i}+T_{ii},

(14d)

and given
u(x)=(1+x)\log_{2}(1+x)+(1-x)\log_{2}(1-x)
, we can define the following function

\displaystyle g_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\big(\alpha_{i}\log_{2}\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}\log_{2}\beta_{i}+\gamma_{i}\log_{2}\gamma_{i}

(15)

\displaystyle\mbox{ }\quad+\delta_{i}\log_{2}\delta_{i}\big)-\frac{1}{2}\quantity\big[u\qty(T_{0i})+u\qty(T_{i0})].

With the above expressions, we can write the measure of LAQC for 2-qubit X states as

\mathcal{L}\quantity(\rho_X)=\max_{i=1,2,3}\quantity\big{g_i\qty(T_{i0},T_{0i},T_{ii})}.

(16)

It should be noted that the above maximization also requires that we impose that the resulting function is well-behaved. That is, it has to be continuous, finite, and null for classical states, as is required by the properties of LAQC
Mundarain and Ladrón de Guevara 2015
. We must point out that these requirements are simply a consequence of the simplified way of presenting the quantifier.

Regarding the measure of residual quantum correlations introduced by Wu et al.
Wu et al. 2015
, we apply the same methodology presented in
Albrecht Q. et al. 2022
;
Bellorin et al. 2022
to determine their measures of classical and quantum correlations. Therefore, a direct calculation shows that

C^{s}\quantity(\rho_X)=\max_{i=1,2,3}\quantity\big{g_i\qty(T_{i0},T_{0i},T_{ii})}.

(17)

If the function maximizing the above expression is
g_{I}
, then the symmetric measure of maximal quantum correlation,
Q^{s}
, is given by the maximal function between the other two functions. That is,

Q^{s}\quantity(\rho_X)=\max_{i\neq{I}}\quantity\big{g_i\qty(T_{i0},T_{0i},T_{ii})}.

(18)

By comparing the above expression with the measure for LAQC in Eq. (
16
), we can readily realize that
\mathcal{L}\quantity(\rho_X)\geq{}Q^{s}\quantity(\rho_X)
.

III
Non-Markovian Quantum Noise Channels

Non-Markovian decoherence is an active field of research in quantum information science and technology. The assumption of Markovian dynamics imposes restrictions on the interactions studied that are not always realistic, especially when one is interested in real-life technological applications instead of theoretical explorations. In this context, analyzing more general situations, where non-Markovian dynamics prevail, is essential.

Although non-Markovian decoherence is generally studied via master equations
Schlosshauer 2007
;
Schlosshauer 2019
, there are some processes that can be studied as extensions of the Kraus operators for the Markovian case. Specifically, several studies focused on non-Markovian phase flipping and dephasing channels
Daffer et al. 2004
;
Kumar et al. 2018
;
Thapliyal et al. 2017
;
Naikoo et al. 2019
;
Mishra et al. 2022
;
Aiache et al. 2025
. In this section, we focus on two examples of non-Markovian phase-flipping channels.

For qubits, the Phase Flipping channel is directly related to the Pauli
\mathbbm{Z}
gate, and we can write the Kraus operators in their canonical form as
\mathbf{K}_{0}=\sqrt{1-p\,}\mathbbm{1}
and
\mathbf{K}_{1}=\sqrt{p\,}\sigma_{z}
, where
\sigma_{z}
is the corresponding Pauli operator, and
p\in[0,1]
is the channel parameter. It can be interpreted as the probability of the phase being flipped or as representing the time evolution, with
p(t)=1-\mathbf{e}^{-\lambda{}t}
. In what follows, we rewrite the Kraus operators as

\mathbf{K}_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{1+\Lambda(t)}{2}\,}\,\mathbbm{1},\quad\mathbf{K}_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{1-\Lambda(t)}{2}\,}\,\sigma_{z},

(19)

where
\Lambda(t)
depends on the particular noise being studied.

When modeling a common bath for both subsystems, we can write the resulting state as

\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{i,j}\mathbf{K}_{ij}\,\rho\,\mathbf{K}_{ij}^{\dagger},

(20)

where
\mathbf{K}_{ij}=\mathbf{K}_{i}\otimes\mathbf{K}_{j}
. For a general 2-qubit X state (
2
), with Bloch parameters
T_{30}
,
T_{03}
,
T_{11}
,
T_{22}
, and
T_{33}
, the phase flipping operation operators only affect the
T_{11}
and
T_{22}
parameters, which become

T^{(PF)}_{11}=\Lambda^{2}(t)\,T_{11},\quad T^{(PF)}_{22}=\Lambda^{2}(t)\,T_{22}.

(21)

Since these parameters are the ones involved in the coherences, when
\Lambda(t)=0
, the state becomes diagonal and, therefore, classical. For Markovian processes, this only occurs when
t\rightarrow\infty
and we only observe an asymptotic death of residual quantum correlations.

III.1
Random Telegraph Noise

Random Telegraph Noise (RTN), also called Burst Noise, is a type of quasi-discrete noise that occurs in electronic devices, especially in semiconductors and ultrathin gate oxide films
Simoen et al. 2011
;
Wold et al. 2012
;
Mulong Luo et al. 2015
. It is characterized by random and sudden step-like transitions in the vicinity of two or more voltage or current levels. Mathematically, this classical signal noise is modeled by a stochastic Markovian process called a telegraph or dichotomous random process
West 2006
.

Although the classical noise and its related statistical process are Markovian, this type of noise can be extrapolated to model random step-like transitions in quantum systems, generally a two-level system. For instance, modeling the random transitions in strong laser-atom interactions can be done by an analogous random process leading to a non-Markovian master equation, a process labeled by Eberly and collaborators as phase telegraph noise
Eberly et al. 1984
.

In 2004, Daffer et al.
Daffer et al. 2004
analyzed master equations defined beyond the canonical GKS generator
Gorini et al. 1976
by introducing a time-dependent integral operator, whose definition includes a well-behaved, continuous kernel function that depends on the memory effects involved in the physical model. With this memory kernel, the authors were able to introduce colored noise in their model and, therefore, depart from a Markovian dynamic.

The authors then studied the conditions for which their non-Markovian master equation defines a completely positive map. In doing so, they discussed the applications to non-Markovian phase damping. Building on these results, Kumar and collaborators
Kumar et al. 2018
adopted a quantum walk perspective to define the noise function
\Lambda(t)
(
19
) for RTN. Based on a damped harmonic oscillator, they obtained that

\Lambda(t)=\mathbf{e}^{-\gamma\,t}\quantity[\cos\omega\gamma\,t + \frac{\sin\omega\gamma\,t}{\omega}],

(22)

where
\omega=\sqrt{\quantity({2a}/{\gamma})^{2}-1\,}
is the frequency of the harmonic oscillator,
\gamma
is the fluctuation rate of the RTN, and
a
is defined within the autocorrelation function for this type of noise. It accounts for the strength of the system-environment coupling.

III.2
Modified Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Noise

The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process is a temporally homogeneous Gauss-Markov process introduced to model the velocity of Brownian motion in a viscous medium
Uhlenbeck and Ornstein 1930
. Some generalizations, specifically those associated with Lévi processes, have been applied in other areas such as financial modeling
Maller et al. 2009
. A modified version of the OU process has been defined as a non-Markovian random Gaussian process with a well-defined Markovian regime limit. Quantum mechanically, this type of noise can be observed in the spin degree of freedom of an electron interacting with a stochastically fluctuating magnetic field
Zhang and Mølmer 2020
;
Turner et al. 2022
.

The time-dependent channel parameter
\Lambda(t)
for the modified OU (MOU) noise is
Kumar et al. 2018
;
Sabale et al. 2024

\Lambda(t)=\mathbf{e}^{-\frac{\Gamma}{2}\quantity[(t+\frac{1}{\gamma}\qty(\vb{e}^{-\gamma{}t}-1)]},

(23)

where
\gamma^{-1}
is the finite correlation time with the environment, and
\Gamma
is the relaxation time, also referred to as the
T_{1}
time.

IV
RQC under non-Markovian Noise

As we already pointed out, the non-Markovian Phase Flip channel will only affect the
T_{11}
and
T_{22}

21

2

For the RTN’s

(24)

On the other hand, for the MOU noise, the

IV.1
Werner states

Introduced in 1989, Werner states are defined as the family of bipartite states that are invariant under local unitary transformations. For 2-qubit Werner states, they are usually written as

V
Summary and Conclusions

We studied the dynamics of residual quantum correlations (RQC) for bipartite qubit systems under a non-Markovian quantum dephasing channel. As channel functions, we used those that model random telegraph noise (RTN) and modified Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise (MOUN). We focused our analysis on X states, which are of special interest due to their theoretical and practical relevance.

Using our previous results obtained for
\mathcal{L}(\rho_{X})
, which quantifies local-available quantum correlations (LAQC), a type of RQC, we derived results for the symmetric measure of quantum correlations
\mathcal{Q}^{s}
introduced by Wu et al.
Wu et al. 2015
in 2-qubit X states. In doing so, we also demonstrated that
\mathcal{L}(\rho_{X})\geq\mathcal{Q}^{s}(\rho_{X})
.

Since the Kraus operators involved in this quantum channel only affect the coherences of the initial state, we were able to draw general conclusions for the dynamics of the two types of RQC analyzed under RTN and MOUN. In particular, we were able to observe the sudden death of RQC driven by RTN, a result we had not seen in our previous analysis using Markovian quantum channels. Moreover, this non-Markovian noise also led to the revival of RQC.

We analyzed three families of X states to exemplify our general results, namely Werner states and maximally nonlocal (MNMS) and maximally entangled mixed states (MEMS). To compare the behavior of RQC, we chose concurrence as a measure of entanglement.

While there is a range of the parameter that defines Werner states for which the measure of RQC is larger than concurrence, that is not the case for MNMS and MEMS. This was also reflected in the way the deaths and revivals of both measures behave for these states. In the Werner states, we found that the revivals of the RQC measure are greater than those of concurrence for a wide range of the parameter
z
. Even more so, for most states starting with a higher value of concurrence than RQC, after the behavior switched and the loss of concurrence is greater than that of RQC, up to
z\sim 0.9
, we have a larger measure of RQC than that of entanglement. For MNMS and MEMS, concurrence was larger than the measure of RQC for all
x
and throughout the system’s evolution.
