Dispersion of Anyon Bloch Bands
Kishore Iyer 1, Andreas Feuerpfeil 2, Valentin Crépel 3, Nicolas Regnault 4,5,6, Christophe Mora 1

Abstract
Fractional Chern insulators (FCIs) are zero magnetic field analogs of fractional quantum Hall states. While the electrons forming an FCI are not subject to an external magnetic field, their anyonic excitations experience a magnetic field with finite-flux due to a many-body Berry phase, whose lattice periodicity generically induces some dispersion. From Laughlin wavefunctions at filling ν = 1 / m \nu=1/m , we analytically construct single-anyon Bloch states in an ideal band, providing a basis to efficiently compute the dispersion. The anyon spectrum exhibits an m m -fold degeneracy in the reduced magnetic Brillouin zone (BZ), which originates from the topological degeneracy of the FCI. From our wavefunctions, we derive the m 2 − m^{2}- fold degeneracy seen in previous works, showing it to be a splicing of anyon momenta into the electronic BZ. Finally, we find that the anyon dispersion bandwidth is controlled by quantum geometry non-uniformity, growing linearly at weak modulation and saturating at strong modulation. Remarkably, higher harmonics of the quantum geometry alone strongly suppress the dispersion, which we attribute to emergent magnetic translation symmetries. When combined with the first harmonic, a positive (negative) second harmonic drives the system toward a second- (first-) harmonic-dominated regime, thereby reducing (enhancing) the bandwidth. Our results offer an analytically controlled method for evaluating anyon spectra in ideal band FCI, shedding light on how non-uniform quantum geometry and emergent symmetries shape the dispersion of anyons.

Electron and anyon translations—
\mathcal{B}(\bm{r})
encodes the quantum geometry of the single particle Chern band
63
;
64

while
\mathcal{K}(\bm{r}+\bm{l_{j}})=\mathcal{K}(\bm{r})

e^{\frac{1}{4l^{2}}\mathcal{B}(\bm{r_{i}})}

Meanwhile, the spinor

\chi(\bm{r}+\bm{l_{j}})=e^{-\frac{i}{2l^{2}}\bm{l}_{j}\times\bm{r}}\chi(\bm{r})

FCI locally feel a non-uniform magnetic field, but no net magnetic field.

Surprisingly, unlike electrons, anyons in a zero-field FCI do feel a net non-zero magnetic field. Under lattice translations of the anyon, the wavefunction picks up a Berry phase
e^{\frac{i}{2ml^{2}}\bm{l}_{j}\times\bm{r}}

As a result, even in the absence of an external magnetic field, the many-body Berry phase of the FCI wavefunctions renders anyons magnetic: they experience a non-uniform magnetic field of
1/m

Consequently, even when the single-particle (electronic) dispersion is flat, interactions together with non-uniform quantum geometry can generate a nontrivial dispersion for the anyons.

Discussion—
FCI anyons exhibit a non-trivial dispersion due to the non-uniform quantum geometry of the underlying Chern band. We construct anyon Bloch wavefunctions, which enable efficient computation of this dispersion. The spectrum exhibits an
m-
fold degeneracy in the anyon magnetic BZ, which we explicitly show to arise from the topological degeneracy of the FCI state. Relating the anyon and electron translation operators, we explain the
m^{2}-
fold degeneracy reported in earlier works to be a splicing of states in the anyon magnetic BZ into the electronic BZ. Moreover, the anyon dispersion of the exact Coulomb ground state shows strong agreement with our trial wavefunctions. The dispersion differs only by an overall scaling up to
s_{1}\sim 4
as shown in Appendix
I
, while for
s_{1}>4
, the groundstate is no longer an FCI
87
.

We find that the anyon dispersion is governed by the strength and the structure of quantum geometry non-uniformity. The bandwidth grows linearly with weak modulation, saturating at large modulation. Remarkably, higher harmonics suppress the dispersion; our analytical framework links this to emergent symmetries where the
n
th harmonic induces an
mn^{2}
-fold degeneracy, driving the system toward a uniform electron distribution. Our numerical results are presented for a simple cosine form of the quantum geometry for clarity; incorporating the single-particle quantum geometry of realistic moiré systems such as twisted bilayer graphene
95
or
\text{MoTe}_{2}

19
is a straightforward extension within our framework.

Our work demonstrates a high degree of analytical control over anyon excitations of FCI states. While we have focused here on the energy dispersion of one anyon, the analytical formalism developed here is quite general and paves the way for several future investigations. In the pursuit of exotic itinerant anyonic phases

73
;
88
;
116
;
90

60
, the topology of anyon Bloch bands may play an important role. Beyond single anyons, our formalism could be adapted to address the effect of anyon dispersion on interactions between multiple anyons
28

111

102

99

21

Appendix B
Electron translation operators and anyon magnetic translation operators

Translation operators move a particle from one position to another and, in a homogeneous system, commute with the Hamiltonian. However, for charged particles even in a uniform magnetic field, ordinary translations fail to commute with the Hamiltonian, since the particles acquire an Aharonov–Bohm phase when they move. This issue is resolved by defining magnetic translation operators, which combine a spatial translation with a compensating phase factor.

As explained in the main text, while electrons in a zero-field FCI experience no net magnetic field, anyons are
inherently
magnetic entities. Their motion accumulates a many-body Berry phase in real space, effectively mimicking the role of a magnetic flux. Hence, we define standard translation operators for electrons, and magnetic translation operators for the quasihole.

For electrons, the translation operator takes the form

T_{\bm{r_{j}}}(\bm{a})=e^{a\partial_{z_{j}}+\bar{a}\bar{\partial}_{z_{j}}},

(S12)

where the exponential differential operator translates the
j-
th electron coordinate, acting on the wavefunction as:

T_{\bm{r_{j}}}(\bm{a})\psi^{\sigma}[\{\bm{r_{i}}\},\bm{\eta}]=\psi^{\sigma}[\{\bm{r_{1}},\dots,\bm{r_{j}}+\bm{a},\dots,\bm{r_{N_{e}}}\},\bm{\eta}].

(S13)

Under this translation, the magnitude of the wavefunction picks up an Aharanov-Bohm phase, which is compensated by an equal and opposite phase coming from the spinor when the translations are multiples of unit cell lengths
34
. Similarly, we define quasihole magnetic translation operators from the way they act on the quasihole wavefunction:

T_{\bm{\eta}}(\bm{a})\psi^{\sigma}[\{\bm{r_{i}}\},\bm{\eta}]=e^{-\frac{i}{2ml^{2}}\text{Im}(\eta\bar{a})}\psi^{\sigma}[\{\bm{r_{i}}\},\bm{\eta+a}]\,.

(S14)

Under this operation, the quasihole is translated, and the wavefunction is multiplied by a compensating phase factor where the symmetric gauge is assumed. The factor
1/m
in the phase reflects that a quasihole carries a charge of
1/m
times that of the electron.

Importantly, and in contrast to the electron translation operators, the quasihole magnetic translation operators are defined only within the subspace spanned by the quasihole wavefunctions given in Eq. (
S2
). This has two main consequences: (i) they are not defined outside the quasihole subspace, and (ii) they act exclusively on quasihole wavefunctions, not on other functions that may depend on
\eta
, such as the normalization factor in Eq. (
S30
) that we will discuss in Appendix
D
.
Within the subspace of quasihole functions, the quasihole magnetic translation operators (MTO) satisfy the commutation relation:

T_{\bm{\eta}}(\bm{a})T_{\bm{\eta}}(\bm{b})=e^{\frac{i}{2ml^{2}}\text{Im}(b\bar{a}-a\bar{b})}~T_{\bm{\eta}}(\bm{b})T_{\bm{\eta}}(\bm{a}).

(S15)

Note that the projective phase gained by commuting the operators is independent of
\bm{\eta}
, depending only on the translations.

Let us now define quasihole translation operators over a
unit cell
:

T_{j}\equiv T_{\bm{\eta}}(\bm{l_{j}}).

(S16)

It is easy to show using Eqs. (
S5
) and (
S15
) that acting on the relevant subspace of wavefunctions, these satisfy the following projective commutation relation:

T_{1}T_{2}=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{m}}T_{2}T_{1}.

(S17)

Similarly, we define electron center of mass translation operators:

\tilde{T}_{j}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{N_{e}}T_{\bm{r_{i}}}(\bm{l_{j}})\,,

(S18)

which commute with each other. As we will show in Appendix
E
, these operators are related to each other by (up to phases):

\tilde{T}_{j}=\left(T_{j}\right)^{q_{j}N_{j}-1}\,,

(S19)

where

(q_{1},q_{2})=(N_{2},N_{1})~\text{mod}~m.

(S20)

\psi^{\sigma}[\{\bm{r_{i}}\},\bm{\eta}]=~e^{\frac{1}{4ml^{2}}\mathcal{B}(\bm{\eta})}\prod_{i=1}^{Ne}e^{\frac{1}{4l^{2}}\mathcal{B}(\bm{r_{i}})}~\chi(\bm{r_{i}})e^{iK_{\sigma}\left(Z+\frac{\eta}{m}\right)}\prod_{\gamma=1}^{m}\vartheta_{1}\left[\frac{Z+\frac{\eta}{m}-Z^{\gamma}_{\sigma}}{L_{1}}\Bigg|\tau\right]\prod_{i=1}^{N_{e}}\vartheta_{1}\left[\frac{z_{i}-\eta}{L_{1}}\Bigg|\tau\right]J(\{z_{i}\}),

(S39)

where the relative terms between the electrons are included in the Jastrow factor
J(\{z_{i}\})=A_{\sigma}\prod_{i<k}\vartheta_{1}\left(\frac{z_{i}-z_{k}}{L_{1}}|\tau\right)^{m}
, which remains unaffected by the translations applied in this Appendix.

We now translate the electron centre of mass by one unit cell in the
1-
direction, and the quasihole by
-(q_{1}N_{1}-1)
unit cells in the same direction, where
q_{j}
is defined in Eq. (
S20
). We get:

\begin{split}&T_{1}^{-(q_{1}N_{1}-1)}~\tilde{T}_{1}~\psi^{\sigma}[\{\bm{r_{i}}\},\bm{\eta}]\\
&=e^{\frac{2i(q_{1}N_{1}-1)}{4ml^{2}}\text{Im}\left[\eta\bar{l}_{1}\right]}e^{\frac{1}{4ml^{2}}\mathcal{B}[\bm{\eta}-(q_{1}N_{1}-1)\bm{l_{1}}]}\prod_{i=1}^{Ne}e^{\frac{1}{4l^{2}}\mathcal{B}(\bm{r_{i}+l_{1}})}~\chi(\bm{r_{i}+l_{1}})e^{iK_{\sigma}\left(Z+\frac{\eta}{m}+\left(N_{e}-\frac{q_{1}N_{1}-1}{m}\right)l_{1}\right)}\\
&\prod_{\gamma=1}^{m}\vartheta_{1}\left[\frac{Z+\frac{\eta}{m}-Z^{\gamma}_{\sigma}}{L_{1}}+\left(N_{e}-\frac{q_{1}N_{1}-1}{m}\right)\frac{l_{1}}{L_{1}}\Bigg|\tau\right]\prod_{i=1}^{N_{e}}\vartheta_{1}\left[\frac{z_{i}-\eta}{L_{1}}+\frac{q_{1}N_{1}l_{1}}{L_{1}}\Bigg|\tau\right]\,J(\{z_{i}\}).\end{split}

(S40)

By definition, the electrons do not experience an external magnetic field, so the combination
e^{\frac{1}{4l^{2}}{\cal B}(\bm{r})}\chi(\bm{r})
does not pick up an Aharanov-Bohm phase and is invariant under the shift by
\bm{l}_{1}
. The quasihole in contrasts acquires a Berry phase
(4ml^{2})^{-1}\{\mathcal{B}[\bm{\eta}-(q_{1}N_{1}-1)\bm{l_{1}}]-\mathcal{B}(\bm{\eta})\}
, which precisely cancels the factor introduced by the magnetic translation
(4ml^{2})^{-1}(q_{1}N_{1}-1)\imaginary(\eta\bar{l}_{1})
.
Now, turning to the second line of Eq. (
S40
), we use
N_{1}l_{1}/L_{1}=1
to get

\left(N_{e}-\frac{q_{1}N_{1}-1}{m}\right)\frac{l_{1}}{L_{1}}=\frac{1}{N_{1}}\frac{N_{\phi}-q_{1}N_{1}}{m}=\frac{1}{N_{1}}\frac{N_{1}(N_{2}-q_{1})}{m}=\frac{N_{2}-q_{1}}{m}

(S41)

By definition,
(N_{2}-q_{1})/m\equiv c_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, allowing us to use the quasiperiodicity relations of the theta function in Eq. (
S8
):

\tilde{T}_{1}~\psi^{\sigma}[\{\bm{r_{i}}\},\bm{\eta}]=(-1)^{c_{1}(m+N_{\phi})+q_{1}N_{e}}~T_{1}^{(q_{1}N_{1}-1)}~\psi^{\sigma}[\{\bm{r_{i}}\},\bm{\eta}]

(S42)

A similar relation can be deduced for translations along the second direction, where

\tilde{T}_{2}\,\psi^{\sigma}[\{\bm{r_{i}}\},\bm{\eta}]=(-1)^{c_{2}(m+N_{\phi})+q_{2}N_{e}}\,T_{2}^{(q_{2}N_{2}-1)}\,\psi^{\sigma}[\{\bm{r_{i}}\},\bm{\eta}],\qquad\qquad c_{2}=\frac{N_{1}-q_{2}}{m}\in\mathbb{Z}

(S43)

Thus, we find the following relation between the electron and quasihole translation operators:

\tilde{T}_{j}=(-1)^{p_{j}}\,T_{j}^{q_{j}N_{j}-1},

(S44)

which depends on the parity
p_{j}=\text{mod}\left[c_{j}(m+N_{\phi})+q_{j}N_{e},~2\right]
. This relation is intuitive; as illustrated in Figure
S1

j=2

Relation between the electron and quasihole momenta

Using the above relation between the electron and quasihole translations, we can relate the electron momenta
\tilde{k}_{j}
and quasihole momenta
k_{j}
by:

\tilde{k}_{j}=\left[k_{j}(q_{j}N_{j}-1)+\frac{p_{j}}{2}\right]~\text{mod}~1\,,

(S45)

where tilde denotes the electron momenta, while the modulus accounts for the fact that the latter lie in the interval
[0,1)
in both directions. We also remind that
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are, respectively,
N_{2}
and
N_{1}
mod
m
, as defined in Eq. (
S20
). The quasihole momenta
k_{j}
are quantized on a grid as given by Eq. (
S34
).
Let’s unpack what the transformation Eq. (
S45
) does. In the
1-
direction:

\tilde{k}_{1}=\left(-k_{1}+q_{1}\frac{\sigma-1}{m}+u_{1}\right)~\text{mod}~1

(S46)

where

u_{1}=\begin{cases}\frac{p_{1}}{2}+\frac{q_{1}}{2}\text{mod}(m-1,2)&N_{\phi}\in\text{even}\\
\frac{p_{1}}{2}+\frac{q_{1}}{2}\text{mod}(m-1,2)+\frac{q_{1}}{2m}&N_{\phi}\in\text{odd}\end{cases}

(S47)

follows from Eq. (
S34
), adding a constant shift to the momenta. This transformation is a rigid isometry on the circle
[0,1)
on which
\tilde{k}_{1}
is defined: it is a reflection combined with a simple rigid translation, effectively reversing the ordering of the electronic momenta (compared to anyon momenta) up to shift. This is confirmed by the constant step size
\tilde{k}_{1}[n_{1}+1]-\tilde{k}_{1}[n_{1}]=-1/N_{1}
(under the identification
0\equiv 1
). Consequently, any continuous degenerate structure in terms of anyon momenta is preserved (but re-indexed) in terms of electronic momenta. On the other hand, in the
2-
direction:

\begin{split}\tilde{k}_{2}&=\left(-k_{2}+\frac{n_{2}q_{2}}{m}+u_{2}\right)\text{mod}~1.\end{split}

(S48)

where
u_{2}=-q_{2}\text{mod}(m-1,2)/(2m)
. The term
n_{2}q_{2}/m~\text{mod}~1
takes one of the values

0,\,\frac{1}{m},\,\dots,\,\frac{m-1}{m}.

Therefore, as
n_{2}
increases by one, the value of
\tilde{k}_{2}
jumps between the
m
subintervals

[0,1/m),\;[1/m,2/m),\;\dots,\;[(m-1)/m,1).

Neighboring
k
-points (whose energies differ only slightly in the reduced Brillouin zone) are redistributed across these
m
domains, leading to the
m
-fold increase in degeneracy when the spectrum is plotted over the electronic Brillouin zone.

As an example, for
m=3
and
p_{2}=0

•

For
n_{2}~\text{mod}~3=0
:

\tilde{k}_{2}=\left(-\frac{n_{2}}{3N_{2}}\right)~\text{mod}~1

(S49)

Since
n_{2}/(3N_{2})\in[0,1/3)
, these values of
\tilde{k}_{2}
lie in the interval
[2/3,1)
for
n_{2}>0
while
n_{2}=0
stays at zero.

•

For
n_{2}~\text{mod}~3=1
:

\tilde{k}_{2}=\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{n_{2}}{3N_{2}}\right)~\text{mod}~1.

(S50)

Since
n_{2}/(3N_{2})\in[0,1/3)
, the above values lie in the interval
[0,1/3)
.

•

Finally, for

\tilde{k}_{2}=\left(\frac{2}{3}-\frac{n_{2}}{3N_{2}}\right)~\text{mod}~1\,,

and by the same reasoning as above, these values lie in the interval

Orthogonality of the anyon Bloch wavefunctions

The anyon Bloch wavefunctions in Eq. (
S30
) are also eigenstates of the electron translation operators. This can be verified explicitly by acting with
\tilde{T}_{1}
and
\tilde{T}_{2}^{m}
:

\tilde{T}_{1}\Psi_{k_{1}k_{2}}[\{\bm{r_{i}}\}]=e^{2\pi i\tilde{k}_{1}}\Psi_{k_{1}k_{2}}[\{\bm{r_{i}}\}],\qquad\tilde{T}_{2}^{m}\Psi_{k_{1}k_{2}}[\{\bm{r_{i}}\}]=e^{2\pi im\tilde{k}_{2}}\Psi_{k_{1}k_{2}}[\{\bm{r_{i}}\}]

(S52)

The relations between the electron and quasihole momenta are given by

e^{2\pi i\tilde{k}_{1}}=(-1)^{N_{2}}e^{iK_{\sigma}L_{1}\frac{N_{2}}{m}}e^{-2\pi ik_{1}}\qquad\qquad e^{2\pi im\tilde{k}_{2}}=(-1)^{N_{1}(N_{\phi}+m)}e^{-2\pi imk_{2}}

(S53)

which are strictly equivalent to Eqs. (
S46
) and (
S48
). Using the unitarity condition
\tilde{T}_{1}^{\dagger}\tilde{T}_{1}=1
, we obtain

\langle\Psi_{k_{1}k_{2}}|\Psi_{k_{1}^{\prime}k_{2}^{\prime}}\rangle=\langle\Psi_{k_{1}k_{2}}|\tilde{T}_{1}^{\dagger}\tilde{T}_{1}|\Psi_{k_{1}^{\prime}k_{2}^{\prime}}\rangle=e^{2i\pi(\tilde{k}_{1}^{\prime}-\tilde{k}_{1})}\langle\Psi_{k_{1}k_{2}}|\Psi_{k_{1}^{\prime}k_{2}^{\prime}}\rangle

(S54)

It follows that the overlap must vanish unless
\tilde{k}_{1}=\tilde{k}_{1}^{\prime}
. Similarly, inserting
(\tilde{T}_{2}^{\dagger})^{m}(\tilde{T}_{2})^{m}=1
shows that
\langle\Psi_{k_{1}k_{2}}|\Psi_{k_{1}^{\prime}k_{2}^{\prime}}\rangle
can be non-zero only if
e^{2i\pi m(\tilde{k}_{2}^{\prime}-\tilde{k}_{2})}=1
. Since
\text{gcd}(N_{2},m)=1
, the allowed electron momenta do not admit distinct values separated by a multiple of
1/m
, and this condition therefore implies
\tilde{k}_{2}^{\prime}=\tilde{k}_{2}
. This proves that the normalized Bloch wavefunctions Eq. (
S30
) form an orthonormal set

\langle\Psi_{k_{1}k_{2}}|\Psi_{k_{1}^{\prime}k_{2}^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta_{k_{1},k_{1}^{\prime}}\delta_{k_{2},k_{2}^{\prime}}

(S55)

Appendix F
Additional symmetries from higher harmonics

In this Appendix, we generically consider a Kähler potential of the form:

\mathcal{K}(\bm{r}_{i})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}4s_{n}\sum_{c=1,2}\frac{1}{|n\vec{b}_{c}|^{2}}\cos(n\vec{b}_c\cdot\vec{r}_i )

(S56)

where
s_{n}
is the strength of the
n-
th harmonic, while
\vec{b}_{c}
are the reciprocal lattice vectors of the torus. The first harmonic has the periodicity of the unit cell. If
s_{n}=0
for all
n>1
, as explained in the main text, the anyon feels the same background potential when translated by multiples of
l_{1/2}
. It follows that the operators
T_{1},T_{2}
commute with the Hamiltonian projected to the subspace of anyon Bloch wavefunctions.

The higher harmonics have a greater periodicity. For clarity, we focus on a single harmonic
N>1
, i.e.,
s_{N}\neq 0
and
s_{n}=0
for
n\neq N
. Then, the Kähler potential is invariant under translations by
l_{1/2}/N
and thus the anyon feels the same background potential under these translations. We introduce new operators:

T^{1/N}_{j}\equiv T_{\eta}(l_{j}/N).

(S57)

that commute with the Hamiltonian. Using Eq. (
S15
), we find the following commutators of these operators with
T_{1},T_{2}^{m}
:

\begin{split}T_{1}T_{2}^{1/N}&=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{mN}}T_{2}^{1/N}T_{1}\,,\\
T_{1}^{1/N}T_{2}&=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{mN}}T_{2}T_{1}^{1/N}.\end{split}

(S58)

With the above commutation relations, the action of these operators on the anyon Bloch wavefunctions are:

\begin{split}T^{1/N}_{1}\Psi_{k_{1},k_{2}}[\{\bm{r}_{i}\},\bm{\eta}]&=\Psi_{k_{1},k_{2}+1/(mN)}[\{\bm{r}_{i}\},\bm{\eta}]\\
T^{1/N}_{2}\Psi_{k_{1},k_{2}}[\{\bm{r}_{i}\},\bm{\eta}]&=\Psi_{k_{1}+1/(mN),k_{2}}[\{\bm{r}_{i}\},\bm{\eta}].\end{split}

(S59)

The operators
T^{1/N}_{1},T^{1/N}_{2}
circulate between the Bloch states labeled by momenta
\left(k_{1}+\frac{i}{mN},k_{2}+\frac{j}{mN}\right)
, with
i,j\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}
. Since these operators commute with the Hamiltonian, the higher harmonics lead to an
mN^{2}-
fold degenerate manifold in the anyon magnetic BZ, with the degeneracy being generated by the symmetry operators
T^{1/N}_{1},T^{1/N}_{2},
and
T_{2}
. From the arguments in Appendix
E
, this
mN^{2}-
fold degeneracy in the anyonic magnetic BZ will turn into a
m^{2}N^{2}-
fold degeneracy in the electronic BZ.

Appendix H
Symmetries and interplay of Kähler harmonics on a rectangular lattice

First, let’s just consider a single finite harmonic
s_{N}
and the associated Kähler potential

\mathcal{K}(\bm{r}_{i})=4s_{N}\sum_{c=1,2}\frac{1}{|N\vec{b}_{c}|^{2}}\cos(N\vec{b}_c\cdot\vec{r}_i )\,.

(S61)

By inserting flux
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=(\pi/N,\pi/N)
, we take
s_{N}\rightarrow-s_{N}
. As this just corresponds to shifting the anyon dispersion, the bandwidth and the overall shape of the anyon dispersion is unaffected by this flux insertion. Therefore, we can conclude that the dispersion for a single finite
s_{N}
only depends on its strength
|s_{N}|
, and not its sign.

Let us now consider the generic situation

\mathcal{K}(\bm{r}_{i})=4\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}s_{n}\sum_{c=1,2}\frac{1}{|n\vec{b}_{c}|^{2}}\cos(n\vec{b}_c\cdot\vec{r}_i )\,,

(S62)

where the smallest non-vanishing harmonic is
s_{N}
and we assume that also
s_{N+1}
is not vanishing. For a generic ideal band, we can expect all
s_{n}
to be non-vanishing and
N=1
.
For a flux insertion to map
s_{n}
to either
\pm s_{n}
,
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
have to be a multiple of
\pi/N
and
\pi/(N+1)
. As these numbers are coprime, we conclude
\phi=\pi
(if
N=1
,
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
have to be a multiple of
\pi/N=\pi
anyway, so in that case, we do not require
s_{N+1}
to be non-zero.).

Therefore, the only constraint by this flux insertion is that taking

s_{n}\rightarrow\begin{cases}-s_{n}&\text{ $n$ odd,}\\
s_{n}&\text{ else}\end{cases}

(S63)

leaves the shape of the dispersion as well as its bandwidth invariant.

In the special case where only
s_{1},s_{2}
are non-zero, we can conclude that the bandwidth only depends on
|s_{1}|,s_{2}
. Indeed, as shown in the main text, at fixed
s_{1}
the bandwidth of the anyon dispersion is suppressed with increasing
s_{2}
. On the contrary, a decreasing
s_{2}
enhances the bandwidth.

This behavior is understood by looking at the minima of the two harmonics. As shown in Figure
S7
(a), for
s_{2}>0
,
the minima of the second harmonic overlaps with the nodes of the first harmonic. Hence, at a fixed
s_{1}
, increasing
s_{2}
reduces the influence of first harmonic leading to a second harmonic dominated dispersion, which as shown in the main text and Appendix
G
has a negligible dispersion compared to that due to the first harmonic. For
s_{2}<0
, some minima of the second harmonic overlap with those of the first harmonic, as shown in Figure
S7
(b). Since the first harmonic minima are reinforced, this leads to a stronger dispersion compared to that at
s_{2}=0
.

Figure S7:

[(a)] and
s_{2}<0
[(b)]. For
s_{2}>0

s_{2}
is increased, the influence of the first harmonic on the anyon dispersion gradually decreases moving toward an

, some minima of the second harmonic (orange crosses) coincide with the first harmonic minima (dark regions). This deepens the first harmonic minima, leading to an enhanced dispersion.

We want to emphasize that this result is special to the rectangular lattice. On e.g. a triangular lattice as the one in tMoTe
2
, the minima (maxima) of the Kähler potential for e.g.
s_{1}
depending on the sign form a triangular (hexagonal) lattice. Flux insertion therefore does not take
s_{1}\rightarrow-s_{1}
and we cannot make any claims about the symmetry of the bandwidth with respect to the sign of
s_{1}
, consistent with the results obtained in Ref.
87
.

Flux threading

In the main paper, we use flux threading in Figure 1 and Figure 2 to increase the resolution for the quasi-hole dispersion and bandwidth. Threading a flux
\phi_{i}
in each direction shifts the single-particle momenta as
\mathbf{k}\rightarrow\mathbf{k}+\frac{\phi_{i}}{2\pi N_{i}}\mathbf{b}_{i}
. As the quasiholes have charge
e^{*}=-e/m
, their many-body momenta are shifted as
k_{i}\rightarrow k_{i}+e^{*}/e\cdot\phi_{i}
under flux insertion. For Figure 1 and Figure 2, we insert flux in increments of
\Delta\phi_{i}=2\pi/6
in both directions.
