Increasing intrinsic protein disorder improves CD8+ immunogenicity for a SARS CoV2 vaccine candidate antigen
Authors: Li Wan, Bingxian Xie, Masahiro Shuda, Maura E O'Malley, Carlos J Camacho, Greg M Delgoffe, Yuan Chang, Patrick S Moore
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCD8+ T cell targeting of the conserved SARS CoV2 RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp, nsp12) may offer a means for cross-protection against diverse coronavirus strains. Native RdRp, however, displays primary sequence immunoevasion (PSI) that inhibits in cis RdRp-derived peptide presentation on major histocompatibility complex class I. We show that this is related to the highly ordered nature of RdRp, which can be reversed by dividing RdRp into two intrinsically disordered, complementary sub-fragments (RdRpFrag). RdRpFrag possesses nearly all of the native peptide epitopes found in the full-length protein. Using a syngeneic MC38 cancer cell mouse model, cellular RdRpFrag expression suppressed tumor growth and markedly increased specific tumor-infiltrating effector T lymphocytes as measured by tetramer assays. mRNA vaccination with WA-1 strain RdRpFrag elicited more potent CD8+ T cell responses than native WA-1 strain RdRp mRNA but did not significantly prevent acute Omicron SARS CoV2 early infection in transgenic hamster challenge studies. These findings suggest highly ordered protein evolution may be an unrecognized PSI mechanism used by viruses to evade T cell recognition. Increasing antigen intrinsic disorder by protein subfragmentation may improve antigenicity for antiviral or antitumor vaccine candidates.Keywords: vaccine, SARS CoV2, RdRp/nsp12, intrinsic disorder, MHC I presentation, immune evasion

Introduction

Some DNA viruses, particularly those having proteins expressed during latent or persistent infection, encode proteins that evade major histocompatibility complex class I (MHC I) antigen presentation through cis-acting mechanisms embedded within their primary sequence, a process we call primary sequence immunoevasion (PSI).1 PSI can be mediated through sequence features such as G-quadruplex-forming mRNA regions,1,2 rare codon usage,3,4,5 or tandem internal repeat codons,6,7 all of which tend to stall ribosomal translation. Translational delay promotes the nascent protein folding into a stable peptide structure that reduces formation of defective ribosomal products (DRiPs),8,9 which reduces antigen peptide presentation required for CD8+ T cell priming and recognition.10 While these known genetic PSI features reduce immune recognition of viral proteins, it is not known whether viral protein folding into highly ordered structures also affects immunoprocessing. Cellular proteins with elevated predicted intrinsic disorder show increased MHC I presentation in the human proteome,11,12 suggesting that highly ordered viral proteins may also have PSI activity. This has importance in the design of subunit viral vaccines to elicit cellular immune responses.

PSI may be especially relevant to viral cancers in which effective CD8+ T cell responses are critical for control and clearance.9 It is well studied for the double-stranded DNA viruses Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) and Kaposi sarcoma herpesvirus (KSHV).7,13 EBV EBNA1 and KSHV LANA114 are latent proteins that escape MHC I recognition through use of mRNAs rich in internal repeats and G-quadruplex motifs.10,15,16 Deletion of these inhibitory domains enhances antigen presentation, offering promising approaches for candidate subunit immunotherapeutic vaccines for EBV- and KSHV-associated malignancies.17,18 In contrast, the relevance of PSI activity to other viral proteins, particularly for acute RNA virus proteins such as those of SARS CoV2, remains largely unexplored.1

SARS CoV2 RNA-dependent RNA polymerase/nonstructural protein 12 (RdRp/nsp12) is essential and highly conserved among SARS CoV2 strains, including variants of concern (VoCs) (Figure 1A). It is conserved also with RdRp proteins from other sarbecoviruses including SARS CoV (96.4% amino acid (aa) identity) and Middle East respiratory syndrome (MERS) CoV (71.3% aa identity) (Figures 1B and S1). Moreover, cell-mediated immunity against RdRp is uncommon following COVID yet provides cross-protective immunity against other coronavirus infections.19 Despite its essential role in viral replication, RdRp is rarely detected in postmortem lung tissues from patients who died of COVID-19.20 The polyprotein mRNA precursor nsp12 open reading frame lacks internal repeats and G-quadruplex sequences and is not known to have PSI CD8+ immunoevasion activity.

Here, we show that native SARS CoV2 RdRp likely evades MHC I presentation by folding into a highly ordered protein.21 Sub-fragmenting RdRp generated protein fragments with increased intrinsic disorder and increased levels of MHC I RdRp peptide presentation compared with the full-length protein. By dividing RdRp into two complementary fragments (called RdRpFrag), we maximized the fraction of disordered content and antigen presentation. In contrast, enforced degradation of RdRp using an N-terminal ubiquitin tag did not markedly increase protein immunogenicity. RdRpFrag, but not parental RdRp, generated tumor-infiltrating effector CD8+ T cells and inhibited tumor growth in a syngeneic cancer cell mouse model. An mRNA vaccine for RdRpFrag elicited high CD8+ T cell responses but did not generate sufficient sterilizing immunity to protect against acute Omicron XBB1 virus in an animal challenge study. These findings show that highly structured viral proteins can evade immune presentation, and this can be reversed by increasing protein intrinsic disorder through protein engineering.

Results

Native SARS CoV2 RdRp suppresses in vitro CD8+ T cell epitope presentation via primary sequence immunoevasion

We evaluated antigen presentation of SARS CoV2 RdRp (WA-1 strain) using a MHC I peptide presentation assay.8,22 Human 293 cells stably expressing the murine H2-Kb MHC allele were transfected with fusion constructs containing codon-optimized RdRp linked to enhanced green fluorescent protein (EGFP) and the SIINFEKL epitope from chicken ovalbumin (OVA) (Figure 2A). This construct enables simultaneous flow cytometry monitoring of protein expression via EGFP and peptide presentation on MHC I using the 25-D1.16 monoclonal antibody (Figure 2B). Antibody presentation was quantified as a presentation ratio (PR = Q2/(Q2+Q3)), reflecting the fraction of cells presenting SIINFEKL in the context of H2-Kb among all EGFP-positive cells.

As shown in Figure 2C, cells expressing RdRp-EGFPOVA exhibited a PR of 2.9%, which was substantially lower than positive control EGFPOVA fusion protein (14.7%), consistent with significant PSI for the full-length RdRp protein. To determine whether enforced degradation after translation improved antigen processing, we fused a cleavable ubiquitin moiety followed by an N-terminal alanine-arginine (per the N-end rule) to RdRp (Ubi-AR-RdRp-EGFPOVA).23,24,25,26 This construct had reduced half-life and increased SIINFEKL presentation approximately 2-fold relative to native RdRp (Figures 2C and S2).

RdRp fragmentation enhances MHC I peptide presentation

RdRp does not encode apparent G-quadruplexes (QGRS Mapper27) or internal repeats, and a codon-optimized RdRp was used in these experiments, so a reason for RdRp PSI activity in these experiments was unclear. To search for an RdRp PSI domain, we focused on known structural domains21 using tiling deletions to generate 10 overlapping sub-fragments (nos. 1–10) spanning the entire nsp12 open reading frame. Fragments were then cloned into the EGFPOVA cassette and tested for SIINFEKL presentation (Figures S3A and S3B). We were unable to localize PR suppression to a single domain (in contrast to EBNA12,15 or LANA110,28), but all fragments had increased SIINFEKL presentation compared with the full-length RdRp (Figure S3C). Two internal deletions of interface and finger domains (nos. 11 and 12, respectively) showed PR suppression values similar to full-length RdRp.

AlphaFold 3.029,30 pLDDT analysis of RdRp fragments in the EGFPOVA cassette (Figures 2D, S4, and S5 show results for fragments alone) was performed to determine whether structural differences could explain the immune presentation range. Full-length RdRp had 0.5% intrinsic disorder residues (pLDDT<50) compared with the 27.8% percent for all residues in the human AlphaFold Database proteome.31 While all fragments showed increased disorder relative to full-length RdRp, disorder was greatest for fragment no. 10 (366-581aa). Presentation for the fragments and full-length protein showed a linear correlation to predicted percent intrinsic disorder (R2 = 0.38, p = 0.03, Figure 2E), consistent with fragmentation disrupting RdRp folding. These predictions were confirmed using IUPred3,32 SPOT_Disorder,33 and DISOPRED334 (Figure S6). Presentation ratios also inversely correlated with fragment length and hydrophobicity and directly with net charge (Figure S7).

To determine whether combinations of sub-fragments could increase presentation, complementing fragments were expressed together, and the total PR was determined. Complementing fragmentation (e.g., an RdRp deletion fragment expressed together with its corresponding insert fragment on a second plasmid) was used so that the full range of potential human HLA allelic RdRp immunodominant epitopes could be present (Figure S3D). This required comparing different numbers of plasmids in multi-plasmid transfections. This was achieved by proportionately reducing the amount of each plasmid in multi-plasmid transfections so that total amounts of transfected EGFPOVA cassette DNA (2 μg) were the same and comparable to the single 2-μg RdRp-EGFPOVA plasmid transfection. Dividing RdRp into fragments increased PR from 2.9% for full-length RdRp up to 16.0% for two fragments (366-581 + Δ366-581) and 26.3% for three fragments (1–130 + 131–249 + 250–932) (Figure S3D). Fragmentation of RdRp into five fragments did not further increase presentation (PR 17.2%).

Based on these findings, we chose the combined complementing fragments nos.10 and 12 for further studies (hereafter called RdRpFrag). RdRpFrag fragment no. 12 encodes RdRp (Δ366-581aa) with the internal finger domain deleted, while fragment 10 encodes the corresponding highly disordered, finger domain insert (366-581aa). This choice was made since it had the highest PR among double fragments tested (Figures S3D and S2D). Although dividing RdRp into three or more fragments could generate even higher PR values (Figure S3D), these multi-combinations were impractical for the stable transfection and antibiotic selection experiments needed in subsequent immunologic studies. In MHC I presentation assays, the RdRpFrag combination increased OVA peptide presentation 3.6-fold over parental native RdRp protein (Figure 2C) and retained all potential parental epitopes except at fragment junctions. This enhancement in H2-Kb-positive 293 cell antigen presentation was consistent across a 40-fold range of DNA transfection doses (Figure S8) and using murine H2-Kb-positive B16-F10 melanoma cells (Figure S9).

Since RdRp does not have sequence hallmarks for PSI, we sought to determine if the unusually low RdRp intrinsic disorder reduced its stability and turnover. Click-iT L-azidohomoalanine (AHA, a methionine analog) pulse-chase labeling was performed for proteins expressing EGFPOVA, RdRp-EGFPOVA, or RdRpFrag-EGFPOVA. Cells were first preincubated with AHA in low methionine media for 1 h and then washed with Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium (DMEM) + 10% FCS at time 0. Cells were then EGFP immunoprecipitated over the time course, and AHA retention was determined. As seen in Figures S10A and S10B, normalized RdRp protein levels were markedly higher than RdRpFrag proteins during the first 10–60 min (the time frame for nascent protein DRiP turnover) but returned to similar levels by 130 min when mature protein turnover becomes predominant.9 Consistent with this result, cycloheximide treatment (100 μg/mL) also revealed RdRpFrag proteins to be less stable than full-length RdRp (Figures S11A and S11B), and immunoblotting in the presence of the proteasome inhibitor MG132 (20 μM) showed increased RdRpFrag proteasomal turnover compared with native RdRp (Figure S11C).

RdRpFrag-expressing cells exhibit increased susceptibility to lysis by activated OT-1 CD8+ T cells

To assess functional consequences of improved antigen presentation, we lentivirus transduced the antigen-EGFPOVA constructs into H2-Kb-restricted mouse MC38 colon adenocarcinoma cells.35 These cells were sorted by flow cytometry so that each construct stably expressed similar antigen levels as measured by EGFP fluorescence (Figure S12). The cells were then tested at various effector:target (E:T) cell ratios in cell killing assays with activated OT-1 effector CD8+ T cells recognizing the OVA epitope36 (Figures 2F and S13).

Native RdRp-expressing cells at E:T = 10 exhibited poor OT-1 cytotoxicity, similar to negative control EGFP-only cells lacking the SIINFEKL epitope (Figure 2F, red line vs. gray line). Consistent with presentation assay results (Figure 2C), ubiquitin-fused RdRp antigen (Ubi-AR-RdRp-EGFPOVA, purple line) modestly increased cytotoxicity, reaching significance by 12 h (Figure 2F). RdRpFrag-expressing cells (dark blue line), however, showed the highest susceptibility to OT-1 killing across all time points, surpassing even the EGFPOVA positive control (green line).

RdRpFrag inhibits antigen-expressing syngeneic tumor growth

Syngeneic MC38 mouse tumor studies allow in vivo assessment of CD8+ T cell responses to processed antigens.35,37 Naive 8-week-old C57BL/6 mice were subcutaneously injected in the flank with 2.5 × 105 of the MC38 cancer cells lentiviral transduced to stably express RdRp-EGFPOVA, RdRpFrag-EGFPOVA, or EGFPOVA (positive control) proteins, and followed for tumor development. Survival time was significantly reduced for mice injected with RdRp-EGFPOVA (Figure 3A, red line) compared with EGFPOVA (green line), consistent with PSI for full-length RdRp, and none of the six RdRp-EGFPOVA tumor-bearing mice survived longer than 17 days. Survival time, however, was significantly increased for mice injected with RdRpFrag-EGFPOVA (blue line) with one mouse each in the EGFPOVA and RdRpFrag-EGFPOVA groups surviving tumor-free until day 90 (Figure 3A). Tumor growth mirrored mouse survival with average tumor size for RdRp-EGFPOVA mice exceeding those of the RdRpFrag-EGFPOVA and EGFPOVA groups by day 9 (Figure 3B). Tumor growth curves were not significantly different between EGFPOVA and RdRpFrag-EGFPOVA groups (Welch’s t test with Holm correction).

RdRpFrag restores CD8+ responses to RdRp protein

To directly measure tumor-specific CD8+ T cell responses to the antigens, mouse tumor experiments were repeated with harvesting at day 8 after subcutaneous injection of MC38 cells expressing EGFPOVA, RdRp-EGFPOVA, or RdRpFrag-EGFPOVA. The tumor-infiltrating, draining lymph node and splenic lymphocytes were isolated and stained using the OVA TCR tetramer (PE-H2-Kb/OVA [SIINFEKL] MHC Tetramer, 1:100, NIH Tetramer Core Facility) by flow cytometry (Figures 3C and 3D). Marked reduction in OVA-specific total CD8+ T cells (Figure 3C) and effector (CD8+, CD44+, CD62L-) T cells (Figure 3D) was present in tumor, draining lymph nodes, and spleen tissues from mice injected with RdRp-EGFPOVA-expressing cells compared with EGFPOVA cells. Notably, minimal OVA-specific effector T cells were present in RdRp-EGFPOVA or Ubi-AR-RdRp-EGFPOVA-expressing tumors (Figure 3D). In contrast, mice injected with RdRpFrag-EGFPOVA had markedly increased anti-OVA total and effector CD8+ T cell populations in all tissues sampled that rivaled, and in some cases exceeded, the EGFPOVA control. The degradable Ubi-AR-RdRp-EGFPOVA construct did not increase OVA-specific total or effector T cells compared with RdRp-EGFPOVA. These data are consistent with nascent RdRpFrag protein, but not mature Ubi-AR-RdRp protein, turnover being responsible for reversion of RdRp primary sequence immune evasion.

To further characterize the functional state of these T cells, exhaustion markers were examined at day 16 post injection (Figures S14 and S15). Mice injected with RdRpFrag-EGFPOVA and EGFPOVA alone, but not RdRp-EGFPOVA, showed increased T cell exhaustion within the tumor microenvironment, as measured by elevated CD39+, PD-1+TIM-3+, and PD-1+LAG-3+ populations, along with reduced expression of the progenitor-associated transcription factor TCF1 (Figure S15). This is consistent with chronic antigen stimulation accompanying the enhanced antitumor immune response for EGFPOVA and RdRpFrag-EGFPOVA that is not present with RdRp-EGFPOVA. Thus, differential tumor responses to the antigens were not due to T cell exhaustion but instead were likely due to failure of full-length RdRp to provoke a T cell response.

RdRpFrag mRNA LNP vaccination elicits robust CD8+ responses

We next assessed whether RdRpFrag could function as an effective mRNA vaccine without EGFP or OVA tags. mRNA lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) were generated for full-length WA-1 strain RdRp (RdRp.FL) and each of the two RdRpFrag protein fragments (Figure S16A). For RdRpFrag vaccination, equal amounts of Δ366-581aa RdRp and 366-581aa RdRp mRNA LNPs were mixed to match the total mRNA dose of the full-length construct (Figure 4A). Administration of 5-μg doses, for example, employed 5 μg of RdRp.FL mRNA while 2.5 μg Δ366-581aa RdRp +2.5 μg 366-581aa RdRp mRNAs were mixed to generate 5 μg total of RdRpFrag mRNA. Vaccine protein expression was confirmed by immunostaining after transfection into 293 cells20 (Figure S16B).

C57Bl/6 mice were primed with 5 μg mock (RdRp mRNA without mRNA capping) or 0.2, 0.5, 2, or 5 μg of RdRp.FL or RdRpFrag vaccines on day 0 and boosted at the same doses on day 4, with spleen and lymph node CD8+ T cells harvested for ELISpot testing on day 9 (Figure 4A). All vaccinations were performed using subcutaneous injection to maximize antigen-presenting cell uptake. RdRpFrag failed to prevent acute VoC SARS CoV2 infection

To determine if RdRpFrag can prevent acute SARS CoV2 VoC infection, a prime/boost/challenge vaccination was performed on transgenic hACE2 hamsters using XBB.1.5 Omicron with RdRpFrag and RdRp mRNA LNPs, and compared with the irrelevant MuVG19/HN mumps virus hemagglutinin-neuraminidase mRNA vaccine.38 Hamsters were subcutaneously vaccinated day 0 and boosted day 21 with 5 μg of mRNA LNPs and then challenged with SARS CoV2 XBB.1.5: NR-59105 at 105 PFU on day 42. No significant differences in daily weight change occurred for RdRpFrag-vaccinated hamsters compared with RdRp- or MuVG19/HN-vaccinated hamsters up to 4 days post challenge (Figure 4D). Similarly, SARS CoV2 virus titers determined by RT-qPCR loads and virus plaque-forming units from nasal washes and oral swabs gathered 2 and 4 days after infection did not differ among the three mRNA vaccines (Figures S19A–S19H). Lung tissues harvested at 4 days after infection also showed no significant differences in SARS CoV2 RT-qPCR loads or virus titers (Figures S19I and S19J). Thus, although RdRpFrag enhanced CD8+ T cell priming, it did not confer protection against acute upper respiratory tract infection in this animal model.

Discussion

We find that the conserved SARS CoV2 RdRp protein is not efficiently processed by the MHC I apparatus and does not elicit an active CTL response. Sequence analysis failed to identify unique features (e.g., coding G-quadruplexes) that typically stall translation. We find instead that the unusually high order for RdRp protein reduces DRiP formation and inhibits cellular immunoprocessing for this protein (Figures 2D and 2E; S10). This is consistent with studies showing elevated rates of proteasomal turnover for proteins with high intrinsic disorder due to enhanced engagement of the target protein with the proteasome.39 Surveys of the human MHC I immunopeptidome reveal intrinsically disordered peptides are also associated with increased antigen presentation.11 These studies support the evidence presented here indicating that nascent protein folding prevents RdRp from entering into the immune sampling machinery. It is not known whether this is a common mechanism for viral escape from immune surveillance.

This effect, however, can be reversed by dividing the full-length RdRp protein into two fragments, each having greater intrinsic disorder and nascent protein turnover. Enforced degradation of full-length RdRp via the N-end rule pathway (Ubi-AR-RdRp) did not boost immunogenicity to similar levels found by fragmentation (Figures 2F, 3C, and 3D), which is most consistent with nascent fragment processing primarily being responsible for antigenicity. While we found the two-fragment RdRpFrag vaccine to be highly antigenic, splitting RdRp into three fragments significantly boosted antigen presentation even further (Figure S3D). The three-fragment candidate could not be explored in immunologic studies due to the technical difficulties in stably expressing three proteins simultaneously. If confirmed by others, these findings provide a convenient and low-cost method for choosing optimal subunit CTL protein antigen candidates based on intrinsic disorder for testing. If a protein of interest has both low intrinsic disorder and low antigenicity, scanning for highly-disordered complementing fragments might provide candidates with improved antigenicity while preserving epitope diversity.

One goal for this study was to explore the feasibility for a universal SARS CoV2 vaccine based on the highly conserved RdRp protein. We found no significant protective immunity, however, against VoC XBB.1 virus challenge by WA-1 strain RdRpFrag vaccination in a limited trial (Figure 4D). Importantly, the lack of primary protective efficacy for RdRpFrag was not unexpected since it mirrors challenges seen for other CD8+ T cell-based vaccines, such as those targeting HIV, where low effector:target cell ratios during early infection stages may limit acute protective efficacy.40

It is also important to acknowledge the constraints in our vaccination trial. Animals were only monitored for 4 days post infection due to protocol limitations, and this brief window may have precluded detection of other subacute or long-term benefits such as reductions in severe disease, prolonged infection, or virus shedding. CD8+ T cell responses are known to reduce disease severity and accelerate recovery for influenza infection,41 but this was not assessed in our challenge studies. It is also possible that the two variant amino acids between XBB.1 and WA-1 RdRp mask a critical immunodominant epitope, which could be overcome with an Omicron strain-based RdRp vaccine. Further, post-autopsy immunohistochemistry studies reveal paradoxical low RdRp expression in severe COVID tissues.20 Other conserved nonstructural proteins, such as nsp13 and nsp14, might be more highly expressed and could serve as more effective CTL vaccine targets.42,43 These proteins could be readily assessed and modified to enhance immunogenicity using the same pipeline described here for RdRp/nsp12.

Our results may have more relevance to cancers associated with chronic tumor virus infections. Nearly one-fifth of cancers globally are caused by only seven tumor viruses.44 For these virus-associated cancers, subunit vaccines targeting CD8+ T cell responses against viral oncogenes are an attractive therapeutic strategy, particularly when delivered using mRNA or viral vectors. This approach is especially appealing as therapeutic vaccines for populations in resource-limited settings. All seven oncogenic viruses establish chronic infections and are under strong evolutionary pressure to evade host CD8+ T cell surveillance.45 Our findings suggest a generalizable approach to overcoming tumor virus protein PSI by combining MHC I presentation assays with computational prediction of protein disorder and other PSI features to re-engineer viral antigens for enhanced cellular antigenicity.

Materials and methods

Plasmids

Construction of EGFPOVA and EGFP-PESTOVA fusion constructs has been previously described.10 To generate the RdRp-EGFPOVA fusion construct, a codon-optimized SARS CoV2 RdRp coding sequence (Table S1) was synthesized (Integrated DNA Technologies, Coralville, IA) and cloned upstream of the EGFP-OVA cassette into the BamHI/EcoRI sites. All RdRp fragments were generated by PCR from the synthesized codon-optimized RdRp template and cloned into the BamHI/EcoRI restriction sites using the same strategy. Ubi-RdRp-EGFPOVA was generated by PCR of the ubiquitin sequence from pRK-HA-Ubiquitin-WT (Addgene, #17608), which was inserted at the N terminus of RdRp-EGFPOVA using SacII/EcoRI sites. A modified version incorporating the N-end rule (Ubi-AR-RdRp-EGFPOVA) was generated by altering the reverse primer used in PCR to introduce a G to A mutation at the C terminus of ubiquitin, immediately followed by an A to R substitution. The pMuLE EF.Bla, pENTR EF1 vector was LR-cloned into pMule Lenti DEST Bla, which was constructed from pMuLe Lenti DEST Neo (Addgene, #62178) by replacing the neomycin resistance cassette with blasticidin S resistance (bsr) using SbfI and XhoI. All plasmids produced for lentiviral transduction were generated by PCR from the respective target gene and inserted into the pLVX EF.Puro plasmid46 at AfeI/SbfI sites. An exception is pLenti-RdRp.Δ366-581aa-EGFPOVA, which was cloned into the pMuLE EF.Bla backbone. All plasmids are listed in Table S2 and were sequence confirmed. All primer sequences used for construct generation are provided in Table S3.

Cell lines

293KbC2 cells10 stably expressing the mouse class I allele H-2Kb22 were maintained in DMEM (Thermo Fisher) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) supplemented with 0.5 mg/mL G418 (Hyclone). B16-F10 cells (ATCC) and MC38 cells (ATCC) were cultured in DMEM containing 10% FBS. All cell lines were maintained at 37°C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2.

To generate MC38 cell lines stably expressing target proteins (e.g., RdRp-EGFPOVA), lentiviral transduction was performed using a second-generation lentiviral packaging system.47 Briefly, 293FT cells (Invitrogen) were co-transfected with the target lentiviral plasmid along with the packaging plasmids pMD2.G and psPAX2. Viral supernatants were harvested 72 h post transfection and used to infect MC38 cells in 6-well plates with polybrene (6 μg/mL), followed by centrifugation in 2000 rcf for 2 h. Infected MC38 cells were incubated for an additional 72 h before selection with puromycin at 5 μg/mL to establish stable cell lines.

For the RdRpFrag-EGFPOVA, dual transduction was performed first with pLVX EF.puro-RdRp.366-581aa-EGFPOVA and selected with puromycin (5 μg/mL), followed by pMuLE EF.Bla-RdRp.Δ366-581aa-EGFPOVA selected by blasticidin (10 μg/mL).

Transfection and cycloheximide/MG132 treatment

293KbC2 cells were seeded in 6-well plates and transfected with 2 μg total DNA complexed with 2 μL of Lipofectamine 2000 (Thermo Fisher). At 24 h post transfection, cells were imaged or collected for immunoblotting or flow cytometric analysis.

To inhibit protein synthesis, cycloheximide (CHX; Sigma) was added to a final concentration of 100 μg/mL. To block proteasomal degradation, MG132 (Sigma) was added at a final concentration of 20 μM. Treatments were applied 24 h post transfection. Control cells received an equivalent volume of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) as vehicle. Cells were harvested 18 h after treatment for immunoblot analysis.

Immunoblotting

Total protein was extracted from transfected cells using radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl, 150 mM NaCl, 1% NP-40, 0.5% deoxycholate, 0.1% SDS, pH 7.4) containing protease inhibitors (0.2 mM Vanadate, 0.3 mM PMSF, 1 μg/mL Leupeptin, 1 μg/mL Pepstatin A, 1 μg/mL Aprotinin). Samples were sonicated with Fisherbrand Model 505 Sonic Dismembrator (Thermo Fisher) at 20% Amp 4x for 5 s each on ice. 2x Laemmli loading buffer (65.8 mM Tris-HCl, pH 6.8, 26.3% glycerol, 2.1% SDS, 0.01% bromophenol blue) and 10% 2-mercaptoethanol were added to samples, which were then separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to nitrocellulose membranes. Membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C with a 1:500-diluted primary rabbit polyclonal antibody to EGFP (Santa Cruz, sc-8334), followed by incubation with a 1:10,000-diluted IRDye800-conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (LI-COR Biotechnology). For a loading control, the rhodamine-conjugated anti-β-tubulin antibody (Bio-Rad) diluted 1:10,000 was reacted for 1 h at room temperature. A ChemiDoc MP Imaging system (Bio-Rad) was used to detect signals. ImageJ was used for quantitation of immunoblot signals.

Click-iT pulse-chase nascent protein turnover assay

293kbC2 cells transfected with EGFPOVA, RdRp-EGFPOVA, or RdRpFrag-EGFPOVA plasmids were cultured in methionine-depleted medium supplemented with 10% dialyzed FCS (Invitrogen) for 45 min. Then, the cells were labeled with 50 μM azidohomoalanine (AHA) for 1 h. The AHA-containing medium was replaced with normal 10% FCS DMEM, and the cells were harvested at 0, 10, 60, and 130 min after the AHA pulse labeling. Cellular proteins were extracted using a lysis buffer containing 25 mM Tris, 137 mM NaCl, 2 mM EDTA, 5% glycerol, 1% Triton X-100, and 1x Halt Protease Inhibitor Cocktail (Thermo Scientific), with a pH of 7.5. 500 μg of total protein was reacted with a mouse anti-GFP antibody (Santa Cruz) overnight. The immune complex was then incubated with Dynabeads Protein G (Invitrogen) for 2 h to precipitate the immune complex. After washing the beads with lysis buffer, 50 μL of SDS buffer (50 mM Tris, 1% SDS, pH 8.0) was added for the subsequent Click-iT reaction. TAMRA-alkyne (Vector Laboratories) was incubated with the immunoprecipitates for 30 min to conjugate the chromophore to the Alexa Fluor 488-labeled GFP-tagged proteins using a Click-&-Go Click Chemistry kit (Vector Laboratories). The conjugated proteins were separated by 11% SDS-PAGE for immunoblotting using a rabbit anti-GFP antibody (Sigma) and an IR800 dye-conjugated anti-rabbit IgG antibody (LI-COR). The AHA and immunoblot signals were detected using BioDockIT (Bio-Rad). The nascent protein level was normalized by dividing the AHA signal by the immunoblot signals from the immunoprecipitated target proteins.

Flow cytometry

293KbC2 cells transfected with target constructs for 48 h in 6-well plates were harvested in 1 mL PBS and washed twice with 500 μL PBS. Cells were then stained with phycoerythrin (PE) anti-mouse MHC class I Kb-SIINFEKL (25-D1.16) (eBioscience) diluted 1:30 for 1 h at 4°C, then washed twice with 500 μL PBS, and stored in 500 μL PBS for analysis on LSRFortessa cell analyzer (BD Bioscience). For each experiment, gating was performed for positive EGFP fluorescence and positive OVA staining, and 30,000 events were collected and analyzed. All experiments were repeated at least three times for reproducibility, with representative experiments shown.

xCELLigene real-time killing assay

100,000 MC38 cells stably expressing the target antigen were seeded onto the E-Plate 96 and incubated for a minimum of 5 h. Subsequently, primary CD8+ T cells, isolated from C57BL/6-Tg(TcraTcrb)1100Mjb/J (Strain #:003831, common name: OT1), were introduced to the E-Plate 96 at various E:T ratios. Following this, the system was left undisturbed for a minimum of 17 h to allow the Agilent xCELLigence RTCA DP system (Santa Clara, CA) to record impedance changes. The RTCA Software Pro was utilized for cytolysis calculations.

Tetramer assay

Subcutaneous injections of 250,000 MC38 cells stably expressing target antigen were administered to C57BL/6 mice. All mice were euthanized on day 7 or day 16 with tumor sizes ranged from 20 mm2 to 40 mm2. Tumor, draining lymph nodes, and spleens were surgically isolated from mice. Tumor tissues were further treated with enzymatic digestion using collagenase IV and DNAse. All tissues were then mechanically disrupted through a 70-μm cell strainer to obtain single-cell suspensions. All samples were treated with 5% mouse serum for 15 min on ice in a blocking step and then stained with CD8β (BV786, BD Bioscience), CD39 (Duha59, Biolegend), CD62L (MEL-14, Biolegend), CD44 (IM7, Biolegend), PD1(135229, BioLegend), Tim3 (119723, BioLegend), Lag3 (563179, BD Horizon), and Zombie NIR for 15 min on ice. Following this, samples were washed once in PBS before staining with PE-H2-Kb-OVA(SIINFEKL)-tetramer (1:100, NIH Tetramer Core Facility) for an additional 15 min. Following surface marker and tetramer staining, we permeabilized the cells using the eBioscience Foxp3/Transcription Factor Staining Buffer Set to allow for intracellular transcription factor detection and then stained with TCF1 (C63D9, Cell Signaling Technology). All samples were then fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde. Flow cytometry analysis involved gating for Zombie negative, CD8β, and PE-OVA-tetramer, followed by CD44 and CD62L in CD8β+ subpopulation. 100,000 events were collected and analyzed per sample. All animal studies were performed in accordance with institutional guidelines and with approval by the IACUC of the University of Pittsburgh (Protocol No. 23073380), under the PHS assurance (D16-00118).

mRNA lipid nanoparticle production

The mRNA was manufactured by RIBOPRO, using a standard and repeatable custom mRNA process offered via the fuRNAce brokerage service, and encapsulated using the L1 lipid formulation. The Cap1-mRNA was manufactured by in vitro transcription employing the RIBOPERFECT RNAP (low-dsRNA T7) with N1mΨ. The poly-A tail was encoded. Purified mRNA was encapsulated in LNPs by microfluidic mixing and dialyzed into TBS-sucrose buffer (pH 7.4).

Tumor challenge in mice

Subcutaneous injections of 250,000 MC38 cells stably expressing EGFPOVA, RdRp-EGFPOVA, and RdRpFrag-EGFPOVA were administered to C57BL/6 mice. Survived mice after 90 days with tumor diminished were euthanized and processed for draining lymph nodes and spleens isolation and ELISpost assays.

mRNA vaccination in mice

RdRp, RdRpFrag (RdRp.366-581+RdRp.Δ366-581), and mock (RdRp mRNA without capping) LNPs were separately injected subcutaneously to mice at 0.2, 0.5, 2, and 5 μg dosing at day 0. A boost injection was performed with the same dosing at day 4. Mice were euthanized at day 9 for CD8+ T cell isolation and ELISpot assays.

CD8+ T cell isolation

Draining lymph nodes and spleens of mice were surgically isolated and mechanically disrupted through a 70-μm cell strainer into single-cell suspensions and mixed.

CD8+ T cells were then negatively selected from the mix by incubating on ice for 15 min with biotin conjugated antibodies: CD19 (115504, BioLegend) at 1:1,000, CD11c (117304, BioLegend) at 1:1,000, Ly-6G/Ly-6C (108404, BioLegend) at 1:1,000, CD11b (101204, BioLegend) at 1:1,000, TCRγ/δ (118103, BioLegend) at 1:1,000, CD45R/B220 (103204, BioLegend) at 1:500, CD49b (108904, BioLegend) at 1:500, CD105 (120404, BioLegend) at 1:500, CD24 (101804, BioLegend) at 1:500, CD16/32(101303, BioLegend) at 1:500, CD25(102004, BioLegend) at 1:1,000, and CD4(100508, BioLegend) at 1:500. MojoSort Streptavidin Nanobeads (BioLegend) were then applied for 15 min, followed by magnet pull-down for 5 min to remove unwanted cells. Flow cytometry analysis involved gating for Zombie negative, CD8β, and PE-OVA-tetramer, followed by CD44 and CD62L in CD8β+ subpopulation. 100,000 events were collected and analyzed per sample.

ELISpot assay

ELISpot were performed with Mouse IFN-γ ELISpot Kit (EL485, R&D System) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. In brief, the selected CD8+ T cells were co-cultured overnight with MC38 cells stably expressing WT RdRp, or EGFPOVA at 500,000 cells/mL each (E:T = 1:1), into a 96-well PVDF-backed microplate. The plate was then sequentially treated with biotinylated anti-IFN-γ antibody, alkaline phosphatases conjugated streptavidin, and BCIP/NBT substrate. Developed spots were manually counted for ΙFN-γ-producing cells.

Hamster vaccination and SARS CoV2 challenge assay

Transgenic hACE2 hamsters (n = 6 per group) were randomly assigned into experimental cohorts and vaccinated subcutaneously on day 0 with 5 μg of LNP-encapsulated mRNA encoding either full-length RdRp, RdRpFrag, or the control MuVG19/HN (mumps virus hemagglutinin-neuraminidase). A homologous booster dose was administered on day 21. On day 42, animals were anesthetized and intranasally challenged with 1 × 105 PFU of SARS CoV2 Omicron variant XBB.1.5 (BEI Resources, NR-59105). Body weight was recorded daily after infection, up to 4 days. Nasal washes and oral swabs were collected on days 2 and 4 post challenge for analysis of viral RNA by quantitative RT-PCR and infectious virus titers by plaque assay. On day 4 post challenge, animals were euthanized, and lung tissues were harvested for virological assessment. All animal experiments were conducted in compliance with institutional animal care and biosafety protocols.

AlphaFold analysis of protein intrinsic disorder

The amino acid sequences of the RdRp fragments were submitted to the AlphaFold Server (AlphaFold 3).29 Predicted Local Distance Difference Test (pLDDT) scores, reflecting the per-residue confidence in each of the predicted structures, were extracted from the resulting output files. pLDDT values were converted to intrinsic disorder scores by inversion (disorder score = 1 − pLDDT/100), using a threshold of 0.5 to classify residues from each fragment as either ordered (pLDDT >50) or disordered (pLDDT <50). To validate these AlphaFold disorder predictions, three independent disorder prediction tools were employed: IUPred3 (ANCHOR2 mode, using context-dependent sequence analysis),32 DISOPRED3 (combining machine learning with evolutionary information),34 and SPOT-Disorder (deep learning approach trained on NMR data).33 Each tool independently predicted residue disorder probabilities on a scale of 0–1, with scores above 0.5 classified as disordered. Consensus disorder predictions were calculated as the mean disorder score across all four tools at each residue position. Inter-tool agreement was quantified by computing agreement score, indicating the level of consensus among each of the prediction methods. Disorder percentages were calculated as (number of disordered residues/total residues) × 100, where disordered residues were those with disorder score above 0.5. Mean disorder levels and standard deviations were calculated for each protein fragment. Results were visualized as per-residue confidence profiles for each fragment, with each profile containing a high-confidence threshold of pLDDT score >70 and a disorder threshold of pLDDT score <50. Results between scores were considered low confidence. Ranked comparisons of disorder across all fragments, multi-tool comparison plots showing consensus disorder predictions, and inter-tool agreement quality metrics were among the other results visualized across all prediction methods.
