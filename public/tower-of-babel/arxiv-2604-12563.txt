Latent community paths in VAR-type models via dynamic directed spectral co-clustering
Younghoon Kim, Changryong Baek

Abstract
This paper proposes a dynamic network framework for uncovering latent community paths in high-dimensional VAR-type models. By embedding a degree-corrected stochastic co-blockmodel (ScBM) into the transition matrices of VAR-type systems, we separate sending and receiving roles at the node level and summarize complex directional dependence in an interpretable low-dimensional form. Our method integrates directed spectral co-clustering with eigenvector smoothing to track how directional groups split, merge, or persist over time. This framework accommodates both periodic VAR (PVAR) models for cyclical seasonal evolution and generalized VHAR models for structural transitions across ordered dependence horizons. We establish non-asymptotic misclassification bounds for both procedures and provide supporting evidence through Monte Carlo experiments. Applications to U.S. nonfarm payrolls distinguish a recurrent business-centered core from more mobile, seasonally sensitive sectors. In global stock volatilities, the results reveal a compact U.S.-centered long-horizon block, a Europe-heavy developed core, and a more dynamic short-horizon reallocation of peripheral and bridge markets.



Keywords—
Periodic autoregression, heterogeneous autoregression, dynamic network, community detection, stochastic block model, spectral clustering

1
Introduction

The analysis of high-dimensional vector autoregressive (VAR) models is often constrained by the curse of dimensionality. Even when regularization techniques yield sparse estimates
(
Basu and Michailidis, 2015
;
Brownlees et al., 2018
;
Barigozzi et al., 2024
, e.g.,)
, direct inspection of individual coefficients—each representing predictive influence in the sense of
Granger, 1969
—rarely provides a coherent account of system-wide dependence. In many macroeconomic and financial applications, the main question is not simply which coefficients are nonzero, but how the system organizes itself into interpretable groups and how that organization changes across seasons or dependence horizons.

To address this, we study a dynamic network framework for
latent community paths
: ordered trajectories of directional groups that capture how clusters of “senders” and “receivers” of shocks split, merge, persist, and recompose over time. By shifting attention from isolated coefficients to the dynamic evolution of group structure, we turn a high-dimensional estimation problem into one of recovering interpretable trajectories of directional influence.

The core of our methodology embeds a degree-corrected stochastic co-blockmodel (ScBM;
Qin and Rohe, 2013
) into the transition matrices of VAR-type systems. While recent literature has advanced block recovery in VAR models via spectral methods
(
Guðmundsson and Brownlees, 2021
;
Brownlees et al., 2022
, e.g.,)
, these approaches largely rely on symmetrization, which inherently suppresses directional roles, or treat network snapshots as static entities. More recently,
Guðmundsson, 2025
moved toward directional groups through an ScBM representation, yet primarily focused on lag aggregation. We overcome these limitations by integrating directed spectral co-clustering, which preserves the asymmetry between sending and receiving roles
(
Rohe et al., 2016
;
Wang et al., 2020
)
, with eigenvector smoothing across ordered stages in the spirit of
Liu et al., 2018
. This yields a dynamic community-recovery scheme tailored to track the continuous evolution of dependent multivariate time series.

We develop this framework in two distinct high-dimensional settings. First, in the periodic VAR (PVAR) model
(
Ursu and Duchesne, 2009
;
Baek et al., 2018
, see, e.g.,)
, we uncover how directed community structures evolve cyclically across seasons, moving beyond static partitions. Furthermore, in the generalized VHAR model, we replace the conventional daily–weekly–monthly aggregation
(
Corsi, 2009
;
Baek and Park, 2021
)
with arbitrary fixed lengths to track how global dependencies transition across ordered short-, medium-, and long-run horizons
(
Lee and Baek, 2023
)
.

The primary theoretical contribution of this paper is establishing rigorous non-asymptotic misclassification bounds for directed community recovery within the ScBM–PVAR and generalized ScBM–VHAR frameworks. The approach is modular by design. We first derive estimation error bounds in operator norm for regularized lasso estimators in high-dimensional time series
(
Wong et al., 2020
)
. These bounds are then integrated with random-graph perturbation arguments for degree-corrected co-blockmodels
(
Qin and Rohe, 2013
)
to control the distance between the estimated and population transition matrices. In a second step, we extend the eigenvector smoothing perturbation argument of Liu et al. (2018) to accommodate ordered chains of left and right singular-vector projectors across evolving seasons or horizons. The resulting bound on the Frobenius norm of row-normalized singular-vector perturbations directly translates into a misclassification rate, completing a clean theoretical pipeline from first-stage estimation error through spectral perturbation to clustering error. The modular structure also allows the co-clustering theory to be updated as tighter first-stage estimators become available. Finally, we provide verifiable sufficient conditions under which the core spectral assumptions hold, connecting the abstract conditions to primitive properties of the ScBM network parameters.

The empirical relevance of our framework is demonstrated in two applications. In the U.S. nonfarm payroll analysis (PVAR), the estimated paths reveal a recurrent business-centered core together with broader seasonal reallocation among more mobile sectors. In the global realized-volatility application (VHAR), the estimated paths reveal a compact U.S.-centered long-horizon block, a Europe-heavy developed core, and pronounced cross-horizon reorganization at shorter horizons. These results show that the proposed framework provides an interpretable account of how directional dependence is reorganized across seasons and horizons.

2
Model description

We introduce three ScBM-based specifications for multivariate autoregressions. ScBM-VAR is the basic building block; ScBM-PVAR and generalized ScBM-VHAR extend it to seasonal and ordered-horizon settings.

2.1
ScBM-VAR model

Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E},\mathcal{W})
be a directed weighted graph with node set
\mathcal{V}=\{1,\ldots,q\}
, edge set
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
, and weights
\mathcal{W}=\{[w]_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}:(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}\}
.
The adjacency matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times q}
satisfies
[A]_{ij}=[w]_{ij}
if
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}
and
0
otherwise, where the nonzero weights satisfy
|[w]_{ij}|\leq w_{\max}
almost surely. We allow
K_{y}
sending communities and
K_{z}
receiving communities, with membership matrices
Y\in\{0,1\}^{q\times K_{y}}
and
Z\in\{0,1\}^{q\times K_{z}}
. Under the ScBM, edge probability between nodes
i
and
j
satisfies

\mathbb{P}((i,j)\in\mathcal{E})=\mathbb{P}([A]_{ij}\neq 0)=[\Theta^{y}]_{ii}[\Theta^{z}]_{jj}[B]_{y_{i}z_{j}},\hskip 10.00002pty_{i}=1,\ldots,K_{y},\ z_{j}=1,\ldots,K_{z},

where
\Theta^{y}
and
\Theta^{z}
are diagonal propensity matrices and
B\in[0,1]^{K_{y}\times K_{z}}
is the community link matrix. Let
\{\mathcal{V}_{k}\}
be a partition of the node set. For an identifiability purpose,

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}[\Theta^{y}]_{ii}=1,\hskip 10.00002ptk=1,\ldots,K_{y},\hskip 20.00003pt\sum_{j\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}[\Theta^{z}]_{jj}=1,\hskip 10.00002ptk=1,\ldots,K_{z}.

The corresponding population adjacency matrix is

\mathcal{A}:=\mathbb{E}[A]=\mu\Theta^{y}YBZ^{\prime}\Theta^{z},

for some constant
\mu>0
.

Let
\{Y_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
be a
q
-dimensional mean-zero time series. The VAR
(p)
model is

Y_{t}=\sum_{h=1}^{p}\Phi_{h}Y_{t-h}+\varepsilon_{t},\hskip 10.00002pt\varepsilon_{t}\sim\mathrm{WN}(0,\Sigma_{\varepsilon}),\hskip 10.00002ptt=1,\ldots,T.

(2.1)

At each lag
h
, the ScBM is embedded in the transition matrix as

\Phi_{h}=\phi_{h}\bigl(P_{h}^{\tau_{h}}\bigr)^{-1/2}A_{h}^{\prime}\bigl(O_{h}^{\tau_{h}}\bigr)^{-1/2},\hskip 20.00003pth=1,\ldots,p,

(2.2)

where
\phi_{h}
is a scalar to ensure the stability of the VAR(
p
) model and

[P_{h}^{\tau_{h}}]_{jj}:=\sum_{k}[A_{h}^{\prime}]_{kj}+\tau_{h},\hskip 20.00003pt[O_{h}^{\tau_{h}}]_{ii}:=\sum_{k}[A_{h}^{\prime}]_{ik}+\tau_{h},

with
\tau_{h}:=q^{-1}\sum_{i,j}[A_{h}^{\prime}]_{ij}
denoting the average out-degree regularizer
(
Chaudhuri et al., 2012
;
Qin and Rohe, 2013
, e.g.,)
, which is known to improve clustering performance under high heterogeneity in degrees. The transpose in (
2.2
) ensures that columns represent lagged senders and rows current receivers. We assume that the latent network is common across lags
h=1,\ldots,p
, and that
\{\phi_{h}\}
is chosen to satisfy

\det(I-\Phi_{1}z-\cdots-\Phi_{p}z^{p})\neq 0,\hskip 20.00003pt|z|\leq 1,

so that the VAR
(p)
model is stable.

2.2
ScBM-PVAR model

For each season
m=1,\ldots,s
, let
G_{m}=(\mathcal{V}_{m},\mathcal{E}_{m},\mathcal{W}_{m})
be a directed weighted graph with adjacency matrix
A_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times q}
, where
[A_{m}]_{ij}=[w_{m}]_{ij}
if
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{m}
and
0
otherwise. We assume that there exists a finite constant
w_{\max,m}>0
such that
|[w_{m}]_{ij}|\leq w_{\max,m}
almost surely for all
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{m}
. Let
Y_{m}\in\{0,1\}^{q\times K_{y_{m}}}
and
Z_{m}\in\{0,1\}^{q\times K_{z_{m}}}
denote the sending and receiving membership matrices, respectively. With propensity matrices
\Theta_{m}^{y},\Theta_{m}^{z}
and community link matrix
B_{m}
, the population adjacency matrix is

\mathcal{A}_{m}:=\mathbb{E}[A_{m}]=\mu_{m}\Theta_{m}^{y}Y_{m}B_{m}Z_{m}^{\prime}\Theta_{m}^{z}.

(2.3)

In addition to the setup of each season, we assume a cyclic evolution of communities across seasons, namely,

Z_{m-1}=Y_{m},\hskip 10.00002ptm=2,\ldots,s,\hskip 20.00003ptY_{1}=Z_{s}.

(2.4)

Note that “sending” and “receiving” denote directional connectivity “within” each season, following the convention of
(
Rohe et al., 2016
;
Wang et al., 2020
, e.g.,)
.
The restriction (
2.4
) implies that the receiving membership in one season is carried over as the sending membership in the next season. Due to this circular structure, the community evolution remains identical with the reversed flow;
Z_{m}=Y_{m-1}
,
m=2,\ldots,s
, and
Z_{1}=Y_{s}
.

The periodic VAR model is

Y_{t}=\sum_{h=1}^{p_{m}}\Phi_{h,m}Y_{t-h}+\varepsilon_{t,m},\hskip 20.00003pt\varepsilon_{t,m}\sim WN(0,\Sigma_{\varepsilon,m}),\hskip 20.00003ptt=1,\ldots,T,

where
p_{m}
may depend on the season. Writing
t=m+ns
yields the equivalent representation

Y_{m+ns}=\sum_{h=1}^{p_{m}}\Phi_{h,m}Y_{m+ns-h}+\varepsilon_{m+ns,m},\hskip 20.00003pt\varepsilon_{m+ns,m}\sim WN(0,\Sigma_{\varepsilon,m}).

The seasonal transition matrices are parameterized as

\Phi_{h,m}=\phi_{h,m}(P_{h,m}^{\tau_{h,m}})^{-1/2}A_{h,m}^{\prime}(O_{h,m}^{\tau_{h,m}})^{-1/2},\hskip 20.00003pth=1,\ldots,p_{m},\hskip 10.00002ptm=1,\ldots,s,

(2.5)

with a scalar
\phi_{h,m}
and

[P_{h,m}^{\tau_{h,m}}]_{jj}:=\sum_{k}[A_{h,m}^{\prime}]_{kj}+\tau_{h,m},\hskip 20.00003pt[O_{h,m}^{\tau_{h,m}}]_{ii}:=\sum_{k}[A_{h,m}^{\prime}]_{ik}+\tau_{h,m},\hskip 20.00003pt\tau_{h,m}:=q^{-1}\sum_{i,j}[A_{h,m}^{\prime}]_{ij}.

Within each season, the latent network is taken to be common across lags, and
\phi_{h,m}
is chosen so that the process is stable.

For both estimation and theory, it is convenient to stack one full seasonal cycle. Assume
T=Ns
so that the sample contains
N
complete cycles, and define

Y_{n}^{*}=\bigl(Y_{ns+s}^{\prime},Y_{ns+s-1}^{\prime},\ldots,Y_{ns+1}^{\prime}\bigr)^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{qs},\hskip 20.00003pt\varepsilon_{n}^{*}=\bigl(\varepsilon_{ns+s}^{\prime},\varepsilon_{ns+s-1}^{\prime},\ldots,\varepsilon_{ns+1}^{\prime}\bigr)^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{qs}.

Then the ScBM-PVAR model can be written in the stacked form

Y_{n}^{*}=\sum_{h=1}^{p^{*}}\Psi_{h}^{*}Y_{n-h}^{*}+\varepsilon_{n}^{*},

(2.6)

where
p^{*}=\lceil(\max_{m=1,\ldots,s}p_{m})/s\rceil
and
\Psi_{h}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{qs\times qs}
is obtained from the original seasonal coefficient matrices
\{\Phi_{h,m}\}_{m=1}^{s}
; see, for example, Section 2 of
Ursu and Duchesne, 2009
. Thus, the ScBM-PVAR model is represented as a VAR
(p^{*})
model on the stacked process
\{Y_{n}^{*}\}
. When
s=1
, this reduces to the ScBM-VAR model in (
2.1
)–(
2.2
).

2.3
Generalized ScBM-VHAR model

Let
1<b_{M}<b_{L}
. The generalized VHAR model is

Y_{t}=\Phi_{(S)}Y_{t-1}^{(S)}+\Phi_{(M)}Y_{t-1}^{(M)}+\Phi_{(L)}Y_{t-1}^{(L)}+\varepsilon_{t},\hskip 10.00002pt\varepsilon_{t}\sim\mathrm{WN}(0,\Sigma_{\varepsilon}),\hskip 10.00002ptt=1,\ldots,T,

(2.7)

where

Y_{t}^{(S)}=Y_{t},\hskip 20.00003ptY_{t}^{(M)}=\frac{1}{b_{M}}\sum_{h=0}^{b_{M}-1}Y_{t-h},\hskip 20.00003ptY_{t}^{(L)}=\frac{1}{b_{L}}\sum_{h=0}^{b_{L}-1}Y_{t-h}.

The effective sample size is
N=T-b_{L}
. When
(b_{M},b_{L})=(5,22)
, the generalized VHAR model reduces to the classical daily-weekly-monthly specification
(
Corsi, 2009
)
. Equation (
2.7
) is equivalent to a constrained VAR(
b_{L}
). If
\Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{b_{L}}
denote the implied VAR(
b_{L}
) transition matrices, then

\Phi_{1}=\Phi_{(S)}+\frac{\Phi_{(M)}}{b_{M}}+\frac{\Phi_{(L)}}{b_{L}},~\Phi_{2}=\cdots=\Phi_{b_{M}}=\frac{\Phi_{(M)}}{b_{M}}+\frac{\Phi_{(L)}}{b_{L}},~\Phi_{b_{M}+1}=\cdots=\Phi_{b_{L}}=\frac{\Phi_{(L)}}{b_{L}}.

(2.8)

Hence, the number of free coefficients decreases from
b_{L}q^{2}
in an unrestricted VAR(
b_{L}
) model to
3q^{2}
. The ScBM restriction is then constructed as, similar to (
2.5
),

\Phi_{(h)}=\phi_{(h)}\bigl(P_{(h)}^{\tau_{(h)}}\bigr)^{-1/2}A_{(h)}^{\prime}\bigl(O_{(h)}^{\tau_{(h)}}\bigr)^{-1/2},\hskip 20.00003pth\in\{S,M,L\},

with a scalar
\phi_{(h)}
and

[P_{(h)}^{\tau_{(h)}}]_{jj}:=\sum_{k}[A_{(h)}^{\prime}]_{kj}+\tau_{(h)},\hskip 20.00003pt[O_{(h)}^{\tau_{(h)}}]_{ii}:=\sum_{k}[A_{(h)}^{\prime}]_{ik}+\tau_{(h)},\hskip 20.00003pt\tau_{(h)}:=q^{-1}\sum_{i,j}[A_{(h)}^{\prime}]_{ij}.

For each
h\in\{S,M,L\}
, the population adjacency matrix
A_{(h)}
is specified by an ScBM with horizon-specific parameters
(\mu_{(h)},\Theta^{y}_{(h)},\Theta^{z}_{(h)},Y_{(h)},Z_{(h)},B_{(h)})
associated with the underlying graph
\mathcal{G}_{(h)}
, similar to (
2.3
). We also assume a bounded-support condition: there exists a finite constant
w_{\max,(h)}>0
such that
|[w_{(h)}]_{ij}|\leq w_{\max,(h)}
almost surely for all
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{(h)}
. Unlike the cyclic PVAR case, these horizons are transient and are interpreted in the natural long-to-short order. We impose the restrictions
Y_{(L)}=Z_{(L)}=Y_{(M)}
and
Z_{(M)}=Y_{(S)}
, so that the long-horizon community persists within the long-horizon block and is then inherited by the medium-horizon sending block, while the medium-horizon receiving block is inherited by the short-horizon sending block. This yields a hierarchical flow from broader horizons to shorter ones, where long-horizon structure constrains medium- and short-horizon interactions
(
Müller et al., 1997
;
Corsi, 2009
)
.

3
Estimation, spectral co-clustering and PisCES

This section presents the estimation-clustering pipeline for the ScBM-VAR, ScBM-PVAR, and generalized ScBM-VHAR models, separating transition-matrix estimation from community recovery.

3.1
Estimation of transition matrices

As the first step, we estimate the transition matrices via ordinary least squares (OLS) as the baseline, particularly in low-dimensional settings. In higher dimensions, we use lasso estimation.

ScBM-VAR and ScBM-PVAR.

For the ScBM-VAR model, either OLS or lasso estimation
(
Basu and Michailidis, 2015
)
is applied directly to the regression in (
2.1
). For the ScBM-PVAR model, we use the stacked representation in (
2.6
). Define

W_{n}^{*}=(Y_{n-1}^{*^{\prime}},\ldots,Y_{n-p^{*}}^{*^{\prime}})^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{qsp^{*}},\hskip 20.00003pt\alpha_{P}^{*}=\mathrm{vec}(\Psi_{1}^{*},\ldots,\Psi_{p^{*}}^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{q^{2}s^{2}p^{*}},

and set

\mathbb{Y}_{P}=(Y_{p^{*}+1}^{*^{\prime}},\ldots,Y_{N}^{*^{\prime}})^{\prime},\hskip 20.00003pt\mathbb{X}_{P}=(W_{p^{*}+1}^{*^{\prime}},\ldots,W_{N}^{*^{\prime}})^{\prime}.

The lasso estimator for the ScBM-PVAR model is

\hat{\alpha}_{P}=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{q^{2}s^{2}p^{*}}}\left\{\frac{1}{N}\left\|\mathrm{vec}(\mathbb{Y}_{P})-(\mathbb{X}_{P}\otimes I_{qs})\alpha\right\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{N,P}\|\alpha\|_{1}\right\},

(3.1)

where
\lambda_{N,P}>0
is a regularization parameter. In the low-dimensional case, the OLS estimator is obtained by removing the
\ell_{1}
penalty and solving the corresponding least-squares problem.

Generalized ScBM-VHAR.

For the generalized ScBM–VHAR model, define

Z=(Y_{b_{L}+1}^{\prime},\ldots,Y_{T}^{\prime})^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times q},\hskip 20.00003ptB=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{(S)}^{\prime}\\
\Phi_{(M)}^{\prime}\\
\Phi_{(L)}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{3q\times q},\hskip 20.00003pt\beta_{V}^{*}=\operatorname{vec}(B),

with
N=T-b_{L}
. Let
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times qb_{L}}
be the unrestricted VAR
(b_{L})
lag-design matrix, and set

X_{e}=XR_{b_{M},b_{L}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 3q},

where
R_{b_{M},b_{L}}\in\mathbb{R}^{qb_{L}\times 3q}
is the deterministic restriction matrix implied by (
2.8
). Then

Z=X_{e}B+E,\hskip 20.00003pt\operatorname{vec}(Z)=(I_{q}\otimes X_{e})\beta_{V}^{*}+\operatorname{vec}(E).

The restricted lasso estimator is

\hat{\beta}_{V}=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{3q^{2}}}\left\{\frac{1}{N}\left\|\operatorname{vec}(Z)-(I_{q}\otimes X_{e})\beta\right\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{N,V}\|\beta\|_{1}\right\},

(3.2)

where
\lambda_{N,V}>0
is a regularization parameter. The restricted OLS estimator is obtained by dropping the
\ell_{1}
penalty.

3.2
Spectral co-clustering for ScBM-VAR-type models

The co-clustering step has the same backbone in all models. Given estimated autoregressive matrices
\hat{\Phi}_{m}
, compute their leading left and right singular vectors, row-normalize them, optionally smooth the associated projection matrices across ordered seasons or horizons, and apply
k
-means to the matched left-right pairs.

ScBM-VAR.

We begin with the ScBM-VAR model, combining the directed co-clustering idea of
Rohe et al., 2016
with the VAR Blockbuster approach of
Guðmundsson and Brownlees, 2021
. Let

\hat{\Phi}=\sum_{h=1}^{p}\hat{\Phi}_{h}^{\prime},\hskip 20.00003ptK=\min(K_{y},K_{z}),

and compute the leading
K
left and right singular vectors
\hat{X}_{L},\hat{X}_{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times K}
. We then row-normalize them as

[\hat{X}_{L}^{*}]_{i\cdot}=\frac{[\hat{X}_{L}]_{i\cdot}}{\|[\hat{X}_{L}]_{i\cdot}\|},\hskip 20.00003pt[\hat{X}_{R}^{*}]_{i\cdot}=\frac{[\hat{X}_{R}]_{i\cdot}}{\|[\hat{X}_{R}]_{i\cdot}\|},\hskip 20.00003pti=1,\ldots,q.

(3.3)

If
K_{y}=K_{z}=K
, we run
k
-means on
(\hat{X}_{L}^{*}\ \hat{X}_{R}^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times 2K}
and use the resulting labels for both sending and receiving communities. Otherwise, we cluster
\hat{X}_{L}^{*}
and
\hat{X}_{R}^{*}
separately using
K_{y}
and
K_{z}
clusters, respectively.

Remark 3.1
.

Due to identifiability constraints in degree-corrected co-block models, only
K=\min(K_{y},K_{z})
is typically identifiable in a stable manner. In practice,
K
can be selected using scree plots or information criteria for low-rank modeling
(
Bai and Ng, 2007
;
Baek et al., 2018
, e.g.,)
.

ScBM-PVAR.
We now extend the procedure to the ScBM-PVAR model and incorporate smoothing across seasons via PisCES
(
Liu et al., 2018
)
. For simplicity, we take
p_{m}=p
for all
m=1,\ldots,s
. For each season
m
, form

\hat{\Phi}_{m}=\sum_{h=1}^{p}\hat{\Phi}_{m,h}^{\prime},

and compute its top
K_{y_{m}}
left and
K_{z_{m}}
right singular vectors
\hat{X}_{m,L}
and
\hat{X}_{m,R}
. We impose the cyclic rank constraints
K_{z_{m-1}}=K_{y_{m}}
for
m=2,\ldots,s
and
K_{z_{s}}=K_{y_{1}}
. Define projector matrices

\hat{U}_{m,L}=\hat{X}_{m,L}\hat{X}_{m,L}^{\prime},\hskip 20.00003pt\hat{U}_{m,R}=\hat{X}_{m,R}\hat{X}_{m,R}^{\prime},\hskip 20.00003ptm=1,\ldots,s,

and initialize
\bar{U}_{m,L}^{(0)}=\hat{U}_{m,L}
and
\bar{U}_{m,R}^{(0)}=\hat{U}_{m,R}
. For a smoothing parameter
\alpha_{N}>0
, PisCES updates the left sequence by

\displaystyle\bar{U}_{1,L}^{(l+1)}

\displaystyle=\Pi(\hat{U}_{1,L}+\alpha_{N}\bar{U}_{2,L}^{(l)};K_{y_{1}}),

\displaystyle\bar{U}_{m,L}^{(l+1)}

\displaystyle=\Pi(\alpha_{N}\bar{U}_{m-1,L}^{(l)}+\hat{U}_{m,L}+\alpha_{N}\bar{U}_{m+1,L}^{(l)};K_{y_{m}}),\hskip 20.00003ptm=2,\ldots,s-1,

\displaystyle\bar{U}_{s,L}^{(l+1)}

\displaystyle=\Pi(\alpha_{N}\bar{U}_{s-1,L}^{(l)}+\hat{U}_{s,L};K_{y_{s}}),

and the right sequence analogously:

\displaystyle\bar{U}_{1,R}^{(l+1)}

\displaystyle=\Pi(\hat{U}_{1,R}+\alpha_{N}\bar{U}_{2,R}^{(l)};K_{z_{1}}),

\displaystyle\bar{U}_{m,R}^{(l+1)}

\displaystyle=\Pi(\alpha_{N}\bar{U}_{m-1,R}^{(l)}+\hat{U}_{m,R}+\alpha_{N}\bar{U}_{m+1,R}^{(l)};K_{z_{m}}),\hskip 20.00003ptm=2,\ldots,s-1,

\displaystyle\bar{U}_{s,R}^{(l+1)}

\displaystyle=\Pi(\alpha_{N}\bar{U}_{s-1,R}^{(l)}+\hat{U}_{s,R};K_{z_{s}}),

where
\Pi(M;K)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nu_{k}\nu_{k}^{\prime}
is the projector onto the span of the top-
K
eigenvectors of
M
. After convergence, write
\bar{U}_{m,L}=\bar{X}_{m,L}\bar{X}_{m,L}^{\prime}
and
\bar{U}_{m,R}=\bar{X}_{m,R}\bar{X}_{m,R}^{\prime}
, row-normalize
\bar{X}_{m,L}
and
\bar{X}_{m,R}
as in (
3.3
), and link adjacent seasons by
k
-means on
(\bar{X}_{m-1,R}^{*}\ \bar{X}_{m,L}^{*})
for
m=2,\ldots,s
and on
(\bar{X}_{s,R}^{*}\ \bar{X}_{1,L}^{*})
for the cyclic pair. When
\alpha_{N}=0
, this reduces to season-wise spectral co-clustering.

Remark 3.2
.

One can estimate
K_{m}=\min(K_{y_{m}},K_{z_{m}})
using scree plots or information criteria and then impose the required rank-matching constraints. Because community labels need not align across seasons, we post-process them by locally reassigning indices to minimize discrepancies between consecutive clustering results. This step affects only the labeling convention, not the fitted model.

ScBM-VHAR.
For the generalized ScBM-VHAR model, we follow the same procedure as for ScBM-PVAR, except that the three “seasons” are now replaced by short-, medium-, and long-horizon components with aggregation lengths
1
,
b_{M}
, and
b_{L}
. Define

(\hat{\Phi}_{1},\hat{\Phi}_{2},\hat{\Phi}_{3})=(\hat{\Phi}_{(L)}^{\prime},\hat{\Phi}_{(M)}^{\prime},\hat{\Phi}_{(S)}^{\prime}).

We then apply the same singular-vector extraction and PisCES smoothing routine with
s=3
, imposing the rank constraints
K_{y_{(L)}}=K_{z_{(L)}}=K_{y_{(M)}}
and
K_{z_{(M)}}=K_{y_{(S)}}
. Under the long-to-short convention, the linked stages are

Y_{(L)}=Z_{(L)}=Y_{(M)},\hskip 10.00002ptZ_{(M)}=Y_{(S)},

(3.4)

and terminal
Z_{(S)}
. After smoothing, we jointly cluster
(\bar{X}_{(L),R}^{*}\ \bar{X}_{(L),L}^{*}\ \bar{X}_{(M),R}^{*})
to recover the first stage, jointly cluster
(\bar{X}_{(M),L}^{*}\ \bar{X}_{(S),R}^{*})
to recover the second stage, and cluster
\bar{X}_{(S),L}^{*}
separately. When
\alpha_{N}=0
, the procedure reduces to horizon-wise spectral co-clustering.

3.3
Cross-validation for PisCES smoothing parameter

We select the PisCES smoothing parameter
\alpha_{N}
by cross-validation, adapting the procedure of
Liu et al., 2018
. Specifically, we apply the method to either seasonal or horizon-specific autoregressive matrices
\{\hat{\Phi}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{s}
as follows:

(i)

Split the off-diagonal entries into
M
folds and define the masked matrices, for
\ell=1,\ldots,M
,

[\hat{\Phi}_{m}^{(\ell)}]_{ij}=\begin{cases}[\hat{\Phi}_{m}]_{ij},&(i,j,m)\notin\ell,\\
0,&(i,j,m)\in\ell,\end{cases}

then compute the rank-
K_{m}
completion

\tilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(\ell)}:=\hat{U}_{m,L}^{(\ell)}\hat{D}_{m}^{(\ell)}\hat{U}_{m,R}^{(\ell)^{\prime}},\hskip 20.00003ptK_{m}=\min(K_{y_{m}},K_{z_{m}}).

(ii)

For each candidate
\alpha_{N}
, apply PisCES algorithm in Section
3.2
with
\alpha_{N}
to
\{\tilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(\ell)}\}_{m=1}^{s}
, obtain the smoothed assignments
y_{m}^{(\alpha_{N},\ell)}
and
z_{m}^{(\alpha_{N},\ell)}
, and estimate

\hat{\Theta}_{m,i}^{(y,\alpha_{N},\ell)}:=\sum_{k}[\tilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(\ell)}]_{ik},\hskip 20.00003pt\hat{\Theta}_{m,j}^{(z,\alpha_{N},\ell)}:=\sum_{k}[\tilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(\ell)}]_{kj},

with block matrix

[\hat{B}_{m}^{(\alpha_{N},\ell)}]_{kr}=\frac{\sum_{(i,j):y_{m,i}^{(\alpha_{N},\ell)}=k,\ z_{m,j}^{(\alpha_{N},\ell)}=r}[\tilde{\Phi}_{m}^{(\ell)}]_{ij}}{\sum_{(i,j):y_{m,i}^{(\alpha_{N},\ell)}=k,\ z_{m,j}^{(\alpha_{N},\ell)}=r}\hat{\Theta}_{m,i}^{(y,\alpha_{N},\ell)}\hat{\Theta}_{m,j}^{(z,\alpha_{N},\ell)}}.

This yields

[\hat{P}_{m}^{(\alpha_{N},\ell)}]_{ij}=\hat{\Theta}_{m,i}^{(y,\alpha_{N},\ell)}\hat{\Theta}_{m,j}^{(z,\alpha_{N},\ell)}[\hat{B}_{m}^{(\alpha_{N},\ell)}]_{y_{m,i}^{(\alpha_{N},\ell)}z_{m,j}^{(\alpha_{N},\ell)}}.

(iii)

Select
\alpha_{N}
by minimizing

H(\ell,\alpha_{N})=\sum_{m=1}^{s}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\Phi}_{m})}{q}\left(1-\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{P}_{m}^{(\alpha_{N},\ell)})}{q}\right)

over a grid on
[0,\alpha_{\max}]
, where
\alpha_{\max}=1/(4\sqrt{2}+2)

(
Liu et al., 2018
, Theorem 1)
.

This criterion may be interpreted as a quadratic approximation to the von Neumann entropy; see, for example, equations (10)–(11) in
Ye et al., 2014
.

4
Theoretical properties

This section develops the theory for the ScBM-PVAR and ScBM-VHAR procedures. The key idea is modularity: once an operator-norm bound for
\hat{\Phi}-\Phi
is available, the downstream co-clustering theory follows by substitution. All proofs are deferred to Appendix
A
.

4.1
Stability

To study the stability of the ScBM-PVAR model, we work with the stacked representation in (
2.6
). Under this representation, the ScBM-PVAR model is a VAR
(p^{*})
model on the stacked process
\{Y_{n}^{*}\}
, where the coefficient matrices
\Psi_{h}^{*}
are inherited from the original seasonal system. This allows us to analyze stability through the corresponding stacked companion form.

Assumption 4.1
.

For the stacked representation (
2.6
) we assume:

(i)

For all complex
z
with
|z|\leq 1
,

\det\!\Bigl(I_{qs}-\Psi_{1}^{*}z-\cdots-\Psi_{p^{*}}^{*}z^{p^{*}}\Bigr)\neq 0.

(ii)

There exist nonnegative numbers
\phi_{h}
such that

\|\Psi_{h}^{*}\|\leq\phi_{h},\hskip 10.00002pth=1,\ldots,p^{*},\hskip 20.00003pt\sum_{h=1}^{p^{*}}\phi_{h}<1.

Condition (i) is the standard VAR stability condition, and condition (ii) is a norm bound that controls the overall scale of the stacked transition matrices, in line with assumptions used in the related models
(
Guðmundsson and Brownlees, 2021
;
Yin et al., 2023
, e.g.,)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Under Assumption
4.1
, the ScBM-PVAR model is stable, i.e., the stacked process
\{Y_{n}^{*}\}
admits a unique and causal stationary solution.

The generalized ScBM-VHAR model in (
2.7
) can be written as a VAR(
b_{L}
) with constrained coefficients as in (
2.8
). Hence, by treating
\{\Phi_{h}\}_{h=1}^{b_{L}}
as the VAR(
b_{L}
) transition matrices, stability is ensured by the usual characteristic-root condition

\det\!\Big(I_{q}-\sum_{h=1}^{b_{L}}\Phi_{h}z^{h}\Big)\neq 0,\hskip 20.00003pt|z|\leq 1.

Since
b_{L}
is fixed, we additionally impose a deterministic norm envelope on the constrained lag matrices; this will be used later in the estimation bounds.

Assumption 4.2
.

For the generalized ScBM-VHAR model with fixed integers
1<b_{M}<b_{L}
, we assume:

(i)

For all complex
z
with
|z|\leq 1
,

\det\!\Big(I_{q}-\sum_{h=1}^{b_{L}}\Phi_{h}z^{h}\Big)\neq 0.

(ii)

There exist nonnegative numbers
\phi_{h}
with
\|\Phi_{h}\|\leq\phi_{h}
for
h=1,\ldots,b_{L}
such that

\sum_{h=1}^{b_{L}}\phi_{h}<1.

Condition (i) is the standard stability condition for a VAR(
b_{L}
) companion system. Condition (ii) is a convenient envelope used below to control the norms of the constrained lag matrices uniformly.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under Assumption
4.2
, the generalized ScBM-VHAR model is stable.

4.2
Consistency of estimators

In this section, we derive the convergence rates for both models. The ScBM-PVAR and generalized ScBM-VHAR models can be analyzed analogously by stacking their respective seasons or horizons. For the ScBM-PVAR model, we define the stacked autoregressive matrix as

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\Phi_{s}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{qs\times q}.

Let
\hat{\Phi}
and
\tilde{\Phi}
denote the sample estimator and its population counterpart, respectively, where
\tilde{\Phi}
is the expectation taken over the network randomness. The total error then decomposes as

\|\hat{\Phi}-\tilde{\Phi}\|\leq\underbrace{\|\hat{\Phi}-\Phi\|}_{\text{estimation error}}+\underbrace{\|\tilde{\Phi}-\Phi\|}_{\text{network randomness}}.

(4.1)

This section focuses on bounding the estimation error,
\|\hat{\Phi}-\Phi\|
, while the network randomness term in (
4.1
) is addressed in the subsequent section.

First, consider when the OLS estimator
\hat{\Phi}^{\mathrm{ols}}
is used. The idea is to view
\{Y_{n}^{*}\}
as a weakly dependent VAR process. A similar approach is also used in the standard PVAR process
(
Ursu and Duchesne, 2009
;
Boubacar Maïnassara and Ursu, 2023
)
. Let
\Sigma_{Y}^{*}:={\rm Var}(Y_{n}^{*})
and
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{*}:={\rm Var}(\varepsilon_{n}^{*})
. Define the normalized process
(\Sigma_{Y}^{*})^{-1/2}Y_{n}^{*}
and its strong mixing coefficients
\alpha_{Y^{*}}(\ell)
in the usual way.

Assumption 4.3
.

For the ScBM-PVAR model we assume:

(i)

\{\varepsilon_{n}^{*}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is ergodic with
\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{n}^{*}]=0
,
{\rm Var}(\varepsilon_{n}^{*})=\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{*}
and
{\rm Cov}(\varepsilon_{n}^{*},\varepsilon_{n-\ell}^{*})=0
for all
\ell\neq 0
.

(ii)

\|\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{*}\|\leq C_{1}
and
\|(\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{*})^{-1}\|\leq C_{2}
for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
.

(iii)

The normalized process
\{(\Sigma_{Y}^{*})^{-1/2}Y_{n}^{*}\}
is strongly mixing with mixing coefficients
\alpha_{Y^{*}}(\ell)\leq\exp(-c_{1}\ell^{\gamma_{1}})

for all
\ell>0
, some
c_{1},\gamma_{1}>0
.

(iv)

For any unit vector
v
and any
\delta>0
,

\mathbb{P}\bigl(|v^{\prime}(\Sigma_{Y}^{*})^{-1/2}Y_{n}^{*}|>\delta\bigr)\leq\exp\!\bigl(1-(\delta/c_{2})^{\gamma_{2}}\bigr)
for some
c_{2},\gamma_{2}>0
(sub-exponential tails).

(v)

The number of cycles
N=\Omega\bigl((qs)^{2/\gamma-1}\bigr)
,
where
1/\gamma=1/\gamma_{1}+1/\gamma_{2}
and
\gamma<1
.

Assumption 4.4
.

Assume that Assumption
4.3
holds for the stable generalized ScBM–VHAR model after replacing the stacked PVAR process by the companion process

V_{t}:=(Y_{t}^{\prime},Y_{t-1}^{\prime},\ldots,Y_{t-b_{L}+1}^{\prime})^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{qb_{L}},\hskip 20.00003pt\Sigma_{V}:=\operatorname{Var}(V_{t}),

and replacing the sample size by
N=T-b_{L}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose Assumption
4.3
holds. Then, for the stable ScBM-PVAR model,

\|\hat{\Phi}^{\mathrm{ols}}-\Phi\|=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{q}{N}}\right).

For the generalized ScBM-VHAR model, we use the restricted VAR(
b_{L}
) representation induced by the horizon lengths
1<b_{M}<b_{L}
, which yields the following rate.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose Assumptions
4.2
and
4.4
hold for the stable generalized ScBM-VHAR model. Then, with
s=3
and
N=T-b_{L}

\|\hat{\Phi}^{\mathrm{ols}}-\Phi\|=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{sq}{N}}\right).

Next we replace OLS by lasso estimation

N

Assumption 4.5
.

For the stacked ScBM-PVAR regression, we assume:

(i)

The vector

\ell_{P}

For generalized ScBM-VHAR model, let

4.3
Consistency of random graphs

As a continuation of Section
4.2
, we now bound
\|\tilde{\Phi}-\Phi\|
, i.e., the deviation arising from random graphs under the ScBM assumptions.

Assumption 4.7
.

For each normalized weighted-adjacency block that enters the construction of
\Phi
, let

\delta:=\min\!\left\{\min_{i}[O]_{ii},\,\min_{j}[P]_{jj}\right\},\hskip 20.00003pt\tau:=q^{-1}\sum_{i,j}[A^{\prime}]_{ij},

and assume

\delta+\tau=\Omega\!\bigl(sqB_{sq}\bigr),\hskip 20.00003ptB_{sq}=\Omega\!\left(\frac{\log(sq)}{sq}\right).

Since the numbers of seasons, horizons, and lag terms are fixed, the same lower bound is assumed to hold uniformly over all such blocks.

This assumption corresponds to the sparse assumption of networks due to the regularization with expected degrees of order at least
\log(sq)
, which allows exact recovery in ScBM-type models
(
Rohe et al., 2016
;
Abbe, 2018

Theorem 4.1
.

Under Assumption
4.7
and the stability conditions above,

\|\tilde{\Phi}-\Phi\|=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(sq)}{sqB_{sq}}}\right)

4.4
Singular vectors and misclassification rates

We now translate the operator-norm bounds from Sections
4.2
and
4.3
into perturbation bounds for the row-normalized singular vectors and the resulting misclassification rates.
For season
m=1,\ldots,s
, let
\hat{X}^{*}_{m,L}
and
\hat{X}^{*}_{m,R}
denote the estimated row-normalized left and right singular vectors, let
X^{*}_{m,L}
and
X^{*}_{m,R}
denote their population counterparts, and let
\bar{X}^{*}_{m,L}
and
\bar{X}^{*}_{m,R}
denote the corresponding PisCES-smoothed versions. Likewise, for horizon
h\in\{S,M,L\}
, let
\hat{X}^{*}_{(h),L}
and
\hat{X}^{*}_{(h),R}
denote the estimated row-normalized singular vectors, let
X^{*}_{(h),L}
and
X^{*}_{(h),R}
denote their population counterparts, and let
\bar{X}^{*}_{(h),L}
and
\bar{X}^{*}_{(h),R}
denote the corresponding PisCES-smoothed versions. Since singular vectors are identified only up to orthogonal transformations, all bounds below are understood after suitable rotations.

The following conditions are often implicit in prior analyses of regularized spectral clustering and directed co-clustering; cf.
Qin and Rohe, 2013
;
Rohe et al., 2016
;
Guðmundsson and Brownlees, 2021
. In the present asymmetric co-clustering framework, we state them explicitly because stable recovery of both the sending and receiving singular spaces is needed for the singular-vector perturbation and misclassification arguments below.

Assumption 4.8
.

For each seasonal or horizon-specific population matrix used in the co-clustering step, we assume:

(i)

the retained singular subspace is separated from the remainder by an eigengap bounded below by some constant
\delta_{0}>0
;

(ii)

the corresponding population singular-vector matrices satisfy a uniform row-norm lower bound of the form

\min_{i}\|[X_{m,\bullet}]_{i\cdot}\|\geq c_{0}q^{-1/2},\hskip 20.00003pt\min_{i}\|[X_{(h),\bullet}]_{i\cdot}\|\geq c_{0}q^{-1/2},

for some constant
c_{0}>0
and
\bullet\in\{L,R\}
;

(iii)

the distinct population row-normalized centroids are separated by at least
\delta_{*}>0
.

All constants are uniform over seasons
m=1,\ldots,s
and horizons
h\in\{S,M,L\}
.

Remark 4.1
.

Assumption
4.8
is stated at a high level in terms of the population singular structure. It is satisfied under standard balanced-block settings with fixed numbers of sending and receiving communities, effective degree-correction weights of order
q^{-1}
, and a reduced block matrix whose nonzero singular values are separated and whose row-normalized block centroids are distinct. A formal sufficient condition is given in Proposition
A.1
of the Appendix.

Theorem 4.2
.

Consider the ScBM-PVAR model, and write

\eta_{P,N}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{q}{N},&\text{if the OLS estimator is used},\\[4.30554pt]
\dfrac{\ell_{P}\log(qs)}{N},&\text{if the lasso estimator in \eqref{e:pvar_lasso} is used}.\end{cases}

Suppose Assumption
4.8
holds and the PisCES smoothing parameter satisfies
\alpha_{N}\in(0,1/(4\sqrt{2}+2))
and
\alpha_{N}
is sufficiently small. Then there exist orthogonal matrices
R_{m,L}
and
R_{m,R}
,
m=1,\ldots,s
, such that, with high probability,

\sum_{m=1}^{s}\sum_{\bullet\in\{L,R\}}\|\bar{X}^{*}_{m,\bullet}-X^{*}_{m,\bullet}R_{m,\bullet}\|_{F}\leq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\!\left(\sqrt{sq\,\eta_{P,N}}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(sq)}{B_{sq}}}+\alpha_{N}\right).

If
\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{y}_{m}
and
\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{z}_{m}
denote the sets of misclustered sending and receiving nodes at season
m
obtained from the PisCES-smoothed singular vectors, then

\frac{1}{sq}\sum_{m=1}^{s}\big(|\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{y}_{m}|+|\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{z}_{m}|\big)\leq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\!\left(\eta_{P,N}+\frac{\log(sq)}{sqB_{sq}}+\alpha_{N}^{2}\right).

Theorem 4.3
.

Consider generalized ScBM-VHAR, and write

\eta_{V,N}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{sq}{N},&\text{if the OLS estimator is used},\\[4.30554pt]
\dfrac{\ell_{V}\log(sq)}{N},&\text{if the lasso estimator in \eqref{e:vhar_lasso} is used},\end{cases}

where
s=3
and
N=T-b_{L}
. Suppose Assumption
4.8
holds and the PisCES smoothing parameter satisfies
\alpha_{N}\in(0,1/(4\sqrt{2}+2))
and
\alpha_{N}

R_{(h),L}
and
R_{(h),R}
,
h\in\{S,M,L\}

\sum_{h\in\{S,M,L\}}\sum_{\bullet\in\{L,R\}}\|\bar{X}^{*}_{(h),\bullet}-X^{*}_{(h),\bullet}R_{(h),\bullet}\|_{F}\leq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\!\bigg(\sqrt{sq\,\eta_{V,N}}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(sq)}{B_{sq}}}+\alpha_{N}\bigg).

\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{y}_{(h)}
and
\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{z}_{(h)}

.

The unsmoothed procedure is recovered by setting

5
Finite-sample performance

We report finite-sample results for the proposed procedures using sparsely generated ScBM-PVAR and generalized ScBM-VHAR models. In each setup, the networks embedded in the transition matrices are generated once and held fixed across replications, ensuring that the innovations are the sole source of randomness. Furthermore, we do not impose degree correction in this simulation study; this allows us to isolate the effects of latent path evolution from the confounding effects of node-specific degree heterogeneity. We present the results based on the PisCES-smoothed lasso estimator, while the corresponding OLS estimation and adjusted Rand index
(
Vinh et al., 2009
, ARI; e.g.,)
comparisons are deferred to Appendix
B
.

5.1
Experiments on ScBM-PVAR models

For each season
m=1,\ldots,4

At the long horizon, one compact block consists of DJI, IXIC, SPX, and N225, forming a distinct U.S.-centered cluster with Nikkei 225 attached to it. The middle horizon sharpens this separation, while the short horizon produces the strongest internal reorganization. This long-to-short progression suggests that near-term volatility spillovers are shaped less by simple geographic proximity than by heterogeneous market roles and transmission channels.

7
Conclusion

This paper establishes a framework for latent community paths in high-dimensional VAR-type models. By combining degree-corrected stochastic co-blockmodels, directed spectral co-clustering, and eigenvector smoothing, the proposed method tracks how directional groups persist, split, merge, and recompose across seasons or dependence horizons. The framework covers both ScBM–PVAR and generalized ScBM–VHAR models. Its theoretical contribution is to provide non-asymptotic guarantees for singular-vector perturbation and community misclassification through a modular link from first-stage estimation error to clustering error.

The empirical applications illustrate the value of this perspective. In U.S. nonfarm payrolls, the estimated paths distinguish a recurrent business-centered core from more mobile sectors with stronger seasonal reallocation. In global realized volatilities, the estimated paths reveal a compact U.S.-centered long-horizon block, a Europe-heavy developed core, and substantial short-horizon reorganization. These findings suggest that latent community paths provide an interpretable summary of dynamic dependence that is difficult to obtain from entrywise coefficient inspection alone.

[X_{L,q}]_{i\cdot}=[\widetilde{\Theta}_{y,q}]_{ii}^{1/2}e_{k}^{\prime}D_{y,q}^{-1/2}R_{y,q}.

Because
R_{y,q}
has orthonormal columns and
D_{y,q}\to D_{y,*}
with positive diagonal limit, the matrix
D_{y,q}^{-1/2}R_{y,q}
has row norms bounded above and below by positive constants uniformly in
q
. Assumption (iii) gives

[\widetilde{\Theta}_{y,q}]_{ii}^{1/2}\asymp q^{-1/2},

uniformly in
i
. Hence

\|[X_{L,q}]_{i\cdot}\|\asymp q^{-1/2},

uniformly over all
i
. The same argument applied to
X_{R,q}
yields

\|[X_{R,q}]_{j\cdot}\|\asymp q^{-1/2},

uniformly over all
j
. Therefore Assumption 4.8(ii) holds.

Finally, we verify Assumption 4.8(iii). For any
i
in sending block
k
,

[X_{L,q}^{*}]_{i\cdot}=\frac{e_{k}^{\prime}D_{y,q}^{-1/2}R_{y,q}}{\|e_{k}^{\prime}D_{y,q}^{-1/2}R_{y,q}\|},

so all nodes in the same sending block share the same row-normalized population centroid. Since
M_{q}\to M_{*}
and the positive singular values of
M_{*}
are simple, the singular-vector matrices
R_{y,q}
and
R_{z,q}
converge, up to the usual columnwise sign convention, to
R_{y,*}
and
R_{z,*}
. Together with
D_{y,q}\to D_{y,*}
and
D_{z,q}\to D_{z,*}
, this implies that the row-normalized sending and receiving centroids converge to the row-normalized rows of
D_{y,*}^{-1/2}R_{y,*}
and
D_{z,*}^{-1/2}R_{z,*}
, respectively. By Assumption (vi), these limiting centroids are pairwise distinct. Since the numbers of communities are fixed, the minimum pairwise distance between distinct centroids is therefore bounded away from zero for all sufficiently large

.
Hence Assumption 4.8(i)–(iii) holds for
\Omega_{q}
.
∎

Lemma A.1
.

Let
J\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed, and for
j=1,\ldots,J
let
U_{j}
and
\hat{U}_{j}
be orthogonal projection matrices of the same rank
K_{j}
. For
0\leq\alpha_{N}<1/(4\sqrt{2}+2)
, define the empirical PisCES map
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}
and the population PisCES map
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}
by

\displaystyle[\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(V)]_{1}

\displaystyle:=\Pi_{K_{1}}(\hat{U}_{1}+\alpha_{N}V_{2}),

\displaystyle[\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(V)]_{j}

\displaystyle:=\Pi_{K_{j}}(\alpha_{N}V_{j-1}+\hat{U}_{j}+\alpha_{N}V_{j+1}),\hskip 18.49988ptj=2,\ldots,J-1,

\displaystyle[\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(V)]_{J}

\displaystyle:=\Pi_{K_{J}}(\alpha_{N}V_{J-1}+\hat{U}_{J}),

and analogously with
\hat{U}_{j}
replaced by
U_{j}
. Let
\bar{U}_{j}
be the empirical PisCES fixed point and let
U_{j}^{(\alpha_{N})}
be the corresponding population fixed point. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
N
, such that

\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\bar{U}_{j}-U_{j}\|_{F}\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\hat{U}_{j}-U_{j}\|_{F}+\alpha_{N}\right).

Proof.

We adapt the contraction argument in Lemma S1.4 of the supplementary material of
Liu et al., 2018
; see also Theorem S1.2 for the Banach fixed-point step therein.

Step 1 (contraction).
Equip the product space of projector chains with the metric

d(U,V):=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|U_{j}-V_{j}\|_{F}.

Fix two projector chains
V=(V_{j})_{j=1}^{J}
and
W=(W_{j})_{j=1}^{J}
.
First, fix
j=2,\ldots,J-1
. Define

A_{j}:=\alpha_{N}V_{j-1}+\hat{U}_{j}+\alpha_{N}V_{j+1},\hskip 18.49988ptB_{j}:=\alpha_{N}W_{j-1}+\hat{U}_{j}+\alpha_{N}W_{j+1}.

Consider the eigenvalue bounds for
A_{j}
. Since
\hat{U}_{j}
is an orthogonal projector of rank
K_{j}
, its eigenvalues are exactly
1
(with multiplicity
K_{j}
) and
0
(with the remaining
multiplicity). Since
V_{j-1}
and
V_{j+1}
are orthogonal projectors, they are positive semidefinite (PSD). Applying Weyl’s inequality to
A_{j}=\hat{U}_{j}+\alpha_{N}(V_{j-1}+V_{j+1})
yields the following:

•

The
K_{j}
-th eigenvalue of
\hat{U}_{j}
is
1
, and
\alpha_{N}(V_{j-1}+V_{j+1})
is PSD, so its smallest eigenvalue is
\geq 0
. Hence

\lambda_{K_{j}}(A_{j})\geq\lambda_{K_{j}}(\hat{U}_{j})+\lambda_{\min}\left(\alpha_{N}(V_{j-1}+V_{j+1})\right)\geq 1.

•

The
(K_{j}+1)
-st eigenvalue of
\hat{U}_{j}
is
0
. Each of
V_{j-1}
and
V_{j+1}
is an orthogonal projector, so
\|V_{j\pm 1}\|_{2}=1
, giving
\lambda_{\max}(\alpha_{N}(V_{j-1}+V_{j+1}))\leq 2\alpha_{N}
.
Hence

\lambda_{K_{j}+1}(A_{j})\leq\lambda_{K_{j}+1}(\hat{U}_{j})+\lambda_{\max}\left(\alpha_{N}(V_{j-1}+V_{j+1})\right)\leq 2\alpha_{N}.

The same argument, with
W_{j\pm 1}
in place of
V_{j\pm 1}
, gives identical bounds for
B_{j}
.
Therefore, the eigengap separating the top-
K_{j}
eigenspace from the rest satisfies

\delta_{j}:=\lambda_{K_{j}}(A_{j})-\lambda_{K_{j}+1}(A_{j})\;\geq\;1-2\alpha_{N}\;>\;0,

where the strict inequality uses
\alpha_{N}<1/(4\sqrt{2}+2)<1/2
. Since
A_{j}-B_{j}=\alpha_{N}(V_{j-1}-W_{j-1})+\alpha_{N}(V_{j+1}-W_{j+1})
, the Davis-Kahan theorem
(
Davis and Kahan, 1970
)
gives

\displaystyle\left\|[\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(V)]_{j}-[\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(W)]_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\|A_{j}-B_{j}\|_{F}}{\delta_{j}}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\alpha_{N}}{1-2\alpha_{N}}\Bigl(\|V_{j-1}-W_{j-1}\|_{F}+\|V_{j+1}-W_{j+1}\|_{F}\Bigr).

Next, for
j=1
, define
A_{1}:=\hat{U}_{1}+\alpha_{N}V_{2}
and
B_{1}:=\hat{U}_{1}+\alpha_{N}W_{2}
.
The same Weyl argument gives
\lambda_{K_{1}}(A_{1})\geq 1
and
\lambda_{K_{1}+1}(A_{1})\leq\alpha_{N}
, so the eigengap is still greater than
1-2\alpha_{N}
. Hence, by the Davis-Kahan Theorem,

\left\|[\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(V)]_{1}-[\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(W)]_{1}\right\|_{F}\;\leq\;\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\alpha_{N}}{1-2\alpha_{N}}\|V_{2}-W_{2}\|_{F}.

A similar argument is applied to
j=J
. Finally, for each
k=1,\ldots,J
, the term
\|V_{k}-W_{k}\|_{F}
appears on the right-hand side as a neighbor contribution from index
j=k-1
and from index
j=k+1
. Since every term
\|V_{k}-W_{k}\|_{F}
appears at most twice in the total sum,

d\left(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(V),\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(W)\right)\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2}\,\alpha_{N}}{1-2\alpha_{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{J}\|V_{k}-W_{k}\|_{F}=\rho(\alpha_{N})\,d(V,W),\hskip 18.49988pt\rho(\alpha_{N}):=\frac{2\sqrt{2}\,\alpha_{N}}{1-2\alpha_{N}}.

Since
\alpha_{N}<1/(4\sqrt{2}+2)
, we have
\rho(\alpha_{N})<1
, so
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}
is a strict contraction. The same argument is applied to
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}
.

Step 2 (empirical and population fixed points).

Let

\bar{U}:=(\bar{U}_{j})_{j=1}^{J}
and
U^{(\alpha_{N})}:=(U_{j}^{(\alpha_{N})})_{j=1}^{J}
. Since both maps are contractions, their fixed points are unique. By the triangle inequality and the contraction bound,

\displaystyle d\bigl(\bar{U},U^{(\alpha_{N})}\bigr)

\displaystyle=d\bigl(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(\bar{U}),\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U^{(\alpha_{N})})\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq d\bigl(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(\bar{U}),\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(U^{(\alpha_{N})})\bigr)+d\bigl(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(U^{(\alpha_{N})}),\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U^{(\alpha_{N})})\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\rho(\alpha_{N})d\bigl(\bar{U},U^{(\alpha_{N})}\bigr)+d\bigl(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(U^{(\alpha_{N})}),\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U^{(\alpha_{N})})\bigr).

Hence

d\bigl(\bar{U},U^{(\alpha_{N})}\bigr)\leq\frac{1}{1-\rho(\alpha_{N})}d\bigl(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(U^{(\alpha_{N})}),\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U^{(\alpha_{N})})\bigr).

Step 3 (perturbation of the map itself).

Fix a projector chain
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{J})
. For
j=2,\ldots,J-1
,

\widehat{A}_{j}:=\alpha_{N}V_{j-1}+\hat{U}_{j}+\alpha_{N}V_{j+1},\hskip 18.49988ptA_{j}:=\alpha_{N}V_{j-1}+U_{j}+\alpha_{N}V_{j+1}.

The same eigengap argument as in Step 1 gives

\bigl\|[\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(V)]_{j}-[\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(V)]_{j}\bigr\|_{F}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1-2\alpha_{N}}\|\hat{U}_{j}-U_{j}\|_{F},

and similarly to
j=1
and
J
. Therefore

d\bigl(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha_{N}}(V),\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(V)\bigr)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1-2\alpha_{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\hat{U}_{j}-U_{j}\|_{F}.

Applying this with
V=U^{(\alpha_{N})}
in Step 2 yields

d\bigl(\bar{U},U^{(\alpha_{N})}\bigr)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(1-\rho(\alpha_{N}))(1-2\alpha_{N})}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\hat{U}_{j}-U_{j}\|_{F}.

Since
\alpha_{N}
is uniformly bounded by
1/(4\sqrt{2}+2)
, the coefficient above is bounded by an absolute constant.

Step 4 (deterministic smoothing bias).

It remains to bound the distance between the population PisCES fixed point and the raw population projector chain. Since
U^{(\alpha_{N})}
is the fixed point of
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}
,

d\bigl(U^{(\alpha_{N})},U\bigr)=d\bigl(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U^{(\alpha_{N})}),U\bigr)

and therefore

d\bigl(U^{(\alpha_{N})},U\bigr)\leq d\bigl(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U^{(\alpha_{N})}),\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U)\bigr)+d\bigl(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U),U\bigr)\leq\rho(\alpha_{N})d\bigl(U^{(\alpha_{N})},U\bigr)+d\bigl(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U),U\bigr).

Hence

d\bigl(U^{(\alpha_{N})},U\bigr)\leq\frac{1}{1-\rho(\alpha_{N})}\,d\bigl(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U),U\bigr).

For each
j=1,\ldots,J
, write

E_{j}:=\alpha_{N}\sum_{j^{\prime}\sim j}U_{j^{\prime}},\hskip 18.49988ptM_{j}:=U_{j}+E_{j},\hskip 18.49988pt[\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U)]_{j}=\Pi_{K_{j}}(M_{j}),

where
j^{\prime}\sim j
denotes the neighbors of
j
in the chain. Since
U_{j}
is a rank-
K_{j}
orthogonal projector and
E_{j}
is positive semidefinite,

\lambda_{K_{j}}(M_{j})\geq 1,\hskip 18.49988pt\lambda_{K_{j}+1}(M_{j})\leq 2\alpha_{N},

so the eigengap is at least
1-2\alpha_{N}
. By the Davis–Kahan theorem,

\bigl\|[\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U)]_{j}-U_{j}\bigr\|_{F}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1-2\alpha_{N}}\,\|E_{j}\|_{F}.

Let
K_{\max}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq J}K_{j}
. Since each
U_{j^{\prime}}
is an orthogonal projector and each index has at most two neighbors,

\|E_{j}\|_{F}\leq\alpha_{N}\sum_{j^{\prime}\sim j}\|U_{j^{\prime}}\|_{F}\leq 2\sqrt{K_{\max}}\,\alpha_{N}.

Therefore

d\bigl(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U),U\bigr)=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\bigl\|[\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{N}}(U)]_{j}-U_{j}\bigr\|_{F}\leq C\,\alpha_{N}

for some constant
C>0
. Since
\alpha_{N}\in[0,1/(4\sqrt{2}+2))
, both
(1-2\alpha_{N})^{-1}
and
(1-\rho(\alpha_{N}))^{-1}
are uniformly bounded, so

d\bigl(U^{(\alpha_{N})},U\bigr)\leq C\,\alpha_{N}.

Combining this with Step 3 proves the lemma. ∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
X,\bar{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times K}
have orthonormal columns, and let
R
be orthogonal. Suppose the population row norms satisfy

\min_{1\leq i\leq q}\|[X]_{i\cdot}\|\geq c_{0}q^{-1/2}

for some constant
c_{0}>0
, and assume the perturbation is small enough that

\|\bar{X}-XR\|_{F}\leq\frac{c_{0}}{2}q^{-1/2}.

Let
X^{*}
and
\bar{X}^{*}
denote the corresponding row-normalized matrices. Then

\|\bar{X}^{*}-X^{*}R\|_{F}\leq Cq^{1/2}\|\bar{X}-XR\|_{F},

for a constant
C>0
depending only on
c_{0}
.

Proof.

The inequality

\left\|\frac{a}{\|a\|}-\frac{b}{\|b\|}\right\|\leq\frac{2\|a-b\|}{\|b\|}

holds whenever
\|a-b\|\leq\|b\|/2
. Apply this row by row with
a=[\bar{X}]_{i\cdot}
and
b=[XR]_{i\cdot}
. The assumed Frobenius bound implies

\|[\bar{X}]_{i\cdot}-[XR]_{i\cdot}\|\leq\|\bar{X}-XR\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{2}\min_{1\leq i\leq q}\|[X]_{i\cdot}\|,

so that the row-wise inequality is applicable. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\bar{X}^{*}-X^{*}R\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\left\|\frac{[\bar{X}]_{i\cdot}}{\|[\bar{X}]_{i\cdot}\|}-\frac{[XR]_{i\cdot}}{\|[XR]_{i\cdot}\|}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{4\|[\bar{X}-XR]_{i\cdot}\|^{2}}{\|[XR]_{i\cdot}\|^{2}}\leq\frac{4q}{c_{0}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|[\bar{X}-XR]_{i\cdot}\|^{2}=\frac{4q}{c_{0}^{2}}\|\bar{X}-XR\|_{F}^{2},

which proves the claim.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
X^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times K}
be a population row-normalized singular-vector matrix whose rows take only finitely many distinct values, and let
\bar{X}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times K}
be its empirical counterpart. Suppose the population centroids are separated by at least
\delta_{*}>0
. If
\bar{\mathcal{N}}
denotes the set of misclustered nodes obtained by applying
k
-means to the rows of
\bar{X}^{*}
, then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
\delta_{*}
, such that

\frac{|\bar{\mathcal{N}}|}{q}\leq\frac{C}{q}\|\bar{X}^{*}-X^{*}R\|_{F}^{2}

for a suitable orthogonal matrix

.

This is the standard
k
-means reduction used in spectral clustering. For completeness, we include the short argument; compare Appendix B of
Rohe et al., 2011
, Theorem 4.4 of
Qin and Rohe, 2013
, and Section F of
Rohe et al., 2016
. Let
C^{*}
and
\bar{C}
denote the population and empirical centroid matrices, respectively. Because
C^{*}
is feasible for the empirical
k
-means problem,

\|\bar{X}^{*}-\bar{C}\|_{F}\leq\|\bar{X}^{*}-C^{*}\|_{F}.

Hence

\|\bar{C}-C^{*}\|_{F}\leq\|\bar{C}-\bar{X}^{*}\|_{F}+\|\bar{X}^{*}-C^{*}\|_{F}\leq 2\|\bar{X}^{*}-C^{*}\|_{F}=2\|\bar{X}^{*}-X^{*}R\|_{F}.

i
is misclustered, then the centroid assigned to it must be at least

∎

\sum_{h\in\{S,M,L\}}\sum_{\bullet\in\{L,R\}}\|\bar{X}_{(h),\bullet}^{*}-X_{(h),\bullet}^{*}R_{(h),\bullet}\|_{F}\leq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\!\left(\sqrt{sq\,\eta_{V,N}}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(sq)}{B_{sq}}}+\alpha_{N}\right).

Moreover, when
\alpha_{N}=0
, the same bound holds with
\bar{X}_{(h),\bullet}^{*}
replaced by
\hat{X}_{(h),\bullet}^{*}
.

Proof.

Repeat the proof of Lemma
A.4
with seasons
m=1,\ldots,s
replaced by horizons
h\in\{S,M,L\}
and with
s=3
fixed. The generalized ScBM-VHAR model uses the restricted VAR(
b_{L}
) representation, but once the operator-norm bound for
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathrm{sym}}-\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathrm{sym}}
is available, the rest of the arguments are identical.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.
Combining Lemmas
4.3
and
4.5
with Theorem
4.1
and the definition of
\eta_{P,N}
, we obtain

\|\widetilde{\Phi}_{S}-\widehat{\Phi}_{S}\|=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\!\left(\sqrt{\eta_{P,N}}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(sq)}{sqB_{sq}}}\right)

for both the OLS and entrywise-lasso first-stage estimators. Consequently, Lemma
A.4
establishes the stated PisCES-smoothed singular-vector bound.

It remains to translate this singular-vector bound into a misclassification bound. Let
\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{m}^{y}
and
\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{m}^{z}
denote the sets of misclustered sending and receiving nodes, respectively, at season
m
, as determined by the PisCES-smoothed singular vectors. Applying Lemma
A.3
to both sets of nodes and averaging over
m=1,\ldots,s
yields

\frac{1}{sq}\sum_{m=1}^{s}\big(|\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{m}^{y}|+|\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{m}^{z}|\big)\leq\frac{C}{sq}\sum_{m=1}^{s}\sum_{\bullet\in\{L,R\}}\|\bar{X}_{m,\bullet}^{*}-X_{m,\bullet}^{*}R_{m,\bullet}\|_{F}^{2}

for some constant
C>0
that depends only on the population centroid-separation constants in Assumption
4.8
(iii). Substituting the singular-vector bound from Lemma
A.4
and applying the basic inequality
(a+b+c)^{2}\lesssim a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{sq}\sum_{m=1}^{s}\big(|\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{m}^{y}|+|\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{m}^{z}|\big)\leq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\!\bigg(\frac{1}{sq}\left[\sqrt{sq\,\eta_{P,N}}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(sq)}{B_{sq}}}+\alpha_{N}\right]^{2}\bigg)\leq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\eta_{P,N}+\frac{\log(sq)}{sqB_{sq}}+\alpha_{N}^{2}\Big).

This establishes the claimed bound. The remark following the theorem follows immediately from the special case
\alpha_{N}=0
in Lemma
A.4
. ∎
