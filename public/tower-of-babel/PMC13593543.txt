Introduction

The Affera dual-energy lattice-tip platform has demonstrated efficacy for atrial fibrillation (AF) ablation and for creation of linear lesions, integrating high-density mapping and focal pulsed-field ablation (PFA) within a single catheter.1,2 In contrast, published experience with the Affera platform for reentrant atrial arrhythmias remains limited, and its role in treatment within the superior vena cava (SVC) has yet to be established.

Case report

A 72-year-old man with a history of pulmonary vein (PV) isolation using cryoballoon ablation 10 years earlier was referred for repeat ablation because of recurrent AF. The patient was taking irbesartan 100 mg once daily, nifedipine controlled-release 40 mg once daily, and edoxaban 60 mg once daily. Because sinus rhythm could not be restored despite intracardiac cardioversion, voltage mapping was performed during ongoing AF using the Affera system and Sphere-9 catheter (Medtronic, Minneapolis, MN). Voltage mapping during AF demonstrated reconnection of the left inferior and right inferior PVs; however, the precise reconnection sites could not be determined. Reisolation of both PVs was performed using an encircling PFA approach with the Sphere-9 catheter.

After PV reisolation, intracardiac cardioversion transiently restored sinus rhythm, but atrial tachycardia (AT) was spontaneously initiated immediately. High-density mapping using the Affera system demonstrated a reentrant circuit at the SVC–right atrium (RA) junction (Figure 1A). The activation sequence in the left atrium was consistent with passive activation from the RA (Figure 1B). The voltage map during AT showed 2 separate low-voltage areas at the SVC-RA junction (Figure 1C). When interpreted together with the activation map, the area between these low-voltage regions was considered to represent the critical isthmus. The Sphere-9 catheter acquires closed-unipolar electrograms from multiple surface microelectrodes referenced to a central indifferent electrode. The recorded electrograms spanned a relatively wide range of the tachycardia cycle length, suggesting a reentrant circuit confined to a limited region around the SVC-RA junction (Figure 2A). The postpacing interval recorded by the Sphere-9 catheter positioned at the SVC-RA junction was nearly identical to the tachycardia cycle length (Figures 2B and 3). After spontaneous termination of AT, both atrial and SVC potentials were recorded on a single electrode of the Affera Sphere-9 catheter, indicating that the recording site was located at the SVC-RA junction. A spiky fragmented potential was observed between these potentials, suggesting the critical isthmus of the reentrant circuit (Figure 4A). The earliest activation site during sinus rhythm was confirmed by high-density mapping with the Affera system (Figure 4B). A circumferential white line drawn at the level of the critical isthmus of the reentrant circuit was superimposed on the sinus activation map. This demonstrated that the critical isthmus was located approximately 2 cm cranial to the earliest sinus activation site and sufficiently distant from the sinus node. Voltage mapping performed during sinus rhythm revealed long myocardial sleeves extending into the SVC and demonstrated an oblique low-voltage area at the presumed SVC-RA junction (Figure 4C). Because a previous case series using a circular multielectrode array catheter demonstrated the safety and efficacy of PFA targeting 2 cm cranial to the earliest sinus activation site,3 circumferential PFA along this line was planned with a 2-cm margin to avoid sinus node dysfunction. Circumferential PFA was performed at the SVC-RA junction (Figure 5A and 5B), and postablation mapping confirmed adequate lesion formation (Figure 5C and 5D). The earliest activation site during sinus rhythm did not change after PFA compared with before ablation. The heart rate during sinus rhythm remained unchanged after PFA. The procedure was concluded after confirming that no tachyarrhythmia was inducible. Chest radiography on the next day revealed no phrenic nerve palsy.

Discussion

This case demonstrates the clinical utility of the Affera mapping system and Sphere-9 catheter for both diagnosis and treatment of an unusual AT involving the SVC-RA junction. High-density mapping allowed detailed assessment of the reentrant circuit and its relationship to the sinus node. In addition, to the best of our knowledge, this case represents the first report focusing on SVC-region treatment using the Affera system and Sphere-9 catheter.

The mechanism of this AT was considered to be reentry involving the SVC-RA junction. In the present case, voltage mapping during sinus rhythm demonstrated an oblique low-voltage area at the presumed SVC-RA junction.4 This area may have served as a substrate for conduction block and slow conduction. In addition, voltage mapping during AT demonstrated a low-voltage area around the presumed critical isthmus, which provided a clearer understanding of the reentrant circuit and the location of the critical isthmus. Interestingly, the voltage maps obtained during sinus rhythm and AT showed different appearances. This finding suggests that the critical isthmus may not have been explained solely by a fixed anatomic low-voltage substrate, but may also have involved a functional component, such as rate-dependent conduction slowing or functional block. Therefore, high-density mapping using the Affera system was useful for understanding the reentrant circuit and identifying the region most likely responsible for maintenance of the tachycardia.

Radiofrequency ablation in the SVC region carries a risk of phrenic nerve injury,5 whereas PFA may reduce collateral damage because of its tissue-selective properties. Nevertheless, sinus node dysfunction remains an important concern when treating arrhythmias near the SVC-RA junction. In the present case, spontaneous termination of AT allowed high-density mapping during sinus rhythm, which enabled identification of the earliest sinus activation site. This confirmed that the critical isthmus of the AT was located approximately 2 cm cranial to this site. Because a previous case series using a circular multielectrode array catheter demonstrated the safety and efficacy of PFA targeting the SVC 2 cm cranial to the earliest sinus activation site, we considered that a similar strategy could be applied in the present case. Circumferential PFA at this level was expected to treat the critical isthmus of the AT and achieve SVC isolation while maintaining an adequate distance from the presumed sinus node region. We selected SVC isolation rather than focal ablation of the critical isthmus alone because this strategy might reduce the risk of AT/AF recurrence by treating the SVC as a possible non-PV trigger source for AF.3, 4, 5 In this case, SVC isolation was successfully achieved using the Affera system and Sphere-9 catheter without sinus node dysfunction or phrenic nerve palsy.
