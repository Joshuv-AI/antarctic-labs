Core EP, Dual Core EP and Composite Generalized Inverses for a Class of Structured Matrices
Faustino Maciala, C. Mendes Araújo, Pedro Patrício

Abstract
. We study generalized inverses for matrices associated with double star digraphs. Explicit block formulas and existence criteria are obtained for core, dual core, core EP, and dual core EP inverses, expressed in terms of explicit algebraic criteria derived from the underlying block structure. Other combined outer pseudoinverses, combining Moore–Penrose and core-type inverses, are derived with existence criteria.

15A09, 05C20, 05C50

1.
Introduction

Generalized inverses are standard tools for treating singular dynamics, constrained least squares problems, and spectral decompositions in linear and networked systems. The Drazin inverse
A^{D}
extends inversion to arbitrary index, while the Moore–Penrose inverse
A^{\dagger}
provides
*
-orthogonal projections and optimality properties. The reader is referred to
benisraelgreville
;
campbell1979
for results on generalized inverses over the field of complex numbers, and to
ChenBook
for a more recent approach to the algebraic theory of generalized inverses.

In graph matrix theory, the interaction between combinatorics and linear algebra has led to explicit formulas for inverses and generalized inverses of matrices associated with several graph families (trees, wheels, bipartite and path based classes), typically via full rank factorizations and Schur complements (
Balaji
;
bookBapat
;
Bapat
;
Catral-grp
;
Catral2
;
Godsil
;
McLeman
;
McDonald
).

A wide range of generalized inverses has been introduced in the literature as extensions of both the Moore–Penrose inverse and the Drazin inverse. These generalizations are typically obtained by modifying certain defining equations or characterization conditions, while selectively preserving, relaxing, or omitting others. Core-type generalized inverses and their variants have been extensively studied in rings with involution and in matrix/operator settings, including pseudo-core versions, core inverse for
2\times 2
matrices and for certain products, and several recent refinements (
Chen2020
;
gao2018pseudo
;
JiWei-coreEP2021
;
ke2019core
;
Mosic2024
;
MosaicMarovt-weighted-coreEP
;
Mosic2021
;
Stanimirovic
;
wang2019right
;
WuEtAl2024
). DMP and MPD inverses were introduced and analyzed by Malik and Thome (
Malik2014
).

We focus on a family of block-structured matrices associated with double star digraphs, where two hub-periphery components are coupled through their central vertices. Despite their simple form, these matrices admit a rich invertibility structure, allowing explicit characterizations and closed-form expressions for several generalized inverses via Schur complement and full rank factorization techniques.

In this paper we address generalized invertibility of matrices associated with
double star digraphs
, namely we study:
(i) core-type inverses based on the existence of Moore–Penrose and group inverses;
(ii) core EP and dual core EP inverses using
(1,3)
– and
(1,4)
–inverses criteria and full rank factorizations, with explicit block formulas; and
(iii) composite outer inverses, namely the Moore–Penrose–core EP inverse (MPCEP), the
{}^{\ast}\!
core EP–Moore–Penrose inverse (
{}^{\ast}\!
CEPMP), generalized dual core (GDC) inverse and generalized core (GC) inverse, deriving block formulas and existence conditions for these compositions.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
covers preliminaries: double star digraphs and associated matrices, Drazin and Moore–Penrose inverses,
(1,3)
– and
(1,4)
–inverses, and the Gram/Schur framework. Section
3
treats core and dual core inverses, with existence conditions and block formulas. Section
4
develops core EP and dual core EP inverses, providing case-wise existence criteria and block expressions. Section
5
derives the compositions of Moore–Penrose and core-type inverses MPCEP and
{}^{\ast}\!
CEPMP, while Section
6
covers the generalized core and dual core inverses, both including existence conditions and block formulas. Finally, Section
7
provides a summary table collecting the existence conditions for all generalized inverses considered in this paper, restricted to the nontrivial cases in which the group inverse does not exist and the Drazin inverse is nonzero.

Preliminaries

All our matrices are over a general field
\mathbb{F}
with involution
\overline{\cdot}
. This involution on
\mathbb{F}
induces an involution
*
on the set of finite matrices over
\mathbb{F}
, defined as follows: if
A=[a_{ij}],
then
A^{*}=[a_{ij}]^{*}=[\overline{a_{ji}}]
. Also,
\overline{A}=\overline{[a_{ij}]}=[\overline{a_{ij}}].

Let
K_{1,m}
and

be two complete bipartite digraphs with central vertices

and

, and vertex sets
\{u\}\cup U
,
U=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}
, and
\{v\}\cup V
,
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
, respectively. The
double star digraph

S_{(m+1),(n+1)}
is defined as the digraph obtained by joining the central vertices
u
and
v
with two directed edges, one in each direction. Equivalently, the vertex set of
S_{(m+1),(n+1)}
is
U\cup V\cup\{u,v\}
, and it contains all directed edges of
K_{1,m}
and
K_{1,n}
; that is, edges from
u
to each
u_{i}
and from each
u_{i}
to
u
, and edges from
v
to each
v_{j}
and from each
v_{j}
to
v
. In addition, the edges
u\to v
and
v\to u
are included, thus completing the structure of the double star digraph. An example of a double star digraph is shown in Figure
1
.

Figure 1.
Double star digraph
S_{(m+1),(n+1)}
with

leaves on the first star (center

n=4

Given a square matrix
A=[a_{ij}]
of order
n
, the
associated digraph

D(A)=(V,E)
is defined to have vertex set
V=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\}
and edge set
E
, where
(w_{i},w_{j})\in E
if and only if
a_{ij}\neq 0
. The entry
a_{ij}
may be regarded as the weight of the directed edge from
w_{i}
to
w_{j}
, and it is equal to zero precisely when such an edge is absent.

The block structure of any matrix associated with a double star digraph reflects the decomposition of the digraph into its two star components: each star contributes a diagonal block corresponding to the interactions between its central vertex and its pendant vertices, while the two directed edges joining the central vertices appear as off-diagonal blocks connecting these components.

Moreover, when different matrices are constructed from a double star digraph, their entries depend both on the chosen ordering of the vertices and on the weights assigned to its edges. However, whenever two such matrices correspond to the same weighted double star digraph – that is, the edge weights are preserved – they are permutation similar. Specifically, if
A
and
B
arise from two distinct vertex orderings but encode the same weighted digraph, then there exists a permutation matrix
P
such that
A=P^{-1}BP
. We shall denote this relation by
A\underset{\text{perm.}}{\approx}B
. Since
P^{*}=P^{-1}
, this is a particular instance of unitary similarity. As a consequence, properties such as the existence of the Drazin inverse, the group inverse, the Moore–Penrose inverse, and the remaining pseudoinverses addressed in this study are independent of the ordering chosen for the vertices.

Let
x
and
y
be vectors of length
m
, and
z
and
w
vectors of length
n
, each having all entries nonzero. Consider the square matrix of order
m+n+2
defined by

(2.1)

M=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&x^{T}&a&0\\
y&0&0&0\\
\hline\cr b&0&0&z^{T}\\
0&0&w&0\end{array}\right],

where
a
and
b
are nonzero elements. Then the digraph
D(M)
corresponds to a double star digraph
S_{(m+1),(n+1)}
and any matrix
A
with associated digraph
D(A)=S_{(m+1),(n+1)}
is permutation similar to a matrix of the form described in (
2.1
).

For later use, we introduce the scalars

(2.2)

\begin{split}&s:=x^{\ast}x,\quad t:=z^{\ast}z,\quad u:=y^{\ast}y,\quad v:=w^{\ast}w,\quad r:=a\overline{a}+w^{\ast}w,\\
&h:=b\overline{b}+y^{\ast}y,\quad p:=b\overline{b}+z^{\ast}z,\quad q:=a\overline{a}+x^{\ast}x,\quad\zeta:=x^{T}y+ab,\\
&\beta:=\zeta\overline{\zeta}+b\overline{b}\,w^{\ast}w,\quad\alpha:=\zeta\overline{\zeta}+a\overline{a}\,z^{\ast}z.\end{split}

These parameters play a central role in the Schur complement and Gram-type reductions that determine the rank behaviour and singularity patterns of matrices associated with double star digraphs. In particular, the special case
\zeta=0
induces structurally singular configurations in which several generalized inverse formulas simplify substantially.

As mentioned above, any matrix associated with a given double star digraph
S_{(m+1),(n+1)}
is unitarily similar to a matrix in the canonical block form (
2.1
). Consequently, no generality is lost by performing the analysis exclusively on matrices of this form. In particular, all issues related to Moore–Penrose, Drazin,
(1,3)
–, and
(1,4)
–invertibility may be treated within this canonical representation.

The Drazin inverse
A^{D}
of a square matrix
A
over an arbitrary field is the unique matrix satisfying

A^{\,k+1}A^{D}=A^{\,k},\qquad A^{D}AA^{D}=A^{D},\qquad AA^{D}=A^{D}A,

where
k
denotes the Drazin index of
A
, written
i(A)
. The Drazin index agrees with the algebraic index determined by the minimal polynomial. Equivalently,
i(A)
is the smallest nonnegative integer
k
such that

\operatorname{rank}(A^{k})=\operatorname{rank}(A^{k+1}).

The group inverse
A^{\#}
is the special case of the Drazin inverse corresponding to matrices of index at most one, in Refs.
Cline1965
;
Cline1968
.

In
McDonald
the authors establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of the group inverse of a matrix of the form (
2.1
). Specifically,
M^{\#}
exists if and only if
x^{T}y\neq 0
and
z^{T}w\neq 0
. In this case, the group inverse is explicitly given by

(2.3)

M^{\sharp}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&(x^{T}y)^{-1}x^{T}&0&0\\
(x^{T}y)^{-1}y&0&0&-a[(x^{T}y)(z^{T}w)]^{-1}yz^{T}\\
\hline\cr 0&0&0&(z^{T}w)^{-1}z^{T}\\
0&-b[(x^{T}y)(z^{T}w)]^{-1}wx^{T}&(z^{T}w)^{-1}w&0\end{array}\right].

When
M
fails to admit a group inverse (that is, when
x^{T}y=0
or
z^{T}w=0
) the analysis must be carried out within the framework of Drazin invertibility, see
Dragana
. In this situation, and without loss of generality, the study reduces to three mutually exclusive and exhaustive cases (see
maciala2025characterization
;
araujo2026
), which constitute the structural basis for the pseudoinvertibility results developed in the subsequent sections. These cases are stated below.

Case I:
x^{T}y=0=z^{T}w.

Then
i(M)=2
and

(2.4)

M^{D}=(ab)^{-1}\!\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&x^{T}&a&0\\
y&0&0&b^{-1}yz^{T}\\
\hline\cr b&0&0&z^{T}\\
0&a^{-1}wx^{T}&w&0\end{array}\right].

Case II:
x^{T}y\neq 0=z^{T}w
and
\zeta=x^{T}y+ab\neq 0.

Then
i(M)=3
and

(2.5)

M^{D}=\zeta^{-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&x^{T}&a&0\\
y&0&0&\zeta^{-1}ayz^{T}\\
\hline\cr b&0&0&\zeta^{-1}abz^{T}\\
0&\zeta^{-1}bwx^{T}&\zeta^{-1}abw&0\end{array}\right].

Case III:
x^{T}y\neq 0=z^{T}w
and
\zeta=x^{T}y+ab=0.

Then
i(M)=5
and

(2.6)

M^{D}=0.

It is worth noting that the situation in which
x^{T}y=0
and
z^{T}w\neq 0
does not introduce a genuinely new configuration: by a suitable permutation similarity, this case reduces to the previously analysed situation where
x^{T}y\neq 0
and
z^{T}w=0
. Consequently, the three cases stated above provide a complete and mutually exclusive description of the Drazin invertibility of matrices associated with double star digraphs that do not admit a group inverse.

Throughout the remainder of the paper, any reference to Case I, Case II, or Case III is to be interpreted with respect to the above tripartite classification.

A matrix
A
is said to be Moore–Penrose invertible (with respect to
*
) if the system of equations

\displaystyle(1)\;AXA=A,\quad(2)\;XAX=X,\quad(3)\;(AX)^{*}=AX,\quad(4)\;(XA)^{*}=XA.

admits a common solution. When it exists, this solution is unique and is denoted by
A^{\dagger}
.

A matrix satisfying equation (1) is called a von Neumann inverse of
A
and is denoted by
A^{-}
; the set of all von Neumann inverses of
A
is written
A\{1\}
. A common solution of (1) and (2) is called a reflexive inverse of
A
, written
A^{+}
. For any two von Neumann inverses
A^{-},A^{=}\in A\{1\}
of
A
, the product
A^{-}AA^{=}
is a reflexive inverse of
A
.

We denote by
A\{1,3\}
(respectively
A\{1,4\}
) the set of matrices satisfying equations (1) and (3) (respectively (1) and (4)); such matrices are called
(1,3)
–inverses (respectively
(1,4)
–inverses) of
A
. We write
A^{(1,3)}
and
A^{(1,4)}
for arbitrary elements of these sets whenever they are nonempty.

We adopt the notation

Proposition 2.1
.

Given a full rank factorization of a matrix

,

(1)

the following are equivalent:

(a)

A\{1,3\}\neq\varnothing

(b)

F^{*}F
is invertible

(c)

\mathscr{R}(A^{*})=\mathscr{R}(A^{*}A)

(d)

\operatorname{rank}(A^{*}A)=\operatorname{rank}(A)

In this case,
(A^{*}A)^{-}A^{*}\in A\{1,3\}
.

(2)

the following are equivalent:

(a)

A\{1,4\}\neq\varnothing

(b)

GG^{*}
is invertible

(c)

\mathscr{R}(A)=\mathscr{R}(AA^{*})

(d)

\operatorname{rank}(AA^{*})=\operatorname{rank}(A)

In this case,
A^{*}(AA^{*})^{-}\in A\{1,4\}

Proof.

We prove part (1); the proof of part (2) follows by a completely analogous argument.

(1b)\Rightarrow(1a)
. We may take, as a reflexive inverse of
F
, any left inverse, that is, a matrix
F^{+}
satisfying
F^{+}F=I
. Likewise, we may choose as a reflexive inverse of
G
any right inverse
G^{+}
such that
GG^{+}=I
.

Let
B
and
C
be matrices of compatible sizes. If
BA^{*}A=CA^{*}A
, then

BG^{*}F^{*}FG=CG^{*}F^{*}FG,

and hence

BG^{*}F^{*}F=CG^{*}F^{*}F.

Since
F^{*}F
is invertible, we obtain
BG^{*}=CG^{*}
, and therefore
BA^{*}=CA^{*}
.

Analogously, if
A^{*}AB=A^{*}AC
then
AB=AC
.

Applying this one-sided
*
-cancellation yields

A(A^{*}A)^{-}A^{*}A=A,

as it follows from

A^{*}A(A^{*}A)^{-}A^{*}A=A^{*}A.

It remains to show that
A(A^{*}A)^{-}A^{*}
is symmetric. To this end, observe that
\left((A^{*}A)^{-}\right)^{*}\in A^{*}A\{1\}
.

The product
A(A^{*}A)^{-}A^{*}
is invariant under the choice of von Neumann inverse of
A^{*}A
; that is,

A(A^{*}A)^{-}A^{*}=A(A^{*}A)^{=}A^{*}

for any
(A^{*}A)^{-},(A^{*}A)^{=}\in A^{*}A\{1\}
. This follows directly from
A^{*}A(A^{*}A)^{-}A^{*}A=A^{*}A(A^{*}A)^{=}A^{*}A
applying the
*
-cancellation. In particular,
A(A^{*}A)^{-}A^{*}=A\left((A^{*}A)^{-}\right)^{*}A^{*}
, and the symmetry of
A(A^{*}A)^{-}A^{*}
follows.

(1a)\Rightarrow(1c)
. Let
X\in A\{1,3\}
. Then
A^{*}=A^{*}AX
, and hence
\mathscr{R}(A^{*})\subseteq\mathscr{R}(A^{*}A)
.

(1c)\Rightarrow(1b)
. If
\mathscr{R}(A^{*})=\mathscr{R}(A^{*}A)
, then there exists a matrix
X
such that
A=XA^{*}A
. Given a full rank factorization
A=FG
, set
F^{+},G^{+}
such that
F^{+}F=I,GG^{+}=I
. The identity

becomes

Multiplying on the left by
F^{+}
and on the right by
G^{+}
yields

I=(F^{+}XG^{*})(F^{*}F),

so

is invertible.

The equivalence
(1c)\Leftrightarrow(1d)
is immediate from the rank identities.
∎

The notion of
core inverse
was introduced by Baksalary and Trenkler in

as a refinement of the group inverse.
For a square matrix

denotes the orthogonal projector onto

A matrix

The dual concept, the

where

We now introduce the notion of
core EP invertibility
, also known as pseudo-core invertibility, as presented in

A
is
said to be core EP invertible if there exists a matrix solution

(i)

\displaystyle\;XA^{m+1}=A^{m},\;\text{for some nonnegative integer }m,

(ii)

\displaystyle\;AX^{2}=X,

(iii)

\displaystyle\;(AX)^{*}=AX.

In case a solution exists, it is unique and is called the core EP inverse of

, denoted by
A^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}

Dually,

is said to be dual core EP invertible if there is a matrix

such that

(i’)

\displaystyle\;A^{m+1}X=A^{m},\;\text{for some nonnegative integer }m,

(ii’)

\displaystyle\;X^{2}A=X,

(iii’)

\displaystyle\;(XA)^{*}=XA.

in which case it is unique and is called the dual core EP inverse of
A
, denoted by
A_{{}_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}}

The notion of a composite generalized inverse obtained by successively

Let
A

This system is consistent whenever

A^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}

and its unique solution is given explicitly by

(2.9)

A^{\dagger,\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}\;=\;A^{\dagger}\,A\,A^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}},

which is called the

of

Assume now that the dual form of the core EP inverse,
A_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}
, exists, as well as the Moore–Penrose inverse
A^{\dagger}
. The
{}^{\ast}\!
core EP–Moore–Penrose inverse
of
A
is the unique
matrix
X
satisfying

XAX=X,\qquad AX=AA_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}AA^{\dagger},\qquad XA=A_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}A.

Whenever
A^{\dagger}
and
A_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}
exist, the system is consistent
and its unique solution is

(2.10)

A_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}},\dagger}\;=\;A_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}\,A\,A^{\dagger},

which is called the
{}^{\ast}\!
CEPMP inverse
of
A
.

By analogy with the MPCEP and
{}^{\ast}\!
CEPMP inverses, other composite generalized
inverses can be formed by combining the Moore–Penrose inverse with the core EP
inverse or with the dual core EP inverse. The
generalized dual core
(GDC) inverse
and the
generalized core
(GC) inverse were introduced in

Mosic2024
. Over a general involutive field, the existence of each of these composite inverses requires the simultaneous existence of the corresponding core-type inverse
and of the Moore–Penrose inverse.

Let
A
be a square matrix and assume that the core EP inverse

A^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}
and the Moore–Penrose inverse
A^{\dagger}
exist.
The
generalized dual core inverse
of
A
, denoted by
A^{\mathrm{GDC}}
, is the unique matrix

X
satisfying

XAX=X,\qquad AX=A^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}A,\qquad XA=A^{\dagger}A^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}A^{2}.

Under these hypotheses this system is consistent, and its
unique solution is given explicitly by

(2.11)

A^{\mathrm{GDC}}\;=\;A^{\dagger}\,A^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}\,A.

Assume now that
A
is such that the dual core EP inverse

A_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}
and the Moore–Penrose inverse
A^{\dagger}
exist.
The
generalized core inverse
of
A
, denoted by
A^{\mathrm{GC}}
, is the unique matrix

X
satisfying

XAX=X,\qquad AX=A^{2}A_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}A^{\dagger},\qquad XA=AA_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}.

Whenever these conditions hold, this system is consistent,
and its unique solution is

(2.12)

A^{\mathrm{GC}}\;=\;A\,A_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}\,A^{\dagger}.

3.
Core and dual core invertibility

A matrix admits a core inverse and a dual core inverse if and only if it admits
both a Moore–Penrose inverse and a group inverse. For matrices associated with double star digraphs, of the form (
2.1
), we have seen that this is equivalent to requiring that all Gram scalars
s,u,t,v
and both coupling terms
x^{T}y
and
z^{T}w
are
nonzero. Under these assumptions, the explicit formulas for
M^{\dagger}
and
M^{\sharp}

given in Lemma
2.1
and in (
2.3
) may be applied directly in the computation of
M^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.6pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\textcircled{\scriptsize$\sharp$}}}}}}
and
M_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.6pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\textcircled{\scriptsize$\sharp$}}}}}}
. The orthogonal projectors
M^{\dagger}M
and
MM^{\dagger}
take the explicit block forms

(3.1)

M^{\dagger}M=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&0&0&0\\[2.0pt]
0&s^{-1}\,\bar{x}x^{T}&0&0\\[2.0pt]
0&0&1&0\\[2.0pt]
0&0&0&t^{-1}\,\bar{z}z^{T}\end{array}\right],\qquad MM^{\dagger}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&0&0&0\\[2.0pt]
0&u^{-1}\,yy^{*}&0&0\\[2.0pt]
0&0&1&0\\[2.0pt]
0&0&0&v^{-1}\,ww^{*}\end{array}\right].

These identities are used in the computation of the core and dual core
inverses of
M
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a matrix with associated double star digraph
S_{(m+1),(n+1)}
of the form (
2.1
), with
x=[x_{i}],y=[y_{i}],z=[z_{i}]
and
w=[w_{i}]

,
z^{T}w\neq 0
and
s,u,t,v\in\mathbb{F}\setminus\{0\}
, where

. Then,

(1)

M
is core invertible and

M^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.6pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\textcircled{\scriptsize$\sharp$}}}}}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&u^{-1}y^{\ast}&0&0\\[2.0pt]
(x^{T}y)^{-1}y&0&0&-av^{-1}(x^{T}y)^{-1}yw^{\ast}\\[2.0pt]
\hline\cr 0&0&0&v^{-1}w^{\ast}\\[2.0pt]
0&-bu^{-1}(z^{T}w)^{-1}wy^{\ast}&(z^{T}w)^{-1}w&0\end{array}\right];

(2)

is dual core invertible and

M_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.6pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\textcircled{\scriptsize$\sharp$}}}}}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&(x^{T}y)^{-1}x^{T}&0&0\\[2.0pt]
s^{-1}\overline{x}&0&0&-as^{-1}(z^{T}w)^{-1}\overline{x}z^{T}\\[2.0pt]
\hline\cr 0&0&0&(z^{T}w)^{-1}z^{T}\\[2.0pt]
0&-bt^{-1}(x^{T}y)^{-1}\overline{z}x^{T}&t^{-1}\overline{z}&0\end{array}\right].

This section completes the analysis of core and dual core invertibility for
matrices associated with double star digraphs. In this setting, core-type
invertibility is entirely determined by the simultaneous existence of the
Moore–Penrose inverse and the group inverse. In the next section we turn to matrices of higher index, for which the group inverse no longer exists and core EP-type inverses provide the appropriate framework.

We now construct explicit von Neumann inverses for

factorizations

\overline{a}\overline{z}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta&az^{T}\end{array}\right]=F_{A}G_{A},\qquad B=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\overline{a}\\
\overline{x}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&x\end{array}\right]=F_{B}G_{B}.

Choosing

F_{A}^{-}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\overline{\zeta}^{-1}&0\end{array}\right],\quad G_{A}^{-}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\zeta^{-1}\\
0\end{array}\right],\quad F_{B}^{-}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\overline{a}^{-1}&0\end{array}\right],\quad\text{and}\quad G_{B}^{-}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a^{-1}\\
0\end{array}\right],

we obtain
F_{A}^{-}F_{A}=I
,
G_{A}G_{A}^{-}=I
and similarly for

A^{-}=G_{A}^{-}F_{A}^{-}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(\zeta\overline{\zeta})^{-1}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right],\qquad B^{-}=G_{B}^{-}F_{B}^{-}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(a\overline{a})^{-1}&0\\

Hence,

\left[(M^{3})^{*}M^{3}\right]^{{}^{-}}(M^{3})^{*}\underset{\text{perm.}}{\approx}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}h^{-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(\zeta\overline{\zeta})^{-1}&0\\

0&\beta^{-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(a\overline{a})^{-1}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right]\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&0&\bar{\zeta}\bar{b}&\bar{\zeta}y^{*}\\
0&0&\overline{a}\overline{b}\overline{z}&\bar{a}\bar{z}y^{*}\\
\hline\cr\bar{a}\overline{\zeta}&\overline{a}\overline{b}w^{*}&0&0\\
\overline{\zeta}\overline{x}&\overline{b}\overline{x}w^{*}&0&0\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&(\zeta h)^{-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\bar{b}&y^{*}\\
0&0\end{array}\right]\\
(a\beta)^{-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\overline{\zeta}&\overline{b}w^{*}\\
0&0\end{array}\right]&0\end{array}\right],

which implies that

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&(\zeta h)^{-1}y^{*}&(\zeta h)^{-1}\bar{b}&0\\
0&0&0&0\\
(a\beta)^{-1}\overline{\zeta}&0&0&(a\beta)^{-1}\overline{b}w^{*}\\
0&0&0&0\end{array}\right]\in M^{3}\{1,3\}.

We may now compute the core EP inverse of

. By Lemma
2.2
, since

i(M)=3
,
M^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}=M^{D}M^{3}(M^{3})^{(1,3)}
. Using the explicit expression of

given in (

) and the explicit expressions of
M^{3}
and
(M^{3})^{(1,3)}
, when

x^{T}y\neq 0
,
z^{T}w=0
,
h\neq 0
and
\beta\neq 0
, a direct block multiplication yields

\displaystyle M^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&h^{-1}y^{*}&h^{-1}\bar{b}&0\\
\beta^{-1}\overline{\zeta}y&0&0&\beta^{-1}\bar{b}yw^{*}\\
\beta^{-1}\overline{\zeta}b&0&0&\beta^{-1}b\bar{b}w^{*}\\
0&bh^{-1}\zeta^{-1}wy^{*}&b\bar{b}h^{-1}\zeta^{-1}w&0\end{array}\right].

We now follow the same strategy to determine the
dual core EP inverse
of

.
In this case, we focus on the set
M^{3}\{1,4\}
. To this end, we first compute the product
M^{3}(M^{3})^{*}
. Using the explicit expressions of
M^{3}
and
(M^{3})^{*}
obtained above, we find

\displaystyle M^{3}(M^{3})^{*}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\zeta\overline{\zeta}q&0&0&\zeta\overline{b}qw^{*}\\

now shows that the existence of a
(1,4)

0\end{array}\right],\quad F_{D}^{-}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}b^{-1}&0\end{array}\right]\quad\text{and}\quad G_{D}^{-}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\overline{b}^{-1}\\
0\end{array}\right],

we obtain
F_{C}^{-}F_{C}=I
,
G_{C}G_{C}^{-}=I
, and analogously for
D
, which yields

C^{-}=G_{C}^{-}F_{C}^{-}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(\zeta\overline{\zeta})^{-1}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right],\qquad D^{-}=G_{D}^{-}F_{D}^{-}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(b\overline{b})^{-1}&0\\

Consequently,

\displaystyle(M^{3})^{*}\left[M^{3}(M^{3})^{*}\right]^{{}^{-}}

\displaystyle\underset{\text{perm.}}{\approx}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&0&\overline{\zeta}\bar{b}&\overline{\zeta}y^{*}\\

and we can assert

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&0&(\alpha b)^{-1}\overline{\zeta}&0\\
(\zeta q)^{-1}\overline{x}&0&0&0\\
\hline\cr(\zeta q)^{-1}\overline{a}&0&0&0\\
0&0&(\alpha b)^{-1}\bar{a}\overline{z}&0\end{array}\right]\in M^{3}\{1,4\}.

We are now in position to compute the dual core EP inverse of
M
.
By Lemma
2.2
, since
i(M)=3
, the dual core EP inverse of
M
is given by
M_{{}_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}}=(M^{3})^{(1,4)}M^{3}M^{D}

,

,

\displaystyle M_{{}_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&\overline{\zeta}\alpha^{-1}x^{T}&\overline{\zeta}\alpha^{-1}a&0\\
q^{-1}\overline{x}&0&0&(q\zeta)^{-1}a\overline{x}z^{T}\\
\hline\cr\overline{a}q^{-1}&0&0&(q\zeta)^{-1}a\overline{a}z^{T}\\

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
M
be a matrix over a field with associated double star digraph
S_{(m+1),(n+1)}
of the form (
2.1
) with
x^{T}y\neq 0
,
z^{T}w=0
and
\zeta=x^{T}y+ab\neq 0
.
Then

(1)

M is core EP invertible if and only if

h=b\overline{b}+y^{*}y\neq 0\text{ and }\beta=\zeta\overline{\zeta}+b\overline{b}w^{*}w\neq 0.

In this case

M^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&h^{-1}y^{*}&h^{-1}\bar{b}&0\\
\beta^{-1}\overline{\zeta}y&0&0&\beta^{-1}\bar{b}yw^{*}\\
\hline\cr\beta^{-1}\overline{\zeta}b&0&0&\beta^{-1}b\bar{b}w^{*}\\
0&bh^{-1}\zeta^{-1}wy^{*}&b\bar{b}h^{-1}\zeta^{-1}w&0\end{array}\right]

(2)

M is dual core EP invertible if and only if

q=a\overline{a}+x^{*}x\neq 0\text{ and }\alpha=\zeta\overline{\zeta}+a\overline{a}z^{*}z\neq 0.

In this case,

M_{{}_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&\overline{\zeta}\alpha^{-1}x^{T}&\overline{\zeta}\alpha^{-1}a&0\\
q^{-1}\overline{x}&0&0&(q\zeta)^{-1}a\overline{x}z^{T}\\
\hline\cr\overline{a}q^{-1}&0&0&(q\zeta)^{-1}a\overline{a}z^{T}\\
0&\bar{a}\alpha^{-1}\overline{z}x^{T}&a\overline{a}\alpha^{-1}\overline{z}&0\end{array}\right].

We finally consider Case III, characterized by the conditions
x^{T}y\neq 0=z^{T}w
and
\zeta=x^{T}y+ab=0
. In this situation, it follows from (
2.6
) that
i(M)=5
and
M^{D}=0
. Consequently, the matrix
M
is nilpotent of index

. Since
M^{5}=0
, we have
\operatorname{rank}(M^{5})=\operatorname{rank}\bigl((M^{5})^{*}M^{5}\bigr)=\operatorname{rank}\bigl(M^{5}(M^{5})^{*}\bigr)=0
.
By Proposition
2.1
, this implies that

(M^{5})\{1,3\}\neq\varnothing
and
(M^{5})\{1,4\}\neq\varnothing
. We may therefore apply Lemma
2.2
. The matrix
M
is both core EP invertible and dual core EP invertible. Moreover, in both cases the corresponding inverse is the zero matrix. This completes the analysis of Case III and, consequently, the proof of the results
of this section.

5.
MPCEP and
{}^{\ast}\!
CEPMP Inverses

In this section we derive explicit formulas for the Moore–Penrose–core EP (MPCEP) inverse
and the
{}^{\ast}\!
core EP–Moore–Penrose (
{}^{\ast}\!
CEPMP) inverse for matrices associated with
double star digraphs.

Since the existence of MPCEP and
{}^{\ast}\!
CEPMP inverses requires, respectively,
the existence of the Moore–Penrose inverse together with core–EP or dual core–EP inverse, the analysis splits according to the three structural cases introduced in Section
2
.

Recall that whenever
M^{\dagger}
and
M^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}
exist, the MPCEP inverse of

2.9

and

CEPMP inverse of

is given by (
2.10
).

The proofs of the following lemmas are immediate consequences of these identities, combined with the explicit
expressions of

or

from (
3.1
) and of

M^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}

and

.

Let
M
be a matrix with associated double star digraph

of the form (

) with

exists and that

is core EP
invertible (equivalently, the scalars

defined in (

) are nonzero).

is given by

\hline\cr\overline{a}r^{-1}&0&0&r^{-1}w^{*}\\

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
M
be a matrix with associated double star digraph
S_{(m+1),(n+1)}
of the form (

) satisfying
x^{T}y\neq 0
,

.

M

) with

CEPMP inverse of

M_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}},\dagger}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&0&\overline{b}p^{-1}&0\\

.

6.
GDC and GC Inverses

In this section we derive explicit block formulas for the generalized dual core (GDC)
inverse and the generalized core (GC) inverse of matrices associated with double star
digraphs.

These inverses are not independent notions: they are composite outer inverses obtained
by combining the Moore–Penrose inverse with the (dual) core EP inverse, as introduced
in
Mosic2024
. Consequently, their existence does not introduce new algebraic
constraints beyond those already required for Moore–Penrose and core EP
invertibility.

The proofs of the following lemmas are immediate consequences of
(
2.11
) and (
2.12
), combined with the explicit
formulas for
M^{\dagger}
from Lemma
2.1
and for

M^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}
or
M_{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}
from Theorems
4.1

and
4.2
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
M
be a matrix with associated double star digraph
S_{(m+1),(n+1)}
of the form (
2.1
) with
x^{T}y=z^{T}w=0
. Assume that
M
is Moore–Penrose and core EP
invertible (equivalently, the scalars
s,u,t,v,r,h
defined in (
2.2
) are nonzero).
Then the GDC inverse of
M
is given by

M^{GDC}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&\overline{a}(br)^{-1}x^{T}&b^{-1}&0\\

\hline\cr a^{-1}&0&0&\overline{b}(ah)^{-1}z^{T}\\

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
M
be a matrix with associated double star digraph
S_{(m+1),(n+1)}
of the form (
2.1
) satisfying
x^{T}y\neq 0
,
z^{T}w=0
and
\zeta=x^{T}y+ab\neq 0
. Assume that
M
is Moore–Penrose invertible and
M^{\mathrel{{}^{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{\textcircled{\scriptsize d}}}}}}

defined in (
2.2
) are nonzero). Then the GDC inverse of
M

M^{GDC}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&\beta^{-1}\overline{\zeta}x^{T}&\beta^{-1}(a\overline{\zeta}+\overline{b}w^{\ast}w)&0\\

.

Let
M
be a matrix with associated double star digraph

. Assume that

exists and that

.

Let
M
be a matrix with associated double star digraph
S_{(m+1),(n+1)}
of the form (
2.1
) with
x^{T}y\neq 0
,
z^{T}w=0
and

. Assume that

and
