Foreword
The annotated bibliographies provided in this document focus on identifying and reviewing
environmental impact statements (EIS), environmental assessments (EA), and scientific literature
relevant to aspects of CO2 pipeline and storage construction and operation. The purpose of these
summaries is to aid stakeholders responsible for the preparation of documents compliant with the
National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) requirements to have access to a quick and
comprehensive guide of the literature that can inform their activities. They aim to inform the
development of EISs and EAs by identifying potential environmental concerns and mitigation
strategies. The annotated bibliographies captured in this document cover the general
environmental assessment and impact topics relevant to CO2 pipeline and storage construction
and operation activities, with the subject of waste creation and handling being specifically pulled
out into its own section. The reasoning behind a dedicated section for waste is that many EAs
and EISs focus on the description of waste and its impacts, be it caused in routine operation or as
a result of an accident.
Below is a summary of the methodology and criteria behind selecting literature.

 1. NEPA Related Document Search Terms:
 ▪ This document outlines the search process for identifying Environmental Assessments
 (EAs) and Environmental Impact Statements (EISs) related to CO 2 projects under
 NEPA. The search utilized the SearchNEPA/Policy AI tool developed by PNNL1,
 targeting terms like CO2 pipelines, underground CO2 storage, CO2 sequestration, and
 CO2 transport.
 ▪ Relevant NEPA documents were sourced from agencies such as the Bureau of Land
 Management (BLM), Bureau of Ocean Energy Management (BOEM), Department of
 Energy (DOE), Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC), and Tennessee
 Valley Authority (TVA).
 ▪ The National Energy Technology Laboratory (NETL) Library was also searched for
 documents addressing CO2-related projects.
 ▪ The identified documents were reviewed for environmental impacts related to the
 construction and operation of CO2 pipelines, injection wells, monitoring wells, and
 underground storage/sequestration.
 ▪ Offshore CO2 pipelines and storage were excluded from the evaluation.

 2. Scientific Article Search for CO2 Transport and Storage:

1
 As of this writing, the SearchNEPA Tool is in its final stages of development and testing and is not yet
available to the general public.

 1

 ▪ Searches were conducted using web browsers and Argonne Library search engines,
 with terms related to CO2 pipeline construction impacts and environmental impacts
 across various domains (e.g., air quality, ecological, human health).
 ▪ For storage-related impacts, emphasis was placed on subsurface CO 2 storage,
 injection technologies, and impacts on aquifers and formations.
 ▪ Recent publications and reference citations were recursively used to identify
 additional articles, focusing on environmental impacts such as air quality, water
 resources, and socioeconomics.

3. Waste Related Document Search Terms:
 ▪ Searches involved web browsers, Argonne Library search engines, US EPA Office of
 Solid Waste information sources, and DOT information sources.
 ▪ Search terms included significant CCS projects (e.g., Fidelis Mountaineer Giga
 System, Sleipner Project) and treatment technologies for carbon capture (e.g., amine-
 related waste treatment techniques).
 ▪ The search also considered pipeline construction, leaks, subsurface storage impacts,
 and the use of CCS for enhanced oil recovery (EOR) as a surrogate for environmental
 impact assessment.
