Tetraphenylethylene-Based Wholly Aromatic Polyimides with Enhanced Photoluminescence Efficiency
Authors: Xingfeng Lei, Tianhao Huang, Yang Liu, Qinyu Yu, Huajie Min, Cheng Li, Qiuyu Zhang, Zhaohui Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Wholly aromatic polyimides (PIs) are promising candidates for next-generation optoelectronic materials owing to their excellent comprehensive properties. However, the strong charge-transfer (CT) interactions present in both solution and solid states typically result in poor photoluminescence (PL) efficiency, posing a significant limitation for conventional wholly aromatic PIs for optoelectronic applications. Tetraphenylethylene (TPE), a well-established fluorophore, is well known for its straightforward synthesis, versatile functionalization capability, and high fluorescence quantum yield (Φ PL) even when decorated with various substituents. Thus, incorporating TPE fluorophore into the PI backbone presents a promising strategy to develop photoluminescent PIs and overcome the inherent drawbacks of traditional conjugated polymers. Herein, TPE-diamines with trans- and cis-configurations were synthesized and subsequently polymerized with 6FDA or BPADA dianhydrides to introduce TPE into the PI main chains (MC-TPE-PIs). However, the resulting MC-TPE-PIs exhibited negligible Φ PL, failing to deliver the anticipated emission properties. To address this limitation, a carboxyl-functionalized TPE derivative (TPE-COOH) was prepared and grafted as a side-chain substituent onto the PI backbone via esterification (SC-TPE-PIs). By tuning the flexibility and electronic effects of the dianhydrides, a series of SC-TPE-PI thin films with varying TPE contents were obtained. Among them, the highest Φ PL reached 13.7%, with a tensile strength of 83.3 MPa, a tensile modulus of 1.78 GPa, a glass transition temperature of 203.1 °C, and a thermal decomposition temperature of 377 °C at 5% weight loss, demonstrating their potential as luminescent polymers for optoelectronic applications.

Introduction

Wholly aromatic polyimides (PIs), a class of special engineering plastics with superior film-forming ability, are well known for their excellent mechanical strength, outstanding heat resistance, good flexibility, and prominent chemical inertness. − These inherent merits have led to their widespread application in aerospace, , microelectronics, , and optoelectronic industries. , Benefiting from such advantages, wholly aromatic PIs are considered promising candidates for next-generation optoelectronic materials. − However, due to strong intermolecular and intramolecular charge transfer (CT) interactions, as well as extensive π-π stacking, most wholly aromatic PIs display yellow to dark yellow coloration, ,,− resulting in poor or even negligible photoluminescence (PL) efficiency, severely impeding their application in polymer-based light-emitting diodes (PLEDs). ,− Therefore, current efforts in engineering luminescent PIs have primarily focused on the suppression of CT effect, either by introducing an alicyclic moiety − or using elaborately designed imide monomers possessing highly twisted configuration or significant steric hindrance. , These strategies have led to substantially enhanced PL efficiency (Φ PL: 60%–89%) while maintaining superior thermal properties (e.g., glass transition temperature as high as 470 °C). ,, Alternatively, directly introducing fluorophores into the PI backbone or tethering them as pendant groups is a much simpler and cost-effective strategy to improve PL efficiency, and impressive achievements have been obtained in Kang’s investigations. ,

Among the reported fluorophores, tetraphenylethylene (TPE) has been most extensively studied owing to its structural simplicity, ease of synthesis, and ready functionalization. − As a highly efficient fluorophore, TPE emits blue fluorescence with an impressive Φ PL in its powdery aggregation state. − Even after derivatization with various substituents, TPE derivatives retain strong luminescence capabilities, making them widely employed in the preparation of polymers with high PL efficiency. − As a consequence, incorporating TPE units into the PI backbone has emerged as a promising strategy to address the weak PL efficiency of PIs. , For instance, Zhang and co-workers synthesized two aggregation-induced emission (AIE)-active luminophores, each bearing two amino groups at the meta- and para-positions of the benzene ring. After polymerization with several dianhydrides, a notably high Φ PL of 89% has been obtainedbut only for the semi-aromatic PI. In contrast, the wholly aromatic PIs exhibited very limited Φ PL (2.9%–4.8%) or were even completely non-luminescent. These results clearly indicate that even highly fluorescent AIEgen incorporation fails to enhance emission efficiency in wholly aromatic PI systems.

Inspired by these findings, we set out to investigate whether covalently integrating fluorophoresspecifically tetraphenylethylene (TPE) unitsinto wholly aromatic PI backbones could enhance their PL efficiency without compromising their outstanding physical properties. Despite notable advances in luminescent PIs, ,,, reports on wholly aromatic TPE-based photoluminescent PIs with tunable Φ PL remain relatively scarce. In this work, we directly introduce amino groups onto the two benzene rings of TPE via McMurry coupling reaction to synthesize a TPE-based diamine monomer, which is anticipated to polymerize with dianhydrides to form TPE-based wholly aromatic PIs. Owing to the central CC bond, the resulting TPE-diamine exists in two isomeric forms: trans- and cis-TPE-diamine (Scheme a). These two isomers have been successfully isolated, and they exhibit distinct Φ PL, polymerization reactivity, and solubility. To modulate the CT strength in the resulting PIs, two dianhydrides with totally different electronic effects were selected, namely, the electron-withdrawing 4,4’-(hexafluoroisopropylidene)­diphthalic anhydride (6FDA) and the electron-donating 4,4’-(4,4’-isopropylidenediphenoxy)­bis­(phthalic anhydride) (BPADA) (Scheme ). Unfortunately, regardless of which TPE-diamine isomer (cis- or trans-) was polymerized with either 6FDA or BPADA, none of the resulting PIs exhibit desired PL efficiency (Figure S41, Supporting Information). To address this issue, another TPE derivative bearing a carboxyl group is further synthesized (Scheme b) and in this case, TPE fluorophore is attached as the side group to the PI main chains via the esterification grafting reaction (Scheme ). By tuning the TPE loading amount, a moderate Φ PL of ∼13.7% in the solid state (both film and powder) has been obtained in the wholly aromatic SC-TPE-BPPI-50. Moreover, this photoluminescent PI film displays a combination of good physical properties, with a tensile strength of 83.3 MPa, a tensile modulus of 1.78 GPa, a glass transition temperature of 203.1 °C, and a 5% thermal decomposition temperature of up to 377 °C. This investigation demonstrates that side-chain grafting is an efficacious strategy for incorporating TPE fluorophore into the wholly aromatic PI backbones, thereby enabling the development of photoluminescent PI films with both high Φ PL and satisfied overall physical performance.

In the current investigation, two series of TPE-based PIs have been engineered and constructed, and they demonstrate quite different fluorescence effects as well as distinct physical properties. In one type of TPE-based PIs, the TPE unit is bonded into the PI main chain (main-chain TPE polyimide, MC-TPE-PI) by directly polymerizing TPE-diamine ( trans -TPE-diamine or cis -TPE-diamine) with dianhydride (6FDA or BPADA). Accordingly, the resultant MC-TPE-PIs were respectively named as MC-trans-TPE-6FPI and MC-trans-TPE-BPPI, meaning that the PI is prepared by polymerizing trans-TPE-diamine with 6FDA or BPADA, while MC-cis-TPE-6FPI and MC-cis-TPE-BPPI imply that the PI is prepared by polymerizing cis-TPE-diamine with 6FDA or BPADA (Scheme ).

In another type of TPE-based PIs, the TPE unit serves as a pendant substituent attached to the PI main chain, yielding a series of side-chain TPE-based PIs (denoted as SC-TPE-PIs). SC-TPE-PIs were prepared by grafting acyl-chlorinated TPE (TPE-COCl) onto PI main chains via an esterification reaction (Scheme ). The hydroxyl content (determining the final TPE amount) of the resulting PIs is adjusted by varying the mole fraction (25%, 35%, and 50%) of the hydroxyl-containing diamine, 2,2-bis­(3-amino-4-hydroxyphenyl)­hexafluoropropane (APAF), in total diamines. The prepared hydroxyl-functionalized precursor PIs were respectively named as 6FPI-25, 6FPI-35, and 6FPI-50, meaning that the PIs were derived from 6FDA dianhydride and the APAF mole fraction accounts for 25%, 35%, and 50% in total diamines. Likewise, BPADA-derived precursors were termed in a similar fashion as BPPI-25, BPPI-35, and BPPI-50. After the esterification grafting reaction, the final SC-TPE-PIs were prepared and termed according to the dianhydride and APAF proportion (25%, 35%, and 50%) in the PI main chain, yielding six PIs named as SC-TPE-6FPI-25, SC-TPE-6FPI-35, SC-TPE-6FPI-50, SC-TPE-BPPI-25, SC-TPE-BPPI-35 and SC-TPE-BPPI-50.

Results and Discussion

The molecular structures of trans -TPE-diamine, cis -TPE-diamine and TPE-COOH are fully analyzed by using FT-IR, 1H-NMR, 13C-NMR and ESI-MS. The results are provided in Figure and Figures S1–S12 (Supporting Information). The absorption bands at ca. 3300–3500 cm–1 and 1620 cm–1 in FT-IR spectra (Figure a) are respectively attributed to the primary amino group and the carbon–carbon double bond in TPE-diamine. The vibrations assigned to the carboxyl group in TPE-COOH can be found at ca. 3300 cm–1 (O–H), 1737 cm–1 (asymmetric CO) and 1687 cm–1 (symmetric CO). The appearance of several small peaks at 2500–2700 cm–1 is also the evidence of the carboxyl group. The signal for carbon–carbon double bond in TPE-COOH appears at ca. 1602 cm–1 (Figure a, top). Additionally, the singlet resonating at ∼5.0 ppm (Ha) is ascribed to the amino proton in TPE-diamine (Figure b, middle and bottom), and the chemical shift locating at 12.75 ppm (Ha) results from carboxyl group in TPE-COOH (Figure b, top). The other proton signals as well as the carbon peaks in 1H- and 13C-NMR spectra (Figures S2–S3, S5–S6, S10–S11, Supporting Information) can also be well assigned. The results from ESI-MS agree well with the theoretical values for all TPE monomers (Figures S4, S7, S12, Supporting Information). All these results indicate that the as-synthesized TPE derivatives have the designed molecular structures.

The optical properties of the as-synthesized TPE-derived functional monomers have been studied using UV-vis absorption spectra, PL spectra, and time-resolved PL (TR-PL), and the results are tabulated in Table and shown in Figure a and Figure S22 (Supporting Information). In dilute THF solution, the three TPE-derived monomers exhibit two intense absorption bands. For trans -TPE-diamine, the bands center at 265 and 350 nm, while for cis -TPE-diamine, they appear at 278 and 342 nm. These bands are attributed to the π-π* transition of the TPE core and the n-π* transition between the benzene ring and amino group. In contrast, TPE-COOH shows an obvious blue shift in its UV absorption bands ascribed to the electron-withdrawing nature of the carboxyl group, with maxima at 238 and 320 nm, which results from the π-π* transition of the TPE core and the n-π* transition between the benzene ring and carboxyl group. Upon excitation at the maximum absorption wavelength (λ max abs), trans -TPE-diamine reveals a bathochromic emission peak of ∼20 nm and over two times higher Φ PL of 37.2% in comparison with cis -TPE-diamine (16.0%) in the powdery state, suggesting markedly different fluorescence behavior in both TPE-diamines and, trans-configuration favors suppression of nonradiative transitions in comparison with cis-configuration, which is evidenced by the lower nonradiative rate constant (k nr) and much higher radiative rate constant (k r) of trans -TPE-diamine relative to cis -TPE-diamine summarized in Table . To further explore how structural differences influence the distinct PL efficiency of these two diamines, we employed time-dependent density functional theory (TD-DFT) at the PBE0/def2-SVP level to elucidate their excited-state (S i ) transition behaviors. The calculation results are illustrated in Figure . The calculations clearly reveal that electrons are delocalized across the entire molecular framework of the TPE-based diamines (Figure a), which facilitates the formation of the local excited (LE) state (π-π*) upon transition from the ground state (S0) to the first excited state (S1). Notably, the substantial overlap between the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels, together with the large oscillator strengths (f) of 0.3798 and 0.3538 for trans - and cis -TPE-diamine, respectively (from S0→S1), indicates that the synthesized TPE-based diamines are promising fluorescent molecules. Moreover, trans -TPE-diamine exhibits superior fluorescence efficiency compared to cis -TPE-diamine, owing to its higher oscillator strength.

In contrast, when the primary amino group is replaced by a carboxyl group, the maximum emission wavelength (λ max em) of TPE-COOH undergoes a hypsochromic (blue) shift to 460 nm, most likely due to the electron-withdrawing effect of the carboxyl group. The Φ PL of TPE-COOH increases sharply to 84.1%, primarily attributable to the significant overlap between the HOMO and LUMO orbitals, which facilitates the LE transition from S0 to S1 with a high f of 0.3687 (Figure ). Moreover, strong intermolecular interactions (e.g., hydrogen bonding) between carboxyl groups tend to further restrict free rotation of the molecular structure, thereby reducing nonradiative transitions. This is consistent with the observed lower k nr and higher k r (Table ). Collectively, these factors endow TPE-COOH with high PL efficiency.

On the basis of the above results, it can be concluded that the type of the functional groups and the molecular configuration could exert significant influence on the optical properties and PL efficiency of TPE derivatives, and this may bring distinct fluorescence properties to the resulting PI films.

After the molecular structures and the optical properties of trans -TPE-diamine and cis -TPE-diamine have been analyzed, four MC-TPE-PIs have been constructed (Scheme ). However, possibly due to the evident steric hindrance of the TPE core, the polymerization reactivity of both trans -TPE-diamine and cis -TPE-diamine is not enough to furnish PIs with adequate molecular weight. As a consequence, a higher reactive 4,4’-oxydianiline diamine (ODA) in 20% mole ratio has to be involved in PI polymerization to promote chain growth. The resulting four MC-TPE-PIs have been fully analyzed using FT-IR,1H-NMR and 19F-NMR (Figures S30–S39, Supporting Information), which confirm the matched molecular structures depicted in Scheme and indicate complete imidization as well as high monomer conversion. The molecular weight (M w) and its distribution (M w/M n) of the resulting MC-TPE-PIs are within the range of 36–88 kg·mol–1 and 1.63–1.74 according to the GPC traces shown in Figure S40 and Table S2 (Supporting Information). It is interesting to note that PIs derived from trans -TPE-diamine have much higher M w than those derived from cis -TPE-diamine, no matter which dianhydride (6FDA or BPADA) is used. This suggests that trans -TPE-diamine has higher polymerization reactivity compared to cis -TPE-diamine. This is consistent with the slightly lower chemical shift of trans -TPE-diamine (4.98 ppm, Figure S2, Supporting Information) than that of cis -TPE-diamine (5.00 ppm, Figure S5, Supporting Information) shown in 1H-NMR spectra. However, such a subtle variation in the amino proton NMR chemical shift (∼0.02 ppm) is marginal and susceptible to solvent effects, making it likely insufficient to account for the higher polymerization reactivity (nucleophilicity) of trans -TPE-diamine relative to its cis-isomer. To gain further insight into this difference, we employed DFT methods to calculate the restrained electrostatic potential (RESP) of the nitrogen atoms in both trans - and cis -TPE-diamine. As shown in Figure S23 and Table S1 (Supporting Information), the RESP of the nitrogen atom in trans -TPE-diamine (−0.6815) is significantly lower (more negative) than that in cis -TPE-diamine (−0.6580), suggesting that the nitrogen atom in the trans-isomer is more likely to launch nucleophilic attacks and therefore exhibits higher reactivity. ,,

All MC-TPE-PIs are readily soluble in common organic solvents and can be cast into flexible and robust thin films as shown in Figure S29 (Supporting Information). The aggregation state of the four MC-TPE-PI films has been explored by wide-angle X-ray diffraction (WAXD). As shown in Figure b, all PI films exhibit broad diffraction halos, reflecting their amorphous nature. Table summarizes the physical properties of the resulting PI films. The inclusion of thermally labile methyl groups and flexible ether linkages in BPADA leads to lower 5% thermal decomposition temperature (T d5%) and glass transition temperature (T g) as well as a declined char yield for MC-TPE-BPPIs compared to MC-TPE-6FPIs. Nevertheless, all MC-TPE-PIs have T d5% close to or exceeding 500 °C and T g above 240 °C, indicating decent thermal stability (Figure c, d and Table ). Benefiting from the higher polymerization reactivity of trans -TPE-diamine and the flexibility of BPADA, MC- trans -TPE-BPPI displays desirable mechanical properties with tensile strength of 78.8 MPa, tensile modulus of 1.76 GPa, and elongation at break up to 33.7% (Figure e and Table ). It is noticeable that cis -TPE-diamine-derived PIs exhibit lowered tensile strength and elongation at break but much higher tensile modulus in comparison with trans -TPE-diamine-derived ones, indicating that cis -TPE-diamine is beneficial for enhancing the chain rigidity (Table ).

Figure f shows the UV-vis absorption spectra of the four MC-TPE-PIs in dilute solution. The absorption spectra exhibit an absorption peak centering at ∼250 nm ascribed to the benzene rings (π-π*) of the polymer chain. A shoulder peak appears at around 325 nm, arising from the absorption of the TPE core. The fluorescence properties of the four MC-TPE-PI films are initially studied by using photoluminescence emission spectra. Unexpectedly, the Φ PL of the four MC-TPE-PI films is fairly low, and they are almost nonluminescent upon UV lamp (365 nm) exposure (Figure S41, Supporting Information). Additionally, the photoluminescence behavior still cannot be observed no matter when MC- trans -TPE-6FPI is in a well-dissolved state (in pure THF) or in the mixed THF/H2O solution (water volume fraction is 95%) when excitated under the corresponding optimum excitation wavelength. These results suggest that the prepared MC-TPE-PIs are nonluminescent in both solution and solid states. This phenomenon, however, seemingly stands in contrast to the classic AIE theory, because embedding TPE moiety into a rigid PI backbone should restrict intramolecular rotation (RIM), which theoretically suppresses nonradiative decay and enhances emission. − Generally, wholly aromatic PIs suffer from poor photoluminescence (PL) efficiency and weak fluorescence quantum yields (Φ PL), primarily due to strong intermolecular and intramolecular CT interactions that induce marked quenching rather than intense emission. ,,, To verify whether the CT effect is responsible for this anomalous nonluminescent behavior in the MC-TPE-PI system, we synthesized and characterized two diimide model compounds based on TPE-diamines, namely cis -M-2 and trans -M-3 (Scheme S1, Figures S13–S15, Supporting Information), and investigated their optical properties via UV-vis spectroscopy (Figure S24, Supporting Information). As indicated, both TPE-diamines display obvious absorption, respectively, in the range of 250–300 nm and 320–400 nm, while the two model compounds exhibit a pronounced UV absorption peak locating at approximately 318–320 nm in THF solution, which generally results from the hybridized local and charge-transfer (HLCT) effects. These involve two primary transitions: a locally excited (LE) π-π* transition and a charge-transfer (CT) π-π* transition between the electron-accepting phthalic imide and the electron-donating amine moiety. We performed time-dependent density functional theory (TD-DFT) calculations at the PBE0/def2-SVP level to characterize the HOMO-LUMO energy levels and the excited-state (S i ) transition behaviors of cis -M-2 and trans -M-3. As illustrated in Figure S26a (Supporting Information), the weak overlap between HOMO and LUMO distributions in both cis -M-2 and trans -M-3 indicates limited LE (π-π*) transition, whereas a pronounced CT effect emerges upon excitation from the ground state (S0) to the first excited state (S1). This is accompanied by negligible oscillator strengths (f) of 0.00076 and 0.00077 for the cis -M-2 and trans -M-3 model compounds, respectively (for the S0→S1 transition), suggesting that the TPE fluorophore bonded into the PI main chains undergoes significant quenching rather than strong emission, consequently giving rise to weak photon emission capabilities. − The pronounced CT effect is consistent with the yellow coloration observed in the four MC-TPE-PI films (Figure S29, Supporting Information). However, we propose that the fluorescence quantum yield and visible-light transparency of PI films can be synergistically enhanced by co-incorporating alicyclic structures (e.g., cyclobutane, cyclohexane, and dimethylcyclobutane moieties) and highly efficient fluorophores (e.g., TPE, its derivatives, and triphenylamine, etc.) into the PI backbone. ,,

The above investigations indicate that, when the TPE fluorophore is incorporated into the PI main chains, it fails to achieve satisfactory PL efficiencywhether in a well-dissolved state or an aggregated statedue to a pronounced CT effect. In light of this, we propose that attaching TPE to the PI backbone as pendant side groups may yield more promising results. In such a configuration, the strong intermolecular and intramolecular CT effects are expected to be significantly suppressed, owing to the considerable steric hindrance introduced by the pendant TPE moieties and the electron-withdrawing nature of the ester linkages. Moreover, with TPE units positioned on the side chains, the integrity and propagation of the PI main chains remain unaffected, thereby enabling the fabrication of PI films that simultaneously achieve high Φ PL and excellent physical properties.

To investigate the feasibility and resultant PL efficiency achieved by incorporating TPE units into the PI side chains, a TPE-based bisimide model compound (M-1) was prepared (Scheme S2, Supporting Information), and its PL properties were investigated. The detailed molecular characterizations of M-1 are presented in Figures S18–S21 (Supporting Information). The HOMO-LUMO energy levels and the excited-state (S i ) transition behavior of M-1 molecule have been illustrated in Figure . As indicated, an evident spatial separation of the HOMO and LUMO distributions of M-1 model compound is clearly indicated accompanying with small HOMO-LUMO bandgaps of approximately 3.13 eV, which suggests limited LE (π-π*) transition and mainly leads to CT occurrence in the first excited state (S1) with negligible oscillator strengths (f) of 0.00019, whereas a pronounced CT effect emerges upon excitation from the ground state (S0) to the first excited state (S1). The small value of f originating from the limited overlap of HOMO-LUMO orbitals for M-1 model compound, hints that the 6FDA- and BPADA-derived PI polymers with TPE fluorophore placed in the PI side chains seemingly undergo significant quenching rather than strong emission. ,, However, the calculation results are obtained based on the isolated state of the M-1 molecule and therefore cannot reflect the actual fluorescence behavior in its aggregated state.

In fact, M-1 exhibits bright green emission in its aggregated state, as shown in the inset of Figure S18 (Supporting Information). To gain deeper insight, interaction region indicator (IRI) mapping and visual analysis of the chemical system’s intermolecular forces were performed using Visual Molecular Dynamics (VMD) software, based on wavefunction files generated by Multiwfn. As illustrated in Figure S28 (Supporting Information), the IRI map reveals pronounced van der Waals interactions within the M-1 molecule. These interactions serve to restrict both single-bond rotations and segmental mobilityeffectively aligning with the well-established restriction of intramolecular motion (RIM) mechanism pioneered by the Tang group. ,− By suppressing nonradiative decay pathways, this rigidified molecular environment preferentially opens radiative decay channels in the solid or aggregated state. Consequently, M-1 exhibits intense PL behavior and displays the AIE feature. As shown in Figure a, M-1 hardly exhibits fluorescence emission in pure THF (well dissolved) and at low water fractions (0–60%). However, when water (as poor solvent) volume fraction (f w) reaches 80%, the PL intensity steeply enhances, and it continues to rise as f w is further increased to 95%, indicative of a typical AIE effect. As a consequence, M-1 demonstrates a decent Φ PL (15.9%) in powdery state, with a PL lifetime (τ PL) being 5.48 ns (Table ). These results suggest that attaching TPE fluorophores as pendant groups to the PI main chains is a promising strategy for endowing the resulting PI systems with favorable PL efficiency.

Via an esterification grafting reaction between acyl chloride and hydroxyl groups, the TPE fluorophore has been successfully introduced into the PI backbone as pendant groups as depicted in Scheme . By varying TPE content (25%–50%) and dianhydride (6FDA or BPADA), six SC-TPE-PIs have been constructed. The PIs before and after grafting have been fully characterized using FT-IR, 1H-NMR, 19F-NMR, and UV-vis spectra (Figures S42–S77, Supporting Information). The spectroscopic results verify the precise polymer structures, and the disappearance of the signal for the hydroxyl group in 1H-NMR spectra confirms the high grafting efficiency (Figures S62, S65, S67, S70, S73, S76, Supporting Information). The M w and molecular weight distribution of the PIs before and after grafting are listed in Table S3 (Supporting Information) with the GPC traces shown in Figures S78 and S79 (Supporting Information). These data indicate adequate molecular weight, enabling the facile fabrication of robust films via solution casting (Figure S80, Supporting Information). WAXD diffraction patterns shown in Figure a indicate that the resulting six SC-TPE-PI films are amorphous in nature.

After the molecular structures of the resulting SC-TPE-PIs have been confirmed, we next explored their PL properties. As shown in Figure a and b, TPE-free BPPI-50 does not emit light in the film state. This is likely due to the strong CT effect within its polymer network, as evidenced by the TD-DFT calculation results on its model compound M–OH presented in Figure S27 (Supporting Information). In sharp contrast, once TPE fluorophore is introduced, SC-TPE-BPPI-50 emits bright green fluorescence in film state (Figure d) under 365 nm UV illumination, suggesting that the inclusion of TPE fluorophore as a side group endows the PI networks with remarkable PL efficiency. The fluorescence emission spectra of the resulting SC-TPE-PI films are given in Figure b. When excited at their optimal wavelength of approximately 330 nm (Figure b), BPADA-derived PIs exhibit maximum emission wavelengths (λ max em) in the range of 507–519 nm. For both BPADA- and 6FDA-derived PIs, λ max em shows a blue shift as the TPE loading increases, which may be attributed to distinct aggregation states of the polymers resulting from different TPE contents. It is interesting to note that SC-TPE-BPPI-25 and SC-TPE-BPPI-35 respectively exhibit a Φ PL of 7.6% and 8.1%, while when further increasing TPE loading amount to 50%, the Φ PL of SC-TPE-BPPI-50 achieves a modest value of 13.7% (Table ). This value is comparable to or slightly higher than those of a few wholly aromatic PIs reported to date, ,,, yet SC-TPE-BPPI-50 remains unsuitable for fabricating PLED/OLED devices due to its limited Φ PL, which arises from the pronounced CT interactions within the wholly aromatic PI backbone. By contrast, when PIs are made using 6FDA as dianhydride, the resulting SC-TPE-6FPI-25 and SC-TPE-6FPI-35 films exhibit an inferior Φ PL less than 1%. When the grafting amount reaches 50%, SC-TPE-6FPI-50 exhibits markedly enhanced green fluorescence upon UV (365 nm) exposure in comparison with the other two SC-TPE-6FPI films (Figures S80, S81, Supporting Information), but SC-TPE-6FPI-50 becomes quite brittle, and as a consequence, only film fragments could be obtained (Figure S81, Supporting Information). The Φ PL of 6FDA-derived PI films being less than that of BPADA-derived ones implies that the electron-withdrawing hexafluoroisopropyl group in 6FDA will enhance the intramolecular CT effect and therefore weakens the PL efficiency of PI films. ,,− The strengthened CT interaction in 6FDA-derived PIs could be visualized by the deeper film color shown in Figure S80 (Supporting Information). As shown in Figure c, the PL behavior of SC-TPE-BPPI-50 in THF/H2O mixed solvent has also been investigated. Similarly to M-1, SC-TPE-BPPI-50 exhibits nonemissive behavior in well-dissolved state and at low water fractions (10%). However, when f w reaches 40%, the PL intensity begins to increase and then rises markedly as f w continuously increases to 95%, with the maximum emission wavelength (λ max em) centering at ∼495 nm, suggestive of an AIE phenomenon. These results indicate that when TPE is tethered to the PI main chain as side groups, the resulting polymer, SC-TPE-BPPI-50, exhibits decent luminescent behavior, consistent with the typical AIE mechanism.

The fluorescence lifetime (τ PL) of the TPE-derived PIs in different aggregation states has been investigated as well (Table , Figure S82, Supporting Information). As shown in Table , it is clearly indicated that τ PL of 6FDA-derived SC-TPE-6FPIs is much longer than that of BPADA-derived ones. Furthermore, as compared in Figure d and Table , we can see that τ PL of powdery SC-TPE-BPPI-50 (6.21 ns) is 24.4% higher than that of its film (4.99 ns), which is comparable to or slightly higher than a few reported photoluminescent polymers. ,,, This indicates that the CT effect and the aggregation forms of polymers also have a certain effect on the fluorescence properties.

The tensile properties of the resulting SC-TPE-PI films are summarized in Figure f and Table . As the TPE loading increases, both tensile strength and modulus steadily decline, regardless of the dianhydride employed, due to the steric hindrance of the “drop-in” pendant TPE groups. These bulky side groups widen interchain spacing, suppress chain entanglement, and weaken intermolecular cohesion, thereby impairing mechanical strength and flexibility. Notably, for SC-TPE-6FPI-50 (50% TPE grafting with fluorinated 6FDA), the presence of fluorine atoms further diminishes interchain interactions, preventing the formation of an intact, free-standing film via solution casting (Figure S81, Supporting Information). In striking contrast, replacing 6FDA with a more flexible dianhydride containing polar oxygen-ether linkages (BPADA) yields SC-TPE-BPPI-50despite an identical TPE grafting ratioexhibiting markedly improved flexibility (elongation at break ∼7%), along with a tensile strength of 83.3 MPa and a tensile modulus of 1.78 GPa.

Thus, while side-chain grafting inevitably compromises interchain interactions, its detrimental impact on mechanical propertiesparticularly flexibilitycan be effectively mitigated by judiciously selecting a flexible, highly polar dianhydride that enhances both backbone flexibility and interchain interactions. This strategy holds promise for fabricating TPE-based PI films with adequate flexibility for practical applications.

For TGA analysis, the T d5% shows a decreasing trend with the increase of APAF (the hydroxyl-containing diamine) amount, regardless of whether the obtained PIs are grafted or which dianhydride is selected (Table , Figure c and Figure S83, Supporting Information). This decline is most likely ascribed to two negative effects arising from the incorporation of the less reactive APAF diamine: a reduction in molecular weight (Table S3, Supporting Information) and the introduction of thermally labile ester linkages formed during the grafting reaction.

Interestingly, such negative effects are not prominently reflected in T g of the resulting PI films, which only display a subtle variation among structure-analogous PI films when compared with the corresponding counterparts except SC-TPE-BPPI-35 (Table ). The almost unchanged T gs for both ungrafted and grafted PI films can be plausibly explained by the introduction of hydrogen-bonding interactions (between hydroxyl and imide carbonyl groups in ungrafted samples) and the increased steric hindrance from the TPE side groups (in grafted samples). Both factors largely compensate for the detrimental impact of the reduced molecular weight.

The presence of hydrogen bonding can be visualized by comparing the initial loss factor (tan δ) curves shown in Figure d and e. For ungrafted PI films, a distinct subglass transition (shoulder peak) emerges: between 175 and 225 °C for BPADA-derived PIs and between 200 and 280 °C for 6FDA-derived ones. The intensity of this shoulder increases roughly with hydroxyl content (Figure d).

However, once the hydroxyl groups are converted to ester linkages, the shoulder peaks mostly vanish for SC-TPE-BPPI-25 and SC-TPE-BPPI-50, while completely disappear for SC-TPE-BPPI-35 (Figure e). This signifies that the subglass transitions are most likely due to the relaxation of hydroxyl groups when hydrogen bonding interactions are disrupted at elevated temperatures. Notably, the T g of the SC-TPE-BPPI-35 film (223.3 °C) is counterintuitively higher than those of SC-TPE-BPPI-25 (206.3 °C) and SC-TPE-BPPI-50 (203.1 °C). This anomaly may be explained by the near-absence of hydroxyl groups within the SC-TPE-BPPI-35 network (the hydroxyl signal is barely detectable in its 1H-NMR spectrum, Figure S73, Supporting Information), while a small residual amount of hydroxyl remains in SC-TPE-BPPI-25 and SC-TPE-BPPI-50 (Figures S70 and S76, Supporting Information). Since both hydrogen bonding and steric hindrance of TPE side groups significantly restrict main-chain segmental motion, the complete conversion of hydroxyl groups in SC-TPE-BPPI-35 may enhance structural rigidity and account for its higher T g. Nevertheless, other contributing mechanisms remain unclear and are currently under investigation.

Conclusions

In summary, TPE-diamine with trans-configuration and cis-configuration and TPE-COOH have been synthesized, and two series of TPE-based PIs, namely MC-TPE-PIs and SC-TPE-PIs, have been constructed through direct polycondensation or esterification grafting reactions. They demonstrate quite different fluorescence effects as well as distinct physical properties. For MC-TPE-PIs, the TPE unit was bonded to the PI main chain, while for SC-TPE-PIs, TPE unit was attached as a pendant substituent to the PI main chain. Although the preparation of MC-TPE-PIs is simpler, the PL efficiency is not that impressive, whereas Φ PL for SC-TPE-PIs is as high as 13.7% when BPADA is selected as dianhydride and TPE amount reaches 50%. Furthermore, SC-TPE-BPPI-50 exhibits a combination of good physical properties, with tensile strength up to 83.3 MPa, tensile modulus of 1.78 GPa, elongation at break of 6.9%, T g of 203.1 °C, and T d5% exceeding 370 °C, making SC-TPE-PI more attractive in the field of optoelectronics. The strategy demonstrated herein opens a new pathway for designing and fabricating wholly aromatic photoluminescent PIs with balanced fluorescence properties and physical performance.
