Mechanistic Insights Into Dose-Dependent Alleviation of Colorectal Cancer Through Artemisinin-Loaded Mesoporous Silica Nanoparticles in 1,2-Dimethylhydrazine-Induced Albino Wistar Rats
Authors: Fatima Zahid, Umair Ilyas, Sara Zahid, Faisal Gulzar, Sampath Chinnam, Abdul Malik, Sara Aiman, Abdul Qayyum
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe current study investigates the suitability of encapsulating the Artemisinin-plant-originated lipophilic drug molecule into polyethylene glycol-coated mesoporous silica nanoparticles in a suitable dose regimen for the specific targeting of the drug in colorectal cancer.MethodologyMesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNPs) were synthesized through the sol-gel method, and Artemisinin was loaded. Then characterization of Artemisinin-loaded mesoporous silica nanoparticles coated with polyethylene glycol (MSN-PEG@Artemisinin) was performed by Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), Zeta analysis, Polydispersity index (PDI) and X-Ray diffraction (XRD) techniques and compared with the standard drug Gemcitabine. The in vivo analysis of 1,2-dimethylhydrazine (DMH) was used to induce colorectal tumors in the colon of inbred male Wistar rats. The treatment group of rats was administered MSN-PEG@Artemisinin through intraperitoneal injection. Hematoxylin and eosin staining were performed to the histopathological examination of tumors.ResultsThe average size of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin was 203.6 ± 64.78 nm with a zeta potential of -10.9mV. PDI was measured at 0.106. FTIR analysis also supported the successful loading of Artemisinin in mesoporous silica nanoparticles with PEG coating without showing interactions. The encapsulation efficiency (EE) and Drug loading (DL) percentages were 82.75% and 33.10%, respectively. XRD indicated a uniform mesoporous structure with a proper hexagonal symmetry. The in-vitro release was carried out in phosphate buffer with 7.4 pH following biphasic system with 26% drug release during the first 2.5 hours and 57% in 24 hours, indicating a good controlled release rate. In-vivo study revealed DMH-induced colorectal rats showed the increased tumor weight (34.8±0.75mg) tumor length (8.2±0.6) and tumor width (6.0±0.5) at a dose level of 0.5 ml/kg. Artemisinin loaded MSNPs significantly (p < .005) suppressed tumor weight (15.6±1.56), tumor length (5.7±0.23) and tumor width (2.3±0.8) at a dose level of 100 mg/kg body weight. Overexpression of 8-OHdG, MMP-7, CA-19-9, KRAS, IL-8, Caspase-8, PD-1 and PDL-1 in CRC, which were successfully treated with MSN-PEG@Artemisinin and standard Gemicitabine ((p≥0.056). MSNPs-PEG@Artemisinin suppressed DMH-induced colorectal carcinogenesis by targeting oxidative stress, KRAS/MMP-7/IL-8 inflammatory signaling, PD-1/PD-L1-mediated immune evasion, and Caspase-8-associated apoptotic dysregulation.ConclusionThese results highlight the potential of modified MSNPs as a versatile drug delivery system for colorectal cancer, providing a viable approach to enhance the therapeutic window of Artemisinin by controlling the dose while reducing the adverse effects of cancer therapies. This research contributes to advancements in pre-clinical studies and to improvements in targeted colorectal cancer therapies by providing insights into the development and use of mesoporous silica nanoparticles as a promising drug delivery system.Keywords: artemisinin, mesoporous silica nanoparticles, polyethylene glycol, colorectal cancer, drug delivery, encapsulation, tumor weight

1. Introduction

Colorectal cancer is one of the deadliest prevailing tumors that are widely spread around the world due to changing lifestyles and eating habits. Unlike other tumors, CRC chemotherapy is more challenging owing to the complexity in disease, increased resistance towards therapy and risk of recurrence. Inspite of major advancements, chemotherapy continues to be a major disadvantage owing to serious adverse effects associated with chemotherapeutic agents. 1

Novel drug delivery systems (DDS) have been designed in recent years in an effort to minimize the side effects of these drugs. Besides successful synthesis of liposomes, niosomes, dendrimers and nanotubes, another inorganic category of DDS has attained a worthwhile attention of researchers in terms of their capability to encapsulate highly lipophilic and pH-sensitive molecules, adjustable pore size, tailoring it for controlled and sustained drug release mechanism and flexible scalability. 2 These characteristics of MSNPs make it a favorable vehicle for rapid drug delivery with convenient methodology including sol-gel synthesis using aqueous media. 3 Some researchers have modified the surface of MSNPs by using targeting moieties to supplement MSNPs selective targeting to tumors whereas, others mainly focused on tumor acidic microenvironment including upregulation of glutathione (GSH) serum levels and acidic pH. Chemically and pH responsive MSNPs are modified to deliver anticancer agents in tumor microenvironment (TME) having low pH. 4 In order to decrease the unnecessary interaction of MSNPs with surrounding environment in response to any stimulus, inorganic polymeric coatings have been utilized to enhance colloidal stability of MSNPs. Such coatings are of great importance in avoiding pre-mature drug release at any pH. 5

Furthermore, some major challenges including short half-life of MSNPs, erythrolysis, endocytosis of nanoparticles by immune system should be taken under consideration proceeding to synthesis and administration of MSNPs. 6 Such problems have been overcome through PEGylation strategy. PEG has been a biocompatible polymer and attains the ability to enhance encapsulated drug’s hydrophilicity with improved release profile at specific pH especially in CRC. Surface coating of MSNPs with polyethylene glycol (PEGylation) makes it a promising DDS with ameliorated pharmacological characteristics and on-demand drug release. 7 Therefore, MSNPs conjugation may increase their retention time (ER) and permeation properties enhancing their implementation in sensing, visualization and on-demand release bolus dose following intravenous infusion regimens. 8

Various secondary metabolites extracted from plant origin are of great importance due to their potential against oxidative stress causing a variety of diseases such as cancer, arthritis, chronic kidney disease (CKD), cardiovascular disease (CVD) and diabetes. 9 However, poor aqueous solubility of major phytocompounds has been a serious issue for direct drug administration and various physical as well as chemical modifications are required to enhance their biocompatibility and bioavailability. 10

Artemisinin is a hydrophobic, semisynthetic, sesquiterpene lactone with a variety of therapeutic activities including antimalarial, anticancer, antioxidative, antigenotoxicity and hepatoprotective applications. 11 Several recent interpretations have proven activity of Artemisinin against MMPs, interleukins facilitating p, Junero-apoptosis and anticarcinogenicity. Furthermore, Artemisinin has shown to trigger immune mechanism in the tumor microenvironment by increasing mitogen-induced lymphoproliferative responses. 12 Moreover, this therapeutic drug may have potential against metastasis and tumor angiogenesis due to its involvement in fibrinolysis pathway. 13

The current study was designed to formulate Artemisinin loaded MSNPs with PEG decorations (MSN-PEG@Artemisinin) in a specific dose regimen to alleviate colorectal cancer. The MSN-PEG@Artemisinin was evaluated using FTIR, Zeta analysis, PDI, XRD, EE%, DL% and In-vitro release. ELISA was also performed using albino Wistar rat model to evaluate cytotoxic and anti-CRC effects of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin.

2. Materials and Methods

The whole experiment was performed in 313 Lab of “Biochemistry of Stress tolerance”, Institute of Molecular Biology and Biotechnology/Center for Research in Molecular Medicine (IMBB/CRiMM), The University of Lahore, Lahore, Pakistan.

2.1. Chemicals, Reagents, and Source of Materials Used in the Study

Artemisinin and Gemcitabine were purchased from the Sigma Chemical Co. (St. Louis, Mo, USA). Tetraethyl orthosilicate (TEOS), cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), polyethylene glycol (PEG) and 1,2-dimethylhydrazine (DMH) were purchased from Sigma Chemical Co. (St. Louis, Mo, USA). ELISA kit was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Corporation (St. Louis, Mo, USA). All these chemicals in the set of experiments were of analytical grade and utilized without any modification.

2.2. Synthesis of Artemisinin Loaded MSNPs (MSN@Artemisinin)

MSNPs were prepared by the Sol-Gel method by Han Y et al., 2024.3,14 In deionized water, CTAB (0.279 g) and ammonia solution (29%) were dissolved with a final volume of 50 ml. This solution was heated at 50°C with continuous stirring, keeping pH 10.3. Then 1.394 ml of TEOS was added in this solution dropwise while stirring continued. The mixture took 20 minutes to get turbid. An overnight magnetic stirring was performed. Afterwards, the prepared MSNPs were filtered and washed thrice using deionized water and ethanol. The nanoparticles were calcinated through rotary evaporation at 60°C. Further, the dried nanoparticles were added in a mixture of ethanol (50 ml) and 37% HCl (60µL) and refluxed at very high temperature (80°C) to extract CTAB surfactant. This procedure was repeated with several washings at 40°C, nanoparticles were vacuum dried. For passive loading of Artemisinin, suspension was prepared by dissolving 20 mg MSNPs in 5 mL drug solution containing 71.4 mg Artemisinin in 5 mL deionized water with continuous stirring. Subsequently, this prepared suspension was incubated for 24 hours at ambient temperature. After centrifugation and washing, the suspension was vacuum dried through rotary evaporation (Figure 1).

Synthesis of PEGylated mesoporous silica nanoparticles loaded with Artemisinin via the Sol-Gel method

2.3. MSN@Artemisinin Surface PEGylation (MSN-PEG@Artemisinin)

PEG 6000 (6 mg) was dissolved in a prepared mixture of 12 mg MSN@Artemisinin dispersed in 4 mL of DMSO with continuous magnetic stirring of 2 hours. 15 PEG6000 was selected for Pegylation due to two reasons:1) Physicochemical attribute:This longer polymer chain forms a hydrophilic steric barrier around the nanoparticle surface, and reduces particle aggregation and enhances colloidal stability. 16 2) Biopharmaceutical attribute: PEG6000 has reduced clearance, thus it has prolonged circulation time essential for achieving steady state plasma drug concentration in a controlled release drug delivery system. 17 Afterwards, this dispersion was centrifuged thrice at 3000 rpm for 10 minutes and lyophilized for several hours (Figure 1).

2.4. Characterization of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin

2.4.1. FTIR Analysis of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin

FTIR interpretation of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin functional groups was performed using JASCO FT/IR 6800 apparatus having spectrum range from 7800-350cm-1. The beam splitter used in the instrument was Ge/KBr.

2.4.2. Zeta Size, Zeta Potential and PDI of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin

Evaluation of nanoparticle size, potential and PDI was performed using Malvern Zetasizer Nano ZS particle size analyzer. DLS technique was utilized for size calculation and MSNPs dispersion (50 µL) was mixed in distilled H2O (0.95 mL) at 25°C. Zeta potential was measured by adding 50 µL of MSNPs dispersion in 0.95 mL NaCl (0.01 M). The data was analyzed through Malvern software.

2.4.3. XRD of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin and Standard Formulation MSN-PEG@Gemcitabine

Mesoporosity and crystallinity of MSNPs were characterized through BRUKER D8 ADVANCE diffractometer. A thin film of powder sample was run with CuKα as source radiation and the data was collected under 2Ɵ=2-25о and analyzed through Diffrac.Suite software.

2.4.4. Encapsulation Efficiency (%) and Drug Loading (%)

To calculate EE% and DL%, the lyophilized powdered sample of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin were added in DW and separated through centrifugation for 15 minutes (14000 rpm). The collected supernatant was estimated through UV spectrum at 263 nm to calculate the exact percentage of drug un-entrapped. Putting the known total concentration of Artemisinin, the un-encapsulated concentration was subtracted from the total concentration.

2.4.5. Determination of Unentrapped Drug Concentration by UV Spectrophotometry

The concentration of unentrapped Artemisinin was quantified using a UV–Visible spectrophotometer at 263 nm. A standard calibration curve was constructed using artemisinin solutions in the concentration range of 2–20 μg/mL, consistent with previously reported spectrophotometric methods. The calibration plot demonstrated excellent linearity (R2 = 0.989). Blank MSNP formulations without artemisinin were analyzed at the same wavelength to assess possible matrix interference and showed negligible absorbance. Following centrifugation, the clear supernatant containing unentrapped drug was collected and analyzed spectrophotometrically, and the drug concentration was calculated from the corresponding calibration curve.” 18

2.4.6. Artemisinin Release From MSN-PEG@Artemisinin

Dialysis tube technique by was used to calculate the drug release from MSN-PEG@Artemisinin that not only diffuses the drug particles into the dialysis bag release medium freely but also allows their complete separation from that medium. 19 MSN-PEG@Artemisinin (10 mg) was suspended in dialysis tube containing PBS (2 mL) at 7.4 pH. Afterwards, this tube was submerged in PBS (38 mL) at 37оC for 24 hours while stirring continuously. 2 mL sample from release medium was drawn at predetermined intervals and further replaced by fresh PBS (2mL). The withdrawn samples were centrifuged and analyzed under UV and cumulative Artemisinin release from MSNPs was calculated.

2.4.7. Release Kinetics

Zero-order, first-order, Higuchi, Korsmeyer’s-Peppas models were applied to calculate the release profile of formulations under consideration.

2.5. In-Vivo Study

Adult Wister male albino rats were taken from the animal house of Institute of Molecular Biology and Biotechnology, Lahore, Pakistan, with body weights ranging from 160 to 200 g. The Ethics Committee approved a study for Scientific Research at the University of Lahore (Approval No: U.S.M./Animal Ethics approval/2009/45/140). A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The experiment was conducted on 40 healthy male rats (150-200 g) which was granted following confirmation that all Institutional guidelines for Animal Use and Care for Research Purposes would be followed. The rats were kept in isolated cages under specific environmental conditions including 12 hour day and night cycle with 22±4°C. In-vivo study was performed in accordance with the ARRIVE (guidelines following 7 days (one week) of assimilation. Forty albino Wistar rats were divided into four groups, each group containing ten rats (n=10) of an average weight of 150-200g. Group A was regarded as normal control group fed with a normal diet and distilled water while group B, C and D were injected on alternate days (two times a week) with 0.5 mL/kg 1,2-dimethylhydrazine intraperitoneally for two months. Further groups C and D were injected intraperitoneally with MSN-PEG@Artemisinin (100 mg/kg b.wt) and Gemcitabine (500 mg/kg b.wt) respectively for one month (Tables 1 and 2).

2.5.1. Serum and Tissue Harvesting

At the end of the experiment, the animals were sacrificed by an intraperitoneal injection of ketamine (Arevipharma GmbH, Radebeul, Germany) (1 mL/g).Using Benchmark Scientific C2570 for centrifugation, 5 mL blood collected through heart puncture during longitudinal rat dissection was centrifuged (1500 rpm, 10 minutes) to separate the serum. The serum sample was stored at -60°C for biochemical evaluation. Colon tissue samples were collected in glass tubes containing 5% formalin for histopathological analysis.

2.5.2. Biochemical Assays

8-hydroxyguanosine (8-OHdG), matrix metalloproteinase 7 (MMP-7), carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA-19-9), Kristen rat sarcoma viral oncogene homolog (KRAS), interleukin 8 (IL-8), Caspase-8, programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) and programmed death ligand 1 (PDL-1) were evaluated using ELISA kits purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Corporation (St. Louis, MO, USA) in IMBB/CRiMM 313 lab of “Biochemistry of Stress tolerance”, The University of Lahore, Lahore, Pakistan.

2.5.3. Histopathological Analysis

For twenty-four hours, the colon segments used for histological investigations were kept in 10% phosphate buffered formalin. Haematoxylin and eosin were used to stain the tissues after they had been prepared using Drury and Wallington’s (1980) paraffin wax embedding procedure. A LABO® trinocular microscope (Labo Microsystems GmbH, Germany) equipped with an Omax 9.0 MP USB Digital Microscope Camera (manufactured in Korea) and an X10 objective lens was used to take pictures of the treated slides. The camera had a 0.5X reduction lens and a 9 megapixel (3488 × 2616 pixel) high quality colour digital camera.

2.6. Statistical Analysis

The results collected were demonstrated as mean ± SD. Statistical analysis was performed using Duncans Multiple Range Test (DMRT) and One Way ANOVA in SPSS Version 10. Significant differences between control and treated animal groups were calculated at p < 0.05.

2.7. Ethical Committee Approval and Participation Consent

Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board of The University of Lahore (Approval No: USM/Animal Ethics approval/2009/[45] [140]) and all animal experiments were performed in accordance with the Institutional Guidelines for the Care and Use of Animals for Scientific Purposes.

3.1. FTIR Analysis

Figure 2A and B exhibit FTIR interpretation of pure Artemisinin and MSN-PEG@Artemisinin respectively. All the chemically active groups were evaluated for molecular interactions between Artemisinin, PEG and specific functional groups of MSNPs. FTIR spectrum of pure Artemisinin showed sharp peaks for C-O-O-C and C=O at 1115 cm-1 and 1733 cm-1 respectively. Moreover, C-O-C, C-O and O-H stretchings were observed at 1204 cm-1, 1021 cm-1 and 3445 cm-1 respectively. More distinct peaks for O-O, C-H and O-O-C vibrations were observed at 835 cm-1, 2952 cm-1 and 871 cm-1 (Figure 1A). Distinct vibrations for MSN-PEG@Artemisinin were exhibited at 2924.06 cm-1, 3435.71 cm-1, 1466.77 cm-1 and 1233.94 cm-1 for stretching alkyl groups, O-H group adherence on MSN surface, C-H bonds and C-O-C bending vibration of ether group that confirmed the arrangement of PEG molecules on nanoparticle surfaces. Also the O-H asymmetric vibration, Si-O-Si within the silica network and Si-OH symmetric bending vibrations were described by the peaks at 1464.77 cm-1, 1079.27 cm-1 and 920.40 cm-1 that explained the successive arrangement of silica network. Moreover, prominent peaks for Artemisinin were recorded at 2924.06 cm-1, 1738 cm-1, 1214.35 cm-1, 1110.73 cm-1, 1016.20 cm-1, 864.30 cm-1 and 842.65cm-1 that confirmed the successive entrapment of Artemisinin within MSNPs (Figure 2B).

3.7.2. Biochemical Analysis of Inflammatory Cytokines

The observed modulation of serum biomarkers reflects the underlying pathophysiological changes associated with DMH-induced colorectal tumorigenesis and the therapeutic efficacy of the administered treatments. All the parameters including 8-OHdG, MMP-7, CA-19-9, KRAS, IL-8, Caspase-8, PD-1 and PDL-1, were significantly raised in Group B indicating the tumor formation in colon and rectum area in albino rats and enhanced oxidative DNA damage, extracellular matrix remodeling, oncogenic signaling activation, and a pro-inflammatory and immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, all of which are characteristic features of colorectal cancer progression. The levels of the foresaid biomarkers were decreased in the serum of group C and D with the treatment of Artemisinin encapsulated in modified controlled drug delivery system and standard drug Gemcitabine respectively. Group C rats that were treated with MSN-PEG@Artemisinin (100mg/kg/day) exhibited significant decrease in serum levels of 8-OHdG (23.87 pg/mL), MMP-7 (60.56 pg/mL), CA-19-9 (18.87U/mL), KRAS (7.79 CT), IL-8 (21.99 pg/mL), caspase 8 (90.52 mg/mL), PD-1 (91.33 mg/mL) and PDL-1 (11.44 µg/mL) as compared to positive control group of rats (Group B), likely to exert its anticancer effects through targeted delivery, enhanced cellular uptake, and sustained release, leading to suppression of oxidative stress, inhibition of inflammatory signaling, and downregulation of immune checkpoint molecules. whereas group D rats treated with standard Gemcitabine@500mg/kg b.wt/day demonstrated a decrease of 8-OHdG (12.44 pg/mL), MMP-7 (48.68 pg/mL), CA-19-9 (11.96 U/mL), KRAS (4.98 CT), IL-8 (26.09 pg/mL), caspase 8 (98.38 mg/mL), PD-1 (88.29mg/mL) and PDL-1 (0.955µg/mL) as compared to DMH intoxicated group of rats (Figure 8). Gemcitabine in Group D mediates cytotoxic effects through inhibition of DNA synthesis and induction of apoptosis in rapidly proliferating tumor cells.

Response of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin following 1,2-Dimethylhydrazine (DMH) induced colorectal cancer in albino rat

Collectively, these findings suggest that treatment with MSN-PEG@Artemisinin (100mg/kg/day) mitigate colorectal carcinogenesis more efficiently by restoring biochemical homeostasis, reducing tumor burden, and modulating key molecular drivers involved in tumor progression and immune evasion as compared to the commericially used drug Gemcitabine @500mg/kg b.wt/day in suitable dosage regimen.

3.8. Histopathological Analysis of the Effect of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin in DMH-Induced Colorectal Cancer in Albino Rats

The findings demonstrated that glandular dysplasia in the submucosal layer, characterized by inflammatory cells, was a marker of substantial colon tissue damage induced by DMH (Figure 9B). The glandular dysplasia was clustered close to the lumen or arranged in several rows. Rats displayed a large number of epithelial cells with pleomorphic nuclei, thick mucin, reduced cell polarity, mitotic hyperactivity (hyperchromasia), anisocytosis, and no goblet cells in addition to foci (Figure 9B). Rats given MSN-PEG@Artemisinin showed normal mucin thickness, reduced nucleus malformation, normal mitotic action, and less colon damage with atypical epithelial cells (Figure 9C). In comparison to the rats that received just DMH, the Gemcitabine animals (100 mg/kg) exhibited reduced mucosal damage and substantial variability in the colon tissue, according to histopathologic detections of the colon tumor portions (Figure 9D).

3.9. Mechanistic Signaling Pathways Targeting Colorectal Carcinogenesis

The proposed mechanistic model demonstrates that 1,2-dimethylhydrazine (DMH)-induced colorectal carcinogenesis in albino rats initiated excessive oxidative stress and DNA oxidation, leading to the accumulation of 8-hydroxyguanosine (8-OHdG), a key biomarker of oxidative DNA damage. Elevated oxidative stress promoted activation and mutation of KRAS, which subsequently stimulated the RAF/MEK/ERK signaling cascade and induced matrix metalloproteinase-7 (MMP-7) expression through NF-κB and AP-1 mediated transcriptional regulation. Increased MMP-7 enhanced extracellular matrix degradation, tumor invasion, and epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-associated signaling, thereby elevating carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA-19-9) expression and metastatic potential. Concurrently, KRAS-driven inflammatory signaling activated IL-8 secretion via STAT3/NF-κB and PI3K/AKT pathways, promoting angiogenesis, tumor proliferation, and immune modulation. Persistent inflammatory signaling further upregulated PD-L1 expression on tumor cells, facilitating PD-1/PD-L1-mediated immune evasion and suppression of T-cell cytotoxicity. In parallel, oncogenic KRAS and IL-8 signaling inhibited Caspase-8 activation through increased expression of anti-apoptotic mediators including c-FLIP, BCL-2, and XIAP, thereby preventing apoptosis and enhancing tumor survival. MSNPs-PEG@Artemisinin exertsed multitargeted anti-cancer activity by attenuating oxidative stress, suppressing KRAS/MAPK and PI3K/AKT/NF-κB signaling, downregulating MMP-7, IL-8, CA-19-9, PD-L1, and anti-apoptotic proteins, while restoring Caspase-8-mediated apoptosis and anti-tumor immune responses. Collectively, this integrated signaling network highlighted the therapeutic potential of MSNPs-PEG@Artemisinin as a nanostructured multi-node inhibitor against colorectal cancer progression (Figure 10).

4. Discussion

Artemisinin has been classified as unique antimalarial drug owing to its remarkable potential in drug repositioning for multiple disorders like carcinomas. Millions of people worldwide have been treated with Artemisinin due to its exceptional acceptability and safety profile. 20 The aim of this research was to synthesize a controlled release MSN-PEG@Artemisinin to enhance its bioavailability and targeting against CRC. The sol-gel method was used to synthesize pH-responsive mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) because this method proceeds in mild reaction conditions and is very cost effective and provides high pore volume for drug loading. Other methods including hydrothermal, microemulsion, Stöber method and aerosol assisted methods require expensive instrumentation, sensitive reaction (thermal) conditions, difficult purification steps and lower pore volume as compared to sol gel technique. 14 Among various surfactants used in sol gel technique, CTAB provides the highly ordered mesoporous structure with strong templates while others including P123, DTAB and tween 80 are less preferred in this specific technique due to less uniformity in particle size, lower structural stability and weak templating abilities. 21 In FTIR spectrum, the encapsulation of Artemisinin in MSN-PEG@Artemisinin is confirmed by the intact peaks of endoperoxide ring at 1110.73 cm-1, lactone ring at 1738 cm-1 and ether band at 1214.35 cm-1. Also peaks at 1079.27 cm-1 and 920.40 cm-1 indicate proper arrangement of silanol groups in mesoporous symmetry. Also the 2924.06 cm-1 for methyl group in the drug loaded MSNPs confirm the presence of Artemisinin within the carrier system. Minor changes in peak intensity are attributed to the confinement of drug within mesoporous channels, thereby supporting successful encapsulation Since there is no new wavelength or bond detected in FTIR spectrum of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin as compared to FTIR spectrum of pure Artemisinin, it indicated that Artemisinin is chemically compatible and stable with PEG polymer and excipients of mesoporous silica nanoparticles. Hence no interaction was developed.

In current study, DLS (dynamic light scattering) interpretation confirms monodispersity with particle uniformity and a very narrow range of size distribution. The polydispersity index (PDI) at 0.106 signifies a tremendously monodisperse system. This level of uniformity is advantageous for reproducibility in drug delivery applications.22,23 Moreover, a prominent sharp peak at -10.9 mV indicates moderate electrostatic repulsion among nanoparticles. This zeta potential suggests moderate colloidal stability, minimizing aggregation and improving dispersion in aqueous media. The stability of Artemisinin loaded MSNPs is further corroborated by the absence of aggregation, which is a crucial factor for their successful application in targeted delivery. 24 Surface modification of MSNPs through PEG coating not only enhanced its dispersion mechanism but also transformed it into a pH responsive DDS for CRC targeting in current research.25,26 Due to high scalability of MSNPs, PEG coated MSNPs have a wide variety of therapeutic and diagnostic applications. 27 PEG coating typically attains a negative charge owing to protonation, dissociation and reverse dissociation; although, the evaluation conditions may cause significant variation in zeta potential values. 28 The prepared nanoparticles exhibited a zeta potential of -10.9mV, indicating a moderately negative surface charge attributed to deprotonated silanol groups present on mesoporous silica nanoparticles. Mesoporous silica nanoparticles remain sufficiently stable at lower values due to steric stabilization and porous structural characteristics. Moreover, PEGylation further contributes to steric stabilization, reducing particle aggregation despite moderate zeta potential values. The obtained zeta potential, together with obtained PDI value suggests reasonable colloidal stability with minimal aggregation tendency. In recent years, researchers have formulated modified and controlled MSNPs using PEG coatings that would act like a gatekeeper with drug release in response to acidic pH (TME) and in a controlled manner. PEG disassembles in low pH environment, like endosomes, releasing the drug gradually.29,30

The X-ray diffraction peaks at 2Ɵ=4.1о and 6.2о signifies differential electronic density with regular periodicity owing to the repetitive and long range porous arrangement of MSNPs. Typically, a sharp diffraction at such a low-angle reflects a wide-range arrangement of mesopores, a characteristic attribute of well-assembled mesoporous silica nanoparticles. 31 Additionally, no extra peak appears in hexagonal network of both samples encourages an effective drug encapsulation within mesoporous silica nanoparticles. 32 The high intensity sharp peak mentioned in diffraction pattern illustrates the synthesis of MSNPs with particle size greater than 100 nm. 33

One major goal of this study was to achieve a surface area for maximum drug loading. The prepared MSNPs introduced a reasonable number and volume of pores with appropriate surface area. The preferred passive mechanism for successful Artemisinin loading enhanced its loading capacity. 34 Also, the particle size of nano formulation relates directly to the encapsulation efficiency for the given formulations. This is because, as the particle size increases, the porosity per particle also increase, letting more drug entrapment. Another basis for this direct relationship is due to the greater internal pore area for drug adsorption and linear pore opening, facilitating better drug diffusion and stable loading inside mesopores. 35 A linear relationship between EE and drug loading capacity of MSNPs was established. The present study demonstrates the encapsulation efficiency and drug loading percentage are given as 82.75% and 33.10%, respectively. This is in complete accordance with the study conducted by Bahria et al., 2025 which reported the encapsulation efficiency of 80.9% and drug loading capacity of 34%.14,21

PEG nanoparticles were used to produce a pH-stimulated controlled DDS. Controlled release patterns could improve the efficacy of anticancer regimens. Nihal et al. used PEG coated MSNPs to stimulate drug release as a function of pH. In his work, he demonstrated a release of 1% drug PEG-NPs after 192 hrs indicating an extremely extended release at physiological pH. 36 Gradual degradation of PEG under low pH contributes to a controlled or sustained release profile. Lactic acid accumulation during tumor growth leads to an acidic TME, exclusively described by Warburg effect (aerobic glycolysis). 37 It is proposed that exposure of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin to a low pH condition, such MSNPs get activated owing to their pH sensitivity characteristics, leading to PEG detachment and Artemisinin release. 38

The drug-release kinetics of PEG-coated mesoporous silica nanoparticles loaded with Artemisinin were evaluated and compared with those of the standard drug, Gemcitabine. MSN-PEG@Artemisinin exhibited a strong Higuchi kinetic fit and Korsmeyer-Peppas kinetic fit indicating a controlled release pattern where the drug is released at a constant rate over time. Furthermore, the Korsmeyer-Peppas model revealed a release component n=0.476, indicative of Fickian transport, which implies a combination of diffusion and erosion mechanisms governing the drug release. Gemcitabine demonstrated an excellent Higuchi model, confirming a diffusion-controlled mechanism. In this work, we evaluated the therapeutic effects of Artemisinin with a pH-responsive and controlled release pattern on the colorectal cancer profile. A rise in 8-OHdG, MMP-7, CA-19-9, KRAS, IL-8, Caspase-8, PD-1, and PDL-1 parameters could be indicative of a colorectal tumor. Our results indicated that all CRC biomarkers could be successfully regulated through MSN-PEG@Artemisinin. MSN-PEG@Artemisinin (100mg/kg/day) exhibited significant decrease in serum levels of 8-OHdG (p≥0.041), MMP-7 (p≥0.059), CA-19-9 (p≥0.087), KRAS (p≥0.034), IL-8 (p≥0.077), caspase 8 (p≥0.021), PD-1 (p≥0.064) and PDL-1 (p≥0.056) as compared to standard drug Gemcitabine (500mg/kg/day b.wt). These findings suggest that a substantial imbalance between the colorectal carcinogenic proteins CA 19-9 and MMP-7 may result in alterations to the mitochondrial pathway of apoptosis and cellular malfunction.39,40

There may be a molecular mechanism for the lower incidence of ACF and the chemoprotective effect of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin in rats treated with DMH. The current supplementation of MSN-PEG@Artemisinin (100 mg/kg) resulted in a significant positive modulation of Caspase-8, PD-1, and PDL-1 protein as well as a noticeable reduction of the appearance of KRAS protein in the rat’s colon. KRAS-driven inflammatory signalling promotes angiogenesis, tumour proliferation, and immunological regulation by activating IL-8 secretion through the STAT3/NF-κB and PI3K/AKT pathways. PD-1/PD-L1-mediated immune evasion and T-cell cytotoxicity are facilitated by persistent inflammatory signalling, which also increases PD-L1 expression on tumour cells. Oncogenic KRAS and IL-8 signalling simultaneously suppress apoptosis and improve tumour survival by inhibiting Caspase-8 activation through increased expression of anti-apoptotic mediators such as c-FLIP, BCL-2, and XIAP. By reducing oxidative stress, inhibiting KRAS/MAPK and PI3K/AKT/NF-κB signalling, downregulating MMP-7, IL-8, CA-19-9, PD-L1, and anti-apoptotic proteins, and restoring Caspase-8-mediated apoptosis and anti-tumor immune responses, MSNPs-PEG@Artemisinin exhibits multitargeted anti-cancer action. When taken as a whole, this integrated signalling network demonstrates the therapeutic potential of MSNPs-PEG@Artemisinin as a nanostructured multi-node inhibitor against the advancement of colorectal cancer.

A well-known histological characteristic of colorectal cancer is colon adenocarcinoma, which occurs when tumors of various sizes and glandular shapes penetrate the muscular layer of colonic tissue. Rats given DMH injections in this study developed a large number of colonic adenomas and adenocarcinomas, with the ileocecal lymph nodes and cecum showing significant organ metastases.41,42 Colon adenomas and adenocarcinomas decrease in number and volume when treated with MSN-PEG@Artemisinin. Furthermore, compared to the cancer control animals, the rats treated with MSN-PEG@Artemisinin had noticeably less total adenocarcinoma fluid (ACF). Research on MSN-PEG@Artemisinin showed that it has a strong inhibitory effect on DMH colorectal adenocarcinoma in rats, as evidenced by reduced ACF values when compared to cancer controls.

5. Limitations of the Study

In addition to overcoming multidrug resistance, mesoporous silica nanoparticles also address the shortcomings of traditional cancer treatment. However, several limitations of using mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNPs) in the study also exist, such as the Enhanced Permeability and Retention (EPR) effect, which is the main mechanism for passive nanoparticle accumulation in tumors, is frequently inconsistent, especially in animal models when contrasted to human patients. Natural compounds are benign, but the nano-carriers themselves, such as silicon dioxide in MSNPs, can accumulate in tissues and produce toxicity or inflammatory reactions, which raises safety concerns. Furthermore, the deep penetration of nanoparticles into the tumor core in solid tumors such as colorectal tumor in rats is restricted by the strong extracellular matrix (ECM) and high interstitial fluid pressure.

6. Conclusion

This work diversifies the proposal of novel anticancer secondary metabolites with remarkable anti-cancerous therapeutics incorporated in advanced pH-responsive MSNPs through much safer, efficacious and precise therapeutic strategies. The present study concluded that in rats with DMH-induced colorectal cancer, Artemisinin-loaded mesoporous silica nanoparticles coated with polyethylene glycol (MSN-PEG@Artemisinin) significantly exhibited apoptotic, antiproliferative, and antioxidant properties. Additionally, MSN-PEG@Artemisinin demonstrated cytoprotective actions by regulating cytokines expressions and significantly reversed DMH-induced biochemical and histopathological changes in colorectal cancer in rats. This proposed hybrid Nano carrier system appears to be promising for effective colon delivery for safe and effective colorectal cancer treatment.
