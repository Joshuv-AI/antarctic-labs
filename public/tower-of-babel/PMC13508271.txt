Real-time healthcare staffing optimization using fuzzy logic and genetic algorithm
Authors: Balveer Saini, Dinesh Kumar Saini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe field of medical research has advanced remarkably in the twenty-first century; however, the healthcare industry faces significant challenges, including rising costs, increasing demand, and constrained resources. These factors make effective management of healthcare systems crucial. Efficient systems are those that better use resources, deliver services, and reduce waiting times.MethodsThe paper addresses the issue of healthcare staffing optimization by presenting a new model, the Fuzzy-Based Time-Dependent Multi-Server, Multi-Queueing (FB-TDMS-MQ) System. This model combines fuzzy logic with a genetic algorithm (GA) to optimize staffing levels based on real-time patient needs and service times, a capability that traditional fixed-staffing models cannot accommodate. To demonstrate the model's application in a real-world setting, a case study is conducted at Dhanwantri Hospital and Research Centre (DHRC) in Jaipur, a multi-specialty hospital. The model's performance is evaluated using simulation studies.ResultsThe implementation of the FB-TDMS-MQ model led to significant improvements. Simulation studies demonstrated that the average “peak-hour waiting time” drastically reduced by 72.73% after incorporating fuzzy logic modifications. Additionally, the GA optimization approach resulted in a 50.2% reduction in average waiting time, showing superior performance compared to static staffing models. The model's sensitivity analysis proved highly useful in the case of unexpected events in the healthcare system.ConclusionsThe FB-TDMS-MQ model proved to be adaptable and effective in real-time healthcare staffing optimization. It demonstrated a reduction in waiting times and the ability to handle unexpected events, showing that the model could optimize staffing dynamically in fast-changing healthcare environments. The real-time control and modifications provided by the model have significant potential for improving hospital management and staff allocation.Keywords: Hybrid queueing approach, FB-TDMS-MQ model, Fuzzy logic, Genetic algorithm, Dynamic healthcare

Introduction

The medical research has advanced a lot in the twenty-first century; however, the rising need for healthcare requires more effective management of healthcare systems. High cost of the economy, increasing demand and constrained resources are among many complex challenges for the healthcare industry. Traditional queueing models—even in the medical field—are based on the current techniques, but there are differences in the intensities of arrival of patients seeking care and the times of service of patients at the point of care [1–6]. Forecasting demand is susceptible to threats posed by predictive analytic models to investigate the variance of demand. To circumvent these problems, a hybrid FB-TDMS-MQ model is then developed in this paper, which will combine fuzzy logic and GA and time-varying multi-server queues. The model can efficiently reschedule the workforce in real-time depending upon the vagaries of the healthcare operational activities, and to generate answers to the present approach problem.

While the previous research gives us a good base case on the improvement of the healthcare system, there are a lot of missing, and quite a bit of it that should be settled to have a real understanding of real-time assessment system like fuzzy logic. Background information is also reviewed to help us to get a clear picture of the current idea.

Queueing theory in healthcare

Recent research by Kosiol et al. [1] and Amin et al. [2] present a systematic framework for fostering innovation in health care by defining its basic principles, critical success factors and hindrances. Elalouf & Wachtel [3] and Santos et al. [4] conducted a detailed look at the queueing problems in emergency departments and wait times, effective queueing strategies, and research methodology. Bai et al. [5] concluded that no amount of change made by human models will be able to increase the efficiency of staffing. While these review studies addressed significant issues, provided descriptive classifications, and outlined challenges, they were not adequately analyzed for fuzzy logic-type systems, which rely on current–time evaluations.

Queueing studies on the COVID-19 pandemic

The latest queueing models, created in adaptation to the situation caused by the isolation of the Coronavirus, emphasize the congestion issues mentioned by Zimmerman et al. [6], who used a queueing model to predict the ventilator capacity in the province of British Columbia during the initial stages of the outbreak of the coronavirus. Saini et al. [7] made an in-depth analysis of healthcare capacity during the "golden hours" of the pandemic. This study looked at the effectiveness of hospitals in providing treatment for the victims when they arrive. The hospital overcrowding was identified in the research through "Transient Act System (TAS)." This investigation reinforces the importance of the 'golden hour' concept in urgent medical attention, highlighting the refined prioritization of patients in an ever-changing setting. Still, the investigations do not show enough research into any platforms like fuzzy logic, which depends on real-time assessments.

Dynamic staffing levels and real-time adaptability

Although the studies on staffing level strategy have been published, they assumed the steady state, which is inevitably less relevant in a dynamic healthcare setting. Ala et al. [8] employ refined remedies within the mixed-integer linear programming framework to tackle the person in need picking challenge for prominent diagnosis-related group therapy. To find the shortest response time for routine and urgent patients, this study used the Poisson distribution and queueing theory. Damirchilo et al. [9] created a queueing theory-deep neural network system to improve manufacturing facility design and transporter selection. It addressed difficult challenges like stochastic facility service times, fluctuating demand, carrier capability, velocity, and batch capacity. Oskuei et al. [10] investigated single-machine scheduling that included varied processing times, sequence-dependent structures for consumable products, and an array of proactive care strategies. Konrad and Liu [11] conducted a real-time assessment of waiting-time allocation in dynamic lineups, a crucial aspect for the healthcare sector. It demonstrated how nonstationary demand and staffing methods influence wait times, driving healthcare simulation. In 2025, Aminah et al. [12] proposed the Accumulative Priority Queue with Finite Horizon (APQ-h), a dynamic scheduling system that used real-time queue management and simulation to optimise emergency department patient flow and wait times. Saini et al. [13] utilized advanced reinforcement learning to enhance online healthcare by elegantly managing doctor accessibility and prioritizing patient treatment according to need and arrival time. This study refined resource allocation through Q-learning, a sophisticated model-free reinforcement learning approach, enhancing patient experience by minimizing wait times and dynamically assigning doctors according to the current queue status. Qureshi et al. [14] created a dynamic queue algorithm to optimise healthcare appointment scheduling and patient flow management using real-time metrics, improving operational efficiency and reducing outpatient department wait times, so making healthcare systems smarter. Compared to a real-time dynamic staffing level strategy, some other studies have attempted to devise one; however, such strategies become irrelevant in changing situations because they are not adaptive and do not use fuzzy logic.

Fuzzy logic in staffing decisions

The integration of fuzzy logic in Queueing model may offer solutions to modern healthcare problem. Fuzzy logic is powerful in resolving agitations within the healthcare system that a conventional model finds difficulty coping with such as fluctuations in arrival time of patients, duration of the services, number of staffs. The studies [15, 16] have used fuzzy logic in staffing decisions to schedule healthcare; the studies have not combined them with the queueing models of varying time to tackle the health care flow and uncertainty. As well as the framework for the home healthcare service that incorporates heuristic search and fuzzy logic is discussed by Sinthamrongruk et al. [17], which is focused on the matching of qualified staff for patients with a high degree of efficiency. However, they do not address particularly a hybrid queueing model for manpower planning and system optimization.

Multi-queue integration

Using methods to set multi-queueing, Hillas et al. [18] gave a critical look into workforce planning to provide a more complete picture of hospital operations. Furthermore, Masmoudi et al. [19] examined routing and scheduling problems related home healthcare. Pereira et al. [20] found that concentrated training to improve internal procedures, infrastructure, instructive sessions and co-creation projects helped to improve healthcare management. These researches are healthcare management structure and assessment, which is missing the dynamic and time-varying components of fuzzy healthcare systems.

Fuzzy logic and Discrete Event Simulation (DES)

Fuzzy logic and optimization approaches have advanced in healthcare staffing optimization literature; however, real-time adaptation is lacking. Sarasu et al. [21] and Vázquez-Serrano et al. [22] highlight static and hybrid models for resource allocation, patient priority, and waiting time reduction without real-time dynamic staffing modifications. Fuzzy systems are employed to handle uncertainty (Azadeh et al., [23]; Lazarashouri & Najafi, [24]), but they are generally limited to emergency rooms or primary healthcare settings, lacking the flexibility needed in fast-changing contexts. A fuzzy decision support system for service quality management by Cui et al. [25] lacks real-time changes and genetic algorithms for optimization. The proposed model can address these limitations by dynamically responding to fluctuating healthcare demands and optimizing staffing levels based on patient needs. The hybrid approach presented in this research offers continuous optimization instead of predefined staffing schedules, making it adaptable to unpredictable healthcare environments, unlike previous works like Muñoz et al. [26]. Thus, this research addresses a significant gap by providing an optimal, real-time staffing solution that blends fuzzy logic's uncertainty management with genetic algorithms' continuous optimization.

Based on queueing model analysis, fuzzy optimization, and healthcare system evaluation, traditional models should be improved to handle uncertainty, inefficiency, and complexity. This research is motivated by the need to address the current healthcare staffing models, especially by addressing particular areas of its priorities. Unlike other methods, such as OptQueue, that rely on static or periodic staffing adjustments, this approach integrates real-time optimization based on fuzzy logic and a GA to make staffing-level decisions that adapt to changing patient demand and uncertainty. This flexibility allows the model to appropriately respond to the real world setting of healthcare. The model's effectiveness in reducing patient wait times as well as improving care efficiency while keeping operations cost effective and financially sustainable is assessed in the study. While traditional Markovian queueing models assume labor parameters, in the proposed approach, fuzzy logic is used to deal with the uncertainties and GA is used for the dynamic optimization of staffing which provides a more robust solution. The model periodically makes updates based on real-time data that helps ensure that it is responsive to patient flow fluctuations. The paper efforts delivering improvement staffing optimization methods in healthcare facilities by proposing hybrid fuzzy-genetic algorithm approach addressing the limitations of conventional models to control the real-time variability.

Comparative justification

In this study, we have chosen Fuzzy Logic and Genetic Algorithm (GA) over machine learning approaches for several key reasons. While machine learning models excel in handling large, labeled datasets, they require significant computational resources and may not effectively address the inherent uncertainties and variability present in healthcare staffing. Fuzzy Logic is particularly suited for managing the imprecise nature of healthcare data, such as fluctuating patient arrivals and service times, by using fuzzy sets and inference rules. This enables real-time staffing adjustments without the need for precise data. Moreover, GA provides an optimization framework that dynamically adjusts staffing levels based on real-time data, making it well-suited for situations with multiple variables and constraints. Unlike machine learning models, which often require extensive training and a large volume of data, Fuzzy Logic and GA offer a more interpretable, flexible, and computationally efficient solution for optimizing staffing in fast-changing healthcare environments.

Methodology

This section presents the process of creating the proposed FB-TDMS-MQ model that is aimed at optimizing workforce planning in fuzzy healthcare systems. This methodology was about the structure of the proposed FB-TDMS-MQ model. This model uses a dynamic strategy, where healthcare workers are not fixed resources but the decision variables. This enables the system to adjust staffing levels as and when demand for patients fluctuates. This is especially important in order to cope with hours of peak demand, such as in emergency situations, where more resources are needed. Unlike conventional static staffing models that are based on set workforce numbers, the current staffing model is guided by the concept of tailoring staffing to optimize the queueing system to minimize waiting times and optimize patient queueing.

In this paper, the term "real-time" optimization means that the system has the ability to dynamically adjust the number of staffing needs according to fluctuations in patient demand and service times. However, instead of continuous updating, the model is based on periodic updates at discrete times of the day usually at the beginning of each shift (for example every 6 to 8 h) This way it is possible to make adjustments according to some actual measurements such as the number of patients coming in, the service time of every consultation, which are recorded and analyzed at short intervals of time. Thus, although there can be periodic updating, it is based on real-time data obtained during each shift, giving the system an ability to adapt to changing conditions. The full description and mathematical formulation are separately described in Sect. "Model Description and Mathematical Formulation".

Arrival rate justification

In this study, we assume that the patient arrivals are Poisson distribution (M) based on the available arrival data of DHRC Jaipur. However, we have added a detailed statistical analysis to check this assumption. The arrival data of patients were gathered during few months and they were recorded with hourly intervals which enabled us to analyze arrival rate distribution. In order to check the validity of the Poisson assumption, the Fig. 2 shows a comparison of observed and expected frequencies for the Poisson distribution.

Comparison of observed and expected patient arrival rates (Poisson distribution)

Figure 2 compares the Poisson distribution models of actual and predicted patient arrival rates for day and night shifts at a hospital facility. The blue and green bars show the observed daytime and nighttime arrival frequency, while the red and orange lines show the Poisson distribution's anticipated values. The night shift has 4 patients every hour, which is distinct from the day shift's 10 patients per hour. This comparison shows how closely the observed data matches the theoretical Poisson distribution, which demonstrates that patient arrivals follow a Poisson process with some natural variability for both time periods.

Service time modeling and upper bound justification

Service times for patient consultations are modeled by fuzzy sets i.e. triangular distribution. This choice is common in the case of exact data sparsity, but good approximations can be made using observations and expert opinion. In this model, the maximum number of minutes for patient service time was set to 15 min, which was derived from empirical and expert opinion.

The upper limit of 15 min was based on the history from DHRC, Jaipur which showed that from an analysis of the service time by the patients for routine consultations, 95% of the cases have been completed within this time. This data was gathered over a period of months and was helpful in determining the limits of patient interaction times. In consultation with healthcare professionals at DHRC, Jaipur including doctors and administration staff it was confirmed that 15 min is taken as the typical upper limit of the duration of consultations for non-complex cases. This expert consensus was used to ensure that the service time values were realistic and aligned with the practices in the hospital. By integrating both empirical information and the expert information, we reached a reasonable and effective upper bound of service time which was then modeled by using triangular fuzzy membership functions.

Model description and mathematical formulation

Queueing system framework

The health care system is structured as a dynamic multi-equipment multi-queue system, in which the rate of the patients’ arrival, the service delivery duration, and the server count are continuously time-dependent and adaptable. So far, the classical combinatorial view of queueing theory has been enhanced to accommodate the dynamic behavior of healthcare departments with time-varying parameters to depict the dynamic nature of the system in a beautifully elegant manner. In this paper, we utilize an advanced M/G/s queueing network with fuzzy-based time-sensitive elements and servers and time-sensitive queueing system to model a health care system. The system has immense functionalities that can be displayed in a detailed mathematical formulation.

Genetic algorithm for staffing optimization

GA is used to optimize healthcare staffing. The GA regulates the staffing to decrease wait times. After examining past 6 to 8 h of arrival rate information, the system will adjust staffing levels and make recommendations for the start of the shifts. This allows for the plans to be practical and falls within existing healthcare practice. The GA is used to choose the best crossover and mutation crew for innovation. Preventive maintenance is an improvement technique that reduces the wait time of patients by meeting the demand over a period. The process is iterated until optimal performance of healthcare workers will be reached.

GA-based staff assignment and constraints

The GA-based staff assignment in the current study is to reduce average waiting times in various queues of patients. However, in order to guarantee the feasibility of the generated staffing solutions, several hard constraints are added in the optimization process. These constraints are created to represent limitations of real-world situations in terms of staff and hospital operations.

Staffing Limits: Each queue in the system has a maximum value of the number of staff that can be assigned based on hospital policy and operational limits. These limits are to guarantee that staffing configurations will not surpass the capability of the hospital or cause inefficiencies by having too many staff. The GA takes these staffing limits into account by limiting the number of healthcare workers assigned to each queue up to predefined limits.

Results and discussion

In this section, the research findings and conclusions of the study, validation, simulation, sensitivity, and case study results will be discussed. The discussion shows the relevance of the results in the context of real life.

After the introduction of the FB-TDMS-MQ model, the decision was made on how to test the Fuzzy Backbone structure of TDMS Model using a real-life case study from "DHRC, Jaipur," a diversified medical team hospital. The operations using the GA method can have a dynamic staffing level, which is a more flexible and efficient scheme than the traditional fixed staffing models as shown in the case study of DHRC, Jaipur. The GA-based staffing optimization resulted in the reduction of average waiting times at all patient queues. During the optimization process, the model considered clear restrictions of staffing limits, skills, and shifts to ensure that all staffing configurations generated by the GA were possible. The penalty mechanism effectively ensured that infeasible solutions such as those that violate staffing limitations or shift rules are discarded and that as a result, optimal staffing assignments were generated that followed hospital operational policies.

The validation of the assumption of arrival rate through statistical analysis gave an approval to use the Poisson distribution in modeling the arrival of patients. This helps to add credibility to the proposed FB-TDMS-MQ model, that will ensure its practical relevance in dynamic settings of the healthcare environment. The assumption on upper limit of 15 min for service time was carefully found out from empirical data and expert judgement from DHRC, Jaipur. In this way, the service time distribution model could build on the actual healthcare operation to ensure that realistic healthcare operation could be represented in the model to optimize staffing and patient flow.

Simulation and validation tests show that the FB-TDMS-MQ model is an effective aid in optimizing hospital staff work and reducing patient waiting time. Fuzzy logic for real-time staff adjustment reduced the peak-hour waiting time by 72.73%, which showed its demand management efficacy. The best hospital staffing strategy, which reduced wait time by 50.2%, was selected using GA. The effectiveness of the model to evaluate the operational performance of the hospital is argued. The system flexibly allocated the number of workers to improve the performance throughout unexpected changes in the demand. The simulation results outperformed FB-TDMS-MQ model. The findings showed that FB-TDMS-MQ has a positive impact on maximizing resources and providing better healthcare to patients. The results of the simulation was validated by comparing it with the real life data from DHRC, Jaipur. The simulated waiting times, patient flow and staffing levels were in close agreement with the real data and can be confident that the model accurately depicts the real healthcare system. In addition, through sensitivity analysis, model robustness was demonstrated under different input parameters, which further proved the model's good performance under different scenarios.

Sensitivity studies have shown that the model is able to accommodate different numbers of arrivals, different service times and different number of staff members. Greater arrival rates and longer service durations result in longer queueing times and waits, therefore, there needs to be time-consistent adjustments to staffing appropriate to demand. Thus, there will be several advantages of FB-TDMS-MQ in terms of healthcare. The comparison with benchmark problems and state-of-the-art algorithms proves that this hybrid fuzzy-genetic algorithm is able to bring a significant improvement in the quality of solutions and also in the computational efficiency. The combination of fuzzy logic with GA offers better flexibility in coping with the uncertainty/variability which exists in healthcare systems, not well represented in other methods such as the Simulated Annealing (SA) or the Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO). With the current system upgrades, this technique of management maximizes queue management and scheduling of staff groups through the patient flow, hence boosting patient flow and service efficiency. While it may prevent data processing from carrying on, the method may be helpful for both healthcare professionals and individuals. The proposed hybrid fuzzy-genetic algorithm approach outperforms the traditional stochastic models and other meta-heuristics approaches in terms of take uncertainty to the next level as well as provide real-time staffing optimization. The model's adapting capability to dynamic situations where patient flow is changing makes it a major advantage over simple models which are static and cannot adjust to variable demands in real life healthcare settings.

Conclusion

The FB-TDMS-MQ is a combination of fuzzy logic and GA that is suggested as a way to optimize healthcare staffing in real time. The real-time optimization strategy lets hospitals modify their personnel as needed based on real-time data collected throughout each shift. This method keeps the staffing model flexible enough to handle fluctuations in patient demand without having to modify it every day, which would be problematic for operations.

Fuzzy logic, GAs, and advanced queueing systems for staffing are considered more effective than deterministic ones. Fuzzy sets, fuzzy rules, and a Fuzzy Inference System that operates in real time using live data have revolutionized the workforce in the healthcare field. The GA-based staff assignment approach was able to achieve the objective of minimization of patient waiting times while meeting important operational constraints. By taking into account staffing limitations, skills and shift rules the modeling made sure that all staffing configurations were not only optimized for efficiency, but feasible given real-world hospital conditions. This makes it possible for the system to adapt to new situations fast enough. This new approach involves the use of several types of medical information to help humans make better decisions.

The FB-TDMS-MQ model was tested at a multispecialty hospital, "DHRC, Jaipur," India. Validation and simulation activities show that the model successfully reduces average wait times, optimizes healthcare resource use, and improves operational flow. Notably, applying fuzzy logic to optimize the workforce in real time resulted in an impressive 72.73% reduction in average waiting times, demonstrating effective demand management. The average patient wait time decreased by 50.2% after the optimal staffing strategy, determined by GA, was implemented. This indicates that the model provides a reliable prediction of hospital performance. The simulation results exceeded initial expectations of the FB-TDMS-MQ model. Sensitivity analysis confirmed that the model can adapt to hospitals of various sizes and socio-economic conditions, making it a versatile tool to enhance healthcare system efficiency. It also shows that the model can handle uncertainties in arrival rates, service times, and staff fluctuations. Adjusting staff levels when necessary may be essential to maintaining smooth operations, especially when service request start and end times vary. The proposed solution offers flexible support in dynamic healthcare environments by effectively managing patient flow and staffing based on specific situations.

Thus, the FB-TDMS-MQ model has a number of advantages over current techniques such as OptQueue. A combination of Fuzzy logic and GA can adjust staffing in real-time, considering the uncertainties and dynamic patient supply and demand, by integrating the two planning methods, so it is more applicable to the practical healthcare environments, and more flexible than static optimization models. This study shows the potential for the FB-TDMS-MQ model to be used for optimal staffing for various healthcare environments.
