Spectral Properties of the Logarithmic Laplacian with Indefinite Weights
Rakesh Arora, Tuhina Mukherjee, Arshi Vaishnavi

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate a weighted eigenvalue problem driven by the Logarithmic Laplacian with indefinite weights. We prove the existence of an unbounded sequence of Lusternik-Schnirelman eigenvalues and show that the first eigenvalue is simple, with the associated eigenfunction having constant sign in the domain. In contrast, eigenfunctions corresponding to higher eigenvalues necessarily change sign. We further establish a nodal domain type inequality relating the higher eigenvalues to the measure of the positive and negative parts of the corresponding eigenfunctions, which is of independent interest. As an application, we prove that the first eigenvalue is isolated. In addition, we obtain alternative variational characterizations of the first and second eigenvalues and establish monotonicity properties of the eigenvalues with respect to both the weight function and the domain.

35P05, 35S15, 35A15, 35R09.

Logarithmic Laplacian, Indefinite weight, Eigenvalue problem.

1.
Introduction

Nonlocal operators have become central in the analysis of phenomena involving long-range interactions. In contrast to classical local operators, their action depends on values of a function over the entire domain, making them particularly suitable for modeling anomalous diffusion, phase transitions and Lévy-type stochastic processes. This intrinsic nonlocality leads to rich mathematical structures but also introduces substantial analytical challenges, requiring the development of generalized functional frameworks and new variational techniques.

A fundamental example is the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
, which arises naturally in probability theory, harmonic analysis and partial differential equations. For
s\in(0,1)
, it is defined by (see
[
23
, Section 3]
)

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=c(N,s)\,\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}~dy,

where
c(N,s):=\frac{2^{2s}\pi^{\frac{-N}{2}}s\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(1-s)}
is a normalization constant.

More recently, attention has turned to operators of logarithmic type, which can be interpreted as limiting cases of fractional operators. In particular, the Logarithmic Laplacian
L_{\Delta}
arises as the first-order expansion of
(-\Delta)^{s}
as
s\to 0^{+}

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=u(x)+sL_{\Delta}u(x)+o(s)\quad\text{in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\;1<p\leq\infty.

For
u\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it admits the integral representation (see
[
18
]
)

L_{\Delta}u(x)=c_{N}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{1}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}~dy-c_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{1}(x)}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}~dy+\rho_{N}u(x),

where
c_{N}:=\pi^{-N/2}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)
,
\rho_{N}:=2\ln 2+\Psi\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)-\gamma
,
\Psi:=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}
is the Digamma function and
\gamma=-\Gamma^{\prime}(1)
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant. The operator was systematically introduced in
[
18
]
and its analytical properties have since been investigated in a growing body of literature, see, for instance,
[
4
,
6
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
27
,
32
]
.

Eigenvalue problems for both local and nonlocal operators are fundamental in understanding qualitative properties of solutions. Eigenvalues reflect important analytical properties of the underlying differential operator and the domain, including positivity, boundary effects and qualitative behavior of solutions (see, for instance,
[
10
,
20
]
). They play a key role in variational methods and spectral theory (see
[
30
,
45
]
). For these reasons, the study of eigenvalue problems has become an essential part of the theory of partial differential equations.

On the other hand, weighted eigenvalue problems have been widely investigated for both local and nonlocal operators, including the classical Laplacian
[
1
,
2
,
12
,
46
]
, the
p
-Laplacian
[
21
]
and the fractional
p
-Laplacian
[
37
]
. From a theoretical perspective, the presence of a weight function enriches the spectral structure of the problem and leads to new analytical challenges. The weight influences the distribution of eigenvalues, the behavior of eigenfunctions and the associated variational framework (see, for instance,
[
13
,
34
]
). Such problems also play a central role in the study of nonlinear and nonlocal equations, since the spectral properties of the underlying operator strongly influence the existence, multiplicity and qualitative behavior of solutions.

For the fractional Laplacian, the eigenvalue problem

(-\Delta)^{s}u=\lambda g(x)u\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega

has been extensively studied in recent years. In the case
g\equiv 1
, the problem was first introduced by Lindgren and Lindqvist in
[
41
]
and subsequently investigated by several authors; see, for instance,
[
9
,
28
,
38
]
. Among the main developments, the existence of an infinite sequence of eigenvalues diverging to infinity was established in
[
38
]
. Further qualitative properties have also been obtained, including the simplicity of the first eigenvalue and a variational characterization of the second eigenvalue
[
9
]
, nodal properties of higher eigenfunctions
[
41
]
and Weyl-type asymptotic estimates
[
38
]
.

The weighted case was later considered in
[
37
]
, under the assumption that
g\in L^{\frac{N}{2s}}(\Omega)
with
g^{+}\not\equiv 0
. Using the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-cohomological index of Fadell and Rabinowitz, the authors proved the existence of an unbounded sequence of minimax eigenvalues, with the first eigenvalue being simple and isolated. In addition, they showed that higher eigenfunctions are nodal and established monotonicity properties of the first two eigenvalues with respect to the weight
g
and the domain
\Omega
. We also refer to
[
7
,
35
,
36
]
for further results on related weighted nonlocal eigenvalue problems. In contrast to the fractional Laplacian, the spectral theory of the Logarithmic Laplacian is still comparatively less developed.

For the Logarithmic Laplacian, the eigenvalue problem

L_{\Delta}u=\lambda u\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega

has attracted increasing attention in recent years. In
[
18
]
, it was shown that, on bounded domains, the operator
L_{\Delta}
possesses a discrete spectrum together with a complete orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions. Moreover, the asymptotic expansion

\lambda_{1,s}=1+s\,\lambda_{1,L}+o(s)\quad\text{as }s\to 0^{+},

where
\lambda_{1,s}
and
\lambda_{1,L}
denote the first eigenvalues of the fractional and Logarithmic Laplacians, respectively, establishes a connection between the corresponding spectral theories. Subsequent developments include the convergence of eigenpairs as
s\to 0^{+}

[
29
]
, as well as spectral estimates such as Weyl-type asymptotics and eigenvalue bounds
[
16
,
40
]
. A fundamental feature distinguishing the Logarithmic Laplacian from the fractional Laplacian is, the first eigenvalue of the Logarithmic Laplacian may become non-positive and failure of maximum principle on sufficiently large domains (see
[
18
,
16
]
).

Motivated by the analytical challenges associated with the Logarithmic Laplacian, in this paper we investigate the weighted eigenvalue problem

L_{\Delta}u=\lambda\omega(x)u\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded domain and
\omega:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is a sign-changing weight function.

The aim of the present work is to develop a variational and spectral framework for problem (
1.1
) while addressing the new challenges arising from nonlocal nature of the operator
L_{\Delta}
and from the presence of the sign-changing weights.

The nonlocal nature of the operator
L_{\Delta}
, together with the indefinite nature of the associated quadratic form (see below (
2.1
)), gives rise to several analytical and technical difficulties that prevent a direct adaptation of the methods developed for the classical and fractional Laplacians. In particular, the bilinear form may change sign due to the presence of a nonlocal contribution involving interactions outside the unit ball, for which no definite sign information is available. As a consequence, the associated energy functional may fail to be bounded from below, creating substantial obstacles in the variational analysis of the minimization problem related to the Logarithmic Laplacian.

Another challenging problem arises when considering eigenvalue problems with sign-changing weights. In contrast to the unweighted setting considered in
[
18
]
, the associated energy functional may no longer be bounded from below, even under weighted normalization constraints. As a consequence, minimizing sequences may escape to infinity, leading to a loss of compactness, thereby obstructing the use of standard variational arguments. These difficulties reveal the necessity of imposing appropriate structural assumptions on the weight in order to establish a suitable variational framework. We refer the reader to the next section for a detailed presentation on the weight function assumptions, the principal difficulties arising in the analysis of the weighted eigenvalue problem (
1.1
) and comparison of our findings with the existing literature.

Outline of the paper:
The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary definitions, notations and preliminary material required for the subsequent analysis and state the main results. In Section
3
, we prove the existence of a sequence of eigenvalues of (
1.1
) diverging to infinity and the principal eigenvalue by means of the Rayleigh quotient coincides with Lusternik-Schnirelman eigenvalue. Section

5
, we study the simplicity and isolation of the first eigenvalue, provide an alternative variational characterization of the second eigenvalue and establish the monotonicity of eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and strict monotonicity of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
with respect to both the domain
\Omega
and the bounded weight function
\omega
. The paper ends with an Appendix
A
which contains the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2

Next, by combining the above result with the optimal compact embeddings established in

Lemma 3.3

5.
Qualitative properties and characterization of eigenvalues

5.1.
On the first eigenvalue

In this subsection, we prove some properties of the first eigenvalue.

Proposition 5.1
.

The first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
is simple.

Proof.

Let
u,v
be two eigenfunctions corresponding to
\lambda_{1}.
Since
u,v
do not change sign in
\Omega,
we can assume
u\geq 0
and
v\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega.
Set
z=\left(\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega)
. It is easy to see that
\int_{\Omega}\omega(x)z^{2}=1,

i.e.,

z\in M
.
Using
[
41
, Lemma 13]
, we have

|z(y)-z(x)|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}|u(y)-u(x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|v(y)-v(x)|^{2}

with equality only when
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfies
u(x)v(y)=u(y)v(x).

By Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality, we have

u(x)u(y)+v(x)v(y)\leq\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(y),

i.e.
,

\frac{u(x)u(y)}{2}+\frac{v(x)v(y)}{2}\leq z(x)z(y),

with equality only when
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfies
u(x)=\alpha u(y)
and
v(x)=\alpha v(y),

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

Thus,

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{L}(z,z)&=\frac{c_{N}}{2}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{|z(x)-z(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy-c_{N}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{z(x)z(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\rho_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{2}~dx\\
&\leq\frac{c_{N}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy+\frac{1}{2}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{|v(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy\right)\\
&\quad-\frac{c_{N}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy+\frac{1}{2}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{v(x)v(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+\rho_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{u^{2}}{2}+\frac{v^{2}}{2}\right)~dx\\
&=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)}{2}+\frac{\mathcal{E}_{L}(v,v)}{2}=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}=\lambda_{1}.\end{split}

Moreover, by the definition of
\lambda_{1}
, we get
\lambda_{1}\leq\mathcal{E}_{L}(z,z)\leq\lambda_{1},
which implies,
\mathcal{E}_{L}(z,z)=\lambda_{1}.
Hence,

\frac{u(x)}{u(y)}=\frac{v(x)}{v(y)}=\alpha,

which further implies,
u(x)=\alpha v(x).
This proves that
\lambda_{1}
is simple.
∎

ω

Thus,

\inf_{A\in F_{2}}\sup_{u\in A}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)\leq\sup_{u\in A}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)=\max_{\delta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathcal{E}_{L}(f(\delta),f(\delta)).

Taking the infimum over all
f\in\mathcal{C}
, we obtain

\lambda_{2}\leq\tilde{\lambda}_{2}.

(5.3)

Next, we prove that

\tilde{\lambda}_{2}=\min\{\lambda>\lambda_{1}:\lambda\text{ is an eigenvalue of \eqref{lambda}}\}.

By
[
22
, Proposition 2.7]
,
\tilde{\lambda}_{2}
is an eigenvalue of (
1.1
). Using Proposition
5.3
and (
5.3
), we get
\lambda_{1}<\tilde{\lambda}_{2}
.
Let
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
be an eigenvalue and
u
be a corresponding eigenfunction.
By Theorem
4.2
, we have
u^{\pm}\neq 0
.
Next, we claim that

\mathcal{E}_{L}(u^{+},u^{-})\leq 0

(5.4)

Using (
4.5
) and the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{L}(\cdot,\cdot)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u^{+})&\geq\frac{c_{N}}{2}{\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\leq 1\end{subarray}}}\frac{|u^{+}(x)-u^{+}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy+\rho_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u^{+})^{2}~dx\\
&\qquad\qquad-c_{N}{\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\geq 1\end{subarray}}}\frac{u^{+}(x)u^{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy=\mathcal{E}_{L}(u^{+},u^{+})\end{split}

which implies the required claim.

Let
\delta=(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
Taking
v=u^{\pm}
in (
2.1
), using
u=u^{+}-u^{-}
and the fact that
u^{+}u^{-}=0
, we get

\mathcal{E}_{L}(u^{+}-u^{-},u^{+})=\lambda\int_{\Omega}\omega(x)(u^{+})^{2}~dx,\quad\mathcal{E}_{L}(u^{+}-u^{-},u^{-})=-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\omega(x)(u^{-})^{2}~dx.

In above, multiplying the first relation by
\delta_{1}^{2}
, the second by
\delta_{2}^{2}
and subtracting, we get

\lambda\left(\delta_{1}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\omega(x)(u^{+})^{2}~dx+\delta_{2}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\omega(x)(u^{-})^{2}~dx\right)=\delta_{1}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u^{+})-\delta_{2}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u^{-}).

Now, using the bilinearity of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
and (
5.4
), we obtain

\begin{split}\lambda&\left(\delta_{1}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\omega(x)(u^{+})^{2}~dx+\delta_{2}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\omega(x)(u^{-})^{2}~dx\right)=\delta_{1}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u^{+})-\delta_{2}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u^{-})\\
&=\delta_{1}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u^{+},u^{+})-(\delta_{1}^{2}+\delta_{2}^{2})\mathcal{E}_{L}(u^{-},u^{+})+\delta_{2}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u^{-},u^{-})\\
&\geq\delta_{1}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u^{+},u^{+})+\delta_{2}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u^{-},u^{-})-2\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u^{-},u^{+})\\
&=\mathcal{E}_{L}((\delta_{1}u^{+}-\delta_{2}u^{-}),(\delta_{1}u^{+}-\delta_{2}u^{-})).\end{split}

Define
f:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to M
as

f(\delta)=\left(\frac{\delta_{1}u^{+}-\delta_{2}u^{-}}{\left(\delta_{1}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\omega(x)(u^{+})^{2}~dx+\delta_{2}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\omega(x)(u^{-})^{2}~dx\right)^{1/2}}\right).

Then,
f\in\mathcal{C}
,

\int_{\Omega}\omega(x)(f(\delta))^{2}~dx=1\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{L}(f(\delta),f(\delta))\leq\lambda.

Taking the maximum over
\mathbb{S}^{1}
and infimum over
\mathcal{C}
, we obtain

\tilde{\lambda}_{2}\leq\lambda.

Combining this with (
5.3
), we get the claimed result.
∎

5.3.
Monotonicity of eigenvalues

In this subsection, we prove that the eigenvalues obtained in Theorem
3.7
are monotonic
w.r.t.
the weight function
\omega
and the domain
\Omega
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\omega_{1},\ \omega_{2}
be weight functions satisfying (

ω
0

)-(

ω
2

) such that
\omega_{1}(x)\leq\omega_{2}(x)
a.e. in
\Omega
.
Then for every
k\geq 1
, we have

\lambda_{k}(\omega_{2})\leq\lambda_{k}(\omega_{1}).

Proof.

Denote
M_{\omega}:=\{u\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\omega(x)u^{2}~dx=1\}.
Since
\omega_{1}\leq\omega_{2}
, then for
u\in M_{\omega_{1}}
we have

1\leq\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)u^{2}~dx.

For
u\in M_{\omega_{1}}
, define

v=\frac{u}{\left(\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)u^{2}~dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\quad\text{such that}\ v\in M_{\omega_{2}}

Moreover,

\mathcal{E}_{L}(v,v)=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)}{\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)u^{2}~dx}\leq\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u).

Let
A\subset\{u\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\omega_{1}(x)u^{2}~dx=1\}
be a closed, symmetric set with genus
\gamma(A)\geq k
.
Define

B=\left\{\frac{u}{\left(\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)u^{2}~dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}:u\in A\right\}.

Note that since
A
is symmetric,
B
is also symmetric. It is easy to deduce that
B\subseteq A
and
B\subseteq\{u\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)u^{2}~dx=1\}
is closed. Consider the map
T:A\to\mathbb{H}(\Omega)
defined as

T(u)=\frac{u}{\sqrt{\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)u^{2}~dx}}.

It is easy to see that
T
is odd, continuous and
T(A)=B.
By Lemma
2.11
(ii), we get
\gamma(T(A))=\gamma(B)\geq\gamma(A)\geq k
.
Hence

\sup_{v\in B}\mathcal{E}_{L}(v,v)\leq\sup_{u\in A}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u).

Taking the infimum over all such sets, we obtain
\lambda_{k}(\omega_{2})\leq\lambda_{k}(\omega_{1}).

∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\Omega_{1}\subsetneq\Omega_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be bounded domains. Then, it holds that

\lambda_{k}(\Omega_{2})\leq\lambda_{k}(\Omega_{1})\quad\text{for all }k\geq 1.

Proof.

Note that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the admissible sets satisfies

F_{k,\Omega_{2}}\subset F_{k,\Omega_{1}}

where
F_{k,\Omega}
is defined as

F_{k,\Omega}:=\{A\subseteq M_{\Omega}\,:\,A=-A\ \text{is closed },\ \gamma(A)\geq k\}

and

M_{\Omega}:=\{u\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega)\,:\,J(u)=1\}.

Finally, the proof follows from the variational characterization of eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
defined in (
2.5
).
∎

In contrast, the previous argument cannot, in general, be extended to establish strict decreasing monotonicity, due to the lack of compactness of the sets
A\in F_{k}
. However, using the alternative characterizations of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
established in Theorem
3.8
and Proposition
5.4
respectively, we derive the strict monotonicity properties of the first and second eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}

w.r.t.
the bounded weight function
\omega
and the domain
\Omega
.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
\omega_{1},\ \omega_{2}
be bounded weight functions such that
\omega_{1}\leq\omega_{2}
in
\Omega
,
\omega_{1}\neq\omega_{2}
. Then,

\lambda_{1}(\omega_{1})>\lambda_{1}(\omega_{2}).

Proof.

Let
e_{1,\omega_{1}}
be the eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{1}(\omega_{1}).
By Proposition
4.1
and
[
39
, Theorem 1.1]
,
e_{1,\omega_{1}}>0
. Since
\omega_{2}>\omega_{1}
on a subset of
\Omega
with positive measure and
e_{1,\omega_{1}}>0
in
\Omega
, we have

\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)e_{1,\omega_{1}}^{2}~dx>\int_{\Omega}\omega_{1}(x)e_{1,\omega_{1}}^{2}~dx=1.

Setting

v=\frac{e_{1,\omega_{1}}}{\left(\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)e_{1,\omega_{1}}^{2}~dx\right)^{1/2}}.

Then,
\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)v^{2}=1
. By (
2.5
), we have

\lambda_{1}(\omega_{2})\leq\mathcal{E}_{L}(v,v)=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{L}(e_{1,\omega_{1}},e_{1,\omega_{1}})}{\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)e_{1,\omega_{1}}^{2}~dx}<\frac{\mathcal{E}_{L}(e_{1,\omega_{1}},e_{1,\omega_{1}})}{\int_{\Omega}\omega_{1}(x)e_{1,\omega_{1}}^{2}~dx}=\mathcal{E}_{L}(e_{1,\omega_{1}},e_{1,\omega_{1}})=\lambda_{1}(\omega_{1}),

which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
be bounded weight functions such that
\omega_{1}<\omega_{2}
in
\Omega
. Then,

\lambda_{2}(\omega_{1})>\lambda_{2}(\omega_{2}).

Proof.

For any
\lambda>\lambda_{1}(\omega_{1})
, we claim that

m_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\lambda}>1,

(5.5)

where

m_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\lambda}=\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)u^{2}~dx:\int_{\Omega}\omega_{1}(x)u^{2}~dx=1,\ \mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)\leq\lambda\right\}.

Note that
m_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\lambda}
is well defined due to (
2.5
).
Since
\omega_{1}<\omega_{2}
in
\Omega
, we have
m_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\lambda}\geq 1
. Let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\{u\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\omega_{1}(x)u^{2}~dx=1\}
be a minimizing sequence for
m_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\lambda}
. Then, by using (

ω
1

) and (
3.1
), we get
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
\mathbb{H}(\Omega)
. Thus,

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{in }\mathbb{H}(\Omega),\text{ upto a subsequence}.

Moreover, by using the weak lower semicontinuity of
\mathbb{H}(\Omega)
norm, Lemma
3.3
(i), (
3.9
) and Theorem
2.6
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{L}(u_{n},u_{n})\leq\lambda,

\int_{\Omega}\omega_{1}(x)u^{2}~dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\omega_{1}(x)u_{n}^{2}~dx=1,

and

\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)u^{2}~dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)u_{n}^{2}~dx=m_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\lambda}.

Using this and
\omega_{1}<\omega_{2}
in
\Omega
, we get

m_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\lambda}=\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)u^{2}~dx>\int_{\Omega}\omega_{1}(x)u^{2}~dx=1,

which proves (
5.5
).
Now, by Proposition
5.5
, we have
\lambda_{2}(\omega_{2})\leq\lambda_{2}(\omega_{1})
. By Proposition
5.4
there exists
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{C^{\prime}},
the set of all odd continuous maps from
\mathbb{S}^{1}
to
\{u\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\omega_{1}(x)u^{2}~dx=1\}
such that

\max_{\delta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathcal{E}_{L}(f_{n}(\delta),f_{n}(\delta))\leq\lambda_{2}(\omega_{1})+\frac{1}{n}.

Since
\omega_{1}<\omega_{2}
in
\Omega
, we have

\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)(f_{n}(\delta))^{2}~dx>\int_{\Omega}\omega_{1}(x)(f_{n}(\delta))^{2}~dx=1\quad\text{for every }\delta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, let

\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta):=\frac{f_{n}(\delta)}{\left(\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)(f_{n}(\delta))^{2}~dx\right)^{1/2}}.

Then,
\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)(f_{n}(\delta))^{2}~dx=1.

It is easy to see that
\tilde{f}_{n}\in\mathcal{C^{\prime\prime}},
the set of all odd continuous maps from
\mathbb{S}^{1}
to
\{u\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)u^{2}~dx=1\}.
Since
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is compact, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find
\delta_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that

\mathcal{E}_{L}(\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta_{n}),\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta_{n}))=\max_{\delta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathcal{E}_{L}(\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta),\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta)).

Using
\lambda_{2}(\omega_{1})+1>\lambda_{1}(\omega_{1})
, by Proposition
5.4
and the above estimate, we have

\lambda_{2}(\omega_{2})\leq\mathcal{E}_{L}(\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta_{n}),\tilde{f}_{n}(\delta_{n}))=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{L}(f_{n}(\delta_{n}),f_{n}(\delta_{n}))}{\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}(x)(f_{n}(\delta_{n}))^{2}~dx}\leq\frac{\lambda_{2}(\omega_{1})+\frac{1}{n}}{m_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\lambda_{2}(\omega_{1})+1}}.

Passing to the limit as

and using (

), we get

\lambda_{2}(\omega_{2})\leq\frac{\lambda_{2}(\omega_{1})}{m_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\lambda_{2}(\omega_{1})+1}}<\lambda_{2}(\omega_{1}),

we get the claimed result.
∎

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
\omega\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\Omega_{1},\ \Omega_{2}
be bounded domains in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
\Omega_{1}
is a proper subset of
\Omega_{2}
. Then,

\lambda_{1}(\Omega_{2})<\lambda_{1}(\Omega_{1}).

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega_{1})
be a positive eigenfunction associated to
\lambda_{1}(\Omega_{1}).

Note that
v=\frac{u}{\sqrt{\int_{\Omega_{2}}\omega(x)\tilde{u}^{2}~dx}}\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega_{2})
and using (
2.5
), we obtain

\lambda_{1}(\Omega_{2})\leq\mathcal{E}_{L}\left(v,v\right)=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)}{\int_{\Omega_{2}}\omega(x)u^{2}~dx}=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)}{\int_{\Omega_{1}}\omega(x)u^{2}~dx}=\lambda_{1}(\Omega_{1}).

The equality holds only if
u
is an eigenfunction associated to
\lambda_{1}(\Omega_{2})
. This is not possible because

|\{x\in\Omega_{2}:\tilde{u}(x)=0\}|>0,

which contradicts the fact that the first eigenfunction is strictly positive in
\Omega
(in view of Proposition
4.1
and the strong maximum principle
[
39
, Theorem 1.1]
). Hence, the required claim.

∎

Appendix A

In this section, we establish a Hardy-type inequality for the operator
L_{\Delta}
, as stated in Theorem
3.1
. The proof is based on differentiating the following Hardy inequality (see
[
24
, inequality (1.2)]
) with respect to the parameter
s
at
s=0
. For any
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{2^{2s}\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{N+2s}{4}\right)}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{N-2s}{4}\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2s}}~dx\leq c_{N,s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}~dx~dy,\end{split}

(1.1)

where
c_{N,s}:=\frac{2^{2s}\pi^{\frac{-N}{2}}s\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(1-s)}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
:

Let
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}.
Note that,

\begin{split}\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{2^{2s}\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{N+2s}{4}\right)}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{N-2s}{4}\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2s}}~dx\right)&=\frac{2^{2s}\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{N+2s}{4}\right)}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{N-2s}{4}\right)}\bigg(2\ln 2+\Psi(\frac{N+2s}{4})\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\Psi(\frac{N-2s}{4})\bigg)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2s}}~dx\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac{2^{2s+1}\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{N+2s}{4}\right)}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{N-2s}{4}\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}\ln|x|}{|x|^{2s}}~dx.\end{split}

Applying Taylor’s expansion about
s=0
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{2^{2s}\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{N+2s}{4}\right)}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{N-2s}{4}\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2s}}~dx&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}~dx+s\bigg[2\bigg(\ln 2+\Psi(\frac{N}{4})\bigg)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}~dx\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\ln|x|~dx\bigg]+o(s).\end{split}

(1.2)

Using Lemma
[
33
, Lemma 3.7]
, we get

c_{N,s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}~dx~dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}~dx+s\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)+o(s).

(1.3)

Substituting (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) in (
1.1
), we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}~dx+s\bigg[2\bigg(\ln 2+\Psi(\frac{N}{4})\bigg)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}~dx-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\ln|x|~dx\bigg]+o(s)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}~dx+s\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u)+o(s).\end{split}

Thus,

2\bigg(\ln 2+\Psi(\frac{N}{4})\bigg)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}~dx-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\ln|x|~dx\leq\mathcal{E}_{L}(u,u).

Hence, the claim follows for all non zero compactly supported functions in
\mathbb{R}^{N}.
The inequality for every
u\in\mathbb{H}(\Omega)
follows by density arguments (
[
18
, Theorem 3.1]
) and the fact that
\Omega
is bounded.

∎
