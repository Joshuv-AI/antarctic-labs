Finite-energy solutions to Einstein-scalar field Lichnerowicz equations on complete Riemannian manifolds
Bartosz Bieganowski, Pietro d’Avenia, Jacopo Schino

Abstract
. We consider the singular elliptic problem of the form − Δ ​ u + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ u = ℬ ⁡ ( x ) ​ | u | 2 ∗ − 2 ​ u + 𝒜 ⁡ ( x ) | u | 2 ∗ ​ u , u ∈ H 1 ​ ( M ) , -\Delta u+V(x)u=\mathcal{B}(x)|u|^{2^{*}-2}u+\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)}{|u|^{2^{*}}u},\qquad u\in H^{1}(M), where the coefficients are allowed to have low regularity. Under natural spectral assumptions on − Δ + V -\Delta+V , geometric assumptions on the manifold M M ensuring the Sobolev embedding H 1 ​ ( M ) ↪ L 2 ∗ ​ ( M ) H^{1}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(M) , and a suitable global integrability/smallness condition involving 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , ℬ \mathcal{B} , and a function ψ ∈ H 1 ​ ( M ) \psi\in H^{1}(M) , we prove the existence of a nonnegative finite-energy supersolution. If, in addition, the Ricci curvature is nonnegative and ℬ ≥ 0 \mathcal{B}\geq 0 , we obtain a positive finite-energy solution. The proof relies on a family of ε \varepsilon -regularized problems, mountain pass arguments, and a limiting procedure in which Harnack’s inequality plays a crucial role in handling the singular term on noncompact manifolds. We also prove a nonexistence result showing that the global integrability condition on 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is, in a precise sense, necessary for the existence of nonnegative supersolutions.

1.
Introduction

The Einstein field equations, introduced by A. Einstein (in their original form) in 1915 (see
[
12
,
13
]
), describe the relation between the geometry of spacetime and the distribution of matter in this spacetime. Einstein-scalar field theories have seen significant developments in recent years, including attempts to explain the acceleration of the expansion of the universe (see
[
24
,
25
]
).

The initial data in the analysis of the Einstein-scalar field equations, whose solutions describe the dynamics of the spacetime, must satisfy certain constraint conditions, which are the Gauss and Codazzi equations.
More precisely, let
(M,\widetilde{g})
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension
N\geq 3
without boundary. The Gauss and Codazzi equations read as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Scal}_{\widetilde{g}}-|K|_{\widetilde{g}}^{2}+(\mathrm{Tr}_{\widetilde{g}}K)^{2}-2\rho=0,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\widetilde{g}}\cdot K-\nabla_{\widetilde{g}}\mathrm{Tr}_{\widetilde{g}}K-J=0,

where
K
is the second fundamental form,
\mathrm{Scal}_{\widetilde{g}}
denotes the scalar curvature,
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
, and
J
is a vector field on
M
. Using the conformal method (see
[
18
]
), i.e., changing the metric
\widetilde{g}=u^{\frac{4}{N-2}}g
, the Gauss and Codazzi equations may be rewritten as the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints (see e.g.
[
5
]
for detailed computations). The Hamiltonian constraint is then of the form

(1.1)

-\Delta_{g}u+Vu=\mathcal{B}u^{2^{*}-1}+\frac{\mathcal{A}}{u^{2^{*}+1}},\quad u>0,

which is called the
Einstein-scalar field Lichnerowicz equation
, where
\Delta_{g}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the manifold
(M,g)
and
2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-2}
.

A good understanding of the behaviour of equation (
1.1
) is crucial, and the existence and qualitative properties of positive solutions to (
1.1
) play a fundamental role.
In the case of a compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, equation (
1.1
) and its variants have been widely studied and are well understood
[
5
,
9
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
20
,
22
,
7
,
6
,
10
,
23
]
. However, the analysis of (
1.1
) in the case of a noncompact domain
M
is a challenging problem. One can look for solutions using the sub- and supersolutions method (see e.g.
[
19
]
), but in this case, one does not find finite-energy solutions (see Definition
1.1
below). The noncompact case was studied also in
[
1
]
, where asymptotically Euclidean manifolds were considered.

We mention here that some variants of singular-type equations (
1.1
), equipped with appropriate boundary conditions, were also studied in the case
M=\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
\Omega
is a bounded domain. We refer the reader to
[
2
,
3
,
8
,
11
,
21
]
.

Having this motivation in mind, in this paper, we fix a complete, smooth,
N
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
without boundary, with
N\geq 3
. Let us underline that
M
may be noncompact.
We consider the following equation

(1.2)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=\mathcal{B}(x)|u|^{2^{*}-2}u+\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)}{|u|^{2^{*}}u},\quad u\in H^{1}(M),

where
H^{1}(M)
denotes the space of functions
u\in L^{2}(M)
such that
|\nabla u|\in L^{2}(M)
– cf. Subsection
2.1
. Here and in the sequel,
\nabla u
denotes the covariant derivative of
u
,
|\nabla u|^{2}=g^{ij}(\nabla u)_{i}(\nabla u)_{j}
, and
\Delta
the Laplace–Beltrami operator.

We impose the following assumptions.

(A)

\mathcal{A}\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(M)
,
\mathcal{A}\geq 0
, and
\mathcal{A}\not=0
.

(B)

\mathcal{B}\in L^{2^{*}}_{\textup{loc}}(M)
and
\mathcal{B}_{+}:=\max\{\mathcal{B},0\}\not=0
.

(V)

V\in L^{\infty}(M)
and
\inf\sigma(-\Delta+V)>0
, where
\sigma
denotes the spectrum of the operator
-\Delta+V
on
L^{2}(M)
.

Clearly, (
(V)
) is satisfied if
V\in L^{\infty}(M)
and
\essinf V>0
.

We collect some definitions that will be used throughout the paper.

Definition 1.1
.

If
u\in H^{1}(M)
satisfies, for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
,

(1.3)

\int_{M}\left[|\mathcal{B}(x)||u|^{2^{*}-1}|\varphi|+\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)|\varphi|}{|u|^{2^{*}+1}}\right]\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}<+\infty,

where

\int_{M}\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)\varphi}{|u|^{2^{*}}u}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}:=\int_{\supp\mathcal{A}}\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)\varphi}{|u|^{2^{*}}u}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g},

then we say that
u
is a
supersolution
of (
1.2
) if and only if, for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
with
\varphi\geq 0
,

(1.4)

\int_{M}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}+\int_{M}V(x)u\varphi\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\geq\int_{M}\mathcal{B}(x)|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\varphi\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}+\int_{M}\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)\varphi}{|u|^{2^{*}}u}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g};

likewise, we say that
u
is a
solution
of (
1.2
) if and only if, for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
, equality holds in (
1.4
).

Next, we say that
u\in H^{1}(M)
has
finite energy
if and only if

\int_{M}\left[|\mathcal{B}(x)||u|^{2^{*}}+\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)}{|u|^{2^{*}}}\right]\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}<+\infty.

Finally, we say that
u\in H^{1}(M)
is
positive
if and only if
\essinf_{B}u>0
for every geodesic ball
B\subset M
.

Note that the finiteness of

\int_{M}|\mathcal{B}(x)||u|^{2^{*}-1}|\varphi|\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}

in (
1.3
) is justified because, as recalled in Subsection
2.1
, the embedding
H^{1}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(M)
need not hold.

At least formally, finite-energy solutions to (
1.2
) are critical points of the energy functional

(1.5)

I(u)=\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{M}\mathcal{B}(x)|u|^{2^{*}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}+\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{M}\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)}{|u|^{2^{*}}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}.

Since there are functions in
H^{1}(M)
such that

\int_{M}\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)}{|u|^{2^{*}}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}=+\infty,

following
[
16
]
, we introduce an approximated problem and solve it via variational methods.

Throughout the manuscript,
S>0
denotes the optimal constant (which depends on
V
) in the embedding
H^{1}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(M)
whenever it holds, cf. Subsection
2.1
.
Moreover, for a function
v\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
v_{+}:=\max\{v,0\}
(respectively,
v_{-}=\max\{-v,0\}
) denotes the positive (respectively, negative) part of
v
, and
B(x,R)
denotes the geodesic ball centered at
x\in M
of radius
R>0
.

Our first result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that the Ricci curvature of
(M,g)
is bounded from below and

(1.6)

\inf_{x\in M}\mathrm{vol}_{g}(B(x,1))>0.

If (
(A)
), (
(B)
), and (
(V)
) hold and there exist
K>0
,
\Theta>0
, and
\psi\in H^{1}(M)
such that

(1.7)

\mathcal{B}\in L^{\infty}(\supp\psi)\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{M}\mathcal{B}(x)|\psi|^{2^{*}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}>0,

(1.8)

\|\psi\|^{2^{*}}\int_{M}\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)}{|\psi|^{2^{*}}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\leq\frac{K}{(|\mathcal{B}_{+}|_{L^{\infty}(\supp\psi)}S)^{N-1}},

and

(1.9)

N\Theta^{2}+(N-2)\frac{|\mathcal{B}_{-}|_{L^{\infty}(\supp\psi)}}{|\mathcal{B}_{+}|_{L^{\infty}(\supp\psi)}}\Theta^{2^{*}}+(N-2)\frac{K}{\Theta^{2^{*}}}\leq 2,

then (
1.2
) admits a nonnegative finite-energy supersolution
u\in H^{1}(M)
.
If, moreover, the Ricci curvature of
(M,g)
is nonnegative and
\mathcal{B}\geq 0
, then
u
is a positive solution to (
1.2
).

Remark 1.3
.

We observe the following facts.

(i)

For a given
\psi\in H^{1}(M)
that verifies (
1.7
), condition (
1.9
) is always satisfied by possibly non-optimal
\Theta
and
K
; see also Subsection
3.1
for a version of (
1.9
) that does not depend on
\psi
. On the other hand, when
\mathcal{B}\geq 0
in
\supp\psi
, for a given
K>0
, the minimum of the left-hand side of (
1.9
) is attained at
\Theta=K^{\frac{N-2}{4(N-1)}}
and equals
2(N-1)K^{\frac{N-2}{2(N-1)}}
. Thus, (
1.9
) holds if and only if
K\leq\bigl(\frac{1}{N-1}\bigr)^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-2}}
. Considering (
1.8
), the optimal value for
K
is
\bigl(\frac{1}{N-1}\bigr)^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-2}}
, which gives a wider range than
[
16
, formula (3.3)]
, where, with the notations of this paper,
K=\bigl(\frac{1}{2(N-1)}\bigr)^{\frac{N}{N-2}}\frac{1}{N-2}
.

(ii)

The reason why we need the assumption
\mathcal{B}\geq 0
to find a solution to (
1.2
) is related to the fact that the
L^{\infty}
norm of a solution to a critical equation need not be controlled by its
H^{1}
norm. See Remark
3.7
for further details.

(iii)

The integrability of
\mathcal{A}|\psi|^{-2^{*}}
, which is a consequence of (
1.8
), is a sort of global condition on
\mathcal{A}
, which is not ensured by (
(A)
) or its stronger variant (
(A1)
) below. It is also a necessary condition for the existence of nonnegative supersolutions, as stated in Theorem
1.7
below.

Let us discuss conditions (
1.7
)-(
1.8
) in a series of examples. Specifically, Example
1.5
demonstrates that such conditions may hold without global assumptions about
\mathcal{B}
. Furthermore, even in the case of a compact manifold

, (
(A)
) and (
(B)
) are weaker than what one would typically need in a variational approach, cf. (

) and (

); in particular, we require far less than
\mathcal{A}
,

, and

to be smooth, as done in
[

]

Example 1.4
.

Suppose that
M=\mathbb{R}^{N}
equipped with the Euclidean metric. In addition to (
(A)
) and (
(B)
), assume that
\mathcal{B}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\mathcal{B}\geq 0
, and

decays exponentially at infinity. Then, every positive

with a polynomial decay at infinity satisfies (
1.7
) and
\mathcal{A}|\psi|^{-2^{*}}\in L^{1}(M)
. In this case, (
1.8
) holds if
|\mathcal{B}|_{L^{\infty}}
is sufficiently small or

\mathcal{A}=\theta\mathcal{A}_{0}
with
\mathcal{A}_{0}|\psi|^{-2^{*}}\in L^{1}(M)
fixed and
\theta>0
sufficiently small.

Example 1.5
.

Assume that the Ricci curvature of
(M,g)
is bounded from below and (
1.6
) holds. Let
B_{1},B_{2}\subset M
be two open geodesic balls such that
\overline{B_{1}}\subset B_{2}
. In addition to (
(A)
) and (
(B)
), assume that
\mathcal{A}
is supported in
\overline{B_{1}}

\psi\in H^{1}(M)

such that

. Then,
\mathcal{A}|\psi|^{-2^{*}}\in L^{1}(M)
. If

is nonnegative and essentially bounded in

, then (

) holds, and (
1.8
) is satisfied if
\mathcal{A}
or
\mathcal{B}
are sufficiently small in the sense of Example

.
If
\mathcal{B}
is essentially bounded but takes both positive and negative values in

, then
\psi
can be suitably chosen for (
1.7
) to hold.

Example 1.6
.

If
M
has infinite measure and
\liminf_{x\to\infty}\mathcal{A}(x)>0
(e.g., if

is constant), then, for every

\mathcal{A}|v|^{-2^{*}}\not\in L^{1}(M)

Let us briefly illustrate our strategy to prove Theorem
1.2
. Due to the singular term in (
1.2
), as anticipated earlier, we perturb the corresponding integral in

, defined in (
1.5
), via a parameter

, obtaining a family of auxiliary problems. At this stage, we work with the stronger assumptions (

) and (
(B1)
), which allow a rigorous variational formulation. After obtaining a family of solutions to the auxiliary problems, we pass to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
; here, to prove that the weak limit is a solution to the original problem, Harnack’s inequality (Proposition
2.1
) is crucial. We highlight that the possible noncompactness of
M
prevents us from using the same argument as in
[
16
]
, where a uniform positive pointwise lower bound was obtained for such a family of solutions. Finally, we approximate the functions
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
satisfying (
(A)
) and (
(B)
) with a sequence of functions satisfying (
(A1)
) and (
(B1)
) and pass again to the limit.

(

), (

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first introduce the Sobolev space
H^{1}(M)
and provide some general and useful properties about it. Then, we present some qualitative aspects of supersolutions to (
1.2
).

2.1.
Sobolev spaces on manifolds

We introduce the space
H^{1}(M)
as the completion of

C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
, the space of
C^{\infty}
functions with compact support in
M
, with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{H^{1}}:=\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\right)^{1/2}.

It is a Hilbert space
[
15
, Proposition 2.2]
with respect to the scalar product

\langle u,v\rangle_{H^{1}}:=\int_{M}\nabla u\nabla v+uv\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}.

In view of (
(V)
), we can consider the equivalent norm

\|u\|:=\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\right)^{1/2}

and the associated scalar product

\langle u,v\rangle=\int_{M}\nabla u\nabla v+V(x)uv\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}.

It is known that the embedding
H^{1}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(M)
may not hold; see, e.g.,
[
15
, Proposition 3.3]
. However, there is a characterization of complete manifolds for which such an embedding holds (
[
15
, Theorem 3.18]
). Assume that the Ricci curvature is bounded from below. Then, the embedding above holds if and only if (
1.6
) is satisfied. In particular, Sobolev embeddings hold true if the Ricci curvature is bounded from below and the injectivity radius is positive (
[
15
, Corollary 3.19]
,
[
4
, Theorem 2.21]
).
Finally,
S>0
denotes the optimal constant such that

|\varphi|_{L^{2^{*}}}^{2^{*}}\leq S\|\varphi\|^{2^{*}}\quad\text{for every }\varphi\in H^{1}(M),

i.e.,

S:=\inf_{\varphi\in H^{1}(M)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|\varphi|_{L^{2^{*}}}^{2^{*}}}{\|\varphi\|^{2^{*}}}.

2.2.
Liouville-type theorems

Proposition 2.1

(Harnack’s inequality)
.

Let
d\geq 0
and assume that the Ricci curvature of
(M,g)
is nonnegative. If
B(R)\subset M
is a geodesic ball of geodesic radius
R>0
and
q>0
is sufficiently small, then there exists
c>0
such that, for every
u\in H^{1}(M)
satisfying

u\geq 0\text{ and }-\Delta u+du\geq 0\quad\text{in }B(R),

there holds

\left(\int_{B(R/8)}u^{q}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\right)^{1/q}\leq c\inf_{B(R/16)}u.

Proof.

The inequality follows as in
[
28
, page 342]
. Indeed, since the Ricci curvature is nonnegative, from the Gromov–Bishop volume comparison theorem and the results in
[
26
]
, the assumptions of
[
28
, Lemma 3.1]
are satisfied.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The same result holds if
M
has a boundary, provided that
B(R)\cap\partial M=\emptyset
(see
[
28
, Lemma 3.1]
).

In view of Proposition
2.1
, we can argue as in
[
14
, Proof of Theorem 8.19]
, obtaining

Proposition 2.3

(Strong minimum principle)
.

Let
d\geq 0
and assume that the Ricci curvature of
(M,g)
is nonnegative. Assume, additionally, that
u\in H^{1}(M)
satisfies
-\Delta u+du\geq 0
. If there exists
\Omega\subset M
open and connected and a geodesic ball
B\subset\Omega
such that

\inf_{B}u=\inf_{\Omega}u\leq 0,

then
u
is constant in
\Omega
.

3.
\varepsilon
-approximations

In this section, in addition to (
(V)
), we consider the following stronger variants of (
(A)
) and (
(B)
):

(A1)

\mathcal{A}\in L^{1}(M)\cap L^{2N/(N+2)}(M)
,
\mathcal{A}\geq 0
, and
\mathcal{A}\not=0
;

(B1)

\mathcal{B}\in L^{\infty}(M)
and
\mathcal{B}_{+}\not=0
.

This section aims to prove the following weaker version of Theorem
1.2
. Then, in the next section, we will exploit the fact that
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
given in (
(A)
) and (
(B)
) can be approximated by functions satisfying (
(A1)
) and (

).

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that the Ricci curvature of
(M,g)
is bounded below and (
1.6
) holds. If (
(A1)
), (
(B1)
), (
(V)
), and (
1.7
)–(
1.9
) hold, then (
1.2
) admits a nonnegative finite-energy supersolution
u\in H^{1}(M)
. If, moreover, the Ricci curvature is nonnegative and
\mathcal{B}\geq 0
, then
u
is a positive solution to (
1.2
).

With the intention of proving Proposition
3.1
, consider, for
\varepsilon>0
, the auxiliary problem

(3.1)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=\mathcal{B}(x)|u|^{2^{*}-2}u+\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)u}{(\varepsilon+u^{2})^{2^{*}/2+1}},\quad u\in H^{1}(M).

Analogously to Definition
1.1
, we say that
u\in H^{1}(M)
solves (
3.1
) if, for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
,

\int_{M}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}+\int_{M}V(x)u\varphi\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}=\int_{M}\mathcal{B}(x)|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\varphi\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}+\int_{M}\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)u\varphi}{(\varepsilon+u^{2})^{2^{*}/2+1}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}.

Such solutions can be found as critical points of the functional
I_{\varepsilon}\colon H^{1}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(3.2)

I_{\varepsilon}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{M}\mathcal{B}(x)|u|^{2^{*}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}+\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{M}\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)}{(\varepsilon+u^{2})^{2^{*}/2}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,
I_{\varepsilon}
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
and, for
u,v\in H^{1}(M)
,

I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u)(v)=\int_{M}\left[\nabla u\nabla v+V(x)uv-\mathcal{B}(x)|u|^{2^{*}-2}uv-\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)uv}{(\varepsilon+u^{2})^{2^{*}/2+1}}\right]\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}.

Proof.

We only need to prove the statement for the rightmost term in (
3.2
), as it is standard for the remaining ones.
Consider

a(x,s):=\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)}{(\varepsilon+s^{2})^{2^{*}/2}},\quad x\in M,\,s\in\mathbb{R}.

Since
a
is smooth with respect to
s
and

\partial_{s}a(x,s)=-2^{*}\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)s}{(\varepsilon+s^{2})^{2^{*}/2+1}},

we first observe that, by (
(A1)
) and the fact that
\mathbb{R}\ni s\mapsto s/(\varepsilon+s^{2})^{2^{*}/2+1}\in\mathbb{R}
is bounded, there exists
C>0
such that, for every
u,v\in H^{1}(M)
, there holds

\int_{M}\frac{\mathcal{A}(x)|uv|}{(\varepsilon+u^{2})^{2^{*}/2+1}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\leq C\int_{M}\mathcal{A}(x)|v|\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\leq|\mathcal{A}|_{L^{2N/(N+2)}}|v|_{L^{2^{*}}}<+\infty.

Now we prove the continuity of the Fréchet derivative. Let
u_{n}\to u
in
H^{1}(M)
and
v\in H^{1}(M)
such that
\|v\|=1
. Up to a subsequence, we can assume that
u_{n}\to u
a.e. in
M
. Since
H^{1}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(M)
, there exists
C>0
, independent of
