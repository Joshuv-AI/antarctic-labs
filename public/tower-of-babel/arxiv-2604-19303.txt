The Cohomology of Solvmanifold SYZ Mirrors
Leonardo F. Cavenaghi, Lino Grama, Ludmil Katzarkov, Pedro Antonio Muniz Martins

Abstract
. This paper investigates the geometric and cohomological properties of non-Kähler Strominger-Yau-Zaslow (herein non-Kähler SYZ) mirror symmetry for dual torus fibrations over solvmanifolds in the sense of Lau, Tseng and Yau. We are mainly concerned with three questions: (a) How the Lau-Tseng-Yau notion of non-Kähler SYZ is related to the mapping of supersymmetric branes between symplectic and complex sides; (b) Finding explicit non-Kähler SYZ mirror pairs determined purely by Lie-theoretic data; (c) better understand the cohomological correspondence in the Lau-Tseng-Yau framework (given by a Fourier-Mukai transform), especially concerning the role of Tseng-Yau cohomology. We prove that the Fourier-Mukai transform introduced by Lau-Tseng-Yau exchanges type-A supersymmetric cycles, which are given by special Lagrangian sections equipped with flat U ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathrm{U}(1) connections, with type-B cycles, corresponding to line bundles whose connections satisfy the deformed Hermitian-Yang-Mills (dHYM) equation. We provide pure Lie-theoretic criteria for the existence of non-Kähler SYZ mirror pairs whose base manifolds are solvmanifolds. Applying these criteria, we construct new explicit families of mirror pairs from almost abelian and generalized Heisenberg Lie groups, and provide a complete classification of such pairs arising from nilpotent Lie groups. To contextualize the role of the Tseng-Yau cohomology, we link it to noncommutative geometry. We introduce the Tseng-Yau and Bott-Chern mirror bicomplexes. We show that (some of) their enclosed cohomologies reduce to the primitive Tseng-Yau and Bott-Chern cohomologies and that for basic forms they are isomorphic under the Fourier-Mukai transform. As a last contribution, we discuss how to explicitly compute the Tseng-Yau and the Bott-Chern cohomology for the non-Kähler SYZ mirror pairs constructed here. Again, this is fully characterized by Lie-theoretic data.

1.3.
The answer to question (b)

On compact symplectic manifolds satisfying the
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}
-lemma, Tseng-Yau cohomology degenerates to standard de Rham cohomology. Seeking further understanding of this behavior, we shift our focus toward noncommutative geometry. Inspired by Brylinski’s definition of Poisson cohomology
[
11
]
and the framework of periodic cyclic homology
[
30
,
35
]
, we introduce a formal variable
u
of degree
+2
to construct the
Tseng-Yau bicomplex

(9)

C_{\breve{X}}^{\bullet}=(\Omega^{\bullet}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})((u)),\mathrm{d}+u\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda},\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda})

on the symplectic side, alongside a “mirror” complex

(10)

\hat{C}_{X}^{\bullet}=(G^{\bullet}((u)),\partial+u\overline{\partial},\partial\overline{\partial})

on the complex side. Here,

(11)

G^{\bullet}\colon\quad\dots\xrightarrow{\partial}\Omega^{0,n}(X)\xrightarrow{\partial}\Omega^{0,n-1}(X)\oplus\Omega^{1,n}(X)\xrightarrow{\partial}\Omega^{0,n-2}(X)\oplus\Omega^{1,n-1}(X)\oplus\Omega^{2,n}(X)\xrightarrow{\partial}\dots

We derive four distinct cohomologies from each bicomplex, which capture “mixed-degree forms”. In particular:

(12)

H_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}^{k}(C^{\bullet}):=\frac{\ker(\mathrm{d}+u\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}\colon C^{k}\longrightarrow C^{k+1})}{\im(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}\colon C^{k}\longrightarrow C^{k})},

and

(13)

H_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}^{k}(\hat{C}^{\bullet}):=\frac{\ker(\partial+u\overline{\partial}\colon\hat{C}^{k}\longrightarrow\hat{C}^{k+1})}{\im(\partial\overline{\partial}\colon\hat{C}^{k}\longrightarrow\hat{C}^{k})},

which we term (both)
Bott-Chern cohomologies
, given their similarities with the usual Bott-Chern cohomology in complex geometry.

We prove (Theorems
4.2
and
4.3
) that these new cohomologies contain (as graded vector spaces) the classical Tseng-Yau and Bott-Chern cohomologies as direct summands, but are generically infinite-dimensional. However, this infinite-dimensional “noise” vanishes when the bicomplexes are restricted to
primitive forms
(i.e., those annihilated by the dual Lefschetz operator
\Lambda
). Theorem
D
can be thought of as a ‘noncommutative geometry’ definition for the primitive Tseng-Yau cohomology:

Theorem D

(=Theorem

)

be a symplectic manifold of dimension

\bigoplus_{m~\mathrm{odd}}PH_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}^{m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}}),&\text{if }k\text{ is odd.}\end{cases}

An analogous isomorphism holds for the primitive Bott-Chern cohomology on the complex mirror
X
.

Lastly, we connect these introduced cohomologies with the mirror symmetry framework proposed by Lau, Tseng, and Yau:

Theorem E

(=Theorem
4.4
)
.

The Fourier-Mukai transform induces the following isomorphism for all
k

(15)

H_{\text{bas},\partial+\overline{\partial}}^{k}(\hat{C}_{X}^{\bullet})\cong H^{k}_{\text{bas},\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(C_{\breve{X}}^{\bullet}).

In the last section of this paper, we explain how one can compute the Tseng-Yau cohomology for the non-Kähler mirror pairs constructed here. We do that with the help of a result due to Angella and Kasuya
[

, which reduces the computation on solvmanifolds to the Lie algebra cohomology of the associated semi-direct product. For almost abelian mirror pairs, we provide a complete, explicit description of both the Tseng-Yau cohomology and its mirror Bott-Chern counterpart. For the generalized Heisenberg group, we do not provide a closed formula due to combinatorial difficulties. However, we present all the ingredients to conclude the result.

Let

Lemma 3.5.

Let
G\ltimes_{\rho}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a semi-direct product Lie group, where
\dim(G)=n
. Denote by
\mathfrak{g}
the Lie algebra of
G
and fix a basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
of
\mathfrak{g}
with structure constants

, i.e.,
[X_{i},X_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}C^{k}_{i,j}X_{k}
. Let
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
be the standard basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then the Lie algebra of
G\ltimes_{\rho}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho_{\ast}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, with basis
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{2n}\}:=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}\}

(with

with respect to this basis (so that
[S_{q},S_{r}]=\sum_{p}c^{p}_{q,r}S_{p}

(69)

c^{p}_{q,r}=\begin{cases}C^{p}_{q,r},&1\leq p,q,r\leq n,\\[4.0pt]
(\rho_{\ast}(X_{q}))_{p-n,\,r-n},&1\leq q\leq n,\;n+1\leq p,r\leq 2n,\\[4.0pt]
-(\rho_{\ast}(X_{r}))_{p-n,\,q-n},&n+1\leq q\leq 2n,\;1\leq r\leq n,\;n+1\leq p\leq 2n,\\[4.0pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The Lie bracket in
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho_{\ast}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is given by:

(70)

[(X,Y),\,(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})]=\bigl([X,X^{\prime}]_{\mathfrak{g}},\;\rho_{\ast}(X)Y^{\prime}-\rho_{\ast}(X^{\prime})Y\bigr),

for
X,X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
Y,Y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. In the combined basis, we compute:

•

For
1\leq i,j,k\leq n
:
[X_{j},X_{k}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}C^{i}_{j,k}X_{i}
, and hence
c^{i}_{j,k}=C^{i}_{j,k}
and
c^{p}_{j,k}=0
for

.

•

For
1\leq j\leq n
and
1\leq a\leq n
(so

,

(71)

[X_{j},Y_{a}]=\rho_{\ast}(X_{j})Y_{a}=\sum_{b=1}^{n}(\rho_{\ast}(X_{j}))_{b,a}Y_{b}.

Thus,
c^{\,n+b}_{\,j,\,n+a}=(\rho_{\ast}(X_{j}))_{b,a}
for

.

•

By antisymmetry, for

and
1\leq j\leq n
:

[Y_{a},X_{j}]=-[X_{j},Y_{a}]=-\sum_{b=1}^{n}(\rho_{\ast}(X_{j}))_{b,a}Y_{b}
, yielding

.

•

For

[Y_{a},Y_{b}]=0
, meaning all structure constants with both lower indices in the translation part identically vanish. ∎

Theorem 3.6.

Consider a semi-direct product Lie group
G\ltimes_{\rho}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
\dim(G)=n
, and let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{2n}\}
be a basis of left-invariant
1
-forms dual to the basis
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}\}
of its Lie algebra. Define the
2
-form:

(72)

\omega=\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}\wedge e_{k+n}.

Let
C^{k}_{i,j}
be the structure constants of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\rho_{\ast}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
be the derivative of the representation
\rho
. Then
\omega
is balanced (i.e.,
\mathrm{d}(\omega^{n-1})=0
) if and only if for every
i=1,\dots,n
:

(73)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\Bigl((\rho_{\ast}(X_{j}))_{i,j}-(\rho_{\ast}(X_{i}))_{j,j}-C^{j}_{i,j}\Bigr)=0.

Proof.

The Maurer-Cartan equations give
\mathrm{d}e_{p}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{q,r}c^{p}_{q,r}e_{q}\wedge e_{r}
. Using the explicit constants derived in Lemma
3.2
, we obtain:

(74)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}e_{i}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}C^{i}_{j,k}\,e_{j}\wedge e_{k},

\displaystyle(1\leq i\leq n),

(75)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}e_{i+n}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}(\rho_{\ast}(X_{j}))_{i,k}\,e_{j}\wedge e_{k+n},

\displaystyle(1\leq i\leq n).

Since
\omega=\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}\wedge e_{k+n}
, its exterior derivative expands as:

(76)

\mathrm{d}\omega=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bigl(\mathrm{d}e_{k}\wedge e_{k+n}-e_{k}\wedge\mathrm{d}e_{k+n}\bigr).

Substituting the differentials:

(77)

\mathrm{d}\omega=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}C^{k}_{i,j}\,e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\wedge e_{k+n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(\rho_{\ast}(X_{j}))_{i,k}\,e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\wedge e_{k+n}.

In the second sum, we utilize antisymmetry to rewrite
\sum_{i,j}(\rho_{\ast}(X_{j}))_{i,k}e_{i}\wedge e_{j}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\bigl((\rho_{\ast}(X_{j}))_{i,k}-(\rho_{\ast}(X_{i}))_{j,k}\bigr)e_{i}\wedge e_{j}
. Therefore:

(78)

\mathrm{d}\omega=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\Bigl((\rho_{\ast}(X_{j}))_{i,k}-(\rho_{\ast}(X_{i}))_{j,k}-C^{k}_{i,j}\Bigr)e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\wedge e_{k+n}.

Next,
\omega^{n-2}
is given by the standard expansion of a power of a sum of
n
commuting terms:

(79)

\omega^{n-2}=(n-2)!\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n-2}\leq n}e_{i_{1}}\wedge e_{i_{1}+n}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{n-2}}\wedge e_{i_{n-2}+n}.

Now,
\mathrm{d}(\omega^{n-1})=(n-1)\,\mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\omega^{n-2}
. Wedging Equations (
78
) and (
79
) yields a
(2n-1)
-form. For a fixed
r\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, consider the standard basis
(2n-1)
-form with the
r
-th translation differential omitted:

(80)

\Phi_{r}=e_{1}\wedge e_{n+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{r}\wedge\widehat{e_{n+r}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n}\wedge e_{2n}.

In the wedge product, a non-zero contribution to
\Phi_{r}
occurs precisely when we select a term from
\mathrm{d}\omega
with indices
\{i,j,k\}
such that
\{i,j,k\}\cup\{i_{1},\dots,i_{n-2}\}=\{1,\dots,n\}
and all
n-1
translation indices are distinct. This mathematically forces
k
to equal either
i
or
j
, and the set
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{n-2}\}
must be
\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{r,s\}
where
s
is the index among
i,j
that matches
k
. A careful parity analysis of the permutation sign shows that the coefficient of
\Phi_{r}
in
(n-1)\mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\omega^{n-2}
is, up to an overall non-zero constant:

(81)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s=1\\
s\neq r\end{subarray}}^{n}\Bigl((\rho_{\ast}(X_{s}))_{r,s}-(\rho_{\ast}(X_{r}))_{s,s}-C^{s}_{r,s}\Bigr).

Hence:

(82)

\frac{1}{(n-1)(n-2)!}\,\mathrm{d}(\omega^{n-1})=\sum_{r=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{s\neq r}\bigl((\rho_{\ast}(X_{s}))_{r,s}-(\rho_{\ast}(X_{r}))_{s,s}-C^{s}_{r,s}\bigr)\right)\Phi_{r}.

Thus,
\mathrm{d}(\omega^{n-1})=0
if and only if for every
r=1,\dots,n
:

(83)

\sum_{s\neq r}\Bigl((\rho_{\ast}(X_{s}))_{r,s}-(\rho_{\ast}(X_{r}))_{s,s}-C^{s}_{r,s}\Bigr)=0.

Renaming the dummy indices

∎

Corollary 3.7.

Let
G
be a Lie group with a complete left-invariant affine structure and a lattice
\Gamma
that makes the induced affine structure on
G/\Gamma
integral. Denote by
\rho
the linear part of the affine representation. Consider the semi-direct product Lie group
G\ltimes_{\rho}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
\dim(G)=n
, and let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{2n}\}
be the standard basis of left-invariant
1
-forms. The
2
-form

(84)

\omega=\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}\wedge e_{k+n}

is balanced if and only if

(85)

(\rho_{*}(X_{i}))_{i,i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\rho_{*}(X_{j}))_{i,j}\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq n.

Equivalently, if
\nabla
denotes the affine connection described by the Christoffel symbols
\Gamma_{j,k}^{i}
, then

(86)

\Gamma^{i}_{i,i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma^{i}_{j,j}\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq n.

Proof.

Because
G
admits a complete left-invariant affine structure with a compatible lattice, it must be unimodular. Thus,
\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{ad}_{X})=0
for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. In terms of structure constants, this means:

(87)

\sum_{j=1}^{n}C^{j}_{i,j}=0\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq n.

Substituting this directly into the result of Theorem
3.2
yields:

(88)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\Bigl((\rho_{\ast}(X_{j}))_{i,j}-(\rho_{\ast}(X_{i}))_{j,j}\Bigr)=0\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq n.

Denote by
\nabla
the affine connection given by the left-invariant affine structure on
G
, and let
\Gamma^{i}_{j,k}
be its corresponding Christoffel symbols. Following the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we established that
\rho_{*}(X_{i})=\nabla_{X_{i}}
. Therefore, Equation (
88
) can be rewritten explicitly in terms of the Christoffel symbols as:

(89)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\left(\Gamma^{i}_{j,j}-\Gamma^{j}_{i,j}\right)=0\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq n.

Recall from the proof of Theorem
3.2
that the trace of the connection vanishes identically:
\mathrm{tr}(Y\mapsto\nabla_{X}Y)=0
for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. Consequently,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma^{j}_{i,j}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. We can therefore split the second summation in Equation (
89
) as:

(90)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\Gamma^{j}_{i,j}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma^{j}_{i,j}\right)-\Gamma^{i}_{i,i}=0-\Gamma^{i}_{i,i}=-\Gamma^{i}_{i,i}.

Substituting this back into Equation (
89
) leaves:

(91)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\Gamma^{i}_{j,j}+\Gamma^{i}_{i,i}=0.

By adding
\Gamma^{i}_{i,i}
to both sides, we complete the sum over all
j
on the left side, resulting in:

(92)

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma^{i}_{j,j}=2\Gamma^{i}_{i,i}\implies\Gamma^{i}_{i,i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma^{i}_{j,j}.

Returning this expression to the
\rho_{\ast}
notation completes the proof.
∎

\bigoplus_{m~\mathrm{even}}H^{m}_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\subset V
.

By completing the basis of
V
, we can find a complementary subspace
Q_{\mathrm{even}}\subset V
such that:

(144)

V=\left(\bigoplus_{m~\mathrm{even}}H^{m}_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\right)\oplus Q_{\mathrm{even}}.

Equation (
138
) then yields the desired isomorphism:

(145)

H^{\mathrm{even}}_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(C^{\bullet})\cong\left(\bigoplus_{m~\mathrm{even}}H^{m}_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\right)\oplus Q_{\mathrm{even}}.

∎

A drawback of the cohomology
H_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(C^{\bullet})
is that the
Q
-term is large, making the space infinite-dimensional even on compact manifolds, as the following example shows.

Example 4.3.

Let
(\breve{X},\breve{\omega})
be a Kähler manifold of real dimension
2
. Then

(146)

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{odd}}_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(C^{\bullet})

\displaystyle=H^{1}_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})

(147)

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{even}}_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(C^{\bullet})

\displaystyle\simeq\left\{f+A_{2}\;\middle|\;\mathrm{d}(f+\Lambda A_{2})=0,\,f\in C^{\infty}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}}),\,A_{2}\in\Omega^{2}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\right\}

where
\Lambda=\iota(\breve{\omega}^{-1})
.

However, it is notable that if we restrict this cohomology to
primitive forms
, we recover the primitive version of the Tseng-Yau cohomology; this is the content of Theorem
4.2
. Let us proceed by recalling some needed concepts.

Recall the definition of the
dual Lefschetz operator
:

(148)

\displaystyle\Lambda\colon\Omega^{k}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Omega^{k-2}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})

(149)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\longmapsto\Lambda A:=\iota(\boldsymbol{\alpha})A.

Definition 4.4.

A
primitive form

B\in\Omega^{m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})
is an
m
-form such that
\Lambda(B)=0
. In what follows, we denote by
\mathcal{P}^{m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})
the space of primitive
m
-forms, and by
\mathcal{P}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2n}\mathcal{P}^{i}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})
the space of all primitive forms.

Tseng and Yau defined in
[
43
]
a primitive version of
H_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(\breve{X})
:

(150)

PH_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}^{k}(\breve{X}):=\frac{\ker\left(\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}\colon\Omega^{k}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\Omega^{k+1}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\oplus\Omega^{k-1}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\right)\cap\mathcal{P}^{k}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})}{\im\left(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}\colon\Omega^{k}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\Omega^{k}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\right)\cap\mathcal{P}^{k}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})}.

Similarly, we can define the notion of primitive cohomology for
HP(C^{\bullet})
:

Definition 4.5.

For the complex
C^{\bullet}=(\Omega(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})((u)),\mathrm{d}+u\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda},\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda})
, using the identification
C^{k}\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Omega^{k-2i}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})
, one can define the
primitive Bott-Chern cohomology
as:

(151)

PH_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}^{k}(C^{\bullet}):=\frac{\ker\left(\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}\colon\bigoplus_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Omega^{k-2m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Omega^{k-2m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\right)\cap\mathcal{P}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})}{\left(\bigoplus_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\im\left(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}\colon\Omega^{k-2m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\Omega^{k-2m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\right)\right)\cap\mathcal{P}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})}.

Proposition 4.6.

For the primitive Bott-Chern cohomology (Definition
4.2
), we have for each
k\geq 0
:

(152)

PH_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}^{k}(C^{\bullet})=\frac{\ker\left(\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}\colon\bigoplus_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Omega^{k-2m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Omega^{k-2m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\right)\cap\mathcal{P}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})}{\bigoplus_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}\left(\mathcal{P}^{k-2m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\right)}.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of
[
43
, Lemma 3.9]
.
∎

Theorem 4.7.

Let
(\breve{X},\breve{\omega})
be a symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
. Then:

(153)

PH^{k}_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(C^{\bullet})\cong\begin{cases}\bigoplus_{m~\mathrm{even}}PH_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}^{m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}}),&\text{if }k\text{ is even,}\\[8.61108pt]
\bigoplus_{m~\mathrm{odd}}PH_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}^{m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}}),&\text{if }k\text{ is odd.}\end{cases}

Proof.

We present the proof for the even case, as the odd case is analogous.

Let
\alpha=\sum^{n}_{i=0}B_{2i}\in\bigoplus_{m~\mathrm{even}}\mathcal{P}^{m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})
, where
B_{2i}\in\mathcal{P}^{2i}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})
for
0\leq i\leq n
. Observe that applying the operator
(\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda})
yields:

(154)

(\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda})(\alpha)=\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}\left(\mathrm{d}B_{2i}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}B_{2(i+1)}\right).

For primitive forms, we have the identity
\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}B_{2k}=\Lambda\mathrm{d}B_{2k}
. Substituting this into the sum gives:

(155)

(\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda})(\alpha)=\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}\left(\mathrm{d}B_{2i}+\Lambda\mathrm{d}B_{2(i+1)}\right).

Therefore,
(\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda})(\alpha)=0
if and only if:

(156)

\mathrm{d}B_{2i}+\Lambda\mathrm{d}B_{2(i+1)}=0\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq n-1.

We can show that this system of equations holds if and only if
\mathrm{d}B_{2i}=0
for all
0\leq i\leq n
. We proceed by descending induction on the index
i
. Because
\breve{X}
has dimension
2n
, any form of degree strictly greater than
2n
must vanish; thus,
B_{2(n+1)}=0
. Consequently, for
i=n
, the equation reduces to
\mathrm{d}B_{2n}=0
. Assuming
\mathrm{d}B_{2(i+1)}=0
for some
i
, the relation
\mathrm{d}B_{2i}+\Lambda\mathrm{d}B_{2(i+1)}=0
implies
\mathrm{d}B_{2i}=0
. By induction,
\mathrm{d}B_{2i}=0
for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

It follows that
(\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda})(\alpha)=0
if and only if
\mathrm{d}B_{2i}=0
for all
0\leq i\leq n
. This implies that the kernel of
(\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda})
on even-degree primitive forms decomposes completely:

(157)

\displaystyle\ker

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}\colon\bigoplus_{m~\mathrm{even}}\Omega^{m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{m~\mathrm{odd}}\Omega^{m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\right)\cap\mathcal{P}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})

(158)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{m~\mathrm{even}}\ker\left(\mathrm{d}\colon\Omega^{m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\to\Omega^{m+1}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\right)\cap\mathcal{P}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}}).

Taking the quotient by the image of
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}
, we obtain the desired isomorphism:

(159)

PH^{\mathrm{even}}_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(C^{\bullet})\cong\frac{\displaystyle\bigoplus_{m~\mathrm{even}}\ker\left(\mathrm{d}\colon\Omega^{m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\to\Omega^{m+1}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\right)\cap\mathcal{P}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})}{\displaystyle\bigoplus_{m~\mathrm{even}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}\left(\mathcal{P}^{m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\right)}=\bigoplus_{m~\mathrm{even}}PH_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}^{m}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}}).

∎

4.3.
Complex cohomologies

Here we build on the discussion established in the previous section analogously aiming at obtaining a “mirror cohomology” to
H_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(C^{\bullet})
for a given non-Kähler SYZ mirror pair.

Let
X
be a complex manifold of dimension
2n
, and consider the complex:

(160)

G^{\bullet}\colon\quad\dots\xrightarrow{\partial}\Omega^{0,n}(X)\xrightarrow{\partial}\Omega^{0,n-1}(X)\oplus\Omega^{1,n}(X)\xrightarrow{\partial}\Omega^{0,n-2}(X)\oplus\Omega^{1,n-1}(X)\oplus\Omega^{2,n}(X)\xrightarrow{\partial}\dots

Defining
G^{k}=\bigoplus_{p=0}^{k}\Omega^{p,n-k+p}(X)
, we equip
G^{\bullet}
with the bicomplex structure
G^{\bullet}=(G,\partial,\overline{\partial})
. Observe that
\partial(G^{k})\subset G^{k+1}
and
\overline{\partial}(G^{k})\subset G^{k-1}
. Hence, the operator
\partial
carries a degree of
+1
, while
\overline{\partial}
carries a degree of
-1
.

Next, let
u
be a formal variable of degree
+2
. We define the formal complex
\hat{C}^{\bullet}:=(G^{\bullet}((u)),\partial+u\overline{\partial})
. The total differential
\partial+u\overline{\partial}
has degree
+1
, and the
k
-th term of the complex is given by:

(161)

\hat{C}^{k}=\left\{\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}u^{i}g_{i}\;\middle|\;g_{i}\in G^{k-2i}\text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Furthermore, we can naturally identify
\hat{C}^{k}
with the direct sum:

(162)

\hat{C}^{k}\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigoplus_{p=0}^{k-2i}\Omega^{p,n-k+2i+p}(X).

With this structure in place, we define the following four cohomologies:

(1)

\partial\overline{\partial}
Cohomology:

(163)

H_{s}^{k}(\hat{C}^{\bullet}):=\frac{\mathrm{Ker}(\partial\overline{\partial}:\hat{C}^{k}\longrightarrow\hat{C}^{k})}{\mathrm{Im}(\partial\overline{\partial}:\hat{C}^{k}\longrightarrow\hat{C}^{k})}

(2)

Periodic Cyclic Cohomology (P.C.):

(164)

HP^{k}(\hat{C}^{\bullet}):=\frac{\mathrm{Ker}(\partial+u\overline{\partial}:\hat{C}^{k}\longrightarrow\hat{C}^{k+1})}{\mathrm{Im}(\partial+u\overline{\partial}:\hat{C}^{k-1}\longrightarrow\hat{C}^{k})}

(3)

Bott-Chern Cohomology:

(165)

H_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}^{k}(\hat{C}^{\bullet}):=\frac{\mathrm{Ker}(\partial+u\overline{\partial}:\hat{C}^{k}\longrightarrow\hat{C}^{k+1})}{\mathrm{Im}(\partial\overline{\partial}:\hat{C}^{k}\longrightarrow\hat{C}^{k})}

(4)

Aeppli Cohomology:

(166)

H_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}^{k}(\hat{C}^{\bullet}):=\frac{\mathrm{Ker}(\partial\overline{\partial}:\hat{C}^{k}\longrightarrow\hat{C}^{k})}{\mathrm{Im}(\partial+u\overline{\partial}:\hat{C}^{k-1}\longrightarrow\hat{C}^{k})}

The proof of Theorem
4.3
is analogous to that of Theorem
4.2
and is therefore omitted.

Theorem 4.8.

Let
X
be a complex manifold of dimension
2n
. There exist subspaces
\hat{Q}_{\mathrm{even}}
and
\hat{Q}_{\mathrm{odd}}
in
H_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}^{k}(\hat{C}^{\bullet})
such that, when
n\equiv 0\pmod{2}
,

(167)

H_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}^{k}(\hat{C}^{\bullet})\cong\begin{cases}\left(\bigoplus_{m\text{ even}}\bigoplus_{p+q=m}H_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}^{p,q}(X)\right)\oplus\hat{Q}_{\mathrm{even}},&k\text{ even}\\[8.61108pt]
\left(\bigoplus_{m\text{ odd}}\bigoplus_{p+q=m}H_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}^{p,q}(X)\right)\oplus\hat{Q}_{\mathrm{odd}},&k\text{ odd}\end{cases}

and when
n\equiv 1\pmod{2}
,

(168)

H_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}^{k}(\hat{C}^{\bullet})\cong\begin{cases}\left(\bigoplus_{m\text{ odd}}\bigoplus_{p+q=m}H_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}^{p,q}(X)\right)\oplus\hat{Q}_{\mathrm{even}},&k\text{ even}\\[8.61108pt]
\left(\bigoplus_{m\text{ even}}\bigoplus_{p+q=m}H_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}^{p,q}(X)\right)\oplus\hat{Q}_{\mathrm{odd}},&k\text{ odd}\end{cases}

Not surprisingly, akin to
H_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(C^{\bullet})
, the cohomology
H_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}(\hat{C}^{\bullet})
is generically infinite-dimensional:

Example 4.9.

Let
X
be a complex manifold of complex dimension
1
. Then:

(169)

\displaystyle H_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}^{\mathrm{odd}}(\hat{C}^{\bullet})

\displaystyle\simeq H^{1,1}_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}(X)\oplus H^{0,0}_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}(X)

(170)

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{even}}_{\partial+\overline{\partial}}(\hat{C}^{\bullet})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{d}\colon\Omega^{1}(X)\to\Omega^{2}(X)).

4.4.
Cohomologies and Non-Kähler SYZ

Given a non-Kähler SYZ mirror pair
(X,\mathsf{\Omega},\omega)
and
(\breve{X},\breve{\omega},\breve{\mathsf{\Omega}})
, the cohomology
H_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(\breve{X})
measures well-behaved supersymmetric deformations of type IIA
\mathrm{SU}(n)
-structures
[
44
]
. The Fourier-Mukai transform of differential forms yields an isomorphism:

(171)

FT\colon\bigoplus^{k}_{p=0}\Omega_{B}^{p,n-k+p}(X)\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\Omega^{k}_{B}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}}).

Lau, Tseng, and Yau established the following equivalence:

Theorem 4.10
(Lau, Tseng, Yau
[
31
, Theorem 4.5]
).

The Fourier-Mukai transform gives an isomorphism of bicomplexes:

(172)

\left(\Omega_{B}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}}),\frac{(-1)^{n}\mathbf{i}}{2}\mathrm{d},\frac{(-1)^{n}\mathbf{i}}{2}\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}\right)\cong\left(\Omega_{B}(X),\partial,\overline{\partial}\right).

Moreover, they prove:

Theorem 4.11
(Lau, Tseng, Yau
[
31

).

Given a non-Kähler SYZ mirror pair
(X,\mathsf{\Omega},\omega)
and

, the Fourier-Mukai transform yields an isomorphism:

(173)

H^{(n-p,q)^{\Delta}}_{B,\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(\breve{X},{\mathbb{C}})\simeq H^{p,q}_{B,\partial+\overline{\partial}}(X),

where
\Delta
is the real polarization on
\breve{X}
induced by the bundle structure and

•

(174)

H^{p,q}_{B,\partial+\overline{\partial}}(X):=\frac{\mathrm{Ker}(\partial+\overline{\partial})\cap\Omega^{p,q}_{B}(X)}{\mathrm{Im}(\partial\overline{\partial})\cap\Omega^{p,q}_{B}(X)}.

is the basic Bott-Chern cohomology, and

•

(175)

H_{B,\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}^{(p,q)^{\Delta}}(\breve{X}):=\frac{\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda})\cap\Omega^{(p,q)^{\Delta}}_{B}(\breve{X})}{\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda})\cap\Omega^{(p,q)^{\Delta}}_{B}(\breve{X})}.

is the

-refined Tseng-Yau cohomology.

Although cohomologies
H_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(C^{\bullet}_{\breve{X}})

, we have:

Theorem 4.12.

The Fourier-Mukai transform induces the following isomorphism for all

(176)

H_{B,\partial+\overline{\partial}}^{k}(\hat{C}_{X}^{\bullet})\cong H^{k}_{B,\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(C_{\breve{X}}^{\bullet}).

5.
The Tseng-Yau cohomology of Solvmanifolds

Consider the semi-direct product
G\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{2n}\}
be a basis of left-invariant
1
-forms for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\mathrm{d}_{e}\rho^{*}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then the exterior derivatives are given by:

(177)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}e_{i}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}C^{i}_{j,k}\,e_{j}\wedge e_{k},

\displaystyle(1\leq i\leq n),

(178)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}e_{i+n}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}(\mathrm{d}_{e}\rho^{\ast}(X_{j}))_{i,k}\,e_{j}\wedge e_{k+n},

\displaystyle(1\leq i\leq n).

The construction of the non-Kähler SYZ mirror pair in Section
2.3
yields a symplectic manifold
(\breve{X},\breve{\omega},\breve{\mathsf{\Omega}})
, which is a solvmanifold obtained as the quotient of
G\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by the lattice
\Gamma\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}\Xi^{*}
. From the definition of the product in
G\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the cohomology classes in
H_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(\breve{X})
can be represented by left-invariant forms, and the
(p,q)
-decomposition of
H_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(\breve{X})
can be computed explicitly from this basis of left-invariant
1
-forms. Theorem
5
below provides a method to compute the cohomology
H_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(\breve{X})
. Applying the Fourier-Mukai transform, one can then compute the corresponding cohomology on the mirror manifold
X
.

Theorem 5.1
(Angella, Kasuya
[
4
, Theorem 3.2]
).

Let
\breve{X}=\Gamma\backslash G
be a solvmanifold endowed with a
G
-left-invariant symplectic structure
\breve{\omega}
. Suppose that the natural inclusion map

(179)

H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathfrak{g};{\mathbb{R}})\longrightarrow H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\breve{X};{\mathbb{R}})

is an isomorphism. Then, the natural inclusion map

(180)

\iota\colon H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(\mathfrak{g};{\mathbb{R}})\longrightarrow H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(\breve{X};{\mathbb{R}})

is also an isomorphism.

In this section, we illustrate this result in two distinct cases. For almost abelian manifolds, we provide an explicit description of their Tseng-Yau cohomology. In the case of the generalized Heisenberg group, the computation is more involved and we only present the necessary components for the complete calculation.

5.1.
Almost abelian manifolds

Let
G={\mathbb{R}}\ltimes_{e^{x_{1}A}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
be an almost abelian Lie group, where
\mathrm{tr}(A)=0
. Assume that there exists
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
such that
e^{t_{0}A}
is conjugate to an integer matrix. Consider
G
equipped with a complete left-invariant affine structure, given by the affine representation
\alpha\colon G\to\mathrm{Aff}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with linear part
\rho(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\mathrm{diag}(1,e^{x_{1}A})
. As shown in Appendix
A
, there exists a lattice
\Gamma
such that the quotient
B=G/\Gamma
has an integral affine structure.

Let
(X,\mathsf{\Omega},\omega)\to B
and
(\breve{X},\breve{\omega},\breve{\mathsf{\Omega}})\to B
be a supersymmetric mirror pair of type IIB and IIA, respectively, as provided in Theorem
3.2
. By Theorem
3.2
, the group
G\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
acts transitively on
\breve{X}
with stabilizer
\Gamma\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}\Xi
. Note that, by the definition of
\rho^{*}
, the quotient
(G\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})/(\Gamma\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}\Xi)
is a solvmanifold. We now proceed to compute the cohomology
H_{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{\Lambda}}(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\mathrm{d}_{e}\rho^{*}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}})
.

Let
\{\breve{e}_{1},\ldots,\breve{e}_{2n}\}
be a basis of left-invariant
1
-forms. The differentials are determined by:

(181)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\breve{e}_{i}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=2}^{n}a_{ki}\breve{e}_{1}\wedge\breve{e}_{k},

\displaystyle(1\leq i\leq n),

(182)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\breve{e}_{i+n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{n}a_{ik}\breve{e}_{1}\wedge\breve{e}_{i+n},

\displaystyle(1\leq i\leq n),

where
\mathrm{d}\breve{e}_{1}=\mathrm{d}\breve{e}_{n+1}=0
. Consider the form
\breve{e}_{I}\wedge\breve{e}_{J+n}\in\bigwedge^{s}\left(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\mathrm{d}_{e}\rho^{*}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\right)
, where
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})
and
J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{r})
are index sets such that
m+r=s
. Expanding the differential, we find:

(183)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\breve{e}_{I}\wedge\breve{e}_{J+n})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l=1}^{m}\sum^{n}_{k=2}a_{i_{l}k}\breve{e}_{1}\wedge\breve{e}_{k}\wedge\breve{e}_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{\breve{e}_{i_{l}}}\wedge\dots\wedge\breve{e}_{i_{m}}\wedge\breve{e}_{j_{1}+n}\wedge\dots\wedge\breve{e}_{j_{r}+n}

(184)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum^{r}_{l=1}\sum^{n}_{k=2}a_{kj_{l}}\breve{e}_{1}\wedge\breve{e}_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\breve{e}_{i_{m}}\wedge\breve{e}_{k+n}\wedge\breve{e}_{j_{1}+n}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{\breve{e}_{j_{l}+n}}\wedge\dots\wedge\breve{e}_{j_{r}+n}

(185)

\displaystyle=-\sum^{m}_{l=1}\sum_{k\in(I\cup\{1\})^{c}}a_{i_{l}k}\breve{e}_{1}\wedge\breve{e}_{k}\wedge\breve{e}_{I\setminus\{i_{l}\}}\wedge\breve{e}_{J+n}

(186)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum^{r}_{l=1}\sum_{k\in(J\cup\{1\})^{c}}a_{kj_{l}}\breve{e}_{1}\wedge\breve{e}_{I}\wedge\breve{e}_{k+n}\wedge\breve{e}_{J\setminus\{j_{l}\}+n}

(187)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(-\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{1\}}a_{ii}+\sum_{j\in J\setminus\{1\}}a_{jj}\right)\breve{e}_{1}\wedge\breve{e}_{I}\wedge\breve{e}_{J+n}.

Consequently, if
1\notin I
, the condition
\mathrm{d}(\breve{e}_{I}\wedge\breve{e}_{J+n})=0
is satisfied if and only if:

(188)

\displaystyle a_{ik}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for all }i\in I,\;k\in(I\cup\{1\})^{c},

(189)

\displaystyle a_{kj}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for all }j\in J,\;k\in(J\cup\{1\})^{c},

(190)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}a_{ii}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in J\setminus\{1\}}a_{jj}.

If
1\in I
, the differential vanishes identically, i.e.,
\mathrm{d}(\breve{e}_{I}\wedge\breve{e}_{J+n})=0
.

Recalling that
\breve{\omega}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\breve{e}_{k}\wedge\breve{e}_{k+n}
, the dual Lefschetz operator acts as:

(191)

\Lambda(\breve{e}_{I}\wedge\breve{e}_{J+n})=\sum_{\sigma\in I\cap J}(-1)^{\xi_{\sigma}}\breve{e}_{I\setminus\{\sigma\}}\wedge\breve{e}_{J\setminus\{\sigma\}+n}

for appropriate signs
(-1)^{\xi_{\sigma}}
. Differentiating this yields:

(192)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\Lambda(\breve{e}_{I}\wedge\breve{e}_{J+n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in I\cap J}(-1)^{\xi_{\sigma}}\mathrm{d}\left(\breve{e}_{I\setminus\{\sigma\}}\wedge\breve{e}_{J\setminus\{\sigma\}+n}\right)

(193)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\sigma\in I\cap J}\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{\sigma\}}\sum_{k\in(I\setminus\{\sigma,1\})^{c}}(-1)^{\xi_{\sigma}}a_{ik}\breve{e}_{1}\wedge\breve{e}_{k}\wedge\breve{e}_{I\setminus\{i,\sigma\}}\wedge\breve{e}_{J\setminus\{\sigma\}+n}

(194)

\displaystyle\qquad\breve{e}_{(I_{1}\cup I_{2}+n+1\cup\{j,i,i+n+1\})\setminus\{\sigma\}}\wedge\breve{e}_{(J\cup\{n+1\})\setminus\{\sigma,j\}+2n+1}.

Appendix A
Existence of lattice

In Example
3.2
, we assumed the existence of a lattice
\Gamma
with the desired properties. It turns out that determining when such lattices exist in this context (and in broader contexts) is an active area of research. In dimension three, lattices related to affine structures are called affine crystallographic groups; a comprehensive discussion on this topic can be found in
[
19
]
. For nilpotent and solvable manifolds, the existence of such lattices was studied in
[
9
]
and
[
3
]
.

Let
G
be a simply connected Lie group. A complete left-invariant affine structure on
G
is equivalent to an affine representation
\alpha\colon G\to\operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Let
\rho\colon G\to\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
be the linear part of
\alpha
, given by:

(265)

\rho(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&e^{x_{1}A}\end{bmatrix}.

In the almost abelian case, Bock
[
9
]
proved the following theorem (see also
[
3
, Proposition 2.3]
).

Theorem 1.1
(Bock
[
9
]
, Andrada–Origlia
[
3
]
).

Let
G={\mathbb{R}}\ltimes_{\phi}{\mathbb{R}}^{2n+1}
be an almost abelian Lie group. Then
G
admits a lattice if and only if there exists a
t_{0}\neq 0
such that
\phi(t_{0})
is conjugate to an integer matrix. The lattice is given by
\Gamma=t_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}\ltimes_{\phi}P^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}^{2n+1}
, where
P\phi(t_{0})P^{-1}
is an integer matrix.

Using this theorem, we are able to prove the following result, which asserts the existence of a lattice such that the left-invariant complete affine structure induced on the quotient
G/\Gamma
is integral.

Theorem 1.2.

Let
G={\mathbb{R}}\ltimes_{e^{x_{1}A}}{\mathbb{R}}^{2n+1}
be an almost abelian Lie group. Assume there exists a real number
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
and a matrix
P\in\mathrm{GL}(2n+1,{\mathbb{R}})
such that
e^{t_{0}A}
is conjugate to an integer matrix via
P
.

Consider a complete left-invariant affine structure on
G
given by the affine representation
\alpha\colon G\to\operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n+2})
, whose linear part
\rho\colon G\to\mathrm{GL}(2n+2,{\mathbb{R}})
is given by:

(266)

\rho(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n+2})=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&e^{x_{1}A}\end{bmatrix}.

Then, there is a lattice
\Gamma=t_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}\ltimes_{e^{x_{1}A}}P^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}^{2n+1}
such that the affine structure induced on the solvmanifold
G/\Gamma
is integral.

Proof.

Since
e^{t_{0}A}
is conjugate to an integer matrix via
P
, the preceding theorem guarantees the existence of the lattice
\Gamma=t_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}\ltimes_{e^{x_{1}A}}P^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}^{2n+1}
.

It remains to prove that the left-invariant affine structure induced on
G/\Gamma
is integral. Since
\pi_{1}(G/\Gamma)\cong\Gamma
, the holonomy of the affine structure is given by the restriction
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\alpha\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\Gamma}}\colon\Gamma\to\operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n+2})
, whose linear part is
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\Gamma}}
. To prove that the induced affine structure is integral, we need to show that the image of
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\Gamma}}
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(2n+2,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Let
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n+2})\in\Gamma
. By the definition of the lattice, the first coordinate must be an integer multiple of
t_{0}
, meaning
x_{1}=kt_{0}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. The linear part evaluated on this element is
\rho(kt_{0},\ldots)=\mathrm{diag}(1,e^{kt_{0}A})
.

We conjugate this representation using the block diagonal matrix
\Xi=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&P\end{bmatrix}
:

(267)

\Xi\,\rho(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n+2})\,\Xi^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&P\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&e^{kt_{0}A}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&P^{-1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&Pe^{kt_{0}A}P^{-1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&(Pe^{t_{0}A}P^{-1})^{k}\end{bmatrix}.

Since
Pe^{t_{0}A}P^{-1}
is an integer matrix by hypothesis, its
k
-th power is also an integer matrix. Thus, the linear holonomy
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\Gamma}}
is conjugate to a matrix in
\mathrm{GL}(2n+2,{\mathbb{Z}})
. Therefore, the left-invariant affine structure induced by
\alpha
on
G/\Gamma
is complete and integral.
∎
