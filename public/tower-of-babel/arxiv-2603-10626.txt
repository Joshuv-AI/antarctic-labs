RO ⁡ ( C p × C p ) \operatorname{RO}(C_{p}\times C_{p}) -graded cohomology of universal spaces and the coefficient ring
Surojit Ghosh, Ankit Kumar

Abstract
. We compute the RO ⁡ ( C p × C p ) \operatorname{RO}(C_{p}\times C_{p}) -graded Bredon cohomology of equivariant universal and classifying spaces associated to families of subgroups, with coefficients in the constant Mackey functor 𝔽 p ¯ \underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}} . An explicit description of the resulting coefficient ring, including its multiplicative structure, is obtained. These computations are then applied to the study of lifts of cohomology operations via the Bredon cohomology of equivariant complex projective spaces.

Introduction

Equivariant stable homotopy theory studies spaces and spectra equipped with a group action, with the goal of understanding the associated equivariant (co)homology theories. Unlike the classical non-equivariant case, equivariant cohomology is naturally graded by the real representation ring
\operatorname{RO}(G)
of a finite group
G
. Its computation reveals new algebraic structures arising from fixed points, transfers, and norms. Explicit descriptions of
\operatorname{RO}(G)
-graded Bredon cohomology remain scarce, and their determination forms a central problem in the field.

A natural point of departure is the computation of the coefficient ring, i.e. the Bredon cohomology of a point with constant Mackey functor coefficients. For
G=C_{p}
, these were established in the classical work of Stong and Lewis
[
22
]
. Subsequent advances include the additive structure of equivariant cohomology for
G=C_{pq}
with both
\underline{A}
and
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
-coefficients
[
4
]
, as well as computations for cyclic groups of square-free order that identify large parts of the multiplicative structure
[
6
]
. For general cyclic groups, selected graded pieces of the cohomology ring were obtained in
[
3
]
. These works rely on tools such as the Tate square
[
14
]
, computations of geometric fixed points, Bredon cohomology techniques, and fixed point methods. Together, they point toward a general challenge: to extend knowledge of the coefficient ring beyond cyclic groups.

For the Klein four group
C_{2}\times C_{2}
(henceforth, we denote it by
\mathcal{K}_{4}
), the cohomology groups were computed in
[
18
]
, while the Mackey functor structure and ring structure of the positive cone of
\pi_{\bigstar}H\underline{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
were determined in
[
10
]
. In this work, we carry this program forward by determining the multiplicative structure of the coefficient ring for
C_{p}\times C_{p}
(for both
p=2
and odd prime
p
) by using the Tate square. Our method relies on a careful analysis of the Bredon cohomology of the universal spaces entering the Tate square. Beyond their immediate computational role, these universal spaces are of independent interest, as they control the structure of power operations in equivariant homotopy theory.

This perspective connects directly to power operations on equivariant
E_{\infty}
-ring spectra. For
N_{\infty}
-operads, the group of power operations is governed by the
E
-cohomology of equivariant classifying spaces of symmetric groups. In particular, explicit computations of power operations for
H\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
and
MU^{G}
with
G
a finite Abelian group play a central role. As a first step, one is led to compute the
\operatorname{RO}(C_{p}\times C_{p})
-cohomology of certain graph complexes for both even and odd primes, which in turn determine the
C_{p}
-equivariant cohomology of
B_{C_{p}}\mathbb{Z}/p
, the
C_{p}
-equivariant classifying space of
C_{p}
, providing a foundation for the study of power operations in equivariant homotopy theory.

Finally, we turn to a third direction of inquiry: the structure of equivariant Steenrod operations. In the classical (non-equivariant) setting, Steenrod operations provide a fundamental layer of structure on cohomology. In the equivariant context, however, explicit descriptions are subtle and comparatively rare. In general, for
E\in\mathrm{Sp}^{G}
, the
\operatorname{RO}(G)
-graded
E
-cohomology operations are given by
\pi^{G}_{\bigstar}(F(E,E))
, while the algebra of co-operations is
\pi^{G}_{\bigstar}(E\wedge E)
. When
E=H\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, these are denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{\bigstar}_{G}
and
\mathcal{A}^{G}_{\bigstar}
, respectively.

For any subgroup
H\leq G
, there is a restriction map

\operatorname{res}^{G}_{H}:\mathcal{A}^{\bigstar}_{G}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\bigstar}_{H}.

Since
\operatorname{RO}(H)
is a retract of
\operatorname{RO}(G)
, one may ask when an operation defined in

\mathcal{A}^{\bigstar}_{H}
can be lifted to an operation in
\mathcal{A}^{\bigstar}_{G}
. Concretely, a cohomology
operation
\theta\colon H\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\to\Sigma^{\alpha}H\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
(of degree
\alpha\in\operatorname{RO}(H)
)
is said to be
liftable
if there exists an operation
\tilde{\theta}\in\mathcal{A}^{\bigstar}_{G}

of the same degree such that, after restricting from
G
to
H
, the action of

\tilde{\theta}
coincides with that of
\theta
.

The central question is therefore: which
H
-equivariant cohomology operations admit lifts to
G
-equivariant operations? For cyclic groups of prime order, Caruso
[
7
]
proved that the only nontrivial liftable operation is the Bockstein homomorphism, using explicit computations of Bredon cohomology of Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces and the structure of the coefficient ring. More recently, in
[
1
]
the author gave an alternative proof of Caruso’s result in the case
G=C_{p}
.

Finally we investigate the analogous question for the group
C_{p}\times C_{p}
, and prove the following:

Theorem E
.

Let
\theta
be a cohomology operation of degree
2r(p-1)
and
2r
for odd
p
and
p=2
case, respectively which does not involve the Böckstein
\beta
.
Then there does not exist an equivariant cohomology operation
\tilde{\theta}
lifting
\theta
.

See Theorem
6.14
.

1.
Preliminaries on Bredon Cohomology

Bredon cohomology is the natural extension of singular cohomology to the equivariant setting, where a group
G
acts on a space
X
. We recall some standard constructions of
G
–spaces.
For an orthogonal representation
V
of a finite group
G
, one has the unit disk
D(V)
and the unit sphere
S(V)
with the natural
G
-action, and the one-point compactification
S^{V}
. The equivariant analogue of CW complexes are
G
-CW complexes
, built inductively from cells of the form
G/H\times D^{n}
for subgroups
H\leq G
, attached along
G/H\times S^{n-1}

In this framework, one replaces constant abelian coefficient groups with a more structured notion: Mackey functors. Furthermore, grading is promoted from the integers
\mathbb{Z}
to the real representation ring
\operatorname{RO}(G)
, allowing one to track cohomological degrees twisted by representations of
G
. For more details, one can check
[
21
]
.

1.1
.

Mackey functors

.

The foundational algebraic object in this theory is the
Burnside category

\mathrm{Burn}_{G}
, whose objects are finite
G
-sets of the form
G/H
, and morphisms are spans of
G
-maps, up to isomorphism and group completion. Explicitly, a morphism
G/H\to G/K
is a formal linear combination of diagrams

G/H\xleftarrow{f}S\xrightarrow{g}G/K

where
S
is a finite
G
-set and
f
,
g
are
G
-maps. The composition in
\mathrm{Burn}_{G}
is given by pullback along these spans.

A
Mackey functor

\underline{M}
for
G
is a contravariant additive functor:

\underline{M}:\mathrm{Burn}_{G}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Ab}.

Equivalently, a Mackey functor consists of a pair of compatible systems of restriction and transfer maps:

\operatorname{res}_{K}^{H}:\underline{M}(G/H)\to\underline{M}(G/K),\quad\mathrm{for}\quad K\leq H,

\mathrm{tr}_{K}^{H}:\underline{M}(G/K)\to\underline{M}(G/H),\quad\mathrm{for}\quad K\leq H,

satisfying natural coherence conditions such as the double coset formula. The category of Mackey functors
\mathrm{Mack}_{G}
is an abelian symmetric monoidal category, with
box product
and internal Hom given by Day convolution.

given by multiplication by
[H:K]
for
K\leq H
. Its dual
\underline{C}^{\ast}
is obtained by interchanging restrictions and transfers, with
\underline{C}^{\ast}(G/H)=\underline{C}(G/H)
on orbits.

1.12

Equivariant universal spaces

.

A

\mathcal{F}

G

G

E\mathcal{F}^{H}=\begin{cases}\simeq\ast&\text{if }H\in\mathcal{F},\\

The

One fundamental cofibre sequence associated with such a family is:

\mathcal{F}

Note that

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{\bigstar}_{\mathcal{K}_{4}}\!\left({\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\right)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{\bigstar-i\rho_{\mathbb{C}}}_{\mathcal{K}_{4}}\!\left({S^{0}};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\right)\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\Big(\widetilde{H}^{\bigstar-i\rho_{\mathbb{C}}-2\alpha_{0}}_{\mathcal{K}_{4}}\!\left({{S^{0}}};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\oplus\widetilde{H}^{\bigstar-i\rho_{\mathbb{C}}-2\alpha_{1}-2\beta}_{\mathcal{K}_{4}}\!\left({{S^{0}}};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\right)\oplus\widetilde{H}^{\bigstar-i\rho_{\mathbb{C}}-2\alpha_{0}-2\alpha_{1}-2\beta}_{\mathcal{K}_{4}}\!\left({{S^{0}}};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\right)\Big).

By Theorem
6.9
, the cohomology of an
r
–fold smash product of
equivariant projective spaces can be expressed as a suspension of the cohomology of a
point. In the odd prime case, each summand has the form

(6.11)

{}{}\widetilde{H}^{\,\bigstar-\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}k^{(j)}_{i}\,(jU_{0}+i\beta)-\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}k^{(j)}_{0}\,jU_{0}-\sum_{s=1}^{r}d_{s}\rho_{\mathbb{C}}}_{\mathcal{G}}\!\left(S^{0};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\right),

where
U_{0}=\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{p-1}\alpha_{\ell}
.

The nonnegative integers
k^{(j)}_{i}
and
k^{(j)}_{0}
satisfy

\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}k^{(j)}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}k^{(j)}_{0}\leq r,

and at least

r-\Biggl(\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}k^{(j)}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}k^{(j)}_{0}\Biggr)

of the integers
d_{1},\dots,d_{r}
are strictly positive.

For the group
\mathcal{K}_{4}
, the analogous
r
-fold smash product of equivariant projective spaces has the summand of shifted suspension of cohomology of a point which have the form

(6.12)

{}\widetilde{H}^{\,\bigstar-\sum_{m=1}^{r}i_{m}\rho_{\mathbb{C}}-k_{1}(2\alpha_{0})-k_{2}(2\alpha_{1}+2\beta)-k_{3}(2\alpha_{0}+2\alpha_{1}+2\beta)}_{\mathcal{G}}(S^{0};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{2}}),

where
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}
are nonnegative integers with

k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}\leq r
, and at least
r-(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3})
of the
d_{s}\geq 0
are
strictly positive.

6.13 Proposition
.

(1)

For an odd prime
p
, the Mackey functor-valued cohomology
\underline{H}^{\bigstar}_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})^{\wedge{(r)}};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\right)
does not contain the constant Mackey functor
\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
for
\bigstar=r\beta+2r(p-1)
.

(2)

For
p=2
, the Mackey functor-valued cohomology
\underline{H}^{\bigstar}_{\mathcal{K}_{4}}\left(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})^{\wedge{(r)}};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\right)
does not contain the constant Mackey functor
\underline{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
for
\bigstar=2r\alpha_{0}+2r
.

Proof.

We prove the statement for odd
p
; the
p=2
case is analogous. Suppose, towards a contradiction, that the constant Mackey functor
\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}

occurs as a summand of

\underline{H}^{\bigstar}_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})^{\wedge r};\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\bigr)

for

\bigstar=r\beta+2r(p-1).

Restricting this copy of
\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
to the subgroup
K=\langle b\rangle

gives the constant Mackey functor
\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
on
K
; in particular

\operatorname{res}_{K}(\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}})=\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
.

By the description in (
6.11
), any summand in degree
\bigstar
, gives a degree of the form

\alpha=r\beta+2r(p-1)-\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}k^{(j)}_{i}\,(jU_{0}+i\beta)-\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}k^{(j)}_{0}\,jU_{0}-\sum_{s=1}^{r}d_{s}\rho_{\mathbb{C}},

Consider the following computation

\bigl|\operatorname{res}_{K}(\alpha)\bigr|=2rp-\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}k^{(j)}_{i}\,(2pj+2i)-\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}2pj\,k^{(j)}_{0}-2p^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{r}d_{s}.

and

\bigl|(\operatorname{res}_{K}(\alpha))^{K}\bigr|=2rp-\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}k^{(j)}_{i}\,2i-4\sum_{s=1}^{r}d_{s}.

If
\bigl|\operatorname{res}_{K}(\alpha)\bigr|=0
, we find on substituting

\bigl|(\operatorname{res}_{K}(\alpha))^{K}\bigr|=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}2pj\,k^{(j)}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}2pj\,k^{(j)}_{0}+(2p^{2}-4)\sum_{s=1}^{r}d_{s}\geq 0.

Claim:
\bigl|(\operatorname{res}_{K}(\alpha))^{K}\bigr|>0
.

If
\bigl|(\operatorname{res}_{K}(\alpha))^{K}\bigr|=0
implies
d_{s}=0
,
k^{(j)}_{i}=0
for all
j\neq 0
which implies
\Sigma_{i=1}^{p-1}k^{(0)}_{i}=r
, but
\bigl|\operatorname{res}_{K}(\alpha)\bigr|=2(rp-\Sigma^{p-1}_{i=1}ik^{(0)}_{i})>0
as
\max\{\Sigma^{p-1}_{i=1}ik^{(0)}_{i}\}=(p-1)r
, contradicting Proposition
1.9
. Therefore, no copy of the constant Mackey
functor
\underline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
can occur in degree
\bigstar=r\beta+2r(p-1)
, completing
the proof for odd
p
.
∎

In the following, we prove that a certain cohomology operation cannot be lifted to
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{K}_{4}
level,
which is the similar result given by Caruso

[
7
]
for

C_{p}
group.

6.14 Theorem
.

Let
\theta
be a cohomology operation of degree
2r(p-1)
and
2r
for an odd
p
and
p=2
case, respectively which does not involve the Böckstein
\beta
.
Then there does not exist an equivariant cohomology operation
\tilde{\theta}
lifting
\theta
.

Proof.

Let
p
be an odd prime. Assume, toward a contradiction, that there exists an equivariant lift

\tilde{\theta}\colon H\underline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\longrightarrow\Sigma^{2r(p-1)}H\underline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

whose underlying non-equivariant map agrees with
\theta
.
Such a map induces, for every
G
-space
X
, a natural transformation of
\operatorname{RO}(\mathcal{G})
-graded Mackey functors

\underline{H}^{\bigstar}_{\mathcal{G}}(X;\underline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\longrightarrow\underline{H}^{\bigstar+2r(p-1)}_{\mathcal{G}}(X;\underline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}),

functorial in
X
.

Now take
X=\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})^{\wedge(r)}
and
\bigstar=r\beta
.
By Theorem
6.11
, there is an inclusion of Mackey functors

\underline{H}^{0}_{\mathcal{G}}(S^{0};\underline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\;\cong\;\underline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\;\hookrightarrow\;\underline{H}^{\,r\beta}_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})^{\wedge(r)};\underline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\bigr).

On the other hand, Proposition
6.13
shows that

\underline{H}^{\,r\beta+2r(p-1)}_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})^{\wedge(r)};\underline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\bigr)

contains no direct summand isomorphic to
\underline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
Consequently, the composite

\underline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\;\hookrightarrow\;\underline{H}^{\,r\beta}_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})^{\wedge(r)};\underline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\bigr)\;\xrightarrow{\;\tilde{\theta}\;}\underline{H}^{\,r\beta+2r(p-1)}_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})^{\wedge(r)};\underline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\bigr)

is necessarily trivial as a morphism of Mackey functors.

Passing to underlying non-equivariant cohomology, the inclusion sends

\underline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\mathcal{G}/e)
to the nonzero class

\mathbb{F}_{p}\{x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{r}\}.

Since
\theta
acts nontrivially on this element, the induced map on underlying cohomology is nonzero.
This contradicts the triviality of the corresponding Mackey functor morphism.
Hence no such lift
\tilde{\theta}
can exist.

For
p=2
case, the argument is analogous. One shows that there is no nontrivial Mackey functor morphism

\underline{H}^{2r\alpha_{0}}_{\mathcal{K}_{4}}\bigl(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})^{\wedge(r)};\underline{\mathbb{F}}\bigr)\longrightarrow\underline{H}^{2r\alpha_{0}+2r}_{\mathcal{K}_{4}}\bigl(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})^{\wedge(r)};\underline{\mathbb{F}}\bigr),

and the same contradiction argument applies.
∎
