Gromov’s Euclidean Endpoint C 0 C^{0} Rigidity for the Positive Mass Theorem
Jiangcheng You and Heng Zhang

Abstract
. We prove Gromov’s Euclidean endpoint C 0 C^{0} rigidity conjecture. Let g g be a smooth complete metric on ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} with non-negative scalar curvature. If | g − g Euc | = o ⁡ ( r − 1 ) , r = | x | → ∞ , |g-g_{\mathrm{Euc}}|=o(r^{-1}),\qquad r=|x|\to\infty, then ( ℝ 3 , g ) (\mathbb{R}^{3},g) is isometric to Euclidean space.

1.
Introduction

The positive mass theorem is a basic bridge between scalar curvature and the geometry of an end. In its classical three-dimensional asymptotically flat form, it asserts that a complete asymptotically flat manifold with non-negative scalar curvature has non-negative ADM mass, and that the zero-mass case is rigid: the manifold is isometric to Euclidean space. The ADM mass was introduced by Arnowitt–Deser–Misner
[
1
]
; the theorem was proved by Schoen–Yau
[
19
,
20
]
and, by a spinorial argument, by Witten
[
21
]
. Under the standard asymptotically flat hypotheses, the coordinate invariance of the ADM mass was established by Bartnik
[
4
]
. We will also use the Green-function formulation and rigidity mechanism of Agostiniani–Mazzieri–Oronzio
[
2
]
.

Scalar curvature is a second-order expression in the metric. Thus the usual asymptotically flat framework imposes differentiable decay assumptions strong enough to make the ADM surface integral meaningful. At the same time, a growing body of work shows that lower scalar-curvature bounds have a notable
C^{0}
character. Gromov proved that non-negative scalar curvature is preserved under
C^{0}
convergence of smooth manifolds to a smooth limit
[
10
,
11
]
; related approaches include Bamler’s Ricci-flow proof
[
6
]
, Burkhardt-Guim’s Ricci-flow definition for
C^{0}
metrics
[
8
]
, and recent quantitative or weak-limit results using smoothing, harmonic functions, and
\mu
-bubbles
[
14
,
16
,
17
]
. The prism and polyhedron rigidity theorems of Li and Brendle
[
15
,
7
]
provide another geometric expression of this same low-regularity principle.

This
C^{0}
viewpoint naturally leads back to the rigidity statement in the positive mass theorem. If non-negative scalar curvature can be detected through such weak metric information, one may ask whether the rigidity conclusion survives when the usual asymptotically flat expansion at infinity is replaced by a purely
C^{0}
rate of convergence to the Euclidean metric. Gromov formulated this problem in
[
11
, Section 3.11]
. More precisely,

Conjecture 1.1

(Gromov’s Euclidean endpoint
C^{0}
rigidity)
.

Assume that
g
is a smooth complete metric on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with non-negative scalar curvature. If

|g-g_{\mathrm{Euc}}|=o(r^{-1}),\qquad r=|x|\to\infty,

then
(\mathbb{R}^{3},g)
is isometric to Euclidean space.

In this work, we show this conjecture is true.

Theorem 1.2
.

Conjecture
1.1
holds true.

We remark that the endpoint decay in Theorem
1.2
is sharp in the following sense. For every

c>0
, there exist smooth non-flat metrics
g
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with non-negative scalar curvature such that

g=\bigl(1+c|x|^{-1}+O(|x|^{-2})\bigr)g_{\mathrm{Euc}}

near infinity. One way to obtain such examples is to start from a small perturbation of the round
metric on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and then conformally blow up a point using the Green function of the
conformal Laplacian; see
[
18
, Remark 4]
. Thus the assumption

o(|x|^{-1})
cannot in general be weakened to an
O(|x|^{-1})
condition.

1.1.
Relation with the isoperimetric PMT of Benatti–Fogagnolo–Mazzieri

Benatti, Fogagnolo, and Mazzieri
[
5
]
proved an isoperimetric version of the positive mass theorem. Here
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathrm{iso}}
denotes Huisken’s isoperimetric mass
[
13
]
, in the exhaustion formulation of
[
5
, Definition 1.2]
: it is the supremum over exhaustions of the limsup of the quasi-local isoperimetric deficit

\mathfrak{m}_{\mathrm{iso}}(\Omega)=\frac{2}{|\partial\Omega|}\left(|\Omega|-\frac{|\partial\Omega|^{3/2}}{6\sqrt{\pi}}\right).

In the case relevant to the present paper,
[
5
, Theorem 2.14]
asserts that if a complete strongly
1
-nonparabolic Riemannian
3
-manifold with non-negative scalar curvature satisfies the topological hypotheses in their Lemma 2.11(2), then

0\leq\mathfrak{m}_{\mathrm{iso}},

and equality holds if and only if the manifold is isometric to the flat
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

This gives a quick route to the non-endpoint version of Conjecture
1.1
. Suppose, for instance, that

|g-g_{\mathrm{Euc}}|=O(r^{-1-\tau}),\qquad\tau>0.

Then
g
is
C^{0}_{\tau}
-asymptotically Schwarzschildian with Schwarzschild mass parameter
0
in the terminology of
[
5
]
. Their Theorem 1.5 gives

\mathfrak{m}_{\mathrm{iso}}=0.

The equality case of
[
5
, Theorem 2.14]
then implies that
(\mathbb{R}^{3},g)
is isometric to Euclidean space.

However, the endpoint assumption

|g-g_{\mathrm{Euc}}|=o(r^{-1})

is not covered
by these theorems as stated. In the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 1.5]
, the
comparison between the quasi-local isoperimetric mass of the weak IMCF
regions and the Schwarzschild model uses the quantitative

O(r^{-1-\tau})
error, with a fixed positive exponent
\tau
, to make
the corresponding error terms vanish at infinity. At the endpoint one no
longer has such a fixed power decay available from the stated hypotheses.
Moreover, since
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathrm{iso}}
is a supremum over all
exhaustions, the fact that the coordinate-ball quasi-local masses tend to
zero under
o(r^{-1})
does not by itself imply

\mathfrak{m}_{\mathrm{iso}}\leq 0
.

1.2.
Relation with the work of Mazurowski–Yao

Recently, Mazurowski and Yao
[
18
]
gave a new proof Theorem
1.2
under the hypothesis

|g-g_{\mathrm{Euc}}|=O(r^{-1-\tau}),\qquad\tau>0.

Their argument has two main components.

First, let
u
be the normalized Green function for the divergence-form operator

Lf=\operatorname{div}(A\nabla f),\qquad A=\sqrt{\det(g_{kl})}\,g^{-1}=I-B.

Away from the pole,

\Delta u=\operatorname{div}(B\nabla u).

Set

X=B\nabla u.

Under the faster decay
B=O(r^{-1-\tau})
, the Caccioppoli estimate for
u
gives
X\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Mazurowski–Yao then define

P_{\infty}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}X

and obtain the rescaled Green expansion: on every fixed annulus,

R\left(Ru(Ry)-|y|^{-1}\right)\longrightarrow\frac{\langle b+P_{\infty},y\rangle}{|y|^{3}}\quad\text{in }W^{1,p}.

Second, they insert this expansion into an averaged Agostiniani–Mazzieri–Oronzio functional
D(a)
, as in
[
18
, Section 3.1]
. The
F
-functional of Agostiniani–Mazzieri–Oronzio is monotone when
R_{g}\geq 0
by
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
, equivalently in the normalization of Mazurowski–Yao by
[
18
, Appendix A, Proposition 22]
. The averaged functional
D
inherits

D(a)\geq 0,\qquad aD(a)\text{ is non-decreasing}.

Mazurowski–Yao show that their Green expansion implies
aD(a)\to 0
. Hence
D\equiv 0
, then
F\equiv 0
, and the rigidity theorem for the
F
-functional gives flatness.

The obstruction at the endpoint is exactly the loss of
X\in L^{1}
. From
B=o(r^{-1})
one only obtains the dyadic estimate

m_{j}:=\int_{2^{j}\leq|x|\leq 2^{j+1}}|X(x)|\,dx\to 0.

The full integral of
X
may diverge. Therefore
P_{\infty}
used in
[
18
]
may not exist.

1.3.
Proof strategy

The proof below keeps the
D/F
-functional mechanism of Mazurowski–Yao
[
18
, Sections 3.1–3.3 and Appendix A]
but replaces the fixed
P_{\infty}
analysis by a scale-dependent one. The first new point is that the endpoint dyadic estimate is still strong enough to define, for each annular scale
R
,

P_{R}^{\sigma}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{|x|\leq\sigma R}X(x)\,dx.

Although
P_{R}^{\sigma}
need not converge, it satisfies

|P_{R}^{\sigma}|=o(\log R).

We prove that the Green function has the following expansion

R\left(Ru(Ry)-|y|^{-1}\right)=\frac{\langle b+P_{R}^{\sigma},y\rangle}{|y|^{3}}+o_{W^{1,p}}(1)

on each fixed annulus, along dyadic scales. The proof requires a weighted convolution construction for
\Delta w=\operatorname{div}X
, a three-region dyadic estimate for
P_{R}^{\sigma}
, and a perturbative elliptic upgrade from
L^{q}
to
W^{1,p}
on fixed annuli. The last estimate is proved in Appendix
B
directly from the constant-coefficient Calderon–Zygmund estimate and a hole-filling iteration.

The second new point is that the
D
-functional satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{D}\left(0,\frac{\langle\mathbf{q},y\rangle}{|y|^{3}}\right)=0\qquad\text{for all }\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

where
\mathcal{L}_{D}
denotes the linearization of
D
. This identity is exact for each
\mathbf{q}
. Hence it remains valid with the moving coefficient
\mathbf{q}=Q_{R}^{\sigma}=b+P_{R}^{\sigma}
. To use this observation at the endpoint we need a quadratic, not merely Fréchet, expansion of
D
at the Euclidean model:

\mathscr{D}(\delta+k,\rho+v)=\mathscr{D}(\delta,\rho)+\mathcal{L}_{D}(k,v)+O\bigl((\|k\|_{C^{0}}+\|v\|_{W^{1,p}})^{2}\bigr).

Since
|Q_{R}^{\sigma}|=o(\log R)
, the quadratic contribution satisfies

R\left(\frac{|Q_{R}^{\sigma}|}{R}\right)^{2}=o(1).

This yields
RD(R)\to 0
along dyadic scales. The monotonicity of
RD(R)
then forces
D\equiv 0
.

Finally, Appendix
C
records the precise endpoint use of the
F/D
machinery. The monotonicity formula is the Agostiniani–Mazzieri–Oronzio formula
[
2
, Theorem 1.1, formula (1.3)]
, written in the present normalization. The rigidity step is invoked as the rigidity statement of
[
2
, Corollary 1.3]
, equivalently the equality case following their monotonicity theorem
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
. The appendix verifies the hypotheses needed to invoke this theorem in the present setting. It also records properness and connectedness of regular Green levels under the endpoint assumptions.

1.4.
Organization

Section
2
proves the basic annular estimates for
X=B\nabla u
. Section
3
constructs the weighted convolution and proves the
L^{q}
expansion depending on
P_{R}^{\sigma}
. Section
4
upgrades this to the
W^{1,p}
Green expansion. Sections
5
–
8
analyze the
D
-functional and prove Theorem
1.2
. Appendix
A
contains elementary dyadic summation facts, Appendix
B
proves the perturbative elliptic estimate, and Appendix
C
records the endpoint
F/D
facts and the precise AMO rigidity input.

2.
Basic setup and annular control

Throughout the paper, unless a subscript
g
,
g_{R}
, or
g_{a}
is displayed, the symbols

\nabla,\quad\Delta,\quad\operatorname{div},\quad\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,\quad|\cdot|,\quad dx,\quad B_{R}

refer to the Euclidean structure in the fixed coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We write
\delta=g_{\mathrm{Euc}}
for the Euclidean metric and
I=(\delta^{ij})
for the identity matrix acting on coordinate one-forms. The notation
\nabla_{g}
,
dv_{g}
,
dA_{g}
, and
\operatorname{div}_{g}
refers to the Riemannian metric
g
.

Let
g
be a smooth complete Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying

(2.1)

\left\lvert g-g_{\mathrm{Euc}}\right\rvert=o(r^{-1}),\qquad r=\left\lvert x\right\rvert.

Define

A=(a^{ij}),\qquad a^{ij}=\sqrt{\det(g_{kl})}\,g^{ij}.

Then
A
is uniformly elliptic and bounded. Moreover,

(2.2)

A=I-B,\qquad\left\lvert B(x)\right\rvert=o(\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{-1}).

Let

Lf=\operatorname{div}(A\nabla f)=\sqrt{\det(g_{kl})}\,\Delta_{g}f.

Let
u
be the positive Green function for
L
with pole at the origin, normalized so that the
g
-flux of
u
across each regular level set is
4\pi
. By the Grüter–Widman Green function estimates for uniformly elliptic divergence-form operators
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
,

(2.3)

\frac{c_{0}}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}\leq u(x)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}\qquad\text{for }\left\lvert x\right\rvert\geq 1.

For
R>0
define

\operatorname{Ann}(R)=B_{8R}\setminus B_{R/8}

and the endpoint modulus

(2.4)

\eta(R)=\sup_{x\in\operatorname{Ann}(R)}R\left\lvert B(x)\right\rvert.

Then

(2.5)

\eta(R)\to 0.

Lemma 2.1

(Caccioppoli estimate)
.

There is a constant
C
independent of
R\geq 1
such that

(2.6)

\int_{\operatorname{Ann}(R)}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{C}{R}.

Proof.

This is the standard Caccioppoli argument for uniformly elliptic divergence-form equations; see for example
[
9
, Chapter 8]
. Choose a cutoff
\chi_{R}
satisfying
\chi_{R}\equiv 1
on
\operatorname{Ann}(R)
, supported in
B_{16R}\setminus B_{R/16}
, and
\left\lvert\nabla\chi_{R}\right\rvert\leq C/R
. Testing
Lu=0
against
\chi_{R}^{2}u
gives

0=\int\langle A\nabla u,\nabla(\chi_{R}^{2}u)\rangle\,dx.

Uniform ellipticity, boundedness of
A
, and Young’s inequality imply

\int\chi_{R}^{2}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{2}\,dx\leq C\int u^{2}\left\lvert\nabla\chi_{R}\right\rvert^{2}\,dx.

Using (
2.3
) on the support of
\nabla\chi_{R}
gives (
2.6
).
∎

Let
X
be any smooth vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
which agrees with
B\nabla u
outside
B_{1/16}
. The arbitrary compactly supported modification near the pole is harmless; changing it only changes the eventual harmonic coefficient by a fixed vector. We have the following endpoint mass bounds for
X
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
R\geq 2
,

(2.7)

\int_{\operatorname{Ann}(R)}\left\lvert X\right\rvert\,dx\leq C\eta(R).

Consequently, if

S_{j}=B_{2^{j+1}}\setminus B_{2^{j}},\qquad m_{j}=\int_{S_{j}}\left\lvert X\right\rvert\,dx,\qquad j\geq 0,

then

(2.8)

m_{j}\to 0.

Moreover,

(2.9)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\left\lvert X(x)\right\rvert}{1+\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}}\,dx<\infty.

Proof.

On
\operatorname{Ann}(R)
,
\left\lvert B\right\rvert\leq\eta(R)/R
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\operatorname{Ann}(R)}\left\lvert X\right\rvert\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta(R)}{R}\int_{\operatorname{Ann}(R)}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta(R)}{R}\left(\int_{\operatorname{Ann}(R)}\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\left\lvert\operatorname{Ann}(R)\right\rvert^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta(R)}{R}\cdot CR^{-1/2}\cdot CR^{3/2}\leq C\eta(R).

Since each
S_{j}
is contained in a fixed finite union of annuli of the form
\operatorname{Ann}(2^{j})
, (
2.8
) follows. Finally,

\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{S_{j}}\frac{\left\lvert X(x)\right\rvert}{1+\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}}\,dx\leq C\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-2j}m_{j}<\infty,

because
m_{j}
is bounded and tends to zero. The compact part is finite by construction.
∎

3.
Endpoint Newtonian potential

Let

K(z)=\frac{z}{\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{3}},\qquad z\neq 0.

Formally, the solution of
\Delta w=\operatorname{div}X
is

(3.1)

w(x)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle K(x-y),X(y)\rangle\,dy.

At the endpoint
X
may not belong to
L^{1}
, so the construction is justified using the weighted estimate (
2.9
).

Lemma 3.1
.

The integral in (
3.1
) is absolutely convergent for a.e.
x
, defines a function
w\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and satisfies

(3.2)

\Delta w=\operatorname{div}X

in the sense of distributions.

Proof.

Since
X\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, let
B(x_{0},r)
be a compact ball. Split the
y
-integration into

E_{1}=B_{2\left\lvert x_{0}\right\rvert+4r+4},\qquad E_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus E_{1}.

For
y\in E_{1}
,

\int_{B(x_{0},r)}\frac{dx}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}\leq Cr,

because
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{-2}
is locally integrable in dimension three. Hence

\int_{B(x_{0},r)}\int_{E_{1}}\frac{\left\lvert X(y)\right\rvert}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}\,dy\,dx\leq Cr\int_{E_{1}}\left\lvert X(y)\right\rvert\,dy<\infty.

For
y\in E_{2}
and
x\in B(x_{0},r)
, one has
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq c\left\lvert y\right\rvert
. Thus

\int_{B(x_{0},r)}\int_{E_{2}}\frac{\left\lvert X(y)\right\rvert}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}\,dy\,dx\leq C\left\lvert B(x_{0},r)\right\rvert\int_{E_{2}}\frac{\left\lvert X(y)\right\rvert}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}}\,dy<\infty

by (
2.9
). Tonelli’s theorem gives
w\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and absolute convergence for a.e.
x
.

For the distributional identity, first truncate

w_{N}(x)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\left\lvert y\right\rvert\leq 2^{N}}\langle K(x-y),X(y)\rangle\,dy.

The preceding estimates show
w_{N}\to w
in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
. If
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then Fubini is justified for
w_{N}
. Since

K(x-y)=-\nabla_{x}|x-y|^{-1},\qquad\Delta_{x}|x-y|^{-1}=-4\pi\delta_{y},

we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(x-y)\Delta\varphi(x)\,dx=-4\pi\nabla\varphi(y).

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w_{N}\Delta\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\left\lvert y\right\rvert\leq 2^{N}}X(y)\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(x-y)\Delta\varphi(x)\,dx\right)dy

\displaystyle=-\int_{\left\lvert y\right\rvert\leq 2^{N}}X(y)\cdot\nabla\varphi(y)\,dy.

Letting
N\to\infty
gives

\int w\Delta\varphi=-\int X\cdot\nabla\varphi,

which is exactly (
3.2
).
∎

For dyadic
R=2^{N}
and for a fixed parameter
\sigma>0
, define

(3.3)

P_{R}^{\sigma}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq\sigma R}X(x)\,dx.

The parameter
\sigma
will be chosen once the target annulus is fixed.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every fixed
\sigma>0
, along dyadic
R\to\infty
,

(3.4)

\left\lvert P_{R}^{\sigma}\right\rvert=o(\log R).

Moreover, if
0<\sigma_{1}<\sigma_{2}<\infty
are fixed, then

(3.5)

P_{R}^{\sigma_{2}}-P_{R}^{\sigma_{1}}=o(1).

Proof.

With
m_{j}
as in Lemma
2.2
, choose an integer
C_{\sigma}
such that
\sigma 2^{N}\leq 2^{N+C_{\sigma}}
for all
N
. Then

\left\lvert P_{2^{N}}^{\sigma}\right\rvert\leq C+\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{0\leq j\leq N+C_{\sigma}}m_{j}.

Since
m_{j}\to 0
, Lemma
A.1
gives

N^{-1}\sum_{0\leq j\leq N+C_{\sigma}}m_{j}\to 0.

Hence
\left\lvert P_{2^{N}}^{\sigma}\right\rvert=o(N)=o(\log R)
.

For (
3.5
), the difference is the integral of
X
over the annulus
\sigma_{1}R<\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq\sigma_{2}R
. This annulus is covered by a number of dyadic shells depending only on
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
, and the mass of each such shell is
o(1)
.
∎

We obtain an expansion for
w
depending on
P_{R}^{\sigma}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}=B_{\Lambda}\setminus B_{\lambda},\qquad 0<\lambda<\Lambda<\infty,

and fix
0<\sigma<\lambda/4
. For every
1\leq q<3/2
, along dyadic
R\to\infty
,

(3.6)

\left\|R^{2}w(R\cdot)-\frac{\langle P_{R}^{\sigma},y\rangle}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{3}}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda})}\to 0.

Proof.

Fix
y\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
and set
x=Ry
. Choose a constant
M>2\Lambda
, for instance
M=4\Lambda+4
. Decompose the
z
-space into

\Omega_{\mathrm{in}}=B_{\sigma R},\qquad\Omega_{\mathrm{mid}}=B_{MR}\setminus B_{\sigma R},\qquad\Omega_{\mathrm{out}}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{MR}.

Then

R^{2}w(Ry)-\frac{\langle P_{R}^{\sigma},y\rangle}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{3}}=I_{\mathrm{in}}(y)+I_{\mathrm{mid}}(y)+I_{\mathrm{out}}(y),

where

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{in}}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{2}}{4\pi}\int_{\left\lvert z\right\rvert\leq\sigma R}\langle K(Ry-z)-K(Ry),X(z)\rangle\,dz,

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{mid}}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{2}}{4\pi}\int_{\sigma R<\left\lvert z\right\rvert<MR}\langle K(Ry-z),X(z)\rangle\,dz,

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{out}}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{2}}{4\pi}\int_{\left\lvert z\right\rvert\geq MR}\langle K(Ry-z),X(z)\rangle\,dz.

For the inner part,
\left\lvert z\right\rvert\leq\sigma R
and
\left\lvert Ry\right\rvert\geq\lambda R
imply

\left\lvert Ry-tz\right\rvert\geq(\lambda-\sigma)R\geq\frac{3}{4}\lambda R,\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1.

Since
\left\lvert\nabla K(\xi)\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{-3}
,

\left\lvert K(Ry-z)-K(Ry)\right\rvert\leq C_{\lambda,\sigma}\frac{\left\lvert z\right\rvert}{R^{3}}.

Therefore

\left\lvert I_{\mathrm{in}}(y)\right\rvert\leq\frac{C}{R}\int_{\left\lvert z\right\rvert\leq\sigma R}\left\lvert z\right\rvert\left\lvert X(z)\right\rvert\,dz\leq\frac{C}{R}\sum_{0\leq j\leq N+C_{\sigma}}2^{j}m_{j}=o(1),

by Lemma
A.1
. This convergence is uniform in
y
.

For the middle part, Minkowski’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\left\lVert I_{\mathrm{mid}}\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda})}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\sigma R<\left\lvert z\right\rvert<MR}\left\lvert X(z)\right\rvert\left(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}}\frac{R^{2q}}{\left\lvert Ry-z\right\rvert^{2q}}\,dy\right)^{1/q}dz.

Writing
Z=z/R
,

\int_{\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}}\frac{R^{2q}}{\left\lvert Ry-z\right\rvert^{2q}}\,dy=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}}\frac{dy}{\left\lvert y-Z\right\rvert^{2q}}\leq C_{q,\lambda,\Lambda,M},

because
2q<3
. Hence

\left\lVert I_{\mathrm{mid}}\right\rVert_{L^{q}}\leq C\int_{\sigma R<\left\lvert z\right\rvert<MR}\left\lvert X(z)\right\rvert\,dz=o(1),

since the latter region consists of only finitely many dyadic shells around scale
R
, with the number depending only on
\sigma
and
M
.

For the outer part,
\left\lvert z\right\rvert\geq MR
and
\left\lvert Ry\right\rvert\leq\Lambda R
imply
\left\lvert Ry-z\right\rvert\geq c\left\lvert z\right\rvert
. Choose an integer
C_{M}
depending only on
M
such that
2^{C_{M}}\leq M
. Then

\left\lvert I_{\mathrm{out}}(y)\right\rvert\leq CR^{2}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\int_{2^{N+C_{M}+\ell}\leq\left\lvert z\right\rvert\leq 2^{N+C_{M}+\ell+1}}\frac{\left\lvert X(z)\right\rvert}{\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{2}}\,dz\leq C\sum_{\ell\geq 0}4^{-\ell}m_{N+C_{M}+\ell}=o(1),

again by Lemma
A.1
. This estimate is uniform in

. Combining the three bounds proves (
3.6
).
∎

4.
The harmonic remainder and the endpoint Green expansion

Set

(4.1)

h=u-w.

Outside a compact set containing the pole, one has
X=B\nabla u
and

\Delta u=\operatorname{div}(B\nabla u)
. Since Lemma
3.1
gives

\Delta w=\operatorname{div}X
, we obtain
\Delta h=0
there in the distributional sense.
Hence
h
is smooth and harmonic there.

Lemma 4.1

(Averaged decay of
w
)
.

For the annuli
\operatorname{Ann}(R)=B_{8R}\setminus B_{R/8}
,

(4.2)

\mathop{\!\!\int\!\!\!\!-}\nolimits_{\operatorname{Ann}(R)}\left\lvert w(x)\right\rvert\,dx\to 0.

Proof.

Let
R=2^{N}
first. The proof is again a three-region estimate. For
x\in\operatorname{Ann}(R)
, decompose the integral defining
w
into
\left\lvert z\right\rvert\leq R/16
,
R/16<\left\lvert z\right\rvert<16R
, and
\left\lvert z\right\rvert\geq 16R
.

For the inner region, write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\left\lvert z\right\rvert\leq R/16}\langle K(x-z),X(z)\rangle\,dz

\displaystyle=\langle K(x),P_{R}^{1/16}\rangle+\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{in}}(x),

where

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{in}}(x):=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\left\lvert z\right\rvert\leq R/16}\langle K(x-z)-K(x),X(z)\rangle\,dz.

The main term is bounded by
C\left\lvert P_{R}^{1/16}\right\rvert R^{-2}=o(1)
. The error satisfies

\left\lvert\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{in}}(x)\right\rvert\leq CR^{-3}\int_{\left\lvert z\right\rvert\leq R/16}\left\lvert z\right\rvert\left\lvert X\right\rvert(z)\,dz=o(R^{-2}),

by Lemma
A.1
.

For the middle region, Tonelli’s theorem and the local integrability of
\left\lvert x-z\right\rvert^{-2}
give, for each fixed
z
,

\int_{\operatorname{Ann}(R)}\frac{dx}{\left\lvert x-z\right\rvert^{2}}\leq CR.

Therefore the averaged contribution is at most

CR^{-3}\cdot R\int_{R/16<\left\lvert z\right\rvert<16R}\left\lvert X\right\rvert(z)\,dz=o(1).

For the outer region,
\left\lvert x-z\right\rvert\geq c\left\lvert z\right\rvert
and hence the contribution is bounded by

C\sum_{\ell\geq 0}4^{-\ell}m_{N+C+\ell}=o(1).

This proves (
4.2
) along dyadic
R
.

For a general
R\to\infty
, choose dyadic
R_{j}\leq R<2R_{j}
. Then
\operatorname{Ann}(R)
is contained in a fixed finite union of annuli comparable to
\operatorname{Ann}(R_{j})
and
\operatorname{Ann}(2R_{j})
, while the volume ratios are uniformly bounded. The dyadic result gives the general result.
∎

The following exterior harmonic expansion holds.

.

The harmonic remainder satisfies

By (

∎

5.
The

- and

-functionals

Assume now that
R_{g}\geq 0
. For
t>0
such that
1/t
is a regular value of

, define

(5.1)

F(t)=4\pi t-t^{2}\int_{\{u=1/t\}}H_{g}\left\lvert\nabla_{g}u\right\rvert\,dA_{g}+t^{3}\int_{\{u=1/t\}}\left\lvert\nabla_{g}u\right\rvert^{2}\,dA_{g}.

Here

H_{g}=-\operatorname{div}_{g}\left(\frac{\nabla_{g}u}{|\nabla_{g}u|}\right)

is the mean curvature of the level set with respect to the normal
\nabla_{g}u/|\nabla_{g}u|
, in the sign convention used throughout the paper, see
[
2
]
or Appendix
C
. By Proposition
C.2
, the right-continuous representative of

satisfies

(5.2)

F(t)\geq 0,\qquad F\text{ is non-decreasing.}

Fix
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,1))
with
\psi\geq 0
and
\int_{0}^{1}\psi=1
. Define

(5.3)

\mathcal{E}(a,s)=\int_{a+s}^{2a+2s}\frac{F(t)}{t^{3}}\,dt,\qquad 0\leq s\leq a,

and

(5.4)

D(a)=\int_{0}^{a}\psi(s/a)\mathcal{E}(a,s)\,ds.

As in Mazurowski–Yao
[
18
, Propositions 14 and 15]
,

(5.5)

D(a)\geq 0,\qquad a\mapsto aD(a)\text{ is non-decreasing}.

Indeed,

aD(a)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\psi(\vartheta)}{(1+\vartheta)^{2}}\left(\int_{1}^{2}\frac{F((1+\vartheta)a\tau)}{\tau^{3}}\,d\tau\right)d\vartheta,

which is monotone in
a
by (
5.2
).

Using the coarea formula and the identity for the derivative of
\int_{\{u=1/t\}}\left\lvert\nabla_{g}u\right\rvert^{2}dA_{g}
recorded in this normalization in
[
18
, Appendix A, Proposition 25]
, one obtains

(5.6)

D(a)=c_{\psi}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi(au(x))\left\lvert\nabla_{g}u(x)\right\rvert^{3}a^{3}\,dv_{g}(x),

where

(5.7)

c_{\psi}=2\pi\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\psi(s)}{1+s}\,ds

and

(5.8)

\phi(t)=\frac{1}{t^{3}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\psi\left(\frac{1}{2t}-1\right)-\psi\left(\frac{1}{t}-1\right)\right].

Since

,

(5.9)

\operatorname{spt}\phi\Subset(1/4,1).

a>0
, set

Here

denotes the coefficient rescaling of the metric matrix, not the pullback metric under

. With this convention,

|\nabla_{g_{a}}u_{a}(y)|=a^{2}|\nabla_{g}u(ay)|,\qquad dv_{g_{a}}(y)=\sqrt{\det(g_{kl})(ay)}\,dy.

After the change of variables

,

(5.10)

Lemma 5.1
.

There is a fixed annulus

\Omega=B_{\Lambda}\setminus B_{\lambda},\qquad 0<\lambda<\Lambda<\infty,

such that for all sufficiently large

\operatorname{spt}\bigl(\phi(\rho)\bigr)\Subset\Omega.

Choose

. Choose

The two-sided Green estimate (

On this fixed annulus define the Banach-space functional

Lemma 5.2
.

One has

Proof.

By Lemma

,
\operatorname{spt}(\phi(\rho))\Subset\Omega
. Since

and

With

Using the definition

we compute

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{-1}\psi\left(\frac{1}{2\tau}-1\right)\,d\tau-\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{-1}\psi\left(\frac{1}{\tau}-1\right)\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\psi(s)}{1+s}\,ds-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\psi(s)}{1+s}\,ds

Quadratic expansion of the

We prove the quadratic expansion at the Euclidean model.

.

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\phi(\rho)\left[\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{tr}k)\left\lvert\nabla\rho\right\rvert^{3}-\frac{3}{2}\left\lvert\nabla\rho\right\rvert\,k(\nabla\rho,\nabla\rho)\right]dy,

We may assume
\theta\leq 1
by decreasing
\delta_{0}
. Since

, so

(6.4)

\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{C^{0}}+\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{r}}\leq C_{r}\beta,\qquad 1\leq r\leq p.

The annulus
\Omega
is separated from the origin, hence
\rho
and
\nabla\rho
are smooth and bounded on
\Omega
. Decreasing
\delta_{0}
if necessary,
\delta+k
is uniformly positive definite and
\rho+v
stays in a compact subset of
(0,\infty)
on which all derivatives of
\phi
are bounded.

First keep the metric Euclidean. Taylor’s theorem gives the pointwise estimate

(6.5)

\phi(\rho+v)=\phi(\rho)+\phi^{\prime}(\rho)v+R_{\phi},\qquad|R_{\phi}|\leq C\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{C^{0}}^{2}.

The elementary cubic inequality

(6.6)

\left||X+Y|^{3}-|X|^{3}-3|X|\langle X,Y\rangle\right|\leq C(|X||Y|^{2}+|Y|^{3})

with
X=\nabla\rho
and
Y=\nabla v
gives

(6.7)

|\nabla(\rho+v)|^{3}=|\nabla\rho|^{3}+3|\nabla\rho|\langle\nabla\rho,\nabla v\rangle+R_{\nabla},\qquad|R_{\nabla}|\leq C(|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{3}).

Multiplying (
6.5
) and (
6.7
), the terms linear in
v
are exactly

\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(\rho)|\nabla\rho|^{3}v\,dy+3\int_{\Omega}\phi(\rho)|\nabla\rho|\langle\nabla\rho,\nabla v\rangle\,dy.

All remaining function-only terms are quadratic. Indeed, using (
6.4
),

\int_{\Omega}|R_{\phi}|\,dy\leq C\beta^{2},\qquad\int_{\Omega}|R_{\nabla}|\,dy\leq C(\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{3}}^{3})\leq C\beta^{2},

and the mixed first-order product obeys

\int_{\Omega}|v|\,|\nabla v|\,dy\leq\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{C^{0}}\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\leq C\beta^{2}.

The remaining products are also quadratic:

\int_{\Omega}|R_{\phi}|(1+|\nabla v|+|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{3})\,dy+\int_{\Omega}|v|\,|R_{\nabla}|\,dy\leq C\beta^{2}.

Thus

(6.8)

\left|\mathscr{D}(\delta,\rho+v)-\mathscr{D}(\delta,\rho)-\mathcal{L}_{D}(0,v)\right|\leq C\beta^{2}.

Now include the metric perturbation. For a positive definite matrix
H
near
I
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, set

\Psi(H,\xi)=\langle H^{-1}\xi,\xi\rangle^{3/2}\sqrt{\det H}.

Uniform Taylor expansion at
I
gives

(6.9)

\Psi(I+S,\xi)=|\xi|^{3}+\mathcal{M}(S,\xi)+R_{\mathcal{M}}(S,\xi),

where

\mathcal{M}(S,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{tr}S)|\xi|^{3}-\frac{3}{2}|\xi|S(\xi,\xi),\qquad|R_{\mathcal{M}}(S,\xi)|\leq C|S|^{2}|\xi|^{3}.

Applying this with
S=k
and
\xi=\nabla(\rho+v)
, the pure metric remainder satisfies

\int_{\Omega}|\phi(\rho+v)R_{\mathcal{M}}(k,\nabla(\rho+v))|\,dy\leq C\alpha^{2}\int_{\Omega}(1+|\nabla v|^{3})\,dy\leq C\alpha^{2}.

It remains to replace the linear metric term

\phi(\rho+v)\mathcal{M}(k,\nabla(\rho+v))
by

\phi(\rho)\mathcal{M}(k,\nabla\rho)
. Since
\nabla\rho
is bounded on
\Omega
, and since
\mathcal{M}
is linear in
k
and cubic in
\xi
,

(6.10)

|\mathcal{M}(k,\nabla(\rho+v))-\mathcal{M}(k,\nabla\rho)|\leq C\alpha(|\nabla v|+|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{3}).

Moreover,

|\phi(\rho+v)-\phi(\rho)|\leq C|v|.

Consequently

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\phi(\rho+v)\mathcal{M}(k,\nabla(\rho+v))-\phi(\rho)\mathcal{M}(k,\nabla\rho)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha\int_{\Omega}(|v|+|\nabla v|+|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{3})dy\leq C\alpha\beta\leq C\theta^{2}.

Combining this estimate with (
6.8
) and the pure metric remainder, the first variation is precisely the expression in (
6.1
), and every leftover term is bounded by
C\theta^{2}
. This proves (
6.3
).
∎

Appendix A
Elementary dyadic estimates

Lemma A.1
.

Let

for

and suppose that

Appendix B
Perturbative elliptic upgrade

This appendix proves the elliptic estimate used in Propositions
4.4
and
4.6
. The applications in the paper involve smooth functions on fixed annuli; accordingly the estimates below are stated for smooth solutions. This avoids any unnecessary weak-solution approximation issue. The constants depend only on the indicated exponents and the inclusion of domains, not on derivatives of the coefficients.

Lemma B.1

(Constant-coefficient local estimate)
.

Let
1\leq q\leq p
,
3<p<\infty
, and let
B_{r}\Subset B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Suppose
v
is smooth,
G\in L^{p}(B_{R};\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and

\Delta v=\operatorname{div}G

in
B_{R}
. Then

(B.1)

\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\leq C\left((R-r)^{-\gamma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{q}(B_{R})}+\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\right),\qquad\gamma=1+3/q-3/p,

where
C
depends only on
p,q
.

Proof.

We first prove the normalized estimate for
B_{1/2}\Subset B_{1}
. Let
z\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{1})
solve

\Delta z=\operatorname{div}G,\qquad z|_{\partial B_{1}}=0.

The Calderon–Zygmund estimate for the Dirichlet Laplacian, see for example
[
9
, Chapter 9]
, gives

\left\lVert\nabla z\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\leq C_{p}\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{1})}.

Since
p>3
, Sobolev–Poincare inequality implies

\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{L^{q}(B_{1})}\leq C_{p,q}\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{1})}.

The difference
h=v-z
is harmonic in
B_{1}
. Harmonic interior estimates give

\left\lVert\nabla h\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{1/2})}\leq C_{p,q}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{q}(B_{1})}.

Combining the last three inequalities yields

\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{1/2})}\leq C\left(\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{q}(B_{1})}+\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\right).

After scaling, the same estimate holds on
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\Subset B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
with the factor
\rho^{-\gamma}
in front of
\|v\|_{L^{q}}
, where

\gamma=1+3/q-3/p>0.

For a general concentric pair
B_{r}\Subset B_{R}
, set
\rho=(R-r)/4
and cover
B_{r}
by balls
B_{\rho}(x_{\ell})
with centers in
B_{r}
so that the enlarged balls
B_{2\rho}(x_{\ell})
have uniformly bounded overlap. Since
B_{2\rho}(x_{\ell})\subset B_{R}
, the scaled estimate applies on each
B_{\rho}(x_{\ell})\Subset B_{2\rho}(x_{\ell})
. Summing the
p
-th powers, using bounded overlap for the
G
term, and using
q\leq p
together with bounded overlap for the
v
term, gives

\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\leq C\left(\rho^{-\gamma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{q}(B_{R})}+\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\right).

Since
\rho
is comparable to
R-r
, this is (
B.1
). The exponent
\gamma
is exactly the scaling loss from the
L^{q}
norm of
v
to the
L^{p}
norm of
\nabla v
.
∎

Lemma B.2

(Hole-filling iteration)
.

Let
0<r_{0}<r_{1}
and let
\Phi:[r_{0},r_{1}]\to[0,\infty)
be bounded. Suppose that for some
0<\theta<1
,
A_{0}\geq 0
, and
\gamma>0
,

(B.2)

\Phi(s)\leq\theta\Phi(t)+\frac{A_{0}}{(t-s)^{\gamma}}\qquad\text{whenever }r_{0}\leq s<t\leq r_{1}.

Then

(B.3)

\Phi(r_{0})\leq C_{\theta,\gamma}\frac{A_{0}}{(r_{1}-r_{0})^{\gamma}}.

Proof.

Choose
\tau\in(0,1)
sufficiently close to
1
so that

(B.4)

\theta\tau^{-\gamma}<1.

Set

r_{k}=r_{0}+(1-\tau^{k})(r_{1}-r_{0}),\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots.

Then
r_{k+1}-r_{k}=(1-\tau)\tau^{k}(r_{1}-r_{0})
. Applying (
B.2
) with
s=r_{k}
and
t=r_{k+1}
gives

\Phi(r_{k})\leq\theta\Phi(r_{k+1})+A_{0}(1-\tau)^{-\gamma}\tau^{-k\gamma}(r_{1}-r_{0})^{-\gamma}.

Iterating from
k=0
to
N-1
gives

\displaystyle\Phi(r_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{N}\Phi(r_{N})+A_{0}(1-\tau)^{-\gamma}(r_{1}-r_{0})^{-\gamma}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\theta^{k}\tau^{-k\gamma}.

Since
\Phi
is bounded and
\theta^{N}\to 0
, the terminal term tends to zero. The series converges by (
B.4
). Letting

proves the estimate, with

C_{\theta,\gamma}=\frac{(1-\tau)^{-\gamma}}{1-\theta\tau^{-\gamma}}.

∎

(Local perturbative

Proposition B.4

(Sequence upgrade from

to

.

Appendix C
Endpoint validity of the
F/D
machinery

This appendix records the functional facts used in Sections
5
–
8
. The purpose is to separate the issue of asymptotic decay from the level-set identities. Throughout this appendix,
g
is a smooth complete metric on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
R_{g}\geq 0
, and
u
is the positive Green function with pole at the origin, normalized by

\int_{\{u=s\}}|\nabla_{g}u|\,dA_{g}=4\pi

for a.e. regular level
s>0
. We assume only that
u\to 0
at infinity and that the level sets in question are compact. In the endpoint situation these properties follow from the two-sided Green estimate (
2.3
).

All level-set integrals below are first understood on regular levels. Equivalently, in the Agostiniani–Mazzieri–Oronzio formulation one integrates over the regular part
\Sigma_{t}\setminus\operatorname{Crit}(u)
and then passes to the locally absolutely continuous representative of the resulting function. Whenever monotonicity is used,
F
denotes this right-continuous non-decreasing representative.

Lemma C.1

(Properness and connectedness of regular levels)
.

For each regular value
s>0
, the level set

\Gamma_{s}=\{u=s\}

is compact and connected.

Proof.

Compactness follows from
u(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
and
u(x)\to\infty
at the pole. Let
\Gamma
be a connected component of
\Gamma_{s}
. By the Jordan–Brouwer separation theorem,
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Gamma
has one bounded component, denoted
\Omega_{\Gamma}
, whose boundary is
\Gamma
. If
\Omega_{\Gamma}
did not contain the pole, then
u
would be harmonic on
\Omega_{\Gamma}
and equal to
s
on
\partial\Omega_{\Gamma}
; the maximum principle would force
u\equiv s
on
\Omega_{\Gamma}
, contradicting that
s
is a regular value. Hence the pole lies in
\Omega_{\Gamma}
for every component
\Gamma
.

Suppose two distinct components
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
existed. Since both bounded regions contain the pole and the components are disjoint, the corresponding bounded regions are nested after relabeling, say

\Omega_{\Gamma_{1}}\Subset\Omega_{\Gamma_{2}}.

The annular region
\Omega_{\Gamma_{2}}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{\Gamma_{1}}}
contains no pole. The function
u
is harmonic there and takes the same boundary value
s
on both boundary components. The maximum principle again gives
u\equiv s
on this annular region, impossible for a regular level. Therefore only one component can occur, and
\Gamma_{s}
is connected.
∎

Proposition C.2

(The
F
-functional in the present normalization)
.

For regular
t>0
, set

\Sigma_{t}=\Gamma_{1/t}=\{u=1/t\}.

Define

(C.1)

F(t)=4\pi t-t^{2}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{g}|\nabla_{g}u|\,dA_{g}+t^{3}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}\,dA_{g},

where

H_{g}=-\operatorname{div}_{g}\left(\frac{\nabla_{g}u}{|\nabla_{g}u|}\right).

Let
\mathrm{II}
denote the second fundamental form of
\Sigma_{t}
and
\mathring{\mathrm{II}}
its trace-free part. Then
F
has a locally absolutely continuous representative, and for a.e.
t
,

(C.2)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(t)=4\pi+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma_{t}}+\frac{|\nabla_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla_{g}u||^{2}}{|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}R_{g}+\frac{1}{2}|\mathring{\mathrm{II}}|^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{2|\nabla_{g}u|}{u}-H_{g}\right)^{2}\right)dA_{g}.

Consequently, if
R_{g}\geq 0
, then

is non-decreasing and

.

Proof.

Formula (
C.2
) is the Agostiniani–Mazzieri–Oronzio monotonicity identity
[
2

, written in the convention
u_{\mathrm{AMO}}=1-u
and with the level parameter

. Its proof is local on regular level sets and uses only the harmonicity of
u
, the Bochner identity, the first variation of level-set geometry, and the flux normalization; it does not require any asymptotic expansion at infinity.

By Lemma
C.1
, each regular level is connected. Hence Gauss–Bonnet gives

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}R_{\Sigma_{t}}\,dA_{g}=4\pi\chi(\Sigma_{t})\leq 8\pi.

Therefore

4\pi-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}R_{\Sigma_{t}}\,dA_{g}\geq 0.

All other terms in (

) are non-negative when
R_{g}\geq 0
. Thus
F^{\prime}\geq 0
a.e., so the right-continuous representative of
F
is non-decreasing.

Near the Green pole the metric is smooth and
u
has the usual Euclidean singular expansion in normal coordinates. Substituting this local expansion into (
C.1
) gives

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}F(t)=0.

Since
F
is non-decreasing,
F(t)\geq 0
for all
t>0
.
∎

Proposition C.3

(Derivative of the Dirichlet level quantity)
.

Let

I(t)=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}\,dA_{g}.

Then
I
is locally absolutely continuous and, for a.e.
t
,

(C.3)

I^{\prime}(t)=-t^{-2}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{g}|\nabla_{g}u|\,dA_{g}.

Proof.

This is the first variation formula for integrals over the level sets
\{u=1/t\}
. If
s=1/t
, the normal speed of the level
\{u=s\}
under variation of
t
is
-t^{-2}/|\nabla_{g}u|
. Applying the standard first variation formula to
|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}
and using
\Delta_{g}u=0
gives (
C.3
). As with the
F
-identity, this is a local computation on compact regular level sets.
∎

Proposition C.4

(The
D
-functional identities)
.

Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,1))
,
\psi\geq 0
,
\int_{0}^{1}\psi=1
, and define
\mathcal{E},D,c_{\psi},\phi
as in (
5.3
)–(
5.8
). Then

D(a)\geq 0,\qquad a\mapsto aD(a)\quad\text{is non-decreasing},

and

(C.4)

D(a)=c_{\psi}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi(au(x))|\nabla_{g}u(x)|^{3}a^{3}\,dv_{g}(x).

After the rescaling
u_{a}(y)=au(ay)
and
g_{a}(y)=g(ay)
,

(C.5)

D(a)=c_{\psi}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi(u_{a}(y))|\nabla_{g_{a}}u_{a}(y)|^{3}\,dv_{g_{a}}(y).

Proof.

Non-negativity and monotonicity are the
D
-functional facts used in
[
18
, Propositions 14 and 15]
. Non-negativity is immediate from
F\geq 0
. For monotonicity, write
s=a\vartheta
and then
t=(1+\vartheta)a\tau
in the defining integral:

aD(a)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\psi(\vartheta)}{(1+\vartheta)^{2}}\left(\int_{1}^{2}\frac{F((1+\vartheta)a\tau)}{\tau^{3}}\,d\tau\right)d\vartheta.

Since
F
is non-decreasing and
\psi\geq 0
, this expression is non-decreasing in
a
.

Let
T=a+s
. Using Proposition
C.3
,

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\frac{F(t)}{t^{3}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{T}^{2T}\left(\frac{4\pi}{t^{2}}-\frac{1}{t}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{g}|\nabla_{g}u|\,dA_{g}+I(t)\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{T}+[tI(t)]_{t=T}^{2T}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{T}+\int_{\{u=1/(2T)\}}\frac{|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}}{u}\,dA_{g}-\int_{\{u=1/T\}}\frac{|\nabla_{g}u|^{2}}{u}\,dA_{g}.

Integrating this identity against
\psi(s/a)\,ds
and applying the coarea formula gives (
C.4
) with the stated
c_{\psi}
and
\phi
. The level bands involved are compact because
u\to 0
at infinity and
u\to\infty
at the pole. The change of variables
x=ay
gives (
C.5
).
∎

Proposition C.5

(
D\equiv 0
forces
F\equiv 0
)
.

If
D(a)=0
for every
a>0
, then the right-continuous monotone representative of
F
vanishes identically.

Proof.

Suppose that
F(t_{0})>0
for some
t_{0}>0
. By monotonicity,
F(t)\geq F(t_{0})>0
for all
t\geq t_{0}
. Choose an interval
I\Subset(0,1)
on which
\psi>0
, choose
\vartheta_{0}\in I
, and set
a=t_{0}/(1+\vartheta_{0})
. For all
\vartheta
in a smaller interval
I_{0}\Subset I
containing
\vartheta_{0}
, the interval
((1+\vartheta)a,2(1+\vartheta)a)
intersects
[t_{0},\infty)
in a set of positive measure. Hence

\int_{(1+\vartheta)a}^{2(1+\vartheta)a}\frac{F(t)}{t^{3}}\,dt>0\qquad\text{for }\vartheta\in I_{0}.

Since
\psi>0
on
I_{0}
, the representation of
D(a)
gives
D(a)>0
, a contradiction.
∎

Proposition C.6

(AMO
F
-rigidity)
.

If
R_{g}\geq 0
and the right-continuous monotone representative of
F
satisfies
F\equiv 0
, then
(\mathbb{R}^{3},g)
is isometric to Euclidean space.

Proof.

We verify the hypotheses in
[
2
, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.3]
. The manifold is smooth, complete, noncompact, and has
R_{g}\geq 0
by assumption. The endpoint decay (
2.1
) makes
g
uniformly equivalent to the Euclidean metric outside a compact set, while smoothness gives uniform equivalence on the compact part. In particular the Green function with Euclidean two-sided bounds constructed in Section
2
exists; equivalently,
(\mathbb{R}^{3},g)
is nonparabolic. The topological condition is also satisfied because

H_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{Z})=0.

Our Green function
u
is the minimal positive Green function normalized to have flux
4\pi
over regular levels, so if
G_{o}
denotes the minimal positive Green function in the normalization used by
[
2
]
, then
u=4\pi G_{o}
. Consequently

u_{\mathrm{AMO}}=1-4\pi G_{o}=1-u

is exactly the maximal distributional solution used in
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
; it tends to
1
at infinity and has the prescribed pole at the origin, with the sign convention of
[
2
]
. The level-set integrals in Proposition
C.2
are the same as the AMO integrals over the regular part of the levels after the change of parameter
t=1/u
and the convention
u_{\mathrm{AMO}}=1-u
.

Their Corollary 1.3 states, under these hypotheses, that if the monotone quantity of Theorem 1.1 has limit zero at infinity, then the manifold is isometric to
(\mathbb{R}^{3},g_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})
. In the present situation
F\equiv 0
, so the required limit is zero. Therefore
[
2
, Corollary 1.3]
applies and gives flatness.
∎
