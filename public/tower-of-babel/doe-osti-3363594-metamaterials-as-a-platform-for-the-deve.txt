Executive Summary
The primary objective of this research program was to explore the fundamental properties of metamaterials
and metasurfaces and their potential for control of energy at the sub-wavelength scale. The effort focuses
on electromagnetic metasurfaces fashioned from dielectrics, which offer a new platform for the discovery
and design of materials with novel structures, functions, and properties. The research activities emphasized
a complete basic research program including conceptual/computational design, machine learning,
fabrication, and characterization. A key component of the project was the use of machine learning to explore
the fundamental properties of these materials and to aid in the discovery, prediction, and design of new
materials.
The major technical objectives were:
1. Machine Learning the Interactions of Light and Matter
2. Zero Rank and Coherent Perfect Absorbers
3. Bound States in the Continuum
4. Spatial-Temporal Metasurfaces
Project Activities and Accomplishments
1. Project Objectives and Overarching Methodology
The primary objective of this research program was to investigate the fundamental physical behavior of
artificial electromagnetic materials—specifically metamaterials and metasurfaces—to achieve
unprecedented control over light-matter interactions in the terahertz (THz) and infrared (IR) regimes. The
initial hypotheses was that manipulating the geometry and constituent materials of sub-wavelength resonant
architectures could yield dynamic, tunable, and low-loss optical devices, overcoming the fundamental
limitations of natural materials and conventional metallic metamaterials.
The methodology necessitated a transition from static metallic structures to dynamic, all-dielectric, and
hybrid material platforms (e.g., graphene, micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS), and thermochromic
materials). The experimental approach combined full-wave electromagnetic simulation, cleanroom
fabrication, Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy, and Terahertz Time-Domain Spectroscopy
(THz-TDS).
2. Summary of Technical Accomplishments
2.1. Active, Dynamic, and Reconfigurable Metamaterials
A significant milestone was the realization of dynamic control over thermal emission and electromagnetic
scattering.
 • Thermochromic and MEMS-Based Infrared Control: Research demonstrated artificial
 thermochromic materials utilizing metamaterial MEMS. These devices exhibited strong
 temperature-dependent emission characteristics that locally circumvent standard Stefan-Boltzmann
 emission limits within specific thermal infrared bands. This architecture allowed for individual
 reconfigurability, enabling spatiotemporal infrared emission patterning operating at modulation
 frequencies up to 110 kHz.

 • Graphene-Based High-Speed Modulation: The program successfully developed a graphene
 metamaterial architecture capable of modulating free-space thermal infrared radiation. By
 eliminating channel resistance to maximize electrostatic modulation speed, the experimental
 devices (100 × 120𝜇𝜇𝑚𝑚2) achieved modulation rates of 2.6 GHz, demonstrating viable frequency-
 selective, all-electronic high-speed infrared devices.
 • Phototunable Dielectric Huygens' Metasurfaces: The capability to optically control the resonant
 eigenmodes of all-dielectric Huygens' metasurfaces was established. Experimental validation at
 1.03 THz yielded an intensity transmission modulation depth of 99.93% and an associated phase
 shift exceeding 𝜋𝜋/2 radians. Temporal coupled-mode theory (TCMT) confirmed that the tuning
 mechanism primarily governed the magnetic dipole-like odd eigenmode, breaking degeneracy and
 eliminating critical coupling.
2.2. All-Dielectric Metamaterials and Perfect Electromagnetic Absorption
The project systematically addressed the limitations imposed by Ohmic losses in metallic metamaterials by
pioneering all-dielectric metasurface alternatives.
 • Degenerate Critical Coupling: The theoretical framework for all-dielectric absorbers was
 rigorously defined utilizing TCMT. It was demonstrated that cylindrical dielectric arrays support
 hybrid electric (𝐸𝐸𝐻𝐻111) and hybrid magnetic (𝐻𝐻𝐸𝐸111) waveguide modes. By tuning the radiative
 loss rates to achieve frequency degeneracy, and matching the material loss rate to the radiative loss
 rate, a state of degenerate critical coupling was achieved.
 • Experimental Realization of All-Dielectric Absorbers: Leveraging this theoretical framework, a
 THz all-dielectric metasurface absorber was experimentally realized. By overlapping electric and
 magnetic dipole resonances, an experimental absorptivity of 97.5% was measured.
 • Zero-Rank Scattering Matrices: The research culminated in the classification of metasurface
 absorbers based on their scattering matrix rank and nullity. The conceptualization of a "Zero-Rank,
 Maximum Nullity" perfect electromagnetic wave absorber was experimentally demonstrated,
 expanding the paradigm of coherent perfect absorbers (CPAs) to systems capable of either coherent
 or incoherent absorption.
2.3. Computational Acceleration via Deep Learning Architectures
To address the high computational overhead inherent in inverse-scattering and numerical electromagnetic
optimization, deep neural network architectures were developed. These multi-layer perceptron (MLP)
networks successfully mapped multidimensional geometric parameter vectors to discretized S-parameter
spectra. Utilizing the novel Fast Forward Dictionary Search (FFDS) algorithm, the inverse modeling
problem was solved without susceptibility to local-minima trapping. The deployed deep learning models
achieved mean squared errors (MSE) on the order of 10−3 and executed parameter predictions five orders
of magnitude faster than conventional rigorous coupled-wave analysis or finite-element solvers.
3. Deviations from Planned Methodology and Impact
While the original project scope heavily emphasized metallic split-ring resonator (SRR) topologies,
empirical findings regarding intrinsic Ohmic dissipation at higher frequencies necessitated a strategic shift
toward all-dielectric and hybrid-graphene architectures. This deviation proved highly advantageous,

yielding the realization of high-Q degenerate critical coupling and ultra-fast (GHz) modulation speeds that
would have remained inaccessible via strictly metallic platforms.

The research program successfully navigated a fundamental transition from static, metallic metamaterial
architectures toward active, hybrid, and all-dielectric platforms to mitigate inherent Ohmic dissipation at
terahertz and infrared frequencies. By integrating graphene and micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS)
with sub-wavelength resonators, the project realized dynamic, real-time control over thermal emission and
free-space scattering. This approach yielded frequency-selective, ultra-fast electrostatic modulation
(achieving rates up to 2.6 GHz) and spatiotemporal thermal infrared patterning, effectively demonstrating
localized circumvention of static Stefan-Boltzmann emission constraints within designated spectral bands.
Investigations into all-dielectric metasurfaces established a rigorous theoretical framework for perfect
electromagnetic absorption devoid of metallic components. Utilizing Temporal Coupled Mode Theory
(TCMT), it was demonstrated that tuning the radiative and intrinsic material loss rates of orthogonal hybrid
waveguide modes (specifically the 𝐸𝐸𝐻𝐻111 and 𝐻𝐻𝐸𝐸111 modes) enables a state of degenerate critical coupling.
This foundational analysis culminated in the experimental realization of near-unity absorption in dielectric
arrays. Furthermore, this led to the formal classification of metasurface absorbers based on their scattering

matrix rank and nullity, culminating in the demonstration of "Zero-Rank, Maximum Nullity" systems. This
expands the paradigm of coherent perfect absorbers (CPAs) to encompass both coherent and incoherent
dissipative regimes.
To efficiently navigate the complex, high-dimensional parameter spaces associated with these advanced
resonant structures, the project formulated and deployed deep neural network architectures for inverse
electromagnetic design. The implementation of multi-layer perceptrons alongside novel search algorithms,
such as Fast Forward Dictionary Search (FFDS), effectively bypassed the non-uniqueness and local-minima
trapping inherent in one-to-many inverse scattering problems. This computational acceleration reduced
optical response prediction times by five orders of magnitude relative to conventional finite-element
solvers, while maintaining strict fidelity to the rigorous solutions of macroscopic Maxwell's equations.
Computational Modeling Specifications
The computational modeling efforts executed under this award relied on: rigorous full-wave
electromagnetic simulations, analytical formulations, and advanced deep learning architectures for rapid
inverse design. The specific parameters, theoretical frameworks, and validation methodologies for each
computational domain are detailed below.
1. Full-Wave Electromagnetic Simulation (Commercial Solvers)
 • Comprehensive three-dimensional numerical simulations were conducted utilizing CST
 Microwave Studio (versions 2014–2019) to determine the scattering parameters, near-field field
 distributions, and resonant absorption characteristics of thermochromic, graphene-based, and all-
 dielectric metasurfaces.
 • Computations were fundamentally grounded in the rigorous solution of macroscopic Maxwell's
 equations. The Frequency Domain solver, utilizing the Finite Element Method (FEM), and the
 Time Domain solver, utilizing the Finite Integration Technique (FIT), were employed depending
 on the specific resonance characteristics and dispersive material properties of the unit cells. To
 model infinite two-dimensional periodic arrays, Floquet-Bloch boundary conditions were applied
 to the unit cell boundaries, while open boundaries or perfectly matched layers (PML) were utilized
 along the axis of electromagnetic wave propagation.
 • Simulations necessitated high-performance computational workstations equipped with multi-core
 processors, substantial RAM, and GPU acceleration to manage dense tetrahedral and hexahedral
 computational meshes.
 • The numerical models were validated through direct comparison with empirical data obtained via
 Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy and Terahertz Time-Domain Spectroscopy (THz-
 TDS). Simulation integrity was ensured by enforcing strict convergence criteria, typically requiring
 an S-parameter variation of less than a specified threshold (e.g., Δ𝑆𝑆 < 0.02) between successive
 adaptive mesh refinement passes.
2. Analytical Frameworks: Temporal Coupled Mode Theory (TCMT)
 • Analytical modeling was formulated to elucidate the fundamental physical mechanisms governing
 wave-matter interactions. This was specifically applied to the analysis of degenerate critical
 coupling, coherent perfect absorption (CPA), and the role of dissipative loss in yielding zero-rank
 scattering matrices.

 • The analysis utilized Temporal Coupled Mode Theory (TCMT). This formalism expresses the
 dynamic amplitude for the eigenmode of the resonators (e.g., hybrid electric 𝐸𝐸𝐻𝐻111 and magnetic
 𝐻𝐻𝐻𝐻111 modes) as coupled ordinary differential equations. The mathematical framework rigorously
 defines the interplay between the intrinsic material loss rates (𝛾𝛾𝑙𝑙𝑙𝑙𝑙𝑙𝑙𝑙 ) and the radiative leakage rates
 (𝛾𝛾𝑟𝑟𝑟𝑟𝑟𝑟 ), dictating the conditions for impedance matching and perfect energy dissipation.
 • The mathematical formulations were validated by extracting specific mode parameters (resonance
 frequencies and Q-factors) from independent numerical eigenfrequency solvers. The analytical
 reflection and transmission coefficients generated by the TCMT models demonstrated excellent
 agreement with both full-wave simulations and experimental S-parameter profiles, confirming the
 validity of the underlying theoretical assumptions.
3. Deep Learning Architectures for Metasurface Design
 • Deep neural networks (DNNs) were developed and deployed for the accelerated forward modeling
 and inverse design of complex all-dielectric metasurfaces. This computational method explicitly
 addressed the high-dimensional nonlinear mapping between metasurface geometry and
 electromagnetic spectral responses.
 • The computational architecture employed multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) mapping
 multidimensional geometric parameter vectors directly to discretized S-parameter spectra. To solve
 the inverse-design problem, novel algorithmic methodologies, such as Fast Forward Dictionary
 Search (FFDS), were formulated to invert the physical relationships without suffering from the
 non-uniqueness and local-minima trapping inherent in one-to-many inverse scattering problems.
 • Model training, hyperparameter optimization, and inference execution were performed utilizing
 dedicated high-performance GPUs and standard machine learning computational frameworks (e.g.,
 PyTorch, TensorFlow).
 • Model performance was quantified using standard loss functions, primarily Mean Squared Error
 (MSE), evaluated on independent test datasets. The deep learning models consistently achieved
 average MSEs on the order of 10−3, predicting optical responses five orders of magnitude faster
 than conventional numerical solvers. All network predictions and proposed inverse designs were
 rigorously validated by cross-referencing the outputs against primary full-wave electromagnetic
 solutions.
