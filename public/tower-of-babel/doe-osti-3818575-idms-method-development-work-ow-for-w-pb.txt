 Executive Summary

The aim of this project is to synthesize a low uranium content, matrix-matched dust reference
material to serve as a surrogate for diluent material present in environment samples collected
during inspection activities and improve analytical eorts of trace anthropogenic actinides.
A major aspect of this eort is the accurate and precise characterization of key elements
within the synthesized reference material. Of greatest concern are those elements which
produce spectral interferences at the actinide mass range under common characterization
techniques such as secondary ion mass spectrometry and inductively coupled plasma - mass
spectrometry. In many cases, robust characterization of these trace analytes in the reference
material is challenging due to matrix eects and sample loss during processing. Conventional
external calibration methods used to determine analyte concentration may thus inaccurately
characterize this reference material. These issues are mitigated by isotope dilution mass
spectrometry, which is agnostic of sample loss during processing and permits sample pu-
rication, resulting in more stable and precise measurement regimes that are metrologically
traceable to certied reference materials.

This report serves as a characterization addendum to Bowden et al. (2025) and documents
the development of isotope dilution mass spectrometry (IDMS) method workows at Sa-
vannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) for the accurate and metrologically traceable
determination of uranium (U), thorium (Th), lead (Pb), and tungsten (W) assay concentra-
tions in environmental matrices. These elements were chosen based on the need to precisely
understand their concentrations due to their interference with actinide (primarily U/Pu)
measurements of environmental media. Dedicated chemical purication methods were de-
veloped for each analyte to isolate target elements from complex environmental matrices
prior to isotopic analysis. These procedures provide high analyte recoveries while eectively
removing matrix elements responsible for spectral interferences and signal suppression.

Existing certied U and Th reference materials were implemented for routine IDMS analysis,
including the development of new Th data processing workows capable of spike stripping
and isotope dilution calculations within a robust, metrologically traceable framework. The
development of Pb and W IDMS methods required the procurement and characterization
of isotopically enriched materials as well as associated data processing methods. The W
capability represents a signicant expansion of SRNL's analytical portfolio, as comparatively
few published analytical workows exist for W IDMS.

Collectively, this work establishes a complete analytical framework for isotope dilution mea-
surements at SRNL, encompassing spike acquisition and characterization, chemical purica-
tion, isotope ratio measurement, assay calibration, and statistically defensible data process-
ing. These capabilities will be applied to the characterization of environmental dust reference
materials produced as part of this project and provide a foundation for future NNSA analyti-
cal eorts requiring highly accurate, precise, and metrologically traceable assay concentration
measurements.

1 Introduction and Motivation
Isotope dilution mass spectrometry (IDMS) is a primary analytical method for the accurate
and precise determination of elemental concentration. This technique involves the mixing and
subsequent isotopic characterization of an isotopically enriched reference material (a `spike'
of known isotopic composition and elemental concentration) with a sample of known isotopic
composition but unknown elemental concentration to calculate the assay concentration in the
sample. This results in an assay concentration with relatively low uncertainties, often with
relative expanded uncertainties below 1%. Because IDMS relies on isotope ratios measured
within the same analytical cycle rather than absolute signal intensity, it is inherently less
sensitive to instrumental drift, variations in sample introduction eciency, plasma instability,
and detector gain. As a result, the accuracy of the measurement depends primarily on the
accuracy of the spike characterization and isotope ratio determination rather than absolute
instrument response in external calibration methods (i.e., intensity-based calibration on an
inductively coupled plasma - mass spectrometer [ICP-MS]), allowing higher accuracy and
metrological traceability.

IDMS has an advantage over other analytical methods in allowing chemical manipulation
to be performed after the spike is mixed with the sample. This permits reconstruction of
the original analyte concentration in the sample even if sample is lost during processing.
This scenario is particularly advantageous for environmental sample characterization, where
samples are composed of complex silicate- and oxide-rich matrices that potentially inter-
fere with accurate analyte measurement. For much of geochemical or national security -
related analytical workows, analytes of interest (e.g., U, Th, etc) are present in relatively
low abundance, generally consisting of parts per million (ppm) or sub-ppm concentrations.
The matrix within which these analytes are present is overwhelmingly dominated by other
analytes. This scenario has two main concerns: 1) a complex matrix can cause plasma sup-
pression or instabilities which decouple the plasma response during sample and reference
material measurements and thus does not allow for accurate calibration, and 2) analytes
react with plasma gases and matrix-derived species to form polyatomic ions which aect
the precision and accuracy of a measurement. The latter eect is particularly impactful to
higher mass analytes, which suer from low mass analyte complexation in the plasma. IDMS
mitigates these issues by allowing a spiked sample to be puried prior to analysis, eectively
stripping the sample of matrix elements.

The disadvantage of IDMS is it is a time intensive method with limited commercially available
isotopic spikes. While U, Pu, and Th have well-established IDMS methods and associated
reference materials for spiking, other elements of interest to NNSA eorts have received lim-
ited IDMS method development. This is a pertinent issue with reference material production,
where highly accurate and precise characterization of key elements is critical. A previous
report from this project (Bowden and Samperton, 2026) identied several elements typically
present in environmental media that posed the greatest interference eect on ICP-MS and
secondary ion mass spectrometry (SIMS) measurements. Pb, W, and Th were identied as
elements that cause signicant interferences on ICP-MS and SIMS actinide measurements
and thus require highly accurate and precise characterization in order to calibrate and guide

 1

analytical workows. Of these, only Th had commercially available reference materials that
can be used for IDMS characterization. W in particular is a challenging element to char-
acterize by ICP-MS without IDMS due to interferences from polyatomic ions that complex
into the W mass range. Developing IDMS capabilities for these elements mitigates many of
these issues.

This report details the development of IDMS methods for U, Th, Pb, and W at Savannah
River National Laboratory (SRNL). The developmental eort required for each element dif-
fered substantially: A U IDMS method has been extensively employed at SRNL in the past,
a suitable Th reference material for IDMS is commercially available (but not yet employed
at SRNL), and Pb and W have no commercially available IDMS reference materials. In
particular, W IDMS remains comparatively uncommon and lacks widely adopted analyti-
cal protocols. The production of these methods consisted of a general workow involving:
1) selection and preparation (i.e., digestion, dilution, etc) of isotopically enriched reference
materials, 2) the development of purication methods to isolate the element of interest from
complex environmental sample matrices, 3) the characterization of these reference materials
via mass spectrometry, and 4) the development of statistically robust and metrologically
traceable algorithms for processing both the mass spectrometry isotopic data and mathe-
matically determining spike composition and content. The methods developed as part of this
report are unique capabilities that will be used for reference material characterization both
for this project's capstone reference material synthesis task and for future NNSA project
scope.

2 Sample Purication
The low concentrations of W, Pb, Th, and U in most environmental samples, coupled with
their ubiquitous presence in laboratory environments, make these elements particularly sus-
ceptible to contamination during chemical processing. Because of this concern, separate
purication schemes were developed for each element, with the exception of Th and U which
are separated in tandem. The alternative approach is to sequentially purify one aliquot of the
sample, passing it through a series of columns with drying and reconstituting steps between
each. This method uses less sample and is thus favored in sample-limited analytical regimes,
but it necessitates that the same sample aliquot is passed through multiple purication and
drying steps, each time increasing the likelihood of contaminating the sample.

Separation was achieved by exploiting dierences in the stability of aqueous halide complexes
and their anity for extraction on anion-exchange resins. Changes in acid concentration and
ligand composition alter the speciation of each element, thereby changing its distribution
coecient between the resin and solution and permitting sequential elution (Kraus and
Nelson, 1958; Korkisch, 1989). For each column separation, a reference material solution
was synthesized from National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) - traceable
reference materials that approximated the composition of a typical soil (here using NIST
Standard Reference Material [SRM] Montana II as a benchmark). This reference material
was then passed through each column, with a separate aliquot taken for approximately every
milliliter (mL) of solution passed into the column. These solutions were then analyzed against

NIST - traceable reference materials on an Agilent 7900 Quadrupole-ICP-MS to determine
the elution prole of each column.

4 Implications
The reference materials produced and characterized in this project provide traceable tools
for the accurate and precise determination of U, Th, Pb, and W in complex environmental
matrices. Although IDMS has been established for several of these elements individually,
integrating these methods into a unied workow for characterization of complex environ-
mental reference materials provides a new analytical capability for SRNL. The W IDMS
method developed here is particularly unique, as published applications of W IDMS remain

or aected by signicant matrix and spectral interferences. Beyond the production of iso-
