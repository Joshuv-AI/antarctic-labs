Introduction

Breast cancer remains the most frequently diagnosed malignancy among women worldwide, accounting for approximately 30% of all female cancers and representing a leading cause of cancer-related mortality [1]. Early detection and accurate classification of breast cancer subtypes are critical for determining appropriate treatment strategies and improving patient survival rates [2]. Histopathological examination of breast tissue biopsies, visualized through hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stained microscopy images, serves as the gold standard for definitive breast cancer diagnosis [3]. Pathologists analyze these images to distinguish between normal tissue, benign lesions, carcinoma in situ, and invasive carcinoma—categories that fundamentally determine clinical management and prognosis [4]. However, manual histopathological analysis is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and subject to inter-observer variability, particularly when diagnosing morphologically ambiguous lesions that exhibit overlapping characteristics across diagnostic categories [5]. In the current treatment era, these morphological categories also provide the diagnostic foundation for biomarker testing, molecular risk assessment, and subtype-adapted systemic therapy selection.

The advent of deep learning has advanced medical image analysis, providing automated solutions for histopathological image classification. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have demonstrated capabilities in extracting hierarchical features from breast cancer histopathological images [6]. Numerous studies have employed CNN architectures including VGG [7], ResNet [8], DenseNet [9], and EfficientNet [10] for breast cancer classification tasks, achieving accuracies ranging from 85% to 99% on various datasets [11]. These approaches have explored diverse strategies including handcrafted feature extraction [12], transfer learning [13], data augmentation [14], ensemble methods [15], and attention mechanisms [16] to enhance classification performance. Despite these advances, conventional CNN architectures face fundamental limitations in capturing long-range dependencies within histopathological images, as their receptive fields are intrinsically constrained by fixed kernel sizes and stacked convolutional layers [17].

Recent transformer-based architectures, particularly Vision Transformers (ViT) [18], have emerged as alternatives by modeling global contextual relationships through self-attention mechanisms. Studies have successfully applied ViT and its variants including DeiT [19], Swin Transformer [20], DaViT [21], CAFormer [22], and hybrid approaches [2] to breast cancer histopathological image classification [23]. However, transformer architectures exhibit quadratic computational complexity with respect to sequence length, rendering them computationally prohibitive for high-resolution medical images [24]. This computational burden limits their practical deployment in clinical settings where computational resources may be constrained. Furthermore, pure transformer models often struggle to capture fine-grained local features essential for distinguishing subtle morphological variations that characterize different breast cancer subtypes [25].

State space models (SSMs) have recently emerged as a promising alternative that addresses the computational limitations of both CNNs and transformers while maintaining strong modeling capabilities. Unlike transformers that employ self-attention mechanisms with quadratic complexity, SSMs formulate sequence modeling as continuous-time dynamical systems that can be efficiently discretized and computed with linear complexity. The selective state space model, exemplified by the Mamba architecture, introduces input-dependent parameterization that enables adaptive filtering of information flow, allowing the model to selectively focus on relevant features while suppressing noise. This selective mechanism proves particularly advantageous for medical image analysis, where discriminative pathological features must be extracted from complex tissue backgrounds containing substantial irrelevant information. The linear computational complexity of SSMs is attractive for longer image-token sequences and future whole-slide extensions, potentially bridging the gap between the local feature extraction capabilities of CNNs and the global modeling strengths of transformers. However, pure SSM-based architectures may not fully capture the multi-scale spatial patterns inherent in histopathological images, where diagnostic features manifest across diverse scales ranging from subcellular structures to tissue-level architecture.

To address the aforementioned challenges, this investigation proposes MD-Mamba, a novel architecture that integrates state space models with multi-scale dilated convolutions and dual-path attention mechanisms for breast cancer histopathological image classification. The architecture first employs selective state space modeling (S6) to capture long-range tissue organization patterns with linear computational complexity, enabling efficient global context modeling while maintaining computational tractability for high-resolution medical images. Subsequently, a Multi-Dilated Convolutions Module (MDCM) is constructed with parallel convolutional branches operating at different receptive field scales (3×3 and 5×5 kernels with dilation rate 2), facilitating simultaneous extraction of fine-grained cellular features and broader tissue architectural patterns across multiple spatial scales. The extracted features are then refined through a dual-path attention mechanism that jointly models spatial and channel-wise dependencies, with spatial attention emphasizing diagnostically relevant tissue regions while channel attention recalibrates feature importance to suppress redundant information. The complete architecture is evaluated on the ICIAR 2018 BACH Challenge dataset [26], demonstrating that MD-Mamba achieves competitive performance compared to existing CNN and transformer-based methods while maintaining computational efficiency through linear-complexity sequence modeling.××

From this perspective, the main contributions of this study are as follows:1.A breast histopathology image-classification model is proposed that integrates selective state space modeling with multi-scale dilated convolutions, addressing the need to represent global tissue architecture and local morphology within one efficient framework.2.A Multi-Dilated Convolutions Module (MDCM) is designed to extract multi-scale local features through parallel dilated convolutional branches, enabling simultaneous capture of pathological patterns manifesting across diverse spatial scales from individual cell morphology to tissue architecture without increasing computational cost.3.A dual-path spatial-channel attention mechanism is developed to improve interpretability by localizing diagnostically relevant tissue regions and recalibrating feature importance, thereby supporting visual review of model decisions.4.Comprehensive experiments are conducted on the ICIAR 2018 BACH Challenge dataset to systematically evaluate the proposed approach. The ablation studies demonstrate that each architectural component contributes meaningfully to overall performance, while comparative analysis shows that MD-Mamba achieves competitive performance across multiple evaluation metrics compared to state-of-the-art CNN and transformer-based methods.

Literature context

CNN-based and transfer learning approaches

Deep convolutional neural networks have been extensively investigated for automated breast cancer histopathological image analysis, demonstrating improvements over traditional handcrafted feature extraction methods [27]. Preprocessing techniques including contrast enhancement and normalization have been shown to improve classification performance [28]. Early CNN-based approaches employed architectures including VGG [7], AlexNet [29], and ResNet [8] as feature extractors, achieving binary classification accuracies exceeding 90% for distinguishing benign and malignant tissues [30]. DenseNet-based models have demonstrated particular effectiveness, leveraging dense connections to alleviate vanishing gradient problems and enhance feature propagation [31,32] proposed DRDA-Net, incorporating dense residual connections with dual-shuffle attention mechanisms to achieve 98.1% accuracy on the BreakHis dataset at 400× magnification. EfficientNet [33] architectures have been successfully adapted for breast cancer classification [34], with [10] introducing Soft Attentive EfficientNet that achieved 98.05% accuracy through attention-guided feature refinement.

Multi-classification approaches that distinguish between specific breast cancer subtypes rather than binary benign/malignant categorization have gained prominence due to their clinical relevance for treatment planning [3,35] combined handcrafted features (Hu moments, Haralick textures, color histograms) with deep neural networks, achieving 97.87% accuracy for multi-class magnification-dependent classification [15] developed CA-BreastNet with coordinated attention mechanisms and specialized enhanced classifying strategy (SECS), reaching 99.75% for binary and 95.69% for eight-class classification. Advanced architectural innovations including capsule networks [17], bilateral multi-dimensional refined space feature attention networks [36], and attention-based mask reconstruction modules [16] have been proposed to enhance feature discrimination and address class imbalance challenges.

Transfer learning has emerged as a dominant paradigm for medical image classification, enabling effective model training despite limited annotated histopathological datasets [8]. Pre-trained models on ImageNet have been successfully fine-tuned for breast cancer classification, with studies demonstrating that transfer learning reduces training time while achieving competitive performance compared to training from scratch [37]. Self-supervised contrastive learning approaches have been explored to enhance transfer learning effectiveness with limited labeled data [6,38] proposed BCHI-CovNet, a lightweight framework employing multiscale depth-wise separable convolutions with second-order pooling, achieving 99.38% accuracy on BACH while reducing computational complexity. MobileNet [39] architectures have been adapted for resource-constrained deployment scenarios, demonstrating that efficient lightweight models can maintain high classification accuracy. Ensemble learning strategies combining multiple deep learning models have shown consistent performance improvements through complementary error reduction [2,23] developed MF-OMKT, employing online mutual knowledge transfer between heterogeneous networks to achieve accuracies of 99.27%-99.84% for binary classification. Knowledge distillation techniques have been explored to transfer knowledge from complex teacher models to efficient student models [40], enabling deployment of high-performance models with reduced computational requirements.

Vision transformers and hybrid architectures

Vision Transformers (ViT) [18] have recently been applied to breast cancer histopathological image classification, leveraging self-attention mechanisms to capture global contextual relationships [2] demonstrated that ViT achieved 99.89% accuracy for binary classification and 98.21% for eight-class classification, outperforming CNN-based approaches including VGG-19 and ConvMixer. Hierarchical vision transformers including Swin Transformer [20] and DeiT [19] have been investigated for their ability to process multi-scale features through shifted window attention and knowledge distillation respectively [41] proposed Deconv-Transformer (DecT), incorporating color deconvolution within transformer architecture to exploit H&E staining properties, achieving 93.02% average accuracy on BreakHis.

Hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures seek to combine the complementary strengths of convolutional feature extraction and transformer-based global modeling [42,43] employed collaborative transfer networks integrating DenseNet backbone with residual collaborative branches, achieving 98.29% accuracy on BreakHis with effective generalization to other breast cancer datasets [43] developed novel deep learning algorithms for molecular subtype classification in whole-slide histopathological images, demonstrating the potential of advanced architectures for comprehensive diagnostic tasks. Despite these successes, transformer-based models face challenges including computational requirements due to quadratic attention complexity and difficulty capturing fine-grained local features essential for subtle morphological discrimination [44].

Multi-scale feature extraction and attention mechanisms

Multi-scale feature extraction has proven critical for breast cancer histopathological image analysis, as diagnostic patterns manifest across diverse spatial scales from individual cell nuclei to tissue architecture [5]. Pyramid-based approaches, multi-branch architectures with varying kernel sizes [45], and patch-based methods [46] have been explored to capture hierarchical feature representations [11] investigated the impact of stain normalization on multi-level feature extraction (low, mid, and high-level features), demonstrating enhanced interpretability and reliability across feature hierarchies.

Attention mechanisms have been extensively integrated to enhance model focus on diagnostically relevant regions and feature channels. Channel attention [10], spatial attention, and hybrid spatial-channel attention [36] have shown effectiveness in suppressing background interference and emphasizing discriminative morphological patterns [16] proposed Attention-based Mask Reconstruction Module (AMRM) that performs mask reconstruction to align extracted features with pathologist expectations, improving classification accuracy across multiple architectures. Adversarial training and generative approaches [47] have been employed to augment limited training data and address class imbalance, further enhancing model robustness and generalization capability.

AI-assisted molecular biomarker stratification

Beyond conventional morphology-based classification, computational pathology may eventually connect H&E morphology with molecular and clinically actionable phenotypes. In breast cancer, estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), HER2/ERBB2, Ki-67, intrinsic molecular subtype, and multigene recurrence scores are established variables for therapeutic selection and prognostic assessment. The present study does not predict these molecular variables, but it provides a morphology-focused image-classification model that could be extended in future paired morpho-molecular datasets.

Accordingly, molecular-marker integration should be framed as a future research direction rather than an empirical conclusion of the present work. Extending MD-Mamba from four-class BACH image classification to multimodal morpho-molecular stratification would require paired H&E whole-slide images, immunohistochemistry, genomic or transcriptomic annotations, treatment information, and outcome data.

Materials and methods

Overall architecture

This design is motivated by the diagnostic structure of breast histopathology: normal lobular and ductal organization, benign proliferative architecture, in situ confinement, and invasive stromal infiltration are expressed at different spatial scales. The S6 branch supplies global organization cues, whereas MDCM supplies local-to-regional morphology, allowing the classifier to combine low-power architectural context with higher-power cellular patterns.

Operationally, each MD-Mamba block receives a flattened spatial token sequence derived from the two-dimensional feature map. The upper branch applies linear projection, depth-wise convolution, non-linear activation, and S6 modeling to capture long-range tissue organization. The lower branch applies the Multi-Dilated Convolutions Module followed by spatial attention to preserve local pathological patterns. The two branches are fused by element-wise multiplication, which acts as an adaptive gate between global context and local morphology before channel-wise recalibration.

The proposed MD-Mamba network adopts a hierarchical encoder architecture that progressively extracts multi-scale features from H&E-stained breast histopathology images while maintaining computational efficiency through state space modeling. The architecture contains four progressive stages with spatial downsampling factors of {4, 8, 16, 32} relative to the input resolution. Each stage stacks MD-Mamba blocks that combine a selective state space pathway for long-range tissue organization with a multi-dilated convolution pathway for local morphology. This design allows the model to move from fine cellular patterns in shallow layers to higher-level tissue architecture in deeper layers while retaining the linear-complexity advantage of state space modeling.{4,8,16,32}

The network begins with a patch embedding module that partitions each resized 224×224 image into non-overlapping patches and projects them into the initial feature space. Between consecutive stages, patch merging reduces spatial resolution by a factor of 2 while doubling the channel dimension, thereby expanding the effective receptive field and increasing semantic abstraction. After the fourth stage, global average pooling aggregates the final feature map into an image-level representation, and a linear classifier produces logits for the Normal, Benign, InSitu, and Invasive categories. MD-Mamba block architecture

Residual connections and linear projections preserve stable feature propagation across stacked blocks. In this formulation, the multiplicative fusion encourages agreement between global architectural evidence and local morphological evidence: regions that are locally suspicious but globally inconsistent, or globally salient but locally weak, are down-weighted relative to features supported by both pathways.

The block can be interpreted as a structured alternative to full self-attention. Instead of computing pairwise token interactions with quadratic cost, MD-Mamba routes global information through S6 and local morphology through dilated convolution. Spatial attention is applied within the local branch to select diagnostically informative regions, whereas channel attention is applied after fusion to recalibrate feature channels associated with nuclear morphology, glandular texture, stromal patterning, and invasive boundaries.

This architectural dichotomy addresses a fundamental limitation in existing deep learning approaches for histopathological analysis, where conventional CNNs excel at local pattern recognition but struggle with long-range dependencies, while pure attention mechanisms provide global modeling capability at quadratic computational cost.

Multi-dilated convolutions module

The lower processing pathway incorporates the Multi-Dilated Convolutions Module (MDCM) to extract multi-scale local features through parallel dilated convolutions operating at complementary receptive field scales. Conventional single-scale convolutions impose a fixed receptive field that may not align with the inherent multi-scale nature of pathological patterns, where diagnostic features manifest across multiple spatial scales from individual cell morphology to tissue architecture. Dilated convolutions address this limitation by introducing gaps (dilation rates) between kernel elements, exponentially expanding the receptive field without increasing computational cost or parameter count.

Dual-path attention mechanism

Following the parallel processing through state space modeling and multi-dilated convolutions, the MD-Mamba block implements a dual-path attention mechanism that jointly refines spatial relationships and channel interdependencies in the fused feature representation. This mechanism addresses a fundamental challenge in histopathological image analysis, where discriminative features must be identified simultaneously across spatial locations (e.g., locations of cellular nuclei clusters) and feature channels (e.g., channels encoding nuclear morphology versus cytoplasmic texture). Traditional attention mechanisms typically process these dimensions sequentially or independently, potentially missing synergistic interactions between spatial and channel-wise feature importance.

Training strategy and loss function

The MD-Mamba network was trained end-to-end with multi-class cross-entropy loss and optimized using AdamW with weight decay regularization. Because BACH is a small benchmark dataset, the training protocol emphasized reproducibility and overfitting control: all random seeds were fixed to 42, the training and validation split was stratified by class, augmentation was applied only to the training subset, and the best checkpoint was selected by validation accuracy with early stopping. Dataset and experimental design

Dataset

The ICIAR 2018 Grand Challenge on Breast Cancer Histology Images (BACH) dataset serves as the primary evaluation benchmark for the proposed MD-Mamba model. This dataset represents a carefully curated collection of breast cancer histopathological images established specifically for automated classification of microscopy images and whole-slide imaging. The dataset was developed through collaboration between multiple pathology centers, ensuring diverse representation of tissue preparation protocols, staining variations, and imaging acquisition conditions that reflect real-world clinical scenarios encountered in diagnostic pathology practice.

The BACH dataset comprises 400 high-resolution microscopy images (2048×1536 pixels) in TIFF format, uniformly distributed across four diagnostic categories that span the spectrum of breast tissue pathology. The classification schema encompasses Normal tissue representing healthy breast architecture with preserved lobular and ductal structures, Benign lesions including fibroadenomas and other non-malignant proliferative changes, Carcinoma in situ (InSitu) representing pre-invasive malignancies confined within basement membranes, and Invasive carcinoma (Invasive) demonstrating infiltrative growth patterns characteristic of malignant transformation. Each category contains exactly 100 images, providing balanced class representation that facilitates unbiased model training and evaluation. All images were digitized at 200x magnification from H&E stained tissue sections, ensuring consistent image quality and diagnostic information content.

As illustrated in Fig. 2, the dataset exhibits substantial morphological diversity within and across diagnostic categories, presenting characteristic challenges for automated classification systems. Normal tissue displays organized glandular architecture with minimal cellular atypia, while benign lesions demonstrate increased cellularity and altered architectural patterns without cytological features of malignancy. In situ carcinomas exhibit cellular proliferation confined within ductal or lobular boundaries, displaying nuclear pleomorphism and increased mitotic activity. Invasive carcinomas are characterized by infiltrative growth patterns, loss of architectural organization, and pronounced cellular atypia. The inter-class morphological variability, combined with intra-class heterogeneity arising from tissue processing variations and staining intensity differences, establishes BACH as a demanding benchmark that requires sophisticated feature extraction and robust classification strategies.Fig. 2Representative BACH histopathology images across Normal, Benign, InSitu, and Invasive categories, illustrating the morphological diversity and diagnostic boundaries evaluated in this study.Fig 2 dummy alt text

The dataset presents several clinically relevant challenges that mirror practical diagnostic scenarios. First, the subtle morphological distinctions between benign and in situ lesions require fine-grained feature discrimination, as both exhibit cellular proliferation with overlapping architectural patterns. Second, variations in tissue preparation, section thickness, and H&E staining intensity introduce appearance variability that classification systems must handle robustly. Third, the presence of tissue artifacts, folding, and edge effects in some images necessitates robust feature representations that capture diagnostic patterns while being invariant to technical variations. These challenges establish the BACH dataset as an appropriate benchmark for evaluating the proposed MD-Mamba architecture, particularly its ability to capture multi-scale pathological patterns through dilated convolutions and long-range tissue organization through state space modeling.

Experimental setup

The experimental framework was implemented in PyTorch 2.0.0 with CUDA 11.8 acceleration on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 GPU with 8 GB memory. The BACH dataset was partitioned into training and validation subsets using a stratified 80:20 split, yielding 320 training images and 80 validation images with balanced class representation. The same partition, preprocessing pipeline, augmentation policy, training schedule, and evaluation protocol were used for MD-Mamba and all baseline models. A fixed random seed (42) was applied to dataset partitioning, weight initialization, and stochastic operations to improve reproducibility.

Training incorporated comprehensive data augmentation techniques to enhance model generalization and mitigate overfitting, which is particularly critical given the limited dataset size of 400 images. The augmentation pipeline included geometric transformations (random rotation up to 20 degrees, horizontal and vertical flipping with probability 0.5, random scaling between 0.9 and 1.1), photometric adjustments (brightness, contrast, and saturation variation within ±15%), and elastic deformations with small displacement fields to model natural tissue deformation. All images were resized to 224×224 pixels to accommodate the input requirements of the MD-Mamba architecture while maintaining computational efficiency. Pixel values were normalized to zero mean and unit variance.

1×10−31×10−5The training strategy employed AdamW with an initial learning rate of 1×10^-3 and weight decay coefficient of 0.05. The learning rate followed cosine annealing decay to 1×10^-5 over 100 epochs, preceded by a 5-epoch warmup phase. Batch size was 32. Model checkpoints were saved by validation accuracy, and early stopping was triggered if no validation-accuracy improvement was observed for 15 consecutive epochs. Because the same validation set was used for checkpoint selection and final reporting, the results should be interpreted as benchmark validation rather than independent test performance.

Comparison methods

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of MD-Mamba, eight state-of-the-art deep learning architectures were selected as baseline methods, spanning efficient CNNs, pure transformer models, and hybrid architectures. MobileNet and EfficientNet represent lightweight CNN architectures optimized for computational efficiency. Vision Transformer (ViT) and Swin Transformer exemplify pure attention-based models that capture global dependencies through self-attention mechanisms. DeiT introduces knowledge distillation strategies for data-efficient transformer training. CAFormer and Convformer integrate convolutional operations with transformer architectures to combine local feature extraction and global modeling. DaViT implements dual attention mechanisms for enhanced feature representation. All baseline models were implemented using identical training configurations, data augmentation strategies, and evaluation protocols to ensure fair comparison.

Implementation details

The MD-Mamba architecture was implemented in PyTorch 2.0.0 with CUDA 11.8 for GPU acceleration. The state space modeling components were implemented using efficient parallel scan algorithms to achieve linear time complexity. For the Multi-Dilated Convolutions Module (MDCM), PyTorch’s native dilated convolution operations were employed with carefully tuned dilation rates to balance receptive field coverage and parameter efficiency. The dual-path attention mechanisms were implemented using standard PyTorch operations including global average pooling, multi-layer perceptrons with ReLU activation, and element-wise multiplication for feature recalibration.

All baseline models were implemented using their official implementations or widely-adopted PyTorch implementations. For fair comparison, input resolution was standardized to 224×224 pixels across all models, and identical preprocessing pipelines were applied. Data loading employed PyTorch’s DataLoader for efficient multi-threaded data preprocessing.

Model checkpointing was performed at the end of each epoch, saving model weights, optimizer states, and training metrics. The best model was selected based on validation accuracy, with early stopping triggered if no improvement was observed for 15 consecutive epochs. All experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 GPU with 8 GB memory. All random seeds (Python, NumPy, PyTorch) were fixed to 42 to ensure reproducibility across experiments.

Finally, adding channel attention yields the full MD-Mamba architecture with 0.9625 accuracy and F1-score of 0.9617, demonstrating that channel-wise recalibration provides complementary benefits by emphasizing feature channels encoding clinically relevant morphological characteristics.

The component removal analysis (Fig. 6b) provides complementary insights into relative module importance. Removing MDCM causes the largest performance drop (0.0337), confirming that multi-scale local feature extraction is critical. Spatial attention removal causes a 0.0162 decrease, while channel attention removal reduces accuracy by 0.0087, suggesting that spatial feature refinement contributes more to overall performance than channel recalibration. Notably, removing both attention mechanisms simultaneously results in 0.9413 accuracy with combined effect (0.0212) less than the sum of individual removals, indicating some functional overlap that provides redundancy in feature refinement.

Discussion, translational relevance, and limitations

This study should be interpreted primarily as a benchmark evaluation of a state-space-based architecture for breast histopathology image classification. The main empirical finding is that combining S6-based long-range modeling, multi-dilated local feature extraction, and spatial-channel attention improved performance on the BACH four-class validation split compared with the evaluated CNN, transformer, and hybrid baselines.

The translational relevance of MD-Mamba is therefore indirect at this stage. The model operates on morphology labels rather than molecular markers, treatment response, survival, or prospectively collected clinical endpoints. Recent computational pathology studies have explored H&E-based prediction of receptor status, recurrence risk, and molecular subtype from whole-slide images [[48], [49], [50]], but the present BACH benchmark does not include the paired molecular or outcome annotations required for such analyses. Its attention maps and feature embeddings may provide a useful starting point for morphology-aware model inspection, but they should not be presented as established image-derived biomarkers. Any biomarker-oriented interpretation would require paired molecular, immune, stromal, treatment, and outcome annotations.

Several limitations are important. The current validation used a small public benchmark with a stratified 80:20 split, yielding only 80 validation images. The same validation set was used for checkpoint selection and final reporting, so the reported performance may be optimistic relative to an independent test set. The study did not include patient-independent external validation, whole-slide inference, multi-scanner or multi-institutional testing, confidence intervals, calibration analysis, or repeated random-split experiments.

Future work should evaluate a locked MD-Mamba model on external whole-slide cohorts with patient-level separation, scanner and staining heterogeneity, predefined endpoints, uncertainty reporting, and pathologist-adjudicated reference standards. Only after such validation should the model be assessed as a decision-support component for triage, region prioritization, or integration with ER, PR, HER2, Ki-67, genomic signatures, and clinical outcome data.

Conclusions

This investigation presents MD-Mamba, a multi-scale dilated state-space architecture for breast histopathology image classification. The model integrates S6-based long-range tissue modeling, multi-dilated convolutions for local morphology, and dual-path spatial-channel attention. On the ICIAR 2018 BACH benchmark split, MD-Mamba achieved 0.9625 accuracy, 0.9654 macro-precision, 0.9625 macro-recall, 0.9617 macro-F1, 0.9956 AUC, and 0.9500 Cohen's kappa under the shared evaluation protocol.

The ablation and sensitivity analyses support the contribution of the proposed components within this benchmark setting: MDCM provided the largest component-level gain, spatial attention improved region-level feature selection, and channel attention provided additional recalibration. These results suggest that combining global state-space modeling with local multi-scale morphology extraction is a promising direction for computational pathology image classification.

Future research should prioritize independent, patient-level external validation; whole-slide inference; confidence intervals and calibration; multi-seed or cross-validation experiments; and paired molecular or outcome data when biomarker-oriented claims are intended. Until such evidence is available, MD-Mamba should be presented as a benchmark-level classification model rather than a clinically validated decision-support or biomarker system.
