Surfaces with parallel mean curvature in warped product spaces
Fernando Manfio, Verônica Reis, Feliciano Vitório

Abstract
In this work, we obtain a geometric description of surfaces M 2 M^{2} of arbitrary codimension in the warped product ℝ × ρ ℚ ϵ n \mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon} , with parallel mean curvature vector field in the normal connection, extending a result by Alencar-do Carmo-Tribuzy [ 2 ] .



Key words:

Parallel mean curvature, warped product, reduction of codimension.

1
Introduction

A classical result concerning surfaces
M^{2}
in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, due to Hopf
[
10
]
, states that any surface homeomorphic to a sphere with constant mean curvature is, in fact, isometric to a round sphere. This theorem was later extended by Chern
[
6
]
to other space forms and, more recently, to surfaces in
3
-dimensional homogeneous manifolds with a
4
-dimensional isometry group by Abresch and Rosenberg
[
1
]
.

A natural class of ambient spaces for which Hopf-type theorems hold is that of product spaces
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
denotes either the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
or the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, according as
\epsilon=1
or
\epsilon=-1
, respectively. A pioneering work in this direction was conducted by Alencar-do Carmo-Tribuzy
[
2
]
, who studied surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
. They proved that either
H
is an umbilic direction or the immersion admits a reduction of codimension to three.

The study of surfaces and, more generally, submanifolds with parallel mean curvature vector field has long been a subject of interest in differential geometry. These submanifolds generalize both minimal and constant mean curvature surfaces by requiring the mean curvature vector
H
to be parallel with respect to the normal connection. As demonstrated by Chen
[
5
]
, Yau
[
14
]
, and Aliás-Dajczer
[
3
]
, for example, this condition imposes significant rigidity and geometric constraints on the immersion.

In this work, we investigate surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field in higher-dimensional warped product spaces
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
. Following the approach in
[
2
]
, our main result does not require global assumptions on the surface
M^{2}
. We show that either
H
is an umbilical direction or the immersion admits a reduction of codimension. Specifically, we establish the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\colon M^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
,
n\geq 5
, be a surface with nonzero parallel mean curvature vector field. Then, one of the following possibilities holds:

(i)

f
is a minimal surface of a umbilical hypersurface of a slice
\{t\}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
.

(ii)

f
is a surface with constant mean curvature in a three-dimensional umbilical or totally geodesic submanifold of a slice
\{t\}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
.

(iii)

f(M^{2})
lies in a totally geodesic submanifold
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{m}_{\epsilon}
,
m\leq 4
, of
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
.

A key step in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is a codimension reduction result for isometric immersions into
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
(cf. Section
3
), which generalizes the results of
[
12
]
for the product space
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
denote either the sphere or the hyperbolic space of dimension
n
, according to whether
\epsilon=1
or
\epsilon=-1
, respectively.
We also denote by
\mathbb{E}^{n+2}
either the Euclidean space or the Lorentzian space of dimension
n+2
, according as
\epsilon=1
or
\epsilon=-1
, respectively. If
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+2})
are the standard coordinates on
\mathbb{E}^{n+2}
with respect to which the flat metric is written as

ds^{2}=\epsilon dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+\ldots+dx_{n+2}^{2},

we will regard
\mathbb{E}^{n+1}
as

\mathbb{E}^{n+1}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+2})\in\mathbb{E}^{n+2}:x_{n+2}=0\},

and the quadric
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
regarded as

\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{E}^{n+1}:\epsilon x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\ldots+x_{n+1}^{2}=\epsilon\},

with
x_{1}>0
if
\epsilon=-1
.

Given an open interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
and a smooth positive function
\rho\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
, let us consider the warped product
I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
endowed with the warped product metric

\langle,\rangle=\pi_{1}^{\ast}dt^{2}+(\rho\circ\pi_{1})^{2}\pi_{2}^{\ast}\langle,\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}},

where
\pi_{1}\colon I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\to I
and
\pi_{2}\colon I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
denote the canonical projections. We will consider the warped product manifold
I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
as a rotational hypersurface of
\mathbb{E}^{n+2}
parametrized by

i(t,x)=(\rho(t)x,h(t)),

(2.1)

where
h\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function satisfying
\rho^{\prime}(t)^{2}+\epsilon h^{\prime}(t)^{2}=1
and
h^{\prime}(t)>0
, for all
t\in I
and
x\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
. Note that the warped product
I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
admits a distinguished unit vector field

\partial_{t}=(\rho^{\prime}(t)x,h^{\prime}(t)),

which is tangent to the first factor
I
, i.e.,
\partial_{t}
is a horizontal vector field along
I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
. If
\overline{\nabla}
denotes the Levi-Civita connection on
I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
, one has

\overline{\nabla}_{Z}\partial_{t}=\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\left(Z-\langle Z,\partial_{t}\rangle\partial_{t}\right),

(2.2)

for every
Z\in T(I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon})
.

Given an isometric immersion
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
, a tangent vector field
T
on
M^{m}
and a normal vector field
\eta
along
f
are defined by

\partial_{t}=f_{\ast}T+\eta.

(2.3)

Using (
2.2
), we obtain by differentiating (
2.3
) that

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}T-A^{f}_{\eta}X=\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\left(X-\langle X,T\rangle T\right)

(2.4)

and

\displaystyle\alpha_{f}(X,T)+\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\eta=-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\langle X,T\rangle\eta,

(2.5)

for all
X\in TM
, where
\alpha_{f}
and
\nabla^{\perp}
denote the second fundamental form and the normal connection of
f
, respectively. Here
A^{f}_{\eta}
stands for the shape operator of
f
in the
direction
\eta
, given by

\langle A^{f}_{\eta}X,Y\rangle=\langle\alpha_{f}(X,Y,\eta\rangle,

for all
X,Y\in TM
.

The Gauss, Codazzi, and Ricci equations for
f
are, respectively

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} R(X,Y)Z\ =\ &\lambda(t)(X\wedge Y)Z+A^{f}_{\alpha_{f}(Y,Z)}X-A^{f}_{\alpha_{f}(X,Z)}Y\\
&+\mu(t)\big(\langle X,T\rangle Y\wedge T-\langle Y,T\rangle X\wedge T\big)Z,\end{aligned}

(2.6)

\displaystyle(\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\alpha_{f})(Y,Z)-(\nabla^{\perp}_{Y}\alpha_{f})(X,Z)=\mu(t)\langle(Y\wedge X)Z,T\rangle\eta

(2.7)

and

\displaystyle R^{\perp}(X,Y)\xi=\alpha_{f}(X,A^{f}_{\xi}Y)-\alpha_{f}(A^{f}_{\xi}X,Y),

(2.8)

where

\lambda(t)=\frac{\epsilon-\rho^{\prime}(t)^{2}}{\rho(t)^{2}}\quad\text{e}\quad\mu(t)=\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)\rho(t)-\rho^{\prime}(t)^{2}+\epsilon}{\rho(t)^{2}}.

(2.9)

Equation (
2.7
) can also be written as

(\nabla_{X}A^{f})(Y,\xi)-(\nabla_{Y}A^{f})(X,\xi)=\mu(t)\langle\eta,\xi\rangle(X\wedge Y)T,

(2.10)

where

(X\wedge Y)T=\langle Y,T\rangle X-\langle X,T\rangle Y.

Although this will not be used in this work, it is worth mentioning that equations (
2.4
)–(
2.8
) completely determine an isometric immersion
f\colon M^{m}\to I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
up to isometries of
I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
(see
[
8
, Theorem 5.1]
).

Remark 2.1
.

Recall that in a warped product
I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
, with
n\geq 2
, the sectional curvature along a plane tangent to
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
is given by
(\epsilon-\rho^{\prime}(t)^{2})/\rho(t)^{2}
. Meanwhile, the sectional curvature along a plane spanned by unit vector
\partial_{t}
and
X
tangent to
I
and
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
, respectively, is
-\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)/\rho(t)
. Therefore,
I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
has constant sectional curvature equal to
c
if and only if

\rho^{\prime}(t)^{2}+c\rho(t)^{2}=\epsilon.

Note that this implies
-\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)/\rho(t)=c
, or equivalently,

\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)\rho(t)-\rho^{\prime}(t)^{2}+\epsilon=0

(see
[
11
, Proposition 4.6]
for further details). Since we assume that our ambient space
I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
does not have constant sectional curvature, we may suppose that the function
\mu(t)
, defined in (
2.9
), is nowhere vanishing.

We will finish this section by relating the second fundamental forms and normal connections of
f
and
\tilde{f}=i\circ f
, where
i
is the inclusion given in (
2.1
). The hypersurface
i\colon I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\to\mathbb{E}^{n+2}
admits a unit normal vector field given by

\hat{N}_{t}=(h^{\prime}(t)x,-\epsilon\rho^{\prime}(t)).

Thus,

\widetilde{\nabla}_{Z}\hat{N}_{t}=\frac{h^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}Z-\left(\frac{h^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}+\epsilon\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)}{h^{\prime}(t)}\right)\langle Z,\partial_{t}\rangle\partial_{t},

for every
Z\in T(I\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon})
, where
\widetilde{\nabla}
is the derivative in
\mathbb{E}^{n+2}
. Hence, the shape operator of
i
in the direction of
\hat{N}_{t}
is given by

A^{i}_{\hat{N}_{t}}Z=-\frac{h^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}Z+\left(\frac{h^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}+\epsilon\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)}{h^{\prime}(t)}\right)\langle Z,\partial_{t}\rangle\partial_{t}.

(2.11)

The normal spaces of
f
and
\widetilde{f}
are related by

TM^{\perp}_{\tilde{f}}=i_{\ast}TM^{\perp}_{f}\oplus span\{N_{t}\},

where
N_{t}
denotes the restriction of
\hat{N}_{t}
along
f
. Given a normal vector field
\xi\in TM^{\perp}_{f}
, we obtain from (
2.11
) that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \widetilde{\nabla}_{X}i_{\ast}\xi&=i_{\ast}\overline{\nabla}_{X}\xi+\alpha_{i}(f_{\ast}X,\xi)\\
&=-\tilde{f}_{\ast}A^{f}_{\xi}X+i_{\ast}\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\xi+\alpha_{i}(f_{\ast}X,\xi)\end{aligned}

(2.12)

Therefore, from (
2.12
) and using Weingarten formula, one has

\displaystyle A^{\tilde{f}}_{i_{\ast}\xi}=A^{f}_{\xi}

(2.13)

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}^{\perp}_{X}i_{\ast}\xi=i_{\ast}\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\xi+\epsilon\left(\frac{h^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}+\epsilon\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)}{h^{\prime}(t)}\right)\langle X,T\rangle\langle\eta,\xi\rangle N_{t},

(2.14)

for every
\xi\in TM^{\perp}_{f}
, where
\widetilde{\nabla}^{\perp}
is the normal connection of
\tilde{f}
.

3
Reduction of codimension

In order to study surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field in the warped product
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
, we need the following result on reduction of codimension of isometric immersions into
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
. We say that an isometric immersion
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
reduces codimension to
l
if
f(M^{m})
is contained in a totally geodesic submanifold
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{m+l-1}_{\epsilon}
of
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
.

We denote by
N_{1}(x)
the
first normal space
of
f
at
x
, i.e., the subspace of
T_{x}M^{\perp}
spanned by its second fundamental form. A condition, due to Erbacher
[
9
]
, for a submanifold of space forms to reduce codimension is that its first normal spaces form a parallel subbundle of the normal bundle. The following result, proved initially for submanifolds in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{c}\times\mathbb{R}
in
[
12
]
, is a version of the aforementioned result of Erbacher for submanifolds in warped products.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
be an isometric immersion and assume that
L:=N_{1}+\text{span}\{\eta\}
is a subbundle of
TM^{\perp}_{f}
of rank
l<n+1-m
and that
\nabla^{\perp}N_{1}\subset L
. Then f reduces codimension to
l
.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.5
) that
\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\eta\in L
, for every
X\in TM
. Since we have
\nabla^{\perp}N_{1}\subset L
by assumption, it follows that
L
is a parallel subbundle of
TM^{\perp}_{f}
.
Let
L^{\perp}
denote the orthogonal complement of
L
in
TM^{\perp}_{f}
. Given
\xi\in L^{\perp}=N_{1}^{\perp}\cap\{\eta\}^{\perp}
, from (
2.14
) and the fact that
L
is a parallel subbundle of
TM^{\perp}_{f}
, we obtain

\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}^{\perp}i_{\ast}\xi=i_{\ast}\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\xi\in i_{\ast}L^{\perp},

where
\widetilde{\nabla}
denotes the derivative of
\mathbb{E}^{n+2}
. This shows that
i_{\ast}L^{\perp}
is a parallel subbundle of
TM^{\perp}_{\tilde{f}}
. On the other hand, it follows from (
2.13
) that

i_{\ast}L^{\perp}\subset\widetilde{N}_{1}^{\perp},

where
\widetilde{N}_{1}(x)
is the first normal space of
\tilde{f}
at
x\in M^{m}
. We obtain from the Weingarten formula for
\tilde{f}
that

\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}i_{\ast}\xi=-\tilde{f}_{\ast}A_{i_{\ast}\xi}^{\tilde{f}}X+\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}^{\perp}i_{\ast}\xi=\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}^{\perp}i_{\ast}\xi\in i_{\ast}L^{\perp},

This implies that
i_{\ast}L^{\perp}
is a constant subspace of
\mathbb{E}^{n+2}
, which is orthogonal to
\partial_{t}
.
Denote by
K
the orthogonal complement of
i_{\ast}L^{\perp}
in
\mathbb{E}^{n+2}
. For any fixed point
x_{0}\in M^{m}
, we have

\tilde{f}(M)\subset\tilde{f}(x_{0})+K.

Since
K
contains
\partial_{t}
and
N_{t}(x_{0})
, it also contains the position vector
\tilde{f}(x_{0})
. Thus, we have
\tilde{f}(x_{0})+K=K
. We conclude that

\tilde{f}(M)\subset(\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon})\cap K=\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{m+l-1}_{\epsilon},

and this completes the proof.
∎

A necessary and sufficient condition for parallelism of the first normal bundle of a submanifold of a space form in terms of its normal curvature tensor
R^{\perp}
and mean curvature vector field
H
was obtained by Dajczer
[
7
]
, whose proof can be adapted to yield the following result for submanifolds of
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
. The corresponding result for submanifolds in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
was obtained in
[
12
, Theorem 1.7]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
be an isometric immersion and assume that
L:=N_{1}+span\{\eta\}
is a subbundle of
TM^{\perp}_{f}
of rank
l<n+1-m
. Then
\nabla^{\perp}N_{1}\subset L
if and only if the following two conditions hold:

(i)

\nabla^{\perp}R^{\perp}|_{L^{\perp}}=0
,

(ii)

\nabla^{\perp}H\in L
.

Proof.

Assuming that
\nabla^{\perp}N_{1}\subset L
, condition
(ii)
is trivially satisfied, since
H\in N_{1}
. To prove
(i)
, first notice that for
\xi\in N_{1}^{\perp}
, the Ricci equation gives

R^{\perp}(X,Y)\xi=\alpha_{f}(X,A^{f}_{\xi}Y)-\alpha_{f}(A^{f}_{\xi}X,Y)=0.

Given
\xi\in L^{\perp}
, we have that
\xi\in N_{1}^{\perp}
. Moreover, by our assumption, one has
\nabla_{Z}^{\perp}\xi\in N_{1}^{\perp}
, for all
Z\in TM
. Thus,

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Z}R^{\perp})(X,Y,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{Z}R^{\perp}(X,Y)\xi-R^{\perp}(\nabla_{Z}X,Y)\xi-R^{\perp}(X,\nabla_{Z}Y)\xi

\displaystyle-R^{\perp}(X,Y)\nabla_{Z}^{\perp}\xi\ =\ 0,

for all
X,Y\in TM
. Conversely, let
\xi\in L^{\perp}
. Since
R^{\perp}(X,Y)\xi=0
for all
X,Y\in TM
, we obtain from
(i)
that

R^{\perp}(X,Y)\nabla_{Z}^{\perp}\xi=0,

for every
X,Y,Z\in TM
. Using the Ricci equation again, we obtain that

\left[A_{\nabla_{Z}^{\perp}\xi},A_{\nabla_{W}^{\perp}\xi}\right]=0,

for every
Z,W\in TM
. Hence, at any point
x\in M
, there exists an orthonormal basis
\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\}
of
T_{x}M
that diagonalizes simultaneously all shape operators
A_{\nabla_{Z}^{\perp}\xi}
, with
Z\in TM
. We claim that

\left\langle\nabla_{Z_{k}}^{\perp}\xi,\alpha_{f}(Z_{i},Z_{j}\right\rangle=0,

for all
1\leq i,j,k\leq n
, which implies that
\nabla_{Z}^{\perp}\xi\in N_{1}^{\perp}
, for all
Z\in TM
. From the choice of the basis
\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\}
, we have

\left\langle\alpha(Z_{i},Z_{j}),\nabla_{Z_{k}}^{\perp}\xi\right\rangle=\left\langle A_{\nabla_{Z_{k}}^{\perp}\xi}Z_{i},Z_{j}\right\rangle=\lambda_{ki}\delta_{ij},

where
\lambda_{k_{i}}
is the eigenvalue of
A_{\nabla_{Z_{k}}^{\perp}\xi}
corresponding to
Z_{i}
. It follows from the Codazzi equation (
2.10
) and the fact that
\xi\in L^{\perp}\subset\{\eta\}^{\perp}
that

A^{f}_{\nabla_{Z_{i}}^{\perp}\xi}Z_{k}=A^{f}_{\nabla_{Z_{k}}^{\perp}\xi}Z_{i}.

This implies that the eigenvalue
\lambda_{ki}
of
A^{f}_{\nabla_{Z_{k}}^{\perp}\xi}
corresponding to
Z_{i}
vanishes unless
k=i
. Therefore,

\left\langle\alpha_{f}(Z_{i},Z_{i}),\nabla_{Z_{k}}^{\perp}\xi\right\rangle=\left\langle A^{f}_{\nabla_{Z_{k}}^{\perp}\xi}Z_{i},Z_{i}\right\rangle=\left\langle A^{f}_{\nabla_{Z_{i}}^{\perp}\xi}Z_{k},Z_{i}\right\rangle=0,\ \textrm{ if }i\neq k.

Finally, the assumption
\nabla^{\perp}H\in L
and
\xi\in L^{\perp}
imply that
\langle\nabla^{\perp}_{Z_{i}}H,\xi\rangle=0
. Therefore,

\left\langle\alpha_{f}(Z_{i},Z_{i}),\nabla_{Z_{i}}^{\perp}\xi,\right\rangle=n\left\langle H,\nabla_{Z_{i}}^{\perp}\xi\right\rangle=-n\langle\nabla^{\perp}_{Z_{i}}H,\xi\rangle=0,

and this concludes the proof.
∎

4
Proof of main result

The proof of Theorem
1.1
makes use of the codimension reduction theorem, seen in Section 1.3, and also of an important fact regarding analytical functions, as discussed below.

Remark 4.1
.

Following the approach for submanifolds in space forms, we say that a surface
M^{2}
immersed in a warped product
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
is real analytic if, when viewed as a graph in local coordinates, its height function is real analytic. Equivalently, if the functions of two real variables that define locally the immersion are real analytic functions. Analyticity is related to elliptic PDE’s. More precisely, the geometric assumption of the isometric immersion
f\colon M^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
has parallel mean curvature vector field
H
, combined with the Gauss, Codazzi, and Ricci equations, forms a closed system of differential equations involving the components of the immersion. Since the surface
M^{2}
and the ambient
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
are analytic manifolds, the the system of PDE’s derived from the fundamental equations of submanifold theory is an analytic system (cf.
[
13
]
; see also
[
4
]
). Solutions to analytic nonlinear elliptic systems of PDE’s are real analytic, and this regularity property follows from the principle of analytic continuation.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since the mean curvature vector
H
is parallel and nonzero, the function

\mu:=\|H\|^{2}

in
M^{2}
is a non-zero constant. Let us first suppose that
A_{H}=\mu I
everywhere on
M^{2}
. We claim that the vector field
T
vanishes identically. Assuming otherwise, there exists an open subset
U
, where
T\neq 0
. Choose a unit vector field
X
on
U
orthogonal to
T
. Then

\langle\alpha_{f}(X,T),H\rangle=\langle A_{H}X,T\rangle=\mu\langle X,T\rangle=0.

(4.1)

By the Codazzi equation (
2.7
) we have

\left\langle(\nabla^{\perp}_{T}\alpha)(X,X)-(\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\alpha)(T,X),H\right\rangle=-\epsilon\mu(t)\|T\|^{2}\langle\eta,H\rangle.

(4.2)

It follows from (
4.1
) and the fact that
\mu
is constant on
M^{2}
that the left-hand-side of (
4.2
) is zero. Thus, the function
\langle\eta,H\rangle
vanishes on
U
, and hence

0=T\langle\eta,H\rangle=\langle\nabla_{T}^{\perp}\eta,H\rangle=\left\langle-\alpha(T,T)-\rho^{\prime}/\rho\langle T,T\rangle\eta,H\right\rangle=-\mu||T||^{2}.

Since
\mu
is a non-zero constant, it follows that
T
vanishes on
U
. This is a contradiction and proves the claim. Therefore, if
A_{H}=\mu I
everywhere on
M^{2}
, then
f(M^{2})
is contained in a slice
\{t_{0}\}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
of
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
and either possibilities
(i)
or
(ii)
holds by
[
14
, Theorem 4]
. Assume now that
A_{H}\neq\mu I
on an open subset
V
of
M^{2}
. Since
H
is parallel in the normal connection of
f
, it follows from the Ricci equation that
[A_{H},A_{\zeta}]=0
, for any point
x\in M^{2}
and every normal vector
\zeta\in T_{x}M^{\perp}_{f}
. Then the fact that
A_{H}
has distinct eigenvalues on
V
implies that the eigenvectors of
A_{H}
are also eigenvectors of
A_{\zeta}
for any
\zeta\in T_{x}M_{f}^{\perp}
, with
x\in V
. Hence all shape operators are simultaneously diagonalizable at any
x\in V
, which implies that
f
has flat normal bundle on
V
by the Ricci equation (
2.8
). In particular, the first normal spaces
N_{1}
of
f
have dimension at most two at any point
x\in V
. Let
W\subset V
be an open subset, where
L=N_{1}+span\{\eta\}
has constant dimension
l\leq 3
. It follows from Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
that
f(W)
lies in a totally geodesic submanifold
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{2+l-1}_{\epsilon}
of
\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
. By analyticity of
f
(see Remark
4.1
), we conclude that
f(M^{2})\subset\mathbb{R}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}^{l+1}_{\epsilon}
.
∎
