Cyclic Sieving Phenomenon for Independent sets of graphs
Jacob A White

Abstract
In this paper, we present examples of the cyclic sieving phenomenon coming from studying independent sets in graphs of a fixed size k k . Given a graph G G , and a cyclic group C C acting on the graph, then C C also acts on the collection of k k -independent sets of G. We exhibit cyclic sieving phenomena for a cyclic group acting on the collection of k k -independent sets of powers of cycle graphs. As a corollary, we also find a closed formula for the number of independent sets of size k k in the power of a cycle graph, and in the power of a path. We also show how the graph construction of whiskering can be used to obtain new cyclic sieving phenomena from old phenomena. We also discuss recursive techniques to exhibit cyclic sieving phenomena for the independent sets of gear graphs, helm graphs, and book graphs.

Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{C}_{n}
be the cyclic group of order
n.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{C}_{n}
acts on a set
X
. For
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{C}_{n}
, we let

Given a set

and a polynomial

, we say that
(X,\mathfrak{C}_{n},f(q))
exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon (abbreviated CSP) if
\mathfrak{C}_{n}
acts on
X

\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{C}_{n}
of order
d
, and any primitive
d
th root of unity

|X^{\mathfrak{g}}|=f(\omega)
. The cyclic sieving phenomenon was first introduced in
[
1
]
, and many examples of CSPs appear in the literature. An excellent survey is given in
[
2
]
.

In this paper we study many natural examples of cyclic sieving coming from graph theory. Throughout this paper, we only focus on simple graphs, with no loops or multiple edges. We let
V(G)
denote the vertex set of a graph
G
.

Given a graph
G
, an automorphism
\mathfrak{g}:V(G)\to V(G)
is a bijection which preserves the adjacency relation of
G
. We say that a group
\mathfrak{G}
acts on

\mathfrak{G}
on

that preserves the adjacency relation. We let

denote the collection of independent sets of

. Given any automorphism
\mathfrak{g}
, and any

is also an element of
\mathcal{I}_{k}(G).
Thus if
\mathfrak{G}
is a group that acts on

, then there is an induced action of
\mathfrak{G}
on

In particular, if a cyclic group
\mathfrak{C}
acts on a graph

\mathcal{I}_{k}(G).
We are interested in finding polynomials

such that

exhibits the CSP. We present several results in this regard.

Our next theorem, which is proven in Section

for which a cyclic group

acts freely. Recall that
\mathfrak{C}
acts freely on a set

if, for every

if and only if

if the

, and this action preserves the adjacency relation of the graph.

Theorem 3
.

Let
k\leq n
. Let
G
be a graph, and let
\mathfrak{C}
be a cyclic group that acts freely on

. Suppose that, for each
j\leq k
, there is a polynomial

such that

Then

acts on

, and

Our final result gives a recursive method to finding CSPs for independent sets of graphs.
Recall that, given a graph
G
, and
x\in G
, we have
|\mathcal{I}_{k}(G)|=|\mathcal{I}_{k}(G\setminus x)|+|\mathcal{I}_{k-1}(G\setminus N[x])|,
where

is the closed neighborhood of
x
. We provide a cyclic-sieving generalization of this fact.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be a cyclic group acting on a graph
G
, such that there is a fixed point
x
. Then
\mathfrak{C}
fixes
N(x)
, and thus
\mathfrak{C}
acts on both
G\setminus x
and
G\setminus N[x]
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be a cyclic group acting on a graph
G
such that
x
is a fixed point of the action. Fix a positive integer
k
. Suppose that we have polynomials
a(q)
and
b(q)
such that
(\mathcal{I}_{k}(G\setminus x),\mathfrak{C},a(q))
and
(\mathcal{I}_{k-1}(G\setminus N[x]),\mathfrak{C},b(q))
both exhibit the CSP. Then
(\mathcal{I}_{k}(G),\mathfrak{C},a+b)
also exhibits the CSP.

We give a proof of the Lemma in Section
4
, where we also use this lemma, and the preceding theorems, to find CSPs related to gear graphs, helm graphs, and book graphs.

2
Cyclic Sieving for Powers of Cycle graphs

Our first step in proving Theorem
1
is to express
i_{k,r,n}(q)
as a generating function. To this end,
we introduce a generating function
i_{k}(G,q)
for graphs with vertex set
[0,n-1].

Given a subset
A\subseteq[0,n-1]
, and a positive integer
r
, we let

\ssum_{r}(A)=-(r+1)\binom{k}{2}+\sum_{a\in A}a.

For example, with
n=12,k=4
, and
r=3
, we have
\ssum_{3}(\{0,5,9,11\})=-4(6)+0+5+9+11=1.

Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
[0,n-1]
. We let
\mathcal{I}_{k}(G)
denote the collection of independent sets of size
k
of
G.
For any
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{k}(G)
,
we define

i(\mathcal{J},q)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{J}}q^{\ssum_{r}(A)}.

We let
i_{k}(G,q)=i(\mathcal{I}_{k}(G),q).
This generating function is a Laurent polynomial. However, for the graphs
G
we focus on,
\ssum_{r}(A)\geq 0
for all
A\in\mathcal{I}_{k}(G)
, and thus
i_{k}(G,q)
is a polynomial.

Recall that the
r
th power of the path graph,
P_{n}^{r}
, is the graph on vertex set
[0,n-1]
, with edges of the form
i\sim i+j
for
1\leq j\leq r
and
i\in[0,n-1].

We see that
\mathcal{I}_{2}(P_{5}^{2})=\{\{0,3\},\{0,4\},\{1,4\}\}
and
i_{2}(P^{2}_{5},q)=1+q+q^{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}3\\
2\end{array}\right].
We have found closed formulas for
i_{k}(P_{n}^{r},q)
and
i_{k}(C_{n}^{r},q).

Proposition 5
.

Let
r\leq n
be positive integers, and let
k
be a positive integer such that
k\leq\frac{n}{r+1}.

Then the following identities hold:

1.

i_{k}(P^{r}_{n},q)=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-rk+r\\
k\end{array}\right]
,

2.

i_{k}(C_{n}^{r},q)=\frac{[rk]}{[k]}i_{k-1}(P_{n-1-2r}^{r},q)+q^{rk}i_{k}(P^{r}_{n-r},q)

3.

i_{k}(C_{n}^{r},q)=\frac{[n]}{[n-rk]}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-rk\\
k\end{array}\right].

Proof.

We prove the first result by induction on
r
.
When
r=0
, then

i_{k}(P_{n}^{0},q)=\sum_{A\subset[0,n]:|A|=k}q^{\ssum_{0}(A)}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
k\end{array}\right].

For
r>1
, let
A\in\mathcal{I}_{k}(P^{r}_{n}).
Write
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}
where
0\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}\leq n-1.

Define
A^{\prime}=\{a_{i}-i+1:i\in[k]\}
. We observe that
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{k}(P_{n-k+1}^{r-1})
, and moreover
\ssum_{r}(A)=\ssum_{r-1}(A^{\prime}).
Hence

i_{k}(P_{n}^{r},q)=i_{k}(P_{n-k+1}^{r-1},q)=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}(n-k+1)-(r-1)k+(r-1)\\
k\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-rk+r\\
k\end{array}\right]

where the middle equality follows from induction.

Now we prove our second identity.
Let
r<n
be positive integers, and let
k
be a positive integer such that
k(r+1)\leq n
.
For a given
j\in[0,r-1]
, define

\mathcal{A}_{j}=\{A\in\mathcal{I}_{k}(C_{n}^{r}):j\in A\}

Define
\varphi_{j}:\mathcal{A}_{j}\to\mathcal{I}_{k-1}(P_{n-2r-1}^{r})
by
\varphi_{j}(A)=\{a-j-r-1:a\in A\setminus\{j\}\}
. Then
\varphi_{j}
is a bijection, and we see that

\ssum_{r}(A)=j+(j+r+1)(k-1)-(r+1)(k-1)+\ssum_{r}(\varphi_{j}(A))=jk+\ssum_{r}(\varphi_{j}(A)).

Thus

i(\mathcal{A}_{j},q)=q^{jk}i_{k-1}(P_{n-2r-1}^{r},q).

We let
\mathcal{B}=\{A\in\mathcal{I}_{k}(C_{n}^{r}):A\cap[0,r-1]=\emptyset\}
. We define
\theta:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{I}_{k}(P_{n-r}^{r})
by

\theta(B)=\{b-r:b\in B\}
. Then
\theta
is a bijection, and for
B\in\mathcal{B}
we have
\ssum_{r}(B)=rk+\ssum_{r}(\theta(B))
.
Thus,

i(\mathcal{B},q)=q^{rk}i_{k}(P_{n-r}^{r},q).

Then we see that

\displaystyle i_{k}(C_{n}^{r},q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}i(\mathcal{A}_{j},q)+i(\mathcal{B},q)=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}q^{jk}i_{k-1}(P_{n-2r-1}^{r},q)+q^{rk}i_{k}(P_{n-r}^{r},q)

\displaystyle=i_{k-1}(P_{n-2r-1}^{r},q)\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}q^{jk}+q^{rk}i_{k}(P_{n-r}^{r},q)=\frac{[rk]}{[k]}i_{k-1}(P_{n-2r-1}^{r},q)+q^{rk}i_{k}(P_{n-r}^{r},q).

The third identity follows from the first two, via

\displaystyle i_{k}(C_{n}^{r},q)

\displaystyle=\frac{[rk]}{[k]}i_{k-1}(P_{n-2r-1}^{r},q)+q^{rk}i_{k}(P_{n-r}^{r},q)

\displaystyle=\frac{[rk]}{[k]}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-2r-1-r(k-1)+r\\
k-1\end{array}\right]+q^{rk}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-r-rk+r\\
k\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{[rk]}{[n-rk]}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-rk\\
k\end{array}\right]+q^{rk}\frac{[n-rk]}{[n-rk]}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-rk\\
k\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{[rk]+q^{rk}[n-rk]}{[n-rk]}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-rk\\
k\end{array}\right]=\frac{[n]}{[n-rk]}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-rk\\
k\end{array}\right].

∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
d\mid n
, and let
\omega
be a primitive

th root of unity. Let

and

be positive integers with

Then

\lim_{q\to\omega}\frac{[a]}{[b]}=\begin{cases}\frac{a/d}{b/d}&d\mid a\\

Let

and suppose that

Then

\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
k\end{array}\right]_{q=\omega}=\binom{n/d}{k/d}

Given a graph
G
, let
\mathfrak{G}
be a group that acts freely on
G
. Let
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}
be an automorphism of order

. We define

as the graph whose vertex sets are the cycles of

, and where two cycles

and

of

are adjacent if and only if there exists

.

Similarly, given

, with

, we let

be the set of cycles

. The map

is a bijection between

Thus we obtain the following lemma:

Lemma 7
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a group that acts freely on a graph

Let

\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{G}
have order

. Let

Then

|\mathcal{I}_{k}(G)^{\mathfrak{g}}|=\begin{cases}|\mathcal{I}_{k/d}(G/\mathfrak{g})|&d\mid k\\

We state one more Lemma:

Lemma 8
.

Let
n
be a positive integer, and let
r>1.

Let
\mathfrak{C}_{n}
be a cyclic group of order
n
acting on
C_{n}^{r}
via rotation.
Let
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{C}_{n}
have order
d
. Then
C_{n}^{r}/\mathfrak{g}\simeq C_{n/d}^{r}.

The isomorphism
\iota:V(C_{n}^{r}/\mathfrak{g})\to V(C_{n/d}^{r})
is given by sending a cycle
C
of
\mathfrak{g}
to
\min C
, its minimum element.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
have order
d
, and let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be cycles of
\mathfrak{g}
that are adjacent in
C^{r}_{n}/\mathfrak{g}.
Define
q
such that
n=qd
. Let
i\in C,j\in C^{\prime}
such that
i<q
and
i
is adjacent to
j
.

Suppose that
i<j<i+r
. If
j<q
, then we have
i
is adjacent to
j
in
C_{q}^{r}
, and these are the minimum elements of the corresponding cycles. If
j>q
, then
i+r-q>j-q
, and
i
is adjacent to
j-q
in
C_{q}^{r},
and both are the minimum elements of the corresponding cycles.

Finally, suppose that
j+r-n>i
. We see then that

. Let

Then

so

is adjacent to

, and both

preserves edge adjacency.

Theorem 9
.

Let
n
be a positive integer, and let
k
and
r
be nonnegative integers such that
(k+1)r\leq n.
Let
\mathfrak{C}_{n}
be the cyclic group of order
n
, which acts on the set
[0,n-1]
by cyclic rotation. Then
\mathfrak{C}
acts on
\mathcal{I}_{k}(C^{r}_{n})
, and

(\mathcal{I}_{k}(C^{r}_{n}),\mathfrak{C}_{n},i_{k}(C^{r}_{n},q))
exhibits the CSP.
In particular, for
d\mid n
,
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{C}
of order

, and

i_{k}(C^{r}_{n},\omega)=|\mathcal{I}_{k}(C_{n}^{r})^{\mathfrak{g}}|=\begin{cases}\frac{n/d}{n/d-rk/d}\binom{n/d-rk/d}{k/d}&d\mid k\\

The theorem is trivially true when

, so throughout we assume

Let

, and let

be a primitive

th root of unity.
We write

\frac{[n]}{[n-k]}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-rk\\

(1)

where we are writing the factors in the numerator from smallest to largest.
We let

for unique integers

and

with

.

Suppose that

. Then the denominator on the right-hand side of Equation (
1
) has

where

, while the numerator has at least

. Thus

\lim_{q\to\omega}\frac{[n]}{[n-rk]}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-rk\\

Suppose that

. Then the numerator and denominator on the right-hand side of Equation (

) have exactly

, which cancel. If we apply Lemma

), we see that, for any

, we have

\lim_{q\to\omega}\frac{[n-rk-k+j]}{[j]}=\begin{cases}\frac{n/d-rk/d-k/d+j/d}{j/d}&d\mid j\\

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to\omega}\frac{[n]}{[k]}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{[n-rk-k+j]}{[j]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n/d}{k/d}\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{n/d-rk/d-k/d+j}{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n/d}{k/d}\binom{n/d-rk/d-1}{k/d-1}.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an element of order

. We know

acts freely on

, and thus so does

. if

\mathcal{I}_{k}(C_{n}^{r})^{\mathfrak{g}}=\emptyset

. Using Lemma

i_{k/d}(C_{n/d}^{r},1)
, we conclude that

\mathcal{I}_{k}(C_{n}^{r})^{\mathfrak{g}}=\begin{cases}\frac{n/d}{n/d-rk/d}\binom{n/d-rk/d}{k/d}&d\mid k\\
0&d\nmid k\end{cases}

The result follows from applying our formula from Proposition
3
, part 3 to evaluate
i_{k/d}(C_{n/d}^{r},1).

∎

3
Whiskering

Fix a positive integer
k
.
Suppose that a cyclic group
\mathfrak{C}
acts on
V(G)
freely, and suppose that for all
j\leq k
there are polynomials
i_{j}(G,q)
such that
(\mathcal{I}_{j}(G),\mathfrak{C},i_{j}(G,q))
exhibits the CSP.
We know that
\mathfrak{C}
acts on
V(w(G)).
Recall that whiskering
G
involves creating an isomorphic copy of
V(G)
, denoted
V^{\prime}
, and adding edges of the form
v\sim v^{\prime}
for
v\in V(G)
. Figure
2
depicts whiskerings of several graphs. Given
j\leq k
, we let
\mathcal{I}_{j,k-j}(w(G))=\{A\in\mathcal{I}_{k}(w(G):|A\cap V|=j\}
. We see that
\mathfrak{C}
acts on
\mathcal{I}_{j,k-j}(w(G)).

To prove Theorem
3
, it suffices to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 10
.

Fix positive integers
j
and
k
with
j\leq k.

Suppose that a cyclic group
\mathfrak{C}
acts on
V(G)
freely, and suppose that there is a polynomial
i_{j}(G,q)
such that
(\mathcal{I}_{j}(G),\mathfrak{C},i_{j}(G,q))
exhibits the CSP.
Then

\left(\mathcal{I}_{j,k-j}(w(G)),\mathfrak{C},i_{j}(G,q)\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-j\\
k-j\end{array}\right]\right)
exhibits the CSP.

Figure 2:
The whiskerings of
C_{6}^{0}
,
C_{6}^{1}
, and
C_{6}^{2}
.

Proof.

Assume
\mathcal{C}
acts freely on
G
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{C}
be an element of order
d
.
Let
\omega
be a primitive
d
th root of unity. Suppose that
d\nmid j.
Let
A\in\mathcal{I}_{j,k-j}(w(G)).
Then
\mathfrak{g}^{d}(A\cap V)\neq A\cap V
, so
\mathcal{I}_{j,k-j}(w(G))^{\mathfrak{g}}=\emptyset.
Similarly,
i_{j}(G,\omega)=0.

Suppose that
d\mid j
, but
d\nmid k-j.
For
A\in\mathcal{I}_{j,k-j}(w(G)),
we have
\mathfrak{g}^{d}(A\cap V^{\prime})\neq A\cap V^{\prime}
, and thus
\mathcal{I}_{j,k-j}(w(G))^{\mathfrak{g}}=\emptyset.
Moreover,
d\mid n-j,
so
\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-j\\
k-j\end{array}\right]_{q=\omega}=0.

Suppose that
d|j.
Then
i_{j}(G,\omega)=|\mathcal{I}_{j}(G)^{\mathfrak{g}}|
. Let
A\in\mathcal{I}_{j}(G)^{\mathfrak{g}}.
Then
\langle\mathfrak{g}\rangle
acts freely on
V^{\prime}\setminus A^{\prime}.
Moreover, there is a
\langle\mathfrak{g}\rangle
-equivariant bijection
\sigma_{A}:\{B\in\mathcal{I}_{j,k-j}(G):B\cap V=A\}\to\{B\subseteq V^{\prime}\setminus A^{\prime}:|B|=k-j\}
given by
\sigma_{A}(S)=S\cap V^{\prime}.
This second set is isomorphic to
\mathcal{I}_{k-j}(C_{n-j}^{0})
, and hence has size
\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-j\\
k-j\end{array}\right]_{q=\omega}
. Since this latter expression does not depend on
A
, we see that
|\mathcal{I}_{k,j-k}(G)^{\mathfrak{g}}|=i_{j}(G,\omega)\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-j\\
k-j\end{array}\right]_{q=\omega}.

Therefore
\left(\mathcal{I}_{j,k-j}(w(G)),\mathcal{C},i_{j}(G,q)\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n-j\\
k-j\end{array}\right]\right)
exhibits the CSP.
∎

5
Observations and Open Questions

The automorphism group of
C_{n}^{r}
is the dihedral group, and thus there are other ways in which
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
can act freely on
C_{n}^{r},
via reflections. In general, for those actions,
(\mathcal{I}_{k}(C_{n}^{r},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},i_{k,r,n}(q))
does not exhibit the CSP.

Finally, we end with a list of questions:

1.

Are there other commonly studied families of graph
G
where we can find polynomials
i_{k}(q)
so that
(G,\mathcal{C},i_{k}(q))
exhibits the CSP? Examples of such graphs include the prism graphs, for instance.

2.

Are there other graph constructions where we can extend instances of CSP from a smaller graph to a larger graph? For example, if
\mathfrak{G}
acts on a graph
G
, then it also acts on the bipartite double cover of
G
, and on the line graph of
G.

3.

Are there other types of graph structures, like dominating sets, for which we can find instances of the CSP?

4.

The powers of cycles
C_{n}^{r}
are examples of circulant graphs, which are graphs where the automorphism group contains a cyclic subgroup
\mathfrak{C}
which acts transitively on
V(G)
. Given a circulant graph
G
, is there an interesting statistic
s_{G,k}
on
\mathcal{I}_{k}(G)
such that

\left(\mathcal{I}_{k}(G),\mathfrak{C},\sum\limits_{A\in\mathcal{I}_{k}(G)}q^{s_{G,k}(A)}\right)

exhibits the CSP?
