A quasi-star is born: formation and evolution of accreting quasi-stars as a metallicity-independent pathway to Little Red Dots
J. Roman-Garza ,, D. Schaerer, C. Charbonnel, T. Fragos, E. Cenci, R. Marques-Chaves, P. A. Oesch, M. Xiao

Abstract
To investigate the rest-frame optical emission of ”Little Red Dots”, we model the formation of and evolution of quasi-stars, i.e. stellar envelopes supported by the accretion luminosity onto a central black hole, originating from rapidly accreting proto-stars reaching the supermassive star regime ( > 10 4 >10^{4} M ⊙ {\rm M}_{\odot} ) and undergoing general relativistic instability. We compute stellar evolution models with net mass gain rates = 0.01 =0.01 , 0.1, and 1 M ⊙ {\rm M}_{\odot} /yr and metallicities Z = 0 Z=0 –0.01. For the mass gain rates ≥ 0.1 \geq 0.1 M ⊙ {\rm M}_{\odot} /yr, stars remain nearly fully convective with T eff ∼ 4000 T_{\mathrm{eff}}\sim 4000 –9000 K. The general relativistic instability leading to central BH formation occurs at M ⋆ ∼ 3.5 × 10 4 M_{\star}\sim 3.5\times 10^{4} M ⊙ {\rm M}_{\odot} ( 6.6 × 10 4 6.6\times 10^{4} M ⊙ {\rm M}_{\odot} ) for M ˙ acc = 0.1 \dot{M}_{\rm acc}=0.1 M ⊙ {\rm M}_{\odot} /yr (1 M ⊙ {\rm M}_{\odot} /yr), at luminosities L ∼ 10 9 L\sim 10^{9} L ⊙ . The lifetime of quasi-stars is estimated to be 10 7 10^{7} – 10 8 10^{8} yr, ∼ \sim 100–1000 times longer than their progenitors. In an environment allowing for rapid accretion the formation, evolution, and properties of quasi-stars are found be essentially independent of metallicity. Comparing the luminosities of our models with those of Little Red Dots at z < 4.5 z<4.5 ( L bol ∼ 10 9.5 L_{\mathrm{bol}}\sim 10^{9.5} – 10 11.5 10^{11.5} L ⊙ ) yields quasi-star masses 10 4.5 10^{4.5} – 10 6.5 10^{6.5} M ⊙ {\rm M}_{\odot} . The observed minimum luminosity of ∼ 10 9.5 \sim 10^{9.5} L ⊙ implies accretion rates ≳ 0.1 \gtrsim 0.1 M ⊙ {\rm M}_{\odot} /yr for Little Red Dots progenitors. Our models offer a metallicity-independent framework supporting quasi-stars as the source of Little Red Dot optical emission, and provide insights into their lifetimes, composition, and progenitor environment.

Our model ”unifies” SMS and QS scenarios for metal-free systems proposed earlier, generalizes them over a broad metallicity range, and proposes a consistent scenario for their formation and evolution, which enforces the possibility that LRDs host QSs.

Overview of the evolution from proto-star to quasi-star

{\rm M}_{\odot}

The nucleosynthesis of rapidly accreting SMS and QS is also worth briefly discussing. As these objects burn H through the CNO cycle until the GRI is reached and remain essentially fully convective throughout, they can synthesize a maximum mass of Nitrogen

M_{N}=\overline{X_{N}}M_{\rm GRI}\leq X^{\rm ini}_{\rm CNO}M_{\rm GRI}\approx(1-1.5)\times 10^{-2}\,10^{[{\rm O/H}]^{\rm ini}}M_{\rm GRI}

The surface abundances of these objects are therefore predicted to be variable with time but mostly determined by the accreting matter, and CNO equilibrium values (high N/O and N/C) can only be reached during a short amount of time or if the objects stop accretion close to or before GRI.

4
Conclusions

As a pathway to understand the physical origin of the rest-optical emission from LRDs, we have calculated stellar evolution models of rapidly accreting stars. We start from low mass-proto-stars that experience a net mass gain and follow their evolution until they become SMS and reach the GRI, which leads to the formation of so-called QS
(
4
;
3
, where a stellar envelope is supported by a central BH, see)
. We considered maximum mass gain rates of 0.001, 0.1 and 1
{\rm M}_{\odot}
/yr, and metallicities from
Z=0
to 0.01. We show that our scenario works independently of metallicity.

We are aware that the employed mass gain rates are in agreement with the mass-inflow rates onto simulated haloes from the cosmological simulations. For example,
7
reports inflow rates range from
\sim 10^{-3}
to
\gtrsim 1

{\rm M}_{\odot}
/yr at
z\simeq 4
, which increase with higher redshifts. The mass growth threshold can also be reached in dense and compact forming massive star clusters with stellar surface densities similar to those estimated for LRDs (typically
>10^{5}

{\rm M}_{\odot}
pc
{}^{-2})
, where both gas-accretion and collisions can concur to the formation of SMS, with no limitation in terms of metallicity, contrary to the DCBH scenarios
(
10
;
21
;
1
;
37
, and references therein)
.

Accreting models with
\dot{M}\geq 0.01

{\rm M}_{\odot}
/yr remain almost fully convective across the entire evolution, with effective temperatures from 4000 to 9000 K
(
15
;
13
;
29
, consistent with)
. Stars accreting at 0.1, 1
{\rm M}_{\odot}
/yr and
Z=10^{-4}
reach the GRI when they have masses of
3.5\times 10^{4}

{\rm M}_{\odot}
and
6.6\times 10^{4}

{\rm M}_{\odot}
respectively. Their luminosity at that instant is
\simeq 10^{9}
L
⊙
.

Significant amounts of material processed by the CNO cycle (N, in particular) can build up during the phase preceding the GRI. However, the continuing mass growth through the QS phase, which is needed to explain the observed luminosity range of LRDs, implies a strong dilution, and thus variable and not very strong chemical enrichment in QS.

The QS lifetime upper limit is estimated on the order of
10^{7}-10^{8}
yr, consistent with the values the numerical models by
40
. This is approximately 2-3 orders of magnitude longer than the lifetime of their stellar progenitors, depending as well on the progenitor’s accretion history.
Therefore, it is much more likely to observe QSs than their progenitors.

Our accreting QS models are in agreement with the effective temperatures and range of bolometric luminosities of observed LRDs, showing
L\sim 10^{9.5-11.5}
L
⊙

(
8
)
. This suggests that LRDs may be dominated by QSs with total masses between
\sim 10^{4.5-6.5}

{\rm M}_{\odot}
.
The mass of their central BH depends on the evolutionary stage of the QS and its accretion history, and the QS mass serves only as its upper limit. Assuming that the QS mass is of the same order as the BH mass
(
11
, as in)
may overestimate the value of the latter.

From our models, the minimum bolometric luminosity of
\simeq 10^{9}
L
⊙
of LRDs implies that the QS progenitors should have a minimum accretion rate of
\simeq 0.1

{\rm M}_{\odot}
/yr. If such minimum observed luminosity was higher it would imply that the minimum accretion rate of the QS progenitors is
>0.1

{\rm M}_{\odot}
/yr and vice versa. The accreting QS scenario does not place a limit on its maximum luminosity, but such quantity provides context on the maximum amount of mass that these objects can reach while comparing them with LRD observations.

Appendix A
Physical assumptions

To account for general relativistic effects, the gravitational constant is modified according to the first order correction given by the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation, which is derived for the Schwarzschild metric. The correction is computed as
(
15
, e.g. see)
:

G_{\rm TOV}=G\left(1+\frac{P}{\rho c^{2}}+\frac{4\pi Pr^{3}}{m_{r}c^{2}}\right)\left(1-\frac{2Gm_{r}}{rc^{2}}\right)^{-1}.

(1)

The evolution of the stars stops when the general-relativistic instability (GRI) criterion by
26
is reached:

\Gamma_{\rm 1}(m_{r})<\frac{4}{3}+4.498\frac{G_{\rm TOV}m_{r}}{rc^{2}}f_{\rm GR}.

(2)

\Gamma_{\rm 1}(m_{r})
corresponds to the value of the adiabatic index at the mass coordinate
m_{r}
, which corresponds to the radial coordinate
r
. The factor
f_{\rm GR}
is introduced and chosen as 1.5 to stop the evolution of the stellar models before they reach the GRI and become numerically unstable. The models stop when they have
\gtrsim 70\%
of the stellar mass needed to reach the GRI given
f_{\rm GR}=1
.

When the GRI is encountered (i.e., Eq.
2
is satisfied) the stellar evolution run is halted. The core of the corresponding supermassive star is expected to collapse into a BH
(
22
;
42
;
4
)
. It then enters a new evolutionary phase as a QS
(
3
)
, where the system is stabilized against gravity primarily by the radiation produced from accretion onto the central BH. Stars that encounter the GRI after the main sequence may experience the general relativistic instability supernova (GRISN) where no black BH is expected to be formed
(
26
;
13
, see)
. For this work we do not explore the cases where the GRISN is triggered.

The evolution of the QS model is computed with the
MESA-QUEST
module
(
39
, see)
. We investigate two limiting cases, one where gas accretion stops as soon as the GRI is encountered, and one where accretion onto the QS continues at the same rate as previously. In both cases, the central boundary condition of the model is set to an initial BH mass of 10
{\rm M}_{\odot}
. The central BH grows in mass through the accretion of the stellar envelope following the convection-limited Bondi accretion rate
(
2
)
, with both convective and radiative efficiencies both set to
0.1
. The BH accretion luminosity is set in terms of the radiative efficiency and accretion rate as
\simeq 0.11\ \dot{M}_{\rm acc}\ c^{2}

(
39
, see)
.

Appendix B
Lifetime of quasi-stars

The aim of this section is provide an analytical prediction for the lifetime of QSs depending on the accretion history during this phase. We define that the QS progenitors have a constant mass gain rate
\dot{M}
, therefore their mass across time is modeled as

M(t)=\dot{M}\,t,\quad{\rm if}\ t<t_{\rm GRI}.

(3)

Where
t_{\rm GRI}
is the instant the GRI is encountered, its exact value depends on the accretion rate, e.g., for an accretion rate of
0.1

{\rm M}_{\odot}
/yr it can be estimated as
t_{\rm GRI}\simeq 10^{4.5}
{\rm M}_{\odot}
/\dot{M}

(
15
, see)
.

After
t_{\rm GRI}
the central BH is formed with an initial mass of
M_{\rm BH,i}
. The BH accretion,
\dot{M}_{\rm BH}
, rate can also be written as a function of the QS mass, following of
40
:

\dot{M}_{\rm BH}=\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}\,\frac{4\pi}{\kappa c}\,\alpha\,G_{\rm TOV}\,M\ ,

(4)

where
\epsilon=0.1
is the radiative efficiency,
\kappa
is the opacity of the material surrounding the BH and set at the inner boundary of the model,
M
is the total mass of the QS. The free parameter
\alpha
is used to account for possible decreases or enhancements in
\dot{M}_{\rm BH}
due to angular momentum transport or magnetic fields, respectively.

For the QS we consider two cases: either the QS continues to accrete at the same rate as its progenitor, or it stops accreting completely. We define the end of the QS phase as the instant the central BH has accreted the remaining stellar envelope, i.e.
M_{\rm BH}=M
.

The mass of the BH grows following Eq.
4
,

\dot{M}_{\rm BH}=\cases{}{2}{\tau^{\prime}}\ M(t_{\rm GRI})&\text{if}\ \dot{M}=0,\\
\frac{2}{\tau^{\prime}}\,\dot{M}\,t\text{if}\ \dot{M}>0.{}\lx@close@alignment

(5)

Where
\tau^{\prime}=\left(\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}\,\frac{2\pi}{\kappa c}\,\alpha\,G\right)^{-1}
, this quantity has units of time.

After integrating over time from
t_{\rm GRI}
until the end of the QS phase, i.e.
t_{\rm end}
, we find that the BH mass is

M_{\rm BH}=\cases{M}_{\rm BH,i}+\frac{2}{\tau^{\prime}}\ M(t_{\rm GRI})\ (t_{\rm end}-t_{\rm GRI})&\text{if}\ \dot{M}=0,\\
M_{\rm BH,i}+\frac{1}{\tau^{\prime}}\,\dot{M}\,(t_{\rm end}-t_{\rm GRI})^{2}\text{if}\ \dot{M}>0.{}\lx@close@alignment

(6)

At
t_{\rm end}
we have
M_{\rm BH}/M=1
. In the case where
\dot{M}=0
we obtain the following asymptotic behavior for
t_{\rm end}
:

t_{\rm end,no\ acc}\sim\cases{\tau^{\prime}}/{2}&\text{if}\ t_{\rm GRI}\ll\tau^{\prime},\\
{3\tau^{\prime}}/{2}\text{if}\ t_{\rm GRI}\rightarrow\tau^{\prime},\\
t_{\rm GRI}\text{if}\ \tau^{\prime}\ll t_{\rm GRI}.{}\lx@close@alignment

(7)

For the case where the QS keeps accreting, its lifetime is determined as:

t_{\rm end,acc}\sim\cases{\tau^{\prime}}&\text{if}\ t_{\rm GRI}\ll\tau^{\prime},\\
\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\ {\tau^{\prime}}\text{if}\ t_{\rm GRI}\rightarrow\tau^{\prime},\\
t_{\rm GRI}+\sqrt{t_{\rm GRI}\ \tau^{\prime}}\text{if}\ \tau^{\prime}\ll t_{\rm GRI}.{}\lx@close@alignment

(8)

In all cases
t_{\rm end,acc}>t_{\rm end,no\ acc}
, and

\frac{t_{\rm end,acc}}{t_{\rm end,no\ acc}}\sim\cases{2}&\text{if}\ t_{\rm GRI}\ll\tau^{\prime},\\
1\text{if}\ \tau^{\prime}\ll t_{\rm GRI}.{}\lx@close@alignment

(9)

As the lifetime of QSs depends mainly on
\tau^{\prime}
, we estimate its value as it depends only on
\epsilon
,
\alpha
and
\kappa
. If
\epsilon=0.1
,
\alpha=1
and
\kappa=0.38
cm
2
/g (this value is extracted from our simulations and is mostly constant during the QS phase), one obtains
\tau^{\prime}\simeq 10^{8}
yr. This value is consistent with the estimates from our simulations. Also, in Appendix
D
one can notice that the estimated lifetime of the accreting QSs is approximately twice as the non-accreting ones, consistent with Eq.
9
.

Lastly, we compare the the lifetime of the QS progenitor to the duration of the QS phase as

\frac{t_{\rm QS\ prog.}}{t_{\rm QS}}=\frac{t_{\rm GRI}}{t_{\rm end}-t_{\rm GRI}}\ .

(10)

For an accretion rate of
0.1

{\rm M}_{\odot}
/yr it corresponds to
t_{\rm GRI}\sim 10^{5}
yr. As
t_{\rm end}\sim 10^{8}
yr, then we estimate, for this particular case, that the QS phase lasts one thousand times more than the life of its progenitor.
If the accretion rate increases, then
t_{\rm GRI}
decreases and vice versa
(
15
, see)
, while
t_{\rm end}
remains mostly constant.

It is important to note that the estimate for
t_{\rm end}
should be considered an upper value for the QS lifetime. Compared with our simulations, the value of
\ddot{M}_{\rm BH}
is higher than the one obtained from Eq.
4
, which being more consistent with
\alpha>1
.
