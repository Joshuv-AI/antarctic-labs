The exceptional locus of a motivic local system
Luca Terenzi

Abstract
. Given a Nori motivic local system over a smooth, connected complex algebraic variety, we define its exceptional locus as a way to measure the variation in the motivic complexity of its stalks. The definition is given explicitly in terms of motivic Galois groups and Artin motives. Our main result is a motivic analogue of the Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan Theorem, asserting that the exceptional locus is a countable union of closed algebraic subvarieties. Moreover, we show that the maximal such subvarieties are defined over any algebraically closed subfield over which the ambient variety and the motivic local system admit models, and that they are stable under Galois conjugation when these models descend to a further subfield. This strengthens and extends previous results by André in the pure case. We obtain a similar geometric description for the splitting locus of the motivic weight filtration. In the case of 1 1 -motivic local systems, the above properties pass to the underlying variations of mixed Hodge structure via André’s fullness theorem.

Introduction

Motivation and goal of the paper

Let
X
be a smooth, connected complex algebraic variety, and let
V
be an admissible, graded-polarizable variation of mixed Hodge structure over
X
.
One can think of
V
as a local system of mixed Hodge structures, and it is natural to ask how the complexity of the stalks
V_{x}
varies with respect to
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
.
This is measured by the (tensorial) Hodge locus of
V
, defined as the set of points
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
such that the mixed Hodge structure
V_{x}
admits more Hodge tensors than the very general stalk.
A fundamental result describes such a locus as a countable union of strict closed algebraic subvarieties of
X
:
in the case of pure variations, this was proved in Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan’s celebrated paper
[
CDK95
]
;
it was later extended to mixed variations by Brosnan–Pearlstein–Schnell in
[
BPS10
]
, where a similar geometric description was provided for the locus where the weight filtration splits.

The result is particularly interesting when the variation
V
is of geometric origin – that is, when it arises from the relative cohomology of
X
-schemes.
In this case, it can be promoted to a Nori motivic local system, the stalks of which are Nori motives rather than Hodge structures.
The main goal of the present paper is to define a natural motivic version of the (tensorial) Hodge locus, for which we establish the analogue of the Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan Theorem;
similarly for the splitting locus of the motivic weight filtration.
We are also able to describe the fields of definition of these loci and to prove their stability under Galois conjugation;
these arithmetic aspects are only partially understood in the Hodge-theoretic setting.
In the case of pure motivic local systems, similar results were obtained by André in
[
And96
]
, using the language of motivated cycles.

Following André’s Tannakian approach, our results are based on the theory of motivic Galois groups.
Our treatment relies systematically on the six-functor formalism for Nori motivic sheaves, initiated by Ivorra–Morel in
[
IM24
]
and completed by the author in
[
Ter26
]
.
Of particular importance are the structural properties of motivic Galois groups of smooth algebraic varieties, established by Jacobsen in
[
Jac26
]
.

History and previous work

The study of the Hodge locus began with Weil’s famous observation about Hodge classes in the relative cohomology of a smooth projective family over a smooth complex algebraic variety, from
[
Wei79
]
.
To fix notation, let
p\colon Y\to X
be such a family, and let
n\geq 0
.
As
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
varies, the rational Hodge structures
V_{x}:=H^{2n}(Y_{x};\mathbb{Q})(n)
assemble into a polarizable pure variation of Hodge structure
V
of weight
0
over
X
.
The
Hodge locus
of
V
is defined as the subset of points
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
such that the stalk
V_{x}
contains more classes of type
(0,0)
compared to the very general stalk.
Since the weight and Hodge filtrations on the stalks of
V
vary holomorphically, this locus is a countable union of strict closed analytic subvarieties of
X
.
However, the Hodge Conjecture predicts that all rational
(0,0)
-classes on
V_{x}
arise from algebraic cycles on the fibre
Y_{x}
.
This would imply that the analytic subvarieties forming the Hodge locus are in fact algebraic.

This expectation was established, unconditionally on the Hodge Conjecture and for arbitrary polarizable pure variations, by Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan in
[
CDK95
, Thm. 1.1]
.
As later proved by Brosnan–Pearlstein–Schnell in
[
BPS10
, Cor. 2]
, the result remains true for arbitrary admissible, graded-polarizable variations of mixed Hodge structure:
this is deduced from the pure case via the theory of higher normal functions.
Using the same method, the authors obtained a similar geometric description for the splitting locus of the weight filtration.
All these results, and particularly the proof of
[
CDK95
, Thm. 1.5]
, are based on substantial input from Complex Analysis.

As explained by André in
[
And92
, § 2]
, it is natural to consider Hodge classes not only on
V
but on all of its tensor combinations at once, also known as Hodge tensors.
It is thus natural to replace the usual Hodge locus of
V
by the
tensorial Hodge locus
, defined as the set of points
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
such that
V_{x}
contains more Hodge tensors than the very general stalk – or, equivalently, such that the Mumford–Tate group of
V_{x}
is smaller than that of the very general stalk.
The main advantage of the tensorial Hodge locus over the usual one is its amenability to group-theoretic techniques.

If the variation
V
arises from a smooth projective family
p\colon Y\to X
, the Hodge locus (both in the usual and in the tensorial version) is expected to be defined over any algebraically closed subfield
k\subset\mathbb{C}
over which the family admits a model:
again, this would follow from the validity of the Hodge Conjecture.
So far, such property has not been established unconditionally in general;
see
[
KOU23
]
,
[
SS16
]
,
[
Sim90
]
and
[
Voi07
]
for partial results in this direction.

Variations arising from smooth projective families are basic examples of
variations of geometric origin
.
All such variations are realizations of finer cohomological objects known as
Nori motivic local systems
, defined by the author in
[
Ter26
, § 6]
:
these can be thought of as local systems with values in the Tannakian category
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})
of Nori motives over
\mathbb{C}
.
There is a canonical monoidal Hodge realization functor

\iota_{\mathbb{C}}^{\textup{Hdg}}\colon\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathsf{MHS},

which is conjectured to be fully faithful, with essential image stable under subquotients.
Thus, given a motivic local system
M
over
X
, with underlying variation
V
, the motivic Galois group of
M_{x}
is expected to coincide with the Mumford–Tate group of
V_{x}
for every
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
.
While the fullness conjecture remains widely open in general,
its validity is known for the Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})
generated by the
1
-motives, thanks to André’s
[
And21
, Thm. 1.2.1]
.

In any case, it is natural to look for an analogue of the (tensorial) Hodge locus in the setting of motivic local systems.
For certain pure motivic local systems, this task was addressed by André in
[
And96
, § 5.2]
, in the framework of pure motives built out of motivated cycles:
here, the
exceptional locus
of a motivic local system
M
is defined as the set of points
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
such that the motivic Galois group of
M_{x}
is smaller than that of the very general stalk.
By
[
And96
, Thm. 5.2]
, this locus is again a countable union of strict closed algebraic subvarieties of
X
.
The proof of this result realizes Weil’s cycle-theoretic arguments in the motivic setting, bypassing the Hodge Conjecture.
However, for general mixed motivic local systems, one cannot apply techniques involving algebraic cycles directly.
It is also unclear whether one can argue by induction starting from the pure case, as in the Hodge-theoretic setting.

Main results

The main contribution of the present paper is to define and study the exceptional locus of a general mixed motivic local system.
Let again
X
be a smooth, connected complex variety.
The abelian category
\mathcal{ML}(X)
of Nori motivic local systems over
X
comes equipped with a monoidal forgetful functor

\iota_{X}\colon\mathcal{ML}(X)\to\mathsf{Loc}(X)

into the usual abelian category of local systems.
Thus, complex points
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
parametrize a natural family of fibre functors for
\mathcal{ML}(X)
, and paths between points induce isomorphisms between the corresponding fibre functors.
For every
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
, one gets a family of motivic Galois groups
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)
, defined as the Tannaka dual of
\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}
at
x
:
these naturally assemble into a local systems of algebraic groups.
The
exceptional locus

\mathsf{EL}(M)\subset X(\mathbb{C})
is designed to measure how far are the subgroups
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M_{x})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)
from forming a sub-local system.
More precisely, we regard
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M_{x})
inside the normal subgroup

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x):=\ker\left\{\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,x)\right\}.

Here,
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,x)
denotes the Tannaka dual of the Artin motivic local systems inside
\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}
:
it is a finite group, conjecturally equal to the group of connected components of
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)
.
Thus the subgroups
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)
define again a local system of algebraic groups, conjecturally connected, and one can check that they indeed contain the
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M_{x})
(see
3.3
).
This leads us to define the exceptional locus by declaring that
x\in\mathsf{EL}(M)
if and only if the inclusion
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M_{x})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)
is not an isomorphism.
The intuition is that, for most points
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
, the stalk
M_{x}
is almost as complex as
M
itself, and this generic behavior drops precisely when
x\in\mathsf{EL}(M)
.
In order to confirm this intuition, we need to understand the geometry of the exceptional locus.
We achieve this as part of our main result:

Theorem

(
3.5
,
3.10
)
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
be a motivic local system which is not Artin.
Then, the following hold:

(1)

The locus
\mathsf{EL}(M)
is a countable union of subsets of
X(\mathbb{C})
of the form
S(\mathbb{C})
, with
S
a strict closed algebraic subvariety of
X
.

(2)

Let
k\subset\mathbb{C}
be a subfield such that
X
admits a model
X_{k}
over
k
and
M
admits a model in
\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})
, and let
\overline{k}\subset\mathbb{C}
denote its algebraic closure.
Then:

(i)

All the maximal closed subvarieties of
X
contained in
\mathsf{EL}(M)
are defined over
\overline{k}
.

(ii)

The natural
\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
-action on
X_{\overline{k}}
stabilizes
\mathsf{EL}(M)
.

While the first part of the theorem pertains to the geometry of the exceptional locus, the second part describes its arithmetic properties.
However, we are led to establish the first part together with assertion (i) in the second part at once:
the idea is that every motivic local system admits a model over some countable subfield
k\subset\mathbb{C}
, and
X
contains only a countable amount of closed subvarieties defined over
\overline{k}
.
Both the definition of the exceptional locus and the statement of the main theorem are inspired by André’s
[
And96
, Thm. 5.2]
.
Our proof, however, builds firmly on the rich structural theory of Nori motivic local systems.
This makes it very clean, both from a conceptual and from a technical viewpoint.

The key step in our proof is to understand the motivic Galois group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M_{x})
when
x
is dense for the Zariski topology of
X_{\overline{k}}
, in which case we always have
x\notin\mathsf{EL}(M)
.
To show this, we reinterpret motivic local systems over
X_{k}
as motives over the function field
k(X_{k})
, and we relate their stalks at Zariski-dense points to motives over the algebraic closure
\overline{k(X_{k})}
.
The success of this argument relies crucially on the invariance of motivic Galois groups under extensions of algebraically closed fields (see
2.11
and
2.15
), which is an important result on its own.

Once the key step is achieved, the rest of our proof is a simple Noetherian induction argument.
The only non-trivial ingredient is Hironaka’s classical work on Embedded Resolution of Singularities
[
Hir64
]
, which allows us to treat possibly singular closed subvarieties of
X_{\overline{k}}
.
To this end, we check in advance that the exceptional locus is a compatible with birational morphisms (see
3.6
).

Assertion (ii) in the second part of the theorem is proved using similar ideas.
The only point that needs attention concerns the functoriality of motivic local systems and motivic Galois groups under
k
-automorphisms of
X_{\overline{k}}
(see
3.11
).
Apart from these technical details, the proof comes down to the invariance of
M
under
\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
-conjugation.

Using similar methods, we are able to study the weight-splitting locus of a motivic local system.
Every
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
carries a canonical motivic weight filtration, which matches with the weight filtration on each stalk
M_{x}
up to shifting by
\dim(X)
.
We define the
weight-splitting locus

\mathsf{WL}(M)\subset X(\mathbb{C})
by declaring that
x\in\mathsf{WL}(M)
if and only if the motive
M_{x}
is the direct sum of its weight-graded pieces – or, equivalently, semi-simple in
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})
.

Theorem

(
4.13
,
4.19
)
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
be a motivic local system which is not semi-simple.
Then, the following hold:

(1)

The locus
\mathsf{WL}(M)
is a countable union of subsets of
\mathsf{EL}(M)
of the form
S(\mathbb{C})
, with
S
a strict closed algebraic subvariety of
X
.

(2)

Let
k\subset\mathbb{C}
be a subfield such that
X
admits a model
X_{k}
over
k
and
M
admits a model in
\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})
, and let
\overline{k}\subset\mathbb{C}
denote its algebraic closure.
Then:

(i)

All the maximal closed subvarieties of
X
contained in
\mathsf{WL}(M)
are defined over
\overline{k}
.

(ii)

The natural
\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
-action on
X_{\overline{k}}
stabilizes
\mathsf{WL}(M)
.

In fact, the same result holds for splitting loci of general short exact sequences of motivic local systems (see
4.22
and
4.23
).

As an application, we obtain a motivic proof of the main structural properties of special loci associated to certain variations of Hodge structure of geometric origin.
Thanks to Tubach’s recent work
[
Tub25a
]
, there is a canonical monoidal Hodge realization functor

\iota_{X}^{\textup{Hdg}}\colon\mathcal{ML}(X)\to\mathsf{VHS}(X).

Again, it is conjectured to be fully faithful, with essential image stable under subquotients.
The conjecture has been established for the Tannakian subcategory
\mathcal{ML}^{1}(X)\subset\mathcal{ML}(X)
generated by the
1
-motivic local systems in
[
Tub25b
, Thm. 5.2.12]
(building on
[
And21
, Thm. 1.2.1]
).
Therefore, our results about
1
-motivic local systems imply analogous results for the underlying variations:

Theorem

(
5.19
,
5.20
)
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{ML}^{1}(X)
be a motivic local system which is not Artin, and write
V:=\iota_{X}^{\textup{Hdg}}(M)
.
Then:

(1)

The (tensorial) Hodge locus of
V
is a countable union of subsets of
X(\mathbb{C})
of the form
S(\mathbb{C})
, with
S
a strict closed algebraic subvariety of
X
.

(2)

Let
k\subset\mathbb{C}
be a subfield such that
X
admits a model
X_{k}
over
k
and
M
admits a model in
\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})
, and let
\overline{k}\subset\mathbb{C}
denote its algebraic closure.
Then:

(i)

All the maximal closed subvarieties of
X
contained in the (tensorial) Hodge locus are defined over
\overline{k}
.

(ii)

The natural
\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
-action on
X_{\overline{k}}
stabilizes the (tensorial) Hodge locus.

If
M
is not semi-simple, the analogous statements hold for the weight-splitting locus of
V
.

While the first assertion is covered by the Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan Theorem, the second assertion is a non-trivial arithmetic result about variations of Hodge structure.
To the best of our knowledge, this result should be already known, as a consequence of the rich arithmetic theory of mixed Shimura varieties – although we were unable to find an explicit reference.
However, the general strategy behind our motivic proof does not rely directly on the theory of mixed Shimura varieties:
in fact, it works for any Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{ML}(X)
on which the Hodge realization is known to satisfy the aforementioned fullness conjecture.
This could lead to novel applications to more general variations of geometric origin in the future.

Related work

As already mentioned, our main result about the exceptional locus is inspired by André’s
[
And96
, Thm. 5.2]
, obtained in the language of pure motives built out of motivated cycles.
As proved by Arapura in
[
Ara13
, Thm. 6.4.1]
, André’s category of pure motives over
\mathbb{C}
is naturally equivalent to the subcategory of semi-simple objects in
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})
.
André’s result describes the behavior of the motivic Galois groups of the stalks of a family of pure motives (as defined in
[
And96
, § 5.2]
).
Typical examples of such families are given by the relative cohomology of a smooth proper morphism.
In general, every such family naturally defines a pure Nori motivic local system, and this construction preserves the motivic Galois groups of the stalks.
For pure motivic local systems arising from families of motives in André’s sense, our results on the exceptional locus are already covered by
[
And96
, Thm. 5.2]
, although in a slightly less precise form.

The main advantage of working directly in the setting of Nori motives is the Tannakian theory of motivic local systems over general varieties:
this allows us to relate the motivic Galois groups of the stalks to the motivic Galois group of the entire family in a natural way, and to measure their difference explicitly.
One could still define Tannakian categories of pure motivic local systems using André’s language of motivated cycles, but only over function fields.
We do not believe that every pure motivic local system over a general smooth variety arises from a family of pure motives in André’s sense.
Therefore, our result on the exceptional locus should be more general than
[
And96
, Thm. 5.2]
even in the pure case.

Structure of the paper

The basic definitions and results about Nori motives, motivic local systems, and motivic Galois groups, are summarized in
Section

1
.
In
Section

2
, they are complemented by some more specific results about motivic Galois groups:
these are well-known to the experts, although not always available at the level of generality needed for our purposes in the literature.
The core of the paper is
Section

3
, where we introduce the exceptional locus of a Nori motivic local system and establish its main geometric and arithmetic properties.
Following the same lines, in
Section

4
we study the splitting locus of the motivic weight filtration – and, more generally, splitting loci of motivic short exact sequences.
The final
Section

5
describes our applications to variations of Hodge structure underlying
1
-motivic local systems.

1.
Recollections on Nori motivic local systems

In this section, we review Nori’s theory of mixed motives and its extension to a theory of mixed motivic sheaves, with a focus on motivic local systems and motivic Galois groups.
The reader already familiar with the theory can safely skip this brief account.

Throughout, we work over a field
k
of characteristic
0
endowed with a complex embedding
\sigma\colon k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

1.1.
Nori motives

We begin by reviewing Nori’s theory of motives over
k
.
For convenience, we do not follow Nori’s original approach (as discussed in
[
HMS17
, § II]
) but rather Ivorra–Morel’s modern approach (as developed in
[
IM24
, §§ 1-2]
).

The category
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
of
Nori motives
over
k
is defined as the universal abelian factorization of the homological functor

\beta_{\sigma,k}\colon\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k)\xrightarrow{\mathsf{Bti}_{\sigma,k}^{*}}D^{b}(\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{H^{0}}\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Here,
\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k)
is Voevodsky’s triangulated category of constructible motives over
k
(as defined in Ayoub’s work
[
Ayo07a
,
Ayo07b
]
) and
\mathsf{Bti}_{\sigma,k}^{*}\colon\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k)\to D^{b}(\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}})
denotes the Betti realization associated to
\sigma
(see
[
Ayo10
, Defn. 2.1]
).
Intuitively,
\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k)
contains the universal homology complexes of all
k
-varieties, and the Betti realization converts them into the usual singular homology complexes.
By construction, Nori’s abelian category comes equipped with a homological functor

\pi_{\sigma,k}\colon\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k)\to\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)

and with a faithful exact functor

\iota_{\sigma,k}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)\to\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}

providing a universal factorization of
\beta_{\sigma,k}
of the form

\beta_{\sigma,k}\colon\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k)\xrightarrow{\pi_{\sigma,k}}\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)\xrightarrow{\iota_{\sigma,k}}\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Intuitively,
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
contains the single universal homology groups of all
k
-varieties.

Given a field extension
k^{\prime}/k
and a complex embedding
\sigma^{\prime}\colon k^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
extending
\sigma
, the Betti realization functors fit into a commutative diagram of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Bti}_{\sigma,k}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Bti}_{\sigma^{\prime},k^{\prime}}^{*}}

where the horizontal arrow denotes the base-change functor on Voevodsky motives (for example, see
[
JT25
, Lem. 1.6]
).
This induces a canonical faithful exact base-change functor

\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)\to\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})

rendering the diagram

(1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\sigma,k}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\sigma^{\prime},k^{\prime}}}

commutative up to natural isomorphism.
This construction is compatible with composition of field extensions.
The following result will be useful later on:

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
\left\{k_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
be a filtered family of subfields of
k
such that
k=\bigcup_{i\in I}k_{i}
;
for every
i\in I
, set
\sigma_{i}:=\sigma|_{k_{i}}
.
Then, the induced functor

\textup{2-colim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{M}_{\sigma_{i}}(k_{i})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)

is an equivalence.

Proof.

Since universal abelian factorizations are compatible with filtered
2
-colimits (see
[
Ter24
, Lem. 5.12]
), this follows from the fact that the analogous functor of Voevodsky motives

\textup{2-colim}_{i\in I}\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k_{i})\to\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k)

is an equivalence (see
[
Ayo14c
, Cor. 3.22]
).
∎

The most remarkable aspect of Nori’s theory is the existence of a tensor product on
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
compatible with the usual tensor product of singular homology groups.
More precisely:

Theorem 1.2

(Nori, see
[
HMS17
, Thm. 9.3.10]
)
.

The abelian category
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
carries a canonical unitary symmetric monoidal structure, which turns it into a neutral Tannakian category over
\mathbb{Q}
with canonical fibre functor
\iota_{\sigma,k}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)\to\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Notation 1.3
.

We let
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma)
denote the Tannaka dual of
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
with respect to the fibre functor
\iota_{\sigma,k}
, and we call it the
motivic Galois group
of
k
.
Similarly, for every
N\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
, we let
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\sigma)
denote the Tannaka dual of the Tannakian subcategory
\langle N\rangle^{\otimes}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
with respect to
\iota_{\sigma,k}
, and we call it the
motivic Galois group
of
N
.

Under Tannaka duality, the inclusion
\langle N\rangle^{\otimes}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
corresponds to a surjection

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\sigma).

Example 1.4
.

The
unit motive
over
k
is defined as

\mathbb{Q}_{k}:=\pi_{\sigma,k}(\mathbf{1}_{k})\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k),

where
\mathbf{1}_{k}
denotes the tensor-unit of
\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k)
.
As usual, the fibre functor induces an equivalence
\iota_{\sigma,k}\colon\langle\mathbb{Q}_{k}\rangle^{\otimes}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
We call an object
N\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)

trivial
if
N\in\langle\mathbb{Q}_{k}\rangle^{\otimes}
or, equivalently, if
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\sigma)=1
.

The easiest examples of non-trivial Nori motives arise from finite extensions of the base field
k
.

Example 1.5

(
[
HMS17
, § 9.4]
; see also
[
JT25
, Ex. 2.8]
)
.

Let
k^{\prime}/k
be a finite field extension, and let
e\colon\textup{Spec}(k^{\prime})\to\textup{Spec}(k)
denote the corresponding finite étale morphism.
The associated
Artin motive
is defined as

\mathbb{Q}_{k^{\prime}/k}:=\pi_{\sigma,k}(e_{*}\mathbf{1}_{k^{\prime}})\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k).

If the extension
k^{\prime}/k
is Galois, the motivic Galois group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\mathbb{Q}_{k^{\prime}/k};\sigma)
is canonically isomorphic to the Galois group
\textup{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
, regarded as a finite constant algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Given a further finite extension
k^{\prime\prime}/k^{\prime}
such that the composite extension
k^{\prime\prime}/k
is again Galois, we have the inclusion
\langle\mathbb{Q}_{k^{\prime}/k}\rangle^{\otimes}\subset\langle\mathbb{Q}_{k^{\prime\prime}/k}\rangle^{\otimes}
inside
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
.
Under Tannaka duality, it corresponds to the usual quotient homomorphism

\textup{Gal}(k^{\prime\prime}/k)\twoheadrightarrow\textup{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k),

regarded as a morphism of algebraic groups over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Notation 1.6
.

We let
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(k)
denote the category of
Artin motives
over
k
, defined as the union of the subcategories
\langle\mathbb{Q}_{k^{\prime}/k}\rangle^{\otimes}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
as
k^{\prime}
varies among all finite extensions of
k
inside a fixed algebraic closure
\overline{k}
:
it is again a Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
.

Recall that, under Tannaka duality, filtered unions of Tannakian subcategories inside an ambient Tannakian category correspond to inverse limits of Tannaka dual groups.
In the case of Artin motives, this means that the Tannaka dual of
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(k)
is canonically isomorphic to the absolute Galois group
\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
, regarded as a pro-finite algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Thus, the inclusion
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(k)\subset\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
determines a canonical surjection

(2)

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma)\twoheadrightarrow\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k).

Given a field extension
k^{\prime}/k
and a complex embedding
\sigma^{\prime}\colon k^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
extending
\sigma
, the base-change functor
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)\to\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})
is canonically monoidal (see
[
HMS17
, § 9.5]
), and so is the natural isomorphism filling the diagram (
1
).
This determines a homomorphism of Tannaka dual groups

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k^{\prime};\sigma^{\prime})\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma),

which in general is neither surjective nor a closed immersion.

The situation is easiest to understand in the case of an algebraic extension.
From now henceforth, fix an algebraic closure
\overline{k}
of
k
as well as a complex embedding
\overline{\sigma}\colon\overline{k}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
extending
\sigma
.
The base-change functor
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)\to\mathcal{M}_{\overline{\sigma}}(\overline{k})
corresponds to a homomorphism

(3)

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\overline{k};\overline{\sigma})\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma),

and we have the following fundamental relation:

Proposition 1.7

(
[
HMS17
, Thm. 9.1.16]
;
see also
[
JT25
, Prop. 2.14]
)
.

The sequence of pro-algebraic groups

1\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\overline{k};\overline{\sigma})\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma)\to\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)\to 1

defined by the homomorphisms (
3
) and (
2
) is exact.

For our purposes, it is convenient to work with a finite-dimensional variant of this result.

Notation 1.8
.

For every
N\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
, we let
\langle N\rangle^{\otimes}_{\textup{Art}}
denote the intersection
\langle N\rangle^{\otimes}\cap\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(k)
inside
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
:
it is again a Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
.
Moreover, we let
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(N;\sigma)
denote the Tannaka dual of
\langle N\rangle^{\otimes}_{\textup{Art}}
with respect to the fibre functor
\iota_{\sigma,k}
.

Remark 1.9
.

By construction,
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(N;\sigma)
is the maximal quotient of
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma)
which factors through both
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\sigma)
and
\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
.
Being an algebraic quotient of the pro-finite group
\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
, it is a finite group, equal to
\textup{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
for a unique finite Galois extension
k^{\prime}/k
.

Corollary 1.10
.

Fix an object
N\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
, and let
\overline{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{\overline{\sigma}}(\overline{k})
denote its image under the base-change functor.
Then, the sequence of algebraic groups

1\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\overline{N};\overline{\sigma})\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\sigma)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(N;\sigma)\to 1

is exact.

Proof.

Right-exactness follows formally from the commutative diagram with surjective vertical arrows

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\overline{k};\overline{\sigma})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\overline{N};\overline{\sigma})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(N;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1,}

in which the upper row is exact (by
1.7
).
To conclude, it remains to show that the arrow
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\overline{N};\overline{\sigma})\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\sigma)
is a closed immersion.
This is equivalent to saying that every object of
\langle\overline{N}\rangle^{\otimes}
is a subquotient of one in the essential image of
\langle N\rangle^{\otimes}
(by
[
DM82
, Prop. 2.21(b)]
), which is clear.
∎

Remark 1.11
.

The pro-algebraic group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\overline{k};\overline{\sigma})
is conjectured to be connected (see
[
HMS17
, Rmk. 9.5.7]
).
If this is the case, the same holds for each algebraic quotient
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\overline{N};\overline{\sigma})
.
In particular, the finite group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(N;\sigma)
is expected to coincide with the group of connected components of
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\sigma)
.

Corollary 1.12
.

Let
N
and
\overline{N}
be as in
1.10
.
Moreover, let
k^{\prime}/k
denote the finite Galois extension with Galois group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(N;\sigma)
;
write
\sigma^{\prime}:=\overline{\sigma}|_{k^{\prime}}
, and let
N^{\prime}
denote the image of
N
in
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})
.
Then, the base-change functor

\langle N^{\prime}\rangle^{\otimes}\to\langle\overline{N}\rangle^{\otimes}

is an equivalence.

Proof.

The thesis is equivalent to the assertion that the closed immersion

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\overline{N};\overline{\sigma})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N^{\prime};\sigma^{\prime})

is in fact an isomorphism.
To this end, in view of
1.10
, it suffices to show that the sequence

1\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N^{\prime};\sigma^{\prime})\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\sigma)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(N;\sigma)\to 1

is exact.
But only exactness in the middle needs to be justified, and this follows from the very definition of the extension
k^{\prime}/k
.
∎

1.2.
Nori motivic sheaves

The definition of Nori motives as a universal abelian factorization can be generalized over arbitrary
k
-varieties
X
:
the abelian category
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
of
Nori motivic perverse sheaves
over
X
is defined in
[
IM24
, § 2.1]
as the universal abelian factorization of the homological functor

\beta_{\sigma,X}\colon\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(X)\xrightarrow{\mathsf{Bti}_{\sigma,X}^{*}}D^{b}_{\sigma}(X)\xrightarrow{{{}^{p}H^{0}}}\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma}(X),

where
\mathsf{Bti}_{\sigma,X}^{*}\colon\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(X)\to D^{b}_{\sigma}(X)
denotes the Betti realization of Voevodsky’s constructible motivic sheaves over

(see
[
Ayo10
, Defn. 2.1]
).
In the case when
X=\textup{Spec}(k)
, one obtains the category of Nori motives introduced in the previous section.

Several basic properties of Nori motives hold for motivic perverse sheaves as well.
For instance, we have the following generalization of
1.1
:

Lemma 1.13
.

Keep the notation and assumptions of
1.1
.
Given a
k
-variety
X
, choose an index
i_{0}\in I
such that
X
admits a model
X_{0}
over
k_{i_{0}}
;
for every
i\in I_{i_{0}/}
, set
X_{i}:=X_{0}\times_{k_{i_{0}}}k_{i}
.
Then, the induced functor

\textup{2-colim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{M}_{\sigma_{i}}(X_{i})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)

is an equivalence.

Proof.

As in the proof of
1.13
, this follows from the fact that the analogous functor of Voevodsky motivic sheaves

\textup{2-colim}_{i\in I}\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(X_{i})\to\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(X)

is an equivalence (see
[
Ayo14c
, Cor. 3.22]
).
∎

The main structural result in the theory of Nori motivic sheaves is the following:

Theorem 1.14

(
[
IM24
, Thm. 5.1]
;
[
Ter26
, Thm. 2.1, Thm. 5.1]
;
[
Tub25a
, Constr. 3.15, Prop. A.5]
)
.

As
X
varies among
k
-varieties, the derived categories
D^{b}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X))
are endowed with a canonical six-functor formalism, and the forgetful functors

D^{b}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X))\xrightarrow{\iota_{X}}D^{b}(\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma}(X))\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}_{\sigma}(X)

commute with the six operations.

The last passage in the equation witnesses Beilinson’s equivalence, as in
[
Bei87
, Thm. 1.3]
.
Note that the result incorporates Nori’s
1.2
, which gives the monoidal structure over
\textup{Spec}(k)
.

Using the six operations, one can easily relate different categories of motivic perverse sheaves in the expected way.
As an example:

Lemma 1.15
.

Let
e\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a finite étale morphism.
Then, the inverse image functor
e^{*}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X^{\prime})
induces an equivalence

\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X^{\prime})^{\textup{Gal}(X^{\prime}/X)}

towards the
\textup{Gal}(X^{\prime}/X)
-equivariant objects, with respect to the
\textup{Gal}(X^{\prime}/X)
-action on
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X^{\prime})
induced by the natural one on
X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

In the special case when
e
is induced by a finite field extension
k^{\prime}/k
, the result is
[
JT25
, Lem. 2.13(2)]
:
the proof comes down to the fact that motivic perverse sheaves form a stack for the étale topology (by
[
IM24
, Prop. 2.7]
).
The same argument works in the general case considered here.
∎

Remark 1.16
.

Suppose that
e
is induced by a finite extension
k^{\prime}/k
, and fix a complex embedding
\sigma^{\prime}\colon k^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
extending
\sigma
.
We can regard
X^{\prime}
either as a
k^{\prime}
-variety or as a
k
-variety.
However, the corresponding categories
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
are canonically equivalent (by
[
IM24
, Prop. 6.11]
, see also
[
Ter26
, Prop. 1.14]
).

1.3.
Nori motivic local systems

Let now
X
be a smooth, connected
k
-variety.
The shift endofunctor
[\dim(X)]\colon D^{b}_{\sigma}(X)\to D^{b}_{\sigma}(X)
identifies the abelian category of ordinary local systems over
X^{\sigma}
with a full abelian subcategory
\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)\subset\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma}(X)
, stable under subquotients and extensions (for example, see
[
JT25
, Lem. 1.29]
).
This naturally generalizes to arbitrary smooth
k
-varieties, by considering one connected component at a time.
In the present paper, we always regard local systems as perverse sheaves in this way.

We are mostly interested in the case when
X
is geometrically connected over
k
–
or, equivalently, when the complex-analytic space
X^{\sigma}
is connected (see
[
SGA1
, Exp. XII, Cor. 2.6]
).
In this case, ordinary local systems over
X^{\sigma}
form a neutral Tannakian category over
\mathbb{Q}
with respect to the usual tensor product.
With our conventions, this corresponds to the shifted tensor product

-\otimes^{\dagger}-:=(-\otimes-)[-\dim(X)]\colon\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)\times\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X).

The Tannaka dual of
\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)
at a point
x\in X^{\sigma}
is the algebraic fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X^{\sigma},x)
, defined as the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic completion of the usual topological fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X^{\sigma},x)
.

For every morphism
f\colon X\to Y
between smooth, connected
k
-varieties, the shifted inverse image functor

f^{\dagger}:=f^{*}[\dim(X)-\dim(Y)]\colon D^{b}_{\sigma}(Y)\to D^{b}_{\sigma}(X)

restricts to a monoidal functor

f^{\dagger}\colon\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(Y)\to\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X).

This is our model for the theory of Nori motivic local systems.
In the following, let
X
be a smooth, geometrically connected
k
-variety.

Notation 1.17

(
[
Ter26
, Defn. 6.1]
)
.

An object
M\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
is called a
motivic local system
if its underlying perverse sheaf
\iota_{X}(M)\in\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma}(X)
belongs to
\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)
.
We let
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
denote the full subcategory of
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
spanned by the motivic local systems:
it is an abelian subcategory, stable under subquotients and extensions.

Motivic local systems inherit a canonical monoidal structure, based on the shifted tensor product

-\otimes^{\dagger}-\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\times\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X).

Theorem 1.18

(
[
Ter26
, Thm. 6.3]
)
.

The abelian category
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
, endowed with the shifted tensor product, is canonically neutral Tannakian over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Here, the adverb "canonically" refers to the existence of the canonical monoidal exact functor
\iota_{\sigma,X}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)
;
the monoidality is inherited from
1.14
.
This yields a natural family of fibre functors on motivic local systems:
for every point
x\in X^{\sigma}
, the fibre functor at
x
is defined as the composite

\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\xrightarrow{\iota_{\sigma,X}}\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)\xrightarrow{x^{\dagger}}\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}},

where
x^{\dagger}:=x^{*}[-\dim(X)]\colon\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the shifted inverse image functor.

Notation 1.19
.

For every point
x\in X^{\sigma}
, we let
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)
denote the Tannaka dual of
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
with respect to the fibre functor at
x
.
Similarly, for every
M\in\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
we let
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x;\sigma)
denote the Tannaka dual of the Tannakian subcategory
\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}\subset\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
with respect to the same fibre functor.

As in the case of Nori motives over fields, a source of non-trivial motivic local systems is the Galois theory of algebraic varieties:

Example 1.20

(
[
Tub26
, Prop. 5.13]
; see also
[
JT25
, Ex. 2.20]
)
.

Let
e\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a finite étale covering.
The associated

is defined as

{{}^{p}\mathbb{Q}}_{X^{\prime}/X}:=\pi_{\sigma,X}(e_{*}\mathbf{1}_{X^{\prime}}[\dim(X)])=e_{*}\pi_{\sigma,X^{\prime}}(\mathbf{1}_{X^{\prime}}[\dim(X)])\in\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X),

where
\mathbf{1}_{X^{\prime}}
denotes the tensor-unit of
\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(X^{\prime})
.
If the covering
X^{\prime}/X
is Galois, the motivic Galois group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}({{}^{p}\mathbb{Q}}_{X^{\prime}/X},x;\sigma)
is canonically isomorphic to the Galois group
\textup{Gal}(X^{\prime}/X)
, regarded as a finite constant algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Notation 1.21
.

We let
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(X)
denote the union of the subcategories
\langle{{}^{p}\mathbb{Q}}_{X^{\prime}/X}\rangle^{\otimes}
as

varies among all connected finite étale covers of
X
, and we call it the category of
Artin motivic local systems
over

:
it is again a Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
.

Generalizing the case of Artin motives over a field, the Tannaka dual of
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(X)
is isomorphic to the étale fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{\textup{{\'{e}}t}}(X,x)
, regarded as a pro-finite algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
.
The inclusion
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(X)\subset\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
determines a canonical surjection

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\textup{{\'{e}}t}}(X,x).

Later in the paper, we need to consider the maximal Artin quotients of finite-dimensional motivic Galois groups.
To this end, we introduce the following notation:

Notation 1.22
.

For every
M\in\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
, we let
\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}_{\textup{Art}}
denote the intersection
\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}\cap\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(X)
inside
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
:
it is again a Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
.
Moreover, we let
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,x;\sigma)
denote the Tannaka dual of
\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}_{\textup{Art}}
with respect to the fibre functor at
x
.

Under Tannaka duality, the monoidal functor
\iota_{\sigma,k}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)
determines a canonical homomorphism of pro-algebraic groups

(4)

\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X^{\sigma},x)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma).

Given a morphism between smooth, geometrically connected
k
-varieties
f\colon X\to Y
, the shifted inverse image functor

f^{\dagger}:=f^{*}[\dim(X)-\dim(Y)]\colon D^{b}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(Y))\to D^{b}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X))

restricts to a monoidal exact functor

f^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(Y)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X).

For every base-point
x\in X^{\sigma}
, this determines a homomorphism of Tannaka dual groups

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(Y,f(x);\sigma).

Note that

restricts to a monoidal functor

f^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(Y)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(X).

To see this, let
e_{Y}\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y

1.4.
The fundamental sequence

As before, let
X
be a smooth, geometrically connected
k
-variety.
We now discuss the relation between motives over
k
, motivic local systems over
X
, and topological local systems over
X^{\sigma}
.
We start from the following observation:

Lemma 1.23

(
[
JT25
, Rmk. 2.19]
)
.

Let
a_{X}\colon X\to\textup{Spec}(k)
denote the structural morphism.
The shifted inverse image functor

a_{X}^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)

is fully faithful, with essential image stable under subquotients.

This means that the induced homomorphism of Tannaka dual groups

(5)

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma)

is surjective (by
[
DM82
, Prop. 2.21(a)]
).
If the base-point
x\in X^{\sigma}
is
k
-rational, this surjection acquires a canonical splitting, induced by the inclusion
x\colon\textup{Spec}(k)\hookrightarrow X
.
This suggests that the structure of
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)
can be better understood when
x
is algebraic enough.
However, since a general
k
-variety might not have
k
-rational points at all, it is convenient to look at all
\overline{k}
-rational points.
We have the following important result:

Theorem 1.24

(
[
Jac26
, Thm. 7.7(1)]
;
[
JT25
, Thm. 2.24]
)
.

For every
x\in X(\overline{k})
, the sequence of pro-algebraic groups

\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X^{\sigma},x)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma)\to 1

defined by the homomorphisms (
4
) and (
5
) is exact;
it is even split-exact if
x\in X(k)
.

The arrow
\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X^{\sigma},x)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)
is in general not a closed immersion, because not every local system on
X^{\sigma}
is a subquotient of one underlying a motivic local system.
The full subcategory of
\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)
spanned by such subquotients is the Tannakian subcategory of
local systems of geometric origin
(see
[
Jac26
, § 7.2]
or
[
JT25
, §§ 1.3, 2.3]
).

Remark 1.25
.

Fix an algebraic base-point
x\in X(\overline{k})
, and let
M\in\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
.
As a consequence of
1.24
, we have the inequality

\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x))\leq\dim(\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(\iota_{X}(M),x))+\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}M)),

where
\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(L,x)
denotes the Tannaka dual of the Tannakian subcategory
\langle L\rangle^{\otimes}\subset\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)
.
If
x\in X(k)
, this follows from the surjectivity of the morphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-varieties

\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(\iota_{X}(M),x)\times\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}M)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)\times\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x),

where the second arrow is the multiplication morphism of
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)
.
As a consequence, we have the bound

\max_{x_{1}\in X(\mathbb{C})}\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x_{1}^{\dagger}M))-\min_{x_{2}\in X(\mathbb{C})}\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x_{2}^{\dagger}M))\leq\dim(\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(\iota_{X}(M),x)),

thanks to the inequality

\max_{x_{1}\in X(\mathbb{C})}\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x_{1}^{\dagger}M))\leq\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)).

In fact, the latter is always an equality.
This is a consequence of
3.5
below.

2.
Remarkable relations among motivic Galois groups

The present section contains two technical results comparing motivic Galois groups arising from different motivic categories:
the first one relates motivic local systems over a smooth variety to motives over its function field,
while the second one describes how these change under suitable extensions of the base field.
Their combination plays a crucial role in the proof of our main results in
Sections

3
and
4
.

As in the previous section, we work over a field
k
of characteristic
0
endowed with a complex embedding
\sigma\colon k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

2.1.
Generic motivic local systems

Let
X
be a smooth, geometrically connected
k
-variety, with function field
k(X)
.
We are going to introduce a generic variant of the category
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
:
it is modeled on the category of
generic local systems

\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(U)
, where the colimit is taken over all non-empty Zariski opens
U\subset X
.
Note that the canonical functor

\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(U)\to\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma}(U)

is an equivalence, because every perverse sheaf restricts to a shifted local system over some smaller non-empty Zariski open.

Notation 2.1
.

We let
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k(X))
denote the universal abelian factorization of the homological functor

\beta_{\sigma,k(X)}\colon\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(U)\xrightarrow{(\beta_{\sigma,U})_{U}}\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma}(U)=\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(U),

and we call it the category of
generic motivic local systems
over
X
.

The terminology is justified by the observation that a generic motivic local system on
X
can be seen as a motivic local system defined over some unspecified Zariski-dense open
U
.
More precisely:

Lemma 2.2
.

The canonical functor

(6)

\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(U)\to\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(U)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))

is an equivalence.

Proof.

Indeed, both arrows are equivalences:
the first one by cofinality of local systems inside perverse sheaves (as explained above), the second one by compatibility of universal abelian factorizations with filtered
2
-colimits (see
[
Ter24
, Lem. 5.12]
).
∎

Note that the abelian category
\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(U)
carries a canonical monoidal structure compatible with that of each
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(U)
.
Moreover, the abelian category
\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(U)
can be regarded as a full monoidal subcategory of the former, stable under subquotients (since this is the case for each
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(U)
inside
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(U)
).
Via the equivalence (
6
), the abelian category
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k(X))
inherits a compatible monoidal structure.

Notation 2.3
.

We let
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(k(X))
denote the full subcategory of
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))
corresponding to
\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(U)
:
it is a monoidal subcategory, stable under subquotients.

The following result identifies motivic local systems on
X
with those generic local systems which extend to the whole of
X
:

Proposition 2.4
.

The monoidal exact functor

\eta_{X}^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\to\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(U)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k(X))

is fully faithful, with essential image stable under subquotients, and similarly for its restriction

\eta_{X}^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(X)\to\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(U)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(k(X)).

Proof.

In the first place, we establish the properties of
\eta_{X}^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k(X))
.
For this, it suffices to establish the same properties for the inverse image functor
j^{*}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(U)
under a Zariski-dense affine open immersion
j\colon U\hookrightarrow X
.
This is proved in
[
JT25
, Lem. 5.12]
, but we sketch the argument here for the reader’s convenience.

As a consequence of
[
BBD82
, Lem. 4.3.2]
, the intermediate extension functor

j_{!*}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(U)\to\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)

is fully faithful, and its restriction to
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(U)
provides a left-inverse to
j^{*}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(U)
.
This implies formally that the latter is fully faithful.
To show that its essential image is stable under subquotients, it suffices to show that
j_{!*}
provides a two-sided inverse to
j^{*}
on subobject lattices of motivic local systems.
This follows from the fact that
j_{!*}
is left-inverse to
j^{*}
on motivic local systems, while
j^{*}
is even left-inverse to
j_{!*}
on motivic perverse sheaves.
This proves the desired properties for the functor
\eta_{X}^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k(X))
.

To deduce the claim for the restriction to Artin motivic local systems, it suffices to note that the same properties hold for the inclusions
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(X)\subset\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
and
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(k(X))\subset\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))
(see
1.20
).
∎

With some care, the results can be translated in Tannaka dual terms:

Corollary 2.5
.

Keep the notation and assumptions of
2.4
.
Let
z\in X^{\sigma}
be a point which is dense for the Zariski topology of
X
.
Then, we have a canonical commutative diagram of motivic Galois groups

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\eta_{X}^{\dagger}M,z;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,z;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(\eta_{X}^{\dagger}M,z;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,z;\sigma),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

where both horizontal arrows are isomorphisms.

Proof.

The Zariski-denseness assumption means that the point
z
lies in
U^{\sigma}
for each Zariski open
U\subset X
, and therefore defines a fibre functor on
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))
.
Therefore,
2.4
yields an equivalence of neutralized Tannakian categories
\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}\xrightarrow{\sim}\langle\eta_{X}^{\dagger}M\rangle^{\otimes}
, which restricts to an equivalence
\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}_{\textup{Art}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\langle\eta_{X}^{\dagger}M\rangle^{\otimes}_{\textup{Art}}
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

The existence of a point
z\in X^{\sigma}
as in
2.5
depends on
k
:
for example, when
k=\mathbb{C}
, no such point exists as soon as
\dim(X)\geq 1

(see
2.8
below for a more thorough discussion).
In any case, the monoidal abelian category
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))
is always neutral Tannakian over
\mathbb{Q}
:
a fibre functor can be obtained by composing the forgetful functor

\iota_{\sigma,k(X)}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))\to\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(U)

with a fibre functor for
\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(U)
, which in turn can be constructed as in
[
BT25
, § 4.4]
.
However, we do not need to pursue this approach here.

2.2.
Nori motives over function fields

As before, let
X
be a smooth, geometrically connected
k
-variety.
We want to make sense of
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))
as a category of Nori motives over the function field
k(X)
.
This is the content of our first auxiliary result:

Proposition 2.7
.

Assume that there exists a complex embedding
\Sigma\colon k(X)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
extending
\sigma
.
Then, there is a canonical monoidal equivalence

(7)

\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))=\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(k(X)),

which restricts to a monoidal equivalence

(8)

\mathcal{ML}^{\textup{Art}}_{\sigma}(k(X))=\mathcal{M}^{\textup{Art}}_{\Sigma}(k(X)).

The proof strategy is based on the following geometric idea, the relevance of which in the motivic setting we learned from the proof of
[
Ayo14b
, Prop. 2.20]
:

Construction 2.8
.

Keep the notation and assumptions of
2.7
.
The complex embedding
\Sigma\colon k(X)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
corresponds canonically to a point
z_{\Sigma}\in X^{\sigma}=(X\times_{k,\sigma}\mathbb{C})(\mathbb{C})
:
namely, the morphism
\textup{Spec}(\mathbb{C})\to X\times_{k,\sigma}\mathbb{C}
induced by the sheaf monomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{X}\subset k(X)\xrightarrow{\Sigma}\mathbb{C}.

By construction, the point
z_{\Sigma}
does not lie on any subset of the form
Y^{\sigma}
with
Y
a strict closed algebraic subvariety of
X
:
in other words, it is dense for the Zariski topology of
X
.

In fact, the rule
\Sigma\mapsto z_{\Sigma}
defines a natural bijection between complex embeddings of
k(X)
extending
\sigma
and points of
X^{\sigma}
which are dense for the Zariski topology of
X
:
the inverse rule
z\mapsto\Sigma_{z}
associates to a Zariski-dense point
z\in X^{\sigma}
the complex embedding
\Sigma_{z}\colon k(X)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
extending the sheaf monomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{X}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k,\sigma}\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{z^{*}}\mathbb{C}.

More generally, for every irreducible closed subvariety
S\subset X
, complex embeddings of
k(S)
extending
\sigma
correspond to points
t\in X^{\sigma}
with Zariski-closure
S
.
∎

Proof of
2.7
.

In the first place, we explain how to obtain the equivalence of abelian categories (
7
). Since both categories are defined as universal abelian factorizations, it suffices to show that the two exact functors on the abelian hull

\beta_{\sigma,k(X)}^{+}\colon\mathfrak{A}(\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k(X)))\to\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(U),\qquad\beta_{\Sigma,k(X)}^{+}\colon\mathfrak{A}(\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k(X)))\to\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}

have the same kernel.
This is proved in
[
Ter26
, Prop. 1.14]
, but we sketch the argument here for the reader’s convenience.
Let
z_{\Sigma}
denote the Zariski-dense point defined by
\Sigma
(as in
2.8
).
Taking the stalk at
z_{\Sigma}
defines a compatible system of fibre functors

z_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}\colon\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(U)\to\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}

for all Zariski-dense opens
U\subset X
, whence an analogous fibre functor on
\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(U)
. Unwinding the definitions, one checks that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k(X))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k(X))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{2-colim}_{U\subset X}\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma}(U)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\beta_{\sigma,U})_{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[-\dim(X)]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta_{\Sigma,k(X)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces z_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}}

The sought-after equality of kernels follows formally from the conservativity of

In order to promote the equivalence of abelian categories (

\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(U)
(resp.
\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k(X))

(\mathsf{Bti}_{\sigma,U}^{*})^{-1}(\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma}(U))
(resp.
(\mathsf{Bti}_{\Sigma,k(X)}^{*})^{-1}(\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}})

Indeed, in view of the way the monoidal structure on Nori motivic perverse sheaves is constructed (see
[
Ter26
, § 2]
), one easily sees that the equivalence (

Moreover, it is also compatible with inverse images under diagonal immersions, in view of the way these are defined (see
[
IM24
, § 4]
).
This implies its compatibility with the internal tensor product.

To deduce the equivalence (

\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(k(X))
inside
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(k(X))
coincides with
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(k(X))
.
To this end, fix a Zariski-dense open

together with a finite étale cover

, and form the Cartesian square of schemes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Spec}(k(X))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{e}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{U}}

\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(k(X))

\eta_{U}^{*}e_{*}\mathbf{1}_{U^{\prime}}=\tilde{e}_{*}\mathbf{1}_{V},

the object
\eta_{U}^{\dagger}({{}^{p}\mathbb{Q}}_{U^{\prime}/U})\in\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))
corresponds to the object
{{}^{p}\mathbb{Q}}_{V/k(X)}\in\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(k(X))
under the equivalence (
7
).
This implies that the essential image of
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(k(X))
in
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(k(X))
is contained inside
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(k(X))
.
Conversely, since every finite étale

-algebra is of the form
\mathcal{O}(V)
for

as above, for a suitable Zariski-dense open

and a suitable finite étale cover

, the whole of
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(k(X))
is contained in the essential image.
∎

We are finally able to deduce our first technical result about motivic Galois groups:

Corollary 2.9
.

Keep the notation and assumptions of
2.7
.
Given
M\in\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
, let

denote the object corresponding to
\eta_{X}^{\dagger}M\in\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))
.
Then, there is a canonical commutative diagram of motivic Galois groups

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\Sigma)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(N;\Sigma)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

Proof.

By construction, the equivalence of

renders the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(k(X))}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\sigma,k(X)}}

Therefore, it determines an equivalence of neutralized Tannakian categories

, which restricts to an equivalence
\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}_{\textup{Art}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\langle N\rangle^{\otimes}_{\textup{Art}}

∎

2.3.
Topological invariance

Let now
k^{\prime}/k
be a field extension, and suppose that we are given a complex embedding
\sigma^{\prime}\colon k^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
extending
\sigma
.
Fix a smooth, geometrically connected
k
-variety
X
, and set
X^{\prime}:=X\times_{k}k^{\prime}
.
Our next goal is to compare the categories
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
and
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})
.
The starting point is the canonical isomorphism of complex varieties

X\times_{k,\sigma}\mathbb{C}=(X\times_{k}k^{\prime})\times_{k^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\mathbb{C}=X^{\prime}\times_{k^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\mathbb{C}.

The induced identification of complex-analytic spaces

X^{\sigma}=(X^{\prime})^{\sigma^{\prime}}

allows us to regard
D^{b}_{\sigma}(X)
as a full stable sub-
\infty
-category of
D^{b}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})
.
As
X
varies, these inclusion functors commute with the six operations, which implies that they are compatible with the perverse
t
-structures:
we get a fully faithful exact functor

\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(X^{\prime}),

which restricts to a monoidal equivalence

\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(X^{\prime}).

For every point
x\in X^{\sigma}
, corresponding to
x^{\prime}\in(X^{\prime})^{\sigma^{\prime}}
, we get an identification of Tannaka dual groups

\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}((X^{\prime})^{\sigma^{\prime}},x^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X^{\sigma},x).

We want to study the analogous picture in the motivic setting.

Construction 2.10
.

In view of the definition of the Betti realization functors, the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{DA}_{\textup{ct}}(X^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta_{\sigma,X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta_{\sigma^{\prime},X^{\prime}}}

commutes up to canonical natural isomorphism (for example, see
[
JT25
, Lem. 1.6]
).
This determines a canonical base-change functor

\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})

making the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\sigma,X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\sigma^{\prime},X^{\prime}}}

commute.
If
X
is a smooth and geometrically connected, by restriction to motivic local systems we get the commutative diagram of monoidal exact functors

(9)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(X^{\prime}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\sigma,X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\sigma^{\prime},X^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

which is compatible with the fibre functors at any point
x\in X^{\sigma}
.

The construction is compatible with motivic shifted inverse image functors.
In particular, writing
a_{X}\colon X\to\textup{Spec}(k)
and
a^{\prime}_{X^{\prime}}\colon X^{\prime}\to\textup{Spec}(k^{\prime})
for the structural morphisms, we get the commutative diagram of monoidal functors

(10)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(X^{\prime}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{X}^{\dagger}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(a^{\prime}_{X^{\prime}})^{\dagger}}

where both vertical arrows are fully faithful (by
1.23
).
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Suppose that the field extension
k^{\prime}/k
is geometrically integral.
Then, the monoidal functor

(11)

\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})

is fully faithful, with essential image stable under subquotients, and similarly for its restriction

(12)

\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{Art}}(X)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\textup{Art}}(X^{\prime}).

Proof.

Since Artin motivic local systems are stable under subquotients inside all motivic local systems (being a Tannakian subcategory), the asserted properties of the functor (
11
) imply the same properties for its restriction (
12
).
So let us prove the statement for the functor (
11
).

To begin with, we reduce ourselves to the case when
X=\textup{Spec}(k)
.
To this end, choose a base-point
x\in X^{\sigma}
, corresponding to
x^{\prime}\in(X^{\prime})^{\sigma^{\prime}}
.
The thesis holds for
X
if and only if the homomorphism
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X^{\prime},x^{\prime};\sigma^{\prime})\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)
is surjective (by
[
DM82
, Prop. 2.21(a)]
).
Under Tannaka duality, the commutative diagrams (
9
) and (
10
) determine a commutative diagram of pro-algebraic groups

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X^{\prime},x^{\prime};\sigma^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X^{\prime},x^{\prime};\sigma^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k^{\prime};\sigma^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X,x;\sigma^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1,}

in which both rows are exact (by
1.24
).
If the thesis holds for
\textup{Spec}(k)
, the right-most arrow in the diagram is surjective (again by
[
DM82
, Prop. 2.21(a)]
).
But then, the surjectivity of the middle vertical arrow follows formally by diagram-chasing.

From now on, assume that
X=\textup{Spec}(k)
and hence
X^{\prime}=\textup{Spec}(k^{\prime})
.
We further reduce ourselves to the case when the extension
k^{\prime}/k
is finitely generated (and still geometrically integral).
To this end, let
\mathcal{I}_{k^{\prime}/k}
denote the filtered poset of all intermediate fields
k\subset L\subset k^{\prime}
which are finitely generated over
k
;
note that each such extension
L/k
is still geometrically integral (since so is the ambient extension
k^{\prime}/k
).
By
1.1
, the canonical functor

\textup{2-colim}_{L\in\mathcal{I}_{k^{\prime}/k}}\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\prime}|_{L}}(L)\to\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})

is an equivalence.
Suppose that the thesis is known to hold for all field extensions
L/k
with
L\in\mathcal{I}_{k^{\prime}/k}
.
Then, using the explicit description of objects and morphisms in a filtered
2
-colimit, we deduce that the thesis also holds for the functor

\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)\to\textup{2-colim}_{L\in\mathcal{I}_{k^{\prime}/k}}\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\prime}|_{L}}(L),

which settles the case of the original extension
k^{\prime}/k
.

Finally, assume that the extension
k^{\prime}/k
is finitely generated and geometrically integral.
Fix a smooth, connected
k
-variety
S
with function field
k^{\prime}
, and note that it is automatically geometrically connected (since the extension
k^{\prime}/k
is geometrically integral, by assumption).
By construction, the base-change functor in the statement factors as

\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)\xrightarrow{a_{S}^{\dagger}}\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(S)\xrightarrow{\eta_{S}^{\dagger}}\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k^{\prime})=\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})

where the last passage witnesses the equivalence of
2.7
.
Since both the first and the second arrow are known to be fully faithful, with essential image stable under subquotients (by
1.23
and
2.4
, respectively), the same holds for the composite functor.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

The conclusion of
2.11
holds, more generally, for the base-change functor on motivic perverse sheaves over any
k
-variety:
this can be deduced by Noetherian induction starting from the case of motivic local systems, by adapting the method of
[
Ter26
, Prop. 1.9]
.

Remark 2.13
.

If
k^{\prime}/k
is an extension of algebraically closed fields, the base-change functor on Artin motivic local systems (
12
) is in fact an equivalence.
This follows essentially from the fact that every finite étale cover of
X^{\prime}
is the base-change of a finite étale cover of
X
(see
[
Stacks
, Prop. 58.8.4]
).

Remark 2.14
.

If the extension
k^{\prime}/k
is not geometrically integral, the functor
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)\to\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})
is still faithful, but not full:
the counter-example is given by non-trivial Artin motives over
k
which become trivial over
k^{\prime}
.

To close the discussion, we deduce our second technical result about motivic Galois groups:

Corollary 2.15
.

Keep the notation and assumptions of
2.11
.
Given
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
, let
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{ML}(X^{\prime})
denote its image under the base-change functor.
Then, for every point
x\in X^{\sigma}
, corresponding to
x^{\prime}\in(X^{\prime})^{\sigma^{\prime}}
, there is a canonical commutative diagram of motivic Galois groups

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M^{\prime},x^{\prime};\sigma^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M^{\prime},x^{\prime};\sigma^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,x;\sigma),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

where both horizontal arrows are isomorphisms.

Proof.

Indeed, by
2.11
, the base-change functor induces an equivalence of neutralized Tannakian categories
\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}\xrightarrow{\sim}\langle M^{\prime}\rangle^{\otimes}
, which restricts to an equivalence
\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}_{\textup{Art}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\langle M^{\prime}\rangle^{\otimes}_{\textup{Art}}
.
∎

3.
The exceptional locus

The present section contains the main results of the paper:
we introduce the exceptional locus of a motivic local systems, and we study its geometric and arithmetic properties.

We work over varieties defined over the complex numbers or subfields thereof.
The complex embedding used to define motivic local systems and their motivic Galois groups will always be the inclusion, and we omit it from the notation.

3.1.
Definition of the exceptional locus

Let
X
be a smooth, connected complex variety.
For every point
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
, the monoidal exact functor
x^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}(X)\to\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})
determines a homomorphism of Tannaka dual groups

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x).

Given a motivic local system
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
, the restricted fibre functor
x^{\dagger}\colon\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}\to\langle x^{\dagger}M\rangle^{\otimes}
determines a closed immersion

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}M)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x).

In particular, we have
\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}M))\leq\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x))
, with equality if and only if the inclusion of motivic Galois groups has finite index.

As
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
varies, the assignment
x\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)
can be thought of as a local system of algebraic groups.
It is natural to wonder how far is the assignment
x\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}M)
from defining a sub-local system.
In order to understand this, it is convenient to remove the contribution of Artin motivic local systems.

Notation 3.1
.

For every point
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
, we set
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x):=\ker\left\{\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,x)\right\}
.

As
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
varies, the assignment
x\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,x)
can be thought of as a quotient local system of
x\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)
.
Therefore, the assignment
x\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)
defines a sub-local system of it.
Since
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,x)
is a finite algebraic group (see
1.9
), the group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)
has finite index inside
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)
.
Hence, its pre-image under the closed immersion
\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(\iota_{X}(M),x)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)
is a normal subgroup of finite index inside
\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(\iota_{X}(M),x)
.
In particular,
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)
contains the image of a normal subgroup of finite index of the discrete group
\pi_{1}(\iota_{X}(M),x)
.
More precisely:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
e\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be the finite Galois covering with Galois group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,x)
, and let
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})
be a pre-image of
x
.
Then, the composite homomorphism

\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}((X^{\prime})^{\sigma^{\prime}},x^{\prime})\to\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X^{\sigma},x)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(\iota_{X}(M),x)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)

factors through
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)
.

Proof.

The homomorphism in the statement equals the composite

\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}((X^{\prime})^{\sigma^{\prime}},x^{\prime})\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(e^{*}\iota_{X}(M),x^{\prime})\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(\iota_{X}(M),x)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x),

which in turn equals the composite

\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}((X^{\prime})^{\sigma^{\prime}},x^{\prime})\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(\iota_{X^{\prime}}(e^{*}M),x^{\prime})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(e^{*}M,x^{\prime})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x).

The latter factors through
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)
, because the composite

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(e^{*}M,x^{\prime})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,x)

is already trivial (by construction).
∎

The group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)
provides a refined upper bound for
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}M)
, as the following observation shows:

Lemma 3.3
.

For every point
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
, the closed immersion
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}M)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)
factors through
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)
.

Proof.

We have to show that the composite homomorphism

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}M)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,x)

is trivial.
Under Tannaka duality, this amounts to showing that, for every given
N\in\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}_{\textup{Art}}
, the motive
x^{\dagger}N\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})
is trivial.
To this end, let
e\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be the finite Galois covering with Galois group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,x)
, and fix a pre-image
x^{\prime}
of
x
in
X^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})
.
We have an isomorphism of motivic Galois groups

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}((x^{\prime})^{\dagger}e^{*}N)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}N),

induced by the isomorphism of motives
(x^{\prime})^{\dagger}e^{*}N=x^{\dagger}N
.
But the group on the left-hand side is trivial, because the object
e^{*}N\in\mathcal{ML}(X^{\prime})
is already trivial (by construction).
∎

In view of the last result, it makes sense to consider the following:

Definition 3.4
.

We define the
exceptional locus
of
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
as the subset
\mathsf{EL}(M)\subset X(\mathbb{C})
consisting of all points
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
such that the closed immersion
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{*}M)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)
is not an isomorphism.

Recall that the group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}M)
is conjectured to be connected (see
1.11
).
Hence, the homomorphism
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{*}M)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)
is expected to factor through the identity component
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{0}(M,x)
.
On the other hand, the finite quotient
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,x)
is conjectured to be exactly the group of connected components of
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)
.
In other words, the subgroup
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)
is conjectured to coincide with
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{0}(M,x)
.
The conclusion of
3.3
is coherent with this expectation.

3.2.
Geometric description

As in the previous subsection, let
X
be a smooth, connected complex variety.
We want to describe the exceptional locus
\mathsf{EL}(M)
of a motivic local system
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
geometrically.
Note that we trivially have
\mathsf{EL}(M)=X(\mathbb{C})
whenever
M
is Artin (by
3.3
above).
In fact, the converse implication holds as well.
This is a consequence of our first main result, which can be stated as follows:

Theorem 3.5
.

For every
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
which is not Artin, the locus
\mathsf{EL}(M)\subset X(\mathbb{C})
is a countable union of subsets of the form
S(\mathbb{C})
, with
S
a strict closed algebraic subvariety of
X
.
Moreover, if
X
admits a model
X_{k}
over an algebraically closed subfield
k\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
M
belongs to the essential image of
\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})
, then each maximal closed subvariety inside
\mathsf{EL}(M)
is defined over
k
as well.

In particular, the locus
\mathsf{EL}(M)
cannot contain a non-empty analytic open subset of
X(\mathbb{C})
unless
M
is Artin, in which case it coincides with
X(\mathbb{C})
.
Our
3.5
is a motivic analogue of Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan’s celebrated result for pure variations of Hodge structure
[
CDK95
, Thm. 1.1]
, extended to admissible mixed variations in
[
BPS10
, Cor. 2]
.

In the course of the proof, we use an auxiliary observation of independent interest:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
p\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a birational morphism between smooth, connected complex varieties.
Then, for every
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
, we have the equality

\mathsf{EL}(p^{*}M)=p^{-1}(\mathsf{EL}(M)).

Proof.

Note that the inverse image functor

p^{\dagger}=p^{*}\colon\mathcal{ML}(X)\to\mathcal{ML}(X^{\prime})

is fully faithful, with essential image stable under subquotients:
indeed, it fits into the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}(X^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}(\mathbb{C}(X))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}(\mathbb{C}(X^{\prime})),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

in which both vertical arrows enjoy the same properties (by
2.4
) and the lower horizontal arrow is even an equivalence (as
p
is birational, by hypothesis).
As a consequence, the same properties hold for the restriction

p^{\dagger}=p^{*}\colon\mathcal{ML}^{\textup{Art}}(X)\to\mathcal{ML}^{\textup{Art}}(X^{\prime}).

(see
1.9
).
This implies that, for every point
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})
, we have a commutative diagram of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(p^{*}M^{\prime},x^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,p(x^{\prime}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(p^{*}M,x^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(M,p(x^{\prime})),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

whence an isomorphism

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(p^{*}M,x^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,p(x^{\prime})).

The latter fits into the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}((x^{\prime})^{\dagger}p^{*}M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(p^{*}M,x^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(p(x^{\prime})^{\dagger}M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,p(x^{\prime})),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

in which the left-most vertical arrow is induced by the isomorphism of motives
(x^{\prime})^{\dagger}p^{*}M=p(x^{\prime})^{\dagger}M
.
Thus, the upper horizontal arrow is an isomorphism if and only if so is the lower horizontal arrow.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

The conclusion of
3.6
holds, more generally, if
p\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is a dominant morphism such that the induced function field extension
\mathbb{C}(X^{\prime})/\mathbb{C}(X)
is geometrically integral.
For this, one needs the analogue of
2.11
for the extension
\mathbb{C}(X^{\prime})/\mathbb{C}(X)
.
One way to obtain this is by applying the usual version of
2.11
with respect to a choice of compatible complex embeddings of
\mathbb{C}(X)
and
\mathbb{C}(X^{\prime})
.
This is legitimate, because the categories of Nori motives and their functoriality ultimately depend only on the scheme and not on similar auxiliary choices (by
[
IM24
, Prop. 6.11]
and
[
Ter24
, Rmk. 2.7(1), Rmk. 3.5(1)]
).

Proof of
3.5
.

Fix a countable, algebraically closed subfield
k\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
X
admits a model
X_{k}
over
k
and
M
is isomorphic to the image of an object
M_{k}\in\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})
under the base-change functor
\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})\to\mathcal{ML}(X_{k}\times_{k}\mathbb{C})=\mathcal{ML}(X)
:
the existence of such a field is granted by
1.13
, applied to the filtered poset of countable subfields of
\mathbb{C}
.
From now on, we tacitly identify
X
with
X_{k}\times_{k}\mathbb{C}
, and similarly for the associated complex-analytic spaces.
By
2.15
, the canonical closed immersion

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M_{k},x)

is an isomorphism for every
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
.
We have to show that
\mathsf{EL}(M)
is a union of subsets of the form
S(\mathbb{C})
, where
S=S_{k}\times_{k}\mathbb{C}
with
S_{k}
a strict closed subvariety of
X_{k}
;
if this is the case, it is automatically a countable union (because
k
is countable, by assumption).

In the first place, we claim that every point
z\in X(\mathbb{C})
which is dense for the Zariski topology of
X_{k}
lies outside
\mathsf{EL}(M)
.
To this end, let
\Sigma_{z}\colon k(X_{k})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
denote the corresponding complex embedding (as in
2.8
), and choose a complex embedding
\overline{\Sigma}_{z}\colon\overline{k(X_{k})}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
extending
\Sigma_{z}
.
By construction, we have a commutative diagram of monoidal exact functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}(k(X_{k}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma_{z}}(k(X_{k}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\overline{\Sigma}_{z}}(\overline{k(X_{k})}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{X_{k}}^{\dagger}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces z^{\dagger}}

compatible with the chosen fibre functors.
Let
N\in\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma_{z}}(k(X_{k}))
denote the object corresponding to the generic motivic local system
\eta_{X_{k}}^{\dagger}M_{k}\in\mathcal{ML}(k(X_{k}))
under
2.7
, and write
\overline{N}
for its image in
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{\Sigma}_{z}}(\overline{k(X_{k})})
.
Under Tannaka duality, we obtain the commutative diagram of algebraic groups

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M_{k},z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(z^{\dagger}M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\eta_{X_{k}}^{\dagger}M_{k};z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\Sigma_{z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\overline{N};\overline{\Sigma}_{z}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

Here, the right-most vertical arrow is an isomorphism by
2.15
.
The three isomorphisms in the left part of the diagram (given by
2.15
,
2.5
, and
2.9
) respect the Artin quotients of the motivic Galois groups.
As a consequence, the closed immersion
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(z^{\dagger}M)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,z)
is an isomorphism if and only if the sequence

1\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\overline{N};\overline{\Sigma}_{z})\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\Sigma_{z})\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(N;\Sigma_{z})\to 1

is exact, which is indeed the case (by
1.10
).
This proves the claim.

It follows that
\mathsf{EL}(M)
is contained inside the union of all subsets of
X(\mathbb{C})
of the form
S(\mathbb{C})
, where
S=S_{k}\times_{k}\mathbb{C}
with
S_{k}
a strict closed subvariety of
X_{k}
.
It remains to show that
\mathsf{EL}(M)
equals the union of some of these subsets
S(\mathbb{C})
.
To this end, fix an irreducible, strict closed subvariety
S_{k}\subset X_{k}
, set
S:=S_{k}\times_{k}\mathbb{C}
, and choose a point
t\in S(\mathbb{C})
which is dense for the Zariski topology of
S_{k}
.
We are going to show that
S(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathsf{EL}(M)
as soon as
t\in\mathsf{EL}(M)
;
since every point of
X(\mathbb{C})
is Zariski-dense inside a unique irreducible closed subvariety of
X_{k}
, this will conclude the proof.

Suppose first that
S_{k}
is smooth over
k
, and let
i\colon S\hookrightarrow X
denote the associated closed immersion.
Then, the homomorphism
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(t^{\dagger}M)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,t)
factors as

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(t^{\dagger}M)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(i^{\dagger}M,t)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,t).

But the first arrow is already known to be an isomorphism (thanks to the above claim, applied to the object
i^{\dagger}M\in\mathcal{ML}(S)
with respect to the point
t
).
Hence, we have
t\in\mathsf{EL}(M)
if and only if the closed immersion

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(i^{\dagger}M,t)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,t)

is not an isomorphism.
If this is the case, the same conclusion holds for every point
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
, which in turn implies that
s\in\mathsf{EL}(M)
, as wanted.

If
S_{k}
is not smooth, let
Z_{k}
denote its singular locus.
By Hironaka’s work on Embedded Resolution of Singularities
[
Hir64
]
, we can find a proper birational morphism
p\colon X^{\prime}_{k}\to X_{k}
from a smooth
k
-variety
X^{\prime}_{k}
such that the Zariski-closure
S^{\prime}_{k}
of
p^{-1}(S_{k}\setminus Z_{k})
inside
X^{\prime}_{k}
is smooth and the induced morphism
S^{\prime}_{k}\to S_{k}
restricts to an isomorphism over
S_{k}\setminus Z_{k}
.
As usual, set
Z:=Z_{k}\times_{k}\mathbb{C}
,
X^{\prime}:=X^{\prime}_{k}\times_{k}\mathbb{C}
, and
S^{\prime}:=S^{\prime}_{k}\times_{k}\mathbb{C}
.
Note that the point
t
lies outside
Z(\mathbb{C})
(being a generic point of
S_{k}
), and so it has a unique pre-image
t^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})
, which is dense for the Zariski topology of
S^{\prime}_{k}
(by construction).
Then, we have the chain of equivalences

t\in\mathsf{EL}(M)\iff t^{\prime}\in\mathsf{EL}(p^{*}M)\iff S^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathsf{EL}(p^{*}M)\iff S(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathsf{EL}(M),

where the first and last passages follow from
3.6
while the middle passage follows from the smooth case.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

A motivic local system
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
is Artin if and only if the motive
x^{\dagger}M\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})
is trivial for all
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

Note that
M
is Artin if and only if
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)=1
for some (or equivalently, for all)
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
.
Hence, if
M
is Artin, we have a fortiori
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}M)=1
for all
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
.
Conversely, if
M
is not Artin, we have
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}M)\neq 1
whenever
x
lies outside
\mathsf{EL}(M)
.
Since
\mathsf{EL}(M)\neq X(\mathbb{C})
by
3.5
, this concludes the proof.
∎

Before moving on, let us note the following useful fact:

Corollary 3.9
.

If
S
is an irreducible, strict closed subvariety of
X
such that
\mathsf{EL}(M)\supset U(\mathbb{C})
for some non-empty Zariski open
U\subset S
, then
\mathsf{EL}(M)\supset S(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

The result is trivial if
M
is Artin or if
\dim(S)=0
.
So suppose that
M
is not Artin and
\dim(S)\geq 1
.
By
3.5
, we can write

\mathsf{EL}(M)=\bigcup_{i\in I}S_{i}(\mathbb{C})

with
I
a countable index set and each
S_{i}
a strict closed subvariety of
X
.
This yields the inclusion

U(\mathbb{C})\subset S(\mathbb{C})\cap\mathsf{EL}(M)=\bigcup_{i\in I}(S\cap S_{i})(\mathbb{C}),

whence the equality

U(\mathbb{C})=\bigcup_{i\in I}(U\cap S_{i})(\mathbb{C}).

Since a positive-dimensional, irreducible complex variety cannot be contained into a countable union of strict closed subvarieties, we must have
U\subset S_{i}
for some
i\in I
.
But then, we must have
S\subset S_{i}
as well (since
S_{i}
is closed).
∎

4.
The weight-splitting locus

Following the approach of the previous section, we study the splitting locus of the weight filtration on motivic local systems over complex varieties.
Our main results generalize to the splitting locus of a short exact sequence of motivic local systems.

4.1.
Recollections on the motivic weight filtration

We start by reviewing the theory of motivic weights, following mostly
[
IM24
, § 6]
.
This is modelled on the analogous theory for mixed
\ell
-adic complexes (see
[

]
and
[

To fix notation, let

be a field of characteristic

endowed with a complex embedding

, and let

be a

-variety.
The abelian category
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
, defined in terms of the Betti realization (as at the beginning of

), admits an alternative presentation in terms of the
\ell
-adic realization of Voevodsky motivic sheaves (by
[

, Prop. 6.11]
).
This determines a canonical faithful exact functor

-adic perverse sheaves (see
[

).
Recall that every object

is endowed with a canonical weight filtration

uniquely characterized by the property that

(resp.

) is of weight at most
a
(resp. at least
a
) for all

.

Notation 4.1
.

For every

, we let

(resp.

spanned by the objects

(resp. at least

).
Moreover, we let

Proposition 4.2

(

[

, Prop. 6.17]
)
.

Every object

uniquely characterized by the property that

and

Every morphism in

is strictly compatible with the weight filtrations.

The whole difficulty in the proof is to show the existence of some filtration as in the statement for every given object

Strict compatibility with respect to a morphism

means that, for every

, the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{2}}

is Cartesian, and follows from the analogous property for the weight filtration of mixed perverse sheaves.

are in direct sum inside

(

For every

Proof.

Indeed,

k
.
Since the abelian subcategory
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\subset\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
is stable under subquotients, it contains all terms of the weight filtration of its objects, as well as all of their graded pieces.

Notation 4.6
.

For every
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, we let
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\leq a}(X)
denote the intersection
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\cap\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}^{w\leq a}(X)
, and similarly for
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\geq a}(X)
and
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w=a}(X)
.
Moreover, we let
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{pure}}(X)
denote the intersection
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\cap\bigoplus_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}^{w=a}(X)=\bigoplus_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w=a}(X)
.

Example 4.7
.

The shifted unit object
{{}^{p}\mathbb{Q}}_{X}\in\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
is pure of weight
\dim(X)
:
this corresponds to the fact that the ordinary unit object
\mathbb{Q}_{X}\in D^{b}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X))
is pure of weight
0
.
It follows that, for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, the duality endofunctor of
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
exchanges
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\leq a+\dim(X)}(X)
with
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\geq-a+\dim(X)}(X)
(as a consequence of
[
BBD82
, § 5.1.14(ii*)]
).

By general Tannakian formalism, the abelian subcategory of semi-simple objects inside a neutral Tannakian category in characteristic
0
is in fact a Tannakian subcategory.
In the case of motivic local systems, we have:

Lemma 4.8
.

The Tannakian subcategory of semi-simple objects inside
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
equals
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{pure}}(X)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
is stable under subquotients inside
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)
, it follows from
4.4
that an object
M\in\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
is semi-simple if and only if it belongs to
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{pure}}(X)
.

One can also check directly that
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{pure}}(X)
is stable under the shifted tensor product on
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
.
Indeed, for all
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
, the shifted tensor product restricts to

-\otimes^{\dagger}-\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\leq a+\dim(X)}(X)\times\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\leq b+\dim(X)}(X)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\leq a+b+\dim(X)}(X)

(by
[
BBD82
, § 5.1.14(ii)]
).
Dually, it restricts to

-\otimes^{\dagger}-\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\geq a+\dim(X)}(X)\times\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\geq b+\dim(X)}(X)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\geq a+b+\dim(X)}(X),

(see
4.7
), and therefore to

-\otimes^{\dagger}-\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w=a+\dim(X)}(X)\times\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w=b+\dim(X)}(X)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w=a+b+\dim(X)}(X).

∎

Notation 4.9
.

For every point
x\in X^{\sigma}
, we let
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{pure}}(X,x)
denote the Tannaka dual of
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{pure}}(X)
with respect to the fibre functor at
x
.

Under Tannaka duality, the inclusion
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{pure}}(X)\subset\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
determines a surjective homomorphism

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{pure}}(X,x;\sigma),

which identifies
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{pure}}(X,x;\sigma)
with the maximal pro-reductive quotient of
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)
.
An object
M\in\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
is semi-simple if and only if the entire category
\langle M\rangle^{\otimes}
is semi-simple.
By Tannaka duality, this happens if and only if the group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x;\sigma)
is reductive for some (or, equivalently, for all)
x\in X^{\sigma}
.

Note that, since the formation of pro-reductive quotients is not functorial with respect to arbitrary homomorphisms of pro-algebraic groups, a general monoidal functor between neutral Tannakian categories needs not send semi-simple objects to semi-simple objects.
In the motivic setting, however, we have the following positive result:

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism between smooth, geometrically connected
k
-varieties.
Then, the monoidal functor
f^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(Y)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
restricts to

f^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{pure}}(Y)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{pure}}(X).

Proof.

Indeed, for every
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, the shifted inverse image functor restricts to

f^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\leq a+\dim(Y)}(Y)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\leq a+\dim(X)}(X)

(by
[
BBD82
, § 5.1.14(i)]
).
Dually, it restricts to

f^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\geq a+\dim(Y)}(Y)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w\geq a+\dim(X)}(X),

(see
4.7
), and therefore to

f^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w=a+\dim(Y)}(Y)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{w=a+\dim(X)}(X).

∎

In particular, for every point
x\in X^{\sigma}
, we have a commutative diagram of pro-algebraic groups

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(Y,f(x);\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{pure}}(X,x;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{pure}}(Y,f(x);\sigma).}

Remark 4.11
.

Suppose that we are given a complex embedding
\Sigma\colon k(X)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
extending
\sigma
.
Then, the equivalence
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))=\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(k(X))
of
2.7
is compatible with weights (as these are defined in terms of the absolute
\ell
-adic realization).

4.2.
Definition and main properties

We want to study the properties of the weight filtration on motivic local systems over complex varieties.
As in
Section

3
, we omit any mention of complex embeddings from the notation,
and we identify the complex-analytic space underlying a given complex variety with the set of its complex points.

In the following, we let
X
be a smooth, connected complex variety.

Definition 4.12
.

We define the
weight-splitting locus
of
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
as the subset
\mathsf{WL}(M)\subset X(\mathbb{C})
consisting of all points
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
such that the motive
x^{\dagger}M\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})
is semi-simple.

The terminology is justified by the above discussion:
a point
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
lies in
\mathsf{WL}(M)
if and only if the weight filtration of
x^{\dagger}M
splits.
Since semi-simple motives form a Tannakian subcategory (see
4.8
), the object
x^{\dagger}M
is semi-simple if and only if the entire category
\langle x^{\dagger}M\rangle^{\otimes}
is semi-simple.
By Tannaka duality, this happens if and only if the motivic Galois group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}M)
is reductive.

Note that we trivially have
\mathsf{WL}(M)=X(\mathbb{C})
as soon as
M
is semi-simple (by
4.10
).
The converse implication holds as well, as a consequence of the following result:

Theorem 4.13
.

For every
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
which is not semi-simple, the locus
\mathsf{WL}(M)
is a countable union of subsets of
\mathsf{EL}(M)
the form
S(\mathbb{C})
, with
S
a strict closed algebraic subvariety of
X
.
Moreover, if
X
admits a model
X_{k}
over an algebraically closed subfield
k\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
M
belongs to the essential image of
\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})
, then each maximal closed subvariety inside
\mathsf{WL}(M)
is defined over
k
as well.

In particular,
M
is semi-simple as soon as so are its stalks at all points of some non-empty analytic open subset of
X(\mathbb{C})
.
Note the close resemblance to the statement of
3.5
.
The starting point is the following semi-simplicity criterion, which we are going to apply to motives over function fields:

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
endowed with a complex embedding
\Sigma\colon K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
;
let
\overline{K}
be an algebraic closure of
K
, and fix a complex embedding
\overline{\Sigma}\colon\overline{K}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
extending
\Sigma
.
Then, a short exact sequence in
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(K)

(14)

0\to N_{1}\to N\to N_{2}\to 0

splits if and only if so does its image in
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{\Sigma}}(\overline{K})

(15)

0\to\overline{N}_{1}\to\overline{N}\to\overline{N}_{2}\to 0.

Proof.

It is obvious that (
15
) splits if so does (
14
), so we only have to show the converse implication.
To this end, let
K^{\prime}/K
be the finite Galois extension with Galois group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(N;\Sigma)
, write
\Sigma^{\prime}:=\overline{\Sigma}|_{K^{\prime}}
, and let

0\to N^{\prime}_{1}\to N^{\prime}\to N^{\prime}_{2}\to 0

denote the image of (
14
) in
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K^{\prime})
.
Since the base-change functor
\langle N^{\prime}\rangle^{\otimes}\to\langle\overline{N}\rangle^{\otimes}
is an equivalence (by
1.12
), the sequence above splits as soon as (
15
) splits.
We have to deduce that, in this case, the sequence (
14
) splits as well.
To this end, note that the base-change functor
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(K)\to\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K^{\prime})
factors through an equivalence

\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(K)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\textup{Spec}(K^{\prime}))^{\textup{Gal}(K^{\prime}/K)}=\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K^{\prime})^{\textup{Gal}(K^{\prime}/K)},

obtained from
1.15
and
1.16
.
Now fix a section
\phi\colon N^{\prime}_{2}\to N^{\prime}
to the epimorphism
N^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow N^{\prime}_{2}
.
By construction, the morphism in
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\textup{Spec}(K^{\prime}))

[K^{\prime}\colon K]^{-1}\sum_{g\in\textup{Gal}(K^{\prime}/K)}g\cdot\phi\colon N^{\prime}_{2}\to N^{\prime}

is a
\textup{Gal}(K^{\prime}/K)
-invariant section, which thus descends to a section
N_{2}\to N
to the original epimorphism
N\twoheadrightarrow N_{2}
.
∎

Corollary 4.15
.

Keep the notation and assumptions of
4.14
.
Then, an object
N\in\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(K)
is semi-simple if and only if so is its image
\overline{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{\overline{\Sigma}}(\overline{K})
.

Proof.

Let again
K^{\prime}/K
be the finite Galois extension with Galois group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\textup{Art}}(N;\Sigma)
, write
\Sigma^{\prime}:=\overline{\Sigma}|_{K^{\prime}}
, and let
N^{\prime}
denote the image of
N
in
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K^{\prime})
.
As in the proof of
4.14
, we reduce ourselves to showing that
N
is semi-simple if and only if so is
N^{\prime}
.
This amounts to showing that the weight filtration of
N
splits if and only if so does the weight filtration of
N^{\prime}
(by
4.4
).
We claim that in fact, for every
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, the short exact sequence in
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(K)

0\to W_{\leq a-1}N\to N\to W_{\geq a}N\to 0

splits if and only if so does the short exact sequence in
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(K^{\prime})

0\to W_{\leq a-1}N^{\prime}\to N^{\prime}\to W_{\geq a}N^{\prime}\to 0.

Since the second sequence is the image of the first one under the base-change functor (see
4.5
), the claim follows from the proof of
4.14
.
∎

An object
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
is semi-simple if and only if the Tannaka dual group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,x)
is reductive for some (or, equivalently, for all)
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
.
The previous results allow us to refine this criterion as follows:

Proposition 4.16
.

An object
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
is semi-simple if and only if the group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)
is reductive for some (or equivalently, for all)
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that, for a well-chosen point
z\in X(\mathbb{C})
, the group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,z)
is reductive if and only if so is its subgroup
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,z)
.
To choose such a point, we proceed as in the proof of
3.5
:
fix a countable, algebraically closed subfield
k\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
X
admits a model
X_{k}
over
k
and
M
is isomorphic to the image of an object
M_{k}\in\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})
under the base-change functor
\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})\to\mathcal{M}(X)
, and let
z\in X(\mathbb{C})
be dense for the Zariski topology of
X_{k}
.
We prove that the conclusion holds for this point.

To this end, let
\Sigma_{z}\colon k(X_{k})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
denote the complex embedding corresponding to
z
(as in
2.8
), and fix a complex embedding
\overline{\Sigma}_{z}\colon\overline{k(X_{k})}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
extending it.
Let
N\in\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma_{z}}(k(X_{k}))
denote the object corresponding to
\eta_{X_{k}}^{\dagger}M_{k}\in\mathcal{ML}(k(X_{k}))
under
2.7
, and write
\overline{N}
for its image in
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{\Sigma}_{z}}(\overline{k(X_{k})})
.
As in the proof of
3.5
, we can identify the closed immersion
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,z)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(M,z)
with the closed immersion
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\overline{N};\overline{\Sigma}_{z})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\Sigma_{z})
.
Hence, we are left to show that
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(N;\Sigma_{z})
is reductive if and only if so is
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(\overline{N};\overline{\Sigma}_{z})
.
But, under Tannaka duality, this recovers precisely the conclusion of
4.15
.
∎

This result is useful to compare the weight-splitting locus to the exceptional locus.
The proof strategy of
4.13
is essentially the same as for
3.5
.
Before getting to it, we record one more observation:

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be any morphism between smooth, connected complex varieties.
Then, for every
M\in\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(Y)
, we have

\mathsf{WL}(f^{\dagger}M)=f^{-1}\mathsf{WL}(M).

Proof.

Indeed, for every point
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
, we have a canonical isomorphism of motives
x^{\dagger}f^{\dagger}M=f(x)^{\dagger}M
.
In particular, the first one is semi-simple if and only if so is the second one.
∎

Proof of
4.13
.

As in the proof of
3.5
, fix a countable, algebraically closed subfield
k\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
X
admits a model
X_{k}
over
k
and
M
is isomorphic to the image of an object
M_{k}\in\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})
under the base-change functor
\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})\to\mathcal{ML}(X_{k}\times_{k}\mathbb{C})=\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
.
From now on, we tacitly identify
X
with
X_{k}\times_{k}\mathbb{C}
.

In the first place, we prove the inclusion
\mathsf{WL}(M)\subset\mathsf{EL}(M)
.
To this end, fix a point
x\in X(\mathbb{C})\setminus\mathsf{EL}(M)
.
By definition, the group
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(x^{\dagger}M)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{\odot}(M,x)
.
Since
M
is not semi-simple (by hypothesis), the latter group is not reductive (by
4.16
).
Under Tannaka duality, this means that
x^{\dagger}M
is not semi-simple, as wanted.

In view of
3.5
, it follows that
\mathsf{WL}(M)
is contained inside the union of all subsets of the form
S(\mathbb{C})
where
S=S_{k}\times_{k}\mathbb{C}
with
S_{k}
a strict closed subvariety of
X_{k}
contained in
\mathsf{EL}(M)
.
It remains to show that
\mathsf{WL}(M)
equals the union of some of these subsets
S(\mathbb{C})
.
To this end, fix an irreducible, strict closed subvariety
S_{k}\subset X_{k}
, set
S:=S_{k}\times_{k}\mathbb{C}
, and choose a point
t\in S(\mathbb{C})
which is dense of the Zariski topology of
S_{k}
.
It suffices to show that
S(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathsf{WL}(M)
as soon as
t\in\mathsf{WL}(S)
.

If
S_{k}
is smooth, let
i\colon S\hookrightarrow X
denote the associated closed immersion.
Since
i^{-1}(\mathsf{WL}(M))=\mathsf{WL}(i^{\dagger}M)
(by
4.17
), it suffices to show that the object
i^{\dagger}M\in\mathcal{ML}(S)
is semi-simple as soon as
t\in\mathsf{WL}(i^{\dagger}M)
.
Indeed, if
i^{\dagger}M
is not semi-simple, then we have
\mathsf{WL}(i^{\dagger}M)\subset\mathsf{EL}(i^{\dagger}M)
(by the above claim, applied to
i^{\dagger}M
), but we know that
t\notin\mathsf{EL}(i^{\dagger}M)
(by
3.5
).

If
S_{k}
is not smooth, we use Embedded Resolution of Singularities
[
Hir64
]
:
this gives us a proper birational morphism
p\colon X^{\prime}_{k}\to X_{k}
from a smooth variety
X^{\prime}_{k}
endowed with a smooth closed subvariety
S^{\prime}_{k}
such that
p
restricts to a proper birational morphism
S^{\prime}_{k}\to S_{k}
.
As usual, set
S^{\prime}:=S^{\prime}_{k}\times_{k}\mathbb{C}
, and let
t^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})
denote the unique pre-image of

.
We have the chain of equivalences

t\in\mathsf{WL}(M)\iff t^{\prime}\in\mathsf{WL}(p^{*}M)\iff S^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathsf{WL}(p^{*}M)\iff S(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathsf{WL}(M),

where the first and last passages follow from
4.17
while the middle passage follows from the smooth case.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.18
.

If
S
is an irreducible, strict closed subvariety of
X
such that
\mathsf{WL}(M)\supset U(\mathbb{C})
for some non-empty Zariski open

, then
\mathsf{WL}(M)\supset S(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

This follows directly from

, in the same way as

follows from

.
∎

Next, we discuss the stability properties of the weight-splitting locus under Galois conjugation.
For this, we restore the notation of
Section

3.3
:
we fix a subfield
k\subset\mathbb{C}
and let
\overline{k}\subset\mathbb{C}
denote its algebraic closure.
By

X
and
M
admit models over
\overline{k}
, the weight-splitting locus
\mathsf{WL}(M)
is defined over
\overline{k}
as well.
We now study what happens when these models further descend to

.

Proposition 4.19
.

Assume that
X
admits a model
X_{k}
over
k
such that
M
belongs to the essential image of
\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})
.
Then, the
\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
-action on
X
induced by the Galois action on

stabilizes the locus
\mathsf{WL}(M)
.

Proof.

We proceed as for
3.10
.
Given an element
\varphi\in\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
, let
f\colon X_{\overline{k}}\xrightarrow{\sim}X_{\overline{k}}
denote the corresponding
k

We have to prove that the permutation map on complex points

To this end, it suffices to recall that we have canonical isomorphisms of motivic Galois groups

∎

4.3.
Variant for short exact sequences

There is an alternative approach to studying the weight-splitting locus of motivic local systems, which leads to slightly more precise results.
It is based on the following notion:

Definition 4.20
.

For every
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the
weight-
a
-splitting locus
of
M\in\mathcal{ML}(X)
as the subset
\mathsf{WL}_{a}(M)\subset X(\mathbb{C})
consisting of all points
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
such that the short exact sequence

0\to W_{\leq a-1}M\to M\to W_{\geq a}M\to 0

splits.

Since the weight filtration on
M
is finite, we trivially have
\mathsf{WL}_{a}(M)=X(\mathbb{C})
for
|a|\gg 0
.
The full weight-splitting locus can be recovered as the essentially finite intersection

\mathsf{WL}(M)=\bigcap_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{WL}_{a}(M).

An alternative way to prove the main properties of the full weight-splitting locus (that is,
4.13
and
4.19
) is to prove the same properties for each
\mathsf{WL}_{a}(M)
.
More generally:

Definition 4.21
.

We define the
splitting locus
of a short exact sequence in
\mathcal{ML}(X)

\mathfrak{S}\colon 0\to M^{\prime}\to M\to M^{\prime\prime}\to 0

as the subset
\mathsf{SL}(\mathfrak{S})\subset X(\mathbb{C})
consisting of all points
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
such that the induced short exact sequence in
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})

x^{\dagger}\mathfrak{S}\colon 0\to x^{\dagger}M^{\prime}\to x^{\dagger}M\to x^{\dagger}M^{\prime\prime}\to 0

splits.

We trivially have
\mathsf{SL}(\mathfrak{S})=X(\mathbb{C})
if the sequence
\mathfrak{S}
is already split.
The converse implication holds as well, in view of the following result:

Theorem 4.22
.

For every short exact sequence in
\mathcal{ML}(X)

\mathfrak{S}\colon 0\to M^{\prime}\to M\to M^{\prime\prime}\to 0

which is not split, the locus
\mathsf{SL}(\mathfrak{S})
is a countable union of subsets of
X(\mathbb{C})
of the form
S(\mathbb{C})
, with
S
a strict closed algebraic subvariety of
X
.
Moreover, if
X
admits a model
X_{k}
over an algebraically closed subfield
k\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
M
belongs to the essential image of
\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})
, then each maximal closed subvariety inside
\mathsf{SL}(\mathfrak{S})
is defined over
k
as well.

Proof.

As in the proof of
4.13
, fix a countable algebraically closed subfield
k\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
X
admits a model
X_{k}
over
k
and
M
is isomorphic to the image of an object
M_{k}\in\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})
under the base-change functor
\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})\to\mathcal{M}(X_{k}\times_{k,\sigma}\mathbb{C})=\mathcal{M}(X)
.
Since this functor is fully faithful, with essential image stable under subquotients (by
2.11
), the entire short exact sequence
\mathfrak{S}
arises from a short exact sequence
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
in
\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})
.

With this set-up, the argument for
4.13
carries through, using directly
4.14
instead of
4.16
.
We leave the details to the interested reader.
∎

For sake of completeness, we also state the stability properties of general splitting loci under Galois conjugation.
To this end, we fix a subfield
k\subset\mathbb{C}
, and we let
\overline{k}\subset\mathbb{C}
denote its algebraic closure.

Proposition 4.23
.

Suppose that
X
admits a model
X_{k}
over
k
such that
\mathfrak{S}
belongs to the essential image of
\mathcal{ML}(X_{k})
.
Then, the
\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
-action on
X
induced by the Galois action on
X_{\overline{k}}
stabilizes the locus
\mathsf{SL}(\mathfrak{S})
.

Proof.

As in the proof of
4.19
, fix an element
\varphi\in\textup{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
, and let
f\colon X\xrightarrow{\sim}X
denote the induced scheme automorphism.
We have to prove that the permutation map on complex points
f\colon X(\mathbb{C})\xrightarrow{\sim}X(\mathbb{C})
stabilizes the subset
\mathsf{SL}(\mathfrak{S})
.

To this end, it suffices to note that we have a canonical isomorphisms of short exact sequences in
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})

f(x)^{\dagger}\mathfrak{S}=x^{\dagger}f^{*}\mathfrak{S}=x^{\dagger}\mathfrak{S}

for every point
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
(by the same argument as in the proof of
3.10
).
This implies that
f(x)\in\mathsf{SL}(\mathfrak{S})
if and only if
x\in\mathsf{SL}(\mathfrak{S})
, which concludes the proof.
∎

5.
Applications to
1
-motivic variations

In this last section, we apply our motivic results to study variations of Hodge structure underlying
1
-motivic local systems.
Before stating these results, we review the Hodge realization of Nori motivic sheaves, focusing on the case of motivic local systems.
Our applications ultimately follow from the properties of the Hodge realization of Nori
1
-motives over an algebraically closed field.

Throughout, we let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
endowed with a complex embedding
\sigma\colon k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

5.1.
The Hodge realization of Nori motivic sheaves

We begin by discussing the Hodge realization of Nori motivic sheaves and its conjectural properties.

For every
k
-variety
X
, we let
\mathsf{MHM}_{\sigma}(X)
denote Saito’s abelian category of
(algebraic) mixed Hodge modules
over
X
, as defined in
[
Sai90
]
.
It comes equipped with a canonical forgetful functor

\mathsf{rat}_{X}\colon\mathsf{MHM}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma}(X).

As
X
varies among
k
-varieties, the derived categories
D^{b}(\mathsf{MHM}_{\sigma}(X))
assemble into a six-functor formalism, in such a way that the triangulated functors

D^{b}(\mathsf{MHM}_{\sigma}(X))\xrightarrow{\mathsf{rat}_{X}}D^{b}(\mathsf{Perv}_{\sigma}(X))\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}_{\sigma}(X)

commute with the six operations (see
[
Sai90
, Thm. 0.1]
).

When
X=\textup{Spec}(k)
, the abelian category
\mathsf{MHM}_{\sigma}(\textup{Spec}(k))
equals Deligne’s category of graded-polarizable mixed Hodge structures (introduced in
[
Del71b
, § 2.3]
), which we write here simply as
\mathsf{MHS}
:
it is neutral Tannakian over
\mathbb{Q}
, with the forgetful functor

\mathsf{rat}_{k}\colon\mathsf{MHS}\to\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}

providing a canonical fibre functor (see
[
PS08
, § 3.1]
).

Notation 5.1
.

We let
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{Hdg}}
denote the Tannaka dual of
\mathsf{MHS}
with respect to the fibre functor
\mathsf{rat}_{k}
, and we call it the
Hodge group
.

By Deligne’s classical work
[
Del71b
,
Del74
]
, the Betti cohomology of
k
-varieties factors naturally through
\mathsf{MHS}
.
By the universal property of Nori motives, this determines a canonical faithful exact monoidal functor

\iota_{k,\sigma}^{\textup{Hdg}}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)\to\mathsf{MHS}

such that the forgetful functor
\iota_{k,\sigma}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)\to\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}
factors as

\iota_{k,\sigma}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)\xrightarrow{\iota_{k,\sigma}^{\textup{Hdg}}}\mathsf{MHS}\xrightarrow{\mathsf{rat}_{k}}\mathsf{vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}

up to monoidal natural isomorphism.
This determines a canonical homomorphism between Tannaka dual groups

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{Hdg}}\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma).

Tubach’s recent work
[
Tub25a
]
extends the Hodge realization to general
k
-varieties:

Theorem 5.2

(Tubach,
[
Tub25a
, Cor. 4.8]
)
.

For every
k
-variety
X
, there exists a canonical
t
-exact Hodge realization functor

\iota_{\sigma,X}^{\textup{Hdg}}\colon D^{b}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X))\to D^{b}(\mathsf{MHM}_{\sigma}(X))

such that the forgetful functor
\iota_{X,\sigma}\colon D^{b}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X))\to D^{b}_{\sigma}(X)
factors as

\iota_{X,\sigma}\colon D^{b}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X))\xrightarrow{\iota_{X,\sigma}^{\textup{Hdg}}}D^{b}(\mathsf{MHM}_{\sigma}(X))\xrightarrow{\mathsf{rat}_{X}}D^{b}_{\sigma}(X)

up to natural isomorphism.
As
X
varies, the functors
\iota_{\sigma,X}^{\textup{Hdg}}
are compatible with the six operations.

Suppose now that
X
is smooth and geometrically connected over
k
, so that the complex-analytic space
X^{\sigma}
is smooth and connected.
In this case, the objects of
\mathsf{MHM}_{\sigma}(X)
whose underlying perverse sheaf is a local system span the abelian subcategory
\mathsf{VHS}_{\sigma}(X)
of
admissible, graded-polarizable variations of mixed Hodge structure
:
endowed with the shifted tensor product, it is neutral Tannakian over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Notation 5.3
.

For every
x\in X^{\sigma}
, we let
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{Hdg}}(X^{\sigma},x)
denote the Tannaka dual of
\mathsf{VHS}_{\sigma}(X)
with respect to the fibre functor at
x
, and we call it the
Hodge group of
X
with base-point
x
.

The Hodge realization of Nori motivic sheaves restricts to a monoidal exact functor

(16)

\iota_{X,\sigma}^{\textup{Hdg}}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathsf{VHS}_{\sigma}(X).

For every point
x\in X^{\sigma}
, this determines a homomorphism between Tannaka dual groups

(17)

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{Hdg}}(X^{\sigma},x)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma).

We have the following Hodge-type conjecture for Nori motivic local systems:

Conjecture 5.4
.

Assume that
k
is algebraically closed.
Then, for every smooth, connected
k
-variety
X
, the Hodge realization functor (
16
) is fully faithful, with essential image stable under subquotients.

Under Tannaka duality, this is equivalent to saying that the homomorphism (
17
) is surjective for one (or equivalently, for all)
x\in X^{\sigma}
(by
[
DM82
, Prop. 2.21(a)]
).

When
k=\mathbb{C}
,
5.4
is stated by Arapura in
[
Ara13
, Conj. 7.2.1]
, using his own notion of Nori motivic local system – which is likely to be very close to ours, although such a comparison has not been worked out.
It is worth noting that the validity of
5.4
over
\textup{Spec}(k)
implies its validity over any smooth connected base.
This follows by comparing the motivic fundamental short exact sequence (see
1.24
) with the following Hodge-theoretic analogue:

Theorem 5.5

(
[
DAE22
, Thm. 4.4]
;
[
Jac26
, Thm. 7.2(1)]
)
.

For every
x\in X^{\sigma}
, the canonical sequence of pro-algebraic groups

\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X^{\sigma},x)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{Hdg}}(X^{\sigma},x)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{Hdg}}\to 1

is split-exact.

As in the motivic setting, the two homomorphisms in the sequence are induced by the monoidal functors
\mathsf{rat}_{X}\colon\mathsf{VHS}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathsf{Loc}_{\sigma}(X)
and
a_{X}^{\dagger}\colon\mathsf{MHS}\to\mathsf{VHS}_{\sigma}(X)
, respectively.

Corollary 5.6
.

For every
x\in X^{\sigma}
, we have a commutative diagram of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X^{\sigma},x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{Hdg}}(X^{\sigma},x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{Hdg}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X^{\sigma},x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1,}

in which both rows are split-exact.

Proof.

The commutativity of the left-most square follows from the compatibility of the Hodge realization with the forgetful functors, while the commutativity of the right-most square follows from its compatibility with the six operations (both of which are granted by
5.2
).
∎

Unfortunately, the full
5.4
is widely out of reach of current techniques, even over
X=\textup{Spec}(k)
.
As explained in the next subsection, the conjecture is known unconditionally in the case of
1
-motivic local systems.

Before moving on, let us mention one last compatibility result:

Proposition 5.7

(
[
Tub25a
, Prop. 4.9]
)
.

For every
k
-variety
X
, the functor
\iota_{X,\sigma}^{\textup{Hdg}}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathsf{MHM}(X^{\sigma})
is compatible with the weight filtrations.

Using the fact that weights on Nori motivic sheaves can be defined via a weight structure (in the sense of Bondarko’s work
[
Bon14
]
), the proof reduces to the compatibility with the six operations (as stated in
5.2
).
By restriction to motivic local systems, we deduce:

Corollary 5.8
.

For every smooth, geometrically connected
k
-variety
X
, the Hodge realization functor restricts to

\iota_{\sigma,X}^{\textup{Hdg}}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{\textup{pure}}(X)\to\mathsf{VHS}_{\sigma}^{\textup{pure}}(X).

Here,
\mathsf{VHS}_{\sigma}^{\textup{pure}}(X)
denotes the Tannakian subcategory of
\mathsf{VHS}_{\sigma}(X)
spanned by the objects with split weight filtration:
it is precisely the subcategory of semi-simple objects in
\mathsf{VHS}_{\sigma}(X)
(because all variations in
\mathsf{VHS}_{\sigma}(X)
are graded-polarizable).

5.2.
Recollections on
1
-motivic local systems

Recall that a
1
-motive
over
k
is a
2
-term complex of
k
-group schemes of the form
[L\to G]
, with
L
an étale lattice and
G
a semi-abelian variety.
Using the dual version of Deligne’s construction
[
Del74
, § 10.1]
, to any
1
-motive one can attach a rational mixed Hodge structure of type
\left\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\right\}
.
The functor into mixed Hodge structures defined in this way is fully faithful whenever
k
is algebraically closed, and even an equivalence when
k=\mathbb{C}
.
This functor factors canonically through
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(k)
.

Notation 5.9
.

We let
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}^{1}(k)
denote the Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{M}(k)
generated by the image of the
1
-motives.
We write
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(k;\sigma)
for its Tannaka dual group with respect to the fibre functor
\iota_{k,\sigma}
.

For motives over
k
, the best known result in the direction of
5.4
is the following:

Theorem 5.10

(
[
And21
, § 1.2.1]
)
.

Suppose that
k
is algebraically closed.
Then, the functor

\iota_{\sigma,k}^{\textup{Hdg}}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\sigma}(k)\to\mathsf{MHS}

is fully faithful, with essential image stable under subquotients.

Under Tannaka duality, this is equivalent to saying that the composite homomorphism

\mathcal{G}_{\textup{Hdg}}\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(k;\sigma)

is surjective (by
[
DM82
, Prop. 2.21(a)]
).

The notion of
1
-motive makes sense over general bases.
For convenience, from now henceforth we assume that the field extension
\mathbb{C}/k
defined by
\sigma
has infinite transcendence degree;
this happens, for instance, when
k
is an algebraic extension of some finitely generated field.
In this way, we can always embed function fields over
k
into
\mathbb{C}
, which allows for a self-contained treatment of
1
-motives over
k
-varieties.

Let
X
be a smooth, geometrically connected
k
-variety, with function field
k(X)
, and fix a complex embedding
\Sigma\colon k(X)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
extending
\sigma
.
Recall the monoidal equivalence
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))=\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(k(X))
given by
2.7
.
Using suitable spreading-out arguments, one sees that the strictly full Tannakian subcategory
\mathcal{ML}^{1}_{\sigma}(k(X))\subset\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))
corresponding to
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\Sigma}(k(X))
is independent of the choice of
\Sigma
.

Notation 5.11
.

We let
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{1}(X)
denote the inverse image of
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{1}(k(X))
under the functor
\eta_{X}^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X))
:
it is a Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
.
For every
x\in X^{\sigma}
, we write
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(X,x;\sigma)
for its Tannaka dual group with respect to the fibre functor at
x
.

Example 5.12
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a pure Shimura datum (in the sense of
[
Del71a
, § 1.5]
), and let
K\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
be a neat open compact subgroup of the adelic points.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{C}
denote the reflex field of the associated pure Shimura variety, and let
S_{K}
denote its canonical model:
it is a smooth, geometrically connected
E
-variety.
Classically, the
canonical construction
is a monoidal exact functor

\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)\to\mathsf{VHS}_{\sigma}(S_{K}),

where
\sigma\colon E\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
denotes the inclusion.
If
(G,\mathcal{H})
is a PEL Shimura datum, this functor can be promoted to the level of relative Chow motives over
S_{K}
, thanks to Ancona’s
[
Anc15
, Thm. 8.6]
.
Composing this with the Nori realization of Voevodsky motivic sheaves (as defined in
[
Tub25a
, Thm. 4.5]
or
[
JT25
, Thm. 6.3]
), we obtain a monoidal exact functor

\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(S_{K}),

which takes values in
\mathcal{ML}^{1}_{\sigma}(S_{K})
:
indeed, the regular representation of
G
is sent to the relative first cohomology of the universal abelian scheme.
As shown in
[
Tub25b
, Prop. 5.2.14]
, this can be generalized to pure Shimura varieties of Hodge type.
∎

Let us note the following stability result:

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a dominant morphism between smooth, geometrically connected
k
-varieties.
Then, the shifted inverse image functor
f^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(Y)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)
restricts to a monoidal exact functor

f^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{1}(Y)\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{1}(X).

Proof.

Consider the monoidal functor

f^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(Y))\to\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X)),

which is well-defined (as
f
is dominant, by assumption).
It sends the Nori
1
-motive over
k(Y)
associated to a given complex
[L\to G]
to the Nori
1
-motive over
k(X)
associated to the base-changed complex
[L_{k(X)}\to G_{k(X)}]
.
This implies that it restricts to a monoidal functor

f^{\dagger}\colon\mathcal{ML}^{1}_{\sigma}(k(Y))\to\mathcal{ML}^{1}_{\sigma}(k(X)).

Taking the inverse images of the two sides in the upper row of the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(Y))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(k(X)),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\dagger}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{Y}^{\dagger}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{X}^{\dagger}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\dagger}}

we obtain the conclusion.
∎

Corollary 5.14
.

Keep the assumptions and notation of
5.13
.
Then, for every
x\in X^{\sigma}
, we have a commutative diagram of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(Y,f(x);\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(X,x;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(Y,f(x);\sigma).}

Proof.

This is dual to the commutative diagram of neutralized Tannakian categories

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}^{1}_{\sigma}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{ML}^{1}_{\sigma}(Y),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\dagger}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\dagger}}

given by
5.13
.
∎

To proceed further, we observe that the Jacobsen’s fundamental sequence can be adapted to
1
-motivic local systems:

Proposition 5.15
.

For every
x\in X(k)
, the sequence of pro-algebraic groups

\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X,x;\sigma)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(X,x;\sigma)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(k;\sigma)\to 1

is split-exact.

Proof.

We have a commutative diagram of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X,x;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X,x;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(X,x;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(k;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1,}

where the upper row is already known to be split-exact (by
1.24
) and all vertical arrows are surjective.
Since the splitting
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(k;\sigma)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}(X,x;\sigma)
is defined by the inclusion of

, it induces a splitting
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(k;\sigma)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(X,x;\sigma)
(by

, applied to the structural morphism

).
This implies at once that the arrow
\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(X,x;\sigma)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(k;\sigma)
is surjective and that its kernel coincides with the image of the arrow
\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X,x;\sigma)\to\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(X,x;\sigma)
.
∎

This allows us to extend André’s
5.10
to motivic local systems, as follows:

Theorem 5.16

(Tubach,
[
Tub25b
, Thm. 5.2.12]
)
.

Suppose that

is algebraically closed.
Then, the Hodge realization functor

\iota_{\sigma,X}^{\textup{Hdg}}\colon\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{1}(X)\to\mathsf{VHS}_{\sigma}(X)

is fully faithful, with essential image stable under subquotients.

Proof.

This is equivalent to saying that the composite homomorphism of Tannaka dual groups

is surjective for some (or equivalently, for all)

(by
[

, Prop. 2.21(a)]
).
But the latter fits into the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X^{\sigma},x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{Hdg}}(X^{\sigma},x;)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{Hdg}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}^{\textup{alg}}(X,x;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(X,x;\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\textup{mot}}^{1}(k)}

by restriction to

-motivic local systems.

and by

, respectively), and the right-most vertical arrow is surjective (as a consequence of

), the middle vertical arrow must be surjective as well.

.

Indeed,

yields an equivalence of neutral Tannakian categories

Corollary 5.18
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{ML}_{\sigma}^{1}(X)
, and write

.

, we have a commutative diagram of the form

, regardless of whether

.

5.3.
Results about loci

As in the previous subsection, we assume that the field extension

\overline{k}

In the following, fix a smooth, geometrically connected

In view of

3.5

.

Theorem 5.20
.

Let

.
Then:

(1)

The Hodge-theoretic weight-splitting locus
