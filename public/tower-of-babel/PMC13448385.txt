TLSB-13 PROGNOSTIC VALUE OF CEREBROSPINAL FLUID CIRCULATING TUMOR DNA IN NSCLC PATIENTS WITH CNS METASTASES: A SYSTEMATIC REVIEW AND META-ANALYSIS
Authors: Ermilo Echeverria-Ortegon, Maria Valentina Villafaña-Rodriguez, Enrique Rivero-Medina, Gabriela Cerón-Azueta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe incidence of central nervous system (CNS) metastases, including brain metastases (BMs) and leptomeningeal metastases (LMs), is increasing among patients with non–small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and management of these complications remains challenging due to highly heterogeneous clinical outcomes, frequent CNS progression, and early mortality. Understanding the molecular characteristics and prognostic biomarkers of CNS metastases is limited by the risks and technical challenges associated with brain tumor sampling. Liquid biopsy approaches have emerged as promising alternatives, and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) has demonstrated superior performance compared with plasma for molecular profiling of CNS tumors and diagnosis of leptomeningeal disease. However, the prognostic significance of CSF ctDNA detection in NSCLC patients with CNS metastases has not been systematically synthesized. A systematic literature search identified studies reporting hazard ratios (HRs) for survival outcomes according to CSF ctDNA detection status. Data was extracted and pooled using a random-effects model. Three studies comprising approximately 700 patients with NSCLC and CNS metastases met inclusion criteria. Across all included studies, detection of CSF ctDNA was consistently associated with worse survival outcomes compared with negative CSF ctDNA status. Pooled analysis demonstrated that patients with detectable CSF ctDNA had significantly increased risk of mortality or progression (HR 2.59, 95% CI 1.47–4.56; p = 0.001). Moderate heterogeneity was observed among studies (I² = 61.5%). These findings suggest that CSF ctDNA detection is associated with poorer prognosis in patients with NSCLC and CNS metastases and may represent a promising biomarker for risk stratification in this population. Further prospective studies are warranted to validate the clinical utility of CSF ctDNA for prognostic assessment and to integrate liquid biopsy approaches into future CNS metastasis management strategies.
