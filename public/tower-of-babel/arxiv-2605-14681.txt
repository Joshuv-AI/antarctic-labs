Lower bound on the mixing time of p p -spin glasses
Anouar Kouraich 1 and Simone Warzel 1,2,3 1 Department of Mathematics, TU Munich, Garching, Germany 2 Munich Center for Quantum Science and Technology, Munich, Germany 3 Department of Physics, TU Munich, Garching, Germany

Abstract
We show that Glauber dynamics for p p -spin glass mixes exponentially slowly at inverse temperatures larger than a constant times ln ⁡ ( p ) / p \ln(p)/p for large enough p p . This is done by analyzing the energy landscape using Gaussian decompositions and establishing a bottleneck bound.



1
Introduction

1.1
Main result

Glauber dynamics corresponding to inverse temperature
\beta
and a system’s Hamiltonian
H:\{-1,1\}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\bm{\sigma}\mapsto H(\bm{\sigma})
on the configuration space of
N
Ising spins is an irreducible, aperiodic Markov chain
designed such that the Gibbs distribution

\pi(\bm{\sigma})\coloneqq Z_{N}(\beta)^{-1}\exp\left(-\beta H(\bm{\sigma})\right),\quad Z_{N}(\beta)\coloneqq\sum_{\bm{\sigma}\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}\exp\left(-\beta H(\bm{\sigma})\right),

(1.1)

is its unique stationary distribution. For Glauber dynamics, the transition matrix
P(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})
characterizing the chain
has the property

P(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})=0\quad\mbox{unless $\displaystyle d(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{|\sigma_{j}-\tau_{j}|}{2}\leq 1$, }

(1.2)

reflecting the fact that
in each time step, one flips at most one spin such that the Hamming distance
d(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})
between the initial configuration
\bm{\sigma}
and the final one
\bm{\tau}
is at most
1
.
The specific choice
P(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})=N^{-1}\left(1+\exp\left[\beta(H(\bm{\tau})-H(\bm{\sigma}))\right]\right)^{-1}
in case
d(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})=1
and accordingly
P(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\sigma})=1-\sum_{\bm{\tau}:\ d(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})=1}P(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})
corresponds to the standard Glauber Markov chain for a mean-field Hamiltonian such as the the Ising spin glass with
p
-spin interaction

H(\bm{\sigma})\coloneqq-\frac{1}{N^{(p-1)/2}}\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{p}=1}^{N}g_{j_{1},\dots,j_{p}}\sigma_{j_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{j_{p}}.

(1.3)

The latter is given in terms of independent and identically distributed standard normal variables
g_{j_{1},\dots,j_{p}}
. The energies hence form a
Gaussian random process on
\{-1,1\}^{N}
with mean zero and covariance,

\mathbb{E}[H(\bm{\sigma})H(\bm{\tau})]=N\left(1-\frac{d(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})}{2N}\right)^{p}=:N\ c_{p}\left(\frac{d(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})}{N}\right),

(1.4)

which only depends on the Hamming distance
d
.
The special case
p=2
corresponds to the SK model
[
35
]
. Taking the limit
p\to\infty
in (
1.4
) one arrives at the simplest Ising spin glass, the random energy model (REM), in which the energies
H(\bm{\sigma})
are uncorrelated.

Spin glasses are prototypes of complex energy landscapes. One measure of their complexity is the time it takes to prepare their Gibbs distribution via Glauber dynamics. For arbitrary initial probability distribution
\mu
on
\{-1,1\}^{N}
, this time is characterized by the mixing time

t_{\textrm{mix}}\coloneqq\min\left\{t\ \Big|\ \sup_{\mu}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{\tau}}\left|\mu P^{t}(\bm{\tau})-\pi(\bm{\tau})\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}\right\},

(1.5)

where
\mu P^{t}
denotes the evolved distribution at time
t\in\mathbb{N}
. The latter is the
t
-fold convolution of the transition kernel:

\mu P^{t}(\bm{\tau})\coloneqq\sum_{\bm{\sigma}_{1}\dots\bm{\sigma}_{t}}\mu(\bm{\sigma}_{t})P(\bm{\sigma}_{t},\bm{\tau})P(\bm{\sigma}_{t-1},\bm{\sigma}_{t})\dots P(\bm{\sigma}_{1},\bm{\sigma}_{2}).

We establish lower bounds on the mixing time, summarized in the following main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider any irreducible, aperiodic Markov chain, which has the Gibbs distribution of the
p
-spin glass (
1.3
) as its stationary distribution and satisfies (
1.2
).
There are constants
c,C\in(0,\infty)
and
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
p\geq p_{0}
and all
\displaystyle\beta>C\ \frac{\ln p}{p}
:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(t_{\textrm{mix}}\geq e^{Nc}\right)=1.

(1.6)

The proof is spelled out in Section
2
.

Even more, it is widely believed that the Glauber dynamics from a random initialization mix exponentially slowly throughout this regime, but a proof remains open.

The existence of the shattering phase for large

Determining the precise value of

2.1
Deterministic bottleneck bound

The proof is based on the Jerrum-Sinclair-Diaconis-Stroock bottleneck bound
[
29
, Theorem 7.4]
:

\frac{1}{4\ t_{\textrm{mix}}}\leq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subset\{-1,1\}^{N}\\
\pi(A)\leq 2^{-1}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\pi(A)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\sigma}\in A\\
\bm{\tau}\not\in A\end{subarray}}\pi(\bm{\sigma})P(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})

(2.1)

In our situation, we will work with balls and their surfaces

B_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\coloneqq\left\{\bm{\sigma}\ |\ d(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\sigma}_{0})\leq rN\right\},\qquad S_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\coloneqq\left\{\bm{\sigma}\ |\ d(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\sigma}_{0})=rN\right\}

of radius
r\in(0,2^{-1})
.
Provided
\bm{\sigma}_{0}
is chosen such that
\pi(B_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0}))\leq 2^{-1}
, we may further estimate the right side of (
2.1
) using (
1.2
) and the normalization
1=\sum_{\bm{\tau}}P(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi(B_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0}))}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\sigma}\in B_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\\
\bm{\tau}\not\in B_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\end{subarray}}\pi(\bm{\sigma})P(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi(B_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0}))}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\sigma}\in S_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\\
\bm{\tau}\not\in B_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\end{subarray}}\pi(\bm{\sigma})P(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})\leq\frac{\pi(S_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0}))}{\pi(B_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq\left|S_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\right|\ \exp\left(\beta\left[H(\bm{\sigma}_{0})-\min_{\bm{\tau}\in S_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})}H(\bm{\tau})\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(N\gamma(r)+\beta\left(H(\bm{\sigma}_{0})(1-c_{p}(r))-\min_{\bm{\tau}\in S_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})}G_{\bm{\sigma}_{0}}(\bm{\tau})\right]\right).

(2.2)

Here and in the following
|\cdot|
denotes the cardinality of a set, such that by the standard bound on the binomial coefficient in terms of the binary entropy function
\gamma
:

\left|S_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\right|=\binom{N}{rN}\leq e^{N\gamma(r)},\qquad\gamma(r)\coloneqq-r\ln r-(1-r)\ln(1-r).

(2.3)

A key ingredient in the last line of (
2.2
) was the Gaussian decomposition:

G_{\bm{\sigma}_{0}}(\bm{\tau})\coloneqq H(\bm{\tau})-H(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\ c_{p}\left(\frac{d(\bm{\sigma}_{0},\bm{\tau})}{N}\right).

(2.4)

To exploit the deterministic bound (
2.2
), we
will pick
\bm{\sigma}_{0}
from the set of large negative fluctuations,

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}\coloneq\left\{\bm{\sigma}\ |\ -H(\bm{\sigma})>\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon)N\right\},

(2.5)

with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
Our deterministic bound will hold on the following event.

Definition 2.1
.

For
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)
and
r\in[N^{-1},2^{-1})
, we define the event
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(r)
by requiring:

1.

|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|>|B_{2r}|
, with the latter the volume of any ball with radius
2rN
, and

2.

For all
\bm{\sigma}_{0}\in\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
:
\displaystyle\min_{\bm{\tau}\in S_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})}G_{\bm{\sigma}_{0}}(\bm{\tau})\geq-N\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon)\delta(1-c_{p}(r))
.

The following summarizes our deterministic bound, and shows that for
\beta
large enough on the above event, the mixing time is exponentially large.

Lemma 2.1
.

On the event
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(r)
there is some
\bm{\sigma}_{0}\in\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
such that
\pi\left(B_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\right)\leq 2^{-1}
, and one has:

4\ t_{\textrm{mix}}\geq\exp\left(N\left[\beta\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon)(1-\delta)(1-c_{p}(r))-\gamma(r)\right]\right).

(2.6)

Proof.

Since
|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|>|B_{2r}|\geq 2
, there are two distinct
\bm{\sigma}_{0},\bm{\tau}_{0}\in\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
such that
B_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\cap B_{r}(\bm{\tau}_{0})=\emptyset
. Hence

\pi(B_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0}))+\pi(B_{r}(\bm{\tau}_{0}))\leq 1,

from which we conclude that at least one of the two terms is smaller than
1/2
.

The bound (
2.6
) is then an immediate consequence of (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and the characterizing property
2
of the event
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(r)
.
∎

2.2
Probabilistic bounds

For a fixed
\bm{\sigma}\in\{-1,1\}^{N}
, the probability of an event of the form featuring in (
2.5
) is given by the error integral

\mathbb{P}(-H(\bm{\sigma})>u\sqrt{N})=\int_{u}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/2}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{2\pi}}=:\Phi(u),

for which we will frequently use the following standard estimates

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}u}\left(1-u^{-2}\right)e^{-u^{2}/2}\leq\Phi(u)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}u}e^{-u^{2}/2}

(2.7)

valid for any
u>0
. The estimate in particular implies that on average, the set
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
is exponentially large, i.e., for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
:

\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|\right]=2^{N}\Phi\left(\sqrt{N}\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon)\right)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi N}\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon)}\left(1-\frac{1}{\beta_{c}^{2}(1-\varepsilon)^{2}N}\right)\exp\left(N\frac{\beta_{c}^{2}}{2}\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)\right)

(2.8)

More importantly,

[
21
]
showed that this expectation is faithful via a standard second moment method.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
21
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
there is some
p(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
p\geq p(\varepsilon)
:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|\right]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right]}=1.

(2.9)

Remark 2.3
.

In the argument of
[
21
]
, it is necessary to choose the parameter
p(\varepsilon)>C\varepsilon^{-1}
for some constant
C>0
.

An immediate corollary is the following bound on the probability of the first event in Definition
2.1
.

Corollary 2.4
.

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
r\in(0,4^{-1})
such that

\gamma(r)\leq 4^{-1}\beta_{c}^{2}\varepsilon
,
there is some
c_{\varepsilon}(r)>0
and
N_{\varepsilon}(r)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N\geq N_{\varepsilon}(r)

\theta_{\varepsilon}(r)\coloneqq\frac{|B_{2r}|}{\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|\right]}\leq e^{-c_{\varepsilon}(r)N}.

(2.10)

Moreover, one also has

\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|>|B_{2r}|\right)\geq\left(1-\theta_{\varepsilon}(r)\right)^{2}\ \frac{\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|\right]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right]}.

(2.11)

Proof.

For an estimate on the ratio in (
2.10
), we use the monotonicity of the binomial coefficients for
2r\leq 2^{-1}
and (
2.3
) to conclude

|B_{2r}|=\sum_{j=0}^{2rN}\binom{N}{j}\leq(2rN+1)\binom{N}{2rN}\leq(2rN+1)e^{N\gamma(2r)}\leq(2rN+1)e^{2N\gamma(r)},

where the last step is by concavity of
\gamma
.
The bound (
2.10
) hence follows from (
2.8
), since
2\gamma(r)\leq 2^{-1}\beta_{c}^{2}\varepsilon

Since
\theta_{\varepsilon}(r)\in(0,1)
, the estimate (
2.11
) is simply the Paley-Zygmund inequality:

\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|>|B_{2r}|\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|>\theta_{\varepsilon}(r)\ \mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|\right]\right)\geq(1-\theta_{\varepsilon}(r))^{2}\ \frac{\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|\right]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right]},

which completes the proof.
∎

It remains to establish a bound on the second event in Definition
2.1
. This will heavily rely on the fact that for any
\bm{\sigma}_{0}
and any
\bm{\tau}
such that
d(\bm{\sigma}_{0},\bm{\tau})=rN
the random variables
G_{\bm{\sigma}_{0}}(\bm{\tau})=H(\bm{\tau})-H(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\ c_{p}\left(r\right)
are Gaussian with mean zero and variance

\mathbb{E}\left[G_{\bm{\sigma}_{0}}(\bm{\tau})^{2}\right]=N\left[1-c_{p}(r)^{2}\right],\quad\mbox{if $d(\bm{\sigma}_{0},\bm{\tau})=rN$.}

(2.12)

Moreover, they are uncorrelated,

\mathbb{E}\left[H(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\ G_{\bm{\sigma}_{0}}(\bm{\tau})\right]=0
,
and hence independent of
H(\bm{\sigma}_{0})
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)
and
r\in(0,2^{-1})
:

\mathbb{P}\left(\mbox{There is $\bm{\sigma}_{0}\in\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}$}:\quad\min_{\bm{\tau}\in S_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})}G_{\bm{\sigma}_{0}}(\bm{\tau})<-N\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon)\delta(1-c_{p}(r))\right)\\
\leq\frac{\sqrt{1+c_{p}(r)}}{2\pi N\beta_{c}^{2}(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\delta\sqrt{1-c_{p}(r)}}\ \exp\left(-\frac{N}{2}\left[\beta_{c}^{2}\left((1-\varepsilon)^{2}\delta^{2}\frac{1-c_{p}(r)}{1+c_{p}(r)}-2\varepsilon\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\right)-2\gamma(r)\right]\right).

(2.13)

Proof.

We use the union bound to estimate the left side of (
2.13
) by

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\sigma}_{0}\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{\sigma}_{0}\in\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}\;\mbox{and}\;\min_{\bm{\tau}\in S_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})}G_{\bm{\sigma}_{0}}(\bm{\tau})<-N\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon)\delta(1-c_{p}(r))\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bm{\sigma}_{0}\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{\sigma}_{0}\in\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}\right)\sum_{\bm{\tau}\in S_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})}\mathbb{P}\left(G_{\bm{\sigma}_{0}}(\bm{\tau})\leq-N\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon)\delta(1-c_{p}(r))\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{N}\Phi(\sqrt{N}\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon))\ \left|S_{r}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})\right|\Phi\left(\sqrt{N}\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon)\delta\frac{1-c_{p}(r)}{\sqrt{1-c_{p}(r)^{2}}}\right).

(2.14)

The assertion then follows from (
2.3
) and the standard estimate (
2.7
) on the error integral.
∎

2.3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be arbitrary and pick
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
small enough such that

x_{\varepsilon,\delta}\coloneqq\frac{3\varepsilon}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\delta^{2}}<1.

We now pick

r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2p}\ln\frac{1+x_{\varepsilon,\delta}}{1-x_{\varepsilon,\delta}}=\frac{\arctanh(x_{\varepsilon,\delta})}{p}.

(2.15)

This ensures that

c_{p}(r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p))\leq e^{-2p\ r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p)}=\frac{1-x_{\varepsilon,\delta}}{1+x_{\varepsilon,\delta}}

and hence

(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\delta^{2}\ \frac{1-c_{p}(r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p))}{1+c_{p}(r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p))}\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\delta^{2}x_{\varepsilon,\delta}=3\varepsilon.

In case
p\geq p_{\varepsilon,\delta}
with
p_{\varepsilon,\delta}\in\mathbb{N}
large enough such that
r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p)<4^{-1}
and

\gamma(r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p))\leq\frac{\beta_{c}^{2}\varepsilon}{4},

(2.16)

the exponent in the right side of (
2.13
) is non-positive and hence the probability converges to zero as
N\to\infty
. With these choices of parameters, Corollary
2.4
and Lemma
2.5
imply that

\mathbbm{P}\left(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p))^{c}\right)\leq 1-\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}|>|B_{2r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p)}|\right)\\
+\mathbb{P}\left(\mbox{There is $\bm{\sigma}_{0}\in\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}$:}\quad\min_{\bm{\tau}\in S_{r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p)}(\bm{\sigma}_{0})}G_{\bm{\sigma}_{0}}(\bm{\tau})<-N\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon)\delta(1-c_{p}(r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p)))\right)

converges to zero as
N\to\infty
. However, on
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p))
, Lemma
2.1
implies an exponential bound on the mixing time provided

\beta>\frac{\gamma(r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p))\ (1+x_{\varepsilon,\delta})}{\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon)(1-\delta)2x_{\varepsilon,\delta}}.

(2.17)

Since

\gamma(r_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p))\leq\frac{2\arctanh(x_{\varepsilon,\delta})}{p}\ln\frac{p}{\arctanh(x_{\varepsilon,\delta})},

this concludes the proof.
∎

On an optimal value for
p_{0}
:

Our main restriction on
p
comes from inequality (
2.16
). For small
\varepsilon
, this condition takes the form

p_{\varepsilon,\delta}\sim\frac{\ln(1/\varepsilon)\,\arctanh(x_{\varepsilon,\delta})}{\varepsilon}.

A suitable choice of
\delta
can reduce this bound substantially. This is especially valuable for even
p
, where one can use a symmetry argument similar to that of
[
34
]
to avoid using Proposition
2.2
, and hence eliminate the lower bound
p(\varepsilon)\geq C/\varepsilon
. However, since we require the set
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
to be nonempty, for smaller values of
p
we must take
\varepsilon
sufficiently large.

We can also optimize the lower bound in (
2.17
) with respect to
\varepsilon
and
\delta
in the regime of asymptotically large
p
. In that case, the lower bound is of the form

C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\frac{\ln p}{p}(1+o_{p}(1)):=\frac{\arctanh(x_{\varepsilon,\delta})(1+x_{\varepsilon,\delta})}{2\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon)(1-\delta)x_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\,\frac{\ln p}{p}(1+o_{p}(1)).

Since
\arctanh(x)\geq x
for all
x\geq 0
, we obtain

C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\geq\frac{1+x_{\varepsilon,\delta}}{2\beta_{c}(1-\varepsilon)(1-\delta)}\geq\frac{1}{2\beta_{c}}
.
For small
\varepsilon
and
\delta
, this lower bound is essentially sharp. Indeed, choose

\delta:=(6\varepsilon)^{1/3}
. Then

x_{\varepsilon,\delta}=\left(\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon\right)^{1/3}(1+O(\varepsilon))
,
and hence

\frac{1}{2\beta_{c}}\leq\min_{0<\varepsilon,\delta,\;x_{\varepsilon,\delta}<1}C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\leq\frac{1}{2\beta_{c}}\left(1+\left(\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon\right)^{1/3}+O(\varepsilon^{2/3})\right).
