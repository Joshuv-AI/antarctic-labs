Dipole Bend Magnets: Design Considerations

Executive Summary
This analysis provides a design of a dipole bend magnet for a beamline, stepping through various considerations
while assembling a design. This report can be used as a basis of a bending dipole magnet applicable to LANSCE, a
scalable point design for other applications, or as a systematic process for developing a magnet design from scratch.
The goal is more to answer, 'Is it reasonable?' than to address correction factors.
In this specific case, the dipole should provide 3 kG within a 6.35-cm (2.5") gap across an area provided by 25×15-
cm pole pieces. This provides roughly 17° and 21° deflection for proton at 3.0 and 2.1 MeV, respectively.

plans to merge H+ and H– beams; a single merging dipole magnet is presumed to be the simplest choice.

Keeping the number of turns high reduces total power dissipation (thanks to 𝑃 = 𝐼 & 𝑅, where 𝐼 decreases while 𝑅
increases), but this increases inductance and makes it more challenging to cool via hollow conductors. Larger wire
cross-section lowers resistance, while a larger-ID hole lower cooling-water head pressure. Developing a magnet
design requires compromises between these competing goals.
For this application, standard hollow conductors are likely sufficient. For example, Luvata 6835 is 6×6-mm square
conductor with a 4-mm ID round hole, providing 23.4-mm2 cross section area [4]. This wire can be wound onto each
of the two pole pieces (25×15-cm rectangle), each of which contain 100 loops. While the yoke perimeter is 80 cm,
we assume extra space and multiple layers, perhaps ~5 cm per loop on average. In addition, each lead requires a bit
more length. A conservative estimate of the total conductor length is 86 m per coil.
Using the cross section and copper resistivity, the coil resistance can be found. In this case, the resistance is about
62 mΩ, and the dissipated power will be about 350 W per coil.

Inductance for Pulsed Applications
If the magnet is to be pulsed, there is an inherent time constant governed by its resistance and inductance: 𝜏 = 𝐿⁄𝑅.

Using this formula, the inductance is calculated to be about 750 mH. If this magnet is intended to be pulsed with a
rise time of roughly 1 msec (for example, to reach >98% between two LANSCE macropulses), the large inductance
is problematic. A few comments are warranted:
  • The theoretical expression above is an upper bound to inductance; real magnets typically measure 20–60% less
 (due to layered-coil fill factor, fringe flux, and nonuniform fields)[3]. Without careful simulations or measure-
 ments, we might estimate the actual inductance to be 400–500 mH.
  • Even at 400–500 mH, the risetime is still going to be multiple seconds, assuming a voltage-regulated pulser
 drives the magnet.
  • A current-regulated pulser trying to ramp at 𝑑𝐼⁄𝑑𝑡 = 75 A⁄7.5 msec = 10 kA/sec requires 400 V. Power sup-
 plies are available that can provide 400 V and 75 A.
  • Capacitor-pulse drivers can provide this peak voltage as well. A series RLC circuit would need to store ~2 kJ
 at 750 V, so the capacitance should be ~7.4 mF.
Yoke material is an important consideration. Solid steel yokes generate eddy currents, and thus evolving magnetic
fields, for seconds after a change in current (for example, the fields in a 1-cm sheet of 1010 steel stabilize with at
𝜏 = 3 sec [7]). Silicon alloys in laminated layers, sometimes called 'electro-steel', is a standard method to signifi-
cantly improve the response time. Other techniques, such as pole shaping (to avoid saturation and high-curl field
distribution) and low-curl field distributions, also help the field to stabilize quickly to a change in driving current [3].
If the application includes pulsing this magnet, it becomes imperative to model the inductance more accurately,
choose appropriate yoke materials, and reconsider the number of windings or the driver circuitry. As 𝐿 ∝ 𝑁 & , a
reduction in the number of loops will significantly change the inductance. Decreasing the pole size is another option.
Increasing the gap reduces inductance but requires higher current to achieve the same field requirement.
