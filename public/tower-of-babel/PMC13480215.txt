Baseline Hypohaptoglobinemia as a Predisposing Factor for Hemolysis-Related Acute Kidney Injury After Pulsed-Field Ablation
Authors: Kohki Nakamura, Takehito Sasaki, Taiki Masuyama, Kentaro Minami, Shingo Yoshimura, Suguru Nishiuchi, Akiko Kodama, Kojiro Hattori, Kenichi Kaseno, Shigeto Naito
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHemolysis-related acute kidney injury (AKI) is an uncommon but important complication of pulsed-field ablation (PFA) for atrial fibrillation. The role of baseline hypohaptoglobinemia remains unclear.Case SummaryA 53-year-old woman with persistent atrial fibrillation underwent pulmonary vein and superior vena cava isolation using a pentaspline PFA catheter with 60 applications. Periprocedural hydration of approximately 1,500 mL was administered. Baseline serum haptoglobin was markedly low at 11 mg/dL (reference range: 43-180 mg/dL, lower detection limit: 10 mg/dL). Serum creatinine increased from 0.66 to 2.68 mg/dL on day 1 and peaked at 5.16 mg/dL on day 3, requiring temporary hemodialysis. Renal function subsequently recovered to baseline.DiscussionMarkedly low baseline haptoglobin may have contributed to susceptibility to severe hemolysis-related AKI, even without an excessively high number of PFA applications.Take-Home MessagePreprocedural hypohaptoglobinemia may be associated with increased susceptibility to AKI after PFA and may support closer monitoring in selected patients.

Past Medical History

She had impaired glucose metabolism and untreated dyslipidemia, with a fasting blood glucose level of 83 mg/dL, hemoglobin A1c of 6.2%, total cholesterol of 240 mg/dL, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol of 148 mg/dL, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol of 71 mg/dL. B-type natriuretic peptide was mildly elevated at 51.1 pg/mL, while left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) was preserved at 60%, and she was in New York Heart Association functional class I. Preprocedural blood tests and contrast-enhanced computed tomography revealed no findings suggestive of hemolytic anemia or liver disease.

Investigations

On admission, the following laboratory findings were within their reference ranges: lactate dehydrogenase, 206 U/L; serum creatinine, 0.66 mg/dL; estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), 76 mL/min/1.73 m2; and hemoglobin, 12.7 g/dL. Urinalysis was negative for proteinuria. The haptoglobin phenotype was 1-1, and the serum haptoglobin level was 11 mg/dL (reference range for phenotype 1-1: 43-180 mg/dL; the assay's lower detection limit: 10 mg/dL). She remained in AF, and transthoracic echocardiography showed a left atrial diameter of 28 mm.

Management

Oral anticoagulation with rivaroxaban 15 mg once daily was continued throughout the periprocedural period. Periprocedural hydration consisted of approximately 500 mL before and 1,000 mL after the procedure. She underwent pulmonary vein (PV) isolation (PVI) and superior vena cava (SVC) isolation using a 31-mm pentaspline pulsed-field ablation (PFA) catheter (FARAWAVE NAV, Boston Scientific). The procedure was guided by a 3-dimensional electroanatomic mapping system (OPAL HDx, Boston Scientific), fluoroscopy, and intracardiac echocardiography. PVI was performed according to the standard pentaspline PFA workflow.1 In a basket configuration, 2 applications were delivered at the same PV ostial position, followed by 2 additional applications after rotating the catheter by approximately 30° to 40°. The same sequence was then repeated in a flower configuration. Additional applications were delivered within the PVs, at the PV antra, and along the carina. An empirical SVC isolation was performed in the basket configuration using the same rotational workflow. Each application consisted of 5 trains of biphasic pulses with an electric field of 2,000 V. A total of 60 PFA applications were delivered, including 56 applications during PVI and 4 during SVC isolation (Figure 1). On postablation day 1, lactate dehydrogenase increased to 393 U/L, serum creatinine increased to 2.68 mg/dL, eGFR decreased to 16 mL/min/1.73 m2, and hemoglobin decreased to 9.9 g/dL. Renal function further worsened, with creatinine peaking at 5.16 mg/dL on day 3. Hemodialysis was initiated the same day and was continued for 2 days. By day 10, creatinine had improved to 2.06 mg/dL. Outcomes and Follow-Up

During 5 months of follow-up, the patient maintained sinus rhythm and showed no recurrent deterioration in renal function.

Discussion

PFA has recently been introduced for catheter ablation of AF, with rapidly expanding clinical use. It can cause intravascular hemolysis and, in some cases, hemolysis-related acute kidney injury (AKI). Previous studies have shown that a greater number of PFA applications is associated with more severe hemolysis and a higher risk of AKI.2, 3, 4, 5 Venier et al2 reported that patients with hemolysis after pentaspline PFA received more applications than those without (median: 75 vs 62, P = 0.011), and postprocedural haptoglobin levels were inversely correlated with the total number of applications. In that study, severe AKI occurred in patients who received markedly greater numbers of applications (126 and 174).2 In the MANIFEST-17K registry, AKI occurred predominantly in patients receiving a markedly greater number of PFA applications (≥96), and temporary hemodialysis was required as a rare but recognized complication of PFA.3 Mohanty et al4 also reported that hemoglobinuria and creatinine elevation occurred predominantly in patients receiving a greater number of PFA applications and that planned postprocedural hydration was independently associated with a lower risk of AKI.4 In the present case, AKI developed after a total of 60 PFA applications, which is lower than the application counts reported in most previously reported cases of severe hemolysis-related AKI.2, 3, 4 Furthermore, although approximately 1,500 mL of periprocedural hydration was administered, it remains uncertain whether more intensive hydration might have mitigated the risk of AKI.

Previous studies have identified several clinical factors associated with AKI after PFA, including older age, higher body mass index, lower renal function, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, structural heart disease, and reduced LVEF.5,6 In contrast, our patient had normal renal function, no proteinuria, mild dysglycemia, and preserved LVEF at baseline, making those established risk factors less compelling explanations for the severity of AKI. Thus, compared with previously reported severe AKI cases, the present case was characterized by a relatively modest application burden, periprocedural hydration, and the absence of major clinical risk factors. This discrepancy suggests that factors beyond PFA application burden alone may influence susceptibility to hemolysis-related AKI after PFA.

Alternative causes of AKI were also considered. Periprocedural blood pressure remained stable, with systolic/diastolic blood pressures ranging from 90-120/50-90 mm Hg, making sustained hemodynamic compromise unlikely. No contrast medium was used. Serum creatine kinase increased from 84 U/L at baseline to 373 U/L (reference range: 41-153 U/L) on postablation day 1, but decreased to 138 U/L by day 2. In addition, there were no clinical signs or symptoms of muscle injury, making clinically significant rhabdomyolysis unlikely. Intraprocedural medications included propofol, dexmedetomidine, pentazocine, and heparin. Drug-related nephrotoxicity cannot be completely excluded.

Haptoglobin is a liver-derived plasma protein that binds cell-free hemoglobin and facilitates its clearance, thereby limiting hemoglobin-mediated oxidative and renal injury.7 In the present case, the baseline serum haptoglobin level was markedly low at 11 mg/dL, only slightly above the assay's lower detection limit, despite no evidence of preablation hemolytic anemia or liver disease. No laboratory or imaging findings suggested an underlying cause of the hypohaptoglobinemia, and its etiology remained unclear. In addition, no prior haptoglobin measurements were available; therefore, we could not determine whether this finding reflected a persistent baseline abnormality or a transient condition. Accordingly, the markedly reduced preablation haptoglobin reserve may have impaired the capacity to neutralize cell-free hemoglobin released after PFA-related hemolysis, thereby predisposing the patient to AKI. In this context, the markedly low baseline haptoglobin level may represent an individual susceptibility factor that lowered the threshold for clinically significant renal injury, even at a lower application count than in previously reported severe AKI cases. However, because postprocedural haptoglobin and plasma-free hemoglobin were not measured, the proposed association between baseline hypohaptoglobinemia and hemolysis-related AKI after PFA remains hypothesis-generating.

Haptoglobin phenotype may also influence susceptibility to hemolysis-related AKI. Haptoglobin has 3 major phenotypes (1-1, 2-1, and 2-2), which arise from different combinations of the HP1 and HP2 alleles and differ in their antioxidant capacity and hemoglobin-binding properties. Feng et al8 demonstrated that, among patients with diabetes mellitus undergoing elective cardiac surgery, the haptoglobin 2-2 phenotype was independently associated with a higher risk of postoperative AKI than the 1-1 and 2-1 phenotypes. Although our patient had the haptoglobin 1-1 phenotype, which would not generally be considered a high-risk phenotype, the absolute baseline haptoglobin concentration may have been more clinically relevant than phenotype alone.

Conclusions

This case suggests that markedly low baseline haptoglobin may be associated with susceptibility to severe hemolysis-related AKI after PFA. In selected patients, particularly when the anticipated PFA application burden or baseline AKI risk is high, preprocedural haptoglobin assessment may be considered for risk awareness and closer postprocedural monitoring.
