1.
Introduction

The study of polynomials and rational functions that induce permutations on finite fields is a classical topic at the intersection of number theory and arithmetic geometry. A polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is called a
Schur polynomial
(or is said to be exceptional) if it induces a bijection on the residue field
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
for infinitely many primes
p
. This direction of research was initiated by Schur
[
17
]
in 1923. His work led to a conjecture, now known as
Schur’s conjecture
, asserting that any such polynomial must be a composition of linear polynomials and Dickson polynomials. Schur’s conjecture was first proven by Fried
[
6
]
, but his proof contains several gaps and errors. Later, Turnwald
[
22
]
gave a corrected proof together with some historical remarks and Müller
[
13
]
gave a simplified proof, which relies on group-theoretic arguments.

The problem naturally extends to rational functions. A rational function
r(x)\in\mathbb{Q}(x)
is said to be
arithmetically exceptional
if it induces a permutation on the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
for infinitely many primes
p
. The classification of such functions is significantly more complex than the polynomial case. Guralnick, Müller, and Saxl
[
7
]
provided a comprehensive analysis of exceptional rational functions, highlighting their connection to monodromy groups and arithmetic geometry.

A rich source of rational functions with interesting dynamical and arithmetic properties are the
Lattès maps
. Originating from the arithmetic of elliptic curves, given
k\in\mathbb{N}
and an elliptic curve
E/K
, the Lattès map
L_{k}
attached to
E
is the unique rational function satisfying
\mathrm{proj}\circ[k]=L_{k}\circ\mathrm{proj}
, where
[k]
is the multiplication-by-
k
endomorphism on
E
, and
\mathrm{proj}:E\to\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
is the
x
-coordinate projection map. In the context of complex dynamics, Milnor
[
12
]
provided a foundational description of these maps, characterizing them as quotients of affine maps on tori.

From an arithmetic perspective, Lattès maps have been the subject of recent intense study. Küçüksakallı
[
10
]
investigated the value sets of Lattès maps over finite fields, while Bell et al.
[
1
]
analyzed the density of periodic points for these maps. Furthermore, the construction of permutation rational functions via isogenies of elliptic curves has been explored by Bisson and Tibouchi
[
3
]
, suggesting a deep link between the endomorphism structure of the underlying curve and the permutation properties of the induced rational map.

Recently, based on computational evidence, Odabaş
[
14
, Ch. 5]
formulated a precise conjecture connecting the exceptionality of
L_{k}
to the torsion structure of the elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. Specifically, the conjecture can be stated as follows.

Conjecture 1

(Odabaş)
.

For any positive integer
k
, the
k
-th Lattès map attached to an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
is arithmetically exceptional if and only if
E
possesses no
k
-torsion point whose
x
-coordinate is rational.

In this paper, we address this conjecture mainly for the class of elliptic curves with complex multiplication (CM). By leveraging the theory of Frobenius distributions for CM elliptic curves and explicit reciprocity laws, we prove the following:

Theorem 2
.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field with class number 1, excluding
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-11})
, and let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with CM by an order
\mathcal{O}
in
K
. Then Conjecture
1
holds for
E
.

The CM field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-11})
is excluded from Theorem
2
, since, as shown in Section
4
, the conjecture turns out to be false in this case.
In particular, for
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-11})
, we show that the conjecture holds precisely when
6\nmid k

(see Proposition
12
),
whereas for
6\mid k
there is a genuine obstruction coming from the splitting of
3
in
K
.
In fact, for any
E/\mathbb{Q}
with CM by the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and any
k
divisible by
6
,
the associated Lattès map
L_{k}
fails to be arithmetically exceptional, regardless of the rationality of the
x
-coordinates of the
k
-torsion points of
E

(see Proposition
11
).
We also give explicit counterexamples, for both CM and non-CM elliptic curves, in Proposition
14
and Proposition
18
, which appear to be overlooked in Odabaş’s experiments.

We organize this paper as follows.
Section
2
collects the permutation criterion for Lattès maps in terms of Frobenius traces,
together with CM background results and reciprocity tools used in the proofs.
In Section
3
we prove Theorem
2
by treating the CM discriminants case-by-case.
Section
4
is devoted to the exceptional field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-11})
, where we identify
the obstruction when
6\mid k
and prove the corresponding positive result for
6\nmid k
.
In Section
5
, we present explicit examples illustrating the main theorem and
counterexamples for the CM field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-11})
. Finally, we discuss the conjecture in a general setting and outline directions for further investigation in Section
6
.

2.
Some auxiliary results

The following key result can be found in
[
1
, Cor. 2.6]
. It should be noted that an equivalent result for CM elliptic curves is stated and proven in
[
10
, Cor. 2.8]
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve which has a good reduction at a prime
p
with the Frobenius trace
a_{p}
. Then the
k
-th Lattès map attached to
E
induces a bijection on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
if and only if
((p+1)^{2}-a_{p}^{2},k)=1
.

We will also invoke the following well-known result of Deuring on Frobenius traces of CM elliptic curves. For references, the reader can consult
[
4
, Thm. 7.2.15]
.

Proposition 4
.

If
E/\mathbb{Q}
has CM by an imaginary quadratic order
\mathcal{O}
of discriminant
D
and
p
is a prime of good reduction and not dividing
D
, then

a_{p}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=-1,\\
\pi+\bar{\pi}&\text{if}\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=1,\end{cases}

for some prime
\pi\in\mathcal{O}
such that
N(\pi)=p
.

It can be seen immediately from Proposition
4
that if
\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=1
(i.e.
p
splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
), then

(2.1)

(p+1)^{2}-a_{p}^{2}=N((\pi-1)(\pi+1))=|E(\mathbb{F}_{p})||E^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|,

where
E^{\prime}
is a quadratic twist of
E
(see, e.g.,
[
1
, Lem. 2.3]
). The next lemma gives us a necessary condition for the divisibility of
N((\pi-1)(\pi+1))
by a given prime.

Lemma 5
.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field with class number
1
,
\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and let
\alpha
be an associate of
\alpha_{0}
. Suppose
\ell
is a rational prime and
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a prime above
\ell
. If
\alpha_{0}\not\equiv u\pmod{\lambda}
and
\alpha_{0}\not\equiv u\pmod{\bar{\lambda}}
for every
u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
, then
\ell\nmid N((\alpha-1)(\alpha+1))
.

Proof.

Assume that
\ell\mid N((\alpha-1)(\alpha+1))
. Then, by multiplicativity of the norm, either
\ell\mid(\alpha-1)(\bar{\alpha}-1)
or
\ell\mid(\alpha+1)(\bar{\alpha}+1)
. Since
\lambda
is a prime above
\ell
and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a unique factorization domain, we have that either
\alpha\equiv\pm 1\pmod{\lambda}
or
\alpha\equiv\pm 1\pmod{\bar{\lambda}}
. Hence
\alpha_{0}\equiv u\pmod{\lambda}
or
\alpha_{0}\equiv u\pmod{\bar{\lambda}}
for some
u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}.

∎

For an integer
n\geq 2
, a number field
L
containing a primitive
n
-th root of unity
\zeta_{n}
, a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}
coprime to
n
, and
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
, we define the
n
-th power residue symbol
\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)_{n}
as

\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)_{n}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\alpha\in\mathfrak{p},\\
\zeta_{n}^{s}&\text{ if }\alpha\notin\mathfrak{p},\end{cases}

where
\zeta_{n}^{s}
is the unique
n
-th root of unity such that
\alpha^{\frac{N\mathfrak{p}-1}{n}}\equiv\zeta_{n}^{s}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}
. It is easily seen from the definition that the symbol
\left(\frac{\cdot}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)_{n}
is multiplicative and, for any positive divisor
d
of
n
,

(2.2)

\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)_{n}^{d}=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)_{n/d}.

If
L
has class number
1
, then
\mathfrak{p}=(\pi)
for some prime
\pi\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
, and, as an abuse of notation, we will replace
\mathfrak{p}
with
\pi
in the above definition. Let
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}=(-1+\sqrt{-3})/2
. Following a notion in
[
8
, §9.3]
, we say that a prime
\pi\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
is
primary
if
\pi\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. The following classical results on reciprocity laws can be found in
[
8
, §9.3]
or
[
11
, §7]
.

Proposition 6

(Cubic reciprocity law)
.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
be primary primes such that
N(\pi_{1})\neq 3,N(\pi_{2})\neq 3,
and
N(\pi_{1})\neq N(\pi_{2})
. Then we have

\ \left(\frac{\pi_{1}}{\pi_{2}}\right)_{3}=\left(\frac{\pi_{2}}{\pi_{1}}\right)_{3}.

Eisenstein established an analogue of Proposition
6
for the sextic residue symbol, commonly known as the sextic reciprocity law. To state the law, we need a notion of
E-primary
primes, which is slightly different from that of primary primes. The original definition of an
E
-primary element in
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
is due to Eisenstein and is stated in
[
11
, §7.3]
. In fact, if
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
is coprime to
6
, then
\alpha
is
E
-primary if and only if

\alpha\equiv\pm 1\pmod{3}\text{ and }\alpha^{3}=A+B\omega\text{ with }A+B\equiv 1\pmod{4}

(see, e.g.,
[
19
, §7]
). Using the above criterion, one sees easily that if
\pi\equiv\pm 1\pmod{3}
is coprime to
6
, then either
\pi
or
-\pi
is
E
-primary.

Proposition 7

(Sextic reciprocity law)
.

If
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
are E-primary primes satisfying
(\pi_{1}\pi_{2},6)=(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=1
, then

\left(\frac{\pi_{1}}{\pi_{2}}\right)_{6}=(-1)^{\frac{N\pi_{1}-1}{2}\frac{N\pi_{2}-1}{2}}\left(\frac{\pi_{2}}{\pi_{1}}\right)_{6}.

3.
Proof of the main theorem

Throughout this paper, for any elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
which has good reduction at a prime
p
with the Frobenius trace
a_{p}
, we denote

A_{p}(E)=(p+1)^{2}-a_{p}^{2}.

As pointed out in Odabaş’s thesis
[
14
, Ch. 5]
, the forward direction of Theorem
2
(without using complex multiplication) is obvious and can be elaborated as follows. Suppose
E
has a
k
-torsion point, say
\alpha
, with rational
x
-coordinate. Then, for any sufficiently large prime
p
, its reduction
\bar{\alpha}
modulo
p
must be a root of the (reduced)
k
-th division polynomial, implying that the induced
k
-th Lattès map
L_{k}:\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
sends both
\bar{\alpha}
and
\infty
to
\infty
. Therefore, it remains to prove the converse. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that
E
has no
k
-torsion point with rational
x
-coordinate. Note that
L_{1}
is the identity map, which is clearly arithmetically exceptional. Hence we may assume that
k>1
. By Proposition
3
, it suffices to show that there are infinitely many primes
p
for which
(A_{p}(E),k)=1
. We consider
E
in different cases, depending on its CM discriminant (i.e. the discriminant of
\mathcal{O}
), which is denoted by
D
. All cases rely crucially on either Dirichlet’s theorem on arithmetic progressions or an explicit version of Chebotarev density theorem, which can be seen as a generalization of Dirichlet’s theorem. We shall also frequently refer to a result of Olson
[
15
]
on the classification of torsion subgroups
E^{\mathrm{tor}}(\mathbb{Q})
of
E(\mathbb{Q})
, which is summarized in Table
1
below.

3.1.
Case
D\in\{-4,-8,-16\}

First, assume that
D\in\{-4,-16\}
, which is corresponding to the case
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})
. Then
E^{\mathrm{tor}}(\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to
C_{2},C_{2}\times C_{2}
, or
C_{4}
. Hence
E
always has a rational
2
-torsion point and
k
must be odd. By Chinese Remainder Theorem and Dirichlet’s theorem, there exist infinitely many primes
p
such that
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and
p\equiv 1\pmod{k}
. Moreover, for all such
p
we have that
\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=-1
(i.e.
p
is inert in
K
) and, by Proposition
4
,

(A_{p}(E),k)=((p+1)^{2},k)=(4,k)=1.

On the other hand, if
D=-8
, then
E^{\mathrm{tor}}(\mathbb{Q})\simeq C_{2}
, so
k
is odd. Hence we can apply similar arguments by taking primes
p
satisfying
p\equiv 5\pmod{8}
and
p\equiv 1\pmod{k}
.

3.2.
Case
D\in\{-7,-28\}

In this case, we have
E^{\mathrm{tor}}(\mathbb{Q})\simeq C_{2}
, so
k
is odd. If
p
is a prime satisfying
p\equiv 5\pmod{7}
and
p\equiv-2\pmod{k}
, then
\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=-1
and

(A_{p}(E),k)=((p+1)^{2},k)=(1,k)=1.

Again, the infinitude of such primes
p
is guaranteed by Chinese Remainder Theorem and Dirichlet’s theorem.

3.3.
Case
D\in\{-19,-43,-67,-163\}

Note first that for each
D
in this set
\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)=\left(\frac{D}{3}\right)=-1
, so
2
and
3
are inert primes in
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D}).
It follows that, for every prime
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
\left|\left(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\lambda\mathcal{O}_{K}\right)^{\times}\right|=N(\lambda)-1\geq 3
. Now let the radical of
k
be written as
\mathrm{rad}(k)=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\cdots\ell_{r}
, where
\ell_{i}^{\prime}s
are (distinct) rational primes and, for each
1\leq i\leq r
, let
\lambda_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
be a prime above
\ell_{i}
. Also, suppose
(|D|)=(\lambda_{D})^{2},
where
\lambda_{D}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Using quadratic reciprocity, one sees that, for any prime
p\neq|D|
,
\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{p}{|D|}\right)
, so
p
is inert in
K
if and only if
p
is a quadratic non-residue modulo
|D|
. Since there are exactly
\frac{|D|-1}{2}
quadratic non-residues modulo
|D|
and
\frac{|D|-1}{2}+2<|D|-1=\left|\left(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\lambda_{D}\mathcal{O}_{K}\right)^{\times}\right|
, we have by Chebotarev density theorem that there are infinitely many primes
\pi\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
\pi\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{\lambda_{i}}
,
\pi\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{\bar{\lambda}_{i}}
, and
\pi\not\equiv t\pmod{\lambda_{D}}
for every
1\leq i\leq r
and every quadratic non-residue
t
modulo
|D|
. Hence any prime
\pi
satisfying the above system of congruences must be a splitting prime. If
N(\pi)=p
, then, using the fact that
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}=\{\pm 1\}
, we immediately have from Lemma
5
that
\ell_{i}\nmid A_{p}(E)
for every
i
. Consequently, we can conclude that
(A_{p}(E),k)=1
for infinitely many primes
p
.

3.4.
Case
D=-12

In this case, we have
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
,
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{Z}+2\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and
j(E)=54000
. Suppose
E
has a short Weierstrass form
y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
, where
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
j(E)=1728\frac{4A^{3}}{4A^{3}+27B^{2}}
, it can be seen easily that
A=-15u^{2}
and
B=22u^{3}
for some
u\in\mathbb{Z}.
By a simple calculation, we have that the associated
3
-rd division polynomial can be factored as

\psi_{3}(x)=3x^{4}+6Ax^{2}+12Bx-A^{2}=3(x-3u)(x^{3}+3ux^{2}-21u^{2}x+25u^{3}).

Therefore,
E
has a
3
-torsion point
P
such that
x(P)=3u
, which is rational. Moreover, since
E^{\mathrm{tor}}(\mathbb{Q})\simeq C_{2}
or
C_{6}
,
E
always has a rational
2
-torsion point, implying
(k,6)=1
. Hence, for any prime
p
satisfying
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
and
p\equiv 1\pmod{k}
,

(A_{p}(E),k)=((p+1)^{2},k)=(4,k)=1.

Again, since the above congruence system is compatible, there must be an infinite number of primes
p
which are solutions to the system.

3.5.
Case
D=-27

In this case, we have
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}),\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{Z}+3\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and
j(E)=-2^{15}\cdot 3\cdot 5^{3}
. By analyzing the
j

E
, one sees that
E
has a parametrized Weierstrass form
y^{2}=x^{3}-120u^{2}x+506u^{3}

u\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}.
Furthermore, the associated
3
-rd division polynomial factors as

\psi_{3}(x)=3(x-6u)(x^{3}+6ux^{2}-204u^{2}x+800u^{3}),

whence
E
has a
3
-torsion point with rational
x
-coordinate. It follows that
(k,6)=1
or
(k,6)=2
. If
(k,6)=1
, as in the previous case, we can simply choose primes
p
satisfying
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
and
p\equiv 1\pmod{k}
. Now suppose
(k,6)=2.
Then
\mathrm{rad}(k)=2m
, where
m=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\ell_{i}
is a (possibly empty) product of distinct primes greater than
3
. For any prime
\ell\notin\{2,3,7\}

above

\left|\left(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\lambda\mathcal{O}_{K}\right)^{\times}\right|\geq 12
. Therefore, by Chebotarev density theorem, we can find infinitely many primes

which simultaneously satisfy the following congruences:

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle\equiv\omega

\displaystyle\pmod{2}

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle\equiv 1

\displaystyle\pmod{3}

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle\equiv\omega

\displaystyle\pmod{7}

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle\equiv a_{i}

\displaystyle\pmod{\lambda_{i}}

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle\equiv b_{i}

\displaystyle\pmod{\overline{\lambda_{i}}},

where
\lambda_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a prime above
\ell_{i}\neq 7
,
a_{i}\not\equiv\pm 1,\pm\omega,\pm\omega^{2}\pmod{\lambda_{i}}
, and
b_{i}\not\equiv\pm 1,\pm\omega,\pm\omega^{2}\pmod{\overline{\lambda_{i}}}
. (If
\ell_{i}
is inert, then we simply consider a single congruence modulo

\lambda_{i}

\overline{\lambda_{i}}

|E(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=|E(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\pi\mathcal{O}_{K})|=p+1-\left(\frac{6\sqrt{-3}u}{\pi}\right)_{2}\epsilon(\pi)(\pi+\bar{\pi}),

3.6.
Case
D=-3

In this case, we have
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}),\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{K},j(E)=0
, and

can be defined by the Weierstrass equation

, where

is a sixth-power-free integer. Moreover, the torsion structure of

can be described explicitly as follows:

(3.1)

E_{d}^{\text{tor}}(\mathbb{Q})\simeq\begin{cases}C_{6},&\text{ if }d=1,\\
C_{3},&\text{ if }d\neq 1\text{ is a square or }d=-432,\\
C_{2},&\text{ if }d\neq 1\text{ is a cube},\\
C_{1},&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

This classification can be found in Exercise 10.19 of

. It should be remarked (

) is a corrected version, as ‘square’ and ‘cube’ are switched in the aforementioned reference. As in the previous case, since
(0,\pm\sqrt{d})

, either
(k,6)=1
or
(k,6)=2
. If
(k,6)=1

and
p\equiv 1\pmod{k}
. Next, assume that
(k,6)=2

\mathrm{rad}(k)=2\prod_{i=1}^{r}\ell_{i}
, where
\ell_{i}^{\prime}s
are primes larger than

[
8

, for any primary splitting prime
\pi\in\mathcal{O}_{K}

\lambda_{i}=\overline{\lambda_{i}}
, we simply take
a_{i}=b_{i}
.) The first congruence implies that these are splitting primes and it is clear that

. Thus it follows immediately from Lemma

that, if

, then
\ell_{i}\nmid|E(\mathbb{F}_{p})||E^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|
for every

, implying
(|E(\mathbb{F}_{p})||E^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|,k)=1
, as desired.
∎

By Proposition
12
, we immediately have the following result.

Corollary 13
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve having complex multiplication by
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-11})
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
6\nmid k
. Then Conjecture
1
is true for the Lattès map
L_{k}
attached to
E
.

The next Proposition provides explicit CM curves showing that the conjecture fails when
6\mid k
. We recall the explicit Weierstrass models for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
with complex
multiplication by an order
\mathcal{O}
of class number one.
Following Jiménez Urroz
[
9
]
, any CM elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}

with discriminant
D\in\{-3,-4,-7,-8,-11,-19,-43,-67,-163\}
admits a short Weierstrass equation of the form

E_{g}:\quad y^{2}=x^{3}+g_{4}(g)\,x+g_{6}(g),

where
g\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and the coefficients
(g_{4}(g),g_{6}(g))
are given explicitly as
quadratic and cubic polynomials in
g
, depending on
D
.
In particular, for
D=-11
, one may take

(g_{4}(g),g_{6}(g))=\left(-\frac{2\cdot 11}{3}g^{2},\,-\frac{7\cdot 11^{2}}{108}g^{3}\right),

which yields a CM model with
j
-invariant
-2^{15}
.
By setting
g=-6u
, this family specializes to the model
E_{u}
below which plays a distinguished role in the obstruction to arithmetic exceptionality when
6\mid k
.

Proposition 14
.

Let
u\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and consider the elliptic curve

E_{u}:\ y^{2}=x^{3}-264u^{2}x+1694u^{3}.

Then
E_{u}
has complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}
and
E_{u}
has no
2
-torsion and
3
-torsion points with rational
x
-coordinate. In particular, for every integer
k\geq 1
with
6\mid k
, there is no
k
-torsion point
P\in E_{u}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
such that
x(P)\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

A direct computation shows that
j(E_{u})=-2^{15}
, so
E_{u}
has CM by
\mathcal{O}
. Also, the
2
-nd
division polynomial associated to
E_{u}
is

\psi_{2}(x)=x^{3}-264u^{2}x+1694u^{3}=u^{3}\Bigl((x/u)^{3}-264(x/u)+1694\Bigr).

If
\psi_{2}
had a rational root
x_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
t:=x_{0}/u\in\mathbb{Q}
would satisfy
t^{3}-264t+1694=0
.
Reducing modulo
5
gives
t^{3}+t+4\in\mathbb{F}_{5}[t]
, which has no root in
\mathbb{F}_{5}
, so
t^{3}-264t+1694
has no
rational root. Hence
\psi_{2}
has no rational root, and there is no
2
-torsion point with rational

x
-coordinate.

Similarly, the
3
-rd division polynomial is

\psi_{3}(x)=3x^{4}-1584u^{2}x^{2}+20328u^{3}x-69696u^{4}=3(x^{2}-22ux+132u^{2})(x^{2}+22ux-176u^{2}).

The two quadratic factors have discriminants
-44u^{2}
and
1188u^{2}
, neither of which is a square in
\mathbb{Q}
.
Thus
\psi_{3}
has no rational root and there is no
3
-torsion point with rational
x
-coordinate.

Finally, suppose
P\in E_{u}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
is a
k
-torsion point with
6\mid k
and
x(P)\in\mathbb{Q}
.
Then
[k/2]P
is a
2
-torsion point and
[k/3]P
is a
3
-torsion point, where at least one of them is not the point at infinity. Moreover, for each
m\geq 1
we have

where
L_{m}\in\mathbb{Q}(x)
is the
m
-th Lattès map attached to
E_{u}
.
In particular, either
x([k/2]P)
or
x([k/3]P)
is rational, contradicting the previous two paragraphs.
∎

An outlook for further exploration

Theorem
2
verifies Odabaş’s conjectural criterion for all CM elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}

except for the unique CM field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-11})
, where Proposition
11
shows a genuinely new obstruction when
6\mid k
.
This naturally suggests several directions in which the present work can be extended or refined.
We single out, in particular, a uniform treatment of the case
k=2
(which does not require CM),
and then outline broader problems motivated by a Galois-representation perspective. The proof of the following proposition stems from Elkies’ answer in a MathOverflow discussion; see also an alternative approach using Galois representations due to Bellaïche
[
2
]
. In this section, we shall denote the group of
n
-torsion points on
E/K
by
E(K)[n]
.

Proposition 16
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. Then the second Lattès map
L_{2}
attached to
E
is arithmetically exceptional if and only if
E
has no nontrivial rational
2
-torsion point.

Proof.

We first write
E:y^{2}=P(x)
, where
P(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is a cubic polynomial and let
E(K)[n]
denote the set of
n
-torsion points on
E/K
, including the point
\mathcal{O}
at infinity. As mentioned in Section
3
, the forward direction is obvious. To prove the converse, assume that
L_{2}
is not arithmetically exceptional. By Proposition
3
, we have that

a_{p}\equiv 0\pmod{2}
for all but finitely many primes
p
. Since
a_{p}=p+1-|E(\mathbb{F}_{p})|
for every good prime
p
, it follows that
|E(\mathbb{F}_{p})|\equiv 0\pmod{2}
for all but finitely many
p
. If
E(\mathbb{F}_{p})[2]=\{\mathcal{O}\}
, then every point on
E/\mathbb{F}_{p}
must be a torsion point of odd order, implying
|E(\mathbb{F}_{p})|\equiv 1\pmod{2}
. Hence we can conclude that
P(x)
has a root in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
for all but finitely many
p
. Now assume to the contrary that
P(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
and let
L
be the splitting field of
P(x)
. Then
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
is either
S_{3}
or
C_{3}
; i.e., it contains a
3
-cycle. For any prime
p
which is unramified in
L
and a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
above
p
, the Frobenius element
\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
acts on the three roots of
P(x)
and its cycle type determines the factorization of
P(x)
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
In particular,
\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a
3
-cycle if and only if
P(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. By the Chebotarev density theorem applied to
L/\mathbb{Q}
, there are infinitely many primes
p
of good reduction for which
\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a
3
-cycle, hence for which
P(x)\bmod p
is irreducible, a contradiction. Therefore,
P(x)
must have a rational root; i.e.,
E
has a nontrivial rational
2
-torsion point.
∎

Remark 17
.

Since the nontrivial rational
2
-torsion points of
E/\mathbb{Q}
are exactly those with rational
x
-coordinate, Proposition
16
confirms Odabaş’s conjecture for
k=2
.
Moreover, Chebotarev gives a density statement: if
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq S_{3}
, then the primes
p
for which
L_{2}
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
have natural density
1/3
; if
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq C_{3}
, the density is
2/3
.

To investigate the problem further for
k>2
, let us first point out additional counterexamples to Conjecture
1
for
non-CM
elliptic curves, which are of a similar nature to those discussed in Section
4
.

Proposition 18
.

For any nonzero integer
u
, consider the elliptic curve

E_{u}:y^{2}=x^{3}-9u^{2}x+12u^{3}.

For any positive integer
k
such that
6\mid k
, we have the following.

(i)

For any prime
p
where
E_{u}
has good reduction,
(A_{p}(E_{u}),k)\neq 1
; i.e., the
k
-th Lattés map attached to
E_{u}
is not arithmetically exceptional.

(ii)

If
P\in E_{u}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})[k]
, then
x(P)\not\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
k
be a positive integer such that
6\mid k
.
To prove (i), we shall consider the good primes
p
in two different cases, depending on whether
3
is a cubic residue modulo
p
. If
3\equiv m^{3}\pmod{p}
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, then it can be checked easily that
(-mu-m^{2}u,0)
is a
2
-torsion point on
E_{u}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Hence
|E_{u}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|
must be even and, by (
2.1
),

(A_{p}(E_{u}),k)=(|E_{u}(\mathbb{F}_{p})||E^{\prime}_{u}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|,k)\geq 2.

Now assume that
3
is not a cubic residue modulo
p
. Then we have that
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
. (Otherwise, for
p=3m+2
, we have
(3^{2m+1})^{3}\equiv 3^{2p-1}\equiv 3\pmod{p}
.) Note that the
3
-rd division polynomial associated to
E_{u}
is

\psi_{3}(x)=3(x^{4}-18u^{2}x^{2}+48u^{3}x-27u^{4})=:3f(x),

whose discriminant is
D=-2^{12}3^{13}u^{12}
. Since
\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right)=1
, it follows that
\psi_{3}(x)
is reducible over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
(see, e.g.,
[
5
]
). Suppose
f(x)
is a product of two quadratic polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Then we have that

f(x)=(x^{2}-Ax+B)(x^{2}+Ax+C),

where
A\neq 0
. Expanding the right-hand side and comparing the coefficients yield
B+C=A^{2}-18u^{2},

B-C=48u^{3}/A
, and
BC=-27u^{4}
. Since
(B+C)^{2}-(B-C)^{2}=4BC
, letting
a=A^{2}
and
v=u^{2}
, one sees that
a(a-18v)^{2}-2304v^{3}=-108v^{2}a,
which is equivalent to

(6.1)

\left(\frac{a-12v}{4v}\right)^{3}=9.

Then it follows immediately from (
6.1
) that

\left(\frac{(a-12v)^{2}}{3(4v)^{2}}\right)^{3}=3,

which contradicts the assumption on
p
. (Note that the multiplicative inverse of
3(4v)^{2}
exists since
p
does not divide the discriminant of
E_{u}
, which is
-2^{6}3^{5}u^{6}
.) Hence the only possibility is that
f(x)
factors as the product of a linear and an irreducible cubic polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. In other words,
f(x)
must have a unique root in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, say
x_{0}
. If
x_{0}^{3}-9u^{2}x_{0}+12u^{3}
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, then
E_{u}
has a
3
-torsion point over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Otherwise, for any non-square
d
,
d(x_{0}^{3}-9u^{2}x_{0}+12u^{3})
must be a square in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and the quadratic twist
E^{\prime}_{u}:d^{-1}y^{2}=x^{3}-9u^{2}x+12u^{3}
has a
3
-torsion point over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Therefore, we have
(|E_{u}(\mathbb{F}_{p})||E^{\prime}_{u}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|,k)\geq 3.

Part (ii) can be proven using arguments similar to those in the proof of Proposition
14
. By the rational root theorem, both
t^{3}-9t+12
and
t^{4}-18t^{2}+48t-27
have no rational roots, so
E_{u}
has no
2
- or
3
-torsion points with rational
x
-coordinates. Therefore, we can conclude that
E_{u}
has no
k
-torsion point whose
x
-coordinate is rational.
∎

Remark 19
.

A direct calculation yields
j(E_{u})=-5184
, so
E_{u}
does not have complex multiplication. We also discover many other non-CM counterexamples which are analogous to the family
E_{u}
. For example, for a nonzero integer
u
, consider the elliptic curve
F_{u}:y^{2}=x^{3}-60u^{2}x+180u^{3}
, which has
j
-invariant
-138240
. One can apply the same arguments as above to show that the statements in Proposition
18
hold for
F_{u}
. In particular, for any prime
p\nmid 10u
, we have that
2\mid A_{p}(F_{u})
if
10
is a cubic residue modulo
p
and
3\mid A_{p}(F_{u})
otherwise. Notably, cases with
6\mid k
are absent from Odabaş’s experimental data.

Based on our main results for CM elliptic curves and the counterexamples in Proposition
14
, Proposition
18
, and Remark
19
, it seems reasonable to reformulate Odabaş’s conjecture as follows.

Conjecture 20
.

For any positive integer
k
such that
6\nmid k
, the
k
-th Lattès map attached to an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
is arithmetically exceptional if and only if
E
possesses no
k
-torsion point whose
x
-coordinate is rational.

Although we are not yet able to prove Conjecture
20
for non-CM elliptic curves, we finish this paper by investigating it via Galois representations.

Let
\ell
be a prime and consider the mod-
\ell
Galois representation

\overline{\rho}_{E,\ell}:\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\Aut(E[\ell])\simeq\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}).

For a prime
p
of good reduction with
p\neq\ell
, the element
\overline{\rho}_{E,\ell}(\mathrm{Frob}_{p})
has
characteristic polynomial congruent to
T^{2}-a_{p}T+p
modulo
\ell
.
Evaluating at
T=\pm 1
gives the congruence

\det\bigl(I_{2}\pm\overline{\rho}_{E,\ell}(\mathrm{Frob}_{p})\bigr)\equiv p+1\pm a_{p}\pmod{\ell}.

Consequently, we have

(6.2)

\gcd\bigl(A_{p}(E),\,m\bigr)=1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \text{for every }\ell\mid m,\ \det\bigl(I_{2}\pm\overline{\rho}_{E,\ell}(\mathrm{Frob}_{p})\bigr)\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}.

This viewpoint packages the permutation condition into a single Chebotarev-type condition.
Indeed, let

\overline{\rho}_{E,m}:\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\Aut(E[m])\simeq\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})

be the combined mod-
m
representation and set

\displaystyle G_{m}

\displaystyle:=\overline{\rho}_{E,m}\bigl(\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\bigr)\ \simeq\ \Gal\bigl(\mathbb{Q}(E[m])/\mathbb{Q}\bigr),

\displaystyle C_{m}

\displaystyle:=\Bigl\{A\in G_{m}:\ \det(I_{2}-A)\det(I_{2}+A)\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\Bigr\}.

For any prime
p\nmid m
of good reduction, by (
6.2
) and Proposition
3
, we have

L_{m}\text{ permutes }\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\overline{\rho}_{E,m}(\mathrm{Frob}_{p})\in C_{m}.

Since every element in
G_{m}
comes from a Frobenius element, Chebotarev density theorem implies the following simple restatement:

(6.3)

L_{m}\text{ is arithmetically exceptional}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C_{m}\neq\emptyset.

Moreover, when
C_{m}\neq\emptyset
, the set of primes
p
of good reduction for which
L_{m}
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
has positive natural density and is equal to
|C_{m}|/|G_{m}|
.

For CM curves, the image groups
G_{m}
admit an explicit description in terms of class field theory and reciprocity,
and the constructions in Sections
3
–
4
can be viewed as producing Frobenius elements landing in
C_{m}
.
The obstruction arising in the counterexamples presented above when
6\mid k
may be interpreted as the emptiness of
C_{k}
. On the other hand, if
E/\mathbb{Q}
is a non-CM elliptic curve, then we have from Serre’s open image theorem
[
18
]
that
\overline{\rho}_{E,\ell}
is surjective for all sufficiently large primes
\ell
. Hence if
m=\mathrm{rad}(k)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\ell_{i}
, where
\ell_{i}
’s are sufficiently large primes, one can deduce using Chinese remainder theorem that
\overline{\rho}_{E,m}
is also surjective; i.e.,
G_{m}\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})
. For any
a\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
such that
a\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{\ell_{i}}
, let

A=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&-a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Then it is clear that
A\in C_{m}
. Hence, by (
6.3
),
L_{k}
is arithmetically exceptional, implying that Conjecture
20
holds for any such
k
. Finally, we pose the following problem, which might be of interest to readers.

Problem 21
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve without CM and let
k\geq 2
, with
m=\mathrm{rad}(k)
.
Give an explicit criterion, in terms of the images of the representations
\rho_{E,m}
that decides whether
C_{m}
is empty.

The notion of arithmetic exceptionality extends naturally to rational functions over a number field
K
, with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
replaced by the residue field
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
at a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Therefore, it would also be interesting to investigate these problems for elliptic curves defined over
K
.
