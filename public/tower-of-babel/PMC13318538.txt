The paradox of treatment: Ciliary effusion induced by travoprost – A case report
Authors: Francisca Teixeira da Costa Maia, Gabriel José Morgado Santos
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis case highlights a rare association between prostaglandin analog therapy and isolated ciliary body effusion.ObservationsTravoprost is a topical prostaglandin analog frequently prescribed as an intraocular pressure-lowering agent. We report the case of an 84-year-old pseudophakic female with primary open-angle glaucoma who developed ciliary effusion after long-term travoprost use. Patient reported decreased vision in her left eye over two weeks. Ophthalmological examination revealed decreased visual acuity, myopic shift, shallowed anterior chamber, and increased intraocular pressure in the left eye. Fundoscopy was unremarkable. Anterior segment optical coherence tomography revealed presumed ciliary body effusion with anterior displacement of the iris-intraocular lens complex. Significant resolution occurred within one week after discontinuing travoprost.Conclusions and importanceIsolated ciliary effusion should be recognized as a rare adverse effect of travoprost, with drug discontinuation playing both diagnostic and therapeutic roles.Keywords: Ciliary effusion, Side effect, Travoprost

Introduction

This case report describes ciliary body effusion induced by travoprost; a topical prostaglandin analog (PGA) used as an intraocular pressure (IOP)-lowering agent to manage glaucoma. The significance of this report lies in documenting this rare adverse event. Raising awareness for this potential complication represents the first step towards timely diagnosis and effective treatment.

Methods

This is a case report from the Ophthalmology Department at Unidade Local de Saúde de Braga. Written informed consent was obtained from the patient to publish this case report and accompanying images. There are no conflicts of interest to declare.

Clinical case presentation

An 84-year-old pseudophakic female with a history of bilateral open-angle glaucoma, managed with trabeculectomy in the right eye (RE) and medical treatment in the left eye (LE) (topical travoprost [0.003%] daily for the past three years), presented with decreased vision in the LE for the past two weeks. There was no other significant medical or surgical history, including the use of any medication previously associated with uveal effusion. There was no history of recent events or introduction of new medications. On ophthalmological examination, her LE best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) was 0.6 using the Decimal chart (comparing to 0.8 at previous evaluation six months earlier), showing a myopic shift with subjective refraction changing from −2.25 diopters to −4.25 -0.50 at 150°. Slit-lamp examination of the anterior segment revealed a shallowed anterior chamber. Fundoscopy showed no signs of choroidal effusion. IOP was 22 mmHg (compared to 16 mmHg at the previous evaluation six months earlier). The RE examination revealed no new clinical findings, with an IOP of 9 mmHg. Anterior segment optical coherence tomography (AS-OCT) demonstrated LE anteriorization of the iris-intraocular lens (IOL) complex, with an anterior chamber depth (ACD) of 2.18 mm, consistent with presumed ciliary effusion (Fig. 1A), while RE exam was unremarkable. Posterior segment optical coherence tomography revealed no new macular or optic nerve abnormalities in either eye.

Anterior segment optical coherence tomography (AS-OCT) of an 84-year-old pseudophakic female with presumed travoprost-induced ciliary effusion in the left eye. (A) At presentation, while on topical travoprost, demonstrating anteriorization of the iris-intraocular lens (IOL) complex with a reduced anterior chamber depth (ACD) of 2.18 mm, consistent with presumed ciliary effusion. (B) Two weeks after travoprost discontinuation, demonstrating posterior repositioning of the iris-IOL complex with normalization of the ACD to 3.77 mm.

There was the clinical suspicion that this was related to the use of travoprost, as LE was the only eye treated with a PGA. It was discontinued and replaced with topical fixed combination of brinzolamide 1% and timolol 0.5%, administered twice daily. One week later, the patient reported significant improvement in LE vision. Two weeks later, her BCVA was 0.8, with a subjective refraction of −2.00 -1.00 at 170°. Slit-lamp examination revealed a wide anterior chamber. AS-OCT confirmed the increase in ACD to 3.77 mm (Fig. 1B). IOP was 16 mmHg. Fundoscopy remained unremarkable.

Discussion

Glaucoma is a multifactorial, progressive optic neuropathy that significantly affects patients’ vision and quality of life. Travoprost is a F2α PGA. PGAs lower IOP by enhancing uveoscleral outflow through metalloproteinase-induced remodeling of type I, III, and IV collagen in the ciliary muscle and sclera, reducing resistance.1,2 These agents are systemically safe, but their local side effects are frequent. The most common include hyperpigmentation of the periocular skin and iris, conjunctival hyperemia, and eyelash anomalies such as hypertrichosis, trichiasis and distichiasis. Some patients experience complex periorbital alterations collectively known as prostaglandin-associated periorbitopathy. Inflammatory side effects, such as anterior granulomatous uveitis and macular edema, are less frequent, and their exact mechanism is not entirely understood. However, they include a disturbance in the blood-aqueous barrier.

Several case reports in the literature describe uveal effusion associated with topical PGA use, including travoprost, occurring immediately or after prolonged treatment. These cases mainly presented as choroidal detachment with or without exudative retinal detachment3, 4, 5, 6, which resolved thoroughly after drug discontinuation. Described associations include anterior segment surgery, such as cataract surgery7 (pseudophakic, as in the presented case, or aphakic eyes), as well as increased episcleral venous pressure related to Sturge-Weber Syndrome.5,6 The pathogenesis is still unclear, although the proposed underlying mechanisms include the increased outflow of aqueous humor resulting in its accumulation in the suprachoroidal space as well as the proinflammatory effects with increased choroidal vascular permeability, already established as a contributing factor to PGA-induced macular edema.7 Some authors suggest hypotony as a contributing factor, but this was not documented in this case.

Notably, an uncommon manifestation of PGA–associated uveal effusion was observed in the present case, confined to isolated ciliary body involvement. The serous fluid exudation to the supraciliary space caused ciliary body detachment along with the ciliary body's rotation and anterior shift, pushing the iris-IOL diaphragm forward, closing the angle and resulting in IOP elevation. The same mechanism occurs in secondary acute closed-angle glaucoma related to sulfonamide drugs like topiramate.8 The unilateral involvement (making a systemic cause less likely) and the rapid resolution following drug discontinuation suggest a causal relationship between travoprost use and the observed uveal effusion.

Sakai et al. reported a phakic patient who developed a circumferential ciliochoroidal effusion 18 days after initiating latanoprost, likely due to increased episcleral venous pressure from Sturge-Weber syndrome counteracting the enhanced uveoscleral outflow induced by PGA.6 In contrast, our pseudophakic patient developed an isolated ciliary body effusion after three years of travoprost therapy, with no systemic or ocular predisposing factors. Both cases showed anterior chamber narrowing and resolved within approximately 20 days after discontinuation, consistent with the washout period of PGAs.9

Our report highlights a rare but significant complication of travoprost, a frequently prescribed glaucoma medication. This report raises awareness of uveal effusion, particularly ciliary effusion, in patients on travoprost who present with decreased vision, myopic shift, and increased IOP. When suspected, discontinuing the medication can serve as diagnostic test and treatment, preventing unnecessary investigation and burden for health care facilities and patients.
