Quantum fidelity on Krein and S S -spaces
Morgan Jones Department of Mathematics University of Oklahoma Norman, Oklahoma, USA morgan.d.jones-2@ou.edu

Abstract




1.
Abstract

The notion of Fidelity for quantum states is a measure of how much two states overlap. In the matrix formalism of quantum mechanics, states are represented by density operators i.e. positive semi-definite matrices with trace equal to
1
in a complex Euclidean space
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. In
[
6
]
, the notion of quantum states on certain Krein spaces with indefinite metric induced by a fundamental symmetry
J
is introduced, these are given the name
J
-states. We will define an analogous notion of measurement for
J
-states to the regular quantum theory and use it to show that a notion of fidelity holds in the Krein setting. We will also show that there exists an analogous result to the Fuchs-Caves measurement holds in this setting. We will then, following the developments in
[
2
]
, extend this definition of fidelity to
U
-quantum states on
S
-spaces. We will demonstrate that the analogous geometric motivation holds in the Krein and
S
-space setting, as holds for quantum fidelity and geometric means of operators.

2.
Introduction

Before we consider the notions of Krein and
S
-space fidelity, we recall the basics of quantum fidelity. We will keep this in line, as in the rest of this paper, with the spirit of
[
13
]
and in this introduction we will recall various useful facts from Chapters 1,2 and 3 from this reference. An alphabet
\Sigma
is a finite set of
n
elements. The space
\mathbb{C}^{\Sigma}
consists of complex functions
u:\Sigma\to\mathbb{C}
. For example if
\Sigma=\{1,\dots,n\}
, then each
u\in\mathbb{C}^{\Sigma}
satisfies

u=(u(1),u(2),\dots,u(n))

This can then be endowed with the standard Euclidean inner-product for such vectors
u
and
v

\langle u,v\rangle u\cdot v=\sum_{a\in\Sigma}\overline{u(a)}v(a)

We note of course that this has the different convention to abstract functional analysis where the complex conjugate is taken in the second factor.

A register
X
is the abstraction of the idea of a set of finite values that some system could take. This could be in the form of an alphabet
\Sigma
, or the cartesian product of several smaller registers. If
X
is a register represented by the alphabet
\Sigma
then
\mathbb{C}^{\Sigma}
is the complex Euclidean space associated to this register. If the register is a compound register
X=(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})
, then this complex Euclidean space is

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{Y}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{n}

Given a complex Euclidean space
\mathcal{X}
, a quantum state is represented by a positive semidefinite linear operator from
\mathcal{X}
to itself with trace equal to
1
. Such matrices are called density operators. Positive semidefinite operators form a cone
\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{X})
, in the vector space of all linear operators on the space
\mathcal{X}
. In physical systems, one obtains information about quantum states via “measurements”. The quantum information formalism of a measurement is a function

\mu:\Sigma\to\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{X})

such that
\sum_{a\in\Sigma}\mu(a)=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}}
. The formalism is that if a system is in the state
P
just before measurement, the probability of picking a given element
a
of a register
\Sigma
is given by
\langle\mu(a),P\rangle
, wher
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the Hilbert-Schmidt inner-product. Given two positive semidefinite operators
P
and
Q
, the quantity known as fidelity is defined as

F(P,Q)=\left\|\sqrt{P}\sqrt{Q}\right\|_{1}=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\sqrt{\sqrt{Q}P\sqrt{Q}}\right)

where the Schatten
1
-norm is taken. This definition is actually the minimization of the quantity

\sum_{a\in\Sigma}\langle\sqrt{\mu(a),P\rangle}\sqrt{\langle\mu(a),Q\rangle}

over all possible measurements
\mu
. This geometrically is the minimization over all possible measurements
\mu
of the marginal distribution of probability vectors
p
and
q

\sum_{a\in\Sigma}\sqrt{p_{a}q_{a}}

where the
a
-th entries of
p
and
q
are
\langle\mu(a),P\rangle
and
\langle\mu(a),Q\rangle
respectively.
The question we will address is what happens when we equip the space of complex linear operators with an indefinite inner product. The first indefinite setting we will consider is that of the Krein space. A Krein space is a space
\mathcal{H}
equipped with an indefinite nondegenerate inner-product
[\cdot,\cdot]

. After we have developed this theory, we will then move into the setting of

4.2.
Finite-dimensional Krein spaces

The following subsection follows from
[
7
]

. A Krein space, in general, is an indefinite inner-product space, where vectors are allowed to have non-positive inner-products with themselves. This is achieved by an involution, normally denoted
J\in L(\mathcal{X})
. There is also the majorant topology property of Krein spaces, but this is irrelevant for us and will not come up, so we take the following to be our definition.

Definition 4.4
.

[
7
]

A Krein space
(\mathcal{X},[\cdot,\cdot]_{J})
is an inner-product space
(\mathcal{X},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
along with a self-adjoint involution
J\in L(\mathcal{X})
where

[\cdot,\cdot]_{J}=\langle J\cdot,\cdot\rangle

and such that
[\cdot,\cdot]
is nondegenerate.

When we say terms such as “Krein matrix”, we mean a matrix as in the above definition.

Since every space we consider is finite-dimensional, we can replace inner-product with Hilbert in the above definition.

In the Krein space, we define
J
-multiplication as follows;

\forall A,B\in L(\mathcal{X}),A\bullet B=AJB

We list several important definitions.

Definition 4.5
.

[
7
]
Let
\mathcal{X}
be a complex Euclidean space and
J
a Krein matrix.

•

The cone of
J
-positive definite matrices,
\mathrm{Pd}_{J}(\mathcal{X})
is defined as the set of

such that

[Ax,x]_{J}>0

for all

nonzero.

•

The cone of
J
-positive semi-definite matrices,
\mathrm{Pos}_{J}(\mathcal{X})
is defined as the set of
A\in L(\mathcal{X})
such that

[Ax,x]_{J}\geq 0

for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

Definition 4.6
.

[
7

Let

,

•

The
J

A^{\#}=JA^{\#}J

[Ax,y]_{J}=[x,A^{*}y]_{J}

•

The

Remark 4.7
.

We notice that any

5.1.
Krein Space States and Measurements

Definition 5.1

[

Given a Krein space

In the regular quantum theory, the notion of measurement is central to the concept of fidelity. Given a complex Euclidean space
\mathcal{X}

\Sigma

\mu:\Sigma\to\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{X})
which satisfies

\Sigma_{a\in\Sigma}\mu(a)=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}}

This definition is made with the understanding that if

represents the state of a register
X

\Sigma
is selected at random, the probability distribution that describes this random selection is the probability vector
p

\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}
such that

p(a)=\langle\mu(a),P\rangle

[
13
, p. 101]
.
Using this formalism, we make the analogous notion for
J

Definition 5.2
.

Given a finite dimensional Euclidean space
\mathcal{X}
, an alphabet
\Sigma
and a fundamental symmetry
J
on
\mathcal{X}
, a
J
-measurement is a function

\mu:\Sigma\to\mathrm{Pos}_{J}(\mathcal{X})

such that
\sum_{a\in\Sigma}\mu(a)=J
.

We make this definition with the analogous measurement interpretation. If the space Krein space
\mathcal{X}
is in the state
\rho
when an element of
\Sigma
is selected at random, the probability distribution that describes this selection is given by

where

5.2.
Definition of
J
-fidelity

5.2.1.
Motivation for definition

In quantum information, fidelity is motivated by the following: given density operators
P,Q\in D(\mathcal{X})
, we aim to minimize the value of

\sum_{a\in\Sigma}\sqrt{p(a)}\sqrt{q(a)}

over all possible measurements where
a
belongs to the alphabet
\Sigma
, and
p,q\in\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)
, and are the vectors produced when we take a particular measurement of a space
\mathcal{X}
which is in the state
P
and
Q
. The values
\sqrt{p(a)}\sqrt{q(a)}
are the Bhattacharyya coefficients
[
13
]
. It was shown by Fuchs and Caves
[
8
]
that the optimal measurement for achieving the fidelity is to measure in the eigenbasis of the geometric mean of the states
P
and
Q^{-1}
;

Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\sqrt{Q}P\sqrt{Q}}Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}

from which the fidelity is defined as

\mathrm{Tr}\left(\sqrt{\sqrt{Q}P\sqrt{Q}}\right)

Hence our, definition of
J
-fidelity should be, given two
J
-states
P
and
Q
(operators
P,Q\in\mathrm{Pos}_{J}(\mathcal{X})
such that
JP,JQ
are density operators
[
6
]
), the minimization over all possible
J
-measurements
\mu
of

\sum_{a\in\Sigma}\sqrt{\langle\mu(a),P\rangle}\sqrt{\langle\mu(a),Q\rangle}

We will denote this value
F_{J}(P,Q)
.

Theorem 5.3
.

The
J
-fidelity is obtained by the value

F_{J}(P,Q)=F(JP,JQ)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\sqrt{\sqrt{{JQ}}JP\sqrt{{JQ}}}\right)

Remark 5.4
.

A measurement that achieve fidelity, in the invertible case, is found by considering the eigenbasis of the
J
-geometric mean of
P
and the
J

Q
in
\mathrm{Pd}_{J}(\mathcal{X})
, which is given by

(5.1)

(Q)^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{J}\bullet\sqrt[J]{(\sqrt[J]{Q})\bullet P\bullet\sqrt[J]{Q}}\bullet(Q)^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{J}

Proof.

We follow the program contained in
[
13

and the program of proof contained in

14
]
to verify the minimization. But, we first verify that the matrix

Assume first of all that

and

are invertible. We see that the the above matrix defined is

\displaystyle(Q)_{J}^{-\frac{1}{2}}JJ\sqrt{JJ\sqrt{JQ}JPJJ\sqrt{JQ}}J(Q)_{J}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(Q_{J}^{\frac{1}{2}})_{J}^{-1}\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}J(Q_{J}^{\frac{1}{2}})_{J}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J(J\sqrt{JQ})^{-1}J\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}JJ(J\sqrt{JQ})^{-1}J

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J(\sqrt{JQ})^{-1}\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}(\sqrt{JQ})^{-1}

and since

We then show that the optimal measurement is with respect to

Multiplying the square roots out this becomes

We then have

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}((Jx_{i}x_{i}^{*})^{*}P)\mathrm{Tr}((Jx_{i}x_{i}^{*})^{*}Q)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(x_{i}x_{i}^{*}JP)\mathrm{Tr}((x_{i}x_{i}^{*}JQ)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\sqrt{(x_{i}^{*}RJQRx_{i})(x_{i}^{*}JQx_{i})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\sqrt{(x_{i}^{*}\lambda_{i}JQ\lambda_{i}x_{i})(x_{i}^{*}JQx_{i})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(JP,JQ)

∎

7.
Weighted Krein space fidelity

The definition of
J
-fidelity is in some ways unattractive as it completely ignores the decomposition of the space into positive and negative parts. There are several possible notions of “weighted”
J
-fidelity. One candidate is to take the regular trace instead of the
J
-trace in the definition of fidelity,

F_{J}^{W}(P,Q)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(J\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}\right)

This definition does have some benefits, especially when
JP
and
JQ
can be expressed in block diagonal form with respect to the decomposition of the space into positive and negative parts. Furthermore, given that
P
and
Q
are
J
-states, we have

-1\leq F_{J}^{W}(P,Q)\leq 1

since

\displaystyle F_{J}^{W}(P,Q)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Pi_{1}\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}-\Pi_{2}\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Pi_{1}\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}\right)-\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Pi_{2}\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left((\Pi_{1}^{2}\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}\right)-\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Pi_{2}^{2}\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Pi_{1}\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}\Pi_{1}\right)-\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Pi_{2}\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}\Pi_{2}\right)

where
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{2}
are the associated projections onto the positive and negative parts of the space.
Since the terms in this subtraction are the traces of projected positive semi-definite operators, whose trace is bounded above by
1
, we then obtain the desired bound. If
|F^{W}_{J}(P,Q)|=1
, then we have

F^{W}_{J}(P,Q)=F_{J}(P,Q)

which then implies
JP=JQ
, and hence
P=Q
. We also then must have that
JP
is invariant under the projections
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{2}
, which implies that
JP
is a singular positive semi-definite operator which is block diagonal with
0
s in the top left or bottom right block. Since a positive semi-definite matrix with a zero block in the top left or bottom right block also must have zero blocks on the off-diagonal blocks (see
[
13
, pp. 144–146]
), we know then that
JP
must be of the form

JP=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

or

JP=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&-A\end{pmatrix}

If the upper bound of
1
is achieved

\displaystyle 1=\left|\mathrm{Tr}\left(J\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|J\|_{\infty}\left\|\sqrt{\sqrt{JQ}JP\sqrt{JQ}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\sqrt{JPJQJPJQ}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}(JPJQ)

\displaystyle\leq\|JP\|_{1}\|JQ\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 1

which means that these inequalities are really equalities. This leads us to the following.

Theorem 7.1
.

J
-quantum states
P
and
Q
satisfy
|F_{J}^{W}(P,Q)|=1
if and only if
P=Q
and
P
is invariant under projection by onto the first or second factor in the decomposition of the space.

Proof.

The only if direction follows from the fact that if

P=Q=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

then
F_{J}^{W}(P,Q)=\mathrm{Tr}(A)
with an analogous result holding for

P=Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&A\end{pmatrix}

The previous exposition shows that if
F_{J}^{W}(P,Q)=1
, we have that
P
must be of the desired form.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

It was observed in conversation with Doctor Javier Alejandro Chavez-Dominguez that since the above implies that

, Theorem

also implies that

So we see this definition has some nice properties, but for non block diagonal operators, the

and

which provide unitary

In keeping with the fact that any Hilbert space can be embedded into a larger Krein space by just extending every vector by zeros with respect to some basis and declaring the additional dimensions to be the “negative part”

respectively where
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{2}
are the orthogonal projections onto
\mathcal{X}_{1}
and
\mathcal{X}_{2}
respectively. For
X,Y\in L(\mathcal{X})
, we can consider the fidelity of their projections and consider the difference between these two values to provide a notion of weighted fidelity, we will call this measurement weighted fidelity and define it as

F_{J}^{M}(P,Q)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\sqrt{\sqrt{\Pi_{1}JQ\Pi_{1}}\Pi_{1}JP\Pi_{1}\sqrt{\Pi_{1}JQ\Pi_{1}}}\right)-\mathrm{Tr}\left(\sqrt{\sqrt{\Pi_{2}JQ\Pi_{2}}\Pi_{2}JP\Pi_{2}\sqrt{\Pi_{2}JQ\Pi_{2}}}\right)

This definition is commutative, satisfies the same bounds, and is easier to handle in general because the decomposition of the space is taken into account inside the square root. However, we lose the off diagonal blocks of
P
and
Q
in the
J

J
-fidelity.

7.1.
Elementary properties of weighted measurement fidelity

We collect a few results by applying regular fidelity properties contained
[
13
]
to this notion of weighted measurement fidelity.

Lemma 7.3
.

Given
P,Q\in\mathrm{{Pos}}_{J}(\mathcal{X})
, we have the following holds

•

The measurement weighted fidelity is continuous at
(P,Q)
.

•

F_{J}^{M}(\lambda P,Q)=F_{J}^{M}(P,\lambda Q)=\sqrt{\lambda}F_{J}^{M}(P,Q)
for all real

.

•

F_{J}^{M}(P,Q)=\|\sqrt{\Pi_{1}JP\Pi_{1}}\sqrt{\Pi_{1}JQ\Pi_{1}}\|_{1}-\|\sqrt{\Pi_{2}JP\Pi_{2}}\sqrt{\Pi_{2}JQ\Pi_{2}}\|_{1}

•

-1\leq F_{J}^{M}(P,Q)\leq 1
.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a complex Euclidean space with Krein matrix
J
and let
P,Q\in\mathrm{Pos}_{J}(\mathcal{X})
and let

\displaystyle\max\{|\mathrm{Tr}(X)|:X\in L(\mathcal{X}_{1}),\begin{pmatrix}\Pi_{1}JP\Pi_{1}&X\\

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\max\{|\mathrm{Tr}(Y)|:Y\in L(\mathcal{X}_{2}),\begin{pmatrix}\Pi_{2}JP\Pi_{2}&Y\\
Y^{*}&\Pi_{2}JQ\Pi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{X}_{2}\oplus\mathcal{X}_{2})\}

We have by
[

, p. 144]
that

F(\Pi_{1}JP\Pi_{1},\Pi_{1}JQ\Pi_{1})=\max\left\{|\mathrm{Tr}(X)|:X\in L(\mathcal{X}_{1}),\begin{pmatrix}\Pi_{1}JP\Pi&X\\
X^{*}&\Pi_{1}JQ\Pi_{1}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{X}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{X}_{1})\right\}

and

F(\Pi_{2}JP\Pi_{2},\Pi_{2}JP\Pi_{2})=\max\left\{|\mathrm{Tr}(Y)|:Y\in L(\mathcal{X}_{2}),\begin{pmatrix}\Pi_{2}JP\Pi_{2}&Y\\
Y^{*}&\Pi_{2}JQ\Pi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{X}_{2}\oplus\mathcal{X}_{2})\right\}

and subtracting these values gives us the weighted measurement fidelity.

8.
Quantum
U
-channels on
S
-spaces:

The notion of Krein quantum states has recently been extended in
[
2
]
to complex Euclidean spaces, where instead of
J
, we use a general unitary to induce an indefinite inner-product on the underlying Hilbert space. Such a space is called an
S
-space. We show that we the expected notion of fidelity from the Krein case extends naturally from to the case of the
S
-space. In this section all underlying spaces are complex Euclidean.

8.1.
Some basic definitions and properties of S-spaces:

Definition 8.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Hilbert space and let
U\in U(\mathcal{X})
. The space
\mathcal{X}
endowed with the sesquilinear form

\displaystyle[x,y]_{U}=\langle U^{*}x,y\rangle\,\,\,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}

is called an
S
-space
[
2
]
.

Analogously to the Krein case, there is a natural notion of the adjoint map

Definition 8.2

(
U
-adjoint.)
.

[
2
]

For every
V\in L(\mathcal{X})
, there exists an operator
V^{\#}=U^{*}V^{*}U
such that

\displaystyle[Vx,y]_{U}=[x,V^{\#}y]_{U}

Indeed, we have

[x,V^{\#}]_{U}=\langle U^{*}x,U^{*}V^{*}Uy\rangle=\langle x,V^{*}Uy\rangle=\langle U^{*}Vx,y\rangle=[Vx,y]_{U}

Definition 8.3

(Cone of
U
-positive semi-definite matrices.)
.

A matrix
P\in L(\mathcal{X})
is
U
positive if
U^{*}P\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{X})
[
2
]
. We let
\mathrm{Pos}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
denote the cone of
U
-positive semi-definite matrices.

Using the sesquilinear form given by
U
, we can endow a different geometry on
\mathrm{Pos}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
by following a similar program to
[
7
]
, in which multiplication is given by

A\bullet B=AU^{*}B

With this definition of multiplication, the matrix
U
acts as the identity since

U\bullet A=UU^{*}A=AU^{*}U=A

Using this definition of multiplication, we may also define several other important notions

Definition 8.4

(
U
-inverse)
.

A matrix
A\in L(\mathcal{X})
is invertible if and only if there exists a
B\in L(\mathcal{X})
such that
A\bullet B=B\bullet A=U
. This matrix
B
must be given by
B=UA^{-1}U

Definition 8.5

(
U
-square root)
.

Given
A\in\mathrm{Pos}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
, we define the
U
-square root to be

A_{U}^{\frac{1}{2}}=U\sqrt{U^{*}A}

We see that the above definition then satisfies

A_{U}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bullet A_{U}^{\frac{1}{2}}=U\sqrt{U^{*}A}U^{*}U\sqrt{U^{*}A}=U(U^{*}A)=A

Proposition 8.6
.

For all invertible
A\in L(\mathcal{X})
, we have
APA^{\#}\in\mathrm{Pos}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
. In other words, the group of
|\Sigma|\times|\Sigma|
invertible matrices in
L(\mathcal{X})
acts as a transitive group action by
U
-conjugation on
\mathrm{Pos}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
.

Definition 8.7
.

We define the
U
-exponential as the map

\exp_{U}(X)=U\exp(U^*X)
which restricts to a bijection

\exp_{U}(X):\mathrm{Herm}_{U}(\mathcal{X})\to\mathrm{Pd}_{U}(\mathcal{X})

The definition of
\mathrm{Herm}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
is obviously the
X\in L(\mathcal{X})
invariant under
U
-conjugation. From the above we can also define
\log_{U}(X)
to be the inverse of
\exp_{U}
when we restrict
\exp_{U}
to
\mathrm{Herm}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
. We can also consider the quantity

\exp_{U}(t\log_{U}(X))=X_{U}^{t}

, as in the
J

X\in\mathrm{Pd}_{U}(\mathcal{X})

8.2.
Geodesics in
\mathrm{Pd}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
.

In this subsection, we repeat some of the program carried out in
[
7
]
, but now in the
S
-space setting.

Let
\Phi_{U}:\mathrm{Pd}_{U}(\mathcal{X})\to\mathrm{Pd}(\mathcal{X})
be the map
\Phi_{U}(X)=U^{*}X
and define
\omega^{U}=\Phi^{*}_{U}(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
. This then satisfies for all

\omega^{U}_{P}(R,S)=\Phi^{*}(\langle R,S\rangle)=\langle U^{*}R,U^{*}S\rangle_{U^{*}P}

for

. Indeed, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle U^{*}R,U^{*}S\rangle_{U^{*}P}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}((U^{*}P)^{-1}U^{*}R(U^{*}P)^{-1}U^{*}S)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}(P^{-1}UU^{*}RP^{-1}UU^{*}S)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}(P^{-1}RP^{-1}S)

Theorem 8.8
.

Let
A,B\in\mathrm{Pd}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
. The map
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathrm{Pd}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
given by

\gamma(t)=A_{U}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bullet((A_{U}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\bullet B\bullet(A_{U}^{-\frac{1}{2}}))_{U}^{t}\bullet A_{U}^{\frac{1}{2}}

is the equation of the geodesic between
A
and
B
in
\mathrm{Pd}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
with respect to
\omega^{U}
.

Proof.

Let
\delta
be the equation of the geodesic between
U^{*}A
and
U^{*}B
in
\mathrm{Pd}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
. This is the curve

\delta(t)=\sqrt{U^{*}A}((\sqrt{U^{*}A})^{-1}U^{*}B(\sqrt{U^{*}A})^{-1})^{t}\sqrt{U^{*}A}

Since multiplication by
U
on
\mathrm{Pd}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
is an isometry of Riemannian manifolds from
(\mathrm{Pd}_{U}(\mathcal{X}),\omega^{U})
to
\mathrm{Pd}(\mathcal{X})
, we need to show that
\gamma(t)=U\delta(t)
for all

. Since

\displaystyle\exp_{U}(t\log_{U}(X))=U\exp(tU^*U\log(U^*X))=U\exp(t\log(U^*X))=U(U^{*}X)^{t}

we have that
X_{U}^{t}=U(U^{*}X)^{t}
for all

. So

{A_{U}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\bullet B\bullet{A_{U}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}=(U(U^{*}A)^{-\frac{1}{2}})(U^{*}BU^{*})(U(U^{*}A)^{-\frac{1}{2}})=U(U^{*}A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(U^{*}B)(U^{*}A)^{-\frac{1}{2}})

Hence we have

\displaystyle\gamma(t)

\displaystyle=A_{U}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bullet((A_{U}^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{-1}\bullet B\bullet(A_{U}^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{-1})_{U}^{t}\bullet A_{U}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=U(U^{*}A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}U^{*}U(U^{*}U(U^{*}A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(U^{*}B)(U^{*}A)^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{t}U^{*}U(U^{*}A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=U(U^{*}A)^{\frac{1}{2}}((U^{*}A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(U^{*}B)(U^{*}A)^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{t}(U^{*}A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=U\delta(t)

8.4.
Motivation of S-space fidelity.

In

J

Given two

across all possible

\mu

\sum_{a\in\Sigma}\sqrt{\langle U^{*}\mu(a),U^{*}P\rangle}\sqrt{\langle U^{*}\mu(a),U^{*}Q\rangle}

we have by the regular quantum theory
that the minimization of this over all

is given by

F(U^{*}P,U^{*}Q)=\mathrm{Tr}(\sqrt{U^{*}Q}U^{*}P\sqrt{U^{*}Q})=\|\sqrt{U^{*}P}\sqrt{U^{*}Q}\|_{1}

Definition 8.12
.

Given two
U
-quantum states,
P
and

-fidelity of

and
Q
to be

F_{U}(P,Q)=F(U^{*}P,U^{*}Q)

We now verify for completeness that this corresponds to taking a

-geometric’ mean of

and

. Again, we are just following the analysis of Chapter 3 of

]

We then show that the optimal measurement is with respect to

since

\displaystyle UR

\displaystyle=Q_{U}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bullet\sqrt[U]{Q_{U}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bullet P\bullet Q_{U}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\bullet Q_{U}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=U(U^{*}Q)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sqrt{U^{*}U\sqrt{U^{*}Q}U^{*}PU^{*}U(\sqrt{U^{*}Q})})U^{*}U(U^{*}Q)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

We then have that

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(U^{*}P,U^{*}Q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{U}(P,Q)

, p. 143]
that

\displaystyle F_{U}\left(\rho,\frac{U\sqrt{U^{*}P}U^{*}\rho\sqrt{U^{*}P}}{\langle U^{*}P,U^{*}\rho\rangle}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\langle U^{*}P,U^{*}\rho\rangle}}F(U^{*}\rho,\sqrt{U^{*}P}U^{*}\rho\sqrt{U^{*}P})=\frac{\langle\sqrt{U^{*}P},U^{*}\rho\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle U^{*}P,U^{*}\rho\rangle}}

and from the assumption on
U^{*}P
we have the result follows.
∎

8.6.
Characterizing S-space fidelity

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a complex Euclidean space with unitary inner-product
[\cdot,\cdot]_{U}
, and let
P,Q\in\mathrm{Pos}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
. We have

\displaystyle F_{U}(P,Q)=\max\left\{\left|\mathrm{Tr}(X)\right|:X\in L(\mathcal{X}),\;\begin{bmatrix}U^{*}P&X\\
X^{*}&U^{*}Q\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{X}\oplus\mathcal{X})\right\}.

In the exact same way as seen in
[
13
, p. 147]
, we obtain the semi-definite program view of fidelity in that the
U
-fidelity
F_{U}(P,Q)
gives the optimal value of the primal problem given by maximizing

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}(X)+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}(X^{*})

subject to

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}U^{*}P&X\\
X^{*}&U^{*}Q\end{bmatrix}\geq 0

for
X\in L(\mathcal{X})
.
We may also obtain the
U
-formulation of Alberti’s theorem

Theorem 8.17

(
U
version of Alberti’s theorem)
.

For
P,Q\in\mathrm{Pos}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
we have

\displaystyle F_{U}(P,Q)^{2}=\inf\{[P,Y]_{U}[Q,Y]_{U}^{-1}:Y\in\mathrm{Pd}_{U}(\mathcal{X})]\}

Proof.

See
[
13
, pp. 148–149]
.
∎

We can also consider
U
-partial traces as in the
J
-case. If
P\in\mathrm{Pos}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
, then there exists a complex Euclidean space
\mathcal{Y}
and a vector
u\in\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
such that

\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}}((U\otimes I)(U^{*}\otimes I)uu^{*}))=U^{*}P

if and only if
\dim(\mathcal{Y})\geq\mathrm{rank}(P)
. We call the function
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}}(U\cdot)
the
U
-partial trace and similarly, we consider the
U
trace to be
\mathrm{Tr}(U\cdot)
(remembering
U\otimes I
is still a unitary) and we will denote the
U
-partial trace
\mathrm{Tr}^{U}_{\mathcal{Y}}
and the
U
-trace
\mathrm{Tr}^{U}
.

8.7.
U-analog of Uhlmann’s theorem

Again, we are able to adapt Uhlmann’s theorem to the
U
-case.

Lemma 8.18
.

Let
A,B\in L(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{X})
be operators for complex Euclidean spaces
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
and endow
\mathcal{X}
with unitary
U
. We have

\displaystyle F_{U}(UAA^{*},UBB^{*})=\|A^{*}B\|_{1}

Proof.

We have that

F_{U}(UAA^{*},UBB^{*})=F(AA^{*},BB^{*})

from which we may then apply the proof of Lemmma 3.21 of
[
13
]
to the operators
AA^{*}
and
BB^{*}
.
∎

Theorem 8.19
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be complex Euclidean spaces and let
U_{1}
be unitary matrix for
\mathcal{X}
and
U_{2}
be a unitary matrix for
\mathcal{Y}
and let
P,Q\in\mathrm{Pos}_{U}(\mathcal{X})
be U-positive semi-definite operators having rank at most
\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{Y})
and let
u\in\mathcal{X}\otimes{Y}
satisfy
\mathrm{Tr}^{U_{1}\otimes U_{2}}_{\mathcal{Y}}((U_{1}^{*}\otimes U_{2}^{*}uu^{*}))=U_{1}^{*}P
. It holds that

\displaystyle F_{U}(P,Q)=\max\{|\langle u,v\rangle|:v\in\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y},\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{U_{1}\otimes U_{2}}((U_{1}^{*}\otimes U_{2}^{*})vv^{*})\}

Proof.

We adapt the proof contained in
[
13
]
with the appropriate adjustments.
Let
A\in L(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{X})
be the operator such that
u=\mathrm{vec}(A)
and let
w\in\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
be the vector satisfying
U^{*}_{1}Q=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{U_{1}\otimes U_{2}}((U_{1}\otimes U_{2})ww^{*})
and let
B\in L(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{X})
be the operator for which
w=\mathrm{vec}(B)
. We then have

\displaystyle\mathrm{max}\{|\langle u,v\rangle|:v\in\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y},\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{U_{1}\otimes U_{2}}((U_{1}^{*}\otimes U_{2}^{*})vv^{*})=U_{1}^{*}Q\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{|\langle u,(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes S)w\rangle|:S\in U(\mathcal{Y})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max{|\langle A,BS^{T}\rangle|:S\in U(\mathcal{Y})}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{|\langle\overline{S},A^{*}B\rangle|:S\in U(\mathcal{Y})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|A^{*}B\|_{1}=F_{U}(U_{1}AA^{*},U_{1}BB^{*})=F_{U}(P,Q)

∎

Corollary 8.20
.

Let
u,v\in\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
be vectors for complex Euclidean spaces
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
equipped with unitary matrices
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
, so that
\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
is equipped with unitary matrix
U_{1}\otimes U_{2}
. We have

\displaystyle F_{U_{1}}\Big(\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{U_{1}\otimes U_{2}}\big((U^{*}_{1}\otimes U^{*}_{2})(uu^{*})\big),\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{U_{1}\otimes U_{2}}\big((U^{*}_{1}\otimes U^{*}_{2})(vv^{*})\big)\Big).

Proof.

Again, we follow the method in
[
13
]
.
Let
A,B\in L(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{X})
be such that
u=\mathrm{vec}(A)
and
v=\mathrm{vec}(B)
.

\displaystyle F_{U_{1}}\Big(\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{U_{1}\otimes U_{2}}\big((U^{*}_{1}\otimes U^{*}_{2})(uu^{*})\big),\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{U_{1}\otimes U_{2}}\big((U_{1}^{*}\otimes U_{2}^{*})(vv^{*})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=F_{U_{1}}(U_{1}AA^{*},\,U_{1}BB^{*})

\displaystyle=\|A^{*}B\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|(A^{*}B)^{T}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{X}}^{U_{1}\otimes U_{2}}\big((U_{1}^{*}\otimes U^{*}_{2})(vu^{*})\big)\right\|_{1}.

∎

9.
Further questions:

The cases of
J
-fidelity and
U
-fidelity are a little boring. We are performing the original analysis of quantum fidelity but in various stages we “twist” or “untwist” the cone of positive (semi)-definite matrices. A more interesting question is what if one relaxes the conditions on the map through which our inner-product is defined? Do we still obtain a natural notion of fidelity, which coincides both with the motivation of minimizing certain Bhattacharryya coefficients through measuring in the eigenbasis of some notion of the geometric mean?
