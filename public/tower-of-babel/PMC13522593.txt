THR-DMPOM model for reliable high-precision evaluation to enhance real-time performance management and optimize resource allocation
Authors: Yue Ma, Liangke Cao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address volatility and local optimality issues arising from multidimensional dynamic data in Human Resource Performance Evaluation (HRPE) within the tourism industry, this study proposes a Tourism Human Resource–Deep Momentum Performance Optimization Model (THR-DMPOM). The development of the model begins with the construction of a 24-item evaluation index system. This system was established through an extensive review of HRPE literature and interviewed with more than 300 managers and employees from four representative tourism enterprises. Following multiple rounds of expert screening, 24 key indicators were finalized. Methodologically, THR-DMPOM introduces recursive momentum accumulation, cross-layer weight smoothing, and adaptive gradient correction mechanisms. These components enable continuous dynamic weight updating and historical gradient propagation across multilayer networks, thereby improving stability in nonlinear and time-varying evaluation environments. Extensive experiments were conducted using datasets from four tourism enterprises. The results show that THR-DMPOM achieves high stability and accuracy in practical applications. The systemic deviation remained within ± 1.0, demonstrating strong robustness in handling complex performance data. In distribution consistency validation, the D-values of THR-DMPOM scores remained close to 1, with p-values consistently exceeding 0.7, indicating strong agreement between model-generated results and historical records. Comparative experiments under high-load and unstable network conditions further confirm the superiority of the proposed model. THR-DMPOM outperforms Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (FAHP) and the Fuzzy Technique for Order of Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (F-TOPSIS) in both accuracy and operational efficiency. Compared with traditional AHP, TOPSIS, and static machine learning methods, which struggle to adapt to dynamic performance fluctuations and nonlinear interdependencies among multiple criteria, THR-DMPOM demonstrates a key advantage in adaptive weight learning. The proposed mechanisms enable continuous weight evolution and historical information sharing across network layers, effectively mitigating local optima and weight oscillation issues in dynamic environments. Introduction

Research background and motivations

Human Resource Performance Evaluation (HRPE) is a core component of enterprise management and plays a critical role in optimizing human resource allocation and improving organizational effectiveness1,2. However, in the tourism industry, where operational conditions are highly dynamic, traditional evaluation methods often struggle to provide accurate and real-time feedback under multidimensional and frequently fluctuating data environments3. This limitation highlights the need for more adaptive and data-driven evaluation frameworks. To address complex optimization and dynamic learning problems, deep learning-based momentum optimization methods have been widely explored in recent years. These methods improve gradient updates by incorporating accumulated historical gradients and inertia terms, enabling more stable convergence and reducing oscillations in non-convex optimization landscapes4–6. Such mechanisms have demonstrated effectiveness in escaping local minima and improving learning stability in complex data environments. In parallel, Multi-Criteria Decision-Making (MCDM) methods have evolved to incorporate uncertainty modeling and parsimonious preference representations to improve evaluation robustness. For instance, the Parsimonious Best Worst Method and Z-number-based approaches enhance fuzzy preference representation in transportation decision problems by explicitly modeling decision-maker hesitation7,8. Moreover, hybrid frameworks integrating LOPCOW, CRADIS, MOOSRA, and MAIRCA have been developed for multi-indicator ranking and robust enterprise performance evaluation9. In addition, combining parsimonious preference information with AHP has been shown to improve consistency in sustainability-related evaluations within complex public systems10. Although these MCDM methods improve decision stability under uncertainty, they still predominantly rely on static weighting schemes or stage-wise updates.

As a result, they remain limited in their ability to continuously adapt to dynamically changing performance data, particularly in tourism HRPE scenarios characterized by strong temporal variability and nonlinear interactions among indicators. Motivated by these limitations, this study proposes a Deep Momentum Network (DMN)-based framework for tourism HRPE. DMN integrates a deep momentum optimization mechanism to address issues such as uneven data distribution and inflexible weight adjustment in multi-criteria evaluation. By enabling adaptive weight learning and capturing multi-dimensional performance interactions, DMN enhances both the responsiveness and analytical robustness of human resource evaluation in dynamic environments11,12.

Research objectives

This study aims to develop a Tourism Human Resource–Deep Momentum Performance Optimization Model (THR-DMPOM) to address the instability and local optimality problems encountered by traditional performance evaluation models when handling multidimensional and dynamically changing data in the tourism industry. Specifically, the model is designed to enable adaptive adjustment of performance weight parameters through momentum-based learning mechanisms. This allows the evaluation process to respond in real time to variations across different performance dimensions. In addition, an accelerated gradient update strategy is introduced to improve the efficiency of integrating nonlinear and high-dimensional evaluation data13. This mechanism replaces static weighting schemes with continuously updated dynamic weights, thereby improving the temporal consistency of multi-criteria evaluation. From a methodological perspective, the use of momentum-driven updates enhances the model’s ability to maintain stable learning behavior under frequently fluctuating data conditions. This contributes to improved robustness in dynamic evaluation environments and supports more consistent decision-making outcomes. Overall, the objective of this study is to construct a more adaptive and stable performance evaluation framework for tourism human resource management, thereby improving the efficiency and reliability of data-driven decision-making in dynamic operational contexts.

Literature review

Current methods for HRPE have become increasingly diverse in both academic research and practical applications. Researchers have developed multi-layered HRPE models from various perspectives, incorporating multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) methods, fuzzy set theory, and data-driven approaches. For example, Goyal et al. (2022) proposed a tourism public service performance model based on the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), evaluating factors such as infrastructure, security services, and public transportation. Their study showed that infrastructure development played a key role in improving public service performance, offering valuable insights for tourism management. However, AHP proved inadequate for handling data dynamism and complexity, limiting its capacity for real-time evaluation14. Similarly, Winarno and Riezky (2023) examined HR performance management in travel companies, using AHP to analyze the weight of factors such as employee skills and motivation. Their findings highlighted the importance of job skills and HR training in enhancing performance, providing useful implications for HRPE in the tourism sector15. Aisyah and Samanhudi (2023) combined the Human Resource Scorecard (HRSC) with AHP to assess employee performance through questionnaire data, applying adequacy and consistency tests before AHP-based analysis. Their study offered quantitative evidence from a financial perspective, emphasizing the role of asset utilization in optimizing performance management16. Nonetheless, AHP’s dependence on questionnaire data and static weight assignments limits its ability to address data dynamism and capture time-series variations in performance.

In recent years, fuzzy decision-making and MCDM methods have been widely applied in HRPE. Barati and Fanati Rashidi (2024) integrated fuzzy AHP with the Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) to develop an evaluation model for employees’ job intentions in the hotel industry. They analyzed the effects of salary benefits, work stress, and job security on employee turnover, finding that salary and benefits were the main drivers, followed by work stress and job insecurity. Although this approach addressed ambiguous and unquantifiable information in HR management, it still struggled to handle dynamic data and multi-level decision-making17. Similarly, Regragui et al. (2024) proposed a decision-making framework based on a sustainability-balanced scorecard and fuzzy Delphi method for healthcare performance evaluation. By combining fuzzy techniques with AHP, they systematically assessed hospital performance through multi-criteria analysis. The model established quantitative evaluation standards using expert opinions and literature reviews, and employed fuzzy AHP to optimize weight distribution, offering a valuable reference for HRPE18. Although these approaches effectively standardize HRPE and optimize weight allocation, they remain limited in handling data dynamics, nonlinear characteristics, and temporal dependencies.

In recent years, deep learning methods have been increasingly applied to complex dynamic data modeling. Yu et al. (2025) proposed a multi-granularity spatiotemporal fusion Transformer, which achieved joint modeling of multi-source time-series data through residual redundancy reduction, spatiotemporal attention, and dynamic fusion mechanisms. Another study developed the GinAR+ framework, which enhanced the stability of multivariate time-series forecasting under missing data conditions via interpolation-based attention and adaptive graph convolution19,20. Cheng and Liu (2025) combined meta-feature learning with heterogeneous ensemble surrogate models and proposed a multi-objective optimization framework to improve optimization efficiency and computational stability in complex dynamic systems21. Cheng et al. (2026) introduced a gated non-stationary information module, frequency-aware attention, and meta-learning mechanisms under non-stationary data environments to strengthen long-term dependency modeling and uncertainty representation22. These studies indicate that deep learning methods are progressively advancing toward complex dynamic data modeling, multi-objective optimization, and non-stationary sequence learning.

Despite these advancements, current HRPE research still faces two major limitations. First, methods primarily based on AHP and fuzzy techniques lack dynamic weight optimization, causing performance evaluations to become unstable as data change. Second, traditional MCDM methods remain inadequate for modeling high-dimensional, non-linear, and time-dependent data. Meanwhile, although recent deep learning methods such as Transformers, graph neural networks, meta-learning, and multi-objective optimization have demonstrated strong representational capability in complex dynamic system modeling, most existing studies have been conducted in domains such as air quality forecasting and battery health management. Their primary objectives are typically focused on spatiotemporal dependency modeling, missing data imputation, or prediction accuracy improvement. However, relatively limited attention has been paid to multi-criteria performance evaluation in tourism HRPE scenarios, particularly the integration of dynamic weight co-optimization and fuzzy evaluation structures. In addition, these approaches still exhibit limitations in maintaining continuity in dynamic performance indicator adjustment and ensuring interpretability of evaluation results. To address these challenges, this study introduces a DMN-based evaluation model that leverages momentum optimization to dynamically adjust weights, thereby improving the accuracy and real-time adaptability of HRPE in the tourism industry.

Research model

Implementation principle of the THR-DMPOM model

The design foundation of the THR-DMPOM lies in the integration of the DMN. The construction process of the DMN can be divided into the following five steps: (1) In the initial layer, a conventional multi-layer neural network structure is used, defining and initializing weight parameters layer by layer to capture the preliminary nonlinear characteristics of the data23. (2) In each layer, momentum algorithms are employed to accumulate gradient information, allowing the current layer’s weight updates to be based not only on the current gradient changes but also to include a momentum term from the previous moment. This enables the model to preserve historical trends and achieve smooth adjustments to dynamic data24,25. (3) During the weight update process, a hyperparameter is introduced to the momentum term to balance the relative influence of new and old gradients, ensuring an effective dynamic response and trend continuation between recent data and historical trends26–28. (4) Based on the multi-layer structure, a recursive momentum accumulation method is applied to propagate and stack gradient information across layers. This guarantees consistency and coherence in weight updates within the network hierarchy, allowing the data features to be gradually optimized29,30. (5) Through the inter-layer momentum flow, the weights achieve nonlinear transformations across the multi-layer structure. This process ultimately converges on the global data at the final layer, completing the coordinated adjustment of network weights and achieving DMN construction and dynamic adaptability. Compared with traditional momentum algorithms such as SGD-M, Nesterov, Adam, AdamW, and AdaGrad, the innovation of the DMN lies primarily in its recursive momentum accumulation and cross-layer optimization mechanisms. Traditional algorithms limit momentum updates to local smoothing within a single-layer parameter space. In contrast, DMN enables recursive momentum accumulation, allowing historical gradients to propagate across multiple network layers. This process ensures that weight updates at each layer share long-term trend information, thereby maintaining dynamic consistency in the global optimization direction. Structurally, DMN introduces inter-layer weight smoothing and adaptive gradient correction mechanisms. These mechanisms adjust layer-specific learning rates based on gradient normalization, ensuring stable convergence when dealing with high-dimensional, non-stationary data. This feature is particularly critical for tourism human resource performance data, which often exhibit significant temporal fluctuations and dimensional correlations—conditions under which traditional momentum algorithms struggle to capture long-term trends effectively. In recent years, as uncertainty in complex decision-making scenarios has increased, several studies have adopted novel fuzzy set extensions to enhance the stability and discriminative power of multi-criteria evaluation. For instance, spherical fuzzy AHP has been applied to improve the representation of fuzzy preferences in spatial decision-making problems34. Pythagorean fuzzy CODAS (Combinative Distance-based Assessment) further strengthens decision discrimination in complex customer segmentation tasks through multidimensional fuzzy relationships35. In addition, bipolar fuzzy MABAC (Multi-Attributive Border Approximation Area Comparison) has been used to enhance uncertainty handling in complex fault detection environments36. Although these methods improve information representation in fuzzy decision systems, their weight updating processes still largely rely on static rules or stage-wise adjustments. As a result, they remain insufficient for continuously evolving dynamic performance data in tourism HRPE scenarios, where seamless coordination between historical gradients and real-time weight updates is required. To address this limitation, THR-DMPOM further integrates recursive momentum accumulation and cross-layer smoothing mechanisms, enabling the model to continuously track performance trends during multi-layer iterative optimization and thereby improving the stability and continuity of multi-criteria evaluation under dynamic conditions.

Unlike traditional single-step static weight allocation, THR-DMPOM performs continuous weight refinement at each update iteration by jointly considering current-layer gradient variations and accumulated momentum from preceding layers. This design ensures that weight updates reflect both short-term performance fluctuations and long-term historical trends. In tourism HRPE scenarios, different indicators often exhibit strong dynamic correlations; for example, Customer Service Quality, Team Collaboration, and Response Efficiency may co-evolve across business cycles. The cross-layer momentum sharing mechanism therefore reduces the impact of localized fluctuations on overall evaluation outcomes and prevents instability in multi-criteria ranking caused by abrupt changes in individual indicators. These findings suggest that adaptive cross-layer weight updating plays a critical role in maintaining both the stability and continuity of multi-criteria evaluation results.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection

Due to the limited accessibility of data from leading tourism enterprises and the dominance of small- and medium-sized firms in China’s tourism industry, this study focuses on four representative small- and medium-sized tourism companies to evaluate the applicability and effectiveness of THR-DMPOM. Data collection began with coordination between the research team and company management to ensure access to multi-level and multidimensional employee performance records covering the past year. The collected data included performance indicators across different employee tiers. Primary data sources were derived from each company’s human resource management systems, including historical records such as daily attendance, task completion rates, and customer feedback. To complement system-based data, structured questionnaires were designed and distributed, and interviews were conducted with managers, department heads, and frontline employees to capture subjective evaluation components that are difficult to quantify. To ensure data quality and consistency, a series of preprocessing steps were applied after collection. These included standardization of data formats and conversion of heterogeneous scoring systems into a unified percentage scale. Missing values were handled using mean imputation and interpolation methods, while outliers were removed based on predefined threshold criteria. Finally, all data were normalized to ensure comparability across different datasets.

In accordance with confidentiality agreements established during data collection, employee identities and detailed performance records are not disclosed in this study. However, the key characteristics of the four companies are summarized in Table 3.

Experimental environment

The construction of the HRPE model is grounded in the development of a rigorous and scientifically validated indicator system. Based on a comprehensive review of domestic and international literature on human resource management and performance evaluation, an initial pool of candidate indicators was first established through systematic literature analysis. To enhance the practicality and contextual relevance of the indicators, an empirical investigation was conducted across four domestic tourism enterprises. Both managerial staff and employees were included, and more than 300 individuals were interviewed. These interviews focused on identifying practical requirements in human resource performance management, which facilitated the refinement, supplementation, and expansion of the initial indicator set. Subsequently, a panel of 12 experts and scholars with extensive experience in management and statistical analysis was organized into four independent evaluation groups. These groups conducted multiple rounds of screening and assessment of the candidate indicators, removing those that were redundant, ambiguously defined, or difficult to obtain in practice37,38. The indicator selection process consisted of three iterative evaluation rounds. The first round involved preliminary filtering based on literature frequency and industry relevance. In the second round, experts scored each indicator using a 1–5 Likert scale, with retention criteria defined as an average score ≥ 3.5 and a coefficient of variation ≤ 0.25. In the third round, the consistency of expert judgments was verified using Kendall’s coefficient of concordance (W = 0.82, p < 0.001), resulting in the final determination of 24 core indicators. The expert panel included four university scholars specializing in human resource management, three experts in statistical modeling, and five tourism enterprise managers. Inter-rater reliability was further evaluated using the intra-class correlation coefficient (ICC = 0.87), confirming the high consistency of expert assessments.

For data preprocessing, missing values were handled using a combination of mean imputation and multiple imputation techniques. Outliers were identified using the ± 3σ criterion and then reviewed by experts to determine whether they should be removed or smoothed. All indicators were normalized using Min–Max scaling to transform values into the [0,1] range, thereby eliminating differences in measurement units. To ensure confidentiality and data integrity, only anonymized and structurally organized datasets were used for modeling, and no raw individual-level information was disclosed. After the full screening process, eight initial indicators were removed, resulting in a final set of 24 indicators, as presented in Table 4.

Bias Analysis

Bias Analysis

A systematic bias analysis was conducted by selecting the same number of employees across the four companies and comparing actual performance data with the evaluation results produced by THR-DMPOM. (2)Distribution Consistency Validation

Distribution Consistency Validation

THR-DMPOM-generated performance scores were compared with historical performance data using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test (K-S test). The K-S test measures the consistency of the score distributions. If the p-value of the K-S statistic exceeds 0.05, the two distributions are considered statistically similar, indicating that the model-generated evaluations align with the overall distribution of real data40–42. If the Kolmogorov–Smirnov (K–S) test yields a p-value greater than 0.05, it indicates that the two distributions are similar, meaning the model’s evaluation aligns with the overall distribution of the actual data. To further quantify the fit and error between the model predictions and actual performance (ground truth), this study employs multiple statistical metrics, including MSE, Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (ρ), Kendall’s τ coefficient, Cohen’s d, and 95% Confidence Interval (CI), to assess systematic bias in the model’s predictions. In the experimental setup, the ground truth is defined as the historical comprehensive performance scores of four tourism enterprises, serving as the reference for evaluating THR-DMPOM predictions. Model training and evaluation follow a consistent dataset partitioning strategy to ensure comparability and statistical stability across different stages. To prevent data leakage, each variable is input independently during parameter updates, and weights are adaptively optimized without incorporating future information or external features, thereby ensuring the objectivity and reproducibility of the model evaluation.

Discussion

As a deep learning–based framework, THR-DMPOM introduces a dynamic momentum network (DMN) optimization mechanism, providing a novel methodological basis for tourism HRPE under multidimensional and dynamic data conditions. The model demonstrates strong adaptability in handling dynamic data variations and multi-criteria evaluation environments. In particular, THR-DMPOM improves the smoothness and precision of weight distribution through momentum accumulation in multi-layered and complex data settings, thereby enhancing both stability and real-time responsiveness. The experimental results further indicate that the model effectively controls systematic bias and maintains consistent performance across different evaluation dimensions. This momentum-driven weight updating mechanism enables the model to capture subtle variations in heterogeneous indicators, improving its capability for multi-dimensional data fusion. Compared with traditional fuzzy AHP and TOPSIS methods, THR-DMPOM shows clear advantages in managing dynamic environments and unstable network conditions, particularly in terms of robustness and resistance to data perturbations. Overall, the model supports more responsive and accurate decision-making in fluctuating environments, thereby improving the effectiveness of human resource management in tourism enterprises. This adaptability highlights its potential applicability to other domains characterized by high-dimensional and dynamically evolving datasets.

However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the momentum-based optimization strategy increases computational complexity, particularly when processing high-dimensional or large-scale datasets, which places higher demands on real-time deployment and system resources. In large-scale tourism enterprises or high-frequency data-stream scenarios, recursive momentum accumulation and cross-layer weight updates may introduce additional synchronization overhead, increasing memory consumption and inference latency. As a result, stricter requirements are imposed on GPU parallelization and real-time deployment environments. At present, experiments were conducted primarily on data from small- and medium-sized tourism enterprises. Therefore, the model has not yet been validated in ultra-large enterprise clusters or distributed online deployment environments. Second, the performance of the model depends heavily on the completeness and quality of the evaluation indicator system. Missing or noisy data may affect the accuracy of weight adjustments. In tourism HR management, where data collection channels are dispersed and seasonal fluctuations are common, these limitations remain difficult to fully eliminate. Despite these constraints, THR-DMPOM still demonstrates strong adaptability and practical potential under current application conditions, particularly in supporting dynamic responsiveness and optimization in complex and uncertain environments. Future work will focus on improving model efficiency and robustness, as well as extending its applicability to large-scale and real-time distributed scenarios.

Conclusion

Research contribution

The THR-DMPOM proposed in this study provides a data-driven framework for comprehensive human resource performance evaluation in the tourism industry. In this context, “dynamic adaptability” refers specifically to the model’s ability to update its internal parameter structure through retraining when new performance data become available. It does not refer to cross-context generalization using independent datasets from different industries. Empirical validation was conducted using data from four tourism enterprises. The model does not include enterprise size or organizational type as input features. As a result, it can be transferred across enterprises with similar structural characteristics, although its applicability across different industries still requires further empirical verification. In the tourism sector, key performance dimensions such as employee service quality, customer feedback, and team collaboration require timely and accurate evaluation. THR-DMPOM addresses this requirement through a momentum-based optimization mechanism that enables adaptive weight adjustment under dynamic data conditions. This allows the model to respond efficiently to small fluctuations in employee performance while maintaining the timeliness and accuracy of evaluation results. Compared with traditional fuzzy AHP and fuzzy TOPSIS methods, THR-DMPOM demonstrates higher efficiency in processing data under high-load operational environments. It also provides more flexible adjustment of evaluation criteria in multi-criteria scenarios, thereby enhancing managers’ ability to obtain a comprehensive view of employee performance.

From an application perspective, THR-DMPOM enables tourism enterprises to achieve more systematic and refined human resource management. It supports managers in identifying key performance indicators, analyzing employee performance more effectively, and optimizing resource allocation decisions. In practical deployment, the model can be integrated into existing Human Resource Management Information Systems (HRMIS) through modular interfaces, enabling automated data input and real-time analysis so that evaluation outputs can directly support recruitment, appraisal, and incentive decisions. System integration can be implemented via secondary development of existing performance management platforms to ensure compatibility and data security. In addition, the real-time feedback capability of THR-DMPOM provides forward-looking analytical support for managerial decision-making. To maximize its effectiveness, enterprises may need to improve the data literacy of employees and managers through targeted training programs, enabling the model to be more effectively embedded into daily decision-making processes. Overall, the proposed framework contributes to more precise human resource strategies, improved organizational efficiency, and enhanced service quality and decision-making capability in tourism enterprises.

In summary, the novelty of THR-DMPOM lies in extending the traditional fuzzy multi-criteria performance evaluation framework through three core mechanisms: Recursive Momentum Accumulation, Inter-layer Weight Smoothing, and Adaptive Gradient Correction. These mechanisms enable the model to continuously track historical gradient dynamics during multi-layer network optimization and to perform dynamic updating and nonlinear co-optimization of performance indicator weights. Unlike conventional fuzzy AHP and TOPSIS methods, which rely on static weights and fixed ranking rules, THR-DMPOM achieves more stable weight adaptation in high-dimensional performance data by enabling cross-layer momentum sharing. This design facilitates the continuous propagation of long-term trend information across network layers. Furthermore, compared with data-driven models such as Ridge regression, LSTM, and XGBoost, which primarily focus on improving predictive accuracy, THR-DMPOM is not limited to prediction-oriented optimization. Instead, it integrates a fuzzy evaluation structure to achieve dynamic coupling and real-time smoothing of multi-criteria weights. As a result, the model exhibits stronger robustness to noise, improved preservation of temporal trends, and enhanced capability in modeling complex interactions among performance indicators under dynamic fluctuations in tourism human resource performance.

Future works and research limitations

Although this study carefully considers the complexity and dynamic characteristics of tourism human resource performance data during model development and validation, several data-related limitations and modeling assumptions may affect the broader applicability of THR-DMPOM. First, the model assumes a relatively complete indicator system and stable data quality. However, in practical tourism industry settings, data are often influenced by regional economic disparities, employee mobility, and operational heterogeneity, which may lead to missing, delayed, or noisy performance indicators. Second, the model assumes short-term stability in the relationships among performance indicators. In real-world scenarios, unexpected events such as public health crises may introduce abrupt nonlinear shifts in data distributions, potentially affecting the stability of momentum-based learning mechanisms. Although THR-DMPOM demonstrates strong adaptability within a multi-layer optimization structure, extreme data sparsity or informational asymmetry may still result in delayed weight updates or convergence to suboptimal solutions. Third, the 24-item performance indicator system used in this study is primarily derived from tourism human resource management contexts. It emphasizes domain-specific factors such as customer service quality, task response efficiency, and team collaboration capability. As a result, the current experimental findings mainly reflect the applicability of THR-DMPOM within the tourism sector. For other domains such as manufacturing, finance, or healthcare, which differ in job structures and performance logic, both the indicator system and dynamic weight relationships may require redesign and further validation. Finally, future research will focus on incorporating uncertainty modeling and data augmentation strategies while maintaining computational efficiency. These improvements aim to enhance the robustness and transferability of THR-DMPOM in heterogeneous and multi-source data environments, thereby expanding its applicability across broader industry scenarios.
