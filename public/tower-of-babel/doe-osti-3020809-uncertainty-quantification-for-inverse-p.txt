 A BSTRACT
 Inverse problems in imaging are commonly solved by optimization or learned surrogates that return
 a single reconstruction, while uncertainty information is often unavailable. In many experimental
 settings, however, uncertainty is required to assess reliability, guide downstream analysis, and
 prioritize additional measurements. In this note, we present a compact uncertainty-quantification
 framework based on local objective curvature, and then specialize it to ptychographic reconstruction.
 We further show how repeated reconstructions can be aggregated in a statistically principled way,
 including a practical implementation path for PtychoNN.

1 Introduction

Inverse problems aim to recover unknown parameters from indirect, noisy, and typically ill-conditioned measurements.
Because these problems are often non-unique or weakly constrained, point estimates alone provide incomplete
information: two reconstructions with similar data fidelity may have substantially different reliability across spatial
regions.
This motivates uncertainty quantification (UQ) that is local, interpretable, and compatible with existing reconstruction
pipelines. A useful route is curvature-based UQ, where local second-order structure of the objective function approxi-
mates posterior spread around a maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate. This approach is attractive because it directly
leverages quantities already computed in optimization.
Ptychography provides a concrete and important setting for such methods. The forward model is nonlinear, the
parameter dimension is high, and gauge ambiguities are intrinsic. Recent learned methods such as PtychoNN provide
fast inference, but practical deployment still benefits from confidence maps and uncertainty-aware fusion across repeated
reconstructions.
