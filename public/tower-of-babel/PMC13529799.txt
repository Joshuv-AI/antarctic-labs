Multi-line LiDAR 3D construction environment modeling and BIM consistency update method for digital twins
Authors: Chen Liu, Xin Xu, Xiaofeng Ding, Guangmei He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractConstruction digital twins aim to maintain an up-to-date, BIM-consistent as-built state from streaming multi-line LiDAR to enable progress verification, dimensional compliance control, and proactive safety management on construction sites. However, converting sequential LiDAR observations into a temporally stable, element-centric BIM state is difficult in realistic AEC environments. Existing approaches mainly fall into geometry-driven Scan-to-BIM registration frameworks and learning-based point cloud perception models, with some system-level digital-twin frameworks built on BIM-centric reasoning. Purely geometric alignment is brittle under occlusion, clutter, and repetitive structures, and registration uncertainty can propagate to downstream progress and deviation conclusions. Meanwhile, generic perception networks are often trained on static frames, lack temporal stabilization, and do not explicitly optimize BIM-consistent element association or geometry-aware alignment cues. In addition, many digital-twin systems expose limited, loosely coupled state representations that hinder causal, reliable site updates. To address these gaps, we propose LiBiDT (LiDAR–BIM Digital Twin), a causal LiDAR–BIM digital-twin model—where “causal” refers to computational online updating in which only past observations enter the current state—that integrates spatio-temporal mapping, geometric–semantic perception, and scan-to-BIM association for consistent state evolution. LiBiDT consists of Spatio-Temporal Global Registration (STGR) with LiDAR–IMU–GNSS priors, Multi-task Perception (MTP) using a PointNet++ backbone with semantic/instance/normal heads, and Scan-to-BIM Association and State Updating (S2B) via normal-guided point-to-plane refinement, bipartite matching with fused overlap/deviation/semantic evidence, and exponential moving average (EMA) stabilization. The Decision and Risk Analytics (DRA) layer turns the BIM-aligned state into auditable construction-management indicators—weighted completion, deviation summaries, and three forward-evaluable safety/compliance hazard flags. Experiments on three public benchmarks (Hilti SLAM, CV4AEC 2024 Scan-to-BIM, and S3DIS Area-5) cover trajectory, perception, and association evaluation. On CV4AEC 2024 Scan-to-BIM, LiBiDT reaches association F1 = 0.837 and Chamfer Distance (CD) / surface Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 3.2/3.5 cm; relative to the perception-replacement baseline (PointNet++ + S2B, F1 = 0.758) the gain is large, while relative to the strongest competing baseline—CoFiNet (Reg) + S2B at F1 = 0.822—the absolute association gain narrows to 0.015 F1 points, with the bigger gains coming from temporal identity consistency (IDF1 0.854 → 0.861) and ID-switch reduction. A held-out tile-stream case study visualises BIM-element state evolution, alignment refinement, EMA-stabilised evidence streams, and hazard-flag firing as the scan progressively covers the floor; a strict temporal validation under active construction will require time-stamped multi-acquisition data and is left to future work. Ablation results confirm that evidence stabilization and multi-cue matching contribute complementary gains in accuracy and temporal consistency, yielding a favorable efficiency–accuracy trade-off.Keywords: Construction digital twin, Multi-line LiDAR, Scan-to-BIM association, Point cloud perception, BIM consistency updateSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Building and infrastructure projects increasingly rely on fine-grained, near-real-time site awareness to support progress verification, quantity takeoff and payment, dimensional compliance control, and proactive safety management. Building Information Modeling (BIM) provides a design-time, element-centric representation, but the as-built scene evolves continuously and often deviates from the planned model. Construction digital twins aim to close this loop by fusing sensing data with BIM to maintain an up-to-date, operationally interpretable state of the site1–3. Among available sensing modalities, multi-line LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) offers dense 3D geometry with robust depth measurements, enabling frequent capture of complex construction scenes; however, turning streaming LiDAR into a temporally consistent, BIM-aligned, element-level state remains challenging due to clutter and occlusion, repetitive structures, coordinate-frame drift, heterogeneous supervision for perception, and the need for causal, on-site operation.

A first family of approaches is geometry-driven Scan-to-BIM alignment and model-based comparison, typically centered on coarse-to-fine registration and handcrafted geometric cues. Representative Scan-to-BIM frameworks exploit planar primitives or congruent sets to initialize the alignment and then refine it4. While effective in controlled settings, these methods are brittle under partial observations, non-model objects, and symmetries that induce ambiguous alignments; moreover, uncertainty in geometric matching can propagate to downstream “progress/quality” conclusions when the digital twin is built from imperfect registrations4. More broadly, systematic reviews note that fully automated, robust Scan-to-BIM remains difficult in realistic Architecture, Engineering, and Construction (AEC) scenarios, especially when the environment is incomplete, cluttered, or only partially scanned5.

A second family focuses on learning-based point cloud understanding. Deep networks for 3D segmentation and feature learning have significantly advanced geometric–semantic perception6–8. Construction-oriented studies further demonstrate that learned scene understanding can support Scan-to-BIM automation and downstream reconstruction9. Nonetheless, these models are commonly trained on static frames and do not, by themselves, enforce temporally stable predictions across a LiDAR stream, nor do they guarantee BIM-consistent element associations and geometry-aware alignment cues needed by Scan-vs-BIM workflows. In addition, instance-level consistency and robust clustering remain non-trivial in cluttered scenes even with discriminative embedding objectives10.

A third family concerns digital-twin systems and construction management analytics (progress, quality, and safety) built on BIM-centric reasoning. Prior work highlights the promise of digital twins for cyber-physical integration but also emphasizes interoperability, real-time updating, and reliable data-to-model synchronization as persistent gaps2,3. On the safety side, BIM-based hazard identification and prevention can encode design-time priors and rules, yet its operational effectiveness hinges on accurate as-built localization of hazards and protections, which is difficult without a robust, continuously updated scan-to-BIM state11. Consequently, construction-management indicators often remain loosely coupled to the sensing and alignment stack, limiting reliability when scene observations are noisy, intermittent, or partially missing.

In summary, existing models provide valuable components, but they do not fully deliver a causal, BIM-consistent digital-twin update that tightly couples spatio-temporal mapping, geometric–semantic perception, element-level association, and construction-management indicators from LiDAR streams. The main problems with these methods are as follows:

Fragmented sensing-to-BIM state updating: Many approaches stop at per-scan registration or per-frame segmentation, without an explicit element-level digital-twin state and a causal update mechanism that is robust to occlusion and intermittent observations2,3.

To address these limitations, we propose LiBiDT (LiDAR–BIM Digital Twin), a four-stage digital-twin model that performs Spatio-Temporal Global Registration (STGR), Multi-task Perception (MTP), Scan-to-BIM Association and State Updating (S2B), and Decision and Risk Analytics (DRA) in a strict causal cascade. The key idea is to treat globally registered LiDAR as a scene-level backbone, attach geometric–semantic predictions to it, and update BIM element states through robust association and temporally filtered evidence, producing an auditable interface of progress and deviation indicators6,12. Throughout the paper, “causal” refers strictly to computational online updating—only past observations enter the current state—rather than structural causality between physical processes; the term is preferred over “online” or “incremental” because it makes the no-look-ahead constraint explicit at the level of the system equations (Eqs. (6)–(10)). The main contributions of our model are as follows:

Causal digital-twin state and windowed spatio-temporal mapping: LiBiDT formalizes an element-centric twin state and updates it causally from a fixed-length LiDAR observation window, leveraging tightly coupled LiDAR–IMU smoothing and mapping to provide a stable scene backbone for downstream tasks2,12.

Geometry–semantic perception coupled with robust scan-to-BIM association: LiBiDT combines multi-task point cloud perception with geometry-aware alignment refinement and bipartite matching using bounded overlap/deviation cues, improving robustness in cluttered and partially observed construction scenes4,6.

The novelty of LiBiDT is not in re-inventing the individual components (LiDAR–IMU SLAM, PointNet++ perception, point-to-plane ICP, bipartite matching, EMA filtering)—each of which is well established in its respective sub-field—but in introducing (i) a single causal digital-twin state vector that explicitly carries per-element evidence and discrete element status as part of the network output, (ii) a forward-evaluable safety/compliance hazard layer (Sec. 4.5) whose thresholds can be re-tuned by site managers without retraining, and (iii) an end-to-end end-to-end visualisation pipeline that ties the abstract’s three operational goals (BIM-consistency, progress, safety) back to observable outputs on a held-out floor. These three elements are either absent or only loosely specified in prior digital-twin frameworks.

Related work

Construction digital twins and BIM-centric site monitoring

Digital twins have evolved from BIM-centric information management toward cyber–physical representations that fuse design intent with continuously updated as-built observations. Early construction digital-twin research emphasized semantic interoperability, data fusion across heterogeneous sources, and lifecycle integration to support decision-making1,2. Recent surveys further consolidate the view that practical construction twins require (i) reliable sensing-to-model alignment, (ii) scalable state representations, and (iii) robust update mechanisms under incomplete and noisy observations3. However, many existing digital-twin frameworks remain system-level blueprints, where the online update is either loosely specified or relies on manual/heuristic associations between sensed data and BIM elements1,3. This gap motivates digital-twin models that explicitly formalize the causal update from spatio-temporally consistent sensing, to geometric–semantic perception, to element-level state evolution and monitoring indicators.

LiDAR-based spatio-temporal mapping and point-cloud perception

A prerequisite for any scan-driven construction twin is a globally consistent scene backbone. LiDAR odometry and mapping methods, ranging from lightweight LOAM variants to tightly coupled LiDAR–inertial smoothing and mapping, provide accurate ego-motion estimation and drift reduction under rapid motion and sparse features12,13. Yet, construction scenes are highly dynamic (equipment, staged materials, partial structures), which can degrade mapping stability and complicate downstream BIM alignment if the perception stage is not jointly considered.

On top of registered point clouds, modern 3D perception increasingly relies on deep point-cloud networks rather than hand-crafted geometry descriptors. Hierarchical set abstraction models (e.g., PointNet++) learn multi-scale local geometry, while large-scale segmentation architectures prioritize efficiency and robustness under non-uniform density and massive scenes6. Convolutional kernels defined directly on point neighborhoods (e.g., KPConv) further improve geometric fidelity for outdoor/structural scenes7. Despite strong recognition performance, generic 3D perception models are typically trained for semantic tasks alone, assume static scenes, and do not explicitly optimize for downstream scan-to-BIM alignment and element-level state updating. Moreover, instance-level grouping often requires additional objectives; discriminative embedding losses provide a practical route but can be brittle under sparse or partial instance supervision10. These limitations suggest that construction twins benefit from multi-task perception designed to output alignment-relevant cues (e.g., normals and instance clusters) together with semantics.

Scan-to-BIM registration, association, and construction analytics

Scan-to-BIM research has long studied how to align and interpret as-built point clouds with as-designed BIM geometry. Classical workflows combine global registration and local refinement: global alignment via correspondence sampling or invariant sets14, followed by iterative closest point (ICP) variants for fine alignment, and sometimes fast global registration to reduce dependence on initialization15. Comprehensive reviews highlight persistent challenges: occlusion, clutter, varying completeness across time, and semantic ambiguity when multiple BIM elements share similar geometry16. Beyond alignment, construction monitoring methods map scans to BIM to infer progress and deviations, typically through geometry-based matching and rule-based status assignment17,18. These strands collectively indicate a missing end-to-end treatment: registration, perception, and association are often optimized in isolation, while monitoring outputs are derived from brittle heuristics rather than a calibrated, temporally robust state. LiBiDT addresses these gaps by producing a globally consistent backbone for perception and alignment, learning geometric–semantic multi-task predictions (including instance embeddings and normals), and performing explicit scan-to-BIM association with bounded evidence filtering to drive element-level state updates.

Tile-stream case study: pipeline-level illustration on a held-out floor

Scope. The CV4AEC 2024 release is a static as-built acquisition: a single point cloud per floor, with no time-stamped re-scans of an active construction site. To illustrate how LiBiDT’s digital-twin update layer would behave during streaming inference, we therefore deterministically partition the held-out floor (05_MedOffice_01_F2, 248 BIM elements, never used for training or hyperparameter selection) into 1,304 overlapping tiles processed in lexicographic order, and run LiBiDT causally tile by tile. We track (a) the BIM-aligned coverage indicator (denoted Completion(t) for consistency with §Construction Management Decision and Risk Analytics (DRA)), (b) detected-weighted and worst-case deviation summaries, and (c) the firing pattern of the three hazard rules of §4.5.

What this illustrates and what it does not. Because the underlying acquisition has no temporal dynamics, Completion(t) here measures coverage growth as the scan progresses, not real construction progress; the hazard rules act on the static as-built geometry rather than on a time-evolving site. The case study is therefore a pipeline-level illustration of how the DRA layer turns a streaming BIM-aligned state into auditable indicators—it is not a substitute for true temporal validation under active construction, which requires multi-epoch time-stamped scans and is left to future work (§Conclusion).

Implementation details

All experiments are implemented in Python 3.10/PyTorch 2.x and run on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. For learnable components (MTP), we train with AdamW, cosine learning-rate scheduling with a 5-epoch warmup, and automatic mixed precision (AMP). Early stopping is enabled on the validation split (Scan-to-BIM patience 20; S3DIS patience 15). Unless otherwise stated, Scan-to-BIM uses batch size 6 and 120 epochs, while S3DIS uses batch size 8 and 100 epochs.

Instance clustering. We set the instance embedding dimension to and obtain clusters using DBSCAN in the joint space of (i) embedding cosine distance and (ii) 3D Euclidean distance, with (embedding) and m (spatial).

Key Scan-to-BIM hyperparameters. Default Scan-to-BIM settings: EMA smoothing , evidence threshold , logistic temperature , voxel size cm for IoU, deviation scale cm, matching weights , and 20 ICP iterations.

STGR baselines (Hilti SLAM)

We compare two open-source LiDAR–IMU baselines: LIO-SAM (LiDAR+IMU)12, a tightly coupled smoothing-and-mapping method, and LeGO-LOAM13, a lightweight ground-optimised LOAM variant. Both are run with the authors’ default factor-graph weights and feature-extraction radii. Our STGR corresponds to LIO-SAM augmented with GNSS priors via the residual term in Eq. (14); the GNSS prior is enabled only on Hilti SLAM, where the dataset provides time-synchronised GNSS messages, and is disabled on the Scan-to-BIM and S3DIS benchmarks (which are indoor and supply no GNSS stream). Trajectory metrics (ATE, RPE) are evaluated using the official Hilti ground-truth protocol with no per-sequence tuning of any baseline.

Perception baselines (S3DIS)

We include two complementary perception baselines on S3DIS Area-5: PointNet++6 as a hierarchical multi-scale aggregator and PointGroup20 as a dual-set instance grouper. PointNet++ semantic numbers are taken from recent benchmark tabulations21,22 on the same Area-5 split with the standard 13-class protocol; PointGroup instance numbers (AP/AP) are from its original publication on the identical split. We do not retrain these baselines because both are well-established Area-5 references; for our own model we run end-to-end training under the exact hyperparameters of Sec. Implementation Details. The S3DIS comparison is intended to demonstrate that LiBiDT’s multi-task perception delivers competitive—not state-of-the-art—perception, since the design priority of MTP is to surface alignment-friendly cues (normals, instance embeddings) for the downstream S2B stage rather than to optimise pure instance segmentation.

Scan-to-BIM baselines (CV4AEC 2024 Scan-to-BIM)

We evaluate three baselines: (i) a controlled PointNet++ + S2B baseline that swaps in a vanilla PointNet++ backbone6 while keeping our matching, EMA filter, and DRA stack; (ii) Scan2BIM-Net23, an end-to-end association competitor that we use without our downstream S2B post-processing because its output is already element-level associations; and (iii) CoFiNet (Reg) + S2B24, where CoFiNet replaces the registration/correspondence component while reusing our perception, matching, and EMA filter. All three baselines and LiBiDT ingest identical tile streams, identical coarse BIM alignments, identical evidence-fusion weights , and identical evaluation thresholds, so any reported difference is attributable to the differing backbone or registration component. Reproducibility scripts, the tile-split file used for the validation/test pool, and the code that runs each baseline under this protocol will be released by the corresponding author upon publication; the same release will include the inference pipeline used to generate the visualisations and the case study (Sec. 4.5).

(2) Scan-to-BIM overall (Association + Geometry + Temporal + Efficiency). Table 2 consolidates the full Scan-to-BIM comparison and we discuss the gains honestly across baselines. Relative to the perception-replacement baseline (PointNet++ + S2B, F1 = 0.758), LiBiDT reaches F1 = 0.837, an absolute gain of 0.079 F1 points; this difference is driven by the multi-task perception, since both methods share the same matching, EMA, and DRA stack. Hyperparameter sensitivity and stability analysis

We conduct a comprehensive sensitivity analysis to understand how temporal stabilization and multi-cue fusion affect the robustness of the Scan-to-BIM digital twin state (Fig. 10).

Hyperparameter sensitivity on Scan-to-BIM. EMA ρ controls the stability–responsiveness trade-off; multi-cue fusion improves both association and geometric consistency.

Regarding the temporal stabilization, the EMA smoothing factor ρ in Eq. (37) governs the trade-off between system responsiveness and stability. We vary ρ from 0.1 to 0.9 and observe two distinct failure modes at the extremes. Low smoothing values (ρ < 0.5) make the element evidence excessively sensitive to transient noise and partial occlusions, resulting in state flickering and a sharp increase in Identity Switches (IDSW). Conversely, overly high values (ρ> 0.7) introduce significant inertia, causing a ’ghosting’ effect where the updated state lags behind physical reality; this latency prevents the system from promptly transitioning elements from ‘Undetected’ to ‘Detected’, thereby degrading Recall. The optimal operating region is identified as , where the accumulated evidence is robust enough to bridge temporary scanning gaps while remaining sensitive enough to capture actual progress updates, effectively maximizing the stabilized F1 score.

Limitations and sensitivity considerations

For balance and to guide further research, we acknowledge five limitations of the current evaluation and outline how each will be addressed.

(L1) GNSS-dropout sensitivity is not quantified. Our STGR component augments LIO-SAM with GNSS priors on Hilti SLAM, where the dataset provides GNSS messages, and is evaluated only there. We have not yet quantified how the trajectory and downstream BIM-aligned indicators degrade under partial or full GNSS dropout (typical for indoor construction stages or shielded sites). Conceptually, the LiDAR–IMU residual term in Eq. (14) continues to provide a state estimate without GNSS, but the absolute drift can be larger; an explicit GNSS-dropout ablation is required before the system is deployed on outdoor-to-indoor transitions.

(L2) Sensitivity to BIM-modelling errors. All deviation, completion, and hazard outputs are evaluated against the supplied BIM elements. If the as-designed BIM contains positional errors (mis-placed walls), missing elements, or coarse abstractions of complex furniture/MEP, the S2B associations will inherit those errors and the DRA outputs may flag them as deviations. The current paper does not characterise this propagation; a controlled BIM-perturbation study (random per-element shifts and class swaps) is a necessary follow-up to provide actionable error budgets to site managers.

(L3) Forward-evaluable hazards rather than learned safety models. The three hazard rules of Sec. 4.5 are deliberately simple and auditable; they are not learned safety classifiers. A practitioner-co-designed rule set with class-specific tolerances, schedule-aware activation, and learned anomaly augmentation is a natural extension once a richer construction-management benchmark is available.

Conclusion

Construction digital twins require accurate alignment between streaming multi-line LiDAR and BIM to support progress verification, compliance checking, and safety monitoring. Yet, maintaining a temporally consistent, element-centric BIM state from LiDAR streams remains challenging. Major difficulties arise from occlusion and clutter, repetitive geometry that causes ambiguous associations, and drift that breaks cross-time consistency. To address these issues, we proposed LiBiDT (LiDAR–BIM Digital Twin), a causal four-stage model that integrates spatio-temporal global registration, multi-task perception, scan-to-BIM association with state updating, and decision analytics. LiBiDT builds a stable scene backbone using windowed LiDAR–IMU–GNSS mapping and predicts semantics, instance embeddings, and normals with a PointNet++ multi-head network. LiBiDT further refines alignment via normal-guided point-to-plane Iterative Closest Point (ICP), performs bipartite matching with fused overlap/deviation/semantic cues, and applies EMA filtering to stabilize element evidence. Experiments on Hilti SLAM, CV4AEC 2024 Scan-to-BIM19, and S3DIS Area-5 show that LiBiDT improves trajectory accuracy and yields gains in association and geometric consistency. On the CV4AEC 2024 Scan-to-BIM benchmark, association F1 reaches 0.837 against 0.758 for the perception-replacement baseline and 0.822 for the strongest competing baseline, CoFiNet (Reg) + S2B; the absolute gain over the latter is therefore 0.015 F1 points, while the larger improvements lie in temporal identity consistency (IDF1 0.854 → 0.861) and ID-switch reduction with practical latency. Ablation studies verify that EMA stabilization and multi-cue matching provide complementary benefits in accuracy and identity stability, and the normal-guided refinement improves geometric error without sacrificing efficiency. On S3DIS Area-5, LiBiDT improves semantic mIoU and overall accuracy over the cited PointNet++ baseline; instance AP/AP remain below the dedicated PointGroup detector, reflecting that perception in LiBiDT is optimised jointly with the alignment-relevant cues that S2B consumes rather than for stand-alone instance segmentation. Beyond numerical evaluation, the held-out tile-stream case study in Sec. 4.5 (Fig. 2) traces a BIM-aligned coverage indicator, deviation summaries, and the firing pattern of three forward-evaluable hazard rules (compliance, clearance, status), connecting the safety/compliance/progress goals of the abstract back to observable, auditable outputs. Future work will broaden the evaluation to multi-site sequences with active scene changes, study sensitivity to GNSS dropout and BIM-modelling errors, and extend the hazard rule set in close collaboration with construction-management practitioners.
