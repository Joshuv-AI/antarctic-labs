2. N IS Annuals

The chairman reported that the Chapter I Task Group had
held meetings on 27 November. and 13 December with representatives
from the member agencies to develop an outline guide and production
s chedule for the NIS Annuals (refer to Item 6 NCM 254).

The group submitted an Outline Guide (Tentative) for the
NIS Annual, dated 21 November 1957, which the NIS Committee
approved. The group proposed that a pilot NIS Annual on Poland be
produced to serve as a basis for determining capabilities and for
developing a feasible production schedule. The NIS Committee
approved the proposal and set 1 February 1958 ae the deadline for
the NIS Annual on Poland, and directed that contributors use a
uniform intelligence cut-off date of l January 1958. The Committee
further stipulated that the NIS Annual on Poland be formally reviewed
by the Committee and, a® a normal practice, that draft copies of
Sections 11 "Significance of New Developments" be distributed to
all members concurrently with their submission to OBI.

The Army member raised the question of including the
additional topic ‘'Biographies" of the Annual as a new requirement
for future and revised Chapters I. since the holders of a Chapter I
would not have this information until its Annual was issued. The
Committee deferred decision for further study and, at the suggestion
of the Navy member „ requested State to develop for the NXS Annual
on Poland as an alternate requirement, in addition to "Biographies,"
a list of high government offices and other important positions with
the names of incumbents.

The Committee discussed a number of other questions,
including the possible use of graphics in the NIS Annuals, It was the
consensus that the Annuals should be kept as simple as possible and
developed gradually in the light of experience. The chairman stressed
the need to provide interim maintenance for Chapters I in view of their
continuing use at the highest levels of Government, He urged the
members to give this problem their personal attention so that the
most effective means can be developed for producing the Annuals,

As a general basis for. their development,, he quoted the following
statement from the tentative Outline Guide:

"The essence of NIS Annual production is the development
of simple and direct procedures for review, editing,, and
publication.. Contributions should be at the level of detail
associated with Chapter I treatment, and are to be submitted
no later than the deadline date of the approved schedule. It
is intended that the substance of the contributions be based
on the latest information readily available to the contributing
agency without additional research; in other words, infor-
mation which i« normally used by an agency in maintaining
its appreciation of foreign situations."

3. NIS Memos

The members were handed draft copies of a revised series
of NIS Memos No, 1 through No. 5, dated 1 December 1957, which
are to supersede the old series of NIS Memos, many of which have
become obsolete or have been incorporated into the revised NIS
Standard Instructions. When approved by the NIS Committee, the
revised NIS Memos will be issued In connection with NIS operational
matters only to those holders of the NIS Standard Instruction®
directly concerned with NIS production.

The chairman requested the members to review the drafts
before the next meeting and to indicate the number of sets of the
revised NIS Memos which their agencies will require.

4„ Definitions of Map Scales

The Committee considered recommendation of the
Chapter DC Subcommittee, contained in memorandum dated
5 December 1957, to the effect that when the terms "large, "
"medium," and "small" are used to define map scales in the
NIS they be defined as follows;

Small Scale - 1.600,000 and smaller
Medium Scale ~ Larger than 1:600,000, but

smaller than 1:75,000
Large Scale ~ 1:75, 00.0 and larger.

The Army member observed that the original Army
proposal was not confined to the use of these definitions in Chapters IX,
but to their use throughout the entire NIS. The Committee deferred
action on this matter until the next meeting.

5. Downgrading of NIS Material

By memorandum of 2 December 195? the Air Force member
submitted for Committee consideration a request from the Directorate
of' Intelligence, Continental Air Command, concerning the downgrading
of certain portions of Sections 81, 82, and 83 in NIS 26 (USSR). It is
planned to utilise this information in the preparation of unclassified
material being prepared for the specialized training of reservists in
the Intelligence career field.

The Army member suggested that revised material in
preparation for the maintenance contribution of Section 81 can be
released for OFFICIAL USE ONLY purposes. The Committee ap-
proved this procedure and requested the Army member to furnish
the revised material to the Air Force member in fulfillment of this
requirement. The Navy and Air Force members stated that the
requested material from Sections 82 and 83 can be declassified for
this purpose.
