Hypoxia and lactate metabolism-related gene COL5A3 promotes triple negative breast cancer progression via DDR1/FAK/PI3K/AKT pathway
Authors: Quan-feng Shao, Zi-ying Wang, Wei Liang, Yuan-yuan Li, Bei Zhu, Wei-xian Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTriple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is a highly aggressive subtype characterized by a lack of hormone receptors and specific molecular targets, leading to limited treatment options, high recurrence rates, and poor clinical prognosis. The Warburg effect in cancer cells generates a tumor microenvironment characterized by hypoxia, low glucose, and high lactate levels, which severely promotes chemoresistance, immune escape, and distant metastasis. Nevertheless, the prognostic significance of hypoxia and lactate metabolism-related genes in TNBC remains largely unclear, and more accurate prediction models are urgently needed.MethodsClinical information and gene expression profiles of patients with TNBC were obtained from online databases. LASSO and multivariate Cox regression analyses were used to establish the hypoxia and lactate metabolism-related prediction model, and the prognostic value was further validated using Kaplan-Meier plotter, receiver operating characteristic curves, and nomogram. Gene set enrichment analysis was performed to evaluate the pathways and molecular functions. Tumor mutation, microsatellite instability, RNA expression-based stemness scores, immune infiltration, and drug susceptibility analyses were performed to identify potential therapeutic targets. Single-cell transcriptome data were processed and quality-controlled using Seurat. Monocle2, CellChat, and ssGSEA analyses were used to investigate the relationship between COL5A3, malignant phenotype, and tumor microenvironment. A series of in vitro experiments was performed to evaluate the biological functions of hub gene COL5A3.ResultsFive genes, COL5A3, LRRC8D, IGFL1, SEPTIN3, and PEG10, were identified to establish the hypoxia and lactate metabolism-related risk score. TNBC patients divided into high-risk or low-risk groups according to the risk score exhibited substantial differences in survival outcome, tumor mutation, immune infiltration, and drug sensitivity. The constructed model showed strong predictive performance, with receiver operating characteristic curves showing area under the curve values of 0.82, 0.86, and 0.90 at 365, 1085, and 1825 days, respectively. Single-cell analysis showed that high COL5A3 expression was associated with greater proliferative capacity, drug resistance, and stemness. COL5A3 is also involved in regulating the interaction between TNBC cells and other cells in the tumor microenvironment. In vitro studies have indicated that COL5A3 could promote tumor proliferation, migration, invasion, and drug resistance via the DDR1/FAK/PI3K/AKT pathway in TNBC cells. OF-1 was initially identified as a candidate compound with potential therapeutic relevance for TNBC.ConclusionA novel hypoxia and lactate metabolism-related gene signature could be used for prognosis prediction in TNBC patients, and the hub gene COL5A3 promotes TNBC progression via the DDR1/FAK/PI3K/AKT pathway. Introduction

Breast cancer is the most common malignancy in women and the leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide[1]. Compared to other subtypes, the treatment of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) remains a great challenge and is characterized by drug resistance and poor prognosis[2]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to identify new biomarkers, establish new prognostic prediction models, and identify potential therapeutic targets[3].

Hypoxia in the tumor microenvironment is an important characteristic of solid tumors and is mainly caused by the surge in oxygen consumption and abnormal angiogenesis of rapidly proliferating cancer cells[4]. Under hypoxic conditions, hypoxia-inducible factor-1α is activated to drive the expression of glycolysis-related genes, promote tumor metabolic reprogramming such as the Warburg effect, and preferentially supply energy through anaerobic glycolysis even under oxygen-rich conditions[5-6]. This metabolic rewiring leads to lactic acid buildup, driving tumor aggressiveness via immune evasion and metabolic symbiosis. Hypoxia induces PD-L1 overexpression and suppresses T cell-mediated immune surveillance, enabling tumor immune escape[7]. Hypoxia-inducible factor-1α activation leads to angiogenesis and enhances vascular permeability and metastatic dissemination[8-9]. Hypoxia enhances DNA repair pathways and reduces reactive oxygen species generation, thereby conferring resistance to radiotherapy and chemotherapy[10-11].

Hypoxia drives tumor cells to reprogram their metabolism toward glycolysis, resulting in accelerated glucose uptake and consequent lactate overproduction[12]. Lactate is a multifunctional regulator of cancer progression and not merely a metabolic byproduct. Lactate is exported into the tumor microenvironment via monocarboxylate transporters, creating an acidic niche that suppresses cytotoxic T lymphocytes and natural killer cell-mediated tumor surveillance, yet activates immunosuppressive Tregs and M2-polarized macrophages[13-16]. Lactate also serves as a metabolic substrate that drives the lactylation modifications of histones and non-histone proteins[17-18]. In TNBC, histone lactylation-driven transcriptional activation of the DNA repair machinery and chemoresistance regulators promotes tumor progression and therapeutic escape[19]. Glycolytic cancer cells form metabolic coupling with oxidized stromal cells, such as cancer-associated fibroblasts, which promote glycolysis and epithelial-mesenchymal transition of TNBC cells by secreting colony-stimulating factors[20].

Hypoxia and lactate metabolism are closely related to the occurrence and development of TNBC. However, the specific mechanisms underlying hypoxia and lactate metabolism in TNBC have not been fully elucidated. Therefore, it is necessary to comprehensively evaluate the impact of hypoxia and lactate metabolism on TNBC prognosis, tumor immune microenvironment, and targeted therapy. In this study, we established a novel prognostic model for TNBC by combining hypoxia-related genes and lactate metabolism-related genes, and identified a potential biomarker for TNBC. This study aimed to use this novel prognostic model and new biomarkers to predict patient prognosis and explore potential therapeutic targets to improve the treatment effect and overall survival of patients with TNBC.

Materials and methods

Data collection and preprocessing

Transcriptome data and clinical information of patients with TNBC were obtained from the TCGA database. After excluding patients with less than 30 days of follow-up and incomplete prognostic information or transcriptome data, a total of 106 TNBC patients were included in the study. The information on TNBC patients from GSE21653, GSE135565, and GSE58812 was obtained from the GEO database and used as validation cohorts. Single-cell RNA sequencing data for TNBC from GSE161529 were downloaded from the GEO database. Hypoxia-related genes and lactate metabolism-related genes were downloaded from the Molecular Signatures Database[21]. After removing duplicates, 200 hypoxia-related genes and 387 lactate metabolism-related genes were identified.

Identification of differentially expressed genes

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between TNBC tumor and paracancerous tissues were identified using the limma package in R language. Overall survival-related genes were identified by Univariate Cox regression analysis using the survival package in R. The intersections of hypoxia-related genes and lactate metabolism-related genes with differentially expressed genes and overall survival-related genes were determined, and 14 hypoxia-related differentially expressed genes (HRDEGs) and 10 lactate metabolism-related differentially expressed genes (LMRDEGs) were finally identified.

Clustering of HRDEGs and LMRDEGs

Based on these 24 genes, consensus clustering was performed using the ConsensusClusterPlus package in R language. The optimal number of clusters "K" was identified using the average consistency within the group and cumulative distribution function (CDF)[22]. Differences in overall survival between group C1 and C2 were calculated using the survival package in the R language. DEGs between group C1 and C2 were identified using the limma package in R.

Construction and validation of a prognostic model based on HRDEGs and LMRDEGs

Based on the DEGs between group C1 and C2, lasso regression and multi-cox regression analyses were performed to establish a gene signature. λmin is selected as the optimal penalty parameter λ. The risk score for each patient was derived from a linear combination of expression values (weighted by coefficients) derived from multivariate Cox regression analysis. . Coefficient i denotes the coefficient of relevant prognostic genes in the multivariate Cox regression model. Patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups using the optimal risk score cutoff value calculated using the Maxstat package in the R language. Kaplan-Meier and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analyses were performed to evaluate the effectiveness of the model. The GSE21653, GSE135565, and GSE58812 cohorts were analyzed in the same manner as the external validations.

Construction of a predictive nomogram

To enhance the accuracy in predicting TNBC prognosis, clinical stage (including T, N, M classifications, and overall stage) and risk score were integrated to construct a nomogram using the nomoR package. ROC curves were generated using the timeroc package in R to assess survival probabilities at 1-, 2-, and 3-years. Nomogram calibration plots were used to compare the predicted and observed survival outcomes at these time points.

Function and pathway enrichment analysis

Five genes from the predictive model were submitted to the GeneMANIA database to retrieve protein-protein interaction networks. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), Gene Ontology (GO), and gene set enrichment analysis were performed using the R clusterProfiler package [23].

Tumor mutation burden, immune infiltration, and drug sensitivity

Tumor mutation burden (TMB) was computed using R package maftools. Microsatellite instability (MSI) values of TNBC patients were assessed using the TCGAplot package in R, while RNA expression-based stemness scores (RNAss) were retrieved from the UCSCXenaTools package in R. To explore immune infiltration in TNBC patients from TCGA cohort, the CIBERSORT algorithm was applied. ESTIMATEScore, StromalScore, and ImmuneScore were computed using the estimate package in R. Finally, to explore potential therapeutic targets for TNBC, drug sensitivity was predicted based on the hypoxia-and lactate metabolism-related prognostic model using the oncoPredict package in the R language.

Single-cell analysis

The R statistical environment (version 4.5.1) was used to perform single-cell analysis. The Seurat package was used for initial data processing and quality control. This process included retaining genes that were detected in a minimum of three cells and discarding cells with mitochondrial read proportions exceeding 15%. In addition, cells with fewer than 100 detected genes were excluded. Cellular annotations were automatically generated using SingleR algorithm. The infercnv algorithm was used to distinguish between malignant and nonmalignant cells. Cell-cell communication was assessed using CellChat, based on a human database. Gene set enrichment scores within single-cell RNA sequencing datasets were calculated using the ssGSEA algorithm. Finally, the developmental trajectory of the TNBC cells was reconstructed using the Monocle2 algorithm.

Cell culture and qRT-PCR

The TNBC cell line MDA-MB-231, MDA-MB-468 and the human mammary epithelial cell line MCF-10A were procured from Procell (Wuhan, China). MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-468 cells were maintained in Dulbecco's modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) (Gibco, CA, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, Gibco, and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. MCF-10A cells were cultured in DMEM containing 5% horse serum, 20 ng/mL epidermal growth factor, and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Procell, Wuhan, China). Both the cell lines were incubated under standard conditions (37°C, 5% CO2). Total RNA was extracted from the cultured cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). Reverse transcription was performed according to the manufacturer's protocol (Vazyme, Jiangsu, China). Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed using Taq Pro Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, Jiangsu, China). Results were quantified using the ΔΔCt method.

Western blot

Proteins extracted from cells using RIPA lysis buffer were quantified using a BCA assay (Pierce™ BCA Protein Assay Kit, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Equal amounts of protein were separated using 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Millipore). After blocking with 5% non-fat milk for 2 h, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies: COL5A3, DDR1, p-DDR1, FAK, p-FAK, PI3K, p-PI3K, AKT, p-AKT (1:1000, Proteintech Group, Wuhan, China), and β-actin (1:5000, Proteintech Group, Wuhan, China). HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5000; Biorbyt) were applied for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using an ECL substrate (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and quantified using the ImageJ software.

Small interfering RNAs transfection

Three small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting COL5A3 and the corresponding negative control were synthesized by RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). Transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 (Thermo Fisher, CA, USA), according to the manufacturer's instructions. Transfection efficiency was verified after 48 h using western blotting and qRT-PCR. The siRNA with the highest knockdown efficiency was selected for the subsequent experiments.

Transwell assay and wound-healing assay

Cell invasion and migration were assessed using transwell and wound healing assays. Log-phase cells were processed, counted, and re-suspended at 1×105 cells/mL (doubled for the invasion experiments). Transwell chambers were placed in 24-well plates with 500 μL of 20% fetal bovine serum-supplemented medium in the lower chamber and 100 μL of serum-free medium containing 1×104 cells in the upper chamber. Following 48 h of incubation, the cells were formaldehyde-fixed, crystal violet-stained, and imaged microscopically. A homogeneous single-cell suspension was generated from log-phase cells, with the density calibrated to 2×106 cells/mL via microscopic counting. Each well of a 6-well plate received 2 mL of suspension (4×106 cells/well), reaching 90% confluency after overnight culture. A sterile 10 μL pipette tip was used to generate uniform scratch wounds, and floating cells were then washed off with PBS and replaced with fresh culture medium. Scratch closure was documented using microscopic imaging at 0, 24, and 48 h post-wounding.

CCK-8 assay

Cells were trypsinized, counted, and seeded in 96-well plates (5,000 cells/well) with six replicate wells per group. Once cell attachment was confirmed, the old medium was replaced with a freshly prepared 1:10 CCK-8-medium mixture. Each well received 110 µL of the mixture, followed by 24-, 48-, and 72-hour incubation under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO2). The absorbance was recorded using a microplate spectrophotometer to assess cell proliferation.

Co-immunoprecipitation

Co-Immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assays were performed as follows. Briefly, cells were lysed on ice in pre-chilled lysis buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors and phosphatase inhibitors. The lysates were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. A portion of the supernatant was reserved as the “Input” control. The remaining supernatant was incubated with pre-washed beads and specific primary antibodies (or isotype-matched IgG as a negative control) overnight at 4 °C with gentle rotation. The next day, the beads were washed three times with lysis buffer. Bound proteins were eluted by boiling the beads in 2×SDS loading buffer at 95 °C for 5 min. Finally, the precipitated protein complexes were analyzed by Western Blot.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analysis were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 and R software (version 4.5.1, http://www.R-project.org/) with specified packages including Survival (version 3.5-7), limma (version 3.56.2), ConsensusClusterPlus (version 1.68.0), NomoR (version 2.1.3), Timeroc (version 0.9.6), Maftools (version 2.18.0), TCGAplot (version 1.4.0), UCSCXenaTools (version 4.0.0), Estimate (version 1.3.9), OncoPredict (version 0.5.2), Ggalluvial (version 0.13.0), Glmnet (version 4.1-8), Maxstat (version 0.7-30), infercnv (version 1.23.0), Seurat (version 5.3.0), CellChat (version 1.6.1), monocle (version 2.36.0), and GSVA version (version 2.2.0). Student's t-test or chi-square test was used to assess the differences between variables. Wilcoxon test was used to evaluate the distribution disparities of tumor-infiltrating immune cells. Pearson's correlation analysis was used to identify the linear relationships among continuous variables. Survival outcomes were analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method, and statistical significance was determined using the log-rank test. Independent prognostic factors were identified using univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazard regression analyses. Spearman's rank correlation was applied to quantify associations among the risk score, TMB, RNAss, MSI, and immune cell infiltration levels. Area under the curve (AUC) was applied to examine the accuracy of the nomogram predictions. p < 0.05 was defined as statistically significant for all analyses. Unless otherwise stated, in all differences analyses, |FC| >1.5.

Results

Identification of HRDEGs and LMRDEGs in TNBC

The analytical framework of this study is shown in Fig. 1. RNA-seq data for 106 TNBC patients and 113 normal breast tissue samples were downloaded from the TCGA database (paired adjacent tissues). To comprehensively identify the key molecular signatures, we performed differential expression analysis by comparing TNBC specimens with normal breast tissue samples from healthy controls. To avoid omissions, using predefined thresholds of |FC| > 1.3 and p < 0.05, this analysis revealed 9,331 DEGs, with 4,447 demonstrating significant upregulation and 4,884 showing downregulation patterns (Fig. 2A). Additionally, 200 hypoxia-related genes and 387 lactate metabolism-related genes were identified from the Molecular Signatures Database. Univariate Cox regression analysis was conducted on RNA-seq data obtained from the TNBC patient cohort. This approach identified 3,488 overall survival related genes. The intersections of hypoxia-related genes and lactate metabolism-related genes with DEGs and overall survival related genes were performed, and 14 HRDEGs and 10 LMRDEGs were finally identified (Fig. 2B).

Clustering based on the HRDEGs and LMRDEGs

Unsupervised clustering was performed on 106 TNBC samples using the 14 HRDEGs and 10 LMRDEGs. When the value of K was determined to be 2, the clustering results were relatively stable, and the CDF delta showed the slowest downward trend (Fig. 2C), indicating that the difference was most significant when TNBC samples were divided into two groups (Fig. 2D). Therefore, samples were divided into C1 and C2 groups (Fig. 2E). The Kaplan-Meier curve was used to analyze the difference in survival between the two groups. The overall survival (OS) of the C1 subgroup was significantly better than that of the C2 subgroup (Fig. 2F), which may be related to the differential expression of the HRDEGs and LMRDEGs between C1 and C2. Sankey’s chart showed that the differences between the C1 and C2 group may be related to the patients’ clinical stage, which was drawn using the ggalluvial package in R language (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Construction of hypoxia and lactate metabolism-related prognostic model for TNBC patients

Based on the DEGs between C1 and C2 (Supplementary Figure 2), we developed a prognostic prediction model via LASSO analysis using the glmnet package in the R language. After selecting the optimal penalty parameter λ (λ = 0.0927677526036291) associated with the minimum five-fold cross-validation (Fig. 3A), five genes were selected as candidate genes, namely, COL5A3, LRRC8D, IGFL1, SEPTIN3, and PEG10 (Fig. 3B). Univariate analysis revealed that all these five genes were associated with survival (Fig. 3C). Notably, LRRC8D, SEPTIN3, and PEG10 were associated with a better prognosis; whereas COL5A3 and IGFL1 were associated with worse prognosis. Finally, these five genes were used to construct a prognostic model using a multivariate Cox regression analysis. A risk score calculation formula was obtained as follows: RiskScore = 0.183529939206765 * COL5A3 - 0.116148206752606 * LRRC8D + 0.0129108060717779 * IGFL1 - 0.187553364273964 * SEPTIN3 - 0.00334183135988184 * PEG10. The risk scores of 106 patients with TNBC in TCGA cohort were calculated. Then, according to the optimal cutoff value (risk score =0.62120117) calculated using the maxstat package in R, the patients were divided into high-risk and low-risk groups. According to the Kaplan-Meier curve (Fig. 3D), the overall survival of patients in the high-risk group was significantly worse than that in the low-risk group. Time-dependent ROC analysis was used to further evaluate the prediction efficiency of the constructed gene signature using the timeroc package in R, with the AUC of 365, 1095, and 1825 days were 0.82, 0.86, and 0.90, respectively (Fig. 3E). Correlation heat maps showed that the expression levels of LRRC8D, SEPTIN3, and PEG10 were higher in the low-risk patients; whereas COL5A3 and IGFL1 expression levels were higher in the high-risk group (Fig. 3F).

Validation of prognostic model for TNBC patients

To validate the accuracy of the prognostic model, we divided patients in the GSE21653, GSE135565, and GSE58812 cohorts into high- and low-risk groups, based on the optimal cutoff value of the risk score calculated using the same formula. Kaplan-Meier curves showed that overall survival time of TNBC patients in the high-risk group was significantly lower than that in the low-risk group in all three validated cohorts (Fig. 4A). ROC curves showed that the prediction model was effective in all three validation cohorts (Fig. 4B). The above results indicate that the prognostic model is accurate in predicting the survival outcome of patients with TNBC.

Construction of a nomogram

Previous studies have shown that patients’ clinical stages might affect their prognosis. Therefore, we used the patients’ clinical stage (including T, N, M, and overall stages) and risk score to establish a nomogram to predict the patient’s prognosis (Fig. 4C). Based on the time-dependent ROC curve, the AUC reached 0.99, 0.99 and 0.90 at 365, 730, and 1095 days, respectively (Fig. 4D). Calibration curves showed the predictive value of the nomogram and displayed high accuracy in predicting the prognosis (Fig. 4E). These results revealed that the nomogram may be a useful method for predicting the prognosis of TNBC patients.

Differences in pathways and molecular functions between the two groups

To better understand the heterogeneity between the two groups, we performed a gene set enrichment analysis based on the DEGs between the high- and low-risk groups (Fig. 5A). KEGG analysis revealed that the extracellular matrix, metabolism-related pathways, and immune-related pathways were significantly enriched (Fig. 5B). GO analysis, including biological processes, cell components, and molecular functions, revealed that the DEGs between the high-risk and low-risk groups were mainly involved in the extracellular matrix, intercellular links, intercellular information transfer, and immune-related pathways (Fig. 5C). Gene set enrichment analysis indicated that the high-risk group was markedly enriched in angiogenesis, coagulation, metabolism, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and the P53 pathway (Supplementary Fig. 3). We uploaded the five genes to predict the function of these genes and relevant genes using the GeneMANIA database. The 20 most relevant genes were selected, and functional analysis revealed that they were mainly involved in the extracellular matrix and collagen fibers (Fig. 5D). Thus, the differences between the high-risk and low-risk groups were mainly related to the tumor microenvironment, tumor metabolism, tumor immunity, and tumor angiogenesis.

Tumor mutation burden (TMB), Microsatellite instability (MSI) and RNA expression-based stemness score (RNAss)

Our study revealed that TP53, TTN, and KMT2D (90.7%, 20.4%, and 13.0%, respectively) mutations were the most frequent in the high-risk group in the TCGA-TNBC cohort; whereas TP53, MUC16, and TTN (93.5%, 25.8%, and 25.8%, respectively) were the most common mutations in the low-risk group. Missense mutations were the main mutation type in both high- and low-risk groups. Furthermore, the difference in the mutational landscape between the high- and low-risk groups revealed heterogeneity between the two groups (Fig. 6A). There is a close correlation between TMB, MSI, and prognosis and efficacy of immunotherapy in cancer patients. Therefore, we analyzed TMB and MSI in the TCGA-TNBC cohort. We performed a survival analysis and found that patients with high TMB had a better prognosis (Fig. 6B). Moreover, patients in the low-risk group tended to have a higher TMB, but the difference was not significant (Fig. 6C). There were substantial differences in TMB among the different N stages (Fig. 6D). The Kaplan-Meier curve revealed a better prognosis in the MSI-H group (Fig. 6E). In addition, we found a positive correlation between MSI and TMB by correlation analysis (Fig. 6F). MSI score was higher in the immunotherapy-sensitive group (Fig. 6G). The stemness of tumor cells is closely related to the degree of tumor malignancy. The stronger the stemness of the tumor cells, the higher their proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and treatment resistance, leading to an improvement in the overall degree of malignancy. We found that there was a worse prognosis in patients with high RNAss, according to survival analysis (Fig. 6H). Meanwhile, there was a positive correlation between the risk score and the RNAss (Fig. 6I). Therefore, we believe that the poorer prognosis of patients in the high-risk group may be related to TMB, MSI, and RNAss. This again illustrates the value of the risk score calculated by the prediction model we constructed.

Immune infiltration between high-risk group and low-risk group

To explore immune infiltration in TNBC patients, we used the CIBERSORT algorithm. The results demonstrated that the distribution of different immune cell types differed between the high- and low-risk groups. The degree of infiltration of Follicular Helper T cells and activated dendritic cells negatively correlated with the risk score (Fig. 6J). Among the five genes, COL5A3 was the most significantly associated with differences in immune infiltration. In addition, Estimatescore, Immunescore, and Stromalscore were calculated using the estimate package in R language, which is commonly used as a key indicator to evaluate tumor microenvironment. The levels of Estimatescore and Stromalscore were significantly different between the high- and low-risk groups (Fig. 6K). In conclusion, there was a difference in immune infiltration between the high- and low-risk groups, which may be a potential factor in the difference in survival prognosis between the two groups.

COL5A3 was identified as the hub gene of the prediction model

Gene enrichment analyses demonstrated that differential pathways between the high- and low-risk groups were involved in the extracellular matrix, immunity, and collagen fibers. By searching the NCBI Entrez Gene, we found that COL5A3 was the gene encoding collagen. Collagen is a major component of the extracellular matrix. Among the five genes, COL5A3 was most strongly associated with immune infiltration (Fig. 6J). Therefore, COL5A3 was identified as a hub gene and used for subsequent in vitro validation. Compared to MCF-10 A, MDA-MB-231 cells had higher COL5A3 expression levels (Fig. 6L). This, along with the bioinformatics results, suggests that COL5A3 could promote TNBC progression.

Single-cell analysis of COL5A3 in TNBC cells

Based on the fact that COL5A3 was used as a hub gene for the prediction model and was associated with poor prognosis while being highly expressed in TNBC cells, we further used TNBC single-cell data (GSE161529) for analysis. We used the Seurat software package to cluster the cells and identify major cell populations. These included B-cells, CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, dendritic cells, endothelial cells, epithelial cells, fibroblasts and macrophages (Fig. 7A). Epithelial cells were identified as malignant cells (TNBC cells) and nonmalignant cells using the inferCNV algorithm. TNBC cells were divided into six subgroups based on the clustering analysis (Fig. 7B). Among these, COL5A3 showed the highest expression in cluster 1 (Fig. 7C). The Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition, Drug Resistance, Proliferation, and Stemness enrichment scores of TNBC cells were determined using ssGSEA via R package GSVA. The results showed that cluster 1 was higher than the other subgroups for all four scores (Fig. 7D). Simultaneously, we performed GO and KEGG analyses of the genes highly expressed in cluster 1. The results showed that they were mainly enriched in the cell cycle, drug binding, DNA repair, cellular senescence, p53 signaling pathway, and other tumor-related pathways (Fig. 7E). Therefore, cluster 1 was considered to have a greater proliferative capacity, drug resistance, and stemness. In addition, using CellChat, we explored ligand-receptor interactions between cluster 1 and other cells, and found that cluster 1 had a higher frequency and strength of interaction with macrophages (Fig. 7F). The bubble chart shows the most significant part of the interaction, which mainly involved proliferation and immune regulation (Fig. 7G). Using the Monocle algorithm to infer the differentiation trajectory of TNBC cells (Fig. 7H), we determined that cluster 1 was mainly distributed in the front part of the trajectory (Fig. 7I).

COL5A3 promoted tumor malignant behavior and the identification of potential drug in TNBC

Three COL5A3-targeting siRNAs and a control siRNA (si-NC) were designed and transfected into MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-468 cells to investigate the effects of COL5A3 on the biological behavior of TNBC cells. qRT-PCR results demonstrated that all three siRNAs reduced COL5A3 levels in two cell lines, with si-RNA3 exhibiting the strongest silencing effect (Fig. 8A). Western blotting results also showed that the knockdown effect of si-RNA3 was the best (Fig. 8B). Based on these findings, we selected si-RNA3 for further experiments to suppress COL5A3 expression. The results of the CCK-8 assay showed that the knockdown of COL5A3 expression significantly inhibited the proliferation of two kinds of TNBC cells (Fig. 8C). In addition, the wound-healing and transwell assays indicated that suppressing COL5A3 markedly impaired two kinds of TNBC cells invasion and migration (Fig. 8D-E). In conclusion, COL5A3 promotes tumor proliferation, migration and invasion in TNBC.

Drug sensitivity prediction

Drug sensitivity was predicted based on the expression of COL5A3 using the oncoPredict package in the R language. These results suggested that the sensitivity to one drug (OF-1) was significantly correlated with the expression of COL5A3 (Fig. 8F), and the higher the expression level of COL5A3, the lower the sensitivity of OF-1. This may provide a new therapeutic drug for TNBC patients. To explore whether OF-1 could be used as an effective therapeutic drug for TNBC, we performed a CCK-8 assay using OF-1. Besides, COL5A3 has been proven to enhance resistance to gemcitabine in pancreatic cancer; however, relevant studies in TNBC are lacking. Therefore, gemcitabine was also selected for use in the CCK-8 assay. First, multiple concentration gradients of OF-1 and gemcitabine were established to evaluate their half-maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC50) in two cell lines. Dose-response assays revealed that the IC50 value (3.51 µM) of OF-1 was comparable to the IC50 value (2.19 µM) of gemcitabine in MDA-MB-231 (Supplementary Table 1), a first-line chemotherapeutic agent for TNBC. As same time, the result revealed that the IC50 value (7.028 µM) of OF-1 was comparable to the IC50 value (2.014 µM) of gemcitabine in MDA-MB-468 (Supplementary Table 2). These results suggest that OF-1 may serve as a potential therapeutic candidate for TNBC. Further experiments were performed to explore the relationship between COL5A3 expression and sensitivity to these two drugs. To exclude the decrease in proliferative capacity brought about by COL5A3 knockdown, the results were normalized. The results demonstrated that after knocking down COL5A3, the sensitivity to OF-1 and gemcitabine was significantly higher in MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-468 cells (Fig. 8G), indicating that high expression of COL5A3 promoted chemotherapy resistance in TNBC.

COL5A3 promoted the tumor malignant behavior via PI3K/AKT pathway in TNBC

To further explore the mechanism by which COL5A3 promotes malignant behavior in TNBC, we performed enrichment analysis based on the level of COL5A3 expression. The results showed that the PI3K/AKT pathway was the most relevant pathway (Fig. 9A). It was well known that the PI3K/AKT pathway drove malignant progression in cancer cells by regulating proliferation, apoptosis, extracellular matrix interactions, and metabolic reprogramming. Previous studies have demonstrated that the PI3K/AKT pathway promotes adhesion, invasion, and migration of cancer cells by interacting with collagen. Therefore, the PI3K/AKT pathway is likely to be downstream of COL5A3. A series of experiments was performed to verify this view. Western blotting showed that the protein expression of p-AKT and p-PI3K decreased markedly, but AKT and PI3K did not decrease when COL5A3 was knocked down (Fig. 9B). Therefore, COL5A3 may be involved in the phosphorylation of PI3K/AKT. Wound healing and transwell assays further demonstrated that IGF-1 supplementation effectively reversed the suppression of TNBC cell invasion and migration caused by COL5A3 silencing (Fig. 9C-D). The CCK-8 assay revealed that the addition of IGF-1 (a PI3K/AKT pathway activator) markedly rescued the TNBC cell proliferation impaired by COL5A3 knockdown (Fig. 9E). These results confirmed that COL5A3 promoted tumor proliferation, migration, and invasion via the PI3K/AKT pathway in TNBC.

To further investigate how extracellular matrix components such as COL5A3 initiate downstream intracellular signal cascades, we reviewed a large number of literature. We found that discoidin domain receptors (DDRs) are likely to be potential membrane surface receptors for collagen, capable of transmitting collagen signals from the extracellular to the intracellular environment. Among them, DDR1 is considered a common transmembrane receptor for all types of collagen [24–25], and studies have shown that DDR1 promotes the invasion and metastasis of breast cancer through FAK [26]. Moreover, numerous studies have demonstrated that the FAK/PI3K/AKT signaling pathway is involved in the malignant progression of various cancers. Therefore, we boldly hypothesized that COL5A3 promotes the malignant progression of triple-negative breast cancer through the DDR1/FAK/PI3K/AKT axis. Then we designed a series of subsequent experiments. The WB assay result showed that after knockdown of COL5A3, the total protein levels of DDR1 and FAK did not significantly decrease, but the phosphorylation levels decreased significantly (Fig. 9F). This initially indicates that COL5A3 promotes the phosphorylation of DDR1 and FAK. Then, considering that DDR1 might be a membrane-binding protein of COL5A3, we conducted a Co-IP eesay to verify their direct binding. The results showed that there was a direct binding between DDR1 and COL5A3 (Fig. 9G). This further demonstrated that COL5A3 transmits signals from the extracellular environment to the intracellular environment through DDR1, thereby promoting the malignant progression of TNBC. Next, the inhibitor of DDR1 was added to the cells, and then the expression of related proteins was detected. The results indicated that after adding the inhibitor of DDR1, the phosphorylation levels of DDR1,FAK, PI3k and AKT all decreased significantly (Fig. 9H). Finally, we introduced a constitutively active FAK construct (Y397E mutant) into COL5A3-knockdown cells. Western blot analysis revealed that, compared to the COL5A3 knockdown group alone, the re-expression of this mutant significantly restored the phosphorylation levels of FAK, PI3K, and AKT (Fig. 9I). In summary, COL5A3 activates the intracellular FAK/PIK/AKT pathway through the transmembrane protein DDR1, thereby promoting the malignant progression of TNBC.

Discussion

TNBC represents the most aggressive subtype of breast cancer with poor prognosis. Despite chemotherapy being the primary treatment, therapeutic efficacy is markedly limited by high tumor heterogeneity, early recurrence rates, and rapid development of drug resistance [27]. Therefore, it is urgent to analyze the molecular characteristics of TNBC and seek reliable novel biomarkers to optimize individualized treatment strategies and improve patient survival.

Hypoxia, a hallmark of the solid tumor microenvironment, arises from abnormal vessel formation and rapid tumor growth, leading to low oxygen levels that activate hypoxia-inducible factors and drive metabolic reprogramming, a process that fuels cancer progression and therapy resistance across multiple cancer types [28]. In breast cancer, a hypoxic microenvironment is strongly associated with increased tumor aggressiveness. It accelerates local invasion and distant metastasis by triggering the epithelial-mesenchymal transition and promoting new blood vessel growth. Additionally, there is a “Warburg effect” in breast cancer cells, which relies on glycolysis to produce lactate [29]. Lactate is not only an energy substrate, but also inhibits T cell function, promotes immune escape by acidifying the microenvironment, and mediates tumor-stroma interaction through the “lactate shuttle” mechanism to accelerate metastasis [30]. In TNBC, the metabolic adaptation induced by hypoxia is more significant, which manifests as enhanced glycolysis dependence and increased lactate secretion. This metabolic feature not only drives immune escape and chemotherapy resistance but is also closely related to the risk of early tumor recurrence and metastasis.

In this study, we identified a predictive model based on hypoxia-related genes and lactate metabolism-related genes, consisting of five genes. Among the five genes, high expression of LRRC8D, SEPTIN3, and PEG10 was associated with a better prognosis, whereas high expression of COL5A3 and IGFL1 was associated with a poor prognosis. Kaplan-Meier and ROC curves validated the predictive accuracy of the hypoxia-lactate prognostic model across multiple independent cohorts. A prognostic nomogram that integrated risk score and clinical stage (including T, N, M stage, and overall stage) was generated, with AUC reaching 0.99, 0.99, 0.90 at 365, 730, and 1085 days. Collectively, these findings highlight the strong predictive capacity and potential of hypoxia and lactate metabolism-related models as clinically actionable tools for predicting TNBC survival.

LRRC8D encodes the Leucine-Rich Repeat-Containing Protein 8D, which is part of the Volume-Regulated Anion Channel (VRCA). The main role of VRCA is to regulate cell swelling, activation, and anion permeability [31]. VRAC can transport cisplatin and carboplatin, and the depletion of LRRC8D induces cisplatin and carboplatin resistance in mouse mammary tumor cells [32]. IGFL1 promotes lung cancer progression by inhibiting apoptosis [33]. IGFL1 may promote an inflammatory response by activating the cytokine TNF-α. High IGFL1 expression promotes the progression of breast cancer through an inflammatory response [34, 35]. The migration and invasion abilities of PEG10 overexpression cells were significantly increased. PEG10 may promote cancer progression by inhibiting TGF-β signaling by interacting with the TGF-β receptor ACVRL1 [36]. In addition, PEG10 overexpression promotes palbociclib resistance in breast cancer cells by altering the cell cycle [37]. SEPTIN3, a member of the septin family primarily expressed in the brain and testis, is a membrane-bound presynaptic protein involved in autophagy [38]. Previous studies have found that SEPTIN3 is overexpressed in TNBC and is associated with poor patient prognosis [39]. SEPTIN3 also promotes cell growth and oncogenesis. SEPTIN3 promotes TNBC cell aggressiveness and proliferation via activation of the Wnt signaling pathway [40]. COL5A3, a member of the collagen triple-helical repeat family, regulates cellular proliferation and motility. Compared with normal breast cells, COL5A3 was significantly highly expressed in TNBC cells and often indicated a poor prognosis in TNBC patients [39]. In pancreatic cancer, COL5A3 enhances gemcitabine resistance through metabolic reprogramming [41]. COL5A3 mediates breast cancer brain metastasis by modulating cancer-associated fibroblast infiltration, and serves as a predictor of therapy resistance [42].

Previous studies have shown that the tumor immune microenvironment is significantly related to the occurrence, development, survival, and prognosis of tumors. Therefore, we performed a series of immune infiltration analyses to compare the high- and low-risk groups. The results showed that there were significant differences in T cell follicular helper and dendritic cell activation between the high and low groups, which were negatively correlated with the risk score. T follicular helper cells can activate CD8+ T cells by secreting IL-21, enhance their killing function, and inhibit tumor growth. Highly activated dendritic cells activate anti-tumor T-cell responses. In conclusion, the low activation degree of anti-tumor associated immune cells may be the reason for the poor prognosis of patients in the high-risk group.

TMB has been validated as a key biomarker for predicting responses to immune checkpoint blockade [43]. In the TCGA-TNBC cohort, patients with H-TMB tumors exhibited better survival outcomes. MSI is defined as the emergence of novel microsatellite alleles owing to insertions or deletions of repeated DNA sequences [40]. Clinically, MSI-H tumors frequently exhibit favorable outcomes, particularly in early stage cancer [44–45]. MSI-H patients had a more sensitive response to immunotherapy and a significantly better prognosis in the TCGA-TNBC cohort. RNAss is a quantitative index established by analyzing the expression patterns of tumor stem cell-related genes, which are used to evaluate the stemness characteristics of tumor cells (i.e., self-renewal, differentiation potential, and drug resistance) [46]. Breast cancer patients with high levels of RNAss tend to exhibit more aggressive disease and worse survival outcomes. Breast cancer cells with high levels of RNAss were less sensitive to anthracyclines and taxanes. RNAss were positively correlated with the risk score, and patients with H-RNAss had a worse prognosis in the TCGA-TNBC cohort. Drug sensitivity analyses were performed between the high- and low-risk groups. The results revealed that the high-risk group exhibited less sensitivity to most standard breast cancer therapies, including chemotherapy and targeted agents, as well as those used for other malignancies. In conclusion, TMB, MSI, and RNAss combined with the risk score calculated by the model could predict the survival prognosis of patients and the effects of therapy.

Four gene enrichment analyses were integrated, including KEGG pathway analysis, GO annotation, gene set enrichment analysis, and the GeneMANIA database. The results revealed that the differences between the two groups were mainly in the extracellular matrix, collagen fibers, metabolism, and immune and intercellular links. Notably, COL5A3, a member of the collagen triple helical repeat family, is involved in the synthesis of collagen fibers. Among the five genes, COL5A3 was most strongly associated with immune infiltration. Therefore, COL5A3 was identified as a hub gene in the constructed prediction model. To further explore the relationship between COL5A3, the malignant phenotype, and the tumor microenvironment, scRNA-seq data were utilized for single-cell analysis. The results demonstrated that high COL5A3 expression was associated with greater proliferative capacity, drug resistance, and stemness. Several typical cell biological experiments were performed, and the results illustrated that the knockdown of COL5A3 in cells inhibited the invasion, migration, and proliferation of the TNBC cell line MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-468. To our knowledge, this is the first study to demonstrate the function of COL5A3 in TNBC cells through biological experiments. Next, we performed a drug toxicity test and the results showed that gemcitabine resistance was associated with high COL5A3 expression, while identifying a potential targeted drug OF-1 in TNBC. In addition, based on a series of studies, we suggest that COL5A3 may fulfill its biological functions via the DDR1/FAK/PI3K/AKT pathway.

In this study, we performed a comprehensive analysis and validation of hypoxia-related genes and lactate metabolism-related genes for the first time to construct a novel prognostic prediction model for TNBC. Simultaneously, the model was combined with common clinical prognostic indicators to establish a nomogram, which had a higher survival prediction accuracy for TNBC patients than the previous prediction model. In addition, the factors that might lead to the difference in prognosis between the high- and low-risk groups were explored, including immune infiltration, functional pathways, TMB, MSI, RNAss, and drug sensitivity. It has been preliminarily confirmed that the hub gene COL5A3 plays a role in promoting cancer, correlating with drug resistance and its mechanism of action in TNBC.

This study has some limitations. First, a prognostic model was developed solely by analyzing retrospective data from public databases, which may need to be validated in a prospective large cohort study. Second, there are some differences between the transcriptome, genome, and proteome of TNBC, which may result in an affected prediction effect. Third, TNBC usually progresses rapidly and develops metastasis and relapses; therefore, short-term survival, disease-free survival, and progression-free survival should also be included. Furthermore, only cell lines were used for experimental validation, but they lack the complex tumor microenvironment (TME) found in vivo, including immune cell infiltration, matrix interactions, and extracellular matrix remodeling. In the future, further verification may be conducted through animal experiments. Finally, the specific mechanism by which COL5A3 promotes tumor progression and drug resistance in TNBC still needs to be further explored.

Conclusion

In conclusion, a novel five-gene prognostic model and nomogram were established in this study, which could not only predict the survival of TNBC patients but could also help surgeons make clinical decisions by predicting immune infiltration, TMB, MSI, RNAss, and drug sensitivity levels. In addition, COL5A3 promotes tumor proliferation, migration, and invasion via the DDR1/FAK/PI3K/AKT pathway.
