Deep neural architecture empowered by explainable artificial intelligence for accurate and early diagnosis of gynaecological cancer using medical images
Authors: Sabah Abdullah Al-Somali, Zenah Mahmoud AlKubaisy, Muhyaddin Rawa, Khalid H. Allehaibi, Rania M. Alhazmi, Mahmoud Ragab
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Early analysis is a necessity for the more effective treatment of cancers. In gynaecological cancers, like endometrial, ovarian, and cervical cancers, the current efforts are aimed at discovering new analytical biomarkers to help decrease the global health burden related to these cancers. In cervical cancer, efficient screening is highly suggested for the prevention of invasive cancer occurrence and death. However, the interpretation of medical images for gynaecological cancer remains prone to human error. Artificial intelligence-based solutions offer numerous medical image challenges to help with the clinical decision support process. This paper presents a Deep Neural Architecture Empowered for Accurate Diagnosis of Gynaecological Cancer (DNAE-ADGC) model using medical imaging. The primary purpose of the paper is to empower gynaecological cancer diagnostics by developing an accurate, efficient, and intelligent detection framework using advanced techniques. Initially, the image pre-processing stage employs a two-level approach named adaptive filter that contains Median-Modified Wiener Filter (MMWF) and Cross Guided Bilateral Filter (CGBF). Followed by, the MobileNetV3Large model was deployed for feature extraction process. Besides, the DNAE-ADGC algorithm is applies the graph convolutional network and gated recurrent unit (GCN-GRU) network for detecting and classifying gynaecological cancer. At last, the explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) technique applies Grad-CAM to develop the transparency, interpretability, and reliability of AI models. The comparative analysis of the DNAE-ADGC method demonstrated an improved accuracy value of 97.92% with other methodologies under the Malhari dataset.

Introduction

As per recent statistics, cancer is the primary cause of death before the age of 70 years in several nations. Gynecologic cancers result from the uncontrolled propagation of abnormal cells in the female generative system, such as the uterus, cervix, ovaries, vagina, vulva, and fallopian tubes1. Cancer occurrence and related death rates differ widely between countries depending on the stage of economic progress and related cultural and behavioural factors. Among the different kinds of gynecologic cancers, breast cancer records the highest occurrence and death rates, followed by cervical cancer (CC)2. CC continues to be one of the most prevalent gynaecologic cancers and holds the second rank globally in terms of frequency and death rates. Globally, CC is the primary malignant tumour in women. There is a decline in the occurrence and death from CC in nations with an established screening system. CC Screening mainly relies on exfoliative cytology. At the same time, the Pap smear test can identify pre-invasive CC, thus significantly lowering the occurrence of invasive disease3. Although it is a reliable screening method, it cannot eliminate CC as the test lacks strong sensitivity. Currently, advanced techniques are introduced to enhance detection. Timely and accurate identification, along with suitable treatment methods, are crucial for a better prognosis and quality of life4. Yet, the evaluation of analytic images and pathology reports is intricate and subject to error. In several medical fields, the expert understanding and hands-on abilities that healthcare workers depend on involve recognising subtle patterns and abnormalities in medical images, such as radiological scans or histopathological slides5. Earlier research has indicated that many women seeking cancer screening are not aware of the most appropriate screening technique based on their age group. Research has also shown that even with insufficient awareness, women neither seek expert advice nor schedule gynaecological checkups. Most of these women receive a diagnosis of gynecologic cancers at a later stage6. Hence, informing women about gynecologic cancer signs may support early recognition and identification, which will make the treatment given more successful and possibly life-saving. Manual visual assessment involves a process that might depend on personal bias and irregularity in diagnosis, causing missed identifications and delayed care7. To address these issues, the utilisation of advanced computational techniques, DL, is designed to enhance the reliability and accuracy of gynaecological cancer recognition, becoming increasingly common and interesting8. A subset of artificial intelligence (AI) is DL, which has worked very effectively in several medical imaging tasks9. DL approaches in the area of gynaecological oncology can assist physicians in diagnosing and classifying cases with higher consistency and reliability, automatically extracting significant visual features from medical images of cases and merging numerous data types to support a diagnosis. Gynaecological cancers are diagnosed through DL, which can ideally increase the early and precise detection10. Therefore, they can support informed, personalised treatment methods and also improve patient outcomes.

Related works

Bilal et al.11 proposed a new ensemble method, which employs the Salp Swarm Algorithm (SSA) to improve CC detection. To enhance feature attention, combined the Convolutional Block Attention Mechanism (CBAM) into every one of these methods. Then, the predictions made from the model in the ensemble are joined using a weighted aggregation model, and then the ensemble classifier is optimised by SSA. Umirzakova et al.12 introduced a lightweight and effective segmentation approach specially developed for cervical cell examination. This approach leverages a MobileNetV2 framework to extract features, guaranteeing a minimum parameter count favourable for practical processing. Moreover, incorporate extreme point supervision to improve segmentation results utilising minimum boundary annotation, instead of full pixel-wise labels. The authors13 presented a CAD-based CC identification through the Gazelle Optimiser Algorithm alongside a DL (CACCD-GOADL) technique. The main motive of this technique is to analyse the CC image. For this purpose, the presented technique utilises an enhanced MobileNetv3 paradigm to capture intricate shapes in Pap smear images. Furthermore, it creates a novel GOA for the parameter tuning of the improved MobileNet-v3 paradigm. Pacal14 developed a high-accuracy framework using the Multi-Axis Vision Transformer (MaxViT) model. The Mobile Bottleneck Convolution (MBConv) blocks are replaced with ConvNeXtv2 blocks, and the Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) blocks are replaced with Gated Residual Network (GRN)-based MLPs to improve generalisation and reduce parameters. Berahmand et al.15 proposed the Nested Ensemble DL for Gynaecological Cancer Prediction (NEDL-GCP) model by integrating neural networks (NNs) namely Convolutional NNs (CNNs), Recurrent NNs (RNNs), and Support Vector Machines (SVMs) as base learners, with J48 and Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) to refine predictions and improve classification accuracy. Muksimova et al.16 proposed RL-CancerNet, utilizes EfficientNetV2 for extracting fine-grained local features, Vision Transformers (ViTs) for capturing global dependencies, and a Reinforcement Learning (RL) agent to address class imbalance. Also, a Supporter Module with Convolutional 3D (Conv3D) and Bi-directional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) layers integrated with attention mechanism improves contextual feature learning. The authors17 presented an Improved Bald Eagle Search Optimiser alongside a DL-driven CC Detection and Classification (IBESODL-CCDC) method. This method aims to automate the detection and classification of CC. In this method, a contrast enhancement procedure is carried out to improve the image quality. Furthermore, this method employs an adapted LeNet methodology for the feature extraction. To diagnose CC, this method utilises an attention-based long short-term memory (ALSTM) method.

Sahoo et al.18 developed a model by using three pretrained models to predict CC early. To combine these model results, a new fuzzy rank-based ensemble is considered by dual nonlinear functions. In contrast to fusion approaches, this framework presents the latest predictions by taking into account the base classifier’s certainty in forecasts. To additionally improve the classification abilities of these methods, merge sophisticated augmentation methodologies. Mishra et al.19 created an automatic CC prediction model that relies on Pap smear images, which utilises a pooling model. In CC detection, image examination is crucial. With developing techniques, computer vision (CV) and CNNs are of prior significance due to their unbelievable ability to deal with object detection and image identification difficulties. Almujally et al.20 utilised Transfer Learning (TL) with the MobileNet CNN and Chi-square feature selection (FS) to detect the most impactful features. Kodipalli, Fernandes, and Dasar21 proposed a model by utilising CNNs incorporated with Transformers and an Ensemble MLP along with classical ML models for classification. Abdel-Salam, Askr, and Hassanien22 utilised AI and DL methods by integrating pre-trained models for feature extraction, Copula Entropy-based Golden Jackal Optimisation (CE-based GJO) for feature selection, Sea Horse Optimiser (SHO) for hyperparameter tuning, and Explainable AI (XAI) for classification. Hussain et al.23 proposed a method by utilising XAI and seven XAI methods, including Saliency, XRAI, Integrated Gradients, Smooth and Integrated Gradients, Grad- and Smoothgrad-CAM, applied to four CNN models, such as AlexNet, VGG16, MobileNet, and InceptionNet, to evaluate model predictions and align them with human expert annotations. Kodipalli et al.24 proposed a model by utilising XAI techniques. A three-stage ensemble method combined with a game-theory model based on SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) is also used to analyse and visualise the most important features. AlMohimeed et al.25 utilised ViT for feature extraction, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) for FS and optimization, and SVM for precise classification. Additionally, XAI via Grad-CAM is employed to interpret model predictions and highlight essential image regions. Nazir et al.26 employed AI, ML, and DL approaches on holographic microscopic imaging data. It integrates pre-trained DL models for feature extraction, classical ML classifiers for classification, and XAI approaches such as Grad-CAM and LIME to interpret model decisions and enhance diagnostic transparency. The authors27 examined a model by implementing a Bilinear Convolutional NN (BCNN) integrated with a Random Projection (RP) paradigm. The approach also employs dyadic feature extraction and bilinear pooling to generate rich composite image representations. Saha et al.28 utilised the OvCan-FIND model, which employs CNNs for feature extraction and comparison with multiple advanced CNN architectures. The existing DL Models for gynaecological cancer diagnosis mostly focus on accuracy but lack robustness for medical images in noisy handling. The data handled with many approaches relies only on CNN-based algorithms and spatial relationships that are complex to capture. Furthermore, most methods work with black-box techniques without providing proper interpretability, which limits clinical trust. The advanced hybrid models that were integrated were extracted for limited usage. Additionally, the image quality could not be enhanced by pre-processing techniques due to insufficient attention. Later, it is necessary to combined structure expands accuracy, expandability, and robustness for initial and dependable analysis of gynaecological cancers.

Proposed materials

This paper presents a DNAE-ADGC model using Medical Imaging. The primary purpose of the paper is to empower gynaecological cancer diagnostics by developing an accurate, efficient, and intelligent detection framework using advanced techniques. The DNAE-ADGC method involves image pre-processing phase, feature representation model, detection, and an XAI method. Figure 1 depicts the complete working flow process of the DNAE-ADGC method. Unlike traditional hybrid models that loosely integrate CNN, GCN, and RNN techniques, the presented architecture proposes a structured and task-specific incorporation: advanced preprocessing employing Median-Modified Wiener Filter and Cross-Guided Bilateral Filter to improve medical image quality, efficient feature extraction via MobileNetV3Large tailored for low-complexity yet high-representation learning, a tightly coupled GCN-GRU mechanism, which acquires both spatial relationships and sequential dependencies in feature maps, and the incorporation of XAI (Grad-CAM) to ensure clinical interpretability. This end-to-end design, integrates optimized preprocessing, lightweight feature extraction, graph-based relational learning, and explainability within a single unified pipeline, distinguishes the proposed model from previous works and establishes its novelty.

Image pre-processing

Initially, a two-level approach named an adaptive filter, which contains MMWF and CGBF is employed to enhance the image quality, contrast and by removing noise. The integration effectively mitigates noise while also conserving crucial edge and fine details in Pap smear images. MMWF effectively handles impulsive and Gaussian noise more robustly, compared to standard filters, and contrast and structural data are improved by CGBF by employing guidance from cross-channel data, resulting in more explicit images that will enhance extraction and subsequent accuracy.

In the pre-processing stage, the method utilises a 2-level adaptive filtering tactic to increase image quality and permit for reliable analysis. The MMWF is initially applied to reduce Gaussian noise and impulse but maintaining edges, incorporating the advantages of the Wiener filter (WF) and the median filter (MF). Then, the CGBF was used to increase image contrast and maintain essential details by considering intensity differences and spatial closeness, as trained by a different image. These two methods guarantee that the images are well‐defined and clean, crucial to segment and extract.

MMWF

By utilising an MF for denoising the background area of a flawed image and suppress impulsive noise when preserving edges by replacing each pixel with the median of its neighborhood. Furthermore, this model utilising the WF to reduce noise while maintaining important structural details29. CGBF

CGBF is an edge-preserving smoothing technique, and it can remove artefacts without affecting the surface quality, preserving image quality and edge. The key characteristics of CGBF is maintains sharp edges while reducing noise and it uses both spatial weights and range weights from the guidance image. The CGBF is used in the recommended structure due to its merits. Similar to a bilateral filter (BF), the CBF preserves borders by considering geometric familiarity and grayscale similarity among neighboring pixels. Using one image for the filter and another for the kernel allows exploration of relationships among nearby pixels. The CGBF prevents gradient reversal and other elementary BF problems. The activation of the ReLU guarantees that only positively contributing areas are highlighted. Therefore, this model aids in recognising the particular portions of the image that most affected the prediction of the model, increasing transparency and confidence of the results.

Result analysis

The model was implemented in Python 3.11.0 and executed on a system with an Intel i5-8600 K processor, GeForce GTX 1050Ti (4GB), 16GB RAM, 250GB SSD, and 1 TB HDD. The training–validation protocol has been standardized with the following parameters: learning rate of 0.001, batch size of 8, dropout rate of 0.5, 50 training epochs, and ReLU activation function. The validation of the DNAE-ADGC model is tested under the Malhari dataset 33, which comprises 318 images under four classes, as presented below in Table 2. This dataset is chosen for its labelled gynaecological cytology images, covering multiple cancerous and precancerous classes, making it appropriate for early diagnosis research. Conclusion

This paper introduces a DNAE-ADGC methodology using medical imaging. The primary purpose of the paper is to empower gynaecological cancer diagnostics by developing an accurate, efficient, and intelligent detection framework using advanced techniques. Primarily, the image pre-processing phase deploys a two-level process named an adaptive filter. Followed by, the feature extraction process is executed by the MobileNetV3Large technique. Besides, the DNAE-ADGC model employs the GCN-GRU method to detect and classify gynaecological cancer. At last, the XAI model applies Grad-CAM to develop the transparency, interpretability, and reliability of AI models. The comparative outcome of the DNAE-ADGC methodology depicted a greater accuracy value of 97.92% over other models under the Malhari dataset. The proposed system enhances the interpretability, robustness and real-world applicability.

The limitations include the reliance on specific dataset characteristics, which may affect generalizability across varied populations. Furthermore, the computational needs remain relatively high, limiting deployment on low-resource devices. Future work may focus on incorporating adaptive learning strategies, exploring multimodal data integration, and improving model explainability to improve usability and broader applicability in real-world medical settings. In the future work, DNAE-ADGC approach can be extended to stronger theoretical justification for the hybrid GCN-GRU algorithm, particularly for medical images. The method will also be tested on multi-institutional and larger databases to increase simplification and decrease Besides, the inclusion of the cross-dataset estimation in future drive be directed to certify clinical dependability. Additionally, the SMOTE and Focal loss techniques will be used to handle the class imbalance problem.
