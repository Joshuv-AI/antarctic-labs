1.
Introduction

Throughout this work, all simplicial complexes will be finite and abstract (see Definition
2.1
) and their homology will be simplicial with coefficients in some field
\mathbb{F}
unless otherwise specified. By triangulated manifold, we mean a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
such that its geometric realization is a closed topological manifold.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex with vertex set
[m]
and for every
J\subseteq[m]
denote by
\mathcal{K}_{J}
the full subcomplex consisting of simplices in
\mathcal{K}
that are subsets of
J
. We say a complex
\mathcal{K}
is
\mathbb{F}
-tight if for all
J\subseteq[m]
, the inclusion
\mathcal{K}_{J}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
induces an injective map in homology with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}
. The idea of tight triangulations of manifolds was introduced in
[
12
]
, inspired by economical embeddings of spaces in an Euclidean space; Künhel and Lutz conjectured that a triangulation is strongly minimal, that is, it has the least number of faces in every dimension only if it is tight. They verified the conjecture for dimensions 1 and 2 in
[
13
]
; dimension 3 was verified by Baguchi, Data, and Spreer in
[
1
]
. Little is known about higher dimensions.

Intuitively a manifold triangulation being tight suggests that if we restrict to a subset of points the topological features of that subcomplex aren’t lost when completing the complex. It is also worth noting that tightness is a global feature of a complex that is obtained by looking at each possible vertex restriction individually. This thought process is where we make the connection to
moment-angle complexes
a central object in the field of Toric Topology (see
[
5
, Ch. 4]
) that encodes the combinatorial information of a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
a subspace of the Euclidean Space
\mathbb{C}^{m}
(see Section 2.3). As it turns out, the cohomology ring of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
splits as a direct sum of the cohomology of every subcomplex of
\mathcal{K}
(see Proposition
2.18
). Let us denote by
\beta(X;\mathbb{F})
the sum of the betti numbers of
X
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}
, the splitting mentioned before and Poincaré-Alexander-Lefschetz duality (Prop.
2.10
) lets us characterize tightness in our first result:

Theorem
3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and
\mathcal{K}
a triangulated closed
\mathbb{F}
-orientable
n
-manifold on
m
vertices. Then

\beta(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}};\mathbb{F})\geq 2^{m-1}(\beta(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{F})-2)+2

with equality if and only if
\mathcal{K}
is
\mathbb{F}
-tight.

This result turns the problem of verifying injectivity of
2^{m}
maps of the form
H_{*}(\mathcal{K}_{J})\to H_{*}(\mathcal{K})
into that of computing the cohomology of the moment-angle complex
\mathcal{K}
.

In
[
14
]
, the authors defined the double homology functor
DH_{*,*}
(originally denoted as
HH
); this construction was designed to solve a stability problem in the use of Tor-complexes for persistent homology
[
2
]
. Apart from this application, this functor encodes non-trivial combinatorial properties of simplicial complexes in relatively small finite-dimensional algebras, raising interest in questions regarding its rank and internal structure

[
8
]
[
17
]
[
19
]
[
6
]
[
15
]
.

In Section 4, we introduce a generalisation of double homology to abelian poset functors of the form
F:2^{[m]}\to\mathcal{A}
and denote it as
H^{*}(F)
. This construction is reminiscent of Khovanov’s work on the Jones’ polynomial
[
11
]
. We refer the reader to
[
7
, §6]
for an exploration of this construction in more generality. For our second result, we introduce a sequence of maps arising from Lefschetz duality
\eta_{q}:H^{*}(H_{q}(\mathcal{K}_{-}))\to H^{*}(H^{n-q}(\mathcal{K}_{-^{c}}))
(see Equation 5) for an
n
-dimensional triangulated manifold

, which defines a new duality when

is tight.

Theorem
4.1
.

For every integer

, the map

is an isomorphism whenever
\mathcal{K}
is tight.

2.2.
Duality

Definition 2.9
.

For a field
\mathbb{F}

-orientable if

Proposition 2.10

(Poincaré-Alexander-Lefschetz duality)
.

If

\mathbb{F}
-orientable
n
-manifold

To effectively use this in the world of abstract simplicial complexes, we will need the following lemma:

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex on
[m]
and
J\subseteq[m]
. There is a strong deformation retraction
|\mathcal{K}|-|\mathcal{K}_{J}|\simeq|\mathcal{K}_{{J^{c}}}|
.

Proof.

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. First we consider the case
\mathcal{K}=2^{[m]}
; consider the homotopy
H:(\Delta_{[m]}-\Delta_{J})\times I\to\Delta_{[m]}-\Delta_{J}
given by

H(x,t)=(1-t)x+t\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{j\in{J^{c}}}x_{j}e_{j}}{\sum\limits_{j\in{J^{c}}}x_{j}}\right)

this is well defined as, since

there’s some
i\in{J^{c}}
such that
x\cdot e_{i}\neq 0
. This is clearly a deformation retraction into
\Delta_{{J^{c}}}
as
H(x,1)\in\Delta({J^{c}})
and whenever
x\in\Delta({J^{c}})
, the second summand in the formula is just
tx
and so
H(x,t)=(1-t)x+tx=x
.

Notice that whenever a point
x
is in a simplex
\sigma
that intersects
\Delta_{J^{c}}
, the homotopy slides it towards its maximal face contained in
\Delta_{J^{c}}
while staying inside of
\sigma
. If we consider a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
and
x\in\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{J}-\mathcal{K}_{J^{c}}
, then
x
is as in the previous observation, meaning that
H
is well defined when intersecting with
\mathcal{K}
, showing this is indeed a strong deformation retract.
∎

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a triangulated closed

-orientable
n
-manifold on
[m]

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{q}(\mathcal{K}_{J})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{q}(\mathcal{K})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{n-q}(\mathcal{K}_{J^{c}})}}

The exactness of this sequence follows from considering the long exact sequence of the pair

2.3.
Tightness

In
[
13
]
, the authors show the absolute curvature of an immersion of a manifold
M
into an Euclidean space is minimal when the immersion is tight, that is, for every closed half space H of the Euclidean space, the inclusion
M\cap H\xhookrightarrow{}M
is injective in homology.

Given a triangulated manifold
\mathcal{K}
on
[m]
, we can consider the geometric realization included into
\mathbb{R}^{m}
; this gives us a combinatorial characterisation of tightness for simplicial complexes.

Definition 2.13
.

For a field
\mathbb{F}
, a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
is
\mathbb{F}
-tight if for every
J\subseteq[m]
the inclusion
\mathcal{K}_{J}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
induces an injective map
H_{*}(\mathcal{K}_{J})\to H_{*}(\mathcal{K})
. We say a complex is tight if it is
\mathbb{F}
-tight for some field
\mathbb{F}
.

Proposition 2.14
.

A triangulation
\mathcal{K}
of a space
X
on
[m]
is
\mathbb{F}
-tight if and only if the embedding
X\xrightarrow{\cong}|\mathcal{K}|\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
\mathbb{F}
-tight.

Proof.

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For each half plane
h
of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
define
J_{h}:=\{j\in[m]:e_{j}\in h\}
; there’s a deformation retraction
|\mathcal{K}|\cap h\simeq|\mathcal{K}_{J_{h}}|
constructed analogously to the one we used in the proof of Lemma
2.11
by projecting to
J_{h}
instead of
J^{c}
.
∎

One interest in tight triangulations of manifolds arises from the well-known Kühnel-Lutz conjecture; we refer the reader to
[
13
]
for more details:

Conjecture 2.15
.

The triangulation of an n-manifold is tight if and only if it’s strongly minimal.

Where by
strongly minimal
we mean its number of faces is minimal in every dimension. This conjecture was shown to be true for
n\leq 3
in
[
1
]
.

Another reason for our interest in tight complexes is that it was recently shown in
[
10
]
that the class of tight manifold triangulation is precisely the class of manifolds
\mathcal{K}
such that the Massey products of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
are all trivial.

3.
A characterisation for tightness

In this section we prove our first theorem: a tight lower bound for the total rank of the moment-angle complex associated to a manifold which is achieved when the triangulation is tight.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and let
\mathcal{K}
be a triangulated closed
\mathbb{F}
-orientable
n
-manifold on
[m]
. For every
J\subseteq[m]
and
q\in\mathbb{Z}
we have that

\beta_{q}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{F})\leq\beta_{q}(\mathcal{K}_{J};\mathbb{F})+\beta_{n-q}(\mathcal{K}_{{J^{c}}};\mathbb{F})

with equality for every
J\subseteq[m]
and for every
q\in\mathbb{Z}
if and only if
\mathcal{K}
is
\mathbb{F}
-tight.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.12
we have that

\displaystyle\beta_{q}(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{F})

\displaystyle=\text{null }p_{q}(J)+\text{rank }p_{q}(J)

\displaystyle=\text{rank }i_{q}(J)+\text{null }\delta_{q}(J)

\displaystyle=\left(\beta_{q}(\mathcal{K}_{J})-\text{null }i_{q}(J)\right)+\left(\beta_{n-q}(\mathcal{K}_{J^{c}})-\text{rank }i_{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\beta_{q}(\mathcal{K}_{J};\mathbb{F})+\beta_{n-q}(\mathcal{K}_{J^{c}};\mathbb{F})-(\text{null }i_{q}(J)+\text{null }i_{q-1}(J))

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{q}(\mathcal{K}_{J};\mathbb{F})+\beta_{n-q}(\mathcal{K}_{{J^{c}}};\mathbb{F})

Equality happens if and only if
\text{null }i_{q}(J)+\text{null }i_{q-1}(J)=0
or equivalently,
\text{null }i_{q}(J)=\text{null }i_{q-1}(J)=0
, if we impose this condition for every
q
and
J
we get that this is equivalent to the definition of tightness.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and
\mathcal{K}
a triangulated closed orientable
n
-manifold on
m
vertices. Then

\beta(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}};\mathbb{F})\geq 2^{m-1}(\beta(\mathcal{K};\mathbb{F})-2)+2

with equality if and only if
\mathcal{K}
is
\mathbb{F}
-tight.

Proof.

Fix a subset
J
and add the inequality in Lemma 3.1 over all indices
q

\beta(\mathcal{K})=\sum_{q}\beta_{q}(\mathcal{K})\leq\sum_{q}\beta_{q}(\mathcal{K}_{J})+\sum_{q}\beta_{n-q}(\mathcal{K}_{{J^{c}}})=\beta(\mathcal{K}_{J})+\beta(\mathcal{K}_{J^{c}}).

If we add over all subsets of
[m]
we get that

(1)

2^{m}\beta(\mathcal{K})\leq\sum_{J\subseteq[m]}\beta(\mathcal{K}_{J})+\sum\beta(\mathcal{K}_{J^{c}})=2\sum_{J\subseteq[m]}\beta(\mathcal{K}_{J}).

\displaystyle\beta(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\subseteq[m]}\tilde{\beta}(\mathcal{K}_{J})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[m]\\
q>0\end{subarray}}\beta_{q}(\mathcal{K}_{J})+\sum_{\emptyset\neq J\subseteq[m]}(\beta_{0}(\mathcal{K}_{J})-1)+\tilde{\beta}_{-1}(\mathcal{K}_{\emptyset})

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\subseteq[m]}\beta(\mathcal{K}_{J})-(2^{m}-1)+1

\displaystyle\beta(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}})+2^{m}-2

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\subseteq[m]}\beta(\mathcal{K}_{J}).

Putting this together with Inequality (
1
) gives us that

2^{m-1}\beta(\mathcal{K})\leq\sum_{J\subseteq[m]}\beta(\mathcal{K}_{J})=\beta(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}})+2^{m}-2.

or equivalently

\beta(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}})\geq 2^{m-1}(\beta(\mathcal{K})-2)+2.

The equality condition is inherited from the condition in Lemma

4.
A duality for tight complexes

It is of particular interest to understand the topology of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
when
\mathcal{K}
is a manifold. However, many of the topological properties of
\mathcal{K}
as a manifold translate poorly to those of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
, in particular,
H^{*}(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}})
will not be a Poincaré duality algebra in general for a manifold
\mathcal{K}
, in fact,
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
satisfies Poincaré duality if and only if
\mathcal{K}
is a
\text{Gorensetein}^{*}
complex
[
4
, Theorem 3.4.5]
.

In this section, we present our second theorem: a duality map in double homology that appears in the special case of
\mathcal{K}
being a tight triangulation.

For every
q\in\mathbb{Z}
, the collection of maps
i_{q}(J)
,
p_{q}(J)
and
\delta_{q}(J)
from Proposition
3.1
assemble into natural transformations
i_{q}
,
p_{q}

\delta_{q}
which fit in the following exact sequence of functors

H_{q}(\mathcal{K}_{-})\xrightarrow{i_{q}}\Delta(H_{q}(\mathcal{K}))\xrightarrow{p_{q}}H^{n-q}(\mathcal{K}_{-^{c}}).

This gives us the following three short exact sequences

(2)

\displaystyle 0\to\text{ker }i_{q}\xhookrightarrow{}H_{q}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}_{-})\xrightarrow{i_{q}}\text{Im }i_{q}\to 0,

(3)

\displaystyle 0\to\text{Im }i_{q}\xhookrightarrow{}\Delta_{q}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K})\xrightarrow{p_{q}}\text{Im }p_{q}\to 0,

(4)

\displaystyle 0\to\text{Im }p_{q}\xhookrightarrow{}H^{n-q}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}_{-^{c}})\xrightarrow{\delta_{q}}\text{ker }i_{q-1}\to 0.

Since
\Delta(H_{q}(\mathcal{K}))
is acyclic, the connecting homomorphisms
\partial_{q}:H^{*}(\text{Im }p_{q})\to H^{*+1}(\text{Im }i_{q})
are isomorphisms. This lets us define for every integer
q
the following map in cohomology:

(5)

{}\eta_{q}:=\partial_{q}^{-1}i_{q}^{*}:H^{*}(H_{q}(\mathcal{K}_{-}))\to H^{*-1}(H^{n-q}(\mathcal{K}_{-^{c}})).

Theorem 4.1
.

The map
\eta_{q}
is an isomorphism whenever
\mathcal{K}
is tight.

Proof.

The long exact sequences in cohomology of sequences (2) and (3) fit in the following commutative diagram with exact rows for every
l
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{l}(\text{ker }i_{q})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{l}(H_{q})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{l}(\text{Im }i_{q})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{l+1}(\text{ker }i_{q})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{l-1}(\text{ker }i_{q-1})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{l-1}(H^{n-q})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{l-1}(\text{Im }p_{q})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{l-2}(\text{ker }i_{q-1})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{q}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{q}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial_{q}^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1^{*}}

where
\eta
is the unique map making that diagram commute. If

-tight then

and
