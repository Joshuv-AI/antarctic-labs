Results for blow-up and sharp lifespan estimates to a weakly coupled system of structurally damped wave equations with critical nonlinearities
Trung Loc Tang, Tuan Anh Dao ∗, The Anh Cung

Abstract
. In this paper, we would like to study a weakly coupled system of semilinear structurally damped wave equations with moduli of continuity in nonlinear terms whose powers belong to the critical curve in the p − q p-q plane. Our aim is to find sharp conditions for these moduli of continuity by investigating the global (in time) existence of small data solutions and a finite blow-up of small data solutions. Furthermore, when the finite blow-up phenomenon occurs, we are going to achieve the sharp lifespan estimate for the local (in time) solution.



1.
Introduction

The semilinear damped wave equations with different nonlinearities have been studied by many authors and various results on the existence or non-existence of global solutions have been investigated. One of the well-known models is the following

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=|u|^{p},\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

with a power exponent
p>1
. Many mathematicians study the global existence of small data solutions to (
1.1
) provided that some restriction on the power
p
of the nonlinearity holds. For instance, the authors in
[
19
]
prove that the solution exists globally when
p>1+2/n
and blows up in finite time if
1<p\leq 1+2/n
for
n\geq 3
. From this observation, we can say that the constant
p_{\text{Fuj}}(n):=1+2/n
, the so-called Fujita exponent, is the critical exponent of (
1.1
). However, the power nonlinearity
\{|u|^{p}\}_{p>1}
seems too rough to verify the critical exponent of (
1.1
), so some authors are interested in the following Cauchy problem:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=|u|^{1+\frac{2}{n}}\mu(|u|),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

Here the function
\mu=\mu(|u|)
stands for the modulus of continuity, which is added to increase the regularity of the nonlinearity compared with the origin equation (
1.1
). The pioneering results are presented in
[
10
]
by Ebert-Girardi-Reissig. They investigate the sharp condition for the nonlinearity to attain both the global small data solution and the blow-up solution. In detail, by denoting the integral

I_{\mu}:=\int_{0}^{c}\frac{\mu(s)}{s}\;ds

with a sufficiently small constant
c>0
they prove that if
I_{\mu}<\infty
, the Sobolev globally exists, on the other hand,
I_{\mu}=\infty
, the solution blows up in finite time. Afterward, Dao-Reissig in
[
7
]
studied the same situation as the equation (
1.2
) but for the weakly coupled system of semilinear damped wave equations, namely

(1.3)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=|v|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}(|v|),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
v_{tt}-\Delta v+v_{t}=|u|^{q^{*}}\mu_{2}(|u|),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\quad v_{t}(0,x)=v_{1}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

where
p^{*},q^{*}
belong to the following critical curve:

\frac{1+\max\{p,q\}}{pq-1}=\frac{n}{2}.

Also,
\mu_{1}=\mu_{1}(|v|)
and
\mu_{2}=\mu_{2}(|u|)
present moduli of continuity. They find out that the system (
1.3
) witnesses the existence of a global solution in the case

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{c}\frac{1}{s}\left(\mu_{1}(s)\right)^{\frac{q^{*}}{q^{*}+1}}\left(\mu_{2}(s)\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{*}+1}}\;ds<\infty,

and a blow-up phenomenon when

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{c}\frac{1}{s}\left(\mu_{1}(s)\right)^{\frac{q^{*}}{q^{*}+1}}\left(\mu_{2}(s)\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{*}+1}}\;ds=\infty.

Quite recently, Chen-Dao in
[
1
]
obtained the sharp lifespan estimate for the problem (
1.3
) in the case
\mu_{1}\equiv\mu_{2}\equiv 1
, specifically,

T_{\varepsilon}\sim\begin{cases}\exp\left(C\varepsilon^{-p-1}\right)&\text{ if }p^{*}=q^{*},\\
\exp\left(C\varepsilon^{-\max\left\{\frac{p^{*}(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}{p^{*}+1},\frac{q^{*}(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}{q^{*}+1}\right\}}\right)&\text{ if }p^{*}\neq q^{*}.\end{cases}

In 2014, the other version of (
1.1
) has been considered by D’Abbicco-Reissig in
[
9
]
, the so-called the semilinear structurally damped wave equations, namely

(1.4)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u_{t}=|u|^{p},\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

for any
\sigma\in[0,1]
. The authors prove that the solution to (
1.4
) exists globally when the exponent fulfills the following conditions:

p>\left\{\begin{aligned} &1+\dfrac{2}{n-2\sigma}&\text{ if }\sigma\in[0,1/2],\\
&1+\dfrac{1+2\sigma}{n-1}&\text{ if }\sigma\in[1/2,1].\end{aligned}\right.

Then, a blow-up result for
p<1+2/(n-2\sigma)
was included in
[
3
]
so we can claim that
p_{\text{crit}}:=1+2/(n-2\sigma)
is sharp with
\sigma\in[0,1/2]
. Also, Dao in
[
3
]
(see more
[
2
]
) works with a weakly couple systems of semilinear structurally damped wave equations with the nonlinearities
|u|^{q},|v|^{p}
as follows:

(1.5)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u_{t}=|v|^{p},\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
v_{tt}-\Delta v+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}v_{t}=|u|^{q},\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\quad v_{t}(0,x)=v_{1}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

He proves that when the initial data belong to
L^{1}
, the critical curve of (
1.5
) is

(1.6)

\frac{1+\max\{p,q\}}{pq-1}=\frac{n-2\sigma}{2}.

It means when
p,q
satisfy

\frac{1+\max\{p,q\}}{pq-1}>\frac{n-2\sigma}{2},

there exists a global solution (in time), meanwhile, if

\frac{1+\max\{p,q\}}{pq-1}<\frac{n-2\sigma}{2},

the solution blows up in finite time. The fact is that it still remains an open question to verify whether there exist global (in time) solutions or not on the critical curve (
1.6
) when
\sigma
are assumed to be any fractional numbers. Motivated by those above papers,
the main interest of our work is to establish both the blow-up result and the sharp lifespan estimates as in the paper

[
7
]

for a weakly coupled system of semi-linear structurally damped wave equations with moduli of continuity in power nonlinear terms when the condition (
1.6
) holds.
For this reason, let us consider the following system:

(1.7)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u_{t}=|v|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}(|v|),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
v_{tt}-\Delta v+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}v_{t}=|u|^{q^{*}}\mu_{2}(|u|),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\quad v_{t}(0,x)=v_{1}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

where the functions
\mu_{1}=\mu_{1}(|v|)
and
\mu_{2}=\mu_{2}(|u|)
are some suitable moduli of continuity. We assume that
\sigma\in[0,1/2]
and
p^{*},q^{*}>1
belong to the critical curve described by (
1.6
). The main reason to only consider
\sigma\in[0,1/2]
comes from the paper
[
9
]
in which the authors have proposed to distinguish between “parabolic like models” in the case
\sigma\in[0,1/2]
, the so-called effective damping, and “hyperbolic like models” or “wave like models” in the case
\sigma\in(1/2,1]
, the so-called noneffective damping. To the best of authors’ knowledge, it seems that nobody has ever succeeded to determine really critical exponent to the semi-linear structurally damped wave equation (
1.4
) with noneffective damping. As far as, it leaves an open problem to explore. From this observation, it is quite natural to restrict our concern in terms of studying (
1.7
) with effective damping only in this paper.

Without loss of generality, if we assume
p^{*}\leq q^{*}
, then the critical curve in the
p-q
plane for (
1.7
) becomes

(1.8)

\frac{1+q^{*}}{p^{*}q^{*}-1}=\frac{n-2\sigma}{2}.

It is worth pointing out that our approach used in this paper contains not only the case
\sigma=0
, which has been proved in
[
7
]
, but also the range
(0,1/2]
. To deal with the latter case, we need to construct new test functions with no compact support, which can treat the fractional Laplacian operator in the equation. Throughout this work, one can recognize that our results give positive answers to some remaining quesions appearing in the previous papers (see Remark
1.2
, later). This is really the novelty of our paper.

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2(1-\sigma)}+\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}}J[u_{0},u_{1},v_{0},v_{1}],

where

(2.8)

s\left(p^{*},q^{*}\right):=\frac{q^{*}-p^{*}}{(1-\sigma)(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}

and the weight function

is defined by

\left(\dfrac{\mu_{1}\left(c(1+t)^{-\gamma}\right)}{\mu_{2}\left(c(1+t)^{-\gamma}\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{*}+1}}&\text{ if \eqref{con3thr2.1} holds},\end{cases}

Remark 2.1
.

If we take

, then we obtain the same results as Theorem 1.1 proved by Dao-Reissig in
[
7
]
.
If

and

and our weakly coupled system becomes equation. If we take

[

Remark 2.2
.

There is no difficulty to achieve the same results for weakly couple system of semilinear structurally damped
\sigma
-evolution equations. Namely, the system contains the term
(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u
and
(-\Delta)^{\sigma}v
. However, we will not discuss any more about this since it is out of the paper’s scope.

The case
\sigma=0
was proved by Dao-Reissig in
[

]
, where they used a kind of test function introduced in
[
13
]
. Our approach is based on their paper, however, these test functions seem to be unable to treat our case when

2.2.
Construction of test functions

At first, we introduce the test function

\varphi=\varphi(x):=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $|x|\leq 1$},\\
(\sqrt[4]{1+(|x|-1)^{4}})^{-1}&\text{ if $|x|\geq 1$}.\end{cases}

Then, we define

\varphi^{*}=\varphi^{*}(x):=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $|x|<1$},\\
\varphi(x)&\text{ if $|x|\geq 1$}.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.3

(
[
6
]
, Lemma 2.3)
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
. Then, the following estimates hold for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\left|(-\Delta)^{s}[\varphi(x)]^{-n-2s}\right|\lesssim[\varphi(x)]^{-n-2s}.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
q>0
. Then, the following estimate holds for any multi-index
\alpha
satisfying
\alpha\geq 1
:

\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}[\varphi(x)]^{q}\right|\lesssim[\varphi(x)]^{q+\alpha},\,\,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof.

The case
|x|<1
is trivial so we consider only the case
|x|\geq 1
. For
|\alpha|\geq 1
, we have the following formula:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}h(f(x))=\sum_{k=1}^{|\alpha|}h^{(k)}(f(x))\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{k}\leq\alpha\\
\left|\gamma_{1}\right|+\cdots+\left|\gamma_{k}\right|=|\alpha|,\left|\gamma_{i}\right|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left(\partial_{x}^{\gamma_{1}}f(x)\right)\cdots\left(\partial_{x}^{\gamma_{k}}f(x)\right)\right),

where
h=h(z)
and
h^{(k)}(z)=\dfrac{d^{k}h(z)}{dz^{k}}
. If we choose
h(z)=z^{-\frac{q}{4}}
and
f(x)=1+(|x|-1)^{4}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}[\varphi(x)]^{q}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{|\alpha|}\left(1+(|x|-1)^{4}\right)^{-\frac{q}{4}-k}\times\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{k}\leq\alpha\\
\left|\gamma_{1}\right|+\cdots+\left|\gamma_{k}\right|=|\alpha|,\left|\gamma_{i}\right|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left|\partial_{x}^{\gamma_{1}}\left(1+(|x|-1)^{4}\right)\right|\cdots\left|\partial_{x}^{\gamma_{k}}\left(1+(|x|-1)^{4}\right)\right|\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{|\alpha|}\left(1+(|x|-1)^{4}\right)^{-\frac{q}{4}-k}\times\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{k}\leq\alpha\\
\left|\gamma_{1}\right|+\cdots+\left|\gamma_{k}\right|=|\alpha|,\left|\gamma_{i}\right|\geq 1\end{subarray}}(|x|-1)^{4-|\gamma_{1}|}\cdots(|x|-1)^{4-|\gamma_{k}|}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{|\alpha|}\left(1+(|x|-1)^{4}\right)^{-\frac{q}{4}-k}(|x|-1)^{4k-|\alpha|}

\displaystyle\lesssim[\varphi(x)]^{q+\alpha},\,\,\forall|x|\geq 1.

Hence, the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
. Let
\varphi
be a smooth function satisfying
\partial^{2}_{x}\varphi\in L^{\infty}
. For any
R>0
, let
\varphi_{R}
be a function defined by

\varphi_{R}(x):=\varphi(R^{-1/2}x)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then,
(-\Delta)^{s}(\varphi_{R})
satisfies the following scaling properties for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

(-\Delta)^{s}(\varphi_{R})(x)=R^{-s}((-\Delta)^{s}\varphi)(R^{-1/2}x).

Proof.

Using a well-known argument (see
[
8
]
, Lemma 3), under the assumption
\partial^{2}_{x}\varphi\in L^{\infty}
, we may remove the principal value of the integral at the origin to conclude

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}\left(\varphi_{R}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{C_{n,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varphi_{R}(x+y)+\varphi_{R}(x-y)-2\varphi_{R}(x)}{|y|^{n+2s}}dy

\displaystyle=-\frac{C_{n,s}}{2R^{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varphi\left(R^{-1/2}x+R^{-1/2}y\right)+\varphi\left(R^{-1/2}x-R^{-1/2}y\right)-2\varphi\left(R^{-1/2}x\right)}{\left|R^{-1/2}y\right|^{n+2s}}d\left(R^{-1/2}y\right)

\displaystyle=R^{-s}\left((-\Delta)^{s}\varphi\right)\left(R^{-1/2}x\right).

This completes our proof.
∎

Next, we introduce the functions

\displaystyle|\eta^{\prime}(t)|+|\eta^{\prime\prime}(t)|\lesssim C\text{ for $t\in[0,\infty)$}.

To estimate the left-hand side (LHS) of (

\displaystyle G_{q}^{\prime\prime}(\rho)=\left(\frac{g_{q}(\rho)}{\rho}\right)^{\prime}

1.7

(u_{0}(x),u_{1}(x),v_{0}(x),v_{1}(x))
in (

\displaystyle 8c^{v}M^{q^{*}-1}\left(\Psi\left(T^{*}\right)\right)^{1-\beta(p^{*},q^{*})q^{*}}\varepsilon^{q^{*}-1}\geq 1.

Hence,

\Psi(T^{*})\geq c\varepsilon^{-\min\left\{\frac{p^{*}-1}{\beta(p^{*},q^{*})},\frac{p^{*}q^{*}-1}{p^{*}-\beta(p^{*},q^{*})(p^{*}q^{*}-1)},\frac{q^{*}-1}{1-\beta(p^{*},q^{*})q^{*}}\right\}}\geq c\varepsilon^{-\frac{q^{*}(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}{q^{*}+1}},

from which we can find the blow-up time

(3.26)

T_{\varepsilon}\geq\Psi^{-1}\left(c\varepsilon^{-\frac{q^{*}(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}{q^{*}+1}}\right),

where
c
is a positive constant independent of the small parameter
\varepsilon
.

The special case
p^{*}=q^{*}
is simpler with an analogous procedure to the proof of the case
p^{*}<q^{*}
by setting
s(p^{*},q^{*})=0
and
\beta(p^{*},q^{*})=0
. More precisely, from (
3.19
) one arrives at

\|v\|_{Y_{2}\left(T^{*}\right)}\leq\varepsilon\left(c_{0}+c_{1}^{v}M^{p^{*}}\Psi\left(T^{*}\right)\varepsilon^{p^{*}-1}\right).

By the arguments used in
[
12
]
, we obtain

(3.27)

T_{\varepsilon}\geq\Psi^{-1}\left(c\varepsilon^{-(p^{*}-1)}\right).

Notice that the function
\Psi(s)
does not coincide with the function
\psi(s)
mentioned in the upper bound. We need one more step to attain the sharp estimate. From (
3.26
), (
3.27
) and the property of the function
\Psi^{-1}(s)
, we can choose a constant
\varepsilon_{0}
small enough such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
,
T_{\varepsilon}\geq R_{1}
, where
R_{1}
is a sufficiently large constant depending on
n,p^{*},q^{*},\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
and will be determined later. Now we need to choose a “better” one of the function
\ell(t)
as follows:

(3.28)

\ell(t)=\mu_{1}\left(C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{1+q^{*}}{(1-\sigma)(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}}\right).

We will prove that we can choose
R_{1}
large enough such that for
t\geq R_{1}
, the above function satisfies

(3.29)

\left(\ell(t)\right)^{-1}\mu_{1}\left(C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{1+q^{*}}{(1-\sigma)(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}}\right)\lesssim C

and

(3.30)

\displaystyle\left(\ell(t)\right)^{q^{*}}\mu_{2}\left(C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{1+p^{*}}{(1-\sigma)(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}}\ell(t)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\mu_{1}\left(C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{1+q^{*}}{(1-\sigma)(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}}\right)\right)^{\frac{q^{*}}{q^{*}+1}}\left(\mu_{2}\left(C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{q^{*}+1}{(1-\sigma)(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{*}+1}}.

Indeed, the estimate (
3.29
) is obvious so we consider the second one (
3.30
). By the change of variable
s=C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{1+q^{*}}{(1-\sigma)(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}}
, we show that the function

h(s):=\frac{\left(\mu_{1}(s)\right)^{\frac{(q^{*})^{2}}{q^{*}+1}}\mu_{2}\left(C_{2}s^{\frac{1+p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}\right)}{\left(\mu_{2}(s)\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{*}+1}}}

is increasing. Indeed, by a simple calculation, one has

h^{\prime}(s)\geq h(s)\left(\left(\frac{1+p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}\right)\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{2}\left(C_{2}s^{\frac{1+p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}\mu_{1}(s)\right)}{\mu_{2}\left(C_{2}s^{\frac{1+p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}\mu_{1}(s)\right)}C_{2}s^{\frac{1+p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}\mu_{1}(s)-\frac{1}{q^{*}+1}\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{2}(s)s}{\mu_{2}(s)}\right).

Hence, it is sufficent to show that

(3.31)

\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{2}\left(C_{2}s^{\frac{1+p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}\mu_{1}(s)\right)}{\mu_{2}\left(C_{2}s^{\frac{1+p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}\mu_{1}(s)\right)}C_{2}s^{\frac{1+p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}\mu_{1}(s)\geq\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{2}(s)s}{\mu_{2}(s)}\text{ for $s\in(0,s_{0}]$},

where
s_{0}
is a sufficient small constant. To claim (
3.31
), we have two properties

•

\displaystyle\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{2}(s)s}{\mu_{2}(s)}
is non-decreasing on
(0,s_{0}]
,

•

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(s)\geq C^{-1}_{2}s^{\frac{q^{*}-p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}
, for
s\in(0,s_{0}]
.

The first one is deduced from the assumption (
1.12
). Indeed, putting

\omega_{1}(s):=\displaystyle\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{2}(s)s}{\mu_{2}(s)},

and using assumption (
1.12
), we have

\omega^{\prime}_{1}(s)=\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{2}(s)}{\mu_{2}(s)}\left(1+\frac{\mu^{\prime\prime}_{2}(s)s}{\mu^{\prime}_{2}(s)}-\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{2}(s)s}{\mu_{2}(s)}\right)\geq 0

for
s\in(0,s_{0}]
if we choose
s_{0}
small enough. Hence,
\omega_{1}(s)
is non-decreasing for
s\in(0,s_{0}]
, we achieve the first property. The second property can be dealt similarly. Let us now denote

\omega_{2}(s):=\frac{\mu_{1}(s)}{s^{\frac{q^{*}-p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}}

to get

\omega^{\prime}_{2}(s)=\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{1}(s)s^{\frac{q^{*}-p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}-\frac{q^{*}-p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}s^{\frac{q^{*}-p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}-1}}{s^{\frac{2(q^{*}-p^{*})}{1+q^{*}}}}=\frac{\omega_{2}(s)}{s}\left(\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{1}(s)s}{\mu_{1}(s)}-\frac{q^{*}-p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}\right).

Due to assumption (
1.12
), we imply that for
s_{0}
is small enough
\omega_{2}(s)
is decreasing, which leads to

\mu_{1}(s)\geq Cs^{\frac{q^{*}-p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}\geq C^{-1}_{2}s^{\frac{q^{*}-p^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}

since
C^{-1}_{2}=C_{1}^{\frac{p^{*}-q^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}
and
C_{1}
can be chosen as large as we want. Therefore, (
3.31
) is true and we conclude that
h(s)
is increasing as well as (
3.30
). Finally, (
3.29
) and (
3.30
) allow us to replace the parameter
\gamma
in the definition of
\ell(t)
by

\frac{q^{*}+1}{(1-\sigma)(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}=\frac{n-2\sigma}{2(1-\sigma)}

and we obtain a new form of
\Psi(t)

\Psi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(1+t)^{-1}\left(\mu_{1}\left(C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{n-2\sigma}{2(1-\sigma)}}\right)\right)^{\frac{q^{*}}{1+q^{*}}}\left(\mu_{2}\left(C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{n-2\sigma}{2(1-\sigma)}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{*}+1}}\;dt.

To end this problem, by the change of variable
C_{0}s^{-\frac{n}{2}+\sigma}=C_{1}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n-2\sigma}{2(1-\sigma)}}
, we have

\Psi(s)=\psi(C_{4}s^{(1-\sigma)^{-1}}-1)+C_{5},

where
C_{4}=\left(\frac{C_{0}}{C_{1}}\right)^{(1-\sigma)^{-1}}
. Then, for
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, we obtain the lower bound

C_{4}(T_{\varepsilon})^{(1-\sigma)^{-1}}-1\geq\begin{cases}\psi^{-1}\left(c\varepsilon^{-\frac{q^{*}(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}{q^{*}+1}}\right)&\text{ if $p^{*}<q^{*}$},\\
\psi^{-1}\left(c\varepsilon^{-p^{*}+1}\right)&\text{ if $p^{*}=q^{*}$},\end{cases}

which is equivalent to

T_{\varepsilon}\gtrsim\begin{cases}\left(\psi^{-1}\left(c\varepsilon^{-\frac{q^{*}(p^{*}q^{*}-1)}{q^{*}+1}}\right)\right)^{1-\sigma}&\text{ if $p^{*}<q^{*}$},\\
\left(\psi^{-1}\left(c\varepsilon^{-p^{*}+1}\right)\right)^{1-\sigma}&\text{ if $p^{*}=q^{*}$}.\end{cases}

Therefore, our proof is completed.
∎

4.
Conclusion and Open problems

Throughout this paper, we investigate the sharp condition of modulus of continuity that guarantees the global existence of solution. Furthermore, when the solution exists locally and blows up in finite time, we achieve the sharp lifespan estimates for the solution. In future work, we are interested in studying the following weakly coupled system of semilinear structurally damped
\sigma
-evolution equations with critical nonlinearities:

(4.1)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u+(-\Delta)^{\delta}u_{t}=|v|^{p^{*}}\mu_{1}(|v|),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
v_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}v+(-\Delta)^{\delta}v_{t}=|u|^{q^{*}}\mu_{2}(|u|),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geq 0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\quad v_{t}(0,x)=v_{1}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

When
\sigma
is any fractional number and
\delta\in[0,\sigma]
, the terms
(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u
and
(-\Delta)^{\delta}v
are quite difficult to handle by our method. To the best of our knowledge, this is still an open question so far.
