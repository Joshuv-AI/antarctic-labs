Clustered vortex helices with compactly supported cross-sectional vorticity in the 3D Euler equations
Averkios Averkiou, Monica Musso, Fang Yu

Abstract
. We consider the three-dimensional incompressible Euler equations for helical flows without swirl. By adapting gluing techniques, we construct the first smooth multi-vortex solution in the whole space ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} exhibiting a cluster of collapsing helical filaments, with the associated cross-sectional vorticity remaining compactly supported in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} for all times. Our result generalises previous collapsing configurations in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} with rapidly decaying vorticity cores, and extends related variational solutions obtained in infinite cylindrical domains.



1.
Introduction

The motion of a three-dimensional inviscid and incompressible fluid without external forcing is described by the Euler equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. For a sufficiently smooth velocity field
\mathbf{u}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0,
vorticity
\vec{\omega}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\nabla\times{\mathbf{u}}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{3},
stream function
\vec{\psi}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
and prescribed initial data
\mathbf{u}(\cdot,0)=u_{0}(x)
, the Euler system in vorticity formulation reads

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\vec{\omega}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla){\vec{\omega}}

\displaystyle=(\vec{\omega}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hbox{in }}\,\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T),

\displaystyle\quad\mathbf{u}=\nabla\times\vec{\psi},

\displaystyle-\Delta\vec{\psi}=\vec{\omega}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hbox{in }}\,\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T),

\displaystyle{\vec{\omega}}(\cdot,0)

\displaystyle=\nabla\times u_{0}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hbox{in }}\,\mathbb{R}^{3}.

We are interested in the rigorous construction of smooth solutions to (
1.1
) with vortex filament structure, where the vorticity remains highly concentrated around a smooth curve evolving over time. Despite the growing interest in the vortex dynamics of such flows in recent years, their study dates back to the foundational works of Helmholtz and Kelvin
[
28
,
32
]
. Formal asymptotic considerations suggest that when the vorticity is concentrated in a thin tubular neighbourhood of radius
\varepsilon>0
around a smooth curve
\mathcal{G}(t),
the associated filament evolves to leading order according to the binormal flow with velocity
\mathcal{O}\left(|\log\varepsilon|\right)
. More precisely, for an arclength parametrisation
\gamma(s,t)
and a circulation constant
\bar{c}
, the formal derivations in
[
11
,
30
]
indicate that the evolution is governed by

\partial_{t}\gamma=\bar{c}|\log\varepsilon|\left(\gamma_{s}\times\gamma_{ss}\right)\ \quad\text{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0,

which one can also express as

(1.2)

\partial_{\tau}\gamma=\bar{c}\varkappa\mathbf{B}_{{\mathcal{G}(\tau)}},\quad t=|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau,

with
\varkappa
denoting the curvature and
\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{G}(\tau)}
the binormal unit vector. In this regime, the
vortex filament conjecture
concerns the rigorous verification that concentrated vorticity structures remain localised and evolve at leading order along the trajectories determined by (
1.2
). Beyond the conditional result in
[
29
]
, significant progress has been achieved in the case of circles and helices, which constitute symmetric solutions of (
1.2
).

For circular vortex filaments, the validity of the binormal law (
1.2
) was first established in
[
22
]
through the construction of
vortex rings
propagating with constant speed
\mathcal{O}\left(|\log\varepsilon|\right)
, with subsequent contributions including
[
16
,
21
,
4
,
2
,
25
]
. In the helical framework, the vortex filament conjecture was rigorously verified in
[
15
]
, where the authors constructed
vortex helices
; see also
[
31
,
6
,
8
,
3
]
and the references therein. Of particular relevance to the present work are the helical multi-vortex solutions in
[
23
,
7
,
24
,
9
,
17
]
, covering among others nearly parallel and collapsing configurations, while results on global well-posedness and long-time dynamics appear in
[
27
,
26
,
5
]
. Finally, helical structures have also been investigated in related models, such as the Ginzburg–Landau
[
14
,
18
]
, the Gross–Pitaevskii
[
10
,
13
]
, and the Schrödinger map
[
33
]
equations.

1.1.
Statement of the main result

This paper is concerned with the rigorous construction of the first smooth global-in-time solution
\vec{\omega}_{\varepsilon}(x,\tau)
to (
1.1
) in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3},
which concentrates, as
\varepsilon\to 0,
near multiple collapsing helices undergoing coupled rotation and translation at nearly identical speeds, with the main novelty that the associated cross-sectional vorticity remains compactly supported in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for all times. We refer to such a solution as a
cluster of vortex helices with compactly supported cross-sectional vorticity
.

In this regard, given an integer
N\geq 1
and
h>0,
we take planar points
P_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(a_{i},b_{i}),\,i=1,\dots,N,
and consider the evolving circular helices
\mathcal{G}_{i}(\tau)
of radius
R_{i}=\sqrt{a_{i}^{2}+b_{i}^{2}}
and common pitch
h>0
, parametrised by arclength as

(1.3)

\gamma_{i}(s,\tau)=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{i}\cos\left(\frac{s-\sigma_{1,i}\tau}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R_{i}^{2}}}\right)-b_{i}\sin\left(\frac{s-\sigma_{1,i}\tau}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R_{i}^{2}}}\right)\\
a_{i}\sin\left(\frac{s-\sigma_{1,i}\tau}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R_{i}^{2}}}\right)+b_{i}\cos\left(\frac{s-\sigma_{1,i}\tau}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R_{i}^{2}}}\right)\\
\frac{hs+\sigma_{2,i}\tau}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R_{i}^{2}}}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad\sigma_{1,i}=\frac{\bar{c}_{i}\,h}{R_{i}^{2}+h^{2}},\quad\sigma_{2,i}=\frac{\bar{c}_{i}\,R_{i}^{2}}{R_{i}^{2}+h^{2}}.

One can readily verify that each
\gamma_{i}
has curvature
\frac{R_{i}}{R_{i}^{2}+h^{2}}
, torsion
\frac{h}{R_{i}^{2}+h^{2}}
, and evolves according to the binormal flow (
1.2
) with
\bar{c}_{i}=\bar{c}\kappa_{i}
, where
\kappa_{i}
is the circulation and
\bar{c}
is determined by the regularisation profile; see (
2.7
). Hereafter, we identify the base point
(a_{i},b_{i},0)
of each helix with the planar point
(a_{i},b_{i})
.

In view of our interest in colliding helical filaments, for a fixed
r_{0}>0
we consider points
P_{i}=(a_{i},b_{i})
in (
1.3
) of the specific form

(1.4)

P_{i}=(r_{0}+\tilde{r},0)+\frac{\widehat{P}_{i}}{|\log\varepsilon|},\quad|\tilde{r}|\leq\delta\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{|\log\varepsilon|},\quad\delta<|\widehat{P}_{i}|<\delta^{-1},\quad i=1,\dots,N,

where
\delta>0
is sufficiently small and independent of
\varepsilon>0.
Since
P_{i}=(a_{i},b_{i})\to(r_{0},0)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,N\},
we infer that the helices
\mathcal{G}_{i}(\tau)
in (
1.3
) collapse onto the same limiting helix as
\varepsilon\to 0.

Furthermore, we assume that the points
\widehat{P}_{i}
in (
1.4
) are uniformly separated, namely there exists a constant
d>0
independent of
\varepsilon>0
such that

(1.5)

\min_{i\neq j}|\widehat{P}_{j}-\widehat{P}_{i}|\geq d,

and we also impose a reflection symmetry on the set
\{\widehat{P}_{1},\dots,\widehat{P}_{N}\}
with respect to the first coordinate axis, i.e.

(1.6)

\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2})\in\{\widehat{P}_{1},\dots,\widehat{P}_{N}\}\iff(\mathcal{P}_{1},-\mathcal{P}_{2})\in\{\widehat{P}_{1},\dots,\widehat{P}_{N}\}.

Finally, for
h>0
and
\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{N}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}>0
, the points
\widehat{P}_{i}
admit the decomposition

(1.7)

\widehat{P}_{i}=P_{i}^{b}+q_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,N,

so that if we write in anisotropic coordinates

(1.8)

{P}^{b}_{i}=\left(\frac{h\,\widetilde{P}^{b}_{i,1}}{\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{0}^{2}}},\widetilde{P}^{b}_{i,2}\right),\quad\widetilde{P}^{b}_{i}=\left(\widetilde{P}^{b}_{i,1},\widetilde{P}^{b}_{i,2}\right),

the configuration
\left(\widetilde{P}^{b}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{P}^{b}_{N}\right)
solves, for all
i=1,\dots,N,
the system of balancing conditions

(1.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}\kappa_{j}\frac{\widetilde{P}^{b}_{i,1}-\widetilde{P}^{b}_{j,1}}{|\widetilde{P}^{b}_{i}-\widetilde{P}^{b}_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\kappa_{i}hr_{0}}{2(h^{2}+r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\alpha\frac{hr_{0}}{\nu^{\prime}(1)\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{0}^{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}\kappa_{j}\frac{\widetilde{P}^{b}_{i,2}-\widetilde{P}^{b}_{j,2}}{|\widetilde{P}^{b}_{i}-\widetilde{P}^{b}_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=0,

for some constants
\alpha>0
and
\nu^{\prime}(1)<0
; see (
2.7
). Here and throughout this work, the system (
1.9
) uniquely determines
\alpha>0
as

(1.10)

\alpha=\frac{-\nu^{\prime}(1)}{2(h^{2}+r_{0}^{2})}\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}^{2}}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}}.

In addition, the terms
{q}_{i}
in (
1.7
) are lower order perturbations, with
|q_{i}|\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Notably, admissible configurations of points
P^{b}=\left(P^{b}_{1},\dots,P^{b}_{N}\right)
satisfying (
1.5
), (
1.6
), (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) are exhibited in
[
2
,
1
]
. In this context, since (
1.9
) is translation-invariant, we call a solution
P^{b}
as in (
1.8
)
nondegenerate
if the linearisation of (
1.9
) at
P^{b}
has a one-dimensional kernel, arising from the symmetry assumption (
1.6
). A more detailed analysis of this condition and its consequences is deferred to Section
9
, where it plays a crucial role in locating the points
\widehat{P}_{i}
in (
1.7
) as small perturbations of
P_{i}^{b}
,
i=1,\dots,N,
thereby determining the adjusted vortex centers of the helices associated with an exact smooth solution of (
1.1
).

The following Theorem is the main result of this work.

Theorem 1
.

Fix an integer
N\geq 1
, constants
r_{0}>0,\,h>0,
and
\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{N}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}>0.
For each
i=1,\dots,N
, denote by
\mathcal{G}_{i}(\tau)
the evolving helix parametrised by (
1.3
), centered at the point
P_{i}=(a_{i},b_{i})
in (
1.4
), and let
\delta_{\mathcal{G}_{i}(\tau)}
designate a uniform Dirac measure supported on
\mathcal{G}_{i}(\tau)
and
\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{G}_{i}(\tau)}
its tangent unit vector. Moreover, assume
that the configuration
P^{b}=\left(P_{1}^{b},\dots,P_{N}^{b}\right)
satisfying (
1.5
), (
1.6
) and (
1.8
), is a nondegenerate solution of (
1.9
). Then, there exist

\tilde{r}_{*}\in\mathbb{R}
,
q_{1},\dots,q_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and specific points of the form

(1.11)

P_{i}=(r_{0}+\tilde{r}_{*},0)+\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\widehat{P}_{i},\quad\widehat{P}_{i}=P_{i}^{b}+q_{i},\quad|\tilde{r}_{*}|,|q_{i}|\lesssim\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{|\log\varepsilon|},\quad i=1,\dots,N,

which determine a smooth global-in-time solution
\vec{\omega}_{\varepsilon}(x,\tau)
of (
1.1
) with cross-sectional vorticity compactly supported in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for all times, such that for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
it satisfies

\vec{\omega}_{\varepsilon}(x,|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau)\rightharpoonup\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}\delta_{\mathcal{G}_{i}(\tau)}\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{G}_{i}(\tau)}\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0,

in the sense of measures.

We now present some remarks on Theorem
1
and its relation to the existing literature.

Remark 1.1
.

Theorem
1
provides the first smooth multi-vortex helical solution
\vec{\omega}_{\varepsilon}(x,\tau)
of (
1.1
) in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
whose vorticity concentrates near collapsing filaments as
\varepsilon\to 0
, while the associated cross-sectional vorticity remains compactly supported in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for all times. More precisely, for all
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small,
\alpha>0
given by (
1.10
) and
P_{i}
as in (
1.11
), it holds

(1.12)

\operatorname{supp}\vec{\omega}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,x_{3},\tau)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B_{\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\left(R_{\frac{x_{3}}{h}-\alpha\tau}P_{i}\right),\quad R_{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},

for all
(x_{3},\tau)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},
hence each planar cross-section of
\vec{\omega}_{\varepsilon}(x,\tau)
is supported in a union of
N
disjoint compact balls. Consequently, for every
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, the solution
\vec{\omega}_{\varepsilon}(x,\tau)
remains confined within
N
non-overlapping helical tubes of radius
\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{2}
, whose vortex cores collapse onto a single helix as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Remark 1.2
.

A related multi-vortex helical solution of (
1.1
) in bounded domains is obtained in
[
17
]
, where the authors consider vorticity initially concentrated near helices of distinct radii and show that the concentration persists in a finite time interval
\tau\in[0,T]
, with cross-sectional vorticity supported in a thin annulus. Nevertheless, their filaments remain uniformly separated at distance
\mathcal{O}(1)
, thus the interaction between filaments is asymptotically negligible compared to self-induced motion, effectively reducing the problem to a single-helix setting. In contrast, our filament separation scale is
\mathcal{O}(|\log\varepsilon|^{-1})
, placing the problem in a strongly coupled interaction regime, while concentration persists for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. For a similar reason related to filament separation, the polygonal solution of
[
3
]
does not fit the current framework. Moreover,
[
7
]
is concerned with clustered helical solutions in infinite cylinders obtained through variational methods. However, their analysis relies crucially on a
C^{1}
expansion of the Green’s function of a uniformly elliptic operator in bounded planar domains. Since this expansion fails in the whole
\mathbb{R}^{2}
due to the loss of uniform ellipticity, their approach does not seem to be directly adaptable to our setting.

1.2.
Helical symmetry with no swirl reduction

Our construction is carried out within the class of helically symmetric flows with no helical swirl
[
20
,
19
]
, where the Euler dynamics in (
1.1
) reduces to a transport-elliptic system in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. More precisely, writing
x=(x^{\prime},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
t=|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\tau
, we consider the pair
\left(w(x^{\prime},\tau),\psi(x^{\prime},\tau)\right)
solving

(1.13)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} |\log\varepsilon|\partial_{\tau}\omega+\nabla^{\perp}\psi\cdot\nabla w&=0&&{\mbox{in}}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(-\infty,+\infty),\\
-\nabla\cdot(K\nabla\psi)&=w&&{\mbox{in}}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(-\infty,+\infty),\end{aligned}\right.

with
(a,b)^{\perp}=(b,-a)
and
K(x_{1},x_{2})
denoting the coefficient matrix

K(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}h^{2}+x_{2}^{2}&-x_{1}x_{2}\\
-x_{1}x_{2}&h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\end{matrix}\right).

If
w(x^{\prime},\tau)
solves (
1.13
), then the vorticity vector

(1.14)

\displaystyle\vec{\omega}(x,\tau)=\frac{1}{h}w\left(R_{-\frac{x_{3}}{h}}x^{\prime},\tau\right)\,\begin{pmatrix}-x_{2}\\
x_{1}\\
h\end{pmatrix},\quad R_{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},

is helically symmetric with no swirl and satisfies (
1.1
). Moreover, seeking rotating solutions of the form

(1.15)

w(x^{\prime},\tau)=W\left(R_{\alpha\tau}x^{\prime}\right),\quad\psi(x^{\prime},\tau)=\Psi\left(R_{\alpha\tau}x^{\prime}\right),\quad x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

reduces (
1.13
) to the semilinear elliptic equation

(1.16)

\nabla_{\tilde{x}}\cdot\left(K(\tilde{x})\nabla_{\tilde{x}}\Psi\right)+F\left(\Psi-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||\tilde{x}|^{2}\right)=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad\tilde{x}=R_{\alpha\tau}x^{\prime}.

Our aim is to find a suitable smooth nonlinear function
F
and a stream function
\Psi
for (
1.16
), so that the
vorticity
W(\tilde{x})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=F\left(\Psi-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||\tilde{x}|^{2}\right)

satisfies

W(\tilde{x})\sim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}\delta_{P_{i}},\quad\operatorname{supp}W(\tilde{x})\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B_{\tilde{\rho}_{\varepsilon}}(P_{i}),

with
P_{i}
as in (
1.4
) and
\tilde{\rho}_{\varepsilon}=o\left(|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\right)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. In this way, the cross-sections are pairwise disjoint since
\min\limits_{i\neq j}|P_{i}-P_{j}|\geq\frac{d}{|\log\varepsilon|}
, and the profile
w(x^{\prime},\tau)=W(R_{\alpha\tau}x^{\prime})
in (
1.15
)
is rigidly rotating with angular speed
\alpha
as in (
1.10
), while remaining compactly supported for all times in the rotated union of planar vortex cores. Consequently, due to (
1.14
) we obtain a smooth
3
D multi-filament solution
\vec{\omega}(x,\tau)
, which is confined in thin non-overlapping helical tubes globally in time.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1
is proved via an Inner–Outer gluing scheme, in the spirit of
[
23
]
. A key novelty of the present work is the choice of nonlinearity
F
in (
1.16
), motivated by the equation
\Delta u+u^{\gamma}_{+}=0\,
in
\,\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
u_{+}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max(0,u)
and
\gamma>3
. This setting differs signifincatly from that in
[
23
]
, where
F(s)\sim e^{s}
. While the latter yields rapidly decaying cross-sectional vorticity, the current choice
F(s)\sim s_{+}^{\gamma}
gives rise to a compactly supported one, as in (
1.12
). Moreover, since our construction involves the operator
\Delta+\gamma\Gamma_{+}^{\gamma-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with
\Gamma
given in (
2.7
), the presence of the radial kernel element
Z_{0}=\mathcal{O}(\log(2+|y|))
in (
7.4
) poses a major technical difficulty, since the quantity
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\gamma\Gamma_{+}^{\gamma-1}Z_{0}\neq 0
induces a strong coupling in the Inner-Outer system (see Proposition
8.1
). In contrast, the Liouville operator
\Delta+e^{u}
employed in
[
23
]
, with
\Delta u+e^{u}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, has a radial kernel element
\widetilde{Z}_{0}=\mathcal{O}(1)
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{u}\widetilde{Z}_{0}=0
, hence the corresponding Inner–Outer system is sufficiently decoupled. To resolve this, we refine the gluing procedure in two main directions. First, we introduce perturbations
\mu_{j}^{*}
in the scaling parameters of Proposition
4.1
to weaken the coupling and obtain solvability of the projected problem in Proposition
8.1
. Second, to make this modification compatible with the overall gluing scheme, we adapt the solution procedure of the Outer problem (see section
6.1
) so that these scaling perturbations are a posteriori sufficiently small in
\varepsilon>0.

Following the preceding discussion, in the sequel we write
x
instead of
\tilde{x}
in (
1.16
) for ease of notation, and dedicate the rest of the paper to solving the problem

(1.17)

\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(K\nabla_{x}\Psi\right)+F\left(\Psi-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}\right)=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}.

As explained earlier, to establish Theorem
1
it suffices to select a suitable nonlinearity
F
and construct a stream function
\Psi
for (
1.17
), so that the induced vorticity
W(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=F\left(\Psi-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}\right)
has the properties

(1.18)

F\left(\Psi-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}\right)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}\delta_{P_{i}},\quad\operatorname{supp}W(x)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B_{\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}(P_{i}),

where
P_{i}=(a_{i},b_{i})\to(r_{0},0)
as
\varepsilon\to 0\,
for all

as can be verified by (
1.4
).

1.3.
Structure of the paper

The organisation of this work is as follows. In Section

, we construct a local regularised approximation of the Green’s function of

in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, based on a radial profile

2.
Local approximate stream function

Let

and consider a point
P=(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
. This section is concerned with the construction of an approximate stream function
\Psi
with the property

(2.1)

-\nabla_{x}\cdot(K\nabla_{x}\Psi)\sim c\delta_{P}

locally around
P
, with

(2.2)

L_{x}=\nabla_{x}\cdot(K\nabla_{x}\cdot)\quad\text{and}\ \quad K=\frac{1}{h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}h^{2}+x_{2}^{2}&-x_{1}x_{2}\\
-x_{1}x_{2}&h^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

As a preliminary step, we analyse the behaviour of
L_{x}
in a small neighbourhood of the point
P
, which will enable us to derive a local approximation of the Green’s function for (
2.2
).

Following
[
23
]
, we introduce the linear change of variables

(2.3)

x-P=A[P]z,\quad A[P]=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{ah}{R\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}&-\frac{b}{R}\\
\frac{bh}{R\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}&\frac{a}{R}\end{pmatrix},

which normalises the coefficient matrix
K
in (
2.2
) at the point
P=(a,b)
.

The following Proposition holds.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

be the variable defined in (
2.3
). Then, the operator

in (

) can be expressed as

where

\displaystyle B_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{h^{2}(R^{2}-r^{2})+z_{2}^{2}(h^{2}+R^{2})}{h^{2}(h^{2}+r^{2})}\right)\partial_{z_{1}z_{1}}+\frac{1}{(h^{2}+r^{2})}\left(\left(z_{1}\frac{h}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}+R\right)^{2}-r^{2}\right)\partial_{z_{2}z_{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-2\frac{\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}{h(h^{2}+r^{2})}z_{2}\left(z_{1}\frac{h}{\sqrt{h^{2}+R^{2}}}+R\right)\partial_{z_{1}z_{2}}

5.

In the present section, the objective is to select a suitable nonlinearity

\Psi_{0}(x)
from (

Inspired by the structure of (

where

Consider the inner region
|A_{i}^{-1}(x-P_{i})|<\frac{\delta}{|\log\varepsilon|}
centered at the vortex point
P_{i}
. In the rescaled variable

\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}\left(\Psi_{0}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}\right)=\nu^{\prime}(1)\log(\delta)\left(1+\tilde{s}(\delta)\right)-\nu^{\prime}(1)\log|\log\varepsilon|-\nu^{\prime}(1)\log(\mu_{i}^{0})-\frac{\alpha}{2\kappa_{i}}|\log\varepsilon||P_{i}|^{2}+o(1),

With this in mind, we define the cut-off

\eta_{i}(s)=\begin{cases}1,\quad\mbox{for}\quad s\geq-\nu^{\prime}(1)\log|\log\varepsilon|-\nu^{\prime}(1)\log(\mu_{i}^{0})-\frac{\alpha}{2\kappa_{i}}|\log\varepsilon||P_{i}|^{2}+2\ell_{i,\varepsilon},\\
0,\quad\mbox{for}\quad s\leq-\nu^{\prime}(1)\log|\log\varepsilon|-\nu^{\prime}(1)\log(\mu_{i}^{0})-\frac{\alpha}{2\kappa_{i}}|\log\varepsilon||P_{i}|^{2}+\ell_{i,\varepsilon},\end{cases}

\begin{cases}\eta_{i}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}\left(\Psi_{0}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}\right)\right)=1\quad\mbox{for}\quad|A_{i}^{-1}(x-P_{i})|\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{|\log\varepsilon|},\\
\eta_{i}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}\left(\Psi_{0}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}\right)\right)=0\quad\mbox{for}\quad|A_{i}^{-1}(x-P_{i})|\geq\frac{\delta}{|\log\varepsilon|}.\end{cases}

Proposition 5.1

We solve (

6.1.
Solution of the Outer Problem

To initiate the resolution of the coupled system formed by (
6.8
) and (
6.11
), we first deal with the Outer problem (
6.11
). For
\sigma>0
and
\beta\in(0,1)
, we make the a priori assumptions that

(6.13)

\phi_{out}\in C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad\|(1+|x|^{2})^{-1}\phi_{out}\|_{\infty}\leq 100,

while
\phi_{in,i}
satisfies

(6.14)

(1+|y|)|D_{y}\phi_{in,i}(y)|+|\phi_{in,i}(y)|\leq\frac{C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|}{1+|y|^{\sigma}},\quad i=1,\dots,N,

with
\mu_{i}
as in Proposition
4.1
and
y=\frac{A_{i}^{-1}(x-P_{i})}{\varepsilon\mu_{i}}
.

In this setting, the following lemma holds.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\phi_{out}(x)
as in (
6.13
) and
\phi_{in,i}(y),\,i=1,\dots,N,
satisfy the estimate (
6.14
). Then, for
\delta>0
sufficiently small and the cut-off functions
\eta_{i},\,\tilde{\eta}_{i},\,i=1,\dots,N,
in (
5.3
) and (
6.3
), there exists a small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
it holds

G\left(\phi_{in},\phi_{out}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(L_{x}\left[\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}\right]-\tilde{\eta}_{i}L_{x}\left[\phi_{in,i}\right]\big),

where
G(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})
is defined in (
6.12
).

Proof.

For fixed
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, we first consider the inner region

|A_{i}^{-1}(x-P_{i})|\leq\frac{\delta_{1}}{2|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}.

Due to (
5.3
), (
6.3
) and the uniform separation of the points in (
1.5
), namely

|P_{j}-P_{i}|\geq\frac{d}{|\log\varepsilon|},\quad\mbox{with}\quad 0<2\delta_{1}<\delta^{2}<\delta<\frac{\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{0}^{2}}}{4h}d,

we have
\tilde{\eta}_{i}=1
and
\tilde{\eta}_{j}=0
for every
j\neq i
, hence the result follows directly since
1-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{\eta}_{j}=1-\tilde{\eta}_{i}=0.

To establish the lemma, it therefore suffices to examine (
6.12
) in the region

|A_{i}^{-1}(x-P_{i})|>\frac{\delta_{1}}{2|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}.

We recall that in the definition of the nonlinearity
F
in (
5.2
), the cut-off
\eta_{i}
was introduced in (
5.3
)-(
5.4
) through
\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}\left(\Psi_{0}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon|\,|x|^{2}\right)=\mathcal{O}(|\log\varepsilon|),
and the transition occurs on a
\mathcal{O}(1)
scale. However, the same cut-off
\eta_{i}
is now evaluated at the perturbed argument
\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}(\Psi_{0}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}+\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}+\phi_{out})
. By (
6.13
) and (
6.14
), the perturbation satisfies

\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}(\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}+\phi_{out})=\mathcal{O}(1)
in any bounded region, whereas on unbounded sets, the term
-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}<0
dominates the quadratic growth
\mathcal{O}(1+|x|^{2})
of the global correction
H_{2\varepsilon}(x)
appearing in
\Psi_{0}
and the outer perturbation
\phi_{out}(x)
(see (
3.5
) and (
3.4
)) for all small
\varepsilon>0.
Consequently, the transition region of
\eta_{i}
differs from that in (
5.3
) only up to multiplicative constants, i.e. there exist universal constants
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
(independent of
\varepsilon>0
) such that

\eta_{i}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}\left(\Psi_{0}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon|\,|x|^{2}+\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}+\phi_{out}\right)\right)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }\left|A_{i}^{-1}(x-P_{i})\right|\leq\dfrac{\lambda_{1}\delta^{2}}{|\log\varepsilon|},\\[4.30554pt]
0,&\text{if }\left|A_{i}^{-1}(x-P_{i})\right|\geq\dfrac{\lambda_{2}\delta}{|\log\varepsilon|}.\end{cases}

In this respect, for the analysis of (
6.12
) we focus on the regions

(6.15)

\displaystyle U_{in,i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{\frac{\delta_{1}}{2|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}<|A_{i}^{-1}(x-P_{i})|<\frac{\lambda_{2}\delta}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right\},\quad U_{out}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left\{|A_{j}^{-1}(x-P_{j})|\geq\frac{\lambda_{2}\delta}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right\},

keeping in mind that for
x\in U_{in,i},
one has
\eta_{j}=\tilde{\eta}_{j}=0
for every
j\neq i
.

Let us first consider
x\in U_{in,i}.
By Proposition
5.1
and (
6.4
), we find

\left(1-\tilde{\eta}_{i}\right)\Big(F^{\prime}\big(\Psi_{0}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}\big)\phi_{out}+S(\Psi_{0})\Big)=0,

since

(6.16)

\operatorname{supp}F^{\prime}\left(\Psi_{0}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}\right)\,\cup\,\operatorname{supp}S(\Psi_{0})\subset\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|A_{j}^{-1}(x-P_{j})|<2\varepsilon\mu_{j}\right\}.

Therefore, the only remaining term to be controlled in the region
U_{in,i}
is

\displaystyle(1-\tilde{\eta}_{i})N_{0}(\varphi)=(1-\tilde{\eta}_{i})F\Big(\Psi_{0}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}+\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}+\phi_{out}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\tilde{\eta}_{i})\kappa_{i}}{\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}\left(\Psi_{0}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}+\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}+\phi_{out}\right)+\nu^{\prime}(1)|\log\varepsilon|+\frac{\alpha}{2\kappa_{i}}|\log\varepsilon||P_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\gamma}_{+}

\displaystyle\times\eta_{i}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}\left(\Psi_{0}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}+\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}+\phi_{out}\right)\right).

For readability, we set

\mathcal{H}_{i}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(1-\tilde{\eta}_{i})F\Big(\Psi_{0}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}+\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}+\phi_{out}\Big),

and establish that
\mathcal{H}_{i}(x)\equiv 0
for all small
\varepsilon>0
due to (
6.13
) and (
6.14
). We split the analysis into three annular regions around
P_{i}
using the variable

z=A_{i}^{-1}(x-P_{i}),\quad z=\varepsilon\mu_{i}y,

corresponding to the transition zones of the cut-offs
\eta_{i}
and
\tilde{\eta}_{i}
.

Case 1
:
\frac{\delta_{1}}{2\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}<|y|\leq\frac{\delta_{1}}{\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}
.

In this region,
\tilde{\eta}_{i}\in(0,1)
and
\eta_{i}=1
, thus Proposition
5.1
and the definitions of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{1,i}(P)
and
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{2,i}(P)
in (
5.7
)-(
5.8
) allow us to write

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(1-\tilde{\eta}_{i}\right)\kappa_{i}}{\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}}\Bigg(\Gamma(y)(1+c_{1,i}\varepsilon\mu_{i}y_{1}+c_{2,i}\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}|y|^{2})+\nu^{\prime}(1)\log\left(1+\frac{\mu_{i}^{*}}{\mu_{i}^{0}}\right)+\varepsilon\mu_{i}y_{1}|\log\varepsilon|\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{1,i}(P)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\mu_{i}y_{2}|\log\varepsilon|\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{2,i}(P)+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}|y|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}\big(\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}(y)+\phi_{out}\left(P_{i}+A_{i}\varepsilon\mu_{i}y\right)\big)\Bigg)^{\gamma}_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(1-\tilde{\eta}_{i}\right)\kappa_{i}}{\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}}\Big(\nu^{\prime}(1)|\log\varepsilon|\mathcal{E}_{1,i}(y)\Big)^{\gamma}_{+},

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1,i}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\log|y|}{|\log\varepsilon|}\left(1+c_{1,i}\varepsilon\mu_{i}y_{1}+c_{2,i}\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}|y|^{2}\right)+\frac{\log\left(1+\frac{\mu_{i}^{*}}{\mu_{i}^{0}}\right)}{|\log\varepsilon|}+\frac{\varepsilon\mu_{i}}{\nu^{\prime}(1)}\Big(y_{1}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{1,i}(P)+y_{2}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{2,i}(P)\Big)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}|\log\varepsilon||y|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}\nu^{\prime}(1)|\log\varepsilon|}\Big(\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}(y)+\phi_{out}\left(P_{i}+A_{i}\varepsilon\mu_{i}y\right)\Big).

Using (
6.13
) and (
6.14
) together with the expansion
\frac{\log|y|}{|\log\varepsilon|}=1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right),
we obtain

\mathcal{E}_{1,i}(y)=\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right)\right)\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta_{1}|\log\varepsilon|^{-2}\right)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\right),

while recalling that
\nu^{\prime}(1)|\log\varepsilon|<0
, we may take
\varepsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small to ensure
\mathcal{E}_{1,i}(y)>0
, so that
\mathcal{H}_{i}(x)=0
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
.

Case 2
:
\frac{\delta_{1}}{\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}<|y|\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}\delta^{2}}{\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|}
.

In this region,
\tilde{\eta}_{i}=0
and
\eta_{i}=1
, hence we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{i}}{\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}}\Bigg(\Gamma(y)(1+c_{1,i}\varepsilon\mu_{i}y_{1}+c_{2,i}\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}|y|^{2})+\nu^{\prime}(1)\log\left(1+\frac{\mu_{i}^{*}}{\mu_{i}^{0}}\right)+\varepsilon\mu_{i}y_{1}|\log\varepsilon|\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{i,1}(P)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\mu_{i}y_{2}|\log\varepsilon|\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{i,2}(P)+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}|y|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}\phi_{out}\left(P_{i}+A_{i}\varepsilon\mu_{i}y\right)\Bigg)^{\gamma}_{+}

Analogously to the previous case, for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
it holds
\mathcal{H}_{i}(x)=0
,
since

\mathcal{H}_{i}(x)=\frac{\kappa_{i}}{\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}}\left(\nu^{\prime}(1)|\log\varepsilon|\mathcal{E}_{2,i}(y)\right)^{\gamma}_{+},

with

\mathcal{E}_{2,i}(y)=\left(1+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{|\log\varepsilon|}\Big)\right)\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\right)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\right).

Case 3:

\frac{\lambda_{1}\delta^{2}}{\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|}<|y|<\frac{\lambda_{2}\delta}{\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|}.

Here
\eta_{i}\in(0,1)
and
\tilde{\eta}_{i}=0
, and a similar argument as in the previous two cases establishes
\mathcal{H}_{i}(x)=0
for all
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small.

We now turn our attention to the outer region
U_{out}
in (
6.15
). By definition,
\tilde{\eta}_{i}=0
and
\eta_{i}=0
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, hence using the support property (
6.16
) we directly have

\bigg(1-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{\eta}_{i}\bigg)\left[F^{\prime}\left(\Psi_{0}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\log\varepsilon||x|^{2}\right)\phi_{out}+S\left(\Psi_{0}\right)+N_{0}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}+\phi_{out}\Big)\right]=0.

∎

Corollary 6.1
.

A key outcome of Lemma
6.1
is that if
\phi_{out}
and
\phi_{in,i},\,i=1,\dots,N,
satisfy (
6.13
) and (
6.14
), then the Outer problem (
6.11
) reduces to

(6.17)

L_{x}[\phi_{out}]=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(L_{x}\left[\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}\right]-\tilde{\eta}_{i}L_{x}\left[\phi_{in,i}\right]\big)\quad\textnormal{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}.

As a consequence, provided that the right-hand side of (
6.17
) decays sufficiently fast at infinity, the problem falls within the class of equations treated in Proposition
7.2
, and therefore the existence of a corresponding solution
\phi_{out}(x)
follows directly.

To verify the decay property, in the variable

z_{i}=A_{i}^{-1}(x-P_{i}),\,z_{i}=\varepsilon\mu_{i}y_{i},

a direct substitution in (
2.4
) gives

(6.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(L_{x}\left[\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}\right]-\tilde{\eta}_{i}L_{x}\left[\phi_{in,i}\right]\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigg[2\nabla_{z_{i}}\tilde{\eta}_{i}\left(\frac{|\log\varepsilon|^{2}|z_{i}|}{\delta_{1}}\right)\cdot\nabla_{z_{i}}\phi_{in,i}(y_{i})+\phi_{in,i}(y_{i})\Delta_{z_{i}}\tilde{\eta}_{i}\left(\frac{|\log\varepsilon|^{2}|z_{i}|}{\delta_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle+B_{0}(z_{i})\left[\tilde{\eta}_{i}\left(\frac{|\log\varepsilon|^{2}|z_{i}|}{\delta_{1}}\right)\phi_{in,i}(y_{i})\right]-\tilde{\eta}_{i}\left(\frac{|\log\varepsilon|^{2}|z_{i}|}{\delta_{1}}\right)B_{0}(z_{i})\left[\phi_{in,i}(y_{i})\right]\Bigg].

For each
i\in\{1,\dots,N\},
one can deduce that the function
L_{x}[\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}]-\tilde{\eta}_{i}L_{x}[\phi_{in,i}]

is bounded and compactly supported in the transition region of the cut-off
\tilde{\eta}_{i}
, thus there exist
C>0
and
\bar{\nu}>2
(independent of
i
) such that

\big|L_{x}[\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}]-\tilde{\eta}_{i}L_{x}[\phi_{in,i}]\big|\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{\bar{\nu}}},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

In particular,

\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|L_{x}\left[\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}\right]-\tilde{\eta}_{i}L_{x}\left[\phi_{in,i}\right]\big|\leq C\varepsilon^{1+\sigma^{*}}

uniformly in
i=1,\dots,N
, for some
0<\sigma^{*}<\sigma
, with
\sigma>0
as in (
6.14
).

To illustrate the estimate above, using (
6.14
), (
6.3
) and the variable
z=A_{i}^{-1}(x-P_{i}),\,z=\varepsilon\mu_{i}y
, we have

\displaystyle\Bigg|\nabla_{z}\tilde{\eta}_{i}\left(\frac{|\log\varepsilon|^{2}|z|}{\delta_{1}}\right)\cdot\nabla_{z}\phi_{in,i}(y)\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}{\varepsilon\mu_{i}}\tilde{\eta}_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{|\log\varepsilon|^{2}|z|}{\delta_{1}}\right)\nabla_{y}\phi_{in,i}(y)

\displaystyle\leq o(1)\frac{\varepsilon^{1+\sigma^{*}}}{1+|x|^{\bar{\nu}}},

with
o(1)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0,\,\bar{\nu}>2
and some
0<\sigma^{*}<\sigma
, while the remaining terms in (
6.18
) can be handled in a similar manner.

As a result, we apply Proposition
7.2
to get the existence of a solution
\phi_{out}\in C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
\beta\in(0,1)
to equation (
6.17
), which satisfies

(6.19)

|\phi_{out}(x)|\leq C\varepsilon^{1+\sigma^{*}}\left(1+|x|^{2}\right),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Finally, since
\phi_{out}(x)
is unique up to a constant, we choose the one with the property

(6.20)

\phi_{out}(r_{0},0)=0.

7.
Inner and Outer Linear Theories

This section collects the linear solvability results and a priori estimates for the Inner and Outer problems introduced in Section
6
. These constitute the main analytic tools for solving the coupled system in (
6.8
) and (
6.11
). Although the linear theory for the Inner problem was established in
[
3
]
, we include a brief sketch of the proofs in the Appendix for the reader’s convenience.

7.1.
Linear Inner Theory

Let
h(y)
be a function satisfying the decay condition

(7.1)

|h(y)|\leq\frac{C}{1+|y|^{2+\sigma}},

for some
\sigma>0
. The current section considers the linear equation

(7.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{y}\phi+\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\phi+h(y)=0{\quad\hbox{in }}\,\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
\Gamma
is given in (
2.7
).

For
\sigma>0
as in (
7.1
) and
\beta\in(0,1)
, we employ the weighted norms

(7.3)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{2+\sigma}=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+|y|)^{2+\sigma}|h(y)|,

\displaystyle\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta}=

\displaystyle\|h\|_{2+\sigma}+\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+|y|)^{2+\sigma+\beta}[h]_{B_{1}(y),\beta},

where for any
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},
we use the standard Hölder seminorm notation

[h]_{A,\beta}=\sup_{\ell_{1}\neq\ell_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in A}\frac{|h(\ell_{1})-h(\ell_{2})|}{|\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}|^{\beta}}.

In
[
12
]
, the authors established that the linear operator
\Delta_{y}+\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}
in (
7.2
) is nondegenerate in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, in the sense that its kernel is generated by the bounded translation modes
Z_{i}(y)=\partial_{y_{i}}\Gamma(y),\ i=1,2
. On the other hand, if one enlarges the class of admissible solutions of (
7.2
) to allow logarithmic growth at infinity, then the kernel becomes three-dimensional, and it is spanned by

(7.4)

Z_{0}(y)=\frac{2}{\gamma-1}\Gamma(y)+y\cdot\nabla_{y}\Gamma(y),\quad Z_{i}(y)=\partial_{y_{i}}\Gamma(y),\ i=1,2.

Further details on the radial scaling mode
Z_{0}
can be found in
[
3
]
*Section 5.2. Here, we note that
Z_{0}(y)=\mathcal{O}\left(\log\left(2+|y|\right)\right)
and
Z_{i}(y)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{1+|y|}\right),\,i=1,2
.

The following lemma holds.

Lemma 7.1
.

Given
\sigma>0
and
\beta\in(0,1)
, consider the norms defined in (
7.3
) and the functions
Z_{i},\,i=0,1,2,
given by (
7.4
). For each
h
with
\|h\|_{2+\sigma}<+\infty
,
there exist a constant
C>0
and a solution
\phi=\mathcal{T}[h]
of equation (
7.2
), that defines a linear operator of
h
and satisfies

\displaystyle(1+|y|)|\nabla\phi(y)|+|\phi(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\,C\left[\,\log\left(2+|y|\right)\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}hZ_{0}\right|+(1+|y|)\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}hZ_{i}\right|+(1+|y|)^{-\sigma}\|h\|_{2+\sigma}\,\right].

Moreover, if

\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta}<+\infty
, it further holds

\displaystyle(1+|y|^{2+\beta})[D^{2}_{y}\phi]_{B_{1}(y),\beta}+\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)\left|D^{2}_{y}\phi(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\,C\left[\,\log\left(2+|y|\right)\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}hZ_{0}\right|+(1+|y|)\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}hZ_{i}\right|+(1+|y|)^{-\sigma}\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta}\,\right].

7.2.
Projected Linearised Inner Problem

In this section, we fix
\delta_{1}>0
sufficiently small, take scaling parameters
\mu_{i},\,i=1,\dots,N
as in Proposition
4.1
, and define

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{2\delta_{1}}{\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}.

For certain scalars
d_{ij},\,j=0,1,2,
and a fixed
i\in\{1,\dots,N\},
we consider the projected linearised problem

(7.5)

\Delta_{y}\phi_{i}+\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\phi_{i}+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i}]+h(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{2}d_{ij}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}Z_{j}\quad\mbox{in}\quad B_{\rho}.

For convenience, we recall that the definition of the functions
Z_{j},j=0,1,2
can be found in (
7.4
), while

(7.6)

\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i}]=b_{i}(y)\phi_{i}+\widetilde{B}_{0}[\phi_{i}],

with
b_{i}(y)=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\Gamma^{\gamma-2}_{+}\right)
and
\widetilde{B}_{0}=\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}B_{0}(\varepsilon\mu_{i}y)
; see (
2.4
) and (
6.7
).

Moreover, for
\sigma>0
,
\beta\in(0,1)
, and any open set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we modify the norms in (
7.3
) taking all suprema over
A
, namely

(7.7)

\|h\|_{2+\sigma,A}=\sup_{y\in A}\left(1+|y|\right)^{2+\sigma}|h(y)|,\quad\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,A}=\sup_{y\in A}\left(1+|y|\right)^{2+\sigma+\beta}[h]_{B_{1}(y)\cap A,\beta}+\|h\|_{2+\sigma,A},

while for
\phi_{i}\in C^{2,\beta}(A)
we define the norm

(7.8)

\|\phi_{i}\|_{*,\sigma,A}=\|D^{2}_{y}\phi_{i}\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,A}+\|D_{y}\phi_{i}\|_{1+\sigma,A}+\|\phi_{i}\|_{\sigma,A}.

With a slight abuse of notation, we will suppress the subscript in (
7.7
) and (
7.8
) when
A=\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

We establish the following result.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\sigma>0
,
\beta\in(0,1)
, and consider the norms defined in (
7.7
) and (
7.8
). Then, for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small and for every
h
satisfying
\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}}<+\infty
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any differential operator
\bar{B}_{i}
of the form (
7.6
) and
\rho=\frac{2\delta_{1}}{\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}
, the projected
problem (
7.5
) has a solution
\phi_{i}=T_{i}[h]
for certain scalars
d_{ij}[h],\,j=0,1,2
, which defines a linear operator of
h
and satisfies

\displaystyle\|\phi_{i}\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}\ \leq\ C\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}}.

Moreover, the coefficients
d_{ij}
admit the expansions

\displaystyle d_{i0}[h]\,=

\displaystyle\,\gamma_{0}\int_{B_{\rho}}hZ_{0}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log(2+\rho)}{1+\rho^{\sigma}}\right)\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}},

\displaystyle d_{ij}[h]\,=

\displaystyle\,\gamma_{j}\int_{B_{\rho}}hZ_{j}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\right)\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}},\ j=1,2,

where

\gamma_{j}^{-1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}Z_{j}^{2},\,j=0,1,2.

7.3.
Linear Outer Theory

We consider the differential operator
L_{x}
defined in (
2.2
), and study the elliptic equation

(7.9)

L_{x}[\psi]+g(x)=0{\quad\hbox{in }}\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
g(x)
is a bounded function satisfying the decay condition

\|g\|_{\bar{\nu}}\,:=\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(1+|x|^{\bar{\nu}}\right)|g(x)|<+\infty,

for some
\bar{\nu}>2
.

The following Proposition holds.

Proposition 7.2
.

There exists a solution
\psi(x)
to (
7.9
) of class
C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
\beta\in(0,1)
, which defines a linear operator
\psi={\mathcal{T}}^{o}(g)
of
g(x)

and satisfies the bound

|\psi(x)|\,\leq\,C\|g\|_{\bar{\nu}}(1+|x|^{2})\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

for a constant
C>0
.

Proof.

See
[
15
]
*Proposition 7.1.

8.
Solvability of the Inner-Outer System

With
\phi_{out}
already obtained in Section
6.1
as a solution of the Outer problem (
6.17
), we now aim to utilise the linear solvability theories developed in Section
7
to construct, for each
i=1,\dots,N
, a solution
\phi_{in,i}
for the Inner problem

\Delta_{y}\phi_{in,i}+\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\phi_{in,i}+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{in,i}]+H_{i}\left(\phi_{in},\phi_{out}\right)=0\quad\mbox{in}\,\,B_{\rho}.

We recall the notation
\phi_{in}=\left(\phi_{in,1},\dots,\phi_{in,N}\right)
,  and that the solution
\phi_{out}
satisfies (
6.19
) and (
6.20
). In addition,
\rho=\frac{2\delta_{1}}{\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}
, while the definitions of
\bar{B}_{i}
and
H_{i}(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})
appear in (
7.6
) and (
6.10
).

As a starting point, we decompose

\phi_{in,i}=\phi_{i,1}+\phi_{i,2},\quad i=1,\dots,N,

so that for the functions
Z_{j}
in (
7.4
) and scalars
d_{ij},j=0,1,2
as in Proposition
7.1
, the function
\phi_{i,1}
solves the auxiliary projected problem

(8.1)

\Delta_{y}\phi_{i,1}+\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\phi_{i,1}+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,1}]+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]+H_{i}(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})=\sum_{j=0}^{2}d_{ij}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}Z_{j}\quad\mbox{in}\quad B_{\rho},

whereas
\phi_{i,2}
is required to satisfy the linear equation

(8.2)

\Delta_{y}\phi_{i,2}+\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\phi_{i,2}+d_{i0}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}Z_{0}=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}.

In this setting, we recognise that the Inner–Outer system in (
6.8
) and (
6.17
) admits a solution of the form

(\phi_{in},\phi_{out}),\quad\phi_{in}=\left(\phi_{1,1}+\phi_{1,2},\dots,\phi_{N,1}+\phi_{N,2}\right),

as long as we further attain the coupled conditions

(8.3)

d_{ij}\left[H_{i}(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]\right]=0,\quad i=1,\dots,N,\,j=1,2.

We postpone the analysis of (
8.3
) to Section
9
, where we appropriately adjust the location of the vortex points
P_{1},\dots,P_{N}
in (
1.4
) to achieve their solvability.

We currently examine the equation for
\phi_{i,2}
in (
8.2
). Setting
y=re^{i\theta},r=|y|,
and denoting by
\xi_{0}
the unique root of
Z_{0}
in
(0,1),
a smooth radial solution of this equation is given by

(8.4)

\displaystyle\phi_{i,2}(r)

\displaystyle=-d_{i0}Z_{0}(r)\int_{\xi_{0}}^{r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}s}{sZ_{0}(s)^{2}}\int_{0}^{s}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}(\rho)Z_{0}^{2}(\rho)\rho\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho

\displaystyle\eqcolon d_{i0}\hat{\phi}_{2}(r).

Since the term

\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}
appearing in (
8.4
) is supported in
B_{1}(0)
, for any
s>1
it holds

\int_{0}^{s}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}(\rho)Z_{0}(\rho)^{2}\rho\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho=\int_{0}^{1}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}(\rho)Z_{0}(\rho)^{2}\rho\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho,

which in turn yields the logarithmic growth bounds

(8.5)

|\hat{\phi}_{2}(r)|\leq C\log(2+r),\quad|\phi_{i,2}(r)|\leq C|d_{i0}|\log(2+r),\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,r>0.

The Proposition below is the main result of this section.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
\sigma>0
and
\beta\in(0,1)
. Then, for all
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, there exist constants
C>0
and
\sigma^{*}\in(0,\sigma
), functions
\phi_{out},\,\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}=\left(\phi_{1,1},\dots,\phi_{N,1}\right),\mathbf{\Phi}_{2}=\left(\phi_{1,2},\dots,\phi_{N,2}\right)
and coefficients
d_{i0},\,i=1,\dots,N,
solving (
6.17
), (
8.1
) and (
8.2
), satisfying

(8.6)

\left\|(1+|x|^{2})^{-1}\phi_{out}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C\varepsilon^{1+\sigma^{*}},\quad\|\phi_{i,1}\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}\leq C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|,\quad|d_{i0}|\leq C(\varepsilon\mu_{i})^{1+\sigma}|\log\varepsilon|^{2+2\sigma},

for all
i=1,\dots,N
.

In the above,
\mu_{i}
denote the scaling parameters of Proposition
4.1
,
\rho=\frac{2\delta_{1}}{\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}
, and
\|\cdot\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}
is defined in (
7.8
).

Proof.

We recast the system as a fixed point problem of the form

(8.7)

\big(\mathbf{\Phi}_{1},(d_{10},\dots,d_{N0})\big)=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\big(\mathbf{\Phi}_{1},(d_{10},\dots,d_{N0})\big),

where, for each
i=1,\dots,N,

\displaystyle\phi_{i,1}

\displaystyle=T_{i}\left[H_{i}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{2},\phi_{out})+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]\right]\in\mathcal{X}_{*}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{\phi\in C^{2,\beta}(B_{\rho})\ :\|\phi\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}<+\infty\},

\displaystyle d_{i0}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(H_{i}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{2},\phi_{out})+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]\right)Z_{0},\quad\gamma_{0}^{-1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}Z_{0}^{2}.

For clarity, we recall that
T_{i}
is the linear operator of Proposition
7.1
,
\phi_{i,2}(y)=d_{i0}\hat{\phi}_{2}(y)
is given in (
8.4
), while Section
6.1
provides the outer solution
\phi_{out}=\phi_{out}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{2})
through Proposition
7.2
, satisfying (
6.19
) and (
6.20
). Moreover, the definitions of
H_{i}
and
\bar{B}_{i}
are introduced in (
7.6
) and (
6.10
).

By means of the Banach’s fixed point Theorem, we seek a solution in the ball

(8.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\Big\{\big(\mathbf{\Phi}_{1},\left(d_{10},\dots,d_{N0}\right)\big)\in\mathcal{X}^{N}_{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}:

\displaystyle\|\phi_{i,1}\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}\leq\widehat{C}\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|,

\displaystyle|d_{i0}|\leq\widehat{C}(\varepsilon\mu_{i})^{1+\sigma}|\log\varepsilon|^{2+2\sigma},\,i=1,\dots,N\Big\},

for some constant
\widehat{C}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
and
\sigma^{*}
, to be chosen later.

In this regard, we first need to show that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\left(\mathcal{B}\right)\subset\mathcal{B}
. Assuming
\big(\mathbf{\Phi}_{1},\left(d_{10},\dots,d_{N0}\right)\big)\in\mathcal{B},
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|H_{i}\left(\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{2},\phi_{out}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\Gamma^{\gamma-2}_{+}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(|\tilde{\eta}_{j}\phi_{j,1}|^{2}+|\tilde{\eta}_{j}\phi_{j,2}|^{2}\right)+|\phi_{out}|^{2}\bigg)+|\widetilde{E}_{i}|+\left(\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}+b_{i}(y)\right)|\phi_{out}|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\right)^{\gamma-1}_{+},

thus we directly get

\left\|H_{i}\left(\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{2},\phi_{out}\right)\right\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}}\leq C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|.

In addition, using (
8.5
) we find

\displaystyle\left|\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\mu_{i}\left(|y||D^{2}_{y}\phi_{i,2}|+|D_{y}\phi_{i,2}|\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\Gamma^{\gamma-2}_{+}\right)\phi_{i,2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|d_{i0}|}{2+|y|}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\Gamma^{\gamma-2}_{+}\right)|d_{i0}|\log(2+|y|),

so we further have

\left\|\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]\right\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}}\leq C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|.

Due to Proposition
7.1
, we then deduce

(8.9)

\displaystyle\left\|\phi_{i,1}\right\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}=\big\|T_{i}\left[H_{i}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{2},\phi_{out})+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]\right]\big\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}\leq C_{1}\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|,

for some
C_{1}>0
.

To proceed, we obtain the estimate

(8.10)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]Z_{0}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|d_{i0}|\int_{B_{\rho}}\frac{Z_{0}}{2+|y|}+\int_{B_{\rho}}\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\Gamma^{\gamma-2}_{+}\right)|d_{i0}|\log(2+|y|)Z_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{|d_{i0}|}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right),

and

(8.11)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}H_{i}\left(\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{2},\phi_{out}\right)Z_{0}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{\rho}}\Gamma^{\gamma-2}_{+}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\big(|\tilde{\eta}_{j}\phi_{j,1}|^{2}+|\tilde{\eta}_{j}\phi_{j,2}|^{2}\big)+|\phi_{out}|^{2}\bigg)Z_{0}+C\int_{B_{\rho}}\widetilde{E}_{i}Z_{0}

\displaystyle+C\int_{B_{\rho}}\left(\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}+\mathcal{O}\big(\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\Gamma^{\gamma-2}_{+}\big)\right)\phi_{out}Z_{0}.

At this stage, we recognise that

(8.12)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}\Gamma^{\gamma-2}_{+}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(|\tilde{\eta}_{j}\phi_{j,1}|^{2}+|\tilde{\eta}_{j}\phi_{j,2}|^{2}\right)+|\phi_{out}|^{2}\bigg)Z_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\phi_{j,1}\|^{2}_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}.

Nevertheless, even though
Z_{0}
is a radial function, recalling the form of
\widetilde{E}_{i}
from Proposition
5.1
, namely

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{i}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{i}\left(\frac{3R_{i}h^{2}+R_{i}^{3}}{2h\left(h^{2}+R_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\varepsilon\mu_{i}y_{1}\Gamma^{\gamma}_{+}+\kappa_{i}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\Bigg[\nu^{\prime}(1)\log\left(1+\frac{\mu^{*}_{i}}{\mu^{0}_{i}}\right)+\left(c_{1,i}\varepsilon\mu_{i}y_{1}+c_{2,i}\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}|y|^{2}\right)\Gamma(y)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\mu_{i}y_{1}|\log\varepsilon|\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{1,i}(P)+\varepsilon\mu_{i}y_{2}|\log\varepsilon|\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{2,i}(P)+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}|y|^{2}\right)\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}|y|^{2}\Gamma^{\gamma-2}_{+}\right),

for the remaining integrals in (
8.11
) we obtain

(8.13)

\int_{B{\rho}}\widetilde{E}_{i}Z_{0}=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{1+\tilde{\sigma}}\right),\quad\int_{B_{\rho}}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\phi_{out}Z_{0}=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{1+\sigma^{*}}\right),

with
\tilde{\sigma}>0
and
\sigma^{*}\in(0,\sigma)
as in (
4.6
) and (
6.19
), respectively.

As already alluded to in Remark
1.3
, the estimate for the second integral in (
8.13
) is a manifestation of the strong coupling between the Inner and Outer problems, since the integrals in (
8.12
) and (
8.13
) involving the inner perturbations
\mathbf{\Phi}_{1},\mathbf{\Phi}_{2}
and the outer correction
\phi_{out}
are of size

\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{1+\sigma^{*}}\right)\gg\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\varepsilon\mu_{i}\right)^{1+\sigma}|\log\varepsilon|^{2+2\sigma}\right)

for all small
\varepsilon>0.

To sufficiently decouple the system and ensure that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{B})\subset\mathcal{B}
, we now make an explicit choice of the perturbations
\mu^{*}_{i}
defining the scaling parameters
\mu_{i}=\mu_{i}^{0}+\mu_{i}^{*},\,i=1,\dots,N
in (
4.1
), and verify a posteriori the estimate in (
4.6
). We notice that in the bounded region

\mathcal{U}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|A_{i}^{-1}(x-P_{i})|<\frac{2\delta_{1}}{|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\Big\},

since
x-P_{i}=A_{i}\varepsilon\mu_{i}y
, we can use the expansion

(8.14)

\phi_{out}(x)=\phi_{out}(P_{i})+\mathcal{O}\big(\varepsilon\mu_{i}|A_{i}y|\|\nabla\phi_{out}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})}\big),

where (
6.19
),(
6.20
), and a standard elliptic estimate give

\|\nabla\phi_{out}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{1+\sigma^{*}}),

with
0<\sigma^{*}<\sigma
as in (
6.19
).

We then require

(8.15)

-\kappa_{i}\nu^{\prime}(1)\log\left(1+\frac{\mu_{i}^{*}}{\mu_{i}^{0}}\right)=\phi_{out}(P_{i}),\quad i=1,\dots,N,

which implies
-\kappa_{i}\nu^{\prime}(1)\log\left(1+\frac{\mu_{i}^{*}}{\mu_{i}^{0}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{1+\sigma^{*}}\right)
due to (
6.19
), so that a posteriori
\tilde{\sigma}=\sigma^{*}
in (
4.6
).

Making use of (
8.14
) and (
8.15
) in (
8.11
) we get

(8.16)

\int_{B_{\rho}}H_{i}\left(\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{2},\phi_{out}\right)Z_{0}=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}\right),

therefore combining (
8.10
) and (
8.16
), Proposition
7.1
yields

(8.17)

\displaystyle|d_{i0}|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{|d_{i0}|}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log(2+\rho)}{1+\rho^{\sigma}}\right)\left\|H_{i}\left(\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{2},\phi_{out}\right)+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]\right\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\varepsilon\mu_{i})^{1+\sigma}|\log\varepsilon|^{2+2\sigma},

for some
C_{2}>0.

Collecting (
8.9
) and (
8.17
), we conclude that if
\widehat{C}>0
in (
8.8
) is chosen sufficiently large (independently of
\varepsilon>0
and
0<\sigma^{*}<\sigma
), then
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{B})\subset\mathcal{B}
.

To complete the proof, it remains to establish that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
in (
8.7
) is a contraction mapping in
\mathcal{B}
. In order to do so, we define

\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{\ell}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{\eta}_{i}\left(\phi^{\ell}_{i,1}+\phi^{\ell}_{i,2}\right)+\phi^{\ell}_{out},\quad\mbox{with}\quad\phi_{out}^{\ell}=\phi_{out}[\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}^{\ell}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{2}^{\ell}],\quad\ell=1,2,

and then set

H_{i}^{\ell}=H_{i}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{\ell},\phi_{out}^{\ell}\right),\quad d^{\ell}_{i0}=\gamma_{0}\int_{R^{2}}\left(H_{i}^{\ell}+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi^{\ell}_{i,2}]\right)Z_{0},\quad\gamma_{0}^{-1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}Z_{0}^{2}.

Using the notation introduced above and assuming
\big(\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{\ell},\left(d_{10}^{\ell},\dots,d_{N0}^{\ell}\right)\big)\in\mathcal{B}
for
\ell=1,2,
we initially deduce from (
6.18
) that

(8.18)

\|\phi^{1}_{out}-\phi^{2}_{out}\|_{\infty}\leq C\bigg((\varepsilon\mu)^{\sigma}|\log\varepsilon|^{\zeta}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\phi^{1}_{j,1}-\phi^{2}_{j,1}\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}+|\log\varepsilon|^{\zeta}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|d_{j0}^{1}-d_{j0}^{2}|\bigg),

for some
\zeta>0
and
\mu
as in (
4.13
).

Furthermore, since

H_{i}^{1}-H_{i}^{2}=\mathcal{N}_{i}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{1}\right)-\mathcal{N}_{i}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{2}\right)+\left(\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}+b_{i}\right)\left(\phi_{out}^{1}-\phi_{out}^{2}\right),

we infer that

\displaystyle|H_{i}^{1}-H_{i}^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C\Gamma^{\gamma-2}_{+}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{\eta}_{j}\big(|\phi_{j,1}^{1}-\phi_{j,1}^{2}|^{2}+|\phi_{j,2}^{1}-\phi_{j,2}^{2}|^{2}\big)+|\phi^{1}_{out}-\phi^{2}_{out}|^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\left(\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}+b_{i}\right)|\phi^{1}_{out}-\phi^{2}_{out}|,

where we obtain the estimate

(8.19)

\displaystyle\|H_{i}^{1}-H_{i}^{2}\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\phi_{out}^{1}-\phi_{out}^{2}\|_{\infty}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\big(\|\phi^{1}_{j,1}-\phi^{2}_{j,1}\|^{2}_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}+|d^{1}_{j0}-d^{2}_{j0}|^{2}\big)+\|\phi_{out}^{1}-\phi_{out}^{2}\|^{2}_{\infty}\Big)

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\|\phi^{1}_{j,1}-\phi^{2}_{j,1}\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}+|d^{1}_{j0}-d^{2}_{j0}|\Big)+\|\phi_{out}^{1}-\phi_{out}^{2}\|_{\infty}\right).

Moreover, due to (
8.4
) and (
8.5
) we have

\bar{B}_{i}[\phi^{1}_{i,2}-\phi^{2}_{i,2}]=\mathcal{O}(|d^{1}_{i0}-d^{2}_{i0}|)\bar{B}_{i}[\hat{\phi}_{2}],\quad\mbox{where}\quad\bar{B}_{i}[\hat{\phi}_{2}]\leq\frac{C\varepsilon\mu_{i}}{2+|y|}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\Gamma^{\gamma-2}_{+}\right)\log(2+|y|),

hence we get

(8.20)

\left\|\bar{B}_{i}[\phi^{1}_{i,2}-\phi^{2}_{i,2}]\right\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}}\leq\frac{\mathcal{O}\left(|d_{i0}^{1}-d_{i0}^{2}|\right)}{(\varepsilon\mu_{i})^{\sigma}|\log\varepsilon|^{2+2\sigma}}.

In addition, Proposition
7.1
gives

(8.21)

\displaystyle|d^{1}_{i0}-d^{2}_{i0}|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log(2+\rho)}{1+\rho^{\sigma}}\right)\left\|H_{i}^{1}-H_{i}^{2}+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi^{1}_{i,2}-\phi^{2}_{i,2}]\right\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{|d_{i0}^{1}-d_{i0}^{2}|}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon\mu_{i})^{\sigma}|\log\varepsilon|^{1+2\sigma}\Bigg[\|\phi_{out}^{1}-\phi_{out}^{2}\|_{\infty}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\|\phi^{1}_{j,1}-\phi^{2}_{j,1}\|^{2}_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}+|d^{1}_{j0}-d^{2}_{j0}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\|\phi_{out}^{1}-\phi_{out}^{2}\|^{2}_{\infty}+\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\big(\|\phi^{1}_{j,1}-\phi^{2}_{j,1}\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}+|d^{1}_{j0}-d^{2}_{j0}|\big)+\|\phi_{out}^{1}-\phi_{out}^{2}\|_{\infty}\Big)\Bigg]

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{|d_{i0}^{1}-d_{i0}^{2}|}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right),

while using (
8.19
), (
8.20
), (
8.21
) and Proposition
7.1
once again, we find

(8.22)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{i,1}^{1}-\phi_{i,1}^{2}\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}=\big\|T_{i}\big[H_{i}^{1}-H_{i}^{2}+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}^{1}-\phi_{i,2}^{2}]\big]\big\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg[\|\phi_{out}^{1}-\phi_{out}^{2}\|^{2}_{\infty}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\|\phi^{1}_{j,1}-\phi^{2}_{j,1}\|^{2}_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}+|d^{1}_{j0}-d^{2}_{j0}|^{2}\Big)+\|\phi_{out}^{1}-\phi^{2}_{out}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\big(\|\phi^{1}_{j,1}-\phi^{2}_{j,1}\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}+|d^{1}_{j0}-d^{2}_{j0}|\big)+\|\phi_{out}^{1}-\phi^{2}_{out}\|_{\infty}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\big(\|\phi^{1}_{j,1}-\phi^{2}_{j,1}\|^{2}_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}+|d^{1}_{j0}-d^{2}_{j0}|^{2}\big)+\|\phi_{out}^{1}-\phi_{out}^{2}\|_{\infty}\Big)\Bigg].

Collecting together (
8.18
), (
8.21
) and (
8.22
), we conclude that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is a contraction mapping in
\mathcal{B}
for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, hence (
8.7
) possesses a fixed point.
∎

9.
Balancing conditions and adjustment of the vortex locations

In Section
8
, we have constructed a solution
(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})=(\phi_{in,1},\dots,\phi_{in,N},\phi_{out})
to the coupled system

\Delta_{y}\phi_{in,i}+\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\phi_{in,i}+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{in,i}]+H_{i}(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})=\sum_{j=1}^{2}d_{ij}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}Z_{j}\,{\quad\hbox{in }}\,\,B_{\rho},\quad i=1,\dots,N,

and

L_{x}[\phi_{out}]+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(L_{x}[\tilde{\eta}_{i}\phi_{in,i}]-\tilde{\eta}_{i}L_{x}[\phi_{in,i}]\right)=0\,{\quad\hbox{in }}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{2}.

For the reader’s convenience, we recall that
\rho=\frac{2\delta_{1}}{\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}
,
\bar{B}_{i}
and
H_{i}(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})
are defined in (
6.9
)-(
6.10
), while the kernel elements
Z_{j},\,j=1,2
are given in (
7.4
). Moreover, the solution
(\phi_{in,1},\dots,\phi_{in,N},\phi_{out})
is obtained in Proposition
8.1
using a fixed point argument, and the corresponding estimates can be found in (
8.6
).

To conclude the proof of Theorem
1
, it remains to obtain an exact solution to the coupled Inner-Outer system in (
6.8
) and (
6.17
), by solving

(9.1)

d_{ij}\left[H_{i}(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]\right]=0,\quad\mbox{for all}\quad i=1,\dots,N,\,j=1,2,

where

d_{ij}\left[H_{i}(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]\right]=\gamma_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(H_{i}(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]\right)Z_{j},\quad\gamma_{j}^{-1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}Z_{j}^{2}.

As will be demonstrated in the remainder of this section, satisfying these conditions requires a suitable choice of the points
P_{1},\dots,P_{N}
and
\tilde{r}
in (
1.4
), thereby identifying the correct vortex locations for an exact solution of (
1.1
).

To begin with, Proposition
7.1
together with (
4.13
) shows that
\|\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]+H_{i}(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}}\lesssim\varepsilon\mu|\log\varepsilon|,
hence

d_{ij}\left[H_{i}(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]\right]=\gamma_{j}\int_{B_{\rho}}\left(H_{i}(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]\right)Z_{j}\,{\mathrm{d}}y+\varepsilon^{1+\sigma}\mathcal{M}(P),

for
\sigma>0
as in (
8.6
).

Here and throughout this section,
\mathcal{M}(P)
denotes a smooth function of
P=(P_{1},\dots,P_{N})
satisfying (
1.4
), which is uniformly bounded as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Its expression may vary from line to line, and even within the same line.

Furthermore, since
H_{i}(\phi_{in},\phi_{out})=\mathcal{N}_{i}\Big(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{\eta}_{j}\phi_{in,j}+\phi_{out}\Big)+\left(\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}+b_{i}\right)\phi_{out}+\widetilde{E}_{i},
one can also write

\int_{B_{\rho}}\bigg[\mathcal{N}_{i}\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\tilde{\eta}_{\ell}\phi_{in,\ell}+\phi_{out}\Big)+\left(\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}+b_{i}\right)\phi_{out}+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i,2}]\bigg]Z_{j}=\varepsilon^{1+\sigma}\mathcal{M}(P),\quad j=1,2.

Consequently, the conditions in (
9.1
) amount to establishing

(9.2)

\int_{B_{\rho}}\widetilde{E}_{i}Z_{j}=\varepsilon^{1+\sigma}\mathcal{M}(P),\quad i=1,\dots,N,\,\,j=1,2.

In light of Proposition
5.1
and the expansion of the approximation error
\widetilde{E}_{i}
, the integral against
Z_{1}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}\widetilde{E}_{i}Z_{1}\,{\mathrm{d}}y=\varepsilon\mu_{i}\kappa_{i}\Bigg[

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3R_{i}h^{2}+R_{i}^{3}}{2h(h^{2}+R_{i}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\int_{B_{\rho}}y_{1}\Gamma^{\gamma}_{+}Z_{1}\,{\mathrm{d}}y+c_{1,i}\int_{B_{\rho}}y_{1}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\Gamma Z_{1}\,{\mathrm{d}}y

\displaystyle+\big(\mathcal{F}_{1,i}\left(P\right)+\log|\log\varepsilon|\mathcal{M}(P)\big)\int_{B_{\rho}}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}y_{1}Z_{1}\,{\mathrm{d}}y+\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}\mathcal{M}(P)\Bigg],

where

\mathcal{F}_{1,i}(P)=|\log\varepsilon|\left(-c_{1,i}\nu^{\prime}(1)-\frac{\alpha R_{i}h}{\kappa_{i}\sqrt{h^{2}+R_{i}^{2}}}\right)+\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\kappa_{j}}{\kappa_{i}}\nu^{\prime}(1)\frac{[A_{j}^{-1}(P_{i}-P_{j})]_{1}}{|A_{j}^{-1}(P_{i}-P_{j})|^{2}}.

Furthermore, upon testing with
Z_{2}
we obtain

\int_{B_{\rho}}\widetilde{E}_{i}Z_{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}y=\varepsilon\mu_{i}\kappa_{i}\Bigg[\mathcal{F}_{2,i}(P)\int_{B_{\rho}}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}y_{2}Z_{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}y+\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}\mathcal{M}(P)\bigg],

with

\mathcal{F}_{2,i}(P)=\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\kappa_{j}}{\kappa_{i}}\nu^{\prime}(1)\frac{[A_{j}^{-1}(P_{i}-P_{j})]_{2}}{|A_{j}^{-1}(P_{i}-P_{j})|^{2}}.

Using the form of the points
P_{1},\dots,P_{N}
in (
1.4
), namely

P_{i}=(r_{0}+\tilde{r},0)+\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\widehat{P}_{i},\qquad P_{i}=(a_{i},b_{i}),\qquad\widehat{P}_{i}=(\hat{a}_{i},\hat{b}_{i}),\qquad i=1,\dots,N,

and defining the rescaled points

\widetilde{P}_{i}=(\tilde{a}_{i},\tilde{b}_{i})=\left(\frac{\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{0}^{2}}}{h}\hat{a}_{i},\hat{b}_{i}\right),

a direct substitution in (
9.2
) together with (
4.7
) and the expansion in (
4.9
) yields the reduced problem

(9.3)

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{\nu^{\prime}(1)R_{i}h}{2(h^{2}+R_{i}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{\alpha R_{i}h}{\kappa_{i}\sqrt{h^{2}+R_{i}^{2}}}\right)+\sum_{j\neq i}\nu^{\prime}(1)\frac{\kappa_{j}}{\kappa_{i}}\frac{[(\widetilde{P}_{i}-\widetilde{P}_{j})]_{1}}{|(\widetilde{P}_{i}-\widetilde{P}_{j})|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{|\log\varepsilon|}\mathcal{M}(P),

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}\nu^{\prime}(1)\frac{\kappa_{j}}{\kappa_{i}}\frac{[(\widetilde{P}_{i}-\widetilde{P}_{j})]_{2}}{|(\widetilde{P}_{i}-\widetilde{P}_{j})|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\mathcal{M}(P).

We now reformulate the reduced system (
9.3
) in complex coordinates by identifying
\widetilde{P}_{j}=\left(\widetilde{P}_{j,1},\widetilde{P}_{j,2}\right)
with
\widetilde{P}_{j}=\widetilde{P}_{j,1}+i\widetilde{P}_{j,2},
which allows us to recast it as a perturbation of the limit problem (
1.9
), which reads

(9.4)

\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\kappa_{j}}{\widetilde{P}_{i}-\widetilde{P}_{j}}=\left(\frac{\kappa_{i}hr_{0}}{2(h^{2}+r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{\alpha hr_{0}}{\nu^{\prime}(1)\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{0}^{2}}}\right),\quad i=1,\dots,N.

With this in mind, our objective is to find a solution to (
9.3
) as a small perturbation of the solution of (
9.4
) by linearisation.

For
i=1,\dots,N,
we define

(9.5)

\mathbf{F}_{i}:\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathbb{C},\quad\mathbf{F}_{i}(\widetilde{P})=\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\kappa_{j}}{\widetilde{P}_{i}-\widetilde{P}_{j}},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad U_{i}(r)=\left(\frac{\kappa_{i}hr}{2(h^{2}+r^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{\alpha hr}{\nu^{\prime}(1)\sqrt{h^{2}+r^{2}}}\right).

Due to the specific choice of
\widetilde{P}^{b}
in (
1.9
), it follows that

\mathbf{F}_{i}\big(\widetilde{P}^{b}\big)=U_{i}(r_{0}),

while the derivative of
\mathbf{F}=\left(\mathbf{F}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{F}_{N}\right)
at
\widetilde{P}^{b}
is given by

(9.6)

{\mathrm{d}}\mathbf{F}_{\widetilde{P}^{b}}=\begin{pmatrix}-\sum\limits_{j\neq 1}\kappa_{j}T_{1j}&\kappa_{2}T_{12}&\cdots&\kappa_{N}T_{1N}\\
\kappa_{1}T_{21}&-\sum\limits_{j\neq 2}\kappa_{j}T_{2j}&\cdots&\kappa_{N}T_{2N}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\kappa_{1}T_{N1}&\kappa_{2}T_{N2}&\cdots&-\sum\limits_{j\neq N}\kappa_{j}T_{Nj}\end{pmatrix},\quad T_{ij}=T_{ji}=\frac{1}{\left(\widetilde{P}_{i}^{b}-\widetilde{P}_{j}^{b}\right)^{2}}.

At this point, we recognise that the vector
\hat{e}=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
lies in
\operatorname{Ker}\left({\mathrm{d}}\mathbf{F}_{\widetilde{P}^{b}}\right)
, while the nondegeneracy condition on the point
\widetilde{P}^{b}
is exactly the requirement that this kernel is one-dimensional, i.e.

\operatorname{Ker}\left(\rm{d}\mathbf{F}_{\widetilde{P}^{b}}\right)=\mbox{span}\{\hat{e}\}.

In order to obtain a solution of (
9.3
) as a small perturbation of
\widetilde{P}^{b},
we introduce
{\mathbf{q}}=({q}_{1},\dots,{q}_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and define
\tilde{\mathbf{q}}=(\tilde{q}_{1},\dots,\tilde{q}_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
by

\tilde{q}_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{0}^{2}}}{h}q_{j}\right)+i\operatorname{Im}(q_{j}),\quad j=1,\dots,N.

Accordingly, we decompose

\widetilde{P}_{j}=\widetilde{P}^{b}_{j}+\tilde{q}_{j}=\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{0}^{2}}}{h}(P_{j}^{b}+q_{j})\right)+i\operatorname{Im}\left(P_{j}^{b}+q_{j}\right),\quad j=1,\dots,N,

which in view of (
9.5
) allows us to rewrite the system in (
9.3
) as

(9.7)

\mathbf{F}_{i}(\widetilde{P})=U_{i}(R_{i})+\hat{\sigma}_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,N,

with
\operatorname{Re}(\hat{\sigma}_{i})=\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{|\log\varepsilon|}\mathcal{M}(P)
and
\operatorname{Im}(\hat{\sigma}_{i})=\frac{\mathcal{M}(P)}{|\log\varepsilon|}.

We next consider the expansions

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(\widetilde{P})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{F}(\widetilde{P}^{b})+{\mathrm{d}}\mathbf{F}_{\widetilde{P}^{b}}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})+\mathcal{O}(|\tilde{\mathbf{q}}|^{2}),

\displaystyle U_{i}(R_{i})

\displaystyle=U_{i}(r_{0})+U^{{}^{\prime}}_{i}(r_{0})\left(\tilde{r}+\frac{h}{\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{0}^{2}}}\frac{\operatorname{Re}\left(\widetilde{P}_{i}^{b}+\tilde{q}_{i}\right)}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\frac{|\tilde{\mathbf{q}}|}{|\log\varepsilon|}+|\tilde{r}|\right)^{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{|\tilde{r}|}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right),

while calculating the derivative
U_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(r_{0})
and substituting the fixed value of
\alpha
in (
1.10
), we deduce that (
9.7
) can be expressed as

(9.8)

{\mathrm{d}}\mathbf{F}_{\widetilde{P}^{b}}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})=\mathcal{J}\left(\tilde{r},\tilde{\mathbf{q}}\right)+\frac{\tilde{r}h}{2}\left(\frac{h^{2}-2r_{0}^{2}}{(h^{2}+r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}}(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{N})-\frac{h^{2}}{\left(h^{2}+r_{0}^{2}\right)^{\frac{5}{2}}}\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}^{2}}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}}(1,\dots,1)\right),

where
\mathcal{J}(\tilde{r},\tilde{\mathbf{q}})
is a smooth function satisfying

(9.9)

\mathcal{J}(0,0)=0,\quad\mathcal{J}_{\tilde{\mathbf{q}}}(0,0)=0,\quad D_{\tilde{r}}\mathcal{J}(0,0)=o(1)\to 0,\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

Moreover, in the admissible range of
\tilde{r}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{q}}
in (
1.4
), one finds
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{J}(\tilde{r},\tilde{\mathbf{q}})=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right)
and
\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{J}(\tilde{r},\tilde{\mathbf{q}})=\mathcal{O}\left(|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\right)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. In addition, since
{\mathrm{d}}\mathbf{F}_{\widetilde{P}^{b}}
has a one-dimensional kernel, then the same is valid for
\left({\mathrm{d}}\mathbf{F}_{\widetilde{P}^{b}}\right)^{T}
. In fact, since
T_{ij}=T_{ji}
in (
9.6
), it is easy to verify that
\operatorname{Ker}\big(({\mathrm{d}}\mathbf{F}_{\widetilde{P}^{b}})^{T}\big)=\mbox{span}\{\hat{\kappa}\}
, with

\hat{\kappa}=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}.

Now, we further note that the linear system in (
9.8
) is solvable if the right-hand side is orthogonal to
\hat{\kappa}.
Since its projection onto
\hat{\kappa}
is equal to

\left(\mathcal{J}(\tilde{r},\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\cdot\hat{\kappa}-\tilde{r}h\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{(h^{2}+r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}^{2}\right)\frac{\hat{\kappa}}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}^{2}},

we initially study the projected problem

(9.10)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}\mathbf{F}_{\widetilde{P}^{b}}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}\left(\tilde{r},\tilde{\mathbf{q}}\right)+\frac{\tilde{r}h}{2}\left(\frac{h^{2}-2r_{0}^{2}}{(h^{2}+r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}}(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{N})-\frac{h^{2}}{\left(h^{2}+r_{0}^{2}\right)^{\frac{5}{2}}}\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}^{2}}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}}(1,\dots,1)\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\mathcal{J}(\tilde{r},\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\cdot\hat{\kappa}-\tilde{r}h\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{(h^{2}+r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}^{2}\right)\frac{\hat{\kappa}}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}^{2}}.

Since
\widetilde{P}^{b}
is a nondegenerate solution, it follows from the properties in (
9.9
) and the Implicit Function Theorem that (
9.10
) admits a unique local solution of the form
\tilde{\mathbf{q}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\tilde{\mathbf{q}}(\tilde{r})
in a neighbourhood of
(\tilde{\mathbf{q}},\tilde{r})=(0,0)
. In particular, the estimates of
\mathcal{J}(\tilde{r},\tilde{\mathbf{q}})
imply that this solution satisfies

|\tilde{\mathbf{q}}(\tilde{r})|\lesssim\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{|\log\varepsilon|}.

Towards obtaining a genuine solution of (
9.7
), the next step is to find a suitable
\tilde{r}=\tilde{r}_{*}
so that

(9.11)

\tilde{r}\mapsto\mathcal{J}\left(\tilde{r},\tilde{\mathbf{q}}(\tilde{r})\right)\cdot\hat{\kappa}-\tilde{r}h\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{(h^{2}+r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}^{2}=0.

Using once more the property

\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{J}(\tilde{r},\tilde{\mathbf{q}})=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, Banach’s fixed point theorem provides a unique solution
\tilde{r}_{*}
for (
9.11
), satisfying

|\tilde{r}_{*}|\lesssim\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{|\log\varepsilon|}.

Collecting the aforementioned results, we deduce that the solution of (
9.7
) is given by

P_{i}=\left(r_{0}+\tilde{r}_{*}+\frac{h}{\sqrt{h^{2}+r_{0}^{2}}}\frac{\operatorname{Re}\left(\widetilde{P}_{i}^{b}+\tilde{q}_{i}(\tilde{r}_{*})\right)}{|\log\varepsilon|},\;\frac{\operatorname{Im}\big(\widetilde{P}_{i}^{b}+\tilde{q}_{i}(\tilde{r}_{*})\big)}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right)^{T},\quad i=1,\dots,N,

which satifies the desired estimates in (
1.11
). This concludes the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Lemma

Let
y=re^{i\theta},\,r=|y|

to obtain the ODEs

(10.1)

L_{k}[\phi_{k}]+h_{k}(r)=0,\quad r\in(0,\infty),\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},

with

L_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\partial_{rr}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}+\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}.

For

, we have that

satisfies

hence if we denote by

the unique root of

, the function

\phi_{0}(r)=-z_{0}(r)\int_{\xi_{0}}^{r}\frac{\rm ds}{sz_{0}(s)^{2}}\int_{0}^{s}h_{0}(\rho)z_{0}(\rho)\rho\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho

is a smooth solution of (
10.1
). Observing that
\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{0}(\rho)z_{0}(\rho)\rho\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}h(y)Z_{0}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y,

we deduce

|\phi_{0}(r)|\,\leq\,C\Big[\,\log(2+r)\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}h(y)Z_{0}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y\Big|\,+\,(1+r)^{-\sigma}\|h\|_{2+\sigma}\Big].

For
|k|=1
, one can verify that
z_{k}(r)=-\Gamma^{\prime}(r)
satisfies
L_{k}[z_{k}]=0
, thus a smooth solution reads

\phi_{k}(r)=\Gamma^{\prime}(r)\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}s}{s\Gamma^{\prime}(s)^{2}}\int_{0}^{s}h_{k}(\rho)\Gamma^{\prime}(\rho)\rho\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho,

with

|\phi_{k}(r)|\,\leq\,C\Big[\,(1+r)\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}h(y)Z_{i}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y\Big|\,+\,(1+r)^{-(1+\sigma)}\|h\|_{2+\sigma}\Big].

For
k=2
, there exists a function
z_{2}(r)
satisfying
L_{2}[z_{2}]=0
and
z_{2}(r)=\mathcal{O}(r^{2})
as
r\to 0
and
r\to\infty
, while for
|k|\geq 2
we note that

\widetilde{\phi}_{k}(r)=\frac{4}{k^{2}}z_{2}(r)\int_{0}^{r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}s}{sz_{2}(s)^{2}}\int_{0}^{s}|h_{k}(\rho)|z_{2}(\rho)\rho\,{\mathrm{d}}\rho

is a positive supersolution for equation (
10.1
). As a result, for
|k|\geq 2
there exists a unique solution
\phi_{k}
with
|\phi_{k}(r)|\leq\widetilde{\phi}_{k}(r)
, hence

|\phi_{k}(r)|\,\leq\,\frac{C}{k^{2}}(1+r)^{-\sigma}\|h\|_{2+\sigma},\quad|k|\geq 2.

Combining the above estimates for each mode, the function

\phi(y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi_{k}(r)e^{ik\theta}

is a solution of (
7.2
) and defines a linear operator of
h
, satisfying

(10.2)

\displaystyle|\phi(y)|\,\leq\,C\left[\,\log(2+|y|)\,\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}hZ_{0}\right|+(1+|y|)\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}hZ_{i}\right|+(1+|y|)^{-\sigma}\|h\|_{2+\sigma}\,\right].

Under the further assumption
\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta}<+\infty
, we next establish analogous estimates for first and second order derivatives of
\phi
. We let
R=|y|\gg 1
, fix the direction
\vec{e}=\frac{y}{|y|}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
so that
y=R\vec{e}
, and consider the rescaled functions

\phi_{R}(\ell)={R^{\sigma}}\phi\left(y+R\ell\right),\quad h_{R}(\ell)=R^{2+\sigma}h\left(y+R\ell\right).

In the neighbourhood
U_{y}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{y+R\ell:|\ell|<\frac{1}{2}\}
, one finds

\Delta_{\ell}\phi_{R}(\ell)+R^{2}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}(y+R\ell)\phi_{R}(\ell)+h_{R}(\ell)=0.

Setting

m_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}hZ_{i}\Big|,\quad i=0,1,2,

we use (
10.2
), the fact that
\|h_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}\big(B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)\big)}\leq C\|h\|_{2+\sigma},
and a standard elliptic estimate to find

(10.3)

\|\nabla_{\ell}\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}\big(B_{\frac{1}{4}}(0)\big)}+\|\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}\big(B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)\big)}\,\leq\,C\Big[m_{0}R^{\sigma}\log R+\sum_{i=1}^{2}m_{i}R^{1+\sigma}+\|h\|_{2+\sigma}\Big].

In addition, since
[h_{R}(\ell)]_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0),\beta}\leq C\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta}
, a combination of interior Schauder estimates and (
10.3
) gives

(10.4)

\|D^{2}_{\ell}\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}\big(B_{\frac{1}{4}}(0)\big)}+[D^{2}_{\ell}\phi_{R}]_{B_{\frac{1}{4}}(0),\beta}\,\leq\,C\Big[m_{0}R^{\sigma}\log R+\sum_{i=1}^{2}m_{i}R^{1+\sigma}+\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta}\Big].

Collecting together (
10.2
), (
10.3
) and (
10.4
), the result of the lemma follows.
∎

Proof of Proposition
7.2
.

As a preliminary step, we consider a standard linear extension operator
h\mapsto\tilde{h}
to the whole
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
so that the support of
\tilde{h}
is contained in
B_{2\rho}
and
\|\tilde{h}\|_{\sigma,\beta}\leq C\|h\|_{\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}}
, where
C>0
is independent of
\rho
. In addition,
we assume that the coefficients of
\bar{B}_{i}
belong in
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, with compact support in
B_{2\rho}
.
In this regard, we study the auxiliary projected problem in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by

(10.5)

\Delta_{y}\phi_{i}+\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\phi_{i}+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i}]+\tilde{h}(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{2}d_{ij}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}Z_{j}{\quad\hbox{in }}\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
d_{ij}=d_{ij}[h,\phi_{i}]
are the scalars defined as

(10.6)

d_{ij}=\gamma_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\tilde{h}(y)+\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i}])Z_{j},\quad\gamma_{j}^{-1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}Z_{j}^{2},\quad j=0,1,2.

For
j=1,2,
since
Z_{j}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{1+|y|}\right)
as
|y|\to\infty,
one finds

\bar{B}_{i}[Z_{j}]\leq C\varepsilon\mu_{i}\left(|y||D^{2}_{y}Z_{j}|+|D_{y}Z_{j}|\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\Gamma^{\gamma-2}_{+}\right)Z_{j}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|}{1+|y|^{2}},

thus we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i}]Z_{j}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi_{i}\bar{B}_{i}[Z_{j}]\leq C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\|\phi_{i}\|_{*,\sigma},\quad j=1,2,

where we used that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{1+|y|^{2+\sigma}}<+\infty
.

On the other hand, since
Z_{0}(y)=\mathcal{O}\left(\log\left(2+|y|\right)\right)
as
|y|\to\infty,
for
j=0
we instead have

\bar{B}_{i}[Z_{0}]\leq\frac{C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|}{1+|y|},

hence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i}]Z_{0}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\mu_{i}\int_{B_{2\rho}}\left(|y||D^{2}_{y}\phi_{i}|+|D_{y}\phi_{i}|\right)Z_{0}+\int_{B_{2\rho}}\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|\Gamma^{\gamma-2}_{+}\right)Z_{0}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\varepsilon\mu_{i}\right)^{\sigma}|\log\varepsilon|^{2\sigma-1}\|\phi_{i}\|_{*,\sigma}.

In addition, we observe that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{\rho}}\tilde{h}Z_{0}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log(2+\rho)}{1+\rho^{\sigma}}\right)\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{\rho}}\tilde{h}Z_{j}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{1+\rho^{1+\sigma}}\right)\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}},\,j=1,2,

and

\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i}]\leq\frac{C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|}{1+|y|^{1+\sigma}}\|\phi_{i}\|_{*,\sigma},

which further implies that

\big\|\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i}]\big\|_{2+\sigma,\beta}\leq\frac{C}{|\log\varepsilon|}\|\phi_{i}\|_{*,\sigma,B_{\rho}}.

To solve (
10.5
), we work in the Banach space
\mathcal{X}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{\phi_{i}\in C^{2,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\ :\|\phi_{i}\|_{*,\sigma}<+\infty\}
and use the linear operator
\mathcal{T}
of Lemma
7.1
to reformulate the equation as

(10.7)

\phi_{i}=\mathcal{A}[\phi_{i}]+\mathcal{H},\quad\phi_{i}\in\mathcal{X},

where

\mathcal{A}[\phi_{i}]=\mathcal{T}\Big[\bar{B}_{i}[\phi_{i}]-\sum_{j=0}^{2}d_{ij}[0,\phi_{i}]\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}Z_{j}\Big],\quad\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{T}\Big[\tilde{h}-\sum_{j=0}^{2}d_{ij}[\tilde{h},0]\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}Z_{j}\Big].

Employing (
10.6
), we obtain

\big\|\mathcal{A}[\phi_{i}]\big\|_{*,\sigma}\leq C\frac{\delta_{1}}{|\log\varepsilon|}\|\phi_{i}\|_{*,\sigma},\quad\|\mathcal{H}\|_{*,\sigma}\leq C\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}},

hence the Contraction Mapping Theorem in
\mathcal{X}
yields a unique solution to fixed point problem (
10.7
) for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, which defines a linear operator of
h
and satisfies

\|\phi_{i}\|_{*,\sigma}\ \leq\ C\|h\|_{2+\sigma,\beta,B_{\rho}}.

To this end, we set
T_{i}[h]=\phi_{i}\big|_{B_{\rho}}
to complete the proof.
∎
