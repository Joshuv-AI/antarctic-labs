In this paper,

Using equivariant Lagrangian correspondences, Lau–Leung–Li

For completely integrable systems arising in toric settings or from toric degenerations, one can apply gradient Hamiltonian flows associated with the components of the moment map to analyze stability. 4.
Disk potential functions and GIT quotients

The aim of this section is to investigate the relationship between the disk potentials of the prequotient and the GIT quotient.

4.1.
Disk potential functions

We begin by briefly recalling the relevant notions from Lagrangian Floer theory, focusing on the construction of the disk potential function. Let
\Lambda
be the Novikov field over
\mathbb{C}
defined by

(4.1)

\colon

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}T^{\lambda_{j}}\in\Lambda\,\bigg|\,a_{j}\in\mathbb{C},\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R},\lambda_{j}\geq 0,\lim_{j\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\infty}\lambda_{j}=\infty\right\}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}T^{\lambda_{j}}\in\Lambda\,\bigg|\,a_{j}\in\mathbb{C},\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R},\lambda_{j}>0,\lim_{j\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\infty}\lambda_{j}=\infty\right\}.\,

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact symplectic manifold of dimension

and let

be a Lagrangian torus. Fix a compatible almost complex structure

\mathrm{ev}_{j}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{k+1}(X,L,J,\beta)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}L,\quad[(\varphi,z_{0},\cdots,z_{k})]\mapsto\varphi(z_{j}).

\mathrm{ev}_{+}\coloneqq(\mathrm{ev}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{ev}_{k})

Applying the smooth correspondence construction of
[

\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{k+1}(X,L,J,\beta)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

4.2.
Stability for moduli spaces of holomorphic disks

We now return to the setting of Section

Definition 4.4

\varphi(\mathbb{D})\subseteq X^{ss}\mbox{ for all $\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(X,L,J,\beta)$}.

When
(X,L,J)
is clear from the context, we refer to
\beta
as semistable or unstable, according to the stability of its moduli space.

Let
\iota\colon X^{ss}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}X
be the inclusion. It induces a homomorphism

\iota_{*}\colon\pi_{2}(X^{ss},L)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\pi_{2}(X,L),\quad\alpha\mapsto\iota_{*}\alpha.

In general, this map is neither injective nor surjective. After compactification, additional homotopy classes whose representatives intersect the unstable locus
X^{us}
may appear. Moreover, distinct classes in
\pi_{2}(X^{ss},L)
may become homotopic in
X
. Nevertheless, if two homotopy classes coincide after compactification, their boundary classes must agree.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\pi_{2}(X^{ss},L)
.
If
\iota_{*}\alpha_{1}=\iota_{*}\alpha_{2}
, then
\partial\alpha_{1}=\partial\alpha_{2}
in
\pi_{1}(L)
.

Proof.

Since
\iota_{*}
induces an isomorphism on
\pi_{1}(L)
, we have

\iota_{*}(\partial\alpha_{1})=\partial(\iota_{*}\alpha_{1})=\partial(\iota_{*}\alpha_{2})=\iota_{*}(\partial\alpha_{2}).

∎

Since our primary interest lies in disk counting invariants, we introduce the sets

•

\Pi(X,L,J)\coloneqq\{\beta\in\pi_{2}(X,L)\mid\mathcal{M}(X,L,J,\beta)\neq\emptyset\}
,

•

\Pi^{(2)}(X,L,J)\coloneqq\{\beta\in\Pi(X,L,J)\mid\mu(\beta)=2\}
.

When the choice of
J
is fixed, we simply write
\Pi(X,L)\coloneqq\Pi(X,L,J)
and
\Pi^{(2)}(X,L)\coloneqq\Pi^{(2)}(X,L,J)
. Let
\beta\in\pi_{2}(X,L)
be a semistable homotopy class. For every holomorphic disk
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(X,L,J,\beta)
, there exists a holomorphic disk
\varphi^{\prime}
in
X^{ss}
such that
\varphi=\iota\circ\varphi^{\prime}
. We define

I^{ss}_{\beta}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in\Pi(X^{ss},L)\mid\iota_{*}\alpha=\beta\}.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(X^{ss},L,J,\alpha)
with
\iota_{*}\alpha=\beta
, the bundle pairs
((\iota\circ\varphi)^{*}TX,(\iota\circ\varphi)^{*}TL)
and
(\varphi^{*}TX^{ss},\varphi^{*}TL)
are canonically identified. It follows that

(1)

the Maslov index of
\alpha
is equal to that of
\iota_{*}\alpha
, and

(2)

if
\beta
is regular, then
\alpha
is also regular.

Assume furthermore that
L
is spin and equip
L
with the same spin structure in both
X
and
X^{ss}
. We then obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\beta\in\Pi^{(2)}(X,L)
be regular and semistable. Then the map

\iota_{*}\colon\coprod_{\alpha\in I^{ss}_{\beta}}\mathcal{M}(X^{ss},L,J,\alpha)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{M}(X,L,J,\beta),\quad[\varphi]\mapsto[\iota\circ\varphi].

is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism.

We obtain the following corollaries.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\beta\in\Pi^{(2)}(X,L)
be regular and semistable. Assume that
\mathcal{M}(X,L,J,\beta)=\overline{\mathcal{M}}(X,L,J,\beta)
. Then

n_{\beta}=\sum_{\alpha\in I^{ss}_{\beta}}n_{\alpha}.

Suppose moreover that the induced map
\iota_{*}\colon\Pi^{(2)}(X^{ss},L)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\Pi^{(2)}(X,L)
is injective. Then, for each
\beta\in\Pi^{(2)}(X,L)
, there exists a unique class
\alpha\in\Pi^{(2)}(X^{ss},L)
such that
\iota_{*}(\alpha)=\beta
. Equivalently,
\#(I^{ss}_{\beta})=1
.

Corollary 4.8
.

For
\alpha\in\Pi^{(2)}(X^{ss},L)
, suppose that
\iota_{*}\alpha
is regular and semistable. Assume that
\mathcal{M}(X,L,J,\beta)=\overline{\mathcal{M}}(X,L,J,\beta)
and

(4.8)

\iota_{*}\colon\Pi^{(2)}(X^{ss},L)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\Pi^{(2)}(X,L)

is injective. Then

n_{\alpha}=n_{\iota_{*}(\alpha)}.

Because of Proposition
4.6
, for a semistable class
\beta\in\pi_{2}(X,L)
, we introduce the notation

(4.9)

\mathcal{M}(X^{ss},L,J,\beta)\coloneqq\coprod_{\alpha\in I^{ss}_{\beta}}\mathcal{M}(X^{ss},L,J,\alpha).

4.3.
Semistable disk potential and GIT quotients

Our goal is to compare the disk potential of
L
in
X
with that of
K=L/G
in the GIT quotient
M\coloneqq X\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathbb{G}
. If a homotopy class
\beta\in\pi_{2}(X,L)
is unstable, then there exists a holomorphic disk
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(X,L,J,\beta)
whose image intersects the unstable locus
X^{us}
. Such a disk disappears upon restriction to the semistable locus
X^{ss}
and therefore does not descend to a holomorphic disk in
M
. These disks may cause compactness issues
\colon
even if
\mathcal{M}(X,L,J,\beta)
is compact, the corresponding moduli space in
X^{ss}
need not be compact.

To exclude this bad situation, we introduce the following notion.

Definition 4.9
.

A relative homotopy class
\beta\in\pi_{2}(X,L)
is called
fully excluded
if every holomorphic disk in
\mathcal{M}(X,L,J,\beta)
intersects the unstable locus, that is,

\varphi(\mathbb{D})\cap X^{us}\neq\emptyset\mbox{ for all $\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(X,L,J,\beta)$}.

From now on, we assume that
L
is torus,
G
is a torus, and the quotient
K\coloneqq L/G
is also a torus. Let
(\theta_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
be a basis of
H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})\simeq\pi_{1}(L)
and consider the induced homomorphism

q_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(L)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\pi_{1}(K).

Since the
G
-action is free, the long exact sequence of homotopy groups associated with the principal
G
-bundle implies that the kernel of
q_{*}
is a primitive sublattice. Since

\pi_{1}(L)\simeq\pi_{1}(K)\times\pi_{1}(G)

After replacing the basis
(\theta_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
if necessary, we may assume that

(1)

(\theta_{i})_{i={s+1}}^{m}
is a basis for
\mathrm{ker}(q_{*})
and

(2)

(\theta_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
projects to a basis for
\pi_{1}(K)
.

Let
(f_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
be the dual basis for
H^{1}(K;\mathbb{Z})
. Extending this basis, we obtain a dual basis
(e_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
of
H^{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})
satisfying
q^{*}f_{i}=e_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
. Let
\mathbf{y}=(y_{i})
(resp.
\mathbf{z}=(z_{i})
) be the variables corresponding to
\exp(e_{i})
for
L
(resp.
\exp(f_{i})
for
K
). Then the disk potential of
L
is expressed as a Laurent polynomial in the variables
\mathbf{y}
.

We now define the
semistable disk potential

W_{L}^{ss}
of
L
. Recall that
n_{\beta}
can be nonzero only if
\mu_{L}(\beta)=2
. We classify all semistable disk classes
\beta
with
\mu_{L}(\beta)=2
. We restrict attention to semistable disk classes of Maslov index two.

Definition 4.10
.

The
semistable disk potential

W_{L}^{ss}
of
L
is defined by

W_{L}^{ss}(\mathbf{y})\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\pi_{2}(X,L);\\
\beta\mbox{\scriptsize{ is semistable}}\end{subarray}}n_{\beta}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}^{\partial\beta\,\cap\,e_{i}}\cdot T^{\omega(\beta)/2\pi}.

Our choice of basis determines the relations

(4.10)

\begin{cases}z_{i}=y_{i}&\mbox{ for $i=1,\cdots,s$}\\
y_{i}=1&\mbox{ for $i=s+1,\cdots,m$.}\end{cases}

Substituting these relations into
W_{L}^{ss}
yields a Laurent polynomial in the variables
\mathbf{z}
, which we denote by

W_{L}^{ss}|_{\mathrm{ker}(q_{*})}(\mathbf{z}).

We state the main theorem of this section, which computes the disk potential of
K
.

Theorem 4.11

(Theorem
B
)
.

Let
G
be a torus and let
\mathbb{G}
denote its complexification. Suppose that
G
acts linearly on a complex vector space
V
. Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}(V)
be a
\mathbb{G}
-invariant projective variety. Let
\mu\colon X\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be a moment map of the Hamiltonian
G
-action on
X
. Let
L
be an oriented
G
-invariant Lagrangian torus of
X
contained in
\mu^{-1}(0)
. Assume that

(1)

G
acts freely on the level set
\mu^{-1}(0)
(and hence on
L
),

(2)

L
is positive and is endowed with the product spin structure induced by the spin structure on
L/G
,

(3)

J
is regular and every unstable homotopy class
\beta\in\Pi^{(2)}(X,L)
is fully excluded,

(4)

\mathrm{ev}_{0}\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}(X,L,J,\beta)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}L
is submersive for every
\beta\in\Pi^{(2)}(X^{ss},L)
,

(5)

both

and
X\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G\simeq X\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathbb{G}
are symplectically Fano.

Then the disk potential of
{L/G}
is equal to
W_{L}^{ss}|_{\mathrm{ker}(q_{*})}(\mathbf{z})
, that is,

W_{L/G}(\mathbf{z})=W_{L}^{ss}|_{\mathrm{ker}(q_{*})}(\mathbf{z}).

Before proving Theorem
4.11
, we collect several auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 4.12
.

The Lagrangian
L
is positive in
X^{ss}
if and only if
K=L/G
is also positive in

.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a nonconstant holomorphic disk in
M
with boundary on
K
and Maslov index
\leq 0
. By Proposition
3.8
, such a disk admits a holomorphic lift to

and by Lemma
3.3
, the lifted disk has the same Maslov index. This contradicts the positivity of

in

. Hence

is positive. The converse immediately follows from Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Lemma 4.13
.

The evaluation map
\mathrm{ev}_{0}\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}(P_{\mathbb{G}},L,J,\beta)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}L
is submersive if and only if
\mathrm{ev}_{0}\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}(M,K,J,\beta)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}K
is submersive.

Proof.

By construction,
T_{p}L\simeq T_{q(p)}K\oplus\mathfrak{g}
. The claim therefore follows from the commutative diagrams (

) and (

).
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.11
.

Let
\beta\in\Pi^{(2)}(X^{ss},L)
. Since
L
is positive and
J
is regular, we have

\mathcal{M}_{1}(X,L,J,\iota_{*}\beta)=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1}(X,L,J,\iota_{*}\beta)

and this moduli space is a compact manifold without boundary of dimension
L
. By Proposition
4.6
and (
4.9
), the inclusion map
\iota\colon X^{ss}\hookrightarrow X
induces an orientation preserving diffeomorphism between
\mathcal{M}_{1}(X^{ss},L,J,\iota_{*}\beta)
and
\mathcal{M}_{1}(X,L,J,\iota_{*}\beta)
.

The Lagrangian torus
L
is positive in
X^{ss}
. If there existed a nonconstant holomorphic disk in
X^{ss}
with Maslov index
\leq 0
, then its image under
\iota
would define a holomorphic disk in
X
with the same Maslov index, contradicting the positivity of
L
in
X
. By Lemma
4.12
, the quotient torus
K=L/G
is also positive. Therefore the disk potential of
K
is well-defined. By Lemma
3.2
and (
4.7
), it takes the form

(4.11)

W_{L/G}(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{\beta\in\pi_{2}(X^{ss},L)}n_{q_{*}\beta}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{s}z_{i}^{\partial(q_{*}\beta)\,\cap\,f_{i}}\cdot T^{\omega(q_{*}\beta)/2\pi}.

Since
M
is symplectically Fano, sphere bubbling does not contribute to
W_{L/G}
. Hence Theorem
3.13
, Corollary
3.14
, and Lemma
4.13
imply that

(4.12)

n_{\beta}=n_{q_{*}\beta}.

Moreover, by Lemma
3.3
and the definition of the reduced symplectic form,

(4.13)

\mu(q_{*}\beta)=\mu(\beta)\mbox{ and }\omega(q_{*}\beta)=\omega(\beta).

Since
q^{*}\colon\mathbb{Z}\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{s}\rangle\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{Z}\langle e_{1},\cdots,e_{s}\rangle
is isomorphic, we have

\partial(q_{*}\beta)\cap f_{i}=q_{*}(\partial\beta\cap q^{*}f_{i})=q_{*}(\partial\beta\cap e_{i})\,\,\mbox{ for $i=1,\cdots,s$.}

Hence

(4.14)

\prod_{i=1}^{s}z_{i}^{\partial(q_{*}\beta)\,\cap\,f_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}z_{i}^{q_{*}(\partial\beta\,\cap\,e_{i})}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}^{\partial\beta\,\cap\,e_{i}}|_{{}_{\mathrm{ker}(q_{*})}}.

Let
\beta\in\pi_{2}(X^{ss},L)
. Since every unstable effective homotopy class is fully excluded, the class
\iota_{*}\beta
is semistable. By Lemma
4.5
, the boundary classes agree whenever two relative classes become identified in
X
. Furthermore, Corollary
4.7
shows that the corresponding disk counting invariants also agree. Consequently, neither the monomials nor the coefficients change after passing from
X^{ss}
to
X
. Combining (
4.12
), (
4.13
), and (
4.14
), we obtain

W_{L/G}(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{\beta}n_{\beta}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}^{\partial\beta\,\cap\,e_{i}}|_{\mathrm{ker}(q_{*})}\cdot T^{\omega(\beta)/2\pi}=W_{L}^{ss}|_{\mathrm{ker}(q_{*})}(\mathbf{z}),

where the summation is taken over all semistable disk classes
\beta\in\pi_{2}(X,L)
of Maslov index two.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

If
X
is not symplectically Fano, then Theorem
4.11
only determines the leading-order contribution to the disk potential. In general, a holomorphic disk with sphere bubbles may contributes to the disk potential.

Moreover, the semistable locus depends on the choice of moment-map level set, and hence so does the corresponding symplectic reduction. As a consequence, the semistable disk potential and the disk potential of the quotient may vary with the choice of reduction.

Remark 5.4

A level

3.14
to recover the disk potential of the total space from that of the quotient. The following elementary example illustrates this “going-up” procedure.

Example 5.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
and consider the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-n)
over the projective line
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
. Its total space is a noncompact toric manifold whose moment polytope is

\left\{\textbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid u_{1}\geq 0,u_{2}\geq 0,nu_{2}-u_{1}+1\geq 0\right\}.

Let
\mu_{T^{2}}
denote the moment map. The second component
\mu_{S^{1}}\coloneqq\mathrm{pr}_{2}\circ\mu_{T^{2}}
generates the fiberwise
S^{1}
-action on
\mathcal{O}(-n)
. Moreover, the complement of the zero section
o_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}
is a holomorphic principal
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-bundle over
\mathbb{C}P^{1}

q\colon P=\mathcal{O}(-n)\backslash{o}_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{C}P^{1}.

Fix
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. The
S^{1}
-action is free on the level set
\mu_{S^{1}}^{-1}(\nu)
, which is diffeomorphic to a lens space. The corresponding symplectic reduction is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
equipped with a multiple of the Fubini–Study form. Let
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
with
u_{2}=\nu
. We denote by
L(\mathbf{u})
the toric Lagrangian fiber over
\mathbf{u}
. Then the disk potential of the quotient Lagrangian
L(\mathbf{u})/S^{1}
is

W_{L(\mathbf{u})/S^{1}}(\mathbf{z})=zT^{u}+\frac{1}{z}T^{1+n\nu-u}.

We explain how to compute the disk potential of
W_{L(\mathbf{u})}
from
W_{L(\mathbf{u})/S^{1}}
. To apply Corollary
3.14
, we first verify that

\mathcal{M}_{1}(P,L(\mathbf{u}),J,\beta)=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1}(P,L(\mathbf{u}),J,\beta).

Since
L(\mathbf{u})/S^{1}
is positive,
L(\mathbf{u})
is also positive by Lemma
4.12
. Let
\beta\in\Pi^{(2)}(P,L(\mathbf{u}))
. Since
q_{*}\beta\in\Pi^{(2)}(\mathbb{C}P^{1},L(\mathbf{u})/S^{1})
is regular, Lemma
3.3
shows that
\beta\in\Pi^{(2)}(P,L(\mathbf{u}))
is also regular. Hence, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{1}(P,L(\mathbf{u}),J,\beta)
is a smooth manifold without boundary.

A priori, however,
\mathcal{M}_{1}(P,L(\mathbf{u}),J,\beta)
need not be compact since a sequence of holomorphic disks may escape to infinity. Nevertheless, by Corollary
3.11
and Lemma
3.12
,

\phi\colon\mathcal{M}_{1}(P,L(\mathbf{u}),J,\beta)/S^{1}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{C}P^{1},L(\mathbf{u})/S^{1},q_{*}J,q_{*}\beta)

is a smooth bijection between smooth manifolds with the same dimension. By the invariance of domain,
\phi
is a homeomorphism. Since the moduli space on the right-hand side is compact, it follows that
\mathcal{M}_{1}(P,L(\mathbf{u}),J,\beta)
is also compact. By Corollary
3.14
, the corresponding counting invariants agree.

The remaining task is to understand how the Laurent monomials change under the lifting procedure. Let
\beta
be the relative homotopy class of a holomorphic disk corresponding to the first term. Then the second term corresponds to the class
[S^{2}]-\beta
. Via the isomorphism
q_{*}
in Lemma
3.2
, these classes lift to relative classes
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\pi_{2}(P,L(\mathbf{u}))
. However, a spherical class in the reduced space does not necessarily lift to a spherical class in
P
. In particular,

\partial\beta_{1}+\partial\beta_{2}\neq 0.

To measure this discrepancy, consider the associated bundle defined by the standard representation, which again yields
\mathcal{O}(-n)
. Let
\beta_{0}\in\pi_{2}(\mathcal{O}(-n),L(\mathbf{u}))
be the class represented by a fiberwise holomorphic disk intersecting the zero section
o_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}
once. By adding a suitable multiple of
\beta_{0}
so that the boundary vanishes, we obtain a spherical class

\alpha\coloneqq\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+a\beta_{0}\in\pi_{2}(\mathcal{O}(-n)).

Since
\alpha
is homotopic to the zero section
o_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}
and
\langle c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(-n)),\alpha\rangle=-n
, it follows that
a=-n
. Consequently,

\partial\beta_{2}=-\partial\beta_{1}+n\partial\beta_{0}

and hence the disk potential of
L(\mathbf{u})
in the total space
P
is

W_{L(\mathbf{u})}(\mathbf{y})=y_{1}T^{u}+\frac{y_{2}^{n}}{y_{1}}T^{1+n\nu-u}.

Remark 5.6
.

As another direction of generalization, consider the base affine space
G/U
where
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
U
is the unipotent radical of a Borel subgroup
B
. The natural projection
\pi\colon G/U\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}G/B
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}(-1)\backslash o_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{C}P^{1}
. In particular, the disk potential computed via our lifting procedure agrees with the Gross–Hacking–Keel–Kontsevich (GHKK) superpotential. which is expressed as a sum of
\theta
-functions. In forthcoming work
[
Kim
]
, we will develop this lifting procedure systematically and recover the GHKK superpotential for the basic affine spaces of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
through holomorphic disk counting.

6.
Disk potential of quadric hypersurfaces

In
[
Kim23
]
, the disk potential of a monotone Lagrangian torus fiber in the Gelfand–Zeitlin (GZ) system of a quadric hypersurface was computed. In this section, we extend
[
Kim23
, Theorem 4.1]
to arbitrary torus fibers, without assuming monotonicity, by applying the techniques developed in this paper together with a degeneration method.

We begin by fixing notation. Let
\mathbf{x}\coloneqq[x_{0}:\dots:x_{n+1}]
be the homogeneous coordinates on
\mathbb{C}P^{n+1}
and consider the quadric hypersurface

\mathcal{Q}^{n}\coloneqq\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{C}P^{n+1}\mid x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x^{2}_{n+1}=0\right\},

equipped with the Kähler form induced by a suitable multiple of the Fubini–Study form on
\mathbb{C}P^{n+1}
.

The quadric
\mathcal{Q}^{n}
carries a completely integrable system, called the
Gelfand–Zeitlin system
, constructed in
[
Thi81
,
GS83
]
. Setting
\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}|x_{j}|^{2}
, we define

•

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\frac{n\sqrt{-1}(x_{0}\overline{x}_{1}-\overline{x}_{0}x_{1})}{\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}}
,

•

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\sqrt{\left(\frac{n\sum_{i=0}^{k}|x_{i}|^{2}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}}\right)^{2}-\left|\frac{n\sum_{i=0}^{k}x_{i}^{2}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}}\right|^{2}}\quad\mbox{for $k=2,\dots,n$}.

The GZ system is given by

\Phi_{\mathrm{GZ}}=(\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{n})\colon\mathcal{Q}^{n}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Its image is the polytope

\Delta^{n}\coloneqq\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid n\geq u_{n}\geq u_{n-1}\geq\dots\geq u_{2}\geq|u_{1}|\right\},

which is a an
n
-simplex. Setting
u_{n+1}\coloneqq n
, we define

\begin{cases}f_{0}\coloneqq\{\mathbf{u}\in\Delta^{n}\mid u_{2}+u_{1}=0\}\\
f_{j}\coloneqq\{\mathbf{u}\in\Delta^{n}\mid u_{j+1}-u_{j}=0\}\quad\mbox{for $j=1,\dots,n$.}\end{cases}

Then
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
are precisely the facets of
\Delta^{n}
.

We now recall the toric degeneration of the GZ system constructed by Nishinou–Nohara–Ueda
[
NNU08
,
NNU10
]
. Consider the following degeneration in stages
\colon

\mathcal{X}\coloneqq\{(\mathbf{x},\textbf{t})\in\mathbb{C}P^{n+1}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\mid x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+t_{3}x_{3}^{2}+\dots+t_{n+1}x^{2}_{n+1}=0\},

where
\textbf{t}=(t_{3},\dots,t_{n+1})
. Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
be the projection and we denote by
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{t}}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(\mathbf{t})
the fiber over
\mathbf{t}
.

For each
k=3,4,\dots,n+1
, define

\mathcal{X}_{(k,t_{k})}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(1,\dots,1,t_{k},0,\dots,0)=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{C}P^{n+1}\mid x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{k-1}^{2}+t_{k}x^{2}_{k}=0\right\}.

We also introduce functions
\Psi_{1},\dots,\Psi_{n}
by

•

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\Phi_{1}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{n\sqrt{-1}(x_{0}\overline{x}_{1}-\overline{x}_{0}x_{1})}{\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}}
,

•

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\frac{n\sum_{i=0}^{k}|x_{i}|^{2}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}}\quad\mbox{for $k=2,\dots,n$}.

Each
\mathcal{X}_{(k,t_{k})}
carries a completely integrable system

(6.1)

\Phi_{(k,t_{k})}\coloneqq(\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{k-1},\Psi_{k},\dots,\Psi_{n})\colon\mathcal{X}_{(k,t_{k})}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By construction,
\Phi_{(n+1,1)}=\Phi_{\mathrm{GZ}}
. On the central fiber
\mathcal{X}_{(3,0)}
, we have

and hence

\Phi_{(3,0)}=(\Psi_{1},\dots,\Psi_{n})\eqqcolon\Psi_{\mathrm{GZ}}

which coincides with the toric moment map on

Both

and

To interpolate between these systems, consider a piecewise linear path

to obtain a completely integrable system over the point

. Taking the limit as

, we obtain a family of completely integrable systems along the line segment
(\delta^{n-1},\dots,\delta^{n-1})\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}(0,\dots,0)
, which converges to the toric moment map

We write

for

We now determine the coefficient of the monomial corresponding to each vertex in (

in

for sufficiently small

corresponds to a holomorphic disk intersecting a toric divisor and the corresponding open Gromov–Witten invariant is equal to one, see
[

It remains to determine whether there exists an additional contribution from holomorphic disks corresponding to the lattice point

For this purpose, we establish the following lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume that
n\geq 3
. Let
\beta
be a relative homotopy class realized by a Maslov index two holomorphic disk whose boundary class corresponds to the lattice point
(0,1,0,\dots,0)
. Then, for all sufficiently small

satisfies

\varphi(\mathbb{D})\cap\left(\bigl(V(x_{0})\cap\dots\cap V(x_{3})\bigr)\cup V(x_{4}x_{5}\dots x_{n+1})\right)=\emptyset.

Proof.

Let
\beta_{0},\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n}
be the basic disk classes corresponding to the vertices in (
6.5
), respectively. Recall that for
n\geq 3
, the induced homomorphism

\psi_{t,*}\colon H_{2}(X_{t},L(\mathbf{u});\mathbb{Q})\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}H_{2}(X_{0},L(\mathbf{u});\mathbb{Q})

is an isomorphism that preserves the Maslov index and the symplectic area, see
[
Kim23

\beta_{j}
the classes
\psi_{t,*}^{-1}(\beta_{j})

Since
\{\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{n}\}
forms a basis for

for

, we may write

\beta=\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}\beta_{j}\quad\mbox{ $c_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}$}.

The conditions that

corresponds to the lattice point
(0,1,0,\dots,0)
via (

c_{0}=c_{1},\,\,c_{2}=\dots=c_{n},\,\,c_{0}+c_{1}-c_{2}=1,\mbox{ and }\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}=1.

It follows that
c_{0}=c_{1}=1/2
and
c_{3}=\dots=c_{n}=0
and hence
\beta=(\beta_{0}+\beta_{1})/2
.

Consider a sequence of holomorphic disks in
X_{t}
representing
\beta
as
t\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}0

. By the classification of Cho–Poddar

, any such disk has minimal symplectic area among classes with boundary

. This completes the proof.
∎

y_{1}
and
y_{2}
survive and all other
y_{\bullet}
becomes
1
under the quotient map
q_{*}
.

Recall that there are two homotopy classes
{\alpha}
and
\alpha^{\prime}
of Maslov index two whose boundary corresponds to
(0,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Let
\widetilde{\alpha}
and
\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime}
are unique homotopy classes in
\pi_{2}(\mathcal{X}_{(3,t_{3})},L(\mathbf{u}))
such that
q_{*}(\widetilde{\alpha})=\alpha
and
q_{*}(\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime})=\alpha^{\prime}
. By Lemma
3.3
and Corollary
4.7
,
\widetilde{\alpha}
and
\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime}
are also regular and the sum of their counting invariants is equal to
2
, that is,

n_{\widetilde{\alpha}}+n_{\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime}}=n_{\alpha}+n_{\alpha^{\prime}}=2.

Moreover,
\partial\widetilde{\alpha}
corresponds to
(0,1,0,\dots,0)
because the disk potential must have the form (
6.6
). By Lemma
6.2
, every holomorphic disk whose boundary class corresponding to
(0,1,0,\dots,0)
lies in the semistable locus. Therefore,

(6.12)

\mathcal{M}_{1}(X^{\prime}_{0},L(\mathbf{u}),J,\iota_{*}\widetilde{\alpha})\cup\mathcal{M}_{1}(X^{\prime}_{0},L(\mathbf{u}),J,\iota_{*}\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime})

are the only moduli spaces of holomorphic disks of Maslov index two that contribute to
y_{2}T^{u_{2}}
. Hence, its coefficient is exactly two. Since the moduli spaces in (
6.12
) are regular, the corresponding counting invariant remains the same for sufficiently small
t
. Hence, the disk potential of
L(\mathbf{u})
in
X^{\prime}_{t}
is of the form (
6.7
).

We have determined the disk potential of
L(\mathbf{u})
for
\mathbf{u}\in\mu_{G}^{-1}(2,\dots,n-1)
. It remains to compute the disk potential of
L(\mathbf{u})
with arbitrary
\mathbf{u}\in\mathrm{Int}(\Delta_{\mathrm{GZ}})
. For
\varphi
in (
6.12
), consider the composition

\eta\circ\varphi\colon(\mathbb{D},\partial\mathbb{D})\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}(X^{\prime}_{0},L(\mathbf{u}))\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}((\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n-2},(S^{1})^{n-2})

where

\eta([x_{0}:x_{1}:\dots:x_{n+1}])\mapsto\left(\frac{x_{3}}{x_{2}},\dots,\frac{x_{n+1}}{x_{2}}\right).

The variety
X_{0}^{\prime}
carries a Hamiltonian
T^{n-2}
-action generated by
(\Psi_{4}-\Psi_{3},\dots,\Psi_{n}-\Psi_{n-1},-\Psi_{n})
and
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n-2}
carries the standard torus action. The map
\eta
is
T^{n-2}
-equivariant with respect to these actions. By Lemma
6.2
,
\varphi
has neither zeros nor poles along the divisor
V(x_{2}\dots x_{n+1})
. Thus
\eta\circ\varphi
is a well-defined holomorphic map and it must be constant by the maximum modulus principle. Consequently, we obtain an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism

\mathcal{M}_{1}(X^{\prime}_{0},L(\mathbf{u}),J,\beta)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathcal{Q}^{2},L(\mathbf{v}),q_{*}J,q_{*}\beta)\times T^{n-2},\quad[(\varphi,z_{0})]\mapsto\bigl([(q\circ\varphi,z_{0})],\eta\circ\varphi\bigr)).

Now consider another level
(\nu_{3},\ldots,\nu_{n})
with
\nu_{3}\leq\cdots\leq\nu_{n}
and choose

By the same argument, we obtain an isomorphism

\mathcal{M}_{1}(X^{\prime}_{0},L(\mathbf{u}^{\prime}),J,\beta)\simeq\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathcal{Q}^{2},L(\mathbf{v}),q_{*}J,q_{*}\beta)\times\widetilde{T}^{n-2},\quad[(\varphi,z_{0})]\mapsto\bigl([(q\circ\varphi,z_{0})],\eta\circ\varphi\bigr)).

. In particular, the corresponding counting invariants agree. Therefore, the disk potential of

Remark 6.4
.

Although
\pi_{2}(\mathcal{Q}^{3},{L}(\mathbf{u}))\simeq\pi_{2}(X^{\prime}_{0},L(\mathbf{u}))
are isomorphic, there is a birth-and-death phenomenon of homotopy classes under compactification or removal of unstable locus.

On one hand,
\pi_{2}(\mathcal{Q}^{3},{L}(\mathbf{u}))\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{4}
is generated by the basic disk classes. Among them, the basic disk class corresponding to the term
1/y_{3}T^{3-u_{3}}
is unstable. The summand generated by this class disappears after passing to the semistable locus.

On the other hand,
\pi_{2}(X^{\prime}_{0},L(\mathbf{u}))\simeq\pi_{2}(\mathcal{Q}^{2},{L}(\mathbf{v}))\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{4}
. The disk classes
{\alpha}
and
{\alpha}^{\prime}
define distinct elements in
\pi_{2}(\mathcal{Q}^{2},{L}(\mathbf{v}))
and similarly
\widetilde{\alpha}
and
\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime}
define distinct elements in
\pi_{2}(X^{\prime}_{0},L(\mathbf{u}))
. The classes
{\alpha}
and
{\alpha}^{\prime}
differ by the class generated by a Lagrangian
2
-sphere. However, after compactification,
\widetilde{\alpha}
and
\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime}
become same. The reason is that the corresponding spherical class can be contracted through the Lagrangian
S^{3}
-fiber, cf.
[
CK20
, Example 7.7]
.
