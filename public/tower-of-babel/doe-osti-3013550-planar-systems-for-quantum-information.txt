A. Executive Summary

This project aims to develop two‐dimensional (2D) moiré materials as a quantum simulator to
implement model Hamiltonians and their phase diagrams. Progress in quantum information
science (QIS) requires the development of advanced quantum materials systems. The rich family
of layered van der Waals materials and their heterostructures present opportunities to create
previously unrealized types of applications for QIS. Specifically, when two layers of van der
Waals materials are overlaid with a small twist angle or/and lattice mismatch, a moiré
superlattice with a period of about ten nanometers is formed. This provides a periodic trapping
potential for electrons. Electrons can tunnel between the traps and repel each other by their
mutual Coulomb interactions. The platform of 2D moiré materials provides many attractive
features, including tunability of length and energy scales, charge density, and even lattice
symmetry. It presents new possibilities for realizing quantum simulation of the many-body
physics in a solid-state platform. This integrated team of six investigators seeks to develop
relevant theoretical treatments to link ab-initio studies of 2D moiré materials to model
Hamiltonians and to evaluate correlated phases predicted by these model Hamiltonians in the
relevant regimes. On the experimental side, the team aims to develop methods to realize a
homogeneous and highly controlled potential landscape for the electrons and to initiate, protect,
and measure their quantum many-body states.

B. Major Findings and Impact

The development of high‐quality transition metal dichalcogenide (TMDC) bulk crystals and their
heterostructures has enabled the realization of moiré superlattices with both the triangular lattice
and honeycomb lattice (C5) symmetries and the observation of a rich family of quantum many-
body phases. Research supported by the award has resulted in 35 research articles and two
review articles (C2, C24) in peer-reviewed journals. Major findings are as follows:

 • Mapping the equilibrium quantum phase diagram of the Hubbard model and comparing it
with theory (C6, C15, C17, C22, C29, C33). Tunable Hubbard models were realized in both
TMDC heterobilayers and homobilayers with a relative twist angle. In the strong-coupling limit,
Mott insulator at one lattice filling (or half-band filling) and Wigner-Mott insulators at
commensurate fractional lattice fillings (due to the presence of longer-range Coulomb
repulsions) were observed. In a triangular lattice, the strong electron correlations, in
combination with geometric and kinetic frustrations, drive a variety of magnetic phenomena,
including kinetic magnetism (magnetism driven by electron hopping) upon doping the Mott
insulator and spin polarons (a bound state of a doped hole and a spin flip). In the moderate-
coupling limit, superconductivity was observed in twisted bilayer WSe2. Furthermore, the local
moments in the Mott insulator provide a lattice of magnetic moments. A tunable Kondo lattice
model is constructed by coupling the Mott insulator in one layer with the itinerant electrons in an
adjacent layer through the Kondo exchange interaction; a heavy fermi liquid was observed (C20,
C28).

 • Discovery of emergent topological phases in moiré TMDCs. Chern insulators, which are
the lattice analogues of the quantum Hall states, can potentially manifest high-temperature
topological orders at zero magnetic field to enable next-generation topological quantum devices.

TMDC moiré materials can be engineered to host flat Chern bands (C4, C7, C10) and realize
these topological states. The integer Chern insulator at one lattice filling and the quantum spin
Hall insulator at two lattice fillings were observed in MoTe2/WSe2 heterobilayers (C14, C16,
C17, C34). The fractional version of these states, the fractional Chern insulator and fractional
quantum spin Hall insulator, were discovered in twisted MoTe2 bilayers (C13, C18).
