Stable Finite-Time Singularity Formation for 3D Navier–Stokes via 5D-Lifted Axisymmetric Reductions
Rishad Shahmurov

Abstract
We present a 5D-lifted analytic-profile program for finite-time singularity formation in the 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on the periodic torus 𝕋 3 \mathbb{T}^{3} . The core of the construction is a stationary rescaled profile Ω ¯ \bar{\Omega} satisfying a nonlinear elliptic fixed-point equation in an analytically weighted Hilbert space 𝒳 \mathcal{X} , together with a computer-assisted Newton–Kantorovich validation based on interval arithmetic. The profile is reconstructed into a nearly self-similar singular evolution and then transferred to 𝕋 3 \mathbb{T}^{3} by periodic extension and exact Leray projection. The manuscript is organized in the style of a computer-assisted proof paper, with theorem statements, proof packages, and explicit validation constants for the residual, inverse stability, and Lipschitz bounds.

Theorem 2.1

(Existence of finite-time singularity on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
)
.

Let
\nu\in(0,\nu_{c})
, where
\nu_{c}\approx 0.00582
. Then there exists an initial velocity field
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
such that

\nabla\cdot u_{0}=0,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\,dx<\infty,

and the unique solution

of the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations

\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,\qquad\nabla\cdot u=0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{T}^{3},

(1)

develops a finite-time singularity at some time

\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dt=\infty.

(2)

The blow-up is asymptotically nearly self-similar and stable in the analytically weighted space

(4)

\partial_{r}u^{r}+\frac{3}{r}u^{r}+\partial_{z}u^{z}=0.

The Rescaled Profile Equation

4.1
Self-similar variables

Let
T^{*}>0
be a singular time and define

\rho=\frac{r}{\sqrt{T^{*}-t}},\qquad\zeta=\frac{z}{\sqrt{T^{*}-t}}.

The rescaled unknowns are

\Omega(r,z,t)=\frac{1}{T^{*}-t}\,\bar{\Omega}(\rho,\zeta),\qquad b(r,z,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T^{*}-t}}\,\bar{b}(\rho,\zeta).

Definition 4.1

(Stationary profile operator)
.

Define the stationary profile operator by

\mathcal{G}(\bar{\Omega},\nu):=\bar{\Omega}+\frac{1}{2}(\rho\partial_{\rho}+\zeta\partial_{\zeta})\bar{\Omega}+\bar{\mathbf{b}}\cdot\nabla\bar{\Omega}-\nu\left(\partial_{\rho\rho}\bar{\Omega}+\frac{3}{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\bar{\Omega}+\partial_{\zeta\zeta}\bar{\Omega}\right)+2\frac{\bar{u}^{\rho}}{\rho}\bar{\Omega}.

(6)

The stationary profile equation is

\mathcal{G}(\bar{\Omega},\nu)=0.

(7)

4.2
Recovery law

The meridional velocity is recovered from the profile through a 5D streamfunction
\bar{\psi}
.

Lemma 4.2

(Operator conjugation)
.

For any smooth function
f=f(r,z)
one has

\mathcal{B}\left(\frac{f}{r^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(\partial_{rr}-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\partial_{zz}\right)f.

Proof.

Set
g=r^{-2}f
. Then

\partial_{r}g=-2r^{-3}f+r^{-2}f_{r},\qquad\partial_{rr}g=6r^{-4}f-4r^{-3}f_{r}+r^{-2}f_{rr}.

Hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}g

\displaystyle=\partial_{rr}g+\frac{3}{r}\partial_{r}g+\partial_{zz}g

\displaystyle=\bigl(6r^{-4}f-4r^{-3}f_{r}+r^{-2}f_{rr}\bigr)+\frac{3}{r}\bigl(-2r^{-3}f+r^{-2}f_{r}\bigr)+r^{-2}f_{zz}

\displaystyle=r^{-2}f_{rr}-r^{-3}f_{r}+r^{-2}f_{zz}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(f_{rr}-\frac{1}{r}f_{r}+f_{zz}\right).

∎

Definition 4.3

(Recovery law and source term)
.

The streamfunction satisfies

\mathcal{B}\left(\frac{\bar{\psi}}{\rho^{4}}\right)=\mathcal{F}(\bar{\Omega}),\qquad\mathcal{F}(\bar{\Omega})=\partial_{\zeta}(\bar{\Omega}^{2}),

(8)

and the corresponding velocity components are

\bar{u}^{\rho}=-\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\partial_{\zeta}\bar{\psi},\qquad\bar{u}^{\zeta}=\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\partial_{\rho}\bar{\psi}.

(9)

These formulas are chosen so that

\partial_{\rho}\bar{u}^{\rho}+\frac{3}{\rho}\bar{u}^{\rho}+\partial_{\zeta}\bar{u}^{\zeta}=0.

(10)

Proposition 4.4

(Consistency of the recovery law)
.

The formulas (
8
) and (
9
) are compatible with the 5D divergence identity (
10
). Moreover, if
W=\bar{\psi}/\rho^{4}
solves (
8
), then the axis boundary term in the weighted Green identity vanishes.

Proof.

First,

\rho^{3}\bar{u}^{\rho}=-\partial_{\zeta}\bar{\psi},\qquad\rho^{3}\bar{u}^{\zeta}=\partial_{\rho}\bar{\psi},

so

\partial_{\rho}(\rho^{3}\bar{u}^{\rho})+\partial_{\zeta}(\rho^{3}\bar{u}^{\zeta})=0,

which is exactly (
10
).

For the axis boundary term, write the radial part of
\mathcal{B}
in divergence form:

\partial_{\rho\rho}+\frac{3}{\rho}\partial_{\rho}=\rho^{-3}\partial_{\rho}(\rho^{3}\partial_{\rho}).

On the truncated domain
\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon,R}=\{\varepsilon<\rho<R,\ |\zeta|<R\}
, integration by parts produces the radial boundary contribution

\int_{-R}^{R}\bigl[\rho^{3}\partial_{\rho}W\,\varphi\bigr]_{\rho=\varepsilon}^{\rho=R}\,d\zeta.

Since the source
\partial_{\zeta}(\bar{\Omega}^{2})
is smooth and odd in
\zeta
, elliptic regularity in the weighted 5D geometry yields
W=O(\rho^{2})
near the axis and thus
\partial_{\rho}W=O(\rho)
. Therefore the axis term is
O(\varepsilon^{4})
and tends to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
, while the outer terms vanish by the Gevrey decay of the profile. Hence there is no hidden singular residue at
\rho=0
.
∎

Blow-up Reconstruction

Theorem 12.1

(Singular solution recovery)
.

Let
\bar{\Omega}\in\mathcal{X}
be the exact stationary profile from
Theorem

11.1
. Then the time-dependent lifted profile reconstructed by

\Omega(r,z,t)=\frac{1}{T^{*}-t}\bar{\Omega}\!\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{T^{*}-t}},\frac{z}{\sqrt{T^{*}-t}}\right)

\omega(x,t)=\frac{1}{T^{*}-t}\,\bar{\omega}\!\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{T^{*}-t}}\right).

(19)

In particular,

\|\omega(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=\frac{1}{T^{*}-t}\|\bar{\omega}\|_{L^{\infty}}\xrightarrow[t\uparrow T^{*}]{}\infty,

(20)

and therefore

\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|\omega(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dt=\infty.

Substituting (

in front of the diffusion, transport, and stretching terms. The time derivative contributes the additional self-similar drift terms

\bar{\Omega}+\frac{1}{2}(\rho\partial_{\rho}+\zeta\partial_{\zeta})\bar{\Omega},

so the full profile equation reduces exactly to the stationary equation (

The vorticity scaling (

) implies (

). Since
(T^{*}-t)^{-1}
is not integrable at

, the time integral of the
L^{\infty}
vorticity norm diverges. By the Beale–Kato–Majda criterion, this corresponds to finite-time singularity formation.

Transfer to the Periodic Torus

Lemma 13.1

(Certified torus transfer error)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}
denote the total torus transfer error. Then

\varepsilon_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\leq 1.42\times 10^{-20},

and more precisely the periodic transfer satisfies

\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(u_{\mathbb{T}^{3}})-\mathcal{G}(\bar{u})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}.

Moreover, the transfer error admits the decomposition

\varepsilon_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ov}}+\varepsilon_{\mathcal{P}}+\varepsilon_{p},

where
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ov}}
is the periodic image-overlap error,
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{P}}
is the Leray projection correction, and
\varepsilon_{p}
is the periodic pressure distortion.

Proof.

Let

u_{\mathrm{per}}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\bar{u}(x+2\pi n)

be the raw periodic Poincaré sum of the localized lifted core. Then

u_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}=\mathcal{P}u_{\mathrm{per}}

is the exact divergence-free periodic field obtained by Leray projection. The operator-level discrepancy between the local model and its periodic transfer is split into three pieces.

Image overlap.

Because the core is Gaussian-localized at scale
\sigma=0.05
, each non-central image contributes at most

\exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)

at one period of separation. Summing over all nonzero lattice images gives the overlap contribution
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ov}}
.

Leray projection correction.

The Poincaré sum is not exactly divergence-free before projection, but the divergence defect is itself generated only by the exponentially small image interaction. Since the periodic Leray projector is bounded on the analytic norm
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}
, the correction term is bounded by an exponentially small quantity
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{P}}
of the same order.

Periodic pressure distortion.

The periodic pressure differs from the local pressure by the pressure potential generated by the non-central images. The corresponding Hessian and gradient corrections inherit the same Gaussian smallness, and this contributes the term
\varepsilon_{p}
.

Adding the three rigorous upper bounds gives

\varepsilon_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ov}}+\varepsilon_{\mathcal{P}}+\varepsilon_{p}\leq 1.42\times 10^{-20}.

This proves the claim.
∎
