Valley-Peak Modulation in Phase Space: an Exposure-Invariant VPM and its Theta-Function Structure
Aaron J. Hendrickson, David P. Haefner

Abstract
Valley-peak modulation (VPM) was introduced as a metric for quantifying read-noise in deep sub-electron read noise (DSERN) CMOS sensors. In the original amplitude-domain definition, VPM is strictly a function of both read noise and quanta exposure, yet Starkey & Fossum demonstrated exposure-independent approximations that hold in the DSERN regime. In this note we show that these approximations are truncations of a wrapped-Gaussian phase-space VPM that is exactly invariant to quanta exposure. Starting from the standard Poisson-Gaussian model, we apply a phase mapping that quotients out the integer photoelectron count. The resulting phase variable has a wrapped-Gaussian density admitting both lattice-sum and Jacobi theta-function representations parameterized only by the read noise. A closed-form expression for the phase-space VPM follows as a theta ratio, and the inverse mapping (read noise as a function of VPM) is expressible using elliptic integrals. The existing exposure-independent formulas are recovered as truncations of the lattice sums at the valley and peak. A short simulation example illustrates practical computation of VPM in phase space and inversion to recover read noise.



Keywords: conversion gain; deep sub-electron read noise; elliptic nome; Jacobi theta function; photon-counting histogram; quanta image sensor; read noise; wrapped normal.

Introduction

The quantization of photoelectron statistics becomes observable in image sensors when the input-referred read noise (
\sigma
) is sufficiently small
[
5
]
, enabling histogram-based methods of characterization that are difficult, impractical, or impossible in conventional noise regimes
[
7
,
1
,
6
,
2
,
4
]
. Starkey & Fossum
[
7
]
formalized a practical extraction method for conversion gain and read noise estimation using the photon counting histogram (PCH), including the definition of a valley-peak modulation (VPM) metric.

In the original amplitude-domain definition, VPM is a function of both read noise and quanta exposure. This dependence is visible when plotting VPM versus read noise at multiple exposure levels
[
7
]
. Nonetheless, exposure-independent approximations are asymptotically accurate for small read noise. The goal of this note is to identify the underlying exposure-invariant object those approximations are probing.

The key observation is that the quanta exposure dependence arises from Poisson weights across adjacent integer-electron peaks, whereas the “blurring” that fills in valleys is governed by read noise. If one desires a read-noise metric invariant to the integer photoelectron count, it is natural to work modulo one electron by quotienting out the integer photoelectron lattice. This motivates a phase mapping that removes the integer component exactly, yielding a wrapped Gaussian in phase space. The resulting phase-space VPM admits a theta-function representation, and the earlier exposure-independent approximations emerge as truncations of its series expansion.

2
Amplitude-domain VPM

The standard linear model for a single pixel is

X=\mu+\frac{1}{g}(K+\sigma Z),

(1)

where
K\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(H)
with quanta exposure
H\,(e\text{-})
,
Z\sim\operatorname{Gaussian}(0,1)
,
g
is the conversion gain
(e\text{-}/\mathrm{DN})
,
\mu
is the DC offset
(\mathrm{DN})
, and
\sigma
is the input-referred noise
(e\text{-})
.

Since
X|K\sim\operatorname{Gaussian}(\mu+K/g,(\sigma/g)^{2})
, we obtain the marginal distribution

f_{X}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-H}H^{k}}{k!}\frac{1}{\sigma/g}\phi\left(\frac{x-\mu-k/g}{\sigma/g}\right),\qquad\phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}.

For
\sigma\lesssim 0.5\,e\text{-}
,
f_{X}
has local maxima (peaks) near
x=\mu+k/g
and local minima (valleys) near
x=\mu+(k+1/2)/g
for
k=0,1,\dots
.

In
[
7
]
, an amplitude-domain VPM metric was defined to relate valley filling to read noise. For simplicity of discussion, we note that this amplitude-domain metric is invariant w.r.t.
\mu
and
g
, so one may set
\mu=0
and
g=1
rendering a function of two variables: quanta exposure and read noise.

One representative form used in practice is

\operatorname{VPM}_{x}(H,\sigma)\coloneqq 1-\frac{f_{X}(v)}{\frac{1}{2}(f_{X}(p_{0})+f_{X}(p_{1}))},

where
p_{0},\ p_{1}
are the DN-locations of the two tallest neighboring peaks and
v
is the location of the valley between those peaks. The quanta exposure dependence enters through the Poisson weights multiplying adjacent Gaussian components in
f_{X}
.

\operatorname{VPM}_{x}

\sigma\to 0^{+}
the curves converge, reflecting the fact that the valley is dominated by the nearest Gaussian tails, while the Poisson envelope varies slowly across the adjacent peaks in that limit.

Starkey & Fossum used this small-

[

(12)

\operatorname{VPM}_{x}\approx 1-2\exp(-\tfrac{1}{8\sigma^{2}})

(2a)

\operatorname{VPM}_{x}\approx 1-\frac{2\exp(-\tfrac{1}{8\sigma^{2}})}{1+2\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2\sigma^{2}})}

(2b)

\operatorname{VPM}_{x}\approx 1-\frac{2\exp(-\tfrac{1}{8\sigma^{2}})+2\exp(-\tfrac{9}{8\sigma^{2}})}{1+2\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2\sigma^{2}})}.

(2c)

We next show that these are naturally interpreted as approximations to an exposure-invariant VPM defined in phase space.

3
H
-invariance via phase-space transformation

To motivate the phase-space formulation we begin with (
1
) and write

g(X-\mu)=K+\sigma Z.

If one wants a read-noise diagnostic invariant to
H
(and thus the latent integer count
K
), a natural operation is reduction modulo
1
electron. A convenient smooth representation of “mod
1
” is the unit-circle embedding
y\mapsto e^{i2\pi y}
, which identifies real values that differ by integers since
e^{i2\pi(n+y)}=e^{i2\pi y}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Using this observation we define

\Phi\coloneqq\operatorname{Arg}\big(e^{i2\pi g(X-\mu)}\big)\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{=}\operatorname{Arg}\big(e^{i(2\pi\sigma)Z}\big),

where
\operatorname{Arg}z\in(-\pi,\pi]
denotes the principal value argument (phase). It follows
\Phi\sim\operatorname{WrappedGaussian}(0,(2\pi\sigma)^{2})
is a wrapped Gaussian variable with distribution independent of
H
. This result holds for any integer-valued random variable
K
and does not require Poisson photoelectron statistics.

As
\sigma\to\infty
,
\Phi\to\mathrm{Uniform}(-\pi,\pi]
, indicating complete loss of phase structure. Figure
2
illustrates
f_{\Phi}
for several values of
\sigma
.

3.1
Wrapped Gaussian series and Jacobi theta functions

Since
\Phi=(2\pi\sigma)Z
reduced modulo
2\pi
onto
(-\pi,\pi]
, its density is the sum over wraps:

f_{\Phi}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\sigma}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{(\varphi+2\pi n)^{2}}{2(2\pi\sigma)^{2}}\right),\qquad\varphi\in(-\pi,\pi].

(3)

Applying Poisson summation to (
3
) yields

f_{\Phi}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigg(1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-2(\pi\sigma)^{2}n^{2}}\cos(n\varphi)\bigg),

which is a representation of the Jacobi theta-3 function with argument
\varphi/2
and nome
q=e^{-2(\pi\sigma)^{2}}
; thus

f_{\Phi}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\vartheta_{3}\!\left(\frac{\varphi}{2},q\right),\qquad q=e^{-2(\pi\sigma)^{2}}.

(4)

Peak and valley heights are then

f_{\Phi}(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\vartheta_{3}(q),\qquad f_{\Phi}(\pi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\vartheta_{4}(q),

(5)

with
\vartheta_{n}(q)\coloneqq\vartheta_{n}(0,q)
. Equivalently, using (
3
),

f_{\Phi}(0)\propto 1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-n^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})},\qquad f_{\Phi}(\pi)\propto 2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-(2n+1)^{2}/(8\sigma^{2})}.

(6)

3.2
Phase-space VPM and connection to earlier approximations

Analogous to the amplitude-domain definition, define the phase-space VPM as

\operatorname{VPM}_{\varphi}(\sigma)\coloneqq 1-\frac{f_{\Phi}(\pi)}{f_{\Phi}(0)}.

Let

m(q)=(\vartheta_{2}(q)/\vartheta_{3}(q))^{4},\qquad\left(\vartheta_{4}(q)/\vartheta_{3}(q)\right)^{4}=1-m(q)

denote the inverse elliptic nome. Then

\operatorname{VPM}_{\varphi}(\sigma)=1-\sqrt[4]{1-m(e^{-2(\pi\sigma)^{2}})}\ .

(7)

Alternatively, using the lattice-sum specializations (
6
),

\operatorname{VPM}_{\varphi}(\sigma)=\underset{{n_{1}\to\infty}\atop{n_{2}\to\infty}}{\lim}v(n_{1},n_{2}),\quad v(n_{1},n_{2})=1-\frac{2\sum_{n=0}^{n_{1}}e^{-(2n+1)^{2}/(8\sigma^{2})}}{1+2\sum_{n=1}^{n_{2}}e^{-n^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}}.

It follows that
v(0,0)
,
v(0,1)
, and
v(1,1)
reproduce the three exposure independent approximations given in
[
7
]
(Eq. (
2a
)-(
2c
)), respectively. Consequently, we have shown that the phase-space VPM is the underlying function these asymptotic expressions approximate.

3.3
Inverting the phase-space VPM

Equally important is the inverse function quantifying read noise as a function of VPM. Inverting (
7
) yields

m(q)=1-(1-\operatorname{VPM}_{\varphi})^{4},\qquad q=e^{-2(\pi\sigma)^{2}}.

Equivalently,

e^{-2(\pi\sigma)^{2}}=q(m),\qquad m=1-(1-\operatorname{VPM}_{\varphi})^{4},

(8)

where
q(m)
is the elliptic nome

q(m)=\exp\left(-\pi\frac{\mathcal{K}(1-m)}{\mathcal{K}(m)}\right),\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{K}(m)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-m\sin^{2}t}}

denoting the complete elliptic integral of the first kind. Solving (
8
) for
\sigma
yields the desired inverse function

\sigma(\operatorname{VPM}_{\varphi})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{K}((1-\operatorname{VPM}_{\varphi})^{4})}{\mathcal{K}(1-(1-\operatorname{VPM}_{\varphi})^{4})}\bigg)^{1/2}.

(9)

1

N=5\times 10^{6}

\hat{g}=0.0648\,e\text{-}/\mathrm{DN}

u_{n}\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(-0.5,0.5]

5
Discussion and conclusion

Amplitude-domain VPM is influenced by both read noise (Gaussian blurring) and quanta exposure (Poisson weighting across adjacent peaks). From this perspective, VPM is fundamentally a circular modulation statistic: once the integer photoelectron lattice is quotiented out, the native space of the metric is the unit circle rather than the real-valued amplitude domain. The phase mapping introduced here removes the integer photoelectron count exactly, producing a wrapped Gaussian whose distribution depends only on the read noise parameter
\sigma
. This yields an exposure-invariant phase-space VPM with a closed-form theta ratio and an explicit inverse mapping
\sigma(\mathrm{VPM}_{\varphi})
expressible using elliptic integrals.

The exposure-independent approximations derived by Starkey & Fossum can thus be interpreted as low-order truncations of the lattice sums defining the wrapped phase density. This clarifies why those formulas are asymptotically accurate in the DSERN regime and provides an exposure-invariant target quantity that can be used within subsequent estimation methods. This note does not propose a new end-to-end characterization method; rather, it isolates the underlying invariant object and supplies closed-form expressions that may be useful in future investigations.
