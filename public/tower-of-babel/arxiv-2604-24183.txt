Conflict-free chromatic index of bipartite graphs
Yuxin Jin, Yuping Gao

Abstract
An edge coloring of a graph G G is called conflict-free if, for every edge, its closed neighborhood contains a color that appears exactly once. The least number of colors required for such a coloring is the conflict-free chromatic index of G G , denoted by χ C ​ F ′ ​ ( G ) \chi^{\prime}_{CF}(G) . Kamyczura, Meszka, and Przybyło conjectured that χ C ​ F ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 3 \chi^{\prime}_{CF}(G)\leq 3 for any bipartite graph G G without isolated vertices. In this paper, we confirm this conjecture. Keywords: conflict-free edge coloring; conflict-free chromatic index; bipartite graph; tree

In this paper, all graphs considered are finite and simple. Let

)

Motivated by frequency assignment in cellular networks, Even, Lotker, Ron, and Smorodinsky
[
2
]
and Smorodinsky

7

initiated the study of the conflict-free chromatic number of graphs. Let

the set of neighbors of

N_{G}[v]:=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}

may be omitted if it is clear from the context. The least integer

such that

admits a vertex

for every vertex

is called the
conflict-free chromatic number
of

Dębski and Przybyło
[
1

proposed the edge variant, known as conflict-free chromatic index. Let
G
be a graph and
\varphi
be an edge
k
-coloring of
G
. We denote by
E_{G}(v)
the set of edges incident with a vertex
v\in V(G)
, and define
E_{G}[uv]:=E_{G}(u)\cup E_{G}(v)
to be the
closed neighborhood
of any given edge
uv\in E(G)
. The subscript
G
may be omitted if clear from context. An edge
uv
is
satisfied
by

if there exists a color which appears exactly once in
E_{G}[uv]
. We make a convention that we use the same terminology when
\varphi
is a partial edge coloring, that is, when
\varphi
assigns colors only to a subset of
E(G)
. The coloring
\varphi
is said to be

if all edges of
G
are satisfied. The least number of colors required for such a coloring is the

of

, denoted by
\chi^{\prime}_{CF}(G)

Since every proper edge coloring is conflict-free, we have
\chi^{\prime}_{CF}(G)\leq\chi^{\prime}(G)

[
5

obtained an asymptotically tight upper bound for regular graphs. Kamyczura, Meszka, and Przybyło
[

. Therefore, a universal upper bound of

Conjecture 1.1

(Kamyczura, Meszka, and Przybyło
[
4
]
)
.

If
G
is a bipartite graph without isolated vertices, then
\chi^{\prime}_{CF}(G)\leq 3

In this paper, we resolve Conjecture
1.1
in Theorem
1.3
by using a new parameter proposed by Kamyczura, Meszka, and Przybyło
[
4
]
. Let
\varphi
be an edge coloring of some subgraph

. Denote by

Lemma 1.2

(Kamyczura, Meszka, and Przybyło
[

]

.

For every graph

\chi^{\prime}_{sCF}(G)\leq\chi^{\prime}_{CF}(G)\leq\chi^{\prime}_{sCF}(G)+1
.

Theorem 1.3

If

\chi^{\prime}_{sCF}(G)\leq 2

By Theorem

Theorem 1.4

If

\chi^{\prime}_{CF}(G)\leq 2\lceil\log_{2}\chi(G)\rceil+1

If

By Brooks’ Theorem, Corollary

1.5
. Let

Lemma 1.6

For all

\chi^{\prime}_{CF}(K_{n})\leq\lceil\log_{2}(n-1)\rceil+1

For all

\chi^{\prime}_{CF}(C_{n})=2

2
Upper bounds on conflict-free chromatic index

In this section, we prove our main results using the concept of a
Y
-dominating set for a bipartite graph
G:=G(X,Y)
, introduced in Subsection
2.1
.

2.1
Preliminaries

Let
G
be a graph. For a vertex subset
S\subseteq V(G)
, let
G[S]
be the subgraph of
G
induced by
S
and
G-S=G[V(G)\setminus S]
.
Let
G:=G(X,Y)
be a bipartite graph. A subset
D\subseteq X
is called a
Y
-dominating set
if
N(D)=Y
, where
N(D)=\cup_{v\in D}N(v)
. Note that such a set exists as
N(X)=Y
. We call
D

minimal
if for every
v\in D
,
N(D\setminus\{v\})\neq Y
. For a
Y
-dominating set
D\subseteq X
and
x\in D
, a vertex
y\in N(x)
is called
a private neighbor of
x
with respect to
D
if
N(y)\cap D=\{x\}
.
The set of private neighbors of
x
is denoted by
P(x)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G:=G(X,Y)
be a bipartite graph and let
D\subseteq X
be a
Y
-dominating set. Then the following three statements hold.

(i)

D
is minimal if and only if for all
x\in D
, we have
P(x)\neq\emptyset
.

(ii)

If
D
is minimal, then the collection
\mathcal{P}=\{P(x)\colon x\in D\}
consists of non-empty, pairwise disjoint subsets of
Y
.

(iii)

If
D
is minimal and
P=\cup_{x\in D}P(x)
, then there exists a matching
M
in
G[D\cup P]
covering
D
.

Proof.

For statement
(i)
,
D
is minimal if and only if for all
x\in D
, the set
D\setminus\{x\}
is not a
Y
-dominating set. Equivalently, there exists a vertex
y\in Y
such that
N(y)\cap D=\{x\}
, meaning
y\in P(x)
.

For statement
(ii)
, we have
P(x)\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in D
by
(i)
. We show that
P(x_{1})\cap P(x_{2})=\emptyset
for all distinct
x_{1},x_{2}\in D
. Suppose not, let
y\in P(x_{1})\cap P(x_{2})
. Then
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\subseteq N(y)\cap D
, contradicting the definition of private neighbor.

Statement
(iii)
follows immediately from Statement
(ii)
by selecting exactly one edge
xy
for each
x\in D
such that
y\in P(x)
. This collection of edges forms the required matching
M
.
∎

2.2
Bipartite graphs

In this subsection, we construct a partial edge 2-coloring of
G
such that all edges in
G
are satisfied. Theorem
1.3
then follows from Lemma
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We may assume that
G:=G(X,Y)
is a connected bipartite graph.
We prove that
\chi^{\prime}_{sCF}(G)\leq 2
and then
\chi^{\prime}_{CF}(G)\leq 3
by Lemma
1.2
.
Let
D\subseteq X
be a minimal
Y
-dominating set of
G
. By Lemma
2.1
(iii)
,
G[D\cup P]
has a matching
M
that covers
D
. We construct a partial edge
2
-coloring
\varphi
of
G
as follows.
For every
xy\in E(M)
, let
\varphi(xy)=1
.
For every vertex
y\in Y\setminus V(M)
, we choose exactly one vertex
x\in N(y)\cap D
(which exists since
D
is a
Y
-dominating set) and let
\varphi(xy)=2
.
All other edges in
G
remain uncolored.

We claim that all edges of
G
are satisfied by
\varphi
and complete the proof of the theorem.
Note that by our construction, every vertex
y\in Y
is incident to exactly one colored edge. Let
xy\in E(G)
be an arbitrary edge with
x\in X
and
y\in Y
. We show that
E_{G}[xy]
contains a unique color in the following two cases.

Case 1:
x\in D
.

In this case,
x
is incident to an edge
xp\in E(M)
with
\varphi(xp)=1
, where
p\in P(x)
. Note that
xp
is the only edge in
E_{G}(x)
colored by 1, because
\varphi
assigns color 1 only to edges in
M
, and
M
is a matching. If there exists some edge
x^{\prime}y\in E(G)
with
\varphi(x^{\prime}y)=1
, then
y\in P(x^{\prime})
and
N(y)\cap D=\{x^{\prime}\}
. Since
x\in N(y)\cap D
, we have
x=x^{\prime}
. It implies that color 1 appears exactly once in
E_{G}[xy]
.

Case 2:
x\in X\setminus D
.

Since each colored edge is incident to a vertex in
D
,
x
is not incident to any colored edge.
If
y\in V(M)
, then
y\in P(x^{\prime})
for some
x^{\prime}\in D
, meaning
x^{\prime}y
is the only edge in
E_{G}(y)
colored by 1. So 1 appears exactly once in
E_{G}[xy]
. Assume that
y\in Y\setminus V(M)
, then color 2 appears exactly once in
E_{G}(y)
by the definition of
\varphi
. Again, since
x
is not incident to any colored edge, color 2 appears exactly once in
E_{G}[xy]
.

In both cases,
xy
is satisfied. Thus,
\chi^{\prime}_{sCF}(G)\leq 2
.
∎

2.3
Upper bound for general graphs

The proof strategy for Theorem
1.4
is similar to the approach used by Kamyczura, Meszka, and Przybyło
[
4
]
. For the sake of completeness, we include the full proof in this subsection.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
G
be a graph without isolated vertices. We first prove that
\chi^{\prime}_{sCF}(G)\leq 2\lceil\log_{2}\chi(G)\rceil
by induction on
\chi(G)
. If
\chi(G)=2
, then the theorem follows from Theorem
1.3
. Assume that
\chi(G)\geq 3
and the theorem holds for all graphs
G^{\prime}
with
\chi(G^{\prime})<\chi(G)
.

Let
t:=\lceil\log_{2}\chi(G)\rceil
and
k:=2^{t-1}
. Then
\chi(G)\leq 2k
. Let
\varphi
be a proper vertex
2k
-coloring of
G
with color classes
V_{1},\ldots,V_{2k}
, where
V_{i}=\emptyset
for
\chi(G)<i\leq 2k
if it exists. Let
A:=\cup_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
and
B:=\cup_{i=k+1}^{2k}V_{i}
. Let
H
be the bipartite graph consisting of all edges between
A
and
B
in
G
, and let
F
be the subgraph of
G
consisting of all the remaining edges, where we do not include potential isolated vertices in
V(H)
or
V(F)
. Since
E(F)\subseteq E(G[A])\cup E(G[B])
, we have
\chi(F)\leq k<\chi(G)
. By the induction hypothesis,
\chi^{\prime}_{sCF}(F)\leq 2\lceil\log_{2}\chi(F)\rceil\leq 2\lceil\log_{2}k\rceil=2(t-1)
. Thus, there exists an edge
2(t-1)
-coloring
\varphi_{1}
of a subgraph of
F
such that all edges in
F
are satisfied. Furthermore, since
H
is bipartite, Theorem
1.3
implies there exists an edge coloring
\varphi_{2}
of a subgraph of
H
using colors
2t-1,2t
such that all edges in
H
are satisfied. Combining
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
, we obtain
\chi^{\prime}_{sCF}(G)\leq 2t=2\lceil\log_{2}\chi(G)\rceil
. By Lemma
1.2
,
\chi^{\prime}_{CF}(G)\leq 2\lceil\log_{2}\chi(G)\rceil+1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.9
by analyzing the property of conflict-free edge coloring.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

First, assume that
\chi^{\prime}_{CF}(T)=2
. Let
\varphi

be a conflict-free edge
2
-coloring of
T
, and let
F:=\{e\in E(T)\colon\varphi(e)=1\}
.
Since both colors are used on
T
,
F
is a nonempty proper subset of
E(T)
.
Let
uv\in E(T)
and let
n_{i}(uv)
denote the number of edges colored by
i
in
E_{T}[uv]
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.
Since
\varphi
is conflict-free, either
n_{1}(uv)=1
or
n_{2}(uv)=1
.

If
uv\in F
, then
n_{1}(uv)=d_{F}(u)+d_{F}(v)-1
and
n_{2}(uv)=(d_{T}(u)-d_{F}(u))+(d_{T}(v)-d_{F}(v))
. So either
d_{F}(u)+d_{F}(v)=2
or
(d_{T}(u)-d_{F}(u))+(d_{T}(v)-d_{F}(v))=1
. Condition
(i)
follows.
If
uv\notin F
, then
n_{1}(uv)=d_{F}(u)+d_{F}(v)
and
n_{2}(uv)=(d_{T}(u)-d_{F}(u))+(d_{T}(v)-d_{F}(v))-1
. So either
d_{F}(u)+d_{F}(v)=1
or
(d_{T}(u)-d_{F}(u))+(d_{T}(v)-d_{F}(v))=2
. Condition
(ii)
follows.

Conversely, assume that there exists a nonempty proper subset
F\subseteq E(T)
satisfying conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
. Let
\varphi
be an edge 2-coloring of
T
such that
\varphi(e)=1
if
e\in F
and
\varphi(e)=2
otherwise.
For any edge
uv\in E(T)
, let
n_{i}(uv)
denote the number of edges colored by
i
in
E_{T}[uv]
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.

If
uv\in F
, then either
n_{1}(uv)=d_{F}(u)+d_{F}(v)-1=1
or
n_{2}(uv)=(d_{T}(u)-d_{F}(u))+(d_{T}(v)-d_{F}(v))=1
by condition
(i)
.
If
uv\notin F
, then either
n_{1}(uv)=d_{F}(u)+d_{F}(v)=1
or
n_{2}(uv)=(d_{T}(u)-d_{F}(u))+(d_{T}(v)-d_{F}(v))-1=1
by condition
(ii)
.
Therefore, all edges of
T
are satisfied by
\varphi
and we have
\chi^{\prime}_{CF}(T)=2
.
∎
