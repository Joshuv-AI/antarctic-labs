Effects of Starch Type and Concentration on the Physicochemical, Antioxidant, and Sensory Properties of BacchusⓇ-Incorporated Gwapyeon
Authors: Yeon-Woo Kim, Jisu Lee, Jiaxiang Zheng, Caglar Doguer, Hyun-Joo Lee, Miae Doo, Jung-Heun Ha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon was formulated as a functional starch-based gel confectionery to evaluate the effects of starch type and concentration on its physicochemical properties, antioxidant capacity, texture, and consumer preference. Three starch types (corn, mung bean, and potato) were applied at three concentration levels (9%－11% for corn and potato starch; 7%－9% for mung bean) under identical processing conditions. Physicochemical characteristics were primarily dependent on the starch type. Mung bean starch formulations exhibited lower pH and higher soluble solid contents; corn starch formulations showed relatively higher pH and lower Brix values; and potato starch formulations displayed intermediate values. Color properties also varied, with mung bean and corn starch gels appearing brighter, whereas the potato starch gels exhibited reduced lightness. Storage stability differed markedly: mung bean starch gels showed pronounced early syneresis, corn starch gels exhibited low but gradually increasing syneresis, and potato starch gels revealed delayed moisture release. Total flavonoid content did not differ among formulations; however, 2,2’-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) radical-scavenging activity varied, with the highest activity observed in the 11% corn starch formulation. Texture profile analysis indicated increased mechanical resistance with higher starch concentrations, with mung bean starch producing the firmest gels and potato starch the softest. Consumer evaluation showed that excessive chewiness and elevated residual perception were associated with lower acceptance. The 9% corn starch formulation showed the highest overall acceptance, whereas purchase intention was relatively higher in the 7% mung bean and 9% corn starch fomulation. The results highlight starch selection as a critical determinant of structural stability, functional retention, and sensory performance in BacchusⓇ-based starch-gel confectionery systems.

INTRODUCTION

Gwapyeon is a traditional Korean starch-gel confectionery that is prepared by heating fruit juice with sugar or honey and inducing gelation by adding starch (Cho et al., 2008). Historical records, including Eumsikdimibang, Jinyeonuigwe, and Jinchanuigwe, document gwapyeon as a ceremonial dish that was served during royal banquets of the Joseon Dynasty (Oh, 2013). Gwapyeon has historically been valued for its refined appearance, mild sweetness, and suitability for formal occasions. Modern formulations have been adapted to contemporary consumer preferences, often using reduced starch content and increased sugar levels to achieve a softer texture with enhanced sweetness (Kim et al., 2010). The term “gwapyeon” is derived from the Korean words “gwa” (fruit) and “pyeon” (piece). Representative varieties include mogwa (quince) (Kim and Park, 2013), bokbunja (Korean black raspberry) (Han et al., 2006), and orange gwapyeon (Kang et al., 2023). Other notable examples include sansa (hawthorn) (Shin and Yoon, 2011), omija (Schisandra chinensis) (Chae and Shin, 2023), and blueberry gwapyeon (Lee et al., 2015). Compared with conventional jelly-type confectionery products, gwapyeon provides a starch-based gel matrix with a softer, less elastic texture, thereby allowing broader modulation of moisture retention, syneresis, and textural attributes by varying the starch type and concentration (Kim and Lee, 2003). This formulation flexibility makes gwapyeon a useful model for investigating structure-property relationships in semisolid functional confectioneries containing liquid ingredients.

Starch plays a central role in determining the structural, physiological, and sensory properties of gwapyeon. During thermal processing, starch undergoes hydration, gelatinization, amylose leaching, and reassociation, thereby forming a three-dimensional gel network (Cai et al., 2014; Sparla et al., 2014). Amylose leaching contributes to the gel network formulation, whereas subsequent reassociation and retrogradation influence the gel stability, moisture retention, and textural changes during storage (Wan et al., 2023). The extent of these transformations governs the firmness, transparency, water-holding capacity, overall textural attributes, and storage stability (Deng et al., 2023; Xia et al., 2024). Starch source strongly influences gel behavior because of the inherent differences in amylose-amylopectin ratio, granule size, crystalline structure, and gelatinization temperature (Martens et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2023). Differences in crystalline structure and retrogradation tendency influence the gel matrix’s ability to retain water and maintain its structural integrity, thereby impacting syneresis and sensory attributes (Li et al., 2024). Grain-, pulse-, and tuber-derived starches display distinct swelling behavior, retrogradation tendencies, and textural characteristics (No and Shin, 2016; Berski et al., 2018). Moreover, starch concentration modulates the gel network density and moisture distribution in a starch-source-dependent manner, thereby affecting syneresis and sensory perception (Kim et al., 2017). Consequently, the starch type and its concentration are critical formulation parameters for starch-based gel confectioneries incorporating liquid functional ingredients.

BacchusⓇ is a commercially available energy beverage that is produced in two formulations, Bacchus DⓇ and Bacchus FⓇ, which primarily differ in taurine content while exhibiting comparable sweetness, color, and sensory characteristics. Bacchus DⓇ, traditionally distributed through pharmacies, contains a higher taurine level and is positioned as a functionally intensive tonic (Dong-A Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., 2026a). In contrast, Bacchus FⓇ is widely available in retail markets, has a lower taurine content, and is more routinely consumed as a general energy beverage (Dong-A Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., 2026b). Among commercially available functional beverages, BacchusⓇ was considered particularly suitable for the present study because of its consumer familiarity, compositional consistency, and established functional image associated with fatigue recovery. In contrast to functional beverages with more variable compositional profiles, including fruit- or herb-based drinks, BacchusⓇ offers a comparatively consistent matrix of taurine, vitamins, and sugars, which supports the reproducible assessment of starch-bioactive interactions. Expansion of BacchusⓇ into gel-type confectionery products, such as BacchusⓇ jelly, demonstrates its compatibility with semisolid food matrices and commercial feasibility beyond beverage applications (Dong-A Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., 2026c). However, systematic investigation of the incorporation of BacchusⓇ into starch-based gel networks remains limited. Thus, clarifying the interactions between BacchusⓇ components and starch-dependent gel structures is crucial for establishing rational formulation strategies for functional starch-gel confectioneries.

Therefore, the present study aimed to develop a novel BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon by replacing conventional fruit juice with BacchusⓇ while maintaining the traditional starch-gel confectionery format. Unlike previously commercialized BacchusⓇ jelly products, the present study incorporated BacchusⓇ into a starch-based gwapyeon matrix, providing a differentiated formulation platform for examining how starch-dependent gel structures affect the physicochemical and sensory properties. As starch-based gel systems exhibit structural and textural characteristics that are different from conventional gelatin- or pectin-based jelly matrices, the gwapyeon format may offer advantages in water retention, syneresis control, and stability of the incorporated functional components. Starch type and concentration were selected as the primary formulation variables. Consequently, the effects of starch type and concentration on physicochemical properties, including pH, Brix, color, syneresis, antioxidant activity, and textural characteristics, were systematically evaluated to elucidate structure-property relationships in BacchusⓇ-based starch gels (Lee et al., 2025a). In addition, consumer preference was evaluated across multiple sensory attributes, including appearance, flavor, texture, overall acceptance, and purchase intention, to assess the sensory feasibility and marketability (Lee et al., 2025a). Integration of instrumental analysis with consumer-based sensory evaluation provided a comprehensive assessment of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon as a commercially viable functional dessert. The findings contribute to formulation-oriented insights that support the modernization and diversification of traditional starch-based confectioneries by incorporating functional beverage ingredients.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Formulation and preparation of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon

BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon was prepared using white sugar (CJ CheilJedang), distilled water (Merck), and three starch types: corn starch (Soyeon Foods), mung bean starch (Chungwon Food), and potato starch (Cheongeun F&B). BacchusⓇ (Dong-A Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) was diluted to 50% with distilled water before incorporation.

Starch concentrations were selected based on the optimal conditions identified through literature reviews (Han et al., 2006; Lee et al., 2015) and preliminary trials. Mung bean starch was applied at relatively lower concentrations (7%－9%) than corn and potato starches because of its higher amylose content and stronger gel-forming ability, which allows for sufficient gel structure formation at reduced solid contents (Li et al., 2023; Qiao et al., 2024). Preliminary trials confirmed that excessive concentrations of mung bean starch produced overly rigid gels with reduced processability during heating and molding.

The formulations presented in Table 1 were prepared using previously published manufacturing procedures (Lee et al., 2015; Kang et al., 2023). The specific starch quantities were as follows:

•Corn starch: 36 g (BC9), 40 g (BC10), and 44 g (BC11)

•Mung bean starch: 28 g (BM7), 32 g (BM8), and 36 g (BM9)

•Potato starch: 36 g (BP9), 40 g (BP10), and 44 g (BP11)

Each formulation was prepared by mixing the starch with 80 g of sugar, 200 mL of BacchusⓇ, and 200 mL of distilled water. The mixture was heated over medium heat with constant stirring (60 rpm) until boiling, maintained at low heat for 2.5 min, and poured into a 15 cm square mold to set. The samples were then left at room temperature (25±2°C) for 4 h and subsequently refrigerated for 4 h to allow complete setting.

Physicochemical properties

For pH analysis, 5 g of each gwapyeon sample was homogenized with 45 mL of distilled water and agitated for 16 h in a shaking incubator (Hanbaek Scientific Co.). The homogenate was centrifuged at 3,000 g for 10 min (Hanil Science Co., Ltd.), and the pH of the supernatant was measured using a pH meter (Orion Star A211, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Soluble solid content was determined in triplicate as Brix values using a digital refractometer (Hanna Instruments, Inc.).

Hunter color properties

Samples were hardened at room temperature for 4 h and cooled at 4°C for an additional 4 h before color analysis. Colorimetric values (L*, a*, and b*) were measured using a colorimeter (LC100, Tintometer Ltd.).

Syneresis

Syneresis was evaluated by quantifying liquid separation during storage. Samples were cut into 2-cm cubes and stored in the refrigerator for 1－5 days. At each time point, liquid that had separated from the gel and accumulated at the bottom of the storage container was carefully recovered using a pipette and weighed. Syneresis (%) was calculated using the following equation:

Syneresis (%)=[Weight of separated liquid (g)/Total weight of gel (g)]×100

Antioxidant properties

Antioxidant capacity was evaluated using ethanolic extracts of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon. A 5-g portion of each sample was homogenized with 45 mL of 70% ethanol and incubated with shaking at room temperature for 16 h. The mixture was then centrifuged at 3,000 g for 10 min, and the supernatant was used for subsequent analyses. Total flavonoid content (TFC) was quantified using a quercetin standard curve and expressed as µg quercetin equivalents per mL (µg QE/mL) (Lee et al., 2024). Thereafter, 2,2’-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) (ABTS) radical-scavenging activity was measured spectrophotometrically and expressed as mg Trolox equivalents per mL (mg TE/mL) (Lee et al., 2025b).

Texture profile analysis (TPA)

For TPA, samples were cut into cubic pieces (approximately 1 cm3). Hardness, adhesiveness, resilience, springiness, cohesiveness, gumminess, and chewiness were determined using a two-cycle compression test with a texture analyzer (TA-XT2, Stable Micro Systems) equipped with a 30 mm diameter flat-bottom cylindrical probe. The instrument settings consisted of a 60% compression strain, a trigger force of 0.5 N, a test speed of 1 mm/s, and pre- and post-test speeds of 2 mm/s. Controlled conditions ensured consistent and accurate evaluation of the texture properties.

Consumer preference evaluation

Consumer sensory evaluation was performed with 50 untrained university students aged 20－29 years, following ethical review and approval by the Institutional Review Board of Kunsan National University (IRB No.: 1040117-202509-HR-021-02). All participants were informed of the study purpose and procedures before participation, and written informed consent was obtained from each participant. Each participant received one piece of each sample, and water was provided between samples to minimize carryover effects. Sensory attributes, including color, clearness, sweetness, sourness, starchiness, chewiness, residual characteristics, overall acceptance, and purchase intention, were assessed using a 9-point hedonic scale, ranging from 1 (extremely dislike) to 9 (extremely like).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism software (version 10.5, GraphPad Software). Each experiment was conducted in triplicate, and results are presented as the mean±standard deviation. Differences among the formulations were analyzed using a one-way analysis of variance, followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison test. Syneresis data were analyzed using two-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison test. Statistical significance was defined at P<0.05.

RESULTS

pH and Brix of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon

Table 2 presents the pH and Brix values of the BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon prepared with different starch types and concentrations. The pH values ranged from 3.28 to 3.46 across all formulations. Mung bean starch formulations generally showed lower pH values, including BM7 (3.36±0.05), BM8 (3.28±0.03), and BM9 (3.34±0.06). The pH values of the corn starch formulations were 3.46±0.01 for BC9, 3.39±0.09 for BC10, and 3.37±0.09 for BC11. BC9 exhibited a significantly higher pH than the mung bean starch formulation of BM8 (P<0.05). Potato starch formulations displayed intermediate values of BP9 (3.45±0.05), BP10 (3.41±0.02), and BP11 (3.45±0.04).

Brix values primarily varied by starch type. Mung bean starch samples revealed higher Brix values compared to other formulations: 2.40±0.26 for BM7, 2.07±0.15 for BM8, and 2.23±0.06 for BM9. Corn starch formulations exhibited lower values, including BC9 (1.53±0.15), BC10 (1.60±0.44), and BC11 (1.77±0.25), with BC9 displaying the lowest Brix value among all treatments (P<0.05). Potato starch formulations exhibited intermediate Brix values of BP9 (1.80±0.00), BP10 (2.20±0.10), and BP11 (1.93±0.12). Overall, mung bean starch formulations produced lower pH and higher Brix values, corn starch formulations showed higher pH and lower Brix values, and potato starch formulations displayed intermediate physicochemical characteristics.

Hunter color values of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon

Supplementary Fig. 1 illustrates the visual appearance of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon, with Hunter color values summarized in Table 3. The lightness (L*) values of the formulations ranged from 38.28 to 47.37. Mung bean starch formulations exhibited higher L* values (BM7: 46.65±1.34, BM8: 47.32±0.76, and BM9: 47.02±0.43). Corn starch formulations showed comparable values (BC9: 46.78±0.86, BC10: 45.23±0.99, and BC11: 47.37±1.04). Potato starch formulations exhibited significantly lower L* values than the mung bean and corn starch formulations (BP9: 40.25±2.08, BP10: 38.28±1.57, and BP11: 39.65±1.95), indicating a darker appearance (P<0.05).

All samples exhibited negative a* values, indicating a greenish color. Mung bean starch formulations showed more negative values (BM7: −3.75±0.24, BM8: −4.27±0.34, and BM9: −4.48±0.26). Corn starch formulations displayed intermediate values (BC9: −3.10±0.30, BC10: −3.28±0.19, and BC11: −3.57±0.48). The potato starch formulations showed significantly lower negative values (BP9: −1.40±0.69, BP10: −1.10±0.74, and BP11: −1.63±0.41), indicating weaker green coloration (P<0.05).

The b* values ranged from 2.50 to 5.78, indicating variation in yellowness. Among the mung bean starch formulations, the b* values were 5.28±1.17 for BM7, 5.78±0.77 for BM8, and 4.18±0.49 for BM9. Corn starch formulations revealed lower b* values of 3.33±1.02 for BC9, 2.75±0.44 for BC10, and 2.50±1.48 for BC11. The potato starch formulations exhibited values of 5.68±2.21 for BP9, 5.73±1.12 for BP10, and 5.18±1.15 for BP11. The corn starch formulations, particularly BC10 and BC11, exhibited significantly lower b* values than those of the mung bean (BM7 and BM8) and potato starch formulations (P<0.05). Overall, the mung bean starch formulations produced brighter, green-yellow surfaces, corn starch formulations exhibited intermediate color characteristics, and the potato starch formulations produced darker appearances.

Syneresis of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon

Table 4 summarizes the syneresis behavior of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon during 5 days of storage. The syneresis values ranged from 0.00% to 13.54% over the storage period, and all formulations except BP11 revealed significant time-dependent changes (P<0.05). Mung bean starch formulations showed the most pronounced syneresis, particularly during the early storage period. BM7 exhibited high initial syneresis, which decreased over time (13.31±2.14% on day 1; 6.20±1.00% on day 2; 4.43±0.61% on day 3; 2.32±0.21% on day 4; and 3.16±0.34% on day 5). BM8 exhibited the highest initial syneresis among all treatments, followed by a continuous decrease during storage (13.54±0.99%; 6.78±0.65%; 3.65±1.43%; 2.01±0.36%; and 1.61±0.19% from day 1 to day 5, respectively). BM9 showed comparatively lower syneresis within the mung bean starch group (7.53±0.95%; 6.50±0.48%; 3.65±0.59%; 2.71±0.33%; and 2.51±0.44% from day 1 to day 5, respectively).

Corn starch formulations exhibited lower syneresis during the early storage period, followed by gradual increases at later stages. BC9 showed detectable syneresis throughout storage (0.23±0.34%; 0.23±0.21%; 0.83±0.18%; 0.44±0.41%; and 2.12±0.39% from day 1 to day 5, respectively). BC10 exhibited minimal syneresis during the first three days, with increases observed thereafter (0.11±0.19%; 0.02±0.03%; 0.12±0.15%; 1.03±0.63%; and 1.34±0.34% from day 1 to day 5, respectively). BC11 showed a gradual increase in syneresis over storage (0.51±0.31%; 0.95±0.37%; 0.75±0.25%; 2.03±0.56%; and 2.35±0.12% from day 1 to day 5, respectively).

Potato starch formulations exhibited delayed syneresis compared to mung bean and corn starch formulations. BP9 showed no detectable syneresis during the first two days, followed by slight increases at later stages (0.00±0.00%; 0.00±0.00%; 0.03±0.06%; 0.14±0.10%; and 2.46±0.45% from day 1 to day 5, respectively). BP10 exhibited no syneresis on day 1, with gradual increases observed thereafter (0.00±0.00%; 0.25±0.22%; 0.54±0.86%; 1.72±0.48%; and 3.24±0.47% from day 1 to day 5, respectively). BP11 exhibited measurable syneresis throughout storage (0.77±0.88%; 2.36±1.54%; 2.40±0.40%; 2.27±0.40%; and 2.43±0.42% from day 1 to day 5, respectively).

Overall, syneresis occurred most prominently and earliest in the mung bean starch formulations, moderately and gradually in the corn starch formulations, and was delayed in the potato starch formulations, which exhibited slower moisture release during the early storage period.

Antioxidant capacities of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon

Table 5 summarizes the antioxidative activities of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon as evaluated by its TFC and ABTS radical-scavenging capacity. The TFC values ranged from 49.71 to 54.80 µg QE/mL, with no significant differences observed among the treatments. The TFC values of the mung bean starch formulations were 53.63±0.03 µg QE/mL for BM7, 52.41±0.78 µg QE/mL for BM8, and 52.96±0.35 µg QE/mL for BM9. Corn starch formulations exhibited a wide range of TFC values, with BC9 (49.71±0.00 µg QE/mL) showing the lowest TFC, BC10 (54.63±0.38 µg QE/mL) the highest, and BC11 (52.55±0.28 µg QE/mL) providing an intermediate level. Potato starch formulations showed comparable values for BP9 (52.59±0.53 µg QE/mL), BP10 (54.80±1.34 µg QE/mL), and BP11 (52.70±0.78 µg QE/mL).

The ABTS radical-scavenging capacity differed significantly among the formulations, ranging from 0.51 to 3.76 mg TE/mL. Mung bean starch formulations showed intermediate ABTS values for BM7 (2.12±0.30 mg TE/mL), BM8 (2.69±0.64 mg TE/mL), and BM9 (2.10±0.48 mg TE/mL). Among the corn starch formulations, BC11 (3.76±1.13 mg TE/mL) exhibited the highest ABTS radical-scavenging activity, with no significant differences among BC9, BC10, and BC11. However, BC11 exhibited significantly higher activity than BM9, BP9, and BP11 (P<0.05). Other corn starch formulations presented comparable values for BC9 (2.33±0.04 mg TE/mL) and BC10 (2.50±0.26 mg TE/mL). Furthermore, the potato starch formulations exhibited the lowest ABTS radical-scavenging capacity for BP9 (0.51±0.93 mg TE/mL), BP10 (1.69±0.03 mg TE/mL), and BP11 (1.36±0.02 mg TE/mL).

TPA of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon

Table 6 presents the TPA results for hardness, adhesiveness, resilience, cohesiveness, springiness, gumminess, and chewiness of the BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon prepared with different starch types and concentrations. Hardness differed significantly among formulations. In the mung bean starch formulations, hardness increased with starch concentration: BM7 (134.46±4.73 g), BM8 (206.42±12.38 g), and BM9 (304.06±15.28 g). BM9 exhibited the highest hardness overall (P<0.05). Corn starch formulations showed intermediate hardness values, including BC9 (169.56±0.23 g), BC10 (245.93±13.51 g), and BC11 (246.16±3.52 g). Potato starch formulations produced substantially softer textures of BP9 (41.54±1.74 g), BP10 (51.50±1.30 g), and BP11 (121.89±5.58 g).

Adhesiveness also varied significantly (P<0.05). The adhesiveness values of mung bean starch were BM7 (−14.30±6.77 g·s), BM8 (−10.73±6.16 g·s), and BM9 (−2.66±3.14 g·s), indicating reduced stickiness with increasing starch concentration. Corn starch formulations showed intermediate adhesiveness values, including BC9 (−6.36±2.97 g·s), BC10 (−12.77±6.01 g·s), and BC11 (−9.79±5.44 g·s). Potato starch formulations exhibited a wider range of adhesiveness values, with BP9 (−3.82±1.68 g·s), BP10 (−20.36±8.16 g·s), and BP11 (−7.77±6.31 g·s).

Furthermore, resilience differed significantly among formulations (P<0.05). Corn starch formulations showed the highest resilience in BC9 (13.71±1.76%), whereas BC10 (6.67±1.58%) and BC11 (4.90±1.41%) exhibited lower values. Mung bean starch formulations showed intermediate resilience, including BM7 (6.59±0.65%), BM8 (6.63±0.97%), and BM9 (10.44±0.41%). Potato starch formulations revealed resilience of BP9 (12.63±1.42%), BP10 (7.79±1.28%), and BP11 (10.19±2.22%).

Cohesiveness values ranged from 0.12 to 0.27 and differed significantly among groups. Lower cohesiveness was observed in the mung bean starch formulations, including BM7 (0.21±0.05), BM8 (0.15±0.02), and BM9 (0.12±0.01). Corn starch formulations showed cohesiveness values BC9 (0.24±0.04), BC10 (0.21±0.04), and BC11 (0.17±0.01). Higher cohesiveness values were observed in the potato starch formulations, including BP9 (0.27±0.01), BP10 (0.27±0.01), and BP11 (0.24±0.05).

In addition, springiness varied significantly depending on starch type and concentration. Mung bean starch formulations exhibited the highest springiness values, including BM7 (73.27±12.59%), BM8 (88.95±0.89%), and BM9 (85.87±10.93%). Corn starch formulations showed lower springiness values, including BC9 (74.11±8.35%), BC10 (42.38±3.69%), and BC11 (33.86±2.33%). Intermediate springiness values were noted for the potato starch formulations of BP9 (58.78±2.40%), BP10 (56.59±3.93%), and BP11 (50.29±9.12%).

Gumminess and chewiness exhibited similar trends (P<0.05). Gumminess was highest in BC10 (51.94±12.19), followed by BC9 (40.15±7.42) and BM9 (36.33±0.80), while BP9 (11.20±0.88) and BP10 (13.67±0.60) exhibited the lowest values. Chewiness was highest in BM9 (31.25±4.68) and BC9 (29.92±7.83), whereas the lowest chewiness values were identified for BP9 (6.58±0.53) and BP10 (7.75±0.79).

Overall, the textural properties of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon were strongly influenced by starch type and concentration. Mung bean starch formulations exhibited greater hardness, springiness, gumminess, and chewiness; corn starch formulations showed intermediate mechanical properties; and the potato starch formulations produced softer textures with lower mechanical resistance.

The overall acceptance of BC9 (4.18±2.05) was significantly higher than that of BM9 (2.80±1.90), BP9 (1.96±1.48), BP10 (1.84±1.54), and BP11 (1.89±1.57) (P<0.05), whereas no significant differences were observed among the remaining formulations.

Furthermore, the purchase intention values were BM7 (3.07±2.30), BM8 (2.76±2.08), BM9 (2.75±2.24), BC9 (2.95±2.18), BC10 (2.69±2.03), BC11 (2.58±1.72), BP9 (1.65±1.55), BP10 (2.05±2.09), and BP11 (1.82±1.79). The purchase intention of BC9 (2.95±2.18) was significantly higher than that of BP9 (1.65±1.55) (P<0.05), and no other significant differences in purchase intention were observed among the formulations. Overall, consumer preference results revealed that significant differences among formulations were limited to specific attributes, including color, sweetness, starchiness, chewiness, residual characteristics, overall acceptance, and purchase intention. In contrast, clearness and sourness did not differ significantly across the formulations.

DISCUSSION

BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon was developed to evaluate whether incorporating a commercially available functional beverage into a traditional starch-based gel confectionery could enhance the functional value without compromising the essential quality attributes. The effects of starch type and concentration on the physicochemical properties, antioxidant behavior, textural characteristics, and consumer acceptance were systematically assessed. Replacing conventional fruit juice with BacchusⓇ provided a formulation strategy to extend a culturally familiar dessert into a modern functional format while preserving its structural identity. All formulations were processed under identical preparation and heating conditions, thereby enabling direct comparison without altering the procedural variables. The experimental design facilitated the interpretation of functional performance beyond the isolated quality parameters by integrating physicochemical, structural, and sensory evaluations. Corn, mung bean, and potato starch were selected to represent distinct molecular structures and hydration behaviors (Ilhan et al., 2020). Differences among these starches allowed for the identification of starch-specific performance patterns within the gel matrix. Previous studies on fruit-based gwapyeon have reported that the starch type strongly influences the texture, clarity, and consumer preference (Kim et al., 2003). Findings from the present study demonstrated that nutraceutical beverages can be successfully incorporated into gel-based confections when the formulation parameters are strategically optimized.

Physicochemical behavior of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon was primarily governed by the starch type rather than the concentration, indicating that intrinsic structural characteristics determined the gel responses to acidic and soluble components. Variation in pH and soluble solid content suggests differential interactions between the starch granules and beverage-derived solutes, which are potentially influenced by hydration kinetics and molecular organization within the gel matrix. Optical properties further reflect the starch-dependent microstructural arrangement. Differences in light scattering and transparency are likely associated with variations in the gel packing density and network homogeneity, as starches with distinct granule sizes and swelling behavior form matrices with different refractive properties (Bravo-Núñez et al., 2019; Min et al., 2023). Mung bean starch, which is characterized by high-amylose content and a pronounced tendency to retrograde, likely promoted rapid network contraction and early moisture expulsion during storage (Li et al., 2015; Zou et al., 2025). In contrast, corn starch exhibited more balanced swelling and hydration behavior, which contributed to moderate gel packing density, improved structural stability, and reduced syneresis (Lin et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024). Potato starch, with larger granules and high swelling capacity, initially retained substantial water but formed less densely interconnected matrices, thus predisposing the system to delayed liquid separation and altered light transmission (Cui et al., 2014; Nagasaki et al., 2021). Collectively, these physicochemical variations indicate that the starch-specific structural properties play a central role in determining gel stability and moisture dynamics.

Antioxidant behavior appeared to be influenced by gel water-holding stability, in combination with starch-dependent structural characteristics. Although the TFC remained comparable across the formulations, variation in ABTS radical-scavenging activity suggested differences in the starch-dependent gel networks’ ability to retain or release water-soluble antioxidant components. However, the relationship was not entirely linear, as mung bean starch formulations exhibited relatively high antioxidant activity despite marked initial syneresis. This pattern suggests that antioxidant behavior was determined by moisture retention during storage, as well as by the initial extractability and release of soluble antioxidant compounds from the gel matrix (No et al., 2016). Formulations with greater moisture stability tended to maintain higher radical-scavenging capacity. In contrast, early or pronounced moisture release was associated with reduced antioxidant activity, likely due to the migration of soluble bioactive compounds during syneresis (Barik et al., 2025).

In the case of mung bean starch, its high-amylose content and rapid gelation may have promoted the efficient initial incorporation or release of BacchusⓇ-derived antioxidant compounds, thereby resulting in relatively high ABTS activity despite reduced physical stability (Li et al., 2023). In addition, the pronounced retrogradation tendency of this starch may have accelerated moisture expulsion, which indicates that antioxidant extractability and syneresis can occur simultaneously rather than in strict inverse relation (Lin et al., 2023). Early retrogradation in the high-amylose systems may have facilitated partial loss of antioxidant compounds despite increased gel firmness (Lin et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024). Conversely, weaker gels with reduced network density may have limited immobilization of soluble antioxidants (Zheng et al., 2024). Antioxidant preservation depends on the structural compatibility between the gel microarchitecture and liquid mobility, both of which are affected by the physicochemical characteristics of the starch type used (İlhan et al., 2022). Therefore, differences in antioxidant activity among the formulations are more likely to reflect the combined effects of initial antioxidant release, starch-dependent matrix interactions, and subsequent syneresis-mediated compound migration than the water-holding stability alone. Previous studies have demonstrated that antioxidant retention in starch-based systems is more strongly influenced by microstructural stability than by the total starch concentration (Gisbert et al., 2022). Syneresis-mediated liquid migration might thus represent a key determinant of functional compound retention in gel-type confectionery systems, with the extent of liquid migration and compound loss likely varying with the starch type (İlhan et al., 2022).

The TPA indicated that mechanical resistance strongly influenced consumer perception. High-concentration mung bean starch gels exhibited increased chewiness; however, excessive chewiness did not correspond with higher hedonic acceptance. In contrast, potato starch gels produced markedly lower hardness and chewiness despite relatively high cohesiveness values, which suggests that structural integrity alone was insufficient to ensure a favorable sensory perception (Zhou et al., 2025). Reduced mechanical resistance, combined with elevated residual characteristics, appeared to negatively influence consumer response. Corn starch formulations displayed intermediate mechanical properties and lower residual characteristics, which corresponded with superior overall acceptance and purchase intention. Consumer preference was optimized within a moderate mechanical range that balanced firmness and postoral persistence (Biltekin et al., 2025). Thus, a sequential mechanism linking starch type to sensory acceptance can be proposed: starch molecular structure influenced gel network organization and hydration behavior, which in turn determined syneresis and liquid mobility during storage (Pulgarín et al., 2023). Moisture dynamics affected antioxidant retention, whereas gel network density governed the mechanical resistance. Mechanical resistance and residual characteristics jointly shaped sensory perception and ultimately influenced the overall acceptance and purchase intention (Toniazzo et al., 2025). At a 9% concentration, corn starch achieved the most favorable performance across these parameters, thereby confirming its selection as a strategic formulation variable rather than a neutral structural component (Teixeira-Lemos et al., 2021).

The study has several limitations that should be acknowledged when interpreting the present findings. Because a control formulation without BacchusⓇ was not included, the observed physicochemical, antioxidant, and sensory responses could not be definitively distinguished from effects that are attributable to the intrinsic properties of the starch matrix itself. Accordingly, the present findings should be interpreted as relative formulation outcomes within a BacchusⓇ-based system rather than as effects that are uniquely attributable to BacchusⓇ. Storage stability was assessed over a limited period, and longer-term evaluation is required to confirm quality maintenance under extended distribution and retail conditions. Furthermore, sensory evaluation was performed with a relatively homogeneous young adult panel, thus limiting its generalizability to broader populations, such as elderly consumers or individuals with reduced chewing capacity, i.e., groups that are especially likely to prefer soft gel-type foods. Microstructural characterization using microscopy and rheological analyses would further clarify the mechanistic relationships among the starch type, gel network formation, and antioxidant retention. From a formulation perspective, incorporating starch blends, dietary fibers, or hydrocolloids may further enhance the gel integrity and mitigate syneresis (Lin et al., 2023). Large-scale manufacturing trials and sensory evaluation across diverse demographic groups are recommended to enhance translational relevance.

In conclusion, the quality outcomes of BacchusⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon were primarily governed by the starch type and secondarily by the starch concentration, thus confirming starch selection as a central determinant of product performance. Corn starch, particularly the 9% formulation, achieved the highest overall acceptance, with intermediate chewiness and moderate starchiness, whereas purchase intention was relatively higher in the 7% mung bean and 9% corn starch formulations. Mung bean starch at higher concentrations increased chewiness without improving acceptance, whereas the potato starch formulations exhibited elevated starchiness and residual characteristics that were associated with lower consumer preference. Starch type functioned as a key determinant of sensory acceptance rather than merely as a structural component. Product behavior followed a sequential mechanism in which the starch type determined gel network density, gel network density influenced syneresis, syneresis affected antioxidant retention, and the combined structural and functional attributes shaped sensory acceptance. The effective incorporation of nutraceutical beverages, such as BacchusⓇ, into traditional confectionery formats may thus help modernize culturally familiar desserts while maintaining nutritional functionality and consumer appeal.
