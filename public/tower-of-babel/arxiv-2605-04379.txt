Introduction

Let
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Any collection of sets is called a family. Given a set
X
, we denote by
2^{X}
,
{X\choose k}
, and
{X\choose\leq k}
the families of all subsets of
X
, subsets of size exactly
k
, and subsets of size at most
k
, respectively. We refer to the family
{[n]\choose k}
as the
k
-th layer of the Boolean lattice
. A (full)
star
is the family of all sets in
{X\choose k}
containing a fixed element.

A
matching
is a family of pairwise disjoint sets. Given a family
\mathcal{F}
, its
matching number

\nu(\mathcal{F})
is the maximum size of a matching in
\mathcal{F}
.
Let us introduce two classical extremal quantities concerning families with bounded matching number. Both were introduced by Erdős in the 1960s:

\displaystyle e(n,s)

\displaystyle=\max\big\{|\mathcal{F}|:\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]},\nu(\mathcal{F})<s\big\},

\displaystyle e_{k}(n,s)

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{|\mathcal{F}|:\mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\choose k},\nu(\mathcal{F})<s\Big\}.

This paper is devoted to the study of the quantity
e(n,s)
. However, these two quantities are closely connected, so we briefly discuss
e_{k}(n,s)
first.

Obviously, we have
e_{k}(n,s)={n\choose k}
for
n<sk
. The famous Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem
[
3
]
states that
e_{k}(n,2)={n-1\choose k-1}
for
n\geq 2k
. Soon after the EKR paper, Erdős made a general conjecture concerning the value of
e_{k}(n,s)
for all
n,k,s
. Let us define several families of
k
-element subsets of
[n]
:

\mathcal{A}_{i}^{(k)}(n,s):=\Bigl\{F\in{[n]\choose k}:|F\cap[si-1]|\geq i\Bigr\},\qquad 1\leq i\leq k.

(1)

It is easy to check that these families do not contain an
s
-matching. Therefore,

e_{k}(n,s)\geq\max\left\{|\mathcal{A}_{i}^{(k)}(n,s)|:1\leq i\leq k\right\}.

(2)

A careful analysis of the values
|\mathcal{A}_{i}^{(k)}(n,s)|
shows that the maximum in (
2
) is always attained for
i=1
or
i=k
. The Erdős Matching Conjecture states that these examples are best possible.

Conjecture 1

(Erdős Matching Conjecture
[
1
]
)
.

For
n\geq sk
,

e_{k}(n,s)=\max\left\{|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(k)}(n,s)|,|\mathcal{A}_{k}^{(k)}(n,s)|\right\}.

(3)

Despite significant effort, the Erdős Matching Conjecture remains open in general. It has been proved for
k\leq 3

[
2
,
7
]
. The maximum in (
3
) is attained at the first term for
n
sufficiently large compared with
sk
, and at the second term for
n
close to
sk
. It is known that for
n
sufficiently large we indeed have
e_{k}(n,s)=|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(k)}(n,s)|
, while for
n
sufficiently close to
sk
we have
e_{k}(n,s)=|\mathcal{A}_{k}^{(k)}(n,s)|
. The best known bounds are due to
[
5
]
and
[
11
]
. In the latter paper, the authors proved the conjecture for
n>\frac{5}{3}sk
and
s\geq s_{0}
.

Theorem 1

(Frankl and Kupavskii
[
11
]
)
.

There exists an absolute constant
s_{0}
such that

e_{k}(n,s)=|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(k)}(n,s)|={n\choose k}-{n-s+1\choose k},

(4)

provided

n\geq\frac{5}{3}(s-1)k-\frac{2}{3}(s-1)\qquad\text{and}\qquad s\geq s_{0}.

The conjecture is also known to hold for
n
very close to
sk
and
s\geq s_{0}(k)
, see
[
6
,
13
]
.

For
n\geq 2ks(1+o(1))
, Frankl and Kupavskii proved a Hilton–Milner-type stability result
[
9
]
. We will need it in the proof of our main result, and we state it precisely in Section
3
. Informally, it says that in this regime of parameters, the largest nontrivial family with
\nu(\mathcal{F})\leq s-1
is a union of
s-2
stars and a Hilton–Milner family.

We now turn to the quantity
e(n,s)
. It turns out that for
s\geq 3
the behaviour of
e(n,s)
depends on the residue of
n
modulo
s
. In what follows, we use the parametrization

n=sm+s-\ell,\qquad 1\leq\ell\leq s.

In 1968, answering a question of Erdős, Kleitman determined
e(sm+s-\ell,s)
for
\ell\in\{1,s\}
.

Theorem 2

(Kleitman
[
12
]
)
.

\displaystyle e(sm-1,s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=m+1}^{sm+s-1}{sm+s-1\choose t},

(5)

\displaystyle e(sm,s)

\displaystyle={sm-1\choose m}+\sum_{t=m+1}^{sm}{sm\choose t}.

(6)

The family achieving equality in (
5
) is simply
{[n]\choose\geq m+1}
. It is not difficult to check that
e(sm,s)=2e(sm-1,s)
. A family achieving equality in (
6
) may be obtained from
{[n]\choose\geq m}
by the
doubling operation
. Given a family
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
, its
doubling
is the family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset 2^{[n+1]}
defined by

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\{F\subset[n+1]:F\cap[n]\in\mathcal{F}\}.

It is easy to check that
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|=2|\mathcal{F}|
and that
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
implies
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})<s
.

Note that the extremal examples in both cases of Theorem
2
may be described in the following way: the extremal family consists of all sets of size at least
m+1
, together with a largest family of
m
-element sets that does not contain an
\ell
-matching. In the case
\ell=1
the latter family is empty, while in the case
\ell=s
it is
\mathcal{A}_{m}^{(m)}(n,s)
. In
[
8
]
, Frankl and Kupavskii introduced the following family.

Definition 1
.

Let
n=sm+s-\ell
, with
0<\ell\leq s
. Set

\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell):=\bigl\{P\subset[n]:|P|+|P\cap[\ell-1]|\geq m+1\bigr\}.

This family consists of all sets of size at least
m+1
, the family
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(m)}(n,\ell)
, and some sets of size less than
m
. It is easy to check that
\nu(\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell))<s
. In
[
8
]
, Frankl and Kupavskii made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 2
.

Suppose that
s\geq 2
,
m\geq 1
, and
n=sm+s-\ell
for some integer
0<\ell\leq\lceil\frac{s}{2}\rceil
. Then

e(sm+s-\ell,s)=|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)|.

(7)

The condition
\ell\leq\lceil\frac{s}{2}\rceil
is needed to ensure that
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(m)}(n,\ell)
is the largest family of
m
-element sets avoiding an
\ell
-matching.
In
[
8
]
and
[
10
]
, Conjecture
2
was proved in several cases.

Theorem 3
.

The equality
e(sm+s-\ell,s)=|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)|
holds in each of the following cases:

\displaystyle\mathrm{(i)}\qquad\ell=2,

\displaystyle\mathrm{(ii)}\qquad m=1,

\displaystyle\mathrm{(iii)}\qquad s\geq\ell m+3\ell+3.

Recently, Kupavskii and Sokolov determined
e(2s+s-\ell,s)
for all
s
and
\ell

[
14
]
. In particular, Conjecture
2
was confirmed for
m=2
. We refer to that paper for a more thorough and up-to-date discussion of the problem.

In this paper, we prove an approximate version of this conjecture for
s
sufficiently large compared to
m
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
n=sm+s-\ell
. For every integer
m
and every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
s_{0}
such that

e(n,s)=|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)|,

(8)

provided
s>s_{0}
and
\ell\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)s
.

We should note that very recently, the authors of the present paper have made a general conjecture concerning the values of
e(n,s)
for all
n,s
and verified it for large
s
‘from the other end’, i.e., for
n=ms+c
and
s>s_{0}(c)
, see
[
15
]
.

Given a family
\mathcal{F}
, we denote by
\mathcal{F}^{(k)}
(
\mathcal{F}^{(\leq k)}
,
\mathcal{F}^{(\geq k)}
) the family of all sets in
\mathcal{F}
of size exactly
k
(at most
k
, at least
k
). In fact, we prove a stronger variant of Theorem
4
: every family avoiding an
s
-matching has at most as many sets in the first
m+1
layers of the Boolean lattice as
\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)
. More precisely, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

For every integer
m
and every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
s_{0}
such that the following holds. Put
n=sm+s-\ell
. If
s>s_{0}
,
\ell\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)s
, and
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
is a family with
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
, then

|\mathcal{F}^{(\leq m+1)}|\leq|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(\leq m+1)}|.

Theorem
5
obviously implies Theorem
4
, since
\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)
contains all sets from
{[n]\choose\geq m+2}
. We note that in
[
8
]
Frankl and Kupavskii conjectured that the statement of Theorem
5
should hold, but managed to prove it, under the assumptions of Theorem
3
, only for the first
m+2
layers of the Boolean lattice. Theorem
5
confirms their conjecture. We also note that if one replaces
m+1
by
m
in its statement, then the resulting claim is false.

2
Overview of the proof

We follow essentially the same overall strategy as in point (iii) of Theorem
3
, but in several places we develop and use a more refined analysis. We work in the regime where
s
is large. In this regime, the quantity
\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}{n\choose i}
is small compared to
{n\choose m}
. At the same time, in our parametrization the
m
-th layer is the largest layer of the Boolean lattice that may contain an
s
-matching. For these reasons, the first and main part of the proof focuses on layers
m
and
m+1
.

The family
\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)
contains all
(m+1)
-element sets and, on the
m
-th layer, the largest family that does not contain an
\ell
-matching (in the regime under consideration, this family is a union of
\ell-1
stars). If an extremal family
\mathcal{F}
with
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
contains more sets than
\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)
on these two layers, then it must contain an
\ell
-matching on the
m
-th layer. Consequently,
\mathcal{F}
cannot contain all
(m+1)
-element sets. In fact, a simple averaging argument shows that
\mathcal{F}
must miss many
(m+1)
-element sets. This is the crucial part of the proof: we need to find the best trade-off between the additional
m
-sets that we gain and the
(m+1)
-sets that we lose. This is also the key point at which our approach differs from that of
[
8
]
and allows us to replace the condition
s\geq\ell m+3\ell+3
by the much weaker condition
s\geq 3\ell
, for sufficiently large
s
.

The conclusion of the first step is that an extremal family
\mathcal{F}
with
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
and
|\mathcal{F}|\geq|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)|
cannot contain an
\ell
-matching on the
m
-th layer. Using the stability result of Frankl and Kupavskii (Theorem
6
), we then deduce that if
\mathcal{F}^{(m)}
is not contained in a union of
\ell-1
stars, then
|\mathcal{F}^{(m)}|
is substantially smaller than
|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(m)}(n,\ell)|
. This loss is large enough to compensate for any possible gains on the layers of size at most
m-1
.

Finally, we analyze the lower layers of the Boolean lattice. We show that if
\mathcal{F}
is not a subfamily of
\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)
, then it must miss either many sets from
\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(m)}
or many sets from
{[n]\choose m+1}
, and therefore it is smaller than
\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)
.

3
Preliminaries and auxiliary calculations

Recall the notion of shifting. Let
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}
and
B=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\}
be
k
-element subsets of
[n]
, with their elements listed in increasing order. We say that
A
can be shifted to
B
if
a_{i}\geq b_{i}
for all
i\in[k]
. A family
\mathcal{F}
is
shifted
if
A\in\mathcal{F}
implies
B\in\mathcal{F}
for every
B
such that
A
can be shifted to
B
. It is well known
[
4
]
that it is enough to prove Theorem
5
for shifted families.

Next, we state the Hilton–Milner-type stability result of Frankl and Kupavskii
[
9
]
. Let us first define the relevant family:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(k)}(n,s)

\displaystyle=\Big\{H\in{[n]\choose k}:H\cap[s-2]\neq\emptyset\Big\}\cup\{[s,s+k-1]\}

\displaystyle\cup\Big\{H\in{[s-1,n]\choose k}:s-1\in H,\ H\cap[s,s+k-1]\neq\emptyset\Big\}.

The family
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}(n,s)
is the union of
s-2
stars with centers in
[s-2]
and the Hilton–Milner family on
[s-1,n]
. Clearly, we have
\nu(\mathcal{H}^{(k)}(n,s))<s
.

Theorem 6

(Frankl and Kupavskii
[
9
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 2sk(1+o(1))
, where
o(1)
is taken with respect to
s\to\infty
. Assume that
\mathcal{G}\subset{[n]\choose k}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{G})<s
and, additionally, that
\mathcal{G}
is not isomorphic to a subfamily of
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(k)}(n,s)
. Then

|\mathcal{G}|\leq|\mathcal{H}^{(k)}(n,s)|=|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(k)}(n,s)|-{n-k-s+1\choose k-1}+1.

We emphasize that any improvement of the constant
2
in Theorem
6
would imply an improvement of the constant
\frac{1}{2}
in the condition
\ell\leq\frac{s}{2}
in Theorems
4
and
5
.

We will also need the following universal bound on
e_{k}(n,s)
.

Lemma 7

(Frankl
[
4
]
)
.

Let
n\geq ks
. Then

e_{k}(n,s)\leq(s-1){n-1\choose k-1}.

It will be more convenient for us to use Lemma
7
in the following slightly modified form. For a family
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
, we denote by
y_{\mathcal{F}}(k)
the number of
k
-element sets that are not in
\mathcal{F}
. That is,
y_{\mathcal{F}}(k)={n\choose k}-|\mathcal{F}^{(k)}|
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
be a family without an
s
-matching, and let
k,t\geq 0
be integers such that
n=ks+t
. Then

y_{\mathcal{F}}(k)\geq\left(1+\frac{t}{k}\right){n-1\choose k-1}.

Proof.

By Lemma
7
we have

|\mathcal{F}^{(k)}|\leq e_{k}(n,s)\leq(s-1){n-1\choose k-1},

and therefore

\displaystyle y_{\mathcal{F}}(k)

\displaystyle={n\choose k}-|\mathcal{F}^{(k)}|\geq{n\choose k}-(s-1){n-1\choose k-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n}{k}-s+1\right){n-1\choose k-1}=\left(1+\frac{t}{k}\right){n-1\choose k-1}.

∎

In the remainder of this section, we provide several useful calculations with binomial coefficients that will be used later. The following lemma quantifies the fact that the lower layers of the Boolean lattice are small.

Lemma 9
.

For
n\geq sk-1
we have

\sum_{i=0}^{k}{n\choose i}\leq\frac{s-1}{s-2}{n\choose k}.

Proof.

For
i\leq k
we have

{n\choose i-1}=\frac{i}{n-i+1}{n\choose i}\leq\frac{k}{n-k+1}{n\choose i}\leq\frac{1}{s-1}{n\choose i}.

Therefore,

\sum_{i=0}^{k}{n\choose i}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{s-1}\right)^{k-i}{n\choose k}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{s-1}\right)^{j}{n\choose k}=\frac{s-1}{s-2}{n\choose k}.

∎

The next lemma will be used in the stability part of the proof: we will compare losses in the
m
-th level of the Boolean lattice with gains in the lower layers relative to
\mathcal{P}(s,m,\ell)
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
n=sm+s-\ell
. If
\ell\geq 2
and
s\geq\frac{3}{2}\ell+m
, then

{n-m-\ell+1\choose m-1}>\frac{\ell}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}{n-1\choose i-1}.

(9)

Proof.

By Lemma
9
, the right-hand side of (
9
) is at most

\frac{\ell}{2}\cdot\frac{s-1}{s-2}{n-1\choose m-2}=\frac{\ell(s-1)(m-1)}{2(s-2)n}{n\choose m-1}.

The left-hand side of (
9
) is

\displaystyle{n-m-\ell+1\choose m-1}

\displaystyle={n\choose m-1}\prod_{i=0}^{m-2}\frac{n-m-\ell+1-i}{n-i}

\displaystyle={n\choose m-1}\prod_{i=0}^{m-2}\left(1-\frac{m+\ell-1}{n-i}\right)

\displaystyle\geq{n\choose m-1}\left(1-\frac{m+\ell-1}{n-m+2}\right)^{m-1}

\displaystyle\geq{n\choose m-1}\left(1-\frac{(m-1)(m+\ell-1)}{n-m+2}\right).

Thus, it is enough to prove

\frac{\ell(s-1)(m-1)}{2(s-2)n}<1-\frac{(m-1)(m+\ell-1)}{n-m+2}.

(10)

By the assumptions of the lemma we have

n-m+2=sm+s-\ell-m+2>sm

and

\frac{\ell}{2(s-2)}\leq\frac{\ell}{3\ell-4}\leq 1.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\ell(s-1)(m-1)}{2(s-2)n}+\frac{(m-1)(m+\ell-1)}{n-m+2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell(s-1)(m-1)}{2(s-2)sm}+\frac{(m-1)(m+\ell-1)}{sm}

\displaystyle<\frac{\ell(s-1)}{2(s-2)s}+\frac{m+\ell-1}{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\left(\frac{\ell}{2}+\frac{\ell}{2(s-2)}+m+\ell-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{s}\left(\frac{3}{2}\ell+m\right)\leq 1.

We conclude that (
10
) holds.
∎

4
Matchings on the
m
-th layer

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
be a shifted family without an
s
-matching, and assume that

|\mathcal{F}^{(\leq m+1)}|\geq|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(\leq m+1)}|.

To prove Theorem
5
, we need to show that
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)
. In this section we prove that
\mathcal{F}^{(m)}
does not contain an
\ell
-matching.

Lemma 11
.

For every integer
m
there exists
s_{0}
such that for all
s\geq s_{0}
and all
\ell\leq\frac{3}{5}s
the following holds. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
be a family avoiding an
s
-matching and satisfying

|\mathcal{F}^{(\leq m+1)}|\geq|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(\leq m+1)}|.

Then
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(m)})<\ell
. Moreover, for any
k\leq\ell
there is no
k
-matching
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\}
in
\mathcal{F}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}|F_{i}|\leq km+k-\ell.

We will use the moreover part in the analysis of smaller sets in Section
5
.
Lemma
11
is a consequence of the following technical lemma.

Lemma 12
.

Let
n=sm+s-\ell
. Assume that there exists
t\geq 0
such that the following three conditions hold:

{(m+1)(s-\ell)-1\choose m}>(\ell+t-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}{n-1\choose i-1}+{n-\ell+1\choose m}-{n-\ell-t\choose m}.

(11)

\left(\frac{t}{m+1}+1\right){n-(\ell+t)m-1\choose m}>(s-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}{n-1\choose i-1}.

(12)

e_{m}(n,\ell+t)={n\choose m}-{n-\ell-t+1\choose m}.

(13)

Then for any
\mathcal{F}
with
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
and

|\mathcal{F}^{(\leq m+1)}|\geq|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(\leq m+1)}|

we have
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(m)})<\ell
. Moreover, for any
k\leq\ell
there is no
k
-matching
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\}
in
\mathcal{F}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}|F_{i}|\leq km+k-\ell.

Let us first derive Lemma
11
from Lemma
12
.

Proof of Lemma
11
using Lemma
12
.

We need to check that for every fixed
m
and all sufficiently large
s
there exists a value of
t
satisfying conditions (
11
), (
12
), and (
13
). We fix
m
and define
t
as the smallest integer satisfying the condition

\frac{1}{m!}\left(\frac{t}{m+1}+1\right)\left(\frac{2m}{5}\right)^{m}>\frac{(m+1)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}.

(14)

We now verify that the three conditions are satisfied for all sufficiently large
s
.

First, let us check (
13
). By Theorem
1
, condition (
13
) holds for
s\geq s_{0}
and

n\geq\frac{5}{3}m(\ell+t).

The latter condition is satisfied provided
s\geq s_{0}(m)
and
\ell\leq\frac{3}{5}s
, since

\frac{5}{3}m(\ell+t)\leq ms+\frac{5}{3}mt=n-(s-\ell)+\frac{5}{3}mt<n.

The last inequality holds for sufficiently large
s
, since
s-\ell\geq\frac{2}{5}s
and
t
is constant once
m
is fixed.

Next, we check (
11
). We have

{(m+1)(s-\ell)-1\choose m}>{\frac{2}{5}(m+1)s-1\choose m},

(15)

and

\displaystyle(\ell+t-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}{n-1\choose i-1}+\left({n-\ell+1\choose m}-{n-\ell-t\choose m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{3}{5}s+t-1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}{n-1\choose i-1}+(t+1){n-\ell\choose m-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{3}{5}s+t-1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}{(m+1)s\choose i-1}+(t+1){(m+1)s\choose m-1}.

For fixed
m
and
t
, the right-hand side of (
15
) is a polynomial in
s
of degree
m
. At the same time, the last displayed bound is a polynomial in
s
of degree

Finally, we check (

\left(\frac{t}{m+1}+1\right){\frac{2}{5}sm-tm-1\choose m},

(16)

and the right-hand side is at most

(17)

For fixed

, both (

) and (

) are polynomials in
s
of degree

. We compare their leading coefficients. The leading coefficient of (

) is

\frac{1}{m!}\left(\frac{t}{m+1}+1\right)\left(\frac{2m}{5}\right)^{m},

\frac{(m+1)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}.

Thus, by (

We emphasize that the constant

Proof of Lemma
12
.

Obviously, it is enough to prove the moreover part, since it implies
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(m)})<\ell
. We argue indirectly. Let
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\}
be a matching such that
F_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
for all
i
,
k\leq\ell
, and

\sum_{i=1}^{k}|F_{i}|\leq km+k-\ell.

Let

X=[n]\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}F_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}\cap 2^{X}.

Note that

|X|\geq n-(km+k-\ell)=(s-k)(m+1),

and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
does not contain an
(s-k)
-matching. Thus, we may apply Lemma
8
and obtain

{X\choose m+1}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\geq\left(1+\frac{|X|-(m+1)(s-k)}{m+1}\right){|X|-1\choose m}\geq{(m+1)(s-k)-1\choose m}.

Therefore,

y_{\mathcal{F}}(m+1)\geq{X\choose m+1}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\geq{(m+1)(s-k)-1\choose m}\geq{(m+1)(s-\ell)-1\choose m}.

(18)

Next, we consider two cases, according to whether
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(\leq m)})<\ell+t
or
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(\leq m)})\geq\ell+t

If

|\mathcal{F}^{(m)}|\leq e_{m}(n,\ell+t)={n\choose m}-{n-\ell-t+1\choose m}.

For
i<m

|\mathcal{F}^{(i)}|\leq e_{i}(n,\ell+t)\leq(\ell+t-1){n-1\choose i-1}.

These bounds, together with (

|\mathcal{F}^{(\leq m+1)}|<|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(\leq m+1)}|.

Indeed,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(\leq m+1)}|-|\mathcal{F}^{(\leq m+1)}|\geq y_{\mathcal{F}}(m+1)-\left(|\mathcal{F}^{(m)}|-|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(m)}|+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}|\mathcal{F}^{(i)}|\right)

\displaystyle\geq{(m+1)(s-\ell)-1\choose m}-\left({n-\ell+1\choose m}-{n-\ell-t\choose m}+(\ell+t-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}{n-1\choose i-1}\right).

If
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(\leq m)})\geq\ell+t

(\ell+t)

Y=[n]\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell+t}G_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}\cap 2^{Y}.

|Y|\geq n-(\ell+t)m=(s-\ell-t)(m+1)+t,

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
does not contain an

\displaystyle y_{\mathcal{F}}(m+1)

\displaystyle\geq{Y\choose m+1}\setminus\mathcal{G}^{\prime}

To bound

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{(i)}|\leq e_{i}(n,s)\leq(s-1){n-1\choose i-1}.

Finally, combining (

\displaystyle|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(\leq m+1)}|-|\mathcal{F}^{(\leq m+1)}|\geq y_{\mathcal{F}}(m+1)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\mathcal{F}^{(i)}|

Proof of Theorem

In this section, we complete the proof of Theorem
5
. Recall that it is enough to prove the theorem for shifted families. Assume that
n=sm+s-\ell
,
\ell\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)s
,
s\geq s_{0}(m,\varepsilon)
, and
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
is a shifted family such that
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
and

|\mathcal{F}^{(\leq m+1)}|\geq|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(\leq m+1)}|.

We also assume that
\ell\geq 2

, since for

[
12
]
and for

[

]
the value

is known and the theorem is already established.

We will prove that

The inclusion on the
(m+1)
-st layer is obvious since

\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(m+1)}={[n]\choose m+1}.

Thus, we may and will exclude the

-st layer from the considerations below.
We begin by proving the inclusion on the
m

Lemma 13
.

We have

\mathcal{F}^{(m)}\subset\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(m)}.

Proof.

We argue indirectly. By Lemma
11
we have
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(m)})<\ell
. Assume that

\mathcal{F}^{(m)}\not\subset\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(m)}.

It is easy to check that for shifted
\mathcal{F}
this implies that
\mathcal{F}^{(m)}
is not isomorphic to a subfamily of
\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(m)}
. Moreover, we have

n\geq sm\geq\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\ell m>(2+\varepsilon)\ell m.

Therefore, for
s\geq s_{0}(\varepsilon)
we may apply Theorem
6
and obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{(m)}|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(m)}(n,\ell)|-{n-m-\ell+1\choose m-1}+1

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(m)}|-{n-m-\ell+1\choose m-1}+1.

(21)

Applying the moreover part of Lemma
11
to
\mathcal{F}^{(i)}
for
i<m
, we get

i\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(i)})>m\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(i)})+\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(i)})-\ell,

Thus, for

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(\leq m+1)}|-|\mathcal{F}^{(\leq m+1)}|

In the first inequality, we used the fact that

In the final step of the proof, we will need a simple observation about cross-dependent families. We say that families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
are
cross-dependent
if there is no
s
-matching
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}\}
such that
F_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
for all
i\in[s]
.

Lemma 14
.

Let
n\geq sk
and let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{s}\subset{[n]\choose k}
be cross-dependent. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{s}|\mathcal{F}_{i}|\leq(s-1){n\choose k}.

(23)

Note that the bound in (
23
) is best possible, since it is attained by taking
\mathcal{F}_{i}={[n]\choose k}
for
i\in[s-1]
and
\mathcal{F}_{s}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}\}
be a random collection of pairwise disjoint sets from
{[n]\choose k}
. The condition
n\geq sk
ensures that such collections exist. Let

\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\mathds{1}\{F_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{i}\}.

On the one hand, we have
\xi\leq s-1
, since
\mathcal{F}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
are cross-dependent. On the other hand,

\mathbb{E}\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{i}|}{{n\choose k}}.

Combining these bounds, we obtain (
23
).
∎

Lemma 15

For all

\mathcal{F}^{(i)}\subset\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)^{(i)}.

We argue indirectly. Assume that there exists
