Safety of a feed additive consisting monensin sodium (Elancoban® G200) for chickens for fattening and chickens reared for laying (Elanco GmbH)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Giovanna Martelli, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Georges Bories, Pier Sandro Cocconcelli, Jürgen Gropp, Guido Rychen, Jaume Galobart, Maria Vittoria Vettori, Rosella Brozzi, Orsolya Holczknecht, Jordi Ortuño, Alberto Navarro‐Villa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the characterisation of the production strain and on the safety for the target species of monensin sodium (Elancoban® G200). The current application is a follow‐up of the re‐evaluation application of monensin sodium, intended for use as a coccidiostat in chickens for fattening at 100–125 mg monensin sodium/kg complete feed, chickens reared for laying at 100–120 mg monensin sodium/kg complete feed and in turkeys at 60–100 mg monensin sodium/kg complete feed. Based on the new data provided, the FEEDAP Panel confirms that the production strain belongs to a new, not yet described species and will be referred to as Streptomyces sp. NRRL B‐67924. Based on the available data, the FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the safety of the additive for the chickens for fattening at the currently authorised maximum level (i.e. 125 mg monensin sodium/kg complete feed). However, the Panel considers that monensin sodium from Elancoban® G200 is safe at a maximum concentration of 100 mg/kg complete feed for chickens for fattening. The same conclusion is extended for chickens reared for laying.

ASSESSMENT

Elancoban® G200 is a feed additive containing monensin sodium, authorised for the prevention of coccidiosis in chickens for fattening (100–125 mg/kg complete feed), chickens reared for laying (100–120 mg/kg complete feed up to 16 weeks of age) and turkeys (60–100 mg/kg complete feed up to 16 weeks of age) with a withdrawal period of 1 day.

In its 2019 re‐evaluation, the FEEDAP Panel could not conclude on the safety of the additive for the target species, consumer, user and environment due to uncertainties related to the production strain, and on the safety for chickens for fattening based on the available tolerance studies. In addition, efficacy for turkeys could not be established (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019).

In a subsequent follow‐up opinion (2023), the FEEDAP Panel concluded that Elancoban® G200 was safe for turkeys under the authorised conditions of use but could not conclude on the safety for chickens for fattening or chickens reared for laying. Moreover, the identification of the production strain could only be established at genus level (Streptomyces sp.).

The applicant provided additional data to address these data gaps, which also included new information/data on the composition of the active substance monensin.

Characterisation

Characterisation of the production strain

The active substance monensin sodium is produced by fermentation with a non‐genetically modified strain of Streptomyces sp. which is deposited in the Agricultural Research Culture collection with the deposit number NRRL B‐67924 (in‐house identifier 730.20). The production strain was fully characterised in a previous opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023). Regarding the identification of the production strain, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that the identification can only be established at genus level (i.e. Streptomyces sp.) and that the production strain may belong to a new species within the genus Streptomyces.

Characterisation of the active substance

In its former opinion, the FEEDAP Panel reported that monensin sodium contains four different chemical forms identified as monensin A, B, C and D (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019). The specifications were established based on the activity of the four forms calculated by multiplying the chemical concentration with their relative biopotencies determined against Enterococcus faecium. Accordingly, the applicant proposed that the specification of the additive is not less than 90% activity monensin A and not less than 95% activity monensin A + B. The FEEDAP Panel also noted that the calculated percentage of biopotency contributions (97.6%, 1.1%, 0.4% and 0.9% for monensin A, B, C and D, respectively) was similar to the chemical composition by mass (94.7, 4.0, 0.3, 0.6).

Safety for the target species

In its opinion on the re‐evaluation of Elancoban® G200 (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019), the FEEDAP Panel could not conclude on the safety of the highest authorised dietary concentration of monensin sodium (125 mg/kg) for chickens for fattening and derive a margin of safety, as none of the studies submitted showed full compliance with the requirements of Regulation (EC) No 429/2008. Subsequently, the applicant provided two new tolerance studies, one in chickens for fattening and another one in turkeys for fattening that were assessed by the FEEDAP Panel in 2023 (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023). The FEEDAP Panel concluded that the additive is safe for turkeys under the currently authorised conditions of use. However, no safe level of monensin sodium for chickens for fattening could be identified due to a dose‐related reduction of the average daily gain and the final body weight starting at 125 mg/kg feed.

In the current submission, the applicant provided two additional studies in chickens for fattening to support the safety of the additive.

The first study 11 was not further considered as it presented several shortcomings (e.g. no overdose group, lack of relevant endpoints for safety).

In a second study, a total of 1000 one‐day‐old male Ross 308 chickens were distributed in pens and randomly allocated to one out of five groups (8 pens/group; 25 birds/pen). 12 The experiment followed a randomised complete block design, with pens distributed over four rooms (2 blocks/room). Birds underwent a two‐phase feeding programme (starter 1–14 days; grower 15–35 days). The diets were based on wheat, soybean meal and soy oil. The experimental diets were either not supplemented (control) or supplemented with Elancoban® 200G 13 to provide 100 (0.8× maximum authorised use level), 125 (1×), 150 (1.2×) or 200 (1.6×) mg monensin sodium/kg complete feed. The concentration of the additive in feed was determined analytically reaching levels slightly below the intended values (i.e. 87%–95%; Table 2). Feeds were offered in crumble (starter) or pelleted (grower) form and birds had ad libitum access to feed and water. Mortality and general health were monitored daily, and dead or culled birds were weighted and necropsied. Body weight and feed intake were recorded at 1, 14 and 35 days per pen basis. Average weight gain and feed to gain ratio was calculated. On days 35, 36 and 37, blood samples were taken from five randomly selected birds per pen (40 birds/treatment) for haematology and clinical blood chemistry analysis. 14 At the same time, the same birds were killed and subjected to necropsy; selected organs 15 were grossly examined, weighed 16 and samples stored for histopathological examination (when organs were affected).

The data were statistically analysed as a complete randomised block design with the pen as the experimental unit. Mortality and necropsy gross findings were analysed by a generalised linear model (logit link function) and a binomial error distribution (Fisher's exact test was conducted when GLIMMIX failed to converge). The other parameters were analysed by linear mixed models (GLMM). In both models, the treatment was considered the fixed effect and room, block(room) and treatment × room were considered as random effects. Linear contrasts and/or Dunnett's multiple comparison test were used to compare each level of monensin sodium to the control. Significance was set at p < 0.05.

Birds of the control group performed as expected according to the production standards for this breed (Table 2). The supplementation with monensin sodium led to a dose‐related decrease in body weight and daily weight gain of birds. This resulted in lower body weight and average daily gain of the two overdose groups (1.2× and 1.6×) relative to the control group; however, the group receiving monensin sodium at the maximum authorised level (1×) did not show significant reductions in these traits relative to the control group. No statistical differences were observed in feed intake or feed‐to‐gain ratio among the experimental groups.

Regarding the organ weights, lower absolute heart and kidney weights were observed in the 1.6× group relative to the control; however, when expressed relative to body weight, no differences were found between the experimental groups.

Macroscopic observations and histopathological analysis did not reveal treatment‐related lesions for any of the organs under evaluation.

Monensin‐treated groups showed no statistical differences compared to the control for most of the haematology and blood chemistry parameters under evaluation. Alkaline phosphatase levels were reduced in all treated groups compared to the control. None of the above‐mentioned differences are considered adverse.

Synopsis on the safety of monensin sodium for chickens for fattening

In its 2023 opinion, the FEEDAP Panel could not identify a safe level of monensin sodium from Elancoban® G200 due to a dose‐related reduction in average daily gain and final body weight starting at 125 mg/kg complete feed; in addition, bird performance was below the objectives of the strain.

In the new tolerance study, bird performance was overall in line with the objectives of the strain. However, relevant limitations were identified that may have reduced the sensitivity of the study to detect adverse effects, including the limited number of replicates (8 pens per group), the high variability of the feed‐to‐gain ratio and uncertainties related to the actual additive concentrations, which were marginally below the intended levels. Despite the apparent improvement observed in the 100 mg/kg group relative to the control, a dose‐related decline in body weight and average daily gain was again observed in the other treated groups. While statistically significant differences relative to the control group were detected only at 150 mg monensin sodium/kg complete feed, a worsening of these performance parameters was already evident at 125 mg/kg complete feed.

Overall, the results of the new tolerance study do not fully remove the uncertainties identified in the previous assessment. The dose‐related impairment of performance observed from 125 mg monensin sodium/kg complete feed onwards, whether statistically significant or numerical, is consistent with earlier evidence and indicates that adverse effects on performance cannot be excluded at this level.

Therefore, taking into account the results of all available tolerance studies and the associated uncertainties, the FEEDAP Panel considers 100 mg monensin sodium/kg complete feed to be the maximum safe level for chickens for fattening. A margin of safety cannot be established. This conclusion is extended to chickens reared for laying.

Conclusions on the safety for the target species

The FEEDAP Panel considers that monensin sodium from Elancoban® 200G is safe at a maximum concentration of 100 mg/kg complete feed for chickens for fattening and chickens reared for laying.

Post‐market monitoring

Field monitoring of Eimeria spp. resistance to monensin sodium should be undertaken, preferably during the latter part of the period of authorisation.

CONCLUSIONS

The new data provided related to the production strain support previous conclusions from the FEEDAP Panel that the production strain belongs to a new and not yet described species. Therefore, the production strain will be referred to as Streptomyces sp. NRRL B‐67924.

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the introduction of the modification in the manufacturing process does not significantly impact the composition of the active substance monensin sodium. The specifications expressed in terms of activity cannot be applied to the new data in the absence of information on relative biopotency among all the forms of monensin present in the additive.

Based on the data available, the FEEDAP Panel could not conclude on the safety of the additive for the chickens for fattening at the currently maximum authorised level (i.e. 125 mg monensin sodium/kg complete feed). However, the Panel considers that monensin sodium from Elancoban® 200G is safe at a maximum concentration of 100 mg/kg complete feed for chickens for fattening and chickens reared for laying.

RECOMMENDATIONS AND/OR REMARKS

The panel recommends that specifications for monensin forms should be based on the mass% instead of the activity.
