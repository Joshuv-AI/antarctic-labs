Computational investigation and machine learning prediction of nanoparticle effects on hemodynamics in patient-specific aortic aneurysms

Mohammad Abdul Karim Khan, Kazi Ekramul Hoque, Mohammad Osman Gani, Nayema Islam Nima, Mohammed Abdul Hannan

ABSTRACT
This study presents a novel computational framework for nanoparticle-assisted drug delivery in abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) by characterizing patient-specific hemodynamics under realistic physiological conditions with integrating an artificial neural network (ANN). The model incorporates silver (Ag) nanoparticles within a fully transient, multiphase computational fluid dynamics (CFD) environment, where pulsatile blood flow is imposed using advanced user-defined function. Blood is modeled as a non-Newtonian fluid to capture its shear-dependent rheological behavior, enabling a more accurate representation of vascular flow dynamics. High-resolution patient-specific geometries are developed to investigate four distinct cases, including healthy and AAA arteries with varying geometrical complexities and multiple daughter vessels, both with and without nanoparticle infusion. The analysis focuses on key clinically relevant hemodynamic indicators, including velocity, normal and tangential wall shear stress (WSS). The results indicate that arterial geometry and blood rheology play a dominant role in governing flow behavior, with the most complex vascular configuration exhibiting the strongest hemodynamic response. Among the investigated cases, model 3 shows the highest velocity, decreasing by 52.94% compared to the healthy artery (model 1), while models 2 and 4 exhibit decreases of 82.35% and 54.90%, respectively at left iliac. The introduction of Ag nanoparticles enhances WSS across all models by 34.72%, 47.32%, 51.92%, and 39.67%, respectively, indicating improved flow modulation and more favorable conditions for drug transport and vascular interaction. Flow visualization reveals distinct regions of acceleration, recirculation, and nanoparticle accumulation, which are critical for understanding localized delivery mechanisms. The ANN confirming the robustness, accuracy, and predictive reliability of the developed models. Finally, the proposed framework establishes a clear relationship between arterial morphology, non-Newtonian blood behavior, and nanoparticle-driven hemodynamic modulation, offering valuable insights for optimizing patient-specific, nanoparticle-based therapeutic strategies in cardiovascular disease management.

Introduction Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a serious and potentially fatal condition characterized by the unusual expansion of the abdominal aorta. Currently, there is no established pharmacological treatment proven to stop or slow its progression effectively. Management often focuses on monitoring and surgical intervention when necessary. This underscores the urgent need for the development of more effective therapeutic strategies capable of delivering drugs directly to the affected regions within the arterial system. In this context, nanoparticle-based drug delivery systems have emerged as a highly promising approach due to their can deliver drugs specifically to target cells, enhance bioavailability, manage the release of drugs and lessen systemic adverse effects. Owing to their tunable physicochemical properties, enhanced permeability, and ability to facilitate targeted delivery, nanoparticles can improve drug bioavailability while minimizing systemic side effects. Consequently, these systems hold significant potential for advancing the treatment of vascular diseases by enabling precise and controlled drug delivery to diseased arterial sites. [ 2 ] illustrated that regarding the mean flow velocity in the segment that was modeled, a larger aneurysm led to the formation of a vortex within the aneurysm and a decrease in the flow velocity surrounding the aneurysm. In the idealized angulated neck AAA model, velocity distributions revealed notable distinctions between the two scenarios (stationary and transient) [ 3 ]. The simulation results [ 4 , 5 ] indicated that aneurysm angulation generates regions of high and low wall shear stress, along with turbulent flow and recirculation within the sac, potentially contributing to aortic wall weakening. Visual analysis revealed [ 6 , 7 ] that regions of low wall shear stress (WSS) corresponded to high wall pressure (WP). Although velocity and blood pressure influenced both WSS and WP, the central contour consistently indicated potential rupture sites. The risk of AAA rupture was mainly assessed by aneurysm diameter; intervention was typically recommended when it exceeds 5.5 cm or shows rapid expansion [ 8 , 9 ]. ILT is associated with regions of low velocity and TAWSS, and high OSI, ECAP, and RRT values [ 10 – 13 ]. Researchers anticipated [ 14 , 15 ] that advances in nanotechnology, gene editing, precision medicine, and AI will enable engineered nanomedicine to enhance drug targeting, prolong circulation, and achieve controlled release more effectively. Non-spherical particles exhibit enhanced interactions with the arterial wall, promoting increased collision and adhesion to the target site [ 20 ]. This study investigates nanoparticle-assisted transport in a patient specific abdominal aortic model with aneurysm, incorporating realistic hemodynamics. Silver (Ag) nanoparticles are introduced to evaluate their effects on WSS, velocity, and pressure, while comparisons are made between healthy and diseased arteries. A transient multiphase CFD approach with UDF-based boundary conditions and non-Newtonian models is employed. The results provide insight into nanoparticle transport and deposition, supporting the potential of Ag nanoparticles for enhanced, site-specific therapy with reduced systemic effects. This study provides understanding of nanoparticle-assisted blood flow by incorporating nanoparticles into a physiologically realistic abdominal artery model with aneurysm. The inclusion of complex geometries reveals how vascular irregularities and flow disturbances govern nanoparticle transport, deposition, and accumulation. A transient multiphase CFD framework with pulsatile boundary conditions enhances predictive accuracy. Finally, the work strengthens the basis for patient-specific, nanoparticle-based drug delivery strategies in abdominal artery disease. Computational methodology The Navier–Stokes equations underpin the simulation of blood flow in patient-specific abdominal aortic geometries, capturing the transient, incompressible behavior of viscous fluids under pressure and viscous forces. Coupled with the continuity equation, they ensure accurate representation of mass conservation in the flow field. Boundary conditions for the computational domain This study considers blood flow under the assumption of laminar, incompressible motion for the non-Newtonian case. The Carreau–Yasuda model is adopted due to its well-established capability to capture the shear-thinning behavior of blood over a wide range of shear rates, as demonstrated in previous studies by [ 52 , 53 ]. Its combination of predictive accuracy, numerical stability, and computational efficiency makes it particularly suitable for simulating complex arterial hemodynamics, including aneurysmal and pulsatile flow conditions. The simulations are carried out in a transient (time-dependent) framework to replicate physiological cardiac cycles. A clinical study of abdominal artery hemodynamics reported an average blood flow velocity of 0.1798 ± 0.0566 m/s [ 54 ] based on digital tracing angiography and nanoparticles size is 40 nm and concentration is 1% volume fraction of silver. In the present simulation, these clinical findings are incorporated, and an inlet velocity of 0.16 m/s and outlet constant pressure is prescribed to represent physiological conditions. The aorta is defined as the inlet boundary, while all branching vessels are assigned as outlets, as summarized in Table 2 . The user defined function (UDF) explores the time-dependent velocity profiles at the point of entrance and the corresponding outlet conditions, as depicted in Fig 3 . A pulsatile inlet velocity profile, representative of the human heartbeat [ 56 , 57 ], is prescribed using a UDF, as illustrated in Fig 3 [ 55 ]. At all outlet boundaries, a constant pressure condition is imposed. The arterial wall is treated as rigid with a no-slip boundary condition, ensuring zero fluid velocity at the wall interface. For the investigation of nanoparticle-based drug delivery, the Eulerian multiphase formulation is employed. In this framework, blood is modeled as the primary continuous phase, while silver (Ag) nanoparticles are considered as the dispersed secondary phase. This approach, commonly referred to as Multiphase Computational Fluid Dynamics (MCFD), enables a detailed representation of particle–blood interactions within the arterial domain. Grid generation and mesh sensitivity assessment The FEM is utilized for spatial discretization due to its robustness, accuracy, and effectiveness in handling complex geometries, particularly the diseased patient specific artery. The computational domain is discretized using an unstructured tetrahedral mesh, which is further refined to improve numerical resolution. A 10-node quadratic tetrahedral element is adopted to represent the three-dimensional domain in Fig 4 , allowing higher-order interpolation and more precise evaluation of velocity, pressure, and other key hemodynamic variables within patient-specific artery. To ensure the reliability of the numerical solution, a mesh independence study is performed. Results and discussion This study investigates key hemodynamic parameters associated with varying aneurysm severities in a patient-specific abdominal artery to assess their influence on aneurysm growth and rupture risk. By analyzing flow behavior within and around the aneurysmal region, it evaluates the mechanical factors governing stability and progression. The findings establish correlations between hemodynamic conditions and aneurysm severity, enhancing the understanding of their combined role in aneurysm development. Such hemodynamic alterations may have important implications for aneurysm progression, wall stress development, and the assessment of rupture risk. Pressure difference in healthy and diseases AAA Hemodynamic pressure distributions provide critical insight into the flow behavior of nanoparticle-enriched blood within diseased abdominal arteries, revealing the complex interactions among vascular geometry, fluid properties, and local biomechanical forces. Fig 10a - 10d depicts the pressure distributions for all investigated abdominal aortic models under both conventional blood flow and nanoparticle-enhanced conditions. In all cases, the incorporation of nanoparticles leads to a noticeable increase in pressure, primarily due to the higher effective density and viscosity of the nanofluid, which elevate the overall flow resistance. For the healthy artery (Model 1), the maximum pressure rises from 1410 Pa to 1857.4 Pa following nanoparticle addition. In the aneurysmal cases, substantially greater pressure levels are observed. Model 2 exhibits an increase from 9553 Pa to 10,640 Pa, while Model 3 records the highest pressure among all configurations, rising from 12,540 Pa to 13,737 Pa. Model 4, despite possessing the largest aneurysmal dilation, shows a comparatively lower peak pressure, increasing from 8730 Pa to 9298 Pa. A pronounced pressure gradient is evident near the inlet region in all diseased models, where the incoming flow undergoes rapid deceleration upon entering the expanded lumen. During the development of the ANN models, particular attention was given to achieving reliable generalization beyond the training data. Overfitting may occur when an ANN captures the specific characteristics of the training dataset rather than the underlying flow relationships, resulting in reduced predictive accuracy for unseen data [ 69 , 70 ]. To minimize this effect, model selection was based on two criteria: low prediction errors for both the training and test datasets and a small difference between their respective errors. The number of neurons in the hidden layer was therefore selected through an iterative trial-and-error procedure, with the final architecture chosen based on its overall predictive performance and generalization capability. To ensure robust model generalization, the complete dataset is randomly partitioned into training (60%), testing (20%), and cross-validation (20%) subsets. The present study demonstrates that the integration of nanoparticle-assisted hemodynamics, non-Newtonian blood rheology, and artificial neural network modeling provides a powerful framework for analyzing flow in diseased abdominal arteries. The non-Newtonian formulation captures the complex rheological behavior of blood more realistically, while nanoparticle suspension enhances transport characteristics and may improve targeted therapeutic delivery. In addition, the artificial neural network accurately identifies hemodynamic patterns and enables rapid, non-invasive assessment of disease severity. The minimal dataset supporting the findings of this current study is provided as S1 Dataset . Collectively, these approaches establish an efficient and reliable computational strategy for advancing nanoparticle-based vascular therapies and supporting clinical decision-making in the management of abdominal arterial disorders. Limitations and future works Several limitations should be acknowledged. The blood was modeled using a simplified non-Newtonian formulation, which cannot fully represent the complex rheological behavior of blood under all physiological conditions. Although patient-specific arterial geometries were considered, the vessel walls were treated as rigid and fluid–structure interaction (FSI) was not included. The nanoparticle analysis was also limited to silver (Ag) nanoparticles; therefore, the findings cannot be directly generalized to other nanoparticle types, such as gold or magnetic nanoparticles, who’s physical and transport properties may differ considerably. Furthermore, temperature effects on blood rheology and nanoparticle transport were not considered. These factors may influence nanoparticle migration and local flow characteristics. Future studies incorporating compliant arterial walls, FSI, thermal effects, alternative nanoparticle materials, and coupled energy transport would provide a more comprehensive representation of nanoparticle-assisted blood flow under physiological conditions.
