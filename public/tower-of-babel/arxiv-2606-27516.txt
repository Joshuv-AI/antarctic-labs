Sharp Bounds for Moments of the Dedekind Zeta Function Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 11M50, 11R42, 11S40 (primary)
Nilmoni Karak, Kamalakshya Mahatab

Abstract
. Assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, we establish upper bounds of conjectural order of magnitude for shifted moments of the Dedekind zeta function associated with a finite Galois extension. This improves results of Milinovich and Turnage-Butterbaugh and extends a recent result of Hagen.



1.
Introduction

Understanding the behavior of
L
-functions on the critical line is a central problem in analytic number theory. A fundamental approach to this problem is the study of moments, which have numerous applications to non-vanishing, value distribution, and correlations of
L
-functions. In 2008, assuming the Riemann Hypothesis (RH), Soundararajan
[
23
]
obtained almost sharp upper bounds for all the positive moments of the Riemann zeta function. He showed that

\int_{0}^{T}\left|\zeta\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}\mathrm{d}t\ll_{k,\varepsilon}T(\log T)^{k^{2}+\varepsilon},

for any
k>0
and every
\varepsilon>0
.
Subsequently, Harper
[
11
]
refined the above bound by removing
\varepsilon
on the power of
\log T
and proved the upper bound of the expected order of magnitude, that is, assuming the RH, for any
k>0
, one has

\int_{0}^{T}\left|\zeta\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}\mathrm{d}t\ll_{k}T(\log T)^{k^{2}}.

(1.1)

The study of moments naturally leads to the consideration of shifted moments, which provide a better understanding of the correlations between values of
L
-functions at different points on the critical line. Also, shifted moments arise naturally in the analysis of lower-order terms and in the study of correlations predicted by random matrix theory. For the Riemann zeta function, shifted moments have been investigated by several authors. In 2011, assuming RH Chandee
[
5
]
proved that for sufficiently large
T
and every
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\prod_{i=1}^{2k}\left|\zeta\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+b_{i})\right)\right|^{a_{i}}\ll_{\bf{a},\varepsilon}T(\log T)^{(a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{2k}^{2})/4+\varepsilon}\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant 2k}\left(\min\left\{\tfrac{1}{|b_{i}-b_{j}|},\log T\right\}\right)^{\tfrac{a_{i}a_{j}}{2}},

where
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{2k})
is a sequence of positive real numbers and
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{2k})
such that
b_{i}\neq b_{j}
when
i\neq j
,
|b_{i}-b_{j}|=O(1)
, and
b_{i}=O(\log T)
. Later, assuming RH, Ng, Shen, and Wong
[
18
]
removed the
(\log T)^{\varepsilon}
in the special case where
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2})
by using Harper’s
[
11
]
method, and they established the bounds in the larger scale
|b_{1}+b_{2}|\leq T^{0.6}
. Recently, under RH, Curran
[
9
]
extended the work of Ng, Shen, and Wong
[
18
]
to arbitrary
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
and gave stronger bounds when the differences
|b_{i}-b_{j}|
are unbounded. More precisely, he proved that

\int_{0}^{T}\prod_{i=1}^{2k}\left|\zeta\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+b_{i})\right)\right|^{a_{i}}\ll_{\bf{a}}T(\log T)^{(a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{2k}^{2})/4}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2k}\left|\zeta(1+i(b_{i}-b_{j})+1/\log T)\right|^{\tfrac{a_{i}a_{j}}{2}}.

This article focuses on extending the upper bound (
1.1
) to moments of the Dedekind zeta function associated with a finite Galois extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
. The Dedekind zeta function associated to the number field
K
is defined as

\zeta_{K}(s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}}\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{a})^{s}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}}\left(1-\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{s}}\right)^{-1}

for
\Re(s)>1
, where the sum and the product are taken over all non-zero integral ideals
\mathfrak{a}
and prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, respectively. The function
\zeta_{K}(s)
is continued analytically to the whole complex plane except at the pole at
s=1
and satisfies the functional equation
\zeta_{K}(1-s)=\chi_{K}(s)\zeta_{K}(s)
, where
[
19
, pp. 254-255]

\chi_{K}(s)=(2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s))^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}|D_{K}|^{s-1/2}(\cos\pi s/2)^{r_{1}+r_{2}}(\sin\pi s/2)^{r_{2}}.

Therefore, if
K
is totally real ,
\zeta_{K}(s)
has zeros at
s=-2k
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and otherwise
\zeta_{K}(s)
has zeros at
s=-k
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. These zeros are called the trivial zeros of
\zeta_{K}(s)
. The Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) for
\zeta_{K}(s)
states that all the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta_{K}(s)
lie on the critical line
\Re(s)=1/2
. The moments of the Dedekind zeta function on the critical line have been studied extensively in the literature. It is well known that the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{K}(s)
factors as a product of Artin
L
-functions. Specifically, if
K/\mathbb{Q}
is a finite Galois extension, then

\zeta_{K}(s)=\prod_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})}}L(s,\chi)^{\chi(1)}.

Here
\widehat{\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})}
denotes the set of irreducible characters of
\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
. Furthermore,

\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})}}\chi(1)^{2}=\bigl|\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})\bigr|=[K:\mathbb{Q}].

Using this factorization and the identity above, the Langlands reciprocity conjecture predicts that, for any fixed real
k>0
,

\int_{0}^{T}\left|\zeta_{K}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}\,dt\sim C_{k,K}\,T(\log T)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]k^{2}}

(1.2)

as
T\to\infty
, where
C_{k,K}
is a positive constant depending only on
k
and
K
. A detailed discussion of this conjecture appears in the work of Heap
[
12
]
. The conjecture (
1.2
) is already known for
k=1
when
K
is a quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
K=\mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{d}]
, where
d
is a fundamental discriminant. In 1970, Motohasi

[
15
]
showed that

\int_{0}^{T}\left|\zeta_{K}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}\mathrm{d}t\sim\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}L\left(1,\chi_{d}\right)^{2}\prod_{p\mid d}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}T(\log T)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]k^{2}},

where
L(s,\chi_{d})
is the Dirichlet
L
-function associated to
\chi_{d}
, the Kronecker symbol of
d
and satisfies the factorization
\zeta_{K}(s)=\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_{d})
. In support of the above prediction (
1.2
), for the finite Galois extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
, Akbary and Fodden
[
1
]
established that for any rational number
k>0
and for sufficiently large
T
,

\int_{0}^{T}\left|\zeta_{K}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}\mathrm{d}t\gg_{K,k}T(\log T)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]k^{2}}.

Subsequently, based on the method of Radziwiłł and Soundararajan
[
20
]
, Sono
[
22
]
extended this lower bound to all real
k>1
.

On the other hand, using the method of Soundararajan
[
23
]
, Milinovich and Turnage-Butterbaugh
[
16
]
proved that, assuming the GRH, if
K
is a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, then for any fixed real
k>0
and any arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
,

\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\zeta_{K}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}\mathrm{d}t\ll_{K,k}T(\log T)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]k^{2}+\varepsilon},

(1.3)

where
T
is sufficiently large.
Recently, Hagen
[
10
]
improved this bound for finite solvable Galois extensions
K/\mathbb{Q}
, proving that, for sufficiently large
T
,

\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\zeta_{K}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}\mathrm{d}t\ll_{K,k}T(\log T)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]k^{2}}.

Thus, the corresponding sharp upper bound for all finite Galois extensions remains unknown. In this paper, we fill this gap by establishing the conjecturally optimal upper bound for a broader class of finite Galois extensions, thereby extending Hagen’s result beyond the solvable setting.

While the above results focus on moments of the Dedekind zeta function, it is natural to consider their shifted analogues. Such moments have been widely studied for the Riemann zeta function
[
5
,
9
]
and Dirichlet L-functions
[
17
,
24
]
, but remain largely unexplored in the setting of Dedekind zeta functions. The purpose of this paper is to address this problem for finite Galois extensions
K
over
\mathbb{Q}
. We have the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{2k})
and

\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{2k})
be two real
2k
-tuples satisfying
a_{j}>0
and

|b_{j}|\leqslant\frac{T}{2}

for every
1\leqslant j\leqslant 2k
, where
2k
is a fixed positive integer.
Suppose that
K
is a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Then the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for
\zeta_{K}(s)
implies that, for sufficiently large
T
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\left|\zeta_{K}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+b_{j})\right)\right|^{a_{j}}\mathrm{d}t\ll T(\log T)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}](a_{1}^{2}+\ldots+a_{2k}^{2})/4}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2k}g\left(|b_{i}-b_{j}|\right)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]\frac{a_{i}a_{j}}{2}},

where
g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is the correlation function defined by

g(x)=\begin{cases}\log T\ &\text{if}\ x\leqslant\frac{1}{\log T}\ \text{or}\ x\geqslant e^{T}\\
\frac{1}{x}\ &\text{if}\ \frac{1}{\log T}\leqslant x\leqslant 10\\
\log\log x\ &\text{if}\ 10\leqslant x\leqslant e^{T}.\end{cases}

The following corollary improves the conditional upper bound of Milinovich and Turnage-Butterbaugh
[
16
]
. Moreover, it extends Hagen’s result
[
10
]
, which was established for finite solvable Galois extensions, to any finite Galois extensions
K/\mathbb{Q}
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
K
be a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and
k>0
be any fixed real number. Then the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for
\zeta_{K}(s)
implies that, for sufficiently large
T
, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\left|\zeta_{K}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}\mathrm{d}t\ll_{K,k}T(\log T)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]k^{2}}.

Proof.

The result follows from Theorem
1.1
by putting
a_{j}=1
and
b_{j}=0
for all
j
.
∎

Conditional upper bounds for large deviation of
\zeta_{K}(1/2+it)

The study of large deviations of
\log|\zeta(1/2+it)|
is closely connected to the moments of the Riemann zeta function. Under the Riemann Hypothesis, a series of works has shown that the large deviation regime
V\asymp\log\log T
shows Gaussian-type decay and plays an important role in determining the size of high moments. Harper’s bound (
1.1
) directly gives that for
\log\log T\leq V\leq 1000\log\log T
,

\text{meas}\{t\in[T,2T]:\log|\zeta(1/2+it)|\geq V\}\ll Te^{-V^{2}/\log\log T}.

However, the Gaussian heuristic suggests that the true order should be smaller by a factor
(\log\log T)^{-1/2}
. More recently, under RH, Arguin, Bailey and Roberts
[
3
]
proved the exact bound that matches the conjectured Gaussian order of magnitude in the regime
V\sim k\log\log T
for every fixed
k>0
. Also, unconditionally, Arguin and Bailey
[
2
]
showed that the same bound for
V\sim k\log\log T
with
0<k<2
. These results motivate the study of analogous large deviation bounds for the Dedekind zeta function.

As an application, we study the distribution of large values of the Dedekind zeta function on the critical line. The following corollary gives an upper bound on the measure of level sets of
\zeta_{K}(1/2+it)
in the interval
[T,2T]
for values of the order
\log\log T
, conditionally on GRH.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
K
be a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and
V\asymp\sqrt{\log\log T}
. Then, assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for
\zeta_{K}(s)
, we have

\frac{1}{T}\textnormal{meas}\left\{t\in[T,2T]:{\log\left|\zeta_{K}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|}\geqslant V\sqrt{\tfrac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}{2}\log\log T}\right\}\ll e^{-\frac{V^{2}}{2}},

for sufficiently large
T
.

Proof.

By Chebyshev’s inequality and Corollary
1.2
, for any fixed
k>0
, we have

\displaystyle\text{meas}\left\{t\in[T,2T]:{\left|\zeta_{K}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|}\geqslant(\log T)^{\sqrt{\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}{2}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant(\log T)^{-2k\sqrt{\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}{2}}}\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\zeta_{K}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ll T(\log T)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]k^{2}-2k\sqrt{\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}{2}}}.

Choosing
k=1/\sqrt{2[K:\mathbb{Q}]}
, we find the above bound is indeed
Te^{-V^{2}/2}
.
∎

It would be interesting to determine whether the bound in Corollary
1.3
can be sharpened by adapting the argument of Arguin, Bailey and Roberts
[
2
,
3
]
.

Coefficients of Dedekind zeta function in short intervals

Let
K
be a number field and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be its ring of integers. For a positive integer
n
, let
r_{K}(n)
denote the number of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of norm
n
. Then for
\Re\left(s\right)>1,
the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{K}(s)
can be written as

\zeta_{K}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r_{K}(n)}{n^{s}}.

(1.4)

Since
\zeta_{K}(s)
has a simple pole at
s=1
, its residue at
s=1
is given by

\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_{K}(s)=\frac{2^{r_{1}}(2\pi)^{r_{2}}h_{K}R_{K}}{w_{K}\sqrt{|d_{K}|}},

(1.5)

where
r_{1}
denotes the number of real embeddings of
K
,
r_{2}
the number of pairs of complex embeddings,
h_{K}
the class number of
K
,
R_{K}
the regulator of
K
,
w_{K}
the number of roots of unity contained in
K
, and
d_{K}
the discriminant of
K
.
Landau’s
[
14
]
classical mean-value estimate for the arithmetic function
r_{K}(n)
is

\sum_{n\leqslant x}r_{K}(n)=\frac{2^{r_{1}}(2\pi)^{r_{2}}h_{K}R_{K}}{w_{K}\sqrt{|d_{K}|}}x+O\!\left(x^{1-2/([K:\mathbb{Q}]+1)}\right).

If
K
is a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, Theorem
1.1
gives an improvement of the bound in
[
16
, Theorem 1.4]
, which studies the distribution of
r_{K}(n)
in short intervals assuming the GRH for
\zeta_{K}(s)
.

We have the following conditional estimate for the variance of
r_{K}(n)
in short interval.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
K
be a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and
\delta=\delta(t)
be a positive and increasing function satisfying
\delta\rightarrow\infty
and
\delta/t\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Then, assuming Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for
\zeta_{K}(s)
, we have

\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\sum_{t<n\leqslant t+\delta}r_{K}(n)-\frac{2^{r_{1}}(2\pi)^{r_{2}}h_{K}R_{K}}{w_{K}\sqrt{|d_{K}|}}\delta\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\ll_{K}\delta T(\log T)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]},

for sufficiently large
T
. Moreover, for any function
\psi(t)\to\infty
,

\sum_{t<n\leqslant t+\delta}r_{K}(n)=\frac{2^{r_{1}}(2\pi)^{r_{2}}h_{K}R_{K}}{w_{K}\sqrt{|d_{K}|}}\delta+O_{K}\!\left(\sqrt{\delta}(\log t)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]/2}\psi(t)\right),

for all
t\in[T,2T]
outside a set of measure
O_{K}\!\left(T/\psi(T)^{2}\right)
.

Proof.

The proof of the first estimate follows exactly as in the proof of
[
16
, Theorem 1.4]
, except that in the last step one uses the bound in Corollary
1.2
instead of the corresponding bound used there. To prove the asymptotic formula,
let

E_{\delta}(t):=\sum_{t<n\leq t+\delta}r_{K}(n)-\frac{2^{r_{1}}(2\pi)^{r_{2}}h_{K}R_{K}}{w_{K}\sqrt{|d_{K}|}}\delta.

By Chebyshev’s inequality and the first part,

\mathrm{meas}\Bigl\{t\in[T,2T]:|E_{\delta}(t)|>\sqrt{\delta}(\log T)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]/2}\psi(T)\Bigr\}\leq\frac{\int_{T}^{2T}|E_{\delta}(t)|^{2}dt}{\delta(\log T)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\psi(T)^{2}}\ll_{K}\frac{T}{\psi(T)^{2}},

which proves the result.
∎

Our bound matches the order of magnitude predicted by the celebrated recipe of Conrey, Farmer, Keating, Rubinstein, and Snaith
[
7
,
8
]
. Theorem
1.1
shows that how
\zeta_{K}(1/2+it)
and
\zeta_{K}(1/2+i(t+b))
are correlated for
T\leqslant t\leqslant 2T
and
b\leqslant T/2
. In particular, it predicts that, on average, the two values behave as fully correlated when
|b|\leqslant 1/\log T
, while for
|b|>1/\log T
the correlation weakens and is measured by the function
g(b)
.

Although our proof follows the general framework of Harper
[
11
]
, a crucial new ingredient is the Chebotarev density theorem. A direct application of Harper’s method to
\zeta_{K}(s)
leads to the upper bound

T(\log T)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}k^{2}}
,
without requiring
K/\mathbb{Q}
to be Galois. By exploiting the splitting behaviour of primes in finite Galois extensions via the Chebotarev density theorem, we improve the exponent from
[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}k^{2}
to the conjecturally optimal value
[K:\mathbb{Q}]k^{2}
. This is the key idea in the paper and is essential for obtaining the sharp bound.

It would be interesting to extend Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
to arbitrary number fields.While Harper’s method applies more generally, our proof of the sharp bound relies heavily on the Galois structure of the field through the Chebotarev density theorem. Consequently, the non-Galois case appears to require new ideas and remains open.

Preliminary Tools

The following auxiliary results will be required repeatedly in the proofs that follow.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N=p_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{\beta_{r}}
, where the
p_{i}
’s are distinct primes and all
\beta_{i}
are positive integers. Then, for a large
T
,

\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{i=1}^{r}(\cos(t\log p_{i}))^{\beta_{i}}\mathrm{d}t=T\Upsilon(n)+O(N),

where
\Upsilon(n)=0
if any one of the exponent
\beta_{i}
is odd, and otherwise

\Upsilon(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{2^{\beta_{i}}}\binom{\beta_{i}}{\beta_{i}/2}.

Proof.

This Lemma is due to Harper
[
11
, Proposition 2.5]
and is a variant of Radziwiłł’s
[
21
, Lemma 4]
result on Selberg’s central limit theorem.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
a(n)
be a sequence of complex numbers and define
D(s)=\sum_{p\leqslant X}a(p)p^{-s}
with
s=\sigma+it
. Suppose
t\in[T,2T]
is such that
|D(s)|\leqslant V
. We then have

\exp\left(2\Re D(s)\right)=\left(1+O\left(e^{-9V}\right)\right)\left|\sum_{j\leqslant 10V}\frac{(D(s))^{j}}{j!}\right|^{2}.

Proof.

The proof follows from
[
13
, Eq. 37]
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis is true. Then, for any
\alpha>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{\cos(\alpha\log p)}{p}

\displaystyle=\log\left|\zeta\left(1+1/\log x+i\alpha\right)\right|+O(1)

\displaystyle\leqslant\begin{cases}\log\log x+O(1)&\text{if}\ \alpha\leqslant 1/\log x\ \text{or}\ \alpha\geqslant e^{x},\\
\log(1/\alpha)+O(1)&\text{if}\ 1/\log x\leqslant\alpha\leqslant 10,\\
\log\log\log\alpha+O(1)&\text{if}\ 10\leqslant\alpha\leq e^{x}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof can be found in
[
24
, Lemma 2]
.
∎

Conditional bounds of
\log\left|\zeta_{K}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|

Taking logarithmic differentiation in (
1.4
),
for
\Re(s)>1
, we have

\frac{\zeta_{K}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\zeta_{K}}(s)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda_{K}(n)}{n^{s}}.

Since
\zeta_{K}(s)
satisfies Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture, we have
|\Lambda_{K}(n)|\leqslant[K:\mathbb{Q}]\Lambda(n)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for
\zeta_{K}(s)
and
\lambda_{0}=0.4912\ldots
denote the unique real number satisfying
e^{-\lambda_{0}}=\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{0}^{2}/2
. Let
T
be sufficiently large with
T\leqslant t\leqslant 2T
Then, for all
\lambda_{0}\leqslant\lambda\leqslant\log x/2
and
\log x\geqslant 2
,

\log\left|\zeta_{K}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|\leqslant\Re\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{\Lambda_{K}(n)}{n^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{\log x}+it}\log n}\frac{\log\left(x/n\right)}{\log x}+\frac{(1+\lambda)}{2}\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log T}{\log x}+O\!\left(\tfrac{1}{\log x}\right),

where the implied constant in the error term depends only on
K
.

Proof.

This can be obtained as a consequence of Theorem 2.1 of Chandee
[
4
]
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for
\zeta_{K}(s)
, and let
T
be large. Then, for any
T\leqslant t\leqslant 2T
and
2\leqslant x\leqslant T^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\log\left|\zeta_{K}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|\leqslant\Re\left(\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{\Lambda_{K}(p)}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\log x}+it}\log p}\frac{\log\left(x/p\right)}{\log x}+\sum_{p\leqslant\min\{\sqrt{x},\ \log T\}}\frac{\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})}{2p^{1+2it}\log p}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt+\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log T}{\log x}+O(1),

where
p
denotes the rational prime.

Proof.

Choosing
\lambda=1
in Lemma
3.4
, we see that

\log\left|\zeta_{K}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|\leqslant\Re\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{\Lambda_{K}(n)}{n^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\log x}+it}\log n}\frac{\log\left(x/n\right)}{\log x}+\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log T}{\log x}+O\!\left(\tfrac{1}{\log x}\right).

Since
|\Lambda_{K}(n)|\leqslant[K:\mathbb{Q}]\Lambda(n)
and
\log(x/n)/(n^{1/\log x}\log x)\leqslant 1
for
n\leqslant x
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{p^{j}\leqslant x}\\
\ j\geqslant 3\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda_{K}(p^{j})}{(p^{j})^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\log x}+it}\log p^{j}}\frac{\log\left(x/p^{j}\right)}{\log x}\ll[K:\mathbb{Q}]\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{p^{j}\leqslant x}\\
\ j\geqslant 3\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{jp^{j/2}}\ll 1.

Hence the lemma follows by the same justification as in
[
11
, Proposition 1]
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for
\zeta_{K}(s)

Lemma 3.7

Chebotarev Density Theorem

Let
K
be a number field and
L
be a finite Galois extension of
K
with Galois group
G=\textnormal{Gal}(L/K)
. If
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal of
K
which is unramified in
L
, then
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]
denotes the conjugacy class of Frobenius automorphisms associated with the prime ideals of
L
lying above
\mathfrak{p}
. For each conjugacy class
C
of
G
, we define

\displaystyle\pi_{C}\left(x,L/K\right):=\#\left\{\mathfrak{p}:\mathfrak{p}\ \text{unramified in}\ L,\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]=C,N_{K/Q}(\mathfrak{p})\leqslant x\right\}.

Then the Chebotarev density theorem
[
6
]
implies that

\pi_{C}\left(x,L/K\right)\sim\frac{|C|}{|G|}\text{Li}(x),\ \ \ \ \ \text{as}\ x\to\infty,

(3.3)

where Li
(x)
is the logarithmic integral

\text{Li}(x)=\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\log t}\sim\frac{x}{\log x}\sim\sum_{p\leqslant x}1,\ \ \ \ \ \text{as}\ x\to\infty.

Now let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension. Applying the Chebotarev density theorem (
3.3
) to the extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
and the identity conjugacy class
C=\{1\}
, we get

\pi_{\{1\}}(x,K/\mathbb{Q})=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\operatorname{Li}(x)+O(x^{1/2}\log x).

Since a rational prime
p
has Frobenius class
\{1\}
if and only if
p
splits completely in
K
and using prime number theorem, it follows that as
x\to\infty
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\ \text{splits completely}\end{subarray}}1=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{p\leqslant x}1+O(xe^{-c\sqrt{\log x}}).

(3.4)

Using the above asymptotic formula (
3.4
), we will prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
K
be a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. For every fixed
\alpha\geqslant 0
, there exist a constant
c>0
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\ \text{splits completely}\end{subarray}}\frac{\cos(\alpha\log p)}{p}=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{\cos(\alpha\log p)}{p}+O_{\alpha}(1),\ \ \ \ \ \text{as}\ x\to\infty,

and in particular,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\ \text{splits completely}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{1}{p}+O(1),\ \ \ \ \ \text{as}\ x\to\infty.

Proof.

Let

\pi_{\text{split}}(x):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\ \text{splits completely}\end{subarray}}1\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \ \pi(x):=\sum_{p\leqslant x}1.

The formula (
3.4
) can be written as

\pi_{\text{split}}(x)=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\pi(x)+R(x),

(3.5)

where
R(x)\ll xe^{-c\sqrt{\log x}}
for some
c>0
.
Using (
3.5
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\ \text{splits completely}\end{subarray}}\frac{\cos(\alpha\log p)}{p}

\displaystyle=\pi_{\text{split}}(x)\frac{\cos(\alpha\log x)}{x}+\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi_{\text{split}}(t)}{t^{2}}(\cos(\alpha\log t)+\alpha\sin(\alpha\log t))\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\left(\pi(x)\frac{\cos(\alpha\log x)}{x}+\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\pi(t)}{t^{2}}(\cos(\alpha\log t)+\alpha\sin(\alpha\log t))\mathrm{d}t\right)+E(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{\cos(\alpha\log p)}{p}+E(x),

where

\displaystyle E(x)

\displaystyle=R(x)\frac{\cos(\alpha\log x)}{x}+\alpha\int_{2}^{x}\frac{R(t)}{t^{2}}(\cos(\alpha\log t)+\alpha\sin(\alpha\log t))\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ll e^{-c\sqrt{\log x}}+(1+\alpha)\int_{2}^{x}\frac{1}{te^{c\sqrt{\log t}}}\mathrm{d}t=O(1).

This completes the proof.
∎

The above lemma is a consequence of the Chebotarev density theorem. It gives a saving of one factor of
[K:\mathbb{Q}]
in the following proposition, which is a key ingredient in the proof of our main theorem.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
K
be a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
p
denote a rational prime. With the above notations, we have

\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{|\mathfrak{h}(p)\Lambda_{K}(p)|^{2}}{p\log^{2}p}=[K:\mathbb{Q}]\sum_{i=1}^{2k}\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{4}\log\log x+[K:\mathbb{Q}]\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2k}\frac{a_{i}a_{j}}{2}\log\left|\zeta\left(1+1/\log x+i\alpha\right)\right|+O(1).

Proof.

For ideal
\mathfrak{a}
in the ring of integer
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, we define

\Lambda_{K}(\mathfrak{a})=\begin{cases}\log N(\mathfrak{p})&\ \text{if}\ \mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{p}^{m},\ \text{for}\ m\geqslant 1\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
N(\mathfrak{a})
is the norm of
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{p}
is the prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{K}.

Then for positive integer
n
,

\Lambda_{K}(n)=\sum_{N(\mathfrak{p}^{m})=n}\log N(\mathfrak{p}).

Since
p
is a rational prime, In
\mathcal{O}_{K},\ p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{e_{1}}\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}^{e_{r}},

where the
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
are the distinct prime ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
lying above
p
with norm
N(\mathfrak{p}_{i})=p^{f_{i}}
. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{r}e_{i}f_{i}=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
.
Since
K/Q
is Galois, all the
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
lying above
p
are conjugate. So
e_{1}=\cdots=e_{r}=e
(say) and
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{r}=f
(say). Then
efr=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
. Now, if
p
is unramified in
K
, then
e=1
, and then
fr=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
. Thus
N(\mathfrak{p}_{i})=p
if and only if
f=1
if and only if
p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\mathfrak{p}_{2}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}
, which is exactly complete splitting. Also, if
p
does not split completely, then

. Therefore,

, which means that no prime ideal has norm

. Finally, for unramified primes
p
, we have

\Lambda_{K}(p)=\begin{cases}[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log p,&\text{if and only if $p$ splits completely,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Since there are only finitely many ramified primes, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{|\mathfrak{h}(p)\Lambda_{K}(p)|^{2}}{p\log^{2}p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\ \text{unramified}\end{subarray}}\frac{|\mathfrak{h(p)}|^{2}|\Lambda_{K}(p)|^{2}}{p\log^{2}p}+O(1)

\displaystyle=[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\ \text{splits completely}\end{subarray}}\frac{|\mathfrak{h}(p)|^{2}}{p}+O(1)

\displaystyle=[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\ \text{splits completely}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2k}\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{4}+\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2k}\frac{a_{i}a_{j}}{2}\cos\left(|b_{i}-b_{j}|\log p\right)\right)

\displaystyle=[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2k}\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\ \text{splits completely}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}+\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2k}\frac{a_{i}a_{j}}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\ \text{splits completely}\end{subarray}}\frac{\cos\left(|b_{i}-b_{j}|\log p\right)}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=[K:\mathbb{Q}]\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2k}\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{4}\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{1}{p}+\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2k}\frac{a_{i}a_{j}}{2}\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{\cos\left(|b_{i}-b_{j}|\log p\right)}{p}\right)+O(1),

where in the last step, we used Lemma
3.8
.
Hence, by Mertens’ theorem and Lemma
3.3
, we conclude the proof of our proposition.
∎

|\Lambda_{K}(p)|\leqslant[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log p
,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{T^{\alpha_{j}}<p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{j+1}}}\frac{|\mathfrak{h}(p)|^{2}\left|\Lambda_{K}(p)\right|^{2}}{p\log^{2}p}\right)^{M}\leqslant\left([K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}\sum_{T^{\alpha_{j}}<p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{j+1}}}\frac{|\mathfrak{h}(p)|^{2}}{p}\right)^{M}<C^{M},

where
C=10[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}a^{2}
. Also,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{C\alpha_{j+1}^{1/2}}{20[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\right)^{M}\leqslant\exp\left(-\frac{M}{2}\log\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{j+1}}\right)+M\log C\right)\leqslant\exp\left(-\frac{\log\left(1/\alpha_{j+1}\right)}{21[K:\mathbb{Q}]\alpha_{j+1}}\right).

Since
\mathcal{J}\ll\log\log\log T
,
\alpha_{0}=0
,
\alpha_{1}=1/(\log\log T)^{2}
, Proposition
3.9
and (
3.3
) give

\sum_{p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{1}}}\frac{\left|\mathfrak{h}(p)\right|^{2}\left|\Lambda_{K}(p)\right|^{2}}{p\log^{2}p}\leqslant\log B.

Now by putting
j=0
, the left hand side of (
5.2
) is
meas
(\mathcal{S}(0))
as the Dirichlet polynomial
\sum_{p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{j}}}
is empty. So the above bounds helps us to obtain that

\displaystyle\text{meas}(\mathcal{S}(0))

\displaystyle\ll T(\log\log\log T)(\log B)\exp\left(-\frac{\log\left(1/\alpha_{1}\right)}{21[K:\mathbb{Q}]\alpha_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll T(\log\log\log T)(\log B)\exp\left(-\frac{2\log\log\log T}{21[K:\mathbb{Q}]}(\log\log T)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\ll T\exp\left(-\frac{(\log\log T)^{2}}{10[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\right),

as required in Lemma
4.2
. Again for
1\leqslant j\leqslant\mathcal{J}-1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}-j\leqslant\frac{\log(1/\alpha_{j})}{\log 20}.

Using the above bounds from
5.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{t\in\mathcal{S}(j)}\exp\left(2\Re\sum_{p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{j}}}\frac{\Theta_{T^{\alpha_{j}}}(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\mathfrak{h}(p)\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ll\log\left(1/\alpha_{j}\right)TB\exp\left(-\frac{\log\left(1/\alpha_{j+1}\right)}{21[K:\mathbb{Q}]\alpha_{j+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll TB\exp\left(-\frac{\log\left(1/\alpha_{j+1}\right)}{100[K:\mathbb{Q}]\alpha_{j+1}}\right).

Hence, we conclude the proof of our lemma.

5.3.
Proof of Lemma
4.3

In this section, we take into account the contribution from the second Dirichlet polynomial

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leqslant\log T}\frac{\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})}{p^{1+2it}\log p}.

We prove only the estimate (
4.3
). The proof of (
4.4
) follows along similar lines, and therefore we omit the details. We start with the following integral

\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\exp\left(2\Re\left(\sum_{p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}\frac{\Theta_{T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\mathfrak{h}(p)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\leqslant\log T}\frac{\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})}{p^{1+2it}\log p}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}t.

(5.26)

To estimate the above integral, we consider a dyadic decomposition on integer parameters
m
satisfying
0\leqslant m\leqslant\log\log T/\log 2
. For every such
m
, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{m}(t):=\sum_{2^{m}<p\leqslant 2^{m+1}}\frac{\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})}{2p^{1+2it}\log p},

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(m):=\left\{t\in[T,2T]:\left|\Re\mathcal{P}_{m}(t)\right|>2^{-m/10},\ \text{but}\ \left|\Re\mathcal{P}_{n}(t)\right|\leqslant 2^{-n/10}\ \text{for all}\ m<n\leqslant\frac{\log\log T}{\log 2}\right\}.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leqslant\log T}\frac{\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})}{2p^{1+2it}\log p}=\sum_{0\leqslant m\leqslant\frac{\log\log T}{\log 2}}\mathcal{P}_{m}(t)+O(1),

(5.27)

where the error term follows from the following estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{\log T<p\leqslant 2\log T}\frac{\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})}{2p^{1+2it}\log p}\ll[K:\mathbb{Q}]\sum_{\log T<p\leqslant 2\log T}\frac{|\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})|}{p}\ll_{K}a(\log\log(2\log T)-\log\log\log T)\ll_{\mathbf{a},K}1,

where
|\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})|<\sum_{j=1}^{2k}a_{j}|p^{-2ib_{j}}|=a.

Therefore, the integral (
5.26
) can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leqslant m\leqslant\frac{\log\log T}{\log 2}}\left(\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{P}(m)}+\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}\cap\left(\cap_{m}\mathcal{P}(m)^{c}\right)}\right)\exp\left(2\Re\left(\sum_{p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}\frac{\Theta_{T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\mathfrak{h}(p)+\sum_{p\leqslant\log T}\frac{\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})}{2p^{1+2it}\log p}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}t.

(5.28)

To obtain (
4.3
), we estimate the integrals on the right-hand side of (
5.28
). Suppose that
t\notin\mathcal{P}(m)
for every
m
, and
|\Re P_{m}(t)|>2^{-m/10}
for some
m
. Let
L
be the largest integer such that
|\Re P_{L}(t)|>2^{-L/10}
. Then
|\Re P_{n}(t)|\leqslant 2^{-n/10}
for all
L+1\leqslant n\leqslant\frac{\log\log T}{\log 2}
, which implies that
t\in\mathcal{P}(L)
, a contradiction. Thus we have
\left|\Re\mathcal{P}_{n}(t)\right|\leqslant 2^{-n/10}
for all
n\leqslant\log\log T/\log 2
which implies
\Re\sum_{p\leqslant\log T}\frac{\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})}{2p^{1+2it}\log p}=O(1)
. Therefore, by Lemma
4.1
the contribution of such
t
to the integral
\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}}
can be bounded and we get

\displaystyle\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}\cap\left(\cap_{m}\mathcal{P}(m)^{c}\right)}\exp\left(2\Re\left(\sum_{p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}\frac{\Theta_{T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}(p)}{2p^{1/2+it}}\mathfrak{h}(p)+\sum_{p\leqslant\log T}\frac{\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})}{2p^{1+2it}\log p}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ \ \ll\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}\cap\left(\cap_{m}\mathcal{P}(m)^{c}\right)}\exp\left(2\Re\left(\sum_{p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}\frac{\Theta_{T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\mathfrak{h}(p)\right)\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ \ \ll T(\log T)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}](a_{1}^{2}+\ldots+a_{2k}^{2})/4}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2k}g\left(|b_{i}-b_{j}|\right)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]\frac{a_{i}a_{j}}{2}}.

(5.29)

Now we want to estimate the first integral on the right hand side of (
5.28
) with
0\leqslant m\leqslant\log\log T/\log 2
. By (
5.27
), for the case that
0\leqslant m\leqslant 2\log\log\log T/\log 2
,

\displaystyle\left|\Re\sum_{p\leqslant\log T}\frac{\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})}{2p^{1+2it}\log p}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{0\leqslant n\leqslant\frac{\log\log T}{\log 2}}\left|\Re\mathcal{P}_{n}(t)\right|+O(1)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{0\leqslant n\leqslant m}\left|\Re\mathcal{P}_{n}(t)\right|+\sum_{m<n\leqslant\frac{\log\log T}{\log 2}}\left|\Re\mathcal{P}_{n}(t)\right|+O(1)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}{2}\sum_{p\leqslant 2^{m+1}}\frac{|\Re\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})|}{p}+\sum_{m<n\leqslant\frac{\log\log T}{\log 20}}\frac{1}{2^{n/10}}+O(1)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]a}{4}\sum_{p\leqslant 2^{m+1}}\frac{1}{p}+\sum_{m<n\leqslant\frac{\log\log T}{\log 20}}\frac{1}{2^{n/10}}+O(1).

(5.30)

Now, by the definition of
\mathcal{P}(m)
and (
5.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\Re\left(\sum_{p\leqslant 2^{m+1}}\frac{\Theta_{T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\mathfrak{h}(p)+\sum_{p\leqslant\log T}\frac{\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})}{2p^{1+2it}\log p}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{p\leqslant 2^{m+1}}\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]a}{\sqrt{p}}+\sum_{p\leqslant 2^{m+1}}\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]a}{2p}+\sum_{m<n\leqslant\frac{\log\log T}{\log 20}}\frac{1}{2^{n/10}}+O(1)=O\left(a[K:\mathbb{Q}]2^{m/2}\right)

and then we have

\displaystyle\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{P}(m)}\exp\left(2\Re\left(\sum_{p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}\frac{\Theta_{T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\mathfrak{h}(p)+\sum_{p\leqslant\log T}\frac{\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})}{2p^{1+2it}\log p}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{O\left(a[K:\mathbb{Q}]2^{m/2}\right)}\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{P}(m)}\exp\left(2\Re\sum_{2^{m+1}<p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}\frac{\Theta_{T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\mathfrak{h}(p)\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{O\left(a[K:\mathbb{Q}]2^{m/2}\right)}\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\exp\left(2\Re\sum_{2^{m+1}<p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}\frac{\Theta_{T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\mathfrak{h}(p)\right)\left(2^{m/10}\Re\mathcal{P}_{m}(t)\right)^{2N}\mathrm{d}t,

(5.31)

where
N:=[2^{3m/4}]
. In the last line, we have used the identity
\left|\Re\mathcal{P}_{m}(t)\right|^{N}=\left(\Re\mathcal{P}_{m}(t)\right)^{N}
as

Now similarly as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, we see

\displaystyle\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\exp\left(2\Re\sum_{2^{m+1}<q\leqslant T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}\frac{\Theta_{T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\mathfrak{h}(p)\right)\left(\Re\mathcal{P}_{m}(t)\right)^{2N}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ll T\exp\left(\sum_{p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}\frac{\left|\mathfrak{h}(p)\right|^{2}\left|\Lambda_{K}(p)\right|^{2}}{p\log^{2}p}\mathbf{1}_{(2^{m+1},T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}]}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\sum_{2^{m}<p_{1},\ldots,p_{N}\leqslant 2^{m+1}}\frac{\Lambda_{K}(p_{1}^{2})\cdots\Lambda_{K}(p_{2N}^{2})}{\log p_{1}\cdots\log p_{2N}}\frac{\mathfrak{h}(p_{1}^{2})\cdots\mathfrak{h}(p_{2N}^{2})}{p_{1}\cdots p_{2N}}\Upsilon(p_{1}\cdots p_{2N})2^{mN/5}

\displaystyle\ll T\exp\left(\sum_{2^{m+1}<p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}\frac{\left|\mathfrak{h}(p)\right|^{2}\left|\Lambda_{K}(p)\right|^{2}}{p\log^{2}p}\right)\left(\sum_{2^{m}<p\leqslant 2^{m+1}}\frac{|\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})|^{2}\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})|^{2}}{2p^{2}\log^{2}p}\right)^{N}\times\frac{2^{mN/5}(2N)!}{2^{2N}N!}.

(5.32)

By Stirling approximation,

\frac{2^{mN/5}(2N)!}{2^{2N}N!}\ll\frac{2^{mN/5}}{2^{2N}}\left(\frac{4N}{e}\right)^{N}\ll\left(\frac{2^{m/5}N}{e}\right)^{N}\leqslant\left(2^{m/5}\cdot 2^{3m/4}\right)^{[2^{3m/4}]}.

(5.33)

Since,

\displaystyle\sum_{2^{m}<p\leqslant 2^{m+1}}\frac{|\mathfrak{h}(p^{2})|^{2}|\Lambda_{K}(p^{2})|^{2}}{2p^{2}\log^{2}p}\leqslant[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}a^{2}\sum_{2^{m}<p\leqslant 2^{m+1}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\leqslant[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}a^{2}\sum_{2^{m}<p\leqslant 2^{m+1}}\frac{1}{2^{2m}}=[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}a^{2}2^{-m}.

(5.34)

5.3

5.3

5.33

5.34

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle e^{O\left(a[K:\mathbb{Q}]2^{m/2}\right)}\left([K:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}a^{2}2^{m/5}\cdot 2^{3m/4}\cdot 2^{-m}\right)^{[2^{3m/4}]}T\exp\left(\sum_{2^{m+1}<p\leqslant T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}}\frac{\left|\mathfrak{h}(p)\right|^{2}\left|\Lambda_{K}(p)\right|^{2}}{p\log^{2}p}\right)
